On the Identifiability from Modulo Measurements under DFT Sensing Matrix
Qi Zhang, Jiang Zhu, Fengzhong Qu, Zheng Zhu, De Wen Soh

Abstract
Unlimited sampling was recently introduced to deal with the clipping or saturation of measurements where a modulo operator is applied before sampling. In this paper, we investigate the identifiability of the model where measurements are acquired under a discrete Fourier transform (DFT) sensing matrix first followed by a modulo operator (modulo-DFT). Firstly, based on the theorems of cyclotomic polynomials, we derive a sufficient condition for uniquely identifying the original signal in modulo-DFT. Additionally, for periodic bandlimited signals (PBSs) under unlimited sampling which can be viewed as a special case of modulo-DFT, the necessary and sufficient condition for the unique recovery of the original signal are provided. Moreover, we show that when the oversampling factor exceeds 3 ​ ( 1 + 1 / P ) 3(1+1/P) , PBS is always identifiable from the modulo samples, where P P is the number of harmonics including the fundamental component in the positive frequency part.

In this paper, we explore the identifiability of a model where measurements are obtained under discrete Fourier transform (DFT) sensing matrix first, followed by a modulo operator (modulo-DFT). Discrete Fourier transform has been widely applied in signal processing, and despite there being a one-to-one mapping between the original signal and the measurements in the absence of the modulo operator, the identifiability of the modulo-DFT model remains unknown. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: First, we derive a sufficient condition for the unique recovery of the original signal from the modulo measurements in the modulo-DFT model based on the theorems of cyclotomic polynomials. In addition, we show that PBSs can be viewed as a special case of modulo-DFT, and the necessary and sufficient condition for the unique identifiability with an ambiguity in the direct current (DC) component are provided. Finally, we show that when the oversampling factor exceeds
3(1+1/P)
, then the PBS can be uniquely identified with an ambiguity in the DC component, where
P
is the number of harmonics including the fundamental component in the positive frequency part.

Identifiability

In this section, a sufficient condition for uniquely identifying the original signal
\mathbf{x}
in modulo-DFT is provided. Before introducing the final theorem, two lemmas are proved in advance. Let
\mathcal{R}_{N}\triangleq\{1,{\rm e}^{{\rm j}2\pi\frac{1}{N}},\cdots,{\rm e}^{{\rm j}2\pi\frac{N-1}{N}}\}
be the set of all roots of

, let

be an integer coefficient polynomial with degree

. In addition, let
\mathcal{R}_{f}\triangleq\{x|f(x)=0\}
be the set of roots of the polynomial

, and
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{R}_{N}\cap\mathcal{R}_{f}
. Lemma
1
shows the relationship between integer coefficient polynomial

and
\mathcal{X}
based on the theorems of cyclotomic polynomials.

Lemma 1

For an integer coefficient polynomial
f(x)
with degree
N
, let
\mathcal{N}\triangleq\{n;n|N\}
denote the set containing all divisors of
N
. The equation
f(x)=A(x^{N}-1)
, where

\forall n\in\mathcal{N},\exists x\in\mathcal{X}
such that
\Phi_{n}(x)=0
where
\Phi_{n}(x)
is the
n

Proof 1

We first prove the necessary part. When

, according to the property that

\displaystyle x^{N}-1=\prod_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\Phi_{n}(x),

(5)

we have
\mathcal{R}_{f}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\{x|\Phi_{n}(x)=0\}
. In addition, we have
\mathcal{R}_{f}=\mathcal{R}_{N}=\mathcal{X}
. Thus, the necessary part can be easily proved.

For the sufficient part, let
T=|\mathcal{N}|
be the cardinality of
\mathcal{N}
and

is a sorted list of
\mathcal{N}
.
r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{T}\in\mathcal{X}
are the roots of cyclotomic polynomials
\Phi_{n_{1}}(x),\Phi_{n_{2}}(x),\ldots,\Phi_{n_{T}}(x)
, respectively. Since the roots of different cyclotomic polynomials must be distinct, it follows that for

. According to the polynomial remainder theorem, we have

\displaystyle f(x)=P_{1}(x)\Phi_{n_{T}}(x)+R_{1}(x),

(6)

where
P_{1}(x)
and
R_{1}(x)
are polynomials with integer coefﬁcients and the degree of

is less than that of
\Phi_{n_{T}}(x)
.
For
f(r_{T})=\Phi_{n_{T}}(r_{T})=0
, we have
R_{1}(r_{T})=0
. Since
\Phi_{n_{T}}(x)
is the minimal polynomial with integer coefficients having a root at
r_{T}
, this implies that
R_{1}(x)=0
. Therefore, we have
f(x)=P_{1}(x)\Phi_{n_{T}}(x)
.
According to the equation
N=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\rm deg}(\Phi_{n_{i}}(x))
where
{\rm deg}(\Phi_{n_{i}}(x))
is the degree of the polynomial
\Phi_{n_{i}}(x)
, we have
{\rm deg}(P_{1}(x))=N-{\rm deg}(\Phi_{n_{T}}(x))\geq{\rm deg}(\Phi_{n_{T-1}}(x))
. Similar to equation (
6
), according to the polynomial remainder

can be written as

\displaystyle P_{1}(x)=P_{2}(x)\Phi_{n_{T-1}}(x)+R_{2}(x),

(7)

where
P_{2}(x)
and
R_{2}(x)
are integer coefficient polynomials.
We have
P_{1}(r_{T-1})=0

\Phi_{n_{T}}(r_{T-1})\neq 0

Similarly, as

is the minimal polynomial with integer coefficients having a root at

. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle f(x)=P_{n_{T}}(x)\Phi_{n_{1}}(x)\cdots\Phi_{n_{T-1}}(x)\Phi_{n_{T}}(x).

(8)

According to equation (
5

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{n=0}^{N-1}\upsilon^{*}_{n+1}{r^{*}}^{n}=0,

\displaystyle D(r^{*})

\displaystyle D_{R}(r)\triangleq\sum\nolimits_{n=0}^{N-1}\Re\{\upsilon_{n+1}\}r^{n}=0,\forall r\in\mathcal{R}.

{\rm gcd}(n+4,n)=2
; thus
{\rm gcd}(N,t-1)=N/n
. For Case
4
, we have
{\rm gcd}(\frac{n+2}{2},n)=1
as
{\rm gcd}(n+2,n)=2
; thus
{\rm gcd}(N,t-1)=N/n
.

We now prove the necessary part by contradiction, i.e.,
\exists n\in\mathcal{N}
and
n\neq 1
such that
N<h(n)(P+1)
, we have that
\mathcal{P}
does not satisfy SCP. In the following, we will show that
\mathcal{P}
does not satisfy the second condition of SCP for all the four cases if
N<h(n)(P+1)
. Below, we will prove that there does not exist an integer in
\mathcal{Q}\triangleq(\mathcal{P}-1)n/N
that is coprime to
n
. For Case
1
,
N<h(n)(P+1)
is equivalent to
\frac{n(P+1)}{N}>\frac{n-1}{2}
. Therefore, the minimum value in
\mathcal{Q}
is greater than
\frac{n-1}{2}
and the maximum value in
\mathcal{Q}
is less than
\frac{n+1}{2}
. As
n
is an odd number, there is no integer in
\mathcal{Q}
. For Case
2
,
N<h(n)(P+1)
is equivalent to
\frac{n(P+1)}{N}>1
. Similarly, it can be obtained that there is no integer in
\mathcal{Q}
. For Case
3
,
N<h(n)(P+1)
is equivalent to
\frac{n(P+1)}{N}>\frac{n-4}{2}
. Therefore, the minimum value in
\mathcal{Q}
is greater than
\frac{n-4}{2}
and the maximum value in
\mathcal{Q}
is less than
\frac{n+4}{2}
, and there are three integers in
\mathcal{Q}
which are
n/2-1,n/2,n/2+1
. As
n/2-1
and
n/2+1
are even integers, and
n>2
, thus
n/2-1,n/2,n/2+1
are not coprime to
n
. For Case
4
,
N<h(n)(P+1)
is equivalent to
\frac{n(P+1)}{N}>\frac{n-2}{2}
. Therefore, the minimum value in
\mathcal{Q}
is greater than
\frac{n-2}{2}
and the maximum value in
\mathcal{Q}
is less than
\frac{n+2}{2}
. As
4|n
,
n/2
is the only integer in
\mathcal{Q}
and is an even number. Therefore,
n/2
is not coprime to
n
. These prove the necessary part.

V
Conclusion

This paper investigates the identifiability of the modulo-DFT model. We provide a sufficient condition for the unique recovery of the original signal from modulo measurements. Additionally, we analyze the identifiability of PBSs, which can be considered as a special case of the modulo-DFT model. We introduce the necessary and sufficient condition for the unique identifiability of the PBSs. Furthermore, we show that the PBS can be uniquely identified when the oversampling rate exceeds
3(1+1/P)
from the modulo samples, where
P
is the number of harmonics including the fundamental component in the positive frequency part.

Analyticity of Steklov Eigenvalues of Nearly-Hyperspherical Domains in ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1}
Chee Han Tan, Robert Viator

Abstract
. We consider the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator (DNO) on nearly-hyperspherical domains in dimension greater than 3. Treating such domains as perturbations of the ball, we prove the analytic dependence of the DNO on the shape perturbation parameter for fixed perturbation functions. Consequently, we conclude that the Steklov eigenvalues are analytic in the shape perturbation parameter as well. To obtain these results, we use the strategy of Nicholls and Nigam (2004), and of Viator and Osting (2020); we transform the Laplace-Dirichlet problem on the perturbed domain to a more complicated, parameter-dependent equation on the ball, and then geometrically bound the Neumann expansion of the transformed DNO. These results are a generalization of the work of Viator and Osting (2020) for dimension 2 and 3.

1.
Introduction

For any integer
d\geq 3
, we call
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
a
nearly-hyperspherical domain
if it is a small perturbation of the unit
(d+1)
-ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\left\{(r,\hat{\theta}):0\leq r\leq 1+\varepsilon\rho(\hat{\theta}),\,\hat{\theta}\in S^{d}\right\}.

Here,
\rho\in C^{s+2}(S^{d})
is the
domain perturbation function
for some
s\in\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
,
S^{d}
is the unit
d
-sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, and
\varepsilon\geq 0
is the
perturbation parameter
which we assumed to be small in magnitude.

For fixed
\rho\in C^{s+2}(S^{d})
, we consider the Steklov eigenvalue problem on the nearly-hyperspherical domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
:

(1)

\displaystyle\Delta u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\textnormal{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\rho,\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\textnormal{ on }\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon},

where
\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\rho,\varepsilon}}=\mathbf{n}_{\rho,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla
is the unit outward normal derivative on the boundary
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
of the perturbed domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. It is well-known
[
GP17
]
that the Steklov spectrum is discrete, real, and nonnegative, and we arrange them in non-decreasing order

0=\sigma_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})<\sigma_{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\leq\sigma_{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\leq\dots,

tending to infinity. The Steklov spectrum matches the spectrum of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator (DNO),
G_{\rho,\varepsilon}\colon H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon})\to H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon})
, given by

\xi\mapsto G_{\rho,\varepsilon}\xi=\mathbf{n}_{\rho,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\rvert_{r=1+\varepsilon\rho},

where
v_{\varepsilon}
is the
harmonic extension
of
\xi
to
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, satisfying

(2)

\displaystyle\Delta v_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\textnormal{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\xi

\displaystyle\textnormal{ on }\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

In previous work
[
VO20
]
, it was proven that
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
is analytic with respect to the perturbation parameter
\varepsilon>0
for nearly-circular domain (
d=1
) and nearly-spherical domain (
d=2
). The goal of this paper is to show that the same result holds for
d\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\rho\in C^{s+2}(S^{d})
, then the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator,
G_{\rho,\varepsilon}\colon H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon})\to H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon})
, is analytic in the perturbation parameter
\varepsilon
. More precisely, if
\rho\in C^{s+2}(S^{d})
, then there exists a Neumann series

G_{\rho,\varepsilon}(\xi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{n}G_{\rho,n}(\xi)

that converges strongly as an operator from
H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})
to
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})
. That is, there exists constants
K_{1}=K_{1}(B,d,s)>0
and
\alpha=\alpha(d)>1
such that

\|G_{\rho,n}\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}\leq K_{1}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{n}

for
A>\alpha\max\{2K_{0}C_{0}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}},M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}\}
.

We prove Theorem
1.1
for all
d\geq 3
simultaneously: the proof can be found in section
5
. The proof follows the strategy of
[
NN04
,
VO20
]
. We first show the analyticity of the harmonic extension,
i.e.,
that for fixed
\xi:\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
the solution
v_{\varepsilon}
to (
2
) is analytic in
\varepsilon>0
; see Section
4
. Using this and a recursive formula for the DNO
G_{\rho,\varepsilon}
, we then prove that
G_{\rho,\varepsilon}
also depends analytically on
\varepsilon>0
, establishing Theorem
1.1
.

The next corollary follows from
[
Kat13
]
and Theorem
1.1
, establishing the analytic dependence on
\varepsilon
of the Steklov eigenvalues
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
of (
1
) within the same disk of convergence as in Theorem
1.1
:

Corollary 1.2
.

The Steklov eigenvalues
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
of (
1
) consist of branches of one or several analytic functions which have at most algebraic singularities near
\varepsilon=0
. The same is true of the corresponding eigenprojections.

The motivation of this paper is to lay the groundwork for the asymptotic study of Steklov eigenvalues of nearly-hyperspherical domains in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
for
d\geq 3
. Asymptotic and perturbative methods have already yielded meaningful results in the study of Steklov shape optimization in two and three dimensions
[
VO18
,
VO22
]
. There, the authors begin with a nearly-circular domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or a nearly-spherical domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and, using the analyticity result proved in
[
VO20
]
, demonstrate that the ball is
not
a shape optimizer for a large class of Steklov eigenvalues, while also obtaining a collection of Steklov eigenvalues which
are
(locally) optimized by the ball. This paper provides the foundation for future work using similar techniques for nearly-hyperspherical domains in dimension greater than three.

2.
Laplacian in Hyperspherical Coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

Given
d\geq 1
, let
\left(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d+1}\right)
denote the
(d+1)
-dimensional Cartesian coordinates. We define the
(d+1)
-dimensional hyperspherical coordinates
\left(r,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{d}\right)
as follows:

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=r\cos\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}\sin\theta_{3}\dots\sin\theta_{d},

\displaystyle x_{2}

\displaystyle=r\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}\sin\theta_{3}\dots\sin\theta_{d},

\displaystyle x_{j}

\displaystyle=r\cos\theta_{j-1}\sin\theta_{j}\sin\theta_{j+1}\dots\sin\theta_{d},\ \ j=3,4,\dots,d,

\displaystyle x_{d+1}

\displaystyle=r\cos\theta_{d},

where
r\geq 0
is the radius of a
(d+1)
-dimensional sphere,
0\leq\theta_{1}\leq 2\pi
is the azimuth, and
0\leq\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\dots,\theta_{d}\leq\pi
are the inclinations. In particular, these correspond to polar coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2})=(r\cos\theta_{1},r\sin\theta_{1})
for
d=1
and spherical coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(r\cos\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2},r\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2},r\cos\theta_{2})
for
d=2
.

The hyperspherical coordinates are an orthogonal curvilinear coordinate system in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. The associated metric tensor
g
is therefore diagonal, with components

g_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{d+1}\frac{\partial x_{k}}{\partial\theta_{i}}\frac{\partial x_{k}}{\partial\theta_{j}}=h_{i}^{2}\delta_{ij},\ \ 0\leq i,j\leq d,

where
\theta_{0}=r
and the scale factors are defined as
h_{0}=1
and
h_{i}=r\prod_{k=i+1}^{d}\sin\theta_{k}
for
i=1,2,\dots,d
; the latter includes the empty product, which gives
h_{d}=r
. The volume element
dV
in hyperspherical coordinates is given by

(3)

dV=\sqrt{\left\lvert{\det{g}}\right\rvert}\,dr\,d\theta_{1}\dots d\theta_{d}=\left(r^{d}\prod_{k=2}^{d}\sin^{k-1}\theta_{k}\right)dr\,d\theta_{1}\dots d\theta_{d}.

For notational convenience, we introduce new variables
\eta_{i}=h_{i}/r
for
i=1,2,\dots,d
and
h=\sqrt{\left\lvert{\det g}\right\rvert}
. The gradient operator in hyperspherical coordinates is defined as

(4)

\nabla=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{1}{h_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\nabla_{S^{d}},

where
\hat{\boldsymbol{r}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{1},\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{2},\dots,\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}
are orthonormal hyperspherical basis vectors. The Laplacian is given in terms of the metric tensor by

(5)

\Delta=\frac{1}{h}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}\left(\frac{h}{h_{i}^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}\right).

Since
h_{i}
is independent of
\theta_{i}
and
h=h_{0}h_{1}\dots h_{d}
is separable in hyperspherical coordinates, (
5
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{1}{h_{i}^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{h}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}\left(h\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}\right)=\frac{1}{r^{d}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{S^{d}},

where
\Delta_{S^{d}}
is the spherical Laplacian on the unit
d
-sphere
S^{d}
, given by

(6)

\Delta_{S^{d}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}^{2}\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}\left(\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}\right).

For
d=2
, we recover the Laplacian in spherical coordinates
(r,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(r,\phi,\theta)
:

\Delta=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\phi^{2}}+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)\right].

3.
Change of Variables

For notational convenience, let
\partial_{r}
and
\partial_{i}
denote the partial derivative with respect to
r
and
\theta_{i}
, respectively, for
i=1,2,\dots,d
. We first consider the problem of harmonically extending a function
\xi(\hat{\theta})
from
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
to
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
:

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{d}}\partial_{r}\left(r^{d}\partial_{r}v\right)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{S^{d}}v

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\textnormal{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=\xi

\displaystyle\textnormal{ on }\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

Following
[
NR01
,
NN04
,
VO20
]
, we introduce the change of variables

(8)

(r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})=\left(\frac{r}{1+\varepsilon\rho(\hat{\theta})},\hat{\theta}\right)

which maps
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
to the unit
(d+1)
-ball
B\coloneqq\Omega_{0}
. The partial derivatives in the new coordinates are given by

\partial_{r}=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\rho(\theta^{\prime})}\partial_{r^{\prime}},\qquad\partial_{i}=\partial_{i^{\prime}}-\frac{\varepsilon r^{\prime}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho(\theta^{\prime})}{1+\varepsilon\rho(\theta^{\prime})}\partial_{r^{\prime}},\ \ i=1,2,\dots,d.

The harmonic extension
v
transforms to

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})=v\left((1+\varepsilon\rho(\theta^{\prime}))r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\right).

The radial component of the Laplacian transforms to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{d}}\partial_{r}\left(r^{d}\partial_{r}v\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{d}(r^{\prime})^{d}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\rho}\partial_{r^{\prime}}\left((1+\varepsilon\rho)^{d}(r^{\prime})^{d}\cdot\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\rho}\partial_{r^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}(r^{\prime})^{d}}\partial_{r^{\prime}}\left((r^{\prime})^{d}\partial_{r^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}\right).

Referring to (
6
), the angular component of the Laplacian transforms to

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\left(\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}\,\partial_{i}v\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,\partial_{i^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}\right)-\frac{\varepsilon r^{\prime}\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho}{1+\varepsilon\rho}\partial_{r^{\prime}}\partial_{i^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\qquad-\varepsilon r^{\prime}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho}{1+\varepsilon\rho}\partial_{r^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,(\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho)^{2}}{(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}r^{\prime}\partial_{r^{\prime}}\left(r^{\prime}\partial_{r^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,\partial_{i^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}\right)-\frac{\varepsilon r^{\prime}\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho}{1+\varepsilon\rho}\partial_{r^{\prime}}\partial_{i^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\qquad-\varepsilon r^{\prime}\left[{\frac{\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho}{1+\varepsilon\rho}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\partial_{r^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}}+\left[\frac{\partial_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho\right)}{1+\varepsilon\rho}-\frac{\varepsilon\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,(\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho)^{2}}{(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}\right]\partial_{r^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,(\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho)^{2}}{(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}\Big[(r^{\prime})^{2}\partial_{r^{\prime}}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}+r^{\prime}\partial_{r^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}\Big]

\displaystyle=\partial_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,\partial_{i^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}\right)-\frac{\varepsilon r^{\prime}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho\right)}{1+\varepsilon\rho}\partial_{r^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2\varepsilon r^{\prime}\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho}{1+\varepsilon\rho}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\partial_{r^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}(r^{\prime})^{2}\sin^{i-1}\theta_{i}^{\prime}\,(\partial_{i^{\prime}}\rho)^{2}}{(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}\left[\partial_{r^{\prime}}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{2}{r^{\prime}}\partial_{r^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}\right].

Substituting the transformed radial and angular components into the Laplace’s equation (
7
), substituting
r^{-2}=(r^{\prime})^{-2}(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{-2}
in front of
\Delta_{S^{d}}
, multiplying by
(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{4}
, and dropping the primes on the transformed variables, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{4}\Delta v

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon\rho)}{r}(\Delta_{S^{d}}\rho)\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}-\frac{2\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon\rho)}{r}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\partial_{i}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(\partial_{i}\rho)^{2}}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\Big[\partial_{r}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{2}{r}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}\Big].

Finally, collecting terms of order
\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon^{2}
, we see that
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the following transformed harmonic extension problem:

(9)

\displaystyle\Delta u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon L_{1}u_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{2}L_{2}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\textnormal{ in }B,

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(1,\theta)

\displaystyle=\xi(\theta)

\displaystyle\textnormal{ on }S^{d},

where
\theta=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{d})
and

\displaystyle L_{1}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=-2\rho\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+(\Delta_{S^{d}}\rho)r^{-1}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}^{2}}r^{-1}\partial_{i}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle L_{2}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=-\rho^{2}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+(\rho\Delta_{S^{d}}\rho)r^{-1}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}+2\rho\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}^{2}}r^{-1}\partial_{i}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(\partial_{i}\rho)^{2}}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\Big[\partial_{r}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}+2r^{-1}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}\Big]

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\rho L_{1}u_{\varepsilon}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(\partial_{i}\rho)^{2}}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\Big[\partial_{r}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}+2r^{-1}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}\Big].

4.
Analyticity of Harmonic Extension

In this section we show that the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of the transformed harmonic extension problem (
9
) is analytic with respect to
\varepsilon
. We begin by formally expanding
u_{\varepsilon}
as a power series in
\varepsilon
:

(10)

u_{\varepsilon}(r,\theta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}(r,\theta)\varepsilon^{n}.

Substituting (
10
) into (
9
) and collecting terms in powers of
\varepsilon
, we see that
u_{0}
satisfies

(11)

\displaystyle\Delta u_{0}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\textnormal{ in }B,

\displaystyle u_{0}(1,\theta)

\displaystyle=\xi(\theta)

\displaystyle\textnormal{ on }S^{d},

and
u_{n}
for
n=1,2,\dots
satisfies the following recursive formula:

(12)

\displaystyle\Delta u_{n}

\displaystyle=L_{1}u_{n-1}+L_{2}u_{n-2}

\displaystyle\textnormal{ in }B,

\displaystyle u_{n}(1,\theta)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\textnormal{ on }S^{d}.

Our first lemma establishes an elliptic estimate for the Poisson’s equation with Dirichlet boundary data, which is analogous to
[
VO20
, Lemma 3.1]
. We now review some basic facts about hyperspherical harmonics before stating Lemma
4.1
; see
[
Ave12
]
for more details.

For
\ell=0,1,2,\dots
, a hyperspherical harmonic of order
\ell
on
S^{d}
is the restriction to
S^{d}
of a homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree
\ell
. Let
\mathbf{H}_{\ell}^{d}
denote the space of all hyperspherical harmonics of order
\ell
on
S^{d}
. The space
\mathbf{H}_{\ell}^{d}
has an orthonormal basis
\{Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta)\}_{m=1}^{N(d,\ell)}
with respect to the
L^{2}
-inner product over
S^{d}
, where
N(d,\ell)
is the dimension of
\mathbf{H}_{\ell}^{d}
. It is well-known that each
Y_{\ell}^{m}
is an eigenfunction of
-\Delta_{S^{d}}
with corresponding eigenvalue
\ell(\ell+d-1)
,
i.e.,

(13)

-\Delta_{S^{d}}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta)=\ell(\ell+d-1)Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta),\ \ 1\leq m\leq N(d,\ell).

A direct computation using Green’s identity, together with (
13
), gives the following integral identity:

(14)

\int_{S^{d}}\left\lvert{\nabla_{S^{d}}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta)}\right\rvert^{2}d\sigma_{d}(\theta)=\int_{S^{d}}\ell(\ell+d-1)\left\lvert{Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta)}\right\rvert^{2}d\sigma_{d}(\theta),

where
d\sigma_{d}
is the area element of
S^{d}
in hyperspherical coordinates, satisfying the relation
dV=d\sigma_{d}(\theta)\,r^{d}dr
; see (
3
). The sum of
\mathbf{H}_{\ell}^{d}
is dense in
L^{2}(S^{d})
, thus any
\xi\in L^{2}(S^{d})
can be expanded in hyperspherical harmonics:

(15)

\xi(\theta)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{N(d,\ell)}\hat{\xi}(\ell,m)Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta),\ \ \hat{\xi}(\ell,m)\coloneqq\int_{S^{d}}\xi(\theta)\overline{Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta)}\,d\sigma_{d}(\theta).

This leads us to define the Sobolev space
H^{s}(S^{d})
of order
s\geq 0
as the subspace of
L^{2}(S^{d})
with norm

\|\xi\|_{H^{s}(S^{d})}^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{N(d,\ell)}\left(1+\ell(\ell+d-1)\right)^{s}\left\lvert{\hat{\xi}(\ell,m)}\right\rvert^{2}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given
d\geq 3
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
K_{0}>0
such that for any
F\in H^{s-1}(B)
and
\xi\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})
, the solution of

\displaystyle\Delta w(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=F(r,\theta)

\displaystyle\qquad\textnormal{ in }B,

\displaystyle w(1,\theta)

\displaystyle=\xi(\theta)

\displaystyle\qquad\textnormal{ on }S^{d},

satisfies the following estimate:

\|w\|_{H^{s+1}(B)}\leq K_{0}\left(\|F\|_{H^{s-1}(B)}+\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}\right).

Proof.

We will prove the result for
s=0
only, as the proof for
s=1,2,\dots
is similar. Since
\xi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})
,
\xi
has a hyperspherical harmonic expansion (
15
). Let us decompose
w=\Phi+V
where

\Phi(r,\theta)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{N(d,\ell)}\hat{\xi}(\ell,m)r^{\ell}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta).

It is clear that
\Phi
is harmonic in
B
and
\Phi=\xi
on
S^{d}
, which means
V
solves the following boundary value problem:

(16a)

\displaystyle\Delta V(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=F(r,\theta)

\displaystyle\qquad\textnormal{ in }B,

(16b)

\displaystyle V(1,\theta)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\qquad\textnormal{ on }S^{d}.

Multiplying (
16a
) by
\overline{V}
and integrating by parts using (
16b
) yields

\displaystyle\|V\|_{H_{0}^{1}(B)}^{2}\coloneqq\|\nabla V\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}=-\langle F,V\rangle_{H^{-1}(B),H_{0}^{1}(B)},

where the latter denotes the duality pairing between
H_{0}^{1}(B)
and
H^{-1}(B)
. Using Poincaré inequality and duality, there exists a constant
C_{B}>0
such that

(17)

\|V\|_{H^{1}(B)}\leq C_{B}\|V\|_{H_{0}^{1}(B)}\leq C_{B}\|F\|_{H^{-1}(B)}.

It remains to show that
\|\Phi\|_{H^{1}(B)}
is controlled by
\|\xi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}
. Since
Y_{\ell}^{m}
is orthonormal in
L^{2}(S^{d})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,m}\left\lvert{\hat{\xi}(\ell,m)}\right\rvert^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{S^{d}}r^{2\ell}\left\lvert{Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta)}\right\rvert^{2}d\sigma_{d}(\theta)\right)r^{d}dr

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,m}\left\lvert{\hat{\xi}(\ell,m)}\right\rvert^{2}\int_{0}^{1}r^{2\ell+d}\,dr=\sum_{\ell,m}\left[\frac{1}{2\ell+d+1}\right]\left\lvert{\hat{\xi}(\ell,m)}\right\rvert^{2}.

Similarly, using (
4
) to compute
\nabla\Phi
and using the integral identity (
14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,m}\left\lvert{\hat{\xi}(\ell,m)}\right\rvert^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{S^{d}}\left(\ell^{2}r^{2(\ell-1)}\left\lvert{Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta)}\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{r^{2\ell}}{r^{2}}\left\lvert{\nabla_{S^{d}}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta)}\right\rvert^{2}\right)d\sigma_{d}(\theta)\right)r^{d}dr

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:SpHarmIBP}}}{{=}}\sum_{\ell,m}\left\lvert{\hat{\xi}(\ell,m)}\right\rvert^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{S^{d}}r^{2(\ell-1)}\left(\ell^{2}+\ell(\ell+d-1)\right)\left\lvert{Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta)}\right\rvert^{2}d\sigma_{d}(\theta)\right)r^{d}dr

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,m}\left\lvert{\hat{\xi}(\ell,m)}\right\rvert^{2}\int_{0}^{1}r^{2(\ell-1)+d}\left(\ell^{2}+\ell(\ell+d-1)\right)dr

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,m}\left[\frac{\ell^{2}+\ell(\ell+d-1)}{2\ell+d-1}\right]\left\lvert{\hat{\xi}(\ell,m)}\right\rvert^{2}.

This allows us to estimate
\|\Phi\|_{H^{1}(B)}^{2}=\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}+\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}
as

(18)

\|\Phi\|_{H^{1}(B)}^{2}=\sum_{\ell,m}\left[\frac{1}{2\ell+d+1}+\frac{\ell^{2}+\ell(\ell+d-1)}{2\ell+d-1}\right]\left\lvert{\hat{\xi}(\ell,m)}\right\rvert^{2}\leq C_{\frac{1}{2}}^{2}\|\xi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}^{2},

for some constant
C_{\frac{1}{2}}>0
. Finally, using
w=\Phi+V
, the desired estimate for
s=0
follows from (
17
) and (
18
) by choosing
K_{0}=\max\left\{C_{B},C_{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}
.
∎

Next we derive recursive estimates for the right hand side of (
12
), which is analogous to
[
VO20
, Lemma 3.2]
. We shall use the following inequalities from
[
NR01
,
NN04
]
: Given an integer
s\geq 0
and
\delta>0
, if
f\in C^{s}(S^{d})
,
u\in H^{s}(B)
,
g\in C^{s+\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(S^{d})
, and
\mu\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})
, there exists a constant
M=M(d,s)
such that

(19a)

\displaystyle\|fu\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\leq M(d,s)\left\lvert{f}\right\rvert_{C^{s}}\|u\|_{H^{s}(B)},

(19b)

\displaystyle\|g\mu\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq M(d,s)\left\lvert{g}\right\rvert_{C^{s+\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}\|\mu\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}.

Here,
C^{k}(S^{d})
is the space of all
C^{k}
-functions on
S^{d}
for integers
k\geq 0
and the Hölder space for nonintegers
k
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Given
d\geq 3
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\rho\in C^{s+2}(S^{d})
. Assume that
K_{1}
and
A
are constants so that

(20)

\|u_{n}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}\leq K_{1}A^{n}\qquad\textnormal{ for all }n<N.

If
A>M(d,s)\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}
, then there exists a constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(d,s)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|L_{1}u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}K_{1}A^{N-1},

\displaystyle\|L_{2}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}K_{1}A^{N-1}.

Proof.

For notation convenience, we suppress the dependence of
M
on
d
and
s
. Throughout this proof we will use the fact that both
\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s}}
and
\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+1}}
are bounded above by
\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}
. First, a close inspection on
L_{1}u_{N-1}
reveals that all differential operators acting on
u_{N-1}
are second order, since we may write
L_{1}u_{N-1}
as follows:

\displaystyle L_{1}u_{N-1}=-2\rho\cdot\Delta u_{N-1}+(\Delta_{S^{d}}\rho)\cdot\frac{\partial_{r}u_{N-1}}{r}+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}}\cdot\frac{1}{h_{i}}\partial_{i}\partial_{r}u_{N-1}.

Estimating each term in
L_{1}u_{N-1}
using the inequality (
19a
) and noting that
\partial_{i}\rho/\eta_{i}
is a first order partial derivative on
S^{d}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|L_{1}u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\leq\|2\rho\Delta u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s}(B)}+\|(\Delta_{S^{d}}\rho)\cdot r^{-1}\partial_{r}u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\|\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}}\cdot\frac{1}{h_{i}}\partial_{i}\partial_{r}u_{N-1}\right\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Multiplier1}}}{{\leq}}2M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s}}\|\Delta u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s}(B)}+M\left\lvert{\Delta_{S^{d}}\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s}}\|r^{-1}\partial_{r}u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}M\left\lvert{\eta_{i}^{-1}\partial_{i}\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s}}\left\|(h_{i})^{-1}\partial_{i}\partial_{r}u_{N-1}\right\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\leq 2M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s}}\|u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}+M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}\|u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+1}}\|u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}

\displaystyle\leq(3+2d)M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}\|u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:RHSAssumption}}}{{\leq}}(3+2d)M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}K_{1}A^{N-1}.

The estimate for
L_{2}u_{N-2}
is similar. Estimating the second term
\rho L_{1}u_{N-2}
in
L_{2}u_{N-2}
using the inequality (
19a
) once and adapting the estimate for
L_{1}u_{N-1}
from above, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\rho L_{1}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\leq M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s}}\|L_{1}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s}(B)}\leq(3+2d)M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}K_{1}A^{N-2}.

Estimating the remaining terms in
L_{2}u_{N-2}
using the inequality (
19a
) twice and noticing that all differential operators acting on
u_{N-2}
are second order, we obtain

\displaystyle\|L_{2}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho L_{1}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s}(B)}+\|\rho^{2}\Delta u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\|\left(\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}}\right)^{2}\left[\partial_{r}^{2}u_{N-2}+2r^{-1}\partial_{r}u_{N-2}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Multiplier1}}}{{\leq}}(3+2d)M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}K_{1}A^{N-2}+M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s}}^{2}\|\Delta u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s}(B)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{d}M^{2}\left\lvert{\eta_{i}^{-1}\partial_{i}\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s}}^{2}\left(\|\partial_{r}^{2}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s}(B)}+2\|r^{-1}\partial_{r}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s}(B)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(3+2d)M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}K_{1}A^{N-2}+M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s}}^{2}\|u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{d}M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+1}}^{2}\left(3\|u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(3+2d)M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}K_{1}A^{N-2}+(1+3d)M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}\|u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:RHSAssumption}}}{{\leq}}(3+2d)M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}K_{1}A^{N-2}+(1+3d)M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}K_{1}A^{N-2}

\displaystyle\leq(4+5d)M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}K_{1}A^{N-1},

assuming
A>M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}
. Finally, the desired result follows by choosing
C_{0}=(4+5d)M
.
∎

Finally, we justify the convergence of (
10
) for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Given
d\geq 3
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\rho\in C^{s+2}(S^{d})
and
\xi\in H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})
, there exists constants
C_{0}
and
K_{0}
and a unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of (
9
) satisfying (
10
) such that

(21)

\|u_{n}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}\leq K_{0}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{n}

for any
A>\max\{2K_{0}C_{0}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}},M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}\}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
VO20
, Theorem 3.3]
. We proceed by induction. The case
n=0
follows from applying Lemma
4.1
to
u_{0}
satisfying (
11
), with
F\equiv 0
:

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}\leq K_{0}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}=K_{0}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{0}.

Suppose inequality (
21
) holds for

. Applying Lemma
4.1
to

satisfying (

), with

\displaystyle\|u_{N}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}\leq K_{0}\left(\|L_{1}u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s}(B)}+\|L_{2}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s}(B)}\right).

with

so that

\displaystyle\|u_{N}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}\leq K_{0}\left(2C_{0}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}K_{1}A^{N-1}\right)

∎

5.
Analyticity of DNO and Steklov Eigenvalues

This last section is devoted to proving analyticity of DNO and subsequently the analyticity of Steklov eigenvalues, with respect to the perturbation parameter
\varepsilon
. Recall that the DNO
G_{\rho,\varepsilon}\colon H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon})\to H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon})
is given by

G_{\rho,\varepsilon}\xi=\mathbf{n}_{\rho,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v\rvert_{r=1+\varepsilon\rho},

where
\mathbf{n}_{\rho,\varepsilon}
is the unit normal vector of
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and
v
is the harmonic extension of
\xi
from
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
to
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, satisfying (
7
). We may represent the hypersurface
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
as the zero level set of the implicit function
J
,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\left\{(r,\hat{\theta})\colon J(r,\hat{\theta})=\frac{r}{1+\varepsilon\rho(\hat{\theta})}-1=0\right\}.

A normal vector to
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is given by the gradient
\nabla J
. Using (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla J

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\rho}\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\cdot\frac{-\varepsilon r\partial_{i}\rho}{(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}=\frac{1}{(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}\left[(1+\varepsilon\rho)\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}-\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}\right].

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{\rho,\varepsilon}=\frac{\nabla J}{\|\nabla J\|_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left((1+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(\partial_{i}\rho)^{2}}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}\left[(1+\varepsilon\rho)\,\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}-\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=M_{\rho,\varepsilon}\left[(1+\varepsilon\rho)\,\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}-\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}\right]

and the DNO is given by

\displaystyle G_{\rho,\varepsilon}\xi

\displaystyle=M_{\rho,\varepsilon}\left[(1+\varepsilon\rho)\partial_{r}v-\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\partial_{i}v\right]\bigg\rvert_{r=1+\varepsilon\rho}

Next we transform the DNO from
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
to
S^{d}
using the change of variables (
8
). Setting

u_{\varepsilon}(r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})=v\left((1+\varepsilon\rho(\theta^{\prime}))r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\right)

and dropping the primes on the transformed variables, we obtain

\displaystyle G_{\rho,\varepsilon}\xi

\displaystyle=M_{\rho,\varepsilon}\left[\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\varepsilon}{r(1+\varepsilon\rho)}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\left(\partial_{i}u_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\varepsilon r\partial_{i}\rho}{(1+\varepsilon\rho)}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\bigg\rvert_{r=1}

\displaystyle=M_{\rho,\varepsilon}\widehat{G}_{\rho,\varepsilon}\xi,

where

(22)

\displaystyle\widehat{G}_{\rho,\varepsilon}\xi

\displaystyle=\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}(1,\theta)-\frac{\varepsilon}{(1+\varepsilon\rho)}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\partial_{i}u_{\varepsilon}(1,\theta)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(\partial_{i}\rho)^{2}}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}(1,\theta).

Since
M_{\rho,\varepsilon}
is clearly analytic near
\varepsilon=0
, we need only show the analyticity of
\widehat{G}_{\rho,\varepsilon}
near
\varepsilon=0
. Multiplying (
22
) by
(1+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}
and collecting terms of order
\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon^{2}
, we see that
\widehat{G}_{\rho,\varepsilon}\xi
satisfies

(23)

\widehat{G}_{\rho,\varepsilon}\xi=\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}(1,\theta)+\varepsilon\Big[(T_{1}u_{\varepsilon})(1,\theta)-2\rho\widehat{G}_{\rho,\varepsilon}\xi\Big]+\varepsilon^{2}\Big[(T_{2}u_{\varepsilon})(1,\theta)-\rho^{2}\widehat{G}_{\rho,\varepsilon}\xi\Big],

where

\displaystyle T_{1}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=2\rho\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\partial_{i}u_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle T_{2}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\rho\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\partial_{i}u_{\varepsilon}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(\partial_{i}\rho)^{2}}{\eta_{i}^{2}}\partial_{r}u_{\varepsilon}.

We now formally expand
\widehat{G}_{\rho,\varepsilon}
as a power series in
\varepsilon
:

(24)

\widehat{G}_{\rho,\varepsilon}\xi=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{n}\widehat{G}_{\rho,n}\xi.

Substituting (
24
) and (
10
) into (
23
) and collecting terms in powers of
\varepsilon
, we see that
\widehat{G}_{\rho,0}
satisfies

(25)

\widehat{G}_{\rho,0}\xi=\partial_{r}u_{0}(1,\theta)

and
\widehat{G}_{\rho,n}
for
n=1,2,\dots
satisfies the following recursive formula:

(26)

\widehat{G}_{\rho,n}\xi=\partial_{r}u_{n}(1,\theta)+(T_{1}u_{n-1})(1,\theta)-2\rho\widehat{G}_{\rho,n-1}\xi+(T_{2}u_{n-2})(1,\theta)-\rho^{2}\widehat{G}_{\rho,n-2}\xi.

The following theorem justifies the convergence of (
24
) for suitably small
\varepsilon>0
and thereby proves Theorem
1.1
for
d\geq 3
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Given
d\geq 3
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\rho\in C^{s+2}(S^{d})
and
\xi\in H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})
, then there exists constants
K_{1}=K_{1}(B,d,s)>0
and
\alpha=\alpha(d)>1
such that

(27)

\|\widehat{G}_{\rho,n}\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}\leq K_{1}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{n}

for
A>\alpha\max\{2K_{0}C_{0}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}},M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}\}
.

Proof.

Throughout this proof we will use two facts: (1) Both
\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s}}
and
\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+1}}
are bounded above by
\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}
, and (2) if
P
be any first order differential operator on
S^{d}
, then the continuity of the trace operator on
H^{s+1}(B)
implies the existence of a constant
C_{B}>0
such that

(28)

\displaystyle\|Pu_{n}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}\leq C_{B}\|Pu_{n}\|_{H^{s+1}(B)}\leq C_{B}\|u_{n}\|_{H^{s+2}(B)}\leq C_{B}K_{0}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{n},

where the last inequality follows from Theorem
4.3
.

We proceed by induction. Set
K_{1}=\beta C_{B}K_{0}
for some constant
\beta>1
. The case
n=0
follows directly from (
25
) and (
28
):

\displaystyle\|\widehat{G}_{\rho,0}\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}K_{0}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+{\frac{3}{2}}}(S^{d})}A^{0}<K_{1}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+{\frac{3}{2}}}(S^{d})}A^{0}.

Now suppose that (
27
) holds for
n<N
. From the recursive formula (
26
), we estimate each term in
\widehat{G}_{\rho,N}\xi
individually. The first term can be estimated using (
28
) with
n=N
:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{r}u_{N}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}K_{0}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N}.

To estimate the terms
2\rho\widehat{G}_{\rho,N-1}\xi
and
\rho^{2}\widehat{G}_{\rho,N-2}\xi
, we use the inequality (
19b
) with
\delta=1/2
together with the induction hypothesis:

\displaystyle 2\|\rho\widehat{G}_{\rho,N-1}\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Multiplier2}}}{{\leq}}2M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+1}}\|\widehat{G}_{\rho,N-1}\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}\leq 2M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}K_{1}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N-1}

\displaystyle\|\rho^{2}\widehat{G}_{\rho,N-2}\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Multiplier2}}}{{\leq}}M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+1}}^{2}\|\widehat{G}_{\rho,N-2}\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}\leq M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}K_{1}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N-2}.

To estimate the terms
T_{1}u_{N-1}(1,\theta)
and
T_{2}u_{N-1}(1,\theta)
, we observe that the differential operators acting on
u_{N-1}
and
u_{N-2}
are first order on
S^{d}
. Using the inequality (
19b
) with
\delta=1/2
and (
28
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{1}u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|2\rho\partial_{r}u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\|\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}}\cdot\frac{\partial_{i}u_{N-1}}{\eta_{i}}\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Multiplier2}}}{{\leq}}2M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+1}}\|\partial_{r}u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}\|\eta_{i}^{-1}\partial_{i}u_{N-1}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Trace}}}{{\leq}}(2+d)M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}C_{B}K_{0}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N-1}

\displaystyle\|T_{2}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho^{2}\partial_{r}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\|\rho\cdot\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}}\cdot\frac{\partial_{i}u_{N-2}}{\eta_{i}}\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\|\left(\frac{\partial_{i}\rho}{\eta_{i}}\right)^{2}\partial_{r}u_{N-2}\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Multiplier2}}}{{\leq}}M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+1}}^{2}\|\partial_{r}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+1}}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}\|\eta_{i}^{-1}\partial_{i}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{d}M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}\|\partial_{r}u_{N-2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Trace}}}{{\leq}}(1+2d)M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}C_{B}K_{0}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N-2}.

Adding all the estimates for the five terms in
\widehat{G}_{\rho,N}\xi
and using the assumption
A>\alpha M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}
for some constant
\alpha>1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widehat{G}_{\rho,N}\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}K_{0}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N}

\displaystyle\qquad+(2+d)M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}C_{B}K_{0}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+2M\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}K_{1}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+2d)M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}C_{B}K_{0}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N-2}

\displaystyle\qquad+M^{2}\left\lvert{\rho}\right\rvert_{C^{s+2}}^{2}K_{1}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N-2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}K_{0}\left[1+\frac{2+d}{\alpha}+\frac{1+2d}{\alpha^{2}}\right]\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N}+K_{1}\left[\frac{2}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\right]\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N}

\displaystyle=C(\alpha,\beta)K_{1}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N},

where we use
K_{1}=\beta C_{B}K_{0}
for
\beta>1
for the equality and

\displaystyle C(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\left[1+\frac{2+d}{\alpha}+\frac{1+2d}{\alpha^{2}}\right]+\frac{2}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}.

Choosing
\alpha>1+\sqrt{2}>1
and
\beta>\frac{\alpha^{2}+\alpha(2+d)+2d+1}{\alpha^{2}-2\alpha-1}>1
, we obtain
C(\alpha,\beta)\leq 1
and so

\displaystyle\|\widehat{G}_{\rho,N}\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(S^{d})}\leq K_{1}\|\xi\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(S^{d})}A^{N},

thereby completing the inductive argument for
n=N
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

and Corollary

.

Homogenization of 2D materials in the Thomas–Fermi–von Weizsäcker theory
Saad Benjelloun, Salma Lahbabi, Abdelqoddous Moussa

Abstract
We study the homogenization of the Thomas–Fermi–von Weizsäcker (TFW) model for 2D materials introduced in Ref. BB . It consists in considering 2D-periodic nuclear densities with periods going to zero. We study the behavior of the corresponding ground state electronic densities and ground state energies. The main result is that these three-dimensional problems converge to a limit model that is one dimensional, similar to the one proposed in Ref. hb . We also illustrate this convergence with numerical simulations and estimate the converging rate for the ground state electronic densities and the ground state energies.

Introduction and main results

In recent years, two-dimensional (2D) materials have become an intensely active research field, marked by landmark discoveries and widespread investigation

Density Functional Theory (DFT) is one of the most widely used simulation tools in electronic structure calculations. It consists of describing the electrons by their density
\rho
and the energy of the system by a functional of
\rho
.
A famous model in this class is the (orbital free) Thomas–Fermi–von Weizsäcker (TFW) model

;

. From a mathematical point of view, an important result in the study of crystals is the thermodynamic limit problem. It consists of proving that when a finite cluster converges to some periodic perfect crystal, the corresponding ground state electronic density and ground state energy per unit volume converge to the periodic equivalent. This program has been carried out for the Thomas–Fermi–von Weizsäcker model for three-dimensional (3D) crystals in
Ref.
catto

and for one- and two-dimensional (1D and 2D) crystals in Ref.
BB
. Other properties of TFW-type models for crystals have been studied in the literature. The seminal work in Ref.

established the basic theory for atoms, molecules and crystals. Subsequent research addressed aspects such as the analysis of defects

;
Erlacher2011
, stochastic materials

, potential symmetry breaking

, rigorous derivation of DFT models for 2D materials
hb
, geometric optimisation
Blanc2001
, and the emergence of periodic phenomena in large atoms
BjergSolovej2024
.

In this paper, we investigate the homogenization of the TFW model for 2D crystals. Our goal is to find a homogeneous material equivalent to a 2D crystal in the limit when the lattice parameter goes to zero. This corresponds to putting more and more (normalized) nuclei in the unit cell, or equivalently looking at the crystal from further and further away. It turns out that the homogenized material can be described by a 1D model, in the same spirit as in Ref.
hb
, which allows reducing computational time and resources required to simulate a 2D material, at least in a zero-order approximation.
Our proof can be generalized if we substitute the
\int\rho^{5/3}
term in the kinetic energy by
\int\rho^{p}
for some
p>\frac{3}{2}
. Note that the strict convexity of the energy functional gives the uniqueness of the ground state, which plays an important role in the proof.

To our knowledge, the closest work to ours is Ref.
Mon
where the authors used the 2D TFW model
BB
to derive macroscopic features of a crystal from the microscopic structure in the presence of an external electric field. The crystal is modeled in the band
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times[-1,1]
and micro–macro limit is taken when the ratio between the atomic spacing and the size of the crystal goes to zero.

I.1
Thomas–Fermi–von Weizsäcker model for finite systems

We present in this section the TFW model for finite systems. Let

m\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},{\mathbb{R}}^{+})

be a finite nuclear charge density. The state of the electrons is described by a non-negative electronic density
\rho\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. The energy functional is given by

{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\right|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{5/3}+\frac{1}{2}D(\rho-m,\rho-m),

(1)

where the first two terms represent the kinetic energy of the electrons and
D(f,g)
is the Coulomb interaction between charge densities
f
and
g
in the Coulomb space
{\mathcal{C}}=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},{\mathbb{R}}),\;\frac{\widehat{f}}{\left|\cdot\right|}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\right\}
. It is defined by

D(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\dfrac{f(x)g(y)}{\left|x-y\right|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=4\pi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{\overline{\widehat{f}(k)}\widehat{g}(k)}{\left|k\right|^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k,

where
\widehat{f}(k)
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
evaluated at
k
. The ground state is given by the following minimization
problem

I^{m}=\inf\left\{{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(\rho),\;\rho\geqslant 0,\;\sqrt{\rho}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m\right\}.

(2)

It is well known that problem (
2
) has a unique minimizer
\rho
(see for instance Ref.
Ben
).
u=\sqrt{\rho}
is the unique solution of the corresponding Euler–Lagrange equation

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\frac{5}{3}u^{7/3}+u\Phi=\lambda u,\\
-\Delta\Phi=4\pi(u^{2}-m),\end{cases}

(3)

where
\displaystyle\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}},\mbox{ and }\Phi=(u^{2}-m)*\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|}
is the mean–field potential.

I.2
Thomas–Fermi–von Weizsäcker model for 2D crystals

We present in this section the TFW model for 2D crystals introduced in Ref.
BB
. 2D crystals are characterized by a nuclear density
m
that has the periodicity of a 2D lattice

{\mathcal{R}}=a_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}+a_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
(a_{1},a_{2})
are two linearly independent vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
(see Figure
1
); namely

m(x_{1}+k_{1},x_{2}+k_{2},x_{3})=m(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\;\forall(k_{1},k_{2})\in{\mathcal{R}}.

Figure 1:
Example of a 2D lattice and its unit cell
\Gamma
.

From now on, we denote by
Q
the unit cell of
{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and by
\Gamma=Q\times{\mathbb{R}}
the unit cell of
{\mathcal{R}}
seen as a lattice in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, we denote by
\underline{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
so that
x=(\underline{x},x_{3})
.

By means of a thermodynamic limit procedure, it has been shown in Ref.
BB
that 2D crystals can be described by a model similar to (
1
)–(
2
) posed in the unit cell
\Gamma
. The main difference is that the 3D Green function
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|x\right|}
is replaced by the 2D periodic Green function
G
solution of

-\Delta G=4\pi\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{R}}\times\left\{0\right\}}\delta_{k}.

An explicit formula of
G
is given by Ref.
BB
, equation 11

G(x)=-\dfrac{2\pi}{\left|Q\right|}\left|x_{3}\right|+\sum_{\underline{k}\in{\mathcal{R}}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\left|{x}-(\underline{k},0)\right|}-\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}}{\left|{x}-\left(\underline{y}+\underline{k},0\right)\right|}\right).

(4)

It can be seen as the sum over the lattice of the Coulomb potential created by a point charge placed at the lattice sites, screened by a uniform background of negative unit charge. A Fourier decomposition of
G
can be found in Ref.
hb
. The 2D crystals energy functional then reads

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}^{m}_{{\rm per}}(\rho)=\int_{\Gamma}\left|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\right|^{2}+\int_{\Gamma}\rho^{5/3}+\frac{1}{2}D_{G}(m-\rho,m-\rho),

(5)

where the Hartree interaction
D_{G}
is given by

\displaystyle D_{G}(f,g):=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\Gamma}{f(x)g(y)}G(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Remark I.1
.

In Ref.
BB
, there is a Coulomb correction term in the energy which comes from the Dirichlet boundary condition at infinity considered in the thermodynamic limit procedure, so that the energy is

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}^{m}_{{\rm per}}}(\rho)={\mathcal{E}}^{m}_{{\rm per}}(\rho)+\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\Gamma}\dfrac{\text{$m(x)m(y)$}}{\left|x-y\right|}.

In our study, we omit this correction term as it does not affect the problem from a mathematical point of view.

The ground state is given by the minimization problem

I^{m}_{{\rm per}}=\inf\left\{{\mathcal{E}}^{m}_{{\rm per}}(\rho),\quad\rho\geqslant 0,\quad\sqrt{\rho}\in X_{{\rm per}},\quad\int_{\Gamma}\rho=\int_{\Gamma}m\right\}

(6)

where

X_{{\rm per}}=\left\{v\in H^{1}_{{\rm per}}(\Gamma),\;\left(1+\left|x_{3}\right|\right)^{1/2}v\in L^{2}_{{\rm per}}(\Gamma)\right\}.

The weight
(1+|x_{3}|)^{1/2}
in the definition of the function space
X_{per}
is there to insure that
\rho:=v^{2}
, with
v\in X_{\rm per}
, satisfies
(1+\left|x_{3}\right|)\rho\in L^{1}(\Gamma)
and thus has a finite interaction energy, as the periodic potential
G
contains a term proportional to
|x_{3}|
.
This problem has been studied in Ref.
BB
, section 3.2.3
, along with some basic properties of the solution, that we summarize in the following Theorem.

Theorem I.2

(Ref.
BB
, Theorem 3.2
)
.

Let
m\neq 0
be a smooth non-negative,
{\mathcal{R}}
-periodic function with compact support w.r.t
x_{3}
.
Then, the minimization problem (
6
) has a unique minimizer
\rho
. Moreover,
u=\sqrt{\rho}
is the unique solution of the corresponding Euler–Lagrange system

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\frac{5}{3}u^{7/3}+u\Phi=\lambda u,\\
-\Delta\Phi=4\pi(u^{2}-m),\\
\end{cases}

(7)

where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In addition,
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\left|u(x)\right|\leqslant\dfrac{C}{1+\left|x_{3}\right|^{3/2}}
, for
\left|x_{3}\right|>1
,
C>0
being a constant independent of the density
m
.

Remark I.3
.

The fact that, in inequality
\textstyle\left|u(x)\right|\leqslant\dfrac{C}{1+\left|x_{3}\right|^{3/2}}
, for
\left|x_{3}\right|>1
, the constant is independent of the density
m
, comes from a supersolution method applied to equation (
7
) (see Ref.
BB
, Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 2.3
).

I.3
Homogenization of 2D materials

In the framework of the Thomas–Fermi (TF) model and the reduced Hartree–Fock model (rHF), the recent work Ref.
hb
studies reduced models for 2D homogeneous materials. The idea is that if the material is homogeneous, the 3D model is equivalent to a 1D model; simpler and less costly to simulate. In the present work, we are interested in the homogenization procedure, which models looking at a crystal macroscopically, from further and further away. Namely, we put more and more nuclei in the unit cell, with the right charge normalization, and we ask the following questions:

•

What is the nuclear density at the limit?

•

Does the ground state electronic density, potential and energy converge?

•

What is the model describing the limit electronic structure?

Let
m
be a nuclear density satisfying the conditions of Theorem
I.2
and consider the following sequence of nuclear densities which consists in putting
N^{2}
small nuclei in the unit cell

m_{N}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):=m(Nx_{1},Nx_{2},x_{3}),\quad\forall x\in\Gamma.

(8)

We note that
m_{N}
is
\frac{1}{N}{\mathcal{R}}
-periodic, which describes a more homogeneous material than the initial one. The limit when
N\rightarrow+\infty
describes a homogeneous material (see Figure
2
).

Figure 2:
The homogenization process for the nuclear density
m_{N}
, illustrated at
N=1,4
and
16
from left to right.

We will show that this model
“converges”
, in some sense that we will precise later, to the following limit model. For a 1D nuclear density
\mu
, we introduce the energy functional

{\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{\mu}(\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\right|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{5/3}+\frac{1}{2}D_{1}(\rho-\mu,\rho-\mu),

(9)

where the 1D Hartree interaction
D_{1}
is defined for functions decaying fast enough by

D_{1}(f,g)=-{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|s-t\right|f(s)g(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Here, the 1D Green kernel
-2\pi\left|t\right|
is used. The ground state of this model is given by the minimization problem

I^{\mu}_{1}=\min\{E^{\mu}(\rho),\;\rho\geqslant 0,\;\sqrt{\rho}\in X_{1},\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu\},

(10)

where

X_{1}=\left\{v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\;(1+\left|\cdot\right|)^{1/2}v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right\}.

The following Theorem, which is a direct consequence of Theorem
I.2
and the definitions of Hartree interaction terms
D_{G}
and
D_{1}
, is similar to Ref.
hb
, Theorems 2.2 and 2.8
, which treat Thomas–Fermi and reduced Hartree–Fock models.

Theorem I.4
.

Let
\mu\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{R}})
. The problem (
10
) has a unique minimizer. Moreover,
u:=\sqrt{\rho}
is the unique solution of the corresponding Euler–Lagrange equations

\begin{cases}-u^{\prime\prime}+\frac{5}{3}u^{7/3}+u\Phi=\lambda u,\\
-\Phi^{\prime\prime}=4\pi(u^{2}-\mu),\end{cases}

where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
and there exists
C>0
, independent of
\mu
, such that for any
\left|t\right|\geqslant 1
,
\left|u\right|\leqslant\frac{C}{1+\left|t\right|^{3/2}}
.

Our main contribution is the following Theorem.

Theorem I.5
.

Let
m
be a nuclear density satisfying the hypothesis of Theorem
I.2
and for
N\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, let
m_{N}
be defined as in (
8
). We denote by
I_{N}=I^{m_{N}}_{{\rm per}}
and by
\rho_{N}
the corresponding ground state given by Theorem
I.2
.
Let
m_{0}(t)=\frac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}m(\underline{x},t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}
and denote by
I_{0}=I^{m_{0}}_{1}
and by
\rho_{0}
the corresponding ground state given by Theorem
I.4
.
The following holds

i.

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}I_{N}=I_{0}
,

ii.

\rho_{N}
converges to
\rho_{0}
in
L^{1}(\Gamma)
, in
L^{p}_{loc}(\Gamma)
for all
1\leqslant p\leqslant 3
, and almost everywhere,

iii.

\sqrt{\rho_{N}}
converges to
\sqrt{\rho_{0}}
weakly in
H^{1}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
.

Our result is a zero order approximation of a finite
N
situation. In homogenization theory of partial differential equations with periodically oscillating coefficients, the two scale convergence technique is usually used to find higher order terms in the approximation
allaire
. Applying a similar approach to our problem is a perspective of this work.

II
Hartree interaction

We recall in this section some properties of the Green function
G
, the Hartree interaction
D_{G}
and prove a uniqueness result of the mean field potential
\Phi
.
In the subsequent proposition, we introduce a convenient decomposition of the kernel
G
, which proves to be useful throughout the paper.

Proposition II.1
.

We have

G(x)=-\dfrac{2\pi}{\left|Q\right|}\left|x_{3}\right|+\dfrac{1}{\left|x\right|}+\psi(x),

where
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
.

Proposition
II.1
is partially proved in Ref.
BB
. We detail the proof here for consistency.

Proof.

We start from the formula (
4
) of the explicit expression of the periodic Green function
G

G(x)=-\dfrac{2\pi}{\left|Q\right|}\left|x_{3}\right|+\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{R}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\left|{x}-(\underline{k},0)\right|}-\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}}{\left|{x}-\left(\underline{y}+\underline{k},0\right)\right|}\right).

(11)

We then isolate the term corresponding to
\underline{k}=0
from the sum

\displaystyle G(x)=-\dfrac{2\pi}{\left|Q\right|}\left|x_{3}\right|

\displaystyle+\left(\dfrac{1}{\left|x-(\underline{0},0)\right|}-\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}}{\left|{x}-\left(\underline{y}+\underline{0},0\right)\right|}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{R}^{*}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\left|{x}-(\underline{k},0)\right|}-\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}}{\left|{x}-\left(\underline{y}+\underline{k},0\right)\right|}\right).

We can thus rewrite
G
as

\displaystyle G(x)=-\dfrac{2\pi}{\left|Q\right|}\left|x_{3}\right|+\dfrac{1}{\left|x\right|}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}}{\left|{x}-(\underline{y},0)\right|}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{R}^{*}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\left|{x}-(\underline{k},0)\right|}-\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}}{\left|{x}-(\underline{y}+\underline{k},0)\right|}\right).

Therefore we can write
G(x)=-\frac{2\pi}{|Q|}|x_{3}|+\frac{1}{|x|}+\psi(x)
, where
\psi(x)
is the sum of the remaining terms

\psi(x)=-\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}}{\left|{x}-(\underline{y},0)\right|}+\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{R}^{*}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\left|{x}-(\underline{k},0)\right|}-\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}}{\left|{x}-(\underline{y}+\underline{k},0)\right|}\right).

Let us show that
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
. The function
\displaystyle x\mapsto\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}}{\left|{x}-(\underline{y},0)\right|}
is continuous on
\Gamma
and goes to zero as
\left|x_{3}\right|\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover, the sum defining
\psi
converges normally on a neighborhood of
0_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}
(see Ref.
BB
, Proposition 3.2
). Therefore it is continuous on that neighborhood, which implies that
\psi
is bounded on
Q\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
, for some
\varepsilon>0
. From Ref.
BB
, Equation 3.12
, there exists
C>0
such that

\sum_{\underline{k}\in({\mathcal{R}})^{*}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\left|{x}-(\underline{k},0)\right|}-\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}}{\left|{x}-(\underline{y}+\underline{k},0)\right|}\right)\leqslant\dfrac{C}{\left|x_{3}\right|^{\alpha}},\quad\forall\alpha<1.

It follows that
x\mapsto\sum_{\underline{k}\in({\mathcal{R}})^{*}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\left|{x}-(\underline{k},0)\right|}-\dfrac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}}{\left|{x}-(\underline{y}+\underline{k},0)\right|}\right)
is bounded on
x\in Q\times({\mathbb{R}}\setminus[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon])
, thus
\psi
is also bounded on the same interval.
∎

As a consequence of the above proposition, we prove useful properties of the potential
\Phi
. Let us introduce some notations. For some domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
1\leqslant p\leqslant{\infty}
, we denote by

L^{p}_{\rm unif}(\Omega)=\left\{f\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),\;\forall r>0,\;\sup_{\text{$B(x,r)$}\subset\Omega}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}<\infty\right\},

where
B(x,r)
is the ball of radius
r
centered at
x
. For
f\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
and
g\in L^{q}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
, the convolution

(g*_{\Gamma}f)(x)=\int_{\Gamma}f(y)g(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

is well-defined in
L^{r}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1+\frac{1}{r}
.

Proposition II.2
.

Let

Y_{{\rm per}}=\left\{f\in L^{1}(\Gamma)\cap L^{5/3}(\Gamma),\;\int_{\Gamma}f=0,\;\left|\cdot\right|f\in L^{1}(\Gamma)\right\}.

The map

\begin{array}[]{rll}Y_{\rm per}&\to&L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\\
f&\mapsto&G*_{\Gamma}f\end{array}

is well-defined and continuous. Moreover, for
f\in Y_{\rm per}
,
G*_{\Gamma}f
is the unique solution, up to an additive constant, of the Poisson equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Phi&\in&L^{1}_{\text{\rm unif }}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\\
-\Delta\Phi&=&4\pi f.\end{array}\right.

(12)

Proof.

From Proposition
II.1
, we have
G(x)=\dfrac{1}{\left|x\right|}-\dfrac{2\pi}{\left|Q\right|}\left|x_{3}\right|+\psi(x)
, with
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
.
We are thus going to bound the three functions

f*_{\Gamma}\left|x_{3}\right|,\;f*_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{\left|x\right|}\;\text{and}\;f*_{\Gamma}\psi

in
L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
. First, since
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
, we have for any
x\in\Gamma

\left|\int_{\Gamma}f(x-y)\psi(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right|\leqslant\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(x-\Gamma)}.

Thus

\left\|f*_{\Gamma}\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leqslant\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}.

We move to
{f}*_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{\left|x\right|}
. We have

\displaystyle\left|f*_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{\left|x\right|}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\left|f(x-y)\right|}{\left|y\right|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Gamma}\left|f(x-y)\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\left|f(x-y)\right|}{\left|y\right|}\mathbbm{1}_{\left|y\right|<1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

For the second term, we use Hölder inequality with
f\in L^{5/3}(\Gamma)
and
\frac{1}{\left|y\right|}\mathbbm{1}_{\left|y\right|<1}\in L^{5/2}(\Gamma)
. We conclude that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\|\frac{1}{\left|x\right|}*_{\Gamma}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}}&\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{5/3}(\Gamma)}\left\|\frac{1}{\left|y\right|}\mathbbm{1}_{\left|y\right|<1}\right\|_{L^{5/2}(\Gamma)}.\end{array}

Regarding the last term, we use the neutrality assumption to write

\int_{\Gamma}f(x-y)\left|y_{3}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\Gamma}f(x-y)(\left|y_{3}\right|-\left|x_{3}\right|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Thus, using the triangle inequality and the
\mathcal{R}
-periodicity of
x\mapsto x_{3}f(x)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma}f(x-y)\left|y_{3}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right|\leqslant\int_{\Gamma}\left|f(x-y)\right|\left|x_{3}-y_{3}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\Gamma}\left|f(y)\right|\left|y_{3}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

We now prove that
G*_{\Gamma}f
is the unique solution of (
12
). The function
G*_{\Gamma}f
is
\mathcal{R}
-periodic, therefore it is bounded over
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and also in
L^{1}_{\text{\rm unif }}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. It satisfies equation (
12
) by the definition of
G
. Let
\Phi
be another solution. Then
h=\Phi-G*_{\Gamma}f\in L^{1}_{\text{\rm unif }}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is a harmonic function over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. By the mean value Theorem for harmonic functions, we have that for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,

h(x)=\frac{3}{4\pi}\int_{B(x,1)}h(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Therefore,

|h(x)|\leqslant\frac{3}{4\pi}\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{B(z,1)}|h(y)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Since
h\in L^{1}_{\text{\rm unif }}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, the supremum is finite, which implies
h
is bounded over
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. By Liouville’s Theorem, we conclude that
h
is constant.
∎

Corollary II.3
.

Let
f\in Y_{per}
, then
\nabla(G*_{\Gamma}f)\in\left(L^{2}(\Gamma)\right)^{3}
and there exists
C>0
, such that

D_{G}(f,f)=\text{$(4\pi)^{-1}$}\left\|\nabla(G*_{\Gamma}f)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leqslant C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\left(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{5/3}(\Gamma)}+\left\||x|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\right).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle D_{G}(f,f)=\int_{\Gamma}(G*_{\Gamma}f)\times f=\text{$-(4\pi)^{-1}$}\int_{\Gamma}(G*_{\Gamma}f)\times\Delta(G*_{\Gamma}f)

In Ref.
BB
, Section 3.2.1
, it is shown using a hybrid Fourier transform, that is discrete in
\underline{x}
and continuous in
x_{3}
, that

D_{G}(f,f)=\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{R}}}\int_{R}\frac{\left|\widehat{f}(k,\xi)\right|^{2}}{\pi(|k|^{2}+\xi^{2})}d\xi.

Hence,

D_{G}(f,f)=(4\pi)^{-1}\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{R}}}\int_{R}\left|\frac{(i2\pi(k,\xi))\widehat{f}(k,\xi)}{\pi(|k|^{2}+\xi^{2})}\right|^{2}d\xi=(4\pi)^{-1}\int_{\Gamma}|\widehat{\nabla(G*_{\Gamma}f)}|^{2}dx.

By the Planchrel-Parseval property
BB
of the hybrid Fourier transform, we conclude that

\displaystyle D_{G}(f,f)=\text{$(4\pi)^{-1}$}\int_{\Gamma}\left|\nabla(G*_{\Gamma}f)\right|^{2}.

Besides, by the previous proposition
G*_{\Gamma}f\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
and

\left\|G*_{\Gamma}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leqslant(\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}+1)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{5/3}(\Gamma)}\left\|\frac{1}{\left|y\right|}\mathbbm{1}_{\left|y\right|<1}\right\|_{L^{5/2}(\Gamma)}+\left\|\left|x\right|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\leqslant C\left\|f\right\|_{Y_{{\rm per}}},

which proves the inequality stated in the corollary.

∎

III
Homogenization procedure: proof of Theorem
I.5

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
I.5
. The strategy of the proof is as follows. We start by proving the convergence of the nuclear densities
(m_{N})_{N}
. Indeed, when
N
increases, the nuclear density
m_{N}
becomes more homogeneous and the sequence
(m_{N})_{N}
converges to the 2D homogeneous density
m_{0}(x_{3})=\frac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}m(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}
(see Lemma
III.3
). Electronic densities
\rho_{N}
are uniformly bounded w.r.t.
N
. We can thus extract convergent subsequences (see Proposition
III.5
). Both convergences give the upper bound
I_{0}\leqslant\liminf I_{N}
.
To prove the lower bound
I_{0}\geqslant\limsup I_{N}
, we use
\rho_{0}
as a test function. The proof
is divided into four steps.

Step 1: Properties of the sequence
(m_{N})_{N}
.

We state below two properties of the sequence
(m_{N})_{N}
.

Lemma III.1
.

For
p\geqslant 1
and
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
x\mapsto f(x_{3})m^{p}(x)\in L^{1}(\Gamma)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q}m_{N}^{p}(\underline{x},x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}=\int_{Q}m^{p}(\underline{x},x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}f(x_{3})m_{N}^{p}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Gamma}f(x_{3})m^{p}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

We have

\int_{Q}m_{N}^{p}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2}=\int_{Q}m^{p}(Nx_{1},Nx_{2},x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\int_{NQ}m^{p}(y_{1},y_{2},x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{2}.

As
m
is
Q
-periodic, then
\displaystyle\int_{NQ}m^{p}(y_{1},y_{2},x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{2}=N^{2}\int_{Q}m^{p}(y_{1},y_{2},x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{2}
. Hence, the first claim is proved. The second claim easily follows.
∎

Remark III.2
.

By the second point of Lemma
III.1
, with
f=1
and
p=1
, we have that
\int_{\Gamma}m_{N}=\int_{\Gamma}m=\int_{\Gamma}m_{0}
, so that any admissible state for
I_{N}
is also an admissible state for
I_{0}
, and vice versa.

Lemma III.3
.

The sequence
(m_{N})_{N}
converges to
m_{0}
weakly in
L^{p}(\Gamma)
for all
1\leqslant p<+{\infty}
.

Proof.

From the
Q
-periodicity of
m
, we have for any
x_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}
(see for instance Ref.
Luk
)

m_{N}(\cdot,\cdot,x_{3})\rightharpoonup m_{0}(x_{3})\text{}\quad\mbox{ in }L^{p}(Q),\quad\text{for all }1\leqslant p<{\infty}.

(13)

For
\varphi\in L^{q}(\Gamma)
, with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
, we have

f_{N}(x_{3}):=\displaystyle\int_{Q}m_{N}(x)\varphi(\underline{x},x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\to f(x_{3}):=m_{0}(x_{3})\int_{Q}\varphi(\underline{x},x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}
a.e. and, by Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|f_{N}(x_{3})\right|&\leqslant\left\|\varphi(\cdot,\cdot,x_{3})\right\|_{L^{q}(Q)}\left(\int_{Q}\left|m_{N}(\underline{x},x_{3})\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\right)^{1/p}\\
&=\left\|\varphi(\cdot,\cdot,x_{3})\right\|_{L^{q}(Q)}\left(\int_{Q}\left|m(\underline{x},x_{3})\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\right)^{1/p}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}).\end{split}

(14)

Thus, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{N}(x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{3}=\int_{\Gamma}m_{N}(x)\varphi(\underline{x},x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{3}=\int_{\Gamma}m_{0}(x_{3})\varphi(\underline{x},x_{3})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

∎

Step 2: Convergence of electronic densities.

The following proposition gives uniform bounds on quantities of interest w.r.t.
N
.

Proposition III.4
.

There exist various constants
C
independent of
N
such that the following bounds hold.

i.

\displaystyle I_{N}\leqslant C
,

ii.

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{N}}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\leqslant C
,

iii.

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}({\Gamma})}\leqslant C
for all
1\leqslant p\leqslant 3
,

iv.

\displaystyle D_{G}(\rho_{N}-m_{N},\rho_{N}-m_{N})\leqslant C
.

Proof.

As
\rho_{N}
is the minimizer of
I_{N}
and since
m
is an admissible test function for
{\mathcal{E}}^{m_{N}}_{\rm per}
, we have

I_{N}={\mathcal{E}}_{per}^{m_{N}}(\rho_{N})\leqslant{\mathcal{E}}_{per}^{m_{N}}(m)=\int_{\Gamma}\left|\nabla\sqrt{m}\right|^{2}+\int_{\Gamma}m^{5/3}+\frac{1}{2}D_{G}(m-m_{N},m-m_{N}).

By Corollary
II.3
applied to
f=m-m_{N}
and Lemma
III.1
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}D_{G}(m-m_{N},m-m_{N})&\leqslant&C\left\|m-m_{N}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\left(\left\|m-m_{N}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}+\left\|m-m_{N}\right\|_{L^{5/3}(\Gamma)}\right.\\
&&\left.+\left\||x|(m-m_{N})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\right)\\
&\leqslant&C\left\|m\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\left(\left\|m\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}+\left\|m\right\|_{L^{5/3}(\Gamma)}+\left\||x|m\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\right),\end{array}

where,
C
denotes a generic positive constant that may differ from one line to the other. The finiteness of
\left\|\nabla\sqrt{m}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}
,
\left\|m\right\|_{L^{5/3}(\Gamma)}
and
\left\||x|m\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}
follows directly from the assumed regularity and compact support of

in
x_{3}
. Points
ii
–

are easily deduced from point
i
.
∎

Proposition III.5
.

There exists a non-negative function
\rho_{0}\in L^{1}(\Gamma)\cap L^{5/3}(\Gamma)
such that, up to a subsequence,

•

(\rho_{N})_{N}
converges to
\rho_{0}
strongly in
L^{1}(\Gamma)
and
L^{p}_{loc}(\Gamma)
for all
1\leqslant p\leqslant 3
, weakly in
L^{p}(\Gamma)
, and almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

•

(\nabla\sqrt{\rho_{N}})
converges to
\nabla\sqrt{\rho_{0}}
weakly in
(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{3}

•

\rho_{0}-m_{0}\in Y_{\rm per}

•

The sequence
\nabla\Phi_{N}=\nabla\left(G*_{\Gamma}(\rho_{N}-m_{N})\right)
converges to
\nabla\Phi_{0}=\nabla\left(G*_{\Gamma}(\rho_{0}-m_{0})\right)
weakly in
(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{3}
.

As a consequence

{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm per}^{m_{0}}(\rho_{0})\leqslant\liminf I_{N}.

Proof.

The sequence
(\sqrt{\rho_{N}})_{N}
is uniformly bounded w.r.t.
N
in
H^{1}(\Gamma)
by Proposition
III.4
. Then, up to a subsequence, it converges to some non-negative function

weakly in
H^{1}(\Gamma)

for all

and almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Besides, by Theorem
I.2
, there exists

such that for any

and any

such that

\left|u_{N}(x)\right|\leqslant\frac{C}{1+\left|x_{3}\right|^{3/2}}.

By the almost everywhere convergence,

satisfies the same estimate, and we have for

the following estimate

\left|x_{3}\right|\rho_{0}(x)\leqslant\rho_{0}(x)1_{\left|x_{3}\right|\leqslant 1}+\frac{C}{1+\left|x_{3}\right|^{3}}\in L^{1}(\Gamma).

Thus
\rho_{0}\in L^{1}(\Gamma)\cap L^{5/3}(\Gamma)
and
\left|x_{3}\right|\rho_{0}\in L^{1}(\Gamma)
.

We show now that
\rho_{N}
converges to
\rho_{0}
strongly in

. Let
\varepsilon>0
and

large enough such that for any

\displaystyle\int_{\left|x_{3}\right|>R}\left|\rho_{N}-\rho_{M}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{R}\int_{\left|x_{3}\right|>R}\left|x_{3}\right|\left|\rho_{N}-\rho_{M}\right|\leqslant\frac{C}{R}\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

By the strong convergence of
\rho_{N}
in

\int_{\left|x_{3}\right|<R}\left|\rho_{N}-\rho_{M}\right|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

which proves that
(\rho_{N})_{N}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{1}(\Gamma)
. Besides,
(\nabla\Phi_{N})_{N}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
. Thus, it weakly converges, up to a subsequence, to

. Since
-\Delta\Phi_{N}=4\pi\left(\rho_{N}-m_{N}\right)
, then

\forall\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}(\Gamma),\quad\int_{\Gamma}\nabla\Phi_{N}\cdot\nabla\varphi=4\pi\int_{\Gamma}\left(\rho_{N}-m_{N}\right)\varphi.

(15)

By definition of the weak limit, the LHS of (

weakly converges to

in

\forall\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}(\Gamma),\quad\int_{\Gamma}W\cdot\nabla\varphi=4\pi\int_{\Gamma}\left(\rho_{0}-m_{0}\right)\varphi.

Therefore

in the sense of distributions. To conclude that

, we cannot directly apply the divergence theorem that needs the decay of

at

, which is, a priori, only

. Instead, we use the hybrid Fourier transform defined in Ref.

, Eq. 3.13
, and obtain, for
\underline{R}
in the dual lattice

and

2i\pi\left(\begin{array}[]{c}R_{1}\\
R_{2}\\

For

and

, we obtain
\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{0}-m_{0}=0
. It follows that
\rho_{0}-m_{0}\in Y_{\rm per}
.
Thus by Proposition
II.2
,

is well-defined. Finally, we show that
\nabla\Phi_{0}=W
. Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3})\in{\mathcal{D}}(\Gamma)^{3}
. We have

, thus

Therefore

\displaystyle\langle\nabla G*_{\Gamma}(\rho_{N}-m_{N}),\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbf{-}\langle\rho_{N}-m_{N},G*_{\Gamma}{\rm div\;}\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle\to\mathbf{-}\langle\rho_{0}-m_{0},G*_{\Gamma}{\rm div\;}\varphi\rangle=\langle\nabla G*_{\Gamma}(\rho_{0}-m_{0}),\varphi\rangle.

∎

Step 3: Identification of the limit.

We show next that
\rho_{0}
is invariant w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
and it is indeed the unique minimizer of
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{m_{0}}
. This will prove that the convergences in Proposition
III.5
hold for the whole sequence and prove points
ii
-
iii
of Theorem
I.5
.

We start by showing that
\rho_{0}
is the unique minimizer of
{\mathcal{E}}^{m_{0}}_{\rm per}
.

Proposition III.6
.

\rho_{0}
is the unique minimizer of
{\mathcal{E}}^{m_{0}}_{\rm per}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho\in\left\{\rho\geqslant 0,\;\sqrt{\rho}\in X_{\rm per},\;\int_{\Gamma}\rho=\int_{\Gamma}m_{0}\right\}
be an admissible test function for
{\mathcal{E}}^{m_{0}}_{\rm per}
. Let us show that

{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm per}^{m_{0}}(\rho_{0})\leqslant{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm per}^{m_{0}}(\rho).

As
\int_{\Gamma}m_{0}=\int_{\Gamma}m_{N}
by Lemma
III.1
,
\rho
is also an admissible test function for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm per}^{m_{N}}
. Thus

I_{N}={\mathcal{E}}_{\rm per}^{m_{N}}(\rho_{N})\leqslant{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm per}^{m_{N}}(\rho).

We know from Proposition
III.5
that

{\mathcal{E}}^{m_{0}}_{\rm per}(\rho_{0})\leqslant\liminf I_{N}\leqslant\liminf{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm per}^{m_{N}}(\rho).

It thus remains to show that
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm per}^{m_{N}}(\rho)\to{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm per}^{m_{0}}(\rho)
, which boils down to showing that

D_{G}(\rho-m_{N},\rho-m_{N})\to D_{G}(\rho-m_{0},\rho-m_{0}).

(16)

We recall that

D_{G}(f,g)=\int_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}f(x)f(y)G(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

with
G(x)=-2\pi\left|x_{3}\right|+\frac{1}{\left|x\right|}+\psi(x)
and
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
.
We denote by
h_{N}=\rho-m_{N}
and
h=\rho-m_{0}
.
We recall as well that for any
x_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
h_{N}(\cdot,x_{3})
converges to
h(\cdot,x_{3})
weakly in
L^{p}(Q)
, for all
1\leqslant p<{\infty}
. Therefore, for any
x_{3},y_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
h_{N}(\cdot,x_{3})h_{N}(\cdot,y_{3})
converges to
h(\cdot,x_{3})h(\cdot,y_{3})
weakly in
L^{p}(Q\times Q)
.
We have
-2\pi\left|x_{3}-y_{3}\right|+\psi(x-y)\in L^{\infty}(Q\times Q)
, and
\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\in L^{p}(Q\times Q)
for any
1\leqslant p<2
. Therefore, for any
x_{3},y_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}

\int_{Q\times Q}h_{N}(x)h_{N}(y)G(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}\to\int_{Q\times Q}h(x)h(y)G(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}.

(17)

To use a dominated convergence argument, we need to bound the LHS of (
17
) uniformly w.r.t.
N
by an
L^{1}
function w.r.t.
(x_{3},y_{3})
. We start with the term with
\psi
. We have

\left|\int_{Q\times Q}h_{N}(x)h_{N}(y)\psi(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}\right|\leqslant\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\int_{Q}\left|h_{N}(x)\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\int_{Q}\left|h_{N}(y)\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y},

with

\int_{Q}\left|h_{N}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\leqslant\int_{Q}{\rho}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}+\int_{Q}{m_{N}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}=\int_{Q}\rho\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}+\int_{Q}{m}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}.

(18)

The RHS of (
18
) is indeed an
L^{1}
function w.r.t. to
x_{3}
and
y_{3}
. We move to the term with
\left|x_{3}\right|
. We have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Q\times Q}h_{N}(x)h_{N}(y)\left|x_{3}-y_{3}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{Q}\left|x_{3}\,h_{N}(x)\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\int_{Q}\left|h_{N}(y)\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}+\int_{Q}\left|y_{3}\,h_{N}(y)\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}\int_{Q}\left|h_{N}(x)\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{Q}\left|x_{3}\right|\left(\rho(x)+m(x)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\right)\left(\int_{Q}\left(\rho(y)+m(y)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left(\int_{Q}\left|y_{3}\right|\left(\rho(y)+m(y)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}\right)\left(\int_{Q}\left(\rho(x)+m(x)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\right).

(19)

Again, the RHS of (
III
) is an
L^{1}
function w.r.t.
x_{3}
and
y_{3}
. Finally, for the term with
\frac{1}{\left|x\right|}
, we split the term as follows

\displaystyle\int_{Q\times Q}h_{N}(x)h_{N}(y)\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q\times Q}\rho(x)\left(\rho(y)-2m_{N}(y)\right)\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\int_{Q\times Q}m_{N}(x)m_{N}(y)\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}.

The first term is bounded by

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Q\times Q}\rho(x)\left(\rho(y)-2m_{N}(y)\right)\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|\rho*\frac{1}{\left|x\right|}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\left\|\rho+2m(\cdot,x_{3})\right\|_{L^{1}(Q)},

which is an
L^{1}
function w.r.t.
x_{3}
. For the second term we use Young inequality with
p=q=r=3/2
. We obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Q\times Q}m_{N}(x)m_{N}(y)\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\underline{y}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|m_{N}(\cdot,x_{3})\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q)}\left\|m_{N}(\cdot,y_{3})\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q)}\left\|\frac{1}{\left|x\right|}\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|m(\cdot,x_{3})\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q)}\left\|m(\cdot,y_{3})\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q)}\times\left\|\frac{1}{\left|\underline{x}\right|}\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q)}.

(20)

As

is compactly supported in
x_{3}
,
x_{3}\mapsto\left\|m(\cdot,x_{3})\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q)}
is also compactly supported and it is an
L^{3/2}
function, thus it is
L^{1}
; which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition III.7
.

The density
\rho_{0}
is invariant w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
, it is indeed the unique minimizer of the 1D model

For any

,

, where

is the translation operator. Thus, by the convexity of the functional
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm per}^{m_{0}}
and the uniqueness of its minimizer (see Theorem

), we deduce that

\rho_{0}
is invariant w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
.
To conclude the proof of the proposition, we notice that for any
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

D_{G}(f,f)=D_{1}(f,f),

as shown in Ref.
hb
, Prop. 3.1
.
∎

Step 4: Convergence of the energy.

We prove in this section point
i
of Theorem
I.5
, which consists of the convergence of the energy.

Proposition III.8
.

We have

I_{0}=\lim I_{N}.

Proof.

By Propositions
III.5
and

, we have

I_{0}\leqslant\liminf I_{N}.

To prove the lower bound on

, we use

(a python implementation can be accessed at
https://github.com/sa3dben/TFW_model
). We adopt the following iterative fixed point scheme: starting form an arbitrary initial guess
u_{0}
, at each fixed point iteration, a linear eigenvalue problem is solved

\left(\Delta+\frac{5}{3}u_{n}^{4/3}+\Phi_{n}\right)u_{n+1}=-\lambda_{1}u_{n+1},

(22)

with
\Phi_{n}
the solution to the Poisson equation
-\Delta\Phi_{n}=4\pi(u_{n}^{2}-\mu)
.
The fixed point iterations are continued until convergence, i.e.
\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|_{2}\leqslant\varepsilon
for some tolerance
\varepsilon>0
. We take
\varepsilon=10^{-6}
in our simulations.

We adopt a periodic setting, with the unit cell
\textstyle\Gamma=Q\times[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]
, and
Q=\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{2}
. We use a Fourier series decomposition approach to solve the linearized equation (
22
).
The solution is expected to be
{\mathcal{R}}-
periodic w.r.t.
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
and decaying at infinity w.r.t.
|x_{3}|
. Taking a large value for
L
, it is reasonable to impose period conditions in the third direction as well. Therefore, we consider the Fourier basis

f_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}e^{i2\pi\left(\underline{x}\cdot\underline{k}+\frac{k_{3}x_{3}}{L}\right)}.

We illustrate the convergence results in Theorem
I.5
using the series of nuclei densities
m_{N}
defined as

m_{N}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\mu(N,x_{1})m_{0}(x_{3})=\frac{5\pi}{2}\left|\cos(N\pi x_{1})\right|\exp\left(-\frac{x_{3}^{2}}{8}\right).

We note that
\int_{Q}\mu(N,x_{1})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2}=\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{Q}\left|\cos(N\cdot\pi x_{1})\right|=1
. We take
L=2\pi
, which proves effective as the function
m_{0}(x_{3})=5\exp\left(-\frac{x_{3}^{2}}{8}\right)
rapidly decreases in the
x_{3}
direction. The same behaviour is assumed for the solutions
u_{N}
, and is validated with 1D simulations for
(u_{0},\rho_{0})
, the 1D solution corresponding to
m_{0}
.

The different functions are sampled on a grid of dimensions
(200\times N,4,300)
. We keep the discretization constant along the
x_{2}
and
x_{3}
-axis as the homogenization is only applied in the
x_{3}
dimension. As we will only compute the zero Fourier mode along
x_{2}
, four points are enough.
We compute the positive Fourier modes up to indices
K=(K_{1},K_{2},K_{3})=(4\times N,0,6)
. On the
x_{3}
-axis, we use the first
7
Fourier modes, found to be sufficient for accurate reconstruction of
m_{N}
and
m_{0}
along that axis. Simulations in 1D prove that the solution
(u_{0}(x_{3}),\rho_{0}(x_{3}))
is also accurately represented by these
7
modes. We assume that this is also the case in 3D for all values of
N
. The choice of
(4N+1)
Fourier modes in the
x_{1}
direction, is motivated by the need for increased Fourier modes as
N
grows, to capture the changing characteristics and new harmonics for
m_{N}
and eventually for the solution
(\rho_{N},u_{N})
. In fact, we know that
(\rho_{N},u_{N})
will rather converge to a constant along the axis
x_{1}
by Theorem
I.5
, however we enforce a hard check for this by computing also large modes for
\rho_{N}
and
u_{N}
.

Different
L^{p}
-norms of the error
e_{N}=\rho_{N}-\rho_{0}
are presented in Figure
3
for values of
N=1,\ldots,5
. We, indeed, observe the convergence stated in Theorem
I.5
.
The same figure shows some convergence rates estimates. We conjecture that we have a theoretical convergence rate of
1/N^{r}
with
\frac{10}{3}<r<4
for the different norms of
e_{N}
and the gradient
L^{2}
norm
\left\|\nabla u_{\text{$N$}}-\nabla{u_{0}}\right\|_{2}
. The discrepancies in Figure
3
at
N=4,5
are probably due to numerical errors.

Remark IV.1
.

It is noteworthy to mention that, for
N\geqslant 6
, and using higher order Fourier modes, the presence of numerical errors hinders a clear observation of further convergence for
e_{N}
.

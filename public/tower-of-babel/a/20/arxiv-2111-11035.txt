Convergence to nonlinear diffusion waves for solutions of M 1 M_{1} model
Nangao Zhang, Changjiang Zhu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we are concerned with the asymptotic behavior of solutions of
M_{1}
model proposed in the radiative transfer fields.
Starting from this model, combined with the compressible Euler equation with damping, we introduce a more general system.
We rigorously prove that the solutions to the Cauchy problem of this system globally exist and time-asymptotically converge to the shifted nonlinear diffusion waves whose profile is self-similar solution to the corresponding parabolic equation governed by the classical Darcy’s law.
Moreover, the optimal convergence rates are also obtained.
Compared with previous results obtained by Nishihara, Wang and Yang in
[
29
]
, we have a weaker and more general condition on the initial data, and the conclusions are more sharper. The approach adopted in the paper is the technical time-weighted energy estimates with the Green function method together.

Key Words:

M_{1}
model, Darcy’s law, nonlinear diffusion waves, time-weighted energy estimates, Green function method.

AMS Subject Classification:
85A25, 35L65, 35B40.

Introduction and main results

Radiative transfer has a huge influence on the hydrodynamic flow in applications such as superorbital atmospheric re-entry, fires or astrophysics. In such regimes, it is important to have a good prediction of the radiative variables. However, solving the full radiative transfer equation is too expensive. It is hence necessary to develop other models for radiative that are cheap, yet accurate enough to give good predictions of the radiative effects. In this case, the
M_{1}
model is an interesting choice (cf.
[
2
,
3
]
). In the present paper, we just consider the scattering part and we omit the role played by the temperature, then the corresponding simplified model reads as follows (cf.
[
1
,
9
]
):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\rho+c\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+c\nabla\cdot P(\rho,u)=-c\sigma\rho u.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Here, the unknown function
\rho=\rho(x,t)\geq 0
denotes the radiative energy, and
u=u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}(1\leq n\leq 3)
denotes the normalized radiative flux. The positive constants
c
and
\sigma
denote the speed of the light and the opacity respectively.
Concerning the radiative pressure
P(\rho,u)
, it is given by

P(\rho,u)=\frac{1}{2}\left((1-\chi(u))\mathbb{I}_{n}+(3\chi(u)-1)\frac{u\otimes u}{|u|^{2}}\right)\rho,

(1.2)

with

\chi(u)=\frac{3+4|u|^{2}}{5+2\sqrt{4-3|u|^{2}}},

(1.3)

where
\mathbb{I}_{n}
is the identity matrix of order
n
and
\left|u\right|\leq 1
.

In this paper, we shall restrict ourselves to the one-dimensional case. We set
c=1
without loss of generality, then (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{t}+\left(\rho u\right)_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\left(\rho u\right)_{t}+\left(\frac{\rho}{3}\right)_{x}+\left(\frac{2\rho u^{2}}{2+\sqrt{4-3u^{2}}}\right)_{x}=-\sigma\rho u,\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

with the following initial data and the far field behaviors

(\rho,u)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},u_{0})(x)\rightarrow(\rho_{\pm},u_{\pm}),\quad\mbox{as}\quad x\rightarrow\pm\infty\quad\mbox{with}\quad\rho_{+}\neq\rho_{-},

(1.5)

where
\rho_{\pm}
and
u_{\pm}
are the constant states.

We are interested in the large time behavior of solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.4
)-(
1.5
). Suppose that
\rho\geq C>0
, then it is more convenient to use the Lagrangian coordinates to explore this system. We consider the coordinate transformation as follows:

x\Rightarrow\int_{(0,0)}^{(x,t)}\rho(y,s)\mathrm{d}y-(\rho u)(y,s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\Rightarrow\tau,

and we still denote the Lagrangian coordinates by
(x,t)
for simplicity.

Let
v=\frac{1}{\rho}
, then the Cauchy problem (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) can be transformed as the following form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle u_{t}+\left(\frac{1}{3v}\right)_{x}-\left(\frac{u^{2}\sqrt{4-3u^{2}}}{v(2+\sqrt{4-3u^{2}})}\right)_{x}=-\sigma u,\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

with initial data

(v,u)|_{t=0}=(v_{0},u_{0})(x)\rightarrow(v_{\pm},u_{\pm}),\quad\mbox{as}\quad x\rightarrow\pm\infty\quad\mbox{with}\quad v_{+}\neq v_{-}.

(1.7)

Due to its complexity, the study on (
1.1
) is quite limited and far from being well. The global existence of smooth solutions with small initial data has been studied by many authors (see
[
9
,
19
,
20
,
25
]
and references therein), and some numerical methods are also considered in
[
1
,
4
]
. However, there are very few studies on the large time behavior of solutions to
M_{1}
model, to the best of our knowledge. In physics, the damping effects usually causes the dynamical system to possess the nonlinear diffusive phenomena, such interesting phenomena for 1-D compressible Euler equations with damping was firstly observed by Hsiao and Liu in
[
10
]
. Here we are also mainly concerned with the nonlinear diffusive phenomena of (
1.6
)-(
1.7
).

Considering the relationship between (
1.6
) and the compressible Euler equations with damping, we expect to obtain more general results including these two systems. So in this paper, we prefer to consider the following more general system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=-\alpha u+(g(u)f(v))_{x},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

with initial data

(v,u)|_{t=0}=(v_{0},u_{0})(x)\rightarrow(v_{\pm},u_{\pm}),\quad\mbox{as}\quad x\rightarrow\pm\infty\quad\mbox{and}~~v_{+}\neq v_{-}.

(1.9)

Here
u=u(x,t)~\mbox{and}~v=v(x,t)>0:\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are unknown variables,
p
is a smooth function of
v
with
p>0
,
g
and
f
are smooth function of
u
and
v
, respectively.
v_{0}(x)
and
u_{0}(x)
are the given initial data, damping constant
\alpha>0
,
v_{\pm}>0
and
u_{\pm}
are constants.

Let us recall some known results about the above system now. For
(g(u)f(v))_{x}\equiv 0
, the system (
1.8
) becomes the compressible Euler equations with linear damping

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=-\alpha u,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}.\end{array}\right.

(1.10)

The global existence and asymptotic behavior of the solutions to the Cauchy problem for (
1.10
) has been extensively studied (see
[
6
,
14
,
19
,
31
]
and references therein). Among them, Hsiao and Liu in
[
10
]
firstly showed the solutions
(v,u)
of (
1.10
) tended time-asymptotically to the nonlinear diffusion waves
(\bar{v},\bar{u})
of the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{v}_{t}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}p(\bar{v})_{xx},\\[5.69054pt]
p(\bar{v})_{x}=-\alpha\bar{u},\end{array}\right.

in the sense

\left\|(v-\bar{v},u-\bar{u})(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(t^{-\frac{1}{2}},t^{-\frac{1}{2}}),

when initial perturbation is small belonging to
H^{3}\times H^{2}
. Then, by taking more detailed but elegant energy estimates, Nishihara in
[
26
]
successfully improved the convergence rates as

\left\|(v-\bar{v},u-\bar{u})(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(t^{-\frac{3}{4}},t^{-\frac{5}{4}}),

provided that small initial perturbation belongs to
H^{3}\times H^{2}
. Subsequently, when the small initial perturbation belonged to
(H^{3}\cap L^{1})\times(H^{2}\cap L^{1})
, by constructing an appropriate approximate Green’s function and using energy methods, Nishihara, Wang and Yang in
[
29
]
further improved the convergence rates as

\left\|(v-\bar{v},u-\bar{u})(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(t^{-1},t^{-\frac{3}{2}}),

which is optimal in the sense comparing with the heat equation.
These conclusions require that both the initial disturbance and the wave strength around a particular diffusion wave are suitably small, some of these restrictions were later partially relaxed by Zhao in
[
32
]
. Later, Mei in
[
24
]
pointed out that the best asymptotic profiles are the solutions for the corresponding nonlinear diffusion equation with some specific selected initial data, and obtained that the convergence rates to the profile is in the form of

\left\|(v-\bar{v},u-\bar{u})(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\ln t,t^{-2}\ln t),

provided that small initial perturbation belongs to
(H^{3}\cap L^{1})\times(H^{2}\cap L^{1})
.
For other studies related to (
1.10
) with nonlinear damping or vacuum, and so on, we can refer to these interesting works (
[

,

21
,

,

,
28
,
34
,
35
]
) and references therein.

When
\alpha=\sigma
,
p(v)=\frac{1}{3v},g(u)=\frac{u^{2}\sqrt{4-3u^{2}}}{2+\sqrt{4-3u^{2}}}
and
f(v)=\frac{1}{v}

1.8
) can be reduced to
M_{1}
model (

) which we will study in the following.
Inspired by these preceding results, in the present paper, we will discuss the convergence to nonlinear diffusion waves for solutions of (
1.8
)-(

), and we will obtain a sharper result which indeed improves those in Nishihara, Wang and Yang

As in
[
10
,
26
]
, the solutions of (
1.8
) time-asymptotically behave as those of Darcy’s law

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{v}_{t}-\bar{u}_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
p(\bar{v})_{x}=-\alpha\bar{u},\end{array}\right.

(1.11)

or

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{v}_{t}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}p(\bar{v})_{xx},\\[5.69054pt]
p(\bar{v})_{x}=-\bar{u},\end{array}\right.

(1.12)

with

(\bar{v},\bar{u})(x,t)\rightarrow(v_{\pm},0),\quad\mbox{as}\quad x\rightarrow\pm\infty.

(1.13)

From
\eqref{1.1}_{1}
and
\eqref{1.5}_{1}
, we have

(v-\bar{v})_{t}-(u-\bar{u})_{x}=0.

(1.14)

Hinted by
\eqref{1.1}_{2}
, we suppose

u(x,t)\rightarrow{\rm e}^{-\alpha t}u_{\pm}\quad\mbox{as}\quad x\rightarrow\pm\infty.

(1.15)

Integrating (
1.14
) with respect to
x
, noting (
1.15
), we obtain

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(v-\bar{v}){\rm d}x={\rm e}^{-\alpha t}(u_{+}-u_{-})=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\frac{u_{+}-u_{-}}{-\alpha}{\rm e}^{-\alpha t}\cdot 1\right),

(1.16)

and hence

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[v(x,t)-\bar{v}(x+x_{0},t)-\frac{u_{+}-u_{-}}{-\alpha}{\rm e}^{-\alpha t}m_{0}(x)\right]{\rm d}x=0,

(1.17)

where
m_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}m_{0}(x){\rm d}x=1.

Integrating (
1.17
) with respect to
t
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}&\left[v(x,t)-\bar{v}(x+x_{0},t)-\frac{u_{+}-u_{-}}{-\alpha}{\rm e}^{-\alpha t}m_{0}(x)\right]{\rm d}x\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[v_{0}(x)-\bar{v}(x+x_{0},0)-\frac{u_{+}-u_{-}}{-\alpha}m_{0}(x)\right]{\rm d}x:=I(x_{0}).\end{split}

(1.18)

Now, Let’s determine
x_{0}
such that
I(x_{0})=0
. Since

\begin{split}I^{\prime}(x_{0})&=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[v_{0}(x)-\bar{v}(x+x_{0},0)-\frac{u_{+}-u_{-}}{-\alpha}m_{0}(x)\right]\right){\rm d}x\\
&=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bar{v}^{\prime}(x+x_{0},0){\rm d}x=-\left[\bar{v}(\infty,0)-\bar{v}(-\infty,0)\right]\\
&=-(v_{+}-v_{-}),\end{split}

(1.19)

then we can obtain

I(x_{0})-I(0)=\int_{0}^{x_{0}}I^{\prime}(y){\rm d}y=-(v_{+}-v_{-})x_{0}.

(1.20)

When
I(x_{0})=0
, we have

x_{0}=\frac{1}{v_{+}-v_{-}}I(0)=\frac{1}{v_{+}-v_{-}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[v_{0}(x)-\bar{v}(x,0)-\frac{u_{+}-u_{-}}{-\alpha}m_{0}(x)\right]{\rm d}x.

(1.21)

Thus, let’s define

V(x,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\left[v(y,t)-\bar{v}(y+x_{0},t)-\hat{v}(y,t)\right]{\rm d}y,

(1.22)

with

\hat{v}(x,t)=\frac{u_{+}-u_{-}}{-\alpha}{\rm e}^{-\alpha t}m_{0}(x).

(1.23)

Putting

\hat{u}(x,t)={\rm e}^{-\alpha t}\left[u_{-}+(u_{+}-u_{-})\int_{-\infty}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y\right].

(1.24)

Then one can immediately obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{v}_{t}-\hat{u}_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\hat{u}_{t}=-\alpha\hat{u}.\end{array}\right.

(1.25)

Combining (
1.8
) and (
1.11
), we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(v-\bar{v}-\hat{v})_{t}-(u-\bar{u}-\hat{u})_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
(u-\bar{u}-\hat{u})_{t}+\bar{u}_{t}+\left[p(v)-p(\bar{v})-g(u)f(v)\right]_{x}+\alpha(u-\bar{u}-\hat{u})=0.\end{array}\right.

(1.26)

Setting

z(x,t)=u(x,t)-\bar{u}(x+x_{0},t)-\hat{u}(x,t),

(1.27)

then from (
1.22
) and (
1.27
), (
1.26
) can be transformed into

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}V_{t}-z=0,\\[5.69054pt]
z_{t}+\left(p^{\prime}(\bar{v})V_{x}\right)_{x}+\alpha z=F_{1}+F_{2},\\[5.69054pt]
(V,z)|_{t=0}:=(V_{0},z_{0})(x)\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad x\rightarrow\pm\infty,\end{array}\right.

(1.28)

or

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}V_{tt}+\left(p^{\prime}(\bar{v})V_{x}\right)_{x}+\alpha V_{t}=F_{1}+F_{2},\\[5.69054pt]
(V,V_{t})|_{t=0}:=(V_{0},z_{0})(x)\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad x\rightarrow\pm\infty,\end{array}\right.

(1.29)

where

F_{1}:=\frac{1}{\alpha}p(\bar{v})_{xt}-\left(p(V_{x}+\bar{v}+\hat{v})-p(\bar{v})-p^{\prime}(\bar{v})V_{x}\right)_{x},

(1.30)

F_{2}:=\left(g(z+\bar{u}+\hat{u})f(V_{x}+\bar{v}+\hat{v})\right)_{x}.

(1.31)

Notations.
In the following,
C
and
c
denote the generic positive constants depending only on the initial data, but independent of the time. For any integer
m\geq 0
, we use
H^{m}
to denote the usual Sobolev space
H^{m}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
. Set
L^{2}=H^{m}
when
m=0
. For simplicity, the norm of
H^{m}
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{m}
with
\|\cdot\|_{0}=\|\cdot\|
.

In order to state our main result, we assume that the following assumptions hold:

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{|x|\leq|u_{\pm}|,{\min\left\{v_{+},v_{-}\right\}\leq y\leq\max\left\{v_{+},v_{-}\right\}}}\{g(x)f^{\prime}(y)-p^{\prime}(y)\}>0,

(1.32)

\displaystyle p,f\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),~p^{\prime}(v)<0~\mbox{for}~\mbox{any}~v>0,

(1.33)

\displaystyle g\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}),~g(0)=g^{\prime}(0)=0.

(1.34)

The following are the main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that (
1.32
)-(
1.34
) hold,
\delta:=|v_{+}-v_{-}|+|u_{+}-u_{-}|
and
\|V_{0}\|_{3}+\|z_{0}\|_{2}
are sufficiency small, Then, there exists a unique time-global solution
(V,z)(x,t)
of (
1.28
), which satisfies

V(x,t)\in C^{k}(0,\infty;H^{3-k}(\mathbb{R})),~~k=0,1,2,3,\quad z(x,t)\in C^{k}(0,\infty;H^{2-k}(\mathbb{R})),~~k=0,1,2,

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}V(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}},\qquad 0\leq k\leq 3,

(1.35)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}z(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}-1},\qquad 0\leq k\leq 2,

(1.36)

\displaystyle(1+t)^{2}\|z_{t}(t)\|+(1+t)^{\frac{5}{2}}(\|z_{xt}(t)\|+\|z_{tt}(t)\|)\leq C.

(1.37)

Furthermore, under the additional assumption that
(V_{0}+\frac{1}{\alpha}z_{0})(x)\in L^{1}
, then the following improved decay estimates hold

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}V(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},\quad 0\leq k\leq 3,

(1.38)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}z(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{k}{2}-1},\qquad 0\leq k\leq 2,

(1.39)

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\frac{9}{4}}\|z_{t}(t)\|+(1+t)^{\frac{11}{4}}(\|z_{xt}(t)\|+\|z_{tt}(t)\|)\leq C.

(1.40)

Remark 1.1
.

It should be noted that in Nishihara, Wang and Yang
[
29
]
, the authors required that the initial perturbation
(V_{0},z_{0})(x)
be sufficiently small in
(H^{3}\cap L^{1})\times(H^{2}\cap L^{1})
. But in our Theorem
1.1
, we require that
(V_{0}+\frac{1}{\alpha}z_{0})(x)\in L^{1}
and the initial perturbation
(V_{0},z_{0})(x)
be sufficiently small in
H^{3}\times H^{2}
, which is weaker than those needed in
[
29
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

As we can see from
[
29
]
, the authors obtained the optimal decay rates
\|\partial_{x}^{k}\partial_{t}^{l}V(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{k}{2}-l}
for
0\leq k+l\leq 3
and
0\leq l\leq 1
, however it was not clarified in the case of
l=2
. Actually, except for
\|z_{tt}(t)\|
, the convergence rates shown in our results Theorem
1.1
are all optimal. As for
\|z_{tt}(t)\|
, we can also use the similar way to obtain an extra time-decay
(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
when
\left(V_{0},z_{0}\right)(x)
is small belonging to
H^{4}\times H^{3}
. Thus in this sense, this results in this paper improves the decay rates obtained in
[
10
,
26
,
29
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

Compared with
[
10
,
26
,
29
]
, in order to close the a priori assumption (
3.1
),
we require an additional technical condition (
1.32
). Notice that if
g(u)\equiv 0
or

, the assumption (

Remark 1.4
.

In fact, as for the case that
v_{+}=v_{-}>0
, the asymptotic profiles of the solutions are expected to be the constant states, we can still obtain the corresponding decay rates which are same as in Theorem
1.1
.

The proof of existence and decay rates in Theorem
1.1
is based on the analysis of the nonlinear diffusion waves and classical energy estimates, as well as Green function method. In fact, compared with former arguments developed in
[
10
,
26
,
29
]
, our conclusions can be regarded as a more general case, the main new ingredients in our analysis lie in the following.

Firstly, as usual, we can obtain the convergence rates of the solutions by the elementary energy estimates and some elaborate computations.
However, since the complexity of the expression for
F_{2}
, the energy estimates become much more complicate and more difficult because we have to face some extra difficult terms, such as
C{\rm e}^{-t}\|V(t)\|_{2}^{2}
in (
3.14
),
C{\rm e}^{-t}\|V_{xx}(t)\|^{2}
in (
3.1
), (
3.27
) and (
3.1
),
\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}gf^{\prime}V_{xx}^{2}{\rm d}x
and in (
3.1
) and so on.
For the first two bad terms, actually, one can easy to see in Lemma
3.3
that
C
is related to
|u_{\pm}|
, since we don’t have the assumption that
|u_{\pm}|\ll 1
, it seems impossible to absorb them with some good terms, but notice that they all have the property of exponential decay, by employing the Gronwall’s inequality, we succeeded in obtaining the desired estimate.
As for the last bad term, we require a technical condition (
1.32
), then it can be absorbed by
-\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{\prime}(\bar{v})V_{xx}^{2}{\rm d}x
. One can see Section
3.1
for more detials. This is a new ingredient in this paper.

The second new ingredient in our analyses lies in the way to obtain the decay estimates (
1.38
)-(
1.40
). As we can see from the dissussions in
[
29
]
, once they got the existence and decay rates of the solutions in the
L^{2}
-framework, by constructing an approximate Green function for the initial perturbations in
L^{1}
-sense, they obtained the improved decay rates
\|\partial_{x}^{k}\partial_{t}^{l}V(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{k}{2}-l}
for
0\leq k+l\leq 3
and
0\leq l\leq 1
. However, the calculation process is quite complicated and tedious, and the case of
l=2
is not clarified.
In this paper, we employ a different strategy to derive the improved decay rates (
1.38
)-(
1.40
). Actually, after obtaining the existence and decay rate of the solution in the
L^{2}
-framework, we give the integral representation of the solution through Green function. Then by analyzing the integral representation of the solution, combined with the weighted energy estimate, we firstly obtain (
1.38
) for
0\leq k\leq 1
.
With all these preparations, by continuing to use weighted energy estimates, we can obtain (
1.38
)-(
1.40
). See Section
3.2
for more detials.
This technique is quite useful and somewhat counterintuitive, it has been successfully used in
[
30
,
8
]
.
We think this approach has at least two advantages: one is that when
0\leq k+l\leq 3
and
0\leq l\leq 2
, we can obtain the optimal decay rates on
\|\partial_{x}^{k}\partial_{t}^{l}V(t)\|
without having to increase the regularity of the initial value, and the other is that the calculation process is much simpler and clearer. By the way, we will also use this approach to help us consider the asymptotic behavior of solutions to (
1.8
) on the quarter plane
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
in the future.

The last new ingredient in our analyses is reflected in the regularity requirement for the initial value. As we can see from
[
29
]
, they obtained their main results under the condition that

|v_{+}-v_{-}|+|u_{+}-u_{-}|+\|V_{0}\|_{3}+\|z_{0}\|_{2}+\|V_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\|z_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

is sufficiently small. Compared with
[
29
]
, the conditions in our result (Theorem
1.1
) are indeed much weaker. The main reason is that we combine Green function theory with weighted energy estimates, which avoids us making complex higher-order estimates using only the integral representation of the solution.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we prepare some preliminaries, which are useful in the proof of Theorem

. Section

is devoted to the proof of the convergence of the solutions

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we are going to introduce some results on some fundamental properties of the nonlinear diffusion waves
(\bar{v},\bar{u})(x,t)
and the correction functions
(\hat{v},\hat{u})(x,t)
, which will be used later.

From the previous works of van Duyn and Peletier in
[
5
]
, we can know that the nonlinear diffusion equation
\eqref{1.6}_{1}
and (
1.13
) have a unique self-similar solution called nonlinear diffusion wave in the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{v}(x,t)=\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+t}}\right):=\phi(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},\\[8.53581pt]
\phi(\pm\infty)=v_{\pm}.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

Substituting
\eqref{2.1}_{1}
into

(3.128)

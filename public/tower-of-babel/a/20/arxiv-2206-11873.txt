A Bourgain-Brezis-Mironescu formula for anisotropic fractional Sobolev spaces and applications to anisotropic fractional differential equations
Ignacio Ceresa Dussel and Julián Fernández Bonder

Abstract
. In this paper we prove Bourgain-Brezis-Mironescu’s type results (cf. [ 4 ] ) (BBM for short) for an energy functional which is strongly related to the fractional anisotropic p-Laplacian. We also provide with the analogous of Maz’ya-Shaposhnikova (see [ 23 ] ) type results for these energies and finally we apply these results to analyze the stability of solutions to anisotropic fractional p − p- laplacian equations.

Introduction

The study of fractional order Sobolev spaces its a classical topic in analysis that has drawn the attention of the mathematical community at least since the middle of the XX century. See, for instance the classical book of Stein
[
27
]
.

In the last decades, there has been an increasing interests in this topic since new applications of these spaces appear to some models in different fields of the natural sciences such as physical models
[
12
,
14
,
17
,
20
,
24
,
28
]
, finance
[
1
,
21
,

]

[
8
,
10
]
, ecology
[
19
,
22
,
25
]
and image processing
[
18
]
.

In particular, a relevant problem is the transition between nonlocal models and local ones. This, for instance, is the transition of the fractional space
W^{s,p}
to the classical Sobolev space
W^{1,p}
as the fractional parameter
s\uparrow 1
and also the case where the fractional parameter
s\downarrow 0
where the space
W^{s,p}
approaches the Lebesgue space
L^{p}
. These problems were analyzed in the seminal papers
[
4
]
(for
s\uparrow 1
) and
[
23
]
(for
s\downarrow 0
).

An interesting application of the results in
[
4
]
is the asymptotic behavior of solutions to the fractional laplacian (or
p-
laplacian) as
s\uparrow 1
. That is, if we consider the problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=f&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

where
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u
is the fractional
p-
Laplace operator defined as

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u(x).=\text{p.v.}(1-s)K_{n,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy,

and
u_{s}
is a solution to (
1.1
), then one expect that as
s\uparrow 1
,
u_{s}
converge to a solution of the local problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

This problem was analyzed, for instance, in
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
15
,
16
]
, and was found to be true in several cases, for instance if
f=f(x)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and if
f=f(x,u)
under some growth control on
f
.

On the other hand, it is interesting to analyze Sobolev spaces and the corresponding operators in the case where some anisotropy is present. This study was carried on in
[
7
]
where the authors analyze the fractional anisotropic Sobolev space, that in each coordinate direction the function has different fractional regularity and different integrability. That is, in
[
7
]
the authors consider
n
different fractional parameters
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
and
n
different integrability parameters
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
and define the space of functions
u(x)=u(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x+he_{i})-u(x)|^{p_{i}}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,dhdx<\infty,\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,n,

where
e_{i}=(0,\dots,\underbrace{1}_{i},\dots,0)
.

In the above mentioned paper
[
7
]
the authors study these fractional anisotropic Sobolev spaces, prove some Sobolev-type immersion theorems and the existence of extremals in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the critical case.

In view of the above discussion, the purpose of this paper is to extend the classical results of
[
4
]
and
[
23
]
to the anisotropic case and then apply these extensions to the study of the asymptotic behavior of solutions to anisotropic fractional
p-
Laplace equations when (some) fractional parameters approaches
1
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let us start by defining the anisotropic spaces that will be considered in this work.

Given
i=1,\dots,n
,
s\in(0,1]
and
p\in(1,\infty)
we define the fractional
i^{th}-
Sobolev space as

W_{i}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }J^{i}_{s,p}(u)<\infty\},

where

J^{i}_{s,p}(u)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(1-s)s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x+he_{i})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dhdx\ &\text{ if }\ &s<1\\
&\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p}\,dx\ &\text{ if }\ &s=1.\end{aligned}\right.

It is easy to see that
W^{s,p}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Banach space with norm

\|u\|_{i,s,p}=\|u\|_{p}+(J^{i}_{s,p}(u))^{1/p},

which is separable and reflexive.

This space consists on
L^{p}
functions that has some mild regularity in the
i^{th}-
variable. In order to control every variable, we proceed as follows. Let
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
and
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
, with
s_{i}\in(0,1]
and
p_{i}\in(1,\infty)
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Then define

W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}W^{s_{i},p_{i}}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

This anisotropic Sobolev space is then a separable and reflexive Banach space with norm

\|u\|_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|u\|_{i,s_{i},p_{i}}.

In most applications, what turns out to be more useful than the norm is the functional
J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}
that is defined as

J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}J^{i}_{s,p}(u)

for
u\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

If we consider
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then we define the anisotropic Sobolev space of functions vanishing at the boundary
\partial\Omega
as

W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)=\{u\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon u|_{\Omega^{c}}=0\}.

Remark 2.1
.

Recall that this is not the most common definition of functions vanishing at the boundary. An alternative definition is

(2.1)

\widetilde{W}^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)=\overline{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)},

where the closure is taken with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}
.

In the classical case, where
s=s_{1}=s_{2}=\dots=s_{n}
and
p=p_{1}=p_{2}=\dots=p_{n}
, it is proved in
[
11
]
that both spaces agree,
\widetilde{W}^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)=W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
, if for instance,
\Omega
is Lipschitz.

We don’t investigate this issue here for the anisotropic case, and will consider the definition given in (
2.1
).

Observe that if we define

p_{\text{min}}=\min\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_{\text{max}}=\max\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}

then, by standard interpolation we have that

W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{u\in L^{p_{\text{min}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{p_{\text{max}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u)<\infty\right\}.

In the
local
anisotropic case, i.e. when
s_{1}=s_{2}=\dots=s_{n}=1
, it is proven in
[
13
]
that the space
W^{1,\mathbf{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is continuously embedded in
L^{\mathbf{p}^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\mathbf{p}^{*}
is the associated critical exponent given by

\mathbf{p}^{*}=\frac{n}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{-1}-1}.

On the other hand, in the general fractional anisotropic case, Sobolev immersion theorems for these spaces was studied in
[
7
]
. In that paper, the authors define the quantities

\overline{\mathbf{s}}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{i}}\right)^{-1},\qquad\overline{\mathbf{s}\mathbf{p}}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{i}p_{i}}\right)^{-1}.

Then, assuming that
\overline{\mathbf{s}\mathbf{p}}<n
the following critical Sobolev exponent is introduced

(2.2)

\mathbf{p}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}}=\mathbf{p}^{*}=\frac{n\overline{\mathbf{s}\mathbf{p}}/\overline{\mathbf{s}}}{n-\overline{\mathbf{s}\mathbf{p}}}.

Under the further assumption that
p_{\text{max}}<\mathbf{p}^{*}
the following Sobolev-Poincaré inequality is proved in
[
7
]
:

0<S=\inf_{u\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\atop\|u\|_{\mathbf{p}^{*}}=1}J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u).

where the constant
S
depends on
n,\mathbf{p}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*}-p_{\text{max}}
and on a lower bound
s_{0}
of
s_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
.

Moreover, a compact-embedding theorem,
W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\subset L^{q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
for every
1\leq q<\mathbf{p}^{*}
is also proved in
[
7
]
.

In the remaining of the section we prove some results on the functionals
J^{i}_{s,p}
that will be helpful in the sequel. When dealing with a single functional, without loss of generality we can assume that
i=1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u
be a function in
W^{1,p}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

\displaystyle J^{1}_{s,p}(u)\leq\frac{1}{p}\left(s\left\|\partial_{x_{1}}u\right\|_{p}^{p}+(1-s)2^{p+1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\right).

Proof.

Given
u\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
it is a well known fact that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq|h|^{p}\|{\partial_{x_{1}}}u\|_{p}^{p},

so

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dxdh=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{|h|^{1+sp}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dh\

\displaystyle+\int_{|h|>1}\frac{1}{|h|^{1+sp}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dh

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+II.

For the first term,

(2.3)

I\leq\left\|\partial_{x_{1}}u\right\|_{p}^{p}2\int_{0}^{1}\frac{h^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh=\frac{1}{p(1-s)}\left\|\partial_{x_{1}}u\right\|_{p}^{p}

and for the second term,

(2.4)

II\leq 2^{p+1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|h|^{1+sp}}dh=2^{p+1}||u||_{p}^{p}\frac{1}{sp}.

Now combining (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) we conclude the proof of the estimate for smooth functions. The result is finished just observing that smooth functions with compact support are dense in
W^{1,p}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
∎

The next lemma shows that the functional
J^{1}_{s,p}
decreases when one regularizes a function
u
with a standard mollifier.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u_{\epsilon}=\rho_{\epsilon}*u
, where
\rho_{\epsilon}
is the standard mollifier. Then

J^{1}_{s,p}(u_{\epsilon})\leq J^{1}_{s,p}(u)

for every
\epsilon>0
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{\epsilon}(x+he_{1})-u_{\epsilon}(h)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(u(x+he_{1}-y)-u(x-y)\right)\rho(y)dy\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(x+he_{1}-y)-u(x-y)\right|^{p}\rho(y)\,dydx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(x+he_{1}-y)-u(x-y)dx\right|^{p}\,dx\right)\rho(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}dx,

where we have used the invariance of the norm with respect to translations and that
\rho

has integral equal to 1. Finally, multiplying by
|h|^{-1-sp}
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}
we get the desired estimate.
∎

Now, we prove a lemma that shows the behavior of
J^{1}_{s,p}
with respect to truncations.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\eta\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\eta=1
on
B_{1}
,
\eta=0
on
B_{2}^{c}
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and
\|\nabla\eta\|_{\infty}\leq 2
. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\eta_{k}(x)=\eta(x/k)
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u_{k}=\eta_{k}u
be the truncation of
u
by
\eta_{k}
then

J^{1}_{s,p}(u_{k})\leq C(n,p)\left(J^{1}_{s,p}(u)+\|u\|_{p}^{p}\right),

where
C(n,p)>0
is independent from k.

Proof.

First, observe that we have

|u_{k}(x+he_{1})-u_{k}(x)|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(|\eta_{k}(x+he_{1})|^{p}|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}+|u(x)|^{p}|\eta_{k}(x+he_{1})-\eta_{k}(x)|^{p}).

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u_{k}(x+he_{1})-u_{k}(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dhdx\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dhdx+

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)|^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\eta_{k}(x+he_{1})-\eta_{k}(x)|^{p}}{h^{1+sp}}\,dh\,dx.

To finish the proof, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\eta_{k}(x+he_{1})-\eta_{k}(x)|^{p}}{h^{1+sp}}\,dh

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{|h|<1}+\int_{|h|\geq 1}\right)\frac{|\eta_{k}(x+he_{1})-\eta_{k}(x)|^{p}}{h^{1+sp}}\,dh

\displaystyle=I+II.

Now,

I\leq\|\partial_{x_{1}}\eta_{k}\|_{\infty}^{p}\int_{|h|<1}\frac{1}{|h|^{1+sp-p}}\,dh=\frac{2^{p+1}}{k^{p}}\frac{1}{p(1-s)}.

Finally,

II\leq 2^{p-1}\|\eta_{k}\|_{\infty}^{p}\int_{|h|\geq 1}\frac{1}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh=\frac{2^{p}}{sp}.

and our lemma has been proved.

∎

The next lemma is proved in
[
4
]
and is crucial in the arguments that follows.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\phi,\psi\colon(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
be measurables functions and suppose that
\phi(2h)\leq\phi(h)
for
h\in(0,1)
and that
\psi
is nonincreasing.Then given
r>-1,

\int_{0}^{1}h^{r}\phi(h)\psi(h)\,dh\geq\frac{r+1}{2^{r+1}}\int_{0}^{1}h^{r}\phi(h)\,dh\int_{0}^{1}h^{r}\psi(h)\,dh.

With the help of Lemma
2.5
we can prove the following immersion result.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s_{1}<s_{2}<1
then

\displaystyle J^{1}_{s_{1},p}(u)\leq 2^{(1-s_{1})p}J^{1}_{s_{2},p}(u)+\frac{(1-s_{1})2^{p+1}}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p},

for all
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

First, let us begin by observing that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dxdh=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dxdh.

Hence, the lemma will be proved if we show that

\displaystyle(1-s_{1})\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+s_{1}p}}dxdh

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{(1-s_{1})p}(1-s_{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+s_{2}p}}dxdh+\frac{(1-s_{1})2^{p}}{s_{1}p}\|u\|_{p}^{p},

Define
F
as

F(h)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}\,dx,

since

\displaystyle F(2h)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x+2he_{1})-u(x+he_{1})|^{p}\,dxdh\right)

\displaystyle+2^{p-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}\,dxdh\right)

\displaystyle=2^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}\,dxdh=2^{p}F(h),

We can take
g(h)=F(h)/h^{p}
and get that
g(2h)\leq g(h)
for all
h>0
.

On the other hand,

(2.5)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dxdh&=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{F(h)}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{g(h)}{|h|^{1-(1-s)p}}\,dh\end{split}

Now, let us consider
0<s_{1}<s_{2}<1
, then

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{g(h)}{|h|^{1-(1-s_{2})p}}\,dh=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{|h|^{1-(1-s_{1})p}}g(h)\frac{1}{|h|^{(s_{2}-s_{1})p}}\,dh

and applying Lemma
2.5
with
r=(1-s_{1})p-1
and
\psi(h)=h^{-(s_{2}-s_{1})p}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{g(h)}{|h|^{1-(1-s_{2})p}}\,dh

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-s_{1})p}{2^{(1-s_{1})p}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{g(h)}{h^{1-(1-s_{1})p}}\,dh\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{h^{1-(1-s_{2})p}}dh

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{(1-s_{1})p}}\frac{1-s_{1}}{1-s_{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{g(h)}{h^{1-(1-s_{1})p}}\,dh.

Recalling (
2.5
)

\displaystyle\frac{1-s_{1}}{2^{(1-s_{1})p}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{R^{n}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+s_{1}p}}\,dxdh

\displaystyle\leq(1-s_{2})\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+s_{2}p}}\,dxdh.

Finally,

\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+s_{1}p}}\,dxdh\leq\frac{2^{p}\|u\|^{p}_{p}}{s_{1}p}

this completes the proof.
∎

3.
BBM-type results for
W^{s,p}_{i}

In this section we extend the results in the seminal paper
[
4
]
to the anisotropic case.
Our first result conserns with the asymptotic behavior of the funcional
J^{1}_{s,p}
as
s\uparrow 1
in a pointwise sense.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
s\in(0,1)
then

\lim_{s\to 1}J^{1}_{s,p}(u)=J^{1}_{1,p}(u).

Proof.

To prove the theorem
3.1
we split the proof in 3 steps.

Step 1:
Let
u\in C^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Arguing as in
[
4
, Theorem 2]
we easily obtain that

\left|\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{p}}-\left|\partial_{x_{i}}u\right|^{p}\right|\leq C|h|,

where
C
depends on the
C^{2}-
norm of
u
and
p
.

Naming

A=\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{p}}-\left|\partial_{x_{i}}u\right|^{p}

and taking the integral over
\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}A\,dh=\int_{|h|\leq 1}A\,dh+\int_{|h|>1}A\,dh,

where by routine integration,

\int_{|h|\leq 1}A\,dx=2C\frac{1}{1+p(1-s)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{|h|>1}A\,dx=2^{p}\frac{\|u\|_{\infty}^{p}}{ps}.

Finally, taking limit
s\to 1
we conclude that

(3.1)

\lim_{s\to 1}s(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}dh=\frac{2}{p}|\partial_{x_{1}}u|^{p}.

Now, the key is look up an integrable majorant
for (
3.1
).

Let

U_{s}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh.

Then since
u\in C^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists
R>0
such that
\mathop{\text{supp}}(u)\subset Q_{R}
, where
Q_{R}=[-R,R]^{n}
.

Thus, we split the problem:

•

If
|x_{1}|\leq 2R

\begin{split}|U_{s}(x)|&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh\\
&=\left(\int_{|h|\leq 3R}+\int_{|h|>3R}\right)\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh\end{split},

where we bound the first integral by

\int_{|h|\leq 3R}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh\leq||\partial_{x_{1}}u||_{\infty}^{p}\frac{3R^{p}}{p(1-s)},

and the second by

\int_{|h|>3R}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh\leq||u||_{\infty}^{p}\frac{3R^{-p(1/2)}}{(1-s)p}.

Then

|s(1-s)U_{s}(x)|\leq C\chi_{Q_{2R}}(x),

where C is a constant depending of
p,R
but not on
s
.

•

If
|x_{1}|>2R
,

\begin{split}|U_{s}(x)|=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x_{1}+h,x^{\prime})|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}dh\leq\left(\frac{2}{|x_{1}|}\right)^{1+sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x_{1},x^{\prime})|^{p}dx_{1}.\end{split}

Where we have used that

|h|\geq|x_{1}|-|x_{1}+h|\geq|x_{1}|-R\geq\frac{1}{2}|x_{1}|
.

Observe that

G(x^{\prime}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x_{1},x^{\prime})|^{p}dx_{1}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})

Therefore,

|s(1-s)U_{s}(x)|\leq C\left(\chi_{Q_{2R}}(x)+\chi_{Q_{2R}^{c}}(x)\left(\frac{2}{|x_{1}|}\right)^{1+(1/2)p}G(x^{\prime})\right)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

To conclude this step, we only have to use the Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem and obtain

(3.2)

\lim_{s\to 1}s(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dhdx=\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\partial_{x_{1}}u\right|^{p}\,dx,

for all
u\in C^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Step 2:
Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Take a sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\partial_{x_{1}}u_{k}\to\partial_{x_{1}}u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Let

(3.3)

[u]_{1,s,p}=(J^{1}_{s,p}(u))^{1/p},\qquad 0<s\leq 1.

Observe that
[\,\cdot\,]_{1,s,p}
defined in (
3.3
) is a seminorm and thus satisfies the triangle inequality. So

\displaystyle\left|[u]_{1,s,p}-[u]_{1,1,p}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|[u]_{1,s,p}-[u_{k}]_{1,s,p}|+|[u_{k}]_{1,s,p}-[u_{k}]_{1,1,p}|+|[u_{k}]_{1,1,p}-[u]_{1,1,p}|

\displaystyle=I+II+III.

Using lemma
2.2
,

I\leq|[u_{k}-u]_{1,s,p}|\leq C(n,p)\left(\|u_{k}-u\|_{p}+\|\partial_{x_{1}}u_{k}-\partial_{x_{1}}u\|_{p}\right).

which converges to zero if
k\to\infty
, uniformly in s. The third term is bounded in much the same way.

Finally, the second term is consequence of Step 1, and so the proof of Step 2 is complete.

Step 3:
Given
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and assume that

(3.4)

\liminf_{s\to 1}J^{1}_{s,p}(u)<\infty,

Let
u_{k,\epsilon}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be given by

u_{k,\epsilon}=\rho_{\epsilon}*(u\eta_{k})

and applying lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
together with (
3.4
), we obtain

\liminf_{s\to 1}J^{1}_{s,p}(u_{k,\epsilon})\leq C,

where

is independent from
k
and
\epsilon>0
.

Next, for any fixed
k
and
\epsilon
, we apply Step 1 and obtain

C\geq\lim_{s\to 1}J^{1}_{s,p}(u_{k,\epsilon})=J^{1}_{1,p}(u_{k,\epsilon}).

Hence, there exist a subsequence
u_{j}=\{u_{k_{j},\epsilon_{j}}\}\subset\{u_{k,\epsilon}\}
such that
\partial_{x_{1}}u_{j}\rightharpoonup\partial_{x_{1}}u
weakly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Thus
u\in W_{1}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. and so the proof of Step 3 follows from Step 2.

This completes the proof of the Theorem.
∎

Next, we will consider the case that, instead of having a fixed function
u

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
0<s_{k}\to 1
when

\{u_{k}\}\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(3.5)

\sup_{k}\|u_{k}\|_{p}<\infty,\quad u_{k}\to u\text{ in }L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{and}\qquad\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}J^{1}_{s_{k},p}(u_{k})<\infty.

Then,

(3.6)

J^{1}_{1,p}(u)\leq\liminf_{s_{k}\to 1}J^{1}_{s_{k},p}(u_{k}).

Proof.

Let
\{u_{k}\}\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that (

be fixed but arbitrary. By the

a.e in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By Fatou’s lemma,

Applying now Lemma

\displaystyle J^{1}_{t,p}(u)\leq 2^{(1-t)p}\liminf_{k}J^{1}_{s_{k},p}(u_{k})+(1-t)\frac{2^{p+1}}{p}M^{p},

Another useful result is the following compactness result for a sequence

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})

To conclude the section, let us now make the connection of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
with the notion of Gamma-convergence.

Recall that in a metric space
(X,d)
, a sequence of functions
f_{k}\colon X\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is said to Gamma converges to a limit function
f
it this two conditions are satisfied:

•

(
\liminf
inequalitty) For every
x\in X
and every sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
such that
x_{k}\to x
, it holds that

f(x)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(x_{k}).

•

(
\limsup
inequality) For every
x\in X
there exists a sequence (called the
recovery sequence
)
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
such that

f(x)\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(y_{k}).

The notion of Gamma convergence is a useful tool in the Calculus of Variations and was introduced by De Giorgi in the 70’s. See
[
9
]
for a throughout introduction to the subject.

Observe that Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
immediately imply that
J^{1}_{s,p}
Gamma converges to
J^{1}_{1,p}
as
s\uparrow 1
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for every
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
bounded. We collect this statement in the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
s_{k}\to 1
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then
J^{1}_{s_{k},p}
Gamma converges to
J^{1}_{1,p}
as
k\to\infty
in
L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Just observe that Theorem
3.2
is exactly the
\liminf
inequality and for the
\limsup
inequality one just have to take the constant sequence
u_{k}=u
as a recovery sequence and apply Theorem
3.1
.
∎

In the forthcoming application (cf. Section
5
) it will be important to have the Gamma convergence of the full functional

J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}}(u).

Moreover we consider a sequence
\mathbf{s}_{k}=(s_{1}^{k},s_{2}^{k},\dots,s_{n}^{k})
where for instance

as

and

Hence
\mathbf{s}_{k}\to\mathbf{s}_{0}=(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{j},s_{j+1},\dots,s_{n})

In principle, it is not true that sums of Gamma convergent function is Gamma convergent (see

]
). However, in this case since the recovery sequence is the constant sequence (and in particular is the same for every function), the Gamma convergence of

Let us collect these results in the next corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
with

for every

. Let

as

The

The

,

∎

4.
Mazya-Shaposhnikova–type results

In this section we work on an adaptation of the seminal result of Maz’ya and Shaposhnikova,
[
23
]
. Namely we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and assume that there exists
s_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
u\in W^{s_{0},p}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then

\lim_{s\to 0}J^{i}_{s,p}(u)=2p^{-1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

Proof.

In
[
23
]
, It is proved that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4.1)

\lim_{s\to 0}s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dxdy=2p^{-1}|S^{n-1}|\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}.

for all
u\in W^{s_{0},p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
s_{0}>0
.

In particular, if we apply this result in dimension
1
to
v(x_{1})=u(x_{1},x^{\prime})
where
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is fixed, we obtain that

\lim_{s\to 0}s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|v(x_{1}+h)-v(x_{1})|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dx_{1}dh=\frac{4}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|v(x_{1})|^{p}\,dx_{1},

which in terms of
u
reads

(4.2)

\lim_{s\to 0}s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x_{1}+h,x^{\prime})-u(x_{1},x^{\prime})|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dx_{1}dh=\frac{4}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x_{1},x^{\prime})|^{p}\,dx_{1},

So, to conclude our result we need to integrate with respect to
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and for that it remains to find an integrable majorant for

V_{s}(x^{\prime}):=s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x_{1}+h,x^{\prime})-u(x_{1},x^{\prime})|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dx_{1}dh.

For that purpose, we proceed as follows

V_{s}(x^{\prime})=s\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{|h|\leq 1}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{|h|\geq 1}\right)\frac{|u(x_{1}+h,x^{\prime})-u(x_{1},x^{\prime})|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dx_{1}dh=I+II.

For the first term

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{|h|\leq 1}\frac{|u(x_{1}+h,x^{\prime})-u(x_{1},x^{\prime})|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dx_{1}dh

\displaystyle\leq s_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{|h|\leq 1}\frac{|u(x_{1}+h,x^{\prime})-u(x_{1},x^{\prime})|^{p}}{|h|^{1+s_{0}p}}\,dx_{1}dh

The inequality came from
s\leq s_{0}
, and thanks to
u\in W^{s_{0},p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we conclude the integrability of this part.
For the second term

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{|h|\geq 1}\frac{|u(x_{1}+h,x^{\prime})-u(x_{1},x^{\prime})|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dx_{1}dh

\displaystyle\leq s\int_{\mathbb{R}}2^{p}|u(x_{1},x^{\prime})|^{p}\int_{|h|\geq 1}\frac{1}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dhdx_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{p}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x_{1},x^{\prime})|^{p}\,dx_{1}

and this last term is integrable in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
since
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

These two bounds conclude the proof.
∎

5.
Applications to partial differential equations.

In this section, we will make use of the results in Section
3
to find connections between nonlocal pseudo
p-
Laplace operators and their local counterparts.

First, we will give some definitions and enforce notations that will be helpful.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded subset. The topological dual spaces of
W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega),W_{i,0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
will be denoted by
W^{-\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
W_{i}^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
respectively.

Now, it is easy to see that the functionals
J^{i}_{s,p}
are Fréchet differentiable for every
i=1,\dots,n
, every
s\in(0,1]
and every
p\in(1,\infty)
. Even more
(J^{i}_{s,p})^{\prime}\colon W^{s,p}_{i,0}(\Omega)\to W^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{i}(\Omega)
is continuous and is given by

\displaystyle\langle(J^{i}_{s,p})^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=

\displaystyle s(1-s)p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x+he_{i})-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(x+he_{i})-u(x))(v(x+he_{i})-v(x))}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dhdx,

if
0<s<1
and

\langle(J^{i}_{1,p})^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u\ \partial_{x_{i}}v\,dx,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
W^{s,p}_{i,0}(\Omega)
and its dual
W^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{i}(\Omega)
.

5.1.
Pseudo fractional
\mathbf{p}-
Laplace operator

Let
s_{i}\in(0,1],i=1,\dots,n
be fractional parameters and define
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
. Next, let
p_{i}\in(1,\infty),i=1,\dots,n
be integrable parameters and define
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
.

Recall that the operator
J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}\colon W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
that was defined as
J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}}
, is then Fréchet differentiable with derivative given by

J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}^{\prime}\colon W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)\to W^{-\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}(\Omega)

J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}^{\prime}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}(J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}})^{\prime}(u).

So, we define the
pseudo anisotropic
\mathbf{p}-
Laplace operator
as

(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}})^{\mathbf{s}}u:=(J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}})^{\prime}(u).

Observe that in the case where
\mathbf{s}=(1,\dots,1)
(that is the pure local case) and
\mathbf{p}=(p,\dots,p)
, then

(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}})^{\mathbf{s}}u:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}\left(|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u\right),

that is the well-known pseudo p-Laplace operator.

Therefore, we want to study the equation

(5.1)

\begin{cases}(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}})^{\mathbf{s}}u=f&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{cases}

and, we say that
u\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of (
5.1
) if

\langle(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}})^{\mathbf{s}}u,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx

for all
v\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
.

In order for a weak solution to be well defined, as usual we need to impose some integrability conditions on the source term
f
.

We consider first the case where
f=f(x)
and then, the Sobolev immersion theorem proved in
[
7
]
requires that
f\in L^{(\mathbf{p}^{*})^{\prime}}(\Omega)
.

Existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to (
5.1
) is then a direct consequence of the direct method in the calculus of variations, since the solution is the unique minimizer of the functional

I(v):=J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(v)-\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx,

which is a strictly convex, coercive and continuous functional in
W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Let us summarize all of this facts into a single statement.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set, let
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
,
s_{i}\in(0,1)
,
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
,
p_{i}\in(1,\infty)
and
\mathbf{p}^{*}
the critical Sobolev exponent defined in (
2.2
). Assume that
p_{\text{max}}<\mathbf{p}^{*}
.

Then, for every
f\in L^{(\mathbf{p}^{*})^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique weak solution
u\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
to (
5.1
).

5.2.
Asymptotic behavior of solutions

The objective of this section is to analyze the asymptotic behavior of the solution to (
5.1
) when (some of the)
s_{i}\to 1
.

To this end assume that
s_{1}^{k},\dots,s_{j}^{k}\to 1
(as
k\to\infty
) and
s_{j+1},\dots,s_{n}
remain fixed and consider
\mathbf{s}_{k}:=(s_{1}^{k},\dots,s_{j}^{k},s_{j+1},\dots,s_{n})
and the functionals

By Proposition
5.2
, there exists a unique minimizer
u_{k}
of

. Hence we want to study the behavior of the sequence

To this end, let us define

Next we make full use of the results in Section 3 and conclude the following theorem:

.

With the preceding notation, the functionals

By Corollary
3.5

]
).

An immediate consequence of this result is the convergence of the solutions of (

) to the corresponding limiting problem.

5.3.
The semilinear-like case

In this subsection, we investigate the case where the source term

depends also on the solution itself

. That is

.

In this case, in order to have the notion of weak solution some conditions on

(
f_{1}
)

is Carathéodory function.

(
f_{2}

and an exponent

|f(x,z)|\leq C(1+|z|^{q-1})

Recall that
(

f

(

f
2

)

Proof of Theorem
5.5
.

First observe that

\langle(J_{\mathbf{s}_{k},\mathbf{p}})^{\prime}(u_{k}),u_{k}\rangle\geq p_{\text{min}}J_{\mathbf{s}_{k},\mathbf{p}}(u_{k}).

Hence, using
(

is bounded in

, we have that

(5.4)

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}J_{\mathbf{s}_{k},\mathbf{p}}(u_{k})<\infty.

Also, since

in

, passing to a subsequence, if necessary, we get that

a.e. in
\Omega
and, moreover, using Theorem

Next, we define
\eta_{k}=f(x,u_{k}(x))=(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}})^{\mathbf{s}_{k}}u_{k}\in W^{-\mathbf{s}_{k},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{k},v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{k})v\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}(1+|u_{k}|^{q-1})|v|\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}|v|\,dx+C\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{q-1}|v|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{\mathbf{p}_{\mathbf{s}_{0}}^{*}}+C\|v\|_{\mathbf{p}_{\mathbf{s}_{0}}^{*}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{(q-1)(\mathbf{p}_{\mathbf{s}_{0}}^{*})^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{1/(\mathbf{p}_{\mathbf{s}_{0}}^{*})^{\prime}}.

Since

\|u_{k}\|_{(q-1)(\mathbf{p}_{\mathbf{s}_{0}}^{*})^{\prime}}\leq C,\quad\text{for every }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence,
\eta_{k}
is bounded in
W^{-\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}(\Omega)

Therefore, up to some subsequence, there exists

taking limit as

Now, we want to identify

For that purpose, we will use the monotonicity of the operator and the fact that

is a weak solution of (

\begin{split}0&\leq\langle(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}})^{\mathbf{s}_{k}}u_{k},u_{k}-v\rangle-\langle(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}})^{\mathbf{s}_{k}}v,u_{k}-v\rangle\\

Taking limit

5.4.
Ground-state solutions

The purpose of this subsection is to investigate further the results of the preceding one and analyze the case where the sequence of solutions
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are
ground-state solutions
to the problem:

(5.5)

\begin{cases}(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}})^{\mathbf{s}_{k}}u_{k}=f(x,u_{k})&\text{in }\Omega\\
u_{k}=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\par\end{cases}

and its limit behavior to

(5.6)

\begin{cases}(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}})^{\mathbf{s}_{0}}u=f(x,u)&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\par\end{cases}

Recall that ground state solutions are minimizers of the energy functional

I_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u)=J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u)-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\,dx,

restricted to the
Nehari manifold

\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}=\{v\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}:\langle(I_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}})^{\prime}(v),v\rangle=0\}.

where
F(x,z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(x,t)\,dt
is the primitive of
f.

On the nonlinearity
f
, besides
(

f
1

)
and
(

f
2

)
, it will be assumed to fulfill the following further structural hypothesis that are standard when consider ground state solutions in nonlinear problems

(
f_{3}
)

\displaystyle\frac{F(x,z)}{|z|^{p_{\text{max}}}}\to\infty
as
|z|\to\infty
uniformly with respect to
x\in\Omega
.

(
f_{4}
)

f(x,z)=\circ(|z|^{p_{max}-1})
as
z\to 0
uniformly with respect to
x\in\Omega
.

(
f_{5}
)

For almost every
x\in\Omega

\frac{f(x,z)}{|z|^{p_{\text{max}}-1}}\text{ is strictly increasing on }(-\infty,0)\cup(0,\infty)

(
f_{6}
)

There is a
\mu>p_{\text{max}}
such that

\mu F(x,z)\leq zf(x,z)

Also, as in the preceding subsection, the exponent
q
in
(

f
2

)
must be subcritical, i.e.
q<\mathbf{p}^{*}
.

It is well known that under hypotheses
(

f
1

)
-
(

f
6

)
a ground state actually exists. In fact, in our context, a ground state solution,
u_{\mathbf{s}}
, is a mountain pass solution.

Remark 5.8
.

Observe that the Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}
has the property that given any nonzero function
u\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique
t>0
such that
tu\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}
. We denote this scalar as
t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}
.

The existence and uniqueness of this scalar
t^{\mathbf{s}}_{u}
, follows by using standard arguments in the Calculus of Variations. That is, given
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
define the function

\phi(t)=J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(tu)-\int_{\Omega}F(x,tu)\,dx.

On the one hand, it is easy to verify that
\phi(0)=0
,
\phi^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
for small values of
t>0
(these follows from the definition of
\phi
and
(

f
4

)
). Moreover, by
(

f
3

)
we obtain that
\phi(t)\to-\infty
when
t\to\infty.
So we conclude the existence of at least one critical point of
\phi.

On the other hand,
\phi^{\prime}(t)
verifies that

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{p_{i}}J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}}(u)-\int_{\Omega}f(x,ut)tu\,dx

\displaystyle=t^{p_{\text{max}}}\Big[C+\underbrace{\sum_{p_{i}\not={p_{\text{max}}}}t^{p_{i}-p_{\text{max}}}J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}}(u)-\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,ut)u^{p_{\text{max}}}}{|ut|^{p_{\text{max}}-1}}\,dx}_{A}\Big].

By
(

f
5

)
,
A
is strictly decreasing, so the critical point must be unique.

By the previous remark, it is easy to check that a ground state solution must satisfy

c_{\mathbf{s}}=I_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u_{\mathbf{s}})=\inf_{v\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\ \sup_{t>0}I_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(tv)>0

The next lemma shows that the mountain pass levels of the ground state solutions of (
5.5
) are uniformly bounded.

Lemma 5.9
.

Under the previous notation,
c_{\mathbf{s}_{0}}\geq\limsup_{\mathbf{s}\to\mathbf{s}_{0}}c_{\mathbf{s}}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{p}}
, then
t^{\mathbf{s}}_{u}u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}
. We first need to analyze the behavior of
t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}
as
\mathbf{s}\to\mathbf{s}_{0}
.

Since
t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}
, we have that

\displaystyle\langle(J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}})^{\prime}(t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u),t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}|^{p_{i}}J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f(x,t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u)t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\mu\int_{\Omega}F(x,t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u)\,dx

\displaystyle=\mu|t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}|^{p_{\text{max}}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,t_{\mathbf{s}}u)|u|^{p_{\text{max}}}}{|t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u|^{p_{\text{max}}}}\,dx.

Now, if we assume that
t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}\geq 1
, we obtain that

p_{\text{max}}J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u)\geq\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,t_{\mathbf{s}}u)|u|^{p_{\text{max}}}}{|t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u|^{p_{\text{max}}}}\,dx

As, by Theorem
3.1
,
J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u)\to J_{\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{p}}(u)<\infty
when
\mathbf{s}\to\mathbf{s}_{0}
, from
(

f
3

)
implies that
\{t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}\}_{\mathbf{s}}
is bounded.

Let
t_{0}\geq 0
be any accumulation point of
\{t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}\}_{\mathbf{s}}
. Let
t_{k}=t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}_{k}}
be a subsequence such that
t_{k}\to t_{0}
as
k\to\infty
. Let us see that
t_{0}\neq 0
. In fact, the Nehari identity gives us

\sum_{i=1}^{n}|t_{k}|^{p_{i}}J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}}(u)=\int_{\Omega}f(x,t_{k}u)t_{k}u\,dx

from where we get

\sum_{i=1}^{n}|t_{k}|^{p_{i}-p_{\text{min}}}J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,t_{k}u)}{|t_{k}u|^{p_{\text{min}}-1}}|u|^{p_{\text{min}}-1}u\,dx

Now, if
t_{0}=0
and
p_{\text{min}}=p_{i_{0}}
, passing to the limit as
k\to\infty
using
(

f
4

)
we deduce that

J_{s_{i_{0}},p_{\text{min}}}^{i_{0}}(u)=0.

But this implies that
u
is independent of
x_{i_{0}}
and thus that
u=0
, a contradiction. Therefore
t_{0}>0
.

Moreover, from
(

f
2

)
and
(

f
4

)
we have that

|f(x,t_{k}u)t_{k}u|\leq\epsilon|t_{k}u|^{p_{\text{min}}}+C_{\epsilon}|t_{k}u|^{q}\leq C(|u|^{p_{\text{min}}}+|u|^{q})\in L^{1}(\Omega),

where
C>0
is independent of
k
. Then, using the dominated convergence theorem ,

\int_{\Omega}f(x,t_{k}u)t_{k}u\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}f(x,t_{0}u)t_{0}u\,dx\quad(\text{as }k\to\infty).

So, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{n}|t_{0}|^{p_{i}}J^{i}_{s_{i}^{0},p_{i}}(u)=\int_{\Omega}f(x,t_{0}u)t_{0}u\,dx.

Since
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{p}},

t_{0}=1
. Then
t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}\to 1
as
\mathbf{s}\to\mathbf{s}_{0}
.

Now, we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{s}}\leq I_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}}(t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u)-\int_{\Omega}F(x,t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u)\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}|t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}|^{p_{i}}J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}}(u)-\int_{\Omega}F(x,t_{u}^{\mathbf{s}}u)\,dx.

We can now take the limit as
\mathbf{s}\to\mathbf{s}_{0}
in the former inequality to obtain

\limsup_{\mathbf{s}\to\mathbf{s}_{0}}c_{\mathbf{s}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}J^{i}_{s_{i}^{0},p_{i}}(u)-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\,dx=I_{\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{p}}(u),

where we have used Theorem
3.1
.

Then, we take the infimum in

to conclude the desired result.
∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
u_{s}\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
be a ground state solution of (
5.5
).
Then there exist two constants
0<c<C<\infty
independent on
s
such that

c\leq J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u_{\mathbf{s}})\leq C

Proof.

Let
u_{\mathbf{s}}\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
be a ground state solution of (
5.5
). By the previuos lemma, there exist a constant
C>0
, such that

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\geq I_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u_{\mathbf{s}})-\frac{1}{\mu}\langle I_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}^{\prime}(u_{\mathbf{s}}),u_{\mathbf{s}}\rangle

\displaystyle=J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u_{\mathbf{s}})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu}J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}}(u_{\mathbf{s}})-\int_{\Omega}\left(F(x,u_{\mathbf{s}})-\frac{1}{\mu}f(x,u_{\mathbf{s}})u_{\mathbf{s}}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)J^{i}_{s_{i},p_{i}}(u_{s})

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{p_{\text{max}}}-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u).

For the lower bound, since
u_{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}
, we have that

(5.7)

p_{\text{min}}J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u_{\mathbf{s}})\leq\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{\mathbf{s}})u_{\mathbf{s}}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\epsilon|u_{\mathbf{s}}|^{p_{\text{max}}}+C_{\epsilon}|u_{\mathbf{s}}|^{q}\right)\,dx.

Now from
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
, we obtain that

(5.8)

\min\{\|u\|_{\mathbf{p}^{*}}^{p_{\text{min}}},\|u\|_{\mathbf{p}^{*}}^{p_{\text{max}}}\}\leq CJ_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u),

where
C
is independent of
u\in W^{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Next, from (
5.7
), using Hölder’s inequality and (
5.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u_{\mathbf{s}})\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\max\{J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u_{\mathbf{s}}),J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u_{\mathbf{s}})^{p_{\text{max}}/p_{\text{min}}}\}

\displaystyle+C_{\epsilon}\max\{J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u_{\mathbf{s}})^{q/p_{\text{max}}},J_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{p}}(u_{\mathbf{s}})^{q/p_{\text{min}}}\}.

Then, choosing

is bounded below away from zero, as we wanted to show.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main result of the section.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
\mathbf{s}_{k}\to\mathbf{s}_{0}

Then the sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is precompact in

, for every

and any accumulation point

and it is a ground state solution to

Proof.

First observe that if
\mathbf{s}_{k}\to\mathbf{s}_{0}
, then
\mathbf{p}^{*}_{k}:=\mathbf{p}_{\mathbf{s}_{k}}^{*}\to\mathbf{p}_{0}^{*}

In view of (

\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
will be bounded in

But, this follows from Lemma

(

f
6

)
. Finally, Theorem

gives the existence of a subsequence
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

It remains to see that

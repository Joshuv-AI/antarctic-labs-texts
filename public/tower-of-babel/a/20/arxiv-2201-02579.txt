Incidence and Laplacian matrices of wheel graphs and their inverses
Jerad Ipsen, Sudipta Mallik

Abstract
It has been an open problem to find the Moore-Penrose inverses of the incidence, Laplacian, and signless Laplacian matrices of families of graphs except trees and unicyclic graphs. Since the inverse formulas for an odd unicyclic graph and an even unicyclic graph are quite different, we consider wheel graphs as they are formed from odd or even cycles. In this article we solve the open problem for wheel graphs. This work has an interesting connection to inverses of circulant matrices.



1
Introduction

Let
G
be a simple graph on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
and
m
edges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m}
with the adjacency matrix
A
and the degree matrix
D
. The
Laplacian matrix

L
and
signless Laplacian matrix

Q
of
G
are defined as
L=D-A
and
Q=D+A
respectively. The vertex-edge
incidence matrix

M
of
G
is the
n\times m
matrix whose
(i,j)
-entry is
1
if vertex
i
is incident with edge
e_{j}
and
0
otherwise. It is well-known that
Q=MM^{T}
. An
oriented incidence matrix

N
of
G
is the
n\times m
matrix obtained from
M
by changing one of the two 1s in each column of
M
to
-1
. It is well-known that
Q=NN^{T}
for any oriented incidence matrix
N
of
G
.

Circulant matrices play a crucial role in this article. A
circulant matrix
of order
n
is an
n\times n
matrix of the form

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}c_{0}&c_{1}&c_{2}&\cdots&c_{n-1}\\
c_{n-1}&c_{1}&c_{2}&\cdots&c_{n-2}\\
c_{n-2}&c_{n-1}&c_{1}&\cdots&c_{n-3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
c_{1}&c_{2}&c_{3}&\cdots&c_{0}\end{array}\right]

which is denoted by
\circulant(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})
. For example, the incidence matrix of a cycle can be written as
\circulant(1,0,\ldots,0,1)
. The following are well-known properties of circulant matrices.

Proposition 1.1
.

[
10
]

(a)

Circulant matrices commute under multiplication.

(b)

The inverse of an invertible circulant matrix is a circulant matrix.

(c)

The inverse of an invertible symmetric circulant matrix is a symmetric circulant matrix.

(d)

If
s
is the row sum of an invertible circulant matrix
C
, then
\frac{1}{s}
is the row sum of
C^{-1}
.

The
Moore–Penrose inverse
of an
m\times n
real matrix
A
, denoted by
A^{+}
, is the
n\times m
real matrix that satisfies the following equations
[
5
]
:

AA^{+}A=A,A^{+}AA^{+}=A^{+},(AA^{+})^{T}=AA^{+},(A^{+}A)^{T}=A^{+}A.

When
A
is invertible,
A^{+}=A^{-1}
.

In 1965, Ijira first studied the Moore-Penrose inverse of the oriented incidence matrix of a graph in
[
11
]
. The same was done by Bapat for the Laplacian and edge-Laplacian of trees
[
3
]
. Further research studied the same topic for different graphs such as distance regular graphs
[
1
,
4
]
. With the emergence of research on the signless Laplacian of graphs
[
6
,
7
]
, Hessert and Mallik studied the Moore-Penrose inverses of the incidence matrix and signless Laplacian of a tree and an unicyclic graph in
[
8
,
9
]
. It has been an open problem to find the Moore-Penrose inverses of the incidence, Laplacian, and signless Laplacian matrices of other families of graphs. Note that the inverse formulas for an odd unicyclic graph and an even unicyclic graph are quite different
[
9
]
. Since wheel graphs are formed from odd or even cycles, they deserve to be investigated first for the inverse formulas of associated matrices. Recently an inverse formula for the distance matrix of a wheel graph has been studied by Balaji et al.
[
2
]
. In section 2, we study the Moore–Penrose inverses of the incidence and signless Laplacian matrices of the wheel graph on
n
vertices. In section 3, we investigate the Moore–Penrose inverses of the oriented incidence and Laplacian matrices of the wheel graph on
n
vertices.

2
Incidence and signless Laplacian matrices

The wheel graph on
n\geq 4
vertices, denoted by
W_{n}
, is obtained from an isolated vertex
v
and a cycle on
n-1
vertices by joining each vertex of the cycle to
v
. In this section first we study the Moore–Penrose inverse of the incidence matrix of
W_{n}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
W_{n}
be the wheel graph on
n
vertices with the incidence matrix
M
given by

M=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}^{T}&\bm{0}^{T}\\
\hline\cr I_{n-1}&C\end{array}\right],

where
C
is the circulant matrix
\circulant(1,0,...,0,1)
of order
n-1
. The Moore-Penrose inverse of
M
is given by

M^{+}=\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{r|c}2\bm{1}&X\\
\hline\cr-\bm{1}&Y\end{array}\right],

where
X=2(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}\left[(n-1)I_{n-1}-J_{n-1}\right]
and
Y=J_{n-1}+C^{T}X
.

Proof.

First note that

CC^{T}+I_{n-1}=\circulant(3,1,0,\ldots,0,1)

is strictly diagonally dominant and consequently invertible. Let

H=\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{r|c}2\bm{1}&X\\
\hline\cr-\bm{1}&Y\end{array}\right],

where
X=2(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}\left[(n-1)I_{n-1}-J_{n-1}\right]
and
Y=J_{n-1}+C^{T}X
. We show that
H=M^{+}
.

\displaystyle MH

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}^{T}&\bm{0}^{T}\\
\hline\cr I_{n-1}&C\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{r|c}2\bm{1}&X\\
\hline\cr-\bm{1}&Y\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}2\bm{1}^{T}\bm{1}&\bm{1}^{T}X\\
\hline\cr 2I_{n-1}\bm{1}-C\bm{1}&I_{n-1}X+CY\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}2(n-1)&\bm{1}^{T}X\\
\hline\cr 2\bm{1}-2\bm{1}&X+CY\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}2(n-1)&\bm{1}^{T}X\\
\hline\cr\bm{0}&X+CY\end{array}\right]

(1)

Since the row sum of
CC^{T}+I_{n-1}=\circulant(3,1,0,\ldots,0,1)
is
5
,
\bm{1}^{T}(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}=\frac{1}{5}\bm{1}^{T}
by Proposition
1.1
. Then

\displaystyle\bm{1}^{T}X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\bm{1}^{T}(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}\left[(n-1)I_{n-1}-J_{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{1}{5}\bm{1}^{T}\right)[(n-1)I_{n-1}-J_{n-1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{5}[(n-1)\bm{1}^{T}-(n-1)\bm{1}^{T}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{0}^{T}.

Now we simplify
X+CY
as follows.

\displaystyle X+CY

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X+C(J_{n-1}+C^{T}X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X+CJ_{n-1}+CC^{T}X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I_{n-1}+CC^{T})X+CJ_{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}[(n-1)I_{n-1}-J_{n-1}]+2J_{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(n-1)I_{n-1}-2J_{n-1}+2J_{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(n-1)I_{n-1}

Putting
\bm{1}^{T}X=\bm{0}^{T}
and
X+CY=2(n-1)I_{n-1}
in (
1
), we get

MH=\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}2(n-1)&\bm{0}^{T}\\
\hline\cr\bm{0}&2(n-1)I_{n-1}\end{array}\right]=I_{n}.

Since
MH=I_{n}
, we have
MHM=M
,
HMH=H
, and
(MH)^{T}=MH
. It remains to show that
HM
is symmetric.

\displaystyle HM

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{r|c}2\bm{1}&X\\
\hline\cr-\bm{1}&Y\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}^{T}&\bm{0}\\
\hline\cr I_{n-1}&C\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}2\bm{1}\bm{1}^{T}+X&XC\\
\hline\cr-\bm{1}\bm{1}^{T}+Y&YC\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}2J_{n-1}+X&XC\\
\hline\cr-J_{n-1}+Y&YC\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}2J_{n-1}+X&XC\\
\hline\cr C^{T}X&J_{n-1}C+C^{T}XC\end{array}\right]\;(\text{since }Y=J_{n-1}+C^{T}X)

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}2J_{n-1}+X&XC\\
\hline\cr C^{T}X&2J_{n-1}+C^{T}XC\end{array}\right]\;(\text{since }J_{n-1}C=2J_{n-1})

To show
HM
is symmetric, it suffices to show that
X
is symmetric. Note that
CC^{T}+I_{n-1}
is a symmetric circulant matrix and so is
(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}
by Proposition
1.1
. Also
(n-1)I_{n-1}-J_{n-1}
is a symmetric circulant matrix. Then so is

X=2(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}\left[(n-1)I_{n-1}-J_{n-1}\right]

as a product of two symmetric circulant matrices.

Thus
H=M^{+}
.
∎

.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
W_{n}
be the wheel graph on
n
vertices with the signless Laplacian matrix
Q
given by

Q=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}n-1&\bm{1}^{T}\\
\hline\cr\bm{1}&B\end{array}\right],

where
B
is the circulant matrix
\circulant(3,1,0,...,0,1)
of order
n-1
. The Moore-Penrose inverse of

is given by

Q^{+}=\frac{1}{4(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{r|c}5&-\bm{1}^{T}\\
\hline\cr-\bm{1}&J_{n-1}+2X\end{array}\right],

where
X=2(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}\left[(n-1)I_{n-1}-J_{n-1}\right]=\circulant(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1})
with

b_{j}=-\frac{2}{5}+\frac{2^{n-j}(n-1)}{\sqrt{5}}\left[\frac{(-3+\sqrt{5})^{j}}{2^{n-1}-(-3+\sqrt{5})^{n-1}}-\frac{(-3-\sqrt{5})^{j}}{2^{n-1}-(-3-\sqrt{5})^{n-1}}\right].

Proof.

First note that
Q=MM^{T}
for the incidence matrix
M
of the form

M=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}^{T}&\bm{0}^{T}\\
\hline\cr I_{n-1}&C\end{array}\right],

where
C
is the circulant matrix
\circulant(1,0,...,0,1)
of order
n-1
. By Theorem
2.1
,

M^{+}=\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{r|c}2\bm{1}&X\\
\hline\cr-\bm{1}&Y\end{array}\right],

where
X=2(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}\left[(n-1)I_{n-1}-J_{n-1}\right]
and
Y=J_{n-1}+C^{T}X

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
W_{n}
be the wheel graph on

and

.

Proof.

First note that

CC^{T}+I_{n-1}=\circulant(3,-1,0,\ldots,0,-1)

is strictly diagonally dominant and consequently invertible. Let

H=\frac{1}{n}\left[\begin{array}[]{r|c}\bm{1}&X\\
\hline\cr\bm{0}&Y\end{array}\right],

and

. We show that

.

\displaystyle NH

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{n}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}^{T}&\bm{0}^{T}\\

\hline\cr\bm{0}&Y\end{array}\right]

\hline\cr-\bm{1}&-X+CY\end{array}\right]

(3)

Since the row sum of

is

,

by Proposition
1.1
. Then

\displaystyle\bm{1}^{T}X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{1}^{T}(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}(J_{n-1}-nI_{n-1})

\displaystyle\bm{1}^{T}(J_{n-1}-nI_{n-1})

\displaystyle(n-1)\bm{1}^{T}-n\bm{1}^{T}

\displaystyle-\bm{1}^{T}.

\displaystyle CY-X

\displaystyle C(-C^{T}X)-X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-CC^{T}X-X

\displaystyle-(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})X

\displaystyle-(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}(J_{n-1}-nI_{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(J_{n-1}-nI_{n-1})

\displaystyle nI_{n-1}-J_{n-1}.

\displaystyle\left(I_{n}-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\right)N

\displaystyle N-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\;\;(\text{since the column sum of N is 0})

We also show HNH=H.

\displaystyle HNH

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\left(I_{n}-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H-\frac{1}{n}HJ_{n}

, we show that

. Note that

\displaystyle HJ_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}&X\\

\displaystyle O

\displaystyle YJ_{n-1}

\displaystyle-C^{T}XJ_{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-C^{T}(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}(J_{n-1}-nI_{n-1})J_{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\;\;(\text{since the row sum of $C^{T}$ is $0$})

\displaystyle HN

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}&X\\

1.1

\hline\cr-I_{n-1}&C\end{array}\right],

where
C
is the circulant matrix
\circulant(1,0,...,0,-1)
of order
n-1
. By Theorem
3.1
,

N^{+}=\frac{1}{n}\left[\begin{array}[]{r|c}\bm{1}&X\\
\hline\cr\bm{0}&Y\end{array}\right],

where
X=2(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}\left[J_{n-1}-nI_{n-1}\right]
and
Y=-C^{T}X
.

\displaystyle L^{+}

\displaystyle=(N^{+})^{T}N^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}^{T}&\bm{0}^{T}\\
\hline\cr X^{T}&Y^{T}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}&X\\
\hline\cr\bm{0}&Y\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}^{T}&\bm{0}^{T}\\
\hline\cr X&Y^{T}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}&X\\
\hline\cr\bm{0}&Y\end{array}\right]\;\;(\text{since $X$ is symmetric})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{1}^{T}\bm{1}&\bm{1}^{T}X\\
\hline\cr X\bm{1}&XX+Y^{T}Y\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}n-1&-\bm{1}^{T}\\
\hline\cr-\bm{1}&XX+Y^{T}Y\end{array}\right]\;\;(\text{since $\bm{1}^{T}X=-\bm{1}^{T}$ and $X\bm{1}=-\bm{1}$})

(4)

Now we simplify
XX+Y^{T}Y
as follows.

\displaystyle XX+Y^{T}Y

\displaystyle=XX-XC(-C^{T}X)

\displaystyle=XI_{n-1}X+XCC^{T}X

\displaystyle=X(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})X

\displaystyle=X(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})(CC^{T}+I_{n-1})^{-1}(J_{n-1}-nI_{n-1})

\displaystyle=X(J_{n-1}-nI_{n-1})

\displaystyle=-J_{n-1}-nX

Plugging
XX+Y^{T}Y=-J_{n-1}-nX
in (
4
), we get

L^{+}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}n-1&-\bm{1}^{T}\\
\hline\cr-\bm{1}&-J_{n-1}-nX\end{array}\right],

where
X
is given by Corollary
3.4
.
∎

Multi-Dimensional Super-Linear Backward Stochastic Volterra Integral Equations
Shengjun Fan Tianxiao Wang, Jiongmin Yong

Abstract
In this paper, a systematic investigation is carried out for the general solvability of multi-dimensional backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (BSVIEs) with the generators being super-linear in the adjustment variable Z Z . Two major situations are discussed: (i) When the free term is bounded with the dependence of the generator on Z Z being of “diagonally strictly” quadratic growth and being sub-quadratically coupled with off-diagonal components; (ii) When the free term is unbounded having exponential moments of arbitrary order with the dependence of the generator on Z Z being diagonally no more than quadratic and being independent of off-diagonal components. Besides, for the case that the generator is super-quadratic in Z Z , some negative results are presented.



Keywords.
Backward stochastic Volterra integral equations, multi-dimensional systems, unbounded free terms, diagonally quadratic generator, sub-quadratic growth.

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered complete probability space on which a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion
W(\cdot)
is defined with
\mathbb{F}=\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
being its natural filtration augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets. Consider the following
backward stochastic Volterra integral equation
(BSVIE, for short):

Y(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,Y(s),Z(t,s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\ \ t\in[0,T],

(1.1)

where
g:\Delta[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, satisfying proper conditions, is called the
generator
of BSVIE (
1.1
), with
\Delta^{*}[0,T]=\{(t,s)\in[0,T]^{2}\bigm|0\leqslant t<s\leqslant T\}
being the upper triangle domain within the square
[0,T]^{2}
, and
\psi(\cdot)
is called a
free term
. The unknown is a pair
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
of processes having certain properties, called an
adapted solution
. For convenience, we refer to
Y(\cdot)
as the
backward term
and refer to
Z(\cdot\,,\cdot)
as the
adjustment term
(since such a term makes it possible to have adaptiveness of
Y(\cdot)
). When
g(t,s,y,z)\equiv g(s,y,z)
and
\psi(t)\equiv\xi
are independent of
t

1.1
) is reduced to the following
backward stochastic differential equation
(BSDE, for short) (of the integral form):

Y(t)=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}g(s,Y(s),Z(s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(s)dW(s),\ \ t\in[0,T].

(1.2)

This is a major motivation of the current paper. Our main results can be briefly summarized as follows. Let us emphasis that all results are for multi-dimensional BSVIEs. Also, although we only consider the case of one-dimensional Brwonian motion, all the results hold for multi-dimensional Brownian motions, just with some modification of notations.

(i) For the case
z\mapsto g(t,s,y,z)
being diagonally strictly quadratic, well-posedness of BSVIEs will be established under the condition that the free term is bounded, allowing off-diagonal components of
z
appear sub-quadratically (see a later section for the precise meaning). Moreover, our results allow the generator to have a mixture of quadratic and linear growth components. Note that in the literature, the results for QBSDEs do not cover those for linear growth BSDEs (
[
24
,
15
]
). Hence, this part of the results are new even for BSDEs.

(ii) For the case
z\mapsto g(t,s,y,z)
being super-linear but sub-quadratic, we will establish the well-posedness with more general free terms than the quadratic BSVIEs. Some componentwise extended convexity/concavity of
z\mapsto g(t,s,y,z)
will be assumed. This part of results are also new for BSDEs because only one-dimensional sub-quadratic BSDEs were studied in the literature (
[
13
]
).

(iii) For the case
z\mapsto g(t,s,y,z)
being super-quadratic,
we will establish some negative results on the well-posedness, either no existence, or no uniqueness. In some sense, these results are extensions of similar ones for BSDEs (
[
8
]
).

Now, let us present another important motivation of the current paper.

A given random variable
\xi\in L^{1}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
can represent the return of some asset position at some future time
T
. A standard
dynamic entropic risk measure
of
\xi
with a
risk aversion parameter

\gamma>0
is defined by

\rho^{1}_{t}(\xi)={1\over\gamma}\log\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[e^{-\gamma\xi}\big],\ \ t\in[0,T],

(1.3)

where
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\,\cdot\,]=\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\,|\,{\cal F}_{t}]
(
[
12
]
). It is known (
[
18
,
17
]
) that the above is a convex risk measure (not coherent), on the set

\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}^{1}=\Big\{\xi\in L^{1}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\bigm|\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[e^{-\gamma\xi}\big]<\infty\Big\}.

Clearly,
L^{\infty}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
is a proper subset of
\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}^{1}
, as the latter also contains some unbounded random variables, besides bounded ones. Let us consider the following BSDE (of integral form)

Y(t)=-\xi+\int_{t}^{T}{\gamma\over 2}|Z(s)|^{2}ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(s)dW(s),\ \ t\in[0,T].

(1.4)

Suppose
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
is the adapted solution of this BSDE.
Then, a straightforward calculation shows that

Y(t)={1\over\gamma}\log\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[e^{-\gamma\xi}\big]=\rho_{t}^{1}(\xi).

This means that the backward term
Y(\cdot)
of the adapted solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
to the BSDE (
1.4
) can be exactly taken as a dynamic entropic risk measure for
\xi
, as long as
\xi\in\mathscr{E}^{1}_{\gamma}
.

Next, we want to extend the above dynamic entropic risk measure to a larger set than
\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}^{1}
. To this end, for any
\alpha>0
, we define

\xi^{\{\alpha\}}=(\xi^{+})^{\alpha}-(\xi^{-})^{\alpha}\equiv\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\xi^{\alpha},\qquad\xi\geqslant 0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle-|\xi|^{\alpha},\ \ \xi<0.\end{array}\right.

This is the odd extension of
\xi^{\alpha}
,
\xi\geqslant 0
to
\mathbb{R}
, symmetric with respect to the origin, which is well-defined on
\mathbb{R}
and is strictly increasing. Now, for
\alpha,\gamma>0
, we introduce

\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}^{\alpha}=\Big\{\xi\in L^{1}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\bigm|\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[e^{-\gamma\xi^{\{\alpha\}}}\big]<\infty\Big\}.

Since

\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[e^{-\gamma\xi^{\{\alpha\}}}\big]=\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[e^{-\gamma\xi^{\alpha}}I_{(\xi\geqslant 0)}\big]+\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[e^{\gamma|\xi|^{\alpha}}I_{(\xi<0)}\big],\quad 0\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[e^{-\gamma\xi^{\alpha}}I_{(\xi\geqslant 0)}\big]\leqslant 1,

we see that

\mathscr{E}^{\alpha}_{\gamma}=\Big\{\xi\in L^{1}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\bigm|\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[e^{\gamma|\xi|^{\alpha}}I_{(\xi<0)}\big]<\infty\Big\}.

Clearly, both
\alpha\mapsto\mathscr{E}^{\alpha}_{\gamma}
and
\gamma\mapsto\mathscr{E}^{\alpha}_{\gamma}
are (strictly) decreasing, in particular,

\mathscr{E}^{1}_{\gamma}\varsubsetneq\mathscr{E}^{\alpha}_{\gamma},\ \ \alpha\in(0,1).

Now, for the return
\xi\in\mathscr{E}^{\alpha}_{\gamma}\setminus\mathscr{E}^{1}_{\gamma}
, inspired by (
1.3
), we may try to define an extended dynamic entropic risk measure of such a
\xi
by the following:

\rho_{t}^{\alpha}(\xi)=\Big({1\over\gamma}\log\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[e^{-\gamma\xi^{\{\alpha\}}}\big]\Big)^{\{{1\over\alpha}\}},\ \ t\in[0,T].

(1.5)

However, the above only satisfies the monotonicity, and it does not even have the translation invariance. Hence, this is not a good dynamic risk measure. To have a reasonable entropic type dynamic risk measure for
\xi\in\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}^{\alpha}
, suggested by the case of
\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}^{1}
, we consider the following BSDE:

Y(t)=-\xi+\int_{t}^{T}{\gamma\over\kappa}|Z(s)|^{2\over 2-\alpha}ds+\int_{t}^{T}Z(s)dW(s),\ \ t\in[0,T],

(1.6)

for some
\kappa>1
, whose generator has a sub-quadratic growth in
Z
(since
{2\over 2-\alpha}\in(1,2)
). According to Fan–Hu
[
13
]
, for any
\xi\in\mathscr{E}^{\alpha}_{\gamma}
, the above BSDE admits a unique adapted solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
satisfying

e^{\frac{\gamma}{\kappa}|Y(t)|^{\alpha}}I_{(Y(t)>0)}\leqslant K\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[e^{\gamma|\xi|^{\alpha}}I_{(\xi<0)}\Big]

(1.7)

for some
K>1
. In the current case, the map
\xi\mapsto Y(t)\equiv Y(t;\xi)
satisfies the monotonicity, translation invariance, and convex. Hence, we may take such a map as an extended entropic dynamic risk measure on the set
\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}^{\alpha}
.
This leads to the study of BSDEs with the generators being sub-quadratic in
Z
.

Next, according to Yong
[
47
]
, Wang–Sun–Yong
[
37
]
, in the one-dimensional case, if instead of the return
\xi
at the terminal time
T
, one has an
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable position process
\psi(\cdot)
(not necessarily
\mathbb{F}
-adapted), to dynamically measure the risk, it will be more proper to consider the following BSVIE:

Y(t)=-\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,Z(t,s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\ \ t\in[0,T],

(1.8)

for a proper generator
g
, where
z\mapsto g(t,s,z)
could be of linear, super-linear (which includes sub-quadratic, quadratic, or even super-quadratic). If
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
is an adapted solution of BSVIE (
1.8
), we can call
Y(\cdot)
an
equilibrium dynamical (entropic) risk measure
of
\psi(\cdot)
. In the case that
\psi(\cdot)
is bounded, and
z\mapsto g(t,s,z)
is quadratic, (the one-dimensional BSVIE) (
1.8
) admits a unique adapted solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
so that we have an
equilibrium entropic dynamical risk measure
for
\psi(\cdot)
(
[
37
]
).

It is common that there are more than one position processes that have to be measured their dynamic risks individually. For example, for a (commercial and/or investment) bank, there might be some loan positions which have quite different features. They are closely related (coupled) and the dynamical risks should be measured separately. Therefore, we naturally have the vector-valued (equilibrium) dynamical risk measure process which satisfies a multi-dimensional BSDEs or BSVIEs. This consideration gives us a motivation of studying multi-dimensional BSVIEs with the generator
g
having various types of growth in
z
. From this, people might see the application potential contained in the main results of the current paper.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Some preliminary is presented in Section 2, including the revisit of relevant BSDE results and some crucial lemmas to be used later. Section 3 is devoted to the case of bounded free terms for diagonally strictly quadratic case and the mixed linear and quadratic case. In Section 4, we discuss BSVIEs with unbounded free for the generators having no more than quadratic growth. Some negative results are presented in Section 5 when the generator is super-quadratic in
Z
. Finally, some concluding remarks are collected in Section 6.

Preliminaries

2.1
Some spaces

Let us first introduce some basic spaces. For each
t\in[0,T]
and any Euclidean space
\mathbb{H}
with the norm
|\cdot|
which could be
\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{p}

\mathbb{H}
is clear from the context and is not necessarily to be emphasized, we will omit
\mathbb{H}
. In particular, we will denote

,

,

, and

, the last two equalities in the above might not be true without certain type of measurability (see Example 5.0.10 in

]
and Example 1.42 in

:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{q}(\Delta^{*}[t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))=\Big\{\zeta:\Delta^{*}[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigm|\forall r\in[t,T],\,\zeta(r,\cdot)\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{q}(r,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(r,s)|^{q}ds\Big)^{p\over q}dr<\infty\Big\}.\end{array}

Also, we let

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{q}(\cdot\,,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})))=\Big\{\varphi:\Delta^{*}[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigm|\forall t\in[0,T],\,\varphi(t,\cdot)\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{q}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|\varphi(t,s)|^{q}ds\Big)^{p\over q}<\infty\Big\}.\end{array}

The case that
p
and/or
q
is equal to
\infty
can be defined in an obvious way. Further, for each
t\in[0,T]
, denote by
\mathscr{T}[t,T]
the set of all
\mathbb{F}
-stopping times
\tau
valued in
[t,T]
. A uniformly integrable
\mathbb{F}
-martingale
M=\{M(t):0\leqslant t\leqslant T\}
with
M_{0}=0
is called a
BMO martingale
on
[0,T]
if

\|M(\cdot)\|^{2}_{{\rm BMO}[0,T]}\triangleq\sup_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[0,T]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\big[|M(T)-M(\tau)|^{2}\big]\Big\|_{\infty}<\infty.

It is known (see
[
25
]
) that for any given
K>0
, there are constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
depending only on
K
such that for any BMO martingale
N(\cdot)
with

\big\|N(\cdot)\big\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[0,T]}\leqslant K,

by setting

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}:=\exp\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}N(t)dW(t)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|N(t)|^{2}dt\Big\}d\mathbb{P},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widetilde{M}(\cdot):=M(\cdot)-\langle M,N\rangle(\cdot),\end{array}

one has that
\widetilde{M}(\cdot)
is a BMO martingale under
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
, and

c_{1}\big\|M(\cdot)\big\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[0,T]}\leqslant\big\|\widetilde{M}(\cdot)\big\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}[0,T]}\leqslant c_{2}\big\|M(\cdot)\big\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[0,T]}.

(2.3)

This means that the BMO norm of the BMO martingale
M(\cdot)
under
\mathbb{P}
is equivalent to that of BMO martingale
\widetilde{M}(\cdot)
under
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
. We now introduce

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\overline{{\rm BMO}}([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})\triangleq\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigm|\varphi(\cdot)\hbox{ is $\mathbb{F}$--progressively measurable and}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\big\|\varphi(\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}=\sup_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T}|\varphi(s)|^{2}ds\Big]\Big\|_{\infty}<\infty\Big\},\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\overline{{\rm BMO}}(\Delta^{*}[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})\triangleq\Big\{\varphi:\Delta^{*}[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigm|\varphi(t,\cdot)\hbox{ is $\mathbb{F}$--progressively measurable}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\hbox{on $[t,T]$, $\hbox{\rm a.s.}~t\in[0,T]$, and}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\big\|\varphi(\cdot\,,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}(\Delta^{*}[0,T])}\negthinspace\negthinspace=\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\big\|\varphi(t,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}\negthinspace<\negthinspace\infty\Big\}.\end{array}

Clearly,

\overline{\rm BMO}([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})\supseteq L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})).

(2.4)

On the other hand, for good enough process
\varphi(\cdot\,,\cdot)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\cdot\,,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})))}^{2}=\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|\varphi(t,s)|^{2}ds=\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|\varphi(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|\varphi(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big]\Big\|_{\infty}\leqslant\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T}|\varphi(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big]\Big\|_{\infty}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\big\|\varphi(\cdot\,,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}(\Delta^{*}[0,T])}.\end{array}

(2.5)

Hence,

\overline{\rm BMO}(\Delta^{*}[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\cdot\,,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}))).

(2.6)

At the moment, it is not clear if the equalities in (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) hold. They will again be related to the question we faced for (
2.1
) with
p=\infty
. Fortunately it is irrelevant whether there are such equalities.

In what follows, for simplicity of the presentation, we will misuse the same notation for different range spaces which can be easily identified from the contexts.

Definition 2.1
.

A pair
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\Delta^{*}[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is called an
adapted solution
of BSVIE (
1.1
) if the equation is satisfied in the usual Itô’s sense.

Note that although in the above definition, we only require
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
to be in a very large space, in our main results, under proper conditions, our adapted solutions will have much better regularity/integrability.

2.2
Results for BSDEs revisited

In this subsection, we recall some relevant results for BSDEs.
Consider the following BSDE of integral form (which could be one-dimensional or multi-dimensional):

Y(t)=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y(s),Z(s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(s)dW(s),\ \ t\in[0,T].

(2.7)

We look at the following classes of problems.

(i) Quadratic BSDEs with bounded terminal terms.

The main feature of such a BSDE is that
z\mapsto f(t,y,z)
has a quadratic growth. Because of this, such an equation is called a
quadratic
BSDE (QBSDE, for short). We introduce the following hypothesis.

(H2.1)
The map
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is measurable such that
t\mapsto f(t,y,z)
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable, for all
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. There exists a non-negative valued process
\alpha(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}))
, a continuous, monotone increasing function
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\phi(0)=0
, and constants
\beta,\lambda\geqslant 0
,
\gamma\geqslant\bar{\gamma}>0
,
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\hbox{\rm sgn$\,$}(y^{i})f^{i}(t,y,z)\leqslant\alpha(t)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\beta|y|\negthinspace+\negthinspace\lambda\sum_{j\neq i}|z^{j}|^{1+\delta}\negthinspace+\negthinspace{\gamma\over 2}|z^{i}|^{2},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant n,\end{array}

(2.8)

and either

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle-\alpha(t)\negthinspace-\negthinspace\beta|y|\negthinspace-\negthinspace\lambda\sum_{j\neq i}|z^{j}|^{1+\delta}\negthinspace+\negthinspace{\bar{\gamma}\over 2}|z^{i}|^{2}\leqslant f^{i}(t,y,z),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant n,\end{array}

(2.9)

or

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\alpha(t)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\beta|y|\negthinspace+\negthinspace\lambda\sum_{j\neq i}|z^{j}|^{1+\delta}\negthinspace-\negthinspace{\bar{\gamma}\over 2}|z^{i}|^{2}\geqslant f^{i}(t,y,z),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant n.\end{array}

(2.10)

Further, it holds that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle|f^{i}(t,y,z)-f^{i}(t,\bar{y},\bar{z})|\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\leqslant\phi(|y|\vee|\bar{y}|)\Big[\big(1+|z|+|\bar{z}|\big)\big(|y-\bar{y}|+|z^{i}-\bar{z}^{i}|\big)+\big(1+|z|^{\delta}+|\bar{z}|^{\delta}\big)\sum_{j\neq i}|z^{j}-\bar{z}^{j}|\Big],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],~y,\bar{y},z,\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},~i=1,2,\cdots,n.\end{array}

(2.11)

Note that in the whole paper, we use the notation
\hbox{\rm sgn$\,$}(x):=I_{(x>0)}-I_{(x\leqslant 0)}
. Condition (
2.9
)–(
2.10
) are referred to as the
diagonally strictly quadratic
condition for the generator
f
in
z
. This condition excludes the case that
z^{i}\mapsto f^{i}(t,y,z)
is of linear growth. The following is Theorem 2.5 of Fan–Hu–Tang
[
15
]
which gives the existence and uniqueness of adapted solutions to QBSDEs with bounded terminal terms.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(H2.1)
hold. Then for any
\xi\in L^{\infty}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, BSDE (
2.7
) admits a unique adapted solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))\times\overline{\rm BMO}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

It is very natural to ask what happens if the terminal state is unbounded for QBSDEs. This leads to the second situation as follows.

(ii) QBSDEs with unbounded terminal terms.

For such a situation, to get a positive result, the following hypothesis has been assumed in the literature.

(H2.2)
Let the measurability condition in (H2.1) hold for
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each
i=1,2,\cdots,n
,
f^{i}
only depends on
(t,y,z^{i},\omega)
(independent of
z^{j}
,
j\neq i
) and
z^{i}\mapsto f^{i}(t,y,z^{i})
is convex for all
(t,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. There exists some
\alpha(\cdot)\in\cap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}^{\mu,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}))

and constants
\beta,\gamma>0
, such that

|f^{i}(t,y,z^{i})|\leqslant\alpha(t)+\beta|y|+{\gamma\over 2}|z^{i}|^{2},\quad\forall(t,y,z^{i})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

(2.12)

and

|f(t,y,z)-f(t,\bar{y},z)|\leqslant\beta|y-\bar{y}|,\quad\forall t\in[0,T],~y,\bar{y},z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\vskip 5.69046pt

(2.13)

The following is Theorem 2.9 of Fan–Hu–Tang
[
15
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(H2.2)
hold. Then, for any
\xi\in\bigcap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}_{{\cal F}_{T}}^{\mu,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, BSDE (
2.7
) admits a unique adapted solution

(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))\in\Big[\bigcap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}^{\mu,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C(0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))\Big]\times\Big[\bigcap_{p\geqslant 1}L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}))\Big].

By (
2.2
), we see that the terminal term in the above could be unbounded. However, the conditions assumed for the generator
f
in (H2.2) is much stronger than that assumed in (H2.1), especially
f^{i}
has to be independent of
z^{j}
,
j\neq i
, although the condition on
\alpha(\cdot)
in (H2.2) is a little weaker. Also, due to the assumption that
z\mapsto f(t,y,z)
is diagonally dependent, the diagonally strictly quadratic growth condition (which is used to take care the sub-quadratic growth of off-diagonal component) is not necessary.

Next, if
z\mapsto f(t,y,z)
is growing superlinearly but subquadratically, and diagonally dependent, then Proposition
2.3
should still be true. It is expected that we might be able to relax the condition on the terminal term. This will lead to the third situation.

(iii) Sub-quadratic BSDEs with unbounded terminal term.

For such a situation, there were only some results for one-dimensional BSDEs, under the following hypothesis:

(H2.3)
Let the measurability condition in (H2.1) hold for
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. There exist some constants
\delta\in(0,1)
,
\beta\geqslant 0
,
\gamma>0
, and
\alpha(\cdot)\in\bigcap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}^{\mu,{2\delta\over 1+\delta}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}))
such that

|f(t,y,z)|\leqslant\alpha(t)+\beta|y|+\gamma|z|^{1+\delta},\quad\forall(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

(2.14)

Moreover,
(y,z)\mapsto f(t,y,z)
is convex for all
t\in[0,T]
.

The following is from Theorem 3.9 in Fan-Hu
[
13
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(H2.3)
hold. Then for any
\xi\in\bigcap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}_{{\cal F}_{T}}^{\mu,{2\delta\over 1+\delta}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, BSDE (
2.7
) admits a unique adapted solution

(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))\in\Big[\bigcap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}^{\mu,{2\delta\over 1+\delta}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}))\Big]\times\Big[\bigcap_{p\geqslant 1}L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}))\Big].

Since for
\delta\in(0,1)
, one has
{2\delta\over 1+\delta}<1
. Consequently,

{\cal E}^{\mu,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}))\subseteq{\cal E}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mu,{2\delta\over 1+\delta}}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R})).

This implies that the condition on
\alpha(\cdot)
assumed in (H2.3) is weaker than that in (H2.2). Also,

{\cal E}^{\mu,1}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\subseteq{\cal E}_{{\cal F}_{T}}^{\mu,{2\delta\over 1+\delta}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}).

Hence, the condition assumed for
\xi
in Proposition
2.4
is weaker than that in Proposition
2.3
. The trade-off is that Proposition
2.4
is only for one-dimensional BSDEs.

Furthermore, we note that for one-dimensional BSDEs with the dependence of the generator on
Z
being no more than quadratic, in Fan-Hu-Tang
[
14
]
and Fan-Hu
[
13
]
, the authors also proved a general existence and uniqueness result under the following hypothesis:

(H2.4)
Let the measurability condition in (H2.1) hold for
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. There exist some constants
\beta\geqslant 0
,
\gamma>0
,
\delta\in(0,1]
, and
\alpha(\cdot)\in\cap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}^{\mu,{2\delta\over 1+\delta}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}))
such that

|f(t,y,z)|\leqslant\alpha(t)+\beta|y|+\gamma|z|^{1+\delta},\quad\forall(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},

(2.15)

|f(t,y,z)-f(t,\bar{y},z)|\leqslant\beta|y-\bar{y}|,\quad\forall(t,y,\bar{y},z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}

(2.16)

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle f(t,y,(1-\theta)z+\theta\bar{z})-\theta f(t,y,\bar{z})\leqslant(1-\theta)\big(\alpha(t)+\beta|y|+\gamma|z|^{1+\delta}\big),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall(t,y,z,\bar{z})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},~\theta\in(0,1).\end{array}

(2.17)

Moreover,
z\mapsto f(t,y,z)
is continuous for all
(t,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
.

Observe that if (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) hold, then for each
(t,y_{1},y_{2},z_{1},z_{2})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
and each
\theta\in(0,1)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle g(t,y_{1},z_{1})-\theta g(t,y_{2},z_{2})\leqslant|g(t,y_{1},z_{1})-g(t,y_{2},z_{1})|+g(t,y_{2},(1-\theta)\hat{z}_{\theta}+\theta z_{2})-\theta g(t,y_{2},z_{2})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\hskip 106.69783pt\leqslant\beta|y_{1}-y_{2}|+(1-\theta)(\alpha(t)+\beta|y_{2}|+\gamma|\hat{z}_{\theta}|^{1+\delta})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\hskip 106.69783pt\leqslant(1-\theta)(\beta|\hat{y}_{\theta}|+\beta|y_{2}|+\alpha(t)+\beta|y_{2}|+\gamma|\hat{z}_{\theta}|^{1+\delta})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\hskip 106.69783pt=(1-\theta)(\alpha(t)+2\beta|y_{2}|+\beta|\hat{y}_{\theta}|+\gamma|\hat{z}_{\theta}|^{1+\delta}),\end{array}

where

\hat{y}_{\theta}:=\frac{y_{1}-\theta y_{2}}{1-\theta}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\hat{z}_{\theta}:=\frac{z_{1}-\theta z_{2}}{1-\theta}.\vskip 2.84544pt

Then, by virtue of (i) of Theorem 5 in Fan-Hu-Tang
[
14
]
for the case
\delta=1
and Theorem 3.9 in Fan-Hu
[
13
]
for the case
\delta\in(0,1)
, the following proposition follows immediately.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(H2.4)
hold. Then for any
\xi\in\bigcap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}_{{\cal F}_{T}}^{\mu,{2\delta\over 1+\delta}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, BSDE (
2.7
) admits a unique adapted solution

(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))\in\Big[\bigcap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}^{\mu,{2\delta\over 1+\delta}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}))\Big]\times\Big[\bigcap_{p\geqslant 1}L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}))\Big].\vskip 2.84544pt

Remark 2.6
.

If the generator
g
satisfies assumption (H2.4) with (
2.17
) replaced with

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle f(t,y,(1-\theta)z+\theta\bar{z})-\theta f(t,y,\bar{z})\geqslant-(1-\theta)\big(\alpha(t)+\beta|y|+\gamma|z|^{1+\delta}\big),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall(t,y,z,\bar{z})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},~\theta\in(0,1),\end{array}

(2.18)

then it is easy to verify that
\bar{g}
must satisfy assumption (H2.4), where

\bar{g}(t,y,z):=-g(t,-y,-z),\quad\forall(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

Consequently, when (
2.17
) in (H2.4) is replaced with (
2.18
), the conclusion of Proposition
2.5
still holds.

Furthermore, it is well known that a convex function with linear growth must be a Lipschitz function. And, it is not hard to check that if
z\mapsto f(t,y,z)
is convex for all
(t,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
and (
2.15
) holds, then (
2.17
) is also true. Then, (H2.4) is weaker than (H2.3) and (H2.2) in the one-dimensional setting. Hence, Proposition
2.5
generalizes Proposition
2.4
and Proposition
2.3
for the one-dimensional case.

In addition, in this paper we will show that (
2.17
) (also (
2.18
)) can imply the continuity of
g
with respect to
z
in (H2.4), see Section 4 for details.

In what follows, we will substantially extend the above results to BSVIEs, by which we mean that some of the extensions are new even for BSDEs.

2.3
A crucial lemma

We now present a lemma which has played a crucial rule in proving the above results, and it will play some important role below when we discuss the situation of unbounded free terms.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
\xi
is an
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable random variable and random field
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is measurable such that for each
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t\mapsto f(t,y,z)
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable.
Let

(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))

be an adapted solution of BSDE (
2.7
)
such that

Y(\cdot)\in\bigcap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}^{\mu,\frac{2\delta}{1+\delta}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and

\hbox{\rm sgn$\,$}\big(Y^{i}(s)\big)f^{i}(s,Y(s),Z(s))\leqslant\alpha_{0}(s)+\beta|Z^{i}(s)|^{1+\delta},\quad s\in[0,T],~1\leqslant i\leqslant n,

(2.19)

for some constants
\beta>0
,
\delta\in(0,1]
, and some map

\alpha_{0}(\cdot)\in\bigcap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}^{\mu,\frac{2\delta}{1+\delta}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+}))
.
Then there exists a constant
K>0
depending only on
(\delta,\beta,T)
such that for each
i=1,\cdots,n
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\bar{z}\equiv(\bar{z}^{1},\cdots,\bar{z}^{k},\bar{z}^{k+1},\cdots,\bar{z}^{n})=(z^{1},\cdots,z^{k},-z^{k+1},\cdots,-z^{n}),\\

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{g}^{i}(t,s,y,z)=g^{i}(t,s,y,z),\qquad~~1\leqslant i\leqslant k,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{g}^{i}(t,s,y,z)=-g^{i}(t,s,\bar{y},\bar{z}),\qquad k+1\leqslant i\leqslant n.\end{array}

Then (H3.1) holds for
\bar{g}(t,s,y,z)
. Thus, (H3.1) and (H3.1)
′
are equivalent in such a sense. In what follows, we will use (H3.1) instead of (H3.1)
′
.

Next, condition (
3.2
) means that the
i
-th component
g^{i}
of the generator
g
has exactly quadratic growth in the
i
-th component
z^{i}
of
z
, and has sub-quadratical growth in the other components of
z
. Such a condition is referred to as the
diagonally strictly quadratic
condition of
z\mapsto g(t,s,y,z)
, which was introduced for BSDEs the first time in
[
14
]
. Roughly speaking, (
3.2
) amounts to saying that

0<\gamma\leqslant\mathop{\underline{\rm lim}}_{|z^{i}|\to\infty}{g^{i}(t,s,y,z)\over|z^{i}|^{2}}\leqslant\mathop{\overline{\rm lim}}_{|z^{i}|\to\infty}{g^{i}(t,s,y,z)\over|z^{i}|^{2}}\leqslant\beta.

Hypothesis (H3.1) is satisfied by many examples. Here is a typical one:

g^{i}(t,s,y,z)=\sqrt{(|y|+1)(|z|+1)}+|z|\cos|z^{i}|-|z|^{1+\delta}+|z^{i}|^{2},\ \ i=1,\cdots,n.

To establish the existence and uniqueness of adapted solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))

.

(3.11)

Let

admits a unique solution

(3.15)

It then follows from (

\widetilde{\eta}(t,r)=\psi(t)+\int_{r}^{T}g(t,s,y(s),\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s))ds-\int_{r}^{T}\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[t,T],

Combining (

Consequently, BSVIE (

) admits a unique adapted solution

(\Delta^{*}[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We now turn to the proof of the second part of Proposition
3.1
. First of all, for any given
k\geqslant 1
, by Young’s inequality that

kn^{1+\delta}|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}\leqslant{\frac{1}{\widetilde{M}}}|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}+K,\ \ s\in[t,T].

(3.17)

where
K
is a constant depending on
\widetilde{M}
,
\delta
and
k
, and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widetilde{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}2\sup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\mathop{\rm esssup}_{\tau}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big\|_{\infty}<\infty,\end{array}

Define process
\widetilde{\zeta}^{i}(t,\cdot)
as
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\widetilde{M}}}\zeta^{i}(t,\cdot)
on
[t,T]
, we see that

\big\|\widetilde{\zeta}^{i}(t,\cdot)\big\|_{\overline{\rm BMO}[t,T]}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.
By John-Nirenberg inequality, we see that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{1}{\widetilde{M}}\int_{t}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\leqslant 2.\end{array}

Therefore, for any
k\geqslant 1
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\exp\Big(k\int_{t}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}ds\Big)\Big]\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\exp\Big(kn^{\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{t}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{1+\delta}ds\Big)\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\ \ =\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp\Big(kn^{\delta}\int_{t}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{1+\delta}ds\Big)\Big]\leqslant\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\exp\Big(kn^{1+\delta}\int_{t}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{1+\delta}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\ \ \leqslant\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\frac{|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}}{\widetilde{M}}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{K}\leqslant 2e^{K}<\infty.\end{array}

This together with the integrability of
\alpha(t,\cdot)
,
y(\cdot)
indicates that

\alpha_{0}(t,\cdot)\in\bigcap_{\mu\geqslant 1}{\cal E}^{\mu,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(t,T;\mathbb{R}_{+}))
, where

\alpha_{0}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\alpha(t,s)+\beta\big(|y(s)|+|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}\big),\vskip 2.84544pt

Taking into account (H3.1), together with BSDE (
3.10
), by virtue of Lemma
2.7
(or
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
),
we have for each
i=1,2,\cdots,n
,
\hbox{\rm a.e.}\;t\in[0,T]
and each
r\in[t,T]
, noting that
\psi(\cdot)
is bounded,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\exp\Big(\gamma|\eta^{i}(t,r)|\Big)+\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big]\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant K\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(K|\psi^{i}(t)|+K\int_{r}^{T}\big[\alpha(t,s)+\beta\big(|y(s)|+|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}\big)\big]ds\Big)\Big]\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant K\exp\Big(K\int_{r}^{T}\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big)\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(K\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}ds\Big)\Big].\end{array}

(3.18)

Note that on the right-hand side, the term
\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}ds
appears in the exponent, which cannot be directly controlled by the term
\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds
. We now make use of the diagonally strictly quadratic condition (
3.2
). Let
i=1,\cdots,n
, and
m\geqslant 1
, For almost every
t\in[0,T]
and each
r\in[t,T]
, we define the following
\mathbb{F}
-stopping time

\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}=\inf\Big\{\tau\in[r,T]\bigm|\int_{r}^{\tau}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\geqslant m\Big\}\land T,

with the convention
\inf\varnothing=+\infty
, and define

X_{m}^{t,r,i}=\eta^{i}(t,r)-\eta^{i}(t,\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i})+\int_{r}^{\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}\big[\alpha(t,s)+\beta\big(|y(s)|+|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}\big)\big]ds.

Then it follows from the first inequality in (
3.2
) and BSDE (
3.10
) that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\gamma\int_{r}^{\sigma^{t,r,i}_{m}}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\leqslant\int_{r}^{\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\big[\alpha(t,s)+\beta\big(|y(s)|+|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}\big)+g^{i}(t,s,y(s),\zeta(t,s))\big]ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\ \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=X_{m}^{t,r,i}+\int_{r}^{\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}\zeta^{i}(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[t,T].\end{array}

(3.19)

This yields that for each
\varepsilon>0
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\exp\Big(\gamma\varepsilon\int_{r}^{\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\leqslant\exp\Big(\varepsilon X_{m}^{t,r,i}\Big)\cdot\exp\Big({3\over 2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{r}^{\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\exp\Big(\varepsilon\int_{r}^{\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}\zeta^{i}(t,s)dW(s)-{3\over 2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{r}^{\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big),\ \ r\in[t,T].\end{array}

(3.20)

Observe that the process

H_{m}^{t,r,i}(\tau):=\exp\Big(3\varepsilon\int_{r}^{\tau\land\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}\zeta^{i}(t,s)dW(s)-{9\over 2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{r}^{\tau\land\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)

is a positive martingale with

. Taking conditional expectation on both sides of (
3.20
) and applying Hölder’s inequality, we obtain that for each
r\in[t,T]
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(\gamma\varepsilon\int_{r}^{\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\negthinspace\leqslant\negthinspace\Big(\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(3\varepsilon X_{m}^{t,r,i}\Big)\Big]\Big)^{1\over 3}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big({9\over 2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{r}^{\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\Big)^{1\over 3}.\end{array}

Consequently, for
\varepsilon\leqslant{2\gamma\over 9}
, we have

\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(\gamma\varepsilon\int_{r}^{\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\}^{2\over 3}\leqslant\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(3\varepsilon X_{m}^{t,r,i}\Big)\Big]\Big\}^{1\over 3},\ \ r\in[t,T].

In view of the definitions of
\sigma_{m}^{t,r,i}
and
X_{m}^{t,r,i}
, sending
m\to\infty
in the above inequality yields that for each
\varepsilon\in(0,{2\gamma\over 9}]
and almost every
t\in[0,T]
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(\gamma\varepsilon\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\}^{2}\leqslant\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(3\varepsilon X_{m}^{t,r,i}\Big)\Big]\\
\ \ \displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big\{\exp\Big[3\varepsilon\Big(\eta^{i}(t,r)-\psi^{i}(t)+\int_{r}^{T}\big[\alpha(t,s)+\beta\big(|y(s)|+|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}\big)\big]ds\Big)\Big]\Big\}\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant K\exp\Big\{3\varepsilon\|\eta(t,r)\|_{\infty}+K\int_{r}^{T}\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big\}\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(3\varepsilon\beta\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}ds\Big)\Big],\ \ r\in[t,T].\end{array}

(3.21)

Thus, it follows from Hölder’s inequality that for almost every
t\in[0,T]
, with
\varepsilon_{0}:={2\gamma\over 9}
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big({\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\over n}\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\}^{2}=\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big({\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\over n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\}^{2}\\
\ \ \displaystyle=\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp\Big({\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\over n}\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\}^{2}\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant\Big\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big({\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\over n}\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]^{n}\Big\}^{1\over n}\Big\}^{2}\\
\ \ \displaystyle=\Big\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\}^{1\over n}\Big\}^{2}\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant\negthinspace K\negthinspace\exp\negthinspace\Big[3\varepsilon_{0}\|\eta(t,r)\|_{\infty}\negthinspace\negthinspace+\negthinspace\negthinspace K\int_{r}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big]\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(3\varepsilon_{0}\beta\negthinspace\int_{r}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\},\ \ r\in[t,T].\end{array}

(3.22)

By Young’s inequality that

3\varepsilon_{0}\beta|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}\leqslant{\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\over n}|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}+K,\ \ s\in[t,T].

(3.23)

Coming back to (
3.22
), by (
3.23
) we deduce that for
\hbox{\rm a.e.}\,t\in[0,T]
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big({\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\over n}\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\}^{2}\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant K\exp\Big(3\varepsilon_{0}\|\eta(t,r)\|_{\infty}\negthinspace\negthinspace+\negthinspace K\int_{r}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big)\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big({\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\over n}\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big],\ \ r\in[t,T],\end{array}

(3.24)

which leads to

\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big({\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\over n}\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\leqslant K\exp\Big(3\varepsilon_{0}\|\eta(t,r)\|_{\infty}+K\int_{r}^{T}\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big),\ \ r\in[t,T].

(3.25)

Therefore, for any
\nu>0

\nu|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}\leqslant{\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\over n^{2}}|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}+K,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(\nu\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}ds\Big)\Big]\leqslant K\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big({\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\over n^{2}}\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant K\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big({\gamma\varepsilon_{0}\over n}\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\}^{1\over n}\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant K\exp\Big[{3\varepsilon_{0}\over n}\|\eta(t,r)\|_{\infty}+K\int_{r}^{T}\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big],\ \ r\in[t,T].\end{array}

(3.26)

Hence, it follows from (
3.18
) and (
3.26
) that for

and
\hbox{\rm a.e.}\,t\in[0,T]

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\exp\Big(\gamma|\eta^{i}(t,r)|\Big)+\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big]\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant K\exp\Big(K\int_{r}^{T}\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big)\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[\exp\Big(K\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta(t,s)|^{1+\delta}ds\Big)\Big]\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant K\exp\Big(K\int_{r}^{T}\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big)\exp\Big[{3\varepsilon_{0}\over n}\|\eta(t,r)\|_{\infty}+K\int_{r}^{T}\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big]\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant K\exp\Big({3\varepsilon_{0}\over n}\|\eta(t,r)\|_{\infty}+K\int_{r}^{T}\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big),\ \ r\in[t,T].\end{array}

(3.27)

Note that
3\varepsilon_{0}={2\gamma\over 3}<\gamma
. The above leads to

\|\eta(t,r)\|_{\infty}\leqslant K_{0}\Big(1+\int_{r}^{T}\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big),\ \ r\in[t,T],

(3.28)

for some absolute constant
K_{0}>0
, independent of
y(\cdot)
, in particular. Also, by enlarging

if necessary, we have that (see (
3.27
))

\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}|\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\leqslant K_{0}\exp\Big(2K_{0}\int_{r}^{T}\|y(s)\|_{\infty}ds\Big),\ \ r\in[t,T].

(3.29)

Now, if

\|y(s)\|_{\infty}\leqslant K_{0}e^{K_{0}(T-s)},\quad\hbox{\rm a.e.}\;s\in[0,T],

then it follows from (

) that for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

\|\eta(t,r)\|_{\infty}\leqslant K_{0}+K_{0}\int_{r}^{T}K_{0}e^{K_{0}(T-s)}ds=K_{0}e^{K_{0}(T-r)},\ \ r\in[t,T].

(3.30)

Then, (
3.8
) follows by letting

in (
3.30
). Furthermore, in view of (
3.29
), one has that for
\hbox{\rm a.e.}\,t\in[0,T]

\big\|\zeta(t,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}\leqslant nK_{0}\exp\Big(2K_{0}e^{K_{0}(T-t)}\Big).

Hence, (
3.9
) holds since

\big\|Z(\cdot\,,\cdot)\big\|_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}(\Delta^{*}[0,T])}=\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\big\|\zeta(t,\cdot)\big\|_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}\leqslant\sqrt{nK_{0}}\exp\Big(K_{0}e^{K_{0}T}\Big).

The proof of Proposition

is then complete.

We are now ready to state and prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(H3.1)
hold. Then for any
\psi(\cdot)\in L_{{\cal F}_{T}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, BSVIE (
1.1
) admits a unique adapted solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\overline{{\rm BMO}}(\Delta^{*}[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.
Let
K_{0},\bar{K}_{0}>0
be the constants in the above proposition. Let

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathscr{B}\triangleq\Big\{(U(\cdot),V(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\overline{{\rm BMO}}(\Delta^{*}[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})\bigm|\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\|U(t)\|_{\infty}\leqslant K_{0}e^{K_{0}(T-t)}\ \ {\rm a.e.}\ t\in[0,T]\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \|V(\cdot\,,\cdot)\|_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}(\Delta^{*}[0,T])}\leqslant\bar{K}_{0}\Big\}\end{array}

which is a convex closed set in the Banach space
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\overline{{\rm BMO}}(\Delta^{*}[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By Proposition
3.1
we know that for each
(y(\cdot),z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in\mathscr{B}
, the following BSVIE

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Y(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,y(s),Z(t,s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\ \ t\in[0,T]\end{array}

(3.31)

admits a unique adapted solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in\mathscr{B}
. That is to say, the map

\Gamma\big(y(\cdot),z(\cdot\,,\cdot)\big)\triangleq\big(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot)\big),\quad\big(y(\cdot),z(\cdot\,,\cdot)\big)\in\mathscr{B}

is well-defined and maps from
\mathscr{B}
to itself. In order to prove the desired result, it suffices to prove that for some
k>0
, the map
\Gamma
is contract in
\mathscr{B}
with the following equivalent norm:

\Big\|\big(U(\cdot),V(\cdot\,,\cdot)\big)\Big\|_{\mathscr{B}_{k}}\triangleq\sqrt{\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\Big\|e^{{kt\over 2}}U(t)\Big\|^{2}_{\infty}+\Big\|e^{{k\cdot\over 2}}V(t,\cdot)\Big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}\Big)}.

(3.32)

Now, for any
(y(\cdot),z(\cdot\,,\cdot)),(\widetilde{y}(\cdot),\widetilde{z}(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in\mathscr{B}
, set

(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))=\Gamma(y(\cdot),z(\cdot\,,\cdot)),\qquad(\widetilde{Y}(\cdot),\widetilde{Z}(\cdot\,,\cdot))=\Gamma(\widetilde{y}(\cdot),\widetilde{z}(\cdot\,,\cdot)).

It follows from Proposition
3.1
that

Y(t)=\eta(t,t),\ Z(t,s)=\zeta(t,s),\ \widetilde{Y}(t)=\widetilde{\eta}(t,t),\ \widetilde{Z}(t,s)=\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s),\ \ (t,s)\in\Delta^{*}[0,T],

(3.33)

where for almost every
t\in[0,T]
,
(\eta(t,\cdot),\zeta(t,\cdot))
and
(\widetilde{\eta}(t,\cdot),\widetilde{\zeta}(t,\cdot))
are respectively the unique solutions of the following BSDEs in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\overline{{\rm BMO}}([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\eta(t,r)=\psi(t)+\int_{r}^{T}g(t,s,y(s),\zeta(t,s))ds-\int_{r}^{T}\zeta(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}(t,r)=\psi(t)+\int_{r}^{T}g(t,s,\widetilde{y}(s),\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s))ds-\int_{r}^{T}\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[t,T].\end{array}\right.

By the definition of
\mathscr{B}
and (
3.33
), for almost every
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\phi(|y(t)|)\leqslant\phi(|y(t)|\vee|\widetilde{y}(t)|)\leqslant\phi\big(K_{0}e^{K_{0}(T-t)}\big)\leqslant\phi\big(K_{0}e^{K_{0}T}\big)

(3.34)

and

\big\|\zeta(t,\cdot)\big\|_{\overline{\rm BMO}[t,T]},\big\|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,\cdot)\big\|_{\overline{\rm BMO}[t,T]}\leqslant\bar{K}_{0}.

(3.35)

The rest of the proof is divided into three steps.

Step 1
.
Girsanov’s transform
.

For
(t,s)\in\Delta^{*}[0,T]
, denote

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\Delta y(s)=y(s)-\widetilde{y}(s),\quad\Delta\eta(t,s)=\eta(t,s)-\widetilde{\eta}(t,s),\quad\Delta\zeta(t,s)=\zeta(t,s)-\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\Delta\eta^{i}(t,s)\negthinspace=\negthinspace\eta^{i}(t,s)\negthinspace-\negthinspace\widetilde{\eta}^{i}(t,s),\quad\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)\negthinspace=\negthinspace\zeta^{i}(t,s)\negthinspace-\negthinspace\widetilde{\zeta}^{i}(t,s),\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant n.\end{array}

Let

\widehat{\zeta}_{i}(t,s)=(\zeta^{1}(t,s),\cdots,\zeta^{i-1}(t,s),\widetilde{\zeta}^{i}(t,s),\zeta^{i+1}(t,s),\cdots,\zeta^{n}(t,s))^{\top},\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant n.

Then

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\zeta(t,s)-\widehat{\zeta}_{i}(t,s)=(0,\cdots,0,\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s),0,\cdots,0)^{\top},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{i}(t,s)-\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)=(\Delta\zeta^{1}(t,s),\cdots,\Delta\zeta^{i-1}(t,s),0,\Delta\zeta^{i+1}(t,s),\cdots,\Delta\zeta^{n}(t,s))^{\top}.\end{array}

Observe that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle g^{i}(t,s,y(s),\zeta(t,s))-g^{i}(t,s,\widetilde{y}(s),\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s))\\
\ \ \displaystyle=g^{i}(t,s,y(s),\zeta(t,s))-g^{i}(t,s,y(s),\widehat{\zeta}_{i}(t,s))+g^{i}(t,s,y(s),\widehat{\zeta}_{i}(t,s))-g^{i}(t,s,\widetilde{y}(s),\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s))\\
\ \ \displaystyle=g^{i}_{z}(t,s)\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)+{\cal G}^{i}(t.s),\end{array}

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle g^{i}_{z}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}{g^{i}(t,s,y(s),\zeta(t,s))-g^{i}(t,s,y(s),\widehat{\zeta}_{i}(t,s))\over\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)}{\bf 1}_{\{\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)\neq 0\}},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle{\cal G}^{i}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}g^{i}(t,s,y(s),\widehat{\zeta}_{i}(t,s))-g^{i}(t,s,\widetilde{y}(s),\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)).\end{array}

By (
3.3
) and (
3.34
), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle|g^{i}_{z}(t,s)|\leqslant\phi\big(K_{0}e^{K_{0}T}\big)\Big(1+|\zeta(t,s)|+|\widehat{\zeta}_{i}(t,s)|\Big),\qquad j\neq i,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle|{\cal G}^{i}(t,s)|\leqslant\phi\big(K_{0}e^{K_{0}T}\big)\Big[\Big(1+|\widehat{\zeta}_{i}(t,s)|+|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)|\Big)|\Delta y(s)|\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j\neq i}\Big(1+|\widehat{\zeta}_{i}(t,s)|^{\delta}+|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)|^{\delta}\Big)|\Delta\zeta^{j}(t,s)|\Big].\end{array}

(3.36)

According to the above notation, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\Delta\eta^{i}(t,r)=\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{r}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\Big(g^{i}_{z}(t,s)\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)+{\cal G}^{i}(t,s)\Big)ds-\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{r}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[t,T].\end{array}

(3.37)

Extend
g^{i}_{z}(t,s)
to be zero for
s\in[0,t)
and let

N_{i}(t,r):=\int_{0}^{r}g^{i}_{z}(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[0,T].

In view of (
3.35
) and (
3.36
), we know that
N_{i}(t,\cdot)
is a BMO martingale under
\mathbb{P}
, and that

\|N_{i}(t,\cdot)\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[0,T]}\leqslant K\Big(1+2\big\|\zeta(t,\cdot)\big\|_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}+\big\|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,\cdot)\big\|_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}\Big)\leqslant K.

(3.38)

Then, by
[
25
, Theorem 2.3]
we know that the stochastic exponential
\mathscr{E}[N_{i}(t,\cdot)](\cdot)
of
N_{i}(t,\cdot)
, given by

\mathscr{E}[N_{i}(t,\cdot)](r)=\exp\Big\{\int_{0}^{r}g^{i}_{z}(t,s)dW(s)-{1\over 2}\int_{0}^{r}|g^{i}_{z}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big\},\ \ r\in[0,T],

is a uniformly integrable martingale under
\mathbb{P}
.

Now, we define the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{t,i}
on
(\Omega,{\cal F}_{T})
by the following:

d\mathbb{P}^{t,i}=\mathscr{E}[N_{i}(t,\cdot)](T)d\mathbb{P}.

Note that

M_{i}(t,r)=\int_{0}^{r}\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[0,T]

is also a BMO martingale under
\mathbb{P}
. It follows from
[
25
, Theorem 3.6]
that the process

\widetilde{W}_{i}(t,r)=W(r)-\int_{0}^{r}g^{i}_{z}(t,s)ds,\ \ r\in[0,T]

(3.39)

is a standard Brownian motion under the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{t,i}
, and that the process

\widetilde{M}_{i}(t,r)=\int_{0}^{r}\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)d\widetilde{W}_{i}(t,s),\ \ r\in[0,T]

the Girsanov’s transform of
M_{i}(t,\cdot)
, is a BMO martingale under the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{t,i}
. Moreover, by (
3.37
) and (
3.39
) we know that for
i=1,\cdots,n
,

\Delta\eta^{i}(t,r)+\int_{r}^{T}\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)d\widetilde{W}_{i}(t,s)=\int_{r}^{T}{\cal G}^{i}(t,s)ds,\ \ r\in[t,T].\vskip 5.69046pt

(3.40)

Step 2
.
Basic estimate.

Fix
i=1,\cdots,n
,
0\leqslant t\leqslant r\leqslant T
and
k>0
. Let
\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}[\xi]
denote the conditional expectation of the random variable
\xi
with respect to
{\cal F}_{r}
under the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{t,i}
. Taking square and
\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}[\cdot]
in both sides of (
3.40
) yields that

|\Delta\eta^{i}(t,r)|^{2}+\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}|\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)=\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big(\Big|\int_{r}^{T}{\cal G}^{i}(t,s)ds\Big|^{2}\Big).

(3.41)

which implies that

e^{kr}|\Delta\eta^{i}(t,r)|^{2}+\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{kr}|\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)=\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big[e^{kr}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}|{\cal G}^{i}(t,s)|ds\Big)^{2}\Big].

(3.42)

Note that

\int_{r}^{T}ke^{ks}\Big(\int_{s}^{T}|\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,u)|^{2}du\Big)ds=\Big(e^{ks}\int_{s}^{T}|\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,u)|^{2}du\Big)\Big|^{T}_{r}+\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds.

As a result,

\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds=\int_{r}^{T}e^{kr}|\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds+k\int_{r}^{T}\Big(\int_{s}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,u)|^{2}du\Big)ds.

(3.43)

Combining (
3.42
) and (
3.43
), we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle e^{kr}|\Delta\eta^{i}(t,r)|^{2}+\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant 2\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big[e^{kr}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}\big|{\cal G}^{i}(t,s)\big|ds\Big)^{2}\Big]+k\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big\{\int_{r}^{T}\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{s}\Big[e^{ks}\Big(\int_{s}^{T}\big|{\cal G}^{i}(t,u)\big|du\Big)^{2}\Big]ds\Big\}\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant 2\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big[e^{kr}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}\big|{\cal G}^{i}(t,s)\big|ds\Big)^{2}\Big]+\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big[\int_{r}^{T}ke^{ks}\Big(\int_{s}^{T}\big|{\cal G}^{i}(t,u)\big|du\Big)^{2}ds\Big].\end{array}

(3.44)

Furthermore, observe by (
3.36
) and Hölder’s inequality that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Big(\int_{r}^{T}|{\cal G}^{i}(t,s)|ds\Big)^{2}\leqslant K\Big\{\int_{r}^{T}\Big[\Big(1+|\zeta(t,s)|+|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)|\Big)|\Delta y(s)|\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j\neq i}\Big(1+|\zeta(t,s)|^{\delta}+|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)|^{\delta}\Big)|\Delta\zeta^{j}(t,s)|\Big]ds\Big\}^{2}\\
\displaystyle\leqslant K\Big\{\Big[\int_{r}^{T}e^{-{k\over 2}s}e^{{k\over 2}s}\Big(1+|\zeta(t,s)|+|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)|\Big)|\Delta y(s)|ds\Big]^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j\neq i}\Big[\int_{r}^{T}e^{-{k\over 2}s}e^{{k\over 2}s}\Big(1+|\zeta(t,s)|^{\delta}+|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)|^{\delta}\Big)|\Delta\zeta^{j}(t,s)|ds\Big]^{2}\Big\}\\
\displaystyle\leqslant K\Big[\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{-ks}ds\Big)\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}\big(1+|\zeta(t,s)|+|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)|\big)^{2}|\Delta y(s)|^{2}ds\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j\neq i}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{-ks}\Big(1+|\zeta(t,s)|^{\delta}+|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)|^{\delta}\Big)^{2}ds\Big)\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta^{j}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\\
\displaystyle\leqslant{Ke^{-kr}\over k}\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}\vartheta(t,s)|\Delta y(s)|^{2}ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+K\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{-{k\over 1-\delta}s}ds\Big)^{1-\delta}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}|\vartheta(t,s)ds\Big)^{\delta}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\\
\displaystyle\leqslant{Ke^{-kr}\over k}\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}\vartheta(t,s)|\Delta y(s)|^{2}ds+{Ke^{-kr}\over k^{1-\delta}}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}\vartheta(t,s)ds\Big)^{\delta}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big),\end{array}

where
\vartheta(t,s):=1+|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}+|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,s)|^{2},\ s\in[t,T]
. Then,

\displaystyle e^{kr}\Big(\int_{r}^{T}\big|{\cal G}^{i}(t,s)\big|ds\Big)^{2}\displaystyle\leqslant{K\over k}\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}\vartheta(t,s)|\Delta y(s)|^{2}ds+{K\over k^{1-\delta}}\Big(1+\int_{r}^{T}\vartheta(t,s)ds\Big)\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds.

(3.45)

Thus, we also have that for each
s\in[t,T]
,

\displaystyle e^{ks\over 2}\Big(\int_{s}^{T}\big|{\cal G}^{i}(t,u)\big|du\Big)^{2}\displaystyle\leqslant{K\over k}\int_{s}^{T}e^{ku\over 2}\vartheta(t,u)|\Delta y(u)|^{2}du+{K\over k^{1-\delta}}\Big(1+\int_{s}^{T}\vartheta(t,u)du\Big)\int_{s}^{T}e^{ku\over 2}|\Delta\zeta(t,u)|^{2}du.

It then follows from the above inequality that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{r}^{T}ke^{ks}\Big(\int_{s}^{T}\big|{\cal G}^{i}(t,u)\big|du\Big)^{2}ds\leqslant K\int_{r}^{T}\Big(e^{ks\over 2}\int_{s}^{T}e^{ku\over 2}\vartheta(t,u)|\Delta y(u)|^{2}du\Big)ds\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+{K\over k^{1-\delta}}\Big(1+\int_{r}^{T}\vartheta(t,s)ds\Big)\int_{r}^{T}\Big(ke^{ks\over 2}\int_{s}^{T}e^{ku\over 2}|\Delta\zeta(t,u)|^{2}du\Big)ds.\\
\ \ \displaystyle={2K\over k}\Big(e^{ks\over 2}\int_{s}^{T}e^{ku\over 2}\vartheta(t,u)|\Delta y(u)|^{2}du\Big|^{T}_{r}+\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}\vartheta(t,s)|\Delta y(s)|^{2}ds\Big)\\
\displaystyle\qquad+{K\over k^{1-\delta}}\Big(1+\int_{r}^{T}\vartheta(t,s)ds\Big)\Big(2e^{ks\over 2}\int_{s}^{T}e^{ku\over 2}|\Delta\zeta(t,u)|^{2}du\Big|^{T}_{r}+2\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant{K\over k}\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}\vartheta(t,s)|\Delta y(s)|^{2}ds+{K\over k^{1-\delta}}\Big(1+\int_{r}^{T}\vartheta(t,s)ds\Big)\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds.\end{array}

(3.46)

Now, we substitute (
3.45
) and (
3.46
) into (
3.44
) to obtain that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle e^{kr}|\Delta\eta^{i}(t,r)|^{2}+\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big[\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big]\\
\ \ \displaystyle\leqslant{K\over k}\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big[\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}\vartheta(t,s)|\Delta y(s)|^{2}ds\Big]+{K\over k^{1-\delta}}\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{r}\Big[\Big(1+\int_{r}^{T}\vartheta(t,s)ds\Big)\int_{r}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big].\end{array}

(3.47)

Step 3
.
Contraction mapping
.

By the definitions of
\vartheta(\cdot\,,\cdot)
,
\mathbb{E}^{t,i}[\,\cdot\,]
and
\mathbb{P}^{t,i}
together with (
2.3
), (
3.35
), (
3.38
) and the energy inequality, it follows that for each
i=1,\cdots,n
, each
t\in[0,T]
and each
\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]
,

\Big\|e^{k\cdot\over 2}\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,\cdot)\Big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}\leqslant K\sup_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]}\left\|\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big]\right\|_{\infty},

(3.48)

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T}\vartheta(t,s)ds\Big]\leqslant K\Big(1+\big\|\zeta(t,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}+\big\|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}\Big)\leqslant K,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(1+\int_{\tau}^{T}\vartheta(t,s)ds\Big)^{2}\Big]\leqslant K\Big(1+\big\|\zeta(t,\cdot)\big\|^{4}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}+\big\|\widetilde{\zeta}(t,\cdot\big\|^{4}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}\Big)\leqslant K,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{2}\Big]\leqslant K\Big\|e^{k\cdot\over 2}\Delta\zeta(t,\cdot)\Big\|^{4}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]},\end{array}

and then, by virtue of Hölder’s inequality, we have

\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{ks}\vartheta(t,s)|\Delta y(s)|^{2}ds\Big]\leqslant K\Big\|e^{k\cdot\over 2}\Delta y(\cdot)\Big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(3.49)

and

\mathbb{E}^{t,i}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(1+\int_{\tau}^{T}\vartheta(t,s)ds\Big)\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{ks}|\Delta\zeta(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big]\leqslant K\Big\|e^{k\cdot\over 2}\Delta\zeta(t,\cdot)\Big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}.

(3.50)

Note that (
3.47
) holds still when
r
is replaced with any
\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]
. It follows from (
3.48
)–(
3.50
) that for almost every
t\in[0,T]
and
i=1,\cdots,n
,

\displaystyle e^{kt}|\Delta\eta^{i}(t,t)|^{2}+\Big\|e^{k\cdot\over 2}\Delta\zeta^{i}(t,\cdot)\Big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}\displaystyle\leqslant{K\over k}\Big\|e^{k\cdot\over 2}\Delta y(\cdot)\Big\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})}+{K\over k^{1-\delta}}\Big\|e^{k\cdot\over 2}\Delta\zeta(t,\cdot)\Big\|^{2}_{\overline{{\rm BMO}}[t,T]}.

Picking
k
large enough, we obtain

\big\|(Y(\cdot)-\widetilde{Y}(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot)-\widetilde{Z}(\cdot\,,\cdot))\big\|_{\mathscr{B}_{k}}\leqslant{1\over 2}\big\|(y(\cdot)-\widetilde{y}(\cdot),z(\cdot\,,\cdot)-\widetilde{z}(\cdot\,,\cdot))\big\|_{\mathscr{B}_{k}}.

This means that
\Gamma
is a contraction mapping on the convex closed
\mathscr{B}
under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{B}_{k}}
, which is just the desired result. The proof is then complete.

Remark 3.3
.

(ii) In

) in Theorem

is weaker than that in

even in the one-dimensional framework.

3.2
A mixed case of linear and quadratic growth

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle g^{i}(t,s,y,(1-\theta)z^{i}+\theta\bar{z}^{i})-\theta g^{i}(t,s,y,\bar{z}^{i})\leqslant(1-\theta)\big[\alpha(t,s)+\beta\big(|y|+|z^{i}|^{1+\delta}\big)\big],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall(t,s)\in\Delta^{*}[0,T],~y,z,\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},~\theta\in(0,1),\end{array}

(4.3)

or

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle g^{i}(t,s,y,(1-\theta)z^{i}+\theta\bar{z}^{i})-\theta g^{i}(t,s,y,\bar{z}^{i})\geqslant-(1-\theta)\big[\alpha(t,s)+\beta\big(|y|+|z^{i}|^{1+\delta}\big)\big],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall(t,s)\in\Delta^{*}[0,T],~y,z,\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},~\theta\in(0,1).\end{array}

(4.4)

Although (H4.1)
′

4.4
) holds, and if

4.3
) holds. Now, we define

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\bar{z}=(\bar{z}^{1},\cdots,\bar{z}^{k},\bar{z}^{k+1},\cdots,\bar{z}^{n})=(z^{1},\cdots,z^{k},-z^{k+1},\cdots,-z^{n}),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\bar{y}=(\bar{y}^{1},\cdots,\bar{y}^{k},\bar{y}^{k+1},\cdots,\bar{y}^{n})=(y^{1},\cdots,y^{k},-y^{k+1},\cdots,-y^{n}),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{g}^{i}(t,s,y,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}z^{i}})=g^{i}(t,s,\bar{y},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{z}^{i}}),\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant k,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{g}^{i}(t,s,y,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}z^{i}})=-g^{i}(t,s,\bar{y},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{z}^{i}}),\quad k+1\leqslant i\leqslant n.\end{array}

Then (H4.1)
′
holds for

if and only if (H4.1) holds for

Next, we introduce the following hypothesis.

(H4.2)
Let (H4.1) hold with (
4.2
) replaced by the following: for each

, and each

Further, if the generator

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widetilde{g}^{\,i}(t,s,y,(1-\theta)z_{1}+\theta z_{2})-\theta\widetilde{g}^{\,i}(t,s,y,z_{2})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=g^{i}(t,s,y,(1-\theta)(z_{0}+z_{1})+\theta(z_{0}+z_{2}))-\theta g^{i}(t,s,y,z_{0}+z_{2})-(1-\theta)g^{i}(t,s,y,z_{0})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant(1-\theta)\big(\alpha(t,s)+\beta|y|+\beta|z_{0}+z_{1}|^{1+\delta}\big)+(1-\theta)|g^{i}(t,s,y,z_{0})|\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant(1-\theta)\big(K(t,s,y,z_{0})+2^{\delta}\beta|z_{1}|^{1+\delta}\big),\end{array}

(4.5)

with

Letting

which means that

|g^{i}(t,s,y,z_{0}+z)-g^{i}(t,s,y,z_{0})|=|\widetilde{g}^{\,i}(t,s,y,z)|\leqslant\big(K(t,s,y,z_{0})+2^{\delta}\beta\big)|z|,\quad\forall z\in(-1,1).

This gives the local Lipschitz of

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{p\geqslant 1}\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|Z^{(m+p)}(t,s)-Z^{(m)}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{q\over 2}\Big]=0,\quad\forall q>1.

Consequently, there exists a process
Z(\cdot\,,\cdot)\in\bigcap_{p\geqslant 1}L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\cdot\,,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})))
such that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|Z^{(/m)}(t,s)-Z(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{q\over 2}\Big]=0,\qquad\forall q>1.

(4.52)

Thus, in view of (
4.46
), (
4.47
) and (
4.52
), by sending
m
to infinity in (
4.24
) we deduce that
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
is a desired solution of BSVIE (
4.22
).
For the above
\eta(t,\cdot)
in (
4.49
), it is easy to see that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\eta(t,r)=\psi(t)+\int_{r}^{T}g(t,s,Y(s),Z(t,s))ds-\int_{r}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[t,T],\end{array}

from which we see that (
4.23
) holds.

Next we look at the uniqueness. Suppose
(\widetilde{Y}(\cdot),\widetilde{Z}(\cdot,\cdot))
is another pair of solution
such that
\widetilde{\eta}(t,\cdot)\in\cap_{p\geqslant 1}{\cal E}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p,\lambda}(\Omega;C([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for almost every
t\in[0,T]
, where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}(t,r):=\widetilde{Y}(t)-\int_{t}^{r}g(t,s,\widetilde{Y}(s),\widetilde{Z}(t,s))ds+\int_{t}^{r}\widetilde{Z}(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[t,T].\end{array}

(4.53)

For each fixed
\theta\in(0,1)
and
(t,s)\in\Delta[0,T]
, define

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\delta_{\theta}\eta(t,s):=\frac{\eta(t,s)-\theta\widetilde{\eta}(t,s)}{1-\theta},\ \ \ \delta_{\theta}Y(t):=\frac{Y(t)-\theta\widetilde{Y}(t)}{1-\theta},\ \ \ \delta_{\theta}\tilde{\eta}(t,s):=\frac{\widetilde{\eta}(t,s)-\theta\eta(t,s)}{1-\theta},\\
\displaystyle\delta_{\theta}\tilde{Y}(t):=\frac{\widetilde{Y}(t)-\theta Y(t)}{1-\theta},\ \ \ \delta_{\theta}Z(t,s):=\frac{Z(t,s)-\theta\widetilde{Z}(t,s)}{1-\theta},\ \ \ \delta_{\theta}\widetilde{Z}(t,s):=\frac{\widetilde{Z}(t,s)-\theta Z(t,s)}{1-\theta}.\end{array}

Following the similar arguments from (
4.33
) to (
4.45
), for each
q>1
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Phi_{\lambda}\Big(\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\big(|\delta_{\theta}Y(t)|+|\delta_{\theta}\widetilde{Y}(t)|\big);q\Big)\Big]\leqslant\widetilde{M}(q),

(4.54)

where
\widetilde{M}(q)
is a positive constant depending on
q
and being independent of
\theta
. Thus, for each
\theta\in(0,1)
, it follows that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}|Y(t)-\theta\widetilde{Y}(t)|^{\lambda}\Big]\leqslant\frac{(1-\theta)^{\lambda}}{2}\widetilde{M}(2),

and then,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}|Y(t)-\widetilde{Y}(t)|^{\lambda}\Big]\leqslant(1-\theta)^{\lambda}\lambda\{\frac{\widetilde{M}(2)}{2}+\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}(t)|\Big]\}<+\infty.\end{array}

Sending
\theta
to
1
, we see that
Y(\cdot)=\widetilde{Y}(\cdot).
Finally, the conclusions of
Z(\cdot,\cdot)=\widetilde{Z}(\cdot,\cdot)
,
\eta(\cdot,\cdot)=\widetilde{\eta}(\cdot,\cdot)
follows from Proposition
4.1
.
The proof is complete.

Remark 4.3
.

Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
are obtained under the condition that
g^{i}(t,s,y,z)
varies with
(t,s,y)
and the
i
th component
z^{i}
of
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for each
i=1,\cdots,n
, see assumption (H4.1) for details. When
g^{i}
also depends on the
z^{j}
for some
j\neq i
, the complexity of the problem increases significantly. We hope to address it in future work.

5
Super Quadratic BSVIEs

In the previous sections, we discuss the case when the generator
g(t,s,y,z)
of the BSVIEs is at most of quadratic growth in
z
. In this part, we begin to look at the last case, i.e., the case of super-quadratic growth of the map
z\mapsto g(t,s,y,z)
.

For later convenience, let us first recall some existing conclusions of the BSDEs case.
Let us consider the following BSDE

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Y(t)=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(Z(s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(s)dW(s),\ \ t\in[0,T],\end{array}

(5.1)

where the free term
\xi
and the generator
f
satisfy the following assumption:

(H5.1)
Random variable
\xi
is bounded
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable and
\mathbb{R}
-valued, and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is convex with

f(0)=0\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \mathop{\overline{\rm lim}}_{|z|\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{f(z)}{|z|^{2}}=\infty.

Since the growth order of
z
in the generator is larger than two, it becomes harder to discuss the integrability of solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
. Hence, in this setting, a pair of
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
taking value in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
is called a bounded solution by which we mean that they satisfy BSDE (
5.1
) for each
t\in[0,T]
such that
Y(\cdot)
is bounded and

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}f(Z(t))dt\right]<+\infty.

The following conclusion comes from
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
(H5.1)
holds. Then there exists a bounded
\xi
such that BSDE (
5.1
) has no bounded solution.

At this moment, one may ask:
what is the sufficient condition of existence of the bounded solution?
According to the above conclusion, one needs to impose proper conditions on
\xi
. To this end, given convex
f
satisfying (H5.1) , let us define

\bar{f}(x):=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\big(zx-f(z)\big),\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

With proper function
q(\cdot)
, let us define

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle U_{t}(\xi):=\mathop{\rm essinf}\Big\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{t}\Big[\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\bar{f}(q(u))du\Big]\Big|\mathbb{Q}\sim\mathbb{P}\Big\},\ \ t\in[0,T],\end{array}

where
\mathbb{Q}
is another probability measure

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}=\exp\left[\int_{0}^{T}q(s)dW(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|q(s)|^{2}ds\right].\end{array}

A bounded random variable
\xi
is said to be
minimal
if

\eta\leqslant\xi,\quad\mathbb{P}(\eta<\xi)>0\quad\mathop{\Rightarrow}\quad U_{0}(\eta)<U_{0}(\xi).

The following result comes from
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose
\xi
is a minimal random variable and
(H5.1)
holds. Then BSDE (
5.1
) admits a bounded solution.

Besides the existence, the uniqueness is another tough issue as is shown in
[
8
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose (H5.1) holds and (
5.1
) admits a bounded solution. Then for each
y<Y(0)
, BSDE (
5.1
) admits a bounded solution
(Y^{\prime}(\cdot),Z^{\prime}(\cdot))
satisfying
Y^{\prime}(0)=y
.

Now let us consider the following BSVIE:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Y(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,Z(t,s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\ \ \ t\in[0,T].\end{array}

(5.2)

We discuss its bounded solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
taking value in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
by which we mean that
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
is a solution of (
5.2
) such that
Y(\cdot)
is bounded and for each
t\in[0,T]
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}g(t,Z(t,s))ds\right]<+\infty.

Similar as (H5.1), for the coefficients in (
5.2
) we use the following assumptions.

(H5.2)
The free term
\psi(\cdot)
is a bounded,
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable and
\mathbb{R}
-valued process, and the generator
g:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is convex in the second variable with, for each
t\in[0,T]
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle g(t,0)=0,\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \mathop{\overline{\rm lim}}_{|z|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{g(t,z)}{|z|^{2}}=\infty.\end{array}

Similar as Proposition
5.1
, we have

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose
(H5.2)
holds. Then there exists a bounded
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable process
\psi(\cdot)
such that BSVIE (
5.2
) has no bounded solution.

Proof.
Let us take
\psi(\cdot)\equiv\bar{\xi}
, where the bounded random variable
\bar{\xi}
is determined by
g(0,\cdot)
such that the following BSDE

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{Y}(t)=\bar{\xi}+\int_{t}^{T}g(0,\bar{Z}(s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}\bar{Z}(s)dW(s),\ \ t\in[0,T],\end{array}

has no bounded solution according to Proposition
5.1
. We prove the conclusion by contradiction. Suppose (
5.2
) admits a bounded solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
. For any
t\in[0,T]
, define

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\eta(t,r):=Y(t)-\int_{t}^{r}g(t,Z(t,s))ds+\int_{t}^{r}Z(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[t,T].\end{array}

It is easy to see that for each
t\in[0,T]
,
(\eta(t,\cdot),Z(t,\cdot))
satisfies the following BSDE:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\eta(t,r)=\bar{\xi}+\int_{r}^{T}g(t,Z(t,s))ds-\int_{r}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[t,T].\end{array}

Let
t=0
, we see that
(\eta(0,\cdot),Z(0,\cdot))
solves

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\eta(0,r)=\bar{\xi}+\int_{r}^{T}g(0,Z(0,s))ds-\int_{r}^{T}Z(0,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[0,T].\end{array}

Obviously, it is contradicted with the choice of
\bar{\xi}
.

Similar as the above, we call a bounded measurable process
\psi(\cdot):[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
minimal if for any
t\in[0,T]
,
\psi(t)
is a minimal random variable.

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose
\psi(\cdot)
is minimal. Then BSVIE (
5.2
) admits a bounded solution under
(H5.2)
. In addition, the bounded solution of (
5.2
) is not unique.

Proof.
Given parameter
t\in[0,T]
, we consider the following BSDE:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\eta(t,r)=\psi(t)+\int_{r}^{T}g(t,\zeta(t,s))ds-\int_{r}^{T}\zeta(t,s)dW(s),\ \ r\in[0,T].\end{array}

Since
\psi(t)
is minimal and
g
satisfies (H5.2), according to Proposition
5.2
, the above BSDE admits a bounded solution
(\eta(t,\cdot),\zeta(t,\cdot))
for almost every
t\in[0,T]
. For each
(t,s)\in\Delta[0,T]
, by defining
Y(t):=\eta(t,t)
and
Z(t,s):=\zeta(t,s)
, we obtain a bounded solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
to BSVIE (
5.2
). Finally, the non-uniqueness is implied by Proposition
5.3
.

Spin-triplet superconductivity from interband effect in doped insulators
Valentin Crépel, Liang Fu

Abstract
Despite being of fundamental importance and potential interest for topological quantum computing, spin-triplet superconductors remain rare in solid state materials after decades of research. In this work, we present a general mechanism for spin-triplet superconductivity in multi-band systems, where a non-retarded pairing interaction between conduction electrons is produced by their electronic repulsion to a third-electron undergoing a virtual interband transition. Our theory is analytically controlled by an interband hybridization parameter, and explicitly demonstrated in doped band insulators with the example of an extended Hubbard model. In light of this theory, we propose that recently discovered dilute superconductors such as ZrNCl and WTe 2 are spin-triplet, and compare the expected consequences of our theory with experimental data.

Spin-triplet superconductors display a plethora of unconventional phenomena, including multi-component order parameter, fractional vortices

Further interest in spin-triplet superconductors is fueled by their prospect as a material platform for topological qubits

;

;

;

However, triplet superconductors are rare to find.
Inspired by superfluid helium-3

, UPt

In recent years, evidence of anisotropic spin-triplet pairing has also been discovered in superconducting

;
Yonezawa et al. 2017
;

;

;
Hashimoto et al. 2013
;
Fu 2014
;
Wan and Savrasov 2014
.
Despite significant effort and progress, the pairing symmetry and/or pairing mechanism of these candidate materials remain to be fully understood.

In this work, we introduce an electronic mechanism for spin-triplet superconductivity in doped insulators, where the pairing of doped electrons arises from interband electronic effects.
By developing a controlled expansion in the interband hybridization, we show that an attractive interaction between two conduction electrons arises from virtual interband transition, as illustrated in Fig.1a.
Since this mechanism involves two electrons forming the pair and a third one undergoing a virtual interband transition, we dub it “three-particle mechanism” for superconductivity
Crépel and Fu 2021
.

The idea of using interband electronic excitations, such as excitons, as a replacement of phonon to mediate superconductivity has a long history
Little 1964
;
Ginzburg and Kirzhnits 1972
;
Allender et al. 1973
;
Hirsch and Scalapino 1986
. However,
experimental evidence of excion-mediated superconductivity remains elusive.
A major challenge is that most proposals rely on metal layers on a separate excitonic medium, which often result in weak coupling between conduction electrons and virtual excitons. Moreover, theoretical works on this subject have only considered s-wave pairing, which is usually disfavored in electron systems with strong repulsive interaction.
Last but not the least, the large energy scale of intermediate excited states means that the induced interaction
is
non-retarded
, in contrast to phonon-mediated pairing.
Thus, new theoretical methods are needed to tackle the problem of superconductivity from repulsive interaction
in multiband systems.

The novelties of our work solve the above problems and challenges. We study multiband systems that naturally host both
excitons and conduction electrons, interacting strongly with each other by electrostatic forces.
Using a two-band Hubbard model as an example, we show with exact solutions
that virtual interband effects lead to spin-triplet pairing of two doped electrons in a band insulator.
The spin-triplet electron pair naturally avoids the large Coulomb repulsion at short distance.

Since our hybridization expansion method is non-perturbative in the interaction strength,
we obtain an asymptotically exact theory of strong-coupling superconductivity at low doping,
without distractions from other competing states and without requiring any bosonic glue.
Our theory provides a mechanism whereby superconductivity arises upon
infinitesimal
doping of a band insulator.
It predicts a direct transition from a band insulator to a superconductor without single-particle gap closing,
and a BEC/BCS crossover as a function of doping concentration.

Our theory sheds light on unconventional superconductivity behaviors in doped band insulators.
We shall focus on two materials: electron-doped ZrNCl and WTe
2
, both of which superconduct at very low doping.
Remarkably, BEC-BCS crossover has recently been observed in two-dimensional ZrNCl
Nakagawa et al. 2021
.
In monolayer WTe
2
, a direct insulator-superconductor transition was found under electrostatic gating
Fatemi et al. 2018
;
Sajadi et al. 2018
.
We will compare our theoretical predictions of spin-triplet superconductivity with the experimental data on these dilute superconductors.
In particular, we highlight the observed increase of
T_{c}
in WTe
2
under a small in-plane magnetic field as
a strong evidence for spin-triplet superconductivity.

III.2
Two-particle bound state

Remarkably,
\mathcal{H}_{f}
features an attractive interaction between conduction electrons forming a spin-triplet state.
This effective attraction surprisingly emerges in the purely repulsive Hubbard model Eq.

In our model, this attraction competes with and wins over the repulsion

It is important to note that in our model, triplet pairing already occurs at

III.3
Superconductivity in the dilute limit

The existence of spin-triplet bound states is suggestive of superconductivity at finite doping.
We now study the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{f}
for doped electrons at finite density on the triangular lattice. The single-particle dispersion, given by
\varepsilon_{\bf k}=2t\sum_{j=1}^{3}\cos({\bf k}\cdot{\bf a}_{j})

K

At sufficiently small density

We now derive an effective continuum theory that captures the long-wavelength behavior of this liquid.
Retaining only fermionic modes

III.4
T_{c}
versus doping

At finite density, the spin-triplet valley-singlet pairing leads to a superconducting state with a vector order parameter

\displaystyle{\bf d}=\langle\psi_{K,\alpha}[\bm{\sigma}(i\sigma_{y})]_{\alpha,\beta}\psi_{K^{\prime},\beta}\rangle.

(9)

This state is fully gapped and isotropic,
i.e.

is constant (see Fig.
3

This property is unusual for triplet superconductors, where the antisymmetry of the Cooper pair wavefunction implies
{\bf d}({\bf k})=-{\bf d}(-{\bf k})
.
For a singly connected Fermi surface centered at
{\bf k}=0
, the condition necessarily requires strong variations of the
{\bf d}
-vector over the Fermi surface, as exemplified in the

B

3

In stark contrast, in our case the presence of two disconnected Fermi surfaces centered around
K
and
K^{\prime}
enables inter-valley spin-triplet pairing with a constant and opposite
{\bf d}
-vector over the Fermi surface of each valley.

Another important feature of our theory is the non-retarded pairing interaction, which spreads over the entire bandwidth of doped charges.
This sharply contrasts with electron-phonon superconductors, where the attractive interaction is cut off by the Debye energy, and thus limited to the vicinity of the Fermi surface.
The absence of energy cutoff changes the expression of the superconducting critical temperature

. We find that the mean-field

depends strongly on the carrier density

Randeria et al. 1990

k_{B}T^{\rm MF}_{c}=\frac{e^{\gamma}}{\pi}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{F}\varepsilon_{b}}\propto\sqrt{xW}\exp\left(-\frac{4\pi}{m|g|}\right).

(10)

where

is the noninteracting Fermi energy. As a consequence, the gap to

.
At very low density where

At high density, lattice effects become important. We have performed mean-field calculations for the lattice model Eq.
2
(see App.

In agreement with the previous discussion, we find that the spin-triplet f-wave pairing channel is the leading instability in our model.

IV
Interaction expansion

In this section, we show that the three-particle mechanism for superconductivity described in Sec.
III
is not restricted to the atomic limit
t_{0}\ll\Delta
, but extends at any value of the tunneling parameter.
To preserve analytical control of interband effects, we work in the small interaction limit, such that interband hybridization remains small.
In this regime, we find evidence of attractive interaction between conduction electrons. using a similar method than in Sec.
III
.
We follow the methodology of Sec.
III
,
i.e.
we first derive an effective model for doped electrons with an unitary transformation, and then study the obtained model for small doping concentrations.

IV.1

We start from the Hamiltonian Eq.

The former is akin to the Hartree-Fock correction of standard many-body perturbation theory:

\begin{split}\delta\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}&=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{{\bf q},\sigma}\left[V_{({\bf q},-,\sigma)({\bf k},+,\uparrow)}^{({\bf k},+,\uparrow)({\bf q},-,\sigma)}+V_{({\bf k},+,\uparrow)({\bf q},-,\sigma)}^{({\bf q},-,\sigma)({\bf k},+,\uparrow)}\right]\\
&-\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{{\bf q}}\left[V_{({\bf k},+,\uparrow)({\bf q},-,\uparrow)}^{({\bf k},+,\uparrow)({\bf q},-,\uparrow)}+V_{({\bf q},-,\uparrow)({\bf k},+,\uparrow)}^{({\bf q},-,\uparrow)({\bf k},+,\uparrow)}\right],\end{split}

(15)

where we have used the spin-independence of
V
, and choose
\uparrow
as a preferred spin index.
This small correction does not change the position of the band minima, which remain degenerate at the
K
and
K^{\prime}
points.
Expanding around these minima in the limit
t_{0}\ll\Delta_{0}
gives
(\varepsilon+\delta\varepsilon)_{\tau K+{\bf k},+}\simeq|{\bf k}|^{2}/(2m^{*})
with

\frac{1}{m^{*}}=\frac{3t_{0}^{2}a^{2}}{2\Delta_{0}^{2}}\left[\Delta_{0}+U_{A}-4V_{0}\right],

(16)

which agrees with the result of Eq.
4
obtained with the kinetic expansion provided interactions are small compared to
\Delta_{0}
.
This offers an important consistency test between the two methods in their overlapping regimes of validity.

IV.2
Attraction in dilute limit

The expressions for the corrections to the scattering vertex
\delta V
being quite involved (see App.
S5
), we simply support here the emergence of attractive interactions in the
f
-wave scattering channel by computing the effective interaction strength in the spin-triplet valley-singlet channel,
i.e.
between equal-spin electrons living in opposite valleys.
Writing
\tilde{V}=V+\delta V
, this coefficient can be expressed as

\begin{split}U_{0}=&\tilde{V}_{(K+)(K^{\prime}+)}^{(K^{\prime}+)(K+)}+\tilde{V}_{(K^{\prime}+)(K+)}^{(K+)(K^{\prime}+)}\\
&-\tilde{V}_{(K+)(K^{\prime}+)}^{(K+)(K^{\prime}+)}-\tilde{V}_{(K^{\prime}+)(K+)}^{(K^{\prime}+)(K+)},\end{split}

(17)

where all spin component are equal.
This coefficient greatly simplifies as the Bloch states at the
K
and
K^{\prime}
point in the upper band are localized on
B
sites,
\Psi_{K/K^{\prime},+}^{A}=0
.
Using this simplification, we end up with

U_{0}=\frac{12t_{0}^{2}V_{0}(U_{B}-3V_{0})}{N_{s}}\sum_{{\bf q}}\frac{|f({\bf q})|^{2}}{(2\varepsilon_{{\bf q},+})^{3}},

(18)

which also agrees with the our results of Sec.
III
in the limit where both methods are analytically controlled (see App.
S5
).
The presence of an effective attraction
U_{0}<0
in the f-wave channel when
U_{B}<3V_{0}
proves that the results of Fig.
3
hold for non-perturbative tunneling amplitudes
t_{0}
.

Although the three-particle mechanism for superconductivity introduced in this article is more easily pictured near the atomic limit (Sec.
III
), it can also apply to band insulators where the conduction band’s width is substantial compared to the single particle gap.
Strong of this generalization, we now compare the predictions of our theory to experimental data on two dilute superconductors.

V
Application to dilute superconductors

Let us recapitulate our results so far.
Using the specific model Eq.
II
as an example, we have demonstrated a general mechanism of spin-triplet superconductivity in doped band insulators with strong repulsive interaction.
Focusing on the low density regime, we have derived a universal continuum model for the two-valley Fermi liquid.
When either tunneling or interactions are small compared to the
n=2
insulator gap, we have shown that virtual interband transitions can produce a sufficiently strong intervalley ferromagnetic interaction that leads to spin-triplet pairing.
A remarkable consequence of our theory is that spin-triplet superconductivity occurs at infinitesimal doping, with a sharp increase
T_{c}\sim\sqrt{x}
.
Based on this theory, we hereby propose that the recently discovered dilute superconductors ZrNCl WTe
2
are spin-triplet.

V.1
ZrNCl

ZrNCl shares many common features with our toy model Eq.
II
.
At the single particle level, it is a band insulator with a large gap
\Delta\sim 2.5
eV
Weht et al. 1999
;
Hase and Nishihara 1999
, where a single band with quadratic minima at the
K
and
K^{\prime}
points is relevant to describe the physics at low carrier density
Heid and Bohnen 2005
;
Yin et al. 2013
.
Upon electron doping, two-dimensional superconductivity arises in the ZrN planes
Tou et al. 2000
;
Tou et al. 2005
, even for extremely dilute samples
x=0.0038

Nakagawa et al. 2021
.
The critical temperatures ranging from
11.5\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
to
19\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
is hard to explain by electron-phonon mechanism, which makes ZrNCl an unconventional superconductor.
A compelling argument against the phonon-mechanism of pairing is the simultaneous enhancement of
T_{c}

Takano et al. 2008
and reduction of electron–phonon interactions, probed by Raman scattering
Kitora et al. 2007
, when the doping decreases from 0.2 to 0.05.

We now highlight that our theory consistently captures the available experimental results on the superconducting state in ZrNCl
Kasahara et al. 2015
, and propose that this material be regarded as a legitimate candidate for spin-triplet pairing.

First, we find pairing at infinitesimal doping above the band insulator, and predict a smooth crossover from a BEC of pairs to a BCS regime as the Fermi energy increases
Crépel and Fu 2021
, which has recently been observed in extremely high quality ZrNCl samples
Nakagawa et al. 2021
.
On the BCS side of this crossover, we expect a gap to critical temperature ratio
2\Delta/k_{B}T_{c}>3.5

Parish 2015
;
Gor’kov and Melik-Barkhudarov 1961
;
Chubukov et al. 2016
.
This is because the induced pairing interaction in Eq.
2
is instantaneous on the time scale of the inverse bandwidth, so that all carriers in the narrow band contribute to the superconducting gap.
This contrasts with phonon-mediated retarded attractions, which only spread over a Debye width near the Fermi level and lead the universal ratio of 3.5.
The gap to
T_{c}
ratio measured by scanning tunneling spectroscopy in ZrNCl is about ten
Ekino et al. 2013
, a sign of strong coupling superconductivity and non-retarded pairing interactions which are well captured by our model.

Second, our theory also allows a fine understanding of the critical temperature observed in ZrNCl.
Using the DFT results for the lattice constant
a=$3.663\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
and the effective mass at the
K
point
m=0.580m_{e}

Tanaka et al. 2015
;
Yun and Lee 2017
, we estimate
t\sim$0.65\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}$
.
With the parameters
U_{B}=4V_{0}=4\Delta
of Fig.
4
, we obtain a critical temperature
T_{c}\sim 0.002t\simeq$15\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$
for
9\%
doping, which lies very close to the experimentally measured value.

Our theory also successfully captures the non-monotonic dependence of
T_{c}
on doping observed in Refs.
Nakagawa et al. 2021
.
The original increase of
T_{c}
following from the BEC/BCS physics, while the subsequent decay of
T_{c}
when
x=5\%\to 20\%

Takano et al. 2008
is interpreted by the larger modulation of the form factor
s_{q}
near
\Gamma-M
lines.
For the parameters of Fig.
4
,
T_{c}
at
x=0.2
equals
2/3
of its maximum value, which almost quantitatively agrees with the measured value of
40\%

Nakagawa et al. 2021
.

Finally, specific heat measurements point towards a change from an almost isotropic to a highly anisotropic order parameter upon increasing the doping level
Kasahara et al. 2009
;
Taguchi et al. 2005
, which is consistent with an f-wave superconducting state.
The unconventional pairing symmetry is further substantiated by the lack of coherence peak in the NMR signal of Ref.
Kotegawa et al. 2014
.

V.2
WTe
2

Monolayer WTe
2
is a topological insulator with spin-helical edge states
Qian et al. 2014
;
Fei et al. 2017
;
Tang et al. 2017
;
Wu et al. 2018
;
Lau et al. 2019
.
Recently, two independent groups discovered a transition from insulating to superconducting state in monolayer WTe
2
under electron doping via electrostatic gating
Fatemi et al. 2018
;
Sajadi et al. 2018
.
Another separate work observed superconductivity in epitaxial thin film of WTe
2

Asaba et al. 2018
.
The origin of superconductivity and the nature of the superconducting state are unknown and have attracted considerable interest
Hsu et al. 2020
;
Xie et al. 2020
.

Our picture is that superconductivity in electron doped WTe
2
is driven by excitonic effects and exhibits spin-triplet pairing.
Indeed, a recent experiment on insulating WTe
2
reported evidence of strong excitonic effects which significantly enhance the single-particle gap
Jia et al. 2020
.
While the electronic structure of WTe
2
is far more complicated than our model used to illustrate the exciton pairing mechanism, they both feature two valleys in the conduction band.
Therefore, electron doped WTe
2
at low density is a two valley Fermi liquid described by our continuum theory Eq.
5
.
If excitons in WTe
2
mediate strong enough inter-valley ferromagnetic exchange, our theory should capture the essential physics of its superconducting state.

This motivates a thorough comparison between the expected consequences of spin-triplet superconductivity to experimental findings on WTe
2
.
First, differential resistance measurements
Fatemi et al. 2018
shows that upon doping, an insulating resistance peak transforms directly into to a superconducting resistance dip, consistent with our picture of an insulator-superconductor transition.
The experimentally observed sharp increase of
T_{c}
with doping
Fatemi et al. 2018
agrees remarkably well with the prediction
T_{c}\sim\sqrt{x}
in the low density regime, as shown in Fig.
5
.
Another prediction of our theory is that single-particle gap does not close across the doping induced insulator-superconductor transition, which can be tested in future tunneling measurements.

Figure 5:

The doping dependence of
T_{c}
, measured in
Fatemi et al. 2018
, is well captured by our low-density prediction Eq.
10
.
b) Perfect agreement is found between the critical temperature measured in Ref.
Asaba et al. 2018

.

The scenario of spin-triplet superconductivity in WTe
2
is supported by the observation of an in-plane critical field much larger than the Pauli limit in both monolayers and thin films
Fatemi et al. 2018
;
Asaba et al. 2018
;
Sajadi et al. 2018
.
The most significant experimental evidence of spin-triplet superconductivity is the initial
increase
of
T_{c}
upon application of an in-plane magnetic field
Fatemi et al. 2018
;
Asaba et al. 2018
.
This behavior is incompatible with s-wave superconductivity (even after considering the effect of spin-orbit interaction
Xie et al. 2020
). On the contrary, the enhancement of
T_{c}
by magnetic field follows naturally from our equal-spin triplet superconducting state in two-valley systems.
This can be seen with the Landau free energy:

F=\alpha({\bf d}\cdot{\bf d}^{*})+\mu{\bf B}\cdot(i{\bf d}\times{\bf d}^{*})+\eta|{\bf B}\cdot{\bf d}|^{2}+\chi B^{2}({\bf d}\cdot{\bf d}^{*}),

(19)

with
\alpha=\kappa(T-T_{c}(B=0))
and
\kappa,\mu,\eta,\chi>0
near
T_{c}
.
Importantly, the magnetic field
{\bf B}
results in a linear Zeeman shift for pairs of total spin
{\bf S}=i({\bf d}\times{\bf d}^{*})
.
The
\eta
term describes the preferred equal-spin pairing with
{{\bf d}}\perp\bf B
,
i.e.
the spins are aligned or anti-aligned with the field.
The last term accounts for orbital effect of the in-plane
B
field, which causes pair breaking.

From the Landau free energy, we obtain

\Delta T_{c}^{B}=\mu B-\chi B^{2},

(20)

with
\Delta T_{c}^{B}=T_{c}(B)-T_{c}(0)
.
Due to the Zeeman effect on triplet pairs,
T_{c}
increases
linearly
with
B
at small field.
This non-analytic dependence on
B
is a consequence of the degeneracy of triplet superconducting states associated with spin degrees of freedom at zero field.
At such large
B
, orbital effect dominates and reduces
T_{c}
. As shown in Fig.
5
, the above theoretical curve
T_{c}(B)
fits excellently with experimental data.

The presence of spin-orbit coupling in WTe
2
is expected to lift the degeneracy between different triplet states.
Due to a plane mirror symmetry perpendicular to the
a
crystallographic axis, an additional term
F_{\rm SOC}=2\gamma|d_{x}|^{2}
is allowed after including the spin-orbit effect.
If
{\bf B}\parallel b
, the linear increase of
T_{c}
is rounded off by
\gamma
according to

\Delta T_{c}^{B}=\sqrt{(\mu B)^{2}+\gamma^{2}}-|\gamma|-\chi B^{2}.

(21)

For small spin-orbit coupling
\gamma<\mu^{2}/2\chi
, the Zeeman term dominates, which still enables the enhancement of
T_{c}
for
{\bf d}\propto(\Delta T_{c}^{B}+\chi B^{2},0,i\mu B)^{T}
.
For larger
\gamma
,
T_{c}(B)
decreases monotonously with field. When
{\bf B}\parallel a
, we should distinguish two cases. First if
\gamma>0
, both spin-orbit and magnetic field favor the vector
{\bf d}\propto(0,1,i)^{T}
, and we recover Eq.
20
.
Then for
\gamma<0
, the original spin degeneracy is fully lifted and we observe a competition between two different triplet states with
{\bf d}\parallel a
and
{\bf d}\perp a
, respectively favored at low and high field.
This leads to

\Delta T_{c}^{B}=\begin{cases}-(\eta+\chi)B^{2}&\text{if: }B<B^{*}\\
\mu B-|\gamma|-\chi B^{2}&\text{if: }B>B^{*}\end{cases},

(22)

which exhibits a kink at
B^{*}\simeq|\gamma|/\mu
where the system undergo a first-order phase transition.
We hope more measurements of
T_{c}
as a function of both in-plane magnetic field strength and direction can be performed to establish the highly unusual behaviour predicted by Eqs.
20
and
22
, which we regard as an unambiguous evidence for triplet superconductivity.

\epsilon=\pm\end{subarray}}\left[1+e^{i\epsilon(K\cdot a_{j})}\right]|\varphi_{S}(K,r+\epsilon a_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,2,3\\
\epsilon=\pm\end{subarray}}\delta_{r,\epsilon a_{j}}\left[V|\varphi_{S}(K,\epsilon a_{j})\rangle+\lambda|\varphi_{S}(K,-\epsilon a_{j-\epsilon})\rangle\right]

\displaystyle+\lambda\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,2,3\\
\epsilon=\pm\end{subarray}}\delta_{r,\epsilon a_{j}}e^{i\epsilon(K\cdot a_{j-\epsilon})}|\varphi_{S}(K,-\epsilon a_{j+\epsilon})\rangle.

(E25)

We then solve this equation numerically for large enough system sizes to extract the ground state energy in each spin sector. Our solution are shown in Fig.
2
of the main text.

While our original model does not include any direct repulsion between
B
sites on the honeycomb lattice, the two-particle bound state we have established is robust against longer range interactions. To study their effect, we further add non-local interaction between conduction electrons to the effective Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and re-solve the two-particle problem.
We find that bound state is destroyed only when the the non-local repulsion becomes comparable to the exciton-induced short-range pairing interaction (which is much larger than the binding energy
\varepsilon_{b}
). If we take into account the direct Coulomb repulsion between nearest-neighbor
B
sites
V^{\prime}
, bound sate persists for
V^{\prime}<2\lambda-V
, or
0.25
eV when the parameters mentioned in the main text are used.
If we include the long-range Coulomb interaction
\frac{e^{2}}{\epsilon r}
fully, bound state exists for
\epsilon a>86.4$\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
(
a
is the lattice constant), which corresponds to
e^{2}/\epsilon a=16.5
meV. Thus, in order for electron pairing to occur in the limit of vanishing doping, it is helpful to have a large
\epsilon
which can result from dielectric screening by a different band.

S3
Continuum Limit

As shown in Fig.
1
c, the kinetic part of the effective Hamiltonian Eq.
2
dominates over interactions. Thus, low-energy fermions live near the two degenerate minima of the single-particle dispersion relation located at the
K
and
K^{\prime}
points in the Brillouin Zone. Our goal here is to derive an effective continuum field theory capturing the physics of the system when fermions remain close to these two valleys.

We start with the momentum representation of Eq.
2

\mathcal{H}_{f}=\sum_{k,\sigma}\varepsilon_{k}f_{k,\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{k,\sigma}+\frac{1}{2N_{s}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,q,p\\
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\end{subarray}}V_{k,q}f_{k,\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{q+p,\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}f_{k+p,\sigma^{\prime}}f_{q,\sigma}

(E26)

with
N_{s}
the number of unit cells in the lattice,
\varepsilon_{k}=2t\sum_{j=1}^{3}\cos(k\cdot a_{j})
and

V_{k,q}=U+2V\sum_{j}\cos[(k-q)\cdot a_{j}]+2\lambda\sum_{j}(e^{ika_{j}+iqa_{j-1}}+e^{-ika_{j-1}-iqa_{j}})+2\tilde{t}\sum_{j}[\cos(k\cdot a_{j})+\cos(q\cdot a_{j})].

(E27)

Due to the quadratic band dispersion near the
K
and
K^{\prime}
points, low energy fermions acquire an effective mass
m=2/(3t_{f}a^{2})
. They also carry an effective SU(4)
\{\uparrow K,\downarrow K,\uparrow K^{\prime},\downarrow K^{\prime}\}
that distinguishes both their spin and their valley degeneracy and enable contact interactions between fermions with the same spin, provided they have opposite valley index.

Let us now focus on the scattering properties of these low energy fermions. Due to momentum conservation, two incoming low-energy fermions from the same valley can only scatter into a pair of fermions living in the same valley. The corresponding vertex interaction reads

V_{c}=V_{K,K}=V_{K^{\prime},K^{\prime}}=6(V-\lambda-\tilde{t})+U.

(E28)

When the electrons are in opposite valley
K
and
K^{\prime}
, they can scatter to a pair in
K
and
K^{\prime}
with the same valley preserving interaction strength
V_{c}
, or exchange valley to end up in
K^{\prime}
and
K
through the vertex

V_{x}=V_{K,K^{\prime}}=V_{K^{\prime},K}=3(4\lambda-2\tilde{t}-V)+U.

(E29)

Introducing different fields for the two valleys

f_{k,\sigma}=\begin{cases}\psi_{k,\sigma,K}&\text{if }k\,\text{near }K\\
\psi_{k,\sigma,K^{\prime}}&\text{if }k\,\text{near }K^{\prime}\end{cases},

(E30)

and accounting for the
V_{c}
and
V_{x}
terms, we find that the following effective interacting Hamiltonian

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{\rm int}&=\frac{V_{c}}{N_{s}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,q,p\\
V=K,K^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\psi_{k,\uparrow,V}^{\dagger}\psi_{p-k,\downarrow,V}^{\dagger}\psi_{p-q,\downarrow,V}\psi_{q,\uparrow,V}+\frac{V_{c}-V_{x}}{N_{s}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,q,p\\
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow\end{subarray}}\psi_{k,\sigma,K}^{\dagger}\psi_{p-k,\sigma,K^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\psi_{p-q,\sigma,K^{\prime}}\psi_{q,\sigma,K}\\
&+\frac{V_{c}}{N_{s}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,q,p\\
V=K,K^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\psi_{k,\uparrow,V}^{\dagger}\psi_{p-k,\downarrow,\bar{V}}^{\dagger}\psi_{p-q,\downarrow,\bar{V}}\psi_{q,\uparrow,V}+\frac{V_{x}}{N_{s}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,q,p\\
V=K,K^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\psi_{k,\uparrow,V}^{\dagger}\psi_{p-k,\downarrow,\bar{V}}^{\dagger}\psi_{p-q,\downarrow,V}\psi_{q,\uparrow,\bar{V}}.\end{split}

(E31)

Let us rearrange these terms in terms of pair operators to make their physical meaning clearer. Valley-polarized spin-singlet electron pairs
S_{V}=f_{V,\downarrow}f_{V,\uparrow}
with
V=K,K^{\prime}
only feel the valley conserving term and therefore exhibit repulsive interaction (
V_{c}>0
). When incoming electrons occupy opposite valleys, the ferromagnetic exchange leads to a total interaction strength
V_{c}+(-1)^{S}V_{x}
depending on the total spin
S
of the pair. As a consequence, the last spin-singlet valley-triplet channel
S_{0}=(f_{K^{\prime},\downarrow}f_{K,\uparrow}-f_{K^{\prime},\uparrow}f_{K,\downarrow})/\sqrt{2}
is also repulsive (
V_{c}+V_{x}>0
).
On the contrary, the three valley-singlet spin-triplet pair states,
T_{\sigma}=f_{K^{\prime},\sigma}f_{K,\sigma}
with
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
and
T_{0}=(f_{K^{\prime},\downarrow}f_{K,\uparrow}+f_{K^{\prime},\uparrow}f_{K,\downarrow})/\sqrt{2}
, all display a low-energy interaction strength
V_{c}-V_{x}=9(V-2\lambda)
, which is negative for a wide range of parameter, as shown in Fig.
3
a.
To summarize, we can rewrite the different contact interaction terms as

\tilde{H}=\int{\rm d}x\sum_{\sigma,V}\psi_{\sigma,V}^{\dagger}\left[\frac{-\nabla^{2}}{2m}\right]\psi_{\sigma,V}+\int\frac{{\rm d}x}{\mathcal{A}}[(V_{c}-V_{x})(T_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}T_{\downarrow}+T_{0}^{\dagger}T_{0}+T_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}T_{\uparrow})+V_{c}(S_{K^{\prime}}^{\dagger}S_{K^{\prime}}+S_{K}^{\dagger}S_{K})+(V_{c}+V_{x})S_{0}^{\dagger}S_{0}],

(E32)

with
\mathcal{A}=2/\sqrt{3}a^{2}
the Brillouin zone area.
This effective field theory describes a four-component Fermi liquid with repulsive interactions in the spin-singlet channel, owing to the large on-site interaction
U
which appears in both
V_{c}
and in
(V_{c}+V_{x})
, and attractive interaction between fermions with total spin one when
V_{c}-V_{x}<0
(see Fig.
3
a). This is the conclusion drawn in Eq.
7
of the main text.

Alternatively, we can replace pair operators by more physical quantities, such as the total density on each valley
\rho_{V}=\psi_{\uparrow,V}^{\dagger}\psi_{\uparrow,V}+\psi_{\downarrow,V}^{\dagger}\psi_{\downarrow,V}
and the total spin on each valley
\bm{s}_{V}=\psi_{\alpha,V}^{\dagger}\bm{\sigma}_{\alpha,\beta}\psi_{\beta,V}
. Together, they allow to represent the exchange term as

T_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}T_{\downarrow}+T_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}T_{\uparrow}+T_{0}^{\dagger}T_{0}-S_{0}^{\dagger}S_{0}=2\bm{s}_{K}\cdot\bm{s}_{K^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{K}\rho_{K^{\prime}}.

(E33)

The valley conserving terms present in all interaction channels can be simply with the total density
\rho_{\rm tot}=\rho_{K}+\rho_{K^{\prime}}

T_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}T_{\downarrow}+T_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}T_{\uparrow}+T_{0}^{\dagger}T_{0}+S_{0}^{\dagger}S_{0}+S_{K}^{\dagger}S_{K}+S_{K^{\prime}}^{\dagger}S_{K^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\rm tot}(\rho_{\rm tot}-1).

(E34)

Together, they allow to rewrite the interaction part of the continuum Hamiltonian as

\tilde{H}_{i}=\frac{1}{2\mathcal{A}}\int{\rm d}x\left[V_{c}\,n_{\rm tot}(n_{\rm tot}-1)-V_{x}\left(4\bm{s}_{K}\cdot\bm{s}_{K^{\prime}}+n_{K}n_{K^{\prime}}\right)\right].

(E35)

This forms makes clear the ferromagnetic interactions between opposite valleys, which are responsible for the formation of triplet pairs. Expanding the total density as a function of
\rho_{K}
and
\rho_{K^{\prime}}
, we find the three coupling constant given in Eq.
6
of the main text

g_{0}=V_{c}/(2\mathcal{A}),\quad g_{1}=(2V_{c}-V_{x})/(2\mathcal{A}),\quad g_{2}=-2V_{x}/\mathcal{A}.

(E36)

S4
Mean-field theory of superconductivity

In this appendix, we carry out a mean-field treatment of the effective Hamiltonian Eq.
E26
to investigate its superconducting behavior. With the mean-field substitution
f_{q^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}f_{q,\sigma}\simeq\delta_{q+q^{\prime}}\langle f_{q^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}f_{q,\sigma}\rangle
, we get the following quadratic mean-field approximation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm mf}=\sum_{k,\sigma}\xi_{q}f_{q,\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{q,\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\left[\tilde{\Delta}_{k,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}f_{k,\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{-k,\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+hc\right],

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{k,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=-\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}V_{k,q}\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

(E37)

with
\xi_{k}=\xi_{-k}=\varepsilon_{k}-\mu
and
\mu
the chemical potential.
It can be rewritten as a sum over a halved Brillouin Zone (denoted with primed sums and products below):

\mathcal{H}_{\rm mf}=\sum_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}\begin{bmatrix}f_{k}^{\dagger}&f_{-k}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{k}&\Delta_{k}\\
\Delta_{k}^{\dagger}&-\xi_{k}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}f_{k}\\
f_{-k}^{\dagger}\end{bmatrix}\,.

(E38)

The order parameters have been gathered in a
2\times 2
matrix

\Delta_{k}=\frac{\tilde{\Delta}_{k}-\tilde{\Delta}_{-k}^{T}}{2}=\frac{-1}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}{\rm Re}\,(V_{k,q})\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

(E39)

and should be computed self-consistently.

S4.1
Pairing symmetries

The explicit expression of
V_{k,q}
allows to decompose this order parameter into spin-singlet and spin-triplet components

\Delta_{k}=\Delta_{s}^{\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\Delta_{j}^{s}\cos(k\cdot a_{j})+\Delta_{j}^{t}\sin(k\cdot a_{j}),

(E40)

which respectively read:

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\left[U+2\tilde{t}(c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3})\right]\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}^{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\left[\lambda(c_{j-1}+c_{j+1})+Vc_{j}+\tilde{t}\right]\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}^{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\left[\lambda(s_{j-1}+s_{j+1})-Vs_{j}\right]\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

(E41)

with
c_{j}=\cos(q\cdot a_{j})
and
s_{j}=\sin(q\cdot a_{j})
.
We can further split these order parameters in terms of irreducible representation of
C_{3v}
that they represent on the triangular lattice.
For the singlet and triplet case, there are two one-dimensional irrep, only one of which can be obtained because of the particular form of
V_{k,q}
and one two dimensional irrep. The two former measure the strength of s-wave and f-wave pairing

\Delta_{s}^{\prime},\quad\Delta_{s/f}=\frac{1}{3}\left[\Delta_{1}^{s/t}+\Delta_{2}^{s/t}+\Delta_{3}^{s/t}\right],

(E42)

while the two dimensional irrep are related to d-wave and p-wave pairing

\displaystyle\Delta_{d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}/p_{x}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\left[\Delta_{1}^{s/t}+\Delta_{2}^{s/t}-2\Delta_{3}^{s/t}\right],

(E43)

\displaystyle\Delta_{d_{xy}/p_{y}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\Delta_{1}^{s/t}-\Delta_{2}^{s/t}\right].

(E44)

The inverse transformations are

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{s/f}+\Delta_{d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}/p_{x}}+\Delta_{d_{xy}/p_{y}},

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{s/f}+\Delta_{d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}/p_{x}}-\Delta_{d_{xy}/p_{y}},

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{s/f}-2\Delta_{d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}/p_{x}}.

(E45)

Finally, singlet pairs cannot be of equal spin, and we can therefore express them as a scalar times the
2\times 2
matrix
i\sigma_{y}
,
e.g.

\Delta_{s}=d_{0}(i\sigma_{y})
with
\delta_{s}
a complex number.
Triplet on the other hand, take the form of a Pauli vector multiplied by
i\sigma_{y}
,
e.g.

\Delta_{f}=(\bm{d}_{f}\cdot\bm{\sigma})(i\sigma_{y})
.

Our mean-field treatment relies on the self-consistent computation of four scalars related to singlet pairing in s-wave (
\Delta_{s}
,
\Delta_{s}^{\prime}
) of d-wave (
\Delta_{d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}}
,
\Delta_{d_{xy}}
), and three vectors describing f-wave (
\Delta_{f}
) or p-wave (
\Delta_{p_{x}}
,
\Delta_{p_{y}}
) pairs. The corresponding self-consistent equations become

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}^{\prime}=\frac{-1}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\left[U+2\tilde{t}(c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3})\right]\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}=\frac{-2}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\left[\tilde{t}+\frac{2\lambda+V}{3}(c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3})\right]\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Delta_{p_{x}}=\frac{-2(\lambda+V)}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\frac{s_{1}+s_{2}-2s_{3}}{6}\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Delta_{p_{y}}=\frac{-2(\lambda+V)}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{2}\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Delta_{d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}}=\frac{2(\lambda-V)}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\frac{c_{1}+c_{2}-2c_{3}}{6}\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Delta_{d_{xy}}=\frac{2(\lambda-V)}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{2}\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}=\frac{2(2\lambda-V)}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\frac{s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}}{3}\langle f_{q,\sigma}f_{-q,\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle.

(E46)

S4.2
Self-consistent conditions

The mean field quadratic Hamiltonian can be diagonalized by a Bogoliubov transformation.
Writing the hermitian matrix

\Delta_{q}\Delta_{q}^{\dagger}=a_{0}+\bm{a}\cdot\bm{\sigma}

(E47)

as a Pauli vector, the eigen-energies read

E_{q,\pm}=\sqrt{\xi_{q}^{2}+a_{0}\pm|\bm{a}|}.

(E48)

The corresponding eigenvectors lead to the following expression for the anomalous correlators

\langle f_{q}f_{-q}^{T}\rangle=\left[g_{q}^{+}+g_{q}^{-}\frac{\bm{a}\cdot\bm{\sigma}}{|a|}\right]\frac{\Delta_{q}}{2},

(E49)

with

g_{q}^{\pm}=\frac{g(E_{q,+})\pm g(E_{q,-})}{2},\;g(E)=\frac{\tanh(\beta E/2)}{E}.

(E50)

When
\Delta_{q}\Delta_{q}^{\dagger}
is simply proportional to the identity, for instance when pairing occurs for spin-singlet,
E_{q,+}=E_{q,-}=E_{q}
and the previous expression simply becomes
\langle f_{q}f_{-q}^{T}\rangle=g(E_{q})\Delta_{q}/2
.

S4.3
Critical temperature

Solving the self-consistent relations of Eq.
S4.1
with the help of Eq.
E49
allows to determine the nature of the superconducting state.
We now consider each pairing channel separately to check a superconducting phase can fully form.

S4.3.1
s-wave

The possibility of an s-wave SC order can be ruled out because
U
is much larger than all the other terms scaling as
t_{0}^{2}/\Delta
. The coupled gap equations for
\Delta_{s}
and
\Delta_{s}^{\prime}
linearized near
T_{c}
read

\begin{bmatrix}\Delta_{s}^{\prime}\\
\Delta_{s}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}UI_{0}+2\tilde{t}I_{1}&UI_{1}+2\tilde{t}I_{2}\\
2\tilde{t}I_{0}+\frac{2(2\lambda+V)}{3}I_{1}&2\tilde{t}I_{1}+\frac{2(2\lambda+V)}{3}I_{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\Delta_{s}^{\prime}\\
\Delta_{s}\end{bmatrix},

(E51)

where
I_{k}=-\sum_{q}\tanh(\beta E_{q}/2)(c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3})^{k}/(2N_{s}E_{q})
and
\beta
the inverse temperature. The matrix in the previous equation must have at least one eigenvalue equal to one for the system to exhibit s-wave symmetry. However, to leading order in
U
, this requires to have

U\left[\frac{2(2\lambda+V)}{3}(I_{0}I_{2}-I_{1}^{2})-I_{0}\right]=0.

(E52)

This equation has no solution because
(-I_{0})>0
has the same sign as
I_{0}I_{2}-I_{1}^{2}>0
(due to Cauchy-Schwarz iequality).
Thus, singlet pairing does not occur happen. It could nevertheless appear for smaller ratios
\Delta/t_{0}
where our perturbation theory breaks down.

S4.3.2
p-wave

The possibility of p-wave pairing can be ruled out as well. Indeed, let’s assume a p-wave SC order and compute the critical temperature
T_{c}
of that state.
Linearizing the gap equation, such that
\bm{a}\simeq\bm{0}
and
E_{q,\pm}\simeq|\xi_{q}|
, we find the coupled equations

\begin{bmatrix}\Delta_{p_{x}}\\
\Delta_{p_{y}}\end{bmatrix}=-\frac{V+\lambda}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\frac{\tanh(\beta E_{q}/2)}{E_{q}}\begin{bmatrix}(s_{1}+s_{2}-2s_{3})^{2}/6&(s_{1}+s_{2}-2s_{3})(s_{1}-s_{2})/6\\
(s_{1}-s_{2})(s_{1}+s_{2}-2s_{3})/2&(s_{1}-s_{2})^{2}/2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\Delta_{p_{x}}\\
\Delta_{p_{y}}\end{bmatrix}\,.

(E53)

Noting that
E_{q}=|\xi_{q}|
is
C_{3}
invariant, while the off-diagonal terms of the equation are not, we can rewrite the diagonal terms as:

\frac{-6}{V+\lambda}=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\frac{\tanh(\beta E_{q}/2)}{E_{q}}\sum_{j}(s_{j}-s_{j+1})^{2},

(E54)

which does not have any solution since the left and right hand sides have opposite signs.

S4.3.3
d- and f-wave

\frac{6}{\lambda-V}=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}\frac{\tanh(\beta E_{q}/2)}{E_{q}}\sum_{j}(c_{j}-c_{j+1})^{2}.

(E55)

(E56)

As explained in the main text,

When f- and d- wave superconductivity may coexist, we generically expect the f-wave critical temperature to dominate due to a larger interaction strength

. This is confirmed by our numerical solution of Eqs.

and

S4.4
Zero temperature superconducting gap

As discussed in the main text, the superconducting gap for f-wave and spin-triplet symmetry may be written as
\Delta_{k}=(\bm{d}_{k}\cdot\bm{\sigma})(i\sigma_{y})
with
\bm{d}_{k}=[\sum_{j}\sin(k\cdot a_{j})]\bm{d}_{f}
and
\bm{d}_{f}
a constant vector that only depends on temperature. Owing to the spin-rotation symmetry of our model, we can choose
\bm{d}_{f}
to lie in the
xy
plane at on point of the Brillouin zone. Moreover, we can eliminate any phase difference between
\Delta_{k}^{\uparrow\uparrow}
and
\Delta_{k}^{\downarrow\downarrow}
, which yields
\bm{d}_{f}=e^{i\theta}(d_{x},id_{y},0)^{T}
with
d_{x,y}
real. With this order parameter, eigen-energies of the mean-field Hamiltonian take the rather simple form

E_{k,\pm}=\sqrt{\xi_{k}^{2}+s_{k}^{2}|d_{x}\pm d_{y}|^{2}}.

(E57)

The self-consistent equation for such symmetry (see Eq.
S4.1
) gives

\begin{bmatrix}d_{x}\\
d_{y}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{2\lambda-V}{3}\begin{bmatrix}a^{+}&a^{-}\\
a^{-}&a^{+}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}d_{x}\\
d_{y}\end{bmatrix},

(E58)

with

a^{\pm}=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{q}s_{q}^{2}g_{q}^{\pm},\quad s_{q}=\sum_{j}\sin(q\cdot a_{j}).

(E59)

To have a non-zero solution, we must have

\left|a^{+}-\frac{3}{2\lambda-V}\right|=|a^{-}|.

(E60)

The solution of this equation with the large total gap
|\bm{d}|
necessarily has
a^{-}=0
. Because
s_{q}^{2}g_{q}^{-}
has the same sign in the entire Brillouin zone, this implies that
E_{q,+}=E_{q,-}
for all momenta. Hence,
d_{x}=0
or
d_{y}=0
in the entire Brillouin zone. In both cases, the net spin carried by the Cooper pairs is zero
Leggett 1975
:

\langle\bm{S}\rangle=\langle i\bm{d}\times\bm{d}^{*}\rangle=0.

(E61)

As a consistency check, we have numerically solved the self-consistent Eq.
E58
and reached the same conclusion. For all parameters considered, our results show
d_{y}=0
, as exemplified in Fig.
S2
for
U_{B}/V_{0}=2
and
U_{B}/V_{0}=4
.

d_{x}

S5
Weakly interacting regime

S5.1
Explicit unitary transformation

We start by isolating the band mixing (or off-diagonal) interaction elements from the others
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\rm od}+\mathcal{V}_{\rm d}
.
More explicitly, we write
\mathcal{V}_{\rm od}=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{C(1234)}V_{43}^{21}\delta_{43}^{21}c_{4}^{\dagger}c_{3}^{\dagger}c_{2}c_{1}
, with
C(1234)
restricting the sum to terms satisfying either
b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}b_{4}=-1
, or having
b_{1}=b_{2}
and
b_{3}=b_{4}
with
b_{1}\neq b_{2}
.

To eliminate these band mixing interaction terms, we use a Schrieffer-Wolff transformation
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=e^{S}\mathcal{H}e^{-S}
, with
S
anti-hermitian.
This unitary transformation can be carried order by order in the small parameter
|\mathcal{V}|/\Delta
, and we write
S=S_{1}+S_{2}+\cdots
with
S_{n}=\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{V}|^{n}/\Delta^{n})
.
Requiring

[H_{0},S_{1}]=\mathcal{V}_{\rm od},

(E62)

gets rid of the direct band-mixing terms in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}+[S,\mathcal{H}]+\frac{1}{2}[S,[S,\mathcal{H}]]+\mathcal{O}(S^{2}\mathcal{H})

(E63)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{0}+\mathcal{V}_{\rm d}+\left[S_{1},\mathcal{V}_{\rm d}+\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\rm od}}{2}\right]+[S_{2},H_{0}]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{|\mathcal{V}|^{3}}{\Delta^{2}}\right).

A possible
S_{1}
satisfying this condition is

S_{1}=\sum_{C(1234)}\frac{V_{43}^{21}\delta_{43}^{21}}{\varepsilon_{4}+\varepsilon_{3}-\varepsilon_{2}-\varepsilon_{1}}c_{4}^{\dagger}c_{3}^{\dagger}c_{2}c_{1}.

(E64)

We can then choose
[S_{2},H_{0}]
to remove the band mixing terms of
[S_{1},\mathcal{V}_{\rm d}+\mathcal{V}_{\rm od}/2]
, and so on.
Note that, because
\mathcal{V}_{\rm d}
contains no band mixing terms, such that
[S_{1},\mathcal{V}_{\rm d}]
is purely off diagonal and therefore completely eliminated by
[S_{2},H_{0}]
.
The second order corrections read
[S_{1},\mathcal{V}_{\rm od}]/2
, which as promised are of order
|\mathcal{V}|^{2}/\Delta
due to the special form of
S_{1}
.

Finally, the effective Hamiltonian for doped charge is obtained by projecting
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
to the subspace where the lower band is fully filled, which amounts to pairing up lower band indices as
c_{a}^{\dagger}c_{b}\to\tilde{\delta}_{a,b}
.

S5.2
Leading corrections

To make analytical progress, we need the explicit form of the dispersion and scattering vertex.
The single particle energy dispersion reads

\varepsilon_{{\bf k},\pm,\sigma}=\varepsilon_{{\bf k},\pm}=\pm\sqrt{(\Delta_{0}/2)^{2}+|t_{0}f({\bf k})|^{2}},

(E65)

with
f({\bf k})=\sum_{j=1}^{3}e^{i({\bf k}\cdot{\bf u}_{j})}
, and
{\bf u}_{j=1,2,3}
the vectors connecting
B
sites to their three nearest neighbors.
The corresponding Bloch eigenvectors are

\Psi_{{\bf k},\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}(\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}\pm\Delta_{0}/2)}}\begin{bmatrix}\mp t_{0}f({\bf k})\\
\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}\pm\Delta_{0}/2\end{bmatrix},

(E66)

They allow us to write the interaction vertex in the band basis as

\begin{split}&V_{43}^{21}=V_{0}f({\bf k}_{4}-{\bf k}_{1})\left[\Psi_{{\bf k}_{4},b_{4}}^{A}\Psi_{{\bf k}_{3},b_{3}}^{B}\right]^{*}\Psi_{{\bf k}_{2},b_{2}}^{B}\Psi_{{\bf k}_{1},b_{1}}^{A}\\
&+\!\!\!\sum_{\tau=A/B}\!\!\!\frac{U_{\tau}}{2}\delta_{(\sigma_{4}=\sigma_{1})\neq(\sigma_{3}=\sigma_{2})}\left[\Psi_{{\bf k}_{4},b_{4}}^{\tau}\Psi_{{\bf k}_{3},b_{3}}^{\tau}\right]^{*}\Psi_{{\bf k}_{2},b_{2}}^{\tau}\Psi_{{\bf k}_{1},b_{1}}^{\tau}.\end{split}

(E67)

The leading corrections to the band dispersion come from
\mathcal{V}_{\rm d}
and are given by Eq.
16
in the main text.
The Hartree-like part, corresponding to the first line of Eq.
16
, takes the explicit form

\begin{split}\delta\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}^{\rm H}&=(6V_{0}C_{-}+U_{A}C_{+})\frac{\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}-\Delta_{0}/2}{2\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}}\\
&+(6V_{0}C_{+}+U_{B}C_{-})\frac{\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}+\Delta_{0}/2}{2\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}},\end{split}

(E68)

with
C_{\pm}=(2N_{s})^{-1}\sum_{{\bf q}}(\varepsilon_{{\bf q},+}\pm\Delta_{0}/2)/\varepsilon_{{\bf q},+}
.
The Fock-like term, second line of Eq.
16
, reads

\delta\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}^{\rm F}=\frac{t_{0}^{2}V_{0}}{2N_{s}}{\rm Re}\,\left[\frac{f^{*}({\bf k})}{\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}}\sum_{{\bf q}}f({\bf k}-{\bf q})\frac{f({\bf q})}{\varepsilon_{{\bf q},+}}\right].

(E69)

These corrections are plotted together with the bare band dispersion for
\Delta_{0}=t=2U_{A}=2U_{B}=10V_{0}
in Fig.
S3
, where we observe that they both admit degenerate minima at the
K
and
K^{\prime}
points.

Figure S3:

Bare dispersion
\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}
and its leading correction
\delta\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}
(multiplied 40 times for visibility) in the weakly interacting limit, shown for
\Delta_{0}=t=2U_{A}=2U_{B}=10V_{0}
.

When
t_{0}\ll\Delta_{0}
, we can approximate
\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}\simeq\Delta_{0}/2+|t_{0}f({\bf k})|^{2}/\Delta_{0}
and expand these corrections in powers of
t_{0}/V_{0}
.
Without too much difficulty, we end up with

\displaystyle\delta\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}^{\rm H}

\displaystyle=\frac{|t_{0}f({\bf k})|^{2}}{\Delta_{0}^{2}}(U_{A}-6V_{0}),

(E70)

\displaystyle\delta\varepsilon_{{\bf k},+}^{\rm F}

\displaystyle=\frac{2t_{0}^{2}V_{0}}{\Delta_{0}^{2}N_{s}}{\rm Re}\,\left[f^{*}({\bf k})\sum_{{\bf q}}f({\bf k}-{\bf q})f({\bf q})\right],

up to an overall global constant.
These expressions allow to obtain the effective mass by expanding around the
K
or
K^{\prime}
point.
For that purpose, we recall
f(K+{\bf k})\simeq\sqrt{3}(k_{x}+ik_{y})a/2
and furthermore find that

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf q}}f(K+{\bf k}-{\bf q})f({\bf q})=\sum_{{\bf q}}f({\bf k}-{\bf q})f({\bf q}+K)

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{{\bf q}}f(-{\bf q})f({\bf q}+K)+{\bf k}\cdot\sum_{{\bf q}}(\nabla f)(-{\bf q})f({\bf q}+K)

\displaystyle=0+\sqrt{3}(k_{x}+ik_{y})a/2,

(E71)

where the last equality is easy to check numerically.
We end up with

(\varepsilon+\delta\varepsilon^{\rm H}+\delta\varepsilon^{\rm F})_{K+{\bf k},+}\simeq\frac{3t_{0}^{2}a^{2}|{\bf k}|^{2}}{4\Delta_{0}^{2}}(\Delta_{0}+U_{A}-4V_{0}),

(E72)

yielding the effective mass given in Eq.
16
.

We now turn to the corrections to the two-body scattering vertex, which are contained in the second order term
X=[S_{1},\mathcal{V}_{\rm od}]/2
.
Direct evaluation of the commutator using the explicit expression of
S_{1}
(Eq.
E64
) gives

X=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{123456}\Gamma_{654}^{321}\delta_{654}^{321}c_{6}^{\dagger}c_{5}^{\dagger}(2c_{4}^{\dagger}c_{3}-\delta_{3,4})c_{2}c_{1},

(E73)

where the indices
653
and
421
originates from the same interaction elements and thus satisfy the conditions given above for the elements of
\mathcal{V}_{\rm od}
and
S_{1}
.
The three-body tensor
\Gamma
takes the form

\displaystyle\Gamma_{654}^{321}=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{0}

\displaystyle\delta_{65}^{30}\delta_{40}^{21}(V_{65}^{03}-V_{65}^{30})(V_{04}^{21}-V_{40}^{21})

(E74)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\times\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{6}+\varepsilon_{5}-\varepsilon_{3}-\varepsilon_{0}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{4}-\varepsilon_{0}}\right].

For later use, we also define
\tilde{\Gamma}
and
\hat{\Gamma}
, which have the same explicit representation except that the sum over
0
is restricted to states in lower band
b_{0}=-
and the upper band
b_{0}=+
, respectively.
To find the two-body corrections from
X
, we can select the terms where two of the indices belong to the lower band and contract them.
Considering all possible pairs compatible with the constraints on
653
and
421
, we end up with

\displaystyle\delta V_{43}^{21}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i,b_{i}=-}\Gamma_{43i}^{i21}+\hat{\Gamma}_{i43}^{2i1}+\hat{\Gamma}_{i43}^{21i}+\hat{\Gamma}_{4i3}^{2i1}+\hat{\Gamma}_{4i3}^{21i}

(E75)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i,b_{i}=-}\tilde{\Gamma}_{i43}^{i21}+\tilde{\Gamma}_{4i3}^{i21}+\tilde{\Gamma}_{43i}^{2i1}+\tilde{\Gamma}_{43i}^{21i}-\sum_{i}\tilde{\Gamma}_{43i}^{i21}.

This expression greatly simplifies when we assume that the four momenta
{\bf k}_{1,2,3,4}
are equal to
K
or
K^{\prime}
, as we do to determine the effective interaction strength in the spin-triplet valley singlet channel
U_{0}
(see main text).
Because
\Psi_{K/K^{\prime},+}^{A}=0
, any term of the form
\Gamma_{654}^{321}
with
6=(K/K^{\prime},+)
or
1=(K/K^{\prime},+)
appearing in
U_{0}
vanishes (see Eq.
E74
).
This completely removes any contribution from on-site interactions on
A
sites
U_{A}
.
Let us first focus on the
V_{0}^{2}
contributions and set
U_{B}=0
for a moment.
This largely simplifies the expression of
U_{0}
, which now reads

\begin{split}U_{0}^{(1)}=2\sum_{q}&\hat{\Gamma}_{(q-)(K+)(K^{\prime}+)}^{(K^{\prime}+)(K+)(q-)}+\hat{\Gamma}_{(q-)(K^{\prime}+)(K+)}^{(K+)(K^{\prime}+)(q-)}\\
&-\hat{\Gamma}_{(q-)(K+)(K^{\prime}+)}^{(K+)(K^{\prime}+)(q-)}-\hat{\Gamma}_{(q-)(K^{\prime}+)(K+)}^{(K^{\prime}+)(K+)(q-)}.\end{split}

(E76)

The two first terms of the sum involve interaction coefficients with momentum transfer
K
and
K^{\prime}
, respectively, which makes them vanish as
f(K)=f(K^{\prime})=0
(see Eq.
E67
).
The other terms contribute equally, and we finally obtain

U_{0}^{(1)}=-\frac{36t_{0}^{2}V_{0}^{2}}{N_{s}}\sum_{{\bf q}}\frac{|f({\bf q})|^{2}}{(2\varepsilon_{{\bf q},+})^{3}}.

(E77)

It is not difficult to check that the contribution proportional to
U_{B}^{2}
vanishes, and we now turn to the crossed
V_{0}U_{B}
corrections.
The calculation proceeds in a similar way, except that one of the interaction element
3V_{0}
is replaced by
U_{B}
and the signs needs to be flipped, such that the exchange part gives

U_{0}^{(2)}=\frac{12t_{0}^{2}V_{0}U_{B}}{N_{s}}\sum_{{\bf q}}\frac{|f({\bf q})|^{2}}{(2\varepsilon_{{\bf q},+})^{3}}.

(E78)

For
t_{0}\ll\Delta_{0}
, we use
\sum_{{\bf q}}|f({\bf q})|^{2}=3N_{s}
to find the simpler form

U_{0}=\frac{36t_{0}^{2}V_{0}(U_{B}-3V_{0})}{\Delta_{0}^{3}},

(E79)

which – remarkably – exactly match the result of the kinetic expansion in the weakly interacting regime

U_{0}^{\rm KE}=V_{c}-V_{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(U_{A},U_{B},V_{0}\ll\Delta_{0})}}{{\simeq}}\frac{36t_{0}^{2}V_{0}(U_{B}-3V_{0})}{\Delta_{0}^{3}}.

(E80)

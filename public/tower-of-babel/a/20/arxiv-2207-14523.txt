The main objective of this note is to give a new and simple proof of
[

(1)

1
Prerequisites

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic. Let
f\in K[[x,y]]
be a non-zero power series without constant term. The power series
f
is reduced if it has not multiple factors.

In what follows we consider the equisingularity invariants of a reduced plane curve
\{f(x,y)=0\}
(see
[
Pf-Pł
]
):
r(f)
is the number of irreducible factors of
f
,
c(f)=\dim_{K}\overline{\cal O}_{f}/{\cal C}
is the
degree of the conductor
, where
{\cal O}_{f}=K[[x,y]]/(f)
,
\overline{{\cal O}_{f}}
is the integral closure of
{\cal O}_{f}
in the total quotient ring of
{\cal O}_{f}
and
{\cal C}
is the
conductor
of
{\cal O}_{f}
, that is the largest ideal in
{\cal O}_{f}
which remains an ideal in
\overline{{\cal O}_{f}}
. Finally the
delta invariant
of
f
is

\delta(f)=\dim_{K}\overline{\cal O}_{f}/{\cal O}_{f}
. Since
{\cal O}_{f}
is Gorenstein we get
c(f)=2\delta(f)
.

If
f\in K[[x,y]]
is irreducible then the
semigroup of values
of
f(x,y)
, denoted by
\Gamma(f)
, is defined as the set of intersection multiplicities
i_{0}(f,g)=\dim_{K}K[[x,y]]/(f,g)
where
g
runs over all power series in
K[[x,y]]

such that
g\not\equiv 0
(mod
f
). This semigroup is numerical, that is
\mathbb{N}\backslash\Gamma(f)
is a finite set. Denote by
c
the
conductor
of
\Gamma(f)
, that is, the smallest element of
\Gamma(f)
such that
c+N\in\Gamma(f)
for any nonnegative integer
N
. The semigroup
\Gamma(f)
admits a minimal system of generators
v_{0}<v_{1}<\cdots<v_{g}
such that
\gcd(v_{0},\ldots,v_{g})=1
. We write
\Gamma(f)=\langle v_{0},\ldots,v_{g}\rangle
. Put
e_{i}:=\gcd(v_{0},\ldots,v_{i})
for
0\leq i\leq h
and
n_{i}=\frac{e_{i-1}}{e_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq g
.

If
f
is irreducible then
c(f)
equals to the conductor
c
of the semigroup
\Gamma(f)
. Consequently

c(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{g}(n_{k}-1)v_{k}-v_{0}+1
.

Let
\mu(f)
be the
Milnor number
of
f
defined as the codimension of the ideal generated by

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x},\frac{\partial f}{\partial y},
that is
\mu(f)=i_{0}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x},\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)
. The
invariant Milnor number
of
f
is defined to be

\bar{\mu}(f)=2\delta(f)-r(f)+1=c(f)-r(f)+1
(see
[
GB-Pł2
]
). If
p=0
then
\bar{\mu}(f)=\mu(f)
(the Milnor formula). If
p>0

\mu(f)
is not an invariant and
\bar{\mu}(f)
plays the role of
\mu(f)
.
Melle and Wall
[
MH-W
]
, based on a result of Deligne
[
D
]
, proved that
\mu(f)\geq\bar{\mu}(f)
.

Any plane reduced curve
\{f(x,y)=0\}
is called a
tame singularity
if
\mu(f)=\bar{\mu}(f)
. If the characteristic of
K
is zero any singularity of plane reduced curve is tame.

Proposition 1.1

1.

For any unit

.

2.

For every reduced power series

See
[
GB-Pł1
, Proposition 1.2, Remark 2.2]
.

If the characteristic of

, page 63]

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p\geq 0
. Consider
f\in K[[x,y]]
a nonzero power series without constant term. Write
f=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}

of

\mathrm{supp}\,f=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\;:\;c_{\alpha\beta}\neq 0\}

\Delta(f)
of

is the convex hull
of
\mathrm{supp}\,f+(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{2}
. The

\mathcal{N}_{f}
of

is the set of compact faces of the boundary of

. We put
|\mathcal{N}_{f}|_{1}=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{N}_{f}}|S|_{1}
,

,

A power series
f\in K[[x,y]]
is

f(x,0)f(0,y)\neq 0
; otherwise we will say that

is

. When

(m,0)

Let

. Recall that the

of
f
is
\mbox{\rm ord}f=\min\{\alpha+\beta\;:\;c_{\alpha\beta}\neq 0\}
and the

of

.

For any segment

we put
\mbox{\rm in}(f,S)=\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\in S}c_{\alpha\beta}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}

. Then

is

Remark 2.1

A power series

is non-degenerate if and only if for any segment

Lemma 2.5

Lemma 2.7
(Newton factorization)

Let
f\in K[[x,y]]
be convenient. Then
f=\prod_{S\in\mathcal{N}_{f}}f_{S}
in
K[[x,y]]
where
f_{S}
are elementary. Moreover, the bidegree of

is
(|S|_{1},|S|_{2})
and
\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}f_{S}=c\cdot\overline{\mbox{\rm in}}(f,S)
, for some
c\in K\backslash\{0\}
.

Proof.
Firstly we prove that any convenient power series is a product of elementary power series. If
f
is elementary of bidegree
(m,n)
then we put
I(f)=\frac{m}{n}
. Let
f=f_{1}\cdots f_{r}
be the factorization into irreducible factors of a convenient power series. Let
\{I(f_{i})\;:\;1\leq i\leq r\}=\{\omega_{j}\;:\;1\leq j\leq s\}
where
\omega_{1}<\omega_{2}<\cdots<\omega_{s}
. For any

we put
A_{j}:=\{k\in\{1,\ldots,s\}\;:\;I(f_{k})=\omega_{j}\}
and

. Then

is an elementary power series and

with

for any

. Let
\mbox{\rm bideg}(g_{k})=(m_{k},n_{k})
. Since

the points

(by convention the empty sum equals zero) are vertices of

. Let

be the segment of
\mathcal{N}_{f}
with vertices

and

for

, so

.
If

then

for some

and we put

. Therefore

where

are elementary,

and
\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}f_{S}=c\cdot\overline{\mbox{\rm in}}(f,S)
for some

.

are non-degenerate for any
S\in\mathcal{N}_{f}.

For any two power series
f,g\in K[[x,y]]
we put
i_{0}(f,g):=\dim_{K}K[[x,y]]/(f,g)
and call it the
intersection multiplicity
of
f
and
g
.

Lemma 2.9

If
\mathcal{N}_{f}=\{S\}
and
\mathcal{N}_{g}=\{T\}
are elementary then

i_{0}(f,g)\geq[S,T]
with equality if and only if
S
and
T
are not parallel or the system of equations
\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}f=0
,
\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}g=0
has the unique solution
(x,y)=(0,0)
.

Proof.
Put
\mbox{\rm bideg}(f):=(m,n)
and
\mbox{\rm bideg}(g):=(m_{1},n_{1})
. We have to check that
i_{0}(f,g)\geq\min\{mn_{1},m_{1}n\}
with equality if and only if
\frac{m}{n}=\frac{m_{1}}{n_{1}}
or the system of equations

\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}f=0
,
\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}g=0
has the only solution
(x,y)=(0,0)
. Put
f(x,y)=\sum_{ij}a_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
. Let
\overrightarrow{w}=(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{2}
. Then

\mbox{\rm ord}_{\overrightarrow{w}}(f):=\inf\{ni+jm\;:\;a_{ij}\neq 0\}=nm
and

\mbox{\rm in}_{\overrightarrow{w}}f:=\sum_{in+jm=nm}a_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}=\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}f
. Let us distinguish two cases.

Case 1:

\frac{m}{n}\neq\frac{m_{1}}{n_{1}}.
We may assume
\frac{m}{n}<\frac{m_{1}}{n_{1}}.
Then
\mbox{\rm ord}_{\overrightarrow{w}}(g)=mn_{1}=\min\{mn_{1},m_{1}n\}
and
\mbox{\rm in}_{\overrightarrow{w}}g=cy^{n_{1}}
for
c\neq 0
. Therefore the system of equations
\mbox{\rm in}_{\overrightarrow{w}}f=0
and
\mbox{\rm in}_{\overrightarrow{w}}g=0
has the unique solution
(x,y)=(0,0)
and we get

i_{0}(f,g)=\frac{\mbox{\rm ord}_{\overrightarrow{w}}f{\mbox{\rm ord}_{\overrightarrow{w}}g}}{mn}=\mbox{\rm ord}_{\overrightarrow{w}}g=mn_{1}=\min\{mn_{1},m_{1}n\},

by
[
GB-Pł2
, Lemma A.1]
.

Case 2:

\frac{m}{n}=\frac{m_{1}}{n_{1}}.
We check
\mbox{\rm ord}_{\overrightarrow{w}}(g)=mn_{1}
and
\mbox{\rm in}_{\overrightarrow{w}}g=\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}g
. Again by
[
GB-Pł2
, Lemma A.1]
we get
i_{0}(f,g)\geq\mbox{\rm ord}_{\overrightarrow{w}}g=mn_{1}=nm_{1}
with equality if the system
\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}f=0
,
\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}g=0
has the unique solution
(x,y)=(0,0)
.

3
Main result

The following theorem is the main result of this note:

Theorem 3.1

Let
f\in K[[x,y]]
be a reduced power series. Then

1.

\bar{\mu}(f)-\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f})\geq r(\mathcal{N}_{f})-r(f)\geq 0
.

2.

If
f
is non-degenerate then
\bar{\mu}(f)=\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f})
and
r(\mathcal{N}_{f})=r(f)
.

The first statement of Theorem
3.1
was proved in
[
Bo-G-M
, Proposition 7]
. We provide a new and simple proof of it.
The proof of Theorem
3.1
is given in Section
4
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.1
we have

Corollary 3.2

(
[
Bo-G-M
, Lemma 4]
)
Let
f\in K[[x,y]]
be a reduced power series. We have
r(f)\leq r(\mathcal{N}_{f})
and
if
f
is non-degenerate then
r(f)=r(\mathcal{N}_{f})
.

Corollary 3.3

(
[
Be-Pe
, Proposition 3.17]
,
[
Bo-G-M
, Proposition 5]
)
Let
f\in K[[x,y]]
. We have
\delta(\mathcal{N}_{f})\leq\delta(f)
and if

f
is non-degenerate then
\delta(\mathcal{N}_{f})=\delta(f)
.

Proof.
From the definition of the invariant Milnor number of
f
and the equality (
5
) we have
\bar{\mu}(f)-\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f})=2(\delta(f)-\delta(\mathcal{N}_{f}))+r(\mathcal{N}_{f})-r(f)
. We use Theorem
3.1
.

Corollary 3.4

(
[
Bo-G-M
, Theorem 9]
)
Let
f\in K[[x,y]]
be a reduced power series. If
f
is strongly non-degenerate then
f
is tame, i.e.,
\mu(f)=\bar{\mu}(f)
.

Proof.
By Kouchnirenko’s planar theorem
[
Bo-G-M
, Proposition 4]
we have
\mu(f)=\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f})
. On the other hand by Theorem
3.1
we get
\bar{\mu}(f)=\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f})
. Therefore
\mu(f)=\bar{\mu}(f)
.

4
Proof of the main result

We begin with the proof of Theorem
3.1
for convenient power series. Firstly we consider the case of elementary power series. Let
f\in K[[x,y]]
be an elementary power series of bidegree
(m,n)
. Let
d:=\gcd(m,n)
. Then the theorem reduces to the following statement:

\bar{\mu}(f)-(n-1)(m-1)\geq d-r(f)\geq 0.

(8)

If
f
is non-degenerate then
\bar{\mu}(f)=(n-1)(m-1)
and
r(f)=d
.

We distinguish two cases. Suppose first that
f
is irreducible, that is
r(f)=1
.

Lemma 4.1

Let
f\in K[[x,y]]
be irreducible with semigroup of values
\Gamma(f)=\langle v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{h}\rangle
. If
c
is the conductor of
\Gamma(f)
then
c\geq(v_{0}-1)(v_{1}-1)+\gcd(v_{0},v_{1})-1
. The equality
c=(v_{0}-1)(v_{1}-1)
holds if and only if
\gcd(v_{0},v_{1})=1
.

Proof.
Let us define Puiseux characteristic sequence
b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{h}
by putting
b_{0}=v_{0}
,
b_{k}=v_{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{h}(n_{i}-1)v_{i}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,h\}.
Note that
\gcd(b_{0},\ldots,b_{k})=e_{k}
for
k\in\{0,\ldots,h\}
and
b_{0}<b_{1}<\cdots<b_{h}
. Moreover
c=\sum_{k=1}^{h}(e_{k-1}-e_{k})(b_{k}-1)
(see for example
[
Pf-Pł
, Chapter 3, p. 58]
. If
e_{1}=1
then
c=(e_{0}-e_{1})(b_{1}-1)=(b_{0}-1)(b_{1}-1)
. Therefore we may assume that
h>1
. We have

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(e_{0}-e_{1})(b_{1}-1)+\sum_{k=2}^{h}(e_{k-1}-e_{k})(b_{k}-1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(e_{0}-e_{1})(b_{1}-1)+\sum_{k=2}^{h}(e_{k-1}-e_{k})(b_{2}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(e_{0}-e_{1})(b_{1}-1)+(e_{1}-1)(b_{2}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(e_{0}-e_{1})(b_{1}-1)+(e_{1}-1)(b_{2}-b_{1}+b_{1}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(e_{0}-1)(b_{1}-1)+(e_{1}-1)(b_{2}-b_{1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(b_{0}-1)(b_{1}-1)+e_{1}-1,\;\;\;\hbox{\rm since }b_{2}-b_{1}\geq 1.

Suppose that
r(f)=1
. Since
\bar{\mu}(f)=c(f)=c
we have, by Lemma
4.1
,
\bar{\mu}(f)\geq(v_{0}-1)(v_{1}-1)+\gcd(v_{0},v_{1})-1
. The power series
f
being unitangent we have
m=\mbox{\rm ord}f(0,y)=\mbox{\rm ord}f
or
n=\mbox{\rm ord}f(x,0)=\mbox{\rm ord}f
. Assume that
m=\mbox{\rm ord}f
. Then
m<n\leq v_{1}
(see
[
GB-Pł3
]
).
If the axis
y=0
has maximal contact with the curve
f(x,y)=0
then
n=v_{1}
and by Lemma
4.1
we get

\bar{\mu}(f)\geq(v_{0}-1)(v_{1}-1)-\gcd(v_{0},v_{1})+1=(m-1)(n-1)-d+1\geq 0.

If
n<v_{1}
then
n\equiv 0
(mod
m
),
d=\gcd(m,n)=m
and we get

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(f)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(v_{0}-1)(v_{1}-1)=(m-1)(v_{1}-n+n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m-1)(n-1)+(v_{1}-1)(m-1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(m-1)(n-1)+m-1=(m-1)(n-1)+d-1.

Suppose that
f
is non-degenerate. Then, by Corollary
2.6
,
d=\gcd(n,m)=1
. Consequently, by Lemma
4.1
,
\bar{\mu}(f)=(m-1)(n-1)+d-1
.

Suppose now that
f
is elementary but
r(f)>1
. Recall that any irreducible convenient power series is elementary.

Lemma 4.2

Let
f
be an elementary power series with
\mbox{\rm bideg}(f)=(m,n)
and
f=f_{1}\cdots f_{r}
the factorization of
f
into irreducible factors with
\mbox{\rm bideg}(f_{i})=(m_{i},n_{i})
. If
d=\gcd(m,n)
and
d_{i}=\gcd(m_{i},n_{i})
then

1.

\frac{m_{i}}{d_{i}}=\frac{m}{d}
and
\frac{n_{i}}{d_{i}}=\frac{n}{d}
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
.

2.

\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}=d
.

Moreover,
r\leq d
with equality if
f
is non-degenerate.

Proof.
By Lemma
2.5
we have
\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}f(x,y)=F(x^{m/d},y^{n/d})
for some homogeneous polynomial
F
of degree
d
.
Since
f_{i}
are elementary
\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}f(x,y)=\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}f_{1}(x,y)\cdots\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}f_{r}(x,y)
. By Lemma
2.5

\mbox{\sc{I}\rm n}f_{i}(x,y)=(a_{i}x^{\frac{m_{i}}{d_{i}}}+b_{i}y^{\frac{n_{i}}{d_{i}}})^{d_{i}}
for some
a_{i},b_{i}\in K
. Then
a_{i}x^{\frac{m_{i}}{d_{i}}}+b_{i}y^{\frac{n_{i}}{d_{i}}}
is an irreducible factor of
F(x^{m/d},y^{n/d})
, which implies
\frac{m_{i}}{d_{i}}=\frac{m}{d}
and
\frac{n_{i}}{d_{i}}=\frac{n}{d}
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
. Since
f(x,0)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}(x,0)
we have
m=\mbox{\rm ord}f(x,0)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mbox{\rm ord}f_{i}(x,0)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\frac{m}{d}
whence
\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}=d
. Obviously
r\leq d
. If
f
is non-degenerate then
f_{i}
are non-degenerate and
d_{i}=1
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
by Corollary
2.6
.
Therefore
r=d
.

By the third statement of Proposition
1.1
we get

\bar{\mu}(f)+r-1=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\bar{\mu}(f_{i})+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}i_{0}(f_{i},f_{j})
. By the irreducible elementary case we have
\bar{\mu}(f_{i})\geq\left(\dfrac{m}{d}d_{i}-1\right)\left(\dfrac{n}{d}d_{i}-1\right)+(d_{i}-1).
Moreover, by Lemma
2.9
,
i_{0}(f_{i},f_{j})\geq\dfrac{mn}{d^{2}}d_{i}d_{j}
. Therefore we get

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(f)+r-1

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left[\left(\frac{m}{d}d_{i}-1\right)\left(\frac{n}{d}d_{i}-1\right)+(d_{i}-1)\right]+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\frac{mn}{d^{2}}d_{i}d_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}^{2}+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}d_{i}d_{j}\right)+\left(\frac{-n-m}{d}+1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle mn-n-m+d.

Whence
\bar{\mu}(f)+r-1\geq(n-1)(m-1)+d-1
which implies the inequality (
8
). If
f
is non-degenerate then
d_{i}=1
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
\bar{\mu}(f_{i})=\left(\frac{m}{d}-1\right)\left(\frac{n}{d}-1\right)
and
i_{0}(f_{i},f_{j})=\frac{mn}{d^{2}}
and the inequalities become equalities. Moreover,
r(f)=r=d
by Lemma
4.2
.

Let us prove now the general case, that is
\sigma:=\sharp\mathcal{N}_{f}>1
. Let
f=\prod_{S\in\mathcal{N}_{f}}f_{S}
be the Newton factorization of
f
. By the third statement of Proposition
1.1
we get

\bar{\mu}(f)+\sigma-1=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{N}_{f}}\bar{\mu}(f_{S})+\sum_{S\neq T}i_{0}(f_{S},f_{T})=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{N}_{f}}\bar{\mu}(f_{S})+\sum_{S\neq T}[S,T],

where
S
and
T
are not parallel. Since
f_{S}
is elementary of bidegree
(|S|_{1},|S|_{2})
we get

\bar{\mu}(f_{S})\geq(|S|_{1}-1)(|S|_{2}-1)+\gcd(|S|_{1},|S|_{2})-r(f_{S}).

A simple calculation shows that

\bar{\mu}(f)+\sigma-1\geq[\mathcal{N}_{f},\mathcal{N}_{f}]-|\mathcal{N}_{f}|_{1}-|\mathcal{N}_{f}|_{2}+\sigma+r(\mathcal{N}_{f})-r(f).

Therefore
\bar{\mu}(f)\geq\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f})+r(\mathcal{N}_{f})-r(f)
. If
f
is non-degenerate then
f_{S}
is non-degenerate. Thus
\bar{\mu}(f_{S})=\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f_{S}})
and
r(f_{S})=r(\mathcal{N}_{f_{S}})=\gcd(|S|_{1},|S|_{2})
. Using the Newton factorization we get
\overline{\mu}(f)=\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f})
. Obviously
r(f)=\sum_{S}r(f_{S})=\sum_{S}\gcd(|S|_{1},|S|_{2})=r(\mathcal{N}_{f})
.

It remains to prove Theorem
3.1
for non-convenient power series.

Let
f(x,y)=x^{d_{1}}y^{d_{2}}g(x,y)
where
g=g(x,y)
is a convenient reduced power series or a unit. We assume that
g(0,0)=0
(if
g(0,0)\neq 0
then
\overline{\mu}(f)=\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f})
and
r(f)=\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f})
).

Because the length of a segment is the same on parallel axes we have

|\mathcal{N}_{f}|_{i}=|\mathcal{N}_{g}|_{i}\;\;\hbox{\rm for $i=1,2$, $[\mathcal{N}_{f},\mathcal{N}_{f}]=[\mathcal{N}_{g},\mathcal{N}_{g}]$ and $r(\mathcal{N}_{f})-r(f)=r(\mathcal{N}_{g})-r(g)$}.

(9)

Since we have already proved Theorem
3.1
for convenient power series we get

\overline{\mu}(g)-\mu(\mathcal{N}_{g})\geq r(\mathcal{N}_{g})-r(g)\geq 0,

(10)

and the equalities
\overline{\mu}(g)=\mu(\mathcal{N}_{g})
and
r(\mathcal{N}_{g})=r(g)
holding for non-degenerate
g
.

By Proposition
1.1
we get

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(f)+2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(x)+\bar{\mu}(y)+\bar{\mu}(g)+2i_{0}(g,x)+2i_{0}(g,y)+2i_{0}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(g)+2\mbox{\rm ord}g(0,y)+2\mbox{\rm ord}g(x,0)+2,

and

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(g)+2|\mathcal{N}_{g}|_{1}+2|\mathcal{N}_{g}|_{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{N}_{g})+r(\mathcal{N}_{g})-r(g)+2|\mathcal{N}_{g}|_{1}+2|\mathcal{N}_{g}|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{N}_{g},\mathcal{N}_{g}]+|\mathcal{N}_{g}|_{1}+|\mathcal{N}_{g}|_{2}+1+r(\mathcal{N}_{g})-r(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{N}_{f},\mathcal{N}_{f}]+|\mathcal{N}_{f}|_{1}+|\mathcal{N}_{f}|_{2}+1+r(\mathcal{N}_{f})-r(f)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f})+r(\mathcal{N}_{f})-r(f).

If
f
is non-degenerate then
g
is non-degenerate and we get
\bar{\mu}(f)=\mu(\mathcal{N}_{f})
and
r(\mathcal{N}_{f})=r(f)
.

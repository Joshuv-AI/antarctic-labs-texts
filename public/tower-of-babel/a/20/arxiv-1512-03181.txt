Abstract.

In this paper we classify the isolated singularities of positive solutions to Choquard equation

\displaystyle\ \ -\Delta u+u=I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\;\;{\rm in}\;\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},\;\;\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0,

where
p>0,q\geq 1,N\geq 3,\alpha\in(0,N)
and

I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(y)^{p}}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy.

We show that any positive solution
u

is a solution of

-\Delta u+u=I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}+k\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\;\mathbb{R}^{N}

in the distributional sense for some
k\geq 0
, where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac mass at the origin.
We prove the existence of singular solutions in the subcritical case:
p+q<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}\;{\rm and}\;p<\frac{N}{N-2},q<\frac{N}{N-2}

and prove that either the solution
u
has removable singularity at the origin
or satisfies
\lim_{|x|\to 0^{+}}u(x)|x|^{N-2}=C_{N}
which is a positive constant. In the supercritical case:
p+q\geq\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}\;{\rm or}\;p\geq\frac{N}{N-2},\;{\rm or}\;q\geq\frac{N}{N-2}
we prove that
k=0
.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the classification of all positive solutions to Choquard equation

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\ \ -\Delta u+u=I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\qquad\ \displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0,\end{array}

(1.1)

where
p>0,\,q\geq 1
,
N\geq 3
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
and

I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(y)^{p}}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}\,dy.

When
N=3
,
\alpha=p=2
and
q=1
, problem (
1.1
) was proposed by P. Choquard as an approximation to Hartree-Fock theory for a one component plasma,
which has been explained in Lieb and Lieb-Simon’s papers
[
21
,
22
]
respectively. It is also called Choquard-Pekar equation after a more early work of S. Paker for describing the quantum mechanics of a polaron at rest
[
32
]
, or sometime the nonlinear Schrödinger-Newton equation in the context of self-gravitating matter
[
36
]
. The Choquard type equations also arise in the physics of multiple-particle systems,
see
[
19
]
. Furthermore, the Choquard type equations appear to be a prototype of the nonlocal problems, which play a fundamental role in some Quantum-mechanical and non-linear optics,
refer to
[
18
,
31
]
. When
\alpha\in(0,2)
, the Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}
is related to the fractional Laplacian, which is a nonlocal operator,
so the Choquard equation (
1.1
) could be divided into a system with the Laplacian in the
linear part of the first equation and fractional Laplacian in the second one. For the related topics on the fractional equation we can refer for example to
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
11
]
.

The study of isolated singularities is initiated by Brezis and Lions in
[
5
]
, where an useful tool to connect the singular solutions of
elliptic equation in punctured domain and the solutions of corresponding elliptic equation in the distributional
sense was built, by the study of

\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta u\leq au+f\quad{\rm in}\;\Omega\setminus\{0\},\quad u>0\;\;{\rm in}\;\Omega\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt]
\quad\ u\in L^{1}(\Omega),\quad\Delta u\in L^{1}(\Omega\setminus\{0\}),\end{array}

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
containing the origin, the parameter
a>0
and function
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.
Later on, the classification of isolated singular problem

\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u=u^{p}&{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{--}\displaystyle u>0&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\end{array}

(1.2)

was performed by Lions in
[
23
]
for
p\in(1,\frac{N}{N-2})
,
by Aviles in
[
1
]
for
p=\frac{N}{N-2}
, by Gidas-Spruck in
[
16
]

for
\frac{N}{N-2}<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
, by Caffarelli-Gidas-Spruck in
[
6
]
for

p=\frac{N+2}{N-2}
. For the case that
p>\frac{N+2}{N-2}
, the classification of isolated singularities
for (
1.2
) is still open. In the particular case of
p\in(1,\frac{N}{N-2})
, any positive solution of (
1.2
)
is a solution of

\displaystyle-\Delta u=u^{p}+k\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega

(1.3)

in the distributional
sense for some
k\geq 0
. Furthermore, for suitable
k
, problem (
1.3
) has at least two positive solutions including the minimal solution. More related topics could be referred to the
references
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
13
,
14
,
26
,
35
]
.

Our interest in this paper is to classify the singularities of positive classical solutions for Choquard equation (
1.1
). Here
u
is said to be a classical solution of (
1.1
) if
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
,
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]
is well-defined in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
u
satisfies (
1.1
) pointwisely. The first result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
N\geq 3
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,

p>0
,
q\geq 1

and
u
is a positive classical solution of (
1.1
) satisfying
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Then
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
there exists
k\geq 0

such that
u
is a solution of

-\Delta u+u=I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}+k\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.4)

in the distributional sense, that is the following identity holds,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[u(-\Delta\xi+\xi)-I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\xi]\,dx=k\xi(0),\quad\forall\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.5)

where
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the space of all the functions in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with compact support.

Furthermore,

(i)
when

p+q\geq\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}\quad{\rm or}\quad p\geq\frac{N}{N-2}\quad{\rm or}\quad q\geq\frac{N}{N-2},

(1.6)

then
k=0
.

(ii)
When

p+q<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}\quad{\rm and}\quad p<\frac{N}{N-2}\quad{\rm and}\quad q<\frac{N}{N-2}\textsterling\textlnot

(1.7)

and if
k=0
, then
u
is a classical solution of

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u+u=I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\qquad\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0;\end{array}

(1.8)

if
k>0
, then
u
satisfies that

\lim_{|x|\to 0^{+}}u(x)|x|^{N-2}=c_{N}k,

(1.9)

where
c_{N}
is the normalized constant.

The solution of (
1.1
) in the distributional sense are sometimes called the very weak solution. We call also the pair exponent
(p,\,q)
is supercritical if (
1.6
) holds and
(p,\,q)
is subcritical if (
1.7
) does. Theorem
1.1
shows that in the supercritical case,
the singularities of positive solutions of (
1.1
) are not visible in the distribution sense by the Dirac mass.
In the subcritical case the solutions of (
1.1
) may have the singularity as
|x|^{2-N}
or removable singularity at the origin.
In the subcritical case and when
k=0
, we improve the regularity of
u
by separating the factors
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}],\,u^{q}
of nonlinearity and using the bootstrap argument,
however, the factors
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}],\,u^{q}
have different growth rates in
L^{t}
estimates and the key point is to balance them; while
k>0
, in order to study (
1.9
),
our strategy is to divide
u
into

u\leq u_{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\Gamma_{i}+k\Gamma_{0},

where
\Gamma_{0}
is the fundamental solution of
-\Delta u+u=\delta_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\Gamma_{i}
are generated by
\Gamma_{0}
but with lower singularities,
u_{n}
is the remainder term.
Our aim here is to find some
n_{0}
such that
u_{n_{0}}
is bounded at the origin. The difficulty in this procedure
is to control the singularity of
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\Gamma_{i}
and to improve the regularity of
u_{n}
at the same time.
To this end, we develop the bootstrap argument, to reduce the singularity of
\Gamma_{n}
first until to be bounded and then to improve the regularity of
u_{n}
without the influence
of singularities from
\Gamma_{n}
.
We mention that
[
18
,
24
,
28
]
show that the nonlinear Choquard equation
admits variational solutions in the case of
q=p-1
, which have no singularities at the origin.
See a survey
[
29
]
and the references therein.

Our second aim of this paper is to decide whether (
1.1
) has singular solutions in the subcritical case. To this end, we shall search the weak solutions of (
1.4
), where the restriction
\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0

in (
1.4
) is viewed as

\lim_{r\to+\infty}{ess{\rm sup}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(0)}}u(x)=0.

Now it is ready to state the existence and nonexistence of weak solutions of (
1.4
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
N\geq 3
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
p>0,\,q\geq 1
satisfy (
1.7
) and denote

k_{q}=\left(\frac{1}{c_{1}(p+q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p+q-1}}\frac{p+q-1}{p+q},

where
c_{1}
is the constant from (
1.11
).
Then there exists
k^{*}\geq k_{q}

such that

(i)
for
k\in(0,k^{*})
, problem (
1.4
) admits a minimal positive weak solution
u_{k}
;

(ii)
for
k>k^{*}
, problem (
1.4
) admits no positive weak solution.

Furthermore, if
k\leq k_{q}
,
u_{k}
is a classical solution of (
1.1
) and satisfies (
1.9
).

When
q=p-1
, V. Moroz and J. Van Schaftingen
[
27
]
have derived groundstates of (
1.8
) for
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}<p<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}

by variational method. Furthermore, this existence result is sharp in the sense that there is no nontrivial regular variational solution to (
1.8
) for

p\leq\frac{N+\alpha}{N}
and
p\geq\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}
. Usually, the derivation of the very weak solution is different by using nonvariational methods.
The solution
u_{k}
of (
1.4
) is derived by iterating procedure:

v_{0}=k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}],\qquad v_{n}=\mathbb{G}[v_{n-1}^{p}]+k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}],

where
\mathbb{G}
is the Green’s operator defined by the Green kernel
G(x,y)
of
-\Delta+id
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Here the main difficulty is to find a barrier function to control the sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
. It is well-known that
in the bounded domain
\Omega
and

\gamma\in(1,\frac{N}{N-2})

the barrier function is constructed by the fact that

\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}^{\gamma}[\delta_{0}]]\leq c_{2}\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\delta_{0}]\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\},

(1.10)

where
c_{2}>0
and
\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\cdot]
is the Green’s operator defined by Green’s kernel of
-\Delta+id
in
\Omega\times\Omega
.
However, the estimate (
1.10
) is no longer valid for
\mathbb{G}
. In order to find a barrier function when the domain is the whole space,
our strategy here is to establish the following estimate

\mathbb{G}[I_{\alpha}[\Phi_{0}^{p}]\Phi_{0}^{q}]\leq c_{1}\Phi_{0}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

(1.11)

where
c_{1}>0
and
\Phi_{0}
satisfies that

-\Delta u+\frac{1}{4}u=\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Recently, M.Ghergu and S.D. Taliaferro
[
15
]
have studied the behavior near the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

for the Choquard-Pekar type inequality

0\leq-\Delta u\leq(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast u^{\lambda})u^{\sigma}\quad{\rm in}\quad B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\}.

(1.12)

Here
u
is assumed to be in
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\cap L^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\ast
is the convolution operator. In particular, they proved that for some suitable range of
\lambda,\sigma
depending on
n
and
\alpha
, the existence of pointwise bounds for nonnegative solutions of (
1.12
). We mention that the nonnegative solutions they considered are superharmonic functions, and the operator
-\Delta+id
in our case make a great difference on the analysis of the singularities and the existence of singular solutions.

We emphasize that in this paper we consider the case where
q\geq 1
.
When
q<1
,
[
28
,
27
]
show that the solutions of problem (
1.1

is well defined and then it may cause the nonexistence. The existence and nonexistence of isolated singular solution of (

2.
Preliminary

We start the analysis from the integrability of the solutions to (
1.1
) near the origin. In what follows, we denote by
c_{i}
a generic positive constant.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
N\geq 3
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
p>0,

q>0
and
u
is a positive classical solution of (
1.1
)
such that
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Then we have

I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(2.1)

Proof.

If
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\not\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{r}(0)}I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\,dx=+\infty

by the facts that
u\geq 0
and
u\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
.
Thus there exists a decreasing sequence
\{R_{n}\}_{n}\subset(0,1)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}R_{n}=0
and

\int_{B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{R_{n}}(0)}I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\,dx=n.

(2.2)

Let
w_{n}
be the solution of

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta w_{n}+w_{n}=\chi_{B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{R_{n}}(0)}I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\ }\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}w_{n}(x)=0,\end{array}

where
\chi_{D}
denotes the standard characteristic function of a domain
D
.
Let
\Gamma_{0}
be the fundamental solution of
-\Delta+id
, then

\lim_{|x|\to 0^{+}}(u+\Gamma_{0})(x)=+\infty\quad{\rm and}\quad\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}(u+\Gamma_{0})(x)=0,

so it follows by Comparison Principle that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

u+\Gamma_{0}\geq w_{n}\quad{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

(2.3)

Note that
G(x,y)\geq c_{3}
for any
x,y\in B_{1}(0)
, then by (
2.2
), we have that

\displaystyle w_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}[\chi_{B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{R_{n}}(0)}I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}](x)=\int_{B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{R_{n}}(0)}G(x,y)I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](y)u(y)^{q}dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{3}\int_{B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{R_{n}}(0)}I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](y)u(y)^{q}dy\ \ =c_{3}n

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle+\infty\quad{\rm as}\ \ n\to\infty,\quad\ \forall x\in B_{1}(0),

which, together with (
2.3
), implies that

u+\Gamma_{0}\equiv+\infty
in
B_{1}(0)
and this is impossible.
Therefore we have that
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
∎

The following asymptotic behavior of positive solutions to problem (
1.1
) plays an important role
in the control of the integrability at infinity.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
N\geq 3
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
p>0,

q\geq 1
and
u
is a positive classical solution of (
1.1
)
such that
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Then
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0))
,

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)=0

(2.4)

and for any
\theta\in(0,1)
, there holds

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)e^{\theta|x|}=0.

(2.5)

Proof.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0)
, we have that

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(y)^{p}}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x)}\frac{u(y)^{p}}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy+\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x)}\frac{u(y)^{p}}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\alpha-N}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x))}^{p}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\alpha-N}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+c_{4}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0))},

thus
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0))
.

Similarly, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2}(0)
and
r\in(0,\frac{|x|}{2})
depending on
|x|
, which will be chosen later,
we have

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r^{\alpha-N}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x))}^{p}\int_{B_{r}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r^{\alpha-N}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+r^{\alpha}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{|x|-r}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r^{\alpha-N}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+r^{\alpha}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(0))}.

Since

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(0))}=0,

then

r:=\min\{\lVert u\rVert^{-\frac{p}{2\alpha}}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(0))},\,\frac{|x|}{4}\}\to+\infty
as
|x|\to+\infty
,
and thus

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}r^{\alpha-N}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0\quad{\rm and}\quad\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}r^{\alpha}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(0))}=0,

which imply that (
2.4
) holds.

Now for any
\theta^{\prime}\in(\theta,1)
, since
q\geq 1
,
there exists
r_{1}>2
such that

I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)u(x)^{q-1}\leq 1-\theta^{\prime}\quad{\rm for}\quad|x|\geq r_{1}

and

-\Delta e^{-\theta^{\prime}|x|}+\theta^{\prime}e^{-\theta^{\prime}|x|}\geq 0,\quad\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0).

Then we have that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u+\theta^{\prime}u\leq 0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-\,\,\,}\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0.\end{array}

It follows by Comparison Principle that

u(x)\leq c_{5}e^{-\theta^{\prime}|x|}\quad{\rm for}\quad|x|\geq r_{1},

which implies that (
2.5
) holds.
∎

When

, exponential decay of the solutions to equation (

Proposition 2.1
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma

, we have that

I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad{\rm and}\quad u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Furthermore, if

for

, then

.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.1
, we know that
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and by Lemma
2.2
,
we have that
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Since

is a positive solution, then

u\geq c_{6}\quad{\rm in}\quad B_{2}(0)\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)

and for
x\in B_{1}(0)\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}(0)\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}\frac{u(x)^{p}}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy\geq c_{7}.

(2.6)

Then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}(0)}u(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|B_{1}(0)|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B_{1}(0)}u(x)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|B_{1}(0)|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{c_{7}}\int_{B_{1}(0)}I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<+\infty,

that is,
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By Lemma
2.2
, it implies that
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

If
u\in L^{t}(B_{1}(0))
for
t\in[1,+\infty]
, it infers by Lemma
2.2
that
u\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

To tackle the singularity estimate (

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
\alpha,\tau\in(0,N)
and
V_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

satisfies

\displaystyle-c_{9}\log|x|&{\rm if}\quad\tau=2,\\[5.69054pt]
c_{9}&{\rm if}\quad\tau<2.\end{array}\right.

(2.7)

Proof.

\displaystyle c_{10}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}\frac{1}{|y|^{\tau}}dy+c_{10}\lVert V_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0))}

\displaystyle c_{10}|x|^{2-\tau}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{|x|}}(0)}\frac{1}{|e_{x}-y|^{N-2}}\frac{1}{|y|^{\tau}}dy+c_{10}\lVert V_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0))}

\displaystyle c_{11}|x|^{2-\tau}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{|x|}}(0)}\frac{1}{1+|y|^{N-2+\tau}}dy+c_{10}\lVert V_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0))}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}c_{9}|x|^{-\tau+2}&{\rm if}\quad\tau>2,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle-c_{9}\log|x|&{\rm if}\quad\tau=2,\\[5.69054pt]
c_{9}&{\rm if}\quad\tau<2,\end{array}\right.

where

. This implies (

Observing that the Riesz potential decays polynomially, it infers that for

\displaystyle c_{10}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}\frac{1}{|y|^{\tau}}dy+c_{10}\lVert V_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle c_{10}|x|^{\alpha-\tau}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{|x|}}(0)}\frac{1}{|e_{x}-y|^{N-\alpha}}\frac{1}{|y|^{\tau}}dy+c_{10}\lVert V_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{12}|x|^{\alpha-\tau}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{|x|}}(0)}\frac{1}{1+|y|^{N-\alpha+\tau}}dy+c_{10}\lVert V_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}c_{9}|x|^{-\tau+\alpha}&{\rm if}\quad\tau>\alpha,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle-c_{9}\log|x|&{\rm if}\quad\tau=\alpha,\\[5.69054pt]
c_{9}&{\rm if}\quad\tau<\alpha.\end{array}\right.

This ends the proof.∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that

,

p>0
,

and

is a positive weak solution of (

Then

\Gamma_{0}
is the fundamental solution of

Proof.

Let

, then

is a weak solution of

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\ \,-\Delta w+w=I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}w(x)=0,\end{array}

in the distribution sense, that is

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(-\Delta\xi+\xi)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\xi dx,\qquad\forall\,\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, denote

\xi_{n}(x)=\mathbb{G}[{\rm sign}(w_{-})](x)\eta_{0}(\frac{x}{n}),\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
t_{-}=\min\{t,0\}
and
\eta_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,1]
is a
C^{\infty}
-function with the support in
B_{2}(0)
and
satisfying
\eta_{0}=1
in
B_{1}(0)
, then
\xi_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any

. Thus,
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle(-\Delta\xi_{n}+\xi_{n})dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(x){\rm sign}(w_{-})(x)\eta_{0}(\frac{x}{n})\,dx+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(x)\nabla\mathbb{G}[{\rm sign}(w_{-})](x)\cdot\nabla\eta_{0}(\frac{x}{n})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(x)\mathbb{G}[{\rm sign}(w_{-})](x)(-\Delta)\eta_{0}(\frac{x}{n})\,dx,

where

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(x){\rm sign}(w_{-})(x)\eta_{0}(\frac{x}{n})\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w_{-}(x)\eta_{0}(\frac{x}{n})\,dx\geq\int_{B_{n}(0)}|w_{-}(x)|\,dx,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(x)\nabla\mathbb{G}[{\rm sign}(w_{-})](x)\cdot\nabla\eta_{0}(\frac{x}{n})\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{14}}{n}\int_{B_{2n}(0)\setminus B_{n}(0)}|w(x)|\,dx,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(x)\mathbb{G}[{\rm sign}(w_{-})](x)(-\Delta)\eta_{0}(\frac{x}{n})\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{14}}{n^{2}}\int_{B_{2n}(0)\setminus B_{n}(0)}|w(x)|\,dx.

Since

and

is non-positive in

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\xi_{n}dx\leq 0,

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}(0)}|w_{-}(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(x)\mathbb{G}[{\rm sign}(w_{-})](x)(-\nabla)\eta_{0}(\frac{x}{n})\,dx\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(x)\mathbb{G}[{\rm sign}(w_{-})](x)(-\Delta)\eta_{0}(\frac{x}{n})\,dx\right|.

Therefore by taking

that is,

u-k\Gamma_{0}\geq 0
a.e. in

∎

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that

,
\alpha\in(0,N)

Proof.

From Proposition

and

First we claim that for any
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
the support in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}

In fact, since
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle L(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{r}}(0)\setminus B_{r}(0)}[u(-\Delta\xi+\xi)-I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\xi]\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{r}}(0)\setminus B_{r}(0)}(-\Delta u+u-I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q})\xi\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

From Theorem 1.1 in
[
23
]
, it implies that

L=k\delta_{0}\quad{\rm for\ some\ }\ k\geq 0,

(3.2)

that is,

L(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[u(-\Delta\xi+\xi)-I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\xi\right]\,dx=k\xi(0),\quad\quad\forall\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(3.3)

Then
u
is a weak solution of (
1.4
) for some
k\geq 0
.

Next we prove that

if
(p,q)
satisfies (
1.6

implies that

It is well known that

Case I:

For

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle k^{p+q}I_{\alpha}[\Gamma_{0}^{p}]\Gamma_{0}^{q}(x)

\displaystyle c_{15}k^{p+q}\bigg(\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{|y|^{(2-N)p}}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}\,dy\bigg)\,|x|^{(2-N)q}

\displaystyle c_{15}k^{p+q}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{|x|}}(0)}\frac{|z|^{(2-N)p}}{|e_{x}-z|^{N-\alpha}}\,dz\bigg)\,|x|^{(2-N)(p+q)+\alpha}

. Indeed, there holds

u=\mathbb{G}[I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}]+k\Gamma_{0}\geq v_{0},

and then

u=\mathbb{G}[I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}]+k\Gamma_{0}\geq\mathbb{G}[I_{\alpha}[v_{0}^{p}]v_{0}^{q}]+k\Gamma_{0}=v_{1}.

We may show inductively that

u\geq v_{n}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The claim follows.

Similarly, if problem (
1.4
) has a positive solution
u
for some
k_{1}>0
, then (
1.4
) admits a minimal solution
u_{k}
for all
k\in(0,k_{1}]
. As a result, the mapping
k\mapsto u_{k}
is increasing.
So we may define

k^{*}=\sup\{k>0:\ (\ref{eq 1.2})\ {\rm has\ minimal\ positive\ solution\ for\ }k\},

then
k^{*}\geq k_{q}.

We next prove that
k^{*}<+\infty
.

Let
\eta_{0}
be a radially symmetric
C^{\infty}_{c}
-function such that
\eta_{0}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2}(0)
and
\eta_{0}=1
in
B_{1}(0)
.
For
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, denote

\xi_{\epsilon}(x)=\eta_{0}(\epsilon x)\mathbb{G}[\eta_{0}](x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

By the direct computation, we have that

\displaystyle-\Delta\xi_{\epsilon}(x)+\xi_{\epsilon}(x)=\epsilon^{2}(-\Delta)\eta_{0}(\epsilon x)\mathbb{G}[\eta_{0}](x)+2\epsilon\nabla\eta_{0}(\epsilon x)\cdot\nabla\mathbb{G}[\eta_{0}](x)+\eta_{0}(\epsilon x)\eta_{0}(x).

Choosing
\epsilon>0
small, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{k}\big(-\Delta\xi_{\epsilon}+\xi_{\epsilon}\big)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{k}(x)[-\epsilon^{2}\Delta\eta_{0}(\epsilon x)\mathbb{G}[\eta_{0}](x)+2\epsilon\nabla\eta_{0}(\epsilon x)\cdot\nabla\mathbb{G}[\eta_{0}](x)

\displaystyle+\eta_{0}(\epsilon x)\eta_{0}(x)]\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}(0)}u_{k}(x)\,dx+c_{33}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{2})\int_{B_{\frac{2}{\epsilon}}(0)\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(0)}u_{k}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{B_{2}(0)}u_{k}(x)\,dx,

where we have used
ess{\rm{sup}}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(0)}u_{k}(x)\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
.

Since

u_{k}\geq k\Gamma_{0}\geq c_{34}k\quad{\rm in}\quad B_{1}(0)

and

I_{\alpha}[u_{k}^{p}]\geq c_{35}k^{p}\quad{\rm in}\quad B_{1}(0)

for some
c_{40}>0
independent of
k
, then

\displaystyle 2\int_{B_{2}(0)}u_{k}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{k}[(-\Delta)\xi_{\epsilon}+\xi_{\epsilon}]\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\alpha}[u_{k}^{p}]u_{k}^{q}\xi_{\epsilon}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{36}k^{p+q-1}\int_{B_{2}(0)}u_{k}(x)\,dx,

where
p+q>1
.
Thus,

k\leq c_{37},

so does
k^{*}
which ends the proof.
\Box

Remark 4.1
.

Concerning the existence of weak solutions of (
1.4
) for
0<k<k^{*}
in the subcritical case, we may consider the stability of the minimal solution
u_{k}
and
then construct the second solution by using Mountain Pass Theorem
[
34
, Theorem 6.1]

5.
Appendix

It is well-known that the Green kernel
G(x,y)
of
-\Delta+id
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
is
\Gamma_{0}(x-y)
, which has exponential decay at infinity.
We recall that
\mathbb{G}[\cdot]
the
Green operator defined as

\mathbb{G}[f](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(x,y)f(y)dy.

Proposition 5.1
.

[
30
, Lemma A.3]

Assume that
h\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
s\geq 1
, then

(i)

\|\mathbb{G}[h]\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c_{38}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\quad{\rm if}\quad\frac{1}{s}<\frac{2}{N};

(5.1)

(ii)

\|\mathbb{G}[h]\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c_{38}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\quad{\rm if}\quad\frac{1}{s}\leq\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2}{N}\quad\rm{and}\quad s>1;

(5.2)

(iii)

\|\mathbb{G}[h]\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c_{38}\|h\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\quad{\rm if}\quad 1<\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2}{N}.

(5.3)

Recall that

I_{\alpha}[h](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{h(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy\quad{\rm for}\ \ h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
\Omega\subset B_{R/2}(0)
for some
R>0
and
h\in L^{s}(B_{R}(0))\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
s\geq 1
. Then

\|I_{\alpha}[h]\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c_{39}\left(\|h\|_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}+\|h\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)\quad{\rm if}\quad\frac{1}{s}<\frac{\alpha}{N},

(5.4)

\|I_{\alpha}[h]\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq c_{39}\left(\|h\|_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}+\|h\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)\quad{\rm if}\quad\frac{1}{s}\leq\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\alpha}{N}\quad{\rm and}\quad s>1

(5.5)

and

\|I_{\alpha}[h]\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq c_{39}\|h\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\quad{\rm if}\quad 1<\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\alpha}{N}.

(5.6)

Proof.

Observe that
|x-y|>\frac{R}{2}
for
x\in\Omega
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)
and then for
x\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle|I_{\alpha}[h](x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{|h(y)|}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}\frac{|h(y)|}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{|h(y)|}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy+(R/2)^{\alpha-N}\lVert h\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Without loss of generality, we can assume

h\geq 0
and in the following, we only need to consider

J_{\alpha}[h](x):=\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{h(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy,\quad x\in\Omega.

First we prove (
5.4
). By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}[h](x)\

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{(N-\alpha)s^{\prime}}}dy\big)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\big(\int_{B_{R}(0)}|h(y)|^{s}dy\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{40}\|h\|_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{(N-\alpha)s^{\prime}}}dy,

where
s^{\prime}=\frac{s}{s-1}
. Since
\frac{1}{s}<\frac{2}{N}
and
(N-\alpha)s^{\prime}<N
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{(N-\alpha)s^{\prime}}}dy=c_{41}\int_{0}^{R}r^{N-1-(N-\alpha)s^{\prime}}dr\leq c_{42}R^{N-(N-\alpha)s^{\prime}},

then (
5.4
) holds.

Next, we prove (
5.5
) for
r\leq s
and (
5.6
) for

r=1
. There holds

\displaystyle\left[\int_{B_{R}(0)}J_{\alpha}[h]^{r}(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{h(y)\chi_{B_{R}(0)}(x)\chi_{B_{R}(0)}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy\bigg]^{r}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{h(x-y)\chi_{B_{R}(0)}(x)\chi_{B_{R}(0)}(x-y)}{|y|^{N-\alpha}}dy\right]^{r}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{r}}.

By Minkowski’s inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\left[\int_{B_{R}(0)}J_{\alpha}[h]^{r}(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{h^{r}(x-y)\chi_{B_{R}(0)}(x)\chi_{B_{R}(0)}(x-y)}{|y|^{(N-\alpha)r}}dx\bigg]^{\frac{1}{r}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}(0)}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h^{r}(x-y)\chi_{B_{R}(0)}(x)\chi_{B_{R}(0)}(x-y)dx\bigg]^{\frac{1}{r}}\frac{1}{|y|^{N-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|h\|_{L^{r}(B_{R}(0))}\int_{B_{2R}(0)}\frac{1}{|y|^{N-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{43}\|h\|_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}.

Finally, we prove (
5.5
) in the case

r>s\geq 1
and
\frac{1}{s}\leq\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2}{N}
, and (
5.6
) for
r>1
and
1<\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\alpha}{N}.

We claim that if
r>s
and
\frac{1}{r^{*}}=\frac{1}{s}-\frac{\alpha}{N}
, the mapping

h\to J_{\alpha}(h)
is of weak-type
(s,r^{*})
in the sense that

|\{x\in\Omega:|J_{\alpha}[h](x)|>t\}|\leq\big(A_{s,r^{*}}\frac{\|h\|_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}}{t}\big)^{r^{*}},\quad h\in L^{s}(B_{R}(0)),\ \ \forall t>0,

(5.7)

where
A_{s,r^{*}}
is a positive constant. Defining

J_{0}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}|x-y|^{\alpha-N}&{\rm if}\quad|x-y|\leq\nu,\\[5.69054pt]
0&{\rm if}\quad|x-y|>\nu\end{array}\right.

for
\nu>0
and
J_{\infty}(x,y)=|x-y|^{\alpha-N}-J_{0}(x,y)
. Then we have that

|\{x\in\Omega:J_{\alpha}[h](x)>2t\}|\leq|\{x\in\Omega:J_{0}[h](x)>t\}|+|\{x\in\Omega:J_{\infty}[h](x)>t\}|,

where
J_{0}[h]=\int_{B_{R}(0)}J_{0}(x,y)h(y)\,dy
and
J_{\infty}[h]=\int_{B_{R}(0)}J_{\infty}(x,y)h(y)\,dy
.
By Minkowski’s inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle|\{x\in B_{R}(0):|J_{0}[h](x)|>t\}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|J_{0}(h)\|^{s}_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}}{t^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|\int_{B_{R}(0)}\chi_{B_{\nu}(x-y)}|x-y|^{\alpha-N}|h(y)|dy\|^{s}_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}}{t^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{[\int_{B_{R}(0)}(\int_{B_{R}(0)}|h(x-y)|^{s}dx)^{\frac{1}{s}}|y|^{\alpha-N}\chi_{B_{\nu}}(y)dy]^{s}}{t^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|^{s}_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}\int_{B_{\nu}}|x|^{-N+\alpha}dx}{t^{s}}=c_{44}\frac{\|h\|^{s}_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}\nu^{\alpha}}{t^{s}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|J_{\infty}[h]\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\int_{B_{R}(0)}\chi_{B_{\nu}^{c}}(x-y)|x-y|^{\alpha-N}|h(y)|dy\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\int_{B_{R}(0)}|h(y)|^{s}dy)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|(\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\nu}(y)}|x-y|^{(\alpha-N)s^{\prime}}dy)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{45}\|h\|_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}\nu^{\alpha-\frac{N}{s}},

where
s^{\prime}=\frac{s}{s-1}
if
s>1
, and if
s=1
,
s^{\prime}=\infty
. Choosing
\nu=(\frac{t}{c_{45}\|h\|_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}})^{\frac{1}{\alpha-\frac{N}{s}}}
, we obtain

\|J_{\infty}[h]\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\leq t,

which means that

|\{x\in\Omega:|J_{\infty}[h](x)|>t\}|=0.

With this choice of
\nu
, we have that

|\{x\in\Omega:|J_{\alpha}[h]|>2t\}|\leq c_{46}\frac{\|h\|^{s}_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}\nu^{\alpha s}}{t^{s}}\leq c_{47}\left(\frac{\|h\|_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}}{t}\right)^{r^{*}}.

The claim
for
r>s
follows from the Marcinkiewicz
interpolation theorem.

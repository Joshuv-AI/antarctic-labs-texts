1.
Introduction and main result

In this paper, we investigate the existence of standing waves

\psi(x,t)=e^{-\frac{iEt}{\hbar}}u(x)
,
E\in\mathbb{R},u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

to the time-dependent nonlinear Schrödinger equation
with an external electromagnetic field

(1.1)

i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=\Big(\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla-A(x)\Big)^{2}\psi+G(x)\psi-f(x,\psi),\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

which arises in various physical contexts such as nonlinear optics
or plasma physics where one simulates the interaction effect among
many particles by introducing a nonlinear term (see
[
28
]
). The function

\psi(x,t)
takes on complex values,
\hbar
is the Planck constant,

i
is the imaginary unit. Here
A
denotes a magnetic potential and the
Schrödinger operator is defined by

\Big(\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla-A(x)\Big)^{2}\psi:=-\hbar^{2}\Delta\psi-\frac{2\hbar}{i}A\cdot\nabla\psi+|A|^{2}\psi-\frac{\hbar}{i}\psi divA.

Actually, in general dimension, the magnetic field
B
is a 2-form where

B_{k,j}=\partial_{j}A_{k}-\partial_{k}A_{j}
; in the case
N=3,B=curlA
.
The function
G
represents an electric potential.

Assuming
f(x,e^{i\theta}u)=e^{i\theta}f(x,u),\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{1}
and substituting
this ansatz
\psi(x,t)=e^{-\frac{iEt}{\hbar}}u(x)
into (
1.1
),
one is led to solve the complex semilinear elliptic equation

(1.2)

\Big(\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla-A(x)\Big)^{2}u+(G(x)-E)u=f(x,u),\,\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

For simplicity, let
V(x)=(G(x)-E)
and assume that
V
is strictly positive
on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}.
The transition from quantum mechanics to classical
mechanics can be formally described by letting
\hbar\rightarrow 0
, and thus the existence of solutions for
\hbar
small has physical interest.
Standing waves for
\hbar
small are usually referred
as semi-classical bound states (see
[
16
]
).

When
A(x)\equiv 0
, problem (
1.2
) arises in various applications, such as chemotaxis, population genetics, chemical reactor theory, and the study of
standing waves of certain nonlinear Schrödinger equations.
In recent years, a considerable amount of work has been devoted to study
wave solutions of (
1.2
) with
A(x)\equiv 0.
Among of them, we refer to

[
5
,
6
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
15
,
21
,
22
,
25
,
27
,
29
]
. Recently, in
[
1
]
, Ao and Wei applying localized energy method obtained infinitely many positive solutions for (
1.2
) with non-symmetric potential.

On the contrary, there are
still relatively few papers which
deal with the case
A(x)\not\equiv 0
,
namely when a magnetic field is present. The first result on magnetic nonlinear Schrödinger
equation is due to Esteban and Lions in
[
14
]
. They obtained
the existence of standing waves to (
1.2
) for
\hbar
fixed and for special classes of
magnetic fields by solving an appropriate minimization problem for the corresponding energy functional in the cases of
N=2,3.

In
[
9
]
, Cao and Tang constructed semiclassical multi-peak solutions for (
1.2
) with bounded vector potentials.
In
[
8
]
, using a
penalization procedure, Cingolani and Secchi extended the result in
[
7
]
to the case of a vector potential
A
,
possibly unbounded. The penalization approach was also used in
[
3
]
by Bartsch, Dancer
and Peng to obtain multi-bump semiclassical bound for problem (
1.2
) with more general nonlinear term
f(x,u)
.
In
[
19
]
, Kurata proved the existence of least energy solution of (
1.2
)
for
\hbar>0
under a condition relating
V(x)
and
A(x)
. In
[
16
,
17
]
,
Helffer studied asymptotic behavior of the eigenfunctions of the Schrödinger
operators with magnetic fields in the semiclassical limit. See also

[
2
]
for generalization of the results and in
[
18
]
for potentials
which degenerate at infinity. In
[
20
]
, Li, Peng and Wang applied the finite reduction method to
obtain infinitely many non-radial complex valued solutions for (
1.2
) with
radial electromagnetic fields satisfying some algebraic decaying conditions.
Liu and Wang in
[
23
]
extends the result to some weaker symmetric conditions.
In
[
26
]
, Pi and Wang obtained multi-bump solutions for (
1.2
) with
\hbar=1,f(x,u)=|u|^{p-2}u
and an electrical potential satisfying a
condition by applying the finite reduction method.

In this paper, inspired by
[
1
,
30
]
, our main idea is to use the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method. We want to point out that the only assumption we need is
the non-degeneracy of the bump. We have no requirements on the structure of the nonlinearity.

If
\hbar=1
,
A(x)=A_{0}+\epsilon\tilde{A}(x)
,
V(x)=1+\epsilon\tilde{V}(x)
and
f(x,u)=f(u)
, then (1.2) is
reduced to the following complex problem

\Big(\displaystyle\frac{\nabla}{i}-A_{0}-\epsilon\tilde{A}(x)\Big)^{2}u+(1+\epsilon\tilde{V}(x))u=f(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}).

For simplicity of notations, in the sequel, we denote

A_{\epsilon}(x)=A_{0}+\epsilon\tilde{A}(x)\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,V_{\epsilon}(x)=1+\epsilon\tilde{V}(x).

Then we are concerned with the following problem

(1.3)

\Big(\displaystyle\frac{\nabla}{i}-A_{\epsilon}(x)\Big)^{2}u+V_{\epsilon}(x)u=f(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}).

In order to state our main result, we give the conditions imposed on
\tilde{A}(x)
,
\tilde{V}(x)
and
f
:

(A_{1})

\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|\tilde{A}(x)|=0
;

(A_{2})

\exists 0<\alpha_{1}<1
,
\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|\tilde{A}(x)|^{2}e^{\alpha_{1}|x|}=+\infty
;

(A_{3})

\exists 0<\alpha_{2}<1
,
\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|div\tilde{A}(x)|^{2}e^{\alpha_{2}|x|}=+\infty
;

(A_{4})

\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|\nabla\tilde{A}(x)|=0
;

(V_{1})

\tilde{V}(x)\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
and
\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|\tilde{V}(x)|=0
;

(V_{2})

\exists 0<\alpha_{3}<1
,
\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|\tilde{V}(x)|e^{\alpha_{3}|x|}=+\infty
;

(f_{1})

f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is of class
C^{1+\delta}
for some
0<\delta\leq 1
,
f^{\prime}(0)=0
;

(f_{2})

f(e^{i\theta}u)=e^{i\theta}f(u),\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{1};

(f_{3})

The equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta w+w=f(w),&w>0\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}w(x)=0,&w(0)=\displaystyle\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(x),\end{array}\right.

has a non-degenerate solution
w
, i.e.,

ker(\Delta-1+f^{\prime}(w))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=span\Big\{\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{N}}\Big\}.

Particularly,
f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u
satisfies
(f_{2}).

Under the above assumptions, the spectrum of the linearized operator

\Delta\varphi-\varphi+f^{\prime}(w)\varphi=\lambda\varphi,\penalty\ \penalty\ \varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

admits the following decompositions

\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\ldots>\lambda_{n}>\lambda_{n+1}=0>\lambda_{n+2}

where each of the eigenfunction corresponding to the positive eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}
decays exponentially.
These eigenfunctions will play an important role in our secondary Lyapunov-Schmidt
reduction(see Section 3 below).

Remark 1.1
.

It is easy to find that
w
is a solution of (
1
) if and
only if
e^{iA_{0}\cdot x}w
is a solution of the following problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\nabla}{i}-A_{0}\Big)^{2}u+u=f(u),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|u(x)|=0,&|u(0)|=\displaystyle\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|,\end{array}\right.

from
which and
(f_{3})
we can deduce that (
1.1
) has a non-degenerate solution
e^{i\sigma+iA_{0}\cdot x}w,

i.e.

ker\Big(-\Big(\frac{\nabla}{i}-A_{0}\Big)^{2}-1+f^{\prime}(w)\Big)=span\Big\{\frac{\partial(e^{i\sigma+iA_{0}\cdot x}w)}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial(e^{i\sigma+iA_{0}\cdot x}w)}{\partial x_{N}},\frac{\partial(e^{i\sigma+iA_{0}\cdot x}w)}{\partial\sigma}\Big\}.

In the sequel, the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is endowed with the standard norm

\|u\|=\Bigl(\int|\nabla u|^{2}+|u|^{2}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which is induced by the inner product

\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle=\int(\nabla u\nabla v+uv).

Denote
\alpha=\min\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\}.

Our main result of this paper is as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
(A_{1})
-
(A_{4})
,
(V_{1})
-
(V_{2})
and
(f_{1})
-
(f_{3})
hold.
Then there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
,
problem (1.3) has infinitely many complex-valued solutions.

In the following, we sketch the main idea in the proof of Theorem 1.2.

We introduce some notations first. Let
\mu>0
be a real number such that
w(x)\leq ce^{-|x|}
for
|x|>\mu
and some constant
c
independent of
\mu

large. Now we define the configuration space

\Omega_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{N},\Omega_{m}:=\Bigl\{\textbf{Q}_{m}=(Q_{1},Q_{2},\ldots,Q_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{mN}:\penalty\ \min_{k\neq j}|Q_{k}-Q_{j}|\geq\mu\Bigl\},\forall m>1.

Let
w
be the non-degenerate solution of (
1
) and
m\geq 1
be an integer. Define the sum of
m
spikes as

w_{Q_{j}}=w(x-Q_{j}),\,\xi_{j}=e^{i\sigma+iA_{0}\cdot(x-Q_{j})},\,z_{Q_{j}}=\xi_{j}w(x-Q_{j})\,\,\text{and}\,\,z_{\textbf{Q}_{m}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{Q_{j}},

where
\sigma\in[0,2\pi]
.

Let the operator be

\mathcal{S}(u)=-\Bigl(\frac{\nabla}{i}-A_{\epsilon}(x)\Bigl)^{2}u-V_{\epsilon}(x)u+f(u).

Fixing
(\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m})=(\sigma,Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m})\in[0,2\pi]\times\Omega_{m},
we define the following functions as the approximate kernels:

D_{j,k}=\frac{\partial(e^{i\sigma+iA_{0}\cdot(x-Q_{j})}w_{Q_{j}})}{\partial x_{k}}\eta_{j}(x),\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ j=1,\ldots,m,k=1,\ldots,N

and

D_{j,N+1}=\frac{\partial(e^{i\sigma+iA_{0}\cdot(x-Q_{j})}w_{Q_{j}})}{\partial\sigma}\eta_{j}(x),j=1,\ldots,m,

where
\eta_{j}(x)=\eta(\frac{2|x-Q_{j}|}{\mu-1})
and
\eta(t)
is a cut off function, such that
\eta(t)=1
for
|t|\leq 1
and
\eta(t)=0
for
|t|\geq\frac{\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}-1}
. Note that the support of
D_{j,k}
belongs to
B_{\frac{\mu^{2}}{2(\mu+1)}}(Q_{j})
.

Applying
z_{\textbf{Q}_{m}}
as the approximate solution and performing the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction, we can show that there exists a constant
\mu_{0}
,
such that for
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
, and
\epsilon<c_{\mu}
, for some constant
c_{\mu}
depending on
\mu
but independent of
m
and
\textbf{Q}_{m}
, we can find
a
\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}
such that

\mathcal{S}(z_{\textbf{Q}_{m}}+\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{N+1}c_{j,k}D_{j,k},

and we can show that
\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}
is
C^{1}
in
(\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}).
This is done in Section 2.

After that, for any
m
, we define a new function

(1.10)

\mathcal{M}(\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m})=J(z_{\textbf{Q}_{m}}+\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}),

we maximize
\mathcal{M}(\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m})
over
[0,2\pi]\times\bar{\Omega}_{m}.

At the maximum point of
\mathcal{M}(\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}),
we show that
c_{j,k}=0

for all
j,k.
Therefore we prove that the corresponding
z_{\textbf{Q}_{m}}+\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}
is a solution of
(
1.3
). By the arguments before, we know that there exists
\mu_{0}
large such that
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and

\epsilon\leq c_{\mu}
and for any
m
, there exists a spike solution to (
1.3
) with
m
spikes in
\Omega_{m}
.
Considering that
m
is arbitrary, then there exists infinitely many spikes solutions for
\epsilon<c_{\mu_{0}}
independent of
m.

There are three main difficulties in the maximization process. Firstly, we need to show that the maximum points will not go to infinity. Secondly, we have to
detect the difference in the energy when the spikes move to the boundary of the configuration space. In the second step, we use the induction method and
detect the difference of the m-th spikes energy and the (m+1)-th spikes energy. A crucial estimate is Lemma 3.2, where we prove that the accumulated error can be
controlled from step
m
to step
m+1
. To this end, we make a secondary Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction. This is done in Section 3.
Compared with
[
1
]
, since there is a magnetic filed in our problem, we have to overcome some new difficulties which involves many technical estimates.

Our paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we carry out Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction. Then we perform a second Liapunov-Schmidt reduction in section 3.
Finally, we prove our main result in section 4.

2.
Finite-dimensional reduction

In this section, we perform a finite-dimensional reduction.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
and we define

(2.1)

E(\cdot):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}e^{-\gamma|\cdot-Q_{j}|},\,\,\,\,\text{where}\,\,\,\,\textbf{Q}_{m}\in\Omega_{m}.

Consider the norm

(2.2)

\|f\|_{*}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}|E(x)^{-1}f(x)|,

which was first introduced in
[
24
]
and also used in
[
1
,
30
]
.
Now we investigate

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L(\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}):=-\Bigl(\displaystyle\frac{\nabla}{i}-A_{\epsilon}(x)\Bigl)^{2}\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}-V_{\epsilon}(x)\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}+f^{\prime}(z_{\textbf{Q}_{m}})\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}\\
=h+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{N+1}c_{j,k}D_{j,k},\penalty\ in\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\displaystyle Re\int\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}\bar{D}_{j,k}=0\penalty\ for\penalty\ j=1,\ldots,m,k=1,\ldots,N+1.\end{array}\right.

Firstly, we give a result which will be used later.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
12
]
, Lemma 3.4)
There exists a constant
C_{N}=6^{N}
such that for any
m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and any

\textbf{Q}_{m}=(Q_{1},Q_{2},...,Q_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{mN},

(2.7)

\sharp\Big\{Q_{j}\Big|\frac{l}{2}\mu\leq|x-Q_{j}|<\frac{(l+1)}{2}\mu\Big\}\leq C_{N}(l+1)^{N-1}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and all
l\in\mathbb{N}.
Particularly, we have

(2.8)

\sharp\Big\{Q_{j}\Big|0\leq|x-Q_{j}|<\frac{\mu}{2}\Big\}\leq C_{N}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
h
with
\|h\|_{*}
bounded and assume that
(\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}},{c_{j,k}})
is a solution to (
2
). Then there exist positive numbers
\mu_{0}

and
C
, such that for all
0<\epsilon<e^{-2\mu},

\mu>\mu_{0}
and
(\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m})\in[0,2\pi]\times\Omega_{m},
one has

(2.9)

\|\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}\|_{*}\leq C\|h\|_{*},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
\mu,m
and
\textbf{Q}_{m}\in\Omega_{m}
.

Proof.

We prove it by contradiction. Assume that there exists a solution
\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}
to (
2
) and
\|h\|_{*}\rightarrow 0
,

\|\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}\|_{*}=1
.

Multiplying the equation in (
2
) by
\bar{D}_{j,k}
and integrating in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we get

(2.10)

Re\int L(\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}})\bar{D}_{j,k}=Re\int h\bar{D}_{j,k}+c_{j,k}\int|D_{j,k}|^{2}.

Considering the exponential decay at infinity of
\frac{\partial w(x)}{\partial x_{k}}
and the definition of
D_{j,k}(k=1,\ldots,N+1)
, we have

(2.11)

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle\int|D_{j,k}|^{2}=\displaystyle\int\Bigl|\bigl(iA_{0,k}z_{Q_{j}}+\frac{\partial w_{Q_{j}}}{\partial x_{k}}\xi_{j}\bigl)\eta_{j}\Bigl|^{2}\\
&\displaystyle=\int A_{0,k}^{2}w_{Q_{j}}^{2}\eta^{2}\Bigl(\frac{2|x-Q_{j}|}{\mu-1}\Bigl)+\int\Bigl(\frac{\partial w_{Q_{j}}}{\partial x_{k}}\Bigl)^{2}\eta^{2}\Bigl(\frac{2|x-Q_{j}|}{\mu-1}\Bigl)\\
&=\displaystyle A_{0,k}^{2}\int w^{2}+A_{0,k}^{2}\int_{B^{C}_{\frac{\mu-1}{2}}(0)}\Bigl[\eta^{2}\Bigl(\frac{2|x|}{\mu-1}\Bigl)-1\Bigl]w^{2}\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\displaystyle\int\Bigl(\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{k}}\Bigl)^{2}+\displaystyle\int_{B^{C}_{\frac{\mu-1}{2}}(0)}\Bigl[\eta^{2}\Bigl(\frac{2|x|}{\mu-1}\Bigl)-1\Bigl)\Bigl]\Bigl(\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{k}}\Bigl)^{2}\\
&=A_{0,k}^{2}\displaystyle\int w^{2}+\int\Bigl(\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{k}}\Bigl)^{2}+O(e^{-\mu}),\penalty\ as\penalty\ \mu\rightarrow+\infty,\penalty\ k=1,2,\ldots,N\end{array}

and

(2.12)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int|D_{j,N+1}|^{2}&=\displaystyle\int\Bigl|iz_{Q_{j}}\eta\Bigl(\frac{2|x-Q_{j}|}{\mu-1}\Bigl)\Bigl|^{2}=\int\Bigl|w_{Q_{j}}\eta\Bigl(\frac{2|x-Q_{j}|}{\mu-1}\Bigl)\Bigl|^{2}\\
&=\displaystyle\int w^{2}+\int_{B^{C}_{\frac{\mu-1}{2}}(0)}\Bigl[\eta^{2}\Bigl(\frac{2|x|}{\mu-1}\Bigl)-1\Bigl]w^{2}=\displaystyle\int w^{2}+O(e^{-\mu}),\penalty\ as\penalty\ \mu\rightarrow+\infty.\end{array}

On the other hand, by Lemma
A.1
we have

(2.13)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Bigl|Re\int h\bar{D}_{j,k}\Bigl|&=\displaystyle\Bigl|Re\int h\bigl(-iA_{0,k}\bar{z}_{Q_{j}}+\frac{\partial w_{Q_{j}}}{\partial x_{k}}\bar{\xi}_{j}\bigl)\eta_{j}\Bigl|\\
&\leq\displaystyle\int|h||A_{0,k}|w_{Q_{j}}|\eta_{j}|+\int|h|\Big|\frac{\partial w_{Q_{j}}}{\partial x_{k}}\Big||\eta_{j}|\\
&\leq C\|h\|_{*}\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{\mu^{2}}{2(\mu+1)}}(Q_{j})}|A_{0,k}|\sum_{j=1}^{m}e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{j}|}w(x-Q_{j})\Bigl|\eta\Bigl(\frac{2|x-Q_{j}|}{\mu-1}\Bigl)\Bigl|\\

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\leq C\|h\|_{*}\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{\mu}{2}}(Q_{j})}e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{j}|}w(x-Q_{j})+C\|h\|_{*}\int_{B_{\frac{\mu}{2}}(Q_{j})}e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{j}|}\Bigl|\frac{\partial w(x-Q_{j})}{\partial x_{k}}\Bigl|\\

and

, as

. Now if we write

\widetilde{D}_{j,k}=\frac{\partial(e^{i\sigma+iA_{0}\cdot(x-Q_{j})}w_{Q_{j}})}{\partial x_{k}}

&\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}}\|_{*}^{1+\delta}\sum_{j=1}^{m}e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{j}|}\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}&|\mathcal{N}(\varphi^{1}_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}})-\mathcal{N}(\varphi^{2}_{\sigma,\textbf{Q}_{m}})|\\

4.

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{m}=J(u_{\bar{\textbf{Q}}_{m}})\\
&\leq\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{m-1}+I(z)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int\tilde{V}(x)|w_{Q_{m+1}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int\epsilon^{2}|\tilde{A}(x)|^{2}|w_{Q_{m+1}}|^{2}\\
&\,\,\,\,\,\,\displaystyle-\frac{\vartheta}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}w(|\bar{Q}_{m}-\bar{Q}_{j}|)+O\Big(e^{-\beta\mu}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}e^{-|\bar{Q}_{m}-\bar{Q}_{j}|}\Big)+O(\epsilon)\\
&\leq\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{m-1}+I(z)-\frac{\vartheta}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}w(|\bar{Q}_{m}-\bar{Q}_{j}|)+O\Big(e^{-\beta\mu}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}e^{-|\overline{Q}_{m}-\bar{Q}_{j}|}\Big)+O(\epsilon).\end{array}

By the definition of the configuration set, we observe that given a ball of size
\mu
, there are at most
C_{N}:=6^{N}
number of non-overlapping ball of
size
\mu
surrounding this ball. Since
|\bar{Q}_{j}-\bar{Q}_{k}|=\mu
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}w(|\bar{Q}_{m}-\bar{Q}_{j}|)=w(|\bar{Q}_{m}-\bar{Q}_{j}|)+\sum_{k\neq j}w(|\bar{Q}_{m}-\bar{Q}_{k}|)

and

(4.33)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq j}w(|\bar{Q}_{m}-\bar{Q}_{k}|)&\leq Ce^{-\mu}+C_{N}e^{-\mu-\frac{\mu}{2}}+\ldots+C_{N}^{k}e^{-\mu-\frac{k\mu}{2}}\\
&\leq\displaystyle Ce^{-\mu}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{j(\ln C_{N}-\frac{\mu}{2})}\leq\displaystyle Ce^{-\mu},\end{array}

if
C_{N}<e^{\frac{\mu}{2}}
, which is true for
\mu
large enough.

Hence, we have

(4.34)

C_{m}\leq C_{m-1}+I(z)+C\epsilon-\frac{\vartheta}{4}w(\mu)+O(e^{-(1+\beta)\mu})<C_{m-1}+I(z),

which contradicts to (
4.5
) in Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Appendix A
Some technical estimates

In this section, we give some technical estimates which are used before.

Denote

\Lambda_{j}:=\Big\{x\Big||x-Q_{j}|\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\Big\},\,\,\,\,\Lambda=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\Lambda_{j}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\Lambda^{C}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Lambda.

Lemma A.1
.

For any
x\in\Lambda_{j}(j=1,...,m)
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we have

(A.1)

\sum_{k=1}^{m}e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{k}|}\leq e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{j}|}+Ce^{-\frac{\gamma\mu}{2}}.

For any
x\in\Lambda^{c}
, we have

(A.2)

\sum_{k=1}^{m}e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{k}|}\leq Ce^{-\frac{\gamma\mu}{2}}.

Proof.

Note that given a ball of size

\mu
, there are at most
C_{N}:=6^{N}
number of non-overlapping ball of size
\mu

surrounding this ball. Since
|x-Q_{j}|\leq\frac{\mu}{2},
we have

|x-Q_{k}|\geq|Q_{k}-Q_{j}|-|x-Q_{j}|\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\,\,\,\,\text{for\penalty\ all}\,\,\,k\neq j.

Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{k}|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{j}|}+\sum_{k\neq j}e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{k}|}\leq e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{j}|}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}C_{N}^{k}e^{-\frac{k\gamma\mu}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{j}|}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{k(\ln C_{N}-\frac{\gamma\mu}{2})}\leq e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{j}|}+O(e^{-(\frac{\gamma\mu}{2}-\ln C_{N})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma|x-Q_{j}|}+Ce^{-\frac{\gamma\mu}{2}},

if
\ln C_{N}<\frac{\gamma\mu}{2}
, which is true for
\mu
large enough.

The proof of (
A.2
) is similar.
∎

Proof of (
4.9
)
.

By direct computation, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Bigl|\displaystyle Re\int\epsilon^{2}|\tilde{A}(x)|^{2}z_{Q}\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}\Bigl|&\leq\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}\Bigl(\int|\tilde{A}(x)|^{2}|z_{Q}|^{2}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\int|\tilde{A}(x)|^{2}|\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}|^{2}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\delta\epsilon^{2}\int|\tilde{A}(x)|^{2}w_{Q}^{2}+C_{\delta}\epsilon^{2}\int|\tilde{A}(x)|^{2}|\varphi_{\sigma,Q}|^{2}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\delta\epsilon^{2}\int|\tilde{A}(x)|^{2}w_{Q}^{2}+C\|\varphi_{\sigma,Q}\|^{2}_{H^{1}},\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Bigl|\displaystyle Re\int\epsilon\tilde{V}(x)z_{Q}\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}\Bigl|&\leq\displaystyle\epsilon\Bigl(\int\tilde{V}(x)|z_{Q}|^{2}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\int\tilde{V}(x)|\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}|^{2}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\delta\epsilon\int\tilde{V}(x)w_{Q}^{2}+C_{\delta}\epsilon\int\tilde{V}(x)|\varphi_{\sigma,Q}|^{2}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\delta\epsilon\int\tilde{V}(x)w_{Q}^{2}+C\|\varphi_{\sigma,Q}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\Bigl|\displaystyle Re\displaystyle\int\epsilon\tilde{A}(x)\Bigl(\frac{\nabla\varphi_{\sigma,Q}}{i}\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}-A_{0}\varphi_{\sigma,Q}\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}\Bigl)\Bigl|\\
&\leq\displaystyle\epsilon\Bigl(\int|\nabla\varphi_{\sigma,Q}|^{2}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\int|\tilde{A}|^{2}|\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}|^{2}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{2}}\displaystyle+C\epsilon\int|\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}|^{2}\leq\displaystyle C\|\varphi_{\sigma,Q}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.\end{array}

Similarly, we can prove

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Bigl|\displaystyle Re\int\epsilon\tilde{A}(x)\Bigl(\frac{\nabla\varphi_{\sigma,Q}}{i}\bar{z}_{Q}-A_{0}\varphi_{\sigma,Q}\bar{z}_{Q}\Bigl)\Bigl|&\leq\displaystyle\delta\epsilon\int|\tilde{A}|^{2}w_{Q}^{2}+C\|\varphi_{\sigma,Q}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.\end{array}

Moreover, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\Bigl|\displaystyle Re\int\frac{\epsilon}{i}\tilde{A}(x)\cdot\nabla w_{Q}\xi_{Q}\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}\Bigl|\\
&=\displaystyle\Bigl|\displaystyle-Re\int\frac{\epsilon}{i}w_{Q}\Bigl(div\tilde{A}\xi_{Q}\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}+i\tilde{A}\cdot A_{0}\xi_{Q}\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}+\xi_{Q}\tilde{A}\cdot\nabla\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma,Q}\Bigl)\Bigl|\\

&\,\,\,\,\,\,\displaystyle+\epsilon\Bigl(\int|\tilde{A}|^{2}w_{Q}^{2}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\int|\nabla\varphi_{\sigma,Q}|^{2}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\delta\epsilon\int|div\tilde{A}|^{2}w_{Q}^{2}+C_{\delta}\epsilon\int\varphi_{\sigma,Q}^{2}+\delta\epsilon\int|\tilde{A}|^{2}w_{Q}^{2}+C_{\delta}\epsilon\int\varphi_{\sigma,Q}^{2}\\

where we can choose

) holds.

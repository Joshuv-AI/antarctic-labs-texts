Spatial correlation functions for non-ergodic stochastic processes of macroscopic systems
J.P. Wittmer, A.N. Semenov, J. Baschnagel

Abstract
Focusing on non-ergodic macroscopic systems we reconsider the variances δ ​ 𝒪 2 \delta{\cal O}^{2} of time averages 𝒪 ⁡ [ 𝐱 ] {\cal O}[\mathbf{x}] of time-series 𝐱 \mathbf{x} . The total variance δ ​ 𝒪 tot 2 = δ ​ 𝒪 int 2 + δ ​ 𝒪 ext 2 \delta{\cal O}^{2}_{\mathrm{tot}}=\delta{\cal O}^{2}_{\mathrm{int}}+\delta{\cal O}^{2}_{\mathrm{ext}} (direct average over all time-series) is known to be the sum of an internal variance δ ​ 𝒪 int 2 \delta{\cal O}^{2}_{\mathrm{int}} (fluctuations within the meta-basins) and an external variance δ ​ 𝒪 ext 2 \delta{\cal O}^{2}_{\mathrm{ext}} (fluctuations between meta-basins). It is shown that whenever 𝒪 ⁡ [ 𝐱 ] {\cal O}[\mathbf{x}] can be expressed as a volume average of a local field 𝒪 𝐫 {\cal O}_{{\bf r}} the three variances can be written as volume averages of correlation functions C tot ​ ( 𝐫 ) C_{\mathrm{tot}}({\bf r}) , C int ​ ( 𝐫 ) C_{\mathrm{int}}({\bf r}) and C ext ​ ( 𝐫 ) C_{\mathrm{ext}}({\bf r}) with C tot ​ ( 𝐫 ) = C int ​ ( 𝐫 ) + C ext ​ ( 𝐫 ) C_{\mathrm{tot}}({\bf r})=C_{\mathrm{int}}({\bf r})+C_{\mathrm{ext}}({\bf r}) . The dependences of the δ ​ 𝒪 2 \delta{\cal O}^{2} on the sampling time Δ ​ τ \Delta\tau and the system volume V V can thus be traced back to C int ​ ( 𝐫 ) C_{\mathrm{int}}({\bf r}) and C ext ​ ( 𝐫 ) C_{\mathrm{ext}}({\bf r}) . Various relations are illustrated using lattice spring models with spatially correlated spring constants.

5.1
General relations for non-ergodic systems

As demonstrated in detail in Appendix

the integrals,

3

5

are solved by

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{tot}}({\bf q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{l}K[{\cal O}_{l{\bf q}}]({\bf q})-{\cal O}^{2}\delta_{{\bf q}{\bf 0}}

(47)

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{int}}({\bf q})

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{c}\mathbf{E}^{k}K[{\cal O}_{ck{\bf q}}-{\cal O}_{c{\bf q}}]({\bf q})

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{ext}}({\bf q})

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{c}K[{\cal O}_{c{\bf q}}]({\bf q})-{\cal O}^{2}\delta_{{\bf q}{\bf 0}}

(with
\delta_{{\bf q}{\bf 0}}

The internal correlation function

and an “external” contribution

(50)

in agreement with Eq. (
6
) stated in the Introduction.

Just as

,
\delta{\cal O}_{\mathrm{int}}
and

and similarly in real space,

- but also the

-dependence drops

Conclusion

6.1
General points made

Extending recent work on stochastic processes in non-ergodic macroscopic systems
spmP1
;
spmP2
;
spmP3

we have investigated the different types of spatial correlation functions
C({\bf r})
related to the
macroscopic variances
\delta{\cal O}^{2}
of observables
{\cal O}[\mathbf{x}]
of time-series
\mathbf{x}
.
As reminded in Sec.
4
the standard total variance
\delta{\cal O}^{2}_{\mathrm{tot}}(\Delta\tau)

is the sum of an internal variance
\delta{\cal O}^{2}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Delta\tau)
and an external variance
\delta{\cal O}^{2}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\Delta\tau)
,
cf. Eq. (
1
).
While
\delta{\cal O}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Delta\tau)\simeq 1/\sqrt{\Delta\tau}
for
\Delta\tau\gg\tau_{\mathrm{b}}
,

\delta{\cal O}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\Delta\tau,V)\simeq\Delta_{\mathrm{ne}}(V)
becomes constant.
One motivation of this work (cf. Sec.
1.2
)
was to understand the
V
-dependence of the non-ergodicity parameter
\Delta_{\mathrm{ne}}
in systems
with (possibly long-ranged) spatial correlations.
The generally important novel key results of this study
were presented in Sec.
5
and Appendix
A
. As shown there,
assuming
{\cal O}[\mathbf{x}]
to be a spatial average of a local field
{\cal O}_{{\bf r}}
,
the three global variances can be written as volume averages of
the three spatial correlation functions

C_{\mathrm{tot}}({\bf r})
,
C_{\mathrm{int}}({\bf r})
and
C_{\mathrm{ext}}({\bf r})
and, moreover,

C_{\mathrm{tot}}({\bf r})=C_{\mathrm{int}}({\bf r})+C_{\mathrm{ext}}({\bf r})

The
\Delta\tau
- and
V
-dependences of the global variances can thus be traced back to
the internal and external correlation functions
C_{\mathrm{int}}({\bf r})
and
C_{\mathrm{ext}}({\bf r})
.

6.2

Focusing on the arithmetic mean

Appendix A
Spatial correlations of periodic microcells

A.1
Some useful general relations

Let us begin by stating several useful general relations for spatial correlation functions
of
d
-dimensional, real, discrete and periodic fields.
As defined by Eq. (
13
) or Eq. (
14
) we consider
the instantaneous correlation function
K[y_{{\bf r}}]({\bf r})
of a field
y_{{\bf r}}

of volume average
y\equiv\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}}y_{{\bf r}}
. Obviously,

K[y_{{\bf r}}-y]({\bf r})=K[y_{{\bf r}}]({\bf r})-y^{2}.

(58)

Let us assume that the field
y_{l{\bf r}}
additionally depends on an index
l
.
We use below the averages
y_{l}=\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}}y_{l{\bf r}}
,
y_{{\bf r}}=\mathbf{E}^{l}y_{l{\bf r}}
and

y=\mathbf{E}^{l}y_{l}=\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}}y_{{\bf r}}
.
Rewriting Eq. (
58
) and summing over
l
gives

\mathbf{E}^{l}K[y_{l{\bf r}}-y_{l}]({\bf r})=\mathbf{E}^{l}K[y_{l{\bf r}}]({\bf r})-\mathbf{E}^{l}y_{l}^{2}.

(59)

Also it is seen by expansion using Eq. (
13
) that

\mathbf{E}^{l}K[y_{l{\bf r}}-\lambda y_{{\bf r}}]({\bf r})=\mathbf{E}^{l}K[y_{l{\bf r}}]({\bf r})-\lambda(2-\lambda)K[y_{{\bf r}}]({\bf r})

(60)

for any real constant
\lambda
.

We remind that
\mathbf{V}^{l}y_{l}=\mathbf{E}^{l}y_{l}^{2}-y^{2}=\mathbf{E}^{l}(y_{l}-y)^{2}
.
Using again Eq. (
58
) and the periodicity of the grid
the variance
\mathbf{V}^{l}y_{l}
may be written as the volume average

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{l}y_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}}C({\bf r})\mbox{ with }

(61)

\displaystyle C({\bf r})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{l}K[y_{l{\bf r}}-y]({\bf r})\equiv\mathbf{E}^{l}K[y_{l{\bf r}}]({\bf r})-y^{2}

(62)

being the
l
-averaged correlation function in real space.
By comparing Eq. (
59
) and Eq. (
62
) we may also write

C({\bf r})=\mathbf{E}^{l}K[y_{l{\bf r}}-y_{l}]({\bf r})+\mathbf{V}^{l}y_{l}

(63)

which using Eq. (
61
) implies
\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}}\mathbf{E}^{l}K[y_{l{\bf r}}-y_{l}]({\bf r})=0
.
It is useful to state two important limits for
C({\bf r})
:

(i)
At the origin we have

C({\bf r}={\bf 0})=\mathbf{V}^{l{\bf r}^{\prime}}y_{l{\bf r}^{\prime}}=\mathbf{E}^{l}\mathbf{V}^{{\bf r}^{\prime}}y_{l{\bf r}^{\prime}}+\mathbf{V}^{l}\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}^{\prime}}y_{l{\bf r}^{\prime}}

(64)

and
(ii)

C({\bf r})
exactly vanishes if and only if

\mathbf{E}^{l}\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}^{\prime}}y_{{\bf r}^{\prime}+{\bf r}}y_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}=\mathbf{E}^{l}\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}^{\prime}}y_{{\bf r}^{\prime}+{\bf r}}\times\mathbf{E}^{l}\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}^{\prime}}y_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}=y^{2}

(65)

as it happens for most (albeit not all) fields for sufficiently large
r=|{\bf r}|
.
See Appendix
B.1
for an exception relevant for the present study.

Moreover, with

being an
l
-independent quantity
it follows from Eq. (
61

\mathbf{V}^{l}(y_{l}-z)=\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}}\ \mathbf{E}^{l}K[(y_{l{\bf r}}-z_{{\bf r}})-(y-z)]({\bf r}).

(66)

Since
\mathbf{V}^{l}y_{l}=\mathbf{V}^{l}(y_{l}-z)
this implies quite generally that

\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}}\mathbf{E}^{l}K[\delta y_{l{\bf r}}]({\bf r})=\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}}\mathbf{E}^{l}K[\delta y_{l{\bf r}}-\delta z_{{\bf r}}]({\bf r})

(67)

with

and
\delta z_{{\bf r}}=z_{{\bf r}}-z

i.e. the correlation function of a field

can be shifted by an

l
-independent field

without
changing the
l
-averaged volume average.

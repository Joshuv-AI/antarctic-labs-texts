Low regularity well-posedness of KP-I equations: the three-dimensional case Thanks: *Corresponding author
Sebastian Herr, Akansha Sanwal, Robert Schippa*

Abstract
. In this paper, low regularity local well-posedness results for the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili–I equation posed in spatial dimension d = 3 d=3 are proved. Periodic, non-periodic and mixed settings as well as generalized dispersion relations are considered. In the weak dispersion regime, these initial value problems show a quasilinear behavior so that bilinear and energy estimates on frequency dependent time scales are used in the analysis.

37L50, 42B37

1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem for the dispersion generalized Kadomtsev–Petviashvili–I equation

\left.\begin{split}\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}D_{x}^{\alpha}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\Delta_{y}u&=\partial_{x}(u^{2}),\\
u(0)&=\phi\in E^{s}(\mathbb{D}),\end{split}\right\}

(KP-I)

for real-valued functions
u:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and dispersion parameter
\alpha\in[2,4]
.
We treat the case of three-dimensional spatial domains
\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{K}_{1}\times\mathbb{K}_{2}\times\mathbb{K}_{3}
, where
\mathbb{K}_{i}\in\{\mathbb{R};\mathbb{T}\}
, for
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
. We write
u=u(x,y,t)
, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{K}_{1}
, and
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{K}_{2}\times\mathbb{K}_{3}
so that
\Delta_{y}=\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}+\partial_{y_{2}}^{2}
.
D^{\alpha}_{x}
is defined via the Fourier transform

(D_{x}^{\alpha}f)\widehat{\,}(\xi,\eta)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta),

If
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{K}_{2}\times\mathbb{K}_{3}
, then we additionally assume that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(x,y)dx=0

to make the linear evolution well-defined, and the nonlinear evolution preserves mean zero.
Formally, real-valued solutions also conserve the
L^{2}(\mathbb{D})
-norm and the energy (Hamiltonian)

\mathbf{e}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{2}|D_{x}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{3}u^{3}+\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{y}u|^{2}dxdy.

(1)

In two dimensions, the KP-I equation has been introduced as a model for the propagation of dispersive waves with small amplitude under weakly transverse effects
[
11
]
and later has been found to be completely integrable
[
3
]
. In this paper, we address the local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem in the three-dimensional case. In
[
2
]
, it was rigorously derived in the long wave transonic limit regime (where the amplitude is close to one) from the Gross-Pitaevskii equation after suitable rescaling, see also
[
12
]
for further information and references.

We consider initial data in Sobolev spaces
E^{s}(\mathbb{D})
of real valued functions, which are defined for
s\geq 0
via

\|f\|_{E^{s}(\mathbb{D})}:=\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}p(\xi,\eta)\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}},

with respect to the three-dimensional product of Lebesgue or counting measures, see Section
2
for more details. The symbol
p(\xi,\eta)=1+\frac{|\eta|}{|\xi|}
is motivated by the last term in energy (
1
) and crucially used in commutator arguments, see Section
5
.

The main result of this paper is

Theorem 1.1
.

(i)

Let
\alpha=2
. The Cauchy problem (
KP-I
) is locally well-posed for initial data
\phi\in E^{s}(\mathbb{D})
provided that
s>2
.

(ii)

Let
\alpha\in(2,4)
and
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{K}_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The Cauchy problem (
KP-I
) is locally well-posed for initial data
\phi\in E^{s}(\mathbb{D})
provided that
s>3-\frac{\alpha}{2}
.

By local well-posedness, we refer to existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence of solutions in function spaces
F^{s}(T)\hookrightarrow C([-T,T];E^{s}(\mathbb{D}))
.
In two dimensions, (
KP-I
) is known to be quasilinear for
\alpha=2
in the sense that it cannot be solved by the contraction mapping principle.
In order to improve the standard energy method, Ionescu–Kenig–Tataru
[
10
]
introduced an approach based on localizations to frequency dependent time scales.
Roughly speaking, this is the strategy we will use for the proof of the main result. The use of frequency-dependent time localization is justified in Appendix
B
,
where we prove on
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{R}^{3}
that for certain
\alpha\geq 2
(
KP-I
) cannot be solved by Picard iteration in anisotropic Sobolev spaces.
The proof of well-posedness crucially makes use of short-time bilinear estimates,
which become effective for resonant interactions (which are transverse).

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
we use frequency-dependent time localization to overcome the derivative loss in the resonant interaction. For
\alpha\in[2,4)
we choose

T(N)=N^{-(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}.

This is again motivated by the bilinear Strichartz estimate we can prove in the resonant case on domains
\mathbb{K}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This suffices to ameliorate one derivative in case a high frequency interacts with a low one in the resonant case.

We remark that in the case
\alpha\in(2,4)
one also obtains the well-posedness result with regularity threshold
s>2
on general domains. This follows by choosing the time scale
T(N)=N^{-1}
instead, we omit the details.
However, it is not clear how to improve the regularity threshold
s>2
on general domains in the case
\alpha\in(2,4)
.

Since
\Delta_{y}
is
\mathrm{O}(2)
-invariant, the result extends to more general
y-
domains with
d_{1}
non-periodic and
d_{2}
periodic transverse directions, where
d_{1}+d_{2}=2
.

The second main result is for the fifth-order KP-I equation, where we can use perturbative arguments. We show a well-posedness result in anisotropic Sobolev spaces
H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{D})
, which are defined for
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 0
via

\|\phi\|_{H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{D})}=\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}\langle\eta\rangle^{s_{2}}\hat{\phi}(\xi,\eta)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha=4
,
s_{1},s_{2}>\frac{1}{2}
, and
\mathbb{D}\in\{\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\}
. The Cauchy problem (
KP-I
) is locally well-posed for initial data
\phi\in H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{D})
, with a real-analytic flow map.

If
u
solves (
KP-I
), then so does

u_{\lambda}(x,y,t):=\lambda^{-\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha+2}}u(\lambda^{-\frac{2}{\alpha+2}}x,\lambda^{-1}y,\lambda^{-\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+2}}t),

(2)

with scaled initial data
\phi_{\lambda}(x,y):=\lambda^{-\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha+2}}\phi(\lambda^{-\frac{2}{\alpha+2}}x,\lambda^{-1}y)
.
For the corresponding homogeneous norms, we observe that

\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\lambda^{\frac{3-\alpha}{\alpha+2}-\frac{2s_{1}}{\alpha+2}-s_{2}}\|\phi\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(3)

Notice that for high frequencies,
E^{s}
corresponds to
H^{s,0}\cap H^{s-1,1}

which indicates that the regularity assumptions both in Theorem
1.1
and in Theorem
1.2
are sub-critical.

Let

s(\alpha)=3-\frac{\alpha}{2}.

We shall prove the following set of estimates for
s^{\prime}\geqslant s>s(\alpha)
:

\left.\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u\|_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}&\lesssim\|u\|_{B^{s^{\prime}}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s^{\prime}}(T)},\\
\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s^{\prime}}(T)}&\lesssim\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)},\\
\|u\|^{2}_{B^{s^{\prime}}(T)}&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{E^{s^{\prime}}}+\|u\|^{2}_{F^{s}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{s^{\prime}(T)}}.\end{array}\right\}

This yields a priori estimates in
E^{s^{\prime}}
for
s^{\prime}\geq s
and small initial data. The large data case will be handled by rescaling.

The second set of estimates yields Lipschitz continuous dependence at the regularity
E^{0}
depending on the norms of the initial data in
E^{s}
: Let
v=u_{1}-u_{2}
be a difference of two solutions. Then, we find the following estimates to hold:

\left.\begin{array}[]{cl}\|v\|_{F^{0}(T)}&\lesssim\|v\|_{B^{0}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}(v(u_{1}+u_{2}))\|_{\mathcal{N}^{0}(T)}\\
\|\partial_{x}(v(u_{1}+u_{2}))\|_{\mathcal{N}^{0}(T)}&\lesssim\|v\|_{F^{0}(T)}(\|u_{1}\|_{F^{s}(T)}+\|u_{2}\|_{F^{s}(T)})\\
\|v\|^{2}_{B^{0}(T)}&\lesssim\|v(0)\|^{2}_{E^{0}}+\|v\|^{2}_{F^{0}(T)}(\|u_{1}\|_{F^{s}(T)}+\|u_{2}\|_{F^{s}(T)}).\end{array}\right\}

Finally, we prove continuous dependence in
E^{s}
using frequency envelopes (cf.
[
9
]
).

We remark that in the companion paper
[
18
]
the second and third author address the dispersion-generalized KP-I equation in the case
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Depending on the dispersion, that problem exhibits semi- and quasilinear behaviour.

Concerning the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we notice that it is a semilinear problem which can be treated by a direct iterative method.
However, in order to avoid a derivative loss, we need to work with critical norms involving
U^{2}
and
V^{2}
spaces.

2.
Notations and function spaces

2.1.
Fourier transform

To prove local well-posedness for large data, we shall rescale the domain. This requires us to consider the rescaled tori
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\lambda\mathbb{Z})
for
\lambda\geq 1
. We have to keep track of the dependence of the estimates with respect to
\lambda
. All quantities are defined so that Plancherel’s theorem is valid with
\lambda
-independent constant.
Let

\mathbb{D}_{\nu}=\mathbb{K}_{\nu}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}),\text{ where }\mathbb{K}_{\lambda}\in\{\mathbb{R};\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\},\quad d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\quad\nu=\lambda^{\frac{2}{\alpha+2}},\quad d_{1}+d_{2}=2.

By symmetry of the equation in
y_{1},y_{2}

we can assume this specific order to cover all cases considered in the main results.
For the dual space, we let
\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}=\{\frac{k}{\lambda}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. The dual domain is defined by

\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}:=\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{K}_{\nu}^{*}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}})\},\text{ where }\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{*}=\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}\text{ and }\mathbb{R}^{*}=\mathbb{R}.

We define the Fourier transform
\hat{f}:\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}
of a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda})
by

\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}}e^{-ix\xi}e^{-iy\cdot\eta}f(x,y)dxdy.

Its inverse is given by

\check{f}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}}e^{ix\xi}e^{iy\cdot\eta}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)d\xi d\eta,

where we use the normalized counting measure in
\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}
, i.e.,

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}}f(k)dk:=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}}f(k),

and Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}
coordinates.
In this setting, Plancherel’s theorem gives

\|f\|_{L_{x,y}^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda})}=(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*})},

Using this notation, the space-time Fourier transform and its inverse are given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}f(\xi,\eta,\tau)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f(\cdot,\cdot,t)}(\xi,\eta)e^{-it\tau}dt\\
\mathcal{F}^{-1}f(x,y,t)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widecheck{f(\cdot,\cdot,\tau)}(x,y)e^{it\tau}d\tau.\end{split}

The dispersion relation of the dispersion-generalized KP-I equation is denoted by

\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\xi+\frac{|\eta|^{2}}{\xi},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{K}_{\nu}^{*}\backslash\{0\},\quad\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}.

(4)

Define the group of unitary operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda})
(restricted to mean zero if
x\in\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
) by

\widehat{S_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}}(\xi,\eta)=e^{it\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)}\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi,\eta).

This is the propagator for the linear homogeneous equation

\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}D_{x}^{\alpha}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\Delta_{y}u=0.

2.3.
Linear Strichartz estimates

Next, we prove an
L^{4}_{x,y,t}
-Strichartz estimate which we will use for estimates of non-resonant interactions. For this purpose, it suffices to take advantage of the evolution in the

Let

with

and
\epsilon>0
if

We recall an

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
\lambda\geq 1
, the following estimate holds:

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{4}_{y,t}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}\times[0,1])}\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}\|f\|_{H^{\epsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}})}.

On Euclidean space, i.e.,
d_{2}=0
this is standard (cf.
[
22

[
1
]

Next, we prove an
L^{4}_{x,y,t}
-Strichartz estimate for the linear propagator
S_{\alpha}
. We will assume certain lower bounds on the frequencies: If the

\nu

Lemma 2.4
.

Consider
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{K}_{\nu}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}
, and
K,N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, and additionally if
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{T}_{\nu}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
, we suppose that
K,N\geq\nu^{-1}
. Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an interval with
|I|\sim K
and
|\xi|\sim N
for
\xi\in I
. Further, let
M\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[\lambda^{-1},\infty)
and
Q_{M}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be any cube of side-length
M
. For all
f\in L^{2}_{x,y}(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F}_{x,y}(f))\subseteq I\times Q_{M}
we have

\|S_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{4}_{x,y,t}(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\times[0,1])}\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{4}}C(N,M)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}

(9)

with
\epsilon
as in Lemma
2.3
and

C(N,M)=\begin{cases}M^{\frac{1}{2}},&\quad M\lesssim 1,\\
\lambda^{\epsilon}(N^{\frac{1}{4}}\vee 1)M^{\epsilon},&\quad M\gg 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

We can use Bernstein’s inequality in the
\xi
-frequencies, Plancherel’s theorem, and Minkowski’s inequality to write

\begin{split}\|\int e^{i(x.\xi+y.\eta+t\xi|\xi|^{\alpha}+t\eta^{2}/\xi)}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)d\xi d\eta\big\|_{L^{4}_{x,y,t}(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\times[0,1])}&\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{4}}\big\|\big(\int d\xi\big|\int d\eta e^{i(y.\eta+t\eta^{2}/\xi)}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\big|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{L^{4}_{y,t}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}\times[0,1])}\\
&\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(\int d\xi\big\|\int d\eta e^{i(y.\eta+t\eta^{2}/\xi)}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\big\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{y,t}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}\times[0,1])}\big)^{1/2}.\end{split}

For
M\lesssim 1
, we use Galilean invariance and Bernstein’s inequality to conclude the bound. If
M\geq 1
, we find for
\xi>0
(the case
\xi<0
is treated by time-inversion)

\big\|\int d\eta e^{i(y.\eta+t|\eta|^{2}/\xi)}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\big\|_{L^{4}_{y,t}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}\times[0,1])}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{4}}\big\|\int d\eta e^{i(y.\eta+t\eta^{2})}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\big\|_{L^{4}_{y,t}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}\times[0,\xi^{-1}])}.

We estimate by Galilean invariance and Strichartz estimates (Lemma
2.3
) on
N^{-1}\vee 1
unit time intervals

\big\|\int d\eta e^{i(y.\eta+t|\eta|^{2}/\xi)}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\big\|_{L^{4}_{y,t}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}\times[0,1])}\lesssim(N^{\frac{1}{4}}\vee 1)M^{\epsilon}\lambda^{\epsilon}\|\hat{f}(\xi,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\eta}},

and finally we take the
L^{2}
-norm in
\xi
.
∎

The estimate could be further improved taking into account frequency dependent time localization, however, this is not required for the present analysis.

We point out that only the
L^{4}_{y,t}
-Strichartz estimates on the two-dimensional torus is not scale-invariant and loses a factor
\lambda^{\epsilon}
.

We note the following consequence for small

(10)

Proof.

After a Littlewood-Paley decomposition in the

We record a consequence for the interaction of two functions with specified frequency and modulation support. We will use this estimate for non-resonant interactions (see Section

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
N_{1},N_{2}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
N_{2}\leq N_{1}
,
L_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
M_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[\lambda^{-1},\infty)
for
i=1,2,3
, and
N_{i}\geq\nu^{-1}
if
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{T}_{\nu}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}}_{\lambda}
. Let
f^{(i)}:\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be supported in
\tilde{D}_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}}
for
i=1,2
. Then the following estimate holds:

\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{D}_{N_{3},M_{3},L_{3}}}(f^{(1)}*f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}C(N_{1},N_{2})N_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{\min}^{\epsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}

with

C(N_{1},N_{2})=\begin{cases}(N_{1}N_{2})^{\frac{1}{4}},\;&N_{2}\geq 1,\\
N_{1}^{\frac{1}{4}},\;&N_{2}\leq 1\leq N_{1},\\
1,\;&N_{1}\leq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

By almost orthogonality, we can suppose that
\text{supp}(f^{(i)})\subseteq I_{i}\times Q_{i}\times\mathbb{R}
with
I_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
an interval of length
N_{\min}
and
Q_{i}
a cube of length
M_{\min}
. Then, we can use two
L^{4}_{x,y,t}
-Strichartz estimates (
9
) to find in case of
N_{2}\geq 1
:

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{D}_{N_{3},M_{3},J_{3}}}(f^{(1)}*f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}&\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}f^{(1)}\|_{L^{4}_{x,y,t}}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}f^{(2)}\|_{L^{4}_{x,y,t}}\\
&\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}N_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}(N_{1}N_{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}M_{\min}^{\epsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

If
N_{2}\leq 1\leq N_{1}
, we find by (
9
)

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{D}_{N_{3},M_{3},L_{3}}}(f^{(1)}*f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}&\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}f^{(1)}\|_{L^{4}_{x,y,t}}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}f^{(2)}\|_{L^{4}_{x,y,t}}\\
&\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}N_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{4}}M_{\min}^{\epsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Lastly, if
N_{1}\leq 1
, (
9
) yields

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{D}_{N_{3},M_{3},L_{3}}}(f^{(1)}*f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}&\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}f^{(1)}\|_{L^{4}_{x,y,t}}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}f^{(2)}\|_{L^{4}_{x,y,t}}\\
&\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}N_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{\min}^{\epsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

,

,

(11)

3.
Resonance and bilinear estimates

We analyze the resonance function to obtain good bilinear estimates in the resonant case. For
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{K}^{*}
(non-vanishing) and
\eta=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

\begin{split}\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{2})&:=\omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})+\omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{2},\eta_{2})-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})\\
&=-\Omega_{KdV}^{(\alpha)}+\frac{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}}\Big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\Big|^{2}.\end{split}

(12)

Above
\Omega^{(\alpha)}_{KdV}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
denotes the resonance for the dispersion generalized KdV equation:

\Omega^{(\alpha)}_{KdV}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})-|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\xi_{1}-|\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}\xi_{2}.

We define the resonant case for the higher-dimensional KP-I equations by

|\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{2})|\ll|\Omega^{(\alpha)}_{KdV}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|.

(13)

Suppose that
|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|\sim\max(|\xi_{1}|,|\xi_{2}|)
, and let
N_{\max},N_{\min}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

N_{\max}\sim\max(|\xi_{1}|,|\xi_{2}|,|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|),\quad N_{\min}\sim\min(|\xi_{1}|,|\xi_{2}|,|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|).

The resonance condition requires

N_{\max}^{\alpha}\sim\Big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\Big|^{2}.

The gradient of the dispersion relation is given by

\nabla\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)=\Big((\alpha+1)|\xi|^{\alpha}-\frac{|\eta|^{2}}{\xi^{2}},\frac{2\eta}{\xi}\Big).

(14)

Consequently, in case of (
13
), we find

|\nabla\omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})-\nabla\omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{2},\eta_{2})|\gtrsim\Big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\Big|\sim N_{\max}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

This means that in case of a resonant interaction with
|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|\sim\max(|\xi_{1}|,|\xi_{2}|)
the waves are transverse.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
I,J
be intervals and
f:J\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. Then

|\{x:f(x)\in I\}|\leqslant\frac{|I|}{\inf_{y}|f^{\prime}(y)|}.

(15)

Proof.

This is a consequence of the mean value theorem. Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in J
be such that
f(x_{1}),f(x_{2})\in I
. Then

|x_{1}-x_{2}|=\frac{|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})|}{|f^{\prime}(\xi)|}\leqslant\frac{|I|}{\inf_{y}|f^{\prime}(y)|}.

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

(Transverse
L^{2}
bilinear estimate)
Let
d_{1}+d_{2}=2
,
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and
u_{1},u_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}\times\mathbb{R})
have their Fourier supports in
\tilde{D}_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}}
for
i=1,2
, respectively, with
N_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
,and additionally
N_{i}\geq\nu^{-1}
for
d_{1}=0
, and
M_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[\lambda^{-1},\infty)
, and let
N\sim\max(N_{1},N_{2})
. Suppose that for frequencies in the Fourier support, the resonance condition (
13
) holds. Then, we have

\|P_{N}(u_{1}u_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,y,t}}\lesssim M_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(d_{2}+\frac{L_{\max}}{N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y,t}}\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y,t}}.

(16)

Proof.

From Plancherel and Cauchy-Schwarz, we have

\begin{split}\|u_{1}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}&=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}}\hat{u}_{1}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\tau_{1})\hat{u}_{2}(\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1},\tau-\tau_{1},)d\xi_{1}d\eta_{1}d\tau_{1}\Big|\\
&\lesssim L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{\xi,\eta,\tau:\,|\xi|\sim N,}|E(\tau,\xi,\eta)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

(17)

where
|\cdot|
denotes the measure on
\mathbb{D}^{*}_{\lambda}
and the set
E(\tau,\xi,\eta)\subset\mathbb{D}^{*}_{\lambda}
is given by

E(\tau,\xi,\eta):=\{(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\in A_{N_{1},M_{1}}:\eqref{eq:rescon}\text{ holds},\;|\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1})|\leqslant L_{\max},(\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1})\in A_{N_{2},M_{2}}\}.

It remains to estimate
|E(\tau,\xi,\eta)|
. Define

\phi(\xi,\xi_{1},\eta,\eta_{1},\tau):=\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1})=\tau-|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\xi_{1}-\frac{|\eta_{1}|^{2}}{\xi_{1}}-|\xi-\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}(\xi-\xi_{1})-\frac{|\eta-\eta_{1}|^{2}}{\xi-\xi_{1}}.

From (
13
), we have

\big|\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial{\eta_{11}}}\big|\gtrsim N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\text{ or }\big|\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial{\eta_{12}}}\big|\gtrsim N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}},

(18)

where
\eta_{1i}
denotes the
i
th component of
\eta_{1}
.

Similarly, in the periodic or mixed setting, we have

|E(\tau,\xi,\eta)|\lesssim N_{\min}\sup_{|\xi_{1}|\sim N_{1},|\xi|\sim N}|E_{1}(\xi,\xi_{1})|,

where
E_{1}(\xi,\xi_{1})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}
is given as

E_{1}(\xi,\xi_{1})=I_{1}(\xi,\xi_{1})\cup I_{2}(\xi,\xi_{1}),

where

\begin{split}I_{i}(\xi,\xi_{1}):=\Big\{|\eta_{1}|\sim M_{1}:\big|\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial{\eta_{1i}}}\big|\gtrsim N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Big\}.\end{split}

Using Lemma
3.1
, we have the following bound

|E_{1}(\xi,\xi_{1})|\lesssim N_{\min}\Big(1+\frac{L_{\max}}{N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\Big)M_{\min},

where the summand
1
could be avoided in case
d_{2}=0
.
Substituting this in (
17
), we get the desired estimate.
∎

5
) and (

) of the function spaces, it suffices to prove that if

\lesssim 1

(a)

\bullet~L\geq N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}

-Strichartz estimates by Lemma

\begin{split}N\sum_{L\geq N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{1}_{D_{N,M,L}}(f^{(1)}\ast f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}&\lesssim_{\lambda}N^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}N_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(f^{(1)})\|_{L^{4}_{x,y,t}}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(f^{(2)})\|_{L^{4}_{x,y,t}}\\

This is acceptable for
N_{1}\leq M_{\min}
. If
N_{1}\geq M_{\min}
, then an application of Lemma
2.7

\begin{split}N\sum_{L\geq N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{1}_{D_{N,M,L}}(f^{(1)}\ast f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}&\lesssim N_{1}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}M_{\min}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}}M_{\min}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim M_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

\bullet~N_{1}^{2-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq L\leq N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}
: We can use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality through Lemma
2.7

\begin{split}N\sum_{N_{1}^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\leq L\leq N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{1}_{D_{N,M,L}}(f^{(1)}*f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}&\lesssim NN_{1}^{-1}N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}M_{\min}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}}M_{\min}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

This is acceptable for
M_{\min}\leq N_{1}
. For
M_{\min}\geq N_{1}
, we can in the same range of
L

L^{4}_{x,y,t}
-Strichartz estimates by Lemma
2.6
after duality and take out the function with highest modulation in
L_{\xi,\eta,\tau}^{2}

N_{2}
, i.e.,
L_{\max}=L_{2}
. In this case, we find

\begin{split}N\sum_{N_{1}^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\leq L\leq N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{1}_{D_{N,M,L}}(f^{(1)}*f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}&\lesssim_{\lambda}N_{1}\log(N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2})(N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{\min}^{0+}(N_{1}N_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim_{\lambda}N_{1}^{\big(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\big)}M_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}+}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(b)

N_{2}\leq N_{1}^{-\alpha}
. which yields

\begin{split}N\sum_{L\geq N_{1}^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{1}_{D_{N,M,L}}(f^{(1)}\ast f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}&\lesssim_{\lambda}N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{4}}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{4}}M_{\min}^{0+}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim_{\lambda}M_{\min}^{0+}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(25)

This is acceptable for
N_{1}\leq M_{\min}^{2}

N\sum_{L\geq N_{1}^{2-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{1}_{D_{N,M,L}}(f^{(1)}\ast f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}\lesssim N_{1}^{-(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}N_{1}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{\min}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}}.

N_{2}\leq N_{1}^{-\alpha}

(c)

N_{1}^{-\alpha}\leq N_{2}\leq 1

25

\bullet~L\geq N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}

\begin{split}N\sum_{L\geq N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{1}_{D_{N,M,L}}(f^{(1)}*f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}&\lesssim N^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}N_{1}^{-1+\frac{\alpha}{4}}M_{\min}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}\\

\bullet~N_{1}^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\leq L\leq N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}

\begin{split}N\sum_{L\geq N_{1}^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{1}_{D_{N,M,L}}(f^{(1)}*f^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta,\tau}}&\lesssim N\log(N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2})M_{\min}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{2-\alpha/2}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}}\\

for

Let

The estimates (

) follow by summation: Suppose

. Summation
over

and
M_{2}
and using

the claim follows from square summation.

Suppose

. In this case summation over

gives

\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{s}\|P_{N,M}\partial_{x}(u_{N_{1}}v_{N_{2},M_{2}})\|_{\mathcal{N}_{N}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{s}\|u_{N_{1}}\|_{F_{N_{1}}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{2}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+}\|v_{N_{2},M_{2}}\|_{F_{N_{2}}}.

for High x High
\to

\to

For High x High

5.
Energy estimates

The purpose of this section is to propagate the energy norms in terms of short-time norms. We shall do this for solutions in
F^{s}
and for differences of solutions in
F^{0}
. The key ingredient
is the dyadic estimate in Proposition
5.2
, to which the estimates are reduced after suitable integration by parts and substitutions.

5.1.
Energy estimates for the solution

We begin with energy estimates for solutions.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\alpha\in[2,4)
. For all
T\in(0,1]
and solutions
u\in C([-T,T];L^{2})\cap L_{T}^{\infty}E^{s}
to the following

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}D_{x}^{\alpha}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\Delta_{y}u&=\partial_{x}(u^{2}),\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{D}_{\lambda},~~t\in[-T,T],\\
u(0)&=\phi,\end{array}\right.

(41)

we have

\|u\|_{B^{s}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{E^{s}}^{2}+\lambda^{0+}\|u\|^{2}_{F^{s}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{r}(T)}

(42)

provided that
s\geq r>3-\frac{\alpha}{2}
.

5.2.
Reductions

We consider the equation (
41
) for the Littlewood-Paley pieces
P_{N}u
. Multiplying this equation by
P_{N}u
and integrating, we get

\sup_{|t_{N}|\leqslant T}\|P_{N}u(t_{N})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leqslant\|P_{N}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sup_{|t_{N}|\leqslant T}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\times[0,t_{N}]}P_{N}u\cdot P_{N}\partial_{x}(u^{2})dxdydt\Big|.

(43)

We write the integrand as

\begin{split}P_{N}uP_{N}(\partial_{x}u^{2})=2P_{N}uP_{N}(u\partial_{x}u)=2P_{N}uP_{N}(P_{\gtrsim N}u\cdot\partial_{x}u)+2P_{N}uP_{N}(P_{\ll N}u\cdot\partial_{x}u):=2(I+II).\end{split}

We can further decompose
I
as

\begin{split}I=P_{N}uP_{N}(P_{\gg N}u\cdot P_{\gg N}\partial_{x}u)+P_{N}uP_{N}(P_{\gtrsim N}u\cdot P_{\lesssim N}\partial_{x}u)=:a+b,\end{split}

while
II
can be written as

II=P_{N}uP_{\ll N}uP_{N}\partial_{x}u+P_{N}u[P_{N}(P_{\ll N}u\partial_{x}u)-P_{\ll N}uP_{N}\partial_{x}u]:=c+d.

We have

\begin{split}2a+2c&=P_{N}u\cdot\partial_{x}(P_{\gg N}u)^{2}+\partial_{x}(P_{N}u)^{2}\cdot P_{\ll N}u.\end{split}

For
b
, we observe that the derivative already hits the low frequency, while for
a+c
, using an integration by parts, we have

\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\times[0,T]}2(a+c)~dxdydt=\sum_{N\ll N_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\times[0,T]}\partial_{x}P_{N}u\cdot P_{N_{1}}u\cdot\tilde{P}_{N_{1}}u~dxdydt+\sum_{N_{1}\ll N}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\times[0,T]}\partial_{x}P_{N_{1}}u\cdot P_{N}u\cdot P_{N}u~dxdydt,

where
\tilde{P}_{N_{1}}=\sum_{N^{\prime}\sim N_{1}}P_{N_{1}}
(and the multiplier is denoted by
\tilde{\phi}_{N_{1}}
).

Next, we treat
d
by the same argument as in
[
10
, eq. 6.10]
to transfer the derivative to the low frequency factor. We have

d=P_{N}u\sum_{N_{1}\ll N}[P_{N}(P_{N_{1}}u\cdot\partial_{x}u)-P_{N_{1}}u\cdot P_{N}\partial_{x}u].

We fix an extension of
u
which we still denote by
u
. We have

\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}[P_{N}(P_{N_{1}}u\partial_{x}u)-P_{N_{1}}uP_{N}\partial_{x}u](\xi,\eta,\tau)\\
=&\int\frac{(\xi-\xi_{1})}{\xi_{1}}(\tilde{\phi}_{N}(\xi)-\tilde{\phi}_{N}(\xi-\xi_{1}))\widehat{P_{N}u}(\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1},\tau-\tau_{1})\widehat{P_{N_{1}}\partial_{x}u}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\tau_{1})d\xi_{1}d\eta_{1}d\tau_{1}=:M(P_{N}u,P_{N_{1}}\partial_{x}u),\end{split}

where the (bilinear) multiplier is

m(\xi,\xi_{1})=\frac{(\xi-\xi_{1})}{\xi_{1}}(\tilde{\phi}_{N}(\xi)-\tilde{\phi}_{N}(\xi-\xi_{1}))\tilde{\phi}_{N_{1}}(\xi_{1}).

Using the mean value theorem,

(\tilde{\phi}_{N}(\xi)-\tilde{\phi}_{N}(\xi-\xi_{1}))=-\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{\phi}_{N}^{{}^{\prime}}(\xi-h\xi_{1})\xi_{1}~dh,

we obtain the uniform boundedness of the multiplier

|m(\xi,\xi_{1})|\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\frac{\xi-\xi_{1}}{N}\tilde{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{\xi-h\xi_{1}}{N}\Big)\Big|~dh\lesssim 1.

To conclude, we have shown that by taking the advantage of the form of the nonlinearity, we can transfer the derivative to the low frequency in all the cases. More precisely, we can assume that our integrand is of the form

P_{N}u\cdot P_{N_{2}}u\cdot(P_{N_{1}}\partial_{x}u)\text{ or }P_{N}u\cdot M(P_{N}u,P_{N_{1}}\partial_{x}u)\quad\text{ with }N_{1}\lesssim N\sim N_{2}

(44)

with a bilinear Fourier-multiplier
M
with bounded symbol
m
.

Considering an integrand of the form
P_{N}u\cdot P_{N_{2}}u\cdot(P_{N_{1}}\partial_{x}u)
, we divide the time interval into sub-intervals
I
of size
N^{-(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}
to estimate the functions in short-time norms. Let
\gamma\in C^{\infty}_{c}([-1,1];\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\gamma^{3}(t-n)\equiv 1.

We write

\begin{split}&\Big|\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\times[0,T]}P_{N}u\cdot P_{N_{2}}u\cdot(P_{N_{1}}\partial_{x}u)~dxdydt\Big|\\
&\quad\lesssim\sum_{n}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\times[0,T]}(\gamma(N^{-(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}t-n)P_{N}u)\cdot(\gamma(N^{-(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}t-n)P_{N_{2}}u)\cdot(\gamma(N^{-(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}t-n)P_{N_{1}}\partial_{x}u)~dxdydt\Big|.\end{split}

(45)

We consider the sets

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\text{supp}(\gamma(N^{-(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\cdot-n))\subseteq(0,T)\},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:0\in\text{supp}(\gamma(N^{-(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\cdot-n))\vee T\in\text{supp}(\gamma(N^{-(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\cdot-n))\}.

Note that
|A|\sim TN^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}
and
|B|\leqslant 4
.
First, we consider the bulk of the cases given by the set

.
We change for
n\in A
in (
45
) to Fourier space after an additional dyadic decomposition for the
y
-frequencies: Let

\begin{split}f^{(1)}_{N_{1},M_{1}}&=\mathcal{F}_{x,y,t}[\gamma(N^{-(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}t-n)P_{N_{1},M_{1}}u],\quad f^{(2)}_{N_{2},M_{2}}=\mathcal{F}_{x,y,t}[\gamma(N^{-(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}t-n)P_{N_{2},M_{2}}u],\\

We make an additional dyadic decomposition in modulation for

according to the time localization:

f^{(i)}_{N_{i},M_{i}}=\sum_{L_{i}\geq N_{3}^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}}f^{(i)}_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}},\qquad f^{(i)}_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}}=\mathbf{1}_{D_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}}}f^{(i)}_{N_{i},M_{i}}.

The same can be imitated for an integrand of the type

because we require a bound in terms of the

norm on the right hand side. Taking into account the additional derivatives and time localization, the proof of Proposition
5.1
reduces to the following estimate:

\sum_{L_{i}\geq N_{3}^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}}\big|\int f^{(1)}_{N_{1},M_{1},L_{1}}\cdot(f^{(2)}_{N_{2},M_{2},L_{2}}\ast f^{(3)}_{N_{3},M_{3},L_{3}})\big|\lesssim_{\lambda}N_{1}^{(0+,\frac{1}{2}+)}N_{3}^{-1}M_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{L_{i}\geq N_{3}^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}

(46)

for
N_{1}\leq N_{2}\sim N_{3}
,
N_{1}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
N_{2},N_{3}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Like in Section

, we suppose that

if

. We always suppose that

, which is the lowest scale for

-frequencies.

Once the above display is proved, we can conclude the proof of (
42
) by (

):
Recall that

p_{\lambda}(\xi,\eta)=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{|\eta|}{|\xi|}.

The constant term is then estimated by the above argument, and we trade
\eta
-factors into the weight and powers of

|\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\begin{cases}(1+|\xi|)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad&|\eta|\lesssim|\xi|,\\
\big(1+\frac{|\eta|}{|\xi|}\big)|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad&|\eta|\gtrsim|\xi|.\end{cases}

This way the factor
M_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is traded to
N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Together with the factor
N_{3}^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}
from the time localization, we see how (

42
) by (
5
). To deal with the second term from the weight, we consider the equation for the Littlewood-Paley piece

:

\|P_{N}\partial_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{y}u(t_{N})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\|P_{N}\partial_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{y}u(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big|\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\times[0,t_{N}]}P_{N}\partial_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{y}uP_{N}(\nabla_{y}u^{2})dxdydt\big|.

and for

(48)

F^{s}
without the weight

. The estimates (

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
N_{1}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
N_{2},N_{3}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
N_{1}\leq N_{2}\sim N_{3}
,
L_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
M_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}},

f^{(i)}_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}_{+})

\text{supp}(f^{(k)})\subseteq D_{N_{k},M_{k},L_{k}}
for

. If
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{T}_{\nu}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{d_{2}}

. Then the following estimate holds:

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{L_{i}\geq N_{3}^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}}f^{(1)}_{N_{1},M_{1},L_{1}}\cdot(f_{N_{2},M_{2},L_{2}}^{(2)}\ast f_{N_{3},M_{3},L_{3}}^{(3)})d\xi d\eta d\tau\Big|\\
&\lesssim_{\lambda}N_{1}^{(0+,\frac{1}{2}+)}N_{3}^{-1}M_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{L_{i}\geq N_{3}^{(2-\frac{\alpha}{2})}}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Proof.

We consider the following cases depending on the size of
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
:

(i)

N_{1}\leq N_{2}^{-\alpha}
: In this case, we do not distinguish resonant and non-resonant interactions. Using Lemma

, we have

\begin{split}\big|\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}}f^{(1)}_{N_{1},M_{1},L_{1}}\cdot(f_{N_{2},M_{2},L_{2}}^{(2)}\ast f_{N_{3},M_{3},L_{3}}^{(3)})d\xi d\eta d\tau\big|&\lesssim_{\lambda}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{\min}^{0+}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{4}}N_{3}^{\frac{\big(\frac{\alpha}{2}-2\big)}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim_{\lambda}N_{1}^{0+}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{4}}N_{3}^{-1-\frac{\alpha}{4}+}M_{\min}^{0+}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

which is sufficient if
N_{3}\leq M_{\min}^{2}
. If
M_{\min}^{2}\leq N_{3}
, we use Lemma
2.7
to obtain

\begin{split}\big|\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}}f^{(1)}_{N_{1},M_{1},L_{1}}\cdot(f_{N_{2},M_{2},L_{2}}^{(2)}\ast f_{N_{3},M_{3},L_{3}}^{(3)})d\xi d\eta d\tau\big|&\lesssim M_{\min}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{\big(\frac{\alpha}{2}-2\big)}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim M_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{0+}N_{2}^{-2+\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{(i)}_{N_{i},M_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(ii)

N_{2}^{-\alpha}\leq N_{1}\leq 1
: We consider the resonant and non-resonant cases as follows:

\bullet~L_{\max}\leq N_{1}N_{2}^{\alpha}
: From the bilinear Strichartz estimate (
16
) applied to
f^{(1)}_{N_{1},M_{1},L_{1}}\ast f^{(2)}_{N_{2},M_{2},L_{2}}
(or
f^{(1)}_{N_{1},M_{1},L_{1}}\ast f^{(3)}_{N_{3},M_{3},L_{3}}

that
u_{n}\to u
in
C_{T}L^{2}
. Moreover, uniform boundedness in
C([0,T],E^{s})
, which was proved in the previous paragraph, implies convergence in
C_{T}E^{\sigma}
for every
0\leq\sigma<s
.
For
\sigma=s
, we shall again use frequency envelopes. To carry out the argument, we consider the approximate solutions
u_{n}^{M}
and
u^{M}
. We use that the initial data converge in all
E^{\sigma}
-norms:

u_{0n}^{M}\to u_{0}^{M}\text{ in }E^{\sigma}\text{ for }\sigma\geq 0.

By the above, we have convergence of the regular solutions in all
E^{\sigma}
-norms:

u_{n}^{M}\to u^{M}\text{ in }C_{T}E^{\sigma},\quad\sigma\geq 0.

We use the triangle inequality to compare
u
and
u_{n}
:

\|u_{n}-u\|_{C_{T}E^{s}}\lesssim\|u_{n}^{M}-u^{M}\|_{C_{T}E^{s}}+\|u^{M}-u\|_{C_{T}E^{s}}+\|u_{n}^{M}-u_{n}\|_{C_{T}E^{s}}.

The first term goes to zero as
n\to\infty
for fixed
M
; the second term goes to zero as
M\to\infty
. It remains to obtain uniform smallness of the third term for large
n
.
For this purpose we consider frequency envelopes
c_{J}^{(n)}
for the initial data
u_{n0}
. By construction, we can argue that

\big(c_{J}^{(n)}\big)_{J\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\to(c_{J})_{J\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\text{ in }\ell^{2}

with
(c_{J})
denoting a sharp frequency envelope for
u_{0}
. Hence, in terms of frequency envelopes, we find

\|u_{n}-u\|_{C_{T}E^{s}}\lesssim\|u_{n}^{M}-u^{M}\|_{C_{T}E^{s}}+\big(\sum_{L\geq M}c_{L}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\big(\sum_{L\geq M}(c^{(n)}_{L})^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

implying

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{C_{T}E^{s}}\lesssim\big(\sum_{L\geq M}c_{L}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0\quad(M\to\infty)

and the proof of continuous dependence is complete.

\hfill\Box

Local well-posedness for fifth order KP-I equation

In the following we prove semilinear local well-posedness

\left.\begin{split}\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}^{5}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\Delta_{y}u&=\partial_{x}(u^{2}),\quad((x,y),t)\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R},\\

(76)

for

,
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R};\mathbb{T}\}
in anisotropic Sobolev spaces

H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{D})=\{\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{D}):\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{s_{1},s_{2}}}=\int|\hat{\phi}(\xi,\eta)|^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s_{1}}\langle\eta\rangle^{2s_{2}}d\xi d\eta<\infty\}

with
s_{1},s_{2}>\frac{1}{2}
as stated in Theorem
1.2
. We show local well-posedness via the contraction mapping principle in adapted
U^{p}
-/
V^{p}
-function spaces. Let
u\in X^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{T}
(to be defined below) be a solution to

\left.\begin{split}\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}^{5}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\Delta_{y}u&=f,\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{D}).\end{split}\right\}

We show the estimates

\left.\begin{split}&\|u\|_{X_{T}^{s_{1},s_{2}}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s_{1},s_{2}}}+\|1_{[0,T]}(t)\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)(\partial_{x}^{5}+\partial_{x}^{-1}\Delta_{y})}f(s)ds\|_{X_{T}^{s_{1},s_{2}}},\\

Appendix A
Anisotropic Leibniz rule on mixed domains

This section is devoted to the proof of Proposition
6.4
. We use the following fractional Leibniz rule, which is based on the well-known Kato-Ponce estimate (cf.
[
4
,
5
]
) for
\alpha\geq 0
,
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
for
1<p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
:

\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}(fg)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}g\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(81)

We also need the above on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}}
. It turns out we can transfer the above estimate for
1<p_{1},q_{2}<\infty
in a straightforward manner by an extension operator. Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
denote a radially decreasing function with
\varphi(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 10
and
\varphi(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 15
. We denote the extension
\tilde{f}=f\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. We have for
k\in\mathbb{N}
clearly

\|\langle\partial\rangle^{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim\|\langle\partial\rangle^{k}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|\langle\partial\rangle^{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

By interpolation, the equivalence also holds for
\langle\partial\rangle^{s}
, and by obvious modification of the extension operator also holds on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}}
. So, let
f,g:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
, and let
\tilde{f}
,
\tilde{g}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
denote their extensions. Then we obtain

\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}(fg)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}(\tilde{f}\tilde{g})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}\tilde{g}\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Clearly,

\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},\quad\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

To argue that

\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

(82)

for
1<p<\infty
, we again use interpolation such that it suffices to show (
82
) for
\alpha=k\in\mathbb{N}
. We use the characterization of
L^{p}
-based Sobolev spaces

\|\langle\partial\rangle^{k}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\simeq\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

from which (
82
) is immediate by the product rule. We have proved the following:

Proposition A.1

(Fractional Leibniz rule on cylinders)
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
d_{1}+d_{2}\geq 1
,
\alpha\geq 0
,
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
for
1<p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
and
1<p_{1},q_{2}<\infty
:

\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}(fg)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}})}\lesssim\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}})}\|g\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}})}+\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}})}\|\langle\partial\rangle^{\alpha}g\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}})}.

(83)

From the above we derive the following anisotropic version. Let
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{K}_{1}\times\mathbb{K}_{2}\times\mathbb{K}_{3}
and write
(x,y)\in\mathbb{K}_{1}\times(\mathbb{K}_{2}\times\mathbb{K}_{3})=\mathbb{D}
. Dual variables will be denoted by
\xi
and
\eta
. We define Fourier multipliers
\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha}
and
\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}
on
\mathbb{D}
by

(\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha}f)\widehat{(}\xi,\eta)=\langle\xi\rangle^{\alpha}\hat{f}(\xi),\quad(\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}f)\widehat{(}\xi,\eta)=\langle\eta\rangle^{\beta}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta).

We are ready for the proof of Proposition
A.2
, whose statement is repeated for convenience:

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{K}_{1}\times\mathbb{K}_{2}\times\mathbb{K}_{3}
with
\mathbb{K}_{i}\in\{\mathbb{R};\mathbb{T}\}
,
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
,
\delta>0
, and
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. Then the following estimate holds:

\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha}\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}(u^{2})\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha}\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}u\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathbb{D})}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}(\mathbb{D})}+\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha+\delta}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}L_{y}^{p}(\mathbb{D})}\|\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L_{y}^{q}(\mathbb{D})}.

(84)

Proof.

We use a paraproduct decomposition for the
x
frequencies. Let
P_{N}
denote dyadic Littlewood-Paley projections in the
x
-frequencies. First note that by the usual fractional Leibniz rule (
81
) we find for the low frequencies:

\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha}\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}P_{\lesssim 1}(u^{2})\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\lesssim\|\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}(u^{2})\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\lesssim\|\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}.

By Littlewood-Paley theory, we write

\|P_{\gtrsim 1}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{xy}}=\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}^{2}.

For
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
we estimate the High-Low interaction as follows:

\begin{split}\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha}\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}P_{N}(P_{N}uP_{\ll N}u)\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}&\sim N^{\alpha}\big\|\|\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}(P_{\sim N}uP_{\ll N}u)\|_{L^{2}_{y}}\big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim N^{\alpha}\big\|\|\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}P_{\sim N}u\|_{L^{2}_{y}}\|P_{\ll N}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{y}}+\|P_{\sim N}u\|_{L_{y}^{p}}\|\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}P_{\ll N}u\|_{L_{y}^{q}}\big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha}\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}P_{N}u\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\|P_{\ll N}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\\
&\quad+N^{-\delta}\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha+\delta}P_{N}u\|_{L_{x}^{2}L^{p}_{y}}\|P_{\ll N}\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{\beta}u\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{y}^{q}}.\end{split}

The claim follows from square summation. The High-High-Low interaction can be estimated likewise.
∎

Theorem B.1
.

Let
\alpha<\frac{15}{7}

is
C^{2}
-differentiable at zero from
H^{\bar{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to
C([-T,T],H^{\bar{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

Proof.

We consider the following Cauchy problem for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}u-D_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\Delta_{y}u&=\partial_{x}(u^{2}),\quad(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R},\\

(85)

Suppose that

85
). Fix

such that the map
\Gamma

C^{2}
and let

u(\gamma,x,y,t)=\gamma S_{\alpha}(t)\phi(x,y)+\int_{0}^{t}S_{\alpha}(t-t^{\prime})u(\gamma,x,y,t^{\prime})u_{x}(\gamma,x,y,t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

(86)

Then

\begin{split}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\gamma}(0,x,y,t)&=S_{\alpha}(t)\phi(x,y)=:u_{1}(x,y,t)\\
\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial\gamma^{2}}(0,x,y,t)&=2\int_{0}^{t}S_{\alpha}(t-t^{\prime})u_{1}(x,y,t^{\prime})\partial_{x}u_{1}(x,y,t^{\prime})=:u_{2}(x,y,t).\end{split}

(87)

The

\|u_{2}(\cdot,\cdot,t)\|_{H^{\bar{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{H^{\bar{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

(88)

We show that there exists initial data
\phi

88

(89)

where

(90)

Here

(Size of the resonance function)

Let

Proof.

\Omega_{\alpha}^{3d}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\mu_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{2},\mu_{2})=\Omega_{\alpha}^{2d}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{2})-\frac{(\xi_{1}\mu_{2}-\xi_{2}\mu_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})},

(93)

|\Omega^{2d}_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{2})|\lesssim N^{\alpha-1}\beta^{2}.

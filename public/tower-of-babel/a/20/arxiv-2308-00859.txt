Homotopy similarity of maps
S. S. Podkorytov

Abstract
Given based cellular spaces X X and Y Y , X X compact, we define a sequence of increasingly fine equivalences on the based-homotopy set [ X , Y ] [X,Y] .



§ 1. Introduction

Let
X
and
Y
be based cellular spaces
( = CW-complexes),

X
compact.
Let

Y^{X}
be the set of based continuous maps

X\to Y

and

\langle Y^{X}\rangle
be the free abelian group associated with
Y^{X}
.
An element
A\in\langle Y^{X}\rangle
, an
ensemble
, has the form

A=\sum_{i}u_{i}\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle<$}a_{i}\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle>$},

(1)

where

u_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and

a_{i}\in Y^{X}
.
Let
{\mathscr{F}}_{r}(X)
be the set of
subspaces

( = subsets containing the basepoint)

T\subseteq X
containing at most
r
points
distinct from the basepoint.
Introduce the subgroup

\langle Y^{X}\rangle^{(r+1)}=\{\,A:\text{$A|_{T}=0$ in $\langle Y^{T}\rangle$ for all $T\in{\mathscr{F}}_{r}(X)$}\,\}\subseteq\langle Y^{X}\rangle.

We have

\langle Y^{X}\rangle=\langle Y^{X}\rangle^{(0)}\supseteq\langle Y^{X}\rangle^{(1)}\supseteq\dotso.

For ensembles
A,B\in\langle Y^{X}\rangle
,
let

A\overset{r}{=}B

mean that

B-A\in\langle Y^{X}\rangle^{(r+1)}
.

For maps
a,b\in Y^{X}
,
we say that

a
is
r
-similar
to
b
,

a\overset{r}{\sim}b,

when there exists an ensemble
A\in\langle Y^{X}\rangle

given by (
1
)
with all
a_{i}\sim a

(
\sim
denotes based homotopy)
such that
A\overset{r}{=}\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle<$}b\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle>$}
.
A simple example is given in Section 3.

Our main results state that
the relation
\overset{r}{\sim}
is an equivalence
(Theorem 8.1)
and
respects homotopy
(Theorem 5.2).
It follows that
we get a sequence of
increasingly fine equivalences on
the based-homotopy set
[X,Y]
.

We conjecture that,
for
0
-connected
Y
,
a map is
r
-similar to the constant map
if and only if
it lifts to the classifying space of
the
(r+1)
th term of the lower central series of
the loop group of
Y
.

A related notion is that of
a homotopy invariant of finite order

[
4
,
5
]
.
A function
f:[X,Y]\to L
,
where
L
is an abelian group,
is called an invariant of
order
at most
r

when
for any ensemble
A\in\langle Y^{X}\rangle

given by (
1
)
the congruence
A\overset{r}{=}0
implies

\sum_{i}u_{i}f([a_{i}])=0.

It is clear that

f([a])=f([b])

if

a\overset{r}{\sim}b

and

f
has order at most
r
.
In § 11,
we give an example of two maps
that
are not
2
-similar
but cannot be distinguished by invariants of order at most
2
.
In the stable dimension range,
invariants of order at most
r

were characterized
in a way similar to
our conjecture about
r
-similarity

[
4
]
.

The relation between

r
-similarity and
finite-order homotopy invariants
is similar to that between

n
-equivalence and
finite-degree invariants in knot theory

[
1
,
2
]
.
The example of § 11
is similar to
that of
[
2
, Remark 10.8]
.

§ 4. Equipment of a cellular space

Let
Y
be a compact
unbased
cellular space.
In this section,
we turn off our convention that

maps
and
homotopies
preserve basepoints.

There exist homotopies

q_{t}:Y^{2}\to Y\quad\text{and}\quad p_{t}:Y^{2}\to[0,1],\qquad t\in[0,1],

such that

q_{0}(z,y)=y,\qquad q_{t}(z,z)=z,\qquad p_{0}(z,y)=0,\qquad p_{t}(z,z)=t,

and,

,

p_{t}(z,y)=0\quad\text{or}\quad q_{t}(z,y)=z.

(3)

Roughly speaking,
the inclusions

,

, form a parametric

By
[
3
, Corollary A.10]
,

Y
is an ENR.
Embed it to

and choose
its neighbourhood
U\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

and
a retraction

.
Choose
\epsilon>0
such that

U
includes all closed balls of radius
\epsilon
with
centres in
Y
.
Consider the homotopy

,

\displaystyle l_{t}(z,y)

\displaystyle=y+\min(\epsilon t/|z-y|,1)(z-y),\qquad z\neq y,

\displaystyle l_{t}(z,z)

\displaystyle=z.

Put

q_{t}(z,y)=r(l_{t}(z,y))\quad\text{and}\quad p_{t}(z,y)=\max(t-|z-y|/\epsilon,0).\displaymath@qed

One can continuously associate

, such that

and

E_{t}(v)(v(0))=v(t)
.

Using Lemma 4.1,
put

E_{t}(v)(y)=\begin{cases}q_{t}(v(0),y)&\text{if $p_{t}(v(0),y)=0$},\\
v(p_{t}(v(0),y))&\text{if $q_{t}(v(0),y)=v(0)$}.\end{cases}

\displaymath@qed

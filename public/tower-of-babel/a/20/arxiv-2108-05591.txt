Top degree Ext-groups and relatively supercuspidal spectra
Li Cai, Yangyu Fan

Abstract
. Relatively supercuspidal representations are analogue of supercuspidal representations in the relative Langlands program. This work studies relatively supercuspidal representations using top degree Ext-groups via the Schneider-Stuhler duality. As examples, the relatively supercuspidal spectra for the Flicker-Rallis case and the diagonal case are determined.



1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field. Let
G
be a reductive group over
F
and
{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))
be the category of complex smooth
G(F)
-representations.
It is an important theorem of
Berstein-Zelevinsky
[
6
]
that for any irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))
, there exists a parabolic subgroup
P=MN\subset G
with Levi factor
M
and
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(M(F))
supercuspidal such that
\pi\hookrightarrow I_{P(F)}^{G(F)}\sigma
(normalized induction). Moreover, the pair
(M,\sigma)
is unique up to
G(F)
-conjugacy, called
the
cuspidal support
of
\pi
.

It is natural to ask how to classify irreducible smooth representations in terms of cuspidal supports.
At least for discrete series representations, the complete theory is available for both

{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
(see
[
33
]
) and classical groups (see
[
20
]
and
[
32
]
).

In the
relative Langlands program
, one considers irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))
distinguished
by a subgroup
H\subset G
, i.e.,
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})\not=0
. The objects analogous to supercuspidal representations are the

relatively supercuspidal
(RSC) representations, i.e., distinguished irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))

such that for any
\ell\neq 0\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})
, the relative matrix
coefficients
\ell(\pi(\cdot)v)
,
v\in\pi
are compactly supported modulo
Z_{G}H(F)
, where
Z_{G}
is the
center of
G
. Consider the case
(G,H)
is a symmetric pair, say
H
is the fixed point of
an involution
\theta
on
G
. Kato-Takano
[
15
, Theorem 7.1]

proves an analogue of the above theorem of
Bernstein-Zelevinsky: for any distinguished
G(F)
-representation
\pi
on
G(F)
, there exist a parabolic subgroup
P
of
G

with
M=P\cap\theta(P)
a Levi subgroup of
P
and a
H\cap M
-RSC
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(M(F))
,
such that
\pi\hookrightarrow I_{P(F)}^{G(F)}\sigma
.
This result gives a way to reduce the local harmonic analysis on

p
-adic symmetric spaces to RSC representations (See, e.g.
[
23
]
).

It is a fundamental problem to determine RSC representations.

For symmetric pairs, Kato-Takano
[
15
, Theorem 6.9]
gives a characterization of relative supercuspidality in terms of Jacquet modules. In
[
17
,
28
,
16
]
, many RSC representions are constructed by considering parabolic inductions of specified supercuspidal representations. One key tool in all these works is computing the Jacquet modules.

In this paper, we will detect RSC representations via top-degree Ext-groups instead of Jacquet modules. It works even for the pairs
(G,H)
which
are not symmetric (See the diagonal case below).

Our approach is motivated by the study of Prasad
[
26
]
on Ext-analogues of
branching law and relates directly to the problem to determine irreducible (non-supercuspidal)

H(F)
-subrepresentations of irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))
(See
[
22
, Proposition 9.1]
for
an inspiring example and also the work of Speh-Venkataraman
[
29
,
30
]
when
F
is Archimedean).

There are two natural questions for RSC representations.

Q1:
Classify RSC representations in terms of cuspidal supports.

The second question is to understand RSC representations in the general framework of Sakellaridis-Venkatesh
on the Plancherel formula for spherical varieties
[
27
]
.
Assume the geometric quotient
X=H\backslash G
is spherical with respect to the right
G
-multiplication.
Under certain conditions, there exists a reductive group
G_{X}
over
F
and a functorial morphism

\iota_{*}:{\widehat{G_{X}}}\longrightarrow{\widehat{G}}

Here,
{\widehat{G_{X}}}
and
{\widehat{G}}

are the unitary duals of
G_{X}
and
G
respectively.

Roughly speaking, it is conjectured that there is an isomorphism of unitary representations

L^{2}(X(F))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{{\widehat{G_{X}}}}\iota_{*}(\sigma)^{\oplus m(\sigma)}d\sigma

where
d\sigma
is the Plancherel measure of
G_{X}
and
m(\sigma)
is certain multiplicity.

Q2
: Classify RSC representations in term of
G_{X}(F)
-representations via
\iota_{*}
.

In fact, we will see from the cases below that

•

(Q1) implies (Q2) assuming
a good understanding of the morphism
\iota_{*}
.

•

a good understanding of the conjectured Plancherel decomposition (at least for the discrete
spectrum) is a key ingredient for (Q1).

The starting point of our method is the following key observation based on the
Schneider-Stuhler duality theorem given by Nori-Prasad (See

[
22
]
and also Theorem
3.5
below).

Lemma 1.1

(See Proposition
4.2
for the general case)
.

Assume
Z_{G}\cap H=Z_{H}
. For any irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{H}(F)\backslash G(F))
with
\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})<\infty
,

\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})\geq\dim{\mathrm{Ext}}_{Z_{H}(F)\backslash H(F)}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}(D(\pi)^{\vee},{\mathbb{C}}).

Moreover,
\pi
is RSC if and only if

0\neq\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=\dim{\mathrm{Ext}}_{Z_{H}(F)\backslash H(F)}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}(D(\pi)^{\vee},{\mathbb{C}}).

Here,

•

{\mathrm{Ext}}_{Z_{H}(F)\backslash H(F)}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}(D(\pi)^{\vee},{\mathbb{C}}):={\mathrm{Ext}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{H}(F)\backslash H(F))}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}(D(\pi)^{\vee},{\mathbb{C}})
is the degree
d^{\prime}(\pi)
Ext-group in
{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{H}(F)\backslash H(F))
.

•

If denoted by
\omega
the central character of
\pi
and
{\mathrm{Rep}}(G,\omega)
the category of
smooth
G(F)
-representations with central character
\omega
,
then
d^{\prime}(\pi)
is the top Ext-degree of
\pi
in
{\mathrm{Rep}}(G,\omega)
, i.e.
for any
\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
and any
i>d^{\prime}(\pi)
,

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})=0
. In fact,
d^{\prime}(\pi)
is the split rank of
Z_{M}\cap[G,G]
where

M
is the Levi subgroup carrying the cuspidal support of
\pi
.

•

D(\pi)
is the Aubert-Zelevinsky involution of
\pi
with

D(\pi)^{\vee}
its smooth dual.

Proof.

Extend
\omega
to
Z_{G}H(F)
by composing with the trivial character on
H(F)
. Denote by
I_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega

(resp.
i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega
) the space
of smooth functions
f:\ G(F)\to{\mathbb{C}}
(resp. with compact support modulo
ZH(F)
) such that

f(zg)=\omega(z)f(g)\quad\forall z\in Z_{G}H(F),\ g\in G(F)

on which
G(F)
acts by right translation. Then

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})={\mathrm{Hom}}_{Z_{G}H(F)}(\pi,\omega)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,I_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega\right)\supset{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega\right).

And
\pi
is
H
-RSC if and only if

0\neq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,I_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega\right)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega\right).

By the Schneider-Stuhler duality for
\pi
,

\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega\right)=\dim{\mathrm{Ext}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}\left(i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega,D(\pi)\right)

By duality and the Frobenius reciprocity law,

{\mathrm{Ext}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}\left(i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega,D(\pi)\right)={\mathrm{Ext}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega^{-1})}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}\left(D(\pi)^{\vee},I_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega^{-1}\right)={\mathrm{Ext}}_{Z_{H}(F)\backslash H(F)}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}\left(D(\pi)^{\vee},{\mathbb{C}}\right)

∎

The Flicker-Rallis case

Let

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

be an
irreducible representation. Then
\pi
is RSC if and only if

\pi=I_{P(F)}^{G(F)}\sigma

for some parabolic subgroup

and some

H\cap M
-distinguished regular supercuspidal
M(F)
-representation
\sigma

Here an irreducible representation

\sigma=\boxtimes_{i}\sigma_{i}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(M(F))

is called

if

are mutually distinct.

Immediate from Theorem
1.2

admitting the Steinberg representation
{\mathrm{St}}=D({\mathbb{C}})\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F))
as a subrepresentation. Let

Corollary 1.3
.

Denote by
{\mathrm{St}}
the Steinberg representation of

with the equality holds iff
\pi=I_{B(F)}^{G(F)}\chi
with
\chi

-distinguished.

Proof.

The top degree
d^{\prime}({\mathrm{St}})=d^{\prime}({\mathbb{C}})=n-1
. By the Schneider-Stuhler duality for

\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))

\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}\left({\mathrm{St}},\pi\right)=\dim{\mathrm{Ext}}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}^{n-1}\left(\pi,{\mathbb{C}}\right).

(Readers may compare with the use of Schneider-Stuhler duality in the proof of Lemma

Note that

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{n-1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})={\mathrm{Ext}}^{n-1}_{F^{\times}\backslash G(F)}(\pi,I_{H(F)}^{G(F)}{\mathbb{C}})={\mathrm{Ext}}^{n-1}_{F^{\times}\backslash G(F)}(i_{H(F)}^{G(F)}{\mathbb{C}},\pi^{\vee})

and the top degree

d^{\prime}(\pi)<n-1
,
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}\left({\mathrm{St}},\pi\right)
. When

\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}\left({\mathrm{St}},\pi\right)=\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}(D(\pi)^{\vee},i_{H(F)}^{G(F)}{\mathbb{C}})\leq 1

\pi=I_{B(F)}^{G(F)}\chi
with
\chi

-distinguished.
∎

Theorem 1.7

(See Theorem

)
.

Assume

is compact.
Then RSC representations are
supercuspidal. If moreover

•

•

Applying Lemma
1.1
, it is quite straightforward to show RSC representations are supercuspidal.
For simplicity,
assume
Z_{G}\cap H=Z_{H}
and
\pi=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))
is RSC with trivial central character.
Then

\displaystyle 0\not={\mathrm{Ext}}_{Z_{H}(F)\backslash H(F)}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}(D(\pi)^{\vee},{\mathbb{C}})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Ext}}_{Z_{H}(F)\backslash H(F)}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}(D(\pi_{1})^{\vee},D(\pi_{2}))

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Ext}}_{Z_{G_{1}}(F)\backslash G_{1}(F)}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}\left(D(\pi_{1})^{\vee},I_{Z_{G_{2}}(F)\backslash G_{2}(F)}^{Z_{G_{1}}(F)\backslash G_{1}(F)}D(\pi_{2})\right)

The nonvanishing implies
d^{\prime}(\pi)\leq d^{\prime}(\pi_{1})
and
d^{\prime}(\pi)\leq d^{\prime}(\pi_{2})
. As
d^{\prime}(\pi)=d^{\prime}(\pi_{1})+d^{\prime}(\pi_{2})
, we have

\pi
is supercuspidal.

The "moreover" part follows immediately from
[

The assumptions in Theorem

•

the group case:

•

When

is non-compact, one can only deduce

In the case

Theorem 1.8

Assume

2.
Preliminaries

Let
G
be a reductive group over
F
. In the following, we will record some general facts on Ext-groups for the categories
{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))
and
{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
with
\omega:\ Z_{G}(F)\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Take any projective object
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
. Then for any closed subgroup
H\subset G
,

(i)

\pi|_{H}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F),\omega|_{Z_{G}\cap H})
is projective and for any
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F),\omega|_{Z_{G}\cap H})
,

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)}(\pi,\mathrm{un}-I_{Z_{G}H}^{G}\sigma)\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F),\omega|_{Z_{G}\cap H})}(\pi|_{H},\sigma)

where
\mathrm{un}-I_{Z_{G}H}^{G}
is the unnormalized induction functor.

(ii)

\pi|_{H}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F))
is projective if
Z_{G}\cap H(F)
is compact.

Proof.

Note that restriction induces an equivalence of categories

{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F),\omega|_{Z_{G}\cap H})\cong{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{G}H(F),\omega).

Then by Frobenius reciprocity law, Item (i) follows from
[
26
, Proposition 2.2]
.

For Item
(ii)
, note that for any
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F))
,

\sigma_{\omega}=\{v\in\sigma\mid z\cdot v=\omega(z)v,\quad\forall\ z\in Z_{G}\cap H(F)\}\subset\sigma

is a direct summand and
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi|_{H},\sigma)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,\sigma_{\omega})
. Thus to show
\pi|_{H}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F))
is projective, it suffices to show that for any surjection

\sigma_{2}\twoheadrightarrow\sigma_{1}

in
{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F),\omega|_{Z_{G}\cap H})
, one has

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi|_{H},\sigma_{2})\twoheadrightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi|_{H},\sigma_{1}).

By the category equivalence

{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F),\omega|_{Z_{G}\cap H})\cong{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{G}H(F),\omega)

and the Frobenius reciprocity law, there is a canonical isomorphism

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F),\omega_{Z_{G}\cap H})}(\pi|_{H},\sigma_{i})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)}(\pi,\mathrm{un}-I_{Z_{G}H}^{G}\sigma_{i}).

As
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
is projective, we are done.
∎

The following lemma is well-known.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
,
G^{\prime}
be reductive groups over
F
. If both
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
and
\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G^{\prime}(F),\omega^{\prime})
are projective,
\pi\boxtimes\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G\times G^{\prime}(F),\omega\boxtimes\omega^{\prime})
is projective.

Proof.

For any
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G\times G^{\prime}(F))
, one has a canonical isomorphism

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G\times G^{\prime}(F)}(\pi\boxtimes\pi^{\prime},\sigma)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}(\pi,{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}(F)}(\pi^{\prime},\sigma)).

Then for any surjection

\sigma_{2}\twoheadrightarrow\sigma_{1}

in
{\mathrm{Rep}}(G\times G^{\prime}(F),\omega\boxtimes\omega^{\prime})
,

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G\times G^{\prime}(F),\omega\boxtimes\omega^{\prime})}(\pi\boxtimes\pi^{\prime},\sigma_{2})\twoheadrightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G\times G^{\prime}(F),\omega\boxtimes\omega^{\prime})}(\pi\boxtimes\pi^{\prime},\sigma_{1})

by the assumption on
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
. Consequently,
\pi\boxtimes\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G\times G^{\prime}(F),\omega\boxtimes\omega^{\prime})
is projective.
∎

Combining Lemma
2.1
and
2.2
, one has the following straightforward corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

If both
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
and
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega^{-1})
are projective, then
\pi\otimes\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{G}(F)\backslash G(F))
is projective.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
and
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega^{-1})
, there are canonical isomorphisms

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{G}(F)\backslash G(F))}(\pi\otimes\sigma,{\mathbb{C}})\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G,\omega)}(\pi,\sigma^{\vee})\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G,\omega^{-1})}(\sigma,\pi^{\vee}),\quad\forall\ i\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Proof.

Take any projective resolution
Q_{\bullet}
of
\sigma
in
{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega^{-1})
. Assume
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
is projective. Then
\pi\otimes Q_{\bullet}
is a projective resolution of
\pi\otimes\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{G}(F)\backslash G(F))
by Corollary
2.3
. Hence the complex
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{G}(F)\backslash G(F))}(\pi\otimes Q_{\bullet},{\mathbb{C}})

computes
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{G}(F)\backslash G(F))}(\pi\otimes\sigma,{\mathbb{C}})
.
Note that

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{G}(F)\backslash G(F))}(\pi\otimes Q_{\bullet},{\mathbb{C}})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G,\omega^{-1})}(Q_{\bullet},\pi^{\vee})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G,\omega)}(\pi,Q_{\bullet}^{\vee}),

so it also computes
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G,\omega^{-1})}(\sigma,\pi^{\vee})
and
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G,\omega)}(\pi,\sigma^{\vee})
.

Note that
\pi
is projective implies

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{G}(F)\backslash G(F))}(\pi\otimes\sigma,{\mathbb{C}})\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G,\omega)}(\pi,\sigma^{\vee})=0,\ \forall\ i\geq 1.

Consequently,
\pi\otimes\sigma
is an acyclic object for the functor
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{Z_{G}(F)\backslash G(F)}(-,{\mathbb{C}})
.

For arbitrary
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
, take any projective resolution
P_{\bullet}
. Then
P_{\bullet}\otimes\sigma
is an acyclic resolution of
\pi\otimes\sigma
with respect to
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{Z_{G}(F)\backslash G(F)}(-,{\mathbb{C}})
. Therefore

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{Z_{G}(F)\backslash G(F)}(P_{\bullet}\otimes\sigma,{\mathbb{C}})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G,\omega^{-1})}(\sigma,P_{\bullet}^{\vee})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G,\omega)}(P_{\bullet},\sigma^{\vee})

computes
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{G}(F)\backslash G(F))}(\pi\otimes\sigma,{\mathbb{C}})
as well as
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega^{-1})}(\sigma,\pi^{\vee})
and
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)}(\pi,\sigma^{\vee})
.
∎

The following Kunneth formula is quite useful.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
26
, Proposition 3.1]
Let
G
,
G^{\prime}
be reductive groups over

. Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))
and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G^{\prime}(F))
such that one of
\pi_{1}
and
\sigma_{1}
has finite length or both

When
\pi_{1}
or

3.
Aubert-Zelevinsky involution and Schneider-Stuhler duality

In this section, we introduce the Aubert-Zelevinsky involution and Schneider-Stuhler duality following
[
22
]
. Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
F
and fix a minimal parabolic subgroup
P_{\emptyset}\subset G
. Then for any finite length
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))
, the
Aubert-Zelevinsky involution
D(\pi)
is the virtual representation in the Grothendieck group

I_{P_{\emptyset}}^{G(F)}(R_{P_{\emptyset}}(\pi))-\sum_{P_{1}}I_{P_{1}}^{G(F)}(R_{P_{1}}(\pi))+\sum_{P_{2}}I_{P_{1}}^{G(F)}(R_{P_{2}}(\pi))\cdots

where
P_{1}
is the next larger parabolics in
G
containing
P_{\emptyset}
,
P_{2}
is next larger parabolics containing
P_{1}
etc and
R_{P_{*}}
is the normalized Jacquet functor with respect to
P_{*}
. For
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))
irreducible,
D(\pi)
is actually given by an honest irreducible
G(F)
-representation (up to sign), which one still denotes by
D(\pi)
, and it is known that
D(D(\pi))=\pi
and
D(\pi^{\vee})=D(\pi)^{\vee}
. Moreover, for any parabolic subgroup
P=MN\subset G
with opposite
P^{-}
and any finite length
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(M(F))
,
D(I_{P(F)}^{G(F)}\sigma)\cong I_{P^{-}(F)}^{G(F)}D(\sigma)
.

In many cases, one can describe the Aubert-Zelevinsky involution explicitly.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
P=MN\subset G
be any parabolic subgroup with Levi factor
M
. Let
W_{G}
(resp.
W_{M}
) be the Weyl group of
G
(resp.
M
) for a maximal split torus in
G
. An irreducible representation
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(M(F))
is called
regular
if
\sigma^{w}\ncong\sigma
for any nontrivial
w\in W_{M}\backslash W_{G}/W_{M}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
22
, Proposition 2.1]
For any regular supercuspidal irreducible
M(F)
-representation
\sigma
,
I_{P(F)}^{G(F)}\sigma
has a unique irreducible sub-representation
S(\sigma)
and a unique irreducible quotient representation
Q(\sigma)
. Moreover, one has
D(S(\sigma))=Q(\sigma)
and
D(Q(\sigma))=S(\sigma)
.

In the case
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
, a standard Levi group
M
has the form
\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{i}}
with
\sum_{i}n_{i}=n
. Moreover, an irreducible
M(F)
-representation
\sigma=\boxtimes\sigma_{i}
is regular if and only if the
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{i}}(F)
-representations
\sigma_{i}
are mutually distinct. When all
n_{i}
are equal and
\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{1}|\det|^{i-1}
,
\sigma
is called a
segment
for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
. Note that the Steinberg representation
{\mathrm{St}}
can be realized as the unique irreducible quotient of

I_{B(F)}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)}|\cdot|^{\frac{1-n}{2}}\boxtimes|\cdot|^{\frac{3-n}{2}}\cdots\boxtimes|\cdot|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
6
,
33
]

Let
P=MN\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
be any standard parabolic subgroup and
\sigma=\boxtimes_{i=1}^{j}\sigma_{i}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(M(F))
be any irreducible supercuspidal representation. Then

.

is a segment, then

-representations arise this way.

.

Let

.

[

4.
Relatively supercuspidal representation

Let
G
be a reductive group over
F
with center
Z_{G}
and
H\subset G
be a reductive

F
-subgroup. Take any smooth character
\omega:\ Z_{G}H(F)\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
. Let
\eta:=\omega|_{H(F)}
and denote
\omega|_{Z_{G}(F)}
again by
\omega
. An irreducible representation
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
is called

•

(
(H,\eta)
-)distinguished
if

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,\eta)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{Z_{G}H(F)}(\pi,\omega)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,I_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega\right)\not=0.

•

(
(H,\eta)
-)relatively discrete series
(when
\omega
is unitary) if
\pi
is
(H,\eta)
-distinguished and

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,L^{2}(Z_{G}H(F)\backslash G(F),\omega)\right)\neq 0,

where
L^{2}(Z_{G}H(F)\backslash G(F),\omega)
is the
L^{2}
-completion of
i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega
.

•

(
(H,\eta)
-)relatively supercuspidal
if
\pi
is distinguished and

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,I_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega\right)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega\right).

When
\eta
is trivial, we usually omit it (or sometimes
(H,\eta)
) in notations. Note that if
\omega
is unitary and
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
is RSC, then
\pi
is RDS.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
such that
\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})<\infty
, one has

\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}\left(\pi,\eta\right)\geq\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega\right)=\dim{\mathrm{Ext}}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F),\eta^{-1}|_{Z_{G}\cap H(F)})}(D(\pi)^{\vee},\eta^{-1}).

If
Z_{G}\cap H(F)\backslash Z_{G}(F)
is compact, one has

\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega\right)=\dim{\mathrm{Ext}}^{d(\pi)}_{H(F)}(D(\pi)^{\vee},\eta^{-1}).

Proof.

Note that

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,\eta)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{Z_{G}H(F)}(\pi,\omega)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}(\pi,I_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega)\supset{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}(\pi,i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega).

By the Schnieder-Stuhler duality in Theorem
3.5
,

\displaystyle\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}(\pi,i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega)

\displaystyle=\dim{\mathrm{Ext}}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)}(i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega,D(\pi))

\displaystyle=\dim{\mathrm{Ext}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega^{-1})}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}\left(D(\pi)^{\vee},I_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega^{-1}\right).

By Frobenius reciprocity and the category equivalence

{\mathrm{Rep}}(Z_{G}H(F),\omega|_{Z_{G}(F)}^{-1})\cong{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F),\omega^{-1}|_{Z_{G}\cap H(F)}),

one deduce

{\mathrm{Ext}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega^{-1})}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}\left(D(\pi)^{\vee},I_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega^{-1}\right)={\mathrm{Ext}}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(H(F),\eta^{-1}|_{Z_{G}\cap H(F)})}(D(\pi)^{\vee},\eta^{-1})

When
Z_{G}\cap H(F)\backslash Z_{G}(F)
is compact, one has

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,i_{Z_{G}H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega\right)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}\left(\pi,i_{H(F)}^{G(F)}\eta\right).

By the Schnieder-Stuhler duality in Theorem
3.5
and Frobenius reciprocity agian,

\displaystyle\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(F)}(\pi,i_{H(F)}^{G(F)}\omega)

\displaystyle=\dim{\mathrm{Ext}}^{d(\pi)}_{G(F)}(i_{H(F)}^{G(F)}\eta,D(\pi))

\displaystyle=\dim{\mathrm{Ext}}_{G(F)}^{d(\pi)}\left(D(\pi)^{\vee},I_{H(F)}^{G(F)}\eta^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim{\mathrm{Ext}}^{d(\pi)}_{H(F)}\left(D(\pi)^{\vee},\eta^{-1}\right).

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F),\omega)
be an irreducible representation with
\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})<\infty

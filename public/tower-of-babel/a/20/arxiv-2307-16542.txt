Abstract
We search for tri-nucleon decays of Ge 76 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge} in the dataset from the GERmanium Detector Array (GERDA) experiment. Decays that populate excited levels of the daughter nucleus above the threshold for particle emission lead to disintegration and are not considered. The ppp-, ppn-, and pnn-decays lead to Cu 73 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cu} , Zn 73 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Zn} , and Ga 73 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga} nuclei, respectively. These nuclei are unstable and eventually proceed by the beta decay of Ga 73 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga} to Ge 73 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge} (stable). We search for the Ga 73 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga} decay exploiting the fact that it dominantly populates the 66.7 keV Ga 73 ​ m {}^{\makebox[15.06837pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73m$}}_{\makebox[15.06837pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga} state with half-life of 0.5 s. The nnn-decays of Ge 76 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge} that proceed via Ge 73 ​ m {}^{\makebox[15.06837pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73m$}}_{\makebox[15.06837pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge} are also included in our analysis. We find no signal candidate and place a limit on the sum of the decay widths of the inclusive tri-nucleon decays that corresponds to a lower lifetime limit of 1.2 × \times 10 26 yr (90% credible interval). This result improves previous limits for tri-nucleon decays by one to three orders of magnitude.

Introduction

The Standard Model in its current form appears to conserve baryon number
B
in all particle interactions. This can be considered as an empirical accidental symmetry. Violation of this symmetry is one of the three Sakharov conditions
Sakharov:1967dj
necessary to explain the observed matter-antimatter asymmetry in the universe.

With a careful choice of the charge assignments for the Standard Model fermions and Higgs boson the Lagrangian is invariant under
Z_{6}
. Under the chosen charge assignment
Z_{6}
is a subgroup of the
U(1)_{2Y-B+3L}
gauge group and hence any processes must satisfy the condition
babu2003gauged
:

2\Delta Y-\Delta B+3\Delta L=0\pmod{6}

where
Y
denotes hypercharge and
\Delta Y
is 0.
L
denotes the lepton number. It is then apparent that the only valid solutions for this condition require
\Delta B
be a multiple of 3. As a result
\Delta B=1
and
\Delta B=2
processes are suppressed while
\Delta B=3
processes can occur via dimension 15 operators
babu2003gauged
. Current limits on the proton lifetime are in the order of 10
34
yr
Workman:2022ynf
which could be attributed to the aforementioned symmetry.

The disappearance of three nucleons from a
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
nucleus will spawn A = 73 daughter
nuclei unless additional nucleons or nuclear clusters are emitted by the daughters;
hence the total decay width
\Gamma_{3}^{tot}
is the sum of two partial decay widths

\Gamma^{c}_{3}
and
\Gamma^{b}_{3}
that quantify the population of the continuum and the bound state
region in the daughter nuclei by tri-nucleon decay.

{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
and for each daughter nucleus the neutron threshold
S_{n}
which is
for
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cu}
and
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Zn}
the lowest threshold for particle emission; with
qualification this holds also for
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
(
S_{p}=8.843
MeV,
S_{\alpha}=6.388
MeV)
and
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
(

MeV) taking the Coulomb barrier for protons
(
\approx
6.5 MeV) and
\alpha
particles (

11 MeV) into account.
In this study we are concerned with inclusive tri-nucleon decays which populate with partial
widths
\Gamma^{b}_{x}
just the bound states, i.e. the levels which are stable against particle
emission, in the daughters,
\Gamma_{3}^{b}=\sum_{x}{(\Gamma_{x}^{b})}
, (x = ppp, ppn, pnn, nnn).

The decays of the ground states of the three unstable daughters
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cu}
,

{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Zn}
, and
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
proceed all via beta decay:
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cu}
decays
to
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Zn}
with a half life of 4.2 s,
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Zn}
decays to
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}

{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
decays to
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
with a half life
of 4.86 h.
Hence we perform an inclusive search since no assumption is made on the specific type of particles that are produced in the tri-nucleon decay. The only assumption is that the daughter nucleus remains intact.
The tagging of
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
beta decay with the 66.7 keV metastable state of
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
allows to probe simultaneously the pnn- as well as the ppn- and ppp-channels (see section 3). This tagging includes nnn-decays without
ionizing particle emission, i.e. ’invisible decays’
heeck2020inclusive
, to the subset of bound states in
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
that decay through the 66.7 keV metastable state.
Hence we perform in the nnn-channel a semi-inclusive search. The constraint to nnn-decays that are invisible in our detectors ensures the absence of pile-up between the signal from nnn-decay and the tagging event, i.e. a gamma transition in
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
.
For the corresponding partial decay width holds
\Gamma_{nnn}^{b^{\prime}}<\Gamma_{nnn}^{b}
. Our measurement will constitute thus the limit for the sum of partial decay widths

\Gamma^{b}_{3}=\Gamma_{ppp}^{b}+\Gamma_{ppn}^{b}+\Gamma_{pnn}^{b}+\Gamma_{nnn}^{b^{\prime}}
of the tri-nucleon process with a partial lifetime limit
\tau_{b}=1/\Gamma^{b}_{3}
.

2
The GERDA experiment

The GERmanium Detector Array (GERDA) experiment
ackermann2013gerda
;
agostini2018upgrade
was located at the Laboratori Nazionali del Gran Sasso (LNGS) of INFN under the Gran Sasso mountain, Italy. The overhead rock provides shielding from atmospheric muons with a mean muon flux
of 3.5x10
-4
s
-1
m
-2

agostini2016flux
. The experiment employed High Purity Germanium (HPGe) detectors in a liquid argon (LAr) cryostat
knopfle2022design
housed within a tank of ultra-pure water instrumented with photomultiplier tubes (PMTs) to tag the Cherenkov light from incident muons. GERDA employed several types of HPGe detectors with different geometries: semi-coaxial and BEGe (Broad Energy Germanium)
agostini2017background
and inverted coaxial
agostini2021characterization
. Above the cryostat was a lock system accessed via a clean room which isolated the LAr in the cryostat from the lab atmosphere and allowed for the insertion and removal of strings of detectors. Figure
2
shows a cross section of the installation with these key features.

Figure 2:
Cross sections of the GERDA experimental apparatus and an enlarged view of the central part (right), the germanium detector array enclosed by the LAr veto system
agostini2018upgrade
.

GERDA’s primary purpose was to search for a signature of neutrinoless double beta
(
0\nu\beta\beta
) decay to probe the Majorana nature of the neutrino
agostini2020final
.
A good candidate source nucleus for
0\nu\beta\beta
decay must not undergo single beta decay.
This condition is fulfilled by
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
for which the process is energetically forbidden.
For this search then, GERDA’s HPGe detectors are enriched in
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
to about 88% and
operated directly in LAr. The LAr has a dual purpose, to cool the detectors to cryogenic temperatures
as well as provide a veto system from the scintillation light of processes depositing energy in
the LAr. Scintillation light is detected by PMTs or silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs) coupled to
wavelength shifting fiber shrouds. The LAr is also used to shield against external background
contributions from
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 238$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{U}
and
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 232$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Th}
decay chains. In both
0\nu\beta\beta

and tri-nucleon decay searches
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
is both the detector and source material allowing
for excellent detection efficiency. Further, the exceptional energy resolution obtainable by
HPGe detectors is well established in the literature

agostini2017background
;
agostini2021characterization
;
agostini2021calibration
.

Tagging
\mathbf{{}^{73}Ga}
beta decays via the
\mathbf{{}^{73m}Ge}
decay

{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
is an unstable isotope that beta decays with a half life of 4.86 h to excited states of the stable isotope
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
. Figure
3
shows the level scheme for
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
populated by this process. Importantly, the initial beta decay does not populate the
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
ground state due to a large nuclear spin difference compared to the
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
ground state (
9/2^{+}
and
3/2^{-}
respectively). 5.9% of decays will directly populate the
1/2^{-}
metastable state at 66.7 keV, all other decays will populate higher energy levels which will decay by gamma emission. Virtually all cascades will transition to the metastable state. Altogether 98.2% of
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
decays will promptly reach the metastable state which functions as a bottleneck in the decay to the
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
ground state. When an event is triggered in the detector the flash ADC is readout for a trace window of 160
\mu
s, the waveform’s leading edge is centred in the trace window at 80
\mu
s.

The initial beta decay and subsequent gamma cascade will trigger an initial event
in the detector corresponding to their summed energies. In the analysis we call
this sum
E_{1}
. Since the metastable state has a half life of 0.499 s its decay
will constitute a separate event trigger with energy 66.7 keV, which we will
refer to as
E_{2}
, in practically all decays.
The energy of the metastable state is such that only a subset of the GERDA Phase II
data in which the trigger threshold was lowered to around 20 keV could be
considered.

The metastable state decays via a two step cascade. Firstly the 66.7 keV state decays to the 13.3 keV state emitting 53.4 keV. This 13.3 keV state is also metastable but with a much shorter half life of 2.95
\mu
s, small compared to the recorded trace window of 160
\mu
s, and will decay shortly afterwards by emitting 13.3 keV. The Figure
4
shows a typical waveform from the decay of the 66.7 keV state.
Whereas a waveform from background events normally exhibits a single leading edge to a maximum amplitude, the waveform of the 66.7 keV metastable state will have two leading edges. The metastable state was previously used in analyses of muon activity in MAJORANA also making use of the unique shape
arnquist2022signatures
.

Our search procedure for the
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
decay is then to consider by delayed coincidences any pair of events with energies
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
recorded in a single detector within 2.5 s of each other (5 half lives of the metastable state) where the first event has the energy
E_{1}

{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}

Monte Carlo simulation of
73
Ga decays

The responses to
73
Ga decays originating from inside the HPGe detectors were simulated using the MaGe software package
boswell2011mage
based on Geant4
agostinelli2003geant4
. MaGe does not provide waveforms of events but instead records energy depositions with position and timing information in defined sensitive regions of the detectors. The timing information was used to group energy depositions within 80
\mu

E_{1}
and
E_{2}
events. Following the clustering a smearing is applied to the energies to simulate the energy resolution of the detectors. The parameters for this smearing are obtained from the energy resolution curves for each detector type
agostini2021calibration
. For the Monte Carlo simulation
10^{7}
primaries were simulated.

energy depositions also deposited energy in the LAr, thus applying the LAr veto to these energies in the search would lead to a significant loss of signal. On the other hand, in the decay of the metastable state rarely energy is deposited in the LAr and the LAr veto can be applied.

5
Risetime cuts

We reconstruct from the GERDA data both
\tau_{60}
and
\tau_{90}
for waveforms. These are the times taken for a waveform to go from 5% to 60% and from 10% to 90% of its maximum amplitude respectively. For the two step
E_{2}=66.7
keV event the 60% threshold is 40 keV and occurs during the first leading edge. The 90% threshold is 60 keV and occurs during the second leading edge. As a result
\tau_{90}
is proportional to the survival time of the 13.3 keV intermediate state.
Typical
\tau_{90}
values are on the order of a few 100 ns for background events, while the 13.3 keV state’s half life is an order of magnitude larger. Signal events generally have significantly longer
\tau_{90}
values than typical events in the data.
\tau_{60}
on the other hand has no dependence on the survival time of the 13.3 keV state and expected
\tau_{60}
values for signal and background events are comparable. Using the risetime information we optimised a threshold cut on the composite variable
\tau_{90}-\tau_{60}
, which is large for signal waveforms and small for background and hence a clear separation is expected between the two distributions. The parameter also implicitly applies a threshold on
\tau_{90}
which is larger for signal events. This risetime optimisation was performed individually for each detector type employed in GERDA with figure
8
showing an optimisation plot for the BEGe detectors.

Figure 8:
Risetime optimisation plot for BEGe detectors. The signal efficiency for the optimum is 91% with 93% background rejection at a time difference threshold of 500 ns. The other detector types exhibit similar performance.

We considered a search region of 40 keV to 72 keV for the 66.7 keV signal. The upper limit of 72 keV is due to the energy resolution of HPGe detectors (energy resolution at 67 keV
\leq 5
keV at full width half maximum). The lower bound arises from the possibility that some energy is lost in the dead layer or the reconstructed energy is lower since the energy integration time of around 1
\mu
s is lower than the half-life of the 13.3 keV state. The lower value of 40 keV is well above the trigger threshold. The limited energy integration time does not influence the risetime reconstruction. There are 4 events surviving our cuts all of which are below 40 keV. No candidates survive in the region of interest. This observation holds even if we abandon the
E_{1}<1600
keV cut such that any gamma cascade from the
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
bound state region up to 6.8 MeV is accepted. Hence we find no evidence for tri-nucleon decays of
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
to the bound states of
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cu}
,
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Zn}
,
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
, and for invisible nnn-decays to
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
.

From the exclusion of the
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
decay in our dataset we set a lower limit on the partial mean lifetime of the tri-nucleon process at 90% credibility.

for the tri-nucleon processes
x = ppp, ppn, and pnn was then calculated with the following formula

\displaystyle\mathrm{\tau_{b}}\geq\frac{1}{S}\frac{N_{a}}{m_{Ge}}\sum\limits_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}{\epsilon_{tot_{i}}}

(1)

where
\mathrm{\tau_{b}}
denotes the partial lifetime for tri-nucleon decays to the bound states of the daughter nuclei.
\mathcal{E}_{i}
denotes the exposure for a particular detector type.
\epsilon_{tot_{i}}
denotes the total analysis efficiency for a detector type.
N_{a}
is Avogadro’s constant, and
m_{Ge}
is the molar mass of the enriched germanium in the detectors.

S
denotes the lower signal limit at 90% CI for no observed background or signal and has a value
of 2.3 counts in a Bayesian analysis. An exposure of 61.89 kg yr corresponds to a lower
limit on
\mathrm{\tau_{b}}
of 1.2x10
26
yr on the aforementioned tri-nucleon decay channels of
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
. The main systematic uncertainty of this analysis arises from the active volume and enrichment fractions of the detectors (4%) which do not significantly contribute to the stated limit and can be neglected.

Above analysis can also be applied to the invisible nnn-decays to bound states of
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
. If
k
denotes the fraction with which these states decay via the 66.7 keV metastable state, the lower limit on the partial lifetime of this process is estimated to be
k\cdot 10^{26}
yr at 90% credibility . Hence a fraction
k
as low as
10^{-3}
would still constrain the partial mean lifetime of the considered invisible nnn-decays to
10^{23}
yr.
No estimates of the value of k are available. The main challenge in calculating this value is the poorly known level scheme of
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
up to the neutron threshold and the unknown reaction mechanism of nnn-decay of
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
.

Previous limits for
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
were set by the MAJORANA collaboration for the inclusive copper channel and for the
exclusive copper and zinc channels assuming the quoted decay channels to be the dominant ones without identifying the particular emitted particles
alvis2019search
. Our result improves on the limits for these channels as well as setting the first limits on both the inclusive gallium and zinc channels. In addition, the limits from our analysis have no model dependence concerning the decay channel.
Inclusive ppp- and ppn-decay studies have also been performed with
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 136$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Xe}

Xe136
yielding limits in the order of 10
23
yr. Inclusive nnn-decays have been searched for with
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 127$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{I}

hazama1994limits
. The deduced limit in the order of 10
23
yr is, however, not a nuclear lifetime but takes shell model combinations of baryons within the nucleus into account.
Current limits for proton
takenaka2020search
and di-nucleon decays
araki2006search
are many orders of magnitude larger providing good motivation to investigate multi-nucleon decays.
Our results represent the most stringent limits on inclusive tri-nucleon decays to date by utilising the unique properties of the gamma cascade of the de-excitation of the metastable state in
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
. LEGEND (Large Enriched Germanium Experiment for Neutrinoless
\beta\beta
Decay), GERDA’s successor, has started to collect data and will eventually offer a much greater exposure for future tri-nucleon decay searches with
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 76$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
. The quantitative study of the nnn-decay to
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ge}
remains a challenge. Moreover, muon induced spallation could also create
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
within HPGe detectors with an identical signature to tri-nucleon decay. Future analyses with higher exposure datasets may then contain such cosmogenic
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 73$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
decays, potentially hampering the stringency of limits that can be obtained. In the case of no signal candidates as in our dataset this problem does not manifest.

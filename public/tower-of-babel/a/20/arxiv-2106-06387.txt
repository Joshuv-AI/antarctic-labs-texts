Canonical models of modular curves and the Galois action on CM-points
C.Daw, B.Zilber

Abstract




We use the theory of canonical models of Shimura varieties to describe the projective limit of the curves
\mathbb{Y}(N),
all
N,
and its automorphism group.
In particular we prove that the Galois group of
{\mathbb{Q}}
(CM) over
{\mathbb{Q}}
is an open subgroup of
an automorphism group of an explicitly given adelic structure.

1
Introduction

The origin of this paper is due to a model-theoretic project which attempts to formalise the notion of an analytic covering space of an algebraic variety in such a way that the formal cover is unique up to isomorphism. See e.g.
[
10
]
or
[
12
]
for a survey on this project. In particular, we are interested in
[
13
]
in the situation that arises in the context of a system of analytic functions

j_{N}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{Y}(N),\ N=1,2\ldots

(1)

where
\mathbb{Y}(N)=\Gamma(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}
is the algebraic curve isomorphic to the modular curve
\mathbb{Y}(N)
but defined over
{\mathbb{Q}},
at the cost of ignoring the
level
N
structure on the modular curve. Such curves (or rather their completed versions
{\mathbb{X}}(N)
) are considered e.g. in
[
14
]
.

The key to obtaining the desired uniqueness result turns out to be the understanding of

\tilde{\mathbb{H}}:=\varprojlim\Gamma(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}

and the action of
\mathrm{Aut}\,{\mathbb{C}}
on it. The latter is reduced to the understanding of

\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{CM}):{\mathbb{Q}}),
where CM is the set of all the CM points on all the
\mathbb{Y}(N).
More precisely, we need to describe the
structure, the algebraic relations on the set of CM-points and the action of
\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{CM}):{\mathbb{Q}})
on CM-points.

We use the well-known machinery of canonical models of Shimura varieties and complex multiplication, see e.g.
[
5
]
, which is essentially an answer to the Langlands conjecture on conjugation of Shimura varieties in
[
9
]
.

1.1

A characterisation of
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
is closely related to a problem raised in
[
9
]
by R.Langlands that was resolved by M.Borovoi, P.Deligne, J.Milne, K.Shih and others. (We only need the case of the product of Shimura curves.)
A key outcome of the theory is the proof of existence and uniqueness of
canonical models
of Shimura varieties.

Recall that a Shimura variety is given by a Shimura datum
(G,X)
, where
X
is a complex manifold and
G
is a reductive linear algebraic group such that
G({\mathbb{R}})
acts on
X.

The corresponding Shimura variety is defined as

\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_{K}:=G({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash(X\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K)

where
\mathbb{A}_{f}
is the ring of finite adeles and
K
runs through the family of open compact subgroups of
G(\mathbb{A}_{f}).
For each
K,
the set
\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_{K}
of double cosets naturally possesses the structure of a complex quasi-projective algebraic variety. The canonical model, which we also denote
\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_{K}
, is a model for
\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_{K}
over an explicit number field
E(G,X)
. For
X=\mathbb{H}^{\pm}:={\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}
and
G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}

we get the family of modular curves
\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_{K}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. We refer to
[
5
]
for a general survey and
[
4
]
for the case of Shimura curves.

Denote

\mathrm{S}:=\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash(\mathbb{H}^{\pm}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})),

the covering of canonical Shimura curves
,
and
\Delta
the subgroup of matrices of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
of the form
\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}a\ 0\\
0\ 1\end{array}\right),

a\in\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}.
Then there is a holomorphic covering map
\mathrm{S}\to\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
which represents
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
as

\tilde{\mathbb{H}}\cong\mathrm{S}/\Delta=\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash(\mathbb{H}^{\pm}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f}))/\Delta.

This essentially reduces the study of
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
to the study of

\mathrm{S}.

1.2

In our analysis it is important to distinguish the full structure on
\mathrm{S}
as a complex manifold with infinitely many connected components, which we name
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Full}},
and the purely algebraic structure
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Pure}},
which is just a set split into connected components with the action of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
on it.

It is clear that a CM-point on the
\mathbb{Y}(N)
comes from a point on
\mathrm{S}
fixed by a
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
which is not in the centre of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})
(the centre of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})
acts trivially on
\mathrm{S}
). We call such points fixed points or, with a slight abuse of terminology, CM-points.

In particular we are interested in substructures

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Full}}(\mathrm{CM})
and
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Pure}}(\mathrm{CM})
of our structures reduced to the sets of their CM-points (also sometimes called
special points
).

Note that by construction
\mathrm{S}
is a union of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
-orbits (Hecke orbits)
S_{\tau}:=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})\cdot\tau,

\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{\pm}

and in particular

\mathrm{CM}=\bigcup_{\sqrt{-m}}\mathrm{CM}_{\sqrt{-m}},\ \ \mathrm{CM}_{\sqrt{-m}}=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})\cdot\sqrt{-m}

where
m
runs in
{\mathbb{N}},
square-free (including
m=1
).

1.3

The main results are as follows.

Theorem A
(see
3.23
(i)).

\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{CM}):{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathrm{Aut}\,\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Full}}(\mathrm{CM})\leq_{\approx}\mathrm{Aut}\,\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Pure}}(\mathrm{CM}),

where
\leq_{\approx}
(”almost equal”)
means that for any finite set
\bar{\tau}
of square-free imaginary quadratics, for
\mathrm{CM}_{\bar{\tau}}=\bigcup\{\mathrm{CM}_{\tau}:\tau\in\bar{\tau}\},

\mathrm{Aut}\,\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Full}}(\mathrm{CM}_{\bar{\tau}})\leq_{\mathrm{open}}\mathrm{Aut}\,\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Pure}}(\mathrm{CM}_{\bar{\tau}}),

(open, and hence finite index subgroup).

The theorem is the basis of the model-theoretic result in our paper
[
13
]
which we expect to be extendable to the general case of Shimura varieties, and so the statement of Theorem A.

The theorem allows, with some extra work, see
4.7
, to characterise the limit structure
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}=\varprojlim\Gamma(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}
and the abstract formalisation of the system (
1
), which is the key to the application in
[
13
]
.

Let
\mathrm{F}
be
the composite of all
{\mathbb{Q}}(\tau)^{\mathrm{ab}}
for all imaginary quadratic
\tau.

Let
P\subset{\mathbb{N}}
be the set of primes, including 1.

Theorem B
(see
3.23
(ii)).

(i)

\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{CM}):{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathrm{Gal}(\mathrm{F}:{\mathbb{Q}})

(ii)

\mathrm{Gal}(\mathrm{F}:{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}})\leq_{\approx}\prod_{p\in P}\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-p})^{\mathrm{ab}}:{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}})

(the RHS can be identified with a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}\,\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Pure}}(\mathrm{CM})
) and

\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-p})^{\mathrm{ab}}:{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}})\cong T^{0}_{\sqrt{-p}}(\mathbb{A}_{f})/(T^{0}_{\sqrt{-p}}(\mathbb{A}_{f})\cap\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}}))

for some 1-dimensional torus
T^{0}_{\sqrt{-p}}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}.

(iii) There is a commutative group
\mathbb{T}
given in terms of an infinite dimensional matrix group over finite adeles
\mathbb{A}_{f},

\mathbb{T}\leq_{\approx}\prod_{p\in P}\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-p})^{\mathrm{ab}}:{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-p}))

and a short exact sequence

1\to\mathbb{T}\to\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{CM}):{\mathbb{Q}})\to\mathrm{C}_{2}^{P}\to 1

where
\mathrm{C}_{2}^{P}
is the infinite product of 2-element groups
\mathrm{C}_{2}(p),

p\in P,
each acting by an involution on the
p
-th co-ordinate of
\mathbb{T}.

We are especially interested in
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
with the structure induced on it from
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Full}},
including the adelic group
\tilde{\mathrm{G}}<\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
acting on
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
by the action which comes from the action of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
on
\mathrm{S}.

This group can
be seen as the completion of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{Q}})
in
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}.
In particular, we establish that
\mathrm{Aut}\,{\mathbb{C}}
acts on
\tilde{\mathrm{G}}
as the Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}:{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times},
by conjugation with elements of
\Delta.

1.4

Note that
Theorem A brings into the subject a Model Theory flavour by focussing on the specific class of structures
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Pure}}
which in Model Theory classification belong to the class of
locally modular geometries of trivial type
. It thus emphasises the analogy with Serre’s open image theorem which characterises

\mathrm{Gal}(\mathrm{k}(\mathrm{Tors}):\mathrm{k})
of torsion points of an elliptic curve
E
(without CM) defined over
\mathrm{k}
as an open subgroup in the linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}).
That is it characterises the structure
E(\mathrm{Tors})
as a
locally modular geometry of linear type, an analogue of
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Pure}}
.

2.1

We follow

Recall that a Shimura variety
\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)
is given by the Shimura datum
(G,X)
where
X
is a complex manifold and
G
is a reductive linear algebraic group defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
together with an action of
G({\mathbb{R}})
on
X
which satisfies certain conditions (SV1–SV3, see
[
5
]
, Definition 5.5. In the full generality of the conjecture of
1.4
we also assume SV5).

Our main interest is in the case when

G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}\mbox{ and }X=\mathbb{H}\cup-\mathbb{H}={\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}

but we also need the cartesian powers of this Shimura datum,

(G^{n},X^{n})
, which are again Shimura data by definition.

2.2

Given a compact open subgroup
K
of
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})

one defines the canonical model for respective Shimura datum to be the scheme of an appropriate (reflex) number field

\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_{K}:=G({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash(X\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f}))/K

which should be understood as the set of double cosets with scheme structure (which we use in a very restricted way) induced by the ring of automorphic forms on
X.
This definition also makes sense when
K
is not open, e.g.
K=\{1\}.
In this case it is still a scheme though not of finite type. See
[
5
]
, Remark 5.30. We are going to use the construction for some compact
K
which are not open in this more general sense without making claims on its nature as a scheme.

When
K
is an open compact in
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
then
\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_{K}
is a complex algebraic variety.

When
K=\{1\}
we get the complex manifold

\mathrm{Sh}(G,X):=G({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash(X\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f}))=\lim_{K}\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_{K}

with uncountably many connected components (this equality assumes certain assumptions which
G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}
satisfies).

The elements
(x,a)\in X\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
are acted upon by
q\in G({\mathbb{Q}})
on the left and by
k\in K
on the right, so

q\cdot(x,a)\cdot k:=(qx,qak),

and the respective elements of
\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_{K}
are written in the form
[x,a]
and by definition of double cosets

[x,a]=[qx,qak].

It will be convenient for us to define
the action of
g\in G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
on the set of cosets, for
K=\{1\},
by multiplication by
g^{-1}
on the right

g*[x,a]:=[x,ag^{-1}].

(2)

If
g=q\in G({\mathbb{Q}})
then we have

q*[x,1]=[x,q^{-1}]=[qx,1],

that is, if we identify
X=X\times\{1\}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_{K}
with
K=\{1\}
then

G({\mathbb{Q}})
acts on
X
exactly by its standard action.
In particular,

qx=x\Rightarrow q*[x,1]=[x,1].

(3)

We now fix our notations. For a modular curve we have

\mathrm{S}_{K}:=\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash(\mathbb{H}\cup-\mathbb{H})\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K,

(4)

For the case

\mathrm{S}:=\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash(\mathbb{H}\cup-\mathbb{H})\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f}).

(5)

Here

acts on pairs

Remark/Corollary.

Consider the embedding

(\mathbb{H},\mathrm{G})\subset(\tilde{\mathbb{H}},\tilde{\mathrm{G}}),

where
\mathrm{G}\cong\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})\cap\tilde{\mathrm{G}}=\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{Q}}).

1.
Clearly, under this embedding special points in
\mathbb{H}
are just quadratic imaginary points.

The action of individual elements
\mathbf{d}^{\prime}_{q}\in\Delta^{\prime}({\mathbb{Q}}_{+}),

\mathbf{d}_{q}\in\Delta({\mathbb{Q}}_{+})
and the scalar matrices
q\cdot\mathbf{I}
are definable.

The scalar matrices act trivially, as identity.

2. More generally, the action of
h\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{Q}})
depends on the choice of an isomorphism

\phi_{\mu}:\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{Q}}),
for
\mu\in\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times},
see
4.3
A(b). However, on
\mathbb{H}
only

h^{\mu}
for
\mu=\pm 1
corresponds to a non-empty curve. Thus
h
defines (in the natural
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
-language) the two-component curve
C_{h}\cup C_{h}^{-1},
where
C_{h}^{-1}
is the graph
of
\mathbf{d}_{-1}\cdot h\cdot\mathbf{d}_{-1}

and the choice of the component depends on
\phi_{\mu}.
(Note that if

h=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}a\ b\\
c\ d\end{array}\right)
then
\mathbf{d}_{-1}\cdot h\cdot\mathbf{d}_{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}a\ -b\\
-c\ \ d\end{array}\right).
)

3. The 4-point relation
R(s_{1},s_{2},s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2})
on
\mathbb{H}
for

s_{i}=p_{i}+q_{i}\sqrt{-m_{i}},

s^{\prime}_{i}=p^{\prime}_{i}+q^{\prime}_{i}\sqrt{-m_{i}},

p_{i},q_{i},p^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}},

q_{i},q^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+},
holds if and only if

q_{1}=q^{\prime}_{1}\ \&\ q_{2}=q^{\prime}_{2}\ \&\ ((p_{1}=p^{\prime}_{1}\ \&\ p_{2}=p^{\prime}_{2})\vee(p_{1}=-p^{\prime}_{1}\ \&\ p_{2}=-p^{\prime}_{2}))

Thus in
\mathbb{H}:

R(s_{1},s_{2},s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2})\ \Leftrightarrow\ \langle s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2}\rangle=\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle\ \vee\ \langle s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2}\rangle=\langle-s^{c}_{1},-s^{c}_{2}\rangle

where
-s^{c}
is the application of complex conjugation to
-s.

4. Respectively, on
\mathbb{Y}(N)=\Gamma(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}=\tilde{\Gamma}(N)\backslash\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
the indiscernible curves
C^{\mu}_{h,K(N)}

are conjugated by elements of the group
\Delta/\Delta(N),
or equivalently, by
\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{N}):{\mathbb{Q}}).
The union
\bigcup_{\mu}C^{\mu}_{h,K(N)}
is a (reducible) algebraic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}.

The
j_{N}
-image of the 4-point relations
R(s_{1},s_{2},s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2})
is a relation on CM-points of
\mathbb{Y}(N),
call it
R_{N}.

The respective set of pairs (called type of
\langle j_{N}(s_{1}),j_{N}(s_{2})\rangle
in Model Theory),

\mathrm{tp}_{N}(\langle j_{N}(s_{1}),j_{N}(s_{2})\rangle):=\{\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle\in\mathbb{Y}(N)\times\mathbb{Y}(N):R_{N}(j_{N}(s_{1}),j_{N}(s_{2}),y_{1},y_{2})\}

is, by
4.3
E(c), a
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-orbit of the pair, a finite Zariski set.

5. Given
g\in\tilde{\mathrm{G}}
the graph of
g,

\mathsf{graph}\,g\subset\tilde{\mathbb{H}}\times\tilde{\mathbb{H}},
has a point in
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
if and only if
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{Q}}).

Indeed, suppose
\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle\in(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H})\cap\mathsf{graph}\,g.

We can represent
s=[\tau,\Delta]
and

s^{\prime}=[\tau^{\prime},\Delta]

\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}.
Then by assumption

[\tau^{\prime},\Delta]=[\tau,g^{-1}\Delta],
which implies

\tau^{\prime}=q\cdot\tau
and

[q\cdot\tau,q\cdot g^{-1}\Delta]=[q\cdot\tau,\Delta],
for some
q\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{Q}}).
That is

q\cdot g^{-1}\in\Delta
which can only happen if
q\cdot g=1,
since

\det q\cdot g\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}.

4.9

The structure
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathrm{Full}}.
The determination of this structure in terms of concrete relations and operations as in
4.6
for
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathrm{Pure}}

would be rather cumbersome for the context of the current paper. Instead we recall that the respective structure
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Full}}
is the structure obtained from
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Pure}}
by adding some algebraic-geometric relations defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
(an
expansion
of the structure
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Pure}}
).
More technically the relationship between the smaller and the bigger structure is described by the close relationship between their automorphism groups in section 3.

Definition.

\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathrm{Full}}
is the expansion of
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathrm{Pure}}
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}},
such that

\mathrm{Aut}\,\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathrm{Full}}=\mathrm{Aut}\,\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{Full}}.

4.10

Corollary.

\mathrm{Aut}\,{\mathbb{C}}
acts on
\tilde{\mathrm{G}}
in
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathrm{Full}}
as

\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}:{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}:

for
\mu\in\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
and
h=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}a\ b\\
c\ d\end{array}\right)\in\tilde{\mathrm{G}},

h^{\mu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}a\ \ \ \mu b\\
\mu^{-1}c\ \ d\end{array}\right).

Proof.
See
4.3
, A(b).

Construction of gravitational instantons with non-maximal volume growth via codimension-1 collapse
Willem Adriaan Salm

Abstract
In this paper, we construct families of gravitational instantons of type ALG, ALG*, ALH and ALH* using a gluing construction. Away from a finite set of exceptional points, the metric collapses with bounded curvature to a quotient of ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} by ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} and a lattice of rank one or two. Depending on whether the gravitational instantons are of type ALG/ALG* or ALH/ALH*, there are either two or four exceptional points respectively that are modelled on the Atiyah-Hitchin manifold. The other exceptional points are modelled on the Taub-NUT metric. There are at most four, respectively eight, of these points in each case.

Introduction

Gravitational instantons are complete, non-compact hyperkähler manifolds of dimension four with
L^{2}
bounded curvature. In the late 70’s,
Eguchi and Hanson (1978)
gave the first non-trivial examples of these spaces.
Over the years, many other constructions of gravitational instantons were found:
Kronheimer (1989)
constructed gravitational instantons using hyperkähler quotients.
Atiyah and Hitchin (1988)
constructed a gravitational instanton using gauge theory.

Ivanov and Roček (1996)
,
Lindström and Roček (1988)
,
Cherkis and Kapustin (1999)
and
Cherkis and Hitchin (2005)
constructed gravitational instantons using twistor methods.

Biquard and Minerbe (2011)
constructed examples using a gluing construction.

Tian and Yau (1990)
and
Hein (2011)
;
Hein (2012)
created examples by solving the complex Monge–Ampère equation.
Finally,
Sun and Zhang (2024)
classified all gravitational instantons.

According to
Chen and Chen (2019)
, there are four classes of gravitational instantons with
r^{-2-\epsilon}
curvature decay. These classes are called ALE (Asymptotically Locally Euclidean), ALF (Asymptotically Locally Flat), ALG and ALH and they are distinguished by their
volume growth
. That is, in a gravitational instanton of type ALE, ALF, ALG or ALH, a ball of radius
r\gg 1
will have volume of order
r^{4}
,
r^{3}
,
r^{2}
or
r
respectively.
If the curvature of a gravitational instanton does not decay with
\mathcal{O}(r^{-2-\epsilon})
rate, it can only be in one of two classes: ALG* or ALH*. In this case, the volume growth will be of order
r^{2}
or
r^{4/3}
respectively.

Although there are now many different constructions, each construction is tailored to a specific class of gravitational instantons. In this paper we show that the gluing construction carried out by
Schroers and Singer (2021)
for ALF gravitational instantons, can be extended to gravitational instantons of type ALG, ALG*, ALH and ALH*. Moreover, we show that this can be done in an explicit, systematic and uniform way, even if, from a geometric and analytic viewpoint, these gravitational instantons behave quite differently.

This construction will also be useful in understanding the boundary of the moduli space of gravitational instantons. As this moduli space is not compact, it is possible that families of gravitational instantons degenerate. Like in the work of
Foscolo (2019)
, we will focus on the limit where a family of gravitational instantons collapse to a flat 3-dimensional space. In order to describe this process explicitly, we will construct a 4-manifold which has the structure of a circle bundle almost everywhere. We will equip it with a metric that is approximately hyperkähler and we will show that when the circle fibres are sufficiently small, this metric can be perturbed into a genuine hyperkähler metric.

2
The approximate solution

In this section, we will construct a 1-parameter family of Riemannian 4-manifolds that is approximately hyperkähler by gluing known examples. First, we need to recall the construction due to
Gibbons and Hawking (1978)
.
In modern language, we need a flat
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(U,g_{U})
with a parallel orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}
, a principal
S^{1}
-bundle
P
over
U
, a connection
\eta
on
P
, and a harmonic function
h\colon U\to(0,\infty)
satisfying
*^{g_{U}}\operatorname{d}h=\operatorname{d}\eta
.
The metric

g^{GH}:=h\cdot g_{U}+h^{-1}\eta^{2}

with the
2
-forms

\omega^{GH}_{i}:=e_{i}\wedge\eta+h*^{g_{U}}e_{i}

is hyperkähler and we call this construction the Gibbons-Hawking ansatz.

Some of the requirements above are redundant. Namely, if
H^{2}(U,\mathbb{Z})
has no torsion, then
P
is fully determined by the cohomology class of
[\operatorname{d}\eta]=[*\operatorname{d}h]
. Moreover, every principal bundle admits a connection
\eta
and this connection can be chosen such that
\operatorname{d}\eta=*\operatorname{d}h
.

One important example of the Gibbons-Hawking ansatz is the Taub-NUT metric.
The Taub-NUT metric with mass
k
is the 1-point completion of the Gibbons-Hawking metric for
h(x)=c+\frac{k}{2|x|}
on
U=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
. It is a smooth hyperkähler manifold if
k=1
. (Hence, if the mass is not specified, one assumes that
k=1
.)

Another important example of a gravitational instanton is due to
Atiyah and Hitchin (1988)
.
Topologically, this space can be viewed as a cohomogeneity-one manifold
2
2

2

A good exposition about this can be found in
Schroers and Singer (2021)
.
. Namely, the Atiyah-Hitchin manifold has a natural
SO(3)
action and its quotient with this
SO(3)
action is the half line
[\pi,\infty)
. A generic orbit is of the form
SU(2)/\langle i,j,k\rangle
, while the orbit over the endpoint of the half line can be identified with
SU(2)/\langle e^{k\phi},j\rangle\simeq\mathbb{R}P^{2}
. Hence, the Atiyah-Hitchin manifold retracts to
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
.

The Atiyah-Hitchin manifold has a metric that is hyperkähler.
To understand its asymptotics, one has to consider its branched double cover. With the above identifications of the fibres, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action of this branched double cover is given by the
j
-multiplication. A generic fibre on this branched double cover is of the form
SU(2)/\langle i\rangle
, which is a degree
-4
circle bundle over
S^{2}
. When the radial parameter is sufficiently large, the metric of the Atiyah-Hitchin manifold on this branched double cover approximates the Taub-NUT metric with mass
-4
with exponentially small error. Also, on this circle bundle over
S^{2}
, the
j
-action descends to the antipodal map on the base space and it identifies the fibres by reversing the orientation, i.e. for any
x
in this circle bundle,
e^{i\phi}\cdot j\cdot x=j\cdot e^{-i\phi}\cdot x
.

2.1
The bulk space

For our gluing construction we will follow a method proposed by
Sen (1997)
:
Namely, he started with the Gibbons-Hawking Ansatz on a punctured
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the harmonic function

h(x)=1+\frac{-4}{2|x|}+\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2|x+p_{i}|}+\frac{1}{2|x-p_{i}|},

where
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
are distinct. Near each singularity
p_{i}
the Gibbons-Hawking metric approximates the Taub-NUT metric and near the origin it approximates the Taub-NUT metric with mass
-4
.

Secondly, he considered the antipodal map on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and he lifted this involution such that near the origin it coincides with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action on the branched double cover of the Atiyah-Hitchin manifold. After taking the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-quotient, he claimed that this hyperkähler space can be made complete by gluing in the Atiyah-Hitchin manifold near the origin and the Taub-NUT space near each
p_{i}
. Although Sen never did this gluing explicitly,
Schroers and Singer (2021)
formalised his argument in their quest of finding geometric models of matter
Atiyah et al. (2012)
.

We start our gluing construction with the construction of the bulk spaces.
That is, we generalize Sen’s use of the Gibbons-Hawking ansatz on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
modulo a non-maximal lattice. In Section
2.2
we make this bulk space complete and equip it with an almost hyperkähler metric.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix once and for all a non-maximal lattice
L
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

•

We call the quotient
B:=\mathbb{R}^{3}/L
, endowed with the flat metric, the base space.

•

We refer to the map
\tau\colon B\to B
that is induced from the map
x\mapsto-x
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as the antipodal map.

•

We denote the fixed point set of
\tau
by
\{q_{j}\}
and we call
q_{j}
a fixed point or a fixed point singularity. Unless specified otherwise, we call the action induced by
\tau
on
B
, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
action on
B
.

Because
L
is non-maximal, the base space
B
can only be diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
or
\mathbb{R}\times T^{2}
. As explained in
Salm (2025)
, each case yields different kinds of gravitational instantons and will require different kinds of analysis. To distinguish these cases we often will write
B=\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
B=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
or
B=\mathbb{R}\times T^{2}
.

When the lattice

is trivial, the only fixed point is the origin. However, in the other cases we have two or four fixed points respectively. Just as in
Sen (1997)
, we pick a certain number of points which we remove from

. It turns out we need to impose some restrictions on the number of singularities, which are given in the following definition. In Lemma

we see the necessity of these requirements.

Definition 2.2
.

Fix once and for all a finite set of points
\{p_{i}\}\in(B\setminus\{q_{j}\})/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. We call an element
p_{i}
a non-fixed point or a non-fixed singularity. When
B=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
or

, the maximum number of non-fixed points must not exceed four or eight respectively.

With abuse of notation, we also denote

as the lift of the non-fixed points to
B
.

Like in the work of
Sen (1997)
, we need to consider the punctured base space before we can consider the Gibbons–Hawking ansatz. Due to technical reasons we need to remove small balls around the fixed-point singularities. This reason will become apparent in Lemma
2.6
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let
\overline{B}_{4\epsilon}(q_{j})
be closed small balls of radius

q_{j}
. Let
\cup_{j}\overline{B}_{4\epsilon}(q_{j})
be their union inside
B
. Let
\cup_{i}\{\pm p_{i}\}
be the union of the non-fixed points in
B
.
We define the punctured base space

as the complement of
\cup_{i}\{\pm p_{i}\}
and
\cup_{j}\overline{B}_{4\epsilon}(q_{j})

B^{\prime}=B\setminus\left(\cup_{i}\{\pm p_{i}\}\bigcup\cup_{j}\overline{B}_{4\epsilon}(q_{j})\right).

(1)

Remark 2.4

The exact choice of the radius for

2.2
The interpolation of the Kähler forms

Following the method by
Sen (1997)

We only need to define a global metric.

and the asymptotic regions of the Atiyah-Hitchin manifolds and Taub-NUT spaces using a suitable gauge transformation. We explain our choice of gauge transformation and we give the interpolated forms explicitly.

3
The deformation problem

To perturb the approximate solution, we phrase the hyperkähler conditions as an elliptic PDE which we solve using the inverse function theorem. To do this, we introduce an alternative definition of hyperkähler manifolds in terms of their Kähler forms. We use this alternative definition to set up the deformation problem. The perturbation argument explained here is a slightly modified version of that used in
Schroers and Singer (2021)

Chen and Chen (2021)

For these ALF spaces the model at infinity is fully determined by the degree of the circle bundle at infinity and the length of its fibre at infinity. These parameters correspond to the number of non-fixed singularities

and
\epsilon

n

\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

In

In their paper they argue that the period map over count the moduli space.
This is because
H_{2}(M_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1},n})
has a
2
-cycle that is represented by a torus at infinity. This
2
-cycle can only reveal information of the model space, which is fixed. Therefore, the dimension of the moduli space of ALG* gravitational instantons with fixed model at infinity is

Chen and Viaclovsky (2024)

Appendix A
Elliptic analysis for ALH/ALH*-gravitational instantons

Almost all elliptic analysis needed for this paper is already done in
Salm (2025)
. However, the choice of weight function in that paper is not suitable for ALH* manifolds. Namely, for the ALH/ALH* case he considered weighted analysis for
exponentially decaying
functions. As a helpful reviewer pointed out, the non-linear term in Proposition
3.17
may also contain
polynomially decaying
terms.

In this appendix, we set up a weighted norm for
M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n}
, so that we can study polynomially decaying functions. We show that in this new case, the elliptic estimates for
\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}
are also uniformly bounded in
\epsilon
. We also show that
\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}
is Fredholm under this new norm, and that

\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{k+2,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{B,n})\oplus\mathbb{R}\phi\to C^{k,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{B,n})

is an isomorphism.

Although we will set up a new weighted norm, most of the calculations are already done in
Salm (2025)
. Namely, the local weighted analysis of ALH/ALH* gravitational instantons shares many similarities with the local weighted analysis for ALG/ALG* gravitational instantons. Therefore, this appendix provides a brief explanation of the modifications needed to the arguments in
Salm (2025)
.

A.1
Weighted analysis

In this appendix we consider the setup given in Section

\Omega
and

\Omega:=r^{-1}h_{\epsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho:=\log r.

(17)

Let

Next, consider the conformally rescaled norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})}=

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{{L}^{2}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})}=

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n}}\left|e^{-\delta\rho}u\right|^{2}\widetilde{\operatorname{Vol}},

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{W^{k,2}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\int_{M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n}}\left|\nabla^{j}_{g_{cf}}(e^{-\delta\rho}u)\right|^{2}_{g_{cf}}\widetilde{\operatorname{Vol}}.

These norms are almost the same as in
Salm (2025)

In this section, we show that there are weighted estimates that are uniformly bounded in
\epsilon

Proposition A.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
and let
P
be the asymptotic region of

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{k+2,\alpha}_{\delta}(P)}\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\|\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{\delta}(P^{\prime})}+\|u\|_{C^{0}_{\delta}(P^{\prime})}\right],\text{or}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{k+2,2}_{\delta}(P)}\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\|\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}u\|_{W^{k,2}_{\delta}(P^{\prime})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\delta}(P^{\prime})}\right]\text{respectively.}

Proof.

We follow the proofs of Theorem 1.4 and Theorem 2.13 in
Salm (2025)
. These proofs consist of the following steps:

1.

Show that the universal cover
\hat{P}
of
P
has bounded geometry and that the bounds of this geometry are uniform with respect to
\epsilon
. That is, show that (the derivatives of) the curvature are uniformly bounded in
\epsilon
and show that the injectivity radius is uniformly bounded below.

2.

Show that
\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{k+2,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{B,n})\oplus\mathbb{R}\phi\to C^{k,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{B,n})
is a uniformly elliptic operator on
\hat{P}
.

3.

Relate the elliptic estimates on
\hat{P}
to estimates on
P
.

To show that the curvature tensor of
\hat{P}
is uniformly bounded in all its derivatives, one can use the Koszul formula on the orthonormal co-frame

\{\operatorname{d}\rho,e^{-\rho}\operatorname{d}\theta,e^{-\rho}\operatorname{d}\phi,\frac{\epsilon}{e^{\rho}h_{\epsilon}}\eta\}.

In practice, we only need to take the exterior derivative of this co-frame and the only non-zero terms are

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}(e^{-\rho}\operatorname{d}\theta)=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{d}\rho\wedge e^{-\rho}\operatorname{d}\theta,\quad\operatorname{d}(e^{-\rho}\operatorname{d}\phi)=-\operatorname{d}\rho\wedge e^{-\rho}\operatorname{d}\phi,\text{ and }

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{e^{\rho}h_{\epsilon}}\eta\right)=

\displaystyle-(\operatorname{d}\rho+\operatorname{d}\log h_{\epsilon})\wedge\frac{\epsilon}{e^{\rho}h_{\epsilon}}\eta+*^{cf}(\operatorname{d}\log h_{\epsilon}\wedge\frac{\epsilon}{e^{\rho}h_{\epsilon}}\eta).

As in the ALG/ALG* case, the derivatives of
\operatorname{d}\log h_{\epsilon}
provide bounds on (the derivatives of) the curvature tensor. The former are uniformly bounded, because Lemma
2.6
implies
h_{\epsilon}=1+\epsilon A+\epsilon Be^{\rho}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-\rho})
for some
A,B>0
. Thus,

\operatorname{d}\log h_{\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon Be^{\rho}}{h_{\epsilon}}\operatorname{d}\rho+\mathcal{O}(e^{-\rho})=\left(1-\frac{1+\epsilon A}{h_{\epsilon}}\right)\operatorname{d}\rho+\mathcal{O}(e^{-\rho}).

Next we show that
\hat{P}
has a uniform lower bound on the injectivity radius. As in
Salm (2025)
we use
Cheeger et al. (1982)
, which states that it is sufficient to show that a geodesic ball has quartic volume growth.
Hence, fix
R>0
and consider the ‘rectangular’ neighbourhood

\operatorname{Rect}_{R}(\rho_{0}):=\left\{(\rho,\theta,\phi,t)\in\hat{P}\colon|\rho-\rho_{0}|<R,|\theta|<e^{\rho}R,|\phi|<e^{\rho}R,|t|<\frac{e^{\rho}h_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}R\right\}.

This rectangle has a circumscribed ball and an inscribed ball, whose radii do not depend on
\epsilon
.
Therefore, it is sufficient to determine the volume growth of
\operatorname{Rect}_{R}(\rho_{0})
, which is
(2R)^{4}
by construction.

Having bounded geometry on
\hat{P}
, we can now study the operator
\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{k+2,\alpha}_{\delta}(\hat{P})\to C^{k,\alpha}_{\delta}(\hat{P})
. As explained in
Salm (2025)
, it is equivalent to studying
L_{\delta}\colon C^{k+2,\alpha}_{cf}(\hat{P})\to C^{k,\alpha}_{cf}(\hat{P})

L_{\delta}:=e^{-\delta\rho}\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}(e^{\delta\rho}\ldots).

Expanding this explicitly, one gets

L_{\delta}u=\Delta_{g_{cf}}u+2\langle\operatorname{d}\log\Omega-\delta\operatorname{d}\rho,\operatorname{d}u\rangle_{g_{cf}}+u\left(-\delta^{2}-\delta\nabla^{*}_{cf}\operatorname{d}\rho+2\delta\langle\operatorname{d}\log\Omega,\operatorname{d}\rho\rangle_{g_{cf}}\right).

Therefore,
L_{\delta}
is uniformly elliptic with respect to
\epsilon
if and only if
\operatorname{d}\log\Omega
is uniformly bounded. This is indeed true by the definition of
\Omega
.

With all these results we can consider the elliptic estimates for
\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}
. According to Theorem 1.2 in
Hebey (1999)
, there exists an
R_{h}>0
, independent of
\epsilon
, such that on any ball of radius

in
\hat{P}
there are so-called harmonic coordinates. In these coordinates

is
C^{k,\alpha}
-equivalent to the flat metric. Hence, the standard Schauder estimate implies that for all
\rho_{0}>0

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{k+2,\alpha}_{\delta}(\operatorname{Rect}_{\frac{R_{h}}{2}}(\rho_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\|\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{\delta}(\operatorname{Rect}_{R_{h}}(\rho_{0}))}+\|u\|_{C^{0}_{\delta}(\operatorname{Rect}_{R_{h}}(\rho_{0}))}\right],

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{k+2,2}_{\delta}(\operatorname{Rect}_{\frac{R_{h}}{2}}(\rho_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\|\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}u\|_{W^{k,2}_{\delta}(\operatorname{Rect}_{R_{h}}(\rho_{0}))}+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\delta}(\operatorname{Rect}_{R_{h}}(\rho_{0}))}\right],

for some uniform

.
Finally, notice that

contains approximately
\frac{e^{3\rho_{0}}R_{h}^{3}}{\epsilon}

∎

, one can see that the formal adjoint of
L_{\delta}
is equal to
L_{1-\delta}
. Hence, using the maximum principle we can conclude

Lemma A.3
.

When
\delta<0
, then

\displaystyle\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to C^{0,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n}),

\displaystyle\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon W^{2,2}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to L^{2}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})

are injective.
When
\delta>1
, then

\displaystyle\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to C^{0,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n}),

\displaystyle\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon W^{2,2}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to L^{2}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})

are surjective.

Like in
Salm (2025)
, there is no
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to C^{0,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})
is an isomorphism. However, we know how the kernel and co-kernel behaves for small values of
\delta
when we consider exponentially decaying weight functions. With this extra information we can make
\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}
an isomorphism in this new norm.

Theorem A.4
.

Let
\delta\in(-1,0)
, and let
\phi
be a non-negative function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
, such that

\phi=r
on
P
, then

\displaystyle\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\oplus\mathbb{R}\phi\to C^{0,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
C^{k,\alpha}_{\delta,\exp}
be the originally weighted spaces in
Salm (2025)
. We know that for
0<-\delta\ll 1
,

\displaystyle\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta,\exp}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\oplus\mathbb{R}\phi\to C^{0,\alpha}_{\delta,\exp}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n}),

is an isomorphism.
Hence,

\displaystyle\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta,\exp}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to C^{0,\alpha}_{\delta,\exp}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n}),

is injective and has a 1-dimensional co-kernel.
The formal adjoint of
\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta,\exp}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to C^{0,\alpha}_{\delta,\exp}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})
is
\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{-\delta,\exp}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to C^{0,\alpha}_{-\delta,\exp}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})
. Therefore, for
0<\delta\ll 1
, the operator

\displaystyle\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta,\exp}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to C^{0,\alpha}_{\delta,\exp}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n}),

is surjective and its kernel only consists of the constant functions.

With this in mind, fix
\delta\in(-1,0)
and
0<\delta^{\prime}\ll 1
and consider the formal adjoint of

\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to C^{0,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n}).

This formal adjoint is

\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{1-\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to C^{0,\alpha}_{1-\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n}).

Hence, if
u
lies in the kernel of this formal adjoint, then
u
is polynomially growing. Therefore,
u\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta^{\prime},\exp}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})
and so
u
is constant. We conclude that the operator

\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\to C^{0,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})

has no kernel and has a 1-dimensional co-kernel.

Finally, we consider the extension

\Omega^{-2}\Delta^{g}\colon C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})\oplus\mathbb{R}\phi\to C^{0,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n}).

Either the dimension of the kernel increases by one, or the dimension of the co-kernel decreases by one.
If the dimension of the kernel increases by one, then there exists a
u\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta}(M_{\mathbb{R}\times T^{2},n})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\Delta(u+\lambda\phi)=0.

u+\phi
is a polynomially growing harmonic function, and so
u+\phi\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\delta^{\prime},\exp}
. By the previous discussion, there exists a
c>0
such that

u+\lambda\phi=c.

Due to the growth rate of
u
and
\phi
, this is a contradiction.
∎

Vanishing Theorem for Hodge Ideals on Smooth Hypersurfaces
Anh Duc Vo

Abstract
. We use a Koszul-type resolution to prove a weak version of Bott’s vanishing theorem for smooth hypersurfaces in ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} and use this result to prove a vanishing theorem for Hodge ideals associated to an effective Cartier divisor on a hypersurface. This extends an earlier result of Mustaţă and Popa.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth complex variety, and
D
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. Recently, Mustaţă and Popa introduced the notion of Hodge ideals
I_{k}(D)
in a series of papers
[
MP19
]
,
[
MP19a
]
and
[
MP20
]
. These ideals are generalizations of multiplier ideals; in fact, one can show that
I_{0}(D)=\mathcal{J}((1-\epsilon)D)
, the multiplier ideal of
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
(1-\epsilon)D
, for any
0<\epsilon\ll 1
. They were introduced with the goal of understanding the singularities and Hodge theory of divisors on smooth varieties; for example,
[
MP19
, Corollary H]
gives the bound for the degree of hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
on which a set of isolated singular points of a divisor imposes independent conditions.

Multiplier ideals satisfy the celebrated Nadel vanishing theorem; see
[
Laz04a
, Theorem 9.4.8]
. In their paper
[
MP19
, Theorem F]
, Mustaţă and Popa obtained an analogous result for Hodge ideals, but required some technical assumptions. When
X
is a projective space or an abelian variety, they also pointed out that these assumptions are in fact not necessary
[
MP19
, Theorem 25.3 and 28.2]
. In this paper, we improve their vanishing result when
X
is a smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. We note that a stronger result than the general statement has also been proven by Dutta in the case of a toric variety
[
Dut20
, Theorem A]
.

To formulate the problem, let
X
be a smooth hypersurface of degree
d\geq 2
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with
n\geq 4
, and
D
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. A special property of projective spaces and toric varieties, which allows us to understand the cohomology of Hodge ideals, is that there exists an Euler-type exact sequence. This exact sequence can be used to construct a Koszul-type resolution of sheaves of differential forms
\Omega^{p}_{X}
. Hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
inherit the Euler sequence via restriction; however, these do not give a resolution of
\Omega^{p}_{X}
, but
\Omega^{p}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}|_{X}
. This makes it challenging to obtain a nice set of conditions for the vanishing theorem; see Section
7
. Fortunately, if we require the divisor
D
to have at worst isolated singularities, i.e. the support of
D
has at worst isolated singularities, we obtain the following theorems in Sections
4
and
5
. Notice that if
n\geq 4
, any line bundle on
X
is the restriction of a line bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
due to the Grothendieck-Lefschetz theorem
[
Har70
, Corollary 3.3]
, so we consider the cases
n\geq 4
and
n=3
separately.

.

,

.

(1)

Since

for

for

(2)

The case

[

As an application, by utilizing the above vanishing theorems, we investigate the classical problem of finding a bound for the degree of hypersurfaces in

on which a set of points imposes independent conditions. This extends a result of Mustaţă and Popa,
[

Theorem C
.

Let

be a complete intersection of a smooth irreducible hypersurface

of degree

and another hypersurface (not necessarily smooth) of degree

, with

. Assume furthermore that

(1)

For each

The vanishing for Hodge ideals in Theorem

. However, the singularities of curves are well-understood with the help of multiplier ideals and adjoint ideals; hence, we will consider only the case

The main ingredient in the proofs of the above results is what we call ”borderline Nakano vanishing”. Recall the Nakano vanishing theorem
[

which says for a smooth complex variety

if

. In section

, we will prove some borderline Nakano vanishings for hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. If

, we obtain

.

Suppose

is a smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degree

with

. Then

(1)

If

, we have

unless

•

and

,

•

i=0
and

, or

•

and

(2)

If

, then

•

For

, we have

if

•

•

When

Remark 2

In section

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Operations on exact sequences

We first discuss the general theory behind the construction of a Koszul-type resolution that will be used throughout the proof of our main results. In what follows, by a
variety
we mean a reduced separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. Consider an exact sequence of vector bundles on a variety
X
:

0\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0.

If
C
is a line bundle, it is well-known that we can form the following short exact sequence for any positive integer
p
:

(2.1)

0\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p}A\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p}B\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p-1}A\otimes C\rightarrow 0.

Dually, if
A
is a line bundle, we get instead:

(2.2)

0\rightarrow A\otimes\bigwedge^{p-1}C\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p}B\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p}C\rightarrow 0.

When
X
is a smooth hypersurface of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, we apply the above operations to the restriction of the Euler sequence on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
to
X

0\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}|_{X}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{n+1}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow 0

to obtain a resolution for
\Omega^{p}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}|_{X}
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth hypersurface of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, then
\Omega^{p}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}|_{X}
admits the following Koszul-type resolution:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(-n-1)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{n+1}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-n)\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\binom{n+1}{p+1}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-p-1)\rightarrow\Omega^{p}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}|_{X}\rightarrow 0.

which is the restriction of the familiar resolution of
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
to
X
.

2.2.
Hodge ideals

In this section, we briefly recall the definition and some properties of Hodge ideals. Let
X
be a smooth complex variety of dimension
n
. We denote by
\mathscr{D}_{X}
the sheaf of differential operators on
X
. A filtered left
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module
(\mathcal{M},F)
is a left
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{M}
equipped with an increasing filtration
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}
by coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules, bounded below and satisfying

F_{k}\mathscr{D}_{X}.F_{l}\mathcal{M}\subseteq F_{k+l}\mathcal{M},

where
F_{k}\mathscr{D}_{X}
is the sheaf of differential operators of order
\leq k
. To a filtered left
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module
(\mathcal{M},F)
, one can associate the de Rham complex

\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{M},F)=[0\rightarrow\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{M}]

with the filtration

F_{k}\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{M},F)=[0\rightarrow F_{k}\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes F_{k+1}\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\omega_{X}\otimes F_{k+n}\mathcal{M}]

placed in degree
-n,\dots,0
. The associated graded complex is

\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{k}\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{M},F)=[0\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{k}\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{k+1}\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\omega_{X}\otimes\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{k+n}\mathcal{M}].

There is a standard correspondence between filtered left and right
\mathscr{D}
-modules given by

\mathcal{M}\mapsto\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{X}=\mathcal{M}^{r}

with the corresponding filtration
F_{p-n}(\mathcal{M}^{r})\cong F_{p}\mathcal{M}\otimes\omega_{X}
. The associated de Rham complex can be written in terms of the filtered right
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module
(\mathcal{M}^{r},F)
as

\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{k}\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{M}^{r},F)=[0\rightarrow\operatorname{\bigwedge}^{n}T_{X}\otimes{gr}^{F}_{k-n}\mathcal{M}^{r}\rightarrow\bigwedge^{n-1}T_{X}\otimes\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{k-n+1}\mathcal{M}^{r}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{k}\mathcal{M}^{r}].

For a reduced effective divisor
D
on
X
, the
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D)\coloneqq\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(kD)
underlies the mixed Hodge module
j_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{U}^{H}[n]

[
Sai90
, Proposition 2.11]
, where
j:U\coloneqq X\setminus D\hookrightarrow X
, and hence is equipped with the Hodge filtration
F
. Moreover,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D)
has the natural pole order filtration on it. Mustaţă and Popa
[
MP19
]
introduce the notion of Hodge ideals
I_{k}(D)
to measure the discrepancy between these two filtrations
F_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D))=I_{k}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}((k+1)D)
. In
[
MP19a
]
and
[
MP20
]
, the authors extend the definition of Hodge ideals to an arbitrary effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
X
. Locally, we can assume that
D=\alpha\cdot\text{div}(h)
for some nonzero regular function
h
and some positive rational number
\alpha
. Denote by
Z
the support of
D
. Consider the left
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*Z)h^{-\alpha}
, the rank
1
free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*Z)
-module with generator the symbol
h^{-\alpha}
, on which a derivation
D
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
acts by

D(wh^{-\alpha})=\big(D(w)-\alpha w\dfrac{D(h)}{h}\big)h^{-\alpha}.

Although
O_{X}(*Z)h^{-\alpha}
does not necessarily underlie a mixed Hodge module, by
[
MP19a
, Lemma 2.6]
, it is a direct summand of a filtered
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module underlying a mixed Hodge module. This induces a canonical Hodge filtration on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*Z)h^{-\alpha}
, which makes it a filtered
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module. The
Hodge ideals

I_{k}(D)
are then defined as

F_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(*Z)h^{-\alpha})=I_{k}(D)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(kZ)h^{-\alpha}.

This definition is, in fact, independent of the choice of
h
and
\alpha
, and hence the Hodge ideals can be defined globally on
X
.

The Hodge ideals play an important role in the study of the singularities of the divisor
D
. For example, it is not difficult to see that
I_{0}(X)=\mathcal{J}((1-\epsilon)D)
, the multiplier ideal associated to the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
(1-\epsilon)D
for any
0<\epsilon\ll 1

[
MP19a
, Proposition 9.1]
. From the properties of multiplier ideals
[
Laz04a
, Definition 9.3.9]
, the pair
(X,D)
is log canonical if and only if
I_{0}(D)
is trivial. If we go one step further, in the case
D
is integral, it can be shown that the Hodge ideal
I_{1}(D)
is contained in the adjoint ideal
\operatorname{adj}(D)
, see
[
MP19
, Theorem C]
. Hence, by
[
Laz04a
, Theorem 9.3.48]
,
D
is normal and has at worst rational singularities if
I_{1}(D)
is trivial. Furthermore, Mustaţă and Popa prove that if
D
is of the form
D=\alpha Z
, where
Z
is a reduced divisor and
0<\alpha\leq 1
, then
Z
is smooth if and only if all Hodge ideals
I_{k}(D)
are trivial
[
MP19a
, Corollary 11.12]
. Thus, the Hodge ideals of
D=\alpha Z
are cosupported inside the singular locus of
Z
where the cosupport of an ideal
I
is the support of
\mathcal{O}_{X}/I
. We denote by
Z_{k}
the closed subscheme of
X
defined by the ideal sheaf
I_{k}(D)
.

We are interested in a vanishing theorem for Hodge ideals. A combination of
[
MP19
, Theorem F]
(in the case
D
is a reduced divisor),
[
MP19a
, Theorem 12.1]
and
[
Che23
, Theorem 1.1]
gives the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
,
D
an effective divisor, and
L
a line bundle on
X
such that
L(-D)
is ample. For some
k\geq 1
, assume that the pair
(X,D)
is reduced
(k-1)
-log canonical i.e
I_{j}(D)=\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z-\lceil D\rceil)
for all
0\leq j\leq k-1
. Then we have:

(1)

If
k\leq n
, and
L(pZ-\lceil D\rceil)
is ample for all
1\leq p\leq k
, then

H^{i}\big(X,\omega_{X}(kZ)\otimes I_{k}(D)\otimes L\big)=0

for all
i\geq 2
. Moreover,

H^{1}\big(X,\omega_{X}(kZ)\otimes I_{k}(D)\otimes L\big)=0

holds if
H^{j}\big(X,\Omega^{n-j}\otimes L((k-j+1)Z-\lceil D\rceil)\big)=0
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.

(2)

If
k\geq n+1
, then
Z
is smooth, and so
I_{k}(D)=\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z-\lceil D\rceil)
. In this case, if
L(kZ-\lceil D\rceil)
is ample, then

H^{i}\big(X,\omega_{X}(kZ)\otimes I_{k}(D)\otimes L\big)=0

for all
i\geq 1

(3)

If
D
is ample, then
(1)
and
(2)
also hold with
L(-D)
nef.

Unlike the Hodge ideals, the module
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*Z)h^{-\alpha}
cannot be defined globally. To guarantee the existence of a global filtered
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module, which is locally isomorphic to
(\mathcal{O}_{X}(*Z)h^{-\alpha},F)
, we need the extra assumption that there exists a line bundle
M
such that
M^{\otimes l}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(lD)
as integral divisors. In
[
Che23
]
, Chen shows that we can remove this global assumption by constructing a global sheaf

\mathcal{M}_{k}(D)\coloneqq\dfrac{\omega_{X}(kZ)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}I_{k}(D)}{\omega_{X}((k-1)Z)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}I_{k-1}(D)},

which is locally isomorphic to
\omega_{X}\otimes gr^{F}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(*Z)h^{-\alpha})
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. The
k
-th Spencer complex of
\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}(D)

Sp_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}(D))\coloneqq[\mathcal{M}_{k}(D)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\bigwedge^{n}T_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{k+1}(D)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\bigwedge^{n-1}T_{X}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{k+n}(D)]

placed in degree
-n,\dots,0
is locally isomorphic to
gr^{F}_{k}DR(\mathcal{O}_{X}(*Z)h^{-\alpha})
. This complex enjoys a similar vanishing theorem as Saito’s vanishing theorem for mixed Hodge modules:

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
Che23
, Theorem 1.2]
)
Let
X
be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension
n
,
D
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
, and
L
a line bundle on
X
. Then we have:

(1)

If
L-D
is ample, then

\mathbb{H}^{i}\big(X,Sp_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}(D))\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}L\big)=0

for any
i>0
and any integer
k
.

(2)

If
D
is ample, then
(1)
also holds for
L-D
nef.

This theorem plays a crucial role in the proof of the vanishing theorem for Hodge ideals of
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors.

3.
Borderline Nakano vanishing for hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}

Because of the well-known Bott vanishing for projective spaces which says that the cohomology groups
H^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega^{p}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(l))=0
unless

•

i=p
and
l=0
,

•

i=0
and
l>p
, or

•

i=n
and
l<p-n
,

see for example
[
CMP17
, Theorem 7.2.3]
,
we only consider hypersurfaces of degree at least
2
. In this section,
X
will denote a smooth hypersurface of degree
d\geq 2
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and
L
a line bundle on
X
unless otherwise noted.

3.1.
The case
n\geq 4

First, we start with a vanishing theorem for the sheaves
\Omega^{p}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}|_{X}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degree
d\geq 2
with
n\geq 4
. We have

H^{i}(X,\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p}|_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l))=0

unless

•

i=p
and
l=0
, or

•

i=p-1
and
l=d
, or

•

i=0
and
l>p
, or

•

i=n-1
and
l<d+p-n
.

Proof.

Consider the following short exact sequence:

Lemma 3.2

(Macaulay’s theorem)
.

(
[
CMP17
, Theorem 7.4.1]
)

Suppose there is an ordered
(n+1)
-tuple of homogeneous polynomials
F_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
of degree
d_{i}
that form a regular sequence. Let
R=\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]/(F_{0},\dots,F_{n})
be the quotient of the polynomial ring by the ideal generated by
\{F_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
. Then:

(1)

R_{k}=0
if
k>\rho=\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_{i}-n-1
and
R_{\rho}\cong\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

The multiplication in the polynomial ring induces a perfect pairing
R_{k}\otimes R_{\rho-k}\rightarrow R_{\rho}
.

We use the well-known fact that the partial derivatives of a homogeneous polynomial giving a smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
form a regular sequence in
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
.

-divisors with isolated singularities

In this section, we prove Theorem
A
. Notice that in the case
d=1
, i.e
X=\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, the condition in part
(3)
is simply
l\geq m-n-1
(this condition will appear in the proof), in which case the assertion is a result due to Mustaţă and Popa, see
[
MP19a
, Variant 12.5]
and
[
Che23
, Variant 4.5]
. Thus in this section, we only consider the case
d\geq 2
.

By Remark
1
, the vanishing of
I_{0}(D)
is simply a consequence of the Nadel vanishing theorem:

H^{1}\big(X,\omega_{X}(D)\otimes I_{0}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l)\big)=0

if
l+\epsilon m>0
for any
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, which is equivalent to
l\geq 0
.

Moreover, if
I_{k}(D)=\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z-\lceil D\rceil)\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-a)
, then our vanishing result is simply a consequence of the Kodaira vanishing theorem

H^{i}\big(X,\omega_{X}(kZ)\otimes I_{k}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l)\big)\cong H^{i}\big(X,\omega_{X}(kZ)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l-a)\big)=0

for all
l
if
1\leq i\neq n-1
and for
(l-a)H+kZ
ample if
i=n-1
. Hence, the theorem is trivial in this case. Thus we may assume that
I_{k}(D)\subsetneq\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z-\lceil D\rceil)
. Recall that the ideals
I_{k}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lceil D\rceil-Z)
are cosupported on the singular locus of
D
, then
\dim(Z_{k})=0
, and hence all higher cohomologies of quasi-coherent sheaves on
Z_{k}
vanish. This argument leads to the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Under the same assumptions in Theorem
A
, we have

H^{i}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{j}\otimes I_{k}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l)\big)=0

for all
i\geq 2
,
i+j\geq n
and
l>a
.

Proof.

This is an immediate application of the Nakano vanishing theorem and the above argument to the exact sequence

\dots\rightarrow H^{i-1}\big(Z_{k},\Omega_{X}^{j}(Z-\lceil D\rceil)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l)|_{Z_{k}}\big)\rightarrow H^{i}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{j}\otimes I_{k}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l)\big)\rightarrow H^{i}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{j}(Z-\lceil D\rceil)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l)\big)\rightarrow\dots

∎

Proof of Theorem
A
.

Parts
(1)
and
(2)
: The term
H^{i}\big(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(kZ)\otimes I_{k}(D)\otimes O_{X}(l)\big)
sits in the following exact sequence

\dots\rightarrow H^{i-1}\big(Z_{k},\mathcal{O}_{X}((k+1)Z-\lceil D\rceil)\otimes O_{X}(l)|_{Z_{k}}\big)\rightarrow H^{i}\big(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(kZ)\otimes I_{k}(D)\otimes O_{X}(l)\big)\rightarrow

\rightarrow H^{i}\big(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}((k+1)Z-\lceil D\rceil)\otimes O_{X}(l)\big)\rightarrow\dots

The first term vanishes since
i-1>\dim(Z_{k})
, while the last term

H^{i}\big(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}((k+1)Z-\lceil D\rceil)\otimes O_{X}(l)\big)

vanishes for all

, and for
(l-a+n+1-d)H+kZ

Part

: Let

H^{1}\big(X,\omega_{X}(kZ)\otimes I_{k}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\big)

0\rightarrow\omega_{X}((k-1)Z)\otimes I_{k-1}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\rightarrow\omega_{X}(kZ)\otimes I_{k}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{k}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\rightarrow 0.

Suppose the theorem holds for

H^{1}\big(X,\mathcal{M}_{k}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\big)

for

. Consider the complex
C^{\bullet}=Sp_{k-n+1}(\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}(D))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l}))[-k]
:

[\mathcal{M}_{0}(D)\otimes\bigwedge^{k}T_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{1}(D)\otimes\bigwedge^{k-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{k}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})]

concentrated in degrees
0
to
k
, with the associated spectral sequence of

:

E_{1}^{p,q}=H^{q}(X,C^{p})\Rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{p+q}(X,C^{\bullet}).

We are interested in the vanishing of
E_{1}^{k,1}
. Note that we have the vanishing of
E_{1}^{k+1,j}=E_{1}^{-1,j}=0
for all

. Moreover, Theorem

for

and
\tilde{l}\geq m

, or more precisely,
E_{r}^{k,1}=E_{r+1}^{k,1}
for all

.

Observe that
E_{r}^{k,1}=E_{r+1}^{k,1}
for all

C^{\bullet}
has length
k+1
, and
E_{r}^{k+r,2-r}=0
because
C^{k+r}=0
for all
r\geq 1
. Thus it suffices to show that
E_{1}^{k-r,r}=0
for
1\leq r\leq k
and
\tilde{l}\geq\max\{k(d-2)+m,k(d-2)+(n-2)(d-1)+a\}
. Now, the term
E_{1}^{k-r,r}=H^{r}\big(X,\mathcal{M}_{k-r}\otimes\bigwedge^{r}T_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\big)
sits in the exact sequence

\dots\rightarrow H^{r}\big(X,\Omega^{n-1-r}_{X}((k-r)Z)\otimes I_{k-r}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\big)\rightarrow E_{1}^{k-r,r}\rightarrow

\rightarrow H^{1+r}\big(X,\Omega^{n-1-r}_{X}((k-r-1)Z)\otimes I_{k-r-1}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\big)\rightarrow\dots
,

in which the last term vanishes if

is ample due to Lemma
4.1
(note that the last term is

if

). On the other hand, the first term sits in the exact sequence

\dots\rightarrow H^{r-1}\big(Z_{k-r},\Omega^{n-1-r}_{X}((k-r)Z)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l}-a)|_{Z_{k-r}}\big)\rightarrow

\rightarrow H^{r}\big(X,\Omega^{n-1-r}_{X}((k-r)Z)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\otimes I_{k-r}(D)\big)\rightarrow

\rightarrow H^{r}\big(X,\Omega^{n-1-r}_{X}((k-r)Z)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l}-a)\big)\rightarrow\dots

By Theorem

, the last term vanishes if

is nef which holds under our assumption. If

, and we are done. Thus we are left with the case

. By comparing to the corresponding exact sequence with
\Omega^{n-2}_{X}
replaced by
\Omega^{n-2}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}|_{X}
, we get the commutative diagram:

H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{n-2}((k-1)Z)

\otimes

\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l}-a)\big)

H^{0}\big(Z_{0},\Omega_{X}^{n-2}((k-1)Z)

\otimes

\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l}-a)|_{Z_{0}}\big)

H^{1}\big(X,\Omega^{n-2}_{X}((k-1)Z)

\otimes

\mathcal{O}_{X}(\tilde{l})\otimes I_{k-1}(D)\big)

The vanishing theorem for Hodge ideals on hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{3}

In this case, the divisor

has dimension

and hence automatically has at worst isolated singularities.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

(1)
For

, the result follows from the Nakano vanishing theorem as in Lemma

.

(2)
For

, we will again argue by induction on

6.
Applications

It is well known that the set of singular points of a nodal irreducible plane curve of degree
m
imposes independent conditions on the linear system of plane curves of degree
m-3
. In the paper
[
Sev46
]
, Severi proved a similar result in dimension
2
, stating that the set of singular points of nodal hypersurfaces of degree
m
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
imposes independent conditions on hypersurfaces of degree at least
2m-5
. Later, Park and Woo generalized Severi’s result to all singular points, and gave similar bounds for isolated singular points on hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, see
[
PW06
]
. By using the Hodge ideals, Mustaţă and Popa gave a different bound for this problem. In this section, we exploit Theorem
A
to obtain a new result for isolated singular points on the complete intersection of two hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Proof of Theorem
C
.

The triviality of the Hodge ideals
I_{k}(D)
is equivalent to the scheme
Z_{k}
being empty (due to
[
MP19
, Theorem A]
for the reduced divisor
D
), hence there is nothing to prove. In the case
Z_{k}
is non-empty, denote by
A
the line bundle

\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}((k+1)D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l)=\mathcal{O}_{X}((k+1)m+l+d-n-1).

Consider the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow A\otimes I_{k}(D)\rightarrow A\rightarrow A\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z_{k}}\rightarrow 0.

For
(1)
, Theorem
A
implies that

H^{1}(X,A\otimes I_{k}(D))=0.

Using the associated long exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{0}(X,A\otimes I_{k}(D))\rightarrow H^{0}(X,A)\rightarrow H^{0}(Z_{k},A\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z_{k}})\rightarrow H^{1}(X,A\otimes I_{k}(D))\rightarrow\dots,

we see that
\text{length}(Z_{k})
is bounded by the dimension of
H^{0}(X,A)
which is exactly

\binom{(k+1)m+l+d-1}{n}-\binom{(k+1)m+l-1}{n}.

For
(2)
, we know by
[
MP19
, Corollary 21.3]
that

I_{k}(D)\subseteq I_{S_{t}},

where
k=\lceil\frac{n}{t}-1\rceil
. Since the restriction map

H^{0}\big(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}((k+1)m+l+d-n-1)\big)\twoheadrightarrow H^{0}(X,A)

is surjective, the result follows immediately from part
(1)
which says that there is a surjection

H^{0}(X,A)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z_{k}}\rightarrow 0.

∎

We can extend this analysis to the study of
(j-1)
-jets along
S_{t}
, just as it was done in
[
MP19
, Corollary 27.3]
. Recall that for a
0
-dimensional subset
S
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, the space of hypersurfaces of degree
a
is said to separate
(j-1)
-jets along
S
if the restriction map

H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(a))\rightarrow\bigoplus_{x\in S}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{j}

is surjective. In particular,
S
imposes independent conditions on hypersurfaces of degree
a
if they separate
0
-jets along
S
. For
t\geq 3
, we put

k_{t,j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\lceil\frac{n-1-t+j}{t}\rceil&j\leq t-1\\
\lceil\frac{n-1-t+j}{t-2}\rceil&j\geq t\end{cases}.

We prove the following.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
D
be a complete intersection of a smooth irreducible hypersurface
X
of degree
d\geq 2
and another hypersurface (not necessarily smooth) of degree
m
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, with
n\geq 4
. Assume furthermore that
D
has at worst isolated singularities. Let
S_{t}
denote the set of singular points on
D
of multiplicity at least
t\geq 3
, and set
l=\max\{k_{t,j}(d-2),k_{t,j}(d-2)+(n-2)(d-1)-m\}
. Then the space of hypersurfaces of degree at least

(k_{t,j}+1)m+l+d-n-1

separates
(j-1)
-jets along
S_{t}
, for each
j\geq 1
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the proof of Theorem
C
, except that we use both
[
MP19
, Theorem E]
and
[
MP19
, Corollary 19.4]
to get

I_{k_{t,j}}(D)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{j}

for every
x\in S_{t}
.
∎

Remark 4
.

(1)

In the case
d=1
i.e
D
is a hypersurface of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, Theorem
C
specializes to
[
MP19
, Theorem G]
for
z_{k}=0
and
[
MP19
, Corollary H]
, while Proposition
6.1
specializes to
[
MP19
, Corollary 27.2]
.

(2)

The conditions in Theorem
C
and Proposition
6.1
are not symmetric in
d
and
m
. In fact, the bound tends to increase faster as
m
increases, compared to
d
. This is a limitation of our method; thus in the case when
D
is the intersection of two smooth hypersurfaces, it is much better to consider
D
as a divisor in the smooth hypersurface with higher degree. In order to obtain symmetric bounds, a new method needs to be employed.

Example
.

(1)

Let
d=m=2
and
n=4
, so that
D
is a complete intersection of two quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
. Theorem
C
says that:

•

S_{2}
and
S_{3}
impose independent conditions on hypersurfaces of degree at least
m-1
.

•

If
t=4
, Theorem
C
shows that

0=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1))\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{S_{t}}

which means
D
has no isolated singularities of multiplicity at least
4
.

(2)

In general, if
d=2,m\leq n-2
and
t=n
, we obtain that any reduced effective Cartier divisor on a smooth quadric hypersurface of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
has no isolated singularities of multiplicity at least
n
. This result can also be proven by using the Nadel vanishing theorem.

7.
The vanishing theorem for divisors with arbitrary singularities

The aim of this section is to give a vanishing theorem for Hodge ideals of a divisor in a hypersurface of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, with arbitrary singularities. While the situation is much more complicated technically than in the isolated singularity case, we can at least provide an algorithmic approach. For simplicity, we consider the case when
D
is a reduced effective divisor. The case when
D
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor can be approached in the same manner but is more complicated. We will use the same method as in Section
4
. First, we proceed by induction on
k
. Given the vanishings for
I_{0}(D),\dots,I_{k-1}(D)
i.e the vanishings of
H^{i}\big(X,\omega_{X}(kD)\otimes I_{j}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l)\big)
for
0\leq j\leq k-1
and appropriate
l
obtained from the induction process, it is equivalent to study the vanishing of
\text{gr}^{F}_{k}\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D)
. Next, by using Theorem
2.2
and the associated spectral sequence of the graded de Rham complex

(\text{gr}^{F}_{k+1-n}\text{DR}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l))[-k],

it suffices to prove the vanishing of

E^{k-r,i+r-1}_{1}=H^{i+r-1}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{n-1-r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l)\otimes\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{k-r}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D))\big)

for all
1\leq r\leq k
. However, since we are not imposing any restriction on the singularities of the divisor
D
, we only have

\dim(Z_{k})\leq\dim(D)-1=n-3,

and this will impose more conditions in the inductive steps.

For fixed
n,d,m
, denote by
A_{i,k,l}
the statement

H^{i}\big(X,\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}((k+1)m+l)\otimes I_{k}(D)\big)=0.

By using a similar strategy as in the proof of Theorem
A
, we will prove the following:

Proposition 7.1
.

In the above setting, the following hold:

(1)

For
i\geq 2
,

A_{i,k-1,l}\quad\text{and}\quad A_{i-1,k-r,rd}\quad\text{ for }\quad 1\leq r\leq k

and

A_{j+t-1,k-r,l+(i+r-j)d-i+j-t}\quad\text{ for }\quad 1\leq r\leq k,i+1\leq j\leq i+r,0\leq t\leq j-i

together imply
A_{i,k,l}
.

(2)

For
i=1
,

A_{1,k-1,l+rd}\quad\text{ for }\quad 0\leq r\leq k-1,

A_{j+t,k-r-1,l+(r-j+1)d+j-t-1}\quad\text{ for }\quad 1\leq r\leq k-1,i+1\leq j\leq i+r,0\leq t\leq j-i,

A_{j+t,k-r,l+(r-j-1)d+j-t+1}\quad\text{ for }\quad 1\leq r\leq k,r\leq j\leq n-3,0\leq t\leq j+1,

and

l\geq(n-r)d-n-(k-r+1)m

together imply
A_{i,k,l}
.

Notice that in the above proposition, the conditions that guarantee
A_{i,k,l}
contain the ones that guarantee both
A_{i+1,k,l}
and
A_{i,k-1,l}
. Roughly speaking, we need ”more conditions” as
i
gets smaller and
k
gets larger.

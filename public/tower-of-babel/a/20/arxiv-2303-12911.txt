Low-dimensional Cox-Ingersoll-Ross process
Yuliya Mishura yuliyamishura@knu.ua, Andrey Pilipenko pilipenko.ay@gmail.com, Anton Yurchenko-Tytarenko antony@math.uio.no

Abstract
The present paper investigates Cox-Ingersoll-Ross (CIR) processes of dimension less than 1, with a focus on obtaining an equation of a new type including local times for the square root of the CIR process. We utilize the fact that non-negative diffusion processes can be obtained by the transformation of time and scale of some reflected Brownian motion to derive this equation, which contains a term characterized by the local time of the corresponding reflected Brownian motion. Additionally, we establish a new connection between low-dimensional CIR processes and reflected Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (ROU) processes, providing a new representation of Skorokhod reflection functions.



Keywords:
Cox-Ingersoll-Ross process, reflected Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, Skorokhod problem, local time.

Introduction

1.1
Background and motivation

The squared Bessel process

X(t)=x_{0}+at+2\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{X(s)}dW(s)

(1.1)

as well as its generalization Cox-Ingersoll-Ross (CIR) process

X(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(a-bX(s)\right)ds+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{X(s)}dW(s)

(1.2)

where
x_{0}\geq 0
,
a>0
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
, and their respective square roots are widely used in various fields, in particular physics (see e.g.
[
8
,
19
]
and the overview in
[
17
, Section I]
) and finance
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
18
]
. One of the reasons for the popularity of these processes lies in the well-known fact (see e.g.
[
20
, Chapter IV, Example 8.2]
) that
a>0
in (
1.2
) implies that
X(t)\geq 0
for all
t\geq 0
with probability 1, which is a natural property for multiple real-life phenomena. Furthermore, if the
Feller condition

2a\geq\sigma^{2}
is satisfied, the paths of
X
in (
1.2
) are strictly positive a.s., which turns out to be very useful in multiple cases. For example, the well-known Heston model
[
18
]
utilizes
Y:=\sqrt{X}
as stochastic volatility and, under the Feller condition,

Y
has the dynamics of the form

Y(t)=\sqrt{x_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{a-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}}{Y(s)}-bY(s)\right)ds+\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t),\quad t\geq 0,

(1.3)

since it is evident that

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{Y(s)}ds<\infty

(1.4)

with probability 1 for all
t\geq 0
. This equation can be used for e.g. simulation purposes (see, for example,
[
1
,
15
,
27
]
); moreover, the measure change procedure associated with the Heston model naturally involves the inverse volatility
1/Y
which has far more transparent properties when
X>0
a.s.

At the same time, empirical considerations indicate that the Feller condition
2a\geq\sigma^{2}
can sometimes be too restrictive and models perform better when it is not satisfied. For instance,
[
22
, Section 3.4]
reports that the joint SPX-VIX fit of the Heston model turns out to be substantially better when the Feller condition is not demanded from the model parameters. Additionally,
[
2
, Example 10.2.6]
indicates that the Heston model with violated Feller condition can reproduce the upward VIX “smirk”. In other words, there are cases when the process
Y=\sqrt{X}
under relatively small values of
a
turns out to be more relevant for reflecting real-life phenomena despite the associated analytical challenges. Nevertheless, the majority of sources in the literature pay more attention to the case when the Feller condition is satisfied. Among notable exceptions, we mention
[
4
,
7
,
9
,
10
]
which discussed the SDEs of the type (
1.3
) when
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}<a<\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}
. It is worth to note a more recent paper
[
25
]
which establishes a connection between
Y=\sqrt{X}
and a reflected Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (ROU) process

Y_{0}(t)=\sqrt{x_{0}}-\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y_{0}(s)ds+\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t)+L_{0}(t),

where
L_{0}
is the corresponding Skorokhod reflection function, i.e. a continuous non-decreasing process that has points of growth exclusively when
Y_{0}(t)=0
and such that
Y_{0}(t)\geq 0
. In particular, it is established that
Y_{0}=\sqrt{X}
when
a=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
in (
1.2
). Additionally,
[
25
, Theorem 2.4]
provides a new representation of
L_{0}
in terms of a limit of the CIR processes: with probability 1, for any positive sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n},~n\geq 1\}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
,
n\to\infty
, and for all
T>0

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|L_{0}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\sqrt{X_{\varepsilon_{n}}(s)}}ds\right|\to 0,\quad n\to\infty,

(1.5)

where

X_{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}+\varepsilon_{n}-bX_{\varepsilon_{n}}(s)\right)ds+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{X_{\varepsilon_{n}}(s)}dW(s).

The representation of
L_{0}
from
[
25
]
described above essentially concerns convergence of the CIR square roots as
a\to\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}+
and does not cover what happens when
a\to\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}-
. The reason is that analytic challenges associated to the process
Y=\sqrt{X}
are especially acute when
0<a<\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
, i.e. when the
dimension
(see e.g.
[
23
]
)
k:=\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}
of the process (
1.2
) is less than 1. Indeed, the integral in (
1.4
) is infinite after the first moment of hitting zero, the representation (
1.3
) does not hold and, furthermore, the process
Y=\sqrt{X}

is not a semimartingale
(see e.g. Example 1.2 and Appendix 1 in
[
24
]
or
[
16
, p. 100]
). In this regard, one must mention important contributions
[
5
,
6
]
which shed light on the behavior of
Y=\sqrt{X}
when
X
is the squared Bessel process (
1.1
) of dimension
k=a\in(0,1)
. There, it is shown that
Y
satisfies the equation of the form

Y(t)=\sqrt{x_{0}}+W(t)+L(t),

(1.6)

where

L(t):=\frac{a-1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{a-2}(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0))dy

(1.7)

with
\ell
being a jointly continuous in
(t,y)
normalized local time such that for any bounded measurable function
f

\int_{0}^{t}f(Y(s))ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(y)y^{a-1}\ell(t,y)dy.

(1.8)

1.2
Main results

In our paper, we consider a more general case of the CIR process (
1.2
) with
b\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<a<\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
(we call such a process a
low-dimensional
CIR) and study the properties of
Y=\sqrt{X}
. More precisely, we represent
Y
as a transformation of a reflected Brownian motion
\widetilde{W}
and use the properties of the local time
L^{\widetilde{W}}
of the latter to study the local time
L^{Y}
of
Y
. Afterwards, we use the connection between
L^{\widetilde{W}}
and
L^{Y}
to get a representation in the spirit of (
1.6
): namely, we prove that
Y
is a strong solution of the equation

Y(t)=\sqrt{x_{0}}-\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)ds+\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t)+L(t),

(1.9)

where

L(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}-a\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-2}\left(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right)dy

(1.10)

with
\ell
being an explicitly given normalized transformation of a local time
L^{Y}
of the process
Y
:

\ell(t,y):=y^{1-\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}}L^{Y}(t,y),

where
\ell(t,0):=\lim_{y\to 0+}\ell(t,y)
is defined by continuity.

Finally, we close the gap of
[
25
]
mentioned above and obtain a representation of the Skorokhod reflection function for the ROU process in terms of CIR processes of dimension
k=\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}<1
.

It is worth noting that our approach is simpler than the one in
[
5
,
6
]
and is based on the following machinery: we notice that Itô’s formula applied to
\sqrt{X(t)+\varepsilon}
followed by moving
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
implies that
Y=\sqrt{X}
satisfies the equation of the form (
1.9
) with
L
represented as an a.s.-limit

L(t):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon_{n}}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{X(s)}{(X(s)+\varepsilon_{n})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds

(1.11)

and
\{\varepsilon_{n},~n\geq 0\}
being some sequence converging to zero. After that, we utilize the fact that the CIR process
X
is a regular diffusion and hence can be obtained from some reflected Brownian motion
\widetilde{W}
by a transformation of time and scale (see e.g.
[
29
, Chapter V, Section 7]
). We find the explicit shape of this transformation, use it to establish the connection between the local times of
\widetilde{W}
and
Y
. Finally, we exploit this link to show that the limit (
1.11
) is equal to (
1.10
). The technique described above seems to be more transparent than the one employed in
[
5
,
6
]
and additionally allows to get a clear intuition behind the process
\ell
in (
1.8
).

Structure of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

2
Preliminary calculations

Let
a,\sigma>0
,
b\geq 0
,
W=\{W(t),~t\geq 0\}
be a Brownian motion, and let us consider the continuous modification of a standard CIR process (
1.2
). Note that, by
[
21
, Chapter IV, Example 8.2]
, the paths of
X
are non-negative with probability 1 provided that
a>0
and hence one can define the square-root process
Y=\{Y(t),~t\geq 0\}:=\{\sqrt{X(t)},~t\geq 0\}
. In order to analyze the dynamics of
Y
, take
\varepsilon>0
and observe that, by Itô’s formula,

\displaystyle\sqrt{X(t)+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{x_{0}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{X(s)}{(X(s)+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds

(2.1)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{bX(s)}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}ds+\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{X(s)}}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}dW(s).

Fix an arbitrary
T>0
and note that the left-hand side of (
2.1
) converges to
Y(t)
uniformly on
[0,T]
with probability 1 as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. It is also evident that

\begin{gathered}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{X(s)}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}ds-\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)ds\right|\to 0\quad a.s.,\qquad\varepsilon\downarrow 0.\end{gathered}

(2.2)

Next, by
[
28
, Chapter XI]
,
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X(s)=0\}}ds=0
and hence, by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, for any
T>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{X(s)}}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}dW(s)-W(t)\right|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{\sqrt{X(s)}}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-1\right)^{2}ds

\displaystyle=4\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{\sqrt{X(s)}}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-1\right)^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X(s)>0\}}ds+4\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X(s)=0\}}ds

\displaystyle=4\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{\sqrt{X(s)}}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-1\right)^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X(s)>0\}}ds\to 0,\quad\varepsilon\downarrow 0.

This implies that for each
T>0

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{X(s)}}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon_{n}}}dW(s)-W(t)\right|\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0,\quad\varepsilon\downarrow 0.

(2.3)

In particular, (
2.1
) as well as convergences (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) imply that the left-hand side of

\displaystyle\sqrt{X(t)+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{x_{0}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{bX(s)}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}ds-\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{X(s)}}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}dW(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{X(s)}{(X(s)+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds

converges uniformly on compacts in probability as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Therefore, there exists a ucp-limit

L(t):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{X(s)}{(X(s)+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds

(2.4)

and the process
Y=\sqrt{X}
satisfies the SDE of the form

Y(t)=Y(0)-\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)ds+\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t)+L(t),

where
Y(0)=\sqrt{x_{0}}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Ucp convergence in (
2.4
) implies that for an arbitrary
T>0
there exists a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n},~n\geq 1\}
(depending on
T
) such that, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\begin{gathered}\left|L(t)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon_{n}}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{X(s)}{(X(s)+\varepsilon_{n})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds\right|\to 0\end{gathered}

(2.5)

with probability 1 as
n\to\infty
. Later on, we will see that the a.s. convergence (
2.5
) holds for an
arbitrary
sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n},~n\geq 1\}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, it will be shown that the set of full probability where (
2.5
) holds can be chosen independently of a particular sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n},~n\geq 1\}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that the process
L
defined by (
2.4
) is continuous a.s. since

L(t)=Y(t)-Y(0)+\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)ds-\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t).

3
Stochastic representation of
L
when
{0<a<\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}}

Our strategy for the analysis of
L
will be as follows. Since the CIR process
X
in (
1.2
) is a non-negative regular diffusion, it can be represented (see e.g.
[
29
, Chapter V, Section 7]
) in the form

X(t)=S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}_{\tau_{t}}\right)

for some change of time
\tau
and change of scale
S
of a reflected Brownian motion
\widetilde{W}=\{\widetilde{W}(t),~t\geq 0\}
. Then, we re-write the integral in the limit (
2.4
) in terms of the local time
L^{\widetilde{W}}=L^{\widetilde{W}}(t,x)
of
\widetilde{W}
and exploit Hölder continuity of the latter to find an explicit representation of
L
in terms of
L^{\widetilde{W}}
.

3.1
CIR process as the transformation of a reflected Brownian motion

In order to implement our approach, we first need to represent the CIR process as a transformation of a reflected Brownian motion. For a given set of parameters
a
,
b
,
\sigma
of the SDE (
1.2
), define a
scale function

S
:
[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
by

S(x):=\int_{0}^{x}y^{-\frac{2a}{\sigma^{2}}}e^{\frac{2by}{\sigma^{2}}}dy

(3.1)

and observe that, since
S
is strictly increasing and
S(\infty)=\infty
, there exists its inverse
S^{-1}
. Define also a
speed measure

m(dx)=\rho(x)dx,

where

\rho(x):=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}x^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-1}e^{-\frac{4b}{\sigma^{2}}x}\mathbbm{1}_{x>0}.

(3.2)

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be the unique strong solution to the CIR equation (
1.2
) with
0<a<\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
. Then there exists a reflected Brownian motion
\widetilde{W}
starting at
S(x_{0})
such that

X(t):=S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(\tau_{t})\right),

(3.3)

where

\tau_{t}:=\varphi^{-1}_{t}

(3.4)

with

\varphi_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\rho\left(S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(s)\right)\right)ds.

(3.5)

Before moving to the proof of Proposition
3.1
, let us make some remarks regarding its formulation.

Remark 3.2
.

1.

The process
\varphi
in (
3.5
) is well-defined. Indeed, let
L^{\widetilde{W}}=\{L^{\widetilde{W}}(t,x),~t\geq 0,~x\geq 0\}
be the local time of
\widetilde{W}
, i.e. for any bounded measurable
f
,

\int_{0}^{t}f(\widetilde{W}(s))ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)L^{\widetilde{W}}(t,x)dx\quad a.s.

Then, with probability 1,

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\rho\left(S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(s)\right)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\left(S^{-1}\left(y\right)\right)L^{\widetilde{W}}(t,y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\left(x\right)S^{\prime}(x)L^{\widetilde{W}}(t,S(x))dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{2a}{\sigma^{2}}-1}e^{-\frac{2b}{\sigma^{2}}x}L^{\widetilde{W}}(t,S(x))dx

\displaystyle<\infty

because
\frac{2a}{\sigma^{2}}-1>-1
and
L^{\widetilde{W}}(t,S(x))=0
for
x>S^{-1}\left(\max_{s\in[0,T]}\widetilde{W}(s)\right)
.

2.

Since
\varphi
is strictly increasing and
\varphi_{\infty}=\infty
with probability 1, its inverse
\tau
in (
3.4
) is well-defined a.s.

Remark 3.3
.

The transformation (
3.3
) is invertible. Indeed, it is straightforward to check that, with probability 1,

\tau_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\rho\left(X(s)\right)}ds,

therefore,

\widetilde{W}(t)=S\left(X\left(\varphi_{t}\right)\right),

where
\varphi=\tau^{-1}
can be expressed as the inverse of the mapping
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\rho\left(X(s)\right)}ds
. For more details on transformations of this type, we refer the reader to
[
21
, Chapter IV, §7]
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We will split the proof into two steps. First, we will follow
[
29
, Chapter V, Section 7, §48]
to prove that, for
some given reflected Brownian motion
\widetilde{W}
, the process
X(t):=S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(\tau_{t})\right)
is the weak solution to the SDE (
1.2
). Then we will utilize the invertability of transformation (
3.3
) outlined in Remark
3.3
to establish the existence of a reflected Brownian motion
\widetilde{W}
together with the required representation for the given CIR process
X
.

Step 1.
Let
\widetilde{Z}=\{\widetilde{Z}(t),~t\geq 0\}
be a standard Brownian motion starting at
\widetilde{Z}(0)=S(x_{0})
. Consider a reflected Brownian motion

\widetilde{W}(t):=|\widetilde{Z}(t)|=S(x_{0})+Z(t)+L^{\widetilde{Z}}(t),

where
Z(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\sign{\widetilde{Z}(s)}d\widetilde{Z}(s)
is a Brownian motion and
L^{\widetilde{Z}}(t)
is the local time of
L^{\widetilde{Z}}
at zero. Put
V(t):=S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(t)\right)
and observe that, by the extension of Itô’s formula in
[
29
, Lemma IV.45.9]
,

\displaystyle V(t)

\displaystyle-V(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(u)\right)\right)^{\frac{2a}{\sigma^{2}}}e^{-\frac{2bS^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(u)\right)}{\sigma^{2}}}d\widetilde{W}(u)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\left(S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(u)\right)\right)^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-1}e^{-\frac{4b}{\sigma^{2}}S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(u)\right)}\left(a-bS^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(u)\right)\right)du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(V(u)\right)^{\frac{2a}{\sigma^{2}}}e^{-\frac{2b}{\sigma^{2}}V(u)}dZ(u)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\left(V(u)\right)^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-1}e^{-\frac{4b}{\sigma^{2}}V(u)}\left(a-bV(u)\right)du.

Hence, by Itô’s formula, for any infinitely differentiable function with compact support
h
,

C_{t}(h):=h(V(t))-h(V(0))-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\left(V(s)\right)^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-1}e^{-\frac{4b}{\sigma^{2}}V(s)}\mathcal{A}h(V(s))ds

(3.6)

is a local martingale, where

\mathcal{A}h(x):=(a-bx)h^{\prime}(x)+\frac{\sigma^{2}x}{2}h^{\prime\prime}(x)

is the generator of (
1.2
). Recall that

X(t)=V(\tau_{t})

and observe that, by (
3.6
), for any infinitely differentiable function with compact support
h
, simple change of variables yields that

\displaystyle C_{\tau_{t}}(h)

\displaystyle=h(V(\tau_{t}))-h(V(0))-\int_{0}^{\tau_{t}}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\left(V(s)\right)^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-1}e^{-\frac{4b}{\sigma^{2}}V(s)}\mathcal{A}h(V(s))ds

\displaystyle=h(X(t))-h(x_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}h(X(s))ds.

Since
C_{\tau_{t}}(h)
,
t\geq 0
, is a local martingale by the optional stopping theorem,

h(X(t))-h(x_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}h(X(s))ds

is also a local martingale and therefore, by
[
29
, V.19–V.20]
,
X
is the weak solution to (
1.2
).

Step 2.
Let now
X
be the unique strong solution to (
1.2
). By Remark
3.3
and Step 1, the process

\widetilde{W}(t):=S(X(\varphi_{t})),

where
\varphi
is defined as the inverse of the mapping

, is a reflected Brownian motion for which
X
admits the representation (
3.3
).

3.2
Characterization of
L
in terms of
L^{\widetilde{W}}

Having the representation (
3.3
) at our disposal, we are now ready to characterize the process
L
from (

) in terms of the local time

Let
X
be the unique strong solution to the SDE (
1.2
) with
0<a<\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
and
\widetilde{W}
be the reflected Brownian motion such that

Denote
L^{\widetilde{W}}=\{L^{\widetilde{W}}(t,x),~t\geq 0,~x\geq 0\}

so that for any bounded measurable

\int_{0}^{t}f(\widetilde{W}(s))ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)L^{\widetilde{W}}(t,x)dx,\quad t\geq 0,

First of all, let us express the local time

in terms of

Proposition 3.4
.

Let

Y(t):=\sqrt{X(t)}=\sqrt{S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(\tau_{t})\right)}

be the square root of the CIR process
X
. Then, for any bounded measurable
f
,

\int_{0}^{t}f(Y(s))ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(y)L^{Y}(t,y)dy,

where, with probability 1,

L^{Y}(t,y)=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}y^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-1}e^{-\frac{2b}{\sigma^{2}}y^{2}}L^{\widetilde{W}}(\varphi_{t},S(y^{2}))

(3.7)

with
\varphi
being defined by (
3.5
).

Proof.

For any bounded measurable
f
, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}f(Y(s))ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}f\left(\sqrt{S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(\tau_{u})\right)}\right)du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\varphi_{t}}f\left(\sqrt{S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(z)\right)}\right)\rho\left(S^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}(z)\right)\right)dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f\left(\sqrt{S^{-1}\left(x\right)}\right)\rho\left(S^{-1}\left(x\right)\right)L^{\widetilde{W}}\left(\varphi_{t},x\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f\left(y\right)\rho\left(y^{2}\right)L^{\widetilde{W}}\left(\varphi_{t},S(y^{2})\right)2yS^{\prime}(y^{2})dy

\displaystyle=:\int_{0}^{\infty}f(y)L^{Y}(t,y)dy.

The final result is obtained by recalling that

\displaystyle L^{Y}(t,y)

\displaystyle=\rho\left(y^{2}\right)L^{\widetilde{W}}\left(\varphi_{t},S(y^{2})\right)2yS^{\prime}(y^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}y^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-1}e^{-\frac{2b}{\sigma^{2}}y^{2}}L^{\widetilde{W}}(\varphi_{t},S(y^{2})).

∎

Define a normalized local time of the process
Y
as follows. Set

\ell(t,y):=y^{1-\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}}L^{Y}(t,y),\quad y>0,

(3.8)

and

\ell(t,0):=\lim_{y\to 0+}\ell(t,y).

Note that
\ell(t,y)
is continuous in
(t,y)
because

\ell(t,y)=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}e^{-\frac{2b}{\sigma^{2}}y^{2}}L^{\widetilde{W}}(\varphi_{t},S(y^{2})).

However, we want to stress that
\ell(t,y)
is a function of the local time
L^{Y}
of the process
Y
without mentioning the auxiliary Brownian motion
\widetilde{W}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
X
be the CIR process satisfying (
1.2
) and
\widetilde{W}
be the reflected Brownian motion such that
X(t)=S^{-1}(\widetilde{W}(\tau_{t}))
,
t\geq 0
. Then, with probability 1, the process
Y=\sqrt{X}
satisfies the SDE of the form

Y(t)=\sqrt{x_{0}}-\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)ds+\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t)+L(t),

(3.9)

where

\displaystyle L(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}-a\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-2}\left(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right)dy.

(3.10)

Moreover,

\displaystyle L(t)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{X(s)}{(X(s)+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}-a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{\varepsilon}{(X(s)+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds.

Remark 3.6
.

Since the SDE (
1.2
) has a strong solution, Theorem
3.5
immediately yields that the SDE (
3.9
)–(
3.10
) also has a strong solution.

Remark 3.7
.

Despite the fact that
\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-2\in\left(-2,-1\right)
, the integral

\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-2}\left|\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right|dy

is finite with probability 1. Indeed, denote
k:=\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}\in(0,1)
and observe that, by (
3.8
) and properties of local time
L^{\widetilde{W}}
,

\sup_{y\geq 1}|\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)|<\infty

(3.11)

with probability 1 for any
t\geq 0
. Moreover, since
L^{\widetilde{W}}(t,\cdot)
is Hölder continuous of order up to
\frac{1}{2}
a.s. (see e.g. calculations in
[
29
, Section IV.44]
), for any
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
and any fixed
t>0
there exists a random variable
C>0
such that, with probability 1,

|\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)|\leq C\cdot(S(y^{2}))^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}.

(3.12)

Hence, on the one hand,

\int_{1}^{\infty}\left|\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right|y^{k-2}dy<\infty\ \mbox{a.s.}

by (
3.11
). On the other hand, take
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{k}{2(2-k)}\right)
and observe that (
3.12
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left|\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right|y^{k-2}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\left(S(y^{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}y^{k-2}dy

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{y^{2}}z^{-\frac{k}{2}}e^{\beta z}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}y^{k-2}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{y^{2}}z^{-\frac{k}{2}}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}y^{k-2}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}y^{-1+\frac{k}{2}-\delta^{\prime}}dy<\infty\ \mbox{a.s.},

where
\delta^{\prime}:=(2-k)\delta\in\left(0,\frac{k}{2}\right)
and
C
is a (random) constant that varies from line to line.

Now we are ready to proceed to the proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

In Section
2
, we obtained the representation (
3.9
) with
L
being a
ucp-limit
of the form

\displaystyle L(t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{X(s)}{(X(s)+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}-a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{\varepsilon}{(X(s)+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds.

Hence, one is left to prove that this limit exists in the sense of a.s. convergence and check that the last equality in (
3.10
) holds.

Let
k:=\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}\in(0,1)
denote the dimension of the CIR process, i.e. we have to study the a.s.-limit of the form

\displaystyle L(t)

\displaystyle:=-\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}-a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{\varepsilon}{(X(s)+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{8}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{\left(X(s)+\varepsilon\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds.

Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon}}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{Y^{2}(s)+\varepsilon}}ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}L^{Y}(t,y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}y^{k-1}\ell(t,y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}y^{k-1}(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0))dy

\displaystyle+\ell(t,0)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}y^{k-1}dy

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\varepsilon}{\left(X(s)+\varepsilon\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\varepsilon}{(Y^{2}(s)+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}L^{Y}(t,y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}\ell(t,y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0))dy

\displaystyle+\ell(t,0)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}dy.

Let us study separately the asymptotics of

\displaystyle I_{1}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}y^{k-1}(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0))dy,

\displaystyle I_{2}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0))dy,

\displaystyle I_{3}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\ell(t,0)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}y^{k-1}dy-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}dy\right)

as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. First, observe that for any
y\geq 0

\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}y^{k-1}|\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)|\leq y^{k-2}|\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)|

and note that by Remark
3.7
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{k-2}\left|\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right|dy<\infty\quad\mbox{a.s.}

Thus, by the dominated convergence theorem, with probability 1,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}I_{1}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}y^{k-1}\left(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right)dy

\displaystyle=(1-k)\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{k-2}\left(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right)dy.

Next, observe that, with probability 1,

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}\left(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right)dy\bigg|

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{1}^{\infty}\left|\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right|y^{k-4}dy

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon C\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{k-4}dy\to 0,\quad\varepsilon\downarrow 0.

On the other hand, take an arbitrary
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{k}{2(2-k)}\right)
, denote
\delta^{\prime}:=(2-k)\delta
and observe that (
3.12
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}\left|\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right|dy

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}(S(y^{2}))^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}dy

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}\left(\int_{0}^{y^{2}}z^{-\frac{k}{2}}e^{\beta z}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}\left(\int_{0}^{y^{2}}z^{-\frac{k}{2}}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{\frac{k}{2}-\delta^{\prime}}dy

\displaystyle=C\varepsilon^{\frac{k}{4}-\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\left(\left({y}/{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\right)^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-\delta^{\prime}}dy,

where
\beta:=\frac{2b}{\sigma^{2}}
. Hence, by substituting
z=y/\sqrt{\varepsilon}
in (
3.14
), we can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}\left|\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right|dy

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{k}{4}-\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(z^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}z^{\frac{k}{2}-\delta^{\prime}}dz

\displaystyle\to 0

with probability 1 as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Summarizing (
3.13
) and (
3.15
), we obtain that, with probability 1,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}I_{2}(\varepsilon)=0.

Finally, integration by parts yields

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{k-1}}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}dy=\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{k+1}}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}dy

and the right-hand side of the last equation is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{k-1}(y^{2}+\varepsilon-\varepsilon)}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{k-1}}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}dy-\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon y^{k-1}}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}dy.

Therefore

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}dy=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}y^{k-1}dy

and

I_{3}(\varepsilon)=\ell(t,0)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-k}{\sqrt{y^{2}+\varepsilon}}y^{k-1}dy-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{(y^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}}y^{k-1}dy\right)=0.

Summarizing all of the above and recalling that
k=\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}
, we finally obtain that with probability 1

\displaystyle L(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{8}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left(I_{1}(\varepsilon)-I_{2}(\varepsilon)+I_{3}(\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{8}\left(1-k\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{k-2}\left(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right)dy

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}-a\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-2}\left(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right)dy

which ends the proof.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Theorem
3.5
implies that the limit

L(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon_{n}}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{X(s)}{(X(s)+\varepsilon_{n})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds

(3.16)

exists a.s. for any sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n},~n\geq 1\}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
and does not depend on the particular choice of the sequence. Moreover, the proof of Theorem
3.5
yields that the existence of the limit (
3.16
) is ensured for all
\omega
such that
L^{\widetilde{W}}(\omega;t,\cdot)
is Hölder continuous. In other words, the set of full probability where (
3.16
) holds can be chosen independently of a particular sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n},~n\geq 1\}
, as anticipated in Remark
2.1
.

4
Discussion of the results

It is evident that the nature of the limit in (
2.4
) heavily depends on the relation between parameters
a
and
\sigma
. Therefore, in order to put our findings from Section
3
into context, let us provide some relevant results from
[
25
]
on the behavior of
Y
when
a\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
.

4.1
Square root of the CIR process when
a\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}

Case I:
a>\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
.

Observe that, if

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{X(s)}}ds=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{Y(s)}ds<\infty\quad a.s.,

(4.1)

then the limit (
2.4
) is equal to

L(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(a-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{Y(s)}ds

by monotone convergence. This is clearly the case for
a\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}
: indeed
a\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}
implies that
X
(and hence
Y
) has strictly positive paths a.s. (see e.g.
[
3
]
or
[
14
]
) and therefore (
4.1
) holds for all
t\geq 0
. It turns out (see e.g.
[
25
, Theorem 2.1(a)]
) that (
4.1
) also holds if
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}<a<\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}
, i.e. one can prove the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

(
[
25
, Theorem 2.1(a)]
)
. Let
a>\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
. Then, for any
t\geq 0
,

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{Y(s)}ds<\infty\quad a.s.

(4.2)

and
Y
a.s. satisfies the SDE of the form

Y(t)=\sqrt{x_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(a-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{Y(s)}ds-\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)ds+\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t).

(4.3)

Remark 4.2
.

Using the same arguments as in
[
9
, Theorem 3.2]
, it is possible to prove that for
a>\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
the process
Y=\sqrt{X}
is the unique
non-negative
strong solution to the SDE (
4.3
). However, if
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}<a<\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}
, (
4.3
) has other strong solutions; moreover, the uniqueness in law does not hold for (
4.3
). For a more detailed discussion of this phenomenon, we refer the reader to
[
7
]
whereas a comprehensive overview of SDEs of the type (
4.3
) can be found in
[
10
]
.

Case II:
{a=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}}
.

The case
a=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
turns out to be different from the one described above: in this regime,
X
can hit zero (see e.g.
[
3
]
or
[
14
]
) and, as noted in e.g.
[
25
, Theorem 2.1(b)]
, (
4.2
) does not hold for all

t>\inf\{s\geq 0~|~Y(s)=0\}.

However, the limit
L
from (
2.4
) has a simple interpretation in terms of Skorokhod reflections (see e.g. the seminal works
[
30
,
31
]
) as summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

(
[
25
, Theorem 2.1(b)]
)
. Let
a=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
and denote
\tau:=\inf\{s\geq 0~|~X(s)=0\}
.

1)

For all
\gamma>0
,

\int_{0}^{\tau+\gamma}\frac{1}{Y(s)}ds=\infty\quad a.s.

2)

The processes
Y:=\sqrt{X}
and
L
defined by (
2.4
) is the (unique) solution to Skorokhod problem

Y(t)=\sqrt{x_{0}}-\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)ds+\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t)+L(t),

(4.4)

with
L
being the corresponding Skorokhod reflection function, i.e. a continuous non-decreasing process starting at 0 with points of growth occurring only at zeros of
Y
and such that
Y(t)\geq 0
.

Remark 4.4
.

Item 2) of Theorem
4.3
states that, when
a=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
, the square root process
Y=\sqrt{X}
coincides with a reflected Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (ROU) process. More details on the latter can be found in e.g.
[
32
]
.

4.2
Comparison to the low-dimensional case

As we have seen in Section
3
, the case
0<a<\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
is arguably the most challenging one and leads to the most involved value of the limit (
2.4
). First of all, note that (
4.1
) does not hold due to Theorem
4.3
together with the comparison theorem for solutions of SDEs (see e.g.
[
20
]
). Next, the limit
L
in (
2.4
) cannot be non-decreasing in
t
as it happens when
a\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
. Indeed, consider
\tau\geq 0
such that
X(\tau)>0
. Then, by a.s. continuity of
X
, there exists a neighborhood
\tau_{-}<\tau<\tau_{+}
such that
X
is bounded away from zero on
(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})
. Denote now
-\delta:=a-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
,
\delta>0
. Then, with probability 1, for all
\tau_{-}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\tau_{+}

\displaystyle L(t_{2})-L(t_{1})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon_{n}}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{X(s)}{(X(s)+\varepsilon_{n})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)ds

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{(X(s)+\varepsilon_{n})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{X(s)+\varepsilon_{n}}}\right)ds

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{X(s)}}ds

\displaystyle<0.

On the other hand,
L
is not strictly decreasing on the entire
[0,T]
: if it is strictly decreasing (and, since
L(0)=0
, non-positive), then
Y\leq U
, where
U
is the standard Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process defined by

U(t)=Y(0)-\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}U(s)ds+\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t).

However, it is not possible since
Y
cannot take negative values.

5
Connection to Skorokhod reflections

Finally, let us present the connection of low-dimensional CIR processes with Skorokhod problems. For
\delta>-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
, consider a family of CIR processes

\{X_{\delta}\}
with
a=a(\delta)=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}+\delta
and defined by

X_{\delta}(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}+\delta-bX_{\delta}(s)\right)ds+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{X_{\delta}(s)}dW(s).

(5.1)

As described above in Sections
3
–
4
, the process
Y_{\delta}:=\sqrt{X_{\delta}}
satisfies the SDE of the form

Y_{\delta}(t)=\sqrt{x_{0}}-\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y_{\delta}(s)ds+\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t)+L_{\delta}(t),

where the term
L_{\delta}
depends on the parameter
\delta
as follows:

—

if
-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}<\delta<0
,

\displaystyle L_{\delta}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}-a\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\frac{4a}{\sigma^{2}}-2}\left(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\frac{4\delta}{\sigma^{2}}-1}\left(\ell(t,y)-\ell(t,0)\right)dy,

where
\ell(t,y)=y^{-\frac{4\delta}{\sigma^{2}}}L^{Y_{\delta}}(t,y)
is the normalized local time of
Y_{\delta}
, see (
3.8
);

—

if
\delta>0
,

L_{\delta}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\delta}{Y_{\delta}(s)}ds

and the integral is well-defined and finite with probability 1;

—

if
\delta=0
,
L_{0}
is the Shorokhod reflection function, i.e. a continuous non-decreasing process with points of growth occurring only at zeros of
Y_{0}
and such that
Y_{0}\geq 0
, which is a symmetric local time of
Y_{0}
at 0; in particular,
Y_{0}
is a reflected Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.

The dynamics of
Y_{\delta}
with
\delta\geq 0
described above allowed
[
25
]
to obtain the following alternative representation to the Skorokhod reflection function
L_{0}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

(
[
25
, Theorem 2.4]
)
.
Let
\{\delta_{n},~n\geq 1\}
be an arbitrary positive sequence such that
\delta_{n}\downarrow 0
,
n\to\infty
. Then, with probability 1, for any
T>0

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{\delta_{n}}(t)-Y_{0}(t)|\to 0

and

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|L_{0}(t)-L_{\delta_{n}}(t)\right|=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|L_{0}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\delta_{n}}{\sqrt{X_{\delta_{n}}(s)}}ds\right|\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.

Theorem
5.1
essentially concerns the case
\delta\to 0+
but does not discuss what happens when
\delta\to 0-
, so we finalize the Section by filling this gap.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\{\delta_{n},~n\geq 1\}
be an arbitrary positive sequence such that
\delta_{n}\downarrow 0
,
n\to\infty
. Then, with probability 1, for any
T>0

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{-\delta_{n}}(t)-Y_{0}(t)|\to 0

(5.2)

and

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|L_{-\delta_{n}}(t)-L_{0}(t)\right|\to 0

(5.3)

as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

By
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
, for any
t\geq 0
,
X_{-\delta_{n}}(t)\leq X_{-\delta_{n+1}}(t)\leq X_{0}(t)
a.s. Moreover, by
[
26
, Theorem 4.1]
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X_{-\delta_{n}}(t)-X_{0}(t)\right|\right]\to 0,\quad n\to\infty,

and hence, for all
t\geq 0
,

Y_{-\delta_{n}}(t)\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}Y_{0}(t),\quad n\to\infty,

and

\int_{0}^{t}Y_{-\delta_{n}}(s)ds\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\int_{0}^{t}Y_{0}(s)ds,\quad n\to\infty.

Therefore, since monotone convergence in probability implies almost sure convergence, for any
t\geq 0

Y_{-\delta_{n}}(t)\to Y_{0}(t)

and

\int_{0}^{t}Y_{-\delta_{n}}(s)ds\to\int_{0}^{t}Y_{0}(s)ds

a.s. as
n\to\infty
and hence, with probability 1,

\displaystyle L_{-\delta_{n}}(t)

\displaystyle=Y_{-\delta_{n}}(t)-\sqrt{x_{0}}+\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y_{-\delta_{n}}(s)ds-\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t)

\displaystyle\to Y_{0}(t)-\sqrt{x_{0}}+\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y_{0}(s)ds-\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t)

\displaystyle=L_{0}(t),\quad n\to\infty.

It remains to note that
Y_{0}
as well as each
Y_{-\delta_{n}}
have a.s. continuous paths and
\{Y_{-\delta_{n}}(t),~n\geq 1\}
is non-decreasing a.s. w.r.t.
n
, which immediately yields (
5.2
) by Dini’s theorem. Similarly,
L_{0}
as well as all
L_{-\delta_{n}}
are continuous with probability 1 and

\displaystyle L_{-\delta_{n}}(t)

\displaystyle=Y_{-\delta_{n}}(t)-\sqrt{x_{0}}+\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y_{-\delta_{n}}(s)ds-\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t)

\displaystyle\leq Y_{-\delta_{n+1}}(t)-\sqrt{x_{0}}+\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y_{-\delta_{n+1}}(s)ds-\frac{\sigma}{2}W(t)

\displaystyle=L_{-\delta_{n+1}}(t),

which implies (
5.3
).
∎

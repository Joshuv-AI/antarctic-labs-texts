Pure contractive multipliers of some reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and Applications
Srijan Sarkar

Abstract
. A contraction T T on a Hilbert space ℋ \mathcal{H} is said to be pure if the sequence { T ∗ n } n \{T^{*n}\}_{n} converges to 0 0 in the strong operator topology. In this article, we prove that for contractions T T , which commute with certain tractable tuples of commuting operators X = ( X 1 , … , X n ) X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}) on ℋ \mathcal{H} , the following statements are equivalent: (i) T T is a pure contraction on ℋ \mathcal{H} , (ii) the compression P 𝒲 ⁡ ( X ) ​ T | 𝒲 ⁡ ( X ) P_{\mathcal{W}(X)}T|_{\mathcal{W}(X)} is a pure contraction, where 𝒲 ⁡ ( X ) \mathcal{W}(X) is the wandering subspace corresponding to the tuple X X . An operator-valued multiplier Φ \Phi of a vector-valued reproducing kernel Hilbert space (rkHs) is said to be pure contractive if the associated multiplication operator M Φ M_{\Phi} is a pure contraction. Using the above result, we find that operator-valued mulitpliers Φ ⁡ ( 𝒛 ) \Phi(\bm{z}) of several vector-valued rkHs’s on the polydisc 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} as well as the unit ball 𝔹 n \mathbb{B}_{n} in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} are pure contractive if and only if Φ ⁡ ( 0 ) \Phi(0) is a pure contraction on the underlying Hilbert space. 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})
be a
\mathcal{E}
-valued normalized reproducing kernel Hilbert space (abbreviated as rkHs) on some domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Our study is based on certain functions called
mutliplers
of
\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})
. The collection of multipliers, denoted by
\mult(\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E}))
, consists of operator-valued functions
\Phi
on
\Omega
for which
\Phi f\in\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})
. Associated to a multiplier, there always exists a multiplication operator on the rkHs. This forms a strong interplay between function theory and operator theory and our objective stems from this connection. More precisely, we are interested in the membership of mulitplication operators (arising from multipliers) in the following class of operators.

Definition 1.1
.

A contraction
T
on
\mathcal{H}
(that is,
\|T\|\leq 1
) is said to be pure if
T^{*m}\rightarrow 0
in the strong operator topology (that is, for all
h\in\mathcal{H},\|T^{*m}h\|\rightarrow 0)
as
m\rightarrow\infty
.

Shift operators on several rkHs’s are natural examples of pure contractions. In particular, shift operator on vector-valued Hardy spaces play a fundamental role. For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
, the
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy space is defined by

H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}):=\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D};\mathcal{E}):a_{n}\in\mathcal{E},n\in\mathbb{N},\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|a_{n}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}<\infty\},

where,
\mathbb{D}
is the open unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
. The scalar-valued Hardy space is denoted by
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and the collection of multipliers turns out to be the set of all bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
, denoted by
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
.
\mult(H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}))
is precisely the operator-valued bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
, denoted by
H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
stands for bounded operators on
\mathcal{E}
.

Using the reproducing property of the Szegö kernel of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, one can easily show that
M_{z}\in\mathcal{B}(H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}))
is a pure contraction. In fact, the remarkable Sz-Nagy-Foias model (see
[
21
]
) for a contraction proves that
a pure contraction upto unitary equivalence is the compression operator
P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{z}|_{\mathcal{Q}}
where,
\mathcal{Q}
is a
M_{z}^{*}
invariant closed subspace of some vector-valued Hardy space
. An important characterization that follows from the above model shows that:
a contraction
T
on
\mathcal{H}
is pure if and only if its characteristic function
\Theta_{T}(z)
is a
inner
function
(see
[
21
]
). Although this is a complete characterization, determining the behaviour of
\Theta_{T}
is a challenging problem in itself and, thus, it is seldom used for checking pure contractions. Hence, it is a natural question to find more examples of pure contractions and more importantly, to find a tractable criterion. Before stating our main question, let us establish the following convention.

Definition 1.2
.

For a rkhs
\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})
, a multiplier
\Phi\in\mult(\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E}))
is said to be
contractive
if
M_{\Phi}
is contraction on
\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})
. Furthermore,
\Phi\in\mult(\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E}))
is said to be
pure contractive
if
M_{\Phi}
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E}).

With this terminology in mind, let us state the problem of our interest.

Question
.

Given a
\mathcal{E}
-valued reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})
, find a tractable necessary and sufficient condition for pure contractive multipliers in
\mult(\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E}))
.

An answer to the above question for vector-valued Hardy spaces on the disc can be derived from a result by Bercovici et al. (Lemma 3.3,
[
8
]
). More precisely,
a contractive operator-valued analytic function
\Phi(z)\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
is pure contractive if and only if
\Phi(0)
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{E}
. Recent works show that similar conclusions hold true for Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
(see
[
13
]
).
It turns out that, to extend this result to a wider class of vector-valued rkHs’s on the polydisc, it is important to identify the role played by shift operators. In the case of some well-studied rkHs’s we know that the shift operators exhibit properties like left-invertibility or being a pure isometry. The following result, obtained recently by the author, shows that it is indeed the fact that the shift operator, on a vector-valued Hardy space on the disc, is a pure isometry that leads to the result by Bercovici et al. (see Corollary 3.3,
[
28
]
).

Proposition 1.3

(paraphrasing [Proposition 3.2,
[
28
]
)
.

Let
T
be a contraction on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and let
V
be any pure isometry on
\mathcal{H}
such that
TV=VT
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{H}
,

(ii)

P_{\mathcal{W}(V)}T|_{\mathcal{W}(V)}
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{W}(V)
,

where,
\mathcal{W}(V):=\mathcal{H}\ominus VH
is the wandering subspace of
V
.

In general, wandering subspaces of operators on Hilbert spaces play an important role in connection with invariant subspaces (see
[
19
,
29
]
). The above result is another instance where, the importance of wandering subspace becomes apparent. In other words, it forms a tractable subset of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
that can be used to determine certain pure contractions. Let us give a few more definitions that are required for describing our main result.

Definition 1.4
.

For a tuple of operators
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the wandering subspace is defined by

\mathcal{W}(X):=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ker X_{i}^{*}=\mathcal{H}\ominus\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\mathcal{H}.

Definition 1.5
.

A tuple of commuting operators
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to possess the wandering subspace property if
\mathcal{H}=\bigvee_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}X^{\bm{k}}\mathcal{W}(X)
.

Returning to our discussion on multipliers: the reader might expect that pure contractive multipliers in higher dimensions (for instance, Hardy space on polydisc) can also be derived from Proposition
1.3
. However, there exists the following difficulty: for a tuple of operators
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
the wandering subspace
\mathcal{W}(X_{i})
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
is a significantly larger set containing
\mathcal{W}(X)
. For instance, if we consider the simple example of
M_{\bm{z}}=(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
(the Hardy space on polydisc), then

\mathcal{W}(M_{\bm{z}})=\mathbb{C},\quad\text{whereas}\quad\mathcal{W}(M_{z_{i}})\cong H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-1})\quad(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}).

Another difficulty that lies in higher dimensions is the absence of a Beurling-Lax-Halmos type result for shift-invariant closed subspaces, which plays an important role in proving Proposition
1.3
. Thus, it is evident that we require new techniques for proving an analogous result that can be applied to higher dimensions. Here, we follow an approach popularized by the methods of Halmos (in
[
19
]
) and Shimorin (in
[
29
]
) by focussing on abstract tuples of operators on Hilbert spaces that behave like shift operators on several rkHs’s. More precisely, we are able to extend Proposition
1.3
to pure contractions commuting with certain tuples of left-invertible operators.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
T
be a contraction on
\mathcal{H}
and let
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
be a
n
-tuple of doubly commuting left-invertible operators on
\mathcal{H}
such that
X_{i}T=TX_{i}
, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Moreover, assume that both
X
and the corresponding
n
-tuple of Cauchy-duals
X^{\prime}=(X_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{n}^{\prime})
possess the wandering subspace property on
\mathcal{H}
. Then the following are equivalent:

T
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{H}
.

P_{\mathcal{W}(X)}T|_{\mathcal{W}(X)}
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{W}(X)
.

An interesting thing to note in the above result is that the role of the wandering subspace property becomes more evident. In Proposition
1.3
, this property was not assumed because a pure isometry always satisfies the wandering subspace property due to the Wold-von Neumann decomposition.

Now, our main idea is to leverage Theorem
1.6
in the setting of rkHs’s. It is well-known that the property of left-invertibility is satisfied by shift operators on several rkHs’s, including the Hardy, (un-weighted and certain weighted) Bergman and Dirichlet spaces on the unit disc (see
[
19
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
22
]
). This property extends to tuples of doubly commuting shift operators on these rkHs’s on the unit polydisc as well. This is achieved through the canonical identification of these spaces with the the tensor product of rkHs’s on the unit disc. Using this identification and Theorem
1.6
, we completely characterize pure contractive multipliers of Hardy/Bergman/Dirichlet spaces on the unit polydisc.

Theorem 1.7
.

For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
, let
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
be the
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy/Bergman/ Dirichlet space on the polydisc. Then for any contractive
\Phi(\bm{z})\in\mbox{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E})
, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

M_{\Phi}
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
.

(ii)

\Phi(0)
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{E}
.

There are several rkhs’s on the open unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, to which we are able to extend the above result by using a different approach. The list includes Drury-Arveson, Hardy, Bergman spaces as well as unitarily invariant
complete Nevanlinna-Pick
spaces (see Theorems
4.4
and
4.6
). Shift operators on these rkHs’s do not have properties like left-invertiblity and therefore, the method used in Section
3
completely fails here. Thus, we look back to Proposition
1.3
and consider an approach that is similar to the proof of Proposition
1.3
by looking for characterizations of shift invariant subspaces. A remarkable result by Clouâtre et al. shows that a Beurling-Lax-Halmos type characterization indeed holds true for spaces with
cnp
factors (
[
11
]
). This result plays an important role in proving our main result for the above-mentioned rkHs’s.

Let us now discuss how this article is arranged. Excluding this present section, the rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains notations, definitions and a brief overview of several results that we will use in this article. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.6
, followed by proving Theorem
1.7
. In Section
4
, we shift our focus to rkHs’s on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. We prove a result that is analogous to Theorem
1.7
, for pure contractive mulitpliers of vector-valued Drury-Arveson/Hardy/Bergman spaces and unitarily invariant
complete Nevanlinna-Pick
spaces on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. We conclude the latter section by applying our results to obtain a characterization for pure contractions belonging to the commutant of certain tuples of commuting operators for which a Wold-von Neumann decomposition type theorem has been established by Eschmeier and Langendörfer in
[
17
]
. Finally, in Section
5
, we apply the results in Section
3
to obtain pure isometric dilation of certain important tuples of commuting contractions appearing in recent papers by Barik et al. (see
[
7
]
and
[
6
]
).

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

The aim of this section is to make this article as much self-contained as possible by recalling definitions and results that are important for this article. We also prove some new results at the end of this section, that will be mainly used in Section
4
.

For any
n
-tuple of bounded operators
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
, a subspace
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{H}
is said to be
X
-joint invariant if
T_{i}\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Let
\bm{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, then

|\bm{m}|:=m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n};\quad\bm{m}!:=m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!;\quad T^{\bm{m}}:=T_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots T_{n}^{m_{n}}.

2.1.
Left-invertible operators

In this subsection is to present a brief overview of the results developed by Shimorin in his remarkable paper
[
29
]
. We recommend the Ph.D. thesis of DeSantis for a comprehensive treatise on this topic
[
14
]
.

The roots of this discussion lies in the classical work of Halmos
[
19
]
in which he established the Beurling’s theorem for vector-valued Hardy spaces. He used the Wold-von Neumann decomposition theorem for isometries, which states that an isometric operator
V
on
\mathcal{H}
always admits a decomposition
V=V|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\oplus V|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}
where,
\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathcal{H}_{1}
are reducing subspaces for
V
such that
V|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}
is a unitary,
V|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}
is a pure isometry and
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1}
. Furthermore, the subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathcal{H}_{1}
have the following precise structure

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}V^{m}\mathcal{H};\quad\mathcal{H}_{1}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}V^{m}\mathcal{W}(V).

In particular, if
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\{0\}
(equivalently,
V
is a pure isometry) then,
V
is unitarily equivalent to
M_{z}
on
H_{\mathcal{W}(V)}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Motivated by this result, we have the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

A tuple of operators
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be analytic if
\bigcap_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\bm{m}|=l}X^{\bm{m}}\mathcal{H}=\{0\}
.

In the case of a single operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
this condition turns out to be
\bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}T^{m}\mathcal{H}=\{0\}
.

The above decomposition result for isometries was extended in a certain sense by Shimorin in
[
29
]
. He showed that operators
T
on
\mathcal{H}
, satisfying any one of the following conditions

(i)

T^{*2}T^{2}-2T^{*}T+I_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 0
;

(ii)

\|Tx+y\|^{2}\leq 2(\|x\|^{2}+\|Ty\|^{2})
.

admits a Wold-von Neumann type decomposition. In particular, if
T
is assumed to be ananlytic, then it has the wandering subspace property on
\mathcal{H}
. It is important to note that operators satisfying any one of the above conditions are always left-invertible. Let us recall some well-known characterizations for general left-invertible operators (see
[
29
]
and it’s list of references).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
T
be a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Then the following are equivalent

(i)

T
is a left-invertible operator,

(ii)

T
is bounded below,

(iii)

T^{*}T
is invertible.

Shimorin in
[
29
]
defined the the Cauchy-dual of a left-invertible operator
T
to be

(2.1)

T^{\prime}:=T(T^{*}T)^{-1}.

It is a simple but important observation that
T^{\prime}
is also a left-invertible operator on
\mathcal{H}
and the Cauchy dual of
T^{\prime}
is
T
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
T
be a left invertible operator, then the following holds

(i)

T(T^{*}T)^{-1}T^{*}
is a orthogonal projection onto
T\mathcal{H}
.

(ii)

\ker T^{\prime*}=\ker T^{*}
.

(iii)

T^{\prime}\mathcal{H}=T\mathcal{H}
.

In [Proposition 2.7,
[
29
]
], Shimorin remarkably observed that
T
and its Cauchy dual
T^{\prime}
has the following dual properties:

(2.2)

\bigvee_{m\in\mathbb{N}}T^{m}\ker T^{*}=(\underset{m\geq 1}{\bigcap}T^{\prime m}\mathcal{H})^{\perp};\bigvee_{m\in\mathbb{N}}T^{\prime m}\ker T^{*}=(\underset{m\geq 1}{\bigcap}T^{m}\mathcal{H})^{\perp}.

This duality between
T
and
T^{\prime}
establishes the following result on wandering subspace property (see Corollary 2.8,
[
29
]
).

Corollary 2.4
.

A left-invertible operator
T
on
\mathcal{H}
possesses the wandering subspace property if and only if its Cauchy dual
T^{\prime}
is analytic.

2.2.
Reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and Multipliers

Let
\Lambda
be a set and
\mathcal{E}
be a Hilbert space. A operator-valued function
K:\Lambda\times\Lambda\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
is said to be a
kernel
function if for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots\lambda_{n}\}\subseteq\Lambda
and
\{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
, the following positivity condition is satisfied

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle K(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})\eta_{j},\eta_{i}\rangle\geq 0.

The above positive semi-definite condition is often denoted by
K\geq 0
. Associated to this kernel function
K
, there exists a unique Hilbert space of
\mathcal{E}
-valued analytic functions denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})
. In particular, the collection
\{K(\cdot,\lambda)\eta:\lambda\in\Lambda,\eta\in\mathcal{E}\}
forms a total set in
\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})
and satisfies the following reproducing property

\langle f,K(\cdot,\lambda)\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})}=\langle f(\lambda),\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}\quad(f\in\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E}),\eta\in\mathcal{E},\lambda\in\Lambda).

From the above identity one can easily compute the norm of the kernel function to be

\|K(\cdot,\lambda)\eta\|_{\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})}^{2}=\|K(\lambda,\lambda)\eta\|^{2}.

A scalar-valued kernel is denoted by
k
and the corresponding rkHs by
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. In this case, the reproducing property turns out to be
f(\lambda)=\langle f,k(\cdot,\lambda)\rangle
. A scalar-valued kernel
k
on
\Lambda
is said to be
irreducible
if
k(x,y)\neq 0
for any
x,y\in\Lambda
and
k_{x}
and
k_{y}
are linearly independent functions for
x\neq y
. Moreover,
k
is said to be
normalized
at some point
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda
if
k(\cdot,\lambda_{0})\equiv 1
.

Definition 2.5
.

A
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
-valued kernel
K
on set
\Lambda
is called
quasi-scalar
if
K=kI_{\mathcal{E}}
, where
k
is a scalar-valued kernel on
\Lambda
.

For domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, a rkHs
\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathcal{O}(\Omega,\mathcal{E})
with a quasi-scalar kernel
K=kI_{\mathcal{E}}
can always be identified with the rkHs
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
via the unitary map
U:\mathcal{H}_{K}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
defined by

U(\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}\eta)=\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}\otimes\eta\quad(\bm{z}\in\Omega,\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\eta\in\mathcal{E}).

Thus, in the case of quasi-scalar kernels, the corresponding rkHs will simply be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
. Let us give a list of important rkHs with quasi-scalar kernels.

Examples
.

In the following,
\mathcal{E}
is any Hilbert space and
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

(i)

H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}):

\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy space on unit polydisc with the Szegö kernel on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
,

\mathbb{S}(\bm{z},\bm{w})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})^{-1}I_{\mathcal{E}}.

(ii)

A_{\alpha,\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
:
\mathcal{E}
-valued weighted Bergman spaces on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
with the Bergman kernel

K(\bm{z},\bm{w})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})^{-2+\alpha}I_{\mathcal{E}}\quad(-1<\alpha<+\infty).

For
\alpha=0
, the
\mathcal{E}
-valued Bergman space on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
is simply denoted by
A_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

(iii)

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n}):

\mathcal{E}
-valued Dirichlet spaces on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
with the Dirichlet kernel

K(\bm{z},\bm{w})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\big(-\frac{1}{z_{i}\bar{w}_{i}}\ln(1-z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})^{-1}\big)I_{\mathcal{E}}

(iv)

H_{m}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{E}):
for any integer
m\geq 0
, the
\mathcal{E}
-valued rkHs on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
with the kernel

K_{m}(\bm{z},\bm{w})=(1-\langle\bm{z},\bm{w}\rangle)^{-m}I_{\mathcal{E}}\quad(\bm{z},\bm{w}\in\mathbb{B}_{n}).

In the case of
m=1
,
H_{1}(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{E})
is the
\mathcal{E}
-valued Drury-Arveson space denoted by
H_{n,\mathcal{E}}^{2}
. For
m=n
,
H_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{E})
is the
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy space on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
denoted by
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
. And for
m=n+1
,
H_{n+1}(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{E})
is the (unweighted) Bergman space on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
denoted by
A_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
.

Here we would like to return to our discussion on left-invertible operators and wandering subspace property for rkHs’s
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. It is well known, that in the following cases,
M_{z}
is a left-invertible and analytic operator satisfying the wandering subspace property on
\mathcal{H}_{k}
.

(i)

M_{z}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))
is an isometry and hence, left-invertible (see
[
19
]
).

(ii)

For
-1<\alpha\leq 0
, the shift operator
M_{z}\in\mathcal{B}(A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D}))
satisfies the operator identity
\|M_{z}f+g\|^{2}\leq 2(\|f\|^{2}+\|M_{z}g\|^{2})
for
f,g\in A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
(see [Theorem 6.15,
[
20
]
).

(iii)

M_{z}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D}))
satisfies
M_{z}^{2}{M_{z}}^{*2}-2M_{z}M_{z}^{*}+I=0
, in other words, by Agler’s convention
M_{z}
is a
2
-isometry and it follows that
M_{z}
is left-invertible (see
[
22
]
).

We can state the following result based on the above discussion and Corollary
2.4
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}
be the Hardy/Bergman/Dirichlet space on
\mathbb{D}
, then both
M_{z}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{k})
and its Cauchy dual
M_{z}^{\prime}
are left-invertible operators and both of them possesses the wandering subspace property on
\mathcal{H}_{k}
.

2.3.
Multipliers

Let
\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces and
K_{i}:\Lambda\times\Lambda\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{i})
be a kernel for
i=1,2
. A function
\Phi:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})
is said to be a
multiplier
if
\Phi f\in\mathcal{H}_{K_{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{K_{1}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})
. The collection of multipliers from
\mathcal{H}_{K_{1}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})
to
\mathcal{H}_{K_{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})
is denoted by
\mult(\mathcal{H}_{K_{1}}(\mathcal{E}_{1}),\mathcal{H}_{K_{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{2}))
. It follows from the closed graph theorem that
\Phi\in\mult(\mathcal{H}_{K_{1}}(\mathcal{E}_{1}),\mathcal{H}_{K_{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{2}))
induces a bounded operator
M_{\Phi}
from
\mathcal{H}_{K_{1}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})
to
\mathcal{H}_{K_{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})
, which for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
\eta\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
satisfies,

M_{\Phi}^{*}(K_{1}(\cdot,\lambda))=K_{2}(\cdot,\lambda)\Phi(\lambda)^{*}\eta,

and the norm on
\mult(\mathcal{H}_{K_{1}}(\mathcal{E}_{1}),\mathcal{H}_{K_{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{2}))
is defined by
\|\Phi\|:=\|M_{\Phi}\|_{op}
(see
[
2
]
for more details). The closed unit ball of the collection of multipliers is denoted by
\mult_{1}(\mathcal{H}_{K_{1}}(\mathcal{E}_{1}),\mathcal{H}_{K_{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{2}))
. In the case of
K_{1}=K_{2}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}_{2}
, the collection of multipliers becomes a Banach algebra and is simply denoted by
\mult(\mathcal{H}_{K_{1}}(\mathcal{E}_{1}))
.

There is an important class of kernels that are motivated from the classical Nevanlinna-Pick problem on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
about the existence of certain multipliers (see
[
2
]
,
[
10
]
).

Definition 2.7
.

A rkhs
\mathcal{H}_{k}
on a set
\Lambda
is said to be a
complete Nevanlinna-Pick
space (or a
cnp
space) if for all positive integers
l,m
, every collection of points
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}\in\Lambda
and every choice of
l\times l
complex matrices
W_{1},\ldots,W_{m}
, the non-negativity of the block matrix

[(k(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})(I-W_{i}W_{j}^{*}))]_{i,j=1}^{n}

is sufficient for the existence of a multiplier
\Phi
with
\|\Phi\|\leq 1
and
\Phi(z_{i})=W_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

A series of fundamental results on cnp spaces is due to Agler and M
C
Carthy. Building from their works, M
C
Cullough
-
Quiggin discovered a tractable criterion for cnp spaces from which the following result follows (see Theorem 7.6,
[
2
]
).

Theorem 2.8

(M
C
Cullough-Quiggin)
.

A normalized kernel
k
is cnp if and only if
1-1/k\geq 0
.

From the above result, it easily follows that
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
,
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D})
and
H_{n}^{2}
are cnp spaces. Apart from these well studied rkHs’s, we are also interested in a certain type of cnp spaces on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. We recommend the article
[
10
]
and its references for the following details.

Definition 2.9
.

A
unitarily invariant
space on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with a kernel of the following form:

k(\bm{z},\bm{w})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\langle\bm{z},\bm{w}\rangle^{n}\quad(\bm{z},\bm{w}\in\mathbb{B}_{n}),

where
\{a_{n}\}_{n}
is a sequence of strictly positive coefficients with
a_{0}=1
.

By definition, a unitarily invariant kernel on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is always normalized at the point
0
. And for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, the collection
\{(a_{|\alpha|}\frac{|\alpha|!}{\alpha!})^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{z}^{\alpha}\}
forms an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. The following is a generalization of [Theorem 7.33,
[
2
]
], and we recommend [Lemma 2.3,
[
10
]
] for a direct proof.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}
be a unitarily invariant rkhs on
\mathbb{B}_{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
with kernel

k(\bm{z},\bm{w})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\langle\bm{z},\bm{w}\rangle^{n}.

Then
\mathcal{H}_{k}
is an irreducible cnp space if and only if

1-\frac{1}{k(\bm{z},\bm{w})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}\langle\bm{z},\bm{w}\rangle^{n}\quad(\bm{z},\bm{w}\in\mathbb{B}_{n})

for some sequence
\{b_{n}\}_{n}
of non-negative numbers.

An intrinsic problem that is associated to any rkHs is a description for
M_{z}
-invariant closed subspaces. The first of such a characterization is due to Beurling

1
]
in the case of

-invariant clsoed subspaces of

as proved by Aleman, Richter and Sundberg in

and also for

as proved by Richter (see

Theorem 2.11

(Beurling)
.

\mathcal{S}
is a
M_{z}
-invariant closed subspace of

if and only if
\mathcal{S}=\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for some inner function
\theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
(that is,
|\theta(\lambda)|=1
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
).

Results like above rarely holds in higher dimensions, for instance, Rudin showed that even in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
such a result does not exist (see
[
23
]
). However, in the case of unit ball there are several rkHs’s which admit Beurling-type characterization of invariant subspaces. A recent result by Clouâtre et al. in
[

proved that rkHs’s with cnp factors admit this property. The list of references in
[

]
gives a brief account of several earlier results in this direction. For our interest, we combine [Theorem 1.1, Proposition 2.5,

Theorem 2.12

(
Clouâtre, Hartz and Schillo
)
.

Let
k
be a kernel on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
and let
s
be a complete Nevanlinna-Pick kernel on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
such that
k/s\geq 0
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Hilbert space and let
\mathcal{M}
be a non-zero closed subspace of

. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The subspace
\mathcal{M}
is
M_{\bm{z}}
-invariant,

(ii)

There exists an auxillary space
\mathcal{F}
and a partially isometric multiplier

such that
\mathcal{M}=\Gamma(\bm{z})(\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{F})
.

2.4.
Wandering subspaces

In this subsection, we are interested in establishing few results on wandering subspaces associated to certain tuples of operators. Let us begin by observing that wandering subspace associated to analytic tuples must be non-zero.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
be a tuple of commuting operators on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\bigcap_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\bm{m}|=l}X^{\bm{m}}\mathcal{H}=\{0\}
and let
\mathcal{M}
be a
X
-joint invariant closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
. Then
\mathcal{M}\neq\{0\}
if and only if
\mathcal{W}(X|_{\mathcal{M}})\neq\{0\}
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\mathcal{M}=\{0\}
implies that
\mathcal{W}(X|_{\mathcal{M}})=\{0\}
. For proving the converse part, let us first observe that for any tuple of operators
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
, a subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{H}
and any
l\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the following

\bigvee_{\underset{|\bm{m}|\geq l}{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}}X^{\bm{m}}(\mathcal{S})=\bigvee_{\underset{|\bm{m}|\geq l}{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}}X^{\bm{m}}(\overline{\mathcal{S}}).

Now,
\mathcal{W}(X|_{\mathcal{M}})=\{0\}
, implies that
\mathcal{M}=\overline{X_{1}\mathcal{M}+\ldots+X_{n}\mathcal{M}}
and therefore, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\mathcal{M}=\bigvee_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}X^{\bm{m}}(\mathcal{M})=\bigvee_{\underset{|\bm{m}|\geq 1}{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}}X^{\bm{m}}(\mathcal{M})=\ldots=\bigvee_{\underset{|\bm{m}|\geq l}{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}}X^{\bm{m}}(\mathcal{M}).

Thus, for any
h\in\mathcal{M}
we always get that
h\in\bigcap_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\bm{m}|=l}X^{\bm{m}}\mathcal{H}
and the latter subspace is
\{0\}
by our assumption. In other words,
\mathcal{M}=\{0\}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Let us end this section by proving a generalization of [Theorem 5.2,
[
9
]
], that gives an explicit description of wandering subspaces in certain cases.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
A=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
and
B=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{n})
be
n
-tuple of commuting operators on Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, respectively. Suppose, there exists a partial isometry
\Pi:\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}
such that
\Pi A_{i}=B_{i}\Pi
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then
\mathcal{S}=\Pi\mathcal{H}_{1}
is a
B
-invariant closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
and moreover,

\mathcal{W}(B|_{\mathcal{S}}):=\mathcal{S}\ominus\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}\mathcal{S}=\Pi((\ker\Pi)^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{W}(A)).

Proof.

Note that by definition,
\mathcal{W}(B|_{\mathcal{S}})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ker P_{\mathcal{S}}B_{i}^{*}|_{\mathcal{S}}
. Since
\Pi
is a partial isometry,
f\in\mathcal{S}
implies that
f=\Pi g
, for some
g\in\ran\Pi^{*}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle f\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ker P_{\mathcal{S}}B_{i}^{*}|_{\mathcal{S}}\Leftrightarrow B_{i}^{*}f\perp\mathcal{S}\quad(\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}),

In other words,
\Pi^{*}B_{i}^{*}f=0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Again,

A_{i}^{*}g=A_{i}^{*}\Pi^{*}\Pi g=\Pi^{*}B_{i}^{*}f\quad(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}),

implies that
\Pi^{*}B_{i}^{*}f=0
if and only if
A_{i}^{*}g=0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Thus,
g\in\mathcal{W}(A)
and therefore,
\mathcal{W}(B|_{\mathcal{S}})=\Pi((\ker\Pi)^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{W}(A))
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Pure contractions commuting with left-invertible operators

This section consists of three sub-sections. In the first sub-section, we prove Theorem
1.6
. In the later sub-sections, we apply this result to obtain our main characterization on multipliers of several reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

Let us begin with a result on commuting orthogonal projections (see [Lemma 2.5,
[
24
]
])

Lemma 3.1
.

If
(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})
is a
n
-tuple of commuting orthogonal projections on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, then
\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ran P_{i}
is a closed subspace and moreover,

P_{\mathcal{L}}=I_{\mathcal{H}}-\prod_{i=1}^{n}(I-P_{i})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}\prod_{j>i}^{n}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-P_{j}).

Definition 3.2
.

A tuple of commuting operators
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
doubly commuting
if
X_{i}^{*}X_{j}=X_{j}X_{i}^{*}
for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
i\neq j
.

For the purpose of our study in this section, we will assume that
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
is a tuple of doubly commuting left-invertible operators on
\mathcal{H}
.

It follows from a straightforward computation that if
X
is a
n
-tuple of doubly commuting left-invertible operators, then the tuple of respective Cauchy duals
X^{\prime}=(X_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{n}^{\prime})
, also forms a tuple of doubly commuting left-invertible operators.

Now, corresponding to the tuple
X
, we have a tuple of commuting orthogonal projections
\big(X_{1}(X_{1}^{*}X_{1})^{-1}X_{1}^{*},\ldots,X_{n}(X_{n}^{*}X_{n})^{-1}X_{n}^{*}\big)
. From Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.3
, we have
\ker X_{i}^{*}=\ker X_{i}^{\prime*}
or equivalently,
X_{i}\mathcal{H}=X_{i}^{\prime}\mathcal{H}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and therefore, by using Lemma
3.1
we get

(3.1)

P_{\mathcal{W}(X)^{\perp}}=P_{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\mathcal{H}}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}(X_{k}^{*}X_{k})^{-1}X_{k}^{*}\prod_{i>k}^{n}P_{\ker X_{i}^{*}}.

Now, let us establish few results that are important for the sequel.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
be a
n
-tuple of doubly commuting left-invertible operators on
\mathcal{H}
such that
both
X
and
X^{\prime}
possess the wandering subspace property on
\mathcal{H}
. If
\mathcal{M}
is a proper (that is,
\mathcal{M}\neq\{0\}
or
\mathcal{H}
)
X
-joint invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{W}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
. Then there exists a non-zero vector
\eta\in\mathcal{M}
such that
X_{i}^{*}\eta\in\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

The assumption
\mathcal{W}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
serves two purposes:

(a)

\mathcal{M}
is not
X
-reducing, since otherwise,
\mathcal{M}\perp\bigvee_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}X^{\bm{m}}\mathcal{W}(X)=\mathcal{H}
, which will contradict the assumption that
\mathcal{M}
is proper.

(b)

for any
\eta\in\mathcal{M}
, there exists some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
for which
X_{i}^{*}\eta\neq 0
as otherwise,
\eta\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
.

Now, let us note that

\mathcal{W}(X)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ker X_{i}^{*}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ker X_{i}^{\prime*}=\mathcal{W}(X^{\prime}),

and therefore, it follows from our assumption that,

\mathcal{H}=\bigvee_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}X^{\prime\bm{m}}\mathcal{W}(X),

This implies that there must exist a non-zero vector
h\in\mathcal{W}(X)
and
\bm{m}\neq 0
such that
X^{\prime\bm{m}}h\not\perp\mathcal{M}
. Let us define

\tilde{m}_{1}:=\min\{m_{1}\in\mathbb{N}:\mathcal{M}\not\perp X^{\prime\bm{m}}h,\text{ where }\bm{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}.

Now, let us recursively define the other co-ordinates. For all
i\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
, let

\tilde{m}_{i}=\min\{m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}:\mathcal{M}\not\perp X^{\prime\bm{m}}h;\bm{m}=(\tilde{m}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{m}_{i-1},m_{i},\ldots,m_{n})\text{ for }(m_{i},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n-i}\},

For
\tilde{\bm{m}}=(\tilde{m}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{m}_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, there exists a non-zero vector
\eta\in\mathcal{M}
,
\zeta\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
and such that

X^{\prime\tilde{\bm{m}}}h=\eta\oplus\zeta.

Note that
\eta\in\mathcal{M}
implies that
X_{i}^{*}\eta\neq 0
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. For any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
for which
\tilde{k_{i}}=0
we have

X_{i}^{*}\eta=X_{i}^{*}X^{\prime\tilde{\bm{m}}}h-X_{i}^{*}\zeta=X^{\prime\tilde{\bm{m}}}X_{i}^{*}h-X_{i}^{*}\zeta=-X_{i}^{*}\zeta\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}.

For any other
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have

X_{i}^{*}\eta=X_{i}^{*}X^{\prime\tilde{\bm{m}}}h-X_{i}^{*}\zeta=X^{\prime\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}}h-X_{i}^{*}\zeta\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp},

where,
\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}=(\tilde{m}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{m}_{i-1},\tilde{m}_{i}-1,\tilde{m}_{i+1},\ldots,\tilde{m}_{n})
. Since
\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}<\tilde{\bm{m}}
it implies that
X^{\prime\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}}h\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
and so
X_{i}^{*}\eta\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

It follows from the Beurling-Lax-Halmos theorem that any
M_{z}
-invariant closed subspace of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is of the form
\mathcal{M}=\Theta H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}
. This implies that for any
\eta\in\mathcal{F}
we get
M_{z}^{*}M_{\Theta}\eta\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
. The above result shows that even though Beurling-Lax-Halmos type results do not hold for
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, we can still obtain a vector
\eta
in the
M_{\bm{z}}
-joint invariant closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
for which
M_{z_{i}}^{*}\eta\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A
be a left-invertible operator on
\mathcal{H}
and let
\{h_{n}\}
be a bounded sequence of vectors in
A\mathcal{H}
. Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow 0}\|A^{*}h_{n}\|=0
implies that
\lim_{n\rightarrow 0}\|h_{n}\|=0
.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists a strictly positive real number
\alpha
such that
\|h_{n}\|\leq\alpha
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Now,
h_{n}\in A\mathcal{H}
implies that there exists
y_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
Ay_{n}=h_{n}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
A
is bounded below there exists
c>0
such that
\|y_{n}\|\leq c\|Ay_{n}\|=c\|h_{n}\|
. Now,

\lim_{n\rightarrow 0}\|h_{n}\|^{2}=\lim_{n\rightarrow 0}\|Ay_{n}\|^{2}\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow 0}\|A^{*}Ay_{n}\|\|y_{n}\|\leq c\lim_{n\rightarrow 0}\|A^{*}h_{n}\|\|h_{n}\|\leq c\alpha\lim_{n\rightarrow 0}\|A^{*}h_{n}\|=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Given a contraction
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, since
\{T^{m}T^{*m}\}_{m}
is a monotonic decreasing sequence of positive operators there exists a positive operator say
A_{T}
, such that
\{T^{m}T^{*m}\}_{n}
converges in the strong operator topology to
A_{T}^{2}
. Moreover, by definition we have
TA_{T}^{2}T^{*}=A_{T}^{2}
which further implies that

\|A_{T}T^{*m}h\|=\|A_{T}h\|\quad(h\in\mathcal{H},m\in\mathbb{N}).

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

(i)\implies(ii):
Since
\mathcal{W}(X)
is a
T^{*}
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

(P_{\mathcal{W}(X)}T|_{\mathcal{W}(X)})^{*m}=T^{*m}|_{\mathcal{W}(X)},

which implies that
P_{\mathcal{W}(X)}T|_{\mathcal{W}(X)}
is a pure contraction.

(ii)\implies(i):
Our aim is to prove that
\ker A_{T}=\mathcal{H}
. Let us first observe that the assumption
P_{\mathcal{W}(X)}T|_{\mathcal{W}(X)}
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{W}(X)
implies that
\mathcal{W}(X)\subseteq\ker A_{T}
. Indeed for any
\eta\in\mathcal{W}(X)

\|A_{T}\eta\|=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|T^{*m}\eta\|=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|\big(P_{\mathcal{W}(X)}TP_{\mathcal{W}(X)})^{*m}\eta\|=0.

Case (I): If
\ker A_{T}
is a
X
-joint reducing subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, then by the above condition we get
\mathcal{H}=\bigvee_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}X^{\bm{m}}\mathcal{W}(X)\subseteq\ker A_{T}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
. This will show that
\ker A_{T}=\mathcal{H}
.

Case (II): Here, we consider the case where
\ker A_{T}
is not a
X
-joint reducing subspace of
\mathcal{H}
. Let us assume that
\ker A_{T}
is a proper subspace of
\mathcal{H}
. Using Lemma
3.3
, we find a non-zero element
\eta\in(\ker A_{T})^{\perp}
such that
X_{i}^{*}\eta\in\ker A_{T}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Now, the orthogonal decompositon in equation (
3.1
) shows that for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\zeta_{m}\in\mathcal{W}(X)
and
h_{j,m}\in X_{j}(X_{j}^{*}X_{j})^{-1}X_{j}^{*}\prod_{i>j}^{n}P_{\ker X_{i}^{*}}
such that

(3.2)

T^{*m}\eta=\zeta_{m}\oplus\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m},

and therefore,

\|T^{*m}\eta\|^{2}=\|\zeta_{m}\|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|h_{j,m}\|^{2}.

Since
T
is a contraction, we have
\|h_{j,m}\|\leq\|\eta\|
for all

and

(3.3)

X_{i}^{*}T^{*m}\eta=X_{i}^{*}\zeta_{m}+X_{i}^{*}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m}=X_{i}^{*}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m}.

If

Thus, using the fact that

Now,
h_{n,m}\in X_{n}\mathcal{H}
and therefore, by applying Lemma
3.4

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|h_{n,m}\|=0
. We will now proceed to establish that
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m}\|=0
. For proving this, we will show that it is sufficient to obtain the following result.

Claim 3.5
.

If
\underset{m\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\|\underset{{j=l+1}}{\overset{n}{\sum}}h_{j,m}\|=0
, then
\underset{m\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\|\underset{{j=l}}{\overset{n}{\sum}}h_{j,m}\|=0
for any

Let us observe from equation (

X_{l}^{*}T^{*m}\eta=X_{l}^{*}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m}=X_{l}^{*}\sum_{j=l}^{n}h_{j,m},

and thus,

\|X_{l}^{*}h_{l,m}\|=\|X_{l}^{*}T^{*m}\eta-X_{l}^{*}\sum_{j=l+1}^{n}h_{j,m}\|,

which further implies that
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|X_{l}^{*}h_{l,m}\|=0
. Now,
h_{l,m}\in X_{l}\mathcal{H}
and again by applying Lemma
3.4
, we obtain

Starting with

as

. Now, for

(3.4)

\|A_{T}\eta\|=\|A_{T}T^{*m}\eta\|=\|A_{T}(\zeta_{m}\oplus\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m})\|\leq\|A_{T}\|\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m}\|\leq\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m}\|.

If we let

, which further implies that

is a proper subspace of

. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2

The above result holds true even if we consider

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|T^{*l}(T^{*m}\eta)\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|T^{*l}(\zeta_{m}\oplus\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m})\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|T^{*l}\zeta_{m}\|+\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|T^{*l}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m})\|

\displaystyle=0+\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|T^{*l}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m})\|

\displaystyle\leq 0+M\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,m}\|=0.

3.1.
Wandering subspace for tuples of operators on tensor product of Hilbert spaces

For a
n
-tuple of left-invertible operators
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
, let us consider the tuple of left-invertible operators
\mathcal{X}=(\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n})
on
\mathscr{H}:=\underset{n\text{ times}}{\underbrace{\mathcal{H}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathcal{H}}}
defined by

\mathcal{X}_{i}=\underset{i-1\text{ times}}{\underbrace{I_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\ldots\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}}}\otimes X_{i}\otimes\underset{n-i\text{ times}}{\underbrace{I_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\ldots\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}}}.

It follows from a straightforward computation that the Cauchy dual of the left-invertible operator
\mathcal{X}_{i}
is

\mathcal{X}_{i}^{\prime}=\underset{i-1\text{ times}}{\underbrace{I_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\ldots\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}}}\otimes X_{i}^{\prime}\otimes\underset{n-i\text{ times}}{\underbrace{I_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\ldots\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}}}.

The corresponding tuple of Cauchy duals is denoted by
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{X}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n}^{\prime})
. Now, let us compute the wandering subspace of these tuples of operators.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
be a
n
-tuple of doubly commuting left-invertible operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Then
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X})=\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})=\mathcal{W}(X_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathcal{W}(X_{n})
.

Proof.

Using the property of left-invertible operators we obtain that

P_{\ker\mathcal{X}_{i}^{*}}=\underset{i-1\text{ times}}{\underbrace{I_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\ldots\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}}}\otimes P_{\ker X_{i}^{*}}\otimes\underset{n-i\text{ times}}{\underbrace{I_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\ldots\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}}}.

Note that
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ker\mathcal{X}_{i}^{*}=\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})
.
Since
X
is a tuple of doubly commuting operators, both
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, are doubly commuting, which further implies that
(P_{\ker\mathcal{X}_{1}^{*}},\ldots,P_{\ker\mathcal{X}_{n}^{*}})
is a commuting tuple of orthogonal projections and therefore,

P_{\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})}=P_{\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ker\mathcal{X}_{i}^{*}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}P_{\ker\mathcal{X}_{i}^{*}}=P_{\ker X_{1}^{*}}\otimes\ldots\otimes P_{\ker X_{n}^{*}}.

Thus,

\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ker\mathcal{X}_{i}^{*}=\ker X_{1}^{*}\otimes\ldots\otimes\ker X_{n}^{*}=\mathcal{W}(X_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathcal{W}(X_{n}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
be a
n
-tuple of doubly commuting left-invertible operators on
\mathcal{H}
such that both
X_{i}
and
X_{i}^{\prime}
possess the wandering subspace property on
\mathcal{H}
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then both
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{X}
possess the wandering subspace property on
\mathscr{H}
.

Proof.

Our aim is to show that

\mathscr{H}=\bigvee_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathcal{X}^{\prime\bm{m}}\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}).

By Lemma
3.7
,
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})=\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}_{1}^{\prime})\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}_{n}^{\prime})
, which implies that

\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{W}(X_{2}^{\prime})\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathcal{W}(X_{n}^{\prime})=\bigvee_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathcal{X}_{1}^{\prime m}\big(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}_{1}^{\prime})\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}_{n}^{\prime}))\big)\subseteq\bigvee_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathcal{X}^{\prime\bm{m}}\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}).

Similarly,

\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{W}(X_{3}^{\prime})\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathcal{W}(X_{n}^{\prime})=\bigvee_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathcal{X}_{2}^{\prime m}\big(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}_{2}^{\prime})\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}_{n}^{\prime})\big)\subseteq\bigvee_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathcal{X}^{\prime\bm{m}}\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}).

Following this process for another
(n-2)
times gives

\mathscr{H}\subseteq\bigvee_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathcal{X}^{\prime\bm{m}}\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})\subseteq\mathscr{H}.

This proves that
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
possesses the wandering subspace property on
\mathscr{H}
. Now
(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})^{\prime}=\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{W}(X_{i}^{\prime})=\mathcal{W}(X_{i})
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
implies that
\mathcal{X}
possesses the wandering subspace property on
\mathscr{H}
. This completes the proof.
∎

M_{\Phi}^{*l}\eta=\zeta_{l}\oplus\sum_{t=1}^{n}h_{t,l},

and therefore,
\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|M_{z_{i}}^{*}\sum_{t=1}^{n}h_{t,l}\|=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Phi}^{*l}\eta\|=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|M_{\Phi}^{*l}M_{z_{i}}^{*}\eta\|=0
, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Thus,

\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{j}{m\choose j+1}\sum_{|\alpha|=j+1}\gamma_{\alpha}M_{\bm{z}}^{\alpha}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\alpha}(\sum_{t=1}^{n}h_{t,l})\|=0.

From equation (
4.2
), we know that

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{j}{m\choose j+1}\sum_{|\alpha|=j+1}\gamma_{\alpha}M_{\bm{z}}^{\alpha}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\alpha}=I_{H_{m}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{E})}-P_{\mathcal{E}},

and therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|\sum_{t=1}^{n}h_{t,l}\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|(I_{H_{m}(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{E})}-P_{\mathcal{E}})(\sum_{t=1}^{n}h_{t,l})\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{j}{m\choose j+1}\sum_{|\alpha|=j+1}\gamma_{\alpha}M_{\bm{z}}^{\alpha}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\alpha}(\sum_{t=1}^{n}h_{t,l})\|=0.

Now,

\displaystyle\|A_{M_{\Phi}}\eta\|=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|A_{M_{\Phi}}M_{\Phi}^{*l}M_{\Gamma}(1\otimes\eta)\|=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|A_{M_{\Phi}}(\zeta_{l}\oplus\sum_{t=1}^{n}h_{t,l})\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|A_{M_{\Phi}}(\sum_{t=1}^{n}h_{t,l})\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|\sum_{t=1}^{n}h_{t,l}\|=0,

implies that
\eta\in\ker A_{M_{\Phi}}
. But
\eta
was assumed to be an element in
(\ker A_{M_{\Phi}})^{\perp}
which is possible only if
\eta=0
. This is a contradiction to our assumption that
\ker A_{M_{\Phi}}
is a proper subspace. Since
\{0\}\neq\mathcal{E}\subset\ker A_{M_{\Phi}}
, the only possibility is that
\ker A_{M_{\Phi}}=H_{m}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{E})
. In other words,
M_{\Phi}
is a pure contraction on
H_{m}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{E})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.5

(
Chen
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
be an unitarily invariant complete Nevanlinna-Pick space on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Then there exist real numbers
c_{|\beta|}
such that
c_{|\beta|}\leq 0
for
|\beta|\geq 1
and for which

k^{-1}(M_{\bm{z}},M_{\bm{z}}^{*})=\sum_{|\beta|=0}^{\infty}c_{|\beta|}\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}M_{\bm{z}}^{\beta}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\beta}=P_{\mathcal{E}}\quad(\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}).

Note that for any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
and
n\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle\langle\sum_{1\leq|\beta|\leq n}c_{|\beta|}\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}M_{\bm{z}}^{\beta}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\beta}h,h\rangle-\langle\sum_{1\leq|\beta|\leq n+1}c_{|\beta|}\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}M_{\bm{z}}^{\beta}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\beta}h,h\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\sum_{|\beta|=n+1}c_{|\beta|}\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}M_{\bm{z}}^{\beta}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\beta}h,h\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\beta|=n+1}-c_{|\beta|}\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}\|M_{\bm{z}}^{*\beta}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Thus,
\{\langle\underset{1\leq|\beta|\leq n}{\sum}c_{|\beta|}\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}M_{\bm{z}}^{\beta}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\beta}h,h\rangle\}_{n}
is a monotonic decreasing sequence of real numbers.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
be an unitarily invariant complete Nevanlinna-Pick space on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Then for any
\Phi\in\mbox{Mult}_{1}(\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E})
, the following are equivalent

(i)

M_{\Phi}
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
,

(ii)

\Phi(0)
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

As before,
(i)
implies
(ii)
is easy to observe. We will prove
(ii)
implies
(i)
.

Note that
\ker A_{M_{\Phi}}
is a
(M_{z_{1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}^{*})
-joint invariant closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
. Similar to the proof of Theorem
4.4
, let us consider the case where
\ker A_{M_{\Phi}}
is a proper subspace that is not
M_{\bm{z}}
-joint reducing. Then by Lemma
4.3
there exists
\eta\in(\ker A_{M_{\Phi}})^{\perp}
such that
M_{z_{i}}^{*}\eta\in\ker A_{M_{\Phi}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Now for any
l\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\zeta_{l}\in\mathcal{E}
and
h_{j,l}\in z_{j}\mathcal{H}
such that

M_{\Phi}^{*l}\eta=\zeta_{l}\oplus\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l},

Thus,
\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|M_{z_{i}}^{*}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}\|=0
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
which further implies that

\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|\sum_{|\beta|=1}c_{|\beta|}\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}M_{\bm{z}}^{\beta}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\beta}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l})\|=0.

From Proposition
4.5
it follows that

\sum_{|\beta|=1}^{\infty}c_{|\beta|}\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}M_{\bm{z}}^{\beta}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\beta}=I_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}}-P_{\mathcal{E}},

and thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}\|=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\langle\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l},\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\langle(I_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}}-P_{\mathcal{E}})(\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}),\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\langle\sum_{|\beta|=1}^{\infty}c_{|\beta|}\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}M_{\bm{z}}^{\beta}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\beta}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}),\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\langle\sum_{1\leq|\beta|\leq m}c_{|\beta|}\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}M_{\bm{z}}^{\beta}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\beta}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}),\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\langle\sum_{|\beta|=1}c_{|\beta|}\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}M_{\bm{z}}^{\beta}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\beta}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}),\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}\rangle

\displaystyle=0\quad(\text{since }\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}\|\leq\eta\text{, for all }l\in\mathbb{N}).

Now,

\displaystyle\|A_{M_{\Phi}}\eta\|=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|A_{M_{\Phi}}M_{\Phi}^{*l}\eta\|=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|A_{M_{\Phi}}(\zeta_{l}\oplus\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l})\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|A_{M_{\Phi}}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l})\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j,l}\|=0,

This implies that
\eta\in\ker A_{M_{\Phi}}
and therefore, the only possibility is
\eta=0
. This is a contradiction to our assumption that
\ker A_{M_{\Phi}}
is proper which further implies that
\ker A_{M_{\Phi}}=\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
, in other words,
M_{\Phi}
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
.
∎

Using Theorem
4.4
, we can establish a result that is analogous to Theorem
1.6
, with respect to certain special tuples of commuting operators. These tuples are indeed special because in a recent paper
[
17
]
, Eschmeier and Langendörfer established a Wold-von Neumann type decomposition theorem for these tuples. Let us give a brief overview of these tuples before moving into our results (see
[
17
]
for more details).

Let
X
be the row operator (denoted by
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})^{n}
) corresponding to a
n
-tuple of commuting operators
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
. Let

X
is said to be

if
\sigma_{X}^{n}(I)\rightarrow 0
in the strong operator topology as

.

Definition 4.7
.

A row operator
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})^{n}
is said to be
regular
at

if there is a real number
\epsilon>0
such that

, the subspace
(X-Z)\mathcal{H}^{n}\subset\mathcal{H}

decomposes into the algebraic direct sum

\mathcal{H}=(X-Z)\mathcal{H}^{n}\oplus\mathcal{W}(X),

where

Z(h_{i})_{i=1}^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}h_{i}
.

The following result is a particular case of [Theorem 3.7, Corollary 3.6,
[

]
].

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})^{n}
be a pure commuting
n
-tuple of operators which is regular at
z=0
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

For some positive integer
m\geq 1
,
X
satisfies the identity
(X^{*}X)^{-1}=(\oplus\Delta_{X})|_{X^{*}\mathcal{H}}
, where
\Delta_{X}=\oplus\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{j}{m\choose j+1}\sigma_{X}^{j}(I_{H})
,

(ii)

X
is unitarily equivalent to
M_{\bm{z}}\in\mathcal{B}(H_{m}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{W}(X)))^{n}
.

In particular, condition
(i)
implies that
X
satisfies the wandering subspace property on
\mathcal{H}
and there exists a unitary
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow H_{m}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{W}(X))
defined by

Uh(\bm{z})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\gamma_{\alpha}P_{\mathcal{W}(X)}L^{\alpha}h\hskip 2.84526pt{\bm{z}}^{\alpha},

where
L:=(X^{*}X)^{-1}X^{*}
. Moreover, the unitary
U
follows the following intertwining property

(4.3)

UX_{i}=(M_{z_{i}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}})U\quad(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}),

and
U(\mathcal{W}(X))=\mathcal{W}(X)
(see [Theorem 3.5,
[
17
]
] for details). We are now ready to state our main result based on these tuples.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})^{n}
be a pure commuting
n
-tuple of operators which is regular at
z=0
and for some positive integer

satisfies
(X^{*}X)^{-1}=(\oplus\Delta_{X})|_{X^{*}\mathcal{H}}
. Let
T
be a contraction on
\mathcal{H}
such that
TX_{i}=X_{i}T
, for all

. Then the following are equivalent:

T
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{H}
,

P_{\mathcal{W}(X)}T|_{\mathcal{W}(X)}
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{W}(X)
.

Proof.

Note that
(i)
implies
(ii)
follows in a manner that is similar to the above results.

(ii)\implies(i):
From equation (
4.3
), it follows that

UTU^{*}(M_{z_{i}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}})=(M_{z_{i}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}})UTU^{*}\quad(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}).

This implies that there exists a contractive multiplier

such that

(see Proposition 4.2,
[

). Now assumption
(b)
implies that

Applications

Recently, the author proved that pair of commuting pure contractions with finite dimensional defect spaces admit pure isometric dilation (see
[
28
]
). The aim of this section is to apply Theorem
1.7
to extend the above result for certain important tuples of commuting pure contractions appearing in recent papers
[
7
]
and
[

]
.

Let

\mathcal{X}^{n}(\mathcal{H}):=\{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):\|X_{i}h\|\leq 1,X_{i}X_{j}=X_{j}X_{i},1\leq i,j\leq n\}

A tuple
X\in\mathcal{X}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be
pure
if
X_{i}
is a pure contraction for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

With respect to the Szegö kernel
\mathbb{S}_{n}(\bm{z},\bm{w})
of the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, let

\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{H}):=\{X\in\mathcal{X}^{n}(\mathcal{H}):\mathbb{S}_{n}^{-1}(X,X^{*})\geq 0\}.

For the sequel, we need a special type of operator-valued multipliers called the
Schur-Agler
class on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
(see
[
2
]
).

Definition 5.1
.

For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
, the Schur-Agler class on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
(denoted by
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{A}_{n}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
) is defined to be the collection of all
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
-valued bounded analytic function on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
for which there exists Hilbert spaces
\{\mathcal{H}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and a unitary

(5.1)

U=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}:\mathcal{E}\oplus(\underset{i=1}{\overset{n}{\oplus}}\mathcal{H}_{i})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}\oplus(\underset{i=1}{\overset{n}{\oplus}}\mathcal{H}_{i}),

such that

\Phi(\bm{z}):=A+BE(\bm{z})(I-DE(\bm{z}))^{-1}C\quad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}),

where
E(\bm{z}):=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}I_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}
.

It follows from a well-known computation that any element in
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{A}_{n}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
is contractive.

Let us begin by observing a result that follows from [Theorem 4.2 and Proposition 4.1,
[
7
]
]. For the sake of completeness we have included the proof of the result.

Lemma 5.2

(
[
7
]
,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
T
be a contraction on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
for which there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
,
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{A}_{n}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
and an isometry
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
(where,
n\geq 1
), such that
\Pi T^{*}=M_{\Psi}^{*}\Pi
and
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\bigvee_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}M_{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}\Pi\mathcal{H}
. If
T
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{H}
, then
\Psi(0)
is a c.n.u. contraction on
\mathcal{K}
.

Proof.

By definition,
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{A}_{n}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
implies that there exist Hilbert spaces
\{\mathcal{H}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and a unitary
U
of the form in equation (
5.1
) such that

\Phi(\bm{z})=A+BE(\bm{z})(I-DE(\bm{z}))^{-1}C\quad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

By the Nagy-Foias decomposition of a contraction there exists an orthogonal decompostion
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{1}
such that

A=\begin{bmatrix}A|_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}&0\\
0&A|_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}\end{bmatrix}:\mathcal{E}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{1},

where,
A|_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}
is a unitary and
A|_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}
is a c.n.u. contraction. With respect to this decomposition of
A
, the transfer function
\Psi(\bm{z})
can be decomposed in the following manner (see [Proposition 3.1,
[
6
]
])

\Psi(z)=\begin{bmatrix}\Psi_{0}(\bm{z})&0\\
0&\Psi_{1}(\bm{z})\end{bmatrix}\quad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}),

where,
\Psi_{0}(\bm{z})=A|_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}
and
\Psi_{1}(\bm{z})=A|_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}+BE(\bm{z})(I-DE(\bm{z}))^{-1}C\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{1})
. The proof will be complete if we can show that
\Pi\mathcal{H}\subseteq H_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, since then
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\bigvee_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}M_{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}\Pi\mathcal{H}\subseteq H_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\subseteq H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
will imply that
\mathcal{E}_{0}=\{0\}
. Now
g\in H_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
implies that there exist
h_{m}\in H_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g=M_{\Psi}^{m}h_{m}
(since
M_{\Psi_{0}}
is a unitary on
H_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
). Thus, for
f\in\Pi\mathcal{H}
we have

|\langle f,g\rangle|=|\langle f,M_{\Psi}^{m}h_{m}\rangle|=|\langle M_{\Psi}^{*m}\Pi h,h_{m}\rangle|=|\langle\Pi X^{*m}h,h_{m}\rangle|\leq\|X^{*n}h\|\|g\|,

Taking limit on both sides as
m\rightarrow\infty
gives
\langle f,g\rangle=0
which implies that
\Pi\mathcal{H}\perp H_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.1.
Tuples associated to von Neumann inequality in polydisc

Let us begin by giving a brief introduction on the tuples of operators appearing in
[
7
]
. A particular class of these tuples were first studied by Grinshpan et al. (
[
18
]
) in connection to multivariate von Neumann inequality. For each
i,p,q\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
p\neq q
, let

\hat{X}_{i}:=\{X\in\mathcal{X}^{n}(\mathcal{H}):(X_{1},\ldots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\ldots,X_{n})\in\mathcal{X}^{n-1}(\mathcal{H})\}.

and

\mathcal{X}_{p,q}^{n}(\mathcal{H}):=\{X\in\mathcal{X}^{n}(\mathcal{H}):\hat{X}_{p},\hat{X}_{q}\in\mathbb{S}_{n-1}(\mathcal{H})\text{ and }\hat{X}_{p}\text{ is pure}\}.

X\in\mathcal{X}_{p,q}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be of
finite rank
if

\mathcal{D}_{\hat{X}_{i}}:=\overline{\mbox{ran}}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathbb{S}^{-1}(\hat{X}_{i},{\hat{X}_{i}}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}},

is a finite dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, for

.
The following result on isometric dilation for these tuples was obtained in [Theorem 5.3,
[
7
]
].

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in\mathcal{X}_{p,q}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
be finite rank, then there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
, inner multiplies
\Phi_{p},\Phi_{q}\in H_{\mathcal{E}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n-1})

and an isometry
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-1})
such that

\Pi X_{i}^{*}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}M_{z_{i}}^{*}\Pi\quad(i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\setminus\{p,q\}),\\
M_{\Phi_{i}}^{*}\Pi\quad(i=p,q),\end{array}\right.

where
\Phi_{p},\Phi_{q}
are inner polynomials of degree atmost one such that

\Phi_{p}(\bm{z})\Phi_{q}(\bm{z})=\Phi_{q}(\bm{z})\Phi_{p}(\bm{z})=z_{p}I_{\mathcal{E}}\quad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n-1}).

In other words,
X
dilates to a
n
-tuple of commuting isometries

(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{p-1}},M_{\Phi_{p}},M_{z_{p+1},}\ldots,M_{z_{q-1}},M_{\Phi_{q}},M_{z_{q+1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})

on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-1})
.

A remarkable contribution of the above result lies in the fact that
M_{\Phi_{p}},M_{\Psi_{q}}
are explicitly determined to be of Berger, Coburn and Lebow type isometries
[
5
]
. In particular, there exists a collection
(\mathcal{E},U,P)
where,
\mathcal{E}
is a Hilbert space,
U
is a unitary and
P
is an orthogonal projection such that

(5.2)

\Phi_{p}(\bm{z})=(P+z_{p}P^{\perp})U^{*};\quad\Phi_{q}(\bm{z})=U(P^{\perp}+z_{p}P).

Using Theorem
1.7
, we can establish the following result on pure isometric dilation.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
be a tuple of commuting pure contractions which belongs to the collection
\mathcal{X}_{p,q}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
and has finite rank. Then there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
such that
X
dilates to a
n
-tuple of commuting pure isometries

(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{p-1}},M_{\Phi_{p}},M_{z_{p+1},}\ldots,M_{z_{q-1}},M_{\Phi_{q}},M_{z_{q+1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})

on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-1})
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
5.2
and Theorem
5.3
we obtain that
\Phi_{p}(0),\Psi_{q}(0)
are c.n.u. contractions on the finite dimensional space
\mathcal{E}
, which further implies that
\Phi(0),\Psi(0)
are pure contractions on
\mathcal{E}
(see
[
21
]
). Now, Theorem
1.7
implies that
M_{\Phi_{p}},M_{\Psi_{q}}
are pure isometries. This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 5.5
.

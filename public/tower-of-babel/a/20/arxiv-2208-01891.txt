Large N N expansion of an integrated correlator in 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM Preprint: RUP-22-18
Yasuyuki Hatsuda, and Kazumi Okuyama

Abstract
Recently Dorigoni, Green and Wen conjectured a remarkable exact formula for an integrated correlator of four superconformal primary operators in 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory. In this work, we investigate its large N N limit in detail. We show that the formula of Dorigoni, Green and Wen can be recast into the sum over the contributions of ( p , q ) (p,q) -strings. Due to the S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℤ ) SL(2,\mathbb{Z}) duality, all the contributions are governed by a single function, typically appearing as the fundamental string contribution. The large order behavior for the perturbative genus expansion of this function allows us to reveal the large N N non-perturbative corrections, which we interpret as the D3-brane instantons in the holographically dual type IIB string theory. The same result is obtained more systematically by using a Laplace-difference equation for the integrated correlator.

Introduction

The holographic duality
between 4d
\mathcal{N}=4

supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory (SYM)
and the type IIB string theory on

In particular, Montonen-Olive duality of
\mathcal{N}=4

;

duality of type IIB string theory

However, implications of the

N
’t Hooft limit

\displaystyle N\to\infty,\qquad g_{\text{YM}}\to 0,\qquad\lambda=g_{\text{YM}}^{2}N:\text{fixed},

(1)

we are focusing on the small

\displaystyle N\to\infty,\qquad g_{\text{YM}}:\text{fixed},

(2)

In recent remarkable papers

;

of
superconformal primary operators in the
{\bf 20^{\prime}}

symmetry of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM. Such integrated correlators were originally introduced in

Chester:2020dja
;

;

The result in

In

Then we focus on the contribution of the
(1,0)

\displaystyle N\to\infty,\qquad g_{\text{YM}}\to\infty,\qquad\widetilde{\lambda}=\frac{16\pi^{2}}{g_{\text{YM}}^{2}}N:\text{fixed}.

(33)

As discussed above,
p(s,N)
should be a solution to (
31
).
Therefore, we substitute the asymptotic series (
32
) into the difference equation (
31
).
To do so, we rewrite
p(s,N\pm 1)
as

\displaystyle p(s,N\pm 1)

\displaystyle=p(s)\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}f_{g}(s)(N\pm 1)^{1+s-2g}

(34)

\displaystyle=p(s)N^{1+s}\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{g}(s)}{N^{2g}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{1+s-2g}{j}\left(\pm\frac{1}{N}\right)^{j}.

In the computation, we need the sum/difference of them, and we have more convenient forms

\displaystyle p(s,N+1)+p(s,N-1)

\displaystyle=2p(s)N^{1+s}\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}N^{-2g}\sum_{h=0}^{g}\binom{1+s-2h}{2g-2h}f_{h}(s),

(35)

\displaystyle p(s,N+1)-p(s,N-1)

\displaystyle=2p(s)N^{s}\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}N^{-2g}\sum_{h=0}^{g}\binom{1+s-2h}{1+2g-2h}f_{h}(s).

After some lengthy computations, we find the following recursion relation:

\displaystyle f_{g}(s)=\sum_{h=0}^{g-1}\frac{(2+4g-2h-s)\Gamma(2+s-2h)}{g(1+2g-2s)\Gamma(3+2g-2h)\Gamma(1+s-2g)}f_{h}(s).

(36)

This equation determines
f_{g}(s)
from
f_{h}(s)
(
0\leq h\leq g-1
). Therefore, starting with
f_{0}(s)=1
, all the functions
f_{g}(s)
are uniquely fixed. One can quickly compute
f_{g}(s)
up to very high genera.

3.2
Pole cancellation

We show a simple consistency test for the large
N
expansion (
29
).
The function
c(s,N)
is defined for arbitrary
s
. However, the coefficients
f_{g}(s)
have poles at
s=3/2,5/2,\dots
, and
p(s)
has poles at
s=-1/2,-3/2,\dots
. This means that
p(s,N)
itself is ill-defined for these values of
s
.
We observe that the combination
p(s,N)+p(1-s,N)
is well-defined for any
s
. All the singular terms in
p(s,N)
are canceled by those in
p(1-s,N)
!
As an example, let us check this property at
s=3/2
.
At order
\mathcal{O}(N^{1/2})
, we find the following singular behaviors:

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 3/2}p(s)N^{1+s}\frac{f_{1}(s)}{N^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3\sqrt{N}}{8\pi^{3/2}(s-3/2)}+\cdots,

(37)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 3/2}p(1-s)N^{2-s}\frac{f_{0}(1-s)}{N^{0}}

\displaystyle=+\frac{3\sqrt{N}}{8\pi^{3/2}(s-3/2)}+\cdots.

Thus the poles at
s=3/2
cancel to each other at this order.
In general we expect that the pole at
s=k+1/2~(k=1,2,\cdots)
is canceled
order by order in the genus expansion

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to k+\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl[p(s)N^{1+s}\frac{f_{g+k}(s)}{N^{2(g+k)}}+p(1-s)N^{2-s}\frac{f_{g}(1-s)}{N^{2g}}\Bigr]=(\text{regular}).

(38)

We have checked this relation for various
k
and
g
.
It would be interesting to find a general proof of this relation.

3.3

The systematic computation of

Here we numerically show that the large

We use the so-called Borel-Padé resummation. Its main advantage is that one can apply it to factorially divergent series.

Let us briefly review the Borel-Padé resummation technique.
Let us consider a formal

\displaystyle F(N)=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\frac{F_{g}}{N^{2g}}.

\displaystyle F_{a}^{\text{Borel}}(N)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\zeta\,e^{-\zeta}\zeta^{a-1}\mathcal{B}_{a}[F]\left(\frac{\zeta}{N}\right)

F_{g}

We apply this procedure to

.
We repeat the computation for various

N

Worldsheet instanton and D-string instanton

4.1
Genus expansion

Let us derive the genus expansion of

\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(\text{0-inst})}(M_{1,0})

We start with the following integral representation
of the full correlator

Collier:2022emf

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{N(N-1)}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}c(s,N)E_{s}^{*}

where

\displaystyle E_{s}^{*}=\Gamma(s)\sum_{(m,n)\neq(0,0)}(M_{m,n})^{-s}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t^{s-1}\Bigl[\Theta(t,\tau)-1\Bigr].

(43)

To proceed, we further use the Fourier expansion of

\displaystyle E_{s}^{*}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\mathrm{i}k\theta}\mathcal{F}_{k}

(44)

where

\mathcal{F}_{k}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &2\Gamma(s)\zeta(2s)(M_{1,0})^{-s}+2\Gamma(1-s)\zeta(2-2s)(M_{1,0})^{s-1},&(k=0),\\
&4|k|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{1-2s}(|k|)\sqrt{\tau_{2}}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi|k|\tau_{2}),&(k\neq 0).\end{aligned}\right.

(45)

Above,
K_{\nu}(x)
denotes the modified Bessel function of

\sigma_{s}(n)
is the divisor function

\displaystyle\sigma_{s}(n)=\sum_{d|n}d^{s}.

(46)

See e.g.
Dorigoni:2021guq
for a derivation of (
45

Our interest is the zero-instanton sector, which is originally given by (

With the help of the integral representation (

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(\text{0-inst})}(M_{1,0})

\displaystyle=\frac{N(N-1)}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}c(s,N)\mathcal{F}_{0}.

(47)

Plugging (

Since there are simple symmetric relations

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}p(1-s,N)\Gamma(1-s)\zeta(2-2s)(M_{1,0})^{s-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{2}-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}p(s,N)\Gamma(s)\zeta(2s)(M_{1,0})^{-s},

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}p(s,N)\Gamma(1-s)\zeta(2-2s)(M_{1,0})^{s-1}

\displaystyle I_{N}

4.2
Worldsheet instanton

After identifying the integration contours, we can discuss the strong coupling expansions.
Let us first consider
G_{g}(\lambda)
.
Our logic in this subsection is as follows. The large
\lambda
expansion of
G_{g}(\lambda)
is Borel non-summable.
This means that the Borel resummation of the large
\lambda
expansion has a discontinuity due to an ambiguity of the resummation.
It is well-known that this discontinuity is related to non-perturbative corrections, which are interpreted as worldsheet instanton corrections in the present case.

The large
\lambda
expansion is obtained by closing the contour along the infinite left semi-circle.
In the following, we will often omit the contour of the
s
-integrals in
G_{g}(\lambda)
and
\widetilde{G}_{g}(\widetilde{\lambda})
if it is the path from
c_{g}-\mathrm{i}\infty
to
c_{g}+\mathrm{i}\infty
.
We first rewrite
G_{g}(\lambda)
in (
58
) as

\displaystyle G_{g}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{ds}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}p(s)f_{g}(s)\Gamma(1-s)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2(n^{2}M_{1,0}N)^{s-1}

(66)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2nT_{F}\int_{0}^{\infty}dwe^{-nA_{F}w}\int\frac{ds}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}p(s)f_{g}(s)\Gamma(1-s)\frac{(4w)^{2-2s}}{\Gamma(3-2s)}

where we used
\zeta(2-2s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2s-2}
and
T_{F}
is defined by

\displaystyle T_{F}=4\sqrt{M_{1,0}N}=2\sqrt{\lambda}.

(67)

At strong coupling, by picking up the poles, this can be written as

\displaystyle G_{g}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2nT_{F}\int_{0}^{\infty}dw\,e^{-nA_{F}w}[\phi_{g}^{+}(w)+\phi_{g}^{-}(w)],

(68)

where
\phi_{g}^{+}(w)
,
\phi_{g}^{-}(w)
are residue contributions from poles on the right/left half
s
-plane, respectively. There are finite number of poles on the strip
0<\text{Re}\,s<c_{g}
, while there are infinite number of poles on the left half
s
-plane.

Since we are interested in the discontinuity, the negative part
\phi_{g}^{-}(w)
is important, which is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{g}^{-}(w)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\beta_{\ell}f_{g}(-\ell-1/2)\frac{(4w)^{2\ell+3}}{\Gamma(2\ell+4)}

(69)

with
\beta_{\ell}
being

\displaystyle\beta_{\ell}=\text{Res}_{s=-\ell-\frac{1}{2}}\left[-\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}p(s)\Gamma(1-s)\right]=\frac{\Gamma\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\ell+\frac{5}{2}\right)\Gamma(2\ell+3)}{2^{4\ell+4}\pi\Gamma^{2}(\ell+1)}.

(70)

As we will see in the next section, the large order behavior of the genus sum is governed by the positive part
\phi_{g}^{+}(w)
.

For the genus-zero part we find the following analytic expression:

\displaystyle\phi_{0}^{-}(w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\beta_{\ell}\frac{(4w)^{2\ell+3}}{\Gamma(2\ell+4)}=-2w^{3}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2},1,w^{2}\Bigr).

(71)

This agrees with the result in
Dorigoni:2021guq
.

\phi_{0}^{-}(w)
has a branch cut along
the positive real
w
-axis running from
w=1
to
w=\infty
.
To see the discontinuity of
\phi_{0}^{-}(w)
across the cut, it is convenient to rewrite it in terms of the complete elliptic integrals

\displaystyle\phi_{0}^{-}(w)=\frac{4w^{3}}{\pi}\Bigl[K(w^{2})-2E(w^{2})\Bigr],

(72)

where
K(w^{2})
and
E(w^{2})
denote the complete elliptic integral of the first and the second kind, respectively.
From the known discontinuity of
K(z)
and
E(z)

\displaystyle K(z+\mathrm{i}0)-K(z-\mathrm{i}0)

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{i}K(1-z),

(73)

\displaystyle E(z+\mathrm{i}0)-E(z-\mathrm{i}0)

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{i}K(1-z)-2\mathrm{i}E(1-z),

we find the discontinuity of
\phi_{0}^{-}(w)

\displaystyle\text{Disc}\,\phi_{0}^{-}(w)

\displaystyle:=\phi_{0}^{-}(w+\mathrm{i}0)-\phi_{0}^{-}(w-\mathrm{i}0)

(74)

\displaystyle=-\frac{8\mathrm{i}w^{3}}{\pi}\Bigl[K(1-w^{2})-2E(1-w^{2})\Bigr]

\displaystyle=4\mathrm{i}w^{3}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2},1,1-w^{2}\Bigr).

This reproduces eq.(5.35) in
Dorigoni:2021guq
.
Then the worldsheet instanton corrections to
G_{0}(\lambda)

is computed by expanding
\text{Disc}\,\phi_{0}^{-}(w)

around

and integrate it term by term

\displaystyle\Delta G_{0}

5.3
Laplace-difference equation

Following
Marino:2008ya
, one can compute the magnetic and the
electric D3-instanton as a trans-series solution of the Laplace-difference equation
obeyed by the exact
\mathcal{G}_{N}

Dorigoni:2021guq

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\tau}-2)\mathcal{G}_{N}=N^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{N+1}+\mathcal{G}_{N-1}-2\mathcal{G}_{N})-N(\mathcal{G}_{N+1}-\mathcal{G}_{N-1}),

(125)

where
\Delta_{\tau}
is the Laplacian
defined in (
5
). Here we show that the Laplace-difference equation fixes both the magnetic D3-instanton and the electric D3-instanton contributions, systematically.

Let us first consider the magnetic D3-instanton.
Since
G^{(\text{mag})}(N,x)
is independent of
\tau_{1}
,
when acting on
G^{(\text{mag})}(N,x)
the Laplacian

\Delta_{\tau}
is replaced by

\displaystyle\tau_{2}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\tau_{2}^{2}}G^{(\text{mag})}(N,x)=\frac{1}{4}\left(x^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}-x\partial_{x}\right)G^{(\text{mag})}(N,x),

(126)

where
x
is related to
\tau_{2}
by

\displaystyle x=\sqrt{\frac{\pi\tau_{2}}{4N}}.

(127)

Thus the magnetic D3-instanton function
should satisfy

\displaystyle\left(\frac{x^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}-x\partial_{x}}{4}-2\right)G^{(\text{mag})}(N,x)

(128)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N^{2}\Bigl[G^{(\text{mag})}(N+1,x_{+})+G^{(\text{mag})}(N-1,x_{-})-2G^{(\text{mag})}(N,x)\Bigr]

\displaystyle-N\Bigl[G^{(\text{mag})}(N+1,x_{+})-G^{(\text{mag})}(N-1,x_{-})\Bigr],

where

\displaystyle x_{\pm}=x(1\pm N^{-1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(129)

At the leading order in the large
N
expansion
of (
128
), the action
A(x)

should satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}x\partial_{x}A(x)=2\sinh\left(\frac{1}{2}A(x)-\frac{1}{4}x\partial_{x}A(x)\right).

(130)

One can check that
A(x)
in (
107
) indeed satisfies
the above equation (
130
).
One can also check that
G^{(\text{mag})}(N,x)
in (
99
)
satisfies (
130
) order by order in the
1/N
expansion.
Alternatively, we can compute
g_{k}(x)
in (
99
)
recursively from the Laplace-difference equation (
130
).
However, the constant term of
g_{k}(x)
is not fixed by the
equation (
130
) alone;
we have to supply the information of
g_{k}(0)=\widetilde{a}_{0,k}
as an input.
With this input, we can compute
g_{k}(x)
recursively up to any
desired order.
Using this result of
g_{k}(x)
, we observed that
the highest and the next highest degree terms of
g_{k}(x)

have the structure

\displaystyle g_{k}(x)=c_{k}\left(x^{4k}+\frac{3k}{2}x^{4k-2}\right)+\mathcal{O}(x^{4k-4}),

where

is determined by the generating function

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k}}{(8N)^{k}}=\frac{1+N}{N}\left(\frac{\Gamma(N+1)}{\sqrt{2\pi N}N^{N}e^{-N}}\right)^{2}.

(132)

Similarly, the electric D3-instanton can be
characterized as a trans-series solution of the following Laplace-difference equation

\displaystyle\left(\frac{y^{2}\partial_{y}^{2}+3y\partial_{y}}{4}-2\right)G^{(\text{ele})}(N,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N^{2}\Bigl[G^{(\text{ele})}(N+1,y_{+})+G^{(\text{ele})}(N-1,y_{-})-2G^{(\text{ele})}(N,y)\Bigr]

\displaystyle-N\Bigl[G^{(\text{ele})}(N+1,y_{+})-G^{(\text{ele})}(N-1,y_{-})\Bigr],

where
y_{\pm}=y(1\pm N^{-1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Again, one can check
that
G^{(\text{ele})}(N,y)
in (
99
)
satisfies (
133

1/N
expansion.
Alternatively, one can compute the coefficient
h_{k}(y)

133

However, as in the case of

133

With this input, we can compute

6
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper, we studied the large
N
limit of the integrated correlator
\mathcal{G}_{N}
. We rewrote the result in
Dorigoni:2021bvj
;
Dorigoni:2021guq
as a sum over
(p,q)
-string contributions. Due to the
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
duality, it is natural that all the
(p,q)
-strings contribute democratically.
We found the recursion relation for
f_{g}(s)
to compute the large
N
expansions of
\mathcal{G}_{N}^{\text{(0-inst)}}(M_{1,0})
up to very high genera.

We showed that the genus expansions
\sum_{g}N^{2-2g}G_{g}(\lambda)
and
\sum_{g}N^{1-2g}\widetilde{G}_{g}(\widetilde{\lambda})
receive non-perturbative corrections,
which correspond to the magnetic D3-instanton correction
G^{(\text{mag})}(N,x)

and
the electric D3-instanton correction
G^{(\text{ele})}(N,y)
, respectively.
The forms of these non-perturbative corrections are constrained by the large order behaviors of
G_{g}(\lambda)
and
\widetilde{G}_{g}(\widetilde{\lambda})
, and given by (
99
). Interestingly,
G^{(\text{mag})}(N,x)
reproduces the D-string instanton correction in the dual ’t Hooft limit
while
G^{(\text{ele})}(N,y)
reproduces the worldsheet instanton correction in the ’t Hooft limit. We confirmed that the same corrections are fixed by the Laplace-difference equation for the integrated correlator.

There are many open questions.
In this paper, we have not studied the D-instanton corrections
in detail
(see appendix
A.3
for the exact
result of one-instanton contribution at finite
N
).
As we emphasized at the end of section
2
,
D-instanton corrections cannot be seen in
the
(p,q)
-string contribution
\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(\text{0-inst})}(M_{p,q})

with fixed
(p,q)
.
We have seen that the
(1,0)
-string contribution
\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(\text{0-inst})}(M_{1,0})

is naturally organized into
G(\lambda)
and
\widetilde{G}(\widetilde{\lambda})
, whose
non-perturbative corrections
e^{-2\sqrt{\lambda}}
and
e^{-2\sqrt{\widetilde{\lambda}}}

are interpreted as the worldsheet instanton and the D-string instanton,
respectively.
However, we do not see the mixed contribution of these
instantons.
On the other hand, in the grand partition function of
ABJM theory on
S^{3}
,
it is known that there are “bound states” of worldsheet instantons and
membrane instantons
Hatsuda:2013gj
.
It turns out that these bound states can be absorbed into the worldsheet instantons
by a certain redefinition of the chemical potential,
which corresponds to the quantum mirror map of the
topological string on local

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

Hatsuda:2013oxa
.
It would be interesting to see if such a structure exists in the
integrated correlator of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM as well.

In a recent paper
Dorigoni:2022zcr
,
the lattice sum representation of the integrated correlator (
4
)
is generalized to the
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM with gauge groups
SO(N)
and
Sp(N)
.
It would be interesting to repeat our analysis with these gauge groups
and see
how the unoriented worldsheet contributions fit into the whole picture.
It would also be interesting to
generalize our analysis to another type of integrated correlator

\partial_{m}^{4}\log Z\big|_{m=0}
.
As discussed in
Chester:2020vyz
;
Collier:2022emf
,
in this correlator there appear
certain generalized Eisenstein series, which satisfy inhomogeneous Laplace
equations on the upper-half
\tau
-plane.
We leave these generalization of our analysis for future works.

Appendix C
Richardson extrapolation

In the numerical study of the asymptotic behavior of
a sequence,
one can use the
standard technique
of Richardson extrapolation
which accelerates the convergence of
sequence towards the leading asymptotics.
Given a sequence
\{S_{m}\}_{m=1,2,\ldots}

\displaystyle S_{m}=s_{0}+\frac{s_{1}}{m}+\frac{s_{2}}{m^{2}}+\cdots,\qquad\lim_{m\to\infty}S_{m}=s_{0},

(181)

its
k
-th Richardson transform is defined as

\displaystyle S_{m}^{(k)}:=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{k+n}(m+n)^{n}S_{m+n}}{n!(k-n)!}.

(182)

After this transformation
the subleading terms in
S_{m}
are canceled up to
m^{-k}
,
i.e.
S_{m}^{(k)}=s_{0}+{\cal O}(m^{-k-1})
and hence
the sequence
S_{m}^{(k)}
has a much faster convergence to
s_{0}

compared to the original
S_{m}
.
However, we lose some data
in this transformation as a cost of the faster convergence:
if we know the original sequence
S_{m}
up to
m=m_{\text{max}}
,
the data of
k
-th Richardson transform
S_{m}^{(k)}

in (
182
)
are available only up to
m=m_{\text{max}}-k
.

One can apply the Richardson extrapolation to estimate
the large order behavior of the perturbative series

\displaystyle G\sim\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}.

(183)

When this series is Borel non-summable, there is an ambiguity
of the form

\displaystyle\Delta G=\pi\mathrm{i}z^{-\frac{b}{2}}e^{-\frac{A}{\sqrt{z}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}z^{n}.

(184)

Then the large
m
behavior of
a_{m}
is related to
\Delta G
as

\displaystyle a_{m}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dz}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z^{m+1}}\Delta G=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}A^{-2m-b+n}\Gamma(2m+b-n).

(185)

As explained in
Marino:2007te
,
one can extract
A,b,f_{n}
by constructing some sequences which converge to
them. First,
A
is extracted from the sequence

\displaystyle A_{m}=2m\sqrt{\frac{a_{m}}{a_{m+1}}}.

(186)

From (
185
) one can see that

A_{m}
approaches
A
as
m\to\infty
. In practice,
A
is estimated numerically
by applying the Richardson extrapolation to the sequence
\{A_{m}\}_{m=1,2,\ldots}
.
Once we find
A
, then we can extract
b
from the sequence

\displaystyle b_{m}=m\left(A^{2}\frac{a_{m+1}}{4m^{2}a_{m}}-1\right)-\frac{1}{2},

(187)

which converges to
b
in the limit
m\to\infty
.
Finally,
f_{n}
can be found from the sequence

\displaystyle f_{n,m}=\frac{1}{A^{-2m-b+n}\Gamma(2m+b-n)}\left[a_{m}-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f_{k}A^{-2m-b+k}\Gamma(2m+b-k)\right],

(188)

which converges to
f_{n}
as
m\to\infty
.
For instance,
f_{0}
can be obtained from the large
m
limit of
f_{0,m}
given by

\displaystyle f_{0,m}=\frac{a_{m}}{A^{-2m-b}\Gamma(2m+b)}.

(189)

Once we know
f_{k}~(0\leq k\leq n-1)
,
one can extract
f_{n}
numerically
from the large
m
asymptotics of the sequence
\{f_{n,m}\}_{m=1,2,\ldots}

using the Richardson extrapolation.

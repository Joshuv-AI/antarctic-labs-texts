1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a classical simple Lie algebra, and
G
be the corresponding adjoint group. We set
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}
to be a nilpotent orbit and
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
to be its universal
G
-equivariant cover. Moreover, let
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}\subset\overline{\mathbb{O}}
be a codimension
2
orbit, and
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}^{\prime}\subset\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
be the preimage of
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}
under the composition
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{O}])\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\overline{\mathbb{O}}
. Here the first map is induced from the covering
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{O}
, and the second is the normalization map. As we show, in many cases
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}^{\prime}
is connected. We note that if
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}^{\prime}
has several connected components, the formal slices to each of the connected components are isomorphic.

Kraft and Procesi in
[
KP82
]
described formal slices to
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}\subset\overline{\mathbb{O}}
, we recall this result in
Theorem

2.2.2
. The main goal of this paper is to provide two different modifications of this famous result. Inspired by results of
[
FJLS15
]
, in
Theorem

2.3.1
we complete the description of the formal slice by adding a symmetry acting on the slice. The second and main goal of this paper is to describe the formal slice to (a connected component of)
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}^{\prime}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
. Note that if
\mathfrak{g}
is of type A, then
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}=\mathbb{O}
, and the description of formal slices is well-known. Therefore, we assume from now on that
\mathfrak{g}
is classical of type B, C or D. We remark that our description includes the symmetry acting on the slice. As an important intermediate step we obtain explicit Lie-theoretic description of partial Namikawa spaces for nilpotent orbit covers. The key idea carried out in
Section

5
is to identify the partial Namikawa space corresponding to a codimension
2
orbit
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}\subset\overline{\mathbb{O}}
with a center of a certain Levi subalgebra of
G
. Thus, we show that this Levi carries all essential information about the slice to
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}^{\prime}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
. In
[
LMM21
, Section 7.5]
this idea gave rise to the notion of an adapted Levi subalgebra. One can view
Section

5
as an explicit computation of adapted Levi subalgebras in classical types.

The plan of the paper is as follows.

In
Section

2
we recall classical facts about nilpotent orbits and state the two main results:
Theorem

2.3.1
and
Theorem

2.4.1
. After that we generalize the notion of Lusztig-Spatenstein induction to covers of the nilpotent orbits. In
Section

3
we recall the description of filtered Poisson deformations of symplectic singularities from
[
Nam11
]
. Namely, filtered Poisson deformations of a symplectic singularity
X
are classified by the points of
\mathfrak{P}/W
, where
\mathfrak{P}
is an affine space, and
W
is a finite group acting on
\mathfrak{P}
. The Namikawa space
\mathfrak{P}
can be recovered from the smooth locus
X^{reg}
and the types of singularities of codimension
2
symplectic leaves in
X
. The main idea of our paper is to compute the space
\mathfrak{P}
for
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
, and deduce the codimension
2
singularities from it. By results of
[
Los22
]
, the space
\mathfrak{P}
for the conical symplectic singularity
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{O}])
can also be described using the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction. In
Section

4
we generalize these results to the covers of nilpotent orbits. In
Section

5
we give an explicit Lie-theoretic description of all partial Namikawa spaces for an affinization of a nilpotent orbit cover and deduce
Theorem

2.3.1
.
In
Section

6
we finish the computation of partial Namikawa spaces for
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
and deduce the singularity of
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}^{\prime}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
, thus proving
Theorem

2.4.1
. Lastly, in
Section

7
we discuss how results of this paper fit into a conjectural symplectic duality between slices between nilpotent orbits.

2.
Nilpotent orbits in classical simple Lie algebras

2.1.
Partitions corresponding to nilpotent orbits

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a classical simple Lie algebra of type B, C or D. We have the following combinatorial description of nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 2.1.1
.

[
CM93
, Theorem 5.1.6]
(1) The nilpotent
O_{n}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{n}
are in one-to-one correspondence with partitions of
n
in which every even part occurs with even multiplicity.

(2) The nilpotent
Sp_{2n}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}_{2n}
are in one-to-one correspondence with partitions of
2n
in which every odd part occurs with even multiplicity.

If

orbit is a single
\Ad(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{n})
orbit. Suppose that
n
is even, let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
be a partition of

\mathbb{O}_{\alpha}
be the corresponding
O_{n}
orbit. The partition
\alpha
is called
very even

satisfies the parity condition (1), and every element of
\alpha

-orbits

\mathbb{O}_{\alpha}^{II}
. If
\alpha
is not very even, the corresponding orbit

is a single
\Ad(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{n})
-orbit.

In the setting of this paper we work with
G
-equivariant covers of
\mathbb{O}
, where

is the corresponding adjoint group. For

be an

-triple in

, and set
\pi_{1}^{G}(\mathbb{O})=Z_{G}(e,h,f)/Z_{G}(e,h,f)^{\circ}

.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
be the partition corresponding to the orbit
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}
. Put
a
to be the number of distinct odd
\alpha_{i}
, and

to be the number of distinct even non-zero

. We have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1.2
.

[
CM93
, Corollary 6.1.6]

(1) If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{2n+1}
, then
\pi_{1}^{G}(\mathbb{O})=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{a-1}
;

(2) If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}_{n}
, then
\pi_{1}^{G}(\mathbb{O})=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{b}
if all even parts have even multiplicity; otherwise
\pi_{1}^{G}(\mathbb{O})=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{b-1}
;

(3) If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{2n}
, then
\pi_{1}^{G}(\mathbb{O})=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\max(0,a-1)}
if all odd parts have even multiplicity; otherwise
\pi_{1}^{G}(\mathbb{O})=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\max(0,a-2)}

2.3.
Kleinian singularities with symmetries

In this subsection we mostly follow the exposition of
[
FJLS15
, Section 1.4.2]
. Let
\Gamma\subset SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be a finite subgroup, and
\Sigma=\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
be the corresponding Kleinian singularity. Let
\rho:\widetilde{\Sigma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\Sigma
be the minimal resolution, and recall that the exceptional divisor
E
of
\rho
is a union of projective lines. The intersection graph of irreducible components of
E
is the Dynkin diagram of a simply laced Dynkin diagram
\Delta
, which we call the Dynkin diagram of
\Gamma
. Any automorphism of
\Sigma
fixes the point
0\in\Sigma
and acts on the irreducible components of the exceptional divisor by permuting them, thus inducing a diagram automorphism of
\Delta
. Thus, we may consider the pairs
(\Sigma,K)
of a Kleinian singularity
\Sigma
and a subgroup
K
of diagram automorphisms of
\Delta
. We say that
K
is a symmetry acting on the singularity
\Sigma
.

In
[
Slo80
, III.6]
Slodowy introduced the following notations for the pairs
(\Sigma,K)

•

B_{k}
for
X
of type
A_{2k-1}
and
K=S_{2}
;

•

C_{k}
for
X
of type
D_{k+1}
and
K=S_{2}
;

•

F_{4}
for
X
of type
E_{6}
and
K=S_{2}
;

•

G_{2}
for
X
of type
D_{4}
and
K=S_{3}
.

The notations are motivated by the following fact. If
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple Lie algebra, then the slice to the subregular orbit in the nilpotent cone is a singularity of the same type as
\mathfrak{g}
. To simplify notations we formally set
B_{0}=A_{1}
.

In
[
FJLS15
]
Fu, Juteau, Levy and Sommers described minimal singularities in nilpotent cones of exceptional Lie algebras and crucially described the symmetry acting on them. We can strengthen the results of

as follows.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be the partitions corresponding to
\mathbb{O}
and
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}

2.4.
Main result

The main goal of this paper is to obtain an analogous result for codimension
2

\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])

\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
is the universal
G
-equivariant cover of
\mathbb{O}
for

The following is the main result of this paper, describing the singularity of

in

.

Let
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}\subset\overline{\mathbb{O}}

(a)
\alpha^{\prime}=(2)

2.5.
Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction

Let
\mathfrak{l}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be a Levi subalgebra, and
\mathbb{O}_{L}\subset\mathfrak{l}
be a nilpotent orbit. We include
\mathfrak{l}
into a parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{l}\ltimes\mathfrak{n}
, and set

to be the corresponding parabolic subgroup of

. Following
[
LS79
]
, we consider the
G
-equivariant moment map
\mu:G\times^{P}(\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{L}\times\mathfrak{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}
. The image contains a unique open

\mathbb{O}
. We say that
\mathbb{O}
is

from
\mathbb{O}_{L}
, and call

for the pair (
\mathbb{O}
,
\mathbb{O}_{L}
). Moreover, if
\mu
is birational then
\mathbb{O}
is called

from
\mathbb{O}_{L}
(see
[
Los22
]
). If the orbit
\mathbb{O}
cannot be birationally induced from a proper Levi subalgebra, we say that
\mathbb{O}
is

.

Let us explain the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction on the level of partitions following
[
CM93
]
. Recall that
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
B
,
C
or
D
. We will use notation
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
to denote the simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{n}
or
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}_{n}
respectively, and
G_{n}
to denote the corresponding adjoint group. Denote by
\mathcal{P}(n)
the set of partitions appearing in
2.1.1
for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
, and say that
l
has the same parity as
\mathfrak{g}
, if
(-1)^{l}=\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{g}}
, where
\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{g}}=1
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{n}
, and
\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{g}}=-1
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}_{n}
. In other words, a partition
\alpha
of
n
corresponds to a nilpotent orbit (or two very even orbits in type
D
) in
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if all parts of
\alpha
of the same parity as
\mathfrak{g}
occur with even multiplicity.

Recall the following well-known fact.

Lemma 2.5.1
.

[
CM93
, Lemma 3.8.1]

Every Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{n}
is
G
-conjugate to a Levi subalgebra of the form
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{t_{1}}\times\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{t_{2}}\times\ldots\times\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{t_{p}}\times\mathfrak{g}_{n-2\sum_{i}t_{i}}
. Here we allow
n-2\sum_{i}t_{i}
is
0
or
1
, which corresponds to the Levi
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{t_{1}}\times\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{t_{2}}\times\ldots\times\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{t_{p}}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{n}
.

By
Lemma

2.5.1
, any induction can be obtained as a sequence of inductions from the Levi subalgebras of form
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{m}\times\mathfrak{g}_{k-2m}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{k}
. Let us recall the combinatorial data associated with such induction. Let
\mathbb{O}_{L}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{k-2m}
be a nilpotent orbit. Formally
\mathbb{O}_{L}
is a nilpotent orbit in a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{k-2m}
, but to avoid clogging the formulas with the zero orbits we always identify it with the nilpotent orbit
\{0\}\times\mathbb{O}_{L}\subset\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{m}\times\mathfrak{g}_{n-2m}=\mathfrak{l}
. Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(k-2m)
be the corresponding partition. Let
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be an orbit induced from the orbit
\mathbb{O}_{L}\subset\mathfrak{l}
, and
\beta\in\mathcal{P}(n)
be the corresponding partition. Consider the partition
\alpha^{m}
of
n
defined by
\alpha^{m}_{i}=\alpha_{i}+2
for
i\leq m
and
\alpha^{m}_{i}=\alpha_{i}
for
i>m
.

Proposition 2.5.2
.

[
CM93
, Theorem 7.3.3]

There are two possible cases:

1)
\alpha^{m}\in\mathcal{P}(n)
. Then
\beta=\alpha^{m}
.

2)
\alpha^{m}\notin\mathcal{P}(n)
. Then
\beta=(\alpha_{1}+2,\ldots,\alpha_{m-1}+2,\alpha_{m}+1,\alpha_{m+1}+1,\alpha_{m+2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
. We call
\beta
the collapse of
\alpha^{m}
. Then
\alpha_{m}=\alpha_{m+1}
, and
\alpha_{m}
has the same parity as
\mathfrak{g}
. Consequently,
\beta_{m}=\beta_{m+1}
, and
\beta_{m}
has the opposite parity to
\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 2.5.3
.

Consider the orbit
\mathbb{O}_{L}\subset\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{11}
corresponding to the partition
\alpha=(7,2,2)
. Let
\mathbb{O}
be the orbit in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{15}
induced from
\{0\}\times\mathbb{O}_{L}\subset\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{2}\times\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{11}
. Then
\alpha^{2}=(9,4,2)\notin\mathcal{P}(15)
, because even numbers
4
and
2
occur once. The orbit
\mathbb{O}
corresponds to the collapse
\beta=(9,3,3)
.

We can extend the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction to the covers of orbits in the following way. Let
\mathbb{O}_{L}\subset\mathfrak{l}
be an adjoint orbit,
L\subset G
be the Levi subgroup with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}
, and
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
be an
L
-equivariant cover of
\mathbb{O}_{L}
. Consider the
G
-equivariant moment map
\mu:G\times^{P}(\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L}])\times\mathfrak{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}
. The image contains a unique open
G
-orbit
\mathbb{O}
that is induced from
\mathbb{O}_{L}
. Set
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}=\mu^{-1}(\mathbb{O})
. Since
\mu
is
G
-equivariant,
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
is a
G
-equivariant cover of
\mathbb{O}
. We say that
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
is
birationally induced
from
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
. If the cover
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
cannot be birationally induced from a proper Levi subalgebra, we say that
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
is
birationally rigid
.

3.
Generalities on symplectic singularities and their deformations

3.1.
Filtered Poisson deformations

Let
A
be a finitely generated Poisson algebra equipped with a grading
A=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}A_{i}
such that
A_{0}=\mathbb{C}
, and the Poisson bracket has degree
-d
, where
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a filtered commutative algebra equipped with a Poisson bracket decreasing the filtration degree by
d
. Moreover, suppose that we have an isomorphism
\theta:\gr\mathcal{A}\simeq A
of Poisson algebras. Such pair
(\mathcal{A},\theta)
is called a
filtered Poisson deformation
of
A
.

By a Poisson scheme we mean a scheme
X
over
\Spec(\mathbb{C})
whose structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is equipped with a Poisson bracket. For example, for any finitely generated Poisson algebra
A
the affine scheme
X=\Spec(A)
is a Poisson scheme. By a filtered Poisson deformation of
X
we understand a sheaf
\mathcal{D}
of filtered Poisson algebras complete and separated with respect to the filtration together with an isomorphism of sheaves of Poisson algebras
\theta:\gr\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

3.2.
Symplectic singularities

Let
X
be a normal Poisson algebraic variety such that the regular locus
X^{reg}
is a symplectic variety. Let
\omega^{reg}
be the symplectic form on
X^{reg}
. Suppose that there exists a projective resolution of singularities
\rho:\widehat{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}X
such that
\rho^{*}(\omega^{reg})
extends to a regular (not necessarily symplectic) form on
X
. Following Beauville
[
Bea00
]
, we say that
X
has symplectic singularities. Recall that we have the following example.

Example 3.2.1
.

[
Pan91
]

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Lie algebra and
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be a nilpotent orbit. Then
X=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{O}])
has symplectic singularities.

We have the following generalization of
Example

3.2.1
. The proof appeared earlier in
[
Los21
, Lemma 2.5]
).

Proposition 3.2.2
.

Let
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
be a connected
G
-equivariant cover of
\mathbb{O}
. The variety
\widehat{X}=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
has symplectic singularities.

We say that an affine Poisson variety
X
is
conical
if
\mathbb{C}[X]
is endowed with a grading
\mathbb{C}[X]=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{C}[X]_{i}
such that
\mathbb{C}[X]_{0}=\mathbb{C}
, and there exist a positive integer
d
such that for any
i,j
and
f\in\mathbb{C}[X]_{i}
,
g\in\mathbb{C}[X]_{j}
we have
\{f,g\}\in\mathbb{C}[X]_{i+j-d}
. Note that we have gradings on
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{O}]
,
\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}]
, such that
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{O}])
and
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
are conical with
d=2
. For
\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}]
see discussion before Theorem
1
in
[
BK92
]
.

3.3.
The Namikawa space
\mathfrak{P}

Recall that a normal algebraic variety
\widetilde{X}
is called
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial if for any Weil divisor it has a nonzero integral multiple that is Cartier.

Proposition 3.3.1
.

[
Los19
, Proposition 2.1]

Let
X
have symplectic singularities. Then there is a birational projective morphism
\rho:\widetilde{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}X
, where
\widetilde{X}
has the following properties:

(a)
\widetilde{X}
is an irreducible, normal, Poisson variety (and hence has symplectic singularities).

(b)
\widetilde{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.

(c)
\widetilde{X}
has terminal singularities.

If
X
is, in addition, conical, then
\widetilde{X}
admits a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action such that
\rho
is
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-equivariant.

Remark 3.3.2
.

In
[
Nam01
]
Namikawa has shown that modulo (a), condition (c) is equivalent to
\codim_{\widetilde{X}}\widetilde{X}/\widetilde{X}^{reg}\geq 4
. In what follows, we will check this condition instead of verifying (c).

Such
\widetilde{X}
is called a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization of
X
. We recall the
Namikawa space
for
X
that is defined as
\mathfrak{P}(X)=H^{2}(\widetilde{X}^{reg},\mathbb{C})
. When the conical symplectic singularity
X
in question is clear, we write
\mathfrak{P}
for
\mathfrak{P}(X)
.

We want to restate the definition of
\mathfrak{P}
in terms of
X
without using a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization. Let
\mathfrak{L}_{1},\mathfrak{L}_{2},\ldots,\mathfrak{L}_{k}
be the codimension
2
symplectic leaves of
X
. Let
\Sigma_{i}^{\wedge}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\wedge 0}/\Gamma_{i}
be the formal slice to
\mathfrak{L}_{i}
. Let
\Sigma_{i}
be
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma_{i}
,
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{i}
be its minimal resolution, and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}=H^{2}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{i},\mathbb{C})
. Recall that we have an ADE classification of finite subgroups of
Sp(2,\mathbb{C})
, and by McKay correspondence the affine space
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}
is isomorphic to the Cartan space of the Lie algebra of the same type. Let
\widetilde{W}_{i}
be the corresponding Weyl group acting on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}
. We have the natural monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{L}_{i})
on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}
and
\widetilde{W}_{i}
by diagram automorphisms. Set
\mathfrak{P}_{i}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}^{\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{L}_{i})}
,
W_{i}=\widetilde{W}_{i}^{\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{L}_{i})}
to be the fixed points of this action. We have the following description of the space
\mathfrak{P}
due to Namikawa
[
Nam11
]
and Losev
[
Los22
]
.

Proposition 3.3.3
.

We have
\mathfrak{P}=H^{2}(X^{reg},\mathbb{C})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{P}_{i}
.

We call
\mathfrak{P}
the
Namikawa space
,
\mathfrak{P}_{0}=H^{2}(X^{reg},\mathbb{C})
the
smooth part of the Namikawa space
, and
\mathfrak{P}_{i}
the
partial Namikawa space
for the symplectic leaf
\mathfrak{L}_{i}
.
Recall the Namikawa-Weyl group, that is
W=\prod_{i=1}^{k}W_{i}
. The importance of
\mathfrak{P}
and
W
is explained by the following fact.

Proposition 3.3.4
.

[
Nam10
]

The isomorphism classes of filtered Poisson deformations of
\mathbb{C}[X]
are classified by the points of
\mathfrak{P}/W
.

4.
Filtered Poisson deformations of
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])

4.1.
\mathbb{Q}
-terminalization of
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])

In
[
Los22
]
Losev computed the Namikawa space
\mathfrak{P}
for
X=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{O}])
. In this section we extend his result to
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
for any
G
-equivariant cover
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
of the orbit
\mathbb{O}
.

Proposition 4.1.1
.

Suppose that
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
is birationally rigid and let
X:=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
. Then
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and terminal.

Remark 4.1.2
.

For the trivial cover
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}\simeq\mathbb{O}
the proposition is known by the works of Namikawa
[
Nam09
]
and Losev
[
Los22
]
.

In
[
Nam22
, Corollary 2.4, Proposition 3.4, Proposition 3.6]
Namikawa proved that
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal for the universal covering
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
of an orbit
\mathbb{O}
with some conditions on the corresponding partition
\alpha
. One can check that if
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
is birationally rigid then
\alpha
satisfies the conditions from loc.cit.

Proof.

We follow the approach of
[
Los22
, Proposition 4.5]
, this argument can be easily generalized to the case of
G
-equivariant covers. First, note that
X^{reg}\backslash\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
is of complex codimension at least
2
in
X^{reg}
, so
H^{2}(X^{reg},\mathbb{C})=H^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L},\mathbb{C})
.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{P}=0
. Let
x\in\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
be a point, and
Z_{G}(x)
be the stabilizer of
x
. As in loc.cit.
H^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{O}},\mathbb{C})=0
implies
\Hom(Z_{G}(x),\mathbb{C}^{\times})
is finite, so by
[
Fu10
, Lemma 4.1]

X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. If
\mathfrak{P}=0
, then
X
has no symplectic leaves of codimension
2
, and by
3.3.2

X
is terminal. It remains to show that
\mathfrak{P}=0
.

In
[
Nam11
]
Namikawa constructed a universal conical Poisson deformation
X_{\mathfrak{P}}
of
X
over
\mathfrak{P}/W
. Pick Zariski generic
\lambda\in\mathfrak{P}/W
, and let
X_{\lambda}
be the fiber over the point
\lambda
, and
\mu_{\lambda}:X_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}
be the moment map. The
G
-action on
X_{\lambda}
has an open orbit that we denote by
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{\lambda}
, and the restriction of
\mu_{\lambda}
to the open orbit is a covering map
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}
for some adjoint orbit
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}
. Let
\xi
be the semisimple part of an element in
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}
and set
\mathfrak{l}
to be the centralizer of
\xi
in
\mathfrak{g}
. We set
L
and
P
to be the corresponding Levi and parabolic subgroups. The argument of Step 3 in
[
Los22
, Proof of Proposition 4.5]
implies that
\xi\neq 0
, and therefore
L\subset G
is a proper Levi subgroup. Let
\mathbb{O}_{L}\subset\mathfrak{l}
be a nilpotent orbit, such that
\xi+\mathbb{O}_{L}\subset\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}
. Choose
\eta\in\mathbb{O}_{L}
and write
\mathbb{O}_{L,\lambda}
for the
L
-orbit of
\xi+\eta\in\mathfrak{l}
. The orbit
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}
is induced from
\mathbb{O}_{L,\lambda}
.

Note that by the construction,
\xi
is semisimple,
\eta
is nilpotent and
[\xi,\eta]=0
.
Since
Z_{G}(\eta+\xi)\subset Z_{G}(\xi)=L
, we have
Z_{L}(\eta+\xi)=Z_{G}(\eta+\xi)
. Since
\pi_{1}^{G}(\mathbb{O}_{\lambda})=Z_{G}(\eta+\xi)/Z_{G}(\eta+\xi)^{\circ}
, it follows that
\pi_{1}^{G}(\mathbb{O}_{\lambda})=\pi_{1}^{L}(\mathbb{O}_{L,\lambda})
, and any
G
-equivariant cover of
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}
can be birationally induced from the corresponding

-equivariant cover of
\mathbb{O}_{L,\lambda}
.

Therefore
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{\lambda}
is birationally induced from some cover
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L,\lambda}
. Since
Z_{L}(\eta+\xi)=Z_{L}(\eta)
, we can identify the components group
Z_{L}(\eta+\xi)/Z_{L}(\eta+\xi)^{\circ}
and
Z_{L}(\eta)/Z_{L}(\eta)^{\circ}
. Let
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
be the cover of
\mathbb{O}_{L}
corresponding to the cover
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L,\lambda}
.

We set
X_{L}=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L}])
, and let
\widetilde{X}_{L}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-terminalization of
X_{L}
. Consider
\widetilde{X}_{\mathbb{C}\xi}=G\times^{P}(\mathbb{C}\xi\times\widetilde{X}_{L}\times\mathfrak{n})
. That is a normal Poisson scheme over
\mathbb{C}\xi
with a Hamiltonian
G
-action, and the fiber over

is
\widetilde{X}^{1}=G\times^{P}(\widetilde{X}_{L}\times\mathfrak{n})
. Let
X_{\mathbb{C}\xi}=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{X}_{\mathbb{C}\xi}])
. Note that the fiber of
X_{\mathbb{C}\xi}
over the point

is
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L,\lambda}])=X_{\lambda}
. The fiber over

is

. The open

-orbit in

is a finite

-equivariant cover
\check{\mathbb{O}}
of
\mathbb{O}
, and

. Since
X_{\mathbb{C}\xi}
and

are flat deformations, we have
\mathbb{C}[X^{1}]\simeq\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]
and

as

, and therefore
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
is not birationally rigid, so we get a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.1.3
.

There is a unique pair of a Levi algebra
\mathfrak{l}
and a birationally rigid cover
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
of an orbit
\mathbb{O}_{L}\subset\mathfrak{l}
, such that
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
is birationally induced from
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
. The pair
(\mathfrak{l},\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L})
is called the

of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
. The space

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-terminalization of
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
, and the Namikawa space
\mathfrak{P}
equals to
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
.

Proof.

The proof is completely analogous to
[
Los22
, Corollary 4.6 and Proposition 4.7]
. The only new thing we have to show is that there exists a birational map
\rho:G\times^{P}(\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L}])\times\mathfrak{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
. Consider the Stein factorization of the generalized Springer map

. We note that since

is affine and normal. It follows that

∎

Remark 4.1.4
.

For the universal cover

of

Corollary 4.1.5
.

Suppose that the cover
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
is birationally induced from a birationally rigid cover
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
of the orbit
\mathbb{O}_{L}\subset\mathfrak{l}
for some

. Note that the only birationally rigid orbit in

is the

cannot be birationally induced from

.

Suppose that

can be birationally induced from the cover

of an orbit

4.2.
Birationality criteria for Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction

In this section we study when the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction on orbits is birational and use it to determine whether a given cover of an orbit can be birationally induced. Let us first introduce the notion of
\alpha
-singularity.

Let
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent orbit, and
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(n)
be the corresponding partition. For every
m\leq n
let
d_{m}=\left[\frac{\alpha_{m}-\alpha_{m+1}}{2}\right]
. We say that an integer
m
is
\alpha
-
singular
if
\alpha_{m}-\alpha_{m+1}\geq 2
, or equivalently,
d_{m}\geq 1
.
Theorem

2.2.2
implies that
\alpha
-singular numbers are in
1
-to-
1
correspondence with partitions of codimension
2
orbits in
\overline{\mathbb{O}}
. We note that one
\alpha
-singular number may correspond to two very even orbits in type
D
, the partitions of these orbits are differed only by the indices
I
,
II
. The singularities to these two orbits are equivalent. We write
\mathbb{O}_{m}
for the union of
\mathbb{O}_{m}^{I}\sqcup\mathbb{O}_{m}^{II}
, and define the slice to
\mathbb{O}_{m}\subset\overline{\mathbb{O}}
by considering the slice to any of the two connected components. In combinatorial terms, we ignore the indices of the partitions. We denote the set of
\alpha
-singular numbers by
\mathcal{S}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 4.2.1
.

Suppose that an orbit
\mathbb{O}
corresponding to the partition
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(n)
is induced from a nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}_{L}
in the Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}
, such that the restriction of
\mathbb{O}_{L}
to each
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}
-factor of
\mathfrak{l}
is the
\{0\}
orbit.

Then
\mathfrak{l}
is
G
-conjugate to
\prod_{i}\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{i}^{k_{i}}\times\mathfrak{g}_{n-\sum_{i}2ik_{i}}
, where
k_{i}\leq d_{i}+1
. Moreover,
k_{i}=d_{i}+1
only if
\alpha_{i-1}>\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i+1}+2k_{i}>\alpha_{i+2}
, and
\alpha_{i}
has the opposite parity to
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

2.5.1
,
\mathfrak{l}
is
G
-conjugate to
\prod_{i}\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{i}^{k_{i}}\times\mathfrak{g}_{n-2\sum ik_{i}}
for some
\{k_{i}\}
. Then
\mathbb{O}
is induced from an orbit
\mathbb{O}_{L^{\prime}}=\{0\}^{k_{i}}\times\mathbb{O}_{L^{\prime}}\subset\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{i}^{k_{i}}\times\mathfrak{g}_{n-2ik_{i}}
, and let
\beta\in\mathcal{P}(n-2ik_{i})
be the partition of
\mathbb{O}_{L^{\prime}}
. Then by
2.5.2
we have
\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{i+1}\geq(\beta_{i}+2k_{i}-1)-(\beta_{i}+1)=\beta_{i}-\beta_{i+1}+2k_{i}-2\geq 2k_{i}-2
. Thus,
k_{i}\leq d_{i}+1

As in

5.
Computing the partial Namikawa spaces

Let us explain the main idea of this paper. Let
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
be a connected
G
-equivariant cover of
\mathbb{O}
, and let
\widehat{X}=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
. In most cases the singularity of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}\subset\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
can be determined by the dimension of the partial Namikawa space space
\mathfrak{P}_{m}=\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\widehat{X})
corresponding to the (connected components of)
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
. Moreover, since
\mathfrak{P}_{m}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}_{m}^{\pi_{1}(\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m})}
, this computation leads to understanding the symmetry of the singularity. In
Section

3.3
the description of the spaces
\mathfrak{P}_{m}
is given purely in terms of the slice to
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}\subset\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
. To achieve our results we need a Lie-theoretic description of
\mathfrak{P}_{m}
that can be deduced from the partition
\lambda
corresponding to
\mathbb{O}
. In the language of
[
LMM21
, Section 7.5]
we explicitly compute the Levi subalgebra adapted to
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
. Let us state the main result, the rest of this technical section is devoted to the proof of it.

Let
(\mathfrak{l},\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{L})
be the birational induction datum corresponding to
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
. By
Lemma

2.5.1
,
\mathfrak{l}\simeq\prod_{i}\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{i}^{t_{i}}\times\mathfrak{g}_{n-\sum_{i}2it_{i}}
.
Lemma

4.2.1
implies that we can rewrite it in the form
\mathfrak{l}\simeq\prod_{i\in S(\alpha)}\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{i}^{k_{i}}\times\prod_{i\in S}\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{i}\times\mathfrak{g}_{n-\sum_{i}2it_{i}}
for some finite set
S
, such that
S\cap S(\alpha)=\emptyset
.

Proposition 5.0.1
.

Using the notations above, we have the following description of the partial Namikawa spaces of
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
.

•

\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}=k_{m}
for all
m\in S(\alpha)
;

•

\dim\mathfrak{P}_{0}=|S|
.

For the sake of clarity, we prefer to explain the result for
X=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{O}])
first. It shows all the ideas of the general case in a setting, where we can avoid the overuse of diacritics.

5.1.
Computing the spaces
\mathfrak{P}_{i}
from the partition
\alpha

Recall from
Section

4.2
the set of
\alpha
-singular elements
\mathcal{S}(\alpha)
, and the numbers
d_{i}
for all
i\leq n
. For

let
\mathbb{O}_{m}
be the corresponding codimension
2
orbit, and
\mathfrak{P}_{m}
be the corresponding partial Namikawa space. When
\mathbb{O}_{m}=\mathbb{O}_{m}^{I}\sqcup\mathbb{O}_{m}^{II}
is the union of two very even orbits we define
\mathfrak{P}_{m}=\mathfrak{P}_{m}^{I}\sqcup\mathfrak{P}_{m}^{II}
, where
\mathfrak{P}_{m}^{*}
is the partial Namikawa space corresponding to the symplectic leaf
\mathbb{O}_{m}^{*}\subset\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{O}])
.

The first step to understand
\mathfrak{P}_{m}
is to explicitly compute the Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}
, such that
\mathbb{O}
is birationally induced from a birationally rigid nilpotent orbit in

Proposition 5.1.1
.

Let

Consider the partition

obtained in the following way. For every number

be the set of

such that

. Define

. It is easy to see that

. We define

are birationally induced from orbits

and

. Suppose that
\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}
is not a cyclic group, then
d_{m}=2k+1
,
k\geq 1
, and
\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}
is the dihedral group of order
4k
. Then
V\cap{V}_{\widehat{X}}=Z(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])\cap Z(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}])=Z(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}])^{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}
, where
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
corresponds to the quotient
\Gamma/\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}
. It is easy to see that the action of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
on
Z(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}])
is not trivial. Then we have
\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\widehat{X})<\dim H^{2}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\widehat{X}})=\dim V_{\widehat{X}}\leq k+2\leq d_{m}-1
, and we get a contradiction.

Thus,
\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}
is a cyclic group of order
{2(d_{m}-1)}
. Since
\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\widehat{X})=d_{m}-1
, the monodromy action on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}_{m}(\widehat{X})
is non-trivial.

∎

It remains to consider the case when
k_{m}=d_{m}+1
, and
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
is a
2
-fold cover of
\mathbb{O}
. This is possible if and only if
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
C
, and
\alpha_{m}
and
\alpha_{m+1}
both appear with multiplicity
1
.

•

If
(\alpha,\beta)
is of type (a), then
d_{m}=1
. The singularity of
\mathbb{O}_{m}\subset\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{O}])
is of type
A_{1}
, and therefore its preimage under the map
\mu:\widehat{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{O}])
is either a union of two singularities of type
A_{1}
, or is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Since
\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\widehat{X})=2
, it must be the former, and the two preimages of a point
x\in\mathbb{O}_{m}
must be in different connected components of
\mu^{-1}(\mathbb{O}_{m})
, thus
\mu^{-1}(\mathbb{O}_{m})
is a disconnected
2
-fold cover of
\mathbb{O}_{m}
.

•

If
(\alpha,\beta)
is of type (b), then
\Gamma
is a dihedral group of order
4(d_{m}-1)
. Assume that
\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}\subset\Gamma
is a subgroup of index
2
. Then
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}\simeq\mathbb{O}_{m}
.

Since
\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\widehat{X})\simeq\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{m}^{d_{m}+1})
,
5.1.3
implies that all deformation directions from
\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)
are common deformation directions for
\Sigma
and
\Sigma_{\widehat{X}}
.

If
\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}
is a cyclic group of order
2(d_{m}-1)
, then the singularity with symmetry
(\Sigma_{\widehat{X}},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
is of type
B_{d_{m}-1}
, see
[
Slo80
, Section 6.2]
. Using the same notations as above,
\dim V\cap{V}_{\widehat{X}}\leq d_{m}-1
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
C
, and
\alpha_{m}
and
\alpha_{m+1}
both appear with multiplicity
1
,
5.1.3
implies that
\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)=d_{m}
. We got a contradiction.

If
\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}
is a dihedral group of order
2(d_{m}-1)
for
d_{m}=2t+1
, then
\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\widehat{X})\leq t+2\leq d_{m}
, and we get a contradiction.

Thus,
\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}=\Gamma
. It remains to show that
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
is a connected cover of
\mathbb{O}_{m}
. Indeed, otherwise, we have
\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\widehat{X})\geq 2d_{m}>d_{m}+1
. Since
\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\widehat{X})=\dim V_{\widehat{X}}
, it implies that the mondromy action is trivial.

6.3.
Singularities of the affinization of the universal cover

Let
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent orbit,
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
be the universal
G
-equivariant cover of
\mathbb{O}
,
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{m}\subset\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
be the preimage of the orbit
\mathbb{O}_{m}
under the natural map
\mu:\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\overline{\mathbb{O}}
. Let
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
be any
G
-equivariant cover of the
m
-determining cover of
\mathbb{O}
, such that
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
is connected, and the singularity (together with its symmetry) of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}\subset\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
is equivalent to the one for the
m
-determining cover of
\mathbb{O}
.

Lemma 6.3.1
.

Let
\check{\mathbb{O}}
be a
2
-fold cover of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
, and
\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m}\subset\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\check{\mathbb{O}}])
be the associated cover of
\mathbb{O}_{m}
. Then:

1)

\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
is a
2
-fold cover of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
;

2)

\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
is a connected cover of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
, and the singularity of
\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\check{\mathbb{O}}])
is equivalent to the one of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
.

Proof.

1) The
2
-fold cover
\check{\mathbb{O}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
induces a surjective map
f:\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\check{\mathbb{O}}])\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
. Therefore,
\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
is a cover of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
. Suppose that
\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m}\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
. Let the singularity of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
be equivalent to
\Sigma_{\widehat{X}}=\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}
. Then, analogously to
Lemma

6.2.1
, the singularity of
\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\check{\mathbb{O}}])
is equivalent to
\Sigma_{\check{X}}=\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma_{\check{X}}
, where
\Gamma_{\check{X}}\subset\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}
is a subgroup of index
2
.

We note that
\check{\mathbb{O}}
is a cover of the
m
-determining cover, and
\check{\mathbb{O}}
is covered by
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\check{X})\simeq\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\widehat{X})
. The singularity
\Sigma_{\widehat{X}}
was already computed, we analyze each case separately to show a contradiction.

•

If
\Sigma_{\widehat{X}}
is of type
A_{1}
, then
\Gamma_{\check{X}}=1
, and therefore
\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\check{X})=0
.

•

If
\Sigma_{\widehat{X}}
is of type
D_{d_{k}}
(with trivial mondoromy), then the analysis is analogous to the situation when
k_{m}=d_{m}+1
, and
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
is a
2
-fold cover of
\mathbb{O}
studied above.

•

If
\Sigma_{\widehat{X}}
is of type
C_{k}
, the analysis is analogous to the one in
6.2.2
.

•

If
\Sigma_{\widehat{X}}
is of type
B_{k}
, then
\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}
is a cyclic group of order
2k
, the only subgroup
\Gamma_{\check{X}}
of index
2
is the cyclic group of order
k
, so
\Sigma_{\check{X}}
is of type
A_{k-1}
. Thus,
\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\check{X})\leq k-1<\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\widehat{X})
.

2) Suppose that
\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m}=\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m,1}\sqcup\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m,2}
is disconnected, but
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
is connected. Then each
\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m,i}
is isomorphic to
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
. We denote the corresponding partial namikawa spaces by
\mathfrak{P}_{m,i}(\check{X})
. Analogously to
Lemma

6.2.1
, the singularity of both copies of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\check{\mathbb{O}}])
is equivalent to
\Sigma=\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma_{\widehat{X}}
. Since
\check{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\widehat{X}
is étale over
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}\cup\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
, one can show that the monodromy actions of
\pi_{1}(\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m})
on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}_{m,i}(\check{X})
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}_{m}(\widehat{X})
coincide. Thus,
\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\check{X})=2\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m,1}(\check{X})=2\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\widehat{X})
. By
6.1.2
,
\dim\check{\mathfrak{P}}_{m}=\dim\widehat{\mathfrak{P}}_{m}
, so we get a contradiction. Therefore
\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
is connected, and the singularity of
\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\check{\mathbb{O}}])
is equivalent to the one of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
. Coincidence of the dimenions of partial Namikawa spaces implies that the symmetries of the two singularities are equivalent.

3) Assume that
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
is disconnected. Then
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
has two connected components, and the singularity of each of them in
\widehat{X}
is of type
A_{1}
. Then
k_{m}=2
. Since there are no non-trivial proper subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, and
\dim\mathfrak{P}_{m}(\check{X})=2
,
\check{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
has two irreducible components, and singularity to each of them is of type
A_{1}
.
∎

Since the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
of the
m
-determining cover
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
can be obtained by a sequence of
2
-fold covers we have the following corollary.

Corollary 6.3.2
.

The cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
has the same number of connected components as
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
, and the singularity (with symmetry) of each of the connected components of
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
is equivalent to the one of a connected component of
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}_{m}
in
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widehat{\mathbb{O}}])
, where
\widehat{\mathbb{O}}
is an
m
-determining cover of
\mathbb{O}
.

6.3.2
together with the computation from
Section

6.2
imply
Theorem

2.4.1
.

7.
Minimal special degenerations and symplectic duality

In this section we discuss how results of this paper fit in a list of observations and conjectures regarding symplectic duality. For the mathematical definition of symplectic duality we refer to
[
BLPW16
]
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
be its Langlands dual, and
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathcal{N}^{\vee}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
be the respective nilpotent cones. Recall from
[
BV85
]
(see also
[
Lus84
]
and
[
Spa82
]
) that there is a duality map
d:\mathcal{N}^{\vee}/G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}/G
from the set of nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
to the set of nilpotent orbit in
\mathfrak{g}
, that we call
Barbasch-Vogan-Lusztig-Spaltenstein (BVLS) duality
. The orbits in the image of
d
are called
special
, and
d
restricts to an order reversing bijection between special nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
and
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\mathbb{O}_{1},\mathbb{O}_{2}\subset\mathcal{N}
be two special nilpotent orbits, such that
\mathbb{O}_{1}\subset\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{2}
. Pick a point
e\in\mathbb{O}_{1}

be the Slodowy slice to the point
e

S_{\mathbb{O}_{1}}^{\mathbb{O}_{2}}
for the intersection
S(e)\cap\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{2}
. The following statement is the main focus of this section.

Expectation 7.1
.

S_{\mathbb{O}_{1}}^{\mathbb{O}_{2}}
is symplectic dual to
S_{d(\mathbb{O}_{2})}^{d(\mathbb{O}_{1})}
. In particular, if
S_{\mathbb{O}_{1}}^{\mathbb{O}_{2}}
is a Kleinian singularity of type A, D, E, then
S_{d(\mathbb{O}_{2})}^{d(\mathbb{O}_{1})}
is the closure of a minimal orbit in the Lie algebra of the same type, i.e. minimal singularity
a
,
d
or
e
.

We note that it is well-known that this expectation is not true as stated, but it holds in many cases, and should serve as a motivation for the refined version stated below.

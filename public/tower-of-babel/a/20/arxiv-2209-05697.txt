Abstract.
The most recent generalization of octonion Fourier transform (OFT) is the
octonion linear canonical transform (OLCT) that has become popular in present era due to its applications in color image and signal processing. On the other hand the applications of Wigner distribution (WD) in signal and image analysis cannot be excluded. In this paper, we introduce novel integral transform coined as the Wigner distribution in the octonion linear canonical transform domain (WDOL). We first propose the definition of
the one dimensional WDOL (1D-WDOL), we extend its relationship with 1D-OLCT and 1D-OFT. Then explore several important properties of 1D-WDOL, such as reconstruction formula,
Rayleigh’s theorem. Second, we introduce the definition of three dimensional WDOL (3D-WDOL) and establish its relationships with the WD associated with quaternion LCT (WD-QLCT) and 3D-WD in LCT domain (3D-WDLCT). Then we study properties like reconstruction formula,
Rayleigh’s theorem and Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma associated with 3D-WDOL. The crux of this paper lies in developing well known uncertainty principles (UPs) including Heisenberg’s UP, Logarithmic UP and Hausdorff-Young inequality associated with WDOL\̇\

Keywords: Octonion linear canonical transform(OLCT); Wigner distribution (WD); Rayleigh’s theorem; Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma ; Uncertainty principle (UP).

2.1.
Octonion algebra

The octonion algebra
\mathbb{O},

[
52
]
is generated by the eighth-order Cayley-Dickson construction. In this
construction, a hyper-complex number
o\in\mathbb{O}
is an ordered pair
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{H}

\displaystyle o

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q_{0},q_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((\xi_{0},\xi_{1}),(\xi_{2},\xi_{3}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{0}+q_{1}.\tau_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\xi_{0}+\xi_{1}.\tau_{2})+(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}.\tau_{2}).\tau_{4}

which has equivalent form

(2.2)

o=s_{o}+\sum_{i=1}^{7}s_{i}\tau_{i}=s_{0}+s_{1}\tau_{1}+s_{2}\tau_{2}+s_{3}\tau_{3}+s_{4}\tau_{4}+s_{5}\tau_{5}+s_{6}\tau_{6}+s_{7}\tau_{7}

thus
o
is a hyper-complex number defined by eight real numbers
s_{i},i=0,1,\dots,7
and seven imaginary units
\tau_{i}
where
i=1,2,\dots,7.
It should be noted that the
\mathbb{O}
is non-commutative and non-associative algebra. We present a table to represent the multiplication
of imaginary units in the Cayley-Dickson algebra of octonions as .
[
43
]

Table I

Multiplication Rules in Octonion Algebra.

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr\cdot&1&\tau_{1}&\tau_{2}&\tau_{3}&\tau_{4}&\tau_{5}&\tau_{6}&\tau_{7}\\
\hline\cr 1&1&\tau_{1}&\tau_{2}&\tau_{3}&\tau_{4}&\tau_{5}&\tau_{6}&\tau_{7}\\
\hline\cr\tau_{1}&\tau_{1}&-1&\tau_{3}&-\tau_{2}&\tau_{5}&-\tau_{4}&-\tau_{7}&\tau_{6}\\
\hline\cr\tau_{2}&\tau_{2}&-\tau_{3}&-1&\tau_{1}&\tau_{6}&\tau_{7}&-\tau_{4}&-\tau_{5}\\
\hline\cr\tau_{3}&\tau_{3}&\tau_{2}&-\tau_{1}&-1&\tau_{7}&-\tau_{6}&\tau_{5}&-\tau_{4}\\
\hline\cr\tau_{4}&\tau_{4}&-\tau_{5}&-\tau_{6}&-\tau_{7}&-1&\tau_{1}&\tau_{2}&\tau_{3}\\
\hline\cr\tau_{5}&\tau_{5}&\tau_{4}&-\tau_{7}&\tau_{6}&-\tau_{1}&-1&-\tau_{3}&\tau_{2}\\
\hline\cr\tau_{6}&\tau_{6}&\tau_{7}&\tau_{4}&-\tau_{5}&-\tau_{2}&\tau_{3}&-1&-\tau_{1}\\

The conjugate of an octonion is defined as

(2.3)

\overline{o}=s_{0}-s_{1}\tau_{1}-s_{2}\tau_{2}-s_{3}\tau_{3}-s_{4}\tau_{4}-s_{5}\tau_{5}-s_{6}\tau_{6}-s_{7}\tau_{7}

Therefore norm is defined by
|o|=\sqrt{o\overline{o}}
and
|o|^{2}=\sum_{i=o}^{7}s_{i}.
Also
|o_{1}o_{2}|=|o_{1}||o_{2}|,\forall o_{1},o_{2}\in\mathbb{O}.

From (
2.1
) it is clear that every
o\in\mathbb{O}
can be reshaped in quaternion form like

(2.4)

o=a+b\tau_{4}

where
a=s_{0}+s_{1}\tau_{1}+s_{2}\tau_{2}+s_{3}\tau_{3}
and
b=s_{4}+s_{5}\tau_{1}+s_{6}\tau_{2}+s_{7}\tau_{3}
are both quaternions. Evidently, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
43
]
Let
a,b\in\mathbb{H},
then

(1)
\tau_{4}a=\overline{a}\tau_{4};
(2)
\tau_{4}(a\tau_{4})=-\overline{a};

(3)\quad(a\tau_{4})\tau_{4}=-a;

(4)
a(b\tau_{4})=(ba)\tau_{4}
;  (5)
(a\tau_{4})b=(a\overline{b})\tau_{4};
(6)
(a\tau_{4})(b\tau_{4})=-\overline{b}a.

It is evident from the above result that, for an octonion
a+b\tau_{4},a,b\in\mathbb{H},
we have

(2.5)

\overline{a+b\tau_{4}}=\overline{a}-b\tau_{4}

and

(2.6)

|a+b\tau_{4}|^{2}=|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
46
]

Let
\tilde{o},\hat{o}\in\mathbb{O}
. Then
e^{\tilde{o}}.e^{\hat{o}}=e^{\tilde{o}+\hat{o}}
iff
\tilde{o}.\hat{o}=\hat{o}.\tilde{o}
.

An octonion-valued function
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\longrightarrow\mathbb{O}
has following explicit form

(2.7)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{0}+f_{1}(x)\tau_{1}+f_{2}(x)\tau_{2}+f_{3}(x)\tau_{3}+f_{4}(x)\tau_{4}+f_{5}(x)\tau_{5}+f_{6}(x)\tau_{6}+f_{7}(x)\tau_{7}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{0}+f_{1}\tau_{1}+(f_{2}+f_{3}\tau_{1})\tau_{2}+[f_{4}+f_{5}\tau_{1}+(f_{6}+f_{7}\tau_{1})\tau_{2}]\tau_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x)+\hat{f}(x)\tau_{4}

where each

2.2.
Octonion Fourier Transform and Octonion Linear Canonical Transform

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{O}
be an octonion-valued function, then 1D octonion Fourier transform (OFT)
[
46
,
53
]
is given by

(2.8)

\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{4}}[f](w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-\tau_{4}2\pi xw}dx,

and its inverse is given by

(2.9)

f(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\tau_{4}}\left\{\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{4}}[f]\right\}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{4}}[f](w)e^{\tau_{4}2\pi xw}dw.

See
[
53
]
for the properties of 1D OFT.

Later, authors in
[
43
,
45
]
introduced 3D OFT of an octonion-valued signal
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{O})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{O})
as

(2.10)

\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{4}}[f]({\bf w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-\tau_{1}2\pi x_{1}w_{1}}e^{-\tau_{2}2\pi x_{2}w_{2}}e^{-\tau_{4}2\pi x_{3}w_{3}}d{\bf x},

where
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
{\bf w}=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and multiplication in above integral is done from left to right. Also the order of imaginary units in (
2.10
) is fixed because octonions are neither commutative nor associative.

The inverse 3D OFT is given as

(2.11)

f({\bf x})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{4}}\left\{\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{4}}[f]\right\}({\bf x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{\tau_{4}2\pi x_{3}w_{3}}e^{\tau_{2}2\pi x_{2}w_{2}}e^{\tau_{1}2\pi x_{1}w_{1}}d{\bf w},

where
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
{\bf w}=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Authors in
[
46
]
introduced octonion linear canonical transform (OLCT) as:

Definition 2.1

(1D-OLCT
[
46
]
)
.

The 1D OLCT of any octonion-valued signal
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{O}),
with respect to the uni-modular matrix
\Lambda=(a,b,c,d)
is given by

(2.12)

\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}_{\tau_{4}}[f](w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)K_{\Lambda}^{\tau_{4}}(x,w)dx,

where

(2.13)

K^{\tau_{4}}_{\Lambda}(x,w)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}e^{\frac{{\tau_{4}}}{2b}\big[ax^{2}-2xw+dw^{2}-\frac{\pi}{2}\big]},\quad b\neq 0\,

with the inversion formula

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}_{\tau_{4}}\}^{-1}[\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}_{\tau_{4}}[f]](x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}_{\tau_{4}}\{f\}(w)K_{\Lambda}^{-\tau_{4}}(x,w)dx,

where
K_{\Lambda}^{-\tau_{4}}(x,w)=K_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{\tau_{4}}(w,x)
and
\Lambda^{-1}=(d,-b,-c,a).

Lemma 2.3
.

[
46
]
The 1D OLCT can be reduced to 1D OFT by following equation:

(2.15)

\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}_{\tau_{4}}[f](w)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{4}}[g]\left(\frac{w}{2\pi|b|}\right)e^{\tau_{4}\left(\frac{d}{2b}w^{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)},

where
g(x)=f(x)e^{\tau_{4}\frac{a}{2b}x^{2}}.

Definition 2.2

(3D-OLCT
[
46
]
)
.

For every octonion-valued signal
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{O})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{O})
, the 3D-OLCT with respect to the matrix parameters
\Lambda_{k}=(a_{k},b_{k},c_{k},d_{k}),
satisfying
det(\Lambda_{k})=1,\quad k=1,2,3
is defined as

(2.16)

\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{4}}\{f\}({\bf w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f({\bf x})K^{\tau_{1}}_{\Lambda_{1}}(x_{1},w_{1})K^{\tau_{2}}_{\Lambda_{1}}(x_{2},w_{2})K^{\tau_{4}}_{\Lambda_{3}}(x_{3},w_{3})d{\bf x}

where
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),\,{\bf w}=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}),
and multiplication in above integral is done from left to right and

(2.17)

K_{\Lambda_{1}}^{\tau_{1}}(x_{1},w_{1})=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b_{1}|}}e^{\frac{{\tau_{1}}}{2b_{1}}\big[a_{1}x^{2}_{1}-2x_{1}w_{1}+d_{1}w^{2}_{1}-\frac{\pi}{2}\big]},\quad b_{1}\neq 0\,

(2.18)

K_{\Lambda_{2}}^{\tau_{2}}(x_{2},w_{2})==\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b_{2}|}}e^{\frac{{\tau_{2}}}{2b_{2}}\big[a_{2}x^{2}_{2}-2x_{2}w_{2}+d_{2}w^{2}_{2}-\frac{\pi}{2}\big]},\quad b_{2}\neq 0\,

and

(2.19)

K_{\Lambda_{3}}^{\tau_{4}}(x_{3},w_{3})=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b_{3}|}}e^{\frac{{\tau_{4}}}{2b_{3}}\big[a_{3}x^{2}_{3}-2x_{3}w_{3}+d_{3}w^{2}_{3}-\frac{\pi}{2}\big]},\quad b_{3}\neq 0.

with the inversion formula

\displaystyle f({\bf x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{4}}\}^{-1}\left[\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{4}}\{f\}\right]({\bf x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{4}}\{f\}(w)K_{\Lambda^{-1}_{3}}^{\tau_{4}}(w_{3},x_{3})K_{\Lambda^{-1}_{2}}^{\tau_{2}}(w_{2},x_{2})K_{\Lambda^{-1}_{1}}^{\tau_{1}}(w_{1},x_{1})d{\bf w},

where
\Lambda^{-1}_{k}=(d_{k},-b_{k},-c_{k},a_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},
for
k=1,2,3.

Further authors in
[
46
]
expanded the kernel of 3D-OLCT as:

\displaystyle K_{\Lambda_{1}}^{\tau_{1}}(x_{1},w_{1})K_{\Lambda_{2}}^{\tau_{2}}(x_{2},w_{2})K_{\Lambda_{3}}^{\tau_{4}}(x_{3},w_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{2\pi|b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}|}}e^{\tau_{1}\theta_{1}}e^{\tau_{2}\theta_{2}}e^{\tau_{4}\theta_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{2\pi|b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}|}}(c_{1}+\tau_{1}s_{1})(c_{2}+\tau_{2}s_{2})(c_{3}+\tau_{4}s_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{2\pi|b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}|}}(c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}+s_{1}c_{2}c_{3}\tau_{1}+c_{1}s_{2}c_{3}\tau_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+

\displaystyle s_{1}s_{2}c_{3}\tau_{3}+c_{1}c_{2}s_{3}\tau_{4}+s_{1}c_{2}s_{3}\tau_{5}+c_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\tau_{6}+s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\tau_{7}),

where
\theta_{k}={\frac{{1}}{2b_{k}}\big[a_{k}x^{2}_{k}-2x_{k}w_{k}+d_{k}w^{2}_{k}-\frac{\pi}{2}\big]},\quad c_{k}=\cos\theta_{k}

3.
Wigner Distribution In The Octonion Linear Canonical Domain

In this section we formally introduce the definition of Wigner distribution associated with octonion linear canonical transform (WDOL)and study its various properties.

According to 1D-OLCT, we can obtain the definition of 1D-WDOL as follows

Definition 3.1

(1D-WDOL)
.

The 1D-WDOL of any octonion-valued signals
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{O}),
with respect to the uni-modular matrix
\Lambda=(a,b,c,d)
is given by

(3.1)

\mathbb{W}_{f,g}^{\tau_{4}}(t,w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\left(t+\frac{x}{2}\right)g^{*}\left(t-\frac{x}{2}\right)K_{\Lambda}^{\tau_{4}}(x,w)dx,

where kernel
K_{\Lambda}^{\tau_{4}}(x,w)
is given by (
2.13
).

Next we note that 1D-WDOL is an 1D-OLCT of a instantaneous correlation of octonion-valued functions
f
and
g
by means of remark as:

Remark 3.1
.

From definition of 1D-OLCT, it is clear that if we take

(3.2)

h_{t}(x)=f\left(t+\frac{x}{2}\right)g^{*}\left(t-\frac{x}{2}\right),

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{f,g}^{\tau_{4}}(t,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}_{\tau_{4}}\left[f\left(t+\frac{x}{2}\right)g^{*}\left(t-\frac{x}{2}\right)\right](w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}_{\tau_{4}}[h_{t}](w).

And by using Lemma
2.3
, we obtain the relation between 1D-WDOL and 1D-OFT as :

(3.4)

\mathbb{W}_{f,g}^{\tau_{4}}(t,w)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{4}}\left[H_{t}\right]\left(\frac{w}{2\pi|b|}\right)e^{\tau_{4}(\frac{d}{2b}w^{2}-\frac{\pi}{4})},

where
H_{t}(x)=h_{t}(x)e^{\tau_{4}\frac{a}{2b}x^{2}}.

Theorem 3.2

(1D-WDOL Reconstruction Formula)
.

For
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{O})
with
g(0)\neq 0
, we have the following inversion formula for 1D-WDOL

(3.5)

\displaystyle f\left(\xi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{g^{*}\left(0\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{W}_{f,g}^{\tau_{4}}\left(\frac{\xi}{2},w\right)K_{\Lambda}^{-\tau_{4}}(\xi,w)dw

Proof.

From
3.4
, we obtain

(3.6)

\sqrt{2\pi|b|}\mathbb{W}_{f,g}^{\tau_{4}}(t,w)e^{-\tau_{4}(\frac{d}{2b}w^{2}-\frac{\pi}{2})}=\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{4}}\left[H_{t}\right]\left(\frac{w}{2\pi|b|}\right),

where
H_{t}(x)=f\left(t+\frac{x}{2}\right)g^{*}\left(t-\frac{x}{2}\right)e^{\tau_{4}\frac{a}{2b}x^{2}}.

Now by the application of (
2.9
), it follows that

\displaystyle f\left(t+\frac{x}{2}\right)g\left(t-\frac{x}{2}\right)e^{\tau_{4}\frac{a}{2b}x^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{2\pi|b|}\mathbb{W}_{f,g}^{\tau_{4}}(t,w)e^{-\tau_{4}(\frac{d}{2b}w^{2}-\frac{\pi}{2})}e^{\tau_{4}x\frac{w}{b}}d\left(\frac{u}{2\pi|b|}\right)

Which implies

\displaystyle f\left(t+\frac{x}{2}\right)g^{*}\left(t-\frac{x}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{W}_{f,g}^{\tau_{4}}(t,w)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}e^{-\tau_{4}(\frac{d}{2b}w^{2}-\frac{\pi}{2})}e^{\tau_{4}2\pi x\frac{w}{2\pi b}}e^{-\tau_{4}\frac{a}{2b}x^{2}}dw

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{W}_{f,g}^{\tau_{4}}(t,w)K_{\Lambda}^{-\tau_{4}}(x,w)dw.

On setting
\frac{x}{2}=t,
and applying change of variable
\xi=2t
, (
3.7
) yields

\displaystyle f\left(\xi\right)g^{*}\left(0\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{W}_{f,g}^{\tau_{4}}\left(\frac{\xi}{2},w\right)K_{\Lambda}^{-\tau_{4}}(\xi,w)dw,

which completes the proof
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Plancherel’s Theorem)
.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{O}),
then the 1D-WDOL satisfies:

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{W}^{\tau_{4}}_{f,g}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{O})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{O})}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{O})}.

Proof.

We know that for
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{O}),
we have

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{O})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)g^{*}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

5.
Conclusion

In the present study, we examined three major objectives: first, we have studied the notion of
1D-WDOL in the framework of time-frequency analysis and examined all
of its basic properties by means of the one dimensional octonion linear canonical transform transforms. Second, we propose the definition of 3D-WDOL and establish the fundamental properties associated with it, which
includes the reconstruction formula, Rayleigh’s theorem and Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma.
Moreover, we also establish the relation of WDOL with 3D-LCT and WD-QLCT. Third, well known uncertainty principles (UPs) including Heisenberg’s UP, Logarithmic UP and Hausdorff-Young inequality associated with WDOL are established.

Abstract
. In [ 2 ] we have introduced a new model to describe front propagation in bushfires. This model describes temperature diffusion in view of an ignition process induced by an interaction kernel, the effect of the environmental wind and that of the fire wind. This has led to the introduction of a new partial differential equation of evolutionary type, with nonlinear terms both of integral kind and involving the gradient of the solution. This equation is, in a sense, “hybrid”, since it encodes both analytical and geometric features of the front propagation. This new characteristic makes the equation particularly interesting also from the mathematical point of view, often falling outside the territory already covered by standard methods. In this paper, we start the mathematical treatment of this equation by establishing the short time and global existence theory for this equation.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider a model for bushfire that we have recently introduced in
[
2
]
.
This model describes the spread of the fire on the basis of relatively simple parameters,
namely the environmental temperature, the ignition temperature of the combustible, the environmental wind, and a convective
effect due to the temperature gradient in the vicinity of the flame
(known in the literature as “pyrogenic flow”).

The model has been confronted with real-world experiments, with numerical simulations compared against data coming from real bushfires in Australia and from lab experiments.

Since this is a new model, the mathematical theory underpinning it is still under development: in this paper we aim at establishing
an existence theory for the partial differential equation at the core of this model.

This partial differential equation is of parabolic type, but it is not completely standard. Therefore, on the one hand, it is tempting to utilize functional analytic techniques, such as fixed-point arguments and iteration methods, to demonstrate the existence of a solution,
but, on the other hand, these methods need to be adapted to the specific model under consideration, which requires some bespoke argument and some ad hoc calculations.

Our bushfire model aims at describing the evolution of the environmental temperature
u
(which will correspond to the solution of the partial differential equation under consideration). When the value of
u
is above an effective ignition temperature
\Theta
, the available fuel burns, producing a forcing term that contributes to the equation through a site interaction described by a kernel
K

(roughly speaking, a burning site tends to increase the temperature of its neighbors).

Two additional effects can be included in our model. First, the environmental wind
\omega
can contribute to the spreading of the fire.
Also, an additional effect due to the moving of the air can take part in this complex phenomenon, on the basis of a convective term, which is modulated by a function
\beta
(typically,
\beta
is a function of the temperature, and one can think that it is supported in the vicinity of the ignition temperature, since the convective effects are expected to be more significant in a neighborhood of the flame, where the gradient temperature is likely to be higher). These additional effects accounting for the movement of the air cannot really stop the propagation of a bushfire, instead they can contribute to its spread: for this reason, these types of term are considered as active only when they point “into the outward direction” of the front.

Though a full mathematical description and a precise list of hypotheses will be given in the main results here below,
in virtue of the above discussion, the main equation that we consider takes the form

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+\int_{\Omega}\big(u(y,t)-\Theta(y,t)\big)_{+}\,K(x,y)\,dy+\left(\left(\omega+\frac{\beta(u)\nabla u}{|\nabla u|^{\gamma}}\right)\cdot\nabla u\right)_{-}\,,

and our unknown function is
u=u(x,t)
.

For concreteness, we focus here on the case of homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions (which is physically relevant,
since, for example, Australia is an island and bushfires are not expected to change, at least in a short time, the temperature of the surrounding ocean). While we refer to
[
2
]
for a comprehensive description of the model, let us mention that the partial differential equation that we consider can also describe the moving front of the fire, corresponding to the level sets of
u
that match the ignition temperature. This provides a “hybrid” model combining the solution of the partial differential equation under consideration and a geometric term given by the mean curvature of the moving front (and, in this setting, the parabolicity of the partial differential equation entails that the equation of the moving front presents the mean curvature term with “the right sign” allowing for short time
smoothing effects).

Of course, bushfire modeling has a long-standing tradition and several different models
and algorithms have been proposed to deal with different aspects of fire ignition and propagation. Though an exhaustive list of different models would go well beyond the scopes of this article, we refer to the surveys in
[
1
,
8
,
6
]
for the description of several models and perspectives, as well as to
[
10
,
4
]
and the reference therein for additional information on the pyrogenic flow.

We now dive into the technical description of our main results. The first result that we present deals with short time existence
and goes as follows:

T_{\star}\in(0,1]

,
\Omega
,

\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}
,

\\
u(x,t)=0\\
\qquad{\mbox{ for $(x,t)\in(\partial\Omega)\times(0,T)$,}}\\

\qquad{\mbox{ for $x\in\Omega$}}\end{cases}

possesses

The proof that we provide of Theorem
1.1
also allows for several generalizations:
for instance, one considers the case of a bounded function

also depending on
x
and
t
, and Lipschitz continuous

u

(e.g., in the setting of

to keep things as simple as possible.

We also point out that the technical condition
\gamma\in(1,2]
corresponds to a requirement on the
relative importance of wind and convection in the equation under consideration:
roughly speaking, while the dependence on the wind is bounded by a term scaling linearly with respect to the gradient of the solution,
the convective term is bounded by a term of the type
|\nabla u|^{2-\gamma}
, hence sublinear when
\gamma\in(1,2]

(stating, in some sense, that the impact of the wind is assumed to be dominant with respect to that of the convection).

The case
\gamma=1
in which wind and convection scale the same is also of interest. When
\beta
is independent of
u

(but possibly depending on space and time, as mentioned above), the proof of Theorem
1.1
would go through
also when
\gamma=1
.

Moreover, under a smallness assumption on the nonlinear terms,
the case of
\beta
depending on
u
and
\gamma=1

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, Lipschitz domain of
\mathds{R}^{n}
.
Let also
\beta\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})
and
\gamma\in(1,2]
.

Then, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
n
,
\Omega
, and
\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})}
, such that, for every
u
,
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

(2.1)

\int_{\Omega}|\beta(u(x))-\beta(v(x))|^{2}|\nabla u(x)|^{2(2-\gamma)}\,dx\leqslant C\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(2-\gamma)}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(\gamma-1)}.

Proof.

We use the Hölder Inequality with exponents
\frac{1}{\gamma-1}
and
\frac{1}{2-\gamma}
to see that

(2.2)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\beta(u(x))-\beta(v(x))|^{2}|\nabla u(x)|^{2(2-\gamma)}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leqslant\left(\int_{\Omega}|\beta(u(x))-\beta(v(x))|^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}\,dx\right)^{\gamma-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{2-\gamma}.\end{split}

Furthermore,

\displaystyle|\beta(u(x))-\beta(v(x))|^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}=|\beta(u(x))-\beta(v(x))|^{\frac{4-2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}|\beta(u(x))-\beta(v(x))|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\Big(|\beta(u(x))|+|\beta(v(x))|\Big)^{\frac{4-2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\|\nabla\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}^{2}|u(x)-v(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant 2^{\frac{4-2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}^{\frac{4-2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\|\nabla\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}^{2}|u(x)-v(x)|^{2}.

This and (
2.2
) give (
2.1
), as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Theta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
K\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)
,
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, and

(2.3)

f_{1,u}(x):=\int_{\Omega}\big(u(y)-\Theta(y)\big)_{+}\,K(x,y)\,dy.

Then,

\|f_{1,u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant 2\|K\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}\,\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+|\Omega|\sup_{\Omega}\Theta_{-}^{2}\right),

where
|\Omega|
is the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
.

Proof.

If
u(y)\geqslant\Theta(y)
then

0\leqslant\big(u(y)-\Theta(y)\big)_{+}=u(y)-\Theta(y)\leqslant u(y)+\sup_{\Omega}\Theta_{-}.

As a result, for all
y\in\Omega
,

\big(u(y)-\Theta(y)\big)_{+}^{2}\leqslant\left(u(y)+\sup_{\Omega}\Theta_{-}\right)^{2}\leqslant 2\left(u^{2}(y)+\sup_{\Omega}\Theta_{-}^{2}\right)

and, that being so, using the Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality we obtain that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\big(u(y)-\Theta(y)\big)_{+}\,K(x,y)\,dy\right)^{2}\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\big(u(y)-\Theta(y)\big)_{+}^{2}\,dy\,\int_{\Omega}K^{2}(x,y)\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant 2\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2}(y)\,dy+|\Omega|\sup_{\Omega}\Theta_{-}^{2}\right)\,\int_{\Omega}K^{2}(x,y)\,dy.

Integrating for
x\in\Omega
, the desired result follows.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\omega\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\beta\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})
,
\gamma\in(1,2]
, and
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Let also

(2.4)

f_{2,u}(x):=\left(\left(\omega(x)+\frac{\beta(u(x))\nabla u(x)}{|\nabla u(x)|^{\gamma}}\right)\cdot\nabla u(x)\right)_{-}\,.

Then,

\|f_{2,u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\left(\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\,|\Omega|^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2-\gamma}\right).

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle|f_{2,u}(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|\omega(x)|\,|\nabla u(x)|+|\beta(u(x))|\,|\nabla u(x)|^{2-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,|\nabla u(x)|+\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\,|\nabla u(x)|^{2-\gamma}.

Therefore, squaring and integrating over
\Omega
,

(2.5)

\|f_{2,u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leqslant 2\left(\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\,\|\nabla u(x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}^{2}\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2(2-\gamma)}\,dx\right).

Now, if
\gamma=2
, from (
2.5
) we obtain that

(2.6)

\|f_{2,u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\left(\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla u(x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\,\sqrt{|\Omega|}\right).

If instead
\gamma\in(1,2)
, we
exploit the Hölder Inequality with exponents
\frac{1}{2-\gamma}
and
\frac{1}{\gamma-1}
to see that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2(2-\gamma)}\,dx\leqslant|\Omega|^{\gamma-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{2-\gamma}=|\Omega|^{\gamma-1}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(2-\gamma)}.

Plugging this information into (
2.5
) we conclude that, in this case,

\|f_{2,u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\left(\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla u(x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\,|\Omega|^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2-\gamma}\right).

From this and (
2.6
) the desired result follows.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Theta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
K\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)
, and
u
,
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Then, in the notation of (

\|f_{1,u}-f_{1,v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since the positive part is a Lipschitz function, with Lipschitz constant controlled by
1

|a_{+}-b_{+}|\leqslant|a-b|.

In particular, taking

On this account,

\displaystyle|f_{1,u}(x)-f_{1,v}(x)|

\displaystyle=

.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, Lipschitz domain of
\mathds{R}^{n}

Let
\omega\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\beta\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})

We observe that

) that

(2.11)

\begin{split}&\|f_{2,u}-f_{2,v}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
\leqslant\;&C\Bigg[\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\Big|^{2(2-\gamma)}\,dx\\
&\qquad+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(2-\gamma)}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(\gamma-1)}\Bigg].\end{split}

Now we exploit the Hölder Inequality with exponents
\frac{1}{2-\gamma}
and
\frac{1}{\gamma-1}
and see that

\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\Big|^{2(2-\gamma)}\,dx\leqslant|\Omega|^{\gamma-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\Big|^{2}\,dx\right)^{2-\gamma}.

Plugging this information into (
2.11
) entails the desired result, up to renaming constants.
∎

2.2.
The fixed-point argument

Here we consider a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
with Lipschitz boundary, we let
T>0
, and we consider the functional spaces

X:=L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad Y:=L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega)).

See e.g.
[
9
]
and the references therein for classical results about the theory of Sobolev spaces of Hilbert and Banach space-valued functions.

Also, we take
g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

For every
f\in Y
, we define
\sigma_{f}=\sigma_{f}(x,t)
to be the solution of the parabolic problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\sigma_{f}(x,t)=\Delta\sigma_{f}(x,t)+f(x,t)&{\mbox{ for $(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)$,}}\\
\sigma_{f}(x,t)=0&{\mbox{ for $(x,t)\in(\partial\Omega)\times(0,T)$,}}\\
\sigma_{f}(x,0)=g(x)&{\mbox{ for $x\in\Omega$.}}\end{cases}

We recall that the above solution indeed exists (say, as a weak solution,
see Theorem 3 on page 356 of
[
3
]
) and it is unique (see
Theorem 4 on page 358 of
[
3
]
).

Additionally (see Theorem 5 on page 360 of
[
3
]
) we have the regularity estimate

(2.12)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\sigma_{f}(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma_{f}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}\sigma_{f}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),\end{split}

for some
C>0
depending on
n
,
\Omega
, and
T
.

We remark that:

Lemma 2.6
.

When
T\in(0,1]
the constant
C
in (
2.12
)
depends on
n
and
\Omega
, but not on
T
.

Proof.

We define
\widetilde{f}(x,t):=f(x,t)\chi_{[0,T]}(t)
and notice that, by the uniqueness of the solution and the fact that
\widetilde{f}=f
when
t\in[0,T]
, we have
\sigma_{\widetilde{f}}=\sigma_{f}
when
t\in[0,T)
.
We also observe that, for all
T\in(0,1]
,

\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{2}((0,1),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}=\iint_{\Omega\times(0,1)}|\widetilde{f}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\,dt=\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T)}|f(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\,dt=\|f\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}.

As a consequence, we can apply (
2.12
) for
T:=1
and obtain that

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|\sigma_{\widetilde{f}}(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma_{\widetilde{f}}\|_{L^{2}((0,1),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}\sigma_{\widetilde{f}}\|_{L^{2}((0,1),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\Big(\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{2}((0,1),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big)\\
&\qquad=C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),\end{split}

with
C>0
depending only on
n
and
\Omega
.

This and the fact that, for all
T\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\sigma_{f}(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma_{f}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}\sigma_{f}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\sigma_{\widetilde{f}}(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma_{\widetilde{f}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}\sigma_{\widetilde{f}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|\sigma_{\widetilde{f}}(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma_{\widetilde{f}}\|_{L^{2}((0,1),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}\sigma_{\widetilde{f}}\|_{L^{2}((0,1),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}

give the desired result.
∎

Now we consider functions
K\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)
,
\Theta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))
,

\omega\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))
, and
\beta\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})
. We also let
\gamma\in(1,2]
.

Given
u\in X
, we define

(2.13)

\begin{split}f_{u}(x,t)&:=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\big(u(y,t)-\Theta(y,t)\big)_{+}\,K(x,y)\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left(\left(\omega(x,t)+\frac{\beta(u(x,t))\nabla u(x,t)}{|\nabla u(x,t)|^{\gamma}}\right)\cdot\nabla u(x,t)\right)_{-}.\end{split}

In this setting, we have:

Lemma 2.7
.

It holds that
f_{u}\in Y
, with

\|f_{u}\|_{Y}\leqslant C\,\left(\|u\|_{X}+\|u\|_{X}^{2-\gamma}+T\sup_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\Theta_{-}\right),

for a suitable
C>0
depending only on
n
,
\Omega
,
\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}
,

\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))}
, and
\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})}
.

Proof.

For a given
t\in(0,T)
, after (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we have that

(2.14)

f_{u}(x,t)=f_{1,u^{(t)}}(x)+f_{2,u^{(t)}}(x),\qquad{\mbox{where}}\qquad{u^{(t)}}(x):=u(x,t).

By Lemmata
2.2
and
2.3
, for any
t\in(0,T)
,

\displaystyle\|f_{u}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|f_{1,u^{(t)}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f_{2,u^{(t)}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\left(\|u^{(t)}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u^{(t)}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2-\gamma}+\sup_{x\in\Omega}\Theta_{-}(x,t)\right),

whence the desired result follows by squaring and integrating for
t\in(0,T)
.
∎

Now we consider the nonlinear operator

X\ni u\longmapsto Au:=\sigma_{f_{u}}.

We have that:

Lemma 2.8
.

For all
u\in X
, we have that
Au\in X
, with

(2.15)

\|Au\|_{X}\leqslant C\left(\|u\|_{X}+\|u\|_{X}^{2-\gamma}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+T\sup_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\Theta_{-}\right).

Moreover, for all
u
,
v\in X
with
\|u-v\|_{X}\leqslant 1
,

(2.16)

\|Au-Av\|_{X}\leqslant C_{u}\,\|u-v\|_{X}^{\alpha}.

Here above, the constants
C>0
and
\alpha
depend only on
n
,
\Omega
,
T
,
\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}
,

\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))}
, and
\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})}
,
while
C_{u}>0
depends also on
\|u\|_{X}
.

Proof.

Owing to (
2.12
),

\displaystyle\|Au\|_{X}=\|\sigma_{f_{u}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}\leqslant C\Big(\|f_{u}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=C\Big(\|f_{u}\|_{Y}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big).

This and Lemma
2.7
yield (
2.15
), as desired.

Now we let
\sigma_{\star}:=\sigma_{f_{u}}-\sigma_{f_{v}}
and
f_{\star}(x,t):=f_{u}(x,t)-f_{v}(x,t)
, and we see that

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\sigma_{\star}(x,t)=\Delta\sigma_{\star}(x,t)+f_{\star}(x,t)&{\mbox{ for $(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)$,}}\\
\sigma_{\star}(x,t)=0&{\mbox{ for $(x,t)\in(\partial\Omega)\times(0,T)$,}}\\
\sigma_{\star}(x,0)=0&{\mbox{ for $x\in\Omega$.}}\end{cases}

For this reason, by (
2.12
),

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{\star}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}\leqslant C\|f_{\star}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}=C\Big(\|f_{u}-f_{v}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\Big)

and therefore

(2.17)

\begin{split}&\|Au-Av\|_{X}=\|\sigma_{f_{u}}-\sigma_{f_{v}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}=\|\sigma_{\star}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\Big(\|f_{u}-f_{v}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\Big).\end{split}

Now, in the notation of (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.14
), and recalling Lemmata
2.4
and
2.5
, we see that, for every
t\in(0,T)
,

\begin{split}&\|f_{u}(\cdot,t)-f_{v}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\quad=\|f_{1,u^{(t)}}+f_{2,u^{(t)}}-f_{1,v^{(t)}}-f_{2,v^{(t)}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\quad\leqslant\|f_{1,u^{(t)}}-f_{1,v^{(t)}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f_{2,u^{(t)}}-f_{2,v^{(t)}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\quad\leqslant C\Big(\|\nabla u^{(t)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2-\gamma}+1\Big)\Big(\|u^{(t)}-v^{(t)}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u^{(t)}-v^{(t)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\gamma-1}+\|\nabla u^{(t)}-\nabla v^{(t)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2-\gamma}\Big).\end{split}

We point out that the last term in the above inequality is set to equal zero when
\gamma=2
, as specified in Lemma
2.5
.

Hence, for all
\epsilon>0
,

\begin{split}&\|f_{u}(\cdot,t)-f_{v}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\epsilon\Big(\|\nabla u^{(t)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(2-\gamma)}+1\Big)\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{C}{\epsilon}\Big(\|u^{(t)}-v^{(t)}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|u^{(t)}-v^{(t)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(\gamma-1)}+\|\nabla u^{(t)}-\nabla v^{(t)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(2-\gamma)}\Big)\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\epsilon\Big(\|\nabla u^{(t)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\Big)\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{C}{\epsilon}\Big(\|u^{(t)}-v^{(t)}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|u^{(t)}-v^{(t)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(\gamma-1)}+\|\nabla u^{(t)}-\nabla v^{(t)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(2-\gamma)}\Big)\end{split}

and thus, integrating in
t\in(0,T)
, and exploiting the Hölder Inequality,

\begin{split}&\|f_{u}-f_{v}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\epsilon\Big(\|u\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}^{2}+1\Big)\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{C}{\epsilon}\Big(\|u-v\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+T^{2-\gamma}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2(\gamma-1)}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+T^{\gamma-1}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2(2-\gamma)}\Big)\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\epsilon\Big(\|u\|_{X}^{2}+1\Big)+\frac{C}{\epsilon}\Big(\|u-v\|_{X}^{2}+\|u-v\|_{X}^{2(\gamma-1)}+\|u-v\|_{X}^{2(2-\gamma)}\Big)\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\epsilon\Big(\|u\|_{X}^{2}+1\Big)+\frac{C}{\epsilon}\Big(\|u-v\|_{X}^{2(\gamma-1)}+\|u-v\|_{X}^{2(2-\gamma)}\Big).\end{split}

In particular, choosing

\epsilon:=\sqrt{\frac{\|u-v\|_{X}^{2(\gamma-1)}+\|u-v\|_{X}^{2(2-\gamma)}}{\|u\|_{X}^{2}+1}},

we gather that

\displaystyle\|f_{u}-f_{v}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\sqrt{\|u\|_{X}^{2}+1}\;\sqrt{\|u-v\|_{X}^{2(\gamma-1)}+\|u-v\|_{X}^{2(2-\gamma)}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{u}\,\sqrt{\|u-v\|_{X}^{2(\gamma-1)}+\|u-v\|_{X}^{2(2-\gamma)}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{u}\,\Big(\|u-v\|_{X}^{\gamma-1}+\|u-v\|_{X}^{2-\gamma}\Big).

Up to renaming constants, this and (
2.17
) give (
2.16
), as desired.
∎

Now we put forth a useful compactness result.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
v_{k}\in X
, with

(2.18)

\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}\Big(\|v_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}v_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\Big)<+\infty.

Then, there exists
v\in X
such that, up to a subsequence,
v_{k}\to v
in
X
as
k\to+\infty
.

Proof.

By (
2.18
),
up to a subsequence, we can assume that

(2.19)

v_{k}
converges weakly to some
\widetilde{v}\in L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega))
as
k\to+\infty

and, up to extracting a further subsequence, we can assume that

(2.20)

v_{k}
converges to some
v
in
L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))

as
k\to+\infty
.

We claim that

(2.21)

v=\tilde{v}.

To this end, in view of (
2.19
) we have that, for every
\phi\in L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{4}(\Omega))
with
\Delta\phi\in L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
,

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\langle\widetilde{v}(\cdot,t),\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\langle\widetilde{v}(\cdot,t),\Delta\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\widetilde{v}(\cdot,t),\Delta^{2}\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\,dt\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\left(\langle\widetilde{v}(\cdot,t),\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\nabla\widetilde{v}(\cdot,t),\nabla\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}\widetilde{v}(\cdot,t),\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\,dt\\
=&\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\langle v_{k}(\cdot,t),\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\nabla v_{k}(\cdot,t),\nabla\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}v_{k}(\cdot,t),\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\,dt\\
=&\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\langle v_{k}(\cdot,t),\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\langle v_{k}(\cdot,t),\Delta\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle v_{k}(\cdot,t),\Delta^{2}\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\,dt.\end{split}

Hence, by (
2.20
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\langle\widetilde{v}(\cdot,t),\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\langle\widetilde{v}(\cdot,t),\Delta\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\widetilde{v}(\cdot,t),\Delta^{2}\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\langle v(\cdot,t),\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\langle v(\cdot,t),\Delta\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle v(\cdot,t),\Delta^{2}\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\,dt.

That is, letting
w:=v-\widetilde{v}
,

(2.22)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle w(\cdot,t),\Big(\phi(\cdot,t)-\Delta\phi(\cdot,t)+\Delta^{2}\phi(\cdot,t)\Big)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt\\
=\;&\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\langle w(\cdot,t),\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\langle w(\cdot,t),\Delta\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle w(\cdot,t),\Delta^{2}\phi(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\,dt\\
=\;&0.\end{split}

Now we choose
\phi\in L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega))
as the minimizer of the functional

\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\Big(|\Delta\psi(x,t)|^{2}+|\nabla\psi(x,t)|^{2}+|\psi(x,t)|^{2}-2w(x,t)\psi(x,t)\Big)\,dx\,dt.

We see that
\phi
is a weak solution of

\begin{cases}\phi(x,t)-\Delta\phi(x,t)+\Delta^{2}\phi(x,t)=w(x,t)&{\mbox{ for $(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)$,}}\\
\phi(x,t)=0&{\mbox{ for $(x,t)\in(\partial\Omega)\times(0,T)$,}}\\
\Delta\phi(x,t)=0&{\mbox{ for $(x,t)\in(\partial\Omega)\times(0,T)$.}}\end{cases}

Actually,
\phi(\cdot,t)\in H^{4}(\Omega)
, see e.g.
Theorem 4 on page 317 of
[
3
]
, hence the equation is also fulfilled a.e., and thus, substituting into (
2.22
),

\int_{0}^{T}\langle w(\cdot,t),w(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt=0.

This gives that
w=0
, which in turn proves (
2.21
).

In particular, from (
2.18
), (
2.19
)
and (
2.21
), if
w_{k}:=v_{k}-v
,

(2.23)

\begin{split}&\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}\|w_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}=\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}\|v_{k}-v\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}=\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}\|\sigma_{f_{u_{k}}}-\widetilde{v}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\qquad\leqslant\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}\|\sigma_{f_{u_{k}}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\widetilde{v}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}<+\infty.\end{split}

Now we use
the Gagliardo-Nirenberg Interpolation Inequality, see e.g. Theorem 1 in
[
5
]

(used here with
a:=\frac{1}{2}
,
j:=1
,
m:=2
,
p:=2
,
r:=2
, and
q:=2
),
obtaining that

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\,\left[\left(\int_{\Omega}|D^{2}w_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|w_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\Omega}|w_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right],\end{split}

for some
C>0
depending only on
n
and
\Omega
.

Hence, integrating for
t\in(0,T)
and using the Hölder Inequality,

\displaystyle\|w_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}|D^{2}w_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|w_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+C\|w_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\sqrt{\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}|D^{2}w_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)\,dt\,\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}|w_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)\,dt}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+C\|w_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\Big(\|w_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}\,\|w_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|w_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\Big).

From this, (
2.20
), and (
2.23
), we conclude that
w_{k}\to 0
(and therefore
v_{k}\to v
)
in
X
.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain of
\mathds{R}^{n}
with
C^{2}
boundary.
Let also
K\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)
,
\Theta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,1))
,

\omega\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,1))
, and
\beta\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})
.

Then, there exists
T_{\star}\in(0,1]
, depending only on
n
,
\Omega
,

\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}
,

\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,1))}
, and
\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})}
, such that if
T\in(0,T_{\star}]
the following claims hold true.

The nonlinear operator
A
satisfies
A:X\to X
.

Also, it is continuous and compact, and the set

(2.24)

\big\{{\mbox{$u\in X$ s.t. $u=\lambda Au$ for some~$\lambda\in[0,1]$}}\big\}

is bounded.

Proof.

Let
T\in(0,1]
.
By Lemma
2.8
we know that
A:X\to X
, and the continuity of
A
is a consequence of (
2.16
).

As for the compactness of
A
, let
u_{k}
be a bounded sequence in
X
.
Then, by Lemma
2.7
, the function
f_{u_{k}}
is bounded in
Y

and, consequently, by (
2.12
),

(2.25)

\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}\|\sigma_{f_{u_{k}}}\|_{H^{1}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}+\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}\|\sigma_{f_{u_{k}}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}<+\infty.

On this account, using Lemma
2.9
with
v_{k}:=\sigma_{f_{u_{k}}}
, we see that
v_{k}=\sigma_{f_{u_{k}}}=Av_{k}
converges to
v
, thus establishing the desired compactness property for the operator
A
.

Let now
u
belong to the set in (
2.24
). Then, by (
2.12
) and
Lemma
2.6
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X}=\lambda\|Au\|_{X}\leqslant\|Au\|_{X}=\|\sigma_{f_{u}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sqrt{\int_{0}^{T}\|\sigma_{f_{u}}(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt}\leqslant\sqrt{T\,\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\sigma_{f_{u}}(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C\sqrt{T}\,\Big(\|f_{u}\|_{Y}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),

with
C>0
depending only on
n
and
\Omega
.

Hence, by Lemma
2.7
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X}\leqslant C\sqrt{T}\,\left(\|u\|_{X}+\|u\|_{X}^{2-\gamma}+T\sup_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\Theta_{-}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right),

with
C>0
now depending only on
n
,
\Omega
,
\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}
,

\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))}
, and
\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})}
.

Now we use Young Inequality with exponents
\frac{1}{2-\gamma}
and
\frac{1}{\gamma-1}
and we see that

\|u\|_{X}^{2-\gamma}\leqslant(2-\gamma)\|u\|_{X}+\gamma-1\leqslant 2\|u\|_{X}+1.

Accordingly, up to renaming
C
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X}\leqslant C\sqrt{T}\,\left(\|u\|_{X}+T\sup_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\Theta_{-}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+1\right),

When
C\sqrt{T}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
, we can reabsorb the term
\|u\|_{X}
to the left-hand side, thus obtaining, up to renaming constants,

\|u\|_{X}\leqslant C\left(\sup_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\Theta_{-}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+1\right),

proving that the set in (
2.24
) is bounded.
∎

With this preliminary work, we can now give the proof of Theorem
1.1
by arguing as follows:

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Corollary
2.10
, the hypotheses of Schaefer’s Fixed-Point Theorem are satisfied (see e.g. Theorem 11.1 in
[
7
]
). This gives the existence of a function
u
satisfying
u=Au=\sigma_{f_{u}}
, that is

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+f_{u},

yielding the desired result in view of (
2.13
).
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The gist of the proof of Theorem
1.2
consists in taking a sequence of parameters
\gamma\searrow 1

and the corresponding solutions
u_{\gamma}
provided by
Theorem
1.1
, then utilizing the parabolic regularity theory to infer uniform bounds and compactness properties
that allow one to pass the solutions to the limit.

The technical details go as follows:

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We use the notation in (
2.13
), writing here
f^{(\gamma)}_{u}

instead of simply
f_{u}
to make explicit the dependence on
\gamma
.

We let
T_{\star}
be given by Theorem
1.1
.
Then, in the interval of time
[0,T_{\star}]
,
we obtain by Theorem
1.1
the existence of a solution
u_{\gamma}
of
\partial_{t}u_{\gamma}=\Delta u_{\gamma}+f^{(\gamma)}_{u_{\gamma}}
, and, in view of (
2.12
),

(3.1)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\star}]}\|u_{\gamma}(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\Big(\|f^{(\gamma)}_{u_{\gamma}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),\end{split}

for some
C>0
depending only on
n
and
\Omega
(recall Lemma
2.6
).

Furthermore, in light of Lemmata
2.2
and
2.3
, setting
{u^{(t)}}:=u(\cdot,t)
, we have that

\begin{split}\|f^{(\gamma)}_{u_{\gamma}}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant\,&\sqrt{2}\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}\,\left(\|u^{(t)}_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\Theta_{-}\right)\\
&\quad+\sqrt{2}\left(\|\omega(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla u^{(t)}_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\,|\Omega|^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}\|\nabla u^{(t)}_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2-\gamma}\right)\\
\leqslant\,&C\Big(\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}+\|\omega(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\Big)\Big(\|u^{(t)}_{\gamma}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+1\Big)\\
\leqslant\,&C\epsilon_{0}\Big(\|u^{(t)}_{\gamma}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+1\Big),\end{split}

up to renaming
C>0
, possibly in dependence of
n
,
\Omega
and
\|\Theta_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,1))}
.

As a result,

\|f^{(\gamma)}_{u_{\gamma}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\leqslant C\epsilon_{0}\Big(\|u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}+1\Big),

up to renaming
C
, which, combined with (
3.1
), gives that

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\star}]}\|u_{\gamma}(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\Big(\epsilon_{0}\|u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}+1+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big).\end{split}

Consequently, if
\epsilon_{0}
is small enough (possibly in dependence of
n
,
\Omega
and
\|\Theta_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,1))}
), we can reabsorb one term to the left-hand side and conclude that

(3.2)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\star}]}\|u_{\gamma}(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\qquad\qquad\leqslant C\Big(1+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big).\end{split}

We now take a sequence
\gamma_{k}\searrow 1
and we obtain from (
3.2
)
by compactness (see Lemma
2.9
) that

(3.3)

u_{\gamma_{k}}

converges, up to a subsequence, to some
u
in
L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,H^{1}(\Omega))
.

Another consequence of (
3.2
) is that

\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}|\partial_{t}u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leqslant C^{2}\Big(1+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big)^{2}

and so, up to a subsequence,

(3.4)

\partial_{t}u_{\gamma_{k}}
converges weakly to some function
U
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T_{\star}))
.

We claim that, in the weak sense,

(3.5)

U=\partial_{t}u.

Indeed, for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega\times(0,T_{\star}))
, we infer from (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}U(x,t)\,\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\partial_{t}u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)\,\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\lim_{k\to+\infty}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)\,\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt=-\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}u(x,t)\,\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt,

which establishes (
3.5
).

It remains to show that
\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+f^{(1)}_{u}
in the weak sense, i.e. (see
[
3
, page 352]
)
that, for a.e.
t\in[0,T_{\star}]
and all
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

(3.6)

\langle\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u(x,t)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}f^{(1)}_{u}(x,t)\,\psi(x)\,dx=0.

To this end, we let
w_{k}:=u-u_{\gamma_{k}}
and, in light of (
3.3
), we recall that

(3.7)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|w_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}=0.

Moreover, by (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), as
k\to+\infty
,

(3.8)

\partial_{t}w_{k}\to 0
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T_{\star}))
.

We also call
\zeta(t)
the left-hand side of (
3.6
)
(in this way, to prove (
3.6
), we will need to show that
\zeta
vanishes almost everywhere).

Since

\langle\partial_{t}u_{\gamma_{k}}(\cdot,t),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}f^{(\gamma_{k})}_{u_{\gamma_{k}}}(x,t)\,\psi(x)\,dx=0,

we find that

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\zeta(t)=\langle\partial_{t}w_{k}(\cdot,t),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla w_{k}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad-\int_{\Omega}\Big(f^{(1)}_{u}(x,t)-f^{(\gamma_{k})}_{u_{\gamma_{k}}}(x,t)\Big)\,\psi(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Let now make the duality
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

more explicit by using the canonical embedding of
L^{2}(\Omega)
into
H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
Specifically, one identifies
L^{2}(\Omega)
with its dual, which is contained in the dual of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
which is identified with
H^{-1}(\Omega)
, therefore, for
h\in L^{2}(\Omega)\subset H^{-1}(\Omega)
, the pairing
\langle h,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
reduces to
\int_{\Omega}h(x)\,\psi(x)\,dx
, which is indeed a bounded linear functional on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Consequently, for every
\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,T_{\star}))
, we deduce from (
3.8
) that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T_{\star}}\langle\partial_{t}w_{k}(\cdot,t),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\,\Psi(t)\,dt=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\partial_{t}w_{k}(x,t)\,\psi(x)\,\Psi(t)\,dx\,dt=0.

Plugging this information into (
3.9
) we conclude that, for every
\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,T_{\star}))
,

(3.10)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T_{\star}}\zeta(t)\,\Psi(t)\,dt&=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Bigg(\,\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\nabla w_{k}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\,\Psi(t)\,dx\,dt\\
&\qquad\qquad-\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\Big(f^{(1)}_{u}(x,t)-f^{(\gamma_{k})}_{u_{\gamma_{k}}}(x,t)\Big)\,\psi(x)\,\Psi(t)\,dx\,dt\Bigg).\end{split}

Also,

\displaystyle\left|\,\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\nabla w_{k}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\,\Psi(t)\,dx\,dt\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant T_{\star}\,|\Omega|\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}|\nabla w_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}\,|\Psi(t)|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant T_{\star}\,|\Omega|\,\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\|\Psi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{\star}))}\,\|w_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}^{2},

which, owing to (
3.7
), is infinitesimal as
k\to+\infty
.

As a consequence, (
3.10
) boils down to

(3.11)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T_{\star}}\zeta(t)\,\Psi(t)\,dt=-\lim_{k\to+\infty}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\Big(f^{(1)}_{u}(x,t)-f^{(\gamma_{k})}_{u_{\gamma_{k}}}(x,t)\Big)\,\psi(x)\,\Psi(t)\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

Now we use the notation in (
2.3
). We also adopt the notation in (
2.4
), but writing
f_{2,u}^{(\gamma)}
in lieu of
f_{2,u}
to emphasize the dependence on
\gamma
.
In this way, we can write that

(3.12)

f^{(\gamma)}_{u}=f_{1,u}+f_{2,u}^{(\gamma)}.

By Lemma
2.4
, we know that

\begin{split}\|f_{1,u}(\cdot,t)-f_{1,{u_{\gamma_{k}}}}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\leqslant\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}\|u(\cdot,t)-{u_{\gamma_{k}}}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&=\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}\|w_{k}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\end{split}

and therefore

\begin{split}&\left|\,\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\Big(f_{1,u}(x,t)-f_{1,u_{\gamma_{k}}}(x,t)\Big)\,\psi(x)\,\Psi(t)\,dx\,dt\right|^{2}\\
&\qquad\leqslant T_{\star}\,|\Omega|\,\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\|\Psi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{\star}))}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\Big(f_{1,u}(x,t)-f_{1,u_{\gamma_{k}}}(x,t)\Big)^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\qquad=T_{\star}\,|\Omega|\,\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\|\Psi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{\star}))}\int_{0}^{T_{\star}}\|f_{1,u}(\cdot,t)-f_{1,{u_{\gamma_{k}}}}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt\\
&\qquad\leqslant T_{\star}\,|\Omega|\,\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\|\Psi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{\star}))}\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\star}}\|w_{k}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt\\
&\qquad=T_{\star}\,|\Omega|\,\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\|\Psi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{\star}))}\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}^{2}\,\|w_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2},\end{split}

which is infinitesimal as
k\to+\infty
, thanks to (
3.7
).

By virtue of this observation and (
3.12
), we can reduce (
3.11
) to

(3.13)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T_{\star}}\zeta(t)\,\Psi(t)\,dt=-\lim_{k\to+\infty}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\Big(f^{(1)}_{2,u}(x,t)-f^{(\gamma_{k})}_{2,u_{\gamma_{k}}}(x,t)\Big)\,\psi(x)\,\Psi(t)\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

Now we remark that

\begin{split}&\Big|f^{(1)}_{2,u}(x,t)-f^{(\gamma_{k})}_{2,u_{\gamma_{k}}}(x,t)\Big|\\
=&\left|\left(\left(\omega(x,t)+\frac{\beta(u(x,t))\nabla u(x,t)}{|\nabla u(x,t)|}\right)\cdot\nabla u(x,t)\right)_{-}-\left(\left(\omega(x,t)+\frac{\beta(u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t))\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)}{|\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)|^{\gamma_{k}}}\right)\cdot\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)\right)_{-}\right|\\
\leqslant&\left|\left(\omega(x,t)+\frac{\beta(u(x,t))\nabla u(x,t)}{|\nabla u(x,t)|}\right)\cdot\nabla u(x,t)-\left(\omega(x,t)+\frac{\beta(u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t))\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)}{|\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)|^{\gamma_{k}}}\right)\cdot\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)\right|\\
\leqslant&\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,1))}\big|\nabla u(x,t)-\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)\big|+\Big|\beta(u(x,t))|\nabla u(x,t)|-\beta(u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t))|\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)|^{2-\gamma_{k}}\Big|\\
\leqslant&\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,1))}\big|\nabla w_{k}(x,t)\big|+\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\Big||\nabla u(x,t)|-|\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)|^{2-\gamma_{k}}\Big|\\
&\hskip 8.50012pt+\big|\beta(u(x,t))-\beta(u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t))\big|\,|\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)|^{2-\gamma_{k}}\\
\leqslant&\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,1))}\big|\nabla w_{k}(x,t)\big|+\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\Big||\nabla u(x,t)|-|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2-\gamma_{k}}\Big|\\
&\hskip 8.50012pt+\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\Big||\nabla u(x,t)|^{2-\gamma_{k}}-|\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)|^{2-\gamma_{k}}\Big|+\sqrt{2}\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})}|u(x,t))-u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\,|\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)|^{2-\gamma_{k}}.\end{split}

Hence, by (
2.10
),

\displaystyle\Big|f^{(1)}_{2,u}(x,t)-f^{(\gamma_{k})}_{2,u_{\gamma_{k}}}(x,t)\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\Big(\big|\nabla w_{k}(x,t)\big|+\big||\nabla u(x,t)|-|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2-\gamma_{k}}\big|+\big|\nabla w_{k}(x,t)\big|^{2-\gamma_{k}}+|w_{k}(x,t)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\,|\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}(x,t)|^{2-\gamma_{k}}\Big),

for some
C>0
.

As a result,

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\Big|f^{(1)}_{2,u}(x,t)-f^{(\gamma_{k})}_{2,u_{\gamma_{k}}}(x,t)\Big|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\Bigg(\|w_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}^{2}+\iint_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\star})}\big||\nabla u(x,t)|-|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2-\gamma_{k}}\big|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad+\|w_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}^{2(2-\gamma_{k})}+\|w_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\|\nabla u_{\gamma_{k}}\|^{2(2-\gamma_{k})}_{L^{2}((0,T_{\star}),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\Bigg),

which is infinitesimal as
k\to+\infty
, thanks to (
3.2
),
(
3.7
),
and the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

From this observation and (
3.13
) we conclude that

\int_{0}^{T_{\star}}\zeta(t)\,\Psi(t)\,dt=0.

Since
\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,T_{\star}))
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\zeta=0
almost everywhere in
[0,T_{\star}]
, as desired.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

4.1.
Toolbox

The gist to prove the global existence result in Theorem
1.3

is that the local existence result in Theorem
1.1
will provide a solution defined
in a uniform interval of time length
T_{\star}
, only depending on
n
,
\Omega
,
\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}
,

\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,1))}
, and
\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})}
, therefore one can iterate
Theorem
1.1
, starting the flow at
u(\cdot,T_{\star})
, thus obtaining a solution up to time
2T_{\star}
, and so on.

The details to make this idea work are perhaps a bit technical, since the equation under consideration is not standard, therefore, for completeness, we provide the complete argument.

This section collects some ancillary results towards the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
The following is a continuity property of the solution at a given time.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T_{\star}
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Let
T\in(0,T_{\star}]
and
u
be given by Theorem
1.1
.

Then,

(4.1)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\Big(1+\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),\end{split}

for some
C>0
depending only on
n
,
\Omega
,
\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}
,

\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))}
,
\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})}
, and
\|\Theta_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))}
.

Moreover, for all
\widetilde{T}\in(0,T]
,

(4.2)

\lim_{\eta\searrow 0}\|u(\cdot,\widetilde{T}-\eta)-u(\cdot,\widetilde{T})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0

and, for all
\widetilde{T}\in[0,T)
,

(4.3)

\lim_{\eta\searrow 0}\|u(\cdot,\widetilde{T}+\eta)-u(\cdot,\widetilde{T})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

Proof.

In the notation of (
2.13
), we deduce from (
2.12
) that

(4.4)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\Big(\|f_{u}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),\end{split}

with
C>0
depending only on
n
and
\Omega
(recall Lemma
2.6
).

Moreover, since the set in (
2.24
) is bounded,

(4.5)

\|u\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\leqslant C\left(\sup_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\Theta_{-}+\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+1\right),

with
C>0
now depending also on
\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}
,

\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))}
, and
\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})}
.

Furthermore, in light of Lemma
2.7
,

\|f_{u}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\leqslant C\,\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}+\sup_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\Theta_{-}\right).

Combining this information and (
4.5
) we obtain that

\|f_{u}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\leqslant C\Big(1+\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),

up to renaming
C
that now may depend also on
\|\Theta_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))}
.

This in turn, together with (
4.4
), gives (
4.1
), as desired.

Now we prove (
4.2
).
For this, we observe that, for all
t\in(0,\eta)
,

\displaystyle|u(x,\widetilde{T}-\eta)-u(x,\widetilde{T})|^{2}\leqslant\left(\int_{0}^{\eta}|\partial_{t}u(x,\widetilde{T}-s)|\,ds\right)^{2}\leqslant\eta\int_{0}^{\eta}|\partial_{t}u(x,\widetilde{T}-s)|^{2}\,ds.

Hence, integrating over
x\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,\widetilde{T}-\eta)-u(\cdot,\widetilde{T})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant\eta\int_{0}^{\eta}\|\partial_{t}u(\cdot,\widetilde{T}-s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,ds.

This and (
4.1
) give that

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,\widetilde{T}-t)-u(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\eta\Big(1+\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),

from which (
4.2
) follows.

The proof of (
4.3
) is analogous to that of (
4.2
).
∎

The following result is the core step to iterate the local result in Theorem
1.1

to obtain a global solution in Theorem
1.3
.
The idea is to “glue together” two solutions of

(4.6)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,\cdot)=\Delta u(x,\cdot)+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(u(y,\cdot)-\Theta(y,\cdot)\big)_{+}\,K(x,y)\,dy\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\displaystyle\left(\left(\omega+\frac{\beta(u)\nabla u}{|\nabla u|^{\gamma}}\right)\cdot\nabla u\right)_{-}\\
\qquad{\mbox{ for $x\in\Omega$,}}\\
u(x,\cdot)=0\\
\qquad{\mbox{ for $x\in\partial\Omega$,}}\end{cases}

whenever the final value of the first solution coincides with the initial value of the second solution.

For this, we let
T_{\star}
be as in Theorem
1.1
and
\widetilde{u}_{0}
be a solution of (
4.6
)
in
[0,T_{\star}]
with initial datum
g_{0}
, as given by Theorem
1.1
.

We now argue recursively to construct a global solution. For all
k\in\mathds{N}
with
k\geqslant 1
, we set
g_{k}:=\widetilde{u}_{k-1}(\cdot,kT_{\star})

and let
\widetilde{u}_{k}
be a solution of (
4.6
)
in the time interval
[kT_{\star},(k+1)T_{\star}]
with initial datum
g_{k}
,
as given by Theorem
1.1
.

Now let
u_{0}:=\widetilde{u}_{0}
and, for all
k\in\mathds{N}
with
k\geqslant 1
,

(4.7)

u_{k}(\cdot,t):=\begin{cases}u_{k-1}(\cdot,t)&{\mbox{ if }}t\in[0,kT_{\star}],\\
\widetilde{u}_{k}(\cdot,t)&{\mbox{ if }}t\in(kT_{\star},(k+1)T_{\star}].\end{cases}

With this notation, we have the following:

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
k\in\mathds{N}
,
we have that
u_{k}
is a solution of (
4.6
) in the time interval
[0,(k+1)T_{\star}]
.

Proof.

When
k=0
, we have that
u_{0}=\widetilde{u}_{0}
is a solution of (
4.6
)
in
[0,T_{\star}]
, as desired.

When
k=1
, we have that

u_{1}(\cdot,t):=\begin{cases}u_{0}(\cdot,t)&{\mbox{ if }}t\in[0,T_{\star}],\\
\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,t)&{\mbox{ if }}t\in(T_{\star},2T_{\star}],\end{cases}

and we claim that

(4.8)

u_{1}
is a solution of (
4.6
) in the time interval
[0,2T_{\star}]
.

To establish this,
we let
\eta>0
(to be taken as small as we wish here below) and
\tau_{\eta}\in C^{\infty}(\mathds{R})
such that
\tau_{\eta}(T_{\star}-t)=\tau_{\eta}(T_{\star}+t)
for all
t\in\mathds{R}
,

\tau_{\eta}=0
in
(T_{\star}-\eta,T_{\star}+\eta)
,
\tau_{\eta}=1
in
\mathds{R}\setminus(T_{\star}-2\eta,T_{\star}+2\eta)
, and
\|\tau_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\leqslant\frac{2}{\eta}
. Given
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,2T_{\star}))
, we let
\phi_{\eta}:=\tau_{\eta}\phi
and we remark that

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,u_{1}(x,t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}_{\eta}(t)\,u_{1}(x,t)\,dt\right|\\
&\qquad=\left|\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\Big(\phi^{\prime}(t)\big(1-\tau_{\eta}(t)\big)-\phi(t)\tau_{\eta}^{\prime}(t)\Big)\,u_{1}(x,t)\,dt\right|\\
&\qquad\leqslant\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\int_{T_{\star}-2\eta}^{T_{\star}+2\eta}|u_{1}(x,t)|\,dt\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left|\int_{T_{\star}-2\eta}^{T_{\star}}\phi(t)\tau_{\eta}^{\prime}(t)u_{0}(x,t)\,dt+\int_{T_{\star}}^{T_{\star}+2\eta}\phi(t)\tau_{\eta}^{\prime}(t)\widetilde{u}_{1}(x,t)\,dt\right|\\
&\qquad\leqslant 2\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\sqrt{\eta}\,\sqrt{\int_{T_{\star}-2\eta}^{T_{\star}+2\eta}|u_{1}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left|\int_{0}^{2\eta}\phi(T_{\star}-s)\tau_{\eta}^{\prime}(T_{\star}-s)u_{0}(x,T_{\star}-s)\,ds\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+\int_{0}^{2\eta}\phi(T_{\star}+s)\tau_{\eta}^{\prime}(T_{\star}+s)\widetilde{u}_{1}(x,T_{\star}+s)\,ds\right|\\
&\qquad\leqslant 2\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\sqrt{\eta}\,\sqrt{\int_{T_{\star}-2\eta}^{T_{\star}+2\eta}|u_{1}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{2}{\eta}\int_{0}^{2\eta}\Big|\phi(T_{\star}-s)u_{0}(x,T_{\star}-s)-\phi(T_{\star}+s)\widetilde{u}_{1}(x,T_{\star}+s)\Big|\,ds\\
&\qquad\leqslant 2\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\sqrt{\eta}\,\sqrt{\int_{T_{\star}-2\eta}^{T_{\star}+2\eta}|u_{1}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{2}{\eta}\int_{0}^{2\eta}\Big|\big(\phi(T_{\star}-s)-\phi(T_{\star}+s)\big)u_{0}(x,T_{\star}-s)\Big|\,ds\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{2}{\eta}\int_{0}^{2\eta}\Big|\phi(T_{\star}+s)\big(\widetilde{u}_{1}(x,T_{\star}-s)-\widetilde{u}_{1}(x,T_{\star}+s)\big)\Big|\,ds\\
&\qquad\leqslant 2\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\sqrt{\eta}\,\sqrt{\int_{T_{\star}-2\eta}^{T_{\star}+2\eta}|u_{1}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt}+8\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\int_{0}^{2\eta}|u_{0}(x,T_{\star}-s)|\,ds\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{2\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}}{\eta}\int_{0}^{2\eta}\big|u_{0}(x,T_{\star}-s)-\widetilde{u}_{1}(x,T_{\star}+s)\big|\,ds\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\sqrt{\eta\int_{T_{\star}-2\eta}^{T_{\star}+2\eta}|u_{1}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{2\eta}\big|u_{0}(x,T_{\star}-s)-\widetilde{u}_{1}(x,T_{\star}+s)\big|^{2}\,ds},\end{split}

for some
C>0
.

As a result, up to renaming
C
line after line,

\begin{split}&\left\|\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}_{\eta}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\int_{\Omega}\left(\eta\int_{T_{\star}-2\eta}^{T_{\star}+2\eta}|u_{1}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{2\eta}\big|u_{0}(x,T_{\star}-s)-\widetilde{u}_{1}(x,T_{\star}+s)\big|^{2}\,ds\right)\,dx\\
&\qquad=C\left(\eta\int_{T_{\star}-2\eta}^{T_{\star}+2\eta}\|u_{1}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{2\eta}\|u_{0}(\cdot,T_{\star}-s)-\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,T_{\star}+s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,ds\right)\end{split}

and therefore

\begin{split}&\lim_{\eta\searrow 0}\left\|\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}_{\eta}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\lim_{\eta\searrow 0}\|u_{0}(\cdot,T_{\star}-2\eta)-\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,T_{\star}+2\eta)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\lim_{\eta\searrow 0}\Big(\|u_{0}(\cdot,T_{\star}-2\eta)-u_{0}(\cdot,T_{\star})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,T_{\star})-\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,T_{\star}+2\eta)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big).\end{split}

From this, (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), it follows that

(4.9)

\lim_{\eta\searrow 0}\left\|\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}_{\eta}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0.

We also notice that, in the weak sense (see e.g.
[
3
, page 285]
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}_{\eta}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T_{\star}-\eta}\phi^{\prime}_{\eta}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt+\int_{T_{\star}+\eta}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}_{\eta}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{T_{\star}-\eta}\phi^{\prime}_{\eta}(t)\,u_{0}(\cdot,t)\,dt+\int_{T_{\star}+\eta}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}_{\eta}(t)\,\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{0}^{T_{\star}-\eta}\phi_{\eta}(t)\,\partial_{t}u_{0}(\cdot,t)\,dt-\int_{T_{\star}+\eta}^{2T_{\star}}\phi_{\eta}(t)\,\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi_{\eta}(t)\,v(\cdot,t)\,dt,

where

v(\cdot,t):=\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{0}(\cdot,t)&{\mbox{ if }}t\in[0,T_{\star}],\\
\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,t)&{\mbox{ if }}t\in(T_{\star},2T_{\star}].\end{cases}

We insert this information into (
4.9
), finding that

(4.10)

\lim_{\eta\searrow 0}\left\|\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt+\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi_{\eta}(t)\,v(\cdot,t)\,dt\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0.

Additionally,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi_{\eta}(t)\,v(\cdot,t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi(t)\,v(\cdot,t)\,dt\right|\leqslant\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\int_{T_{\star}-2\eta}^{T_{\star}+2\eta}|v(\cdot,t)|\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}\sqrt{4\eta\int_{T_{\star}-2\eta}^{T_{\star}+2\eta}|v(\cdot,t)|^{2}\,dt}

and consequently

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\searrow 0}\left\|\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi_{\eta}(t)\,v(\cdot,t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi(t)\,v(\cdot,t)\,dt\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant 4\lim_{\eta\searrow 0}\eta\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}^{2}\iint_{\Omega\times(T_{\star}-2\eta,T_{\star}+2\eta)}|v(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant 4\lim_{\eta\searrow 0}\eta\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R})}^{2}\Big(\|\partial_{t}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((T_{\star}-2\eta,T_{\star}),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((T_{\star},T_{\star}+2\eta),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad=0,

thanks to (
4.1
).

This and (
4.10
) give that

\left\|\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt+\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi(t)\,v(\cdot,t)\,dt\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0,

whence

\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,u_{1}(\cdot,t)\,dt=-\int_{0}^{2T_{\star}}\phi(t)\,v(\cdot,t)\,dt.

This gives that
v=\partial_{t}u_{1}
in the weak sense (see e.g.
[
3
, page 285]
).

Now we
adopt the short notation in (
2.13
) and
use that
u_{0}
is a solution in
[0,T_{\star}]
and
\widetilde{u}_{1}
is a solution in
[T_{\star},2T_{\star}]
:
thus (see
[
3
, page 352]
), we have that, for a.e.
t\in[0,2T_{\star}]
and all
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}u_{1}(\cdot,t),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{1}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}f_{u_{1}}(x,t)\,\psi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle v(\cdot,t),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{1}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}f_{u_{1}}(x,t)\,\psi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\begin{cases}&\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}u_{0}(\cdot,t),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{0}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}f_{u_{0}}(x)\,\psi(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad{\mbox{ for a.e. }}t\in[0,T_{\star}],\\
&\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,t),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\widetilde{u}_{1}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}f_{\widetilde{u}_{1}}(x)\,\psi(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad{\mbox{ for a.e. }}t\in[T_{\star},2T_{\star}],\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle\qquad=0,

yielding the desired result in (
4.8
).

Recursively one also proves that
u_{k}
is a solution of (
4.6
) in the time interval
[0,(k+1)T_{\star}]

for all
k\geqslant 2
, thus completing the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

4.2.
The iteration argument

We can now complete the proof of the global in time existence result:

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, one obtains from Theorem
1.1

a solution
for a time interval
[0,T_{\star}]
, with
T_{\star}>0
depending only on
n
,
\Omega
,

\|K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)}
,

\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,1))}
, and
\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R})}
.

Thus, one defines recursively
u_{k}
as in (
4.7
)
and, in light pof Lemma
4.2
, obtains a solution in the time interval
[0,(k+1)T_{\star}]
for all
k\in\mathds{N}
with
k\geqslant 1
,
as desired.
∎

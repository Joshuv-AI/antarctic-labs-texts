A nonconforming finite element method for an elliptic optimal control problem with constraint on the gradient
Kamana Porwal and Pratibha Shakya

Abstract




Abstract.
This article is concerned with the nonconforming finite element method for distributed elliptic optimal control problems with pointwise constraints on the control and gradient of the state variable.
We reduce the minimization problem into a pure state constraint minimization problem. In this case, the solution of the minimization problem can be characterized as fourth-order elliptic variational inequalities of the first kind. To discretize the control problem we have used the bubble enriched Morley finite element method. To ensure the existence of the solution to discrete problems three bubble functions corresponding to the mean of the edge are added to the discrete space. We derive the error in the state variable in
H^{2}
-type energy norm. Numerical results are presented to illustrate our analytical findings.

Key words.
Elliptic optimal control problem, The nonconforming finite element method, variational inequality, a priori error estimates, control constraint, gradient state constraint

AMS subject classifications.
49J20, 49K20, 65N15, 65N30.

2
The control problem

This section is devoted to the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of the control problem.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded convex domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. We use the notation
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
for Sobolev spaces on
\Omega
with norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{m,p}(\Omega)}
and seminorm
|\cdot|_{W^{m,p}(\Omega)}
. We set
W_{0}^{m,p}\Omega)=\{v\in W^{m,p}(\Omega):\,v|_{\partial\Omega}=0\}.
In addition
C
denotes a positive generic constant independent of the mesh parameter.

If the domain
\Omega
is convex, then the regularity theory of the elliptic equation says that there exists a unique solution
y\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of (
1.2
).
Using
u=-\Delta y
, the minimization problem (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle\text{Find}\;{y}^{*}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle argmin_{y\in\mathcal{K}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(y-y_{d})^{2}\,dx+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta y)^{2}\,dx\Big],

\displaystyle=argmin_{y\in\mathcal{K}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}(y,y)-(y_{d},y)\Big],

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\{v\in W^{2,r}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\,|\nabla v|\leq 1\;\;\text{and}\;\;u_{a}\leq-\Delta v\leq u_{b}\;\;\text{in}\;\Omega\,\,\,\text{with}\;\;r>2\},

(2.2)

and the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
be defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\beta\int_{\Omega}(\Delta v)(\Delta w)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}vw\,dx,

\displaystyle=\beta\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\Big)\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\Big)dx+\int_{\Omega}vw\,dx

and
(v,w)=\int_{\Omega}vw\,dx.

To ensure that there exists a solution we assume the following conditions holds:

There exists
y\in W^{2,r}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies (i)
|\nabla y|<1
in
\Omega
and (ii)
u_{a}\leq(-\Delta y)\leq u_{b}
.

Remark 2.1
.

The above conditions are known as the Slater condition. These conditions play a crucial role in state-constrained optimal control problems.

The bilinear form
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric, bounded and coercive on
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. The closed convex set
\mathcal{K}
is nonempty. From the classical theory (cf.
[
37
]
), the unique solution
{y}^{*}\in W^{2,r}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) is characterized by the variational inequality

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(y,y-y^{*})\geq(y_{d},y-y^{*}).

(2.3)

From the Lagrangian approach, we have the following existence results.

Theorem 2.2
.

We have the following Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions for (
2.3
).

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}({y}^{*},v)=\int_{\Omega}{y}_{d}v\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lambda(-\Delta v)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla{y}^{*}\,d\mu

(2.4)

together with the complementary condition

\displaystyle\lambda\geq 0\;\;\;\;\;\text{if}\;\;-\Delta{y}^{*}=u_{a},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\lambda\leq 0\;\;\;\;\;\text{if}\;\;-\Delta{y}^{*}=u_{b},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\lambda=0\;\;\;\;\;\text{otherwise},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\langle|\nabla{y}^{*}|^{2},\mu\rangle_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{*}}\leq\langle\nabla v\nabla{y}^{*},\mu\rangle>_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{*}}\;\;\;\;\;\forall\,\nabla v\in\mathcal{C}(\bar{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;\;|\nabla v|\leq 1.

(2.8)

The adjoint equation is given by: Find
p^{*}\in L^{s}(\Omega)\;(s<2)
satisfy
\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}=1
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p^{*}(-\Delta v)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}({y}^{*}-y_{d})v\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}\cdot\nabla v\,d\mu\,\,\forall v\in W^{2,s^{\prime}}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(2.9)

The following regularity result for the adjoint state
{p}^{*}
is taken from
[
47
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For two dimensional domain, there exists constants
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}>0
such that
\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}\geq 1-\frac{2}{r}
, and
the solution of the adjoint equation
{p}^{*}\in W^{\gamma,r^{\prime}}(\Omega)\subset W^{\gamma,s}(\Omega)
.

An use of (
2.4
) and (
2.9
) leads to

\displaystyle\lambda=p^{*}+\beta(-\Delta{y}^{*}),

(2.10)

An application of the complementary conditions (
2.5
)-(
2.7
) gives

\displaystyle-\Delta{y}^{*}=P_{[u_{a},u_{b}]}\Big(-p^{*}/\beta\Big),

(2.11)

where
P_{[u_{a},u_{b}]}
denote the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\Omega)
onto
\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):\;u_{a}\leq v\leq u_{b}\}
defined by

\displaystyle P_{[u_{a},u_{b}]}(v):=\max\{u_{a},\min\{u_{b},v\}\}.

Corollary 2.4
.

Assume that
y^{*}
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta{y}^{*}=P_{[u_{a},u_{b}]}(-{p}^{*}/\beta)\;\;\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
{y}^{*}=0\;\;\text{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Then,
{y}^{*}\in H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)
.

An application of (
2.10
), Lemma
2.3
and Corollary
2.4
gives
\lambda\in W^{\gamma,s}(\Omega)
with
s<2
.
Use of the regularity of the adjoint variable, regularity of
\lambda
and integration by parts, will give
\mu\in W^{-\gamma,s}(\Omega)
.

Finite element discretization

This section is devoted to the finite element approximation of the minimization problem.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}

\displaystyle W_{h}=\{v_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega):v_{h}|_{T}\in span\{b_{T1},b_{T2},b_{T3}\}\;\;\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}

(3.1)

Let
V_{M}
denote the Morley finite element
[
45
]
space is defined as

\displaystyle V_{M}=

\displaystyle\{v_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega):\,v_{h}|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(T),\;v_{h}\,\text{is continuous at the vertices and the normal derivative of}\,v_{h}

\displaystyle\;\;\text{is continuous at the midpoint of the edges,}\,\text{the degree of freedom of}\,v_{h}\,\text{vanish on}\,\partial\Omega\},

The discrete finite element space
V_{h}

[

be defined by

\displaystyle V_{h}=V_{M}\oplus W_{h}.

The discrete form of the convex set
\mathcal{K}
be defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{h}:=\{v_{h}\in V_{h}:|I_{h}\nabla v_{h}|\leq 1\;\;\text{and}\;\;Q_{h}u_{a}\leq Q_{h}(-\Delta_{h}v_{h})\leq Q_{h}u_{b}\},

(3.2)

where
Q_{h}
denote the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\Omega)
onto the space of piecewise constant functions. The projection
I_{h}
be defined by

\displaystyle I_{h}(\nabla v_{h})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|T|}\int_{T}\nabla v_{h}\,dx,

The finite element approximation of the minimization problem (
2.1
) is defined as follows: Find

\displaystyle{y}^{*}_{h}=argmin_{y_{h}\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{h}(y_{h},y_{h})-(y_{d},y_{h})\Big],

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(v_{h},w_{h})=\beta\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}D^{2}v_{h}:D^{2}w_{h}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}v_{h}w_{h}\,dx

(3.4)

and the term

D^{2}v_{h}:D^{2}w_{h}=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}v_{h}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\Big)\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}w_{h}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\Big)
.
The mesh dependent norm
\|\cdot\|_{h}
defined by

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{h}^{2}=\mathcal{A}_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})=\beta\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}|v_{h}|_{H^{2}(T)}^{2}+\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

(3.5)

and we have the following estimates

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{h}(v_{h},w_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\|v_{h}\|_{h}\|w_{h}\|_{h}\;\;\;\;\;\forall v_{h},w_{h}\in[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]+V_{h},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{2}\|v_{h}\|_{h}^{2}\;\;\;\;\;\;\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(3.7)

The interpolation operator
\pi_{h}.
The interpolation operator
\pi_{h}:H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow V_{h}
is defined by

\displaystyle(\pi_{h}\xi)(p)

\displaystyle=\xi(p)\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\forall p\in\mathcal{V}_{h},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{e}\pi_{h}\xi\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{e}\xi\,ds\;\;\;\;\;\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{e}\frac{\partial(\pi_{h}\xi)}{\partial n}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{e}\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial n}\,ds\;\;\;\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}.

(3.10)

We have the following interpolation error estimates

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}h_{T}^{k}|\xi-\pi_{h}\xi|_{H^{k}(T)}\leq Ch^{\gamma+2}|\xi|_{2+\gamma,2,T}

(3.11)

Use of
3.9
gives

\displaystyle\int_{T}\nabla(\pi_{h}\xi)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{T}0\,dx+\int_{\partial T}(\pi_{h}\xi)\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\int_{e}(\pi_{h}\xi)\,ds=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\int_{e}\xi\,ds=\int_{T}\nabla\xi\,dx,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{|T|}\int_{T}\nabla(\pi_{h}\xi)\,dx\Big|=\Big|\frac{1}{|T|}\int_{T}\nabla\xi\,dx\Big|\leq\frac{1}{|T|}\int_{T}|\nabla\xi|\,dx\leq\frac{1}{|T|}|T|\leq 1.

An application of (

\displaystyle\int_{T}\Delta(\pi_{h}\xi)\,dx=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\int_{e}\frac{\partial(\pi_{h}\xi)}{\partial n}\,ds=\int_{T}(\Delta\xi)\,dx\;\;\;\;\;\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

and hence

\displaystyle Q_{h}(\Delta_{h}(\pi_{h}\xi))=Q_{h}(\Delta\xi)\;\;\;\;\;\forall\xi\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(3.13)

Use of (

The enriching operator

E_{h}.
The enriching operator

\displaystyle E_{h}:V_{h}\rightarrow M_{h}\subset H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

\displaystyle(E_{h}v_{h})(p)

\displaystyle\int_{e}\frac{\partial(E_{h}v_{h})}{\partial n}\,ds

Lemma 3.1

For

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{h}(\xi,v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})|\leq Ch^{\gamma}\|\xi\|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{h}\;\;\;\;\;\forall\xi\in H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega).

(3.22)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{h}(\xi,v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})|\leq\|\xi\|_{h}\|v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h}\|_{h}\leq C\|\xi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|v_{h}\|_{h}\;\;\;\;\forall\xi\in H^{2}(\Omega).

(3.23)

Let

,

and

, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(\xi,v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{\int_{T}D^{2}\xi:D^{2}(v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})\,dx+\int_{T}\xi(v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})\,dx\Big\}

\displaystyle~~~~~+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\xi(v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})\,dx

\displaystyle=-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\nabla(\Delta\xi)\cdot\nabla(v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})\,dx+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}[D^{2}\xi-\overline{(D^{2}\xi)}_{e}]:[[\nabla(v-E_{h}v)\otimes n]]_{e}\,ds

\displaystyle~~~~~+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\xi(v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})\,dx

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3},

where

and
[[\nabla(v-E_{h}v)\otimes n]]_{e}
over the triangles that share a common edge
e
.
Now, we estimate
E_{i}|_{i=1,2,3}
separately.
The term

is estimated as follows

\displaystyle E_{1}\leq\Big|\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\nabla(\Delta\xi)\cdot\nabla(v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq|\Delta\xi|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}|v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h}|_{H^{1}(T)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch|\xi|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\|v_{h}\|_{h}.

(3.25)

An application of the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality together with (

) yields

\displaystyle E_{2}\leq\Big|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}[D^{2}\xi-\overline{(D^{2}\xi)}_{e}]:[[\nabla(v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})\otimes n]]_{e}\,ds\Big|

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}|e|^{-1}\|D^{2}\xi-\overline{(D^{2}\xi)}_{e}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}|e|\|[[\nabla(v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})\otimes n]]_{e}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch|\xi|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\|v_{h}\|_{h}.

Use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (

) yields

\displaystyle E_{3}\leq Ch^{2}\|v_{h}\|_{h}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(3.27)

Combining (
3.25
) and (
3.27
), we get

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{h}(\xi,v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})|\leq Ch|\xi|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\|v_{h}\|_{h}\;\;\forall\xi\in H^{3}(\Omega).

(3.28)

It follows from the method of interpolation
H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)=[H^{2}(\Omega),H^{3}(\Omega)]_{2,\gamma}
, (
3.23
) and (
3.28
) that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{h}(\xi,v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h})|\leq Ch^{\gamma}\|\xi\|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}\|v_{h}\|_{h}\;\;\;\;\;\forall\xi\in H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega),

and hence we get the desired result.
∎

Using Lemma

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{h}(\pi_{h}\xi,v_{h})-\mathcal{A}(\xi,E_{h}v_{h})|\leq Ch^{\gamma}\|\xi\|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}\|v_{h}\|_{h}\;\;\forall\xi\in H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\;\;v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(3.29)

This completes the proof.

In this section, we derive the convergence property of the state variable in
H^{2}
-type norm.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
y^{*}
and
y_{h}^{*}
be the solutions of (
2.3
) and (
3.15
), respectively. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}\leq Ch^{\tau},

(4.1)

where
\tau=min\{\gamma-\delta,1-\gamma-\delta,\gamma\}
,
C
is a positive constant independent of mesh parameter
h
.

Proof.

To estimate the error
\|{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}
. We split the error as

\displaystyle\|{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}\leq\|{y}^{*}-\pi_{h}{y}^{*}\|_{h}+\|\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}.

(4.2)

Use of (
3.11
) yields the bound the first term of the above equation. Now we estimate the second term as follows: An application of (
3.7
) yields

\displaystyle C\|\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-y^{*}_{h}\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{A}_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{A}_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})-(y_{d},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}^{*},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})-(y_{d},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}^{*},E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}))-(y_{d},E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}))

\displaystyle~~~~+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}^{*},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}-E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}))-(y_{d},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}-E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})).

(4.3)

Now we estimate the terms of the right hand side of (
4
) as follows: an use of (
3.6
) together with (
3.11
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}\|_{h}\|\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}\|\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}.

(4.4)

Using the Cauchy Schwartz inequality, (
3.18
) and (
3.20
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}^{*},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}-E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}))

\displaystyle-(y_{d},\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}-E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}))

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}\|\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}.

(4.5)

Using the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker condition (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}^{*},E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}))-(y_{d},E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}))

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lambda(-\Delta E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}))~dx

\displaystyle~~~~+\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}\nabla E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})\,d\mu.

(4.6)

First we estimate the term related to the control constraints of (
4.6
).
Use of the complementary conditions (
2.5
)-(
2.7
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda[-\Delta E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})]dx=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}[-\Delta E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})]dx+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{2}[-\Delta E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})]dx,

(4.7)

where

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\begin{cases}\lambda\;\;\;\;\;\text{if}\;\;-\Delta y=u_{a}\\
0\;\;\;\;\;\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=\begin{cases}\lambda\;\;\;\;\;\text{if}\;\;-\Delta y=u_{b}\\
0\;\;\;\;\;\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(4.9)

Here
\lambda_{1}=\max(\lambda,0)
and
\lambda_{2}=\min(\lambda,0)
. Therefore,
a standard interpolation error estimate yields

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{j}-Q_{h}\lambda_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\,h^{\gamma-\delta}|\lambda_{j}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}\;\;\;\;\;\text{for}\;\;j=1,2.

(4.10)

To estimate the first term of (
4.7
), add and subtract the terms, to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}[-\Delta E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})]dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}[-\Delta(E_{h}\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*})]\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}(u_{a}-Q_{h}u_{a})\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}Q_{h}(u_{a}+\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}[\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-Q_{h}(\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})]\,dx

\displaystyle=:\hat{E}_{1}+\hat{E}_{2}+\hat{E}_{3}+\hat{E}_{4}.

(4.11)

From (
3.13
) and (
3.21
), we have

Q_{h}\Delta(E_{h}\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*})=0,

Using (
3.19
) and (
4.10
) to obtain

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}[-\Delta(E_{h}\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*})]\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{1}-Q_{h}\lambda_{1})[-\Delta(E_{h}\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*})]\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\,h^{(2\gamma-\delta)}|\lambda_{1}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}|y^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\hat{E_{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}(u_{a}-Q_{h}u_{a})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{1}-Q_{h}\lambda_{1})(u_{a}-Q_{h}u_{a})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{(2+\gamma-\delta)}|\lambda_{1}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}\|u_{a}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}.

(4.12)

An application of (
2.5
), (
3.2
), (
3.21
) and (
4.8
) gives

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}Q_{h}(u_{a}+\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}(Q_{h}u_{a}+Q_{h}(\Delta_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}))\,dx\leq 0,

The term
\hat{E}_{4}
can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}[\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-Q_{h}(\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})]\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}[\Delta(E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-{y}^{*})-Q_{h}\Delta(E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-{y}^{*})]\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}(\Delta{y}^{*}-Q_{h}\Delta{y}^{*})\,dx,

and we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}(\Delta{y}^{*}-Q_{h}\Delta{y}^{*})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{1}-Q_{h}\lambda_{1})(\Delta{y}^{*}-Q_{h}\Delta{y}^{*})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2\gamma-\delta}|\lambda_{1}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}|\Delta{y}^{*}|_{H^{\gamma}(\Omega)},

since
\Delta{y}^{*}\in H^{\gamma}(\Omega)
. Finally, (
3.19
) and (
4.10
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}[\Delta(E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-{y}^{*})-Q_{h}\Delta(E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-{y}^{*})]\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{1}-Q_{h}\lambda_{1})[\Delta(E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-{y}^{*})]\,dx

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\gamma-\delta}|\lambda_{1}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}\Big(|E_{h}({y}^{*}_{h}-\pi_{h}{y}^{*})|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+|E_{h}\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\gamma-\delta}|\lambda_{1}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}\,\Big(\|\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}+h^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}\Big).

(4.13)

Thus,

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{4}\leq Ch^{\gamma-\delta}|\lambda_{1}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}\,\Big(\|\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}+h^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}\Big)+Ch^{2\gamma-\delta}|\lambda_{1}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}|\Delta{y}^{*}|_{H^{\gamma}(\Omega)}.

Combining the estimates
\hat{E}_{i}|_{i=1,2,3,4}
to have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{1}[-\Delta E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})]\,dx\leq\hat{C}h^{\gamma-\delta}\,|\lambda_{1}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}\Big(\|\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}+h^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}\Big).

(4.14)

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{2}[-\Delta E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})]\,dx\leq\hat{C}h^{\gamma-\delta}|\lambda_{2}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}\,\Big(\|\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}+h^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{W^{2+\gamma,2}(\Omega)}\Big).

(4.15)

Altogether (
4.7
), (
4.12
), (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda[-\Delta E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})]\,dx\leq\hat{C}h^{\gamma-\delta}\Big(|\lambda_{1}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}+|\lambda_{2}|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}\Big)\,\Big(\|\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}+h^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}\Big).

(4.16)

We estimate the second term of (
4.6
) related to the gradient state constraint

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}\nabla E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(\nabla E_{h}\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(\nabla E_{h}\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-\nabla{y}^{*})\,d\mu+\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(\nabla{y}^{*}-I_{h}\nabla{y}^{*}_{h})\,d\mu

\displaystyle~~~~+\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(I_{h}\nabla{y}^{*}_{h}-I_{h}\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,d\mu+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\bar{y}(I_{h}\nabla E_{h}\bar{y}_{h}-\nabla E_{h}\bar{y}_{h})\,d\mu

\displaystyle=:G_{1}+G_{2}+G_{3}+G_{4}.

We estimate each term separately. For
G_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(\nabla E_{h}\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-\nabla{y}^{*})\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla E_{h}\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-\nabla{y}^{*}\|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1+\delta}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{1+\delta}\|{y}^{*}\|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}.

(4.17)

Use of complementary condition (
2.8
) yields

\displaystyle G_{2}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(\nabla{y}^{*}-I_{h}\nabla{y}^{*}_{h})\,d\mu\leq 0,

(4.18)

and the term
(I_{h}\nabla{y}^{*}_{h}-I_{h}\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})=0
by the property of the enriching map
E_{h}
gives

\displaystyle G_{3}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(I_{h}\nabla{y}^{*}_{h}-I_{h}\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,d\mu=0.

(4.19)

For
G_{4}
, we split the term as follows

\displaystyle G_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(I_{h}\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(I_{h}(\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-\nabla{y}^{*})-(\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-\nabla{y}^{*}))\,d\mu+\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(I_{h}\nabla{y}^{*}-\nabla{y}^{*})\,d\mu

\displaystyle=:\hat{G}_{1}+\hat{G}_{2}.

Using the regularity of
\mu

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(I_{h}(\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-\nabla{y}^{*})-(\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-\nabla{y}^{*}))\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C\|I_{h}(\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-\nabla{y}^{*})-(\nabla E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-\nabla{y}^{*})\|_{W^{\gamma,\frac{2}{1-\delta}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2(\frac{1-\delta}{2}-\frac{1}{2})}h^{1-\gamma}|E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-{y}^{*}|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{1-\delta-\gamma}|E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-{y}^{*}|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{1-\delta-\gamma}\Big[|E_{h}({y}^{*}_{h}-\pi_{h}{y}^{*})|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+|E_{h}\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{1-\delta-\gamma}[\|{y}^{*}_{h}-\pi_{h}{y}^{*}\|_{h}+h^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{W^{2+\gamma,2}(\Omega)}].

(4.20)

Note that

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}(I_{h}\nabla{y}^{*}-\nabla{y}^{*})\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\|I_{h}\nabla{y}^{*}-\nabla{y}^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

Combine the estimate of
\hat{G}_{1}
and
\hat{G}_{2}
gives

\displaystyle G_{4}\leq Ch^{1-\delta-\gamma}\Big[\|{y}^{*}_{h}-\pi_{h}{y}^{*}\|_{h}+h^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}\Big]+Ch^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}.

(4.21)

Altogether (
4.17
)-(
4.21
) and use the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla{y}^{*}\nabla E_{h}(\pi_{h}{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h})\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{1+\delta}|{y}^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}+Ch^{1-\delta-\gamma}\Big[\|{y}^{*}_{h}-\pi_{h}{y}^{*}\|_{h}+h^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}\Big]

\displaystyle+Ch^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{W^{2+\gamma,2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\gamma}|{y}^{*}|_{H^{2+\gamma}(\Omega)}+Ch^{1-\delta-\gamma}\|{y}^{*}_{h}-\pi_{h}{y}^{*}\|_{h}.

(4.22)

An application of (

Estimate (

Polynomial rate of mixing for the heterochaos baker maps with mostly neutral center Thanks: Keywords : piecewise affine map; mixing; decay of correlations
Hiroki Takahasi and Masato Tsujii

Abstract
. For the heterochaos baker maps whose central direction is mostly neutral, we prove that correlations for Hölder continuous functions decay at an optimal polynomial rate of order n − 3 / 2 n^{-3/2} . Our method of proof relies on a description of the action of a reduced Perron-Frobenius operator by means of a comparison to the symmetric simple random walk with an absorbing wall, aka ‘gambler’s ruin problem’.

1.
Introduction

The heterochaos baker maps
[
19
,
20
,
25
]
are piecewise affine maps on the square
[0,1]^{2}
or the cube
[0,1]^{3}
.
Like Smale’s horseshoe map
[
23
]
which serves as a model for uniformly hyperbolic systems,
the heterochaos baker maps are one of the simplest models for partially hyperbolic systems. Despite the complexity of their dynamics, the simplicity of their definitions enables a rather precise description of their dynamical properties.

Decay of correlations is a key metric for gauging the level of chaos. Fast decay rates indicate that the associated time series become asymptotically independent. Exponential decay of correlations is usually obtained as a consequence of some hyperbolicity of the underlying system
[
29
]
.
Typical examples of systems
with subexponential decay of correlations are maps with neutral fixed points (see e.g.,
[
10
,
14
,
17
,
22
,
30
]
), and the mechanism that slows down decay rates is essentially one-dimensional.
To shed lights on higher dimensional mechanisms leading to subexponential decay of correlations,
new concrete examples are required.

For the heterochaos baker maps
with mostly expanding or contracting center,
exponential decay of correlations was established in
[
24
]
.
The aim of this paper is to establish subexponential decay of correlations for the maps with mostly neutral center.
Below we introduce the heterochaos baker maps, and state our main result.

1.1.
The heterochaos baker maps

Let
M\geq 2
be an integer. We define the hetrochaos baker maps
f_{a}\colon[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]^{2}
and
f_{a,b}\colon[0,1]^{3}\to[0,1]^{3}
, where both parameters
a
,
b
range over the interval
(0,\frac{1}{M})
.
We write
(x_{u},x_{c})
and
(x_{u},x_{c},x_{s})
for the coordinates on
[0,1]^{2}
and
[0,1]^{3}
respectively.

For
a\in(0,\frac{1}{M})
, define a piecewise affine expanding map
\tau_{a}\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
by

\tau_{a}(x_{u})=\begin{cases}\vskip 2.84526pt\displaystyle{\frac{x_{u}-(k-1)a}{a}}&\text{ on }[(k-1)a,ka),\ k\in\{1,\ldots,M\},\\
\displaystyle{\frac{x_{u}-Ma}{1-Ma}}&\text{ on }[Ma,1].\end{cases}

Consider a set
D
of
2M
symbols

D=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{M}\}\cup\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{M}\}.

For each symbol
\gamma\in D
, define a rectangular domain
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+}
in
[0,1]^{2}
by

\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}^{+}=\left[(k-1)a,ka\right)\times\left[0,1\right]\text{ for }k\in\{1,\ldots,M\}

and

\Omega_{\beta_{k}}^{+}=\begin{cases}\vskip 2.84526pt\left[Ma,1\right]\times\displaystyle{\left[\frac{k-1}{M},\frac{k}{M}\right)}&\text{ for }k\in\{1,\ldots,M-1\},\\
\left[Ma,1\right]\times\displaystyle{\left[\frac{M-1}{M},1\right]}&\text{ for }k=M.\end{cases}

We now define
f_{a}\colon[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]^{2}
by

\begin{split}f_{a}(x_{u},x_{c})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\left(\tau_{a}(x_{u}),\frac{x_{c}}{M}+\frac{k-1}{M}\right)}&\text{ on }\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}^{+},\ k\in\{1,\ldots,M\},\\
\displaystyle{\left(\tau_{a}(x_{u}),Mx_{c}-k+1\right)}&\text{ on }\Omega_{\beta_{k}}^{+},\ k\in\{1,\ldots,M\}.\end{cases}\end{split}

The map
f_{a}
in the case
M=2
is illustrated in
Figure

.

The maps
f_{a,b}
are extensions of
f_{a}
as skew products with contracting affine maps.
Put
\Omega_{\gamma}=\Omega_{\gamma}^{+}\times[0,1]
for
\gamma\in D
. We define
f_{a,b}\colon[0,1]^{3}\to[0,1]^{3}

by

\begin{split}f_{a,b}(x_{u},x_{c},x_{s})=\begin{cases}(f_{a}(x_{u},x_{c}),(1-Mb)x_{s})&\text{ on }\Omega_{\alpha_{k}},\ k\in\{1,\ldots,M\},\\
\displaystyle{\left(f_{a}(x_{u},x_{c}),bx_{s}+1+b(k-M-1)\right)}&\text{ on }\Omega_{\beta_{k}},\ k\in\{1,\ldots,M\}.\end{cases}\end{split}

The map
f_{a,b}
in the case
M=2
is illustrated in

The maps
f_{a}

f_{a,b}
with
M=2
,
a=\frac{1}{3}
,
b=\frac{1}{6}

were introduced in
[
19
]
, and the above generalized definitions were introduced in
[
25
]
. In
[
21
]
, some variants of
f_{a,b}
were considered in the context of fractal geometry and homoclinic bifurcations of three dimensional diffeomorphisms. Iterated function systems intimately related to
f_{a,b}
was considered in

.
In
[

The dynamics of
f=f_{a,b}
is partially hyperbolic in the following sense.
Under the iteration of
f
,
the
x_{u}
-direction is expanding and the
x_{s}
-direction is contracting, while
the
x_{c}
-direction is central:
contracting by factor
\frac{1}{M}
on
\bigcup_{k=1}^{M}\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}
and expanding by factor
M
on
\bigcup_{k=1}^{M}\Omega_{\beta_{k}}
.
Since
f
is a skew product over the one-dimensional map
\tau_{a}
,
the ergodicity of
\tau_{a}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\#\left\{0\leq i<n\colon f^{i}(x)\in\bigcup_{k=1}^{M}\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}\right\}=aM\text{ for Lebesgue a.e. $x\in[0,1]^{3}$.}

If
a\in(0,\frac{1}{2M})
(resp.
a\in(\frac{1}{2M},\frac{1}{M})
), then the
x_{c}
-direction is non-uniformly expanding (resp. contracting) for Lebesgue almost every initial point.
We classify
f
into three types according to the dynamics in the
x_{c}
-direction:

•

a\in(0,\frac{1}{2M})
(mostly expanding center),

•

a\in(\frac{1}{2M},\frac{1}{M})

(mostly contracting center),

•

a=\frac{1}{2M}
(mostly neutral center).

The parameter
a=\frac{1}{2M}
is a bifurcation point at which the ‘central Lyapunov exponent’ crosses zero.
Bifurcations of similar types that involve a Lyapunov exponent crossing zero were considered in physics literature
[
1
,
15
,
16
,
28
]
,
and recently in
[
11
,
26
]
with mathematically rigorous treatments.

1.2.
Statement of the result

In what follows we assume that
f_{a,b}
preserves the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]^{3}
, denoted by
m
. This assumption is equivalent to the condition
a+b=\frac{1}{M}
(See
[
20
, Lemma 3.14]
).
For a pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of functions in
L^{2}([0,1]^{3})
,
define their correlation
by

\mathrm{Cor}(\varphi,\psi)=\left|\int\varphi\psi{\rm d}m-\int\varphi{\rm d}m\int\psi{\rm d}m\right|.

Note that
\mathrm{Cor}(\varphi,\psi)=0
if and only if
\varphi
and
\psi
are independent with respect to
m
.
Since
(f,m)
is mixing
[
20
,
24
]
,
for any pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of
L^{2}
functions we have

\mathrm{Cor}(\varphi,\psi\circ f^{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Of interest to us is how fast

\mathrm{Cor}(\varphi,\psi\circ f^{n})
decays as
n\to\infty
.
For a general pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of
L^{2}
functions, the decay of their correlation can become arbitrarily slow. To obtain a reasonable result, we assume that
\varphi
and
\psi
are Hölder continuous. For
\theta\in(0,1]
and
d=1,2,3
let
C^{\theta}([0,1]^{d})
denote the space of Hölder continuous functions on
[0,1]^{d}
with exponent
\theta
equipped with the Hölder norm
\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}}=\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}+|\varphi|_{C^{\theta}}
, where

{\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}=\sup_{x\in[0,1]^{d}}|\varphi(x)|}\ \text{ and }\ |\varphi|_{C^{\theta}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in[0,1]^{d}\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{\theta}},

and

|x-y|
denotes the Euclidean distance between
x
and
y
in
[0,1]^{d}
.

Based on a numerical experiment, it was conjectured in
[
20
]
that the decay rate of correlations
for Hölder continuous functions
is of polynomial order
n^{-3/2}
, and that this rate is optimal.
The following theorem settles this conjecture.

Main Theorem
.

For any
\theta\in(0,1]
there exists
C>0
such that for any pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of functions in
C^{\theta}([0,1]^{3})
and
the map
f=f_{\frac{1}{2M},\frac{1}{2M}}
with mostly neutral center,
we have

(1.1)

\mathrm{Cor}(\varphi,\psi\circ f^{n})\leq C\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}}\|\psi\|_{C^{\theta}}n^{-3/2}\quad\text{for all $n\geq 1$}.

The exponent
-3/2
is optimal, in that
there exist a pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of functions in
C^{\theta}([0,1]^{3})
and a constant
C(\varphi,\psi)>0
such that we have

(1.2)

\mathrm{Cor}(\varphi,\psi\circ f^{n})\geq C(\varphi,\psi)n^{-3/2}\quad\text{for all $n\geq 1$.}

The above Main Theorem complements
[
24
, Theorem B]
that established
the exponential decay of correlations for Hölder continuous functions for maps with mostly expanding or contracting center.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
24
]
, Theorem B)
.

If
a\in(0,\frac{1}{M})\setminus\{\frac{1}{2M}\}
,
then for any
\theta\in(0,1]
there exists
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that
any pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of functions in
C^{\theta}([0,1]^{3})
, there exists
C=C(\varphi,\psi)>0
such that for
f=f_{a,\frac{1}{M}-a}
we have

{\rm Cor}(\varphi,\psi\circ f^{n})\leq C\lambda^{n}\quad\text{ for all $n\geq 1$.}

In
[
24
]
, Theorem
1.1
was proved by constructing a tower with exponential tails, and then applying the general result of Young
[
29
]
. For known results on exponential decay of correlations for some partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms obtained by applying Young’s result
[
29
]
, see
[
4
,
5
,
7
]
for example.
In the case
a=\frac{1}{2M}
we can still construct a tower,
but the Lebesgue measure cannot be lifted to a finite measure on the tower,
and so
Young’s general result
[
30
]
on subexponential decay of correlations does not apply.

1.3.
Outline of the proof of the Main Theorem and organization of the paper

The rest of this paper consists of three sections.
We only consider the heterochaos baker map on
[0,1]^{3}
with mostly neutral center

f=f_{\frac{1}{2M},\frac{1}{2M}}.

Throughout Sections
2
and
3
we assume
M=2
.
In Section
2
we begin by introducing a reduced Perron-Frobenius operator that extracts
the dynamics of
f
in the
x_{c}
-direction.
Interestingly, the action of this operator can be described by the Markov process of the symmetric simple random walk with an absorbing wall, aka ‘gambler’s ruin problem’ (see
[
9
]
). With this description we deduce key polynomial estimates on the action of the reduced operator. In Section
3
, from these estimates we recover polynomial estimates on the action of the original Perron-Frobenius operator, and prove the Main Theorem in the case
M=2
.
In Section
4
we indicate necessary minor modifications to deal with the case
M>2
.

1.4.
Remarks on
T
,
T^{-1}
transformations

The heterochaos baker maps are closely related to
T
,
T^{-1}
transformations
[
12
,
18
]
.
Let
(X,S,\mu)
and
(Y,T,\nu)
be two ergodic probability preserving transformations, with
T
invertible and let
h\colon X\to\mathbb{Z}
be measurable.
The associated
T,T^{-1}
transformation is the map

(x,y)\in X\times Y\mapsto(S(x),T^{h(x)}y)\in X\times Y.

For instance, one may take
X=Y=[0,1]
,
S=\tau
,
h=\mathbbm{1}_{[\frac{1}{2},1]}-\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\frac{1}{2})}
, where
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
denotes the indicator function for a set
A\subset[0,1]
.
The definition does not work with
T(y)=My
mod
1
as
T
is not invertible, but one may be able to subvert this obstacle by using the first coordinate
x_{u}
to decide which branch of
T^{-1}
to use. With this kind of analogy, the case with non-zero drift
(
\int h{\rm d}\mu\neq 0
) corresponds to the case
a\neq\frac{1}{2M}
and the case with zero drift
(\int h{\rm d}\mu=0
)
corresponds to the case
a=\frac{1}{2M}
.
The mechanism for exponential mixing of
f_{a}
,
f_{a,\frac{1}{M}-a}
with
a\neq\frac{1}{2M}

proved in
[
24
]
is similar to that of generalized
T,T^{-1}
transformations with non-zero drift
[
8
, Theorem 4.1(a)]
. For generalized
T
,
T^{-1}
transformations
with zero drift, polynomial mixing rates faster than
n^{-1}
were obtained in

[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
8
, Theorem 4.7]
.

2.
Polynomial estimates on Reduced Perron-Frobenius operator

The aim of this section is to establish
polynomial decay estimates on the reduced Perron-Frobenius operator.

Hypothesis throughout Section 2 and Section 3:
M=2
.

In Section
2.1
we introduce this operator
\mathcal{P}_{0}
, together with the original Perron-Frobenius operator
\mathcal{P}
, and state the main polynomial decay estimates in Theorem
2.1
. In Section
2.2
we introduce a decomposition of the function space on which
\mathcal{P}_{0}
acts, and in Section
2.3
start to analyze this action. In Section
2.4
we compare the action of
\mathcal{P}_{0}
with the symmetric simple random walk with an absorbing wall, and deduce key estimates.
We establish a polynomial lower bound in Section
2.5
, and
complete the proof of the statement of Theorem
2.1
in Section
2.6
.

2.1.
Reduced Perron-Frobenius operator

Let
f\colon[0,1]^{3}\to[0,1]^{3}
be the heterochaos baker map with mostly neutral center. The Perron-Frobenius operator

\mathcal{P}\colon L^{1}([0,1]^{3})\to L^{1}([0,1]^{3})
associated to
(f,m)
is given by

\mathcal{P}u=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}m}\int_{f^{-1}(\cdot)}u{\rm d}m\ \text{ for }u\in L^{1}([0,1]^{3}).

Disregarding sets with Lebesgue measure zero, we have

(2.1)

\mathcal{P}u=u\circ f^{-1}.

For
u,v\in L^{2}([0,1]^{3})
we have

\langle u,v\circ f\rangle=\langle\mathcal{P}u,v\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the
L^{2}
inner product:

\langle u,v\rangle=\int u\cdot\overline{v}\;{\rm d}m.

Let
L^{1}_{c}([0,1]^{3})
denote the subspace of
L^{1}([0,1]^{3})
that consists of functions depending only on
x_{c}
. We naturally
identify
L^{1}_{c}([0,1]^{3})
with
L^{1}([0,1])
. Let
\Pi\colon L^{1}([0,1]^{3})\to L^{1}_{c}([0,1]^{3})
denote the projection defined by

(\Pi u)(x_{c})=\int_{[0,1]}\int_{[0,1]}u(s,x_{c},t){\rm d}s{\rm d}t.

We introduce a
reduced Perron-Frobenius operator

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\Pi\circ\mathcal{P}:L^{1}_{c}([0,1]^{3})\to L^{1}_{c}([0,1]^{3}).

We will give a precise description of this operator.
Let

L^{2}_{0}([0,1])=\left\{u\in L^{2}([0,1])\colon\langle u,1\rangle=0\right\}.

Note that
\mathcal{P}_{0}(L^{2}_{0}([0,1]))\subset L^{2}_{0}([0,1])
.
The aim of this section is to prove the next theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists a constant
C>1
such that for any pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of functions in

C^{\theta}([0,1])\cap L^{2}_{0}([0,1])
, we have

|\langle\mathcal{P}_{0}^{n}\varphi,\psi\rangle|\leq Cn^{-3/2}\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}}\|\psi\|_{C^{\theta}}\quad\text{for all $n\geq 1$}.

Moreover, the exponent
-3/2
is optimal, in that there exist a pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of functions in

C^{\theta}([0,1])\cap L^{2}_{0}([0,1])

and a constant
C(\varphi,\psi)>0
such that

|\langle\mathcal{P}_{0}^{n}\varphi,\psi\rangle|\geq C(\varphi,\psi)n^{-3/2}\quad\text{for all }n\geq 1.

2.2.
Decomposition of the Hilbert space

It is convenient to
analyze the action of the reduced Perron-Frobenius operator
\mathcal{P}_{0}
using the Haar wavelets
[
27
]
. Define a function

\chi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\chi(x)=\begin{cases}\vskip 2.84526pt1&\text{if }\displaystyle{x\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right)},\\
-1&\text{if }\displaystyle{x\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For
\ell
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
define a function
\chi_{\ell,k}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
by

\chi_{\ell,k}(x)=\chi(2^{\ell-1}x-k).

Note that
\chi_{1,0}=\chi|_{[0,1]},

{\rm supp}(\chi_{\ell,k})=[k2^{-\ell+1},(k+1)2^{-\ell+1}],

and

(2.2)

\left|\sum_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\chi_{\ell,k}(x)\right|=1\ \text{ for all $x\in[0,1)$.}

The system
\{2^{(\ell-1)/2}\cdot\chi_{\ell,k}\}_{\ell,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of functions is an orthonomal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

[
27
, Theorem 1.4]
.
Hence the subsystem
\{2^{(\ell-1)/2}\cdot\chi_{\ell,k}\colon\ell\geq 1,\ 0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}\}
is an orthonormal basis of the space

L^{2}_{0}([0,1]).

For each
\ell\geq 1
, let
H_{\ell}
denote the
2^{\ell}
-dimensional closed linear subspace of
L^{2}_{0}([0,1])
that is spanned by the functions
\{\chi_{\ell,k}\colon 0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}\}
.
The Hilbert space
L^{2}_{0}([0,1])
is decomposed into the mutually orthogonal subspaces
H_{\ell}
:

(2.3)

L^{2}_{0}([0,1])=\overline{\bigoplus_{\ell\geq 1}H_{\ell}}.

Each
\varphi\in L^{2}_{0}([0,1])
is uniquely written as

(2.4)

\varphi=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell},\quad\varphi_{\ell}\in H_{\ell}.

Since
\{2^{(\ell-1)/2}\chi_{\ell,k}\colon\ell\geq 1,0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}_{0}([0,1])
, we have

(2.5)

\varphi_{\ell}=\sum_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\langle\varphi,\chi_{\ell,k}\rangle 2^{\ell-1}\chi_{\ell,k}.

2.3.
The action of the reduced Perron-Frobenius operator

The operator
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is written as the sum

(2.6)

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}+\mathcal{P}_{\beta},

where

(2.7)

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}u(x)&=\begin{cases}\vskip 2.84526pt\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}u(2x)}&\text{ if }\displaystyle{x\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right)},\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}u(2x-1)}&\text{ if }\displaystyle{x\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right],}\end{cases}\end{split}

and

(2.8)

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{\beta}u(x)&=\frac{1}{4}u\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4}u\left(\frac{x+1}{2}\right)\quad\text{ for }x\in[0,1].\end{split}

The two operators
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\beta}
reflect the contraction in the
x_{c}
-direction on
\Omega_{\alpha_{1}}\cup\Omega_{\alpha_{2}}
and
the expansion in the
x_{c}
-direction on
\Omega_{\beta_{1}}\cup\Omega_{\beta_{2}}
respectively.
Note that if
u\in L^{1}([0,1])
is strictly increasing (resp. decreasing) then
\mathcal{P}_{0}u
is strictly increasing (resp. decreasing).

We have

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(H_{\ell})\subset H_{\ell+1}\ \text{ and }\ \mathcal{P}_{\beta}(H_{\ell+1})\subset H_{\ell}\ \text{ for all $\ell\geq 1$,}

and

\mathcal{P}_{\beta}(H_{1})=\{0\}.

See
Figure
3
.

For the operator norm
\|\cdot\|
with respect to the
L^{\infty}
norm, it is easy to check

(2.9)

\|\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}|_{H_{\ell}}\|=\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathcal{P}_{\beta}|_{H_{\ell+1}}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\ \text{ for all $\ell\geq 1$}.

Lemma 2.2
.

(a)

If
\varphi\in C^{\theta}([0,1])\cap L^{2}_{0}([0,1])
, then for all
\ell\geq 1

we have

\|\varphi_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2^{-\theta\ell}\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}}.

(b)

If
\varphi\in L^{2}_{0}([0,1])

is strictly increasing (resp. decreasing), then
for all
\ell\geq 1
we have

\max_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\langle\varphi,\chi_{\ell,k}\rangle<0\quad\left(\text{resp. }\min_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\langle\varphi,\chi_{\ell,k}\rangle>0\right).

Proof.

For
0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}
, we have

\begin{split}|\langle\varphi,\chi_{\ell,k}\rangle|&=\left|\int_{2^{-(\ell-1)}k}^{2^{-(\ell-1)}\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\varphi(x){\rm d}x-\int^{2^{-(\ell-1)}(k+1)}_{2^{-(\ell-1)}\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\varphi(x){\rm d}x\right|\\
&=\left|\int_{2^{-(\ell-1)}k}^{2^{-(\ell-1)}\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\big(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x+2^{-\ell})\big){\rm d}x\right|\\
&\leq 2^{-\theta\ell}\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}}\cdot 2^{-\ell},\end{split}

and thus

\|\varphi_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\max_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}|\langle\varphi,\chi_{\ell,k}\rangle|\cdot 2^{\ell-1}\cdot\|\chi_{\ell,k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2^{-\theta\ell}\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}},

as required in (a). Since

\langle\varphi,\chi_{\ell,k}\rangle=\int_{2^{-(\ell-1)}k}^{2^{-(\ell-1)}\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\big(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x+2^{-\ell})\big){\rm d}x,

2.4.
Comparison with gambler’s ruin problem

The symmetric simple random walk on
\mathbb{N}
with an absorbing wall at
0
is a well-known model in probability theory, aka ‘gambler’s ruin problem’.
In this model, a particle starts at an integer point in
\mathbb{N}
, moves to the right or left with equal probability
1/2
. The particle is absorbed, or disappears from the system once it reaches the point
0
.

Suppose that the particle is initially distributed at
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with probability
q^{(0)}_{\ell}
:

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}q_{\ell}^{(0)}=1
.
The probability
q^{(n)}_{\ell}
with which the particle is at
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
at time
n\geq 1
is given by the recursion formula

(2.10)

q^{(n)}_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\vskip 2.84526pt\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}q^{(n-1)}_{\ell-1}+\frac{1}{2}q^{(n-1)}_{\ell+1}}&\quad\text{if $\ell\geq 2$,}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}q^{(n-1)}_{\ell+1}}&\quad\text{if $\ell=1$.}\end{cases}

The total probability
\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}q_{\ell}^{(n)}
decreases as
n
increases due to the absorption at
0
.
For
\ell
,
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\geq 1
, let
p_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{(n)}
denote the probability with which the particle starts at
\ell
and reaches
\ell^{\prime}
at time
n
without passing
0
. We have

(2.11)

q_{\ell}^{(n)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell^{\prime}\geq 1\\
|\ell^{\prime}-\ell|\leq n\end{subarray}}q^{(0)}_{\ell^{\prime}}p^{(n)}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}.

Taking an appropriate initial probability distribution, one can estimate
\varphi_{\ell}^{(n)}

in Lemma
2.3
in terms of
q_{\ell}^{(n)}
as follows.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\theta}([0,1])\cap L^{2}_{0}([0,1])
,
\varphi\not\equiv 0
, and for
n\geq 0

write
\mathcal{P}_{0}^{n}\varphi=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell}^{(n)}
,
\varphi_{\ell}^{(n)}\in H_{\ell}
. Let

(q_{\ell}^{(n)})_{n=0,\ell=1}^{\infty}

be given by (
2.10
) and the initial condition

(2.12)

q^{(0)}_{\ell}=C_{\varphi}^{-1}\|\varphi^{(0)}_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}\quad\text{with }C_{\varphi}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\|\varphi^{(0)}_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}\in(0,\infty).

Then, for all
n\geq 0
we have

(2.13)

\|\varphi_{\ell}^{(n)}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{\varphi}q^{(n)}_{\ell}\ \text{ for all }\ell\geq 1.

If we further assume that
\varphi
is strictly monotone, then the inequality in (
2.13
) becomes the equality.

Proof.

The boundedness
of
C_{\varphi}
follows from
Lemma
2.2
(a).
We prove (
2.13
) by induction on
n
. For
n=0
,
(
2.13
) is obvious
from (
2.12
). Let
k\geq 0
and suppose
(
2.13
) holds for
n=k
. Comparing the inequalities in Lemma
2.3
with (
2.10
), we get (
2.13
) for
n=k+1
. The proof is complete.
∎

In order to estimate
q_{\ell}^{(n)}
from above, we compute the transition probability
p_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{(n)}
and
give an upper bound when both
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
are small relative to
n
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For all
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\geq 1
and
n\geq 1
with
|\ell-\ell^{\prime}|\leq n
, we have

p_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{(n)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{2^{-n}\left(\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}}{2}}-\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell-\ell^{\prime}}{2}}\right)}&\quad\text{if $n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}$ is even,}\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Although the derivation of this formula is standard in probability theory, we give a proof for completeness.
If
n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}
is odd, then clearly
p^{(n)}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=0
. Suppose
n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}
is even.
Consider the symmetric simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
. For each
n\geq 1

let
A_{n}
denote the set of paths that start from
\ell
and reach
\ell^{\prime}
at time
n
(possibly passing
0
).
Let
B_{n}
denote the set of paths that start from
\ell
and reach
\ell^{\prime}
at time
n
passing
0
at least once. Clearly we have

2^{n}p^{(n)}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\#A_{n}-\#B_{n},

and

\#A_{n}=2^{n}\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}}{2}}.

Let
C_{n}
denote the set of paths that start from
\ell
and reach
-\ell^{\prime}
at time
n
. We have

\#C_{n}=2^{n}\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell-\ell^{\prime}}{2}}.

Let
(s_{k})_{k=0}^{n}
be an arbitrary path in
B_{n}
:

s_{0}=\ell
,
s_{n}=\ell^{\prime}
and
s_{k}=0
for some
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
.
Let
\nu=\max\{1\leq k\leq n-1\colon s_{k}=0\}
and define another path
(s_{k}^{\prime})_{k=0}^{n}
by

s_{k}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}s_{k}&\quad\text{for $k\leq\nu$,}\\
-s_{k}&\quad\text{for $k>\nu$.}\end{cases}

Clearly, this correspondence of paths is a bijection from
B_{n}
to
C_{n}
, and so
\#B_{n}=\#C_{n}
. Hence the desired equality holds.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

For all sufficiently large
n\geq 1
and
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\geq 1
such that
n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}
is even and
\max\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}\leq n^{1/4}
, we have

C_{0}^{-1}n^{-3/2}\ell\ell^{\prime}\leq p_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{(n)}\leq C_{0}n^{-3/2}\ell\ell^{\prime},

for some constant
C_{0}>1
independent of
n,\ell,\ell^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We put

P_{n,\ell,\ell^{\prime}}=2^{-n}\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell-\ell^{\prime}}{2}},

which is the transition probability from
\ell
to
-\ell^{\prime}
of the symmetric simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
.
Using Stirling’s approximation for factorials,
we obtain

(2.14)

\frac{1}{C\sqrt{n}}\leq 2^{-n}\binom{n}{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}+n^{1/4}\rfloor}\leq P_{n,\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\leq 2^{-n}\binom{n}{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}},

for some constant
C>1
independent of
n,\ell,\ell^{\prime}
.
A direct calculation shows

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}}{2}}}{\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell-\ell^{\prime}}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\left(\frac{n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}}{2}\right)!\left(\frac{n+\ell-\ell^{\prime}}{2}\right)!}\cdot\frac{\left(\frac{n-\ell-\ell^{\prime}}{2}\right)!\left(\frac{n+\ell+\ell^{\prime}}{2}\right)!}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\frac{n+\ell+\ell^{\prime}}{2}\right)!}{\left(\frac{n+\ell-\ell^{\prime}}{2}\right)!}\cdot\frac{\left(\frac{n-\ell-\ell^{\prime}}{2}\right)!}{\left(\frac{n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}}{2}\right)!}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}\frac{n+\ell+\ell^{\prime}-2k}{2}\prod_{k^{\prime}=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}\frac{2}{n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}-2k^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}\frac{n+\ell+\ell^{\prime}-2k}{n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}-2k}=\prod_{k=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}\frac{1+\frac{\ell+\ell^{\prime}-2k}{n}}{1-\frac{\ell-\ell^{\prime}+2k}{n}},

and thus

(2.15)

2^{-n}\left(\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}}{2}}-\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell-\ell^{\prime}}{2}}\right)=P_{n,\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}\frac{1+\frac{\ell+\ell^{\prime}-2k}{n}}{1-\frac{\ell-\ell^{\prime}+2k}{n}}-1\right).

By the assumption
\max\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}\leq n^{1/4}
and the simple estimate

1+x\leq e^{x}
when
|x|
is small,
we have

\begin{split}\prod_{k=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}\frac{1+\frac{\ell+\ell^{\prime}-2k}{n}}{1-\frac{\ell-\ell^{\prime}+2k}{n}}&=\prod_{k=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}\left(1+\frac{\frac{2\ell}{n}}{1-\frac{\ell-\ell^{\prime}+2k}{n}}\right)\leq\prod_{k=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}\exp\left(\frac{\frac{2\ell}{n}}{1-\frac{\ell-\ell^{\prime}+2k}{n}}\right)\\
&\leq\prod_{k=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}\exp\left(\frac{3\ell}{n}\right)=\exp\left(\frac{3\ell\ell^{\prime}}{n}\right).\end{split}

Plugging this estimate into the right-hand side of (
2.15
), and then using
(
2.14
) and another simple estimate

e^{x}\leq 1+x+x^{2}
when
|x|
is small, we obtain

\displaystyle 2^{-n}\left(\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}}{2}}-\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell-\ell^{\prime}}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq P_{n,\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{3\ell\ell^{\prime}}{n}\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4Cn^{-1/2}\cdot\frac{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{n}=4Cn^{-3/2}\ell\ell^{\prime}.

Similarly, by
\max\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}\leq n^{1/4}
and
1+x\geq e^{x/2}
when
x>0
is small, we have

\prod_{k=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}\frac{1+\frac{\ell+\ell^{\prime}-2k}{n}}{1-\frac{\ell-\ell^{\prime}+2k}{n}}\geq\exp\left(\frac{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{n}\right).

Plugging this estimate into the right-hand side of (
2.15
),
and then using (
2.14
) and
e^{x}\geq 1+x
when
|x|
is small, we obtain

\displaystyle 2^{-n}\left(\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell+\ell^{\prime}}{2}}-\binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell-\ell^{\prime}}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq P_{n,\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{n}\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}n^{-1/2}\cdot\frac{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{n}=C^{-1}\ell\ell^{\prime}n^{-3/2}.

Taking
C_{0}=4C
yields the desired inequalities.
∎

3.
Proof of the Main Theorem in the case
M=2

In this section we complete the proof of the Main Theorem for the case
M=2
using the original Perron-Frobenius operator
\mathcal{P}
in (
2.1
). We take a pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of functions in
C^{\theta}([0,1]^{3})
and estimate their correlation

\mathrm{Cor}(\varphi,\psi\circ f^{n})=\left|\langle\mathcal{P}^{n}\varphi,\psi\rangle-\langle\varphi,1\rangle\langle\psi,1\rangle\right|.

Since the correlation does not change even if we subtract constant functions from
\varphi
and
\psi
, we may assume
\langle\varphi,1\rangle=\langle\psi,1\rangle=0
without loss of generality.

The argument below is mostly parallel to that of the proof of Theorem
2.1
in Section
2
, but has some important differences. The obvious difference is that
\mathcal{P}
acts on the space of functions on
[0,1]^{3}
.
In Section
3.1
we introduce a few notations that we will work with. One important difference from Section
2
is that instead of the

L^{\infty}
norm we work with an anisotropic norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta/2}
introduced
in Section
3.2
. In
Section
3.3
and Section
3.4
we analyze the action of
\mathcal{P}
,
and complete the proof of the Main Theorem in Section
3.5
.

3.1.
A decomposition of the Hilbert space

In order to trace the argument in Section
2
, we regard functions on
[0,1]^{3}
as
tensor products of functions of
two variables
(x_{u},x_{s})\in[0,1]^{2}
and
functions of one variable
x_{c}\in[0,1]
. For
u\in L^{2}([0,1]^{2})
and
v\in L^{2}([0,1])
, we write
u\otimes v\in L^{2}([0,1]^{3})
for the function

(3.1)

u\otimes v(x_{u},x_{c},x_{s})=u(x_{u},x_{s})v(x_{c}).

For
d=2,3
we set

L^{2}_{0}([0,1]^{d})=\{u\in L^{2}([0,1]^{d}\colon\langle u,1\rangle=0\}.

We are going to consider the operator
\mathcal{P}
acting on the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{0}([0,1]^{3})
.

We write
\chi_{0}
for the constant function on
[0,1]
that takes value
1
.
Recall that the collection of functions
\{\chi_{0}\}\cup\{\chi_{\ell,k}\colon\ell\geq 1,0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}\}
is an orthogonal basis of
L^{2}([0,1])
.
For each
\ell\geq 0
, let
\hat{H}_{\ell}
denote the infinite dimensional subspace of
L^{2}_{0}([0,1]^{3})
given by

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}u_{\ell,k}\otimes\chi_{\ell,k}\colon u_{\ell,k}\in L^{2}_{0}([0,1]^{2})\right\}\ \text{ if $\ell\geq 1$,}

and

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=\{u_{0}\otimes\chi_{0}\colon u_{0}\in L^{2}_{0}([0,1]^{2})\}.

The space
L^{2}_{0}([0,1]^{3})
is decomposed into the mutually orthogonal subspaces
\hat{H}_{\ell}
:

L^{2}_{0}([0,1]^{3})=\overline{\bigoplus_{\ell\geq 0}\hat{H}_{\ell}}.

Unlike the space
L_{0}^{2}([0,1])
in (
2.3
)
we now have the component
\hat{H}_{0}
. This is the main point to be worked out in Section
3.4
.

Under the notation (
3.1
),
each function
\varphi\in L_{0}^{2}([0,1]^{3})
is uniquely written as

(3.2)

\varphi=\varphi_{0,0}\otimes\chi_{0}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\varphi_{\ell,k}\otimes\chi_{\ell,k},

so that
\varphi_{0,0}\otimes\chi_{0}\in\hat{H}_{0}
and
\sum_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\varphi_{\ell,k}\otimes\chi_{\ell,k}\in\hat{H}_{\ell}
, where

\displaystyle\varphi_{0,0}(x_{u},x_{s})

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}\varphi(x_{u},t,x_{s}){\rm d}t\ \text{ and }

\displaystyle\varphi_{\ell,k}(x_{u},x_{s})

\displaystyle=2^{\ell-1}\int_{[0,1]}\varphi(x_{u},t,x_{s})\chi_{\ell,k}(t){\rm d}t.

The same reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
2.2
(a) yields the estimates

\|\varphi_{0,0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}},

and

\sup_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\|\varphi_{\ell,k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2^{-\theta\ell}\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}}\ \text{ for all }\ell\geq 1.

3.2.
Anisotropic norms

For an element
u(x_{u},x_{s})
of
L^{2}([0,1]^{2})
, we write
u(\cdot,x_{s})
for the function
x_{u}\mapsto u(x_{u},x_{s})
with the second variable
x_{s}
fixed.
Let
0<\theta<1
.
We define
\hat{C}^{\theta}([0,1]^{2})\subset L^{2}([0,1]^{2})
as the subspace of functions
u(x_{u},x_{s})\in L^{\infty}([0,1]^{2})
such that the function
u(\cdot,x_{s})
belongs to
C^{\theta}([0,1])
for all
x_{s}\in[0,1]
. For a function

\hat{C}^{\theta}([0,1]^{2})
and
x_{s}\in[0,1]
, we define

|u(\cdot,x_{s})|_{C^{\theta}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{u},x^{\prime}_{u}\in[0,1]\\
x_{u}\neq x^{\prime}_{u}\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(x_{u},x_{s})-u(x^{\prime}_{u},x_{s})|}{|x_{u}-x^{\prime}_{u}|^{\theta}}.

In the same spirit as that of the anisotropic norms in
[
2
]
, we introduce the following norm on the space
\hat{C}^{\theta}([0,1]^{2})
:

(3.3)

\|u\|_{\theta/2}=|u|_{\infty}+|u|_{\theta/2}

where

|u|_{\infty}=\int_{[0,1]}\|u(\cdot,x_{s})\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm d}x_{s}\ \text{ and }\ |u|_{\theta}=\int_{[0,1]}|u(\cdot,x_{s})|_{C^{\theta}}{\rm d}x_{s}.

Note that the subscript in (
3.3
) is
\theta/2
and not
\theta
, which is intentional.
Clearly we have

(3.4)

\|u\|_{L^{1}}\leq|u|_{\infty}\leq\|u\|_{\theta/2}.

For each
\ell\geq 1
, we define a subspace
\hat{H}_{\ell,\hat{C}^{\theta}}
of
\hat{H}_{\ell}
by

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\ell,\hat{C}^{\theta}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}u_{\ell,k}\otimes\chi_{\ell,k}\colon u_{\ell,k}\in\hat{C}^{\theta}([0,1]^{2})\right\}.

For the case
\ell=0
, we set

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0,\hat{C}^{\theta}}

\displaystyle=\left\{u_{0,0}\otimes\chi_{0}\colon u_{0,0}\in\hat{C}^{\theta}([0,1]^{2})\cap L^{2}_{0}([0,1]^{2})\right\}.

By definition, each
u\in\hat{H}_{\ell,\hat{C}^{\theta}}
is written in the form

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}u_{\ell,k}\otimes\chi_{\ell,k}\quad\text{ if }\ell\geq 1

and

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=u_{0,0}\otimes\chi_{0}\quad\text{ if }\ell=0

with
u_{\ell,k}\in\hat{C}^{\theta}([0,1]^{2})
.
We define a norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta/2}^{(\ell)}
on
\hat{H}_{\ell,\hat{C}^{\theta}}
by

\|u\|_{\theta/2}^{(\ell)}=\begin{cases}\max_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\|u_{\ell,k}\|_{\theta/2}&\quad\text{ if }\ell\geq 1,\\
\|u_{0,0}\|_{\theta/2}&\quad\text{ if }\ell=0.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\varphi\in C^{\theta}([0,1]^{3})
, we write

\varphi=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell}
with
\varphi_{\ell}\in\hat{H}_{\ell}
. Then we have

\|\varphi_{\ell}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|\varphi_{\ell}\|_{\theta/2}^{(\ell)}\ \text{ for all }\ell\geq 0.

Proof.

We have
\varphi_{\ell}=\sum_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\varphi_{\ell,k}\otimes\chi_{\ell,k}
for
\ell\geq 1
and

\varphi_{0}=\varphi_{0,0}\otimes\chi_{0}
.
For
\ell\geq 1
, using (
2.2
) and (
3.4
), we see

\|\varphi_{\ell}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\max_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\|\varphi_{\ell,k}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\max_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\|\varphi_{\ell,k}\|_{\theta/2}=\|\varphi_{\ell}\|_{\theta/2}^{(\ell)}.

Using (
3.4
) we also have

\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|\varphi_{0,0}\|_{L^{1}}=\|\varphi_{0,0}\|_{\theta/2}^{(0)}
.
∎

Below we prove two technical lemmas on the new norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta/2}^{(\ell)}

that will be used later.
The next lemma is an analogue of Lemma
2.2
(a).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\theta}([0,1]^{3})\cap L^{2}_{0}([0,1]^{3})
and write

\varphi=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell}
,
\varphi_{\ell}\in\hat{H}_{\ell}
. Then we have

\|\varphi_{\ell}\|_{\theta/2}^{(\ell)}\leq 2\cdot 2^{-(\theta/2)\ell}\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}}\ \text{ for all }\ell\geq 0.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{0,0}
and
\varphi_{\ell,k}
be the
coefficients in the expansion (
3.2
)
of
\varphi
. It suffices to show that

\|\varphi_{0,0}\|_{\theta/2}\leq 2\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}}

and

\max_{0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}}\|\varphi_{\ell,k}\|_{\theta/2}\leq 2\cdot 2^{-(\theta/2)\ell}\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}}\ \text{ for all }\ell\geq 1.

Let
\ell\geq 1
and
0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}
. An argument similar to the one in the proof of Lemma
2.2
gives

|\varphi_{\ell,k}|_{\infty}\leq 2^{-\theta\ell}|\varphi|_{C^{\theta}}.

Observe that

|\varphi_{\ell,k}|_{\theta/2}\leq\sup_{x_{s}\in[0,1]}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{u},x_{u}^{\prime}\in[0,1]\\
x_{u}\neq x^{\prime}_{u}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|\int_{[0,1]}(\varphi(x_{u},x_{c},x_{s})-\varphi(x^{\prime}_{u},x_{c},x_{s}))\cdot 2^{\ell-1}\chi_{\ell,k}(x_{c}){\rm d}x_{c}\right|}{|x_{u}-x^{\prime}_{u}|^{\theta/2}}.

From an obvious estimate

|\varphi(x_{u},x_{c},x_{s})-\varphi(x^{\prime}_{u},x_{c},x_{s})|\leq|\varphi|_{C^{\theta}}\cdot|x_{u}-x^{\prime}_{u}|^{\theta},

we have

\left|\int_{[0,1]}(\varphi(x_{u},x_{c},x_{s})-\varphi(x^{\prime}_{u},x_{c},x_{s}))\cdot 2^{\ell-1}\chi_{\ell,k}(x_{c}){\rm d}x_{c}\right|\leq|\varphi|_{C^{\theta}}\cdot|x_{u}-x^{\prime}_{u}|^{\theta}.

Besides, by an estimate similar to that in the proof of Lemma
2.2
(a), we have

\left|\int_{[0,1]}(\varphi(x_{u},x_{c},x_{s})-\varphi(x^{\prime}_{u},x_{c},x_{s}))\cdot 2^{\ell-1}\chi_{\ell,k}(x_{c}){\rm d}x_{c}\right|\leq 2^{-\theta\ell}|\varphi|_{C^{\theta}}.

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[0,1]}(\varphi(x_{u},x_{c},x_{s})-\varphi(x^{\prime}_{u},x_{c},x_{s}))\cdot 2^{\ell}\chi_{\ell,k}(x_{c}){\rm d}x_{c}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\min\left\{2^{-\theta\ell},|x_{u}-x^{\prime}_{u}|^{\theta}\right\}\cdot|\varphi|_{C^{\theta}}\leq 2^{-\theta\ell/2}|x_{u}-x^{\prime}_{u}|^{\theta/2}\cdot|\varphi|_{C^{\theta}}.

We conclude
\|\varphi_{\ell,k}\|_{\theta/2}\leq 2\cdot 2^{-\theta\ell/2}|\varphi|_{C^{\theta}}
as required. We obtain the same estimate on
\varphi_{0,0}
by a similar argument.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I
be a non-degenerate closed subinterval of
[0,1]
. Put

R=I\times[0,1]
and suppose that
g\colon R\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is an invertible affine map of the form
g(x_{u},x_{s})=(\alpha x_{u}+p_{u},\beta x_{s}+p_{s})
with
0<\beta<1<\alpha
such that
g(R)=[0,1]\times J
with
J\subset[0,1]
. For a function
u\in\hat{C}^{\theta}([0,1]^{2})
, define

\tilde{u}\colon[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\tilde{u}(x_{u},x_{s})=\begin{cases}u\circ g^{-1}(x_{u},x_{s}),&\text{ if $(x_{u},x_{s})\in g(R)$,}\\
0,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then we have
\tilde{u}\in\hat{C}^{\theta}([0,1]^{2})
and

\|\tilde{u}\|_{\theta/2}\leq|\beta|\|u\|_{\theta/2}.

Proof.

For
(x_{u},x_{s})\in g(R)
, we set

g^{-1}(x_{u},x_{s})=(x_{u}^{\prime},x_{s}^{\prime})
.
By definition we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{u}|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\|\tilde{u}(\cdot,x_{s})\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm d}x_{s}\leq\int_{[0,1]}\|u(\cdot,x_{s}^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}}\beta{\rm d}x_{s}^{\prime}\leq|\beta||u|_{\infty},

and similarly

\displaystyle|\tilde{u}|_{\theta/2}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}|\tilde{u}(\cdot,x_{s})|_{C^{\theta/2}}{\rm d}x_{s}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,1]}|\alpha|^{-\theta/2}|u(\cdot,x_{s}^{\prime})|_{C^{\theta/2}}\beta{\rm d}x^{\prime}_{s}\leq|\alpha|^{-\theta/2}|\beta|\,|u|_{\theta/2}\leq|\beta|\,|u|_{\theta/2}.

These estimates yield the claim of the lemma.
∎

,
we apply the first inequality in Lemma
3.8
and then the second inequality in Lemma

to deduce that

\displaystyle|I\!I|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^{-\theta k}\|\mathcal{P}_{10}\circ\mathcal{P}_{*}^{n-k-1}\varphi\|_{L^{1}}\|\psi\|_{C^{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^{-\theta k}\|\pi_{0}\circ\mathcal{P}\|_{L^{1}}\|\mathcal{P}_{*}^{n-k-1}\varphi\|_{L^{1}}\|\psi\|_{C^{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^{-\theta k}\|\pi_{0}\circ\mathcal{P}\|_{L^{1}}C_{1}\max\{n-k-1,1\}^{-3/2}\hat{C}_{\varphi}\|\psi\|_{C^{\theta}}.

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^{-\theta k}(n-k)^{-3/2}\leq Cn^{-3/2}.

|I|+|I\!I|\leq C_{2}n^{-3/2}\hat{C}_{\varphi}\|\psi\|_{C^{\theta}}

for some constant

,
\psi
,

.

We next estimate the term
I\!I\!I

and Lemma

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{00}^{n}\varphi\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{00}^{n}\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\beta}:\hat{H}_{0,\hat{C}^{\theta}}\to\hat{H}_{0,\hat{C}^{\theta}}\|^{n}\cdot\|\varphi_{0}\|_{\theta/2}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-n}\|\varphi_{0}\|_{\theta/2}^{(\ell)}\leq 2^{-n}\cdot 2\cdot\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}}

for all

, where

denotes the operator norm with respect to the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\theta/2}^{(0)}
.
Hence we obtain

|I\!I\!I|\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{00}^{n}\varphi\|_{L^{1}}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2^{-n}C\|\varphi\|_{C^{\theta}}\|\psi\|_{C^{\theta}}

for some constant
C>0
independent of
\varphi,\psi,n
.

In order to estimate the last term

,
note that
\mathcal{P}_{01}\circ\mathcal{P}^{n-k-1}_{00}\varphi\in\hat{H}_{1}

4.
Remarks on the case
M>2

In this last section
we explain how to modify the argument in the previous sections for the case
M=2
to prove the Main Theorem for
M>2
.

4.1.
Upper bound on correlations

In the case
M=2
, we have used the Haar wavelets and proceeded with explicit computations. However, the Haar wavelets is actually not very necessary and we can extend all the arguments in the previous sections to the case
M>2
at the cost of losing explicitness in computation.
For each integer
\ell\geq 0
we consider the partition

\xi_{\ell}=\left\{\left[\frac{k-1}{M^{\ell}},\frac{k}{M^{\ell}}\right)\colon k\in\{1,\ldots,M^{\ell}\}\right\}

of
[0,1]
up to Lebesgue null subsets, and
let
K_{\ell}
denote the set of functions which are constant on each element of
\xi_{\ell}
. Note that
K_{0}
is the one-dimensional space spanned by the constant functions on
[0,1]
. For each
\ell\geq 1
, we define
H_{\ell}
to be the
L^{2}
orthogonal complement of
K_{\ell-1}
in
K_{\ell}
:

H_{\ell}=\{u\in K_{\ell}\colon\langle u,v\rangle=0\text{ for all }v\in K_{\ell-1}\}.

In terms of probability theory,
H_{\ell}
is the space of
\xi_{\ell}
-measurable functions whose conditional expectations with respect to the partition
\xi_{\ell-1}
vanish.
If
M=2
, the space
H_{\ell}
is the
2^{\ell-1}
dimensional space spanned by
\chi_{\ell,k}\in K_{\ell}
for
0\leq k<2^{\ell-1}
.
We put
H_{0}=K_{0}
.
Then we have

K_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell}H_{\ell^{\prime}},

and

L^{2}_{0}([0,1])=\overline{\bigoplus_{\ell\geq 1}H_{\ell}}.

Similarly to
Section
2.1
and Section
2.3
, we define an operator
\mathcal{P}_{0}\colon L^{1}([0,1])\to L^{1}([0,1])
by

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}+\mathcal{P}_{\beta},

where

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}u(x)=\frac{1}{2}u(Mx-k+1)\quad\text{on $\left[\frac{k-1}{M},\frac{k}{M}\right)$, $k\in\{1,\ldots,M\}$}

and

\mathcal{P}_{\beta}u(x)=\frac{1}{2M}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}u\left(\frac{x+k}{M}\right).

Note that
\mathcal{P}_{0}(L^{2}_{0}([0,1]))\subset L^{2}_{0}([0,1])
,
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(H_{\ell})\subset H_{\ell+1}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\beta}(H_{\ell+1})\subset H_{\ell}
for all
\ell\geq 1

and

\mathcal{P}_{\beta}(H_{1})=\{0\},

and we have the estimates corresponding to
the inequalities in Lemma
2.3
. Hence we obtain the claim corresponding to
Theorem
2.1
, by comparing the transition induced by
\mathcal{P}_{0}
between the components
H_{\ell}
with the Markov process of the symmetric simple random walk with an absorbing wall. We then deduce the upper bound in
(
1.1
) from
Theorem
2.1
in the same manner as in Section
3
.

4.2.
Lower bound on correlations

In order to get the lower bound (
1.2
) in the Main theorem, it is enough to prove Lemma
2.7
in the case
M>2
. For each
\ell\geq 1
, we say a function
u\in H_{\ell}
is
\xi
-strictly increasing

if
u(x)<u(y)
for two points
x<y
on
[0,1]
that belong to an element of
\xi_{\ell-1}
and belong to distinct elements of
\xi_{\ell}
. The set
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
of
\xi
-strictly increasing functions of
H_{\ell}
form a convex cone
and satisfies
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{C}_{\ell})\subset\mathcal{C}_{\ell+1}
,

\mathcal{P}_{\beta}(\mathcal{C}_{\ell+1})\subset\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
for all
\ell\geq 1
and

\mathcal{C}_{0}=\{0\}
.

In order to follow the argument in the case
M=2
, instead of the
L^{\infty}
norm we consider the following norm on
H_{\ell}
:

\|\varphi\|_{*}^{(\ell)}=\max_{I\in\xi_{\ell-1}}\left(\sup_{I}\varphi-\inf_{I}\varphi\right).

Let
\varphi\in C^{\theta}([0,1])\cap L^{2}_{0}([0,1])
.
If we write
\mathcal{P}_{0}^{n}\varphi=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell}^{(n)}
,
\varphi_{\ell}^{(n)}\in H_{\ell}
for
n\geq 1
, then we have

(4.1)

\|\varphi^{(n+1)}_{\ell}\|_{*}^{(\ell)}\leq\begin{cases}\vskip 2.84526pt\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\|\varphi^{(n)}_{\ell-1}\|_{*}^{(\ell-1)}+\frac{1}{2}\|\varphi^{(n)}_{\ell+1}\|_{*}^{(\ell+1)}}&\text{ if }\ell\geq 2,\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\|\varphi^{(n)}_{\ell+1}\|_{*}^{(\ell+1)}}&\text{ if }\ell=1.\end{cases}

If
\varphi
is strictly increasing on
[0,1]
in the usual sense, then each component in the decomposition

\varphi=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell}
,
\varphi_{\ell}\in H_{\ell}

is
\xi
-strictly increasing, and
\varphi_{\ell}^{(n)}
is

\xi
-strictly increasing too.
Hence, the inequalities in (
4.1
) are actually equalities. This yields the lower bound in
Lemma
2.7
.

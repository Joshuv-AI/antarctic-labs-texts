Estimation of Tsallis entropy for exponentially distributed several populations
Naveen Kumar

Abstract
We study the estimation of Tsallis entropy of a finite number of independent populations, each following an exponential distribution with the same scale parameter and distinct location parameters for q > 0 q>0 . We derive a Stein-type improved estimate, establishing the inadmissibility of the best affine equivariant estimate of the parameter function. A class of smooth estimates utilizing the Brewster technique is obtained, resulting in a significant improvement in the risk value. We computed the Brewster-Zidek estimates for both one and two populations, to illustrate the comparison with best affine equivariant and Stein-type estimates. We further derive that the Bayesian estimate, employing an inverse gamma prior, which takes the best affine equivariant estimate as a particular case. We provide a numerical illustration utilizing simulated samples for a single population. The purpose is to demonstrate the impact of sample size, location parameter, and entropic index on the estimates.



Keywords—
Tsallis entropy, best affine equivariant estimate, Stein’s estimate, Brewster-Zidek estimate, Bayesian estimate

2
BAEE for the Tsallis entropy of multiple exponential populations

In this section, we derive the Tsallis entropy for
k(\geq 1)
independent populations
P_{i}(i=1,2,...,k)
, where each population is distributed with a probability density function,

p_{i}(x;u_{i},\sigma)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sigma}e^{-\frac{x-u_{i}}{\sigma}},&\text{if $x>u_{i}$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2.1)

Let us consider a random sample
\{x_{i}^{1},x_{i}^{2},...,x_{i}^{n}\}
drawn from the population
P_{i}
, it can be observed that the Tsallis entropy for the corresponding distribution exists finitely if and only if
q>0
, and given by

S_{q}(P_{i})=\frac{1}{q-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{q{\sigma}^{q-1}}\right).

(2.2)

It is well known that the Tsallis entropy exhibits
q
-additivity for independent distributions and that the measure of randomness is unaffected by the location parameter, our first step is to determine the Tsallis entropy for
k
number of independent exponential distributions with same scale and distinct location parameter.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given
k
independent exponential distributions as in equation(
2.1
), the joint Tsallis entropy is
S_{q}(P_{1},P_{2},...,P_{k})=\frac{1}{q-1}\left(1-{\left[1+(1-q)S_{q}(P_{i})\right]}^{k}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta=\frac{1}{q{\sigma}^{q-1}}
, then for equation(
2.2
), we can write

\displaystyle S_{q}(P_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}(1-\Delta).

From the
q-
additivity of Tsallis entropy, we get

\displaystyle S_{q}(P_{1},P_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}(1-\Delta)\left(\Delta+1\right),

\displaystyle S_{q}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}(1-\Delta)\left(\Delta^{2}+\Delta+1\right),

\displaystyle S_{q}(P_{1},P_{2},...,P_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}(1-\Delta)(\Delta^{k-1}+\Delta^{k-2}+\cdots+\Delta+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}(1-\Delta^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\left[1-{\left(\frac{1}{{q\sigma^{q-1}}}\right)}^{k}\right]=\frac{1}{q-1}-\frac{1}{(q-1){q^{k}}{\sigma}^{k(q-1)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\left(1-{\left[1+(1-q)S_{q}(P_{i})\right]}^{k}\right).

∎

This motivate us to consider the equivariant estimation problem for the function
\Theta(\sigma)=1/\sigma^{k(q-1)}
for a fixed value of
k
and
q
, in order to estimate tsallis entropy for a given sample, under the quadratic error function
L(t)={(t-1)}^{2}
, which is a strictly bowl-shaped function with minimum at
t=1
, can be expressed as,

L\left(\frac{\delta(X)}{\Theta(\sigma)}\right)={\left(\left(\frac{\delta(X)}{\Theta(\sigma)}\right)-1\right)}^{2}.

(2.3)

Based on the
i
-th random sample
\{x_{i}^{1},x_{i}^{2},...,x_{i}^{n}\}
, for
({\mu}_{i},\sigma)
,
(X_{i}^{(1)},Y_{i})
forms a complete and sufficient statistics, where,
X_{i}^{(1)}=min_{1\leq j\leq n}X_{i}^{j}
and
Y_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{i}^{j}
.
X_{i}^{(1)}
and
Z_{i}
are distributed independently, where
Z_{i}=Y_{i}-nX_{i}^{(1)}
. Also
X_{i}^{(1)}
is exponentially distributed with parameters (
{\mu}_{i}
,
\sigma/n
) and
2Z_{i}/{\sigma}
follows
\chi_{2(n-1)}^{2}
(see page no. 43 of
[
21
]
). Define
T=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(X_{i}^{j}-X_{i}^{(1)}\right)
then for
(\bar{\mu},\sigma)
,
(X^{(1)},T)
forms a complete and sufficient statistics where
\bar{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{k})
. Also
X^{(1)}
and
T
are independently distributed. Utilizing the additivity characteristic of the chi-square distribution, we can infer that
2T/\sigma
follows a chi-square distribution with
2k(n-1)
degrees of freedom. The plug-in estimator of
\Theta(\sigma)
, employing the maximum likelihood estimator(MLE) of
\sigma
, is expressed as
{(kn)}^{k(q-1)}/{T}^{k(q-1)}
.

Consider the affine transformations,
h_{r,s_{i}}(x_{ij})=rx_{i}^{j}+s_{i},
where
r>0,s_{i}\in\mathbb{R},

j=1,...,n
, and
i=1,...,k
. Let
\bar{s}=(s_{1},...,s_{k})
and
g_{r,\bar{s}}=(g_{r,s_{1}},...,g_{r,s_{k}})
, under the transformations

, we observe that,

(\bar{\mu},\sigma)\rightarrow(r\bar{\mu}+\bar{s},r\sigma),\ \ \ \ (X^{(1)},T)\rightarrow(rX^{(1)}+\bar{s},rT).

(2.4)

Accordingly, we get
\Theta(\sigma)=1/{\sigma}^{k(q-1)}\rightarrow 1/r^{k(q-1)}{\sigma}^{k(q-1)}
. The loss function (
2.3
), if
{\delta}\rightarrow{\delta}/r^{k(q-1)}
, is invariant under the group
G_{r,\bar{s}}
of affine transformation. The obtained affine equivariant estimator has the
general form

{\delta}_{c}(X^{(1)},T)=\frac{c}{T^{k(q-1)}},

(2.5)

where
c
is any constant. The density functions of random variable
T
, needed to calculate the expectations in subsequent results is given by

f_{T}(t)=\frac{t^{k(n-1)-1}e^{-\frac{t}{\sigma}}}{{\sigma}^{k(n-1)}{\Gamma{(k(n-1))}}},\text{ \ \ \ $t>0$,}

(2.6)

In the next theorem, the BAEE
of
\Theta(\sigma)
is derived.

Theorem 2.1
.

For the quadratic error function(
2.3
), the BAEE of
\Theta(\sigma)
exists for
q<(n+1)/2
, and is
{\delta}_{c_{0}}(X^{(1)},T)
, where
c_{0}=\frac{\Gamma(k(n-1)+k(1-q))}{\Gamma(k(n-1)+2k(1-q))}
.

Proof.

The risk value of the estimator is given by

R(\sigma,\delta)=E_{\overline{\mu},\sigma}\left(L\left(\frac{\delta}{\Theta(\sigma)}\right)\right).

Using the general form of equivariant estimator and from equivariant property, simplification gives

\begin{split}R(\sigma,\delta)&=E_{\overline{0},1}{\left(c{T}^{k(1-q)}-1\right)}^{2}\\
&={c^{2}}E_{\overline{0},1}(T^{2k(1-q)})-{2c}E_{\overline{0},1}(T^{k(1-q)})+1.\end{split}

(2.7)

Here
\overline{0}=(0,0,...,0)
is used. Differentiating right side with respect to
c
, gives isolated point

\begin{split}c_{0}&=\frac{E_{\overline{0},1}{(T}^{k(1-q)})}{E_{\overline{0},1}{(T}^{2k(1-q)})}\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(k(n-1)+k(1-q))}{\Gamma(k(n-1)+2k(1-q))}.\end{split}

We utilize the moments of
\frac{2T}{\sigma}\sim{\chi_{(2k(n-1))}^{2}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

The risk function of the equivariant estimator is equal for all parameter values. The risk value of minimum risk equivariant estimator
{\delta}_{c_{0}}(X^{(1)},T)
, is computed in the following corollary

Corollary 1
.

For the quadratic error function (
2.3
), the BAEE
{\delta}_{c_{0}}(X^{(1)},T)
has the risk value
1-\frac{{\left(\Gamma(k(n-1)+k(1-q))\right)}^{2}}{\Gamma(k(n-1)+2k(1-q))\Gamma(k(n-1))}
.

Proof.

As
2T/{\sigma}\sim{\chi}_{2k(n-1)}^{2}
and for a chi-square distributed random variable
X
with degrees of freedom
d
, the
m
-th moments, where
m>-\frac{k}{2}
, can be expressed as
E(X^{m})=\frac{2^{m}\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+m)}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}
. So from (
2.7
), we get

\begin{split}R(\sigma,\delta)&=1-\frac{{\left(E_{0,1}{(T}^{k(1-q)})\right)}^{2}}{E_{0,1}{(T}^{2k(1-q)})}\\
&=1-\frac{{\left(\Gamma(k(n-1)+k(1-q))\right)}^{2}}{\Gamma(k(n-1)+2k(1-q))\Gamma(k(n-1))}.\end{split}

∎

In the next corollary, the confidence level of the equivariant estimate is derived.

Corollary 2
.

For a given level of confidence
1-{\alpha}(0<\alpha<1)
, the confidence interval for
\Theta(\sigma)
, is
\left({\pi}/{({\chi}_{k(n-1),1-\frac{\alpha}{2}})}^{k(1-q)},{\pi}/{({{\chi}_{k(n-1),\frac{\alpha}{2}}})}^{k(1-q)}\right)
, where
{\pi}=(2T)^{k(1-q)}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{i}={\pi}/c_{i},\ \ i=1,2
, then for
0<\alpha<1
, consider

\begin{split}P\left(\pi_{2}<\sigma^{k(1-q)}<\pi_{1}\right)&\geq 1-\alpha\\
\implies P\left(c_{1}<{\left(\frac{2T}{\sigma}\right)}^{k(1-q)}<c_{2}\right)&\geq 1-{\alpha}\end{split}

As
2T/\sigma
takes non-negative values only so using the monotonic behaviour of
{\left(\frac{2T}{\sigma}\right)}^{1-q}
, we have

P\left({c_{1}}^{{\frac{1}{k(1-q)}}}<\frac{2T}{\sigma}<{{c_{2}}^{\frac{1}{k(1-q)}}}\right)\geq 1-{\alpha}

From the distribution of
2T/\sigma
, we get
{c_{1}}^{\frac{1}{k(1-q)}}={{\chi}_{k(n-1),\frac{\alpha}{2}}}
and
{c_{2}}^{\frac{1}{k(1-q)}}={{\chi}_{k(n-1),1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we show the inadmissibility of BAEE by showing the existence of an improved estimate. For this, the Stein-type estimate is derived in next section.

Lemma 3.1
.

If

\frac{\Gamma(x-k)}{\Gamma(x)}

Proof.

For

\begin{split}{\left(\frac{\Gamma(x-k)}{\Gamma(x)}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}&=\frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(x-k)\Psi(x-k)-\Gamma(x-k)\Gamma(x)\Psi(x)}{{(\Gamma(x))}^{2}}\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(x-k)}{\Gamma(x)}\left(\Psi(x-k)-\Psi(x)\right)<0\end{split}

Above last inequality is from that digamma function

∎

Note 1

Bayes Estimator

In this section, we look at the Bayesian estimate of
\Theta(\sigma)
, for the quadratic loss function given in (
2.3
). Based on the existing literature, we take inverse gamma distributions, denoted as
IG(\alpha,\beta)
with density

g_{\alpha,\beta}(\sigma)=\frac{{\alpha}^{\beta}}{\Gamma(\beta)}{\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)}^{\beta+1}e^{-\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sigma}\right)},\textit{ \ \ \ }\sigma>0,\ \ \alpha>0,\ \ \beta>0,

(4.33)

for the parameter
\sigma
as a prior distribution. Using the transformation of random variables, the density function for the random variable
T
, conditional on
\sigma
values, is

f(t|\sigma)=\frac{t^{k(n-1)-1}e^{-\frac{t}{\sigma}}}{{\sigma}^{k(n-1)}\Gamma(k(n-1))},\textit{ \ \ \ where \ \ $0<t<\infty.$}

(4.34)

Now the posterior density using Bayes formula is,

h(\sigma|t)=\frac{f(t|\sigma)g_{\alpha,\beta}(\sigma)}{f(t)}.

(4.35)

where the probability density function
f(t)
of
T
, independent of
\sigma
, is computed as

f(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t|\sigma)g_{\alpha,\beta}(\sigma)d{\sigma}=\frac{{\alpha}^{\beta}x^{k(n-1)-1}}{\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(k(n-1))}x^{-(k(n-1)+\beta)}\Gamma(k(n-1)+\beta).

Using the (
4.35
), the posterior density is given by

h(\sigma|t)=\frac{{\sigma}^{-(k(n-1)+\beta+1)}e^{-\left(\frac{x+\alpha}{\sigma}\right)}}{{(x+\alpha)}^{-(k(n-1)+\beta)}\Gamma(k(n-1)+\beta)}.

(4.36)

As (
2.3
), is the quadratic error function, the Bayes estimator for
\Theta(\sigma)
, is computed using the relation obtained after minimizing the corresponding risk value, we get

\begin{split}{\delta}_{\alpha,\beta}^{B}(T)&=\frac{{E({\sigma}^{k(1-q)}}|T)}{{E({\sigma}^{2k(1-q)}}|T)}\\
&=\frac{1}{{(T+\alpha)}^{k(q-1)}}\frac{\Gamma(k(n-1)+k(1-q)+\beta)}{\Gamma(k(n-1)+2k(1-q)+\beta)}.\end{split}

(4.37)

which is the Bayesian estimate based on the prior inverted gamma distribution
IG(\alpha,\beta)
and new information from the sample of size
n
from
k

. Bayesian framework incorporate many estimation techniques as a particular case.

Concluding Remarks

We have computed the Tsallis entropy for

. We considered the problem of estimating the Tsallis entropy for

Introduction

We study the graphs of the high-dimensional Weierstrass functions

(1.1)

W(x)=W^{\phi}_{\lambda,\,b}(x)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{{\lambda}^{n}\phi(b^{n}x)},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

where

\phi(x):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a non-constant
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic Lipschitz function.
The famous example by Weierstrass,
\phi(x)=\cos(2\pi x)

[
14
]

[
2
]
later extended it naturally to the complex case with
\phi(x)=e^{2\pi ix}

[
6
]
,
[

[

Set

denote the orthogonal projection to the linear space

(1.2)

q=\max_{V<\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\text{dim }V\>:\>\pi_{V}\circ W^{\phi}_{\lambda,\,b}\text{ is Lipschitz}\right\}.

We can now state our main results.

Main Theorem 1
.

Let
b\geq 2
be an integer,

and

\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote a
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic real-analytic function.
Then

\text{dim}_{B}\,\text{graph}W=\text{dim}_{H}\,\text{graph}W=\min\left\{\log_{\lambda^{-1}}b,\,1+\left(d-q\right)\left(1+\log_{b}\lambda\right)\right\}.

Remark 1.1
.

The graph of function

is the attractor of an IFS with contraction rates of

and
\lambda

Thus, we may expect that the right side of (

. But

is indeed possible. (For instance, with a
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic analytic function

The smoothness of

Given the integer

under different parameters

(1/b,1)

Define,

For this purpose, we present the following result:

Main Theorem 2

Let

be an integer, and let

\mathbb{Z}
-periodic

\lambda\in(1/b,1)

These theorems generalize [33 Main Theorem] to the high dimension case.

2.1.
Main findings

Throughout this paper, we fix an integer
b\geq 2
.
Let
\varLambda=\{0,1,\ldots,b-1\}
,

\varLambda^{\#}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\varLambda^{n}
and
\Sigma=\varLambda^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
.
Ledrappier
[
24
]
applies ergodic theory techniques in smooth dynamical systems to analyze the graphs of Takagi functions.
This exploration is motivated by the observation: the graph of function
W^{\phi}_{\lambda,\,b}
serves as an invariant repeller within the expanding dynamical system

T:[0,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(2.1)

T(x,y)=\left(bx\mod 1,\frac{y-\phi(x)}{\lambda}\right).

The slopes of the strong stable manifolds of this system can be expressed as follows: for
\textbf{j}=j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}\cdot\cdot\cdot\in\Sigma
, set

(2.2)

Y(x,\textbf{j})=Y_{\lambda,\,b}^{\phi}(x,\textbf{j})=-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{\gamma^{n}\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{x}{b^{n}}+\frac{j_{1}}{b^{n}}+\frac{j_{2}}{b^{n-1}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\frac{j_{n}}{b}\right)},\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}

where

\gamma=\frac{1}{b\lambda}\in\left(\frac{1}{b},1\right).

when
d=1
,
the separation properties of the functions
Y(x,\textbf{j})

are particularly important in
[
4
,
37
,
33
]
.
Specifically, we
[
33
]
introduced a soft separation condition and its degenerated counterpart. To analyze the case

d\geq 2
, we need to generalize these notations to high-dimensional spaces.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
\lambda\in(1/b,1)
, we say that a
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic
C^{1}
function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies

•

the condition (H) if

(2.3)

Y_{\lambda,\,b}^{\pi_{A}\phi}(x,\,\textbf{j})-Y_{\lambda,\,b}^{\pi_{A}\phi}(x,\,\textbf{i})\nequiv 0,\quad\forall\,\textbf{j}\neq\textbf{i}\in\Sigma

for any one-dimensional linear space
A<\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

•

the condition (H
∗
) if

Y_{\lambda,\,b}^{\phi}(x,\,\textbf{j})-Y_{\lambda,\,b}^{\phi}(x,\,\textbf{i})\equiv 0,\quad\forall\,\textbf{j},\,\,\textbf{i}\in\Sigma.

The condition (H) is regarded as a form of separation property. With this notation, we extend
[
33
, Theorem B]
from

d=1
to the case
d\geq 1
. Specifically, we obtain the following.

Theorem A
.

If an analytic
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies the condition (H) for an integer
b\geq 2
and
\lambda\in(1/b,1)
, then

\text{dim}_{B}\,\text{graph}W=\text{dim}_{H}\,\text{graph}W=\min\left\{\log_{\lambda^{-1}}b,\,1+d+d\log_{b}\lambda\right\}.

The ideas of Theorem A.

\text{graph}W
serves as the invariant repeller for the expanding map
T
defined by (
2.1
). The local inverse of
T
can be represented as
g_{i}(x,y)=\left(\frac{x+i}{b},\lambda y+\phi(\frac{x+i}{b})\right)
where

. Consequently, the graph of
W
also acts as the attractor for the nonlinear IFS
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
. By Falconer’s findings
[
9
]
, we anticipate that the dimension of
\text{graph}W
equals the self-affine dimension, given by
\min\left\{\log_{\lambda^{-1}}b,\,1+d+d\log_{b}\lambda\right\}
.

Let
\mu
be the lift of the standard Lebesgue measure on
[0,1)
to the graph of
W
. Ledrappier
[
24
]
demonstrates, through inverse limits, that for most
\textbf{j}\in\Sigma
, the measures
\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu
are exact dimensional, with
\text{dim}(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)
equal to a constant
\alpha
. Here,
\pi_{\textbf{j}}
is the projection along the strong unstable manifold of the dynamical system

. Hence, it suffices to show that

, as stated in Theorem
3.1
.

Bárány et al.
[
5
]
developed powerful methods to study self-affine measures on the plane (for recent advances, see
[
18
,
30
]
), which were used to completely solve the dimension of Takagi function, i.e.
\phi(x)=\text{dist}(x,\,\mathbb{Z})
and
b=2
.
Building on their ideas, we
[
33
]
developed methods to handle the nonlinear case and proved Theorem A for
d=1
.
However, this approach encounters challenges when
d>1
. Notably, Hochman’s Entropy Inverse Theorem
[
16
, Theorem 2.8]
plays a crucial role in
[
33
]
, but its higher-dimensional version
[
17
, Theorem 2.8]
becomes more intricate.
In this paper, we fix a good
\textbf{j}\in\Sigma
. We utilize
[
17
, Theorem 4.12]
to establish an Entropy Inverse Theorem for measures
\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu
, as outlined in Theorem
4.1
. This theorem yields saturated properties for the majority of
\{\pi_{\textbf{i}}\mu\}_{\textbf{i}\in\Sigma}

However, employing
[

, Theorem 2.8]
directly only provides local saturated properties for

Specifically, assuming the claim of Theorem A’ (see §
3.1
) fails, we obtain a sequence of
\textbf{j}\in\Sigma
such that the corresponding measure convolution entropy does not increase, as shown in Theorem C (see §

are indispensable.

Remark 2.1
.

Given a linear space

3.1.
Ledrappier-Young Theory

In a metric space
X
, a probability measure
\omega
is called
exact-dimensional
if there exists a constant
\beta\geq 0
, such that for
\omega
-a.e.
x
,

(3.1)

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\omega\left(\mathbf{B}(x,r)\right)}{\log r}=\beta.

We denote
\text{dim }\omega=\beta
, referring to it as the dimension of
\omega
.
The Ledrappier–Young theory, discussed in
[
23
]
, examines the exact dimensional properties of ergodic measures under given smooth diffeomorphisms.
To apply it to the differential homomorphism, one considers the inverse limit (see e.g.
[
24
,
29
,
38
]
).

In this subsection, we fix
\lambda\in\left(\frac{1}{b},1\right)
, and consider a
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic
C^{2}
function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

Through direct calculation, we establish the following claim:

3.2.
Entropy

In this subsection, we outline fundamental entropy properties that are frequently used in our paper, often without additional mention. These properties can be found in
[
17
, Section 3.1]
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{B})
be a measurable space. Given a probability measure
\omega
on
\Omega
and a countable partition
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{B}
of
\Omega
, we define the
entropy
as follows:

H(\omega,\mathcal{Q})=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}-\omega(Q)\log_{b}\omega(Q),

where the common convention
0\log 0=0
is adopted.
We denote by
\mathcal{Q}(x)
the member of
\mathcal{Q}
containing
x
. If
\omega(\mathcal{Q}(x))>0
, the conditional measure

\omega_{\mathcal{Q}(x)}(A)=\omega_{x,\,\mathcal{Q}}(A)=\frac{\omega(A\cap\mathcal{Q}(x))}{\omega(\mathcal{Q}(x))}

is called a
\mathcal{Q}
-component
of
\omega
.
In this paper we always assume that
H(\omega,\mathcal{Q})<\infty
. Note that
we have the following upper bound:

(3.9)

H(\omega,\mathcal{Q})\leq\log_{b}\#\{Q\in\mathcal{Q}\>:\>\omega(Q)>0\}.

Given another countable partition
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{B}
, we define the
conditional entropy
as:

H(\omega,\mathcal{Q}\mid\mathcal{D})=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D},\,\omega(D)>0}\omega(D)H(\omega_{D},\mathcal{Q}).

When
\mathcal{Q}
is a
refinement
of
\mathcal{D}
, meaning

\mathcal{Q}(x)\subset\mathcal{D}(x)
for each
x\in\Omega
, we have:

H(\omega,\mathcal{Q}\mid\mathcal{D})=H(\omega,\mathcal{Q})-H(\omega,\mathcal{D}).

Partitions
\mathcal{D},\,\mathcal{Q}
are
C
-
commensurable
if each element of
\mathcal{D}
intersects with at most
C
elements of
\mathcal{Q}
, vise versa, where
C\geq 1
is a constant.
Notably,
when
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are
C
-commensurable:

(3.10)

|H(\omega,\mathcal{Q})-H(\omega,\mathcal{D})|=O(C).

Set,

\mathcal{Q}\vee\mathcal{D}:=\left\{Q\cap D\>:\>Q\in\mathcal{Q}\text{ and }D\in\mathcal{D}\right\}.

Let
\omega^{\prime}
be another Borel probability measure on
\Omega
. For
t\in[0,1]
, we observe the the
Concavity of entropy
:

tH(\omega,\mathcal{Q})+(1-t)H(\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{Q})\leq H(t\omega+(1-t)\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{Q}),

and the
Concavity of conditional entropy
:

tH(\omega,\mathcal{D}\mid\mathcal{Q})+(1-t)H(\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{D}\mid\mathcal{Q})\leq H(t\omega+(1-t)\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{D}\mid\mathcal{Q}).

We collectively refer to the above two well-known inequalities as
Concavity
, which will be extensively utilized in our paper.

In contrast, we also have the
Convexity of entropy
:

(3.11)

H(t\omega+(1-t)\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{Q})\leq\log_{b}2+tH(\omega,\mathcal{Q})+(1-t)H(\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{Q}).

In particular, we shall often consider the case where
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{B}
denotes the Borel
\sigma
-algebra. Let
\mathcal{L}_{n}
denote the partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into
b
-adic intervals of level
n
, that is:

(3.12)

\mathcal{L}_{n}=\left\{\left[\frac{j_{1}}{b^{n}},\,\frac{j_{1}+1}{b^{n}}\right)\times\ldots\times\left[\frac{j_{d}}{b^{n}},\,\frac{j_{d}+1}{b^{n}}\right)\>:\>j_{1},\,j_{2},\ldots j_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

There is a constant
C>0
with the following properties:
for any affine map
f(x)=ax+c
, where
a>0
,
c\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(3.13)

\left|H(f\omega,\mathcal{L}_{n+[\log_{b}a]})-H(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{n})\right|\leq C;

given measurable functions
g,g^{\prime}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\sup_{x}|g(x)-g^{\prime}(x)|\leq b^{-n}
, then

(3.14)

\left|H(g\omega,\mathcal{L}_{n})-H(g^{\prime}\omega,\mathcal{L}_{n})\right|\leq C.

We denote by
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set of all compactly supported Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For an exact dimensional probability measure
\omega\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, its dimension is closely related to the entropy, as demonstrated in the following fact (cf.
[
43
, Theorem 4.4]
). See also
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\omega\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is exact dimensional, then

\text{dim }\omega=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{n}).

Utilizing Proposition
3.1
, Theorem
3.1
, and the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem yields:

(3.15)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\frac{1}{n}H(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{n})d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})=\alpha.

4.
Entropy inverse theorem

In this section, we will utilize Hochman’s results
[
17
, Theorem 4.12]
to establish an entropy inverse theorem for measures
\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu
, which will serve as a cornerstone in proving Theorem A’.
Throughout this section, we fix
\lambda\in(1/b,1)
and assume that
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic
C^{2}
function.

Hochman
[
17
]
introduced the following notations to study the structures of two measures, where the convolution entropy does not increase.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
V<\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a linear space and
\varepsilon>0
. A measure
\omega\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is said to be
(V,\,\varepsilon)
-concentrated if there is a translate
V+y
of
V
such that
1-\varepsilon
of the mass of
\omega
lies within a
\varepsilon
-distance of
V+y
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
V<\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a linear space,
\varepsilon>0
and
m\geq 1
. A measure
\omega\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
(V,\varepsilon,m)
-saturated , if

\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)\geq\frac{1}{m}H\left(\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)+\text{dim}V-\varepsilon.

Recall that
\pi_{V}
denotes the orthographic projection function from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
onto the subspace
V
, while
V^{\bot}
represents the orthogonal complement of
V
.
It’s worth noting that
\alpha=\alpha(\phi)
is defined in Theorem
3.1
.
With these definitions, we can now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
R\geq 1,\,\varepsilon>0
and
m\geq M(R,\varepsilon)
, there exists
\delta=\delta(R,\varepsilon,m)>0

such that
the following holds for
n\geq N(R,\varepsilon,m,\delta)
.
Consider a measure
\eta\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\text{diam}\left(\text{supp}(\eta)\right)\leq R
. Suppose there exists
\textbf{j}\in\Sigma
such that

\frac{1}{n}H\left(\eta*\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{n}\right)<\frac{1}{n}H\left(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{n}\right)+\delta

and

\left|\frac{1}{n}H\left(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{n}\right)-\alpha\right|\leq\delta.

Then, there exists a sequence of linear spaces
V_{0},V_{1},\ldots,V_{n-1}<\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(4.1)

\mathbb{P}^{\eta}_{0\leq i<n}\left(\eta_{x,\,i}\text{ is }(V_{i},\varepsilon/b^{i})-\text{concentrated}\right)>1-\varepsilon

and

(4.2)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\left(\left\{\textbf{u}\in\Sigma\>:\>\pi_{\textbf{u}}\mu\text{ is }(V_{i},\varepsilon,m)-\text{saturated}\right\}\right)>1-\varepsilon.

Remark 4.1
.

The first assumption is standard in Hochman’s entropy inverse theorem
[
16
,
17
, Theorem 2.8]
, so we also call Theorem
4.1
entropy inverse theorem for measure
\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu
. Additionally, the second condition holds for almost all
\textbf{j}\in\Sigma
when
n
is large enough.

Basic properties

The next lemma is from
[

.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\eta,\omega\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\frac{1}{m}H\left(\eta*\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)\geq\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)-O(\frac{1}{m}).

Given a linear space
V<\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we define

\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}:=\pi^{-1}_{V}\mathcal{L}_{m}

following
[
17
]
. For any
\omega\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

(4.3)

H\left(\pi_{V}\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)=H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}\right).

It is easy to verify that,

(4.4)

\mathcal{L}_{m}\text{ and }\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}\vee\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V^{\bot}}\text{ are }O(1)-\text{commensurable}.

This implies:

\begin{split}H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)&=H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}\vee\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V^{\bot}}\right)+O(1)=H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}\vee\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V^{\bot}}\mid\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}\right)+H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}\right)+O(1)\\
&=\int H\left(\omega_{\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}(x)},\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}\vee\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V^{\bot}}\right)d\omega(x)+H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}\right)+O(1)\\
&=\int\left(H\left(\omega_{\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}(x)},\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)+O(1)\right)d\omega(x)+H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}\right)+O(1).\end{split}

Thus we obtain:

(4.5)

H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)=H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\mid\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}\right)+H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V}\right)+O(1)\quad\text{for any linear space }V<\mathbb{R}^{d}.

This will be frequently used in our paper without further explanation.
If
\omega
is
(V,\varepsilon,m)
-saturated, meaning
\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)-\frac{1}{m}H\left(\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)\geq\text{dim}V-\varepsilon
, then (
4.5
) implies:

(4.6)

\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\mid\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\geq\text{dim}V-\varepsilon-O(\frac{1}{m}).

Lemma 4.2

Given a linear space

Notation.
For

, denote by

the unique integer satisfying

\lambda^{\hat{n}}\leq b^{-n}<\lambda^{\hat{n}-1}.

Given

and
\omega\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for all

and

write,

(4.8)

Q^{\omega}_{m}(i,y)=\int\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\lambda^{\hat{i}}(\pi_{\textbf{a}}\mu),\mathcal{L}_{m+i}\right)\,d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{a}).

Recall
\omega_{y,\,i}=\frac{1}{\omega({\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)})}\omega|_{\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)}
and
\lambda^{\hat{i}}(\pi_{\textbf{a}}\mu)=S_{\lambda^{\hat{i}}}(\pi_{\textbf{a}}\mu)

\text{The diameter of }\text{supp}\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\lambda^{\hat{i}}(\pi_{\textbf{a}}\mu)\right)\text{ is bounded by }O(1/{b^{i}}).

Utilizing this and (

Q^{\omega}_{m}(i,y)=O(1)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Given
R\geq 1
, consider a measure
\omega\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\text{diam}\left(\text{supp}(\omega)\right)\leq R
.
For any integers
n>m\geq 1
and
\textbf{j}\in\Sigma
, it follows that

\frac{1}{n}H\left(\omega*\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{n}\right)\geq\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{0\leq i<n}\left(Q^{\omega}_{m}(i,y)\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{mR}{n}\right).

Proof.

For any
0\leq i<n
, let’s recall that
\omega=\mathbb{E}_{i}^{\omega}(\omega_{y,\,i})
and
\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu=\frac{1}{b^{\hat{i}}}\sum_{\textbf{z}\in\varLambda^{\hat{i}}}\pi_{\textbf{j}}g_{\textbf{z}}\mu
. Combining this with Lemma
3.2
and the concavity, we can deduce

\begin{split}\frac{1}{n}&H\left(\omega*\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{n}\right)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega*\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{i+m}\mid\mathcal{L}_{i}\right)+O\left(\frac{mR}{n}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}_{i}^{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{b^{\hat{i}}}\sum_{\textbf{z}\in\varLambda^{\hat{i}}}\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\pi_{\textbf{j}}g_{\textbf{z}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{i+m}\mid\mathcal{L}_{i}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{mR}{n}\right)\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<n}^{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{b^{\hat{i}}}\sum_{\textbf{z}\in\varLambda^{\hat{i}}}\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\pi_{\textbf{j}}g_{\textbf{z}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{i+m}\mid\mathcal{L}_{i}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{mR}{n}\right).\end{split}

Since
Q^{\eta}_{m}(i,y)=O(1)
, we only need to prove the following:

Claim 3
.

For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
m,i>0
with
\gamma^{\hat{i}}\leq b^{-m}
, it follows that

\frac{1}{b^{\hat{i}}}\sum_{\textbf{z}\in\varLambda^{\hat{i}}}\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\pi_{\textbf{j}}g_{\textbf{z}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{i+m}\mid\mathcal{L}_{i}\right)=Q^{\omega}_{m}(i,y)+O(\frac{1}{m}).

By combining

\pi_{\textbf{j}}g_{\textbf{z}}(x,y)=\lambda^{\hat{i}}\pi_{\textbf{z}^{*}\textbf{j}}(x,y)+\pi_{\textbf{j}}g_{\textbf{z}}(0,0)
with (
4.9
), we obtain:

(4.10)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{m}&H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\pi_{\textbf{j}}g_{\textbf{z}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{i+m}\mid\mathcal{L}_{i}\right)=\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\lambda^{\hat{i}}(\pi_{\textbf{z}^{*}\textbf{j}}\mu),\mathcal{L}_{i+m}\right)+O(\frac{1}{m}).\end{split}

Since
\parallel\pi_{\textbf{z}^{*}\textbf{j}}-\pi_{\textbf{z}^{*}\textbf{j}^{\prime}}\parallel_{\infty}=O(\gamma^{\hat{i}})=O(1/b^{m})
holds for each
\textbf{j}^{\prime}\in\Sigma
, we can conclude that

\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\lambda^{\hat{i}}(\pi_{\textbf{z}^{*}\textbf{j}}\mu),\mathcal{L}_{i+m}\right)=\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\lambda^{\hat{i}}(\pi_{\textbf{z}^{*}\textbf{j}^{\prime}}\mu),\mathcal{L}_{i+m}\right)+O(\frac{1}{m})

by (
3.14
). By combining this with (
4.10
), we derive the following relationship:

\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\pi_{\textbf{j}}g_{\textbf{z}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{i+m}\mid\mathcal{L}_{i}\right)=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\lambda^{\hat{i}}(\pi_{\textbf{z}^{*}\textbf{j}^{\prime}}\mu),\mathcal{L}_{m+i}\right)\,d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j}^{\prime})+O(\frac{1}{m}).

Combining this with the definitions of
\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
, we obtain:

\begin{split}\frac{1}{b^{\hat{i}}}\sum_{\textbf{z}\in\varLambda^{\hat{i}}}&\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\pi_{\textbf{j}}g_{\textbf{z}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{i+m}\mid\mathcal{L}_{i}\right)=\frac{1}{b^{\hat{i}}}\sum_{\textbf{z}\in\varLambda^{\hat{i}}}\int\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\lambda^{\hat{i}}(\pi_{\textbf{z}\textbf{j}^{\prime}}\mu),\mathcal{L}_{m+i}\right)\,d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j}^{\prime})+O(\frac{1}{m})\\
&=\int\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i}*\lambda^{\hat{i}}(\pi_{\textbf{a}}\mu),\mathcal{L}_{m+i}\right)\,d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{a})+O(\frac{1}{m}).\end{split}

Therefore, Claim

holds by the definitions of

.
∎

Theorem C
.

,

(C.2)

\frac{1}{n}H\left(\eta*\pi_{\textbf{z}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{n}\right)\leq\alpha+\delta

(C.3)

\left|\frac{1}{n}H\left(\pi_{\textbf{z}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{n}\right)-\alpha\right|<\delta
,

where

5.1.
Saturated properties of
\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu

The subsequent lemma is already included in the proof of

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
R\geq 1
, let
V<\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a linear space. Consider a measure
\omega\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\text{diam}\left(\text{supp}(\omega)\right)\leq R

m\geq t\geq 1

\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m}^{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left(\omega_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}\mid\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\right)\geq\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\mid\mathcal{L}_{m}^{V^{\bot}}\right)-O\left(\frac{tR}{m}+\frac{1}{t}\right).

Proof.

Using Lemma
3.2
with
\mathcal{L}_{i}^{V^{\bot}}=\pi_{V^{\bot}}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{i}
and
(\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega)_{y,\,i}=(\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega)_{\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)}
, we get:

(5.1)

\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}^{V^{\bot}}_{m}\right)=\frac{1}{m}H\left(\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m}^{\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega)_{\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{tR}{m}\right).

By Lemma
3.2
and (
4.5
), we have:

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{m}H\left(\omega,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m}^{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left(\omega_{\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{tR}{m}\right)\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m}^{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left(\omega_{\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}\mid\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\right)+\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m}^{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left(\omega_{\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{tR}{m}+\frac{1}{t}\right).\end{split}

Combining this with (
5.1
), we only need to prove for each
0\leq i<m
:

(5.2)

\mathbb{E}_{i}^{\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega)_{\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}\right)\right)\geq\mathbb{E}_{i}^{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left(\omega_{\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\right)+O(\frac{1}{t}).

Easy to confirm:
\left(\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega\right)_{\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)}=\pi_{V^{\bot}}(\omega_{\pi_{V^{\bot}}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)})=\pi_{V^{\bot}}(\omega_{\mathcal{L}^{V^{\bot}}_{i}(y)})
. Consequently,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{i}^{\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{V^{\bot}}\omega)_{\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}\right)\right)=\mathbb{E}_{i}^{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left(\omega_{\mathcal{L}^{V^{\bot}}_{i}(y)},\mathcal{L}^{V^{\bot}}_{t+i}\right)\right)\\
&\geq\mathbb{E}_{i}^{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left(\omega_{\mathcal{L}_{i}^{V^{\bot}}\vee\mathcal{L}_{i}^{V}\vee\mathcal{L}_{i}(y)},\mathcal{L}^{V^{\bot}}_{t+i}\right)\right)\end{split}

by cancavity. Combining this with the convexity of entropy, (
5.2
) holds.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For any linear space

, we have

\alpha-\alpha_{V^{\bot}}\geq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\int\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m}^{\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}\mid\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j}).

We note that
\alpha_{V^{\bot}}=\alpha_{V^{\bot}}(\phi)
is defined in Corollary
3.1
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we obtain:

\begin{split}\alpha&=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int\frac{1}{m}H\left(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})=\lim_{t\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\int\left(\frac{1}{m}H\left(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)+O\left(\frac{t}{m}+\frac{1}{t}\right)\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})\\
&=\lim_{t\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\int\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m}^{\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}\right)\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j}).\end{split}

Thus, we aim to prove:

(5.3)

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\liminf_{m\to\infty}\int\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m}^{\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})\geq\alpha_{V^{\bot}}.

Let
1\ll t\ll m
.
By the definition of conditional entropy, we have

\mathbb{E}^{\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu}_{i}\left(H\left((\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\right)=H\left(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\mid\mathcal{L}_{i}\right)\quad\quad\text{for each }i\in\mathbb{N},\,\textbf{j}\in\Sigma.

Combining this
with (
3.5
) and
\pi_{\textbf{j}}g_{\textbf{a}}(x,y)=\lambda^{|\textbf{a}|}\pi_{{\textbf{a}}^{*}\textbf{j}}(x,y)+\pi_{\textbf{j}}g_{\textbf{a}}(0,0)
, we get:

\begin{split}&\int\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m}^{\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})=\int\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{t}H\left(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\mid\mathcal{L}_{i}\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})\\

&=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\int\frac{1}{b^{\hat{i}}}\sum_{\textbf{a}\in\Lambda^{\hat{i}}}\frac{1}{t}H\left(\pi_{\textbf{a}^{*}\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{t}^{V^{\bot}}\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})+O(\frac{1}{t}).\end{split}

Combining this with
\sup_{\textbf{j}^{\prime}\in\Sigma}\parallel\pi_{\textbf{a}^{*}\textbf{j}}-\pi_{\textbf{a}^{*}\textbf{j}^{\prime}}\parallel_{\infty}=O(b^{-t})
for

\int\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m}^{\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})\geq\int\frac{1}{t}H\left(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}^{V^{\bot}}_{t}\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})+O\left(\frac{t}{m}+\frac{1}{t}\right).

Let

Lemma 5.3
.

Given
V\in Gr_{\ell}(d)
, consider a sequence of integers
0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots
and subspaces
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots\in Gr_{\ell}(d)
such that
V_{k}\to V
.
Then we have:

\alpha-\alpha_{V^{\bot}}\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int\frac{1}{m_{k}}H\left(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{m_{k}}\mid\mathcal{L}_{m_{k}}^{V_{k}^{\bot}}\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j}).

Proof.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, let integers
t,k\geq 1
be such that
\varepsilon^{-1}\ll t\ll k
. Therefore, by Lemma
5.2
, we have:

(5.4)

\alpha-\alpha_{V^{\bot}}+\varepsilon\geq\int\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m_{k}}^{\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}\mid\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j}).

Now, combining
V^{\bot}_{k}\to V^{\bot}
with
\varepsilon^{-1}\ll t\ll k
, (
3.14
), and Lemma
5.1
, we get:

\begin{split}&\int\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m_{k}}^{\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}\mid\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V^{\bot}}\right)\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})\\
&=\int\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m_{k}}^{\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)-\frac{1}{t}H\left(\pi_{V^{\bot}}\left(b^{i}(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i}\right),\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})+O(\frac{1}{t})\\
&=\int\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m_{k}}^{\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)-\frac{1}{t}H\left(\pi_{V_{k}^{\bot}}\left(b^{i}(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i}\right),\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})+O\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{t}{m_{k}}\right)\\
&=\int\mathbb{E}_{0\leq i<m_{k}}^{\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu}\left(\frac{1}{t}H\left((\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu)_{y,\,i},\mathcal{L}_{t+i}\mid\mathcal{L}_{t+i}^{V_{k}^{\bot}}\right)\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})+O\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{t}{m_{k}}\right)\\
&\geq\int\frac{1}{m_{k}}H\left(\pi_{\textbf{j}}\mu,\mathcal{L}_{m_{k}}\mid\mathcal{L}_{m_{k}}^{V_{k}^{\bot}}\right)d\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\textbf{j})-\varepsilon.\end{split}

Combining this with (
5.4
), then our claim holds.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem A’

The following lemma is a well-known fact from
[

]
, and detailed proof can be found in
[

.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
p_{0}>0
and
R\geq 1
. For
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}(p_{0},R)
and
m\geq M(p_{0},\varepsilon,R)
, the following holds:
Consider

Proof of Theorem A’.

We only need to prove the following claim:

Claim 4
.

Either
\alpha(\phi)=\min\{d,\log_{\lambda^{-1}}b\}
, or there exists a linear space
V<\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
0<\text{dim}(V)<d
and

\alpha(\phi)-\alpha_{V^{\bot}}(\phi)\geq\text{dim}(V).

In particular, this claim implies that Theorem A’ holds directly when
d=1
. For
d>1
, using induction, we assume Theorem A’ holds for dimensions up to
d-1
. To establish the case for dimension
d
, we assume
\alpha(\phi)<\min\{d,\log_{\lambda^{-1}}b\}
. Let
V<\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the linear space as stated in Claim
4
.

Considering
\pi_{V^{\bot}}\phi
as a function with a range in
\mathbb{R}^{\text{dim}(V^{\bot})}
, satisfying condition (H) because
\phi
satisfies it, we obtain
\alpha\left(\pi_{V^{\bot}}\phi\right)=\min\{\text{dim}(V^{\bot}),\log_{\lambda^{-1}}b\}
. Combining this with
\alpha_{V^{\bot}}(\phi)=\alpha\left(\pi_{V^{\bot}}\phi\right)
and Claim
4
, we reach a contradiction, confirming Theorem A’ for dimension
d
.

Now, let’s prove Claim
4
. Suppose
\alpha(\phi)<\min\{d,\log_{\lambda^{-1}}b\}
.
Let
p_{0}>0
and
R\geq 1
be as specified in Theorem C. Let
\varepsilon_{k}>0
be such that
R\ll\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}
and
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
. Then, we define a sequence of
\delta_{k}>0
and integers
m_{k},n_{k}\geq 1
, for
k=1,2,\ldots
, such that
\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}\ll m_{k}\ll\delta_{k}^{-1}\ll n_{k}
.
Consequently,
there exist measures
\eta_{k}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\text{dim}\left(\text{supp}(\eta)\right)\leq R
, and
\textbf{j}_{k}\in\Sigma
satisfying (C.1), (C.2) and (C.3) for

and
\delta_{k}
according to Theorem C. Combining this with Theorem
4.1
and
R\ll\varepsilon^{-1}_{k}\ll m_{k}\ll\delta_{k}^{-1}\ll n_{k}
, we can find a sequence of linear spaces
V_{0,\,k},V_{1,\,k},\ldots,V_{n_{k}-1,\,k}<\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) hold for
\varepsilon_{k}
,
m_{k}
and
\eta_{k},\,\textbf{j}_{k}
.

When

, Markov’s inequality, as applied to equations (

) and (

), guarantees the existence of a set

such that

. Furthermore, for every

(5.5)

\mathbb{P}^{\eta_{k}}_{i}\left((\eta_{k})_{x,\,i}\text{ is }(V_{i,\,k},\varepsilon_{k}/b^{i})-\text{concentrated}\right)>1-O(\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}),

(5.6)

\nu^{\,\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\left(\left\{\textbf{w}\in\Sigma\>:\>\pi_{\textbf{w}}\mu\text{ is }(V_{i,\,k},\varepsilon_{k},m_{k})-\text{saturated}\right\}\right)>1-O(\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}).

If there is a

Furthermore, if

6.
Proofs of Theorem B and Corollary
1.2

In this section, we will first provide an equivalent characterization of the condition (H
∗
) using Fourier coefficients. Then, we will combine this with the theories of linear algebra and complex analysis to complete the proof of Theorem B, and finally conclude with the proof of Corollary
1.2
.

6.1.
Fourier Analysis of (H
∗
)

The following directly follows from
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Given
\lambda\in(1/b,1)
and a
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic
C^{1}
function
\hat{f}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\hat{f}
satisfies the condition (H
∗
);

(ii)

There exist
\textbf{i}\neq\textbf{j}\in\Sigma
such that

Y^{\hat{f}}_{\lambda,\,b}(x,\,\textbf{i})\equiv Y^{\hat{f}}_{\lambda,\,b}(x,\,\textbf{j});

(iii)

there is a real
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic
C^{1}
function
\psi
such that

\hat{f}(x)=\lambda\psi(bx)-\psi(x)+Constant.

Remark 6.1
.

(iii) implies
\psi(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{n}}\left(\hat{f}(x/b^{n})-\hat{f}(0)\right)+\psi(0)

through direct calculation. Consequently, if
\hat{f}
is
C^{k}
for some
k\geq 1
, then
\psi
is also a
C^{k}
function.

Proof.

Define the function
h_{\textbf{i}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
for any
\textbf{i}\in\Sigma
as:

(6.1)

h_{\textbf{i}}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{n}}\left[\,\hat{f}\left(\frac{x+i_{1}+i_{2}b\ldots+i_{n}b^{n-1}}{b^{n}}\right)-\hat{f}\left(\frac{i_{1}+i_{2}b\ldots+i_{n}b^{n-1}}{b^{n}}\right)\,\right].

Hence, (
2.2
) implies
h^{\prime}_{\textbf{i}}(x)\equiv-Y^{\hat{f}}_{\lambda,\,b}(x,\textbf{i})
. With
h_{\textbf{i}}(0)=0
, we have:

h_{\textbf{i}}(x)\equiv h_{\textbf{j}}(x)\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad Y^{\hat{f}}_{\lambda,\,b}(x,\textbf{i})\equiv Y^{\hat{f}}_{\lambda,\,b}(x,\textbf{j})

equivalent for all
\textbf{i},\textbf{j}\in\Sigma
. Combining this with
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
, our claim holds.
∎

For any
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic continuous function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the
m
-th Fourier coefficient of
\phi
as follows:

(6.2)

a_{m}(\phi)=\int_{0}^{1}\phi(x)e^{-2\pi i\,mx}dx.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose
\lambda\in(1/b,1)
and
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic function with
C^{3}
continuity. Then
\phi
satisfies the condition (H
∗
) if and only if:

(6.3)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{tb^{k}}(\phi)\cdot\lambda^{-k}=0,\quad\quad\text{for each }t\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ with }b\nmid t.

Proof.

If
\phi
satisfies the condition (H
∗
), then by Lemma
6.1
and Remark
6.1
, there exists a
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic
C^{3}
function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi(x)=\lambda\psi(bx)-\psi(x)+Constant
. Comparing Fourier coefficients after expanding both sides into Fourier series, we get:

a_{m}(\phi)=\begin{cases}a_{m/b}(\psi)\cdot\lambda-a_{m}(\psi),&b\mid m;\\
-a_{m}(\psi),&b\nmid m.\end{cases}

Hence, for any integer
t
such that
b\nmid t
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have:

-a_{tb^{n}}(\psi)=a_{tb^{n}}(\phi)+a_{tb^{n-1}}(\phi)\cdot\lambda+\ldots+a_{t}(\phi)\cdot\lambda^{n},

This implies
-a_{tb^{n}}(\psi)\cdot\lambda^{-n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{tb^{k}}(\phi)\cdot\lambda^{-k}
.
By combining this with
|a_{tb^{n}}(\psi)|=O(\frac{1}{tb^{n}})
and
\frac{1}{b\lambda}\in(1/b,1)
, (
6.3
) holds.

If (
6.3
) holds for every integer
t
such that
b\nmid t
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

(6.4)

d_{tb^{n}}=-\left(a_{tb^{n}}(\phi)+a_{tb^{n-1}}(\phi)\cdot\lambda+\ldots+a_{t}(\phi)\cdot\lambda^{n}\right).

This implies
d_{tb^{n}}=-\lambda^{n}\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}a_{tb^{k}}(\phi)\cdot\lambda^{-k}
. Given
|a_{tb^{k}}(\phi)|=O(\frac{1}{t^{3}b^{3k}})
, we conclude
|d_{tb^{k}}|=O(\frac{1}{t^{3}b^{3k}})
. Combining this with (
6.4
), we obtain that the
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic function
\psi(x):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}d_{n}\cdot e^{2\pi i\,nx}
is a real
C^{1}
function, and
\phi(x)=\lambda\psi(bx)-\psi(x)+Constant
through Fourier coefficient analysis. Thus,
\phi(x)
satisfies the condition (H
∗
) by Lemma
6.1
.
∎

6.2.
Proof of Theorem B

In this subsection, we fix a
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic
C^{3}
function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Specifically, we denote
\phi(x)=(\phi_{1}(x),\phi_{2}(x),\ldots,\phi_{d}(x))
. We list all positive integers
t
(such that
b\nmid t
) in ascending order as
t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots
.

For any
1\leq j\leq d
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}+
, we define functions
R_{j,\,m},\,I_{j,\,m}:[1/b,1]\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

(6.5)

R_{j,\,m}(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\text{Re}\left\{a_{t_{m}b^{n}}(\phi_{j})\right\}\cdot\lambda^{-n}\quad\quad I_{j,\,m}(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\text{Im}\left\{a_{t_{m}b^{n}}(\phi_{j})\right\}\cdot\lambda^{-n},

where
\text{Re}\{\cdot\}
denotes the real part and
\text{Im}\{\cdot\}
denotes the imaginary part.
Because
\phi
is a
C^{3}
function, we have
|a_{t_{m}b^{n}}(\phi_{j})|=O\left(\frac{1}{t_{m}^{3}b^{3n}}\right)
. Consequently,
R_{j,\,m}
and
I_{j,\,m}
are real analytic.

Given
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\lambda\in[1/b,1]
, let’s define the matrix
A_{\lambda,\,m}\in M_{d,\,2m}(\mathbb{R})
as follows:

(6.6)

A_{\lambda,\,m}=\begin{pmatrix}R_{1,\,1}(\lambda)&\ldots&R_{1,\,m}(\lambda)&I_{1,\,1}(\lambda)&\ldots&I_{1,\,m}(\lambda)\\
R_{2,\,1}(\lambda)&\ldots&R_{2,\,m}(\lambda)&I_{2,\,1}(\lambda)&\ldots&I_{2,\,m}(\lambda)\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
R_{d,\,1}(\lambda)&\ldots&R_{d,\,m}(\lambda)&I_{d,\,1}(\lambda)&\ldots&I_{d,\,m}(\lambda)\end{pmatrix}.

The kernel space of
A_{\lambda,\,m}
is defined as:

(6.7)

\text{Ker }A_{\lambda,\,m}=\left\{\,y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,yA_{\lambda,\,m}=0\,\right\}.

We observe that
\text{Ker }A_{\lambda,\,m+1}\subset\text{Ker }A_{\lambda,\,m}.

Recall that
q^{\prime}(\phi,\lambda,b)
is defined by (
2.4
).

Lemma 6.2
.

For any
\lambda\in(1/b,1)
, we have

q^{\prime}(\phi,\lambda,b)=\text{dim}\left(\cap_{m=1}^{\infty}\text{Ker }A_{\lambda,\,m}\right).

Proof.

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\pi_{\mathbb{R}y}\phi
satisfies the condition (H
∗
). Considering
\pi_{\mathbb{R}y}\phi
as a function with range in
\mathbb{R}
, we have:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{t_{m}b^{n}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{j}\phi_{j}\right)\cdot\lambda^{-n}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{t_{m}b^{n}}(\phi_{j})\cdot\lambda^{-n}=0\quad\quad\forall\,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

by Proposition
6.1
. Combining this with (
6.5
), we get:

\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{j}\cdot R_{j,\,m}(\lambda)=0\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{j}\cdot I_{j,\,m}(\lambda)=0,\quad\quad\forall\,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Hence,
y\in\cap_{m=1}^{\infty}\text{Ker }A_{\lambda,\,m}
by (
6.7
), implying
q^{\prime}\leq\text{dim}\left(\cap_{m=1}^{\infty}\text{Ker }A_{\lambda,\,m}\right)
by the arbitrariness of
y
and the definition of
q^{\prime}
.
By Lemma
6.1
and similar methods,
\pi_{\cap_{m=1}^{\infty}\text{Ker }A_{\lambda,\,m}}(\phi)

∗
), implying

\text{dim}\left(\cap_{m=1}^{\infty}\text{Ker }A_{\lambda,\,m}\right)\leq q^{\prime}
by the maximality of
q^{\prime}
.
∎

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
B=(b_{i,\,j})_{n\times m}\in M_{n,\,m}(\mathbb{R})

1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n

1\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{k^{\prime}}\leq m

(6.8)

B\left(\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\},\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k^{\prime}}\}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}b_{i_{1},\,j_{1}}&\ldots&b_{i_{1},\,j_{k^{\prime}}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
b_{i_{k},\,j_{1}}&\ldots&b_{i_{k},\,j_{k^{\prime}}}\end{pmatrix}.

For any
1\leq k\leq\min\{m,n\}
, let

(6.9)

L_{k}(B)=\sum_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\},\,J\subset\{1,\ldots,m\},\,\#I=\#J=k}\left(\text{det}\,B(I,J)\right)^{2},

where
\text{det}(\cdot)
denotes the determinant.
The following is a basic fact in linear algebra.

Lemma 6.3
.

For any matrix
B\in M_{n,\,m}(\mathbb{R})
, we have:

n-\text{dim}\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>:\>yB=0\right\}=\max_{1\leq k\leq\min\{m,n\}}\left\{k\>:\>L_{k}(B)\neq 0\right\}.

For any
1\leq j\leq d
, define the function

(6.10)

D_{j}(\lambda)=\left(L_{j}\left(A_{1}(\lambda)\right),\,L_{j}\left(A_{2}(\lambda)\right),\ldots\right),

where

is defined by (
6.6
) (set,

if

).
Recall that
p^{\prime}(\phi,b)
is defined by (
2.5
).

Lemma 6.4
.

For any
\lambda\in(1/b,1)
, we have

(6.11)

d-q^{\prime}(\phi,\lambda,b)=\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\left\{j\>:\>D_{j}(\lambda)\neq 0\right\}.

Furthermore, we get:

(6.12)

d-p^{\prime}(\phi,b)=\max_{1\leq j\leq d,\,\lambda\in(1/b,1)}\left\{j\>:\>D_{j}(\lambda)\neq 0\right\}.

Proof.

By (
2.5
), we only need to prove (

q^{\prime}(\phi,\lambda,b)=\text{dim }\left(\text{ker }A_{\lambda,\,m}\right)\quad\text{for any }m\geq m_{0}.

Combining this with Lemma

Proof of Theorem B.

Since

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

We no longer fix the function
\phi
, and denote notations with a superscript
\phi
to indicate their dependence on the choice of
\phi
. By Lemma
6.4
, we have

(6.15)

K=\bigcup_{\lambda\in(1/b,1)}\left\{\phi\in X\>:\>D^{\phi}_{d}(\lambda)\neq 0\right\}.

To prove that

is open, we need to establish the following claim.

Claim 5
.

For any
\lambda\in(1/b,1)
,

, and

, the linear functionals

Specifically, combining this with (

) and (

), we conclude that
\left\{\phi\in X\,:\,D^{\phi}_{d}(\lambda)\neq 0\right\}
is open. Consequently,

is open as well by (
6.15
). Now, we prove the claim. Since

b^{n}\cdot\left|\,a_{t_{m}b^{n}}(\phi_{j})\,\right|\leq\parallel\phi^{\prime}_{j}\parallel_{\infty}\leq\parallel\phi\parallel_{r},\quad\quad\text{for }n\in\mathbb{N},

\left|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{t_{m}b^{n}}(\phi_{j})\cdot\lambda^{-n}\right|\leq\frac{b\lambda}{b\lambda-1}\parallel\phi\parallel_{r}.

Combining this with (

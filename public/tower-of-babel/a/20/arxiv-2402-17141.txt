Group Action Approaches in Erdos Quotient Set Problem
Will Burstein

Abstract
Let 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} denote the finite field of q q elements. For E ⊂ 𝔽 q d E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d} , denote the distance set Δ ⁡ ( E ) = { ‖ x − y ‖ 2 := ( x 1 − y 1 ) 2 + ⋯ + ( x d − y d ) 2 : ( x , y ) ∈ E 2 } \Delta(E)=\{\|x-y\|^{2}:=(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(x_{d}-y_{d})^{2}:(x,y)\in E^{2}\} . The Erdos quotient set problem was introduced in Iosevich et al. 2019 where it was shown that for even d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 that if | E | ⊂ 𝔽 q 2 |E|\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2} such that | E | >> q d / 2 |E|>>q^{d/2} , then Δ ⁡ ( E ) Δ ⁡ ( E ) := { s t : s , t ∈ Δ ( E ) , t ≠ 0 } = 𝔽 q d \frac{\Delta(E)}{\Delta(E)}:=\{\frac{s}{t}:s,t\in\Delta(E),t\not=0\}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d} . The proof of the latter result is quite sophisticated and in Pham 2023 , a simple proof using a group-action approach was obtained for the case of q ≡ 3 mod 4 q\equiv 3\mod 4 when d = 2 d=2 . In the q ≡ 3 mod 4 q\equiv 3\mod 4 setting, for each r ∈ ( 𝔽 q ) 2 r\in(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{2} , Pham 2023 showed if E ⊂ 𝔽 q E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q} , then V ⁡ ( r ) := # ⁡ { ( a , b , c , d ) ∈ E 2 : ‖ a − b ‖ 2 ‖ c − d ‖ 2 = r } >> | E | 4 q V(r):=\#\left\{(a,b,c,d)\in E^{2}:\frac{\|a-b\|^{2}}{\|c-d\|^{2}}=r\right\}>>\frac{|E|^{4}}{q} . In this work we use group action techniques in the q ≡ 3 mod 4 q\equiv 3\mod 4 setting, for d = 2 d=2 and improve the results of Pham 2023 by removing the assumption on r ∈ ( 𝔽 q ) 2 r\in(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{2} . Specifically we show if d = 2 d=2 and q ≡ 3 mod 4 q\equiv 3\mod 4 , then for each r ∈ 𝔽 q ∗ r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*} , V ⁡ ( r ) ≥ | E | 4 2 ​ q V(r)\geq\frac{|E|^{4}}{2q} if | E | ≥ 2 ​ q |E|\geq\sqrt{2}q for all r ∈ 𝔽 q r\in\mathbb{F}_{q} . Finally, we improve the main result of Bhowmik & Rakhmonov 2023 using our proof techniques from our quotient set results.

Introduction

Let
q=p^{n}
for some prime
p
and
n\geq 1
.
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denotes the finite field of
q
elements. Given
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, the Erdos Distance problem asks what that best lower bound of

\Delta(E):=\left\{\|x-y\|^{2}\ =(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(x_{d}-y_{d})^{2}:x,y\in E\right\}

is. The work of
Iosevich & Rudnev 2005
formulated the problem and showed that if
|E|\geq 2q^{(d+1)/2}
, then
\Delta(E)=\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Hart et al. 2007
showed that the
(d+1)/2
exponent is sharp when
d
is odd. Moreover, if
d
is even, it is shown in
Hart et al. 2007
that the exponent must be at least
d/2
, for the possibility of
|\Delta(E)|>>q
. It is conjectured that if
d
is even and
|E|>>q^{d/2}
, then
|\Delta(E)|>>q
. In the more general
k
-simplex setting,
Bennett et al. 2013
the results of
Iosevich & Rudnev 2005
are generalized bringing in group-action machinery.

There have been various special cases where the
(d+1)/2
exponent has been improved. The current results are in the case when
d=2
.
Chapman et al. 2011
proved that if
q\equiv 3\mod 4
,
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, and
|E|>>q^{4/3}
, then

|E|>q/2
. In the more general framework of
Bennett et al. 2013
, the latter result was achieved with the removal of the
q\equiv 3\mod 4
assumption. In
Murphy et al. 2022
, when
q=p
,
p
an odd prime, the exponent has been improved to
5/4
.

A similar problem to the Erdos distance problem is the Erdos quotient problem. The Erdos quotient set is defined as

\frac{\Delta(E)}{\Delta(E)}:=\left\{\frac{a}{b}:a\in\Delta(E),b\in\Delta(E)^{*}\right\}.

Iosevich et al. 2019
formulated the Erdos quotient problem and obtained the result below using Fourier Analytic techniques.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem 1.1 of
Iosevich et al. 2019
)
Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}

d
even. Then if
|E|\geq 9q^{\frac{d}{2}}
, we have

\mathbb{F}_{q}=\frac{\Delta(E)}{\Delta(E)}.

If
d\geq 3
is odd and
|E|\geq 6q^{q/2}
, then

(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d})^{2}\subset\frac{\Delta(E)}{\Delta(E)}.

Iosevich et al. 2019
prove both their results of Theorem
1.1
are sharp in the exponent,
d
.

One drawback of the result of Theorem
1.1
is that the proof is quite sophisticated and for a fixed
r
, does not say how many distinct
(a,b,c,d)\in E^{4}
such that
\|a-b\|^{2}/\|c-d\|^{2}=r
. In a restricted setting, the next result below alleviates the latter two drawbacks with a simple proof. The proof doesn’t use Fourier analysis and instead uses a group-action approach.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Theorem 1.2 of
Pham 2023
) Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
with
q\equiv 3\mod 4
. Assume that

|E|>>q
. Then for each
r\in(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{2}
, the number of quadruples
(a,b,c,d)\in E^{4}
such that

\|a-b\|^{2}/\|c-d\|^{2}=r
is at least

>>|E|^{4}/q.

In particular,

(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{2}\subset\frac{\Delta(E)}{\Delta(E)}.

In this work, when
d=2
, we get the result of Theorem
1.2
for all of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in the
q\equiv 3\mod 4
setting.

Recently,

generalized the result of
Iosevich et al. 2019
for quadratic forms. We state the result below after introducing the next definition. For
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
define

V(r):=\#\left\{(a,b,c,d)\in E^{4}:r=\frac{\|a-b\|^{2}}{\|c-d\|^{2}}\right\}.

For simplicity, we state the relevant main results of

in the specific case of the quadratic form
Q(x)=\|x\|^{2}

Theorem 1.3
.

(Theorem 1.6 in
Iosevich et al. 2023
)
Assume that
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
,
d\geq 2

If
d\geq 2

If

is odd and
|E|\geq\frac{11}{6}q^{(d+1)/2}

\displaystyle V(r)\geq\frac{2|E|^{4}}{363q}.

r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}

In Theorem
1.3
, similar results to Theorem

The construction of the matrices

Proof.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.
The proof is similar to Theorem
1.2
. Assume that
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Then
\sqrt{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. For any characteristic,
p
, it is a basic fact from the theory of finite fields that there is some
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
a^{2}+b^{2}=r
. We construct the diagonal matrix,

A=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\begin{bmatrix}a&-b\\
b&a\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(2)

Note that
A
is orthogonal by construction. Denote
O(2)
, the group of orthogonal matrices in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
and
\theta\in O(2)
. Define the set,

\eta_{\theta}(z)=\{(u,v)\in E^{2}:u-\sqrt{r}\theta Av=z\}.

Consider the sum,

\sum_{\theta\in O(d),z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}\eta_{\theta}(z)=|E|^{2}|O(d)|.

By the Cauchy Schwarz inequality,

|E|^{4}|O(2)|^{2}=\left(\sum_{\theta\in O(2),z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}\eta_{\theta}(z)\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{\theta,z}\eta_{\theta}(z)^{2}q^{2}|O(2)|.

It follows that,

\displaystyle\frac{|E|^{4}|O(2)|}{q^{2}}\leq\sum_{\theta,z}\eta_{\theta}(z)^{2}.

\sum_{\theta,z}\eta_{\theta}(z)^{2}
counts the number of
(u,v,w,x)\in E^{4}
such that
\|u-v\|^{2}=r\|w-x\|^{2}
modulo double counting by the stabilizer of
c-d
for each
(c,d)\in E^{2}
. The stabilizer of each
(c,d)\in E^{2}\backslash\text{diag}(E)
has
|O(1)|
elements (see page 3 of
Bennett et al. 2013
).

The number of tuples,
(u,v,w,x)
in the sum,
\sum_{\theta,z}\eta_{\theta}(z)^{2}
, where
u=v
and
w=x
are counted
\sum_{\theta\in O(2),z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}\eta_{\theta}(z)=|E|^{2}|O(2)|
times.

Define

N_{0}=\#\{(u,v,w,x)\in E^{4}:\|u-v\|^{2}=r\|w-x\|^{2}=0\text{ and }u-v,w-x\not=0\}.

Since
q\equiv 3\mod 4
,
N_{0}=0
. Thus, if
|E|\geq\sqrt{2}q
,

V(r)\geq\frac{1}{|O(1)|}\left(\sum_{\theta,z}\eta_{\theta}(z)^{2}-|E|^{2}|O(2)|-|O(1)|N_{0}\right)

=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\theta,z}\eta_{\theta}(z)^{2}-|E|^{2}|O(2)|\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|E|^{4}(q+1)}{q^{2}}-|E^{2}|(q+1)\right).

From above, if
|E|\geq\sqrt{2}q
, then
V(r)\geq\frac{|E|^{4}(q+1)}{4q^{2}}
, and we’re done.
∎

The Exactness of the ℓ 1 \ell_{1} Penalty Function for a Class of Mathematical Programs with Generalized Complementarity Constraints
Yukuan Hu, Xin Liu

Abstract
In a Mathematical Program with Generalized Complementarity Constraints (MPGCC), complementarity relationships are imposed between each pair of variable blocks. MPGCC includes the traditional Mathematical Program with Complementarity Constraints (MPCC) as a special case. On account of the disjunctive feasible region, MPCC and MPGCC are generally difficult to handle. The ℓ 1 \ell_{1} penalty method, often adopted in computation, opens a way of circumventing the difficulty. Yet it remains unclear about the exactness of the ℓ 1 \ell_{1} penalty function, namely, whether there exists a sufficiently large penalty parameter so that the penalty problem shares the optimal solution set with the original one. In this paper, we consider a class of MPGCCs that are of multi-affine objective functions. This problem class finds applications in various fields, e.g., the multi-marginal optimal transport problems in many-body quantum physics and the pricing problem in network transportation. We first provide an instance from this class, the exactness of whose ℓ 1 \ell_{1} penalty function cannot be derived by existing tools. We then establish the exactness results under rather mild conditions. Our results cover those existing ones for MPCC and apply to multi-block contexts.

Introduction

A Mathematical Program with Complementarity Constraints (MPCC)
takes the form

\begin{array}[]{cl}\min\limits_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}&g(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\\
\mathrm{s.}~\mathrm{t.}&\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq 0,\\
&\mathbf{x}\geq 0,~\mathbf{y}\geq 0,~\left\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\right\rangle=0,\end{array}

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
g:\mathbb{R}^{2m}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathbf{h}:\mathbb{R}^{2m}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
. MPCC has found wide applications in economics and engineering design. For a review on this topic, interested readers may refer to
[
27
,
30
]
and the references therein. If
g
and
\mathbf{h}
are affine, the MPCC
equation

1.1
reduces to a Linear Program with Complementarity Constraints (LPCC). For problems with multiple variable blocks,
the complementarity relationships imposed between each block pair leads to the following Mathematical Program with Generalized Complementarity Constraints (MPGCC)

\begin{array}[]{cl}\min\limits_{\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{n}}&\tilde{g}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{n})\\
\mathrm{s.}~\mathrm{t.}&\tilde{\mathbf{h}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{n})\leq 0,\\
&\mathbf{x}_{i}\geq 0,~i=1,\ldots,n,\\
&\left\langle\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j}\right\rangle=0,~\forall~i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:i\neq j,\end{array}

(1.2)

where
\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
,
\tilde{g}:\mathbb{R}^{mn}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}:\mathbb{R}^{mn}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
.

It is worth mentioning that MPCC
equation

1.1

1.2
violate standard constraint qualifications at any feasible point, such as the Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification (MFCQ)
[
16
]
. Consequently, the local solutions, and of course optimal solutions, do not necessarily satisfy the associated Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions, which renders these problems particularly difficult to cope with. It can also be proved that globally solving a general MPCC or MPGCC is NP-complete
[

,

Instead of facing the original MPCC

Literature Review

,

There are also works dedicated to the

P

,

]
show the exactness based upon the finiteness of extreme point sets; see also earlier works

,

,

\mathbf{x}_{j}^{*}\in\overline{\Omega_{j}^{+}}
be its extreme-point optimal solution, whose existence is ensured similarly. Clearly, it holds, for any
j\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
, that

f_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{*},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{j-1}^{*},\mathbf{x}_{j}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j+1},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n})\leq f_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{*},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{j-1}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j},\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j+1},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n}).

Together, we have a chain

f_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{*},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{n}^{*})\leq f_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{*},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n})\leq\cdots\leq f_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{2},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n})\leq f_{\beta}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n}).

Note that due to the separability of
\Omega
,
\bar{\Omega}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\Omega_{i}^{+}}
and
\mathbf{z}^{*}:=(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{*},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{n}^{*})\in\bar{\Omega}
. Moreover, since
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
is an optimal solution of
equation

Pβ
, one has
\mathbf{z}^{*}\in S^{\mathrm{opt}}_{\beta}
in view of the last inequality chain. This completes the proof.
∎

Utilizing the finiteness of
\bar{\Omega}
and
1
, we could show the following partial exact penalty result.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
1
holds. Then there exists
\bar{\beta}>0
such that
\bar{S}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{\beta}\subseteq S^{\mathrm{opt}}\subseteq S^{\mathrm{opt}}_{\beta}
for any
\beta\geq\bar{\beta}
.

Proof
.

We begin with the proof of
\bar{S}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}\subseteq S^{\mathrm{opt}}
for all sufficiently large
\beta
by contradiction. Suppose that there exists
\{\beta^{(k)}>0\}\to\infty
and a corresponding
\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}\subseteq\bar{S}_{\beta^{(k)}}^{\mathrm{opt}}\setminus S^{\mathrm{opt}}
(due to
1
). Clearly, if
\mathbf{z}\in S_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}
for some
\beta>0
and
p(\mathbf{z})=0
, then
\mathbf{z}\in S^{\mathrm{opt}}
. It then holds that
p(\mathbf{z}^{(k)})>0
for each
k
. Since
\{\Omega_{i}^{+}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are polyhedral, one has
\left|\bar{\Omega}\right|<\infty
and

\inf_{l}f(\mathbf{z}^{(l)})>-\infty~\text{and}~\inf_{l}p(\mathbf{z}^{(l)})>0.

(3.2)

Given any
\mathbf{z}
feasible for
equation

P
, it holds, for any
k
, that

\displaystyle\infty>f(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{z})+\beta^{(k)}p(\mathbf{z})\geq f_{\beta^{(k)}}(\mathbf{z}^{(k)})

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{z}^{(k)})+\beta^{(k)}p(\mathbf{z}^{(k)})

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{l}f(\mathbf{z}^{(l)})+\beta^{(k)}\inf_{l}p(\mathbf{z}^{(l)}).

Based upon
equation

3.2
, we reach a contradiction after passing
k
to
\infty
in the last inequality. Therefore, there exists
\bar{\beta}>0
such that
\bar{S}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}\subseteq S^{\mathrm{opt}}
holds for any
\beta\geq\bar{\beta}
.

Now we show the second inclusion, i.e., given
\beta\geq\bar{\beta}
, for any
\mathbf{z}^{*}\in S^{\mathrm{opt}}
and
\mathbf{z}\in\Omega
,
f_{\beta}(\mathbf{z}^{*})\leq f_{\beta}(\mathbf{z})
. By
1
,
\bar{S}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}\neq\emptyset
. Pick
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in\bar{S}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}
. From the first inclusion, we know that
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in S^{\mathrm{opt}}
. Hence
f_{\beta}(\mathbf{z}^{*})=f(\mathbf{z}^{*})=f(\hat{\mathbf{z}})=f_{\beta}(\hat{\mathbf{z}})
. Since
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in S^{\mathrm{opt}}_{\beta}
, we further have
f_{\beta}(\hat{\mathbf{z}})\leq f_{\beta}(\mathbf{z})
. Combining with the last equality chain, one concludes that
f_{\beta}(\mathbf{z}^{*})\leq f_{\beta}(\mathbf{z})
.
∎

To complete the exactness, it suffices to show the reverse direction:
S_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}\subseteq S^{\mathrm{opt}}
. We begin with the following lemma, which states that the optimal solution of
equation

Pβ
with only one non-extreme variable block must be optimal for
equation

P
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
1
holds. For a given
\beta\geq\bar{\beta}
, let
\hat{\mathbf{z}}:=(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n})\in S_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}
. If there exists only one index
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\notin\overline{\Omega_{i}^{+}}
, then
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in S^{\mathrm{opt}}
.

Proof
.

Without loss of generality, we assume
i=1
. Suppose, on the contrary, that
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\notin S^{\mathrm{opt}}
. Then we must have
p(\hat{\mathbf{z}})>0
. Since
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in S_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}
,
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}
solves the linear program
equation

3.1
and
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\in\mathrm{rbd}~\Omega_{1}^{+}
. Due to
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\notin\overline{\Omega_{1}^{+}}
,
equation

3.1
is degenerate on a face, say
\hat{F}_{1}
, of
\Omega_{1}^{+}
where
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}
lies and, therefore, each point in
\hat{F}_{1}
solves
equation

3.1
. In particular, by
2
, each extreme point of
\hat{F}_{1}
, together with
\{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\}_{j=2}^{n}
, solves
equation

P
. Denote by
\{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(i)}\}_{i\in\hat{I}_{1}}
the extreme points of
\hat{F}_{1}
, where
\hat{I}_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is a finite index set. Note that
\hat{F}_{1}
is convex compact. By Minkowski’s theorem (see, e.g.,
[
31
, Corollary 18.5.1]
),
\hat{F}_{1}
is the convex hull of
\{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(i)}\}_{i\in\hat{I}_{1}}
. Since
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\notin\overline{\Omega_{1}^{+}}
,
\left|\hat{I}_{1}\right|\geq 2
.

Pick any two elements in
\hat{I}_{1}
, e.g.,
i=1,2
. Then
(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(i)},\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{2},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n})\in S^{\mathrm{opt}}
and
p(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(i)},\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{2},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n})=0
for
i=1,2
. Since the function
p
is linear with respect to the first block, one must have
p(\mathbf{x}_{1},\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{2},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n})=0
for any
\mathbf{x}_{1}\in\ell(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(1)},\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(2)})
.

Invoking the arguments in the former paragraph repeatedly, we could deduce that the function
p\equiv 0
on
\mathrm{rbd}~\hat{F}_{1}
. If
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\in\mathrm{rbd}~\hat{F}_{1}
, we then reach a contradiction. Otherwise, since
\hat{F}_{1}
is convex compact,
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}
must lies on the line segment connected by two points in
\mathrm{rbd}~\hat{F}_{1}
. Again, since
p
is linear with respect to the first block,
p(\hat{\mathbf{z}})=0
, leading to a contradiction. The proof is complete.
∎

We then prove the exactness result by mathematical induction.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
1
holds. For a given
\beta\geq\bar{\beta}
,
S_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}=S^{\mathrm{opt}}
.

Proof
.

The “
\supseteq
” part is ensured by
2
. Below, we deal with the “
\subseteq
” part. We prove by mathematical induction that, for
r\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
, any
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in S_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}
with only
r
indice
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
for which
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i_{s}}\notin\overline{\Omega_{i_{s}}^{+}}
,
s=1,\ldots,r
, must belong to
S^{\mathrm{opt}}
. It is easy to check that if this claim holds true, the desired result will follow. The cases
r=0
and
r=1
is valid in view of
2
and
2
, respectively. Now suppose the claim is valid for
r=t
where
t\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
and we show the
r=t+1
case.

Without loss of generality, we assume
i_{s}=s
for
s=1,\ldots,t+1
. Suppose otherwise
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\notin S^{\mathrm{opt}}
. Then we have
p(\hat{\mathbf{z}})>0
. Since
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in S_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}
,
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t+1}
solves the following linear program

\min_{\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\in\Omega_{t+1}^{+}}~~f_{\beta}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{t+1},\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t+2},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n}).

(3.3)

Due to
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t+1}\notin\overline{\Omega_{t+1}^{+}}
,
equation

3.3
is degenerate on a face, say
\hat{F}_{t+1}
, of
\Omega_{t+1}^{+}
where
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t+1}
lies and, therefore, each point in
\hat{F}_{t+1}
solves
equation

3.3
. By the optimality of objective value, each extreme point of
\hat{F}_{t+1}
, together with
\{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\}_{j\neq t+1}
, solves
equation

Pβ
. Denote by
\{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+1}^{(i)}\}_{i\in\hat{I}_{t+1}}
the extreme points of
\hat{F}_{t+1}
, where
\hat{I}_{t+1}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is a finite index set, and let
\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(i)}:=(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t},\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+1}^{(i)},\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t+2},\ldots,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n})
for any
i\in\hat{I}_{t+1}
. Then
\{\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(i)}\}_{i\in\hat{I}_{t+1}}\subseteq S_{\beta}^{\mathrm{opt}}
. For each
i\in\hat{I}_{t+1}
, notice that there are only
t
variable blocks in
\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(i)}
that do not lie in their corresponding extreme point sets. By induction, we get
\{\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(i)}\}_{i\in\hat{I}_{t+1}}\subseteq S^{\mathrm{opt}}
and, therefore,
p(\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(i)})=0
for any
i\in\hat{I}_{t+1}
. By similar arguments on
\hat{F}_{t+1}
as in
2
, we could arrive a contradiction. Hence, the claim is true for
r=t+1
.

By mathematical induction, the claim holds true for any
r\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
, which completes the proof.
∎

4
Conclusions and Perspectives

In this paper, we prove the exactness of the
\ell_{1}
penalty function for the model
equation

P
. Without restrictive assumptions such as the strict complementarity condition or the positive-multiplier nondegeneracy condition in the literatures, we manage to show
S^{\mathrm{opt}}=S^{\mathrm{opt}}_{\beta}
for all sufficiently large
\beta
by exploiting the multi-affine structure of the objective function and generalized complementarity constraints. Our results cover those existing ones for LPCC and apply to the multi-block settings with nonlinear objective functions. We also put forward an instance of
equation

P
, the exactness of whose
\ell_{1}
penalty function cannot be implied by the existing results, but is ensured by ours.

The computational aspects of the model
equation

P
deserve future investigation. Specifically, in virtue of the applications in
[
21
]
, it is essential to design tailor-made efficient solvers that produce approximate optimal solutions for
equation

Pβ
.

Moreover, the results stated in this paper can be easily extended to a class of multi-affine optimization problems in the form

\begin{array}[]{cl}\min\limits_{\mathbf{z}}&\tilde{f}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{n})\\
\mathrm{s.}~\mathrm{t.}&\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\Omega_{i},~i=1,\ldots,n,\\
&h_{j}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{n})=0,~j=1,\ldots,q,\end{array}

where
\tilde{f}
,
h_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{mn}\to\mathbb{R}
(
j=1,\ldots,p
) are multi-affine,
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are polyhedrons; for
j=1,\ldots,q
,
h_{j}\geq 0
over
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Omega_{i}
. The related
\ell_{1}
penalty function
\tilde{f}_{\beta}(\mathbf{z})=\tilde{f}(\mathbf{z})+\beta\sum_{j=1}^{q}h_{j}(\mathbf{z})
. For more details on the general multi-affine optimization, see
[
14
,
18
]
and the references therein.

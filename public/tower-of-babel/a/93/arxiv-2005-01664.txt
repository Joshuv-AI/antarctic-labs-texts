Cancellation for ( G , n ) (G,n) -complexes and the Swan finiteness obstruction
John Nicholson

Abstract
. In previous work, we related homotopy types of finite ( G , n ) (G,n) -complexes when G G has periodic cohomology to projective ℤ ​ G \mathbb{Z}G -modules representing the Swan finiteness obstruction. We use this to determine when X ∨ S n ≃ Y ∨ S n X\vee S^{n}\simeq Y\vee S^{n} implies X ≃ Y X\simeq Y for finite ( G , n ) (G,n) -complexes X X and Y Y , and give lower bounds on the number of homotopically distinct pairs when this fails. The proof involves constructing projective ℤ ​ G \mathbb{Z}G -modules as lifts of locally free modules over orders in products of quaternion algebras, whose existence follows from the Eichler mass formula. In the case n = 2 n=2 , difficulties arise which lead to a new approach to finding a counterexample to Wall’s D2 problem.

1.2.
Applications to Wall’s D2 problem

We now consider the case
n=2
in more detail. Recall that every finite presentation
\mathcal{P}
for a group
G
has an associated presentation complex
X_{\mathcal{P}}
which is a finite 2-complex with
\pi_{1}(X_{\mathcal{P}})\cong G
. Conversely, every (connected) finite 2-complex
X
with
\pi_{1}(X)\cong G
is homotopy equivalent to
X_{\mathcal{P}}
for some finite presentation
\mathcal{P}
for
G
.
We say that two finite presentations
\mathcal{P}
,
\mathcal{Q}
are homotopy equivalent if
X_{\mathcal{P}}
,
X_{\mathcal{Q}}
are homotopy equivalent (see Section
9
for more details).

For a finite presentation
\mathcal{P}
define its
deficiency

\Def(\mathcal{P})
to be the number of generators minus the number of relators. For a finitely presented group
G
, define the
deficiency

\Def(G)
to be the maximum deficiency across all finite presentations for
G
.
Note that
\chi(X_{\mathcal{P}})=1-\Def(\mathcal{P})
and so
N(G,2)
is the number of homotopy classes of presentations of
G
with maximal deficiency.

Let
G
be a group with 4-periodic cohomology.
It is a consequence Theorems
A
and
B
that, if
G
has the D2 property, then we would expect non-cancellation examples for finite 2-complexes over
G
provided
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)
is sufficiently large. For example, this applies when
G=Q_{28}
is the quaternion group of order 28 since
G
has the D2 property and
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)=3

[
Nic21b
, Theorem 8.11]
.

However, if we are interested in the D2 property itself, we could instead view Theorems
A
and
B
as a constraint that needs to be satisfied in order for the D2 property to hold.
For example, let
Q_{4n}
denote the quaternion group of order
4n
which has standard presentation
\mathcal{P}_{\std}=\langle x,y\mid x^{n}=y^{2},y^{-1}xy=x^{-1}\rangle
. Since
\Def(Q_{4n})=0
, the presentations
\mathcal{P}
with maximal deficiency are
balanced
in that
\Def(\mathcal{P})=0
.
If
Q_{4n}
has the D2 property for all
n\geq 2
, then
m_{\mathbb{H}}(Q_{4n})=\lfloor n/2\rfloor
implies that
N(Q_{4n},2)\gg e^{\lambda n}
for all
\lambda>0
and
n
sufficiently large.
In
[
MP21
, Section 3]
, Mannan-Popiel introduced a family of presentations

\mathcal{P}_{\MP}^{r}=\langle x,y\mid x^{n}=y^{2},y^{-1}xyx^{r-1}=x^{r}y^{-1}x^{2}y\rangle

which, for certain

, present

. Note that, if
r=0

, then
\mathcal{P}_{\MP}^{r}
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathcal{P}_{\std}
.
It was proposed in
[

]
that the

In Proposition

does not have the D2 property or

This has the following broad generalisation.

(ii)

Remark 1.3

Whilst we have restricted to

This would be in contrast to the situation for finite abelian groups

1.3.
Applications to the classification of 4-manifolds

In
[

-complexes is the homotopy classification of

-complex

The case

-manifolds which are stably diffeomorphic but not homotopy equivalent are of the form

,

where

,

are homotopically distinct finite 2-complexes with certain finite abelian fundamental groups

]
.

Let

be a finite group with
4
-periodic cohomology and

. Then, conditional on

having the D2 property, Theorem
A
implies that there exists homotopically distinct finite 2-complexes

for

with

and
\chi(X_{1})=\chi(X_{2})
. This raises the question of whether the

contain further examples of stably diffeomorphic closed smooth 4-manifolds which are homotopically distinct.

Problem 1.4
.

Let
G
have
4
-periodic cohomology with
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)\geq 3
.
Do there exist homotopically distinct finite
2
-complexes
X
,
Y
with fundamental group
G
and
\chi(X)=\chi(Y)
such that

,
M(Y)
are homotopically distinct?

One difficulty with tackling this problem is that, if
G
has

-periodic cohomology and
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)\geq 3
then we do not know whether homotopically distinct finite 2-complexes

,
Y
with fundamental group
G
and

actually exist unless we (somehow) know that

In general, we can still apply Theorem

(see also Remark

(b)) which is the same statement as Theorem

but with

replaced by the set of homotopy types of finite D2-complexes

Recently, Adem-Hambleton showed that every finite D2-complex

finite has an associated closed topological 4-manifold

. It may therefore be possible to use Theorem

We will not explore Problem

1.4.
The action of
\Aut(G)
on general projective modules

We will conclude by considering two special properties that the projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules
P_{(G,n)}
have, and ask whether these properties are shared by arbitrary projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules. This is of purely algebraic interest.

In order to compute
\HT(G,n)
from
\PHT(G,n)\cong[P_{(G,n)}]
, we needed to quotient by a certain action of
\Aut(G)
on
[P_{(G,n)}]
which was defined using
\psi:\Aut(G)\to(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
.
Let
C(\mathbb{Z}G)
denote the projective class group and
T_{G}
the Swan subgroup which is generated by the
(I,r)
where
(r,|G|)=1
. The existence of the action was dependent on the following property of

:

(P1)

If
\theta\in\Aut(G)
, then
[(P_{(G,n)})_{\theta}]=[P_{(G,n)}]\in C(\mathbb{Z}G)/T_{G}
, i.e.
[P_{(G,n)}]\in(C(\mathbb{Z}G)/T_{G})^{\Aut(G)}
.

Given such an action, the second property that the
P_{(G,n)}
have is a consequence of Theorem
A
:

(P2)

[P_{(G,n)}]
has cancellation if and only if
[P_{(G,n)}]/\Aut(G)
has cancellation.

These properties each hold for free modules since the
\Aut(G)
action on a free module is trivial. This implies that the class
\mathbb{Z}G\in[\mathbb{Z}G]
is a fixed point under the
\Aut(G)
-action and so
[\mathbb{Z}G]
has cancellation if and only if
[\mathbb{Z}G]/\Aut(G)
has cancellation.

In contrast to this, we show that properties (P1) and (P2) fail for arbitrary projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules, even in the case where
G
has periodic cohomology. For (P1), we show that, if
G=C_{p}
for
p\geq 23
prime, then there exists
[P]\in C(\mathbb{Z}G)
and
\theta\in\Aut(G)
such that
[P_{\theta}]\neq[P]\in C(\mathbb{Z}G)/T_{G}
(see Theorem
10.3
).
For (P2) note that, if
G=Q_{28}
is the quaternion group of order 28, then
T_{G}=0
and
\Aut(G)
acts trivially on
C(\mathbb{Z}G)
. We will prove the following, which is stated later as Theorem
10.4
.

Theorem 1.5
.

There exists a projective
\mathbb{Z}Q_{28}
-module
P
such that
[P]
has non-cancellation and
[P]/\Aut(Q_{28})
has cancellation. Here
\theta\in\Aut(Q_{28})
acts on
[P]
by sending
P_{0}\mapsto(P_{0})_{\theta}
where
P_{0}\in[P]
.

If

P_{0}\mapsto(P_{0})_{\theta}\otimes(I,\psi(\theta))
for any

of rank one.
For example,

acts on the stably free class
[\mathbb{Z}G]
for all finite groups

via the trivial map
\psi(\theta)=1
for all
\theta\in\Aut(G)
. More generally, if

has periodic cohomology, then

[

, Section 7]
).

Recall that, if

for any

is an

-module, we can define the

of

by

as the tensor product

f_{\#}(M)=S\otimes_{R}M

which is defined since

as a right

-module and

as a left
R
-module. We will view this as a left
S
-module where left-multiplication by

is given by

x\in S
and
m\in M
.

For example, a group homomorphism
f:G\to H
can be viewed as a ring homomorphism
f:\mathbb{Z}G\to\mathbb{Z}H
by sending
\sum_{g\in G}x_{g}g\mapsto\sum_{g\in G}x_{g}f(g)
where
x_{g}\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
M
is a
\mathbb{Z}G
-module then
f_{\#}(M)=\mathbb{Z}H\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}M
coincides with the induced module.

Recall that a subgroup
N\leq G
is
characteristic
if every
\varphi\in\Aut(G)
has
\varphi(N)=N
and we say that a quotient
f:G\to H
is characteristic if
\Ker(f)\leq G
is a characteristic subgroup.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f:G\twoheadrightarrow H
be a characteristic quotient and let
\theta\in\Aut(G)
. If
M
is a
\mathbb{Z}G
-module, then

f_{\#}(M_{\theta})\cong f_{\#}(M)_{\bar{\theta}}.

Proof.

Since
f
is characteristic, there is a map
\bar{\cdot}:\Aut(G)\to\Aut(H)
such that
f\circ\theta=\bar{\theta}\circ f
for all
\theta\in\Aut(G)
. The result now follows from
[
Nic20
, Corollary 8.4]
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f:G\twoheadrightarrow H
and
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
, then

f_{\#}((I_{G},r))\cong(I_{H},r).

Proof.

By
[
DM85
, Remark 2.3]
, we have that
(I_{G},r)\cong(\Sigma_{G},r^{-1})
where
\Sigma_{G}=\sum_{g\in G}g
denotes the group norm. Hence it suffices to prove instead that
f_{\#}((\Sigma_{G},r))\cong(\Sigma_{H},r)
.
Let
d=|G|/|H|
and, since
(d,r)=1
, there exists
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
ad+br=1
. Now define

\psi:(\Sigma_{H},r)\to f_{\#}((\Sigma_{G},r))=\mathbb{Z}H\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}(\Sigma_{G},r)

by sending
r\mapsto 1\otimes r
and
\Sigma_{H}\mapsto a(1\otimes\Sigma_{G})+b\Sigma_{H}(1\otimes r)
, and it is straightforward to check that
\psi
is a
\mathbb{Z}H
-module isomorphism.
∎

Our main result gives a possible way to show how
[P]/\Aut(G)
has non-cancellation when
f:G\twoheadrightarrow H
is a characteristic quotient and
f_{*}=\bar{\cdot}:\Aut(G)\to\Aut(H)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f:G\twoheadrightarrow H
is a characteristic quotient and
P\in P(\mathbb{Z}G)
. If
\Aut(G)
acts on
[P]
and
\bar{P}=f_{\#}(P)
, then there is a surjection of graded trees

f_{\#}:[P]/\Aut(G)\twoheadrightarrow[\bar{P}]/\IM(f_{*})

where the action of
\IM(f_{*})\subseteq\Aut(H)
on
[\bar{P}]
is induced by
f
.

Proof.

If
\otimes
denotes the tensor product of
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules, then it is easy to see that
f_{\#}(M\otimes N)\cong f_{\#}(M)\otimes N
for all
M
,
N
. Combining with Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
gives

\displaystyle f_{\#}((I_{G},r)\otimes P_{\theta})

\displaystyle\cong(I_{H},r)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}H}P_{\theta}\cong((I_{H},r)\otimes\mathbb{Z}H)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}H}P_{\theta}

\displaystyle\cong(I_{H},r)\otimes(\mathbb{Z}H\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}H}P_{\theta})\cong(I_{H},r)\otimes\bar{P}_{\bar{\theta}}

where
\bar{\cdot}:\Aut(G)\to\Aut(H)
. Since
[(I_{G},r)\otimes P_{\theta}]=[P]
, this implies that
[(I_{H},r)\otimes\bar{P}_{\bar{\theta}}]=[\bar{P}]
and so
\Aut(H)
acts on
[\bar{P}]
by sending
P_{0}
to
(I_{H},\varphi(\theta))\otimes P_{0}
.

Now note that the map
f_{\#}:[P]\to[\bar{P}]
is well-defined and surjective by
[
Swa83
, Theorem A10]
. By the argument above,
f_{\#}
respects these actions and so induces a map
f_{\#}:[P]/\Aut(G)\to[\bar{P}]/\Aut(H)
which is necessarily surjective. ∎

We will now show how this can be applied in the case where

has periodic cohomology. First note the following:

Lemma 3.4
.

If

has periodic cohomology and

has a quotient

Proof.

Since

, the possible types are I and II by Theorem

. If
H
is the binary polyhedral quotient of maximal order, then Proposition
2.2
implies

for some

. If
b=\#\bm(G)
, then Theorem

implies
b=1,2,3
.

Let

. If

, then Proposition

implies
m=m_{\mathbb{H}}(Q_{4n})=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor\leq\frac{n}{2}
and so

.
If

, then
G
has type IIa. If
Q_{8m_{i}}
are the maximal binary polyhedral quotients for

, then the proof of Theorem

implies that

if

m=(m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3})-2

∎

In fact, the quotient

is always characteristic due to the following.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
f:G\twoheadrightarrow H
where

has periodic cohomology and

Proof.

Let

. Then

By combining this with Theorem

Corollary 3.6
.

Let

-periodic cohomology and

. Then there is a surjection of graded trees

.

Note that the two bounds for

4.
Cancellation for the Swan finiteness obstruction

The aim of this section will be to use the results in Section
2
to prove the following cancellation result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
have
k
-periodic cohomology, let
n=ik
or
ik-2
for some
i\geq 1
and let
P_{(G,n)}
be a projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-module for which
[P_{(G,n)}]=\sigma_{k}(G)\in C(\mathbb{Z}G)/T_{G}
.
Then
[P_{(G,n)}]
has cancellation if and only if
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)\leq 2
.

The forward implication, that
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)\geq 3
implies
[P_{(G,n)}]
has non-cancellation, follows immediately by combining Lemma
3.4
with
[
Swa83
, Theorem A]
(see also Lemma
7.2
). Note that it also follows from the stronger statement that
[P_{(G,n)}]/\Aut(G)
has non-cancellation, which will be proven in Section
7
.
The remainder of this section will be devoted to the proof of the backward direction, that
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)\leq 2
implies
[P_{(G,n)}]
has cancellation.

Recall that, if
G
is finite,
I=\Ker(\varepsilon:\mathbb{Z}G\to\mathbb{Z})
is the augmentation ideal and
(r,|G|)=1
, then
(I,r)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}G
is a projective ideal. Since
(I,r)\cong(I,s)
if
r\equiv s
mod
|G|
, we often write
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
,
As noted in
[
Nic20
, Section 4.2]
, if
P
is a projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-module and
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
, then we can define
(I,r)\otimes P
to be a (left)
\mathbb{Z}G
-module since
(I,r)
is a two-sided ideal. We can use this action to show the following.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group let
P,Q
be projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules such that
[P]-[Q]\in T_{G}
. Then there is an isomorphism of graded trees

[P]\cong[Q]

given by sending
P_{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}G^{m}\mapsto((I,r)\otimes P_{0})\oplus\mathbb{Z}G^{m}
for some
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
where
P_{0}
is a rank one projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-module and
m\geq 0
is an integer.

Proof.

Let
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
be such that
[Q]=[P]+[(I,r)]\in C(\mathbb{Z}G)
. By
[
Swa60a
]
, an arbitrary element of
[P]
has the form
P_{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}G^{m}
where
P_{0}
is a rank one projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-module and
m\geq 0
. By
[
Nic20
, Lemma 4.15]
, we have that

[(I,r)\otimes P_{0}]=[(I,r)]+[P_{0}]=[Q]

and so
P_{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}G^{m}\mapsto((I,r)\otimes P_{0})\oplus\mathbb{Z}G^{m}
defines a map
[P]\to[Q]
which preserves the rank of the projective modules.
To see that it is bijective note that, if
r,s\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
, then
(I,r)\otimes(I,s)\cong(I,rs)
and so the map has inverse

P_{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}G^{m}\mapsto((I,r^{-1})\otimes P_{0})\oplus\mathbb{Z}G^{m}
.
∎

In particular, if
P
and
Q
are both representatives of the finiteness obstruction
\sigma_{k}(G)
, then
[P]\cong[Q]
.
Hence, it suffices to prove Theorem
4.1
for
any
representative
P_{(G,n)}
of the finiteness obstruction.

If
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)=0
, i.e.
G
satisfies the Eichler condition, then
[P_{(G,n)}]
automatically has cancellation by the Swan-Jacobinski theorem
[
Swa80
, Theorem 9.3]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
G
satisfies the Eichler condition, then
\mathbb{Z}G
has projective cancellation, i.e.
[P]
has cancellation for all projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules
P
.

If
\sigma_{k}(G)=0
, then
\mathbb{Z}G
is a representative of the finiteness obstruction and we know already from
[
Nic21b
, Theorem 7.1]
that
[\mathbb{Z}G]
has cancellation if and only if
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)\leq 2
.
It therefore suffices to restrict our attention to those groups with
\sigma_{k}(G)\neq 0
and
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)\neq 0
and so, by Theorem
2.11
, it remains to prove Theorem
4.1
in the case where
G
has type II with
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)=1
or
2
, or type IV and
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)=2
.

In order to deal with these cases, we will appeal to the following which is also a consequence of
[
Nic21b
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f:G\twoheadrightarrow H
where
G
has periodic cohomology and
H
is a binary polyhedral group such that
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)=m_{\mathbb{H}}(H)\leq 2
. If
P\in P(\mathbb{Z}G)
is such that
f_{\#}([P])\in T_{H}
, then
[P]
has cancellation.

Proof.

Let
r_{H}\in(\mathbb{Z}/|H|)^{\times}
be such that
f_{\#}([P])=[(I,r_{H})]
. By Lemma
3.2
, the induced map
T_{G}\to T_{H}
is surjective and so there exists
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
such that
f_{\#}((I,r))\cong(I,r_{H})
, i.e.
r\equiv r_{H}
mod
|H|
.
By
[
Swa60a
]
, there exists
P_{0}\in[P]
of rank one.
By
[
Nic20
, Lemma 4.15]
, we have that

f_{\#}([(I,r^{-1})\otimes P_{0}])=f_{\#}([(I,r^{-1})]+[P])=[(I,r_{H}^{-1})]+[(I,r_{H})]=0\in C(\mathbb{Z}H)

where we used that
[P_{0}]=[P]
. By
[
Swa83
, Theorem I]
, we know that
[\mathbb{Z}H]
has cancellation and so
f_{\#}((I,r^{-1})\otimes P_{0})\cong\mathbb{Z}H
. Since the map
\mathbb{Z}H^{\times}\to K_{1}(\mathbb{Z}H)
is surjective
[
MOV83
, Theorems 7.15-7.18]
, the conditions of
[
Nic21b
, Theorem 5.1]
are met and so
[(I,r^{-1})\otimes P_{0}]
has cancellation. By Proposition
4.2
, this implies that
[P]=[P_{0}]
has cancellation. ∎

Type II

If
G
has type II and
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)=1
or
2
, then
\mathcal{B}(G)=\{Q_{8}\}
or
\{Q_{16}\}
. Hence, for

or

with

. By
[
Swa83
, Theorems III,IV]
, we have that
C(\mathbb{Z}Q_{8})=T_{Q_{8}}
and

and so

automatically. Hence

has cancellation, by Lemma

.

Type IV

If
G
has type IV, then
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)=m_{\mathbb{H}}(\widetilde{O})=2
and there exists a quotient
f:G\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{O}
. Recall that
Q_{12}\leq\widetilde{O}
and that this is unique up to conjugacy
[
Swa83
, Lemma 14.3]
. We will need the following lemma, the proof of which is contained in the proof of
[

, Theorem 7.2]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
P
is a projective
\mathbb{Z}\widetilde{O}
-module, then
[P]\in T_{\widetilde{O}}
if and only if
[\Res_{Q_{12}}^{\widetilde{O}}(P)]=0\in C(\mathbb{Z}Q_{12})
.

Let
N=\Ker(f)
and let
H=f^{-1}(Q_{12})
which is a subgroup of

for which
N\unlhd H\leq G
and
Q_{12}=H/N
. By
[
Nic21b
, Lemma 7.10]
, we have a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathbb{Z}G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathbb{Z}\widetilde{O})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathbb{Z}H)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathbb{Z}Q_{12})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\#}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Res}^{G}_{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Res}^{\widetilde{O}}_{Q_{12}}}

It follows from general properties of finiteness obstructions

that, if

5.
The Eichler mass formula

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and let
\Lambda
be an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-order in a finite-dimensional semi-simple
K
-algebra
A
.
It is a standard fact (see, for example,
[
Swa80
, Lemma 2.1]
) that, if
M
is a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module, then
M
is projective if and only if
M
is locally projective, i.e. for all
p
prime,
M_{p}=M\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is projective over
\Lambda_{p}=\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
where
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the
p
-adic integers.

In the case where
K=\mathbb{Q}
,
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}G
and
A=\mathbb{Q}G
for
G
a finite group, then
M
projective implies that
M_{p}
is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}G
-module for all
p
prime
[
Swa70
, Theorem 2.21, 4.2]
. In particular, in this case,
M
is projective if and only if
M
is locally free.

Define the
locally free class group

C(\Lambda)
to be the equivalence classes of locally free modules up to the relation
P\simeq Q
if
P\oplus\Lambda^{i}\cong Q\oplus\Lambda^{j}
for some
i,j\geq 0
. By abuse of notation, we write
[P]
to denote both the class
[P]\in C(\Lambda)
and, where convenient, the set of isomorphism classes of projective modules
P_{0}
where
[P_{0}]=[P]
.

Define the
class set

\Cls\Lambda
as the set of isomorphism classes of rank one locally free
\Lambda
-modules, which is finite by the Jordan-Zassenhaus theorem
[
CR81
, Section 24]
.
Equivalently, this is the set of locally principal fractional
\Lambda
-ideals, under the relation
I\sim J
if there exists
\alpha\in A^{\times}
such that
I=\alpha J
(see
[
SV19
]
). This comes with the stable class map

[\,\cdot\,]_{\Lambda}:\Cls\Lambda\to C(\Lambda)

which sends
P\mapsto[P]
and is surjective since every locally free
\Lambda
-module
P
is of the form
P_{0}\oplus\Lambda^{i}
where
P_{0}\in\Cls\Lambda
and
i\geq 0

[
Frö75
]
. Define
\Cls^{[P]}(\Lambda)
to be
[\,\cdot\,]_{\Lambda}^{-1}([P])
, i.e. the rank one locally free modules in
[P]
, and let
\SF(\Lambda)
be
\Cls^{[\Lambda]}(\Lambda)
, i.e. the set of rank one stably free modules.

We say that
\Lambda
has
locally free cancellation
if
P\oplus\Lambda\cong Q\oplus\Lambda
implies
P\cong Q
for all locally free
\Lambda
-modules
P
and
Q
. It follows from the discussion above that
\Lambda
has locally free cancellation if and only if
[\,\cdot\,]_{\Lambda}
is bijective, i.e.
\#\Cls\Lambda=\#C(\Lambda)
.

Similarly we say that
\Lambda
has
stably free cancellation
when
P\oplus\Lambda^{i}\cong\Lambda^{j}
implies that
P\cong\Lambda^{j-i}
, or equivalently, if
\#\SF(\Lambda)=1
.

If
X\subseteq\Cls\Lambda
, then we can define the
mass
of
X
to be

\mass(X)=\sum_{I\in X}\frac{1}{[O_{L}(I)^{\times}:\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}]}.

Recall that a quaternion algebra
A
over
K
is
totally definite
if
A
is ramified over all archimedean places
\nu
, i.e.
A\otimes K_{\nu}
is a division algebra over
K_{\nu}
. Note that every complex place
\nu
splits since the only quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{C}
is
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. In particular, if
A
is totally definite, then
K
must be a totally real field.

Let
\zeta_{K}(s)
be the Dedekind zeta function, let
h_{K}=|C(\mathcal{O}_{K})|
be the class number of
K
and let
\Delta_{K}
be the discriminant of
K
. The following was proven in
[
Eic37
]
.

Theorem 5.1

(Eichler mass formula)
.

Let
A
be a totally definite quaternion algebra over
K
and let
\Lambda
be a maximal
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-order in
A
. If
n=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
, then

\mass(\Cls\Lambda)=\frac{2\zeta_{K}(2)}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\cdot|\Delta_{K}|^{3/2}\cdot h_{K}\cdot\hskip-5.69054pt\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\disc(A)}(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})-1).

The following was first shown by Vignéras in
[
Vig76
]
, though a simplified proof can be found in
[
SV19
, Theorem 5.11]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
A
be a totally definite quaternion algebra over
K
and let
\Lambda
be a maximal
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-order in
A
. If
P
,
Q
are locally free
\Lambda
-modules, then

\mass(\Cls^{[P]}(\Lambda))=\mass(\Cls^{[Q]}(\Lambda)).

In particular, this implies that

\mass(\Cls^{[P]}(\Lambda))=\frac{\mass(\Cls\Lambda)}{|C(\Lambda)|}
,
where
C(\Lambda)
denotes the class group of locally free
\Lambda
-modules.

It was shown by Eichler that

|C(\Lambda)|=h_{K}\cdot[(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{+}:(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{2}]

where
(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{+}
denotes the group of totally positive units, i.e. those units
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
for which
\sigma(u)>0
for all embeddings
\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
The following can be shown using the results above as well as lower bounds on
|\Delta_{K}|^{1/[K:\mathbb{Q}]}
in terms of
[K:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
A
be a totally definite quaternion algebra over
K
and let
\Lambda
be a maximal
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-order in
A
.
If
\Lambda
has stably free cancellation, then
[K:\mathbb{Q}]\leq 6
.

Remark 5.4
.

This was proven by Hallouin-Maire
[
HM06
, Theorem 1]
, though it is worth noting that part of their result was incorrect as stated (see
[
Sme15
]
).

In the notation of
[
Swa83
, Section 3]
, define the Eichler constant

\ei_{K}=\frac{2\zeta_{K}(2)|\Delta_{K}|^{3/2}}{(2\pi)^{2d}}=\frac{(-1)^{d}\zeta_{K}(-1)}{2^{d-1}}\in\mathbb{Q}

where
d=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
and where the second equality comes from the functional equation for
\zeta_{K}(s)
. This is rational since
\zeta_{K}(-1)\in\mathbb{Q}
.
Another constraint on the fields
K
over which stably free cancellation can occur is as follows.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
A
be a totally definite quaternion algebra over
K
and let
\Lambda
be a maximal
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-order in
A
.
If
\Lambda
has stably free cancellation, then the numerator of
\zeta_{K}(-1)
(or, equivalently,
\ei_{K}
) is a power of
2
.

Proof.

If
\Lambda
has stably free cancellation, then
\mass(\SF(\Lambda))=[\Lambda^{\times}:\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}]^{-1}
since
\Lambda
a maximal order implies
O_{L}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
, i.e. the numerator is
1
. By Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
, we also have that

\mass(\SF(\Lambda))=\frac{\ei_{K}}{[(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{+}:(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{2}]}\cdot\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\disc(A)}(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})-1).

Note that
[(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{+}:(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{2}]
is a power of

since
(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{2}\subseteq(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
and
[(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{\times}:(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{2}]=2^{d}
by Dirichlet’s unit theorem.
Since
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathbb{Z}
, this implies that the numerator of
\ei_{K}
, or equivalently
\zeta_{K}(-1)
, is a power of
2
. ∎

6.
Orders in quaternionic components of
\mathbb{Q}G

Recall that, for a finite group
G
, the rational group ring
\mathbb{Q}G
is semisimple and so admits a decomposition into simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras. For the quaternion groups of order
4n\geq 8
, we have

\mathbb{Q}Q_{4n}\cong\mathbb{Q}Q_{4n}^{\text{ab}}\times\prod_{d\geq 3,d\mid n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1}))\times\prod_{d\geq 3,d\nmid n,\,d\mid 2n}\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{d},j]

and

\mathbb{Q}Q_{4n}^{\text{ab}}\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q}C_{4}\cong\mathbb{Q}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{Q}(i),&\text{if $n$ is odd}\\
\mathbb{Q}C_{2}^{2}\cong\mathbb{Q}^{4},&\text{if $n$ is even}\end{cases}

where
Q_{4n}^{\text{ab}}
denotes the abelianisation of
Q_{4n}
, where
\zeta_{d}=e^{2\pi i/d}\in\mathbb{C}
is a primitive
d
th root of unity, and
\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{d},j]\subseteq\mathbb{H}
sits inside the real quaternions.
This is stated on
[
Swa83
, p75]
though a more detailed proof can be found in
[
Joh03
, p48-51]
. In order to apply the results of Section
5
, it will be helpful to note that

\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{n},j]\cong\left(\frac{(\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{n}^{-1})^{2},-1}{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}+\zeta_{n}^{-1})}\right).

This says that
\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{n},j]
is the quaternion algebra with centre
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}+\zeta_{n}^{-1})
and which is a free
K
-algebra of rank 4 with basis
\{1,\alpha,\beta,\alpha\beta\}
where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{n},j]
are such that
\alpha^{2}=(\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{n}^{-1})^{2}
and
\beta^{2}=-1
.
Further details, as well as a proof, can be found in
[
Joh03
, p51]
.
It is straightforward to check that
\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{n},j]
is totally definite for
n\geq 3
(see, for example,
[
Swa83
, Lemma 4.3]
).

If
n_{i}
are distinct positive integers such that
n_{i}\nmid n
and
n_{i}\mid 2n
for
1\leq i\leq k
, then define
\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}
to be the image of
\mathbb{Z}Q_{4n}
under the projection of
\mathbb{Q}Q_{4n}
onto
A_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{n_{i}},j]
. For example,
\mathbb{Z}Q_{4n}
projects onto
\Lambda_{2n}=\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{2n},j]
for all
n\geq 2
.

Fix the standard presentation
Q_{4n}=\langle x,y\mid x^{n}=y^{2},yxy^{-1}=x^{-1}\rangle
and recall that, for
n\geq 3
, we have

\Aut(Q_{4n})=\{\theta_{a,b}:a\in(\mathbb{Z}/{2n})^{\times},b\in\mathbb{Z}/{2n}\}

where
\theta_{a,b}(x)=x^{a}
and
\theta_{a,b}(y)=x^{b}y
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
4n\geq 12
. If
n_{i}
are distinct positive integers such that
n_{i}\nmid n
and
n_{i}\mid 2n
for
1\leq i\leq k
, then the map
f:\mathbb{Z}Q_{4n}\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}
induces a map

f_{*}:\Aut(Q_{4n})\to\Aut(\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}).

Proof.

It follows from
[
Joh03
, p48-51]
that the map

f:\mathbb{Q}Q_{4n}\twoheadrightarrow A_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}

is given by
x\mapsto(\zeta_{n_{1}},\cdots,\zeta_{n_{k}})
,
y\mapsto(j,\cdots,j)
. If
\bar{\zeta}=(\zeta_{n_{1}},\cdots,\zeta_{n_{k}})
and
\bar{j}=(j,\cdots,j)
, then
\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}=\langle\bar{\zeta},\bar{j}\rangle
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-order in
A_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}
. For
a\in(\mathbb{Z}/{2n})^{\times}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}/{2n}
, define

\bar{\theta}_{a,b}:\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}\to\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}

\bar{\zeta}\mapsto\bar{\zeta}^{a}=(\zeta_{n_{1}}^{a},\cdots,\zeta_{n_{k}}^{a}),\quad\bar{j}\mapsto\bar{\zeta}^{b}\bar{j}=(\zeta_{n_{1}}^{b}j,\cdots,\zeta_{n_{k}}^{b}j)

which we can extend to be a ring homomorphism. Hence
\bar{\theta}_{a,b}\in\Aut(\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}})
and it is easy to see that
f\circ\theta_{a,b}=\bar{\theta}_{a,b}\circ f
. This implies that there is an induced map
f_{*}:\Aut(Q_{4n})\to\Aut(\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}})
where
f_{*}(\theta_{a,b})=\bar{\theta}_{a,b}
. ∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
and suppose
\psi\in\mathbb{Z}G
is such that
\mathbb{Z}G/(\psi)
is torsion-free and
(\varepsilon(\psi),r)=1
. If
f:\mathbb{Z}G\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}G/(\psi)
, then

f_{\#}((I,r))\cong\mathbb{Z}G/(\psi).

Proof.

First consider the exact sequence of
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules

0\to(I,r)\xrightarrow{i}\mathbb{Z}G\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}\to 0.

If
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}G/(\psi)
, then we can apply
\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}-
to the above sequence to get

0\to\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}(I,r)\xrightarrow{1\otimes i}\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}\mathbb{Z}G\xrightarrow{1\otimes\varepsilon}\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})\to 0

which is exact since
\Tor_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}G}(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z},\Lambda)=0
since
\Lambda
is torsion-free. Since
f_{\#}((I,r))\cong\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}(I,r)
and
\Lambda\cong\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}\mathbb{Z}G
, it suffices to show that
1\otimes i
is an isomorphism, i.e. that
\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})=0
.
Now
\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})
and the associativity of tensor product implies that

\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})\cong(\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/\varepsilon(\psi))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})=0

since
r
and
\varepsilon(\psi)
are coprime. ∎

The following is an extension of Theorem
3.3
to this setting.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}Q_{4n}\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}
where

and
n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}
are distinct positive integers such that

and

. If

acts on

on

.

Proof.

Let
n_{1},\cdots,n_{t}
be the set of all

such that

and

, where

. It follows from

that

. In particular,

For the rest of this section, we will consider the cancellation problem for orders of the form

. We begin by considering the case

.

6.1.
Cancellation for quaternionic orders

First note that
\Lambda_{2n}=\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{2n},j]
is a
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-order in the quaternion algebra
\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{2n},j]
with centre
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2n}+\zeta_{2n}^{-1})
. We can therefore apply the results in Section
5
to get:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
2n=16,22
or
2n\geq 26
with
2n\neq 30,42
.
Then
\Lambda_{2n}
does not have stably free cancellation.

Proof.

If
\Lambda_{2n}
has stably free cancellation, then so does
\Gamma_{2n}
where
\Lambda_{2n}\subseteq\Gamma_{2n}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{2n},j]
is a maximal order by
[
Swa83
, Theorem A10]
. Since
[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2n}+\zeta_{2n}^{-1}):\mathbb{Q}]=\frac{1}{2}\varphi(2n)
, it follows from Theorem
5.3
that
\varphi(2n)\leq 12
, i.e. if
2n\leq 30
or
2n=36,42
. It remains to consider the cases
2n=16,22,26,28,36
. If
\ei_{n}=\ei_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}+\zeta_{n}^{-1})}
, then Proposition
5.5
implies that the numerator of
\ei_{2n}
is a power of
2
for
2n=16,22,26,28,36
. However, by
[
Swa83
, Table II]
, we have that

\ei_{16}=\frac{5}{48},\quad\ei_{22}=\frac{5}{132},\quad\ei_{26}=\frac{19}{156},\quad\ei_{28}=\frac{13}{21},\quad\ei_{36}=\frac{31}{36}

which is a contradiction. ∎

Remark 6.5
.

Theorem
5.3
and Proposition
5.5
do not characterise which orders have stably free cancellation, even among the
\Lambda_{2n}
. For example,
\Lambda_{42}
has
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=6
,
\ei_{K}=\frac{1}{6}

[
Swa83
, Table II]
but does not have stably free cancellation
[
Swa83
, p88]
.

It is possible to show that
\Lambda_{2n}
has stably free cancellation in all other cases.
It can be shown using
[
SV19
, Table 2]
that
\Lambda_{2n}
has cancellation in all classes for all remaining cases other than
2n=20,24
. The cases
2n=20,24
can then be dealt with either using a MAGMA program, or by explicitly computing
\mass(\SF(\Lambda_{2n}))
and showing it is equal to
[\Lambda^{\times}:\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}]^{-1}
.

We also note the following bounds which we will use in the proof of Theorem
B
.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2n}+\zeta_{2n}^{-1})
.
If
P\in\Cls(\Lambda_{2n})
, then

\#\Cls^{[P]}(\Lambda_{2n})\geq\frac{2|\Delta_{K}|^{3/2}}{2^{t_{2n}}(2\pi)^{\varphi(2n)}}\geq e^{\frac{3}{8}\varphi(n)\log n+O(n\log\log n)}

where
t_{2n}=\ord_{2}([(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{+}:(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{2}])
.

Proof.

To get the first inequality, note that
\#\Cls^{[P]}(\Lambda_{2n})\geq\mass(\Cls^{[P]}(\Lambda_{2n}))
. We can then apply Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
and note that
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})\geq 2
for all
\mathfrak{p}\mid\disc(\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{2n},j])
to get that:

\mass(\Cls^{[P]}(\Lambda_{2n}))\geq\frac{2\zeta_{K}(2)|\Delta_{K}|^{3/2}}{(2\pi)^{\varphi(2n)}\cdot[(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{+}:(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{2}]}\geq\frac{2|\Delta_{K}|^{3/2}}{2^{t_{2n}}(2\pi)^{\varphi(2n)}}

where we note that
\zeta_{K}(2)\geq 1
by the Euler product formula.

Recall that
(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
and so
t_{2n}\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]=\varphi(2n)
. In order to compute
\Delta_{K}=\Delta_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
, we will use that

\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2n})/\mathbb{Q}}=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2n})/K})\Delta_{K/\mathbb{Q}}^{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2n}):K]}.

Since
\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2n})/K}=(\zeta_{2n}-\zeta_{2n}^{-1})^{2}
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2n})/\mathbb{Q}}=\left(2n\prod_{p\mid 2n}p^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\right)^{\varphi(2n)}
, we get that

\Delta_{K}=c_{2n}^{-1}\left(2n\prod_{p\mid 2n}p^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\varphi(2n)}

where
c_{2n}=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{2n}-\zeta_{2n}^{-1})
which has
c_{2n}=2
if
n=2^{k}
,
c_{2n}=\sqrt{p}
if
n=p^{k}
for
p
an odd prime and
c_{2n}=1
otherwise. If
B
denotes the bound above, we have that:

\displaystyle\textstyle\log B

\displaystyle\geq\textstyle\frac{3}{4}\varphi(2n)(\log 2n-\sum_{p\mid 2n}\frac{\log p}{p-1})-\frac{3}{2}\log c_{n}-\varphi(2n)\log 4\pi-\log 2

\displaystyle\geq\textstyle\frac{3}{4}\varphi(2n)\log 2n+O(n\log\log n)

since
c_{2n}\leq n
and by using that
\sum_{p\mid 2n}\frac{\log p}{p-1}=O(\log\log n)
by
[
Swa83
, Lemma 2.7]
. This makes sense since
\varphi(n)\geq O(n/\log\log n)
by standard results and so

\varphi(2n)\log 2n\geq O(n\log n/\log\log n)\geq O(n\log\log n)

since
\log n/(\log\log n)^{2}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. We note finally that
\varphi(2n)\geq\frac{1}{2}\varphi(n)
and
\log 2n\geq\log n
to get the desired inequality.
∎

6.2.
Cancellation for higher quaternionic orders

We will now consider the case
k\geq 2
. Firstly note that, for positive integers
n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}
, there exists
n\geq 1
such that
n_{i}\nmid n
,
n_{i}\mid 2n
if and only if
\nu_{2}(n_{i})=r
for all
i
and some
r\geq 1
, i.e. for all
i
,
n_{i}=2^{r}m_{i}
for some
m_{i}
odd. From the definition, it is possible to show that

\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}\cong\mathbb{Z}Q_{4n}/(\Phi_{n_{1}}(x)\,\Phi_{n_{2}}(x)\,\cdots\,\Phi_{n_{k}}(x))

where
\Phi_{n}(x)
denotes the
n
th cyclotomic polynomial. It follows that, if
S\subseteq\{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}\}
, then there is a quotient
\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda_{S}
. By
[
Swa83
, Theorem A10]
this implies that, if
\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}
has stably free cancellation, then
\Lambda_{S}
has stably free cancellation for all
S\subseteq\{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}\}
.

In particular, by Lemma
6.4
, we have that
\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}
does not have stably free cancellation except possibly if, for all
i
, we have
n_{i}\leq 14
or
n_{i}=18,20,24,30
. By the discussion above, this is equivalent to

\{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}\}\subseteq\begin{cases}\{2,6,10,14,18,30\},&\text{if $r=1$}\\
\{4,12,20\},&\text{if $r=2$}\\
\{8,24\}&\text{if $r=3$}\end{cases}

where we note that the cases with
r\geq 4
do not arise.

For a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, define a graph
\mathcal{G}(S)
with vertices the elements of
S
and where
a,b\in S
are connected by an edge if
a/b=p^{r}
for some
p
prime and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
r\geq 1
and let
n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}
be such that
\nu_{2}(n_{i})=r
for all
i
. If
\mathcal{G}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{k})
is not connected, then there exists a splitting
\{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}\}=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}
with
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
non-empty such that
\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}\cong\Lambda_{S_{1}}\times\Lambda_{S_{2}}
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{G}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{k})
is not connected then, by definition, there exists a splitting
\{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}\}=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}
with
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
non-empty and such that there are no edges between any
a\in S_{1}
and
b\in S_{2}
. Now consider
\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}=\mathbb{Z}Q_{4n}/(\Phi_{n_{1}}\,\cdots\,\Phi_{n_{k}})
where, for example,
n=\frac{1}{2}\text{lcm}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{k})
.

If
a\in S_{1}
and
b\in S_{2}
, then
a/b\neq p^{r}
for all

and so
(\Phi_{a},\Phi_{b})=1
by results of Diederichsen
[

]
(see also
[
Swa83
, Theorem B1]
). In particular, this implies that

\textstyle(\prod_{n_{i}\in S_{1}}\Phi_{n_{i}},\prod_{n_{i}\in S_{2}}\Phi_{n_{i}})=1

and so
\mathbb{Z}Q_{4n}/(\Phi_{n_{1}}\,\cdots\,\Phi_{n_{k}})\cong\mathbb{Z}Q_{4n}/(\prod_{n_{i}\in S_{1}}\Phi_{n_{i}})\times\mathbb{Z}Q_{4n}/(\prod_{n_{i}\in S_{2}}\Phi_{n_{i}})
which completes the proof since
\Lambda_{S_{j}}\cong\mathbb{Z}Q_{4n}/\prod_{n_{i}\in S_{j}}\Phi_{n_{i}}
for

.
∎

Note that, if
\Lambda_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}\cong\Lambda_{S_{1}}\times\Lambda_{S_{2}}

has stably free cancellation if and only if

and

The following cases were calculated by Swan in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}[j]}

is prime and

, then

N(Q_{4p},n)\geq e^{\frac{3}{8}p\log p+O(p\log\log p)}\geq e^{\frac{3}{4}m\log m+O(m\log\log m)}.

9.
Applications to group presentations

We will adopt the convention that a finite presentation
\mathcal{P}=\langle x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\mid r_{1}=r_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,r_{m}=r_{m}^{\prime}\rangle
, defined in terms of relations, is identified with the presentation
\mathcal{P}=\langle x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\mid r_{1}(r_{1}^{\prime})^{-1},\cdots,r_{m}(r_{m}^{\prime})^{-1}\rangle
defined in terms of relators. This allows us to associate to
\mathcal{P}
a canonical presentation complex
X_{\mathcal{P}}
. We say that two presentations
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are
homotopy equivalent
, written
\mathcal{P}\simeq\mathcal{Q}
, if
X_{\mathcal{P}}\simeq X_{\mathcal{Q}}
are homotopy equivalent as spaces.

Given a finite presentation
\mathcal{P}=\langle x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\mid r_{1},\cdots,r_{m}\rangle
for a group
G
, we can construct another presentation for
G
by applying one of the following operations to
\mathcal{P}
:

(A)

Replace a relator
r_{i}
by
\omega r_{i}\omega^{-1}
for some word
\omega\in F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
. (
Conjugation
)

(B)

For
i\neq j
, replace a relator
r_{i}
by
r_{i}r_{j}
. (
Right-multiplication
)

(C)

Replace a relator
r_{i}
by
r_{i}^{-1}
. (
Inversion
)

The operations (A)-(C) are known as
Q
-transformations
and we say that two presentations
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are
Q
-equivalent
, written
\mathcal{P}\simeq_{Q}\mathcal{Q}
if they are related by a sequence of
Q
-transformations.
The following are consequences of the operations (A)-(C) and so induce
Q
-equivalences
[
Met76
, Footnote 1]
:

(D)

For
i\neq j
, replace a relator
r_{i}=a\cdot b
with
a\cdot r_{j}\cdot b
for words
a,b\in F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
(
Insertion
)

(E)

For
i\neq j
, replace
r_{i}
with
r_{j}
and
r_{j}
with
r_{i}
(
Permutation
)

If
\mathcal{P}\simeq_{Q}\mathcal{Q}
, then
\mathcal{P}\simeq\mathcal{Q}

[
Met76
, p8]
. Finding
Q
-equivalences between presentations is therefore a convenient way to prove that two presentations are homotopy equivalent. Note that the converse is far from true and there are many intermediate equivalence relations (see
[
HAMS93
, Chapters III & XII]
).

Remark 9.1
.

We previously defined the homotopy type of a presentation defined by relations
r_{i}=r_{i}^{\prime}
by first viewing it as a presentation defined by relators
r_{i}(r_{i}^{\prime})^{-1}
. One nice consequence of the discussion above is that the resulting homotopy type is independent of the part of the word we ‘cut’ the equality by to turn it into a relator since different choices are related by operations of type (A) and (C).

Fix
n\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{P}_{\std}=\langle x,y\mid x^{n}=y^{2},y^{-1}xy=x^{-1}\rangle
denote the standard presentation for
Q_{4n}
. For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider the presentations

\mathcal{P}_{\MP}^{r}=\langle x,y\mid x^{n}=y^{2},y^{-1}xyx^{r-1}=x^{r}y^{-1}x^{2}y\rangle

which were mentioned in Section
1.2
of the introduction.
We will now prove the following, which was claimed in the introduction.
Note that, in statement (ii), there is no assumption that
\mathcal{P}_{\MP}^{r}
is a presentation for
Q_{4n}
.

Proposition 9.2
.

(i)

If
r=0
or
1
, then
\mathcal{P}_{\MP}^{r}\simeq\mathcal{P}_{\std}
.

(ii)

If
r\equiv s\,\,\text{mod}\,n
, then
\mathcal{P}_{\MP}^{r}\simeq\mathcal{P}_{\MP}^{s}
.

Proof.

In each case, we will show that the pairs of presentations are
Q
-equivalent.

(i) If
r=0
, the second relator becomes
(y^{-1}xyx^{-1})(y^{-1}x^{2}y)^{-1}
. After conjugation, this becomes
yx^{-1}y^{-1}x^{-1}
which simplifies to
yxy^{-1}x
after inversion and further conjugation. The case
r=1
is similar.

(ii) For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, we will show that
\mathcal{P}_{\MP}^{r}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\MP}^{r+n}
are
Q
-equivalent. The second relator of
\mathcal{P}_{\MP}^{r}
is of the form
ax^{r}bx^{-r}
for some
a,b\in F(x,y)
. Using insertion, we can replace this with
ax^{r+n}y^{-2}bx^{-r}
. By insertions and inversions, we can replace
y^{-2}
with
x^{n}
and vice versa. By doing this, we can move
y^{-2}
past
b
to put the second relator in the form
ax^{r+n}by^{-2}x^{-r}
. Finally, we apply insertion and inversion once more to replace this with
ax^{r+n}bx^{-(r+n)}
which is the second relator of
\mathcal{P}_{\MP}^{r+n}
.
∎

The remainder of this section will be devoted to the proof of Corollary
C
. We will begin with the following, which is a simple counting argument. Recall that an
(n,m)
-presentation
is a presentation with
n
generators and
m
relators.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
t\geq 1
be an integer. Then:

\#\{\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{P}\text{ a $(2,2)$-presentation, $\ell(\mathcal{P})\leq t$}\}\leq e^{6t}.

Proof.

Each presentation
\mathcal{P}
has the form

\mathcal{P}=\langle x,y\mid r,s\rangle
for
r,s\in F_{2}=F(x,y)
.
Hence we have

\displaystyle\#\{\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{P}\text{ a $(2,2)$-presentation, $\ell(\mathcal{P})\leq t$}\}=\#\{(r,s)\in F_{2}^{2}:|r|+|s|\leq t\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{t}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\#\{(r,s)\in F_{2}^{2}:|r|=j,|s|=i-j\}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{t}\sum_{j=0}^{i}4^{j}4^{i-j}\leq(t+1)^{2}4^{t}

where we used that
\#\{r\in F_{2}:|r|=k\}\leq 4^{k}
since each letter is of the form
x,x^{-1},y,y^{-1}
.

Finally, note that
\log((t+1)^{2}4^{t})=t\log 4+2\log(t+1)\leq t(\log 4+4)\leq 6t
.
∎

We will now prove the following which implies Corollary
C
by letting
f(n)=n
be the constant function and letting
G=Q_{4n}
so that
m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)=\lfloor n/2\rfloor
.

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
\lambda>0
and let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a function for which
n\log n/(f(n)\log\log n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Then there exists a constant
N_{\lambda}
such that, if
G
has
4
-periodic cohomology and
m=m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)>N_{\lambda}
, then at least one of the following holds:

(i)

G
does not have the
D2
property.

(ii)

G
has a balanced presentation which is not homotopy equivalent to a
(2,2)
-presentation
\mathcal{P}
with
\ell(\mathcal{P})\leq\lambda f(m)
.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>0
and a function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with the desired property.
Let
G
have 4-periodic cohomology and suppose
G
does not satisfy conditions (i) or (ii). It suffices to show that
m=m_{\mathbb{H}}(G)
is bounded

Since
G
does not satisfy (i), it has the D2 property. By
[
Nic21b
, p6532]
, this implies that
\Def(G)=0
and so
N(G,2)
is the number of balanced presentations up to homotopy equivalence. On the other hand, since
G
does not satisfy (ii), every balanced presentation is homotopy equivalent to a
(2,2)
-presentation
\mathcal{P}
with
\ell(\mathcal{P})\leq\lambda f(m)
. This implies that

N(G,2)\leq\#\{\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{P}\text{ a $(2,2)$-presentation, $\ell(\mathcal{P})\leq\lambda f(m)$}\}\leq e^{6\lambda f(m)}

where the second inequality is by Lemma
9.3
.

Since
G
has the D2 propery, Theorem

implies that there is a constant
C>0
for which

N(G,2)\geq e^{C\tfrac{m\log m}{\log\log m}}

and so
m\log m/(f(m)\log\log m)\leq 6\lambda/C
. By assumption, we have that
n\log n/(f(n)\log\log n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Since

is constant, this implies that

is bounded.
∎

10.
Examples

Let
G
have
k
-periodic cohomology, let
n=ik
or
ik-2
and let
P_{(G,n)}\in P(\mathbb{Z}G)
be such that
[P_{(G,n)}]=\sigma_{ik}(G)\in C(\mathbb{Z}G)/T_{G}
. The classes
[P_{(G,n)}]\in C(\mathbb{Z}G)
have two special properties. Firstly:

(P1)

If

, then
[(P_{(G,n)})_{\theta}]=[P_{(G,n)}]\in C(\mathbb{Z}G)/T_{G}
.

This was necessary in order to define our action of
\Aut(G)
on the class
[P_{(G,n)}]
since
\theta\in\Aut(G)
sends
P\mapsto(I,\psi(\theta))\otimes P_{\theta}
for

of rank one and some
\psi:\Aut(G)\to(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
. It is well-defined since

[(I,\psi(\theta))\otimes P_{\theta}]=[(I,\psi(\theta))]+[P_{\theta}]=[P_{(G,n)}],

i.e.
[P_{(G,n)}]-[(P_{(G,n)})_{\theta}]\in T_{G}
. The second property follows from Theorem
A
:

(P2)

[P_{(G,n)}]
has cancellation if and only if
[P_{(G,n)}]/\Aut(G)
has cancellation.

The aim of this section will be to give examples to show that both properties are false for general projective modules over groups with periodic cohomology.

Lemma 10.7

Lemma 10.8

By

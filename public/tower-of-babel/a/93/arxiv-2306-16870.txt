The aggregation-diffusion equation with the intermediate exponent
Shen Bian, Jiale Bu

Abstract
We consider a Keller-Segel model with non-linear porous medium type diffusion and nonlocal attractive power law interaction, focusing on potentials that are less singular than Newtonian interaction. Here, the nonlinear diffusion is chosen to be 2 ​ d d + 2 ​ s < m < 2 − 2 ​ s d \frac{2d}{d+2s}<m<2-\frac{2s}{d} in which case the steady states are compactly supported. We analyse under which conditions on the initial data the regime that attractive forces are stronger than diffusion occurs and classify the global existence and finite time blow-up of solutions. It is shown that there is a threshold value which is characterized by the optimal constant of a variant of Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality such that the solution will exist globally if the initial data is below the threshold, while the solution blows up in finite time when the initial data is above the threshold.

Introduction

The present paper concerns the following aggregation-diffusion equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta u^{m}-\nabla\cdot\left(u\nabla c\right),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{array}\right.

Here
d\geq 3
and the diffusion exponent
m>1
.
u
represents the density of cells, the chemoattractant
c
is governed by a fractional diffusion process and can be expressed by the convolution of
u(x,t)
with Riesz potential, that’s

\displaystyle c(x,t)=(-\Delta)^{-s}u=\mathcal{K}\ast u=c_{d,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy,\quad c_{d,s}=\frac{\Gamma(d/2-s)}{\pi^{d/2}4^{s}\Gamma(s)},

(3)

where the potential is less singular than Newtonian interaction, i.e.

\displaystyle 2<2s<d.

(4)

The parabolic equation on
u
gives nonnegative solutions
u(x,t)\geq 0,c(x,t)\geq 0
. Another property we will use is the conservation of the total number of cells

\displaystyle M:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u_{0}(x)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x,t)dx.

(5)

Initial data will be assumed throughout this paper

\displaystyle u_{0}\in L_{+}^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),~~\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x|^{2}u_{0}(x)dx<\infty,~~\nabla u_{0}^{m}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

(6)

The nonlinear diffusion
m>1
models the local repulsion of cells while the attractive nonlocal term models cell movement toward chemotactic sources or attractive interaction between cells due to cell adhesion
[
8
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
23
,
24
]
. The main characteristic of equation (
1
) is the competition between the diffusion and the nonlocal aggregation which may lead to finite-time blow-up or global existence. This is well presented by the free energy

\displaystyle F(u)=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u^{m}dx-\frac{c_{d,s}}{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(x,t)u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy.

(7)

As a matter of fact, (
1
) can be recast as

\displaystyle u_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(u\nabla\mu\right),

(8)

where
\mu
is the chemical potential

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{m}{m-1}u^{m-1}-c.

(9)

Multiplying (
8
) by
\mu
and integrating it in space yield

\displaystyle\frac{dF(u)}{dt}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u|\nabla\mu|^{2}dx=0.

(10)

This implies that
F[u(\cdot,t)]
is non-increasing with respect to time.

Let us mention that equation (
1
) possesses a scaling invariance which leaves the
L^{p}
norm invariant where

\displaystyle p:=\frac{d(2-m)}{2s}

(11)

and produces a balance between the diffusion and the aggregation terms. Indeed, if
u(x,t)
is a solution to (
1
), then
u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{\frac{2s}{2-m}}u\left(\lambda x,\lambda^{2+\frac{2s(m-1)}{2-m}}t\right)
is also a solution to (
1
) and this scaling preserves the
L^{p}
norm
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.
The exponent
m=2-2s/d
produces a balance in the mass invariant scaling
u_{\lambda}=\lambda^{d}u\left(\lambda x,\lambda^{d+2-2s}t\right)
of the diffusion term

\lambda^{dm+2}\Delta u_{\lambda}^{m}
and the aggregation term
\lambda^{2d+2-2s}\nabla\cdot\left(u_{\lambda}\nabla\mathcal{K}\ast u_{\lambda}\right)
. Under the mass invariant scaling, we have that for the subcritical case
m>2-2s/d
, the aggregation dominates the diffusion for low density and prevents spreading. While for high density, the diffusion dominates the aggregation thus blow-up is precluded. On the contrary, for the supercritical case
1<m<2-2s/d,
the aggregation dominates the diffusion for high density (large
\lambda
) and the density may have finite-time blow-up. While for low density (small
\lambda
), the diffusion dominates the aggregation and the density has infinite-time spreading. These behaviors also appear in many other physical systems such as thin film, Hele-Shaw, stellar collapse as well as the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
[
7
,
15
,
28
,
29
]
.

Our main goal in this paper is to classify the global existence and finite time blow-up of solutions by the initial data for the supercritical case
1<m<2-2s/d
. The main tool for the analysis of (
1
) is a variant of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, see Section

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy\leq C_{*}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}}\|f\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}}.

(12)

The identification of the equality case in (

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{W(x)W(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy=C_{*}\|W\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}}\|W\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}}.

Moreover, Lemma

Here

In the case

For

Existence criterion

Following

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=\Delta u^{m}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\Delta u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\cdot\left(u_{\varepsilon}\nabla c_{\varepsilon}\right),~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~t>0,\\

A variant to the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality

In this section, we are going to show the existence of the extremal function for the modified HLS inequality (
12
) which will be used to prove dynamical behaviors of solutions to (
1
). Here several steps are required to the proof which borrows some arguments from
[
4
]
. Firstly, we establish a variant to the HLS inequality.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\frac{2d}{d+2s}<m<2-\frac{2s}{d}.
For
u\in L_{+}^{1}\cap L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),
we put

\displaystyle h(u):=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy.

(65)

Then

\displaystyle C_{*}:=\sup_{u\neq 0}\frac{h(u)}{\|u\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}}\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}}}<\infty.

(66)

Proof.

Applying the HLS inequality (
40
) with
f=h=u
and
\beta=d-2s
we obtain

\displaystyle h(u)=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy\leq C_{dsm}\|u\|_{\frac{2d}{d+2s}}^{2}\leq C_{dsm}\|u\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}}\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}},

(67)

where we have used Hölder’s inequality with

1<\frac{2d}{d+2s}<m
. Consequently,
C_{*}\leq C_{dsm}
.
\Box

We next turn to the existence of maximisers for the VHLS inequality which can be proved by similar arguments as in
[
4
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 8
.

(The existence of
C_{*}
)  Let
\frac{2d}{d+2s}<m<2-\frac{2s}{d}
. There exists a non-negative, radially symmetric and non-increasing function
W\in L^{1}\cap L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

h(W)=C_{*}

with the normalization
\|W\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\|W\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=1.
In addition, there is
R>0
such that
W
solves the following Euler-Lagrange equation

\displaystyle\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}C_{*}W^{m-1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{W(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy-\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}C_{*}suppW,&|x|<R,\\
0,&|x|>R.\end{array}\right.

Moreover,
R<\infty
for
m>\frac{2d}{d+2s}.

Proof.

For simplicity, we will use
\|u\|_{r}=\|u\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
throughout this proof. Setting

\displaystyle J(u):=\frac{h(u)}{\|u\|_{1}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}}\|u\|_{m}^{\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}}},~~u\in L^{1}\cap L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

we consider a maximizing sequence
\{u_{j}\}\in L^{1}\cap L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}J(u_{j})=C_{*}.

Firstly, we prove that the maximizing sequence
\{u_{j}\}
can be assumed to be non-negative, radially symmetric and non-increasing such that
\|u_{j}\|_{1}=\|u_{j}\|_{m}=1
. The
second step will show that the supremum can be achieved, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}u_{j}=W,\quad J(W)=C_{*}

with
\|W\|_{1}=\|W\|_{m}=1.
Finally, the Euler-Lagrange equation solved by
W
is obtained.

Step 1
(Radially symmetric, non-increasing
assumption)  We denote the scaling

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{j}:=\alpha|u_{j}(\lambda x)|

with

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda=\|u_{j}\|_{1}^{\frac{m}{d(m-1)}}\|u_{j}\|_{m}^{-\frac{m}{d(m-1)}}\\
\alpha=\lambda^{d}\|u_{j}\|_{1}^{-1}.\end{array}\right.

Then a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}_{j}\|_{1}=1,~~\|\bar{u}_{j}\|_{m}=1,

and

\displaystyle J(\bar{u}_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\bar{u}_{j}(x)\bar{u}_{j}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy}{\|\bar{u}_{j}\|_{1}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}}\|\bar{u}_{j}\|_{m}^{\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}}}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\lambda^{d+2s}}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|u_{j}(x)||u_{j}(y)|}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{h(|u_{j}|)}{\|u_{j}\|_{1}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}}\|u_{j}\|_{m}^{\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}}}=J(|u_{j}|).

We see that
J(u_{j})
is invariant under the scaling. Now we claim
that the maximizing sequence
{u_{j}}
could be a non-negative, radially
symmetric and non-increasing function. Denoting
u_{j}^{*}
as the symmetric
decreasing rearrangement of
\bar{u}_{j}
, the Riesz’s rearrangement
inequality
[
22
, pp. 87]
implies that

\|u_{j}^{*}\|_{1}=\|\bar{u}_{j}\|_{1}=1,~\|u_{j}^{*}\|_{m}=\|\bar{u}_{j}\|_{m}=1
and

\displaystyle J(u_{j}^{*})

\displaystyle=h(u_{j}^{*})\geq h(\bar{u}_{j})=J(\bar{u}_{j})=J(|u_{j}|)\geq J(u_{j}).

This entails that
\{u_{j}^{*}\}
is also a maximizing sequence and thus we
may assume that the maximizing sequence
u_{j}
is a non-negative,
radially symmetric and decreasing function with

\|u_{j}\|_{1}=\|u_{j}\|_{m}=1
.

Step 2
(Existence of the supremum)  In
this step, we will show that the maximizing sequence is convergent.
In fact, since
u_{j}
is non-negative and non-increasing, we find that

\displaystyle 1=\|u_{j}\|_{1}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u_{j}(x)dx

\displaystyle=d\alpha_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{j}(r)r^{d-1}dr

\displaystyle\geq d\alpha_{d}\int_{0}^{R}u_{j}(r)r^{d-1}dr

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{d}u_{j}(R)R^{d}

which provides
u_{j}(R)\leq\alpha_{d}^{-1}R^{-d}
. Likewise,

u_{j}(R)\leq\alpha_{d}^{-1/m}R^{-d/m}
. So that

\displaystyle u_{j}(R)\leq G(R):=C_{0}\inf\left\{R^{-d},~R^{-d/m}\right\},~~\mbox{ for any}~R>0.

(70)

Now we have known that
u_{j}
is bounded in
(R,\infty)
, then the
Helly’s selection principle
[
22
, pp. 89]
deduces that
there are a sub-sequence of
{u_{j}}
(without relabeling for
convenience) such that

\displaystyle u_{j}\to W~~\mbox{pointwisely},

where
W
is a non-negative and non-increasing function. Besides, the
fact
1<\frac{2d}{d+2s}<m
implies that

\displaystyle\left\|G(|x|)\right\|_{2d/(d+2s)}^{2d/(d+2s)}=d\alpha_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}G(r)^{\frac{2d}{d+2s}}r^{d-1}dr=C\left[\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{r^{\frac{d}{m}\frac{2d}{d+2s}}}r^{d-1}dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{d\frac{2d}{d+2s}}}r^{d-1}dr\right]<\infty.

So we can derive from the HLS inequality that
h(G)<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{j}\leq G(|x|),\\
h(G)<\infty,\\
u_{j}\to W~~\mbox{pointwisely}\end{array}\right.

together with the dominated convergence theorem ensures

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}h(u_{j})=h(W).

(71)

Hence we have
h(W)=C_{*}
. Finally, we show
J(W)=C_{*}.
Since
u_{j}\to W
pointwisely, Fatou’s lemma tells us that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|W\|_{1}\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{1}\leq 1,\\
\|W\|_{m}\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{m}\leq 1\end{array}\right.

which results in

\displaystyle C_{*}=\lim_{j\to\infty}J(u_{j})\geq J(W)=\frac{h(W)}{\|W\|_{1}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}}\|W\|_{m}^{\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}}}\geq h(W)=C_{*}.

(72)

Therefore, we conclude that
J(W)=C_{*}
which in turn implies that
\|W\|_{1}=\|W\|_{m}=1
.

Step 3
(The Euler-Lagrange equation solved by
W
)  Without loss of generality, we may assume that for any
\rho>0

\displaystyle\Omega:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\Big|W(x)>0,~|x|<\rho\right\},

\displaystyle\partial\Omega:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\Big|W(x)=0,~|x|=\rho\right\}.

Consider
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),~\lambda>0
and there exists

\lambda_{0}>0
such that
W+\lambda\varphi
in
\Omega
for

0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
. Then a tedious calculation shows that

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}J(W+\varepsilon\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|W\|_{1}^{2a}\|W\|_{m}^{2b}}\Big(2\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{b}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\varphi(x)W(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle-ah(W)\|W\|_{1}^{a-1}\|W\|_{m}^{b}\int_{\Omega}\varphi dx-bh(W)\|W\|_{m}^{b-m}\|W\|_{1}^{a}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}W^{m-1}\varphi dx\Big),

(73)

where
a=\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)},b=\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}.
Using the assumption that
\|W\|_{1}=\|W\|_{m}=1,
(
3
) implies that
W
solves the following equation

\displaystyle 2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{W(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy=\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}C_{*}W^{m-1}+\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}C_{*}suppW,~~a.e.~~x\in\Omega.

(74)

Since
W\in L^{1}\cap L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),
it follows from the weak Young inequality that
\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2s}}\ast W\in L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for each
r\in\Big(\frac{d}{d-2s},\frac{1}{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{2s}{d}}\Big].
In particular,
\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2s}}\ast W
and
W^{m-1}
both belong to
L^{\frac{m}{m-1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
due to
m>\frac{2d}{d+2s}.
This property together with (
74
) allows us to assert
\rho<\infty
, and thus we claim that
\Omega
is bounded. If not, we choose a sequence of point
|x_{n}|\to\infty
with
x_{n}\in\Omega,
owing to
m>\frac{2d}{d+2s}
we have that
\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}C_{*}W(x_{n})^{m-1}+\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}C_{*}suppW>0
whereas the sequence
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{W(y)}{|x_{n}-y|^{d-2s}}dy\to 0
, a contradiction.
\Box

F(u)
that

\displaystyle H(u):=\|u\|_{1}^{a}F(u)=\frac{1}{m-1}\|u\|_{1}^{a}\|u\|_{m}^{m}-\frac{c_{d,s}}{2}\|u\|_{1}^{a}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy.

(80)

On the one hand, we can infer from (
66
) that

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{m-1}\|u\|_{1}^{a}\|u\|_{m}^{m}-\frac{c_{d,s}}{2}C_{\ast}\|u\|_{1}^{a+a_{0}}\|u\|_{m}^{b_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\|u\|_{1}^{a}\|u\|_{m}^{m}-\frac{c_{d,s}}{2}C_{\ast}\left(\|u\|_{1}^{a}\|u\|_{m}^{m}\right)^{\beta},

(81)

where the parameters are endowed with

\displaystyle a_{0}=\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)},~~b_{0}=\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)},~~\beta=\frac{d-2s}{d(m-1)}.

(82)

On the other hand, plugging the radial function
W
achieved in Lemma
8
into
H(u),

H(W)
reads

\displaystyle H(W)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{m}-\frac{c_{d,s}}{2}C_{\ast}\|W\|_{1}^{a+a_{0}}\|W\|_{m}^{b_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|u\|_{m}^{m}-\frac{c_{d,s}}{2}C_{\ast}\left(\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{m}\right)^{\beta}.

(83)

Let us set

\displaystyle g(x)=\frac{1}{m-1}x-\frac{c_{d,s}}{2}C_{\ast}x^{\beta}.

(84)

Combining (
4
) with (
4
) gives

\displaystyle g\left(\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{m}\right)=H(W)\geq g\left(\|u\|_{1}^{a}\|u\|_{m}^{m}\right)

(85)

for all
u\in L^{1}\cap L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Hence we deduce that the maximum point of
g(x)
is attained by
x_{*}=\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{m}
where
x_{*}
fulfills

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(x_{*})=\frac{1}{m-1}-\frac{c_{d,s}C_{\ast}}{2}\beta x_{*}^{\beta-1}=0.

(86)

In other words,

\displaystyle\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{m}=\left(\frac{2}{(m-1)c_{d,s}C_{\ast}\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}.

(87)

Coming back to
H(u)
, in case that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{1}^{a}F\left(u_{0}\right)<\|W\|_{1}^{a}F\left(W\right),

(88)

there is a
0<\delta<1
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{1}^{a}F\left(u_{0}\right)<\delta\|W\|_{1}^{a}F\left(W\right).

(89)

Because
F(u)
is non-increasing with respect to time, it turns out to be

\displaystyle g\left(\|u\|_{1}^{a}\|u\|_{m}^{m}\right)\leq H(u)=\|u\|_{1}^{a}F(u)\leq\|u_{0}\|_{1}^{a}F(u_{0})<\delta\|W\|_{1}^{a}F\left(W\right)

(90)

after using the mass conservation
\|u_{0}\|_{1}=\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{1}
for any
t>0.
Since for any
x<\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{m},

g(x)
is a strictly increasing function. So whenever we have

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{1}^{a}\|u_{0}\|_{m}^{m}<\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{m},

(91)

(
90
) implies that there exists a
\mu_{1}<1
such that for any
t>0,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{1}^{a}\|u\|_{m}^{m}<\mu_{1}\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{m}.

(92)

Reversely, for any
x>\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{m},

g(x)
is strictly decreasing. So if

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{1}^{a}\|u_{0}\|_{m}^{m}>\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{m},

(93)

we use once more (
90
) to demonstrate that there exists a
\mu_{2}>1
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{1}^{a}\|u\|_{m}^{m}>\mu_{2}\|W\|_{1}^{a}\|W\|_{m}^{m}.

(94)

This completes the proof.
\Box

4.1
Proof of global existence
(i)
of Theorem
3

A direct consequence of (
77
) and the blow-up criterion is the following global existence result.

Proposition 10
.

(Global existence)
Under assumption (
6
), (
75
) and (
76
), the weak solution obtained in Theorem
5
exists on
[0,\infty).

Proof.

Firstly, the usefulness of (
77
) will be illustrated to show that the
L^{r}\left(r>m-1+(2s-2)m/d\right)
norm is uniformly bounded for any
t>0.
Then the solution can be proved to be uniformly bounded in time by the iterative method. Finally, we demonstrate that the local weak solution achieved in Theorem
5
exists globally.

(i)
The theory is based on the following a priori estimate for solutions to (
1
). It is obtained after multiplying the equation by
ru^{r-1}
.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u^{r}dx+\frac{4mr(r-1)}{(m+r-1)^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\nabla u^{\frac{m+r-1}{2}}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{d,s}(d-2s)(2s-2)(r-1)\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u^{r}(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2-2s}}dxdy.

(95)

Due to
2s>2,
we utilize the HLS inequality (
40
) with
f=u^{r}(x)
and
h=u(y)
such that

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u^{r}(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2-2s}}dxdy\leq C_{dsr}\|u^{r}\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|u\|_{L^{q_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=C_{dsr}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\frac{r+1}{1+(2s-2)/d}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{r+1},

(96)

where
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{d+2-2s}{d}=2
with
q_{1}=rq=\frac{r+1}{1+(2s-2)/d}
,
C_{dsr}
is a bounded constant depending on
d,s,r
. Now choosing
\frac{r+1}{1+(2s-2)/d}>m
and employing the GNS inequality
[
2
, (2.4)]
lead us to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\frac{r+1}{1+(2s-2)/d}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{r+1}\leq C\left\|\nabla u^{\frac{m+r-1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{a\theta}\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{a(1-\theta)\frac{m+r-1}{2}},

(97)

where

\displaystyle a=\frac{2(r+1)}{m+r-1},~~\theta=\frac{\frac{m+r-1}{2m}-\frac{(m+r-1)(1+(2s-2)/d)}{2(r+1)}}{\frac{m+r-1}{2m}-\frac{d-2}{2d}}.

(98)

Next we can use Young’s inequality with

\displaystyle a\theta=2\left(1+\frac{\frac{2-m}{2m}-\frac{2s}{2d}}{\frac{m+r-1}{2m}-\frac{d-2}{2d}}\right)<2,~~\mbox{for}~~m>\frac{2d}{d+2s}

(99)

to arrive at

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\frac{r+1}{1+(2s-2)/d}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{r+1}\leq\frac{2mr}{c_{d,s}(d-2s)C_{dsr}(2s-2)(m+r-1)^{2}}\left\|\nabla u^{\frac{m+r-1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+C\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{(r+1)(1-\theta)/(1-a\theta/2)},

(100)

where the constant
C_{dsr}
is defined as in (
96
).

(ii)
To go further and tackle
\|u\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
, using condition (
77
) which expresses

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<C,~~\mbox{for~any}~t>0,

(101)

we can infer from (
4.1
), (
96
) and (
100
) to state

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{r}+\frac{2mr(r-1)}{(m+r-1)^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\nabla u^{\frac{m+r-1}{2}}\right|^{2}dx\leq C.

(102)

On the other hand, GNS inequality gives rise to

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{r}\leq\left\|\nabla u^{\frac{m+r-1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{2r\theta}{m+r-1}}\|u\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{r(1-\theta)}\leq\left\|\nabla u^{\frac{m+r-1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+C\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right),

(103)

where
\theta=\frac{\frac{m+r-1}{2}-\frac{m+r-1}{2r}}{\frac{m+r-1}{2}-\frac{d-2}{2d}}
.
Combining (
102
) with (
103
) allows us to conclude that for any
r>m-1+(2s-2)m/d

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<C,~~\mbox{for~any}~t>0.

(104)

(iii)
We apply Moser iterative method to obtain the uniformly boundedness of the weak solution,

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right),~~\mbox{for any}~~t>0.

(105)

Recalling Proposition
6
, there are
T_{w}
and a free energy solution
u(x,t)
to (
1
) in
[0,T_{w})
with initial condition
u_{0}.
Thanks to (

), it is sufficient to show that

by Theorem

. Hence we obtain a global free energy solution to (

\Box

4.2
Proof of finite time blow-up
(
ii
)
of Theorem
3

In this subsection, we start with large initial data and utilize the standard argument relying on the evolution of the second moment of solutions as originally done in
[
19
]
to prove the finite time blow-up result in Theorem
3
.

Proposition 11
.

(Finite time blow-up)
Let
a=\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{2d-2s-dm}
and
u(x,t)
be a weak free energy solution obtained in Theorem
5
on
[0,T_{w})
. Assume
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x|^{2}u_{0}(x)dx<\infty,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{a}F(u_{0})<\|W\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{a}F(W)
. If

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{a}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}>\|W\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{a}\|W\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m},

(106)

then the solution satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m_{2}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x|^{2}u(x,t)dx=\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2s)}{m-1}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u^{m}dx+2(d-2s)F(u),~0<t<T_{w}.

(107)

Here
T_{w}<\infty
and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
blows up at finite time in the sense that
\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to T_{w}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\infty
.

Proof.

Here we show the formal computations, the passing to the limit from the approximated problem (
2
) can be done on account of
[
2
, Theorem 2.11]
without any further complication. For simplicity, we shall use
\|u\|_{r}
instead of
\|u\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
.

The main tool is the evolution of the second moment with time. By integrating by parts in (
1
) and symmetrising the second term we compute

\displaystyle\|u\|_{1}^{a}\frac{d}{dt}m_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{1}^{a}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x|^{2}u(x,t)dx=\|u\|_{1}^{a}\left(2d\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u^{m}dx-c_{d,s}(d-2s)\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(x,t)u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2s)}{m-1}\right)\|u\|_{m}^{m}\|u\|_{1}^{a}+2(d-2s)F(u)\|u\|_{1}^{a}.

(108)

We next apply Proposition
9
with some
\mu_{2}>1
and the fact
m<2-2s/d
to obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{1}^{a}\frac{d}{dt}m_{2}(t)

\displaystyle<\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2s)}{m-1}\right)\mu_{2}\|W\|_{m}^{m}\|W\|_{1}^{a}+2(d-2s)F(u_{0})\|u_{0}\|_{1}^{a}

\displaystyle<\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2s)}{m-1}\right)\mu_{2}\|W\|_{m}^{m}\|W\|_{1}^{a}+2(d-2s)F(W)\|W\|_{1}^{a}

\displaystyle=\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2s)}{m-1}\right)(\mu_{2}-1)\|W\|_{m}^{m}\|W\|_{1}^{a}<0.

(109)

Here we have used the non-increasing of
F(u)
in time and the identity

\displaystyle 2(d-2s)F(W)\|W\|_{1}^{a}=-\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2s)}{m-1}\right)\|W\|_{m}^{m}\|W\|_{1}^{a}

(110)

which can be derived by combining (
4
) and (
87
). So we can infer from (
109
) that there exists a
T>0
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T}m_{2}(t)=0
.

In addition, using Hölder’s inequality one has

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x)dx=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}u(x)dx+\int_{|x|>R}u(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{d}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}m_{2}(t).

(111)

Choosing
R=\left(\frac{Cm_{2}(t)}{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{d+2}}
gives

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{1}=\|u\|_{1}\leq c\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{2}{d+2}}m_{2}(t)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}

(112)

which yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\geq\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T}\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{1}^{(d+2)/2}}{cm_{2}(t)^{d/2}}=\infty.

(113)

Thus the proof is completed.
\Box

5
Conclusions

This paper concerns equation (
1
) for the intermediate exponent
\frac{2d}{d+2s}<m<2-\frac{2s}{d}
. It is shown that there is a threshold value which is characterized by the optimal constant of a variant of HLS inequality
\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy\leq C_{*}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{d(m-1)}}\|f\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{m(d-2s)}{d(m-1)}}
to classify the global existence and finite time blow-up of non-negative solutions, and the equality of the modified HLS inequality is given by the radial steady state
W(x)
of equation (
1
). Precisely, in the case that the initial free energy
F(u_{0})
is less than
\|W\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{2d-2s-dm}}F(W)/\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{2d-2s-dm}},
there exists a global weak solution satisfying the free energy inequality when the initial data satisfies
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{2d-2s-dm}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}<\|W\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{2d-2s-dm}}\|W\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}
while the solution blows up in finite time provided by the condition
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{2d-2s-dm}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}>\|W\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{(d+2s)m-2d}{2d-2s-dm}}\|W\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}
.

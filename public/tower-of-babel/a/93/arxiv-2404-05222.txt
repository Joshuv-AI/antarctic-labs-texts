Fractional Hardy inequalities and capacity density
Lizaveta Ihnatsyeva, Kaushik Mohanta, Antti V. Vähäkangas

Abstract
. We prove that a pointwise fractional Hardy inequality implies a fractional Hardy inequality, defined via a Gagliardo-type seminorm. The proof consists of two main parts. The first one is to characterize the pointwise fractional Hardy inequality in terms of a fractional capacity density condition. The second part is to show the deep open-endedness or self-improvement property of the fractional capacity density, which we accomplish in the setting of a complete geodesic space equipped with a doubling measure. These results are new already in the standard Euclidean setting.

1.
Introduction

This paper brings together the pointwise fractional Hardy inequalities introduced in

[
10
]
and the recent developments on self-improvement of nonlocal capacity density conditions from
[
39
,
7
,
6
,
36
]
.
The motivation for our nonlocal problems comes from their local analogues.
Recall that a closed set
E
in a metric measure space, equipped with a doubling measure, supports a pointwise
p
-Hardy inequality
if there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{H}}
such that inequality

(1)

|u(x)|\leq C_{\mathrm{H}}\,\operatorname{dist}(x,E)(M_{2\operatorname{dist}(x,E)}(g^{p})(x))^{1/p}\,

holds for all

x\in X\setminus E
, whenever
u
is a Lipschitz function on
X
such that
u=0
in
E
and
g
is
an upper gradient of
u
; we refer to
(
12
) for the definition
of the restricted maximal operator
M_{2\operatorname{dist}(x,E)}
.
The pointwise
p
-Hardy inequality in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
g=\lvert\nabla u\rvert
was first independently studied
by Kinnunen–Martio in
[
31
]
and by Hajłasz in
[
19
]
.
Korte–Lehrbäck–Tuominen proved in
[
32
]

that a pointwise

-Hardy inequality is equivalent to
the so-called

-capacity density condition of

. By using
this equivalence and the deep result on the self-improvement of the
p
-capacity density condition,
see below, it was shown in
[
32
]

that the pointwise
p
-Hardy inequality (
1
) implies the integral Hardy inequality

(2)

\int_{X\setminus E}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,E)^{p}}\,d\mu(x)\leq C\int_{X}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,,

where
E
,
u
,
g
, and

are as in (

).

The key fact above is the self-improvement, meaning that

p
-capacity density condition implies
an

\hat{p}
-capacity density condition, for some
1<\hat{p}<p
.
The
p
-capacity density condition, also known as uniform
p
-fatness, appears in Hardy inequalities, potential theory
and PDE’s, we refer to
[
25
,
30
]
.
The self-improvement property of this condition was first discovered by Lewis
[
39
]

using potential theory in Euclidean spaces.
His result has been followed by other works incorporating different techniques, such as nonlinear potential theory
[
43
,
5
]
and
Hardy inequalities
[
33
,
37
]
. A direct
proof for the self-improvement of pointwise Hardy inequalities by using curves is given in
[
15
,
14
]
.
For a survey on Hardy inequalities, and their connections
to the

-capacity density condition, we refer to
[

,
12
,
30
]
.

Our main contribution is an extension of the approach and results in
[
32
]
to the nonlocal setting of fractional Sobolev spaces and their generalizations. Some of the first steps were already taken in
[
10
]
, where
the following nonlocal or fractional variant of inequality (

We assume that
1<p,q<\infty
and
0<\beta<1
.
A closed set
E
in
X
supports the
pointwise
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality if
there exists a constant
c_{H}>0
such that

(3)

\lvert u(x)\rvert\leq c_{H}\operatorname{dist}(x,E)^{\beta}\bigl(M_{2\operatorname{dist}(x,E)}\bigl((G_{u,\beta,q,B(x,2\operatorname{dist}(x,E))})^{p}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{1/p}\,,

whenever
x\in X
satisfies

and
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a continuous function
such that

on

is the restricted
maximal operator as in (

G_{u,\beta,q,B(x,2\operatorname{dist}(x,E))}(y)=\biggl(\int_{B(x,2\operatorname{dist}(x,E))}\frac{|u(y)-u(z)|^{q}}{d(y,z)^{\beta q}\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\,d\mu(z)\biggr)^{1/q}\,,\quad y\in X\,.

The following theorem is one of our main results: under suitable assumptions, the pointwise
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality implies
the integral version of the
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality.
We also prove a closely related Theorem
9.3
, which is a stronger
result by Fatou’s lemma.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete geodesic space equipped with a doubling measure
\mu

which satisfies the quantitative doubling condition (
8
),
with exponent
Q>0
,
and the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
10
), with exponent
\sigma>0
.
Let
1<p,q<\infty
and
0<\beta<1
be such that

(4)

Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)<\beta<\frac{\sigma}{p}\,.

Assume that an unbounded closed set

supports the

(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality. Then the integral

-Hardy inequality

\begin{split}\int_{X\setminus E}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,E)^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)\leq C\int_{X}\biggl(\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{d(x,y)^{\beta q}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\biggr)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)\end{split}

holds whenever

is a continuous function
such that

on

. Here the constant

is independent of

.

When

, the seminorm on the right of (

) is a generalization of the well known Gagliardo seminorm, which is used to define a fractional Sobolev space in the standard Euclidean setting; see

[
8
,
12
,
38
,
46
]
for expositions on fractional Sobolev spaces
that have attracted plenty of interest in recent years.
When

is the Lebesgue
measure in the Euclidean space

, then (

as
n(1/p-1/q)<\beta<n/p
.
This restriction on parameters and
additional assumptions on
X

come from limitations of our methods, and
it is unclear to us to which extent these restrictions can be relaxed.
On the other hand, Theorem

and our other main results are new even in
a Euclidean space equipped with the Lebesgue measure.
Other capacity related sufficient conditions for the integral Hardy inequality (
5
) in the special case

are given in
[
13
,
26
]
.
These papers employ the Lewis’ result
[

]
on the self-improvement of the Riesz

-capacity density condition in Euclidean spaces, see the discussion further below.

Our overall strategy for the proof of Theorem

\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,E)^{\beta p}}\leq C\left(M_{2\operatorname{dist}(x,E)}\bigl((G_{u,\beta,q,B(x,2\operatorname{dist}(x,E))})^{\hat{p}}\bigr)(x)\right)^{p/\hat{p}}\,,

We prove this self-improvement of a pointwise Hardy inequality, that is, Theorem

,
in two main parts.
In the first part we prove Theorem

, which shows that the pointwise

-Hardy inequality (

In the second part we prove Theorem

-capacity density condition is self-improving;

[

Finally, we make some remarks on the line of research developed in

]
,
[
7
]
,
[

]
,

]
.
The origins of our results can be traced to the seminal work by Lewis

]
, who proved that a Riesz

-capacity density condition in Euclidean spaces is doubly self-improving, that is, self-improving in both parameters

. More recently, a Hajłasz

-capacity density condition in metric spaces was introduced in
[

]
by using Hajłasz gradients of order

. It was also shown that this condition is doubly self-improving, if

is a complete geodesic space. The comparability of Riesz and Hajłasz

, where Lewis’ result was also transferred to complete geodesic spaces.
A flexible and

. The Riesz

-capacity density

.

,

-capacity density condition
is equivalent to a Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin

-capacity density

and

complement this line of research, the former proving the

-independence of the fractional capacity, and the latter

Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we assume that

For the proof of Lemma

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
1\leq p<p^{*}<\infty
,

B\subset X
is a ball and

is a measurable function. Assume that there exists a constant

for each

. Then

\bigg(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\bigg)^{1/p}\leq 2^{1/p}\bigg(\frac{C_{0}p}{p^{*}-p}\bigg)^{1/p^{*}}\mu(B)^{-1/p^{*}}\,.

If

, then
(\beta,p,p,q)
-Poincaré inequalities follow from Lemma

.
The following lemma shows that these inequalities hold

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that

and

. Then

supports the

)

and

Since

8

and constant

, both depending on

only. By

. We denote

Let

be a ball and let

By adapting the proof of

, Theorem 3.4]
, we obtain a constant

such that

(16)

\begin{split}\mu(\{x\in B:\lvert u(x)-u_{2B}\rvert>s\})\,s^{p^{*}}\leq Cr^{\beta p^{*}}\mu(B)^{1-p^{*}/p}\biggl(\int_{2B}G_{u,\beta,q,2B}(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\biggr)^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}\end{split}

More specifically, it is clear from the proof that the

Lemma

then implies

(17)

\begin{split}\bigg(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{B}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\bigg)^{1/p}&\leq 2\bigg(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{2B}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\bigg)^{1/p}\\

If

3.
Fractional relative capacity

Here we define a variant of the
fractional relative capacity,
compare to
[
41
, Definition 7.1]
and see also
[
11
]
and
[
42
, §11]
.
In Lemma
3.4
we give estimates for relative capacities of balls.
Lower bounds for relative capacities of closed sets in terms of a
Hausdroff content are given in Lemma
3.7
. At the end of this section we recall a fractional version of
Maz’ya’s capacitary Poincaré inequality.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
0<\beta<1
,
1\leq q,p<\infty
and
\Lambda\geq 2
.
Let
B\subset X
be a ball and let
E\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
be a closed set. Then we write

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(E,2B,\Lambda B)=\inf_{\varphi}\int_{\Lambda B}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{q}}{d(x,y)^{\beta q}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\biggr)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)\,,

where the infimum is taken over all continuous functions

\varphi\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\varphi(x)\geq 1
for every
x\in E
and

\varphi(x)=0
for every
x\in X\setminus 2B
.

Remark 3.2
.

Suppose that
\varphi\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a continuous function such that
\varphi(x)\geq 1
for every
x\in E
and

\varphi(x)=0
for every
x\in X\setminus 2B
, where
B
and
E
are
as in Definition
3.1
.
By considering the function
v=\max\{0,\min\{\varphi,1\}\}

and using inequality (
15
), it is clear
that the infimum in Definition
3.1
can be taken
either over
all continuous and bounded functions
or
over
all continuous functions that are integrable on balls
, in both cases such that
\varphi(x)\geq 1
for every
x\in E
and

\varphi(x)=0
for every
x\in X\setminus 2B
.
In particular, our Definition
3.1
coincides with the relative fractional capacity defined in
[
10
, Definition 4.1]
.

Let us estimate relative capacities of balls.
Lemma
3.4
(i) and a monotonicity argument show that the
fractional relative capacities in Definition
3.1
are always finite.
In order to prove this, we use the
following lemma from
[
10
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\alpha>0
.
There is a constant
C(\alpha,c_{\mu})>0
such that

\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\leq C(\alpha,c_{\mu})r^{\alpha}\

for every
x\in X
and
r>0
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
and
0<\beta<1
.

(i)

Assume that
\Lambda\geq 2
,

x_{0}\in X
and
r>0
. Then

\mathrm{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(\overline{B(x_{0},r)},B(x_{0},2r),B(x_{0},\Lambda r))\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta,p,q,\Lambda)r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x_{0},r))\,.

(ii)

Assume that
X
is connected
and that
\Lambda>2
.
Assume that
x_{0}\in X
and
r>0
is such that
r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
.
Then

r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x_{0},r))\leq C(\beta,p,q,c_{\mu},\Lambda)\mathrm{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(\overline{B(x_{0},r)},B(x_{0},2r),B(x_{0},\Lambda r))\,.

Proof.

We show (i) first. Denote
B=B(x_{0},r)
. Let

\varphi(x)=\max\Bigl\{0,1-r^{-1}\operatorname{dist}(x,B)\Bigr\}\,

for every
x\in X
.
Then
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
in
X
,
\varphi=1
in
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
,
\varphi=0
in
X\setminus 2B
,
and
\varphi
is an
r^{-1}
-Lipschitz function in
X
.
Since
\varphi
is admissible for the relative capacity, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(\overline{B},2B,\Lambda B)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Lambda B}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{q}}{d(x,y)^{\beta q}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\biggr)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)\,.

x\in\Lambda B
. Since
|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq d(x,y)/r

for each

\int_{\Lambda B}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{q}}{d(x,y)^{\beta q}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\leq r^{-q}\int_{B(x,2\Lambda r)}\frac{d(x,y)^{q(1-\beta)}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\leq C(\beta,q,c_{\mu},\Lambda)r^{-\beta q}.

\mathrm{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(\overline{B},2B,\Lambda B)\leq C(\beta,p,q,c_{\mu},\Lambda)r^{-\beta p}\mu(\Lambda B)\leq C(\beta,p,q,c_{\mu},\Lambda)r^{-\beta p}\mu(B)\,,

and condition (i) follows.

B=B(x_{0},r)
and
\lambda=\min\{3,\Lambda\}>2

Choose any
\varphi
from the non-empty set of test functions for
the relative capacity
\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(\overline{B},2B,\Lambda B)
.
By replacing
\varphi
with
\max\{0,\min\{\varphi,1\}\}
, if necessary, we may assume that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
in
X
.
Thus
\varphi
is continuous on
X
,
\varphi=1
on
\overline{B}
, and
\varphi=0
on
B\setminus 2B
.

) holds with
\kappa=2/\lambda
.

\lambda r<4r<\operatorname{diam}(X)/2

\mu(2B)=\mu(B(x_{0},2r))\leq c_{R}\,\mu(B(x_{0},\lambda r))\leq c_{R}\,\mu(B(x_{0},\Lambda r))=c_{R}\,\mu(\Lambda B)\,.

\displaystyle 0\leq\varphi_{\Lambda B}=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}\varphi(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq\frac{\mu(2B)}{\mu(\Lambda B)}\leq c_{R}<1.

in

The following theorem characterizes the fractional capacity
density condition in connected metric spaces.
This result is a nonlocal or fractional analogue of
[
32
, Theorem 2]
.
To our knowledge, Theorem
4.6
is new in this generality.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq q<\infty
and
0<\beta<1
. Assume that
X
is connected.
Let
E\subset X
be a closed set.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

E
satisfies the fractional
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition (
19
).

(ii)

E
supports the boundary
(\beta,p,p,q)
-Poincaré inequality (
20
).

(iii)

E
supports the pointwise
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality (
21
).

Proof.

Since the metric space
X
is connected, the measure
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
10
), with
constants
\sigma>0
and
c_{\sigma}>0
.
By Lemma
2.5
, we see that

X
supports the
(\beta,p,p,q)
-Poincaré inequality. Hence, the implication (i) to (ii) follows from Lemma
4.3
.
Lemma
4.4
shows that (ii) implies (iii). The last
implication from (iii) to (i) follows
from Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Suppose that a closed set
E\subset X
supports the
pointwise
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality (

), for
some

and
0<\beta<1

From Definition

In the light of Theorem

, a corresponding monotonicity result holds for

Under certain restrictions,

Our main result, Theorem

5.
Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin seminorms

One of our main goals is to show
a self-improvement property for
the fractional capacity density condition (

In order to show this result, we make use of a self-improvement
property of a closely related Hajłasz–Triebel-Lizorkin capacity density condition, which was recently established in
[

Our basic strategy of proof is to show that these two capacity density conditions are equivalent under certain assumptions.
For this purpose, we first recall local variants of Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin seminorms in this section. Later we need a Sobolev–Poincaré type inequality in Lemma
5.3

First we

[

, by adapting the global definition in
[

]
.

Definition 5.1

\beta
-Hajłasz gradient of

in
\Omega

a set

such that
\mu(E)=0
and for all

k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and

satisfying
2^{-k-1}\leq d(x,y)<2^{-k}
, we have

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}(g_{k}(x)+g_{k}(y))\,.

We denote by
\mathbb{D}^{\beta}_{\Omega}(u)
the collection of all fractional

\beta
-Hajłasz gradients of

in
\Omega
.

Fix

,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and an open set
\Omega\subset X
. For a sequence

of measurable

\Omega

\big\|g\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\,l^{q})}=\big\|\|g\|_{l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}})}\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,,

where

\|g\|_{l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}})}=\begin{cases}\big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|g_{k}|^{q}\big)^{1/q},&\quad\text{when }1\leq q<\infty,\\
\;\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f_{k}|,&\quad\text{when }q=\infty.\end{cases}

We use the following
Fefferman–Stein vector-valued maximal function inequality, we refer to
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
for a proof.
If
1<p<\infty
and
1<q\leq\infty
, then there
exists a constant

such that for all sequences
(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of measurable

we have

\lVert(Mg_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}\leq C\lVert(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}\,,

where

is the centered maximal function of

for each

,
see (
11
).

Definition 5.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
0<\beta<1
. The homogeneous Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin space
\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\Omega)
is the seminormed space of all measurable functions
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
on
an open set
\Omega\subset X
such that

\lvert u\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\Omega)}=\inf_{g\in\mathbb{D}^{\beta}_{\Omega}(u)}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;l^{q})}<\infty\,.

Notice that
\lvert u\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\Omega)}\leq\lvert u\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,\hat{q}}(\Omega)}
whenever

.
We remark that
\lvert u\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\Omega)}
is a seminorm, but not a norm since it vanishes on constant functions.

The following Sobolev–Poincaré inequality is from

. In the case

Indeed, if

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set,
0<\beta<1
,
0<\varepsilon\leq\beta
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

be a measurable function. We denote by

the collection of all sequences

of nonnegative measurable functions for which there exists a set

with

such that, for all

, we have

We use the following two-sided comparison of the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin seminorm, which is essentially from
[
34
, Theorem 2.1]
. For convenience of the reader
and completeness, we recall the proof below.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set,

1\leq p,q<\infty
,
0<\beta<1
,
0<\varepsilon\leq\beta
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Assume that

is a measurable function. Then there exists a constant

Proof.

Let
{g}\in\mathbb{D}^{\beta}_{\Omega}(u)
and let
E\subset\Omega

be the associated exceptional set of measure zero as in Definition
5.1
.
Fix
x,y\in\Omega\setminus E
.

Taking sum, we have

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|u(x)-u(y)|\mathbf{1}_{[2^{-k-1},2^{-k})}(d(x,y))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}d(x,y)^{\beta}\left(g_{k}(x)+g_{k}(y)\right)\mathbf{1}_{[2^{-k-1},2^{-k})}(d(x,y))

\displaystyle\leq d(x,y)^{\beta-\varepsilon}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{-k\varepsilon}\left(g_{k}(x)+g_{k}(y)\right)\mathbf{1}_{[2^{-k-N},\infty)}(d(x,y)).

Hence, we have
{g}\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\beta,\varepsilon,N}_{\Omega}(u)

{g}\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\beta,\varepsilon,N}_{\Omega}(u)

and let
E\subset\Omega

be the associated exceptional set of measure zero as in Definition
6.3
.
Define, for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}

Now, if

and

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|

\displaystyle\leq d(x,y)^{\beta-\varepsilon}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{-j\varepsilon}\left(g_{j}(x)+g_{j}(y)\right)\mathbf{1}_{[2^{-j-N},\infty)}(d(x,y))

\displaystyle\leq d(x,y)^{\beta-\varepsilon}\sum_{j=k-N}^{\infty}2^{-j\varepsilon}\left(g_{j}(x)+g_{j}(y)\right)

\displaystyle\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}\sum_{j=k-N}^{\infty}2^{(k-j+1)\varepsilon}\left(g_{j}(x)+g_{j}(y)\right)=d(x,y)^{\beta}\left(h_{k}(x)+h_{k}(y)\right)\,.

We have shown that

. By Lemma
6.1
with
a=2^{\varepsilon}>1
and
b=q

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\ell^{q}}^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\sum_{j=k-N}^{\infty}2^{(k-j+1)\varepsilon}g_{j}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=2^{(N+1)q\varepsilon}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\sum_{j=k-N}^{\infty}2^{(k-(j+N))\varepsilon}g_{j+N-N}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=2^{(N+1)q\varepsilon}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}2^{(k-j)\varepsilon}g_{j-N}\right)^{q}\leq C(N,q,\varepsilon)\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{j-N}^{q}=C(N,q,\varepsilon)\lVert g\rVert_{\ell^{q}}^{q}.

Hence

for all
{g}\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\beta,\varepsilon,N}_{\Omega}(u)
,
from which the second inequality follows.
∎

In this section, we first define

]
, see Definition

The seminorm comparison in Section

Definition 7.1

Let

Remark 7.2

8.

Lemma
3.4
(i) implies that

(48)

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta,p,q)r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\,.

On the other hand, by the simple estimate in
[
6
, Remark 5.2]

and (
47
),
we obtain

(49)

r^{-d}\mu(B(x,r))\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,d}_{r}(\overline{B(x,r)})\leq C(c_{0})\mathcal{H}^{\mu,d}_{r}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\,.

Let
\{B(x_{k},r_{k})\}_{k}
be a countable cover of
E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
, satisfying

B(x_{k},r_{k})\cap\overline{B(x,r)}\neq\emptyset

and
0<r_{k}\leq 5\Lambda r

for all
k
. If
0<r_{k}\leq r
for all
k
, then by Definition
3.6
of the Hausdorff content we have

\mathcal{H}^{\mu,d}_{r}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\leq\sum_{k}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))\,r_{k}^{-d}\,,

and thus, by (
49
),

(50)

r^{-d}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(c_{0})\sum_{k}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))\,r_{k}^{-d}\,.

On the other hand, if
r<r_{k}
for some
k
, then we may assume that
r<r_{1}\leq 5\Lambda r

and recall that
B(x_{1},r_{1})\cap\overline{B(x,r)}\neq\emptyset
.
In this case we obtain, using also the doubling condition (
7
), that

(51)

\begin{split}r^{-d}\mu(B(x,r))&\leq C(d)r_{1}^{-d}\mu(B(x_{1},3r_{1}))\\
&\leq C(d,c_{\mu})\mu(B(x_{1},r_{1}))\,r_{1}^{-d}\leq C(d,c_{\mu})\sum_{k}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))\,r_{k}^{-d}\,.\end{split}

By taking infimum over all such covers of
E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
, we conclude from
(
50
) and (
51
) that

(52)

r^{-d}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(c_{0},d,c_{\mu})\mathcal{H}^{\mu,d}_{5\Lambda r}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}).

By assumption, the measure
\mu
satisfies the reverse doubling condition (
9
), with constants
\kappa=1/2=2/\Lambda

and
0<c_{R}<1
.
By using Theorem
3.7
with
\eta=d/\beta
, we get

(53)

r^{-d}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(\beta,q,p,d,c_{R},c_{\mu},c_{0})r^{\beta p-d}\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\,.

Combining (
48
) and (
53
) gives

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\leq C\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\,,

where
C=C(\beta,q,p,d,c_{R},c_{\mu},c_{0})
.
We have shown that the set
E
satisfies the fractional
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition (
19
).
∎

We are now ready to show the first version of our main result;
the crucial point to observe is that
inequality
0<d<\beta p
in
condition (iii) of Theorem
8.4
is open-ended. Therefore, the two other
equivalent conditions in the theorem are also open-ended or self-improving.
The proof of the implication from (ii) to (iii) is significantly more difficult than proofs of the other implications.
This deep implication relies on
[
36
, Theorem 11.4]
which, in turn,
is via

[
6
, Theorem 6.4]
based on
[
7
, Theorem 9.5]
.
Recall that a doubling measure
\mu
in a geodesic space satisfies inequalities (
8
) and (
10
) for some exponents
Q>0
and
\sigma>0
, respectively.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete geodesic space equipped with a doubling measure
\mu

which satisfies the quantitative doubling condition (
8
),
with exponent
Q>0
,
and the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
10
), with exponent
\sigma>0
.
Let

1<p,q<\infty
and
0<\beta<1
be such that

Q(1/p-1/q)<\beta<\sigma/p
.
Then the following
conditions are equivalent for a closed set
E\subset X
:

(i)

E
satisfies the fractional
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition (
19
).

(ii)

E
satisfies the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition (
46
),
with some constants
\Lambda\geq 41
and
c_{1}\leq 1/80
.

(iii)

E
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition (
47
) for some
0<d<\beta p
.

Proof.

Assume that condition (i) holds. Let
c_{0}>0
and
\Lambda>2
be the constant in
(
19
).
Denote
\Lambda_{1}=\max\{41,9\Lambda\}
and
\Lambda_{2}=\Lambda_{1}/9\geq\Lambda
.
Fix
x\in E
and
0<r<(1/80)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
By
[
36
, Theorem 4.3(a)]
, there is a constant
C_{1}=C(c_{\mu},\beta,p,\Lambda)>0
such that

(54)

\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda_{1}r))\leq C_{1}r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\,.

Since
X
is geodesic, it is connected, and we can use
Lemma
3.4
(ii) to get

(55)

r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(\beta,p,q,c_{\mu},\Lambda)\mathrm{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\,.

By combining (
54
) and (
55
), we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda_{1}r))

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(\beta,p,q,c_{\mu},\Lambda)\mathrm{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\,.

Since
E
satisfies the fractional
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition (
19
), with constants
c_{0}>0
and
\Lambda>2
, and
\Lambda_{2}\geq\Lambda
, we can continue to estimate

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda_{1}r))

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(\beta,p,q,c_{\mu},c_{0},\Lambda)\mathrm{cap}_{\beta,p,q}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda_{2}r))\,.

Theorem
7.3
(i) then implies that

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda_{1}r))

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(\beta,p,q,c_{\mu},c_{0},c_{Q},Q,\Lambda)\mathrm{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda_{1}r))\,,

for all
x\in E
and
0<r<(1/80)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
Therefore (ii) holds.

The implication from (ii) to (iii) follows from
[
36
, Theorem 11.4]
.
For the final implication we notice that, since
X
is connected, the measure
\mu
satisfies the reverse doubling condition (
9
), with constants
\kappa=1/2

and
0<c_{R}=C(c_{\mu})<1
.
Hence, the implication from (iii) to (i) follows from Theorem
8.3
.
∎

The following corollary explicates a three way self-improvement property of the fractional capacity density condition in complete geodesic spaces, under suitable restrictions on the parameters, most notable of which are the inequalities
Q(1/p-1/q)<\beta<\sigma/p
.

Theorem 8.5
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete geodesic space equipped with a doubling measure
\mu

which satisfies the quantitative doubling condition (
8
),
with exponent
Q>0
,
and the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
10
), with exponent
\sigma>0
.
Let

1<p,q<\infty
and
0<\beta<1
be such that

Q(1/p-1/q)<\beta<\sigma/p
.
Assume that a closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the fractional
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition (
19
).
Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

E
satisfies also the fractional
(\hat{\beta},\hat{p},\hat{q})
-capacity density condition (
19
), if

(56)

\lvert\beta-\hat{\beta}\rvert<\varepsilon\,,\qquad\lvert p-\hat{p}\rvert<\varepsilon\qquad\text{ and }1\leq\hat{q}<\infty\,.

Proof.

Theorem
8.4
shows that
E
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition (
47
) for some
0<d<\beta p
.
Choose
\varepsilon>0
such that the following inequalities

1<\hat{p}<\infty
,
0<\hat{\beta}<1
,
and

0<d<\hat{\beta}\hat{p}
hold for all

\hat{\beta}
,
\hat{p}
satisfying the (
56
). This is clearly possible, since all
of the three inequalities are open-ended and the parameters

\beta
and
p
satisfy them.

Now, assume that (
56
) holds for the chosen
\varepsilon>0
.
In particular,

E
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition (
47
) for
0<d<\hat{\beta}\hat{p}
.
Since
X
is connected, the measure
\mu
satisfies the reverse doubling condition (
9
), with constants
\kappa=1/2

and
0<c_{R}=C(c_{\mu})<1
.
The other assumptions of Theorem
8.3
are also satisfied, with parameters
\hat{\beta}
,
\hat{p}
and
\hat{q}
, and the theorem implies
that
E
satisfies the fractional
(\hat{\beta},\hat{p},\hat{q})
-capacity density condition (
19
).
∎

9.
Fractional Hardy inequalities

A combination of Theorems
8.5
and
4.6
provides
three ways of self-improvement for a pointwise Hardy inequality in complete geodesic spaces, in terms of
\beta
,
p
and
q
satisfying suitable restrictions.
Here we make use of this result and show
that a pointwise
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality implies the following
variant of the integral
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
,
0<\beta<1
, and let
E\subset X
be a closed set.
We say that
E
supports the ball
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy
inequality if there are constants
c_{I}>0
and
\lambda\geq 1
such that

(57)

\begin{split}\int_{B\setminus E}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,E)^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)\leq c_{I}^{p}\int_{\lambda B}\biggl(\int_{\lambda B}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{d(x,y)^{\beta q}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\biggr)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)\end{split}

whenever
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a continuous function
such that
u=0
on
E
and
B=B(x_{0},R)
is a ball with

x_{0}\in E
and
0<R<\operatorname{diam}(E)/8
.

Assuming that a closed set
E\subset X
satisfies a pointwise
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality (
21
), then

\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,E)^{\beta p}}\leq c_{H}^{p}M_{2\operatorname{dist}(x,E)}\bigl((G_{u,\beta,q,B(x,2\operatorname{dist}(x,E))})^{p}\bigr)(x)\,,

whenever
0<\operatorname{dist}(x,E)<\operatorname{diam}(E)/8
and
u
is as in (
21
).
Integration of this inequality does not readily yield (
57
), since
the restricted maximal operator is typically not bounded in
L^{1}(X)
. The following self-improvement of
the pointwise
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality in terms of the
p
-parameter allows us to apply a variant of this argument in the proof of Theorem
9.3
.

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete geodesic space equipped with a doubling measure
\mu

which satisfies the quantitative doubling condition (
8
),
with exponent
Q>0
,
and the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
10
), with exponent
\sigma>0
.
Let

1<p,q<\infty
and
0<\beta<1
be such that

Q(1/p-1/q)<\beta<\sigma/p
.
If a closed set
E\subset X
supports the pointwise
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality (
21
), then there exists an exponent
1<\hat{p}<p
such that

E
supports the pointwise
(\beta,\hat{p},q)
-Hardy inequality (
21
).

Proof.

Theorem
4.6
shows that the closed set
E
satisfies the fractional
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition (
19
).
By Theorem
8.5
, there exists
1<\hat{p}<p
such that
E
satisfies the fractional
(\beta,\hat{p},q)
-capacity density condition (
19
).
Now

E
supports also the pointwise
(\beta,\hat{p},q)
-Hardy inequality (
21
), with a constant
c_{H}>0
,
by Theorem
4.6
.
∎

Let us emphasize that the implication from (i) to (ii) is
the main novelty in Theorem
9.3
.

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete geodesic space equipped with a doubling measure
\mu

which satisfies the quantitative doubling condition (
8
),
with exponent
Q>0
,
and the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
10
), with exponent
\sigma>0
.
Let

1<p,q<\infty
and
0<\beta<1
be such that

Q(1/p-1/q)<\beta<\sigma/p
.
Then the following
conditions are equivalent for a closed set
E\subset X
:

(i)

E
supports the pointwise
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality (
21
).

(ii)

E
supports the ball
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality (
57
).

Proof.

It is clear that condition (ii) implies that
E
supports the boundary
(\beta,p,p,q)
-Poincaré inequality (
20
).
Since

X
is connected, the implication from (ii) to (i) follows from Theorem
4.6
.
It suffices to show the implication from (i) to (ii).
Assuming condition (i), we observe from Theorem
9.2
that there exists
1<\hat{p}<p
such that

E
supports the pointwise
(\beta,\hat{p},q)
-Hardy inequality (
21
).
By using this fact, we show that (
57
) holds.

Let
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function
such that
u=0
on
E
.
Let
B=B(x_{0},R)
be a ball with

x_{0}\in E
and
0<R<\operatorname{diam}(E)/8
.
Fix a point
x\in B\setminus E
.
Then

0<\operatorname{dist}(x,E)\leq d(x,x_{0})<R<\operatorname{diam}(E)/8

and

B(x,2\operatorname{dist}(x,E))\subset 3B
.
Therefore, by applying the pointwise
(\beta,\hat{p},q)
-Hardy inequality (
21
)
and inequality (
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,E)^{\beta p}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{H}^{p}\bigl(M_{2\operatorname{dist}(x,E)}\bigl((G_{u,\beta,q,B(x,2\operatorname{dist}(x,E))})^{\hat{p}}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{p/{\hat{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{H}^{p}\bigl(M_{2\operatorname{dist}(x,E)}\bigl((G_{u,\beta,q,3B})^{\hat{p}}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{p/{\hat{p}}}\leq c_{H}^{p}\bigl(M\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{3B}(G_{u,\beta,q,3B})^{\hat{p}}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{p/\hat{p}}.

Hence, by integrating over the set
B\setminus E
, we obtain

\int_{B\setminus E}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,E)^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)\leq c_{H}^{p}\int_{X}\bigl(M\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{3B}(G_{u,\beta,q,3B})^{\hat{p}}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{p/{\hat{p}}}\,d\mu(x).

Since
{\hat{p}}<p
, the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function theorem
[
4
, Theorem 3.13]

implies

\int_{B\setminus E}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,E)^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)\leq C(c_{H},{\hat{p}},p,c_{\mu})\int_{3B}G_{u,\beta,q,3B}(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x).

We have shown that the set
E
supports
the ball
(\beta,p,q)
-Hardy inequality (
57
), with constants

c_{I}=C(c_{H},{\hat{p}},p,c_{\mu})
and
\lambda=3
.
∎

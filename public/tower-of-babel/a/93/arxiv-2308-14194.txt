Generalized equations of hydrodynamics in fractional derivatives
P. Kostrobij, B. Markovych, I. Ryzha, M. Tokarchuk

Abstract
We present a general approach for obtaining the generalized transport equations with fractional derivatives using the Liouville equation with fractional derivatives for a system of classical particles and the Zubarev non-equilibrium statistical operator (NSO) method within the Gibbs statistics. We obtain the non-Markov equations of hydrodynamics for the non-equilibrium average values of densities of particle number, momentum and energy of liquid in a spatially heterogeneous medium with a fractal structure. For isothermal processes ( β = 1 / k B ​ T = c ​ o ​ n ​ s ​ t \beta=1/k_{B}T=const ), the non-Markov Navier–Stokes equation in fractional derivatives is obtained. We consider models for the frequency dependence of memory function (viscosity), which lead to the Navier–Stokes equations in fractional derivatives in space and time.

IV
Navier–Stokes equation in fractional derivatives with memory

In the case
\beta(\vec{r};t)=\beta
(isothermal processes),
the equations of generalized hydrodynamics will have the following form:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left\langle\hat{n}(\vec{r})\right\rangle^{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\int d\mu_{\alpha}(\vec{r}^{\prime})\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\varepsilon(t^{\prime}-t)}D^{\alpha}_{\vec{r}}:\overleftrightarrow{\eta}(\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime};t,t^{\prime}):D^{\alpha}_{\vec{r}^{\prime}}\,\beta(\vec{r}^{\prime};t^{\prime})\vec{v}(\vec{r}^{\prime};t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

This system of equations describes viscoelastic processes and takes into account the spatial fractality of the system and memory effects in the generalized particle viscosity coefficient
\overleftrightarrow{\eta}(\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime};t,t^{\prime})
in the Gibbs statistics.
The spatial fractality of the system obviously affects the processes of particle transport,
which can appear as temporal multifractality with characteristic relaxation times.
It is known that non-equilibrium correlation functions
\overleftrightarrow{\eta}(\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime};t,t^{\prime})
cannot be calculated exactly,
so certain approximations are used based on physical considerations.
In the time interval
-\infty\div t
,
particle transport processes in a spatially heterogeneous system can be characterized by a set of relaxation times,
which are related to the nature of particles interaction with particles of a medium with a fractal structure.
To reveal temporal multifractality in the generalized Navier–Stokes equation (
25
),
we use the following approximation for the generalized particle viscosity coefficient:

\overleftrightarrow{\eta}(\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime};t,t^{\prime})=W_{\eta}(t,t^{\prime})\overleftrightarrow{\eta}(\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime}),

where
W_{\eta}(t,t^{\prime})
can be defined as the function of memory in time.
Taking this into account,
the equation (
25
) can be presented in the form:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\langle\hat{\vec{p}}(\vec{r})\rangle^{t}+D^{\alpha}_{\vec{r}}:\langle\overleftrightarrow{T}(\vec{r})\rangle_{\alpha,rel}^{t}=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\varepsilon(t^{\prime}-t)}W_{\eta}(t,t^{\prime})(t,t^{\prime})\bar{\eta}(\vec{r};t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(26)

where

\bar{\eta}(\vec{r};t^{\prime})=\int d\mu_{\alpha}(\vec{r}^{\prime})D^{\alpha}_{\vec{r}}:\overleftrightarrow{\eta}(\vec{r},\vec{r}):D^{\alpha}_{\vec{r}^{\prime}}\,\beta\vec{v}(\vec{r}^{\prime};t^{\prime}).

Next,
we apply the Fourier transform to equation (
26
),
as a result,
in the frequency image we get:

i\omega\langle\hat{\vec{p}}(\vec{r})\rangle^{\omega}+D^{\alpha}_{\vec{r}}:\langle\overleftrightarrow{T}(\vec{r})\rangle_{\alpha,rel}^{\omega}=W_{\eta}(\omega)\bar{\eta}(\vec{r};\omega).

(27)

We present the frequency dependence of the memory function in the form:

W_{\eta}(\omega)=\frac{1}{(i\omega)^{\xi-1}},\quad 0<\xi\leqslant 1.

Then equation (
27
) can be presented in the form:

(i\omega)^{1-\xi}i\omega\langle\hat{\vec{p}}(\vec{r})\rangle^{\omega}+(i\omega)^{1-\xi}D^{\alpha}_{\vec{r}}:\langle\overleftrightarrow{T}(\vec{r})\rangle_{\alpha,rel}^{\omega}=(i\omega)^{1-\xi}\bar{\eta}(\vec{r};\omega).

(28)

Next,
we will use the Fourier transform to fractional derivatives of functions:

L\left(\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scriptsize 0}D_{t}^{1-\xi}f(t);i\omega\right)=(i\omega)^{1-\xi}L(f(t);i\omega),

where

\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scriptsize 0}D_{t}^{1-\xi}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\xi)}\frac{d}{dt}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{f(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{1-\xi}}\,d\tau

is the Riemann–Liouville fractional derivative.
Using it,
the inverse transform in equation (
28
) to the time dependence gives the generalized equation of the Navier–Stokes type for describing viscoelastic processes taking into account spatial fractality:

\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scriptsize 0}D_{t}^{\xi}\big\langle\hat{\vec{p}}(\vec{r})\big\rangle^{t}+\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scriptsize 0}D_{t}^{\xi-1}D^{\alpha}_{\vec{r}}:\big\langle\overleftrightarrow{T}(\vec{r})\big\rangle_{\alpha,rel}^{t}=\int d\mu_{\alpha}(\vec{r}^{\prime})D^{\alpha}_{\vec{r}}:\overleftrightarrow{\eta}(\vec{r},\vec{r}):D^{\alpha}_{\vec{r}^{\prime}}\,\beta\vec{v}(\vec{r}^{\prime};t).

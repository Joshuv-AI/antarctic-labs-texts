Multiple normalized solutions to a system of nonlinear Schrödinger equations
Jarosław Mederski, Andrzej Szulkin

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Consider the system of time-dependent nonlinear Schrödinger equations of the form

\mathrm{i}\frac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial t}-\Delta\Phi_{i}=g_{i}(\Phi),\quad i=1,\ldots,K.

In this paper we look for solutions which are normalized by prescribing the
L^{2}
-bounds for
\Phi=(\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{K})
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\Phi_{i}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,dx=\rho_{i}^{2}\quad\hbox{for }i=1,\ldots,K.

Here
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{K})\in(0,\infty)^{K}
represents the total number of atoms in Bose–Einstein condensation
[
31
,
17
]
or
the power supply in nonlinear optics
[
12
]
. For a particular power-type nonlinearity in three dimensions,
seeking for solitary wave solutions of the form
\Phi_{i}(x,t)=u_{i}(x)e^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{i}t}
leads to the
system of time-independent Schrödinger equations

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta u_{i}+\lambda_{i}u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\beta_{i,j}u_{i}|u_{i}|^{p/2-2}|u_{j}|^{p/2}\quad\mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
u_{i}\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{i}|^{2}\,dx=\rho_{i}^{2}\quad\text{for }i=1,\ldots,K,\end{array}\right.,

where we assume
\beta_{i,i}>0
and
\beta_{i,j}=\beta_{j,i}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i,j=1,\ldots,K
.
Here

(\lambda,u)=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{K},u_{1},\ldots,u_{K})\in\mathbb{R}^{K}\times H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}

are the unknown and
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}
is the subspace of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}
whose all components are radial.
Note that this covers the most important example from the physical point of view which is
p=4
; see
[
12
,
31
,
15
,
28
]
and the references therein. We look for solutions in
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}
and not in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}
in order to get some additional compactness for the functional
J
defined below.

Recall that Kwong
[
25
]
showed that there exists a unique positive radial solution to the equation

-\Delta u+u=u^{p-1}.

After the rescaling

u_{\beta_{1,1}}:=\alpha u(\gamma\cdot),

for suitable
\alpha,\gamma>0
this gives a unique positive radial solution to (
1.1
) with
K=1
.
In general, if
K\geq 2
, a scaling-type argument fails.
It is well known that weak solutions to (
1.1
) are critical points of the energy functional
J|_{\mathcal{S}}
given by

J(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq K}\beta_{i,j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{i}|^{p/2}|u_{j}|^{p/2}\,dx,\quad u\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}

where

(1.2)

{\mathcal{S}}:=\left\{u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{K})\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}\ :\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{i}|^{2}\,dx=\rho_{i}^{2}\quad\text{for }i=1,\ldots,K\right\}.

The constants
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
in (
1.1
) appear as the Lagrange multipliers. Similarly as for one equation, a global minimizer for
J
restricted to
{\mathcal{S}}
can only exist in the
L^{2}
-subcritical case (
2<p<\frac{10}{3}
), and in the
L^{2}
-critical case (
p=\frac{10}{3}
) provided that
\rho_{i}
are sufficiently small
[
38
,
29
,
34
,
10
]
. If
p>\frac{10}{3}
, then
\inf_{\mathcal{S}}J=-\infty
and the purpose of this work is to construct a minimax theory for
J
on
{\mathcal{S}}
. Therefore, from now on we assume that
\frac{10}{3}<p<6
.

Let us recall that every critical point of the functional above belongs to
W^{2,q}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}
for all
q<\infty
and satisfies the Pohožaev
[
11
]
and the Nehari identities

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx=\frac{6}{p}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq K}\beta_{i,j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{i}|^{p/2}|u_{j}|^{p/2}\,dx-3\sum_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{i}|^{2}\,dx

and

J^{\prime}(u)[u]+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{i}|^{2}\,dx=0.

By a linear combination of the two equalities above it is easily checked that every solution
u
satisfies the
Nehari-Pohožaev identity

(1.3)

M(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{3(p-2)}{2p}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq K}\beta_{i,j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{i}|^{p/2}|u_{j}|^{p/2}\,dx=0.

In a usual way (cf. e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
32
]
) we introduce the constraint

(1.4)

{\mathcal{M}}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}\setminus\{0\}:M(u)=0\right\}

which contains all nontrivial solutions to (
1.1
) and does not depend on
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{K})
.

In the case of two equations Bartsch, Jeanjean and Soave proved in
[
3
]
that for
p=4
and every sufficiently small or sufficiently large
\beta:=\beta_{1,2}=\beta_{2,1}>0
there exists a solution
(u,\lambda)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times(0,\infty)^{2}
to (
1.1
). See also
[
7
]
for an extension and
[
2
,
26
]
for more general exponents, or
[
4
,
5
]
for
\beta<0
. Moreover, each component of
u
is positive, radial, radially nonincreasing, of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
and

J(u)=\inf_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap{\mathcal{M}}}J

provided that
\beta>0
is sufficiently large – see
[
32
]
where also a more general nonlinearity has been considered.

While there are several multiplicity results concerning normalized solutions for a single equation (under different assumptions on the right-hand side), the problem of multiple normalized solutions to (
1.1
) with
K\geq 2
has been little investigated. To our knowledge there are only a few results, even in the case
p=4
which is most interesting from the point of view of physics. Here we can mention that
a second positive solution to (
1.1
) has been found by Gou and Jeanjean
[
19
]
for
\beta>0
small and
K=2
. Next, Bartsch and Soave
[
6
]
observed that the system (
1.1
) with
K=2
is invariant with respect to the involution
(u_{1},u_{2})\mapsto(u_{2},u_{1})
provided that
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}
,
\beta_{1,1}=\beta_{2,2}
and
p=4
. Inspired by
[
14
]
they showed that for any
\beta<-\beta_{1,1}=-\beta_{2,2}
the problem (
1.1
) has infinitely many pairs
(u_{1},u_{2})
,
(u_{2},u_{1})
(
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
) of positive radial solutions in this particular situation.

The main purpose of this work is to show that if
K=2
and
m\geq 1
, then for
\beta_{1,2}=\beta_{2,1}
large enough there exist at least
m
distinct solutions to (
1.1
). We impose no restrictions on
\rho_{i}
and
\beta_{i,i}>0
.

We call a solution to (
1.1
) a
ground state
if it minimizes
J
on
{\mathcal{S}}\cap{\mathcal{M}}
.

Now we state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
4\leq p<6
and suppose that
K=2
. For any
m\geq 1
there is a constant
\beta_{m}>0
such that if

\beta:=\beta_{1,2}=\beta_{2,1}>\beta_{m}
,
then (
1.1
) either has infinitely many solutions or at least
m
solutions
u^{j}\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}
such that
J(u^{1})<J(u^{2})<\cdots<J(u^{m})
. One of these solutions has all components positive and is a ground state.

Note that the system (
1.1
) is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{K}
-invariant where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}:=\{\pm 1\}
, i.e. if
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{K})
is a solution to (
1.1
), then so are
(\pm u_{1},\ldots,\pm u_{K})
(any combination of signs). These solutions will be called the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{K}
-
orbit
(or simply the
orbit
) of
u
. In particular, there are at least
m
different
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{2}
-orbits of solutions in Theorem
1.1
.

For general systems with
K\geq 2
and
\beta_{i,j}>0
we are only able to prove the existence of one solution under rather restrictive assumptions.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
4\leq p\leq\frac{14}{3}
and
K\geq 2
. If

(1.5)

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathcal{I}}\subset\{1,\ldots,K\},\\
1\leq|{\mathcal{I}}|\leq K-1\end{subarray}}\Big(\max_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\{\beta_{i,i}\rho_{i}^{\frac{6-p}{2}}\}+\frac{|{\mathcal{I}}|-1}{|{\mathcal{I}}|^{\frac{3p-10}{4}}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in{\mathcal{I}}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\big\{\beta_{i,j}(\rho_{i}\rho_{j})^{\frac{6-p}{4}}\big\}\Big)<\frac{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq K}\beta_{i,j}(\rho_{i}\rho_{j})^{p/2}}{\big(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\rho_{i}^{2}\big)^{\frac{3}{4}(p-2)}}

and
\beta_{i,j}>0
for all
i,j
, then (
1.1
) has a solution
u\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}
such that
J(u)=\inf_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap{\mathcal{M}}}J
. So
u
is a ground state and we may assume all its components are positive.

Here
|{\mathcal{I}}|
is the cardinality of the set
{\mathcal{I}}
. Note that if
K=2
, then
|{\mathcal{I}}|-1=0
and (
1.5
) holds for
\beta:=\beta_{1,2}=\beta_{2,1}
sufficiently large.
For general
K\geq 2
the following is true.

Proposition 1.3
.

A sufficient condition for (
1.5
) to hold is that
4\leq p\leq\frac{14}{3}
,
\rho_{i}=\rho
for all
i
,
\beta_{i,j}=\beta
for all
i\neq j
and
\beta
is sufficiently large.

Proof.

If
\rho_{i}=\rho
and
\beta_{i,j}=\beta
, then (
1.5
) can be re-written as

(1.6)

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathcal{I}}\subset\{1,\ldots,K\},\\
1\leq|{\mathcal{I}}|\leq K-1\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}\max_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\{\beta_{i,i}\}+\frac{|{\mathcal{I}}|-1}{|{\mathcal{I}}|^{\frac{3p-10}{4}}}\Big)<\frac{\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\beta_{i,i}+K(K-1)}{K^{\frac{3}{4}(p-2)}}.

So the conclusion will follow if we can show that
(K-2)K^{\frac{3}{4}(p-2)}(K-1)^{-\frac{3p-10}{4}}<K(K-1)
, i.e.

(1.7)

(K-2)K^{\frac{3p-10}{4}}<(K-1)^{\frac{3p-6}{4}}.

Note that here we have replaced
|{\mathcal{I}}|
in (
1.6
) with
K-1
because the function
t\mapsto(t-1)/t^{\frac{3p-10}{4}}
is increasing for
t>0
.
By the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means we get

(K-2)^{\frac{4}{3p-6}}K^{\frac{3p-10}{3p-6}}<\frac{4}{3p-6}(K-2)+\frac{3p-10}{3p-6}K\leq K-1

where the second inequality is a consequence of the assumption
p\leq\frac{14}{3}
. Clearly, this implies (
1.7
).
∎

Remark 1.4
.

We would like to mention that a variant of the condition (
1.5
) has appeared in
[
3
]
for a system of
K
equations in the particular case
p=4
. However,
[
3
, Lemma 5.3]
is not true as stated. It will become true after replacing
the condition
[
3
, (1.14)]
appearing in
[

with

\frac{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{2}\Big)^{3}}{\Big(\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\beta_{ij}a_{i}^{2}a_{j}^{2}\Big)^{2}}<\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathcal{I}}\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}\\

Under this condition
[
3
, Theorem 1.5]
is a special case of Theorem
1.2
in the present paper.

As will be seen later, we shall give a variant of condition (
1.5
) which implies the existence of at least
m
solutions

for any

(see Lemma

below). However, if

, we are able to check this condition only for

and it is an open question whether Theorem

can be generalized for systems with at least three equations.

Even for the system with
K=2
one encounters a considerable difficulty in the variational approach because not all Palais-Smale sequences need to converge strongly. In

, if

is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence, then

after passing to a subsequence and one can show

. However, since the embedding of

into

is not compact,

may not have the

-norms as required. In many cases the strong convergence can be shown at the ground state level but it is unclear what happens at higher levels. The only paper we know of where this issue could be dealt with is the already mentioned
[
6
]
. Unfortunately, the approach used there cannot be carried over to more general situations.

In order to get multiple normalized solutions to (
1.1
) we study the energy functional
J|_{\mathcal{S}}
and the crucial step is to show that each Palais-Smale sequence at
c
which approaches
{\mathcal{M}}
either has a convergent subsequence or converges weakly to a semitrivial solution of the equations in (

), see Theorem
1.5
below. The proof is based on the Morse index, Cwikel-Lieb-Rozenblum theorem
[
13
,
27
,
33
]
and a Liouville-type result in an exterior domain (see Lemma
A.1
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
c>0
and
4\leq p<6
.
Let
(u^{n})\subset{\mathcal{S}}
be a sequence such that
J(u^{n})\to c
,
J|_{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\prime}(u^{n})\to 0
and
M(u^{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Then
(u^{n})
is bounded, so
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{K})
after passing to a subsequence,
J(u)=c
(hence
u\neq 0
) and for each
i=1,\ldots,K
either
u_{i}=0
or
u_{i}^{n}\to u_{i}
in
H_{rad}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

We emphasize that although in this paper we mainly focus on the case
\beta_{i,j}>0
, the result above holds true regardless of the signs of
\beta_{i,j}
for
i\neq j
.

In
[
4
]
it has been shown that for
K=2
,
p=4
and
\beta<0
the system (
1.1
) has a solution which is of mountain pass type. The same case has also been considered in
[
22
]
where (among other things) a different and simpler argument was given. A natural question to ask is whether there also exists a ground state. We show in the next theorem that this is not the case.
Let

be the unique positive solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta u+\lambda u=\beta_{i,i}u^{p-1}\quad\mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
u\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}\,dx=\rho_{i}^{2}.\end{array}\right.

As before,
\lambda
appears as a Lagrange multiplier.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\frac{10}{3}<p<6
and suppose that
K\geq 2
and
\beta_{i,j}<0

. Then

\inf_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap{\mathcal{M}}}J=\min\big\{J(0,\ldots,u_{\beta_{i,i}},\dots,0):\;i=1,\ldots,K\big\}

and (

in (

)
(

)
.

Note that if

are prescribed and positive while

are free, then there are no ground state solutions in

if

and all

,

.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

In order to study the compactness of Palais-Smale sequences of
J
on
{\mathcal{S}}
we apply the Cwikel-Lieb-Rozenblum theorem
[
13
,
27
,
33
]
. We also need a Liouville-type lemma outside a large ball. More precisely,
suppose that the potential
V
in the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfies
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)|x|^{2}=0
. If
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and there is
\rho>0
such that
u\geq 0
and
-\Delta u+V(x)u\geq 0
for
|x|\geq\rho
, then

\inf\{u(x):|x|=M\}=0
for any
M\geq\rho
(so in particular,
u(x)=0
for
|x|\geq\rho
if
u\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
). A more general version of this result is proved in Appendix
A
since we believe it can be interesting in itself. It extends the results in
[
4
,
21
]
.

The main novelty here is that we show that regardless of the signs of
\beta_{i,j}
for
i\neq j
, if

\lambda_{i}=0
, then
u_{i}=0
also when we a priori do not know whether

u_{i}\geq 0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.
First of all, note that
M(u^{n})\to 0
implies

(2.1)

\frac{3p-10}{6(p-2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u^{n}|^{2}\,dx=J(u^{n})-\frac{2}{3(p-2)}M(u^{n})\to c>0

and hence
(u^{n})
is bounded in
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}
.
Therefore we may assume
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{K})
in
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}
and
u^{n}\to u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{K}
.
Note also that

J^{\prime}(u^{n})+\lambda^{n}u^{n}=o(1)

for some Lagrange multipliers
\lambda^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
(here
\lambda^{n}u^{n}
should be understood as an element of the dual space of
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
). It follows that

(2.2)

-\lambda^{n}_{i}\rho_{i}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u^{n}_{i}|^{2}\,dx-\sum_{1\leq j\leq K}\beta_{i,j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u^{n}_{i}|^{p/2}|u^{n}_{j}|^{p/2}\,dx+o(1),

so
(\lambda^{n})
is bounded and passing to a subsequence,
\lambda^{n}\to\lambda
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
. Observe that

J^{\prime}(u)+\lambda u=0

and as a solution,
u
satisfies the Nehari-Pohožaev identity (
1.3
).
Thus
M(u)=0
and therefore

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|\nabla u^{n}|^{2}-|\nabla u|^{2})\,dx=\frac{3(p-2)}{2p}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq K}\beta_{i,j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|u^{n}_{i}|^{p/2}|u_{j}^{n}|^{p/2}-|u_{i}|^{p/2}|u_{j}|^{p/2})\,dx+o(1)\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.
So
J(u)=c
and in particular,
u\neq 0
.

We shall show that if
u_{i}\neq 0
, then
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
. Arguing by contradiction, suppose
\lambda_{1}=0
and
u_{1}\neq 0
. Then
\partial_{u_{1}}J(u)=0
. Let

(\Omega_{k})_{k=1}^{m}
be the sequence of nodal domains of
u_{1}
and let
u_{1,k}:=u_{1}
on
\Omega_{k}
and
u_{1,k}=0

\alpha\geq\beta>0
where
\beta
replaces 2 on the right-hand side).

In order to prove (
4.4
), note that

\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq m}a_{i}a_{j}\leq(m-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{2}=(m-1)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\Big)^{2}-(m-1)\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq m}a_{i}a_{j}.

Hence

\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq m}a_{i}a_{j}\leq\frac{m-1}{m}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\Big)^{2}

and

\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq m}a_{i}a_{j}\leq m(m-1)\Big(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\Big)^{2}\leq m(m-1)\Big(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{q}\Big)^{2/q}

where the last inequality is a special case of
[
20
, (2.10.4)]
.
∎

Lemma 4.2

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
m\geq 1
,

c_{m}(\boldsymbol{\beta})<c_{0}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathcal{I}}\subset\{1,\ldots,K\}\\

s_{u}^{\frac{3p-10}{2}}=\frac{|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}}{\frac{3(p-2)}{2p}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq K}\beta_{i,j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{i}|^{p/2}|u_{j}|^{p/2}\,dx}

5.
Nonexistence of ground states

Denote
J_{i}(v):=J(0,\ldots,v,\ldots,0)
and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Big\{u\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}\,dx=\rho_{i}^{2}\Big\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\big\{u\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):M_{i}(u):=M_{i}(0,\dots,u,\ldots,0)=0\big\}

for
i=1,\ldots,K
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.
We prove the nonexistence of radial ground states. The proof in the non-radial case is exactly the same except that in the definitions of
{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{S}}_{i},{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
one needs to replace
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

As we have mentioned earlier, by the uniqueness result of Kwong
[
25
]
the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta u+\lambda u=\beta_{i,i}|u|^{p-2}u,\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u^{2}\,dx=\rho_{i}^{2}\end{array}\right.

has a unique positive ground state solution
u_{i}:=u_{\beta_{i,i}}
which is in
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Thus

J(u_{i})=\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{i}}J_{i}.

Denote
c_{i}=J_{i}(u_{i})
and assume without loss of generality that
c_{1}\leq c_{i}
for all
i
. We claim that
c_{1}
is the ground state level for (
1.1
) and that it is not attained. To prove this we first show that no level
c\leq c_{1}
is attained by
J
on
{\mathcal{M}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}
and then exhibit a sequence
(v^{n})\subset{\mathcal{M}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}
such that
J(v^{n})\to c_{1}
.

Choose
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{K})\in{\mathcal{M}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}
. Then (cf. (
1.3
))

0=M(w)\geq M_{1}(w_{1})+\cdots+M_{K}(w_{K})

because
\beta_{i,j}<0
for all
i\neq j
.
Hence at least one of
M_{i}(w_{i})
is
\leq 0
. Using (
2.1
),
M(w)=0
and the equality
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla(s\star w)|^{2}\,dx=s^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx
, we obtain

J(s_{w}\star w)=\frac{3p-10}{6(p-2)}s_{w}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx=\frac{3p-10}{6(p-2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|\nabla w_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|\nabla w_{K}|^{2})\,dx

(
s_{w}=1
because
w\in{\mathcal{M}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}
). Likewise,

J_{i}(s_{w_{i}}\star w_{i})=\frac{3p-10}{6(p-2)}s_{w_{i}}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla w_{i}|^{2}\,dx,\quad i=1,\ldots,K.

Note that in order that
s_{w}
and
s_{w_{i}}
exist it is necessary to have
p>\frac{10}{3}
.
Choose
i
with
M_{i}(w_{i})\leq 0
. Since
M_{i}(s\star w_{i})>0
for
s<s_{w_{i}}
and
<0
for
s>s_{w_{i}}
, we have
s_{w_{i}}\leq 1
and since
s_{w_{i}}\star w_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{i}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
,

\displaystyle J(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{3p-10}{6(p-2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx>\frac{3p-10}{6(p-2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla w_{i}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3p-10}{6(p-2)}s_{w_{i}}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla w_{i}|^{2}\,dx\geq c_{i}\geq c_{1}.

So no level
c\leq c_{1}
is attained by
J
on
{\mathcal{M}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}
.

Let
u^{n}:=(u_{1},s_{n}\star u_{2},\ldots,s_{n}\star u_{K})
where
s_{n}\to 0^{+}
. Then
u^{n}\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
|\nabla(s_{n}\star u_{i})|_{2}\to 0
and
|s_{n}\star u_{i}|_{p}\to 0
for
2\leq i\leq K
. Set
u_{1}^{n}:=u_{1}
and
u_{i}^{n}:=s_{n}\star u_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq K
. Since
|u_{i}^{n}|_{p}\to 0
for
2\leq i\leq K
,

\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq K}\beta_{i,j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{i}^{n}|^{p/2}|u_{j}^{n}|^{p/2}\,dx>0

for
n
large enough. For such
n
,
s_{u^{n}}
exists,
s_{u^{n}}\star u^{n}\in{\mathcal{M}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}
and using (
3.1
) we obtain

s_{u^{n}}^{\frac{3p-10}{2}}=\frac{2p}{3(p-2)}\,\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u^{n}|^{2}\,dx}{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq K}\beta_{i,j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{i}^{n}|^{p/2}|u_{j}^{n}|^{p/2}\,dx}\to\frac{2p}{3(p-2)}\,\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}\,dx}{\beta_{1,1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{1}|^{p}\,dx}=s_{u_{1}}^{\frac{3p-10}{2}}=1

Appendix A
A Liouville-type lemma

Let
V\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\rho))
and let
A:=-\Delta+V
be a Schrödinger operator on
L^{2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B(0,\rho)}\big)
. We assume
V
satisfies the condition

(V)

V(x)\leq\frac{N(4-N)}{4}|x|^{-2}
for
|x|\geq\rho
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
N\in\{3,4\}
.
Suppose that
Au\geq 0
,
u\geq 0
and
(V)
hold for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\rho)
. Then

\inf\{u(x):|x|=M\}=0
for any
M\geq\rho
. If, in addition,
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B(0,\rho)})
, then
u=0
.

Note that if
N=4
, then
V^{+}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\rho)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
c:=\inf\{u(x):|x|=M\}>0
for some
M\geq\rho
. We may assume
M=\rho
.
Let
v(x):=c\rho^{\alpha}|x|^{-\alpha}
, and note that

\int_{|x|\geq\rho}|v|^{2}\,dx=\infty
,
\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{R\leq|x|\leq R+1}|v|^{2}\,dx=0
and
\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{R\leq|x|\leq R+1}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx=0
provided
\frac{N-1}{2}<\alpha\leq\frac{N}{2}
. Choosing
\alpha=\frac{N}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle(-\Delta+V^{+})v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\rho^{\alpha}\big(-\alpha(\alpha+2-N)|x|^{-\alpha-2}+V^{+}(x)|x|^{-\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\rho^{\alpha}\Big(-\alpha(\alpha+2-N)+\frac{N(4-N)}{4}\Big)|x|^{-\alpha-2}=0.

Therefore
(-\Delta+V^{+})(u-v)\geq Au-(-\Delta+V^{+})v\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\rho)
and
u-v\geq 0
for
|x|=\rho
. Let
R>\rho
and
\eta_{R}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a nonnegative function such that
|\nabla\eta_{R}|\leq C
,
\eta_{R}(x)=1
for
x\in B(0,R)
and
\eta_{R}(x)=0
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,R+1)
. Testing the inequality
(-\Delta+V^{+})(u-v)\geq 0
with
(u-v)^{-}\eta_{R}\leq 0
, we obtain

(-\Delta+V^{+})(u-v)(u-v)^{-}\eta_{R}\leq 0

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\rho)
. Hence

(A.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\rho)}\big(|\nabla(u-v)^{-}|^{2}+V^{+}(x)|(u-v)^{-}|^{2}\big)\eta_{R}\,dx\leq-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\rho)}(\nabla(u-v)^{-}\nabla\eta_{R})(u-v)^{-}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\rho)}\big(|\nabla(u-v)^{-}|^{2}+|(u-v)^{-}|^{2}\big)|\nabla\eta_{R}|\,dx.

Since
\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{R\leq|x|\leq R+1}|v|^{2}\,dx=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{R\leq|x|\leq R+1}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx=0
, we obtain

(A.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\rho)}\big(|\nabla(u-v)^{-}|^{2}+|(u-v)^{-}|^{2}\big)|\nabla\eta_{R}|\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\int_{B(0,R+1)\setminus B(0,R)}|\nabla(u-v)|^{2}+|u-v|^{2}\,dx\to 0\text{ as }R\to\infty.

It follows from (
A.1
), (
A.2
) and the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\rho)}|\nabla(u-v)^{-}|^{2}\,dx=0,

so
c_{1}:=(u-v)^{-}
is constant for
|x|>\rho
. Note that

u-v=(u-v)^{+}+c_{1}\geq c_{1}
, thus
u\geq v+c_{1}
and

\int_{|x|\geq\rho}|u|^{2}\,dx\geq\int_{|x|\geq\rho}|v+c_{1}|^{2}\,dx=\infty.

We get a contradiction since
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Therefore
\inf\{u(x):|x|=M\}=0
for any
M\geq\rho
, hence
u\geq 0
. If
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B(0,\rho)})
, then
u=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B(0,\rho)}
by the maximum principle.
∎

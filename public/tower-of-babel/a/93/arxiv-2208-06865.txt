Resolution Guarantees for the Reconstruction of Inclusions in Linear Elasticity Based on Monotonicity Methods
Sarah Eberle, Bastian Harrach

Abstract
We deal with the reconstruction of inclusions in elastic bodies based on monotonicity methods and construct conditions under which a resolution for a given partition can be achieved. These conditions take into account the background error as well as the measurement noise. As a main result, this shows us that the resolution guarantees depend heavily on the Lamé parameter μ \mu and only marginally on λ \lambda .



Keywords
:
resolution guarantees, inverse problem, linear elasticity, detection and reconstruction of inclusions, monotonicity methods

1
Introduction

In this paper we deal with the detection and reconstruction of inclusions in elastic bodies based on monotonicity methods, where the main focus lies on the so-called "resolution guarantees". Our results are of special importance, when considering simulations based on real data.

Before we introduce the definition of the resolution guarantees we state the setting.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2
or
3
) be a bounded and connected open set
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega=\Gamma=\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}
,
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\cap\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}=\emptyset
,
where
\Gamma_{\textup{D}}
and
\Gamma_{\textup{N}}
are the corresponding Dirichlet and Neumann boundaries.

We assume that
\Gamma_{\textup{D}}
and
\Gamma_{\textup{N}}
are relatively open and connected.
Further,
\Omega
is divided into
N
disjoint open subsets
(\omega_{s})_{s=1,\ldots,N}
, i.e. a pixel partition, such that

\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{s=1,\ldots,N}\overline{\omega_{s}}
.

We base our considerations on the work
[
16
]
, where the resolution guarantees for the electrical impedance tomography (EIT) problem were considered.

Definition 1
.

An inclusion detection method that yields a reconstruction
\mathcal{D}_{R}
to the true inclusion
\mathcal{D}
is
said to fulfill a resolution guarantee w.r.t. a partition
(\omega_{s})_{s=1,\ldots,N}\subseteq\Omega
if

(i)

\omega_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
implies
\omega_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{R}
for
s\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}

(i.e. every element that is covered by the inclusion will be marked in the reconstruction),

(ii)

\mathcal{D}=\emptyset
implies
\mathcal{D}_{R}=\emptyset

(i.e. if there is no inclusion then no element will be marked in the reconstruction).

Remark 1
.

Obviously, a reconstruction guarantee will not hold true for arbitrarily fine partitions. The achievable resolution will
depend on the number of applied boundary forces, the inclusion contrast, the background error and the measurement noise.

We aim to construct conditions under which a resolution for a given partition can be achieved. These conditions take into account the background error
as well as the measurement noise. Thus, we show that it is generally possible to give rigorous guarantees in linear elasticity.

2
Problem Statement and Setting

We take a look at the continuous and discrete setting and introduce the corresponding problems as well the required assumptions concerning the inclusion, the background as well as the measurement error.

2.1
Continuous case

We start with the introduction of the problems of interest, e.g., the
direct
as well as the
inverse problem

of stationary linear elasticity.

For the following, we define

\displaystyle L_{+}^{\infty}(\Omega):=\{w\in L^{\infty}(\Omega):\underset{x\in\Omega}{\text{ess\,inf}}\,w(x)>0\}.

Let
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the displacement vector,
\mu,\lambda:\Omega\to L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega)
the Lamé parameters,

\hat{\nabla}u=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{T}\right)
the symmetric gradient,
n
is
the normal

vector pointing outside of
\Omega
,
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d}
the boundary force and
I
the
d\times d
-identity matrix.
We define the divergence of a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
via

\nabla\cdot A=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\dfrac{\partial A_{ij}}{\partial x_{j}}e_{i}
, where
e_{i}
is a unit vector
and
x_{j}
a component of a vector from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The boundary value problem of linear elasticity (
direct problem
) is

that
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}
solves

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(\lambda(\nabla\cdot u)I+2\mu\hat{\nabla}u\right)

\displaystyle=0\quad\mathrm{in}\,\,\Omega,

(1)

\displaystyle\left(\lambda(\nabla\cdot u)I+2\mu\hat{\nabla}u\right)n

\displaystyle=g\quad\mathrm{on}\,\,\Gamma_{\textup{N}},

(2)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\quad\mathrm{on}\,\,\Gamma_{\textup{D}}.

(3)

From a physical point of view, this means that we deal with an elastic test body
\Omega
which is fixed (zero displacement)
at
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
(Dirichlet condition) and apply a force
g
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
(Neumann condition).
This results in the displacement
u
, which is measured on the boundary
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
.

The equivalent weak formulation of the boundary value problem (
1
)-(
3
) is
that
u\in\mathcal{V}
fulfills

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}2\mu\,\hat{\nabla}u:\hat{\nabla}v+\lambda\nabla\cdot u\,\nabla\cdot v\,dx=\int_{\Gamma_{\textup{N}}}g\cdot v\,ds\quad\text{ for all }v\in\mathcal{V},

(4)

where

\mathcal{V}:=\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}:\,v_{|_{\Gamma_{\textup{D}}}}=0\right\}
.

We want to remark that for
\lambda,\mu\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega)
, the existence and uniqueness of a solution to the variational formulation (
4
) can be shown by
the Lax-Milgram theorem (see e.g., in
[
4
]
).

Neumann-to-Dirichlet operator and its monotonicity properties

Measuring boundary displacements that result from applying forces to
\Gamma_{\textup{N}}
can be modeled by the
Neumann-to-Dirichlet operator
\Lambda(\lambda,\mu)
defined by

\displaystyle\Lambda(\lambda,\mu):L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d}\rightarrow L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d}:\quad g\mapsto u_{|_{\Gamma_{\textup{N}}}},

where
u\in\mathcal{V}
solves (
1
)-(
3
).

This operator is self-adjoint, compact and linear

(see Corollary 1.1 from
[
5
]
).
Its associated bilinear form is given by

\displaystyle\langle g,\Lambda(\lambda,\mu)h\rangle=\int_{\Omega}2\mu\,\hat{\nabla}u^{g}_{(\lambda,\mu)}:\hat{\nabla}u^{h}_{(\lambda,\mu)}+\lambda\nabla\cdot u^{g}_{(\lambda,\mu)}\,\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{(\lambda,\mu)}\,dx,

(5)

where
u_{(\lambda,\mu)}^{g}
solves the problem (
1
)-(
3
) and
u_{(\lambda,\mu)}^{h}

the corresponding problem with boundary force
h\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}})^{d}
.

Another important property of
\Lambda(\lambda,\mu)
is its Fréchet differentiability (for the corresponding proof
see
Lemma 2.3 in

[
5
]
).
For directions
\hat{\lambda},\hat{\mu}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the derivative

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda,\mu)(\hat{\lambda},\hat{\mu}):L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d}\rightarrow L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d}

is the self-adjoint compact linear operator associated to the bilinear form

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda,\mu)(\hat{\lambda},\hat{\mu})g,h\rangle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}2\hat{\mu}\,\hat{\nabla}u^{g}_{(\lambda,\mu)}:\hat{\nabla}u^{h}_{(\lambda,\mu)}+\hat{\lambda}\nabla\cdot u^{g}_{(\lambda,\mu)}\,\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{(\lambda,\mu)}\,dx.

Note that for
\hat{\lambda}_{0},\hat{\lambda}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{0},\hat{\mu}_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with

\hat{\lambda}_{0}\leq\hat{\lambda}_{1}\text{ and }\hat{\mu}_{0}\leq\hat{\mu}_{1}

we obviously have

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda,\mu)(\hat{\lambda}_{0},\hat{\mu}_{0})\geq\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda,\mu)(\hat{\lambda}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{1}),

(6)

in the sense of
quadratic forms
.

The
inverse problem
we consider here is the following:

\displaystyle\text{ \it Find the support of }(\lambda-\lambda_{0},\mu-\mu_{0})^{T}\text{ \it knowing the Neumann-to-Dirichlet operator }\Lambda(\lambda,\mu).

2.2
Discrete case

Next, we go over to the discrete case and take a look at the bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with piecewise smooth boundary representing the elastic object.
Further on, let
\lambda,\mu:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be the real valued Lamé parameter distribution inside
\Omega
.

We apply the forces
g_{l}
on the Neumann boundary of the object, where the location of their support is denoted by

\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}^{(l)}\subseteq\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
,
l=1,\ldots,M
. We assume that the patches are disjoint. Thus, the discrete boundary value problem is given by

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(\lambda(\nabla\cdot u)I+2\mu\hat{\nabla}u\right)

\displaystyle=0\,\,\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega,

(7)

\displaystyle\left(\lambda(\nabla\cdot u)I+2\mu\hat{\nabla}u\right)n

\displaystyle=g_{l}\quad\text{on}\,\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}^{(l)},

(8)

\displaystyle\left(\lambda(\nabla\cdot u)I+2\mu\hat{\nabla}u\right)n

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on}\,\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}^{(i)},\,i\neq l,

(9)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\,\,\quad\text{in}\,\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}.

(10)

The resulting displacement measurements are represented by the discrete version of
\Lambda(\lambda,\mu)
:

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda,\mu)=\left(\Lambda_{l}^{(k)}(\lambda,\mu)\right)_{k,l=1,\ldots,M}

(11)

with

\displaystyle\Lambda_{l}^{(k)}(\lambda,\mu):=\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}^{(l)}}g_{l}\cdot u^{(k)}\,ds

and
u^{(k)}
solves the boundary value problem (
7
)-(
10
) for a boundary load
g_{k}
.

Assumptions regarding the inclusion, the background as well as the measurement error

In the following, we introduce our assumptions and definitions concerning the Lamé parameters for the inclusion and background
including their error considerations.

(a)

Distribution of Lamé parameter
(\lambda(x),\mu(x))
:

\displaystyle(\lambda(x),\mu(x))=\begin{cases}(\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(x),\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(x)),&\quad x\in\mathcal{D},\\
(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(x),\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(x)),&\quad x\in\Omega\setminus\mathcal{D},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{D}
denotes the unknown inclusion and
\mathcal{B}
the background.

(b)

Background error
\epsilon^{\lambda},\epsilon^{\mu}\geq 0
:

\displaystyle|(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(x),\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(x))-(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})|\leq(\lambda_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda},\mu_{0}\epsilon^{\mu})\quad\text{for all}\,x\in\Omega\setminus\mathcal{D},

where the background Lamé parameters
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(x)
and
\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(x)
approximately agree with known positive constants
\lambda_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
.

(c)

Inclusion contrast
c^{\lambda},c^{\mu}>0
:

We distinguish between the following two cases

either
(\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(x),\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(x))-(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})\geq(c^{\lambda},c^{\mu})\,\,\text{for all}\,x\in\mathcal{D}

or
\quad\,\,\,\,(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})-(\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(x),\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(x))\geq(c^{\lambda},c^{\mu})\,\,\text{for all}\,x\in\mathcal{D}
,

where the lower bounds
c^{\lambda}
and
c^{\mu}
are known.

(d)

Measurement noise
\delta\geq 0
:

\displaystyle||{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda,\mu)-{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu)||\leq\delta,

i.e., we assume that
{\bm{\Lambda}(\lambda,\mu)}
is determined up to noise level
\delta>0
.

\displaystyle\geq\langle g,\Lambda(\lambda_{2},\mu_{2})g\rangle-\langle g,\Lambda(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})g\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}2(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})\hat{\nabla}u_{1}:\hat{\nabla}u_{1}+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\nabla\cdot u_{1}\nabla\cdot u_{1}\,dx.

(13)

Lemma 2

(Lemma 2 from
[
5
]
)
.

Let
(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}),(\lambda_{2},\mu_{2})\in L_{+}^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L_{+}^{\infty}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\langle g

\displaystyle,\Lambda(\lambda_{2},\mu_{2})g\rangle-\langle g,\Lambda(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})g\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}2\left(\mu_{2}-\frac{\mu_{2}^{2}}{\mu_{1}}\right)\hat{\nabla}u_{2}:\hat{\nabla}u_{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\left(\lambda_{2}-\frac{\lambda_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\nabla\cdot u_{2}\nabla\cdot u_{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}2\frac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)\hat{\nabla}u_{2}:\hat{\nabla}u_{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\left(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\right)\nabla\cdot u_{2}\nabla\cdot u_{2}\,dx.

(15)

Corollary 1

(Corollary 3.2 from

For

(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})
and the Lamé parameters of the inclusion by

Tests for exact and noisy data

.

Standard monotonicity test (Corollary 2.4 from
[
5
]
)

Let
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

4
Resolution Guarantees

In this section we formulate the algorithms for the monotonicity tests, i.e., the standard monotonicity tests
as well as the linearized tests and follow the considerations in
[
16
]
, where resolution guarantees for EIT were analysed.

4.1
Algorithms

Before we take a look at the algorithms for the reconstruction, we define the corresponding notations which we will use in the following. We set

\displaystyle(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})

\displaystyle:=(\lambda_{0}(1-\epsilon^{\lambda}),\mu_{0}(1-\epsilon^{\mu})),

\displaystyle(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}})

\displaystyle:=(\lambda_{0}(1+\epsilon^{\lambda}),\mu_{0}(1+\epsilon^{\mu})),

\displaystyle(\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{\min}})

\displaystyle:=(\lambda_{0}+c^{\lambda},\mu_{0}+c^{\mu}),

\displaystyle(\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{\max}})

\displaystyle:=(\lambda_{0}-c^{\lambda},\mu_{0}-c^{\mu}),

where the quantities are given in Subsection
2.2
assumptions (a)-(d).

4.1.1
Algorithms for standard monotonicity tests

We now formulate the algorithms for the standard monotonicity tests. We start with the case that

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{\min}}\right)>(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}})

and it holds that

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}-\lambda_{0},\mu_{\mathcal{D}}-\mu_{0}\right)\geq\left(c^{\lambda},c^{\mu}\right).

Algorithm 1
.

Mark each resolution element
\omega_{s}
for which

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)+\delta{\bf I}\geq{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu),\quad s\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\},

where

\displaystyle\tau_{s}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle:=\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus\omega_{s}}+\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{\min}}\chi_{\omega_{s}},

\displaystyle\tau_{s}^{\mu}

\displaystyle:=\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus\omega_{s}}+\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{\min}}\chi_{\omega_{s}}.

Then the reconstruction
\mathcal{D}_{R}
is given by the union of the marked resolution elements.

Further on, we take a look at the special case for "weaker" Lamé parameter inclusions and consider the case

\displaystyle(\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{\max}})<(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}).

(17)

Algorithm 2
.

Mark each resolution element
\omega_{s}
for which

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)-\delta{\bf I}\leq{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu),\quad s\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\},

where

\displaystyle\tau_{s}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle:=\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus\omega_{s}}+\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{\max}}\chi_{\omega_{s}},

\displaystyle\tau_{s}^{\mu}

\displaystyle:=\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus\omega_{s}}+\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{\max}}\chi_{\omega_{s}}.

4.1.2
Algorithms for linearized monotonicity tests

Replacing the monotonicity test for the case

\left(\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{\min}}\right)>(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}})
, i.e.

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)+\delta{\bf I}\geq{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu)

with their linearized approximations yields the linearized monotonicity test

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}\right)+{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\prime}\left(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}\right)(\kappa^{\lambda}\chi_{\omega_{s}},\kappa^{\mu}\chi_{\omega_{s}})+\delta{\bf I}\geq{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu),

where
\kappa^{\lambda},\kappa^{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
is a suitable contrast level defined in the following algorithm. Further, we assume the
\lambda_{\max}
and
\mu_{\max}
are global bounds with

\displaystyle\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\max},

\displaystyle\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\max}

for all
x\in\Omega
.

Algorithm 3
.

Mark each resolution element
\omega_{s}
for which

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{s}+\delta{\bf I}\geq{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu),

where

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{s}:={\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}\right)+{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\prime}\left(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}\right)(\kappa^{\lambda}\chi_{\omega_{s}},\kappa^{\mu}\chi_{\omega_{s}}),

with

\displaystyle\kappa^{\lambda}

\displaystyle:=(c^{\lambda}+\lambda_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda})\dfrac{\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}}{\lambda_{\max}},

(18)

\displaystyle\kappa^{\mu}

\displaystyle:=(c^{\mu}+\mu_{0}\epsilon^{\mu})\dfrac{\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}}{\mu_{\max}}.

(19)

Then the reconstruction
\mathcal{D}_{R}
is given by the union of the marked resolution elements.

As for the standard monotonicity test, we formulate the linearized test for inclusions with weaker Lamé parameter which fulfill
(\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{\max}})<(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})
.

Algorithm 4
.

Mark each resolution element
\omega_{s}
for which

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{s}-\delta{\bf I}\leq{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu),

where

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{s}:={\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}}\right)+{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\prime}\left(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}}\right)(\kappa^{\lambda}\chi_{\omega_{s}},\kappa^{\mu}\chi_{\omega_{s}})

with

\displaystyle\kappa^{\lambda}

\displaystyle:=-(c^{\lambda}+\lambda_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda}),

(20)

\displaystyle\kappa^{\mu}

\displaystyle:=-(c^{\mu}+\mu_{0}\epsilon^{\mu})

(21)

for
s\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
.

Then the reconstruction
\mathcal{D}_{R}
is given by the union of the marked resolution elements.

4.2
Formulation of theorems

We will analyse the algorithms in more detail and take a look at the required theorems.

4.2.1
Theorems for standard monotonicity tests

Theorem 1
.

The reconstruction of Algorithm
1
fulfills the resolution guarantees if

\displaystyle\nu<-2\delta\leq 0

with

\displaystyle\nu:=\max_{s=1,\ldots,N}\left(\min\left(\mathrm{eig}\left[{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)-{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}})\right]\right)\right).

Proof.

We start with the consideration of part (i) from Definition
1
and let
\omega_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. Then

\displaystyle(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu})=\left(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus\omega_{s}}+\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{\min}}\chi_{\omega_{s}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus\omega_{s}}+\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{\min}}\chi_{\omega_{s}}\right)\leq(\lambda,\mu).

The knowledge, that from
(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})\leq(\lambda_{2},\mu_{2})
it follows that

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})\geq{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda_{2},\mu_{2}),

(22)

implies that

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)\geq{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda,\mu).

Hence,

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)+\delta{\bf I}\geq{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu),

so that
\omega_{s}
will be marked by the algorithm.

This shows that part (i) of the resolution guarantee is satisfied.

To prove part (ii) of the resolution guarantee, assume that
\mathcal{D}=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{D}_{R}\neq\emptyset
.
Then there must be an index
s\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
with

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)+\delta{\bf I}\geq{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu).

Again, with the monotonicity relation (
22
), we obtain

\displaystyle-2\delta{\bf I}

\displaystyle\leq{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)-\left(\delta{\bf I}+{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu)\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)-{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)-{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}})

and thus
\nu\geq-2\delta
, which is a contradiction.
∎

All in all, this theorem gives a rigorous yet conceptually simple criterion to check whether a given resolution guarantee is valid or not.

Remark 2
.

Given a partition
\left(\omega_{s}\right)_{s=1,\ldots,N}
and bounds on the background, we obtain
\nu
from calculating

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}})

by solving the boundary value problem (
7
)-(
10
). If this yields a negative value for
\nu
,
then the resolution guarantee holds true up to a measurement error of
\delta<-\dfrac{\nu}{2}
.

Next, we formulate the corresponding theorem for case (
17
).

Theorem 2
.

The reconstruction of Algorithm
2
fulfills the resolution guarantee if

\displaystyle\nu>2\delta\geq 0

with

\displaystyle\nu:=\min_{s=1,\ldots,N}\left(\max\left(\mathrm{eig}\left[{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)-{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})\right]\right)\right).

Proof.

The proof of part (i) of the resolution guarantee is analogous to the proof of part (i) in the theorem before.

To show part (ii) of the resolution guarantee, assume that
\mathcal{D}=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{D}_{R}\neq\emptyset
. Then there must be an index
s\in\{1,2\ldots,N\}
with

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)-\delta{\bf I}

\displaystyle\leq{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda,\mu)+\delta{\bf I}.

Using the results from before, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}\left(\tau_{s}^{\lambda},\tau_{s}^{\mu}\right)-2\delta{\bf I}

\displaystyle\leq{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})

and thus
\nu\geq 2\delta
, which is a contradiction.
∎

4.2.2
Theorems for linearized monotonicity tests

Theorem 3
.

The reconstruction of Algorithm
3
fulfills the resolution guarantee if

\displaystyle\nu<-2\delta\leq 0

with

\displaystyle\nu:=\max_{s=1,\ldots,N}\left(\min\left(\mathrm{eig}\left[{\bf T}_{s}-{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\max}})\right]\right)\right).

Proof.

First, let
\omega_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and let
g_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
,
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
. In a body with interior Lamé parameters
(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})
, let
u_{g_{j}}
be the corresponding displacements resulting from applying the boundary load
g_{j}
to the
j
-th patch.

\displaystyle g_{j}^{T}\left({\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})-{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda,\mu)\right)g_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}}{\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})\nabla\cdot u_{g_{j}}\nabla\cdot u_{g_{j}}+2\dfrac{\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}}{\mu}(\mu-\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})\hat{\nabla}u_{g_{j}}:\hat{\nabla}u_{g_{j}}\,dx

and since
\omega_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
implies
\lambda-\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}\geq(c^{\lambda}+\lambda_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda})\chi_{\omega_{s}}
and
\mu-\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}}\geq(c^{\mu}+\mu_{0}\epsilon^{\mu})\chi_{\omega_{s}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})-{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda,\mu)\geq-{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\prime}(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})(\kappa^{\lambda}\chi_{\omega_{s}},\kappa^{\mu}\chi_{\omega_{s}}).

Hence, we obtain that

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{s}+\delta{\bf I}

\displaystyle={\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})+{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\prime}(\lambda_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{\min}})(\kappa^{\lambda}\chi_{\omega_{s}},\kappa^{\mu}\chi_{\omega_{s}})+\delta{\bf I}

\displaystyle\geq{\bm{\Lambda}}(\lambda,\mu)+\delta{\bf I}

\displaystyle\geq{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\delta}(\lambda,\mu).

For the proof of (ii), the reader is referred to the corresponding proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

5
Conclusion and outlook

Our main focus was the construction of conditions under which a resolution for a given partition can be achieved.
Thus, our formulation takes both the background error as well as the measurement noise into account. The numerical simulations showed that for more testcubes our method is less stable w.r.t.
\epsilon^{\lambda},\epsilon^{\mu}
and
\eta
. This behaviour is expected since more as well as smaller pixels are to be reconstructed with the same amount of data from the Neumann patches.
As a main
result, the resolution guarantees depend heavily on the Lamé parameter
\mu

and only marginally on
\lambda
.
Finally, we want to remark that the algorithm is more stable w.r.t.
\epsilon^{\lambda},\epsilon^{\mu}
as w.r.t.
\eta
.
All in all, our results are of special importance, when considering simulations based on real data, e.g., in
[
8
]
or in the framework of monotonicity-based regularization (see, e.g.
[
6
]
).

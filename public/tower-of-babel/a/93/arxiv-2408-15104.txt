Probing the Page transition via approximate quantum error correction
Haocheng Zhong

Abstract
In recent years, there is a huge progress in understanding the black hole information problem, and the key is that the black hole entropy of radiation should be calculated by the island formula, which describes the Page curve to ensure the unitarity of black hole evaporation. In the paper, we find that the black hole evaporation interpreted by the island formula can be understood in the language of approximate quantum error correction. Furthermore, the Page transition, as a special property of the Page curve, should be understood as the property of approximate quantum error correction itself, i.e. a general class of quantum systems under certain conditions from approximate quantum error correction can also exhibit phenomenon similar to the Page transition.

2.1
Notation

Before we begin, we need to clarify our notations and conventions. We use
\mathcal{H}
to denote the Hilbert space of a system in the relevant content, and the set of linear operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted as
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. The set of density matrices in
\mathcal{H}
is denoted as
s(\mathcal{H})\subset\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. If a system is labeled by a letter, e.g.
A
, then we write
\mathcal{H}_{A}
as the corresponding Hilbert space and
\left|A\right|
as the dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{A}
. States and operators on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
are also labeled with a subscript, e.g.
|\psi\rangle_{A}\in\mathcal{H}_{A},O_{A}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
. The product sign
\otimes
denotes either tensor product for states, e.g.
|\psi\rangle\otimes|\phi\rangle
, or Kronecker product for operators, e.g.
O_{A}\otimes I_{B}
where
I
is the identity. For abbreviation, we will simplify them as
|\psi\rangle|\phi\rangle
and
O_{A}
respectively, but sometimes we will keep the sign explicit to remind the readers. Whether
O_{A}
is an operator on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
or a larger space should be distinguished by the states it acts on.

2.2
Quantum channel

We first introduce the notion of
quantum channel
. The content is based on
Chatwin-Davies:2021nhs
which readers are encouraged to consult for more detailed discussions.

Definition 2.1
.

Given two systems
A,B
, a
quantum channel

\mathcal{N}
from
A
to
B
is a linear, completely positive and trace-preserving (CPTP) map from density matrices to density matrices:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}:s(\mathcal{H}_{A})

\displaystyle\rightarrow s(\mathcal{H}_{B})

(3)

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle\mapsto\rho_{B}

Linearity
is to make sure that an ensemble maps to another ensemble with the same probability distribution, i.e.

\mathcal{N}(p_{i}\rho_{i})=p_{i}\mathcal{N}(\rho_{i})

(4)

Trace-preserving
ensures that an operator with unital trace gets mapped to another operator with unital trace, and a map between operators is
positive
if it sends a positive semi-definite operator to another positive semi-definite operator. Both properties should be required because density matrices are semi-definite and have unital trace by definition. Instead of positivity, we require a stronger condition called

:

\mathcal{N}\otimes I_{E}:\rho_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E}\mapsto\rho_{B}\otimes\sigma_{E},\quad\forall\sigma_{E}\in s(\mathcal{H}_{E})

(5)

is positive for any auxiliary system
E
. Practically speaking, completely positivity is to ensure that the channel works suitably even though the system is coupled with environment, which is usually the case in an actual computation process.

Two examples of quantum channel are useful in the paper:

•

For any unitary
U\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, there exists a quantum channel
\mathcal{N}_{U}
from
\mathcal{H}
to itself:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{U}:s(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\rightarrow s(\mathcal{H})

(6)

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\mapsto U\rho U^{\dagger}

•

For any
isometry

V:\mathcal{H}_{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E}
, there exists a quantum channel
\mathcal{N}_{V}
from
A
to
B
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{V}:s(\mathcal{H}_{A})

\displaystyle\rightarrow s(\mathcal{H}_{B})

(7)

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle\mapsto\text{Tr}_{E}\left(V\rho_{A}V^{\dagger}\right)

where “isometry” means
V^{\dagger}V=I_{A}
and
VV^{\dagger}=\Pi_{BE}
, i.e. projection onto
V(\mathcal{H}_{A})\subset\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E}
.

In fact, for any quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
there exists an isometry

satisfying (

), which is also called the

of
\mathcal{N}
, if a suitable auxiliary system

is chosen

One important feature of quantum channel is that, the

monotonically decreases under a quantum channel, which is described as follows,

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a quantum channel from system

S(\rho|\sigma)\geq S(\mathcal{N}(\rho)|\mathcal{N}(\sigma)),\quad\forall\rho,\sigma\in s(\mathcal{H}_{A})

2.3
Approximate quantum error correction

Our next important notion is the
quantum error correction
(QEC), which is to protect computing process against errors (or noise) which may affect the outcome of computation. One basic idea is
encoding
the system into a larger one such that the original information can be protected when the enlarged system is only partly affected. One is encouraged to consult
nielsen2010quantum
;
PreskillNotes
for a general introduction.

We now introduce one QEC example for comprehension: the three-qutrit code
Almheiri_2015
, to illustrate the ideas of QEC. Suppose Alice wants to send Bob a state based on a qutrit:

|\psi\rangle=a_{0}|0\rangle+a_{1}|1\rangle+a_{2}|2\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{2}a_{i}|i\rangle

(9)

If Bob’s apparatus can
not
get access to
one
qutrit, then since the state is based on one qutrit, he can no longer receive the information, i.e. the qutrit is erased from Bob’s viewpoint. A better idea is that Alice should send a three-qutrit state instead of a single qutrit state:

|\tilde{\psi}\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{2}a_{i}|\tilde{i}\rangle

(10)

where we define a set of new bases,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(|000\rangle+|111\rangle+|222\rangle)

(11)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{1}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(|012\rangle+|120\rangle+|201\rangle)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(|021\rangle+|102\rangle+|210\rangle).

which spans the three-dimensional subspace of the total three-qutrit space
\mathcal{H}
, and we call it the
code subspace
denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{code}\subset\mathcal{H}
. Nows if Alice sends this alternative state
|\tilde{\psi}\rangle
to Bob, and we assume that Bob still can not get access to one of the three qutrits. Without loss of generality, we assume that it is the third qutrit which is erased. Bob can access to the first two qutrits, and he should implement the following operation
U_{12}
to the first two qutrits:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}|00\rangle\mapsto|00\rangle&|11\rangle\mapsto|01\rangle&|22\rangle\mapsto|02\rangle\\
|01\rangle\mapsto|12\rangle&|12\rangle\mapsto|10\rangle&|20\rangle\mapsto|11\rangle\\
|02\rangle\mapsto|21\rangle&|10\rangle\mapsto|22\rangle&|21\rangle\mapsto|20\rangle\end{array}

(12)

which “transforms” the new basis into the original basis as follows,

\left(U_{12}\otimes I_{3}\right)|\widetilde{i}\rangle=|i\rangle\otimes|\chi\rangle

(13)

where
|\chi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(|00\rangle+|11\rangle+|22\rangle)
. Therefore, when Bob applies
U_{12}
to
|\tilde{\psi}\rangle
, he finds that

\left(U_{12}\otimes I_{3}\right)|\widetilde{\psi}\rangle=|\psi\rangle\otimes|\chi\rangle

(14)

i.e. Bob can apply
U_{12}
to
|\tilde{\psi}\rangle
then read the first qutrit to receive the original information
|\psi\rangle
! The process is what we mean by error correction: we encode our information into a large system
(|\psi\rangle\mapsto|\tilde{\psi}\rangle)
then use
U_{12}
to recover the original information against the erasure of the third qutrit. Note that there exists permutation symmetry between qutrits in
\mathcal{H}_{code}
, which ensures that
U_{23},U_{31}
also exist, i.e. Bob can recover the original information against the erasure of the first qutrit or the second qutrit as well.

Formally, we consider a
logical space
or
code (sub)space

\mathcal{H}_{code}
which contains the
true
degrees of freedom of the system, and a
physical space

\mathcal{H}_{phys}
describing the system
redundantly
such that
\mathcal{H}_{code}\subset\mathcal{H}_{phys}
, which we call the
subspace description
. Equivalently, if we regard
\mathcal{H}_{code}
and
\mathcal{H}_{phys}
as two
independent
spaces, i.e.
\mathcal{H}_{code}
is not regarded as the subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{phys}
, then the encoding process is manifested by an isometric coding:

V:\mathcal{H}_{code}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{phys}

(15)

which can induce a quantum channel from the code space to the physical space. We call it the
encoding description
. In the case of the three-qutrit code, the isometric coding is given by

\displaystyle|\widetilde{0}\rangle\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(|000\rangle+|111\rangle+|222\rangle)

(16)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{1}\rangle\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(|012\rangle+|120\rangle+|201\rangle)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{2}\rangle\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(|021\rangle+|102\rangle+|210\rangle).

which one can compare with (
11
) described by the subspace description.

After encoding, the protected information will go through some latent errors or noise, and we still need to
recover
and
decode
the outcome to receive the information. The whole process is illustrated in Fig
2
where we use a quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
to represent encoding and noise, and another quantum channel
\mathcal{R}
, which is called the

, to represent recovery and decoding. If the whole process is qualified to be a good QEC code, we expect that

\tilde{\rho}\equiv(\mathcal{\mathcal{R}}\circ\mathcal{N})(\rho)\approx\rho,\quad\forall\rho\in s(\mathcal{H}_{code})

(17)

Figure 2:
Extracted from
Chatwin-Davies:2021nhs
. Quantum error correction can be realized by a series of quantum channels, where
\mathcal{N}
represents encoding and noise, and
\mathcal{R}
represents recovery and decoding.

We say
\mathcal{N}
is
reversible
if
\mathcal{R}
exists, and is

if the equality holds in (

). How do we know if a channel is exactly reversible or not? There is a theorem due to Petz & Ohya
ohya2004quantum
which can be demonstrated as follows,

Theorem 2.3
.

A quantum channel
\mathcal{N}:s(\mathcal{H}_{A})\rightarrow s(\mathcal{H}_{B})
is exactly reversible if and only if

S(\rho|\sigma)=S(\mathcal{N}(\rho)|\mathcal{N}(\sigma)),\quad\forall\rho,\sigma\in s(\mathcal{H}_{A})

(18)

Furthermore, the
Petz map
:

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{\sigma},\mathcal{N}}~:~\gamma~\mapsto~\sigma^{1/2}\mathcal{N}^{\dagger}\left[\mathcal{N}(\sigma)^{-1/2}\,\gamma\,\mathcal{N}(\sigma)^{-1/2}\right]\sigma^{1/2},\quad\forall\gamma\in s(\mathcal{H}_{B})

(19)

is the recovery channel:

(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{\sigma},\mathcal{N}}\circ\mathcal{N})(\rho)=\rho,\quad\forall\rho\in s(\mathcal{H}_{A})

(20)

Practically, it is very hard for a quantum channel to be reversible exactly, so we need a criterion for a channel to be reversible in an approximate sense. Due to theorem
2.2
and theorem

, we say that
\mathcal{N}
is only

reversible if and only if

S(\rho|\sigma)-S(\mathcal{N}(\rho)|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))<\delta

(21)

for some infinitesimal positive
\delta
. The procedure described in Fig
2
together with (

(AQEC), see
Junge_2018
and also earlier works
barnum2000reversing
;
HIAI_2011
;
Mandayam_2012
.

Given

3.2
Threshold of the Page transition

In fact, different states result in different geometries in gravitational theory, hence we have different geometric configurations for
\tilde{\rho}
and
\tilde{\sigma}
(i.e.
X^{\{\rho\}}\neq X^{\{\sigma\}}
in Fig
1
where the superscript denotes which density matrix is associated) which may result in the non-vanishing threshold value. During the verification in the last section we implicitly assume that the Hilbert space factorization
\mathcal{H}^{sc}=\mathcal{H}^{sc}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{sc}_{I\cup R}
is the same for different density matrices, which is not necessarily true. Meanwhile, if two density matrices give two hugely different Hilbert space factorizations will make our derivation in the last section no longer valid.

Therefore, one additional constraint should be manually added:

•

Two different density matrices which we choose to calculate the relative entropy should correspond to black holes with the same macroscopic quantities
.

To be specific, when we calculate
\Delta_{rel}S
, we can only choose density matrices from
s(\mathcal{H}_{macro,\{\ell\}})\subset s(\mathcal{H}^{sc})
where the subscript “macro” denotes that the states within give the same macroscopic quantities and “
\{\ell\}
” collectively specifies all possible macroscopic quantities. The constraint is natural because for observers localized in
R
, they should only observe the macroscopic quantities while the microscopic details are still hidden inside the horizon. The constraint results in the fact that the two density matrices we choose should approximately give the same radius of black hole horizon, which further implies that
X^{\{\rho\}}\approx X^{\{\sigma\}}
as the QES is slightly deviated from the horizon. The other consequence is that the black holes for two density matrices should be formed in the same time. As a counter example, two same-size black holes with one of which being in the early time of evaporation while the other being in the later time of evaporation have vastly different entropies of radiation, because the former do not have island contribution while the latter does.

After carefully specifying which configuration we are considering in the above calculation, one can perform a similar procedure in the last subsection to show that after
t_{Page}
we have

\Delta S_{rel}(\tilde{\rho}|\tilde{\sigma})\equiv S(\tilde{\rho}^{\{\rho\}}_{I\cup R}|\tilde{\sigma}^{\{\sigma\}}_{I\cup R})-S(\rho_{R}^{\{\rho\}}|\sigma_{R}^{\{\sigma\}})=\delta_{1}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma})+\delta_{2}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma}),\quad\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma}\in s(\mathcal{H}_{macro,\{\ell\}})

(48)

where

\displaystyle\delta_{1}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma})\equiv S(\tilde{\rho}_{I\cup R}^{\{\rho\}}|\tilde{\sigma}_{I\cup R}^{\{\sigma\}})-S(\tilde{\rho}_{I\cup R}^{\{\sigma\}}|\tilde{\sigma}_{I\cup R}^{\{\sigma\}}),

(49)

\displaystyle\delta_{2}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma})\equiv\left(S(\tilde{\rho}_{I\cup R}^{\{\rho\}})+\mathcal{L}_{X}^{\{\rho\}}\right)-\left(S(\tilde{\rho}_{I\cup R}^{\{\sigma\}})+\mathcal{L}_{X}^{\{\sigma\}}\right)

(50)

According to our constraint that
X^{\{\rho\}}\approx X^{\{\sigma\}}
,
\delta_{1}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma})
is an infinitesimal value. As for
\delta_{2}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma})
, we recognize that the term inside round bracket is exactly what is extremized by QES in (
1

\delta_{2}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma})

among all

\delta\equiv\text{max}_{\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma},\{\ell\}}\left(\delta_{1}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma})+\delta_{2}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma})\right),\quad\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma}\in s(\mathcal{H}_{macro,\{\ell\}})

In the paper, we have verified that the black hole evaporation does satisfy the criterion (

•

Gravity plays “almost” no role.

•

Our definition of the Page transition requires the notion of
local
observers.

The first statement may be a bit misleading. By it we mean that gravity only manifests itself in two ways. The first one gives the area term in (

2.4
allows arbitrary value of
\mathcal{L}_{A}

The second statement requires the locality of observers. By “locality” we mean that observers are only able to access degrees of freedom of some certain subregions, while any other subregion which is space-like separated to the accessible subregion is blind to those observers. For example, provided a density matrix

R

. In the case of black hole evaporation, what we focus on is the entropy of radiation, which is only accessible to observers in

2.4

Compare the last two results we find that there must exists
|\chi\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}},|\bar{\chi}\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}

U^{\dagger}_{\bar{A}}|\chi_{j}\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}}=|j\rangle_{\bar{A}_{1}}|\chi\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}},\quad U^{\dagger}_{A}|\bar{\chi}_{i}\rangle_{\bar{A}_{2}A}=|i\rangle_{A_{1}}|\bar{\chi}\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}

U^{\dagger}_{A}U^{\dagger}_{\bar{A}}|\tilde{ij}\rangle=|i\rangle_{A_{1}}|j\rangle_{\bar{A}_{1}}|\chi\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}=|j\rangle_{\bar{A}_{1}}|i\rangle_{A_{1}}|\bar{\chi}\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}

i.e. in fact we have
|\chi\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}=|\bar{\chi}\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}

|\tilde{ij}\rangle=U_{A}U_{\bar{A}}\left(|ij\rangle_{A_{1}\bar{A}_{1}}|\chi\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}\right)

where
|ij\rangle_{A_{1}\bar{A}_{1}}\equiv|i\rangle_{A_{1}}|j\rangle_{\bar{A}_{1}}
, then for
\tilde{\rho}\equiv\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}|\tilde{ij}\rangle\langle\tilde{ij}|

\tilde{\rho}=U_{A}U_{\bar{A}}\left(\rho_{A_{1}\bar{A}_{1}}\otimes\chi_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}\right)U^{\dagger}_{A}U^{\dagger}_{\bar{A}}

(68)

where we define

\rho_{A_{1}\bar{A}_{1}}\equiv\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}|ij\rangle_{A_{1}\bar{A}_{1}}\langle ij|_{A_{1}\bar{A}_{1}},\quad\chi_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}\equiv|\chi\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}\langle\chi|_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}

(69)

Now the reduced density matrix
\tilde{\rho}_{A}\equiv\text{Tr}_{\bar{A}}\tilde{\rho}

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{A}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\bar{A}}\left[U_{A}U_{\bar{A}}\left(\rho_{A_{1}\bar{A}_{1}}\otimes\chi_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}\right)U^{\dagger}_{A}U^{\dagger}_{\bar{A}}\right]

(70)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\bar{A}}\left[U_{A}\left(\rho_{A_{1}\bar{A}_{1}}\otimes\chi_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}\right)U^{\dagger}_{A}\right]

\displaystyle=U_{A}\text{Tr}_{\bar{A}}\left(\rho_{A_{1}\bar{A}_{1}}\otimes\chi_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}\right)U^{\dagger}_{A}

\displaystyle=U_{A}\left(\rho_{A_{1}}\otimes\chi_{A_{2}}\right)U^{\dagger}_{A}

\chi_{A_{2}}\equiv\text{Tr}_{\bar{A}_{2}}\chi_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}

\displaystyle S(\tilde{\rho}_{A})=S\left(U_{A}\left(\rho_{A_{1}}\otimes\chi_{A_{2}}\right)U^{\dagger}_{A}\right)=S\left(\rho_{A_{1}}\otimes\chi_{A_{2}}\right)=S(\rho_{A_{1}})+S(\chi_{A_{2}})

where the second equality makes use of the fact the von Neumann entropy is invariant under unitary transformations. Notice that
\tilde{\rho}_{a}\equiv\text{Tr}_{\bar{a}}\tilde{\rho}=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\tilde{i}\rangle_{a}\langle\tilde{i}|_{a}
has exactly the same eigenvalues as
\rho_{A_{1}}=\sum_{i}p_{i}|i\rangle_{A_{1}}\langle i|_{A_{1}}
such that

S(\rho_{A_{1}})=-\sum_{i}p_{i}\log p_{i}=S(\tilde{\rho}_{a})

(72)

S(\chi_{A_{2}})
as
\mathcal{L}_{A}
we obtain

\displaystyle S(\tilde{\rho}_{A})=\mathcal{L}_{A}+S(\tilde{\rho}_{a})

and likewise for
\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{A}}\equiv\text{Tr}_{A}\tilde{\rho}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{a}}\equiv\text{Tr}_{a}\tilde{\rho}
, we have

S(\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{A}})=\mathcal{L}_{\bar{A}}+S(\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{a}})

(74)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{A}}=S(\chi_{\bar{A}_{2}}),~\chi_{\bar{A}_{2}}\equiv\text{Tr}_{A_{2}}\chi_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}
. Notice that
\chi_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}=|\chi\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}\langle\chi|_{A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}}
is a pure state on
A_{2}\bar{A}_{2}
by definition, then we know that
S(\chi_{\bar{A}_{2}})=S(\chi_{A_{2}})
, or equivalently,

\mathcal{L}_{A}=\mathcal{L}_{\bar{A}}

(2)\Rightarrow(3)

Before we start, we recall how von Neumann entropy varies when we perturb the density matrix:

\delta S(\rho)\equiv S(\rho+\delta\rho)-S(\rho)\approx-\text{Tr}\left(\delta\rho\log\rho\right)

(76)

Now we can proceed. Given a density matrix
\tilde{\sigma}
on
\mathcal{H}_{code}
and its reduced density matrices
\tilde{\sigma}_{A},\tilde{\sigma}_{a}
, using (
55
) we have

\displaystyle S(\tilde{\sigma}_{A})=\text{Tr}_{a}\left(\tilde{\sigma}_{a}\mathcal{L}_{A}\right)+S(\tilde{\sigma}_{a})

(77)

where we use
\text{Tr}_{a}\tilde{\sigma}_{a}=1
and promote
\mathcal{L}_{A}
to an operator of the form
\mathcal{L}_{A}I_{a}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{a})
. Now we expand in terms of
\tilde{\sigma}
in first order:

\text{Tr}_{A}\left(\delta\tilde{\sigma}_{A}\tilde{K}^{\sigma}_{A}\right)=\text{Tr}_{a}\left[\delta\tilde{\sigma}_{a}\left(\tilde{K}^{\sigma}_{a}+\mathcal{L}_{A}I_{a}\right)\right]

(78)

where
\tilde{K}^{\sigma}_{A}=-\log\tilde{\sigma}_{A}
and
\tilde{K}^{\sigma}_{a}=-\log\tilde{\sigma}_{a}
are modular Hamiltonians. Both sides are linear in
\delta\tilde{\sigma}
such that we can integrate it to
\tilde{\rho}
:

\text{Tr}_{A}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{A}\tilde{K}^{\sigma}_{A}\right)=\text{Tr}_{a}\left[\tilde{\rho}_{a}\left(\tilde{K}^{\sigma}_{a}+\mathcal{L}_{A}I_{a}\right)\right]=\text{Tr}_{a}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{a}\tilde{K}^{\sigma}_{a}\right)+\mathcal{L}_{A}

(79)

Now with (
55
) we find that

\displaystyle S(\tilde{\rho}_{A}|\tilde{\sigma}_{A})

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{A}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{A}\tilde{K}^{\sigma}_{A}\right)-S(\tilde{\rho}_{A})

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{a}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{a}\tilde{K}^{\sigma}_{a}\right)+\mathcal{L}_{A}-\left(\mathcal{L}_{A}+S(\tilde{\rho}_{a})\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{a}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{a}\tilde{K}^{\sigma}_{a}\right)-S(\tilde{\rho}_{a})

\displaystyle=S(\tilde{\rho}_{a}|\tilde{\sigma}_{a})

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle S(\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{A}}|\tilde{\sigma}_{\bar{A}})

\displaystyle=S(\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{a}}|\tilde{\sigma}_{\bar{a}})

(80)

(3)\Rightarrow(4)
:
The relative entropy vanishes if and only if the two density matrices coincide, so if provided (
59
), we are given a condition that

\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{A}}=\tilde{\sigma}_{\bar{A}}\Leftrightarrow\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{a}}=\tilde{\sigma}_{\bar{a}}

(81)

Let us consider a state
|\tilde{\phi}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{code}
and a Hermitian operator
\tilde{O}_{a}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{a})
, we can construct a new state
|\tilde{\psi}\rangle
as follows,

|\tilde{\psi}\rangle\equiv e^{i\tilde{O}_{a}s}|\tilde{\phi}\rangle

(82)

for some real constant
s
. We now define two density matrices on
\mathcal{H}_{code}
:

\tilde{\sigma}\equiv|\tilde{\psi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\psi}|,\quad\tilde{\rho}\equiv|\tilde{\phi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\phi}|

(83)

then we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{\bar{a}}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{a}|\tilde{\psi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\psi}|

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{a}\left[e^{i\tilde{O}_{a}s}|\tilde{\phi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\phi}|e^{-i\tilde{O}_{a}s}\right]

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{a}|\tilde{\phi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\phi}|

\displaystyle=\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{a}}

By condition (
81
), we must have

\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{A}}=\tilde{\sigma}_{\bar{A}}

(84)

which further implies

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\bar{A}}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{A}}X_{\bar{A}}\right)=\text{Tr}_{\bar{A}}\left(\tilde{\sigma}_{\bar{A}}X_{\bar{A}}\right),\quad\forall X_{\bar{A}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\bar{A}})

(85)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{A\bar{A}}\left(|\tilde{\phi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\phi}|X_{\bar{A}}\right)=\text{Tr}_{A\bar{A}}\left(|\tilde{\psi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\psi}|X_{\bar{A}}\right)

(86)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{\phi}|X_{\bar{A}}|\tilde{\phi}\rangle=\langle\tilde{\psi}|X_{\bar{A}}|\tilde{\psi}\rangle

(87)

For infinitesimal
\lambda
, we expand the right-hand side of the last result in first order to obtain

\langle\tilde{\phi}|[\tilde{O}_{a},X_{\bar{A}}]|\tilde{\phi}\rangle=0,\quad\forall X_{\bar{A}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\bar{A}})

(88)

Notice that the above derivation requires
\tilde{O}_{a}
to be Hermitian, but any operator can be rewritten as a complex linear combination of Hermitian operators as follows,

\tilde{O}_{a}=\frac{\tilde{O}_{a}+\tilde{O}^{\dagger}_{a}}{2}+i\frac{\tilde{O}_{a}-\tilde{O}^{\dagger}_{a}}{2i}

(89)

where
\frac{\tilde{O}_{a}+\tilde{O}^{\dagger}_{a}}{2}
and
\frac{\tilde{O}_{a}-\tilde{O}^{\dagger}_{a}}{2i}
are both Hermitian. Therefore, it is suffice to derive our result by using Hermitian operators only.

Similarly, if we start with (
58
), we will obtain

\langle\tilde{\phi}|[\tilde{O}_{\bar{a}},X_{A}]|\tilde{\phi}\rangle=0,\quad\forall X_{A}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})

(90)

for any operator
\tilde{O}_{\bar{a}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\bar{a}})
.

(4)\Rightarrow(1)

Before our proof, we outline our strategy. We first introduce two auxiliary systems
R,\bar{R}
with
\left|R\right|=\left|a\right|,\left|\bar{R}\right|=\left|\bar{a}\right|
and a state
|\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\bar{R}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{code}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\bar{R}}\otimes\mathcal{H}
defined by

|\phi\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|R\right|\left|\bar{R}\right|}}\sum_{i,j}|i\rangle_{R}|j\rangle_{\bar{R}}|\tilde{ij}\rangle

(91)

Next we introduce a set of orthonormal states
|\chi_{j}\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}}\in\mathcal{H}_{A_{2}\bar{A}}
and
|\chi\rangle_{\bar{R}A_{2}\bar{A}}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|\bar{R}\right|}}\sum_{j}|j\rangle_{\bar{R}}|\chi_{j}\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}}
, then we define a state
|\phi^{\prime}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\bar{R}}\otimes\mathcal{H}
by

|\phi^{\prime}\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|R\right|\left|\bar{R}\right|}}\sum_{i,j}|i\rangle_{R}|j\rangle_{\bar{R}}|i\rangle_{A_{1}}|\chi_{j}\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|R\right|}}\sum_{i}|i\rangle_{R}|i\rangle_{A_{1}}|\chi\rangle_{\bar{R}A_{2}\bar{A}}

(92)

These two states corresponds to two pure density matrices
\rho\equiv|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|,~\rho^{\prime}\equiv|\phi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\phi^{\prime}|
. To prove that there exists a unitary operator
U_{A}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
such that

|\tilde{ij}\rangle=U_{A}\left(|i\rangle_{A_{1}}|\chi_{j}\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}}\right)

(93)

we can instead define
\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}=\text{Tr}_{A}\rho
then try to prove that

\text{Tr}_{A}\rho^{\prime}=\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}

(94)

which tells us that
\rho^{\prime},\rho
are both purifications of
\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}
. Two different purifications differ by a unitary transformation, i.e. there exist a unitary
U_{A}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
such that

|\phi\rangle=U_{A}|\phi^{\prime}\rangle\quad\Rightarrow\quad|\tilde{ij}\rangle=U_{A}\left(|i\rangle_{A_{1}}|\chi_{j}\rangle_{A_{2}\bar{A}}\right)

(95)

which will end the proof.

We digress a bit to mention a useful statement. It is possible to find an alternative operator which acts on different space but still does the same work as the original one. To be more specific, consider a bipartite system
XY
with an arbitrary state
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{Y}
which is in general of the form

|\psi\rangle=C^{ab}|a\rangle_{X}|b\rangle_{Y}

(96)

where
|a\rangle_{X},|b\rangle_{Y}
represents bases in respective spaces and Einstein summation is understood. Suppose we have operators
O_{X}\in\mathcal{H}_{X},O_{Y}\in\mathcal{H}_{Y}
which satisfy

O_{X}|\psi\rangle=O_{Y}|\psi\rangle

(97)

i.e. they are identical when acting on states in
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{Y}
. We would like to see how the components of the two operators are related. We further assume that

O_{X}|a\rangle_{X}=O^{ca}_{X}|c\rangle_{X},\quad O_{Y}|b\rangle_{Y}=O^{db}_{Y}|d\rangle_{X}

(98)

where
O^{ca}_{X},O^{db}_{Y}
are constants. We then have

\displaystyle O_{X}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=C^{ab}O^{ca}_{X}|c\rangle_{X}|b\rangle_{Y}

\displaystyle=O_{Y}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=C^{ab}O_{Y}^{db}|a\rangle_{X}|d\rangle_{Y}=C^{ca}O_{Y}^{ba}|c\rangle_{X}|b\rangle_{Y}

After modifying dummy indices, we obtain

O^{ca}_{X}C^{ab}=C^{ca}O_{Y}^{ba}

(99)

or in matrix notation

O_{X}C=CO_{Y}^{T}

(100)

This result tells us that how we can find a new operator which acts on the complementary space but does the same work as the given one.

Now back to our business. Given (
60
) for an arbitrary operator
\tilde{O}_{a}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{a})
, we have:

\langle\tilde{ij}|[\tilde{O}_{a},X_{\bar{A}}]|\tilde{ij}\rangle=0,\quad\forall X_{\bar{A}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\bar{A}})

(101)

By the statement we just mentioned, we can find an alternative operator
O_{R}
which satisfies

O_{R}|\phi\rangle=\tilde{O}_{a}|\phi\rangle

(102)

such that

\langle\phi|[O_{R},X_{\bar{A}}]|\phi\rangle=0

(103)

We can further introduce an operator acting on different space in the above equality:

\langle\phi|[O_{R},X_{\bar{A}}Y_{\bar{R}}]|\phi\rangle=0,\quad\forall Y_{\bar{R}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\bar{R}}

(104)

because
Y_{\bar{R}}
commutes with both
O_{R}
and
X_{\bar{A}}
. It implies that

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}\left(\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}[O_{R},X_{\bar{A}}Y_{\bar{R}}]\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\left(\rho[O_{R},X_{\bar{A}}Y_{\bar{R}}]\right)

\displaystyle=\langle\phi|[O_{R},X_{\bar{A}}Y_{\bar{R}}]|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=0

where we define
\rho=|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|
and
\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}=\text{Tr}_{A}\rho
. Rearranging left-hand side gives

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}\left(\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}O_{R}X_{\bar{A}}Y_{\bar{R}}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}\left(\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}X_{\bar{A}}Y_{\bar{R}}O_{R}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}\left(O_{R}\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}X_{\bar{A}}Y_{\bar{R}}\right)

which further implies

[\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}},O_{R}\otimes I_{\bar{R}\bar{A}}]=0,\quad\forall\tilde{O}_{a}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{a})

(105)

It constraints
\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}
to be of the form

\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}=I_{R}\otimes M_{\bar{R}\bar{A}}

(106)

where
M_{\bar{R}\bar{A}}
is an operator to be determined. We note that

\rho_{\bar{R}\bar{A}}=\text{Tr}_{R}\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}=\left|R\right|M_{\bar{R}\bar{A}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad M_{\bar{R}\bar{A}}=\frac{1}{\left|R\right|}\rho_{\bar{R}\bar{A}}

(107)

such that

\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}}=\rho_{R}\otimes\rho_{\bar{R}\bar{A}},\quad\rho_{R}\equiv\frac{1}{\left|R\right|}I_{R}

(108)

As for
\rho^{\prime}
, we can directly compute its trace over

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\left|R\right|}\sum_{i,j}\left(|i\rangle_{R}\langle j|_{R}\right)\otimes\left(|i\rangle_{A_{1}}\langle j|_{A_{1}}\right)\otimes\chi

(109)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{A}\rho^{\prime}=\rho_{R}\otimes\text{Tr}_{A_{2}}\chi

(110)

where
\chi\equiv|\chi\rangle_{\bar{R}A_{2}\bar{A}}\langle\chi|_{\bar{R}A_{2}\bar{A}}
. If we compare it with (

), we find that condition (

\text{Tr}_{A_{2}}\chi=\rho_{\bar{R}\bar{A}}

(111)

i.e.
\chi
is a purification of
\rho_{\bar{R}\bar{A}}
by introducing
A_{2}
. Now our goal becomes proving that purifying
\rho_{\bar{R}\bar{A}}
by
A_{2}
is possible, which is true as long as
\left|A_{2}\right|\geq\text{rank}(\rho_{\bar{R}\bar{A}})
.

Notice that (

\text{rank}(\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}})=\text{rank}(\rho_{R})\text{rank}(\rho_{\bar{R}\bar{A}})=\left|R\right|\text{rank}(\rho_{\bar{R}\bar{A}})

Since

\left|A\right|\geq\text{rank}(\rho_{\bar{R}R\bar{A}})=\left|R\right|\text{rank}(\rho_{\bar{R}\bar{A}})

Also by assumption
\left|A\right|=\left|A_{1}\right|\left|A_{2}\right|
and

, we further have

(114)

Therefore, it is possible to purify
\rho_{\bar{R}\bar{A}}
by subsystem
A_{2}
, which concludes our proof, and we can prove (

) in exactly the same way.

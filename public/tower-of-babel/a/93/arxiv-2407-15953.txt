A Universal Relation Between Intermittency and Dissipation in Turbulence
F. Schmitt, A. Fuchs, J. Peinke, M. Obligado

Abstract
The relationship between fundamental quantities of turbulent flows like the dissipation constant C ε C_{\varepsilon} and the intermittency factor μ \mu , expected to be constant for homogeneous isotropic turbulence, is explored with respect to their relationship for a wider class of non-ideal turbulent flows. For the energy cascade, C ε C_{\varepsilon} is known to reflect its basic overall properties, while μ \mu quantifies the emerging intermittency down the cascade. Using a very large dataset of turbulent wakes, grid-generated turbulence and an axisymmetric jet, we find that μ \mu is inversely proportional to C ε C_{\varepsilon} , providing a new empirical principle .



Turbulence is still one of the major unsolved problems in physics
Frisch 1995
. The lack of a theory of turbulent flows hinders our modelling capabilities in several fields, as there are many situations where turbulence has a crucial influence, with turbulent wakes generated by wind turbines being currently among the most relevant industry-related applications
Stevens and Meneveau 2017
;
Neunaber et al. 2020
. The most common approach to tackle this problem is to define equations where factors or exponents are obtained by dimensional arguments or experiments. Within this approach, a relevant question that arises concerns the universality of these constants
Praskovsky and Oncley 1994
;
Renner et al. 2002
. Since the publication of Kolmogorov’s phenomenology in 1941 (K41)
Kolmogorov 1991
, there has been intense work to shed light on this question. This was enhanced by the publication of Kolmogorov’s revised theory in 1962
Kolmogorov 1962
, as further constants appeared. Although there were some deviations in experimental results, the overall opinion – at least until the 1990s – was that such constants, for sufficiently large Reynolds numbers, are universal for fully developed turbulence, which led to a focus on their numerical values
Praskovsky and Oncley 1994
;
Sreenivasan 1995
;
Arneodo et al. 1996
;
Praskovsky and Oncley 1997
;
Yeung and Zhou 1997
.

Among all relevant parameters, two of the most important constants to describe turbulent flows are, arguably, the dissipation constant
C_{\varepsilon}
and the intermittency factor
\mu
. The former has been identified as one of the most important parameters to describe turbulence
Lumley 1992
, as it can be directly linked to the energy cascade
Vassilicos 2015
. On the other hand, the constant
\mu
, first introduced by Kolmogorov’s 1962 theory
Kolmogorov 1962
, and later redefined by Castaing and collaborators
Castaing et al. 1990
;
Arneodo et al. 1996
, quantifies departures from self-similarity within the inertial range. Despite its relevance, a physical interpretation of
\mu
is still missing.
Furthermore, over the last two decades, the universality of
C_{\varepsilon}
has been challenged by several experimental and numerical studies
Vassilicos 2015
.

The dissipation constant is defined as

C_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon\>L}{u^{\prime 3}},

(1)

with
\varepsilon
the mean turbulent kinetic energy dissipation rate,
u^{\prime}
the RMS value of the streamwise fluctuating velocity and
L
the integral length scale. The importance of
C_{\varepsilon}
for modelling arises from the fact that it gives an estimation of the mean dissipation rate, which is often needed to have a closed system of equations
George 1989
. According to K41 phenomenology, considering stationary, homogeneous turbulence,
C_{\varepsilon}
should depend on boundary conditions, being constant for sufficiently large values of the Taylor-length based Reynolds number
Re_{\lambda}=u^{\prime}\,\lambda/\nu
, with
\lambda
the Taylor length scale and
\nu
the kinematic viscosity of the fluid.

In several free-shear flows, results not consistent with K41 phenomenology have been reported, and the value of
C_{\varepsilon}
at the centreline has been found to decrease with increasing
Re_{\lambda}

Vassilicos 2015
;
Ortiz-Tarin et al. 2021
. More precisely, for this so-called non-equilibrium cascade,
C_{\varepsilon}
is expected to be linear with
\sqrt{Re_{G}}/Re_{\lambda}
(with
Re_{G}
a Reynolds number that depends on the inlet properties of the flow, such as the wind tunnel inflow velocity and the diameter of the wake-generating object). Most of the work on this topic aims at quantifying the dependence of
C_{\varepsilon}
over a broad range of Reynolds numbers. These studies concern laminar inflows, and little attention has been paid to further details of the energy cascade, such as emerging intermittency.

The intermittency factor
\mu
is used to characterise deviations from K41
and is a measure of how “fast” the cascade propagates to smaller scales
. This deviation is seen in the changing statistics of the averaged velocity increments
\delta_{u}(r):=u(x+r)-u(x)
(
r
being the spatial increments), which can be quantified using the scale-dependent shape factor
\Lambda^{2}(r)
.
\Lambda^{2}
,
that is linked to the dispersion of the logarithm of the – also scale-dependent – energy dissipation
Kolmogorov 1962
, can be estimated as

Chilla et al. 1996
,

\Lambda^{2}(r)=\frac{\ln\left(F(\delta_{u}(r))/3\right)}{4},

(2)

where
F(\delta_{u}(r))
denotes the flatness. In the inertial range, for
\eta\ll r\ll L
(with
\eta=(\nu^{3}/\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{4}}
, being the Kolmogorov length scale), the scale dependence is expected to follow the relation
Kolmogorov 1962
;
Castaing et al. 1990
,

{\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda_{0}^{2}+\mu/9\;\ln(L/r),}

(3)

with
\Lambda_{0}^{2}
a constant that depends on the properties of the flow (see also figure
1
).
Note, that the relation (
3
) is consistent with the power-law scaling behavior of the energy spectral density in the inertial range, as discussed below (see equation
4
).

Within this rationale, the non-Gaussianity of the velocity increment distributions can be quantified by a single scalar parameter. The value of
\mu
has been commonly accepted to be constant at large values of the
Re_{\lambda}
. The reported values range between
0.2
and
0.4
for homogeneous isotropic turbulence (HIT) and a value of
0.26
has been established as representative of turbulent flows
Arneodo et al. 1996
;
Vinnes et al. 2023
;
Neunaber et al. 2020
.

Despite intense research on turbulent flows, no relation between
\mu
and
C_{\varepsilon}
has been predicted nor found. Furthermore, the universality of these constants remains an open question, with a growing amount of evidence pointing against this feature. While most studies deal with homogeneous turbulence, in this study we also focus on inhomogeneous flows.
Hence, we present a detailed experimental work on turbulent wakes supported by previously published datasets on grid-generated turbulence and a turbulent axisymmetric jet.

We carried out wind tunnel experiments in LEGI, Grenoble by generating a turbulent wake behind porous and impermeable objects under different inflow conditions. The facility is a closed-circuit system with test section dimensions of 0.75

To reduce the blockage, the cylinders were directly screwed to the walls of the tunnel while the disks were hold at the tunnel’s centreline with four thin piano wires which went through taped holes in the tunnel walls. For varying the inflow, both the entrance of the test section and the tunnel freestream velocity were modified. The integral length scale

Having a closer look on the data we see that for each set of boundary conditions, a linear relation between

We continue by investigating how two other
relevant parameters behave, namely the Kolmogorov constant

C_{k}
and the power law governing the energy spectral density
within the inertial range.
Following the notation of
Mydlarski and Warhaft 1996
,

{E(k)=C_{k}\>\>\varepsilon^{2/3}\>k^{-5/3}\>(k\>\eta)^{-\gamma+5/3},}

(4)

the K41 prediction for the spectra is complemented with the correction
(k\>\eta)^{-\gamma+5/3}
. K41 is therefore recovered when
\gamma=5/3
and, for large values of
Re_{\lambda}
,

is a constant (commonly accepted to be around 0.5 for HIT
Sreenivasan 1995
). The value of 5/3 emerges through a dimensional argument which followed the first two similarity hypotheses of Kolmogorov. Over the last years, several works reported values of

that deviate from the expected 5/3 value

;
Rodriguez Imazio et al. 2023
;
Neunaber et al. 2020

Re_{\lambda}
, most likely due to intermittency corrections. On the other hand, less evidence of deviations from
C_{k}\,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\approx}\,0.5
has been reported in the literature

To conclude, based on the study of a very large number of velocity time series, the joint and individual behaviour of the dissipation constant and the intermittency factor were quantified, as well as the spectral law of turbulence. In contrast to the common approach of more restrictive data selection to obtain more precise results on turbulence, we adopted a less restrictive data selection method. This follows the idea of Vassilicos
Vassilicos 2015
, which allows for the investigation of states of non-equilibrium turbulence. For the different canonical turbulent flows that compose our dataset, our analysis leads to the proposition of a new relation,

\mu\>C_{\varepsilon}=\alpha=\text{const.},

(5)

replacing the previous proposition that these quantities should be individually constant.

Figure
3
b indicates that there is no Reynolds number dependence of the product
\mu\>C_{\varepsilon}
. This is a remarkable result. It has long been claimed that certain turbulent properties are universal, i.e., independent of the type of flow, for large enough Reynolds numbers. Together with the relations to other turbulent constants (shown in figure
4
), this new proposed relation combines the dimensionless energy dissipation as an overall quantity of turbulent states with the statistics of velocity fluctuations at two points. Note that the power spectrum and the intermittency factor fully capture the two-point statistics
Morales et al. 2012
. Thus, we observe a new universality combining overall constants (
C_{\varepsilon}
or
C_{k}
) with the two-point statistics of the velocity field.

As a remark, it should be pointed out that our results do not imply the absence of Reynolds number effects. For example, the depth of the cascade is still expected to depend on
Re_{\lambda}
, influencing the range of the power law of the power spectrum and therefore the intermittency at the smallest scales (which are also expected to be determined by the Reynolds number). We also observe that the evolution of
C_{\varepsilon}
with the Reynolds number follows different linear relations sections for different flows, as discussed above.

Next, we set our result in the context of the
\Pi
-theorem of Vaschy-Buckingham
Vaschy 1892
;
Buckingham 1914
.
C_{\varepsilon}
and
\mu
can also be viewed as two
\Pi
-parameters for dissipation and the scale dependency of the fluctuations of dissipation. For different turbulent flow configurations, it is a challenge to find the functional relation between these
\Pi
-parameters. For a prominent example, see the Rayleigh-Bénard convection
Ahlers et al. 2009
. In this sense, our result reads as
\mu\>C_{\varepsilon}=\Pi_{1}\cdot\Pi_{2}=\text{const.}\neq f(Re)
.

Further, a relevant consequence of equation (
5
) is that we can define
\mu
as,

\mu=\frac{u^{\prime 3}\>\alpha}{\varepsilon\>L}.

(6)

This relation has the advantage that the quantities on the right-hand side are much easier to measure and converge than
\mu
(when estimated using standard approaches).

While our results appear to be applicable to a large variety of flows, further work is needed to assess whether our findings apply to other inhomogeneous and unsteady flows, which may lead to an even greater validity of our new law for turbulent flows.

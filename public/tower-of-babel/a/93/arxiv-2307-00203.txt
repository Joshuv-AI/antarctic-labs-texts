Abstract
. For the symplectic Grassmannian SpG ​ ( 2 , 2 ​ n ) \text{SpG}(2,2n) of 2 2 -dimensional isotropic subspaces in a 2 ​ n 2n -dimensional vector space over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero endowed with a symplectic form and with the natural action of an n n -dimensional torus T T on it, we characterize its irreducible T T -invariant subvarieties. 1.
Introduction

Given the action of the maximal torus
T
on a Grassmannian, it is very hard to characterize the
T
-invariant subvarieties, and despite being an important problem little is known in the literature. In this paper we solve this problem for the symplectic Grassmannian
\text{SpG}(2,2n)
. We are able to characterize all the
T
-invariant subvarieties of this symplectic Grassmannian.

Combinatorics and algebraic groups are intimately related, and the article
[
7
]
of Gelfand, Goresky, MacPherson and Serganova showed how matroids are important to understand the
T
-invariant subvarieties. Since not all symplectic matroids of rank two are representable over
\mathbb{C}
(see
[
2
, Section 3.4.3]
), one of the important results in this paper is to characterize which ones are representable.

Coxeter matroids play the same role for other types of flag varieties that matroids play for the type-A Grassmannian. Part of their importance rests on
the relations between them and the geometry of the corresponding flag varieties. A natural setting where Coxeter matroids appear is when one considers a semisimple algebraic group
G
over
an algebraically closed field
K
of characteristic zero, a parabolic subgroup
P\subseteq G
, a maximal torus
T\subseteq P
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
and Weyl group
W
, and the projective variety
X=G/P
. Then one has:

Theorem 1.1

(See
[
2
]
)
.

If
\mu:X\longrightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}
is the corresponding moment map, then
\mu(\overline{Tx})
is a Coxeter matroid associated to the Weyl group
W
for every
x\in X
.

A basic example of this situation is the Grassmannian
G(d,n)
for
0<d<n
, which is a quotient
G(d,n)=G/P
for
G=\text{GL}_{n}(K)
and
P=P_{n,d}
the subgroup of block matrices of the form
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
0&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)
where

and

. The maximal torus

is the subgroup of diagonal matrices with non-zero entries, and the Weyl group

is the symmetric group
S_{n}
on
n
letters.
For any

, the polytope
\mu(\overline{Tx})
is then a Coxeter matroid associated to
{S}_{n}
which is representable over

the set of these objects, which are just called

(1.1)

G(d,n)=\bigsqcup_{M\in M^{d}_{S_{n}}(K)}G_{M},

in a disjoint union of the (nonempty) locally closed sets

. The set

is called the
(symmetric) thin Schubert cell
of

associated to

. These thin Schubert cells are

(1.2)

where

and the action of

Our aim now is to apply the program from
[

We have that for any

Our main results

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The Grassmannian of lines
G(2,2n)

In this section we recall some basic facts about Grassmannians of lines. Unless otherwise stated, order arguments on
[2n]
will be made with respect to the usual order:
1<2<\cdots<2n
.

Let

denote the Grassmannian variety of
2
-dimensional vector subspaces in a complex vector space of dimension
2n
, which we consider embedded in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
by means of the Plücker map.
The algebraic
2n
-dimensional torus
T^{\prime}
of invertible diagonal matrices
\text{diag}(\lambda_{i})=\text{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2n})
with
\lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
acts on a point

by scaling the columns of a matrix representation of it. Thus,
if

has Plücker coordinates

and
\text{diag}(\lambda_{i})\in T^{\prime}
, then
\text{diag}(\lambda_{i})\cdot x
has Plucker coordinates

.

2.2.
S_{2n}
-Matroids of rank 2

Given
x\in G(2,2n)
with coordinates
[x_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}(x)=\{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[2n]}{2}\>:\>x_{i,j}\neq 0\}
. Let
\mu_{T^{\prime}}:G(2,2n)\to\mathbb{R}^{2n}
be the corresponding moment map. If
\{e_{i}\>:\>1\leq i\leq 2n\}
is the canonical basis of

, then
\overline{T^{\prime}\cdot x}
is a (normal) toric variety of dimension

satisfying

(2.1)

\mu_{T^{\prime}}(\overline{T^{\prime}\cdot x})=\text{Conv}\{e_{i}+e_{j}\>:\>\{i,j\}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}(x)\}.

Conversely, we can recover the set
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}(x)
from the polytope
\mu_{T^{\prime}}(\overline{T^{\prime}\cdot x})
by taking its set of integral points:
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}(x)=\mu_{T^{\prime}}(\overline{T^{\prime}\cdot x})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
.
In particular, if
x=[x_{\{i,j\}}]
satisfies
x_{\{i,j\}}\neq 0
for all
\{i,j\}\in\binom{[2n]}{2}
, then
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}(x)=\mu_{T^{\prime}}(G(2,2n))
is the hypersimplex

(2.2)

\Delta(2,2n)=\text{Conv}\Big\{e_{1}+e_{j}\>:\>\{i,j\}\in\binom{[2n]}{2}\Big\}\subset H

which is a lattice polytope of maximal dimension
2n-1
inside the the affine hyperplane
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\>:\>\sum_{i}x_{i}=2\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

If
x\in G(2,2n)
, we call either the subpolytope
\mu_{T^{\prime}}(\overline{T^{\prime}\cdot x})\subset\Delta(2,2n)
or the pair

the
S_{2n}
-
matroid
of

, and we denote it by

.

Remark 2.2
.

We have that
M^{\prime}(x)
is an
S_{2n}
-matroid of rank 2 on
[2n]
, and every such matroid is of this form. This means that the set
M^{2}_{S_{2n}}
of

-matroids of rank 2 on

coincides with the set

of
S_{2n}
-matroids of rank 2 representable over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Definition 2.3
.

A non-intersecting family of
[2n]
is a family
\Pi=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{l}\}
of
l\geq 2
non-empty, pairwise disjoint subsets of
[2n]
. Sometimes we refer to the sets
P_{i}
as the bags of

.

A matroid of rank 2 on
[2n]
is a pair
M=([2n],\Pi)
, where
\Pi
is a non-intersecting family of
E_{n}
. The set of bases
B(M)
of this matroid is the set of partial
2
-transversals of
\Pi
. See
[
6
]
, where they were called
partitions
.

2.3.
The quotient set
M^{2}_{S_{2n}}/S_{2n}

Following
[
6
, Section 4]
,
given
\tau\in S_{2n}
and
\{i,j\}\in\binom{[2n]}{2}
, we define
\tau(\{i,j\})=\{\tau(i),\tau(j)\}
. We can define an action of
S_{2n}
on
M^{2}_{S_{2n}}
as follows: if
M=([2n],B)\in M^{2}_{S_{2n}}
and
\tau\in S_{2n}
, then
\tau\cdot M:=([2n],\tau(B))
. The resulting orbit space

can be described through partitions as follows.

We denote by
\Pi_{2n}
the set of formal expressions
\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}k_{i}
such that
\ell\geq 2
,
1\leq k_{i}<2n
, and
w(\pi)=\sum_{i}k_{i}\leq 2n
. Then
M^{2}_{S_{2n}}/S_{2n}\cong\Pi_{2n}
as sets (cf.
[
6
, Theorem 4.8]
). Furthermore, there is a canonical section
\Pi_{2n}\hookrightarrow M^{2}_{S_{2n}}/S_{2n}
constructed as follows: for
\pi=\prod_{i=1}^{l}k_{i}\in\Pi_{2n}
with
k_{1}\geq\cdots\geq k_{l}
and
w(\pi)=\sum_{i}k_{i}
, we will form a particular non-intersecting family
\{P_{k_{1}},\ldots,P_{k_{l}}\}
of
[w(\pi)]=\{1,\ldots,w(\pi)\}
, which we will also call

, defined by:

(2.3)

P_{k_{i}}=\begin{cases}\{1,\ldots,k_{1}\},&\text{ if }i=1,\\
\{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i-1}+1,\ldots,k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}\},&\text{ if }i>1.\\
\end{cases}

We denote by

the loopless matroid on

defined by the partition (

), that is,

is not a basis if and only if

The section

sends

to

, where
(U_{1}^{0})^{\oplus{[n-w(\pi)]}}
is the uniform matroid on

of rank 0, i.e., it only consists of loops.

Remark 2.4
.

With these observations we can see that any matroid of rank 2 is representable. If
M
is a matroid with equivalence class

, then there exists
\tau\in S_{n}
such that
\tau(M)=M_{\pi}
. It is easy to construct a linear space

of dimension 2 such that

, namely, we choose

2.4.
BC_{n}
-Matroids of rank 2

The source for this part is

(1) We say that

We will consider the standard admissible ordering

(3) For any admissible order

(1)

We say that

(2)

Given

The number of admissible orders on

3.
The Symplectic Grassmannian of isotropic lines

Given a positive integer
n
, let
V
be complex vector space
V
of dimension
2n
endowed with a non-degenerate alternating bi-linear form
\omega
. Given another integer
1\leq k\leq n
, an element
W\in G(k,2n)=G(k,V)
is isotropic if
\omega(W,W)=0
. As a set,
the
symplectic Grassmannian
is

\text{SpG}(k,2n)=\{W\in G(k,2n):W\text{ is isotropic}\}.

This is a projective variety that can be described from different perspectives, which we now recall.

3.1.
As a homogeneous space

Choosing an adequate basis of the symplectic vector space
(V,\omega)
, the Gram matrix of the symplectic form is
J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&J_{n}\\
-J_{n}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, where
J_{n}
is the
n\times n
matrix with
1
’s in its antidiagonal. This defines the involution
\text{GL}_{2n}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\text{GL}_{2n}
as
A\mapsto J(A^{t})^{-1}J^{-1}
, where
J^{-1}=-J
. Hence,
the symplectic group is
\text{Sp}_{2n}=\{A\in\text{GL}_{2n}\>:\>\sigma(A)=A\}
, The natural transitive action of
\text{GL}_{2n}
on the Grassmannian
G(k,2n)
restricts to an action of
\text{Sp}_{2n}
on the symplectic Grassmannian
\text{SpG}(k,2n)
, which is transitive by the Witt Extension Theorem
[
8
]
.
The Borel group of
\text{Sp}_{2n}
is the intersection
B=B^{\prime}\cap\text{Sp}_{2n}

of the Borel subgroup
B^{\prime}
of
\text{GL}_{2n}
with
\text{Sp}_{2n}
. Thence, writing the Grassmannian as a homogeneous space

G(k,2n)\cong\text{GL}_{2n}/P^{\prime},

where
P^{\prime}=\text{Stab}_{\text{GL}_{2n}}(W)\subseteq\text{GL}_{2n}
a maximal parabolic subgroup, the stabilizer of an element
W\in G(k,2n)
, and using that the action of
\text{Sp}_{2n}
is transitive, we can write

It is known that

is a smooth
projective irreducible variety of dimension

.
We have:

(1)

The natural map

is a closed embedding.

(2)

B\omega P\subset B^{\prime}\omega P^{\prime}
for any

and moreover,

(3)

In particular, the Schubert cells in
\text{SpG}(k,2n)\cong\text{Sp}_{2n}/P
are restrictions to
\text{Sp}_{2n}/P
of the Schubert cells in
\text{GL}_{2n}/P^{\prime}

(3.1)

in

As a linear section of the Grassmannian

Lemma 3.8
.

If

, then the stabilizer of any element, in the thin Schubert cell that it defines under the action of the torus

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
M=(E_{n},B)
be a matroid,
G_{M}\subset G(2,2n)
be the thin Schubert cell associated to it and let
\mathcal{H}=Z(s)
for
s
as in (
3.3
).

(1)

If there is no pair
\{j,j^{*}\}\in B
, then
G_{M}\subset\mathcal{H}
.

(2)

If there is only one pair
\{j,j^{*}\}\in B
, then
G_{M}\cap\mathcal{H}=\emptyset
.

(3)

If there are at least two pairs of the form
\{j,j^{*}\}\in B
, then
G_{M}\not\subset\mathcal{H}
,

G_{M}\cap\mathcal{H}\neq\emptyset
and for any
x\in G_{M}
, we have that
T^{\prime}x\cap\mathcal{H}
is a hypersurface on
T^{\prime}x
and therefore it is irreducible.

Proof.

(1) By definition, the only coordinates that are different from zero for any point in
G_{M}
are precisely those corresponding to pairs
\{i,j\}\in B
, therefore if no
\{j,j^{*}\}
is in
B
, all corresponding coordinates in the equation
s
are zero and so is their sum.

(2) By the same reason, if only one such coordinate is in
B
, that coordinate, and only that coordinate among the
x_{j,j^{*}}
is different from zero, so the sum can not be zero and the intersection is empty.

(3) Finally, if there are at least two such pairs in
B
, then it is always possible to find a point in
G_{M}
where their sum is zero, since the only restriction imposed by
M
is that they are not zero each.

Now let
x=(a_{i,j})\in G_{M}
and
\lambda=\diag(\lambda_{j})\in T^{\prime}
. Then
\lambda\cdot x\in G_{M}\cap\mathcal{H}

if and only if

\sum_{\{j,j^{*}\}\in M}\lambda_{j}\,\lambda_{j^{*}}a_{j,j^{*}}=0.

which defines a polynomial equation on the
\lambda_{j}
’s and the
\lambda_{j^{*}}
’s and therefore

T^{\prime}x\cap\mathcal{H}
is isomorphic to the hypersurface on
T^{\prime}
defined by this equation. In particular it is irreducible.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

If

Now we show its irreducibility. If

there is nothing to prove. On the other hand, if

but

Definition 4.4

In

]

(2)

Note that if

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
M_{1},M_{2}
be two matroids of degree at least
2
such that

\pi(M_{1})=\pi(M_{2})
. Then, there exists a matroid

such that:

(i)

\pi(M)=\pi(M_{1})=\pi(M_{2})
.

(ii)

If
\mu_{i}

\mu_{1}(M)=M_{1}
and
\mu_{2}(M)=M_{2}

Proof.

If
A(M_{1})\subsetneq A(M_{2})
we must create only the base
\{i,i^{*}\}
for
i\in A(M_{2})\setminus A(M_{1})
without changing the other bases. We will do this by induction.
If
A(M_{2})\setminus A(M_{1})=\{i\}
, and
M_{1}
is given by the disjoint family
\Pi=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{\ell}\}
, it follows that, reordering if necessary, there exists
P_{i}=\{i,i^{*}\}
such that
M_{2}=\sigma_{P_{i}|i}(M_{1})
, which proves the first case.

For
the general case,
A(M_{2})\setminus A(M_{1})=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}
with
m\geq 2
. Recall that
given
\{a,b,c,d\}\subset E_{n}
, their three diagonals are

\{\{a,b\},\{c,d\}\},\quad\{\{a,c\},\{b,d\}\},\quad\{\{a,d\},\{b,c\}\}.

We denote by
\rho(\{a,b,c,d\})
the octahedral Grassmann-Plücker relation involving these three diagonals.
Now, for every pair
i,k
in the set
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}
, we have that
\{i,i^{*}\},\{k,k^{*}\}
are bases of
M_{2}
, which means that for any
x\in G_{M_{2}}
, the product
x_{\{i,i^{*}\}}x_{\{k,k^{*}\}}
is nonzero in the octahedral Grassmann-Plücker relation
\rho(\{i,i^{*},k,k^{*}\})
. Hence, there is at least another diagonal
D
whose product is nonzero.
On the other hand, neither
\{i,i^{*}\}
nor
\{k,k^{*}\}
are bases for
M_{1}
(thus,
y_{\{i,i^{*}\}}y_{\{k,k^{*}\}}=0
for any

) and the above diagonal
D
should also appear in
M_{1}
, thus the Grassmann-Plücker relation
\rho(\{i,i^{*},k,k^{*}\})
tells us that actually the third diagonal appears also in
M_{1}
. Hence all three diagonals must appear in
M_{2}
.

Let
\Pi(M_{1})=\{P_{a}\}_{a}
and
\Pi(M_{2})=\{Q_{b}\}_{b}
. It follows that
i,i^{*}\in P_{a}
,
k,k^{*}\in P_{b}
with

, and

,

,

and

.

We claim that
Q_{i}=\{i\}
and

. Suppose that this is not the case, say
\ell\in Q_{i}
with

, then

is a common basis of both matroids, but then we erase
\{i,i^{*}\}
from
M_{1}
when merging
Q_{i}
and
Q_{i^{*}}
, which is a contradiction.

Now, if
\ell\in P_{a}-\{i,i^{*}\}
, then
\{\ell,i\}
and

, which is a contradiction since they do not appear in

. It follows that

.

Therefore,
M_{1}
is given by the disjoint family
\Pi=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{\ell}\}
, and for every
i\in A(M_{2})\setminus A(M_{1})

such that

M_{2}=\sigma_{i_{m}|i_{m}}\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{i_{1}|i_{1}}(M_{1}).

This concludes the proof in the case when
A(M_{1})\subsetneq A(M_{2})
and
the case

is similar.

We now suppose that
A(M_{1})\not\subset A(M_{2})
and
A(M_{2})\not\subset A(M_{1})
.
The simplest sub-case is when
A(M_{2})\setminus A(M_{1})=\{i\}
and
A(M_{1})\setminus A(M_{2})=\{j\}
. Since these matroids have degree 2, there is
k\in A(M_{2})\cap A(M_{1})
with

.

Since
i\in A(M_{2})\setminus A(M_{1})
and
k\in A(M_{2})\cap A(M_{1})
, we have that
\{i,i^{*}\},\{k,k^{*}\}
are bases of
M_{2}
, which means that
x_{\{i,i^{*}\}}x_{\{k,k^{*}\}}\neq 0
in the octahedral Grassmann-Plücker relation
\rho(\{i,i^{*},k,k^{*}\})
, so there is at least another diagonal
D
whose product is nonzero.
On the other hand,
\{i,i^{*}\}
is not a basis for
M_{1}
(thus,
y_{\{i,i^{*}\}}y_{\{k,k^{*}\}}=0
) and the diagonal
D
also appears in
M_{1}
. Hence, the Grassmann-Plücker relation
\rho(\{i,i^{*},k,k^{*}\})
implies that the third diagonal appears in
M_{1}
. Hence all three diagonals must appear in
M_{2}
.
Setting

\Pi(M_{1})=\{P_{a}\}_{a}
and
\Pi(M_{2})=\{Q_{b}\}_{b}
, it follows that
i,i^{*}\in P_{a}
,

and

, with

Thus

. Similarly we find

The next simplest case is when

, (thus,

.

Assume

\displaystyle B(N)

\displaystyle=\left\{\{m,n\}\,|\,m\in P_{a},n\in P_{b}\quad\text{for some }1\leq a,b\leq s;a\neq b\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{\{t,j\}\,|\,t\in P_{k},j\in A(M)\quad\text{for some }1\leq k\leq s\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{\{t,j^{*}\}\,|\,t\in P_{k},j\in A(M)\quad\text{for some }1\leq k\leq s\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{\{j_{a},j_{b}\}\,|\,\text{for }1\leq a<b\leq r\right\}\cup\left\{\{j_{a},j_{b}^{*}\}\,|\,\text{for }1\leq a,b\leq r,a\neq b\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{\{j_{b}^{*},j_{a}^{*}\}\,|\,\text{for }1\leq a<b\leq r\right\}

and

B(M)=B(N)\cup\left\{\{j,j^{*}\}\,|\,j\in A(M)\right\}.

We claim that
\diag(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n^{*}},\dots,\lambda_{1^{*}})\in\operatorname{Stab}_{T^{\prime}}(N)
if and only if

(4.8)

\lambda_{t}=\lambda_{j_{\ell}}=\lambda_{j_{\ell}^{*}}

for all

, for all

1\leq\ell\leq r
. In particular, this implies that
\diag(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n^{*}},\dots,\lambda_{1^{*}})\in\operatorname{Stab}_{T}(N)
if and only if

(4.9)

\lambda_{t}=\lambda_{j_{\ell}}=\lambda_{j_{\ell}^{*}}=\pm 1.

for all

, for all

and for all

Once more, the additional conditions imposed to be in

Again, to simplify notation we will write down the proof when

Under this simplified assumption,
\diag(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n^{*}},\dots,\lambda_{1^{*}})\in\operatorname{Stab}_{T^{\prime}}(N)
if and only if

\lambda_{t}\lambda_{j_{1}}=\lambda_{t}\lambda_{j_{1}^{*}}=\lambda_{t}\lambda_{j_{2}}=\lambda_{t}\lambda_{j_{2}^{*}}=\lambda_{j_{1}}\lambda_{j_{2}}=\lambda_{j_{1}}\lambda_{j_{2}^{*}}=\lambda_{j_{1}^{*}}\lambda_{j_{2}}=\lambda_{j_{1}^{*}}\lambda_{j_{2}^{*}}

) and henceforth also Equation (

For each

By Corollary

Definition 5.1
.

Let
M=(E,\Pi)
be a matroid with
\Pi=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{\ell}\}
. The length of
M
is
\ell(M)=\ell
, and the weight of
M
is

.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
N
be a
\mathbb{C}
-representable symplectic matroids of rank
2
.
Then,
\dim\text{\rm SpG}_{N}=\dim T-\dim\operatorname{Stab_{T}(M)}+\dim\text{\rm Sp}\mathcal{G}_{N}
, where
M=(E_{n},\Pi)
is a symmetric lifting of
N
of maximal degree. Moreover, we have

\dim\text{\rm SpG}_{N}=\begin{cases}0+0,&\text{if }\ell(P)=2,\\
[w(M)-1]+[\ell(M)-3],&\text{if }\ell(M)>2,\deg(M)<1,\\
[w(M)-\deg(M)]+[\ell(M)+\deg(M)-5],&\text{if }\ell(M)>2,\deg(M)>1,\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

If
M
is a symmetric lifting of
N
of maximal degree, then it satisfies
\overline{\text{SpG}_{N}}=\overline{G_{M}|_{H}}
, thus we have
\dim\text{SpG}_{N}=\dim G_{M}
if
\deg M<1
, and
\text{SpG}_{N}=\dim G_{M}-1
if deg

.

On the other hand, by
[
6

we have
\dim G_{M}=w(M)+\ell(M)-4
, and if
\ell(M)>2
, then
\dim G_{M}=[w(M)-1]+[\ell(M)-3]
where the first summand is the dimension of the fiber and the second is the dimension of the basis in the torus bundle decomposition of
G_{M}
.

If the degree is 0, then the thin Schubert cell is contained in the hyperplane, so the dimension of the whole cell and the the dimension of the symplectic cell remain the same.

If the degree is not 0, the dimension of the thin Schubert cell

is

∎

Discrepancies and their means
M.M. SKRIGANOV

Abstract
. It is shown that the discrepancy function for point distributions on a torus is expressed by an explicit formula in terms of its mean values on sub-tori. As an application of this formula, a simple proof of a theorem of Lev [ 2 ] on the equivalence of L ∞ L_{\infty} – and shifted L q L_{q} –discrepancies is given.

11K38

The point distribution problem on the

d
-dimensional
torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

is conveniently considered as a periodic problem on the covering space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
For
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
Y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{d})\in{\mathbb{K}}^{d}=[0,1]^{d}
,
we define the periodic discrepancy function by

\displaystyle L(X,Y)=\chi(X,Y)-v(Y),

(1)

where

\chi(X,Y)=\prod\nolimits_{j=1}^{d}\chi(x_{j},y_{j}),\,\,v(Y)=\prod\nolimits_{j=1}^{d}v(y_{j}),v(y)=y,

and

\displaystyle\chi(x,y)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $\{x\}<y$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(2)

here
\{x\}
is the fractional part of
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
y\in[0,1]
.
It is clear that
\chi(X,Y)
is the indicator function of the periodic
collection of rectangular boxes

\displaystyle{\mathscr{B}}(Y)=\bigcup\nolimits_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\prod\nolimits^{d}_{j=1}[m_{j},y_{j}+m_{j}).

The mean value of the discrepancy function (1) has the form

\displaystyle\lambda(X)=\int\nolimits_{{\mathbb{K}}^{d}}L(X,Y)\,\mathrm{d}Y=\prod\nolimits_{j=1}^{d}(1-\{x_{j}\})-2^{-d}.

(3)

Let
[d]=(1,\dots,d)
denote the set of coordinate indexes. For subsets

J\subseteq[d]
we introduce the partial discrepancy functions by

\displaystyle L_{J}(X,Y)=\chi_{J}(X,Y)-v_{J}(Y),

(4)

where

\chi_{J}(X,Y)=\prod\nolimits_{j\in J}\chi(x_{j},y_{j}),\,\,v_{J}(Y)=\prod\nolimits_{j\in J}v(y_{j}),

The corresponding mean values have the form

\displaystyle\lambda_{J}(X)=\int\nolimits_{{\mathbb{T}}^{|J|}}L_{J}(X,Y_{J})\,\mathrm{d}Y_{J}=\prod\nolimits_{j\in J}(1-\{x_{j}\})-2^{-|J|},

(5)

where
{\mathbb{T}}^{|J|}\subseteq{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
denotes the sub-torus corresponding to the
subset
J\subseteq[d]
, and
|J|
denotes the number of elements of
J
.

The quantities (
4
), (
5
) depend on the projections

X_{J}=(x_{j})_{j\in J}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|J|},Y_{J}=(y_{j})_{j\in J}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{|J|}
, but not on
the additional variables

X_{J^{\prime}}=(x_{j})_{j\in J^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|J^{\prime}|},Y_{J^{\prime}}=(y_{j})_{j\in J^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{|J^{\prime}|}
,
where
J^{\prime}=[d]\setminus J
denotes the compliment of
J
. For
J=[d]
, we write

L_{[d]}(X,Y)=L(X,Y),\,\lambda_{[d]}(X)=\lambda(X)
, and for the empty set,
we put

L_{\emptyset}(X,Y)=0,\,\lambda_{\emptyset}(X)=0
.

Definition.

For a periodic function
f(X)=f(X_{J},X_{J^{\prime}}),X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
a vector

Y_{J}=(y_{j})_{j\in J}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{|J|}
, the
alternant
is defined by

\displaystyle f^{(alt)}(X\,|\,Y_{J})=f^{(alt)}(X_{J},X_{J^{\prime}}\,|\,Y_{J})=\sum\nolimits_{\Theta_{J}}(-1)^{|\Theta_{J}|}f(X_{J}-\Theta_{J}\cdot Y_{J},X_{J^{\prime}}),

(6)

where
\Theta_{J}=(\theta_{j})_{j\in J}\in\{0,1\}^{|J|}
are the
vertexes of the cube
{\mathbb{K}}^{|J|}
, and summation in (
6
) is taken over
all such
vertexes,
\Theta_{J}\cdot Y_{J}=(\theta_{j}y_{j})_{j\in J}
and
|\Theta_{J}|=\sum\nolimits_{j\in J}\theta_{j}
.

Remark.
If
f(X)=\prod\nolimits_{j\in J}f_{j}(x_{j})
,
then

\displaystyle f^{(alt)}(X\,|\,Y_{J})=\prod\nolimits_{j\in J}f^{(alt)}_{j}(x_{j}\,|\,y_{j})=\prod\nolimits_{j\in J}(f(x_{j})-f(x_{j}-y_{j})),

(7)

and if at least one of the functions
f_{j}
is constant, then

f^{(alt)}=0
.

Theorem (Main Identity).

The discrepancy function
L(X,Y)

satisfies the
identity

\displaystyle L(X,Y)=\sum\nolimits_{J\subseteq[d]}\,v_{J^{\prime}}(Y)\,\lambda^{(alt)}_{J}(X\,|\,Y_{J}).

(8)

Proof.

Let
\omega(x)=\frac{1}{2}-\{x\}
, then
\int\nolimits^{1}_{0}\omega(x)\mathrm{d}x=0
.
We put

\displaystyle\omega_{J}(X)=\prod\nolimits_{j\in J}\omega(x_{j})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\omega_{\emptyset}(X)=0.

(9)

The indicator function (
2
) can be written in the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\chi(x,y)&=y-\{x\}+\{x-y\}\\
&=y+\omega(x)-\omega(x-y)=y+\omega^{(alt)}(x\,|\,y),\end{split}

(10)

This formula can be proved by considering the graph of the function

\{x\}-\{x-y\},x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Substituting (
10
) into (
1
) and using (
9
) and
(
7
), we obtain

\displaystyle L(X,Y)=\sum\nolimits_{J\subseteq[d]}\,v_{J^{\prime}}(Y)\,\omega^{(alt)}_{J}(X\,|\,Y_{J}).

For the mean value (
5
), we find

\displaystyle\lambda_{J}(X)=\prod\nolimits_{j\in J}(2^{-1}+\omega(x_{j}))-2^{-|J|}=\sum\nolimits_{I\subseteq J}\,2^{-|J\setminus I|}\,\,\omega_{I}(X).

(11)

Let us calculate the alternant of the mean value (
11
). We have

\displaystyle\lambda^{(alt)}_{J}(X\,|\,Y_{J})=\omega^{(alt)}_{J}(X)+\sum\nolimits_{I\subset J}\,2^{-|J\setminus I|}\,\,\omega^{(alt)}_{I}(X\,|\,Y_{J}).

By the above Remark
\omega^{(alt)}_{I}(X\,|\,Y_{J})=0
for proper subsets

I\subset J
, since

\omega_{J}(X)=\prod\nolimits_{j\in I}\omega(x_{j})\prod\nolimits_{j\in J\setminus I}1
.
Therefore,
\lambda^{(alt)}_{J}(X\,|\,Y_{J})=\omega^{(alt)}_{J}(X\,|\,Y_{J})
, and
Theorem follows.
∎

We consider periodic point distributions
{\mathscr{D}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathscr{D}}+M={\mathscr{D}},M\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},

with a finite set of residues
{\mathscr{D}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
. Notice that instead of point
distributions,
arbitrary periodic complex Borel measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
finite on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
could
be considered but
we do not consider such a generalization in order not to complicate the
notation.

We define the local discrepancy

\displaystyle L[{\mathscr{D}},Y]=\sum\nolimits_{X\in{\mathscr{D}}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}L(X,Y),

(12)

and the
L_{q}
– discrepancies

\displaystyle L_{q}[{\mathscr{D}}]=\left(\int\nolimits_{{\mathbb{K}}^{d}}|L[{\mathscr{D}},Y]|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}Y\right)^{1/q},\,0<q<\infty,\quad\quad L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}]=\sup\nolimits_{Y\in{\mathbb{K}}^{d}}|L[{\mathscr{D}},Y]|.

We also introduce the shifted discrepancies

\displaystyle L_{q}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]=\sup\nolimits_{Z\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}L_{q}[{\mathscr{D}}+Z],\quad\quad L_{\infty}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]=\sup\nolimits_{Z\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}+Z],

and their mean values

\displaystyle\lambda_{J}[{\mathscr{D}}]=\sum\nolimits_{X\in{\mathscr{D}}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\lambda_{J}(X),\quad\quad\lambda^{*}_{J}[{\mathscr{D}}]=\sup\nolimits_{Z\in{\mathbb{K}}^{d}}\lambda_{J}[{\mathscr{D}}+Z].

The above Theorem immediately implies the following.

Lemma 1.

The local discrepancy
L[{\mathscr{D}},Y]
satisfies the
identity

\displaystyle L[{\mathscr{D}},Y]=\sum\nolimits_{J\subseteq[d]}\,v_{J^{\prime}}(Y)\,\lambda^{(alt)}_{J}\,[{\mathscr{D}}\,|\,Y_{J}],

(13)

where
\lambda^{(alt)}_{J}[{\mathscr{D}}\,|\,Y_{J}]=\sum\nolimits_{X\in{\mathscr{D}}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\,\lambda^{(alt)}_{J}(X\,|\,Y_{J})
.

The discrepancy
L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}]
satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}]\leq\sum\nolimits_{J\subseteq[d]}\,2^{|J|}\,\lambda^{*}_{J}[{\mathscr{D}}].

(14)

Proof.

The identity (
13
) follows from (
8
). The definition
(
6
) implies

\lambda^{(alt)}_{J}[{\mathscr{D}}\,|\,Y_{J}]\leq 2^{|J|}\,\lambda^{*}_{J}[{\mathscr{D}}],

and the inequality (
14
) follows, since
0\leq v_{J^{\prime}}(Y)\leq 1
.
∎

The
L_{q}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]
-discrepancies can be easily estimated by means from below.

Lemma 2.

For
1\leq q\leq\infty
and any subset
J\subseteq[d]
,
the discrepancy
L_{q}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]

satisfies the
inequality

\displaystyle L_{q}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]\geq 2^{d-|J|}\,\lambda^{*}_{J}[{\mathscr{D}}].

(15)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle L_{q}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]\geq L_{1}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]

\displaystyle=\sup\nolimits_{Z\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\int\nolimits_{{\mathbb{K}}^{d}}|L[{\mathscr{D}}+Z,Y]|\,\mathrm{d}Y\geq\sup\nolimits_{Z\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\left|\int\nolimits_{{\mathbb{K}}^{d}}L[{\mathscr{D}}+Z,Y]\,\mathrm{d}Y\right|

\displaystyle\geq\sup\nolimits_{Z\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\left|\lambda[{\mathscr{D}}+Z]\right|=\lambda^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}].

For simplicity, we put
Z=(Z_{J},Z_{J^{\prime}})\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},Z_{J}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{|J|},Z_{J^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{|J^{\prime}|}
, and continue

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]

\displaystyle=\sup\nolimits_{Z_{J}}\sup\nolimits_{Z_{J^{\prime}}}|\lambda[{\mathscr{D}}+Z]|\geq\sup\nolimits_{Z_{J}}\int\nolimits_{{\mathbb{K}}^{|J^{\prime}|}}|\lambda[{\mathscr{D}}+Z]|\,\mathrm{d}Z_{J^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq\sup\nolimits_{Z_{J}}\left|\int\nolimits_{{\mathbb{K}}^{|J^{\prime}|}}\lambda[{\mathscr{D}}+Z]\,\mathrm{d}Z_{J^{\prime}}\right|=2^{-|J^{\prime}|}\sup\nolimits_{Z_{J}}|\lambda[{\mathscr{D}}+Z_{J}]|=2^{d-|J|}\,\lambda^{*}_{J}[{\mathscr{D}}],

that completes the proof.
∎

The next simple fact is well–known, see, for example,
[
2
, p. 4]
. For
completeness, we give
a short proof.

Lemma 3.

The
L_{\infty}
– and
L_{\infty}^{*}
–
discrepancies
are equivalent:

\displaystyle L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}]\,\leq\,L_{\infty}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]\,\leq\,3^{d}L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}].

(16)

Proof.

The left inequality (
16
) is obvious. Let us prove
the right. For
y\in[0,1],\,z\in[0,1)
, we introduce the notation

\displaystyle\delta_{y,z}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if \,$y+z\geq 1$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

The shifted indicator function, see
(
2
) and (
10
), can be written in the form

\displaystyle\chi(x+z,y)=\chi(x,y+{z})[1-\delta_{y,z}]+\chi(x,y+{z}-1)\,\delta_{y,z}-\chi(x,{z})+\chi(x,1)\,\delta_{y,z},

and similarly
v(y)=v(y+z)[1-\delta_{y,z}]+v(y+z-1)\,\delta_{y,z}-v(z)+v(1)\,\delta_{y,z}.
Moreover, each of these formulas contains at most three
non-zero terms.
Substituting these formulas into the definitions (
1
) and
(
12
), we find that the shifted discrepancy can be written as the sum

L[{\mathscr{D}}+Z,Y]=\sum\nolimits_{k}c_{k}\,L[{\mathscr{D}},V_{k}],

with some vectors
V_{k}=V_{k}(Y,Z)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{d}
and coefficients
c_{k}=c_{k}(Y,Z)
equal
either

\pm 1
or
0
.
Moreover, the sum contain at most
3^{d}
non-zero terms. This implies the
right inequality (
16
).
∎

Lev
[
2
]
established the equivalence of
L_{\infty}
– and

L_{q}^{*}
– discrepancies:

L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}]\,\cong\,L_{q}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]
,
for all
q\geq 1
with the implicit constants depending only on the
dimension
d
. Another proof of the equivalence was given later by
Kolountzakis, we refer to the survey article
[
1
]
by Chen for a
detailed discussion of these issues.

The equivalence of the
L_{\infty}
– and

L_{q}^{*}
– discrepancies can be easily
derived from the foregoing statements.
We will formulate and prove
the corresponding result in the following somewhat more general form.

Corollary (Lev’s Equivalence).

For
0<q<\infty
, the

L_{\infty}
– and

L_{q}^{*}
– discrepancies are equivalent
:

\displaystyle 3^{-d}\,L^{*}_{q}[{\mathscr{D}}]\,\leq\,\,L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}]\,\leq\,C_{d,q}\,L^{*}_{q}[{\mathscr{D}}],

(17)

where the constant

\displaystyle C_{d,q}=\begin{cases}\,(5/2)^{d},&\text{if $1\leq q<\infty$,}\\
\,(5/2)^{d/q}\,3^{d/q\,-d},&\text{if $0<q<1.$}\end{cases}

(18)

We did not seek to obtain the best constant
C_{d,q}
in (
17
),
however, we note that
the formula (
18
) correctly reflects the order of the constant for
large and small
q
.

Proof of Corollary.

The proof consists of three steps.

(i)
For
0<q<{\infty}
, the lower bound (
17
) follows
from Lemma 3:

L^{*}_{q}[{\mathscr{D}}]\,\leq L_{\infty}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]\,\leq\,3^{d}L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}],

since
L^{*}_{q}[{\mathscr{D}}]
is a non-decreasing function of
q>0
.

(ii)
For
1\leq q<\infty
,
the upper bound (
17
) follows from Lemma 1 and Lemma 2.
Substituting (
15
) into (
14
), we obtain

\displaystyle L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}]\leq 2^{-d}\,\left(\sum\nolimits_{J\subseteq[d]}\,\,2^{2|J|}\right)\,\,L_{q}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]=(5/2)^{d}\,\,L_{q}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}].

(iii)
Finally, for
0<q<1
, the upper bound (
17
) follows from
the logarithmic convexity of
L_{q}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]
as a function of
q>0
.
By the standard interpolation at points
q<1<p
, we obtain

\displaystyle L_{1}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]\leq(L_{q}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}])^{q\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\,(L_{p}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}])^{p\frac{1-q}{p-q}}.

This inequality takes the form

\displaystyle L_{1}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]\leq(L_{q}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}])^{q}\,(L_{\infty}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}])^{1-q},

(19)

as
p\to\infty
.

The bounds

(5/2)^{-d}\,L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}]\leq L_{1}^{*}[{\mathscr{D}}]
and
L^{*}_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}]\leq 3^{d}\,L_{\infty}[{\mathscr{D}}],

are already established.
Substituting these bounds into (
19
), we obtain the upper bound
(
17
) with the constant (
18
).

The proof of Corollary is completed.
∎

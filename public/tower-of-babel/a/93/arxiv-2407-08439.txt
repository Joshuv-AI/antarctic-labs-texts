A fitted space-time finite element method for an advection-diffusion problem with moving interfaces
Quang Huy Nguyen, Van Chien Le, Phuong Cuc Hoang, Thi Thanh Mai Ta

Abstract
This paper presents a space-time interface-fitted finite element method for solving a parabolic advection-diffusion problem with a nonstationary interface. The jumping diffusion coefficient gives rise to the discontinuity of the solution gradient across the interface. We use the Banach-Nečas-Babuška theorem to show the well-posedness of the continuous variational problem. A fully discrete finite-element based scheme is analyzed using the Galerkin method and unstructured interface-fitted meshes. An optimal error estimate is established in a discrete energy norm under a globally low but locally high regularity condition. Some numerical results corroborate our theoretical results.

1.2
Relevant literature

The advection-diffusion problem (
1
) arises in various engineering applications of flows and other physical phenomena. The unknown function
u
may represent the concentration of a pollutant subject to the diffusion effect and is transported by a stream moving at velocity
\mathbf{v}
. Alternatively, it can represent the electron concentration in an electromagnetic device. In addition, the advection-diffusion equation (
1
) can model the eddy-current problem in electromagnetics
[
4
]
, the heat transfer
[
5
]
, or the induction heating process
[
6
]
, which involve moving multiple-component systems.

The difficulties of solving an interface problem are not only the non-smoothness of the solution but also related to the advection component. More specifically, the discontinuity of the diffusion coefficient
\kappa
at the interface results in a solution with a discontinuous gradient across the interface. Consequently, classical finite element methods applied to this class of problems converge with only sub-optimal orders, e.g.,
[
7
,
2
]
. In handling the issue, two major approaches have been extensively investigated over the last few decades: interface-unfitted and interface-fitted methods. The former approach does not require a triangulation that fits the interface. Instead, it accurately approximates discontinuous quantities by adjusting local finite element basis functions on interface elements. Examples of this class include the multiscale finite element method (MsFEM)
[
8
]
, the immersed finite element method (IFEM)
[
9
]
, and the extended finite element method (XFEM)
[
10
]
. In contrast, the interface-fitted methods generate a mesh that matches the interface, i.e., the mesh prevents the interface from cutting through an element or resolves the interface approximately to a certain precision
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

When the interface evolves, we cope with a moving discontinuity. The interface-unfitted approach takes advantage of allowing interface-independent simplicial triangulations, which is convenient when the interface depends on time. In
[
14
]
, the authors combined the XFEM with a backward Euler time discretization to solve a moving-interface problem. Lehrenfeld and Reusken in
[
15
,
16
]
proposed a second-order XFEM scheme using a space-time discontinuous Galerkin discretization. More recently, Badia et al.
[
17
]
presented the aggregation finite element method (AgFEM) for tackling the ill-conditioning and small cut cell issues in most interface-unfitted schemes. However, one standing drawback of these interface-unfitted methods is that they require special space-time quadrature rules on cut elements, resulting in high computational costs. In contrast to the success of interface-unfitted schemes, interface-fitted methods have received very little attention in solving moving-interface problems. The reason is that the re-meshing procedure at each time step introduces additional errors in interpolating two consecutive meshes, which can exceed the feasible effort.

Related to our setting, we also refer to the studies
[
18
,
19
]
that utilized the arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE) technique to solve moving-domain or moving-interface problems. This technique allows for good domain or interface resolution but is less practical when dealing with large deformations.

1.3
Contributions and outline

Our work resolves a challenge of the interface-fitted approach and proposes a new interface-fitted strategy for moving-interface advection-diffusion problems. We introduce a numerical scheme combining a space-time discretization with an interface-fitted mesh continuous Galerkin approximation. In contrast to all mentioned studies invoking the classical time-stepping methods or time-discontinuous Galerkin methods, our approach treats the time variable as another spatial variable. It solves the problem (
1
) in the space-time setting. The literature refers to this strategy as the space-time finite element method, introduced by Steinbach in
[
20
]
for solving non-interface heat equations. In this way, we can employ fully unstructured space-time meshes and cope with geometrically complicated interfaces. Furthermore, our approach takes advantage of the accuracy and practicality of interface-fitted methods with neither the implementation of costly re-meshing algorithms nor the computation of interaction integrals over moving interfaces or enrichment elements.

The space-time finite element method has been used in recent studies for solving moving-interface or moving-domain problems
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
. The authors of these works provided standard error estimates under the assumption that the solution is sufficiently smooth across the interface. This regularity assumption, however, is typically not satisfied in practical situations when the coefficient
\kappa
is discontinuous. In our work, we instead consider a globally low regularity solution. To rectify the non-smooth property of the solution and derive a priori error analysis for the discretization scheme, we combine the space-time technique
[
20
]
with the Stein extension operators
[
24
]
, which allows us to interpolate functions with locally high but globally low regularity. We arrive at an optimal convergence rate with respect to a mesh-dependent energy norm under an appropriate regularity condition. In the case of a weaker assumption, the convergence rate is nearly optimal when the spatial domain is one-dimensional (1D) and sub-optimal for the two-dimensional (2D) spatial domain. To the best of our knowledge, these types of error bounds do not appear anywhere else in the literature on moving-interface problems using the space-time method.

The paper is divided as follows. The following section provides necessary functional settings, followed by a variational formulation of the problem (
1
) in a space-time Sobolev space. The Banach-Nečas-Babuška theorem is involved in proving the well-posedness of the continuous variational problem. Section
3
is devoted to a Galerkin finite element discretization of this problem. We present the solvability of the discrete problem and derive a priori error estimates in a discrete energy norm under different smoothness assumptions. Some numerical experiments are performed in Section
4
to verify the theoretical analysis. Finally, Section
5
contains some conclusion remarks.

Well-posedness

Given a source term

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{\varphi\in\Ys\setminus\left\{{0}\right\}}\dfrac{a\left(u,\varphi\right)}{\left\lVert{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\Ys}}\geq C\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Xs}\qquad\qquad\forall u\in\Xs_{0}.

(4)

Proof.

For each
u\in\Xs_{0}
, we denote by

the unique solution to the auxiliary equation

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla z\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\left\langle{\partial_{t}u,\phi}\right\rangle_{\Ys^{\prime}\times\Ys}\qquad\qquad\forall\phi\in\Ys.

(5)

From Riesz’s representation theorem, it is clear that
\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert_{\Ys}=\left\lVert{\partial_{t}u}\right\rVert_{\Ys^{\prime}}
. The idea is to choose
\varphi=z+\lambda u\in\Ys
in (
4
) with sufficiently large
\lambda>0
. We immediately have

a\left(u,\varphi\right)=a\left(u,z+\lambda u\right)=a\left(u,z\right)+\lambda a\left(u,u\right).

For the first term, we use the Cauchy inequality to arrive at

\displaystyle a\left(u,z\right)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla z\cdot\nabla z\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u\right)z+\kappa\nabla u\cdot\nabla z\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ys}-\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\left(\dfrac{C}{4\varepsilon}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ys}+\varepsilon\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right)-\left(\dfrac{1}{4\varepsilon}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ys}+\varepsilon\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ys}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-C\varepsilon\right)\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ys}-\dfrac{C}{\varepsilon}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ys},

for any
\varepsilon>0
. We invoked the inequality
\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert_{\Ys}
for all
z\in\Ys
in the final step. In handling the term
a\left(u,u\right)
, we first deal with the advection part by utilizing the divergence theorem. Noting that
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t)=0
for all
\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)\in\Omega\times[0,T]
and

on
\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u\right)u\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\left(u^{2}\mathbf{v}\right)-u^{2}\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

(6)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\left(u^{2}\mathbf{v}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}u^{2}\mathbf{v}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\Omega}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=0,

with
\textbf{n}_{\Omega}
the unit outward normal to
\partial\Omega
. Then, we apply the integration by parts formula
[
28
, Lemma 7.3]
to get

\displaystyle a\left(u,u\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\partial_{t}u,u}\right\rangle_{\Ys^{\prime}\times\Ys}+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u\right)u+\kappa\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u\left(\cdot,0\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Ys}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Ys}^{2},

owing to
u\left(\cdot,0\right)=0
. Therefore, we end up with

a\left(u,\varphi\right)\geq\left(1-C\varepsilon\right)\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ys}+\left({\lambda-\dfrac{C}{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ys}=\left(1-C\varepsilon\right)\left\lVert{\partial_{t}u}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ys^{\prime}}+\left({\lambda-\dfrac{C}{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ys}.

We hence fix a small enough
\varepsilon>0
, then select an appropriate
\lambda>0
to obtain

In addition, we have

\left\lVert{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\Ys}=\left\lVert{z+\lambda u}\right\rVert_{\Ys}\leq\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert_{\Ys}+\lambda\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Ys}=\left\lVert{\partial_{t}u}\right\rVert_{\Ys^{\prime}}+\lambda\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Ys}\leq C\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Xs},

which leads to

\sup_{\varphi\in\Ys\setminus\left\{{0}\right\}}\dfrac{a(u,\varphi)}{\left\lVert{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\Ys}}\geq C\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Xs}\qquad\qquad\forall u\in\Xs_{0}.

Let

be the finite element space of continuous element-wise linear functions on

with zero values on

. We consider the following discrete variational problem of (

that solves

where the bilinear form

with

the mesh-dependent approximation of
\kappa

\kappa_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right):=\begin{cases}\kappa_{1}&\text{for }\,(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q_{1,h},\\
\kappa_{2}&\text{for }\,(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q_{2,h}.\end{cases}

The bilinear form
a_{h}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
differs from
a\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
in the coefficient
\kappa_{h}
substituting
\kappa
. For establishing the solvability of the problem (

), we introduce the following seminorm associated with

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}\kappa_{i}\left|{\nabla v}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall v\in\Hs^{1,0}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right).

Please note that this seminorm is defined on the space
\Hs^{1,0}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)
, which allows a jump of functions across the interface
\Gamma^{\ast}
. When restricted on
\Ys
, it defines an equivalent norm, also denoted by
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
. In practice, this norm is more favorable than
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\Ys}
since it involves the approximation
\kappa_{h}
, which is a constant on each element. Next, we consider the following auxiliary problem: For
v\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)
, determine
z_{h}\left(v\right)\in\Vs_{h}
such that

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\kappa_{h}\nabla z_{h}\left(v\right)\cdot\nabla\phi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}\partial_{t}v\,\phi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall\phi_{h}\in\Vs_{h}.

(10)

It is clear that the problem (
10
) has a unique solution. Choosing
\phi_{h}=z_{h}\left(v\right)
gives us

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(v\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}\partial_{t}v\,z_{h}\left(v\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\left\lVert{\partial_{t}v}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)}\left\lVert{z_{h}\left(v\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{\partial_{t}v}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(v\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|},

where we invoked the following property in the last step

\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq C{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\qquad\qquad\forall v\in\Ys.

(11)

We arrive at the useful inequality

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(v\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq C\left\lVert{\partial_{t}v}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall v\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right).

(12)

Now, we can introduce the following mesh-dependent norm analog to
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\Xs}

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}:={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(v\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\qquad\qquad\forall v\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right).

(13)

Hence, the stability condition for the bilinear form
a_{h}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
shall be established with respect to the discrete norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}
. The following result is a discrete version of the continuous inf-sup condition (
4
).

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{\varphi_{h}\in\Vs_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{a_{h}\left(u_{h},\varphi_{h}\right)}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}\geq C{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}\qquad\qquad\forall u_{h}\in\Vs_{h}.

(14)

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the continuous problem. For all
u_{h},\varphi_{h}\in\Vs_{h}
, it holds that

a_{h}\left(u_{h},\varphi_{h}\right)=\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u_{h}\right)\varphi_{h}+\kappa_{h}\nabla u_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t.

We choose
\varphi_{h}:=z_{h}+\lambda u_{h}
for some sufficiently large
\lambda>0
and arrive at

a_{h}\left(u_{h},\varphi_{h}\right)=a_{h}\left(u_{h},z_{h}\right)+\lambda a_{h}\left(u_{h},u_{h}\right),

where
z_{h}:=z_{h}\left(u_{h}\right)\in\Vs_{h}
is the solution to the problem (
10
). By using the Cauchy inequality and (
11
), we have

\displaystyle a_{h}\left(u_{h},z_{h}\right)

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u_{h}\right)z_{h}+\kappa_{h}\nabla u_{h}\cdot\nabla z_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}-\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\left(\dfrac{C}{4\varepsilon}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\lVert{z_{h}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right)-\left(\dfrac{1}{4\varepsilon}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\varepsilon{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-C\varepsilon\right){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}-\dfrac{C}{\varepsilon}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2},

for any
\varepsilon>0
. On the other hand, we apply the integration by parts formula and proceed as in (
6
) to obtain

a_{h}\left(u_{h},u_{h}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u_{h}\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\geq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}.

Therefore, we can bound the discrete bilinear form
a_{h}\left(u_{h},\varphi_{h}\right)
as follows

a_{h}\left(u_{h},\varphi_{h}\right)\geq\left(1-C\varepsilon\right){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\left({\lambda-\dfrac{C}{\varepsilon}}\right){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\geq C{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}.

Finally, we invoke the estimate
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}+\lambda u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+\lambda{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq C{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}
to complete the proof.
∎

The unique solvability of the discrete problem (
9
) follows from the discrete Banach-Nečas-Babuška theorem

29
, Theorem 26.6]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Given

. There exists a unique solution

to the problem (

3.2
Auxiliary results

We emphasize again that the bilinear form
a_{h}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
is non-conformal to
a\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, since the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is non-conformal to
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
in the discrepancy region. In order to establish the desired a priori error estimates, we need some auxiliary estimates of functions in this region. For
i=1,2
, the mismatch between
Q_{i}
and

is defined by

S_{h}^{1}:=Q_{1,h}\setminus\overline{Q_{1}}=Q_{2}\setminus\overline{Q_{2,h}},\qquad\qquad S_{h}^{2}:=Q_{2,h}\setminus\overline{Q_{2}}=Q_{1}\setminus\overline{Q_{1,h}},

and
S_{h}:=S_{h}^{1}\cup S_{h}^{2}
(see Figure
3
). We define by
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}=\left\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\mid K\cap\Gamma^{\ast}\neq\varnothing\right\}
the set of all interface elements. The following lemma collects some geometry approximation properties of interface elements from Lemma 3 in
[
30
]
and Lemma 3.3.4 in
[
31
]
. The results were proved with

and can be extended to the case

.

Illustration of the discrepancy region
S_{h}=S_{h}^{1}\cup S_{h}^{2}
with

. The red curve represents the space-time interface
\Gamma^{\ast}
, the black diagonal represents the discrete interface

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{T}^{\ast}_{h}
and assume that
\Gamma^{\ast}
is of class
\Cs^{2}
. Then, the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
resolves the space-time interface
\Gamma^{\ast}
to the second order, i.e.,

\operatorname{dist}\left(\Gamma_{h}^{\ast}\cap K;\Gamma^{\ast}\cap K\right)\leq Ch^{2}.

For all
u\in\Hs^{1}\left(S_{h}\right)
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
u
and
h
such that

\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(S_{h}\cap K\right)}\leq C\left(h\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Gamma^{\ast}\cap K\right)}+h^{2}\left\lVert{\Ds u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\cap K\right)}\right),

(15)

where we see the trace of
u
on
\Gamma^{\ast}
from
S_{h}
and
\Ds:=\left(\nabla,\partial_{t}\right)^{\top}
is the space-time gradient operator. It holds for the area or volume of the discrepancy region
S_{h}
that

\left|{S_{h}}\right|\leq Ch^{2}.

(16)

A technical difficulty arises from the mismatch between
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and the subdomains
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
is that the gradient of
u
jumps across
\Gamma^{\ast}
whereas the discrete functions exhibit a similar discontinuity over the approximated interface
\Gamma_{h}^{\ast}
. To deal with the mismatch and analyze the bilinear forms in the discrepancy region
S_{h}
, we follow the ideas in
[
32
]
employing some appropriate extension operators. In particular, for any fixed

, let
u\in\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
and
u_{i}:=u_{\mid Q_{i}}\in\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{i}\right)
be its restriction to the subdomain
Q_{i}\ (i=1,2)
. We employ the smooth Stein extensions
\tilde{u}_{i}\in\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{T}\right)\ (i=1,2)
to the whole domain
Q_{T}
. These extensions have the following properties

\tilde{u}_{i}=u_{i}\quad\text{ in }Q_{i},\quad\left\lVert{\tilde{u}_{i}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{u_{i}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{i}\right)},\quad i=1,2,

(17)

given that
\Gamma^{\ast}
is Lipschitz continuous (see
[
24
, Section VI.3.1]
). We define a map
\pi:\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)\rightarrow\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)
by

\pi u\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right):=\begin{cases}\tilde{u}_{1}\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)&\text{for }\,\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)\in Q_{1,h},\\
\tilde{u}_{2}\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)&\text{for }\,\left(\boldsymbol{x},t\right)\in Q_{2,h}.\end{cases}

(18)

Please note that
\pi u
can be discontinuous across
\Gamma_{h}^{\ast}
. The following lemma looks at the difference between
u
and
\pi u
. By definition,
u-\pi u
vanishes everywhere except
S_{h}
, and it belongs to
\Hs^{s}(S_{h})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u\in\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
and
\pi u\in\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)
be the function defined in (
18
). There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\lVert{\Ds\left(u-\pi u\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}\leq Ch\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}.

(19)

Proof.

For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}
, we use (
15
) to obtain

\left\lVert{\Ds\left(u-\pi u\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\cap K\right)}\leq C\left(h^{2}\left\lVert{\Ds\left(u-\pi u\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Gamma^{\ast}\cap K\right)}+h^{4}\left\lVert{\Ds^{2}\left(u-\pi u\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\cap K\right)}\right).

We sum over all elements
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ast}
. For the integral on
\Gamma^{\ast}
, we apply the trace inequality and (
17
) to arrive at

\left\lVert{\Ds\left(u-\pi u\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Gamma^{\ast}\right)}\leq 2\left(\left\lVert{\Ds u}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Gamma^{\ast}\right)}+\left\lVert{\Ds\pi u}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Gamma^{\ast}\right)}\right)\leq C\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)},

where the constant
C
depends on
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
, but not on
Q_{1,h}
and
Q_{2,h}
. We finish the proof.
∎

We also recall the interpolation estimate for a Lagrangian interpolant. Note that for

3.3
A priori error estimates

In Section
2
, we proved the well-posedness of the problem (
3
) in the space
\Xs_{0}
, which only requires
\partial_{t}u\in\Ys^{\prime}
. However, one needs additional regularities of the solution
u
to study the discretization error. More particularly, we assume that the solution
u\in\Xs_{0}
of the problem (
3
) satisfies
u\in\Hs^{0,1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\cap\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
, with
s>\frac{d+3}{2}
. Extending the ideas in
[
32
]
, we study the error
u-u_{h}
by choosing the discrete norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\pi u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}
. This choice is appropriate for employing numerical quadratures and allows us to better understand the mismatch’s effect in
S_{h}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u\in\Xs_{0}
and
u_{h}\in\Vs_{h}
be the solutions of the variational problems (
3
) and (
9
), respectively. If
u\in\Hs^{0,1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\cap\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
for given
s>\frac{d+3}{2}
, then there holds the following estimate

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\pi u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}\leq Ch\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}.

(21)

Proof.

First of all, we note that
u\in\Xs_{0}\cap\Hs^{0,1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\subset\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, which implies that
[u]_{\ast}=0
. In addition, if
u\in\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
with
s>\frac{d+3}{2}
, then we are able to employ the interpolant
I_{h}
. We start with the triangle inequality

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\pi u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\pi u-I_{h}u\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|I_{h}u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}.

(22)

In what follows, we denote
w:=\pi u-I_{h}u
for convenience. By invoking (
13
), we have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(w\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\leq\max\left\{\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\right\}\left\lVert{\nabla w}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{2}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(w\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}.

The first term is already bounded in (
20
). For the second term, we employ (
12
) and (
20
) to obtain

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(w\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq C\left\lVert{\Ds w}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)}\leq Ch\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}.

Thus

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}\leq Ch\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}.

(23)

Next, we estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
22
) by using (
14
). It follows that

C{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|I_{h}u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}\leq\sup_{\varphi_{h}\in\Vs_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{a_{h}\left(I_{h}u-u_{h},\varphi_{h}\right)}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}=\sup_{\varphi_{h}\in\Vs_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{a_{h}\left(I_{h}u,\varphi_{h}\right)-a\left(u,\varphi_{h}\right)}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}=\sup_{\varphi_{h}\in\Vs_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{I_{1}-I_{2}}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}\partial_{t}\,\tilde{u}_{i}\,\varphi_{h}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}_{i}\,\varphi_{h}+\kappa_{i}\nabla\tilde{u}_{i}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t-a\left(u,\varphi_{h}\right),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}\partial_{t}\,\tilde{u}_{i}\,\varphi_{h}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}_{i}\,\varphi_{h}+\kappa_{i}\nabla\tilde{u}_{i}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t-a_{h}\left(I_{h}u,\varphi_{h}\right).

We first deal with
I_{1}
. Since
u\in\Hs^{0,1}\left(Q_{T}\right)
and the associated integrand vanishes everywhere besides on
S_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{S_{h}}\partial_{t}\left(\pi u-u\right)\varphi_{h}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\left(\pi u-u\right)\varphi_{h}+\left(\kappa_{h}\nabla\left(\pi u\right)-\kappa\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

(24)

\displaystyle\leq C{\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\pi u-u\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}}\left\lVert{\varphi_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}+C\left(\left\lVert{\nabla\pi u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}+\left\lVert{\nabla u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}\right){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

To treat the first part on the right-hand side, note that the term
\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\pi u-u\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}
is bounded in (
19
), and from (
11
), we have

\left\lVert{\varphi_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}\leq C{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi_{h}\in\Vs_{h}.

Now, we handle the two norms
\left\lVert{\nabla\pi u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}
and
\left\lVert{\nabla u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}
by invoking the technique in Lemma 2.1 of
[
2
]
. Take
\left\lVert{\nabla\pi u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}
for instance. Please note that given
s>\frac{d+3}{2}
, the Sobolev embedding theorem says that
\HHs^{s-1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow\CCs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)
. Therefore, we can use (
16
) and (
17
) to get

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\nabla\pi u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}\leq\left|{S_{h}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert{\nabla\pi u}\right\rVert_{\CCs\left(\overline{S_{h}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\max{\left\{\left\lVert{\nabla\tilde{u}_{1}}\right\rVert_{\CCs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)},\left\lVert{\nabla\tilde{u}_{2}}\right\rVert_{\CCs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\max{\left\{\left\lVert{\nabla\tilde{u}_{1}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{s-1}\left(Q_{T}\right)},\left\lVert{\nabla\tilde{u}_{2}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{s-1}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right\}}\leq Ch\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)},

In this way, we can bound
I_{1}
by

I_{1}\leq Ch\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

For estimating
I_{2}
, we utilize (
11
) to arrive at

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}\partial_{t}\tilde{u}_{i}\,\varphi_{h}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}_{i}\,\varphi_{h}+\kappa_{i}\nabla\tilde{u}_{i}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t-a_{h}\left(I_{h}u,\varphi_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}\partial_{t}w\,\varphi_{h}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla w\,\varphi_{h}+\kappa_{i}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\left({\left\lVert{\Ds w}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)}}\left\lVert{\varphi_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{\nabla w}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert{\Ds w}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|},

which, by means of (
20
), implies that

I_{2}\leq Ch\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Finally, we end up with

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|I_{h}u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}\leq Ch\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

(25)

We combine (
25
) with (
23
) and (
22
) to obtain the result. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

From a computational perspective, higher-order finite elements can be freely used instead of being limited to space-time
P_{1}
elements. However, the error achieved will not be better than the one using
P_{1}
elements. The reason is that a priori error estimates depend not only on the type of finite elements used but also on the discretization error of the interface. Since
\left|{S_{h}}\right|\leq Ch^{2}
(cf. Lemma
3.2
), the orders of a priori error estimates will not improve even when higher-order finite elements are employed.

To achieve better results, we need to invoke a higher-order interface-fitted approximation method, generalizing our strategy. This topic has been studied by Li et al. for elliptic interface problems
[
36
]
. For our problem, we believe their method is well-suited to be combined with our method. However, to the best of our knowledge, extending their result to the problem (
1
) remains an open question and will be a potential direction for future research.

In case of a weaker regularity condition
u\in\Hs^{0,1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\cap\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
, we have the following a priori error estimate, which is now different between one and two spatial dimensions.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
u\in\Xs_{0}
and
u_{h}\in\Vs_{h}
be the solutions of the variational problems (
3
) and (
9
), respectively. Assume that
u\in\Hs^{0,1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\cap\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
. Then, there exists
h_{0}>0
such that for all
h<h_{0}
, the following estimate holds

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\pi u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}\leq C\begin{Bmatrix}h\sqrt{\left|{\log h}\right|}\\
h^{\frac{2}{3}}\end{Bmatrix}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\qquad\quad\text{for }\begin{Bmatrix}d=1\\
d=2\end{Bmatrix}.

(26)

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the previous theorem. The only differences are the estimates of
\left\lVert{\nabla\pi u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}
and
\left\lVert{\nabla u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}
in (
24
). We consider the former term since the latter can be treated similarly. We recall that the embedding
\HHs^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\hookrightarrow\LLs^{p}\left(Q_{T}\right)
holds true for all
p\in[1,\infty)
when
d=1
and for
p\in[1,6]
when
d=2
. In the following, we consider
p\geq 2
if
d=1
and set
p=6
in the other case while using the same notation. One can get from (
16
) that

\left\lVert{\nabla\pi u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}\leq\left|{S_{h}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\left\lVert{\nabla\pi u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{p}\left(S_{h}\right)}\leq Ch^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left\lVert{\nabla\pi u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{p}\left(S_{h}\right)},

which implies

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\nabla\pi u}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(S_{h}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\left\lVert{\nabla\tilde{u}_{1}}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{p}\left(Q_{T}\right)}^{p}+\left\lVert{\nabla\tilde{u}_{2}}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{p}\left(Q_{T}\right)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(27)

\displaystyle\leq C\begin{Bmatrix}h^{1-\frac{2}{p}}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
h^{1-\frac{2}{6}}\end{Bmatrix}\left(\left\lVert{\nabla\tilde{u}_{1}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right)}^{p}+\left\lVert{\nabla\tilde{u}_{2}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\begin{Bmatrix}h^{1-\frac{2}{p}}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
h^{1-\frac{2}{6}}\end{Bmatrix}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\leq C\begin{Bmatrix}h\sqrt{\left|{\log h}\right|}\\
h^{\frac{2}{3}}\end{Bmatrix}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\qquad\quad\text{for }\begin{Bmatrix}d=1\\
d=2\end{Bmatrix}.

In this step, we employed from Lemma 2.1 in
[
37
]
the Sobolev inequality

\left\lVert{\nabla\tilde{u}_{i}}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{p}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq Cp^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert{\nabla\tilde{u}_{i}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right)},

(28)

which holds true for all
p\in\left[2,\infty\right)
when
d=1
, with
C
independent of
p
. Moreover, one can easily prove that there exists
h_{0}>0
such that for all
h<h_{0}
, the function
f\left(p\right)=h^{-\frac{2}{p}}p^{\frac{1}{2}}
achieves its global minimum at
p=-4\log h
. Therefore, we arrive at

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{S_{h}}\partial_{t}\left(\pi u-u\right)\varphi_{h}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\left(\pi u-u\right)\varphi_{h}+\left(\kappa_{h}\nabla\left(\pi u\right)-\kappa\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\begin{Bmatrix}h\sqrt{\left|{\log h}\right|}\\
h^{\frac{2}{3}}\end{Bmatrix}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\qquad\quad\text{for }\begin{Bmatrix}d=1\\
d=2\end{Bmatrix}.

Hence, we have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|I_{h}u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}\leq C\begin{Bmatrix}h\sqrt{\left|{\log h}\right|}\\
h^{\frac{2}{3}}\end{Bmatrix}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\qquad\quad\text{for }\begin{Bmatrix}d=1\\
d=2\end{Bmatrix}.

(29)

The estimate (
26
) follows by substituting (
23
) and (
29
) into (
22
).

Remark 3.3
.

Providing that
u\in\Hs^{0,1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\cap\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
with
s>\frac{d+3}{2}
, we obtained an optimal error estimate with respect to the discrete norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\pi u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}
in Theorem
3.2
. However, in some scenarios when the solution is rougher, let us say
u\in\Hs^{0,1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\cap\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
, the estimate (
21
) turns out to be not applicable. In this case, we arrived at a nearly optimal error estimate in one spatial dimension and a sub-optimal estimate in two spatial dimensions (Theorem
3.3
). The nearly optimal bound
\mathcal{O}\left(h\sqrt{\left|{\log h}\right|}\right)
for
d=1
mainly relies on the Sobolev inquality (
28
), which is unfortunately not available for
d=2
(in our case, 3D space-time domain
Q_{T}
).

There are better bounds in the literature
[
15
,
9
]
. However, these studies relied on the interface-unfitted mesh methods, which require more effort to compute interaction integrals over the moving interface. Instead, we discretize the space-time domain using an interface-fitted mesh and avoid invoking special quadrature rules on cut elements. Moreover, the optimal error estimate can be recovered when the solution is sufficiently smooth on each subdomain.

From the numerical point of view, the error
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\pi u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}
is appropriate for studying the discrete problem (
9
) since
\pi u
and
\kappa_{h}
are discontinuous through the discrete interface instead of the continuous one. However, it is worth noting that we also arrive at the same convergence rate when using the error
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}
or
\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Ys}
. For instance, we can combine (
19
), (
21
), and (
26
) to get the following result:

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
u\in\Xs_{0}
and
u_{h}\in\Vs_{h}
be the solutions of the variational problems (
3
) and (
9
), respectively. Assume that
u\in\Hs^{0,1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\cap\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
for given
s>\frac{d+3}{2}
. Then, the following estimate holds

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}\leq Ch\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\qquad\qquad\text{for }d=1\text{ or }2.

If we only have
u\in\Hs^{0,1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\cap\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
, then there exists
h_{0}>0
such that for all
h<h_{0}
, it holds the following

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}\leq C\begin{Bmatrix}h\sqrt{\left|{\log h}\right|}\\
h^{\frac{2}{3}}\end{Bmatrix}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}\qquad\quad\text{for }\begin{Bmatrix}d=1\\
d=2\end{Bmatrix}.

Remark 3.4
.

The case
d=3
contains several challenges, both theoretically and computationally. From a theoretical perspective, it requires the condition
s>\frac{3+3}{2}=3
for the embedding
\HHs^{s-1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow\CCs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)
, making the assumption
u\in\Hs^{0,1}\left(Q_{T}\right)\cap\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
becomes quite strict and difficult to achieve in practice. Therefore, we will be trying to use the
\Ls^{2}
-projection instead of the interpolant
I_{h}
to relax the smoothness condition. However, this approach requires proving a result that differs from Lemma
3.4
, which will be investigated in the future.

On the other hand, from a computational standpoint, discretizing a virtual space-time domain
Q_{T}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
is a complex task with high computational cost. Please refer to
[
38
,
39
]
and the references therein. The approach for constructing a 4-dimensional virtual mesh that fits the interface could be based on the boundary-conforming method by von Danwitz et al.
[
39
]
. This would be one of the ongoing directions of our research.

5
Conclusions

Based on the interface-fitted strategy, we have proposed a new space-time finite element method to solve an advection-diffusion problem with moving subdomains. Unlike other space-time approaches, we treat the time and spatial variables similarly. We have shown an optimal error estimate with respect to a discrete energy norm under a specific globally low but locally high regularity condition. When the solution is less regular, the scheme is nearly optimal in one spatial dimension and sub-optimal in two spatial dimensions. We have also presented various numerical experiments illustrating the convergence behavior and the method’s effectiveness.

In the coming research, we intend to investigate the numerical analysis for moving-interface problems when the solution is less regular, let us say
u\in\Hs^{2,1}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
. This situation is reasonable and more practical since the formulation does not invoke the second-order derivative of
u
with respect to time. In addition, we would extend the proposed finite element scheme to solve moving-interfaces problems with fully discontinuous advection and diffusion coefficients, as studied in
[
4
,
6
]
. A more straightforward setting in
[
41
,
42
]
considering a discontinuous advection coefficient but a continuous diffusion coefficient also justifies an intensive investigation using the proposed interface-fitted finite element method, which might give rise to an optimal convergence rate under lower regularity assumptions.

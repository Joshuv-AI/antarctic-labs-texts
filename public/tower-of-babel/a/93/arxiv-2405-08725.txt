1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with the shifted moments

(1)

M_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(T)=\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))|^{2\beta_{k}}dt,

where
\bm{\alpha}=\bm{\alpha}(T)=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
and
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1}\ldots,\beta_{m})
satisfy
|\alpha_{k}|\leq T/2
and
\beta_{k}\geq 0
.
These were first studied in general by Chandee
[
5
]
. In
[
8
]
, the author proved the upper bound

M_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(T)\ll_{\bm{\beta}}T(\log T)^{\beta_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\beta_{m}^{2}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq m}|\zeta(1+i(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k})+1/\log T)|^{2\beta_{j}\beta_{k}}

assuming the Riemann hypothesis.
The goal of this paper is to obtain the corresponding lower bound, showing that these bounds are of the correct order or magnitude.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume the Riemann hypothesis. If
\beta_{k}\geq 0
and
|\alpha_{k}|\leq T/2
for
k=1,\ldots,m,
then

M_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(T)\gg_{\bm{\beta}}T(\log T)^{\beta_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\beta_{m}^{2}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq m}|\zeta(1+i(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k})+1/\log T)|^{2\beta_{j}\beta_{k}}

for
T
sufficiently large in terms of
\bm{\beta}
.

Note that the order of magnitude in this bound is consistent with the formula for the shifted moments predicted by the famous work of Conrey Farmer Keating Rubenstein and Snaith
[
7
]
.
The reader may consult
[
8
]
for more background on the shifted moments and a discussion of the previous estimates for
M_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(T)
.

The method of proof is similar to a principle pioneered in the works of Heap and Soundararajan
[
14
]
and Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
25
]
.
These works demonstrate that if one can asymptotically evaluate the twisted
2k^{\text{th}}
moment of a family of
L
-functions for some integer
k
, then one can obtain sharp lower bounds for the
2\beta^{\text{th}}
moments for all
\beta\geq 0
.
We will need a more elaborate argument in our setting since there are more parameters for the shifted moments.
To explain the method, we will first introduce some notation.
Set

\beta_{\ast}:=\sum_{k\leq m}\max(1,\beta_{k}).

We will choose a sequence of parameters
T_{j}=T^{c_{j}}
, where

c_{0}=0\text{ and }c_{j}=\frac{e^{j}}{(\log_{2}T)^{2}}

for
j>0
, where throughout this paper we will use
\log_{j}
to denote the
j
-fold iterated logarithm.
Let
L
be the largest integer such that
T_{L}\leq T^{\delta}
where
0<\delta<e^{-1000\beta_{\ast}}
is some small constant depending on
\bm{\beta}
to be chosen later.
For any
X
let

\mathcal{P}_{1,X}(s)=\sum_{p\leq T_{1}}\frac{1}{p^{s+1/\log X}}\frac{\log X/p}{\log X}+\sum_{p\leq\log T}\frac{1}{2p^{2s}},

and given any
2\leq j\leq L
define

\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(s)=\sum_{p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}\frac{1}{p^{s+1/\log X}}\frac{\log X/p}{\log X}.

Remark
.

In our proof, we could have also used the simpler Dirichlet polynomial

\sum_{p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}\frac{1}{p^{s}}

in place of
\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(s)
for all
1\leq j\leq L
.
However the
\mathcal{P}_{j,X}
will appear when using the Riemann hypothesis, and using the
\mathcal{P}_{j,X}
from the onset will reduce the total number of mean value calculations.

If
\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(s)
is not too large, then (see lemma
2.2
) we will be able to efficiently approximate
\exp(\beta\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(s))
with the following Dirichlet polynomial

\mathcal{N}_{j,X}(s;\beta):=\sum_{m\leq 100\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{j}}\frac{\beta^{m}\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(s)^{m}}{m!},

where
K_{j}=c_{j}^{-3/4}
for
j\geq 1
.
The set

\mathcal{G}:=\left\{t\in[T/2,5T/2]:|P_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|\leq K_{j}\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq L\right\}.

consists of the
t\in[T/2,5T/2]
for which
\mathcal{N}_{j,T_{L}}(s;\beta)
is a good approximation to
\exp(\beta\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{L}}(s))
.
We will be computing moments over the set

\mathcal{G}_{m}:=\left\{t\in[T,2T]:t+\alpha_{k}\in\mathcal{G}\text{ for all }1\leq k\leq m\right\}.

Fix a smooth function
w
such that
1_{[5/4,7/4]}(t)\leq w(t)\leq 1_{[1,2]}
, and suppose for a moment that we could compute the integral

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{m}}\prod_{k\leq m}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j\leq L}\exp\left((\beta_{k}-1)\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))+\beta_{k}\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}-i(t+\alpha_{k}))\right)w(t/T)\ dt.

Then Hölder’s inequality implies

|\mathcal{I}_{0}|\leq M_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(T)^{1/p}\times|\mathcal{J}|^{1/q}\times\prod_{k\leq m}|\mathcal{I}_{k}|^{1/r_{k}},

where

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{4\beta_{\ast}},\quad\frac{1}{r_{k}}=\frac{1}{2m}-\frac{\beta_{k}}{4m\beta_{\ast}},\quad\frac{1}{q}=1-\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{k\leq m}\frac{1}{r_{k}}

are conjugate exponents,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{k}=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{m}}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))|^{2m}\prod_{j\leq L}

\displaystyle\exp\left(2(\beta_{k}-m)\textup{Re }\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))\right)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq m\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\leq L}\exp\left(2\beta_{\ell}\textup{Re }\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{\ell}))\right)w(t/T)dt,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{m}}\prod_{k\leq m}\prod_{j\leq L}\exp\left(2\beta_{k}\textup{Re }\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))\right)w(t/T)dt.

Therefore, Theorem
1.1
will follow from the following three propositions.

Proposition 1.2
.

For large
T

\mathcal{J}\ll_{\bm{\beta}}T(\log T)^{\beta_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\beta_{m}^{2}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq m}|\zeta(1+i(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k})+1/\log T)|^{2\beta_{j}\beta_{k}}.

Proposition 1.3
.

Assuming the Riemann hypothesis, for large
T

|\mathcal{I}_{0}|\gg_{\bm{\beta}}T(\log T)^{\beta_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\beta_{m}^{2}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq m}|\zeta(1+i(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k})+1/\log T)|^{2\beta_{j}\beta_{k}}.

Proposition 1.4
.

Assuming the Riemann hypothesis, for large
T
and
1\leq k\leq m

\mathcal{I}_{k}\ll_{\bm{\beta}}T(\log T)^{\beta_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\beta_{m}^{2}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq m}|\zeta(1+i(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k})+1/\log T)|^{2\beta_{j}\beta_{k}}.

To prove Propositions
1.2
and
1.4
,
we will use the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{m}
to show that

\mathcal{J}\ll\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{k\leq m}|\mathcal{N}_{T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k});\beta_{k})|^{2}dt

and

\mathcal{I}_{k}\ll\int_{\mathcal{G}_{m}}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))|^{2m}|\mathcal{N}_{T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k});\beta_{k}-m)|^{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq m\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{N}_{T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{\ell});\beta_{\ell})|^{2}dt,

where

\mathcal{N}_{X}(s;\beta):=\prod_{j\leq L}\mathcal{N}_{j,X}(s;\beta).

Therefore
\mathcal{J}
can be controlled by computing the mean value of a Dirichlet polynomial, and the
\mathcal{I}_{k}
can be bounded by computing twisted
2m^{\text{th}}
moments of the zeta function.
Both of these quantities can be controlled as
\mathcal{N}_{T_{L}}(s;\beta)
is a short Dirichlet polynomial.
For the latter task, we will need to invoke the Riemann hypothesis when
m>2
.

The proof of Proposition
1.3
is a bit more difficult. The first step is to show that
\mathcal{I}_{0}
is approximately equal to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}_{m}}\prod_{k\leq m}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))\mathcal{N}_{T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k});\tfrac{1}{2}(\beta_{k}-1))^{2}\overline{\mathcal{N}_{T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k});\tfrac{1}{2}\beta_{k})}^{2}w(t/T)\ dt

using the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{m}
and the Riemann hypothesis.
To compute this quantity, we will write

\int_{\mathcal{G}_{m}}=\int_{T}^{2T}-\int_{[T,2T]\setminus\mathcal{G}_{m}}.

The integral over the entire interval
[T,2T]
is a shifted pure
m^{\text{th}}
moment of zeta twisted by a Dirichlet polynomial, which we can calculate unconditionally because
\mathcal{N}_{T_{L}}(s;\beta)
is a short Dirichlet polynomial.
To bound the integral over
[T,2T]\setminus\mathcal{G}_{m}
, we will first decompose this set according to which subsum
\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{\ell});\beta)
is unusually large for some
\ell
, and then bound the contribution of such
t
by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K_{j}^{2r}}\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{k\leq m}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))|

\displaystyle\cdot|\mathcal{N}_{T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k});\tfrac{1}{2}(\beta_{k}-1))\mathcal{N}_{T_{L}}\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k});\tfrac{1}{2}\beta_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times|\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{\ell}))|^{2r}\ dt

for some large integer
r
. To bound this quantity we are again forced to use the Riemann hypothesis, at least when
m>2
.
We can estimate this integral using the same method in
[
8
]
.

We now have all the necessary tools to begin our proof of Theorem
1.1
.
To help simplify the notation going forward, we will omit subscripts depending on
\bm{\beta}
from all big-O or Vinogradov asymptotic notation.
The reader should keep in mind that all of the implicit constants in this notation can, and usually will, depend on
\bm{\beta}
.
We will also implicitly assume that
T
is sufficiently large in terms of
\bm{\beta}
.
After covering some preliminary results, we will prove Proposition
1.2
, followed by Proposition
1.3
, and we will conclude the proof by establishing Proposition
1.4
.

2.
Preliminary Results

To control the size of zeta on the half line, we will use the following lemma due to Soundararajan
[
27
]
and Harper
[
12
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume RH, let
t\in[T,2T]
, and
|\alpha|\leq T/2
. Then for
2\leq X\leq T^{2}

\displaystyle\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}

\displaystyle+i(t+\alpha))|\leq\textup{Re }\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+1/\log X+i(t+\alpha)}}\frac{\log X/p}{\log X}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq\min(\sqrt{X},\log T)}\frac{1}{2p^{1+2i(t+\alpha)}}+\frac{\log T}{\log X}+O(1).

We will often Taylor expand exponentials of the
\mathcal{P}_{j,X}
in order to turn moment generating function calculations into computing mean values of short Dirichlet polynomials– a much simpler task.
The following lemma will be used frequently to accomplish this task.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\beta\leq\beta_{\ast}
and
|\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(s)|\leq K_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq L
, then

\exp(\beta\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(s))=(1+O(e^{-50\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{j}}))^{-1}\mathcal{N}_{j,X}(s;\beta).

Proof.

Since
|\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(s)|\leq 2K_{j}
, Taylor expansion gives

\mathcal{N}_{j,X}(s;\beta)=\exp(\beta\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(s))+O(e^{-100\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{j}}).

By assumption
\exp(-2K_{j}\beta_{\ast})\leq|\exp(\beta\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(s))|\leq\exp(2K_{j}\beta_{\ast})
, so the claim follows.
∎

Once we have reduced the proof to a number of mean value calculations, we will use a few standard results to compute these averages.
The first result is due to Montgomery and Vaughan (see for example theorem 9.1 of
[
16
]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Given any complex numbers
a_{n}

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\sum_{n\leq N}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{it}}\right|^{2}dt=(T+O(N))\sum_{n\leq N}|a_{n}|^{2}.

We will also make use of the property that Dirichlet polynomials supported on distinct sets of primes are approximately independent in the mean square sense.
The precise formulation we will use is the following splitting lemma which appears in equation (16) of
[
14
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose for
1\leq j\leq J
we have
j
disjoint intervals
I_{j}
and Dirichlet polynomials
A_{j}(s)=\sum_{n}a_{j}(n)n^{-s}
such that
a_{j}(n)
vanishes unless
n
is composed of primes in
I_{j}
.
Then if
\prod_{j\leq J}A_{j}(s)
is a Dirichlet polynomial of length
N

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{j\leq J}|A_{j}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}dt=(T+O(N))\prod_{j\leq J}\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}|A_{j}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}dt\right)

Finally, the following result due to Soundararajan
[
27
, lemma 3]
will simplify calculations for high moments of Dirichlet polynomials supported on primes.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
r
be a natural number and suppose
N^{r}\leq T/\log T
. Then given any complex numbers
a_{p}

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\sum_{p\leq N}\frac{a_{p}}{p^{it}}\right|^{2r}dt\ll Tr!\left(\sum_{p\leq N}|a_{p}|^{2}\right)^{r}.

To compute all the mean values that will arise, we will need two more ingredients.
The first is a special case of lemma 3.2 of
[
18
]
, which will let us estimate a sum over primes that will appear frequently throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.6
.

Given
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
and
X\geq 2

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq X}

\displaystyle\frac{\cos(\delta\log p)}{p}=\log|\zeta(1+1/\log X+i\delta)|+O(1).

The second and final ingredient we will need is a good estimate for the coefficients of the Dirichlet polynomials

\prod_{k\leq m}\mathcal{N}_{j,X}(s+i\alpha_{k};\beta_{k}):=\sum_{n}\frac{b_{j,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(n)}{n^{s}}.

To this end, denote
a_{X}(p):=\log(X/p)p^{-1/\log X}/\log X
and define multiplicative functions
g_{X}
and
h_{X}
by

g_{X}(p^{r};\beta):=\frac{\beta^{r}a_{X}(p)^{r}}{r!}

and

h_{X}(p^{r};\beta):=g_{X}(p^{r};\beta)+1_{p\leq\log T}\sum_{t=1}^{r/2}\frac{\beta^{r-t}a_{X}(p)^{r-t}}{2^{t}t!(r-2t)!}.

Next define
c_{1}(n)
to be 1 if
n
can be written as
n=n_{1}\cdots n_{r}
where
r\leq 100\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{1}
and each
n_{i}
is either a prime
\leq T_{1}
or a prime square
\leq\log T
.
Finally for
2\leq j\leq L
set
c_{j}(n)
to be 1 if
n
is the product of at most
100\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{j}
not necessarily distinct primes in
(T_{j-1},T_{j}]
.
In proposition 3.1 of
[
8
]
it was shown that

Proposition 2.7
.

For
2\leq j\leq L

\mathcal{N}_{j,X}(s;\beta)=\sum_{p\mid n\Rightarrow p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}\frac{g_{X}(n;\beta)c_{j}(n)}{n^{s}}.

If

\mathcal{N}_{1,X}(s;\beta)=\sum_{p\mid n\Rightarrow p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}\frac{f_{X}(n;\beta)}{n^{s}}

then
f_{X}(n;\beta)\leq h_{X}(n;\beta)c_{1}(n)
and
f_{X}(p;\beta)=g_{X}(p;\beta)
.

Therefore
b_{1,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(n)
is the
m
-fold Dirichlet convolution of
f_{X}(n;\beta_{k})n^{-i\alpha_{k}}
and
b_{j,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(n)
is the
m
-fold convolution of
g_{X}(n;\beta_{k})c_{j}(n)n^{-i\alpha_{k}}
for
2\leq j\leq L
.
We will make use of two more sets of coefficients.
Let
b^{\prime}_{j,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(n)
be the
m
-fold convolution of
h_{X}(n;\beta_{k})n^{-i\alpha_{k}}1_{p|n\Rightarrow p\in(T_{0},T_{1}]}
when
j=1
and the
m
-fold convolution of
g_{X}(n;\beta_{k})n^{-i\alpha_{k}}1_{p|n\Rightarrow p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}
when
2\leq j\leq L
.
Finally, let
b^{\prime\prime}_{1,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(n)
be the
m
-fold convolution of
h_{X}(n;\beta_{k})1_{p|n\Rightarrow p\in(T_{0},T_{1}]}
when
j=1
and the
m
-fold convolution of
g_{X}(n;\beta_{k})1_{p|n\Rightarrow p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}
when
2\leq j\leq L
.
Note
b^{\prime}_{j,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
and
b^{\prime\prime}_{j,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
are multiplicative, and
|b_{j,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(n)|,|b^{\prime}_{j,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(n)|\leq b^{\prime\prime}_{j,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(n)
.
All the information we will need about these coefficients is contained in lemma 3.2 of
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

For
1\leq j\leq L
and
p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]

b_{j,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(p)=a_{X}(p)\sum_{k=1}^{m}\beta_{k}p^{-i\alpha_{k}},

and
b^{\prime\prime}_{j,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(p)\leq\beta_{\ast}
. If
r\geq 2

b^{\prime\prime}_{j,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(p^{r})\leq\frac{\beta_{\ast}^{r}m^{r}}{r!}

holds whenever
2\leq j\leq L
or
p>\log T
, and otherwise

b^{\prime\prime}_{1,X,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(p^{r})\leq m\beta_{\ast}^{r}r^{2m}e^{-r\log(r/m)/2m+2r}.

3.
Proof of Proposition
1.2

We will begin with the evaluation of

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{m}}\prod_{k\leq m}\prod_{j\leq L}\exp\left(2\beta_{k}\textup{Re }\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))\right)w(t/T)dt,

which is the simplest computation.
To compute this mean value, we apply lemma
2.2
and then extend the integral over the entire set
[T,2T]
to find

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\ll\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{k\leq m}|\mathcal{N}_{T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k});\beta_{k})|^{2}dt.

Here we have used that
\prod_{j\leq L}(1+O(e^{-50\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{j}}))^{2}=O(1)
.
Note
\mathcal{N}_{j,X}(s;\beta)
is a Dirichlet polynomial of length
\leq T_{j}^{100m\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{j}}
when
j>1
and of length
\leq T_{1}^{200m\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{1}}
when
j=1
, so

\prod_{k\leq m}\mathcal{N}_{X}(s+i\alpha_{k};\beta_{k})

has length at most
T_{1}^{200m\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{1}}T_{2}^{100m\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{2}}\cdots T_{L}^{100m\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{L}}\leq T^{1/10}
by choice of
T_{j},K_{j}
and
L
.
Therefore using lemma
2.4
in tandem with proposition 3.3 of
[
8
]
we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle\ll T\prod_{j\leq L}\left(\prod_{p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}\left(1+\frac{|b_{j,T_{L},\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(p)|^{2}}{p}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)+O(e^{-50\beta_{\ast}^{2}K_{j}})\right)

\displaystyle\ll T(\log T)^{\beta_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\beta_{m}^{2}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq m}|\zeta(1+i(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k})+1/\log T)|^{2\beta_{j}\beta_{k}},

where the latter bound follows from an application of lemma
2.6
. This concludes the proof of Proposition
1.2
. ∎

4.
Proof of Proposition
1.3

Recall

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{m}}\prod_{k\leq m}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j\leq L}\exp\left((\beta_{k}-1)\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))+\beta_{k}\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}-i(t+\alpha_{k}))\right)w(t/T)\ dt.

To compute this, we will first need to replace the exponential by a Dirichlet polynomial.
To accomplish this, we will write
\mathcal{I}_{0}
as a telescoping sum and control the size of the intermediate increments.
For
0\leq J\leq L
denote

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathcal{I}_{0}^{(J)}=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{m}}\prod_{k\leq m}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j\leq J}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k});\tfrac{1}{2}(\beta_{k}-1))^{2}\overline{\mathcal{N}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k});\tfrac{1}{2}\beta_{k})}^{2}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{J<j\leq L}

\displaystyle\exp\left((\beta_{k}-1)\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))+\beta_{k}\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{L}}(\tfrac{1}{2}-i(t+\alpha_{k}))\right)w(t/T)\ dt.

Since
\mathcal{I}_{0}=\mathcal{I}_{0}^{(0)}
, we may decompose

\mathcal{I}_{0}=\mathcal{I}_{0}^{(L)}-\sum_{J\leq L}\left(\mathcal{I}_{0}^{(J)}-\mathcal{I}_{0}^{(J-1)}\right)

To prove Proposition
1.3
we will give a lower bound for
|I_{0}^{(L)}|
and then show that

\sum_{J\leq L}\big|\mathcal{I}_{0}^{(J)}-\mathcal{I}_{0}^{(J-1)}\big|\leq\frac{|I_{0}^{(L)}|}{2}.

To control these differences, we will use the following consequence of lemma
2.2
and the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{m}

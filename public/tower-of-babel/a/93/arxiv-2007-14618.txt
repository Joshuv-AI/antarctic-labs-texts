More Fibonacci-Bernoulli relations with and without balancing polynomials


Abstract
We continue our study on relationships between Bernoulli polynomials and balancing (Lucas-balancing) polynomials. From these polynomial relations, we deduce new combinatorial identities with Fibonacci (Lucas) and Bernoulli numbers. Moreover, we prove a special identity involving Bernoulli polynomials and Fibonacci numbers in arithmetic progression. Special cases and some corollaries will highlight interesting aspects of our findings. Our results complement and generalize these of Frontczak (2019).

1
Motivation and preliminaries

Let
B_{n}(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{C}
, be the
n
-th Bernoulli polynomial defined by

H(x,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{ze^{xz}}{e^{z}-1}\qquad(|z|<2\pi)

and
B_{n}=B_{n}(0)
being the
n
-th Bernoulli number
[
1
]
.

Let further
B_{n}^{*}(x)
be the
n
-th balancing polynomial
[
2
]
, i.e., polynomials defined by the recurrence

B_{n}^{*}(x)=6xB_{n-1}^{*}(x)-B_{n-2}^{*}(x),\qquad n\geq 2,

with the initial terms
B_{0}^{*}(x)=0
and
B_{1}^{*}(x)=1
. Similarly, Lucas-balancing polynomials are defined by

C_{n}(x)=6xC_{n-1}(x)-C_{n-2}(x),\qquad n\geq 2,

with the initial terms
C_{0}(x)=1
and
C_{1}(x)=3x
. See
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
for more information about these polynomials. The numbers
B_{n}^{*}(1)=B_{n}^{*}
and
C_{n}(1)=C_{n}
are called balancing and Lucas-balancing numbers, respectively (see entries
A001109
and
A001541
in the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences
[
11
]
).

Connections between Bernoulli polynomials
B_{n}(x)
and balancing polynomials
B_{n}^{*}(x)
are interesting, as they also give relations involving Bernoulli numbers and Fibonacci numbers (we refer to the papers
[
3
,
6
,
12
]
). The links are

B_{n}^{*}\Big(\frac{L_{2m}}{6}\Big)=\frac{F_{2mn}}{F_{2m}}\,,\qquad C_{n}\Big(\frac{L_{2m}}{6}\Big)=\frac{L_{2mn}}{2},

(1)

B_{n}^{*}\Big(\frac{i}{6}L_{2m+1}\Big)=i^{n-1}\frac{F_{(2m+1)n}}{F_{2m+1}}\,,\qquad C_{n}\Big(\frac{i}{6}L_{2m+1}\Big)=i^{n}\frac{L_{(2m+1)n}}{2},

(2)

where
m
is a nonnegative integer,
i=\sqrt{-1}
, and
F_{n}
and
L_{n}
denote Fibonacci and Lucas numbers, respectively. These sequences are defined by
F_{0}=0,F_{1}=1,L_{0}=2,L_{1}=1
and
X_{n}=X_{n-1}+X_{n-2}
for
n\geq 2
(entries
A000045
and
A000032
in
[
11
]
).

Recently, Frontczak
[
3
]
showed, among other things, that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
n\equiv k\,(\!\!\bmod 2)\end{subarray}}^{n}\!{n\choose k}B^{*}_{k}(x)(2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{n-k}B_{n-k}=nC_{n-1}(x).

(3)

Goubi
[
7
]
instantly “improved” this relation to

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}B^{*}_{k}(x)(2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{n-k}B_{n-k}=n\bigl(C_{n-1}(x)-\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}B_{n-1}^{*}(x)\bigr).

(4)

We point out, that since
B_{2n+1}=0
for
n\geq 1
, the only non-zero contribution in Goubi’s sum on the left comes from the index
k=n-1
, which obviously equals

{n\choose n-1}B^{*}_{n-1}(x)(2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\Big)=-n\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}B_{n-1}^{*}(x).

So, the identities (
3
) and (
4
) are actually equivalent and the “improvement” is a trivial reformulation.
Nevertheless, to keep the notation simple, we will renounce the mod notation and work with the second formulation.

In this paper, we prove more relations between Bernoulli polynomials and balancing polynomials. The proofs are based
on our recent findings concerning exponential generating functions for these polynomials.
From these polynomial relations, we deduce new combinatorial identities with Fibonacci (Lucas) and Bernoulli numbers. Moreover, we prove a special identity involving Bernoulli polynomials
and Fibonacci numbers in arithmetic progression. Some consequences are stated as corollaries.

2
New Bernoulli-balancing relations

The next lemma
[
4
]
deals with exponential generating functions for balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials. It will play a key role in the first part of the paper.

Lemma 1
.

Let
b_{1}(x,z)
and
b_{2}(x,z)
be the exponential generating functions of odd and even indexed balancing polynomials, respectively.
Then

\displaystyle b_{1}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{2n+1}^{*}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\bigl(3x\sinh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\cosh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)\bigr)

and

b_{2}(x,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{2n}^{*}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\sinh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z).

Similarly, we have for Lucas-balancing polynomials

\displaystyle c_{1}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{2n+1}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}\bigl(3x\cosh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\sinh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)\bigr)

and

c_{2}(x,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{2n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}\cosh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z).

We start with the following results involving even indexed balancing polynomials.

Theorem 2
.

For each
n\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}(12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{n-k}B_{n-k}B^{*}_{2k}(x)=6xn\bigl(C_{2n-2}(x)-\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}B_{2n-2}^{*}(x)\bigr).

(5)

Proof.

From

\frac{2}{e^{2x}-1}=\coth x-1

we get

H(0,12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)=6xz\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigl(\coth(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)-1\bigr).

This yields

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}

\displaystyle\bigl(12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{n-k}B_{n-k}B^{*}_{2k}(x)\Big)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=b_{2}(x,z)H(0,12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)

\displaystyle=6xze^{(18x^{2}-1)z}\bigl(\cosh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)-\sinh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)\bigr)

\displaystyle=6xzc_{2}(x,z)-6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z\,b_{2}(x,z)

\displaystyle=6x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n\bigl(C_{2n-2}(x)-\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}B_{2n-2}^{*}(x)\bigr)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 6
.

For each
n\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}(12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{n-k}B^{*}_{2k}(x)B_{n-k}(x)=6nx\bigl(18x^{2}-1+6x(2x-1)\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{n-1}.

(8)

Proof.

Since
H(x,z)=\frac{z}{2}\frac{e^{(x-1/2)z}}{\sinh\!\frac{z}{2}}

it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}(12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{n-k}

\displaystyle=b_{2}(x,z)H(x,12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)

\displaystyle=6xze^{(18x^{2}-1)z+6x(2x-1)\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z}

\displaystyle=6x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n\big(18x^{2}-1+6x(2x-1)\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)^{n-1}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

The proof is complete.

3
A special polynomial identity

Equations (
6
), (
9
) and (
10
) give rise to the question, if there is a connection between them. The answer to that question is positive, as will be shown in the next theorem. The theorem generalizes Theorem 9 in
[
3
]
, which has been generalized in a different way in
[
6
]
.
The proof of the extension presented here does not require the notion of balancing polynomials.

Theorem 13
.

Let
\alpha
be the golden ratio,
\alpha=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
, and
\beta=(1-\sqrt{5})/2=-1/\alpha
. Then, for each
n\geq 0
,
j\geq 1
, and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, we have the relations

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}F_{jk}(\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}B_{n-k}(x)=nF_{j}\big((\sqrt{5}x+\beta)F_{j}+F_{j-1}\big)^{n-1}

(15)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}F_{jk}(-\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}B_{n-k}(x)=nF_{j}\big((\alpha-\sqrt{5}x)F_{j}+F_{j-1}\big)^{n-1}.

(16)

Proof.

Let
F(z)
be the exponential generating function for
(F_{jn})_{n\geq 0},j\geq 1
. Evidently, the Binet formula for
F_{jn}
gives

F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{jn}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\big(e^{\alpha^{j}z}-e^{\beta^{j}z}\big).

Now we use the relations
\alpha^{j}=\alpha F_{j}+F_{j-1}
and
\beta^{j}=\beta F_{j}+F_{j-1}
,
to write

F(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}e^{(1/2F_{j}+F_{j-1})z}\sinh\Big(\frac{\sqrt{5}F_{j}}{2}z\Big).

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}F_{jk}(\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}B_{n-k}(x)\Big)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=F(z)H(x,\sqrt{5}F_{j}z)

\displaystyle=F_{j}z\,e^{((x-1/2)\sqrt{5}F_{j}+1/2F_{j}+F_{j-1})z}

\displaystyle=F_{j}z\,e^{((\sqrt{5}x+\beta)F_{j}+F_{j-1})z}.

This proves the first equation. The second follows upon replacing
x
by
1-x

and using
B_{n}(1-x)=(-1)^{n}B_{n}(x)

[
1
, Proposition 9.1.3]
and
\alpha-\beta=\sqrt{5}
.
∎

When
x=0
, then

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}(\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}F_{jk}B_{n-k}=nF_{j}\beta^{j(n-1)}=\frac{n}{2}F_{j}\big(L_{j(n-1)}-\sqrt{5}F_{j(n-1)}\big),

which generalizes (
6
). Similarly,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}(-\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}F_{jk}B_{n-k}=nF_{j}\alpha^{j(n-1)}=\frac{n}{2}F_{j}\big(L_{j(n-1)}+\sqrt{5}F_{j(n-1)}\big).

A combination of both yields

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}(\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)F_{jk}B_{n-k}=nF_{j}L_{j(n-1)}.

Corollary 15
.

Let
n
,

and

be integers with
n

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}(\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}\big(q^{1-(n-k)}-1\big)F_{jk}B_{n-k}=nF_{j}q^{1-n}\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}\big(r\alpha^{j}+(q-r)\beta^{j}\big)^{n-1}.

(18)

Proof.

The multiplication theorem
[
1
, Proposition 9.1.3]

\frac{1}{q}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{q-1}B_{n}\Big(x+\frac{r}{q}\Big)=\frac{B_{n}(qx)}{q^{n}}

gives

\big(q^{1-(n-k)}-1\big)B_{n-k}=\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}B_{n}\Big(\frac{r}{q}\Big).

Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle=nF_{j}\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}\Big(\Big(\sqrt{5}\,\frac{r}{q}+\beta\Big)F_{j}+F_{j-1}\Big)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=nF_{j}q^{1-n}\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}\big(\sqrt{5}rF_{j}+q(\beta F_{j}+F_{j-1})\big)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=nF_{j}q^{1-n}\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}\big(r\alpha^{j}+(q-r)\beta^{j}\big)^{n-1}.

∎

1.1
Background

The Gaussian Moments Conjecture is a number theoretical manifestation of the Random Wave Conjecture on the randomness of automorphic forms in the spectral aspect. For Eisenstein series, we can either consider the regularized moments of them, or the moments of its truncation. On Eisenstein series of fixed levels, we have the results on the fourth moments by Spinu
[
14
]
, Humphries
[
9
]
, Djanković and Khan
[
7
,
8
]
.
In the previous paper, we let the level to increase and reduced the fourth moment of newform Eisenstein series attached to a cusp with

\displaystyle\langle|E_{\mathfrak{a}}(\cdot,\frac{1}{2}+iT,\chi)|^{2},|E_{\mathfrak{a}}(\cdot,\frac{1}{2}+iT,\chi)|^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{reg}}=I_{1}+I_{2},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=\nu^{-1}(N)\sum_{u}\frac{\Lambda^{2}(\frac{1}{2},u)|\Lambda(\frac{1}{2}+2iT,u\otimes\psi)|^{2}}{|\Lambda(1+2iT,\psi)|^{2}}+\mathrm{continuous}\medspace\medspace\mathrm{spectrum},

for some primitive
\psi
mod
N
delicately decided by
\chi
and
\mathfrak{a}
, and

\displaystyle I_{2}=\nu^{-1}(N)\Big(\frac{24}{\pi}\log^{2}N+O(\frac{L^{\prime\prime}}{L}(1+2iT,\psi))+O(\log N\log\log N\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+2iT,\psi))\Big).

(1)

Here we multiply
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
by
\nu(N):=[SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma_{0}(N)]
for clarity, and the sum over

traverses an orthonormal basis of the discrete spectrum spanned by Maaß forms.

In this article, we present a more hypothetical approach to see what the main terms shall be. Before we state the results, we first describe the conditionality of our research.

1.2
Assumptions

For newform Eisenstein series, the period integrals ofteh involve logarithmic derivatives of Dirichlet L-functions. When studying the Quantum Unique Ergodicity (
QUE

[

\displaystyle\frac{\langle|E|^{2},\phi\rangle}{\langle 1,\phi\rangle}=\frac{6}{\pi}\log N+\frac{12}{\pi}\mathrm{Re}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+2iT,\psi)+O(N^{-\frac{1}{8}+\varepsilon}),

2.4
The recipe of conjecturing moments of L-functions

There is a recipe to make conjectures on the moments of L-functions, due to B. Conrey, D. Farmer, J. Keating, M. Rubinstein and N. Snaith
[
6
]
. We call it “the recipe” for short throughout this article.
To summarize, we can approximate the average of L-functions by

\displaystyle\sum_{u}\Big|\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{u}(n)}{n^{s}}+\gamma_{u}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{b_{u}(n)}{n^{1-s}}\Big|^{2k},

and after we expand the
2k
-th power as

\displaystyle\sum_{u}\underbrace{\text{Product of Root Numbers}}_{A_{u}}\cdot\underbrace{\text{Weighted Multi-Dirichlet Series}}_{B_{u}},

we can instead estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{u}(\text{expected mean of }A_{u}\text{ over the family of }u)\cdot B_{u}.

For each type (unitary, orthogonal, symplectic) of L-functions, there are cancellations underneath such that no big loss is expected in the transformation. Needless to say, a rigorous proof that justifies the two expressions to have the same size appears unavailable for the moment.

Remark 6
.

We introduce the recipe for moments of L-functions for convenience, but it is also applicable to hybrid moments of
L
-functions, which is the case for our Theorem
1
with (
5
) and (
6
).

c\in(0,\tfrac{\log\log\log N}{\log N})
such that
L(1+s,\psi)=O(\sqrt{\log N})
for
\real s=-c
. Since the integrand of (
31
) has no poles other than
s=0
for
\real s\in(-c,3)
, we have

\displaystyle\eqref{3pole}=\frac{12}{N\pi L^{2}(1,\psi)}\Big(\underset{s=0}{\text{Res }}H(s)+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\underset{(-c)}{\int}H(s)ds\Big),

with

\displaystyle H(s)=\frac{\Gamma^{4}(\frac{1+s}{2})}{s\Gamma(1+s)}(\pi Y)^{-s}\frac{\zeta^{2}(1+s)L^{2}(1+s,\psi)}{\zeta(2+2s)}\prod_{p\mid N}(1+\frac{1}{p^{1+s}})^{-1}.

For the integral we have

\displaystyle\frac{12}{N\pi L^{2}(1,\psi)}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\underset{(-c)}{\int}H(s)ds\ll\frac{(\log\log N)^{2}}{N}Y^{c}L^{2}(1-c,\psi)\sum_{p\mid N}p^{-1+c}\ll\frac{\log N(\log\log N)^{3}}{N}Y^{c}.

On the other hand, since

\displaystyle\zeta(1+s)=\frac{1}{s}+\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{1}s+O(s^{2}),

for some constant
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\underset{s=0}{\text{Res }}H(s)=(2\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{0}^{2})K(0)+2\gamma_{0}K^{\prime}(0)+K^{\prime\prime}(0),

(32)

where

\displaystyle K(s)=\frac{\Gamma^{4}(\frac{1+s}{2})}{\Gamma(1+s)}(\pi Y)^{-s}\frac{L^{2}(1+s,\psi)}{\zeta(2+2s)}\prod_{p\mid N}(1+\frac{1}{p^{1+s}})^{-1}.

The following computation is straightforward (recall (
2
)):

\displaystyle K(0)

\displaystyle=6L^{2}(1,\psi)\frac{N}{\nu(N)}

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=K(0)\Big(-\log Y+2\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1,\psi)+\sum_{p\mid N}\frac{p^{-1}\log p}{1+p^{-1}}+O(1)\Big)

\displaystyle K^{\prime\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=K(0)\Big((\frac{K^{\prime}(s)}{K(s)})^{\prime}|_{s=0}+(\frac{K^{\prime}(0)}{K(0)})^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=K(0)\Big(\sum_{p\mid N}\frac{p^{-1}\log^{2}p}{1+p^{-1}}+O((\log\log N)^{2})\Big).

Thus (
32
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{12}{N\pi L^{2}(1,\psi)}\underset{s=0}{\text{Res }}H(s)=\frac{72}{\nu(N)}\Big(\sum_{p\mid N}\frac{p^{-1}\log^{2}p}{1+p^{-1}}+O((\log\log N)^{2})\Big).

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{p\mid N}\frac{p^{-1}\log^{2}p}{1+p^{-1}}\leq\log N\sum_{p\mid N}\frac{\log p}{1+p}\ll\log N\log\log N,

so (
32
) is bounded by
\nu^{-1}(N)\log N\log\log N
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\eqref{3pole}\ll\frac{\log N(\log\log N)^{3}}{N}Y^{c}+\frac{\log N\log\log N}{\nu(N)}=o(\frac{\log^{2}N}{\nu(N)}).

This solves the first part of (
26
).

4.3
Cauchy’s inequality

Now we are left to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{F}}\Big(|E^{Y}_{\mathfrak{a}}|^{2}\overline{E^{Y}_{\mathfrak{a}}}e_{\mathfrak{a}}+|E^{Y}_{\mathfrak{a}}|^{2}E^{Y}_{\mathfrak{a}}\overline{e_{\mathfrak{a}}}\Big)d\mu=2\int_{\mathcal{F}}(E^{Y}_{\mathfrak{a}})^{3}e_{\mathfrak{a}}d\mu.

By Cauchy’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{F}}(E^{Y}_{\mathfrak{a}})^{3}e_{\mathfrak{a}}d\mu\leq\Big(\int_{\mathcal{F}}(E^{Y}_{\mathfrak{a}})^{2}e^{2}_{\mathfrak{a}}d\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{F}}(E^{Y}_{\mathfrak{a}})^{4}d\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Our work on (
29
) has shown the first integral is
o(\frac{\log^{2}N}{\nu(N)})
, and the second integral has the same (big O) bound by assumption, so we have completed the proof.

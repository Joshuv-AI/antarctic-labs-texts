KSB smoothings of surface pairs
János Kollár

Abstract
. We describe KSB smoothings of log canonical surface pairs ( S , D ) (S,D) , where D D is a reduced curve. In sharp contrast with the D = ∅ D=\emptyset case, cyclic quotient pairs always have KSB smoothings, usually forming many irreducible components.



At the boundary of the moduli space of smooth surfaces, we find surfaces with log canonical singularities. Quotient singularities form the largest—and usually most troublesome—subclass.

The universal deformation space
\operatorname{Def}(S)
of a quotient singularity is quite complicated. The irreducible components were enumerated in
[
KSB88
,
Ste91a
]
; their number grows exponentially with the multiplicity.
However, for the moduli of surfaces, we are interested only in those deformations of
S
for which
K_{S}
lifts to a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier divisor; see Paragraph
9
.
These are now called
KSB deformations,
replacing the name
\mathrm{qG}
-deformations
used in
[
KSB88
]
.

By
[
LW86
,
KSB88
]
, quotient singularities either have no KSB smoothings, or
the KSB smoothings form a single, easy to describe, irreducible component of the deformation space; see Paragraph
10
.

The aim of this note is to study the analogous question for the moduli theory of simple normal crossing surface pairs.
The normal singularities at the boundary are log canonical pairs
(S,D)
, where
S
itself has quotient singularities whenever
D\neq\emptyset
. If the singularity is a cyclic quotient, then
D
can have 1 or 2 local branches, and if the singularity is a dihedral quotient, then
D
has 1 local branch;
see Notation
12
for details.
The 3 types behave quite differently.

1.
Irreducible components of KSB deformation spaces

We enumerate the irreducible components of
\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S,D)
—the
space of KSB deformations
—where we now require
K_{S}+D
to lift to a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier divisor; see Definitions
13
–
14
.

We use the dual graph—as in
Notation
12
—to specify a pair
(S,D)
.

Theorem 1
.

The irreducible components of

\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}\bigl(\bullet\ -\ c_{1}\ -\ c_{2}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s}\ -\ \bullet\bigr)

are in one-to-one correspondence with the
irreducible components of

\operatorname{Def}\bigl(c_{1}\ -\ c_{2}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s}\bigr),

and, in each of them, a general deformation is smooth.

Theorem 2
.

The irreducible components of

\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}&2&\\
&|&\\
2\ -&c_{1}&\!\!\!\!-\ c_{2}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s}\ -\ \bullet\end{array}\right)

are in one-to-one correspondence with the
irreducible components of

(note that
c_{1}
is omitted)

\operatorname{Def}\bigl(c_{2}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s}\bigr),

and, in each of them, a general deformation has 2 singularities, which are
(2\ -\ \bullet)
.

Proposition 3
.

The singularities
(c_{1}\ -\ c_{2}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s}\ -\ \bullet)
are KSB rigid.

Remarks 4
.

(4.1)

The dimensions of the irreducible components of
\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S,D)
are computed in (
38
) for the cyclic cases, and in (
40
) for the dihedral cases.

(4.2)

For the cyclic quotients in Theorem
1
,
the image of

\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}\bigl(\bullet\ -\ c_{1}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s}\ -\ \bullet\bigr)\ \to\ \operatorname{Def}\bigl(c_{1}\ -\cdots\ -\ c_{s}\bigr)

is nowhere dense, with a few exceptions; see (
39
).

(4.3)

For the dihedral pairs
(S,D)
in Theorem
2
,
only some irreducible components of
\operatorname{Def}(S)
contain an
irreducible component of
\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S,D)
, see (
26
).

(4.4)

For quotient singularities
S
,
[
KSB88
, 3.14]
gives an algorithm to
enumerate the irreducible components of
\operatorname{Def}(S)
, though in practice this can be very cumbersome if there are many exceptional curves. For cyclic quotients
S
as in Theorem
1
,
[
Ste91a
]
gives a better method. As a consequence,
[
Ste91a
]
shows that if
S
has multiplicity
m=m(S)
, then
\operatorname{Def}(S)
has at most
\tfrac{1}{m-1}\tbinom{2(m-2)}{m-2}

irreducible components, and equality holds for ‘most’ cyclic quotients of a given multiplicity. Note that
m(S)=2+\sum(c_{i}-2)
.

Other results.

If we look at pairs
(S,dD)
for
d<1
, the situation changes dramatically. For the pairs
(S,dD)
as in Theorems
1
–
2
, there are either no KSBA deformations (as in Definition
13
) for any
d
, or a single
irreducible component for every
d
.
For almost all of the pairs
(S,dD)
as in Proposition
3
, there are
either no KSBA deformations for any
d
, or a single
irreducible component for a unique value of
d
, see (
51
). For
d\in(\frac{5}{6},1]
we give a complete answer in Section
8
.
For
d\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, all lc pairs
(S,dD)
are listed in
[
Kol13
, Sec.3.3]
. Using it,
the method should give a full answer for
d\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
. I checked only some examples, but did not find any other pairs
(S,dD)
with a KSBA smoothing and
\frac{1}{2}<d<1
. Examples with
d=\frac{1}{2}
are given in (
53
).

Sketch of the proofs.

Let
(S,D)
be a pair as in
Theorems
1
–
2
, fix an irreducible component of

\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S,D)
, and let

g:({\mathbf{S}},{\mathbf{D}})\to(0,{\mathbb{D}})

be a general 1-parameter deformation in it. That is, the fiber over
0
is

({\mathbf{S}},{\mathbf{D}})_{0}\cong(S,D)
, and
{\mathbb{D}}
denotes either a complex disc or the germ of a smooth curve.

We prove in (
42
) that the general fiber of
g
is smooth in the cyclic case, and has at worst
A_{1}
singularities in the dihedral case. Thus, as we recall in (
23
), by
[
KSB88
, 3.5]
there is a
proper, birational morphism
\pi:{\mathbf{S}}_{P}\to{\mathbf{S}}
such that

•

K_{{\mathbf{S}}_{P}}
is
\pi
-ample, and

•

the central fiber

S_{P}
has only Du Val and
\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{rn^{2}}(1,arn{-}1)
singularities.

(See Notation
6
.
The latter are called T-singularities (
10
.1), and

S_{P}\to S
is a
P-modification, see Definition
24
).

Let
{\mathbf{D}}_{P}
denote the birational transform of
{\mathbf{D}}
on

{\mathbf{S}}_{P}
. Then

K_{{\mathbf{S}}_{P}}+{\mathbf{D}}_{P}\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\pi^{*}\bigl(K_{{\mathbf{S}}}+{\mathbf{D}}\bigr)
, hence

g\circ\pi:({\mathbf{S}}_{P}+{\mathbf{D}}_{P})\to{\mathbb{D}}

is a KSB deformation of
S_{P}
and also of
(S_{p},{\mathbf{D}}_{P}|_{S_{P}})
;
we call these doubly KSB deformations in Definition
43
.
We show in (
44
) that
{\mathbf{D}}_{P}|_{S_{P}}=D_{P}+E_{P}
, where

E_{P}\subset S_{P}
is the reduced exceptional divisor.

A well known argument (
29
) then gives a natural morphism

\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P},D_{P}+E_{P})\to\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S,D)
, which is
finite and birational onto our irreducible component.

In the cyclic case of Theorem
1
, a quick argument shows that every P-modification leads to an irreducible component of

\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S,D)
, see (
38
).

In the dihedral case of Theorem
2
, the irreducible components of
\operatorname{Def}(S)
are enumerate in (
26
), following
[
Ste91a
,
Ste93
]
.
Then in (
27
–
28
) we describe which P-modification lead to an irreducible component of

\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S,D)
. Putting these together gives
Theorem
2
.

Proposition
3
directly follows from
[
Kol23
, 2.23]
, see (
15
.1). ∎

Infinitesimal computations.
Usually the space of KSB deformations
\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S,D)
is not reduced. For cyclic pairs, the method of
[
AK19
]
is used to determine its tangent space in Section
9
, but a complete description is known only in a few cases.

Other applications.

Many of the results apply to more general rational singularities
S
. However, not every irreducible component of
\operatorname{Def}(S)

is obtained from a P-modification.

The conjectures in
[
Kol91
, Sec.6]
ask whether every irreducible component of
\operatorname{Def}(S)
is obtained in a similar way, using a notion of P-modification that is more general than the one in Definition
24
. A positive answer has been known for quotient singularities
[
KSB88
, 3.9]
and for points of multiplicity
\leq 4

[
dJvS91
,
Ste91b
]
. The recent papers
[
PS22
,
JS23
]
develop a method to prove the conjecture in many new cases. In
[
PS22
]
this is illustrated by the

W(p,q,r)
series of singularities, but their method applies more broadly. (The
W(p,q,r)
series was discovered by Wahl around 1980, see
[
Wah21
]
for a recent survey.) In
[
JS23
]
the conjecture is proved for most weighted homogeneous singularities.

Especially for the M-modification version as in
[
BC94
]
or (
29
), the
singularities with a rational homology disc smoothing—classified by
[
BS11
]
—may form the natural class to work with.

Higher dimensions.
For an lc pair
(X,D)
of arbitrary dimension with reduced
D
,
[
KK23
]
shows that a flat deformation is a KSB deformation iff the KSB condition (
13
) is satisfied by a general surface section.
Thus our results give information not only for surface pairs, but for higher dimensions as well.

2.
Quotient singularities

The classification of surface quotient singularities and their dual graphs are given in
[
Bri68
]
.
We need detailed information about the cyclic (type
A
) and dihedral (type
D
) cases; we recall these and fix our notation.
The tetrahedral, octahedral and icosahedral quotients (type
E
) will appear only in examples.

Notation 5
.

We work over the complex numbers and let
\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
denote the germ of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
at the origin. For our purposes, we may work with a complex analytic germ,
the spectrum of
{\mathbb{C}}[[x,y]]
, or the spectrum of the Henselisation of

{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]_{(x,y)}
.
For most situations in this paper one can choose global coordinates, so we can even work with
0\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
.

S_{n,q}
and
S^{d}_{n,q}
will denote the cyclic and dihedral
quotients as in Notation
6
and
8
.
The curves
B_{n,q}\subset S_{n,q}
,
D_{n,q}\subset S_{n,q}
and
D^{d}_{n,q}\subset S^{d}_{n,q}
are defined in (
12
).

The minimal resolution of a surface
S
is denoted by
\mu:S^{\rm m}\to S
.
For a curve
D\subset S
, its birational transform on
S^{\rm m}
is denoted by
D^{\rm m}
.

Notation 6

(Cyclic quotients)
.

We write
S_{n,q}:=\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\frac{1}{n}(1,q)

where the group action is
(x,y)\mapsto(\epsilon x,\epsilon^{q}y)

for some primitive
n
th root of unity
\epsilon
. The action is free outside the origin iff
(n,q)=1
. In general, if
n=n_{1}(n,q),q=q_{1}(n,q)
, then

\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{n}(1,q)\cong\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{n_{1}}(1,q_{1}).

Let
q^{\prime}
denote the multiplicative inverse of
q
modulo
n
. Then

S_{n,q}\cong S_{n,q^{\prime}}
; the isomorphism interchanges the coordinates.

For the dual graph of the minimal resolution, we use
c_{i}:=-(C_{i}^{2})
, the negative of the self-intersection, to denote the vertex corresponding to the curve
C_{i}\subset S_{n,q}^{\rm m}
.
For
S_{n,q}
the dual graph is

{\mathcal{D}}(S_{n,q})=\quad c_{1}\ -\ c_{2}\ -\cdots\ -c_{s},

where the
c_{i}
are obtained from the continued fraction expansion

\frac{n}{q}=c_{1}-\cfrac{1}{c_{2}-\cfrac{1}{c_{3}-\cdots}}

We use
[c_{1},\dots,c_{s}]
to denote this continued fraction, and

S(c_{1},\dots,c_{s})\cong S_{n,q}
the resulting singularity.

7
Resolution of cyclic quotients.

An explicit construction of the minimal resolution is in
[
Jun08
]
;
see
[
Rei93
]
for a very accessible treatment.
Here we use an inductive procedure as in
[
Kol07
, 2.31]
.

For
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{xy}/\frac{1}{n}(1,q)
, the quotient of the blow-up
of the ideal sheaf
(x^{q},y)
gives a proper, birational morphism

\pi_{1}:S_{1}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{xy}/\frac{1}{n}(1,q)
. It is covered by 2 charts

(7.1)

(Singular chart)
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{x_{1}y_{1}}/\frac{1}{q}(1,-n)
, where

x=x_{1}y_{1}^{1/n},y=y_{1}^{q/n}
.

(7.2)

(Smooth chart)
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{x_{2}y_{2}}
, where

x=x_{2}^{1/n},y=y_{2}x_{2}^{q/n}
.

Iterating this blow-up gives the minimal resolution.
The
\pi_{1}
-exceptional curve
E_{1}
is
(y_{1}=0)
(resp.
(x_{2}=0)
).
Thus the extended dual graph is

[formula]

(x-\mbox{axis})\ -\ c_{1}\ -\ c_{2}\ -\cdots\ -c_{s}\ -\ (y-\mbox{axis}).

Notation 8

(Dihedral quotients)
.

Let
S^{d}_{n,q}
denote the singularity whose
minimal resolution dual graph is

\begin{array}[]{ccc}&2&\\
&|&\\
2\ -&c_{1}&\!\!\!\!-\ c_{2}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s}\end{array}

where
\frac{n}{q}=[c_{1},\dots,c_{s}]
. The curves marked
2
are denoted by
C^{\prime}_{0},C^{\prime\prime}_{0}
.
We also use the notation

S(2^{2},c_{1},\dots,c_{s}):=S^{d}_{n,q}.

One usually assumes
s\geq 2
, though later it will be convenient to allow
s=1
.

Claim
8
.1.

S^{d}_{n,q}
is also the quotient of

S_{N,Q}:=\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{2q(n-q)}\bigl(1,2n^{\prime}(n-q)+1\bigr),\quad\mbox{where}\quad qq^{\prime}=nn^{\prime}+1,

by the involution induced by
(x,y)\mapsto(\epsilon^{q}y,x)
, where
\epsilon
is any primitive
2q(n-q)
-th root of unity.
This gives the correspondence

\begin{array}[]{cll}(n,q)&\mapsto&\bigl(N=2q(n-q),\ Q=2n^{\prime}(n-q)+1\bigr),\quad\mbox{and}\\
(N,Q)&\mapsto&\bigl(n=q+\tfrac{1}{2}(N,Q-1),\ q=N/(N,Q-1)\bigr).\end{array}

(So
N
is even and
Q^{2}\equiv 1\mod N
.)

To see these, contract the curves
C^{\prime}_{0},C^{\prime\prime}_{0}
and
C_{2},\dots,C_{s}
, we get

\bar{S}^{d}_{n,q}\to S^{d}_{n,q}
. There is
a single exceptional curve
\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with two
A_{1}
points on it, plus the singularity coming from

S(c_{2},\dots,c_{s})
. Thus
\bar{S}^{d}_{n,q}
has a double cover, ramified only at the
A_{1}
points. The corresponding double cover of
S^{d}_{n,q}
is a cyclic quotient singularity,
whose dual graph is

[formula]

c_{s}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{2}\ -\ \tilde{c}_{1}\ -\ c_{2}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s}

where
\tilde{c}_{1}=2(c_{1}-1)
.
We claim that this is the quotient singularity
S_{N,Q}
.
To see this, start with
S_{N,Q}
and blow up the origin.
In the
(x,\frac{y}{x})
-chart we get

{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{2q(n-q)}\bigl(1,2n^{\prime}(n-q)\bigr)\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{q}\bigl(1,n^{\prime}\bigr).

Next, write

\tfrac{q}{m}:=[c_{2},\dots,c_{s}],
so
n=c_{1}-\tfrac{m}{q}
,
and note that

nn^{\prime}\equiv-1\mod q
, so
mn^{\prime}\equiv 1\mod q
.
Therefore

\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{q}\bigl(1,n^{\prime}\bigr)\cong\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{q}\bigl(1,m\bigr)

which gives
S(c_{2},\dots,c_{s})
.

The exceptional curve of the blow-up is pointwise fixed by a subgroup of order
2(n-q)
. Taking the corresponding quotient we have a smooth surface and the self-intersection of the curve is
-2(n-q)
. Then we have a
\frac{1}{q}
-action, which changes the self-intersection to
-2(n-q)/q=-2(n/q)+2
. Then we resolve the
\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{q}\bigl(1,m\bigr)
singularities. The self-intersection becomes
-2\lceil{n/q}\rceil+2=-2c_{1}+2
, as claimed.

Finally
(x,y)\mapsto(\epsilon^{q}y,x)
descends to the involution on

S_{N,Q}
. ∎

9
KSB deformations.

Let
X_{0}
be a surface with quotient (more generally log canonical) singularities only. A
KSB deformation
of
X_{0}

is a flat morphism to a local scheme
g:X\to(0,B)
such that

X_{0}\cong g^{-1}(0)
, and

(9.1)

\omega_{X/B}^{[m]}
is flat over
B
, and commutes with all base changes
(0,B^{\prime})\to(0,B)
for every
m
.

If
B
is reduced, then, by
[
Kol23
, 2.3 and 4.7]
this is equivalent to

(9.2)

\omega_{X/B}^{[m]}
is locally free for some
m>0
.

The notion was introduced in
[
KSB88
]
, and called
\mathrm{qG}
-deformation
there. Note also that the 2 versions are not equivalent already over

B=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{C}}[\epsilon]
; see
[
AK19
]
.

10
KSB smoothings.

By
[
LW86
, 5.9]
and
[
KSB88
, 3.10]
, quotient singularities that admit a KSB smoothing—frequently called
T-singularities—
are either Du Val or of the form

[formula]

\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{uv}/\tfrac{1}{rn^{2}}(1,arn-1),\quad\mbox{where $(a,n)=1$ and $n>1$.}\quad

More generally, these are the singularities that have a KSB deformation with Du Val generic fiber.
(There are a few more cases that have nontrivial KSB deformations, but no KSB smoothings; see
[
Kol23
, 2.29]
.)
By
[
Wah81
]
, the dual resolution graphs are obtained as follows.
We start with

4\quad\mbox{or}\quad 3\ -\ 2\ -\ \cdots\ -\ 2\ -\ 3,

and successively apply the operation

c_{1}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s-1}\ -\ c_{s}\quad\mapsto\quad 2\ -\ c_{1}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s-1}\ -\ (c_{s}+1),

or its symmetric version.

Starting with
(4)
gives the
\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{uv}/\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}(1,an-1)

singularities, and starting with
(3\ -\ 2\ -\ \cdots\ -\ 2\ -\ 3)

(with
r{-}2
curves marked
2
) gives the

\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{uv}/\tfrac{1}{rn^{2}}(1,arn-1)
singularities.

Taking the quotient of (
10
.1)
by the subgroup of order
rn
shows that

[formula]

\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{uv}/\tfrac{1}{rn^{2}}(1,arn-1)\cong(xy=z^{rn})/\tfrac{1}{n}(1,-1,a),

where
x=u^{rn},y=v^{rn},z=uv
.
Using the second representation, the universal KSB deformation is given by

[formula]

\bigl(xy=z^{rn}+\textstyle{\sum}_{i=0}^{r-1}t_{i}z^{in}\bigr)/\tfrac{1}{n}(1,-1,a,{\mathbf{0}}).

Thus the KSB deformation space is smooth and has dimension
r
.

For
r=1
we get a 1-dimensional KSB deformation space.
As observed by
[
BC94
]
, the
r>1
cases can be reduced to the
r=1
cases as follows.
Change the deformation (
10
.3) to the factored form

\bigl(xy=\textstyle{\prod}_{i=1}^{r}(z^{n}-s_{i})\bigr)/\tfrac{1}{n}(1,-1,a,{\mathbf{0}}),

and repeatedly blow up the divisors
(x=z^{n}-s_{i}=0)

to obtain a small, cerpant modification, which is a flat KSB deformation of its central fiber. The central fiber is a modification of

\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{rn^{2}}(1,arn{-}1)
that has
r
singularities of the form

\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}(1,an{-}1)
, connected by
r-1
curves whose birational transforms are
(-1)
-curves on the minimal resolution of the central fiber. Using Notation
11
, the dual graph is the following.

[formula]

{\boxed{\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}(1,an{-}1)}-}\ 1\ {-\boxed{\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}(1,an{-}1)}-}\ \cdots\ {-\boxed{\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}(1,an{-}1)}-}\ 1\ {-\boxed{\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}(1,an{-}1)}}

This construction leads to the notion of M-modifications in (
24
).

Note that, as we repeatedly contract
(-1)
-curves in (
10
.4), we never contract the curves on the two ends. These show the following.

Claim
10
.5.
The minimal resolution of

\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{rn^{2}}(1,arn{-}1)
and the minimal resolution of (
10
.4) are isomorphic along the birational transforms of the
x
and
y
axes. ∎

Claim
10
.6.

The map
\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{\mathbf{s}}\to\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{\mathbf{t}}
, given by the elementary symmetric functions, is a Galois cover of degree
n!
. ∎

Notation 11
.

For
\frac{n}{q}=[c_{1},\dots,c_{s}]

a_{1}\ {-\boxed{\tfrac{n}{q}}-}\ a_{2}\quad\mbox{resp.}\quad a_{1}\ {-\boxed{\tfrac{n}{q}}}

3.
Surface pairs with reduced boundary

Notation 12
.

Let
(S,D)
be an lc surface pair, where
D
is a reduced curve.
There are 3 possible local normal forms at points
p\in D\subset S
; see for example
[
Kol13
, Sec.3.3]
.

(12.1)

(Cyclic, plt)

(S_{n,q},B_{n,q}):=\bigl(\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{xy},(x=0)\bigr)/\tfrac{1}{n}(1,q)
, where
(n,q)=1
. The minimal resolution dual graph is the following, where
\frac{n}{q}=[c_{1},\dots,c_{s}]
and
\bullet
denotes the birational transform of
B_{n,q}
.

c_{1}\ -\ c_{2}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s}\ -\ \bullet

(12.2)

(Cyclic, non-plt)

(S_{n,q},D_{n,q}):=\bigl(\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{xy},(xy=0)\bigr)/\tfrac{1}{n}(1,q)
, where
(n,q)=1
.
The minimal resolution dual graph is

\bullet\ -\ c_{1}\ -\ c_{2}\ -\ \cdots\ -\ c_{s}\ -\ \bullet,

where
\bullet
denotes a branch of the birational transform of
D_{n,q}
.
Note that
D_{n,n{-}1}
is the Du Val singularity
(uv=w^{n})
. These are the only ones for which
D_{n,q}
is Cartier and they behave exceptionally in many respects.

(12.3)

(Dihedral)
The pair

4.
Q-modifications

P-modifications were introduced in
[
KSB88
]
(under the name P-resolution) to describe smoothings of quotient singularities. A variant, using M-modifications is developed in
[
BC94
]
. To handle arbitrary deformations of pairs we introduce
Q-modifications. These are actually very natural objects from the point of view of birational geometry, and many basic results on P- and M-modifications
hold for Q-modifications.

Definition 16
.

Let
(s,S)
be a rational surface singularity. A proper, birational morphism
\pi:S_{Q}\to S
is a
Q-modification

if

(16.1)

K_{S_{Q}}
is
\pi
-nef, and

(16.2)

S_{Q}
has only quotient singularities. (So ‘Q’ for quotient.)

Notation 17
.

Let
(0,S)
be a rational singularity with minimal resolution

\mu:S^{\rm m}\to S
. Let
\pi:\bar{S}\to S
be a proper, birational morphism.
Assume that
\bar{S}
is normal with minimal resolution

\bar{\mu}:\bar{S}^{\rm m}\to\bar{S}
.
Then
\bar{S}^{\rm m}
dominates
S^{\rm m}
, giving a commutative diagram

[formula]

\textstyle{\bar{S}^{\rm m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\bar{\mu}}
\scriptstyle{\pi^{m}}
\textstyle{\bar{S}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{S^{\rm m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau}
\scriptstyle{\mu}
\textstyle{S}

We can write

[formula]

K_{\bar{S}^{\rm m}}\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\bar{\mu}^{*}K_{\bar{S}}-\bar{\Delta},\quad\mbox{and}\quad K_{\bar{S}^{\rm m}}\sim(\pi^{m})^{*}K_{S^{\rm m}}+F,

where
\bar{\Delta}
is effective with
\operatorname{Supp}\bar{\Delta}\subset\operatorname{Ex}(\bar{\mu})
,
and
F
is effective with
\operatorname{Supp}F=\operatorname{Ex}(\pi^{m})
.

Let
\bar{A}^{\rm m}\subset\bar{S}^{\rm m}
be an irreducible curve that is exceptional over

, with images

and
\bar{A}\subset\bar{S}
. (We use

if the image is a point.)
Then (
17
.2) gives that

[formula]

(\bar{A}\cdot K_{\bar{S}})=(A^{\rm m}\cdot K_{S^{\rm m}})+\bigl(\bar{A}^{\rm m}\cdot F\bigr)+\bigl(\bar{A}^{\rm m}\cdot\bar{\Delta}\bigr).

From this we conclude the following.

(17.4)

If
\bar{A}\neq\emptyset
and
A^{\rm m}\neq\emptyset
, then

(\bar{A}\cdot K_{\bar{S}})\geq 0
, with equalty holding iff

(A^{\rm m}\cdot K_{S^{\rm m}})=0
and
\tau
is an isomorphism along

.

(17.5)

If

, then

(\bar{A}\cdot K_{\bar{S}})
depends only on the intersection numbers of the curves in
\operatorname{Ex}(\bar{\mu})\cup\operatorname{Ex}(\pi^{m})
.

Lemma 18
.

Using the notation of (
17
), assume that
K_{\bar{S}}
is
\pi
-nef.
Then

-K_{\bar{S}^{\rm m}}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly equivalent to an effective linear combination of curves, that are exceptional for

Proof. By (

.2)

-nef,

-K_{\bar{S}}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly equivalent to an effective linear combination of
\pi
-exceptional curves. ∎

Remark 19
.

This is especially strong if

has quotient singularities. Then, by
[
KSB88
, 3.13]
, there is a unique, maximal

is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly equivalent to an effective linear combination of
\pi^{\prime}
-exceptional curves.
This gives an effective procedure to construct all Q-modifications.

Lemma 20
.

Let
\pi:\bar{S}\to S
be a Q-modification and
\pi^{m}
as in (
17
.1).
Then
\pi^{m}
is a composite of a
sequence of blow ups

\bar{S}^{\rm m}=:Y_{r}\to Y_{r-1}\to\cdots\to Y_{1}\to Y_{0}:=S^{\rm m},

where each

is the blow-up of a singular point of the
image if
\bar{\Delta}
in
Y_{i}
.

Proof. By (
17
.2)
K_{\bar{S}^{\rm m}}\sim\bar{\mu}^{*}K_{\bar{S}}-\bar{\Delta}
,
where

\lfloor{\bar{\Delta}}\rfloor=0
since
\bar{S}
has only quotient singularities.

For any

\bar{\Delta}_{j}
denote the push-forward of
\bar{\Delta}
to
Y_{j}
. Then
K_{Y_{r}}+\bar{\Delta}_{r}\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\bar{\mu}^{*}K_{\bar{S}}

.
Let now

denote the
exceptional curve of

. Then
(K_{Y_{i+1}}\cdot F_{i+1})=-1
,
so
(\bar{\Delta}_{i+1}\cdot F_{i+1})\geq 1
. Since
\lfloor{\bar{\Delta}_{j}}\rfloor=0
,

must intersect at least 2

\bar{\Delta}_{i+1}
(different from

).
The images of these in

show that the blow-up center is

\bar{\Delta}_{i}
.
∎

These show that Q-modifications are combinatorial objects that are

In fact, the following much stronger result holds, which generalizes

To state it,
consider a graph with vertices

.

Proposition 21
.

Let
S_{1},S_{2}
be surfaces with rational singularities
and

isomorphism onto an induced

(21.1)

\tau_{1}:S_{1}^{\rm m}\to S_{1}\leftarrow\bar{S}_{1}

, and

(21.2)

Q-modifications
\tau_{2}:S_{2}^{\rm m}\to S_{2}\leftarrow\bar{S}_{2}
of

is supported on

.

\bar{S}_{1}
and
\bar{S}_{2}
have the same singularities.

Proof. Given a Q-modification of
S_{1}
, we construct a Q-modification of
S_{2}
as follows.

By (
20
),
\pi_{1}^{\rm m}:\bar{S}_{1}^{\rm m}\to S_{1}^{\rm m}
is obtained by repeatedly blowing up singular points of the exceptional divisor. We do the same blow ups to
get
\bar{S}_{2}^{\rm m}\to S_{2}^{\rm m}
. Then

lifts to an
embedding
\bar{\jmath}
of the dual graph of
\operatorname{Ex}(\bar{S}_{1}^{\rm m}\to S_{1})

into the dual graph of
\operatorname{Ex}(\bar{S}_{2}^{\rm m}\to S_{2})
.

Next we get

by contracting certain

\operatorname{Ex}(\bar{S}_{1}^{\rm m}\to S_{1})
.
Using
\bar{\jmath}
, we construct

\bar{\mu}^{\prime}_{2}:\bar{S}_{2}^{\rm m}\to\bar{S}^{\prime}_{2}

by contracting the corresponding
curves of
\operatorname{Ex}(\bar{S}_{2}^{\rm m}\to S_{2})
.
By construction,
\bar{S}_{2}
has the same singularities as
\bar{S}_{1}
.

We need to show that
K_{\bar{S}_{2}}
has nonnegative intersection number with
every exceptional curve of
\bar{S}_{2}\to S_{2}
.
For images of curves in
\operatorname{Ex}(\pi_{2}^{\rm m})
this follows from (
17
.5). For images of curves in
\operatorname{Ex}(\mu_{2})
this follows from (
17
.4).

Conversely, let
\tau_{2}:S_{2}^{\rm m}\to S_{2}\leftarrow\bar{S}_{2}
be a Q-modification of
S_{2}
as in (
21
.2).
Then
(
20
) shows that

\bar{S}_{2}^{\rm m}\to S_{2}^{\rm m}
is obtained by repeatedly blowing up
singular points of
j({\mathcal{D}}(S_{1}))
. This shows that the above procedure can be reversed to get
\tau_{1}:S_{1}^{\rm m}\to S_{1}\leftarrow\bar{S}_{1}
of

.
∎

Definition 22
.

Let

C^{\prime}_{0},C^{\prime\prime}_{0}
but not
C_{1}
. If
C_{2},\dots,C_{i}
are contracted to an
A_{i-1}
point by
\tau_{T}
, then
C^{\prime}_{0},C^{\prime\prime}_{0},C_{1},\dots,C_{i}
are contracted to a
D_{i+2}
point by
\tau_{D}
.
Finally if
C_{2}
is not contracted, then

C^{\prime}_{0},C^{\prime\prime}_{0},C_{1}
are contracted to an
A_{3}
point by
\tau_{D}
.

The main reason to study
P- and M-modifications is the following, essentially proved in
[
KSB88
, 3.9]
and
[
BC94
]
.

Theorem 29
.

Let
(0,S)
be a rational surface singularity,
\pi_{P}:S_{P}\to S
a P-modification, and

\pi_{MP}:S_{M}\to S_{P}
the corresponding M-modification.
Then there is a commutative diagram

[formula]

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\operatorname{Univ}_{\rm KSB}(S_{M})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c_{MP}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\operatorname{Univ}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c_{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\operatorname{Univ}(S)\\[4.30554pt]
u_{M}\downarrow&&u_{P}\downarrow&&u_{S}\downarrow\\
\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{M})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{MP}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\operatorname{Def}(S)\end{array}

where

(29.2)

\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{M})
and

\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P})
are smooth of the same dimension,

(29.3)

\tau_{MP}:\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{M})\to\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P})
is finite and Galois,

(29.4)

\tau_{P}:\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P})\to\operatorname{Def}(S)

is a finite, birational morphism onto an irreducible component of

\operatorname{Def}(S)
.

Sketch of proof. For the existence of the diagram we need to show that the
morphisms
\pi_{P}:S_{P}\to S
and

\pi_{MP}:S_{M}\to S_{P}
extend to
their flat deformations

c_{P}:\operatorname{Univ}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P})\to\operatorname{Univ}(S)\quad\mbox{and}\quad c_{MP}:\operatorname{Univ}_{\rm KSB}(S_{M})\to\operatorname{Univ}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P}).

The relevant property is the vanishing of

R^{1}(\pi_{P})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{P}}
and of
R^{1}(\pi_{MP})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{M}}
.
The proof is summarized in
[
KM92
, 11.4]
, which in turn relies mainly
on
[
Wah76
]
.

Since KSB deformations are locally unobstructed, the obstruction to smoothness lies in
H^{2}
of the tangent sheaf. If
X\to U
is proper of fiber dimension
\leq 1
, and
U
is affine, then
H^{i}
of any coherent sheaf on
X
vanishes for
i\geq 2
. This implies smoothness in (
29
.2); see (
41
) for details.

The relation between
P- and M-modifications is established in
[
BC94
]
.
The Galois group in (
29
.3) is a product of reflection groups, determined by the singularities of
S_{P}
, see
[
BC94
]
and (
10
.6) for a key example.

In order to show (
29
.4), note first that
every fiber of
u_{P}
is a P-modification of the corresponding deformation of
S
. We check in (
57
) that P-modifications
\pi_{P}:S_{P}\to S
do not have nontrivial 1-parameter deformations fixing
S
.
This shows that
\tau_{P}
is finite.
As in
[
Art74
]
,
\tau_{P}

is onto an irreducible component of
\operatorname{Def}(S)

by openness of versality.
Thus
\tau_{P}:\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P})\to\operatorname{Def}(S)

is a finite cover. In order to show that it has degree 1, let

T\to\operatorname{Def}(S)
be a morphism from the spectrum of a DVR that maps the closed (resp. generic) point the closed (resp. generic) point.
By what we already proved, there is a finite
T^{\prime}\to T
such that the pull-back of
\operatorname{Univ}(S)
to
T^{\prime}\to T\to\operatorname{Def}(S)
is obtained from a KSB deformation of
S_{P}
. We may assume that
T^{\prime}/T
is Galois. As noted in
[
KSB88
, p.312]
,
the action of
\operatorname{Gal}(T^{\prime}/T)
lifts to an action on this KSB deformation.
Taking the quotient shows that the pull-back of
\operatorname{Univ}(S)
to
T\to\operatorname{Def}(S)
is obtained from a KSB deformation of
S_{P}
. Thus

T\to\operatorname{Def}(S)
lifts to
T\to\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S)
, so

\tau_{P}
is birational. ∎

Remark
29
.5.
By
[
Ste91a
]
,
\tau_{P}

is an isomorphism onto an irreducible component of
\operatorname{Def}(S)
for cyclic quotients, but
there are dihedral examples where
\tau_{P}

is not an isomorphism.
Also,
\tau_{P}
is birational but not necessarily finite
for the more general
P-modifications considered in
[
Kol91
, Sec.6]
.

6.
Tangent sheaves

In order to compute the dimensions of the deformation spaces,
we need various results on tangent and logarithmic tangent sheaves for
modifications of rational singularities. These are mostly taken from

[
Lau73a
,
Lau73b
]
and

[
LP07
,
KLP12
]
. Note that
[
LP07
,
KLP12
]
focus on the global aspects, which tend to be more subtle.
See also
[
PPS23
,
RU22
,
DRU22
]
for similar computations.

30
Tangent and logarithmic tangent sheaves.

Let
S
be a smooth surface and
D\subset S
a smooth curve with normal bundle
N_{D}
.
There is a natural map
T_{S}\to N_{D}
. Its kernel is the

logarithmic tangent bundle
of the pair
(S,D)
, denoted by

T_{S}(-\log D)
. Thus we have
an exact sequence

[formula]

0\to T_{S}(-\log D)\to T_{S}\to N_{D}\to 0.

The restriction of
T_{S}(-\log D)
to
D
sits in an exact sequence

[formula]

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{D}\to T_{S}(-\log D)|_{D}\to T_{D}\to 0.

Locally, if
D=(y=0)
, then the kernel
{\mathcal{O}}_{D}
is generated by

y\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}
and the quotient
T_{D}
by

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}
.

Next assume that
S
is smooth but
D\subset S
is a nodal curve
with normalization
\tau:\bar{D}\to D
. The
immersed normal sheaf
of
D
is

[formula]

N_{\bar{D}}:=\tau_{*}\operatorname{coker}\bigl[T_{\bar{D}}\to\tau^{*}T_{S}\bigr].

Then (
30
.1) becomes

0\to T_{S}(-\log D)\to T_{S}\to N_{\bar{D}}\to 0,

where
T_{S}(-\log D)
is locally free. More generally, if
D=D_{1}+D_{2}
, then we have

[formula]

0\to T_{S}(-\log(D_{1}+D_{2}))\to T_{S}(-\log D_{1})\to N_{\bar{D}_{2}}\to 0.

The sequence (
30
.2) now gives

[formula]

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{\bar{D}}\to\tau^{*}T_{S}(-\log D)\to T_{\bar{D}}(-N)\to 0,

where
N\subset\bar{D}
is the preimage of the nodes of
D
.

If
S
is a normal surface and
D\subset S
a reduced curve, then there is a finite subset
P\subset S
such that
(S\setminus P,D\setminus P)
is a smooth pair. Let
j:S\setminus P\lhook\joinrel\to S
be the natural injection.
The
tangent sheaf
of
S
and the
logarithmic tangent sheaf
of
(S,D)
are defined as

[formula]

T_{S}:=j_{*}T_{S\setminus P},\quad\mbox{and}\quad T_{S}(-\log D):=j_{*}\bigl(T_{S\setminus P}(-\log(D\setminus P))\bigr).

In the log canonical case, we have the following close analog of (
30
.4).

Lemma 31
.

Let
(S,D_{1}+D_{2})
be a log canonical pair,

\mu:S^{\rm m}\to S
the minimal resolution, and
D^{\rm m}_{i}:=\mu^{-1}_{*}D_{i}
with
normalizations
\tau_{i}:\bar{D}^{\rm m}_{i}\to D^{\rm m}_{i}
. Then
there is an exact sequence

[formula]

0\to T_{S}(-\log(D_{1}+D_{2}))\to T_{S}(-\log(D_{1}))\to\mu_{*}N_{\bar{D}^{\rm m}_{2}}\to 0.

Proof. Over the smooth locus of
S
, this is (
30
.4).
Thus it remains to check what happens at the singular points that are contained in
D_{1}+D_{2}
. For this we can use local analytic coordinates.

If a finite group
G
acts with isolated fixed points on a surface
X
, then the local sections of
T_{X/G}
are the
G
-invariant local sections of
T_{X}
.
Thus the tangent sheaf of
S_{n,q}=\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{xy}/\frac{1}{n}(1,q)
is generated by

[formula]

x\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x},y^{q^{\prime}}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x},y\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y},x^{q}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}.

If
B_{x}\subset S_{n,q}
is the image of the
x
-axis, then we see that

T_{S}(-\log B_{x})
is generated by

x\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x},y^{q^{\prime}}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x},y\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}
, and the quotient

T_{S}/T_{S}(-\log B_{x})
is generated by
x^{q}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}
.

For the minimal resolution of
S_{n,q}
we use the chart given in
(
7
.2). The chain rule gives that

(\pi_{1})_{*}\bigl(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}}\bigr)=x^{q}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}.

That is,
T_{S}/T_{S}(-\log B_{x})
is naturally isomorphic to the
normal bundle of
B_{x}^{\rm m}\subset S^{\rm m}_{n,q}
. This shows (
31
.1) at the cyclic quotient points.

At a dihedral point
(S^{d}_{n,q},B^{d}_{n,q})
, the minimal resolution can be obtained by first taking a double cover

(S_{N,Q},D_{N,Q})\to(S^{d}_{n,q},B^{d}_{n,q})
, resolving
S_{N,Q}
and then quotienting out by the involution. The latter is fixedpoint-free along the birational transform of
D_{N,Q}
, so the normal bundle computation for the birational transform of
B^{d}_{n,q}
is the same as for the birational transform of
D_{N,Q}
.
Finally note that
Q^{2}\equiv 1\mod N
, so the
quotient
T_{S}/T_{S}(-\log B_{x})
is generated by

x^{Q}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}+y^{Q}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}
,
where
x,y
are the orbifold coordinates on
S_{N,Q}
.
This shows (
31
.1) at the dihedral quotient points. ∎

Proposition 32
.

Let
(X,D)
be a log canonical pair with quotient singularities. Let
\pi:X^{\rm m}\to X
be either the minimal resolution,
or the blow-up of a node of
D
where
X
is smooth.
Let
E^{\rm m}
be the exceptional curve and
D^{\rm m}
the birational transform of
D
. Then

(32.1)

\pi_{*}T_{X^{\rm m}}\bigl(-\log(E^{\rm m}+D^{\rm m})\bigr)=T_{X}(-\log D)
,

(32.2)

R^{1}\pi_{*}T_{X^{\rm m}}\bigl(-\log(E^{\rm m}+D^{\rm m})\bigr)=R^{1}\pi_{*}T_{X^{\rm m}}\bigl(-\log(E^{\rm m})\bigr)=0
, and

(32.3)

H^{i}\bigl(X,T_{X}(-\log D)\bigr)=H^{i}\bigl(X^{\rm m},T_{X^{\rm m}}\bigl(-\log(E^{\rm m}+D^{\rm m})\bigr)\bigr)
for every
i
.

Proof.
For (
32
.1) the
blow-up of a node of
D
is a simple computation.
At a singular point of
X
,
it suffices to show the
D=\emptyset
case. That is, all local generators of

T_{X}
lift to sections of

T_{X^{\rm m}}\bigl(-\log E^{\rm m}\bigr)
.
This should hold for all normal singularities (
33
), but here is an argument for quotient singularities.

Write
S=\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/G
. Let
\pi:B_{0}\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\to\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
the blow-up of the origin with exceptional curve
E
. By direct computation,

\pi_{*}T_{B_{0}\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}}(-\log E)=m_{0}T_{\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}}
,
where
m_{0}
is the maximal ideal at the origin.
Set
\pi^{\prime}:S^{\prime}:=B_{0}\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/G\to S
with exceptional curve
E^{\prime}
.
Taking
G
-invariants gives that

\pi_{*}T_{S^{\prime}}(-\log E^{\prime})=(m_{0}T_{\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}})^{G}
.
Now note that the
G
-action on
T_{\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}}/m_{0}T_{\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}}
has no fixed vectors,
so
(m_{0}T_{\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}})^{G}=T_{\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}}^{G}=T_{S}
.

The
G
-action on
B_{0}\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
leaves
E
invariant, so

S^{\prime}
has only cyclic quotient singularities.
Unfortunately, iterating the above process does not give a resolution, see
[
Kol07
, 2.29]
. However, for cyclic quotients we can use the blow-up charts
(
7
.1–2). By direct computation

x\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}=nx_{2}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}-qy_{2}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}},

proving (
32
.1).

The
D=\emptyset
case of
(
32
.2) is discussed in (
35
.1).
For general
D
, we use (
30
.4) to get

0\to T_{X^{\rm m}}\bigl(-\log(E^{\rm m}+D^{\rm m})\bigr)\to T_{X^{\rm m}}\bigl(-\log(E^{\rm m})\bigr)\to N_{D^{\rm m}}\to 0.

Pushing this forward, we get that

R^{1}\pi_{*}T_{X^{\rm m}}\bigl(-\log(E^{\rm m}+D^{\rm m})\bigr)\cong\operatorname{coker}\bigl[T_{S}\to\pi_{*}N_{\bar{D}^{\rm m}}\bigr],

and the latter is 0 by (
31
).
The Leray spectral sequence now gives (
32
.3).∎

Remark
32
.4.
Let
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
be the blow-up of a smooth point with exceptional curve
E
. Then

h^{1}(X^{\prime},T_{X^{\prime}}(-\log E))=h^{1}(X,T_{X})+2
.

Complement
32
.5.
The computations also show that if all the singularities are

(S_{n,q},D_{n,q})
, then
T_{X}(-\log D)
is locally free
and
\pi^{*}T_{X}(-\log D)\cong T_{X^{\rm m}}\bigl(-\log(E^{\rm m}+D^{\rm m})\bigr)
.

Question 33
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and
\pi:X^{\rm c}\to X
its canonical modification. Every automorphism of
X
lifts to an automorphism of
X^{\rm c}
, and, if
X
is proper, then
H^{0}(X,T_{X})
is the tangent space of the automorphism group. Thus one can expect that

[formula]

\pi_{*}T_{X^{\rm c}}=T_{X}.

[
HM94
, 4.1]
says that if
X
is local and complete, then again

H^{0}(X,T_{X})
is the tangent space of the automorphism group, suggesting that
(
33
.1) indeed holds.

The caveat is that both objects are infinite dimensional, so one would need to check that the above finite-dimensional intuition works out in general.

We can now compute the global contribution to the dimension of our
deformation spaces.

Proposition 34
.

Let
S
be a affine surface with rational singularities and
D\subset S
a reduced curve. Let

\mu:S^{\rm m}\to S
be the minimal resolution with reduced exceptional
set
E^{\rm m}
, and

\pi:{\bar{S}}\to S
a Q-modification with reduced exceptional set
\bar{E}
. Let
D^{\rm m}
resp.
\bar{D}
denote the birational transforms of
D
on
S^{\rm m}
resp.
\bar{S}
. Assume that
(\bar{S},\bar{E}+\bar{D})
is lc and

(S^{\rm m},E^{\rm m}+D^{\rm m})
is normal crossing.

For an irreducible component
\bar{E}_{i}\subset\bar{E}
, let

e_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
be the negative of the self-intersection of its
birational transform on the minimal resolution of
{\bar{S}}
.
Then

(34.1)

h^{1}\bigl({\bar{S}},T_{\bar{S}}(-\log\bar{D})\bigr)=h^{1}\bigl({\bar{S}},T_{\bar{S}}(-\log(\bar{E}+\bar{D}))\bigr)+\textstyle{\sum}_{i\in I}(e_{i}-1)
, and

(34.2)

h^{1}\bigl({\bar{S}},T_{\bar{S}}(-\log(\bar{E}+\bar{D}))\bigr)=h^{1}\bigl(S^{\rm m},T_{S^{\rm m}}(-\log(E^{\rm m}+D^{\rm m}))\bigr).

Proof.
(
31
) gives an exact sequence

0\to T_{\bar{S}}\bigl(-\log(\bar{E}+\bar{D})\bigr)\to T_{\bar{S}}(-\log\bar{D})\to\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{\bar{E}_{i}}(-e_{i})\to 0.

Here
h^{0}\bigl(\bar{E}_{i},{\mathcal{O}}_{\bar{E}_{i}}(-e_{i})\bigr)=0
since
e_{i}>0
, so taking cohomologies gives the first claim.
For the second, we use the notation of (
17
), and claim that

h^{1}\bigl(\bar{S}^{\rm m},T_{\bar{S}^{\rm m}}(-\log(\bar{F}^{\rm m}+\bar{D}^{\rm m}))\bigr)

equals both terms in (
34
.2), where
\bar{F}^{\rm m}
is the reduced exceptional set of
\bar{S}^{\rm m}\to S
.
For
\bar{S}^{\rm m}\to\bar{S}
we use the minimal resolution case of (
32
).
Next, since
\bar{S}^{\rm m}\to S^{\rm m}
is a composite of node blow-ups by (
20
), we can repeatedly use the
node blow-up case of (
32
). ∎

7.
Dimension formulas

We compute the dimension of the irreducible components of various deformation spaces using invariants of the singularity and its P-modifications.
The formulas are simplest when
S
is determined by
{\mathcal{D}}(S)
.

35
.

Let
(s,S)
be a rational surface singularity with
minimal resolution
\mu:S^{\rm m}\to S
and reduced exceptional curve

C^{\rm m}=\cup_{i\in I}C_{i}
.

By
[
Ser06
, Sec.3.4.4]
,
H^{1}\bigl(S^{\rm m},T_{S^{\rm m}}(-\log C^{\rm m})\bigr)

is the tangent space to those deformations of
S^{\rm m}
where every
C_{i}
lifts. Equivalently, these are those deformations of
S
that preserve the dual graph. The dimension of this tangent space is usually hard to compute, but it is known in several important cases.

Quotient singularities
35
.1.
The dual graph determines
the singularity, hence
H^{1}\bigl(S^{\rm m},T_{S^{\rm m}}(-\log C^{\rm m})\bigr)=0
.
This has been long known; see for example
[
Bri68
]
,

[
BPV84
, III.5.1]
or

[
Kol13
, 3.32]
.

Taut singularities
35
.2.
These are the singularities that are determined by their dual graphs.
For these
H^{1}\bigl(S^{\rm m},T_{S^{\rm m}}(-\log C^{\rm m})\bigr)=0
.
A rather laborious result of
[
Lau73a
,
Lau73b
]
says that
this vanishing implies tautness, with a few exceptions; these are enumerated in
[
Lau73b
, 3.2]
.
The complete list of taut singularities is given in
[
Lau73b
]
.

A simple case is when
{\mathcal{D}}(S)
has only 1 fork, which has degree 3 and self-intersection
\leq-3
. This is used for the
W(p,q,r)
singularities studied in
[
PS22
]
.

Weighted homogeneous singularities
35
.3.

For these
{\mathcal{D}}(S)
has only 1 fork. Let
d_{0}
denote its degree and
-c_{0}
its self-intersection. By
[
Lau73b
, 4.1.III]
, if

c_{0}\geq 2d_{0}-3
then

H^{1}\bigl(S^{\rm m},T_{S^{\rm m}}(-\log C^{\rm m})\bigr)=d_{0}-3
.

These are used
in
[
JS23
]
under the (mostly) weaker assumption

c_{0}\geq d_{0}+3
.

For P-modifications, we need only the simplest numerical
invariants.

Definition 36
.

Let
\pi:S_{P}\to S
be a
P-modification of a normal singularity. Let

\{p_{j}\in S_{P}:j\in J_{P}\}\quad\mbox{resp.}\quad\{E_{i}\subset S_{P}:i\in I_{P}\}

denote the singular points of
S_{P}
, respectively
the irreducible, exceptional curves of
\pi:S_{P}\to S
.
Set
r_{j}=r
if
p_{j}
is of type
A_{r},D_{r},E_{r}
or

\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/\tfrac{1}{rn^{2}}(1,arn{-}1)
for some
n>1
.
Note that
r_{j}=\dim\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(p_{j},S_{P})
by (
10
.2).

Let
e_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
denote the negative of the self-intersection of the
birational transform of
E_{i}
on the minimal resolution of
S_{P}
.

We can now state the first dimension formula.

Proposition 37
.

Let
\pi:S_{P}\to S
be a P-modification of an affine surface with rational singularities, and
I_{P},J_{P}
as in (
36
).
Let
S^{\rm m}\to S
be the minimal resolution, with reduced exceptional curve
C^{\rm m}
.
Then

[formula]

\dim\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P})=\textstyle{\sum}_{j\in J_{P}}r_{j}+\textstyle{\sum}_{i\in I_{P}}(e_{i}-1)+h^{1}\bigl(S^{\rm m},T_{S^{\rm m}}(-\log C^{\rm m})\bigr).

In particular, if
S
has taut singularities, then

[formula]

\dim\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P})=\textstyle{\sum}_{j\in J_{P}}r_{j}+\textstyle{\sum}_{i\in I_{P}}(e_{i}-1).

Proof. First note that, by (
41
),

\dim\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{P})=\textstyle{\sum}_{j\in J_{P}}\dim\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(p_{j},S_{P})+h^{1}(S_{P},T_{S_{P}}).

Here
r_{j}=\dim\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(p_{j},S_{P})
by (
10
.2),
this explains the summand
\textstyle{\sum}_{j\in J_{P}}r_{j}
.
The computation of
h^{1}(S_{P},T_{S_{P}})
is the
D=0
special case of (
34
). ∎

For KSB deformations of pairs we have the following.
The dihedral case is discussed in (
40
).

Theorem 38
.

Let
\pi:(S_{P},D_{P}+E_{P})\to(S,D):=(S_{n,q},D_{n.q})

.

Proof. If
p_{j}

or of

Remark 39
.

Comparing (
37
) and

Various aspects of the first order deformation theory of cyclic quotients are worked out in

; the references below are to the version in

[

Fix a pair
(S,D):=\bigl(\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{xy},(xy=0)\bigr)/\tfrac{1}{n}(1,q)
.
Using
\partial_{x}:=x(\partial/\partial x)
and
\partial_{y}:=y(\partial/\partial y)
,
the first order deformation space
T^{1}(S)
of
S
has a basis consisting of certain operators

{\mathcal{D}}=\tfrac{\alpha\partial_{x}-\beta\partial_{y}}{M},\quad\mbox{where}\quad\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad M=x^{a}y^{b}.

Since
\omega_{S}(D)\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
, we can think of the curve
D
as determined by

{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\to\omega_{S}^{-1}\quad\mbox{given by}\quad 1\mapsto\tfrac{xy}{dx\wedge dy}.

The lifting criterion
[
Kol23
, 6.58]
says that

\frac{xy}{dx\wedge dy}
lifts to a first order deformation determined by

{\mathcal{D}}=({\alpha\partial_{x}-\beta\partial_{y}})/(x^{a}y^{b})
iff

{\mathcal{D}}(xy)-xy\nabla{\mathcal{D}}=(b\beta-a\alpha)\tfrac{xy}{x^{a}y^{b}}

equals
0
, as an element in
H^{1}(\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\},{\mathcal{O}})
.
The latter holds if either
b\beta-a\alpha=0
or
\min\{a,b\}\leq 1
.

Once
\frac{xy}{dx\wedge dy}
lifts to a first order deformation

S[\epsilon]
, the lift
D[\epsilon]
of
D
is given by the induced

\omega_{S[\epsilon]/k[\epsilon]}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S[\epsilon]}
, thus

D[\epsilon]
is also flat over
k[\epsilon]
. Hence

\bigl(S[\epsilon],D[\epsilon]\bigr)
is a KSB deformation of
(S,D)
as in
[
Kol23
, 6.21–22]
.

We can now look at the basis of
T^{1}(S)
given in
[
Kol23
, 6.73]
.
The above condition is satisfied by the basis elements
\partial_{x}/M_{1},\partial_{y}/M_{r-1}
,
by a 1-dimensional subspace of each
\bigl\langle\partial_{x}/M_{i},\partial_{y}/M_{i}\bigr\rangle
for
2\leq i\leq r{-}2
, and by all the basis elements in the list
[
Kol23
, 6.73.2]
. Since the
r
in
[
Kol23
, 6.73]
is
m(S)-1
(where
m(S)
is the multiplicity), we have proved the following.

Theorem 54

Set

. Then

has codimension

in

. ∎

The philosophy of
[
Kol23
]
says that in order to get the optimal moduli theory, we should establish properties of 1-parameter deformations, and then formulate versions of these properties over arbitrary bases.
The second step is not necessarily unique.
For deformations of pairs, this poses the following.

Question 56
.

Is there a good way to impose additional requirements for KSB deformations of a pair to make
\operatorname{Def}_{\rm KSB}(S_{n,q},D_{n,q})
smooth?

10.
Deformations of modifications

We prove that Q-modifications
X\to S
have no (small or large) KSB deformations over reduced bases that keep
S
fixed. (See (
59
) for other deformations and non-reduced bases.)
More generally, we have the following.

Theorem 57
.

Let
S
be a normal surface,
B
a smooth, irreducible curve, and
g:Y\to S\times B
a proper birational morphism,
Y
normal. Assume that

(57.1)

g_{b}:Y_{b}\to S
is birational for every
b\in B
, and

(57.2)

there is a dense, open subset
B^{\circ}\subset B
such that, for every
b\in B^{\circ}
,

Y_{b}\to S
is a Q-modification.

Then,
Y\to B
is trivial. That is, for any
b\in B
,

[formula]

\bigl(Y\to S\times B\to B\bigr)\cong\bigl(Y_{b}\times B\to S\times B\to B\bigr).

Proof. Over a dense, open subset
B^{*}\subset B^{\circ}
,
the minimal resolutions
\{Y_{b}^{\rm m}\to Y_{b}:b\in B^{*}\}

form a flat family. By (
20
),
we have only finitely many choices for the centers of the blow-ups giving

Y_{b}^{\rm m}\to S^{\rm m}
, so the
\{Y_{b}^{\rm m}:b\in B^{*}\}
,
and hence also
\{Y_{b}:b\in B^{*}\}
, form localy trivial families.

Thus the family is also trivial over
B

by (
58
). ∎

Lemma 58
.

Let
g:Y\to X\times B
be a projective morphism such that

Y
is normal and

g_{b}:\operatorname{red}(Y_{b})\to X_{b}
is birational for every
b
.
Assume that there is a dense, open
B^{\circ}\subset B
such that

(Y^{\circ}\to B^{\circ})\cong((Y^{\prime}\times B^{\circ})\to B^{\circ})
for some
normal
Y^{\prime}
. Then
(Y\to B)\cong((Y^{\prime}\times B)\to B)
.

Proof. Let
H
be relatively ample on
Y\to X\times B
and let
H^{\prime}
be its
birational transform on
(Y^{\prime}\times B)\to B
.

H^{\prime}
is Cartier over
B^{\circ}
, so Cartier by
[
Ram63
,
Sam62
]
; see also

[
Kol23
, 4.21]
.
Thus
H^{\prime}
is relatively ample on
(Y^{\prime}\times B)\to B
.
Thus
(Y\to B)\cong((Y^{\prime}\times B)\to B)

by
[
MM64
]
; see
[
Kol23
, 11.39]
for the form that we use. ∎

Example 59
.

M-modifications have nontrivial deformations, as
the next examples show.

(
59
.1) Let
S_{P}\to S
be any P-modification whose construction involves blowing up a point
p\in S^{\rm m}
. We can move the point

p
along the exceptional curve and contract in the family to get a
nontrivial flat deformation of
S_{P}\to S
(keeping
S
fixed).
Here the canonical class of the generic fiber is not relatively nef.
The simplest example is the singularity
3\ -\ 3
. After one blow-up, the central and generic fibers are

4\ -\ 1\ -\ 4\quad\mbox{resp.}\quad 3\ -\ 4\ -\ 1.

All curves marked
4
are then contracted. The central fiber is an
M-modification. In the generic fiber, the canonical class has degree
-\frac{1}{2}
on the image of the
curve marked
1
.

(
59
.2) For
A_{n}:=(xy=z^{n+1})
, the miniversal deformation of
A_{n}
is

\bigl(xy=z^{n+1}+\textstyle{\sum}_{i=0}^{n-1}t_{i}z^{i}\bigr),

while the miniversal deformation of its minimal resolution is obtained from

\bigl(xy=\textstyle{\prod}_{i=0}^{n}(z-s_{i})\bigr),\quad\mbox{(subject to $\textstyle{\sum}s_{i}=0$)}\quad

by repeatedly blowing up the divisors
(x=z-s_{i}=0)
as in (
10
.3).

Let
\sigma_{j}
be the
j
th elementary symmetric polynomial in the
s_{i}
.
Then, over the Artin ring
k[s_{0},\dots,s_{n}]/(\sigma_{0},\dots,\sigma_{n})
, we get
a deformation of the minimal resolution which contracts to the
trivial deformation of
A_{n}
.

A similar result holds for all Du Val singularities by
[
Art74
]
,
and for M-resolutions by
[
BC94
]
.

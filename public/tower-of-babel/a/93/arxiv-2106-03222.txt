Abstract
. In a recent paper [ 15 ] , Hilbert space operators T T with the property that each sequence of the form { ‖ T n ​ h ‖ 2 } n = 0 ∞ \{\|T^{n}h\|^{2}\}_{n=0}^{\infty} is conditionally positive definite in a semigroup sense were introduced. In the present paper, this line of research is continued in depth in the case of unilateral weighted shifts. The conditional positive definiteness of unilateral weighted shifts is characterized in terms of formal moment sequences. The description of the representing triplet, the main object canonically associated with such operators, is provided. The backward extension problem for conditionally positive definite unilateral weighted shifts is solved, revealing a new feature that does not appear in the case of other operator classes. Finally, the flatness problem in this context is discussed, with an emphases on unexpected differences from the analogous problem for subnormal unilateral weighted shifts.

1.
Introduction

The celebrated Lambert’s criterion for
subnormality states that a bounded linear
operator
T
on a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is
subnormal if and only if for every
h\in\mathcal{H}
,
the sequence
\{\|T^{n}h\|^{2}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is
positive definite as a function on the additive
semigroup of all nonnegative integers
\mathbb{Z}_{+}

(see
[
17
]
, see also
[
21
, Theorem
7]
). In the harmonic analysis on

*
-semigroups presented in
[
4
]
,
related classes of functions appear that play
important role in various branches of
mathematics and probability theory. Among them,
the class of conditionally positive definite
functions prevails. In a recent paper

[
15
]
, a new class of operators,
called conditionally positive definite (CPD for
brevity), was introduced and studied in depth.
The operator
T
is said to be CPD if for every

h\in\mathcal{H}
, the sequence
\{\|T^{n}h\|^{2}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is conditionally
positive definite as a function on the semigroup

\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. The class of CPD operators contains in
particular subnormal operators
[
11
,
8
]
,
complete hypercontractions of order
2

[
7
]
,
3
-isometries

[
1
,
2
,
3
]
and many others.

In this paper we study CPD unilateral weighted
shifts with positive weights, the issue not
included in
[
15
]
. The study is
preceded by necessary preparations related to
selected properties of conditionally positive
definite sequences (see Section
2
).
The main characterization of CPD unilateral
weighted shifts given in Theorem
3.1
is
written in the spirit of the classical Berger
theorem on subnormal unilateral weighted shifts

[
10
,
12
]
. Namely, a unilateral
weighted shift
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
with weights

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is
subnormal if and only if the formal moment
sequence
\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
associated with
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

(see (
3.1
)) is a Stieltjes moment
sequence. In other words, there is a one-to-one
correspondence between subnormal unilateral
weighted shifts and (non-degenerate) Stieltjes
moment sequences. In turn, CPD unilateral
weighted shifts are in a one-to-one
correspondence with normalized CPD sequences of
exponential growth whose terms are all positive
and whose representing measures are supported in
the closed half-line
[0,\infty)
(see
Theorems
3.1
and
4.1
).

The essential difference between the above two
characterizations relies on the fact that
Stieltjes moment sequences have all terms
nonnegative, while CPD ones do not. For this
reason, we devoted one section for finding
necessary and sufficient conditions for
positivity of CPD sequences (see
Section
5
). It is worth emphasizing
that, unlike Stieltjes moment sequences which
are represented by single parameter (a positive
measure), CPD ones are represented by three
parameters coming from a Lévy-Khinchin type
formula. Note that the Berger theorem (see
Corollary
3.2
) can be derived from
Theorem
3.1
by using some scaling
results from
[
15
]
.

Another major difference between subnormal and CPD unilateral
weighted shifts is revealed when solving the backward
extension problem. Our solution given in Theorem
7.2

(see also Theorem
7.3
) is the basis for constructing
a CPD unilateral weighted shift with weights
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots)
for which the extended unilateral
weighted shift with weights
(t,\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots)
is CPD for any positive real number
t
(see
Example
7.7
). This differs from solving the
subnormal backward extension problem (see

[
9
, Proposition 8]
).

It was Stampfli who noticed that if two consecutive weights
of a subnormal unilateral weighted shift
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
are equal,
then all weights of
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
(except perhaps the first one)
are equal to each other (see
[
19
, Theorem 6]
). As
shown in Theorems
8.1
and
8.3
, this is
no longer true in the case of CPD unilateral weighted shifts.
Namely, the smallest number of consecutive equal weights is
four. If we allow the consecutive weights to be equal to one,
then the number decreases to
2
(see
Theorems
8.2
and
8.4
). The
corresponding counterexamples confirming the minimality of
these numbers are given in Examples
8.5
and

8.6
.

The main object canonically associated with CPD operators is
the (operator) representing triplet. The description of the
representing triplet for CPD unilateral weighted shifts is
provided in Theorem
6.1
. The question of when the
components of this triplet are compact operators is discussed
in detail in Propositions
6.2
,
6.3
and

6.4
, and Example
6.5
.

We refer the reader to the classical treatise on
weighted shifts
[
18
]
.

of sequences of positive real numbers with the
first term equal to
1
. The celebrated Berger
theorem states that a unilateral weighted shift

W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is subnormal if and only if the
corresponding sequence
\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is a
Stieltjes moment sequence (see

[
10
,
12
]
). In other words, the
transformation
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mapsto\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

is a one-to-one correspondence between the set
of weights of subnormal unilateral weighted
shifts and the set of non-degenerate Stieltjes
moment sequences with compactly supported
representing probability measures. We will show
that a similar effect occurs in the case of CPD
unilateral weighted shifts. Namely, the
transformation
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mapsto\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

gives a one-to-one correspondence between the
set of weights of CPD unilateral weighted shifts
and the set of normalized CPD sequences of
exponential growth with positive terms and with
representing measures supported in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(see
Theorem
3.1
, see also
Theorem
4.1
). The main difference
between these two characterizations is that the
terms of a non-degenerate Stieltjes moment
sequence are automatically positive (cf. (
2.12
)), while some terms of a CPD
sequence may not be. What is more, it is not
easy to determine CPD sequences with positive
weights. One of our goals in this paper is to
describe CPD sequences of exponential growth
with positive terms.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a bounded sequence of
positive real numbers. Then the following conditions are
equivalent:

(i)

the operator
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is CPD,

(ii)

there exist
b\in\mathbb{R}
,
c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

and a compactly supported finite Borel measure
\nu

on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\nu(\{1\})=0
and

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{n}=1+bn+cn^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\nu(x),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(3.5)

Moreover, the triplet
(b,c,\nu)
appearing in
(ii) is unique and

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(\nu)\subseteq\big[0,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\hat{\lambda}_{n}^{1/n}\big].

We call the triplet
(b,c,\nu)
appearing in
Theorem
3.1
(ii) the
scalar
representing triplet
of
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We begin by observing that since
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is bounded,
the sequence
\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is of exponential growth.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Apply (
3.2
) and
Theorem
2.5
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) It follows from
(
3.2

\displaystyle\|W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}h\|^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\langle h,e_{j}\rangle|^{2}\|W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}e_{j}\|^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\langle h,e_{j}\rangle|^{2}\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{n+j}}{\hat{\lambda}_{j}},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\,h\in\ell^{2}.

According to Theorem
2.3

for all

4.
Modelling CPD unilateral weighted shifts

It follows from Theorem
3.1
that if a
unilateral weighted shift
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is CPD, then
the sequence
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
has positive
real terms and takes the form (
3.5
).
Now we show the converse, namely if
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
is
a sequence of positive real numbers which is of
the form (
3.5
), then
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
, where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is a bounded
sequence of positive real numbers and so the
unilateral weighted shift
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is CPD.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of
real numbers such that

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}>0,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}=1+bn+cn^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

,

\mathbb{R}_{+}

such that
\nu(\{1\})=0

defined by

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(4.3)

is bounded and the unilateral weighted shift

is CPD. Moreover,

\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}

Proof.

In view of Theorem

and the obvious

\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}

to show that the sequence
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is bounded,
or equivalently that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}<\infty.

with the standard convention that

and

\displaystyle p(n)

\displaystyle=1+b(n+1)+n\nu(\mathbb{R}_{+})+c(n+1)^{2},

\displaystyle=1+bn+cn^{2}.

Secondly, we observe that according to (
2.2
),
(
2.4

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{p(n)+\max\{0,\vartheta\}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu}{q(n)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(4.5)

Thirdly, using (

), (

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{Q_{n}}{n}\mathrm{d}\nu=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{1-x}\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\quad\text{whenever $\vartheta\leqslant 1$.}

Case 1.

\vartheta>1

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{[\theta,\vartheta]}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{[\theta,\vartheta]}(x^{n-2}+2x^{n-3}+\ldots+(n-1)x^{0}\big)\mathrm{d}\nu(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{[\theta,\vartheta]}x^{n-2}\mathrm{d}\nu(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant\theta^{n-2}\nu([\theta,\vartheta]),\quad n\geqslant 2.

(4.7)

This in turn yields

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu}=0.

(4.8)

Dividing the numerator and denominator of the last
fraction in (
4.5
) by
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu
and using (
4.8

(
4.4
) holds.

Case 2.

c>0
.

One can show that there exists

\displaystyle 0<p(n)\leqslant\alpha q(n)\textrm{ for $n$ large enough.}

(4.9)

Hence by (

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{p(n)+\max\{0,\vartheta\}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu}{q(n)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu}\overset{\eqref{du-du-2}}{\leqslant}\max\{\alpha,\vartheta\},

).

Case 3.

\vartheta\leqslant 1
,

and

.

Since

we get

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{du-du-1}}{\leqslant}\frac{p(n)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu}{q(n)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu}=\frac{\frac{p(n)/n}{\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{Q_{n}}{n}\mathrm{d}\nu}+1}{\frac{q(n)/n}{\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{Q_{n}}{n}\mathrm{d}\nu}+1},\quad n\geqslant 2.

\vartheta\leqslant 1

We can argue as follows:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\frac{1}{n}+\frac{n+1}{n}b+\frac{n+1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{Q_{n+1}}{n+1}\mathrm{d}\nu}{\frac{1}{n}+b+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{Q_{n}}{n}\mathrm{d}\nu}

\displaystyle\hskip-4.30554pt\overset{\eqref{Eun-J}}{=}\frac{b+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{1-x}\mathrm{d}\nu(x)}{b+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{1-x}\mathrm{d}\nu(x)}=1.

(4.10)

Case 5.

\vartheta\leqslant 1
,
c=0
,

and

First, observe that

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{gl-odd2}}{=}1+n\Big(b+\int_{[0,1)}\frac{Q_{n}}{n}\mathrm{d}\nu\Big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{rnx-1}}{=}1+n\bigg(\Big(b+\int_{[0,1)}\frac{1}{1-x}\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\Big)-\int_{[0,1)}\frac{1-x^{n}}{n(1-x)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\bigg)

\displaystyle\hskip 4.30554pt=1-\int_{[0,1)}\frac{1-x^{n}}{(1-x)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\nu(x),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.11)

This implies that the sequence
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

is monotonically decreasing and so by (

),

for all

, which

∎

5.
Positivity of CPD sequences

Compared to Berger’s characterization of

6.

In this section we provide an explicit

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\overset{\eqref{self-map}}{=}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}}=\sqrt{\theta},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(8.14)

Since
1-u-Z=X=0
and
u\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we
infer from (
8.14
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}=\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\theta}\overset{\eqref{zurv}}{=}Z=1-u\leqslant 1.

(8.15)

Using (
8.13
), (
8.14
) and
(
8.15
), we verify that the measure
\mu

given by (
8.2
) satisfies (
3.7
),
so by Corollary
3.2
,
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is
subnormal and
\mu
is its Berger measure. This
completes the proof.
∎

If the weights in the sequence (
8.1
)
are equal to
1
, then we can reduce their
number to two (
\lambda_{0}
is still excluded).

Theorem 8.2
.

Suppose that
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is a CPD unilateral
weighted shift with weights

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and

\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
is such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\kappa}=\lambda_{\kappa+1}=1.

Then
\lambda_{0}\leqslant 1=\lambda_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is subnormal with the Berger
measure
\mu
given by

\displaystyle\mu=(1-\lambda_{0}^{2})\delta_{0}+\lambda_{0}^{2}\delta_{1}.

Proof.

We will modify the proof of
Theorem
8.1
. Set
\gamma_{n}=\hat{\lambda}_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Let
(b,c,\nu)
be
the scalar representing triplet of
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
.
First note that

\displaystyle\gamma^{(\kappa)}_{n}=\gamma_{\kappa},\quad n=0,1,2,

(8.16)

and thus

\displaystyle(\triangle^{2}\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\kappa)})_{0}=0.

This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}x^{\kappa}(\nu+2c\delta_{1})(\mathrm{d}x)\overset{\eqref{huru}}{=}\rho_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\overset{\eqref{trookj}}{=}(\triangle^{2}\boldsymbol{\gamma})^{(\kappa)}_{0}\overset{\eqref{fateu}}{=}(\triangle^{2}\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\kappa)})_{0}=0.

Since
\kappa\geqslant 1
, we deduce that
c=0
and

\mathrm{supp}(\nu)\subseteq\{0\}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}\overset{\eqref{wnezero}}{=}\big(1-\nu(\{0\})\big)+\widetilde{Y}n,\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(8.17)

where
\widetilde{Y}:=b+\nu(\{0\})
, and
consequently

\displaystyle\gamma_{\kappa}\overset{\eqref{kolynu}}{=}\gamma^{(\kappa)}_{n}\overset{\eqref{dua2}}{=}\widetilde{X}+\widetilde{Y}n,\quad n=0,1,2,

where
\widetilde{X}=1-\nu(\{0\})+\kappa\widetilde{Y}
. This implies that
\widetilde{Y}=0
and thus by (
8.17
), we get

\displaystyle 1-\nu(\{0\})=\gamma_{n}=\gamma_{1}=\lambda_{0}^{2},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore
\lambda_{0}\leqslant 1
and
\lambda_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}}=1
for all

n\in\mathbb{N}
. The remaining part of the proof
proceeds as in Theorem
8.1
.
∎

We now consider the case of the first four equal
weights, none of which is equal to
1
.

Theorem 8.3
.

Suppose that
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is a CPD unilateral
weighted shift with weights

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
such
that

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}\neq 1.

Then
\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is the
unilateral shift.

Proof.

We again modify the proof of
Theorem
8.1
. For the reader’s
convenience, we will point out the most
important differences. First, in
(
8.9
) we have
u=0
. Consequently,
the identity (
8.10
) takes the form

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}=X+Yn+Z\theta^{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Further differences appear in the formulas
(
8.13
) and (
8.14
), so now we have

\gamma_{n}=Z\theta^{n}
and
\lambda_{n}=\sqrt{\theta}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Finally, arguing as in the proof of
Theorem
8.2
, we obtain the variant
of this theorem for
\kappa=0
.

Theorem 8.4
.

Suppose that
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is a CPD unilateral
weighted shift with weights

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
such
that

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}=1.

Then
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is the unilateral shift.

We now show that the numbers of consecutive
equal weights appearing in
Theorems
8.1
,
8.2
,

8.3
and
8.4
are optimal. The
first counterexample concerns the case of three
consecutive equal weights. It is noteworthy that
if two consecutive weights of a subnormal
unilateral weighted shift are equal, all
weights, except the first, are equal (see

[
19
, Theorem 6]
). Thus, for subnormal
unilateral weighted shifts the optimal number of
consecutive equal weights is exactly
2
.

Example 8.5
.

Fix
\theta\in(1,\infty)\backslash\{3\}
and
define the triplet
(b,c,\nu)
by
b=\theta-1
,
c=0
and

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1}{2}(\theta-1)^{2}\big(\delta_{\frac{1}{3}\theta}+\delta_{\frac{5}{3}\theta}\big).

Let
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be the
CPD sequence given by (
4.2
). Clearly,
by (
2.2
),
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
satisfies
(
4.1
). Applying
Theorem
4.1
, we get a CPD unilateral
weighted shift
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
with weights
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
such that

\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}
. Straightforward
computations yield

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}=\theta^{n},\quad n=0,1,2,3,

(8.18)

and

\displaystyle\gamma_{4}=\frac{1}{9}\theta^{2}(13\theta^{2}-8\theta+4).

(8.19)

(Note that the expression on the right-hand side
of (
8.19
) is equal to
\theta^{4}
if and
only if
\theta=1
, which is not the case, so

\gamma_{4}\neq\theta^{4}
.) Hence, by
(
4.3
) and (
8.18
) we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\sqrt{\theta}\neq 1,

(8.20)

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}=\frac{\sqrt{13\theta^{2}-8\theta+4}}{3\sqrt{\theta}}\neq\sqrt{\theta}.

(8.21)

This shows that Theorem
8.3
is no
longer true if the number of consecutive equal
weights is decreased to
3
.

To cover the case
\kappa\geqslant 1
discussed in
Theorem
8.1
, we will use
Proposition
7.5
. With notation as in this
proposition, note that
n_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\infty
and

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}=g_{1}(\theta),

(8.22)

where
g_{1}\colon(1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is defined
by

\displaystyle g_{1}(x):=\frac{4x^{2}-8x+9}{5x},\quad x\in(1,\infty).

Since

\displaystyle g_{1}(x)-1=\frac{(x-1)(4x-9)}{5x},\quad x\in(1,\infty),

we see that

\displaystyle g_{1}(x)\geqslant 1,\quad x\in\Big[\frac{9}{4},\infty\Big).

(8.23)

Fix any
\theta\in\big[\frac{9}{4},\infty\big)\setminus\{3\}
. Using (
8.22
) and
(
8.23
) and applying Proposition
7.5

with
p=1
, we conclude that
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
has a CPD

\infty
-step backward extension. Summarizing,
we have proved that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists a sequence
\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq(0,\infty)
such that the unilateral
weighted shift
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(n)}}
with weights

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(n)}=(t_{n},\ldots,t_{1},\boxed{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}},\lambda_{3},\ldots),

is CPD. By (
8.20
), (
8.21
) and
Theorems
8.1
and
8.3
,

\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}\neq 1
and
t_{1}\neq\lambda_{0}
. This means that regardless of
the value of
\kappa\geqslant 1
,
Theorem
8.1
ceases to be true if
the number of consecutive equal weights is
decreased to
3
.

Applying Proposition
7.5
, now to any

p\geqslant 1
, and using computer simulations, one
can confirm that the above conclusion is true
for
\theta\in(1,\infty)\backslash\{3\}
.

\diamondsuit

The second counterexample shows that the number
of consecutive equal weights appearing in
Theorems
8.2
and
8.4
is
optimal. It is only interesting for
non-subnormal CPD unilateral weighted shifts
because the class of subnormal operators, unlike
CPD ones, is scalable (see

[
15
, Corollary 3.4.7]
).

Example 8.6
.

Let
\nu
be a compactly supported finite Borel
measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\nu(\{1\})=0
and
let
c\in(0,\infty)
. Set
b=-c
. Let

\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be the CPD
sequence defined by (
4.2
). Because of
(
2.2
),
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
satisfies
(
4.1
). Applying
Theorem
4.1
, we obtain a CPD
unilateral weighted shift
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
with weights

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
such
that
\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}
. Since
c>0
,
one can infer from (
4.3
) that

\lambda_{0}=1
and
\lambda_{1}\neq 1
, which
means that
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is not the unilateral shift.
In fact,
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is not subnormal because
c>0

(use Theorem
6.1
and
[
15
, Theorem
3.4.1]
). This shows that
Theorem
8.4
is no longer true if the
first weight is equal to
1
.

Finally, assuming additionally that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{x^{k}}\mathrm{d}\nu(x)<\infty

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and applying
Corollary
7.6
to the above unilateral
weighted shift
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
, we conclude that
regardless of the value of
\kappa\geqslant 1
,
Theorem
8.2
ceases to be true if

\lambda_{\kappa}=1
(cf. Example
8.5
).

\diamondsuit

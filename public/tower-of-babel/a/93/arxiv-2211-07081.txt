Abstract
. We study anti-symmetric solutions about the hyperplane { x n = 0 } \{x_{n}=0\} to the following fractional Hardy-Hénon system { ( − Δ ) s 1 u ( x ) = | x | α v p ( x ) , x ∈ ℝ + n , ( − Δ ) s 2 v ( x ) = | x | β u q ( x ) , x ∈ ℝ + n , u ( x ) ≥ 0 , v ( x ) ≥ 0 , x ∈ ℝ + n , \left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}u(x)=|x|^{\alpha}v^{p}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\ &(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}v(x)=|x|^{\beta}u^{q}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\ &u(x)\geq 0,\ \ v(x)\geq 0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\end{aligned}\right. where 0 < s 1 , s 2 < 1 0<s_{1},s_{2}<1 , n > 2 ​ max ⁡ { s 1 , s 2 } n>2\max\{s_{1},s_{2}\} . Nonexistence of anti-symmetric solutions are obtained in some appropriate domains of ( p , q ) (p,q) under some corresponding assumptions of α , β \alpha,\beta via the methods of moving spheres and moving planes. Particularly, for the case s 1 = s 2 s_{1}=s_{2} , one of our results shows that one domain of ( p , q ) (p,q) , where nonexistence of anti-symmetric solutions with appropriate decay conditions holds true, locates at above the fractional Sobolev’s hyperbola under appropriate condition of α , β \alpha,\beta .

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study anti-symmetric solutions about the hyperplane
\{x_{n}=0\}
to the following system involving fractional Laplacian

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}u(x)=|x|^{\alpha}v^{p}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
&(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}v(x)=|x|^{\beta}u^{q}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
&u(x)\geq 0,\ \ v(x)\geq 0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
&u(x^{\prime},x_{n})=-u(x^{\prime},-x_{n}),\ \ v(x^{\prime},x_{n})=-v(x^{\prime},-x_{n}),\ \ \ \ x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

where
s_{1},s_{2}\in(0,1)
,
n>\max\{2s_{1},2s_{2}\}
,
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|x_{n}>0\}
and
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1})
.

The fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}\,(0<s<1)
is a nonlocal operator defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=C(n,s)P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy,

where
P.V.
stands for the Cauchy principal value and
C(n,s)=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1-\cos\xi}{|\xi|^{n+2s}}d\xi\Big)^{-1}
(see
[
2
,
11
]
).
Let

L_{2s}=\Big\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|}{1+|x|^{n+2s}}dx<+\infty\Big\}.

Then for
u\in L_{2s},(-\Delta)^{s}u
can be defined in distributional sense (see
[
34
]
)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{s}u\varphi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi dx,\ \ \ \ \text{for any}\ \ \varphi\in\mathcal{S}.

Moreover,
(-\Delta)^{s}u
is well defined for
u\in L_{2s}\cap C_{loc}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
We call
(u,v)
a classical solution of (
1
) if
(u,v)\in(L_{2s_{1}}\cap C^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\times(L_{2s_{2}}\cap C^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and satisfies (
1
).

As well known, the method of moving planes and moving spheres play an important role in proving the nonexistence of solutions. Chen et al.
[
3
,
4
]
introduced direct method of moving planes and moving spheres for fractional Laplacian, which have been widely applied to derive the symmetry, monotonicity and nonexistence and even a prior estimates of solutions for some equations involving fractional Laplacian. In such process, some suitable forms of maximum principles are the key ingredients. The method of moving planes in integral forms is also a vital tool for classification of solutions (see
[
5
]
).

Recently, Li and Zhuo in
[
19
]
classified anti-symmetric classical solutions of Lane-Emden system (
1
) in the case of
s_{1}=s_{2}=:s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha=\beta=0
. They established the following Liouville type theorem.

Proposition 1.1
.

[
19
]

Given
0<p,q\leq\frac{n+2s}{n-2s},
assume that
(u,v)
is an anti-symmetric classical solution of
system (
1
). If
0<pq<1
or
p+2s>1
and
q+2s>1
, then
(u,v)\equiv(0,0)
.

As a corollary of Proposition 1, the nonexistence results in the larger space
L_{2s+1}
follows immediately for the case
p+2s>1
,
q+2s>1
.

Proposition 1.2
.

[
19
]

Assume that
u
and
v\in L_{2s+1}\cap C^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfy the system (
1
). Then if
0<p,q\leq\frac{n+2s}{n-2s}
,
p+2s>1
and
q+2s>1
,
(u,v)\equiv(0,0)
is the only solution.

The nonexistence of anti-symmetric classical solutions to the corresponding scalar problem were shown in
[
37
]
.

The following Hardy-Hénon system with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions has been investigated widely

(2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}u(x)=|x|^{\alpha}u^{p},\ \ u(x)\geq 0,\ \ &x\in\Omega,\\
&(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}v(x)=|x|^{\beta}v^{q},\ \ v(x)\geq 0,\ \ &x\in\Omega,\\
&u(x)=v(x)=0,\ \ \ \ &x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

There are enormous nonexistence results of (
2
) for the case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We list some main results as follows.

If
s_{1}=s_{2}=1
, for
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
, system (
2
) is the well-known Hénon-Lane-Emden system. It has been conjectured that the Sobolev’s hyperbola

\Big\{p>0,q>0:\frac{n+\alpha}{p+1}+\frac{n+\beta}{q+1}=n-2\Big\}

is the critical dividing curve between existence and nonexistence of solutions to (
2
). Particularly, the Hénon-Lane-Emden conjecture states that the system (
2
) admits no nonnegative non-trivial solutions if
p>0
,
q>0
and
\frac{n+\alpha}{p+1}+\frac{n+\beta}{q+1}>n-2
. For
\alpha=\beta=0
, this conjecture has been completely proved for radial solutions (see
[
23
,
32
]
). However, for non-radial solutions, the conjecture is only fully answered when
n\leq 4
(see
[
29
,
33
,
35
]
). In higher dimensions, the conjecture was partially solved. Felmer and Figueriredo
[
14
]
showed that the system (
2
) admits no classical positive solutions if

0<p,q\leq\frac{n+2}{n-2}\ \ \ \ \text{and }\ \ (p,q)\neq\Big(\frac{n+2}{n-2},\frac{n+2}{n-2}\Big).

Busca and Manásevich
[
1
]
proved the conjecture if

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\geq\frac{n-2}{2}\ \ \text{and}\ \ (\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\neq\Big(\frac{n-2}{2},\frac{n-2}{2}\Big),

where

\alpha_{1}=\frac{2(p+1)}{pq-1},\ \ \ \ \alpha_{2}=\frac{2(q+1)}{pq-1},\ \ pq>1.

When
\alpha,\beta>0
, Fazly and Ghoussoub
[
13
]
showed that the conjecture hold for dimension
n=3
under the assumption of the
boundness of positive solutions, Li and Zhang
[
21
]
removed this assumption and
proved this conjecture for dimension
n=3
. When
\min\{\alpha,\beta\}>-2
, the conjecture is proved for bounded solutions in
n=3
(see
[
27
]
).

If
s_{1}=s_{2}=:s\in(0,1),\alpha,\beta\geq 0
, there are fewer nonexistence results of solutions to system (
2
) in the case of
p>0
,
q>0
and
\frac{n+\alpha}{p+1}+\frac{n+\beta}{q+1}>n-2s
, namely the case that
(p,q)
locates at bottom left of the fractional Sobolev’s hyperbola

\Big\{p>0,q>0:\frac{n+\alpha}{p+1}+\frac{n+\beta}{q+1}=n-2s\Big\}
. For
\alpha=\beta=0
, Quass and Xia in
[
30
]
proved that there exist no classical positive solutions to (
2
) provided that

(3)

\alpha^{s}_{1},\alpha^{s}_{2}\in\Big[\frac{n-2s_{1}}{2},n-2s_{1}\Big),\ \ \ \ \text{and }\ \ (\alpha^{s}_{1},\alpha^{s}_{2})\neq\Big(\frac{n-2s}{2},\frac{n-2s}{2}\Big),

where

\alpha_{1}^{s}=\frac{2s(q+1)}{pq-1},\ \ \alpha_{2}^{s}=\frac{2s(p+1)}{pq-1},\ \ p,q>0,\ \ pq>1.

Note that the region (
3
) of
(p,q)
contains the following region

\Big\{(p,q):\frac{n}{n-2s}<p,q\leq\frac{n+2s}{n-2s},\ \ \text{and }\ \ (p,q)\neq\Big(\frac{n+2s}{n-2s},\frac{n+2s}{n-2s}\Big)\Big\}.

As
\min\{\alpha,\beta\}>-2s
, Peng
[
26
]
derived that the system (
2
) admit no nonnegative classical solutions if
0<p<\frac{n+2s+2\alpha}{n-2s}
and
0<q<\frac{n+2s+2\beta}{n-2s}
.

For scalar equation (i.e.,
s_{1}=s_{2}:=s,\alpha=\beta,p=q,u=v
), in the Laplacian case, if
\alpha=0
, a celebrated Liouville type theorem was showed by Gidas and Spruck
[
17
]
for
1<p<\frac{n+2}{n-2}
; if
\alpha\leq-2
and
p>1
, there is no any positive solution (see
[
16
,
25
]
); if
\alpha>-2
and
1<p<\frac{n+2+2\alpha}{n-2}
, Phan and Souplet
[
28
]
derived a Liouville theorem for bounded solutions; if
0<p\leq 1
, the nonexistence result was proved by Dai and Qin
[
8
]
for any
\alpha
. We also refer to
[
15
,
24
]
and references therein. For the scalar fractional Laplacian case, Chen, Li and Li
[
3
]
, Jin, Xiong and Li
[
18
]
proved the nonexistence results for
\alpha=0
and
0<p<\frac{n+2s}{n-2s}.
If
\alpha>-2s
, Dai and Qin
[
9
]
showed a Liouville type theorem for optimal range
0<p<\frac{n+2s+2\alpha}{n-2s}
.

For the case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, there are the following several main results.

If
s_{1}=s_{2}=1
,
\alpha=\beta=0
,
\min\{p,q\}>1
, a Liouville Theorem is proved for bounded solutions by Chen, Lin and Zou
[
6
]
. If
s_{1}=s_{2}=:s\in(0,1)
, for
\alpha=\beta=0
, the nonexistence of positive viscosity bounded solutions to system (
2
) was showed by Quaas and Xia in
[
31
]
. For
\alpha,\beta>-2s
, if
p\geq\frac{n+2s+\alpha}{n-2s}
and
q\geq\frac{n+2s+\beta}{n-2s}
, Duong and Le
[
12
]
obtained the nonexistence of solutions satisfying the following decay at infinity

u(x)=\mbox{o}(|x|^{-\frac{4s+\beta}{q-1}})\ \ \text{and}\ \ v(x)=\mbox{o}(|x|^{-\frac{4s+\alpha}{p-1}}).

For general
s_{1},s_{2}\in(0,1)
, Le in
[
22
]
concluded a Liouville type theorem. Precisely, they obtained that if
1\leq p\leq\frac{n+2s_{1}+2\alpha}{n-2s_{2}}
,
1\leq q\leq\frac{n+2s_{2}+2\alpha}{n-2s_{1}}
and
(p,q)\neq(\frac{n+2s_{1}+2\alpha}{n-2s_{2}},\frac{n+2s_{2}+2\alpha}{n-2s_{1}}
),
\alpha>-2s_{1}
and
\beta>-2s_{2}
, then
(u,v)\equiv(0,0)
is the only nonnegative classical solutions to system (
2
). More nonexistence results for general nonlinearities in a half space can be seen in
[
10
,
36
]
.
For the corresponding scalar problem of (
2
) with Laplacian and
\alpha=\beta=0
, Gidas and Spruck
[
17
]
obtained the nonexistence of nontrivial nonnegative classical solution of (
2
) for
1<p\leq\frac{n+2}{n-2}
. For the corresponding scalar problem (
2
) with fractional Laplacian and
\alpha=\beta=0
, Chen, Li and Li
[
3
]
showed that
u\equiv 0
is the only nonnegative solution to (
2
) for
1<p\leq\frac{n+2s}{n-2s}
. Recently a Liouville type theorem of the corresponding scalar problem for
1<p<\frac{n+2s+2\alpha}{n-2s},\alpha>-2s
and
s\in(0,1]
was established by Dai and Qin in
[
9
]
.

In this paper we will study nonexistence of anti-symmetric classical solutions to the system (
1
) for general
\alpha,\beta,p,q
.

For
\alpha>-2s_{1}
and
\beta>-2s_{2}
, we denote

\mathcal{R}_{sub}:=\Big\{(p,q)|0<p\leq\frac{n+2s_{1}+2\alpha}{n-2s_{2}},0<q\leq\frac{n+2s_{2}+2\beta}{n-2s_{1}},(p,q)\neq\Big(\frac{n+2s_{1}+2\alpha}{n-2s_{2}},\frac{n+2s_{2}+2\beta}{n-2s_{1}}\Big)\Big\}.

Note that for the case
s_{1}=s_{2}
, the set
\mathcal{R}_{sub}
locates at bottom left of the preceding fractional Sobolev’s hyperbola.

Throughout this paper, we always assume
s_{1},s_{2}\in(0,1)
,
n>\max\{2s_{1},2s_{2}\}
.
and use
C
to denote a general positive constant whose value may vary from line to line even the same line.
Our main results are as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
(p,q)\in\mathcal{R}_{sub}
, assume that
(u,v)
is a classical solution of the system (
1
). For either one of the following two cases

(i)
\min\{p+2s_{1},p+2s_{1}+\alpha\}>1
and
\min\{q+2s_{2},q+2s_{2}+\beta\}>1
,

(ii)
0<pq<1
with
\alpha\geq-2s_{1}pq
,
\beta\geq-2s_{2}pq
,

we have that
(u,v)=(0,0)
.

The nonexistence results (i) of Theorem 1 can be extended to a larger space.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
(p,q)\in\mathcal{R}_{sub}
and
(u,v)\in(L_{2s_{1}+1}\cap C^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\times(L_{2s_{2}+1}\cap C^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
satisfies the system (
1
). Then for the case that
\min\{p+2s_{1},p+2s_{1}+\alpha\}>1
and
\min\{q+2s_{2},q+2s_{2}+\beta\}>1
,
(u,v)\equiv(0,0)
is the only solution.

Combining our anti-symmetric property, in the process of Theorem
1.1
, we only utilized the extended spaces
L_{2s_{1}+1},L_{2s_{2}+1}
instead of the usual spaces
L_{2s_{1}},L_{2s_{2}}
in the case that
\min\{p+2s_{1},p+2s_{1}+\alpha\}>1
and
\min\{q+2s_{2},q+2s_{2}+\beta\}>1
. One can see that Theorem
1.2
is a direct corollary of (i) of Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 1.1
.

Our results of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are the extension to general
s_{1},s_{2},\alpha,\beta
of the nonexistence results of Li and Zhuo
[
19
]
(see preceding Propositions
1.1
and
1.2
) except one critical point of
(p,q)
.

Remark 1.2
.

When
s_{1}=s_{2}
,
p=q
,
\alpha=\beta
and
u=v
, the results of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are the nonexistence of nontrivial classical solutions to the corresponding scalar problem.

Under appropriate decay conditions of
u
and
v
at infinity, we can extend the nonexistence result of classical solutions of (
1
) to an unbounded domain of
(p,q)
. Particularly, this unbounded domain, except at most a bounded sub-domain, locates at above the preceding fractional Sobolev’s hyperbola for the case
s_{1}=s_{2}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
p\geq\frac{n+2s_{1}+\alpha}{n-2s_{2}}
,
q\geq\frac{n+2s_{2}+\beta}{n-2s_{1}}
,
\alpha>-2s_{2}
,
\beta>-2s_{1}
. Assume
(u,v)
is a classical solution of system (
1
) satisfying

\mathop{\overline{\lim}}\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{u(x)}{|x|^{a}}\leq C\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathop{\overline{\lim}}\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{v(x)}{|x|^{b}}\leq C,

for some
C>0
, where
a=-\frac{2s_{1}+2s_{2}+\beta}{q-1}
and
b=-\frac{2s_{1}+2s_{2}+\alpha}{p-1}
. Then
(u,v)\equiv(0,0)
.

Remark 1.3
.

The results of Theorem
1.3
are new even if for the corresponding scalar problem with
\alpha=0
.

Remark 1.4
.

When
\alpha,\beta
are positive, define the region

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{sup}:=

\displaystyle\left\{(p,q)|\frac{n+2s_{1}+\alpha}{n-2s_{2}}\leq p\leq\frac{n+2s_{1}+2\alpha}{n-2s_{2}},\frac{n+2s_{2}+\beta}{n-2s_{1}}\leq p\leq\frac{n+2s_{2}+2\beta}{n-2s_{1}},\right.

\displaystyle\left.(p,q)\neq\left(\frac{n+2s_{1}+2\alpha}{n-2s_{2}},\frac{n+2s_{2}+2\beta}{n-2s_{1}}\right)\right\}.

Note that
\mathcal{R}_{sup}
is contained in the nonexistence region of
(p,q)
obtained in Theorem
1.1
. Hence, if
(p,q)\in\mathcal{R}_{sup}
, Theorem
1.1
tells us that the results of Theorem
1.3
still hold true without the decay conditions.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce and prove some necessary lemmas.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
12
]
)
Let
s\in(0,1)
and
w(y)\in L_{2s}\cap C_{loc}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfy

w(y)=-w(y^{-}_{\lambda})
, where
y^{-}_{\lambda}=(y^{\prime},2\lambda-y_{n})
for any real number
\lambda
. Assume there exists
x\in\Sigma_{\lambda}
such that

w(x)=\mathop{\inf}\limits_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}w(y)<0\ \ \text{ and }\ \ (-\Delta)^{s}w(x)+c(x)w(x)\geq 0,

where
\Sigma_{\lambda}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|x_{n}<\lambda\}
. Then we have the following claims

(i) if

\mathop{\lim\inf}\limits_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{2s}c(x)\geq 0,

there exists a constant
R_{0}>0
(depending on c, but independent of
w
) such that

|x|<R_{0}.

(ii) if
c
is bounded below in
\Sigma_{\lambda}
, there exists a constant
\ell>0
(depending on the lower bound of
c
, but independent of
w
) such that

d(x,T_{\lambda})>\ell,

where
T_{\lambda}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|x_{n}=\lambda\}.

We want to point out that the constant
\ell
is non-increasing about
\lambda
, since
\ell
is non-decreasing about the lower bound of
c
,
which can be seen from the proof of Proposition
2.1
in
[
12
]
.

In order to apply the method of moving planes to prove the nonexistence, we need to establish the following estimate.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
w(y)\in L_{2s}\cap C_{loc}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfy
w(y)=-w(y^{-}_{\lambda})
. Assume there exists
x\in\Sigma_{\lambda}
such that

w(x)=\mathop{\inf}\limits_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}w(y)<0
. Then we have

(-\Delta)^{s}w(x)\leq C(n,s)\Big[w(x)d^{-2s}+\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(w(x)-w(y))\Big(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}-\frac{1}{|x-y^{-}_{\lambda}|^{n+2s}}\Big)dy\Big],

where
d=d(x,T_{\lambda})
and the constant
C(n,s)
is positive.

Proof.

Applying the definition of fractional Laplacian, we have

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}w(x)

\displaystyle=C(n,s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy

\displaystyle=C(n,s)\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy+C(n,s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy

\displaystyle=C(n,s)\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy+C(n,s)\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\frac{w(x)+w(y)}{|x-y^{-}_{\lambda}|^{n+2s}}dy

(4)

\displaystyle=C(n,s)\Big[\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(w(x)-w(y))\Big(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}-\frac{1}{|x-y^{-}_{\lambda}|^{n+2s}}\Big)dy+\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\frac{2w(x)}{|x-y^{-}_{\lambda}|^{n+2s}}dy\Big].

By an elementary calculation (see
[
7
]
), we derive

\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\frac{2w(x)}{|x-y^{-}_{\lambda}|^{n+2s}}dy\cong C(n,s)w(x)d^{-2s}.

Hence, combining this and (
2
), we complete the proof of Lemma
2.1
.∎

In order to apply the method of moving spheres to prove the nonexistence, we need to establish a similar estimate as that of Lemma
2.1
. To this end, we need introduce some notations. For any real number
\lambda>0
, we denote

S_{\lambda}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,|x|=\lambda\},

B_{\lambda}^{+}=B_{\lambda}^{+}(0)=\{|x|<\lambda\,|\,x_{n}>0\}.

Let
x^{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda^{2}x}{|x|^{2}}
be the inversion of the point
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
about the sphere
S_{\lambda}
and
x^{*}=(x^{\prime},-x_{n})
. Denote

B_{\lambda}^{-}=\{x|x^{*}\in B_{\lambda}^{+}\},\ \ (B_{\lambda}^{+})^{C}=\{x|x^{\lambda}\in B_{\lambda}^{+}\},\ \ (B_{\lambda}^{-})^{C}=\{x|x^{\lambda}\in B_{\lambda}^{-}\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
w(x)\in L_{2s}\cap C_{loc}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
satisfy

(5)

w(x)=-w(x^{*})\text{ and }w(x)=-\big(\frac{\lambda}{|x|}\big)^{n-2s}w(x^{\lambda}),\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

Assume there exists
\tilde{x}\in B_{\lambda}^{+}
such that
w(\tilde{x})=\inf_{B_{\lambda}^{+}}w(x)<0
. Then we have

(-\Delta)^{s}w(\tilde{x})\leq C(n,s)\Big[w(\tilde{x})\Big((\lambda-|\tilde{x}|)^{-2s}+\frac{\delta^{n}}{\tilde{x}_{n}^{n+2s}}\Big)+\int_{B_{\lambda}^{+}}(w(\tilde{x})-w(y))h_{\lambda}(\tilde{x},y)dy\Big],

where

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

. Suppose that
(u,v)
is a nonnegative classical solution to the following system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}u(x)=|x|^{\alpha}v^{p}(x),\ \,u(x)\geq 0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
&(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}v(x)=|x|^{\beta}u^{q}(x),\ \,v(x)\geq 0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
&u(x)=-u(x^{*}),\ \ v(x)=-v(x^{*}),\ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u(x)\geq C\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2s_{1}}}-\frac{1}{|x^{*}-y|^{n-2s_{1}}}\Big)|y|^{\alpha}v^{p}(y)dy,\\
&v(x)\geq C\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2s_{2}}}-\frac{1}{|x^{*}-y|^{n-2s_{2}}}\Big)|y|^{\beta}u^{p}(y)dy,\end{aligned}\right.

where

is a positive constant.

Proof

Define a cut off function

\eta(x)=1

v_{R}(x)=C\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2s_{2}}}-\frac{1}{|x^{*}-y|^{n-2s_{2}}}\Big)\eta_{R}(y)|y|^{\beta}u^{q}(y)dy.

Note that
(u_{R}(x),v_{R}(x))
is a solution to the following system

(11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}u_{R}(x)=\eta_{R}(x)|x|^{\alpha}v^{p}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
&(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}v_{R}(x)=\eta_{R}(x)|x|^{\beta}u^{q}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
&u_{R}(x)=-u_{R}(x^{*}),\ \ v_{R}(x)=-v_{R}(x^{*}),\ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Let

and
V_{R}(x)=v(x)-v_{R}(x)
. From (
10
) and (

), we derive

(12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}U_{R}(x)=|x|^{\alpha}v^{p}(x)-\eta_{R}(x)|x|^{\alpha}v^{p}(x)\geq 0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
&(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}V_{R}(x)=|x|^{\beta}u^{q}(x)-\eta_{R}(x)|x|^{\beta}u^{q}(x)\geq 0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
&U_{R}(x)=-U_{R}(x^{*}),\ \ V_{R}(x)=-V_{R}(x^{*}),\ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

By the definitions of
U_{R}(x)
and
V_{R}(x)

\mathop{\varliminf}\limits_{|x|\to\infty}U_{R}(x)\geq 0\text{ and }\mathop{\varliminf}\limits_{|x|\to\infty}V_{R}(x)\geq 0,

where we used the assumptions

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}U_{R}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=C(n,s_{1})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{U_{R}(\hat{x})-U_{R}(y)}{|\hat{x}-y|^{n+2s_{1}}}dy

\displaystyle=C(n,s_{1})\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\frac{U_{R}(\hat{x})-U_{R}(y)}{|\hat{x}-y|^{n+2s_{1}}}dy+C(n,s_{1})\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\frac{U_{R}(\hat{x})+U_{R}(y)}{|\hat{x}-y^{*}|^{n+2s_{1}}}dy

\displaystyle=C(n,s_{1})\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}(U_{R}(\hat{x})-U_{R}(y))\Big(\frac{1}{|\hat{x}-y|^{n+2s_{1}}}-\frac{1}{|\hat{x}-y^{*}|^{n+2s_{1}}}\Big)+\frac{2U_{R}(\hat{x})}{|\hat{x}-y^{*}|^{n+2s_{1}}}\Big]dy

\displaystyle<0.

Similarly, one has

,

(57)

\bar{U}_{\rho}(x)\geq 0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \bar{V}_{\rho}(x)\geq 0,\ \ x\in\Sigma_{\rho},

where
\varepsilon
is determined later.

If (
57
) is not true, then for any
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
, there exists
\rho_{k}\in(\rho_{0},\rho_{0}+\varepsilon_{k})
and
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\cap\Sigma_{\rho_{k}}
such that

(58)

\bar{U}_{\rho_{k}}(x_{k})=\inf_{\Sigma_{\rho_{k}}}\{\bar{U}_{\rho_{k}}(x),\bar{V}_{\rho_{k}}(x)\}<0.

Similar argument as that of (
53
) gives that

(59)

(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}\bar{U}_{\rho_{k}}(x_{k})+c(x_{k})\bar{U}_{\rho_{k}}(x_{k})\geq 0,

where
c(x)=-|x|^{-\bar{\tau}_{1}}p\bar{v}^{p-1}(x)
. From (
49
) and the decay conditions of
u
and
v
, we deduce that

(60)

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{x\to\infty}|x|^{2s_{1}}c(x)=0\text{ and }c(x)\text{ is bounded below in }\Sigma_{\rho_{k}},

where we used the assumption
\alpha>-2s_{2}
. Then from Proposition
2.1
we know that there exists
\ell_{k}>0
and
R_{0}>0
such that

(61)

x_{k}\in B_{R_{0}}(0)\cap\Sigma_{\rho_{k}-\ell_{k}}.

Denote
\ell_{0}(>0)
as the constant given in Proposition
2.1
corresponding to the half space
\Sigma_{\rho_{0}+1}
.
Combining the remark about the monotonicity of
\ell
with respect to
\lambda
below Proposition
2.1
, (
61
) and the fact that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
, we have that

(62)

x_{k}\in B_{R_{0}}(0)\cap\Sigma_{\rho_{0}-\frac{\ell_{0}}{2}}.

If
\rho_{0}-\frac{\ell_{0}}{2}\leq 0
, then (
62
) contradicts with the fact that
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. If
\rho_{0}-\frac{\ell_{0}}{2}>0
, due to (
56
) and continuity of
\bar{u}
, we know that there exists
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\frac{\ell_{0}}{2})
such that for any
\varepsilon_{k}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
and
\rho\in(\rho_{0},\rho_{0}+\varepsilon_{k})
,

\bar{U}_{\rho}(x)\geq 0,\ \ \ \ x\in\overline{B_{R_{0}}(0)\cap\Sigma_{\rho_{0}-\frac{\ell_{0}}{2}}}.

This contradicts with (
62
) and (
58
). Hence, we derive that for any
\rho\in(\rho_{0},\rho_{0}+\varepsilon^{\prime})
with
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
small enough,

\bar{U}_{\rho}(x)\geq 0,\ \ x\in\Sigma_{\rho}.

Similarly, we may verify that there exists
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}>0
such that for any
\rho\in(\rho_{0},\rho_{0}+\varepsilon^{\prime\prime})
the inequality holds

\bar{V}_{\rho}(x)\geq 0,\ \ x\in\Sigma_{\rho}.

Let
\varepsilon=\min\{\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}\}
, then (
57
) follows immediately and hence (
55
) holds, which yields that
\rho_{0}=+\infty
.

The result
\rho_{0}=+\infty
indicates that both
\bar{u}(x)
and
\bar{v}(x)
are monotone increasing along the
x_{n}
-axis. This contradicts with the asymptotic behaviors (
49
). Therefore,
(\bar{u},\bar{v})=(0,0)
, which yields that
(u,v)=(0,0)
.
We complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Dual process in the two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet diffusion
Robert C. Griffiths, Monash University

Abstract




The two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet diffusion takes values in the infinite ordered simplex and extends the celebrated infinitely-many-neutral-alleles model, having a two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet stationary distribution.
Here we identify a dual process for this diffusion and obtain its transition probabilities. The dual is shown to be given by Kingman’s coalescent with mutation, conditional on a given configuration of leaves. Interestingly, the dual depends on the additional parameter of the stationary distribution only through the test functions and not through the transition rates. After discussing the sampling probabilities of a two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet partition drawn conditionally on another partition, we use these notions together with the dual process to derive the transition density of the diffusion. Our derivation provides a new probabilistic proof of this result, leveraging on an extension of Pitman’s Pólya urn scheme, whereby the urn is split after a finite number of steps and two urns are run independently onwards. The proof strategy exemplifies the power of duality and could be exported to other models where a dual is available.

Keywords
:
Pólya urn;
Kingman’s coalescent;
lines of descent;
Pitman sampling formula;
transition density.

Here we relate the dual process of (

In particular, the dual transition rates still depend only on the parameter

This fact may somewhat be surprising. Indeed, it is well-known that a Kingman’s coalescent tree with
n
leaves can be realised by sampling lineages sequentially, starting from the root, according to the Blackwell–MacQueen Pólya urn scheme, i.e., (
2
) with
\alpha=0

2
) and the stationary distribution of the two-parameter diffusion, it would thus be natural to expect that
\alpha
plays a role in the law of the dual process. In fact, this parameter has been shown to have a key role in the construction of the diffusion
[

,
12
]
or of its labelled counterpart
[
33
]
. One would then expect
\alpha
to influence the deletion of groups both through mutation and coalescence. Our results instead indicate that these two effects of

on the block-counting rates balance each other out perfectly, giving a zero net effect.
A possible explanation is related to the fact that the dual process describes the
conditional
genealogy of a sample given its allelic partition. As an effect of exchangeability, conditionally on the frequencies, the parameters only contribute to the law of the coalescent’s
block-counting process
, keeping track of the total number of surviving lineages, regardless of their types.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we collect some notation together with some necessary preliminary notions. In Section
3
we fully characterize the dual process of the two-parameter diffusion. In Section
4
we discuss the sampling distributions related to drawing a partition from the two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet model conditional on another, already observed, partition. These distributions, together with the dual process, are then used in Section
5
where we derive the transition density of the two-parameter diffusion using the identified dual. This transition density was found in
[
29
]
through analytical tools.
Our derivation uses instead a probabilistic approach, which exploits a “split”version of Pitman’s two-parameter urn scheme (
2
), namely is a system of two such urns that grow conditionally independently given the same vector of initial observations. The split urns give, in the limit, a bivariate distribution with identical
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}
marginal laws, whereby the size of the common initial sample measures the strength of the dependence between the two coordinates. It turns out that the transition density of the two-parameter diffusion can be interpreted as the conditional distribution of one urn composition given the other in such a construction, where the size of the initial sample is
random
and its distribution is given by the block-counting process of the dual.
Our probabilistic derivation of the transition density therefore exemplifies the power of duality and could be reproduced, at least in principle, to identify the transition density for models when this is unknown, but a dual is nonetheless available, e.g., for coupled Wright–Fisher diffusions
[
23
]
.

3
The dual process

In this section we fully characterize a dual process for the two-parameter diffusion induced by symmetric monomial functions. This is shown to be a pure-death continuous-time Markov chain on integer partitions which coincides with the process counting the number of surviving lineages of each type in a Kingman’s coalescent tree, going backward in time, conditional on a starting unlabelled partition of “leaves”. \alpha

g_{\eta}(x)
as a function of
\eta
, it is plain that
A_{\theta}
defines a pure-death process
\mathcal{D}_{t}
with rates as in the claim. Note that when
|\eta|=1
the rate is null, as
g_{\eta}\propto 1
and
L_{\alpha,\theta}g_{\eta}=0
by Lemma
3.2
.
Now the fact that
L_{\alpha,\theta}g_{\eta}(x)=A_{\theta}g_{\eta}(x)
implies (
14
) follows from Corollary 4.4.13 in
[
21
]
in light of the boundedness of
g_{\eta}(x)
(cf. also Proposition 1.2 in
[
48
]
).
∎

Here
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is the process describing the group sizes in Kingman’s coalescent with mutation at time
t
. The transition rates only depend on the parameter
\theta
as in the one-parameter model, while the dependence on the second parameter
\alpha
is only through (
11
).

The next proposition identifies the transition function of the dual. To this end, let

(16)

D_{t}:=|\mathcal{D}_{t}|

be a death process on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
that counts the number of groups in
\mathcal{D}_{t}
. This jumps from
n
to
n-1
at rate
\lambda_{n}
as in (
12
), and it is well known
[
39
,
67
]
that its transition probabilities are

(17)

d_{nl}^{\theta}(t)=\sum_{k=l}^{n}e^{-\lambda_{k}t}(-1)^{k-l}\frac{(2k+\theta-1)(l+\theta)_{(k-1)}}{l!(k-l)!}\frac{n_{[k]}}{(\theta+n)_{(k)}},\quad 1\leq l\leq n,

where
a_{[k]}=a(a-1)\cdots(a-k+1)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{[0]}=1
, and
d_{n0}^{\theta}(t)=1-\sum_{l\geq 1}d_{nl}^{\theta}(t)
. Note here that
\lambda_{k}>0
for
k\geq 2
even when
-1<\theta\leq 0
, and so
d_{nl}^{\theta}(t)>0
for
2\leq l\leq n
.

In the limit when
n\rightarrow\infty
, the last factor on the right in (
17
) is replaced by 1, yielding

(18)

d_{l}^{\theta}(t)=\sum_{k\geq l}e^{-\lambda_{k}t}(-1)^{k-l}\frac{(2k+\theta-1)(l+\theta)_{(k-1)}}{l!(k-l)!},\quad l\geq 1.

See, e.g.,
[
40
]
.
This process first appeared in
[
39
]
, and counts the number of non-mutant edges back in time from the leaves towards the root in a Kingman’s coalescent tree, when the mutation rate is
\theta/2\geq 0
along edges of the tree. It is also commonly known as
block-counting process
of Kingman’s coalescent with mutation. Moreover, it indexes a mixture expansion for the transition function of the Fleming–Viot process with parent independent mutation, a measure-valued (labelled) version of (
1
) when
\alpha=0
. See
[
19
]
, Theorem 1.1. In our setting, we show it plays a key role in the transition function expansion of the two-parameter diffusion, a key argument of which is given by the next proposition.

Define the hypergeometric probabilities

(19)

{\cal H}(\omega|\eta)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\cdots\leq i_{\ell(\omega)}}\ \sum_{(\kappa_{(1)},\ldots,\kappa_{(\ell(\omega))})=\omega}\frac{{\eta_{i_{1}}\choose{\kappa_{1}}}\cdots{\eta_{i_{\ell(\omega)}}\choose{\kappa_{i_{\ell(\omega)}}}}}{{|\eta|\choose|\omega|}},\quad\quad\omega\subset\eta.

Here
{\cal H}(\omega|\eta)
is the probability of obtaining an unlabelled colour configuration
\omega
when sampling
|\omega|
balls without replacement uniformly from an urn containing a colour configuration
\eta
.
The
|\omega|
balls may be sampled all at once or one by one.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{t}
be as in Theorem
3.3
. Then its transition probabilities are

(20)

q^{\theta}_{\eta\omega}(t):=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{D}_{t}=\omega|\mathcal{D}_{0}=\eta)={\cal H}(\omega|\eta)d^{\theta}_{|\eta||\omega|}(t),\quad\quad\omega\subset\eta,\quad\quad|\eta|>1,

and zero otherwise, with

d^{\theta}_{nm}(t)
for
n\geq m\geq 1
as in (
17
). In particular,
\eta=(1)
is an absorbing state.

Proof.

Let
n=|\eta|
. The process
{\cal D}_{t}
in Theorem
3.3
has overall rates
\lambda_{n}
and the embedded chain has transition probabilities

(21)

p^{\downarrow}(\eta,\eta^{\prime})=\frac{{n-1\choose\eta^{\prime}}\chi(\eta^{\prime},\eta)}{{n\choose\eta}}=a_{\eta_{i}}(\eta)\frac{\eta_{i}}{n},\quad\quad\eta_{i}^{\prime}=\eta_{i}-1,\ \eta_{j}^{\prime}=\eta_{j},\ j\neq i.

It is easy to realize the transition probability (
21
) by removing uniformly at random one ball after another, without replacement, from an urn.
Let
\eta
be the initial color frequency of balls in the urn. If one ball is randomly deleted from from the urn, then the probability that the remaining balls have frequency
\eta^{\prime}
is

a_{\eta_{i}}(\eta)\frac{\eta_{i}}{n}=p^{\downarrow}(\eta,\eta^{\prime})
. After
n-m
deletions a sample of size
m
with colour configuration
\omega
is obtained with probability

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega=\omega_{n-m}\subset\cdots\subset\omega_{1}\subset\omega_{0}=\eta}p^{\downarrow}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})p^{\downarrow}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\cdots p^{\downarrow}(\omega_{n-m-1},\omega_{n-m})

\displaystyle=\frac{{n-m\choose\omega}}{{n\choose\eta}}\sum_{\omega=\omega_{n-m}\subset\cdots\subset\omega_{1}\subset\omega_{0}=\eta}\chi(\omega,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{{n-m\choose\omega}}{{n\choose\eta}}\text{dim}(\omega,\eta),

where

\text{dim}(\omega,\eta):=\sum_{\omega=\omega_{n-m}\subset\cdots\subset\omega_{1}\subset\omega_{0}=\lambda}\chi(\omega,\eta).

After
n
deletions there will be an empty color frequency
\omega=\emptyset
.
Denote
\text{dim}(\eta)=\text{dim}(\emptyset,\eta)
and note that
\text{dim}(\eta)={n\choose\eta}
in agreement with

1={n\choose\eta}^{-1}{0\choose\emptyset}\text{dim}(\emptyset,\eta)
.
Sampling
m
balls without replacement is equivalent to the above deletion process, and the sampling probability is
{\cal H}(\omega|\eta)
as in (
19
). Thus

\displaystyle q_{\eta\omega}^{\theta}(t)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{D}_{t}=\omega)=\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{D}_{t}=\omega|D_{t}=|\omega|)\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(D_{t}=|\omega|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathcal{D}_{t}=\omega|D_{t}=|\omega|)d_{|\eta||\omega|}^{\theta}(t)={\cal H}(\omega|\eta)d_{|\eta||\omega|}(t).

Finally, the fact that
\eta=(1)
is absorbing follows immediately from Theorem
3.3
through the fact that
L_{\alpha,\theta}{P}_{\eta}=0
when
\eta=(1)

in Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Note that Proposition
3.4
accommodates the full range of values for
\theta>-\alpha
, where
0\leq\alpha<1
. In fact, even when
-1<\theta<0
, the transition probabilities in (
20
) are positive for any
\eta
such that
|\eta|>1
, through (
13
) and specifically the fact that
\lambda_{n}>0
for all
n>1
; cf. (
12
). The fact that formally
\lambda_{1}<0
when
-1<\theta<0
is immaterial, as the dual process is absorbed in
\eta=(1)
.

4
Two-parameter conditional partition structures

In order to derive the transition density of the two-parameter diffusion through duality, we first need to explore in some detail the conditional distribution of a partition
\eta
, generated from the two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet model (e.g., through (
2
)), conditional on having already observed a subset partition
\omega\subset\eta
. This can be done by appealing to the following generalized Pólya urn scheme.

Let
\ell=|\omega|
, and suppose an urn contains a single white ball and
\ell
non-white balls, whose colours are denoted by
Y^{\ell}=(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{\ell})
, with
r\leq\ell
different colours.
Define
\pi:Y^{\ell}\to\omega
to be the function that maps the sample
Y^{\ell}
into the partition
\omega=(\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{r})\in\Gamma_{\ell}
, determined by the equivalence relation on colours, whereby
Y_{i},Y_{j}
are in the same group if
Y_{i}=Y_{j}
. For every group
\omega_{j}
, one ball is assigned mass
1-\alpha
and the remaining
\omega_{j}-1
balls are assigned mass
1
. The single white ball in the urn is assigned mass
\theta+\alpha r
. Balls are then drawn sequentially as follows. If the white ball is drawn, a ball of a new colour is added to the urn and assigned mass
1-\alpha
, and the mass of the white ball is increased by
\alpha
. If a coloured ball is drawn, it is replaced in the urn together with an additional ball of mass
1
of the same colour. This urn model is an extension of (
2
) and
[
45
]
.

When the total number of balls in the urn is
n
, denote the colours of the
n-\ell
new balls by

X^{n-\ell}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-\ell})
. Let the colour configuration be denoted by
\pi(X^{n-\ell})
for the new sample and
\pi(X^{n-\ell},Y^{\ell})
for the combined sample. We are interested in the distribution of these colour configurations conditional on
Y^{\ell}
. To this end, denote by
\text{Dir}(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r})
a Dirichlet distribution with parameters
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r})
on the
(r-1)
-dimensional simplex, and let a direct sum notation
\oplus
indicate an accumulation of the points of two point processes. Finally, denote by
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}
a
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}
distribution conditional on having observed the partition
\omega
, and by
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}
the corresponding expectation.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\omega\in\Gamma_{\ell},\eta\in\Gamma_{n}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-\ell}
. Then
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}
equals in distribution the descending arrangement of

(22)

Z_{\ell,r}\mathrm{Dir}(\omega_{1}-\alpha,\ldots,\omega_{r}-\alpha)\oplus(1-Z_{\ell,r})\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta+\alpha r},

where
Z_{\ell,r}
has Beta distribution
\mathrm{Beta}(\ell-r\alpha,\theta+r\alpha)
, independent of everything else. Furthermore,

(23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\pi(X^{n-\ell})=\gamma|\pi(Y^{\ell})=\omega)=

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}\big[\vec{P}_{\gamma}\big],

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\pi(X^{n-\ell},Y^{\ell})=\eta|\pi(Y^{\ell})=\omega)=

\displaystyle\,{\cal H}(\omega|\eta)\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\eta}\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\omega}\big]},

with
\cal H
is in (
19
).

Proof.

Equations (
22
) and (
23
) follow from the exchangeability of draws in the above described urn model, together with Corollary 20 in
[
62
]
.
Consider now a path from
\omega
to
\eta
. Let

\omega^{i}=\pi(X^{i},Y^{\ell}),1\leq i\leq n-\ell
,
\ell^{i}=|\omega^{i}|
and
r^{i}
be the number of colours in
\omega^{i}
. Set
\omega^{0}=\omega
. When
\pi(X^{n-\ell},Y^{\ell})=\eta
, there is a path
\omega=\omega^{0}\subset\cdots\subset\omega^{n-\ell}=\eta
. Let
\chi_{B}(\omega^{i},\omega^{i+1})=a_{\omega^{i}_{k}}(\omega^{i})
if
\omega^{i+1}
is obtained from
\omega^{i}
by adding
1
to an existing
k
-th component or
\chi_{B}(\omega^{i},\omega^{i+1})=1
if a new component is added to
\omega^{i+1}
.
Then

\mathbb{P}(\omega^{i+1}|\omega^{i})=\chi_{B}(\omega^{i},\omega^{i+1})\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\omega^{i+1}}\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\omega^{i}}\big]}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\small a_{\omega_{k}^{i}}(\omega^{i})}\frac{\omega_{k}^{i}-\alpha}{\theta+\ell^{i}},\quad&\text{if a coloured ball is drawn,}\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\theta+\alpha r^{i}}{\theta+\ell^{i}},&\text{if the white ball is drawn.}\end{cases}

Note that
\chi_{B}(\omega,\eta)
and
\chi(\omega,\eta)
are conjugate, i.e.

\chi_{B}(\omega,\eta)=\frac{a_{1}(\omega)!\cdots a_{|\omega|}(\omega)!}{a_{1}(\eta)!\cdots a_{|\eta|}(\eta)!}\chi(\omega,\eta).

Evaluating the probability of a path from
\omega
to
\eta
, leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\eta|\omega)=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\omega=\omega^{0}\subset\cdots\subset\omega^{n-\ell}=\eta}\prod_{i=0}^{n-\ell-1}\chi_{B}(\omega^{i},\omega^{i+1})\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\omega^{i+1}}\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\omega^{i}}\big]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\left(\sum_{\omega=\omega^{0}\subset\cdots\subset\omega^{n-w}=\eta}\prod_{i=0}^{n-\ell-1}\chi_{B}(\omega^{i},\omega^{i+1})\right)\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\eta}\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\omega}\big]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{a_{1}(\omega)!\cdots a_{\ell}(\omega)!\dim(\omega,\eta)}{a_{1}(\eta)!\cdots a_{n}(\eta)!}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\eta}\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\omega}\big]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\binom{\ell}{\omega}\dim(\omega,\eta)}{\binom{n}{\eta}}\frac{\binom{n}{\eta}\frac{1}{a_{1}(\eta)!\cdots a_{n}(\eta)!}\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\eta}\big]}{\binom{\ell}{\omega}\frac{1}{a_{1}(\omega)!\cdots a_{\ell}(\omega)!}\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\omega}\big]}={\cal H}(\omega|\eta)\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\eta}\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\omega}\big]}

which gives the second claim.
∎

Note now that when
\gamma
gives the counts in the new sample and
\eta
the counts in the combined sample, we have

\mathbb{P}(\pi(X^{n-\ell})=\gamma|Y^{\ell})=\mathbb{P}(\pi(X^{n-\ell},Y^{\ell})=\eta|Y^{\ell})

and therefore

(24)

M_{n,\omega}(\eta):=\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}\big[\vec{P}_{\gamma}\big]={\cal H}(\omega|\eta)\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}[\vec{P}_{\eta}]}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}[\vec{P}_{\omega}]}.

Hence
M_{n,\omega}(\eta)
is also a partition structure with representing measure
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}
. We conclude the section with a representation of
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}
alternative to (
22
).

Lemma 4.2
.

\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}
in Proposition
4.1
can be written

\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}(dy)=\frac{\vec{P}_{\omega}(y)}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\omega}\big]}\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}(dy).

Proof.

Let
V
have distribution
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}
and
Y^{\ell}
be a sample from the urn.
By Lemma
2.1
,

\mathbb{P}(\pi(Y^{\ell})=\omega,V\in dy)=\vec{P}_{\omega}(y)\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}(dy).

Since the marginal distribution are
\mathbb{P}(\pi(Y^{\ell})=\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\omega}\big]
, by Bayes’ theorem

\mathbb{P}(V\in dy|\pi(Y^{\ell})=\omega)=\frac{\vec{P}_{\omega}(y)}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\omega}\big]}\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}(dy).

From (
23
) in Proposition
4.1
,
\mathbb{P}(V\in dy|\pi(Y^{\ell})=\omega)=\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}(dy)
, leading to the result.
∎

5
Derivation of the transition density

In this section we apply the results of the previous two sections, namely the identified dual process together with conditional two-parameter partition structures, to derive the transition density of the two-parameter diffusion.
In preparation to this task, recall that the two-parameter diffusion is reversible with stationary distribution
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}

[
60
]
. The transition probability
P(t,x,dy)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}
, and its transition density
p(t,x,y)
was shown in
[
29
]
to be

(25)

p(t,x,y)=1+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{m}t}q_{m}(x,y),

where

q_{m}(x,y)=\frac{2m-1+\theta}{m!}\sum_{n=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-n}{m\choose n}(n+\theta)_{(m-1)}p_{n}(x,y),\quad\quad m=2,3,\ldots,

with
p_{0}(x,y)=1
and

p_{n}(x,y)=\sum_{|\eta|=n}\frac{\vec{P}_{\eta}(x)\vec{P}_{\eta}(y)}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\eta}\big]},\ \ \ \ n\geq 1.

The proof of this fact in
[
29
]
leverages on a spectral representation by expanding on a proof by
[
17
]
for the the one-parameter case, i.e., the infinitely-many-neutral-alleles model. Previously,
[
38
]
had obtained (
25
) in the one-parameter case as a limit from a model with finitely-many types (cf. also Proposition 4.3 in
[
43
]
), and
[
19
]
the corresponding version for the labelled model. Analogous transition structures also appear in a family of diffusions defined through the Jack graph, see
[
70
]
and references therein.

Here it is useful to emphasize that there are two possible expansions for the transition density of the model at hand: a spectral expansion in terms of reproducing kernel orthogonal polynomials on
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}
, and an expansion as mixture of two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet distributions.
The equivalence of the two forms was explained in
[
42
,
44
]
for the one parameter case and in
[
69
]
(cf. Theorem 2.1) for the two-parameter case. In particular,
[
69
]
showed that (
25
) is the same as

(26)

p(t,x,y)={\tilde{d}_{1}^{\theta}}(t)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}d_{n}^{\theta}(t)p_{n}(x,y),\quad\theta>-1,

where

(27)

\tilde{d}_{1}^{\theta}(t):=1-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}d_{n}^{\theta}(t).

Note that algebraically

\tilde{d}_{1}^{\theta}(t)=d_{0}^{\theta}(t)+d_{1}^{\theta}(t)

with both
d_{0}^{\theta}(t),d_{1}^{\theta}(t)
non-negative when
\theta\geq 0
, but for
-1<\theta<0
these may not be individually non-negative.

Here we concentrate on the expansion (
26
) and show how this can be derived using the dual process of Theorem
3.3
.
To this end, we need the following corollary of the same theorem.

Corollary 5.1
.

For
\eta\in\Gamma_{n}
, we have

(28)

\mathbb{E}_{x}\big[{P}_{\eta}\big(X(t)\big)\big]=\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\eta}\big]\Bigg(\tilde{d}_{n1}^{\theta}(t)+\sum_{\ell=2}^{n}d^{\theta}_{n\ell}(t)\sum_{|\omega|=\ell,\omega\subset\eta}{\cal H}(\omega|\eta)\frac{{P}_{\omega}(x)}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[{P}_{\omega}\big]}\Bigg),

with
\tilde{d}_{n1}^{\theta}(t)
defined as in (
27
) in relation to (
17
).

Proof.

The proof is immediate by expanding the right-hand side of (
14
) and using (
20
).
∎

Note that in light of (
5
), we can replace
{P}_{\eta}
and
{P}_{\omega}
with
\vec{P}_{\eta}
and
\vec{P}_{\omega}
in (
28
). In this equation there are three sampling probabilities, namely

\displaystyle M_{n}(\eta)=\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}[\vec{P}_{\eta}],\quad M_{n,t}(\eta)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\vec{P}_{\eta}(X(t))],\quad M_{n,\omega}(\eta)={\cal H}(\omega|\eta)\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}[\vec{P}_{\eta}]}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}[\vec{P}_{\omega}]}.

Here
M_{n}(\eta)
is as in (
8
) and it is interpreted as the law of the partition
\eta
taken at stationarity;
M_{n,t}(\eta)
is the law of
\eta
relative to the conditional distribution of
X(t)
, given the initial state
X(0)=x
; finally
M_{n,\omega}(\eta)
is the law of
\eta
conditional of having observed
\omega\subset\eta
, which was the object of Section
4
(cf. (
24
)).
Since all three are partition structures, we can define the respective associated measures

(29)

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(dy)=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\eta|=n}M_{n}(\eta)\delta_{\frac{\eta}{n}}(dy),

\displaystyle\mu_{n,t,x}(dy)=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\eta|=n}\mathbb{E}_{x}[\vec{P}_{\eta}(X_{t})]\delta_{\frac{\eta}{n}}(dy),

\displaystyle\nu_{\omega,n}(dy)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\subset\eta}M_{n,\omega}(\eta)\delta_{\eta/n}(dy).

Lemma
2.1
deals with the convergence of
\mu_{n}
, while the following, which in fact is an application of the former, with the convergence of
\nu_{n,\omega}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\nu_{n,\omega}
be as in (
29
). Then
\nu_{n,\omega}
converges weakly to
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}
.

Proof.

Recall from Section
4
that
\pi(X^{n-\ell},Y^{\ell})
denotes the partition of
n
induced by the joint sample
(X^{n-\ell},Y^{\ell})
.
By Kingman’s representation theorem (cf. Section
2
),
\pi(X^{n-\ell},Y^{\ell})\big/n
converges almost surely to a random variable
Z
with values in
\overline{\nabla}_{\infty}
. Because the conditional distribution of
\pi(X^{n-\ell},Y^{\ell})\big/n
is

\nu_{n,\omega}(dy)=\sum_{|\eta|=n,\ \omega\subset\eta}{\cal H}(\omega|\eta)\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\eta}\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\omega}\big]}\delta_{\eta/n}(dy),

we know that
\nu_{n,\omega}(dy)
converges weakly to the conditional distribution
\mathbb{P}(Z\in dy|Y^{\ell})
. Conditioning on
Y^{\ell}
, we know
\pi(X^{n-\ell})\big/n
and
\pi(X^{n-\ell},Y^{\ell})\big/n
will both converge to
Z
almost surely. Then
Z
has distribution
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}
due to Lemma
2.1
and equation (
23
).
∎

The following Proposition uses the above results to obtain the transition function of the diffusion.

Proposition 5.3
.

Given
X(0)=x
,
X(t)
has distribution

(30)

P(t,x,dy)=\tilde{d}_{1}^{\theta}(t)\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}(dy)+\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}d^{\theta}_{\ell}(t)\sum_{\omega:|\omega|=\ell}\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}(dy)\vec{P}_{\omega}(x),

with
\tilde{d}_{1}^{\theta}(t)
as in (
27
).

Proof.

From Corollary
5.1
we have

\displaystyle\mu_{n,t,x}(dy)=

\displaystyle\tilde{d}_{n,1}^{\theta}(t)\mu_{n}(dy)+\sum_{\ell=2}^{n}d^{\theta}_{n\ell}(t)\sum_{|\omega|=\ell}\vec{P}_{\omega}(x)\nu_{\omega,n}(dy).

Then for any bounded continuous function
f
on
\overline{\nabla}_{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\nabla}_{\infty}}f(y)\mu_{n,t,x}(dy)=\tilde{d}_{n,1}^{\theta}(t)\int_{\overline{\nabla}_{\infty}}f(y)\mu_{n}(dy)+\sum_{\ell=2}^{n}d^{\theta}_{n\ell}(t)\sum_{|\omega|=\ell}\vec{P}_{\omega}(x)\int_{\overline{\nabla}_{\infty}}f(y)\nu_{\omega,n}(dy).

We also know that
\mu_{n}
converges weakly to
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}
from Lemma
2.1
and
\nu_{\omega,n}
converges weakly to
\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}
from Lemma
5.2
. Then the bounded convergence theorem implies

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\overline{\nabla}_{\infty}}f(y)\mu_{n,t,x}(dy)=

\displaystyle\,\tilde{d}_{1}^{\theta}(t)\int_{\overline{\nabla}_{\infty}}f(y)\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}(dy)

\displaystyle\,+\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}d^{\theta}_{\ell}(t)\sum_{|\omega|=\ell}\vec{P}_{\omega}(x)\int_{\overline{\nabla}_{\infty}}f(y)\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}(dy).

Since Lemma
2.1
also implies that
\mu_{n,t,x}(dy)
converges weakly to
P(t,x,dy)
, we can conclude that

P(t,x,dy)=\tilde{d}_{1}^{\theta}(t)\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}(dy)+\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}d^{\theta}_{\ell}(t)\sum_{|\omega|=\ell}\vec{P}_{\omega}(x)\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta|\omega}(dy)

giving the result.
∎

The transition function obtained in Proposition
5.3
can be interpreted in terms of Pólya urn schemes. More specifically, we can exploit the Pólya urn representation discussed in Section
4
to construct, for each
t\geq 0
, a random variable
X(t)
with distribution
P(t,x,dy)
as in Proposition
5.3
. In short, this is obtained by branching the urn scheme after the observation of a sequence that generates a configuration
\omega
, and letting the two resulting split urns evolve independently onwards. This approach is inspired by a similar construction in
[
43
]
for the one-parameter case (see also
[
41
]
for a connection with Wright–Fisher diffusion bridges).

Let
\{U_{n},n\geq 1\}
be the process driven by the Pólya urn scheme described in Section
4
, where the urn starts with a single white ball and
U_{n}
is the configuration of balls after
n
draws. The urn scheme is run for a random number of draws
D_{t}
to obtain a configuration
U_{D_{t}}
, where

\mathbb{P}(D_{t}=\ell)=d_{\ell}^{\theta}(t),~\ell\geq 1,

and
D_{t}
is as in (
16
) and
d_{\ell}^{\theta}(t)
as in (
18
).
The urn is then split and two urns are run independently for
n-D_{t}
additional draws, for
n>D_{t}
, both beginning at
U_{D_{t}}
. This produces two random partitions
\eta
and
\tilde{\eta}
, induced by the two resulting urn configurations. Conditional on the partition
\omega
obtained at step
D_{t}
, these partitions are independent and with common distribution (
24
), namely

(31)

\mathbb{P}(U_{n}=\eta|U_{D_{t}}=\omega)={\cal H}(\omega|\eta)\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\eta}\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\omega}\big]}.

The two urns, denoted
\{(U_{n},U^{\prime}_{n}),n\geq 1\}
, are thus coupled, with joint distribution

\mathbb{P}(U_{n}=\eta,U^{\prime}_{n}=\eta^{\prime})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}d_{\ell}^{\theta}(t)\sum_{|\omega|=\ell}\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\theta}\big[\vec{P}_{\omega}\big]\mathbb{P}(\eta|\omega)\mathbb{P}(\eta^{\prime}|\omega),

where
\mathbb{P}(\eta|\omega)
is a shorthand notation for (
31
) and we set
\mathbb{P}(\eta|\omega)=0
if
|\omega|>|\eta|
, and similarly for
\eta^{\prime}
.
Consider now the measure on
\overline{\nabla}_{\infty}\times\overline{\nabla}_{\infty}

\nu_{n}(dx,dy)=\sum_{|\eta|=n}\sum_{|\eta^{\prime}|=n}\mathbb{P}(U_{n}=\eta,U^{\prime}_{n}=\eta^{\prime})\delta_{\frac{\eta}{n}}(dx)\delta_{\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{n}}(dy).

By another application of Lemma
2.1
,
\nu_{n}(dx,dy)
converges weakly to

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}d_{\ell}^{\theta}(t)p_{\ell}(x,y)\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}(dx)\mathrm{PD}_{\alpha,\theta}(dy).

This probabilistic construction shows that the conditional distribution of
X(t)
given
X(0)
, where
X
is the two-parameter diffusion, is the same as (
26
), at a fixed time
t
.

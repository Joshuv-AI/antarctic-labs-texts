Finite Sample Identification of Wide Shallow Neural Networks with Biases
Massimo Fornasier, Timo Klock, Marco Mondelli, Michael Rauchensteiner

Abstract
Artificial neural networks are functions depending on a finite number of parameters typically encoded as weights and biases. The identification of the parameters of the network from finite samples of input-output pairs is often referred to as the teacher-student model , and this model has represented a popular framework for understanding training and generalization. Even if the problem is NP-complete in the worst case, a rapidly growing literature – after adding suitable distributional assumptions – has established finite sample identification of two-layer networks with a number of neurons m = 𝒪 ⁡ ( D ) m=\mathcal{O}(D) , D D being the input dimension. For the range D < m < D 2 D<m<D^{2} the problem becomes harder, and truly little is known for networks parametrized by biases as well. This paper fills the gap by providing constructive methods and theoretical guarantees of finite sample identification for such wider shallow networks with biases. Our approach is based on a two-step pipeline: first, we recover the direction of the weights, by exploiting second order information; next, we identify the signs by suitable algebraic evaluations, and we recover the biases by empirical risk minimization via gradient descent. Numerical results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach.

In this paper, we give theoretical guarantees on the recovery of both

Technical tools and innovations.

Weight identification:
Instead of considering higher order tensor decompositions (and, hence, higher order differentiation of the network) as done by
[
30
]
, here we follow the strategy of
[
25
]
, which exploits the information coming from the Hessians. However, while
[
25
]
require the weights to be linearly independent (and, thus,

), we tackle the challenging overcomplete case
m>D
. Furthermore, for the identification of the weights we use (
2
), namely a robust non-linear program over vectors, which is significantly less computationally expensive than the minimum rank selection of
[
25
]
. Our analysis improves upon
[

]
by allowing to go beyond a linear scaling between

and

, and it takes advantage of the new insights provided by
[
32
]
on the subspace power method.

Shift identification:
Differently from
[
25
,

]
, we set up an empirical risk minimization problem, and we solve it via gradient descent. Our proof of convergence is based on certain kernel matrices, which are reminiscent of those appearing in the
neural tangent kernel (NTK)

[

. The NTK perspective has been used to prove global convergence of gradient descent for shallow

[

. The technical innovations of our paper with respect to this line of work are as follows. First, we exchange the role between input variable

Second, we extend the NTK theory to handle networks with biases. The weight recovery consists of two steps. First, we leverage the fact that approximated Hessians of the network expose the weights according to

Next, the weights are uniquely identified (up to a sign) as the

4
Identification of the signs and shifts

By leveraging the fact that
differentiation exposes the weights of the network as components of the tensor

\nabla^{n}f(x)=\sum^{m}_{k=1}g^{(n)}(x^{\top}w_{k}+{\tau}_{k})w_{k}^{\otimes n}
for
n=2
, Theorem
3
gives that

{\hat{w}}_{k}\approx{s}_{k}{w}_{k}
for some signs
{s}_{k}\in\{-1,+1\}
. In this section, we show how to recover
the remaining parameters (shifts and signs) for a given set of ground truth weights
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{m}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{D-1}
which are sufficiently incoherent and approximated by
\{{\hat{w}}_{1},\dots,{\hat{w}}_{m}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{D-1}
up to a sign. This recovery can be broken down into two steps. First, we find the correct signs and good initial shifts (cf. Section
4.1
); once the parameters are known, a student network can be initialized from these starting values. Second, the shifts of the student network are refined by empirical risk minimization via gradient descent (cf. Section
4.2
).

4.1
Parameter initialization

Our initialization strategy is centered around the recovery of the quantities
\mathcal{C}_{2}=(\mathcal{C}_{2,1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{2,m})
and
\mathcal{C}_{3}=(\mathcal{C}_{3,1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{3,m})
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{n,k}:=s_{k}^{n}g^{(n)}({\tau}_{k}),\quad\text{ for }k\in[m],\quad n\in\{2,3\}.

(19)

If
g
satisfies
(M1)
, then
g^{(3)}
does not change sign on the interval
(-\tau_{\infty},\tau_{\infty})
due to the monotonicity of
g^{(2)}
. Hence, we can infer the sign
s_{k}
from
\mathcal{C}_{3,k}

is monotone on

, it admits an inverse, which allows for the recovery of
\tau_{k}
from

\langle\nabla^{n}f(x),{\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes n}\rangle
, namely, the directional derivatives of the network
f
along the approximated weights. We consider the following linear system representation of the directional derivatives. Computing the derivative for
x=0
reveals

\displaystyle\langle\nabla^{n}f(0),{\hat{w}}_{\ell}^{\otimes n}\rangle=\sum^{m}_{k=1}s_{k}^{n}g^{(n)}(\tau_{k})\langle s_{k}w_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle^{n}.

Denote by
\tilde{G}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
the matrix with entries
(\tilde{G}_{n})_{\ell,k}={\langle{\hat{w}}_{\ell},s_{k}w_{k}\rangle}^{n}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{n}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}\langle\nabla^{n}f(0),{\hat{w}}_{1}^{\otimes n}\rangle\\
\vdots\\
\langle\nabla^{n}f(0),{\hat{w}}_{m}^{\otimes n}\rangle\end{bmatrix}:=T_{n}.

(20)

In (
20

is a vector containing all directional derivatives of

evaluated at
0
along the recovered weights
{\hat{w}}_{1},\dots,{\hat{w}}_{m}
. These directional derivatives can be approximated from only
\mathcal{O}(n)
evaluations of the network by numerical differentiation (cf.
(G2)
), which allows us to compute
\tilde{T}_{n}\approx T_{n}
. Provided the weight approximations are sufficiently accurate and incoherent in the sense of Definition
2
, the matrix
\tilde{G}_{n}
is invertible and can be estimated by
(\widehat{G}_{n})_{\ell,k}:=\langle{\hat{w}}_{\ell},{\hat{w}}_{k}\rangle^{n}
.
Therefore, we obtain
\mathcal{C}_{n}\approx\tilde{G}_{n}^{-1}\tilde{T}_{n}\approx\widehat{G}_{n}^{-1}\tilde{T}_{n}
. This strategy is summarized in Algorithm
3
detailed in supplementary materials, and the robustness analysis of Proposition

makes all the approximations rigorous.
This procedure could be carried out for any order of directional derivatives, allowing us to benefit from the higher incoherence of
\langle w_{k}^{\otimes n},w_{\ell}^{\otimes n}\rangle=\langle w_{k},w_{\ell}\rangle^{n}
. However, for the sake of simplicity and to be more aligned with our network model, we combine only the second and third order directional derivatives.

4.2
Local convergence of gradient descent

So far, we have obtained weight approximations

6
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we give an algorithm with provable guarantees for the finite sample identification of shallow networks
with biases
, where the number of neurons
m
is roughly
\mathcal{O}(D^{2})
. By doing so, we improve upon previous work
[
25
,
30
]
, which neglects the role of biases and is limited to
m=\mathcal{O}(D)
. Let us finally mention that
[
23
,
22
]
have provided partial results on finite sample identification of deep networks. Thus, giving a complete pipeline for the deep case, which keeps into full consideration also the important role of biases, is an interesting future direction.

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(M)^{2}=\sigma_{m}(MM^{\top})=\sigma_{m}\left(\sum^{N_{h}}_{i=1}\operatorname{vec}(\nabla^{2}f(X_{i}))\otimes\operatorname{vec}(\nabla^{2}f(X_{i}))\right).

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma_{m}\left(\sum^{N_{h}}_{i=1}A_{i}\right)\leq(1-s)\sigma_{m}\left(\sum^{N_{h}}_{i=1}\mathbb{E}A_{i}\right)\right)\leq D^{2}\exp\left(-(1-s)^{2}\sigma_{m}\left(\sum^{N_{h}}_{i=1}\mathbb{E}A_{i}\right)/2K\right)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\operatorname{vec}(\nabla^{2}f(X_{i}))\otimes\operatorname{vec}(\nabla^{2}{f}(X_{i}))\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{D}}\left\lVert\operatorname{vec}(\nabla^{2}{f}(x))\right\rVert^{2}_{2}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{D}}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}{f}(x)\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

Combining this with (

Let us shortly recall the setting of Theorem

which falls into the class of networks described in Section

{\widehat{W}}\approx W
and shift approximations
{\hat{\tau}}\approx\tau
of

. The parameters
({\widehat{W}},{\hat{\tau}})
give rise to a neural network
\hat{f}(\cdot,{\widehat{W}},{\hat{\tau}})
which is architecturally identical to
{f}
, and, depending on the accuracy of the previous algorithmic steps, we would already expect some agreement in terms of
\hat{f}\approx f
. Given network evaluations

of
{f}
, we consider further improvement of the approximated shifts
{\hat{\tau}}
by empirical risk minimization of the objective

\displaystyle J({\hat{\tau}})=\frac{1}{2{N_{\text{train}}}}\sum^{{N_{\text{train}}}}_{i=1}\left(\hat{f}(x_{i},{\hat{\tau}})-y_{i}\right)^{2},

(69)

via gradient descent given by

\displaystyle{\hat{\tau}}^{(n+1)}={\hat{\tau}}^{(n)}-\gamma\nabla J({\hat{\tau}}^{(n)}).

(70)

In Theorem
4
, we prove a local convergence result with the guarantee that, for sufficiently large
N_{\text{train}}
,
\|{\hat{\tau}}^{(n)}-{\tau}\|_{2}
is roughly

\displaystyle\|{\hat{\tau}}^{(n)}-{\tau}\|_{2}\lesssim\frac{m^{1/2}\log(m)^{3/4}}{D^{1/4}}\left(\|{\widehat{W}}-W\|_{F}+\frac{\Delta_{W,O}^{1/2}}{D^{1/2}}+\left\|\sum^{m}_{k=1}w_{k}-{\hat{w}}_{k}\right\|_{2}\right).

where

\Delta_{W,O}=\sum^{m}_{k\neq k^{\prime}}\left|\langle{\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k},{\hat{w}}_{k^{\prime}}-w_{k^{\prime}}\rangle\right|.

Proof sketch.

For the proof, we rely on an idealized loss given by a quadratic functional in
{\hat{\tau}}
:

\displaystyle J_{*}({\hat{\tau}})=({\hat{\tau}}-\tau)^{\top}A({\hat{\tau}}-\tau),

(71)

with

\displaystyle A:=\frac{1}{2{N_{\text{train}}}}\sum^{N_{\text{train}}}_{i=1}\nabla\hat{f}(x_{i},{\tau})\nabla\hat{f}(x_{i},{\tau})^{\top}.

(72)

The proof can then be broken down into two steps. First, in Lemma
8
, it is shown that
J_{*}
is strictly convex by estimating a lower bound on the minimal eigenvalue
\lambda_{m}(A)
of
A
. The proof relies on techniques from the NTK literature to first control the spectrum of
\mathbb{E}_{X_{1},\ldots,X_{N_{\text{train}}}\sim{\cal N}(0,\operatorname{Id}_{D})}[A]
by leveraging
(M2)
and the incoherence of
{\hat{w}}_{1},\dots,{\hat{w}}_{m}
. In particular, Lemma
8
implies that minimizing
J_{*}
via the gradient descent iteration given by

\displaystyle{\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(n+1)}={\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(n)}-\gamma\nabla J_{*}({\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(n)})={\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(n)}-\gamma A({\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(n)}-\tau)

(73)

with step-sizes
\gamma\leq 1/\|A\|

does necessarily converge to the global minimum attained at
{\hat{\tau}}_{*}=\tau
. As a second step, we control the perturbation between the iterations
{\hat{\tau}}^{(n)},{\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(n)}
, when starting them from an identical vector
{\hat{\tau}}^{(0)}={\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(0)}
. In particular, in Lemma
14
it is shown that the difference
\|{\hat{\tau}}^{(n)}-{\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(n)}\|_{2}
adheres to

\displaystyle\|{\hat{\tau}}^{(n)}-{\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(n)}\|_{2}\leq\xi^{n}\|{\hat{\tau}}^{(0)}-{\tau}\|_{2}+\left(1-\xi^{n}\right)\Delta_{W},

provided
{\hat{\tau}}^{(0)}
is sufficiently close to the optimal solution
\tau
, and where
\Delta_{W}
is an error term which satisfies
\Delta_{W}\to 0
as
\|{\widehat{W}}-{W}\|_{F}\to 0
and
\xi\in[0,1)
. By the triangle inequality, we then bound the distance of the original gradient descent iteration (
70
) to
\tau
via

\displaystyle\|{\hat{\tau}}^{(n)}-\tau\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\hat{\tau}}^{(n)}-{\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(n)}\|_{2}+\|{\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(n)}-\tau\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\xi^{n}\|{\hat{\tau}}^{(0)}-{\tau}\|_{2}+\left(1-\xi^{n}\right)\Delta_{W}+(1-\gamma\lambda_{m}(A))^{n}\|{\hat{\tau}}_{*}^{(0)}-\tau\|_{2}\to\Delta_{W},

for
n\to\infty
. Hence, we establish that the iteration
{\hat{\tau}}^{(n)}
settles in an area around the optimal shifts
\tau
that is determined by the initial and irreparable error present in the weight approximation
{\widehat{W}}
of
{W}
.

Organisation of this section.

Subsection
C.1
is dedicated to analyze the matrix
A
in (
72
) in expectation (over
x_{i}
’s) and proves the well-posedness. Subsection
C.2
analyzes the perturbation between gradient descent on the idealized objective
J_{*}

. Subsection

C.1
Well-posedness of the idealized objective in expectation

We begin this section with a short primer on Hermitian expansions, a technical tool which is commonly used in the NTK literature. Afterwards, we prove the well-posedness of
A
in (
72
) in expectation.

C.1.1
A primer on Hermitian expansions

The Hermitian polynomials form an orthonormal basis of the
L_{2}
space,
weighted by the Gaussian kernel
w_{G}
, which we denote as
L_{2}(\mathbb{R},w_{G})
. The
r
-th Hermitian polynomial is defined as

\displaystyle h_{r}(y):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r!}}(-1)^{r}\exp\Big(\frac{y^{2}}{2}\Big)\frac{d^{r}}{dy^{r}}\exp\Big(\frac{-y^{2}}{2}\Big).

Any function
h\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R},w_{G})
can be expanded as
h\equiv\sum_{r}\mu_{r}(h)h_{r}

with Hermitian coefficients
\mu_{r}(h)
as

\displaystyle\mu_{r}(h):=\int h(y)h_{r}(y)w_{G}(y)dy.

As per Assumption
(M1)
the first three derivatives of
g
are bounded, hence
\max_{k\in[3]}\|g_{\tau}^{(k)}\|_{\infty}<\infty
for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. It is easy to
check that this implies that these functions lie within
L_{2}(\mathbb{R},w_{G})
.

Lemma 5
.

Assume
h
is bounded, then
h\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\omega_{G})
and

\displaystyle\sum_{r\geq 0}\mu_{r}(h)^{2}\leq\sqrt{2\pi}\|h\|_{\infty}^{2}

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(t)^{2}\exp(-t^{2}/2)dt\leq\sqrt{2\pi}\|h\|_{\infty}^{2}<\infty.

The second statement follows from the fact that
L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\omega_{G})
is a Hilbert space and the hermite polynomials form an orthonormal system within that space.
∎

We further assume in
(M2)
that
g^{(1)}
is not a polynomial of degree three or less, implying that
also
g^{(1)}_{\tau}
is not a polynomial of degree three or less. Since
h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}
form
a basis for the space of affine functions, this implies
g^{(1)}_{\tau}\not\in\textrm{Span}(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})
.
In particular,
\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{\tau})\neq 0
for some
r\geq 4
and any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. In the following,
we denote

\omega:=\min_{\tau\in[-{\tau}_{\infty},{\tau}_{\infty}]}\sum_{r\geq 4}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}(\cdot+\tau))^{2}>0,

which depends only on the activation function
g^{(1)}
and the shift bound
\tau_{\infty}
. Lastly,
a useful property of Hermitian expansions and the Hermitian basis is the following identity.

Lemma 6

(
[
41
, Lemma D.2]
)
.

For two unit norm vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
and every
k,\ell\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\operatorname{Id}_{D})}\left[h_{k}(v^{\top}X)h_{\ell}(u^{\top}X)\right]=\delta_{k\ell}\langle u,v\rangle^{k},

where
\delta_{k\ell}=1
if
k=\ell
and 0 otherwise.

C.1.2
Well-posedness in expectation

The central object of study in this section is the matrix

\displaystyle E:=\mathbb{E}_{X_{1},\ldots,X_{N_{\text{train}}}\sim{\cal N}(0,\operatorname{Id}_{D})}[A].

(74)

We prove its well-posedness in Lemma
8
. The proof relies on the observation that
E
is actually equal to a sum of positive semidefinite Grammian matrices as shown in Lemma
7
.

Lemma 7
.

Assume that
(M1)
holds, and let
E
be defined as in (
74
). Then, we have

\displaystyle E=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}T_{r}T_{r}^{\top},\quad\textrm{ where }T_{r}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{\tau_{1}})\operatorname{vec}({\hat{w}}_{1}^{\otimes r})\\
\vdots\\
\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{\tau_{m}})\operatorname{vec}({\hat{w}}_{m}^{\otimes r})\\
\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times D^{r}}.

In particular, we have
E\succcurlyeq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}T_{r}T_{r}^{\top}
for any subset
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, where
A\succcurlyeq B
means
A-B
is positive semidefinite.

Proof.

The matrix
A
can be written as

\displaystyle A_{k\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2{N_{\text{train}}}}\sum_{i=1}^{{N_{\text{train}}}}g^{(1)}({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\top}X_{i}+\tau_{k})g^{(1)}({\hat{w}}_{\ell}^{\top}X_{i}+{\tau}_{\ell})

and the corresponding expectation reads

\displaystyle E_{k\ell}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{X\sim{\cal N}(0,\operatorname{Id}_{D})}\left[g^{(1)}_{{\tau}_{k}}({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\top}X)g^{(1)}_{{\tau}_{\ell}}({\hat{w}}_{\ell}^{\top}X)\right].

Now, note that
g^{(1)}_{\tau}=g^{(1)}(\cdot+\tau)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R},w_{H})
for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
by
(M1)
and Lemma
5
. Hence,
g^{(1)}_{\tau}
has
a Hermitian expansion and we can write

\displaystyle E_{k\ell}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{X\sim{\cal N}(0,\operatorname{Id}_{D})}

\displaystyle\left[\Bigg(\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\tau}_{k}})h_{r}({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\top}X)\Bigg)\left(\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\tau}_{\ell}})h_{r}({\hat{w}}_{\ell}^{\top}X)\right)\right].

Using now Lemma
6
to express expectations of scalar products of Hermite polynomials, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{k\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\tau}_{k}})\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\tau}_{\ell}})\langle{\hat{w}}_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle^{r},

which can equivalently be written as
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}T_{r}T_{r}^{\top}
.
The second part of the statement follows from the fact that each individual matrix
T_{r}T_{r}^{\top}
is a positive
semidefinite Grammian matrix.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
E
be defined as in (
74
)
and assume that the approximated weights satisfy
\|{\hat{w}}_{k}\|_{2}=1
and
(A2)

for some universal constant
c_{2}
.
Furthermore, assume the activation function adheres to
(M2)
. Then,
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{m}(E)\geq\omega-C(m-1)\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{2},

(75)

where
\omega
and
C
are constants depending only on
g
and
\tau_{\infty}
. Specifically, we have

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\min_{\tau\in[-\bar{\tau}_{\infty},\bar{\tau}_{\infty}]}\sum_{r\geq 4}\left(\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}(\cdot+\tau))\right)^{2},

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}c_{2}^{2}\max_{\tau,\widetilde{\tau}\in[-\tau_{\infty},\tau_{\infty}]}\sum_{r\geq 4}\left|{\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{\tau})\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{\widetilde{\tau}})}\right|.

Proof of Lemma
8
.

To simplify the notation, we introduce the shorthand
\mu_{r,k}:=\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\tau}_{k}})
.
By Lemma
7
we have
E\succcurlyeq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\geq 4}T_{r}T_{r}^{\top}
, so we concentrate on the
expression on the right hand side. As
\left\lVert{{\hat{w}}}_{k}\right\rVert_{2}=1
for all
k\in[m]
, we first note that we can rewrite
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\geq 4}T_{r}T_{r}^{\top}

as
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\geq 4}T_{r}T_{r}^{\top}=D_{4}+O_{4}
,
where the matrix
D_{4}
is given by

\displaystyle D_{4}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Diag}\Big(\sum_{r\geq 4}\mu_{r,1}^{2},\ldots,\sum_{r\geq 4}\mu_{r,m}^{2}\Big)

and the remainder
O_{4}
equals
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in 4}T_{r}T_{r}^{\top}
with its diagonal
set to
0
. To show (
75
), we compute a lower eigenvalue bound
for
D_{4}
and an upper eigenvalue bound for
O_{4}
independently, and then complete the argument with
Weyl’s eigenvalue perturbation bound
[
62
]
. The smallest
eigenvalue of
D_{4}
can be read from the diagonal and is given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}(D_{4})=\frac{1}{2}\min_{k\in[m]}\sum_{r\geq 4}\mu_{r,k}^{2}\geq\omega>0.

For the spectral norm of
O_{4}
we use
L_{1}/L_{\infty}
-Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities and
\left\lVert{\hat{w}}_{k}\right\rVert_{2}=1
for all
k\in[m]
. Specifically, for any unit norm vector
u
we have

\displaystyle u^{\top}O_{4}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{\ell\neq k}u_{k}u_{\ell}\sum_{r\geq 4}\mu_{r,k}\mu_{r,\ell}\langle{\hat{w}}_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{\ell\neq k}\left|{u_{k}}\right|\left|{u_{\ell}}\right|\sum_{r\geq 4}\left|{\mu_{r,k}\mu_{r,\ell}}\right|\left|{\langle{\hat{w}}_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle}\right|^{r}.

By dragging out the maximum of the sums over Hermitian coefficients, we further bound

\displaystyle u^{\top}O_{4}u\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2}\max_{\tau,\widetilde{\tau}\in[-\tau_{\infty},\tau_{\infty}]}\sum_{r\geq 4}\left|{\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{\tau})\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{\widetilde{\tau}})}\right|\Big)\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{\ell\neq k}\left|{u_{k}}\right|\left|{u_{\ell}}\right|\left|{\langle{\hat{w}}_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle}\right|^{4}.

The trailing factor is, for all unit norm
u
, bounded by the spectral norm of the matrix

(\widehat{O}_{4})_{ij}:=\begin{cases}0,&\textrm{ if }i=j,\\
\left|{\langle{\hat{w}}_{i},{\hat{w}}_{j}\rangle}\right|^{4},&\textrm{ else }.\end{cases}

(76)

Therefore we have
u^{\top}O_{4}u\leq C_{g,\tau_{\infty}}\|\widehat{O}_{4}\|
for all unit norm
u
, and with the constant
C_{g,\tau_{\infty}}
given as

C_{g,\tau_{\infty}}=\frac{1}{2}\max_{\tau,\widetilde{\tau}\in[-\tau_{\infty},\tau_{\infty}]}\sum_{r\geq 4}\left|{\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{\tau})\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{\widetilde{\tau}})}\right|,

and only dependent on
g
and the shift bound
\tau_{\infty}
. By Gershgorin’s circle theorem we further have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{O}_{4}\|\leq\max_{k\in[m]}\sum_{\ell\neq k}^{m}|(\widehat{O}_{4})_{k\ell}|\leq(m-1)\left(\frac{c_{2}\log m}{D}\right)^{2},

where we used the fact that
{\hat{w}}_{1},\ldots,{\hat{w}}_{m}
satisfy
(A2)
. ∎

C.2
Controlling the perturbation from the idealized GD iteration

This section is concerned with the divergence between the two gradient descent iterations in (
70
) and (
73
). We start with a number of auxiliary results that control certain series involving the Hermite coefficients of the activation and its derivatives. These technical statements are needed to control the perturbation in the GD iteration that is caused by the weight approximation. The bounds enable Lemma
13
which provides an upper bound for the difference between the gradients
\nabla J({\hat{\tau}}),\nabla J_{*}({\hat{\tau}})
, defined in (
69
), (
71
), respectively, w.r.t. the accuracy of the estimated weights and shift initializations.

C.2.1
Controlling perturbation from weigths

The first part of this section is concerned with estimating a series that contains elements

\displaystyle S_{r,\ell}

\displaystyle:=\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})\left(\langle{\hat{w}}_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle^{r}-\langle w_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle^{r}\right),

(77)

where
\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}}),\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})
correspond to the
k
-th and
\ell
-th Hermite coefficient of the function
g_{\tau_{k}}(\cdot)=g(\cdot+\tau_{k})
,
g_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}}^{\prime}(\cdot)=g^{(1)}(\cdot+{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell})
, respectively. These coefficients are assumed to be uniformy bounded for all
r\geq 0
which is a consequence of
(M2)
and Lemma
5
.
The following results pave the way for perturbation bound w.r.t. estimated weights and we use the following shorthands to keep the expressions more compact:

\displaystyle\Delta_{W,F}

\displaystyle=\|{\widehat{W}}-W\|_{F},

(78)

\displaystyle\Delta_{W,O}

\displaystyle=\sum^{m}_{k\neq k^{\prime}}\left|\langle{\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k},{\hat{w}}_{k^{\prime}}-w_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\right|.

(79)

Lemma 9
.

Consider weights and approximated weights
(w_{k})_{k\in[m]},({\hat{w}}_{k})_{k\in[m]}
of unit norm as before that both fulfill
(A2)
and (i) in Lemma
3
, as well shifts
(\tau_{k})_{k\in[m]},({\hat{\tau}}_{k})_{k\in[m]}
within
[-\tau_{\infty},\tau_{\infty}]
for some
\tau_{\infty}<\infty
. Let
S_{r,\ell}
be defined as in (
77
) and assume that
g
fulfills the assumption
(M1)
-
(M2)
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for
m\geq D
,

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}S_{r,\ell}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{2}\max_{k,\ell\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})^{2}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})^{2}{\left(1+m\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{r/2}\right)}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\Delta_{W,F}^{2}+\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{(r-1)/2}\Delta_{W,O}\right].

Furthermore, for any fixed
R\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r=2}^{R}2^{r}\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}S_{r,\ell}^{2}\leq\frac{Cm\log m}{D}\left(\Delta_{W,F}^{2}+\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{1/2}\Delta_{W,O}\right),

where the constant
C>0
additionally depends on
R
.

Proof of Lemma
9
.

Throughout this proof we use the convention that, for any vector, we have
v^{\otimes 0}=1
,
1\otimes 1=1
and
v\otimes 1=1\otimes v=v
, which will be relevant for the case
r=1
.
We start with a chain of equalities that uses elementary properties of the Frobenius inner product:

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}S_{r,\ell}^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}\left[\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})\left(\langle{\hat{w}}_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle^{r}-\langle w_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle^{r}\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}\left[\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})\langle{\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle\left(\sum^{r}_{i=1}\langle{\hat{w}}_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle^{r-i}\langle w_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle^{i-1}\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}\left[\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})\langle{\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle\left\langle\sum^{r}_{i=1}{\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}^{\otimes r-1}\right\rangle\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}\left[\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})\left\langle({\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k})\otimes\sum^{r}_{i=1}\left({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)}\right),{\hat{w}}_{\ell}^{\otimes r}\right\rangle\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}\left[\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})\left\langle\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})({\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k})\otimes\sum^{r}_{i=1}\left({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)}\right),{\hat{w}}_{\ell}^{\otimes r}\right\rangle\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})^{2}\left\langle\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})({\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k})\otimes\sum^{r}_{i=1}\left({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)}\right),{\hat{w}}_{\ell}^{\otimes r}\right\rangle^{2}.

At this stage, we separate the coefficients depending on
\ell
such that

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}\left[\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})\left(\langle{\hat{w}}_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle^{r}-\langle w_{k},{\hat{w}}_{\ell}\rangle^{r}\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{\ell\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})^{2}\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}\left\langle\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})({\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k})\otimes\sum^{r}_{i=1}\left({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)}\right),{\hat{w}}_{\ell}^{\otimes r}\right\rangle^{2}.

Now, note that the set of tensors
({\hat{w}}_{\ell}^{\otimes r})_{\ell\in[m]}
forms a frame whose upper frame constant is bounded by the upper spectrum of the Grammian
(\widehat{G}_{r})_{ij}=\langle{\hat{w}}_{i},{\hat{w}}_{j}\rangle^{r}
, see also Lemma
16
. Due to Lemma
17
which relies on Gershgorin’s circle theorem we know there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that for
D
sufficiently large the operator norm of
\widehat{G}_{r}
obeys

\displaystyle\|\widehat{G}_{r}\|\leq C{\left(1+m\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{r/2}\right)}.

As a consequence, we get

\displaystyle\max_{\ell\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})^{2}\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}\left\langle\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})({\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k})\otimes\sum^{r}_{i=1}\left({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)}\right),{\hat{w}}_{\ell}^{\otimes r}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\max_{\ell\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})^{2}\|\widehat{G}_{r}\|\left\|\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})({\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k})\otimes\sum^{r}_{i=1}\left({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)}\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\max_{\ell\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})^{2}{\left(1+m\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{r/2}\right)}\left\|\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})({\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k})\otimes\sum^{r}_{i=1}\left({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)}\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}.

(80)

Denote now
\Delta_{k,r}:=\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})({\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k})
and
T_{k,r}:=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\left({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)}\right)
, then

\displaystyle\left\|\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})({\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k})\otimes\sum^{r}_{i=1}\left({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)}\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{k,k^{\prime}=1}\langle\Delta_{k,r}\otimes T_{k,r},\Delta_{k^{\prime},r}\otimes T_{k^{\prime},r}\rangle=\sum^{m}_{k,k^{\prime}=1}\langle\Delta_{k,r},\Delta_{k^{\prime},r}\rangle\langle T_{k,r},T_{k^{\prime},r}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum^{m}_{k=1}\|\Delta_{k,r}\|_{2}^{2}\|T_{k,r}\|_{F}^{2}+\sum^{m}_{k\neq k^{\prime}}\langle\Delta_{k,r},\Delta_{k^{\prime},r}\rangle\langle T_{k,r},T_{k^{\prime},r}\rangle.

(81)

Using
\|w_{k}\|_{2}=\|{\hat{w}}_{k}\|_{2}=1
we get

\displaystyle\|T_{k,r}\|_{F}\leq\sum^{r}_{i=1}\|{\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)}\|_{F}\leq\sum^{r}_{i=1}\|{\hat{w}}_{k}\|_{2}^{r-i}\|w_{k}\|_{2}^{i-1}=r,

such that the left part of (
81
) can be estimated by

\begin{split}\sum^{m}_{k=1}\|\Delta_{k,r}\|_{2}^{2}\|T_{k,r}\|_{F}^{2}&\leq r^{2}\max_{k\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})^{2}\sum^{m}_{k=1}\|{\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\\
&=r^{2}\max_{k\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})^{2}\|{\widehat{W}}-W\|_{F}^{2}.\end{split}

(82)

To bound the right part of (
81
) first note that, for
k\neq k^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\langle T_{k,r},T_{k^{\prime},r}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum^{r}_{i,i^{\prime}=1}\left\langle{\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)},{\hat{w}}_{k^{\prime}}^{\otimes(r-i^{\prime})}\otimes w_{k^{\prime}}^{\otimes(i^{\prime}-1)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C\sum^{r}_{i,i^{\prime}=1}\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{(r-1)/2}=Cr^{2}\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{(r-1)/2},

for some absolute constant
C
, which follows from the pairwise incoherence
(A2)
as well as point (i) of Lemma
3
. Therefore, the right part of (
81
)
is bounded by

\begin{split}\sum^{m}_{k\neq k^{\prime}}\langle\Delta_{k,r},\Delta_{k^{\prime},r}\rangle\langle T_{k,r},T_{k^{\prime},r}\rangle&\leq Cr^{2}\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{(r-1)/2}\sum^{m}_{k\neq k^{\prime}}\left|\langle\Delta_{k,r},\Delta_{k^{\prime},r}\rangle\right|\\
&\leq Cr^{2}\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{(r-1)/2}\max_{k\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})^{2}\sum^{m}_{k\neq k^{\prime}}\left|\langle{\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k},{\hat{w}}_{k^{\prime}}-w_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\right|.\end{split}

(83)

Plugging in (
82
) and (
83
) into (
81
) yields

\displaystyle\left\|\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})({\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k})\otimes\sum^{r}_{i=1}\left({\hat{w}}_{k}^{\otimes(r-i)}\otimes w_{k}^{\otimes(i-1)}\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}

(84)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Cr^{2}\max_{k\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})^{2}\left[\|{\widehat{W}}-W\|_{F}^{2}+\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{(r-1)/2}\sum^{m}_{k\neq k^{\prime}}\left|\langle{\hat{w}}_{k}-w_{k},{\hat{w}}_{k^{\prime}}-w_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\right|\right].

(85)

Combining this with (
80
) yields the desired first statement

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}S_{r,\ell}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{2}\max_{k,\ell\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})^{2}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})^{2}{\left(1+m\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{r/2}\right)}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\Delta_{W,F}^{2}+\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{(r-1)/2}\Delta_{W,O}\right].

For the second statement, note that
\max_{k\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g_{\tau_{k}})^{2}
is bounded due to
(M2)
and
\max_{\ell\in[m]}\mu_{r}(g^{(1)}_{{\hat{\tau}}_{\ell}})^{2}
is bounded according to Lemma
5
. Hence it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{r=2}^{R}2^{r}\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}S_{r,\ell}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=2}^{R}2^{r}Cr^{2}{\left(1+m\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{r/2}\right)}\left[\Delta_{W,F}^{2}+\left(\frac{\log m}{D}\right)^{(r-1)/2}\Delta_{W,O}\right]

Lemma 16
.

Let
w_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
for
k=1,\dots,m
, and denote by
G_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
the Grammian matrix associated with
(w_{k}^{\otimes n})_{k\in[m]}
, which is given by
(G_{n})_{ij}=\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle^{n}
. Then, for any n-mode tensor
T\in\mathbb{R}^{{D\times\dots\times D}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{k=1}\langle T,w_{k}^{\otimes n}\rangle^{2}\leq\|G_{n}\|\|T\|^{2}_{F}.

(119)

Proof.

First note that we can express the Frobenius inner product as an ordinary inner product over
\mathbb{R}^{D^{n}}
with the help of the
\operatorname{vec}(\cdot)
operator, since
\langle T,w_{k}^{\otimes n}\rangle=\langle\operatorname{vec}(T),\operatorname{vec}(w_{k}^{\otimes n})\rangle
. Let us denote

\displaystyle W_{n}:=\Big(\operatorname{vec}(w_{1}^{\otimes n})\Big|\dots\Big|\operatorname{vec}(w_{m}^{\otimes n})\Big)\in\mathbb{R}^{D^{n}\times m}.

Then, the following chain of inequalities hold

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{k=1}\langle T,w_{k}^{\otimes n}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum^{m}_{k=1}\langle\operatorname{vec}(T),\operatorname{vec}(w_{k}^{\otimes n})\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum^{m}_{k=1}\operatorname{vec}(T)^{\top}\operatorname{vec}(w_{k}^{\otimes n})\operatorname{vec}(w_{k}^{\otimes n})^{\top}\operatorname{vec}(T)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{vec}(T)^{\top}W_{n}W_{n}^{\top}\operatorname{vec}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\|W_{n}^{\top}W_{n}\|\cdot\|\operatorname{vec}(T)\|_{2}^{2}=\|W_{n}^{\top}W_{n}\|\|T\|_{F}^{2}.

Since
\|W_{n}^{\top}W_{n}\|=\|G_{n}\|
, this finishes the proof.
∎

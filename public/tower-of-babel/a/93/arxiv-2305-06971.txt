Blow-up for the 3D intercritical inhomogeneous NLS with inverse-square potential
Luccas Campos, Mykael Cardoso and Luiz Gustavo Farah

Abstract
. In this paper we study the focusing inhomogeneous 3D nonlinear Schrödinger equation with inverse-square potential in the mass-supercritical and energy-subcritical regime. We first establish local well-posedness in H ˙ a s c ∩ H ˙ a 1 \dot{H}_{a}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}_{a}^{1} , with s c = 3 / 2 − ( 2 − b ) / 2 ​ σ s_{c}=3/2-(2-b)/2\sigma . Next, we prove the blow-up of the scaling invariant Lebesgue norm for radial solutions and also, with an additional restriction, in the non-radial case.

Introduction

Here, we are interested in the following range of the parameters:

(1.10)

\displaystyle u\in C\left([0,T];H^{1}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right)\cap L^{q}\left([0,T];H^{1,r}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right)

where

-admissible pair.

They also proved the existence of a ground state and a dichotomy between global existence and blow up below the ground state threshold. Furthermore, they proved small data scattering, constructed wave operators in

and proved that suitable space-time bounds imply scattering in the intercritical case. Later on, An, Jan and Kim

We are interested here in studying blow-up rates for (INLS)

. To that end, we first obtain the local theory in

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

Let

Theorem 1.3

Assume

,

\frac{2-b}{3}<\sigma<2-b

We emphasize that our previous studies about the INLS model in

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\left(|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}u\right)\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}(B^{R}_{\pm})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\left\||x|^{-b}\right\|_{L^{\frac{3}{b\pm\varepsilon}}_{x}(B^{R}_{\pm})}+\left\||x|^{-b-1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{3}{b+1\pm\varepsilon}}_{x}(B^{R}_{\pm})}\right)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\alpha_{\pm}}_{x}}^{2\sigma+1}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{\pm\varepsilon}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\alpha_{\pm}}_{x}}^{2\sigma+1}.

Now, let
q
,
\beta_{\pm}
be such that
(q,p)
,
(\beta_{+},\alpha_{+})
and
(\beta_{-},\alpha_{-})
are
L^{2}
-admissible pairs. One can easily check that, for
\theta_{\pm}=\sigma(1-s_{c})\mp\varepsilon/2
, the following condition is satisfied

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\theta_{\pm}+\frac{2\sigma+1}{\alpha_{\pm}},

then, by the Hölder inequality in the time variable and the equivalence of Sobolev norms, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}u\right)\right\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{T}L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(T^{\theta_{+}}R^{\varepsilon}+T^{\theta_{-}}R^{-\varepsilon}\right)\|\mathcal{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{a}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}^{2\sigma+1}.

Finally, taking
R=T^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a>0
,
0<b<\frac{3}{2}
and
\frac{2-b}{3}<\sigma<2-b
. Then, for
I=[-T,T]
we have

(3.9)

\left\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}\left(|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}u\right)\right\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2};I)}\lesssim T^{\sigma(1-s_{c})}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}^{2\sigma}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}.

Remark 3.3
.

Here, due to the product rule, we are required to estimate quantities comparable to
(D^{s_{c}}|x|^{-b})|u|^{2\sigma}u
and to
|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}D^{s_{c}}u
. However, unlike the previous Lemma, since fractional derivatives are not localized in space, one cannot simply split the estimate on a ball and its exterior. Indeed, it is
not
expected that

(3.10)

\|D^{s_{c}}(|x|^{-b}f)\|_{L_{x}^{p}(B_{\pm}^{R})}\lesssim\|D^{s_{c}}|x|^{-b}\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}(B_{\pm}^{R})}\|f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}+\||x|^{-b}\|_{L_{x}^{p_{2}}(B_{\pm}^{R})}\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{q_{2}}}

for general
f
, even for suitable
p,p_{j},q_{j}
, due to the non-local character of the fractional derivatives. A more appropriate version of the Leibniz rule, adapted to deal with this case, can be found in
[
13
]
, and the nonlinear estimates there can be readily adapted to the proof of this Lemma. Nevertheless, we present another approach here, involving a well-applied localization, and a Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. Indeed, we let
\psi
be a smooth, nonincreasing, radially symmetric function such that

(3.11)

\psi(x)=\begin{cases}1,&|x|\leq 1\\
0,&|x|\geq 2,\end{cases}

and let
\psi_{R}(x)=\psi(x/R)
for all
x
and
R>0
. We then claim

Claim 3.4
.

We have, for all
0\leq s\leq 1
,

(3.12)

\|D^{s}(|x|^{-b}\psi_{R})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{b+s+\varepsilon}}}\lesssim R^{\varepsilon},

and

(3.13)

\|D^{s}(|x|^{-b}(1-\psi_{R}))\|_{L^{\frac{3}{b+s-\varepsilon}}}\lesssim R^{-\varepsilon}.

Proof of the Claim.

We use Sobolev embedding to deduce

(3.14)

\|D^{s}(|x|^{-b}\psi_{R})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{b+s+\varepsilon}}}\lesssim\|\nabla(|x|^{-b}\psi_{R})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{b+1+\varepsilon}}}\lesssim R^{\varepsilon},

since
|\nabla|x|^{-b}||\psi_{R}|=|x|^{-b-1}|\psi_{R}|
and since
|x|^{-b}|\nabla\psi_{R}|\lesssim R^{-b-1}
and
\nabla\psi_{R}
is supported on
R\leq|x|\leq 2R
. Similar calculations give the estimate for the term with
(1-\psi_{R})
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, consider the
L^{2}
-admissible pairs
(q,p)
,
(\beta_{+},\alpha_{+})
and
(\beta_{-},\alpha_{-})
given by (
3.2
). From relation (
3.3
), we also have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b+s_{c}\pm\varepsilon}{3}+2\sigma\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{\pm}}-\frac{1}{3}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{\pm}}-\frac{s_{c}}{3}\right).

The Lemma
2.1
(Leibniz and Chain rules), Hölder and Sobolev inequalities yield

(3.16)

\displaystyle\|D^{s_{c}}(|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}u)\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D^{s_{c}}(|x|^{-b}\psi_{R})\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3}{b+s_{c}+\varepsilon}}}\|\nabla u\|^{2\sigma}_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{+}}}\|D^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{+}}}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\quad+\||x|^{-b}\psi_{R}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3}{b+\varepsilon}}}\|\nabla u\|^{2\sigma}_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{+}}}\|D^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{+}}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\quad+\|D^{s_{c}}(|x|^{-b}(1-\psi_{R}))\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3}{b+s_{c}-\varepsilon}}}\|\nabla u\|^{2\sigma}_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{-}}}\|D^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{-}}}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\quad+\||x|^{-b}(1-\psi_{R})\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3}{b-\varepsilon}}}\|\nabla u\|^{2\sigma}_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{-}}}\|D^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{-}}}.

Next, from Claim
3.4
, we get

\|D^{s_{c}}(|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}u)\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}\lesssim R^{\varepsilon}\|\nabla u\|^{2\sigma}_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{+}}}\|D^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{+}}}+R^{-\varepsilon}\|\nabla u\|^{2\sigma}_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{-}}}\|D^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{x}^{\alpha_{-}}}.

Finally, using the relation (
3.7
), the Hölder inequality in the time variable and the equivalence of Sobolev norms imply

(3.20)

\|\mathcal{L}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}_{a}(|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}u)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{T}L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}}\lesssim\left(T^{\theta_{+}}R^{\varepsilon}+T^{\theta_{-}}R^{-\varepsilon}\right)\|\mathcal{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{a}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}^{2\sigma}\|\mathcal{L}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}_{a}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}

and the choice
R=T^{\frac{1}{2}}
finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
a>0
,
0<b<2
,
\frac{2-b}{3}<\sigma<2-b
. Then, there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that, for
I=[-T,T]
we have

(3.21)

\left\|\mathcal{|}x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}v\right\|_{S^{\prime}(\dot{H}^{-s_{c}};I)}\lesssim\left[\left(T^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{2}}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}\right)^{1-\delta}\left(\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}\right)^{\delta}\right]^{2\sigma}\|v\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}};I)}.

Proof.

For a fixed
\sigma>0
, take
s\in(s_{c},1)
such that

(3.22)

\sigma<\frac{1-s_{c}}{s-s_{c}}.

So, since
2\sigma(s-s_{c})+1<3-2s_{c}
, we can find
\ell
satisfying

\max\left\{\frac{6}{3-2s_{c}},\frac{6}{2\sigma(s-s_{c})+1+\sigma-\varepsilon}\right\}<\ell<\frac{6}{2\sigma(s-s_{c})+1+\varepsilon},

for some
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, such that
(m,\ell)
is
\dot{H}^{-s_{c}}
-admissible and
(\tau,\ell)
is
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
-admissible. Also consider the pairs
(\nu_{\pm},\mu_{\pm})
such that

(3.23)

\displaystyle\mu_{\pm}=\frac{6\sigma\ell}{\ell(3+2\sigma s-b\mp\varepsilon)-6}.

It is straightforward to check that
2<\mu_{\pm}<3
, so we can define the
L^{2}
-admissible pairs
(\nu_{-},\mu_{-})
and
(\nu_{+},\mu_{+})
. Now, setting

\zeta_{\pm}=\sigma(s-s_{c})\mp\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

we get

(3.24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell^{\prime}}=\frac{b\pm\varepsilon}{3}+2\sigma\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{\pm}}-\frac{s}{3}\right)+\frac{1}{\ell}\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\frac{1}{m^{\prime}}=\zeta_{\pm}+\frac{2\sigma}{\nu_{\pm}}+\frac{1}{\tau}.

Then, applying Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embedding we have, using the same notation of the previous lemmas, that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\left\||x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}v\right\|_{L^{m^{\prime}}_{T}L^{\ell^{\prime}}_{x}(B^{R}_{\pm})}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\zeta_{\pm}}R^{\pm\varepsilon}\|D^{s}u\|_{L^{\nu_{\pm}}_{T}L^{\mu_{\pm}}_{x}}^{2\sigma}\|v\|_{L^{\tau}_{T}L^{\ell}_{x}}.

Moreover, by interpolation and the equivalence of Sobolev norms (
1.7
) we obtain

(3.26)

\displaystyle\|D^{s}u\|_{L^{\nu_{\pm}}_{T}L^{\mu_{\pm}}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Du\|^{1-\delta}_{L^{\nu_{\pm}}_{T}L^{\mu_{\pm}}_{x}}\|D^{s_{c}}u\|^{\delta}_{L^{\nu_{\pm}}_{T}L^{\mu_{\pm}}_{x}}

(3.27)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{a}u\|_{L^{\nu_{\pm}}_{T}L^{\mu_{\pm}}_{x}}^{1-\delta}\|\mathcal{L}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}_{a}u\|_{L^{\nu_{\pm}}_{T}L^{\mu_{\pm}}_{x}}^{\delta},

for
\delta=(1-s)/(1-s_{c})\in(0,1)
. Collecting the previous two estimates, we obtain

(3.28)

\displaystyle\left\||x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}v\right\|_{L^{m^{\prime}}_{T}L^{\ell^{\prime}}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(T^{\zeta_{+}}R^{\varepsilon}+T^{\zeta_{-}}R^{-\varepsilon}\right)\left(\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}^{1-\delta}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}^{\delta}\right)^{2\sigma}\|v\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}};I)}.

Again setting
R=T^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we find

(3.29)

T^{\zeta_{+}}R^{\varepsilon}+T^{\zeta_{-}}R^{-\varepsilon}=T^{\sigma(s-s_{c})}=T^{\sigma(1-\delta)(1-s_{c})},

completing the proof.

∎

3.2.
Local theory

Next, we use the above nonlinear estimates to obtain the local well-posedness in
\dot{H_{a}^{s_{c}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\dot{H}_{a}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let

X=\left(\bigcap_{(q,p)\in\mathcal{A}_{0}}L^{q}\left([-T,T];\dot{H}^{s_{c},p}_{a}\cap\dot{H}^{1,p}_{a}\right)\right)\bigcap\left(\bigcap_{(\overline{q},\overline{p})\in\mathcal{A}_{s_{c}}}L^{\overline{q}}\left([-T,T];L^{\overline{p}}\right)\right)

and

\|u\|_{T}=\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{S\left(L^{2};I\right)}+\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u\|_{S\left(L^{2};I\right)}+\|u\|_{S\left(\dot{H}^{s_{c}};I\right)},

where
I=[-T,T]
.

For
m>1
and
T>0
define the set

S(m,T)=\{u\in X:\|u\|_{T}\leq m\}

with the metric

d_{T}(u,v)=\|u-v\|_{S\left(\dot{H}^{s_{c}};I\right)}.

By standards arguments
(S(m,T),d_{T})
is a complete metric space (see Cazenave
[
17
, Theorem 4.4.1]
and also
[
16
, Appendix A]
). We shall show that
G=G_{u_{0}}
defined by

(3.30)

G(u)(t)=e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{a}}u_{0}+i\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\mathcal{L}_{a}}\left(|x|^{-b}|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)\right)ds,

has a fixed point in
S(m,T)
for a suitable choice of
m
and
T
. Indeed, it follows from the Strichartz inequalities in Lemma
2.5
, and also, from Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, that, for some
c>1
,

(3.31)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}G(u)\|_{S(L^{2};I)}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+cT^{\sigma(1-s_{c})}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|^{2\sigma+1}_{S(L^{2};I)},

and

(3.32)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}G(u)\|_{S(L^{2};I)}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+cT^{\sigma(1-s_{c})}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}^{2\sigma}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}.

Moreover, from Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
3.5
, we also have

\|G(u)\|_{S\left(\dot{H}^{s_{c}};I\right)}\leq c\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+c\left[\left(T^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{2}}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}\right)^{1-\delta}\left(\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};I)}\right)^{\delta}\right]^{2\sigma}\|u\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}};I)}.

Now, by fixing
m\geq\max\{1,2c\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}_{a}\cap\dot{H}^{1}_{a}}\}
and choosing
T\in(0,1)
such that

(3.33)

T^{\sigma(1-s_{c})}<\left(\frac{1}{4cm^{2\sigma}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\delta}},

we obtain that
G(u)\in S(m,T)
, if
u\in S(m,T)
and therefore
G
is well defined.
To prove that
G
is a contraction we first recall the elementary inequality

(3.34)

\left||x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}u-|x|^{-b}|v|^{2\sigma}v\right|\lesssim|x|^{-b}\left(|u|^{2\sigma}+|v|^{2\sigma}\right)|u-v|.

Then, again from Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
3.5
, we get for
u,v\in S(m,T)
that

\displaystyle d_{T}(G(u),G(v))\leq 2cT^{\sigma(1-s_{c})(1-\delta)}m^{2\sigma}d_{T}(u,v).

Therefore, for
T\in(0,1)
satisfying (
3.33
),
G
is a contraction on
S(m,T)
. Finally, by the contraction mapping principle we have a unique
u\in S(m,T)
such that
G(u)=u
and the proof is completed.

∎

3.3.
A Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality and Blow-up alternative

We start recalling the celebrated Caffareli-Khon-Nirenberg inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Proposition 3.6

(Caffareli-Khon-Nirenberg inequality)
.

There exists
C>0
such that the following inequality holds for all
f\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(3.35)

\int|x|^{-b}|f|^{2\sigma+2}\,dx\leq C\|\nabla f\|^{\theta}_{L^{2}}\|f\|^{2\sigma+2-\theta}_{L^{p}}.

if and only, if the following relations hold

(3.36)

\displaystyle\frac{3-b}{3}=\frac{\theta}{6}+\frac{2\sigma+2-\theta}{p}

where
b<3
,
p\geq 1
and
0<\theta<2\sigma+2
.

Proof.

This is the 3D version of the main result in Caffareli, Khon and Nirenberg
[
8
]
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

We note that:

1.

Taking
p=2
in the inequality (
3.35
), we have the well-known Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

(3.37)

\displaystyle\int|x|^{-b}|f|^{2\sigma+2}\,dx\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}^{2\sigma s_{c}+2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2\sigma(1-s_{c})},

where the best constant was established by the third author in
[
23
]
.

2.

For
p=\sigma_{c}=\frac{2N\sigma}{2-b}
, the inequality (
3.35
) is given by

(3.38)

\int|x|^{-b}|f|^{2\sigma+2}\,dx\leq C\|\nabla f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|f\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}.

In
[
16
]
, joint with Guzmán, the sharp constant for above inequality was obtained.

3.

Since
a>0
, we have that (see relation (
1.6
))

\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}}\sim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}.

Thus, it follows from inequality (
3.35
) that there exists an optimal constant
K_{a}>0
such that

(3.39)

\int|x|^{-b}|f|^{2\sigma+2}\,dx\leq\frac{\sigma+1}{K_{a}^{2\sigma}}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|f\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}.

We will refer to the constant
K_{a}
later in this work (see Proposition
4.3
).

Next, we prove a blow-up alternative which follows as a consequence of the local theory obtained in Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 3.8

(Blow-up alternative)
.

Let
a>0
,
0<b<\frac{3}{2}
and
\frac{2-b}{3}<\sigma<2-b
. If
u
is a solution to the IVP (
1.1
) in
\dot{H_{a}^{s_{c}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\dot{H}_{a}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with finite maximal positive time of existence
0<T^{*}<+\infty
, then there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(3.40)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{c}{(T^{*}-t)^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{2}}},\,\,\,\textit{for all}\,\,\,t\in[0,T^{*}).

Proof.

Assume,
by contradiction, that there exists a sequence
t_{n}\nearrow T^{\ast}
such that, for all
n
,

(3.41)

(T^{*}-t_{n})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{2}}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}<\frac{1}{n}.

Using the same notation as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we define

(3.42)

X_{n}(t)=\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{\beta_{+}}_{[t_{n},t]}L^{\alpha_{+}}_{x}}+\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{\beta_{-}}_{[t_{n},t]}L^{\alpha_{-}}_{x}}.

From the Duhamel formula and estimate (
3.41
), for
t\in[t_{n},T^{*})
, there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

(3.43)

\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{[t_{n},t]}L^{2}_{x}}+X_{n}(t)\leq C_{0}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{C_{0}}{(n\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}})^{2\sigma}}[X_{n}(t)]^{2\sigma+1}.

Consider the function
f(x)=x-C_{0}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}-\frac{C_{0}}{(n\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}})^{2\sigma}}x^{2\sigma+1}
. A simple computation revels that it has a global maximum at
x_{n}^{\ast}=\frac{n\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}}{[C_{0}(2\sigma+1)]^{1/2\sigma}}
,
f(x_{n}^{\ast})=\left(\frac{2\sigma n}{(2\sigma+1)[C_{0}(2\sigma+1)]^{1/2\sigma}}-C_{0}\right)\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}
and
f(0)<0
. Thus, for
n_{0}>\frac{\left[C_{0}(2\sigma+1)\right]^{\frac{2\sigma+1}{2\sigma}}}{2\sigma}
we have that
f(x_{n_{0}}^{\ast})>0
.

Now, from (
3.43
) we have that
f(X_{n_{0}}(t))\leq 0
, for all
t\in[t_{n_{0}},T^{*})
. A continuity argument then implies that
X_{n_{0}}(t)\leq x_{n_{0}}^{\ast}
and therefore, from inequality (
3.43
), we deduce

(3.44)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{[t_{n_{0}},T^{*})}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{n_{0}})\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{C_{0}}{(n_{0}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{n_{0}})\|_{L^{2}})^{2\sigma}}(x_{n_{0}}^{*})^{2\sigma+1}

(3.45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{n_{0}}{[C_{0}(2\sigma+1)]^{(2\sigma+1)/2\sigma}}\right)C_{0}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{n_{0}})\|_{L^{2}},

which implies

\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{[0,T^{*})}L^{2}_{x}}<\infty
, by continuity.

On the other hand, the bound
X_{n_{0}}(t)\leq x_{n_{0}}^{\ast}
and assumption (
3.41
) also implies

(3.46)

(t-t_{n_{0}})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{2}}X_{n_{0}}(t)<\frac{1}{[C_{0}(2\sigma+1)]^{1/2\sigma}},\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,t\in[0,T^{*}).

Moreover, again from Duhamel and the proof of estimate (
3.32
), we can write, for
t_{n_{0}}\leq t<T^{*}
,

(3.47)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};[t_{n_{0}},t])}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u(t_{n_{0}})\|_{L^{2}}+C_{0}\left[(t-t_{n_{0}})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{2}}X_{n_{0}}(t)\right]^{2\sigma}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};[t_{n_{0}},t])}

(3.48)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u(t_{n_{0}})\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma+1}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};[t_{n_{0}},t])}.

We can then absorb the last term of the right-hand side in the left-hand side and let
t\nearrow T^{*}
to obtain

(3.49)

\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u\|_{S(L^{2};[t_{n_{0}},T^{*}))}\leq\frac{(2\sigma+1)C_{0}}{2\sigma}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u(t_{n_{0}})\|_{L^{2}},

which is a contradiction, since
\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{[0,T^{*})}L^{2}_{x}}+\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{s_{c}}{2}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{[0,T^{*})}L^{2}_{x}}
cannot be finite, otherwise
T^{*}=+\infty
.

Remark 3.9
.

The constant
c>0
in the previous proposition can be chosen independently of
u
. Indeed, from its proof,one can choose

(3.50)

c=\frac{2\sigma}{\left[C_{0}(2\sigma+1)\right]^{\frac{2\sigma+1}{2\sigma}}}.

Remark 3.10
.

The previous proposition can be adapted to the INLS equation (
1.3
), showing that the lower bound

\|\mathcal{\nabla}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{c}{(T^{*}-t)^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{2}}},\,\,\,\textit{for all}\,\,\,t\in[0,T^{*}),

always hold for
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{1}
blow-up solution in the INLS setting, improving our previous results
[
14
, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3]
and
[
15
, Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 1.5]
.

∎

4.
Blow-up solutions in
\dot{H}_{a}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}_{a}^{1}

4.1.
A virial-type estimate

Consider
u\in C([0,\tau_{*}]:\dot{H}_{a}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}_{a}^{1})
a solution to (

), we obtain

(4.70)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{2s_{c}}_{v}(\tau_{0})}\left|\int\varphi_{\tilde{R}}|v(\tau_{0})|^{2}\,dx-\int\varphi_{\tilde{R}}|v_{0}|^{2}\,dx\right|<\varepsilon

which concludes the proof of Proposition
4.4
.

∎

4.4.
Existence of blow-up solutions and lower bound for the blow-up rate

We are now in position to prove our main results. We start with the existence of blow-up solutions.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
u\in C([0,+\infty):\dot{H}_{a}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}_{a}^{1})
be a global solution with
E[u_{0}]\leq 0
. In view of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality (
3.39
) and
a>0
, we deduce that

(4.71)

\displaystyle 0\geq E[u_{0}]\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left[1-\left(\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}}{K_{a}}\right)^{2\sigma}\right].

and therefore the assumptions of Proposition
4.3
are satisfied which implies

(4.72)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau_{*}}(\tau_{*}-\tau)\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,d\tau\leq C_{u_{0}}\tau_{*}^{1+s_{c}},\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\tau_{*}>0.

Define
\lambda_{u}(\tau)=\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(\tau)\|^{-\frac{1}{1-s_{c}}}_{L^{2}}
. By previous inequality there exists a sequence
\tau_{n}\nearrow+\infty
such that

(4.73)

\displaystyle\lambda_{u}(\tau_{n})\geq C_{u_{0}}\sqrt{\tau_{n}},\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\,n\in\mathbb{N},

and in particular
\lambda_{u}(\tau_{n})\nearrow+\infty
. Therefore, since
u_{0}\in\dot{H}_{a}^{s_{c}}\subset L^{\sigma_{c}}
, the relation (
4.28
) implies

(4.74)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{(D\lambda_{u}(\tau_{n}))^{2s_{c}}}\int_{|x|\leq D\lambda_{u}(\tau_{n})}|u_{0}|^{2}\,dx=0,\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\,D\geq 0.

On the other hand, we can apply Proposition
4.4
at time
\tau_{n}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, since
F_{n}=\frac{\sqrt{\tau_{n}}}{\lambda_{u}(\tau_{n})}\leq C_{u_{0}}
, there exists
D_{*}
, independent of
n
, such that

(4.75)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{u}^{2s_{c}}(\tau_{n})}\int_{|x|\leq D_{*}\lambda_{u}(\tau_{n})}|u_{0}|^{2}\,dx\geq C_{2}>0,

reaching a contradiction with (
4.74
). Therefore the solution cannot exist for all positive time completing the proof.
∎

Next, we refine the analysis to show a lower bound for the blow-up rate of the critical Sobolev norm.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
u_{0}\in\dot{H_{a}^{s_{c}}}\cap\dot{H}_{a}^{1}
so that the maximal time of existence
T^{\ast}>0
of the corresponding solution
u
to (
1.1
) is finite. The inequality (
3.40
) yields

(4.76)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{c}{(T^{*}-t)^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{2}}},\,\,\,\textit{for all}\,\,\,t\in[0,T^{*}).

For
\sigma_{c}=\frac{6\sigma}{2-b}
, we shall prove that

(4.77)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}\geq|\log(T^{\ast}-t)|^{\alpha},\,\,\,as\,\,\,t\to T^{\ast},

for a universal constant
\alpha=\alpha(N,\sigma,b)>0
, which implies the same lower bound for the critical Sobolev norm
\dot{H}_{a}^{s_{c}}
. The proof follows the ideas introduced by Merle and Raphäel
[
40
]
and is very similar to the proof of Theorem 1.2 in
[
14
]
. Below we sketch the main steps. First, for
t
close enough to
T^{*}
, define the renormalization of
u
given by

(4.78)

\displaystyle v^{(t)}(x,\tau)=\lambda_{u}^{\frac{2-b}{2\sigma}}(t)\bar{u}(\lambda_{u}(t)x,t-\lambda^{2}_{u}(t)\tau),

where
\lambda_{u}(t)=\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)\|^{-\frac{1}{1-s_{c}}}_{L^{2}}
.
One can check that
v^{(t)}(\tau)\in C([0,\tau^{(t)}]:\dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{1})
is a solution to (
1.1
) with
\tau^{(t)}={t}/{\lambda_{u}^{2}(t)}
and also Propositions
4.3
and
4.4
can be applied to
v^{(t)}(\tau_{0})
for any
\tau_{0}\in\left[0,{1}/{\lambda_{u}(t)}\right]
(see
[
14
, Lemma 6.1]
). From now on, to simplify the notation, we write
v=v^{(t)}
.

Define
N(t)=-\log\lambda_{u}(t)
. Since
\|u(t)\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}=\|v(0)\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}
, in view of inequality (
4.76
), the problem is reduced to find
\alpha=\alpha(N,\sigma,b)>0
such that, for
t
close enough to
T^{\ast}
, we have

(4.79)

\displaystyle\|v(0)\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}\geq[N(t)]^{\alpha}.

Recalling the constants
K_{a}
and
\alpha_{2}
given in (
3.39
) and (
4.52
), respectively, we define

(4.80)

\displaystyle L(t)=[100[M(t)]^{\alpha_{2}}]^{\frac{1}{2(1-s_{c})}},\,\,\,\mbox{where}\,\,\,M(t)=\frac{4\|v(0)\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}}{K_{a}}\geq 2.

If
L(t)\geq e^{\frac{\sqrt{N(t)}}{2}}
, then it is easy to see that (
4.79
) holds with
\alpha=1
. On the other hand, if
L(t)<e^{\frac{\sqrt{N(t)}}{2}}
, we pick times
\tau_{i}\in[0,e^{i}]
, for all
i\in[\sqrt{N(t)},N(t)]
, such that

(4.81)

\displaystyle F(\tau_{i})\leq L(t)\,\,\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\,\,\,\frac{1}{10L(t)}e^{\frac{i-1}{2}}\leq\lambda_{v}(\tau_{i})\leq\frac{10}{L(t)}e^{\frac{i}{2}},

where
F(\tau)=\sqrt{\tau}\left/\right.\lambda_{v}(\tau)
(see the proof of inequality (6.10) in
[
14
]
). Moreover, for this choice of
\tau_{i}
, there exist universal positive constants
\alpha_{4}(N,\sigma,b)
and
c_{4}(N,\sigma,b)
such that

(4.82)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}|v(0)|^{\sigma_{c}}\,dx\geq\frac{c_{4}}{[M(t)]^{\alpha_{4}s_{c}\sigma_{c}}}

where

(4.83)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{i}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N};\,\frac{\lambda_{v}(\tau_{i})}{[M(t)]^{\alpha_{4}}}\leq|x|\leq[M(t)]^{\alpha_{4}}\lambda_{v}(\tau_{i})\right\},

(see the proof of inequality (6.12) in
[
14
]
).

To finish the proof, let
p(t)>1
an integer such that

(4.84)

\displaystyle 10^{3}[M(t)]^{2\alpha_{4}}\leq e^{\frac{p(t)}{2}}\leq 10^{7}[M(t)]^{2\alpha_{4}}.

If
p(t)\geq\sqrt{N(t)}
, then simple computations imply (
4.79
) with
\alpha=1/4\alpha_{4}
. In the remaining case
p(t)<\sqrt{N(t)}
consider
(i,i+p(t))\in[\sqrt{N(t)},N(t)]\times[\sqrt{N(t)},N(t)]
. From(
4.81
) and (
4.84
) we get

(4.85)

\displaystyle\lambda_{v}(\tau_{i+p(t)})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{e^{\frac{i+p(t)-1}{2}}}{10L(t)}\geq\frac{10^{3}[M(t)]^{2\alpha_{4}}e^{\frac{i-1}{2}}}{10L(t)}\geq[M(t)]^{2\alpha_{4}}\frac{10e^{\frac{i}{2}}}{L(t)}\geq[M(t)]^{2\alpha_{4}}\lambda_{v}(\tau_{i}),

which implies that the annuli
\mathcal{C}_{i}
and
\mathcal{C}_{i+p(t)}
given by (
4.83
) are disjoints. In this case, for
t
close enough to
T^{\ast}
, there are at least
\frac{N(t)}{10p(t)}
disjoint annuli, thus (
4.82
) yields

(4.86)

\displaystyle\|v(0)\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}^{\sigma_{c}}\geq\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{N(t)}{10p(t)}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{kp(t)+\sqrt{N(t)}}}|v(0)|^{\sigma_{c}}\,dx\geq\frac{c_{4}}{[M(t)]^{\alpha_{4}\sigma_{c}s_{c}}}\frac{\sqrt{N(t)}}{10}=C\frac{\sqrt{N(t)}}{\|v(0)\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}^{\alpha_{4}\sigma_{c}s_{c}}},

which implies (
4.79
), finishing the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

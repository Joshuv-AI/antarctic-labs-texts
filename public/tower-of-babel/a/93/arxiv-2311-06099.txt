On the structure of flat chains with finite mass
Giovanni Alberti & Andrea Marchese

Abstract
. We prove that every flat chain with finite mass in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with coefficients in a normed abelian group G G is the restriction of a normal G G -current to a Borel set. We deduce a characterization of real flat chains with finite mass in terms of a pointwise relation between the associated measure and vector field. We also deduce that any codimension-one real flat chain with finite mass can be written as an integral of multiplicity-one rectifiable currents, without loss of mass. Given a Lipschitz homomorphism ϕ : G ~ → G \phi:\tilde{G}\to G between two groups, we then study the associated map π \pi between flat chains in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with coefficients in G ~ \tilde{G} and G G respectively. In the case G ~ = ℝ \tilde{G}=\mathbb{R} and G = 𝕊 1 G=\mathbb{S}^{1} , we prove that if ϕ \phi is surjective, so is the restriction of π \pi to the set of flat chains with finite mass of dimension 0 0 , 1 1 , d − 1 d-1 , d d .



1.
Introduction

Currents with finite mass are not geometrically significant, for instance because for any
k\leq d
there are non trivial
k
-dimensional currents in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite mass supported on a single point.
The simplest classes of currents which are geometrically significant are normal currents
and flat chains with finite mass. For example, if
\mu_{T}
is the measure associated to a
k
-dimensional
normal current
T
, see §
2.5
, then
\mu_{T}(E)=0
for every set
E
of vanishing
k
-dimensional integralgeometric measure, and the same holds for
a
k
-dimensional flat chain with finite mass, see
[
7
, Theorem 4.2.14]
.

In fact normal currents and flat-chains with finite mass share many similar properties,
and therefore it is natural to ask what are the relations between these two notions.
It is immediate to check that if
T
is a
k
-dimensional normal current, then
\rho T
is a flat chain with finite mass for every density function
\rho\in L^{1}(\mu_{T})
.
It is also easy to see that the converse of this result is true if
k=d
; namely every
d
-dimensional flat chain of finite mass in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is (represented by) an
L^{1}
function.

However, to the best of our knowledge, the converse of this result for arbitrary
dimension
k
is not available in the literature; discussing this converse
and some related questions is the purpose of this paper.

Our first result is the following theorem, which is proved in Section
3
; the
necessary definitions are recalled in Section
2
. In the sequel,
K
will denote a convex, compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with nonempty interior and
G
is a normed Abelian group.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

, and let

be such that
\mathds{M}(T)<\infty
. For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists

and a Borel set

such that

(i)

\partial T^{\prime}=0
,

(ii)

T=T^{\prime}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vrule width=0.51663pt,height=5.16663pt,depth=0.12915pt\vrule width=3.44444pt,height=0.38748pt,depth=0.12915pt}}{\mathbin{\vrule width=0.51663pt,height=5.16663pt,depth=0.12915pt\vrule width=3.44444pt,height=0.38748pt,depth=0.12915pt}}{\mathbin{\hskip 0.6458pt\vrule width=0.38748pt,height=3.61664pt,depth=0.08612pt\vrule width=2.41109pt,height=0.3014pt,depth=0.08612pt}\hskip 0.6458pt}{\mathbin{\vrule width=0.3014pt,height=2.58334pt,depth=0.04303pt\vrule width=1.72218pt,height=0.25832pt,depth=0.04303pt}}E

In particular, if

is an

Section

focuses on

flat chains with finite mass. For every

, we associate to every positive Radon measure

on

a map

.

Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure on

be a Borel

The following statements are equivalent:

\tau(x)\in V_{k}(\mu,x)
for

In

, Theorem

are used to characterize those multilinear differential operators which are closable and to derive some results concerning the structure of metric currents in the Euclidean space.

We now move our attention to codimension-one real flat chains. We deduce the following result from Theorem

. See §

for the definition of integral of measures.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
T\in\mathscr{F}_{d-1}(K)
with
\mathds{M}(T)<\infty
. Then there exist a compact interval

and for every

a multiplicity-one rectifiable current

such that the map

Remark 1.4
.

When
G
is a finite dimensional vector space, currents with coefficients in
G
can be defined by duality with certain differential forms, and a
G
-current is uniquely determined by its
components
, which are currents with real coefficients, see
[

, §4]
. Moreover it is easy to see that a
G
-current is a flat

-chain if and only if its components are real flat chains. Hence the natural generalization of Theorem

holds. Analogously, a
G
-current is rectifiable if and only if its components are rectifiable currents, hence the first part of Theorem
1.3
remains valid, even though the property

might fail, because typically the mass of a
G
-current is strictly smaller than the sum of the masses of its components.

In Section
5
, we address the following question. A surjective Lipschitz homomorphism
\phi:\tilde{G}\to G
between two groups, induces a surjective map

between flat chains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with coefficients in
\tilde{G}
and

respectively. We ask whether or not

and

. This allows us to partially extend Theorem

and Theorem

to other groups than finite dimensional vector spaces.

2.
Currents with coefficients in groups

In this section we collect the fundamental notions concerning flat chains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with coefficients in a normed Abelian group. For a thorough discussion about this topic, we refer the reader to the seminal paper
[
8
]
and to the more recent
[
6
]
.

2.1.
Polyhedral chains with coefficients in a normed group

Let
G=\left(G,+\right)
denote an Abelian group. A
norm
on
\mathrm{G}
is any function
\|\cdot\|\colon G\to\mathbb{R}

satisfying the following properties:

(i)

\|g\|\geq 0
for every
g\in G
, and
\|g\|=0
if and only if
g=0
;

(ii)

\|-g\|=\|g\|
for every
g\in G
;

(iii)

\|g+h\|\leq\|g\|+\|h\|
for every
g,h\in G
.

We will assume that
(G,\|\cdot\|)
is a complete metric space, with respect to the natural metric
{\rm d}(g,h):=\|g-h\|
for
g,h\in G
.

Let
K
be a convex, compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A
k
-dimensional
polyhedral chain
in
K
with coefficients in
G
(or simply a polyhedral
G
-chain) is a formal
finite
linear combination

P=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}g_{\ell}\llbracket\sigma_{\ell}\rrbracket,

(2.1)

where
g_{\ell}\in G
and
\llbracket\sigma_{\ell}\rrbracket
are the integral currents associated to the non-overlapping oriented
k
-simplexes
\sigma_{\ell}\subset K
. A
refinement
of
P
is any
k
-dimensional polyhedral
G
-chain of the form

\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\sum_{h=1}^{H_{\ell}}g_{\ell}^{h}\,\llbracket\sigma_{\ell}^{h}\rrbracket,

where
\sigma_{\ell}^{h}\cup\ldots\cup\sigma_{\ell}^{H_{\ell}}=\sigma_{\ell}
and
g_{\ell}^{h}=g_{\ell}
if
\sigma_{\ell}^{h}
has the same orientation of
\sigma_{\ell}
or
g_{\ell}^{h}=-g_{\ell}
otherwise. We identify two polyhedral
G
-chains if they have a common refinement.

The sum of two polyhedral
G
-chains
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
can be defined by taking refinements such that the corresponding simplexes are either non overlapping or they coincide and then by considering their formal sum, identifying
g_{1}\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket+g_{2}\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket
with
(g_{1}+g_{2})\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket
. With respect to such operation, the set of
k
-dimensional polyhedral
G
-chains in
K
is a group which will be denoted
\mathscr{P}_{k}^{G}(K)
. Given a polyhedral
G
-chain
P\in\mathscr{P}_{k}^{G}(K)
as in (
2.1
), we denote by supp
(P)
its support, that is the set
\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{N}\{\sigma_{\ell}:g_{\ell}\neq 0\}
and the

mass
of
P
is defined by

\mathds{M}(P):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\|g_{\ell}\|\mathcal{H}^{k}(\sigma_{\ell})\,.

(2.2)

2.2.
Rectifiable currents with coefficients in a normed group

The group of
k
-dimensional
Lipschitz
G
-chains
in
K
is given by

\begin{split}\mathscr{L}_{k}^{G}(K):=\bigg\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}g_{\ell}\cdot(\gamma_{\ell})_{\sharp}\llbracket\sigma_{\ell}\rrbracket\,\colon\,&\mbox{each $\sigma_{\ell}$ is an oriented $k$-simplex in $\mathbb{R}^{k}$,}\\
&\mbox{$g_{\ell}\in G$ and $\gamma_{\ell}\colon\sigma_{\ell}\to K$ is Lipschitz}\bigg\}\,,\end{split}

where
\gamma_{\sharp}
is the
push-forward
according to the Lipschitz map
\gamma
.

The mass functional can be easily extended to
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{G}(K)
. The
\mathds{M}
-completion of
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{G}(K)
is the group
\mathscr{R}_{k}^{G}(K)
of
k
-dimensional
rectifiable currents
with coefficients in
G
.

2.3.
Boundary and flat norm

If
P\in\mathscr{P}_{k}^{G}(K)
has the form (
2.1
) then the
boundary
of
P
is the
(k-1)
-dimensional polyhedral
G
-chain defined by

\partial P:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}g_{\ell}\partial\llbracket\sigma_{\ell}\rrbracket\,.

The
flat norm
of
P
is the quantity

\mathds{F}(P):=\inf\left\{\mathds{M}(Q)+\mathds{M}(P-\partial Q)\,\colon\,Q\in\mathscr{P}_{k+1}^{G}(K)\right\}\,.

2.4.
Flat
G
-chains

The
\mathds{F}
-completion of
\mathscr{P}_{k}^{G}(K)
is the group
\mathscr{F}_{k}^{G}(K)
of
k
-dimensional
flat
G
-chains
in
K
. The notion of mass extends to
\mathscr{F}_{k}^{G}(K)
by relaxation. Moreover, the flat norm of
T\in\mathscr{F}_{k}^{G}(K)
can be computed as

\mathds{F}(T)=\inf\left\{\mathds{M}(Q)+\mathds{M}(F-\partial Q)\,\colon\,Q\in\mathscr{F}^{G}_{k+1}(K)\right\}\,.

(2.3)

A flat
G
-chain
T
of finite mass such that
\partial T
has also finite mass is called a
normal current
with coefficients in
G
. We denote by
\mathscr{N}_{k}^{G}(K)
the set of
k
-dimensional normal currents in
K
with coefficients in
G
.

There is a close relationship between flat chains and rectifiable currents with coefficients in a group. Being unable to find a reference for the following elementary fact in the setting that we consider, we include the short proof.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
T\in\mathscr{F}_{k}^{G}(K)
. Then there exist
R\in{\mathscr{R}}^{G}_{k}(K)
and
S\in{\mathscr{R}}_{k+1}^{G}(K)
such that
T=R+\partial S
. In particular, if
\mathds{M}(T)<\infty
, then
\mathds{M}(\partial S)<\infty
.

Let
P_{n}
be a sequence in
\mathscr{P}_{k}^{G}(K)
such that
\mathds{F}(T-P_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\mathds{F}(P_{n+1}-P_{n})\leq 2^{-n-1}
for every
n
. For every
n
let
R_{n}\in\mathscr{P}_{k}^{G}(K)
and
S_{n}\in\mathscr{P}_{k+1}^{G}(K)
be such that

P_{n+1}-P_{n}=R_{n}+\partial S_{n}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathds{M}(R_{n})+\mathds{M}(S_{n})\leq 2\mathds{F}(P_{n+1}-P_{n})\leq 2^{-n}.

Note that the sequences
Z_{n}:=P_{1}+\sum_{h\leq n}R_{h}
and
Q_{n}:=\sum_{h\leq n}S_{h}
converge in mass; we denote the limits
R
and
S
respectively and we observe that, being limits in mass of rectifiable currents, they are also rectifiable. Since
P_{n+1}=Z_{n}+\partial Q_{n}
for every
n
, passing the equality in the limit, we conclude that
T=R+\partial S
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

It follows immediately from Proposition
2.1
that every flat
G
-chain of finite mass is rectifiable provided a
boundary rectifiablity theorem
holds. Namely, whenever all the rectifiable
G
-currents
T\in\mathscr{R}_{k+1}^{G}(K)
such that
\mathds{M}(\partial T)<\infty
also satisfy
\partial T\in\mathscr{R}_{k}^{G}(K)
, see also
[
17
]
.

The following is a well known characterization of flat chains with finite mass, see
[
7
, §4.1.17]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
T\in\mathscr{F}_{k}^{G}(K)
with
\mathds{M}(T)<\infty
. Then
T
is a limit in mass of normal currents.

If
\mathds{M}(T)<\infty
, then the current
S
in the proof of Proposition
2.1
satisfies
\mathds{M}(\partial S)<\infty
. Moreover the currents
Z_{n}
are normal and converge in mass to
R
, hence the currents
Z_{n}+\partial S
are normal and they converge in mass to
T
.
∎

Currents with coefficients in

When

, see

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We begin with an elementary approximation lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T

. For every

Let

As a guiding example, one may consider the case

5.5
) we have
P_{n}=\hat{R}_{n}+\partial\hat{S}_{n}
and by (
5.4
) and (
5.6
)

\mathds{M}(\hat{R}_{n})\leq\mathds{M}(P_{0})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathds{M}(T)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mathds{M}(T)

so that

\mathds{M}(\partial\hat{S}_{n})\leq\mathds{M}(P_{n})+\mathds{M}(\hat{R}_{n})\leq(2+2\varepsilon)\mathds{M}(T).

(5.7)

Let
\tilde{P}_{0}
and
\tilde{R}_{n}\in\mathscr{P}_{k}^{\tilde{G}}(K)
be the polyhedral
\tilde{G}
-chains defined replacing each multiplicity
g
in
P_{0}
and in
R_{n}
respectively with an element
\tilde{g}
such that
\phi(\tilde{g})=g
and
\|\tilde{g}\|\leq C\|g\|
, which exists by (
5.2
).
Observe that by (
5.4
) we have
\mathds{M}(\tilde{P}_{0})\leq C(1+\varepsilon/2)\mathds{M}(T)
and moreover by (
5.6
)

\mathds{M}(\tilde{R}_{n})\leq C\mathds{M}(R_{n})\leq C2^{-n-2}\varepsilon\mathds{M}(T).

Hence the sequence
\tilde{Z}_{n}:=\tilde{P}_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\tilde{R}_{i}
is a Cauchy sequence (in mass) so it converges to a flat chain
\tilde{Z}
. Moreover

\pi\tilde{Z}_{n}=\hat{R}_{n}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathds{M}(\tilde{Z})\leq C(1+\varepsilon)\mathds{M}(T).

(5.8)

Similarly let
\tilde{X}_{n}\in\mathscr{P}_{k}^{\tilde{G}}(K)
be defined replacing each multiplicity
g
in
\partial\hat{S}_{n}
with
\tilde{g}
as above, so that

\mathds{M}(\tilde{X}_{n})\leq C\mathds{M}(\partial\hat{S}_{n})

(5.9)

Observe that
\tilde{X}_{n}
might fail to be a boundary, but we still have

\pi(\partial\tilde{X}_{n})=\partial(\pi(\tilde{X}_{n}))=\partial(\partial\hat{S}_{n})=0.

Hence by Definition
5.3
and combining (
5.7
) and (
5.9
), there exists
\tilde{Y}_{n}\in\mathscr{P}_{k}^{\tilde{G}}(K)
with

\pi(\tilde{Y}_{n})=0,\quad\partial\tilde{Y}_{n}=\partial\tilde{X}_{n},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathds{M}(\tilde{Y}_{n})\leq D\mathds{M}(\tilde{X}_{n})\leq CD(2+2\varepsilon)\mathds{M}(T).

(5.10)

Setting
W_{n}:=\tilde{X}_{n}-\tilde{Y}_{n}
and combining (
5.7
), (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) we obtain

\partial W_{n}=0,\quad\pi(W_{n})=\partial\hat{S}_{n}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathds{M}(W_{n})\leq C(D+1)(2+2\varepsilon)\mathds{M}(T).

(5.11)

Since
G
is locally compact, there exists
W\in\mathscr{F}_{k}^{\tilde{G}}(K)
such that, up to subsequences,
\mathds{F}(W_{n}-W)\to 0
and

\mathds{M}(W)\leq C(D+1)(2+2\varepsilon)\mathds{M}(T).

(5.12)

Eventually, setting
\tilde{T}:=\tilde{Z}+\tilde{W}
and combining (
5.8
) and (
5.11
), the continuity of
\pi
wrt the flat norm and the fact that
P_{n}=\hat{R}_{n}+\partial\hat{S}_{n}
implies that
\pi(\tilde{T})=T
and moreover the estimates in (
5.8
) and (
5.12
) yield

\mathds{M}(\tilde{T})\leq C(2+2D)(1+\varepsilon)\mathds{M}(T).

∎

The next lemma provides the main tool to answer Question
5.1
for
k=d-1
. The proof uses a strategy already employed in
[
4
,
12
]
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
G
and
\tilde{G}
be as in Assumption
5.5
. Let
P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}^{G}(K)
. Then there exists
\tilde{P}\in\mathscr{P}_{d}^{\tilde{G}}(K)
such that

\pi(\tilde{P})=P,\quad\mathds{M}(\tilde{P})\leq 3\mathds{M}(P),\quad{\mbox{a}nd}\quad\mathds{M}(\partial\tilde{P})\leq 5\mathds{M}(\partial P).

Remark 5.8
.

We note that Lemma
5.7
implies the validity of Statement
\mathcal{P}_{m}
of
[
10
, §4.1]
for
m=n
with a constant
C
which is independent of
p
. This can be obtained simply dividing by
p
the multiplicities of the current
P
in such statement, so that the
{\rm mod}(p)
equivalence relation on
\mathbb{Z}
turns into the
\mod(1)
equivalent relation on
\mathbb{Z}/p\subset\mathbb{R}
. In turn, Statement
\mathcal{S}_{m}
of
[
10
, §4.1]
follows from Statement
\mathcal{P}_{m}
with the same constant, see
[
10
, Remark 4.2]
and the two statements imply positive answers to
[
10
, Question 3.5 and Question 3.7]
.

Write
P
in the form
P:=\sum_{i}g_{i}\llbracket\sigma_{i}\rrbracket
, for
g_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
as in (
2.1
) the
\sigma_{i}
being endowed with the standard orientation of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Accordingly we can write
\partial P:=\sum_{j}(g_{j}^{+}-g_{j}^{-})\llbracket S_{j}\rrbracket
, where
S_{j}
are
(d-1)
-simplexes and
g_{j}^{\pm}
is the multiplicity of the
d
-simplex
\sigma_{j}^{\pm}
appearing in the decomposition of
P
having
S_{j}
in the boundary and such that the positive orientation on
\sigma_{j}^{\pm}
induces on
S_{j}
a positive (resp. negative) orientation (we simply set
g_{j}^{\pm}=0
if there is no such
d
-simplex
\sigma_{j}^{\pm}
). Let us now fix
\theta\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})
to be chosen later. For every
g\in[0,1)
let us denote

\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}(\theta):=\begin{cases}g,&\text{if}\ g\leq\theta;\\
g-1,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(5.13)

Consider now the polyhedral
\tilde{G}
-chain
\tilde{P}:=\sum_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}\llbracket\sigma_{i}\rrbracket
. Obviously
\pi(\tilde{P})=P
. Since
\|\tilde{g}\|_{\tilde{G}}\leq 3\|g\|_{G}
for every
g\in G
, then we have

\mathds{M}(\tilde{P})\leq 3\mathds{M}(P).

Moreover, denoting
S:=\bigcup_{j}S_{j}
we have

\mathds{M}(\partial P)=\int_{S}\|g^{+}-g^{-}\|_{G}d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=\sum_{j}\|g_{j}^{+}-g_{j}^{-}\|_{G}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S_{j})

and similarly

\mathds{M}(\partial\tilde{P})=\int_{S}\|\tilde{g}^{+}-\tilde{g}^{-}\|_{\tilde{G}}d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=\sum_{j}\|\tilde{g}_{j}^{+}-\tilde{g}_{j}^{-}\|_{\tilde{G}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S_{j}).

Recalling that the definition of
\tilde{g}
(and henceforth of
\tilde{P}
) depends on
\theta
, we encode this dependence in the notations
\tilde{g}_{\theta}^{\pm}
and
\tilde{P}_{\theta}
. Integrating in
\theta\in[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}]
, we get

\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\mathds{M}(\partial\tilde{P}_{\theta})d\theta=\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\int_{S}\|\tilde{g}^{+}_{\theta}-\tilde{g}^{-}_{\theta}\|_{\tilde{G}}d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}d\theta=\int_{S}\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\|\tilde{g}^{+}_{\theta}-\tilde{g}^{-}_{\theta}\|_{\tilde{G}}d\theta d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.

Observe that if
\theta
does not belong to the open segment with extremes
g^{-}
and
g^{+}
(whose length is
\|g^{+}-g^{-}\|_{G}
), we have

\|\tilde{g}^{+}_{\theta}-\tilde{g}^{-}_{\theta}\|_{\tilde{G}}=\|g^{+}-g^{-}\|_{\tilde{G}}\leq 3\|g^{+}-g^{-}\|_{G},

and otherwise we have trivially

\|\tilde{g}^{+}_{\theta}-\tilde{g}^{-}_{\theta}\|_{\tilde{G}}\leq 1.

Hence we deduce that

\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\mathds{M}(\partial\tilde{P}_{\theta})d\theta\leq\int_{S}\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}3\|g^{+}-g^{-}\|_{G}d\theta d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}+\int_{S}\|g^{+}-g^{-}\|_{G}\cdot 1d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=\frac{5}{2}\mathds{M}(\partial P),

which implies that there exists
\theta\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})
such that
\mathds{M}(\partial\tilde{P}_{\theta})\leq 5\mathds{M}(\partial P)
.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
G
and
\tilde{G}
be as in Assumption
5.5
and let
k=d-1
. Then the answer to Question
5.1
is positive.

We aim at proving the validity of the bounded ratio property
BR(d-1,D)
of Definition
5.3
with
D=6
, which suffices to prove Theorem
5.9
thanks to Proposition
5.4
. Consider
Q\in\mathscr{P}_{d-1}^{\tilde{G}}(K)
with
\pi(\partial Q)=0
. By the cone construction, we can write
\pi(Q)=\partial S
for some
S\in\mathscr{P}_{d}^{G}(K)
with

\mathds{M}(S)\leq C(K)\mathds{M}(\pi(Q))\leq C(K)\mathds{M}(Q).

By Lemma
5.7
there exist
\tilde{S}\in\mathscr{P}_{d}^{\tilde{G}}(K)
with

\pi(\tilde{S})=S,\quad\mathds{M}(\tilde{S})\leq 3\mathds{M}(S)\leq 3C(K)\mathds{M}(Q),\quad{\mbox{a}nd}\quad\mathds{M}(\partial\tilde{S})\leq 5\mathds{M}(\partial S)\leq 5\mathds{M}(Q).

Now set
Q^{\prime}:=Q-\partial\tilde{S}
. We have
\partial Q^{\prime}=\partial Q
,

\pi(Q^{\prime})=\pi(Q)-\pi(\partial\tilde{S})=\pi(Q)-\partial S=0,

and

\mathds{M}(Q^{\prime})\leq\mathds{M}(Q)+\mathds{M}(\partial\tilde{S})=6\mathds{M}(Q).

∎

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
G
and
\tilde{G}
be as in Assumption
5.5
and let
k=1
. Then the answer to Question
5.1
is positive.

We aim at proving the validity of the bounded ratio property
BR(1,D)
of Definition
5.3
with
D=1
, which suffices to prove Theorem
5.9
thanks to Proposition
5.4
.
For every
P\in\mathscr{P}_{1}^{\tilde{G}}
as in (
2.1
), let

\mathcal{I}_{P}:=\{\ell\in\{1,\dots,N\}:\phi(g_{\ell})\neq 0_{G}\}

and denote
\hat{P}:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{P}}g_{\ell}\llbracket\sigma_{\ell}\rrbracket
. Observe that, since
\pi(\partial(Q-\hat{Q}))=0
, then
\pi(\partial\hat{Q})=0
. We claim that there exists
Z\in\mathscr{P}_{1}^{\tilde{G}}(K)
such that

\partial Z=0,\quad\mathds{M}(\hat{Q}-Z)\leq\mathds{M}(\hat{Q}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{I}_{\hat{Q}-Z}\subsetneqq\mathcal{I}_{Q}.

The validity of the claim implies the validity of the bounded ratio property
BR(1,1)
: indeed, iterating at most
N
times, we find a current
Z^{\prime}\in\mathscr{P}_{1}^{\tilde{G}}(K)
such that

\partial Z^{\prime}=0,\quad\mathds{M}(\hat{Q}-Z^{\prime})\leq\mathds{M}(\hat{Q}),\quad\mbox{and}\mathcal{I}_{\hat{Q}-Z^{\prime}}=\emptyset,

so that the current

Q^{\prime}:=Q+Z^{\prime}=(Q-\hat{Q})+(\hat{Q}-Z^{\prime})

yields

\pi(Q^{\prime})=0,\quad\partial Q^{\prime}=\partial Q,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathds{M}(Q^{\prime})\leq\mathds{M}(Q).

The proof of the claim is analogous to that of
[
5
, Lemma A.1]
. Firstly we notice that the support of
\hat{Q}
contains a loop, namely a chain of segments
\sigma_{\ell_{1}},\dots,\sigma_{\ell_{m}}
such that, up to a possible change of orientation, the second endpoint of
\sigma_{\ell_{i}}
coincides with the first endpoint of
\sigma_{\ell_{i+1}}
, for every
i=1,\dots,m-1
and the second endpoint of
\sigma_{\ell_{m}}
coincides with the first endpoint of
\sigma_{\ell_{1}}
. This is an immediate consequence of the fact that the multiplicity
\theta
of every point in the boundary of
\hat{Q}
satisfies
\phi(\theta)=0_{G}
, but for every
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{P}
we have
\phi(g_{\ell})\neq 0_{G}
. We implicitly switch the sign of
g_{\ell_{i}}
whenever in the loop described above there is change in the orientation of
\sigma_{\ell_{i}}
.
Denote

\theta_{+}:=\min_{i=1,\dots,m}\{\min\{g_{\ell_{i}}-g:g\in\phi^{-1}(0),g<g_{\ell_{i}}\}\};

\theta_{-}:=\min_{i=1,\dots,m}\{\min\{g-g_{\ell_{i}}:g\in\phi^{-1}(0),g>g_{\ell_{i}}\}\}.

Define
Z_{\pm}:=\pm\theta_{\pm}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\llbracket\sigma_{\ell_{i}}\rrbracket
. Observe that
\partial Z_{\pm}=0
and that
\mathcal{I}_{\hat{Q}-Z_{\pm}}\subsetneqq\mathcal{I}_{Q}
. Moreover the sign of the multiplicity of
\hat{Q}-Z_{\pm}
on each segment
\sigma_{\ell_{i}}
is either equal to the sign of
g_{\ell_{i}}
or zero, and this implies that

\mathds{M}(\hat{Q}-Z_{\pm})=\mathds{M}(\hat{Q})\mp\theta_{\pm}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\sigma_{\ell_{i}})\sign(g_{\ell_{i}}),

hence either
Z:=Z_{+}
or
Z:=Z_{-}
has the desired property.
∎

Fourier analysis in spaces of shifts
A. Yu. Ulitskaya

Abstract
. In this paper, we develop a continual analog of decomposition over orthogonal bases in spaces generated by equidistant shifts of a single function. By doing so, we obtain an explicit expression for best approximation by spaces of shifts in L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L_{2}(\mathbb{R}) . The result is formulated in terms of classical Fourier transform and tends to have various applications in approximation by spaces of shifts and, in particular, in spline approximation. Keywords: spaces of shifts, best approximation, Fourier transform

2.
Introduction

Spaces generated by shifts of a single function play a significant role in approximation theory, wavelets theory, and applications.
Approximative properties of such spaces are of wide interest; see, for example
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

Moreover, spaces of shifts often become optimal approximating subspaces in the sense of widths. This means that constants in estimates for approximation of certain classes of functions by spaces of shifts cannot be decreased by passing to another approximating subspace of the same dimension (or, in the infinite dimensional case, average dimension).
The most classical result on this subject is probably optimality of spline spaces for Sobolev classes of functions (see, for example,
[
4
,
5
]
). In the general situation, extremality results for approximation by spaces of shifts were obtained by Vinogradov (
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
) in periodic and non-periodic spaces
C
and
L_{1}
.

In
[
9
]
Vinogradov and Ulitskaya studied the following problem of
L_{2}
-approximation by spaces of shifts. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and periodic function
B\in L_{1}
consider the space
\mathcal{S}_{B,n}
of functions
s
that are defined on
\mathbb{R}
and representable in the form

s(x)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{2n-1}\beta_{j}B\left(x-\frac{j\pi}{n}\right).

It is easy to show that the space
\mathcal{S}_{B,n}
coincides with the linear span of the functions

\Phi_{B,l}(x)=\Phi_{B,n,l}(x)=\frac{1}{2n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{2n-1}e^{\frac{ilj\pi}{n}}B\left(x-\frac{j\pi}{n}\right),\quad l\in[1-n:n].

If the set
\{B\left(\cdot-\frac{j\pi}{n}\right)\}_{j=1-n}^{n}
is linearly independent, the functions
\Phi_{B,l}
form an orthogonal basis in the space
\mathcal{S}_{B,n}
. For
m<n
, denote by
\mathcal{S}^{\times}_{B,n,m}
the linear span of the set
\{\Phi_{B,l}\}_{l=1-m}^{m-1}
.

We find optimality criteria for approximation of Sobolev classes of periodic functions by the spaces
\mathcal{S}_{B,n}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\times}_{B,n,m}
in
L_{2}
. These criteria are given in terms of Fourier coefficients of the function
B
. The proof is based on Fourier series decomposition over orthogonal bases in spaces of shifts formed by the functions
\Phi_{B,l}
. This decomposition allows us to find an explicit expression for best approximation by
\mathcal{S}_{B,n}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\times}_{B,n,m}
. Later this technique was used in
[
10
]
to obtain a criteria of optimality of spaces under consideration for classes of periodic convolutions.

Similar approximation problems in the space
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
force us to develop an analog of this method in continual case. In this paper, we elaborate the Fourier transform method in spaces of shifts and obtain an explicit expression for best mean-square approximation by these spaces in terms of classical Fourier transform.

Let

\displaystyle\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)

\displaystyle D_{B,\sigma}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\widehat{B}(y+2\nu\sigma)\right|^{2}.

\mathbf{\Phi}
3

\Phi_{B,\sigma}\in L_{2}\left(\left[\,0,\frac{\pi}{\sigma}\right]\times[\,-\sigma,\sigma]\right)

\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma}}\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left|\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)\right|^{2}dy\,dx=\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|B\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

Since
\left\{B\left(x-\frac{j\pi}{\sigma}\right)\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
for a.e.

\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,\cdot)\in L_{2}[-\sigma,\sigma]
for a.e.

\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left|\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)\right|^{2}dy=\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|B\left(x-\frac{j\pi}{\sigma}\right)\right|^{2}.

This yields

\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma}}\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left|\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)\right|^{2}dy\,dx=\frac{1}{2\sigma}\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|B\left(x-\frac{j\pi}{\sigma}\right)\right|^{2}dx=\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|B\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

∎

\mathbf{\Phi}
6
.

The following relations hold:

\displaystyle\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y+2\sigma)=\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y),\quad\Phi_{B,\sigma}\left(x+\frac{\pi}{\sigma},y\right)=e^{i\frac{\pi}{\sigma}y}\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y),

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)}=\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,-y).

This property is clear. Moreover, it implies that the function

\mathbf{\Phi}
9

In

\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)=\sum\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{B}(y+2\nu\sigma)e^{i(y+2\nu\sigma)x}.

(3)

Proof.

First we point out that one can obtain the equality

\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma}}\left|\sum\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{B}(y+2\nu\sigma)e^{i(y+2\nu\sigma)x}\right|^{2}dx\,dy=\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|B\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})},

(4)

using the same argument as in the proof of
\Phi 1
.
Indeed, since
\left\{\widehat{B}(y+2\nu\sigma)\right\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma}}\left|\sum\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{B}(y+2\nu\sigma)e^{i(y+2\nu\sigma)x}\right|^{2}dx\,dy=\frac{\pi}{\sigma}\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\sum\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\widehat{B}(y+2\nu\sigma)\right|^{2}\,dy=

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\sigma}\|\widehat{B}\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|B\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Recall that a set
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
of elements of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is called

with constants

\beta\in\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

(5)

If we assume that only the right-hand inequality in (
5

\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}

.

\mathbf{\Phi}
12

Let
\left\{B\left(\cdot-\frac{j\pi}{\sigma}\right)\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}B(t)\overline{\Phi_{B,\sigma}(t,y)}\,dt=2\pi D_{B,\sigma}(y)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left|\,\int\limits_{-n}^{n}B(t)\overline{\sum\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{B}(y+2\nu\sigma)e^{i(y+2\nu\sigma)t}}\,dt-2\pi\sum\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\widehat{B}(y+2\nu\sigma)\right|^{2}\right|^{2}dy=

\displaystyle=4\pi^{2}\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left|\sum\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\widehat{B}(y+2\nu\sigma)}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-n}^{n}B(t)e^{-i(y+2\nu\sigma)t}\,dt-\widehat{B}(y+2\nu\sigma)\right)\right|^{2}dy.

The following theorem is an analog of the Plancherel theorem for elements of spaces of shifts.

(10)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}S(x)\overline{s(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=2\pi\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\zeta_{B,\sigma}(S,y)\overline{\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)}D_{B,\sigma}(y)\,dy.

Proof.

Let us prove (

Using (
8
) and (
9
), we derive to

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}|s(x)|^{2}dx=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}s(x)\overline{s(x)}\,dx=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{-n}^{n}\overline{s(x)}\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)\,dy\,dx=

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)\int\limits_{-n}^{n}\overline{s(x)}\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)\,dx\,dy=

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)\overline{\int\limits_{-n}^{n}s(x)\overline{\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)}\,dx}\,dy=2\pi\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\left|\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)\right|^{2}D_{B,\sigma}(y)\,dy.

Passage to the limit under the integral sign is valid by Lemma 1.

Interchanging the order of integration is justified since for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
the function

(x,y)\mapsto\overline{s(x)}\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)

is integrable on
[\,-n,n]\times[\,-\sigma,\sigma]
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-n}^{n}|s(x)|\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}|\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)|\left|\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)\right|\,dy\,dx\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}|\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)|^{2}dy\right)^{1/2}\int\limits_{-n}^{n}|s(x)|\left(\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left|\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)\right|^{2}dy\right)^{1/2}dx\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}|\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)|^{2}dy\right)^{1/2}\left(\int\limits_{-n}^{n}|s(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int\limits_{-n}^{n}\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}|\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)|^{2}dy\,dx\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

Formula (
11
) is derived from (
10
) in a standard way.
∎

The Fourier transform and the partial Fourier integral of the function
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to the system
\Phi_{B,\sigma}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\zeta_{B,\sigma}(f,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi D_{B,\sigma}(y)}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{\Phi_{B,\sigma}(t,y)}\,dt,

(12)

\displaystyle J_{B,\sigma,\rho}(f,x)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\zeta_{B,\sigma}(f,y)\Phi_{B,\sigma}(x,y)\,dy,\quad 0<\rho\leqslant\sigma.

(13)

Let us show that the integral on the right-hand side of (
12
) converges in
L_{2}[\,-\sigma,\sigma]
. It suffices to prove that the sequence

\left\{y\mapsto\int\limits_{-n}^{n}f(t)\overline{\Phi_{B,\sigma}(t,y)}\,dt\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

(14)

is a Cauchy sequence in
L_{2}[\,-\sigma,\sigma]
. Take
\varepsilon>0
. Since the sequence

\left\{y\mapsto\int\limits_{-n}^{n}f(t)e^{-iyt}\,dt\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is convergent in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists such number
N\in\mathbb{N}
that for every
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
n>k>N
, the following inequality holds:

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\;\int\limits_{[\,-n,n]\setminus[\,-k,k]}f(t)e^{-iyt}\,dt\right|^{2}dy<\frac{\varepsilon}{\|D_{B,\sigma}\|_{L_{\infty}[\,-\sigma,\sigma]}}.

Therefore, for such
n
and
k
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left|\int\limits_{-n}^{n}f(t)\overline{\Phi_{B,\sigma}(t,y)}\,dt-\int\limits_{-k}^{k}f(t)\overline{\Phi_{B,\sigma}(t,y)}\,dt\right|^{2}dy=

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left|\;\int\limits_{[\,-n,n]\setminus[\,-k,k]}f(t)\overline{\Phi_{B,\sigma}(t,y)}\,dt\right|^{2}dy=

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left|\sum\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\widehat{B}(y+2\nu\sigma)}\int\limits_{[\,-n,n]\setminus[\,-k,k]}f(t)e^{-i(y+2\nu\sigma)t}\,dt\right|^{2}dy\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant\|D_{B,\sigma}\|_{L_{\infty}[\,-\sigma,\sigma]}\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left(\sum\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\;\int\limits_{[\,-n,n]\setminus[\,-k,k]}f(t)e^{-i(y+2\nu\sigma)t}\,dt\right|^{2}\right)\,dy=

\displaystyle=\|D_{B,\sigma}\|_{L_{\infty}[\,-\sigma,\sigma]}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\;\int\limits_{[\,-n,n]\setminus[\,-k,k]}f(t)e^{-iyt}\,dt\right|^{2}dy<\varepsilon.

This means that (
14
) is a Cauchy sequence in
L_{2}[\,-\sigma,\sigma]
.

Since
1/D_{B,\sigma}\in L_{\infty}[\,-\sigma,\sigma]
, we get
\zeta_{B,\sigma}(f)\in L_{2}[\,-\sigma,\sigma]
, which implies
J_{B,\sigma,\rho}(f)\in\mathbb{S}_{B,\sigma,\rho}
and, in particular,
J_{B,\sigma,\rho}(f)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Furthermore, for
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
s\in\mathbb{S}_{B,\sigma,\rho}
, by formulas (
8
) and (
12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{s(t)}\,dt=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{-n}^{n}f(t)\overline{s(t)}\,dt=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{-n}^{n}f(t)\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\overline{\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)}\overline{\Phi_{B,\sigma}(t,y)}\,dy\,dt=

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\overline{\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)}\int\limits_{-n}^{n}f(t)\overline{\Phi_{B,\sigma}(t,y)}\,dt\,dy=

\displaystyle=2\pi\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\overline{\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)}\zeta_{B,\sigma}(f,y)D_{B,\sigma}(y)\,dy,

while the equality (
11
) yields

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}J_{B,\sigma,\rho}(f,t)\overline{s(t)}\,dt=2\pi\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\overline{\zeta_{B,\sigma}(s,y)}\zeta_{B,\sigma}(f,y)D_{B,\sigma}(y)\,dy.

This implies that
f-J_{B,\sigma,\rho}(f)\perp\mathbb{S}_{B,\sigma,\rho}
,
i.e., that
J_{B,\sigma,\rho}(f)
is an orthogonal projection of
f
on
\mathbb{S}_{B,\sigma,\rho}
. In addition,

\displaystyle 2\pi\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\left|\zeta_{B,\sigma}(f,y)\right|^{2}D_{B,\sigma}(y)\,dy=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|J_{B,\sigma,\rho}(f,x)\right|^{2}dx\leqslant\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx.

(15)

Now we provide an explicit expression of the new Fourier transform and best approximation by spaces of shifts in terms of trigonometric Fourier transform.

Theorem 2
.

If
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then

\displaystyle\zeta_{B,\sigma}(f)=\frac{1}{D_{B,\sigma}}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\widehat{B}(\cdot+2k\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\cdot+2k\sigma)\;\text{ in }\;L_{2}[\,-\sigma,\sigma],

(16)

\displaystyle E^{2}(f,\mathbb{S}_{B,\sigma,\rho})_{2}=2\pi\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{f}(y)\right|^{2}dy-\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\frac{1}{D_{B,\sigma}(y)}\left|\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\widehat{B}(y+2k\sigma)}\widehat{f}(y+2k\sigma)\right|^{2}dy\right).

(17)

Proof.

By formulas (
12
), (
3
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left|\zeta_{B,\sigma}(f,y)-\frac{1}{D_{B,\sigma}(y)}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\widehat{B}(y+2k\sigma)}\widehat{f}(y+2k\sigma)\right|^{2}dy=

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\left|\frac{1}{D_{B,\sigma}(y)}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\widehat{B}(y+2k\sigma)}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-n}^{n}f(t)e^{-i(y+2k\sigma)t}dt-\widehat{f}(y+2k\sigma)\right)\right|^{2}dy\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-n}^{n}f(t)e^{-i(y+2k\sigma)t}dt-\widehat{f}(y+2k\sigma)\right|^{2}dy=

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-n}^{n}f(t)e^{-iyt}dt-\widehat{f}(y)\right|^{2}dy=0.

Using properties of orthogonal projection, we get

E^{2}(f,\mathbb{S}_{B,\sigma,\rho})_{2}=\|f-J_{B,\sigma,\rho}f\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|f\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}-\|J_{B,\sigma,\rho}f\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

This implies, by the Plancherel theorem and formulas (
15
) and (
16
), the following equality:

\displaystyle E^{2}(f,\mathbb{S}_{B,\sigma,\rho})_{2}=2\pi\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{f}(y)\right|^{2}dy-2\pi\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\left|\zeta_{B,\sigma}(f,y)\right|^{2}D_{B,\sigma}(y)\,dy=

\displaystyle=2\pi\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{f}(y)\right|^{2}dy-\int\limits_{-\rho}^{\rho}\frac{1}{D_{B,\sigma}(y)}\left|\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\widehat{B}(y+2k\sigma)}\widehat{f}(y+2k\sigma)\right|^{2}dy\right).

∎

Factorizations of regular graphs of infinite degree
Marcin Stawiski stawiski@agh.edu.pl

Abstract
Let ℋ = ( H i : i < α ) \mathcal{H}=(H_{i}\colon i<\alpha) be an indexed family of graphs for some ordinal number α \alpha . A family 𝒢 = ( G i : i < α ) \mathcal{G}=(G_{i}\colon i<\alpha) of edge-disjoint subgraphs of a graph G G such that G i G_{i} is isomorphic to H i H_{i} for every i < α i<\alpha and ⋃ { E ⁡ ( G i ) : i < α } = E ⁡ ( G ) \bigcup\{E(G_{i})\colon i<\alpha\}=E(G) is called a ℋ \mathcal{H} -decomposition of G G . A ℋ \mathcal{H} -decomposition of G G such that every element of ℋ \mathcal{H} is a spanning subgraph of G G is called a ℋ \mathcal{H} -factorization of G G . Let κ \kappa be an infinite cardinal. Kőnig showed in 1936 that every κ \kappa -regular graph has a factorization into perfect matchings. Andersen and Thomassen proved in 1980 that every κ \kappa -regular connected graph has a κ \kappa -regular spanning tree. We generalize both these results by proving the existence of a factorization of every κ \kappa -regular graph into λ \lambda -regular subgraphs for every non-zero λ ⩽ κ \lambda\leqslant\kappa . We investigate indexed families 𝒯 = ( T i : i < κ ) \mathcal{T}=(T_{i}\colon i<\kappa) of graphs without isolated vertices such that every connected κ \kappa -regular graph has a 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -factorization. The full classification of such families is given. Keywords : infinite graphs, trees, decompositions, factorizations, packings, regular graphs, graph factors Mathematics Subject Classifications : 05C63, 05C51, 05C70, 05C05, 03E05

Introduction

The study of matchings and related notions is arguably one of the most popular topics in graph theory.
This includes matchability, factorizations, packings, and decompositions.
One of the most studied problems in this area is a 1-factorization, which is a decomposition into perfect matchings.
The most natural necessary condition for the existence of a 1-factorization of a given graph is its regularity. In the case of finite graphs this condition is far from being sufficient, even for very simple classes of graphs. The same applies for infinite locally finite graphs (graphs without vertices of infinite degree). However, it turns out that this condition is indeed sufficient in the case of non-locally-finite graphs, as it was shown by Kőnig
[
6
]
in 1936.
Similarly, Andersen and Thomassen
[
1
]
proved in 1980 the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(Andersen, Thomassen
[
1
]
)
.

If
\kappa
is an infinite cardinal, then graph
G
has a spanning
\kappa
-regular tree if and only if
G
is
\kappa
-regular.

In this paper we focus on possibly the most general decomposition properties for which the only necessary condition is the regularity of a given connected graph. We prove that regularity is a necessary and sufficient condition for non-locally-finite graphs even for the strongest of these properties.
This covers the mentioned problems such as matchability, factorizations, packings and decompositions, and generalizes the mentioned results of Kőnig, Andersen and Thomassen. Variants of well-known conjectures by Ringel
[
7
]
from 1963 and Gyárfás
[
4
]
from 1977 for non-locally-finite graphs and their further strengthenings are discussed in this paper. The proofs of all these variants for non-locally-finite graphs shall follow from the main theorem of this paper.

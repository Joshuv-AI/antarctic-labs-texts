Minimal model program for log canonical pairs on complex analytic spaces
Makoto Enokizono and Kenta Hashizume

Abstract
. We study the minimal model program for lc pairs on projective morphism between complex analytic spaces. More precisely, we generalize the results by Birkar [ B12 ] and the second author [ H20 ] to the setup by Fujino [ F22a ] .

1.
Introduction

In
[
F22a
]
, Fujino studied the minimal model theory for projective morphisms between complex analytic spaces.
More precisely, he generalized the minimal model theory for Kawamata log terminal (klt, for short) pairs by Birkar, Cascini, Hacon, and M
c
Kernan
[
BCHM10
]
to complex analytic setting.
Compared to the algebraic case
[
BCHM10
]
, the Noetherian property of the structure sheaves does not hold in general, and some fundamental notions, for example, the canonical divisor and the
\mathbb{Q}
-factoriality, is more delicate in the analytic setting.
These difficulties make the setup of the minimal model theory in the complex analytic case more complicated.
By considering the minimal model theory over a Stein neighborhood of an appropriate Stein compact set, Fujino
[
F22a
]
established the first step of the minimal model theory for projective morphisms between complex analytic spaces.
For other generalizations of the minimal model theory in the complex analytic setting, see
[
HP15
]
,
[
HP16
]
, and
[
CHP16
]
by Campana, Höring, and Peternell, and
[
LM22
]
by Lyu and Murayama.

The goal of this paper is to develop the minimal model theory for log canonical (lc, for short) pairs in the complex analytic setting.
In this paper, we will follow the setup in
[
F22a
]
.
As in
[
F22a
]
or Definition
2.1
, we say that a projective morphism
\pi\colon X\to Y
of complex analytic spaces and a compact subset
W\subset Y
satisfies the property (P) if the following conditions hold:

(P1)

X
is a normal complex variety,

(P2)

Y
is a Stein space,

(P3)

W
is a Stein compact subset of
Y
, and

(P4)

W\cap Z
has only finitely many connected components for any analytic subset
Z

which is defined over an open neighborhood of
W
.

Let us quickly explain these properties.
The property (P2) corresponds to the affine property of the base scheme in the algebraic case.
The property (P3) says that
W
has a fundamental system of Stein open neighborhood.
This property is used to shrink
Y
around
W
in each step of the minimal model program (MMP, for short) keeping (P2).
The property (P4) is used to suppose the Noetherian property of “germ of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
around
W
” as in
[
F22a
, Theorem 2.10]
and the finiteness of the relative Picard rank of
\pi
over
W
.

The following theorems are the main results of this paper.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
complex analytic spaces
are always assumed to be Hausdorff and second countable.

Analytic varieties
are reduced and irreducible complex analytic spaces.

In this section, we collect definitions and basic results used in this paper.
For the notations and definitions, see
[
F22a
]
.

A
contraction

\pi\colon X\to Y
is a projective morphism between analytic varieties such that
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
.
For any contraction
\pi\colon X\to Y
, if
X
is a normal analytic variety then
Y
is also a normal analytic variety.
For an analytic variety
X
and an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
X
, a
log resolution of

(X,D)
is a projective bimeromorphism
g\colon X^{\prime}\to X
from a non-singular analytic variety
X^{\prime}
such that the exceptional locus
{\rm Ex}(g)
of
g
is pure codimension one and
{\rm Ex}(g)\cup{\rm Supp}\,g_{*}^{-1}D
is a simple normal crossing divisor.

Let
X
be a complex analytic space.
A subset
S
of
X
is said to be
analytically meagre
if
S\subset\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}Y_{n}
, where each
Y_{n}
is a locally closed analytic subset of
X
such that
{\rm codim}_{X}(Y_{n})\geq 1
.
An
analytically sufficiently general point of
X
is a point which is not contained in an analytically meagre subset.
Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism of analytic spaces.
Then an
analytically sufficiently general fiber of
f
is the inverse image of an analytically sufficiently general point of
Y
.

Definition 2.1

(Property (P), see
[
F22a
]
)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism of complex analytic spaces, and let
W\subset Y
be a compact subset.
In this paper, we will use the following conditions:

(P1)

X
is a normal complex variety,

(P2)

Y
is a Stein space,

(P3)

W
is a Stein compact subset of
Y
, and

(P4)

W\cap Z
has only finitely many connected components for any analytic subset
Z

which is defined over an open neighborhood of
W
.

We say that
\pi\colon X\to Y
and
W\subset Y
satisfy (P)
if the conditions (P1)–(P4) hold.

Complex analytic space

We collect basic facts on complex analytic varieties.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a proper bimeromorphism between normal analytic varieties.
Then
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
and there exists an Zariski open subset
U\subset X
over which
f
is a biholomorphism and
{\rm codim}_{X}(X\setminus U)\geq 2
.

Proof.

It is well known.
∎

Theorem 2.3

(cf.
[
BM97
, Section 13]
)
.

Let
X
be an analytic variety and let
\mathcal{I}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
.
Then there exists a projective bimeromorphism
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
from a non-singular analytic variety
X^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{I}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}
is an invertible sheaf on
X^{\prime}
and there exists an effective
f
-exceptional Cartier divisor
E^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
such that
{\rm Supp}\,E^{\prime}={\rm Ex}(f)
and
-E^{\prime}
is
f
-ample.

Remark 2.4
.

The resolution

in Theorem
2.3
is not necessarily a finite sequence of blow-ups.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a contraction from a normal analytic variety to an analytic space.
Then
\pi^{-1}(U)
is a normal variety for any connected open subset
U\subset Y
.

Proof.

Fix a connected open subset
U\subset Y
and we put
X_{U}=\pi^{-1}(U)
.
For any point
x\in X
, the stalk
\mathcal{O}_{X,\,x}
is an integral domain.
Hence
\mathcal{O}_{X_{U},\,x^{\prime}}
is an integral domain for any point
x^{\prime}\in X_{U}
.
Therefore
X_{U}
is a disjoint union of normal varieties.
If there are at least two connected components, then we can write
X_{U}=X^{\prime}_{U}\amalg X^{\prime\prime}_{U}
for some closed subsets
X^{\prime}_{U}
and
X^{\prime\prime}_{U}
of
X_{U}
.
Since
\pi|_{X_{U}}
is proper,
\pi(X^{\prime}_{U})
and
\pi(X^{\prime\prime}_{U})
are closed subsets of
U
and

U=\pi(X^{\prime}_{U})\cup\pi(X^{\prime\prime}_{U})
.
Since
U
is connected, there exists a point
y\in\pi(X^{\prime}_{U})\cap\pi(X^{\prime\prime}_{U})
.
Then
f^{-1}(y)=(f^{-1}(y)\cap X^{\prime}_{U})\amalg(f^{-1}(y)\cap X^{\prime\prime}_{U})
, which contradicts the fact that
\pi
is a contraction.
Thus
X_{U}
is a normal variety.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism from a normal analytic variety
X
to an analytic space
Y
, and let
W\subset Y
be a compact subset such that
W\cap Z
has only finitely many connected components for any analytic subset
Z
which is defined over an open neighborhood of
W
(see also the condition
({\rm P}4)
in
[
F22a
]
).
Then there exists an open subset
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
containing
W
such that if
\pi^{-1}(Y^{\prime})=\coprod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is the decomposition of
\pi^{-1}(Y^{\prime})
into connected components
X^{\prime}_{\lambda}
, then there are only finitely many connected components, which we denote by
X^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\,X^{\prime}_{l}
, such that their images on
Y
by
\pi
intersect
W
.
Furthermore, these
X^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\,X^{\prime}_{l}
satisfy the following properties.

•

X^{\prime}_{i}
is a normal complex variety for all
1\leq i\leq l
, and

•

for any
1\leq i\leq l
and open subset
U\subset Y^{\prime}
containing
W
, there exists a unique connected component
X_{U}^{(i)}
of
\pi^{-1}(U)\cap X^{\prime}_{i}
such that
\pi(X_{U}^{(i)})\cap W\neq\emptyset
, and furthermore,
X_{U}^{(i)}
is a normal analytic variety.

Proof.

We may assume
\pi(X)\cap W\neq\emptyset
because otherwise we may set
Y^{\prime}=Y\setminus\pi(X)
.
Let
\widetilde{\pi}\colon X\to\widetilde{Y}
be the Stein factorization of
\pi
, and let
f\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
be the induced finite morphism.
We set
\widetilde{W}=f^{-1}(W)
.
Then
\widetilde{W}
is compact, and
[
F22a
, Theorem 2.13]
shows that
\widetilde{W}\cap\widetilde{Z}
has only finitely many connected components for any analytic subset
\widetilde{Z}
which is defined over an open neighborhood of
\widetilde{W}
.
In particular,
\widetilde{W}
has only finitely many connected components
\widetilde{W}_{1},\,\cdots,\,\widetilde{W}_{l}
.
Then we can take open subsets
\widetilde{Y}_{1},\,\cdots,\,\widetilde{Y}_{l}
of
\widetilde{Y}
such that
\widetilde{Y}_{i}\supset\widetilde{W}_{i}
for every
i
and
\widetilde{Y}_{i}\cap\widetilde{Y}_{i^{\prime}}=\emptyset
for any indices
i
and
i^{\prime}
.
Let
Y^{\prime}
be the complement of
f(Y\setminus\coprod_{i=1}^{l}\widetilde{Y}_{i})
.
Since
f
is finite,
Y^{\prime}
is open, and
Y^{\prime}\supset W
.

We check that this
Y^{\prime}
satisfies the condition of Lemma
2.6
.
By construction, we have

\coprod_{i=1}^{l}\widetilde{W}_{i}=\widetilde{W}\subset f^{-1}(Y^{\prime})\subset\coprod_{i=1}^{l}\widetilde{Y}_{i}.

By the connectedness of each
\widetilde{W}_{i}
and considering the decomposition
f^{-1}(Y^{\prime})=\coprod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\widetilde{Y}^{\prime}_{\gamma}
of
f^{-1}(Y^{\prime})
into connected components, we can find connected components
\widetilde{Y}^{\prime}_{1},\,\cdots,\,\widetilde{Y}^{\prime}_{l}
such that
\widetilde{W}_{i}\subset\widetilde{Y}^{\prime}_{i}\subset\widetilde{Y}_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq l
, and the other connected components of
f^{-1}(Y^{\prime})
cannot intersect
\widetilde{W}
.
For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we put
X^{\prime}_{\gamma}=\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\widetilde{Y}^{\prime}_{\gamma})
.
Then

X^{\prime}:=\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(f^{-1}(Y^{\prime}))=\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}\left(\coprod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\widetilde{Y}^{\prime}_{\gamma}\right)=\coprod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}X^{\prime}_{\gamma}.

Since
\widetilde{\pi}\colon X\to\widetilde{Y}
is a contraction, all
X^{\prime}_{\gamma}
are connected.
Therefore,
X^{\prime}=\coprod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}X^{\prime}_{\gamma}
is the decomposition of
X^{\prime}
into connected component, and a connected component
X^{\prime}_{\gamma}
of
X^{\prime}
satisfying
\pi(X^{\prime}_{\gamma})\cap W\neq\emptyset
is one of
X^{\prime}_{1},\,\cdots,\,X^{\prime}_{l}
.

To finish the proof, it is sufficient to prove that
X^{\prime}_{1},\,\cdots,\,X^{\prime}_{l}
satisfy the two properties of Lemma
2.6
.
By applying Lemma
2.5
to
\widetilde{\pi}\colon X\to\widetilde{Y}
and
\widetilde{Y}^{\prime}_{1},\,\cdots,\,\widetilde{Y}^{\prime}_{l}
, it follows that
X^{\prime}_{1},\,\cdots,\,X^{\prime}_{l}
are normal analytic varieties.
Therefore the first assertion of Lemma
2.6
holds.
To prove the second assertion of Lemma
2.6
, we take an open subset
U\subset Y^{\prime}
containing
W
.
Then

\coprod_{i=1}^{l}\widetilde{W}_{i}=\widetilde{W}\subset f^{-1}(U)\subset\coprod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\widetilde{Y}_{\gamma}.

By considering the decomposition of
f^{-1}(U)
into connected components, we may find connected components
\widetilde{U}_{1},\,\cdots,\,\widetilde{U}_{l}
of
f^{-1}(U)
such that
\widetilde{W}_{i}\subset\widetilde{U}_{i}\subset\widetilde{Y}^{\prime}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq l
, and the other connected components of
f^{-1}(U)
cannot intersect
\widetilde{W}
.
For each

X_{U}^{(i)}=\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\widetilde{U}_{i})

By the same argument as in the case of

are normal analytic varieties and connected components of

, and each

is the unique connected component of
\pi^{-1}(U)\cap X^{\prime}_{i}

\pi(X_{U}^{(i)})\cap W\neq\emptyset

2.2.
Stable base locus

We collect basic results on stable base loci of
\mathbb{R}
-divisors.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism from a normal analytic variety
X
to a Stein space
Y
.
Let
D
be a Cartier divisor on
X
.
Then

\bigcap_{D\sim E\geq 0}{\rm Supp}\,E={\rm Supp}\,\bigl({\rm Coker}(\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\to\mathcal{O}_{X})\bigr)

set-theoretically.
The left hand hand side is denoted by
{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|
.

Proof.

Pick
x\in X
.
We put

T:={\rm Supp}\,\bigl({\rm Coker}(\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\to\mathcal{O}_{X})\bigr).

If
x\not\in{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|
, then there is an effective divisor
E\sim D
such that
x\not\in{\rm Supp}\,E
.
Taking
\phi\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
corresponding to
E
, then we can easily check
\phi\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)_{x}=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
.
Therefore,
x\not\in T
.
From this, we have

{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|\supset T
.

If
x\not\in T
, then there exist an open subset
U\subset Y
and an element
\psi\in\Gamma(U,\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
such that
{\rm Supp}\,({\rm div}(\psi)+D|_{\pi^{-1}(U)})\not\ni x
.
Since
Y
is Stein,
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is generated by global sections, in other words,
\Gamma(Y,\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))\cdot\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}=(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))_{y}
for any
y\in Y
.
By considering the case
y=f(x)
, we can find an open subset
V\subset U
and elements
\varphi\in\Gamma(Y,\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
and
\tau\in\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
such that
\psi|_{V}=\varphi|_{V}\cdot\tau
.
We put

E^{\prime}:={\rm div}(\varphi)+D.

Then
E^{\prime}\geq 0
.
We also have
{\rm Supp}\,E^{\prime}\not\ni x
because

E^{\prime}|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}=({\rm div}(\varphi)+D)|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}\leq{\rm div}(\varphi|_{V}\cdot\pi^{*}\tau)+D|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}={\rm div}(\psi|_{V})+D|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}

and
{\rm Supp}\,({\rm div}(\psi)+D|_{\pi^{-1}(U)})\not\ni x
.
From this, we have
x\not\in{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|
.
Hence
{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|\subset T
.

By the above argument, we have
{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|=T
, which we wanted to prove.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism from a normal analytic variety
X
to a Stein space
Y
.
Let
D
be a globally
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
.
We put

|D|_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\{E\,|\,D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}E\geq 0\},\quad{\rm and}\quad|D/Y|_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\{E\,|\,D\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\,Y}E\geq 0\}.

The notations are different from
[
F22a
, Section 10]
.
Then we have

\begin{split}{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\mathbb{Q}}=&{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D/Y|_{\mathbb{Q}}\\
=&\bigcap_{mD\,:\,{\rm Cartier}}{\rm Supp}\,\bigl({\rm Coker}(\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-mD)\to\mathcal{O}_{X})\bigr)\end{split}

set-theoretically.

Proof.

For each
m>0
such that
mD
is Cartier, we put

T_{m}:={\rm Supp}\,\bigl({\rm Coker}(\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-mD)\to\mathcal{O}_{X})\bigr).

By Lemma
2.7
, we have

\bigcap_{mD\,:\,{\rm Cartier}}T_{m}=\bigcap_{mD\,:\,{\rm Cartier}}{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|mD|,

and we can easily check that the right hand side is equal to
{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
It is also clear that
{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\mathbb{Q}}\supset{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D/Y|_{\mathbb{Q}}
since we have
|D|_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset|D/Y|_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Therefore, we only need to prove
{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D/Y|_{\mathbb{Q}}\supset\bigcap_{m}T_{m}
.
Pick a point
x\not\in{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D/Y|_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Then there exist an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
E
on
X
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
G
on
Y
such that
D+\pi^{*}G\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}E
and
{\rm Supp}\,E\not\ni x
.
Let
U\subset Y
be an open subset such that
U\ni x
and some multiple of
G
is a principal divisor on
U
.
Such
U
exists since
G
is globally
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier on any relatively compact open subset.
Then there exist a positive integer
l
, a meromorphic function
\phi
on
X
, and a meromorphic function
\psi
on
Y
such that
lD
is Cartier,
lG|_{U}={\rm div}(\psi)
, and

(lD+{\rm div}(\phi\cdot\pi^{*}\psi))|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}=lE|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}.

This implies
(\phi\cdot\pi^{*}\psi))|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}\in\Gamma(U,\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(lD))
.
Therefore, we see that
x\not\in T_{l}
.
From this fact, we have
{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D/Y|_{\mathbb{Q}}\supset\bigcap_{m}T_{m}
.
Now we have

\bigcap_{mD\,:\,{\rm Cartier}}T_{m}={\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\mathbb{Q}}\supset{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D/Y|_{\mathbb{Q}}\supset\bigcap_{mD\,:\,{\rm Cartier}}T_{m},

and therefore Proposition
2.8
holds.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism from a normal analytic variety
X
to a Stein space
Y
.
Let
D
be a globally
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
.
We set

|D|_{\mathbb{R}}:=\{E\,|\,D\sim_{\mathbb{R}}E\geq 0\},\quad{\rm and}\quad|D/Y|_{\mathbb{R}}:=\{E\,|\,D\sim_{\mathbb{R},\,Y}E\geq 0\}.

The notations are different from
[
F22a
, Section 10]
.
Then, for any non-empty open subset
U\subset Y
, we have

({\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\mathbb{R}})|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}={\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}|_{\mathbb{R}}=({\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D/Y|_{\mathbb{R}})|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}={\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}/U|_{\mathbb{R}}

set-theoretically.

Proof.

Let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{p}r_{i}D_{i}
be the decomposition of
D
into Cartier divisors on
X
.
It is easy to check that we only need to prove that
({\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\mathbb{R}})|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}\subset{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}/U|_{\mathbb{R}}
.
We denote the morphism
\pi^{-1}(U)\to U
by
\pi_{U}
.

Fix a point
x\in\pi^{-1}(U)
that is not contained in
{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}/U|_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then there exist finitely many real numbers
s_{1},\,\cdots,\,s_{m}
, bimeromorphic functions
\phi_{1},\cdots,\,\phi_{m}
on
U
, an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
G
on
U
, and an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
E
on
\pi^{-1}(U)
such that

D|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}+\pi^{*}_{U}G+\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_{j}\cdot{\rm div}(\pi^{*}_{U}\phi_{j})=E

and
{\rm Supp}\,E\not\ni x
.
Let
V\subset U
be a relatively compact open subset such that
V\ni f(x)
.
Then we can write
G|_{V}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}G_{k}
, where
G_{k}
are Cartier divisors on
V
.
By shrinking
V
around
x
if necessary, we may assume that
G_{k}
are principal divisors, which we denote by
{\rm div}(\gamma_{k})
, for all
1\leq k\leq n
.
Now we have

\sum_{i=1}^{p}r_{i}D_{i}|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}\cdot{\rm div}(\pi^{*}_{V}\gamma_{k})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_{j}\cdot{\rm div}(\pi^{*}_{U}\phi_{j})|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}=E|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}.

Since
V
is relatively compact,
D_{i}|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}
,
{\rm div}(\pi^{*}_{V}\gamma_{k})
,
{\rm div}(\pi^{*}_{U}\phi_{j})|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}
, and
E|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}
have only finitely many components.
By the argument from convex geometry, we can find finitely many positive real numbers

r^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,r^{\prime}_{q}
,
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors
D^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,D^{\prime}_{q}
on
X
that are finite
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combinations of
D_{1},\cdots,\,D_{p}
, and an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
E^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,E^{\prime}_{q}
on
\pi^{-1}(V)
such that

•

\sum_{l=1}^{q}r^{\prime}_{l}=1
and
\sum_{l=1}^{q}r^{\prime}_{l}D^{\prime}_{l}=D
,

•

{\rm Supp}\,E^{\prime}_{l}={\rm Supp}\,E|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}
for all
1\leq l\leq p
and
\sum_{l=1}^{q}r^{\prime}_{l}E^{\prime}_{l}=E|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}
, and

•

D^{\prime}_{l}|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}E^{\prime}_{l}
.

By the second property, it follows that
{\rm Supp}\,E^{\prime}_{l}\not\ni x
.
By this fact and the third property, there exists a positive integer
I
such that all
ID_{l}
are Cartier and

x\not\in{\rm Supp}\,\bigl({\rm Coker}(\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(ID^{\prime}_{l})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-ID^{\prime}_{l})\to\mathcal{O}_{X})\bigr)

for all
1\leq l\leq q
.
By Proposition
2.8
, we have
x\not\in{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D_{l}|_{\mathbb{Q}}
for all
1\leq l\leq p
.
Since
r_{1},\cdots,\,r_{q}
are positive real numbers and
\sum_{l=1}^{q}r^{\prime}_{l}D^{\prime}_{l}=D
, which is the first property stated above, we have
x\not\in{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\mathbb{R}}
.
This shows
x\not\in({\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\mathbb{R}})|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}
.

By the above argument, for any
x\not\in{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}/U|_{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
x\not\in({\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\mathbb{R}})|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}
.
Therefore we have
({\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\mathbb{R}})|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}\subset{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}/U|_{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

2.3.
Iitaka fibration

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism from a non-singular analytic variety
X
to an analytic space
Y
, and let
W\subset Y
be a compact subset.
Let
D
be an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
such that
D\sim_{\mathbb{R},\,Y}E
for some effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
E
on
X
.
For each
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we consider the ideal sheaf

\mathcal{I}_{l}:={\rm Im}\bigl(\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lfloor lE\rfloor)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\lfloor lE\rfloor)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\bigr)\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}.

There exists a resolution
f_{l}\colon X^{\prime}_{l}\to X
of
X
such that
\mathcal{I}_{l}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{l}}
is an invertible sheaf on
X^{\prime}_{l}
.
We may write
\mathcal{I}_{l}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{l}}=\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{l}}(-F^{\prime}_{l})
with an effective Cartier divisor
F^{\prime}_{l}
on
X^{\prime}_{l}
.
We put
M^{\prime}_{l}:=f_{l}^{*}\lfloor lE\rfloor-F^{\prime}_{l}
.
Then there is a natural isomorphism

(\pi\circ f_{l})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{l}}(M^{\prime}_{l})\simeq\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lfloor lE\rfloor)

and the morphism

(\pi\circ f_{l})^{*}(\pi\circ f_{l})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{l}}(M^{\prime}_{l})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{l}}(-M^{\prime}_{l})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{l}}

is surjective, in other words,
M^{\prime}_{l}
is globally generated over
Y
.
Hence we can construct a contraction
\phi_{l}\colon X^{\prime}_{l}\to Z_{l}
over
Y
between normal analytic varieties and a Cartier divisor
H_{l}
on
Z_{l}
such that
Z_{l}
is projective over
Y
,
H_{l}
is very ample over
Y
, and
M_{l}\sim\phi_{l}^{*}H_{l}
.
We take
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
{\rm dim}\,Z_{m}
takes the maximum among the dimension of
Z_{l}
varying
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. We call the meromorphic map
X\dashrightarrow Z:=Z_{m}
an
Iitaka fibration over
Y
associated to
D
.
Note that
Z
is not unique, but
Z
is determined up to bimeromorhic equivalence.

Let
X\to Y^{\prime}
be the Stein factorization of
\pi\colon X\to Y
.
Then the induced meromorphic map
Z\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
is a contraction and for any analytically sufficiently general point
p\in Y^{\prime}
, the map
X_{p}\dashrightarrow Z_{p}
of the fibers over
p
is the usual Iitaka fibration associated to
D|_{X_{p}}
.

2.4.
Negativity lemma

In this subsection, we prove variants of negativity lemma.

Definition 2.10

(Movable divisor)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism from a normal analytic variety to an analytic space, and let
U\subset Y
be an open subset.
We say that an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
D
on
X
is
movable over
U
if any irreducible component of
{\boldsymbol{\rm Bs}}|D|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}/U|_{\mathbb{R}}
has codimension at least two.
Let
W\subset Y
be a subset.
We say that an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
D
on
X
is
movable over a neighborhood of
W
if
D
is movable over an open subset
V
containing
W
.

Definition 2.11

(Very exceptional divisor,
[
B12
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a contraction of normal analytic varieties.
We say that an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
X
is
very exceptional over
Y
if
D
is vertical and for any prime divisor
Q
on
Y
, there is a prime divisor
P
on
X
such that
\pi(P)=Q
and
P
is not a component of
D
.

Remark 2.12
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a contraction of normal analytic varieties and
D
a very exceptional
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
.
Let
U\subset Y
be an connected open subset, and we put
X_{U}=\pi^{-1}(U)
,
D_{U}=D|_{X_{U}}
, and
\pi_{U}=\pi|_{X_{U}}\colon X_{U}\to U
.
Then
D_{U}
is very exceptional over
U
.
Indeed, picking any prime divisor
Q^{\prime}
on
U
, it is sufficient to find a prime divisor
P^{\prime}
on
X_{U}
such that
\pi_{U}(P^{\prime})=Q^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime}
is not a component of
D_{U}
.
We may assume the existence of a prime divisor
\tilde{P}\subset{\rm Supp}\,D_{U}
such that
\pi_{U}(\tilde{P})=Q^{\prime}
because otherwise the existence of such
P^{\prime}
as above is obvious.
Then
Q^{\prime}
is an irreducible component of
\pi({\rm Supp}\,D)|_{U}
.
In particular, there is a prime divisor
Q_{0}
on
Y
such that
Q^{\prime}
is an irreducible comonent of
Q_{0}|_{U}
.
Since
D
is very exceptional over
Y
, we can find a prime divisor
P_{0}
on
X
such that
\pi(P_{0})=Q_{0}
and
P_{0}
is not a component of
D
.
Then
P_{0}|_{X_{U}}\not\subset D_{U}
over general points of
Q^{\prime}
, and therefore we can find a prime divisor

on
X_{U}
such that
\pi_{U}(P^{\prime})=Q^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime}
is not a component of
D_{U}
.
Hence, we see that
D_{U}
is very exceptional over
U
.

Example 2.13
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a contraction of normal analytic varieties, and let
D
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
.
If
\pi({\rm Supp}\,D)
has codimension at least two in
Y
, then
D
is very exceptional over
Y
.
In particular, any exceptional divisors in bimeromorphisms are also very exceptional divisors.

Lemma 2.14

(cf.
[
B12
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a contraction of normal analytic varieties.
Let
D=E-F
be an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
, where
E
and
F
are effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisors having no common components.
Suppose that
E
is very exceptional over
Y
and there is an analytically meagre subset
S\subset{\rm Supp}\,E
such that for any curve
C\subset{\rm Supp}\,E
contained in a fiber of
\pi
, if
C\not\subset S
then
(D\cdot C)\geq 0
.
Then
-D\geq 0
.

Proof.

We will get a contradiction by assuming
E\neq 0
.
By taking a Stein factorization of
\pi
, we may assume that
\pi
is a contraction.
By shrinking
Y
around a point, we may assume that
Y
is Stein.

Step 1
.

In this step, we reduce the problem to the case
{\rm dim}\,\pi({\rm Supp}\,E)=0
.

If
{\rm dim}\,\pi({\rm Supp}\,E)>0
, we take a sufficiently general hyperplane section
H
of
Y
.
Then
H
and
\pi^{-1}(H)
are normal analytic varieties (see, for example,
[
M82
, (II.5) Theorem]
), and
\pi^{-1}(H)\to H
is a contraction.
It also follows that
E|_{\pi^{-1}(H)}
is very exceptional over
H
.
It is easy to check that
S\cap\pi^{-1}(H)
is an analytically meagre subset of
{\rm Supp}\,E|_{\pi^{-1}(H)}
, and for any curve
C^{\prime}\subset{\rm Supp}\,E|_{\pi^{-1}(H)}
contained in a fiber of
\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(H)}
, if
C^{\prime}\not\subset S\cap\pi^{-1}(H)
then
(D|_{\pi^{-1}(H)}\cdot C^{\prime})\geq 0
.
Therefore, it is sufficient to get a contradiction after replacing
\pi\colon X\to Y
,
D
, and
S
with
\pi^{-1}(H)\to H
,
D|_{\pi^{-1}(H)}
, and
S\cap\pi^{-1}(H)
respectively.

Repeating this discussion, we may assume
{\rm dim}\,\pi({\rm Supp}\,E)=0
.

Step 2
.

In this step, we treat the case
{\rm dim}\,Y\geq 2
.

We set
d:={\rm dim}\,X
.
We take a sufficiently general hyperplane sections
A_{1},\,\cdots,A_{d-2}
of
X
.
We put
X^{\prime}=A_{1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{d-2}
, and let
\pi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
be the Stein factorization of
X^{\prime}\hookrightarrow X\to Y
.
Then
\pi^{\prime}
is a bimeromorphism between normal analytic surfaces.
We put
D^{\prime}=D|_{X^{\prime}}
,
E^{\prime}=E|_{X^{\prime}}
, and
F^{\prime}=F|_{X^{\prime}}
.
Then
E^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime}
are the effective and the negative part of
D^{\prime}
, respectively, and
E^{\prime}
is
\pi^{\prime}
-exceptional since
{\rm dim}\,\pi({\rm Supp}\,E)=0
.
Moreover,
S\cap X^{\prime}
is a union of points, and we have
(D^{\prime}\cdot E_{i})\geq 0
for every component
E_{i}
of
E^{\prime}
.
This contradicts the analytic analog of Hodge index theorem
[
G62
, p367]
.

We finish the case
{\rm dim}\,Y\geq 2
.

Step 3
.

In this step, we treat the case
{\rm dim}\,Y=1
.

In this case,
Y
is a non-singular curve.
We put
y:=\pi({\rm Supp}\,E)
, and we define

t:={\rm inf}\{u\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}|-D+u\pi^{*}y\geq 0\}.

Then
-D+t\pi^{*}y\geq 0
, and there exist a component
P
of
E
and a component
Q
of
\pi^{*}y
such that
P\cap Q\neq\emptyset
,
{\rm coeff}_{P}(-D+t\pi^{*}y)=0
, and
{\rm coeff}_{Q}(-D+t\pi^{*}y)>0
.
By cutting out
P
with sufficiently general hyperplane sections, we can find a curve
C\subset P
such that

(-D+t\pi^{*}y)\cdot C=((F-E+t\pi^{*}y)\cdot C)>0.

Now recall the hypothesis that for any curve
C\subset{\rm Supp}\,E
contained in a fiber of
\pi
, if
C\not\subset S
then
(D\cdot C)\geq 0
.
Since
\pi(C)
is a point and
C
passes through a very general point, we have

(-D+t\pi^{*}y)\cdot C=(-D\cdot C)\leq 0.

Thus, we get a contradiction.

By the above argument, we get a contradiction.
Therefore, Lemma
2.14
holds.
∎

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a contraction of normal analytic varieties and

a subset.
Let

be an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on

, where

and

are effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisors having no common components.

is very exceptional over

and there exist an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor

on

and a sequence of non-negative real numbers

∎

2.5.
Singularities of pairs

In this subsection, we define singularities of pairs.
We recommend the reader to read
[
F22a
, Section 3]
for basic properties of pairs.

Let
X
be a normal analytic variety.
The
canonical sheaf

\omega_{X}
of
X
is the unique reflexive sheaf whose restriction to the non-singular locus
X_{\rm sm}
is isomorphic to
\Omega_{X_{\rm sm}}^{{\rm dim}X}
.
Let
\Delta
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
.
We say that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier at
x\in X
if there exist an open neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x
and a Weil divisor
K_{U_{x}}
on
U_{x}
with
\mathcal{O}_{U_{x}}(K_{U_{x}})\simeq\omega_{X}|_{U_{x}}
such that
K_{U_{x}}+\Delta|_{U_{x}}
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier at
x
.
We simply say that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier when
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier at any point
x\in X
.
Unfortunately, we can not define
K_{X}
globally with
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})\simeq\omega_{X}
.
It only exists locally on
X
.
However, we use the symbol
K_{X}
as a formal divisor class with
an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})\simeq\omega_{X}
and call it the
canonical divisor
of
X
if there is no danger of confusion.

A
pair

(X,\Delta)
consists of a normal analytic variety
X
and an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
\Delta
on
X
such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.
Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair.
Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a proper bimeromorphism from a normal analytic variety
Y
.
We take a Stein open subset
U
of
X
where
K_{U}+\Delta|_{U}
is a well defined
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
U
.
Then we
can define
K_{f^{-1}(U)}
and
K_{U}
such that
f_{*}K_{f^{-1}(U)}=K_{U}
, and we can write

K_{f^{-1}(U)}=f^{*}(K_{U}+\Delta|_{U})+E_{U}

as usual.
Note that
E_{U}
is a well defined
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
f^{-1}(U)
such that
f_{*}E_{U}=-\Delta|_{U}
.
Then we have

K_{Y}=f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)+\sum_{E}a(E,X,\Delta)E

such that
\bigl(\sum_{E}a(E,X,\Delta)E\bigr)|_{U}=E_{U}
.
We note that
\sum_{E}a(E,X,\Delta)E
is a globally well defined although
K_{X}
and
K_{Y}
are well defined only locally.

A pair
(X,\Delta)

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be an lc pair and

a compact subset.
Then there are only finitely many lc centers of

Proof.

Let

be a log resolution of

.
We can write

2.7.
Bimeromorphic map

The following lemma is well known to experts but useful to discuss steps of MMP.

Lemma 2.25
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
and
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y
be projective morphisms from normal analytic varieties to an analytic space
Y
, and let
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
be a bimeromorphic contraction over
Y
.
Let
D
be an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
such that
-D
is ample over
Y
.
Suppose that
\phi_{*}D
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier and ample over
Y
.
Let
g\colon W\to X
and
g^{\prime}\colon W\to X^{\prime}
be the normalization of the graph of
\phi
.
We put

E:=g^{*}D-g^{\prime*}(\phi_{*}D).

Then
E
is effective and
{\rm Supp}\,E={\rm Ex}(g)\cup{\rm Ex}(g^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Note that
g
and
g^{\prime}
are bimeromorphisms.
The effectivity of
E
follows from the negativity lemma (Corollary
2.16
).
We also have
{\rm Supp}\,E\subset{\rm Ex}(g)\cup{\rm Ex}(g^{\prime})
.
Therefore, it is sufficient to prove
{\rm Supp}\,E\supset{\rm Ex}(g)\cup{\rm Ex}(g^{\prime})
.

Shrinking
Y
if necessary, we may assume that
Y
is a Stein space.
By Theorem
2.2
, all fibers of
g
and
g^{\prime}
are connected.
Pick any
x\in g({\rm Ex}(g))
.
Then there exists a Zariski open subset
U
of
g^{-1}(x)
such that for any
u\in U
, by cutting out by hyperplane sections on
W
passing through
u
, we can find a curve
C\subset g^{-1}(x)
such that
C\ni u
and
g^{\prime}(C)
is not a point.
Here, we used Bertini type theorem for Cartier divisors on
W
which are very ample over
Y
.
Then

(E\cdot C)=\bigl(g^{*}D-g^{\prime*}(\phi_{*}D)\bigr)\cdot C=-(\phi_{*}D\cdot g^{\prime}(C))<0.

Therefore
{\rm Supp}\,E\ni u
.
Then
{\rm Supp}\,E\supset U
.
Since
{\rm Supp}\,E\cap g^{-1}(x)
is closed in
g^{-1}(x)
, we have
{\rm Supp}\,E\supset g^{-1}(x)
.
Thus,
{\rm Supp}\,E\supset{\rm Ex}(g)
.
Similarly, we have
{\rm Supp}\,E\supset{\rm Ex}(g^{\prime})
.
From these facts, we have
{\rm Supp}\,E\supset{\rm Ex}(g)\cup{\rm Ex}(g^{\prime})
.
∎

Definition 4.1

\begin{split}\sigma_{P}(mD;X/Y)_{\mathbb{Z}}=&{\rm inf}\{{\rm coeff}_{P}(E)\,|\,E\geq 0,\,E\sim\lfloor mD\rfloor\}+{\rm coeff}_{P}(\{mD\})\\
=&{\rm inf}\{{\rm coeff}_{P}(E^{\prime})\,|\,E^{\prime}\geq 0,\,E^{\prime}\sim mD\}\end{split}

hold (see also the discussion in
[
Na04
, III, §4.a]
).
From this, we have

\begin{split}\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)=&{\rm inf}\{{\rm coeff}_{P}(E)\,|\,E\geq 0,\,E\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}D\}.\end{split}

This implies that the following properties hold:

•

For any
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, we have
\sigma_{P}(tD;X/Y)=t\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)
.

•

If
D=D^{\prime}+B
, where
D^{\prime}
is a
\pi
-big
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor and
B
is effective, then

\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)\leq\sigma_{P}(D^{\prime};X/Y)+{\rm coeff}_{P}(B).

•

If
D=D_{1}+D_{2}
, where
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are
\pi
-big
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisors, then

\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)\leq\sigma_{P}(D_{1};X/Y)+\sigma_{P}(D_{2};X/Y).

We will check that
\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)=0
when
D
is
\pi
-ample.
When
D
is
\pi
-ample, we can write
D=\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}A_{i}
for some
r_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\pi
-ample Cartier divisors
A_{i}
on
X
.
By the third property, it is sufficient to check that
\sigma_{P}(r_{i}A_{i};X/Y)=0
holds for every
1\leq i\leq l
.
By
[
Na04
, III, 4.1. Lemma]
and shrinking
Y
around a point of
\pi(P)
, we may assume that every
A_{i}
has only finitely many components.
Then the equality
\sigma_{P}(r_{i}A_{i};X/Y)=0
follows from the standard argument.
In this way, we have
\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)=0
when
D
is
\pi
-ample.

Since
\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)={\rm inf}\{{\rm coeff}_{P}(E)\,|\,E\geq 0,\,E\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}D\}
, it is easy to see that

\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)\geq{\rm inf}\{{\rm coeff}_{P}(E)\,|\,E\in|D|_{\mathbb{R}}\}.

We also have

{\rm inf}\{{\rm coeff}_{P}(E)\,|\,E\in|D|_{\mathbb{R}}\}\geq{\rm inf}\{{\rm coeff}_{P}(E)\,|\,E\in|D/Y|_{\mathbb{R}}\}.

Pick
G\geq 0
such that
D-G
is
\pi
-ample.
For any
E\in|D/Y|_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, the divisor
D-E+\epsilon(D-G)
is
\pi
-ample, and therefore
we have

\begin{split}(1+\epsilon)\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)=&\sigma_{P}((1+\epsilon)D;X/Y)\\
=&\sigma_{P}(D-E+\epsilon(D-G)+(E+\epsilon G);X/Y)\\
\leq&\sigma_{P}((D-E+\epsilon(D-G));X/Y)+{\rm coeff}_{P}(E)+\epsilon\cdot{\rm coeff}_{P}(G)\\
=&{\rm coeff}_{P}(E)+\epsilon\cdot{\rm coeff}_{P}(G).\end{split}

Taking the limit
\epsilon\to 0
, we have

\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)\leq{\rm coeff}_{P}(E)
for any
E\in|D/Y|_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Hence

{\rm inf}\{{\rm coeff}_{P}(E)\,|\,E\in|D/Y|_{\mathbb{R}}\}\geq\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y).

Therefore Lemma
4.3
holds.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In Lemma
4.3
, the Steinness of base space is crucial.
Even in the algebraic case
[
LX23
, Section 3]
, the quasi-projectivity of the base variety is crucial.
For example, consider a complete but not projective variety
Y
with the trivial Picard group (see, for example,
[
CLS11
, Example 4.2.13]
).
Let
X\to Y
be a blow-up at a smooth point, and let
E
be the exceptional divisor.
Then
-E
is clearly big over
Y
, but there is no effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
E^{\prime}
such that
E^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{R},\,Y}-E
because
Y
has the trivial Picard group.
In this case, the invariant

{\rm inf}\set{{\rm coeff}_{P}(E^{\prime})}{E^{\prime}\in|-E/Y|_{\mathbb{R}}}

cannot be defined since
|-E/Y|_{\mathbb{R}}
is empty.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism from a non-singular analytic variety
X
to an analytic space
Y
.
Let
D
be a
\pi
-big
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
,
P
a prime divisor on
X
, and
A
a
\pi
-big
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
.
If
Y
is Stein, then the equality

\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)=\underset{\epsilon\to 0+}{\rm lim}\sigma_{P}(D+\epsilon A;X/Y)

holds.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.3
, for any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
we have

\sigma_{P}(D+\epsilon A;X/Y)\leq\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)+\epsilon\sigma_{P}(A;X/Y).

By taking the limit, we have

\underset{\epsilon\to 0+}{\rm lim}\sigma_{P}(D+\epsilon A;X/Y)\leq\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y).

On the other hand, take
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
rD-A\sim_{\mathbb{R}}E
for some effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
E
on
X
(
[
F22a
, Lemma 2.53]
).
Then, for any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
we have

\begin{split}\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)\leq(1+r\epsilon)\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)=&\sigma_{P}(D+\epsilon A+\epsilon(rD-A);X/Y)\\
\leq&\sigma_{P}(D+\epsilon A;X/Y)+\epsilon\cdot{\rm coeff}_{P}(E).\end{split}

From this, we have

\sigma_{P}(D;X/Y)\leq\underset{\epsilon\to 0+}{\rm lim}\sigma_{P}(D+\epsilon A;X/Y).

Therefore Lemma
4.5
holds true.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism from a normal analytic variety
X
to a Stein space

.
Let
W\subset Y
be a compact subset.
Fix a resolution
\widetilde{X}\to X
of
X
.
Let
D
be a
\pi
-pseudo-effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on

and let

be (possibly infinite) the number of prime divisors

on

holds.

has only finitely many connected components for any analytic subset

({\rm P}4)
in
[

]

Proof.

By replacing
X
with
\widetilde{X}
, we may assume that

is non-singular.
We may assume

because otherwise there is nothing to prove.
If

, then there are prime divisors

on

and non-zero elements

such that

Then neither

is a zero divisor and

.
By Theorem

(

), we have

By Theorem

\begin{split}0<&\sigma_{P_{1}}(Q;X/Y)=\sigma_{P_{1}}(Q^{\prime}+(Q-Q^{\prime});X/Y)\\
\leq&\sigma_{P_{1}}(Q^{\prime};X/Y)+\sigma_{P_{1}}(Q-Q^{\prime};X/Y)=0+0=0.\end{split}

Thus we get a contradiction.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism from a normal analytic variety
X
to a Stein space
Y
, and
let
W\subset Y
be a subset.
Let
D
be a
\pi
-pseudo-effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
.
Let
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a projective bimeromorphism from a normal variety

.
If
N_{\sigma}(D;X/Y,W)
is well defined as an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
and
P_{\sigma}(D;X/Y,W)
is nef over

is
\mathbb{R}
-Carter and the relation

is well defined as an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on

holds, and

is nef over

.

Proof.

Since
D
and
P_{\sigma}(D;X/Y,W)
are
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier,

is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.
For any prime divisor
Q^{\prime}
on

, we have

\begin{split}\sigma_{Q^{\prime}}(f^{*}D;X^{\prime}/Y)=&\sigma_{Q^{\prime}}(f^{*}P_{\sigma}(D;X/Y,W)+f^{*}N_{\sigma}(D;X/Y,W);X^{\prime}/Y)\\
\leq&\sigma_{Q^{\prime}}(f^{*}P_{\sigma}(D;X/Y,W);X^{\prime}/Y)+\sigma_{Q^{\prime}}(f^{*}N_{\sigma}(D;X/Y,W);X^{\prime}/Y)\\
\leq&0+{\rm coeff}_{Q^{\prime}}(f^{*}N_{\sigma}(D;X/Y,W)),\end{split}

where the final inequality follows from Theorem
4.7
(
2
) and Lemma
4.11
.
This shows that

is well defined as an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X^{\prime}
and we have

N_{\sigma}(f^{*}D;X^{\prime}/Y,W)\leq f^{*}N_{\sigma}(D;X/Y,W).

To prove the converse relation, we may replace
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
by

for some projective bimeromorphism

Therefore, by replacing
f
with a resolution of
X
, we may assume that
X^{\prime}
is non-singular.
We put

Then

is

-exceptional.
We also have

\begin{split}E^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{R},\,X}&N_{\sigma}(f^{*}D;X^{\prime}/Y,W)-f^{*}D=-P_{\sigma}(f^{*}D;X^{\prime}/Y,W),\end{split}

and
P_{\sigma}(f^{*}D;X^{\prime}/Y,W)
is a limit of movable divisors over
W
.
By applying the negativity lemma (Corollary
2.15
) to
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
,
\pi^{-1}(W)
, and

, there is

an open subset of
X
such that
E^{\prime}|_{f^{-1}(U)}\geq 0
.
By construction, any component

of

satisfies
(\pi\circ f)(E^{\prime}_{i})\cap W\neq\emptyset
.
Therefore

, from which we have

N_{\sigma}(f^{*}D;X^{\prime}/Y,W)\geq f^{*}N_{\sigma}(D;X/Y,W).

In this way, we see that Theorem
4.13
holds.

Log MMP for log abundant lc pairs

.

be a sequence of steps of a

-MMP over

around

with scaling of an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

.

be the image of

on

.
By Theorem
4.7
(
6
), we may freely shrink
Y
.
By shrinking

around
W
, we may assume that
i
steps of the
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP over
Y
around
W

(X_{0}:=X,\Delta_{0}:=\Delta)\dashrightarrow(X_{1},\Delta_{1})\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow(X_{i},\Delta_{i})

is represented by a bimeromorphic contraction

over

.
Let

and

-exceptional

-divisor on

\mathbb{R}
-divisor on

around

to

By our hypothesis

, Proof of Proposition 3.3]

-divisor

) and shrinking

By the third condition of Lemma

[

]

[
F22a
]

around

(X,\Delta)

(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})

around

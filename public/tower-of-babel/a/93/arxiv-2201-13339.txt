The L ∞ L^{\infty} estimates for parabolic complex Monge-Ampere and Hessian equations
Xiuxiong Chen, Jingrui Cheng

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider a version of parabolic complex Monge-Ampere equations, and use a PDE approach similar to Phong et al to establish L ∞ L^{\infty} and Hölder estimates. We also generalize the L ∞ L^{\infty} estimates to parabolic Hessian equations.



Dedicated to Prof. Lawson for his 80th birthday

1.
introduction

This work tries to make the first step to develop a parabolic analogue of the uniform
L^{\infty}
and Hölder continuity estimates for the complex Monge-Ampere and Hessian equations.

The question of deriving
L^{\infty}
and Hölder estimates for the solution to the complex Monge-Ampere has been studied extensively in the last decades, with minimal assumptions on the right hand side. In the pioneering work of Kolodziej
[
13
]
, he proved that if
e^{F}\in L^{1}(\log L)^{p}
for some
p>n
, then we have
L^{\infty}
apriori bound for the solution
\varphi
. He also showed that the solution is continuous. Later on,
[
10
]
proved the Hölder continuity of the solution to complex Monge-Ampere in the case of Dirichlet problem in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
In the compact setting without boundary,
[
2
]
proved the Hölder continuity of solutions to complex Monge-Ampere when the right hand side is in
L^{p}(\omega_{0}^{n})
for some
p>1
.
When it comes to Hessian equations, Dinew and Kolodziej in
[
5
]
derived the
L^{\infty}
estimates for the
k-
Hessian equations on compact Kähler manifolds, under some integrability assumption of the right hand side.

All the works above are done using methods from pluri-potential theory. Since these works, it has been a major question whether such results can be obtained by pure PDE method.
This question has been answered for the case of bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and by Phong et al for the case of compact Kähler manifolds.

As to the flow problems, the one that is considered most in the literature takes the following form:

(1.1)

\partial_{t}\varphi=\log(\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{\omega_{0}^{n}})-F(t,z).

There are many works concerning (
1.1
), see for example
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
.

In this work, we propose to consider a different parabolic version for complex Monge-Ampere equation than (
1.1
), for which we can prove the
L^{\infty}
estimates very similar to the classical result by Kolodziej. Moreover, we show that it is possible to prove stability estimates very similar to
[
10
]
Theorem 1.1, from which the Hölder continuity readily follows by an approximation technique, developed in
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
3
]
. The approach we take to prove these results are purely PDE, hence our work can be seen as a generalization of
[
16
]
and
[
11
]
to parabolic setting.

In order to motivate the form of the parabolic
equation we will be considering, we would like to go back to the real case. Krylov-Tso (see
[
12
]
and
[
15
]
) developed a parabolic version of Alexandrov maximum principle, which says that:

Let
u
be a function defined on
[0,T]\times\Omega
where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\sup_{[0,T]\times\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\partial_{P}([0,T]\times\Omega)}u+C_{n}(\mbox{diam}\Omega)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\bigg(\int_{E}|\partial_{t}u\det D_{x}^{2}u|dtdx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}.

In the above,
\partial_{P}([0,T]\times\Omega)
is the parabolic boundary, given by
(\{0\}\times\Omega)\cup([0,T]\times\partial\Omega)
, and
E
is the set of
(t,x)
on which
\partial_{t}u\geq 0
and
D_{x}^{2}u\leq 0
.

The integrand on the right hand side is given by
\partial_{t}u\det(-D_{x}^{2}u)
, hence it is very natural to take this product as our parabolic operator, so that the parabolic (real) Monge-Ampere becomes:

(-\partial_{t}u)\det(D_{x}^{2}u)=f\geq 0.

The admissible solutions we are considering
are that
\partial_{t}u\leq 0
and
D_{x}^{2}u\geq 0
.

In the complex setting, it naturally generalizes to
(-\partial_{t}\varphi)(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}
, and the class of admissible solutions are given by
\partial_{t}\varphi\leq 0
,
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\geq 0
.
Hence in the context of compact Kähler manifold, the equation reads:

(1.2)

\begin{split}&(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\omega_{\varphi}^{n}=e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n},\\
&\varphi(0,\cdot)=\varphi_{0}.\end{split}

Since our goal is to derive apriori estimates, we will assume that the solutions are all smooth, so that our calculations are all justified. But the bound we obtain only have the said dependence quantitatively.

In the above, we assume that the initial data
\varphi_{0}
is
\omega_{0}
-psh and is bounded, and the admissible solutions we are looking for satisfies
\partial_{t}\varphi\leq 0
,
\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\geq 0
.
In order to study the convergence behavior of
\varphi
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, it is necessary to do a normalization first:
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi-h(t)
. Then the equation is transformed to:

(h^{\prime}(t)-\partial_{t}\tilde{\varphi})\omega_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{n}=e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n}.

Here the function
h(t)
is so chosen in order to satisfy the volume compatibility condition:

h^{\prime}(t)\int_{M}\omega_{0}^{n}=\int_{M}e^{F(t,\cdot)}\omega_{0}^{n}.

We plan to investigate the convergence question as
t\rightarrow\infty
in future works, but for now, we will only fix
0<T<\infty
and derive estimates on
[0,T]\times M
.

We will establish the following result regarding the parabolic complex Monge-Ampere equation, which can be seen as a parabolic analogue of Kolodziej’s
C^{0}
estimate:

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider the equation (
1.2
) on
[0,T]\times M
. Assume that the right hand side has
L^{1}(\log L)^{p}
integrability for some
p>n+1
. In other words, we assume that

Ent_{p}(F):=\int_{[0,T]\times M}e^{F}(|F|^{p}+1)\omega_{0}^{n}dt<\infty.

We also assume that
\varphi_{0}
is also uniformly bounded.
Then
||\varphi||_{L^{\infty}}
is uniformly bounded depending only on
||\varphi_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}
,
T
,
Ent_{p}(F)
, the background metric,
p
and
n
.

After this, we consider the issue of Hölder contiuity of the solution when the initial value
\varphi_{0}\in C^{\bar{\alpha}}
. We show that:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\varphi
solve (
1.2
), with
\varphi_{0}\in C^{\bar{\alpha}}
for some
0<\bar{\alpha}<1
and
e^{F}\in L^{p_{0}}(\omega_{0}^{n}dt)
for some
p_{0}>1
. Then for any
0\leq s<t\leq T
, and any
\alpha<\frac{2}{1+q_{0}(n+1)}
,

|\varphi(t,x)-\varphi(s,x)|\leq C(t-s)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

For any
x,\,y\in M
, and
t\in[0,T]
, and any
\alpha\leq\bar{\alpha}
,
\alpha<\frac{2}{1+q_{0}(n+1)}
,

|\varphi(t,x)-\varphi(t,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha}.

Here
q_{0}=\frac{p_{0}}{p_{0}-1}
, and the constant
C
depends only on the background metric,
n
,
||e^{F}||_{L^{p_{0}}}
, the
C^{\bar{\alpha}}
norm of
\varphi_{0}
,
T
and choice of
\alpha<\frac{2}{1+q_{0}(n+1)}
.

Next we will consider more general Hessian type equations, in the form:

f(-\partial_{t}\varphi,\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=e^{F}.

In the above
(h_{\varphi})_{j}^{i}=(\omega_{0})^{i\bar{k}}(\omega_{\varphi})_{j\bar{k}}
and
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]
denotes the eigenvalues of
h_{\varphi}
(which can be shown to be invariant under holomorphic coordinate change). We also assume that
f
is a
C^{1}
function in terms of its variables. The more precise structural assumptions on
f
are set forth in the last section, where we will also give some examples of
f
satisfying our assumptions.

Under these assumptions, we have the following
L^{\infty}
estimates for the solution to the Hessian equation:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\varphi
be a solution to the equation
f(-\partial_{t}\varphi,\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=e^{F}
on
[0,T]\times M
, where
f
satisfies the above structural assumption and
(-\partial_{t}\varphi,\lambda[h_{\varphi}])\in\Gamma
. Assume also that for some
p>n+1
, we have

Ent_{p}(F):=\int_{[0,T]\times M}e^{(n+1)F}|F|^{p}\omega_{0}^{n}dt<\infty.

Then we can estimate
||\varphi||_{L^{\infty}}
depending only on
||\varphi_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}
,
T
, the background metric,
Ent_{p}(F)
and
n
.

The plan of the paper is as follows:

In section 2, we will derive estimates for the parabolic complex Monge-Ampere equation, including the
L^{\infty}
estimates as well as Hölder estimates.

In section 3, we generalize the
L^{\infty}
estimates to more general Hessian equations.

2.
The parabolic complex Monge-Ampere equation

In this section, we consider the parabolic complex Monge-Ampere equation:

(2.1)

\begin{split}&(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\omega_{\varphi}^{n}=e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n},\\
&\varphi|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0}.\end{split}

Here we only consider solutions which are
\omega_{0}
-psh and that
\partial_{t}\varphi\leq 0
. The function
F(t,x)
on the right hand side is given. We also assume that the initial value
\varphi_{0}
is bounded.

First we show that
\sup_{M}\varphi
is bounded.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
\int_{[0,T]\times M}e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n}dt<\infty
, then we have

|\sup_{M}\varphi|\leq C.

Here
C
depends on the background metric, an upper bound for
\int_{[0,T]\times M}e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n}dt
and
||\varphi_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}
.

Proof.

Since
\partial_{t}\varphi\leq 0
, we have

\sup_{M}\varphi(t,\cdot)\leq\sup_{M}\varphi_{0}\leq||\varphi_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}.

To estimate the lower bound for
\sup_{M}\varphi
, we have to use the equation. We consider the
I
-functional, defined as:

I(\varphi)=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{M}\varphi\sum_{j=0}^{n}\omega_{0}^{n-j}\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{j}.

To estimate the lower bound of
\sup_{M}\varphi
, we will first get a lower bound for the
I
functional, then we will get a lower bound for
\int_{M}\varphi\omega_{0}^{n}
, then we obtain a lower bound for
\sup_{M}\varphi
, using some well-known arguments.

It is straightforward to find that

\frac{d}{dt}I(\varphi)=\int_{M}\partial_{t}\varphi\omega_{\varphi}^{n}=-\int_{M}e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n}.

Therefore, for any
t^{\prime}\in[0,T]
, we have that

I(\varphi)=I(\varphi_{0})+\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{d}{dt}I(\varphi)dt=I(\varphi_{0})-\int_{[0,t^{\prime}]\times M}e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n}dt

Therefore, we get that
I(\varphi)
is bounded below on
t^{\prime}\in[0,T]
, with a lower bound having the said dependence.

Now we estimate a lower bound for
\int_{M}\varphi\omega_{0}^{n}
. We can compute:

\begin{split}&\int_{M}\varphi\omega_{0}^{n}-I(\varphi)=\int_{M}\varphi\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\omega_{0}^{n-j}\wedge(\omega_{0}^{j}-\omega_{\varphi}^{j})\\
&=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{M}\varphi\sum_{j=0}^{n}\omega_{0}^{n-j}\wedge\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}(-\varphi)\sum_{l=0}^{j-1}\omega_{0}^{j-1-l}\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{l}\\
&=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{M}\sqrt{-1}\partial\varphi\wedge\bar{\partial}\varphi\wedge\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{j-1}\omega_{0}^{n-1-l}\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{l}\geq 0.\end{split}

So
\int_{M}\varphi\omega_{0}^{n}
is bounded below as well. On the other hand, it is well known that for any
\omega_{0}
-psh function
\varphi
, one has:

|\frac{1}{vol(M)}\int_{M}\varphi\omega_{0}^{n}-\sup_{M}\varphi|\leq C.

Here
C
depends only on the background metric. So we obtain that
\sup_{M}\varphi
is also bounded from below.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain that

Corollary 2.2
.

There exists constant
\alpha_{0}>0
depending only on the background metric, such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{M}e^{-\alpha_{0}\varphi}\omega_{0}^{n}\leq C.

Here
C
depends on the background metric, an upper bound for
\int_{[0,T]\times M}e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n}dt
and
||\varphi_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi(t,\cdot)
is
\omega_{0}
-psh for each
t\in[0,T]
, we have that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{M}e^{-\alpha_{0}(\varphi-\sup_{M}\varphi)}\omega_{0}^{n}\leq C.

From Lemma
2.1
,
\sup_{M}\varphi
is uniformly bounded, with the said dependence.
∎

2.1.
Existence of smooth solution with smooth data

Since later on, we will need to use the solution to equation (
2.1
) as auxiliary functions for estimates, we need to establish the solvability of (
2.1
) when the data is smooth. More precisely, we have:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\varphi_{0}
be a smooth function on
M
with
\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{0}>0
and
F(t,x)
is a smooth function on
[0,T]\times M
. Then there exists a unique smooth solution to (
2.1
) on
[0,T]\times M
starting from
\varphi_{0}
such that
-\partial_{t}\varphi>0
and
\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi>0
.

Proof.

Uniqueness is quite easy to see, thanks to the maximum principle. Indeed, if there are two such smooth solutions
\varphi
and
\bar{\varphi}
, we can consider
\varphi-\varepsilon t-\bar{\varphi}
. Assuming it has maximum at
(t_{0},x_{0})
with
t_{0}>0
, then we would have

\partial_{t}(\varphi-\varepsilon t-\bar{\varphi})|_{(t_{0},x_{0})}\geq 0,\,\,\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}(\varphi-\varepsilon t-\bar{\varphi})|_{(t_{0},x_{0})}\leq 0.

This would imply

\varepsilon+(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\leq-\partial_{t}\bar{\varphi},\,\,\,\omega_{\varphi}^{n}\leq\omega_{\bar{\varphi}}^{n}.

Multiplying, we get

\varepsilon\omega_{\varphi}^{n}+(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\omega_{\varphi}^{n}\leq(-\partial_{t}\bar{\varphi})\omega_{\bar{\varphi}}^{n}.

This gives
\varepsilon\omega_{\varphi}^{n}\leq 0
, which contradicts
\omega_{\varphi}>0
. Hence
\varphi-\bar{\varphi}-\varepsilon t\leq(\varphi-\bar{\varphi}-\varepsilon t)|_{t=0}=0
. Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we get
\varphi\leq\bar{\varphi}
.

It only remains to show existence, and we can run a continuity method as follows: for
s\in[0,1]
,

(2.2)

\begin{split}&(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\omega_{\varphi}^{n}=e^{sF}\omega_{0}^{n},\\
&\varphi(0,\cdot)=s\varphi_{0}.\end{split}

When
s=0
, it has a trivial solution
\varphi(t,x)=-t
.

To show openness, we need to linearize the equation, and the linearized operator is:

Lu=-\partial_{t}u+(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\Delta_{\varphi}u.

Since
-\partial_{t}\varphi>0
, the operator
L
is uniformly parabolic. Hence it would be standard to show the openness of the continuity path.

Now it only remains to show closeness of the continuity path, for which we have to derive the apriori estimates.

First we would like to derive the equation for
\partial_{t}\varphi
. Denote
v=\partial_{t}\varphi
, we can differentiate (
2.2
) to get

\begin{split}&-\partial_{t}v+s\partial_{t}Fv+(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\Delta_{\varphi}v=0,\\
&v|_{t=0}=-\frac{e^{sF(0,x)}\omega_{0}^{n}}{\omega_{\varphi_{0}}^{n}}.\end{split}

Let
k>0
, we define
\bar{v}=ve^{kt}
, then in terms of
\bar{v}
, the equation reads:

(2.3)

\begin{split}&-\partial_{t}\bar{v}+(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\Delta_{\varphi}\bar{v}=\bar{v}(-k-s\partial_{t}F),\\
&-M_{0}\leq\bar{v}|_{t=0}\leq-\varepsilon_{0}.\end{split}

In the above, if we take
k>0
such that
-k-s\partial_{t}F<0
, then we see that
\bar{v}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{0}
on
[0,T]\times M
by maximum principle. In particular, we get
v\leq-\varepsilon_{1}
on
[0,T]\times M
.

On the other hand, if
k<0
such that
-k-s\partial_{t}F>0
, then from (
2.3
), we get that
-\partial_{t}\bar{v}+(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\Delta_{\varphi}v\leq 0
, so that
\bar{v}\geq-M_{0}
.

Hence from the above arguments, we see that:

(2.4)

-M_{1}\leq\partial_{t}\varphi\leq-\varepsilon_{1}.

This implies that in particular,
\varphi
is uniformly bounded on
[0,T]\times M
.

Next we estimate the second derivative of
\varphi
. Now we put
u=e^{-C\varphi}(n+\Delta\varphi)
and denote
e^{G}=\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{\omega_{0}^{n}}
, then we can compute:

\begin{split}&-\partial_{t}u=(-C)(-\partial_{t}\varphi)u+e^{-C\varphi}\Delta(-\partial_{t}\varphi)=(-C)(-\partial_{t}\varphi)u+e^{-C\varphi}\Delta(e^{sF-G})\\
&\geq(-C)(-\partial_{t}\varphi)u+e^{-C\varphi}e^{sF-G}(s\Delta F-\Delta G)\\
&=(-\partial_{t}\varphi)(-Cu+e^{-C\varphi}s\Delta F-e^{-C\varphi}\Delta G).\end{split}

On the other hand, from Yau’s calculation in
[
17
]
:

\Delta_{\varphi}u\geq e^{-C\varphi}(-C)\Delta_{\varphi}\varphi(n+\Delta\varphi)+e^{-C\varphi}\frac{R_{i\bar{i}k\bar{k}}(1+\varphi_{i\bar{i}})}{1+\varphi_{k\bar{k}}}+e^{-C\varphi}\Delta G-e^{-C\varphi}R.

In the above calculation, we took normal coordinates at a point, and
R_{i\bar{i}k\bar{k}}
are curvature tensors of
\omega_{0}
,
R
is the scalar curvature of
\omega_{0}
. Hence if we take
C
large enough, we would get that

\begin{split}&\Delta_{\varphi}u\geq e^{-C\varphi}tr_{\varphi}\omega_{0}(n+\Delta\varphi)-e^{-C\varphi}Cn(n+\Delta\varphi)+e^{-C\varphi}\Delta G-e^{-C\varphi}R\\
&=e^{-C\varphi}\big(tr_{\varphi}\omega_{0}u-Cnu+\Delta G-R\big).\end{split}

Hence if we define
L=-\partial_{t}+(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\Delta_{\varphi}
, we get that

(2.5)

\begin{split}&Lu\geq(-\partial_{t}\varphi)e^{-C\varphi}\big(-C(n+1)u-R+s\Delta F+tr_{\varphi}\omega_{0}u\big)\\
&\geq(-\partial_{t}\varphi)e^{-C\varphi}\big(-C(n+1)u-R+s\Delta F+e^{-\frac{G}{n-1}}(n+\Delta\varphi)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}u\big).\end{split}

In the above, we note that
-\partial_{t}\varphi=e^{sF-G}
. Also we have shown a bound for
-\partial_{t}\varphi
:
-M_{1}\leq\partial_{t}\varphi\leq-\varepsilon_{1}
, it follows that
G
is bounded, since
F
is assumed to be smooth hence bounded.
Therefore, it follows from (
2.9
) that

Lu\geq(-\partial_{t}\varphi)e^{-C\varphi}\big(-C(n+1)u-R+s\Delta F+cu^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\big).

Hence if
u
achieves maximum at
(t_{0},x_{0})
, then at
(t_{0},x_{0})
, we would get

0\geq-C(n+1)u-R+s\Delta F+cu^{\frac{n}{n-1}}.

This implies an upper bound for
u
at
(t_{0},x_{0})
.
So we have shown that
\omega_{\varphi}\leq C\omega_{0}
. That
\omega_{\varphi}\geq\frac{1}{C}\omega_{0}
follows from that
\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{\omega_{0}^{n}}=e^{G}
is bounded from above and below.
Hence the equation becomes uniformly parabolic and higher regularity follows from standard bootstrap.
∎

2.2.
Estimate the
L^{\infty}
bound

The first step is to establish a Moser-Trudinger type inequality, similar to Lemma 1 in
[
11
]
:

Proposition 2.4
.

Denote
A_{s}=\int_{[0,T]\times M}(-\varphi-s)^{+}e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n}dt
.
Then there exists a constant
\beta_{0}>0
, depending only on the background metric, and there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on the background metric, such that for any
s\geq||\varphi_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{M}e^{\beta_{0}A_{s}^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}((-\varphi-s)^{+})^{\frac{n+2}{n+1}}}\omega_{0}^{n}\leq C\exp(CE).

Here

E=\int_{[0,T]\times M}(-\varphi)e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n}dt.

To prove this, we use the solution to an auxiliary problem. Let
\eta_{j}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\eta_{j}(x)\rightarrow\max(x,0)
as
j\rightarrow\infty
and
\eta_{j}(x)>x
for
x>0
(for example, we could take
\eta_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x+\sqrt{x^{2}+j^{-1}})
. We let
\psi_{j}
be the solution to the following problem:

(2.6)

\begin{split}&(-\partial_{t}\psi_{j})\omega_{\psi_{j}}^{n}=\frac{\eta_{j}(-\varphi-s)e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n}}{A_{j,s}},\\
&\psi_{j}|_{t=0}=0.\end{split}

Here

A_{j,s}=\int_{[0,T]\times M}\eta_{j}(-\varphi-s)e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n}dt.

The existence of such a
\psi_{j}
is guaranteed by Proposition
2.4
.
The above proposition will follow immediately once we prove:

Lemma 2.5
.

There exists dimensional constant
c_{n}
and
C_{n}
such that

c_{n}A_{j,s}^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}(-\varphi-s)^{\frac{n+2}{n+1}}\leq-\psi_{j}+C_{n}A_{j,s}.

Proof.

Define the operator
L
to be:

Lu=-\partial_{t}u+(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\Delta_{\varphi}u.

Let
\varepsilon=(\frac{1}{2}n^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\frac{n+1}{n+2})^{\frac{n+1}{n+2}}A_{s}^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}
,
\Lambda=(\frac{1}{2}n^{\frac{n}{n+1}})^{-(n+1)}\frac{n+1}{n+2}A_{s}
, we define
\Phi_{j}=\varepsilon(-\varphi-s)-(-\psi_{j}+\Lambda)^{\frac{n+1}{n+2}}
.
Then we may compute (we suppress the subscript
j
for the convenience of notations):

\begin{split}&L\Phi=-\partial_{t}\Phi+(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\Delta_{\varphi}\Phi=-\varepsilon(-\partial_{t}\varphi)+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}(-\partial_{t}\psi)\\
&+(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\big(\varepsilon(-\Delta_{\varphi}\varphi)+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\Delta_{\varphi}\psi+\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{2}}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{n+3}{n+2}}|\nabla_{\varphi}\psi|^{2}\big)\\
&\geq-\varepsilon(-\partial_{t}\varphi)+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}(-\partial_{t}\psi)+(-\partial_{t}\varphi)\big(\varepsilon tr_{\varphi}g-\varepsilon n\\
&+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}tr_{\omega_{\varphi}}\omega_{\psi}-\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}tr_{\varphi}g\big).\end{split}

Because of our choice of
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
, we have that

\varepsilon\geq\frac{n+1}{n+2}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\geq\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}.

Moreover, we use arithmetic-geometric inequality to obtain:

tr_{\omega_{\varphi}}\omega_{\psi}\geq n\big(\frac{\omega_{\psi}^{n}}{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}\big)^{\frac{1}{n}}=n\big(\frac{-\partial_{t}\varphi}{-\partial_{t}\psi}\frac{\eta_{j}(-\varphi-s)}{A_{s}}\big)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Therefore

C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\geq\rho_{\theta\varepsilon}\varphi-\varphi\geq\frac{c}{(\theta\varepsilon)^{2n-2}}\int_{B(z,\frac{\varepsilon}{2})}\Delta\varphi(t,z^{\prime})dV(z^{\prime})-C\varepsilon^{2}.

Then the desired Hölder continuity follows from the following elementary result:

Lemma 2.24
.

Let
u
be a bounded function defined on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
. Assume that there exists
r_{0}>0
,
C_{0}>0
,
0<\alpha<1
, such that for any
z\in\Omega^{\prime}
, any
0<r<r_{0}
, we have

\int_{B(z,r)}|\Delta u|(z^{\prime})dV(z^{\prime})\leq C_{0}r^{n-2+\alpha}.

Then

\sup_{x,y\in\Omega^{\prime}}|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha}.

Here the constant
C
depends on dimension,
r_{0}
,
dist(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)
,
C_{0}
, and
||u||_{L^{\infty}}
.

Proof.

Choose
\Omega_{1}
such that
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega_{1}\subset\subset\Omega
. Denote
f=\Delta u
, we define

\bar{u}(x)=\int_{\Omega_{1}}f(y)G(x,y)dV(y).

Here
G(x,y)
is the fundamental solution to
\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let us assume that
n\geq 3
so that we have
G(x,y)=c_{n}|x-y|^{2-n}
.
Then
\Delta(u-\bar{u})=0
on
\Omega_{1}
and
\Delta\bar{u}=f
. We just need to do Hölder estimate for
\bar{u}
. Choose
x_{1},\,x_{2}\in\Omega^{\prime}
such that
r=|x_{1}-x_{2}|<\frac{r_{0}}{10}
. Denote
x_{*}=\frac{1}{2}(x_{1}+x_{2})
, we can then compute

(2.25)

\begin{split}&\bar{u}(x_{1})-\bar{u}(x_{2})=\int_{B(x_{*},3r)}f(y)G(x_{1},y)dV(y)-\int_{B(x_{*},3r)}f(y)G(x_{2},y)dV(y)\\
&+\int_{\Omega_{1}-B(x_{*},3r)}f(y)(G(x_{1},y)-G(x_{2},y))dV(y)\end{split}

For the first term above,

(2.26)

\begin{split}&|\int_{B(x_{*},3r)}f(y)G(x_{1},y)dV(y)|\leq\int_{B(x_{1},4r)}|f(y)|c_{n}|x_{1}-y|^{2-n}dV(y)\\
&=\int_{0}^{4r}c_{n}\rho^{2-n}d\rho\int_{\partial B(x_{1},\rho)}|f(y)|d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)=\int_{0}^{4r}c_{n}\rho^{2-n}\frac{d}{d\rho}\int_{B(x_{1},\rho)}|f(y)|dV(y)\\
&=c_{n}(4r)^{2-n}\int_{B(x_{1},4r)}|f(y)|dV(y)+\int_{0}^{4r}c_{n}(n-2)\rho^{1-n}\int_{B(x_{1},\rho)}|f(y)|dV(y)d\rho\\
&\leq c_{n}(4r)^{2-n}C_{0}(4r)^{n-2+\alpha}+\int_{0}^{4r}c_{n}(n-2)\rho^{1-n}C_{0}\rho^{n-2+\alpha}\leq Cr^{\alpha}.\end{split}

The estimate for the second term is the same. We now look at the last term. First we note that for
y\notin B(x_{*},3r)
, it holds:

\begin{split}&|G(x_{1},y)-G(x_{2},y)|\leq|x_{1}-x_{2}|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|\nabla_{x}G((1-t)x_{1}+tx_{2},y)|\\
&\leq C_{n}|x_{1}-x_{2}|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|(1-t)x_{1}+tx_{2}-y|^{1-n}\leq C_{n}^{\prime}|x_{1}-x_{2}||x_{*}-y|^{1-n}.\end{split}

Therefore,

(2.27)

\begin{split}&|\int_{\Omega_{1}-B(x_{*},3r)}f(y)(G(x_{1},y)-G(x_{2},y))dV(y)|\leq\int_{\Omega_{1}-B(x_{*},3r)}|f(y)|C_{n}^{\prime}r|x_{*}-y|^{1-n}dV(y)\\
&\leq C_{n}^{\prime}r\big(\int_{B(x_{*},r_{0})-B(x_{*},3r)}|f(y)||x_{*}-y|^{1-n}dV(y)+\int_{\Omega_{1}-B(x_{*},r_{0})}|f(y)||x_{*}-y|^{1-n}dV(y)\big).\end{split}

In the first term above, we have:

(2.28)

\begin{split}&\int_{B(x_{*},r_{0})-B(x_{*},3r)}|f(y)||x_{*}-y|^{1-n}dV(y)=\int_{3r}^{r_{0}}\rho^{1-n}d\rho\int_{\partial B(x_{*},\rho)}|f(y)|d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\\
&=\int_{3r}^{r_{0}}\rho^{1-n}d\rho\frac{d}{d\rho}\int_{B(x_{*},\rho)}|f(y)|dV(y)\leq r_{0}^{1-n}\int_{B(x_{*},r_{0})}|f(y)|dV(y)\\
&+\int_{3r}^{r_{0}}(n-1)\rho^{-n}\int_{B(x_{*},\rho)}|f(y)|dV(y)d\rho\leq r_{0}^{1-n}\int_{B(x_{*},r_{0})}|f(y)|dV(y)\\
&+\int_{3r}^{r_{0}}(n-1)\rho^{-n}C_{0}\rho^{n-2+\alpha}d\rho\leq C(r_{0})+Cr^{-1+\alpha}.\end{split}

For the second term in (
2.27
), one has

(2.29)

\int_{\Omega_{1}-B(x_{*},r_{0})}|f(y)||x_{*}-y|^{1-n}dV(y)\leq r_{0}^{1-n}\int_{\Omega_{1}-B(x_{*},r_{0})}|f(y)|dV(y).

Combining (
2.25
)-(
2.29
), we would get that
|u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})|\leq Cr^{\alpha}
.
∎

3.
L^{\infty}
estimate for more general parabolic Hessian equations

In this section, we would like to generalize the above
L^{\infty}
estimates to more general Hessian equations:

(3.1)

\begin{split}&f(-\partial_{t}\varphi,\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=e^{F},\\
&\varphi|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0}.\end{split}

f(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
is a
C^{1}
function defined on a cone
\Gamma\subset\{(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{n}):\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}>0\}
. Here
(h_{\varphi})_{j}^{i}=g^{i\bar{k}}(g_{\varphi})_{j\bar{k}}
.
The eigenvalues of
(h_{\varphi})_{j}^{i}
does not depend on the choice of local coordinates, which we will denote as
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]
.

We also assume that
\Gamma
contains the positive cone
\Gamma_{+}
given by
\{(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{n}):\lambda_{0}>0,\cdots,\lambda_{n}>0\}
.
Moreover, we assume the following conditions, similar to the conditions assumed in
[
11
]
:

(1)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0
for any
0\leq i\leq n
,

(2)

f
is a symmetric function in terms of
\lambda_{1}
,
\cdots
,
\lambda_{n}
.

(3)

For some
c_{0}>0
, we have
\partial_{0}f\det(\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}})\geq c_{0}
on the positive cone
\Gamma_{+}
.

(4)

For some
C_{0}>0
such that
\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\leq C_{0}f
on the positive cone
\Gamma_{+}
.

Remark 3.1
.

The first condition above just guarantees that our equation is parabolic. The second condition is a usual assumption for Hessian equation. Note that condition 4 can be guaranteed as long as
f
is a homogeneous function in terms of its variables. The above considered parabolic complex Monge-Ampere equation is only a special case by taking
f(\lambda)=\big(\Pi_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}\big)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}
, where
\Gamma=\Gamma_{+}=\{\lambda_{0}>0,\,\cdots,\lambda_{n}>0\}
.
We can give a few examples which satisfies the conditions (1)-(4) above:

Example 1
.

Let
f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
be a positive function defined on
\Gamma
which is contained in
\{(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}):\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}>0\}
and contains
\{(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}):\lambda_{i}>0,\,1\leq i\leq n\}
. Assume also that
f
satisfies the conditions assumed in
[
11
]
, namely
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0
for
1\leq i\leq n
,
f
symmetric in
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
,
\det(\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}})\geq c_{0}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\leq C_{0}f
on the positive cone.

Then the function
g(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=\big(\lambda_{0}f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\big)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}
satisfies the conditions (1)-(4) above, as a function defined on
\{\lambda_{0}>0\}\times\Gamma
. In particular,
g(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=(\lambda_{0}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}))^{\frac{n}{n+1}}
for
1\leq k\leq n
and
g(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=\big(\lambda_{0}(\frac{\sigma_{k}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})}{\sigma_{l}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})})^{\frac{1}{k-l}}\big)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}
for
1\leq l<k\leq n
satisfy conditions (1)-(4).

Example 2
.

For any
1\leq k\leq n+1
, define
f(\lambda)=(\sigma_{k}(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{n}))^{\frac{1}{k}}
, or
f(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=(\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{n}))^{\frac{1}{k-l}}
for
n\geq k>l\geq 1
will satisfy the above conditions.

In order to establish the
L^{\infty}
bound, first we wish to establish the analogue of Proposition
2.4
:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a solution to (
3.1
) such that
\varphi_{0}
is bounded. Denote
A_{s}=\int_{[0,T]\times M}(-\varphi-s)^{+}e^{F}\omega_{0}^{n}dt
. Then there exists constants
\beta_{0}>0
,
C>0
, depending only on the background metric, the structural constants
c_{0}
and
C_{0}
, such that for any
s\geq||\varphi_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{M}e^{\beta_{0}A_{s}^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}((-\varphi-s)^{+})^{\frac{n+2}{n+1}}}\omega_{0}^{n}\leq C\exp(CE).

Here
E=\int_{[0,T]\times M}(-\varphi)e^{(n+1)F}\omega_{0}^{n}dt.

Similar to Proposition
2.4
, we let
\psi_{j}
be defined as:

\begin{split}&(-\partial_{t}\psi_{j})\omega_{\psi_{j}}^{n}=\frac{\eta_{j}(-\varphi-s)e^{(n+1)F}\omega_{0}^{n}}{A_{j,s}},\\
&\psi_{j}|_{t=0}=0\end{split}

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists constants
c
and
C
, depending only on dimension and the structural constant
c_{0}
,
C_{0}
from
f
, such that

c_{n}A_{j,s}^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}((-\varphi-s)^{+})^{\frac{n+2}{n+1}}\leq-\psi_{j}+C_{n}A_{j,s}.

Proof.

The calculation follows similar lines as Lemma
2.5
.
We define the operator
L
to be:

Lu=-\partial_{0}f\partial_{t}u+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}\partial_{j\bar{k}}u.

The following calculations are done pointwisely at any
z\in M
, and we assume that the local coordinates have been chosen so that
g_{i\bar{j}}=\delta_{ij}
, so that under this coordinate, one has:

Lu=-\partial_{0}f\partial_{t}u+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}\partial_{j\bar{i}}u.

We choose
\varepsilon=(\frac{n+1}{n+2})^{\frac{n+1}{n+2}}c_{0}^{\frac{1}{n+2}}C_{0}^{-\frac{n+1}{n+2}}
,
\Lambda=\varepsilon^{-(n+2)}(\frac{1}{2}\frac{n+2}{n+1})^{n+2}
and we similarly define

\Phi=\varepsilon(-\varphi-s)-(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\frac{n+1}{n+2}}.

Then we can compute

(3.2)

\partial_{t}\Phi=\varepsilon(-\partial_{t}\varphi)-\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}(-\partial_{t}\psi).

Also

(3.3)

\Phi_{i\bar{j}}=-\varepsilon\varphi_{i\bar{j}}-\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}(-\psi_{i\bar{j}})+\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{2}}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{n+3}{n+2}}\psi_{i}\psi_{\bar{j}}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}&\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}\Phi_{i\bar{j}}=-\varepsilon\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}\varphi_{i\bar{j}}+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}\psi_{i\bar{j}}+\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{2}}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{n+3}{n+2}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}\psi_{i}\psi_{\bar{j}}\\
&\geq-\varepsilon\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(\omega_{\varphi})_{i\bar{j}}+\varepsilon\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g_{i\bar{j}}+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(\omega_{\psi})_{i\bar{j}}-\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g_{i\bar{j}}\end{split}

Combining, we get

\begin{split}&L\Phi\geq-\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\varphi\partial_{0}f+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(\omega_{\varphi})_{i\bar{j}})+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)\\
&+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(\omega_{\psi})_{i\bar{j}}+(\varepsilon-\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}})\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g_{i\bar{j}}\end{split}

According to our choice of constants, we have

\varepsilon\geq\frac{n+1}{n+2}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}.

So that

\begin{split}&L\Phi\geq-\varepsilon\big(\partial_{t}\varphi\partial_{0}f+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(\omega_{\varphi})_{i\bar{j}}\big)+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)\\
&+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(\omega_{\psi})_{i\bar{j}}.\end{split}

Now we assume that
\Phi
achieves positive maximum at
(t_{0},x_{0})
. As before,
\Phi\leq 0
at
t_{0}=0
because of our choice of
s
, so that we must have
t_{0}>0
. Then at
(t_{0},x_{0})
, it holds:

\partial_{t}\Phi(t_{0},x_{0})\geq 0,\,\,\,\Phi_{i\bar{j}}(t_{0},x_{0})\leq 0.

It follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we have

-\partial_{t}\varphi(t_{0},x_{0})>0,\,\,(\omega_{0})_{i\bar{j}}+\varphi_{i\bar{j}}(t_{0},x_{0})>0.

In particular, this means that
(-\partial_{t}\varphi(t_{0},x_{0}),(\omega_{\varphi})_{i\bar{j}}(t_{0},x_{0}))\in\Gamma_{+}
.
Here we assumed to have chosen holomorphic normal coordinates at
x_{0}
so that
(\omega_{0})_{i\bar{j}}(x_{0})=\delta_{ij}
. At
(t_{0},x_{0})
, we have

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\varphi\partial_{0}f+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(\omega_{\varphi})_{i\bar{j}}\leq C_{0}f,\\
&\partial_{0}f\det(\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}})\geq c_{0}.\end{split}

This would imply that

\begin{split}&L\Phi\geq-\varepsilon C_{0}f+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(\omega_{\psi})_{i\bar{j}}\\
&\geq-\varepsilon C_{0}f+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)+n\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\big(\det(\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}})\det(\omega_{\psi})_{i\bar{j}})\big)^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
&\geq-\varepsilon C_{0}f+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)+n\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}(\frac{c_{0}}{\partial_{0}f}\frac{(-\varphi-s)^{+}e^{(n+1)F}A_{s}^{-1}}{-\partial_{t}\psi})^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
&=-\varepsilon C_{0}f+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)\\
&+\frac{n(n+1)}{n+2}c_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}A_{s}^{-\frac{1}{n}}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}((-\varphi-s)^{+})^{\frac{1}{n}}\frac{f^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}{(\partial_{0}f)^{\frac{1}{n}}(-\partial_{t}\psi)^{\frac{1}{n}}}.\end{split}

Therefore, we get

\begin{split}&L\Phi\geq-\varepsilon C_{0}f+\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi-\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)\\
&+\frac{n(n+1)}{n+2}c_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}A_{s}^{-\frac{1}{n}}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}((-\varphi-s)^{+})^{\frac{1}{n}}(-\partial_{t}\psi)^{-\frac{1}{n}}f^{\frac{n+1}{n}}(\partial_{0}f)^{-\frac{1}{n}}.\end{split}

Now we use the inequality that

Ay^{n}+By^{-1}\geq n^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}A^{\frac{1}{n+1}}B^{\frac{n}{n+1}}.

Here
y=(-\partial_{t}\psi)^{\frac{1}{n}}
,
A=\frac{n+1}{n+2}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\partial_{0}f
,
B=\frac{n(n+1)}{n+2}c_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}A_{s}^{-\frac{1}{n}}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}((-\varphi-s)^{+})^{\frac{1}{n}}f^{\frac{n+1}{n}}(\partial_{0}f)^{-\frac{1}{n}}
.
Therefore, we get, at
(t_{0},x_{0})
.

L\Phi\geq-\varepsilon C_{0}f+\frac{n+1}{n+2}c_{0}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}((-\varphi-s)^{+})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}A_{s}^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}f.

Note that since
\Phi(t_{0},x_{0})>0
, we have that

((-\varphi-s)^{+})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}>\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}.

Therefore, at
(t_{0},x_{0})
, we have

0\geq L\Phi\geq-\varepsilon C_{0}f+\frac{n+1}{n+2}c_{0}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}A_{s}^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}f>0.

The last
>0
follows from our choice of
\varepsilon

and we get a contradiction and we must have
\Phi\leq 0.

∎

As a direct consequence, we get that

Proposition 3.4
.

Denote
A_{s}=\int_{[0,T]\times M}(-\varphi-s)^{+}e^{(n+1)F}\omega_{0}^{n}dt
. Then there exist constants
\beta_{0}>0
, depending only on the background metric, the structural constants
c_{0}
,
C_{0}
and
n
, such that for any
s\geq||\varphi_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{M}e^{\beta_{0}A_{s}^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}((-\varphi-s)^{+})^{\frac{n+2}{n+1}}}\omega_{0}^{n}\leq C\exp(CE).

Here

E=\int_{[0,T]\times M}(-\varphi)e^{(n+1)F}\omega_{0}^{n}.

We still lack a uniform bound for the term
E
. In the case of complex Monge-Ampere, we did it quite easily, using the exponential integral bound for the
\omega_{0}
-psh function. However, in the case of complex Hessian equations, all we have is that
(-\partial_{t}\varphi,\lambda[h_{\varphi}])\in\Gamma
, and we no longer have an exponential bound for the solution
\varphi
. So we have to work harder to achieve this bound. To be more precise, we have the following estimates:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\varphi
solves (
3.1
) such that the right hand side satisfies
Ent_{p}(F):=\int_{[0,T]\times M}e^{(n+1)F}(|F|^{p}+1)\omega_{0}^{n}dt<\infty
for some
p>0
. Then we have:

(1)

If
p<n+1
, then there exists a constant
\beta_{0}
, which depends only on the structural constants
c_{0}
,
C_{0}
, background metric, and upper bound for
Ent_{p}(F)
, such that

\sup_{[0,T]}\int_{M}\exp\big(\beta_{0}((-\varphi)^{+})^{\frac{n+1}{n+1-p}}\big)\omega_{0}^{n}\leq C.

Here
C
has the same dependence as
\varepsilon
, but additionally on
T
and
||\varphi_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}
.

(2)

If
p\geq n+1
, then for any
N>0
, there exists a constant
\beta_{0}
, which depends only on the structural constants
c_{0}
,
C_{0}
, the background metric, and upper bound for
Ent_{p}(F)
, such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{M}\exp\big(\beta_{0}((-\varphi)^{+})^{N}\big)\omega_{0}^{n}\leq C.

Here
C
has the same dependence as
\varepsilon
, but additionally on
T
,
||\varphi_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}
and the choice of
N
.

The proof of this proposition follows similar ideas as the proof for the Moser-Trudinger inequality above (Proposition
2.6
and
3.4
).

Let
\psi
be the solution to the following problem:

(3.4)

\begin{split}&(-\partial_{t}\psi)\omega_{\psi}^{n}=\frac{e^{(n+1)F}(F^{2}+1)^{\frac{p}{2}}\omega_{0}^{n}}{\int_{[0,T]\times M}e^{(n+1)F}(F^{2}+1)^{\frac{p}{2}}\omega_{0}^{n}dt},\\
&\psi|_{t=0}=0.\end{split}

Note that the integral of the right hand side of (
3.4
) is 1, and the initial value of
\psi
is 0, Corollary
2.2
implies that

(3.5)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{M}e^{-\alpha_{0}\psi}\omega_{0}^{n}\leq C_{*}.

Here both
\alpha_{0}
and
C
depends only on the background metric.

We have the following lemma holds:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\varphi
solve (
3.1
) and
s\geq||\varphi_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}

We have:

(1)

If
p<n+1
, then there exist constants
\varepsilon
,
\Lambda
, depending only on the structural constants
c_{0}
,
C_{0}
, background metric, an upper bound for
Ent_{p}(F)
, such that

\varepsilon(-\varphi-s)\leq(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\frac{n+1}{n+1-p}}+C.

Here
C
has the same dependence as
\varepsilon
,
\Lambda
, but additionally on
T
.

(2)

If
p\geq n+1
, then for any
N>0
, there exists constants
\varepsilon
,
\Lambda
, depending on the structural constants
c_{0}
,
C_{0}
, the background metric, an upper bound for
Ent_{p}(F)
, and
N
such that

\varepsilon(-\varphi-s)\leq(-\psi+\Lambda)^{N}+C.

Here
C
has the same dependence as
\varepsilon
,
\Lambda
, but additionally on
T
.

Proof.

For
p<n+1
, we choose
\alpha=1-\frac{p}{n+1}
, and if
p\geq n+1
, we choose
\alpha=\frac{1}{N}
.
We define

\rho=-\varepsilon t+\varepsilon(-\varphi-s)-(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha}.

In the above

\varepsilon=\frac{1}{C_{0}}(n+1)\alpha\big(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{n+1}\big)^{\frac{p}{n+1}}\big(c_{0}Ent_{p}^{-1}(F)\big)^{\frac{1}{n+1}},\,\,\,\Lambda=(\frac{4\alpha}{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}.

Then we may compute

\begin{split}&L\rho=\partial_{0}f(-\rho_{t})+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}\rho_{j\bar{k}}=-\partial_{0}f(-\varepsilon-\varepsilon\partial_{t}\varphi-\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}(-\partial_{t}\psi))\\
&+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}\big(-\varepsilon\varphi_{j\bar{k}}+\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}\psi_{j\bar{k}}+\alpha(1-\alpha)(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-2}\psi_{j}\psi_{\bar{k}}\big)\\
&\geq-\varepsilon\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\varphi)+\varepsilon\partial_{0}f+\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)+\varepsilon\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(-1)[h_{\varphi}]^{i}_{j}+\varepsilon\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ii}}\\
&+\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}(\omega_{\psi})_{j\bar{k}}-\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ii}}.\end{split}

Because of our choice of
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
, we have that

(3.6)

\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\geq\alpha\Lambda^{\alpha-1}.

So that in the above,

\varepsilon\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ii}}-\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ii}}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{i\bar{i}}}.

So that we get

\begin{split}&L\rho\geq-\varepsilon\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\varphi)+\varepsilon\partial_{0}f+\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)\\
&+\varepsilon\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(-1)[h_{\varphi}]_{j}^{i}+\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}(\omega_{\psi})_{j\bar{k}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ii}}.\end{split}

Assume that
\rho
achieves positive maximum on
[0,T]\times M
at
(t_{0},x_{0})
. We may choose a neighborhood of
x_{0}
such that it is equivalent to a ball
B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
under local coordinates. We choose
\theta=\min(1,\frac{\varepsilon r_{0}^{2}}{400})
, and let
\eta
be a cut-off function on
M
satisfying the following conditions:

\eta(x_{0})=1,\,\,\eta\equiv 1-\theta,\,\textrm{on $M-B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})$},\,\,|\nabla\eta|\leq\frac{10\theta}{r_{0}},\,\,|D^{2}\eta|\leq\frac{10\theta}{r_{0}^{2}}.

Then we can compute

\begin{split}&L(e^{\rho}\eta)=e^{\rho}\eta L\rho+e^{\rho}\eta\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}\rho_{j}\rho_{\bar{k}}+e^{\rho}L\eta+2e^{\rho}Re\big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}\rho_{j}\eta_{\bar{k}}\big)\\
&\geq-\varepsilon e^{\rho}\eta(\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\varphi)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}[h_{\varphi}]_{j}^{i})+e^{\rho}\varepsilon\partial_{0}f\eta\\
&+e^{\rho}\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}\big(\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}(\omega_{\psi})_{j\bar{k}}\big)\eta+e^{\rho}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ii}}\eta\\
&+e^{\rho}L\eta+e^{\rho}\eta\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}\rho_{j}\rho_{\bar{k}}+2e^{\rho}Re(\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}\rho_{j}\eta_{\bar{k}}).\end{split}

In the last term above, we can estimate:

2e^{\rho}Re\big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}\rho_{j}\eta_{\bar{k}}\big)\geq-e^{\rho}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}\rho_{j}\rho_{\bar{k}}-e^{\rho}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}\eta_{j}\eta_{\bar{k}}\geq-e^{\rho}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}\rho_{j}\rho_{\bar{k}}-\frac{100\theta^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}}e^{\rho}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ii}}.

Also

e^{\rho}L\eta\geq-e^{\rho}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ii}}\frac{10\theta}{r_{0}^{2}}.

Therefore

\begin{split}&L(e^{\rho}\eta)\geq-\varepsilon e^{\rho}\eta\big(\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\varphi)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(\omega_{\varphi})_{i\bar{j}}\big)\\
&+\varepsilon e^{\rho}\partial_{0}f\eta+e^{\rho}\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}(\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}(\omega_{\psi})_{i\bar{j}})\eta\\
&+e^{\rho}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g_{i\bar{j}}\big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\frac{100\theta}{r_{0}^{2}}-\frac{100\theta^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}}\big)\eta.\end{split}

Because of our choice of
\varepsilon
and
\theta
, we have that

(3.7)

\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\frac{100\theta}{r_{0}^{2}}-\frac{100\theta^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}}>0.

Therefore

(3.8)

\begin{split}&L(e^{\rho}\eta)\geq e^{\rho}\eta\big(-\varepsilon\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\varphi)-\varepsilon\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}[h_{\varphi}]^{i}_{j}+\varepsilon\partial_{0}f\\
&+\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}(\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}(\omega_{\psi})_{j\bar{k}})\big).\end{split}

If we put
u=e^{\rho}\eta
and denote the right hand side of (
3.8
) to be
R
, then (
3.8
) is equivalent to:

-\partial_{t}u+\frac{1}{\partial_{0}f}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}\partial_{i\bar{j}}u\geq\frac{1}{\partial_{0}f}R.

We wish to apply the parabolic Alexandrov maximum principle (Lemma ?) in
[0,T]\times B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
so that

(3.9)

\sup_{[0,T]\times B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}e^{\rho}\eta\leq\max\big(\sup_{[0,T]\times\partial B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}e^{\rho}\eta,\sup_{\{0\}\times B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}e^{\rho}\eta\big)+C_{n}r_{0}\bigg(\int_{E}\frac{(\frac{1}{\partial_{0}f}R^{-})^{2n+1}}{(\det(\frac{1}{\partial_{0}f}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}))^{2}}\omega_{0}^{n}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}.

In the above,
R^{-}=\max(R,0)
,
E
is the set of
(t,x)
on which
\partial_{t}u\geq 0
,
D^{2}u\leq 0
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that

(3.10)

\sup_{[0,T]\times M}\rho=K>0.

Also from our choice of
x_{0}
, we would have
\sup_{[0,T]\times B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}e^{\rho}\eta\geq e^{K}
. On the right hand side, we have

(3.11)

\sup_{[0,T]\times\partial B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}e^{\rho}\eta\leq(1-\theta)e^{K},\,\,\,\sup_{\{0\}\times B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}e^{\rho}\eta\leq 1.

The second inequality is due to that, according to our choice of
s
, we have
\rho\leq 0
for
t=0
.
It only remains to estimate the integral in (
3.9
). We claim that:

Claim 3.7
.

On the set
E
, we have that
\partial_{t}\varphi<0
,
\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi>0
. In particular, we have that on
E
,
(-\partial_{t}\varphi,\lambda[h_{\varphi}])\in\Gamma_{+}
. (the positive cone)

Indeed, on the set
E
, we have that
\partial_{t}u\geq 0
,
u_{i\bar{j}}\leq 0
, which translates to

0\leq\partial_{t}u=e^{\rho}\partial_{t}\rho\eta=e^{\rho}(-\varepsilon-\varepsilon\partial_{t}\varphi+\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}\partial_{t}\psi),

Note that
\partial_{t}\psi\leq 0
, we immediately have
\partial_{t}\varphi<0
.
On the other hand,

\begin{split}&0\geq u_{i\bar{j}}=e^{\rho}\rho_{i\bar{j}}+e^{\rho}\rho_{i}\rho_{\bar{j}}+e^{\rho}\eta_{i\bar{j}}+e^{\rho}(\rho_{i}\eta_{\bar{j}}+\rho_{\bar{j}}\eta_{i})\\
&\geq e^{\rho}\big(-\varepsilon\varphi_{i\bar{j}}+\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{1-\alpha}\psi_{i\bar{j}}+\alpha(1-\alpha)(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-2}\psi_{i}\psi_{\bar{j}}\big)\\
&+e^{\rho}\rho_{i}\rho_{\bar{j}}-e^{\rho}\frac{100\theta}{r_{0}^{2}}g_{i\bar{j}}-e^{\rho}\rho_{i}\rho_{\bar{j}}-e^{\rho}\eta_{i}\eta_{\bar{j}}\\
&\geq e^{\rho}\big(-\varepsilon(g_{i\bar{j}}+\varphi_{i\bar{j}})+\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}(g_{i\bar{j}}+\psi_{i\bar{j}})+(\varepsilon-\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}\\
&-\frac{100\theta}{r_{0}^{2}}-\frac{100\theta^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}})g_{i\bar{j}}\big).\end{split}

Note that because of (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we have that
\varepsilon-\alpha(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}-\frac{100\theta}{r_{0}^{2}}-\frac{100\theta^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}}>0
. Also we have
g_{i\bar{j}}+\psi_{i\bar{j}}>0
, therefore
g_{i\bar{j}}+\varphi_{i\bar{j}}>0
and the claim is proved.

The above Claim
3.7
guarantees that we can use structural assumption (3) and (4) to estimate the integral on the right hand side of (
3.9
).
We first have that

(3.12)

\int_{E}\frac{(\frac{1}{\partial_{0}f}R^{-})^{2n+1}}{(\det(\frac{1}{\partial_{0}f}\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}))^{2}}\omega_{0}^{n}dt=\int_{E}\frac{\partial_{0}f(R^{-})^{2n+1}}{(\partial_{0}f\det(\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}))^{2}}\omega_{0}^{n}dt\leq\int_{\{R<0\}\cap E}c_{0}^{-2}\partial_{0}f(R^{-})^{2n+1}\omega_{0}^{n}dt.

Now we go back to (
3.8
), and found that for
(t,x)\in E
, we have, using the structural assumption on
f
:

\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\varphi)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}[h_{\varphi}]_{i}^{j}\leq C_{0}f.

Also from Arithemetic-Geometric Inequality:

\begin{split}&\partial_{0}f(-\partial_{t}\psi)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}g^{i\bar{k}}(\omega_{\psi})_{j\bar{k}}\geq(n+1)\big(\partial_{0}f\det(\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}})(-\partial_{t}\psi)\frac{\omega_{\psi}^{n}}{\omega_{0}^{n}}\big)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\\
&\geq(n+1)\big(c_{0}Ent_{p}^{-1}(F)e^{(n+1)F}(F^{2}+1)^{\frac{p}{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}.\end{split}

Therefore, on the set
E
, the right hand side of (
3.8
) satisfies

(3.13)

R\geq e^{\rho}\eta f\big(-\varepsilon C_{0}+\varepsilon\frac{\partial_{0}f}{f}+\alpha(n+1)c_{0}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}Ent_{p}^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}(F)(F^{2}+1)^{\frac{p}{2(n+1)}}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\alpha-1}\big).

Therefore, on the set
E\cap\{R<0\}
, we have that

\begin{split}&\partial_{0}f\leq C_{0}f,\,\,\,(F^{2}+1)^{\frac{p}{2(n+1)}}\leq\varepsilon C_{0}\alpha^{-1}(n+1)^{-1}c_{0}^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}Ent_{p}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}(F)(-\psi+\Lambda)^{1-\alpha},\\
&R^{-}\leq e^{\rho}\eta f(\varepsilon C_{0}).\end{split}

Therefore

(3.14)

f=e^{F}\leq\exp\big((\varepsilon C_{0}\alpha^{-1}(n+1)^{-1})^{\frac{n+1}{p}}c_{0}^{-\frac{1}{p}}Ent_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}(F)(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\frac{(1-\alpha)(n+1)}{p}}\big).

Combinning (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), we may continue the estimate in (
3.12
):

\begin{split}&\int_{\{R<0\}\cap E}c_{0}^{-2}\partial_{0}f(R^{-})^{2n+1}\omega_{0}^{n}dt\leq\int_{[0,T]\times M}c_{0}^{-2}C_{0}f(\varepsilon C_{0})^{2n+1}e^{(2n+1)\rho}f^{2n+1}\omega_{0}^{n}dt\\
&\leq\int_{[0,T]\times M}c_{0}^{-2}\varepsilon^{2n+1}C_{0}^{2n+2}e^{(2n+1)\rho}\\
&\times\exp\big((2n+2)(\varepsilon C_{0}\alpha^{-1}(n+1)^{-1})^{\frac{n+1}{p}}c_{0}^{-\frac{1}{p}}Ent_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}(F)(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\frac{(1-\alpha)(n+1)}{p}}\big)\omega_{0}^{n}dt.\end{split}

Note that because of our choice of
\varepsilon
, we have that

(2n+2)(\varepsilon C_{0}\alpha^{-1}(n+1)^{-1})^{\frac{n+1}{p}}c_{0}^{-\frac{1}{p}}Ent_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}(F)\leq\alpha_{0}.

Here
\alpha_{0}
is the
\alpha
-invariant of the class
[\omega_{0}]
. Therefore

(3.15)

\begin{split}&\int_{\{R<0\}\cap E}c_{0}^{-2}\partial_{0}f(R^{-})^{2n+1}\omega_{0}^{n}dt\\
&\leq e^{(2n+1)K}\int_{[0,T]\times M}c_{0}^{-2}\varepsilon^{2n+1}C_{0}^{2n+2}\exp\big(\alpha_{0}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{\frac{(1-\alpha)(n+1)}{p}}\big)\omega_{0}^{n}dt\\
&\leq e^{(2n+1)K}c_{0}^{-2}\varepsilon^{2n+1}C_{0}^{2n+2}\int_{[0,T]\times M}\exp\big(-\alpha_{0}\psi+\alpha_{0}\Lambda)\omega_{0}^{n}dt\\
&\leq e^{(2n+1)K}c_{0}^{-2}\varepsilon^{2n+1}C_{0}^{2n+2}e^{\alpha_{0}\Lambda}T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{M}e^{-\alpha_{0}\psi}\omega_{0}^{n}.\end{split}

We have seen in (
3.5
) that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{M}e^{-\alpha_{0}\psi}\omega_{0}^{n}\leq C_{*}.

Here
C_{*}
depends only on the background metric.
So that after combinning (
3.9
), (
3.10
), (
3.11
), (
3.15
), we obtain that

(3.16)

e^{K}\leq\max\big((1-\theta)e^{K},e^{||\rho_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}}\big)+T^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}C_{n}r_{0}e^{K}\varepsilon(c_{0}^{-2}C_{0}^{2n+2}e^{\alpha_{0}\Lambda}C_{*})^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}.

Therefore, if we choose
T
small enough so that

(3.17)

T^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}C_{n}r_{0}\varepsilon(c_{0}^{-2}C_{0}^{2n+2}e^{\alpha_{0}\Lambda}C_{*})^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}=\frac{\theta}{2},

we would have that the max in (
3.16
) is achieved by
e^{||\rho_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}}
, which gives us that
K\leq||\rho_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}.

Denote the

given by (
3.17

, then we get an estimate for
\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\rho
.

given by (

, we may repeat the same argument to estimate

Here we state without proof the following parabolic Alexandrove maximum principle we used in the above proof (see

]
, Theorem 7.1):

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain and
u
be a smooth function on
[0,T]\times\Omega
, such that

-\partial_{t}u+a^{ij}\partial_{ij}u\geq f.

Then

\sup_{[0,T]\times\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\partial_{P}([0,T]\times\Omega)}u+C_{n}(\mbox{diam}\Omega)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\bigg(\int_{E}\frac{(f^{-})^{n+1}}{\det a^{ij}}dxdt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}.

In the above,
\partial_{P}([0,T]\times\Omega)=(\{0\}\times\Omega)\cup([0,T]\times\partial\Omega)
,
f^{-}=\max(-f,0)
and

E
is the set of
(t,x)
on which
\partial_{t}u\geq 0
and
D_{x}^{2}u\leq 0
.

So we have shown that, the right hand side of the estimate in Proposition
3.4
is uniformly bounded with the said dependence. Then we can proceed in the same way as Section 2 to obtain the
L^{\infty}
bound (note that from Lemma
2.7
and on, we only used the Moser-Trudinger inequality but not the equation).

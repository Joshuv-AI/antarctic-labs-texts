Defect capturing and charging dynamics and their effects on magneto-transport of electrons in quantum wells
Andrii Iurov, 1 Danhong Huang, 2 Godfrey Gumbs, 3 Paula Fekete, 4 and Fei Gao 5

Abstract
The defect corrections to polarization and dielectric functions of Bloch electrons in quantum wells are first calculated. Following this, we derive the first two moment equations from Boltzmann transport theory and apply them to explore defect effects on magneto-transport of Bloch electrons. Meanwhile, we obtain analytically the momentum-relaxation time and mobility tensor for Bloch electrons making use of the screened defect-corrected polarization function. Based on quantum-statistical theory, we further investigate the defect capture and charging dynamics by employing a parameterized physics model for defects to obtain defect wave functions. After this, both capture and relaxation rates, as well as density for captured Bloch electrons, are calculated self-consistently as functions of temperature, doping density and different defect types. By applying the energy-balance equation, the number of occupied energy levels and chemical potential of defects are determined, with which the transition rate for defect capturing is obtained. By using these results, defect energy-relaxation, capture and escape rates, and Bloch-electron chemical potential are obtained self-consistently. At the same time, the Bloch-electron energy- and momentum-relaxation rates, as well as the current suppression factor, are also investigated quantitatively. Finally, by combining all these studies together, the temperature dependence of the Hall and longitudinal mobilities is demonstrated for Bloch electrons in either single- or multi-quantum wells, which can be utilized for quantifying burst noise in transistors and blinking noise in photo-detectors.

are the static and optic dielectric constants of the host semiconductor, while

{\cal B}_{n,\ell,m}(|\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|}+\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{\|}|,z)
of a defect is

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{n,\ell,m}(|\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|}+\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{\|}|,z)\equiv\int d^{2}{\bf r}_{\|}\,\psi_{n,\ell,m}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\|},z)\,\texttt{e}^{-i({\bf q}_{\|}+{\bf k}_{\|})\cdot{\bf r}_{\|}}

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi}\,\texttt{e}^{im\theta_{{\bf k}_{\|}+{\bf q}_{\|}}}(-i)^{m}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dr_{\|}\,r_{\|}J_{m}(|\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|}+\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{\|}|r_{\|})\,R_{n,\ell}\left(\sqrt{r^{2}_{\|}+z^{2}}\right)\,\overline{Y}_{\ell,m}\left(z/\sqrt{r_{\|}^{2}+z^{2}}\right)\ .

In Eq. (
38
),

\psi_{n,\ell,m}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\|},z)
has been employed from Eq. (
67
),
J_{m}(x)
is the first-kind Bessel function of order
m
, and

\displaystyle{\theta_{{\bf k}_{\|}+{\bf q}_{\|}}=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{k_{y}+q_{y}}{k_{x}+q_{x}}\right)}
is the angle between the wave vector
\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{\|}+\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|}=\{k_{x}+q_{x},k_{y}+q_{y}\}
and positive
x
-axis.
Here, the prefactor in front of the integral with respect to
r_{\|}
in Eq. (
38
) only contributes a
2\pi
factor in Eq. (
37
).

Based on the calculated transition rate
{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},\xi)
in Eq. (
36
) and
thermal-equilibrium energy-level occupation
f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})
in Eq. (
75
), as well as using the defect-relaxation time approximation,
we arrive at the following dynamical equation for non-equilibrium occupation
{\cal P}_{n}
of bound electrons on the
n
th energy level of a defect, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left(1-{\cal P}_{n}\right)\frac{2}{\rho_{0}(u_{0}){\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\,f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})\,{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)-{\cal P}_{n}\,\frac{2}{\rho_{0}(u_{0}){\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\,\left[1-f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})\right]

(39)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\texttt{e}^{-\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}/k_{B}T}\,{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)+\left(1-{\cal P}_{n}\right)\sum_{n^{\prime}>n}\,{\cal D}_{n,n^{\prime}}(T)\,{\cal P}_{n^{\prime}}-{\cal P}_{n}\sum_{n>n^{\prime}}\,{\cal D}_{n^{\prime},n}(T)\left(1-{\cal P}_{n^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(1-{\cal P}_{n}\right)\sum_{n^{\prime}<n}\,{\cal D}_{n,n^{\prime}}(T)\,{\cal P}_{n^{\prime}}\,\texttt{e}^{-|E_{n}-E_{n^{\prime}}|/k_{B}T}-{\cal P}_{n}\sum_{n<n^{\prime}}\,{\cal D}_{n^{\prime},n}(T)\left(1-{\cal P}_{n^{\prime}}\right)\texttt{e}^{-|E_{n}-E_{n^{\prime}}|/k_{B}T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{{\cal P}_{n}-f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})}{\tau^{\rm d}_{n}(T,u_{0})}\ ,

where the non-radiative transition rate
{\cal D}_{n^{\prime},n}(T)
from the
n
th to the
n^{\prime}
th energy level is

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{n^{\prime},n}(T)=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\,\exp\left\{-[1+2N_{0}(\Omega_{0})]\,{\cal S}_{0}+\frac{|E_{n}-E_{n^{\prime}}|}{2k_{B}T}\right\}\,\left(1-\frac{|E_{n}-E_{n^{\prime}}|}{\hbar\Omega_{0}{\cal S}_{0}}\right)^{2}\frac{{\cal S}_{0}}{\hbar\Omega_{0}}\,

(40)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\infty}\,I_{\alpha}(\eta_{0})\,\delta\left(\alpha-\frac{|E_{n}-E_{n^{\prime}}|}{\hbar\Omega_{0}}\right)\,\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime},m,m^{\prime}}\,\left|\langle\psi_{n^{\prime},\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\|},z)|U_{\rm ep}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\|},z)|\psi_{n,\ell,m}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\|},z)\rangle\right|^{2}\ .\ \ \ \ \ \

In addition, the defect-averaged transition rate
{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T,\xi)
in Eq. (
36
) becomes

{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)=\frac{1}{N_{\rm d}}\int\limits_{-{\cal L}_{0}/2}^{{\cal L}_{0}/2}d\xi\,\rho_{\rm d}(\xi)\,{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T,\xi)\ ,

(41)

the
n
th-level
defect-relaxation time
,
\tau^{\rm d}_{n}(T,u_{0},E^{*})
, is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau^{\rm d}_{n}(T,u_{0},E^{*})}=\frac{2}{\rho_{0}(u_{0}){\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\,{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)\left\{f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})+\left[1-f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})\right]\texttt{e}^{-\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}/k_{B}T}\right\}

(42)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{n^{\prime}>n}\,{\cal D}_{n,n^{\prime}}(T)\,f_{0}(E_{n^{\prime}}-E^{*})+\sum_{n^{\prime}<n}\,{\cal D}_{n,n^{\prime}}(T)\,f_{0}(E_{n^{\prime}}-E^{*})\,\texttt{e}^{-|E_{n}-E_{n^{\prime}}|/k_{B}T}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{n>n^{\prime}}\,{\cal D}_{n^{\prime},n}(T)\left[1-f_{0}(E_{n^{\prime}}-E^{*})\right]+\sum_{n<n^{\prime}}\,{\cal D}_{n^{\prime},n}(T)\left[1-f_{0}(E_{n^{\prime}}-E^{*})\right]\texttt{e}^{-|E_{n}-E_{n^{\prime}}|/k_{B}T}\ ,\ \ \ \ \ \

and
\displaystyle{N_{\rm d}=\int\limits_{-{\cal L}_{0}/2}^{{\cal L}_{0}/2}d\xi\,\rho_{\rm d}(\xi)}
represents the total number of defects in a single quantum-well structure.
For
k_{B}T\sim\hbar\Omega_{0}<\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}
(i.e., insignificant single-phonon emission regime) and
{\cal D}_{n^{\prime},n}(T)\ll{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)
(i.e., neglecting much weaker interlevel non-radiative transitions within a defect),
Equation (
42
) further leads to a statistically-averaged defect-relaxation time
\tau^{\rm d}_{\rm rel}(T,u_{0},E^{*})
for all captured electrons
inside a defect, yielding

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau^{\rm d}_{\rm rel}(T,u_{0},E^{*})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{n^{2}{\cal P}_{n}}{\tau^{\rm d}_{n}(T,u_{0},E^{*})}\approx\frac{2}{Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,n^{2}{\cal P}_{n}\,

(43)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\rho_{0}(u_{0}){\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\,{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)\left[f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})\left(1-\texttt{e}^{-\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}/k_{B}T}\right)+\texttt{e}^{-\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}/k_{B}T}\right]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{4}{Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,n^{2}f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})\,\frac{1}{\rho_{0}(u_{0}){\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\,{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left\{f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})+\left[1-f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})\right]\,\texttt{e}^{-\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}/k_{B}T}\right\}\ .

As a result, we obtain the non-equilibrium occupation function
{\cal P}_{n}(T,E^{*},u_{0})

for the
n
th energy level of a defect, given by

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{n}(T,u_{0},E^{*})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})

(44)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\tau^{\rm d}_{\rm rel}(T,u_{0},E^{*})\left[1-f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})\right]\frac{2}{\rho_{0}(u_{0}){\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\,f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})\,{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)\ ,

which can be employed for calculating any average physical quantities associated with a defect.
Meanwhile, using the calculated
{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)
and
f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})
, we obtain
the statistically-averaged capture rate
\displaystyle{\gamma_{n}^{({\rm c})}(T,u_{0})}
for subband electrons by the
n
th energy level of a defect, that is,

\gamma_{n}^{({\rm c})}(T,u_{0})=\frac{2}{\rho_{0}(u_{0}){\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\,\,{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)\,f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})\ .

(45)

Again, this gives rise to a statistically-averaged capture rate
\gamma^{\rm d}_{\rm cap}(T,u_{0},E^{*})
by a defect, i.e.,

\displaystyle\gamma^{\rm d}_{\rm cap}(T,u_{0},E^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,2n^{2}\,\gamma_{n}^{({\rm c})}(T,u_{0})\left(1-P_{n}\right)

(46)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,2n^{2}\left[1-f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})\right]\frac{2}{\rho_{0}(u_{0}){\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\,\,{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)\,f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})\ .\ \ \ \ \ \

In a similar way, we also find a statistically-averaged escape rate
\gamma^{\rm d}_{\rm esc}(T,u_{0},E^{*})
by a defect, given by

\displaystyle\gamma^{\rm d}_{\rm esc}(T,u_{0},E^{*})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,2n^{2}f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})\,\texttt{e}^{-\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}/k_{B}T}

(47)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\rho_{0}(u_{0}){\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\,\,{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)\left[1-f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})\right]\ ,

where
\gamma^{\rm d}_{\rm esc}(T,u_{0},E^{*})<\gamma^{\rm d}_{\rm cap}(T,u_{0},E^{*})
if
\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}>k_{B}T
.
Finally, from Eq. (
44
) we get the density
\rho_{\rm cap}(T,u_{0},E^{*})
for total captured subband electrons by all defects in a quantum well, yielding

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm cap}(T,u_{0},E^{*})=N_{\rm d}\left\{\tau^{\rm d}_{\rm rel}(T,u_{0},E^{*})\left[\gamma^{\rm d}_{\rm cap}(T,u_{0},E^{*})-\gamma^{\rm d}_{\rm esc}(T,u_{0},E^{*})\right]\right\}\rho_{0}(u_{0})

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{\rm d}\,\tau^{\rm d}_{\rm rel}(T,u_{0},E^{*})\,\frac{2}{{\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\,\frac{1}{Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,2n^{2}{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left\{f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})\left[1-f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})\left(1-\texttt{e}^{-\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}/k_{B}T}\,\right)\right]-f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})\,\texttt{e}^{-\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}/k_{B}T}\right\}\ .\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \

In combination with Eqs. (
46
) and (
48
), the charge-conservation law in Eq. (
27
) turns into

\displaystyle n_{\rm dop}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(u_{0})+\rho_{\rm cap}(T,u_{0},E^{*})=\frac{2}{{\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\,\left(f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})+\frac{N_{\rm d}\tau^{\rm d}_{\rm rel}(T,u_{0},E^{*})}{Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,2n^{2}{\cal T}_{n}(k_{\|},T)\,\right.

(49)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left.\left\{f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})\left[1-f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})\left(1-\texttt{e}^{-\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}/k_{B}T}\,\right)\right]-f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})\,\texttt{e}^{-\Delta E_{n,k_{\|}}/k_{B}T}\right\}\right)\ ,

from which the chemical potential
u_{0}
can be obtained as the root of Eq. (
49
) for any fixed values of
n_{\rm dop}
and
T
, as well as for
E^{*}
determined from Eq. (
75
).

VII
Conclusions and Remarks

In conclusion, we have calculated the defect corrections to the polarization and dielectric functions of Bloch electrons in quantum wells. Meanwhile,
we have also derived the first two moment equations from the semi-classical Boltzmann transport theory and applied them for investigating defect effects on magneto-transport of electrons.
By using this defect-corrected polarization function, we have further acquired analytically both the momentum-relaxation time and the mobility tensor for Bloch electrons.
Using quantum-statistical theory, we have explored defect capture and charging dynamics based on a parameterized quantum-mechanics model for defects and by employing a finite-range probability function.
Consequently, we have obtained both the capture, escape and relaxation rates and the density of captured Bloch electrons as functions of temperature, doping density and various types of defects.
Such a microscopic-level theory is very important and applicable to many electronic systems, including accurately quantifying burst noise in transistors and blinking noise in photo-detectors.

In particular, by employing the energy-balance equation, we have first determined the number of occupied energy levels
N^{*}
and the chemical potential
E^{*}
of defects for captured Bloch electrons as a function of temperature
T
with various values of defect binding energy and binding radius. Meanwhile, we have also studied the transition rate
{\cal T}(k_{\|},T)
for defect-capturing process as functions of Bloch-electron wave number
k_{\|}
and
T
. Based on calculated
E^{*}
and
{\cal T}(k_{\|},T)
, we have further explored the defect energy-relaxation
\tau^{d}_{rel}
, capture
\gamma^{d}_{cap}
and escape
\gamma^{d}_{esc}
rates, in addition to the self-consistent chemical potential
u_{0}(T)
of Bloch electrons, as functions of
T
and doping density
n_{dop}
. On the other hand, we have further calculated the average Bloch-electron energy-relaxation
1/\tau_{0}(T)
and momentum-relaxation
1/\tau_{p}(T)
rates, as well as the Bloch-electron current suppression factor
s_{c}(T,u_{0},E^{*})
, as a function of
T
. Finally, we have investigated the magnetic-field
B_{3}
and
T
dependence of both Hall
\mu_{H}(T,B_{3})
and longitudinal
\mu_{L}(T,B_{3})
mobilities of Bloch electrons in single and triple quantum wells.

Appendix A
Energy-Relaxation Time

By using the detailed-balance condition, the energy-relaxation time
\tau_{\rm d}(k_{\|})
initially introduced in Eq. (
22
) for defects can be calculated according to

\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm d}(k_{\|})}={\cal W}_{\rm in}(k_{\|})+{\cal W}_{\rm out}(k_{\|})=\frac{4m^{*}\sigma_{\rm d}}{\pi\hbar^{3}}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,\left|U_{0}(2k_{\|}|\cos\theta|)\right|^{2}\ ,

(50)

where
\sigma_{\rm d}
is the point-defect areal density. The scattering-in rate in Eq. (
50
) for electrons in the final
{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}_{\|}
-state is

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{\rm in}(k_{\|})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\sigma_{\rm d}}{\hbar{\cal A}}\,\sum_{{\bf q}_{\|}}\,\left|U_{0}(q_{\|})\right|^{2}\left[f_{{\bf k}_{\|}-{\bf q}_{\|}}\,\delta(\varepsilon_{{\bf k}_{\|}}-\varepsilon_{{\bf k}_{\|}-{\bf q}_{\|}})+f_{{\bf k}_{\|}+{\bf q}_{\|}}\,\delta(\varepsilon_{{\bf k}_{\|}}-\varepsilon_{{\bf k}_{\|}+{\bf q}_{\|}})\right]

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4m^{*}\sigma_{\rm d}}{\pi\hbar^{3}}\right)f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}})\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,\left|U_{0}(2k_{\|}|\cos\theta|)\right|^{2}\ ,

and the scattering-out rate for electrons in the initial
{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}_{\|}
-state is

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{\rm out}(k_{\|})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\sigma_{\rm d}}{\hbar{\cal A}}\,\sum_{{\bf q}_{\|}}\,\left|U_{0}(q_{\|})\right|^{2}

(52)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[(1-f_{{\bf k}_{\|}+{\bf q}_{\|}})\,\delta(\varepsilon_{{\bf k}_{\|}+{\bf q}_{\|}}-\varepsilon_{{\bf k}_{\|}})+(1-f_{{\bf k}_{\|}-{\bf q}_{\|}})\,\delta(\varepsilon_{{\bf k}_{\|}-{\bf q}_{\|}}-\varepsilon_{{\bf k}_{\|}})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4m^{*}\sigma_{\rm d}}{\pi\hbar^{3}}\right)\,[1-f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}})]\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,\left|U_{0}(2k_{\|}|\cos\theta|)\right|^{2}\ .

For simplicity, we have introduced the notations, i.e.,
f_{{\bf k}_{\|}}\equiv f_{0}(\varepsilon_{k_{\|}}-u_{0})
.
We further assume that electrons are remotely doped, temperature
T
is low, and areal electron density
\rho_{0}
is low.
As a result, we can neglect the electron scatterings with ionized impurities, lattice phonons and other electrons,

For the electron-defect scattering in a quantum well, its interaction introduced in Eqs. (
51
) and (
52
) takes the form

\displaystyle\left|U_{0}(q_{\|})\right|^{2}=\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a_{\rm d}}\right)^{2}\,\left[\frac{Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})\,e^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\rm d}}\right]^{2}\,\frac{\texttt{e}^{-q^{2}_{\|}\Lambda^{2}_{\|}/2}}{(q_{\|}+q_{\rm s})^{2}}

(53)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-{\cal L}_{0}/2}^{{\cal L}_{0}/2}d\xi\,\rho_{\rm d}(\xi)\left[\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dz\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz^{\prime}\,\left|{\cal F}_{0}(z)\right|^{2}\left(\texttt{e}^{-q_{\|}|z-z^{\prime}|}+\texttt{e}^{-q_{\|}|z+z^{\prime}|}\right){\cal Q}_{1}(q_{\|},z^{\prime}-\xi)\right]^{2},\ \ \ \ \

where

is the charge number of defects,

and
a_{0}=5\,
Å are the void and point-vacancy binding radii,

the dielectric constant of the host semiconductor,

Mobility Tensor

From the force-balance equation in Eq. (
23
), by using
\displaystyle{\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\tau}$}_{p}}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\tau_{j}}\,\delta_{ij}}
for simplicity,
we get the following two inhomogeneous linear equations for
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{d}=\{v_{1},v_{2},0\}

\left(1+q\tau_{1}r_{12}B_{3}\,\right)v_{1}-q\tau_{1}r_{11}B_{3}\,v_{2}=q\tau_{1}\left(r_{11}E_{1}+r_{12}E_{2}\right)\ ,

(54)

q\tau_{2}r_{22}B_{3}\,v_{1}+\left(1-q\tau_{2}r_{21}B_{3}\right)v_{2}=q\tau_{2}\left(r_{21}E_{1}+r_{22}E_{2}\right)\ ,

(55)

where we have used the notations
{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}=\{0,0,B_{3}\}
,
{\mbox{\boldmath$E$}}=\{E_{1},E_{2},0\}
, and
\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\cal M}$}}^{-1}=\{r_{ij}\}
for

.
By defining the coefficient matrix
\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\cal C}$}}
for the above linear equations, i.e.,

\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\cal C}$}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1+q\tau_{1}r_{12}B_{3}&-q\tau_{1}r_{11}B_{3}\\
q\tau_{2}r_{22}B_{3}&1-q\tau_{2}r_{21}B_{3}\end{array}\right]\ ,

(56)

as well as the source vector
s
, given by

{\mbox{\boldmath$s$}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}q\tau_{1}(r_{11}E_{1}+r_{12}E_{2})\\
q\tau_{2}(r_{21}E_{1}+r_{22}E_{2})\end{array}\right]\ ,

(57)

we can reduce the above linear equations to a matrix equation
\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\cal C}$}}\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{d}={\mbox{\boldmath$s$}}
with the formal solution

\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{d}=\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\cal C}$}}^{-1}\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$s$}}
. Explicitly, we find the solution
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{d}=\{v_{1},\,v_{2},\,0\}

for
j=1,\,2
by

v_{j}=\frac{Det\{\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\Delta}$}}_{j}\}}{Det\{\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\cal C}$}}\}}\ ,

(58)

where

\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\Delta}$}}_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}q\tau_{1}(r_{11}E_{1}+r_{12}E_{2})&-q\tau_{1}r_{11}B_{3}\\
q\tau_{2}(r_{21}E_{1}+r_{22}E_{2})&1-q\tau_{2}r_{21}B_{3}\end{array}\right]\ ,

(59)

\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\Delta}$}}_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1+q\tau_{1}r_{12}B_{3}&q\tau_{1}(r_{11}E_{1}+r_{12}E_{2})\\
q\tau_{2}r_{22}B_{3}&q\tau_{2}(r_{21}E_{1}+r_{22}E_{2})\end{array}\right]\ .

(60)

Moreover, by assuming
r_{ij}=0
for
i\neq j
,
\displaystyle{r_{jj}=\frac{1}{m_{j}^{\ast}}}
and introducing the notation
\displaystyle{\mu_{j}=\frac{q\tau_{j}}{m_{j}^{\ast}}}
, we find

\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\cal C}$}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-\mu_{1}B_{3}\\
\mu_{2}B_{3}&1\end{array}\right]\ ,

(61)

\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\Delta}$}}_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mu_{1}E_{1}&-\mu_{1}B_{3}\\
\mu_{2}E_{2}&1\end{array}\right]\ ,

(62)

\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\Delta}$}}_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\mu_{1}E_{1}\\
\mu_{2}B_{3}&\mu_{2}E_{2}\end{array}\right]\ ,

(63)

and

Det\{\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\cal C}$}}\}=1+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\,B_{3}^{2}\ ,

Det\{\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\Delta}$}}_{1}\}=\mu_{1}E_{1}+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\,B_{3}E_{2}\ ,

Det\{\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\Delta}$}}_{2}\}=\mu_{2}E_{2}-\mu_{2}\mu_{1}\,B_{3}E_{1}\ .

From the above results, we finally arrive at

\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\mu}$}}(B_{3})=\frac{1}{1+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}B_{3}^{2}}\,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mu_{1}&\mu_{1}\mu_{2}B_{3}\\
-\mu_{2}\mu_{1}B_{3}&\mu_{2}\end{array}\right]\ .

(64)

If we further assume
q=-e
,
m_{1}^{\ast}=m_{2}^{\ast}=m^{\ast}
and
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=\tau_{p}
, we obtain

Det\{\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\cal C}$}}\}=1+\mu^{2}_{0}B_{3}^{2}
,

Det\{\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\Delta}$}}_{1}\}=-\mu_{0}E_{1}+\mu_{0}^{2}B_{3}E_{2}
, and

Det\{\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\Delta}$}}_{2}\}=-\mu_{0}E_{2}-\mu_{0}^{2}B_{3}E_{1}
,
where
\displaystyle{\mu_{0}=\frac{e\tau_{p}}{m^{\ast}}}
. This gives rise to

\tensor{\mbox{\boldmath${\mu}$}}({\mbox{\boldmath$B$}})=-\frac{\mu_{0}}{1+\mu_{0}^{2}B_{3}^{2}}\,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-\mu_{0}B_{3}\\
\mu_{0}B_{3}&1\end{array}\right]\ .

(65)

Appendix C
Defect Effective Potential and Occupation

The Schrödinger equation for electrons bounded to a defect can be formally written as
dft-3

-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{\ast}}\,\nabla_{{\bf r}}^{2}\,\psi_{\nu}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})+[V_{L}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})-V_{D}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})]\,\psi_{\nu}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})=E_{\nu}\,\psi_{\nu}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})\ ,

(66)

where
V_{L}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})
is the full crystal lattice-potential energy,

V_{D}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})\approx V_{L}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})-U_{A}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})
represents the deformed lattice-potential energy by a defect,

U_{A}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})
is the local atomic-potential energy for a point vacancy,

m^{\ast}
is the effective mass of Bloch electrons,
and
\psi_{\nu}({\bf r})
and
E_{\nu}
represent the eigen-function and eigen-energy of defect-bound electrons, respectively.
The accurate calculation of
V_{D}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})
can be done with first-principles density-functional theory for a point defect.
dft-1
;
dft-2
;
dft-3

For simplicity, we assume
\displaystyle{U_{A}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})=-\frac{Z_{d}e^{2}}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\rm s}r}}
for a spherical defect with
Z_{d}

approximately as a net charge number and
\epsilon_{\rm s}
being the dielectric constant of a host material.
Moreover, we assume the defect-state energy levels
\displaystyle{E_{n}=-\frac{E_{\rm d}}{n^{2}}}

for the principal quantum number
n=1,\,2,\,3,\,\cdots
,
E_{\rm d}
for the binding energy,
and
a_{\rm d}
for the void radius, as well as the corresponding wave functions

\displaystyle\psi_{\nu}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\psi_{n,\ell,m}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})=R_{n,\ell}(r^{*})\,\overline{Y}_{\ell,m}(\cos\theta)\,\frac{\texttt{e}^{im\phi}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\ ,

\displaystyle R_{n,\ell}(r^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N^{(1)}_{n\ell}\,\exp\left(-\frac{r^{*}}{n}\right)\,\left(\frac{2r^{*}}{n}\right)^{\ell}\,L^{\{2\ell+1\}}_{n+\ell}\left(\frac{2r^{*}}{n}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{\ell,m}(\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}\,N^{(2)}_{\ell m}\,P^{m}_{\ell}(\cos\theta)\ ,

(67)

where
\mbox{\boldmath$r$}\equiv\{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\|},z\}
is a position vector,
the angular-momentum quantum number
\ell=0,\,1,\,2,\,\cdots,\,n-1
,
the magnetic quantum number
m=0,\,\pm 1,\,\pm 2,\,\cdots,\,\pm\ell
,
r=\sqrt{r_{\|}^{2}+z^{2}}
,
\displaystyle{\cos\theta=\frac{z}{r}}
,
r^{*}=r/a_{\rm d}
,
and the two normalization factors are given by

\displaystyle N^{(1)}_{n\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left(\frac{2}{na_{\rm d}}\right)^{3}\frac{(n-\ell-1)!}{2n[(n+\ell)!]^{3}}}\ ,

\displaystyle N^{(2)}_{\ell,m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{(2\ell+1)\,(\ell-|m|)!}{2\,(\ell+|m|)!}}\ .

Additionally,
L_{n}^{\{\alpha\}}(x)
in Eq. (
67
) are the generalized Laguerre polynomials, and
P_{\ell}^{m}(x)
are the associated Legendre polynomials.

Using the obtained bound-electron energy levels
E_{n}=-E_{\rm d}/n^{2}
and wave functions
\psi_{n,\ell,m}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})
in Eq. (
67
), the effective potential energy

U_{\rm eff}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})
of a charged defect takes the form

U_{\rm eff}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})=\frac{2e^{2}}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\rm s}}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})\sum_{\ell,m}\,\int d^{3}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime}\,\frac{\left|\psi_{n,\ell,m}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime})\right|^{2}}{|\mbox{\boldmath$r$}-\mbox{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime}|}\ ,

(68)

where
f_{0}(x)=\left[1+\exp\left(x/k_{B}T\right)\right]^{-1}
is the Fermi function,

E^{*}\in\max\{-E_{\rm d},\,0\}
is the Fermi energy for bound electrons inside a defect, and
T
is the lattice temperature.
If we simply replace
\left|\psi_{n,\ell,m}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime})\right|^{2}
in Eq. (
68
) by a function
\delta(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime})
,
U_{\rm eff}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})
reduces to the well-known Coulomb potential energy

\displaystyle{U_{\rm c}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})=\frac{Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})\,e^{2}}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\rm s}r}}
with

Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,n^{2}f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})

(69)

as an effective charge number,
where
k_{B}T
much less than the level separation
\Delta E_{n}
is assumed in the last step, and
both the orbital and spin degeneracies of bound electrons are included.

By introducing a two-dimensional (2D) Fourier transform for a quantum-well system, we have

\frac{1}{|\mbox{\boldmath$r$}-\mbox{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime}|}=\frac{2\pi}{{\cal A}}\sum_{{\bf q}^{\prime}_{\|}}\,\exp[i\mbox{\boldmath$q$}^{\prime}_{\|}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\|}-\mbox{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime}_{\|})]\,\frac{\texttt{e}^{-q^{\prime}_{\|}|z-z^{\prime}|}}{q^{\prime}_{\|}}\ ,

(70)

where
{\cal A}
is the cross-sectional area of the quantum well. Then, from Eq. (
68
) we get the 2D Fourier transformed
U_{\rm eff}(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|},z)
, given by

\displaystyle U_{\rm eff}(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|},z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\|}\,U_{\rm eff}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\|},z)\,\exp(-i\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\|})

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\rm s}q_{\|}}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,f_{0}(E_{n}-E^{*})\int dz^{\prime}\,\texttt{e}^{-q_{\|}|z-z^{\prime}|}\,{\cal Q}_{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|},z^{\prime})\ ,\ \ \ \

where the partial form factor
{\cal Q}_{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|},z^{\prime})
is defined as

{\cal Q}_{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|},z^{\prime})=\int d^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime}_{\|}\,\exp(-i\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime}_{\|})\,\sum_{\ell,m}\,\left|\psi_{n,\ell,m}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime}_{\|},z^{\prime})\right|^{2}\ .

(72)

By approximating

\left|\psi_{n,\ell,m}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\|},z)\right|^{2}\approx\frac{1}{\pi}\,\left(\frac{1}{a_{\rm d}}\right)^{3}\,\exp\left[-(2/a_{\rm d})\,(r_{\|}^{2}+z^{2})^{1/2}\right]

for the lowest
n=1
eigenstate, we arrive at an explicit expression

U_{\rm eff}(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{\|},z)\approx\frac{Z_{\rm eff}(T,E^{*})\,e^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\rm s}(q_{\|}+q_{s})}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz^{\prime}\,\texttt{e}^{-q_{\|}|z-z^{\prime}|}\,\texttt{e}^{-q_{\|}^{2}\Lambda^{2}_{\|}/4}\,{\cal Q}_{1}(q_{\|},z^{\prime})\ ,

(73)

where

{\cal Q}_{1}(q_{\|},z)=2\left(\frac{1}{a_{\rm d}}\right)^{3}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dr_{\|}\,r_{\|}J_{0}(q_{\|}r_{\|})\,\texttt{e}^{-(2/a_{\rm d})\,\sqrt{r_{\|}^{2}+z^{2}}}\ ,

(74)

J_{0}(x)
is the first-kind Bessel function of order zero, and its contribution becomes negligible
for either
r_{\|}/a_{\rm d}\gg 1
or
|z|/a_{\rm d}\gg 1
.

Physically, we still need to determine the value of
E^{*}
for thermal-equilibrium distribution of trapped electrons inside a defect.
Considering the increased Coulomb repulsion by filling more and more electrons into a defect,
we first introduce the following
quantum-mechanical energy-balance equation
, i.e.,

\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\rm s}}\,\sum_{s=1}^{n^{*}}\,\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}\,\frac{2\ell+1}{|\bar{\cal R}_{n^{*}+1}-\bar{r}_{s,\ell}|}-\left[\frac{\hbar\omega_{0}}{2}+\frac{E_{\rm d}}{(n^{*}+1)^{2}}\right]=0\ ,

(75)

where
n^{*}(E_{\rm d},a_{\rm d})\in\max\{1,2,3,\cdots\}
, which represents the index of the topmost occupied energy level of the defect, is the root of Eq. (
75
),

\displaystyle{\frac{\hbar\omega_{0}}{2}}
the lowest subband edge,
and the quantum-mechanically averaged orbital radii of trapped electrons are

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{s,\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\,\left|R_{s,\ell}(r)\right|^{2}r^{3}dr=s^{2}a_{\rm d}\,\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{s^{2}}\right)\right]\ ,

(76)

\displaystyle\bar{\cal R}_{n^{*}+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{*}+1}\,\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{n^{*}}\,\bar{r}_{n^{*}+1,\ell}\ .

(77)

For this obtained index
n^{*}(E_{\rm d},a_{\rm d})
, the total number
N^{*}(E_{\rm d},a_{\rm d})
for captured electrons is calculated as

\displaystyle{N^{*}(E_{\rm d},a_{\rm d})=2\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n^{*}}\,s^{2}}
including spin degeneracy.
Next, for a given value
N^{*}(E_{\rm d},a_{\rm d})
resulting from the root of Eq. (
75
), we further put forward the following
particle-number conservation equation

to include the thermal effect at temperature
T
, yielding

2\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{n^{2}}{1+\exp[(E_{n}-E^{*})/k_{\rm B}T]}-N^{*}(E_{\rm d},a_{\rm d})=0\ ,

where the temperature-dependent

is the root of Eq. (
78
) and represents the “chemical potential” for captured electrons in a defect.

Vector-Valued Heckman-Opdam Polynomials: a Steinberg variation
Maarten van Pruijssen

Abstract
. We develop a theory of Jacobi polynomials for parabolic subgroups of finite reflection groups that specializes to the cases studied by Heckman and Opdam in which the whole group and the trivial group are considered. For the intermediate cases we combine results of Steinberg and Heckman and Opdam to obtain new examples of families of vector-valued orthogonal polynomials with properties similar to those of the usual Jacobi polynomials. Most notably we show that these polynomials, when suitably interpreted as vector-valued polynomials, are determined up to scaling as simultaneous eigenfunctions of a commutative algebra of differential operators. We establish an example in which the vector-valued Jacobi polynomials can be identified with spherical functions for a higher K K -type on a compact symmetric pair with restricted root system of Dynkin type A 2 A_{2} . We also describe how to obtain new examples of matrix-valued orthogonal polynomials in several variables.

Introduction

-invariant Laurent polynomials on

Let

be the set of positive roots of

.

.
Let

W/W_{I}

The space

The space

and

Denote by
\mathbf{H}(R_{+},k)
Lusztig’s graded Hecke algebra that acts on
\mathbb{C}[P]

the commutative subalgebra generated by the Cherednik operators

, where
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{a}\otimes\mathbb{C}
. The algebra
S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
of
W_{I}
-invariant operators acts on the space
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}
by differential-reflection operators.
The space
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}
can be identified with the space of invariant vector-valued Laurent polynomials,
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}\cong\left(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]\right)^{W}
. The corresponding action of the elements in
S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{h})
is now by differential operators, the coefficients being matrix-valued Laurent polynomials. Note that these considerations break down if we consider, more generally, a reflection subgroup, because the above observations rely on relations in
\mathbf{H}(R_{+},k)
for simple reflections.
The idea to make
W
-invariant vector-valued polynomials out of
W_{I}
-invariant polynomials was already used by Steinberg
[
9
, L.2.4]
. At the same time,
W
-invariant vector-valued Laurent polynomials also occur naturally when restricted spherical functions are considered, see the first application below.

We point out two applications of this class of Jacobi polynomials . For the first application we recall that the Jacobi polynomials
p_{S}(\lambda,k)
can be identified with the zonal spherical functions of a compact symmetric pair whose restricted root system is of type
R
, once the multiplicity
k
is related to the dimensions of the restricted root spaces. The root system
R
of type
A_{2}
occurs as the restricted root system for the compact symmetric pairs

(\mathrm{SU}(3),\mathrm{SO}(3)),\quad(\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(3),\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{SU}(3)),\quad(\mathrm{SU}(6),\mathrm{Sp}(6)).

The spherical functions related to the defining representation of the symmetric subgroup in each case, can be identified with the Jacobi polynomials
p_{\{s\}}(\lambda,k)
, where
s\in W
is a simple reflection. Our identification is based on the comparison of generators of
S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{h})^{<s>}
, viewed as differential operators acting on the vector-valued Laurent polynomials, with the differential operators found by Shimeno
[
8
]
.

The second application is a link to the theory of vector- and matrix-valued orthogonal polynomials in several variables. Only in this discussion we make the distinction between Laurent polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[P]
and genuine polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W}
(which is a polynomial ring) whereas usually we refer to elements in
\mathbb{C}[P]
as polynomials.

The isomorphism
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}\cong\mathbb{C}[P]^{W}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|}
based on Steinberg’s result gives an interpretation of the Jacobi polynomials as a family of vector-valued orthogonal polynomials. The labeling of the orthogonal polynomials suggests that we can pack them together to obtain matrix-valued orthogonal polynomials. In this way we obtain new examples families of orthogonal vector- and matrix-valued polynomials that are at the same uniquely determined (up to scaling) as simultaneous eigenfunctions of a commutative algebra of differential operators.
In rank one this interpretation is a reasonable cosmetic operation leading to Gauss’ hypergeometric functions, but for higher rank it seems an unsatisfying descriptions of the Jacobi polynomials.

Historically, in the research of matrix-valued orthogonal polynomials, it has been fruitful to try to find analogs of properties of scalar-valued orthogonal polynomials. For example, for a while it has been an open question whether families of matrix-valued orthogonal polynomials exist for which there is a second-order differential operator that is symmetric and that has the polynomials as simultaneous eigenfunctions.

The first examples that answer this question affirmatively came about using spherical functions on compact Lie groups, see
[
4
]
. These examples have have been vastly generalized in
[
7
]
using spherical varieties and their combinatorics.
In this framework there are examples of families of matrix-valued orthogonal polynomials in any number of variables and of sizes that can become arbitrarily large. However, the parameters of the polynomials from this theory are discrete, being dimensions of root spaces. Also, the commutative algebra of differential operators remains rather abstract as a subquotient of the universal enveloping algebra.

The new class of vector- and matrix-valued orthogonal polynomials from the theory in this paper has the advantage that the multiplicity function is more flexible and that the algebra of differential operators is better understood. Many interesting questions about the properties of these new Jacobi polynomials, for example about their various normalizations and closer ties with the representation theory of compact Lie groups, remain to be investigated.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we establish a basis of standard
W_{I}
-invariant polynomials, partly based on Steinberg’s observations. In Section
3
we consider a partial ordering on the labels of the invariant polynomials and we apply the Gram-Schmidt process to make up a new basis, for which it is
a priori
not clear that polynomials with incomparable labels are orthogonal. This issue is resolved in Section
4
using differential-reflection operators. On the way we have to establish a couple of results where the partial ordering is understood relative to the parabolic subgroup
W_{I}
. In Section
5
we make vector-valued Laurent polynomials out of the invariant polynomials and observe that the reflection part of the differential-reflection operators translates into matrix-coefficients of the differential operators. In this section we also calculate a specific example that is not one of the extreme cases. We pick up that example in Section
6
to show that the vector-valued Laurent-polynomials can be identified with the spherical functions on a specific compact Riemann symmetric pair
(U,K)
with specific irreducible
K
-representation. In Section
7
we formulate a recipe to make vector- and matrix-valued orthogonal polynomials that are determined as simultaneous eigenfunctions (up to scaling) of a commutative algebra of differential operators.

.

3.
Jacobi polynomials

Following
[
6

with a Hermitean inner product

given by

(1)

(\phi,\psi)_{k}=\int_{T}\overline{\phi(t)}\psi(t)\delta_{k}(t)dt,\quad\phi,\psi\in\mathbb{C}[P]

\delta_{k}=\prod_{\alpha\in R}(e^{\alpha/2}-e^{-\alpha/2})^{k_{\alpha}}
and

To obtain a basis of orthogonal Laurent polynomials we use the following partial ordering on
P_{I}^{+}
. Recall that the subset

\lambda\preceq\mu\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad\mu-\lambda\in Q^{+}.

Let
\lambda_{+}
be the unique element in

\overline{v}(\lambda)\in W^{\lambda_{+}}

\overline{v}(\lambda)\lambda_{+}=\lambda

Given

\lambda\leq_{\emptyset}\mu\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(1)&\lambda_{+}\preceq\mu_{+},\\
(2)&\mbox{if $\lambda_{+}=\mu_{+}$ then $\overline{v}(\lambda)\leq_{W}\overline{v}(\mu)$ in the Bruhat ordering $\leq_{W}$ on $W$.}\end{array}\right.

v(\lambda)\alpha>0
. It follows that
\ell(v(\lambda)s_{\alpha})>\ell(v(\lambda))
. The Lifting Property
[
1
, Prop.2.2.7]
implies
v(\lambda)\leq_{W}v(\mu)s_{\alpha}=v(s_{\alpha}\mu)
and hence
s_{\alpha}\mu\leq_{\emptyset}\lambda
. If
s_{\alpha}\mu=\lambda
, then also
\mu=\lambda
, which violates
\mu<_{\emptyset}\lambda
. We conclude that
s_{\alpha}\mu<_{\emptyset}\lambda
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

If
w\in W_{I,\lambda}
and
\lambda\in P^{+}_{I}
, then
wE(\lambda,k)=E(\lambda,k)
.

Proof.

Write
E(\lambda,k)=e^{\lambda}+\sum_{\mu<_{\emptyset}\lambda}c_{\mu}e^{\mu}
and use Lemma
3.4
to see that
wE(\lambda,k)=e^{\lambda}+\sum_{\mu<_{\emptyset}\lambda}d_{\mu}e^{\mu}
has the same type of expansion as
E(\lambda,k)
.
If
\nu<_{\emptyset}\lambda
, then

(wE(\lambda,k),e^{\nu})_{k}=(E(\lambda,k),e^{w^{-1}\nu})_{k}=0,

because
w^{-1}\nu<_{\emptyset}\lambda
(Lemma
3.4
) and the defining property of
E(\lambda,k)
. We conclude that
wE(\lambda,k)=E(\lambda,k)
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\lambda\in P^{+}_{I}
and
\mu\in P
with
\mu_{+}=\lambda_{+}
. If
\mu<_{\emptyset}\lambda
, then
\mu_{I,+}<_{\emptyset}\lambda
.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.3
we have
\overline{v}(\mu)^{-1}=\overline{v}(\mu_{I,+})^{-1}\overline{v}_{I}(\mu)^{-1}\in W^{I}W_{I}
so
\overline{v}(\mu_{I,+})^{-1}\leq_{W}\overline{v}(\mu)^{-1}
, being a subword of a reduced expression. It follows that
\overline{v}(\mu_{I,+})<_{W}\overline{v}(\lambda)
from which
\mu_{I,+}<_{\emptyset}\lambda
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

We have
p_{I}(\lambda,k)=\sum_{w\in(W_{I})^{\lambda}}wE(\lambda,k)
for
\lambda\in P^{+}_{I}
.

Proof.

The terms that occur in the sum
\sum_{w\in(W_{I})^{\lambda}}wE(\lambda,k)
are multiples of
e^{w\mu}
where
\mu<_{\emptyset}\lambda
and
w\in W_{I}
. By Lemma
3.6
, the weights
w\mu\in P_{I}^{+}
with this properties satisfy
w\mu<_{\emptyset}\lambda
. It follows that
\sum_{w\in(W_{I})^{\lambda}}wE(\lambda,k)=m_{I}(\lambda)+\sum_{\mu<_{I}\lambda}c_{\lambda,\mu}m_{I}(\mu)
has the same type of expansion as
p_{I}(\lambda)
.

Let
\nu<_{I}\lambda
and
w\in W_{I}
. If
\nu_{+}\prec\lambda_{+}
then
w\nu<_{\emptyset}\lambda
for all
w\in W
and
(E(\lambda,k),e^{w\nu})_{k}=0
. If
\lambda_{+}=\nu_{+}
then either
w\nu
and
\lambda
are not comparable, in which case
(E(\lambda,k),e^{w\nu})_{k}=0
, or
w\nu
and
\lambda
are comparable. In the latter case, suppose
\overline{v}(\lambda)\leq_{W}\overline{v}(w\nu)
or equivalently
\overline{v}(\lambda)^{-1}\leq_{W}\overline{v}(w\nu)^{-1}
.
By Lemma
3.3
we have
\overline{v}(w\nu)^{-1}=\overline{v}(\nu)^{-1}\overline{v}_{I}(w\nu)^{-1}
and since the map
W=W^{I}W_{I}\to W^{I}:w^{\prime\prime}w^{\prime}\mapsto w^{\prime\prime}
is order-preserving (cf.
[
1
, Prop.2.5.1]
) we have
\overline{v}(\lambda)^{-1}\leq_{W}\overline{v}(\nu)^{-1}
, contradicting
\nu<_{\emptyset}\lambda
.
Hence
\overline{v}(\lambda)>_{W}\overline{v}(w\nu)
and in turn
\lambda>_{\emptyset}w\nu
. We conclude that also in this case we have
(E(\lambda,k),e^{w\nu})_{k}=0
, whence
(E(\lambda,k),m_{I}(\nu))_{k}=0
and in turn
(\sum_{w\in(W_{I})^{\lambda}}wE(\lambda,k),m_{I}(\nu))_{k}=0
.
∎

4.
Orthogonality

In the extreme cases
I=\emptyset
and
I=S
it is known that the Jacobi polynomials are pairwise orthogonal, see
[
6
, Cor.2.11]
and
[
3
, Cor.1.3.13]
respectively. For the intermediate cases we invoke the same argument. Let
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{a}\otimes\mathbb{C}
.

The graded Hecke algebra
\mathbf{H}(R^{+},k)
is isomorphic to
S(\mathfrak{h})\otimes\mathbb{C}[W]
as a vector space and it acts on
\mathbb{C}[P]
as described in
[
6
, Cor. 2.9]
. Most importantly,
\xi\in S(\mathfrak{h})
acts by the Cherednik operator (Def. 2.1 of loc.cit.)

D_{\xi}(k)=\partial_{\xi}+\sum_{\alpha>0}k_{\alpha}\alpha(\xi)\frac{1}{1-e^{-\alpha}}(1-s_{\alpha})-\rho(k)(\xi)

which is a differential-reflection operator that is symmetric with respect to the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{k}
on
\mathbb{C}[P]
. The Cherednik operator
D_{\xi}(k)
has
E(\lambda,k)
as an eigenfunction,

D_{\xi}(k)E(\lambda,k)=\widetilde{\lambda}(\xi)E(\lambda,k),

where
\widetilde{\lambda}=\lambda-v(\lambda)^{-1}\rho(k)
.
Note that
\widetilde{\lambda}=\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}k_{\alpha}\epsilon(\lambda(\alpha^{\vee}))\alpha
where
\epsilon(x)=x/|x|
if
x\neq 0
and
\epsilon(0)=-1
.
The Cherednik operators
D_{\xi}(k),\xi\in\mathfrak{h}
commute so we can evaluate a polynomial
q\in S(\mathfrak{h})
in the Cherednik operators to obtain
q(D(k))\in\mathbf{H}(k)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
q\in S(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
then
q(D(k))
commutes with the action of
W_{I}
on
\mathbb{C}[P]
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from
[
6
, Prop.1.1(2)]
.
∎

The differential-reflection operators
q(D(k))
with
q\in S(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
leave
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}
invariant. The restriction of
q(D(k))
to
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}
is denoted by
D_{I,q}
. Note that each
D_{I,q}
is symmetric with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{k}
.
The Jacobi polynomials
p_{I}(\lambda,k)
are eigenfunctions for the differential-reflection operators
D_{I,q}
with
q\in S(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
,

D_{I,q}(p_{I}(\lambda,k))=\sum_{w\in(W_{I})^{\lambda}}D_{I,q}\left(wE(\lambda,k)\right)=\sum_{w\in(W_{I})^{\lambda}}q(\widetilde{\lambda})wE(\lambda,k)=q(\widetilde{\lambda})p_{I}(\lambda,k).

To show that the polynomials
q\in S(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
separate the points
\widetilde{\lambda}
with
\lambda\in P^{+}_{I}
we invoke the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\lambda\in P^{+}_{I}
,
w^{I,\lambda}_{0}\in W_{I,\lambda}
the longest element in
W_{I}
that fixes
\lambda
and
w^{I}_{0}\in W_{I}
the longest element. Then

\widetilde{\lambda}=w^{I,\lambda}_{0}(\lambda-w_{0}^{I}v(w_{0}^{I}\lambda)^{-1}\rho(k))
.

Remark 4.3
.

Lemma
4.2
applied to
I=\emptyset
gives back the definition of
\widetilde{\lambda}
. Applied to
I=S
gives
\widetilde{\lambda}=w^{\lambda}_{0}(\lambda-w_{0}v(\lambda_{-})\rho(k))=w^{\lambda}_{0}(\lambda+\rho(k))
, because
v(\lambda_{-})=e
. The latter case has been observed by Heckman, cf.
[
6
, Prop.2.10]
.

Proof.

We have to show
v(\lambda)=v(w_{0}^{I}\lambda)w^{I}_{0}w^{I,\lambda}_{0}
.
Along the same lines as the proof of Lemma
3.3
we have
v(w^{I}_{0}\lambda)\in W^{I}
and
v(\lambda)=w^{\prime\prime}w^{\prime}\in W^{I}W_{I}
with
w^{\prime\prime}=v(w_{0}^{I}\lambda)
and
w^{\prime}
the shortest element in
W_{I}
that sends
\lambda\in P^{+}_{I}
to
-P^{+}_{I}
, i.e.
w^{\prime}=w^{I}_{0}w^{I,\lambda}_{0}
.
∎

The description of
P^{+}_{I}
in Proposition
2.2
was based on the Steinberg weights which were used to cover
P^{+}_{I}
with translates of
P^{+}
. Instead, we can also cover
P
and
P_{I}^{-}
with translates of
P^{-}
,

(2)

P=\bigcup_{w\in W}w^{-1}(\mu_{w}-P^{-}),\quad P^{-}_{I}=\bigcup_{w\in W^{I}}w^{-1}(\mu_{w}-P^{-}),

where
\mu_{w}=-\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi,w^{-1}\alpha<0}\varpi_{\alpha}
. The proof is the same as that of Proposition
2.2
.
It follows that for
\lambda\in P^{-}_{I}
we have
\lambda\in v(\lambda)^{-1}(\mu_{v(\lambda)}-P^{-})
and
v(\lambda)\in W^{I}
. Moreover

\widetilde{\lambda}=\lambda-v(\lambda)^{-1}\rho(k)\in v(\lambda)^{-1}(\mu_{v(\lambda)}-P^{-})\subset P^{-}_{I},

from which we conclude that the map
P^{-}_{I}\to P^{-}_{I},\lambda\mapsto\widetilde{\lambda}
is injective.
However, if
\lambda\in P^{+}_{I}
then
\widetilde{\lambda}
need not be in
P^{+}_{I}
and there is
a priori
no reason why
P^{+}_{I}\to P/W_{I},\lambda\to[\widetilde{\lambda}]
would be injective.

Proposition 4.4
.

The polynomials in
S(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
seperate the points
\widetilde{\lambda}
where
\lambda\in P^{+}_{I}
.

Proof.

The map
P^{+}_{I}\to P^{+}_{I},\lambda\mapsto\lambda-w^{I}_{0}v(w^{I}_{0}\lambda)^{-1}\rho(k)
is the
w^{I}_{0}
-conjugation of the injective map
P_{I}^{-}\to P_{I}^{-},\lambda\mapsto\widetilde{\lambda}
and hence injective.
Let
\lambda\in P^{+}_{I}
and
q\in S(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
. We have

q(\widetilde{\lambda})=q(\lambda-w_{0}^{I}v(w_{0}^{I}\lambda)^{-1}\rho(k))

by Lemma
4.2
. Since
S(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
separates the points of
P^{+}_{I}
, it certainly separates the points of a subset.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

The Jacobi polynomials are pairwise orthogonal.

Proof.

The polynomials
p_{I}(\lambda)
are eigenfunctions of the invariant operators
D_{I,q},q\in S(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
with eigenvalues
q(\widetilde{\lambda})
and for each pair
p_{I}(\lambda)\neq p_{I}(\lambda^{\prime})
with
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in P^{+}_{I}
there exists a polynomial
q\in S(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
with
q(\widetilde{\lambda})\neq q(\widetilde{\lambda^{\prime}})
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

In view of the description (
2
) of
P^{-}_{I}
we obtain an alternative description of
P_{I}^{+}
, namely

P_{I}^{+}=\bigcup_{w\in W^{I}}w^{I}_{0}w^{-1}(\mu_{w}-P^{-}).

From this we obtain alternative Steinberg weights and in turn alternative generators for the
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W}
-module
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}
. For example, in type
A_{2}
we see that the generators from Example
2.3
may be replaced by

1,e^{\varpi_{1}-\varpi_{2}}+e^{\varpi_{2}}
and
e^{\varpi_{1}}
.

5.
Vector-valued Jacobi polynomials

The vector space
\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]
is generated by functions
\delta_{wW_{I}}:W/W_{I}\to\mathbb{C}
(the characteristic function of
wW_{I}
on
W/W_{I}
). We order the basis elements
\delta_{uW_{I}}
in such away that
\delta_{uW_{I}}
comes before
\delta_{vW_{I}}
if
\ell(u^{\prime\prime})<\ell(v^{\prime\prime})
, where
u=u^{\prime\prime}u^{\prime},v=v^{\prime\prime}v^{\prime}\in W^{I}W_{I}
.

The space
\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]
of
\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]
-valued Laurent polynomials caries the diagonal
W
-action
w(\phi\otimes\delta_{uW_{I}})=(w\phi)\otimes\delta_{wuW_{I}}
. The space of
W
-invariants for this action,

\left(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]\right)^{W},

is a module over
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W}
by
\chi(\sum\phi_{u}\otimes\delta_{uW_{I}})=\sum(\chi\phi_{u})\otimes\delta_{uW_{I}}
for
\chi\in\mathbb{C}[P]^{W}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The map

\Gamma:\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}\to\left(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]\right)^{W},\Gamma(\phi)=\sum_{u\in W^{I}}u(\phi\otimes\delta_{eW_{I}})

is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W}
-modules.

Proof.

Note that
\Gamma(\phi)
is
W
-invariant because the sum can be taken over
W=W^{I}W_{I}
. The first component of
\Gamma(\phi)
is
\phi
, so
\Gamma
is injective. If
\Phi=\sum\phi_{u}\otimes\delta_{uW_{I}}\in\left(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W^{\prime}]\right)^{W}
, then
\phi_{e}
is
W_{I}
-invariant, hence
\Phi=\Gamma(\phi_{e})
. We conclude that
\Gamma
is a vector space isomorphism and it clearly is also an isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W}
-modules.
∎

The space
\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]
is also endowed with a Hermitean inner product, denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{I,k}
for
k_{\alpha}\geq 0
, defined by

(\Phi,\Psi)_{I,k}=\int_{T}\mathrm{tr}(\overline{\Phi(t)}\Psi(t))|\delta_{k}(t)|dt,\quad\Phi,\Psi\in\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}],

where
\mathrm{tr}(\sum_{v\in W^{I}}\Phi_{v}\otimes\delta_{vW_{I}})=\sum_{v\in W^{I}}\Phi_{v}
is just the sum of the components. Note that
(\cdot,\cdot)_{I,k}
is
W
-invariant.
If
\Phi,\Psi\in\left(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]\right)^{W}
, then
\Phi=\Gamma(\phi)
and
\Psi=\Gamma(\psi)
for some
\phi,\psi\in\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}
and

\mathrm{tr}(\overline{\Phi}\Psi)|\delta_{k}|=\sum_{v\in W^{I}}v\left(\overline{\phi}\psi\right)|\delta_{k}|

is
W
-invariant because
|\delta_{k}|
is
W
-invariant and
\overline{\phi}\psi
is
W_{I}
-invariant. Hence

(\Phi,\Psi)_{I,k}=|W^{I}|(\phi,\psi)_{k}
and we see that
\Gamma
respects the orthogonality.
Let
P_{I}(\lambda,k)=\Gamma(p_{I}(\lambda,k))
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The family
\{P_{I}(\lambda,k)\mid\lambda\in P_{I}^{+}\}
is an orthogonal basis of the vector space
\left(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]\right)^{W}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Corollary
4.5
and Lemma
5.1
.
∎

The action of a differential-reflection operator
D_{I,q}
(
q\in S(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
) on
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}
is transferred to an action on
\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]
by the the push-forward
\gamma=\Gamma_{*}
,

(3)

\gamma(D_{I,q})=\sum_{u\in W^{I}}u\circ D_{I,q}\circ u^{-1}\otimes\delta_{uW_{I}}.

We want to show that the restriction of
\gamma(D_{I,q})
to
(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}])^{W}
is the restriction of a differential operator on
\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]
to the space of
W
-invariants. Note that
\gamma(D_{I,q})
is not well defined since it depends on the choice of coset representatives in
W/W_{I}
. However, the restriction to
(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}])^{W}
is well defined.

Following
[
3
, Ch.1]
, let
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(R)
be the unital algebra generated by
\frac{1}{1-e^{-\alpha}},\alpha\in R_{+}
and

\mathbb{D}_{I,\mathcal{R}}=\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}])\otimes S(\mathfrak{h})

the algebra of differential operators on
\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]
with coefficients in the algebra
\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}])
. Denote by
\pi_{I}:W\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}])
the representation of
W
on
\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]
.
The action of
W
on
\mathbb{D}_{I,\mathcal{R}}
is given by
w(f\otimes A\otimes\partial_{q})=(wf)\otimes(\pi_{I}(w)A)\otimes\partial_{wq}
on simple tensors. Define

\mathbb{D}_{I,\mathcal{R}}^{W}=\{D\in\mathbb{D}_{I,\mathcal{R}}\mid w(D)=D\mbox{ for all $w\in W$}\},

an algebra of
W
-invariant differential operators on the space of
\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]
-valued Laurent polynomials.
Let

\mathbb{D}R_{I,\mathcal{R}}=\mathbb{D}_{I,\mathcal{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}[W]

and define for
D,E\in\mathbb{D}_{I,\mathcal{R}}
and
v,w\in W
the multiplication

(D\otimes w)\cdot(E\otimes v)=(D\circ w(E))\otimes wv,

which gives
\mathbb{D}R_{I,\mathcal{R}}
the structure of an associative algebra.
The action of an element
D\otimes w\in\mathbb{D}R_{I,\mathcal{R}}
on
\Phi\in\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]
is given by
D(w\Phi)
.
For an element
P\in\mathbb{D}R_{I,\mathcal{R}}
of the form
P=\sum_{w\in W}P_{w}\otimes w
we define the linear map
\beta:\mathbb{D}R_{I,\mathcal{R}}\to\mathbb{D}_{I,\mathcal{R}}
by
\beta(P)=\sum_{w\in W}P_{w}
. Finally, define by
\mathbb{D}R_{I,\mathcal{R}}^{1\otimes\mathbb{C}[W]}
the commutator of
1\otimes\mathbb{C}[W]
in
\mathbb{D}R_{I,\mathcal{R}}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

The restriction
\beta:\mathbb{D}R_{I,\mathcal{R}}^{1\otimes\mathbb{C}[W]}\to\mathbb{D}_{I,\mathcal{R}}
is a homomorphism with image in
\mathbb{D}_{I,\mathcal{R}}^{W}
.

Proof.

The statement for
I=S
is shown in
[
3
, Lem.1.2.1,Lem.1.2.2]
and the general case follows
mutatis mutandis
from these considerations.
∎

The homomorphism (
3
) maps
S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}
into
\mathbb{D}R_{I,\mathcal{R}}^{1\otimes\mathbb{C}[W]}
.
We obtain an algebra homomorphism
\beta\circ\gamma:S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}}\to\mathbb{D}_{I,\mathcal{R}}^{W}
. The image of
\beta\circ\gamma
is the algebra of differential operators restricted to
(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}])^{W}
and we denote it by

\mathbb{D}(I,k)=\beta(\gamma(S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{h})^{W_{I}})).

Note that

\beta(\gamma(D_{I,q})))(\Gamma(\phi))=\gamma(D_{I,q}))\Gamma(\phi)=\Gamma(D_{I,q}(\phi))

for
\phi\in\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}
, from which it follows that
\beta\circ\gamma
is injective.

Lemma 5.4
.

The algebra
\mathbb{D}(I,k)
is a commutative algebra of differential operators on
\left(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]\right)^{W}
. The basis elements
P(\lambda,k)
are determined uniquely (up to scaling) as simultaneous eigenfunctions of
\mathbb{D}(I,k)
.

Example 5.5
.

Consider
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the standard basis
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})
and with the standard inner product. Let
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3})
be the basis of
(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{*}
dual to the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The orthocomplement of
e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}
is denoted by
\mathfrak{a}
.
Let
\Sigma=\{\pm(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}),\pm(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{3}),\pm(\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3})\}
be the root system of type
A_{2}
. The roots are denoted by
\alpha_{ij}=\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}
and the reflections by
s_{ij}
. Note that
W=S_{3}
, the permutation group of
\{1,2,3\}
. We fix the parabolic subgroup
W^{\prime}=<s_{23}>
. We identify the
W
-module
\mathbb{C}[W/W^{\prime}]
with
\mathbb{C}^{3}
on which
W
acts by permuting the standard basis vectors.

Let
\xi_{1}=\frac{1}{3}(2,-1,-1),\xi_{2}=\frac{1}{3}(-1,2,-1)
and
\xi_{3}=\frac{1}{3}(-1,-1,2)
be elements of
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{a}\otimes\mathbb{C}
. Then

S(\mathfrak{h})^{W^{\prime}}=\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+\xi_{1}\xi_{3}+\xi_{2}\xi_{3}]

with
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+\xi_{1}\xi_{3}+\xi_{2}\xi_{3}
algebraically independent. Our aim is to calculate the differential operators acting on the vector-valued Laurent polynomials that correspond to these two generators. In view of the relation with the spherical functions in Section
6
it is convenient to consider the root system
R=2\Sigma
with
R_{+}=\{2\alpha_{12},2\alpha_{13},2\alpha_{23}\}
the set of positive roots and
\Pi=\{2\alpha_{12},2\alpha_{23}\}
the set of simple roots. Accordingly
\Sigma_{+}=\frac{1}{2}R_{+}
.

We proceed to calculate
\beta(\gamma(\xi_{1}))
and
\beta(\gamma(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+\xi_{1}\xi_{3}+\xi_{2}\xi_{3}))
.
Let
p_{1}\in\mathbb{C}[2P]^{W^{\prime}}
and
p_{2}=s_{12}p_{1},p_{3}=s_{13}p_{1}
. Then
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\in\left(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}^{3}\right)^{W}
and

D_{\xi_{1}}(k)(p_{1})=\partial_{\xi_{1}}p_{1}+2k\left(\frac{1}{1-e^{-2\alpha_{12}}}(p_{1}-p_{2})+\frac{1}{1-e^{-2\alpha_{13}}}(p_{1}-p_{3})\right)-2kp_{1}.

It is now easy to obtain expressions for
s_{12}D_{\xi_{1}}(k)(p_{1}),s_{13}D_{\xi_{1}}(k)(p_{1})
and we get

\beta(\gamma(\xi_{1}))=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\xi_{1}}&0&0\\
0&\partial_{\xi_{2}}&0\\
0&0&\partial_{\xi_{3}}\end{pmatrix}+\\
k\begin{pmatrix}\frac{e^{\alpha_{12}}+e^{-\alpha_{12}}}{e^{\alpha_{12}}-e^{-\alpha_{12}}}+\frac{e^{\alpha_{13}}+e^{-\alpha_{13}}}{e^{\alpha_{13}}-e^{-\alpha_{13}}}&-\frac{2}{1-e^{-2\alpha_{12}}}&-\frac{2}{1-e^{-2\alpha_{13}}}\\
-\frac{2}{1-e^{2\alpha_{12}}}&-\frac{e^{\alpha_{12}}+e^{-\alpha_{12}}}{e^{\alpha_{12}}-e^{-\alpha_{12}}}+\frac{e^{\alpha_{23}}+e^{-\alpha_{23}}}{e^{\alpha_{23}}-e^{-\alpha_{23}}}&-\frac{2}{1-e^{-2\alpha_{23}}}\\
-\frac{2}{1-e^{2\alpha_{13}}}&-\frac{2}{1-e^{2\alpha_{23}}}&-\frac{e^{\alpha_{13}}+e^{-\alpha_{13}}}{e^{\alpha_{13}}-e^{-\alpha_{13}}}-\frac{1e^{\alpha_{23}}+e^{-\alpha_{23}}}{e^{\alpha_{23}}-e^{-\alpha_{23}}}\end{pmatrix}

where we have used the identity
\frac{2}{1-e^{-2\alpha}}-1=\frac{e^{\alpha}+e^{-\alpha}}{e^{\alpha}-e^{-\alpha}}
. To calculate

-\frac{e^{\alpha_{1}}+e^{-\alpha_{1}}}{(e^{\alpha_{1}}-e^{-\alpha_{1}})^{2}}&\frac{2}{(e^{\alpha_{1}}-e^{-\alpha_{1}})^{2}}+\frac{2}{(e^{\alpha_{3}}-e^{-\alpha_{3}})^{2}}&-\frac{e^{\alpha_{2}}-e^{-\alpha_{2}}}{(e^{\alpha_{2}}-e^{-\alpha_{2}})^{2}}\\
-\frac{e^{\alpha_{3}}+e^{-\alpha_{3}}}{(e^{\alpha_{3}}-e^{-\alpha_{3}})^{2}}&-\frac{e^{\alpha_{2}}+e^{-\alpha_{2}}}{(e^{\alpha_{2}}-e^{-\alpha_{2}})^{2}}&\frac{2}{(e^{\alpha_{3}}-e^{-\alpha_{3}})^{2}}+\frac{2}{(e^{\alpha_{2}}-e^{-\alpha_{2}})^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

Proposition 6.5
.

We have

T\circ\beta(\gamma(\xi_{1}))\circ T^{-1}=\mathcal{D}_{1},

T\circ\beta(\gamma(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+\xi_{2}\xi_{3}+\xi_{1}\xi_{3}))\circ T^{-1}=\mathcal{D}_{2}-\mathcal{D}_{1}-4k^{2}-\frac{1}{3}.

Proof.

The differential operators
\beta(\gamma(\xi_{1}))
and
\beta(\gamma(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+\xi_{2}\xi_{3}+\xi_{1}\xi_{3}))
have been calculated in Example
5.5
. The conjugation with
T
is a tedious calculation that is based, among other things, on the identities

(9)

e^{\omega_{1}}\circ(\partial_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{2}^{\prime}+\partial_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{3}^{\prime}+\partial_{2}^{\prime}\partial_{3}^{\prime})\circ e^{-\omega_{1}}=\partial_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{2}^{\prime}+\partial_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{3}^{\prime}+\partial_{2}^{\prime}\partial_{3}^{\prime}+\frac{2}{3}\partial_{1}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{3}\partial_{2}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{3}\partial_{3}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{3}\\
=\partial_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{2}^{\prime}+\partial_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{3}^{\prime}+\partial_{2}^{\prime}\partial_{3}^{\prime}+\partial_{1}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{3},

and

-4\frac{e^{-\alpha}}{(1-e^{-2\alpha})^{2}}=-2\frac{e^{\alpha}+e^{-\alpha}}{(e^{\alpha}-e^{-\alpha})^{2}}-\frac{2}{e^{\alpha}-e^{-\alpha}}

and is left for the reader.
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

The spherical functions of type
\mu_{1}
for the compact symmetric pairs

in this section can be identified with the Jacobi polynomials from Example

, up to multiplication with the function

Proof.

The spaces spanned by the orthogonal families of functions can be identifies and so can the commutative algebras that determine the individual members of the familes.

7.
Application II

To describe the
W
-invariant polynomials as genuine polynomials we follow
[
11
]
.
The
W
-invariant Laurent polynomials on
A
constitute the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W}=\mathbb{C}[\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{n}]
, where
n=\mathrm{rank}R
. The image of the map
\chi:T\to\mathbb{C}^{n}:t\mapsto(\chi_{1}(t),\ldots,\chi_{n}(t))
is denoted by
\Omega=\chi(A)
and it is compact and contained in a totally real subspace
\mathbb{R}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For
p,q\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
have

\int_{A}\overline{p(\chi(t))}q(\chi(t))\delta_{k}(t)dt=\int_{\Omega}\overline{p(x)}q(x)w_{k}(x)dx

for some specific weight function
w_{k}
for which
w_{k}(\chi)
is the product of
\delta_{k}
and the absolute value of the Jacobian of
\chi
. The zero set of the latter is in general contained in the zero set of the former.

We proceed to describe the space
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}
also as a space of genuine polynomials. Recall that we have fixed a total ordering on
W^{I}
in which
u
comes before
v
if
\ell(u)\leq\ell(v)
. We use this to identify
\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]=\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|}
.

The isomorphism
\mathbb{C}[P]^{W_{I}}\to\mathbb{C}[P]^{W}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|}
defined by
\sum_{v\in W^{I}}f_{v}\phi_{v}\mapsto(f_{v},v\in W^{I})
induces an isomorphism
\left(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]\right)^{W}\to\mathbb{C}[P]^{W}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|}
given by

\sum_{v\in W^{I}}f_{v}\Gamma(\phi_{v})\mapsto(f_{v},v\in W^{I}).

Let
\Phi_{I}
be
|W^{I}|\times|W^{I}|
matrix whose columns are
\Gamma(\phi_{v}),v\in W^{I}
.
Using the identification
\mathbb{C}[W/W_{I}]=\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|}
, we see that the corresponding map

(\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|})^{W}\to\mathbb{C}[P]^{W}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|}

is given by point wise multiplication with the inverse of
\Phi_{I}
. Note that

\det\Phi_{I}=\prod_{\alpha>0}(e^{\alpha/2}-e^{-\alpha/2})^{n_{\alpha}}

where
n_{\alpha}
is the number of pairs in
W/W_{I}
that is interchanged by
s_{\alpha}
upon left multiplication, see
[
9
, Lemma 2.9]
. Let
\mathbb{M}_{I}=\mathrm{Mat}(\mathbb{C},|W^{I}|\times|W^{I}|)
on which we have the usual Hermitean adjoint which we denote by
C\mapsto C^{*}
.

Let
\mathcal{W}_{I}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\otimes\mathbb{M}_{I}
be defined by
\mathcal{W}_{I}(\chi)=\Phi_{0}^{*}\Phi_{0}
.
Then
\mathcal{W}_{I}w_{k}
is a matrix weight, i.e. for
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|}
the pairing

(10)

\langle Q_{1},Q_{2}\rangle_{I,k}=\int_{\Omega}Q_{1}(x)^{*}\mathcal{W}_{I}(x)Q_{2}(x)w_{k}(x)dx

defines an inner product. By construction the map

\left((\mathbb{C}[P]\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|})^{W},(\cdot,\cdot)_{I,k}\right)\to\left(\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{I,k}\right),

given by multiplication with
\Phi^{-1}_{I}
, is a unitary isomorphism.

Using the identifications from above we define
\mathcal{P}_{I}(\lambda,k)\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|}
by

\mathcal{P}_{I}(\lambda,k)(\chi)=\Phi_{I}^{-1}P_{I}(\lambda,k).

The family
(\mathcal{P}_{I}(\lambda,k),\lambda\in P^{+}_{I})
is an orthogonal basis of
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|}
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{I,k}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{D}(I,k)=\{\Phi_{I}^{-1}\circ D\circ\Phi_{I}\mid D\in\mathbb{D}(I,k)\}
. Then
\mathcal{D}(I,k)
is an algebra of differential operators acting on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|}
. The
\mathcal{P}_{I}(\lambda,k),\lambda\in P^{+}_{I}
are simultaneous eigenfunctions and are in fact separated as such, by construction.
Since
\mathcal{D}(I,k)
acts on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|W^{I}|}
, we see that its coefficients are
\mathbb{M}_{I}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
-valued.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{I}(\sigma)\in\mathbb{M}_{I}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the
\mathbb{M}_{I}
-valued polynomial whose columns are
\mathcal{P}_{I}(v^{-1}(\lambda_{v}+\sigma),k),v\in W^{I}
. Then
(\mathcal{M}_{I}(\sigma,k),\sigma\in P^{+})
is a family of matrix-valued orthogonal polynomials with respect to (
10
), now interpreted as an
\mathbb{M}_{I}
-valued inner product. These polynomials are uniquely determined up to scaling by
\mathbb{M}
from the right, as simultaneous eigenfunctions of the commutative algebra
\mathcal{D}(I,k)
, the characters now taking values in subalgebra of the diagonal matrices in
\mathbb{M}_{I}
which act on the polynomials on the right.

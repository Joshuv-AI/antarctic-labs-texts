Top cell attachment for a Poincaré Duality complex
Stephen Theriault

Abstract
. Let M M be a simply-connected closed Poincaré Duality complex of dimension n n . Then M M is obtained by attaching a cell of highest dimension to its ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -skeleton M ¯ \overline{M} . Conditions are given for when the skeletal inclusion M ¯ ⟶ i M \overline{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}M has the property that Ω ​ i \Omega i has a right homotopy inverse. This is an integral version of the rational statement that such a right homotopy inverse always exists provided the rational cohomology of M M is not generated by a single element. New methods are developed in order to do the integral case. These lead to p p -local versions and recover the full rational statement. Families for which the integral statement holds include moment-angle manifolds and quasi-toric manifolds.

1.
Introduction

An important problem in homotopy theory is to determine the effect on homotopy groups
of a cell attachment. Let
X
be a simply-connected
CW
-complex and suppose that
there is a homotopy cofibration

S^{n-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y

where
f
attaches a cell to
X
to obtain
Y
. It is natural to ask how
\pi_{\ast}(X)
and
\pi_{\ast}(Y)

are related.

In rational homotopy theory the attaching map
f
is called
rationally inert
if
\pi_{\ast}(i)
is
a surjection. This is equivalent to saying that, rationally, the loop map

\Omega X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega i}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega Y

has a right homotopy inverse. The equivalence between the rational homology of a loop space
and the universal enveloping algebra of the rational homotopy Lie algebra of the space also allows
for a description of rational inertness in Lie theoretic terms. Rationally inert maps have been studied
in depth
[
FT
,
HL1
,
HL2
]
, along with their variants
[
B
,
HeL
]
, and play an important role in the
growth of rational homotopy groups
[
FHT
]
and a solution to a
higher dimensional version of Whitehead’s asphericity problem
[
A
]
.

The notion of inertness was generalized in
[
T
]
to an integral context. The attaching map
f

is called
inert
if the loop map

\Omega X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega i}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega Y

has a right homotopy inverse. This implies that the homotopy groups of
Y
retract off the homotopy
groups of
X
. This is interesting because it says that the cell attachment has the special property
of killing off homotopy groups
but not creating any new ones. It was shown that the attaching map for the top dimensional cell
of several families of
(n-1)
-connected
2n
-dimensional and
(n-1)
-connected
(2n+1)
-dimensional
Poincaré duality complexes is inert. This integral version of inertness has been developed further
in recent work
[
Ch1
,
Ch2
,
H
,
HT
]
.

Halperin and Lemaire
[
HL1
]
proved the dramatic statement that the attaching map for the top
cell of any Poincaré Duality complex
M
is rationally inert provided the rational cohomology of
M
is
not generated by a single element. In this paper we give conditions for when an analogous integral
statement is true. It should be emphasized that in the rational case the argument depends on
using the Sullivan algebra as a model for rational homotopy theory. No such model exists integrally,
so new methods had to be developed. These build on work in
[
BT2
,
T
]
that develops
a framework for constructing loop space decompositions in the context of homotopy fibrations
with a section after looping. The key is to enhance this setup with a homotopy co-action that
allows for a splitting, thereby identifying certain fibres. This should have further applications.

To state our results, let
M
be a simply-connected, closed

-dimensional Poincaré
Duality complex. Let
\overline{M}
be the

-skeleton of

where

attaches the top cell to

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be an
(m-1)
-connected, closed Poincaré Duality complex of dimension

Note that the hypothesis that

implies that

is not a sphere. The condition that there is a map

Duality complexes with this property are given. One family of examples is moment-angle
manifolds, which arise in toric topology. If

\ell

A

.

Theorem 1.2

The rational case

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
M
be a simply-connected closed Poincaré Duality complex of dimension
n
,
where

. If the rational cohomology of
M
is not generated by a single element then
the attaching map for the top cell of
M
is inert.

Proof.

Suppose that
M
is
(m-1)
-connected. As
H^{\ast}(M;\mathbb{Q})
is not generated by a single
element,
M
is not a sphere, so Poincaré Duality implies that
2m\leq n
. Let
x\in H^{m}(M;\mathbb{Q})

be a generator. Then
x
is represented by a map

r\colon M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}K(\mathbb{Q},m)
.
Let
\bar{x}\in H^{m}(M;\mathbb{Q})
be the dual of
x
. The Hurewicz isomorphism implies that
there is a map

s\colon S^{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M

with Hurewicz image
\bar{x}
. The duality between
\bar{x}
and

implies that the composite

S^{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\longrightarrow}}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\longrightarrow}}K(\mathbb{Q},m)

is homotopic to the inclusion of the bottom cell.

Case 1:
m
is odd
. Then
K(\mathbb{Q},m)\simeq S^{m}
, so we may regard
r
as a map

M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{m}
.
As
r\circ s
has degree one,
r\circ s
is homotopic to the identity map. Thus
r
has a right
homotopy inverse. The rational version of either Theorem
5.2
or Theorem
6.3

then implies that the attaching map for the top cell of
M
is inert.

Case 2:
m
is even
. Then we may write

as
BS^{2m-1}
and
regard

as a map

M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\longrightarrow}}BS^{2m-1}
.
After looping the composite

S^{2m-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle E}}{{\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\rightarrow}}\Omega S^{2m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega(r\circ s)}}{{\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\rightarrow}}S^{2m-1}

is a homotopy equivalence, where

is the suspension. Thus
\Omega r
has a right
homotopy inverse. If
M_{1}
is the homotopy fibre of
r
, then by
[
Go
,
Q
]

M_{1}

is a closed Poincaré Duality complex, so Theorem
9.1

implies that if the attaching map for the top cell of

is inert then the attaching
map for the top cell of
M
is inert. Therefore we are reduced to considering

. The

, giving a sequence of Poincaré Duality complexes

M_{k}\longrightarrow M_{k-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{0}=M
,
where, for

is the homotopy fibre of a map

M_{i-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{i-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}BS^{2m_{i-1}-1}

with

having a right homotopy inverse, and the attaching map for the top cell

-connected for

is
a sphere. For if

is
(m^{\prime}-1)
-connected for

satisfies Case 1, in

is a sphere.

To go further, return to

and consider rational cohomology. The map

represents
a class

. Fibering

, there is an associated principal fibration

S^{2m-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M

10.
Two applications

We consider two applications of the inertness property. The first is to identify the homotopy fibre
of the inclusion

\overline{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}M
,
and the second is to generate more examples via connected sums and determine the
homotopy type of their loop spaces.

10.1.
The homotopy fibre of the inclusion
\overline{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}M

The inertness property of the attaching map
f
of the top cell for
M
says that the map

\Omega\overline{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega i}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega M

has a right homotopy inverse. The complementary factor can be identified; more
strongly, the homotopy fibre of
i
can be identified.

Given maps

f\colon\Sigma A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}X

and

g\colon\Sigma B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}X

their
Whitehead product
is denoted by

[f,g]\colon\Sigma A\wedge B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}X.

Let

ev\colon\Sigma\Omega X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}X

be the canonical evaluation map. The following was proved in
[
BT2
]
.

Lemma 10.1
.

Suppose there is a homotopy cofibration

\Sigma A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y

and
\Omega i
has a right homotopy inverse

s\colon\Omega Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega X
.
Then there is a homotopy fibration

\Sigma A\vee(\Sigma A\wedge\Omega Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f\perp[f,\gamma]}}{{\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\rightarrow}}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\rightarrow}}Y

where
\gamma
is the composite

\Sigma\Omega Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Sigma s}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Sigma\Omega X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle ev}}{{\longrightarrow}}X
.
Further, this fibration splits after looping to give a homotopy equivalence

\Omega X\simeq\Omega Y\times\Omega(\Sigma A\vee(\Sigma A\wedge\Omega Y)).

\hfill\Box

In our case, for the Poincaré Duality complexes in Theorem
5.2
, there is
a homotopy cofibration

S^{n-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M

that attaches the top cell and
\Omega i
has a right homotopy inverse. Lemma
10.1

then immediately implies the following.

Proposition 10.2
.

Let
M
be a simply-connnected closed Poincaré Duality complex satisfying
the hypotheses of Theorem
5.2
. Then there is a homotopy fibration

S^{n-1}\vee(S^{n-1}\wedge\Omega M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f\perp[f,\gamma]}}{{\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\rightarrow}}\overline{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\relbar\!\!\rightarrow}}M

that splits after looping to give a homotopy equivalence

\Omega\overline{M}\simeq\Omega M\times\Omega(S^{n-1}\vee\Sigma^{n-1}\Omega M)
.

If instead
M
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
6.3
then this homotopy
fibration and homotopy equivalence for
\Omega\overline{M}
hold after localization
away from
\mathcal{P}
.
\hfill\Box

For example, rationally, any suspension is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres.
Thus the homotopy fibre of
i
in Proposition
10.2
is rationally a wedge of spheres.
More generally, if
\Omega M
was known to be homotopy equivalent to a product of
spheres and loops on spheres, either in the integral or local case, then the homotopy fibre
of
i
in Proposition
10.2
would correspondingly be homotopy equivalent to a wedge
of spheres. An explicit example is the following.

Example 10.3
.

An elaboration on certain quasi-toric manifolds
.
By Example
8.7
, the attaching map for the top cell of a quasi-toric manifold
M^{2n}

is inert. As noted in that example, there is a homotopy fibration

\mathcal{Z}_{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{2n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\longrightarrow}}BT^{m-n}

where
\Omega h
has a right homotopy inverse and
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is a moment-angle manifold.
Suppose that the associated polytope

is a product of simplices,

P=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\Delta^{m_{\ell}}
. Then, by
combining
[
BP
, Example 2.2.9 (4), Proposition 4.1.3 and Example 4.1.2 (4)]
, there are homeomorphisms

\mathcal{Z}_{K}\cong\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{Z}_{\partial\Delta^{m_{j}}}\cong\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}S^{2m_{j}+1}.

Using the right homotopy inverse for

we therefore obtain homotopy equivalences

\Omega M^{2n}\simeq\big(\prod_{i=1}^{m-n}S^{1}\big)\times\Omega\mathcal{Z}_{K}\simeq\big(\prod_{i=1}^{m-n}S^{1}\big)\times\big(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\Omega S^{2m_{j}+1}\big).

Consequently,
\Sigma^{2n-1}\Omega M^{2n}
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge

Therefore, by Proposition
10.2
there is a homotopy fibration

S^{2n-1}\vee W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{M^{2n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{2n}

. Note that
all this happens without any localization.

10.2.
Connected sums

Theorems
5.2
,
6.3
and
7.5
give many examples
of Poincaré Duality complexes with the property that the attaching map for the top cell is inert.
These can be used to generate more examples due to the following result
[
T
, Theorem 9.1]
. Note
that if
M
and
N
are simply-connected closed
n
-dimensional Poincaré
Duality complexes with
(n-1)
-skeletons
\overline{M}
and
\overline{N}
respectively,
then the
(n-1)
-skeleton of

is
\overline{M}\vee\overline{N}
.

Theorem 10.4
.

Let
M
and
N
be simply-connected closed
n
-dimensional Poincaré Duality complexes,
where
n\geq 2
. Let
\overline{M}
and
\overline{N}
be the
(n-1)
-skeletons of
M

and
N
respectively. If the attaching map

for the top cell of
M
is inert then the following hold:

(a)

there is a homotopy equivalence

\Omega(M\#N)\simeq\Omega M\times\Omega(\Omega M\ltimes\overline{N})
;

(b)

the attaching map for the top cell of
M\#N
is inert: the loop map

\Omega(\overline{M}\vee\overline{N})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega(M\#N)

has a right homotopy inverse.

Theorem
10.4
(b) says that if the attaching map for the top cell of
M
is inert
then so is the attaching map for the top cell of
M\#N
for any
N
, regardless of the behaviour
of the attaching map for the top cell of
N
. This lets us generate new examples freely. For instance,
if
M^{2n}
is a quasi-toric manifold of dimension
2n
then, by Example
8.7
, the attaching
map for its top cell is inert. So if
N
is any

Duality complex then the attaching map for the top cell of

is inert.

Further, Theorem
10.4
(a) gives a homotopy decomposition for
\Omega(M\#N)

in terms of
\Omega M
and
\overline{N}
. For instance, continuing Example
10.3
,
for any quasi-toric manifold

associated to a simply polytope that is a product of simplices and
for any

as in the previous paragraph, there is a homotopy equivalence

\Omega(M^{2n}\#N)\simeq\Omega M^{2n}\times\Omega(\Omega M^{2n}\ltimes\overline{N})
,
which may be refined by substituting in the homotopy equivalence

\Omega M^{2n}\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{m-n}S^{1}\times\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\Omega S^{2m_{j}+1}

from Example
10.3
.

Theorem
10.4
also works equally well in a local setting, in which case the
connected sum is given a topological interpretation as the sum of the attaching maps for
the top cells.

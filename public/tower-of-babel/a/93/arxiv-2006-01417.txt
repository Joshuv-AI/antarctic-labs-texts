New Separation of Variables for the Classical XXX and XXZ Heisenberg Spin Chains
Guido MAGNANO † and Taras SKRYPNYK † ‡ {}^{\dagger}{}^{\ddagger}

Abstract
We propose a non-standard separation of variables for the classical integrable XXX and XXZ spin chains with degenerate twist matrix. We show that for the case of such twist matrices one can interchange the role of classical separating functions A ⁡ ( u ) A(u) and B ⁡ ( u ) B(u) and construct a new full set of separated variables, satisfying simpler equation of separation and simpler Abel equations in comparison with the standard separated variables of Sklyanin. We show that for certain cases of the twist matrices the constructed separated variables can be directly identified with action-angle coordinates.

Introduction

Completely integrable Hamiltonian systems admitting Lax representation
[
12
]
have been an object of constant interest in physics and mathematics during the last forty years.

An important problem in the theory of integrable systems still to be solved in general is the problem of variable separation. The separated variables
x_{i}
,
p_{j}
,
i,j=1,\dots,N
are a set of (quasi)canonical coordinates such that the following system of equations is
satisfied
[
24
]

\displaystyle\Phi_{i}(x_{i},p_{i},I_{1},\dots,I_{N},C_{1},\dots,C_{r})=0,\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

where
\Phi_{i}
are certain functions,

are Poisson-commuting integrals of motion,
C_{i}
are Casimir functions and
N
is half of the dimension of the phase space.

The separated coordinates (whenever they exist) allow one to write Abel-type equations (see Section
2.1
) which, in their turn, provide a possibility to solve explicitly the Hamilton equations of motion upon resolving the corresponding Abel–Jacobi inversion problem.
Separated variables are also important when solving quantum
integrable models
[
24
]
. That is why the construction of variable separation is
a central issue in the theory of both classical and quantum integrable systems.

In order to construct separated variables in many cases one can use a pair of
separating functions

u

are determined as zeroes of

\displaystyle B(x_{i})=0,\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

(1.1)

and (quasi)momenta are obtained as values of
A(u)
in these zeros

\displaystyle p_{i}=A(x_{i}),\qquad i=1,\dots,N.

(1.2)

In the so-called Lax-integrable case (i.e., when the Hamilton equations of motion can be written in Lax form), a prescription to obtain separating functions

for
\mathfrak{gl}(2)

[

2
Separation of variables

2.1
Definitions and notations

Let us recall the definitions of Liouville integrability and
separation of variables in the general theory of Hamiltonian
systems. An integrable Hamiltonian system with
N
degrees of
freedom is determined on a
2N
-dimensional symplectic manifold

\mathcal{M}
(symplectic leaf in
(\mathcal{P},\{\ ,\ \})
and
N
independent functions (first integrals)
I_{j}
commuting with respect to the Poisson bracket

\displaystyle\{I_{i},I_{j}\}=0,\qquad i,j=1,\dots,N

(for the Hamiltonian
H
of the system may be taken any first integral
I_{j}
).

To find separated variables means to find
(at least locally) a set of coordinates
x_{i}
,
p_{j}
,
i,j=1,\dots,N
such that there exist
N
relations

\displaystyle\Phi_{i}(x_{i},p_{i},I_{1},\dots,I_{N},C_{1},\dots,C_{r})=0,\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

where
C_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,r
are Casimir functions and the coordinates
x_{i}
,
p_{j}
,
i,j=1,\dots,N
are
canonical

\displaystyle\{x_{i},p_{j}\}=\delta_{ij},\qquad\{x_{i},x_{j}\}=0,\qquad\{p_{i},p_{j}\}=0,\qquad\forall\,i,j=1,\dots,N.

In the present paper it will be convenient for us to work with quasi-canonical coordinates satisfying the following Poisson brackets

\displaystyle\{x_{i},p_{j}\}=\delta_{ij}p_{j},\qquad\{x_{i},x_{j}\}=0,\qquad\{p_{i},p_{j}\}=0,\qquad\forall\,i,j=1,\dots,N.

Clearly the variables
x_{i}
,
\phi_{j}=\log p_{j}
will be canonical then.

It is possible to show that the coordinates of separation
x_{i}
satisfy the Abel-type equations

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}(x_{i},p_{i},I_{1},\dots,I_{N},C_{1},\dots,C_{r})}{\partial I_{k}}}{\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}(x_{i},p_{i};I_{1},\dots,I_{N},C_{1},\dots,C_{r})}{\partial p_{i}}}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\frac{{\rm d}x_{i}}{{\rm d}t_{j}}=-\delta_{kj},\qquad\forall\,k,j=1,\dots,N,

(2.1)

and similar Abel-type equations are satisfied by the momenta of separation
p_{i}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}(x_{i},p_{i},I_{1},\dots,I_{N},C_{1},\dots,C_{r})}{\partial I_{k}}}{\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}(x_{i},p_{i};I_{1},\dots,I_{N},C_{1},\dots,C_{r})}{\partial x_{i}}}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\frac{{\rm d}p_{i}}{{\rm d}t_{j}}=\delta_{kj},\qquad\forall\,k,j=1,\dots,N.

(2.2)

These equations are the last step before the integration of the classical equations of motion.

\displaystyle\{A(u),B(v)\}=\alpha(u,v)B(u)-\beta(u,v)B(v),

\displaystyle\{A(u),A(v)\}=a(u,v)B(u)-a(v,u)B(v).

\displaystyle r(u,v)=\frac{\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}X_{ij}\otimes X_{ji}}{u-v}

(4.1)

and describe the corresponding Lax matrices

, satisfying the quadratic brackets (

As it is well-known, the Lax matrices of the spin-chain models satisfying the quadratic brackets (

\displaystyle L^{(1,2,\dots,N)}(u)=L^{(\nu_{1})}(u)\cdots L^{(\nu_{N})}(u),

(4.2)

where

are written as follows

\displaystyle L^{(\nu_{k})}(u)=1+\frac{1}{u-\nu_{k}}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}S^{(k)}_{ji}X_{ij}.

Here
\nu_{i}\neq\nu_{j}
, when
i\neq j
,
i,j=1,\dots,N
and the Poisson brackets among the coordinates
S^{(n)}_{ij}
,
S^{(m)}_{kl}
are those of the direct sum
\mathfrak{gl}(2)^{\oplus N}

\displaystyle\big\{{S}_{ij}^{(m)},{S}_{kl}^{(n)}\big\}=\delta^{mn}\big(\delta_{kj}{S}_{il}^{(m)}-\delta_{il}{S}_{kj}^{(m)}\big).

Hereafter we will be interested in the Lax matrices of the following form

\displaystyle L(u)=L^{(1,2,\dots,N)}(u)C,

(4.3)

where
C
is an arbitrary constant matrix
C=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}c_{ij}X_{ij}
.
By the virtue of the fact that in the case of the

-matrix (

\displaystyle[r(u,v),C\otimes C]=0,

the Lax matrix (

) with a rational

The Lax matrix (
4.3
) is a Lax matrix of the
XXX type
\mathfrak{gl}(2)
Heisenberg spin chain with twisted periodic boundary conditions
defined by the constant twist matrix
C

[

]
.

4.2
Integrals and Casimir functions

Let us now consider the integrable system on
\mathfrak{gl}(2)^{\oplus N}
defined with the help of the Lax matrix (
4.3
). Its mutually Poisson-commuting integrals of motion are constructed from the characteristic polynomial of the Lax matrix
L(u)

\displaystyle I(w,u)=\det(L(u)-w\,{\rm Id})=w^{2}-w\operatorname{tr}L(u)+\det L(u).

The function
\det L(u)=\det C\det L^{(1,2,\dots,N)}(u)
is a generating function of the Casimir functions of the quadratic brackets (
3.2
). The corresponding Casimir functions it contains consist of different combinations of the linear and quadratic Casimir functions of
\mathfrak{gl}(2)^{\oplus N}

\displaystyle c_{k}=S^{(k)}_{11}+S^{(k)}_{22},\qquad C_{k}=S^{(k)}_{11}S^{(k)}_{22}-S^{(k)}_{12}S^{(k)}_{21}.

The generating function of integrals of motion is

\displaystyle I(u)=\operatorname{tr}L(u).

More explicitly, we have that

\displaystyle I(u)=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}(\nu_{i}-u)}\left((-1)^{N}(c_{11}+c_{22})u^{N}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}I_{k}u^{N-k}\right),

where the Hamiltonians
I_{k}
are non-homogeneous polynomials of the degree up to
k
in the dynamical variables. In particular, we have that

\displaystyle I_{1}=-\left(\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}c_{ij}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}S^{(k)}_{ij}+\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N}\nu_{l}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}c_{ii}\right),

\displaystyle I_{2}=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}c_{ij}\sum\limits_{l,m=1,\,l<m}^{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2}S^{(m)}_{ik}S^{(l)}_{kj}+\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}c_{ij}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N}\nu_{l}-\nu_{k}\right)S^{(k)}_{ij}+\sum\limits_{k,l=1,\,k<l}^{N}\nu_{l}\nu_{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}c_{ii}.

The integral
I_{2}
may be viewed as a quadratic Hamiltonian of the classical spin chain of
N
spins. It evidently does not have the Heisenberg-type form. The Heisenberg-type Hamiltonians are obtained from
I(u)
(in the quantum case it works in the representation of
\mathfrak{gl}(2)^{\oplus N}
algebra by Pauli matrices) using formulae of the following type
[
24
]

\displaystyle H_{n}=\left(\frac{{\rm d}^{n}\ln I(u)}{{\rm d}u^{n}}\right)\bigg|_{u=0}.

We will not consider or use the Hamiltonians
H_{n}
here: they do not enter directly into the spectral curve nor into the equation of separation, and therefore are not useful for our purposes.

Remark 4.1
.

We have preserved seemingly non-important constant terms in the integrals of motion because they make non-trivial contributions to the equations of separation.

4.3
Non-standard variable separation: general degenerated case

4.3.1

The following theorem holds true.

Theorem 4.2

Let us assume that the matrix

4
4

4

In the case

Let the coordinates

if and only if

\displaystyle\det C\equiv c_{11}c_{22}-c_{12}c_{21}=0.

The equations of separation have in this case the following form

\displaystyle c_{12}p_{i}=c_{11}I(x_{i}),\qquad i=1,\dots,N.

(4.4)

Remark 4.3
.

One may wonder if the exchange of roles between
A(u)
and
B(u)
is not trivially equivalent to swapping the Lax matrix elements
L_{11}(u)
and
L_{21}(u)
by a mere change of Lax representation. However, this is not so: indeed, one might take as a new Lax matrix the product
L^{C_{0}}\equiv C_{0}L(u)

.
5
5

5

In the quantum case a similar trick was used in the paper
[

In this case we have

\displaystyle B^{C_{0}}(u)\equiv L^{C_{0}}_{21}(u)=L_{11}(u)=A(u),\qquad A^{C_{0}}(u)\equiv L^{C_{0}}_{11}(u)=L_{21}(u)=B(u).

But in this way the integrals of motion are also changed — instead of the generating function

\displaystyle I(u)=\operatorname{tr}L(u)=L_{11}(u)+L_{22}(u)

one obtains a new generating function

\displaystyle I^{C_{0}}(u)=\operatorname{tr}L^{C_{0}}(u)=L_{12}(u)+L_{21}(u).

After the change, the Sklyanin recipe yields separation variables for
another

A(u)
and

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

First of all let us observe, that by the virtue of the relation (

r

\displaystyle\{B(u),B(v)\}=0,

\displaystyle\{A(u),B(v)\}=\frac{B(u)A(v)-B(v)A(u)}{u-v},

\displaystyle\{A(u),A(v)\}=0.

From this and the Proposition
2.1
(with the reversed role of the function

and

,

defined as above are (quasi)canonical

\displaystyle\{x_{i},x_{j}\}=0,\qquad\{p_{i},p_{j}\}=0,\qquad\{x_{i},p_{i}\}=p_{i},\qquad\forall\,i=1,\dots,N.

Now it is left to show that they satisfy the equations of separation. We will prove them for any Lax matrix of the form

\displaystyle L(u)=\tilde{L}(u)C,

where
\tilde{L}(u)
is any Lax matrix satisfying quadratic brackets (

\displaystyle B(u)=\tilde{L}_{21}(u)c_{11}+\tilde{L}_{22}(u)c_{21},\qquad A(u)=\tilde{L}_{11}(u)c_{11}+\tilde{L}_{12}(u)c_{21},

\displaystyle I(u)=\tilde{L}_{11}(u)c_{11}+\tilde{L}_{12}(u)c_{21}+\tilde{L}_{21}(u)c_{12}+\tilde{L}_{22}(u)c_{22}.

\displaystyle I(u)=\frac{c_{12}}{c_{11}}B(u)+A(u)+\frac{c_{11}c_{22}-c_{12}c_{21}}{c_{11}}\tilde{L}_{22}(u).

(4.5)

The necessity follows from the results of

∎

4.3.2
The Abel equations

The important step in the theory of classical variable separation is re-writing of the equations of motion in the Abel form. This permits one to integrate the equations of motion for
x_{i}
resolving the so-called Abel–Jacobi inversion problem.

The following proposition holds true.

Proposition 4.7
.

The equations of motion for the coordinates of separation
x_{i}
described in the previous subsection are written in the Abel-type form as follows

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{x_{i}^{N-j}}{(-1)^{N}(c_{11}+c_{22})x_{i}^{N}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}I_{k}x_{i}^{N-k}}\frac{{\rm d}x_{i}}{{\rm d}t_{k}}=-\delta^{j}_{k},\qquad j,k=1,\dots,N,

(4.6)

where
\frac{{\rm d}x_{i}}{{\rm d}t_{k}}=\{I_{k},x_{i}\}
,
k=1,\dots,N
, etc.

Idea of the proof.

The Abel equations (
4.6
) are the consequences of the general Abel-type equations (
2.1
) and are calculated using the explicit form of the equations of separation in the considered case.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Let us remark that the equations (
4.6
) are easily integrated and the corresponding Abel–Jacobi problem is resolved in terms of the elementary functions.

6
Conclusion and discussion

In the present paper we have constructed non-standard separated variables for the classical integrable XXX and XXZ spin chains with the degenerated twist matrix reversing the role of the functions
A(u)
and
B(u)
in the corresponding separating algebra. We have also obtained the Abel-type equations for the corresponding coordinates/momenta of separation. We have shown that for special cases of the degenerated twist matrices our separated variables can be identified with the action-angle coordinates from the Liouville theorem.

It would be interesting and physically important to find quantum and higher rank analogues of the proposed separated variables. These problems are open.

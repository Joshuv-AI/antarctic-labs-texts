Global Rigidity of Triangulated Manifolds
James Cruickshank, Bill Jackson, Shin-ichi Tanigawa

Abstract
We prove that if G G is the graph of a connected triangulated ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -manifold, for d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , then G G is generically globally rigid in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} if and only if it is ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -connected and, if d = 3 d=3 , G G is not planar. The special case d = 3 d=3 verifies a conjecture of Connelly. Our results actually apply to a much larger class of simplicial complexes, namely the circuits of the simplicial matroid. We also give two significant applications of our main theorems. We show that the characterisation of pseudomanifolds with extremal edge numbers given by the Lower Bound Theorem extends to circuits of the simplicial matroid. We also prove the generic case of a conjecture of Kalai concerning the reconstructability of a polytope from its space of stresses. The proofs of our main results adapt earlier ideas of Fogelsanger and Whiteley to the setting of global rigidity. In particular we verify a special case of Whiteley’s vertex splitting conjecture for global rigidity. Keywords: global rigidity, triangulated manifold, simplicial complex, vertex splitting, lower bound theorem. MSC: Primary: 52C25. Secondary: 05E45, 52B05, 05C10.

Introduction

The study of the rigidity of triangulated surfaces can be traced back to a claim made by Euler in 1776 that “A closed spatial figure allows not changes, as long as it is not ripped apart.” Giving a formal proof for Euler’s claim turned out to be a challenging problem. A celebrated result of Cauchy in 1813 implies that every triangulated convex spherical surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is rigid.
Gluck
[
12
]
showed that every generic triangulated spherical surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is rigid.
Rather surprisingly, Connelly
[
6
,
7
]
disproved Euler’s original claim by constructing a flexible triangulated spherical surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
It is natural to also consider the rigidity of triangulations of other closed surfaces.
Fogelsanger
[
10
]
was able to extend Gluck’s theorem by showing that every generic triangulated surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is rigid.

In this paper we will relax the hypothesis at the end of Euler’s claim and ask
whether a closed spacial figure can change if it is ripped apart and then re-assembled. We will consider the version of the problem in which the faces of a triangulated surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
are re-assembled so that all adjacencies are preserved and we will allow the re-assembled faces to pass through each other.
The re-assembled surface may differ from the original surface even when the original surface is generic.
For example, if
a triangulated surface has a vertex
v
of degree three which does not lie
in the affine plane spanned by its three neighbours,
then we can obtain a non-congruent surface
by reflecting
v
in this plane.
The same trick can be applied whenever the underlying graph of the triangulated surface has a vertex-separator of size three. This suggests that we need to add the condition that the underlying graph is 4-connected to ensure that a triangulated surface is uniquely defined by its faces and their adjacencies, but this is still not strong enough to guarantee uniqueness.
A result of
Hendrickson
[
15
]
(Theorem
1.1
below) implies that every generic triangulated spherical surface can be changed to a non-congruent triangulated surface by re-assembling its faces, no matter how high the connectivity of its underlying graph is.
We will show that spherical surfaces provide the only such examples:
every generic triangulated non-spherical surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is uniquely defined by its faces and their adjacencies whenever its underlying graph is 4-connected.
(Combining our result with a recent result by Gortler, Theran, and Thurston
[
14
]
, it further implies that
every generic triangulated non-spherical surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is uniquely determined by its list of edge lengths and its number of vertices.)

We can use the language of rigidity theory to give a more precise description of our results.
A
d
-dimensional
framework
is defined as a pair
(G,p)
consisting of a graph
G
and a map
p:V(G)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We will also say that
(G,p)
is a

of
G
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Two realisations

and
(G,q)

of

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

are

if
(G,p)
can be obtained from
(G,q)
by an isometry of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, i.e., a combination of translations, rotations
and reflections. The framework
(G,p)
is
globally rigid
if every framework which has the
same edge lengths as
(G,p)
is congruent to
(G,p)
. It is
rigid
if every continuous motion of
the vertices of
(G,p)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
which preserves the edge lengths results in a framework which
is congruent to
(G,p)
.

A framework
(G,p)
is

if the multiset of coordinates of the points
p(v),v\in V(G)
,
is algebraically independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Gluck
[
12
]
showed that the rigidity of a generic framework
depends only on its underlying graph.
This enables us to define
a graph

as being
rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
if some (or equivalently every) generic realisation of
G

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is rigid.
Analogous, but much deeper, results of Connelly
[
8
]
and Gortler, Healy and Thurston
[
13
]

imply that the global rigidity of a generic framework depends only on its underlying graph and allows us to define a graph

G
as being
globally rigid
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
if some (or equivalently every) generic realisation of
G
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is globally rigid.
Finding a combinatorial characterisation of rigid or globally rigid graphs in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
is a major open problem in discrete geometry.
One family of graphs for which generic rigidity in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is well understood is the graphs (1-skeletons) of triangulated
(d-1)
-manifolds. Whiteley
[
29
]
extended Gluck’s Theorem by showing that the graphs of
(d-1)
-polytopes are rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for all
d\geq 3
. This result was in turn extended to
graphs of triangulated
(d-1)
-manifolds for all

by Kalai
[
18
]
and subsequently for all

[
10

A fundamental necessary condition for any non-complete graph

|E(G)|\geq d|V(G)|-\binom{d+1}{2}.

(1)

Thus Fogelsanger’s Rigidity Theorem implies that the graph
of any triangulated connected

The following result of Hendrickson

Theorem 1.1

(Hendrickson’s necessary conditions)
.

Let

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Then

is either a complete graph on at most

G
is
(d+1)
-connected and

is rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Hendrickson’s second necessary condition and (

must satisfy

This implies, in particular, that a triangulation of the 2-sphere (equivalently, a plane triangulation) cannot be globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
Since this argument applies only to spherical surfaces, Connelly
[
4
]
asked if the global rigidity of triangulations of non-spherical surfaces is characterised by their 4-connectivity.
We will answer both this question and its generalisation to triangulated

Theorem 1.2

Let

or

is
(d+1)
-connected and, when

Theorem

Their approach,
based on Whiteley’s vertex splitting operation (defined in Section

This approach was developed for the surfaces

and

in
[

Shinoki

This approach is inevitably limited by the fact that the number of irreducible triangulations of a fixed surface grows rapidly with respect to its genus.
We will instead adopt the approach taken by Fogelsanger in his PhD thesis

His ingenious idea is to consider a more general family

This family can be defined as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

Then

2
Preliminary results on rigidity

Let
d
be a positive integer.
A basic step in deciding whether a given
d
-dimensional framework
(G,p)
is rigid is to check its infinitesimal rigidity by calculating the rank of its
rigidity matrix
. This is the matrix of size
|E|\times d|V(G)|
in which each row is indexed by an edge, sets of
d
consecutive columns are indexed by the vertices, and the row indexed by the edge
e=uv
has the form:

\bordermatrix{&&u&&v&\cr e=uv&0\dots 0&p(u)-p(v)&0\dots 0&p(v)-p(u)&0\dots 0}.

The space of
infinitesimal motions
of
(G,p)
is given by the right kernel of
R(G,p)
, and
(G,p)
is
infinitesimally rigid
if we have
\rank R(G,p)=d|V(G)|-\binom{d+1}{2}
when
|V(G)|\geq d
and
\rank R(G,p)=\binom{|V(G)|}{2}
when
|V(G)|\leq d-1
, since this will imply that every vector in
\ker R(G,p)
is an infinitesimal isometry of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
It is a fundamental fact in rigidity theory that rigidity and infinitesimal rigidity are equivalent properties for generic frameworks.
Since the rank of
R(G,p)
will be maximised whenever
(G,p)
is generic,
this implies that a graph
G
is rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
if and only if some realisation of
G
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is infinitesimally rigid.

We say that a graph
G
is
minimally rigid
if it is rigid and
G-e
is not rigid for every
e\in E(G)
.
If
G
is minimally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
|V(G)|\geq d
, then

R(G,p)
is row independent with
\rank R(G,p)=d|V(G)|-\binom{d+1}{2}
for some
d
-dimensional realisation
(G,p)
. This in turn
implies that
|E(G)|=d|V(G)|-\binom{d+1}{2}
and

|E(H)|\leq d|V(H)|-\binom{d+1}{2}\quad\text{for all subgraphs $H$ of $G$ with $|V(H)|\geq d$}.

(2)

We next introduce two well-known graph operations that preserve infinitesimal rigidity.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph.
The
d
-dimensional
0
-extension
operation creates a new graph from
G
by adding a new vertex
v
and
d
new edges incident to
v
. It is well-known that the
d
-dimensional 0-extension operation preserves generic rigidity in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
This follows from the following more general statement for arbitrary frameworks.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
(G,p)
is a framework in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
v
is a vertex in
G
.
If
(G-v,p|_{V-v})
is infinitesimally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\{p(v)-p(u):u\in N_{G}(v)\}
spans
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
then
(G,p)
is infinitesimally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
and
v\in V
with neighbour set
N_{G}(v)
the
(
d
-dimensional) vertex splitting operation
constructs a new graph
G^{\prime}
by deleting
v
, adding two new vertices
v^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime\prime}
with
N_{G}(v^{\prime})\cup N_{G}(v^{\prime\prime})=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\}
and
|N_{G}(v^{\prime})\cap N_{G}(v^{\prime\prime})|\geq d-1
. Whiteley
[
30
]
showed that vertex splitting preserves generic rigidity in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. More precisely he proved

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
(G,p)
is an infinitesimally rigid framework in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and that
G^{\prime}
is obtained from
G
by a vertex splitting operation which splits a vertex
v
of
G
into two vertices
v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}
. Suppose further that, for some
X\subseteq N_{G^{\prime}}(v^{\prime})\cap N_{G^{\prime}}(v^{\prime\prime})
with

, the points in
\{p(w)\,:\,w\in X+v\}
are affinely independent in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then
(G^{\prime},p^{\prime})
is infinitesimally rigid for some
p^{\prime}
with
p^{\prime}(v^{\prime})=p(v)
and
p^{\prime}(w)=p(w)
for all
w\in V(G)\setminus\{v\}
.

Suppose that
(G_{1},p_{1})
and
(G_{2},p_{2})
are infinitesimally rigid frameworks in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that, for
i=1,2
the affine span of
p_{i}(V(G_{i}))
has dimension at least
d-1
, and such that
p_{1}(v)=p_{2}(v)
for
v\in V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})
. Define

by

for

. The
infinitesimal rigidity gluing property for frameworks
says that
(G_{1}\cup G_{2},p)
is infinitesimally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if the affine span of
p(V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2}))
has dimension at least
d-1
.

In particular, the

rigidity gluing property
states that,
for any two rigid graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|V(G_{i})|\geq d
for

,
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if

|V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})|\geq d
.
This intuitively clear fact is one of the characteristic properties of graph rigidity.
It is also a key tool in the proof of Fogelsanger’s Rigidity Theorem.
The analogous
global rigidity gluing property
states that,
for any two globally rigid graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|V(G_{i})|\geq d+1
for
i=1,2
,
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if
|V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})|\geq d+1
.

G_{1}\cup K(X)
and
G_{2}\cup H
are globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

3
Simplicial circuits

3.1
Definitions and elementary properties

Throughout the remainder of this paper we will be interested in non-trivial simplicial
k
-circuits as defined in the introduction. However, our analysis will focus mostly on either the set of facets (i.e., faces of maximal dimension) or on the graph of these objects.
Thus we will identify a pure abstract simplicial
k
-complex with its set of
k
-simplices. Furthermore, it will also be useful for technical reasons explained in Section
3.2
to allow our objects to have multiple copies of the same
k
-simplex.
Thus for an integer
k\geq 0
we will refer to a multiset
{\cal S}
of
(k+1)
-sets
as a
simplicial
k
-multicomplex
, and
its elements as
k
-simplices
.
In situations where the elements of
{\cal S}
are known to be distinct, we will refer to
{\cal S}
as a
simplicial
k
-complex
. We emphasise here that all of our main results concern objects which are simplicial
k
-complexes and are thus the set of facets of an abstract simplicial
k
-complex in the standard meaning of this term.

For
0\leq r\leq k
, an
r
-face
of a simplicial
k
-multicomplex
{\cal S}
is a set
R
with
|R|=r+1
and
R\subseteq S
for some
S\in{\cal S}
.
We also define the empty set to be the unique
(-1)
-face of
{\cal S}
.
We will often refer to the
0
-faces of
{\cal S}
as
vertices
and the
1
-faces as
edges
.
The
graph

G({\cal S})
of
{\cal S}
is the simple graph
with vertex set
V({\cal S})=\bigcup_{S\in{\cal S}}S
and edge set
E({\cal S})=\{uv\,:\,\{u,v\}\subseteq S\in{\cal S}\}
.
We will refer to a
(k+1)
-clique of
G({\cal S})
which is not a
k
-simplex in
{\cal S}
as a
non-facial
(k+1)
-clique
of
G({\cal S})
.
Given a vertex
v\in V({\cal S})
, we use
{\cal S}_{v}
to denote the multiset of
k
-simplices of
{\cal S}
which contain
v
.
More generally, given a subset
F\subseteq V({\cal S})
of
{\cal S}
, define
{\cal S}_{F}
to be the multiset of
k
-simplices of
{\cal S}
which contain
F
. We set
{\cal S}_{F}=\emptyset
in cases where
F\not\subseteq V({\cal S})
. We also define the
link
of
F
in
{\cal S}
to be the multiset
lk_{\cal S}(F)=\{U\setminus F:U\in{\cal S}_{F}\}
.

For
k\geq 1
two
k
-simplices
S,S^{\prime}\in{\cal S}
are
adjacent
if
|S\cap S^{\prime}|\geq k-1
.
The simplicial
k
-complex
{\cal S}
is
strongly connected
if any two
k
-simplices in
{\cal S}
are joined by a
strong path
i.e., a sequence of
k
-simplices in which consecutive pairs are adjacent. It is a
k
-pseudomanifold
if it is strongly connected and every
(k-1)
-face belongs to exactly two
k
-simplices.
It is a
triangulated
k
-manifold
if its geometric realisation is a topological
k
-manifold.
These definitions imply that every triangulated connected
k
-manifold is a
k
-pseudomanifold, see for example
[
18
]
.

For

k\geq 0

we define the boundary
\partial{\cal S}

of a simplicial
k
-complex
{\cal S}

to be the set of all
(k-1)
-faces of
{\cal S}

which are contained in an odd number of
k
-simplices of
{\cal S}
i.e.,
\partial S=\{F\subseteq V({\cal S}):|F|=k,|{\cal S}_{F}|\equiv 1\mod 2\}
. Note that our definition excludes
\partial{\cal S}
from containing multiple copies of the same
(k-1)
-simplex.
Thus, for
k\geq 1
,

\partial S
is a simplicial
(k-1)
-complex.
For
k=0
,
\partial{\cal S}=\{\emptyset\}
if
|{\cal S}|
is odd and
\partial{\cal S}=\emptyset
is
|S|
if even.
For example, if
{\cal S}_{1}=\{\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\},\{v_{1},v_{3},v_{4}\}\}
and
{\cal S}_{2}=\{\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\},\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}\}
,
then
\partial{\cal S}_{1}=\{\{v_{1},v_{2}\},\{v_{2},v_{3}\},\{v_{3},v_{4}\},\{v_{4},v_{1}\}\}
and
\partial{\cal S}_{2}=\emptyset
.

The following elementary observations will be used repeatedly throughout the remainder of the paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
{\cal S}
and
{\cal T}
are simplicial
k
-complexes.

(a)

For any
F\subseteq V({\cal S})\cup V({\cal T})
,
({\cal S}\triangle{\cal T})_{F}={\cal S}_{F}\triangle{\cal T}_{F}
.

(b)

\partial({\cal S}\triangle{\cal T})=\partial{\cal S}\triangle\partial{\cal T}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
U\in{\cal S}\cup{\cal T}
and
F\subseteq U
. Then

U\in({\cal S}\triangle{\cal T})_{F}\Leftrightarrow U\in{\cal S}\triangle{\cal T}\Leftrightarrow U\in{\cal S}_{F}\triangle{\cal T}_{F}
which proves (a).
For (b), let
F
be a
(k-1)
-face of
{\cal S}\cup{\cal T}
. Then, using (a),
F\in\partial({\cal S}\triangle{\cal T})\Leftrightarrow|{\cal S}_{F}\triangle{\cal T}_{F}|\equiv 1\mod 2\Leftrightarrow|{\cal S}_{F}|+|{\cal T}_{F}|\equiv 1\mod 2\Leftrightarrow F\in\partial{\cal S}\triangle\partial{\cal T}_{F}
.
∎

For
k\geq 0
a simplicial
k
-multicomplex
{\cal S}
is said to be a
simplicial
k
-cycle
if

\partial{\cal S}=\emptyset
i.e.,
{\cal S}
is
a
k
-cycle in the corresponding chain complex.
For example a simplicial 1-cycle is the edge set of an even multigraph (without loops) i.e., a multigraph in which each vertex has even degree.
We say that
{\cal S}
is a
simplicial
k
-circuit
if it is a non-empty simplicial
k
-cycle and no proper nonempty subfamily of
{\cal S}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle.
Note that a simplicial
k
-multicomplex consisting of two copies of the same
k
-simplex is a simplicial
k
-circuit. Such a simplicial
k
-circuit is called
trivial
.
Hence a non-trivial simplicial
k
-circuit cannot contain two copies of the same
k
-simplex.
We emphasise in particular that a non-trivial simplicial
k
-circuit is a simplicial
k
-complex.
For example, every non-trivial simplicial 1-circuit is the edge set of a ‘cycle graph’ i.e., a connected 2-regular graph with at least three vertices.
The set of facial triangles in a triangulated connected 2-manifold is an example of a simplicial 2-circuit.

Simplicial
k
-cycles have the nice property that,
if
{\cal S}
and
{\cal S}^{\prime}
are simplicial
k
-cycles with
{\cal S}^{\prime}\subseteq{\cal S}
, then
{\cal S}\setminus{\cal S}^{\prime}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle. This implies that every nonempty simplicial
k
-cycle can be partitioned into simplicial
k
-circuits. We can use this observation to show that every pseudomanifold is a simplicial circuit.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{\cal S}
be a
k
-pseudomanifold for some
k\geq 1
.
Then
{\cal S}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit.

Proof.

Since
{\cal S}
is a
k
-pseudomanifold, every
(k-1)
-face belongs to exactly two
k
-simplices of
{\cal S}
. This implies that
\partial{\cal S}=\emptyset
so
{\cal S}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle and hence
{\cal S}
can be partitioned into
simplicial
k
-circuits. In particular,
{\cal S}
contains a
simplicial
k
-circuit
{\cal C}
.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
{\cal S}\neq{\cal C}
.
Then we can choose two
k
-simplices
S\in{\cal C}
and
S^{\prime}\in{\cal S}\setminus{\cal C}
. Let
{\cal T}
be the set of all
k
-simplices in
{\cal S}
which can be reached by a strong path beginning at
S
. The facts that
{\cal C}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit and every
(k-1)
-face of
{\cal S}
belongs to exactly two
k
-simplices of
{\cal S}

imply that
{\cal T}\subseteq{\cal C}
. This in turn implies that
S^{\prime}\not\in{\cal T}
and contradicts the hypothesis that
{\cal S}
is a pseudomanifold.
∎

Note that for
k\geq 2
the class of simplicial
k
-circuits is strictly bigger than the class of
k
-pseudomanifolds. For example, suppose that
{\cal T}
is a triangulation of the 2-sphere and suppose that
u,v\in V({\cal T})
are distinct vertices with exactly one common neighbour
w
. We can construct a non-planar simplicial 2-circuit by identifying
u
and
v
. More precisely, let
{\cal T}^{\prime}
be obtained from
{\cal T}
by replacing every 2-simplex
S
that contains
v
with
S-v+u
. Then, one readily checks that
{\cal T}^{\prime}
is a simplicial 2-circuit and that the 1-face
\{u,w\}
is contained in four
2
-simplices. Thus
{\cal T}^{\prime}
is not a 2-pseudomanifold.

Our next two lemmas are elementary observations that we will use repeatedly in the rest of the paper. The first
corresponds to the standard result in algebraic topology that says that the square of the boundary homomorphism in the chain complex of a simplicial set is zero. We give an elementary proof for completeness.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\cal S}
be a
simplicial
k
-multicomplex with
k\geq 1
.
Then
\partial{\cal S}
is a simplicial
(k-1)
-cycle.

Proof.

Let
A
be a
(k-2)
-face of
\partial{\cal S}
and let

X_{A}=\{(B,C):C\in{\cal S},A\subseteq B\subseteq C,|B|=k\}
. Given
C
there are exactly two choices for
B
, so
|X_{A}|
is even. On the other hand, given
B
there are an odd number of choices for
C
if and only if
B\in\partial{\cal S}
. Therefore
|\{B\in\partial{\cal S}:A\subseteq B\}|\equiv|X_{A}|\equiv 0\mod 2
as required.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
{\cal S}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle and
F\subseteq V({\cal S})
is an
i
-face of
{\cal S}
for some
0\leq i\leq k-1
. Then
lk_{\cal S}(F)
is a simplicial
(k-i-1)
-cycle.

Proof.

Let
W
be a
(k-i-2)
-face of
lk_{\cal S}(F)
.
The mapping
Y\mapsto Y\cup F
is a bijection between the
(k-i-1)
-simplices of
lk_{\cal S}(F)
that contain
W
and the
k
-simplices of
{\cal S}
that contain
W\cup F
.
The lemma follows immediately from this.
∎

Let
{\cal K}_{k}
be the simplicial
k
-complex obtained by taking every
(k+1)
-subset of a set of size
k+2
. For example,

{\cal K}_{1}
is the set of edges of a triangle and
{\cal K}_{2}
is the set of faces of a tetrahedron.
It is not difficult to see that
{\cal K}_{k}
is a pseudomanifold and hence

{\cal K}_{k}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit by Lemma
3.2
.
We will continue to use
K_{k}
for the complete graph on
k
vertices. Thus
G({\cal K}_{k})=K_{k+1}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
k\geq 0
, let
{\cal S}
be a nonempty simplicial
k
-cycle without repeated
k
-simplices.
Then
|V({\cal S})|\geq k+2
, and
|V({\cal S})|=k+2
if and only if

{\cal S}
is isomorphic to
{\cal K}_{k}
.

Proof.

If
|V({\cal S})|\leq k+1
then
{\cal S}
has at most one
k
-simplex and so cannot be a nonempty simplicial
k
-cycle, contradicting our hypothesis. So
|V({\cal S})|\geq k+2
.
Now suppose that
|V({\cal S})|=k+2
. Pick any
U\in{\cal S}
and let
w
be the unique vertex in
V({\cal S})\setminus U
. Since
{\cal S}
is a
k
-cycle that does not contain any repeated
k
-simplex, it follows that, for each
u\in U
, there must be a
k
-simplex of
{\cal S}
, different from
U
, that contains
U-u
. In other words
U-u+w\in{\cal S}
. Therefore every
(k+1)
-subset of
V({\cal S})
is a
k
-simplex of
{\cal S}
and so
{\cal S}
is isomorphic to
{\cal K}_{k}
.
∎

Let
{\cal L}_{k}
be the simplicial
k
-complex obtained from the union of two copies of
{\cal K}_{k}
with exactly one
k
-simplex in common by deleting their common
k
-simplex.
For example,
{\cal L}_{1}
is the edge set of a 4-cycle, and
{\cal L}_{2}
is the set of facets of a hexahedron.
It is not difficult to see that
{\cal L}_{k}
is a pseudomanifold and hence
is a simplicial
k
-circuit by Lemma
3.2
.
We will show that
{\cal L}_{k}
is the unique simplicial
k
-circuit on
k+3
vertices whose graph is not complete.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
k\geq 1
, let
{\cal S}
be a non-trivial simplicial

k
-cycle without repeated
k
-simplices.
Suppose that
|V({\cal S})|=k+3
.
If
k\leq 2
, then
{\cal S}
is isomorphic to
{\cal L}_{k}
.
If
k\geq 3
, then either
G({\cal S})\cong K_{k+3}
or
{\cal S}
is isomorphic to
{\cal L}_{k}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G({\cal S})\not\cong K_{k+3}
.
Choose two non-adjacent vertices
u
and
v
in
G({\cal S})
and put
X=V({\cal S})\setminus\{u,v\}
.
Lemma
3.3
tells us that
\partial({\cal S}_{u})
is a simplicial
(k-1)
-cycle.
Note that since
{\cal S}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle that does not have any repeated
k
-simplex, it follows that
\partial({\cal S}_{u})=\{U-u:U\in{\cal S}_{u}\}
. Also
v\not\in V(\partial({\cal S}_{u}))
since
uv\not\in E({\cal S})
. Therefore
V(\partial({\cal S}_{u}))\subseteq X
.
Lemma
3.5
now implies that equality holds and
\partial({\cal S}_{u})
is isomorphic to
{\cal K}_{k-1}
. This in turn gives
\partial({\cal S}_{u})=\partial\{X\}
and hence

{\cal S}_{u}\triangle\{X\}

is a simplicial
k
-cycle.
Lemma
3.5
now implies that
{\cal S}_{u}\triangle\{X\}
is isomorphic to
{\cal K}_{k}
. By symmetry we also have that
{\cal S}_{v}\triangle\{X\}
is isomorphic to
{\cal K}_{k}
. Hence
{\cal S}
is isomorphic to
{\cal L}_{k}
.

It is straightforward to check that
{\cal L}_{k}
is the unique simplicial
k
-circuit with
k+3
vertices when
k=1,2
.
∎

A fundamental property of graphs of triangulated connected 2-manifolds is that
they are
3
-connected and every 3-vertex-separator is a non-facial 3-clique.
Our next lemma shows that this property extends to non-trivial simplicial
k
-circuits.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
{\cal S}
be a non-trivial simplicial
k
-circuit with
k\geq 2
.

(a)

G({\cal S})
is
(k+1)
-connected.

(b)

If
X
is a
(k+1)
-vertex separator of
G({\cal S})
then
X
is a non-facial
(k+1)
-clique of
G({\cal S})
and there are simplicial
k
-circuits
{\cal S}^{\prime}
and
{\cal S}^{\prime\prime}
such that
{\cal S}^{\prime}\cap{\cal S}^{\prime\prime}=\{X\}
and
{\cal S}={\cal S}^{\prime}\triangle{\cal S}^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof.

Suppose
G({\cal S})=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
with
V(G_{1})\setminus V(G_{2})\neq\emptyset\neq V(G_{2})\setminus V(G_{1})
. Let
X=V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})
. Let
{\cal S}_{1}=\{S\in{\cal S}:S\subseteq V(G_{1})\}
and
{\cal S}_{2}={\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}_{1}
and note that
{\cal S}_{1},{\cal S}_{2}
are both proper subsets of
{\cal S}
. Then
{\cal S}={\cal S}_{1}\triangle{\cal S}_{2}
and
V({\cal S}_{1})\cap V({\cal S}_{2})\subseteq X
.
Since
{\cal S}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit, we have
\partial{\cal S}=\emptyset\neq\partial{\cal S}_{1}
. This implies that
\partial{\cal S}_{1}=\partial{\cal S}_{2}\neq\emptyset
and
V(\partial{\cal S}_{1})\subseteq X
. Since
\partial{\cal S}_{1}
is a simplicial
(k-1)
-cycle without repeated
(k-1)
-simplices by Lemma
3.3
, we can use Lemma
3.5
and the fact that every simplicial
(k-1)
-cycle contains a simplicial
(k-1)
-circuit to deduce that
|X|\geq|V(\partial{\cal S}_{1})|\geq k+1
and hence
G({\cal S})
is
(k+1)
-connected. This proves (a).

Suppose
|X|=k+1
. Then Lemma
3.5
tells us that
\partial{\cal S}_{1}\cong{\cal K}_{k-1}
and, since
k\geq 2
,
X
is a clique in
G({\cal S})
. In addition, since
\partial\{X\}\cong{\cal K}_{k-1}
, we have
\partial{\cal S}_{1}=\partial\{X\}
and hence
\partial({\cal S}_{1}\triangle\{X\})=\emptyset
. The hypothesis that
{\cal S}
is simplicial
k
-circuit now gives

so
X
is a non-facial
k
-clique of
G({\cal S})
. Finally Let
S^{\prime}={\cal S}_{1}\cup\{X\}
and
{\cal S}^{\prime\prime}={\cal S}_{2}\cup\{X\}
. Then
{\cal S}^{\prime}
and
{\cal S}^{\prime\prime}
are non-empty simplicial
k
-cycles,
{\cal S}={\cal S}^{\prime}\triangle{\cal S}^{\prime\prime}
and
{\cal S}^{\prime}\cap{\cal S}^{\prime\prime}=\{X\}
. Suppose, for a contradiction that
{\cal R}\subsetneq{\cal S}^{\prime}
is a nonempty simplicial
k
-cycle. Then
{\cal S}^{\prime}\setminus{\cal R}^{\prime}
is also nonempty simplicial
k
-cycle and one of
{\cal R}
or
{\cal S}^{\prime}\setminus{\cal R}
is contained in
{\cal S}_{1}
, contradicting the
fact that
{\cal S}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit. Therefore
{\cal S}^{\prime}
, and similarly
{\cal S}^{\prime\prime}
, is a simplicial
k
-circuit.
∎

Since our main aim is to characterise global rigidity for graphs of simplicial
k
-circuits when

, henceforth we will assume (unless otherwise stated) that this is the case. We observe in passing that the only graphs of simplicial

-circuits that are (globally) rigid in

are

and
K_{3}
.

3.2
Contractions

We define a contraction operation for two vertices in a simplicial multicomplex.
This operation will be used in inductive proofs throughout this paper. We will see that simplicial cycles are closed under this operation. The same is not true for simplicial circuits but Fogelsanger showed that this problem can be circumvented by choosing a partition of the contracted complex into simplicial circuits and then using the pre-images of these simplicial circuits to construct the Fogelsanger decomposition of the original circuit. We will obtain some preliminary results on these pre-images in this subsection and use them to define the Fogelsanger decomposition in the next subsection. Although the presentation is simplified, the results in both subsections are essentially due to Fogelsanger
[
10
]
.

Given a simplicial
k
-multicomplex

{\cal S}
and distinct vertices
u,v\in V({\cal S})
,
we can obtain a new
simplicial
k
-multicomplex

{\cal S}/uv
from
{\cal S}
by deleting all
k
-simplices in
{\cal S}_{\{u,v\}}
, and replacing every
k
-simplex
S\in{\cal S}
which contains
v
but not
u
by a new
k
-simplex
S^{\prime}=S-v+u
. We say that
{\cal S}/uv
is obtained from
{\cal S}
by
contracting
v
onto
u
.
For each
k
-simplex
S\in{\cal S}
with
\{u,v\}\not\subseteq{\cal S}
, we denote the
k
-simplex in
{\cal S}/uv
corresponding to
S
by
\gamma_{uv}(S)
.
Thus
\gamma_{uv}
is a bijection between the
k
-simplices of
{\cal S}
which do not contain
\{u,v\}
and the
k
-simplices of
{\cal S}/uv
.
Note that if
S_{1},S_{2}\in{\cal S}
with
u\in S_{1}
,
v\in S_{2}
and
S_{1}-u=S_{2}-v
then

\gamma_{uv}(S_{2})
and
\gamma_{uv}(S_{1})
are two distinct copies of the same
k
-simplex in
{\cal S}/uv
.
Thus our contraction operation may transform a simplicial
k
-complex into a simplicial
k
-multicomplex – this is precisely the reason why we allow repetitions of
k
-simplices. We could of course delete repetitions from our new family but we prefer not to do this because of the following lemma and corollary which we will use repeatedly.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
{\cal S}
be a simplicial
k
-multicomplex,
u,v\in V({\cal S})
be distinct vertices and
F\subseteq V({\cal S})-v
,
|F|=k
. Then

|({\cal S}/uv)_{F}|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|{\cal S}_{F}|&\text{ if }u\not\in F,\\
|{\cal S}_{F}|+|{\cal S}_{F-u+v}|-2|{\cal S}_{F+v}|&\text{ if }u\in F.\end{array}\right.

(3)

Proof.

If
u\not\in F
, then
{\cal S}_{F}
is contained in
\gamma_{uv}^{-1}({\cal S}/uv)
and it follows that
\gamma_{uv}
induces a bijection between
{\cal S}_{F}
and
({\cal S}/uv)_{F}
.
On the other hand, if
u\in F
then

({\cal S}/uv)_{F}
is in one-to-one correspondence with the multiset of
k
-faces of
{\cal S}
that contain exactly one of
F
or
F-u+v
.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Suppose that
{\cal S}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle for some
k\geq 1
and that
u,v\in V({\cal S})
are distinct vertices. Then
{\cal S}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-cycle.

Proof.

Pick any
F\subseteq V({\cal S}/uv)
with
|F|=k
.
Since
{\cal S}
is simplicial
k
-cycle the terms
|{\cal S}_{F}|
and
|{\cal S}_{F-u+v}|
on the right hand side of (
3
) are even. Hence,
|({\cal S}/uv)_{F}|
is even.
∎

in a graph

:
we construct a new simple graph

from

.

We often denote

by

when

is an edge of

and say that

is obtained from

by
contracting

onto

.
These definitions are illustrated in Figure

.
Note that our definition of edge contraction for graphs does not create multiple edges.

The edge-contraction operations for complexes and graphs are compatible in the sense that
G({\cal S}/e)=G({\cal S})/e
whenever

has the property that every

lies in at least one
k
-simplex in

which does not contain

Our next result implies that this property always holds when

is a nonempty simplicial

Lemma 3.10
.

For
k\geq 2
let
{\cal S}
be a nonempty simplicial
k
-cycle that does not contain any repeated
k
-simplex. Let
uv\in E({\cal S})
and
x,y\in V({\cal S})
be distinct vertices such that
\{x,y\}\neq\{u,v\}
.
Then

(a)

{\cal S}
contains a
k
-simplex which contains
\{u,v\}
but not
\{x,y\}
.

(b)

|N_{G({\cal S})}(u)\cap N_{G({\cal S})}(v)|\geq k
, with equality if and only if
lk_{\cal S}(uv)\cong{\cal K}_{k-2}
.

Proof.

Let
U
be a
k
-simplex of
{\cal S}
that contains
\{u,v\}
. We may assume that
U
also contains
\{x,y\}
.
Since
\{u,v\}\neq\{x,y\}
, we may assume, by symmetry, that

x\not\in\{u,v\}
. Since
{\cal S}
is a cycle without repeated
k
-simplices there must be a
k
-simplex
U^{\prime}
, distinct from
U
that contains the
(k-1)
-face
U-x
. This proves (a).

For (b), we observe that
lk_{\cal S}(uv)
is a nonempty simplicial
(k-2)
-cycle without repeated
(k-2)
-simplices and
V(lk_{\cal S}(uv))\subseteq N_{G({\cal S})}(u)\cap N_{G({\cal S})}(v)
. Now statement (b) follows from Lemma
3.5

∎

The idea behind the Fogelsanger decomposition of a simplicial
k
-circuit
{\cal S}
is that, if
{\cal S}/e
is not a simplicial
k
-circuit for
e=uv\in E({\cal S})
, we choose a partition
\{{\cal S}_{1}^{\prime},\dots,{\cal S}_{m}^{\prime}\}
of
{\cal S}/e
into simplicial
k
-circuits
and consider the pre-image
{\cal S}_{i}:=\gamma_{e}^{-1}({\cal S}_{i}^{\prime})
.
Although each
{\cal S}_{i}
may not be a simplicial
k
-circuit, Fogelsanger found an explicit way to transform it into a simplicial circuit by constructing a set of
k
-simplices
{\cal S}_{i}^{*}
, each containing
\{u,v\}
, such that
{\cal S}_{i}\triangle{\cal S}^{*}_{i}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit.
Lemma
3.12
describes this transformation.

First we record an elementary observation that will be used in the proof.

Lemma 3.11
.

Suppose that
{\cal T}
is a simplicial
k
-complex and
\emptyset\neq Z\subseteq V({\cal T})
such that
{\cal T}={\cal T}_{Z}
. Then, for any
K\subseteq V({\cal T})
such that
Z\subseteq K
,

K\in{\cal T}\Leftrightarrow K-z_{0}\in\partial{\cal T}\text{ for some }z_{0}\in Z\Leftrightarrow K-z\in\partial{\cal T}\text{ for every }z\in Z.

Proof.

If
K\in{\cal T}
and
z\in Z\subseteq K
then, since
{\cal T}={\cal T}_{Z}
,
K
is the unique element of
{\cal T}
than contains
K-z
and so
K-z\in\partial{\cal T}
. On the other hand, if
K\subseteq V({\cal T})
satisfies
Z\subseteq K
and
K-z
is a
(k-1)
-face of
{\cal T}
then, since
{\cal T}={\cal T}_{Z}
, it follows that
K\in{\cal T}
.
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

Suppose
{\cal S}
is a simplicial
k
-complex and
u,v\in V({\cal S})
.
Let

{\cal S}^{\dagger}=\{K\subseteq V({\cal S}):\{u,v\}\subseteq K\mbox{ and $K-u,K-v$ are $(k-1)$-faces of ${\cal S}$}\}

and put
{\cal S}^{*}=\{K\in{\cal S}^{\dagger}:K-u,K-v\in\partial{\cal S}\}
.

(a)

If
{\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{\prime}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle for some
{\cal S}^{\prime}\subseteq{\cal S}^{\dagger}
then
{\cal S}^{\prime}={\cal S}^{*}
.

(b)

{\cal S}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-cycle if and only if
{\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle.

(c)

If
{\cal S}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-circuit then
{\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit.

Proof.

(a) Suppose
{\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{\prime}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle for some
{\cal S}^{\prime}\subseteq{\cal S}^{\dagger}
. Then
\partial({\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{\prime})=\emptyset
so
\partial{\cal S}=\partial{\cal S}^{\prime}
.
By definition
{\cal S}^{\dagger}
is a simplicial
k
-complex, so
{\cal S}^{*}
and
{\cal S}^{\prime}
must also be simplicial
k
-complexes. Now, applying Lemma
3.11
, with
{\cal T}={\cal S}^{\prime}
and
Z=\{u,v\}
,
we have that
K\in{\cal S}^{\prime}
if and only if
K-u,K-v\in\partial{\cal S}^{\prime}=\partial{\cal S}
. Hence, by the definition of
{\cal S}^{*}
,
K\in{\cal S}^{\prime}
if and only if
K\in{\cal S}^{*}
, and
{\cal S}^{\prime}={\cal S}^{*}
follows.

(b) We first suppose that
{\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle. Then
{\cal S}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-cycle since
{\cal S}/uv=({\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*})/uv
and contraction preserves the property of being a simplicial
k
-cycle.

We next suppose that
{\cal S}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-cycle.
We will show that
\partial({\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*})
is empty.
Let
F
be a
(k-1)
-face of
{\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*}
.

First consider the case
\{u,v\}\cap F=\emptyset
. Then, using Lemma
3.11
we see that
F\not\in\partial{\cal S}^{*}
. Using Lemma
3.8
and the fact that
{\cal S}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-cycle, we have

|{\cal S}_{F}|=|({\cal S}/uv)_{F}|\equiv 0\mod 2
. So
F\not\in\partial{\cal S}
and so
F\not\in\partial({\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*})
.

Next suppose that
\{u,v\}\cap F=\{u\}
.
Using Lemma
3.8
and the fact that

{\cal S}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-cycle we see that

|{\cal S}_{F-u+v}|+|{\cal S}_{F}|\equiv 0\mod 2
.
Therefore,
F\in\partial{\cal S}\Leftrightarrow F-u+v\in\partial{\cal S}
.
By the definition of
{\cal S}^{*}
, this is equivalent to
F+v\in{\cal S}^{*}
.
By Lemma 3.11, this is equivalent to saying that
F+v\in\partial{\cal S}^{*}
and
F-u+v\in\partial{\cal S}^{*}
.
It follows that

F\not\in\partial({\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*})

and
F-u+v\not\in\partial({\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*})
.

Now suppose that
\{u,v\}\cap F=\{v\}
. Then applying the argument of the previous paragraph to
F-v+u
shows that
F\not\in\partial({\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*})
.

The previous three paragraphs tell us that every
(k-1)
-face in

\partial({\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*})
contains
\{u,v\}
.
Now applying Lemma
3.11
to
\partial({\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*})
we see that
\partial(\partial({\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*}))
is nonempty, contradicting Lemma
3.3
.

(c) Suppose
{\cal S}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-circuit.
Then
{\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle by (b). Let
{\cal R}^{\prime}\subseteq{\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*}
be a simplicial
k
-circuit.
Let
{\cal R}^{\prime\prime}={\cal R}/uv
if
\{u,v\}\subseteq V({\cal R}^{\prime})
, or
{\cal R}^{\prime\prime}=\gamma_{uv}({\cal R}^{\prime})
if
\{u,v\}\not\subseteq V({\cal R}^{\prime})
. Then

{\cal R}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq{\cal S}/uv

is a simplicial
k
-cycle and, since
{\cal S}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-circuit,

{\cal R}^{\prime\prime}={\cal S}/uv
.
This tells us that the sets of
k
-simplices of
{\cal R}^{\prime}
and
{\cal S}
which do not contain
\{u,v\}
are the same and hence that
{\cal R}^{\prime}={\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{\prime}
for some
{\cal S}^{\prime}\subseteq{\cal S}^{\dagger}
. We can now use (a) to deduce that
{\cal S}^{\prime}={\cal S}^{*}
and hence
{\cal R}^{\prime}={\cal S}\triangle{\cal S}^{*}
.
∎

We will frequently apply Lemma

to a subcomplex of a larger simplicial complex. Consider, for example, the simplicial

3.3
Fogelsanger decompositions

We can now give Fogelsanger’s remarkable decomposition
result for simplicial
k
-circuits.
Since this is a key tool in our analysis, we will provide a detailed description of its properties.
We will see that Fogelsanger’s Rigidity Theorem follows easily from these properties.

Definition.

Let
{\cal S}
be a non-trivial simplicial
k
-circuit and

uv\in E({\cal S})
.
Then
{\cal S}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-cycle and we may choose a partition
\{{\cal S}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,{\cal S}_{m}^{\prime}\}
of
{\cal S}/uv
into simplicial
k
-circuits.

For all
1\leq i\leq m
, let

{\cal S}_{i}=\gamma_{uv}^{-1}({\cal S}_{i}^{\prime})
.
Then
\{u,v\}\subseteq V({\cal S}_{i})
since, if not,
{\cal S}_{i}
would be a simplicial
k
-circuit properly contained in
{\cal S}
, contradicting the fact that
{\cal S}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit.
Let

{\cal S}_{i}^{*}=\{K\subseteq V({\cal S}_{i}):\{u,v\}\subseteq K\mbox{ and }K-u,K-v\in\partial{\cal S}_{i}\}\mbox{ and }{\cal S}_{i}^{+}={\cal S}_{i}\triangle{\cal S}_{i}^{*}.

Then
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}={\cal S}_{i}\cup{\cal S}_{i}^{*}
, since
{\cal S}_{i}\cap{\cal S}_{i}^{*}=\emptyset
, and
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
is a
simplicial
k
-circuit by Lemma
3.12
(c).
We say that
\{{\cal S}_{1}^{+},\dots,{\cal S}_{m}^{+}\}
is a
Fogelsanger decomposition
for
{\cal S}
with respect to
uv
.

The definition above can also be used for nonadjacent vertices
u,v
but we will not need this case.
We also note that, in general, the Fogelsanger decomposition of
{\cal S}
need not be unique since the partition
\{{\cal S}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,{\cal S}_{m}^{\prime}\}
of
{\cal S}/uv
into simplicial
k
-circuits need not be unique.

Consider the simplicial 2-circuit

. The simplicial 2-cycle

on the right can be decomposed into three (trivial) simplicial 2-circuits:

;

.
Then

;
{\cal T}_{2}=\{S_{3},S_{4}\}
;

. This gives rise to a Fogelsanger decomposition for

with respect to

consisting of three simplicial 2-circuits:

where

;

.

Our next lemma gives some key properties of Fogelsanger decompositions.

Lemma 3.13
.

Suppose
{\cal S}
is a non-trivial simplicial
k
-circuit,
uv\in E({\cal S})
. Let
\{{\cal S}_{1}^{+},\dots,{\cal S}_{m}^{+}\}
be a Fogelsanger decomposition of
{\cal S}
with respect to
uv
.
Then:

(a)

{\cal S}_{i}^{+}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-circuit for all
1\leq i\leq m
;

(b)

{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
is a non-trivial simplicial
k
-circuit for all
1\leq i\leq m
and each
K\in{\cal S}_{i}^{+}\setminus{\cal S}
is a clique of
G({\cal S})
which contains
\{u,v\}
;

(c)

each
k
-simplex of
{\cal S}
that does not contain
\{u,v\}
lies in a unique
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
. Moreover
{\cal S}=\triangle_{i=1}^{m}{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
;

(d)

uv\in E({\cal S}_{i}^{+})
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}E({\cal S}_{i}^{+})=E({\cal S})
;

(e)

for all non-empty
I\subsetneq\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, there exists
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}\setminus I
and a non-facial
(k+1)
-clique
K
of
G({\cal S})
such that
K\in(\triangle_{i\in I}{\cal S}_{i}^{+})\cap{\cal S}_{j}^{+}
and
\{u,v\}\subseteq K
.

Proof.

We adopt the notation used in the definition of a Fogelsanger decomposition.

(a)
By construction,
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}/uv={\cal S}_{i}/uv={\cal S}_{i}^{\prime}
which is a simplicial
k
-circuit.

(b)
The facts that each
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit and each
K\in{\cal S}_{i}^{+}\setminus{\cal S}
is a non-facial clique of
G({\cal S})
which contains
\{u,v\}
follow from the definition of a Fogelsanger decomposition. It only remains to show each
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
is non-trivial. We have
|{\cal S}_{i}|=|{\cal S}_{i}^{\prime}|\geq 2
since
{\cal S}_{i}^{\prime}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit, and
{\cal S}_{i}
does not contain multiple copies of the same
k
-simplex since
{\cal S}_{i}\subseteq{\cal S}
. Since

{\cal S}_{i}^{+}={\cal S}_{i}\triangle{\cal S}_{i}^{*}={\cal S}_{i}\cup{\cal S}_{i}^{*}
by definition, we have
{\cal S}_{i}\subseteq{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
. Hence
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit with at least two distinct
k
-simplices so is non-trivial.

(c)
Choose
S\in{\cal S}
with
\{u,v\}\not\subseteq S
. Then
\gamma_{uv}(S)

belongs to a unique simplicial
k
-circuit
{\cal S}_{i}^{\prime}
in the partition of
{\cal S}/e
into simplicial
k
-circuits. Hence
S\in{\cal S}_{i}
and
S\not\in{\cal S}_{j}
for all
i\neq j
. Furthermore
S\not\in{\cal S}_{j}^{*}
for all
1\leq j\leq m
since each
k
-simplex of
{\cal S}_{j}^{*}
contains
\{u,v\}
. Hence
S\in{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
and
S\not\in{\cal S}_{j}^{+}
for all
i\neq j
.

For the second part of
(c)
let
{\cal R}={\cal S}\triangle(\triangle_{i=1}^{m}{\cal S}_{i}^{+})
. Each
k
-simplex in
{\cal R}
contains
\{u,v\}
by the previous paragraph. Also
{\cal R}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle without repeated
k
-simplices. If
{\cal R}\neq\emptyset
then, by Lemma
3.11
,
\partial{\cal R}\neq\emptyset
contradicting the fact that
{\cal R}
is a simplicial
k
-cycle.
Therefore
{\cal R}=\emptyset
and
{\cal S}=\triangle_{i=1}^{m}{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
.

(d)

If
m=1
then
{\cal S}={\cal S}_{1}^{+}
by part (c) and
(d)
holds trivially. Hence we may assume that
m\geq 2
.
We first show that
uv\in E({\cal S}_{i}^{+})
. Since
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
is non-trivial for all
1\leq i\leq m
by
(b)
, Lemma
3.10
implies that each
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
contains a
k
-simplex which does not contain
\{u,v\}
. We can now use the fact that
m\geq 2
and the first part of
(c)
to deduce that
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}\not\subseteq{\cal S}
. The second part of
(b)
now gives
uv\in E({\cal S}_{i}^{+})
.

The assertion that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}E({\cal S}_{i}^{+})=E({\cal S})
follows immediately from the fact that each
S\in{\cal S}
belongs to at least one
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
by
(c)
.

(e)
Let
{\cal S}_{I}=\triangle_{i\in I}{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
.
Since
{\cal S}_{j}^{+}\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq j}{\cal S}_{k}^{+}\neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq j\leq m
,

\emptyset\neq{\cal S}_{I}\neq{\cal S}
.
Also, since
{\cal S}_{I}
is a non-empty simplicial
k
-cycle and
{\cal S}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit,

{\cal S}_{I}\not\subseteq{\cal S}
. Hence we can choose
K\in{\cal S}_{I}\setminus{\cal S}
.
Then

K
is a non-facial clique of
G({\cal S})
which contains
\{u,v\}
by
(b)
. We can now use

(c)
to deduce that
K
belongs to an even number of the sets in the Fogelsanger decomposition of
{\cal S}
. On the other hand, the fact that

K\in{\cal S}_{I}
implies that
K
belongs to an odd number of the sets
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}
for
i\in I
. Hence
K
belongs to an odd number of sets
{\cal S}_{j}^{+}
for
j\not\in I
.
∎

We can now see that Fogelsanger’s Rigidity Theorem is a corollary of Lemma
3.13
.

Theorem 3.14

(Fogelsanger’s Rigidity Theorem
[
10
]
)
.

Let
{\cal S}
be a simplicial
k
-circuit with
k\geq 2
. Then
G({\cal S})
is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
|V({\cal S})|
. The theorem holds for the trivial simplicial
k
-circuit so we may suppose that
{\cal S}
is non-trivial.
Take any edge
uv\in E({\cal S})
, and consider a Fogelsanger decomposition
\{{\cal S}_{1}^{+},\dots,{\cal S}_{m}^{+}\}
with respect to
uv
.
Each
{\cal S}_{i}^{+}/uv
is a simplicial
k
-circuit by Lemma
3.13
(a)
and hence
G({\cal S}_{i}^{+}/uv)
is rigid by induction.
We have
S_{i}^{+}
is a non-trivial simplicial
k
-circuit and
uv\in E(S_{i}^{+})
by Lemma
3.13
(b)
and
(d)
. We can now apply
the first part of Lemma
3.10
to deduce that
u
and
v
have at least
k
common neighbours in
S_{i}^{+}
, and then use Lemma
2.2
and the fact that
G({\cal S}_{i}^{+}/uv)=G({\cal S}_{i}^{+})/uv
to deduce that each
G({\cal S}_{i}^{+})
is rigid.
Lemma
3.13
(e)
implies that we can reorder the parts of the Fogelsanger decomposition so that, for all
2\leq i\leq m
,

\left|V({\cal S}_{i})\cap\mbox{$\bigcup_{j=1}^{i-1}$}V({\cal S}_{j})\right|\geq k+1.

(4)

The rigidity of
G({\cal S})
now follows from the gluing property of rigidity and a simple induction argument together with the fact that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}G({\cal S}_{i}^{+})=G({\cal S})
by Lemma
3.13
(d)
.
∎

3.4
Subcomplexes with small boundary

In this short subsection we derive some structural properties of a subcomplex of a simplicial circuit which has a small boundary.
Lemma
3.7
implies that non-trivial simplicial
k
-circuits are
(k+1)
-connected. Since Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
both require
(k+2)
-connectivity, we will need a tool to analyse the case when a simplicial
k
-circuit in a Fogelsanger’s decomposition is not
(k+2)
-connected.
In such a case, our approach will be to decompose the simplicial circuit into smaller circuits. Lemmas
3.15
and
3.16
below will enable us to do this.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
{\cal S}
be a simplicial
k
-circuit for some
k\geq 1
,
{\cal S}_{1}\subsetneq{\cal S}
, and
X=V(\partial{\cal S}_{1})
with
|X|=k+1
. Then either
{\cal S}_{1}=\{X\}
, or,
X\not\in{\cal S}_{1}
and
{\cal S}_{1}\cup\{X\}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit.

Proof.

Suppose that
{\cal S}_{1}\neq\{X\}
.
Since
\partial{\cal S}_{1}
is a simplicial
(k-1)
-cycle by Lemma
3.3
and
|V(\partial{\cal S}_{1})|=|X|=(k-1)+2
,
Lemma
3.5
tells us that
\partial S_{1}
is the set of all subsets of
X
of size
k
.
This implies that
\partial S_{1}=\partial\{X\}
so
{\cal S}_{1}\triangle\{X\}
is a
simplicial
k
-cycle, which is nonempty since
{\cal S}_{1}\neq\{X\}
.
If
X\in{\cal S}_{1}
then
{\cal S}_{1}\triangle\{X\}\subsetneq{\cal S}
, contradicting the fact that
{\cal S}
is a simplicial circuit. So
X\not\in{\cal S}_{1}
and
{\cal S}_{1}\triangle\{X\}={\cal S}_{1}\cup\{X\}
is a nonempty simplicial
k
-cycle.

To see that
{\cal S}_{1}\triangle\{X\}
is a simplicial
k
-circuit,
suppose, for a contradiction, that
{\cal R}
is a nonempty simplicial
k
-cycle with
{\cal R}\subsetneq{\cal S}_{1}\cup\{X\}
. Let

|V_{1}\cap V_{2}|\geq t
. The lemma is trivially true if

is

-connected so we may assume that

has a

-vertex separator

. If

. Hence

has the

-cleavage property.

Let

be the

-cleavage graphs of

at a

-vertex separator

. Then each

-block of

-block of

for some

and

.
If

, then every

-block of

is a

-block of

and the lemma follows by applying induction to

.
Hence we may assume that

.
∎

We have seen that the graphs of simplicial

Lemma 4.4
.

.

Proof of Theorem

Suppose

5
The strong cleavage property

Our proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
use an induction based on taking a Fogelsanger decomposition.
As noted in the introduction, the fact that the graphs of the simplicial
k
-circuits in a
Fogelsanger decomposition need not be
(k+2)
-connected means that we cannot apply the induction hypothesis directly to these graphs. Instead, we apply it to their
(k+2)
-blocks. This leads us to our definition of the
strong
d
-cleavage property. The fact that the characterisations of global rigidity given by Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
are different means we have to work with slightly different definitions for
k=2
and
k\geq 3
.

Let
G
be a graph with the
d
-cleavage property.
When
d=3
, we say that
G
has the
strong
3
-cleavage property

if every
4
-block of
G
is a plane triangulation or is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
When
d\geq 4
, we say that
G
has the
strong
d
-cleavage property
if every
(d+1)
-block of
G
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Our goal in this section is to prove a ‘gluing result’ for graphs with the strong
d
-cleavage property, Corollary
5.12
below, which will enable us to combine the graphs of the simplicial circuits in a Fogelsanger decomposition in such a way that we preserve the strong
d
-cleavage property.

5.1
Edge addition lemma

To obtain our gluing result for graphs with the strong
d
-cleavage property,
we first show that the strong
d
-cleavage property is closed under edge addition. We will need the following concepts. Two vertices
u,v
in a graph
G
are
globally linked in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

if, for every generic
(G,p)
,
\|q(u)-q(v)\|=\|p(u)-p(v)\|
for every
(G,q)
that is equivalent to
(G,p)
.
It is easy to see that, if
u,v
are globally linked, then
G
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

if and only if
G+uv
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We define the
globally linked closure of
G
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(G)
, to be the graph obtained from
G
by adding an edge between all pairs of globally linked vertices of
G
. Thus
G
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
if and only if
\mathrm{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
is a complete graph.
Also, using the fact that a plane triangulation with at least 4 vertices is not globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and Theorem
5.1
below, it follows that if
G
is a 4-connected plane triangulation then
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)=G
.

Now if
G
is a graph with the
d
-cleavage property and
D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{t}
are the
(d+1)
-blocks of
G
, then

\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(D_{1}),\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(D_{2}),\ldots,\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(D_{t})
are the
(d+1)
-blocks of
\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(G)
.
(Note that if
u
and
v
are vertices in distinct components in
G-X
for some
d
-vertex separator
X
then
u
and
v
are not globally linked in
G
.)
Hence, if
G
has the strong
d
-cleavage property, then for all
1\leq i\leq t
, either
\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(D_{i})
is a complete graph or
d=3
and
\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(D_{i})
is a plane triangulation.
We will use this observation to show that the strong
d
-cleavage property is closed under edge addition.
The case when
d=3
,
G
is a plane triangulation and
G+e
is 4-connected follows from the following global rigidity theorem for braced triangulations due to Jordán and Tanigawa
[
17
]
. We will use this result as a base case in our inductive proof when
d=3
.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a plane triangulation and
B
be a set of edges of
K(V)
such that
B\not\subseteq E
and
G+B
is 4-connected. Then
G+B
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

We also need the following technical lemma when
d=3
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
be a graph with the strong
3
-cleavage property.
Suppose that the 4-block tree of
G
is a path.
Then
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
contains a spanning plane triangulation
\overline{G}
whose 4-block tree is also a path.
Moreover, if
D
is a 4-block of
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
and
|V(D)|\neq 5
, then
\overline{D}=D\cap\overline{G}
is a
4-block of
\overline{G}
.

Proof.

Since the 4-block tree of
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
is a path, each 4-block of
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
contains at most two 3-vertex separators of
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
.
For each
4
-block
D
of
G
we will define
a spanning plane triangulation
\overline{D}\subseteq D
with the property that each of the
3
-vertex separators of
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
contained in
D
is a face of
\overline{D}
, and then put

\overline{G}=\bigcup_{D\text{ a 4-block of }G}\overline{D}.

If
D
is a planar 4-block of
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
, let
\overline{D}=D
.
If
D
is a non-planar 4-block of
\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(G)
with
|V(D)|\geq 6
then
D
is a complete graph and we can choose a spanning subgraph

\overline{D}
of
D
that is a 4-connected plane triangulation such that each of the 3-vertex-separators of

\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
contained in
V(D)
induces a face of
\overline{D}
.

It remains to consider the case where
D
is non-planar and
|V(D)|=5
. Then
D\cong K_{5}
and we can choose an edge
xy
in
E(D)
as follows. If
D
contains a unique 3-vertex-separator,
X
, of
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
then choose
x\in X
and
y\not\in X
. If
X,Y
are distinct 3-vertex-separators of

\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
contained in
D
then choose
x\in X\setminus Y
and
y\in Y\setminus X
.
Set
\overline{D}=D-xy
and observe that
\overline{D}
is a 3-connected plane triangulation such that each separator of
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
contained in
D
induces a face of
\overline{D}
.
Also observe that there is a new 3-vertex separator,
Z=V(D)\setminus\{x,y\}
, of
\overline{G}
, which is not a separator of
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(G)
and that
x
and
y
lie in distinct
3
-cleavage graphs of
\mathrm{cl}_{3}^{*}(\overline{G})
at
Z
.
It follows that the 4-block tree of
\overline{G}
is a path.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with the strong
d
-cleavage property
for some
d\geq 3
and
e=uv
be an edge of
K(V)
with
e\notin E(G)
.
Then
G+e
has the strong
d
-cleavage property.
In particular, if
G+e
is
(d+1)
-connected,
then
G+e
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Proof.

Since the
d
-cleavage property is preserved by edge addition,
G+e
has the
d
-cleavage property.
We proceed by induction on the number of
(d+1)
-blocks in
G
.
Let
P=D_{0}X_{1}D_{1}\ldots X_{s}D_{s}
be a shortest path in the
(d+1)
-block tree
T
of
G
from a
(d+1)
-block
D_{0}
which contains
u
to a
(d+1)
-block
D_{k}
which contains
v
.

Suppose
s=0
. Then
u
and
v
both belong to
D_{0}
.
If
D_{0}
is globally rigid then
D_{0}+e
is globally rigid.
On the other hand, if
D_{0}
is a plane triangulation then
d=3
and
we can use Theorem
5.1
and the hypothesis that
e\notin E(G)
, to again deduce that

D_{0}+e
is globally rigid. This in turn implies that
G+e
has the strong
d
-cleavage property
since its
(d+1)
-block tree can be obtained from that of
G
by replacing
D_{0}
with
D_{0}+e
.

Thus we may assume
s\geq 1
and hence
e
is not an edge of
\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(G)
. Replacing
G
by
\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(G)
and using the fact that
\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(G)\subseteq\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(G+e)
, we may assume that each
(d+1)
-block of
G
is either a complete graph or a plane triangulation (and that the second alternative is only possible when
d=3
).

Let
G^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{s}D_{i}
. Then

G^{\prime}+e
is a
(d+1)
-block of
G+e
and all other
(d+1)
-blocks of
G+e
are
(d+1)
-blocks of
G
. Hence it will suffice to show that
G^{\prime}+e
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
If
d=3
then this follows from
Lemma
5.2
and Theorem
5.1
. Hence we may assume that
d\geq 4
.
Let
p
be a generic realisation of
G^{\prime}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Since
D_{i}
is complete for
0\leq i\leq s
and
|X_{i}|=d
for
1\leq i\leq s
,
the set of equivalent realisations of
(G^{\prime},p)
has exactly
2^{s}
congruence classes,
each of which can be obtained from

by choosing

and then recursively reflecting the vertices of
\bigcup_{j\geq i}V(D_{j})

in the hyperplanes containing the vertices of

for each

.
Since
u,v\notin\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}X_{j}
, the composition of these reflections does not preserve the length of the edge
e
(unless
I=\emptyset
), and hence

(G^{\prime}+e,p)
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(see
[
11
, Lemma 3.10]
).
Thus

has the strong
d
-cleavage property.
∎

5.2
Gluing results for graphs with the strong
d
-cleavage property

We first derive a result for the special case of plane triangulations.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose
G_{1},G_{2}
are distinct plane triangulations and both
G_{2}
and
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
are
4
-connected. Then
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

Proof.

Let
|X|=V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})
.
The statement follows from Theorem
5.1
if
X=V(G_{1})
.
So we may suppose that
4\leq|X|<|V(G_{1})|
.
If
V(G_{2})=X
then, using the fact that no 4-connected plane triangulation can properly contain a plane triangulation with at least 4 vertices, it follows that
G_{1}\cup G_{2}\neq G_{1}
and then, by Theorem
5.1
, that
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
is globally rigid. Therefore we may assume that
|X|<|V(G_{2})|
.

We prove the global rigidity of
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
by applying Theorem
2.3
.
For this,
we need to show that

\begin{split}&\text{$G_{2}$ has an $x_{1}x_{2}$-path which is internally disjoint from $X$}\\
&\text{for two vertices $x_{1},x_{2}\in X$ with $x_{1}x_{2}\notin E(G_{2})$.}\end{split}

(7)

To see this, let
G^{\prime}
be a connected component of
G_{2}-X

and
Y
be the set of vertices in
X
which are adjacent to
G^{\prime}
.
The hypothesis that
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
is
4
-connected implies that
|Y|\geq 4
.
Since
G_{2}
is a
4
-connected plane triangulation,

G_{2}
has no
K_{4}
-subgraph, and hence
G_{2}[Y]
is not complete. Thus we may choose two non-adjacent vertices
x_{1},x_{2}\in Y
. Then
G_{2}
has an
x_{1}x_{2}
-path with all its internal vertices disjoint from
X
.

By (
7
),

(G_{2},X)
has a rooted
(H,X)
-minor where
H
is the graph with
V(H)=X
and
E(H)=\{x_{1}x_{2}\}
.
In addition, the 4-connectivity of
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
implies that
G_{1}\cup K(X)
is 4-connected and we can now use the facts that
|X|\geq 4
and that no 4-connected plane triangulation can properly contain a plane triangulation with at least 4 vertices to deduce that
G_{1}\cup K(X)\neq G_{1}
. Then
G_{1}\cup K(X)
and
G_{2}\cup H
are both globally rigid by Theorem
5.1
. Hence
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
is globally rigid by Theorem
2.3
.
∎

We can now prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be distinct graphs with the strong
d
-cleavage property
for some
d\geq 3
.
Suppose that

K_{d}\subseteq G_{1}\cap G_{2}
and
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
is
(d+1)
-connected.
Then
G
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that the theorem is false and that
(G_{1},G_{2})
is a counterexample chosen so that the ordered triple defined by the following three parameters is as small as possible in the lexicographic order.

(A1) The total number of
(d+1)
-blocks in
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
.

(A2) The total number of planar 4-blocks in
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
when
d=3
. (This parameter is set to zero when
d\geq 4
.)

(A3)
|V(G_{1})\triangle V(G_{2})|
.

Since
\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(G_{1})\cup\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(G_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{cl}_{d}^{*}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})
, we may assume that each
(d+1)
-block of
G_{i}
is a complete graph or a planar triangulation for both
i=1,2
, and that the second alternative can only occur when
d=3
.
We refer to each vertex of
V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})
as a
shared vertex
,
and each copy of
K_{d}
in
G_{1}\cap G_{2}
as a
shared
K_{d}
.
A
(d+1)
-block
D
in
G_{1}
(respectively
G_{2}
) is said to be a
linking block
if

D
contains a shared
K_{d}
and
|V(D)\cap V(G_{2})|\geq d+1
(respectively
|V(D)\cap V(G_{1})|\geq d+1
).
A
linking block pair of
G
is a pair
(D_{1},D_{2})
where
D_{i}
is a linking block in
G_{i}
for both
i=1,2
,

|V(D_{1})\cap V(D_{2})|\geq d+1
and
D_{1}\cap D_{2}
contains a shared
K_{d}
. (Note that the third condition follows from the second condition when
d\geq 4
since each
D_{i}
is complete in that case.)

We first investigate properties of linking block pairs.

Claim 5.6
.

Suppose that
G
has a linking block pair
(D_{1},D_{2})
.
Then
V(D_{2})\cap V(G_{1})\subseteq V(D_{1})
and
V(D_{1})\cap V(G_{2})\subseteq V(D_{2})
.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
V(D_{2})\cap V(G_{1})\not\subseteq V(D_{1})
.
Choose a shortest path
P=D_{1,0}X_{1,1}D_{1,1}\dots X_{1,k_{1}}D_{1,k_{1}}
in the
(d+1)
-block tree of
G_{1}
from
D_{1,0}:=D_{1}
to a
(d+1)
-block
D_{1,k_{1}}
of
G_{1}
that contains a vertex
x
in
(V(D_{2})\cap V(G_{1}))\setminus V(D_{1})

6.1

The overall strategy in our proof of Theorems

In this section, we prove Theorem

.
A key ingredient in our proof is the concept of ‘coincident rigidity’.

In light of Theorem

.

8
Applications and conjectures

8.1
Rigidity and global rigidity of
M
-connected simplicial complexes

Let
{\cal S}
be a simplicial
k
-complex.
Recall that the
k
-simplicial matroid
{\cal M}({\cal S})
is a linear matroid on
{\cal S}
represented by the
k
-th boundary operator
\partial_{k}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
of the simplicial chain complex of
{\cal S}
.
We say that
{\cal S}
is
M
-connected
if
{\cal M}({\cal S})
is a connected matroid i.e.
{\cal M}({\cal S})
has a circuit that contains
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
for any
S_{1},S_{2}\in{\cal S}
. We can use the fact that simplicial
k
-circuits have the (strong)
(k+1)
-cleavage property to extend Theorems
3.14
,
1.3
and
1.4
to
M
-connected simplicial complexes.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
{\cal S}
be an
M
-connected simplicial
k
-complex
and
k\geq 2
. Then
G({\cal S})
is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
.
In addition, if
B\subseteq E(K(V({\cal S})))
then
G({\cal S})+B
is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
if and only if it is
(k+2)
-connected and is not a plane triangulation when
k=2
.

Proof.

Rigidity follows immediately from Theorem
3.14
since it implies that every pair of vertices of
G({\cal S})
is contained in a rigid subgraph. The necessity of the conditions for global rigidity follows immediately from Theorem
1.1
. To see sufficiency,
choose a simplex
S_{0}\in{\cal S}
.
Since
{\cal S}
is
M
-connected,
for each
S\in{\cal S}\setminus\{S_{0}\}
,
{\cal M}({\cal S})
has a circuit
{\cal C}_{S}
(or equivalently, a
k
-simplicial circuit) that contains
S_{0}
and
S
.
By Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
,
G({\cal C}_{S})
has the strong
(k+1)
-cleavage property.
Note that, for any
S_{1},S_{2}\in{\cal S}\setminus\{S_{0}\}
,
both
{\cal C}_{S_{1}}
and
{\cal C}_{S_{2}}
contain
S_{0}
,
so
G({\cal C}_{S_{1}})
and
G({\cal C}_{S_{2}})
share at least
k+1
vertices.
Hence, by Corollary
5.12
,

\bigcup_{S\in{\cal S}\setminus\{S_{0}\}}G({\cal C}_{S})
has the strong
(k+1)
-cleavage property.
Moreover,
\bigcup_{S\in{\cal S}\setminus\{S_{0}\}}G({\cal C}_{S})
is a spanning subgraph of
G({\cal S})
.
So
G({\cal S})+B
has the strong
(k+1)
-cleavage property by Lemma
5.3
.
This and the
(k+2)
-connectivity of
G({\cal S})+B
imply sufficiency.
∎

Theorem
8.1
yields an algorithm that can verify if a graph
G
is rigid or globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{k+1}
.

1.

Compute the set
{\cal A}_{G}
of all
(k+1)
-cliques in
G
.

2.

If
V({\cal A}_{G})\neq V(G)
then stop.

3.

If
V({\cal A}_{G})=V(G)
compute the
(k-1)
-face-to-
k
-face incidence matrix of
{\cal A}_{G}
(with rows indexed by
k
-faces). Let
\cal{M}
be the row matroid of this matrix over
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

4.

Compute the connected components of the matroid
\cal{M}
. If none of these components is spanning (i.e. has vertex set equal to
V(G)
) then stop. If some component of
\cal{M}
is spanning then

(a)

G
has the strong cleavage property.

(b)

G
is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
.

(c)

G
is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
if and only if it is
(k+2)
-connected and, in the case
k=2
, non-planar.

In general the algorithm above cannot be used to decide if a given graph is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
. For example
K_{6,6}
is known to be globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, but
{\cal A}_{K_{6,6}}
is empty (with
k=2
).

However, for any simplicial
k
-complex
{\cal S}
,

G=G({\cal S})
implies
{\cal S}\subseteq{\cal A}_{G}
.
This fact and Theorem
8.1
imply that the above
algorithm (combined with tests for
(d+1)
-connectivity and planarity) correctly verifies the rigidity and the global rigidity of
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
if
G
contains a spanning subgraph that is the graph of an
M
-connected simplicial
k
-complex.
In particular, it correctly verifies if a simplicial
k
-circuit graph
G
is globally rigid in polynomial-time without necessarily computing a simplicial
k
-circuit
{\cal S}
with
G=G({\cal S})
. Developing an efficient recognition algorithm for the class of simplicial
k
-circuit graphs is an interesting, but perhaps difficult, open problem.

We also note that Theorem
8.1
and the algorithm above illustrate an advantage of the class of simplicial
k
-circuits over the class of simplicial
k
-manifolds. It does not seem easy to find an analogous certifying algorithm that relies only on the global rigidity characterisation for simplicial
k
-manifolds.

8.2
Rigidity of abstract simplicial complexes

Theorem
8.1

gives a sufficient condition for the graph of an abstract simplicial complex to be rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
The body and hinge theorems of Tay
[
26
]
and Katoh and Tanigawa
[
19
]
give other such conditions.
It would be of interest to find yet more sufficient conditions for the graph of a simplicial complex to be rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. For example:

Conjecture 8.2
.

The graph of an abstract simplicial
(d+1)
-complex is rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
whenever it is
(2d-1)
-connected.

A suitably large ring of copies of
K_{2d-2}
in which consecutive pairs have a
K_{d-1}
in common and non-consecutive pairs are disjoint shows that the connectivity bound of
2d-1
in this conjecture would be best possible. The conjecture holds when
d=1
since every connected graph is rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
. We can use the proof technique of
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
to verify the conjecture when
d=2
, and
[
3
, Theorem 7.1]
tells us that the hypotheses of the conjecture imply
C^{1}_{2}
-cofactor rigidity (which is conjectured to be the same as rigidity in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
).

8.3
A lower bound conjecture for simplicial circuits

As mentioned in the introduction, Fogelsanger’s Rigidity Theorem implies that the bound for the number of edges (1-faces) in Barnette’s Lower Bound Theorem extends from connected triangulated
(d-1)
-manifolds to simplicial
(d-1)
-circuits.
We conjecture that this extension applies to faces of other dimensions as well.
More precisely let
f_{j}({\cal S})
be the number of
j
-dimensional faces of a simplicial complex
{\cal S}
and, for
k\geq 1
, let

\varphi_{j}(n,k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n\binom{k+1}{j}-j\binom{k+2}{j+1}&\text{ for }1\leq j\leq k-1\\
kn-(k+2)(k-1)&\text{ for }j=k\end{array}\right.

Conjecture 8.3
.

Suppose that
{\cal S}
is a non-trivial simplicial
k
-circuit with
n
vertices. Then, for
1\leq j\leq k
,

f_{j}({\cal S})\geq\varphi_{j}(n,k).

(25)

Moreover, equality holds in (
25
) for any
1\leq j\leq k
if and only
if either
k=1
, or
k=2
and
{\cal S}
is a triangulated 2-sphere, or
k\geq 3
and
{\cal S}
is a stacked
k
-sphere.

Note that the standard proof technique of reducing the Lower Bound Theorem to the case
j=1
does not translate easily to this setting since the class of simplicial circuits is not link closed. In general the link of a vertex in a simplicial
k
-circuit will be a simplicial
(k-1)
-cycle but not necessarily a simplicial
(k-1)
-circuit.

8.4
Polytopes and stresses

For a framework
(G,p)
, an element of the left kernel of its rigidity matrix
R(G,p)
is called a
stress
of
(G,p)
.
Let
S(G,p)
be the space of all stresses of
(G,p)
.
In the context of the reconstruction problem for polytopes, Kalai, see
[
23
, Conjecture 5.7]
, conjectured that a simplicial

d
-polytope
P
is uniquely determined up to affine equivalence by its underlying graph and its space of stresses of its underlying framework whenever
d\geq 4
and
G(P)
is
(d+1)
-connected.
Since the boundary complex of a simplicial

d
-polytope is a triangulated
(d-1)
-manifold, our characterisation of global rigidity for graphs of simplicial circuits solves this conjecture whenever
p
is generic. We will need the following results on stresses to see this.

For a stress
\omega
of a framework
(G,p)
, the corresponding
stress matrix

\Omega
is the Laplacian matrix of
G
weighted by
\omega
.
Gortler, Healy, and Thurston
[
13
]
proved that a generic
d
-dimensional framework
(G,p)
with
n
vertices is globally rigid if and only if it has a
full rank stress
i.e., there is a stress
\omega\in S(G,p)
such that
\rank\Omega=n-(d+1)
.
Also, a well-known observation by Connelly (see, e.g.,
[
8
,
13
]
) says that, if
\omega
is a full rank stress of
(G,p)
and
(G,q)
is another realisation of
G
which has
w
as a stress, then
q
is an affine image of
p
.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
d\geq 3
, and
(G,p)
be a generic framework in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
G
is the graph of a simplicial
(d-1)
-circuit,
G
is
(d+1)
-connected and
G
is non-planar when
d=3
.
Suppose that
(G,q)
is a realisation of
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
S(G,p)=S(G,q)
.
Then
q
is an affine image of
p
.

Proof.

Since
G
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
, and
p
is generic, the above mentioned result of Gortler, Healy, and Thurston implies that
(G,p)
has a full rank stress
\omega
. We can now use Connelly’s observation and the hypothesis that

S(G,p)=S(G,q)
to deduce that
q
is an affine image of
p
.
∎

Specializing Theorem
8.4
to simplicial
d
-polytopes, we obtain Theorem
1.7
. We note that, after this paper was first submitted, Murai, Novik and Zheng proved Kalai’s conjecture for all convex simplicial
d
-polytopes where

[

]
. The case of non-generic convex

The Segal-Bargmann Transform on Classical Matrix Lie Groups Journal: Journal of Functional Analysis
Alice Z. Chan

Abstract
We study the complex-time Segal-Bargmann transform 𝐁 s , τ K N \mathbf{B}_{s,\tau}^{K_{N}} on a compact type Lie group K N K_{N} , where K N K_{N} is one of the following classical matrix Lie groups: the special orthogonal group SO ⁡ ( N , ℝ ) \SO(N,\mathbb{R}) , the special unitary group SU ⁡ ( N ) \SU(N) , or the compact symplectic group Sp ⁡ ( N ) \Sp(N) . Our work complements and extends the results of Driver, Hall, and Kemp on the Segal-Bargman transform for the unitary group U ⁡ ( N ) \U(N) . We provide an effective method of computing the action of the Segal-Bargmann transform on trace polynomials , which comprise a subspace of smooth functions on K N K_{N} extending the polynomial functional calculus. Using these results, we show that as N → ∞ N\to\infty , the finite-dimensional transform 𝐁 s , τ K N \mathbf{B}_{s,\tau}^{K_{N}} has a meaningful limit 𝒢 s , τ \mathscr{G}_{s,\tau} which can be identified as an operator on the space of complex Laurent polynomials. Keywords: Segal-Bargmann transform; Heat kernel analysis on Lie groups

Introduction

1.1
The classical Segal-Bargmann transform

In this paper, we consider a complex-time generalization of the classical Segal-Bargmann transform on the matrix Lie groups
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\SU(N)
, and
\Sp(N)
, and analyze its limit as
N\to\infty
. We begin with a brief discussion of the classical Segal-Bargmann transform and its generalizations to Lie groups of compact type. For
t>0
, let
\rho_{t}
denote the heat kernel with variance
t
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\rho_{t}(x)=(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp(-|x|^{2}/2t).

This has an entire analytic continuation to
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, given by

(\rho_{t})_{\mathbb{C}}(z)=(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{z\cdot z}{2t}\right).

If
f\in L^{1}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is of sufficiently slow growth, we can define

(B_{t}f)(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\rho_{t})_{\mathbb{C}}(z-y)f(y)\,dy.

(1.1)

The map
f\mapsto B_{t}f
is called the classical
Segal-Bargmann transform
, due to the work of the eponymous authors in
[
2
,
1
,
17
,
15
,
16
]
. If we let
\mu_{t}
denote the heat kernel on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, now with variance
\frac{t}{2}
:

\mu_{t}(z)=(\pi t)^{-d}\exp(-|z|^{2}/t),

(1.2)

then the main result is that the map

B_{t}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\rho_{t})\to\mathcal{H}L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d},\mu_{t})

is a unitary isomorphism, where
\mathcal{H}L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d},\mu_{t})
is the space of square-integrable holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

1.2
Main definitions and theorems

In
[
10
]
, Hall showed that the Segal-Bargmann transform can be extended to compact Lie groups, and in
[
6
]
, the transform was further extended by Driver to Lie groups of compact type. Later, the behavior of the transform on the unitary group
\U(N)
as
N\to\infty
was analyzed independently by Cébron in
[
5
]
and Driver, Hall, and Kemp in
[
8
]
. The authors of the latter paper subsequently introduced a complex-time generalization of the Segal-Bargmann transform for all compact type Lie groups in
[
9
]
. We use this version of the transform (see Definition
1.1
) in our analysis of the special orthogonal group
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
, the special unitary group
\SU(N)
, and the compact symplectic group
\Sp(N)
. We now outline the main results of this paper, deferring a fuller discussion of the requisite background and definitions to Section
2
.

Let
K
denote a Lie group of compact type with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
. Then
K
possesses a left-invariant metric whose associated Laplacian
\Delta_{K}
is bi-invariant (cf. (
2.1
)). For each
s>0
, there is an associated heat kernel
\rho_{s}^{K}\in C^{\infty}(K,(0,\infty))
satisfying

\left(e^{\frac{s}{2}\Delta_{K}}f\right)(x)=\int_{K}f(xk)\rho_{s}^{K}(k)\ dk=\int_{K}f(k)\rho_{s}^{K}(xk^{-1})\ dk\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{for all $f\in L^{2}(K)$}.

(1.3)

Let
K_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the complexification of
K
and
\mathbb{C}_{+}=\{\tau=t+iu\,:\,t>0,u\in\mathbb{R}\}
denote the right half-plane. As shown in
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
, the heat kernel
(\rho_{s}^{K})_{s>0}
possesses a unique entire analytic continuation

\rho_{\mathbb{C}}^{K}:\mathbb{C}_{+}\times K_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{C}

such that
\rho_{\mathbb{C}}^{K}(s,x)=\rho_{s}(x)
for all
s>0
and
x\in K\subseteq K_{\mathbb{C}}
. Thus we can proceed as in the classical case and define the Segal-Bargmann transform for compact type Lie groups using a group convolution formula similar to (
1.1
) (see
[
6
]
for details).

Definition 1.1

(Complex-time Segal-Bargmann transform)
.

Let
s>0
and
\tau=t+i\theta\in\mathbb{D}(s,s)
, and let
\rho_{s}^{K}
and
\rho_{\mathbb{C}}^{K}
be as above. For
z\in K_{\mathbb{C}}
and
f\in L^{2}(K,\rho_{s}^{K})
, define

(B_{s,\tau}^{K}f)(z):=\int_{K}\rho_{\mathbb{C}}^{K}(\tau,zk^{-1})f(k)\,dk\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{for }z\in K_{\mathbb{C}}.

(1.4)

The complex-time Segal-Bargmann transform
B_{s,\tau}^{K}
is an isometric isomorphism between certain
L^{2}
and holomorphic
L^{2}
spaces on
K
and
K_{\mathbb{C}}
:

Theorem 1.2

(Driver, Hall, and Kemp,
[
9
, Theorem 1.6]
)
.

Let
K
be a connected, compact-type Lie group, and set
s>0
and
\tau\in\mathbb{D}(s,s)
. Let
\mu_{s,\tau}^{K_{\mathbb{C}}}
denote the (time-rescaled) heat kernel on
K_{\mathbb{C}}
defined by (
2.4
). Then
B_{s,\tau}^{K}f
is holomorphic on
K_{\mathbb{C}}
for each
f\in L^{2}(K,\rho_{s}^{K})
. Moreover,

B_{s,\tau}^{K}:L^{2}(K,\rho_{s}^{K})\to\mathcal{H}L^{2}(K_{\mathbb{C}},\mu_{s,\tau}^{K_{\mathbb{C}}})

(1.5)

is a unitary isomorphism.

If we set
K=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s=\tau>0
, we recover the main result for the classical Segal-Bargmann transform from Section
1.1
.

Every compact Lie group
K
has a faithful representation. Hence, viewing
K
as a matrix group, we can extend
B_{s,\tau}^{K}
to matrix-valued functions. Let
\M_{N}(\mathbb{R})
denote the algebra of
N\times N
real matrices with unit
I_{N}
, and let
\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
denote the algebra of
N\times N
complex matrices. Let
\GL_{N}(\mathbb{C})
denote the group of all invertible matrices in
\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Define a scaled Hilbert-Schmidt norm on
\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
by

\|A\|^{2}_{\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})}:=\frac{1}{N}\Tr(AA^{*}),\hskip 14.22636ptA\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}),

(1.6)

where
\Tr
denotes the usual trace.

Definition 1.3

(Boosted Segal-Bargmann transform)
.

Let
K\subseteq\GL_{N}(\mathbb{C})
be a compact matrix Lie group with complexification
K_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
F
be an
\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
valued function on
K
or
K_{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
\|F\|_{\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})}
denote the scalar-valued function
A\mapsto\|F(A)\|_{\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})}
. Fix
s>0
and
\tau\in\mathbb{D}(s,s)
, and let

\displaystyle L^{2}(K,\rho_{s}^{K};\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}))

\displaystyle=\{F:K\to\M_{N}(\mathbb{C});\,\|F\|_{\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})}\in L^{2}(K,\rho_{s}^{K})\},\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}

\displaystyle L^{2}(K_{\mathbb{C}},\mu_{s,\tau}^{K_{\mathbb{C}}};\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}))

\displaystyle=\{F:K_{\mathbb{C}}\to\M_{N}(\mathbb{C});\,\|F\|_{\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})}\in L^{2}(K_{\mathbb{C}},\mu_{s,\tau}^{K_{\mathbb{C}}})\}.

The norms defined by

\displaystyle\|F\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K,\rho_{s}^{K};\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}))}

\displaystyle:=\int_{K}\|F(A)\|^{2}_{\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})}\,\rho_{s}^{K}(A)\,dA

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|F\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K_{\mathbb{C}},\mu_{s,\tau}^{K_{\mathbb{C}}};\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}))}

\displaystyle:=\int_{K_{\mathbb{C}}}\|F(A)\|^{2}_{\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})}\,\mu_{s,\tau}^{K_{\mathbb{C}}}(A)\,dA

(1.8)

make these spaces into Hilbert spaces. Let
\mathcal{H}L^{2}(K_{\mathbb{C}},\mu_{s,\tau}^{K_{\mathbb{C}}};\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}))\subseteq L^{2}(K_{\mathbb{C}},\mu_{s,\tau}^{K_{\mathbb{C}}};\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}))
denote the subspace of matrix-valued holomorphic functions.

The
boosted Segal-Bargmann transform

\mathbf{B}_{s,\tau}^{K}:L^{2}(K,\rho_{s}^{K};\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}))\to\mathcal{H}L^{2}(K_{\mathbb{C}},\mu_{s,\tau}^{K_{\mathbb{C}}};\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}))

is the unitary isomorphism determined by applying
B_{s,\tau}^{N}
componentwise; i.e.,

\mathbf{B}_{s,\tau}^{K}(f\cdot A)=B_{s,\tau}^{K}f\cdot A\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{for }f\in L^{2}(K,\rho_{s}^{K})\mbox{ and }A\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}).

In this paper, we consider the case when
K=K_{N}
is one of
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\SU(N)
, or
\Sp(N)
. We provide an effective method of computing
\mathbf{B}_{s,\tau}^{K_{N}}
on the space of polynomials, and show that in this setting, the transform has a meaningful limit as
N\to\infty
. However, the boosted Segal-Bargmann transform typically does not preserve the space of polynomials on
K
. For example, consider the polynomial
P_{N}(A)=A^{2}
on
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
. Let
\Tr
denote the usual trace and set
\tr=\frac{1}{N}\Tr
. From Example
4.11
, we see that

(\mathbf{B}_{s,t}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}P_{N})(A)=\frac{1}{N}(1-e^{-t})I_{N}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-t}(1+e^{\frac{2t}{N}})A^{2}-\frac{N}{2}e^{-t}(-1+e^{\frac{2t}{N}})A\tr A,

(1.9)

This example highlights that in general,
\mathbf{B}_{s,\tau}^{K}
maps the space of polynomial functions on
K
into the larger space of
trace polynomials
, first introduced in
[
8
, Definition 1.7]
, which we now briefly describe (see Section
3.2
for the full details).

Let
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
denote the algebra of polynomials in the commuting variables
u
,
u^{-1}
, and
\mathbf{v}=\{v_{\pm 1},v_{\pm 2},\ldots\}
. For each
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
, there is an associated trace polynomial
P_{N}^{(1)}
on
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
. For now, it is enough to think of
P_{N}^{(1)}(A)
as a certain “polynomial” function of
A
and
\tr(A)
. If
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
is a function of the variable
u
alone, then
P_{N}^{(1)}
is simply the corresponding polynomial on
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
. For example, the trace polynomial on
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
associated with
P(u;\mathbf{v})=u^{5}
is
P_{N}^{(1)}(A)=A^{5}
. On the other hand, (
1.9
) is the trace polynomial on
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
associated with

f(u;\mathbf{v})=\frac{1}{N}(1-e^{-t})+\frac{1}{2}e^{-t}(1+e^{\frac{2t}{N}})u^{2}-\frac{N}{2}e^{-t}(-1+e^{\frac{2t}{N}})uv_{1}\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}].

In addition, for
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
, there are analogous trace polynomials
P_{N}^{(2)}
and
P_{N}^{(4)}
on
\SU(N)
, and
\Sp(N)
, resp. (see Definition
3.5
for precise definitions).

To simplify the statement of our results, we introduce the following notation: for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let

K_{N}^{(1)}=\SO(N,\mathbb{R}),\hskip 14.22636ptK_{N}^{(2)}=\SU(N),\hskip 14.22636ptK_{N}^{(2^{\prime})}=\U(N),\hskip 14.22636ptK_{N}^{(4)}=\Sp(N),

(1.10)

and set
K_{N,\mathbb{C}}^{(\beta)}=(K_{N}^{(\beta)})_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\Delta_{N}^{(\beta)}=\Delta_{K_{N}^{(\beta)}}
for
\beta=1,2,2^{\prime},4
. In addition, we write
\rho_{s}^{N,(\beta)}=\rho_{s}^{K_{N}^{(\beta)}}
and
\mu_{s,\tau}^{N,(\beta)}=\mu_{s,\tau}^{K_{N,\mathbb{C}}^{(\beta)}}
for the heat kernels on
K_{N}^{(\beta)}
and
K_{N,\mathbb{C}}^{(\beta)}
, respectively.

Our first theorem is an intertwining result showing that
\Delta_{N}^{(\beta)}P_{N}^{(\beta)}
can be computed by applying a certain pseudodifferential operator on
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
to
P
and then considering the corresponding trace polynomial.

Theorem 1.4

(Intertwining formulas for
\Delta_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}
,
\Delta_{\SU(N)}
, and
\Delta_{\Sp(N)}
)
.

For
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there are pseudodifferential operators
\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(\beta)}
acting on
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
(see Definition
3.7
) such that, for each
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
,

\Delta_{N}^{(\beta)}P_{N}^{(\beta)}=[\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(\beta)}P]_{N}.

(1.11)

Moreover, for all
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
and
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
,

e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\Delta_{N}^{(\beta)}}P_{N}^{(\beta)}=[e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(\beta)}}P]_{N}

(1.12)

For each such
\beta
,
\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(\beta)}
takes the form

\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(\beta)}=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(\beta)}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{(\beta)},

(1.13)

where
\mathcal{L}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(\beta)}
are first order pseudodifferential operators and
\mathcal{L}_{2}^{(\beta)}
is a second order differential operator, all independent of
N
.

The exponential
e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(\beta)}}
is well defined on
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
(see Corollary
3.11
). The proof of Theorem
1.4
for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
and
\Sp(N)
can be found on p.
3.2
and p.
5.3
, resp. We do not provide a full proof of the
\SU(N)
version of the theorem, as it is very similar to the
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
and
\Sp(N)
cases; the proof is discussed on p.
6
.

Our next result shows that we can use
e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(\beta)}}
to explicitly compute the Segal-Bargmann transform on trace polynomials.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
s>0
and
\tau\in\mathbb{D}(s,s)
. For
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
, the Segal-Bargmann transform on the trace polynomial
P_{N}^{(\beta)}
can be computed as

\mathbf{B}_{s,\tau}^{K_{N}^{(\beta)}}P_{N}^{(\beta)}=[e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(\beta)}}P]_{N}.

(1.14)

The proof of Theorem
1.5
for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
and
\Sp(N)
can be found on p.
3.2
and p.
5.3
, resp. The proof of this theorem for the
\SU(N)
case is similar, and is discussed on p.
6
.

Our main result concerns the limit of
\mathbf{B}_{s,\tau}^{K_{N}^{(\beta)}}
on polynomials as
N\to\infty
. While the range of the boosted Segal-Bargmann transform is the space of trace polynomials, as
N\to\infty
the image concentrates back onto the smaller subspace of Laurent polynomials.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
s>0
,
\tau\in\mathbb{D}(s,s)
, and
f\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]
. Then there exist unique
g_{s,\tau},h_{s,\tau}\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{B}_{s,\tau}^{K_{N}^{(\beta)}}f_{N}-[g_{s,\tau}]_{N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K_{N,\mathbb{C}}^{(\beta)},\mu_{s,\tau}^{N,(\beta)};\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}))}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right),

(1.15)

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{B}_{s,\tau}^{K_{N}^{(\beta)}})^{-1}f_{N}-[h_{s,\tau}]_{N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K_{N}^{(\beta)},\rho_{s}^{N,(\beta)};\M_{N}(\mathbb{C}))}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

(1.16)

for
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
.

.

The map

Remark 1.8
.

The fact that the Segal-Bargmann transform on matrices in
\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
has a meaningful limit
\mathscr{G}^{t}
as
N\to\infty
is due to Biane
[
4
, Proposition 13]
. For the special case when
s=t>0
, the free Segal-Bargmann transform
\mathscr{G}_{t,t}
of this paper is equivalent to
\mathscr{G}^{t}
, which Biane called the
free Hall transform
. A two-parameter generalization of this transform, called the
free unitary Segal-Bargmann transform
,
\mathscr{G}_{s,t}
, where
s>t/2>0
, was introduced in
[
8
]
. This version of the free Segal-Bargmann transform was also investigated by Ho in
[
11
]
; in particular, he gave an integral kernel for the large-
N
limit of the Segal-Bargmann transform over
\U(N)
. In this paper, we study a slight generalization of
\mathscr{G}_{s,t}
by allowing the time parameter
\tau
to be complex, incorporating results from
[
9
]
on the complex-time Segal-Bargmann transform. When
\tau=t>0
, the free Segal-Bargmann transform
\mathscr{G}_{s,\tau}
of Definition
1.7
is precisely the same as the operator
\mathscr{G}_{s,t}
of
[
8
]
.

Remark 1.9
.

Many of the results in this paper on the Segal-Bargmann transform for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\SU(N)
, and
\Sp(N)
correspond to work done by Driver, Hall, and Kemp on the Segal-Bargmann transform on
\U(N)
. The
\U(N)
analogue of our intertwining formulas, Theorem
1.4
, is contained in
[
8
, Theorem 1.18]
, where it is shown that
\Delta_{\U(N)}
has the intertwining formula

\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(2^{\prime})}=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(2^{\prime})}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{(2^{\prime})}

for certain pseudodifferential operators
\mathcal{L}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(2^{\prime})}
, and
\mathcal{L}_{2}^{(2^{\prime})}
on the trace polynomial space
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
(cf. Remark
3.13
), where the operator
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(2^{\prime})}
is identically zero (as it is for
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(2)}
in the
\SU(N)
case).

The
same
operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}
appears in the intertwining formulas for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\U(N)
,
\SU(N)
, and
\Sp(N)
. As
N\to\infty
, it is the operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}
that drives the large-
N
behavior of the transform; this is the key to our main result on the free Segal-Bargmann transform, Theorem
1.6
, which states that the large-
N
limit of the Segal-Bargmann transform on
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\SU(N)
, and
\Sp(N)
is the same operator
\mathscr{G}_{s,\tau}
. Moreover, we emphasize that the rate of convergence in
all
cases is
O(1/N^{2})
, as it is in the analogous result for the
\U(N)
case,
[
8
, Theorem 1.11]
. This is somewhat surprising, since there is a nontrivial term of order
1/N
in the intertwining formulas for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
and
\Sp(N)
that does not appear in the
\U(N)
and
\SU(N)
cases. That we still obtain
O(1/N^{2})
convergence for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
and
\Sp(N)
is due to the fact that in the scalar version of their intertwining formulas (cf. Theorems
3.20
and
5.5
), the term of order
1/N
is a first order differential operator. As a result, the
1/N
terms are “washed away” in the covariance estimates (cf. the proof of Theorem
4.10
in the
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
case for a more precise explanation; the
\Sp(N)
case is similar).

2
Background

In this section, we expand on the background required to prove our main results. In particular, we provide a brief overview of the heat kernel results used in constructing the Segal-Bargmann transform, and conclude by setting some notation that will be used for the remainder of the paper.

2.1
Heat kernels on Lie groups

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
. An inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{k}}
on
\mathfrak{k}
is
\Ad
-invariant
if, for all
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathfrak{k}
and all
k\in K
,

\left\langle\Ad_{k}X_{1},\Ad_{k}X_{2}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{k}}=\left\langle X_{1},X_{2}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{k}}.

A group whose Lie algebra possesses an
\Ad
-invariant inner product is called
compact type
.

The Lie groups
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\SU(N)
, and
\Sp(N)
studied in this paper are compact type (and are, in fact, compact).

Compact type Lie groups have the following property.

Proposition 2.2

([
[
14
]
, Lemma 7.5])
.

If
K
is a compact type Lie group with a fixed
\Ad
-invariant inner product, the
K
is isometrically isomorphic to a direct product group, i.e.
K\cong K_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some compact Lie group
K_{0}
and some nonnegative integer
d
.

Let
K
be a compact type Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
, and fix an
\Ad
-invariant inner product on
\mathfrak{k}
. Choose a right Haar measure
\lambda
on
K
; we will write
dx
for
\lambda(dx)
and
L^{2}(K)
for
L^{2}(K,\lambda)
. If
\beta_{\mathfrak{k}}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{k}
, we define the Laplace operator
\Delta_{K}
on
K
by

\Delta_{K}=\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{k}}}\widetilde{X}^{2},

(2.1)

Definition 2.3

2.2
Notation and definitions

The classical Segal-Bargmann transform is an isometric isomorphism from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
onto
\mathcal{H}L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
. In order to extend the Segal-Bargmann transform to Lie groups, we note that every connected Lie group has a
complexification
defined by a certain representation-theoretic universal property, mimicking the relationship between
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{C}^{d}
(see
[
9
, Section 2]
). For the present purposes, it is enough to know the concrete complexifications of
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\SU(N)
, and
\Sp(N)
, which we describe below.

Let
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
denote the
special orthogonal group
, defined by
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})=\{A\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{R})\,:\,AA^{\intercal}=I_{N},\,\det(A)=1\}.
Its complexification,
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})_{\mathbb{C}}
, is given by
\SO(N,\mathbb{C})=\{A\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})\,:\,AA^{\intercal}=I_{N},\,\det(A)=1\}.
The Lie group
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
has Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})=\{X\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{R})\,:\,X^{\intercal}=-X\},
while
\SO(N,\mathbb{C})
has Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{C})=\{X\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})\,:\,X^{\intercal}=-X\}.

The
unitary group
,
\U(N)
, is defined by
\U(N)=\{A\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})\,:\,AA^{*}=I_{N}\}
. The Lie algebra of
\U(N)
is
\mathfrak{u}(N)=\{X\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})\,:\,X^{*}=-X\}
. The
special unitary group

\SU(N)
is the subgroup of
\U(N)
consisting of matrices with determinant
1
. The complexification of
\SU(N)
is the special linear group
\SL(N,\mathbb{C})=\{A\in\GL_{N}(\mathbb{C})\,:\,\det(A)=1\}
. The Lie algebra of
\SU(N)
is
\mathfrak{su}(N)=\{X\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})\,:\,X^{*}=-X,\Tr(X)=0\}
, and the Lie algebra of
\SL(N,\mathbb{C})
is
\mathfrak{sl}(N,\mathbb{C})=\{X\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})\,:\,\Tr(X)=0\}
.

There are two standard realizations of the compact symplectic group
\Sp(N)
, first as a group of
N\times N
matrices over the quaternions, and second as a group of
2N\times 2N
matrices over
\mathbb{C}
. It is more convenient for us to work with the latter realization, which we introduce here; we address the former realization in Section
5.6
. Our choice of realization means that we will often have to add a normalization factor of
\frac{1}{2}
when working with
\Sp(N)\subseteq\M_{2N}(\mathbb{C})
.

Set

\Omega_{0}=\left[\begin{array}[]{rr}0&1\\
-1&0\\
\end{array}\right]\in\M_{2}(\mathbb{C}).

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\Omega=\Omega_{N}:=\diag(\underbrace{\Omega_{0},\Omega_{0},\ldots,\Omega_{0}}_{N\text{ times}})\in\M_{2N}(\mathbb{C}).

We define the
complex symplectic group
by
\Sp(N,\mathbb{C})=\{A\in\M_{2N}(\mathbb{C})\,:\,A^{\intercal}\Omega A=\Omega\}.
The Lie algebra of
\Sp(N,\mathbb{C})
is
\mathfrak{sp}(N,\mathbb{C})=\{X\in M_{2N}(\mathbb{C})\,:\,\Omega X^{\intercal}\Omega=X\}.
The
compact symplectic group

\Sp(N)
consists of the elements of
\Sp(N,\mathbb{C})
which are also unitary, i.e.

\Sp(N)=\Sp(N,\mathbb{C})\cap\U(2N)=\{A\in\M_{2N}(\mathbb{C})\,:\,A^{\intercal}\Omega A=\Omega,A^{*}A=I_{2N}\}.

(2.5)

The Lie algebra of
\Sp(N)
is
\mathfrak{sp}(N)=\{X\in M_{2N}(\mathbb{C})\,:\,\Omega X^{\intercal}\Omega=X,X^{*}=-X\}.

The complexification of
\Sp(N)
is
\Sp(N,\mathbb{C})
.

The groups
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\SU(N)
, and
\Sp(N)
can be thought of as (special) unitary groups over
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathbb{H}
, resp. For notational convenience, throughout the paper we associate the parameters
\beta=1,2,2^{\prime},4
with
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\SU(N)
,
\U(N)
, and
\Sp(N)
, resp., where
\beta
refers to the dimension of
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathbb{H}
as associative algebras over
\mathbb{R}
.

In order for the large-
N
limit of the Segal-Bargmann transform to converge in a meaningful way, we require the following normalizations of the trace and Hilbert-Schmidt inner products.

Notation 2.4
.

For
A\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, define

\displaystyle\tr(A)=\frac{1}{N}\Tr(A),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tr}(A)=\frac{1}{2N}\Tr(A),

(2.7)

where
\Tr
denotes the usual trace. We use
\tr
when applying the Segal-Bargmann transform on
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
and
\SU(N)
, and
\widetilde{\tr}
when applying the Segal-Bargmann transform on
\Sp(N)
.

We also define inner products on
\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\mathfrak{su}(N)
, and
\mathfrak{sp}(N)
by

\displaystyle\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2}\Tr(XY^{*})=\frac{N^{2}}{2}\tr(XY^{*}),\hskip 14.22636ptX,Y\in\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R}),\hskip 14.22636pt

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{su}(N)}

\displaystyle=N\Tr(XY^{*})=N^{2}\tr(XY^{*}),\hskip 14.22636ptX,Y\in\mathfrak{su}(N),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{sp}(N)}

\displaystyle=N\Tr(XY^{*})=2N^{2}\widetilde{\tr}(XY^{*}),\hskip 14.22636ptX,Y\in\mathfrak{sp}(N).

(2.10)

3
The Segal-Bargmann transform on
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})

In this section and the next, we prove Theorem
1.4
, Theorem
1.5
, and Theorem
1.6
for the Segal-Bargmann transform on
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
. We begin with a set of “magic formulas” which are the key ingredient to proving the
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
version of the intertwining formula for
\Delta_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}
of Theorem
1.4
. These formulas, which give simple expressions for certain quadratic matrix sums, are the analogues of the “magic formulas” of
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
for
\U(N)
. They allow us to compute the Segal-Bargmann transform for trace polynomials on
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
(
1.5
for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
). We then prove a second intertwining formula for the operator
\mathcal{A}_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}
which is used in proving the limit theorems of Section
4
.

3.1
Magic formulas and derivative formulas

Lemma 3.1

(Elementary matrix identities)
.

Let
E_{i,j}\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
denote the
N\times N
matrix with a
1
in the
(i,j)
th entry and zeros elsewhere. For
1\leq i,j,k,\ell\leq N
and
A=(A_{i,j})\in\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, we have

\displaystyle E_{i,j}E_{k,\ell}

\displaystyle=\delta_{j,k}E_{i,\ell}

(3.1)

\displaystyle E_{i,j}AE_{i,j}

\displaystyle=A_{j,i}E_{i,j}

(3.2)

\displaystyle E_{i,j}AE_{j,i}

\displaystyle=A_{j,j}E_{i,i}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Tr(AE_{i,j})

\displaystyle=A_{j,i}

(3.4)

Proof.

For
1\leq a,b,\leq N
,

[E_{i,j}E_{k,\ell}]_{a,b}=\sum_{h=1}^{N}[E_{i,j}]_{a,h}[E_{k,\ell}]_{h,b}.

Note that
[E_{i,j}]_{a,h}[E_{k,\ell}]_{h,b}
equals
1
precisely when
a=i
,
b=\ell
, and
j=k
, and
h=j=k
, and equals
0
otherwise, so
[E_{i,j}E_{k,\ell}]_{a,b}=\delta_{j,k}\delta_{a,i}\delta_{b,\ell}
, which proves (
3.1
).

We now make the following observations: for any
N\times N
matrix
A
,
AE_{i,j}
is the matrix which is all zeros except for the
j
th column, which is equal to the
i
th column of
A
. Hence the only (possibly) nonzero diagonal entry is the
(j,j)
th entry, which is
A_{j,i}
. This proves (
3.4
). Similarly,
E_{i,j}A
is the matrix which is all zeros except for the
i
row, which is the
j
th row of
A
. Putting these observations together yields (
3.2
) and (
3.3
).
∎

Proposition 3.2

(Magic formulas for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
)
.

For any
A,B\in M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}X^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{N-1}{N}I_{N}=-I_{N}+\frac{1}{N}I_{N}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}XAX

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}A^{\intercal}-\tr(A)I_{N}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\tr(XA)X

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}(A^{\intercal}-A)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\tr(XA)\tr(XB)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}(\tr(A^{\intercal}B)-\tr(AB))

(3.8)

Proof.

As shown in Theorem 3.3 of
[
8
]
, the quantity
\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}XAX
is independent of the choice of orthonormal basis. Hence to see (
3.6
), we may set
\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}
to be the orthonormal basis

\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}=\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}(E_{i,j}-E_{j,i})\right\}_{1\leq i<j\leq N}.

By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}(E_{i,j}-E_{j,i})A(E_{i,j}-E_{j,i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}[E_{i,j}AE_{i,j}-E_{i,j}AE_{j,i}-E_{j,i}AE_{i,j}+E_{j,i}AE_{j,i}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}(E_{i,j}AE_{i,j}-E_{i,j}AE_{j,i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}A_{j,i}E_{i,j}-\sum_{i\neq j}A_{j,j}E_{i,i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}A_{j,i}E_{i,j}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i,i}E_{i,i}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i,i}E_{i,i}-\sum_{i\neq j}A_{j,j}E_{i,i}

\displaystyle=A^{\intercal}-\Tr(A)I_{N},

and dividing by
N
proves (
3.6
). Equation (
3.5
) then follows from (
3.6
) by setting
A=I_{N}
.

Similarly, we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}\Tr(A(E_{i,j}-E_{j,i}))(E_{i,j}-E_{j,i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}[\Tr(AE_{i,j})E_{i,j}-\Tr(AE_{i,j})E_{j,i}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-\Tr(AE_{j,i})E_{i,j}+\Tr(AE_{j,i})E_{j,i}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}[\Tr(AE_{i,j})E_{i,j}-\Tr(AE_{j,i})E_{i,j}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}A_{j,i}E_{i,j}-\sum_{i\neq j}A_{i,j}E_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}A_{j,i}E_{i,j}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i,i}E_{i,i}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i,i}E_{i,i}-\sum_{i\neq j}A_{i,j}E_{i,j}

\displaystyle=A^{\intercal}-A,

which is equivalent to (
3.7
). Equation (
3.8
) now follows from (
3.7
) by multiplying by
B
and taking
\tr
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Our proof of the magic formulas for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
is primarily computational, relying on the elementary matrix identities of Lemma
3.1
. These results can also be obtained more abstractly. For
\beta\in\{1,2,2^{\prime},4\}
, let
\mathfrak{k}_{N}^{(\beta)}
denote
\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\mathfrak{su}(N)
,
\mathfrak{u}(N)
, and
\mathfrak{sp}(N)
, resp., and let
\beta_{\mathfrak{k}_{N}^{(\beta)}}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{k}_{N}^{(\beta)}
. In
[
13
, Lemma 1.2.1]
, Lévy provides an explicit decomposition of the
Casimir element

C_{\mathfrak{k}_{N}^{(\beta)}}
, defined as the tensor

C_{\mathfrak{k}_{N}^{(\beta)}}=\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{k}_{N}^{(\beta)}}}X\otimes X.

Let

T=\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}E_{a,b}\otimes E_{b,a}\mbox{\hskip 14.22636ptand\hskip 14.22636pt}P=\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}E_{a,b}\otimes E_{a,b}.

For
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}\}
, set
\mathbf{I}(\mathbb{K})=\{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}\}\cap\mathbb{K}
and define two elements

and
\Co^{\mathbb{K}}
of

by

\Real^{\mathbb{K}}=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbf{I}(\mathbb{K})}\gamma\otimes\gamma^{-1}\mbox{\hskip 14.22636ptand\hskip 14.22636pt}\Co^{\mathbb{K}}=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbf{I}(\mathbb{K})}\gamma\otimes\gamma.

Then for
\beta\in\{1,2^{\prime}4\}
(where
2^{\prime}
corresponds to the value

), the Casimir element

is given by

C_{\mathfrak{k}_{N}^{(\beta)}}=\frac{1}{\beta N}\left(-T\otimes\Real^{\mathbb{K}}+P\otimes\Co^{\mathbb{K}}\right),

and for
\beta=2
,
C_{\mathfrak{su}(N)}=C_{\mathfrak{u}(N)}-\frac{1}{N^{2}}iI_{N}\otimes iI_{N}
. This decomposition can be used to compute any expression of the form
\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{k}_{N}^{(\beta)}}}B(X,X)
, where

is an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear map. In particular, the magic formulas for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
,
\SU(N)
, and
\Sp(N)
(cf. Propositions
3.2
,
6.2
, and
5.10

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\widetilde{X}\tr(A^{m})\cdot\widetilde{X}A^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\left[\tr\left(-\sum_{j=m+1}^{0}A^{j}XA^{m-j}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{p}A^{k}XA^{p-k}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{p}A^{k}\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\left(\sum_{j=m+1}^{0}\tr(XA^{m})\right)XA^{p-k}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-m)A^{k}\left(\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\tr(XA^{m})X\right)A^{p-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{mp}{N^{2}}(A^{p-m}-A^{p+m}).

For
m\geq 0
and
p<0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\widetilde{X}\tr(A^{m})\cdot\widetilde{X}A^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\left[\tr\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}A^{j}XA^{m-j}\right)\left(-\sum_{k=p+1}^{0}A^{k}XA^{p-k}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=p+1}^{0}A^{k}\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tr(XA^{m})\right)XA^{p-k}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=p+1}^{0}mA^{k}\left(\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\tr(XA^{m})X\right)A^{p-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{mp}{N^{2}}(A^{p-m}-A^{p+m}).

Finally, for
m,p<0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\widetilde{X}

\displaystyle\tr(A^{m})\cdot\widetilde{X}A^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\left[-\tr\left(\sum_{j=m+1}^{0}A^{j}XA^{m-j}\right)\left(-\sum_{k=p+1}^{0}A^{k}XA^{p-k}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=p+1}^{0}A^{k}\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\left(\sum_{j=m+1}^{0}\tr(XA^{m})\right)XA^{p-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=p+1}^{0}(-m)A^{k}\left(\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\tr(XA^{m})X\right)A^{p-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-m)(-p)}{N^{2}}(A^{p-m}-A^{p+m}).

This proves (
3.14
).
∎

3.2
Intertwining formulas for
\Delta_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}

In this section, we describe the trace polynomial functional calculus that will be necessary for the proof of Theorem
1.4
, which contains the intertwining formulas for
\Delta_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}
,
\Delta_{\SU(N)}
, and
\Delta_{\Sp(N)}
. The space of trace polynomials was first introduced in
[
8
, Definition 1.7]
, where it was used to prove analogous intertwining results for
\Delta_{\U(N)}
. Using this framework, we then prove Theorem
1.4
for the
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
case.

The computations in this section are related to, but not quite the same as, those used to establish the intertwining formulas for
\Delta_{\U(N)}
in
[
8
, Sections 3-4]
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]
denote the algebra of
Laurent polynomials
in a single variable
u
:

\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]=\left\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}u^{k}\,:\,a_{k}\in\mathbb{C},a_{k}=0\mbox{ for all but finitely many }k\right\},

(3.16)

with the usual polynomial multiplication. Let
\mathbb{C}[u]
and
\mathbb{C}[u^{-1}]
denote the subalgebras consisting of polynomials in
u
and
u^{-1}
, respectively.

Let
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{v}]
denote the algebra of polynomials in countably many commuting variables
\mathbf{v}=\{v_{\pm 1},v_{\pm 2},\ldots\}
, and let
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
denote the algebra of polynomials in the commuting variables
\{u,u^{-1},v_{\pm 1},v_{\pm 2},\ldots\}
.

We now define the
trace polynomial functional calculus
: for
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
, we have

\displaystyle P_{N}^{(1)}(A)

\displaystyle=[P^{(1)}]_{N}(A):=P(u;\mathbf{v})|_{u=A,v_{k}=\tr(A^{k}),k\neq 0},\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{$A\in\SO(N,\mathbb{R})$ or $\SO(N,\mathbb{C})$},

\displaystyle P_{N}^{(2)}(A)

\displaystyle=[P^{(2)}]_{N}(A):=P(u;\mathbf{v})|_{u=A,v_{k}=\tr(A^{k}),k\neq 0},\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{$A\in\SU(N)$ or $\SL(N,\mathbb{C})$},

\displaystyle P_{N}^{(4)}(A)

\displaystyle=[P^{(4)}]_{N}(A):=P(u;\mathbf{v})|_{u=A,v_{k}=\widetilde{\tr}(A^{k}),k\neq 0},\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{$A\in\Sp(N)$ or $\Sp(N,\mathbb{C})$}.

Functions of the form
P_{N}^{(\beta)}
, where
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
, are called
trace polynomials
. We will often suppress the superscripts for
P_{N}^{(\beta)}
and write
P_{N}
when it is clear from context which version of the trace polynomial functional calculus is being used.

As an illustrative example, the trace polynomial
P_{N}^{(1)}=P_{N}
on
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
associated with

is

P_{N}(A)=\tr(A)\tr(A^{-3})^{6}A^{2}-3\tr(A^{5})^{2}I_{N}.

We now introduce the pseudodifferential operators on
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
that appear in our intertwining formulas.

Definition 3.6
.

Let

Notation 3.8

For

Theorem 3.9

(Partial product rule)
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
. For
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
and
Q\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{v}]
, the operator
\alpha\mathcal{L}_{0}+\beta\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(1)}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\alpha\mathcal{L}_{0}+\beta\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(1)}\right)(PQ)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\alpha\mathcal{L}_{0}+\beta\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(1)}\right)P\right)Q+P\left(\left(\alpha\mathcal{L}_{0}+\beta\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(1)}\right)Q\right).

(3.30)

Proof.

From Definition
3.7
, recall that

\mathcal{L}_{0}=-(\mathcal{N}_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{1})-2\mathcal{Y}_{1}-2\mathcal{Y}_{2},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(1)}=\mathcal{N}_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{1}+2\mathcal{Z}_{1}+2\mathcal{Z}_{2}.

Observe that
\mathcal{N}_{0}
,
\mathcal{Y}_{2}
,
\mathcal{Z}_{1}
are first order differential operators on
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{v}]
, and so satisfy the product rule on
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
. On the other hand,
\mathcal{N}_{1}
,
\mathcal{Y}_{1},
and
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
annihilate
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{v}]
and satisfy, for
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
and
Q\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{v}],

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}(PQ)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{N}_{1}P)Q,\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{Y}_{1}(PQ)=(\mathcal{Y}_{1}P)Q,\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{Z}_{2}(PQ)=(\mathcal{Z}_{2}P)Q.

Putting this together shows (
3.30
).
∎

Definition 3.10
.

The
trace degree
of a monomial in
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
is defined to be

\deg\left(u^{k_{0}}v_{1}^{k_{1}}v_{-1}^{k_{-1}}\cdots v_{m}^{k_{m}}v_{-m}^{k_{-m}}\right)=|k_{0}|+\sum_{1\leq|j|\leq m}|j|k_{j}.

(3.31)

We define the trace degree of any element in
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
to be the highest trace degree of any of its monomial terms. For
m\geq 0
, we define the subspace
\mathbb{C}_{m}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]\subseteq\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
to be

\mathbb{C}_{m}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]=\{P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]\,:\,\deg P\leq m\},

(3.32)

consisting of polynomials of trace degree
\leq m
. Note that
\mathbb{C}_{m}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
is finite-dimensional and
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}\mathbb{C}_{m}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
m,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
. The operators
\alpha\mathcal{L}_{0}+\beta\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(1)}

\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(1)}

\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(2)}

Corollary 3.12
.

For any

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\tau}{2}(\alpha\mathcal{L}_{0}+\beta\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(1)})}(PQ)

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\mathcal{L}^{k}(PQ)

3.3

\mathcal{A}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}_{s,\tau}

We now prove an intertwining formula for

that is analogous to the intertwining formula for

in Theorem

. To do so, we use the word polynomial space introduced in

Notation 3.15
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define
\mathscr{E}_{m}
to be the set of words
\varepsilon:\{1,\ldots,m\}\to\{\pm 1,\pm*\}
. We denote the length of a word
\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}_{m}
by
|\varepsilon|=m
and set
\mathscr{E}=\cup_{m\geq 0}\mathscr{E}_{m}
. We define the
word polynomial space

\mathscr{W}
to be

\mathscr{W}=\mathbb{C}\left[\{v_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}}\right],

the space of polynomials in the commuting variables
\{v_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}_{m}}
. For
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
not both zero, we define the words

\displaystyle\varepsilon(j,k)

\displaystyle=\overbrace{(\pm 1,\ldots,\pm 1,}^{|j|\text{ times}}\overbrace{\pm*,\ldots,\pm*)}^{|k|\text{ times}}\in\mathscr{E}_{|j|+|k|},

(3.47)

where the first
|j|
slots contain
+1
if
j>0
and
-1
if
j<0
, and the last
|k|
slots contain
+*
if
k>0
and
-*
if
k<0
. We set
v_{\varepsilon(0,0)}=1
.

Notation 3.16
.

For
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m})\in\mathscr{E}_{m}
and
A\in\SO(N,\mathbb{C})
, we define
A^{\varepsilon}=A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}}
, where
A^{+*}:=A^{*}
and
A^{-*}:=(A^{*})^{-1}
. If
P\in\mathscr{W}
, we define a function
P_{N}:\SO(N,\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C}
by

P_{N}(A)=P(\mathbf{V}(A)),

where

\mathbf{V}(A)=\{V_{\varepsilon}(A)\,:\,\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}\}

and

V_{\varepsilon}(A)=\tr(A^{\varepsilon})=\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}}).

Notation 3.17
.

We define the inclusion maps
\iota,\iota^{*}:\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{v}]\hookrightarrow\mathscr{W}
, with
\iota
linear and
\iota^{*}
conjugate linear, by

\iota(v_{k})=v_{\varepsilon(k,0)},\hskip 14.22636pt\iota^{*}(v_{k})=v_{\varepsilon(0,k)}.

(3.48)

The maps
\iota
and
\iota^{*}
intertwine with the evaluation map in the following way: for
Q\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{v}]
,

[\iota(Q)]_{N}(A)=Q_{N}(A),\hskip 14.22636pt[\iota^{*}(Q)]_{N}(A)=Q_{N}(A)^{*}.

(3.49)

Definition 3.18
.

The
trace degree
of a monomial
\prod_{j=1}^{m}v_{\varepsilon_{j}}^{k_{j}}\in\mathscr{W}
is defined to be

\deg\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}v_{\varepsilon_{j}}^{k_{j}}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}|k_{j}||\varepsilon_{j}|,

and the trace degree of an arbitrary element of
\mathscr{W}
is the highest trace degree of any of its monomial terms.

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
\mathscr{W}_{m}
denote the finite dimensional subspace of
\mathscr{W}

\mathscr{W}_{m}=\{P\in\mathscr{W}\,:\,\deg(P)\leq m\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[\{v_{\varepsilon}\}_{|\varepsilon|\leq m}],

so that
\mathscr{W}=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{W}_{m}
.

Theorem 3.19
.

Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\tau=t+i\theta\in\mathbb{C}
. There are collections
\left\{Q_{\varepsilon}^{s,\tau}\,:\,\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}\right\}
,
\left\{R_{\varepsilon}^{s,\tau}\,:\,\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}\right\}
, and
\left\{S_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{s,\tau}\,:\,\varepsilon,\delta\in\mathscr{E}\right\}
in
\mathscr{W}
such that:

1.

for each
\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}
,
Q_{\varepsilon}^{s,\tau}
and
R_{\varepsilon}^{s,\tau}
are certain finite sums of monomials of trace degree
|\varepsilon|
such that

\mathcal{A}_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}V_{\varepsilon}=[Q_{\varepsilon}^{s,\tau}]_{N}+\frac{1}{N}[R_{\varepsilon}^{s,\tau}]_{N},

(3.50)

2.

for
\varepsilon,\delta\in\mathscr{E}
,
S_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{s,\tau}
is a certain finite sum of monomials of trace degree
|\varepsilon|+|\delta|
such that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}}\left[\left(s-\frac{t}{2}\right)(\widetilde{X}_{\ell}V_{\varepsilon})(\widetilde{X}_{\ell}V_{\delta})+\frac{t}{2}(\widetilde{Y}_{\ell}V_{\varepsilon})(\widetilde{Y}_{\ell}V_{\delta})-\theta(\widetilde{X}_{\ell}V_{\varepsilon})(\widetilde{Y}_{\ell}V_{\delta})\right]=\frac{1}{N^{2}}[S_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{s,\tau}]_{N}.

For the proof below, we use the following conventions: let
\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}=\{X_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{d_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}}

\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})

\beta_{+}=\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}
and
\beta_{-}=i\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}

(3.52)

In addition, we will be liberal in our use of
\pm
to denote a sign that may vary for different terms and on different sides of an equation, since we will not require a precise formula for our word polynomials beyond what is described in the theorem statement.

Proof.

Fix a word
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m})\in\mathscr{E}
. Then for each
X\in\beta_{\pm}
and
A\in\SO(N,\mathbb{C})
,

(\widetilde{X}V_{\varepsilon})(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}}),

(3.53)

so

\displaystyle(\widetilde{X}^{2}V_{\varepsilon})(A)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX^{2})^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})

(3.54)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{k}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})

(3.55)

Applying magic formula (
3.5
) to sum each term in (
3.54
) over
\beta_{\pm}
, we have

\sum_{X\in\beta_{\pm}}\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX^{2})^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})=\pm\frac{N-1}{N}\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})=\pm\frac{N-1}{N}V_{\varepsilon}(A).

(3.56)

Summing over
1\leq j\leq m
now gives

\sum_{X\in\beta_{\pm}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX^{2})^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})=\frac{N-1}{N}n_{\pm}(\varepsilon)V_{\varepsilon}(A),

(3.57)

where

and
|n_{\pm}(\varepsilon)|\leq|\varepsilon|
. For (
3.55
), we can express each term in the sum as

\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{k}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})=\pm\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{0}}XA^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{1}}XA^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}}),

(3.58)

so
{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{0}}
,
{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{1}}
, and
{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}}
are substrings of
\varepsilon
such that
{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{0}}{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{1}}{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}}=\varepsilon
. Summing (
3.58
) over
X\in\beta_{\pm}
by magic formula (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\beta_{\pm}}\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots

\displaystyle(AX)^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{k}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})

\displaystyle=\pm\left[\frac{1}{N}\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{0}}(A^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{1}})^{-1}A^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}})-\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{0}}A^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}})\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{1}})\right]

(3.59)

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{1}{N}V_{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{3}}(A)\pm V_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{0}}{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}}}(A)V_{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{1}}(A),

(3.60)

where
{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{3}}
is the word of length
|\varepsilon|
such that
V_{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{3}}(A)=\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{0}}(A^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{1}})^{-1}A^{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}})
. Thus summing (
3.55
) over
X\in\beta_{\pm}
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\beta_{\pm}}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}

\displaystyle\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{k}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\pm V_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{3}}}(A)+\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\pm V_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{0}}{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}}}(A)V_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{1}}}(A).

We define word polynomials corresponding to (
3.3
) by

\displaystyle Q_{\varepsilon,0}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\pm v_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{3}}}

(3.61)

\displaystyle Q_{\varepsilon,1}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\pm v_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{0}}{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}}}v_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{1}}}.

(3.62)

Now if
X\in\beta_{+}
and
Y=iX\in\beta_{-}
, then

(\widetilde{Y}V_{\varepsilon})(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AY)^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})=i\sum_{j=1}^{m}\pm\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}}),

(3.63)

and

\displaystyle(\widetilde{X}\widetilde{Y}V_{\varepsilon})(A)=

\displaystyle i\sum_{j=1}^{m}\pm\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX^{2})^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})

(3.64)

\displaystyle+2i\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\pm\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{k}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}}).

(3.65)

A similar argument to the above shows that summing (

gives

\sum_{X\in\beta_{+}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\pm\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX^{2})^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})=\frac{N-1}{N}\eta(\varepsilon)V_{\varepsilon}(A)

where

and

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\beta_{+}}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\pm

\displaystyle\tr(A^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdots(AX)^{\varepsilon_{k}}\cdots A^{\varepsilon_{m}})

(3.67)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}V_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{3}}}(A)+\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\pm V_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{0}}{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}}}(A)V_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{1}}}(A).

We define corresponding word polynomials

\displaystyle Q_{\varepsilon,2}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\pm v_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{3}}}

(3.68)

\displaystyle Q_{\varepsilon,3}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\pm v_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{0}}{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}}}v_{{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{1}}}.

Putting this together, we define

\displaystyle Q_{\varepsilon}^{s,\tau}

\displaystyle=\left(s-\frac{t}{2}\right)\left(n_{+}(\varepsilon)v_{\varepsilon}+2Q_{\varepsilon,1}^{+}\right)+\frac{t}{2}\left(n_{-}(\varepsilon)v_{\varepsilon}+2Q_{\varepsilon,1}^{-}\right)-\theta\left(i\eta(\varepsilon)+2Q_{\varepsilon,3}\right),

\displaystyle=\left(s-\frac{t}{2}\right)\left(-n_{+}(\varepsilon)v_{\varepsilon}+2Q_{\varepsilon,0}^{+}\right)+\frac{t}{2}\left(-n_{-}(\varepsilon)v_{\varepsilon}+2Q_{\varepsilon,0}^{-}\right)-\theta\left(-i\eta(\varepsilon)+2Q_{\varepsilon,2}\right).

By construction,

For part (2), fix

with

. For each

\varepsilon(j)
and

are particular cyclic permutations of
\varepsilon

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\beta_{\pm}}(\widetilde{X}V_{\delta})(A)(\widetilde{X}V_{\varepsilon})(A)

Theorem 3.20

(Intertwining formula for
\mathcal{A}_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}
)
.

Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\tau=t+i\theta\in\mathbb{C}
, and let
\left\{Q_{\varepsilon}^{s,\tau}\,:\,\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}\right\}
,
\left\{R_{\varepsilon}^{s,\tau}\,:\,\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}\right\}
, and
\left\{S_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{s,\tau}\,:\,\varepsilon,\delta\in\mathscr{E}\right\}
be as in Theorem
3.19
. Define first and second order differential operators

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}}Q_{\varepsilon}^{s,\tau}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}

(3.79)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}}R_{\varepsilon}^{s,\tau}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}

(3.80)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{2}^{s,\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\varepsilon,\delta\in\mathscr{E}}S_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{s,\tau}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v_{\varepsilon}\partial v_{\delta}}

(3.81)

Then for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

,

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}P_{N}=\left[\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{2}^{s,\tau}\right)P\right]_{N}.

(3.82)

Proof.

For each

, we apply the chain rule to get

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}^{2}P_{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}}\widetilde{X}\left[\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}\right)(\mathbf{V})\cdot(\widetilde{X}V_{\varepsilon})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}}\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}\right)(\mathbf{V})\cdot(\widetilde{X}^{2}V_{\varepsilon})+\sum_{\varepsilon,\delta\in\mathscr{E}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial v_{\varepsilon}\partial v_{\delta}}\right)(\mathbf{V})\cdot(\widetilde{X}V_{\varepsilon})(\widetilde{X}V_{\delta}).

Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}P_{N}=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathscr{E}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}\right)(\mathbf{V})\cdot\mathcal{A}_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}V_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\varepsilon,\delta\in\mathscr{E}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial v_{\varepsilon}\partial v_{\delta}}\right)(\mathbf{V})\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}}\bigg[\left(s-\frac{t}{2}\right)(\widetilde{X}_{\ell}V_{\varepsilon})(\widetilde{X}_{\ell}V_{\delta})

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\frac{t}{2}(\widetilde{Y}_{\ell}V_{\varepsilon})(\widetilde{Y}_{\ell}V_{\delta})-\theta(\widetilde{X}_{\ell}V_{\varepsilon})(\widetilde{Y}_{\ell}V_{\delta})\bigg].

The result now follows from Theorem
3.19
.

and

[\mathcal{B}(P,Q)]_{N}(A)=\tr[P_{N}(A)Q_{N}(A)^{*}]\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{for all }A\in\SO(N,\mathbb{C}).

4.1
Concentration of measures

The following lemma is an essential component of our main limit theorems. It is a known result (see
[
12
, Corollary 6.2.32]
); we include a direct proof here for convenience.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X,Y\in M(N,\mathbb{C})
and suppose
\|\cdot\|
is a submultiplicative matrix norm. Then

\|e^{X+Y}-e^{X}\|\leq\|Y\|e^{\|X\|}e^{\|Y\|}.

Proof.

For
n\geq 0
, note that

\|(X+Y)^{n}-X^{n}\|\leq(\|X\|+\|Y\|)^{n}-\|X\|^{n},

where the inequality follows by expanding
(X+Y)^{n}
, which includes an
X^{n}
, then applying the submultiplicativity of the matrix norm, and then recombining terms. Hence

\displaystyle\|e^{X+Y}-e^{X}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(X+Y)^{n}}{n!}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{X^{n}}{n!}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\|(X+Y)^{n}-X^{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}[(\|X\|+\|Y\|)^{n}-\|X\|^{n}]

\displaystyle=e^{\|X\|+\|Y\|}-e^{\|X\|}.

It remains to show that
e^{\|X\|+\|Y\|}-e^{\|X\|}\leq\|Y\|e^{\|X\|}e^{\|Y\|}
, which is equivalent to showing that
e^{y}-1\leq ye^{y}
for
y\geq 0
. Rearranging, this inequality is equivalent to
1-y\leq e^{-y}
, which holds, in fact, for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
: by Bernoulli’s inequality,

e^{-y}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1-\frac{y}{n}\right)^{n}\geq 1-y.\qed

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional normed vector space over
\mathbb{C}
and suppose that that
L_{0}
,
L_{1}
, and
L_{2}
are operators on
V
. Then there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}<\infty
depending on
L_{0},L_{1},L_{2},\|\cdot\|_{V}
such that for
N
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle\|e^{L_{0}+\frac{1}{N}L_{1}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}L_{2}}-e^{L_{0}}\|_{\End(V)}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{N},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|e^{L_{0}+\frac{1}{N}L_{1}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}L_{2}}-e^{L_{0}+\frac{1}{N}L_{1}}\|_{\End(V)}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{N^{2}}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|e^{L_{0}+\frac{1}{N}L_{1}}-e^{L_{0}}\|_{\End(V)}\leq\frac{C_{3}}{N}

(4.3)

where
\|\cdot\|_{\End(V)}
is the operator norm on
\End(V)
induced by
\|\cdot\|_{V}
. Hence if
\varphi\in V^{*}
is a linear functional, then for
N
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle|\varphi(e^{L_{0}+\frac{1}{N}L_{0}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}L_{2}}v)-\varphi(e^{L_{0}}v)|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{N}\|\varphi\|_{V^{*}}\|v||_{V},

(4.4)

\displaystyle|\varphi(e^{L_{0}+\frac{1}{N}L_{1}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}L_{2}}v)-\varphi(e^{L_{0}+\frac{1}{N}L_{1}}v)|\leq\frac{C_{2}}{N^{2}}\|\varphi\|_{V^{*}}\|v||_{V}

(4.5)

\displaystyle|\varphi(e^{L_{0}+\frac{1}{N}L_{1}}v)-\varphi(e^{L_{0}}v)|\leq\frac{C_{3}}{N}\|\varphi\|_{V^{*}}\|v||_{V}

(4.6)

where
\|\cdot\|_{V^{*}}
is the dual norm on
V^{*}
.

Proof.

We set
X=L_{0}
and
Y=\frac{1}{N}L_{1}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}L_{2}
as in Lemma
4.1
, so

\displaystyle\|e^{L_{0}+\frac{1}{N}L_{1}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}L_{2}}-e^{L_{0}}\|_{\End(V)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\|L_{1}\|_{\End(V)}e^{\|L_{0}\|_{\End(V)}}e^{\left\|\frac{1}{N}L_{1}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}L_{2}\right\|_{\End(V)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\|L_{1}\|_{\End(V)}e^{\|L_{0}\|_{\End(V)}}e^{\frac{1}{N}\left\|L_{1}\right\|_{\End(V)}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\left\|L_{2}\right\|_{\End(V)}}.

Choose
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
e^{\frac{1}{N_{0}}\left\|L_{1}\right\|_{\End(V)}+\frac{1}{N_{0}^{2}}\left\|L_{2}\right\|_{\End(V)}}\leq 2
. Then (
4.1
) holds for all
N\geq N_{0}
by setting
C_{1}=2\|L_{1}\|_{\End(V)}e^{\|L_{0}\|_{\End(V)}}
. Equation (
4.4
) then follows. The proofs of (
4.2
), (
4.3
), and subsequently (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), are similar.
∎

We now introduce notation and establish a few preliminary results which we will use for proving Theorem
1.6
for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
in the next section.

Definition 4.3
.

Define a family of holomorphic functions
\{\nu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
on
\mathbb{C}
by setting
\nu_{0}(\tau)\equiv 1
and for
k\neq 0
,

\nu_{k}(\tau)=e^{-\frac{|k|}{2}\tau}\sum_{j=0}^{|k|-1}\frac{(-\tau)^{j}}{j!}|k|^{j-1}\binom{|k|}{j+1}.

(4.7)

For
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
, define the
trace evaluation map

\pi_{\tau}:\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]\to\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]
by

(\pi_{\tau}P)(u)=P(u;\mathbf{v})|_{v_{k}=\nu_{k}(\tau),k\neq 0}.

(4.8)

For a concrete example, if
P(u;\mathbf{v})=v_{3}v_{-7}^{2}u^{6}+9v_{1}v_{-4}^{5}
, then

(\pi_{\tau}P)(u)=\nu_{3}(\tau)\nu_{-7}(\tau)^{2}u^{6}+9\nu_{1}(\tau)\nu_{-4}(\tau)^{5}.

The following result is a key tool in proving our main limit theorems.

Theorem 4.4

(Biane,
[
3
]
)
.

For each
s>0
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\U(N)}\tr(U^{k})\,\rho_{s}^{\U(N)}(U)\,dU=\nu_{k}(s).

For
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
, let
P(u;\mathbf{1})=P(u;\mathbf{v})_{v_{k}=1,k\neq 0}
. Using the intertwining formula (
3.45
) for the
\U(N)
case and Theorem
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\U(N)}\tr(U^{k})\,\rho_{s}^{\U(N)}(U)\,dU=\lim_{N\to\infty}(e^{\frac{s}{2}\Delta_{\U(N)}}\tr[(\cdot)^{k}])(I_{N})

(4.9)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}(e^{\frac{s}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{0}-\frac{1}{N^{2}}(2\mathcal{K}_{1}^{-}+\mathcal{K}_{2}^{-}))}v_{k})(\mathbf{1}).

(4.10)

Since
\mathcal{L}_{0}
,
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{-}
, and
\mathcal{K}_{2}^{-}
are linear operators which preserve the finite-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{C}_{k}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
, we consider
(e^{\frac{s}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{0}-\frac{1}{N^{2}}(2\mathcal{K}_{1}^{-}+\mathcal{K}_{2}^{-}))}v_{k})(\mathbf{1})
to be the evaluation of the linear functional
\varphi(Q):=Q(\mathbf{1})
on the finite-dimensional space
\mathbb{C}_{m}[\mathbf{v}]
. Hence Corollary
4.2
applies, so taking the limit as
N\to\infty
, we have

\nu_{k}(s)=(e^{\frac{s}{2}\mathcal{L}_{0}}v_{k})(\mathbf{1}).

(4.11)

This yields the following version of Theorem
4.4
for the
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
case, which is due to Lévy
[
13
]
.

Lemma 4.5

(Lévy,
[
13
]
)
.

For
s>0
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}\tr(A^{k})\,\rho_{s}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}(A)\,dA=\nu_{k}\left(s\right).

(4.12)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}\tr(A^{k})\,\rho_{s}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}(A)\,dA

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}(e^{\frac{s}{2}\Delta_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}}\tr[(\cdot)^{k}])(I_{N})

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}(e^{\frac{s}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(1)}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{(1)})}v_{k})(\mathbf{1})=(e^{\frac{s}{2}\mathcal{L}_{0}}v_{k})(\mathbf{1})

where we have again applied Corollary
4.2
as in (

).
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For
Q\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{v}]
and
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\left(e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathcal{L}_{0}}Q\right)(\mathbf{1})=\pi_{\tau}Q.

(4.13)

Proof.

By Theorem
3.9
,
e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathcal{L}_{0}}
is a homomorphism of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{v}]
. Hence it suffices to show that

\left(e^{\tau\mathcal{L}_{0}}v_{k}\right)(\mathbf{1})=\pi_{\tau}(v_{k})=\nu_{k}\left(\tau\right)

(4.14)

for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. To see that it holds for all
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
, we expand the left-hand side as a power series in
\tau
. Fix a norm
\|\cdot\|_{W_{k}}
on the finite-dimensional vector space
W_{k}:=\mathbb{C}_{k}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
. Let
\|\cdot\|_{\End(W_{k})}
denote the operator norm on
\End(W_{k})
induced by
\|\cdot\|_{W_{k}}
, and let
\|\cdot\|_{W_{k}^{*}}
denote the corresponding dual norm on
W_{k}
. Let
\varphi
be the linear functional acting on
W_{k}
defined by
\varphi(P)=P(\mathbf{1})
. Then for any
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\left|\left(e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathcal{L}_{0}}v_{k}\right)(\mathbf{1})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{n}(\mathcal{L}_{0})^{n}v_{k}\right)(\mathbf{1})\right|=\left|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{n}\varphi((\mathcal{L}_{0})^{n}v_{k})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{|\tau|}{2}\right)^{n}\|\varphi\|_{W_{k}^{*}}\|\mathcal{L}_{0}\|^{n}_{\End(W_{k})}\|v_{k}\|_{W_{k}},

and the right-hand side converges by the ratio test. Thus the function
\tau\mapsto\left(e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathcal{L}_{0}}v_{k}\right)(\mathbf{1})
is analytic on the complex plane. By (
4.11
), this function agrees with the entire function
\tau\mapsto\nu_{k}\left(\tau\right)
for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, and so it also agrees for all
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
s>0
and
\tau=t+i\theta\in\mathbb{D}(s,s)
. For any
Q\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{v}]
,

\displaystyle\left(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}}\iota(Q)\right)(\mathbf{1})=\pi_{s-\tau}Q,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}}\iota^{*}(Q)\right)(\mathbf{1})=\overline{\pi_{s-\tau}Q}.

(4.16)

Proof.

First, observe that if
f:\SO(N,\mathbb{C})\to\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
is holomorphic, then
\widetilde{JX}f=i\widetilde{X}f
for all
X\in\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})
. Thus

\mathcal{A}_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}f|_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}=\sum_{X\in\beta_{\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb{R})}}\left[\left(s-\frac{t}{2}\right)\widetilde{X}^{2}-\frac{t}{2}\widetilde{X}^{2}-i\theta\widetilde{X}^{2}\right]f=(s-\tau)\Delta_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}f.

In particular, since
Q_{N}
is a trace polynomial,

e^{\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}}Q_{N}=e^{\frac{1}{2}(s-\tau)\Delta_{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})}}Q_{N}.

(4.17)

Applying intertwining formulas (
3.82
) and (
1.12
) to the left side and using (
3.49
) gives

\left[e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{2}^{s,\tau}}\iota(Q)\right]_{N}=[e^{\frac{1}{2}(s-\tau)\mathcal{D}_{N}^{(1)}}Q]_{N}.

(4.18)

Hence for
Q\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{v}]
,

\left(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{2}^{s,\tau}}\iota(Q)\right)(\mathbf{1})=\left(e^{\frac{1}{2}(s-\tau)(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(1)}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{(1)})}Q\right)(\mathbf{1}).

(4.19)

If

, then we can view the left and right sides as the evaluation of a linear functional
\varphi(P)=P(\mathbf{1})
on the finite-dimensional spaces
\mathscr{W}_{m}
and
\mathbb{C}_{m}[\mathbf{v}]
, respectively. Thus we may apply Corollary
4.2
to take the limit as

, which yields

\left(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}}\iota(Q)\right)(\mathbf{1})=\left(e^{\frac{1}{2}(s-\tau)\mathcal{L}_{0}}Q\right)(\mathbf{1}).

(4.20)

By Lemma

, the expression on the right is

. Equation (
4.16
) is proved similarly, using the fact that if
f:\SO(N,\mathbb{C})\to\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
is antiholomorphic, then
\widetilde{JX}f=-i\widetilde{X}f
for all

.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
s>0
,
\tau\in\mathbb{D}(s,s)
, and

. Then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}\tr(A^{k})\,\mu_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}(A)\,dA=\nu_{k}\left(s-\tau\right).

(4.21)

Proof.

We use (
2.4
) and intertwining formula (

4.2
The free Segal-Bargmann transform for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})

In this section, we prove our main result on the large-
N
limit of the Segal-Bargmann transform for
\SO(N,\mathbb{R})
. The free Segal-Bargmann transform
\mathscr{G}_{s,\tau}
and the free inverse Segal-Bargmann transform
\mathscr{H}_{s,\tau}
appearing in Theorem
1.6
have explicit formulas, which we first describe.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{0}
be the pseudodifferential operator on
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
from Definition
3.7
. We define the
free Segal-Bargmann transform
to be the map
\mathscr{G}_{s,\tau}:\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]\to\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]
given by

\mathscr{G}_{s,\tau}=\pi_{s-\tau}\circ e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathcal{L}_{0}},

(4.22)

and we define the

to be the map
\mathscr{H}_{s,\tau}:\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]\to\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]
given by

\mathscr{H}_{s,\tau}=\pi_{s}\circ e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}\mathcal{L}_{0}}.

(4.23)

Before proving Theorem
1.6
, we require the following result.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
s>0
and
\tau=t+i\theta\in\mathbb{D}(s,s)
. For any
P\in\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]

\displaystyle\|P_{N}-[\pi_{s}P]_{N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\rho_{s}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{R})})}=O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right),\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}

(4.24)

\displaystyle\|P_{N}-[\pi_{s-\tau}P]_{N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})})}=O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

(4.25)

This result shows that with respect to the heat kernel measure, the space of trace polynomials concentrates onto the space of polynomials as

.

Proof.

We first show (
4.25
). It suffices to prove the result for polynomials of the form
P(u;\mathbf{v})=u^{k}Q(\mathbf{v})
for

and

) then holds on all of
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1};\mathbf{v}]
by the triangle inequality. For such a polynomial,

P(u;\mathbf{v})-\pi_{s-\tau}P(u;\mathbf{v})=u^{k}[Q(\mathbf{v})-\pi_{s-\tau}Q]=u^{k}R_{s-\tau},

where we let
R_{s-\tau}=Q-\pi_{s-\tau}Q
. Hence for
A\in\SO(N,\mathbb{C})
,

\displaystyle\|P_{N}(A)-[\pi_{s-\tau}P]_{N}(A)\|^{2}_{\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})}

\displaystyle=\tr\left(A^{k}[R_{s-\tau}]_{N}(A)[R_{s-\tau}]_{N}(A)^{*}A^{*k}\right)

\displaystyle=\tr(A^{k}A^{*k})[R_{s-\tau}]_{N}(A)[R_{s-\tau}]_{N}(A)^{*}

\displaystyle=[v_{\varepsilon(k,k)}\iota(R_{s-\tau})\iota^{*}(R_{s-\tau})]_{N}(A).

(4.26)

Along with intertwining formula (
3.82
), this allows us to compute

\displaystyle\|P_{N}-[\pi_{s-\tau}P]_{N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})})}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{s,\tau}^{\SO(N,\mathbb{C})}}\left(\|P_{N}-[\pi_{s-\tau}P]_{N}\|^{2}_{\M_{N}(\mathbb{C})}\right)(I_{N})

(4.27)

\displaystyle=\left(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{2}^{s,\tau}}(v_{\varepsilon(k,k)}\iota(R_{s-\tau})\iota^{*}(R_{s-\tau}))\right)(\mathbf{1})

(4.28)

By the triangle inequality, the last line is bounded by

\displaystyle\left|\left(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{s,\tau}_{N}}(v_{\varepsilon(k,k)}\iota(R_{s-\tau})\iota^{*}(R_{s-\tau}))\right)(\mathbf{1})-\left(e^{{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}}}(v_{\varepsilon(k,k)}\iota(R_{s-\tau})\iota^{*}(R_{s-\tau}))\right)(\mathbf{1})\right|

(4.29)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\left|\left(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}}(v_{\varepsilon(k,k)}\iota(R_{s-\tau})\iota^{*}(R_{s-\tau}))\right)(\mathbf{1})\right|.

(4.30)

If
m=\deg v_{\varepsilon(k,k)}\iota(R_{s-\tau})\iota^{*}(R_{s-\tau})
, we can interpret (
4.29
) as the evaluation of the linear functional
\varphi(R)=R(\mathbf{1})
on
\mathscr{W}_{m}
, so by Corollary
4.2
,

\displaystyle\left|\left(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{s,\tau}_{N}}(v_{\varepsilon(k,k)}\iota(R_{s-\tau})\iota^{*}(R_{s-\tau}))\right)(\mathbf{1})-\left(e^{{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}}}(v_{\varepsilon(k,k)}\iota(R_{s-\tau})\iota^{*}(R_{s-\tau}))\right)(\mathbf{1})\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

(4.31)

To bound (

), recall from Theorem
3.20
that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}
is a first-order differential operator on
\mathscr{W}_{m}
, so

is an algebra homomorphism, i.e.

\displaystyle e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}}

\displaystyle(v_{\varepsilon(k,k)}\iota(R_{s-\tau})\iota^{*}(R_{s-\tau}))

\displaystyle=e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}}v_{\varepsilon(k,k)}\cdot e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}}\iota(R_{s-\tau})\cdot e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}}\iota^{*}(R_{s-\tau}).

For any

\left|(e^{{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}}}T)(\mathbf{1})\right|\leq\left|(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}+\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{s,\tau}}T)(\mathbf{1})-(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}}T)(\mathbf{1})\right|+\left|(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}}T)(\mathbf{1})\right|

(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}}\iota(R_{s-\tau}))(\mathbf{1})=(e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{s,\tau}}\iota^{*}(R_{s-\tau}))(\mathbf{1})=0.

5

\Sp(N)

In this section, we prove the

1.4
,
1.5
, and
1.6

5.1

The

\mathbf{j}

\displaystyle\mathbf{j}\mathbf{k}

Let

On the Barnes double gamma function
Shahen Alexanian, Alexey Kuznetsov

Abstract
We aim to achieve the following three goals. First of all, we collect all known definitions, transformation properties and functional identities of Barnes double gamma function G ⁡ ( z , τ ) G(z;\tau) . Second, we derive an algorithm for numerically computing the double gamma function and present its complete asymptotic expansion as z → ∞ z\to\infty . Third, we derive some new properties, including a new product identity and new representations of the gamma modular forms C ⁡ ( τ ) C(\tau) and D ⁡ ( τ ) D(\tau) .



Keywords
: Barnes double gamma function, asymptotic expansion, Bernoulli numbers

2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
: Primary 33B15, Secondary 30E15, 33F05

Introduction

The double gamma function
G(z;\tau)
was introduced and thoroughly studied by Barnes in 1899
[
1
]
. It seems that this function was studied previously by Alexeiewski and other researchers: Barnes even calls it
Alexeiewski’s function
in
[
1
]
and in §26 in
[
2
]
. The modern convention is to call
G(z;\tau)
the double gamma function or the Barnes double gamma function. In the last three decades this special function has appeared more and more frequently in solutions to problems in probability
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
, number theory
[
7
,
8
]
and physics
[
9
]
and it has been a fertile object of study in its own right for its many interesting properties and connections to other special functions
[
10
,
11
]
.

We will present two definitions of the double gamma function
G(z;\tau)
as infinite products. First, for

\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
we define
C(\tau)
and
D(\tau)
through the following asymptotic expansions as
m\to+\infty

\displaystyle C(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}\psi(k\tau)+\frac{1}{2}\psi(m\tau)-\frac{1}{\tau}\ln\left(\frac{\Gamma(m\tau)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)-\frac{\tau}{12}\psi^{\prime}(m\tau)

(1)

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau^{3}}{720}\psi^{(3)}(m\tau)-\frac{\tau^{5}}{30240}\psi^{(5)}(m\tau)+\frac{\tau^{7}}{1209600}\psi^{(7)}(m\tau)+O(m^{-9}),

\displaystyle D(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}\psi^{\prime}(k\tau)+\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime}(m\tau)-\frac{1}{\tau}\psi(m\tau)-\frac{\tau}{12}\psi^{\prime\prime}(m\tau)

(2)

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau^{3}}{720}\psi^{(4)}(m\tau)-\frac{\tau^{5}}{30240}\psi^{(6)}(m\tau)+\frac{\tau^{7}}{1209600}\psi^{(8)}(m\tau)+O(m^{-10}).

Here
\psi(z)=\frac{d}{dz}\ln(\Gamma(z))
is the digamma function (see
[
12
]
). We would like to emphasize that the above equations
(
1
) and (
2
) serve two purposes: they give us definitions of
C(\tau)
and
D(\tau)
if we take a limit
m\to+\infty
of the right-hand side of each equation and they also provide a practical way to compute
C(\tau)
and
D(\tau)
if we take
m
a large positive integer.
The asymptotic expansions (
1
) and (
2
) can be found on pages 362-363 in
[
1
]
(the higher order terms presented here were obtained by Euler-Maclaurin summation – these are useful when computing
C(\tau)
and
D(\tau)
numerically). Barnes
[
1
]
calls these two functions
C(\tau)

and
D(\tau)

the gamma modular forms
, and we will follow this convention. Next, we define

a(\tau):=-\gamma\tau+\frac{\tau}{2}\ln(2\pi\tau)+\frac{1}{2}\ln(\tau)-\tau C(\tau),\;\;b(\tau):=-\frac{\pi^{2}\tau^{2}}{6}-\tau\ln(\tau)-\tau^{2}D(\tau),

(3)

where
\gamma=-\psi(1)=0.577215...
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.
Now we can define the double gamma function
G(z;\tau)
(for
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
and
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
) as an entire function of
z
of order two having the Weierstrass infinite product representation:

G(z;\tau):=\frac{z}{\tau}e^{a(\tau)\frac{z}{\tau}+b(\tau)\frac{z^{2}}{2\tau}}\prod\limits_{m\geq 0}\prod\limits_{n\geq 0}{}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(1+\frac{z}{m\tau+n}\right)e^{-\frac{z}{m\tau+n}+\frac{z^{2}}{2(m\tau+n)^{2}}},

(4)

where the prime in the second product means that the term corresponding to
m=n=0
is omitted.
According to the definition (
4
), it is clear that the function
G(z;\tau)
has zeros on the lattice
m\tau+n
,
m\leq 0
,
n\leq 0
(we note that the zeros are all simple if and only if
\tau
is not rational).

The second definition of
G(z;\tau)
is through a single infinite product involving the classical gamma function:

G(z;\tau)=\frac{1}{\tau\Gamma(z)}e^{\tilde{a}(\tau)\frac{z}{\tau}+\tilde{b}(\tau)\frac{z^{2}}{2\tau^{2}}}\prod\limits_{m\geq 1}\frac{\Gamma(m\tau)}{\Gamma(z+m\tau)}e^{z\psi(m\tau)+\frac{z^{2}}{2}\psi^{\prime}(m\tau)},

(5)

where we denoted

\tilde{a}(\tau)=a(\tau)-\gamma\tau,\qquad\tilde{b}(\tau)=b(\tau)+\frac{\pi^{2}\tau^{2}}{6}.

(6)

It is easy to see that both infinite products in (
4
) and
(
5
) converge absolutely. Barnes
[
1
]
proved the following crucial result: the two definitions
(
4
) and (
5
) are consistent and the function
G(z;\tau)
satisfies the normalization condition
G(1;\tau)=1
and the
two functional equations

G(z+1;\tau)=\Gamma\left(\frac{z}{\tau}\right)G(z;\tau),\;\;\;G(z+\tau;\tau)=(2\pi)^{\frac{\tau-1}{2}}\tau^{-z+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(z)G(z;\tau).

(7)

These last three properties can serve as the third definition of the Barnes double gamma function: it is a unique function that is entire in
z
and analytic in
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
and which satisfies the two functional equations (
7
) and is normalized via
G(1;\tau)=1
. As we will see later, this third definition is very useful when proving various identities and transformation formulas involving
G(z;\tau)
.

The double gamma function
G(z;\tau)
is closely related to the symmetric version of the double gamma function
\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
, which was studied by Barnes in
[
2
]
. This function is symmetric in
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
, it satisfies the functional identities

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(z+\omega_{1};\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{\omega_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{z}{\omega_{2}}}}{\Gamma(\frac{z}{\omega_{2}})}\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(z+\omega_{2};\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{\omega_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{z}{\omega_{1}}}}{\Gamma(\frac{z}{\omega_{1}})}\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

and the normalization condition

\Gamma_{2}(\omega_{1};\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\sqrt{2\pi/\omega_{2}}
. The function
\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
can be expressed in terms of
G(z;\tau)
as follows:

\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=(2\pi)^{\frac{z}{2\omega_{1}}}\omega_{2}^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}+\frac{z(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})}{2\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}-1}G\Big(\frac{z}{\omega_{1}};\frac{\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}\Big)^{-1}.

(8)

This result is established in §26 in
[
2
]
.
To be more precise, the above four equations hold only when
|{\textnormal{arg}}(\omega_{1})-{\textnormal{arg}}(\omega_{2})|<\pi
, where the arguments of
\omega_{i}
lie in
(-\pi,\pi)
. However, this case is all we need for our purposes and the reader can find the corresponding equations for the general case in
[
2
]
.

The double gamma function enjoys a number of other important properties and transformation identities:

(i)

A reflection formula: for
\textnormal{Im}(\tau)>0

-2\pi{\textnormal{i}}\tau G\left(\frac{1}{2}+z;\tau\right)G\left(\frac{1}{2}-z;-\tau\right)=\frac{\left(-e^{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}z};q\right)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}},

(9)

where
q:=e^{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}\tau}
and
(a;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{n\geq 0}(1-aq^{n})
is the
q
-Pocchammer symbol.

(ii)

A modular transformation

G(z;\tau)=(2\pi)^{\frac{z}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)}\tau^{\frac{z-z^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{z}{2}-1}G\left(\frac{z}{\tau};\frac{1}{\tau}\right).

(10)

(iii)

A multiplication formula: for any integers
p,q\in{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle G(z;p\tau/q)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{1}{2p\tau}(z-1)(qz-p\tau)}(2\pi)^{-\frac{q-1}{2}(z-1)}

(11)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod\limits_{i=0}^{p-1}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{q-1}\frac{G((z+i)/p+j\tau/q;\tau)}{G((1+i)/p+j\tau/q;\tau)}.

This formula has two important corollaries: setting
q=1
and re-scaling
z\mapsto pz
gives us

G(pz;p\tau)=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{p-1}\frac{G(z+i/p;\tau)}{G((1+i)/p;\tau)},

(12)

whereas if we set
q=p
and re-scale
z\mapsto pz
we get

G(pz;\tau)=p^{\frac{1}{2\tau}(pz-1)(pz-\tau)}(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}(p-1)(pz-1)}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{p-1}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{G(z+(i+j\tau)/p;\tau)}{G((1+i+j\tau)/p;\tau)}

(13)

(iv)

A product identity

G(z;\tau)=\Big(\frac{1+\tau}{\tau}\Big)^{\frac{z^{2}}{2\tau}-(1+\tau)\frac{z}{2\tau}+1}(2\pi)^{-\frac{z}{2\tau}}G(z+1;1+\tau)G(z/\tau;1+1/\tau).

(14)

(v)

An integral representation: for
\textnormal{Re}(z)>0
and
\textnormal{Re}(\tau)>0

\displaystyle\ln G(z;\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{e^{-\tau x}-e^{-zx}}{(1-e^{-x})(1-e^{-\tau x})}-z\frac{e^{-\tau x}}{1-e^{-\tau x}}

(15)

\displaystyle\qquad+(z-1)\Big(\frac{z}{2\tau}-1\Big)e^{-\tau x}+\frac{e^{-x}}{1-e^{-x}}\bigg)\frac{dx}{x}.

The reflection formula (
9
) was established in §13 in
[
1
]
.
Identity (
10
) follows by setting
\omega_{1}=1
and
\omega_{2}=\tau
in (
8
) and using the fact that

\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{2},\omega_{1})
. Formula
(
11
) follows from (
8
) and the formula for
\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1}/p,\omega_{2}/q)
on page 359 in
[
2
]
. Formula (
15
) can be found in
[
13
]
[Section 5]. Identity (
14
) seems to be new: we present the proof of this formula in Section
4
. In the same Section
4
we also present direct proofs of
(
10
) and (
11
), which do not rely on the symmetric double gamma function
\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
.

Our main goals in this paper are to develop an algorithm for numerical computation of
G(z;\tau)
and to state a complete asymptotic expansion of this function as
z\to\infty
. In Section
2
we will introduce and study two sequences of polynomials which will be needed for achieving these goals in Section
3
. The main results in Section
3
are: (i) Theorem
1
, which gives a formula that can be used to numerically compute
G(z;\tau)
; (ii) Theorem
2
and Proposition
3
, which state a complete asymptotic expansion of
G(z;\tau)
as
z\to\infty
and discuss the properties of the constant term in this expansion; (iii) Proposition
4
where we present new formulas for the gamma modular forms
C(\tau)
and
D(\tau)
.

2
Two sequences of polynomials

We recall that the sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
of the Bernoulli numbers is defined via the generating function

\frac{u}{e^{u}-1}=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}B_{n}\frac{u^{n}}{n!}.

(16)

It is known that
B_{n}
are all rational numbers,
B_{2m+1}=0
for
m\geq 1
and the first six non-zero values are

B_{0}=1,\;B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2},\;B_{2}=\frac{1}{6},\;B_{4}=-\frac{1}{30},\;B_{6}=\frac{1}{42},\;B_{8}=-\frac{1}{30},\dots

The Bernoulli polynomials are defined as

B_{n}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}B_{n-k}x^{k}

and have the generating function

\frac{ue^{xu}}{e^{u}-1}=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}B_{n}(x)\frac{u^{n}}{n!}.

(17)

Now we define the first sequence of polynomials that will be important for us later:

q_{n}(\tau):=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}B_{k}B_{n-k}\tau^{k},\;\;\;n\geq 0.

(18)

If
n
is even then
q_{n}(\tau)
is a polynomial of degree
n
.
If
n=2m+1\geq 3
is odd then the only non-zero terms in the above sum are those with
k=1
and
k=n-1
, so that in this case
q_{n}(\tau)
is a polynomial of degree
n-1
given by

q_{2m+1}(\tau)=-(m+\tfrac{1}{2})B_{2m}\times\tau(1+\tau^{2m-1}).

(19)

The list of polynomials
q_{n}(\tau)
for
0\leq n\leq 21
is presented in Appendix
A
. In the next result we collect some properties of polynomials
q_{n}(\tau)
.

Proposition 1
.

(i)

The exponential generating functions of polynomials
\{q_{n}(\tau)\}_{n\geq 0}
is

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}q_{n}(\tau)\frac{u^{n}}{n!}=\frac{\tau u^{2}}{(e^{u}-1)(e^{\tau u}-1)}.

(20)

(ii)

For
n\geq 0
we have
q_{n}(\tau)=\tau^{n}q_{n}(\tau^{-1})
.

(iii)

The polynomials
q_{n}(\tau)
can be computed recursively via

q_{n}(\tau)=-\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(1+\tau)^{k+2}-1-\tau^{k+2}}{(k+1)(k+2)}q_{n-k}(\tau)

(21)

by starting with
q_{0}(\tau)=1
.

(iv)

The polynomials
q_{n}(\tau)
satisfy the following summation identities: for
n\geq 0
,
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
y\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}q_{k}(\tau)=(-1)^{n}q_{n}(-\tau),

(22)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+1}{k}q_{k}(\tau)=(n+1)B_{n}\tau^{n},

(23)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+1}{k+1}(B_{k+1}(y)-B_{k+1})y^{n-k}q_{n-k}(\tau)=(n+1)yq_{n}(\tau y),

(24)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+1}{k+1}(B_{k+1}(y)-B_{k+1})\Big(\frac{\tau}{y}\Big)^{k}q_{n-k}(\tau)=(n+1)yq_{n}\Big(\frac{\tau}{y}\Big).

(25)

Proof.

Formula (
20
) follows from (
16
) and (
18
). The identity
q_{n}(\tau)=\tau^{n}q_{n}(\tau^{-1})
from item (ii) follows at once from the definition (
18
). To prove (
21
) we first derive a series expansion

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau u^{2}}(e^{u}-1)(e^{\tau u}-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau u^{2}}(e^{(1+\tau)u}-e^{u}-e^{\tau u}+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\big((1+\tau)^{n+2}-1-\tau^{n+2}\big)\frac{u^{n}}{(n+2)!}.

Using the above series and (
20
) we obtain

1=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}q_{n}(\tau)\frac{u^{n}}{n!}\times\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\big((1+\tau)^{n+2}-1-\tau^{n+2}\big)\frac{u^{n}}{(n+2)!},

and inspecting the coefficient in front of
u^{n}
(with
n\geq 1
) we conclude that

\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(1+\tau)^{k+2}-1-\tau^{k+2}}{(k+1)(k+2)}q_{n-k}(\tau)=0,

which is equivalent to (
21
).

Identity (
22
) is established by comparing the coefficients in front of
u^{n}
in the identity

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q_{n}(-\tau)\frac{u^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau u^{2}}{(1-e^{-u})(e^{\tau u}-1)}

\displaystyle=e^{u}\times\frac{\tau u^{2}}{(e^{u}-1)(e^{\tau u}-1)}=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\frac{u^{n}}{n!}\times\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}q_{n}(\tau)\frac{u^{n}}{n!}.

To prove (
23
) we expand each term in square brackets in

\Big[\frac{e^{u}-1}{u}\Big]\times\Big[\frac{\tau u^{2}}{(e^{u}-1)(e^{\tau u}-1)}\Big]=\Big[\frac{\tau u}{e^{\tau u}-1}\Big]

in power series in
u
(with the help of Taylor series for
e^{u}
and formulas
(
16
) and (
20
)) and compare the coefficients in front of
u^{n}
. Similarly, formula (
24
) is obtained by expanding each term in square brackets in
the identity

\Big[\frac{e^{yu}-1}{e^{u}-1}\Big]\times\Big[\frac{\tau(yu)^{2}}{(e^{yu}-1)(e^{\tau(yu)}-1)}\Big]=y\times\Big[\frac{(y\tau)u^{2}}{(e^{u}-1)(e^{(y\tau)u}-1)}\Big]

and comparing the coefficients in front of
u^{n}
.
One would also need to use the following series expansion

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\frac{B_{n+1}(y)-B_{n+1}}{n+1}\times\frac{u^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{yu}-1}{e^{u}-1},

which is easily obtained from (
16
) and (
17
).
Finally, formula (
25
) follows from (
24
) and identity

q_{n}(\tau)=\tau^{n}q_{n}(\tau^{-1})
.

To state our main results in Section

, we will also need a sequence of polynomials

\{P_{n}(z;\tau)\}_{n\geq 1}

P_{n}(z;\tau):=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n+2}{k+2}q_{n-k}(\tau)z^{k-1}.

(26)

It is clear that
P_{n}(z;\tau)
is a polynomial with rational coefficients; it is a monic polynomial of degree
n-1
in
z
and a polynomial of degree not greater than
n-1
in
\tau
. The first five polynomials
P_{n}(z;\tau)
are

Proposition 2
.

(i)

The sequence of polynomials

\{P_{n}(z;\tau)\}_{n\geq 1}
has generating function

\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}P_{n}(z;\tau)\frac{u^{n}}{(n+2)!}=\frac{\tau}{z^{3}}\times\frac{e^{uz}-1-uz-\frac{u^{2}z^{2}}{2}}{(e^{u}-1)(e^{\tau u}-1)}.

(27)

(ii)

For
n\geq 1
we have
P_{n}(z/\tau;1/\tau)=\tau^{1-n}P_{n}(z;\tau)
.

(iii)

An alternative expression for
P_{n}(z;\tau)
is

P_{n}(z;\tau)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n+2}{k+2}\tau^{n-k}B_{n-k}\tilde{B}_{k+2}(z),

(28)

where
\tilde{B}_{k}(z)
are polynomials of degree

defined by

\tilde{B}_{k}(z)=z^{-3}\Big[B_{k}(z)-B_{k}(0)-B_{k}^{\prime}(0)z-B_{k}^{\prime\prime}(0)\frac{z^{2}}{2}\Big],\;\;\;k\geq 3.

(iv)

For
n\geq 2
the polynomials
P_{n}(z;\tau)
can be computed recursively via

\displaystyle P_{n}(z;\tau)=z^{n-1}-\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom{n+2}{k+2}\frac{(1+\tau)^{k+2}-1-\tau^{k+2}}{(n-k+1)(n-k+2)}P_{n-k}(z;\tau),

(29)

P_{1}(z;\tau)=1

)

in the series expansion of

is equal to
P_{n}(z;\tau)/(n+2)!
. Identity
P_{n}(z/\tau;1/\tau)=\tau^{1-n}P_{n}(z;\tau)
follows from the definition of
P_{n}(z;\tau)
(formula (
26
))
and result in item (ii) in Proposition
1
. Formula (
28
) is easily derived from
(

) and series expansion

\displaystyle\frac{e^{uz}-1-uz-\frac{u^{2}z^{2}}{2}}{z^{3}u(e^{u}-1)}=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\tilde{B}_{n+2}(z)\times\frac{u^{n}}{(n+2)!},

which follows from (

). Finally, (

) is proved in exactly the same way as formula (

) in Proposition

and we leave the details to the reader.

3
Main results

First we will address the question of how to numerically compute the Barnes double gamma function
G(z;\tau)
. If
z=m\tau+n
with either
m
or
n
positive, then
G(z;\tau)
can be computed via functional equations (
7
) and the fact that
G(1;\tau)=1
. On the other hand, if
\tau=q/p
is a rational number, then the problem of computing
G(z;\tau)
is reduced with the help of (
11
) to the problem of numerically evaluating
G(z)=G(z;1)
, which can be done efficiently by an algorithm presented in
[
14
]
. Here we present an algorithm for computing values of
G(z;\tau)
for complex values of
z
and/or
\tau
, which only requires being able to evaluate logarithm of the gamma function in the entire complex plane (for this one could use Lanczos approximation
[
15
]
or approximations developed in

[
14
]
.)

Formula (
5
) represents the double gamma function
G(z;\tau)
as an infinite product involving gamma functions. We take the truncated part of this infinite product as an approximation of the Barnes double gamma function

G_{N}(z;\tau):=\frac{1}{\tau\Gamma(z)}e^{\tilde{a}(\tau)\frac{z}{\tau}+\tilde{b}(\tau)\frac{z^{2}}{2\tau^{2}}}\prod\limits_{m=1}^{N}\frac{\Gamma(m\tau)}{\Gamma(z+m\tau)}e^{z\psi(m\tau)+\frac{z^{2}}{2}\psi^{\prime}(m\tau)}

(30)

where
\tilde{a}(\tau)
and
\tilde{b}(\tau)
are defined in (
6
). In the next result we present the complete asymptotic expansion in powers of
N^{-1}
of the error term of this approximation.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
z\in{\mathbb{C}}

and
|\arg(\tau)|<\pi
. Then for any
M=1,2,3,\dots
we have

G(z;\tau)=G_{N}(z;\tau)\exp\Big(z^{3}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{M}\frac{(-\tau)^{-k-1}P_{k}(z;-\tau)}{k(k+1)(k+2)}\times N^{-k}+O(N^{-M-1})\Big),

(31)

as
N\to+\infty
.

Proof.

We fix the values of
z
and
\tau
and denote

r_{m}(z;\tau):=\ln\Gamma(m\tau)-\ln\Gamma(z+m\tau)+z\psi(m\tau)+\frac{z^{2}}{2}\psi^{\prime}(m\tau)

(32)

and

\displaystyle R_{N}(z;\tau):=\sum\limits_{m=N+1}^{\infty}r_{m}(z;\tau).

(33)

We assume that
N
is big enough, so that the circle

\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}\;:\;|w-N\tau|\leq 2|z|\}
does not intersect the negative half-line
(-\infty,0]
. Since
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
, this condition implies that for
m>N
we have
z+m\tau\notin(-\infty,0]
so that all the terms in (
33
) are well defined.
It is clear from (
5
) that

G(z;\tau)=G_{N}(z;\tau)\times\exp\big(R_{N}(z;\tau)\big),

(34)

thus to prove (
31
) we need to find an asymptotic expansion of
R_{N}(z;\tau)
in powers of
N^{-1}
.

Our first step is to write the Taylor expansion with the remainder term

\displaystyle\ln\Gamma(z+m\tau)

\displaystyle=\ln\Gamma(m\tau)+z\psi(m\tau)

\displaystyle+\frac{z^{2}}{2!}\psi^{\prime}(m\tau)+\dots+\frac{z^{M+2}}{(M+2)!}\psi^{(M+1)}(m\tau)+{\epsilon}_{M+2}(z,m\tau).

From the above series and (
32
) we obtain

r_{m}(z;\tau)=-\sum\limits_{l=1}^{M}\frac{z^{l+2}}{(l+2)!}\psi^{(l+1)}(m\tau)-{\epsilon}_{M+2}(z,m\tau).

Using the Taylor polynomial approximation with the error term in the form

f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}f^{(k)}(0)+\frac{z^{n+1}}{n!}\int_{0}^{1}f^{(n+1)}(sz)(1-s)^{n}ds,

with
f(z)=\ln\Gamma(z+m\tau)
and
n=M+2
, coupled with the maximum modulus principle, we obtain an upper bound

|{\epsilon}_{M+2}(z,m\tau)|\leq\frac{|z|^{M+3}}{(M+3)!}\times\max\limits_{|w|=|z|}|\psi^{(M+2)}(w+m\tau)|

It is known (see formula 5.15.9 in
[
16
]
) that
\psi^{(l+1)}(z)
has asymptotic expansion

\psi^{(l+1)}(z)\sim\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}\frac{(j+l)!}{(j)!}\frac{(-1)^{j+l}B_{j}}{z^{l+j+1}},\;\;\;z\to\infty.

Moreover, this asymptotic expansion is uniform in domains of the form

|\arg(z)|<\pi-\delta
, for arbitrary small
\delta>0
.
Therefore we have an estimate

{\epsilon}_{M+2}(z,m\tau)=O(m^{-M-2})

and we can write down an asymptotic expansion of
r_{m}(z;\tau)
in powers of
m^{-1}
:

\displaystyle r_{m}(z;\tau)

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{l=1}^{M}\frac{z^{l+2}}{(l+2)!}\psi^{(l+1)}(m\tau)+O(m^{-M-2})

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{l=1}^{M}\frac{z^{l+2}}{(l+2)!}\times\bigg\{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}\frac{(j+l)!(-1)^{j+l}B_{j}}{(j)!(m\tau)^{l+j+1}}\bigg\}+O(m^{-M-2}).

On the other hand, using the Euler-Maclaurin formula we establish the following asymptotic expansion, valid for any
s>1
:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=N+1}^{\infty}m^{-s}\sim\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}\frac{(s)_{k-1}}{(k)!}B_{k}N^{1-s-k},\;\;\;N\to+\infty.

Here
(s)_{k}:=s(s+1)\dots(s+k-1)
is the Pochhammer symbol.
From the above two formulas and (
33
) we obtain
an asymptotic expansion of
R_{N}(z;\tau)
in powers of
N^{-1}
:

\displaystyle R_{N}(z;\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m=N+1}^{\infty}r_{m}(z;\tau)=-\sum\limits_{l=1}^{M}\frac{z^{l+2}}{(l+2)!}

\displaystyle\times\bigg\{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}\frac{(-1)^{j+l}B_{j}(j+l)!}{(j)!\tau^{l+j+1}}\times\sum\limits_{k=0}^{M}\frac{(l+j+1)_{k-1}}{(k)!}B_{k}N^{-j-k-l}\bigg\}+O(N^{-M-1}).

Next we note that
(j+l)!(l+j+1)_{k-1}=(l+j+k-1)!
, we introduce a new variable of summation
i=j+k+l
and collect the terms in front of
N^{-i}
(for
i=1,2,\dots,M
) to obtain

\displaystyle R_{N}(z;\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}N^{-i}(i-1)!(-\tau)^{-i-1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{i}\frac{z^{l+2}}{(l+2)!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{i-l}\frac{(-\tau)^{k}B_{k}B_{i-l-k}}{(k)!(i-l-k)!}+O(N^{-M-1})

\displaystyle=z^{3}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}N^{-i}(i-1)!(-\tau)^{-i-1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{i}\frac{z^{l-1}}{(l+2)!(i-l)!}q_{i-l}(-\tau)+O(N^{-M-1})

\displaystyle=z^{3}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\frac{(-\tau)^{-i-1}P_{i}(z;-\tau)}{i(i+1)(i+2)}\times N^{-i}+O(N^{-M-1}).

The above result and (
34
) give us the desired formula (
31
).

Unfortunately, this asymptotic expansion of

. To state this result, we denote by

C(\tau)
converges to zero as
m\to+\infty
. Thus in the limit
m\to+\infty
the above formula gives us the desired result (
45
).

Let us now establish (
47
). Assume that
\textnormal{Re}(\tau)>0
. From (
15
) we find

\frac{d}{dz}\ln G(z;\tau)\Big|_{z=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{xe^{-\tau x}}{(1-e^{-x})(1-e^{-\tau x})}-\frac{e^{-\tau x}}{1-e^{-\tau x}}-\frac{1}{2\tau}e^{-\tau x}\bigg)\frac{dx}{x},

which implies

\displaystyle C(\tau)+\frac{d}{dz}\ln G(z;\tau)\Big|_{z=\tau}=\frac{1}{2\tau}\ln(2\pi)+I

(49)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\tau}\ln(2\pi)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{e^{-x}}{\tau x}\Big(\frac{1}{1-e^{-x}}+1-\frac{\tau}{2}\Big)-\frac{e^{-\tau x}}{x(1-e^{-\tau x})}-\frac{1}{2\tau x}e^{-\tau x}\bigg)dx.

We separate the integral
I
in the above formula as follows:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=I_{1}-I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}

(50)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{1-e^{-x}}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{x}\Big)\frac{e^{-x}}{\tau x}dx-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{1-e^{-\tau x}}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\tau x}\Big)\frac{e^{-\tau x}}{x}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\tau}\Big(e^{-x}(1+x(1-\tau))-e^{-\tau x}\Big)x^{-2}dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1+\tau}{2\tau}\Big(e^{-x}-e^{-\tau x}\Big)x^{-1}dx.

To compute
I_{1}
we evaluate for
\textnormal{Re}(w)>2

\displaystyle f_{1}(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{1-e^{-x}}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{x}\Big)e^{-x}x^{w-1}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{w-1}dx}{e^{x}-1}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}x^{w-1}dx-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}x^{w-2}dx

\displaystyle=\Gamma(w)\zeta(w)-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(w)-\Gamma(w-1)

\displaystyle=\Gamma(w+1)\Big(\frac{\zeta(w)-\frac{1}{2}}{w}-\frac{1}{w(w-1)}\Big),

where we have used well known integral representation formulas for the Riemann zeta function
and for the Gamma function. The function in the right-hand side is analytic in
\textnormal{Re}(w)>-1
and it is clear that
(1/\tau)f_{1}(w)\to I_{1}
as
w\to 0^{+}
. To compute this limit we use the fact that

\zeta(w)=-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{w}{2}\ln(2\pi)+O(w^{2}),\;\;\;w\to 0

and we obtain

I_{1}=\frac{1}{\tau}\Big(1-\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi)\Big).

(51)

A change of variables
x\mapsto x/\tau
gives us an identity
I_{2}=\tau I_{1}
. The third integral is computed via the same trick as above: we evaluate for
\textnormal{Re}(w)>0

\displaystyle f_{3}(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(e^{-x}(1+x(1-\tau))-e^{-\tau x}\Big)x^{w-1}dx

\displaystyle=\Gamma(w)+(1-\tau)\Gamma(w+1)-\tau^{-w}\Gamma(w)

\displaystyle=\Gamma(w+2)\Big(\frac{1-\tau^{-w}}{w(w+1)}+\frac{1-\tau}{w+1}\Big),

observe that this function is analytic for
\textnormal{Re}(w)>-2

and taking the limit as
w\to-1
we obtain

I_{3}=\frac{1}{\tau}\lim\limits_{w\to-1}f_{3}(w)=\frac{1}{\tau}(\tau-1-\tau\ln(\tau)).

Finally, with the help of Frullani integral
[
18
]
we evaluate

I_{4}=\frac{1+\tau}{2\tau}\ln(\tau).

Combining the above expressions for
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
I_{3}
and
I_{4}
we have

I=I_{1}-I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}=\frac{\tau-1}{2\tau}\ln\Big(\frac{2\pi}{\tau}\Big),

and this formula together with (
49
) gives us (
47
) for
\textnormal{Re}(\tau)>0
. The fact that this result holds in the larger domain
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
follows by analytic continuation.

The proof of (
46
) and (
48
) follows exactly the same strategy as the proof of (
45
) and (
47
) and is left to the reader.

Proof of the product identity (
14
).

We consider a function

f(z)=\frac{G(z;\tau)}{G(z+1;1+\tau)G(z/\tau;1+1/\tau)}.

It is easy to check that this function is entire and zero-free: the zeros of the denominator cover the same quadrant of the lattice

\{m\tau+n\;:\;m,n\leq 0\}
as the zeros of the numerator, see Figure
2
. Since the Barnes
G
-function is an entire function of order two (this follows from the Weierstrass product representation (
4
)), we conclude that
f(z)=\exp(A+Bz+Cz^{2})
for some constants
A,B,C
. Our aim is to prove that

f(z)=\Big(\frac{1+\tau}{\tau}\Big)^{\frac{z^{2}}{2\tau}-(1+\tau)\frac{z}{2\tau}+1}(2\pi)^{-\frac{z}{2\tau}}.

(53)

There are several methods that could be used to determine the three constants
A,B,C
. The first method is based on computing the asymptotics of
\ln f(z)
as
z\to\infty
via (
36
). The second method would be to choose
A
,
B
and
C
so that the function

g(z)=e^{A+Bz+Cz^{2}}G(z+1;1+\tau)G(z/\tau;1+1/\tau)

satisfies the same functional equations (
7
) as
G(z;\tau)
and to enforce the normalization condition
g(1)=1
. Here we will follow the third method: we will compute the values of
f(0)
,
f(\tau)
and
f(1+\tau)
and will then check that the function given in
(
53
) agrees with these values.

First, we note that

G(z;\tau)=(z/\tau)(1+o(1))
as
z\to 0
(this follows from (
4
)), thus we obtain

f(0)=(1+\tau)/\tau
.
Formula (
35
) helps us compute

f(\tau)=\Big(\frac{1+\tau}{\tau}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Similarly, using the first formula in (
7
) we find
G(1+\tau;\tau)=G(\tau;\tau)
and we conclude that

f(1+\tau)=\Big(\frac{1+\tau}{\tau}\Big)(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2\tau}-\frac{1}{2}}.

Now one can check that the function in the right-hand side of (
53
) has the same values at
f(0)
,
f(\tau)
and
f(1+\tau)
computed above. Since a polynomial of degree two is uniquely determined by its values at three distinct points, this ends the proof of
(
14
).

Proof of the modular transformation (
10
).
Let
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
be fixed. Consider the entire function

f(z)=(2\pi)^{\frac{z}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)}\tau^{\frac{z-z^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{z}{2}-1}G\left(\frac{z}{\tau};\frac{1}{\tau}\right),\;\;\;z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

We will check that
f
satisfies both functional equations (
7
) and the normalization condition

f(1)=1
: this will imply that
f(z)=G(z;\tau)
.

Using the second identity in (
7
) we obtain

\displaystyle f(z+1)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{z+1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)}\tau^{\frac{z+1-(z+1)^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{z+1}{2}-1}G\left(\frac{z}{\tau}+\frac{1}{\tau};\frac{1}{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{z+1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)}\tau^{\frac{z+1-(z+1)^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{z+1}{2}-1}(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2\tau}-\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{\frac{z}{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{z}{\tau}\Big)G\left(\frac{z}{\tau};\frac{1}{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=\Gamma\Big(\frac{z}{\tau}\Big)(2\pi)^{\frac{z}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)}\tau^{\frac{z-z^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{z}{2}-1}G\left(\frac{z}{\tau};\frac{1}{\tau}\right)=\Gamma\Big(\frac{z}{\tau}\Big)f(z).

Similarly, using the first identity in (
7
) we find

\displaystyle f(z+\tau)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{z+\tau}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)}\tau^{\frac{z+\tau-(z+\tau)^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{z+\tau}{2}-1}G\left(\frac{z}{\tau}+1;\frac{1}{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{z+\tau}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)}\tau^{\frac{z+\tau-(z+\tau)^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{z+\tau}{2}-1}\Gamma(z)G\left(\frac{z}{\tau};\frac{1}{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{\tau-1}{2}}\tau^{-z+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(z)(2\pi)^{\frac{z}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)}\tau^{\frac{z-z^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{z}{2}-1}G\left(\frac{z}{\tau};\frac{1}{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{\tau-1}{2}}\tau^{-z+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(z)f(z).

Since
f
satisfies the two functional equations
(
7

f(1)=1
(which follows from (
35
)), we conclude that
f(z)=G(z;\tau)
.

Proof of the multiplication formula (
11
).

We will follow a similar path as in the above proof of
(
14
). Let
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
and
p,q\in{\mathbb{N}}
be fixed. Consider the entire function

h(z)=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{p-1}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{q-1}\frac{G((z+i)/p+j\tau/q;\tau)}{G((1+i)/p+j\tau/q;\tau)}.

The zeros of
G((z+i)/p+j\tau/q;\tau)
are

z=-(j+qn)\frac{p\tau}{q}-(i+pn)
,
m,n\geq 0
. The union of these sets as
i
ranges from
0
to
p-1
and
j
ranges from
0
to
q-1
is precisely the lattice
\{-m\tau-n\;:\;m,n\geq 0\}
, thus the function
H(z)=G(z;p\tau/q)/h(z)
has no zeros. It is also an entire function of second order, which satisfies
H(1)=1
, thus we can write

H(z)=\exp(B(z-1)+C(z-1)^{2})
for some constants
B
and
C
. To find constants
B
and
C
we will compute
H(0)
and
H(p\tau/q)
.

As
z\to 0
we have
G(z;p\tau/q)=zq/(p\tau)(1+o(1))
and

\displaystyle h(z)

\displaystyle=G(z/p;\tau)\times\prod\limits_{0\leq i\leq p-1}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq q-1\\
i+j>0\end{subarray}}\frac{G((z+i)/p+j\tau/q;\tau)}{G((1+i)/p+j\tau/q;\tau)}

\displaystyle=z/(p\tau)(1+o(1))\prod\limits_{j=1}^{q-1}\frac{G(j\tau/q;\tau)}{G(1+j\tau/q;\tau)}

\displaystyle=z/(p\tau)(1+o(1))\prod\limits_{j=1}^{q-1}\Gamma(j/q)^{-1}=z/(p\tau)(1+o(1))\times(2\pi)^{\frac{1-q}{2}}q^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Here in the third step we used the first functional equation from (
7
) and
in the last step we used the multiplication formula for the gamma function. The above results imply
H(0)=(2\pi)^{(q-1)/2}q^{1/2}
.

Next we set
z=p\tau/q
and compute using (
35
)

G(p\tau/q;p\tau/q)=(2\pi)^{\frac{p\tau-q}{2q}}(p\tau/q)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the same method as above we also compute

\displaystyle h(p\tau/q)

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{p-1}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{q-1}\frac{G(i/p+(j+1)\tau/q;\tau)}{G((1+i)/p+j\tau/q;\tau)}

\displaystyle=G(\tau;\tau)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\frac{G(i/p+\tau;\tau)}{G(i/p;\tau)}\times\prod\limits_{j=1}^{q-1}\frac{G(j\tau/q;\tau)}{G(1+j\tau/q;\tau)}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{(\tau-1)/2}\tau^{-1/2}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}(2\pi)^{(\tau-1)/2}\tau^{-i/p+1/2}\Gamma(i/p)\times\prod\limits_{j=1}^{q-1}\Gamma(j/q)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{(p\tau-q)/2}(p\tau/q)^{-1/2}

and we conclude that
H(p\tau/q)=(2\pi)^{(p\tau-q)(1-q)/(2q)}
. Using these two values of
H(0)
and
H(p\tau/q)
and the fact that
H(1)=1
we conclude that

H(z)=q^{\frac{1}{2p\tau}(z-1)(qz-p\tau)}(2\pi)^{-\frac{q-1}{2}(z-1)},

which ends the proof of (
11
).

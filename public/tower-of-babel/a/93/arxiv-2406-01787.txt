Bosonization of 2+1 dimensional fermions on the surface of topological insulators
Andrea Cappelli, Lorenzo Maffi, Riccardo Villa

Abstract
Three-dimensional topological insulators can be described by an effective field theory involving two ‘hydrodynamic’ Abelian gauge fields. The action contains a bulk topological BF term and a surface term, called loop model. This describes the massless 2+1 dimensional excitations and provides them with a semiclassical, yet non-trivial conformal invariant dynamics. Given that topological insulators are originally fermionic, this physical setting is ideal for realizing the bosonization of massless fermions in terms of gauge fields. Building on earlier analyses of the loop model, we find that fermions belong to the solitonic spectrum and can be described by Wilson lines, through the generalization of 1+1 dimensional vertex operators. Their correlation functions agree with conformal invariance. The bosonic loop model is then mapped into a fermionic theory by using the general construction of fermionic topological phases described in the literature. It requires the identification of the characteristic one-form ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} symmetry of the bosonic theory and its gauging, which originates the fermion number ( − 1 ) F (-1)^{F} , the spin sectors and the time reversal symmetry obeying 𝒯 2 = ( − 1 ) F {\cal T}^{2}=(-1)^{F} . These results are detailed for the effective action and the partition function on the geometry S 2 × S 1 S^{2}\times S^{1} .

Introduction

In this work we analyze the effective field theory of 3+1 dimensional
topological insulators
[
1
,
2
]
,
which is based on the BF topological theory, with bulk and surface
terms
[
3
]
. In addition, the boundary dynamics is given by the
non-local Abelian gauge theory called loop model, which involves two
gauge fields, respectively ‘electric’ and ‘magnetic’
[
4

The loop model possesses several interesting features: it is conformal
invariant with a critical line, analogous to the compact boson in 1+1
dimensions; it is invariant under 2+1 dimensional particle-vortex
duality; and it matches both the selfdual mixed-dimension
QED_{4,3}
for large
number of flavors

and the three-dimensional

In an earlier work by some of the authors, the loop model was quantized
and the partition function was obtained on the generalized cylinder

of solitonic states
[
4
]

In the present study, the corresponding field operators are

Abelian fields, which are analogous to order and disorder fields

[
5
,
6
]

Their two-point functions are found to be conformal invariant
with scaling dimensions matching the solitonic spectrum in the partition

The effective field theory description of topological phases

mirroring what happens in the quantum Hall effect, where the
2+1d Chern–Simons theory in the bulk matches the 1+1d boundary
conformal theory bosonizing Weyl fermions
[
7
]
.
In one dimension higher, the bulk-boundary correspondence again
provides a guide for defining the field operators, their quantum
numbers and statistics, starting from the bulk topological theory and
then extending them to the boundary, where they acquire the
semiclassical solvable dynamics given by the loop model. Research on
bosonization in higher dimensions has a long history

[
6
,
5
,
8
]
and recently received a
lot of attention

[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]

The fermion excitation is described in the loop model by a pair of order and disorder fields, whose charges requires a modification of the soliton spectrum, allowing half-integer flux quantization of gauge fields.

It turns out that the modified quantization is one element

[
13

The first step is the identification of the one-form

This new field allows for half–integer flux quantization

In the second part of this work, we work out the detailed map between bosonic to fermionic theories, using the partition function
and the effective action, both including the loop model dynamics.

5

In section

The additional, one–form

Finally, we show that two bosonic theories, with BF coupling

Bosonic effective field theory of topological
insulators

2.1
Bulk topological theory

In this section we review the description of (fractional) topological
insulators developed in previous works
[
18
,
4
]
. It is based on the bulk topological BF action

[
3
]
together with the conformal invariant boundary dynamics
given by the loop model
[
19
,
12
]
.

The degrees of freedom in the bulk are supposed to be point
particles and vortex lines, associated to
currents
J^{\mu}
and
J^{\mu\nu}
, respectively.
Being conserved, they can be parameterized as

J^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial_{\nu}b_{\rho\sigma},\qquad\qquad J^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial_{\rho}a_{\sigma},

(2.1)

in terms of two Abelian
U(1)
’hydrodynamic’ gauge fields, the one–form

a=a_{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}
and the two–form

b=\frac{1}{2}b_{\mu\nu}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}
.

The bulk action is

S_{bulk}[b,a,A]=i\int_{\mathcal{M}_{4}}\frac{k}{2\pi}b\mathrm{d}a+\frac{1}{2\pi}b\mathrm{d}A-\frac{\theta}{8\pi^{2}}\mathrm{d}a\mathrm{d}A\,,

(2.2)

where
A
is the background electromagnetic field.

The action (
2.2
) correctly reproduces the basic features
of topological insulators.
Indeed, it is time–reversal (TR) invariant by requiring the
parameter of the third term to be
\theta=\pi\sim-\pi
, being

\theta=0
for trivial insulators. Upon integration of the
hydrodynamic matter fields
a,b
, one obtains the induced action

S_{ind}[A]=i\frac{\theta}{8\pi^{2}k}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{4}}\mathrm{d}A\,\mathrm{d}A\,,

(2.3)

which expresses the response of the system to background changes. For

k=1
this is the standard electromagnetic response of topological
insulators which can also be derived from a bulk Dirac fermion in the
limit of large mass
[
7
]
. For integer

k\neq 1
, the theory (
2.2
) describes interacting
electrons forming the so-called fractional topological insulators, in
analogy with the physics of the quantum Hall effect
1
1

1

As in
that case, the response action (
2.3
) involves the
fractional coupling constant
1/k
and thus is not well-defined
globally
[
20
]
.

[
7
]
.
The basic excitations are point-like and loop-like,
with fractional charge and fractional monodromy phases.

In order to see these features, we couple the
a,b
fields to point particle moving along
\xi(t)

and vortex line sources in
\xi(t,\sigma)
, by adding the following terms to (
2.2
):

\begin{split}&\Delta S_{bulk}=-i\int_{\mathcal{M}_{4}}a_{\mu}\mathcal{J}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}b_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu},\\
&\mathcal{J}^{\mu}=N_{0}\,\dot{\xi}^{\mu}\delta^{(3)}(x-\xi(t)),\qquad\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}=M_{0}\,\dot{\xi}^{\mu}\times\xi^{{}^{\prime}\nu}\delta^{(2)}(x-\xi(t,\sigma)),\end{split}

(2.4)

with respective charges
N_{0},M_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The equations of motion for
the
a,b
fields obtained from the action (
2.2
),
(
2.4
) and the definitions (
2.1
) imply

\begin{split}&J^{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}b_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{k}{\cal J}^{\mu},\\
&J^{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}a_{\beta}=\frac{1}{k}{\cal J}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta}\,.\end{split}

(2.5)

The currents can be integrated across surfaces
\Sigma
and volumes

{\cal V}
encircling the lines and points, respectively, leading to the following bulk charges

\begin{split}{\cal Q}&=\int_{\cal V}J^{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\cal V}\mathrm{d}b=\frac{N_{0}}{k},\\
{\cal Q}_{T}&=\int_{\Sigma}J^{0i}n^{i}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}a=\left(\frac{M_{0}}{k}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}A\right),\end{split}

(2.6)

where
n^{i}
is the unit vector orthogonal to the surface
\Sigma
.
These equations shows that both excitations have charges in units of

1/k
, as expected. The second equation expresses the charge density
along the vortex line, whose aspects will be better described
later.

The main feature of the BF action is to attach fractional fluxes to
charges, leading to Aharonov-Bohm phases for point particles
encircling vortex lines in three-dimensional space
[
4
]
,

\frac{\Theta}{2\pi}=\frac{N_{0}M_{0}}{k}\,.

(2.7)

2.2
Boundary theory: the loop model

Topological insulators possess (interacting) massless excitations at
their surface. The bulk theory (
2.2
)
determines the type of boundary fields and the topological part of the
boundary action (no Hamiltonian), as follows

S_{surf}[\zeta,a,A]=i\int_{\mathcal{M}_{3}}\frac{k}{2\pi}\zeta\mathrm{d}a+\frac{1}{2\pi}\zeta\mathrm{d}A-\frac{1}{8\pi}a\mathrm{d}A,

(2.8)

where
a
is the bulk field brought to the boundary

\mathcal{M}_{3}=\partial\mathcal{M}_{4}
and
\zeta
is the reduction to the boundary of
the two-form,
b|_{{\cal M}_{3}}=\mathrm{d}\zeta
.
The whole action involving bulk and
boundary terms (
2.2
), (
2.8
),
is invariant under local gauge transformations,

b\rightarrow b+\mathrm{d}\beta
,
\zeta\rightarrow\zeta+\beta
and
a\rightarrow a+d\varphi
.

The last term in the action, once the
a,\zeta
fields are integrated out,
determines the boundary response, given by the Chern-Simons action

\int A\mathrm{d}A
with coupling constant
1/2k
. For
k=1
, this the
well-known parity (and time-reversal) global anomaly of 2+1
dimensional fermions, which is canceled by the theta term in the bulk
action (
2.2
), according to the bulk-boundary
correspondence.

2

A deeper description of global anomaly

In effective field theory

natural generalization to
k>1

[
7

. As a consequence of anomaly

The boundary action (

(2.9)

where
g_{0}
is a coupling constant,
\partial^{2}=\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\mu}

and
\partial^{-1}
is the Green function of

\partial\equiv\sqrt{-\partial^{2}}

\frac{1}{\partial(x,y)}=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{(x-y)^{2}}.

(2.10)

All in all, the whole action for the boundary degrees of freedom is

3

Hereafter omitting the subscript to
S_{surf}
.

\begin{split}S[\zeta,a,A]&=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{3}}k\zeta\mathrm{d}a+\zeta\mathrm{d}A+\frac{g_{0}}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{3}}a_{\mu}\frac{-\delta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}}{\partial}a_{\nu}=\\
&=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{3}}k\zeta\mathrm{d}a+\zeta\mathrm{d}A+\frac{g_{0}}{8\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{3}}f_{\mu\nu}\frac{1}{\partial}f_{\mu\nu},\end{split}

(2.11)

where
f_{\mu\nu}
the field strength of the
a_{\mu}
field.
This theory is called the ‘loop model’, because it can also be written

This kind of dynamics was

The loop model (

The integration of the field

This is the same response of 2+1d fermions to quadratic order

Thus, the loop model reproduces a feature of the

The kernel of the loop model satisfies the following

\begin{split}\int j_{\mu}D_{\mu\nu}^{-1}j_{\nu}&=\int\zeta_{\mu}D_{\mu\nu}\zeta_{\nu},\\
D_{\mu\nu}(x,y)&=\frac{-\delta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}}{\partial}(x,y),\qquad\qquad j_{\mu}=\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho}\partial_{\nu}\zeta_{\rho}.\end{split}

(2.13)

Using this identity we can integrate out the field

S[\zeta,A]=\frac{k^{2}}{4\pi g_{0}}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{3}}\zeta_{\mu}\frac{-\delta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}}{\partial}\zeta_{\nu}+\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{3}}\zeta\mathrm{d}A.

Properties of the loop model

In the following, we list some properties of this 2+1 dimensional

S^{2}\times\mathbb{R}

2.3.1
Soliton charges

A standard fact of topological matter is that bulk excitations correspond
to solitonic sectors of the boundary theory, with associated conserved
charges. Let us see how the flat bulk connections
a,b
with
characteristic quantized fluxes
(
2.6
) extend to the boundary, where
b=\mathrm{d}\zeta
.
A bulk point source generates the flux
\int\mathrm{d}\zeta
across the
boundary, which remains invariant when the source is brought
to the boundary.
The bulk vortex line can be open or closed; in the open case, it hits the
boundary once or twice, with endpoints becoming another kind of
point-like excitation with flux
\pm\int\mathrm{d}a
. The line can end
inside the ball at the singular center, which can be thought of as
the zero-radius limit of an inner boundary sphere, not considered
here for simplicity (see Fig.
1
).
A closed vortex line can be fully inside the bulk or lay on the boundary.

D^{3}=S^{2}\times I

S^{2}
. The vortex lines are drawn in red, they can finish

x_{0}

The fluxes associated to point-like excitations in the boundary
are therefore

Q=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S^{2}}\mathrm{d}\zeta=\frac{N_{0}}{k},\qquad\qquad Q_{T}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S^{2}}\mathrm{d}a=\frac{M_{0}}{k}.

(2.15)

The corresponding solitons of the theory (
2.11
) are
solutions of the equations of motion with boundary conditions suitable
for these charges.

Note that the
\zeta
flux characterizes a soliton with electric charge

, owing to the original coupling to the electromagnetic background
in (

), thus the field
\zeta
is ‘magnetic’. The field

corresponding solitons have magnetic charge

(historically called

2.3.2
Self–duality

Particle-vortex duality in 2+1 dimensions is, roughly speaking,
equivalent to switching the
coupling to the
A
background from electric to magnetic
[
9
,
10
]
. This is
achieved by replacing
A_{\mu}
with the auxiliary
dynamical field
kc_{\mu}
, which itself couples to
A
by a
mixed Chern-Simons term.
The original and dual theories, respectively
Z[A]
and

\widetilde{Z}[A]
, are thus related by

\widetilde{Z}[A]=\int{\cal D}c_{\mu}\,Z[kc_{\mu}]\exp\left(i\frac{1}{2\pi}\int c\,\mathrm{d}A\right),

(2.16)

which is a kind of quantum analog of the Legendre transform
[
4
]
.
Upon insertion of the induced action (
2.12
) for

Z[kc_{\mu}]
and integration, one finds that the loop model is
selfdual, with the following map between couplings, fields and charges

g_{0}\ \leftrightarrow\ \widetilde{g}_{o}=\frac{k^{2}}{g_{0}},\qquad\zeta_{\mu}\ \leftrightarrow\ \widetilde{\zeta}_{\mu}=a_{\mu},\qquad N_{0}\ \leftrightarrow\ \widetilde{N}_{0}=M_{0}.

(2.17)

These relations can be proven by introducing the coupling to
the
kc_{\mu}
field in the action (
2.11
):
integrating over
\zeta_{\mu}
and solving for
c_{\mu}
leads to the expression

\widetilde{S}[a,A]=\frac{g_{0}}{4\pi}\int a_{\mu}\frac{-\delta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}}{\partial}a_{\nu}+\frac{i}{2\pi}\int a\mathrm{d}A\,,

(2.18)

which should be compared to (
2.14
).
The correspondence between the charges
(N_{0},M_{0})
confirms that
the monodromy phase (
2.7
) is selfdual, as expected.

Note that the factor of
k
in the duality transformation
(
2.17
), extending the map considered in the
literature
[
9
]
, is rather natural for charges and monodromies,
which should be independent of the field-theory description.

2.3.3
Phase diagram

The loop model possesses a critical line parameterized by the coupling

g_{0}
, where
g_{0}\geq k
can be chosen owing to its self-duality. This
result was obtained by numerical simulations
[

]
and
is confirmed by Peierls arguments
[

]
. It is
found that the monopole condensation of

[

]
is
absent in the loop model, due to the non-local interaction: one is
left with a Coulomb-like phase, analogous to that occurring in 1+1d XY

Another interesting fact is that the loop model can be shown to
correspond to the large

limit of mixed-dimension

,
involving 2+1d fermions and 3+1d photons
[

]
. This
theory is exactly particle-vortex self-dual and possesses a critical

[

,

27

. The direct
relation with this fermionic theory strongly suggests that 2+1

2.3.4
Solitonic spectrum and conformal invariance

The partition function of the loop model has been obtained on the the
generalized cylinder
S^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, with radius
R
and time
u

[
4
]
.
The conformal map
r=R\exp(u/R)
with flat Euclidean space,
where
r^{2}=(x_{\mu})^{2}
, implies that time translations on the cylinder
correspond to dilatations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Thus, canonical quantization on the
cylinder determines energy levels which corresponds to
scaling dimensions
\Delta
of fields in the conformal theory,
schematically,

Z=\sum_{\Delta}e^{-\beta\Delta/R}.

(2.19)

The quantization requires a Hamiltonian formulation which is
actually nontrivial due to the
non–local nature of the action (
2.11
). It was obtained
by rewriting the loop model as a local theory in four
dimensions (with the extra dimension just a fictitious one),
leading to the result
4
4

4

Clearly, the partition function is computed on
the periodic time interval
L=\beta=1/k_{B}T
, thus the geometry is
actually
S^{2}\times S^{1}
.

\begin{split}Z[S^{2}\times S^{1}]&=Z_{osc}\,Z_{sol}=Z_{osc}\sum_{N_{0},M_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(-\frac{\beta}{R}\Delta_{N_{0},M_{0}}\right),\\
\Delta_{N_{0},M_{0}}&=\lambda\left(\frac{N_{0}^{2}}{g_{0}}+g_{0}\frac{M_{0}^{2}}{k^{2}}\right).\end{split}

(2.20)

In this equation,
Z_{sol}
is obtained by evaluating the classical action on
soliton configurations, leading to the spectrum of scaling dimensions

\Delta_{N_{0},M_{0}}
, and
Z_{osc}
describes fluctuations around
classical solutions.
The soliton dimensions confirm the selfduality of the theory
(
2.17
). The dimensionless parameter
\lambda=1/(MR)
is
left undetermined, being related to the value of the infrared cutoff

M
needed in the calculation. It will be fixed later by computing
correlators of soliton fields (see section
3.3
).
The term
Z_{osc}
has the form
5
5

5

This is actually the square of the generating
function of plane partitions or MacMahon function.

[
4
]

Z_{osc}=\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1-q^{k}\right)^{2k}},\qquad\qquad q=e^{-\beta/R}.

(2.21)

This expression is instructive
because it checks conformal invariance of the theory at the quantum
level: the Casimir energy vanishes, corresponding to no conformal
anomaly in 2+1d, and the dimensions
\Delta
of descendant fields are
integer spaced. These results confirm that the non-local nature of
the loop model causes no problem for conformal invariance.

2.3.5
Topological order

The 3+1d BF theory (
2.2
) on the geometry

D^{3}\times S^{1}
has
k
degenerate ground states (topological order),
as easily obtained by standard methods (see
e.g.

[
18
]
). Therefore,
the partition function of boundary excitations (
2.20
)
should involve
k
corresponding topological sectors.
They can be obtained by writing
M_{0}=\ell+km_{0}
, with
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}

and
m_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, as follows

Z=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}Z_{\ell},\qquad\ Z_{\ell}=Z_{osc}\sum_{N_{0},m_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left[-\frac{\beta\lambda}{R}\left(\frac{N_{0}^{2}}{g_{0}}+g_{0}\left(\frac{\ell}{k}+m_{0}\right)^{2}\right)\right].

(2.22)

A single anyonic sector
Z_{\ell}
, characterized by fractional charge

M_{0}/k=\ell/k+m_{0}
, can be selected by placing an opposite
fractional charge in the interior of the sphere, and requiring integer
total charge in the system. This is the standard choice for the
quantum Hall effect in the geometry of the spatial disk
D^{2}
with a
single boundary circle
S^{1}

[
28
]
.

We remark that the loop model action could be modified by adding
further interactions consistent with conformal invariance; in these
cases, the coupling constant
g_{0}
and dimensions

renormalized. For example, one could introduce three-body interactions
suggested by the

theory at order

the decomposition in
k

property. Many results on bosonization found later only depend on the
topological part of the bulk and boundary actions, and thus are valid
for more general dynamics. In this sense, the simple quadratic
interaction of the loop model can be considered as a reference case.

3
Soliton fields: topology and dynamics

In this section we construct the soliton fields,

Wilson lines (open loops) of the connections

In the last subsection, we shall add the dynamical term in the

The solitonic fields are first obtained in the case of

3.1

3.1.1
Bulk-boundary correspondence for vertex operators

Having discussed the definition of charged operators both in
the bulk and boundary of the QHE system, we can compare

We clearly see that 1+1d vertex operators (

The fact that fermion fields can be written in terms of
Wilson lines of effective gauge fields

It shows that bosonization can be understood in the physical setting

and the bulk-boundary correspondence.

The direct, perturbative derivation of
\braket{\mu\mu^{\dagger}}
is
as follows. The path-integral expression with two independent
Wilson lines (
3.14
) can be written,
using the
\braket{\zeta_{\mu}\zeta_{\nu}}
kernel (

\displaystyle\braket{\mu_{M_{0}}(x)\mu_{M_{0}}^{\dagger}(y)}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\exp\left(-iM_{0}\int^{x}_{x_{0},\gamma}\zeta\right)\exp\left(iM_{0}\int^{y}_{y_{0},\gamma^{\prime}}\zeta\right)\right\rangle=e^{\Lambda},

(3.31)

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{0}M^{2}_{0}}{\pi k^{2}}\int^{x}_{x_{0},\gamma}d\xi^{\mu}\int^{y}_{y_{0},\gamma^{\prime}}d\eta_{\mu}\frac{1}{(\xi-\eta)^{2}}.

(3.32)

The quantity
\Lambda
strongly depends on the shape of the two lines

\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
: on dimensional grounds, it can be estimated

\Lambda\underset{R\gg a}{\sim}-\lambda\frac{R}{a}+c\log\left(\frac{R}{a}\right)+\dots,\qquad\qquad(\lambda,c>0),

(3.33)

where
R,a
are, respectively, the larger and smaller lengths over
which the integrations in (
3.32
) extend, which depend
on the shape of
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
lines.
Actually, once the loop-model interaction is present (
g_{0}\neq 0
),
these contours can
fluctuate. Thus, the correlator (
3.31
) should involve a
summation over all possible shapes, while keeping the end-points
fixed. This is to be contrasted with the topological limit (
g_{0}=0
),
where (
3.31
) is shape independent and the summation can be
neglected.

We recall that the phase diagram of the loop model is actually
two-dimensional owing to the additional Maxwell term

t/M\int(\partial_{\mu}\zeta_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\zeta_{\mu})^{2}

[
4
,
23
]
:
the critical line parameterized by
g_{0}
divides two phases

t\lessgtr 0
where large loops are suppressed or proliferate.
Therefore, at criticality the linear term in (
3.33
) (perimeter
law) should vanish by a compensation between energy and entropy,
the latter originating from the sum over fluctuating loops
included in (
3.31
).
Indeed, the perimeter law is incompatible with conformal invariance.

We evaluate the correlator (
3.31
) by joining the contours
(
x_{0}=y_{0}
) and considering the shortest straight line going between

x,y
. This configuration is dominating in the phase where large loops
are suppressed, slightly off the critical line.
We then compute (
3.32
) for the
gauge-invariant square of the correlator (
3.31
) and obtain,

\displaystyle\langle:\mu_{M_{0}}(x)\mu_{M_{0}}^{\dagger}(x+\varepsilon)::\mu_{M_{0}}(y+\varepsilon)\mu_{M_{0}}^{\dagger}(y):\rangle=\left\langle e^{-iM_{0}\int^{y}_{x}\zeta}\ e^{iM_{0}\int^{y+\varepsilon}_{x+\varepsilon}\zeta}\right\rangle=e^{\Sigma},

(3.34)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Sigma\underset{|x-y|\gg\varepsilon}{=}\frac{g_{0}M^{2}_{0}}{\pi k^{2}}\left(\frac{\pi|x-y|}{\varepsilon}-\log\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

(3.35)

In this calculation,
\varepsilon
is the distance separating the two
parallel
(x,y)
lines, corresponding to the point-splitting of operators.
Assuming that the perimeter term is canceled
by entropy at criticality, we remarkably obtain the
expected conformal correlator (
3.29
) found earlier
from monopole configurations. That derivation, involving point-like
configurations, luckily did not involve entropy arguments.

In conclusion, we have shown that the loop model dynamics implies
power-law correlators for the soliton fields,

\braket{\sigma(x)\sigma^{\dagger}(y)}=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2\Delta_{\sigma}}},\qquad\qquad\braket{\mu(x)\mu^{\dagger}(y)}=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2\Delta_{\mu}}},

(3.36)

with scaling dimensions
\Delta_{\sigma},\Delta_{\mu}

in agreement with the expression of the partition function (
2.20
).

Let us add some remarks.

•

Wilson lines as proxies.

The simpler derivation of
\mu,\sigma
correlators involves the
monopole configurations (
3.29
), (
3.26
), in
which the Wilson line is only used to create localized charges. This
is certainly correct in the topological limit of the theory
(
g_{0}=0)
: for non-vanishing
g_{0}
, additional, chargeless terms
could enter in the definition of the
\mu,\sigma
fields to comply with
conformal invariance. In such a case, Wilson line expressions should
be simply considered as proxies for the actual conformal primary
fields.

•

Unconstrained Wilson loops as topological defects.

We also saw that
\mu,\sigma
correlators correspond
to classes of fluctuating loops having two points
x,y
fixed, which
depend logarithmically on
|x-y|
. They should be
contrasted with closed loops without fixed points, which should describe
topological defects of the conformal theory. As such, they should not
depend on the interaction in the loop model action (
2.11
),
and remain the same as in the topological limit (
g_{0}=0
).
A hint of this fact is that monodromy phases are unaffected
(see (
3.20
)). Furthermore, ‘hard’ defects would
break conformal invariance. In reality, defects may be topological
for
g_{0}\neq 0
by balancing energy and entropy at criticality.
We do not have a proof of this fact, but it is natural to assume that
the conformal theory possesses topological defects.

•

Altogether these results let us conclude that the loop
model is a non–trivial 2+1 dimensional conformal
field theory. It originates in semiclassical limits of
electrodynamics, and it displays several interesting nonperturbative
phenomena in 2+1 dimensions, which can be explicitly analyzed.

4
Fermionic theory on the topological insulator
boundary

4.1
Fermion field

In this section we introduce the field which represents the fermion in
the loop model. In the following, we shall provide
evidences for this proposal and describe the modifications of the theory which are needed for incorporating fermionic
features. These results will achieve a bosonization of 2+1

mentioned earlier. This bosonization presents several aspects

The fermionic field

We are led to propose the following form

(4.8)

We used

(4.9)

The fields

The argument can be clearly made in reverse.

The second needed fact is a general property of

This picture helps to build an intuitive understanding of what is going

After these remarks, we can now discuss the bosonization in

We start from a bosonic field theory with partition function

•

All in all, we are led to the following conclusion. A

We now remark that each

On continuum and discrete formulations.

The

A more rigorous descriptions of the anomalies is discrete:

are higher cup products which do not have clear counterparts in

In the following, we rederive the anomalies in discrete form

The compensating bulk action is, under gauge transformations

S_{bulk}=i\pi\int B\cup B.

(4.22)

We remark that the first two terms in the anomaly (

We remind the main steps involved:

4.3.1
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{(1)}
symmetry and its anomaly

The first step will take advantage of previous results for the toric

We now proceed to gauge this symmetry by coupling the theory to the

The loop model action (

4.5
Time reversal symmetry and the B field

We now discuss how the introduction of the
B
field fits with the
time-reversal symmetry
{\cal T}
of topological insulators.
17
17

17

In this section we turn back to Minkowski space to treat time
reversal.

Since the bulk and boundary action take the original form without

B
once written in terms of tilded variables
(cf. (
4.37
), (
4.60
)),
these can be set to transform as the original fields too. Therefore, we assume

{\cal T}:\ (\tilde{a}_{0},\tilde{a}_{i})\rightarrow(\tilde{a}_{0},-\tilde{a}_{i}),\quad(\tilde{\zeta}_{0},\tilde{\zeta}_{i})\rightarrow(-\tilde{\zeta}_{0},\tilde{\zeta}_{i}),\quad(\tilde{b}_{0i},\tilde{b}_{ij})\rightarrow(-\tilde{b}_{0i},\tilde{b}_{ij}).

(4.61)

The compatibility between transformations of original and tilded variables
is as follows. The map
\mathrm{d}a+B=\mathrm{d}\tilde{a}
is consistent
with time reversal provided that
B
transform as a ’vector’
two–form,

{\cal T}:\;(B_{0i},B_{ij})\rightarrow(B_{0i},-B_{ij}).

(4.62)

The map between
b
and
\tilde{b}
is instead consistent assuming that the pseudo–two–form
b
mixes with
B
under

{\cal T}:b\rightarrow b+4B
.

As usual, the bulk plus boundary action is time reversal invariant,
but the individual actions (
4.37
) and (
4.60
)
are invariant up to the sign of the anomaly terms. The response
actions, respectively (
4.38
) and (
4.57
),
also change sign.

Now we turn to the action of time reversal on physical
states. According to the earlier discussion on bosonization,
the fermionic theory (
4.41
)
has a zero–form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry generated by

\int B
, which is identified with the fermion number symmetry,

(-1)^{F}=e^{i\int_{S^{2}}B}.

(4.63)

As said, this is consistent with the two sectors in the theory
having, respectively, bosonic states (
4.33
), with
\int B=0
,
and fermionic states (
4.34
), with
\int B=\pi
.
For fermionic topological insulators, time reversal transformations obey

{\cal T}^{2}=(-1)^{F}
; therefore, in our description
(
4.63
), we should have

{\cal T}^{2}=e^{i\int_{S^{2}}B}.

(4.64)

The action of time reversal on states consistent with (
4.64
)
is achieved as follows. We consider products of
\sigma
and
\mu
fields
(
3.14
), in their closed loop version,
with allowed values of
(N_{0},M_{0})
in the two sectors of the fermionic

4.42
),

\Phi=e^{-iN_{0}\int\tilde{a}-iM_{0}\int\tilde{\zeta}}.

(4.65)

A naive action of time reversal is obtained by using
the field transformations (
4.61
) in (
4.65
) and
antiunitarity, leading to

{\cal T}\Phi{\cal T}^{-1}\sim e^{-iN_{0}\int\tilde{a}+iM_{0}\int\tilde{\zeta}}.

(4.66)

This cannot be correct, because it obeys
{\cal T}^{2}=1

Therefore, we are led to add a non-trivial
B

factor to the transformation, as follows:

{\cal T}\Phi{\cal T}^{-1}\equiv e^{i\int_{S^{2}}\frac{B}{2}}\,e^{-iN_{0}\int\tilde{a}+iM_{0}\int\tilde{\zeta}}.

(4.67)

We remark that the phase is trivial,
\int B=0
, for all bosonic
states in the even sector of the theory, thus
{\cal T}^{2}=1
.
In the odd sector,
\int B=\pi
, the phase is not vanishing
and furthermore it obeys:

{\cal T}\exp(i\int_{S^{2}}B/2){\cal T}^{-1}=\exp(i\int_{S^{2}}B/2)

(cf. (
4.62
)). We find,

{\cal T}^{2}\Phi{\cal T}^{-2}={\cal T}\left(e^{i\int_{S^{2}}\frac{B}{2}}e^{-iN_{0}\int\tilde{a}+iM_{0}\int\tilde{\zeta}}\right){\cal T}^{-1}=e^{i\int_{S^{2}}B}\Phi=(-1)^{F}\Phi.

(4.68)

In conclusion, the definition (
4.67
) of
time-reversal transformations correctly realizes

{\cal T}^{2}=(-1)^{F}
.

Bosonized fermions and Dirac spinors.

The standard presentation of 2+1d Dirac fields in terms
of bi-spinors and Pauli matrices is a reducible representation
of the Poincaré group. Indeed, spin is a pseudoscalar and irreducible
representations are one dimensional

[
45
,
46
]

the spinors decompose in two irreducible one-dimensional representations

are solutions of the Dirac equation.
Time reversal transformations are realized on spinors by

\eta
is a phase,
\sigma_{2}
a Pauli matrix
and

map one into another under

In the bosonization language of this paper, these two one-dimensional
irreducible representations should be identified with the fermion
(
4.50

(
4.67

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we developed the analysis of the effective field theory
of 3+1 dimensional topological insulators, based on the bulk BF action

[
3
]
and the boundary loop model providing the dynamics
for surface excitations
[
4
]
.
Among the interesting features of this model motivating our study, we
remark the presence of a critical line with conformal invariance, and of
solitonic states with electric and magnetic charges
N_{0}
and
M_{0}
,
whose spectrum of scaling dimensions is known exactly.

We showed that the solitonic local operators
\sigma(x),\mu(x)

can be represented by open Wilson lines of the two gauge fields
a,\zeta
,
but also in terms of monopoles of the dual field,
employing results of Refs.
[
6
,
17
,
5
]
.
These identifications are exact in the topological limit of the
loop model, where the interaction strength
g_{0}
vanishes.
For
g_{0}\neq 0
, we computed the soliton correlation functions and verified their conformal invariance, with
scaling dimensions in agreement with the results of the partition function.
Actually, the derivation is simpler in terms of the dual monopoles,
while that using Wilson lines requires some assumptions on the
dynamics of fluctuating loops at criticality.
We then defined the fermion field
\psi
as the product
\sigma\mu
with
charges
(N_{0},M_{0})=(1,1/2)
.

The bosonization of 2+1 dimensional fermions not only requires
the identification of the fermion field, but also of other properties, such as the fermion number
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry
(-1)^{F}
,
the spin sectors and the time reversal symmetry obeying

{\cal T}^{2}=(-1)^{F}
. These features have been implemented in the bosonic loop model by using
the construction of
Refs.
[
15
,
16
]
.
This was originally formulated for bulk topological theories
on triangulated manifolds, but can be extended to the continuum
and for boundary theories, which include a topological part
and an interaction term (for
g_{0}\neq 0
).

The map leading from the bosonic to the fermion theory
starts from the identification of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{(1)}

one-form symmetry generated by fermion loops, and its universal
anomaly. Once this anomaly is canceled by the bulk, the symmetry
is gauged by a coupling to a dynamical
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
flat two-form field
B
.
The following results follows:

•

The
B
field implements the half-integer magnetic charge of the fermion, and
divides the bosonic spectrum into sectors. The
B
field also generates
the fermion number symmetry and enters in the definition of time reversal
transformations.

•

The gauging of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{(1)}
symmetry determines the spin sectors
of the fermionic theory. Furthermore, physical states are selected
by the requirement of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{(1)}
gauge invariance.

The new ingredients of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{(1)}
gauge symmetry and
B
field
are very important for the correct definition of a fermionic theory and thus fermionic topological phases. The standard effective theory based on
the BF theory is not sufficient and only applies to bosonic topological phases.
Actually, we showed that two BF theories, with
k=1
and
k=2
couplings,
are mapped into the same fermionic theory.

The fact that the BF theory by itself is not sufficient for describing a fermionic topological phase is actually expected on general grounds: it is believed that while bosonic phases have a TQFT effective description, the low energy description of a fermionic phase is a spin TQFT
[
1
,
16
,
42
]
. In this work, we made explicit this difference by using bosonization methods to show how the theory partition function (and thus the physical states) changes under the fermionization map.

A related aspect is the interplay between fermionization of
bulk and boundary theories, needed for accommodating respective
fermionic excitations, higher-form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetries
and anomalies. In this work, we studied the map for
the boundary theory and did not delve into the proper definition of
bulk fermions.

The 2+1 bosonization discussed in this work can be further
investigated by analyzing other aspects of the fermionic theory and
other geometries. The bosonization of 3+1 dimensional relativistic
fermions is also very interesting for its potential application to
nonperturbative physics of fundamental interactions, and can
be similarly formulated by considering 4+1d
topological theories and topological insulators
[
49
]
.

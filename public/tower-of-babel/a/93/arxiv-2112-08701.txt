High dimensional logistic entropy clustering
Edouard GENETAY, Adrien SAUMARD, R’emi COULAUD

Abstract




Minimization of the (regularized) entropy of classification probabilities
is a versatile class of discriminative clustering methods. The classification
probabilities are usually defined through the use of some classical
losses from supervised classification and the point is to avoid modelisation of the full data distribution by just optimizing the law of the labels conditioned on
the observations. We give the first theoretical study of such methods,
by specializing to logistic classification probabilities. We prove
that if the observations are generated from a two-component isotropic
Gaussian mixture, then minimizing the entropy risk over a Euclidean
ball indeed allows to identify the separation vector of the mixture.
Furthermore, if this separation vector is sparse, then penalizing
the empirical risk by a
\ell_{1}
-regularization term allows to
infer the separation in a high-dimensional space and to recover its
support, at standard rates of sparsity problems. Our approach is based
on the local convexity of the logistic entropy risk, that occurs if
the separation vector is large enough, with a condition on its norm
that is independent from the space dimension. This local convexity
property also guarantees fast rates in a classical, low-dimensional
setting.

Introduction

The clustering problem can be described as follows: given a measurable
space
\mathcal{X}
, a sample
(X_{1},...,X_{n})\in\mathcal{X}^{n}
,
and an integer
K\geq 2

In particular, to each data

.
If the function

is deterministic, then the task is termed “hard
clustering”. If the function
Y
is random, the distribution of
the labels
Y(x)
, for
x\in\mathcal{X}
, being characterized by
the uplets
(\mathbb{P}(Y(x)=1),...,\mathbb{P}(Y(x)=K))
, then the
clustering task is said to be “soft”. In the soft clustering case,
a common approach - called the modelling approach - is to model the
distribution of the data, typically as a mixture distribution, and
to directly relate the probabilities
(\mathbb{P}(Y(x)=1),...,\mathbb{P}(Y(x)=K))

to the parameters of the mixture
[
6
]
. One can then
reduce to a hard clustering by assigning each point

to the maximizer
of classification probabilities (or choose one at random amongst the
maximizers if it is non-unique). Hard clustering algorithms include
the celebrated K-means
[
29
,
43
,
31
]
,
hierachical clustering
[
23
]
, spectral clustering
[
36
]

among others.

Particularly developed in the machine learning community for its flexibility
when addressing complex data, the so-called “discriminative approach”
to clustering amounts to model the classification probabilities
(\mathbb{P}(Y(x)=1),...,\mathbb{P}(Y(x)=K))
,
which can be understood as the conditional probabilities of the labels
with respect to the position
x
. Proceeding this way indeed avoids
the modelling of the whole distribution of data and often reduces
to encode in the classification probabilities, the frontiers separating
the clusters. In general, this is done through the use of classical
learning losses such as the logistic, the Hinge or the Conditional
Random Fields loss
[
14
,
20
]
.
More formally, one puts the constraint of
\mathbb{P}(Y(x)=k)
,

,
being proportional to
\exp(\ell(\beta_{k},x))
, for a vector
\beta_{k}

and a loss
\ell

for some feature map

\varphi

In addition, these losses were primarily introduced for supervised
learning and in order to transfer them to the unsupervised setting,
one has to define what would be a desirable (unobserved) label. Arguably,
when classifying data, one would prefer to be as sure as possible
of its cluster choice. This is equivalent to saying that the maximum
of classification probabilities would be as close to one as possible.
Hence, a natural criterion to infer a labelling function, would be
to define

The associated theoretical target is

(3)

Often, one has to restrict the search among vectors

. The empirical distribution

In this article

3
Minimising the risk over a
L_{2}
-ball

Recall that

\beta_{0}\in\underset{\beta\in B_{2}\left(0,R\right)}{\arg\min}\left\{\mathcal{R}\left(\beta\right)\right\},

where the radius
R
will be fixed later. Let us investigate the
geometry of the risk
\mathcal{R}
defined by the logistic entropy.

Proposition 1
.

The risk is symmetric,
\mathcal{R}\left(\beta\right)=\mathcal{R}\left(-\beta\right)
,
and the risk value
\mathcal{R}\left(\beta\right)
with
\left\|\beta\right\|_{2}=r

fixed is decreasing with respect to
\left|\beta^{t}a\right|
.

Proposition
1
states that the risk
is symmetric around zero, and that its values on a sphere are increasing
with respect to the distance to the line
\mathbb{R}a
. Its proof
can be found in Section
5.1
.

Proposition 2
.

The function
\lambda\mapsto\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda\beta\right)

is decreasing for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

In Proposition
2
, it is proved that
the risk is decreasing on semi-lines starting at zero. For a proof
of this result, see Section
5.1
. From
Propositions
1
and
2
,
we characterize the minimizers of the risk over a
L_{2}
-ball.

Corollary 3
.

The minimum of
\mathcal{R}\left(\beta\right)

on
B_{2}\left(0,R\right)
is reached at
\pm\beta_{0}
where
\beta_{0}:=Ra/\left\|a\right\|_{2}
.

From Corollary
3
, we deduce
that estimating
\beta_{0}
or its opposite directly gives an estimation
of the best labelling function
Y_{*}
for our clustering problem.
A look at the proof of Propositions
1

and
2
shows that these results, and
hence Corollary
3
, hold true
in the more general setting where the distribution of
Z
is only
assumed to be spherically symmetric.

In order to tackle the estimation of a sparse separation vector

,
the following property will be helpful.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\beta_{0}=Ra/\left\|a\right\|_{2}

and let
\Lambda_{min}
be the smallest eigenvalue of the Hessian

d_{\beta_{0}}^{2}\mathcal{R}
at
\beta_{0}
. Take a parameter

\nu=0.95
,

R\geq\sqrt{x_{1}+0.08}
(

and assume that
\left\|a\right\|_{2}\geq 2R
, then

\Lambda_{min}\geq\frac{\nu}{4}\left(\Phi^{c}\left(\left\|a\right\|_{2}-\frac{x_{1}}{R}\right)-\Phi^{c}\left(\left\|a\right\|_{2}+\frac{x_{1}}{R}\right)\right).

Theorem
4
states that if the radius
R
and
the mean vector

are sufficiently large, then the risk defined
by the logistic entropy is locally strongly convex around
\beta_{0}
.
The risk is not convex over the whole
L_{2}
-ball

,
but this local convexity is very convenient, since it allows to deduce

Lemma 5

Set
\beta_{0}
the unique minimum of

\mathcal{R}\left(\cdot\right)
on
\Psi_{U}:=\left\{\beta\in B_{2}\left(0,R\right):\beta^{t}U>0\right\}

is a random variable uniformly distributed on the unit

-ball. Assume that

and

We have

\underset{\beta\in\Psi_{U}}{\inf}\frac{\mathcal{E}\left(\beta,\beta_{0}\right)}{\left\|\beta-\beta_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}}\geq c_{0}>0

c_{0}=L_{0}\frac{\left(\left\|a\right\|_{2}-R\right)^{6}}{\left\|a\right\|_{2}^{8}R^{2}}.\exp\left(-\left\|a\right\|_{2}R-2R^{2}\right)

for a numerical constant

(

holds).

The quadratic growth of the excess risk stated in Lemma

will turn out to be a keystone to prove the oracle inequality for
the excess risk of the minimizer of empirical risk regularized by
a
\ell_{1}
penalty (see Section

The proof of Lemma

is postponed to Section

4
An oracle inequality in high dimension

Recall that
\beta_{0}=Ra/\left\|a\right\|_{2}
is a minimizer
of the risk over the
L_{2}
-ball of radius
R
:
\beta_{0}\in\underset{\beta\in B_{2}(0,R)}{\arg\min}\mathcal{R}\left(\beta\right)

Set

and where

is a random variable uniformly distributed on the unit

\mathbb{P}(\beta_{0}^{t}U=0)=0

or its opposite belongs to

. Without
loss of generality, we assume that

and analyze

We investigate the behavior of the following estimator,

\hat{\beta}:=\underset{\beta\in\Psi_{U}}{\arg\min}\left\{\mathcal{R}_{n}\left(\beta\right)+\lambda\left\|\beta\right\|_{1}\right\}.

(4)

Set also the empirical process
V_{n}\left(\beta\right):=\left(P_{n}-P\right)\left(\rho_{\beta}\right)
.
For some

define the event

\mathcal{T}:=\left\{\sup_{\beta\in B_{2}\left(0,R\right)}\frac{\left|V_{n}\left(\beta\right)-V_{n}\left(\beta_{0}\right)\right|}{\left\|\beta-\beta_{0}\right\|_{1}\lor\lambda_{0}}\leq 2T\lambda_{0}\right\},

where

>0 is to be fixed in the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

Fix

. Assume that
\beta_{0}
-
or equivalently

-sparse, for some integer

,

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(1+e^{Z^{t}\beta}\right)-\frac{Z^{t}\beta e^{Z^{t}\beta}}{1+e^{Z^{t}\beta}}\right].

. The criterion
can be seen as a function of

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\left(\beta\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(1+e^{\mu+rN}\right)-\frac{\left(\mu+rN\right)e^{\mu+rN}}{1+e^{\mu+rN}}\right]=:\mathcal{R}\left(\mu,r\right).

(7)

Its derivative with respect to
\mu
is:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{R}\left(\mu,r\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\mu}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(1+e^{\mu+rN}\right)-\frac{\left(\mu+rN\right)e^{\mu+rN}}{1+e^{\mu+rN}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{d}{d\mu}\log\left(1+e^{\mu+rN}\right)-\frac{d}{d\mu}\frac{\left(\mu+rN\right)e^{\mu+rN}}{1+e^{\mu+rN}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{e^{\mu+rN}}{1+e^{\mu+rN}}-\left(\frac{e^{\mu+rN}}{1+e^{\mu+rN}}+\frac{\left(\mu+rN\right)e^{\mu+rN}}{1+e^{\mu+rN}}+\left(\mu+rN\right)e^{\mu+rN}\frac{-e^{\mu+rN}}{\left(1+e^{\mu+rN}\right)^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[-\frac{\left(\mu+rN\right)e^{\mu+rN}}{1+e^{\mu+rN}}+\left(\mu+rN\right)e^{\mu+rN}\frac{e^{\mu+rN}}{\left(1+e^{\mu+rN}\right)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\left(\mu+rN\right)e^{\mu+rN}}{1+e^{\mu+rN}}\left(\frac{e^{\mu+rN}}{1+e^{\mu+rN}}-1\right)\right]

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{R}\left(\mu,r\right)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\left(\mu+rN\right)e^{\mu+rN}}{\left(1+e^{\mu+rN}\right)^{2}}\right].

Let us define

has the sign of
\mu
,
which gives the result.
∎

Proof of Lemma

\mathcal{R}

\beta_{0}

-\beta_{0}

\Psi_{U}=\left\{\beta\in B_{2}\left(0,R\right):\beta^{t}U>0\right\},

U

-ball. The probability that

0

then with probability
1
we have

and
there is therefore only one vector among
\beta_{0}
and
-\beta_{0}

that satisfies
\beta_{0}^{t}U>0
. We call
\beta_{0}
the vector
satisfying both
\mathcal{R}\left(\beta_{0}\right)
is the minimum
of
\mathcal{R}\left(\cdot\right)

\beta_{0}^{t}U>0
.

Take
\beta\in\Psi_{U}
and let
\varepsilon\in\left(0,R\right)
,
we are about to control
\mathcal{E}\left(\beta,\beta_{0}\right)

B_{2}\left(\beta_{0},\varepsilon\right)

\left\{\nu\in B_{2}\left(0,R\right):\beta_{0}^{t}\nu>0\right\}\setminus B_{2}\left(\beta_{0},\varepsilon\right)

\Psi_{U}
. To bypass this

\mathcal{R}

to
0

\beta\notin\left\{\nu\in B_{2}\left(0,R\right):\beta_{0}^{t}\nu>0\right\}

\mathcal{E}\left(\beta,\beta_{0}\right)=\mathcal{E}\left(-\beta,\beta_{0}\right)

\mathcal{E}\left(\cdot,\beta_{0}\right)

\Psi_{U}

Case 1

Lemma 17
.

\forall x\geq 3
,
\alpha(x)-\varphi(x)\geq xe^{-x}\left(\frac{x_{1}+0.08-1}{x}-4e^{-x}\right)

\alpha-\varphi

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{-2x}}{\left(1+e^{-x}\right)^{3}}\left(1+e^{x}+x-xe^{x}\right)-\left(x-x_{1}-0.08\right)e^{-x}

\displaystyle=\frac{xe^{-2x}}{\left(1+e^{-x}\right)^{3}}\left(e^{x}-\frac{e^{x}}{x}-1-\frac{1}{x}\right)-x\left(1-\frac{x_{1}+0.08}{x}\right)e^{-x}

\displaystyle=xe^{-x}\left[\frac{e^{-x}}{\left(1+e^{-x}\right)^{3}}\left(e^{x}-\frac{e^{x}}{x}-1-\frac{1}{x}\right)-\left(1-\frac{x_{1}+0.08}{x}\right)\right]

and
\delta=x_{1}+0.08
.

\displaystyle\alpha(x)-\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=xe^{-x}\left[\left(1-3e^{-x}+R(x)\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{x}-e^{-x}-\frac{e^{-x}}{x}\right)-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{x}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=xe^{-x}\left[\left(-1+\delta\right)\frac{1}{x}+\left(-1-3\right)e^{-x}+\left(-1+3\right)\frac{e^{-x}}{x}+3e^{-2x}+3\frac{e^{-2x}}{x}+R(x)\left(1-\frac{1}{x}-e^{-x}-\frac{e^{-x}}{x}\right)\right]

Lemma 32
.

\forall\beta\in B_{2}\left(0,R\right)
,
if
\beta_{0}\in\partial B_{2}\left(0,R\right)
then
\left\langle\beta_{0}-\beta,\beta_{0}\right\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\beta-\beta_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}
.

Proof.

Decompose
\beta
as
\beta_{\perp}+\beta_{\parallel}
on
\beta_{0}^{\perp}\oplus Vect\left(\beta_{0}\right)

and note that
\exists\lambda_{\beta}\in[-1,1],\beta_{\parallel}=\lambda_{\beta}\beta_{0}
.
We have

\frac{\left\langle\beta_{0}-\beta,\beta_{0}\right\rangle}{\left\|\beta-\beta_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}}=\frac{\left\langle\beta_{0}-\beta_{\parallel},\beta_{0}\right\rangle}{\left\|\beta_{\perp}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\beta_{\parallel}-\beta_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}}=\frac{\left(1-\lambda_{\beta}\right)R^{2}}{\left\|\beta_{\perp}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left(1-\lambda_{\beta}\right)^{2}R^{2}}

Furthermore, we have
\left\|\beta\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq R^{2}

and by pythagora’s theorem
\left\|\beta_{\perp}\right\|_{2}^{2}\in\left[0,R^{2}-\lambda_{\beta}^{2}R^{2}\right]
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\beta_{0}-\beta,\beta_{0}\right\rangle}{\left\|\beta-\beta_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(1-\lambda_{\beta}\right)R^{2}}{\left(1-\lambda_{\beta}^{2}\right)R^{2}+\left(1-\lambda_{\beta}\right)^{2}R^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\lambda_{\beta}}{1-\lambda_{\beta}^{2}+\left(1-2\lambda_{\beta}+\lambda_{\beta}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}.

∎

Lemma 38
.

With
G\left(X\right):=\left\|X\right\|_{\infty}

and
a
and
X
defined in the section Notations: if
M_{n}=\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{2\log d}+\sqrt{2\log n}

then

\mathbb{E}\left(G\left(X\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right)\leq 2\left(M_{n}+1\right)\frac{e^{-2\sqrt{\log d\log\left(1+n\right)}}}{n}

and

\mathbb{E}\left(G\left(X\right)^{2}I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right)\leq 2\left(M_{n}^{2}+\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}+1\right)\frac{e^{-2\sqrt{\log d\log\left(1+n\right)}}}{n}.

Proof.

First note that for
y\geq\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}
,

\displaystyle P\left(G\left(X\right)>y\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\max_{j}\left|Z_{j}\right|>y-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\leq dP\left(\left|Z_{1}\right|>y-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2de^{-\frac{\left(y-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}.

Take now
M\geq\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}+1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(G\left(X\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}^{\infty}y\frac{dP\left(G\left(X\right)>y\right)}{dy}dy

\displaystyle=\left[-yP\left(G\left(X\right)>y\right)\right]_{M}^{\infty}+\int_{M}^{\infty}1\times P\left(G\left(X\right)>y\right)dy

\displaystyle\leq 2Mde^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}+\int_{M}^{\infty}\left(y-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)\times P\left(G\left(X\right)>y\right)dy

\displaystyle=2Mde^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}+2\int_{M}^{\infty}\left(y-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)\times de^{-\frac{\left(y-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}dy

\displaystyle=2Mde^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}+2\left[-de^{-\frac{\left(y-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}\right]_{M}^{\infty}

\displaystyle=2Mde^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}+2de^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle=2\left(M+1\right)de^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(G\left(X\right)^{2}I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}^{\infty}y^{2}\frac{dP\left(G\left(X\right)>y\right)}{dy}dy

\displaystyle=\left[-y^{2}P\left(G\left(X\right)>y\right)\right]_{M}^{\infty}+\int_{M}^{\infty}y\times P\left(G\left(X\right)>y\right)dy

\displaystyle=2M^{2}de^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}+\int_{M}^{\infty}\left(y-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)\times P\left(G\left(X\right)>y\right)dy+\int_{M}^{\infty}\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\times P\left(G\left(X\right)>y\right)dy

\displaystyle\leq 2M^{2}de^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}+2de^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}+2\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\int_{M}^{\infty}\underset{\geq 1}{\underbrace{\left(y-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)}}\times de^{-\frac{\left(y-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}dy

\displaystyle\leq M^{2}de^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}+de^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}+2\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}de^{-\frac{\left(M-\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(M_{n}+1\right)de^{-\frac{\left(\sqrt{2\log d}+\sqrt{2\log\left(1+n\right)}\right)^{2}}{2}}

Lemma 39
.

Assume that
\left\|a\right\|_{2}\geq 2R\approx 2.548
.
Set

F\left(X\right):=G\left(X\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}+\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right],

. Moreover,
take the following constants:
M_{n}:=\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{2\log d}+\sqrt{2\log\left(1+n\right)}
,

\lambda_{0}:=3LM_{n}n^{-1/2}\left(5\sqrt{3\log\left(2d\right)}\log n+4\right)
.
It holds:
\forall T>0
,

P\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F\left(X^{(i)}\right)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}T}{L}\right)\leq 4\frac{L^{2}}{\lambda_{0}^{2}T^{2}}\frac{M_{n}^{2}+\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}+1}{n^{2}}.

Proof.

Note that with our choice of
\lambda_{0}
, we have by Lemma
38
:
\lambda_{0}T/L\geq 2\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right]
.
Hence,

\displaystyle P\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F\left(X^{(i)}\right)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}T}{L}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(G\left(X^{(i)}\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X^{(i)}\right)>M_{n}\right\}}+\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right]\right)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}T}{L}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(G\left(X^{(i)}\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X^{(i)}\right)>M_{n}\right\}}-\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right]\right)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}T}{L}-2\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{V}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}G\left(X^{(i)}\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X^{(i)}\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right)}{\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}T}{L}-2\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right]\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(G\left(X\right)^{2}I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right)}{n\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}T}{L}-2\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right]\right)^{2}}.

From Lemma
38
, we get

\displaystyle P\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F\left(X^{(i)}\right)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}T}{L}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(G\left(X\right)^{2}I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right)}{n\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}T}{L}-2\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X\right)I_{\left\{G\left(X\right)>M_{n}\right\}}\right]\right)^{2}}.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\left(M_{n}^{2}+\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}+1\right)\frac{e^{-2\sqrt{\log d\log\left(1+n\right)}}}{n}}{n\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}T}{L}-4\left(M_{n}+1\right)\frac{e^{-2\sqrt{\log d\log\left(1+n\right)}}}{n}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2L^{2}\frac{M_{n}^{2}+\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}+1}{n^{2}\lambda_{0}^{2}T^{2}}\frac{e^{-2\sqrt{\log d\log\left(1+n\right)}}}{\left(1-4L\frac{M_{n}+1}{n\lambda_{0}T}e^{-2\sqrt{\log d\log\left(1+n\right)}}\right)^{2}}.

n\geq 2

\displaystyle L\frac{M_{n}+1}{n\lambda_{0}T}e^{-2\sqrt{\log d\log\left(1+n\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq L\frac{M_{n}+1}{n\lambda_{0}}

\displaystyle=L\frac{M_{n}+1}{nT.\frac{3LM_{n}\left(5\sqrt{3\log\left(2d\right)}\log n+4\right)}{\sqrt{n}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\frac{1}{M_{n}}}{3\sqrt{n}T\left(5\sqrt{3\log\left(2d\right)}\log n+4\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\frac{1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\log 3}}}{\sqrt{2}\left(5\sqrt{3\log 2}.\log 2+4\right)}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{8}.

Finally, we conclude that

\displaystyle P\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F\left(X^{(i)}\right)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}T}{L}\right)

\displaystyle<4L^{2}\frac{M_{n}^{2}+\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}+1}{n^{2}\lambda_{0}^{2}T^{2}}.

∎

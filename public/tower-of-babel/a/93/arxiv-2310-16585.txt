(non)-Matching and (non)-periodicity for ( N , α ) (N,\alpha) -expansions
Cor Kraaikamp and Niels Langeveld

Abstract
. Recently a new class of continued fraction algorithms, the ( N , α CLOSE (N,\alpha )-expansions, was introduced in [ KL17 ] for each N ∈ ℕ N\in\mathbb{N} , N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 and α ∈ ( 0 , N − 1 ] \alpha\in(0,\sqrt{N}-1] . Each of these continued fraction algorithms has only finitely many possible digits. These ( N , α ) (N,\alpha) -expansions ‘behave’ very different from many other (classical) continued fraction algorithms; see also [ Nak81 , dJKN22 , dJK23 , CK ] for examples and results. In this paper we will show that when all digits in the digit set are co-prime with N N , which occurs in specified intervals of the parameter space, something extraordinary happens. Rational numbers and certain quadratic irrationals will not have a periodic expansion. Furthermore, there are no matching intervals in these regions. This contrasts sharply with the regular continued fraction and more classical parameterised continued fraction algorithms, for which often matching is shown to hold for almost every parameter. On the other hand, for α \alpha small enough, all rationals have an eventually periodic expansion with period 1. This happens for all α \alpha when N = 2 N=2 . We also find infinitely many matching intervals for N = 2 N=2 , as well as rationals that are not contained in any matching interval.

Starting with
[
CMPT10
]

Definition 1

We say that (stable) matching holds for

For each of the above mentioned families, when

1.1.
Periodicity of rational numbers for small
\alpha

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
0<\alpha\leq\xi_{N}-1
, where
\xi_{N}
is a positive number such that
\xi_{N}=\frac{N}{N-2+\xi_{N}}

and let
t_{0},s_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
be such, that
{\rm gcd}\{t_{0},s_{0}\}=1
and
t_{0}/s_{0}\in[\alpha,\alpha+1]
. Set for
k\geq 1
,

\frac{t_{k}}{s_{k}}=T_{N,\alpha}^{k}\left(\tfrac{t_{0}}{s_{0}}\right),

where
t_{k},s_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\rm gcd}\{t_{k},s_{k}\}=1
. Then there exists an
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
t_{n}=s_{n}=1
, and thus that the
(N,\alpha
)-expansion of
t_{0}/s_{0}
is (eventually) periodic, with period-length 1, and where the period consists of only the digit
N-1
:

\frac{t_{0}}{s_{0}}=[0;d_{1},\dots,d_{n},\overline{N-1}]_{N};

(as usual the bar indicates the period).

Proof.

Note that
\xi_{N}
is a quadratic irrational (as
N^{2}+4\neq\Box
) and that
1<\xi_{N}=\frac{-(N-2)+\sqrt{N^{2}+4}}{2}<2
. So if
0<\alpha\leq\xi_{N}-1
we have that
0<\alpha<1
. In case
t_{0}/s_{0}=1
and
{\rm gcd}\{t_{0},s_{0}\}=1
(and thus that
t_{0}=1=s_{0}
), we see that
T_{N,\alpha}(1)=\tfrac{N}{1}-(N-1)=1
, and therefore
1=[0;\overline{N-1}]_{N}
. We will consider the cases
\tfrac{t_{0}}{s_{0}}\in[\alpha,1)
and
\tfrac{t_{0}}{s_{0}}\in(1,\alpha+1)
separately.

In case
\tfrac{t_{0}}{s_{0}}\in[\alpha,1)
we find that

\tfrac{t_{1}}{s_{1}}=T_{N,\alpha}(\tfrac{t_{0}}{s_{0}})=\frac{Ns_{0}-d_{1}t_{0}}{t_{0}},

and since
{\rm gcd}\{t_{1},s_{1}\}=1
,
s_{1}|t_{0}
, and
t_{0}/s_{0}<1
, we find that

s_{1}\leq t_{0}<s_{0}.

By definition of
\xi_{N}
we see that one of the
N
-expansions of
\xi_{N}
is given by:

\xi_{N}=\frac{N}{\displaystyle N-2+\frac{N}{\displaystyle N-2+\frac{N}{\displaystyle N-2+\ddots}}}=[0;\overline{N-2}]_{N}.

Hence if
\alpha=\xi_{N}-1
, we see that
T_{\alpha}(\alpha+1)=N/\xi_{N}-(N-1)=\alpha
, and therefore for all
0<\alpha\leq\xi_{N}-1
all partial quotients must be at least
N-1
.

So in case
\tfrac{t_{0}}{s_{0}}\in(1,\alpha+1)
we have that
d_{1}=N-1
, since
1<\tfrac{t_{0}}{s_{0}}<\xi_{N}
, and therefore that

(8)

\frac{t_{1}}{s_{1}}=T_{N,\alpha}(\tfrac{t_{0}}{s_{0}})=\frac{Ns_{0}-(N-1)t_{0}}{t_{0}}.

Also note that
\tfrac{t_{1}}{s_{1}}<1
, since for every
x\in(1,\alpha+1)
we have that
T_{N,\alpha}(x)<T_{N,\alpha}(1)=1
. Once more applying
T_{\alpha}
yields that:

\frac{t_{2}}{s_{2}}=T_{N,\alpha}^{2}(\tfrac{t_{0}}{s_{0}})=\frac{Nt_{0}-d_{2}(Ns_{0}-(N-1)t_{0})}{Ns_{0}-(N-1)t_{0}},

and since
{\rm gcd}\{t_{2},s_{2}\}=1
and

, we find that

s_{2}\leq Ns_{0}-(N-1)t_{0}<s_{0}.

Since

is a sequence of positive integers, and
s_{k}>s_{k+1}
in case
\tfrac{t_{k}}{s_{k}}\in[\alpha,1)
, and
s_{k}>s_{k+2}
in case

, there must exist a positive integer

we must have that

, and the expansion of

is periodic from that point on, with period length 1 and partial quotient

.

1.2.
Quadratic irrationals

Just like for the regular continued fraction expansion, quadratic irrationals

\displaystyle A_{0}\left(\frac{N}{x_{1}+d_{1}}\right)^{2}+B_{0}\left(\frac{N}{x_{1}+d_{1}}\right)+C_{0}

\displaystyle=0

(11)

\displaystyle C_{0}x_{1}^{2}+(NB_{0}+2d_{1}C_{0})x_{1}+N^{2}A_{0}+NB_{0}d_{1}+C_{0}d_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

where (

. When setting

we find that

is a solution to

A_{n}x_{n}^{2}+B_{n}x_{n}+C_{n}=0

where for

(13)

\displaystyle A_{n+1}=C_{n}

(14)

\displaystyle B_{n+1}=NB_{n}+2d_{n+1}C_{n}

(15)

\displaystyle C_{n+1}=N^{2}A_{n}+NB_{n}d_{n+1}+C_{n}d_{n+1}^{2}

For the determinant we calculate

\displaystyle B_{n+1}^{2}-4A_{n+1}C_{n+1}

\displaystyle=(NB_{n}+2d_{n+1}C_{n})^{2}-4C_{n}(N^{2}A_{n}+NB_{n}d_{n+1}+C_{n}d_{n+1}^{2})

\displaystyle=N^{2}(B_{n}^{2}-4A_{n}C_{n}).

Using this iteratively we find

Remark 1
.

In Proposition

Discretized sum-product type problems: Energy variants and Applications
Quy Pham, Thang Pham, Chun-Yen Shen

Abstract
In this paper, we provide estimates for the additive discretized energy of ∑ c ∈ C | { ( a 1 , a 2 , b 1 , b 2 ) ∈ A 2 × B 2 : | ( a 1 + c ​ b 1 ) − ( a 2 + c ​ b 2 ) | ≤ δ } | δ , \sum_{c\in C}|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A^{2}\times B^{2}:|(a_{1}+cb_{1})-(a_{2}+cb_{2})|\leq\delta\}|_{\delta}, that depend on non-concentration conditions of the sets. Our proof follows the Guth-Katz-Zahl approach (2021) with appropriate changes along the way clarifying and optimizing many of the steps. Several applications will also be discussed. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 68R05, 11B75

Introduction

Let
\delta,\sigma\in(0,1)
. A set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is called
\delta
-discretized if it is a union of closed intervals of length
\delta
. A
\delta
-discretized set
A
is called a
(\delta,\sigma)
-set if
|A|\approx\delta^{1-\sigma}
and it satisfies the non-concentration condition
|A\cap I|\lesssim|I|^{\sigma}|A|
for all intervals
I
. Roughly speaking, we can consider a
(\delta,\sigma)
-set as a discrete analogue of a set of Hausdorff dimension
\sigma
.

Bourgain
[
2
]
proved that any
(\delta,\sigma)
-set cannot be approximately closed under both addition and multiplication. More precisely, if
A\subset[1,2]
is a
(\delta,\sigma)
-set, then there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(\sigma)>0
such that

\max\left\{|A+A|,|A\cdot A|\right\}\gtrsim\delta^{-\epsilon}|A|.

(1)

Here by
|X|
, we mean the Lebesgue measure of
X
. This result settles a conjecture of Katz and Tao in
[
8
]
for
\sigma=1/2
. The recent work of Guth, Katz, and Zahl
[
6
]
provides a new proof of (
1
) with explicit exponent
\epsilon
, namely, the estimate (
1
) holds for any
0<\epsilon<\frac{\sigma(1-\sigma)}{4(7+3\sigma)}
.

In this paper, we explore more deeper properties of discretized sum-product type problems with an emphasis on energy variants and applications of the
A+cB
problem. Let us first start with the following theorem of Bourgain in
[
2
]
. Recall that a set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is called
\delta
-separated if every two elements in
A
have distance greater than
\delta
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\gamma,\eta>0
, there exist
\epsilon_{0},\epsilon>0
, depending only on
\alpha,\gamma,\eta
, such that the following holds for all sufficiently small
\delta>0
.

Let
\nu
be a probability measure on
[0,1]
satisfying
\nu(B(x,r))\leq r^{\gamma}
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\delta<r\leq\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
. Let additionally
A\subset[0,1]
be a
\delta
-separated set with
|A|\geq\delta^{-\alpha}
, which also satisfies the non-concentration condition
|A\cap B(x,r)|\leq r^{\eta}|A|
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\delta\leq r\leq\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
.

Then, there exists a point
c\in\text{spt}(\nu)
such that

|A+cA|_{\delta}\geq\delta^{-\epsilon}|A|.

Here
|\cdot|_{\delta}
refers to the
\delta
-covering number of
A
, namely the size of the smallest covering of
A
by intervals of length
\delta
.

In the above theorem,
c\in\text{spt}(\nu)
. If
C
is a
\delta
-separated set satisfying
|C\cap B(x,r)|\leq r^{\gamma}|C|
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\delta\leq r\leq\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
, we choose the uniformly distributed probability measure
\nu
on the
\delta
-neighbourhood of
C
such that
\nu(B(x,r))\lesssim r^{\gamma}
, namely,
\nu(X)=\frac{|X\cap C(\delta)|}{|C|_{\delta}}.

The theorem above tells us that there exists
c\in\text{spt}(\nu)
such that

|A+cA|_{\delta}\geq\delta^{-\epsilon}|A|.

This implies that there exists
c\in C
with
|A+cA|_{\delta}\geq\delta^{-\epsilon}|A|
. This can be explained as follows. Assume
c_{\nu}
is such an element in
\text{spt}(\nu)
and
c\in C
is an element such that
|c-c_{\nu}|\leq\delta
. We observe that if
\mathcal{C}
is a covering of
A+cA
by
\delta
-balls, then for each ball in
\mathcal{C}
, adding two translations to the left and to the right by
\delta
, we would have a covering of
A+c_{\nu}A
. This gives the desired conclusion.

Orponen
[
15
]
recently obtained a stronger result that extends Bourgain’s result for different sets.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<\beta\leq\alpha<1
and
\eta>0
. Then, for every
\gamma\in((\alpha-\beta)/(1-\beta),1]
, there exist
\epsilon,\epsilon_{0},\delta_{0}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
, depending only on
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta
, such that the following holds. Let
\delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
with
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
, and let
A,B\subset\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,1]
satisfy the following hypotheses:

(A)

|A|\leq\delta^{-\alpha}
.

(B)

|B|\geq\delta^{-\beta}
, and
B
satisfies the following Frostman (non-concentration) condition:

|B\cap B(x,r)|\leq r^{\eta}|B|,~~\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}},~\delta\leq r\leq\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}.

Further, let
\nu
be a Borel probability measure with
\text{spt}(\nu)\subset[\tfrac{1}{2},1]
, satisfying the Frostman condition
\nu(B(x,r))\leq r^{\gamma}
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\delta\leq r\leq\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
. Then, there exists a point
c\in\text{spt}(\nu)
such that

|A+cB|_{\delta}\geq\delta^{-\epsilon}|A|.

Orponen also made the conjecture that the sharp lower bound for
\gamma
should be
\gamma>\alpha-\beta
.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in(0,1)
with
\alpha\geq\beta
and
\gamma>\alpha-\beta
. Assume that
A,B\subset[0,1]
and
C\subset[1/2,1]
are
\delta
-separated sets with cardinalities
|A|\leq\delta^{-\alpha}
,
|B|=\delta^{-\beta}
, and
|C|=\delta^{-\gamma}
. Assume moreover that
|B\cap B(x,r)|\lesssim r^{\beta}|B|
and
|C\cap B(x,r)|\lesssim r^{\gamma}|C|
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
r>0
. Then, there exist
\epsilon=\epsilon(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)>0
and a point
c\in C
such that
|A+cB|_{\delta}\gtrsim_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\delta^{-\epsilon}|A|
.

This conjecture is made based on a number of examples in the discrete setting. Let
A,B,C
be finite sets in
\mathbb{R}
. It is well-known that one can use the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem
[
19
]
to show that if
|B||C|\gtrsim|A|
then there exists
c\in C
such that
|A+cB|\gtrsim|A|
. For reader’s convenience, we reproduce the argument here. For any
c\in C
, let
L_{c}
be the set of lines of the form
x=r-cy
with
r\in A+cB
. Let
L=\cup_{c\in C}L_{c}
. Then it is clear that
|L|\leq|C|\max_{c\in C}|A+cB|
. We observe that
I(A\times B,L)\geq|A||B||C|
. Thus, the Szemerédi-Trotter incidence theorem gives

|A||B||C|\lesssim|A|^{2/3}|B|^{2/3}|C|^{2/3}\max_{c\in C}|A+cB|^{2/3},

which gives

\max_{c\in C}|A+cB|\gtrsim|A|^{1/2}|B|^{1/2}|C|^{1/2}.

In other words, if one wishes to have
|A+cB|\gtrsim|A|^{1+\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon>0
, then the condition
|B||C|\gtrsim|A|^{1+2\epsilon}
is needed. The following example, taken from
[
15
]
, also tells us that this condition is sharp. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

A_{n}=\left\{\frac{1}{n^{1/2}},~\frac{2}{n^{1/2}},\ldots,1\right\},~B_{n}=\left\{\frac{1}{n^{1/4}},~\frac{2}{n^{1/4}},\ldots,1\right\}=C_{n}.

We can check that for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|A_{n}+C_{n}B_{n}|\leq n^{\epsilon}|A|
.
The same happens in the finite field setting. We refer the reader to
[
17
]
for more discussions.

If we assume
A
and
B
are Ahlfors-David regular sets in
[0,1]
, then Conjecture
1.3
is known to be true in
[
14
]
. In fact, in
[
14
]
, Orponen proved a much stronger statement as follows. Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}
be closed sets, where
A
is
\alpha
-regular and
B
is
\beta
-regular. Then

\dim_{H}\left\{c\in\mathbb{R}\colon\dim_{H}(A+cB)<\alpha+\frac{\beta(1-\alpha)}{2-\alpha}\right\}=0.

(2)

For general sets, in another paper
[
16
]
, Orponen proved the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
0<\beta\leq\alpha<1
and
\sigma>0
. Then there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(\alpha,\beta,\sigma)>0
such that if
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}
are Borel sets with
\dim_{H}(A)=\alpha
,
\dim_{H}(B)=\beta
, then

\dim_{H}\{c\in\mathbb{R}\colon\dim_{H}(A+cB)\leq\alpha+\epsilon\}\leq\frac{\alpha-\beta}{1-\beta}+\sigma.

In particular,

\dim_{H}\left\{c\in\mathbb{R}\colon\dim_{H}(A+cB)=\alpha\right\}\leq\frac{\alpha-\beta}{1-\beta}.

To prove this theorem, as mentioned in
[
16
]
, the following upgraded version of Theorem
1.2
is crucial.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
0<\beta\leq\alpha<1
and
\eta>0
. Then, for every
\gamma\in((\alpha-\beta)/(1-\beta),1]
, there exist
\epsilon_{0},\epsilon,\delta_{0}\in(0,1/2]
, depending only on
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta
, such that the following holds. Let
\delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
with
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
, and let
A,B\subset\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,1]
satisfy the following hypothesis:

•

|A|\leq\delta^{-\alpha}

•

|B|\geq\delta^{-\beta}
, and
B
satisfies the following Frostman condition

|B\cap B(x,r)|\leq r^{\eta}~|B|,~~\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}},\delta\leq r\leq\delta^{\epsilon}_{0}.

Further, let
\nu
be a Borel probability measure with
\text{spt}(\nu)\subset[0,1]
, and satisfying the Frostman condition
\nu(B(x,r))\leq r^{\gamma}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<r<\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
. Then, there exists
c\in\text{spt}(\nu)
such that the following holds: if
G\subset A\times B
is any subset with
|G|\geq\delta^{\epsilon}|A||B|
, then

|\pi_{c}(G)|_{\delta}\geq\delta^{-\epsilon}|A|,

where
\pi_{c}(a,b)=a+cb.

For the most recent progress on this topic, we refer the reader to
[
18
]
for more details.

The main purpose of this paper is to study the energy variant of this problem. More precisely, let
\delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
, and
A,B\subset\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,1]
and
C\subset[1/2,1]
be
\delta
-separated sets.
Suppose that

\sum_{c\in C}|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A^{2}\times B^{2}:|(a_{1}+cb_{1})-(a_{2}+cb_{2})|\leq\delta\}|_{\delta}=\frac{1}{K}\cdot|A|_{\delta}^{3/2}|B|_{\delta}^{3/2}|C|.

We aim to give an explicit lower bound of
K
and discuss a number of applications of this energy estimate.

1.1
Main result

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta\in(0,1)
with
\alpha\geq\beta
. There exist
\epsilon_{0},\delta_{0}\in(0,1/2]
depending on
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta
such that the following holds.

Let
A,B\subset\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,1]
and
C\subset[1/2,1]
be
\delta
-separated sets with
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
.
Assume in addition that
|A|\leq\delta^{-\alpha}
,
|B|\geq\delta^{-\beta}
,
|C|=\delta^{-\gamma}
, and

\displaystyle|B\cap B(x,r)|\lesssim r^{\eta}|B|,~|C\cap B(x,r)|\lesssim r^{\gamma}|C|,\quad~\forall x\in\mathbb{R},~\delta\leq r\leq\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}.

Suppose that

\sum_{c\in C}|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A^{2}\times B^{2}:|(a_{1}+cb_{1})-(a_{2}+cb_{2})|\leq\delta\}|_{\delta}=\frac{1}{K}\cdot|A|_{\delta}^{3/2}|B|_{\delta}^{3/2}|C|.

Then, for all
\epsilon>0
,
K
can be bounded from below by

\displaystyle K\gtrsim|\log\delta|^{O(1)}\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\min\{M_{0},M_{1},M_{2},M_{3},M_{4}\},

where

M_{0}=\delta^{-\frac{\gamma-\beta}{4}},~~\eqref{m0}

M_{1}=\delta^{-\frac{\gamma+2\beta+\epsilon_{0}\eta}{4}},~~\eqref{eq_K_nonempty_R}

M_{2}=\max\left\{\delta^{-\frac{3\gamma-8\beta+1-\epsilon_{0}(3-\gamma)}{16m_{2}+36m_{1}}}\,,\delta^{-\frac{4\gamma-9\beta+1-\epsilon_{0}(3-\eta)}{16m_{2}+36m_{1}}}\right\},~~(\ref{eq:16},\ref{eq:17})

M_{3}=\max\left\{\delta^{-\frac{4\gamma-10\beta-\epsilon_{0}(3-\gamma)}{16m_{2}+44m_{1}}}\,,\delta^{-\frac{5\gamma-11\beta-\epsilon_{0}(2-\eta)}{16m_{2}+44m_{1}}}\,,\delta^{-\frac{5\gamma-12\beta+\eta-\epsilon_{0}(2-\gamma)}{16m_{2}+44m_{1}}}\,,\delta^{-\frac{6\gamma-13\beta+\eta-\epsilon_{0}(1-\eta)}{16m_{2}+44m_{1}}}\right\},~~(\ref{eq:18},\ref{eq:19},\ref{eq:20},\ref{eq:21})

M_{4}=\max\left\{\delta^{-\frac{6\gamma-14\beta-\epsilon_{0}(3-\gamma)}{20m_{2}+54m_{1}}}\,,\delta^{-\frac{7\gamma-16\beta+\eta-\epsilon_{0}(2-\gamma)}{20m_{2}+54m_{1}}}\,,\delta^{-\frac{7\gamma-15\beta-\epsilon_{0}(2-\eta)}{20m_{2}+54m_{1}}}\,,\delta^{-\frac{8\gamma-17\beta+\eta-\epsilon_{0}(1-\eta)}{20m_{2}+54m_{1}}}\right\},~~(\ref{eq:27},\ref{eq:28},\ref{eq:29},\ref{eq:30})

for positive constants
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 1
(given by the Balog-Szmerédi-Gower theorem
2.2
below).

Sketch of main ideas:

We briefly discuss methods/techniques we use to prove this theorem. We recall that the main purpose is to find lower bounds of
K
in the following identity:

\sum_{c\in C}|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A^{2}\times B^{2}:|(a_{1}+cb_{1})-(a_{2}+cb_{2})|\leq\delta\}|_{\delta}=\frac{1}{K}\cdot|A|_{\delta}^{3/2}|B|_{\delta}^{3/2}|C|.

The first step is to prove the existence of “good” subsets
B^{\prime}\subset B
,
C^{\prime}\subset C
, point
c^{*}\in C
, and constant
\rho\in\big[\frac{1}{K},1\big)
such that

|c^{*}B^{\prime}+cB^{\prime}|_{\delta}\lesssim(\rho K)^{4m_{2}+6m_{1}}|B^{\prime}|,~~\forall c\in C^{\prime},

|B^{\prime}\pm B^{\prime}|_{\delta}\lesssim(\rho K)^{2m_{2}+2m_{1}}|B^{\prime}|,

|B^{\prime}|\gtrsim\frac{|B|}{(\rho K)^{m_{1}}},\quad|C^{\prime}|\gtrsim\frac{|C|\rho|\log\delta|^{-1}}{(\rho K)^{4m_{1}}}.

To prove the energy theorem, we use Lemma
2.5
in the next section to give a lower bound for the set
D=(B^{\prime}-b_{1})\cap(b_{2}-b_{3})C^{\prime}
, for some
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in B^{\prime}
. More precisely, we have

|D|\gtrsim\frac{|C||\log\delta|^{-1}}{K^{4m_{1}}|B|}.

Note that we will need
|C|\geq K^{4m_{1}}|B|
to guarantee that
D
is non-empty. The proof then proceeds by establishing upper and lower bounds on sum sets of the form
|d_{1}\widetilde{D}+d_{2}\widetilde{D}+\cdots+d_{2}\widetilde{D}|_{\delta},
for elements
d_{i}\in C^{{}^{\prime}}-C^{{}^{\prime}}
and the set
\widetilde{D}
is an appropriately large subset of
D
satisfying small sum set condition. To prove an upper bound, we rely on the
\delta
-covering variant of Plünnecke’s inequality, Lemma
4.1
, and the estimates of Theorem
3.1
, making use of the fact that
D\subset B-b_{1}
. To obtain a lower bound, we follow the Guth-Katz-Zahl approach
[
6
]
, which is the discretized analog of Garaev’s method
[
5
]
over finite fields, with appropriate changes along the way clarifying and optimizing many of the steps. One of the biggest challenges in this approach is to optimize all parameters, roughly speaking, based on the definition of
D
, in the proof, if we want to bound the
d
-covering of
D
, for some
d\geq\delta
, then there are two ways one can proceed: either using the non-concentration condition on
C
or the non-concentration condition on
B
. Using the condition from only one set might imply an empty range. Overall, in the end, we have at least
64
ranges for
K
. This requires much work to figure out the best range for our purposes. It is very natural to ask if the argument presented in this paper can be improved to solve Conjecture
1.3
completely. At least to us, it is not possible when working with the set
D
. This can be seen clearly from the fact that we need
|C|\geq K^{4m_{1}}|B|
to guarantee
D\neq\emptyset
. So the range
\gamma>\alpha-\beta
is not sufficient for this purpose. The reader should keep in mind that the lower bounds of
K
only depend on the sets
B
and
C
, and are independent of the size or structural properties of
A
. Therefore, this approach has potential for further applications on other sum-product type questions. One might ask if the other sum-product type results over finite fields in
[
3
,
7
,
9
,
12
,
13
]
can be formulated and studied in this setting. We hope to address this question in a sequel paper.

1.3
An energy theorem via incidence bounds

In this section, we present an energy theorem, which will be proved by using a point-tube incidence bound of Dabrowski, Orponen, and Villa in
[
4
]
.

Compared to the first energy theorem, this theorem requires
\delta\leq r\leq 1
instead of
\delta\leq r\leq\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
for some
0<\epsilon_{0}<1
and no non-concentration condition on
C
is needed.

Recall that given
\delta
-separated sets
A,B,C
, we define
K
by

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in C}|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A^{2}\times B^{2}:|(a_{1}+cb_{1})-(a_{2}+cb_{2})|\leq\delta\}|_{\delta}=\frac{1}{K}\cdot|A|_{\delta}^{3/2}|B|_{\delta}^{3/2}|C|.

(3)

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in(0,1)
satisfying
\alpha+\beta>1
. Let
\delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
, and let
A,B\subset\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,1]
,
C\subset[1/2,1]
be
\delta
-separated sets satisfying
|A|=\delta^{-\alpha},|B|=\delta^{-\beta}
,
|C|=\delta^{-\gamma}
, and

|A\cap B(x,r)|\leq Mr^{\alpha}|A|,~~|B\cap B(x,r)|\leq Mr^{\beta}|B|,~\forall~x\in\mathbb{R},~\delta\leq r\leq 1,

for some
M>1
.
Then
K
, defined as in (
3
), can be bounded from below by

K\gtrsim\delta^{\frac{\alpha-3\beta-4\gamma+2\gamma(\alpha+\beta)-\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+2}{2(3-\alpha-\beta)}}.

In the statement of Theorem
1.9
, by taking
\alpha=\beta>1/2
and
\gamma>1/2
, then we have the bound

K\gtrsim\delta^{\frac{-\gamma(4-4\alpha)+2-2\alpha}{2(3-2\alpha)}}.

As in the previous section, we immediately obtain a
\delta
-covering result for the
A+cB
problem.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in(0,1)
satisfying
\alpha+\beta>1
. Let

The main theorem in this section is the following. Roughly speaking, it says that if we have the energy equality

\sum_{c\in C}|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A^{2}\times B^{2}:|(a_{1}+cb_{1})-(a_{2}+cb_{2})|\leq\delta\}|_{\delta}=\frac{1}{K}\cdot|A|_{\delta}^{3/2}|B|_{\delta}^{3/2}|C|,

then one can find subsets
B^{\prime}\subset B
and
C^{\prime}\subset C
such that the sets
B^{\prime}+B^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}+cB^{\prime}
have small
\delta
-covering for all
c\in C^{\prime}
. This theorem can be viewed as the discretized version of
[
11

due to Murphy and Petridis in the finite field setting.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
, and
A,B\subset\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,1]
and
C\subset[1/2,1]
be
\delta
-separated sets.
Suppose that

\sum_{c\in C}|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A^{2}\times B^{2}:|(a_{1}+cb_{1})-(a_{2}+cb_{2})|\leq\delta\}|_{\delta}=\frac{1}{K}\cdot|A|_{\delta}^{3/2}|B|_{\delta}^{3/2}|C|.

There exist subsets
B^{\prime}\subset B
,
C^{\prime}\subset C
, points
c^{*}\in C
, and

such that

|c^{*}B^{\prime}+cB^{\prime}|_{\delta}\lesssim(\rho K)^{4m_{2}+6m_{1}}|B^{\prime}|,~~\forall c\in C^{\prime},

|B^{\prime}\pm B^{\prime}|_{\delta}\lesssim(\rho K)^{2m_{2}+2m_{1}}|B^{\prime}|,

|B^{\prime}|\gtrsim\frac{|B|}{(\rho K)^{m_{1}}},|C^{\prime}|\gtrsim\frac{|C|\rho|\log\delta|^{-1}}{(\rho K)^{4m_{1}}}.

For each
c\in C

We are given

\sum_{c\in C}f_{\delta}(A,cB)\gtrsim\frac{(|A|_{\delta}|B|_{\delta})^{3/2}|C|}{K}.

(6)

Observe that

so that we have

.
By dyadic pigeonholing, we can find an integer

and a set

Notice that

and the fact that

|C_{1}|\gtrsim|C|\rho(\log K)^{-1}.

If

For each

\displaystyle\frac{K^{(4m_{2}+5m_{1})(2k+2)}|B|^{2k+2}}{|c^{*}D^{\prime}|_{\delta}^{2k+1}}\lesssim\frac{|B|^{4k+3}|\log\delta|^{2k+1}K^{(4m_{2}+9m_{1})(2k+1)+4m_{2}+5m_{1}}}{|C|^{2k+1}},

(17)

where the latter follows by
|c^{*}D|_{\delta}\sim|D|_{\delta}\gtrsim|D|\gtrsim\frac{|C||\log\delta|^{-1}}{|B|K^{4m_{1}}}
.

For the first term, there are two ways to bound it, which will give two different bounds. One way is to use the non-concentration condition on
C
, and the other way is to use the non-concentration condition on
B
.

(i) First approach: Non-concentration on
C
.

Recall that
D^{\prime}\subset(b_{2}-b_{3})C^{\prime}
is a
\delta
-refinement of
D
and
|b_{2}-b_{3}|\geq\delta
by the
\delta
-separated property of
B
. In particular, this yields that

|D^{\prime}|_{d}\sim\left|(b_{2}-b_{3})^{-1}D\right|_{d/(|b_{2}-b_{3}|)},

where
(b_{2}-b_{3})^{-1}D\subset C^{\prime}
.

Let
I
be an arbitrary interval of length
d/|b_{2}-b_{3}|
. From assumption, we know that

\frac{d}{|b_{2}-b_{3}|}\geq d>\delta^{\kappa}=\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
.
Thus, the non-concentration condition on the set
C
implies that

|C\cap I|\lesssim\frac{d}{|b_{2}-b_{3}|}|C|\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}\sim d|b_{2}-b_{3}|^{-1}|C|\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}.

Since
c^{*}
is greater than
1/2
, using the above estimate and (
14
), we will have an estimate for
|c^{*}D^{\prime}|_{d}
, namely

|c^{*}D^{\prime}|_{d}\gtrsim\frac{|D|}{d|b_{2}-b_{3}|^{-1}|C|\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}}\gtrsim\frac{|\log\delta|^{-1}|b_{2}-b_{3}|}{d\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}|B|K^{4m_{1}}}.

Substituting this and (
17
) into (
16
), one has the upper bound

|d_{1}D+d_{2}D+\cdots+d_{2}D|_{\delta}\lesssim\frac{|B|^{4(k+1)}K^{(8m_{2}+18m_{1})(k+1)}|\log\delta|^{2k+2}}{|b_{2}-b_{3}||C|^{2k+1}}\cdot\max\{|d_{1}|,|d_{2}|\}\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}.

(ii) Second approach: Non-concentration on
B

From hypothesis, we know that
|c_{3}-c_{4}|\geq\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
. By a similar argument, using the non-concentration assumption on
B
instead, we have

|c^{*}D^{\prime}|_{d}\gtrsim\frac{|D|}{d\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\eta-1)}|B|}\gtrsim\frac{|C||\log\delta|^{-1}}{|B|^{2}K^{4m_{1}}d\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\eta-1)}}.

Putting this into (
16
), we obtain the bound

\frac{|B|^{4k+5}K^{(8m_{2}+18m_{1})(k+1)}|\log\delta|^{2k+2}}{|C|^{2k+2}}\cdot\max\{|d_{1}|,|d_{2}|\}\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\eta-1)}.

We complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

We remark that the above lemma is most effective when
k=1
. For
k>1
, we have the following refinement.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
d_{1}=c_{1}-c_{2}
and
d_{2}=c_{3}-c_{4}
with
c_{i}\in C^{\prime}
. Assume that
|c_{1}-c_{2}|\leq|c_{3}-c_{4}|
and
|c_{3}-c_{4}|>\delta^{\kappa}=\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
. Then, for a bounded positive integer
k\geq 1
and for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\widetilde{D}\subset D
with
|\widetilde{D}|\geq\delta^{\epsilon}|D|
, such that the following holds.

(i)

With the non-concentration on
C
, one has

\displaystyle|d_{1}\widetilde{D}+\underbrace{d_{2}\widetilde{D}+\cdots+d_{2}\widetilde{D}}_{k~\text{terms}}|_{\delta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\frac{|B|^{2k+6}K^{(2m_{2}+5m_{1})k+14m_{2}+31m_{1}}|\log\delta|^{k+3}}{|C|^{k+2}|b_{2}-b_{3}|}\cdot\max\{|d_{1}|,|d_{2}|\}\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}.

(ii)

With the non-concentration on
B
, one has

\displaystyle|d_{1}\widetilde{D}+\underbrace{d_{2}\widetilde{D}+\cdots+d_{2}\widetilde{D}}_{k~\text{terms}}|_{\delta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\frac{|B|^{2k+7}K^{(2m_{2}+5m_{1})k+14m_{2}+31m_{1}}|\log\delta|^{k+3}}{|C|^{k+3}}\cdot\max\{|d_{1}|,|d_{2}|\}\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\eta-1)}.

Proof.

As in the previous proof, we identify a
\delta
-refinement
D^{\prime}
of
D
, which will be needed when applying Lemma
2.3
later. Then we apply Lemma
2.4
to find a subset
\widetilde{D}\subset D^{\prime}
such that
|\widetilde{D}|\geq\delta^{O(\epsilon)}|D|
for any given
\epsilon>0
, so that

|\widetilde{D}+\widetilde{D}|_{\delta/d_{2}}\lesssim\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\frac{|D+D|_{\delta}}{|D|}|d_{2}\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}.

On the other hand, using Theorem
3.2
and (
14
), we have

\frac{|D+D|_{\delta}}{|D|}\lesssim\frac{K^{2m_{2}+m_{1}}|B|}{|D|}=\frac{K^{2m_{2}+5m_{1}}|B|^{2}|\log\delta|}{|C|}.

Thus, when considering the sum of
k
terms, applying Lemma
2.3
, we have

|\underbrace{d_{2}\widetilde{D}+\cdots+d_{2}\widetilde{D}}_{k\text{ terms}}|_{\delta}\lesssim\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\frac{K^{(2m_{2}+5m_{1})(k-1)}|B|^{2(k-1)}|\log\delta|^{k-1}}{|C|^{k-1}}\cdot|d_{2}\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}.

Next, applying (
17
) from the proof of the previous lemma with
k=1
, one obtains

|c^{\ast}\widetilde{D}+d_{1}D+d_{2}D|_{\delta}\lesssim\frac{|B|^{7}K^{16m_{2}+32m_{1}}|\log\delta|^{3}}{|C|^{3}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle|c^{*}\widetilde{D}+d_{1}\widetilde{D}+\underbrace{d_{2}\widetilde{D}+\cdots+d_{2}\widetilde{D}}_{k\text{ terms}}|_{\delta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|d_{2}\widetilde{D}+d_{2}\widetilde{D}+\cdots+d_{2}\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}}{|d_{2}\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}}\cdot\frac{|c^{*}\widetilde{D}+d_{1}\widetilde{D}+d_{2}\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}}{|d_{2}\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}}\cdot|d_{2}\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\frac{|B|^{7}K^{16m_{2}+32m_{1}}|\log\delta|^{3}}{|C|^{3}}\cdot\frac{K^{(2m_{2}+5m_{1})(k-1)}|B|^{2(k-1)}|\log\delta|^{k-1}}{|C|^{k-1}}.

This gives the bound

\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\frac{|B|^{2k+5}K^{(2m_{2}+5m_{1})k+14m_{2}+27m_{1}}|\log\delta|^{k+2}}{|C|^{k+2}}.

(18)

Now based on the same argument as in the previous proof, we use different non-concentration conditions to bound the following estimate

|d_{1}\widetilde{D}+\underbrace{d_{2}\widetilde{D}+\cdots+d_{2}\widetilde{D}}_{k\text{ terms}}|_{\delta}\lesssim|c^{*}\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}^{-1}|c^{*}\widetilde{D}+d_{1}\widetilde{D}+\underbrace{d_{2}\widetilde{D}+\cdots+d_{2}\widetilde{D}}_{k\text{ terms}}|_{\delta}.

(i) Non-concentration on
C
:

Using the non-concentration condition on
C
, one has the first term is bounded below by

|c^{*}\widetilde{D}|_{d}\gtrsim\frac{|D|}{d|b_{2}-b_{3}|^{-1}|C|\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}}\gtrsim\frac{|\log\delta|^{-1}|b_{2}-b_{3}|}{|B|K^{4m_{1}}d\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}}.

Combining with the estimate for the second term (
18
), we get

\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\frac{|B|^{2k+6}K^{(2m_{2}+5m_{1})k+14m_{2}+31m_{1}}|\log\delta|^{k+3}}{|C|^{k+2}|b_{2}-b_{3}|}\cdot\max\{|d_{1}|,|d_{2}|\}\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}.

(ii) Non-concentration on
B
:

In this case, the only difference is that

|c^{*}\widetilde{D}|_{d}\gtrsim\frac{|D|}{d\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\eta-1)}|B|}\gtrsim\frac{|C||\log\delta|^{-1}}{|B|^{2}K^{4m_{1}}d\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\eta-1)}}.

This follows by the assumption on
B
. Therefore, we obtain the bound

\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\frac{|B|^{2k+7}K^{(2m_{2}+5m_{1})k+14m_{2}+31m_{1}}|\log\delta|^{k+3}}{|C|^{k+3}}\cdot\max\{|d_{1}|,|d_{2}|\}\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\eta-1)}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We now consider the set

R:=R(\widetilde{D})=\left\{\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{3}-d_{4}}:d_{i}\in\widetilde{D},|d_{3}-d_{4}|>\delta^{\kappa}\right\}.

Since
R(\widetilde{D})\subseteq R(C^{{}^{\prime}})
and
C^{{}^{\prime}}\subset[1/2,1]
, we have that
R\subset[-\delta^{-\kappa}/2,\delta^{-\kappa}/2].

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
\widetilde{D}
is non-empty. We have either
R
is non-empty or

\displaystyle K\gtrsim|\log\delta|^{O(1)}\delta^{-\frac{\gamma-2\beta+\kappa\eta}{4}}.

(19)

Proof.

To prove that
R
is non-empty, it is enough to show that there are at least two elements
d_{3},d_{4}\in\widetilde{D}
such that
|d_{3}-d_{4}|>\delta^{\kappa}=\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
. Since the set
\widetilde{D}
is assumed to be non-empty, there exists
d_{3}\in\widetilde{D}
. If
|\widetilde{D}|\gtrsim\delta^{\epsilon_{0}\eta}|B|
, then the existence of
d_{4}
follows from the non-concentration of
B
. Otherwise, from the lower bound on the size of
\widetilde{D}
in (
14
), we have

K^{4m_{1}}\gtrsim\frac{|C||\log\delta|^{-1}}{|B|^{2}\delta^{\epsilon_{0}\eta}}.

From this, we will get the lower bound for
K
and complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

Note that we also have
0,1\in R
. Choose a positive integer
m
so that
2^{-m}\sim\delta^{1-2\kappa}|b_{2}-b_{3}|
. Define
s=2^{-m}
.

The following result was proved in
[
6
]
, and it turns out to be useful for our proofs.

Lemma 4.4
.

At least one of the following two statements must hold.

(A):

There exists a point
r\in R\cap[0,1]
with

\max\Big\{\operatorname{dist}(r/2,\ R),\ \ \operatorname{dist}\Big(\frac{r+1}{2},\ R\Big)\Big\}\geq s.

(B):

|R\cap[0,1]|_{s}\gtrsim s^{-1}.

As in
[
6
]
, we refer to the case
(A)
as the “gap” case, and
(B)
as the “dense” case. We proceed to obtain lower bounds for
K
corresponding to each case
(A)
and
(B)
.

4.1
Dense case

Suppose we are in the dense case
(B)
, that is
|R\cap[0,1]|_{s}\gtrsim s^{-1}
. By pigeonholing, we can select points
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}\in C^{{}^{\prime}}
with
|c_{3}-c_{4}||b_{2}-b_{3}|>\delta^{\kappa}
and
|c_{1}-c_{2}|\leq|c_{3}-c_{4}|
so that

\left|\bigg\{(d_{1},...,d_{4})\in{\widetilde{D}}^{4}\colon\Big|\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{3}-d_{4}}-\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{c_{3}-c_{4}}\Big|<\delta^{1-2\kappa}|b_{2}-b_{3}|,\ |d_{3}-d_{4}|>\delta^{\kappa}\bigg\}\right|\lesssim|\widetilde{D}|^{4}\delta^{1-2\kappa}|b_{2}-b_{3}|.

(20)

Here and below, we write
d_{1}^{\prime}-d_{2}^{\prime}=(b_{2}-b_{3})(c_{1}-c_{2})
and
d_{3}^{\prime}-d_{4}^{\prime}=(b_{2}-b_{3})(c_{3}-c_{4})
.

Observe that
|c_{3}-c_{4}|>\delta^{\kappa}=\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
. Applying Lemma
4.2
with
k=1
, the following quantity

\displaystyle\big|(d_{1}^{\prime}-d_{2}^{\prime})\widetilde{D}+(d_{3}^{\prime}-d_{4}^{\prime})\widetilde{D}\big|_{\delta|b_{2}-b_{3}|}=\left|(c_{1}-c_{2})\widetilde{D}+(c_{3}-c_{4})\widetilde{D}\right|_{\delta}

is bounded from above by

\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\frac{|B|^{8}K^{16m_{2}+36m_{1}}|\log\delta|^{4}}{|C|^{3}|b_{2}-b_{3}|}\cdot|c_{3}-c_{4}|\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}

(21)

if we use the non-concentration condition on
C
, and by

\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\frac{|B|^{9}K^{16m_{2}+36m_{1}}|\log\delta|^{4}}{|C|^{4}}\cdot|c_{3}-c_{4}|\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\eta-1)},

(22)

if we use the non-concentration condition on
B
instead.

We will now establish a lower bound for
\big|(d_{1}^{\prime}-d_{2}^{\prime})\widetilde{D}+(d_{3}^{\prime}-d_{4}^{\prime})\widetilde{D}\big|_{\delta|b_{2}-b_{3}|}
. Define
Q\subset{\widetilde{D}}^{4}
to be the set of quadruples obeying

(d_{3}^{\prime}-d_{4}^{\prime})d_{1}+(d_{1}^{\prime}-d_{2}^{\prime})d_{4}=(d_{3}^{\prime}-d_{4}^{\prime})d_{2}+(d_{1}^{\prime}-d_{2}^{\prime})d_{3}+O(\delta|b_{2}-b_{3}|).

(23)

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, one has
\big|(d_{1}^{\prime}-d_{2}^{\prime})\widetilde{D}+(d_{3}^{\prime}-d_{4}^{\prime})\widetilde{D}\big|_{\delta}\gtrsim|\widetilde{D}|^{4}/|Q|
. Thus our goal is now to find an upper bound for
|Q|
. Note that equation (
23
) can be written as

d_{1}+\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{c_{3}-c_{4}}d_{4}=d_{2}+\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{c_{3}-c_{4}}d_{3}+O(\delta|d_{3}^{\prime}-d_{4}^{\prime}|^{-1}|b_{2}-b_{3}|),

which implies that

\left|\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{3}-d_{4}}-\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{c_{3}-c_{4}}\right|\lesssim\delta|d_{3}^{\prime}-d_{4}^{\prime}|^{-1}|d_{3}-d_{4}|^{-1}|b_{2}-b_{3}|.

(24)

At this step, we consider two separate cases:

(i): At least
\frac{|Q|}{2}
quadruples
(d_{1},...,d_{4})\in Q
satisfy
|d_{3}-d_{4}|>\delta^{\kappa}
. For each such quadruple, from inequality (
24
) above, one has

\left|\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{3}-d_{4}}-\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{c_{3}-c_{4}}\right|\lesssim\delta^{1-2\kappa}|b_{2}-b_{3}|.

Comparing with (
20
), we see that the number of such quadruples is
\lesssim|\widetilde{D}|^{4}\delta^{1-2\kappa}|b_{2}-b_{3}|
. Thus if at least half the quadruples
(d_{1},...,d_{4})\in Q
satisfy
|d_{3}-d_{4}|>\delta^{\kappa}
, then

\big|(c_{1}-c_{2})\widetilde{D}+(c_{3}-c_{4})\widetilde{D}\big|_{\delta}\gtrsim\frac{|\widetilde{D}|^{4}}{|Q|}\gtrsim\delta^{2\kappa-1}|b_{2}-b_{3}|^{-1}.

Combining with the upper bound in (
21
), we have

\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\frac{|B|^{8}K^{16m_{2}+36m_{1}}|\log\delta|^{4}}{|b_{2}-b_{3}||C|^{3}}\cdot|c_{3}-c_{4}|\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}\gtrsim\frac{\delta^{2\kappa-1}}{|b_{2}-b_{3}|}.

This infers that

K^{16m_{2}+36m_{1}}\gtrsim|\log\delta|^{O(1)}\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\frac{|C|^{3}}{\delta^{1-2\kappa}|c_{3}-c_{4}|\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}|B|^{8}}.

In other words, one obtains a lower bound for
K
, namely

K\gtrsim|\log\delta|^{O(1)}\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\delta^{-\frac{3\gamma-8\beta+1-\kappa(3-\gamma)}{16m_{2}+36m_{1}}}.

(25)

Here we use the fact that
1>|c_{3}-c_{4}|>\delta^{\kappa}
.
Similarly, if we use the bound (
22
) in place of (
21
), we have

K\gtrsim|\log\delta|^{O(1)}\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\delta^{-\frac{4\gamma-9\beta+1-\kappa(3-\eta)}{16m_{2}+36m_{1}}}.

(26)

(ii) More than half of the quadruples
(d_{1},...,d_{4})\in Q
satisfy
|d_{3}-d_{4}|\leq\delta^{\kappa}
. We will make use of non-concentration assumptions on
C
and
B
again to estimate the upper bound for the number of these quadruples. As a result, we will obtain the corresponding lower bounds for
K
.

(a) Non-concentration on
C
: We begin by choosing elements
d_{1},d_{4}\in\widetilde{D}
.
Since
\widetilde{D}\subset(b_{2}-b_{3})C
, according to the Frostman condition on
C
and the requirement that
|d_{3}-d_{4}|\leq\delta^{\kappa}
, the number of admissible
d_{3}
is at most

\displaystyle|\widetilde{D}\cap B(d_{4},\delta^{\kappa})|\leq\frac{|C|\delta^{\kappa}}{|b_{2}-b_{3}|}\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}.

Here we used the fact that

\frac{\delta^{\kappa}}{|b_{2}-b_{3}|}\geq\delta^{\kappa}=\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
.

Next, observe that from (
24
),
d_{2}
must lie in an interval of length at most
\delta|c_{3}-c_{4}|^{-1}
.
Notice that since
\epsilon_{0}+\kappa<1
, we must have

\frac{\delta}{|c_{3}-c_{4}||b_{2}-b_{3}|}=\frac{\delta}{|d_{3}^{\prime}-d_{4}^{\prime}|}<\delta^{1-\kappa}<\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}.

The Frostman condition on
C
yields that the number of admissible
d_{2}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\delta^{\gamma}|C||c_{3}-c_{4}|^{-\gamma}|b_{2}-b_{3}|^{-\gamma}=|d_{3}^{\prime}-d_{4}^{\prime}|^{-\gamma}\lesssim\delta^{-\kappa\gamma}.

Thus the set of quadruples of this type has size at most

\frac{|\widetilde{D}|^{2}|C|\delta^{\kappa}\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}}{|b_{2}-b_{3}|\delta^{\kappa\gamma}}
.
From (
14
), one has

|(c_{1}-c_{2})\widetilde{D}+(c_{3}-c_{4})\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}\gtrsim\frac{|\widetilde{D}|^{4}}{|Q|}\gtrsim\frac{|\widetilde{D}|^{2}|b_{2}-b_{3}|}{|C|\delta^{\kappa+\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}\delta^{-\kappa\gamma}}\gtrsim\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\frac{|C||\log\delta|^{-2}|b_{2}-b_{3}|}{K^{8m_{1}}|B|^{2}}.

Altogether, the lower and the upper bounds from (
21
) imply that

K^{16m_{2}+44m_{1}}\gtrsim|\log\delta|^{O(1)}\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\frac{|C|^{4}}{|B|^{10}}\frac{|b_{2}-b_{3}|^{2}}{|c_{3}-c_{4}|\delta^{\epsilon_{0}(\gamma-1)}}.

Using the fact that
|b_{2}-b_{3}||c_{3}-c_{4}|>\delta^{\kappa}
, one gets

K\gtrsim|\log\delta|^{O(1)}\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\delta^{-\frac{4\gamma-10\beta-\kappa(3-\gamma)}{16m_{2}+44m_{1}}}.

(27)

On the other hand, if we use (
22
) instead of (
21
), we have

K\gtrsim|\log\delta|^{O(1)}\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\delta^{-\frac{5\gamma-11\beta-\kappa(2-\eta)}{16m_{2}+44m_{1}}}.

(28)

(b) Non-concentration on
B
:

Similarly, for given elements
d_{4}\in\widetilde{D}
, the Frostman condition on
B
implies that the number of admissible
d_{3}
is at most

|B|\delta^{\kappa\eta}
.

Next, observe that from inequality (
24
), we must have
d_{2}
lie in an interval of length at most
\delta|c_{3}-c_{4}|^{-1}
, for any fixed
d_{1}\in\widetilde{D}
.
Because
\epsilon_{0}+\kappa<1
, one has

\delta|c_{3}-c_{4}|^{-1}<\delta^{1-\kappa}<\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
.
Hence the number of admissible
d_{2}
is at most
\delta^{\eta}|B||c_{3}-c_{4}|^{-\eta}
, where we make use of the Frostman condition on
B
.

Altogether, the set of quadruples of this type has size at most

\frac{|\widetilde{D}|^{2}|B|^{2}\delta^{\kappa\eta+\eta}}{|c_{3}-c_{4}|^{\eta}}
, which implies

|(c_{1}-c_{2})\widetilde{D}+(c_{3}-c_{4})\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}\gtrsim\frac{|\widetilde{D}|^{4}}{|Q|}\gtrsim\frac{|\widetilde{D}|^{2}|c_{3}-c_{4}|^{\eta}}{|B|^{2}\delta^{\kappa\eta+\eta}}\gtrsim\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\frac{|C|^{2}|\log\delta|^{-2}}{|B|^{4}K^{8m_{1}}\delta^{\eta}}.

Then combining this estimate with the upper bound (
21
), one has

K\gtrsim|\log\delta|^{O(1)}\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\delta^{-\frac{5\gamma-12\beta+\eta-\kappa(2-\gamma)}{16m_{2}+44m_{1}}}.

(29)

Using inequality (
22
) instead, one has

K\gtrsim|\log\delta|^{O(1)}\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\delta^{-\frac{6\gamma-13\beta+\eta-\kappa(1-\eta)}{16m_{2}+44m_{1}}}.

(30)

4.2
Gap case

In this section, we will suppose that we are in the gap case
(A)
. This means that there exists
r\in R\cap[0,1]
so that either

(A.1)

r/2
is at least
s
-separated from
R
or

(A.2)

\frac{r+1}{2}
is at least
s
-separated from
R
, where
s\sim\delta^{1-2\kappa}|b_{2}-b_{3}|
.

Notice that it follows from the definition of
\widetilde{D}
that

r=\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{c_{3}-c_{4}}

for some
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}\in C^{\prime}
with
|c_{3}-c_{4}|\geq|c_{1}-c_{2}|
and
|c_{3}-c_{4}|\geq\delta^{\kappa}=\delta^{\epsilon_{0}}
.

In Case (A.1), we write
r/2=e_{1}/e_{2}
with
e_{1}=x_{1}
and
e_{2}=x_{2}+x_{2}
, where
x_{1},x_{2}\in C^{\prime}-C^{\prime}
.

In Case (A.2), we write
\frac{r+1}{2}
as
e_{1}/e_{2}
with
e_{1}=x_{1}+x_{2},\ e_{2}=x_{2}+x_{2}
, where
x_{1},x_{2}\in C^{\prime}-C^{\prime}
.

The first task in this section is to find a lower bound on
|e_{1}\widetilde{D}+e_{2}\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}
. One needs to keep in mind that
|e_{2}|\sim|c_{3}-c_{4}|
.

Define
Q\subset\widetilde{D}^{4}
to be the set of quadruples obeying

e_{2}d_{1}+e_{1}d_{4}=e_{2}d_{2}+e_{1}d_{3}+O(\delta).

(31)

As in the dense case, we only need to find an upper bound for
|Q|
. Since by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, one has
|e_{1}\widetilde{D}+e_{2}\widetilde{D}|_{\delta}\gtrsim\frac{|\widetilde{D}|}{|Q|}
.

Dividing equation (
31
) by
e_{2}
gives

\left|\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{3}-d_{4}}-\frac{e_{1}}{e_{2}}\right|\lesssim\delta|e_{2}|^{-1}|d_{3}-d_{4}|^{-1}.

(32)

Assume that there exists a quadruple
(d_{1},...,d_{4})\in Q
such that
|d_{3}-d_{4}|\geq\delta^{\kappa}
. In other words, we have
\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{3}-d_{4}}\in R
. Then using the fact that
|b_{2}-b_{3}||e_{2}|\gtrsim\delta^{\kappa}
, equation (
32
) implies

\left|\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{3}-d_{4}}-\frac{e_{1}}{e_{2}}\right|\lesssim\delta^{1-2\kappa}{|b_{2}-b_{3}|}.

Since we are in the gap case,
r=e_{1}/e_{2}
is at least
s\sim\delta^{1-2\kappa}|b_{2}-b_{3}|
separated from
R
, which is a contradiction. It turns out that every quadruple in
Q
satisfies
|d_{3}-d_{4}|\leq\delta^{\kappa}
.

As in the dense case, by using the non-concentration assumptions on
C
and

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
0<n<d
and
M\geq 1
. Let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{A}(d,n)
be a
\delta
-separated set of
n
-planes, and let
P\subset B(1)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a
\delta
-separated
(\delta,t,M)
-set with
t>d-n
, i.e.

|P\cap B(x,r)|_{\delta}\leq Mr^{t}|P|_{\delta},~\forall~x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},~r>0.

For
r>0
, define
\mathcal{I}_{r}(P,\mathcal{V})=\{(p,V)\in P\times\mathcal{V}:p\in V(r)\}
. Then we have

|\mathcal{I}_{M\delta}(P,\mathcal{V})|\lesssim_{M,d,t}|P|\cdot|\mathcal{V}|^{n/(d+n-t)}\cdot\delta^{n(t+1-d)(d-n)/(d+n-t)}.

Here, the Grassmannian
\mathcal{A}(d,n)
is equipped with the metric
d_{\mathcal{A}}
defined as follows. For
V,W\in\mathcal{A}(d,n)
, let
V_{0}
,
W_{0}
, and
a\in V_{0}^{\perp},b\in W_{0}^{\perp}
be unique subspaces and vectors such that

V=V_{0}+a,~W=W_{0}+b.

The distance between
V
and
W
is defined by

d_{\mathcal{A}}(V,W)=||\pi_{V_{0}}-\pi_{W_{0}}||_{op}+|a-b|,

where
||\cdot||_{op}
is the operator norm.

In the plane, a direct computation shows that the distance between two lines
y=ax+b
and
y=cx+d
is

\left|\frac{(a,-1)}{|(a,-1)|}-\frac{(c,-1)}{|(c,-1)|}\right|+\left|\frac{b}{|(a,-1)|}-\frac{d}{|(c,-1)|}\right|.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

The first part is identical with Theorem
3.2
. For the second part, since

are
\delta
-separated, the number of tuples

.

infers that the point

C
and

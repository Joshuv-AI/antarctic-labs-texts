K-moduli of log del Pezzo pairs and variations of GIT
Jesus Martinez-Garcia, Theodoros Stylianos Papazachariou, Junyan Zhao

Abstract
. We study the K-moduli of log del Pezzo pairs formed by a del Pezzo surface of degree d d and an anti-canonical divisor. These moduli spaces naturally depend on one parameter, providing a natural problem in variations of K-moduli spaces. For degrees 2, 3, 4, we establish an isomorphism between the K-moduli spaces and variations of Geometric Invariant Theory compactifications, which generalizes the isomorphisms in the absolute cases established by Odaka–Spotti–Sun and Mabuchi–Mukai.

Introduction

K-stability has successfully provided the construction of moduli spaces of Fano varieties and log Fano pairs. The general K-moduli theorem (cf. Theorem

It is also worth noting that recent years have witnessed significant progress in understanding the K-moduli of Fano threefolds. In particular, many of these K-moduli compactifications have been explicitly related to GIT or VGIT constructions, which greatly facilitates the study of their global properties (e.g., see

The goal of this paper is to study log del Pezzo pairs of degree

For degrees

Our first result establishes isomorphisms between the VGIT moduli spaces and the K-moduli spaces for the above log del Pezzo pairs, preserving both wall-crossing structures.

Theorem 1.1

(cf. Theorem
3.8
)
.

Let
c\in(0,1)
be a rational number and
d=3,4
. Let
\mathcal{M}^{\GIT}_{d}(t(c))
be the GIT moduli stack of log del Pezzo pairs of degree
d
. Let
t(c)=\frac{9c}{8+c}
if

, and
t(c)=\frac{6c}{5+c}
if

. Then there is an isomorphism between Artin stacks

{\mathcal{M}}^{K}_{d}(c)\simeq{\mathcal{M}}^{\GIT}_{d}(t(c)),

which descends to an isomorphism
{\overline{M}}^{K}_{d}(c)\simeq{\overline{M}}^{\GIT}_{d}(t(c))
between the corresponding good moduli spaces. In particular, these isomorphisms commute with the wall-crossing morphisms.

In
[
35
,
31
]
and

]

is small (but not beyond the second chamber). Theorem
1.1
, in conjunction with
[
35
,
31
,
70

thus gives a full geometric description of all the log pairs represented in the K-moduli space for all
c

Del Pezzo surfaces of degree

-K_{Y}
is base-point free and induces a

\mathbb{P}^{2}

to that of
(\mathbb{P}^{2},\frac{1}{2}C+cC^{\prime})

[

\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein smoothing to pairs

Natural GIT compactifications can be considered for pairs

\overline{M}^{\GIT}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(t)\coloneqq\big(\mathbb{P}H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(4)\right)\times\mathbb{P}H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1)\right)\big)\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu/\mkern-6.0mu/\mkern-3.0mu}_{\mathcal{O}(a,b)}\PGL(3),

. Similarly, let
\mathcal{M}^{\GIT}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(t)

It is well known that smooth del Pezzo surfaces of degree

can degenerate to a double cover
Y

branched along an octic hyperelliptic curve

2

\mathbb{P}(1,1,4)

\overline{M}^{K}_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,4)}(c)

\overline{M}^{\GIT}_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,4)}(t)\coloneqq\left(\mathbb{P}H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(8)\right)\times\mathbb{P}H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\right)\right)\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu/\mkern-6.0mu/\mkern-3.0mu}_{\mathcal{O}(a,b)}\PGL(2)

Theorem 1.2

(cf. Corollary
4.2
, Theorems
4.11
,
4.18
,
4.3
,
4.25
)
.

Let
c\in(0,1)
be a rational number, and
\overline{M}^{K}_{2}(c)
be the K-moduli space of log del Pezzo pairs
(Y,cD)
of degree
2
, where
D\in|-K_{Y}|
. If
c>\frac{1}{4}
, then we have a canonical isomorphism

{\mathcal{M}}^{K}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(c)\ \simeq\ \left[\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}^{ss}_{t(c)}/\PGL(3)\right],

where
t(c)=2c
. The latter quotient stack is obtained by taking the Kirwan blowup
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}^{ss}_{t(c)}
of the VGIT moduli stack
\mathcal{M}^{\GIT}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(t(c))
at the point parameterizing the pair
(2C,L)
, where
C
is a smooth conic and

is a line intersecting

\overline{M}^{K}_{2}(c)\ \simeq\ \overline{M}^{K}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(c)\ \simeq\ \widetilde{\mathcal{U}}^{ss}_{t}\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu/\mkern-6.0mu/\mkern-3.0mu}_{\mathcal{O}(a,b)}\PGL(3),

where
t(c)=\frac{b}{a}
. For any
c\in(0,1)
, the following holds.

(1)

If
(X,\frac{1}{2}C+cC^{\prime})
is a K-semistable pair in

, then
X\simeq\mathbb{P}^{2}

.

(2)

A pair

is K-(semi/poly)stable if and only if the pair
(C,C^{\prime})
is GIT

t(c)=2c
.

(3)

A pair
(\mathbb{P}(1,1,4),\frac{1}{2}C+cC^{\prime})
is K-(semi/poly)stable if and only if
(C,C^{\prime})
is GIT
{}_{t^{\prime}(c)}
-(semi/poly)stable, where
t^{\prime}(c)=\frac{12c}{1-c}
. Moreover, there is a canonical isomorphism

\overline{M}^{K}_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,4)}(c)\ \simeq\ \overline{M}^{\GIT}_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,4)}(t^{\prime}(c)).

(semi/poly)stability. We refer the reader to Appendix

For higher degrees

Theorem 1.3

(cf. Theorem

,

, Wall crossings of K-moduli)
.

Let

(1)

If

(2)

Preliminaries

Convention

Throughout this paper, we work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
. By a surface, we mean a connected normal projective algebraic surface over
\mathbb{C}

2.1.
K-stability of log Fano pairs

In this section, we follow conventions and notation from
[
12
]
and
[
7
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety, and
D
be an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. Then the pair
(X,D)
is called a log Fano pair if
-(K_{X}+D)
is an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor and a log del Pezzo pair if in addition
\dim(X)=2
. A normal projective variety
X
is called a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety if
(X,0)
is a Kawamata log terminal (klt) log Fano pair. A (
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano) variety of dimension
2
is a del Pezzo surface.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,D)
be an
n
-dimensional log Fano pair, and
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
which is
\mathbb{Q}
-linear equivalent to
-k(K_{X}+D)
for some positive rational number
k\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then a normal test configuration (abbreviated TC)
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})
of
(X,D;L)
consists of

•

a normal projective variety
\mathcal{X}
with a flat projective morphism
\pi:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
;

•

a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
ample over
\mathbb{A}^{1}
;

•

a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on the polarized variety
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
such that
\pi
is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant, where
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is equipped with the standard
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action;

•

a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant isomorphism between the restriction
(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{0};\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{0}})
and
(X;L)\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\})
;

•

an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathcal{D}
is the Zariski closure of
D\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\})
in
\mathcal{X}
, under the identification between
\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{0}
and
X\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\})
.

A TC is called

•

a product test configuration if

(2.1)

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})\simeq(X\times\mathbb{A}^{1},D\times\mathbb{A}^{1};p_{1}^{*}L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(l\mathcal{X}_{0}))

for some
l\in\mathbb{Z}
;

•

a trivial test configuration if it is a product TC and the isomorphism (
2.1
) is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant, where the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
X
is trivial;

•

a special test configuration if
\mathcal{L}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-k(K_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{A}^{1}}+\mathcal{D})
and
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}+\mathcal{X}_{0})
is purely log terminal (plt).

The generalized Futaki invariant of a normal test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})/\mathbb{A}^{1}
is

\Fut(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})\coloneqq\frac{1}{(-K_{X}-D)^{n}}\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\cdot\frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{n+1}}{k^{n+1}}+\frac{(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{n}.(K_{\overline{\mathcal{X}}/\mathbb{P}^{1}}+\overline{\mathcal{D}}))}{k^{n}}\right),

where
(\overline{\mathcal{X}},\overline{\mathcal{D}};\overline{\mathcal{L}})
is the natural compactification of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Now we define K-stability for log Fano pairs.

Definition 2.3
.

A log Fano pair
(X,D)
is called

(1)

K-semistable if
\Fut(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})\geq 0
for any normal test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})/\mathbb{A}^{1}
and any
k\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
L
is Cartier;

(2)

K-polystable if it is K-semistable, and
\Fut(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})=0
for some TC
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})
if and only if it is a product TC.

(3)

K-stable if it is K-semistable, and
\Fut(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})=0
for some TC
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})
if and only if it is a trivial TC.

There is an equivalent formulation of K-(semi)stability in terms of birational geometry, by means of the following invariants:

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(X,D)
be an
n
-dimensional log Fano pair, and
E
a prime divisor on a normal projective variety
Y
, where
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is a birational morphism. Then the log discrepancy of
(X,D)
with respect to
E

A_{(X,D)}(E)\coloneqq 1+\coeff_{E}(K_{Y}-\pi^{*}(K_{X}+D)).

The expected vanishing order of
(X,D)
with respect to
E
(also referred to as the
S
-invariant
) is

S_{(X,D)}(E)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(-K_{X}-D)^{n}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\vol(\pi^{*}(-K_{X}-D)-xE)dx,

where
\tau
is the pseudo-effective threshold

\tau=\tau(\pi^{*}(-K_{X}-D);E)=\sup\{x\,\colon\,\pi^{*}(-K_{X}-D)-xE\text{ is big}\}.

The
\beta
-invariant of
(X,D)
with respect to
E
is then

\beta_{(X,D)}(E)\coloneqq A_{(X,D)}(E)-S_{(X,D)}(E).

(X,D)

[

Let

4.

2

A smooth del Pezzo surface
Y
of degree
2
admits a natural double cover
Y\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
defined by the linear series
|-K_{Y}|
, branched along a quartic curve
C_{4}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Under this cover a divisor
D\in|-K_{Y}|
maps to a line
L\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
, giving a crepant double cover

(Y,cD)\ \longrightarrow\ \big(\mathbb{P}^{2},\tfrac{1}{2}C_{4}+cL\big).

We will study the K-moduli of pairs
(X,\tfrac{1}{2}C+cC^{\prime})
admitting a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein smoothing to
(\mathbb{P}^{2},\tfrac{1}{2}C_{4}+cL)
for all rational numbers
c\in(0,1)
, and prove that the resulting K-moduli spaces are isomorphic to those of the log del Pezzo pairs
(Y,cD)
of degree
2
with
D\in|-K_{Y}|
(cf. Theorem
4.3
).

We begin by making the K-moduli problem precise, in particular the notion of a family of such pairs and hence of the associated stacks. Defining a family of pairs is subtle, and considerable effort has gone into addressing this subtlety; see
[
50
, Chapter 8]
. As noted above, a general pair of interest
(\mathbb{P}^{2},\tfrac{1}{2}C_{4}+cL)
has a boundary divisor with two components, so we require every family of pairs to satisfy the generalized Kollár condition (cf.
[
69
, Definition 3.1]
).

Let
\mathcal{M}^{K}_{2}(c)
(resp.
\overline{M}^{K}_{2}(c)
) denote the K-moduli stack (resp. space) with reduced scheme structure of smoothable log del Pezzo pairs of degree
2
. Let
\mathcal{M}^{K}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(c)
be the K-moduli stack of
(\tfrac{1}{2},c)
-marked pairs
(X,\frac{1}{2}C+cC^{\prime})
admitting a smoothing to
(\mathbb{P}^{2},\tfrac{1}{2}C_{4}+cL)
(see
[
50
, Definition 8.4]
and
[
83
, Remark 7.20]
), and let
\overline{M}^{K}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(c)
be the corresponding good moduli space. In fact, here one can impose a stronger condition in defining the functor
{\mathcal{M}}^{K}_{2}(c)
: one can require that for any family
(\mathscr{X},\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{C}+c\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\rightarrow T

Lemma 4.1

In this section, we prove Theorem

Theorem 4.3

There is a natural isomorphism

Remark 4.4

It is worth noting that the two corresponding K-moduli stacks are not isomorphic. Indeed, not every K-semistable del Pezzo surface of degree 2 arises as a double cover of a degeneration of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. For example, let

Proof.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(X,\frac{1}{2}C+cC^{\prime})
be a K-semistable pair in
{\mathcal{M}}^{K}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(c)
. Then the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein deformation of
(X,\frac{1}{2}C+cC^{\prime})
is unobstructed.

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow X
be the canonical covering stack, and let

\mathfrak{C}\coloneqq\pi^{*}C
and
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}\coloneqq\pi^{*}C^{\prime}
. By Corollary
4.2
,
X\simeq\mathbb{P}^{2}
or
X\simeq\mathbb{P}(1,1,4)
. In the first case
\mathfrak{X}=X
. In the second, the local class group at the singular point is
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
, generated by
\mathcal{O}(1)
, and
K_{X}=\mathcal{O}(-6)
has class
2
, of order
2
. The relations
3C+4K_{X}\sim 0
and
3C^{\prime}+K_{X}\sim 0
give
[C]=0
and

[C^{\prime}]=[K_{X}]
, so both lie in the subgroup trivialised by the index one cover. Hence
\mathfrak{C}
,
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
and
K_{\mathfrak{X}}
are Cartier on
\mathfrak{X}
. By
[
37
, Proposition 3.7]
(see also
[
3
, Theorem 5.3.6]
),
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein deformations of

(X,C,C^{\prime})
are identified with deformations of the triple

(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{C}^{\prime})
, and since the two divisors deform
independently once a deformation of the ambient stack is fixed, we have

\Def^{\QG}(X,C,C^{\prime})\ =\ \Def(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{C}^{\prime})\ =\ \Def(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{C})\times_{\Def(\mathfrak{X})}\Def(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}).

We claim that
\Def^{\QG}(X)=\Def(\mathfrak{X})
is smooth. If
X={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
this is
clear, as
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is rigid. If
X={\mathbb{P}}(1,1,4)
, then
X
is a del Pezzo
surface whose unique singularity
p
is the T-singularity

\frac{1}{4}(1,1)
. By
[
36
, Proposition 3.1]
, there are no
local-to-global obstructions, i.e. the restriction map

\Def^{\QG}(X)\longrightarrow\Def^{\QG}_{\loc}(X,p)

is smooth. Moreover,
\Def^{\QG}_{\loc}(X,p)
is itself smooth: the index one
cover of
\frac{1}{4}(1,1)
is the hypersurface singularity

A_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(1,1)
, so local
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein deformations of
p
are
the
\mu_{2}
-equivariant deformations of
A_{1}
, which are unobstructed.
Hence
\Def^{\QG}(X)
is smooth in both cases.

We next show that the forgetful map

\Def(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{C})\rightarrow\Def(\mathfrak{X})
is smooth; the argument for

\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
is identical. Write
L\coloneqq{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{C})
, which is a
line bundle on
\mathfrak{X}
by Corollary
4.2
since
\mathfrak{X}
is smooth. Since
\pi
is cohomologically
affine and
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{X}}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}
, we have

H^{i}(\mathfrak{X},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{X}})=H^{i}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})=0
for
i=1,2
, so
L
extends
uniquely to every infinitesimal deformation of
\mathfrak{X}
. The obstruction to
extending the section of
L
cutting out
\mathfrak{C}
lies in

H^{1}(\mathfrak{X},L)
. Again since
\pi
is cohomologically affine,

H^{1}(\mathfrak{X},L)=H^{1}(X,\pi_{*}L)
, where
\pi_{*}L
is the rank one reflexive
sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\deg C)
; explicitly,
\pi_{*}L
is
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(4)
or

{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(1,1,4)}(8)
for
C
, and
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(1)
or
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(1,1,4)}(2)

for

. The intermediate cohomology of the reflexive sheaves

on
a weighted projective space vanishes for every

, and hence

, and
\Def(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{C})\rightarrow\Def(\mathfrak{X})
is smooth.

Therefore
\Def(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{C}^{\prime})
, being the fiber product of two
smooth morphisms of deformation functors over a smooth base, is smooth, and
the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein deformations of
(X,\frac{1}{2}C+cC^{\prime})
are
unobstructed.
∎

The composition morphism

Proposition 4.10
.

The morphism
\phi(c)
is bijective.

Proof.

Since
\overline{M}^{K}_{2}(c)
is irreducible (as it is smooth and connected by Lemma

) and the morphism
\phi(c)
is dominant (as log smooth pairs are dense in both domain and target), then
\phi(c)
is surjective by the properness of the K-moduli. Suppose now
(X_{1},\frac{1}{2}C_{1}+cC_{1}^{\prime})
and
(X_{2},\frac{1}{2}C_{2}+cC_{2}^{\prime})
are two K-polystable pairs such that their double covers
(Y_{1},cD_{1})
and
(Y_{2},cD_{2})
are isomorphic. Then
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are isomorphic to either
\mathbb{P}^{2}
or
\mathbb{P}(1,1,4)
by Corollary
4.2
. Note
(X_{i},\frac{1}{2}C_{i})
is canonical if and only if
Y_{i}
is canonical. If
X_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, then
Y_{i}
is given by

in
\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,2)
and the double cover

gives us that

, so

is Cartier. Moreover, since

is K-semistable, then it is klt, and hence

has Du Val singularities. If
X_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}(1,1,4)
, then

does not contain the singularity of
X_{i}
since the pair is K-semistable (by Lemma
4.1
), and thus
Y_{i}
has exactly two non-ADE singularities. By the assumption that
(Y_{1},cD_{1})\simeq(Y_{2},cD_{2})
, we know that
X_{1}\simeq X_{2}
. Now, we assume that
(Y_{1},cD_{1})=(Y_{2},cD_{2})=(Y,cD)
and denote the double covers by
\pi_{1}:Y\rightarrow X_{1}
and
\pi_{2}:Y\rightarrow X_{2}
.

Assume first that
X_{i}\simeq\mathbb{P}(1,1,4)
, and denote by
p_{i}
the singularity of
X_{i}
. Denote by
L_{i}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}}(1)
the class of a line on
X_{i}\simeq\mathbb{P}(1,1,4)
. Then the local Picard group
\Pic(p_{i}\in X_{i})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
with
L_{i}
a generator. Since
Y
is a Klt
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety, it is simply connected
[
77
]
, so
Y
has no non-trivial torsion line bundle. As
\pi_{i}
is étale near

, then

is torsion, and thus trivial. It follows from
[

that

(\pi_{i})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\pi^{*}_{i}(2L_{i}))\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}}(2L_{i})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}}(-2L_{i}),

and thus

Similarly, we have that

(\pi_{i})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}}(4L_{i}))\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}}(4L_{i})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}},

and thus

H^{0}(X_{1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}(4L_{1}))\oplus\mathbb{C}\simeq H^{0}(Y,\pi_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}(4L_{1}))\simeq H^{0}(Y,\pi_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(4L_{2}))\simeq H^{0}(X_{2},\mathcal{O}_{X_{2}}(4L_{2}))\oplus\mathbb{C}.

(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
of

and an element

which is isomorphism to
\pi_{i}
once we take the image. It follows that the two double covers
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
differ by an automorphism of
\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,2)
, and hence they are isomorphic. Therefore, we conclude that
(X_{1},\frac{1}{2}C_{1}+cC_{1}^{\prime})\simeq(X_{2},\frac{1}{2}C_{2}+cC_{2}^{\prime})
.

Similarly, if

, then the same argument shows that
(X_{1},\frac{1}{2}C_{1}+cC_{1}^{\prime})\simeq(X_{2},\frac{1}{2}C_{2}+cC_{2}^{\prime})
. Therefore, the morphism
\phi(c)
is injective, concluding the proof.

, we may therefore assume that
f_{2}=uv
. At the two roots
0,\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the criterion in Proposition
4.16
becomes
\operatorname{mult}_{0}(f_{8})\leq 4
and
\operatorname{mult}_{\infty}(f_{8})\leq 4
. At every other point it becomes
\operatorname{mult}_{p}(f_{8})\leq 4+\tau
, which is automatic since
\deg(f_{8})=8
and
\tau>4
. Thus the
\operatorname{GIT}_{\tau}
-semistability condition is precisely the semistability condition for the action of

\operatorname{Stab}_{\PGL(2)}([uv])\simeq\mathbb{G}_{m}\rtimes\mu_{2}

on
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(8))
. The analogous statement for polystability follows from the same numerical criterion. By Proposition
4.23
, this is exactly the GIT stability condition induced on the exceptional divisor of the Kirwan blow-up.

Consequently, the GIT-polystable fibers of the family over

\widetilde{U}_{t}^{\mathrm{ss}}
are exactly the K-polystable pairs classified in Theorem
4.21
. The remainder of the proof is the same as the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 5.15]
: the additional degree-one boundary is carried equivariantly throughout the construction, and Proposition
4.23
identifies its restriction on the exceptional slice with the fixed square-free quadratic
f_{2,0}
. Hence the closed-point, separatedness, quasi-finiteness, and representability arguments of loc. cit. apply without change. Alper’s criterion
[
6
, Proposition 6.4]
therefore shows that

\psi_{t}:[\widetilde{\mathcal{U}_{t}}^{\mathrm{ss}}/\PGL(3)]\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{K}(c)

is an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 4.26
.

There are two reasons for Theorem
4.25
not to extend to
c\in(0,\frac{1}{4}]
. Firstly, Proposition
4.16
characterises GIT
t
(semi-)stability of pairs
(\mathbb{P}(1,1,4),\frac{1}{2}C+cC^{\prime})
in terms of the multiplicity of the defining (binary form) equations of
C
and
C^{\prime}
at each
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In particular, one can use readily show that if
C
is general and
C^{\prime}
is a a double line passing through the singular point of
\mathbb{P}(1,1,4)
, then the log pair is GIT
t
-stable and, by Theorem
4.18
, also K-stable. These pairs would have to appear ‘over’ pairs
(\mathbb{P}^{2},2Q+cL)
where
L
is tangent to
Q
by construction. But one may check by applying the Hilbert-Mumford criterion that such pairs are GIT
t
-unstable for all
t>0
, thus they are not appearing in
\mathcal{M}^{\GIT}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(t(c))
and thus they will not appear in the Kirwan blow-up.

The second reason is the mirror of the first one: again by Proposition
4.16
and Theorem
4.18
, we may conclude that pairs
(\mathbb{P}(1,1,4),C+cC^{\prime})
where
C
and
C^{\prime}
are defined by binary forms
f_{8}
and
f_{2}
, respectively, such that
f_{8}
has a root of multiplicity larger than
4+c
away from the two roots of
f_{2}
are K-unstable. There are such examples for
c<\frac{1}{4}
appearing on the exceptional divisor of the Kirwan blow-up.

We believe an explicit global quotient description of the K-moduli for
d=2
should be possible for all
c
, but the construction would have to be different, starting by taking a different polarization on the blow-up than the natural one given by the Kirwan blow-up (i.e. use a different polarisation not close to the pullback of the polarisation used before blowing up).

5.1.
The case for
d=7

Before starting the proof, we give an explanation of the idea of the proof. The easy observation is that every K-wall
c=c_{i}
is detected by at least one boundary pair, in the following sense: there is a pair
(X,D)
such that
(X,c_{i}D)
is K-semistable, but
(X,cD)
is K-unstable for any
0<c<c_{i}
. Such a pair need not be unique; several different surfaces or strata in the corresponding anticanonical linear series may give the same value
c_{i}
. This multiplicity is harmless for our purposes, since below we analyze all possible surfaces
X
with Du Val singularities and stratify the corresponding linear series
|-K_{X}|
, so that coincident contributions merely produce the same wall.

By volume comparison, we know that
X
has at worst Du Val singularities, then for each possible surface
X
with Du Val singularities, we can analyze the stability region for divisors in
|-K_{X}|
. Equivalently, we give a stratification of the linear series
|-K_{X}|
for any possible
X
.

There are only two different del Pezzo surfaces of degree
d=7
with Du Val singularities. The first one, which we will denote by
X
, is smooth, and it is classically obtained by blowing up
\mathbb{P}^{2}
along two points
p=(0:0:1)
and
q=(0:1:0)
. Let
l\subseteq X
be the proper transform of the line
\{x=0\}
through
p
and
q
. The second surface, which we denote by
X^{\prime}
, has precisely one
A_{1}
-singularity. The surface
X
can be realized by blowing a point in the only
(-1)
-curve of
\mathbb{F}_{1}
and contracting the proper transform of such curve to the singularity (cf.
[
39
, Theorem 3.4]
). We will denote this curve by
E
.

Proposition 5.3
.

If a pair
(X,cD)
is K-semistable, then
c\geq\frac{4}{25}
; if a pair
(X^{\prime},cD^{\prime})
is K-semistable, then
c\geq\frac{2}{9}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
(X,cD)
is K-semistable. Then we have that
\beta_{(X,cD)}(l)\geq 0
. Since
A_{(X,cD)}(l)\leq 1
, and
S_{(X,cD)}(l)=\frac{25}{21}(1-c)
, then we must have
c\geq\frac{4}{25}
. Similarly, one can show that

\beta_{(X^{\prime},cD^{\prime})}(E)=A_{(X^{\prime},cD^{\prime})}(E)-\frac{9}{7}(1-c)\leq 1-\frac{9}{7}(1-c)\leq 0

if
c\leq\frac{2}{9}
.
∎

In fact, by the same computation as in the proof, one can obtain the following results.

Corollary 5.4
.

If
D\in|-K_{X}|
has
l
as a component, then
(X,cD)
is K-unstable for any
0<c<1
; If
D^{\prime}\in|-K_{X^{\prime}}|
passes through the
A_{1}
-singularity, then
(X^{\prime},cD^{\prime})
is K-unstable for any
0<c<1
.

Let us first deal with the smooth surface
X
. A member
C\in|-K_{X}|
is the proper transform of a plane cubic curve
\overline{C}
with
p,q\in\overline{C}
. Thus, we can identify
\overline{C}
with a cubic polynomial

yz(ay+bz)+xf_{2}(y,z)+x^{2}f_{1}(y,z)+x^{3}.

Note that
a=b=0
if and only if
l
is in the support of
C
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
C\in|-K_{X}|
be a curve as above. If
ab\neq 0
, then
(X,cC)
is K-semistable for
c=\frac{4}{25}
; if either
a=0
or

(but not both), then
(X,cC)
is K-semistable for
c=\frac{2}{9}
, but K-unstable for
c<\frac{2}{9}
.

Proof.

If
ab\neq 0
, then the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action defined by
t\cdot(x:y:z)=(tx:y:z)
induces an isotrivial degeneration from
(X,cC)
to
(X,cC_{0})
, where
C_{0}
is the proper transform of the plane cubic
yz(ay+bz)=0
, consisting of three lines through
(1:0:0)
. These lines are distinct if and only if

. The pair
(X,cC_{0})
is a
\mathbb{T}
-pair of complexity-one, so one can apply Theorem
2.10
. Indeed, note that
C_{0}
is not a toric divisor and the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action above is in

on

if and only if

-invariant with respect to any vertical divisor is positive. Thus

is K-semistable, and so is

by openness of K-semistability.

If
ab=0
, then we may assume that
a=1
and
b=0
. In this case, we claim that the coefficient of
z^{2}
in
f_{2}(y,z)
is non-zero. In fact, if otherwise, then the
\beta

for any

is K-semistable for

, but K-unstable for

.
∎

To show that

is the first wall, we need to show that any other

(which in this case we know it can only be

) does not induce a wall before

. Since by Corollary

, when the pair

is K-semistable for some

must not have

is smooth. Furthermore, if

and

, for a point

[

, we have

7(1-c)^{2}=(-K_{Y}-cD)^{2}\leq\frac{9}{4}\widehat{\operatorname{vol}}(x,Y,D)\leq\frac{9}{4}\widehat{\operatorname{vol}}(x,Y).

Now let us focus on the surface

with an

-singularity

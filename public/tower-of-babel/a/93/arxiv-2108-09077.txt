Abstract
. We prove a self-improvement property of a capacity density condition for a nonlocal Hajłasz gradient in complete geodesic spaces. The proof relates the capacity density condition with boundary Poincaré inequalities, adapts Keith–Zhong techniques for establishing local Hardy inequalities and applies Koskela–Zhong arguments for proving self-improvement properties of local Hardy inequalities. This leads to a characterization of the Hajłasz capacity density condition in terms of a strict upper bound on the upper Assouad codimension of the underlying set, which shows the self-improvement property of the Hajłasz capacity density condition.

1.
Introduction

We introduce a Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition in terms of Hajłasz gradients of order
0<\beta\leq 1
, see Sections
3
and
4
. Our main result, Theorem
9.6
, states that this condition is doubly open-ended, that is, a Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition is self-improving both in
p
and in
\beta
if
X
is a complete geodesic space.
The study of such conditions can be traced back to the seminal work by Lewis
[
24
]
, who established self-improvement of Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density conditions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. His result has been followed by other works incorporating different techniques often in metric spaces, like nonlinear potential theory
[
27
,
3
]
, and local Hardy inequalities
[
23
]
.

A distinctive feature of our paper is that we prove the self-improvement of a capacity density condition
for a nonlocal gradient for the first time in metric spaces.
We make use of a recent advance
[
19
]
in Poincaré inequalities, whose self-improvement properties were originally shown by Keith–Zhong in their celebrated work
[
16
]
.
In this respect, we join the line of research
initiated in
[
20
]
, and continued in
[
6
,
5
]
, for bringing together the seemingly distinct self-improvement properties of capacity density conditions and Poincaré inequalities.

We use various techniques and concepts in the proof of Theorem
9.6
.
The fundamental idea is to use a geometric concept, more precisely the upper Assouad codimension, and characterize the capacity density with a strict upper bound on this codimension.
Here we are motivated by the recent approach from
[
4
]
, where the Assouad codimension bound is
used to give necessary and sufficient conditions for certain fractional Hardy inequalities; we also refer to
[
22
]
.
The principal difficulty is to prove a strict bound on the codimension. To this
end we relate the capacity density condition to boundary Poincaré inequalities, and we show their self-improvement roughly speaking in two steps: (1) Keith–Zhong estimates on maximal functions and (2) Koskela–Zhong estimates on Hardy inequalities. For these purposes, respectively,
we adapt the maximal function methods from

[
19
]
and the local Hardy arguments from
[
23
]
.

One of the main challenges our method is able to overcome is the nonlocal nature of Hajłasz gradients
[
8
]
. More specifically, if a function
u
is
constant in a set
A\subset X
and
g
is a Hajłasz gradient of
u
, then
g\mathbf{1}_{X\setminus A}
is not necessarily
a Hajłasz gradient of
u
. This fact makes it impossible to directly use the standard localization techniques. More specifically, there is no access to neither pointwise glueing lemma nor pointwise Leibniz rule, both of which are used while proving similar self-improvement properties
for capacity density conditions involving
p
-weak upper gradients, for example, by the Wannebo approach
[
30
]
that was used in
[
23
]
to show corresponding local Hardy inequalities. The Hajłasz gradients satisfy nonlocal versions of these basic tools, both of which we employ in our method.

There is a clear advantage to working with Hajłasz gradients: Poincaré inequalities hold for all measures, see Section
3
. Other types of gradients,
such as
p
-weak upper gradients
[
1
]
, do not have this property and therefore corresponding Poincaré inequalities need to be assumed a priori, as was the case in previous works such as
[
27
,
3
,
23
,
6
,
5
]
. We remark that this requirement already excludes many doubling measures
in
{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with Euclidean distance
[
2
]
.

Our method has also a disadvantage. We need to assume that

X
is a complete geodesic space. These assumptions provide us Lemma
5.3
,
Theorem
3.8
, Lemma
2.6
, Lemma
2.5
, and few other useful properties.
We do not know how far these two conditions could be relaxed.
In particular, it would be interesting to know if our main result, Theorem
9.6
, could be
extended to the more general setting of complete and connected metric spaces.

The outline of this paper is as follows. After a brief discussion on notation and preliminary concepts in Section
2
, Hajłasz gradients are introduced in Section
3
along with their calculus and various Poincaré inequalities. Capacity density condition is discussed in Section
4
, and some preliminary sufficient and necessary bounds on the Assouad codimension are given in Section
5
. The most technical part of the work is contained in Sections
6
,
7
and
8
, in which the analytic framework of the self-improvement is gradually developed. Finally, the main result is given in Section
9
,
in which we show that various geometrical and analytical conditions are equivalent to the capacity density condition. The geometrical conditions are open-ended by definition, and hence all analytical conditions
are seen to be self-improving or doubly open-ended.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the setting from
[
19
]
.
Our results are based on
quantitative estimates and absorption arguments, where
it is often crucial to track the dependencies of constants quantitatively.
For this purpose, we will use the following notational convention:

C({\ast,\dotsb,\ast})
denotes a positive constant which quantitatively
depends on the quantities indicated by the
\ast
’s but whose actual
value can change from one occurrence to another, even within a single line.

2.1.
Metric spaces

Unless otherwise specified, we assume that
X=(X,d,\mu)
is a metric measure space equipped with a metric
d
and a
positive complete Borel
measure
\mu
such that
0<\mu(B)<\infty

for all balls
B\subset X
, each of which is always an open set of the form

B=B(x,r)=\{y\in X\,:\,d(y,x)<r\}

with
x\in X
and
r>0
.
As in
[
1
, p. 2]
,
we extend
\mu
as a Borel regular (outer) measure on
X
.
We remark that the space
X
is separable
under these assumptions, see
[
1
, Proposition 1.6]
.
We also assume that
\#X\geq 2
and
that the measure
\mu
is
doubling
, that is,
there is a constant
c_{\mu}>1
, called
the
doubling constant of
\mu
, such that

(2.1)

\mu(2B)\leq c_{\mu}\,\mu(B)

for all balls
B=B(x,r)
in
X
.
Here we use for
0<t<\infty
the notation
tB=B(x,tr)
.
In particular, for all balls
B=B(x,r)
that are centered at
x\in A\subset X
with radius

r\leq\mathrm{diam}(A)
, we have that

(2.2)

\frac{\mu(B)}{\mu(A)}\geq 2^{-s}\bigg(\frac{r}{\mathrm{diam}(A)}\bigg)^{s}\,,

where
s=\log_{2}c_{\mu}>0
. We refer to
[
12
, p. 31]
.
If
X
is connected, then the doubling measure
\mu
is also

reverse doubling
in the sense that
there is a constant
0<c_{R}=C(c_{\mu})<1
such that

(2.3)

\mu(B(x,r/2))\leq c_{R}\,\mu(B(x,r))

for every
x\in X
and
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(X)/2
.
See for instance
[
1
, Lemma 3.7]
.

2.2.
Geodesic spaces

Let
X
be a metric space satisfying the conditions stated in Section
2.1
.
By a
curve
we mean a nonconstant, rectifiable, continuous
mapping from a compact interval of
{\mathbb{R}}
to
X
; we tacitly assume
that all curves are parametrized by their arc-length.
We say that
X
is a
geodesic space
, if
every pair of points in
X

can be joined by a curve whose length is equal to the distance between the two points.
In particular, it easily follows that

(2.4)

0<\operatorname{diam}(2B)\leq 4\operatorname{diam}(B)

for all balls
B=B(x,r)
in a geodesic space
X
.
Since geodesic spaces are connected, the measure
\mu
is
reverse doubling in a geodesic space
X
in the sense that
inequality (
2.3
) holds.

The following lemma is
[
14
, Lemma 12.1.2]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
X
is a geodesic space and
A\subset X
is a measurable set.
Then the function

r\mapsto\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap A)}{\mu(B(x,r))}\,:\,(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}

is continuous whenever
x\in X
.

The second
lemma, in turn, is
[
19
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
B=B(x,r)
and
B^{\prime}=B(x^{\prime},r^{\prime})
are two balls in a geodesic space
X
such
that
x^{\prime}\in B
and
0<r^{\prime}\leq\mathrm{diam}(B)
. Then

\mu(B^{\prime})\leq c_{\mu}^{3}\mu(B^{\prime}\cap B)
.

2.3.
Hölder and Lipschitz functions

Let
A\subset X
.
We say that

u\colon A\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
\beta
-Hölder function,
with
an exponent
0<\beta\leq 1
and a constant

0\leq\kappa<\infty
, if

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq\kappa\,d(x,y)^{\beta}\qquad\text{ for all }x,y\in A\,.

If
u\colon A\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
\beta
-Hölder function, with a constant
\kappa
, then the classical McShane extension

(2.7)

v(x)=\inf\{u(y)+\kappa\,d(x,y)^{\beta}\,:\,y\in A\}\,,\qquad x\in X\,,

defines a
\beta
-Hölder function
v\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
with the constant
\kappa
,
which satisfies

v|_{A}=u
; we refer to
[
12
, pp. 43–44]
.
The set of all
\beta
-Hölder functions
u\colon A\to{\mathbb{R}}

is denoted by
\operatorname{Lip}_{\beta}(A)
.
The
1
-Hölder functions are also called
Lipschitz functions
.

{\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
and
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}

3.
Hajłasz gradients

We work with Hajłasz
\beta
-gradients of order
0<\beta\leq 1
in a metric space
X
.

Definition 3.1
.

For each function
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we let

\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)

be the (possibly empty) family of all measurable functions
g\colon X\to[0,\infty]
such that

(3.2)

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}\big(g(x)+g(y)\big)

almost everywhere, i.e., there exists an exceptional set
N=N(g)\subset X
for which
\mu(N)=0
and
inequality (
3.2
) holds for every
x,y\in X\setminus N
.
A function
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(u)
is called a Hajłasz
\beta
-gradient of the function
u
.

The Hajłasz
1
-gradients in metric spaces are introduced in
[
9
]
.
More details on these gradients and their applications can be found, for instance, from
[
8
,
10
,
28
,
29
,
31
]
.
The following basic properties are easy to verify for all
\beta
-Hölder functions
u,v\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}

(D1)

\lvert a\rvert g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(au)
if
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
;

(D2)

g_{u}+g_{v}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u+v)
if
g_{u}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
and
g_{v}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(v)
;

(D3)

If
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Lipschitz function with constant
\kappa
, then

\kappa g\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(f\circ u)
if
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(u)
.

There are both disadvantages and advantages to working with
Hajłasz gradients.
A technical disadvantage is their nonlocality
[
8
]
.
For instance, if
u
is constant on some set
A\subset X
and
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(u)
, then

g\mathbf{1}_{X\setminus A}
need not belong to
\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(u)
.
By the so-called glueing lemma, see for instance
[
1
, Lemma 2.19]
, the corresponding localization property holds for so-called
p
-weak upper gradients,
which makes their application more flexible.
However, the following nonlocal glueing lemma
from
[
19
, Lemma 6.6]

holds in the setting of Hajłasz gradients.

We recall the proof for convenience.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0<\beta\leq 1
and let
A\subset X
be a Borel set.
Let
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a
\beta
-Hölder function and
suppose that
v\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}

is such that
v|_{X\setminus A}=u|_{X\setminus A}
and
there exists a constant
\kappa\geq 0
such that

\lvert v(x)-v(y)\rvert\leq\kappa\,d(x,y)^{\beta}

for all
x,y\in X
.
Then

g_{v}=\kappa\,\mathbf{1}_{A}+g_{u}\mathbf{1}_{X\setminus A}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(v)

whenever
g_{u}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.

Proof.

Fix a function
g_{u}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
and let

N\subset X
be the exceptional set such that
\mu(N)=0
and
inequality (
3.2
) holds for every
x,y\in X\setminus N
and with
g=g_{u}
.

Fix
x,y\in X\setminus N
. If
x,y\in X\setminus A
, then

\lvert v(x)-v(y)\rvert=\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}\big(g_{u}(x)+g_{u}(y)\big)=d(x,y)^{\beta}\big(g_{v}(x)+g_{v}(y)\big)\,.

If
x\in A
or
y\in A
, then

\lvert v(x)-v(y)\rvert\leq\kappa\,d(x,y)^{\beta}\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}\big(g_{v}(x)+g_{v}(y)\big)\,.

By combining the estimates above, we find that

\lvert v(x)-v(y)\rvert\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}\big(g_{v}(x)+g_{v}(y)\big)

whenever
x,y\in X\setminus N
. The desired conclusion
g_{v}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(v)
follows.
∎

The following nonlocal generalization of the Leibniz rule is from
[
10
]
.
The proof is recalled for the convenience of the reader. The nonlocality is reflected
by the appearence of the two global terms
\lVert\psi\lVert_{\infty}
and
\kappa
in the statement below.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
0<\beta\leq 1
, let

u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a bounded
\beta
-Hölder function, and let
\psi\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a bounded
\beta
-Hölder
function with a constant
\kappa\geq 0
. Then
u\psi\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
\beta
-Hölder function and

(g_{u}\lVert\psi\lVert_{\infty}+\kappa\lvert u\rvert)\mathbf{1}_{\{\psi\not=0\}}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u\psi)

for all
g_{u}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.
Here
\{\psi\not=0\}=\{y\in X:\psi(y)\not=0\}
.

Proof.

Fix
x,y\in X
. Then

(3.5)

\begin{split}\lvert u(x)\psi(x)-u(y)\psi(y)\rvert&=\lvert u(x)\psi(x)-u(y)\psi(x)+u(y)\psi(x)-u(y)\psi(y)\rvert\\
&\leq\lvert\psi(x)\rvert\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert+\lvert u(y)\rvert\lvert\psi(x)-\psi(y)\rvert\,.\end{split}

Since
u
and
\psi
are both bounded
\beta
-Hölder functions in
X
, it follows
that
u\psi
is
\beta
-Hölder in
X
.

Fix a function
g_{u}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
and let

N\subset X
be the exceptional set such that
\mu(N)=0
and
inequality (
3.2
) holds for every
x,y\in X\setminus N
and with
g=g_{u}
.
Denote
h=(g_{u}\lVert\psi\lVert_{\infty}+\kappa\lvert u\rvert)\mathbf{1}_{\{\psi\not=0\}}
. Let
x,y\in X\setminus N
. It suffices to show that

\lvert u(x)\psi(x)-u(y)\psi(y)\rvert\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}(h(x)+h(y))\,.

By (
3.5
), we get

(3.6)

\begin{split}\lvert u(x)\psi(x)-u(y)\psi(y)\rvert&\leq\lvert\psi(x)\rvert d(x,y)^{\beta}(g_{u}(x)+g_{u}(y))+\lvert u(y)\rvert\kappa d(x,y)^{\beta}\\
&=d(x,y)^{\beta}\left(\lvert\psi(x)\rvert(g_{u}(x)+g_{u}(y))+\kappa\lvert u(y)\rvert\right)\,.\end{split}

Next we do a case study. If
x,y\in\{\psi\not=0\}
, then by (
3.6
) we have

\displaystyle\lvert u(x)\psi(x)-u(y)\psi(y)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}\left(g_{u}(x)\lVert\psi\rVert_{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{\psi\not=0\}}(x)+(g_{u}(y)\lVert\psi\rVert_{\infty}+\kappa\lvert u(y)\rvert)\mathbf{1}_{\{\psi\not=0\}}(y)\right)

\displaystyle\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}(h(x)+h(y))\,.

If
x\in X\setminus\{\psi\not=0\}
and
y\in\{\psi\not=0\}
, then

\displaystyle\lvert u(x)\psi(x)-u(y)\psi(y)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}\left(\kappa\lvert u(y)\rvert\mathbf{1}_{\{\psi\not=0\}}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=d(x,y)^{\beta}h(y)\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}(h(x)+h(y))\,.

The case
x\in\{\psi\not=0\}
and
y\in X\setminus\{\psi\not=0\}
is symmetric
and the last case is trivial.
∎

A significant advantage of working with Hajłasz gradients is that Poincaré inequalities are always valid
[
29
,
31
]
. The same is not true for the usual
p
-weak upper
gradients, in which case a Poincaré inequality often has to be assumed.

The following theorem gives a
(\beta,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality for any
1\leq p<\infty
.
This inequality relates the Hajłasz gradient to the given measure.

Theorem 3.7
.

Suppose that
X
is a metric space.
Fix exponents

1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
.
Suppose that

u\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
and that
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.
Then

\bigg(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{B}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\bigg)^{1/p}\leq 2\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta}\bigg(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\bigg)^{1/p}

holds whenever
B\subset X
is a ball.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 5.15]
.
Let

N=N(g)\subset X
be the exceptional set such that
\mu(N)=0
and
(
3.2
) holds for every
x,y\in X\setminus N

By Hölder’s inequality

Theorem 3.8
.

Suppose that

Fix exponents

and a constant

,

and

, such that

\bigg(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{B}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta}\bigg(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}g(x)^{q}\,d\mu(x)\bigg)^{1/q}

4.
Capacity density condition

In this section we define the capacity density condition. This condition is based
on the following notion of variational capacity, and it is weaker than the well known measure density condition. We also prove
boundary Poincaré inequalities for sets satisfying a capacity density condition. This is done with the aid of so-called Maz
′
ya’s inequality, which provides
an important link between Poincaré inequalities and capacities.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<\beta\leq 1
, and let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set.
The variational
(\beta,p)
-capacity of a subset
F\subset\Omega
with
\mathrm{dist}(F,X\setminus\Omega)>0
is

\cp_{\beta,p}(F,\Omega)=\inf_{u}\inf_{g}\int_{X}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,,

where the infimums are taken over all
\beta
-Hölder functions
u
in
X
, with
u\geq 1
in
F
and
u=0
in
X\setminus\Omega
, and over all
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.

Remark 4.2
.

We may take the infimum in Definition
4.1
among all
u
satisfying additionally
0\leq u\leq 1
. This follows by considering the
\beta
-Hölder function function
v=\max\{0,\min\{u,1\}\}
since
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(v)
by Property (D3).

Definition 4.3
.

A closed set
E\subset X
satisfies a
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition,
for
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
, if there
exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

(4.4)

\cp_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r))\geq c_{0}r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.

Example 4.5
.

We say that a closed set
E\subset X
satisfies a measure density condition, if there exists
a constant
c_{1}
such that

(4.6)

\mu(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\geq c_{1}\mu(B(x,r))

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
Assume that the metric space
X
is connected,
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
, and that a set
E\subset X
satisfies
a measure density condition. Then it is easy to show that
E
satisfies a
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition, see below. We remark that the measure density condition has been applied in

[
17
]
to study Hajłasz Sobolev spaces
with zero boundary values on
E
.

Fix
x\in E
and
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
. We aim to show that (
4.4
) holds. For this purpose, we write
F=E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
and
B=B(x,r)
.
Let
u\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
be such that
0\leq u\leq 1
,
u=1
in
F
and
u=0
in
X\setminus 2B
.
Let also
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.
Recall that
X
is connected. Hence, by the properties of
u
and the reverse doubling inequality (
2.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq u_{4B}=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt4B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{4B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{4B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{4B}}u(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq\frac{\mu(2B)}{\mu(4B)}\leq c_{R}<1.

If
y\in F
, we have
v(y)=1
and therefore

\lvert u(y)-v_{4B}\rvert\geq 1-u_{4B}\geq 1-c_{R}=C(c_{\mu})>0.

Applying the measure density condition (
4.6
) and the
(\beta,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality, see Theorem
3.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle c_{1}\mu(B)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(F)\leq C(c_{\mu},p)\int_{F}\lvert u(y)-u_{4B}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},p)\int_{4B}\lvert u(y)-u_{4B}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},p)r^{\beta p}\int_{4B}g(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\leq C(c_{\mu},p)r^{\beta p}\int_{X}g(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,.

By taking infimum over functions
u
and
g
as above, we see that

\mathrm{cap}_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},2B)=\mathrm{cap}_{\beta,p}(F,2B)\geq C(c_{1},c_{\mu},p)r^{-\beta p}\mu(B)\,.

This shows that
E
satisfies a
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition (
4.4
).

The following Maz
′
ya’s inequality
provides a link between capacities and Poincaré inequalities.
We refer to
[
25
, Chapter 10]
and
[
26
, Chapter 14]

for further details on such inequalities.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<\beta\leq 1
, and let
B(z,r)\subset X
be a ball.
Assume that
u
is a
\beta
-Hölder function in
X
and
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(p)
such that

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\leq\frac{C}{\cp_{\beta,p}\bigl(\{u=0\}\cap\overline{B(z,\frac{r}{2})},B(z,r)\bigr)}\int_{B(z,r)}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.

Here
\{u=0\}=\{y\in X:u(y)=0\}
.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 5.47]
, which in turn is based on
[
1
, Theorem 5.53]
.
Let
M=\sup\{\lvert u(x)\rvert:x\in B(z,r)\}<\infty
.
By considering
\min\{M,\lvert u\rvert\}
instead of
u
and using (D3), we may assume that
u
is a bounded
\beta
-Hölder function in
X
and that
u\geq 0
in
B(z,r)
.
Write
B=B(z,r)
and

u_{B,p}=\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}u(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\mu(B)^{-\frac{1}{p}}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(B)}<\infty\,.

If
u_{B,p}=0
the claim is true, and thus we may assume that
u_{B,p}>0
.
Let

\psi(x)=\max\Bigl\{0,1-(2r^{-1})^{\beta}d\bigl(x,B(z,\tfrac{r}{2})\bigr)^{\beta}\Bigr\}\,

for every
x\in X
.
Then
0\leq\psi\leq 1
,
\psi=0
in
X\setminus B(z,r)
,
\psi=1
in
\overline{B(z,\tfrac{r}{2})}
,
and
\psi
is a
\beta
-Hölder function in
X
with a constant
(2r^{-1})^{\beta}
.
Let

v(x)=\psi(x)\biggl(1-\frac{u(x)}{u_{B,p}}\biggr)\,,\qquad x\in X\,.

Then

v=1
in
\{u=0\}\cap\overline{B(z,\tfrac{r}{2})}
and
v=0
in
X\setminus B(z,r)
.
By Lemma
3.4
, and properties (D1) and (D2), the function
v
is
\beta
-Hölder in
X
and

g_{v}=\left(\frac{g}{u_{B,p}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{\infty}+(2r^{-1})^{\beta}\bigg\lvert 1-\frac{u}{u_{B,p}}\bigg\rvert\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\psi\not=0\}}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(v)\,.

Here we used the fact that
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
by assumptions. Now, the pair
v
and
g_{v}
is admissible for testing the capacity. Thus, we obtain

(4.8)

\begin{split}&\cp_{\beta,p}\bigl(\{u=0\}\cap\overline{B(z,\tfrac{r}{2})},B(z,r)\bigr)\leq\int_{X}g_{v}(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\qquad\leq\frac{C(p)}{(u_{B,p})^{p}}\int_{B}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)+\frac{C(p)}{r^{\beta p}(u_{B,p})^{p}}\int_{B}\lvert u(x)-u_{B,p}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.\end{split}

We use Minkowski’s inequality and the
(\beta,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality in Theorem
3.7

to estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.8
), and obtain

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{B,p}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{B}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\lvert u_{B,p}-u_{B}\rvert\\
&\qquad\leq Cr^{\beta}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\mu(B)^{-\frac{1}{p}}\bigl\lvert\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}({B})}-\lVert u_{B}\rVert_{L^{p}({B})}\bigr\rvert.\end{split}

we have

\begin{split}\mu(B)^{-\frac{1}{p}}\bigl\lvert\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}({B})}-\lVert u_{B}\rVert_{L^{p}({B})}\bigr\rvert&\leq\mu(B)^{-\frac{1}{p}}\lVert u-u_{B}\rVert_{L^{p}({B})}\\

Together with (

) this gives

\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{B,p}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq Cr^{\beta}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}},

The claim follows by reorganizing the terms.

5.
Necessary and sufficient geometrical conditions

In this section we adapt the approach in
[
4
]
by
giving necessary and sufficient geometrical conditions for the
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition.
These are given in terms of the following upper Assouad codimension

[
15
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

When
E\subset X
and
r>0
, the
open
r
-neighbourhood of
E

is the set

E_{r}=\{x\in X:d(x,E)<r\}.

The upper Assouad codimension of
E\subset X
,
denoted by
\ucodima(E)
, is
the infimum of all
Q\geq 0
for
which there is
c>0
such that

\frac{\mu(E_{r}\cap B(x,R))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\geq c\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{Q}

for every
x\in E
and all
0<r<R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
If
\operatorname{diam}(E)=0
, then
the restriction
R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
is removed.

Observe that a larger
set has a smaller Assouad codimension.
We need suitable versions of Hausdorff contents
from
[
22
]
.

Definition 5.2
.

The (
\rho
-restricted) Hausdorff content of codimension
q\geq 0

of a set
F\subset X
is defined by

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{\rho}(F)=\inf\Biggl\{\sum_{k}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))\,r_{k}^{-q}:F\subset\bigcup_{k}B(x_{k},r_{k})\text{ and }0<r_{k}\leq\rho\Biggr\}.

The following lemma is
[
22
, Lemma 5.1]
.
It provides a lower bound for the Hausdorff
content of a set truncated in a fixed ball in terms of the measure and radius of the truncating ball.
The proof uses completeness via construction of a compact Cantor-type set inside
E
, to which
mass is uniformly distributed by a Carathéodory construction.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
X
is a complete metric space.
Let
E\subset X
be a closed set, and assume that

\ucodima(E)<q
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(5.4)

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\geq Cr^{-q}\mu(B(x,r))

for every
x\in E
and all
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.

On the other hand, Hausdorff contents gives a lower bound for capacity by following lemma.
The proof is based on a covering argument, where the
covering balls are chosen by chaining.
The proof is a more sophisticated variant of the argument given in Example
4.5
.
Similar
covering arguments via chaining have been widely used; see for instance
[
13
]
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that
X
is a connected metric space.
Let
0<\beta\leq 1
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
0<\eta<p
.
Assume that

F\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}

r^{\beta(p-\eta)}\cp_{\beta,p}(F,2B)\geq C\mathcal{H}^{\mu,\beta\eta}_{20r}(F)\,.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
4

for our purposes.
Let

be such that

in

in

u=0
in

Let also

We aim to cover the set

and write

,

Observe that

and

if

By the above properties of
u
and the reverse doubling inequality (

), we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq u_{B_{0}}=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}u(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq\frac{\mu(2B)}{\mu(4B)}\leq c_{R}<1.

\displaystyle=C(1-c_{R})\leq C\lvert u(x)-u_{B_{0}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lvert u_{B_{j+1}}-u_{B_{j}}\rvert\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(B_{j})}{\mu(B_{j+1})}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}\lvert u(y)-u_{B_{j}}\rvert\,d\mu(y)

.

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)\subset\{B_{x}\,:\,x\in Q^{\lambda}\}\,,\qquad\lambda>\lambda_{Q}/2\,,

of
stopping balls
such that
Q^{\lambda}\subset\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)}5B
.
Let us remark that, by the condition (W2) and stopping inequality (
7.17
), we have
B\subset U^{\lambda}

if

B\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)
and
\lambda>\lambda_{Q}/2
.

7.3.
Level set estimates

Next we prove
two technical results: Lemma
7.18
and Lemma
7.28
.
We follow the approach in
[
19
]
quite closely, but
we give details since technical modifications are required.
A counterpart of the following lemma can be found also in
[
16
, Lemma 3.1.2]
.
Recall that
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
is a fixed number and

\alpha=\beta p^{2}/(2(s+\beta p))>0
with
s=\log_{2}c_{\mu}>0
.

Lemma 7.18
.

Suppose that
\lambda>\lambda_{Q}/2
and
let
B\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)

be such that
\mu(U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda})<\mu(B)/2
.
Then

(7.19)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\mathrm{diam}(B)^{\beta p}}\int_{U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\qquad\leq C(p,c_{\mu})2^{-k\alpha}(2^{k}\lambda)^{p}\mu(U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda})+\frac{C(p,c_{\mu})}{\mathrm{diam}(B)^{\beta p}}\int_{B\setminus U^{2^{k}\lambda}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.\end{split}

Proof.

Fix
x\in U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda}\subset B
and consider
the function
h\colon(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

r\mapsto h(r)=\frac{\mu(U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda}\cap B(x,r))}{\mu(B\cap B(x,r))}=\frac{\mu(U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda}\cap B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,r))}\cdot\bigg(\frac{\mu(B\cap B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,r))}\bigg)^{-1}\,.

By Lemma
2.5
and the fact that
B
is open, we find that
h\colon(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous.
Observe that
U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda}=U^{2^{k}\lambda}\cap B
is also open. Since
h(r)=1
for small values of
r>0

and
h(r)<1/2
for
r>\operatorname{diam}(B)
, we have
h(r_{x})=1/2
for some
0<r_{x}\leq\operatorname{diam}(B)
.
Write
B^{\prime}_{x}=B(x,r_{x})
. Then

(7.20)

\frac{\mu(U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda}\cap B^{\prime}_{x})}{\mu(B\cap B^{\prime}_{x})}=h(r_{x})=\frac{1}{2}

and

(7.21)

\frac{\mu((B\setminus U^{2^{k}\lambda})\cap B^{\prime}_{x})}{\mu(B\cap B^{\prime}_{x})}=1-\frac{\mu(U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda}\cap B^{\prime}_{x})}{\mu(B\cap B^{\prime}_{x})}=1-h(r_{x})=\frac{1}{2}\,.

The
5r
-covering lemma
[
1
, Lemma 1.7]

gives us a countable disjoint family
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}\subset\{B^{\prime}_{x}\,:\,x\in U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda}\}

such that
U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda}\subset\bigcup_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}5B^{\prime}
.
Then
(
7.20
) and (
7.21
) hold for every ball
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
; namely,
by denoting
B^{\prime}_{I}=U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda}\cap B^{\prime}
and

{B^{\prime}_{O}}=(B\setminus U^{2^{k}\lambda})\cap B^{\prime}
,
we have the following comparison identities:

(7.22)

\mu(B^{\prime}_{I})=\frac{\mu(B\cap B^{\prime})}{2}=\mu({B^{\prime}_{O}})\,,

where all the measures are strictly positive. These identities
are important and they are used several times throughout the remainder of this proof.

We multiply the left-hand side of (
7.19
)
by
\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta p}
and then estimate as follows:

(7.23)

\begin{split}\int_{U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda}}&\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,d\mu\leq\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}\int_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,d\mu\\
&\leq 2^{p-1}\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}\mu(5B^{\prime}\cap B)\lvert u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}+2^{p-1}\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}\int_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}\lvert u-u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu\,.\end{split}

By (
2.1
) and Lemma
2.6
,
we find that

(7.24)

\mu(5B^{\prime}\cap B)\leq\mu(8B^{\prime})\leq c_{\mu}^{3}\mu(B^{\prime})\leq c_{\mu}^{6}\mu(B\cap B^{\prime})

for all
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
.
Hence, by the comparison identities (
7.22
),

(7.25)

\begin{split}2^{p-1}&\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}\mu(5B^{\prime}\cap B)\lvert u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}\leq C(p,c_{\mu})\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}\mu({B^{\prime}_{O}})\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt{B^{\prime}_{O}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,d\mu\\
&=C(p,c_{\mu})\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}\int_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,d\mu\leq C(p,c_{\mu})\int_{B\setminus U^{2^{k}\lambda}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,d\mu\,.\end{split}

This concludes our analysis of the ‘easy term’ in
(
7.23
).
In order to treat the remaining term therein, we do need some preparations.

Let us fix a ball
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
that satisfies

\int_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}\lvert u-u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu\not=0
.
We claim that

(7.26)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}\lvert u-u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu\leq C(p,c_{\mu})2^{-k\alpha}(2^{k}\lambda)^{p}\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta p}\,.

In order to prove this inequality, we fix a number
m\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(7.27)

(2^{m}\lambda)^{p}\operatorname{diam}(5B^{\prime})^{\beta p}=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}\lvert u-u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu\,.

Let us first consider the case
m<k/2
. Then
m-k<-k/2
, and since always
\alpha<p/2
, the desired inequality (
7.26
) is obtained case as follows:

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}\lvert u-u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=2^{(m-k)p}(2^{k}\lambda)^{p}\operatorname{diam}(5B^{\prime})^{\beta p}

\displaystyle\leq 10^{p}\,2^{-kp/2}(2^{k}\lambda)^{p}\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta p}\leq C(p)2^{-k\alpha}(2^{k}\lambda)^{p}\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta p}\,.

Next we consider the case
k/2\leq m
.
Observe from (
7.24
) and
the comparison identities (
7.22
) that

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}\lvert u-u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}\lvert u-u_{5B^{\prime}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu+2^{p-1}\lvert u_{5B^{\prime}}-u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p+1}c_{\mu}^{6}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt5B^{\prime}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}}}\lvert u-u_{5B^{\prime}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu\leq 2^{p+1}c_{\mu}^{6}(2^{k}\lambda)^{p}\mathrm{diam}(5B^{\prime})^{\beta p}\,,

where the last step follows from condition (W3) and the fact that
5B^{\prime}\supset{B^{\prime}_{O}}\not=\emptyset
.
By taking also (
7.27
) into account, we see that

2^{mp}\leq 2^{p+1}c_{\mu}^{6}2^{kp}
.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle(2^{m}\lambda)^{p}\operatorname{diam}(5B^{\prime})^{\beta p}\mu(B^{\prime}\cap B)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}\lvert u-u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,d\mu+2^{p-1}\mu(5B^{\prime}\cap B)\lvert u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p+1}c_{\mu}^{6}\int_{B}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,d\mu\leq 2\cdot 32^{p}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c_{\mu}^{11}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lambda^{p}\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta p}\mu(B)\,,

where the last step follows from the fact that
B\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)
in combination with
inequality (
7.17
).
In particular, if
s=\log_{2}c_{\mu}
then by inequality (
2.2
)
and Lemma
2.6
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{diam}(5B^{\prime})}{\operatorname{diam}(B)}\bigg)^{s+\beta p}

\displaystyle\leq 20^{s}\frac{\operatorname{diam}(5B^{\prime})^{\beta p}\mu(B^{\prime})}{\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta p}\mu(B)}\leq 20^{s}\cdot c_{\mu}^{3}\frac{\operatorname{diam}(5B^{\prime})^{\beta p}\mu(B^{\prime}\cap B)}{\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta p}\mu(B)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 20^{s}\cdot 32^{p}\cdot c_{\mu}^{14}\cdot 2^{-mp}\leq 2\cdot 20^{s}\cdot 32^{p}\cdot c_{\mu}^{14}\cdot 2^{-kp/2}\,.

This, in turn, implies that

\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{diam}(5B^{\prime})}{\operatorname{diam}(B)}\bigg)^{\beta p}\leq 2\cdot 20^{s}\cdot 32^{p}\cdot c_{\mu}^{14}\cdot 2^{\frac{-k\beta p^{2}}{2(s+\beta p)}}=C(p,c_{\mu})2^{-k\alpha}\,.

Combining the above estimates, we see that

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}\lvert u-u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu=(2^{m}\lambda)^{p}\operatorname{diam}(5B^{\prime})^{\beta p}\leq C(p,c_{\mu})2^{-k\alpha}(2^{k}\lambda)^{p}\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta p}\,.

That is, inequality (
7.26
) holds also in the present case
k/2\leq m
.
This concludes the proof of inequality (
7.26
).

By using (
7.24
) and (
7.22
) and inequality (
7.26
), we estimate the second term in (
7.23
) as follows:

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}\int_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}\lvert u-u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}c_{\mu}^{6}\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}\mu(B^{\prime}_{I})\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{5B^{\prime}\cap B}}\lvert u-u_{{B^{\prime}_{O}}}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C(p,c_{\mu})2^{-k\alpha}(2^{k}\lambda)^{p}\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta p}\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}\mu(B^{\prime}_{I})

\displaystyle\leq C(p,c_{\mu})2^{-k\alpha}(2^{k}\lambda)^{p}\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta p}\mu(U^{2^{k}\lambda}_{B})\,.

Inequality (
7.19
) follows by collecting the above estimates.
∎

The following lemma is essential for the proof of Theorem
7.6
, and it is the only place
in the proof where
the capacity density condition is needed. Recall from Lemma
6.6
that
this condition implies a local boundary Poincaré inequality, which is used here one single time.

Lemma 7.28
.

Let
\lambda>\lambda_{Q}/2
and
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
. Then

(7.29)

\begin{split}\lambda^{p}\mu(Q^{\lambda})\leq C(\beta,p,c_{\mu})\biggl[\frac{(\lambda 2^{k})^{p}}{2^{k\alpha}}\mu(U^{2^{k}\lambda})+\frac{K}{k^{p}}\sum_{j=k}^{2k-1}(\lambda 2^{j})^{p}\mu(U^{2^{j}\lambda})+K\int_{U^{\lambda}\setminus U^{4^{k}\lambda}}g^{p}\,d\mu\biggr]\,.\end{split}

Proof.

By the covering property
Q^{\lambda}\subset\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)}5B
and doubling condition (
2.1
),

\lambda^{p}\mu(Q^{\lambda})\leq\lambda^{p}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)}\mu(5B)\leq c_{\mu}^{3}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)}\lambda^{p}\mu(B)\,.

Recall also that
B\subset U^{\lambda}
if
B\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)
.
Therefore, and using the fact that
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)
is a disjoint family, it suffices
to prove that inequality

(7.30)

\begin{split}\lambda^{p}\mu(B)\leq C(\beta,p,c_{\mu})\biggl[\frac{(\lambda 2^{k})^{p}}{2^{k\alpha}}\mu(U^{2^{k}\lambda}_{B})+\frac{K}{k^{p}}\sum_{j=k}^{2k-1}(\lambda 2^{j})^{p}\mu(U_{B}^{2^{j}\lambda})+K\int_{B\setminus U^{4^{k}\lambda}}g^{p}\,d\mu\biggl]\end{split}

holds for every
B\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)
.
To this end, let us fix a ball
B\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(Q)
.

If
\mu(U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda})\geq\mu(B)/2
, then

\lambda^{p}\mu(B)\leq 2\lambda^{p}\mu(U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda})=2\frac{(\lambda 2^{k})^{p}}{2^{kp}}\mu(U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda})\leq 2\frac{(\lambda 2^{k})^{p}}{2^{k\alpha}}\mu(U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda})\,,

which suffices for the required local estimate (
7.30
).
Let us then consider the more difficult case
\mu(U_{B}^{2^{k}\lambda})<\mu(B)/2
.
In this case, by the stopping inequality (
7.17
),

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}\mu(B)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q}}(x_{B})}{\mathrm{diam}(B)^{\beta p}}\int_{B}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q}}(x_{B})}{\mathrm{diam}(B)^{\beta p}}\int_{X}\Bigl(\mathbf{1}_{B\setminus U^{2^{k}\lambda}}(x)+\mathbf{1}_{U^{2^{k}\lambda}_{B}}(x)\Bigr)\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.

By Lemma

7.18
it suffices to estimate
the integral over the set

B\setminus U^{2^{k}\lambda}=B\setminus U^{2^{k}\lambda}_{B}
;
observe that the measure of this set is strictly positive.
We remark that the local boundary Poincaré
inequality in Lemma
6.6
will be used to estimate this integral.

Fix a number
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
B\subset Q^{*}
, it follows from Lemma
7.14
that the restriction
u|_{E_{Q}\cup(B\setminus U^{2^{i}\lambda})}
is a

\beta
-Hölder function with a constant
\kappa_{i}=C(\beta,c_{\mu})2^{i}\lambda
.
We can now use the McShane extension (
2.7
) and extend

u|_{E_{Q}\cup(B\setminus U^{2^{i}\lambda})}

to a function
u_{2^{i}\lambda}\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}

that is
\beta
-Hölder with the constant
\kappa_{i}

and satisfies the restriction identity

u_{2^{i}\lambda}(x)=u(x)

for all
x\in E_{Q}\cup(B\setminus U^{2^{i}\lambda})
.
Observe that
u_{2^{i}\lambda}=0
in
E_{Q}
, since
u=0
in
E_{Q}
.

The crucial idea that was originally used by Keith–Zhong in
[
16
]
is to consider the function

h(x)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=k}^{2k-1}u_{2^{i}\lambda}(x)\,,\qquad x\in X\,.

We want to apply Lemma
3.3
. In order to do so, observe that
u_{2^{i}\lambda}|_{X\setminus A}=u|_{X\setminus A}
,
where

A=X\setminus(B\setminus U^{2^{i}\lambda})=X\setminus(B\setminus U_{B}^{2^{i}\lambda})=(X\setminus B)\cup U^{2^{i}\lambda}_{B}\,.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.3

and properties (D1)–(D2), we obtain that

g_{h}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=k}^{2k-1}\Bigl(\kappa_{i}\mathbf{1}_{(X\setminus B)\cup U^{2^{i}\lambda}_{B}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}}+g\mathbf{1}_{B\setminus U^{2^{i}\lambda}}\Bigr)\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(h)\,.

Observe that
U^{2^{k}\lambda}_{B}\supset U^{2^{(k+1)}\lambda}_{B}\supset\dotsb\supset U^{2^{(2k-1)}\lambda}_{B}\supset U^{4^{k}\lambda}_{B}
.
By using these inclusions it is straightforward to show that the following
pointwise estimates are valid in
X
,

\begin{split}\mathbf{1}_{B}g_{h}^{p}&\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=k}^{2k-1}\Bigl(\kappa_{i}\,\mathbf{1}_{U_{B}^{2^{i}\lambda}}+g\mathbf{1}_{B\setminus U^{2^{i}\lambda}}\Bigr)\bigg)^{p}\\
&\leq 2^{p}\bigg(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=k}^{2k-1}\kappa_{i}\,\mathbf{1}_{U_{B}^{2^{i}\lambda}}\bigg)^{p}+2^{p}g^{p}\mathbf{1}_{B\setminus U^{4^{k}\lambda}}\\
&\leq\frac{C(\beta,p,c_{\mu})}{k^{p}}\sum_{j=k}^{2k-1}\bigg(\sum_{i=k}^{j}2^{i}\lambda\bigg)^{p}\mathbf{1}_{U_{B}^{2^{j}\lambda}}+2^{p}g^{p}\mathbf{1}_{B\setminus U^{4^{k}\lambda}}\\
&\leq\frac{C(\beta,p,c_{\mu})}{k^{p}}\sum_{j=k}^{2k-1}(\lambda 2^{j})^{p}\mathbf{1}_{U_{B}^{2^{j}\lambda}}+2^{p}g^{p}\mathbf{1}_{B\setminus U^{4^{k}\lambda}}\,.\end{split}

Observe that
h\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
is zero in
E_{Q}
and
h
coincides with
u
on
B\setminus U^{2^{k}\lambda}
,
and recall that
g_{h}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(h)
. Notice also that
B\subset Q^{*}
and
x_{B}\in E_{Q}
.
The local boundary Poincaré inequality in Lemma
6.6
implies
that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q}}(x_{B})}{\mathrm{diam}(B)^{\beta p}}\int_{B\setminus U^{2^{k}\lambda}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)=\frac{\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q}}(x_{B})}{\mathrm{diam}(B)^{\beta p}}\int_{B}\lvert h(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{B}g_{h}(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\beta,p,c_{\mu})K}{k^{p}}\sum_{j=k}^{2k-1}(\lambda 2^{j})^{p}\mu(U_{B}^{2^{j}\lambda})+2^{p}K\int_{B\setminus U^{4^{k}\lambda}}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.

The desired local inequality (
7.30
) follows by combining the estimates above.
∎

Completing proof of Theorem

We complete the proof as in

]
.
Recall that
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
\beta
-Hölder function with

u=0
in
E_{Q}
and
that

M^{\sharp}u+M^{E_{Q}}u=M^{\sharp,p}_{\beta,\mathcal{B}_{0}}u+M^{E_{Q},p}_{\beta,\mathcal{B}_{0}}u\,.

Let us fix a function
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.
Observe that the left-hand side of
inequality (
7.7
) is finite. Without loss of generality, we may further assume
that it is nonzero.
By Lemma
7.11
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}}\big(M^{\sharp}u(x)+M^{E_{Q}}u(x)\big)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(p,c_{\mu})\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\int_{B_{0}}\big(M^{E_{Q}}_{Q}u(x)\big)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)+C(\beta,p,c_{\mu})\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\int_{B_{0}}g(x)^{p-\varepsilon}(x)\,d\mu(x)\,.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}}\big(M^{E_{Q}}_{Q}u(x)\big)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)=\int_{Q^{*}}\big(M^{E_{Q}}_{Q}u(x)\big)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)=(p-\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-\varepsilon}\mu(Q^{\lambda})\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\,.

Since
Q^{\lambda}=Q^{*}=Q^{2\lambda}
for every
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{Q}/2)
,
we find that

\displaystyle(p-\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{\lambda_{Q}/2}\lambda^{p-\varepsilon}\mu(Q^{\lambda})\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-\varepsilon)}{2^{p-\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{Q}/2}(2\lambda)^{p-\varepsilon}\mu(Q^{2\lambda})\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(p-\varepsilon)}{2^{p-\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{p-\varepsilon}\mu(Q^{\sigma})\,\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{p-\varepsilon}}\int_{Q^{*}}\big(M^{E_{Q}}_{Q}u(x)\big)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)\,.

On the other hand, by Lemma
7.28
, for each
\lambda>\lambda_{Q}/2
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{p-\varepsilon}\mu(Q^{\lambda})\leq C(\beta,p,c_{\mu})\lambda^{-\varepsilon}\biggl[\frac{(\lambda 2^{k})^{p}}{2^{k\alpha}}\mu(U^{2^{k}\lambda})+\frac{K}{k^{p}}\sum_{j=k}^{2k-1}(\lambda 2^{j})^{p}\mu(U^{2^{j}\lambda})+K\int_{U^{\lambda}\setminus U^{4^{k}\lambda}}g^{p}\,d\mu\,\biggr].

Since
p-\varepsilon>1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{Q^{*}}\big(M^{E_{Q}}_{Q}u(x)\big)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2(p-\varepsilon)\int_{\lambda_{Q}/2}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-\varepsilon}\mu(Q^{\lambda})\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq C(\beta,p,c_{\mu})(I_{1}(Q)+I_{2}(Q)+I_{3}(Q))\,,

where

\displaystyle I_{1}(Q)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{k\varepsilon}}{2^{k\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\lambda 2^{k})^{p-\varepsilon}\mu(U^{2^{k}\lambda})\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle I_{2}(Q)

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{k^{p}}\sum_{j=k}^{2k-1}2^{j\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}(2^{j}\lambda)^{p-\varepsilon}\mu(U^{2^{j}\lambda})\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\,,

\displaystyle I_{3}(Q)

\displaystyle=K\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\varepsilon}\int_{U^{\lambda}\setminus U^{4^{k}\lambda}}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\,.

We estimate these three terms separately. First,

\displaystyle I_{1}(Q)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{k(\varepsilon-\alpha)}}{p-\varepsilon}\int_{B_{0}}\big(M^{\sharp}u(x)+M^{E_{Q}}u(x)\big)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{k(\varepsilon-\alpha)}\int_{B_{0}}\big(M^{\sharp}u(x)+M^{E_{Q}}u(x)\big)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)\,.

Second,

\displaystyle I_{2}(Q)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{k^{p}}\sum_{j=k}^{2k-1}2^{j\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}(2^{j}\lambda)^{p-\varepsilon}\mu(U^{2^{j}\lambda})\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{k^{p}(p-\varepsilon)}\bigg(\sum_{j=k}^{2k-1}2^{j\varepsilon}\bigg)\int_{B_{0}}\big(M^{\sharp}u(x)+M^{E_{Q}}u(x)\big)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K4^{k\varepsilon}}{k^{p-1}}\int_{B_{0}}\big(M^{\sharp}u(x)+M^{E_{Q}}u(x)\big)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu\,.

Third, by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle I_{3}(Q)

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{B_{0}\setminus\{M^{\sharp}u+M^{E_{Q}}u=0\}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\varepsilon}\mathbf{1}_{U^{\lambda}\setminus U^{4^{k}\lambda}}(x)\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C(k,\varepsilon)K\int_{B_{0}\setminus\{M^{\sharp}u+M^{E_{Q}}u=0\}}g(x)^{p}(M^{\sharp}u(x)+M^{E_{Q}}u(x))^{-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)\,.

Combining the estimates above, we arrive at the desired conclusion.
∎

8.
Local Hardy inequalities

We apply Theorem
7.4
in order to obtain a local Hardy
inequality, see (
8.2
) in Theorem
8.1
. This inequality is then shown to be self-improving, see Theorem
8.4
, and
in this respect we follow the strategy in
[
23
]
.
However, we remark that the easier Wannebo approach
[
30
]
for establishing local Hardy inequalities as in
[
23
]

is not available to us, due to absence of pointwise Leibniz and chain rules
in the setting of Hajłasz gradients.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
X
be a geodesic space. Let

1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
. Let
E\subset X
be a closed
set which satisfies the
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition with a constant
c_{0}
.
Let
B_{0}=B(w,R)
be a ball with
w\in E
and
R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
Let
E_{Q}
be the truncation
of
E
to the Whitney-type ball
Q
as in Section
6
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(\beta,p,c_{\mu},c_{0})
such that

(8.2)

\int_{B(w,R)\setminus E_{Q}}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)\leq C\int_{B(w,R)}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)

holds whenever
u\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
is such that
u=0
in
E_{Q}
and
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
be such that
u=0
in
E_{Q}
and let
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.
Lemma
7.14
implies that

\lvert u(x)\rvert\leq C(\beta,c_{\mu})d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta}\left(M^{\sharp,p}_{\beta,\mathcal{B}_{0}}u(x)+M^{E_{Q},p}_{\beta,\mathcal{B}_{0}}u(x)\right)

for all
x\in Q^{*}
. Therefore

\int_{Q^{*}\setminus E_{Q}}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)\leq C(\beta,p,c_{\mu})\int_{B(w,R)}\left(M^{\sharp,p}_{\beta,\mathcal{B}_{0}}u(x)+M^{E_{Q},p}_{\beta,\mathcal{B}_{0}}u(x)\right)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.

By Theorem
7.4
, we obtain

(8.3)

\int_{Q^{*}\setminus E_{Q}}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)\leq C(\beta,p,c_{\mu},c_{0})\int_{B(w,R)}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.

It remains to bound the integral over
B(w,R)\setminus Q^{*}
. Since
E_{Q}\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle Q}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{Q}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle Q}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{Q}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle Q}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{Q}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle Q}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{Q}}}
and
Q^{*}=4Q
, we have
d(x,E_{Q})\geq 3r_{Q}>R/64

for all
x\in B(w,R)\setminus Q^{*}
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B(w,R)\setminus Q^{*}}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)\leq\frac{64^{\beta p}}{R^{\beta p}}\int_{B(w,R)}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3^{p}64^{\beta p}}{R^{\beta p}}\left(\int_{B(w,R)}\lvert u(x)-u_{B(w,R)}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)+\mu(B(w,R))\lvert u_{B(w,R)}-u_{Q^{*}}\rvert^{p}+\mu(B(w,R))\lvert u_{Q^{*}}\rvert^{p}\right)\,.

By the
(\beta,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality in Lemma
3.7
,

\displaystyle\int_{B(w,R)}\lvert u(x)-u_{B(w,R)}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2^{p}\operatorname{diam}(B(w,R))^{\beta p}\int_{B(w,R)}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\leq C(p)R^{\beta p}\int_{B(w,R)}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.

For the second term, we have

\displaystyle\mu(B(w,R))\lvert u_{B(w,R)}-u_{Q^{*}}\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mu(B(w,R))\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ^{*}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q^{*}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q^{*}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q^{*}}}\lvert u(x)-u_{B(w,R)}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(c_{\mu})\int_{B(w,R)}\lvert u(x)-u_{B(w,R)}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\leq C(p,c_{\mu})R^{\beta p}\int_{B(w,R)}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.

For the third term, we have
d(x,E_{Q})\leq d(x,w)<4r_{Q}<R
for every
x\in Q^{*}
. Thus

\displaystyle\mu(B(w,R))\lvert u_{Q^{*}}\rvert^{p}\leq C(c_{\mu})\int_{Q^{*}\setminus E_{Q}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\leq R^{\beta p}\int_{Q^{*}\setminus E_{Q}}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)\,.

Applying inequality (
8.3
), we get

\mu(B(w,R))\lvert u_{Q^{*}}\rvert^{p}\leq C(\beta,p,c_{\mu},c_{0})R^{\beta p}\int_{B(w,R)}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.

The desired inequality follows by combining the estimates above.
∎

Next we improve the local Hardy inequality
in Theorem
8.1
. This is done
by adapting the Koskela–Zhong truncation argument from
[
21
]
to the setting
of Hajłasz gradients; see also

[
23
]
and
[
18
, Theorem 7.32]
whose proof we modify to our purposes.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
X
be a geodesic space. Let

1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
. Let
E\subset X
be a closed
set which satisfies the
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition with a constant
c_{0}
.
Let
B_{0}=B(w,R)
be a ball with
w\in E
and
R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
Let
E_{Q}
be the truncation
of
E
to the Whitney-type ball
Q
as in Section
6
,
and let
C_{1}=C_{1}(\beta,p,c_{\mu},c_{0})
be the constant in (
8.2
), see Theorem
8.1
.
Then there exist
0<\varepsilon=\varepsilon(p,C_{1})<p-1

and
C=C(p,C_{1})
such that inequality

(8.5)

\int_{B(w,R)\setminus E_{Q}}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p-\varepsilon}}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta(p-\varepsilon)}}\,d\mu(x)\leq C\int_{B(w,R)}g(x)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)

holds whenever
u\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
is such that
u=0
in
E_{Q}
and
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
C_{1}\geq 1
in (
8.2
).
Let
u\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
be such that
u=0
in
E_{Q}
and let
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.
Let
\kappa\geq 0
be the
\beta
-Hölder constant of
u
in
X
.
By redefining
g=\kappa
in the exceptional set
N=N(g)
of measure zero, we may assume that
(
3.2
) holds for all
x,y\in X
.
Let
\lambda>0
and define
F_{\lambda}=G_{\lambda}\cap H_{\lambda}
, where

G_{\lambda}=\bigl\{x\in B(w,R):g(x)\leq\lambda\bigr\}

and

H_{\lambda}=\{x\in B(w,R):\lvert u(x)\rvert\leq\lambda d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta}\}.

We show that the restriction of
u
to
F_{\lambda}\cup E_{Q}
is
a
\beta
-Hölder function with a constant
2\lambda
. Assume
that
x,y\in F_{\lambda}
. Then (
3.2
) implies

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}\left(g(x)+g(y)\right)\leq 2\lambda d(x,y)^{\beta}\,.

On the other hand, if
x\in F_{\lambda}
and
y\in E_{Q}
, then

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert=\lvert u(x)\rvert\leq\lambda d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta}\leq 2\lambda d(x,y)^{\beta}\,.

The case
x\in E_{Q}
and
y\in F_{\lambda}
is treated in the same way. If
x,y\in E_{Q}
, then
\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert=0
.
All in all, we see that
u
is a
\beta
-Hölder function in
F_{\lambda}\cup E_{Q}
with a constant
2\lambda
.

We apply the McShane extension
2.7
and extend the restriction
u|_{F_{\lambda}\cup E_{Q}}

to a
\beta
-Hölder function function
v
in
X
with constant
2\lambda
.
Then
v=u=0
in
E_{Q}
and
v=u
in
F_{\lambda}
, thus

g_{v}=g\mathbf{1}_{F_{\lambda}}+2\lambda\mathbf{1}_{X\setminus F_{\lambda}}\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(v)

by Lemma
3.3
.

By applying Theorem
8.1
to the
function
v
and its Hajłasz
\beta
-gradient
g_{v}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{(B(w,R)\setminus E_{Q})\cap F_{\lambda}}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(w,R)\setminus E_{Q}}\frac{\lvert v(x)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{F_{\lambda}}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)+C_{1}2^{p}\lambda^{p}\mu(B(w,R)\setminus F_{\lambda})\,.

Since
H_{\lambda}=F_{\lambda}\cup(H_{\lambda}\setminus G_{\lambda})
and
C_{1}\geq 1
, it follows that

(8.6)

\begin{split}&\int_{(B(w,R)\setminus E_{Q})\cap H_{\lambda}}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\qquad\leq C_{1}\int_{F_{\lambda}}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)+C_{1}2^{p}\lambda^{p}\mu(B(w,R)\setminus F_{\lambda})\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{(H_{\lambda}\setminus E_{Q})\setminus G_{\lambda}}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta p}}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\qquad\leq C_{1}\int_{G_{\lambda}}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)+C_{1}2^{p}\lambda^{p}\bigl(\mu(B(w,R)\setminus F_{\lambda})+\mu(H_{\lambda}\setminus G_{\lambda})\bigr)\\
&\qquad\leq C_{1}\int_{G_{\lambda}}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)+C_{1}2^{p+1}\lambda^{p}\bigl(\mu(B(w,R)\setminus H_{\lambda})+\mu(B(w,R)\setminus G_{\lambda})\bigr).\end{split}

Here
\lambda>0
was arbitrary, and thus we conclude that (
8.6
) holds for every
\lambda>0
.

Next we multiply (
8.6
) by
\lambda^{-1-\varepsilon}
,
where
0<\varepsilon<p-1
, and integrate with respect to
\lambda
over the set
(0,\infty)
.
With a change of the order of integration on the left-hand side, this gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{B(w,R)\setminus E_{Q}}\biggl(\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta}}\biggr)^{p-{\varepsilon}}\,d\mu(x)\leq C_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-1-\varepsilon}\int_{G_{\lambda}}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{1}2^{p+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1-\varepsilon}\bigl(\mu(B(w,R)\setminus H_{\lambda})+\mu(B(w,R)\setminus G_{\lambda})\bigr)\,d\lambda\,.

By the definition of
G_{\lambda}
, we find that the first term
on the right-hand side is dominated by

\frac{C_{1}}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{B(w,R)}g(x)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)\,.

Using the definitions
of
H_{\lambda}
and
G_{\lambda}
, the second term on the right-hand side
can be estimated from above by

\frac{C_{1}2^{p+1}}{p-\varepsilon}\biggl(\int_{B(w,R)\setminus E_{Q}}\biggl(\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta}}\biggr)^{p-{\varepsilon}}\,d\mu(x)+\int_{B(w,R)}g(x)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)\biggr).

By combining the estimates above, we obtain

(8.7)

\begin{split}&\int_{B(w,R)\setminus E_{Q}}\biggl(\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta}}\biggr)^{p-{\varepsilon}}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\qquad\leq C_{2}\int_{B(w,R)\setminus E_{Q}}\biggl(\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta}}\biggr)^{p-{\varepsilon}}\,d\mu(x)+C_{3}\int_{B(w,R)}g(x)^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)\,,\end{split}

where
C_{2}=C_{1}2^{p+1}\frac{\varepsilon}{p-\varepsilon}

and
C_{3}=C_{1}\bigl(1+2^{p+1}\frac{\varepsilon}{p-\varepsilon}\bigr)
. We choose

0<\varepsilon=\varepsilon(C_{1},p)<p-1
so small that

C_{2}=C_{1}2^{p+1}\frac{\varepsilon}{p-\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2}\,.

This allows us to absorb the first term in the right-hand side of (
8.7
) to the left-hand side.
Observe that this term is finite, since
u
is
\beta
-Hölder in
X
and
u=0
in
E_{Q}
.
∎

9.
Self-improvement of the capacity density condition

As an application of Theorem
8.4
, we strengthen Theorem
5.8
in complete geodesic spaces.
This leads to the conclusion that the Hajłasz capacity density condition
is self-improving or doubly open-ended in such spaces.
In fact, we characterize the Hajłasz capacity density condition in various geometrical and
analytical quantities, the latter of which are all shown to be doubly open-ended.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
X
be a geodesic space. Let

1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
. Let
E\subset X
be a closed
set which satisfies the
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition with a constant
c_{0}
.
Then
there exists
\varepsilon>0
, depending
on
\beta
,
p
,
c_{\mu}
and
c_{0}
, such that

\ucodima(E)\leq\beta(p-\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Let
w\in E
and
0<r<R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
Let
E_{Q}
be the truncation
of
E
to the ball
Q\subset B_{0}=B(w,R)
as in Section
6
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be as in Theorem
8.4
.
Observe that

E_{Q,r}=\{x\in X\,:\,d(x,E_{Q})<r\}\subset\{x\in X\,:\,d(x,E)<r\}=E_{r}\,.

Hence, it suffices to show that

(9.2)

\frac{\mu(E_{Q,r}\cap B(w,R))}{\mu(B(w,R))}\geq c\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{\beta(p-\varepsilon)},

where the constant
c
is independent of
w
,
r
and
R
.

If
r\geq R/4
, then the claim is clear since
\bigl(\frac{r}{R}\bigr)^{\beta(p-\varepsilon)}\leq 1
and

\mu(E_{Q,r}\cap B(w,R))\geq\mu(B(w,R/4))\geq C(\mu)\mu(B(w,R))\,.

The claim is clear also if
\mu(E_{Q,r}\cap B(w,R))\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu(B(w,R))
.
Thus we may assume that
r<R/4
and that

\mu(E_{Q,r}\cap B(w,R))<\tfrac{1}{2}\mu(B(w,R))
, whence

(9.3)

\mu(B(w,R)\setminus E_{Q,r})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\mu(B(w,R))>0.

Let us now consider the
\beta
-Hölder function
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

u(x)=\min\{1,r^{-\beta}d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta}\}\,,\qquad x\in X.

Then
u=0
in
E_{Q}
,
u=1
in
X\setminus E_{Q,r}
, and

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq r^{-\beta}d(x,y)^{\beta}\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

We aim to apply Theorem
8.4
. Recall also that
w\in E_{Q}
. Thus we obtain

(9.4)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{0}\setminus E_{Q}}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p-\varepsilon}}{d(x,E_{Q})^{\beta(p-\varepsilon)}}\,d\mu(x)&\geq R^{-\beta(p-\varepsilon)}\int_{B_{0}\setminus E_{Q}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\geq R^{-\beta(p-\varepsilon)}\int_{B_{0}\setminus E_{Q,r}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\geq R^{-\beta(p-\varepsilon)}\mu(B(w,R)\setminus E_{Q,r})\\
&\geq 2^{-1}R^{-\beta(p-\varepsilon)}\mu(B(w,R))\,,\end{split}

where the last step follows from (
9.3
).

Since
u=1
in
X\setminus E_{Q,r}
and

u
is a
\beta
-Hölder function with a constant
r^{-\beta}
,
Lemma
3.3
implies that

g=r^{-\beta}\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q,r}}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.
Observe that

\int_{B_{0}}g^{p-\varepsilon}\,d\mu\leq r^{-\beta(p-\varepsilon)}\mu(E_{Q,r}\cap B_{0})=r^{-\beta(p-\varepsilon)}\mu(E_{Q,r}\cap B(w,R))\,.

Hence, the claim (
9.2
) follows from (
9.4
) and
Theorem
8.4
.
∎

The following theorem is a compilation of the results in this paper. It states the equivalence of some geometrical conditions (1)–(2) and analytical conditions (3)–(6), one of which is the capacity density condition.
We emphasize that the capacity density condition (3)
is characterized in terms of the upper Assouad codimension (1); in fact, this characterization follows
immediately from Theorem
5.7
and Theorem
9.1
.

Theorem 9.5
.

Let
X
be a complete geodesic space. Let

1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
. Let
E\subset X
be a closed
set. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\ucodima(E)<\beta p
.

(2)

E
satisfies the Hausdorff content
density condition (
5.4
) for some
0<q<\beta p
.

(3)

E
satisfies the
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition.

(4)

E
satisfies the local
(\beta,p,p)
-boundary Poincaré inequality (
6.7
).

(5)

E
satisfies the maximal
(\beta,p,p)
-boundary Poincaré inequality
(
7.5
).

(6)

E
satisfies the local
(\beta,p,p)
-Hardy inequality (
8.2
).

Proof.

Finally, we state the main result of this paper, Theorem
9.6
. It is the self-improvement or double open-endedness
property of the
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition. Namely, in addition to
integrability exponent
p
, also the order
\beta
of fractional differentiability can be lowered.
A similar phenomenon is observed
in
[
24
]
for Riesz capacities in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Theorem 9.6
.

Let
X
be a complete geodesic space, and let

1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
. Assume that
a closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition.
Then there exists
0<\delta<\min\{\beta,p-1\}
such that

E
satisfies the
(\gamma,q)
-capacity density condition for all

\beta-\delta<\gamma\leq 1
and
p-\delta<q<\infty
.

Proof.

We have
\ucodima(E)<\beta p
by Theorem
9.5
.
Since
\lim_{\delta\to 0}(\beta-\delta)(p-\delta)=\beta p
,
there exists

0<\delta<\min\{\beta,p-1\}
such that

\ucodima(E)<(\beta-\delta)(p-\delta)
.
Now if
\beta-\delta<\gamma\leq 1
and
p-\delta<q<\infty
, then

\ucodima(E)<(\beta-\delta)(p-\delta)<\gamma q\,.

The claim
follows from Theorem
9.5
.
∎

A similar argument shows that the analytical conditions (4)–(6) in Theorem
9.5
are also doubly open ended. The geometrical conditions (1)–(2) are open-ended by definition.

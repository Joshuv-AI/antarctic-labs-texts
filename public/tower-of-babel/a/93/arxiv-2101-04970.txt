Can we make sense out of “Tensor Field Theory”?
V. Rivasseau & F. Vignes-Tourneret

Abstract




Can we make sense out of “Tensor Field Theory”?

\dedicatory

{fabsmfabstract}

We continue the constructive program about tensor field theory through
the next natural model, namely the rank five tensor theory with
quartic melonicinteractions and propagator inverse of the
Laplacian on
U(1)^{5}
. We make a first step towards its construction by establishing its power counting, identifiying the divergent graphs and performing a careful study of (a slight modification of) its RG flow. Thus we give strong evidence that this just renormalizable tensor field theory is non perturbatively asymptotically free.

4
Perturbative renormalization

4.1
Renormalized 1PI functions

The renormalization consists in a melonic BPHZ scheme which is given by BPHZ-like normalization conditions at zero external momenta, but restricted to the divergent sector, namely melonic graphs
8
8

8

The true BPHZ prescription in standard field theory imposes conditions on the full 1PI functions of the theory, not just their melonic part. This is because all 1PI graphs diverge in standard field theory. In this tensorial theory since non-melonic graphs are convergent the full BPHZ prescription is not minimal, and differs from the melonic BPHZ prescription only by unnecessary finite renormalizations.
.

The standard renormalization procedure expresses the 1PI correlation
functions in terms of renormalized quantities through a Taylor
expansion:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{b}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq C_{b}^{-1}-\Sigma^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{b}},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{b}}(\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{b}}(0)+\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{mr}}(\mathbf{n})=\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{b}}(0)+\mathbf{n}^{2}\frac{\partial\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{b}}}{\partial\mathbf{n}^{2}}(0)+\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{r}}(\mathbf{n}),

\displaystyle\overline{\Gamma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4,\textit{b}}(n_{c},\overline{n}_{c})

\displaystyle=\overline{\Gamma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4,\textit{b}}(0,0)+\overline{\Gamma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4,\textit{r}}(n_{c},\overline{n}_{c})

where the subscript
mr
means mass-renormalized, and

r
means full renormalization, with

m_{r}^{2}=Z_{b}m_{b}^{2}-\Sigma^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{b}}(0),\quad 1=Z_{r}=Z_{b}-\frac{\partial\overline{\Sigma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{b}}}{\partial n_{c}^{2}}(0),\quad\overline{\Gamma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4,\textit{b}}(0,0)=-g_{r}Z_{r}^{2}=-g_{r}.

Consequently we have the usual renormalization conditions

\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{mr}}(0)=\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{r}}(0)=0,\quad\frac{\partial\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{r}}}{\partial n^{2}}(0)=0,\quad\overline{\Gamma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4,\textit{r}}(0,0)=0.

The full
melonic
two-point function is therefore

G^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{b}}(\mathbf{n})=\frac{\kappa_{j_{\text{max}}}(\mathbf{n})}{\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{b}}(\mathbf{n})}=\frac{\kappa_{j_{\text{max}}}(\mathbf{n})}{m_{r}^{2}+\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{mr}}(\mathbf{n})}=\frac{\kappa_{j_{\text{max}}}(\mathbf{n})}{\mathbf{n}^{2}+m_{r}^{2}+\Gamma^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{r}}(\mathbf{n})}

so that in particular
G^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2,\textit{b}}(0)=m_{r}^{-2}
.

4.2
Mass renormalization

Let us start by performing the mass renormalization, and postpone the wave-function and four-point
coupling constant renormalization to the next section. Indeed mass renormalization is simpler as it does not involve
renormalons
[
Riv91
]
. So throughout this section we keep the bare coupling constant
g_{b}
, and the bare wave-function normalization
Z_{b}
.

The mass renormalization subtracts recursively the value of all
subinsertions at
0
external momentum. Hence, recalling
eq.

3.3
, the monochrome
melonic mass-renormalized self-energy
\overline{\Sigma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{mr}}
obeys the closed equation

\overline{\Sigma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{mr}}(n_{c})=\overline{\Sigma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{b}}(n_{c})-\overline{\Sigma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{b}}(0)=-g_{b}Z_{b}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{5}}\kappa_{j_{\text{max}}}(\mathbf{p})\frac{\delta_{p_{c},n_{c}}-\delta_{p_{c},0}}{Z_{b}\mathbf{p}^{2}+m_{r}^{2}-\sum_{c^{\prime}}\overline{\Sigma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{mr}}(p_{c^{\prime}})}.

The sum over
\mathbf{p}
in the equation above diverges only
logarithmically as
j_{\text{max}}\to\infty
. The total mass counterterm is

\displaystyle\delta_{m}

\displaystyle=m_{r}^{2}-Z_{b}m_{b}^{2}=g_{b}Z_{b}^{2}\sum_{c}\delta_{m}^{c},

\displaystyle\delta_{m}^{c}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{5}}\frac{\kappa_{j_{\text{max}}}(\mathbf{p})\,\delta_{p_{c},0}}{Z_{b}\mathbf{p}^{2}+m_{r}^{2}-\sum_{c^{\prime}}\overline{\Sigma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{mr}}(p_{c^{\prime}})}=\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}}\frac{\kappa_{j_{\text{max}}}(0,\mathbf{p})}{Z_{b}\mathbf{p}^{2}+m_{r}^{2}-\sum_{c^{\prime}\neq c}\overline{\Sigma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{mr}}(p_{c^{\prime}})}

where we used that
\overline{\Sigma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{mr}}(0)=0
. Remark that
\delta_{m}^{c}
is independent of
c
, so that in fact

\delta_{m}=5g_{b}Z_{b}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}}\frac{\kappa_{j_{\text{max}}}(0,\mathbf{p})}{Z_{b}\mathbf{p}^{2}+m_{r}^{2}-\sum_{c=1}^{4}\overline{\Sigma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{mr}}(p_{c})}.

4.3
Effective renormalization

We want to perform only the effective (or “useful”) part of the coupling constant and
wave-function renormalizations, that is when the
inner loop slice is higher than the external one.

4.3.1
Multiscale decomposition

After full mass renormalization, all correlation functions, as formal
power series in
g_{b}
, are expressed as sums over Feynman graphs
with mass-renormalized amplitudes containing mass-renormalized
propagators,
i.e.

C_{mr}(\mathbf{n})\coloneqq\frac{\kappa_{j_{\text{max}}}(\mathbf{n}^{2})}{Z_{b}\mathbf{n}^{2}+m_{r}^{2}}.

Multiscale analysis amounts to decompose the
(mass-renormalized here) propagator into a sum of sclice propagators

C^{\raisebox{0.3pt}{\scalebox{.6}{[$j$]}}}_{mr}(\mathbf{n})
where for each

j\in\mathinner{\left\{0,1,\dotsc,j_{\text{max}}\right\}}
,
C^{\raisebox{0.3pt}{\scalebox{.6}{[$j$]}}}_{mr}(\mathbf{n})
ensures that

\mathbf{n}^{2}
is of order
M^{2j}
. Similarly to
Section

1.2
we define

\displaystyle\kappa_{j}(\mathbf{n}^{2})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbf{1}_{[-\frac{5}{2},\frac{5}{2}]}\star\chi_{\frac{3}{2}}(M^{-2j}\mathbf{n}^{2})=\kappa(M^{-2j}\mathbf{n}^{2}),\quad 0\leqslant j\leqslant j_{\text{max}}

and decompose
\kappa_{j_{\text{max}}}
as follows

\displaystyle\kappa_{j_{\text{max}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{j_{\text{max}}}\eta_{j},\quad\eta_{j}=\kappa_{j}-\kappa_{j-1}\text{ for $1\leqslant j\leqslant j_{\text{max}}$,}\quad\eta_{0}=\kappa_{0}.

Provided
M^{2}>2
,
\eta_{j}
(
j>0
) is positive, smooth, and satisfies

\eta_{j}(\mathbf{n}^{2})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\mathbf{n}^{2}<M^{-2}M^{2j}$ or $\mathbf{n}^{2}>2M^{2j}$,}\\
1&\text{if $\frac{2}{M^{2}}M^{2j}\leqslant\mathbf{n}^{2}\leqslant M^{2j}$.}\end{cases}

As a consequence, we define
C^{\raisebox{0.3pt}{\scalebox{.6}{[$j$]}}}_{mr}(\mathbf{n})
as

\eta_{j}(\mathbf{n}^{2})\mathinner{\left(Z_{b}\mathbf{n}^{2}+m_{r}^{2}\right)}^{-1}
so that

C_{mr}(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{j=0}^{j_{\text{max}}}C^{\raisebox{0.3pt}{\scalebox{.6}{[$j$]}}}_{mr}(\mathbf{n})
. The
decomposition of each propagator in the amplitude
A_{mr}(G)
of any
Feynman graph
G
allows to write

A_{mr}(G)=\sum_{\mu\in[j_{\text{max}}]^{e(G)}}A_{mr}^{\mu}(G).

e(G)
is the number of internal edges of
G
.
\mu
is called a scale
attribution and corresponds to choosing one index
j_{\ell}
in
[j_{\text{max}}]
for each
internal edge
\ell
of
G
so that the corresponding propagator is
C^{\raisebox{0.3pt}{\scalebox{.6}{[$j_{\ell}$]}}}_{mr}
.

4.4

This section is devoted to proving that the effective wave-functions and coupling constants are analytic functions of the
bare coupling

(in a disk of radius going to

According to

According to

, the monochrome 1PI generating function

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4}

\displaystyle=x\frac{\mathinner{\bigl(1-5\overline{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{\textup{mel}}\bigr)}^{2}}{\mathinner{\bigl(1-5\overline{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{\textup{mel}}\bigr)}^{2}-x}

which, from

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4}

\displaystyle=x\frac{1-5x-5\overline{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{\textup{mel}}}{1-6x-5\overline{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{\textup{mel}}}.

By a very simple application of the transfer theorems of Flajolet and Sedgewick
[

, chapter VI]
, the coefficients of

are asymptotically equal to
\frac{20^{n}}{64\sqrt{\pi n^{3}}}

{hproof}

The asymptotic value of the coefficients of
\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4}
is fixed by its behaviour near its dominant singularity, namely the one closest to the origin. From

4.1
,

\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2}(x)=\frac{1}{10}\mathinner{\bigl(1-\sqrt{1-20x}\bigr)}

so that
\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4}
has a branch point at

and other possible singularities at the roots of

. It turns out that this last equation has no complex solution on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant\frac{1}{20}}
(but it has two roots on the second sheet of the Riemann surface of the square root). It can indeed be checked that
1-20x=(12x-1)^{2}
has two roots,
0
and
\frac{1}{36}
, which are solutions of
-\sqrt{1-20x}=12x-1
. As a consequence, the asymptotic behaviour of the coefficients of
\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!2}
is given by the coefficients of its expansion around

. But

\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4}=\frac{1}{16}-\frac{1}{32}\sqrt{1-20x}+\bO(1-20x)

so that

[x^{n}]\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4}\sim_{n\to\infty}\frac{20^{n}}{64\sqrt{\pi n^{3}}}.

Remembering the definitions of
Z_{j}
and
g_{j}Z_{j}^{2}
(see

), we have

\displaystyle Z_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq Z_{b}-\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{c}^{2}}\overline{\Sigma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\textit{mr};\geqslant j+1}(0)=Z_{b}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(g_{b}Z_{b}^{2})^{n}A_{n}(m_{r}^{2},Z_{b},j_{\text{max}},j),

(4.2)

\displaystyle g_{j}Z_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\overline{\Gamma}^{\textup{mel}}_{\!4,\textit{b};\geqslant j+1}(0,0)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(g_{b}Z_{b}^{2})^{n}B_{n}(m_{r}^{2},Z_{b},j_{\text{max}},j).

(4.3)

5
Holomorphic RG flow

In

{assumption}

The series

{assumption}

The discrete RG flow

Throughout this section, we will be interested in Cauchy problems with

5.1
Parabolic holomorphic local dynamics

Our first objective is to understand the qualitative behaviour of the approximate RG
flow (
5.1
) by invoking the theory of holomorphic dynamical
systems. To this aim, we need to recall some classical definitions and
theorems, see
[
Aba10
]
for example.

Definition 6
(Holomorphic dynamical system).

Let
M
be a complex manifold, and
p\in M
. A (discrete)

holomorphic local dynamical system
at
p
is a holomorphic map

f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}U\to M
such that
f(p)=p
, where
U\subseteq M
is an open
neighbourhood of
p
; we shall assume that
f\neq\textup{id}_{U}
. We shall
denote by
\textup{{End}}(M,p)
the set of holomorphic local dynamical systems
at
p
.

We will only consider the case
M=\mathbb{C}
and
p=0
.

Definition 7
(Stable set).

Let
f\in\textup{{End}}(M,p)
be a holomorphic local dynamical system defined on an open set
U\subseteq M
. Then the stable set
K_{f}
of
f
is

K_{f}\coloneqq\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}f^{\circ(-k)}(U).

In other words, the stable set of
f
is the set of all points
z\in U
such that the orbit
\mathinner{\{f^{\circ k}(z)\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}}k\in\mathbb{N}\}}
is well-defined. If

z\in U\setminus\mathinner{\{K_{f}\}}
, we shall say that
z
(or its orbit)
escapes from
U
. Clearly,
p\in K_{f}
and so the stable set is never
empty (but it can happen that
K_{f}=\mathinner{\left\{p\right\}}
).

Definition 8
(Conjugation).

We say that
f,g\in\textup{{End}}(\mathbb{C},0)
are holomorphically conjugated if there
exists a holomorphic map
h
such that
h\circ f=g\circ h
.

Definition 9
(Classification).

Let
f\in\textup{{End}}(\mathbb{C},0)
be given by
f(z)=\lambda z+\sum_{j\geqslant 2}a_{j}z^{j}
. We say that
f
is

1.

hyperbolic if
|\lambda|\neq 1
,

2.

parabolic if
\lambda^{q}=1
for some

q\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathinner{\left\{0\right\}}
,

3.

elliptic if
|\lambda|=1
and
\lambda^{q}\neq 1
for all
q\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathinner{\left\{0\right\}}
.

The RG flow we consider here is thus a parabolic dynamical system
(
\lambda=1
).

Definition 10
(Multiplicity).

Let
f\in\textup{{End}}(\mathbb{C},0)
be a holomorphic local dynamical system with a parabolic fixed point at the
origin. Then we can write:

f(z)=e^{2i\pi p/q}z+a_{r+1}z^{r+1}+\bO(z^{r+2}),

with
a_{r+1}\neq 0
.
r+1
is called the multiplicity of
f
.

Definition 11
(Directions).

Let
f\in\textup{{End}}(\mathbb{C},0)
be tangent to the identity of multiplicity
r+1\geqslant 2
. Then a unit vector
v\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
is an attracting (resp. repelling) direction for
f
at the origin if
a_{r+1}v^{r}
is real
negative (resp. real positive).

Clearly, there are
r
equally spaced attracting directions, separated
by
r
equally spaced repelling directions: if
a_{r+1}=|a_{r+1}|e^{i\alpha}
, then
v=e^{i\theta}
is attracting (resp. repelling) if and only if

\theta=\frac{2k+1}{r}\pi-\frac{\alpha}{r}\quad\mathinner{\left(\text{resp.{} $\theta=\frac{2k}{r}\pi-\frac{\alpha}{r}$}\right)}.

It turns out that to every attracting direction is associated a
connected component of
K_{f}\setminus\mathinner{\left\{0\right\}}
.

Definition 12
(Basins).

Let
v\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
be an attracting direction for an
f\in\textup{{End}}(\mathbb{C},0)

tangent to the identity. The basin centerd at
v
is the set of
points
z\in K_{f}\setminus\mathinner{\left\{0\right\}}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}f^{\circ k}(z)=0
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}f^{\circ k}(z)/|f^{\circ k}(z)|=v
. If
z
belongs to the
basin centered at
v
, we shall say that the orbit of
z
tends to

0
tangent to
v
.

Definition 13
(Petals).

An attracting petal centered at an attracting direction
v
of an

f\in\textup{{End}}(\mathbb{C},0)
tangent to the identity is an open simply connected

f
-invariant set
P\subseteq K_{f}\setminus\mathinner{\left\{0\right\}}
such that a point

z\in K_{f}\setminus\mathinner{\left\{0\right\}}
belongs to the basin centered at
v
if and only if its orbit intersects
P
. In other words, the orbit of a point tends
to
0
tangent to
v
if and only if it is eventually contained in

P
. A repelling petal (centered at a repelling direction) is an attracting petal for the inverse of
f
.

Theorem 14
(Leau-Fatou flower).

Let
f\in\textup{{End}}(\mathbb{C},0)
be a holomorphic local dynamical system tangent to the identity with multiplicity
r+1\geqslant 2
at the
fixed point. Let

be the
attracting (resp. repelling) directions of

1.

petals

angle between their central directions is

2.

\displaystyle f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}_{+}\times U

Theorem 22

Let

Proof 5.4
.

By the Leau-Fatou flower theorem, there exists an attracting petal
directed along the positive real axis, see

By a biholomorphic change of variable

This comes from a theorem of G. Szekeres, the proof of which can be

[

. Let us assume
that
0
is a pole of

of order

,

, and note
the corresponding residue

Let us explain how to

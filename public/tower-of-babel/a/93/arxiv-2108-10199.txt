‘Anti-Commutable’ Pre-Leibniz Algebroids and Admissible Connections
Tekin Dereli, Keremcan Doğan Department of Physics, Koç University, 34450 Sarıyer, İstanbul, Turkey

Abstract
The concept of algebroid is convenient as a basis for constructions of geometrical frameworks. For example, metric-affine and generalized geometries can be written on Lie and Courant algebroids, respectively. Furthermore, string theories might make use of many other algebroids such as metric algebroids, higher-Courant algebroids or conformal Courant algebroids. Working on the possibly most general algebroid structure, which generalizes many of the algebroids used in the literature, is fruitful as it creates a chance to study all of them at once. Local pre-Leibniz algebroids are such general ones in which metric-connection geometries are possible to construct. On the other hand, the existence of the ‘locality operator’, which is present for the right-Leibniz rule for the bracket, necessitates the modification of torsion and curvature operators in order to achieve tensorial quantities. In this paper, this modification of torsion and curvature is explained from the point of view that the modification is applied to the bracket instead. This leads one to consider ‘anti-commutable’ pre-Leibniz algebroids which satisfy an anti-commutativity-like property defined with respect to a choice of an equivalence class of connections. These ‘admissible’ connections are claimed to be the necessary ones while working on a geometry of algebroids. This claim is due to the fact that one can prove many desirable properties and relations if one uses only admissible connections. For instance, for admissible connections, we prove the first and second Bianchi identities, Ricci identity, Cartan structure equations, Cartan magic formula, the construction of Levi-Civita connections, the decomposition of connection in terms of torsion and non-metricity. These all are possible because the modified bracket becomes anti-symmetric for an admissible connection so that one can apply the machinery of almost- or pre-Lie algebroids. We investigate various algebroid structures from the literature and show that they admit admissible connections which are metric-compatible in some generalized sense. Moreover, we prove that local pre-Leibniz algebroids that are not anti-commutable cannot be equipped with a torsion-free, and in particular Levi-Civita, connection.



Keywords
: Pre-Leibniz Algebroids, Admissible Connections, Bianchi Identities, Cartan Formalism, Lie Algebroids, Generalized Geometry

Introduction

Algebroid structures have been increasingly studied in order to construct geometrical frameworks which can be utilized to create a suitable background for classical field theories in physics. For instance, metric-affine geometries on a smooth manifold can be written on the tangent bundle of a manifold where this tangent bundle equipped with the Lie bracket becomes a Lie algebroid. The usual geometrical notions can be easily generalized on an arbitrary Lie algebroid

. The new bracket yields a Courant algebroid structure on the generalized tangent bundle
[

]

[

[

in which the generalization of higher-Courant algebroids provides the mathematical basis
[

[

[

[

All the algebroids above provide special cases of local pre-Leibniz algebroids which are defined with the least possible assumptions. It is then natural to ask for a more all-inclusive framework on these pre-Leibniz algebroids. In order to achieve this comprehensive scheme, metric-connection geometries on local pre-Leibniz algebroids are constructed in our previous paper
[
12
]
by expanding the ideas of
[
13
]

Since pre-Leibniz algebroids are defined with respect to an arbitrary bracket which needs not necessarily be anti-symmetric, both the left- and right-Leibniz rules are given separately, where in the former case an extra term with a ‘locality operator’ is included. This situation creates a difficulty for defining the torsion and curvature operators, so one needs to modify the usual forms of these maps in order to have tensorial quantities. The same difficulty shows up in all of the geometries above as they have non-vanishing locality operators in general. Because of this modification, the geometrical meaning of some quantities are not clear cut
[
13
]
. One main motivation in this paper is to understand better why this modification is necessary and what it means from a more familiar geometrical point of view. We will show that this modification can be understood as the modification of the bracket instead of the torsion and curvature operators. This leads us to the definition of ‘anti-commutable’ pre-Leibniz algebroids, where the bracket is modified in such a way that it becomes anti-symmetric, so that one can use the machinery on almost-Lie algebroids. This definition of ‘anti-commutability’ depends on a choice of an equivalence class of
E
-connections, which we call ‘admissible’. Many algebroids in the literature turns out to be a special case of anti-commutable pre-Leibniz algebroids with the appropriate choice of admissible connections. As proven below, this admissibility condition is usually just a metric-compatibility condition in some generalized sense. Our most important claim in this paper is to only consider admissible linear
E
-connections while working on a geometry in an algebroid setting because the anti-symmetry of the modified bracket sustains many important properties of the usual metric-affine geometry.

Organization of the paper is as follows: In the second section of the paper, we will briefly summarize the necessary geometrical information about the usual metric-affine geometry on a smooth manifold. This section will set the notation of the paper while defining and outlining important properties of torsion, curvature and non-metricity tensors. In the second section, these constructions will be carried out on local pre-Leibniz algebroids. Several different algebroid structures will be introduced, together with the anti-commutable pre-Leibniz algebroids. Some examples of such anti-commutable pre-Leibniz algebroids from the literature and admissible connections on them will be discussed. The modification of the torsion and curvature operators will be investigated in terms of the modification of the bracket. As mentioned already, the anti-symmetry of these new brackets leads to many crucial relations, including the first and second Bianchi identities, the Ricci identity, Cartan structure equations, decomposition of connections in terms of torsion and non-metricity, construction of Levi-Civita connections.

It must be noted that every construction here will be assumed to be in the smooth category, and the Einstein’s summation convention for repeated indices is used. Moreover, one should be aware that the definitions of some algebroids (especially the ones with almost and pre prefixes) in the literature differ from paper to paper, so one should be careful about the assumptions of the certain work.

Metric-Affine Geometry on Smooth Manifolds

Any (Hausdorff, paracompact, orientable) manifold
M
comes with two naturally constructed fiber bundles; namely the tangent bundle
T(M)
and its dual cotangent bundle
T^{*}(M)
. By using the tensor products of these bundles, one can get the set of
(q,r)
-type tensors, denoted by
Tens^{(q,r)}(M)
. The set of vector fields is denoted by
\mathfrak{X}(M)
, which is equipped with the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
, that makes
\mathfrak{X}(M)
into a Lie algebra. The Lie bracket can be extended into whole tensor algebra by the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{V}:Tens^{(q,r)}(M)\to Tens^{(q,r)}(M)
. On a local frame
(X_{a})
, the anholonomy coefficients
\{\gamma^{c}_{\ ab}\}
are defined by
\gamma^{c}_{\ ab}:=\langle e^{c},[X_{a},X_{b}]\rangle
, where
(e^{a})
is the dual local coframe. The map
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\Omega^{p}(M)\times\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\Omega^{p-1}(M)
is given by
\langle\omega,V\rangle:=\iota_{V}\omega
, for all
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M),V\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, where
\iota_{V}:\Omega^{p}(M)\to\Omega^{p-1}(M)
denotes the interior product with respect to a vector field
V
, and
\Omega^{p}(M)
is the set of all (exterior differential)
p
-forms. The set of all forms is equipped with a degree-1 graded derivation, called the exterior derivative
d:\Omega^{p}(M)\to\Omega^{p+1}(M)
:

\displaystyle d\omega(V_{1},\ldots,V_{p+1}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq p+1}(-1)^{i+1}V_{i}\left(\omega(V_{1},\ldots,\check{V}_{i},\ldots,V_{p+1})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq p+1}(-1)^{i+j}\omega\left([V_{i},V_{j}],V_{1},\ldots,\check{V}_{i},\ldots,\check{V}_{j},\ldots,V_{p+1}\right),

(2.1)

where
\check{V}_{i}
indicates that
V_{i}
is excluded. The square of the exterior derivative vanishes, which follows from the Jacobi identity for the Lie bracket. The exterior derivative, interior product and the action of the Lie derivative on
p
-forms are related by the Cartan magic formula:

\mathcal{L}_{V}=d\iota_{V}+\iota_{V}d.

(2.2)

Metric-affine geometries are defined by a triplet
(M,g,\nabla)
where
M
is a manifold,
g
is a metric, and
\nabla
is an affine connection on
M
. The torsion and curvature operators of an affine connection
\nabla
are defined as

\displaystyle T(\nabla)(U,V):=\nabla_{U}V-\nabla_{V}U-[U,V],

\displaystyle R(\nabla)(U,V)W:=\nabla_{U}\nabla_{V}W-\nabla_{V}\nabla_{U}W-\nabla_{[U,V]}W,

(2.3)

for all
U,V,W\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Moreover, for a given metric
g
and an affine connection
\nabla
, their corresponding non-metricity tensor is defined by

Q(\nabla,g):=\nabla g.

(2.4)

On a local frame
(X_{a})
, the torsion, curvature and non-metricity components read

\displaystyle T(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}=\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}-\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ cb}-\gamma^{a}_{\ bc},

\displaystyle R(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bcd}=\ X_{b}\left(\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ cd}\right)-X_{c}\left(\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bd}\right)+\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ cd}\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ be}-\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ bd}\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ ce}-\gamma^{e}_{\ bc}\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ ed},

\displaystyle Q(\nabla,g)_{abc}=X_{a}(g_{bc})-\Gamma(\nabla)^{d}_{\ ab}g_{dc}-\Gamma(\nabla)^{d}_{\ ac}g_{bd},

(2.5)

where the connection coefficients are defined as

\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}:=\langle e^{a},\nabla_{X_{b}}X_{c}\rangle.

(2.6)

Torsion and curvature operators satisfy the first and second algebraic Bianchi identities:

\displaystyle R(\nabla)(U,V)W+cycl.=(\nabla_{U}T(\nabla))(V,W)+T(\nabla)(T(\nabla)(U,V),W)+cycl.,

\displaystyle(\nabla_{U}R(\nabla))(V,W)W^{\prime}+cycl.=R(\nabla)(U,T(\nabla)(V,W))W^{\prime}+cycl.,

(2.7)

where
cycl.
means the addition of cyclic permutations in
U,V,W
. Here, the action of the connection is extended to the whole tensor algebra by the Leibniz rule. They also satisfy the Ricci identity

\nabla^{2}_{U,V}W-\nabla^{2}_{V,U}W=R(\nabla)(U,V)W-\nabla_{T(\nabla)(U,V)}W,

(2.8)

where the second order covariant derivative is given as

\nabla^{2}_{U,V}W:=\nabla_{U}\nabla_{V}W-\nabla_{\nabla_{U}V}W.

(2.9)

The Ricci identity (
2.8
) is often considered as the definition of the curvature tensor for torsion-free connections.

The torsion and curvature operators satisfy the following anti-symmetry rules

\displaystyle T(\nabla)(U,V)=-T(\nabla)(V,U),

\displaystyle R(\nabla)(U,V)W=-R(\nabla)(V,U)W,

(2.10)

for all
U,V,W\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, so that one can define the torsion and curvature 2-forms as

\displaystyle T^{a}(\nabla)(U,V):=\langle e^{a},T(\nabla)(U,V)\rangle,

\displaystyle R^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)(U,V):=\langle e^{a},R(\nabla)(U,V)X_{b}\rangle.

(2.11)

The non-metricity and connection 1-forms are defined by

\displaystyle Q_{ab}(\nabla,g)(V):=Q(\nabla,g)(V,X_{a},X_{b}),

\displaystyle\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)(V):=\langle e^{a},\nabla_{V}X_{b}\rangle.

(2.12)

On the other hand the components of the forms and tensors are related by

T^{a}(\nabla)_{bc}=T(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc},\qquad R^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)_{cd}=R(\nabla)^{a}_{\ cdb},\qquad Q_{ab}(\nabla,g)_{c}=Q(\nabla,g)_{cab}.

(2.13)

Similarly, the connection coefficients and connection 1-form components are related by

\displaystyle\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)_{c}=\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ cb}.

(2.14)

By making use of these forms, the metric-affine geometries can be conveniently described in the language of forms. For example, the definitions of torsion and curvature operators become the first and second Cartan structure equations, respectively:

\displaystyle T^{a}(\nabla)=de^{a}+\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge e^{b},

\displaystyle R^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)=d\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)+\omega^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge\omega^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla).

(2.15)

Similarly, the algebraic Bianchi identites (
2.7
) lead to the differential Bianchi identities

\displaystyle dT^{a}(\nabla)+\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge T^{b}(\nabla)=R^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge e^{b},

\displaystyle dR^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)+\omega^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge R^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla)=R^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge\omega^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla),

(2.16)

which can be directly proven by evaluating the exterior derivative of the Cartan structure equations.

According to the fundamental theorem of semi-Riemannian geometry, for every metric
g
, there is a unique metric-
g
-compatible and torsion-free affine connection
{}^{g}\nabla
, which is called the Levi-Civita connection corresponding to
g
. It can be defined via the Koszul formula

\displaystyle 2g(^{g}\nabla_{U}V,W)=

\displaystyle\ U(g(V,W))+V(g(U,W))-W(g(U,V))

\displaystyle-g([V,W],U)-g([U,W],V)+g([U,V],W).

(2.17)

On a local frame, the Levi-Civita connection coefficients can be evaluated as

\Gamma(^{g}\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}=\frac{1}{2}g^{ad}\left[X_{b}\left(g_{cd}\right)+X_{c}\left(g_{bd}\right)-X_{d}\left(g_{bc}\right)-\gamma^{e}_{\ cd}g_{eb}-\gamma^{e}_{\ bd}g_{ec}+\gamma^{e}_{\ bc}g_{ed}\right].

(2.18)

Moreover, the metric
g
, torsion
T(\nabla)
and non-metricity
Q(\nabla,g)
tensors are necessary and sufficient to determine uniquely the affine connection
\nabla
given by:

\displaystyle 2g(\nabla_{U}V,W)=

\displaystyle\ 2g(^{g}\nabla_{U}V,W)

\displaystyle-Q(\nabla,g)(U,V,W)-Q(\nabla,g)(V,U,W)+Q(\nabla,g)(W,U,V)

\displaystyle-g(T(\nabla)(V,W),U)-g(T(\nabla)(U,W),V)+g(T(\nabla)(U,V),W).

(2.19)

On a local frame, this expression can be written as the following decomposition of the connection coefficients
[
14
]
:

\displaystyle\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}=

\displaystyle\Gamma(^{g}\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}+\frac{1}{2}g^{ad}\Big[-Q(\nabla,g)_{bdc}+Q(\nabla,g)_{dcb}-Q(\nabla,g)_{cbd}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\ \ -g_{ec}T(\nabla)^{e}_{\ bd}+g_{ed}T(\nabla)^{e}_{\ bc}-g_{eb}T(\nabla)^{e}_{\ cd}\Big].

(2.20)

Metric-Connection Geometry on Anti-Commutable Pre-Leibniz Algebroids

\nabla
, one can evaluate an equivalence class
[L]_{\nabla}
of multilinear maps. Or, the most general case would be the one with the equivalence class
[(L,\nabla)]
, where neither
L
and
\nabla
are fixed. The most natural case seems to be the one in which the multilinear map
L
is fixed, and the equivalence class
[\nabla]_{L}
of linear
E
-connections is evaluated accordingly. This leads us to the following definition:

Definition 3.4
.

On an anti-commutable almost-Leibniz algebroid, linear
E
-connections in the equivalence class
[\nabla]_{L}
are called admissible.

Main motivation behind these admissible linear
E
-connections comes from the torsion and curvature operators. The naive generalizations of the torsion and curvature operators (
2.3
) do not work for local almost-Leibniz algebroids, as the
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
-multilinearity does not hold so that they are not tensorial.
In
[
12
]
, we proved that there is an affine map
{}^{A}\mathfrak{T}:\nabla\mapsto\ ^{A}[\cdot,\cdot]_{\nabla}
from the affine space of linear
E
-connections to local almost-Leibniz brackets on
E
defined by

{}^{A}[u,v]_{\nabla}:=\nabla_{u}v-\nabla_{v}u+A(u,v),

(3.11)

for any
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear map
A:\mathfrak{X}(E)\times\mathfrak{X}(E)\to\mathfrak{X}(E)
satisfying

\displaystyle A(fu,v)

\displaystyle=L(Df,u,v)+fA(u,v),

\displaystyle A(u,fv)

\displaystyle=fA(u,v),

(3.12)

for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{X}(E),f\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Trivially, this also implies that adding the
A
term to the definition of the
E
-torsion operator would make it tensorial. For a local almost-Leibniz algebroid, the
E
-torsion operator suggested in
[
13
]
is given by

T(\nabla)(u,v):=\nabla_{u}v-\nabla_{v}u-[u,v]_{E}+L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v).

(3.13)

Note that the extra term
L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)
satisfies the necessary conditions (
3.12
), but the geometric meaning behind this modification was missing. We claim that the use of admissible linear
E
-connections might clarify the geometric interpretation: One should work with an admissible linear
E
-connection when trying to construct a metric-connection geometry on an algebroid structure. The choice of an
E
-connection changes the choice of the bracket so that these two structures are not completely independent from each other. This interpretation can be seen as follows. The modification of the
E
-torsion operator can be considered as a modification of the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}
:

[u,v]_{E}^{\nabla}:=[u,v]_{E}-L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v).

(3.14)

This new bracket will be called “
the modified bracket
”, and in terms of it, the
E
-torsion operator (
3.13
) becomes

T(\nabla)(u,v)=\nabla_{u}v-\nabla_{v}u-[u,v]_{E}^{\nabla}.

(3.15)

One can analogously define “
modified anholonomy coefficients
” as

\gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}:=\langle e^{a},[X_{b},X_{c}]_{E}^{\nabla}\rangle=\gamma^{a}_{\ bc}-\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ db}L^{ad}_{\ \ ec},

(3.16)

so that the
E
-torsion coefficients can be written as

\displaystyle T(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}

\displaystyle=\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}-\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ cb}-\gamma^{a}_{\ bc}+\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ db}L^{ad}_{\ \ ec},

\displaystyle=\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}-\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ cb}-\gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}.

(3.17)

One can observe that every local almost-Leibniz bracket in the range of the map
{}^{A}\mathfrak{T}
is an anti-commutable bracket when
A
is chosen as
L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)
:

{}^{A}[u,v]_{\nabla}+\ ^{A}[v,u]_{\nabla}

\displaystyle=\left[\nabla_{u}v-\nabla_{v}u+A(u,v)\right]+\left[\nabla_{v}u-\nabla_{u}v+A(v,u)\right]

\displaystyle=A(u,v)+A(v,u)

\displaystyle=L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)+L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}v,u)

(3.18)

More generally, the maps
S
and
A
are directly related by
S(u,v)=A(u,v)+A(v,u)
.

The modified bracket makes it clear the following simple, yet important, fact:

Proposition 3.2
.

For an almost-Leibniz algebroid, an
E
-torsion-free linear
E
-connection
\nabla
has to be admissible.

Proof.

In terms of the modified bracket the
E
-torsion operator of a linear
E
-connection
\nabla
is written as (
3.15
):

T(\nabla)(u,v)=\nabla_{u}v-\nabla_{v}u-[u,v]_{E}^{\nabla},

which implies for the
E
-torsion-free case:

[u,v]_{E}^{\nabla}=\nabla_{u}v-\nabla_{v}u.

As the right-hand side is anti-symmetric in
u
and
v
, the modified bracket has to be anti-symmetric. Hence,
\nabla
is admissible.
∎

The modified bracket (
3.14
) is related with different algebroid structures. Inspired from the dull algebroid definition in
[
9
]
, we define almost-dull algebroids:

Definition 3.5
.

The triplet
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
is called an almost-dull algebroid, if
(E,\rho)
is an anchored vector bundle and the bracket satisfies

[f_{1}u,f_{2}v]_{E}=f_{1}f_{2}[u,v]_{E}+f_{1}\rho(u)(f_{2})v-f_{2}\rho(v)(f_{1})u,

(3.19)

for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}),u,v\in\mathfrak{X}(E)
.

Note that, this property (
3.19
) is equivalent to the fact that an almost-dull algebroid is a local almost-Leibniz algebroid whose locality operator vanishes.

Definition 3.6
.

An almost-Leibniz algebroid
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
is called an almost-Lie algebroid if the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}
is anti-symmetric.

Every almost-Lie algebroid is an almost-dull algebroid due to the anti-symmetry of the bracket. Moreover, almost-dull algebroids that are not almost-Lie constitute examples of almost-Leibniz algebroids which are not anti-commutable.

Proposition 3.3
.

Given some
L
, if
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
is an almost-Leibniz algebroid, then
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}^{\nabla})
is an almost-dull algebroid. Moreover, if
\nabla
is an admissible linear
E
-connection, then
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}^{\nabla})
is an almost-Lie algebroid.

Proof.

We need to show that the modified bracket
[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla}
satisfies the condition (
3.19
). Equivalently, we can show that
[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla}
is a local almost-Leibniz bracket with a vanishing locality operator. First, we prove the right-Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle[u,fv]_{E}^{\nabla}

\displaystyle=[u,fv]_{E}-L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,fv)

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(f)v+f[u,v]_{E}-fL(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(f)v+f\left\{[u,v]_{E}-L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)\right\}

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(f)v+f[u,v]_{E}^{\nabla},

so that
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla})
is an almost-Leibniz algebroid. Next, we prove the left-Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle[fu,v]_{E}^{\nabla}

\displaystyle=[fu,v]_{E}-L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}(fu),v)

\displaystyle=-\rho(v)(f)u+f[u,v]_{E}+L(Df,u,v)-L(e^{a},\rho(X_{a})(f)u+f\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)

\displaystyle=-\rho(v)(f)u+f[u,v]_{E}+L(Df,u,v)-L(Df,u,v)-fL(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)

\displaystyle=-\rho(v)(f)u+f\left\{[u,v]_{E}-L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)\right\}

\displaystyle=-\rho(v)(f)u+f[u,v]_{E}^{\nabla}+0.

This means that the locality operator on the almost-Leibniz algebroid
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla})
can be chosen as 0. Hence, we proved that
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla})
is an almost-dull algebroid. Moreover, if
\nabla
is admissible, then

[v,u]_{E}^{\nabla}=[v,u]_{E}-L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}v,u)=-[u,v]_{E}+L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)=-[u,v]_{E}^{\nabla}

where we used the definition (
3.8
) of an admissible linear
E
-connection. This means that the modified bracket
[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla}
is anti-symmetric, so that
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla})
is an almost-Lie algebroid.
∎

The fact that
[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}^{\nabla}
is anti-symmetric for an admissible linear
E
-connection
\nabla
is the reason behind the adjective “
anti-commutable
”. In some sense, one can make the bracket anti-commutative (or anti-symmetric). One natural question is that which of these modified brackets
[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla}
correspond to the anti-symmetrization of the original bracket
[.,.]_{E}
. This is the case when an admissible linear
E
-connection
\nabla^{\prime}
satisfies
L(e^{a},\nabla^{\prime}_{X_{a}}u,v)=L(e^{a},\nabla^{\prime}_{X_{a}}v,u)
. In this case, the anholonomy coefficients decomposes into its anti-symmetric and symmetric parts respectively as:

\gamma^{a}_{\ bc}=\gamma(\nabla^{\prime})^{a}_{\ bc}+\Gamma(\nabla^{\prime})^{d}_{\ ab}L^{ea}_{\ \ dc}.

(3.20)

Corollary 3.2
.

For an admissible linear
E
-connection
\nabla
, the
E
-torsion operator
T(\nabla)
is anti-symmetric:

T(\nabla)(u,v)=-T(\nabla)(v,u),

(3.21)

for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{X}(E)
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that the modified bracket
[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla}
is anti-symmetric for an admissible linear
E
-connection
\nabla
. The
E
-torsion operator
T(\nabla)
can be seen as the
E
-torsion operator on the almost-Lie algebroid
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla})
without any modification.
∎

In order to define an
E
-curvature operator, one needs to assume that the anchor respects the brackets.

Definition 3.7
.

An almost-Leibniz algebroid
(E,\rho,

[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
is called a pre-Leibniz algebroid if

\rho([u,v]_{E})=[\rho(u),\rho(v)],

(3.22)

for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{X}(E)
.

Moreover, to achieve tensorial curvature one needs to introduce a locality projector, which we defined in
[
12
]
. As the locality projectors are related to the kernel of the anchor, one needs to assume that the anchored vector bundle is regular, i. e. the kernel of the anchor is of locally constant rank, so that the kernel defines a subbundle.

Definition 3.8
.

On a regular local almost-Leibniz algebroid
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E},L)
, a locality projector is defined as a
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
-linear map
\mathcal{P}:\mathfrak{X}(E)\to\mathfrak{X}(E)
such that

1.

The image of the projected locality operator
\hat{L}:=\mathcal{P}L
is a subset of the kernel of the anchor, i.e.
im(\hat{L})\subset ker(\rho)
,

2.

The restriction of the locality projector on the kernel of the anchor is the identity map, i.e.
\mathcal{P}|_{ker(\rho)}=id_{ker(\rho)}
.

With the help of a locality projector, one can define the
E
-curvature operator.

Definition 3.9
.

Given a locality projector
\mathcal{P}
on
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E},L)
, the
E
-curvature operator of a linear
E
-connection
\nabla
is defined by

R(\nabla)(u,v)w:=\nabla_{u}\nabla_{v}w-\nabla_{v}\nabla_{u}w-\nabla_{[u,v]_{E}}w+\nabla_{\hat{L}(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)}w.

(3.23)

Similar to the
E
-torsion operator, one can modify the bracket in order to express the
E
-curvature operator more naturally on an algebroid structure. In order to understand what type of bracket will be the result of this new modification, we need pre-dull algebroids.

Definition 3.10
.

An almost-dull algebroid
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
is called a pre-dull algebroid if the equation (
3.22
) is satisfied
[
9
]
.

Note that such an algebroid is called a “dull algebroid” in the reference
[
9
]
, but it is more appropriate to call it pre-dull for the purposes of this paper. Moreover, a general caution is in place; in the literature many algebroid structures are defined by using different names, so one should be careful about the assumptions in a specific article.

Definition 3.11
.

A pre-Leibniz algebroid
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
is called a pre-Lie algebroid if the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}
is anti-symmetric.

Every pre-Lie algebroid is automatically a pre-dull algebroid due to the anti-symmetry of the bracket. Note that the
E
-curvature operator (
3.23
) can be written by using the modified bracket (
3.14
) as follows

R(\nabla)(u,v)w:=\nabla_{u}\nabla_{v}w-\nabla_{v}\nabla_{u}w-\nabla_{[u,v]_{E}^{\nabla}}w-\nabla_{(1-\mathcal{P})L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)}w.

(3.24)

Hence, one can see that it is not the same as the
E
-curvature operator on the pre-Lie algebroid
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}^{\nabla})
. Actually,
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}^{\nabla})
is not even a pre-Lie algebroid in the most general case. It is a pre-Lie algebroid only if the image of the locality projector is already a subset of the kernel of the anchor; in this case one does not need the locality projector. However, one can have another modification of the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}
by using the locality projector, which will be called “
the projected modified bracket
”:

[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}:=[u,v]_{E}-\hat{L}(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)=[u,v]_{E}-\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v).

(3.25)

By using this projected modified bracket, the
E
-curvature operator (
3.23
) can be written as

R(\nabla)(u,v)w:=\nabla_{u}\nabla_{v}w-\nabla_{v}\nabla_{u}w-\nabla_{[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w.

(3.26)

Similarly, one can define “
projected modified anholonomy coefficients
” as

\hat{\gamma}(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}:=\langle e^{a},[X_{b},X_{c}]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}\rangle=\gamma^{a}_{\ bc}-\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ db}\hat{L}^{ad}_{\ \ ec}=\gamma^{a}_{\ bc}-\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ db}L^{af}_{\ \ ec}\mathcal{P}^{d}_{\ f},

(3.27)

so that
E
-curvature coefficients can be written as

\displaystyle R(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bcd}=

\displaystyle\ \rho(X_{b})\left(\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ cd}\right)-\rho(X_{c})\left(\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bd}\right)+\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ cd}\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ be}

\displaystyle-\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ bd}\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ ce}-\gamma^{e}_{\ bc}\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ ed}+\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ gd}\Gamma(\nabla)^{f}_{\ eb}\hat{L}^{ge}_{\ \ fc},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \rho(X_{b})\left(\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ cd}\right)-\rho(X_{c})\left(\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bd}\right)+\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ cd}\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ be}

\displaystyle-\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ bd}\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ ce}-\hat{\gamma}(\nabla)^{e}_{\ bc}\Gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ ed}

(3.28)

Proposition 3.4
.

Given some
L
, if
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
is a pre-Leibniz algebroid, then
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})
is a pre-dull algebroid. Moreover, if
\nabla
is an admissible linear
E
-connection, then
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})
is a pre-Lie algebroid.

Proof.

We need to show that the projected modified bracket
[.,.]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}
is a local almost-Leibniz bracket with a vanishing locality operator. First, we prove the right-Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle[u,fv]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}

\displaystyle=[u,fv]_{E}-\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,fv)

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(f)v+f[u,v]_{E}-\mathcal{P}(fL(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v))

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(f)v+f[u,v]_{E}-f\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(f)v+f\left\{[u,v]_{E}-\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)\right\}

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(f)v+f[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},

so that
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})
is an almost-Leibniz algebroid. Next, we prove the left-Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle[fu,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}

\displaystyle=[fu,v]_{E}-\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}(fu),v)

\displaystyle=-\rho(v)(f)u+f[u,v]_{E}+L(Df,u,v)-\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\rho(X_{a})(f)u+f\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)

\displaystyle=-\rho(v)(f)u+f[u,v]_{E}+L(Df,u,v)-\mathcal{P}L(Df,u,v)-\mathcal{P}(fL(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v))

\displaystyle=-\rho(v)(f)u+f[u,v]_{E}+L(Df,u,v)-L(Df,u,v)-f\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)

\displaystyle=-\rho(v)(f)u+f\left\{[u,v]_{E}-\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)\right\}

\displaystyle=-\rho(v)(f)u+f[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}+0.

This means that the locality operator on the almost-Leibniz algebroid
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})
can be chosen as 0. Hence, we proved that
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})
is an almost-dull algebroid. Note that here we used the fact that
\rho(L(Df,u,v))=0
for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{X}(E),f\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
, so that
\mathcal{P}L(Df,u,v)=L(Df,u,v)
. Next, we prove that the projected modified bracket is a pre-dull bracket:

\displaystyle\rho\left([u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\left([u,v]_{E}-\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)\right)

\displaystyle=\rho([u,v]_{E})-\rho(\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v))

\displaystyle=\rho([u,v]_{E})-0

\displaystyle=[\rho(u),\rho(v)].

Hence,
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})
is a pre-dull algebroid. Next, we prove that the projected modified bracket is
[.,.]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}
is anti-symmetric for an admissible linear
E
-connection
\nabla
. In order to prove this, we need to observe that the definition

[u,v]_{E}+[v,u]_{E}=L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)+L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}v,u),

implies that

\rho\left([u,v]_{E}+[v,u]_{E}\right)=\rho\left(L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)+L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}v,u)\right).

As the bracket
[.,.]_{E}
is a pre-Leibniz bracket, then the left-hand side of this equation vanishes:

\rho\left([u,v]_{E}+[v,u]_{E}\right)=\rho\left([u,v]_{E}\right)+\rho\left([v,u]_{E}\right)=[\rho(u),\rho(v)]+[\rho(v),\rho(u)]=0,

which follows from the anti-symmetry of the usual Lie bracket. Hence, we get

\rho\left(L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)+L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}v,u)\right)=0,

which means that
L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)+L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}v,u)
is in the kernel of the anchor
\rho
, so that

\mathcal{P}\left(L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)+L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}v,u)\right)=L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)+L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}v,u).

The
\mathbb{R}
-linearity of
\mathcal{P}
and the admissibility of
\nabla
imply that

[u,v]_{E}+[v,u]_{E}=\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)+\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}v,u).\\

In other words,
\mathcal{P}L\in[L]_{\nabla}
. Now with this information, we can prove the anti-symmetry of the projected modified bracket for an admissible linear
E
-connection:

[v,u]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}=[v,u]_{E}-\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}v,u)=-[u,v]_{E}-\mathcal{P}L(e^{a},\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)=-[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}.

Hence we proved
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})
is a pre-Lie algebroid for an admissible linear
E
-connection
\nabla
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

For an admissible linear
E
-connection
\nabla
, the
E
-curvature operator satisfies the following anti-symmetry property

R(\nabla)(u,v)w=-R(\nabla)(v,u)w,

(3.29)

for all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{X}(E)
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that for an admissible linear
E
-connection, the projected modified
[.,.]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}
is anti-symmetric. The
E
-curvature operator
R(\nabla)
can be seen as the
E
-curvature operator on the pre-Lie algebroid
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})
without any modification.
∎

Before going on with the detailed properties of admissible linear
E
-connections, we should focus on some examples from the literature.

•

Almost-Lie algebroids:
Every almost-Lie algebroid is an almost-dull algebroid, so one can consider them as local almost-Leibniz algebroids with a vanishing locality operator. As
L=0
, every linear
E
-connection is admissible. In particular,
(T(M),id_{T(M)},[\cdot,\cdot],0)
is a local almost-Leibniz algebroid. In the usual metric-affine geometry one can consider any arbitrary connection and this is coherent with the fact that every linear
E
-connection on an almost-Lie algebroid is admissible. Every
pre-Lie
and
Lie algebroid
, where the letter also satisfies the Jacobi identity, is an almost-Lie algebroid. Moreover,
H
-twisted Lie algebroids

[
18
]
that satisfy a modification of the Jacobi identity by an
E
-3-form is also an almost-Lie algebroid. Hence, everything is also valid for these algebroids.

•

Almost-Courant algebroids:
An almost-Leibniz algebroid
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
whose bracket satisfies

[u,v]_{E}+[v,u]_{E}=g^{-1}(D(g(u,v))),

(3.30)

for some
E
-metric
g
is called an almost-Courant algebroid, which is denoted by
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E},g)

[
12
]
. Every
Courant algebroid
is in fact an almost-Courant algebroid, so everything is also valid for Courant algebroids, which are the necessary mathematical structure in generalized geometries.
Pre-Courant algebroids
in the sense of
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
and
H
-twisted Courant algebroids

[
21
]
are also almost-Courant algebroids by definition. Moreover, every
metric algebroid
is also an almost-Courant algebroid
[
6
]
which underlies the para-Hermitian formulation of double field theory.

Proposition 3.5
.

On an almost-Courant algebroid
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E},g)
, a linear
E
-connection is admissible if and only if it is
E
-metric-
g
-compatible.

Proof.

First, we need to observe that every almost-Courant algebroid can be considered as a local almost-Leibniz algebroid with the locality operator

L(\Omega,u,v)=g(u,v)g^{-1}(\Omega),

so that the admissibility condition (
3.8
) becomes

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)g^{-1}(e^{a})+g(\nabla_{X_{a}}v,u)g^{-1}(e^{a})

\displaystyle=g^{-1}(D(g(u,v)))

\displaystyle=g^{-1}(\rho(X_{a})(g)(u,v)e^{a})

\displaystyle=\rho(X_{a})(g(u,v))g^{-1}(e^{a}).

This implies that (and is implied by)

\rho(X_{a})(g(u,v))-g(\nabla_{X_{a}}u,v)-g(u,\nabla_{X_{a}}v)=0,

which becomes by using the
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
-linearity of the anchor
\rho
, the
E
-metric
g
and the first component of the linear
E
-connection
\nabla

Q(\nabla,g)(w,u,v)=0,

so that a linear
E
-connection
\nabla
is admissible if and only if it is
E
-metric-

Definition 3.15

Proposition 3.10

For an admissible linear

\displaystyle T^{a}(\nabla)=d(\nabla)e^{a}+\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge e^{b},

\displaystyle R^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)=\hat{d}(\nabla)\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)+\omega^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge\omega^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla).

(3.48)

Proof.

We will prove the first Cartan structure equation by observing

\displaystyle e^{a}(\nabla_{u}v)

\displaystyle=e^{a}(\nabla_{u}(e^{b}(v)X_{b}))

\displaystyle=e^{a}\left(\rho(u)(e^{b}(v))X_{b}+e^{b}(v)\nabla_{u}X_{b}\right)

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(e^{b}(v))e^{a}(X_{b})+e^{b}(v)e^{a}(\nabla_{u}X_{b})

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(e^{a}(v))+e^{a}(\nabla_{u}X_{b})e^{b}(v).

The right-hand side of the first Cartan structure equation reads

\displaystyle d(\nabla)e^{a}(u,v)+(\omega(\nabla)^{a}_{\ b}\wedge e^{b})(u,v)

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(e^{a}(v))-\rho(v)(e^{a}(u))-e^{a}\left([u,v]_{E}^{\nabla}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\ \omega(\nabla)^{a}_{\ b}(u)e^{b}(v)-\omega(\nabla)^{a}_{\ b}(v)e^{b}(u)

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(e^{a}(v))-\rho(v)(e^{a}(u))-e^{a}\left([u,v]_{E}^{\nabla}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\ e^{a}(\nabla_{u}X_{b})e^{b}(v)-e^{a}(\nabla_{v}X_{b})e^{b}(u).

With the above observation, this becomes

\displaystyle d(\nabla)e^{a}(u,v)+(\omega(\nabla)^{a}_{\ b}\wedge e^{b})(u,v)

\displaystyle=e^{a}(\nabla_{u}v)-e^{a}(\nabla_{v}u)-e^{a}\left([u,v]_{E}^{\nabla}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{a}(T(u,v))=T^{a}(\nabla)(u,v).

\displaystyle\hat{d}(\nabla)\omega^{a}_{\ b}(u,v)+(\omega(\nabla)^{a}_{\ b}\wedge\omega(\nabla)^{c}_{\ b})(u,v)

\displaystyle\quad\ -e^{a}\left(\nabla_{[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}X_{b}\right)+e^{a}(\nabla_{u}X_{c})e^{c}(\nabla_{v}X_{b})

\displaystyle\quad\ -e^{a}(\nabla_{v}X_{c})e^{c}(\nabla_{u}X_{b})

\displaystyle=e^{a}(\nabla_{u}\nabla_{v}X_{b})-e^{a}(\nabla_{v}\nabla_{u}X_{b})-e^{a}\left(\nabla_{[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}X_{b}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{a}(R(\nabla)(u,v)X_{b})=R^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)(u,v).

\hat{T}(\nabla)(u,v):=\nabla_{u}v-\nabla_{v}u-[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},

\displaystyle(\nabla_{u}R(\nabla))(v,w)w^{\prime}+cycl.=R(\nabla)(u,\hat{T}(\nabla)(v,w))w^{\prime}+\nabla_{[[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime}+cycl.,

\displaystyle R(\nabla)(u,v)w+cycl.

\displaystyle=\nabla_{u}\nabla_{v}w-\nabla_{v}\nabla_{u}w-\nabla_{[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w

Now we observe that

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}-\nabla_{[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w=\hat{T}(\nabla)(w,[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})+[w,[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}

\displaystyle(\nabla_{u}\hat{T}(\nabla))(v,w)=\nabla_{u}(\hat{T}(\nabla)(v,w))-\hat{T}(\nabla)(\nabla_{u}v,w)-\hat{T}(\nabla)(v,\nabla_{u}w),

which implies

\displaystyle R(\nabla)(u,v)w+cycl.

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{u}\hat{T}(\nabla))(v,w)+\hat{T}(\nabla)(\nabla_{u}v,w)+\hat{T}(\nabla)(v,\nabla_{u}w)

\displaystyle\quad+(\nabla_{v}\hat{T}(\nabla))(w,u)+\hat{T}(\nabla)(\nabla_{v}w,u)+\hat{T}(\nabla)(w,\nabla_{v}u)

\displaystyle\quad+(\nabla_{w}\hat{T}(\nabla))(u,v)+\hat{T}(\nabla)(\nabla_{w}u,v)+\hat{T}(\nabla)(u,\nabla_{w}v)

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{T}(\nabla)(u,[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})+\hat{T}(\nabla)(v,[w,u]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})+\hat{T}(\nabla)(w,[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})

\displaystyle\quad+[u,[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}+[v,[w,u]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}+[w,[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}.

By using the fact that the projected
E
-torsion operator is anti-symmetric for an admissible linear
E
-connection, we get

R(\nabla)(u,v)w+cycl.=(\nabla_{u}\hat{T}(\nabla))(v,w)+\hat{T}(\nabla)(\nabla_{u}v-\nabla_{v}u-[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},w)+[u,[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}+cycl.,

which is the desired first Bianchi identity. For the second identity, we start with

\displaystyle(\nabla_{u}R(\nabla))(v,w)w^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{u}(R(\nabla)(v,w)w^{\prime})-R(\nabla)(\nabla_{u}v,w)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(v,\nabla_{u}w)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(v,w)\nabla_{u}w^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{u}\left(\nabla_{v}\nabla_{w}w^{\prime}-\nabla_{w}\nabla_{v}w^{\prime}-\nabla_{[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\ R(\nabla)(\nabla_{u}v,w)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(v,\nabla_{u}w)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(v,w)\nabla_{u}w^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{u}\nabla_{v}\nabla_{w}w^{\prime}-\nabla_{u}\nabla_{w}\nabla_{v}w^{\prime}-\nabla_{u}\nabla_{[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\ -R(\nabla)(\nabla_{u}v,w)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(v,\nabla_{u}w)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(v,w)\nabla_{u}w^{\prime}

Next, we observe

\displaystyle R(\nabla)([v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},u)w^{\prime}=\nabla_{[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}\nabla_{u}w^{\prime}-\nabla_{u}\nabla_{v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime}-\nabla_{[[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},u]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime},

\displaystyle R(\nabla)(v,w)\nabla_{u}w^{\prime}=\nabla_{v}\nabla_{w}\nabla_{u}w^{\prime}-\nabla_{w}\nabla_{v}\nabla_{u}-\nabla_{[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}\nabla_{u}w^{\prime},

which implies

\displaystyle\nabla_{u}\nabla_{[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime}=-R(\nabla)([v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},u)w^{\prime}+\nabla_{[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}\nabla_{u}w^{\prime}-\nabla_{[[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},u]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime},

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}\nabla_{w}\nabla_{u}w^{\prime}-\nabla_{w}\nabla_{v}\nabla_{u}w^{\prime}=R(\nabla)(v,w)\nabla_{u}w^{\prime}+\nabla_{[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}\nabla_{u}w^{\prime}.

Inserting these in the cyclic combination and canceling out, we get

\displaystyle(\nabla_{u}R(\nabla))(v,w)w^{\prime}+cycl.

\displaystyle=R(\nabla)([v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},u)w^{\prime}+\nabla_{[[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},u]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}

\displaystyle\quad\ +R(\nabla)([w,u]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},v)w^{\prime}+\nabla_{[[w,u]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\ +R(\nabla)([u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},w)w^{\prime}+\nabla_{[[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\ -R(\nabla)(\nabla_{u}v,w)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(w,\nabla_{v}u)w^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\ -R(\nabla)(\nabla_{v}w,u)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(u,\nabla_{w}v)w^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\ -R(\nabla)(\nabla_{w}u,v)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(v,\nabla_{u}w)w^{\prime}.

Now, as the
E
-curvature operator is anti-symmetric for an admissible linear
E
-connection, we get

\displaystyle(\nabla_{u}R(\nabla))(v,w)w^{\prime}+cycl.

\displaystyle=R(\nabla)(u,\nabla_{v}w)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(u,\nabla_{w}v)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(u,[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})w^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\ +R(\nabla)(v,\nabla_{w}u)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(v,\nabla_{u}w)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(v,[w,u]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})w^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\ +R(\nabla)(w,\nabla_{u}v)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(w,\nabla_{v}u)w^{\prime}-R(\nabla)(w,[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}})w^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\ +\nabla_{[[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime}+\nabla_{[[v,w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},u]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}+\nabla_{[[w,u]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime}

\displaystyle=R(\nabla)(u,\hat{T}(\nabla)(v,w))w^{\prime}+\nabla_{[[u,v]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}},w]_{E}^{\hat{\nabla}}}w^{\prime}+cycl.,

which is the desired second Bianchi identity.
∎

These are identical to the usual Bianchi identities except for the associator term. Similarly, the Ricci identity (
3.37

\nabla^{2}_{u,v}w-\nabla^{2}_{v,u}w=R(\nabla)(u,v)w-\nabla_{\hat{T}(\nabla)(u,v)}w.

(3.53)

Hence, in the absence of projected

-torsion, the

Proposition 3.12
.

For an admissible linear
E
-connection
\nabla

\displaystyle\hat{d}(\nabla)\hat{T}^{a}(\nabla)+\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge\hat{T}^{b}(\nabla)=R^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge e^{b}+\hat{d}(\nabla)^{2}e^{a},

\displaystyle\hat{d}(\nabla)R^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)+\omega^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge R^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla)=R^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge\omega^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla)+\hat{d}(\nabla)^{2}\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla).

Proof.

These differential identities can be proven by taking the projected
E
-exterior derivative of Cartan structure equations. Let us start with the first one by taking the projected
E
-exterior derivative of the equation (

\displaystyle\hat{d}(\nabla)\hat{T}(\nabla)

\displaystyle=\hat{d}(\nabla)\left\{\hat{d}(\nabla)e^{a}+\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge e^{b}\right\}

\displaystyle=\hat{d}(\nabla)^{2}e^{a}+\hat{d}(\nabla)\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge e^{b}-\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge\hat{d}(\nabla)e^{b}

\displaystyle=\hat{d}(\nabla)^{2}e^{a}+\hat{d}(\nabla)\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge e^{b}-\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge\left\{\hat{T}^{b}(\nabla)-\omega^{b}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge e^{c}\right\}

\displaystyle=\hat{d}(\nabla)^{2}e^{a}+\left\{\hat{d}(\nabla)\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)+\omega^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge\omega^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla)\right\}\wedge e^{b}-\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge\hat{T}^{b}(\nabla)

\displaystyle=\hat{d}(\nabla)^{2}e^{a}+R^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge e^{b}-\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)\wedge\hat{T}^{b}(\nabla),

which implies the first differential Bianchi identity. For the second equation, we take the projected
E
-exterior derivative of the second Cartan structure equation (

\displaystyle\hat{d}(\nabla)R^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)

\displaystyle=\hat{d}(\nabla)\left\{\hat{d}(\nabla)\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)+\omega^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge\omega^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla)\right\}

\displaystyle=\hat{d}(\nabla)^{2}\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)+\hat{d}(\nabla)\omega^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge\omega^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla)-\omega^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge\hat{d}(\nabla)\omega^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla)

\displaystyle=\hat{d}(\nabla)^{2}\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)+\left\{R^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)-\omega^{a}_{\ d}(\nabla)\wedge\omega^{d}_{\ c}(\nabla)\right\}\wedge\omega^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\omega^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge\left\{R^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla)-\omega^{c}_{\ d}(\nabla)\wedge\omega^{d}_{\ b}(\nabla)\right\}

\displaystyle=\hat{d}(\nabla)^{2}\omega^{a}_{\ b}(\nabla)+R^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\omega^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla)-\omega^{a}_{\ c}(\nabla)\wedge R^{c}_{\ b}(\nabla)

which implies the second differential Bianchi identity.
∎

Note that both of these equations are identical with (

Proposition 3.13
.

A linear
E
-connection is
E
-Levi-Civita if and only if it is admissible and
E
-Koszul.

Proof.

First, let us assume that
\nabla
is an
E
-Koszul connection. One can directly evaluate the components of the
E
-torsion and
E
-non-metricity tensors:

\displaystyle T(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ dc}L^{ad}_{\ \ eb}+\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ db}L^{ad}_{\ \ ec}-\gamma^{a}_{\ bc}-\gamma^{a}_{\ cb}\right],

\displaystyle Q(\nabla,g)_{abc}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\gamma^{f}_{\ bc}+\gamma^{f}_{\ cb}-\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ dc}L^{fd}_{\ \ eb}-\Gamma(\nabla)^{e}_{\ db}L^{fd}_{\ \ ec}\right]g_{fa}.

By using the modified anholonomy coefficients, these can be written as

\displaystyle T(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ bc}-\gamma(\nabla)^{a}_{\ cb}\right\},

\displaystyle Q(\nabla,g)_{abc}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\gamma(\nabla)^{f}_{\ bc}-\gamma(\nabla)^{f}_{\ cb}\right\}g_{fa}.

For an admissible linear
E
-connection the modified bracket is anti symmetric so that the modified anholonmy coefficients are anti-symmetric in their lower indices. Hence, the
E
-torsion and
E
-non-metricity components vanish for an admissible
E
-Koszul connection, so it is an
E
-Levi-Civita connection.

For the other direction, we use the fact that an
E
-Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
is
E
-torsion-free by definition, and by the proposition (
3.2
), it has to be admissible. In order to show it is also

-Koszul, we use the ideas identical to the proof of the usual Koszul formula (
2.17
). We start with the following fact due to

-metric-

\rho(g(v,w))=g(\nabla_{u}v,w)+g(v,\nabla_{u}w).

By using the
E
-torsion-freeness condition, this becomes

\rho(u)(g(v,w))=g(\nabla_{v}u,w)+g(\nabla_{u}w,v)+g([u,v]_{E}^{\nabla},w).

Calculating the cyclic permutation in
u,v,w
and evaluating

\rho(u)(g(v,w))+\rho(v)(g(w,u))-\rho(w)(g(u,v)),

we get

\displaystyle 2g(\nabla_{u}v,w)=

\displaystyle\ \rho(u)(g(v,w))+\rho(v)(g(u,w))-\rho(w)(g(u,v))

\displaystyle+g([w,v]_{E}^{\nabla},u)-g([u,w]_{E}^{\nabla},v)-g([v,u]_{E}^{\nabla},w).

As we already proved that
\nabla
is admissible, the anti-symmetry of the modified bracket
[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla}
yields the desired result.
∎

This proposition implies that there are no

Proposition 3.14
.

If
\nabla
is an admissible linear
E
-connection, then

\displaystyle 2g(\nabla_{u}v,w)=

\displaystyle\ 2g(^{g}\tilde{\nabla}_{u}v,w)

\displaystyle-Q(\nabla,g)(u,v,w)-Q(\nabla,g)(v,u,w)+Q(\nabla,g)(w,u,v)

\displaystyle-g(T(\nabla)(v,w),u)-g(T(\nabla)(u,w),v)+g(T(\nabla)(u,v),w).

(3.57)

where
{}^{g}\tilde{\nabla}
is the unique
E
-Levi-Civita connection on the almost-Lie algebroid
(E,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}^{\nabla})

Proof.

This can be proven by copying the Schouten’s trick for evaluating the symmetric and anti-symmetric parts of the
E
-connection. The anti-symmetric part is related to the
E
-torsion:

\nabla_{u}v-\nabla_{v}u=T(\nabla)(u,v)+[u,v]_{E}^{\nabla}\quad\implies\quad g(\nabla_{u}v-\nabla_{v}u,w)=g(T(\nabla)(u,v),w)+g([u,v]_{E}^{\nabla},w).

For the symmetric part we consider the combination

Q(\nabla,g)(u,v,w)+Q(\nabla,g)(v,w,u)-Q(\nabla,g)(w,u,v),

which yields

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{u}v+\nabla_{v}u,w)

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(g(v,w))+\rho(v)(g(u,w))-\rho(w)(g(u,v)

\displaystyle\quad\ -g([v,w]_{E}^{\nabla},u)-g([u,w]_{E}^{\nabla},v)

\displaystyle\quad\ -Q(\nabla,g)(u,v,w)-Q(\nabla,g)(v,w,u)+Q(\nabla,g)(w,u,v),

\displaystyle\quad\ -g(T(\nabla)(v,w),u)-g(T(\nabla)(u,w),w).

Note that in the last step the admissibility is crucial, because it is necessary to use the anti-symmetry of the modified bracket and the
E
-torsion operator. Combining the anti-symmetric and symmetric parts, and singling out the unique
E
-Levi-Civita connection
{}^{g}\tilde{\nabla}
on the almost-Lie algebroid
(E,\rho,[.,.]_{E}^{\nabla})

∎

4
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we introduced and then investigated geometrical structures on anti-commutable pre-Leibniz algebroids. Many algebroids studied in the literature are special cases for these anti-commutable pre-Leibniz algebroids. Thus, the presented geometrical framework on them automatically applies to a variety of different cases. The most crucial point of this work is the definition of admissible linear
E
-connections according to which the bracket satisfies a property like anti-commutativity (
3.8
). We claim that only such admissible linear
E
-connections should be considered while constructing a geometry on an algebroid. We prove that the admissibility condition is equivalent to a metric-compatibility condition in some generalized sense for many existing algebroids in the literature. This is the reason why Courant algebroid connections are necessarily
E
-metric-compatible in order to carry useful properties. In terms of the admissible linear
E
-connections, we offer a new point of view that the modification of
E
-torsion and
E
-curvature operators should be considered as a modification of the bracket instead. Hence one may use the beneficial features of almost- and pre-Lie algebroids, and prove many of the desirable properties and relations that hold in usual metric-affine geometries on a smooth manifold. These include the first and second Bianchi identities, the Ricci identity, Cartan structure equations, Cartan magic formula and the decomposition of linear
E
-connections in terms of its
E
-torsion and
E
-non-metricity.

There are several important structures in the setting of manifolds that can be studied at the algebroid level. For instance, Weyl invariant theories
[
28
]
on an anti-commutable pre-Leibniz algebroid seems a promising case. We actually dealt with conformal, projective and Weyl structures on pre-Leibniz algebroids, which led us to the current work of this paper because many crucial properties of these structures heavily depend on the anti-commutativity of the Lie bracket. Hence, working on an anti-commutable pre-Leibniz algebroid sustains a natural framework for these structures. We plan to investigate different compatibility conditions for these structures as in the work of Matveev and Scholz in the usual geometrical setting
[
29
]
. A fruitful way to proceed might be to combine these ideas and Brans-Dicke theories
[
30
]
since the dilaton field is an important ingredient of string theories so that it is naturally geometrized in an algebroid setting. In future, we wish to work comprehensively on this topic so that the dependence on the light-cone structure can be relaxed due to the existence of a dilaton. Moreover, as the
E
-curvature map is anti-symmetric, one can consider it as an
End(E)
-valued
E
-2-form. This
E
-2-form would make it possible to work on a pre-quantization scheme on algebroids by introducing a line bundle with an
E
-connection whose
E
-curvature 2-form is symplectic. Additionally, in the near future we also plan to work on the algebroid version of statistical structures and information geometry
[
31
]
. More importantly, we plan to investigate the admissibility condition on different algebroids, including the

[

, omni-Lie algebroids

,

[

and
G

. Especially,
E

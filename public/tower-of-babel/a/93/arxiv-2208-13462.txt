Minimizers for the energy of eccentricity matrices of trees
Iswar Mahato, M. Rajesh Kannan

Abstract
The eccentricity matrix of a connected graph G G , denoted by ℰ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{E}(G) , is obtained from the distance matrix of G G by keeping the largest nonzero entries in each row and each column and leaving zeros in the remaining ones. The ℰ \mathcal{E} -eigenvalues of G G are the eigenvalues of ℰ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{E}(G) . The eccentricity energy (or the ℰ \mathcal{E} -energy) of G G is the sum of the absolute values of all ℰ \mathcal{E} -eigenvalues of G G . In this article, we determine the unique tree with the minimum second largest ℰ \mathcal{E} -eigenvalue among all trees on n n vertices other than the star. Also, we characterize the trees with minimum ℰ \mathcal{E} -energy among all trees on n n vertices.

05C50, 05C05.

1
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we consider finite, simple and connected graphs. Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. The number of vertices in
G
is the order of
G
. The
adjacency matrix
of a graph
G
on
n
vertices, denoted by
A(G)
, is the
n\times n
matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by the vertex set of
G
and the entries are defined as

A(G)_{uv}=\begin{cases}\text{$1$}&\quad\text{if $u$ and $v$ are adjacent,}\\
\text{0}&\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The
distance

d_{G}(u,v)
between the vertices
u,v\in V(G)
is the length of a minimum path between them in
G
, and define
d(u,u)=0
for all
u\in V(G)
. The
distance matrix

D(G)
of
G
, is the
n\times n
matrix with its rows and columns are indexed by the vertices of
G
and the
(u,v)
-th entry is equal to
d_{G}(u,v)
. The
eccentricity

e_{G}(u)
of a vertex
u\in V(G)
is given by
e_{G}(u)=\max\{d_{G}(u,v)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v\in V(G)\}
. The maximum eccentricity of all vertices of
G
is the
diameter
of
G
, which is denoted by
\diam(G)
. A
diametrical path
in
G
is a path whose length is equal to the diameter of
G
.

The
eccentricity matrix

\mathcal{E}(G)
of a graph
G
of order
n
is the
n\times n
matrix indexed by the vertices of
G
and the entries are defined as

\mathcal{E}(G)_{uv}=\begin{cases}\text{$d_{G}(u,v)$}&\quad\text{if $d_{G}(u,v)=\min\{e_{G}(u),e_{G}(v)\}$,}\\
\text{0}&\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The eccentricity matrix
\mathcal{E}(G)
of a graph
G
is a real symmetric matrix, and hence all of its eigenvalues are real. The eigenvalues of
\mathcal{E}(G)
are the
\mathcal{E}
-eigenvalues of
G
, in which the largest one is the
\mathcal{E}
-spectral radius of
G
.
Let
\xi_{1}>\xi_{2}>\ldots>\xi_{k}
be all the distinct
\mathcal{E}
-eigenvalues of
G
. Then, the
\mathcal{E}
-spectrum of
G
is defined as

\Spec_{\varepsilon}(G)=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{cccc}\xi_{1}&\xi_{2}&\ldots&\xi_{k}\\
m_{1}&m_{2}&\ldots&m_{k}\\
\end{array}}\right\},

where
m_{i}
is the multiplicity of
\xi_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
.

Although the eccentricity matrix is obtained from the distance matrix, some of the properties of the eccentricity matrix are substantially different from those of the distance matrix. For example, the distance matrix of a connected graph is always irreducible, whereas the eccentricity matrix of a connected graph need not be irreducible
[
17
]
. The eccentricity matrices of trees need not be invertible but the distance matrices of trees are always invertible
[
8
]
. As the eccentricity matrices have different nature compared to the distance matrices, so it is interesting to study their spectral properties based on the combinatorial structure of the graphs. In
[
17
]
, Wang et al. proved that the eccentricity matrices of trees are irreducible and investigated the relations between the
\mathcal{E}
-eigenvalues and the
A
-eigenvalues of graphs. Wang et al.
[
19
]
obtained some bounds for the
\mathcal{E}
-spectral radius of graphs and determined the corresponding extremal graphs; Mahato et al.
[
7
]
investigated the spectra and the inertia of eccentricity matrices of various classes of graphs; Wei et al.
[
20
]
studied the extremal problems for
\mathcal{E}
-spectral radius of trees and determined the trees on
n
vertices with minimum
\mathcal{E}
-spectral radius; Lei et al.
[
6
]
characterized the graphs whose second least
\mathcal{E}
-eigenvalue is greater than
-\sqrt{15-\sqrt{193}}
and proved that all these graphs are determined by their
\mathcal{E}
-spectrum. Recently, He and Lu
[
3
]
Characterized the trees with maximum
\mathcal{E}
-spectral radius among all trees on
n
vertices with fixed odd diameter. Wei et al.
[
22
]
gave an ordering of the trees with a given diameter regarding their
\mathcal{E}
-spectral radii and characterized the trees with second minimum
\mathcal{E}
-spectral radius. Very recently, Mahato and Kannan
[
10
]
determined the inertia of eccentricity matrices of trees and characterized the trees for which the
\mathcal{E}
-eigenvalues are symmetric about the origin. For more advances on the eccentricity matrices of graphs, we refer to
[
1
,
5
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
18
,

]
.

One of the main applications of eccentricity matrix is in chemical graph theory. Randić, Orel, and Balaban
[
14
]
indicated that the eccentricity matrix appears very sensitive to the branching pattern of molecular graphs studied in theoretical chemistry. In
[
17
]
, the authors gave some applications of this novel matrix in terms of molecular descriptors. Recently, Wang et al.
[
15
]
described some applications of eccentricity matrix to the boiling point of hydrocarbons. For more details about the applications of eccentricity matrices of graphs, we refer to
[
13
,
14
,
15
,

The second largest eigenvalues of the adjacency, Laplacian, and distance matrix of a graph are important graph invariants in spectral graph theory and are well studied in the literature. In this article, we consider the second largest eigenvalue of eccentricity matrices of trees and characterize the trees with the minimum second largest
\mathcal{E}
-eigenvalue.

In
[
16
]
, Wang et al. introduced the
eccentricity energy (or the
\mathcal{E}
-energy)
of a graph

E_{\mathcal{E}}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\xi_{i}(G)|,

–eigenvalues of

-energy of

.

In
[
16
]
, Wang et al. studied the

-energy of graphs and determined the
\mathcal{E}
-energies of paths, cycles, and double stars. Also, they obtained some bounds for the

[

constructed a pair of graphs with the same

[

, the authors studied the

-energy of the coalescence of complete graphs and the coalescence of two cycles. Lei et al.
[

obtained an upper bound for the

-energy of graphs and characterized the extremal graphs. Recently, Mahato and Kannan
[

studied the

-energy of a complete multipartite graph always increases due to an edge deletion. Motivated by these works, in this article, we study the

-energy of trees and characterize the trees with minimum

n

For an

, where
n_{+}(A),

n_{-}(A)
and

denote the number of positive, negative and zero eigenvalues of
A
, respectively. For a real number

denotes the greatest integer less than or equal to

, and
\lceil x\rceil
denotes the least integer greater than or equal to

. In the following theorem, we state the well-known Interlacing Theorem.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
4
]
, Interlacing Theorem)
.

Let
M
be a real symmetric matrix of order
m
, and let
N
be a principal submatrix of order
n<m
. If
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{m}
are the eigenvalues of
M
and
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\mu_{n}
are the eigenvalues of
N
, then
\lambda_{m-n+i}\leq\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Now, we recall the definition of quotient matrix and equitable partition.

Definition 2.1

(
[
2
]
, Equitable partition)
.

Let
A
be a real symmetric matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by the set
X=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. Let
\pi=\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}\}
be a partition of
X
. The
characteristic matrix

C
is the
n\times m
matrix whose
j
-th column is the characteristic vector of
X_{j}

(j=1,2,\ldots,m)
. Let
A
be partitioned according to
\pi
as follows

A=\left[{\begin{array}[]{cccc}A_{11}&A_{12}&\ldots&A_{1m}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}&\ldots&A_{2m}\\
\vdots&\ldots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{m1}&A_{m2}&\ldots&A_{mm}\\
\end{array}}\right],

where
A_{ij}
denotes the submatrix (block) of
A
formed by rows in
X_{i}
and the columns in
X_{j}
. If
q_{ij}
denotes the average row sum of
A_{ij}
, then the matrix
Q=(q_{i,j})
is the
quotient matrix
of
A
. If the row sum of each block
A_{ij}
is constant, then the partition
\pi
is the
equitable partition
.

The following theorem is known for the spectrum of a quotient matrix corresponding to an equitable partition.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
Q
be the quotient matrix of any square matrix
A
corresponding to an equitable partition. Then the spectrum of
A
contains the spectrum of
Q
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T
be a tree on
n\geq 4
vertices with diameter
3
. Then,

\xi_{2}(T)\geq\sqrt{\frac{13n-35-\sqrt{169n^{2}-974n+1417}}{2}}

with equality if and only if
T\cong T_{n,3}^{0,n-4}
.

Proof.

Since
T
is a tree of diameter
3
, therefore
T\cong T_{n,3}^{a,b}
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.3
, it follows that

\xi_{2}(T)=\xi_{2}(T_{n,3}^{a,b})=\sqrt{\frac{(9ab+13a+13b+17)-\sqrt{(9ab+13a+13b+17)^{2}-64(a+1)(b+1)}}{2}}.

Since
a+b=n-4

(0\leq a\leq\lfloor\frac{n-4}{2}\rfloor)
, therefore

\xi_{2}(T)=\sqrt{\frac{9a(n-4-a)+13n-35-\sqrt{(9a(n-4-a)+13n-35)^{2}-64(a(n-4-a)+n-3)}}{2}}.

If
a=0
, then
T\cong T_{n,3}^{0,n-4}
and
\xi_{2}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})=\sqrt{\frac{13n-35-\sqrt{169n^{2}-974n+1417}}{2}}
. Now, consider the function

f(x)=9x(n-4-x)+13n-35-\sqrt{(9x(n-4-x)+13n-35)^{2}-64(x(n-4-x)+n-3)}.

Therefore,

f^{\prime}(x)=9(n-4-2x)\bigg(1-\frac{9x(n-4-x)+(13n-35)-\frac{32}{9}}{\sqrt{(9x(n-4-x)+13n-35)^{2}-64(x(n-4-x)+n-3)}}\bigg).

Since
n\geq 4
, therefore

\displaystyle\sqrt{(9x(n-4-x)+13n-35)^{2}-64(x(n-4-x)+n-3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\Big(9x(n-4-x)+13n-35-\frac{32}{9}\Big)^{2}+\frac{2304n-5632}{81}}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 9x(n-4-x)+13n-35-\frac{32}{9}.

Hence,

\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})=\sqrt{\frac{13n-35+\sqrt{(13n-37)^{2}-12(n-4)}}{2}}.

Since
13n-37>12(n-4)
, therefore

\displaystyle\sqrt{(13n-37)^{2}-12(n-4)}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\sqrt{(13n-37)^{2}-(13n-37)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(13n-37)(13n-38)}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 13n-38.

Hence,

\sqrt{\frac{13n-35+\sqrt{(13n-37)^{2}-12(n-4)}}{2}}>\sqrt{\frac{13n-36+13n-38}{2}}=\sqrt{13n-37}.

Again,
\sqrt{(13n-37)^{2}-12(n-4)}<13n-37
implies that

\sqrt{\frac{13n-35+\sqrt{(13n-37)^{2}-12(n-4)}}{2}}<\sqrt{\frac{13n-35+13n-37}{2}}=\sqrt{13n-36}.

Thus,
\sqrt{13n-37}<\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})<\sqrt{13n-36}
.
∎

It is known that the tree
T_{n,3}^{0,n-4}

\mathcal{E}
-spectral radius among all trees on

vertices. In the following theorem, we prove that the tree
T_{n,3}^{0,n-4}
has the minimum
\mathcal{E}
-spectral radius among all trees on

vertices with diameter

.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
T
be a tree on
n\geq 16
vertices with diameter
d\geq 8
. Then,

\xi_{1}(T)>\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4}).

Proof.

If
T
is a tree on
n\geq 16
vertices with diameter
d=8
, then, by Theorem
2.6
, we have
\xi_{1}(T)\geq\xi_{1}(T_{n,8}^{\lfloor\frac{n-9}{2}\rfloor,0,\lceil\frac{n-9}{2}\rceil})
. Again, if
T
is a tree on
n\geq 16
vertices with diameter
d\geq 9
, then, by Lemma
2.6
, Lemma
2.7
and Theorem
2.5
, it follows that
\xi_{1}(T)\geq\xi_{1}(T_{n,9}^{\lfloor\frac{n-10}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n-10}{2}\rceil})
. Therefore, to prove the required result it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(T_{n,8}^{\lfloor\frac{n-9}{2}\rfloor,0,\lceil\frac{n-9}{2}\rceil})>\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\xi_{1}(T_{n,9}^{\lfloor\frac{n-10}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n-10}{2}\rceil})>\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4}).

Case 1.
Let
n\geq 16
be odd. From Theorem
2.8
and Theorem
2.7
, it follows that

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(T_{n,8}^{\frac{n-9}{2},0,\frac{n-9}{2}})=4+\sqrt{18n-4}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\xi_{1}(T_{n,9}^{\frac{n-11}{2},\frac{n-9}{2}})=\sqrt{\frac{36n+69+\sqrt{4104n+5913}}{2}}.

Note that
18n-4>13n-36
and
\frac{36n+69+\sqrt{4104n+5913}}{2}>13n-36
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.3
, we have
\xi_{1}(T_{n,8}^{\frac{n-9}{2},0,\frac{n-9}{2}})=4+\sqrt{18n-4}>\sqrt{13n-36}>\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})
and
\xi_{1}(T_{n,9}^{\frac{n-10}{2},\frac{n-10}{2}})=\sqrt{\frac{36n+69+\sqrt{4104n+4617}}{2}}>\sqrt{13n-36}>\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})
.

Case 2.
Let
n\geq 16
be even. From Theorem
2.8
, it follows that
\xi_{1}(T_{n,8}^{\frac{n-10}{2},0,\frac{n-8}{2}})
is the largest root of
f(x)=0
, where

f(x)=x^{4}-(36n-8)x^{2}-256x+324n^{2}-1296n+716.

Therefore,

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)=4(x^{3}-(18n-4)x-64),\qquad f^{\prime\prime}(x)=4(3x^{2}-18n+4).

Thus,
f^{\prime}(x)
is monotonically decreasing for
x\in(0,\sqrt{\frac{18n-4}{3}})
and monotonically increasing for
x\in(\sqrt{\frac{18n-4}{3}},\infty)
. Note that
f^{\prime}(0)<0
, therefore
f^{\prime}(x)=0
has a unique positive root, say
x_{0}
. Since
(23n+27)^{2}-(169n^{2}-974n+1417)=8(45n^{2}+277n-86)>0
, we have

\displaystyle f^{\prime}\big(\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})\big)=

\displaystyle 4\Big(\big(\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})\big)^{3}-(18n-4)\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})-64\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})\Big(\big(\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})\big)^{2}-(18n-4)\Big)-256

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})\bigg(\frac{13n-35+\sqrt{169n^{2}-974n+1417}}{2}-18n+4\bigg)-256

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})\bigg(23n+27-\sqrt{169n^{2}-974n+1417}\bigg)-256<0.

This implies that
\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})<x_{0}<\xi_{1}(T_{n,8}^{\frac{n-10}{2},0,\frac{n-8}{2}})
.

Again, by Theorem
2.7
, we have
\xi_{1}(T_{n,9}^{\frac{n-10}{2},\frac{n-10}{2}})=\sqrt{\frac{36n+69+\sqrt{4104n+4617}}{2}}>\sqrt{13n-36}>\xi_{1}(T_{n,3}^{0,n-4})
.
∎

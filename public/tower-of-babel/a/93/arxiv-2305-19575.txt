On the Linear Convergence of Policy Gradient under Hadamard Parameterization
Jiacai Liu, Jinchi Chen, Ke Wei

Abstract
The convergence of deterministic policy gradient under the Hadamard parameterization is studied in the tabular setting and the linear convergence of the algorithm is established. To this end, we first show that the error decreases at an O ⁡ ( 1 k ) O(\frac{1}{k}) rate for all the iterations. Based on this result, we further show that the algorithm has a faster local linear convergence rate after k 0 k_{0} iterations, where k 0 k_{0} is a constant that only depends on the MDP problem and the initialization. To show the local linear convergence of the algorithm, we have indeed established the contraction of the sub-optimal probability b s k b_{s}^{k} (i.e., the probability of the output policy π k \pi^{k} on non-optimal actions) when k ≥ k 0 k\geq k_{0} . Keywords. Policy gradient, Hadamard parameterization, linear convergence

Introduction

Reinforcement learning (RL) is a type of machine learning technique for solving sequential decision problems and has recently achieved great success in many areas, such as games
[
27
,
32
,
6
,
35
]
, robotics
[
11
,
17
,
25
]
and various other real applications
[
1
,
9
,
26
]
. Typically, RL can be modeled as a Markov decision process (MDP) represented by a tuple
\mathcal{M}\left({\cal S},{\cal A},P,r,\gamma,\mu\right)
, where
{\cal S}
is the state space,
{\cal A}
denotes the action space,
P(s^{\prime}|s,a)
is the transition probability or density from state
s
to state
s^{\prime}
under action
a
,
r:{\cal S}\times{\cal A}\times{\cal S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the reward function,
\gamma\in[0,1)
is the discounted factor and
\mu
is the probability distribution of the initial state
s_{0}
. Here we restrict our attention to the tabular setting where
{\cal S}
and
{\cal A}
are finite, i.e.
|{\cal S}|<\infty
and
|{\cal A}|<\infty
. Let
\Delta({\cal A})
be the probability simplex over the set
{\cal A}
, defined as

\displaystyle\Delta\left({\cal A}\right)=\left\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\cal A}|}:\theta_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{|{\cal A}|}{\theta_{i}}=1\right\}.

(1.1)

Given a stationary policy
\pi:{\cal S}\rightarrow\Delta({\cal A})
, the state value function at
s\in{\cal S}
and the state-action value function at
(s,a)\in{\cal S}\times{\cal A}
are defined as

\displaystyle V^{\pi}\left(s\right)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left\{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}{\gamma^{t}r\left(s_{t},a_{t},s_{t+1}\right)}|s_{0}=s,\pi\right\},

(1.2)

\displaystyle Q^{\pi}\left(s,a\right)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left\{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}{\gamma^{t}r\left(s_{t},a_{t},s_{t+1}\right)}|s_{0}=s,a_{0}=a,\pi\right\}.

(1.3)

Overall, the goal of RL is to a find a policy that maximizes the weighted average of the state values under the initial distribution
\mu
, namely to solve

\displaystyle\underset{\pi}{\max}\,V^{\pi}\left(\mu\right).

(1.4)

Here
V^{\pi}\left(\rho\right):=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\rho}\left\{V^{\pi}\left(s\right)\right\}
for any
\rho\in\Delta({\cal S})
.

Overall, there are two typical categories of RL algorithms. Value-based methods, such as value iteration
[
28
]
, Q-learning
[
36
]
and DQN
[
27
]
, seek the optimal policy based on the idea of fixed point iteration or approximate dynamic programming. In contrast, policy-based methods, including policy gradient
[
28
]
, TRPO
[
29
]
and PPO
[
30
]
reformulate (
1.4
) as an optimization problem based on differentiable parameterized policies and then solve the problem by optimization methods.

In this work, we focus on the policy optimization based methods
[
37
,
34
,
16
]
. Using a differentiable parameterized policy
\pi_{\theta}
where
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, (
1.4
) can be written as a finite dimensional optimization problem:

\displaystyle\underset{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\max}\,V^{\pi_{\theta}}\left(\mu\right).

(1.5)

The most widely studied parameterizations in tabular setting are direct parameterization:

\displaystyle\pi_{\theta}\left(a|s\right)=\theta_{s,a}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\sum_{a\in{\cal A}}{\theta_{s,a}}=1,

(1.6)

and the softmax parameterization:

\displaystyle\pi_{\theta}\left(a|s\right)=\frac{\exp\left\{\theta_{s,a}\right\}}{\sum_{a\in{\cal A}}{\exp\left\{\theta_{s,a}\right\}}}.

(1.7)

Policy gradient (PG) is a first order method for solving (
1.5
).
Besides, natural policy gradient (NPG)
[
13
]
is an important variant of PG, which can be viewed as a preconditioned first order method
[
5
]
. Moreover, it is shown that the NPG method can be cast as a special case of the policy mirror dscent (PMD) method
[
31
]
.

1.1
Motivation

A recent work
[
18
]
in optimization shows that converting an optimization problem on a standard simplex into one on a unit sphere may have several advantages, such as the projection onto the unit sphere admits a simple closed-form solution and the corresponding algorithm can be significantly faster empirically. In the RL setting, the objective (
1.4
) can be seen as an optimization problem on the Cartesian product of simplices. Inspired by
[
18
]
, we consider the Hadamard parameterization for the RL problem:

\displaystyle\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s)=\theta_{s}\odot\theta_{s}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\theta_{s}\in\mathbb{S}^{|{\cal A}|-1},

(1.8)

which leads to an optimization problem constrained on the Cartesian product of unit spheres. Hadamard PG utilizes the Riemannian gradient ascent to solve this problem. As far as we know, the study of the Hadamard parameterization has not been well-received in RL. A most related work is
[
23
]
, which studies the escort policy

\displaystyle\pi_{\theta}(a|s)=\frac{\left|\theta_{s,a}\right|^{p}}{\sum_{a^{\prime}\in{\cal A}}{\left|\theta_{s,a^{\prime}}\right|^{p}}},

(1.9)

in which the
p=2
case can be seen as the normalized version of the Hadamard parameterization. It is worth noting that the policy sequence
\pi^{k}

generated by the Hadamard PG with a constant step size
\eta
is the same as that generated by the escort PG with
p=2
and the step sizes
\eta_{k}(s)=\eta\cdot\left\|\theta_{s}^{k}\right\|^{2}_{p}
(algorithm investigated in
[
23
]
; see Section
2.3
for more details of the equivalence), where
\theta^{k}
is the output parameter at the
k
-th iteration. Thus the sublinear convergence result established in
[
23
]
is also applicable to Hadamard PG. The goal of this paper is to provide an improved sublinear convergence analysis for Hadamard PG compared with the one in
[
23
]
and meanwhile show that Hadamard PG also enjoys local linear convergence based on the sub-optimal probability analysis framework.

\left|{\cal S}\right|

1.3
More Notations and Organization

Advantage function.

Recalling the definitions of the state value function (
1.2
) and the state-action value function (
1.3
), the advantage function of a policy
\pi
is defined as

\displaystyle A^{\pi}\left(s,a\right):=Q^{\pi}\left(s,a\right)-V^{\pi}\left(s\right).

The advantage function
A^{\pi}(s,a)
measures how well a single action is compared with the current policy
\pi
.

State visitation probability.

The discounted state visitation probability from any initial state
s_{0}\in{\cal S}
is defined as

\displaystyle d_{s_{0}}^{\pi}\left(s\right):=\left(1-\gamma\right)\mathbb{E}\left\{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}{\gamma^{t}\mathbf{1}[s_{t}=s]}|s_{0},\pi\right\},

where
\mathbf{1}[\cdot]
is the indicator function. Moreover, define
d_{\rho}^{\pi}\left(s\right):=\mathbb{E}_{s_{0}\sim\rho}\left\{d_{s_{0}}^{\pi}\left(s\right)\right\}
for any
\rho\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
. A direct computation yields that

\displaystyle d_{\rho}^{\pi}\left(s\right)\geq\left(1-\gamma\right)\mathbb{E}_{s_{0}\sim\rho}\{\mathbf{1}[s_{0}=s]\}\geq\left(1-\gamma\right)\min_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\,\rho(s)\quad\mbox{for any policy }\pi.

(1.10)

Optimal values and optimal policies.

The optimal values are defined as the supremum over the all possible polices, i.e.

\displaystyle V^{*}\left(s\right):=\underset{\pi}{\mathrm{sup}}\,V^{\pi}\left(s\right),\quad Q^{*}\left(s,a\right):=\underset{\pi}{\mathrm{sup}}\,Q^{\pi}\left(s,a\right),

(1.11)

If a policy
\pi^{*}
satisfies
\mathrm{supp(}\pi^{*}(\cdot|s))\subseteq\,\underset{a\in{\cal A}}{\text{argmax}}\,\,Q^{*}\left(s,a\right)
for any state
s\in{\cal S}
, then it maximizes the state value function at all states simultaneously,

V^{\pi^{*}}\left(s\right)=V^{*}\left(s\right),\quad\forall s\in{\cal S}.

Such policies are also referred to as the
optimal policies
. Here, we use
\text{supp}(\cdot)
to denote the support set of any probability distribution.

Other useful notations in the proofs.

Given a state
s\in{\cal S}
, we use
{\cal A}_{s}^{\ast}
to denote the set of optimal actions at state
s
,

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{s}^{\ast}=\arg\max_{a\in{\cal A}}Q^{\ast}(s,a).

Given a policy
\pi
,
we use
b_{s}^{\pi}
to denote the probability of
\pi
on non-optimal actions at state
s
,

\displaystyle b_{s}^{\pi}=\sum_{a\notin{\cal A}_{s}^{\ast}}\pi(a|s).

When
{\cal A}^{*}_{s}\neq{\cal A}
, we also define the following two quantities:

\displaystyle\widehat{V}^{*}\left(s\right):=\underset{a\notin{\cal A}_{s}^{*}}{\min}\,Q^{*}\left(s,a\right),\quad\widetilde{V}^{*}\left(s\right):=\underset{a\notin{\cal A}_{s}^{*}}{\max}\,Q^{*}\left(s,a\right),

(1.12)

We also define the optimal advantage function gap
\Delta
as

\displaystyle\Delta:=\underset{s\in\tilde{S},a\notin\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}}{\min}\left|A^{\pi^{*}}(s,a)\right|=\underset{s\in\tilde{S}}{\min}\,\{V^{*}(s)-\tilde{V}^{*}(s)\},

(1.13)

where
\widetilde{S}=\{s\in{\cal S}:{\cal A}^{*}_{s}\neq{\cal A}\}
denotes the set of states that have non-optimal actions.
The well-known Bellman optimality equation states that

\displaystyle V^{*}\left(s\right)=\underset{a\in\mathcal{A}}{\max}\,Q^{*}\left(s,a\right).

Thus for any
\rho\in\Delta({\cal A})
that supported on
{\cal A}\setminus{\cal A}^{*}_{s}
, it can be verified that

\displaystyle\widehat{V}^{\ast}\left(s\right)\leq\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\rho}\left\{Q^{*}\left(s,a^{\prime}\right)\right\}\leq\widetilde{V}^{\ast}\left(s\right).

When
{\cal A}^{\ast}_{s}={\cal A}
, we let
\widehat{V}^{\ast}(s)=0
and
\widetilde{V}^{\ast}(s)=V^{*}(s)
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2.1
, we derive the expression of the PG algorithm under the Hadamard parametrization, and the main theoretical results are provided in Section
2.2
. In Section
2.3
, we introduce an equivalent expression of the algorithm based on the normalized Hadamard parametrization which does not require explicit projection onto the unit sphere. The proofs of the convergence results are provided in Section
3
while the proofs of the useful lemmas are deferred to Section
4
. Finally, we conclude this paper with a short discussion in Section
5
.

2.1
Algorithm

Recall that in the definition of the Hadamard parameterization (see (
1.8
)), the vector
\theta_{s}
for every state
s\in{\cal S}
is restricted to the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{|{\cal A}|-1}
. Thus the Riemannian gradient ascent algorithm can be used to solve the corresponding policy optimization problem. To compute the Riemannian gradient, we can first compute the Euclidean gradient and then projection it onto the tangent space. Let
g^{E}=[(g_{1}^{E})^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,(g_{|{\cal S}|}^{E})^{\mathsf{T}}]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\cal S}|\cdot|{\cal A}|}
,
where
g_{s}^{E}:=\nabla_{\theta_{s}}V^{\pi_{\theta}}\left(\mu\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\cal A}|}
is the Euclidean gradient with respect to parameter
\theta_{s}
, and let
g_{s,a}^{E}
be the
a
-th entry of
g_{s}^{E}
. According to the policy gradient theorem
[
33
]
, the Euclidean gradient of the objective (
1.5
) associated with any policy
\pi
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V^{\pi}\left(\mu\right)}{\partial\pi\left(a|s\right)}=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{\pi}\left(s\right)Q^{\pi}\left(s,a\right).

Then it follows from the chain rule that

\displaystyle g_{s,a}^{E}=\frac{\partial V^{\pi_{\theta}}\left(\mu\right)}{\partial\theta_{s,a}}=\frac{\partial V^{\pi_{\theta}}\left(\mu\right)}{\partial\pi_{\theta}\left(a|s\right)}\cdot\frac{\partial\pi_{\theta}\left(a|s\right)}{\partial\theta_{s,a}}=\frac{2\theta_{s,a}}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{\pi_{\theta}}\left(s\right)Q^{\pi}\left(s,a\right).

Noting that the tangent space of
\mathbb{S}^{|{\cal A}|-1}
at each
\theta_{s}
is given by

\displaystyle T_{\theta_{s}}\mathbb{S}^{|{\cal A}|-1}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\cal A}|}:\left<y,\theta_{s}\right>=0\right\},

the projection of
g^{E}_{s}
onto
T_{\theta_{s}}\mathbb{S}^{|{\cal A}|-1}
(i.e. the Riemannian gradient, denoted
g_{s}
) is given by

\displaystyle g_{s}=(I-\theta_{s}\theta_{s}^{\mathsf{T}})g^{E}_{s}.

Elementwisely, we have

\displaystyle g_{s,a}

\displaystyle=g_{s,a}^{E}-\theta_{s,a}(\theta_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}g_{s}^{E})

\displaystyle=g_{s,a}^{E}-\theta_{s,a}\sum_{a^{\prime}}\theta_{s,a^{\prime}}g^{E}_{s,a^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\theta_{s,a}}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{\pi_{\theta}}\left(s\right)Q^{\pi}\left(s,a\right)-\theta_{s,a}\sum_{a^{\prime}}\frac{2\theta_{s,a^{\prime}}^{2}}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{\pi_{\theta}}\left(s\right)Q^{\pi}\left(s,a^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\theta_{s,a}}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{\pi_{\theta}}\left(s\right)\left(Q^{\pi}(s,a)-\sum_{a^{\prime}}\theta_{s,a^{\prime}}^{2}Q^{\pi}(s,a^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\theta_{s,a}}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)A^{\pi}(s,a).

(2.1)

After updating
\theta_{s}
towards the Riemannian gradient ascent direction, an additional normalization step is needed to ensure that the new estimate will be on the unit sphere. More precisely, letting
\eta
be the step size, the basic update can be written as

\displaystyle\theta_{s}\leftarrow\frac{\theta_{s}+\eta g_{s}}{\left\|\theta_{s}+\eta g_{s}\right\|_{2}}.

(2.2)

Substituting the expression of
g_{s,a}
into (
2.2
) yields that

\displaystyle\theta_{s,a}\leftarrow\frac{\theta_{s,a}\left(1+\frac{2\eta d_{\mu}^{\pi}\left(s\right)}{1-\gamma}A^{\pi}\left(s,a\right)\right)}{\sqrt{1+\frac{4\eta^{2}\left(d_{\mu}^{\pi}\left(s\right)\right)^{2}}{\left(1-\gamma\right)^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\pi\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left\{\left(A^{\pi}\left(s,a^{\prime}\right)\right)^{2}\right\}}}.

(2.3)

The complete description of the algorithm is presented in Algorithm
1
, where we simplify the notations
d^{\pi_{\theta^{k}}}_{\mu}
,
V^{\pi_{\theta^{k}}}
,
Q^{\pi_{\theta^{k}}}
,
A^{\pi_{\theta^{k}}}
,
\pi_{\theta^{k}}
to
d^{k}_{\mu}
,
V^{k}
,
Q^{k}
,
A^{k}
,
\pi^{k}
, respectively.
Note that all the entries of the initial parameters
\theta^{0}
must be non-zeros, otherwise the parameters will not be updated.

step size
\eta>0
, initial parameters

\pi_{s,a}^{k}=\left(\theta_{s,a}^{k}\right)^{2},~\forall s\in{\cal S},a\in{\cal A}.

d^{k}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\theta_{s,a}^{k+1}=\frac{\theta_{s,a}^{k}\left(1+\frac{2\eta d_{\mu}^{k}\left(s\right)}{1-\gamma}A^{k}\left(s,a\right)\right)}{\sqrt{1+\frac{4\eta^{2}\left(d_{\mu}^{k}\left(s\right)\right)^{2}}{\left(1-\gamma\right)^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\pi^{k}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left\{\left(A^{k}\left(s,a^{\prime}\right)\right)^{2}\right\}}},\quad\forall s\in{\cal S},a\in{\cal A};

2.2
Theoretical results

In this section, we provide the convergence results for Algorithm
1

under standard assumptions. In Theorem
2.1
, the global convergence of the algorithm is established. Theorem
2.2
shows that the state value error decreases at an
O(\frac{1}{k})
rate for all the iterations. Furthermore, we show that in Theorem
2.3
that the algorithm enjoys a linear convergence rate after
k_{0}
iterations and overall displays a global linear convergence.

Assumption 2.1

(Bounded Reward)
.

r(s,a)\in[0,1],\forall(s,a).

Assumption 2.2

(Sufficient Exploration)
.

\tilde{\mu}:=\underset{s\in{\cal S}}{\min}\,\mu\left(s\right)>0
.

Remark 2.1
.

The existing global convergence results of the PG methods relies heavily on this assumption. Whether gradient ascent will globally converge if this condition is not met remains an open problem
[
2
]
. When this condition is met, it follows from (
1.10

Assumption 2.3

There exists at least one state that has non-optimal actions, i.e.

. Otherwise, it is trivial that any policy is an optimal policy.

Theorem 2.1

(Global Convergence)
.

For any constant step size
\eta=\frac{(1-\gamma)^{2}}{4}\kappa

and the initial parameters

\displaystyle\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\,\,V^{k}\left(\rho\right)=V^{*}\left(\rho\right).

(2.6)

Theorem 2.2

(Sublinear Convergence)
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
2.1
, the value functions of the policies generated by Algorithm

converge to the optimal value with a sublinear rate,

\displaystyle V^{*}\left(\mu\right)-V^{k}\left(\mu\right)\leq\frac{g\left(\kappa,\lambda\right)^{-1}}{k},

where
g\left(\kappa,\lambda\right)=\frac{2\kappa\tilde{\mu}^{2}\left(1-\gamma\right)^{4}\lambda}{4+\kappa^{2}}

Theorem 2.3

(Linear Convergence)
.

For any

and

, under the conditions of Theorem

, the value functions of the policies generated by Algorithm

\displaystyle V^{*}\left(\mu\right)-V^{k}\left(\mu\right)\leq\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\cdot\left(1-\rho\left(\delta,\kappa,\gamma\right)\right)^{k-k_{0}\left(\delta,\kappa,\gamma\right)},

where

and

Remark 2.2
.

It can be observed that there are two problem-dependent parameters
\tilde{\mu}
and
\Delta
in
\rho\left(\delta,\kappa,\gamma\right)
. Firstly, the parameter
\tilde{\mu}
essentially comes from the lower bound for the visitation measure term
d_{\mu}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
in the Riemannian gradient expression (
2.1
). Due to the existence of this term, the effective step size is indeed
\eta\cdot\min_{s}d_{\mu}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)\propto\eta\cdot\tilde{\mu}
(since
(1-\gamma)\tilde{\mu}
provides a uniform lower bound for
d_{\mu}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s))
, which means smaller
\tilde{\mu}
leads to smaller effective step size, and thus slower convergence. Secondly, recall that
\Delta
is the optimal advantage function gap, and small
\Delta
means that it is difficult to tell the optimal actions apart from non-optimal ones. Thus, it is reasonable for an algorithm to have a slow convergence rate when
\Delta
is small.

2.2

The proof of the sublinear convergence of Hadamard PG is substantially different from for example the proof for the sublinear convergence of simplex PG and softmax PG in

]

3.3
Proof of Theorem
2.3

The analysis of the linear convergence relies heavily on the contraction of the optimality measure
b^{k}_{s}
(probability of the output policy
\pi^{k}
on non-optimal actions), which is also used in
[
15
]
. More precisely, we show that the sub-optimal probabilities
b^{k}_{s}
satisfy (see Lemma
3.5
)

b^{k}_{s}-b^{k+1}_{s}\geq C^{k}\cdot b^{k}_{s}

for sufficiently large
k
, where the constant
C^{k}
depends on the policy
\pi^{k}
. Plugging this result into Lemma
3.2
yields the linear convergence of Hadamard PG after some calculations.

It is worth noting that, based on
b_{s}^{k}
, we can give an intuitive explanation on why softmax PG with a constant step size has at most a sublinear convergence rate while softmax NPG and Hadamard PG with a constant step size can have a local linear convergence rate. First, the update rules of these three algorithms in the policy space are given by

\displaystyle\text{Softmax PG}:

\displaystyle\quad\pi^{k+1}\left(a|s\right)\propto\pi^{k}\left(a|s\right)\cdot\exp\{\eta\cdot\frac{d^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\cdot A^{k}(s,a)\cdot{\pi^{k}\left(a|s\right)}\},

\displaystyle\text{Softmax NPG}:

\displaystyle\quad\pi^{k+1}\left(a|s\right)\propto\pi^{k}\left(a|s\right)\cdot\exp\{\eta\cdot\frac{d^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\cdot A^{k}(s,a)\},

\displaystyle\text{Hadamard PG}:

\displaystyle\quad\pi^{k+1}\left(a|s\right)\propto\pi^{k}\left(a|s\right)\cdot(1+2\eta\cdot\frac{d^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\cdot A^{k}(s,a))^{2},

where
\eta
is a constant step size.
Lemma
3.2
shows that the convergence of an algorithm is indeed controlled by the convergence of the probability of the output policy on non-optimal actions, that is
b^{k}_{s}
. If the global convergence is established, one has
\forall a\notin\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}:\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\pi^{k}\left(a|s\right)=0
. Thus the policy update ratio in softmax PG will satisfy
\forall a\notin\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}:\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{\pi^{k+1}(a|s)}{\pi^{k}\left(a|s\right)}=1
due to the existence of
\pi^{k}(a|s)
within the exponential, which roughly implies that
\forall s\in\mathcal{S}:\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{b_{s}^{k+1}}{b_{s}^{k}}=1
. Therefore the linear convergence (even locally) of softmax PG cannot be established. However, for Hadamard PG and softmax NPG, the policy update ratio of any non-optimal action will not be significantly slowed down by the factor
\pi^{k}\left(a|s\right)
and is only determined by the advantage function
A^{k}\left(s,a\right)
. Owing to this, we could have
\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{b_{s}^{k+1}}{b_{s}^{k}}<1
since
A^{k}\left(s,a\right)
is negative for non-optimal actions, and thus the linear convergence can be established.

.

\displaystyle\geq(1-b_{s}^{k})b_{s}^{k}\cdot z_{s}^{k}-\left|\sum_{a\in{\cal A}_{s}^{\ast}}\pi^{k}(a|s)\phi_{s,a}^{k}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\min(1-b_{s}^{k},b_{s}^{k})\left(\frac{z_{s}^{k}}{2}-\max_{s,a}\left|\phi_{s,a}^{k}\right|\right).

(4.23)

In what follows, we will control
z_{s}^{k}
and
|\phi_{s,a}^{k}|
, respectively.

•

Bounding
z_{s}^{k}
.

Recall the definition of

\displaystyle z_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle=(V^{\ast}(s)-e_{s}^{k})\left(1-\frac{2\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\cdot V^{k}(s)\right)+\frac{\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\left(\left(V^{\ast}(s)\right)^{2}-(d_{s}^{k})^{2}\right).

According to the Jensen inequality, one has

\displaystyle\widehat{V}^{*}(s)\leq e_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left\{Q^{*}(s,a^{\prime})\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left\{\left(Q^{\ast}(s,a^{\prime})\right)^{2}\right\}}=d_{s}^{k}\leq\widetilde{V}^{*}(s),

which implies that
V^{\ast}(s)-e_{s}^{k}\geq V^{\ast}(s)-d_{s}^{k}\geq V^{\ast}(s)-\widetilde{V}^{*}(s)>0
. Thus,

\displaystyle z_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle=(V^{\ast}(s)-e_{s}^{k})\left(1-\frac{2\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\cdot V^{k}(s)\right)+\frac{\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\left(\left(V^{\ast}(s)\right)^{2}-(d_{s}^{k})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(V^{\ast}(s)-d_{s}^{k})\left(1-\frac{2\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\cdot V^{k}(s)\right)+\frac{\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\left(\left(V^{\ast}(s)\right)^{2}-(d_{s}^{k})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(V^{\ast}(s)-d_{s}^{k})\left(1+\frac{\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\left(V^{\ast}(s)+d^{k}_{s}-2V^{k}(s)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=(V^{\ast}(s)-d_{s}^{k})\left(1-\frac{\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\left(V^{\ast}(s)-d^{k}_{s}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=(V^{\ast}(s)-d_{s}^{k})\left(1-\frac{\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=(V^{\ast}(s)-d_{s}^{k})\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=(V^{\ast}(s)-\tilde{V}^{\ast}(s))\left(\kappa-\frac{\kappa}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3\kappa\Delta}{4}

(4.24)

•

Bounding
|\phi_{s,a}^{k}|
. By the definition, we have

\displaystyle|\phi_{s,a}^{k}|

\displaystyle=\left|\ell_{s,a}^{k}\left(1-\frac{2\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\cdot V^{k}(s)\right)+\frac{\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\cdot\tilde{\ell}_{s,a}^{k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\ell_{s,a}^{k}\right|\cdot\left|1-\frac{2\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\cdot V^{k}(s)\right|+\frac{\eta d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}\cdot\left|\tilde{\ell}_{s,a}^{k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\ell_{s,a}^{k}\right|+\frac{\eta}{1-\gamma}\cdot\left|\tilde{\ell}_{s,a}^{k}\right|.

We will control
\left|\ell_{s,a}^{k}\right|
and
\left|\tilde{\ell}_{s,a}^{k}\right|
, respectively.

–

Bounding
\left|\ell_{s,a}^{k}\right|
. Recall the definition

\displaystyle\ell_{s,a}^{k}=\gamma\left(\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a^{\prime}),a^{\prime}\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left\{\delta_{s^{\prime}}^{k}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}-\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left\{\delta_{s^{\prime}}^{k}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}\right).

A direct computation yields that

\displaystyle\left|\ell_{s,a}^{k}\right|

\displaystyle=\gamma\left|\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left\{\delta_{s^{\prime}}^{k}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}-\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a^{\prime})}\left\{\delta_{s^{\prime}}^{k}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\left|\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left\{\delta_{s^{\prime}}^{k}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left\{\left|\delta_{s^{\prime}}^{k}\right|\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}

\displaystyle=\gamma\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left\{\left|V^{\ast}(s^{\prime})-V^{k}(s^{\prime})\right|\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}.

–

Bounding
\left|\tilde{\ell}_{s,a}^{k}\right|
. Similarly, one has

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{\ell}_{s,a}^{k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{2}\left|\left(\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left\{\delta_{s^{\prime}}^{k}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}\right)^{2}-\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left\{\left(\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left\{\delta_{s^{\prime}}^{k}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}\right)^{2}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+2\gamma\left|\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left\{Q^{\ast}(s,a^{\prime})\cdot\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left\{\delta_{s^{\prime}}^{k}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}-Q^{\ast}(s,a)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left\{\delta_{s^{\prime}}^{k}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{2}\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\gamma V^{\ast}(s)\cdot\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\gamma^{2}\cdot\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}+2\gamma\cdot\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right)\cdot\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\gamma^{2}\cdot\max_{s}(V^{\ast}(s))+2\gamma\cdot\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right)\cdot\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\gamma^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\gamma}+2\gamma\cdot\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right)\cdot\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}+2\gamma}{1-\gamma}\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}.

Combining them together, we have

\displaystyle|\phi_{s,a}^{k}|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\ell_{s,a}^{k}\right|+\frac{\eta}{1-\gamma}\cdot\left|\tilde{\ell}_{s,a}^{k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\gamma+\frac{\eta}{1-\gamma}\frac{\gamma^{2}+2\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\gamma+\frac{\eta}{1-\gamma}\frac{3\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left(\gamma+\frac{(1-\gamma)^{2}\kappa}{4}\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\frac{3\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{2}\gamma\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}.

(4.25)

Plugging (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) into (
4.23
) yields that

\displaystyle T_{s}

\displaystyle\geq\min(1-b_{s}^{k},b_{s}^{k})\left(\frac{z_{s}^{k}}{2}-\max_{s,a}\left|\phi_{s,a}^{k}\right|\right)

(4.26)

\displaystyle\geq\min(1-b_{s}^{k},b_{s}^{k})\left(\frac{3\kappa\Delta}{8}-\frac{5}{2}\gamma\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}\right).

(4.27)

Submitting it into (
4.17
) completes the proof.

4.5
Proof of Lemma
3.6

Firstly, we show that
1-b_{s}^{k}>0
.
Recall that

\displaystyle g_{s,a}^{k}=\frac{2}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{\pi^{k}}(s)\theta_{s,a}^{k}A^{k}(s,a)\text{ and }\bm{\theta}_{s}^{k+1}=\frac{\bm{\theta}_{s}^{k}+\eta\bm{g}_{s}^{k}}{\left\|\bm{\theta}_{s}^{k}+\eta\bm{g}_{s}^{k}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{2}}}}\text{ for }s\in{\cal S}.

For any state
s\in{\cal S}
, a direct computation yields that

\displaystyle\pi^{k+1}(a|s)

\displaystyle=\pi^{k}(a|s)\cdot\frac{\left(1+\eta g_{s,a}^{k}/\theta_{s,a}^{k}\right)^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}\left\|\bm{g}_{s}^{k}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{2}}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{k}(a|s)}{1+\eta^{2}\left\|\bm{g}_{s}^{k}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{2}}}^{2}}\left(1+\eta g_{s,a}^{k}/\theta_{s,a}^{k}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi^{k}(a|s)}{1+\eta^{2}\left\|\bm{g}_{s}^{k}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{2}}}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{4\eta}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{k}(s)A^{k}(s,a)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi^{k}(a|s)}{1+\eta^{2}\left\|\bm{g}_{s}^{k}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{2}}}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{4\eta}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{k}(s)\left|A^{k}(s,a)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}\frac{\pi^{k}(a|s)}{1+\eta^{2}\left\|\bm{g}_{s}^{k}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{2}}}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{4\eta}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}\frac{\pi^{k}(a|s)}{1+\eta^{2}\left\|\bm{g}_{s}^{k}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{2}}}^{2}}\left(1-\kappa\right),

where step (a) follows from
d_{\mu}^{k}\leq 1
and
|A^{k}(s,a)|\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
and step (b) is due to
\eta=\frac{(1-\gamma)^{2}\kappa}{4}
. Since
\pi^{0}(a|s)>0
, it can be seen that
\pi^{k}(a|s)>0
for all
k\geq 1
. Thus we have

\displaystyle 1-b_{s}^{k}=1-\sum_{a\notin{\cal A}_{s}^{\ast}}\pi^{k}(a|s)=\sum_{a\in{\cal A}_{s}^{\ast}}\pi^{k}(a|s)>0

for all
k\geq 0
. Moreover, Lemma
3.5
implies that the sequence
\{b_{s}^{k}\}_{k\geq k_{0}}
is nonincreasing when
\underset{s\in\mathcal{S}}{\max}\left(V^{*}\left(s\right)-V^{k}\left(s\right)\right)
is sufficiently small. Thus by Theorem
2.1
, one can conclude that there exist a constant
k_{0}
such that
\{b_{s}^{k}\}_{k\geq k_{0}}
is nonincreasing for
k\geq k_{0}
and thus we have
\lambda:=\underset{k\geq 0}{\inf}\left\{\underset{s\in{\cal S}}{\min}(1-b_{s}^{k})\right\}>0
.

5
Discussion

In this paper, we consider Hadamard parameterization in policy gradient and the local linear convergence of Hadamard PG is established in the tabular setting provided the exact gradient can be accessed. For future work, we would like to see whether the analysis can be extended to a broader range of step sizes.
In addition, it is interesting to study the convergence of the algorithm in the function approximation setting as well as in random sampling setting.
Moreover, it is likely to accelerate the algorithm using the idea of natural gradient and meanwhile establish the convergence of the corresponding algorithm.

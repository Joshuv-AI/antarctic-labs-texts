The Collatz Conjecture & Non-Archimedean Spectral Theory - Part I.5 - How To Write The (Weak) Collatz Conjecture As A Contour Integral
by Maxwell C. Siegel

Abstract
. Let q q be an odd prime, and let T q : ℤ → ℤ T_{q}:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z} be the Shortened q ​ x + 1 qx+1 map, defined by T q ​ ( n ) = n / 2 T_{q}\left(n\right)=n/2 if n n is even and T q ​ ( n ) = ( q ​ n + 1 ) / 2 T_{q}\left(n\right)=\left(qn+1\right)/2 if n n is odd. The study of the dynamics of these maps is infamous for its difficulty, with the characterization of the dynamics of T 3 T_{3} being an alternative formulation of the famous Collatz Conjecture . This series of papers presents a new paradigm for studying such arithmetic dynamical systems by way of a neglected area of ultrametric analysis which we have termed ( p , q ) \left(p,q\right) -adic analysis , the study of functions from the p p -adics to the q q -adics, where p p and q q are distinct primes. In this, the first-and-a-halfth paper of the series, as a first application, we show that the numen χ q \chi_{q} of T q T_{q} can be used in conjunction with the Correspondence Principle (CP) and classic complex-analytic tools of analytic number theory to reformulate the study of periodic points of T q T_{q} in terms of a contour integral via an application of Perron’s Formula to a Dirichlet series generated by χ q \chi_{q} and the function M q M_{q} introduced in the first paper in this series, for which we establish functional equations, which we use to derive their meromorphic continuations to the left half-plane. The hypergeometric growth of the series as Re ​ ( s ) → − ∞ \textrm{Re}\left(s\right)\rightarrow-\infty seems to preclude direct evaluation of the contour integrals via residues, but asymptotic results may still be achievable.

Remark 2

Recall the identity:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\chi_{q}\left(2\mathfrak{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q\chi_{q}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)+1}{2}

Almost every useful result for

2.
An Analysis of the Dirichlet Series of
\chi_{q}

As stated in the introduction, everything we will do here will end

\chi_{q}
.
First, let us examine the abscissa of absolute convergence. To do

\chi_{q}

Proposition 1
.

The summatory functions of
\chi_{q}

and
M_{q}
satisfy:

(2.1)

\sum_{n=0}^{2^{N}-1}\chi_{q}\left(n\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{N}{4}2^{N}&\textrm{if }q=3\\
2^{N}\frac{\left(\frac{q+1}{4}\right)^{N}-1}{q-3}&\textrm{if }q\geq 5\end{cases}

(2.2)

\sum_{n=0}^{2^{N}-1}M_{q}\left(n\right)=\frac{q}{q-1}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{N}-\frac{1}{q-1}

Proof: We use an iterative-recursive method based on the functional
equations of
\chi_{q}
and
M_{q}
. This method is fundamental,
and will be used in varying guises throughout this series of papers.

First, define:

(2.3)

S_{q}\left(N\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{N}-1}\chi_{q}\left(n\right)

We then split the index of summation modulo
2
and apply
\chi_{q}
’s
functional equations:

\displaystyle S_{q}\left(N\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{2^{N-1}-1}\left(\chi_{q}\left(2n\right)+\chi_{q}\left(2n+1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{2^{N-1}-1}\left(\frac{\chi_{q}\left(n\right)}{2}+\frac{q\chi_{q}\left(n\right)+1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{2^{N-1}-1}\frac{1}{2}+\frac{q+1}{2}\underbrace{\sum_{n=0}^{2^{N-1}-1}\chi_{q}\left(n\right)}_{S_{q}\left(N-1\right)}

This gives us the recursion relation:

(2.4)

S_{q}\left(N\right)=2^{N-2}+\frac{q+1}{2}S_{q}\left(N-1\right)

Nesting this gives:

\displaystyle S_{q}\left(N\right)

\displaystyle=2^{N-2}+\frac{q+1}{2}S_{q}\left(N-1\right)

\displaystyle\left(\textrm{Use (\ref{eq:First recursion relation}) for }S_{q}\left(N-1\right)\right);

\displaystyle=2^{N-2}+2^{N-3}\frac{q+1}{2}+\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{2}S_{q}\left(N-2\right)

\displaystyle\left(\textrm{Use (\ref{eq:First recursion relation}) for }S_{q}\left(N-2\right)\right);

\displaystyle=2^{N-2}+2^{N-3}\frac{q+1}{2}+2^{N-4}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{3}S_{q}\left(N-3\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{N}S_{q}\left(0\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}2^{N-2-k}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{k}

Since:

(2.5)

S_{q}\left(0\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{2^{0}-1}\chi_{q}\left(n\right)=\chi_{q}\left(0\right)=0

we conclude that:

(2.6)

S_{q}\left(N\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}2^{N-2-k}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{k}=2^{N-2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{q+1}{4}\right)^{k}=\begin{cases}\frac{N}{4}2^{N}&\textrm{if }q=3\\
2^{N}\frac{\left(\frac{q+1}{4}\right)^{N}-1}{q-3}&\textrm{if }q\geq 5\end{cases}

Now, let:

(2.7)

S_{q}\left(N\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{N}-1}M_{q}\left(n\right)

Then, applying the same method as above, using the functional equations:

(2.8)

\displaystyle M_{q}\left(2n\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{q}\left(n\right)}{2},\textrm{ if }n\geq 1

(2.9)

\displaystyle M_{q}\left(2n+1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{qM_{q}\left(n\right)}{2},\textrm{ if }n\geq 0

we obtain:

\displaystyle S_{q}\left(N\right)

\displaystyle=1+\overbrace{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{2^{N-1}-1}M_{q}\left(n\right)}^{\textrm{evens}}+\overbrace{\frac{q}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{N-1}-1}qM_{q}\left(n\right)}^{\textrm{odds}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{1}{2}\left(S_{q}\left(N-1\right)-M_{q}\left(0\right)\right)+\frac{q}{2}S_{q}\left(N-1\right)

\displaystyle\left(M_{q}\left(0\right)=1\right);

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{q+1}{2}S_{q}\left(N-1\right)

Nesting yields:

(2.10)

S_{q}\left(N\right)=\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{N}S_{q}\left(0\right)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{n}

Here:

(2.11)

S_{q}\left(0\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{2^{0}-1}M_{q}\left(n\right)=M_{q}\left(0\right)=1

and so, we get:

(2.12)

For clarity’s sake, we record the following elementary
estimate on partial sums of Dirichlet series in terms of the Big-O
behavior of the coefficients’ summatory function.

Proposition 2
.

Let
c
be an integer

\geq 2
, let
a\left(n\right)
be a non-negative real-valued function,
and suppose that:

(2.13)

\sum_{n=0}^{c^{N}-1}a\left(n\right)\ll b^{N}\textrm{ as }N\rightarrow\infty

for some real

. Then:

(2.14)

\left|\sum_{n=0}^{c^{N}-1}\frac{a\left(n\right)}{\left(n+1\right)^{s}}\right|\ll\left(\frac{b}{c^{\sigma}}\right)^{N}\textrm{ as }N\rightarrow\infty

where
s=\sigma+it
. Consequently, the Dirichlet series converges
absolutely for
\textrm{Re}\left(s\right)>\log_{c}b
.

Proof: Using
Abel’s summation formula
, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{c^{N}-1}\frac{a\left(n\right)}{\left(n+1\right)^{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c^{Ns}}\sum_{n=0}^{c^{N}-1}a\left(n\right)+s\int_{1}^{c^{N}}\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor-1}a\left(n\right)}{x^{s+1}}dx

\displaystyle\left(c^{y}=x;c^{y}\ln c=dx\right);

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c^{Ns}}\sum_{n=0}^{c^{N}-1}a\left(n\right)+s\ln c\int_{0}^{N}\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\left\lfloor c^{y}\right\rfloor-1}a\left(n\right)}{c^{sy}}dy

Thus, for
\textrm{Re}\left(s\right)=\sigma
:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{N}\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\left\lfloor c^{y}\right\rfloor-1}a\left(n\right)}{c^{sy}}dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{N}\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\left\lfloor c^{y}\right\rfloor-1}a\left(n\right)}{c^{\sigma y}}dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\int_{k}^{k+1}\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\left\lfloor c^{y}\right\rfloor-1}a\left(n\right)}{c^{\sigma y}}dy

\displaystyle\left(a\left(n\right)\geq 0\right);

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\int_{k}^{k+1}\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{c^{k+1}-1}a\left(n\right)}{c^{\sigma y}}dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{2^{k+1}-1}a\left(n\right)\right)\frac{c^{-\sigma k}-c^{-\sigma\left(k+1\right)}}{\sigma\ln c}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}b^{k+1}\frac{c^{-\sigma k}-c^{-\sigma\left(k+1\right)}}{\sigma\ln c}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(b/c^{\sigma}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\ll\left(b/c^{\sigma}\right)^{N}

So:

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=0}^{c^{N}-1}\frac{a\left(n\right)}{\left(n+1\right)^{s}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c^{N\sigma}}\sum_{n=0}^{c^{N}-1}a\left(n\right)+\sigma\ln c\int_{0}^{N}\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\left\lfloor c^{y}\right\rfloor-1}a\left(n\right)}{c^{ys}}dy

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{c^{N\sigma}}b^{N}+\left(b/c^{\sigma}\right)^{N}

\displaystyle\ll\left(b/c^{\sigma}\right)^{N}

Q.E.D.

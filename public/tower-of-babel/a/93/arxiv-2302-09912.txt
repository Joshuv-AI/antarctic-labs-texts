Seiberg–Witten differentials on the Hitchin base
Ugo Bruzzo ¶§♯⋆ and Peter Dalakov †‡

Abstract
. In this note we describe explicitly, in terms of Lie theory and cameral data, the covariant (Gauss–Manin) derivative of the Seiberg–Witten differential defined on the weight-one variation of Hodge structures that exists on a Zariski open subset of the base of the Hitchin fibration.

1.
Introduction

The base of the Hitchin integrable system (
[
Hit87
]
) supports a family of cameral curves, and, as a consequence,
carries various Hodge-theoretic and differential-geometric structures (
[
DM96
]
,
[
DM
]
,
[
Mar
]
).
In particular, the Zariski open subset of the base,
corresponding to smooth cameral curves with generic ramification carries a weight-one variation of Hodge structures (VHS) with a Seiberg–Witten differential.
Our goal in this note is to describe the covariant (Gauss–Manin) derivative of the Seiberg–Witten differential explicitly in terms of Lie theory and
cameral data.

We recall now the main ingredients and constructions, starting with the Hodge-theoretic ones.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a complex manifold.
Recall that a polarised

\mathbb{R}
-VHS of weight
w\in\mathbb{Z}
on
\mathcal{B}
consists of
data
(\mathcal{V},\nabla,\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{R}},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet},S)
, where:

•

\mathcal{V}
is a holomorphic vector bundle on
\mathcal{B}

•

\nabla:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{B}}
is a flat (holomorphic) connection, called
the Gauss–Manin connection

•

\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
is a real,
\nabla
-flat subbundle, satisfying
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}
, called
real structure

•

\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is a decreasing
filtration
of
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{F}^{0}
,
the Hodge filtration

•

S:\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{V}\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}}
is a non-degenerate,
(-1)^{w}
-symmetric,
\nabla
-flat pairing,
\mathbb{R}
-valued on
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{R}}
, called
polarisation

such that

(1)

\nabla(\mathcal{F}^{p})\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{p-1}\otimes\Omega_{\mathcal{B}}^{1}

Griffiths transversality

(2)

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{F}^{p}\oplus\overline{\mathcal{F}^{w+1-p}}

Hodge structure
,

or, in terms of the Hodge bundles
\mathcal{H}^{p,w-p}:=\mathcal{F}^{p}\cap\overline{\mathcal{F}^{w-p}}
,

(2’)

\mathcal{V}=\bigoplus_{p}\mathcal{H}^{p,w-p}

(3)

S(\mathcal{F}^{p},\mathcal{F}^{w+1-p})=0

(4)

i^{2p-w}S(v,\overline{v})>0
for
v\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H}^{p,w-p})
,
v\neq 0
.

The notions of polarised
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS or
\mathbb{Q}
-VHS are introduced analogously, by replacing
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{R}}
with appropriate locally constant
sheaves
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
or
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Z}
- or
\mathbb{Q}
-modules, respectively.

The prototypical example is that of a geometric VHS, i.e., one arising from a family of compact Kähler (e.g., projective) manifolds.

By Griffiths Transversality,
\nabla
induces an
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-module homomorphism

\mathcal{F}^{p}/\mathcal{F}^{p+1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}^{p-1}/\mathcal{F}^{p}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{B}}

and hence, taking a direct sum over the different
p
, an
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-module homomorphism

\theta=[\nabla]:\bigoplus_{p}\mathcal{F}^{p}/\mathcal{F}^{p+1}\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{p}\mathcal{F}^{p}/\mathcal{F}^{p+1}\right)\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{B}},

which satisfies

\theta\wedge\theta=0
.

The pair

\left(E=\bigoplus_{p}\mathcal{F}^{p}/\mathcal{F}^{p+1},\theta\right)

is an example of a
Higgs bundle
on
\mathcal{B}
. This example played an important rôle in Carlos Simpson’s
study of Higgs bundles on higher-dimensional varieties (
[
Sim92
]
,
[
Sim88
]
).

Consider a polarised
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS
(\mathcal{V},\nabla,\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet},S,\ldots)
of weight
w=1
. An

abstract Seiberg–Witten differential
on it is a section
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{V}^{1})
, for which the
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}
-module homomorphism

T_{\mathcal{B}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{0},\ v\longmapsto\nabla_{v}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}

factors through an
isomorphism

(1)

T_{\mathcal{B}}\simeq\mathcal{V}^{1}.

Given such data, we obtain a refinement of the weight-1 filtration

\mathcal{V}^{1}\subseteq\mathcal{V}^{0}

to a weight-3 filtration

\underbrace{\mathcal{F}^{3}}_{=\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}}\subseteq\underbrace{\ \mathcal{F}^{2}\ }_{\mathcal{V}^{1}}\subseteq\underbrace{\mathcal{F}^{1}}_{=(\mathcal{F}^{3})^{\perp}}\subseteq\underbrace{\mathcal{F}^{0}}_{\mathcal{V}^{0}}

For links to projective special Kähler geometry (“
N=2
supergravity”) and weight-3 VHS, satisfying the Calabi–Yau condition,
one can check
[
HHP10
]
[§4, §8.3].

Furthermore, given such data, there is an associated fibration of complex tori
\mathcal{J}:=\mathcal{V}/(\mathcal{V}^{1}+\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathcal{B}
,
whose vertical bundle is
\textrm{Vert}=\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{1}
. The polarisation
S
gives rise to an isomorphism

\textrm{Vert}\simeq(\mathcal{V}^{1})^{\vee}
, and hence
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}
induces, by composition with the dual of its defining isomorphism
T_{\mathcal{B}}\simeq\mathcal{V}^{1}
,
an isomorphism
i_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}:\textrm{Vert}\to T^{\vee}_{\mathcal{B}}
. Such an isomorphism is also induced by a choice of symplectic form on
\mathcal{J}
.
There is unique symplectic form
\omega_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
on
\mathcal{J}
, which induces
i_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and such that the
0
-section is Lagrangian.

We next recall the construction of the family of cameral covers over the Hitchin base, and introduce a weight-1 VHS with a Seiberg–Witten differential
on it.

First, we fix the following data:

•

A simple complex Lie group
G

Two explicit examples of invariant polynomials – for

-bundle with fibre

.

The above isomorphism can also be considered from an integrable systems viewpoint.
Indeed, consider the universal family of generic cameral curves

The relative Prym fibration

The isomorphism (

to a vector field

Let

Theorem A
.

Once the
main and additional data are chosen, the isomorphism (
5
)
\nabla^{GM}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}

maps
{\bf g}\in T_{b}\mathscr{B}=H^{0}(X,\bigoplus_{i}K_{X}^{d_{i}})
to the section

\left(\nabla^{GM}_{\bf g}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}\right)_{b}=-\left.\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left(\iota(D{\bf I})^{-1}\cdot\pi^{\ast}{\bf g}\right)_{i}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}}\lambda_{i}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}}=-\left.\iota(D{\bf I})^{-1}\cdot\pi^{\ast}{\bf g}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}}.

In particular, for
l=2
we have that

\left(\nabla^{GM}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}\right)_{b}:\ H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{d_{1}}\oplus K_{X}^{d_{2}})\longrightarrow H^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{b},K_{\widetilde{X}_{b}}^{\oplus 2})^{W}

sends

g_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}
to

(7)

\left(\nabla^{GM}_{\bf g}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}\right)_{b}=-\left.\left(\frac{\begin{vmatrix}\pi^{*}g_{1}&\iota\partial_{2}I_{1}\\
\pi^{*}g_{2}&\iota\partial_{2}I_{2}\\
\end{vmatrix}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}\det\iota D{\bf I}}\lambda_{1}+\frac{\begin{vmatrix}\iota\partial_{1}I_{1}&\pi^{*}g_{1}\\
\iota\partial_{1}I_{2}&\pi^{*}g_{2}\\
\end{vmatrix}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}\det\iota D{\bf I}}\lambda_{2}\right)\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}}=-\frac{1}{\det\iota D{\bf I}}\left.\begin{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}\pi^{*}g_{1}&\iota\partial_{2}I_{1}\\
\pi^{*}g_{2}&\iota\partial_{2}I_{2}\\
\end{vmatrix}\\
\\
\begin{vmatrix}\iota\partial_{1}I_{1}&\pi^{*}g_{1}\\
\iota\partial_{1}I_{2}&\pi^{*}g_{2}\\
\end{vmatrix}\end{bmatrix}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}}.

cameral cover, and the vector bundle
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{B}}\simeq R^{1}p_{*}^{W}(\mathfrak{t}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{B}})
.
The relative holomorphic Poincaré Lemma gives a
quasi-isomorphism
p^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{B}}\simeq_{quis}\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathscr{B}}
,
leading to
\mathcal{V}\simeq R^{1}p_{*}^{W}(\mathfrak{t}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathscr{B}})
.

The Hodge bundles, as for geometric VHS, are determined by the naive filtration of
\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathscr{B}}
,
see
[
Voi07a
]
[§10.2] and
[
Voi07b
]
[§5.1]. In our case of weight one,

\Omega^{\bullet\geq 1}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathscr{B}}[-1]\subseteq\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathscr{B}}
determines a subbundle

\mathcal{V}^{1}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
, as
R^{1}p_{*}^{W}(\mathfrak{t}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Omega^{\bullet\geq 1}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathscr{B}}[-1])\simeq R^{0}p_{*}^{W}(\mathfrak{t}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathscr{B}})
.

The Gauss–Manin connection
\nabla^{GM}:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathscr{B}}
can be defined in either topological
or holomorphic terms.
The topological description relies on Ehresmann’s theorem, i.e., on the
C^{\infty}
-local triviality of

p:\mathcal{X}\to\mathscr{B}
. In this case, the homotopy-invariance of de Rham cohomology implies that

\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a locally constant sheaf and
\nabla^{GM}
can be described by a Cartan–Lie formula.
For geometric VHS this is described, e.g., in
[
Voi07a
]
[§8.2].

The holomorphic description of
\nabla^{GM}
is discussed in
[
HHP10
]
[§8], following

[
KO68
]
, see also
[
Del71
]
and
[
Voi07b
]
[§5.1].
The Koszul–Leray
filtration on
\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{X}}
gives rise to a spectral sequence, for which

(E_{1}^{\bullet,0},d_{1})
is identified with
(\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathscr{B}}(\mathcal{V}),\nabla^{GM})
.

One has the following result.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
HHP10
]
, Proposition 8.2)
.

The isomorphisms
\nabla^{GM}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}
and
\beta
(
25
) coincide. That is,

\nabla^{GM}_{\xi}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}=\beta(\xi),\

for all tangent vectors
\xi\in T_{b}\mathscr{B}
and all
b\in\mathscr{B}
.

The result is proved by an explicit hypercohomology calculation, using the Čech resolution
of the relative de Rham complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathscr{B}},d)
.

4.
Proof of Theorem A

We now turn to the proof of our main result,
Theorem A
.
Recall that in the statement of the theorem we use the algebra homomorphism
\iota
from Equation (
22
),
so
\iota(D{\bf I})^{-1}
is a global meromorphic section of
\underline{End}\left(\mathbb{C}^{l}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\pi^{\ast}K_{X}^{k}\right)

with poles along the zeros of
\det\iota D{\bf I}
.
That is,
the homogeneous polynomials
\partial_{i}I_{j}\in\textrm{Sym}^{d_{j}-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{\vee})
are considered as global sections of
\pi_{b}^{\ast}K_{X}^{d_{j}-1}
, or, after
restriction to
\widetilde{X}_{b}
, as sections of
p_{b}^{\ast}K_{X}^{d_{j}-1}
.

Using Cramer’s formula and the fact that
\iota
is an algebra homomorphism, we can rewrite the right side of (
6
) as
a linear combination of (restrictions of)
\lambda_{i}
with coefficients of the kind

\left.\frac{\det\left[\iota\partial_{1}{\bf I},\ldots,p_{b}^{\ast}{\bf g},\ldots,\iota\partial_{l}{\bf I}\right]}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}\det\iota D{\bf I}}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}}\in\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{X}_{b}),

i.e., global meromorphic functions on
\widetilde{X}_{b}
, since both the numerator and the denominator belong to

H^{0}\left(\widetilde{X}_{b},p_{b}^{\ast}K_{X}^{\sum_{i}d_{i}-l+1}\right)
.

Now we prove Theorem A.
Let us fix
{\bf g}\in T_{b}\mathcal{B}=H^{0}(X,\bigoplus_{i}K_{X}^{d_{i}})
and denote by
{\bf s}\in H^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{b},\mathfrak{t}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}K_{\widetilde{X}_{b}})
the image of
{\bf g}
under
the isomorphism (
5
).
Let us also denote by
\widetilde{{\bf s}}
the section from the right hand side of Equation (
6
), i.e.,

\widetilde{{\bf s}}=-\left.\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left(\iota(D{\bf I})^{-1}\cdot\pi^{\ast}{\bf g}\right)_{i}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}}\lambda_{i}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}}.

This is a meromorphic section of
\mathfrak{t}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}K_{\widetilde{X}_{b}}
with poles at most along the ramification of
p_{b}:\widetilde{X}_{b}\to X
.
We are going to prove that
{\bf s}=\widetilde{{\bf s}}
. We use Theorem
3.1
and the representation of the
isomorphism
\beta
from Equation (
25
) is a composition of two maps.

As a first step, we show that

(27)

\left.{\bf s}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}\backslash\textrm{Ram}(p_{b})}=\left.\widetilde{{\bf s}}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}\backslash\textrm{Ram}(p_{b})}

For that we restrict the cameral cover to the complements of the ramification and branch divisors

p_{b}:\widetilde{X}_{b}\backslash\textrm{Ram}(p_{b})\longrightarrow X\backslash\textrm{Bra}(p_{b})

and choose
U\subseteq X\backslash\textrm{Bra}(p_{b})
,
biholomorphic to an open disk (via
\psi:U\to\Delta
).
In this case,
\widetilde{X}_{U}\subseteq\widetilde{X}\cap\left(\det\iota D{\bf I}\neq 0\right)
has
|W|
(analytic) connected components,
each isomorphic to
U
, labelled by the different
Weyl chambers

\widetilde{X}_{U}=\widetilde{X}_{U}^{1}\coprod\ldots\coprod\widetilde{X}_{U}^{|W|}.

We choose (an analytic) local coordinate
z
on
U

and use
z
(i.e., its pullback
p_{b}^{\ast}z
) as a coordinate on
\widetilde{X}_{U}\subseteq\widetilde{X}_{b}\backslash\textrm{Ram}(p_{b})
.

Then, setting
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
for the coordinate vector of
p_{b}^{\ast}{\bf g}
,

(\phi^{-1})^{\ast}\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\begin{pmatrix}-\boldsymbol{\beta}^{\prime}&\left.D{\bf I}\right|_{\phi(\widetilde{X}_{U})}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\left.(D{\bf I})\right|^{-1}_{\phi(\widetilde{X}_{U})}((\phi^{-1})^{\ast}\boldsymbol{\gamma})\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}^{\oplus l}(\Delta),

i.e., we obtain a lift
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
of
\boldsymbol{\gamma}

(28)

(\phi^{-1})^{\ast}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}(\left.(D{\bf I})^{-1}\right|_{\phi(\widetilde{X}_{U})}(\phi^{-1})^{\ast}\boldsymbol{\gamma})_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha_{i}}\in\Gamma(\phi(\widetilde{X}_{U}),\left.(\phi^{-1})^{*}T_{M}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{U}}).

Note that the expression for
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
is well-defined on
\widetilde{X}_{U}
: away from ramification,
we can solve locally-analytically for

\alpha_{i}
in terms of
z
, so
(D{\bf I})^{-1}
, when restricted to a connected component of
\phi(\widetilde{X}_{U})
, is actually a
section of
\textrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{l})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}^{an}_{\Delta}
.

Then, using the lift
\widetilde{{\bf g}}
from Equation (
28
), we obtain

\left.\widetilde{{\bf g}}\lrcorner\omega\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{U}}=-\phi^{\ast}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(\left.(D{\bf I})\right|_{\phi(\widetilde{X}_{U})}^{-1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right)_{i}\otimes e_{i}\otimes[dz]\right)\\
-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left.\frac{\phi^{\ast}\left(\left.(D{\bf I})\right|_{\phi(\widetilde{X}_{U})}^{-1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right)_{i}}{\alpha_{i}}\lambda_{i}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{U}},

as
(\phi^{-1})^{\ast}\lambda_{i}=\alpha_{i}[dz]\otimes e_{i}
.
We write
[dz]
rather than
dz
since the cotangent sheaf of
\widetilde{X}_{U}
is a quotient of
\left.\Omega^{1}_{M_{U}}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{U}}
.
This is precisely the expression for
\widetilde{{\bf s}}
from Equation (
6
), written locally.

Having shown (
27
), we now note that the sheaf of meromorphic sections of a holomorphic vector bundle on a smooth curve is trivial (see e.g.
[
Gun67
]
, p.76; see also
[
Var
]
, Lemma 31.25.3). As two meromorphic functions that coincide away from a finite set of points are equal, equation (
27
) shows that
{\bf s}=\widetilde{{\bf s}}
.
Since the two sections
{\bf s}
and
\widetilde{{\bf s}}
are equal, and
{\bf s}
is known to be
W
-invariant, so is
\widetilde{{\bf s}}
.
∎

5.
Examples

5.1.
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})

For completeness, we start with the simplest case of

G=SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. The Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
of diagonal traceless
2\times 2
matrices is identified with
\mathbb{C}
via

\alpha(A)=A_{11}
and we
take the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-invariant polynomial
I=\det
, i.e.,
I(\alpha)=-\alpha^{2}
. The cameral (and spectral) curve

\widetilde{X}_{b}\subseteq\textrm{tot }K^{2}_{X}
has equation
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{2}=\pi^{\ast}b
, for
b\in\mathcal{B}=H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{2})
.
Then, for generic
b
, the isomorphism (
5
)

\textstyle{\left(\nabla^{GM}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}\right)_{b}:}
\textstyle{T_{b}\mathcal{B}=H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{b},K_{\widetilde{X}_{b}})^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{-}}

is given by

(29)

{\bf g}\longmapsto\left(\nabla^{GM}_{\bf g}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}\right)_{b}=\left.\frac{\pi^{\ast}{\bf g}}{2\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{2}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}}=-\left.\frac{\pi^{\ast}{\bf g}}{2\sigma}\lambda\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}}=\left.\frac{\pi^{\ast}{\bf g}}{2\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}}

where
\sigma=-\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{2}\in H^{0}(M,\pi^{\ast}K_{X}^{2})
is the tautological section (
10
)
of
{\bf U}=K_{X}^{2}
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}

is the Liouville (Seiberg–Witten) form.

5.2.
SL_{3}(\mathbb{C})

Consider
G=SL_{3}(\mathbb{C})
, with the standard choices of Borel (upper-triangular) and Cartan (diagonal) subgroups.
Here
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
is the subspace of diagonal traceless

3\times 3
matrices
and
W=S_{3}
.
If we set
\alpha_{1}(A)=A_{11}-A_{22}
,
\alpha_{2}(A)=A_{22}-A_{33}
(two simple positive roots), then we can choose the invariant polynomials to be

(30)

\left|\begin{array}[]{l}I_{1}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}\\
I_{2}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=-2\alpha_{1}^{3}-3\alpha_{1}^{2}\alpha_{2}+3\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}^{2}+2\alpha_{2}^{3}.\\
\end{array}\right.

In fact, these are
I_{1}(A)=-3(A_{11}A_{22}+A_{11}A_{33}+A_{22}A_{33})
and
I_{2}(A)=-27\det A
.

Consequently, the cameral curve
\widetilde{X}_{b}
, corresponding to a generic section
b=(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathcal{B}=H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{2})\oplus H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{3})

is cut out in
M=\textrm{tot }(K_{X}^{2}\oplus K_{X}^{3})
by the equations

(31)

\left|\begin{array}[]{l}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{2}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{2}=\pi^{\ast}b_{1}\\
-2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{3}-3\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}+3\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{2}+2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{3}=\pi^{\ast}b_{2}\\
\end{array}\right.

and

\left(\nabla^{GM}_{\bf g}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}\right)_{b}=\left.\frac{1}{\det\iota D{\bf I}}\begin{bmatrix}3\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{2}-6\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}-6\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{2}&2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}\\
-6\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{2}-6\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}+3\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{2}&-2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}g_{1}\\
g_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}},

where

\det\iota D{\bf I}=27\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}).

5.3.
G_{2}

It is well-known (
[
Hum78
]
[p.103]) that the
G_{2}
root system can be embedded in the
B_{3}
root system – and that in fact,
this can be done in a way that simple roots
of the former are expressed as linear combinations of simple roots of the latter. An explicit description of such an embedding can be obtained by extending
the
calculations in
[
AvM80
]
[§4], but we do not need this now.
Using this embedding, we can take the
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}

to consist of diagonal matrices of the form

h=\textrm{diag}(-a-b,-a,-b,0,b,a,a+b)
,
for
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
. Two simple roots
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
are, e.g.,
b
and
a-b
, i.e.,

\alpha_{1}\left(h\right)=h_{55},\ \alpha_{2}\left(h\right)=h_{66}-h_{55}.

The six positive roots are then
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
,
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
,
2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
,
3\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
,
3\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}
.
The characteristic polynomial of
h\in\mathfrak{t}
is

\det(h-\lambda E_{7})=-\lambda^{7}+\lambda^{5}2I_{1}(h)-\lambda^{3}I_{1}^{2}(h)+\lambda I_{2}(h),

where, if
we use
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
as coordinates on
\mathfrak{t}
, we have for the invariants

(32)

\left|\begin{array}[]{l}I_{1}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=3\alpha_{1}^{2}+3\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}\\
I_{2}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=4\alpha_{1}^{6}+12\alpha_{1}^{5}\alpha_{2}+13\alpha_{1}^{4}\alpha^{2}_{2}+6\alpha_{1}^{3}\alpha_{2}^{3}+\alpha_{1}^{2}\alpha_{2}^{4}.\\
\end{array}\right.

The eigenvalues of a matrix from
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{7}
are
0,\pm\lambda_{1},\pm\lambda_{2},\pm\lambda_{3}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\lambda_{i}=0
. The two invariants are, respectively,
\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{3}^{2})

and
(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3})^{2}
.

Consequently, the cameral curve
\widetilde{X}_{b}
, corresponding to a generic section
(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathcal{B}=H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{2})\oplus H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{6})

is cut out in
M=\textrm{tot }(K_{X}^{2}\oplus K_{X}^{6})
by the equations

(33)

\left|\begin{array}[]{l}3\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{2}+3\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{2}=\pi^{\ast}b_{1}\\
4\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{6}+12\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{5}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}+13\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{4}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{2}_{2}+6\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{3}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{3}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{4}=\pi^{\ast}b_{2}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Now, identifying the adjoint quotient
\chi:\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{t}/W
with
{\bf I}=(I_{1},I_{2}):\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
, we obtain
that under the isomorphism from Theorem A a section
{\bf g}=(g_{1},g_{2})^{T}\in H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{2})\oplus H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{6})=T_{b}\mathscr{B}
is mapped to

\left(\nabla^{GM}_{\bf g}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{SW}\right)_{b}
, i.e.,

\left.\frac{1}{\det\iota D{\bf I}}\begin{bmatrix}-2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{2}(6\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{3}+13\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}+9\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{2}+2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{3})&3\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}+2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}\\
2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}(12\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{4}+30\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{3}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}+26\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}^{2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{2}+9\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{3}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}^{4})&-6\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}-3\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\pi^{\ast}g_{1}\\
\pi^{\ast}g_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}\right|_{\widetilde{X}_{b}},

where

(34)

\displaystyle\det\iota D{\bf I}=-2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2})(2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2})(3\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2})(3\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}+2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}).

We see that in all examples
\det D{\bf I}
is a constant multiple of the product of all positive roots. In fact, this follows from
a classical result of Steinberg
[
Ste60
]
. Hence
(\det D{\bf I})^{2}
is proportional to the discriminant
\mathfrak{D}
of
\mathfrak{g}
– the product of all
roots. Being
W
-invariant, the discriminant can be expressed as a polynomial in the generators of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}
– here,
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.
We sketch a possible way of obtaining this expression without too much brute force. Using the embedding
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}_{7}
,
we can identify
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
as
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}
, up to reordering
\lambda_{i}
’s. Consequently,

(35)

\begin{split}\mathfrak{D}&=\alpha_{1}^{2}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{2}(2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{2}\alpha_{2}^{2}(3\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{2}(3\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2})^{2}\\
&=\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}\lambda_{3}^{2}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})^{2}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3})^{2}.\end{split}

The product of the first three terms is
I_{2}
. The product of the last three terms is a polynomial of degree
6
, and
hence must be a linear combination of
I_{1}^{3}
and
I_{2}
, and so one checks
immediately that

\mathfrak{D}=I_{2}(4I_{1}^{3}-27I_{2}).

Consequently, the restriction of
\iota\mathfrak{D}
to the universal cameral cover
\mathcal{X}

is the pull-back of a section of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{B}}\otimes H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{12})
, namely,

\mathscr{B}\ni(b_{1},b_{2})\longmapsto b_{2}(4b_{1}^{3}-27b_{2}).

It is the Lie derivative of this section that enters the Balduzzi–Pantev formula and its
generalization (
[
Bal06
]
,
[
BD14
]
).

We refer the reader to the beautiful papers
[
KP94
]
,

[
Hit07
]
for additional details on the
G_{2}
-Hitchin system, including
Langlands duality and the description of Hitchin fibres.

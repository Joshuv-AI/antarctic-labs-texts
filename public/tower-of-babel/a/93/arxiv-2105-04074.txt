Abstract
. Through examples, we illustrate how to compute differential operators on a quotient of an affine semigroup ring by a radical monomial ideal, when working over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.



Introduction

In this paper, we provide illustrative examples and visualizations of some differential operators on the quotient of an affine semigroup ring by a radical monomial ideal.
These examples motivate our work in
[
BCK+21
]
.
For a finitely-generated algebra
R
over a field, let
D(R)
denote the ring of differential operators of
R
and use
\ast
to denote an action of a differential operator.
A foundational result in this area relates the ring of differential operators of an arbitrary quotient of a polynomial ring by an ideal
J
to differential operators on that polynomial ring and the idealizer of
J
.

Proposition 1.1
.

[
SS88
, Propostion 1.6]

Let
S
be the coordinate ring of a nonsingular affine variety over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
, and let
J
be an
S
-ideal. Then there is an isomorphism

(1.1)

D\left(\frac{S}{J}\right)\cong\frac{\mathbb{I}(J)}{JD(S)},

where
\mathbb{I}(J):=\{\delta\in D(R)\mid\delta\ast J\subset J\}
, which is called the
idealizer
of
J
.

The phrasing of
[
SS88
, Proposition 1.6]
given above differs from the original by making use of the equivalence of the conditions, with notation as above, of
\delta JD(S)\subset JD(S)
and
\delta\ast J\subset J

[
ST01
, Lemma 2.3.1]
.
Traves
[
Tra99
]
uses Proposition
1.1
to give concrete descriptions of rings of differential operators of
Stanley–Reisner rings, and Saito and Traves
[
ST01
]
use the same to compute rings of differential operators of affine semigroup rings.
For a non-regular affine semigroup ring
R_{A}
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
, Proposition
1.1
fails, even for a radical monomial ideal
J
in
R_{A}
.
However, the differential operators on
R_{A}
that induce maps on
R_{A}/J
are precisely those in
\mathbb{I}(J)
.
Further, there is an embedding of rings

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{I}(J)}{D(R_{A},J)}\hookrightarrow D\left(\frac{R_{A}}{J}\right),

so the description of
D(R_{A})
by Saito and Traves can still be used to compute this subring of
D(R_{A}/J)
.

One primary goal of this article is to visually illustrate the computation of
\mathbb{I}(J)/D(R_{A},J)
when
R_{A}
is an affine semigroup ring and
J
is a radical monomial ideal in
J
; in
[
BCK+21
]
, we provide an explicit formula for this computation.
In particular, the pictures we provide explain how to compute differential operators of the form

D(I,J):=\{\delta\in D(R)\mid\delta\ast I\subseteq J\},

where
I
and
J
are subsets of the ring
R
, and
\ast
denotes an action by a differential operator.
These sets were originally instrumental in both the work of Smith and Stafford
[
SS88
]
and of Musson
[
Mus89
, Section 1]
, and they are essential building blocks of our computations, as well.

Our second major goal in this paper is to compare
JD(R_{A})
and
D(R_{A},J)
for an affine semigroup ring
R_{A}
and radical monomial ideal
J
of
R_{A}
.
Towards this end, the first set of examples we consider consists of quotients of the coordinate rings of rational normal curves.
These quotients are all isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}[x,y]/\langle xy\rangle
,
which is handled in
[
Tra99
]
.
From the standpoint of comparing
JD(R_{A})
and
D(R_{A},J)
, what we will see is that

JD(R_{A})=D(R_{A},J)
for the rational normal curves of degrees
1
and
2
but fail to coincide in all degrees larger than
2
.
For rational normal curves of degree at most
2
,

J
is principally generated and so is
JD(R_{A})
.
In this case, it is straightforward to see (by definition) that
D(R_{A},J)
is also principal and is isomorphic to
JD(R_{A})
.
However, for degree
n>2
,
J
has
n-1
generators.
We will see that this number of generators greatly impacts
\mathbb{I}(J)/JD(R_{A})
but not
\mathbb{I}(J)/D(R_{A},J)
.

In
[
BCK+21
]
,
we consider cases where

(1.2)

JD(R_{A})=D(R_{A},J),

for the particular ideal
J
generated by all monomials corresponding to the interior of the semigroup, i.e.,
J=\omega_{R_{A}}
.
When
R_{A}
is Gorenstein and normal, (
1.2
) holds since
\omega_{R_{A}}
is principal. We show that the converse is also true; that is, (
1.2
) is equivalent to
R_{A}
being a Gorenstein ring.

To provide intuition beyond the two-dimensional case, we also offer a three-dimensional normal affine semigroup ring
R_{A}
for which we compute
\mathbb{I}(J)/D(R_{A},J)
for two choices of
J
.
Then, we return to the two-dimensional setting to consider a scored but not normal example, as well as a non-scored example, computing differential operators for quotients of both rings.

2.
Background and Notation

In this section, we fix notation and conventions to be assumed throughout the article. Although the results we discuss in this paper hold over any algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, we will use in this illustrated view the field of complex numbers for the sake of simplicity. Having fixed notation, we will then state and discuss
[
ST01
, Theorem 3.2.2]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a
k\times\ell
matrix with entries in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\mathbb{N}A
denote the semigroup in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
that is generated by the columns of
A
.
The
affine semigroup ring
determined by
A
is

R_{A}=\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}A]=\bigoplus_{{\bm{a}}\in\mathbb{N}A}\mathbb{C}\cdot{\bm{t}}^{\bm{a}},

where
{\bm{t}}^{\bm{a}}=t_{1}^{a_{1}}t_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots t_{k}^{a_{k}}
for
\bm{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{N}A
.
Throughout this article, we assume that the group generated by the columns of
A
is the full ambient lattice, so
\mathbb{Z}A=\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, and also that the real positive cone over
A
,
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A
, is pointed, meaning that it is strongly convex.

Definition 2.2
.

A semigroup
\mathbb{N}A
is
normal
if
\mathbb{N}A=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A\cap\mathbb{Z}A
.
A semigroup
\mathbb{N}A
is
scored
if the difference
(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A\cap\mathbb{Z}A)\setminus\mathbb{N}A
consists of a finite union of hyperplane sections of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A\cap\mathbb{Z}A
that are all parallel to facets of the cone
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A
.
An affine semigroup ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}A]
is said to be
normal
, or
scored
, if
\mathbb{N}A
is normal, or scored, respectively.
Note that normal semigroups are scored.

When we write a facet
\sigma
of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A
(or
A
or
\mathbb{N}A
),
we will always mean the integer points in the corresponding facet of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A
.
When
A
is normal, this is the same as the semigroup generated by the columns of
A
that lie in the corresponding facet of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A
.

Throughout, we use
\langle-\rangle
to indicate ideals in the commutative rings
R_{A}
or the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[\theta]=\mathbb{C}[\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k}]
.
It will be clear from the context if the ideals are in
R_{A}

or in
\mathbb{C}[\theta]
.

Notice that the
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded prime ideals in
\mathbb{R}_{A}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the faces of
A
(or
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A
), as a face
\tau
of
A
corresponds to the multigraded prime
R_{A}
-ideal

P_{\tau}:=\left\langle\bm{t}^{\bm{d}}\,\big|\,\bm{d}\in\mathbb{N}A\setminus\tau\right\rangle.

In this paper, we compute rings of the form
\mathbb{I}(J)/D(R_{A},J)
, where
J
is a radical monomial ideal in
R_{A}
; as such,
J
is always as intersection of primes of the form
P_{\tau}
, for various faces
\tau
of
A
.

We are mainly following the description
of Saito and Traves
[
ST01
]
,
although Musson and Van den Bergh describe the ring of differential operators of a toric ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}A]
first in
[
Mus87
]
,
[
Mus94
]
and
[
MVdB98
]
when viewed as a subring of the ring of differential operators of the Laurent polynomials, i.e.,
D(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}^{k}])=\mathbb{C}\{t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{k}^{\pm 1},\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{k}\}/\sim
,
where
\partial_{i}
denotes the differential operator
\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}

and
\sim
denotes the usual relations on the free associative algebra
\mathbb{C}\{t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{k}^{\pm 1},\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{k}\}
that describe the behavior of differential operators.
To explain
the differential operators of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}A]
as presented by Saito and Traves,
note first that
D(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}^{k}])
carries a
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-grading, where
e_{i}
denotes the
i
-th column of the identity matrix
I
and
\deg(t_{i})=e_{i}=-\deg(\partial_{i})
. Note also that if
\bm{a}_{i}
is a column of
A
, then
\deg(\bm{t}^{\bm{a}_{i}})=\bm{a}_{i}
.
Set
\theta_{j}=t_{j}\partial_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq k
, and
set

\Omega({\bm{d}}):=\{\bm{a}\in\mathbb{N}A\mid\bm{a}+{\bm{d}}\notin\mathbb{N}A\}=\mathbb{N}A\setminus(-{\bm{d}}+\mathbb{N}A).

We note also that for any
\bm{t}^{\bm{m}}\in R_{A}
and
f(\theta)\in\mathbb{C}[\theta]
,
f(\theta)\ast\bm{t}^{\bm{m}}=f(\bm{m})\bm{t}^{\bm{m}}
.
The
idealizer of
\Omega({\bm{d}})
is defined to be

\mathbb{I}(\Omega({\bm{d}})):=\langle f(\theta)\in\mathbb{C}[\theta]=\mathbb{C}[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\ldots,\theta_{k}]\mid f(\bm{a})=0\text{ for all }\bm{a}\in\Omega({\bm{d}})\rangle,

which is viewed as an ideal in the ring
\mathbb{C}[\theta]
, where
\theta_{i}=t_{i}\partial_{i}\in D(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}^{d}])
is of degree
\bm{0}
.
We will soon see that
\mathbb{I}(\Omega({\bm{d}}))
consists of
f(\theta)
such that
\bm{t}^{\bm{d}}f(\theta)\in D(R_{A})
.

To compute
\mathbb{I}(\Omega({\bm{d}}))
for a normal semigroup ring, consider a facet
\sigma
of
A
, recalling that by this we mean all lattice points on the corresponding facet of the cone
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A
.
The
primitive integral support function

h_{\sigma}
is a uniquely determined linear form on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that:

(1)

h_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A)\geq 0
,

(2)

h_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}\sigma)=0
, and

(3)

h_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{k})=\mathbb{Z}.

We write
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{m}
for the facets of
A
,
so that for the remainder of the paper, we set

h_{j}:=h_{j}(\theta)=h_{\sigma_{j}}(\theta).

For a non-negative integer
n
, we will use the following notation to denote this
descending factorial
:

\left(\alpha,n\right)!:=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n}(\alpha-i)=\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-n),

where
\alpha
is a function, which could be constant or already evaluated.
For example,

\left(h_{j},h_{j}({-\bm{d}})-1)\right)!=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{h_{j}({-\bm{d}})-1}(h_{j}-i)

will be a common expression throughout this article, as it is a factor in the generator of the idealizer
\mathbb{I}(\Omega(\bm{d}))
. To streamline our presentation, we make the convention that
\left(\alpha,n\right)!=1
for all
n<0
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
ST01
, Theorem 3.2.2]

If
R_{A}
is a pointed, normal affine semigroup ring with
\mathbb{Z}A=\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, then

\displaystyle D(R_{A})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{{\bm{d}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\bm{t}^{\bm{d}}\cdot\mathbb{I}(\Omega({\bm{d}})),

(2.1)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{{\bm{d}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\bm{t}^{\bm{d}}\cdot\left\langle\prod\limits_{h_{i}({\bm{d}})<0}\left(h_{i},h_{i}(-{\bm{d}})-1\right)!\right\rangle,

where the outer product runs over those
j=1,2,\dots,m
with
h_{j}({\bm{d}})<0
.

We now turn to an example that illustrates Theorem
2.3
, as well as our notation.

Example 2.4
.

Let
R=\mathbb{C}[s,st,st^{2},st^{3}]
, where
\deg(s)=1=\deg(t)
.
Let
\partial_{1}
and
\partial_{2}
denote the partial derivatives with respect to
s
and
t
, and let
\theta_{1}=s\partial_{1}
and
\theta_{2}=t\partial_{2}
.
By Theorem
2.3
,

D(R)_{\bm{d}}
, for some
\bm{d}=(d_{1},d_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, is generated by an element in the form of

s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot f(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
,
where
f(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
is a polynomial in
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
.

To understand the philosophy from
[
ST01
]
, fix
\bm{d}=(d_{1},d_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and consider a differential operator on

R=k[s,st,st^{2},st^{3}]
of the form
\delta=s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}f(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
. By definition,
\delta\ast(s^{m_{1}}t^{m_{2}})\in R

for any monomial
s^{m_{1}}t^{m_{2}}\in R
. We can check that

\delta\ast s^{m_{1}}t^{m_{2}}=f(\bm{m})s^{d_{1}+m_{1}}t^{d_{2}+m_{2}}.

In particular, if
s^{d_{1}+m_{1}}t^{d_{2}+m_{2}}\notin R
then we must have that
\delta\ast s^{m_{1}}t^{m_{2}}=0
so that
f(\bm{m})=0
.
In summary,
\delta\ast(s^{m_{1}}t^{m_{2}})
may be nonzero if and only if
s^{d_{1}+m_{1}}t^{d_{2}+m_{2}}\in R
.

These multidegrees
\bm{m}=(m_{1},m_{2})
where
\delta\ast s^{m_{1}}t^{m_{2}}
vanishes are exactly the points in
\Omega({\bm{d}})
. We illustrate examples of
\Omega({\bm{d}})
for specific

To explicitly compute the polynomial
f=f(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
for a fixed
\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we will
use the primitive integral support functions for the two facets of

, which are
h_{1}=\theta_{2}
and
h_{2}=3\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}

Specifically,
\mathbb{I}(\Omega(-1,2))

f(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=h_{2}(h_{2}-1)(h_{2}-2)(h_{2}-3)(h_{2}-4)=(h_{2},4)!,

For
\bm{d}=(-1,2)

h_{2}(-\bm{d})=5

As shown in Example
2.4
,
\mathbb{I}(\Omega({\bm{d}}))
is an ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\theta]
, and any polynomial
f(\theta)\in\mathbb{C}[\theta]
has multidegree

; for any monomial
{\bm{t}}^{\bm{m}}\in R_{A}
,

f(\theta)\ast{\bm{t}}^{\bm{m}}=f(\bm{m})\bm{t}^{\bm{m}}
, which belongs to
R_{A}
.
So to determine if the
\bm{d}
-th graded piece of
D(R_{A})
applied to a monomial lands in
R_{A}
or an
R_{A}
-ideal

, it is enough to test the membership of any monomial
\bm{t}^{\bm{d}+\bm{m}}
and then adjust the

Membership failure for
R_{A}
only occurs if
\bm{m}
lies in
\mathbb{N}A
but outside of the cone
-\bm{d}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A

, failure is more likely to occur. Since the monomials whose exponents lie on a face of

is a prime ideal associated to a face that contains

In either case, there are only finitely many potential linear forms to be determined, and they are of the form

where

is the primary support function of a facet

We include here the ring of differential operators of an ordinary double point

Example 2.5
.

The ring of differential operators on
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
is the Weyl algebra
W=\mathbb{C}\{x,y,\partial_{x},\partial_{y}\}
, which is the free associative algebra generated by
x,y,\partial_{x},\partial_{y}
, subject to the relations:

\{xy-yx,\partial_{x}\partial_{y}-\partial_{y}\partial_{x},\partial_{x}y-y\partial_{x},\partial_{y}x-x\partial_{y},\partial_{x}x-x\partial_{x}+1,\partial_{y}y-y\partial_{y}+1\}.

The Weyl algebra
W
is a graded ring with
\deg(x)=\deg(y)=1=-\deg(\partial_{x})=-\deg(\partial_{y})
.

For the ordinary double point ring
R=\mathbb{C}[x,y]/\langle xy\rangle
,
Traves shows in
[
Tra99
, Theorem 3.5]
that the idealizer of
\langle xy\rangle
in
W
is also graded and generated by

\{1,x^{m},y^{n},x^{m}y^{n},x^{m}\partial_{x}^{i},y^{n}\partial_{y}^{j},x^{m}y^{n}\partial_{x}^{i}\partial_{y}^{j}\mid i\geq m>0,j\geq n>0\}.

Notice in particular that
x^{m}\partial_{x}^{m}
and
y^{n}\partial_{y}^{n}
both have degree 0.
Setting
\theta_{x}=x\partial_{x}
and
\theta_{y}=y\partial_{y}
,
from the Weyl algebra relations, it follows that

\partial_{x}^{i}=x^{-i}\cdot\prod\limits_{\ell=0}^{i-1}(\theta_{x}-\ell)=x^{-i}\left(\theta_{x},i-1\right)!\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{y}^{j}=y^{-j}\cdot\prod\limits_{\ell=0}^{j-1}(\theta_{y}-\ell)=\left(\theta_{y},j-1\right)!.

Hence
x^{m}\partial_{x}^{i}=x^{m-i}\cdot\left(\theta_{x},i-1\right)!
and
y^{n}\partial_{y}^{j}=y^{n-j}\cdot\left(\theta_{y},j-1\right)!
,
and
x^{m}y^{n}\partial_{x}^{i}\partial_{y}^{j}
has multidegree
(m-i,n-j)
in
W
.
In fact,

W=\bigoplus\limits_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}x^{m}y^{n}\cdot\langle\left(\theta_{x},m-1\right)!\left(\theta_{y},n-1\right)!\rangle,

which is a presentation of the Weyl algebra using the Saito–Traves approach from Theorem
2.3
.
From this viewpoint, Traves showed that

\mathbb{I}(\langle xy\rangle)_{(m,n)}=\begin{cases}x^{m}y^{n}\cdot\mathbb{C}[\theta_{x},\theta_{y}]&\text{ if }m,n\geq 0,\\
x^{m}y^{n}\cdot\langle\left(\theta_{x},-m\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }m<0,n\geq 0,\\
x^{m}y^{n}\cdot\langle\left(\theta_{y},-n\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }n<0,m\geq 0,\\
x^{m}y^{n}\cdot\langle\left(\theta_{x},-m\right)!\left(\theta_{y},-n\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }m,n<0.\end{cases}

Further,
\langle xy\rangle W
can be expressed as a multigraded
W
-ideal that is contained in
\mathbb{I}(\langle xy\rangle)
as follows:

\langle xy\rangle W_{(m,n)}=\begin{cases}x^{m}y^{n}\cdot\mathbb{C}[\theta_{x},\theta_{y}]&\text{ if }m,n>0,\\
x^{m}y^{n}\cdot\langle\left(\theta_{x},-m\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }m\leq 0,n>0,\\
x^{m}y^{n}\cdot\langle\left(\theta_{y},-n\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }n\leq 0,m>0,\\
x^{m}y^{n}\cdot\langle\left(\theta_{x},-m\right)!\left(\theta_{y},-n\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }m,n\leq 0.\\
\end{cases}

Now, applying
Proposition
1.1
yields a computation for the ring of differential operators for the ordinary double point
R=\mathbb{C}[x,y]/\langle xy\rangle
:

(2.2)

D\left(\frac{\mathbb{C}[x,y]}{\langle xy\rangle}\right)_{(m,n)}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }mn\neq 0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{C}[\theta_{x},\theta_{y}]}{\langle\theta_{x}\theta_{y}\rangle}&\text{ if }m=n=0,\\
x^{m}\cdot\displaystyle\frac{\langle\left(\theta_{x},-m\right)!\rangle}{\langle\left(\theta_{x},-m\right)!\theta_{y}\rangle}&\text{ if }m\neq 0,n=0,\\
y^{n}\cdot\displaystyle\frac{\langle\left(\theta_{y},-n\right)!\rangle}{\langle\left(\theta_{y},-m\right)!\theta_{x}\rangle}&\text{ if }n\neq 0,m=0.\\
\end{cases}

We next fix some notation to be used in the remainder of this paper.
Whenever the dimension of the semigroup ring under consideration is

as our variables, we will use
s,t
.
Further, when considering subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}

7.
Non-normal examples

Thus far, we have restricted our attention to normal semigroup rings.
However, Saito and Traves determined the ring of differential operators for all saturated affine semigroup rings
in
[
ST01
, Theorem 3.3.1]
(see also
[
ST04
, Theorem 2.1]
),
and we can use this work to compute
\mathbb{I}(J)
,
D(R_{A},J)
and
\mathbb{I}(J)/D(R_{A},J)
for non-normal
R_{A}
and a graded radical
R_{A}
-ideal
J
.
We broaden slightly from normal to
scored
semigroup rings, see Definition
2.2
.
We include two examples; one is scored and the other is not.
However, the two quotient rings modulo the interior ideals are isomorphic and we will see that the expressions for
\mathbb{I}(J)/D(R_{A},J)
in both rings are isomorphic.

Example 7.1
.

Consider the matrix

A=\begin{bmatrix}2&3&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
,
which is associated to the semigroup ring
R_{A}=\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}A]=\mathbb{C}[s^{2},s^{3},t]
.
Since
R_{A}
is not normal, note that integer points in the faces of
A
and
\mathbb{N}A
differ from those in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A
.
We let

\sigma_{1}=\mathbb{N}\{e_{2}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{2}=\mathbb{N}\{e_{1}\}

be the integer points in the facets of the cone
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}A
.
The primitive integral support functions of
\mathbb{N}A
are

h_{1}=h_{1}(\theta)=\theta_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad h_{2}=h_{2}(\theta)=\theta_{2}.

The prime ideals associated to the facets of
A
are

P_{1}=\langle s^{2},s^{3}\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad P_{2}=\langle t\rangle.

Set

J=P_{1}\cap P_{2}=\langle s^{2}t,s^{3}t\rangle
.
Note that
R_{A}
is a Gorenstein ring that is not normal, and, in this case, the ideal
J
, which is the interior, is not the canonical module of
R_{A}
.

Figure 7.1
.

Chambers for
\mathbb{C}[s^{2},s^{3},t]

We will again use
chambers
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
to aid in our computations of the graded pieces of

\mathbb{I}(J)/D(R_{A},J)
;
in particular, we show that

\mathbb{I}(J)/D(R_{A},J)
is only nonzero in multidegree

, and both the green and dark green make

and

Eriksen showed in
[

, Proposition 2.1]
that the ring of differential operators of a numerical semigroup ring
R=\mathbb{C}[t^{\Gamma}]
is

D(R)=\bigoplus\limits_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\left\langle\prod\limits_{\gamma\in\Omega(d)}(\theta-\gamma)\right\rangle,

where
\Omega(d)=\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid\gamma+d\notin\Gamma\}

We encode these polynomial generators using
G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)
, where

G_{\bm{d}}(\theta):=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }d_{1}\in\mathbb{N}\langle 2,3\rangle,\\
h_{1}&\text{ if }d_{1}=1,\\
h_{1}-1&\text{if }d_{1}=-1,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\left(h_{1},1-d_{1}\right)!}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{1}+d_{1})}&\text{ if }d_{1}\leq-2.\end{cases}

By Theorem
2.3
, for any
\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,

D(R_{A})_{\bm{d}}=s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\left\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)\left(h_{2},-d_{2}-1\right)!\right\rangle.

To determine
\mathbb{I}(J)
or
D(R_{A},J)
, we must determine the lines of multidegrees that contain operators that, when applied to monomials in
J
or
R
, produce elements in
R\setminus J
.
Note that given
\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}A
,

(7.1)

\displaystyle s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)\left(h_{2},-d_{2}-1\right)!\ast s^{m_{1}}t^{m_{2}}=G_{\bm{d}}(\bm{m})(h_{2}(\bm{m}),-d_{2}-1)!\cdot s^{d_{1}+m_{1}}t^{d_{2}+m_{2}}.

If
\bm{d}\in\textbf{C1}
and
s^{m_{1}}t^{m_{2}}\in J
, then the result of (
7.1
) will remain in
J
.
Thus, for all
\bm{d}\in\textbf{C1}
,
D(R_{A},J)_{\bm{d}}=s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)\rangle
.

Next, for
{\bm{d}}\in\textbf{C2}
,
when
\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}A\cap\{x=-d_{1}\}
, then the result of (
7.1
) is a constant times a monomial in
\sigma_{1}
, which does not belong to
J
.
Hence, for all
\bm{d}\in\textbf{C2}
,

\mathbb{I}(J)_{\bm{d}}=s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})\rangle.

Further, if
\bm{d}\in\textbf{C2}\cup(\sigma_{1}\setminus\{\bm{0}\})

D(R_{A},J)_{\bm{d}}=s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})\rangle.

For
{\bm{d}}\in\textbf{C4}
,
when
\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}A\cap\{y=-d_{2}\}
, then the result of (
7.1
) is a constant times a monomial in
\sigma_{2}
, which does not belong to
J
.
Hence, for all
\bm{d}\in\textbf{C4}
,

\mathbb{I}(J)_{\bm{d}}=s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)\left(h_{2},-d_{2}\right)!\rangle.

Further, if
{\bm{d}}\in\textbf{C4}\cup(\sigma_{2}\setminus\{\bm{0}\})
, then

D(R_{A},J)_{\bm{d}}=s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle\left(h_{2},-d_{2}\right)!\rangle.

Finally, for
{\bm{d}}\in\textbf{C3}
,
if
\bm{m}\in\{x=-d_{1}\}\cap\{\bm{c}\mid s^{c_{1}}t^{c_{1}}\in J\}
or if
\bm{m}\in\{y=-d_{2}\}\cap\mathbb{N}A
, respectively,
then the result of (
7.1
)
is either a constant times a monomial in
\sigma_{1}
or
\sigma_{2}
, respectively.
Hence,

\mathbb{I}(J)_{\bm{d}}=s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})\left(h_{2},-d_{2}\right)!\rangle.

Finally, if
{\bm{d}}\in\textbf{C3}\cup(-\sigma_{1})\cup(-\sigma_{2})

then

D(R_{A},J)_{\bm{d}}=s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})\left(h_{2},-d_{2}\right)!\rangle.

Gathering these computations,

\mathbb{I}(J)_{\bm{d}}=\begin{cases}s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)\rangle&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}\in\textbf{C1},\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})\rangle&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}\in\textbf{C2},\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})\left(h_{2},-d_{2}\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}\in\textbf{C3},\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)\left(h_{2},-d_{2}\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}\in\textbf{C4},\end{cases}

and

D(R_{A},J)_{\bm{d}}=\begin{cases}s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)\rangle&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in\textbf{C1}\setminus(\sigma_{1}\cup\sigma_{2}),\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})\rangle&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}\in(\textbf{C2}\setminus\sigma_{2})\cup(\sigma_{1}\cap\{d_{2}>0\}),\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})\left(h_{2},-d_{2}\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}\in\textbf{C3}\cup\{\bm{0}\}\cup(-\sigma_{1})\cup(-\sigma_{2}),\\

\mathbb{I}(J)/D(R_{A},J)
as follows:

\left(\dfrac{\mathbb{I}(J)}{D(R_{A},J)}\right)_{\bm{d}}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}\notin\mathbb{Z}{\sigma_{1}}\cup\mathbb{Z}{\sigma_{2}},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{C}[\theta]}{\langle h_{1}h_{2}\rangle}&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}=\bm{0},\\

s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\displaystyle\frac{\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)\rangle}{\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)h_{1}\rangle}&\text{ if }d_{1}=0,d_{2}>0,\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\displaystyle\frac{\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)\left(h_{2},-d_{2}\right)!\rangle}{\langle h_{1}G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)\left(h_{2},-d_{2}\right)!\rangle}&\text{ if }d_{1}=0,d_{2}<0.\end{cases}

In this case,
JD(R_{A})=D(R_{A},J)
, so (
1.2
) happens to hold in this example.
To see this, compute
(JD(R_{A}))_{\bm{d}}
by looking at
\mathbb{I}(\Omega(\bm{d}-(2,1))
and
\mathbb{I}(\Omega(\bm{d}-(3,1))
for any
\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
.
For an example of how showing this equality works, when
(d_{1}-2,d_{2}-1)
and
(d_{1}-3,d_{2}-1)
are both in
\textbf{C2}\setminus\sigma_{2}

G_{(d_{1}-2,d_{2}-1)}(\theta)=\displaystyle\frac{\left(h_{1},3-d_{1}\right)!}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{1}+d_{1}-2)}\quad\text{and}\quad G_{(d_{1}-3,d_{2}-1)}(\theta)=\displaystyle\frac{\left(h_{1},4-d_{1}\right)!}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{1}+d_{1}-3)}.

Both of these polynomials are divisible by
G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})

G_{(d_{1}-2,d_{2}-1)}(\theta)=\displaystyle\frac{\left(h_{1},3-d_{1}\right)!}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{1}+d_{1}-2)}=G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})(h_{1}+d_{1}-3)

G_{(d_{1}-2,d_{2}-1)}(\theta)=\displaystyle\frac{\left(h_{1},4-d_{1}\right)!}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{1}+d_{1}-2)}=G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})(h_{1}+d_{1}-2)(h_{1}+d_{1}-4).

Further,

1=(h_{1}+d_{1}-3)^{2}-(h_{1}+d_{1}-2)(h_{1}+d_{1}-4),

which implies that

\left\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})\right\rangle=\left\langle G_{(d_{1}-2,d_{2}-1)}(\theta),G_{(d_{1}-3,d_{2}-1)}(\theta)\right\rangle.

Hence for
\bm{d}\in\textbf{C2}
, there is an equality

D(R_{A},J)_{\bm{d}}=s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)(h_{1}+d_{1})\rangle=(JD(R_{A}))_{\bm{d}}
.

In

Saito and Traves found that for any

D(R_{\widetilde{A}})_{\bm{d}}=s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\mathbb{I}(\Omega({\bm{d}}))
, where

(7.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(\Omega({\bm{d}}))=\begin{cases}\left\langle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{2}\left(h_{i},h_{i}({-\bm{d}})-1\right)!\right\rangle&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}\notin e_{1}-\mathbb{N}\widetilde{A},\\
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{2}\left(h_{i},h_{i}(-{\bm{d}})-1\right)!\cdot&\\
\qquad\left\langle h_{1}+d_{1}-1,h_{2}+d_{2}\right\rangle&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}\in e_{1}-\mathbb{N}\widetilde{A}.\end{cases}

To compute
\mathbb{I}(\widetilde{J})
, we first determine which elements of
\mathbb{I}(\Omega(d))
are already in
\mathbb{I}(\widetilde{J})
.
To do this, we first break up (
7.2
) into cases by chamber:

\mathbb{I}(\widetilde{J})_{\bm{d}}=\begin{cases}s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\mathbb{I}(\Omega({\bm{d}}))&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}\in\textbf{C1},\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle\left(h_{1},h_{1}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in\textbf{C2}\setminus(-\sigma_{2}),\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\left(h_{1},h_{1}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\cdot\langle h_{1}+d_{1}-1,h_{2}\rangle&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in(-\sigma_{2}),\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\left\langle\left(h_{1},h_{1}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\left(h_{2},h_{2}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\right\rangle&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in\textbf{C3},\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle\left(h_{2},h_{2}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in\textbf{C4}.\end{cases}

Similarly,

D(R_{\widetilde{A}},\widetilde{J})_{\bm{d}}=\begin{cases}s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\mathbb{C}[\theta]&\text{ if }s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\in\widetilde{J},\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle\left(h_{1},h_{1}(-{\bm{d}})\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in(\textbf{C2}\setminus(-\sigma_{2}))\cup(\sigma_{1}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}),\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle\left(h_{2},h_{2}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in\textbf{C4}\setminus(-\sigma_{1}),\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\langle\left(h_{1},h_{1}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\left(h_{2},h_{2}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\rangle&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in\textbf{C3}\cup(-\sigma_{1})\cup(-\sigma_{2}).\end{cases}

Putting these together, the graded piece of
\mathbb{I}(\widetilde{J})/D(R_{\widetilde{A}},\widetilde{J})
at
{\bm{d}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{I}(\widetilde{J})}{D(R_{\widetilde{A}},\widetilde{J})}_{\bm{d}}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}\notin\mathbb{Z}\sigma_{1}\cup\mathbb{Z}\sigma_{2},\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\mathbb{C}[\theta]}{\langle h_{1}\rangle}&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in\sigma_{1}\setminus\{\bm{0}\},\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\mathbb{C}[\theta]}{\langle h_{2}\rangle}&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in\sigma_{2}\setminus\{\bm{0},(1,0)\},\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\mathbb{C}[\theta]}{\langle\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\rangle}&\text{ if }\bm{d}=\bm{0},\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle}{\langle h_{2}\rangle}&\text{ if }{\bm{d}}=(1,0),\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\left(h_{1},h_{1}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\langle h_{1}+d_{1}-1,h_{2}\rangle}{\langle\left(h_{1},h_{1}({-\bm{d}})\right)!h_{2}\rangle}&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in(-\sigma_{2})\setminus\{\bm{0}\},\\
s^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\langle\left(h_{2},h_{2}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\rangle}{\langle h_{1}\left(h_{2},h_{2}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\rangle}&\text{ if }\bm{d}\in(-\sigma_{1})\setminus\{\bm{0}\}.\end{cases}

Looking at the graded pieces of
\mathbb{I}(J)/D(R_{A},J)
from Example
7.1
and noting that
G_{\bm{d}}(\theta)=\mathbb{C}[\theta]
for
\bm{d}\in(\sigma_{1})\cup(\sigma_{2}\setminus\{\textbf{0},(1,0)\}
, it follows that
\left(\mathbb{I}(J)/D(R_{A},J)\right)_{\bm{d}}
is identical to
\left({\mathbb{I}(\widetilde{J})}/{D(R_{\widetilde{A}},\widetilde{J})}\right)_{\bm{d}}
for all
\bm{d}
except
\bm{d}=(1,0)
and
\bm{d}\in(-\sigma_{2})\setminus\{\textbf{0}\}
.
So we only need to exhibit that the graded pieces are isomorphic for
\bm{d}\in\{(1,0)\}\cup(-\sigma_{2})\setminus\{\textbf{0}\}
. Note that for
d_{1}<0
and
d_{1}\neq-1
,

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\displaystyle\frac{\left(h_{1},1-d_{1}\right)!}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{1}+d_{1})}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\displaystyle\frac{\left(h_{1},1-d_{1}\right)!\,h_{2}}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{1}+d_{1})}\right\rangle}\cong\displaystyle\frac{\langle\left(h_{1},1-d_{1}\right)!\rangle}{\langle\left(h_{1},1-d_{1}\right)!\,h_{2}\rangle}

by the Third Isomorphism Theorem, and

\dfrac{\left(h_{1},h_{1}({-\bm{d}})\right)!\langle h_{1}+d_{1}-1,h_{2}\rangle}{\langle\left(h_{1},h_{1}({-\bm{d}})\right)!h_{2}\rangle}\cong\displaystyle\frac{\langle\left(h_{1},1-d_{1}\right)!\rangle}{\langle\left(h_{1},1-d_{1}\right)!\,h_{2}\rangle}

by the Second Isomorphism Theorem.
A similar argument gives an isomorphism in
the remaining multidegrees.
Combining the information on all graded pieces,
we have explicitly shown that

\dfrac{\mathbb{I}(\widetilde{J})}{D\left(R_{\widetilde{A}},\widetilde{J}\right)}\cong\dfrac{\mathbb{I}(J)}{D\left(R_{A},J\right)}.

Lifting of vector-valued automorphic forms
Jitendra Bajpai, Subham Bhakta

Abstract
. A recent study by the first author [ 2 ] demonstrated that admissible vector-valued automorphic forms have admissible lifts. In this article, we study the specific case of logarithmic vector-valued automorphic forms, exploring their lifts and providing explicit computations of their Fourier coefficients.

11F03, 11F55, 30F35

1.
Introduction

Let
\bar{\mathrm{G}}\subset\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
be a Fuchsian group of the first kind, and
\rho:\bar{\mathrm{G}}\to\text{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be any finite-dimensional representation. A vector-valued automorphic form (vvaf) of
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
of weight
k\in 2\mathbb{Z}
is a
\mathbb{C}^{m}
-valued meromorphic function
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
on the complex upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
which has a certain functional and cuspidal behaviour with respect to
\rho
. For details and explanation, see Section
2
. The theory of vector-valued automorphic forms has many important applications not only in mathematics but also in physics. For more details on the importance of their theory, see the introduction of
[
2
,
5
]
.

We call a representation
\rho
admissible if
\rho(\gamma)
is diagonalizable for every parabolic element
\gamma\in\bar{\mathrm{G}}.
In that case, we call the associated vvaf an admissible vvaf. Now if we take
\bar{\mathrm{H}}
to be a finite index subgroup of
\bar{\mathrm{G}},
then any representation
\rho
of
\bar{\mathrm{H}}
can be induced to a representation
\widetilde{\rho}
of
\bar{\mathrm{G}}.
It turns out that, a vvaf
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
of
\bar{\mathrm{H}}
associated to
\rho
can also be induced to a vvaf
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau)
of
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
associated to
\widetilde{\rho}
, see Section
3.2
. In short, vvaf
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau)
, which is a lift of vvaf
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
will be referred to as
lifted form
throughout the article. In
[
2
]
, the first author showed that the induction of an admissible representation is admissible. Hence, the induced function
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau)
is also a weakly holomorphic admissible vvaf associated with the induced representation. We are primarily interested in the case when
\rho
is not admissible. We furthermore assume that any eigenvalue of the image by
\rho
of a parabolic element is unitary, a technical condition that will be explained in Section
1.2
. A fundamental example of a non-admissible
\rho
verifying the unitary condition is the defining representation
\Gamma\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Vector-valued automorphic forms satisfying both of these conditions are called
logarithmic vector-valued automorphic forms
. Knopp and Mason
[
6
,
7
]
and Gannon
[
5
]
studied this case when
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
is the modular group. Recently, the authors studied them for any Fuchsian group of the first kind in
[
3
]
.

In this article, we discuss what happens to logarithmic vector-valued automorphic forms after lifting them. We shall work with the Fuchsian groups of the first kind
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
, and group
\mathrm{G}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
denotes the image of
\mathrm{G}
by the natural map
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. To prepare ourselves for further discussions, we provide a quick example of a logarithmic vvaf.

Example 1.1
.

An elementary example of logarithmic vvaf is
\mathbb{X}(\tau)=\left({\tau\atop 1}\right)
of weight
-1
for the full modular group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})=\left<s=\left({0\atop 1}{-1\atop\,\,0}\right),t=\left({1\atop 0}{1\atop 1}\right)\right>
, with respect to the representation
\rho:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, which is the defining representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, that is
\rho(s)=s
and
\rho(t)=t
.

1.1.
Fuchsian groups

Let
\mathbb{H}
be the upper half plane and
\mathbb{H}^{*}=\mathbb{H}\cup\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be the extended upper half plane. It is well known that,
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acts on
\mathbb{H}^{*}
. For any
\gamma=\overline{\left({a\atop c}{b\atop d}\right)}\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the action of
\gamma
is defined by the Möbius action
\gamma\cdot\tau=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}.

A Fuchsian group is a discrete subgroup
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
of PSL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
for which
\bar{\mathrm{G}}\backslash\mathbb{H}
is a Riemann surface with finitely many punctures. Because of the discreteness, a fundamental domain exists for the action on
\mathbb{H}
. When it has a finite area, the corresponding Fuchsian group is of the first kind.

An element of
\gamma\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is called a parabolic element, if
|\text{tr}(\gamma)|=2.
A point
\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{*}
is said to be a fixed point of
\gamma\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
if
\gamma\cdot\tau=\tau.
If
\gamma=\overline{\left({a\atop c}{b\atop d}\right)}
is a parabolic element then its fixed point
\tau=\frac{a\mp 1}{c}
when
a+d=\pm 2
and
c\neq 0
, in addition
\tau=\infty
when
c=0
. Moreover, we call an element
\left({a\atop c}{b\atop d}\right)\in\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
as parabolic, if the corresponding element
\overline{\left({a\atop c}{b\atop d}\right)}\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is parabolic.

Let
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
be a subgroup of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. A point
\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{*}
is called a cusp of
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
(equivalently, of
\mathrm{G}
) if it is fixed by some nontrivial parabolic element of
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
(equivalently, of
\mathrm{G}
). Let
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{G}}
denote the set of all cusps of
\mathrm{G}
and we define
\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{G}}^{*}=\mathbb{H}\cup\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{G}}
to be the extended upper half plane of
\mathrm{G}
. For example: if
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
then
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{G}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
and
if
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
then
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{G}}=\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}
, is the
\mathrm{G}
-orbit of cusp
\infty
.

For any
\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{G}}
, let
\bar{\mathrm{G}_{\tau}}=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{G}|\gamma\cdot\tau=\tau\}
be the stabilizer subgroup of
\tau
in
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
. For any
{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{G}}
,
\bar{\mathrm{G}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}
is an infinite order cyclic subgroup of
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
. If
{\mathfrak{c}}=\infty
then
\bar{\mathrm{G}_{\infty}}
is generated by
\overline{\left({1\atop 0}{h_{{\infty}}\atop 1}\right)}
, for a unique real number
h_{{\infty}}>0
, which is called the cusp width of the cusp
\infty
. Throughout the article, we shall denote
t_{\infty}=\left({1\atop 0}{h_{{\infty}}\atop 1}\right).
In case of
{\mathfrak{c}}\neq\infty
,
\bar{\mathrm{G}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}
is generated by
\overline{A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\left({1\atop 0}{h_{{{\mathfrak{c}}}}\atop 1}\right)A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{-1}}
for some smallest real number
h_{{\mathfrak{c}}}>0
, called the cusp width of the cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
, where
A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}=\left({{\mathfrak{c}}\atop 1}{-1\atop\ 0}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
so that
A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}(\infty)={\mathfrak{c}}
. For rest of the article, we shall denote
t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}=A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\left({1\atop 0}{h_{{{\mathfrak{c}}}}\atop 1}\right)A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{-1}
. The reader may note that the stabilizer subgroup of any cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
in
\mathrm{G}
is not cyclic. This is essentially because, the stabilizer group
\mathrm{G}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
contains both
t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
and
-t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
, and in particular,
-I\in\mathrm{G}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
. This is not possible because
-I
is a non-trivial element of finite order.

1.2.
Results

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathrm{G}\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
be any group, and
\mathrm{H}
be any finite index subgroup of
\mathrm{G}
. Suppose that
-I\in\mathrm{H}
, and
\bar{\mathrm{H}},\penalty\ \bar{\mathrm{G}}
are Fuchsian groups of the first kind. Let
\rho:\mathrm{H}\to\mathrm{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be any finite dimensional representation, and
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
be a weakly holomorphic vvaf for
\mathrm{H}
associated to
\rho
. Then we have the following.

(a)

Let
{\mathfrak{c}}
be an arbitrary cusp of
\mathrm{G}
and
\{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}|1\leq i\leq n_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\}
be a set of inequivalent cusps of
\bar{\mathrm{H}}
for which

\bar{\mathrm{G}}\cdot{\mathfrak{c}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}\bar{\mathrm{H}}\cdot{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}.

If
\rho(t_{i})
is not diagonalizable for some
i,
then
\widetilde{\rho}(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})
is not diagonalizable, where
t_{i}
is a stabilizer of cusp
c_{i}
such that
\overline{t_{i}}
is the generator of the stabilizer subgroup of the cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}
in
\bar{\mathrm{H}}
.

(b)

Let
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
be a weakly holomorphic logarithmic vvaf associated with
\rho.
Then the lifted form
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau)
is a weakly holomorphic logarithmic vvaf, associated with the induced representation
\widetilde{\rho}
of the same weight as
\mathbb{X}(\tau).

The first author
[
2
]
showed that if all of the
\rho(t_{i})
are diagonalizable, then
\widetilde{\rho}(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})
is also diagonalizable. The author achieved this by explicitly writing down the eigenbasis of
\widetilde{\rho}(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})
, in terms of the eigenbasis of all the
\rho(t_{i})
s.

We show that the converse of
[
2
, Theorem 4.2]
is true, in part
(a)
of the theorem above. Our approach diverges, opting for a simpler method that circumvents the necessity of explicitly deriving the eigenbasis. Instead, we prove that a certain power of
\widetilde{\rho}(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})
adopts a block diagonal structure, with at least one block remaining nondiagonalizable.

Yet, using the methodology outlined in
[
2
]
, we carry on the task of writing down the eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\rho}(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})
and explicitly compute their multiplicities, in Lemma
4.3
. Although part
(a)
of Theorem
1.2
could theoretically be derived from Lemma
4.3
, we leverage this stronger outcome to facilitate the explicit derivation of the Fourier coefficients of the lift, as presented later in Theorem
1.3
.

For any even integer
k,
it has been shown in
[
2
]
that there exists a bijection between the set of all admissible vvaf of weight
k
associated to
\rho
and the set of all admissible vvaf associated to
\widetilde{\rho}
of weight
k.
In this article, we shall define this isomorphism as a lift and work with this. Furthermore, we also have an analogous isomorphism for the logarithmic vector-valued automorphic forms, and we note this in Proposition
4.4
.

As a consequence of the lifting result for admissible vector-valued automorphic forms, the first author showed the existence of weakly holomorphic admissible vector-valued automorphic forms. In the same spirit, we shall discuss the existence of weakly holomorphic logarithmic vector-valued automorphic forms in the form of Proposition
5.3
and Corollary
5.4
. Some technical issues are arising in this process, and we address them in Sections
4
and
5
.

In the second half of the article, we study the relations between the Fourier coefficients of
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
and its lift
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau).
To get a Fourier expansion of
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
at the cusps of
\bar{\mathrm{H}},
we need to assume that
\rho(\gamma)
has only unitary eigenvalues for every parabolic element
\gamma
in
\mathrm{H}.
In particular to have Fourier expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau)
at the cusps of
\mathrm{G},
we need to ensure that
\widetilde{\rho}(\gamma)
has only unitary eigenvalues for every parabolic element
\gamma
of
\mathrm{G}
. This requirement stems from the fact that the presence of non-unitary eigenvalues implies a non-polynomial growth of
\rho
, as discussed in
[
3
, Sect. 6]
. On the other hand, the necessity of such polynomial growth is demonstrated in
[
3
, Thm. 1.2]
. Moreover, the first author explicitly described them in the case of admissible vector-valued automorphic forms. Here we do the same for logarithmic vector-valued automorphic forms, and as a consequence, we deduce the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G}
and
\rho
be as in Theorem
1.2
with index
[\mathrm{G}:\mathrm{H}]=d
. Let
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
be a weakly holomorphic vector-valued automorphic form associated to
(\mathrm{H},\rho)
of weight
0
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau)
be the lifted form associated to
(\mathrm{G},\widetilde{\rho}).
Then there exists a family of weakly holomorphic vector-valued automorphic forms
\{\mathbb{X}^{(i)}(\tau)|1\leq i\leq d\}
associated to some
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
conjugates of
(\mathrm{H},\rho)
with the following property: let
\mathbb{X}^{(i)}[n]
be the
n^{th}
Fourier coefficient of
\mathbb{X}^{(i)}(\tau),
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}[n]
be the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
Fourier coefficient associated to the lifted form
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau).
Then there exists a set of rationals
\{m_{i},n_{i},r_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq d\}
, such that

\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}[n]=\left(r_{i}\mathbb{X}^{(i)}[m_{i}n+n_{i}]\right)^{t}_{1\leq i\leq d}.

Furthermore, the quantities
\{m_{i},n_{i},r_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq d\}
depend only on
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G}
and
\rho
, but not on the associated vvaf
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
.

2.
Vector-valued automorphic forms

This section reviews the basics of vector-valued automorphic forms that we need to understand and prove the main results of the article. In
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
, the authors studied vector-valued automorphic forms associated with Fuchsian groups of the first kind of even weight.

Given an element
\gamma=\left({a\atop c}{b\atop d}\right)\in\mathrm{G},
we denote
j(\gamma,\tau)=c\tau+d.
However, if we take an element
\gamma=\overline{\left({a\atop c}{b\atop d}\right)}\in\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),
we can not define

j(\gamma,\tau)
in this way because of one possible sign factor. In
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
, this sign factor was redundant as the authors worked with even weights vector-valued automorphic forms, and therefore, it always comes with even power. Therefore, the even weight is necessary to define
j(\cdot,\cdot)
. In this article, we shall mainly work inside
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. This will allow us a slight improvement, i.e. we can work with any integer weight, not just the even ones. Our treatment of vector-valued automorphic forms in this section closely follows
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Stroke operator)
.

If
\mathbb{X}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
is a vector-valued meromorphic function,
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
k
be a complex number, we define a vector-valued meromorphic function
\mathbb{X}|_{k}\gamma
on
\mathbb{H}
by setting
\mathbb{X}|_{k}\gamma(\tau)=j(\gamma,\tau)^{-k}\mathbb{X}(\gamma\tau),

where
j(\gamma,\tau)=c\tau+d
when
\gamma=\left({a\atop c}{b\atop d}\right).

It is needless to mention that the function
j(\gamma,\tau)^{k}
is well defined because
j(\gamma,\cdot):\penalty\ \mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a non-vanishing function. One can show that the Stroke operator induces a right group action of
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on the space of vector-valued meromorphic functions on
\mathbb{H}
. For this article, we shall restrict to integer
k
. We are interested in the vector-valued meromorphic functions with finite order poles at the cusps. More precisely, let us first make the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{X}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
be a vector-valued meromorphic function.

•

We say that
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
has
moderate growth at
\infty
when there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
Y>0
such that
\|\mathbb{X}(\tau)\|<\mathrm{exp}(cy)
when
y>Y
, where
y=\mathrm{Im}\tau.

•

we say that
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
has
moderate growth at
{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathbb{R}
with respect to
k\in\mathbb{Z}
when
\mathbb{X}|_{k}A_{\mathfrak{c}}
has moderate growth at
\infty
.

Furthermore, we shall refer to the composition of
X(\tau)
with the projection map to each coordinate of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, as the components of
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
. To introduce vector-valued automorphic forms, we first discuss the representations of
\mathrm{G}
. For us, these representations are divided into two types. Namely, admissible and logarithmic (non-admissible) ones. Let us describe them in the following form.

Definition 2.3
.

Consider a representation
\rho:\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
such that for every parabolic element
\gamma\in\mathrm{G}
, the eigenvalues of
\rho(\gamma)
are all unitary. We say that
\rho
is an
admissible representation
of
\mathrm{G}
if
\rho(\gamma)
is diagonalizable for every parabolic element
\gamma\in\mathrm{G}
. Otherwise, we say that
\rho
is a
logarithmic representation.

Any parabolic element in
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
is power of
\overline{t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}
, for some cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{G}}.
In particular, any parabolic element in
\mathrm{G}
is power of
\pm t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
. Note that
\rho(-I)
is always diagonalizable. Therefore, we are really talking about diagonalizability and the unitary condition at the finitely many parabolic elements
\{t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\}_{{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{G}}}
. We shall now separately treat the vector-valued meromorphic functions associated with them.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
be a Fuchsian group of the first kind,
k
be an integer,
\rho:\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be an admissible representation, and
\mathbb{X}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
be a vector-valued meromorphic function. Then, we say that
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is a weakly holomorphic
admissible vvaf
of weight
k
with respect to
\rho
if it satisfies the following properties.

•

It is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H},
with moderate growth at any cusp of
\mathrm{G},

•

It has finitely many poles in the closure of a fundamental domain of
G^{\prime}
, and

•

\mathbb{X}|_{k}\gamma(\tau)=\rho(\gamma)\mathbb{X}(\tau),\penalty\ \forall\gamma\in\mathrm{G}.

Remark 2.5
.

(a)

If there exists a non-zero
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
satisfying
\mathbb{X}|_{k}\gamma(\tau)=\rho(\gamma)\mathbb{X}(\tau),\penalty\ \forall\gamma\in\mathrm{G}
, then we must have that
\rho(I)=e^{-i\pi k}\rho(-I).

(b)

Of course, a representation
\rho
satisfies
\rho(-I)=e^{i\pi k}\rho(I),
only if
k
is an integer. Moreover,
\rho
can be considered a representation of
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
if and only if
\rho(-I)=\rho(I).
Equivalently when the associated vvaf has even weight. In other words, only a vvaf of even weight is associated with
\mathrm{G},
is a vvaf of even weight associated with
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
. Therefore we are studying the problem in a bit more generality, even in the case of admissible forms.

(c)

If
\rho
is any irreducible representation, it turns out that
\rho(-I)
must be one of the
\pm I.
This is a simple fact from linear algebra.

Recall that
\overline{t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
(up-to conjugation) generates
\bar{\mathrm{G}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}
. Since
\rho
is admissible, we can write
\rho(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})=P_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\mathrm{diag}\left(\lambda_{1,{\mathfrak{c}}},\lambda_{2,{\mathfrak{c}}},\cdots,\lambda_{m,{\mathfrak{c}}}\right)P_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{-1}
for any cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
of
\mathrm{G}.
To get the Fourier expansion around the cusps, we denote

\widetilde{q}_{{}_{\mathfrak{c}}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}e^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}A_{{}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}^{-1}\tau}{h_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}}&\mathrm{if}\penalty\ {\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathfrak{C}_{{}_{\mathrm{G}}},{\mathfrak{c}}\neq\infty\\
e^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}{h_{\infty}}}&\mathrm{if}\penalty\ {\mathfrak{c}}=\infty.\end{array}\,.\right.

Following
[
3
, Proposition 3.11]
, at the cusp
\infty
we have the following Fourier expansion,

\mathbb{X}(\tau)=P_{\infty}\widetilde{q}^{\Lambda}P_{\infty}^{-1}\sum_{n=-M_{\infty}}^{\infty}\mathbb{X}_{[n]}\widetilde{q}^{n}.

For any other cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}\neq\infty,
consider
\mathbb{Y}(\tau)=\mathbb{X}|_{k}A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}(\tau),
and then

\mathbb{X}(\tau)=\mathbb{Y}|_{k}A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{-1}(\tau)=({\mathfrak{c}}-\tau)^{-k}\mathbb{X}_{0}(A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{-1}\tau).

Following this, we have the Fourier expansion

(1)

\mathbb{X}(\tau)=(\tau-{\mathfrak{c}})^{-k}P_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\widetilde{q}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\ \Lambda_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}P_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{-1}\sum_{n=-M_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}^{\infty}\mathbb{X}^{{}^{{\mathfrak{c}}}}_{[n]}\ \widetilde{q}_{{}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}^{\ n},\ \mathbb{X}^{{}^{{\mathfrak{c}}}}_{[n]}\in\mathbb{C}^{d},

where we denote
\widetilde{q}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\ \Lambda_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}
to be the diagonal matrix
(\widetilde{q}^{\mu(\lambda_{1,{\mathfrak{c}}})},\widetilde{q}^{\mu(\lambda_{2,{\mathfrak{c}}})},\cdots,\widetilde{q}^{\mu(\lambda_{m,{\mathfrak{c}}})}).
In particular, note that the Fourier coefficients do not depend on the choice of the diagonalizing matrix. Note that
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is a weakly holomorphic admissible vvaf if
M_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
is always finite.

A weakly holomorphic admissible vvaf
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
of weight
k
is called
holomorphic
if it has no poles in
\mathbb{H}
and, for any cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
of
\mathrm{G}
, the function
\mathbb{X}|_{k}A_{\mathfrak{c}}(\tau)
is bounded in some half-plane (contained in
\mathbb{H}
), that is to say simply that
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is holomorphic everywhere in
\mathbb{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{G}}
. It is called a weight
k

cusp form
if, for any cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
, the function
\mathbb{X}|_{k}A_{\mathfrak{c}}(\tau)
approaches to
0
as
y\to\infty
. This is same as saying that
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is a holomorphic admissible vvaf if
M_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\leq 0
, and an admissible cusp form if
M_{{\mathfrak{c}}}<0
for any cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
of
\mathrm{G}.

We are interested in the representation
\rho
of
\mathrm{G}
, which is not admissible. In other words,
\rho(\gamma)
is not diagonalizable for some parabolic element
\gamma\in\mathrm{G}
. We call such a representation logarithmic. In this case we can write
\rho(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})
in the Jordan canonical form as

P_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\begin{pmatrix}\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace J_{m(\lambda_{1,{\mathfrak{c}}}),\lambda_{1,{\mathfrak{c}}}}&&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace&J_{m(\lambda_{2,{\mathfrak{c}}}),\lambda_{2,{\mathfrak{c}}}}&&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace&&\ddots&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace&&&J_{m(\lambda_{k,{\mathfrak{c}}}),\lambda_{k,{\mathfrak{c}}}}\\
\lx@nobreakspace\end{pmatrix}P_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{-1},

for any cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
of
\mathrm{G},
where the Jordan block
J_{m,\lambda}
is defined to be

\begin{pmatrix}\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\lambda&&&&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\lambda&\ddots&&&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace&&\lambda&&\lambda\\
\end{pmatrix}_{m\times m},

which is conjugate to the canonical Jordan block. We also set
R(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})
to be the collection of the eigenvalues of
\rho(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}),
which may not necessarily be pairwise distinct.

Let
\mathbb{X}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
be a vector-valued meromorphic function, and
\rho:\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be a logarithmic representation. Suppose that for some integer
k,\penalty\ \mathbb{X}(\tau)
satisfies

•

\mathbb{X}|_{k}\gamma=\rho(\gamma)\mathbb{X},\penalty\ \forall\gamma\in\mathrm{G},

•

\rho(I)=e^{i\pi k}\rho(-I).

Now let
{\mathfrak{c}}
be any arbitrary cusp of
\mathrm{G}.
Then for each eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\rho(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})
there are
\widetilde{q}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
-expansions

(2)

h_{\lambda,j,{\mathfrak{c}}}(\tau)=P_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\widetilde{q}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\mu(\lambda)}P_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{-1}\sum_{n=-M_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}^{\infty}\mathbb{X}^{{\mathfrak{c}}}[\lambda,j,n]\widetilde{q}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{n},\penalty\ 0\leq j\leq m(\lambda)-1

such that, at the cusp
\infty

(3)

\mathbb{X}(\tau)=\sum_{\lambda\in R(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})}\sum_{j=0}^{m(\lambda)-1}(\log\widetilde{q})^{j}h_{\lambda,j,\infty}(\tau),

and at the cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}(\neq\infty)
,

(4)

\mathbb{X}(\tau)=(\tau-{\mathfrak{c}})^{-k}\sum_{\lambda\in R(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})}\sum_{j=0}^{m(\lambda)-1}(\log\widetilde{q}_{{\mathfrak{c}}})^{j}h_{\lambda,j,{\mathfrak{c}}}(\tau),

where
m(\lambda)
is the size of one of the Jordan blocks associated with the eigenvalue
\lambda.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{X}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
be a vector-valued holomorphic function and suppose there exists an integer
k
such that
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
satisfies (
3
) and (
4
) above with respect to a logarithmic representation
\rho:\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
. Then, we say that
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is a weakly holomorphic logarithmic vvaf of weight
k
, if for any cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
of
\mathrm{G}
, all of the
\widetilde{q}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
expansions
h_{\lambda,j,{\mathfrak{c}}}(\tau)
start from some finite
n.

In particular, we say that
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is holomorphic at the cusp
\infty
when
M\leq 0
, and holomorphic at the cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
when
\mathbb{X}|_{k}A_{\mathfrak{c}}(\tau)
is holomorphic at the cusp
\infty
, or equivalently when
M_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\leq 0.
If
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is holomorphic at all cusps, then we say that
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is a holomorphic logarithmic vvaf. In addition, we say that
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is a logarithmic vector-valued cusp form if
M_{{\mathfrak{c}}}<0
for any cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
of
\mathrm{G}.

3.
Properties of lifted vector-valued automorphic forms

In this section, we shall recall the induction of representations and introduce vvaf associated with them. We closely follow
[
2
]
throughout this section and develop the main tools to prove the main results of this article.

3.1.
A special choice of the representatives

Let us first recall the usual setup:
\mathrm{G}\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
be any group, and
\mathrm{H}
be any finite index subgroup of
\mathrm{G}
. Suppose that the index is
d,\penalty\ -I\in\mathrm{H}
, and
\bar{\mathrm{H}},\penalty\ \bar{\mathrm{G}}
are Fuchsian groups of the first kind. Fix any cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{C}}_{{}_{\mathrm{G}}}
and let
{\mathfrak{c}}_{1},\cdots,{\mathfrak{c}}_{\mathfrak{n}_{{}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}}
be the representatives of the
\bar{\mathrm{H}}
-inequivalent cusps which are
\bar{\mathrm{G}}
-equivalent to the cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
, so we have
\bar{\mathrm{G}}\cdot{\mathfrak{c}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\mathfrak{n}_{{}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}}\bar{\mathrm{H}}\cdot{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}.
In particular, one can write

\mathrm{G}\cdot{\mathfrak{c}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\mathfrak{n}_{{}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}}\mathrm{H}\cdot{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}},

since two cusps are
\bar{\mathrm{H}}
equivalent, if and only if, they are
\mathrm{H}
equivalent. Therefore, for each
i
we get an
A_{i}\in\mathrm{G}
with
A_{i}({\mathfrak{c}})={\mathfrak{c}}_{i}
. Let us denote
k_{{}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}
be the cusp width of
{\mathfrak{c}}
in
\mathrm{G}
and
h_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}
be the cusp width of
{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}
in
\mathrm{H}
. Then a set of coset representatives of
\mathrm{H}
in
\mathrm{G}
can be taken to be
g_{ij}=t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{j}A_{i}^{{}^{-1}}
for all
1\leq i\leq n_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
and
0\leq j<h_{i}
, where
h_{i}=\frac{h_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}}{k_{{}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}}\in\mathbb{Z}.
It turns out that,
\sum_{1\leq i\leq n_{\infty}}h_{i}=d
. Setting
t_{i}=A_{i}t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{h_{i}}A_{i}^{-1}
, note that any element of the stabilizer group
\mathrm{H}_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}
can be written as power of
\pm t_{i}.

Let
\rho
be a representation of rank
m
associated to
\mathrm{H},
and denote
\widetilde{\rho}:=\text{Ind}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{G}}(\rho)
to be the induction of
\rho.
With the choice of coset representatives
\{g_{i,j}\}
of
\mathrm{H}
in
\mathrm{G}
as described above, we can write
\widetilde{\rho}(t_{\mathfrak{c}})
in the block diagonal form, where each block is of size
mh_{i}\times mh_{i},\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ 1\leq i\leq n_{{\mathfrak{c}}}.
Moreover, these blocks are in the lower-diagonal form whose right top block is
\rho(t_{i}),
and all other blocks are in the lower diagonal entry is
I_{m\times m}.
More precisely, it is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace 0&&&&\rho(t_{i})\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace I&\ddots&&&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace&&I&&0\\
\end{pmatrix}_{mh_{i}\times mh_{i}}.

For a more detailed discussion of Fuchsian groups, we refer the reader to
[
2
, Section 4]
. Here we are pointing out the similar facts over their pullbacks in
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}).

3.2.
Lifting of vector-valued automorphic forms

Let
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G}
and
\rho
be as in the previous section, and
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
be a weakly holomorphic vvaf associated to
(\rho,\mathrm{H})
. Now one may ask for a lifted form
\widetilde{X}(\tau),
which is also a weakly holomorphic vvaf associated with
\widetilde{\rho}.
Fix a cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
of
\mathrm{G}
and let
\{g_{i,j}\}
be the set of coset representatives of
\mathrm{H}
in
\mathrm{G},
as described earlier. We then define the induced function
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}^{dm}
by setting,

\tau\mapsto\bigg(\mathbb{X}(g_{i,j}^{-1}\tau)\bigg)^{\mathfrak{t}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\\
0\leq j<h_{i}\end{subarray}}.

The reader can note that, given any cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
of
\mathrm{G},
we are uniquely lifting the vvaf
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
to
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}(\tau),
because
g_{i,j}
are well defined. Of course,
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}(\tau)
is just a vector-valued holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H}
right now, because
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is a weakly holomorphic vvaf. To make sure that
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}(\tau)
is a weakly holomorphic vvaf (be it admissible or logarithmic), we first need to check the functional equation with respect to the induced representation
\widetilde{\rho}
. Note that,

\displaystyle\footnotesize\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}(\gamma\tau)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{X}(\gamma_{1}^{-1}\gamma\tau)\\
\mathbb{X}(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma\tau)\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{X}(\gamma_{d}^{-1}\gamma\tau)\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{X}(\gamma_{1}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{1}}\gamma_{j_{1}}^{-1}\tau)\\
\mathbb{X}(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{2}}\gamma_{j_{2}}^{-1}\tau)\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{X}(\gamma_{d}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{d}}\gamma_{j_{d}}^{-1}\tau)\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}j(\gamma_{1}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{1}},\tau)^{k}\rho(\gamma_{1}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{1}})\mathbb{X}(\gamma_{j_{1}}^{-1}\tau)\\
j(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{2}},\tau)^{k}\rho(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{2}})\mathbb{X}(\gamma_{j_{2}}^{-1}\tau)\\
\vdots\\
j(\gamma_{d}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{d}},\tau)^{k}\rho(\gamma_{d}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{d}})\mathbb{X}(\gamma_{j_{d}}^{-1}\tau)\\
\end{array}\right)\,,

where
\{\gamma_{i}|1\leq i\leq d\}
is the set
\left\{g_{i,j}|1\leq i\leq n_{{\mathfrak{c}}},0\leq j<h_{i}\right\}.
To satisfy the functional equation property, we need that

j(\gamma_{i}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{i}},\tau)^{k}=j(\gamma,\tau)^{k},\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ 1\leq i\leq d,\gamma\in\mathrm{G}.

We can make sure this if,
r_{2}(\gamma_{i}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{i}})=r_{2}(\gamma),\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ 1\leq i\leq d,\penalty\ \gamma\in\mathrm{G},
or simply if the weight
k=0,
where
r_{2}(\cdot)
denotes the second row of the corresponding matrix. Of course, it is unlikely that
r_{2}(\gamma_{i}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{j_{i}})=r_{2}(\gamma),\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ 1\leq i\leq d,\penalty\ \gamma\in\mathrm{G}
would always hold. So to be on the safer side, we stick to the weight
k=0
case to make sure that the functional equation is satisfied. Before discussing the moderate growth condition, let us first define a suitable lift for any arbitrary weight case. For this, let us first recall a reduction trick introduced in
[
2
]
. The idea is to find a scalar-valued cusp form
\Delta_{\mathrm{G}}(\tau)
of non-zero weight, which is holomorphic on
\mathbb{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{G}}
and nonzero everywhere, except at
\infty.
For instance if
\mathrm{G}
is given by the modular group
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),
then one can take

\Delta_{\mathrm{G}}(\tau)=(\eta(\tau))^{24}=q\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})^{24},

where
\eta(\tau)
is the Dedekind eta function. For the existence in the general case, the reader may look at the exposition in
[
2
, Section 4]
.

Now given a weakly holomorphic admissible or logarithmic vvaf
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
of weight
k,
let us denote
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\tau)=\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{-k/w_{\mathrm{H}}}(\tau)\mathbb{X}(\tau),
where
w_{\mathrm{H}}
is the weight of
\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}(\tau).
It is clear that,
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\tau)
is a weakly holomorphic vvaf of weight
0,
associated to the representation
\rho\otimes\nu_{\mathrm{H}}^{-k},
where
\nu_{\mathrm{H}}
is the rank
1
representation associated to
\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{1/w_{\mathrm{H}}}(\tau).
One can then consider a reduction to a weight
0
automorphic form by
\mathbb{X}(\tau)\mapsto\mathbb{X}_{0}(\tau).
In particular, we now have a recipe to lift to a vvaf of arbitrary weight by considering the map

\mathbb{X}(\tau)\mapsto\mathbb{X}_{0}(\tau)\mapsto\widetilde{\mathbb{X}_{0}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}(\tau)\Delta_{\mathrm{G}}^{k/w_{\mathrm{G}}}(\tau):=\Delta_{\mathrm{G}}^{k/w_{\mathrm{G}}}(\tau)\bigg(\mathbb{X}_{0}(g_{i,j}^{-1}\tau)\bigg)^{\mathfrak{t}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\\
0\leq j<h_{i}\end{subarray}}.

Note that
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}_{0}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}(\tau)\Delta_{\mathrm{G}}^{k/w_{\mathrm{G}}}(\tau)
satisfies the functional equation with respect to the representation
(\widetilde{\rho\otimes\nu_{\mathrm{H}}})^{-k}\otimes\nu_{\mathrm{G}}^{k}=\widetilde{\rho}.
Therefore, we refine our definition of lift by setting

\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}(\tau):=\widetilde{\mathbb{X}_{0}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}(\tau)\Delta_{\mathrm{G}}^{k/w_{\mathrm{G}}}(\tau).

If
\infty
is a cusp of
\mathrm{G},
then we set
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau):=\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{(\infty)}(\tau)
as the definition of lifted form. If not, we pick any cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}
of
\mathrm{G}
and set
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau):=\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}|_{k}A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}(\tau),
which satisfies the required functional equation with respect to the representation
A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{-1}\gamma A_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\mapsto\widetilde{\rho}(\gamma).

3.3.
Preservation of the cuspidal properties

Before studying the behavior at the cusps, we first need to have a better understanding of the representatives of
\mathrm{H}
in
\mathrm{G}.
Suppose that
\infty
is a cusp of
\mathrm{G}
and
\left\{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}|1\leq i\leq n_{\infty}\right\}
are the cusps of
\mathrm{H}
lying under
\infty,
and
\{g_{i,j}\}
be the set of coset representatives of
\mathrm{H}
in
\mathrm{G}
as described before, with the important property that,
g_{i,j}({\mathfrak{c}}_{i})=\infty.
The following lemma is about a comparison with the set of all coset representatives
\left\{g_{i,j}|1\leq i\leq n_{\infty},0\leq j<h_{i}\right\}
inside
\mathrm{G}
and the set
\left\{A_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}|1\leq i\leq n_{\infty}\right\}
inside
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
a_{i,j}
and
\alpha_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

g_{i,j}\tau=a_{i,j}^{2}A_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}^{-1}\tau+jh_{\infty}+\alpha_{i,j},\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ \tau\in\mathbb{H}.

Proof.

Recall that,
g_{i,j}=t_{\infty}^{j}A_{i}^{-1}
where
A_{i}(\infty)={\mathfrak{c}}_{i}.
We also know that
A_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}(\infty)={\mathfrak{c}}_{i},
in particular,
A_{i}^{-1}A_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}(\infty)=\infty.
On the other hand,
A_{i}^{-1}A_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}\in\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}).
In particular,
A_{i}^{-1}A_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}=\left({a\atop 0}{\alpha\atop 1/a}\right)
for some
a,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.
We then have,

g_{i,j}\tau=t_{\infty}^{j}A_{i}^{-1}\tau=t_{\infty}^{j}\left({a\atop 0}{\alpha\atop 1/a}\right)A_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}^{-1}\tau=a^{2}A_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}^{-1}\tau+jh_{\infty}+\alpha a.

This completes the proof by taking
a_{i,j}:=a
and
\alpha_{i,j}:=\alpha a.

∎

We recall that any classical holomorphic modular form does not have weight
0,
unless it is a constant function. In those cases, the representation under consideration has a finite image. However, the same may not be true if the representation does not have a finite image. Consider the representation
\mathbb{I}_{0}:\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),
given by
\gamma\mapsto\gamma,
and the holomorphic function
\mathbb{Y}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
given by
\tau\mapsto(\tau,1)
. Note that
\mathbb{Y}(\tau)
is a holomorphic logarithmic vvaf of weight
-1
associated with
\mathbb{I}_{0}
. In particular,
\mathbb{Y}^{\prime}(\tau):=\mathbb{Y}(\tau)\Delta(\tau)^{\frac{1}{12}}
is a non-constant holomorphic logarithmic vvaf of weight
0
associated to the representation
\rho^{\prime}:=\mathbb{I}_{0}\otimes\nu_{\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}.
In fact, following
[
5
]
, we know that the space of holomorphic logarithmic vvaf is a free module of rank two over the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[E_{4},E_{6}]
, generated by
\mathbb{Y}^{\prime}(\tau)
and its modular derivative.

Moreover, the representation
\rho^{\prime}:\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
indeed have infinite image, because

\left\lVert\rho^{\prime}(t^{n})\right\rVert=\left\lVert\mathbb{I}_{0}\otimes\nu_{\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(t^{n})\right\rVert=\left\lVert\mathbb{I}_{0}(t^{n})\right\rVert=n,

which follows from the explicit description of
\nu_{\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
, see
[
2
, Page 7]
. Gannon in
[
5
]
gave an explicit description of the logarithmic representations of rank
2
, associated to
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and they all differ from
\rho^{\prime}
by some character of
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

We are now ready to prove the required cuspidal properties of the lifted forms.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{c}}
be an arbitrary cusp of
\mathrm{G}
and
\left\{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}|1\leq i\leq n_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\right\}
be the set of all inequivalent cusps of
\mathrm{H}
lying under
{\mathfrak{c}}.
Then we have the following.

(i)

If
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
has moderate growth at all the cusps
\left\{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}|1\leq i\leq n_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\right\},
then the lifted form
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}(\tau)
has moderate growth at
{\mathfrak{c}}
as well.

(ii)

If
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is holomorphic (or vanishes) at all the cusps
\left\{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}|1\leq i\leq n_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\right\},
then the lifted form
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}(\tau)
has same properties at the cusp
{\mathfrak{c}},
provided that the weight of
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is
0.

Proof.

For both of the parts, it is enough to prove that
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{({\mathfrak{c}})}(\tau)
satisfy the required cuspidal properties at the cusp
\infty.

Let us first prove part
(i)
. It is clear that
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\tau)
has moderate growth at all the cusps
\left\{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}|1\leq i\leq n_{\infty}\right\},
because any power of
\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}(\tau)
has the same property. Now we shall show that all the components of
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}_{0}(\tau)
have moderate growth at
\infty.
Let
\mathbb{Y}(\tau):=\mathbb{X}_{0}(g_{i,j}^{-1}\tau)
be such a component. Since
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\tau)
has moderate growth at all the cusps
\left\{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}|1\leq i\leq n_{\infty}\right\}
there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\left\lVert\mathbb{X}_{0}(\tau)\right\rVert\ll|e^{2\pi icA_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}^{-1}\tau}|
as
\text{im}(\tau)\to\infty.
It follows from Lemma
3.1
that,
\left\lVert\mathbb{Y}(\tau)\right\rVert\ll|e^{2\pi ic^{\prime}\tau}|,
for some constant
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},
as
\text{im}(\tau)\to\infty.
This shows that,
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}_{0}(\tau)
has moderate growth at
\infty.
On the other hand, any power of
\Delta_{\mathrm{G}}(\tau)
has moderate growth at
\infty
as well, and this completes the proof of part
(i).

Let us now prove part
(ii)
. To show that
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau)
is holomorphic (or vanishes) at the cusp
\infty,
we need to show that all the components are bounded (or vanishes) as
\text{im}(\tau)\to\infty.
The constant
c^{\prime}
appearing in the previous paragraph is
0
when
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is holomorphic and negative when
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is a cuspform. Moreover, it can also be seen from Lemma
3.1
that the constants
c
and
c^{\prime}
from the previous paragraph are a positive multiple of each other. Therefore
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau)
has the similar cuspidal properties as
\mathbb{X}(\tau).
This shows that the lifted form also shares the same cuspidal properties when the weight is
0.

∎

4.
Lifting of logarithmic vector-valued automorphic forms

It was proved, by the first author in
[
2
]
, that the induction of an admissible representation is admissible. In this section, we shall study the induction of non-admissible, i.e., logarithmic representations. In this regard, we have the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{c}}
be an arbitrary cusp of
\mathrm{G}
and
\{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}|1\leq i\leq n_{{\mathfrak{c}}}\}
be the set of inequivalent cusps of
\mathrm{H}
for which
\mathrm{G}\cdot{\mathfrak{c}}=\bigcup H\cdot{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}.
Then,

(i)

If
\rho(t_{i})
is not diagonalizable for some
i,
then
\widetilde{\rho}(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})
is not diagonalizable.

(ii)

In particular if
\rho
is a logarithmic representation, then
\widetilde{\rho}
is a logarithmic representation as well.

Proof.

Let us start with considering the coset representatives
\{g_{i,j}\}
of
\mathrm{H}
in
\mathrm{G}
from Section
3.1
. We first claim that, for any pair
(i,j)
, some non-trivial power of
\gamma_{i,j}=g_{i,j}^{-1}t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}g_{i,j}
is in
\mathrm{H}
. To prove this, we start by noting that there exists
n_{i,j,1}
and
n_{i,j,2}
such that
g_{i,j}^{n_{i,j,1}}\mathrm{H}=g_{i,j}^{n_{i,j,2}}\mathrm{H}
. This is because
\mathrm{H}
has a finite index in
\mathrm{G}.
In particular, for each pair
(i,j),
there exists some integer
n_{i,j}
such that
g_{i,j}^{-1}t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{n_{i,j}}g_{i,j}\in\mathrm{H}
. Let us now consider
n=\text{lcm}\left\{n_{i,j}|1\leq i\leq n_{{\mathfrak{c}}},1\leq j\leq h_{i}\right\}
. In particular,
g_{i,j}^{-1}t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{n}g_{i,j}\in\mathrm{H}
for each pair
(i,j).
Therefore,
\widetilde{\rho}(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{n})
is a block diagonal matrix where each block is of the form
\rho(g_{i,j}^{-1}t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{n}g_{i,j})
. Note that
g_{i,j}^{-1}t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{n}g_{i,j}\in\mathrm{H}
and fixes the cusp
{\mathfrak{c}}_{i},
hence it is some non-trivial power of
t_{i}.
Let us write
g_{i,j}^{-1}t_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{n}g_{i,j}=t_{i}^{m},
where
m\neq 0
is an integer. By the assumption, there exists some
i
for which
\rho(t_{i})
can be written in Jordan normal form, with a Jordan block, say
J_{\lambda}
, of size greater than
1.
In particular,
\rho(t_{i}^{m})
can be written in a block diagonal form, where one of the blocks is
J_{\lambda}^{m},
which is not diagonalizable for any integer
m\neq 0
. This completes the proof of part
(i)
.

For the proof of part

4.1.

Let us first recall the usual setup. Fix a cusp

The first author studied this when

Lemma 4.3

Let

be any arbitrary cusp of
\mathrm{G},
and

be the set of inequivalent cusps of

Then, the following statements are true.

(i)

The eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\rho}(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})
are precisely given by the set

where

is the number of distinct eigenvalues of

and

is the set of all
h_{i}^{th}

(ii)

If there is a Jordan block of size

in

associated to the eigenvalue

then there is a Jordan block in
\widetilde{\rho}(t_{{\mathfrak{c}}})

Before proving Lemma
4.3
, we need to discuss generalized permutation matrices. These matrices are square matrices where each row and column contain precisely one non-zero entry. Unlike classical permutation matrices where the non-zero entry must be
1

is isomorophic to
S_{h}\rtimes\Delta_{h}(\mathbb{R})
, where
S_{h}
is the symmetric group, and
\Delta_{h}(\mathbb{R})
be the set of diagonal matrices in
\text{GL}_{h}(\mathbb{R})

on

by permuting coordinates. With this identification, or simply by matrix multiplications, the reader may note that

\begin{pmatrix}\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace 0&&&&a\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace 1&\ddots&&&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace&&1&&0\\
\end{pmatrix}^{h}_{h\times h}=aI_{h},\penalty\ \forall a\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

Similarly, we have the following for any non-zero matrix

(5)

\begin{pmatrix}\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace 0&&&&A\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace I_{m}&\ddots&&&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace&&I_{m}&&0\\
\end{pmatrix}^{h}_{hm\times hm}=AI_{hm}.

To prove

5.
Existence of logarithmic vector-valued automorphic forms

In this section, we shall discuss the existence of vvaf associated with a given representation. To begin with, let us first recall
[
2
, Section 4]
the scalar-valued cusp form
\Delta_{\mathrm{G}}(\tau)
of non-zero weight, which is holomorphic on
\mathbb{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{G}}
and nonzero everywhere, except at
\infty
. Moreover, let
\nu_{\mathrm{H}}
be the rank
1
representation associated to
\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{1/w_{\mathrm{H}}}(\tau)

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be any Fuchsian group of the first kind, and
k
be any integer. There exists a weakly holomorphic logarithmic vvaf of weight
k,
associated with some representation of
\mathrm{G}.

Proof.

Consider the representation
\mathbb{I}:\mathrm{G}\to\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),
given by
\gamma\mapsto\gamma.
Now consider the vector-valued holomorphic function
\mathbb{X}_{0}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
given by
\tau\mapsto(\tau,1).
Note that

\mathbb{X}_{0}\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=(c\tau+d)^{-1}\mathbb{X}_{0}(\tau),\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ \left({a\atop c}{b\atop d}\right)\in\mathrm{G},\penalty\ \tau\in\mathbb{H}.

Of course, we can not conclude about the weight from here if
c=0
and
d=1
for every element in
\mathrm{G}.
However, that is not the case because
\mathrm{G}
is a Fuchsian group of the first kind. Therefore,
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
is a weakly holomorphic logarithmic vvaf of weight
-1
associated with
\mathbb{I}.
Then
\mathbb{X}_{k}(\tau):=\mathbb{X}_{0}\Delta_{\mathrm{G}}^{\frac{k+1}{w_{\mathrm{G}}}}(\tau)
is a weakly holomorphic logarithmic vvaf of weight
k
associated with the representation
\rho:=\mathbb{I}\otimes\nu_{\mathrm{G}}^{k+1}.

∎

Now one may naturally ask if given any representation
\rho:\mathrm{G}\to\text{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
can we find a non-trivial weakly-holomorphic vvaf associated to
\rho
whose components are linearly independent? Of course, the interesting question is when the components are linearly independent; otherwise, one could take a zero function. The first author confirmed this for admissible representations having a finite image, and in
[
1
, Chapter 5]
, the same is confirmed for any admissible representations with rational exponents
2
2

2

In other words, all the eigenvalues of the image of any parabolic element is rational.
, associated with any genus zero Fucshian groups of the first kind. Furthermore, in
[
1
, Chapter 8]
, explicit generators are produced for the rank
2
case when the Fuchsian group is a modular triangle group. An extension to rank

for the modular group is done by Candelori et. al. in
[
4
]
.

The main goal of this section is to study this for logarithmic vector-valued automorphic forms. More precisely, we consider the following question.

Question 5.2
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be any Fuchsian group of the first kind. Given a logarithmic representation
\rho
of
\mathrm{G}
, can we find a non-trivial logarithmic vvaf associated with
\rho
whose components are linearly independent?

For the logarithmic representations associated with the modular group, the existence could be deduced from
[
5

0
Fuchsian group of the first kind.

Before proving our main result of this section, let us quickly recall the result of
[
2
]
. As mentioned, the author assumed that the image of
\rho
has a finite image and all the eigenvalues of
\rho(\gamma)
are unitary for any parabolic element
\gamma\in\mathrm{G}.
In this case we must have that,
\mu(\cdot)
of all such eigenvalues are rational.

Now for the logarithmic case, we do not have the luxury to assume that the representation has a finite image. If
\rho
is not admissible, then there exists some parabolic element
\gamma\in\mathrm{G}
such that
\rho(\gamma)
has at least one non-trivial Jordan block, say

Suppose this block is of size
m(\lambda)\times m(\lambda).
Since it is non-trivial, we may assume that
1<m(\lambda)\leq m.
If
\rho
has finite image, then the set
\{J_{\lambda}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
must also be finite. Note that,

J_{\lambda}^{n}=\lambda^{n}(I_{m(\lambda)}+N)^{n}=\lambda^{n}\sum_{0\leq i\leq m(\lambda)}\binom{n}{i}N^{i},

where

is the corresponding nilpotent matrix. In particular, for any large

of modulus
\gg\binom{n}{m(\lambda)}.
Therefore, we can not assume that the representation has a finite image for the logarithmic case. Here we would like to give a partial answer to Question
5.2
in the following form.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G}
and
\rho
be as in Theorem
1.2

\psi
be a representation associated to
\mathrm{G}
such that
\widetilde{\rho}=\psi

\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau)
associated to
(\psi,\mathrm{G})
with linearly independent components if and only if there exists a weakly holomorphic logarithmic vvaf
\mathbb{X}(\tau)
associated to
(\rho,\mathrm{H})
with linearly independent components.

Proof.

It is enough to work with only weight
0
forms because multiplication by a scalar-valued non-zero function does not change the linear independence. Suppose that there exists
(\rho,\mathrm{H})
with an associated weakly holomorphic logarithmic vvaf
\mathbb{X}(\tau).
It follows from Theorem
1.2
that
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau)
is a weakly holomorphic logarithmic vvaf for
\text{Ind}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{G}}(\rho)=\psi.
We are now done for the if part, provided that the set
\left\{\mathbb{X}(g_{i,j}^{-1}\tau)|1\leq i\leq n_{\infty},0\leq j<h_{i}\right\}
is a linearly independent set. If not, we fix a point
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
such that

are pairwise distinct, and denote

\mathrm{H}

In general, we show that the constant

\mathrm{H}
in
\mathrm{G}.
In particular, the exponent does not change when

In
[
3
]
, the authors remarked that

Lemma 6.1

\left\lVert\widetilde{\rho}(\gamma)\right\rVert\ll_{\rho,\mathrm{G}}\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert^{\alpha},\penalty\ \forall\gamma\in\mathrm{G}.

It follows from the definition of induced representation that,

\left\lVert\widetilde{\rho}(\gamma)\right\rVert\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq d\\
\gamma_{i}\gamma\gamma_{j}^{-1}\in\mathrm{H}\end{subarray}}\left\lVert\rho(\gamma_{i}\gamma\gamma_{j}^{-1})\right\rVert.

On the other hand, it follows from the assumption on
\rho,
and the semi multiplicative property of

that
\left\lVert\rho(\gamma_{i}\gamma\gamma_{j}^{-1})\right\rVert\ll_{\rho,\mathrm{G}}\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert^{\alpha}.
∎

is a holomorphic vvaf as well. As a consequence of Lemma

be an admissible representation of
\mathrm{H}
. Let
\infty

\mathrm{G}

\left\{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}|1\leq i\leq n_{\infty}\right\}

lying under

For each such

either

Proof of Theorem

Admissible case.

In this case,
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}[n]

\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}(\tau)
at

\infty,
write

and

\widetilde{\rho}(t_{\infty})=P_{\infty}\mathrm{diag}\left(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\frac{\Lambda_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}+j}{h_{i}}}\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n_{\infty},\\
0\leq j<h_{i}\end{subarray}}P^{-1}_{\infty}.

It follows from Corollary 4.7 of

]

\left(\mathbb{X}(g^{-1}_{i,j}\tau)\right)^{t}_{0\leq j<h_{i}}={P^{\prime}}_{i}\mathrm{diag}\left(\widetilde{q}^{\frac{\Lambda_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}+j}{h_{i}}}\right)_{0\leq j<h_{i}}{P^{\prime}}_{i}^{-1}\left(\sum_{n=-M_{i,j^{\prime}}}^{\infty}\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}_{i,j^{\prime}}[n]\widetilde{q}^{n}\right)^{t}_{0\leq j^{\prime}<h_{i}},

Denoting

, and using the identity that
h_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}=h_{i}h_{\infty},
we have

(6)

\displaystyle{P^{\prime}}_{i}^{-1}\left(\mathbb{X}(g^{-1}_{i,j}\tau)\right)^{t}_{0\leq j<h_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left(\widetilde{q}^{\frac{\Lambda_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}+j}{h_{i}}}\sum_{n=-M_{i,j}}^{\infty}{\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}}_{i,j}[n]\widetilde{q}^{n}\right)^{t}_{0\leq j<h_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}{h_{\infty}}\frac{\Lambda_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}+j}{h_{i}}}\sum_{n=-M_{i,j}}^{\infty}{\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}}_{i,j}[n]e^{\frac{2n\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}{h_{\infty}}}\right)^{t}_{0\leq j<h_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}{h_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}}\Lambda_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}}\sum_{n=-M_{i,j}}^{\infty}{\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}}_{i,j}[n]e^{\frac{2(nh_{i}+j)\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}{h_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}}}\right)^{t}_{0\leq j<h_{i}}.

On the other hand,
\mathbb{X}|_{k}g_{i,j}^{-1}(\tau):=\mathbb{X}|_{0}g_{i,j}^{-1}(\tau)=\mathbb{X}(g_{i,j}^{-1}\tau)

and

\mathrm{H}.
In other words,

(7)

\displaystyle P_{i}^{-1}\mathbb{X}(g_{i,j}^{-1}\tau)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}{h_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}}\Lambda_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}}\sum_{n=-M^{\prime}_{i,j}}^{\infty}\mathbb{X}^{\prime(i,j)}[n]e^{\frac{2n\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}{h_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{i}}}},\penalty\ \forall j\leq h_{i},

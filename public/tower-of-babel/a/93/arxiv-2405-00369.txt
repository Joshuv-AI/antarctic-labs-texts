Singular velocity of the Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations near boundary in the half space


Abstract
. Local behaviors near boundary are analyzed for solutions of the Stokes and Navier-Stoke equations in the half space with localized non-smooth boundary data. We construct solutions of Stokes equations whose velocity field is not bounded near boundary away from the support of boundary data, although velocity and gradient velocity of solutions are locally square integrable. This is an improvement compared to known results in the sense that velocity field is unbounded itself, since previously constructed solutions were bounded near boundary, although their normal derivatives are singular. We also establish singular solutions and their derivatives that do not belong to L loc q L^{q}_{\rm{loc}} near boundary with q > 1 q>1 . For such examples, there corresponding pressures turn out not to be locally integrable. Similar construction via a perturbation argument is available to the Navier-Stokes equations near boundary as well. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. primary 35Q30, secondary 35B65. Keywords and phrases: Singualr velocity Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations, local regularity near boundary

Introduction

In this paper, we study local regularity of the Stokes and the Navier-Stokes equations near flat boundary.
Let
B^{+}_{r}:=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}:|x|<r,x_{n}>0\}
,
n\geq 2
. We consider the Stokes system near local boundaries.

(1.1)

u_{t}-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=0\qquad\mbox{div }u=0\quad\mbox{ in }\,Q_{2}^{+}:=B^{+}_{2}\times(0,4),

where
u
indicates the velocity of the fluid and
\pi
is the associated pressure. We emphasize that no-slip boundary condition is given only on the flat boundary, i.e.

(1.2)

u=0\quad\mbox{ on }\,\Sigma:=(B_{2}\cap\{x_{n}=0\})\times(0,4).

Similar situation can be examined for the Navier-Stokes equations, namely

(1.3)

u_{t}-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\pi=0,\qquad\mbox{div }u=0\quad\mbox{ in }\,Q_{2}^{+}

together with the boundary condition (
1.2
).

Unlike the heat equation, due to non-local effect of incompressible fluid flow, we cannot expect local smoothing property for (
1.1
) or (
1.3
) near boundary.
Indeed, the first author constructed a weak solution of (
1.1
) whose normal derivatives near boundary are unbounded. To be more precise, the singular solution, denoted by
u
, constructed in
[
11
]
is locally bounded weak solutions in
Q_{1}^{+}
, namely,

(1.4)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1}^{+})}<\infty,\qquad\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}=\infty.

In
[
12
]
, such construction was extended to the Navier-Stokes equations as well and various types of blow-up rates were established near boundary.
We remark that constructed singular solutions in
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
are due to nonlocal effect of solutions in the half-space caused by compactly supportd singular boundary data.

Meanwhile, in
[
15
]
, Seregin and S̆verák constructed a Hölder continuous solution up to boundary in the form of shear flow whose
normal derivatives are, however, unbounded near boundary in the half-space. It is remarkable that such a solution
satisfies homogeneous initial and boundary conditions and the solution, however, is not of finite energy in the half space in contrast to ones constructed in
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
.

On the other hand, a certain type of local maximal regularity was proved near boundary via a priori estimate.
More precisely, in
[
14
, Proposition 2]
it was shown in three dimensions that
for given
p,q\in(1,2]
the solution of the Stokes equations (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfies the following a priori estimate:
For any
r
with
p\leq r<\infty

(1.5)

\left\|u_{t}\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}+\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}+\left\|\nabla\pi\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}\leq C\left(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}W^{1,p}_{x}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\left\|\pi\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{1}^{+})}\right).

Furthermore, due to parabolic embedding, it follows that

(1.6)

\left\|u\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{t}{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}<\infty,\qquad 0<\alpha<2-\frac{2}{q}.

It is worth to mention that the estimates (
1.5
) is optimal, since the authors constructed in
[
8
]

an example that satisfies (
1.5
) but any higher integrability of the example fails (see also
[
12
]
).

We remark very recently that local boundary regularity of solutions was improved for the Stokes system, when boundary condition is given as Navier-boundary condition, not no-slip boundary condition (see
[
10
]
).

So far, singular solutions that has been constrructed in above references are bounded, in fact, Hölder continuous but the normal derivatives of velocity are singular near boundary in the half space.
One may wonder whether or not there exists a singular solution that is unbounded itself near boundary.
Our main objective is to construct a weak solution of the Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations sucht that velocity field is singular near boundary in the half space (the definition of weak solutions of Stokes equations is given in section
3
).
Furthermore, we are also interested in establishing an example so that velocity fileld is bounded but it does not belong to
{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}
, class of Hölder continuous functions, with any exponent
\alpha>0
near boundary.
We remark that since we are looking for solutions that are not Hölder continuous functions itself, due to above a priori estimate (
1.5
), the associated pressure is unlikely to belong to
L^{p}_{\rm loc}
,
p>1
, which turns out to be the case in examples of our construction.
Our main resutls will be precisely stated in the next section.

&\approx\max(1,\left|t-1\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi(|t-1|)),\end{split}

where we used (
2.8
).

On the other hand, we note that

vanishes for

. Furthermore, it follows from (

) that

\displaystyle w^{N}_{i}(x,t)=2\int_{A}D_{x_{i}}N(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},x_{n})g_{n}(y^{\prime},t)dy^{\prime}=2T_{i}g^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(x^{\prime})\phi(1-t),

where

Since
T_{i}g^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(x^{\prime})
is positive and finite, we have

for

and

, which implies via (

\displaystyle w^{N}_{i}(x,t)\approx\phi(1-t){\bf I}_{\left\{t<1\right\}}.

It remains to estimate

.
Since

for

,
we note that

for

and

. Using (

) and (

), for

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{i}^{\mathcal{B}}(x,t)&=\int_{0}^{t}D_{x_{n}}\Gamma_{1}(x_{n},t-s)g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(s)\int_{A}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}-z^{\prime},t)D_{z_{i}}N(z^{\prime},0)dz^{\prime}g^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}ds\\

We separately treat (
4.4
) for the cases that
t<1
and for

.

\bullet

(The case

) and (
(ii)

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{i}^{\mathcal{B}}(x,t)&\approx-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{x_{n}}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{4(t-s)}}\phi(1-s)ds\\

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|w_{i}^{\mathcal{B}}(x,t)\right|\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054pt1&\mbox{if}\,\,\phi(t)=\left|\ln t\right|^{-1}\\
\phi(1-t)&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

We complete the proof of (

) for

.

4.2.

\bullet

2.31

2.33

(ii)

\bullet

) and (

x_{n}<\frac{1}{2}
and
1-t<t_{1}
,

(4.37)

\displaystyle p(x,t)\approx-c\phi^{\prime}(1-t).

We prove the first part of (
2.46
) for
t<1
.

\bullet

(The case
1<t
)

Since
p_{1}(x,t)=p_{3}(x,t)=0
for
t>1
,
Lemma
2.4
implies that

(4.38)

\displaystyle\begin{split}p_{22}&=-2\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Delta^{\prime}g_{n}(y^{\prime},s)\big(D_{x_{n}}N(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},x_{n})+(I+J_{1})(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},x_{n},t)\big)dy^{\prime}ds\\
&\leq c\int_{0}^{1}\big(x_{n}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+(t-s)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{8(t-s)}}\big)\phi(1-s)ds\\
&\leq c\big(1+x_{n}\max(1,(t-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi(t-1))\big)\end{split}

and

(4.39)

\displaystyle\begin{split}p_{21}&=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\phi(1-s)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}-z^{\prime},t-s)D_{x_{n}}N(z^{\prime},x_{n})dz^{\prime}g_{n}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}ds\\
&\approx\int_{0}^{1}\big(x_{n}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}+(t-s)^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{(t-s)}}\big)\phi(1-s)ds\\
&\approx x_{n}(t-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi(t-1)+1.\end{split}

Hence, we have

(4.40)

\displaystyle c_{1}x_{n}(t-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi(t-1)-c_{2}\leq p_{21}(x,t)\leq c_{3}x_{n}(t-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi(t-1)+c_{4},

which implies the second part of (
2.46
) for
t>1
, and thus we complete the proof of (
2.46
).
∎

5.

To prove Propositiion
2.10
, we consider the following equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptW_{t}-\Delta W+\nabla Q=f\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times(0,T),\\
\vskip 5.69054pt{\rm div}\,W=0\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times(0,T),\\

where

.
We recall some known results for the system (
5

Proposition 5.1
.

(
[
7
, Proposition 2.3]
)
Let

. Suppose that
f={\rm div}\,{\mathcal{F}}
with
F\in L^{q}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}))
,
{\mathcal{F}}|_{x_{n}=0}=0
.
Then, the solution
W
of (
5
) satisfies

(5.4)

\displaystyle\|\nabla W\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}))}\leq c\|{\mathcal{F}}\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}))}.

Proposition 5.2
.

(
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
)
Let
1<p,q<\infty
. Assume that
f={\rm div}{\mathcal{F}}
with
{\mathcal{F}}\in L^{q_{1}}(0,\infty;L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}))
, where
p_{1},q_{1}
satisfy

n/p_{1}+2/q_{1}=n/p+2/q+1
with
q_{1}\leq q
and
p_{1}\leq p
. Then, there is the unique
very weak solution
W\in L^{q}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}))
of the Stokes equations
(
5
) such that
W

satisfies

\|W\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}))}\leq c\|{\mathcal{F}}\|_{L^{q_{1}}(0,\infty;L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}))}.

Proposition 5.3
.

[
3
, Theorem 2.2]

Let
n+2<p<\infty
. Let
f={\rm div}\,{\mathcal{F}}
with
{\mathcal{F}}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times(0,T))
. Then, there exists unique very weak solution,

Proposition 5.4
.

[
9
, Proposition 3.4]

Suppose that

with
1<p_{1},q_{1}<\infty
.
If

and
\frac{2}{q_{1}}+\frac{n}{p_{1}}-1\leq\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}
,

(5.5)

\|Q\|_{L^{q}(0,1;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}))}<c\|f\|_{L^{q_{1}}(0,1;L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}))}.

Our aim is to establish the existence of solution
v
for
(
2.52
) satisfying
v\in L^{2q}(({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty))

and
\nabla v\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty))
for fixed
q>\frac{n+2}{2}
. In order to do that, we
consider the iterative scheme for (
2.52
), which is
given as follows: For a positive integer
m\geq 1

\displaystyle v^{m+1}_{t}-\Delta v^{m+1}+\nabla q^{m+1}=-{\rm div}\,\left(v^{m}\otimes v^{m}+v^{m}\otimes U+U\otimes v^{m}+U\otimes U\right),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad{\rm div}\,v^{m+1}=0

on

5.1
and Proposition

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla v^{2}\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times(0,T))}&\leq c\||U|^{2}\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times(0,T))}\leq c\|U\|^{2}_{L^{2q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times(0,T))}\leq c\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2q}},\\

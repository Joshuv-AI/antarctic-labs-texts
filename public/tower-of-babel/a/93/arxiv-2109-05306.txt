Laplacian State Transfer on Graphs with an Edge Perturbation Between Twin Vertices
Hiranmoy Pal

Abstract
We consider quantum state transfer relative to the Laplacian matrix of a graph. Let N ⁡ ( u ) N(u) denote the set of all neighbors of a vertex u u in a graph G G . A pair of vertices u u and v v are called twin vertices of G G provided N ⁡ ( u ) ∖ { v } = N ⁡ ( v ) ∖ { u } N\left(u\right)\setminus\left\{v\right\}=N\left(v\right)\setminus\left\{u\right\} . We investigate the existence of quantum state transfer between a pair of twin vertices in a graph when the edge between the vertices is perturbed. We find that removal of any set of pairwise non-adjacent edges from a complete graph with a number of vertices divisible by 4 4 results Laplacian perfect state transfer (or LPST) at π 2 \frac{\pi}{2} between the end vertices of every edge removed. Further, we show that all Laplacian integral graphs with a pair of twin vertices exhibit LPST when the edge between the vertices is perturbed. In contrast, we conclude that LPST can be achieved in every complete graph between the end vertices of any number of suitably perturbed non-adjacent edges. The results are further generalized to obtain a family of edge perturbed circulant graphs exhibiting Laplacian pretty good state transfer (or LPGST) between twin vertices. A subfamily of which is also identified to admit LPST at π 2 \frac{\pi}{2} . Keywords: Perfect state transfer, Pretty good state transfer, Circulant Graph, Spectra of graphs. MSC: 05C50, 15A16, 81P40.

LPST on Edge Perturbed Graphs

Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
\left\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}\right\}
, and let the Laplacian matrix of
G
be
L
. If
u_{a}
and
u_{b}
are two distinct non-adjacent vertices of
G
then Laplacian matrix of
G+\left\{u_{a},u_{b}\right\}
, where
u_{a}
and
u_{b}
are joined by a new edge, becomes

L^{+}=L+\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)^{T},

where
{\bf e}_{a}
and
{\bf e}_{b}
are the characteristic vectors corresponding to
u_{a}
and
u_{b}
, respectively. Again, if
u_{a}
and
u_{b}
are two adjacent vertices of
G
then the Laplacian matrix of the edge perturbed graph
G-\left\{u_{a},u_{b}\right\}
is

L^{-}=L-\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)^{T}.

Observe that
L^{+}
and
L^{-}
are perturbations of the Laplacian matrix
L
with the rank one matrix
M=\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)^{T}
. Here we consider a more general perturbation

L^{\alpha}=L+\alpha M,~\alpha\in{\mathbbm{R}}.

The matrix
L^{\alpha}
can be realised as the Laplacian matrix of
G+\alpha\left\{u_{a},u_{b}\right\}
where the weight of the edge between
u_{a}
and
u_{b}
in
G
is increased by
\alpha
.
Let
N(u)
denote the set of all neighbors of a vertex
u
in
G
. We prove the following result mentioned in
[
24
]
. Notably, the condition in the following Lemma guarantees the existence of an automorphism of the graph
G
swapping
u_{a}
,
u_{b}
and fixing other vertices.

Lemma 1
.

If
N\left(u_{a}\right)\setminus\left\{u_{b}\right\}=N\left(u_{b}\right)\setminus\left\{u_{a}\right\}
then the matrices
L
and
M
commute.

Proof.

Enough to consider the following cases as the problem is symmetric in
a
and
b
. Since the Laplacian is a symmetric matrix, we observe that

{\bf e}_{a}^{T}LM{\bf e}_{a}={\bf e}_{a}^{T}L\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)={\bf e}_{a}^{T}L{\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{a}^{T}L{\bf e}_{b}={\bf e}_{a}^{T}L{\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}^{T}L{\bf e}_{a}={\bf e}_{a}^{T}ML{\bf e}_{a}.

Further, for any
k\notin\left\{a,b\right\}
, we have
{\bf e}_{k}^{T}LM{\bf e}_{k}=0={\bf e}_{k}^{T}ML{\bf e}_{k}.
Now the degrees of
u_{a}
and
u_{b}
are equal, and therefore
{\bf e}_{a}^{T}L{\bf e}_{a}={\bf e}_{b}^{T}L{\bf e}_{b}
. Hence we have

\displaystyle{\bf e}_{a}^{T}LM{\bf e}_{b}={\bf e}_{a}^{T}L\left(-e_{a}+{\bf e}_{b}\right)=-{\bf e}_{a}^{T}Le_{a}+{\bf e}_{a}^{T}L{\bf e}_{b}=\left({\bf e}_{a}^{T}-{\bf e}_{b}^{T}\right)L{\bf e}_{b}={\bf e}_{a}^{T}ML{\bf e}_{b}.

For an arbitrary
k\notin\left\{a,b\right\}
, notice that
{\bf e}_{a}^{T}LM{\bf e}_{k}=0
. Since
N\left(u_{a}\right)\setminus\left\{u_{b}\right\}=N\left(u_{b}\right)\setminus\left\{u_{a}\right\},
we have

{\bf e}_{a}^{T}ML{\bf e}_{k}=\left({\bf e}_{a}^{T}-{\bf e}_{b}^{T}\right)L{\bf e}_{k}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we determine the transition matrix of the edge perturbed graph as follows. Recall that
M=\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)^{T}
and therefore

M^{2}=\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)^{T}\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)^{T}=2\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)^{T}=2M.

In general, we obtain
M^{k}=2^{k-1}M,
whenever
k\geq 1.
Observe that

\displaystyle\exp{\left(-i\alpha tM\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\left(-i\alpha t\right)^{k}}{k!}M^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\left(-i\alpha t\right)^{k}}{k!}2^{k-1}M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+\frac{1}{2}\left(\exp{\left(-2i\alpha t\right)}-1\right)M.

If
L
and

commute then

\displaystyle\exp{\left(-itL^{\alpha}\right)}=\exp{\left(-it\left(L+\alpha M\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp{\left(-itL\right)}\exp{\left(-i\alpha tM\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp{\left(-itL\right)}\left[I+\frac{1}{2}\left(\exp{\left(-2i\alpha t\right)}-1\right)M\right],

which determines the transition matrix of the perturbed graph in terms of the transition matrix of the unperturbed graph. We incorporate the above observation as follows.

Proposition 1
.

G
be a graph on

. Suppose that

and
u_{b}

and

3
LPGST on Edge Perturbed Graphs

In the previous section, we have considered Laplacian state transfer between twin vertices in a graph, and found conditions under which these graphs exhibit perfect state transfer. The conclusions can further be generalized to have LPGST in certain graphs. The following inference are immediate from the proof of Theorem
3
, and therefore we omit the proof for convenience.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose the conditions of Proposition
1
is satisfied. If the unperturbed graph
G
exhibits Laplacian pretty good state transfer between the vertices
u_{p}
and
u_{q}
with respect to the sequence
\tau_{k}\in{\mathbbm{R}}
, then so does the edge perturbed graph with Laplacian
L^{\alpha}
provided one of the following holds:

1.

p,q\in\left\{a,b\right\}
with
\alpha\tau_{k}\in\pi{\mathbbm{Z}}
,

2.

p,q\not\in\left\{a,b\right\}
.

Moreover, if
p\in\left\{a,b\right\}
and
q\not\in\left\{a,b\right\}
, then there exists no Laplacian pretty good state transfer in the perturbed graph between
u_{p}
and
u_{q}
.

Next we include another conclusion which can be deduced immediately from the proof of Theorem
4
.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose the conditions of Proposition
1
is satisfied. Let the unperturbed graph is almost periodic at
u_{p}
with respect to the sequence
\tau_{k}\in{\mathbbm{R}}
. Then the following holds:

1.

If
p\in\left\{a,b\right\}
with
2\alpha\tau_{k}\in\pi(2{\mathbbm{Z}}+1)
, then the edge perturbed graph exhibits Laplacian pretty good state transfer between
u_{a}
and
u_{b}
with respect to
\tau_{k}
.

2.

If
p\not\in\left\{a,b\right\}
, then the edge perturbed graph is almost periodic at the vertex
u_{p}
with respect to
\tau_{k}
.

Now we use Theorem
5
and Theorem
6
to find a class of edge perturbed circulant graphs exhibiting LPGST. Before that we revisit the proof of Theorem 2.8 in
[
22
]
to establish the following result that plays a crucial role in classifying LPGST in circulant graphs. We use the resources as placed in
[
22
]
and avoid unnecessary repetitions to prove the result. It is noteworthy that the continuous-time quantum walk relative to adjacency matrix has been considered in the following.

Theorem 7
.

Let
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
,
n=2^{k}
and consider the circulant graph
Cay\left({\mathbbm{Z}}_{n},S\right).
If each divisor
d
of
n
satisfies
\left|S\cap S_{n}(d)\right|\equiv 0\pmod{4}
then
Cay\left({\mathbbm{Z}}_{n},S\right)
is almost periodic with respect to a sequence in
\left(4{\mathbbm{Z}}+1\right)\frac{\pi}{2}
.

Proof.

Recall that the eigenvalues of the cycle
C_{n}
are

\lambda_{l}=2\cos{\left(\frac{2l\pi}{n}\right)},~l=0,1,\ldots,n-1.

Now the distinct positive eigenvalues of
C_{n}
are linearly independent over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
as appears in the proof of Theorem 2.8 in
[
22
]
. For
1\leq l\leq\frac{n}{4}-1
, let us choose
\alpha_{l}=-\frac{\lambda_{l}}{4}
. By the Kronecker approximation theorem, for
\delta>0
there exist
q,m_{1},\ldots,m_{\frac{n}{4}-1}\in{\mathbbm{Z}}
such that for
l=1,\ldots,\frac{n}{4}-1

\displaystyle\left|q\lambda_{l}-m_{l}-\alpha_{l}\right|<\frac{\delta}{2n\pi},\;\emph{i.e,}\;\left|\left((4q+1)\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\lambda_{l}-2m_{l}\pi\right|<\frac{\delta}{n}.

(8)

Note that
\lambda_{l}=-\lambda_{\frac{n}{2}-l}=-\lambda_{\frac{n}{2}+l}=\lambda_{n-l}
holds for
1\leq l\leq\frac{n}{4}-1
, and

\lambda_{0}=2,~\lambda_{\frac{n}{4}}=0,~\lambda_{\frac{n}{2}}=-2\text{ and }\lambda_{\frac{3n}{4}}=0.

If
\theta_{l}
denotes the eigenvalues of
Cay\left({\mathbbm{Z}}_{n},S\right)
then

\theta_{l}=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{s\in S}\lambda_{ls}=\sum\limits_{d\mid n}\frac{1}{2}\left[\sum\limits_{s\in S\cap S_{n}(d)}\lambda_{ls}\right].

Since each divisor
d~\left(\neq\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{4}\right)
of
n
satisfy
\left|S\cap S_{n}(d)\right|\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, using Equation
8
and the triangle inequality, we obtain that for
\delta>0
there exists
t\in\frac{\pi}{2}\left(4{\mathbbm{Z}}+1\right)
so that for each
l
there exists an integer
l^{\prime\prime}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\theta_{l}t-2l^{\prime\prime}\pi\right|<\delta.

The uniform continuity of exponential function implies that for
\epsilon>0
, there exists
t\in\frac{\pi}{2}\left(4{\mathbbm{Z}}+1\right)
such that
\left|\exp{\left[-i\theta_{l}t\right]}-1\right|<\epsilon.
If
H_{S}(t)
is the transition matrix relative to the adjacency matrix of
Cay\left({\mathbbm{Z}}_{n},S\right)
then we obtain the following as given in the proof of Theorem 2.8 in
[
22
]
.

\displaystyle\left|\left[H_{S}(t)\right]_{0,0}-1\right|=\frac{1}{n}\left|\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(\exp{\left[-i\theta_{l}t\right]}-1\right)\right|<\epsilon.

Hence
Cay\left({\mathbbm{Z}}_{n},S\right)
is almost periodic with respect to a sequence in
\frac{\pi}{2}\left(4{\mathbbm{Z}}+1\right)
as concluded in the proof of Theorem 2.8 in
[
22
]
.
∎

7
also holds true when considering continuous-time quantum walk relative to the Laplacian matrix. Now suppose
k\in{\mathbbm{N}},

n=2^{k}
, and consider a circulant graph
Cay\left({\mathbbm{Z}}_{n},S\right)
. It can be observed that the vertices
0
and
\frac{n}{2}
are twin vertices of
Cay\left({\mathbbm{Z}}_{n},S\right)
if and only if
S=\frac{n}{2}-S
. In fact, if
S=\frac{n}{2}-S
then for each
x\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}
the pair of vertices
x
and
\frac{n}{2}+x
are twin vertices of

.

Corollary 3
.

Let
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
,
n=2^{k}
and consider a circulant graph
Cay\left({\mathbbm{Z}}_{n},S\right).
Suppose
S=\frac{n}{2}-S
and each divisor

n
satisfies
\left|S\cap S_{n}(d)\right|\equiv 0\pmod{4}.
If a new edge is added between a pair of twin vertices in

\tau_{k}\in\frac{\pi}{2}\left(4{\mathbbm{Z}}+1\right).

Since

and

By Theorem
7

Auxiliary open quantum system for the Floquet quantum master equation
Fei Liu

Abstract
By directly using the probability formulas of quantum trajectories, we construct an auxiliary open quantum system for a periodically driven open quantum system whose dynamics is governed by the Floquet quantum master equation. This auxiliary system can generate a quantum trajectory ensemble that is consistent with the canonical quantum trajectory ensemble. We find that, at a long time limit, though the Lindblad operators are modified, the coherent dynamics of the auxiliary system is the same as that of the original system. A periodically driven two-level quantum system is used to illustrate this construction.

;

;

Generally, microcanonical trajectory ensembles can be regarded as a set of trajectories

In contrast, in a canonical trajectory ensemble, constraints are imposed on the trajectory ensemble-averaged values of observables. Although these two ensembles seem to be very distinct, at long time limits and under certain conditions, they have been rigorously proven to be equivalent by using large deviation theory

Recently, Carollo et al

It has been well established that the quantum master equations can be unraveled into quantum trajectories and, in particular, that these trajectories have a classic probability interpretation

;
Carmichael 1993
;
Wiseman and Milburn 2010
;
Liu 2018

;
Cuetara et al. 2015
;
Gelbwaser-Klimovsky et al. 2015

;
Kohler et al. 1997
;
Breuer and Petruccione 1997
;
Alicki et al. 2006

II
Floquet quantum master equation and trajectory unraveling

Assume that a periodically modulated quantum system interacts with a heat bath and that the inverse temperature of the heat bath is
\beta
. The Hamiltonian of the quantum system is
H(t)
and
H\left(t+2\pi/\Omega\right)=H(t)
, where
\Omega
is the driving frequency. According to the Floquet theorem
Zeldovich 1967
;
Shirley 1965
, the Hamiltonian satisfies an eigenvalue equation:

\displaystyle{\cal H}(t)|u_{n}(t)\rangle=\epsilon_{n}|u_{n}(t)\rangle,

(1)

where
{\cal H}(t)=H(t)-i\hbar\partial_{t}
is the Floquet Hamiltonian and
\epsilon_{n}
and
|u_{n}(t)\rangle
(
n
=
1,\cdots,N
) are the quasi-energy and Floquet bases, respectively. Because the quasi-energy
\epsilon_{n}-q\hbar\Omega
with basis
\exp(-iq\Omega t)|u_{n}(t)\rangle
is also the solution of Eq. (
1
), where
q
is an arbitrary integer, we restrict the quasi-energies in a zone of size
\hbar\Omega
. Under the assumption of a weak system-bath coupling condition and appropriate time-scale separation, the evolution of the reduced density matrix of the quantum system
\rho(t)
can be described by the Floquet quantum master equation
Grifoni and Hänggi 1998
;
Breuer and Petruccione 1997
;
Alicki et al. 2006
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho(t)={\cal L}(t)[\rho(t)]=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H(t),\rho(t)]+D(t)[\rho(t)].

(2)

The
D(t)
term in the generator
{\cal L}(t)
represents dissipation and dephasing due to the interaction between the system and the heat bath and is expressed as

\displaystyle D(t)[\rho]=\sum_{\omega}r(\omega)\left[A(\omega,t)\rho A^{\dagger}(\omega,t)-\frac{1}{2}\left\{A^{\dagger}(\omega,t)A(\omega,t),\rho\right\}\right],

where the summation is performed with respect to all possible Bohr frequencies
\omega
, which equal
(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})/\hbar+q\Omega
, and
q
is a certain integer. The Bohr frequencies may be positive or negative but always appear in pairs. In the same equation,
A(\omega,t)
and
A^{\dagger}(\omega,t)
are the Lindblad operators and are related by
A^{\dagger}(\omega,t)=A(-\omega,t)
due to the Hermitian characteristics of the interaction Hamiltonian. Note that the Lindblad operators are also the eigenoperators of the Floquet Hamiltonian
Breuer and Petruccione 1997
:

\displaystyle[{\cal H}(t),A(\omega,t)]=-\hbar\omega A(\omega,t),\hskip 5.69046pt[{\cal H}(t),A^{\dagger}(\omega,t)]=\hbar\omega A^{\dagger}(\omega,t).

(3)

Because the heat bath is always in the thermal state with the inverse temperature
\beta
, the Fourier transformation
r(\omega)
of the correlation function of the heat bath satisfies the Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (KMS) condition
Breuer et al. 2000
:
r(-\omega)=r(\omega)\exp(-\beta\hbar\omega)
.

Eq. (
2
) can be unraveled into the dynamics of individual quantum systems
Carmichael 1993
;
Plenio and Knight 1998
;
Breuer et al. 2000
;
Wiseman and Milburn 2010
. The evolution of each system is alternately composed of continuous processes and discrete random jumps. Assume that the jumps occur at time
t_{i}
with Bohr frequency
\omega_{i}
, where
i=1,\cdots,M
and
M
denotes the total number of jumps. When the evolution ends at time
T
, a quantum trajectory is generated and is denoted as
\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M}=\{\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{M}\}
. Further assuming that the density matrixes of these individual quantum systems at time
t
(
\leq T
) are
\widetilde{\rho}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},t)
, the solution of Eq. (
2
) is equal to a quantum trajectory ensemble average:

\displaystyle\rho(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumint_{M=0}^{\infty}{\cal D}(t)\hskip 2.84544ptp(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},t){\widetilde{\rho}}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},t),

where the sum-integral symbol means that we sum with respect to all possible quantum trajectories and integrate over all possible times
Liu and Xi 2016
;
Liu 2018
;
Liu and Su 2020
,

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\rho}}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},t)=\frac{1}{p(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},t)}G_{t,t_{M}}\left(J_{t_{M}}\left(G_{t_{M},t_{M-1}}\left(\cdots J_{t_{1}}\left(G_{t_{1},0}\left(\rho_{0}\right)\right)\cdots\right)\right)\right),

(13)

where
\rho_{0}
is the density matrix at initial time
0
and the denominator
p(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},t)
is the probability distribution of observing the quantum trajectory
\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M}
, which is simply equal to the trace of the numerator.
In Eq. (
13
), the symbols
G
and
J
denote superoperators, and their action regions are explicitly indicated by the round brackets. The superoperator
G_{t_{i},t_{i-1}}={\cal T}_{-}\exp[\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}d\tau{L}_{0}(\tau)]
and its generator is

\displaystyle L_{0}(t)[O]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H(t),O]-\frac{1}{2}\sum r(\omega)\left\{A^{\dagger}(\omega,t)A(\omega,t),O\right\},

(14)

where
{\cal T}_{-}
is the time-ordering operator.
J_{t_{i}}(O)
is a shorthand expression of the jump superoperator
r(\omega_{i})A(\omega_{i},t_{i})OA^{\dagger}(\omega_{i},t_{i})
.

The quantum trajectory ensemble that we are interested in is as follows. At initial time
0
, the individual quantum systems are in one of the Floquet pure states
|u_{m}(0)\rangle\langle u_{m}(0)|
, which is randomly selected based on a probability distribution
p_{m}
,
m=1,\cdots,N
. That is, the quantum ensemble is initially in a mixed state:
\rho_{0}=\sum_{m=1}^{N}p_{m}|u_{m}(0)\rangle\langle u_{m}(0)|
. The quantum system evolves, and a quantum trajectory
\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M}
is recorded. At the end time
T
, the system is measured in the Floquet basis, and a pure state
|u_{n}(T)\rangle\langle u_{n}(T)|
is obtained. It is not difficult to argue that, if the joint probability distribution of observing the quantum trajectory with the special initial and terminal bases is
p_{m,n}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T)=p_{m}p_{m|n}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T)
, the conditional probability distribution equals
Liu and Xi 2016
;
Liu 2018

\displaystyle p_{m|n}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T)=\langle u_{n}(T)|G_{T,t_{M}}\left(J_{t_{M}}\left(G_{t_{M},t_{M-1}}\left(\cdots J_{t_{1}}\left(G_{t_{1},0}\left(|u_{m}(0)\rangle\langle u_{m}(0)|\right)\right)\cdots\right)\right)\right)|u_{n}(T)\rangle.

(15)

III
Canonical quantum trajectory ensemble

In this paper, we investigate canonical quantum trajectory ensembles
Garrahan et al. 2009
;
Chetrite and Touchette 2013
;
Chetrite and Touchette 2014
. To define such an ensemble, we choose a time-integrated observable of interest, namely, the stochastic heat current along quantum trajectories. According to the interpretation of quantum measurements
Breuer and Petruccione 1997
;
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
;
Liu 2018
, the occurrence of jump events along a quantum trajectory
\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M}=\{\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{M}\}
indicates that the quantum system exchanges quanta
\hbar\omega_{i}
with the heat bath. From a thermodynamic point of view, these quanta represent the discrete heat released to the environment
Breuer 2003
;
Roeck and Maes 2006
;
Crooks 2008
;
Horowitz 2012
;
Hekking and Pekola 2013
;
Manzano et al. 2015
;
Liu and Xi 2016
. Hence, given a quantum trajectory
\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M}
with duration
T
, the heat current is equal to
j=Q_{T}/T
, and the total heat production is

\displaystyle Q_{T}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M})=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\hbar\omega_{i}.

(16)

Note that our object of interest is different from the average rate of quantum jumps, which was the focus of Carollo et al.
Carollo et al. 2018
. Nevertheless, the extension of our formulas to the latter case is direct. Given the observable, in a canonical quantum trajectory ensemble, the probability distribution of the quantum trajectory
\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M}
with the initial Floquet basis
|u_{m}(0)\rangle
and terminal Floquet basis
|u_{n}(T)\rangle
is
Garrahan et al. 2009
;
Chetrite and Touchette 2013
;
Chetrite and Touchette 2014

\displaystyle p_{m,n}({\chi},\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T)=\frac{1}{\Phi_{T}(\chi)}p_{m,n}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T)e^{\chi Q_{T}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M})},

(17)

where the normalized factor
\Phi_{T}(\chi)
is the moment generating function,

\displaystyle\Phi_{T}(\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=1}^{N}\sumint_{M=0}^{\infty}{\cal D}(t)\hskip 2.84544ptp_{m,n}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T)e^{\chi Q_{T}(\overrightarrow{\omega})}.

IV.2
Long time limit case

Although the previous arguments are generally valid and the equations are formally correct, it is not very clear whether Eq. (
34
) always achieves a solution that can satisfy both the initial and the terminal conditions of Eq. (
31
). In addition, these results do not depend on any special properties of Floquet open quantum systems. Now we focus on the long time limit case. An interesting feature of these open quantum systems is that, at a long time limit, Eq. (
2
) evolves into a periodic limit cycle
\rho(T)=\rho(T+2\pi/\Omega)

Breuer et al. 2000
;
Szczygielski et al. 2013
;
Grifoni and Hänggi 1998
and

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\rho(T)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}P_{n}|u_{n}(T)\rangle\langle u_{n}(T)|,

(37)

where
P_{n}
,
n=1,\cdots,N
, is the stationary probability distribution, which is uniquely determined by the generator of Eq. (
2
). Importantly, under the same situation, the stochastic heat (
16
) obeys the large deviation principle
Touchette 2008
;
Gasparinetti et al. 2014
;
Cuetara et al. 2015
;
Liu and Su 2020
:
p(j)\asymp\exp[-TI(j)]
, where the symbol
\asymp
denotes an asymptotically exponential approximation and
I(j)
is the rate function. The scaled cumulant generating function is

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\Lambda_{T}(\chi)=\Lambda(\chi),

(38)

where the time-independent
\Lambda(\chi)
is the maximum eigenvalue of the generator
{\cal L}_{\chi}(t)
.
The rate function and scaled cumulant generating function are related
by the Legendre transform,

\displaystyle I(j)=\max_{\chi}\{j\chi-\Lambda(\chi)\}.

(39)

Appendix A explains Eq. (
37
) and the large deviation principle.
Hence, we introduce the logarithmic equivalence between the canonical quantum trajectory ensemble and the trajectory ensemble generated by the auxiliary quantum system as that in classical stochastic systems

;
Jack and Sollich 2010
;
Chetrite and Touchette 2013
;
Chetrite and Touchette 2014

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\ln\frac{p^{\prime}_{m,n}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T)}{p_{m,n}({\chi},\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T)}\rightarrow 0.

(40)

Correspondingly, the conditions (
31
) are relaxed to

\displaystyle l_{\chi}(0)|u_{m}(0)\rangle\propto|u_{m}(0)\rangle\hskip 5.69046pt{\rm and}\hskip 5.69046ptl_{\chi}(T)|u_{n}(T)\rangle\propto|u_{n}(T)\rangle.

(41)

Then, the relationship between the moment generating functions of the auxiliary and original quantum systems can be intuitively obtained as

\displaystyle{\Phi}^{\prime}_{T}(\chi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=1}^{N}\sumint_{M=0}^{\infty}{\cal D}t\hskip 2.84544pte^{\chi^{\prime}Q_{T}(\overrightarrow{\omega})}p^{\prime}_{m,n}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Phi_{T}(\chi)}\sum_{m,n=1}^{N}\sumint_{M=0}^{\infty}{\cal D}t\hskip 2.84544pte^{(\chi^{\prime}+\chi)Q_{T}(\overrightarrow{\omega})}\frac{p^{\prime}_{m,n}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T)}{p_{m,n}(\chi,\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T)}p_{m,n}(\overrightarrow{\omega}_{M},T).

\displaystyle{\Lambda}^{\prime}(\chi^{\prime})=\Lambda(\chi^{\prime}+\chi)-\Lambda(\chi).

(60)

This agrees with the crucial result obtained by Carolla et al. for the time-independent quantum master equations

;

\displaystyle l_{\chi}^{2}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}l^{2}_{n}(\chi)|u_{n}(t)\rangle\langle u_{n}(t)|.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(i\hbar\partial_{t}|u_{n}(t)\rangle+\epsilon_{n}|u_{n}(t)\rangle)\langle u_{n}(t)|+H.c

\displaystyle H(t).

1

\delta

\langle\langle u_{m}|A|u_{n}\rangle\rangle_{q}
is the

\displaystyle\langle\langle u_{m}|A|u_{n}\rangle\rangle_{q}=\frac{\Omega}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi/\Omega}\langle u_{m}(t)|A|u_{n}(t)\rangle e^{iq\Omega t}.

(65)

Using this expression and performing a straightforward argument, we find that the square bracket term in Eq. (
33

l_{\chi}(t)
is also diagonal, we conclude that the sum of the third term and its Hermitian conjugation is exactly zero.
In addition, we can also verify that the Lindblad operators

and
A^{\prime{\dagger}}(\omega,t)
of the auxiliary quantum system are still the eigenoperators of the Floquet Hamiltonian
{\cal H}(t)
.

Before closing this section, we want to point out that the scaled cumulant generating function
\Lambda^{\prime}(\chi^{\prime})
of the auxiliary quantum system satisfies a matrix equation analogous to Eq. (

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bf R^{\prime}}(\chi^{\prime})\overrightarrow{l^{\prime 2}}(\chi^{\prime})=\Lambda^{\prime}(\chi^{\prime})\overrightarrow{l^{\prime 2}}(\chi^{\prime}),

(66)

where the matrix
\widetilde{\bf R^{\prime}}(\chi^{\prime})
is determined by the generator
{\cal L}^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}}(t)

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigate the construction of an auxiliary open quantum system that can generate a canonical quantum trajectory ensemble. The dynamics of the original quantum system is governed by the Floquet quantum master equation. The probability formula of the quantum trajectory is explicitly used. We see that the theory on quantum trajectory ensembles is highly analogous to the theory on classical trajectory ensembles. Since most of the studies in the literature are concerned about trajectory ensembles conditioned on simple time-integrated observables, it shall be interesting to explore complex quantities, e.g., stochastic efficiency.

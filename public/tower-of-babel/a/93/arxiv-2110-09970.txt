Graphs with all holes the same length
Linda Cook, Jake Horsfield, Myriam Preissmann, Cléophée Robin, Paul Seymour, Ni Luh Dewi Sintiari, Nicolas Trotignon, Kristina Vušković

Abstract
A graph is ℓ \ell -holed if all its induced cycles of length at least four have length exactly ℓ \ell . We give a complete description of the ℓ \ell -holed graphs for each ℓ ≥ 7 \ell\geq 7 .



1
Introduction

A
hole
in a graph is an induced cycle of length at least four (graphs in this paper are finite and have no loops or
parallel edges).
Berge graphs and even-hole-free graphs have decomposition theorems that are deep and useful (see
[
3
,
6
]
), but not fully
satisfactory,
and we do not know explicit constructions that will generate all the graphs of either type. What if we restrict the lengths of holes
much more severely; can we then give explicit constructions?
In this direction, the simplest class is the class where holes of all lengths are excluded. This is the class of chordal graphs,
and it has a structural description that is fully understood
[
7
]
. The next simplest is to exclude holes of all lengths
except one, and this is what we study here. As we will see, the description is complex, but when the permitted hole length is at
least seven, our description fully describes the structure of
all the graphs in the class. Incidentally, when all holes have length five, we do not know a complete description, but
such graphs were studied
[
12
]
in the context of algebraic combinatorics and commutative algebra.

If
\ell\geq 4
is an integer, we say a graph is
\ell
-holed
if all its holes have length exactly
\ell
.
How can we make the most general
\ell
-holed graph?
There are a few cases that come to mind immediately: chordal graphs; cycles; pyramids in which
every path
from apex to base has the same length; thetas in which every path between the two ends of the theta has the same length; and prisms
in which every path between the two triangles of the prism has the same length. (We will define all these terms later.)
And we can enlarge these in trivial ways, for instance by overlapping them on clique cutsets, but finding further examples is not so easy.
It turns out that when
\ell\geq 7
, the most general example has a family resemblance to these easy ones.

Before we give the construction, let us digress a little: how can we test if a graph is
\ell
-holed in polynomial time? This is easy
for each given
\ell
, but obtaining an algorithm with running time a polynomial in
|G|
and independent of
\ell
is not so clear.
(
|G|
denotes the number of vertices of a graph
G
.)
But here is one simple way. There is an algorithm due to Berger, Seymour and Spirkl
[
1
]
that, given two vertices
s,t

of a graph
G
, tests whether there is an induced path between
s,t
with length more than the distance between
s,t
.
(Its running time is
\mathcal{O}(|G|^{18})
.) For each three-vertex induced path
a\hbox{-}b\hbox{-}c
of
G
, delete
b
and all its neighbours except

a,c

from
G
, forming
G^{\prime}
say, and first test whether there is an
a-c
path in
G^{\prime}
(if not, move on to the next three-vertex path);
check that the distance in
G^{\prime}
between
a,c
is
\ell-2
(if not,
G
is not
\ell
-holed and we stop);
and use the algorithm
of
[
1
]
to check that there is no induced path in
G^{\prime}
between
a,c
with length more than
\ell-2
(if there is such a path,
the graph
is not
\ell
-holed and we stop). If after processing all three-vertex paths, we still have not determined that
G
is not

\ell
-holed, then it is
\ell
-holed and we stop. This has running time
\mathcal{O}(|G|^{21})
. It can be done faster with
more complication, making use of the structure theorem proved in this paper, and
such an algorithm appears in Jake Horsfield’s thesis
[
8
]
.

Let us return to constructing
\ell
-holed graphs.
One way to make a larger
\ell
-holed graph from two smaller ones is via clique cutsets.
We say that
X\subseteq V(G)
is a
clique cutset
of
G
if
G[X]
is a complete graph and
G\setminus X
is disconnected.
(
G[X]
denotes the subgraph of
G
induced on
X
, and
G\setminus X

is the subgraph of
G
obtained by deleting
X
.)
If
G_{1},G_{2}
are two
\ell
-holed graphs, and
X_{i}
is a clique of
G_{i}

for
i=1,2
, both of the same cardinality and with
|X_{i}|<|G_{i}|
, and we identify each vertex of
X_{1}
with a vertex of
X_{2}
bijectively, the graph
G

we produce is
\ell
-holed, and admits a clique cutset; and every graph with a clique cutset can be built by this operation.
Consequently, to understand
\ell
-holed graphs in general, it suffices to understand those
with no clique cutset.

There is another “trivial” way to make larger
\ell
-holed graphs from smaller ones; add a new vertex adjacent to all
old vertices. We call a vertex of
G
adjacent to all the other vertices of
G
a
universal vertex
.
Thus, we would like
to describe all
\ell
-holed graph with no clique cutset and no universal vertex. We were not able to do this for
\ell=4,5,6
,
but we have a complete description for all
\ell\geq 7
.

There is a third way to enlarge
\ell
-holed graphs to larger ones; choose a vertex and replace it by a set of pairwise
adjacent vertices,
each with the same neighbours as the original vertex (and one another). If two adjacent vertices have exactly the same neighbours,
we call them
adjacent twins
. We could assume there are no adjacent twins, without loss of generality, but it makes little
difference, so usually we will not do so.

Some terminology: two disjoint subsets
X,Y
of a graph are
complete
(to each other) if every vertex of
X
is adjacent to every vertex in
Y
, and
anticomplete

(to each other) if there are no edges between
X,Y
. Incidentally, we will sometimes use expressions such as “
G
-adjacent” and “
G
-neighbour”
when we want to make clear which graph we are using, as we will often have different graphs with the same vertex set.

An

of a set

means a sequence enumerating the members of
X
. Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
be an ordering of

.
We say a vertex

is

of the ordering if for all

with

are adjacent then

are adjacent.
An

means a clique together with
some ordering of it. We will often use the same notation for an ordered clique and the (unordered) clique itself, leaving the
ordering to be understood when it is needed.
A
half-graph
is a bipartite graph with no induced two-edge matching.
Let

be disjoint subsets of

Take orderings

and

is an edge
then

is an edge; or, equivalently, each vertex in

is adjacent to an initial segment of

,
and each vertex in

is adjacent to an initial segment of

.
Thus,

is a half-graph if and only if there are orderings of

that

Half-graphs will be of great importance in this paper. For instance, let
G
be an
\ell
-holed graph where
\ell\geq 5
,
and let

be disjoint cliques of
G
. Since there is no 4-hole
with two vertices in

and two in

(a

means a hole of length

), it follows that

is a half-graph. If

are disjoint cliques of

, we say that

are

if

is a half-graph. If

are anticomplete, then

are compatible
if and only if there is no induced four-vertex path in

with first vertex in

This is also equivalent to asking that there are orderings of

and

that
G[X,Y]
and

both obey.

be a graph with vertex set partitioned into sets

•

To describe our main result we will need arborescences.
An

is a tree with its edges directed in such a way that no two edges have a common head; or equivalently, such that

3
Blow-ups of cycles

Our strategy to prove

is to choose a maximal “apexed
\ell

graph can attach to it. But this only works in graphs that contain apexed

Here are some types of graph that we will need:

•

A

is a graph that is the union of three paths
R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}
, each with the same pair of ends,
each of length more than one, and pairwise vertex-disjoint except for their ends.

•

A

is a graph that is the union of three paths

and three additional edges

has ends

for

, and
R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}
are pairwise vertex-disjoint
except for their common end

, and at least two of
R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}

have length at least two.

•

A
prism
is a graph that is the union of three paths
R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}
and six additional edges

a_{1}a_{2},a_{2}a_{3},a_{3}a_{1},b_{1}b_{2},b_{2}b_{3},b_{3}b_{1}

has ends
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
for

, and

are pairwise

•

A

is a graph consisting of a cycle

of length at least four, and one additional vertex

, where

has at least three neighbours and at least one non-neighbour in

5

Let

be an integer. A

in a graph

of induced subgraphs of

•

•

is a path with ends

We call

. (Sadly for the metaphor, we will draw
semigates with the bars vertical and the sides horizontal, because they fit better on the page that way.) Note that
a theta makes a 3-bar semigate with

|A|=1
and

, and a prism makes one with

Let

and

and

if either

or

. (Note that the
sides are getting smaller and the bars are getting bigger.)
A

-bar semigate is a

-bar gate
if there is no strictly better
k
-bar semigate. For every
k
-bar semigate, there is a
k
-bar gate
which is better than it.
Given a gate in this notation, we want to see what we can say about
A
and the vertices

, and the same in
B
, when

is
\ell
-holed.

In general, if

are vertices of some graph

denotes the distance in

between

.
We begin with the following trivial but very useful observation:

5.1

Let
(A,B,P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})
be a
k
-bar gate in
G
. Then for
1\leq i\leq k
, either
a_{i}
has at least two neighbours in
A
, or
a_{i}=a_{j}

for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\setminus\{i\}
.

Proof.
Suppose that
a_{1}\neq a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}
say, and
a_{1}
has degree at most one in
A
. Since
A
is connected and
a_{2}\in V(A)
,
it follows that
a_{1}
has a neighbour

. But then
(A\setminus\{a_{1}\},B,P_{1}^{\prime},P_{2},\ldots,P_{k})
is a strictly better
k
-bar semigate, where
P_{1}^{\prime}

is the path obtained by adding

and the edge

to

, a contradiction. This proves
5.1
.

A

in a graph

is a vertex

such that

is disconnected.

5.2

Let
(A,B,P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})
be a
k
-bar gate in
G
, and suppose that
A
has a cut-vertex. There exist distinct

g,h,i,j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that

d_{A}(a_{g},a_{h})+d_{A}(a_{i},a_{j})+2\leq d_{A}(a_{g},a_{i})+d_{A}(a_{h},a_{j})=d_{A}(a_{g},a_{j})+d_{A}(a_{h},a_{i}).

Proof.
Let
v
be a cut-vertex of
A
, and let
C,D
be distinct components of
A\setminus\{v\}
.

(1)
There exist distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that

and
d_{A}(a_{i},a_{j})<d_{A}(a_{i},v)+d_{A}(a_{j},v)
.

Let

be the set of

with
i\in V(C)
, and for each
i\in I
, let
Q_{i}
be an induced path of

between

of length

, and let

be the union of the vertex sets of the paths

. Since

((A\setminus V(C))\cup G[X],B,P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})

is not a strictly better

. In particular, since

, it follows that

Let

for some

By

,
a_{i}
has degree at least two in

. Not

.

direct and inverse acoustic scattering by global rough surfaces
Chengyu Wu, Jiaqing Yang

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate on the forward and inverse acoustic scattering problem by an infinite rough interface in a lossless medium for both the cases that the real transmission coefficient μ ≠ 1 \mu\neq 1 and μ = 1 \mu=1 . We first prove the existence of the unique solution in the bounded and continuous function space utilizing the integral equation method through an elaborate analysis, which also leads to the L p L^{p} well-posedness. Then we carefully consider the singularity of the solutions to the problem with incident point source or hypersingular point source, where a simple and novel perspective is given for the derivation of the singularity. Finally, a global uniqueness theorem for the inverse problem is proven on the unique determination of the unbounded rough surface, the transmission coefficient and the wave number in the lower half plane from the measurements of the near field only on a line segment above the interface at a fixed frequency.



keywords

global rough surfaces, transmission scattering problem, boundary integral equations, well-posedness, singularity analysis, inverse scattering, global uniqueness.

35L05, 45E10, 78A46.

Introduction

The present paper concerns the direct and inverse problem of scattering of time harmonic waves by an infinite rough interface. A transmission condition is imposed on the rough surface, and the medium above and below the rough surface are assumed to be homogeneous and isotropic. By the phrase rough surface, we denote a surface which is a (usually nonlocal) perturbation of an infinite plane surface such that the whole surface lies within a finite distance of the original plane. Rough surface scattering problems have arisen more and more interests in recent years due to their large number of applications in various fields, e.g., in optics, acoustics, geophysical exploration and underwater detection, and have been studied extensively through numerical and analytic methods (see, e.g.,

Till now, despite the numerous literatures on numerical algorithms, much less rigorous mathmatical results concerning the rough surface have been carried out. The strict analysis on the direct rough surface scattering problems can date back to the end of the last centery.
In 1996, Chandler-Wilde and Ross
[
19
]
considered the Dirichlet boundary value problem for the Helmholtz equation in a nonlocally perturbed half-plane and proposed a new boundary integral equation formulation using the impedance Green’s function for an half-plane. They proved that the integral equation is well-posed in the space of bounded and continuous functions with the boundary being both Lyapunov and a small perturbation of a flat boundary. Later in
[
22
]
, Chandler-Wilde and Zhang proposed a radiation condition called the upward propagating radiation condition for the two-dimensional rough surface scattering problem, which generalizes both the classical Sommerfeld radiation condition and the Rayleigh expansion condition for diffraction gratings. They studied the aforementioned Dirichlet problem and proved the uniqueness of the solution when the boundary is piecewise Lyapunov. In addition, based on the previous results
[
19
]
, they showed the existence of the solution satisfying the new radiation condition in the case that the whole surface is both Lyapunov and a small perturbation of a flat boundary. Chandler-Wilde, Ross and Zhang
[
20
]
then further proved the well-posedness of this Dirichlet problem with the upward propagating radiation condition for general Lyapunov rough surfaces and all wavenumbers empolying the results
[
26
]
on the solvability of integral equations on the real line. From then on, a rather complete theory about the integral equation method for two-dimensional rough surface scattering problem was established. Many other models concerning the rough surface and inhomogeneous layer were investigated (see, e.g.,

).
These work showed the well-posedness of the corresponding problem through the integral equation method in a similar manner, which is followed by the present study, where the uniqueness was obtained by establishing the integral representation and some relating basic inequalities for the solutions and utlizing the technical lemma
[

, Lemma A]
, and the existence proof depended on the general results on the solvability of weakly singular second-kind integral equations on unbounded regions in
[

We also refer to

It should be noticed that the majority of the preceding work are concerning the half-plane problems, while less attention has been paid to the whole space problems, which require a substantially more elaborate analysis due to the presence of the transmitted wave. Particularly,

\mu\neq 1

\mu\neq 1

After establishing the well-posedness, we turn to further study the property of the solutions to the transmission scattering problem with point source and hypersingular point source incident waves, which is well known that plays a key role in the inverse scattering problem (see, e.g.,

Here we give the first rigorous proof for such thought. We propose a simple and novel perspective to show that the solutions restricted to the neighborhood of the source position and the interface do possess the same singularity as the singular incidence in the

\mu

Our method mainly based on the detailed analysis on the integral equations and can be applied to many other cases, such as the electromagnetic and elastic scattering whether by bounded regions or unbounded structures. This local property of the solutions is an important corollary to our well-posedness theorem and also plays a fundamental part in the uniqueness proof of the inverse problem.

The second part of this paper is devoted to the inverse problem for uniquely determining the scatterer. There exist many uniqueness results on inverse scattering problems on periodic structures

,

However, the methods utilized in these cases usually rely heavily on the a priori knowledge on the surface at infinity and thus can not be extended to the general unbounded non-periodic rough surfaces. Due to the great difficulty, few related work are available on the uniqueness results for the inverse rough surface scattering. We refer to
[

,

for the determination of impenetrable rough surfaces. As for penetrable rough surfaces,
Bao et al.

7

\mu=1

\mu=1

[

by Yang et al., where the uniqueness of inverse scattering from bounded penetrable obstacles is proved under both the situations of
\mu=1
and
\mu\neq 1

Their main idea is to construct a well-posed interior transmission problem in a small domain near the interface using the scattered fields corresponding to the incident point source or hypersingular point source, which motivates us to study the local property of these solutions in the rough surface case and also construct such interior transmission problem.
With these preparations, we prove that the entire scatterer, i.e., the unbounded penetrable rough surface, the transmission coefficient and the wave number below the surface can be uniquely determined by the measured near field only on a line segment above the rough surface.

The remaining part of the paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we introduce the mathmatical formulation for the model transmission problem in details, and present some useful notations and inequalities. Based on the previous work on the upward (downward) propagating radiation condition, in section
3

6

The model problem

In this section, we present the mathmatical model of the forward transmission scattering problem, and introduce some notations and estimates used thoughout the paper.

Denote by
B_{r}(x)
the open disk centered at
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with radius
r>0
. For
h\in{\mathbb{R}}
, define
U_{h}^{\pm}:=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}|x_{2}\gtrless h\}
and
\Gamma_{h}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}|x_{2}=h\}

the set of functions bounded and continuous in

\|\psi\|_{\infty,V}:=\sup_{x\in V}|\psi(x)|
. For simplicity, we abbreviate

. For

, denote by
BC^{0,\alpha}(V)
the Banach space of functions
\varphi\in BC(V)
which are uniformly Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha
, equipped with the norm

\|\varphi\|_{0,\alpha,V}:=\|\varphi\|_{\infty,V}+\sup\limits_{x,y\in V,x\neq y}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

For an open set
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
v\in L^{\infty}(V)
, denote by
\partial_{j}v
,
j=1,2

\partial v(x)/\partial x_{j}
. Let

under the norm
\|\varphi\|_{1,V}:=\|\varphi\|_{\infty,V}+\|\partial_{1}\varphi\|_{\infty,V}+\|\partial_{2}\varphi\|_{\infty,V}
. We further introduce some spaces of functions on the boundary
\Gamma

BC^{1,\alpha}(\Gamma):=\{\varphi\in BC(\Gamma)|{\rm Grad\,}\varphi\in BC^{0,\alpha}(\Gamma)\}

Moreover, for a bounded domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with a
C^{2}
boundary, we introduce the Hilbert spaces
H^{1}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
defined by

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\Delta}(\Omega):=\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)|u\in H^{1}(\Omega),~\Delta u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}

with the inner product

\displaystyle(u,v)_{H^{1}_{\Delta}(\Omega)}:=(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+(\Delta u,\Delta v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},~\forall u,v\in H^{1}_{\Delta}(\Omega),

where
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
denotes the set of distributions defined on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Given
f\in BC^{1,\alpha}({\mathbb{R}})
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
, with

and
f^{+}:=\sup_{x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}}f(x_{1})
, define

by

\displaystyle D^{+}:=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}|x_{2}>f(x_{1})\},

\displaystyle D^{-}:=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}|x_{2}<f(x_{1})\},

so that the interface
\Gamma
is

\partial D^{+}=\partial D^{-}=\Gamma:=\{x=(x_{1},f(x_{1}))|x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}\}.

When we want to show the dependence of the regions and interface on the function
f
, we write
D^{\pm}_{f}
and
\Gamma_{f}
. Denote by
n(x)=(n_{1}(x),n_{2}(x))
the unit normal vector to
\Gamma
at
x\in\Gamma
pointing out of
D^{+}
, and
\partial_{n}=n_{1}\partial_{1}+n_{2}\partial_{2}
and
\partial_{\tau}=n_{2}\partial_{1}-n_{1}\partial_{2}
the usual normal and tangent derivatives on
\Gamma
.

Now we formulate the interface problem which models the scattering of acoustic waves by the penetrable unbounded obstacle
D^{-}
. The incident plane wave
u^{i}(x)=e^{ik_{+}x\cdot d}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, with
d=(d_{1},d_{2})\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
the propagation direction, will produce a scattered wave
u_{+}
in
D^{+}
and a transmitted wave
u_{-}
in
D^{-}
. Note that one could also consider other types of incident waves, e.g., the so-called point source or hypersingular point source waves. The waves

satisfy the Helmholtz equations in
D^{\pm}
, and the transmission boundary conditions on
\Gamma
. To be specific, the forward transmission scattering problem associated with the scatterer
(\Gamma,k_{-},k_{+},\mu)
is modeled by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u_{+}+k_{+}^{2}u_{+}=0&{\rm in}~D^{+},\\[5.69054pt]
\Delta u_{-}+k_{-}^{2}u_{-}=0&{\rm in}~D^{-},\\[5.69054pt]
u_{+}+u^{i}=u_{-}&{\rm on}~\Gamma,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{+}}{\partial n}+\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial n}=\mu\frac{\partial u_{-}}{\partial n}&{\rm on}~\Gamma,\end{array}\right.

where

(\Gamma,k_{-},k_{+},\mu)
from the knowledge of the scattered wave

In order to guarantee the uniqueness of problem (
2

For

\Phi_{k}(x,y):=\frac{i}{4}H_{0}^{(1)}(k|x-y|),~~~x\neq y,

Definition 1
.

Given a domain

\displaystyle v(x)=O(r^{-1/2}),~~~\frac{\partial v(x)}{\partial r}-ikv(x)=o(r^{-1/2}),~r=|x|,

(2.6)

The conditions (

(G,k)

Definition 2
.

(

,

]

Given a domain

\displaystyle v(x)=2\theta\int_{\Gamma_{h}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{k}(x,y)}{\partial y_{2}}\varphi(y)ds(y),~x\in U_{h}^{+}~(x\in U_{h}^{-}),

(2.7)

Denote by UPRC

For

Note that the existence of the integral (

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\Phi_{k}(x,y)}{\partial y_{2}}\right|\leq C|x_{2}-y_{2}|\left(|x-y|^{-2}+|x-y|^{-3/2}\right),~x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},~x\neq y,

in some extents correspond to the diffraction grating case (see, e.g., the conditions in

,
[

[

4
Existence of solution

In this section, we equivalently reduce the TSP to a system of boundary integral equation on
\Gamma
and show the existence of the unique solution to the TSP by proving the uniqueness result of the corresponding boundary integral equations and combining some existing work.

In this section we always assume that
\Gamma\in BC^{1,1}
if not otherwise stated. For
a<f^{-}
define the single- and double-layer potentials:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{k_{+},a}^{+}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Gamma}G_{k_{+}}^{+\mathcal{(I)}}(x,y;a)\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in U_{a}^{+}\setminus\Gamma,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}_{k_{+},a}^{+}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial n(y)}G_{k_{+}}^{+\mathcal{(I)}}(x,y;a)\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in U_{a}^{+}\setminus\Gamma.

And define the boundary integral operators:

\displaystyle(S_{k_{+},a}^{+}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Gamma}G_{k_{+}}^{+\mathcal{(I)}}(x,y;a)\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in\Gamma,

\displaystyle(K_{k_{+},a}^{+}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial n(y)}G_{k_{+}}^{+\mathcal{(I)}}(x,y;a)\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in\Gamma,

\displaystyle(K_{k_{+},a}^{\prime+}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial n(x)}G_{k_{+}}^{+\mathcal{(I)}}(x,y;a)\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in\Gamma,

\displaystyle(T_{k_{+},a}^{+}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\partial}{\partial n(x)}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial n(y)}G_{k_{+}}^{+\mathcal{(I)}}(x,y;a)\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in\Gamma.

Further, for
a>f^{+}
similarly define the layer potential operators
\mathcal{S}_{k_{-},a}^{-},\mathcal{D}_{k_{-},a}^{-}
for
x\in U_{a}^{-}\setminus\Gamma
and the boundary integral operators
S_{k_{-},a}^{-},K_{k_{-},a}^{-},K_{k_{-},a}^{\prime-},T_{k_{-},a}^{-}
for
x\in\Gamma
. For detailed properties of these operators, we refer the readers to
[
42
, Lemma 4.1-4.4]
.

We seek for the solution to the TSP in the form of

\displaystyle u_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{D}_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{+}\varphi)(x)+(\mathcal{S}_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{+}\psi)(x),~~x\in D^{+},

(4.1)

\displaystyle u_{-}(x)

\displaystyle=\mu^{-1}[(\mathcal{D}_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{-}\varphi)(x)+(\mathcal{S}_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{-}\psi)(x)],~~x\in D^{-}.

(4.2)

Then by
[
42
, Lemma 4.1-4.4]
we can equivalently reduce the TSP to the system of boundary integral equation on
\Gamma
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\chi=G

(4.3)

where
\chi=(\varphi,\psi)^{T}
are unknown density functions,
G=(g_{1},g_{2})^{T}
with

\displaystyle g_{1}\in BC^{1,\alpha}(\Gamma),~g_{2}\in BC^{0,\alpha}(\Gamma),~\alpha\in(0,1),

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}K_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{+}-\mu^{-1}K_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{-}+(1+\mu)(2\mu)^{-1}I&S_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{+}-\mu^{-1}S_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{-}\\
T_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{-}-T_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{+}&K_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{\prime-}-K_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{\prime+}+I\\
\end{array}\right)

We note that by the known results in
[
42
, Section 4.3]
, to show the existence of solution to the equation (
4.3
), it suffices to prove that it has at most one solution, which is the Theorem
13
below.

Lemma 12
.

(
[
42
, Lemma 4.5]
)

Suppose conditions
(
4.4
)
be fulfilled and
\chi=(\varphi,\psi)^{T}
be a solution of the equation
(

)
. Then
\varphi\in BC^{1,\alpha}(\Gamma)
and
\psi\in BC^{0,\alpha}(\Gamma)
with the same

as in
(
4.4
)
.

Theorem 13
.

If
(\mu-1)(k_{+}^{2}-k_{-}^{2}\mu)\geq 0
and
k_{+}^{2}\neq k_{-}^{2}\mu
, then equation
(
4.3
)
has at most one solution, i.e., the operator
\mathcal{M}
is injective.

Proof.

It suffices to show the homogeneous version of equation (
4.3
) has only the trivial solution. Suppose
\chi=(\varphi,\psi)^{T}\in[BC(\Gamma)]^{2}
solve the equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\chi=0,

(4.6)

Define
u_{-}
and
u_{+}
by (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), respectively, with the corresponding
\varphi
and
\psi
. By Lemma
12
we know
\varphi\in BC^{1,\beta}(\Gamma)
and
\psi\in BC^{0,\beta}(\Gamma)
for all
\beta\in(0,1)
. It then follows from
[
42
, Lemma 4.1,4.4]
and Theorem
10
that
u_{\pm}=0
in
D^{\pm}
.

We now further consider the same functions
u_{-}
and
u_{+}
in the domains
D^{+}_{h_{+}}
and
D^{-}_{h_{-}}
, respectively. Applying
[
42
, Lemma 4.1]
we see that for
x\in\Gamma

\displaystyle[u_{-}(x)]^{+}-[u_{-}(x)]^{-}=\mu^{-1}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle,~[\partial_{n}u_{-}(x)]^{+}-[\partial_{n}u_{-}(x)]^{-}=-\mu^{-1}\psi(x),

(4.7)

\displaystyle[u_{+}(x)]^{+}-[u_{+}(x)]^{-}=\varphi(x)

\displaystyle,~[\partial_{n}u_{+}(x)]^{+}-[\partial_{n}u_{+}(x)]^{-}=-\psi(x),

(4.8)

where
[\cdot]^{+}
and
[\cdot]^{-}
denote the limits on
\Gamma
from
D^{+}
and
D^{-}
, respectively. Since
u_{\pm}=0
in
D^{\pm}
, we derive that

\displaystyle-\varphi(x)=[u_{+}(x)]^{-}=-\mu[u_{-}(x)]^{+},~\psi(x)=[\partial_{n}u_{+}(x)]^{-}=-\mu[\partial_{n}u_{-}(x)]^{+}

(4.9)

for
x\in\Gamma
. Set

\displaystyle D^{-}_{*}:=D^{+}_{h_{+}},~~~D^{+}_{*}:=D^{-}_{h_{-}},

\displaystyle v_{-}(x):=-\mu u_{-}(x)~~\text{in}~D^{-}_{*},~~~v_{+}(x):=u_{+}(x)~~\text{in}~D^{+}_{*}.

It can be verified from (
2.14
) and (
4.9
) that
v_{-}
and
v_{+}
satisfy the following boundary value problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta v_{\pm}+k_{\pm}^{2}v_{\pm}=0&{\rm in}~D^{\pm}_{*},\\[5.69054pt]
[v_{-}]^{+}=[v_{+}]^{-},~~[\partial_{n}v_{-}]^{+}=[\partial_{n}v_{+}]^{-}&{\rm on}~\Gamma,\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{2}v_{-}=ik_{-}v_{-}&{\rm on}~\Gamma_{h_{+}},\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{2}v_{+}=-ik_{+}v_{+}&{\rm on}~\Gamma_{h_{-}},\end{array}\right.

and by
[
42
, Lemma 4.4]
we have

.

Define

\displaystyle D^{\pm}_{*}(A,B):=\{x\in D^{\pm}_{*}|A<x_{1}<B\},~~\Gamma(A,B):=\{x\in\Gamma|A<x_{1}<B\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{h_{\pm}}(A,B):=\{x\in\Gamma_{h_{\pm}}|A<x_{1}<B\},~~l_{\pm}(A):=\{x\in D^{\pm}_{*}|x_{1}=A\}.

By (
4
) and Green’s formula, the same as in (
3.5
)-(
3.7
), we obtain that

\displaystyle k_{-}\int_{\Gamma_{h_{+}}(A,B)}|v_{-}|^{2}ds+k_{+}\int_{\Gamma_{h_{-}}(A,B)}|v_{+}|^{2}ds

\displaystyle={\rm Im}\left(\widetilde{R}_{-}(A)-\widetilde{R}_{-}(B)+\widetilde{R}_{+}(A)-\widetilde{R}_{+}(B)\right),

(4.15)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{\pm}(P):=\int_{l_{\pm}(P)}\overline{v}_{\pm}\partial_{1}v_{\pm}ds,~P=A,B.

Since

, we have
|\widetilde{R}_{\pm}(P)|
are uniformly bounded for
P\in(-\infty,+\infty)
. Then by (
4.15
) we deduce that
v_{-}|_{\Gamma_{h_{+}}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{h_{+}})
and
v_{+}|_{\Gamma_{h_{-}}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{h_{-}})
. Again due to

, we further yield that

\displaystyle v_{-}(x_{1},h_{+})\rightarrow 0~~\text{and}~~v_{+}(x_{1},h_{-})\rightarrow 0~~\text{as}~|x_{1}|\rightarrow+\infty.

(4.16)

Now integrating by parts in
D^{\pm}_{*}
, the same as in (
3.8
)-(
3.14
), we derive that

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma(A,B)}|v_{-}|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\;C\left(\int_{\Gamma_{h_{+}}(A,B)}|v_{-}|^{2}ds+\int_{\Gamma_{h_{-}}(A,B)}|v_{+}|^{2}ds\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\hat{R}_{-}(A)+\hat{R}_{-}(B)+\hat{R}_{+}(A)+\hat{R}_{+}(B)\right),

(4.17)

where

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\pm}(P):=\int_{l_{\pm}(P)}|\partial_{1}v_{\pm}\partial_{2}\overline{v}_{\pm}|+|\overline{v}_{\pm}\partial_{1}v_{\pm}|ds,~P=A,B.

Also we have
|\hat{R}_{\pm}(P)|
are uniformly bounded for

. It then follows that
-\varphi=v_{-}|_{\Gamma}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
and thus

\displaystyle\varphi(x)\rightarrow 0~~\text{as}~|x_{1}|\rightarrow+\infty.

(4.18)

From (
4.16
) and (
4.18
), by the same arguments in the proof of
[
42
, Lemma 4.6]
, we can prove that
v_{\pm}(x)\rightarrow 0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
uniformly in
\overline{D^{\pm}_{*}}
, which implies

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{P\rightarrow\pm\infty}|\widetilde{R}_{\pm}(P)|=0,~~j=1,2.

In view of (
4.15
), we get
v_{-}=0
on
\Gamma_{h_{+}}
and
v_{+}=0
on
\Gamma_{h_{-}}
. Moreover, by the boundary condition on
\Gamma_{h_{\pm}}
in (

), we obtain that
\partial_{2}v_{-}=0
on
\Gamma_{h_{+}}
and
\partial_{2}v_{+}=0
on
\Gamma_{h_{-}}
, which indicates by Holmgren’s uniqueness theorem that
v_{\pm}=0
in
D^{\pm}_{*}
. Hence,
u_{-}=0
in
D^{+}_{h_{+}}
and
u_{+}=0
in
D^{-}_{h_{-}}
. Finally, by the jump relations (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), it follows that
\varphi=\psi=0
on
\Gamma

∎

With Theorems
10
,
13
and the results in
[
42
, Section 4.3]
, we now state our main theorem for the TSP.

Theorem 14
.

Suppose the scatterer
(\Gamma,k_{-},k_{+},\mu)
satisfies
k_{\pm},\mu>0
,
(\mu-1)(k_{+}^{2}-k_{-}^{2}\mu)\geq 0
and
k_{+}^{2}\neq k_{-}^{2}\mu
. Given
g_{1}\in BC^{1,\alpha}(\Gamma)
and
g_{2}\in BC^{0,\alpha}(\Gamma)
, for
h_{-},h_{+}\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
h_{-}<f^{-}<f^{+}<h_{+}
, the TSP has exactly one solution
(u_{+},u_{-})
in the form of

\displaystyle u_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{D}_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{+}\varphi)(x)+(\mathcal{S}_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{+}\psi)(x),~~x\in D^{+},

\displaystyle u_{-}(x)

\displaystyle=\mu^{-1}[(\mathcal{D}_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{-}\varphi)(x)+(\mathcal{S}_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{-}\psi)(x)],~~x\in D^{-},

such that
u_{\pm}\in C^{2}(D^{\pm})\cap C^{1}(\overline{D}^{\pm})\cap BC^{1}(\overline{D}^{\pm}_{h_{\pm}})
. Here,
\chi:=(\varphi,\psi)^{T}\in BC^{1,\alpha}(\Gamma)\times BC^{0,\alpha}(\Gamma)
is the unique solution to the boundary integral equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\chi=G

(4.19)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}K_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{+}-\mu^{-1}K_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{-}+(1+\mu)(2\mu)^{-1}I&S_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{+}-\mu^{-1}S_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{-}\\
T_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{-}-T_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{+}&K_{k_{-},h_{+}}^{\prime-}-K_{k_{+},h_{-}}^{\prime+}+I\\
\end{array}\right)

and
G=(g_{1},g_{2})^{T}
, where the integral operator
\mathcal{M}
is bijective (hence boundedly invertible) in
[BC(\Gamma)]^{2}
. Moreover, in
D^{\pm}_{h_{\pm}}
,
u_{\pm}
depend continuously on
\|g_{1}\|_{\infty,\Gamma}
and
\|g_{2}\|_{\infty,\Gamma}
, while
{\rm NewA}u_{\pm}
depend continuously on
\|g_{1}\|_{1,\alpha,\Gamma}
and
\|g_{2}\|_{0,\alpha,\Gamma}
.

Remark 15
.

From
[
1
]
we see that the unique solvability of the integral equation
(
4.19
)
in
L^{p}

(1\leq p\leq\infty)
spaces can be obtained from the unique solvability in the space of bounded and continuous functions. In other words, the integral opeator
\mathcal{M}
is also bijective and boundedly invertible in
[L^{p}(\Gamma)]^{2}
.

5
Singularity of solution

In this section, we aim to derive the singularity of the solutions to the TSP corresponding to the incident point source or hypersingular point source in the
H^{1}
sense, which is of great significance in the uniqueness proof of the inverse problem and also interesting on their own rights. Here we give a novel and simple perspective to investigate on the singularity of the solutions with singular incidence, which can be applied to many other cases.

Lemma 16
.

For fixed
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
k,k_{1}\geq 0
,
\Phi_{k_{1}}(\cdot,y)-\Phi_{k}(\cdot,y)\in W^{2,p}(B_{1}(y))
with
1\leq p<\infty
.

Proof.

Since
\Phi_{k_{1}}-\Phi_{k}=\Phi_{k_{1}}-\Phi_{0}+\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{k}
, it suffices to show that
\Phi_{k}(\cdot,y)-\Phi_{0}(\cdot,y)\in W^{2,p}(B_{1}(y))
for
k>0
. First we remind two recurrence formulas for the Hankel functions, i.e.,

\displaystyle H^{(1)\prime}_{0}(z)=-H^{(1)}_{1}(z)~~~{\rm and}~~~H^{(1)}_{1}(z)+zH^{(1)\prime}_{1}(z)=zH^{(1)}_{0}(z).

Then by direct calculation we see that

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(\Phi_{k}(x,y)-\Phi_{0}(x,y))=\frac{x_{i}-y_{i}}{|x-y|}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{|x-y|}-\frac{i}{4}kH_{1}^{(1)}(k|x-y|)\right),

and

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{ij}(\Phi_{k}(x,y)-\Phi_{0}(x,y))

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{ij}|x-y|^{2}-2(x_{i}-y_{i})(x_{j}-y_{j})}{|x-y|^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{|x-y|}-\frac{i}{4}kH_{1}^{(1)}(k|x-y|)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{i}{4}\frac{(x_{i}-y_{i})(x_{j}-y_{j})}{|x-y|^{2}}k^{2}H_{0}^{(1)}(k|x-y|),

where
i,j=1,2
and
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker symbol. Further, we recall that as
z\rightarrow 0^{+}
(see details in
[
35
]
)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{i}{4}H_{0}^{(1)}(z)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{z}\right|\leq C,~~{\rm and}~~\left|\frac{1}{2\pi z}-\frac{i}{4}kH_{1}^{(1)}(kz)\right|\leq Cz|\ln(z)|,

which implies that for
x\in B_{1}(y)
and
i,j=1,2
,

\displaystyle|\Phi_{k}(x,y)-\Phi_{0}(x,y)|\leq C,~~~|\partial_{i}(\Phi_{k}(x,y)-\Phi_{0}(x,y))|\leq C,

\displaystyle|\partial_{ij}(\Phi_{k}(x,y)-\Phi_{0}(x,y))|\leq C|\ln|x-y||.

Thus, the assertion follows.
∎

Theorem 17
.

Suppose the scatterer
(\Gamma,k_{-},k_{+},\mu)
is under the assumptions in Theorem
14
. Suppose further
\Gamma\in C^{2,\lambda}

(or~f\in C^{2,\lambda}({\mathbb{R}}))
with
\lambda\in(0,1]
. For
x_{0}\in\Gamma
and
\delta>0
small enough, define
x_{j}:=x_{0}-(\delta/j)n(x_{0})\in D^{+}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the unique solutions
(u^{(j)}_{+},u^{(j)}_{-})
to the TSP with the incident point source
u^{i}_{j}(x)=\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j}),j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, satisfy that

\displaystyle\left\|u^{(j)}_{-}-\frac{2}{\mu+1}\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C,

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
D_{0}
is a
C^{2,\lambda}
bounded domain such that
B_{2\delta}(x_{0})\cap D^{-}\subset D_{0}\subset D^{-}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4
and Remark
5
, we see that
\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,x_{j})
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, satisfy the DPRC for
k_{-}
in
D^{-}
, which implies that
(u^{(j)}_{+},u^{(j)}_{-}-\mu^{-1}\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,x_{j}))
is the unique solution to the TSP with the boundary data

\displaystyle g_{1,j}=\mu^{-1}\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,x_{j})-\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j}),

\displaystyle g_{2,j}=\frac{\partial\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,x_{j})}{\partial n(x)}-\frac{\partial\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})}{\partial n(x)}.

By the regularity derived in Lemma
16
, the Sobolev embedding and the decay property of the Hankel functions at infinity, we have
\|g_{2,j}\|_{0,\alpha,\Gamma}\leq C
uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Choose
R>0
large enough such that
\overline{D}_{0}\subset B_{R}(x_{0})
. Then there exists a cut off function
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

and

\displaystyle\chi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\overline{B}_{R+1}(x_{0}),\\[5.69054pt]

We can also find a

bounded domain

. Define

as the solutions to the TSP with

and
\tilde{g}_{2,j}=0
. Clearly,
\|g_{1,j}-\tilde{g}_{1,j}\|_{1,\alpha,\Gamma}\leq C
uniformly for

, which yields that
\|u^{(j)}_{-}-\mu^{-1}\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,x_{j})-w_{2,j}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C

Further, we know that
w_{2,j}
has the form

for some

with
-h<f^{-}<f^{+}<h
, where
\Phi_{j}=(\varphi_{j},\psi_{j})^{T}
satisfies
\mathcal{M}\Phi_{j}=G_{j}
with

. Direct calculation shows that
\|\tilde{g}_{1,j}\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq C
for

, which indicates by remark
15
that
\varphi_{j},\psi_{j}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{p}(\Gamma)
with
1\leq p<\infty
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Now define
\widetilde{w}_{2,j}(x)=\mu^{-1}[\mathcal{D}_{k_{-},h}^{-}(\chi\varphi_{j})(x)+\mathcal{S}_{k_{-},h}^{-}(\chi\psi_{j})](x)
,

. For a moment, we extend

to be zero in

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}_{2,j}(x)\;

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial}{\partial n(y)}G_{k_{-}}^{-}(x,y;h)\chi(y)\varphi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.+\int_{\partial\Omega}G_{k_{-}}^{-}(x,y;h)\chi(y)\psi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right),~~x\in D^{-}.

We can also obtain that

\displaystyle w_{2,j}(x)-\widetilde{w}_{2,j}(x)\;

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\int_{\Gamma\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}G_{k_{-}}^{-}(x,y;h)(1-\chi(y))\varphi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.+\int_{\Gamma\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{\partial}{\partial n(y)}G_{k_{-}}^{-}(x,y;h)(1-\chi(y))\psi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right)

\displaystyle|w_{2,j}(x)-\widetilde{w}_{2,j}(x)|\;

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Gamma\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}(1+|x_{1}-y_{1}|)^{-3/2}(|\varphi_{j}(y)|+|\psi_{j}(y)|)ds(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Gamma\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}(|\varphi_{j}(y)|+|\psi_{j}(y)|)ds(y)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}+\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)})\leq C

Now we investigate
\widetilde{w}_{2,j}

and

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Gamma\cap B_{R+1}(x_{0})}\frac{\partial}{\partial{n(y)}}\left(\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,y_{h}^{\prime})+P_{k_{-}}^{-}(x-y_{h}^{\prime})\right)\chi(y)\varphi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}

\displaystyle\;+\left\|\int_{\Gamma\cap B_{R+1}(x_{0})}\left(\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,y_{h}^{\prime})+P_{k_{-}}^{-}(x-y_{h}^{\prime})\right)\chi(y)\psi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C

from the regularity of
\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,y_{h}^{\prime})
and
P_{k_{-}}^{-}(x-y_{h}^{\prime})
, which further implies that

\left\|\widetilde{w}_{2,j}(x)-\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,y)}{\partial n(y)}\chi(y)\varphi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C

uniformly for

. Due to the positive distance between

and

. Then by the first equation in
\mathcal{M}\Phi_{j}=G_{j}
, we see that

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}(x)-\frac{2(1-\mu)}{1+\mu}\chi(x)\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mu}{1+\mu}\left(\mu^{-1}K_{k_{-},h}^{-}-K_{k_{+},-h}^{+}\right)(\varphi_{j})(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\mu}{1+\mu}\left(\mu^{-1}S_{k_{-},h}^{-}-S_{k_{+},-h}^{+}\right)(\psi_{j})(x),~x\in\Gamma.

Analyzing similarly as
w_{2,j}-\widetilde{w}_{2,j}
, it follows from the positive distance between
\Gamma\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})
and
\Gamma\cap B_{R-\varepsilon_{0}}(x_{0})
that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mu^{-1}K_{k_{-},h}^{-}-K_{k_{+},-h}^{+}\right)[(1-\chi)\varphi_{j}]\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma\cap B_{R-\varepsilon_{0}}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\;+\left\|\left(\mu^{-1}S_{k_{-},h}^{-}-S_{k_{+},-h}^{+}\right)[(1-\chi)\psi_{j}]\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma\cap B_{R-\varepsilon_{0}}(x_{0}))}\leq C.

By the bounded embedding from
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
into
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
and
[
11
, Corollary 3.7]
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\frac{\partial\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,y)}{\partial n(y)}-\frac{\partial\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,y)}{\partial n(y)}\right)\chi(y)\varphi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\;+\left\|\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\mu^{-1}\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,y)-\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,y)\right)\chi(y)\psi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C.

Moreover, because of
|x-y_{\pm h}^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon>0
for all
x\in\Gamma\cap B_{R-\varepsilon_{0}}(x_{0})
and
y\in\Gamma\cap B_{R+1}(x_{0})
, it is deduced that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Gamma\cap B_{R+1}(x_{0})}\frac{\partial}{\partial{n(y)}}\left(\Phi_{k_{\pm}}(x,y_{\mp h}^{\prime})+P_{k_{\pm}}^{\pm}(x-y_{\mp h}^{\prime})\right)\chi(y)\varphi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma\cap B_{R-\varepsilon_{0}}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\;+\left\|\int_{\Gamma\cap B_{R+1}(x_{0})}\left(\Phi_{k_{\pm}}(x,y_{\mp h}^{\prime})+P_{k_{\pm}}^{\pm}(x-y_{\mp h}^{\prime})\right)\chi(y)\psi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma\cap B_{R-\varepsilon_{0}}(x_{0}))}\leq C,

which indicates that

\left\|\varphi_{j}(x)-\frac{2(1-\mu)}{1+\mu}\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma\cap B_{R-\varepsilon_{0}}(x_{0}))}\leq C

uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then from
[
41
, Theorem 6.13]
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,y)}{\partial n(y)}\chi(y)\left(\varphi_{j}(y)-\frac{2(1-\mu)}{1+\mu}\Phi_{k_{+}}(y,x_{j})\right)ds(y)\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\varphi_{j}-\frac{2(1-\mu)}{1+\mu}\Phi_{k_{+}}(\cdot,x_{j})\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma\cap B_{R-\varepsilon_{0}}(x_{0}))}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left.\left\|\chi\left(\varphi_{j}-\frac{2(1-\mu)}{1+\mu}\Phi_{k_{+}}(\cdot,x_{j})\right)\right\|_{H^{-2}(\partial\Omega)}\right)\leq C,

and hence

\left\|w_{2,j}(x)-\frac{2(1-\mu)}{\mu(1+\mu)}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,y)}{\partial n(y)}\chi(y)\Phi_{k_{+}}(y,x_{j})ds(y)\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C.

Since
\{x_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a positive distance from
\partial\Omega\setminus\overline{D}_{0}
and
\partial D_{0}\setminus\Gamma
, we derive

\left\|w_{2,j}(x)-\frac{2(1-\mu)}{\mu(1+\mu)}\int_{\partial D_{0}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{0}(x,y)}{\partial n(y)}\Phi_{0}(y,x_{j})ds(y)\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C

by the regularity of
\Phi_{k_{\pm}}-\Phi_{0}
.

Now define

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{j}(x)=2\int_{\partial D_{0}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{0}(x,y)}{\partial n(y)}\Phi_{0}(y,x_{j})ds(y),~~~x\in D_{0}.

Set
h_{j}(x)=\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})
. Then we see that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta(\widetilde{f}_{j}+h_{j})=0&{\rm in}~D_{0},\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{j}(x)+h_{j}(x)=2\int_{\partial D_{0}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{0}(x,y)}{\partial n(y)}\Phi_{0}(y,x_{j})ds(y)&{\rm on}~\partial D_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Again by
[
11
, Corollary 3.7]
we obtain that the trace of
\widetilde{f}_{j}+h_{j}
on
\partial D_{0}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1/2}(\partial D_{0})
. Thus the classic elliptic regularity gives
\|\widetilde{f}_{j}+h_{j}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C
uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It immediately follows that

\left\|w_{2,j}(x)+\frac{1-\mu}{\mu(1+\mu)}\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C.

Therefore, finally we deduce that

\left\|u^{(j)}_{-}-\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-\frac{1-\mu}{\mu(1+\mu)}\right)\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C

uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which is the desired estimate.
∎

Theorem 18
.

Suppose the scatterer
(\Gamma,k_{-},k_{+},\mu)
is under the assumptions in Theorem
14
. Suppose further
\Gamma\in C^{2,\lambda}

(or~f\in C^{2,\lambda}({\mathbb{R}}))
with
\lambda\in(0,1]
. Follow the notations
x_{0},x_{j}
and
D_{0}
in Theorem
17
. Define
y_{j}:=x_{0}+(\delta/j)n(x_{0})\in D^{-}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the unique solutions
(\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{+},\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{-})
to the TSP corresponding to the incident hypersingular point source
\widetilde{u}^{i}_{j}={\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})\cdot n(x_{0})
satisfy the estimate

\left\|\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{-}-\frac{2}{\mu+1}{\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})\cdot n(x_{0})-v_{j}\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C

uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where

v_{j}(x)=\frac{2\mu(1-\mu)}{(\mu+1)^{2}}\int_{\partial D_{0}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{0}(x,y)}{\partial n(y)}({\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{0}(y,x_{j})+{\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{0}(y,y_{j}))\cdot n(x_{0})ds(y),~x\in D_{0},

with
\|v_{j}\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}\leq C
uniformly in
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

We know from Lemma
4
and Remark
5
that
{\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,y_{j})\cdot n(x_{0})
satisfy the UPRC for
k_{+}
in
D^{+}
and
{\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,x_{j})\cdot n(x_{0})
satisfy the DPRC for
k_{-}
in
D^{-}
. Then it can be verified that
(\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{+}+(1-\mu)(\mu+1)^{-1}{\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,y_{j})\cdot n(x_{0}),\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{-}-2(\mu+1)^{-1}{\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,x_{j})\cdot n(x_{0}))
solves the TSP with the boundary data
g_{m,j}=\widetilde{g}_{m,j}+\hat{g}_{m,j}
,
m=1,2
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}_{1,j}=\frac{2}{\mu+1}{\rm NewA}_{x}\left(\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,x_{j})-\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})\right)\cdot n(x_{0}),

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}_{2,j}=\frac{2\mu}{\mu+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial n(x)}{\rm NewA}_{x}\left(\Phi_{k_{-}}(x,x_{j})-\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})\right)\cdot n(x_{0}),

and

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{1,j}=\frac{1-\mu}{\mu+1}{\rm NewA}_{x}\left(\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})+\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,y_{j})\right)\cdot n(x_{0}),

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{2,j}=\frac{\mu-1}{\mu+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial n(x)}{\rm NewA}_{x}\left(\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})-\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,y_{j})\right)\cdot n(x_{0}).

Direct calculation shows that
\|\hat{g}_{1,j}\|_{\infty,\Gamma}+\|\hat{g}_{2,j}\|_{\infty,\Gamma}\leq C
uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. From the proof of Lemma
16
, we see that
\|\widetilde{g}_{1,j}\|_{0,\alpha,\Gamma}+\|\widetilde{g}_{1,j}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial D_{0}\cap\Gamma)}+\|\widetilde{g}_{2,j}\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq C
uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
1\leq p<\infty
. Therefore, arguing analogously as in the proof of Theorem
17
, we would obtain the desired estimate. The detailed proof is thus omitted.

To show the estimate for
v_{j}
, we first set
\phi_{j}(x)=({\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})+{\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{0}(x,y_{j}))\cdot n(x_{0})
,
x\in\partial D_{0}
. It then can be derived that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\partial D_{0}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{0}(x,y)}{\partial n(y)}\phi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{g\in L^{2},\|g\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}=1}\left|\int_{D_{0}}\left(\int_{\partial D_{0}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{0}(x,y)}{\partial n(y)}\phi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right)g(x)dx\right|

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{g\in L^{2},\|g\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}=1}\left|\int_{\partial D_{0}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial n(y)}\int_{D_{0}}\Phi_{0}(x,y)g(x)dx\right)\phi_{j}(y)ds(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{g\in L^{2},\|g\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}=1}\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial n(y)}\int_{D_{0}}\Phi_{0}(x,y)g(x)dx\right\|_{L^{1}(\partial D_{0})}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial D_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup\limits_{g\in L^{2},\|g\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}=1}\|g\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial D_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C

uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, since the volume potential operator is bounded from
L^{2}(D_{0})
into
H^{2}(D_{0})
and the boundary trace operator is bounded from
H^{2}(D_{0})
into
L^{1}(\partial D_{0})
.
∎

Remark 19
.

It should be noticed that in Theorems
17
and
18
, the assumptions in Theorem
14
are used to only ensure the existence of the singular solutions and have nothing to do with the proof of the singularity.

6
The inverse problem

This section is devoted to the uniqueness proof of the inverse problem. More precisely, we want to identify the scatterer
(\Gamma,k_{-},k_{+},\mu)
given
k_{+}
and the measurements of the scattered field with the incident point source. In particular, the measurements are taken only on a line segment

\Gamma_{c,d}:=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Gamma_{c}||x_{1}|\leq d\}

with the measurement width
d>0
and measurement height
c>f^{+}
. With the previous results on well-posedness and singularity, our proof would be a standard application of the novel technique first proposed in
[
52
]
, which is mainly based on constructing a well-posed interior transmission problem in a sufficiently small domain and then using the singularity of the solutions to derive a contradiction.

Let
D^{+}_{m},D^{-}_{m}
be the upper and lower half planes, respectively, separated by the interface
\Gamma_{f_{m}}
,
m=1,2
. Denote by
(u_{m,+}(\cdot,y),u_{m,-}(\cdot,y))
the scattered wave and transmitted wave to the TSP associated with the scatterer
(\Gamma_{f_{m}},k_{m,-},k_{+},\mu_{m})
and the incident point source
u^{i}(\cdot,y)=\Phi_{k_{+}}(\cdot,y)
for
y\in D^{+}_{m}
,
m=1,2
. Now we present our main theorem for the inverse problem in this paper.

Theorem 20
.

Suppose the scatterers
(\Gamma_{f_{m}},k_{m,-},k_{+},\mu_{m})
,
m=1,2
are under the assumptions in Theorem
17
. If
u_{1,+}(x,y)=u_{2,+}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\Gamma_{c,d}
, then
(\Gamma_{f_{1}},k_{1,-},\mu_{1})=(\Gamma_{f_{2}},k_{2,-},\mu_{2})
.

Proof.

Following the same procedure in the proof of
[
49
, Theorem 4.1]
, from
u_{1,+}(x,y)=u_{2,+}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\Gamma_{c,d}
we can get that
u_{1,+}(x,y)=u_{2,+}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in D^{+}_{1}\cap D^{+}_{2}
. In the following proof, we show
k_{1,-}=k_{2,-}
in the last part.

(i)We first consider the case that
\mu_{1}\neq 1
and
\mu_{2}\neq 1
. We want to prove
\Gamma_{f_{1}}=\Gamma_{f_{2}}
and
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
in this case. Suppose
\Gamma_{f_{1}}\neq\Gamma_{f_{2}}
, we can find a point
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{f_{1}}\setminus\Gamma_{f_{2}}
. There is no loss of generality if we further let
f_{1}(x_{0})>f_{2}(x_{0})
. Then there exists
\delta>0
small enough such that
B_{2\delta}(x_{0})\cap\Gamma_{f_{2}}=\emptyset
. Define

x_{j}:=x_{0}-\frac{\delta}{j}n(x_{0})\in B_{2\delta}(x_{0}),~j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Since
f_{1}\in C^{2,\alpha}({\mathbb{R}})
, there exists a small
C^{2,\lambda}
bounded domain
D_{0}
such that
B_{2\delta}(x_{0})\cap D_{1}^{-}\subset D_{0}\subset D_{1}^{-}\setminus\overline{D}_{2}^{-}
.

For simplicity, we set
(u^{(j)}_{m,+},u^{(j)}_{m,-})=(u_{m,+}(\cdot,x_{j}),u_{m,-}(\cdot,x_{j}))
and
u_{m,j}=\Phi_{k_{+}}(\cdot,x_{j})+u^{(j)}_{m,+}
in
D^{+}_{m}
for
m=1,2
and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It is seen that
(u^{(j)}_{1,-},u_{2,j})
satisfy the following interior transmission problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta V_{j}-V_{j}=h_{1,j}&{\rm in}~D_{0},\\[5.69054pt]
\Delta W_{j}-W_{j}=h_{2,j}&{\rm in}~D_{0},\\[5.69054pt]
V_{j}-W_{j}=g_{1,j}&{\rm on}~\partial D_{0},\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\rho\frac{\partial V_{j}}{\partial n}-\frac{\partial W_{j}}{\partial n}=g_{2,j}&{\rm on}~\partial D_{0},\end{array}\right.

with
(V_{j},W_{j})=(u^{(j)}_{1,-},u_{2,j})
,
\rho=\mu_{1}
and

\displaystyle h_{1,j}=-(k_{1,-}^{2}+1)u^{(j)}_{1,-},\quad h_{2,j}=-(k_{+}^{2}+1)u_{2,j},

\displaystyle g_{1,j}=(u^{(j)}_{1,-}-u_{2,j})|_{\partial D_{0}},\quad g_{2,j}=\left.\left(\displaystyle\mu_{1}\frac{\partial u^{(j)}_{1,-}}{\partial n}-\frac{\partial u_{2,j}}{\partial n}\right)\right|_{\partial D_{0}}.

Clearly,
g_{1,j}=g_{2,j}=0
on
\widetilde{\Gamma}:=\partial D_{0}\cap\Gamma
by the boundary conditions in the TSP. Since
\mu_{1}\neq 1
, by
[
9
, Theorem 6.7]
we obtain that

\displaystyle\|u^{(j)}_{1,-}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}+\|u_{2,j}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq

\displaystyle\;C\left(\|u^{(j)}_{1,-}\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}+\|u_{2,j}\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\|g_{1,j}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial D_{0})}+\|g_{2,j}\|_{H^{-1/2}(\partial D_{0})}\right)

(6.6)

with
C>0
independent of
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It follows from Theorem
17
and the uniform boundedness of
\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})
in
L^{2}(D_{0})
that
\|u^{(j)}_{1,-}\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}\leq C
. Due to the positive distance between
B_{2\delta}(x_{0})
and
\Gamma_{f_{2}}
, we deduce that
\|\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})\|_{1,\alpha,\Gamma_{f_{2}}}+\|\partial_{n}\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})\|_{0,\alpha,\Gamma_{f_{2}}}\leq C
and thus
\|u^{(j)}_{2,+}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C
uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
by Theorem
14
, which implies
\|u_{2,j}\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}\leq C
. Then we show that
g_{1,j}
and
g_{2,j}
are uniformly bounded in
H^{1/2}(\partial D_{0})
and
H^{-1/2}(\partial D_{0})
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, respectively. In fact, by Theorem
17
, it can be derived that

\displaystyle\|g_{1,j}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial D_{0})}+\|g_{2,j}\|_{H^{-1/2}(\partial D_{0})}

\displaystyle=\|g_{1,j}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial D_{0}\setminus\widetilde{\Gamma})}+\|g_{2,j}\|_{H^{-1/2}(\partial D_{0}\setminus\widetilde{\Gamma})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u^{(j)}_{1,-}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0}\setminus\overline{B_{2\delta}(x_{0})})}+\|u^{(j)}_{2,+}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.+\|\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})\|_{H^{1}(D_{0}\setminus\overline{B_{2\delta}(x_{0})})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C.

with
C>0
independent of
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Therefore, by (
6.6
) we have
\|u_{2,j}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C
uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which is a contradiction since
\|u^{(j)}_{2,+}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C
and
\|\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\rightarrow\infty
as
j\rightarrow\infty
. Thus
\Gamma_{f_{1}}=\Gamma_{f_{2}}=\Gamma
.

Next we show
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
. Suppose not, then
\mu_{1}/\mu_{2}\neq 1
. It can be verified that
(u^{(j)}_{1,-},u^{(j)}_{2,-})
is the solution to the interior tranmission problem (
6
) with
(V_{j},W_{j})=(u^{(j)}_{1,-},u^{(j)}_{2,-})
,
\rho=\mu_{1}/\mu_{2}
and

\displaystyle h_{1,j}=-(k_{1,-}^{2}+1)u^{(j)}_{1,-},\quad h_{2,j}=-(k_{2,-}^{2}+1)u^{(j)}_{2,-},

\displaystyle g_{1,j}=(u^{(j)}_{1,-}-u^{(j)}_{2,-})|_{\partial D_{0}},\quad g_{2,j}=\left.\left(\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{2}}\frac{\partial u^{(j)}_{1,-}}{\partial n}-\frac{\partial u^{(j)}_{2,-}}{\partial n}\right)\right|_{\partial D_{0}}.

Also,
g_{1,j}=g_{2,j}=0
on
\widetilde{\Gamma}
. Arguing analogously as above, again from
[
9
, Theorem 6.7]
and Theorem
17
, it is obtained that
\|u^{(j)}_{1,-}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0})}\leq C
uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which however contradicts with Theorem
17
. Hence,
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
.

(ii)Now we consider the case
\mu_{1}=1
or
\mu_{2}=1
. Without loss of generality, we let
\mu_{1}=1
. Suppose
\Gamma_{f_{1}}\neq\Gamma_{f_{2}}
. Then we can find a
x_{0}
such that one of the following is satisfied:

(a)
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{f_{1}}\setminus\Gamma_{f_{2}}
and
f_{1}(x_{0})>f_{2}(x_{0})
;
(b)
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{f_{2}}\setminus\Gamma_{f_{1}}
and
f_{2}(x_{0})>f_{1}(x_{0})
.

As in (i) we also have
x_{0},\delta,x_{j}
and
D_{0}
with the same properties. Denote by
(\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{m,+},\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{m,-})
the solutions to the TSP with respect to the incident hypersingular point source
\widetilde{u}^{i}_{j}={\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})\cdot n(x_{0})
for
m=1,2
and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Set
\widetilde{u}_{m,j}={\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{k_{+}}(x,x_{j})\cdot n(x_{0})+\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{m,+}
in
D^{+}_{m}
. Again from the proof of
[
49
, Theorem 4.1]
, we can get that
\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{1,+}(x)=\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{2,+}(x)
for all
x\in D^{+}_{1}\cap D^{+}_{2}
and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Note that this also holds when
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}\neq 1
.

In case (a), analog to (
6
),
(\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{1,-},\widetilde{u}_{2,j})
solves the following another type interior transmission problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{1,-}+k_{1,-}^{2}\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{1,-}=0&{\rm in}~D_{0},\\[5.69054pt]
\Delta\widetilde{u}_{2,j}+k_{+}^{2}\widetilde{u}_{2,j}=0&{\rm in}~D_{0},\\[5.69054pt]
\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{1,-}-\widetilde{u}_{2,j}=\hat{g}_{1,j}&{\rm on}~\partial D_{0},\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{1,-}}{\partial n}-\frac{\partial\widetilde{u}_{2,j}}{\partial n}=\hat{g}_{2,j}&{\rm on}~\partial D_{0},\end{array}\right.

with
\hat{g}_{1,j}=u^{(j)}_{1,-}-u_{2,j}
and
\hat{g}_{2,j}=\partial_{n}u^{(j)}_{1,-}-\partial_{n}u_{2,j}
satisfying
\hat{g}_{1,j}=\hat{g}_{2,j}=0
on
\widetilde{\Gamma}
. Define
u_{j}=(1-\chi_{1})(\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{1,-}-\widetilde{u}_{2,j})
, where
\chi_{1}
is a cut off function such that
\chi_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
0\leq\chi_{1}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2\delta}(x_{0})},\\[5.69054pt]
1,&{\rm in}~B_{3\delta/2}(x_{0}).\end{array}\right.

Then we see that
u_{j}\in H^{1}_{\Delta}(D_{0})
and
u_{j}=\hat{g}_{1,j}
,
\partial_{n}u_{j}=\hat{g}_{2,j}
on
\partial D_{0}
. Since
k_{+}\neq k_{1,-}
under the conditions in Theorem
14
, by the classic results in
[
10
]
, we know that we can let
D_{0}
be sufficiently small such that the problem (
6
) is well-posed in
L^{2}(D_{0})\times L^{2}(D_{0})
and

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{1,-}\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}+\|\widetilde{u}_{2,j}\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}\leq C\|u_{j}\|_{H^{1}_{\Delta}(D_{0})}.

We claim now that
\|u_{j}\|_{H^{1}_{\Delta}(D_{0})}\leq C
uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It suffices to show that
\|\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{1,-}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0}\setminus\overline{B_{3\delta/2}(x_{0})})}+\|\widetilde{u}_{2,j}\|_{H^{1}(D_{0}\setminus\overline{B_{3\delta/2}(x_{0})})}\leq C
, which is a direct application of Theorem
18
and the positive distance between
B_{2\delta}(x_{0})
and
\Gamma_{f_{2}}
. Hence, we get
\|\widetilde{u}^{(j)}_{1,-}\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}\leq C
and thus
\|{\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})\cdot n(x_{0})\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}\leq C
by Theorem
18
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\Gamma_{f_{1}}=\Gamma_{f_{2}}=\Gamma
. Then following similarly as the arguments in (i), we can obtain that
\mu_{2}=\mu_{1}=1
.

In case (b), if
\mu_{2}\neq 1
, for
(u_{1,j},u_{2,-}^{(j)})
we can construct interior transmission problem in the form of (

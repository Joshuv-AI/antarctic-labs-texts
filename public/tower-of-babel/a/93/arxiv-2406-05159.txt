Volume preserving nonhomogeneous Gauss curvature flow in hyperbolic space
Yong Wei, Bo Yang, Tailong Zhou

Abstract
. We consider the volume preserving flow of smooth, closed and convex hypersurfaces in the hyperbolic space ℍ n + 1 \mathbb{H}^{n+1} with speed given by a general nonhomogeneous function of the Gauss curvature. For a large class of speed functions, we prove that the solution of the flow remains convex, exists for all positive time t ∈ [ 0 , ∞ ) t\in[0,\infty) and converges to a geodesic sphere exponentially as t → ∞ t\to\infty in the smooth topology. A key step is to show the L 1 L^{1} oscillation decay of the Gauss curvature to its average along a subsequence of times going to the infinity, which combined with an argument using the hyperbolic curvature measure theory implies the Hausdorff convergence.

1.
Introduction

Let
X_{0}:M^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
be a smooth embedding such that
M_{0}=X_{0}(M)
is a smooth, closed and convex hypersurface in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. We consider the volume preserving curvature flow
X:M^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
satisfying

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=&~\Big(\phi(t)-f(K(x,t))\Big)\nu(x,t),\\
X(\cdot,0)=&~X_{0}(\cdot),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\nu
is the unit outward normal of
M_{t}=X(M,t)
,
K
is the Gauss curvature of
M_{t}
and

(1.2)

\phi(t)=\frac{1}{|M_{t}|}\int_{M_{t}}f(K)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

such that the domain
\Omega_{t}
enclosed by
M_{t}
has a fixed volume
|\Omega_{t}|=|\Omega_{0}|
along the flow (
1.1
). We assume that the function
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following conditions:

Assumption 1.1
.

f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and
C^{2}
differentiable in
(0,+\infty)
, and satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

f(x)>0
,
f^{\prime}(x)>0
for
x>0
;

(2)

\lim\limits_{x\to{+\infty}}f(x)=+\infty
;

(3)

\exists\Theta>0
such that
f(x)\leq\Theta f^{\prime}(x)x
for
x>0
;

(4)

xf^{\prime\prime}(x)+f^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
for
x>0
.

Remark 1.2
.

A particular example of functions satisfying Assumption
1.1
is

f(x)=x^{\alpha},\quad\alpha>0.

In this case, the flow (
1.1
) is the classical volume preserving
\alpha
-Gauss curvature flow (
1.5
) in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, which was considered by the authors in
[
43
]
. Other natural examples include the linear combination of powers

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}x^{k_{i}},\quad a_{i}>0,\quad k_{i}>0

and

\displaystyle f(x)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{x}-1,

\displaystyle f(x)=

\displaystyle x^{\alpha}\ln(x+1),\quad\alpha>0.

Moreover, any positive strictly increasing convex
C^{2}
function
f
with
f(0)=0
satisfies Assumption
1.1
.

Before describing our main result, we recall the following definitions of convexity of hypersurfaces in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.

Definition 1.3
.

(1)

A smooth closed hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is called convex if all of its principal curvatures
\kappa_{i},i=1,\cdots,n
are positive everywhere on
M
.
We also say that a hypersurface is weakly convex if all principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}\geq 0
.

(2)

A smooth closed hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is called
h
-convex (also called horospherically convex), if all of its principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}\geq 1,i=1,\cdots,n
everywhere on
M
.

(3)

We say that a smooth closed hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
has positive sectional curvatures if its sectional curvatures
K(e_{i},e_{j})=\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}-1>0
for all
i\neq j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. This condition is weaker than
h
-convexity but is stronger than the convexity
\kappa_{i}>0,i=1,\cdots,n.

Our main result of this paper is the following convergence result for the flow (
1.1
) with convex initial hypersurface.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X_{0}:M^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
be a smooth embedding such that
M_{0}=X_{0}(M)
is a closed convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Then the volume preserving flow (
1.1
) with
f
satisfying Assumption
1.1
has a unique smooth convex solution
M_{t}
for all time
t\in[0,\infty)
, and as
t\to\infty
the solution
M_{t}
converges smoothly and exponentially to a geodesic sphere of radius
\rho_{\infty}
which encloses the same volume as
M_{0}
.

The volume preserving mean curvature flow

(1.3)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=(\phi(t)-H)\nu(x,t)

was introduced by Huisken
[
27
]
in 1987 for convex hypersurfaces in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and it has been proved that for any smooth convex initial hypersurface, the flow (
1.3
) converges smoothly to a round sphere. There are further generalizations of the flow (
1.3
) for convex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
H
replaced by more general curvature functions including powers of the
k
th mean curvature
\sigma_{k}(\kappa),k=1,\cdots,n
. See
[
6
,
11
,
10
,
14
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
39
]
for instance. In particular, Bertini and Sinestrari
[
11
]
considered the volume preserving non-homogeneous mean curvature flow of convex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

(1.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=(\phi(t)-f(H))\nu(x,t),

for a large class of nonhomogeneous function
f(H)
of the mean curvature with
f
satisfying some conditions.

The analogue of the flow (
1.3
) in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
was first studied by Cabezas-Rivas and Miquel
[
12
]
in 2007 assuming that the initial hypersurface is
h
-convex. The nonhomogeneous version (
1.4
) in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
was considered by Bertini and Pipoli
[
9
]
also assuming
h
-convexity initially. Generalizations of the flow (
1.3
) in hyperbolic space with
H
replaced by more general curvature functions were studied in
[
3
,
4
,
26
,
32
,
42
]
. In most cases, the
h
-convexity is assumed in order to prove the smooth convergence of the solution to geodesic spheres. The main reasons are that the
h
-convexity is convenient for the analysis of the curvature evolution equations and also
h
-convexity implies that the outer radius of the enclosed domain is uniformly controlled by its inner radius. In
[
3
]
, the first author with Andrews and Chen proved the smooth convergence of volume preserving
k
th mean curvature flows in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
for initial hypersurfaces with positive sectional curvatures. This condition is weaker than
h
-convexity but still stronger than the convexity.

Recently, the authors
[
43
]
considered the volume preserving
\alpha
-Gauss curvature flow

(1.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=(\phi(t)-K^{\alpha})\nu(x,t)

for convex hypersurfaces in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. We showed that for any
\alpha>0
, the flow (
1.5
) evolves any smooth, closed convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
to a geodesic sphere. The key ingredient we used in
[
43
]
is the projection method via the Klein model of the hyperbolic space which was described earlier by the first author and Andrews in
[
4
]
. Based on this we treated the flow as an equivalent flow in the Euclidean space and this allows us to derive a time-dependent positive lower bound on the principal curvatures, and a time-dependent upper bound on the Gauss curvature
K
. A continuity argument then implies that the flow exists for all positive time. Moreover, for the convergence result we used the curvature measure theory for convex bodies in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
to show the Hausdorff convergence and an Alexandrov reflection argument to control the center of the inner ball of the evolving domains. The curve case of (
1.5
) was also treated earlier by the first and second authors in
[
44
]
. The results in
[
44
,
43
]
are the first results on non-local type volume preserving curvature flows in the hyperbolic space with only convexity required on the initial hypersurface.

Our Theorem
1.4
in this paper generalizes the results
[
44
,
43
]
on volume preserving
\alpha
-Gauss curvature flow (
1.5
) to volume preserving nonhomogeneous Gauss curvature flow (
1.1
) for a large class of nonhomogeneous functions. In this paper, instead of projecting the flow (
1.1
) to the Euclidean space as in
[
43
]
, we prove the curvature lower bound directly by analyzing an auxiliary function (see §
3.2
for details)

(1.6)

Q(p,t)=\log{\mathfrak{b}}+A\rho,

where
\mathfrak{b}
is the largest reciprocal of the principal curvatures
\kappa_{i},i=1,\cdots,n
, and
\rho
is the radial function of the evolving hypersurface
M_{t}
on the time interval when
M_{t}
is star-shaped with respect to some point
p_{0}
. This avoids the use of more complicated projection method. To show the upper bound on the Gauss curvature, we employ Tso’s method as in
[
43
]
. Since we only assumed convexity and the speed function
f
is not necessarily homogeneous, the analysis of
f
and the terms involving global term
\phi(t)
need to be carefully treated.

For the long time existence of the flow, we need to show that the solutions remain smooth as long as the curvatures are bounded. For general non-homogeneous function
f
, the flow (
1.1
) may not be concave with respect to spacial second derivatives, we can not apply Krylov-Safonov’s result to derive the
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate of the solution. Instead, we apply a result in
[
7
, Theorem 6]
and view

as an increasing function of a concave operator
K^{1/n}
. To study the asymptotical behavior of
M_{t}
as
t\to\infty
, we note that the a prior estimates we obtained depend on the time and may degenerate as time
t\to\infty
. To overcome this difficulty, as in
[
43
]
we employ the curvature measure theory for convex bodies in hyperbolic space: For general nonhomogeneous function, we still have the monotonicity of the quermassintegral
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})
of the evolving domain
\Omega_{t}
. This together with the long time existence of the flow implies that

(1.7)

\int_{M_{t_{i}}}{|K-\bar{K}|d\mu_{t_{i}}}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\,\,t_{i}\to\infty

along a subsequence of times
t_{i}\to\infty
, where
\bar{K}
denotes the average integral of
K
. Then we can argue as in
[
43
]
to conclude the Hausdorff convergence using curvature measure theory and Alexandrov reflection method, and then improve it to the smooth convergence.

Throughout the proof, the presence of the nonhomogeneous speed function

introduces some technical difficulties. Assumption
1.1
is crucial for proving the a prior estimates of the flow (
1.1
). In fact, the flow (
1.1
) is parabolic and has short-time existence due to item (
1
); items (
3
) and (
4
) are used to prove the time-dependent lower bound on the principal curvatures (see §

); and items (
2
) - (
3
) are used to derive the time-dependent upper bound on the Gauss curvature of the flow (see §

).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some preliminary results concerning the geometry of hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space, the evolution equations for geometric quantities along the flow (
1.1
) and the quermassintegrals for bounded domains in hyperbolic space.

2.1.
Hyperbolic space

The hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1},n\geq 1
can be viewed as a warped product manifold
(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}})
with

g_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}=d\rho^{2}+\sinh^{2}\rho g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}},

where
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
is the round metric on unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Let
D
be the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. The vector field
V=\sinh\rho\partial_{\rho}
is a conformal Killing field satisfying
DV=\cosh\rho g_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}.

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with a smooth boundary
M=\partial\Omega
. Then
M
is a smooth convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. We denote by
g_{ij},h_{ij}
and
\nu
the induced metric, the second fundamental form and the unit outward normal vector of
M
respectively. The eigenvalues of the Weingarten tensor
h_{i}^{j}:=g^{jk}h_{ki}
are the principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
. As
M
is convex, there exists a point
p_{0}\in\Omega
such that
M
is star-shaped with respect to
p_{0}
and can be written as the radial graph
M=\{(\rho(\theta),\theta),~\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}
with respect to
p_{0}
for a smooth function
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. Equivalently, the support function of
M
with respect to the point
p_{0}\in\Omega
defined by

u=\langle V,\nu\rangle=\langle\sinh\rho\partial_{\rho},\nu\rangle

is positive everywhere on
M
. It is well known that (see e.g.
[
25
, §4]
)

(2.1)

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle=\rho_{i}\rho_{j}+\sinh^{2}\rho\sigma_{ij},

(2.2)

\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sinh^{2}\rho+|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2}}}(-(\sinh\rho)\rho_{ij}+2(\cosh\rho)\rho_{i}\rho_{j}+\sinh^{2}\rho\cosh\rho\sigma_{ij}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2}/\sinh^{2}\rho}}\left(1,-\frac{\rho_{1}}{\sinh^{2}\rho},\cdots,-\frac{\rho_{n}}{\sinh^{2}\rho}\right),

(2.4)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\frac{\sinh^{2}\rho}{\sqrt{\sinh^{2}\rho+|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2}}},

where
\bar{\nabla}
is the covariant derivative on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
with respect to the round metric
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}=(\sigma_{ij})
and
\rho_{i}=\bar{\nabla}\rho,\rho_{ij}=\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\rho
. It follows that the Gauss curvature of
M
can be expressed as a function of
\rho
and its up to second derivatives:

(2.5)

K=\frac{\det h_{ij}}{\det g_{ij}}=\frac{\det(-\sinh\rho\rho_{ij}+2\cosh\rho\rho_{i}\rho_{j}+\sinh^{2}\rho\cosh\rho\sigma_{ij})}{(\sinh^{2}\rho+|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2})^{\frac{n+2}{2}}(\sinh\rho)^{2(n-1)}}.

The following lemma will be used to prove the lower bound of the principal curvatures.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have

(2.22)

\nabla_{i}\rho=\langle\partial_{\rho},e_{i}\rangle,\qquad\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\rho=\coth\rho\left(g_{ij}-\nabla_{i}\rho\nabla_{j}\rho\right)-\frac{uh_{ij}}{\sinh\rho}.

Proof.

Since

is a conformal Killing vector field, i.e.,

\langle D_{X}(\sinh\rho\partial_{\rho}),Y\rangle=\cosh\rho\langle X,Y\rangle

, we have (see, e.g.,
[

)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\cosh\rho=

\displaystyle\langle\sinh\rho\partial_{\rho},e_{i}\rangle,

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\cosh\rho=

\displaystyle\cosh\rho g_{ij}-uh_{ij}.

Observing that

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\cosh\rho=

\displaystyle\sinh\rho\nabla_{i}\rho,

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\cosh\rho=

\displaystyle\sinh\rho\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\rho+\cosh\rho\nabla_{j}\rho\nabla_{i}\rho,

2.3.
Quermassintegrals

Let
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{H}^{n+1})
be the set of compact convex sets in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with nonempty interior. For any
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{H}^{n+1})
, the quermassintegrals of
\Omega
are defined as follows (see
[
40

1
1

1

Note that the definition for
\mathcal{A}_{k}
given here is the same as that for

given in

]
up to a constant. In fact, we have
\mathcal{A}_{k}=(n+1)\binom{n}{k}W_{k+1}

(2.24)

\mathcal{A}_{k}(\Omega)=(n-k)\binom{n}{k}\frac{\omega_{k}\cdots\omega_{0}}{\omega_{n-1}\cdots\omega_{n-k-1}}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{k+1}}{\chi(L_{k+1}\cap\Omega)dL_{k+1}}

for

, where

denotes the area of

\mathcal{L}_{k+1}
is the space of
(k+1)

in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
\binom{n}{k}=\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}
. The function

is defined to be 1 if

and to be 0 otherwise. In particular,

If the boundary
M=\partial\Omega
is smooth (or at least of class

), we can define the principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n})
as the eigenvalues of the Weingarten matrix

of
M

, the

th mean curvature

of

These include the mean curvature

and Gauss curvature

as special cases.
In the smooth case, the quermassintegrals and the curvature integrals of a smooth convex domain

in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
are related as follows:

(2.25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}(\Omega)=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}{\sigma_{1}}d\mu-n\mathcal{A}_{-1}(\Omega),

(2.26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k}(\Omega)=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}{\sigma_{k}}d\mu-\frac{n-k+1}{k-1}\mathcal{A}_{k-2}(\Omega),\quad k=2,\cdots,n.

The quermassintegrals for smooth domains satisfy a nice variational property (see
[

(2.27)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{A}_{k}(\Omega_{t})=(k+1)\int_{M_{t}}\eta\sigma_{k+1}d\mu_{t},\quad k=0,\cdots,n-1

along any normal variation with velocity

The quermassintegrals defined by (

is defined as

\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,L):=\mathrm{inf}\{\lambda>0:\Omega\subset B_{\lambda}(L)\,\text{and}\,L\subset B_{\lambda}(\Omega)\},

where
B_{\lambda}(L):=\{q\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}|~\mathrm{d}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}(q,L)<\lambda\}
.

\displaystyle-\frac{f^{\prime}K}{\mathfrak{b}}\biggl[(1+c_{3}A)\left(\sum_{k}b^{kk}\right)\mathfrak{b}-\left(n+\frac{f}{f^{\prime}K}\right)\mathfrak{b}^{2}

(3.24)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{nAu}{\sinh\rho}\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{b}H-n\biggr],

where we have thrown away the negative terms
-\frac{f}{\mathfrak{b}}
and
-Af\sqrt{1+\frac{|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2}}{\sinh^{2}{\rho}}}
in the second inequality of (

). Since

(3.25)

\sum_{k}{b^{kk}}>\mathfrak{b},\quad\frac{f}{f^{\prime}K}\leq\Theta,\quad\frac{u}{\sinh\rho}\leq 1,\quad H\geq\frac{n}{\mathfrak{b}},

where
\Theta
is the constant in item (
3
) in Assumption
1.1
, substituting (
3.25
) into (
3.24
) gives the following estimates

(3.26)

Q_{2}\leq-f^{\prime}K\Big((1+c_{3}A-n-\Theta)\mathfrak{b}-nA\Big).

Choosing

(3.27)

A=\frac{n+\Theta}{c_{3}},

which depends only on
n,M_{0}
and
\Theta
, we see that if

(3.28)

\mathfrak{b}>\frac{n(n+\Theta)}{c_{3}}

then
Q_{2}<0
.

Combing (
3.17
) with (
3.20
) and (
3.28
), we know that if

\mathfrak{b}>\max\left\{\frac{Ac_{4}+\sqrt{A^{2}c_{4}^{2}+4}}{2},\frac{n(n+\Theta)}{c_{3}}\right\}=:a_{1},

where
A=\frac{n+\Theta}{c_{3}}
, then at the point
(\bar{p},\bar{t})
, we have

0\leq\partial_{t}Q-f^{\prime}\dot{K}^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\ell}{Q}=Q_{1}+Q_{2}<0,

which leads to a contradiction. Therefore we have

(3.29)

\mathfrak{b}(\bar{p},\bar{t})\leq~a_{1}.

Since
(\bar{p},\bar{t})
is a maximum point of the function
Q
, Combining (
3.15
) with (
3.29
), we have

(3.30)

Q(p,t)\leq\max\left\{\log\max_{p\in M}{\mathfrak{b}(p,t_{0})}+2Ac_{2},\log a_{1}+2Ac_{2}\right\}

for
(p,t)\in M\times[t_{0},\min\{T,t_{0}+\tau\})
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}(p,t)\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\max_{p\in M}{\mathfrak{b}(p,t_{0})},a_{1}\right\}\mathrm{e}^{A(2c_{2}-\frac{c_{1}}{2})}

(3.31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\max\left\{\max_{p\in M}{\mathfrak{b}(p,t_{0})},a_{1}\right\}\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{1}}

for
(p,t)\in M\times[t_{0},\min\{T,t_{0}+\tau\})
, where
\Lambda_{1}=\frac{n+\Theta}{a_{1}}(2c_{2}-\frac{c_{1}}{2})
depending only on
n,M_{0}
and
\Theta
.

Note that
t_{0}\in[t_{0}-\frac{\tau}{2},\min\{T,t_{0}+\frac{\tau}{2}\})
. Applying the above argument for the time interval
[t_{0}-\frac{\tau}{2},\min\{T,t_{0}+\frac{\tau}{2}\})
gives

(3.32)

\max_{p\in M}{\mathfrak{b}(p,t_{0})}\leq\max\left\{\max_{p\in M}{\mathfrak{b}(p,t_{0}-\frac{\tau}{2})},a_{1}\right\}\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{1}}.

Combining (
3.2
) with (
3.32
) and the fact that
\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{1}}\geq 1
, we have

(3.33)

\mathfrak{b}(p,t)\leq\max\left\{\max_{p\in M}{\mathfrak{b}(p,t_{0}-\frac{\tau}{2})},a_{1}\right\}\mathrm{e}^{2\Lambda_{1}}.

By repeating the argument finitely many times, we finally get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}(p,t)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\max_{p\in M}{\mathfrak{b}(p,0)},a_{1}\right\}\mathrm{e}^{\left(\left[\frac{2t_{0}}{\tau}\right]+2\right)\Lambda_{1}}

(3.34)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\max_{p\in M}{\mathfrak{b}(p,0)},a_{1}\right\}\mathrm{e}^{\left(\frac{2t}{\tau}+2\right)\Lambda_{1}}:=\Lambda_{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\Lambda_{1}}{\tau}t}

for all
(p,t)\in M\times[t_{0},\min\{T,t_{0}+\tau\})
, where
[\cdot]
denotes the integer part of a real constant, and
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}:=\max\left\{\max_{p\in M}{\mathfrak{b}(p,0)},a_{3}\right\}\mathrm{e}^{2\Lambda_{1}}
are constants depending only on
n,M_{0}
and
\Theta
. Since

is arbitrary, we conclude that the principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}
of the solution
M_{t}
of the flow (
1.1
) satisfy

\kappa_{i}\geq\Lambda_{2}^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2\Lambda_{1}}{\tau}t},\qquad i=1,\dots,n,

for all time
t\in[0,T)
. This completes the proof of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

3.3.
Upper bound of Gauss curvature

Now we prove the upper bound of the Gauss curvature for the solution
M_{t}
along the flow (
1.1
).

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
M_{t},\ t\in[0,T)
be the smooth solution of the flow (
1.1
) starting from a smooth closed convex hypersurface
M_{0}
. If
T<\infty
, then there exists a constant
C
depending on
n,M_{0},\Theta
and
T
such that the Gauss curvature
K
of
M_{t}
satisfies

\max_{M_{t}}K\leq C

for any
t\in[0,T)
, where
\Theta
is the constant in item (
3
) of Assumption
1.1
.

Proof.

For any given
t_{0}\in[0,T)
, let
B_{\rho_{0}}(p_{0})
be the inball of
\Omega_{t_{0}}
centered at some point
p_{0}\in\Omega_{t_{0}}
, where
\rho_{0}=\rho_{-}(t_{0})
. Consider the support function
u(x,t)=\sinh\rho_{p_{0}}(x)\langle{\partial_{\rho_{p_{0}}},\nu}\rangle
of
M_{t}
with respect to the point
p_{0}
, where
\rho_{p_{0}}(x)
is the distance function in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
to the point
p_{0}
. Since
M_{t}
is convex for all
t\in[0,T)
, by (
3.6
) we have

(3.35)

2c\leq u\leq\sinh(2c_{2})

on
M_{t}
for all
t\in\left[t_{0},\min\{T,t_{0}+\tau\}\right)
. We define the auxiliary function as in
[
41
]

W=\frac{f(K)}{u-c},

which is well-defined for
t\in\left[t_{0},\min\{T,t_{0}+\tau\}\right)
. We shall apply the maximum principle to the evolution equation of
W
to derive the upper bound of
K
.

Combining (
2.8
) and (
2.21
), we compute that along the flow (
1.1
) the function
W
evolves as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{W}=

\displaystyle f^{\prime}\dot{K}^{ij}\left(W_{ij}+\frac{2}{u-c}u_{i}W_{j}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi(t)}{u-c}\Big(f^{\prime}(HK-\sigma_{n-1}(\kappa))+W\cosh{\rho_{p_{0}}}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{f}{(u-c)^{2}}(f+nf^{\prime}K)\cosh{\rho_{p_{0}}}(x)

\displaystyle-\frac{cf}{(u-c)^{2}}f^{\prime}HK-Wf^{\prime}\sigma_{n-1}(\kappa)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f^{\prime}\dot{K}^{ij}\left(W_{ij}+\frac{2}{u-c}u_{i}W_{j}\right)+\frac{\phi(t)}{u-c}f^{\prime}\sigma_{n-1}(\kappa)

(3.36)

\displaystyle+(1+n\frac{f^{\prime}K}{f})W^{2}\cosh{\rho_{p_{0}}}(x)-c\frac{f^{\prime}K}{f}HW^{2}.

Let
\widetilde{W}(t)=\max_{M_{t}}W(x,t)
. Noting that
f(K)=(u-c)W
, by the definition (
1.2
) and the upper bound (
3.35
) of
u
, we have:

\displaystyle\phi(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|M_{t}|}\int_{M_{t}}f(K)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{M_{t}}f(K(\cdot,t))\leq(\sinh(2c_{2})-c)\widetilde{W}.

By the lower bound on the principal curvatures in Lemma
3.3
, we also have

\displaystyle\sigma_{n-1}(\kappa)=

\displaystyle K(\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}+\cdots\frac{1}{\kappa_{n}})

(3.37)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle nK(\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\kappa_{i})^{-1}\leq nK\Lambda_{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\Lambda_{1}}{\tau}T}.

It follows that

(3.38)

\frac{\phi(t)}{u-c}f^{\prime}\sigma_{n-1}(\kappa)\leq~n(\sinh(2c_{2})-c)\Lambda_{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\Lambda_{1}T}{\tau}}\frac{f^{\prime}K}{f}\widetilde{W}^{2}.

Since
H\geq nK^{1/n}
, the last term of (
3.3
) satisfies

(3.39)

-c\frac{f^{\prime}K}{f}HW^{2}\leq-nc\frac{f^{\prime}K^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}{f}W^{2}.

Substituting (
3.38
) and (
3.39
) into (
3.3
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{W}\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}^{2}\cosh(2c_{2})+n\widetilde{W}^{2}\frac{f^{\prime}K}{f}\Big((\sinh(2c_{2})-c)\Lambda_{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\Lambda_{1}T}{\tau}}

(3.40)

\displaystyle\quad+\cosh(2c_{2})-cK^{1/n}\Big).

Denote

\bar{c}=(nc)^{-n}\left((\cosh(2c_{2})+1)\Theta+n(\sinh(2c_{2})-c)\Lambda_{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\Lambda_{1}T}{\tau}}+n\cosh(2c_{2})\right)^{n},

which depends on
n,M_{0},\Theta
and
T<\infty
. Suppose that

\widetilde{W}(t)\geq\frac{f(\bar{c})}{c}.

Then for any
x\in M_{t}
with
W(x,t)=\widetilde{W}(t)
, there holds

\displaystyle f(K(x,t))=

\displaystyle W(x,t)(u-c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}(t)(u-c)\geq f(\bar{c}).

As
f
is strictly increasing, this implies that

(3.41)

K(x,t)\geq\bar{c}

for any
x\in M_{t}
with
W(x,t)=\widetilde{W}(t)
. Substituting (
3.41
) into (
3.3
) and noting that
f^{\prime}K/f\geq 1/\Theta
by item (
3
) of Assumption
1.1
, we have

(3.42)

\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{W}(t)\leq-\widetilde{W}^{2}(t)

whenever
\widetilde{W}(t)\geq{f(\bar{c})}/{c}
. It follows that

(3.43)

\widetilde{W}(t)\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{\widetilde{W}^{-1}(t_{0})+t-t_{0}},\frac{f(\bar{c})}{c}\right\}

for all time
t\in\left[t_{0},\min\{T,t_{0}+\tau\}\right)
.

For
t_{0}=0
, we obtain from (
3.43
) the upper bound

\widetilde{W}(t)\leq\max\left\{\widetilde{W}(0),\frac{f(\bar{c})}{c}\right\},\quad\forall t\in[0,\min\{\tau,T\})

and so

\displaystyle f(K)=

\displaystyle(u-c)W

(3.44)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sinh(2c_{2})\max\left\{\widetilde{W}(0),\frac{f(\bar{c})}{c}\right\},\quad\forall t\in[0,\min\{\tau,T\}).

Next, for
t_{0}=\tau/2
, the estimate (
3.43
) implies

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{\widetilde{W}^{-1}(t_{0})+t-t_{0}},\frac{f(\bar{c})}{c}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{t-t_{0}},\frac{f(\bar{c})}{c}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{2}{\tau},\frac{f(\bar{c})}{c}\right\}

for
t\in[\tau,\min\{3\tau/2,T\})
, and so

(3.45)

f(K)\leq\sinh(2c_{2})\max\left\{\frac{2}{\tau},\frac{f(\bar{c})}{c}\right\}

for
t\in[\tau,\min\{3\tau/2,T\})
. Repeating the above argument for
t_{0}=m\tau/2(m\geq 2)
, we can get the estimate (
3.45
) for
t\in[\frac{(m+1)\tau}{2},\min\{\frac{(m+2)\tau}{2},T\})
, which covers the whole time interval
[0,T)
.

Combining (
3.3
), (
3.45
) and the fact that
f
is an increasing function with
\lim\limits_{x\to\infty}f(x)=+\infty
(see items (
1
) and (
2
) in Assumption
1.1
), we obtain the upper bound
K\leq C
for a constant
C
depending on
n,M_{0},\Theta
and
T
. This completes the proof of Proposition
3.5
.
∎

4.
Long time existence

In this section, we prove the long time existence of the flow (
1.1
). We need to show that the solution remains smooth as long as the curvatures are bounded. To prove this, we need the following result which is a special case of Theorem 6 in
[
7
]
(see also
[
2
, Theorem 3.2]
).

Theorem 4.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
u\in C^{4}(\Omega\times[0,T))
be a function satisfying

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u=F(D^{2}u,Du,u,x,t),

where
F
is
C^{2}
and is elliptic, i.e.,
\lambda I\leq(\dot{F}^{ij})\leq\Lambda I
for some constants
\Lambda>\lambda>0
. Suppose that
F
can be written as
F=\varphi(G(D^{2}u,Du,u,x,t))
, where
G
is concave with respect to
D^{2}u
and
\varphi
is an increasing function on the range of
G
. Then in any relatively compact
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
and for any
\tau\in(0,T)
we have

\|u\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\Omega^{\prime}\times(\tau,T))}\leq C,

where
\beta\in(0,1)
depends on
n,\lambda
and
\Lambda
, and
C
depends on
\lambda,\Lambda,\|u\|_{C^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T))}
,
\tau,\mathrm{dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)
and the bounds on the first and second derivatives of
G
.

The advantage of the above theorem is that it allows to relax the concavity hypothesis of the usual regularity theorem for fully nonlinear parabolic equation.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
M_{0}
be a smooth closed convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
M_{t}
be the smooth solution of the flow (
1.1
) starting from
M_{0}
with
\phi(t)
given by (
1.2
) and the speed function satisfies the assumption
1.1
. Then
M_{t}
remains convex and exists for all time
t\in[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

We will argue by contradiction. Let
[0,T)
be the maximal interval such that the solution of the flow (
1.1
) exists with
T<\infty
. Then combining Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.5
yields that the principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n})
of
M_{t}
satisfy

(4.1)

0<\underline{\kappa}_{0}\leq\kappa_{i}\leq\overline{\kappa}_{0},\quad i=1,\dots,n

for all time
t\in[0,T)
, where the constants
\underline{\kappa}_{0},\overline{\kappa}_{0}
depend on
n,M_{0},\Theta
and
T
.

To prove the long time existence of the solution
M_{t}
of the flow (
1.1
), we need to derive the higher order regularity estimates. Recall that up to a tangential diffeomorphism, the flow equation (
1.1
) is equivalent to the following scalar parabolic equation

(4.2)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\rho=\left(\phi(t)-f(K)\right)\sqrt{1+\frac{|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2}}{\sinh^{2}{\rho}}},

of the radial graph function
\rho
over
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
K=K(\bar{\nabla}^{2}\rho,\bar{\nabla}\rho,\rho)
is expressed in (
2.5
) and
\bar{\nabla}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection with respect to the round metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Denote the right hand side of (
4.2
) by
F[\bar{\nabla}^{2}\rho,\bar{\nabla}\rho,\rho,t]
.

For any
t_{0}\in[0,T)
, we consider the solution of
M_{t}
in the time interval
[t_{0},\min\{t_{0}+\tau,T\})
. Since only
f(K)
in
F[\bar{\nabla}^{2}\rho,\bar{\nabla}\rho,\rho,t]
depends on
\bar{\nabla}^{2}\rho
, we calculate that

\displaystyle\dot{F}^{ij}=

\displaystyle-f^{\prime}\frac{\partial K}{\partial\rho_{ij}}\sqrt{1+\frac{|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2}}{\sinh^{2}{\rho}}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime}\sinh\rho}{(\sinh^{2}\rho+|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2})^{\frac{n+2}{2}}(\sinh\rho)^{2(n-1)}}\frac{\partial\det h_{ij}}{\partial h_{ij}}\sqrt{1+\frac{|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2}}{\sinh^{2}{\rho}}},

where
h_{ij}
is expressed in (
2.2
). The
C^{0},C^{1}
estimates obtained in (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and the curvature bound (
4.1
) implies that
F
is elliptic, i.e.,
\lambda I\leq(\dot{F}^{ij})\leq\Lambda I
for some constants
\Lambda>\lambda>0
depending on
n,M_{0},\Theta
and
T
. Moreover, since it’s well known that
K^{1/n}
is a concave operator with respect to second derivatives, and
f(K)
is a strictly increasing function of
K^{1/n}
, by Theorem
4.1
we derive a
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate on
\rho
, see also the arguments in
[
14
,
34
]
for the
C^{2,\gamma}
estimate of the solutions to volume preserving curvature flows. Then by the parabolic Schauder theory (see
[
31
]
), we can deduce all higher order regularity estimates of
\rho
on
[t_{0},\min\{t_{0}+\tau,T\})
. As
t_{0}
is arbitrary, we can obtain the smoothness of the flow for all time
t\in[0,T)
and a standard continuation argument then shows that
T=+\infty
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Note that the curvature estimate (
4.1
) of the solution
M_{t}
of the flow (
1.1
) depends on time
t
and may degenerate as time
t\to\infty
. To study the asymptotical behavior of
M_{t}
as
t\to\infty
, we still need to get an uniform curvature estimate which does not depend on time. This will be obtained in the next two sections.

5.
Hausdorff convergence

In this section, we prove the monotonicity of the quermassintegral
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})
, the subsequential Hausdorff convergence of the solution
M_{t}
of (
1.1
) and the convergence of the center of the inner ball of
\Omega_{t}
to a fixed point.

Denote the average integral of the Gauss curvature by

(5.1)

\bar{K}=\frac{1}{|M_{t}|}\int_{M_{t}}{Kd\mu_{t}}=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{n}(\Omega_{t})+\frac{1}{n-1}\mathcal{A}_{n-2}(\Omega_{t})}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\Omega_{t})},

where the second equality is due to (
2.26
). It follows from the monotonicity (
2.28
) of quermassintegrals with respect to inclusion of convex sets and the estimates on inner radius and outer radius in Lemma
3.1
that there exists uniform positive constants
m_{1},m_{2},a
and
b
depending only on
n
and
M_{0}
, such that

(5.2)

0<m_{1}\leq|M_{t}|=A_{0}(\Omega_{t})\leq m_{2},\quad a\leq\bar{K}\leq b.

5.1.
Monotonicity for
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
M_{t}
be a smooth convex solution of the volume preserving flow (
1.1
). Denote
\Omega_{t}
the domain enclosed by
M_{t}
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})
is monotone decreasing in time
t
, which is strictly decreasing unless
\Omega_{t}
is a geodesic ball.

Proof.

From the evolution equation (
2.27
) for the quermassintegrals of
\Omega_{t}
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})=n\int_{M_{t}}{K(\phi(t)-f(K))d\mu_{t}}.

Since
\phi(t)
is defined as in (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{|M_{t}|}\left(\int_{M_{t}}Kd\mu_{t}\int_{M_{t}}f(K)d\mu_{t}-|M_{t}|\int_{M_{t}}Kf(K)d\mu_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=n\int_{M_{t}}{f(K)(\bar{K}-K)\,d\mu_{t}}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=-n\int_{M_{t}}{(f(K)-f(\bar{K})(K-\bar{K}))}\,d\mu_{t}\leq 0

due to the assumption that
f
is strictly increasing. Moreover, equality holds in (
5.3
) at some time
t
if and only if
K
is a constant on
M_{t}
, which means that
M_{t}
is a geodesic sphere by the Alexandrov type theorem for hypersurfaces with constant Gauss curvature in the hyperbolic space (see
[
36
]
).
∎

5.2.
Subsequential Hausdorff convergence

We first prove the following estimate on the
L^{1}
oscillation decay of Gauss curvature:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
M_{0}
be a smooth closed and convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
M_{t}
be the smooth solution of the flow (
1.1
) starting from
M_{0}
. Then there exists a sequence of times
\{t_{i}\},t_{i}\to\infty
, such that

(5.4)

\int_{M_{t_{i}}}{|K-\bar{K}|d\mu_{t_{i}}}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\,\,t_{i}\to\infty.

Proof.

By the evolution equation (
5.3
) and the long time existence of the flow (
1.1
), we have

n\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{M_{t}}{(f(K)-f(\bar{K}))(K-{\bar{K}})d\mu_{t}}dt\leq{\mathcal{A}_{n-1}}(\Omega_{0})<\infty.

Therefore there exists a sequence of times
t_{i}\to\infty
such that

(5.5)

\int_{M_{t_{i}}}{(f(K)-f(\bar{K}))(K-{\bar{K}})d\mu_{t_{i}}}\to 0.

Denote the subset
Y_{t}\subset M_{t}
as
Y_{t}=\{p\in M_{t}|K(p)=\bar{K}\}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{M_{t}}{|K-\bar{K}|}\,d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M_{t}\setminus Y_{t}}{\frac{|K-\bar{K}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|f(K)-f(\bar{K})|^{\frac{1}{2}}}|K-\bar{K}|^{\frac{1}{2}}|f(K)-f(\bar{K})|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,d\mu_{t}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\left(\int_{M_{t}\setminus Y_{t}}{\frac{|K-\bar{K}|}{|f(K)-f(\bar{K})|}}\,d\mu_{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{(I)}\left(\int_{M_{t}}{(f(K)-f(\bar{K}))(K-{\bar{K}})d\mu_{t}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, we show that the term
(I)
of (
5.6
) is uniformly bounded from above. We divide the set
M_{t}\setminus Y_{t}
into three disjoint subsets

M_{t}\setminus Y_{t}=Z_{1}\sqcup Z_{2}\sqcup Z_{3},

where the subsets
Z_{1},Z_{2}
and
Z_{3}
are defined as

(5.7)

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle=\{p\in M_{t}\setminus Y_{t}|0<K(p)\leq\frac{a}{2}\},

(5.8)

\displaystyle Z_{2}

\displaystyle=\{p\in M_{t}\setminus Y_{t}|\frac{a}{2}<K(p)<\frac{3b}{2}\},

(5.9)

\displaystyle Z_{3}

\displaystyle=\{p\in M_{t}\setminus Y_{t}|K(p)\geq\frac{3b}{2}\}.

Using (
5.2
), we calculate as follows:

(i)

If
p\in Z_{1}
, since
f
is an increasing function of
K
, we have

\frac{|K-\bar{K}|}{|f(K)-f(\bar{K})|}=\frac{\bar{K}-K}{f(\bar{K})-f(K)}\leq\frac{b}{f(a)-f(\frac{a}{2})}

and hence

(5.10)

\int_{Z_{1}}{\frac{|K-\bar{K}|}{|f(K)-f(\bar{K})|}}\,d\mu_{t}\leq\frac{m_{2}b}{f(a)-f(\frac{a}{2})}.

(ii)

If
p\in Z_{2}
, then by Lagrange’s mean value theorem, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|K-\bar{K}|}{|f(K)-f(\bar{K})|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(\xi)},\quad\text{the value $\xi$ is taken between $K$ and $\bar{K}$}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in[\frac{a}{2},\frac{3b}{2}]}\left\{\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(x)}\right\}=:c

and hence

(5.11)

\int_{Z_{2}}{\frac{|K-\bar{K}|}{|f(K)-f(\bar{K})|}}\,d\mu_{t}\leq m_{2}c.

(iii)

If
p\in Z_{3}
, we have

\frac{|K-\bar{K}|}{|f(K)-f(\bar{K})|}=\frac{K-\bar{K}}{f(K)-f(\bar{K})}\leq\frac{K}{f(\frac{3b}{2})-f(b)}

and hence

\displaystyle\int_{Z_{3}}{\frac{|K-\bar{K}|}{|f(K)-f(\bar{K})|}}\,d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{f(\frac{3b}{2})-f(b)}\int_{M_{t}}{K}\,d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{|M_{t}|\bar{K}}{f(\frac{3b}{2})-f(b)}

(5.12)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m_{2}b}{f(\frac{3b}{2})-f(b)}.

Combining (
5.10
)-(
(iii)
), we conclude that the term
(I)
of (
5.6
) is uniformly bounded from above. Then by (
5.5
), we complete the proof of Lemma
5.2
.
∎

With the estimate (
5.4
) in hand, we can argue as in
[
43
, Lemma 6.3]
using curvature measure theory for convex bodies in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
to get the subsequential Hausdorff convergence of
M_{t}
to a geodesic sphere. We state the result in the following lemma and refer the readers to
[
43
, Lemma 6.3]
for the proof.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
M_{0}
be a smooth, closed convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
M_{t}
be the smooth solution of the flow (
1.1
) starting from
M_{0}
. Then there exists a sequence of times
\{t_{i}\},t_{i}\to\infty
, such that
M_{t_{i}}
converges to a geodesic sphere
S_{\rho_{\infty}}(p)
in Hausdorff sense as
t_{i}\to\infty
, where
p
is the center of the sphere and the radius
\rho_{\infty}
is determined by the fact that
S_{\rho_{\infty}}(p)
encloses the same volume of
M_{0}
.

5.3.
Convergence of the center of the inner ball

As in
[
43
]
, since we do not have the analogous stability estimate as in
[
38
, Eq.(7.124)]
for the hyperbolic case, we can not apply the argument in
[
6
]
to deduce from Lemma
5.3
the Hausdorff convergence of
M_{t}
to the geodesic sphere for all time
t\to\infty
. However, if we denote
p_{t}
as the center of the inner ball of
\Omega_{t}
, we can still prove that
p_{t}
converges to the fixed point
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
for all time
t\to\infty
using the Alexandrov reflection and the subsequential Hausdorff convergence of
M_{t}
in Lemma
5.3
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
be the center of the limit geodesic sphere
S_{\rho_{\infty}}(p)
in Lemma
5.3
. Take an arbitrary direction
z\in T_{p}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Let
\gamma_{z}
be the normal geodesic line (i.e.
|\gamma^{\prime}|=1
) through the point
p
with
\gamma_{z}(0)=p
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{z}(0)=z
, and let
H_{z,s}
be the totally geodesic hyperplane in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
that is perpendicular to
\gamma_{z}
at
\gamma_{z}(s),s\in\mathbb{R}
. We use the notation
H_{z,s}^{+}
and
H_{z,s}^{-}
for the half-spaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
determined by
H_{z,s}
as follows:

H_{z,s}^{+}:=\bigcup_{s^{\prime}\geq s}H_{z,s^{\prime}},\qquad H_{z,s}^{-}:=\bigcup_{s^{\prime}\leq s}H_{z,s^{\prime}}.

For a bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, denote

\Omega_{z}^{+}(s)=\Omega\cap H_{z,s}^{+},\qquad\Omega_{z}^{-}(s)=\Omega\cap H_{z,s}^{-}.

The reflection map across
H_{z,s}
is denoted by
R_{\gamma_{z},s}
. We define

\displaystyle S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{s\in\mathbb{R}~|~R_{\gamma_{z},s}(\Omega_{z}^{+}(s))\subset\Omega_{z}^{-}(s)\},

\displaystyle S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\sup\{s\in\mathbb{R}~|~R_{\gamma_{z},s}(\Omega_{z}^{-}(s))\subset\Omega_{z}^{+}(s)\}.

The Alexandrov reflection argument implies that
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})
is non-increasing in
t
for each
z
(see
[
4
, Lemma 6.1]
). By the definitions of
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})
and
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega_{t})
, we have
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega_{t})\leq S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})
. Since
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega_{t})=-S_{\gamma_{-z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})
, we also have that
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega_{t})
is non-decreasing in
t
for each
z
. Note that the paper
[
4
]
deals with the flow with
h
-convex initial hypersurfaces, the argument in Lemma 6.1 of
[
4
]
works for convex solutions as well. The readers may refer to
[
19
,
16
,
20
]
for more details on applications of the Alexandrov reflection method in extrinsic curvature flows.

The subsequential Hausdorff convergence in Lemma
5.3
implies that there exists a sequence of
d_{i}\rightarrow 0
such that
M_{t_{i}}\subset B_{\rho_{\infty}+d_{i}}(p)/B_{\rho_{\infty}-d_{i}}(p)
. Take an arbitrary direction
z\in T_{p}{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}
, we have shown in
[
43
]
*Lemma 6.6 that

(5.13)

\displaystyle-C\sqrt{d_{i}}\leq S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega_{t_{i}})\leq S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t_{i}})\leq C\sqrt{d_{i}}

for some
C=C(\rho_{\infty})
. The monotonicity of
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}
and
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}
then implies

(5.14)

\displaystyle-C\sqrt{d_{i}}\leq S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega_{t})\leq S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})\leq C\sqrt{d_{i}},\qquad\forall t\geq t_{i}.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in M_{t}
be the points such that
d(x_{1},p)=\max_{x\in M_{t}}d(x,p)
and
d(x_{2},p)=\min_{x\in M_{t}}d(x,p)
. There exists a geodesic
\gamma_{z}
passing through
p
(with
\gamma_{z}(0)=p
) and totally geodesic hyperplane
H_{z,C\sqrt{d_{i}}}
orthogonal to both
\gamma_{z}
and the geodesic connecting
x_{1}
to
x_{2}
. Using
H_{z,C\sqrt{d_{i}}}
as the reflecting hyperplane and noting (
5.14
), we can estimate that
\max_{x\in M_{t}}d(x,p)-\min_{x\in M_{t}}d(x,p)\leq Cd_{i}^{1/4}
for any
t\geq t_{i}
. This implies that

(5.15)

\displaystyle\text{d}_{\mathcal{H}}(\partial\Omega_{t},\partial B_{\rho_{-}(t)}(p_{t}))\leq Cd_{i}^{1/4},\qquad\forall~t\geq t_{i},

where we denote
p_{t}
as the center of an inner ball
B_{\rho_{-}(t)}(p_{t})
of
\Omega_{t}
.

Consider another geodesic (still denoted by
\gamma_{z}
) which passes through
p
and
p_{t}
with
\gamma_{z}(0)=p
and
\gamma_{z}(s_{t})=p_{t}
. We claim that

(5.16)

\displaystyle S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega_{t})-\text{d}_{\mathcal{H}}(\partial\Omega_{t},\partial B_{\rho_{-}(t)}(p_{t}))\leq~s_{t}\leq~S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})+\text{d}_{\mathcal{H}}(\partial\Omega_{t},\partial B_{\rho_{-}(t)}(p_{t})).

In fact, denote
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})
by
\bar{s}_{t}
. If
p_{t}\in H^{-}_{z,\bar{s}_{t}}
, we obviously have
s_{t}\leq S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})
; If
p_{t}\in H^{+}_{z,\bar{s}_{t}}
, we have
B_{\rho_{-}(t)}(p^{\prime}_{t}):=R_{\gamma_{z},\bar{s}_{t}}(B_{\rho_{-}(t)}(p_{t}))\subset\Omega_{t}
. It follows that

\displaystyle 2(s_{t}-S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t}))=

\displaystyle\text{d}_{\mathcal{H}}(\partial B_{\rho_{-}(t)}(p^{\prime}_{t}),\partial B_{\rho_{-}(t)}(p_{t}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\text{d}_{\mathcal{H}}(\partial\Omega_{t},\partial B_{\rho_{-}(t)}(p_{t})).

This proves the second inequality of (
5.16
). The proof of the first inequality of (
5.16
) is similar.

Finally, combining (
5.14
) – (
5.16
), we have

\displaystyle d(p_{t},p)=|s_{t}|\leq

\displaystyle~Cd_{i}^{1/2}+Cd_{i}^{1/4},\qquad\forall~t\geq t_{i},

which implies that
d(p_{t},p)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

6.
Smooth convergence

In this section, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
. Firstly, we prove the following uniform estimate for the principal curvatures of
M_{t}
along the flow (
1.1
).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
M_{0}
be a smooth, closed and convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, and
M_{t}
be the smooth solution of the flow (
1.1
) starting from
M_{0}
. Then there exists constants
\underline{\kappa}
,
\overline{\kappa}
depending only on
n,M_{0}
and
\Theta
such that the principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}
of
M_{t}
satisfy:

(6.1)

\underline{\kappa}\leq\kappa_{i}\leq\overline{\kappa},\qquad i=1,\dots,n

for all time
t\in[0,+\infty)
, where
\Theta
is the constant in item (
3
) of Assumption
1.1
.

Proof.

Since the center
p_{t}
of an inner ball of
\Omega_{t}
converges to a fixed point
p
as
t\to\infty
and the inner radius of
\Omega_{t}
has a positive lower bound
\rho_{-}(t)\geq c_{1}
, there exists a sufficiently large time
t^{*}
, depending on
c_{1}
and hence depending only on
n
and
M_{0}
, such that
d(p_{t},p)<{c_{1}}/{4}
for
t\geq t^{*}
. Then we have:

(6.2)

B_{c_{1}/4}(p)\subset\Omega_{t},\qquad\forall~t\geq t^{*}.

Applying Proposition
3.3
to the time interval
[0,t^{*})
and
[t^{*},\infty)
respectively gives a uniform lower bound for the principal curvatures of
M_{t}
for all time
t>0
. In fact, on the time interval
[0,t^{*})
, the estimate (
3.10
) implies that the principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}
of
M_{t}
satisfy

(6.3)

k_{i}\geq\Lambda_{2}^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2\Lambda_{1}}{\tau}t^{*}},\qquad t\in[0,t^{*}).

While for time
t\in[t^{*},\infty)
, since
B_{c_{1}/4}(p)\subset\Omega_{t}
for all time
t\in[t^{*},\infty)
. Then by the estimate (
3.2
) in the proof of Proposition
3.3
, we have

\mathfrak{b}(p,t)\leq\max\left\{\max_{p\in M}{\mathfrak{b}(p,t^{*})},a_{3}\right\}\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{1}}

for all
(p,t)\in M\times[t^{*},\infty)
. This together with (
6.3
) implies that the principal curvatures of
M_{t}
are uniformly bounded from below by a positive constant
\underline{\kappa}
which depends only on
n,M_{0}
and
\Theta
.

Once we have the uniform lower bound for the principal curvatures, the uniform upper bound for the Gauss curvature
K
follows easily from the proof of Proposition
3.5
. In fact, the upper bound (
3.3
) for
\sigma_{n-1}(\kappa)
in the proof of Proposition
3.5
now has the form

\sigma_{n-1}(\kappa)\leq\frac{n}{\underline{\kappa}}K,

where the coefficient of
K
does not depend on time
t
.

Therefore, combining the uniform upper bound on
K
and the lower bound
\kappa_{i}\geq\underline{\kappa}
, there exists a constant
\overline{\kappa}
such that
\kappa_{i}\leq\overline{\kappa}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
. This completes the proof of Lemma
6.1
.
∎

It follows from Lemma
6.1
that the flow (
1.1
) is uniformly parabolic for all time
t>0
. Then an argument similar to that in the proof of Theorem
4.2
can be applied to show that all derivatives of curvatures are uniformly bounded on
M_{t}
for all
t>0
. This together with Lemma
5.3
implies there exists a sequence of times
t_{i}\to\infty
, such that
M_{t_{i}}
converges smoothly to a geodesic sphere
S_{\rho_{\infty}}(p)
as
t_{i}\to\infty
.

The full time convergence and the exponential convergence can be obtained by studying the linearization of the flow (
1.1
). For each sufficiently large time
t_{k}
, we write
M_{t_{k}}
as the graph of the radial function
\rho_{t_{k}}(\cdot)
over
\mathbb{S}^{n}
centered at
p_{t_{k}}
. For time
t
sufficiently close to
t_{k}
, we rewrite the flow equation (
1.1
) as the scalar parabolic PDE

(6.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\rho&=\left(\phi(t)-f(K)\right)\sqrt{1+{|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2}}/{\sinh^{2}\rho}},\quad t>t_{k}\\
\rho(\cdot&,t_{k})=\rho_{t_{k}}(\cdot).\end{aligned}\right.

Note we can assume the oscillation of
\rho_{t_{k}}-\rho_{\infty}
is sufficiently small by choosing
t_{k}
large enough. By a direct computation, the linearized equation of the flow (
6.4
) about the geodesic sphere of radius
\rho_{\infty}
is given by

(6.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\eta=\frac{(\coth\rho_{\infty})^{n-1}f^{\prime}}{\sinh^{2}\rho_{\infty}}\left(\bar{\Delta}\eta+n\eta-\frac{n}{|\mathbb{S}^{n}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}{\eta\,d\sigma}\right).

where
f^{\prime}
is taken the value at the point
x=(\coth\rho_{\infty})^{n}
.

Since the oscillation of
\rho_{t_{k}}-\rho_{\infty}
is sufficiently small, it follows exactly in
[
12
]
, using
[
22
]
, that the solution
\rho(\cdot,t)
of (
6.4
) starting at
\rho_{t_{k}}(\cdot)
exists for all time and converges exponentially to a constant
\rho_{\infty}
. This means that the hypersurface
\overline{M}_{t}=
graph
\rho(\cdot,t)
solves (
1.1
) with initial condition
M_{t_{k}}
and by uniqueness
\overline{M}_{t}
coincides with
M_{t}
for
t\geq t_{k}
, and hence the solution
M_{t}
of (
1.1
) with initial condition
M_{0}
converges exponentially as
t\to\infty
to the geodesic sphere of radius
\rho_{\infty}
(without correction of ambient isometry). This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

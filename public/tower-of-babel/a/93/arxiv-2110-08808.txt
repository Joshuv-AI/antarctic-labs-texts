Difference Operators for wreath Macdonald polynomials
Daniel Orr, Mark Shimozono

Abstract
. We give explicit q q -difference operators acting diagonally on wreath Macdonald P P -polynomials in finitely many variables.



1.
Introduction

Wreath Macdonald polynomials

[
Hai2
]
originate from the geometry of
\Gamma
-fixed loci in Hilbert schemes of points in the plane, where
\Gamma
is a finite cyclic subgroup of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
. Unlike the ordinary Macdonald polynomials (which are recovered when
\Gamma
is trivial), their very
existence
relies on the use of extremely deep geometric methods
[
BF
,
BK
]
.

A recent result of Wen
[
Wen
]
provides a somewhat more direct characterization of wreath Macdonald polynomials. This result is based on the
quantum toroidal algebra

[
GKV
]
of type
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
, where
r=|\Gamma|
, together with a remarkable symmetry of this algebra known as Miki’s automorphism
[
Mi
]
. However, Wen’s result is still quite far from an explicit characterization of wreath Macdonald polynomials.
One encounters serious technical obstacles when trying to use it to extract concrete information about wreath Macdonald polynomials, of which very little is in fact known.

The aim of this note is to introduce a new family of
q
-difference operators for wreath Macdonald polynomials, which are a
direct
generalization of Macdonald’s original
q
-difference operators for ordinary Macdonald polynomials. In particular, we provide explicit first-order
q
-difference operators
\{\mathsf{M}^{(i)}\}_{i\in I}
(where
I=\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
) which act diagonally on wreath Macdonald
P
-polynomials in finitely many variables with an
I
-graded version of the ordinary Macdonald operator eigenvalues (see Theorem
4.2
). Our operators are sufficient to uniquely determine these polynomials and their lifts to multi-symmetric functions.

We have a complete proof that our operators act diagonally on wreath Macdonald polynomials, but we postpone it to
[
OSW
]
, where we will also construct higher order wreath Macdonald difference operators. Our approach is based on: (i) Wen’s result mentioned above, and (ii) Negut’s shuffle realization of the quantum toroidal algebra
[
Ne
]
. Based on the rich applicability of Macdonald’s original difference operators, we anticipate a number of applications of the operators
\{\mathsf{M}^{(i)}\}_{i\in I}
, which we will take up in future papers.

2.
Macdonald polynomials

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
be the symmetric group and consider the space
\Lambda_{N}=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{N}}
of symmetric polynomials in variables
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
. Let
\mathbb{Y}
be the set of all integer partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dotsc)
where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dotsm\geq 0
, and let
\mathbb{Y}_{N}
be the set of all integer partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{N})
with at most
N
parts.

2.1.
Origins

The
Macdonald operator
is the
q
-difference operator

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{tx_{k}-x_{l}}{x_{k}-x_{l}}\cdot\mathsf{T}_{q,x_{k}}

where the rational functions appearing in
\mathsf{M}
are understood as multiplication operators and

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}_{q,x_{k}}f(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})

\displaystyle=f(x_{1},\dotsc,qx_{k},\dotsc,x_{N}).

The operator
\mathsf{M}
stabilizes
\Lambda_{N}
, i.e., the denominators clear and the result is symmetric. The importance of this operator stems from the fact that it belongs to a quantum integrable system:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Mac
]
)
.

(1)

There exist explicit, algebraically independent
q
-difference operators
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{1},\mathsf{M}_{2},\dotsc,\mathsf{M}_{N},

satisfying
[\mathsf{M}_{k},\mathsf{M}_{l}]=0
for all
1\leq k,l\leq N
.

(2)

The operators
\mathsf{M}_{1},\dotsc,\mathsf{M}_{N}
have a joint eigenbasis
\{P_{\lambda}\}
indexed by
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{N}
. The symmetric polynomials
P_{\lambda}
are uniquely characterized as their joint eigenfunctions, together with the normalization

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}\in m_{\lambda}+\bigoplus_{\mu\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\reflectbox{\rotatebox{90.0}{$\nshortmid$}}}\cr$\triangleright$\cr}}\mkern-3.0mu}$}}\lambda}\mathbb{K}m_{\mu}

where
m_{\lambda}
denotes a monomial symmetric polynomial and
\unlhd
is the dominance order on partitions (restricted to
\mathbb{Y}_{N}
).

The symmetric polynomials
P_{\lambda}
are the
Macdonald polynomials
(of type
GL_{N}
). We will call
\mathsf{M}_{2},\dotsc,\mathsf{M}_{N}
the
higher Macdonald operators
. The eigenvalues of
\mathsf{M}
are:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}\cdot P_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=e(q,t)P_{\lambda},\qquad e(q,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}q^{\lambda_{k}}t^{N-k}.

The eigenvalues of
\mathsf{M}_{k}
are given by substituting the summands of
e(q,t)
into the elementary symmetric function
e_{k}
.

The Macdonald polynomials
P_{\lambda}
can be stabilized into elements of the
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
\Lambda
of symmetric functions in infinitely many variables
[
Mac
]
. One also considers the modified Macdonald symmetric functions
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}
, which are obtained from
P_{\lambda}
by an explicit transformation (see, e.g.,
[
Hai2
]
).

2.2.
Geometry of Hilbert schemes

The modified Macdonald symmetric functions
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}
are significant due to their positivity when expanded in Schur symmetric functions, which was the content of Macdonald’s famous
positivity conjecture
. Haiman’s proof
[
Hai2
]
of this conjecture recast it as a statement about the geometry of Hilbert schemes
\mathrm{Hilb}_{m}=\mathrm{Hilb}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
of points in the plane. The deepest part of Haiman’s proof concerned the existence and properties of a rank
m!
vector bundle
P_{m}
on
\mathrm{Hilb}_{m}
known as the Procesi bundle.

It is convenient to work with the union
\mathrm{Hilb}=\bigsqcup_{m\geq 0}\mathrm{Hilb}_{m}
of all Hilbert schemes. Let us consider their localized
T
-equivariant
K
-groups:

\displaystyle K_{T}(\mathrm{Hilb})_{\mathrm{loc}}=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}K_{T}(\mathrm{Hilb}_{m})_{\mathrm{loc}}

where
T=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
acts naturally on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and hence on
\mathrm{Hilb}
, and
\mathrm{loc}
stands for localization with respect to equivariant scalars
R(T)\cong\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1},t^{\pm 1}]
, which is achieved by tensoring with
\mathbb{K}\cong\mathrm{Frac}(R(T))
. The points of
\mathrm{Hilb}
can be identified with ideals
I\subset\mathbb{C}[x,y]
of finite codimension. By the localization theorem,
K_{T}(\mathrm{Hilb})_{\mathrm{loc}}
has a
\mathbb{K}
-basis given by the classes of
\{[I_{\lambda}]\}
of
T
-fixed points in
\mathrm{Hilb}
, which are precisely the monomial ideals
I_{\lambda}=(y^{\lambda_{1}},xy^{\lambda_{2}},x^{2}y^{\lambda_{3}},\dotsm)
where
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}
is any partition.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
Hai1
]
)
.

The Procesi bundles
P_{m}
induce an isomorphism of
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces

(2.4)

\displaystyle K_{T}(\mathrm{Hilb})_{\mathrm{loc}}\cong\Lambda

under which a fixed point class
[I_{\lambda}]
is sent to the modified Macdonald function symmetric function
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}
. This isomorphism realizes
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}
as the bigraded
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
-character of the fiber
P_{m}|_{I_{\lambda}}
, where
|\lambda|=m
, implying the Macdonald positivity conjecture.

3.
Wreath Macdonald polynomials

For a fixed integer
r>1
, we consider the finite cyclic subgroup

\Gamma=\Bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}\zeta&\\
&\zeta^{-1}\end{pmatrix}:\zeta^{r}=1\Bigg\}\subset SL_{2}(\mathbb{C}).

Haiman
[
Hai2
]
proposed a generalization of the
geometric
theory of Macdonald polynomials, replacing
\mathrm{Hilb}
by the
\Gamma
-fixed locus
\mathrm{Hilb}^{\Gamma}
consisting of finite-codimensional ideals
I\subset\mathbb{C}[x,y]
which are
\Gamma
-invariant.
1
1

1

In fact, Haiman’s generalization applies conjecturally to arbitrary finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
; however, we only consider cyclic
\Gamma
, as very little is known outside of this case.
Let
\chi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the representation of
\Gamma
given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\chi:\begin{pmatrix}\zeta&\\
&\zeta^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\zeta

The irreducible representations of
\Gamma
are given by powers of
\chi
:
\mathrm{Irr}(\Gamma)=\{\chi^{i}:i\in I\}
where
I=\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
. The McKay correspondence identifies
I
with the vertices of the Dynkin diagram of type
A^{(1)}_{r-1}
. Let
Q_{\mathrm{aff}}=\oplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
be the root lattice of the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{r}
,
Q_{\mathrm{aff}}^{+}=\oplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{i}
the positive affine root cone, and
\delta=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}
the minimal imaginary root. Then the fixed locus
\mathrm{Hilb}^{\Gamma}
is the disjoint union of connected components
\mathrm{Hilb}^{\Gamma}_{\alpha}
indexed by
\alpha=\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}\alpha_{i}\in Q_{\mathrm{aff}}^{+}
, namely
2
2

2

The name “wreath” originates from the fact that
\mathrm{Hilb}^{\Gamma}_{\alpha}
is a crepant resolution of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}/\Gamma_{n}
for some
n
(determined by
\alpha
), where
\Gamma_{n}=\Gamma^{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is a wreath product group.
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hilb}^{\Gamma}_{\alpha}=\{I\in\mathrm{Hilb}^{\Gamma}:\text{$R/I$ has $\Gamma$-character $\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}\chi^{i}$}\}.

\mathrm{Hilb}^{\Gamma}
and
\mathrm{Hilb}
share the same
T
-fixed points
\{I_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}}
, since
\Gamma\subset T
.

3.1.
Haiman’s conjecture

In
[
Hai2
]
Haiman conjectured the following generalization of Theorem
2.2
: there exists a wreath Procesi bundle on each component of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{\Gamma}
and that together these afford an isomorphism

(3.2)

\displaystyle K_{T}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{\Gamma})_{\mathrm{loc}}\cong\Lambda^{\Gamma}:=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in Q}\Lambda^{\otimes I}

with properties generalizing Macdonald positivity, where
Q=Q_{\mathrm{aff}}/\mathbb{Z}\delta
is the root lattice of
\mathfrak{sl}_{r}
. Parallel to Theorem
2.2
, the images of fixed point classes
\{[I_{\lambda}]\}
under this isomorphism are the modified
wreath Macdonald symmetric functions

\tilde{H}^{\Gamma}_{\lambda}
, whose precise definition will be recalled below.

Remark 3.1
.

In the isomorphism (
3.2
), the element
\gamma\in Q
records the
r
-core of a partition
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}
indexing a fixed-point class on the left-hand side. The factors in the tensor power
\Lambda^{\otimes I}
of symmetric functions have to do with the
r
-quotient of
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}
, under the core-quotient decomposition
[
JK
]
.

Haiman’s conjecture was proved by Bezrukavnikov and Finkelberg
[
BF
]
. Their proof relies on deep, non-explicit results of Bezrukavnikov and Kaledin
[
BK
]
involving quantization in positive characteristic.

4.
Wreath Macdonald difference operators

4.1.
Symmetric functions and symmetric polynomials

Our difference operators will characterize the images of
\{P^{\Gamma}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}}

under projections to finitely many variables.
Let
N_{\bullet}=(N_{i})_{i\in I}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{I}
be a dimension vector and consider the polynomial ring

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pol}_{N_{\bullet}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{K}[\{x^{(i)}_{k}:i\in I,1\leq k\leq N_{i}\}]

and its subring

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{N_{\bullet}}=(\mathbf{Pol}_{N_{\bullet}})^{\mathfrak{S}_{N_{\bullet}}}

consisting of polynomials which are invariant with respect to the product of symmetric groups

\mathfrak{S}_{N_{\bullet}}=\prod_{i\in I}\mathfrak{S}_{N_{i}}
,
with the factor
\mathfrak{S}_{N_{i}}
permuting the variables
\{x^{(i)}_{1},\dotsc,x^{(i)}_{N_{i}}\}
, for each
i\in I
. We write
N=\sum_{i\in I}N_{i}
for the total number of variables.

We write

\pi_{N_{\bullet}}:\Lambda^{\otimes I}\to\Lambda_{N_{\bullet}}

for the natural projection, which is given on simple tensors
f=\otimes_{i\in I}f_{i}
by

(4.2)

\displaystyle\pi_{N_{\bullet}}(f)=\prod_{i\in I}f_{i}(x^{(i)}_{1},\dotsc,x^{(i)}_{N_{i}}).

For
f\in\Lambda^{\otimes I}
we use the shorthand
f(X_{N_{\bullet}})
to denote
\pi_{N_{\bullet}}(f)
.

For
\gamma\in Q
indexing a summand of
\Lambda^{\Gamma}
, a dimension vector
N_{\bullet}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{I}
will be called
\gamma
-compatible
if it satisfies

(4.3)

\displaystyle N_{i}-N_{i-1}

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},-\gamma\rangle,\qquad\text{for each $i\in I$.}

where the subscripts in
I=\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
are understood in a cyclic sense and
\alpha^{\vee}_{0}
means the classical projection of the
0
-th affine simple coroot.

In what follows, we have to assume that
N_{\bullet}
is
\gamma
-compatible. But there is no loss of generality in this assumption, since (
4.3
) continues to hold if we add a constant to each
N_{i}
. Thus we can make the components of
N_{\bullet}
as large as needed to fully capture the
P_{\lambda}^{\Gamma}
as symmetric functions.

4.2.
Difference operators

Now we are ready to introduce our wreath Macdonald difference operators. The Macdonald operator
\mathsf{M}
in (

x_{k}\ (1\leq k\leq N)

\mathsf{M}^{(i)}
are expressed as sums over all nonempty subsets of the variables
\bigcup_{i\in I}\{x^{(i)}_{1},\dotsc,x^{(i)}_{N_{i}}\}
with at most one variable selected at each vertex. We record these subsets as pairs
\underline{J}=(J,\mathsf{k})
, where
J
is a nonempty subset of
I
and
\mathsf{k}
is an integer-valued function on
J
such that
1\leq\mathsf{k}(j)\leq N_{j}
for each
j\in J
; the variables chosen to be in our subset are
\{x^{(j)}_{\mathsf{k}(j)}\}_{j\in J}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all such pairs and consider some
\underline{J}=(J,\mathsf{k})\in\mathcal{S}
. For any
i\in I
, we define:

\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(i)}=q^{m}x_{\mathsf{k}(i+m)}^{(i+m)}
, where
m\geq 0
is minimal such that

.

For
j\in J
we have

\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(j)}=x^{(j)}_{\mathsf{k}(j)}
, which is simply the variable at vertex
j
selected by
\underline{J}
. The remaining
\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(i)}
for
i\in I\setminus J
are obtained by cyclically propagating the selected variables and multiplying by a factor of
q
at each step.

e_{\lambda}^{(i)}(q,t)\in\mathbb{K}
are given by (recall that
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(\Gamma)
satisfies
\chi^{r}=1
):

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}e_{\lambda}^{(i)}(q,t)\chi^{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}q^{\lambda_{k}}t^{N-k}\chi^{k-\lambda_{k}}\in R(T\times\Gamma)\cong\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1},t^{\pm 1},\chi^{\pm 1}].

Example
.

Continuing the example above, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{\underline{J}}^{(1)}=\frac{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(1)}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(0)}}

\displaystyle\cdot\frac{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(2)}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(2)}-t\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(0)}}\frac{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(0)}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(0)}-t\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(1)}}\frac{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(1)}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(1)}-t\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(2)}}\cdot\frac{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(2)}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(2)}-q^{-1}\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(1)}}

\displaystyle\cdot\frac{t\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(2)}-x^{(1)}_{1}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(2)}-x^{(2)}_{1}}\frac{t\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(2)}-x^{(1)}_{2}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(2)}-x^{(2)}_{2}}\cdot\frac{t\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(0)}-x^{(2)}_{1}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(0)}}\frac{t\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(0)}-x^{(2)}_{2}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(0)}-x^{(0)}_{2}}\cdot\frac{t\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(1)}-x^{(0)}_{1}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(1)}}\frac{t\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(1)}-x^{(0)}_{2}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{J}}^{(1)}-x^{(1)}_{2}}

and

\mathsf{T}_{q,\underline{J}}(f)=f(qx^{(1)}_{1},x^{(0)}_{2}\mid q^{2}x^{(0)}_{1},x^{(1)}_{2}\mid x^{(2)}_{1},x^{(2)}_{2})
.

We hope this example convinces the reader that not even the most clever guessing would lead from the ordinary Macdonald operator
\mathsf{M}
to its wreath generalization
\mathsf{M}^{(i)}
. One really needs the quantum toroidal algebra
\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{r}
to find these operators.

4.3.
Recovering ordinary Macdonald operators

Let us take
\Gamma=1
. Then
I=\{0\}
and
N_{\bullet}=(N_{0})=(N)
. The only possibly subsets of variables are singletons
\{x^{(0)}_{k}\}
where
1\leq k\leq N
. In terms of
\underline{J}=(J,\mathsf{k})
, we have
J=I
and
\mathsf{k}(0)=k
. The operator
\mathsf{T}_{q,\underline{J}}
sends
x^{(0)}_{k}
to
qx^{(0)}_{k}
, which agrees with
\mathsf{T}_{q,x^{(0)}_{k}}
in (
2.1
). Finally, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{\underline{J}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}\cdot\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{N}(tx^{(0)}_{k}-x^{(0)}_{l})}{\displaystyle x^{(0)}_{k}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{N}(x^{(0)}_{k}-x^{(0)}_{l})}=-\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{tx^{(0)}_{k}-x^{(0)}_{l}}{x^{(0)}_{k}-x^{(0)}_{l}}

and we see that
\mathsf{M}^{(0)}
reduces to
\mathsf{M}
from (
2.1
).

4.4.
Relation to Shoji’s operators

We also mention an interesting similarity between our operators and some introduced by Shoji
[
Sho
]
.
Let us consider only the terms in (
4.4
) which have
J=I
, meaning that a variable is selected at each vertex in
I
. Then, for
\underline{I}=(I,\mathsf{k})
, we have
\mathsf{X}_{\underline{I}}^{(j)}=x^{(j)}_{\mathsf{k}(j)}
for all
j\in I
and

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{\underline{I}}^{(i)}=(-1)^{r}\frac{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{I}}^{(i)}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{I}}^{(0)}}\prod_{j\in I\setminus\{i-1\}}\frac{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{I}}^{(j+1)}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{I}}^{(j+1)}-q^{-1}\mathsf{X}_{\underline{I}}^{(j)}}\cdot\prod_{j\in I}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq\mathsf{k}(j)\end{subarray}}^{N_{j}}\frac{t\mathsf{X}_{\underline{I}}^{(j+1)}-x^{(j)}_{l}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{I}}^{(j)}-x^{(j)}_{l}}.

Shoji
[
Sho
]
considered the operator

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}=\sum_{\underline{I}=(I,\mathsf{k})}\prod_{j\in I}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq\mathsf{k}(j)\end{subarray}}^{N_{j}}\frac{t\mathsf{X}_{\underline{I}}^{(j+1)}-x^{(j)}_{l}}{\mathsf{X}_{\underline{I}}^{(j)}-x^{(j)}_{l}}\cdot\mathsf{T}_{q,\underline{I}}

and used it to characterize a different family of Macdonald-like polynomials associated with the wreath product groups
\Gamma_{n}
. One might expect these to relate to wreath Macdonald polynomials. Haiman observed some “accidental” agreement between the two
[
Hai2
]
. Our operators clarify the genuine difference between Shoji’s polynomials and wreath Macdonald polynomials. The operators
\mathsf{M}^{(i)}
for wreath Macdonald polynomials are substantially more complicated than Shoji’s operator
\mathsf{S}
.

4.5.
The proof

Let us give an indication of how we discovered Theorem
4.2
and how we will prove it and extend it to higher wreath Macdonald operators in
[
OSW
]
. The operators
\{\mathsf{M}^{(i)}\}_{i\in I}
arise from the
horizontal Heisenberg subalgebra
in the quantum toroidal
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
algebra
\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{r}

[
GKV
]
acting on the space
\Lambda^{\Gamma}
, which affords the vertex representation of
[
Sai
]
.

In its natural presentation,
\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{r}
comes equipped with an obvious Heisenberg subalgebra, the
vertical Heisenberg subalgebra
. In both the vertex representation
\Lambda^{\Gamma}
and the geometric representation
K_{T}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{\Gamma})_{\mathrm{loc}}
constructed in
[
VV
]
, the vertical Heisenberg algebra acts in a straightforward way. In contrast, it is difficult to describe the horizontal Heisenberg subalgebra and its actions on
\Lambda^{\Gamma}
and
K_{T}(\mathrm{Hilb})_{\mathrm{loc}}
. Miki’s automorphism
[
Mi
]
swaps the vertical and horizontal Heisenberg subalgebras of
\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{r}
.

Extending a result of
[
FT
,
SV
]
to the wreath setting, Wen
[
Wen
]
proved that the isomorphism (
3.2
) respects the actions of
\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{r}
on both sides, up to some nontrivial scalars and a twist by the Miki automorphism.
Thus, Wen’s result implies that the horizontal Heisenberg algebra acts in
\Lambda^{\Gamma}
like the vertical Heisenberg algebra acts in
K_{T}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{\Gamma})_{\mathrm{loc}}
. From this one immediately deduces that the horizontal Heisenberg algebra acts diagonally on wreath Macdonald polynomials. With some care, this leads to the right-hand side of the eigenoperator equation (
4.7
) in Theorem
4.2
.

Obtaining the left-hand side, i.e., the explicit formula for the wreath Macdonald operators
\mathsf{M}^{(i)}
, requires substantial additional work. Using some known Miki automorphism images from
[
T
]
, we transport degree-one elements in the horizontal Heisenberg algebra to the shuffle algebra
[
Ne
]
and use this to compute the action on symmetric polynomials by a very careful iterated residue computation and further cancellation. In the passage from symmetric functions in infinitely many variables to symmetric polynomials in finitely many variables, skewing operators must be converted to the
q
-translations
\mathsf{T}_{q,\underline{J}}
in (
4.6
). When the dust settles, one obtains the formula for
\mathsf{M}^{(i)}
in (
4.4
).

Finally, we note that we have extensively verified Theorem
4.2
using software for wreath Macdonald polynomials developed in Sage by the second named author.

Learning Sparse Graphons and the Generalized Kesten-Stigum Threshold
Emmanuel Abbe Shuangping Li Allan Sly

Abstract
The problem of learning graphons has attracted considerable attention across several scientific communities, with significant progress over the recent years in sparser regimes. Yet, the current techniques still require diverging degrees in order to succeed with efficient algorithms in the challenging cases where the local structure of the graph is homogeneous. This paper provides an efficient algorithm to learn graphons in the constant expected degree regime. The algorithm is shown to succeed in estimating the rank- k k projection of a graphon in the L 2 L_{2} metric if the top k k eigenvalues of the graphon satisfy a generalized Kesten-Stigum condition.

Discussions of assumptions.

The condition
\int_{0}^{1}Q(x,y)dy=q
means that the random graph
G
has a constant expected degree. This has been the most challenging regime in learning or community detection problems because in this case, degree of vertices or any simple acyclic subgraph counts yield no information towards communities. Moreover, the Benjamini-Schramm limit of the graph is a Galton-Watson branching process independent of the vertex label at the root.

For a low rank graphon
Q
, the assumption that all nonzero eigenvalues satisfy
|\mu_{i}|>\sqrt{\mu_{1}}
is a natural generalization of the Kesten-Stigum condition that requires a single eigenvalue to satisfy this inequality. First studied in the context of multi-type branching processes, it has played a central role in the study of the SBM. In particular in the symmetric 2-block cases it determines the threshold for consistent estimation of the model and for weakly recovering the communities. The threshold is equivalent to the eigenvalues of the non-backtracking matrix lying outside the bulk spectrum.

For the assumption of the simple eigenvalues, we believe that our algorithm can still generate a good estimator even if the eigenvalues are not simple. We have the assumption here for technical purposes. Proving the more general results requires more detailed analysis of the non-backtracking matrix.

3
Algorithm

Our algorithm makes use of the non-backtracking matrix
B
. The matrix has been used in various network problems including community detection of stochastic block models
[
27
,
14
]
. The non-backtracking walk on a graph avoids returning to the vertex visited in the previous step, and can be viewed as the operator resulting from a linearization of the belief propagation algorithm
[
27
,
1
]
. This additional requirement of ‘non-backtracking’ makes its spectrum behave well: in certain sparse cases, when
\rho_{n}=O(1/n)
, top eigenvalues of
B
approximate top eigenvalues of
Q
. This is in contrast to the top eigenvectors of the adjacency matrix that concentrate around high degree vertices in the sparse regime, and top eigenvalue of order
(\log n/\log\log n)^{1/2}

[
26
]
. Therefore classical spectral clustering algorithms using the adjacency matrix are not helpful in the sparse regime. In our problem, the non-backtracking matrix
B
will be important in approximating eigenvalues and extracting moments of the eigenfunctions of
Q
. For a graph
G=(V,E)
, the non-backtracking matrix is indexed by the set of oriented edges
\vec{E}=\{(u,v):\{u,v\}\in E\}
, where each entry equals

\displaystyle B_{ef}=\mathbbm{1}(e_{2}=f_{1})\mathbbm{1}(e_{1}\neq f_{2})=\mathbbm{1}(e_{2}=f_{1})\mathbbm{1}(e\neq f^{-1}).

Here for any
e=(u,v)\in\vec{E}
, we define
e_{1}=u
,
e_{2}=v
,
e^{-1}=(v,u)
. We define
\lambda_{k}
to be the
k
-th eigenvalue of
B
and
\xi_{k}
to be the
k
-th normalized eigenvector of
B
. We order eigenvalues in descending order in magnitude and define
B_{k}(v)=\sum\limits_{e\in E_{1}:e_{2}=v}\xi_{k}(e)
.

Our algorithm consists of several steps:

1. We compute top eigenvalues of the non-backtracking matrix
B
. We then determine the approximate number of eigenvalues that have a magnitude larger than
\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}
. We call this number
K
.

2. We extract joint moments of
f_{i}
through subgraph counts. For a vector
(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k})\in[K]^{k}
, we count a weighted sum of
k
-leaves stars in
G
, where the weight of each star is defined as
\prod_{j=1}^{k}B_{i_{j}}(v_{j})
, where
v_{i}
are leaves. This gives us an estimate of
\int_{[0,1]}f_{i_{1}}(x)\cdots f_{i_{k}}(x)dx
.

3. We consider the random variables
f_{1}(U),\cdots,f_{K}(U)
where
U
is Uniform on
[0,1]
. We use Lengendre polynomials to approximate their joint distribution function and compute the coefficients of the polynomials to match up the moment information from step 2.

4. We sample according to the approximated joint distribution of
f_{1}(U),\cdots,f_{K}(U)
. From there we obtain a piecewise constant approximation of
Q
.

Some Discussions.
In our algorithm, we utilize the spectrum of the non-backtracking matrix to approximate eigenvalues of
Q
. There are actually a few other ways to do this. One of them is using cycle counts similarly to
[
30
]
, but stabilizing the counts to account for the different magnitudes of eigenvalues as in
[
2
]
. We use here the spectrum of
B
in our algorithm because we can get good controls of the error terms and because we anyway need eigenvectors of
B
.

To see why the subgraph counts gives us an estimate of the moment, we consider the inner product
\mathcal{C}_{ij}:=\sum_{v\in V}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}B_{i}(v)f_{j}(X_{v})
. We will show that
\mathcal{C}_{ij}
is asymptotically
\delta_{ij}
after some normalization. We define
Q^{X}
as the matrix such that
Q^{X}(u,v)=Q(X_{u},X_{v})
. Let
Q^{X}_{u}
be the
u
-th column vector of
Q^{X}
. Similarly we define
f_{i}^{X}
as the vector such that
f_{i}^{X}(v)=f_{i}(X_{v})
. So the sum of
k
-leaf stars with weight
\prod_{j=1}^{k}B_{i_{j}}(v_{j})
is approximately

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in V}\prod_{j=1}^{k}B_{i_{j}}^{t}\frac{Q^{X}_{u}}{n}=n^{-k/2}\sum_{u\in V}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{B_{i_{j}}^{t}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\mu_{\ell}f_{\ell}^{X}f_{\ell}^{X}(u)\approx Cn^{-k/2}\sum_{u\in V}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{i_{j}}f_{i_{j}}(X_{u})

\displaystyle\approx Cn^{-k/2+1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{i_{j}}\int_{[0,1]}f_{i_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}dx.

This allows us to estimate the joint moments of eigenfunctions of
Q
.

We pick Legendre polynomials to approximate our functions because they work well in modelling bounded support functions. See
[
32
]
for a one dimensional example of estimating functions with given moments. In our polynomial approximation step, there is one caveat in the computation. The joint distribution of
(f_{1}(U),\cdots,f_{K}(U))
is degenerate: it is supported on a one dimensional curve. So we convolve it with a mollifier to make the distribution function well-defined on
\mathbb{R}^{K}
and smooth. When the mollifier is small enough, the convolved distribution approximates the original function well.

Function

Polynomial_Approximation
(
P_{\alpha},\delta,N
)

We use the following bump function as our mollifier. Define a bump function
\Psi_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to be

\displaystyle\Psi_{\delta}(x)=\begin{cases}\exp(-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}-x^{2}}),&x\in(-\delta,\delta)\\

For any multi-index

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}(\delta)=\sum\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}P_{\beta}\prod_{i=1}^{K}{\alpha_{i}\choose\beta_{i}}\mathbb{E}[N_{\delta}^{\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}}],

We use Legendre polynomials to approximate our mollified distribution. Let

be Legendre polynomials, normalized by
\int_{-1}^{1}(L_{i}(x))^{2}dx=1
. Recall that these polynomials are defined uniquely (apart from sign) by the following requirements:

\displaystyle L_{i}(x)\text{ is a polynomial }

\displaystyle deg(L_{i}(x))=i

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}L_{i}(x)L_{j}(x)dx=\delta_{ij}.

We also define Legendre polynomials in dimension
K
as follows. For a multi-index
\alpha
, define

\displaystyle L_{\alpha}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})=\prod_{i=1}^{K}L_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i}).

They are polynomials of degree
|\alpha|
and satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{[-1,1]^{K}}L_{\alpha}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})L_{\beta}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})d\mathbf{x}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}.

Define
C_{ij}
to be the coefficients of Legendre polynomials:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i}C_{ij}x^{j}=L_{i}(x).

Define
C^{\otimes K}
to be the Kronecker product of
K
copies of
C
, i.e.,
C^{\otimes K}_{\alpha,\beta}=\prod_{i=1}^{K}C_{\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}}
. Now, we consider scaled Legendre polynomials on
[-\frac{2M}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}},\frac{2M}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}]
, satisfying the same conditions as above except the integration is over
[-\frac{2M}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}},\frac{2M}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}]
. Denote them as
\tilde{L}
. Write
\kappa=\frac{2M}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}
. Then,

\displaystyle\tilde{L}_{\alpha}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})=\frac{1}{\kappa^{K/2}}L_{\alpha}(x_{1}/\kappa,\cdots,x_{K}/\kappa).

Define
\tilde{C}
such that
\tilde{C}_{ij}=C_{ij}/\kappa^{j+1/2}
. Define
\tilde{C}^{\otimes K}
to be the Kronecker product of
K
copies of
\tilde{C}
. Now we compute

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{\alpha}(\delta)=\sum\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}\tilde{C}^{\otimes K}_{\beta}M_{\beta}(\delta).

Finally, for a given
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, compute

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})=\sum\limits_{0\leq\alpha\leq\mathbf{N}}\hat{\rho}_{\alpha}(\delta)\tilde{L}_{\alpha}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K}).

Define
\hat{h}_{N}^{+}:=\hat{h}_{N}\mathbbm{1}[\hat{h}_{N}\geq 0]
.

Function

Graphon_Estimation
(
\hat{h}_{N}^{+},K,\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{K}
)

Take
m=n
. We sample vectors
Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{m}
from a distribution proportional to
\hat{h}_{N}^{+}
mutually independently. For any
1\leq i\leq K
, compute

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{i}(x)=Z_{\lceil xm\rceil}(i).

We compute

\displaystyle\hat{Q}(x,y)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{i}\hat{f}_{i}(x)\hat{f}_{i}(y).

Complexity.
With high probability, the running time of our algorithm is
O(n\log n)
. The non-backtracking matrix
B
is typically sparse with
O(n)
non-zero entries. Therefore, extracting
K

O(n\log n)
by the power algorithm. This implies that the complexity of
Non_Backtracking_Matrix
is with high probability
O(n\log n)
. The rest of the algorithm runs in time
O(n)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq\alpha\leq\mathbf{N}}\left(e_{2}\sum\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{K}{\alpha_{i}\choose\beta_{i}}\mathbb{E}[N_{\delta}^{\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}}]\right)^{2}

We note that being the

\displaystyle|\mu_{i}f_{i}(x)|=|\int_{0}^{1}Q(x,y)f_{i}(y)dy|\leq(\int_{0}^{1}Q(x,y)^{2}dy)^{1/2}\leq M

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}=\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}u(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})\tilde{L}_{\alpha}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})d\mathbf{x}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}=\sum\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}\tilde{C}^{\otimes K}_{\beta}\mu_{\alpha}.

One way to approximate
u
is to consider the following term

\displaystyle h_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K}):=\sum\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq\mathbf{N}}\rho_{\beta}\tilde{L}_{\beta}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K}).

Define

\displaystyle t_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K}):=\sum\limits_{\cup_{i}\{\beta:\beta_{i}>N\}}\rho_{\beta}\tilde{L}_{\beta}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K}),

\displaystyle t_{i,N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K}):=\sum\limits_{\beta:\beta_{i}>N}\rho_{\beta}\tilde{L}_{\beta}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K}).

We define
H_{N}
to be a
(N+1)
by
(N+1)
matrix such that its
(i,j)
-th entry equals to
\frac{2}{i+j-1}
, when
i+j
is even and
0
elsewhere. Write
\mathrm{Cond}(H_{N})
as its condition number.

Lemma 5.10
.

If

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\alpha\leq N}|M_{\alpha}(\delta)-\mu_{\alpha}(\delta)|^{2}\leq e_{3}^{2},

then

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|\hat{h}_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})-h_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\leq e_{3}^{2}\kappa^{2N+1}(\mathrm{Cond}(H_{N}))^{K}.

Proof.

Recall that
\tilde{C}_{ij}
are scaled Legendre polynomial coefficients. Define
\tilde{C}
to be a
(N+1)
by
(N+1)
matrix such that its
(i+1,j+1)
-th entry equals to
\tilde{C}_{ij}
. Define a matrix
\tilde{H}_{N}=(\tilde{C}^{T}\tilde{C})^{-1}
. Note that the
(i,j)
-th entry of
\tilde{H}_{N}
equals to
\frac{2\kappa^{i+j-1}}{i+j-1}
, when
i+j
is even and
0
elsewhere. The reason is as follows. Define
\tilde{M}=\tilde{C}^{-1}
. Write
\tilde{M}_{ij}
as
(i+1,j+1)
-th entry of
\tilde{M}
. Then

\displaystyle x^{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\tilde{M}_{ij}\tilde{L}_{j}(x).

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]}x^{i}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\tilde{C}_{kj}x^{j}dx=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\tilde{C}_{kj}\frac{(1-(-1)^{i+j+1})\kappa^{i+j+1}}{i+j+1}=\tilde{M}_{ik}.

Therefore,
\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{H}_{N})\geq\kappa^{-2N-1}\lambda_{\min}(H_{N}).
We write
\tilde{H}_{N}^{\otimes K}
as the Kronecker product of
K
copies of
\tilde{H}_{N}
. Write
M(\delta)
and
\mu(\delta)
as vector of
M_{\alpha}(\delta)
and
\mu_{\alpha}(\delta)
respectively. Then

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|\hat{h}_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})-h_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})|^{2}d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\langle(H_{N}^{\otimes K})^{-1}(M(\delta)-\mu(\delta)),M(\delta)-\mu(\delta)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq e_{3}^{2}\kappa^{2N+1}(\mathrm{Cond}(H_{N}))^{K}.

The last inequality follows from the fact that the largest eigenvalue of
\lambda_{\max}(H_{N})\geq 1
.
∎

In order to control the tail terms, we need to have the following inequality first.

Lemma 5.11
.

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|\frac{d}{dx_{i}}u(x)|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\leq(2\kappa)^{K}\delta^{-2K-8}.

Proof.

We denote by
T
the distribution of
(f_{1}(U),\cdots,f_{K}(U))
. We consider it as a continuous linear functional on
C^{\infty}([-\kappa,\kappa]^{K})
. We also define
\Psi_{\delta}^{\otimes K}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})=\Psi_{\delta}(x_{1})\cdots\Psi_{\delta}(x_{K})
. Then

\displaystyle u(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})=(T*C_{\delta}\Psi_{\delta}^{\otimes K})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K}),

where
C_{\delta}
is the normalization constant of
\Psi_{\delta}^{\otimes K}
such that
\|C_{\delta}\Psi_{\delta}^{\otimes K}\|_{1}=1
. Thus

\displaystyle\|\frac{d}{dx_{i}}u(x)\|_{L_{\infty}([-\kappa,\kappa]^{K})}

\displaystyle=C_{\delta}\|T*\frac{d}{dx_{i}}\Psi_{\delta}^{\otimes K}\|_{L_{\infty}([-\kappa,\kappa]^{K})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\|\frac{d}{dx_{i}}\Psi_{\delta}^{\otimes K}\|_{L_{\infty}([-\kappa,\kappa]^{K})}

\displaystyle=C_{\delta}\|\frac{-2x_{i}}{(\delta^{2}-x_{i}^{2})^{2}}\Psi_{\delta}^{\otimes K}\|_{L_{\infty}([-\kappa,\kappa]^{K})}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{-K-4}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|\frac{d}{dx_{i}}u(x)|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\leq(2\kappa)^{K}\delta^{-2K-8}.

∎

Lemma 5.12
.

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|t_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\leq\frac{K^{2}2^{K}\kappa^{K+2}\delta^{-2K-8}}{(N+1)^{2}}.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dx}[(1-x)(1+x)L_{i}^{\prime}(x)]=i(i+1)L_{i}(x),

the differential equation for Legendre polynomials. So

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dx_{i}}[(1-x_{i})(1+x_{i})\frac{d}{dx_{i}}L_{\alpha}(x)]=\alpha_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)L_{\alpha}(x).

and

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dx_{i}}[(\kappa-x_{i})(\kappa+x_{i})\frac{d}{dx_{i}}\tilde{L}_{\alpha}(x)]=\alpha_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)\tilde{L}_{\alpha}(x).

We multiply both sides by
u
and do integration by parts twice,

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}\tilde{L}_{\alpha}(x)\frac{d}{dx_{i}}[-(\kappa-x_{i})(\kappa+x_{i})\frac{d}{dx_{i}}u(x)]d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}\tilde{L}_{\alpha}(x)u(x)d\mathbf{x}=\alpha_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)\rho_{\alpha}.

By Parseval’s theorem and integration by parts,

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\alpha_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)|\rho_{\alpha}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}u(x)\frac{d}{dx_{i}}[-(\kappa-x_{i})(\kappa+x_{i})\frac{d}{dx_{i}}u(x)]d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}(\kappa-x_{i})(\kappa+x_{i})|\frac{d}{dx_{i}}u(x)|^{2}d\mathbf{x}.

By Pareval’s theorem and the definition of
t_{i,N}
,

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|t_{i,N}|^{2}d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha:\alpha_{i}>N}\rho_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(N+1)^{2}}\sum_{\alpha:\alpha_{i}>N}\alpha_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)\rho_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(N+1)^{2}}\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}(\kappa-x_{i})(\kappa+x_{i})|\frac{d}{dx_{i}}u(x)|^{2}d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{K}\kappa^{K+2}\delta^{-2K-8}}{(N+1)^{2}}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|t_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\leq K\sum_{i=1}^{K}\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|t_{i,N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{K})|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\leq\frac{K^{2}2^{K}\kappa^{K+2}\delta^{-2K-8}}{(N+1)^{2}}.

∎

Combining Lemma
5.8
, Lemma
5.9
, Lemma
5.10
, Lemma
5.12
and our definition of
N
and
\delta
, we have that with high probability,

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|\hat{h}_{N}-u|^{2}d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq e_{2}^{2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}\mathbb{E}[(1+N_{\delta})^{i}]^{2}\right)^{K}\kappa^{2N+1}(\mathrm{Cond}(H_{N}))^{K}+\frac{K^{2}2^{K}\kappa^{K+2}\delta^{-2K-8}}{(N+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-K-14}e_{0}^{2}K^{-2}\lambda_{1}^{-4}\kappa^{-K-8},

as
n
goes to infinity. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|\hat{h}_{N}-u|d\mathbf{x}\leq\frac{e_{0}}{2^{7}K\lambda_{1}^{2}\kappa^{4}}.

Notice that this also implies that

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|\hat{h}_{N}^{+}-\hat{h}_{N}|d\mathbf{x}\leq\frac{e_{0}}{2^{7}K\lambda_{1}^{2}\kappa^{4}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|\hat{h}_{N}^{+}-u|d\mathbf{x}\leq\frac{e_{0}}{2^{6}K\lambda_{1}^{2}\kappa^{4}}.

Further,

\displaystyle\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{K}}|\frac{\hat{h}_{N}^{+}}{\|\hat{h}_{N}^{+}\|_{1}}-u|d\mathbf{x}\leq\frac{e_{0}}{2^{5}K\lambda_{1}^{2}\kappa^{4}},

as
n
goes to infinity with high probability.

5.4
Graphon Estimation

Recall that in
Graphon_Estimation
, we sampled
Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{m}
according
\hat{h}_{N}^{+}
mutually independently. For any
1\leq i\leq K
, we defined

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{i}(x)=Z_{\lceil xm\rceil}(i).

and set

\displaystyle\hat{Q}(x,y)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{i}\hat{f}_{i}(x)\hat{f}_{i}(y).

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
U_{1},\cdots,U_{m}
be iid random variables following Uniform
[0,1]
distribution. Then there exists a measure preserving map
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}|f_{k}(U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil})-f_{k}(x)|^{2}dx\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(P)}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

as
m
goes to infinity.

Proof.

Define a permutation
\sigma\in S_{m}
such that
U_{\sigma(1)}\leq U_{\sigma(2)}\leq\cdots\leq U_{\sigma(m)}
. For
i\in[m]
, define

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\frac{\sigma(i)-i}{m}+x,\ \ x\in(\frac{i-1}{m},\frac{i}{m}].

Then
U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}
is a piecewise constant monotone function in
(0,1]
. For any
x\in(0,1]
, we can write down the distribution of
U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}
explicitly. For
\frac{i-1}{m}<x\leq\frac{i}{m}
,
U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}
follows Beta
(i,m-i+1)
distribution. We will show that, for any
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\int_{[0,1]}|f_{k}(U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil})-f_{k}(x)|^{2}dx>\alpha]\rightarrow 0.

As
f_{k}
is a bounded function, it is in
L_{2}([0,1])
. Fix
0<\epsilon<\alpha/3
. Using the density of continuous functions in
L_{2}([0,1])
, we find a continuous function
g:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\|f_{k}-g\|_{2}<\epsilon
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\int_{[0,1]}|f_{k}(U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil})-f_{k}(x)|^{2}dx>\alpha]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\frac{i-1}{m}}^{\frac{i}{m}}|f_{k}(U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil})-f_{k}(x)|^{2}dx>\alpha].

Notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\frac{i-1}{m}}^{\frac{i}{m}}|f_{k}(U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil})-f_{k}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{k}(U_{\sigma(i)})-g(U_{\sigma(i)})|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\frac{i-1}{m}}^{\frac{i}{m}}|g(U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil})-g(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{1}|g(x)-f_{k}(x)|^{2}dx

In order for the left hand side to be larger than
\alpha
, at least one of terms on the right hand side should be larger than
\alpha/3
. The third term is not possible by definition. So we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\frac{i-1}{m}}^{\frac{i}{m}}|f_{k}(U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil})-f_{k}(x)|^{2}dx>\alpha]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{k}(U_{\sigma(i)})-g(U_{\sigma(i)})|^{2}dx>\alpha/3]+\mathbb{P}[\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\frac{i-1}{m}}^{\frac{i}{m}}|g(U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil})-g(x)|^{2}dx>\alpha/3].

The first term equals

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{k}(U_{i})-g(U_{i})|^{2}dx>\alpha]\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\alpha}.

To control the second term, consider the event

A_{i}:=\{|U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}-\frac{i}{m}|\leq m^{-\frac{1}{4}}\}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}-\frac{i}{m}\geq m^{-\frac{1}{4}}]=\mathbb{P}[Y_{i}\leq i]\leq e^{-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{m}},

by Chernoff bound, where
Y_{i}
follows Binomial
(m,\frac{i}{m}+m^{-\frac{1}{4}})
distribution. Similarly, when
\frac{i}{m}-m^{-\frac{1}{4}}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}\leq\frac{i}{m}-m^{-\frac{1}{4}}]=\mathbb{P}[Y_{i}\geq i]\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m}},

by Chernoff bound, where
Y_{i}
follows Binomial
(m,\frac{i}{m}-m^{-\frac{1}{4}})
distribution. Therefore, if we define
A:=\{\forall i:|U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}-\frac{i}{m}|\leq m^{-\frac{1}{4}}\}
, then
\mathbb{P}[A]\geq 1-2me^{-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{m}}
. Note that there exists
\delta
, such that
|g(x)-g(y)|<\alpha/3
, whenever
|x-y|<\delta
. So when
m
is large,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\frac{i-1}{m}}^{\frac{i}{m}}|g(U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil})-g(x)|^{2}dx>\alpha/3]\leq\mathbb{P}[A^{c}]\leq 2me^{-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{m}}.

Therefore, by taking arbitrarily small
\epsilon
, we have that for any
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\int_{[0,1]}|f_{k}(U_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil})-f_{k}(x)|^{2}dx>\alpha]\rightarrow 0.

∎

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{m}
be iid random vectors following the distribution proportional to
\hat{h}_{N}^{+}
. Then there exists a measure preserving map
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}|Z_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}(i)-f_{i}(x)|^{2}dx\leq\frac{e_{0}}{4K\lambda_{1}^{2}\kappa^{2}}

with high probability.

Proof.

Let
U_{1},\cdots,U_{m}
be iid random variables following Uniform
[0,1]
distribution. Define
W_{i}=(f_{1}(U_{i}),\cdots,f_{K}(U_{i}))
. Define
Y_{i}=(f_{1}(U_{i})+N_{1,i},\cdots,f_{K}(U_{i})+N_{K,i})
, where
N_{j,i}
follows a distribution proportional to
\Psi_{\delta}
jointly independently. We consider a coupling of
(Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{m})
and
(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m})
such that
Z_{i}\neq Y_{i}
with probability
1-\|\frac{\hat{h}_{N}^{+}}{\|\hat{h}_{N}^{+}\|_{1}}-u\|_{TV}
. Take
\phi
as in the previous lemma. Then

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}|Z_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}(i)-f_{i}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}|W_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}(i)-f_{i}(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{[0,1]}|Y_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}(i)-W_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}(i)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{[0,1]}|Z_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}(i)-Y_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}(i)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}|W_{\lceil\phi(x)m\rceil}(i)-f_{i}(x)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|N_{i,j}|^{2}+\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|Z_{j}(i)-Y_{j}(i)|^{2}.

The first term is controlled by the previous lemma. For the second term, note that each term in the summation is independent and each term satisfies
|N_{i,j}|^{2}\leq\delta^{2}\leq\frac{e_{0}}{2^{4}K\lambda_{1}^{2}\kappa^{2}}
. For the third term, note that with high probability,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|Z_{j}(i)-Y_{j}(i)|^{2}]\leq 4\kappa^{2}\|\frac{\hat{h}_{N}^{+}}{\|\hat{h}_{N}^{+}\|_{1}}-u\|_{TV}\leq 4\kappa^{2}\frac{e_{0}}{2^{5}K\lambda_{1}^{2}\kappa^{4}}=\frac{e_{0}}{2^{3}K\lambda_{1}^{2}\kappa^{2}}.

All together, the statement follows.
∎

Lemma 5.15
.

There is a measure preserving map
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\mu_{i}f_{i}(x)f_{i}(y)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{i}\hat{f}_{i}(\phi(x))\hat{f}_{i}(\phi(y))|^{2}dxdy<e_{0},

with high probability as n goes to infinity.

Proof.

We separate the left hand side into two terms,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\mu_{i}f_{i}(x)f_{i}(y)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{i}\hat{f}_{i}(\phi(x))\hat{f}_{i}(\phi(y))|^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\mu_{i}f_{i}(x)f_{i}(y)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(x)f_{i}(y)|^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}|\lambda_{i}f_{i}(x)f_{i}(y)-\lambda_{i}\hat{f}_{i}(\phi(x))\hat{f}_{i}(\phi(y))|^{2}dxdy.

Note that with high probability the first term is bounded by
O(\frac{1}{\log(n)})
. With high probability, the second term is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{i}^{2}\int_{[0,1]}|f_{i}(x)|^{2}dx\int_{[0,1]}|f_{i}(y)-\hat{f}_{i}(\phi(y))|^{2}dy

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{i}^{2}\int_{[0,1]}|\hat{f}_{i}(\phi(y))|^{2}dy\int_{[0,1]}|f_{i}(x)-\hat{f}_{i}(\phi(x))|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2K\lambda_{1}^{2}\kappa^{2}\sup_{i\in[K]}\int_{[0,1]}|f_{i}(x)-\hat{f}_{i}(\phi(x))|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e_{0}}{2}.

Therefore, the statement follows.
∎

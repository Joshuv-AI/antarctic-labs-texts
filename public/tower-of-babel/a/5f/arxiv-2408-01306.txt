Abstract
In this article, we are interested in whether a product of three consecutive integers a ​ ( a + 1 ) ​ ( a + 2 ) a(a+1)(a+2) divides another such product b ​ ( b + 1 ) ​ ( b + 2 ) b(b+1)(b+2) . If this happens, we prove that there is some gaps between them, namely b ≫ a ​ ( log ⁡ a ) 1 / 6 ( log ⁡ log ⁡ a ) 1 / 3 b\gg\frac{a(\log a)^{1/6}}{(\log\log a)^{1/3}} . We also consider other polynomial sequences such as a 2 ​ ( a 2 + l ) a^{2}(a^{2}+l) dividing b 2 ​ ( b 2 + l ) b^{2}(b^{2}+l) for some fixed integer l l . Our method is based on effective Liouville-Baker-Feldman theorem.

Introduction and main results

Consider products of consecutive positive integers
1\cdot 2=2,\,2\cdot 3=6,\,3\cdot 4=12,\ldots
. Observe that
3\cdot 4=2(2\cdot 3)
. So, one may ask if there are infinitely many integers
1\leq a<b
such that
b(b+1)=2a(a+1)
. Multiplying by
4
and completing the squares, we get

(4b^{2}+4b)=2(4a^{2}+4a)\;\;\text{ or }\;\;(2b+1)^{2}-2(2a+1)^{2}=-1.

(1)

By the theory of Pell equation, all the positive integer solutions of
x^{2}-2y^{2}=-1
are generated by
x_{n}+y_{n}\sqrt{2}=(1+\sqrt{2})^{2n-1}
. One can prove by induction that all
x_{n}
’s and
y_{n}
’s are odd numbers. Therefore, we have infinitely many
a
and
b
satisfying (
1
), namely

a=\frac{y_{n}-1}{2}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;b=\frac{x_{n}-1}{2}.

Hence, it can occur infinitely often that products of consecutive integers share the same prime factors, and one such product is the double of another one.

Next, it is natural to consider products of three consecutive positive integers
1\cdot 2\cdot 3=6,\,2\cdot 3\cdot 4=24,\,3\cdot 4\cdot 5=60,\ldots
. Note that
4\cdot 5\cdot 6=2(3\cdot 4\cdot 5)
. Again, one may ask if there are infinitely many positive integers
a<b
such that
b(b+1)(b+2)=2a(a+1)(a+2)
. It turns out that the answer is negative in this case. We deduce this by connecting the question to rational approximation of algebraic numbers and applying effective Liouville-Baker-Feldman theorem in the areas of Diophantine approximation and transcendental numer theory.

Theorem 1
.

The Diophantine equation
b(b+1)(b+2)=2a(a+1)(a+2)
has
a=3
and
b=4
as its only positive integer solution.

Given a positive integer

for some positive integers

,
[

]

[

is not an integer multiple of some power of linear or quadratic polynomial. The interested readers can refer to
[

]

[

]
for more general gap prinicple of higher order. As an immediate consequence of the above, we have

Given integers

Initial transformation

In this section, we relate the Diophantine equation
b(b+l)(b+2l)=ta(a+l)(a+2l)
to rational approximation. First, we make a change of variables
x=a+l
and
y=b+l
. Then, the equation becomes
y(y^{2}-l^{2})=tx(x^{2}-l^{2})
. Let
D=\gcd(x,y)
,
x=Du
and
y=Dv
with relatively prime integers
v>u>0
. Then, we have

v(D^{2}v^{2}-l^{2})=tu(D^{2}u^{2}-l^{2})\;\;\text{ or }\;\;D^{2}(v^{3}-tu^{3})=(v-tu)l^{2}.

In particular,
D^{2}\mid(v-tu)l^{2}
and we can rewrite the above as

v^{3}-tu^{3}=-s\;\;\text{ where }\;\;s=\frac{(tu-v)l^{2}}{D^{2}}.

(3)

Now, observe that

s\mid\gcd((tu-v)l^{2},v^{3}-tu^{3})\mid\gcd((t^{3}u^{3}-v^{3})l^{2},v^{3}-tu^{3})\mid\gcd((t^{3}-t)l^{2}u^{3},v^{3}-tu^{3})\mid(t-1)t(t+1)l^{2}

(4)

as
v^{3}-tu^{3}
and
u^{3}
are relatively prime. Thus,
|s|\leq t^{3}l^{2}
and it follows from (
3
) and (
4
) that

\Big|t-\frac{v^{3}}{u^{3}}\Big|\leq\frac{t^{3}l^{2}}{u^{3}}\;\;\text{ or }\;\;\Big|\sqrt[3]{t}-\frac{v}{u}\Big|\leq\frac{t^{7/3}l^{2}}{u^{3}}

(5)

which becomes a rational approximation question.

(6)

When

.

When

When

,or
6
are not possible. If

, then
v^{3}-54\leq v-6
or

are also impossible.

When

, (
6
) becomes
v^{3}-128=\frac{v-8}{D^{2}}
. One has
v=5
and

as solutions. Translating back to

and

are not possible. If

From now on, we assume

(7)

We apply a result of Bennett

. Then, one can use a simple computer program to verify that

4
Tools from Diophantine approximation and algebraic number theory

Suppose
\alpha
is a real algebraic number of degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then Liouville’s theorem states that there exists some constant
c_{\alpha}>0
such that

\Big|\alpha-\frac{y}{x}\Big|\geq\frac{c_{\alpha}}{x^{n}}

for all integers
x,y
with
x\neq 0
. The exponent
n
was reduced to
2+\epsilon
by a series of works by Thue, Siegel, Dyson, and Roth. However, these are ineffective results. Using linear forms of logarithms, Baker
[
1
]
and Feldman
[
14
]
subsequently derived effective improvements of the form

\Big|\alpha-\frac{y}{x}\Big|\geq\frac{c_{\alpha}}{x^{n-\tau_{\alpha}}}

(8)

for some
c_{\alpha},\tau_{\alpha}>0
. We will use the following explicit result of Bugeaud
[
6
]
: Let
A\geq e
be an upper bound for the height of
\alpha
, and
R
be the regulator of
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
. Then (
8
) is true with

c_{\alpha}=A^{-n^{2}}\exp(-10^{27n}n^{14n}R),\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\tau_{\alpha}=(10^{26n}n^{14n}R)^{-1}.

(9)

Thus, we need information about the regulator of algebraic number fields. Given an algebraic number field
K
over
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
n
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, let
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}
be the group of units of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and

g_{K}:=2^{r_{1}}h_{K}R_{K}/w_{K}

where
r_{1}
is the number of real embeddings of
K
,
h_{K}
is the class number of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
R_{K}
is the regulator of
K
, and
w_{K}
is the order of the torsion subgroup of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}
. Siegel
[
21
]
gave an effective version of Landau’s bound
[
17
]
on
g_{K}
:

g_{K}<4\Bigl(\frac{2}{n-1}\Big)^{n-1}\sqrt{|d_{K}|}\log^{n-1}|d_{K}|

(10)

where
d_{K}
is the discriminant of
K
. For pure cubic fields, Barrucand, Loxton and Williams
[
4
]
proved the following better bound:

h_{K}R_{K}<\frac{1}{6\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{|d_{K}|}\log|d_{K}|.

(11)

When applying the above results, our algebraic number
\alpha
is of the form
\sqrt[3]{m}
or
\sqrt[4]{m}
for some integer
m>1
. Hence, we need information on the discriminant of pure cubic and quartic fields.

For pure cubic fields
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{m})
, Dedekind partitioned them into Type I or Type II depending on whether
3
is wildly ramified or is tamely ramified. Recently, Harron
[
16
]
determined the shapes of pure cubic fields nicely, and we quote his Lemma 2.1 here. Suppose
m=ab^{2}
where
a
and
b
are relatively prime, squarefree, positive integers. For
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{m})
,

d_{K}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-3^{3}a^{2}b^{2},&\text{ if $K$ is of Type I },\\
-3a^{2}b^{2},&\text{ if $K$ is of Type II }.\end{array}\right.

Hence, by (
11
),

|d_{K}|\leq 27m^{2}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;R_{K}<m\log 6m.

(12)

For pure quartic fields, we quote the following result of Funakura
[
15
, Corollary 1]
: Suppose
m=ab^{2}c^{3}
where (i)
a\neq 1
,
b
and
c
are pairwise relatively prime squarefree numbers; (ii)
b
and
c
are positive; (iii)
|a|\geq c
if
a
is odd; (iv)
c
is odd; (v)
m\neq-4
. For
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{m})
,

d_{K}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-2^{2}a^{3}b^{2}c^{3},&\text{ if }m\equiv 1\pmod{8}\;\text{ or }\;28\pmod{32},\\
-2^{4}a^{3}b^{2}c^{3},&\text{ if }m\equiv 4\pmod{16},\;5\pmod{8}\;\text{ or }\;12\pmod{32},\\
-2^{8}a^{3}b^{2}c^{3},&\text{ if }m\equiv 2\pmod{4}\;\text{ or }\;3\pmod{4}.\end{array}\right.

(13)

By unique prime factorization, we can write
m=uv^{2}w^{3}s^{4}
for some relatively prime squarefree numbers
u,v,w>0
, and some integer
s>0
. Clearly,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{m})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{uv^{2}w^{3}})
.

If
u>1
is even, then
w
must be odd and we can apply (
13
) with
m=uv^{2}w^{3}
. Hence,
|d_{K}|\leq 256m^{3}
.

If
u=1
, then we note that
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{t})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{t^{3}})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{wv^{2}u^{3}})
. Hence, we can apply (
13
) with
m=wv^{2}u^{3}
and get
|d_{K}|\leq 256m^{3}
.

If
w
is even, then
u
must be odd and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{t})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{t^{3}})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{wv^{2}u^{3}})
. Hence, we can apply (
13
) with
m=wv^{2}u^{3}
and get
|d_{K}|\leq 256m^{3}
.

If
u>1
is odd and
w
is odd, then we have either

or
u<w
. In the former case, we can simply apply (
13
) with
m=uv^{2}w^{3}
and get
|d_{K}|\leq 256m^{3}
. In the latter case, we use
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{m})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{m^{3}})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{wv^{2}u^{3}})
and apply (
13
) with
m=wv^{2}u^{3}
and still get

.

In summary,

|d_{K}|\leq 256m^{3}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;R_{K}<15m^{3/2}\log^{3}8m

(14)

by (
10
) as real quartic field

has
\pm 1
as its only torsion elements in
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}
and

(
\sqrt[4]{m}\mapsto\sqrt[4]{m}
and
\sqrt[4]{m}\mapsto-\sqrt[4]{m}
).

), we have

\frac{t^{-16}\exp(-10^{143}t^{3/2}\log^{3}8t)}{x^{4-1/(10^{142}t^{3/2}\log^{3}8t)}}<\Big|\sqrt[4]{t}-\frac{y}{x}\Big|\leq\frac{|l|t^{5/4}}{x^{4}}.

Therefore,
x^{1/(10^{142}t^{3/2}\log^{3}8t)}<|l|t^{69/4}\exp(10^{143}t^{3/2}\log^{3}8t)
which implies

\log x\leq 10^{286}\log(|l|+2)\cdot t^{3}\log^{6}8t\;\;\text{ or }\;\;t\geq\frac{(\log x)^{1/3}}{10^{96}\sqrt[3]{\log(|l|+2)}\,(\log\log x)^{2}}

for
x
sufficiently large (in terms of
l
). From (
17
) and

\log Dx\leq\log\sqrt{|l|t}x^{2}\;\text{ or }\;\log x\geq\frac{1}{2}\log Dx-\log\log Dx-\frac{1}{4}\log|l|\geq\frac{1}{3}\log Dx

for

sufficiently large (in terms of

). Hence,

and Theorem
3
follows from the observation that

).
∎

Proof.

Integrability from categorification and the 2-Kac-Moody Algebra hank.chen@uwaterloo.ca fgirelli@uwaterloo.ca
Hank Chen, Florian Girelli

Abstract
The theory of Poisson-Lie groups and Lie bialgebras plays a major role in the study of one dimensional integrable systems; many families of integrable systems can be recovered from a Lax pair which is constructed from a Lie bialgebra associated to a Poisson-Lie group [ 1 , 2 ] . A higher homotopy notion of Poisson-Lie groups and Lie bialgebras has been studied using Lie algebra crossed-modules and L 2 L_{2} -algebras [ 3 , 4 , 5 ] , which gave rise to the notion of (strict) Lie 2-bialgebras and Poisson-Lie 2-groups . In this paper, we use these structures to generalize the construction of a Lax pairs and introduce an appropriate notion of higher homotopy integrability. Within this framework, we introduce a higher homotopy version of the Kac-Moody algebra, with which the 2-Lax equation can be rewritten as a zero 2-curvature condition in 2+1d. An explicit characterization of our higher Kac-Moody algebra will be given, and we also demonstrate how it governs the 2-Lax pairs and the symmetries of the 3d topological-holomorphic integrable field theory derived by one of the authors (HC) recently. This 3d theory thus serves as an example of a physical system that exhibits the sort of 2-graded integrability that we have devised here.

Introduction

It is well-known since the 19th century that physical classical systems can be described by a
Poisson structure
on the space
M
of configurations, which is a bilinear skew-symmetric map
\{-,-\}
called the
Poisson bracket
on the space
C^{\infty}(M)
of smooth functions on
M
satisfying the Leibniz rule and the Jacobi identity.
The classical dynamics, under a chosen
Hamiltonian
function
H\in C^{\infty}(M)
, of the observables
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
in the system is governed by the Poisson bracket on
M
, through the following differential equation

\dot{f}=\{H,f\},

with some appropriate initial conditions. The Poisson structure also serves as the precursor to canonical Dirac quantization, in the sense that
\{-,-\}
contributes to the quantum commutator
[-,-]
to first order in the quantum deformation parameter (usually denoted by
\hbar
), hence Poisson geometry in general plays a central role in the understanding of various physical systems, both classical and quantum.

A manifold
M
equipped with a Poisson structure
\{-,-\}
together with the choice of a Hamiltonian
(M,\{-,-\},H)
is called a
Hamiltonian system
. A particularly special class of Hamiltonian systems has "enough" conserved quantities — namely functions
f
with
\dot{f}=0
— such that the dynamics of
H
can be separately described completely on the level sets of these conserved quantities. More precisely, we call
(M,\{-,-\},H)

(completely) integrable
if there exist
n

in involution, namely
\{f_{i},f_{j}\}=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
, where
n=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dim}M
. This is because such a system
\{f_{i}\}_{i}
of conserved quantities partitions the Poisson manifold into leaves that are invariant under the dynamics generated by
H
.

Finding constants of motion is not an easy task, and integrable systems in general are difficult to characterize. To get a handle on them, the theory of
Lax pairs
had been developed
[
6
,
7
,
1
]
. A Lax pair
(L,P)
is a tuple of maps
M\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
into a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that the
Lax equation

\dot{L}=\{H,L\}=[L,P]

(1.1)

holds. If the Hamiltonian system
(M,\{-,-\},H)
admits a Lax pair, this Lax equation implies that the following
trace polynomials
on any representation space
V
of
\mathfrak{g}

f_{V,k}=\operatorname{tr}_{V}\rho(L)^{k},\qquad\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)

are constants of motion in involution
[
2
]
for any
k
. In particular, the eigenvalues of
L
are conserved quantities, hence for
\mathfrak{g}
of sufficiently large rank,
(M,\{-,-\},H)
is integrable
[
1
]
. By the trace-determinant relation
\exp\circ\operatorname{tr}=\operatorname{det}\circ\exp
, these trace polynomials

can be summed into the
monodromy determinants

[
1
,
8
,
9
,
10
]

M_{V}=\operatorname{det}_{V}\tilde{\rho}(e^{L})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}f_{V,k}

(1.2)

labeled by (irreducible) representations

of
G
corresponding to

Finding a Lax pair on a general Hamiltonian system, if one even exists, is itself a difficult problem. However, if we are given a Poisson-Lie group

11

Indeed, from the bialgebra

2
Integrable systems from Lie bialgebras: a review

We will review first how to construct a Lax pair by picking a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a
r
-matrix. We then recall the construction in the Wess-Zumino-Witten model.

2.1
Lax pair

In this section, we first recall how
r
-matrix associated to a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
can be used to define a Lie algebra 1-cocycle, which upon integration will give rise to a Poisson bivector on the group
G
. We will then use this Poisson structure to construct the Lax pair.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
denote a Lie algebra over the field
k
. Recall that a quasitriangular classical
r
-matrix
r\in\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes 2}
on
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the
modified classical Yang-Baxter equation

\llbracket r^{\wedge},r^{\wedge}\rrbracket=\omega=-\llbracket r^{\odot},r^{\odot}\rrbracket,

where
\llbracket-,-\rrbracket
is the Schouten bracket and
r=r^{\wedge}+r^{\odot}
decomposes into skew-symmetric
r^{\wedge}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\wedge 2}
and symmetric
r^{\odot}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\odot 2}
components.

The classical Yang-Baxter equation imposes the
\operatorname{ad}
-invariance of the symmetric part
r^{\odot}
, which makes it into a quadratic Casimir. Non-degenerate elements therein determines on
\mathfrak{g}
a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
\langle-,-\rangle=\langle-,-\rangle_{r^{\odot}}
, which is unique (the Killing form) if
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple. Define the space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
dual to
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to this non-degenerate bilinear form by
g=\langle-,X\rangle\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
for each
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. We thus understand
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
as the space of linear functionals
g
on
\mathfrak{g}
given by the evaluation pairing
g(X)=r^{\odot}(g,x)
.

The skew-symmetric part
r^{\wedge}
defines a 1-cocycle
\psi\in Z^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{\wedge 2})
given by

\psi X=(\operatorname{ad}_{X}\otimes 1+1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{X})r^{\wedge},\qquad X\in\mathfrak{g},

and induces the Lie bracket
[-,-]_{*}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defined with respect to the bilinear form
r^{\odot}
as

\langle[g,g^{\prime}]_{*},X\rangle=[g,g^{\prime}]_{*}(X)=(g\wedge g^{\prime})(\psi X)=\langle g\wedge g^{\prime},\psi X\rangle,\quad g,g^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Hence, the classical
r
-matrix
r=r^{\odot}+r^{\wedge}
can be used to define a map
\varphi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
that we can use to define the dual Lie bracket

\displaystyle\varphi(X)

\displaystyle=(r^{\wedge})^{ij}\langle X,T_{i}\rangle T_{j}

\displaystyle[g,g^{\prime}]_{*}

\displaystyle=\langle-,[\varphi(X),X^{\prime}]+[X,\varphi(X^{\prime})]\rangle,\qquad X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}

(2.1)

where
g=\langle-,X\rangle
and
g^{\prime}=\langle-,X^{\prime}\rangle
and
\{T_{i}\}_{i}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. The map
\varphi
allows to identify yet another Lie bracket
[-,-]_{r}=[-,-]\circ(\varphi\otimes 1+1\otimes\varphi)
on
\mathfrak{g}
, which will be used in (
2.2
) later to define a particular Poisson bracket on functions on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

We now integrate
\psi
to a group 1-cocycle
\hat{\psi}:G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\wedge 2}
, and define the corresponding
Poisson bivector
,
\Pi
,

\psi(X)=-\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\hat{\psi}_{\exp sX},\qquad\Pi_{x}=(L_{x})_{*}\hat{\psi}_{x},

via the tangent pushforward
L_{*}
of the left-multiplication on
G
for each
X\in\mathfrak{g},x\in G
. Identifying
\mathfrak{g}
with left-invariant tangent vector fields on
G
, we induce the natural Poisson bracket

\{F,F^{\prime}\}=\Pi(F\otimes F^{\prime}),\qquad F,F^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(G)

on
G
. The Jacobi identity follows from the
cocycle condition

(\operatorname{ad}_{x}\otimes 1+1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{x})\psi=0
,
x\in G
. In section
3
, a review of the 2-graded version of the classical
r
-matrix and Poisson Lie groups will be given following
[
3
,
4
]
.

If we use the non-degenerate pairing
r^{\odot}
to identify the cotangent fibres with
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we induce a Poisson bracket

\{\phi,\phi^{\prime}\}^{*}_{r}(g)=\langle g,[d_{g}\phi,d_{g}\phi^{\prime}]_{r}\rangle,\quad\phi,\phi^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\quad g\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},

(2.2)

called the
Kirillov-Kostant Poisson bracket
on
C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})

[
2
]
, where the bracket
[-,-]^{\prime}
is defined with the map
\varphi
as in (
2.1
). The quadratic Casimir
r^{\odot}
is invariant under the coadjoint action of the Lie group
G
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,

\langle\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{x}g,X\rangle=\langle g,\operatorname{Ad}_{x^{-1}}X\rangle,

where
x\in G
and
\operatorname{Ad}
is the adjoint representation of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

This then allows us to construct invariant Hamiltonians
H
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
from

. This is significant, as the Hamiltonian system
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{-,-\}^{*},H)
is automatically integrable
[
2
]
. Indeed, it admits a Lax pair
(L,P):\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, satisfying

\dot{L}=\{H,L\}_{r}^{*}=[L,P]

(2.3)

with (recall repeated indices are summed)

L:g\mapsto(r^{\odot})^{ij}\langle g,T_{i}\rangle T_{j},\qquad P:g\mapsto\varphi(d_{g}H)=(r^{\wedge})^{ij}\langle T_{i},d_{g}H\rangle T_{j}

(2.4)

in terms of the basis
\{T_{i}\}_{i}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, in which
L
is given by
[
2
]

\{L,L\}^{*}_{r}=[L\otimes 1+1\otimes L,r^{\wedge}].

This construction of a canonical Lax pair on the Hamiltonian system
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{-,-\}^{*},H)
is the key result that will be generalized in section
4

2.2
Zero curvature representation of the Lax equation

A very interesting fact regarding the Lax equation (
2.3
) is that it can equivalently be represented as a zero curvature condition

\dot{A}_{x}-\partial_{x}A_{t}+[A_{t},A_{x}]=0

for a connection
A=A_{t}dt+A_{x}dx
in 1+1d. The easiest way to see this is to introduce the so-called
Kac-Moody algebra

\widehat{\Omega_{k}\mathfrak{g}}
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(over
\mathbb{C})
. This object is linearly isomorphic as a vector space to the direct sum
\Omega\mathfrak{g}\oplus k\mathbb{C}
, where
\Omega\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Map}(S^{1},\mathfrak{g})=C^{\infty}(S^{1})\otimes\mathfrak{g}
denotes the (infinite-dimensional) space of
\mathfrak{g}
-valued smooth functions on the circle
S^{1}
, called the
loop algebra
.

We now review some basic facts about the Kac-Moody current algebra from the loop algebra perspective, following
[
1
,
51
,
52
]

\widehat{\Omega_{k}\mathfrak{g}}
is given by the following

\displaystyle[X\oplus\xi,X^{\prime}\oplus\xi^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=[X,X^{\prime}]\oplus 2\lambda\int_{S^{1}}dx\langle X,\frac{d}{dx}X^{\prime}\rangle,\qquad X,X^{\prime}\in\Omega\mathfrak{g},\penalty\ \xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C},

(2.5)

where
x\in S^{1}
is a local coordinate on the circle. This structure can be understood as arising from a Lie algebra central extension

k\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\widehat{\Omega_{k}\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\Omega\mathfrak{g}

given by a Lie algebra 2-cocycle
k\in Z^{1}(\Omega\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{C})
given by
[
52
]

k(X,X^{\prime})=2\lambda\int_{S^{1}}dx\langle X,\frac{d}{dx}X^{\prime}\rangle,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a scaling parameter called the

.

Remark 2.1
.

Is is sometimes convenient for the representation theory to append an additional generator

The Kac-Moody algebra has also equipped a pairing

(2.6)

from which the defining relation

\langle\operatorname{ad}_{X\oplus\xi}^{*}X^{\prime\prime}\oplus\xi^{\prime\prime},X^{\prime}\oplus\xi^{\prime}\rangle=-\langle[X\oplus\xi,X^{\prime}\oplus\xi^{\prime}],X^{\prime\prime}\oplus\xi^{\prime\prime}\rangle

Now suppose

2.3
Lax formulation of the 2d Wess-Zumino-Witten model

The Kac-Moody algebra plays a central role in the so-called
Wess-Zumino-Witten model

[
54
,
55
,
10
]
. This is a 2d conformal sigma model
g:M^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined on a 2d surface
M^{2}
valued in a compact simple Lie group
G
. The action is given by

W[g]=\lambda\int_{M^{2}}\langle g^{-1}dg,\star_{2}g^{-1}dg\rangle+\frac{\lambda}{4}\int_{B^{3}}\langle\tilde{g}^{-1}d\tilde{g},[\tilde{g}^{-1}d\tilde{g},\tilde{g}^{-1}d\tilde{g}]\rangle,

where
\langle-,-\rangle
is the canonical Killing form and
\lambda
is called the
level
of the theory. The second term in
W
, called the
Wess-Zumino term
, requires the extension
\tilde{g}:B^{3}\rightarrow G
of
g
to a 3d contractible manifold
B^{3}
with
\partial B^{3}=M^{2}
. More precisely,
\tilde{g}
is a null-homotopy of
g
from
\Sigma
to the point. This term serves to quantize the level
\lambda\in 4\pi\mathbb{Z}

[
15
]
.

Let
(z,\bar{z})\in M^{2}
denote local complex coordinates on the worldsheet
M^{2}
. The equations of motion for the Wess-Zumino-Witten model concerns the conservation

\partial_{\bar{z}}J=0,\qquad\partial_{z}\bar{J}=0

of the currents
J=\lambda\partial_{z}gg^{-1},\bar{J}=\lambda g^{-1}\partial_{\bar{z}}g
. By virtue of them being given respectively by the right and left Maurer-Cartan forms of
G
, they each define a flat
G
-connection on the worldsheet
M^{2}
, and hence give rise to a Lax pair,

(L,P)=(J_{x},J_{t}),\qquad(\bar{L},\bar{P})=(\bar{J}_{x},\bar{J}_{t}),

where
z=x+it
in terms of real coordinates. It can be shown that these Lax pairs/currents
J,\bar{J}
satisfy Poisson brackets given precisely by the Kac-Moody algebra
\widehat{\Omega_{k}\mathfrak{g}}

[
55
]
.

From the Polyakov-Wiegmann formula,
W
acquires a two-sided global symmetry
g\mapsto\bar{\Omega}^{-1}g\Omega
parameterized by holmorphic/anti-holomorphic symmetry parameters
\Omega,\bar{\Omega}
. By considering infinitesimal
\Omega\sim 1+\omega
variation of the currents under this symmetry, one can deduce
[
39
]

\displaystyle\delta_{\bar{\omega}}J

\displaystyle=\delta_{\omega}\bar{J}=0,

\displaystyle\delta_{\omega}J

\displaystyle=[\omega,J]+\lambda\partial_{z}\omega,

\displaystyle\delta_{\bar{\omega}}\bar{J}

\displaystyle=[\bar{\omega},\bar{J}]+\lambda\partial_{\bar{z}}\bar{\omega},

which ensures that the Wess-Zumino-Witten model has global symmetries given by the Kac-Moody algebra
\widehat{\Omega_{k}\mathfrak{g}}
, with
\lambda
playing the role of the central charge as in (
2.5

) from the Lax pairs
(L,P),(\bar{L},\bar{P})

[
1
]
,

M_{\mu,\bar{\mu}}=\operatorname{det}_{V_{\mu,\bar{\mu}}}(P\exp L,P\exp\bar{L})

(2.9)

labeled by irreducible representations

of the
global
Kac-Moody group
\widehat{\Omega_{k}G}
(see
[
51

3.1
Lie 2-algebras and Lie 2-groups

Definition 3.1
.

A Lie algebra crossed-module
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd,[-,-]_{0})
is the data of a pair of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g}_{-1},[-,-]{{{}^{(\bf{-1})}}})
,
(\mathfrak{g}_{0},[-,-]_{0})
, a Lie algebra derivation
\rhd:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
and a Lie algebra homomorphism

(called the
t
-map

t(X\rhd Y)=[X,tY]_{0},\qquad[Y,Y^{\prime}]{{{}^{(\bf{-1})}}}=(tY)\rhd Y^{\prime},

(3.1)

as well as the 2-Jacobi identities

\displaystyle[X,[X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}]_{0}]_{0}+[X^{\prime},[X^{\prime\prime},X]_{0}]_{0}+[X^{\prime\prime},[X,X^{\prime}]_{0}]_{0}=0,

\displaystyle X\rhd(X^{\prime}\rhd Y)-X^{\prime}\rhd(X\rhd Y)-[X,X^{\prime}]_{0}\rhd Y=0,

(3.2)

\forall X,X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},
and
\forall Y,Y^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
.

We shall denote a Lie algebra crossed-module by
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\xrightarrow{t}\mathfrak{g}_{0},\rhd,[-,-]_{0})

[
5
]
.

It is well-known that Lie algebra crossed-modules are equivalent to
strict

L_{2}
-algebras
[
3
,
59
]

Definition 3.2
.

A
strict

L_{2}
-algebra is a strict 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Explicitly, one has a graded space
\mathfrak{g}\cong V_{-1}\oplus V_{0}
equipped with
n

\mu_{n}\in\operatorname{Hom}^{2-n}(\mathfrak{g}^{n\wedge},\mathfrak{g})
given by

n=1:\quad\mu_{1}:V_{-1}\rightarrow V_{0},\qquad n=2:\quad\mu_{2}=[-,-]:(V_{0}\oplus V_{-1})\otimes(V_{0}\oplus V_{-1})\rightarrow(V_{0}\oplus V_{-1})

such that the following
Koszul conditions
are satisfied,

\displaystyle[X,X^{\prime}]=-[X^{\prime},X],\quad[X,Y]=-[Y,X],\quad\mu_{1}[X,Y]=[X,\mu_{1}Y],\quad[\mu_{1}Y,Y^{\prime}]=[Y,\mu_{1}Y^{\prime}],

\displaystyle[[X,X^{\prime}],X^{\prime\prime}]+[[X^{\prime\prime},X],X^{\prime}]+[[X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}],X]=0,\qquad[[X,X^{\prime}],Y]+[[X,Y],X^{\prime}]+[X,[X^{\prime},Y]]=0,

where
X,X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\in V_{0}
,
Y,Y^{\prime}\in V_{-1}
.

It is convenient to write the graded bracket
\mu_{2}=[-,-]:V_{i}\otimes V_{j}\rightarrow V_{i+j}
,
-2\leq i+j\leq 0
, in terms of the degree
i,j\mod 2
of
\mathfrak{g}\cong V_{-1}\oplus V_{0}
, such that

\mu_{2}(Y+X,Y\textquoteright+X\textquoteright)=[X,X^{\prime}]+\big([X,Y^{\prime}]+[Y,X^{\prime}]\big),\qquad X,X^{\prime}\in V_{0},\penalty\ Y,Y^{\prime}\in V_{-1}.

(3.3)

We shall also extend
\mu_{1}
to the full space
V_{0}\oplus V_{-1}
by
\mu_{1}(Y+X)=\mu_{1}Y
.

Given a Lie algebra crossed-module
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\xrightarrow{t}\mathfrak{g}_{0},\rhd,[-,-]_{0})

\mathfrak{g}_{-1}=V_{-1},\mathfrak{g}_{0}=V_{0}
and

from the bracket
[-,-]_{0}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
as well as the Lie algebra action

\mu_{2}(Y+X,Y\textquoteright+X\textquoteright)=[X,X^{\prime}]_{0}+\big(X\rhd Y^{\prime}-X^{\prime}\rhd Y\big),\qquad X,X^{\prime}\in V_{0},\penalty\ Y,Y^{\prime}\in V_{-1}.

It is then simple to check that the Lie algebra crossed-module conditions imply precisely the Koszul conditions; in particular, the Peiffer identity implies the Koszul identity

[\mu_{1}Y,Y^{\prime}]=[tY,Y^{\prime}]=[Y,Y^{\prime}]{{}^{(\bf{-1})}}=-[Y^{\prime},Y]{{}^{(\bf{-1})}}=-[tY^{\prime},Y]=-[\mu_{1}Y^{\prime},Y]=[Y,\mu_{1}Y^{\prime}]

as required. Conversely, given a strict
L_{2}
-algebra, one may recover a Lie algebra crossed-module with the above procedure, provided one
defines
the bracket
[-,-]{{{}^{(\bf{-1})}}}
on

by

[Y,Y^{\prime}]{{{}^{(\bf{-1})}}}\equiv[\mu_{1}Y,Y^{\prime}],

(3.4)

whence the Koszul conditions guarantee that this bracket is skew-symmetric and satisfies the Jacobi identity.

Due to this result, we will use "

4
2-graded integrability

The goal in this section is to lay out the general theory of 2-graded integrable systems, and derive an appropriate notion of a "2-graded Lax equation" as a categorification of the usual Lax equation. Then, once this is achieved, we specialize to the dual crossed-module

We begin with a 2-graded space

4.1

Conclusion

In this paper, we have defined a notion of 2-graded integrability inherited from the structure of a Poisson-Lie 2-group, which can be interpreted as a categorification of the usual notion of integrability for 1d systems to 2-dimensions. We have explicitly generalized various integrability theorems from the Lie 1-algebra case as proven in
[
2
]
, and wrote down an explicit formula for the canonical 2-Lax pair that one can construct from a solution of the 2-CYBE in a Lie 2-bialgebra
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. We have also found that, through the
t
-map of the underlying Lie 2-bialgebra, the usual 1-Lax equation is in fact hidden in our definition of a 2-Lax pair. This result can be understood as an integrable "bulk-boundary" relation.

Another major result in this paper is a new proposal for a higher homotopy extension of the usual Kac-Moody algebra, which we dubbed the "2-Kac-Moody algebra"
\widehat{\Sigma_{s}\mathfrak{g}}
. We found that its structure very naturally allowed us to cast the 2-graded Lax equation in terms of a zero 2-curvature condition. The fact that we have found
\widehat{\Sigma_{s}\mathfrak{g}}
to admit a coistropic splitting and host a homotopy Manin triple structure echoes the work of
[
50
]
, where a derived homotopy extension of the Kac-Moody algebra as a Manin triple was also studied.

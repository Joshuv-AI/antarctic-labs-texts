Abstract
. We construct some analog of cubical Bloch’s higher Chow groups. Instead of considering cycles in X × 𝔸 n X\times\mathbb{A}^{n} we consider varieties Y Y over X X together with a distinguished element in the n n -th exterior power of the multiplicative group of the field of fraction on Y Y . This definition allows us to make sense of a cycle in X × 𝔸 n X\times\mathbb{A}^{n} intersecting faces improperly as an element in this complex. We prove that this complex is well-defined and study its basic properties: flat pullback, the localization sequence etc. As an application we prove that the cohomology of this complex in degrees m − 1 , m m-1,m and weight m m isomorphic to the cohomology of polylogarithmic complex.



1.
Introduction

Everywhere we work over
\mathbb{Q}
. So any abelian group is supposed to be tensored by
\mathbb{Q}
. Fix some field
K
of characteristic zero. All varieties are defined over
K
.

Let
X
be an algebraic scheme over
K
. The definition of Bloch’s higher Chow group of
X
can be found in
[
Blo86
]
. We use cubical version
[
Lev94
]
. This group is defined as cohomology of certain complex
z^{r}(X,2r-*)
. The group
z^{r}(X,n)
is a quotient of the group of codimension
r
algebraic cycles on
X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{1\})^{n}\cong X\times\mathbb{A}^{n}
intersecting all faces properly by the subgroup of degenerate cycles. The differential is given by the signed sum of intersections with codimension one faces.

Consider the simplest case when
X
is a point. In this case the data of an irreducible cycle on
({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{1\})^{n}
is the same as the data of an algebraic variety
Z
together with
n
rational functions
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
on
Z
satisfying certain conditions. The functions
\xi_{i}
are just restrictions of coordinate functions to
Z
.

The main idea of this paper is to replace the data
(Z,\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})
by the data
(Z,w)
, where
w
is an arbitrary element in
\Lambda^{n}(K(Z)^{\times})
. Here
K(Z)^{\times}
is the multiplicative group of the field of rational functions on
Z
considered as an abelian group. The element
(Z,\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})
corresponds to
(Z,\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{n})
. The differential is given by so-called tame symbol map defined by A. Goncharov
[
Gon95
]
.

In particular, any irreducible cycle
Z\subset({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{1\})^{n}
not lying in the union of faces gives an element in this complex. This cycle may intersect faces of codimension two and higher improperly. This is what makes our complex different from classical higher Chow group.

Let us make this idea precise. We recall that
an alteration
is a proper surjective morphism which is generically finite.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be an algebraic scheme over
K
and
m,j
be integers. Set
p=\dim X+m-j
and
n=2m-j
. Denote by
\widetilde{\Lambda}(X,m)_{j}
a vector space freely generated by the isomorphism classes of the triples
(Y,a,f)
, where:
Y
is a variety over
K
of dimension
p
,
f\colon Y\to X
is a proper morphism and
a\in\Lambda^{n}(K(Y)^{\times})
. Denote by
[Y,a,f]\in\widetilde{\Lambda}(X,m)_{j}
the corresponding element.
Denote by
\Lambda(X,m)_{j}
the quotient of
\widetilde{\Lambda}(X,m)_{j}
by the following relations:

[\widetilde{Y},\varphi^{*}(a),f\circ\varphi]=(\deg\varphi)[Y,a,f].

[Y,a+b,f]=[Y,a,f]+[Y,b,f].

[Y,\lambda\cdot a,f]=\lambda[Y,a,f].

In this formula
\varphi\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
is any alteration and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
. The map
\varphi^{*}
is defined by the formula

\varphi^{*}(\alpha_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\alpha_{n})=\varphi^{*}(\alpha_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge\varphi^{*}(\alpha_{n})
. We remark that we write group law in the group
\Lambda^{n}K(Y)^{\times}
additively. So in the case
n=1
addition in this group corresponds to
multiplication
of functions.

Below we will give a precise definition of the differential in this complex and will show that in this way we get a well-defined complex. The fact that
d^{2}=0
will follow from some version of Parshin reciprocity law
[
Par75
,
Bol23
]
.

1.1.
The statement of the main results

We will prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

The complex
\Lambda(X,m)
is well-defined. It satisfies the following properties:

(1)

(Functoriality) The complex
\Lambda(\cdot,m)
is covariantly functorial for proper maps and contravariantly functorial for flat maps.

(2)

(Localization) Let
Z
be a closed subset of
X
of codimension
k
. Denote by
i
the embedding
Z\hookrightarrow X
and by
j
the complementary open embedding
U\hookrightarrow X
. We have the following exact sequence of complexes:

0\to\Lambda(Z,m-k)[-2k]\xrightarrow{i_{*}}\Lambda(X,m)\xrightarrow{j^{*}}\Lambda(U,m)\to 0.

(3)

Denote by
z^{m}(X,2m-*)
the complex calculating cubical Bloch’s higher Chow group in weight
m
. There is the canonical morphism of complexes

z^{m}(X,2m-*)\to\Lambda(X,m)_{*}.

Conjecture 1.3
.

The map
z^{m}(X,2r-*)\to\Lambda(X,m)_{*}
is a quasi-isomorphism.

It is interesting that in our case, the localization sequence holds on the level of complexes. Compare our proof with nontrivial arguments from
[
Blo94
]
.

We recall that
K
is the base field.
The following statement is of independent interest.

Theorem 1.4
.

The group
\Lambda(K,m)/\im(d)
is generated by the elements of the form
[Y^{\prime}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1},a,f].

Even in the case
j=m-1
, the corresponding statement for cubical Bloch’s higher Chow group is not known (see
[
Ger91
]
for the simplicial case).

The second part of this paper is devoted to the connection of the complex
\Lambda(K,m)
with polylogarithmic complex defined by A. Goncharov in
[
Gon94
,
Gon95
]
. Denote weight
m
polylogarithmic complex of the field
K
by
\Gamma(K,m)
.

Let
C
be a complex. Denote by
\tau_{\geq k}C
its canonical truncation. Define a morphism of complexes

\mathcal{T}_{\geq m-1}(m)\colon\tau_{\geq m-1}\Gamma(K,m)\to\tau_{\geq m-1}\Lambda(K,m)

as follows. The element
\{a\}_{2}\wedge c_{3}\wedge\dots\wedge c_{m}
goes to

[{\mathbb{P}}^{1},t\wedge(1-t)\wedge(1-a/t)\wedge c_{3}\wedge\dots\wedge c_{m}].

The element
c_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge c_{m}
goes to

[\spec K,c_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge c_{m}]
.

The construction of this map goes back to Totaro
[
Tot92
]
. See also
[
BK94
,
GGL09
,
GMS99
,
Zha07
]
.
Here is our main result:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero. The map
\mathcal{T}_{\geq m-1}(m)
is a quasi-isomorphism. In particular for
j=m-1,m
we have

H^{j}(\Lambda(\spec K,m))\cong H^{j}(\Gamma(K,m)).

Moreover, when
K
is algebraically closed the map
\mathcal{T}_{\geq m-1}(m)
is an isomorphism of complexes.

It follows from the proof of this theorem, that in the case
m=2
the map
\mathcal{T}_{\geq 1}(2)
can be lifted to a map
\mathcal{T}(2)\colon\Gamma(K,2)\to\Lambda(K,2)
. It is surprising, because in the case of higher Chow group there is no natural map between the corresponding complexes. The reason is that Abel’s five-term relation holds only modulo coboundary.

1.2.
Preliminary definitions

We recall that we work over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
K
be a field.
An algebraic scheme over
K
is a finite type scheme over
K
.
A variety
is an algebraic scheme which is integral and separated. Throughout the article we assume that the field
K
has characteristic zero. All schemes are assumed to be equidimensional.
An alteration

\varphi\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
is a proper surjective morphism between integral schemes which is generically finite. Unless otherwise stated, we always assume
\widetilde{Y}
to be a smooth variety.

Let
(F,\nu)
be a discrete valuation field. Denote
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}=\{x\in F|\nu(x)\geq 0\},m_{\nu}=\{x\in F|\nu(x)>0\}
and
\overline{F}_{\nu}=\mathcal{O}_{\nu}/m_{\nu}
. We recall that an element
a\in F^{\times}
is called
a uniformiser
if
\nu(a)=1
and
a unit
if
\nu(a)=0
. For
u\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
denote by
\overline{u}
its residue class in
\overline{F}_{\nu}
.

The proof of the following proposition can be found in
[
Gon95
]
:

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
(F,\nu)
be a discrete valuation field and
n\geq 1
. There is a unique map
\ts_{\nu}\colon\Lambda^{n}(F^{\times})\to\Lambda^{n-1}\overline{F}_{\nu}^{\times}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

When
n=1
we have
\ts_{\nu}(a)=\nu(a)
.

(2)

For any units
u_{2},\dots u_{n}\in F
we have
\ts_{\nu}(x\wedge u_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge u_{n})=\nu(x)\wedge\overline{u_{2}}\dots\wedge\overline{u_{n}}
.

The map
\ts_{\nu}
is called
tame symbol map
. Throughout the paper we will repeatedly use the following lemma:

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
(F,\nu)
be a discrete valuation field. Let
k,n
be two natural numbers satisfying the condition
k<n
. Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in F^{\times}
such that
\nu(a_{k+1}),\dots,\nu(a_{n})=0
. Then the following formula holds:

\ts_{\nu}(a_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge a_{n})=\ts_{\nu}(a_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge a_{k})\wedge\overline{a_{k+1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\overline{a_{n}}.

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety and
D\subset X
be an irreducible divisor. Denote by
\nu_{D}
a discrete valuation corresponding to
D
. Abusing notation we write
\ts_{D}
for
\ts_{\nu_{D}}
.

Let
Y
be a variety. Denote
\Lambda^{n}(Y):=\Lambda^{n}(K(Y)^{\times}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q})
. Here
K(Y)^{\times}
is the multiplicative group of the field
K(Y)
considered as an abelian group.

Let
\varphi\colon Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
be a dominant morphism between varieties. Denote by
\varphi^{*}
the map
K(Y_{2})^{\times}\to K(Y_{1})^{\times}
given by the formula
\varphi^{*}(\alpha)=\alpha\circ\varphi
. We will denote by the same symbol the map
\Lambda^{n}(Y_{2})\to\Lambda^{n}(Y_{1})
given by the formula
\varphi^{*}(\alpha_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\alpha_{n})=\varphi^{*}(\alpha_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge\varphi^{*}(\alpha_{n})
.

Let
D_{1}\subset Y_{1},D_{2}\subset Y_{2}
be irreducible divisors such that
\varphi(D_{1})=D_{2}
. Denote by
e(D_{1},D_{2})
the multiplicity of
\varphi^{-1}(D_{2})
along
D_{1}
. This number can be computed as
\ord_{D_{1}}(\varphi^{*}(\alpha))
, where
\alpha
is any rational function on
Y_{2}
satisfying
\ord_{D_{2}}(f)=1
.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
\varphi\colon Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
be a dominant morphism between smooth varieties. Let
D_{1}\subset Y_{1},D_{2}\subset Y_{2}
be two divisors such that
\overline{\varphi(D_{1})}=D_{2}
. Denote by
\overline{\varphi}
the natural map
D_{1}\to D_{2}
. Then for any
a\in\Lambda^{n}(Y)
we have:

\ts_{D_{1}}(\varphi^{*}(a))=e(D_{1},D_{2})(\overline{\varphi})^{*}(\ts_{D_{2}}(a)).

Proof.

We can assume that
a=\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{n}
such that
\ord_{D_{1}}{\xi_{1}}=1
and
\ord_{D_{1}}(\xi_{i})=0
for any
i>1
. The number
\ord_{D_{1}}(\varphi^{*}(\xi_{i}))
is equal to
e(D_{1},D_{2})
if
i=1
and is equal to
0
otherwise. Now the statement follows from Lemma
1.7
.
∎

Let us assume that
\varphi
is a morphism of finite degree. We have a finite extension
K(D_{1})\subset K(D_{2})
. Denote its degree by
f(D_{1},D_{2})
. Denote by
Div(\varphi)_{0}
the set of divisors contracted under
\varphi
. Let
D\subset Y_{2}
be an irreducible divisor. Denote by
Div(\varphi,D)
the set of irreducible divisors
D^{\prime}\subset Y_{1}
such that
\varphi(D^{\prime})=D
. There is a bijection between the set
Div(\varphi,D)
and the set of extensions of the discrete valuation
\nu_{D}
to the field
K(Y_{1})
. The following statement is well known:

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
\varphi\colon Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
be an alteration. Let
D\subset Y_{2}
be an irreducible divisor. The following formula holds:

\deg\varphi=\sum\limits_{D^{\prime}\in Div(\varphi,D)}e(D^{\prime},D)f(D^{\prime},D).

For a divisor
D=\sum\limits_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}[D_{\alpha}]
on
X
, denote by
|D|

its support
defined by the formula
\sum\limits_{\alpha}[D_{\alpha}]
. A divisor
D
is called
supported on a simple normal crossing divisor
if
|D|
is a simple normal crossing divisor. Let
x\in X
. A divisor
D
is called
supported on a simple normal crossing divisor locally at
x
, if the restriction of this divisor to some open neighborhood of the point
x
is supported on a simple normal crossing divisor.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety and
x\in X
. An element
a\in\Lambda(X)^{n}
is called
strictly regular at
x
if
a
can be represented in the form

\sum_{\beta}n_{\beta}\alpha_{1}^{\beta}\wedge\dots\wedge\alpha_{n}^{\beta}

such that the divisor
\sum\limits_{\beta,j}|(\alpha_{j}^{\beta})|
is supported on a simple normal crossing divisor locally at
x
.

The element
a
is called
strictly regular
if this condition holds for any
x\in X
.

The definition of Bloch’s higher Chow group can be found in
[
Lev94
]
. Denote by
\square^{n}=({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{1\})^{n}
the algebraic cube of dimension
n
. Subvarieties of
X\times\square^{n}
given by the equations of the form
z_{i}=0,\infty
are called faces. Let
\widetilde{z}_{p}(X,n)
be the free abelian group generated by dimension
p
cycles on
X\times\square^{n}
intersecting all faces properly. We have the natural projections
\pi_{i,n}\colon\square^{n}\to\square^{n-1}
given by the formula
\pi_{i,n}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})=(t_{1},\dots\hat{t_{i}},\dots,t_{n})
. Denote by
D_{p}(X,n)\subset\widetilde{z}_{p}(X,n)
a subgroup generated by the images of the maps
(id\times\pi_{i,n})^{*}\colon\widetilde{z}_{p-1}(X,n-1)\to\widetilde{z}_{p}(X,n),i=1,\dots,n
. Finally define

z_{p}(X,n)=\widetilde{z}_{p}(X,n)/D_{p}(X,n).

One can define a differential on
z_{p}(X,n)
given by the alternating sum of intersections with codimension one faces
[
Lev94
]
(we use the convention that the intersection with the face given by the equation
z_{1}=0
has positive sign). In this way we get a complex

\dots\to z_{p+1}(X,n+1)\to z_{p}(X,n)\to z_{p-1}(X,n-1)\to\dots

It is convenient to pass to motivic grading. As in the case of the complex
\Lambda
, denote this complex by
CH(X,m)
, where
z_{p}(X,n)
sits in degree
2\dim X+n-2p
and
m=\dim X+n-p
.

1.3.
Definition of the complex
\Lambda(X,m)

Definition 1.11
.

Let
X
be an algebraic scheme. Let
m,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Set
p=\dim X+m-j,n=2m-j
. We have
m=\dim X+n-p
and
j=2\dim X+n-2p
. Throughout the paper we will use the pairs of integers
(m,j),(n,p)
interchangeably.

Definition 1.12
.

Denote by
\widetilde{\Lambda}(X,m)_{j}
a vector space freely generated by the isomorphism classes of the triples
(Y,a,f)
, where:
Y
is a variety over
K
of dimension
p
,
f\colon Y\to X
is a proper morphism and
a\in\Lambda^{n}(K(Y)^{\times}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q})
. Denote by
[Y,a,f]\in\widetilde{\Lambda}(X,m)_{j}
the corresponding element.
Denote by
\Lambda(X,m)_{j}
the quotient of
\widetilde{\Lambda}(X,m)_{j}
by the following relations:

[\widetilde{Y},\varphi^{*}(a),f\circ\varphi]=(\deg\varphi)[Y,a,f].

[Y,a+b,f]=[Y,a,f]+[Y,b,f].

[Y,\lambda\cdot a,f]=\lambda[Y,a,f].

In this formula
\varphi\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
is any alteration and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
. The map
\varphi^{*}
is defined by the formula

\varphi^{*}(\alpha_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\alpha_{n})=\varphi^{*}(\alpha_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge\varphi^{*}(\alpha_{n})
.

Let us formulate this definition in a more categorical fashion. Denote by
{\mathcal{V}ar}_{X,p}
the category of all dimension
p
proper varieties over
X
and their proper dominant morphisms.

Consider the functor
\Lambda^{n}
on this category. Its value on some variety
Y\in{\mathcal{V}ar}_{X,d}
is equal to the vector space
\Lambda^{n}(K(Y)^{\times}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Q}})
. For a morphism
\varphi\colon Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
the corresponding map
\Lambda^{n}(Y_{2})\to\Lambda^{n}(Y_{1})
is defined by the formula
\Lambda^{n}(\varphi)(a)=\dfrac{1}{\deg{\varphi}}(\varphi^{*}(a))
.

Definition 1.13
.

\Lambda(X,m)_{j}=\colim\limits_{Y\in{\mathcal{V}ar}_{X,p}}\Lambda^{n}(Y).

As the category
{\mathcal{V}ar}_{X,d}
is essentially small this colimit is a well-defined vector space over
\mathbb{Q}
. It is easy to see that the two definitions bellow are equivalent.

For a variety
Y
, a proper morphism
f\colon Y\to X
and
a\in\Lambda^{n}(Y)
, we denote the corresponding element in
\Lambda(X,m)_{j}
by
[Y,a,f]
. We will abbreviate it to
[Y,a]
in the case when
X
is a point or the map
f
is clear from context. For any proper dominant morphism
\varphi\colon Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
we have
[Y_{2},a,f]=\dfrac{1}{\deg\varphi}[Y_{1},\varphi^{*}(a),f\circ\varphi]
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety,
f\colon Y\to X
be a proper morphism,
a\in\Lambda^{n}(Y)
and
D\subset Y
be an irreducible divisor. Define

\ts_{D}(a,f)=[D,\ts_{D}(a),\left.{f}\right|_{D}].

Define a map
d\colon\Lambda(X,m)_{j}\to\Lambda(X,m)_{j+1}
as follows. Let
[Y,a,f]\in\Lambda(X,m)_{j}
. Choose an alteration
\varphi\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
with smooth
\widetilde{Y}
such that the element
\varphi^{*}(a)
would be strictly regular. Such an alteration exists by results of
[

]
. Define

d([Y,a,f])=\dfrac{1}{\deg\varphi}\sum\limits_{D\subset\widetilde{Y}}\ts_{D}(\varphi^{*}(a),f\circ\varphi).

In this formula the sum is taken over all irreducible divisors
D\subset\widetilde{Y}
. In the next section we will show that this expression does not depend on
\varphi
and is well-defined.

2.
The complex
\Lambda(X,m)
is well-defined

The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be an algebraic scheme over
K
. The complex
\Lambda(X,m)
is well-defined.

2.1.
Some vanishing statements

The goal of this subsection is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
Y
be a smooth variety and
f\colon Y\to X
be a proper morphism. Let
a\in\Lambda^{n}(Y)
be strictly regular. Then for any alteration
\varphi\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
and any divisor
E\subset\widetilde{Y}
contracted under
\varphi
we have
\ts_{E}(\varphi^{*}(a),f\circ\varphi)=0\in\Lambda(X,m)_{j+1}
.

To prove this theorem we need several lemmas.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
Y
and
R
be varieties. Let
f_{1}\colon Y\to R,f_{2}\colon R\to X
be proper morphisms and assume that
f_{1}
is dominant. Assume that the corresponding extension
K(R)\subset K(Y)
has positive transcendent degree. Then for any
a\in\Lambda^{n}(R)
, we have

[Y,f_{1}^{*}(a),f_{2}\circ f_{1}]=0.

Remark 2.4
.

The cycles of this form correspond to so-called degenerate cycles in higher Chow group. So this lemma shows that these elements are zero in
\Lambda(X,m)
automatically and we do not need to take quotient by these elements.

Proof.

Denote by
s
the transcendent degree of the extension
K(R)\subset K(Y)
. Choose a transcendence basis
t_{1},\dots,t_{s}
of
K(Y)
over
K(R)
. It is easy to see that the extension

K(R)(t_{1},\dots,t_{s})\subset K(Y)

is finite. This implies that there is a rational map
g\colon Y\dashrightarrow R\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{s}
of finite degree such that
f_{1}=\pi\circ g
, where
\pi\colon R\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{s}\to R
is the natural projection. There is a proper birational morphism
\varphi\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
such that the map
g_{2}=g\circ\varphi
is regular. As
\pi\circ g_{2}=f_{1}\circ\varphi
is proper the morphism
g_{2}
is proper. We have:

\displaystyle[Y,f_{1}^{*}(a),f_{2}\circ f_{1}]=[\widetilde{Y},\varphi^{*}f_{1}^{*}(a),f_{2}\circ f_{1}\circ\varphi]=

\displaystyle[\widetilde{Y},g_{2}^{*}\pi^{*}(a),f_{2}\circ\pi\circ g_{2}]=\deg(g_{2})[T\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{s},\pi^{*}(a),f_{2}\circ\pi].

Thus we have reduced the statement to the case when
Y=R\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{s}
and
f_{1}=\pi
. Let
\theta
be some morphism
\theta\colon({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{s}\to({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{s}
of degree
l,l>1
. We have

\displaystyle[R\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{s},\pi^{*}(a),f_{2}\circ\pi]=

\displaystyle 1/l[R\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{s},(id\times\theta)^{*}\circ\pi^{*}(a),f_{2}\circ\pi\circ(id\times\theta)]=

\displaystyle 1/l[R\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{s},\pi^{*}(a),f_{2}\circ\pi].

It follows that
[R\times({\mathbb{P}}^{s}),\pi^{*}(a),f_{2}\circ\pi]=0
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
Y
and
R
be varieties. Let
f_{1}\colon Y\to R
,
f_{2}\colon R\to X
be two proper morphisms and assume that
f_{1}
is dominant. Assume that
n\leq\dim Y-\dim R
and in the case
n=\dim Y-\dim R
we additionally assume that
n>0
. Let
n^{\prime}\geq 0
. For any
a\in\Lambda^{n}(Y)
and
b\in\Lambda(n^{\prime})(R)
we have

[Y,a\wedge f_{1}^{*}(b),f_{2}\circ f_{1}]=0.

Remark 2.6
.

It can be deduced from this lemma that for
m<0
we have

\Lambda(m)_{j}=0.

The corresponding statement for Bloch’s higher Chow group is clear.

Proof.

We can assume that
a=\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{n}
. Consider a rational map
g\colon Y\dashrightarrow R\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}
given by

2.2.

The following lemma is a direct corollary of Lemma

and the definition of

Lemma 2.10
.

Let

and

be smooth varieties. Let

be a proper morphism and

be an alteration. Let

Proposition 2.11
.

The map

is well-defined.

Proof.

Let

be a proper morphism and

. We recall that the element

is defined by the formula

\dfrac{1}{\deg\varphi}\sum\limits_{D\subset\widetilde{Y}}\ts_{D}(\varphi^{*}(a),f\circ\varphi),

where
\varphi\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
is any alteration such that the element
\varphi^{*}(a)
is strictly regular. We only need to prove that this element does not depend on
\varphi
.

Let
\varphi_{1}\colon\widetilde{Y}_{1}\to Y
and
\widetilde{Y}_{2}\to Y
be two such alterations. There are alterations
\varphi_{31}\colon\widetilde{Y}_{3}\to Y_{1},\varphi_{32}\colon\widetilde{Y}_{3}\to Y_{2}
such that
\varphi_{1}\circ\varphi_{31}=\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{32}
and the element
(\varphi_{1}\circ\varphi_{31})^{*}(a)=(\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{32})^{*}(a)
is strictly regular. This shows that we only need to check the following statement. Let
\varphi\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
be an alteration and
a\in\Lambda^{n}(Y)
. Assume that the elements
a
and
\varphi^{*}(a)
are strictly regular. Then the following formula holds:

\deg(\varphi)\sum\limits_{D\subset Y}\ts_{D}(a,f)=\sum\limits_{D\subset\widetilde{Y}}\ts_{D}(\varphi^{*}(a),f\circ\varphi).

We have:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{D\subset\widetilde{Y}}\ts_{D}(\varphi^{*}(a)),f\circ\varphi)=

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{D\in Div(\varphi)_{0}}\ts_{D}(\varphi^{*}(a)),f\circ\varphi)+\sum\limits_{D\subset Y}\sum\limits_{D^{\prime}\in Div(\varphi,D)}\ts_{D^{\prime}}(\varphi^{*}(a),f\circ\varphi).

The first term is equal to zero by Theorem
2.2
. By Lemma
2.10
and Lemma

the second term is equal to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{D\subset Y}\sum\limits_{D^{\prime}\in Div(\varphi,D)}e(D^{\prime},D)f(D^{\prime},D)\ts_{D}(a,f)=

\displaystyle=\deg(\varphi)\sum\limits_{D\subset Y}\ts_{D}(a,f).

The proposition is proved.
∎

of irreducible subvarieties of codimensions

and

(resp.

to

(resp.

there are precisely two

. Moreover these two elements have opposite signs. The particular case of this theorem when

. The case

.

.

It is enough to show that for any subvariety

of codimension

(The sum is taken over all irreducible divisors

Choose some point

and assume that

of the point

The statement follows.

h_{K(t)}(t\wedge(1-t)\wedge(1-a/t)\wedge c_{4}\wedge\dots\wedge c_{m+1})=-\{a\}_{2}\otimes c_{4}\wedge\dots\wedge c_{m+1}.

Proof.

Let

b=\{t\}_{2}\otimes(1-a/t)\wedge c_{4}\wedge\dots\wedge c_{m+1}.

By the definition of the map
h_{K(t)}
we know that

Tot_{k(t)}(b)=h_{k(t)}\delta_{m+1}(b).
We have

Tot_{k(t)}(b)=-\{a\}_{2}\otimes c_{4}\wedge\dots\wedge c_{m+1},\delta_{m+1}(b)=t\wedge(1-t)\wedge(1-a/t)\wedge c_{4}\wedge\dots\wedge c_{m+1}.

The statement follows.
∎

6.2.
The construction of the lift

Definition 6.7
.

Let
F\in\mathbf{Fields}_{1}
. A valuation
\nu\in\dval(F(t))
is called
general
if it corresponds to some irreducible polynomial over
F
. The set of general valuations are in bijection with the set of all closed points on the affine line over
F
, which we denote by
\mathbb{A}_{F,(0)}^{1}
. A valuation is called
special
if it is not general. Denote the set of general (resp. special) valuations by
\dval(F(t))_{gen}
(resp.
\dval(F(t))_{sp}
).

Remark 6.8
.

Let
F\in\mathbf{Fields}_{1}
. Let us realize
F
as a field of fractions on some smooth projective curve
X
over
k
. Set
S=X\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. It can be checked that a valuation
\nu\in\dval(K(S))
is special in the following two cases:

(1)

There is a birational morphism
p\colon\widetilde{S}\to S
, and the valuation
\nu
corresponds to some irreducible divisor
D\subset\widetilde{S}
contracted under
p
.

(2)

The valuation
\nu
corresponds to some of the divisors
X\times\{\infty\},\{a\}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},a\in X
.

Otherwise, the valuation
\nu
is general.
It follows from this description that if
\nu
is a special valuation different from
X\times\{\infty\}
, then the residue field
\overline{F(t)}_{\nu}
is isomorphic to
K(t)
.

Definition 6.9
.

Let
F\in\mathbf{Fields}_{1}
,
j,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\nu\in\dval(F(t))_{gen}
and
a\in\Gamma(\overline{F(t)}_{\nu},m)_{j}
.
A lift
of the element
a
is an element
b\in\Gamma(F(t),m+1)_{j+1}
satisfying the following two properties:

(1)

\ts_{\nu}(b)=a
and

(2)

for any general valuation
\nu^{\prime}\in\dval(F(t))_{gen}
different from
\nu
, we have
\ts_{\nu^{\prime}}(b)=0
.

The set of all lifts of the element
a
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}(a)
.

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
F\in\mathbf{Fields}_{1}
and
j\in\{m,m+1\}
. For any
\nu\in\dval(F(t))_{gen}
and
a\in\Gamma(\overline{F(t)}_{\nu},m+1)_{j}
, the following statements hold:

(1)

The set
\mathcal{L}(a)
is non-empty.

(2)

Let us assume that
j=m+1
. For any
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(a)
, the element
b_{1}-b_{2}
can be represented in the form
a_{1}+\delta_{m+2}(a_{2})
, where
a_{1}\in\Lambda^{m+2}F^{\times}
and
a_{2}\in\Gamma(F(t),m+2)_{m+1}
such that for any
\nu\in\dval(F(t))_{gen}
the element
\ts_{\nu}(a_{2})
lies in the image of the map
\delta_{m+1}
.

The proof of this theorem relies on Lemma 2.8 and Proposition 2.9 from loc.cit. Both of these statements were formulated for arbitrary
m
. The proof of Theorem 2.6. from loc.cit. is quite formal and does not use any specific properties of the case
m=2
and can be easily generalised to arbitrary
m
.

6.3.
Parshin reciprocity law

We need the following statement:

Theorem 6.11
.

Let
L\in\mathbf{Fields}_{2}
and
j\in\{m+1,m+2\}

\sum\limits_{\mu^{\prime}\in\val(\overline{L}_{\mu})}\ts_{\mu^{\prime}}\ts_{\mu}(b)=0.

\sum\limits_{\mu\in\dval(L)}\sum\limits_{\mu^{\prime}\in\val(\overline{L}_{\mu})}\ts_{\mu^{\prime}}\ts_{\mu}(b)=0.

The proof of this theorem is completely similar to the proof of Theorem 2.10 from

]
.

6.4.
The proof of Theorem
6.2

Denote by
Set
the category of sets.
Define a contravariant functor

\mathrm{RecMaps}\colon\mathbf{Fields}_{1}\to\textbf{Set}

as follows. For any
F\in\mathbf{Fields}_{1}
the set
\mathrm{RecMaps}(F)
is equal to the set of all lifted reciprocity maps on
F
. If
j\colon F_{1}\hookrightarrow F_{2}
then
\mathrm{RecMaps}(j)(h_{F_{2}})=h_{F_{1}}
,where
h_{F_{1}}(\alpha_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\alpha_{m+1}):=\dfrac{1}{\deg j}h_{F_{2}}(j(\alpha_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge j(\alpha_{m+1}))
. The fact that in this way we get a functor can be proved similarly to
[
Bol23
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Let
F\in\mathbf{Fields}_{1}
,
\nu\in\dval(F(t))
. Denote the field
F(t)
by
L
. Our goal is to construct the map
\mathcal{N}_{\nu}\colon\mathrm{RecMaps}(F)\to\mathrm{RecMaps}(\overline{L}_{\nu})
. We will do this in the following three steps:

(1)

We will define this map when
\nu
is a special valuation.

(2)

Using the construction of the lift from Section
6.2
, for any general valuation
\nu\in\dval(L)
, we will define a map

\mathcal{N}_{\nu}\colon\mathrm{RecMaps}(F)\to Hom(\Gamma(\overline{L}_{\nu},m+1)_{m+1}\to\Gamma(K,m)_{m-1}/\im(\delta_{m})).

(3)

Using Theorem
6.11
from Section
6.3
, we will show that for any
h\in\mathrm{RecMaps}(F)
, the map
\mathcal{N}_{\nu}(h)
is a lifted reciprocity map on the field
\overline{L}_{\nu}
. So,
\mathcal{N}_{\nu}
gives a map
\mathrm{RecMaps}(F)\to\mathrm{RecMaps}(\overline{L}_{\nu})
.

Denote by
\nu_{\infty,F}\in\dval(F(t))
the discrete valuation corresponding to the point
\infty\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{F}
. Let
\nu
be special. If
\nu=\nu_{\infty,F}
then define
\mathcal{N}_{\nu}(h)=h
(here we have used the identification of
\overline{L}_{\nu_{\infty,F}}
with
F
). In the other case we have
\overline{L}_{\nu}\simeq K(t)
(see Remark
6.8
). In this case define
\mathcal{N}_{\nu}(h)
to be the unique lifted reciprocity map from Proposition
6.5
. We have defined
\mathcal{N}_{\nu}
for any
\nu\in\dval(L)_{sp}
.

Let
h\in\mathrm{RecMaps}(F)
. Define a map
H_{h}\colon\Lambda^{m+2}L^{\times}\to\Gamma(K,m)_{m-1}/\im(\delta_{m})
by the following formula:

H_{h}(b)=-\sum\limits_{\mu\in\dval(L)_{sp}}\mathcal{N}_{\mu}(h)(\ts_{\mu}(b)).

This sum is well-defined by Corollary
6.14
.

Definition 6.15
.

Let
\nu\in\dval(L)_{gen}
. Define a map

\mathcal{N}_{\nu}\colon\mathrm{RecMaps}(F)\to\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{m+1}\overline{L}_{\nu}^{\times},\Gamma(K,m)_{m-1}/\im(\delta_{m}))

as follows. Let

Similarly to Section 3.1 of loc.cit. it can be shown that this definition is well-defined and gives a map

We omit details.

Let

\mathbf{Fields}_{1}

be some generator of

over

The map

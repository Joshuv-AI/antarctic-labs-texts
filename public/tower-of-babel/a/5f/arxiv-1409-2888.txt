Extended Møller-Plesset perturbation theory for dynamical and static correlations
Takashi Tsuchimochi, Troy Van Voorhis

Abstract
We present a novel method that appropriately handles both dynamical and static electron correlation in a balanced manner, using a perturbation theory on a spin-extended Hartree-Fock (EHF) wave function reference. While EHF is a suitable candidate for degenerate systems where static correlation is ubiquitous, it is known that most of dynamical correlation is neglected in EHF. In this work, we derive a perturbative correction to a fully spin-projected self-consistent wave function based on second-order Møller-Plesset perturbation theory (MP2). The proposed method efficiently captures the ability of EHF to describe static correlation in degeneracy, combined with MP2’s ability to treat dynamical correlation effects. We demonstrate drastic improvements on molecular ground state and excited state potential energy curves and singlet-triplet splitting energies over both EHF and MP2 with similar computational effort to the latter.



Introduction.

An efficient and accurate treatment of both dynamical and static electron correlation effects has been elusive in electronic structure theory. Single reference methods such as second-order Møller-Plesset perturbation theory (MP2) and coupled-cluster singles and doubles (CCSD) achieve high accuracy in computed observables for non-degenerate systems,
Bartlett07
but it is well known that they cannot describe static correlation in degenerate systems. This failure is undoubtedly attributed to the reference wave function: Hartree-Fock (HF). A HF reference is qualitatively inadequate for (nearly-) degenerate systems where the true wave function is multi-determinantal in nature. Complete active space self-consistent field (CASSCF) resolves this problem by treating all the configurations in an active space, yielding a multi-reference state, and usually represents a good starting point when an appropriate active space is chosen. When the residual dynamical correlation is included through a perturbative correction
Hirao92
;
Andersson94
or configuration interaction (CI), CASSCF can achieve very accurate results both for the ground state and excited states. However, none of these are black-box, and their computational cost is very expensive.

Yet another approach to tackling static correlation may be spin-extended HF (EHF),
Lowdin55
;
Mayer80
which is also called spin-projected HF. The idea behind it is to optimize orbitals of a broken symmetry Slater determinant
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
, called a
deformed
state, projected by a spin-projection operator
\hat{P}
so that the total energy of the projected state,

\displaystyle E_{\rm EHF}=\frac{\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{P}^{{\dagger}}\hat{H}\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle}{\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{P}^{{\dagger}}\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle}=\frac{\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{H}\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle}{\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle},

(1)

is variationally minimized. This approach in particular is called variation-after-projection (VAP), not to be confused with projection-after-variation (PAV), which has been widely used in quantum chemistry.
\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle
spans a large part of the Hilbert space, and thus is expected to capture most of static correlation in a black-box manner, i.e., no active space is required. At the same time, for this reason, it has long been thought in quantum chemistry that the full spin-projection is computationally demanding and horribly complicated, even for PAV. Recently, Jim
\rm\acute{e}
nez-Hoyos
et al.
,
Jimenez12
however, have shown a feasible and clear way to accomplish VAP by using the spin-projection operator of the general integral form,

\displaystyle{\hat{P}}^{s}_{mk}=|s;m\rangle\langle s;k|=\frac{2s+1}{8\pi^{2}}\int D_{mk}^{s*}(\Omega)\hat{R}(\Omega)d\Omega,

(2)

instead of the famous L
{\rm\ddot{o}}
wdin projector.
Lowdin55

Here
s
is the total spin,
m
and
k
are the spin angular momentum,
D_{mk}^{s}(\Omega)=\langle s;m|{\hat{R}}(\Omega)|s;k\rangle
is the Wigner matrix, and
\hat{R}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=e^{i\alpha\hat{S}_{z}}e^{i\beta\hat{S}_{y}}e^{i\gamma\hat{S}_{z}}
is a unitary rotation operator. With this formalism, the computational effort for EHF is known to be similar to that of mean-field methods.

Although EHF efficiently describes static correlation, it neglects a vast amount of dynamical correlation, which is necessary for chemical accuracy. In order to remedy this, there have been recently extensive work attempting to incorporate the residual dynamical correlation into EHF, in the context of density functional correlation
Garza13
as well as non-orthogonal CI,
Rodriguez13
with promising results. In this work, we propose a perturbative approach based on MP2, which we shall hereby term extended MP2 (EMP2). Since MP2 correlation is almost exclusively of dynamical character, EMP2 should provide a seamless description of both static and dynamical correlation effects. A similar idea was pursued for PAV in the late 1980s,
Schlegel86
;
Knowles88A
but was immediately abandoned due to its enormous computational cost even for approximate projection and many undesired features such as pronounced derivative discontinuities in potential energy surfaces. Below we show that, with the present scheme, all of these obstacles can be thoroughly resolved with full spin-projection.

Theory.

Throughout this Communication, we restrict ourselves to the cases where
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
is an eigenstate of
{\hat{S}}_{z}
but not of
{\hat{S}}^{2}
, i.e. an unrestricted HF type determinant, and thus
\hat{P}=\hat{P}_{mm}^{s}
. We will also adopt the conventional notations of orbital indices:
i,j,k,l
for occupied,
a,b,c,d
for virtual, and
p,q,r,s
for all orbitals.

Perturbation approaches for projected wave functions have been proposed by many others.
Amos70
;
Peierls73
;
Knowles88A
Here we will derive our own scheme. We start by partitioning the Hamiltonian into
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\lambda\hat{V}
, such that

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}|\Phi_{0}\rangle=E_{0}|\Phi_{0}\rangle.

(3)

We remind the reader that
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
is the broken symmetry deformed state.
Given the Sch
{\rm\ddot{o}}
dinger equation,

\displaystyle\hat{H}|\Psi\rangle={\cal E}|\Psi\rangle,

(4)

\cal E
and
|\Psi\rangle
are expanded around
E_{0}
and
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
to find
n
-th order energies and wave functions,
E_{n}
and
|\Phi_{n}\rangle
. The MP2 energy expression then becomes

\displaystyle E^{(0)}_{\rm MP2}=\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{H}|\Phi_{0}+\Phi_{1}\rangle=E_{\rm HF}^{(0)}+E_{2}^{(0)}

(5)

where
E_{\rm HF}^{(0)}
and
E_{2}^{(0)}
are the HF energy and the second order perturbation correlation energy of the deformed state, and
|\Phi_{1}\rangle
is the first order wave function, which we will define later for our case. Note that we have not yet defined
\hat{H}_{0}
. Nevertheless, it is an independent particle symmetry broken Hamiltonian, and
|\Phi_{1}\rangle
consists of up to doubly excited determinants from
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
.

Here our goal is to derive a perturbation theory that begins with
|\Psi_{\rm EHF}\rangle\equiv\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle
and accomplishes the exact energy at the infinite order limit. Because
|\Psi_{\rm EHF}\rangle
has no well-defined independent particle Hamiltonian, however, one faces the difficulty of defining an appropriate zeroth order Hamiltonian. It should be clear that
\hat{H}_{0}
defined in Eq.(
3
) is not suitable, as
|\Psi_{\rm EHF}\rangle
is
not
its eigenstate. Hence,
we consider the expansion of
\cal E
and
|\Psi\rangle
in the projected space around
|\Psi_{\rm EHF}\rangle
. In the present scheme, our expansion for the wave function is based on the MP partitioning, given by

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle+\lambda\hat{P}|\Phi_{1}\rangle+\cdots.

(6)

This is possible because the exact wave function can always be chosen as an eigenstate of
\hat{P}
, i.e.,
\hat{P}|\Psi\rangle=\Lambda|\Psi\rangle
. Note that the spaces spanned by
\hat{P}|\Phi_{n}\rangle
are not orthogonal one another, and are necessarily overcomplete.
Amos70
;
Peierls73

Eq. (
6
) allows us to write the exact energy in the intermediate normalization,

\displaystyle{\cal E}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\Psi_{\rm EHF}|\hat{H}\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}+\lambda\Phi_{1}+\cdots\rangle}{\langle\Psi_{\rm EHF}|\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}+\lambda\Phi_{1}+\cdots\rangle}

(7)

\displaystyle=E_{\rm EHF}+\lambda{\cal E}_{2}+\lambda^{2}{\cal E}_{3}\cdots,

(8)

which achieves our goal, i.e.,
{\cal E}(0)=E_{\rm EHF}
and
{\cal E}(1)={\cal E}
. All the perturbative information is then carried in Eq.(
6
) and one is free from defining a zeroth order Hamiltonian.
{\cal E}_{2}
parallels second order Rayleigh-Schr
{\rm\ddot{o}}
dinger perturbation theory. Thus, we refer to it as the second order energy.
Expanding
{\cal E}(\lambda)
around
\lambda_{0}=0
and setting
\lambda=1
, we find

\displaystyle E_{\rm EMP2}=E_{\rm EHF}+{\cal E}_{2},

(9)

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{2}=\frac{\langle\Phi_{0}|(\hat{H}-E_{\rm EHF})\hat{P}|\Phi_{1}\rangle}{\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle}.

(10)

This formalism has various desired features. First, each term is rigorously defined by the magnitude of order parameter
\lambda
.
Second, there is no singles contribution from
|\Phi_{1}\rangle
due to the generalized Brillouin theorem,

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{0}|(\hat{H}-E_{\rm EHF})\hat{P}a_{a}^{\dagger}a_{i}|\Phi_{0}\rangle=0,

(11)

when the EHF state is stationary, similar to the property in the conventional MP
n
theory.
1
1

1

In fact, Eq.(
11
) is the EHF Fock matrix element defined as
\partial E_{\rm EHF}/\partial P^{(0)}_{ia}
with normalization
\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle
.
Last, and perhaps most importantly, the perturbation series are spin-projected at all orders, including
|\Phi_{1}\rangle
. In fact, it can be shown that Eq.(
10
) may be seen as the fully spin-projected MP2 if
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
is the stationary unrestricted HF state. That is, by defining a projector onto the complementary space orthogonal to
|\Psi_{\rm EHF}\rangle
,

\displaystyle\hat{O}=1-\frac{\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{P}}{\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle}=1-\frac{|\Psi_{\rm EHF}\rangle\langle\Psi_{\rm EHF}|}{\langle\Psi_{\rm EHF}|\Psi_{\rm EHF}\rangle},

(12)

Eqs.(
9
-
10
) are elegantly rewritten as

\displaystyle E_{\rm EMP2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{H}\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}+\hat{O}\Phi_{1}\rangle}{\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{P}|\Phi_{0}\rangle}.

(13)

This clearly indicates that the last term in the numerator of Eq.(
13
) lives in the space orthogonal to
|\Psi_{\rm EHF}\rangle
to eliminate double-counting of correlation effects, and is subject to spin-projection. We also note that the occurrence of double-counting is a natural consequence because, again, the basis Eq.(
6
) is overcomplete.

Now we shall move our attention to the definition of
{\hat{H}}_{0}
and thus
|\Phi_{1}\rangle
for EMP2. The performance of a perturbation theory critically depends on
{\hat{H}}_{0}
.
Since we rely on the MP expansion of the deformed state, i.e. Eq.(
6
),
a physical choice for
\hat{H}_{0}
is given by HF-like orbital energies evaluated from
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
. They are indeed an appropriate candidate in view of Eq.(
13
): EMP2 may be regarded as the full spin-projection of broken-symmetry MP2 for some special case. Also, this scheme is guaranteed to reduce to the regular MP2 when
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
is already a spin-eigenstate or
\hat{P}=1
, providing a seamless connection.

At the stationary state,
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
is not an eigenfunction of a sum of Fock operators, which is
\hat{H}_{0}
of the conventional MP2.
However,
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
is still a Slater determinant, and
|\Psi_{\rm EHF}\rangle
is invariant with respect to a unitary rotation among
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
. Hence, we diagonalize the occupied-occupied (
oo
) block and virtual-virtual (
vv
) block of the deformed Fock matrix, defined as

\displaystyle F^{(0)}_{pq}=h^{(0)}_{pq}+\sum_{rs}P^{(0)}_{rs}\langle pr||qs\rangle,

(14)

with the deformed density matrix
P^{(0)}_{rs}=\langle\Phi_{0}|a_{s}^{\dagger}a_{r}|\Phi_{0}\rangle
, and choose
\hat{H}_{0}=\sum_{p}\varepsilon_{p}a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{p}
with
\varepsilon_{p}=F^{(0)}_{pp}
. The orbital basis of this particular choice has been referred to as semi-canonical orbitals in literature.
Consequently, we will have not only doubles but also, potentially, singles contributions in
|\Phi_{1}\rangle
,

\displaystyle|\Phi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{ia}|\Phi_{i}^{a}\rangle\frac{F^{(0)}_{ia}}{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{a}}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{ijab}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle\frac{\langle ij||ab\rangle}{\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j}-\varepsilon_{a}-\varepsilon_{b}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ia}t_{i}^{a}|\Phi_{i}^{a}\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{ijab}t_{ij}^{ab}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle,

(15)

because
F_{ia}^{(0)}
are nonzero in general. As mentioned above, however, all the singles contribution strictly vanish through
\hat{P}
due to the generalized Brillouin theorem, Eq.(
11
). Thus we only require the second term.

Finally, we discuss how one evaluates the projected coupling terms
\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{H}\hat{P}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle
and
\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{P}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle
that appear in Eq.(
10
). In practice, each term can be decomposed to a discretized grid integration as

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{H}\hat{P}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}^{N_{\rm grid}}w_{g}\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{H}\hat{R}_{g}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle,

(16)

where
w_{g}
are the grid weights and
\hat{R}_{g}
is the rotation operator defined earlier but for each grid point
g
.
Jimenez12
The brute-force calculation of this term with the generalized Wick theorem
RingSchuck
would require
{\cal O}(N^{4})
for each matrix element and therefore it gives rise to a total complexity of
{\cal O}(N^{8}N_{\rm grid})
for all the double substitutions, which is intractable. To ameliorate the computational effort, we will take a couple of steps.

Inserting the identity operator,
1=|\Phi_{0}\rangle+\sum_{kc}|\Phi_{k}^{c}\rangle+\cdots
, between
{\hat{H}}
and
\hat{R}_{g}
in Eq. (
16
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{H}\hat{R}_{g}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle=

\displaystyle E_{\rm HF}^{(0)}\langle\Phi_{0}|\hat{R}_{g}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle+\sum_{kc}F^{(0)}_{kc}\langle\Phi_{k}^{c}|\hat{R}_{g}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{klcd}\langle kl||cd\rangle\langle\Phi_{kl}^{cd}|\hat{R}_{g}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle.

(17)

In this way, only the rotation couplings
\langle\Phi_{kl}^{cd}|\hat{R}_{g}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle
, etc, are to be evaluated.

Here, the important realization is that
\hat{R}_{g}
will
not
mix orbitals with one another, but instead independently rotate each spin orbital to give general spin orbitals (i.e.,
\alpha
and
\beta
spins are mixed). Therefore, the
{\hat{R}}_{g}
rotation on an excited determinant
|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle=a_{a}^{\dagger}a_{b}^{\dagger}a_{j}a_{i}|\Phi_{0}\rangle
is
identical
to the corresponding excitation of the rotated determinant
|{{}^{g}\Phi}\rangle\equiv\hat{R}_{g}|\Phi_{0}\rangle
,

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{g}|\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle=c_{a}^{\dagger}c_{b}^{\dagger}c_{j}c_{i}|{{}^{g}\Phi}\rangle=|^{g}\Phi_{ij}^{ab}\rangle,

(18)

where
c_{p}^{\dagger}
and
c_{p}
are the rotated creation and annihilation operators,
c_{p}^{\dagger}=\hat{R}_{g}a^{\dagger}_{p}\hat{R}_{g}^{\dagger}
, etc, and
{}^{g}|\Phi\rangle=\prod_{i}c_{i}^{\dagger}|-\rangle
with
|-\rangle
being the bare vacuum. Then, the rotation couplings in Eq.(
17
) are realized as just the overlaps between excited non-orthogonal general HF (GHF) determinants.

This fact is particularly useful for our purpose, because all the simplicities in HF determinants are still available for
|^{g}\Phi\rangle
. Among the most important ones is the corresponding pair theorem.
Amos61
One can biorthogonalize the orbitals of
|\Phi_{0}\rangle
and
|{{}^{g}\Phi}\rangle
,
|p\rangle
and
|{{}^{g}q}\rangle
, by performing a singular value decomposition of the
oo
and
vv
blocks of the overlap matrix
{{}^{g}S}_{pq}=\langle p|{{}^{g}q}\rangle
. By the aforementioned theorem, which of course holds for GHF determinants, the resulting
{{}^{g}\bf S}
matrix in the corresponding orbital basis is banded: not only the
oo
and
vv
blocks but also the
ov
and
vo
blocks can be chosen to be diagonal. This greatly simplifies the overlap evaluation
Yost13
and makes it possible to retain only
{\cal O}(N^{4})
cost for the contraction of Eq.(
17
), using the significant sparsity of
\langle\Phi_{kl}^{cd}|{{}^{g}\Phi_{ij}^{ab}}\rangle
with a very simple algorithm. The limiting step of EMP2 is thus the computation and transformation of
t
amplitudes as well as two electron integrals as in the regular MP2, which scales as
{\cal O}(N^{5})
. Note that the final energy is invariant with respect to these orbital rotations.

.
We have implemented Eq.(
10
) in our in-house quantum chemistry program with the proposed contraction scheme. All the calculations were done with a 6-31G basis to enable the direct comparison with the exact full CI (FCI) results, except the hydrogen molecule. In the top panel of Figure
1
, we depict the potential energy curve of H
2

As is well known, the MP2 energy is accurate in the short range where a tremendous amount of dynamical correlation is required, but it completely fails when a bond is stretched, due to its inability to describe static correlation. It is evident that the almost opposite event is observed in EHF. It dissociates H
2
exactly, being less accurate in the vicinity of the equilibrium bond length, R
e
. As one would expect, EMP2 eliminates these disadvantages. It gives even slightly better energies than MP2 near R
e
where static correlation is considered negligible, while it starts to gain static correlation seamlessly toward the dissociation limit. Overall, the potential curve of EMP2 is in excellent agreement with FCI; the mean absolute error (MAE) is only 1.1 kcal/mol. These behaviors of correlation effects can be seen generically. The bottom panel of Figure
1
shows the deviation of the total energy from FCI in the hydrogen fluoride molecule dissociation. Again, the conventional MP2 becomes notoriously worse after R

Å due to the degeneracy appearing. While the EHF error is mostly flat and goes to the correct dissociation limit (although not size-consistent

We close our discussions by stressing once again that the method presented here achieves a black-box treatment of accurate dynamical and static correlation with a moderate computational effort similar to the conventional MP2.

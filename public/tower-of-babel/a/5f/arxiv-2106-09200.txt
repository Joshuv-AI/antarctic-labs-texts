Abstract
. In this paper we study the Lascar group over a hyperimaginary 𝒆 \boldsymbol{e} . We verify that various results about the group over a real set still hold when the set is replaced by 𝒆 \boldsymbol{e} . First of all, there is no written proof in the available literature that the group over 𝒆 \boldsymbol{e} is a topological group. We present an expository style proof of the fact, which even simplifies existing proofs for the real case. We further extend a result that the orbit equivalence relation under a closed subgroup of the Lascar group is type-definable. On the one hand, we correct errors appeared in [ 6 , 5.1.14-15] and produce a counterexample. On the other, we extend Newelski’s Theorem in [ 12 ] that ‘a G-compact theory over a set has a uniform bound for the Lascar distances’ to the hyperimaginary context. Lastly, we supply a partial positive answer to a question raised in [ 4 , 2.11] , which is even a new result in the real context.

The Lascar (automorphism) group of a first-order complete theory and its quotient groups such as the Kim-Pillay group and the Shelah group have been central themes in contemporary model theory. The study on those groups enables us to develop Galois theoretic correspondence between the groups and their orbit-equivalence relations on a monster model such as Lascar types, Kim-Pillay types, and Shelah strong types. The notions of the Lascar group and its topology are introduced first by D. Lascar in
[
9
]
using ultraproducts. Later more favorable equivalent definition is suggested in
[
7
]
and
[
11
]
, which is nowadays considered as a standard approach. However even a complete proof using the approach of the fundamental fact that the Lascar group is a topological group is not so well available.
For example in
[
2
]
, its proof is left to the readers, while the proof is not at all trivial. As far as we can see, only in
[
14
]
, a detailed proof is written.

Aforementioned results are for the Lascar group over
\emptyset
, or more generally over a real set
A
.
In this paper we study the Lascar group over a hyperimaginary
\boldsymbol{e}
and verify how results on the Lascar group over
A
can be extended to the case when the set is replaced by
\boldsymbol{e}
. Indeed this attempt was made in
[
8
]
(and rewritten in
[
6
, §5.1]
). However those contain some errors, and moreover a proof of that the Lascar group over
\boldsymbol{e}
is a topological group is also missing.
In this paper we supply a proof of the fact in a detailed expository manner. Our proof is more direct and even simplifies that for the group over
\emptyset
in
[
14
]
. We correct the mentioned errors in
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
, as well. In particular we correct the proof of that the orbit equivalence relation under a closed normal subgroup of the Lascar group over
\boldsymbol{e}
is type-definable over
\boldsymbol{e}
. Moreover we extend Newelski’s Theorem in
[
12
]
to the hyperimaginary context. Namely we show that if
T
is G-compact over
\boldsymbol{e}
then there is

such that for any hyperimaginaries
\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c}

\boldsymbol{b}\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}\boldsymbol{c}
iff the Lascar distance between
\boldsymbol{b}
and

is

We also generalize the notions of
relativized
Lascar groups introduced in
[
3
]

Lastly, we prove a new result even in the real context, which is a partial positive answer to a question raised in

By

Being ‘type-definable’ means being ‘type-definable over some real set.’
When

As is well-known, an

Recall that a

\emptyset

Occasionally we may write hyperimaginaries using boldface letters such as

The

\exists z_{1}z_{2}(\operatorname{tp}_{z_{1}z_{2}}(bc)\wedge F(x,z_{1})\wedge L(c,z_{2}))

over

\operatorname{tp}(b_{F}/c_{L})=\operatorname{tp}(d_{F}/c_{L})
.
We say

Real or imaginary tuples in

Notice that a sequence of hyperimaginaries

For the rest of this paper, we fix

, an
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation,

For simplicity we may write a formula in a type, say

of

, respectively. In this section we show that the Lascar group over
\boldsymbol{e}

).

without a proof. But we cannot find any stated proof in the literature showing that the proof
for a real parameter (as in

]

2.
Type-definability of orbit equivalence relations

Given a subgroup
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})
, we write
x\equiv^{H}_{\boldsymbol{e}}y
for
|x|=|y|=\alpha
to denote the orbit equivalence relation
on
{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}
under
H
, that is for
b,c\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}
,
b\equiv^{H}_{\boldsymbol{e}}c
iff there is
h\in H
such that
c=h(b)
. More generally if
F
is an
\emptyset
-type-definable
equivalence relation on
{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}
, then
x_{F}\equiv^{H}_{\boldsymbol{e}}y_{F}
is the orbit equivalence relation on
{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}/F
under
H
, which is coarser than
F
.

In this section we aim to show that
given closed
H^{\prime}\leq\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(T,\boldsymbol{e})
and
H=\pi^{-1}(H^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})
,

x_{F}\equiv^{H}_{\boldsymbol{e}}y_{F}
is a type-definable bounded equivalence relation; and in addition if

H^{\prime}\unlhd\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(T,\boldsymbol{e})
then
x_{F}\equiv^{H}_{\boldsymbol{e}}y_{F}
is
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant (Corollary
2.4
).
This result is claimed to be proved in
[
8
]
(restated in
[
6
, Lemma 5.1.6(1)]
) but the proof contains an error: There for each complete hyperimaginary type
p
over a hyperimaginary,
\Psi_{p}(x,y)
type-defines a bounded equivalence relation on the solution set of
p
, but the description after this there need not work to extend
\Psi_{p}(x,y)
to the whole monster model keeping it type-definable and bounded.

When
\boldsymbol{e}=\emptyset
and

H^{\prime}=\overline{\{\operatorname{id}\}}\unlhd\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(T)
, a correct proof using an ultraproduct argument is given in
[
11
, Lemma 4.18]
.
Here we supply a direct proof (not appealing to the ultraproduct method) of the general case result while we still utilize ideas in
[
11
]
.

We recall the following folklore first.

Fact 2.1
.

Let
F
be a type-definable equivalence relation on
{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}
which is
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant. Then there is an
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation

F^{\prime}
such that for any
b\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}
,

b_{F}
and
ba/F^{\prime}
are interdefinable over
\boldsymbol{e}
.

Proof.

Since
F
is
\boldsymbol{e}(=a/E)
-invariant,
F
is type-definable over
a
by say
F(x,y;a)
. Then for
p(x)=\operatorname{tp}(a)
, we put

F^{\prime}(xz,yw)\equiv(F(x,y;z)\wedge E(z,w)\wedge p(z)\wedge p(w))\vee xz=yw.

It is not hard to check that
F^{\prime}
satisfies the statement.
∎

The equivalence of (1) and (3) of the following proposition is proved in
[
11
, Lemma 1.9]
when
\boldsymbol{e}=\emptyset
.
The argument is essentially the same, but modifications are made to handle the hyperimaginary parameter.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
b
be any small tuple in
{\mathcal{M}}
, and let
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})
.
The following are equivalent.

(1)

H=\operatorname{Aut}_{b_{F}\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})
for some
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant type-definable equivalence relation
F
.

(2)

H=\operatorname{Aut}_{(ba/L)\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})
for some
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation
L

such that
ba/L\in\operatorname{dcl}(b\boldsymbol{e})
.

(3)

\operatorname{Aut}_{b\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})\leq H
and the orbit of
b
under
H
is type-definable.

Proof.

Let
X
be the orbit of
b
under
H
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): This follows from Fact
2.1
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3): Since
ba/L\in\operatorname{dcl}(b\boldsymbol{e})
, we have

\operatorname{Aut}_{b\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})\leq H
. Moreover
X
is type-defined by

\exists y(L(xy,ba)\wedge xy\equiv_{\boldsymbol{e}}ba).

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1): Since
X
is invariant under
H
, it is also invariant under
\operatorname{Aut}_{b\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})
, so type-definable by some
b\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant partial type
\Phi(x,b,a)
(*) where
\Phi(x,y,z)\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}
.
We define

F^{*}(x,y)\equiv\exists z(\Phi(x,y,z)\wedge\operatorname{tp}_{x}(b/\boldsymbol{e})\wedge\operatorname{tp}_{yz}(ba/\boldsymbol{e})).

Claim
.

F^{*}(x,y)
is an equivalence relation on the solution set of
\operatorname{tp}(b/\boldsymbol{e})
.

Proof of Claim.

Let
d\equiv_{\boldsymbol{e}}b
be given, hence
d=f(b)
for some
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})
.
Then
\vDash\Phi(b,b,a)
implies
\vDash\Phi(d,d,f(a))
and so
F^{*}(d,d)
holds.

To show symmetry, assume
F^{*}(c,d)
holds for some
c,d\models\operatorname{tp}(b/\boldsymbol{e})
. Thus there is

a^{\prime}
such that
da^{\prime}\equiv_{\boldsymbol{e}}ba
and
\Phi(c,d,a^{\prime})
holds. Hence there also is

c^{\prime}
such that

cd\equiv_{\boldsymbol{e}}c^{\prime}b
, and for some
g\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})
we have

g(cda^{\prime})=c^{\prime}ba^{\prime\prime}
and
\Phi(c^{\prime},b,a^{\prime\prime})
holds. Note that
ba\equiv_{\boldsymbol{e}}ba^{\prime\prime}
and as said in (*) above,
\Phi(x,b,a^{\prime\prime})
also type-defines
X
. Hence
c^{\prime}\in X
and there is
f\in H
such that
c^{\prime}=f(b)
. Note that

f^{-1}(b)\in X
as well and
\Phi(f^{-1}(b),b,a^{\prime\prime})
holds. Hence by applying
g^{-1}.f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})
we have

\Phi(d,c,a^{\prime\prime\prime})
where
a^{\prime\prime\prime}=g^{-1}.f(a^{\prime\prime})
and
ca^{\prime\prime\prime}\equiv_{\boldsymbol{e}}ba
. Therefore we have
F^{*}(d,c)
.

For transitivity, assume
F^{*}(u,v)
and
F^{*}(v,w)
. Hence there are
a_{0},a_{1}
such that
va_{0}\equiv_{\boldsymbol{e}}ba\equiv_{\boldsymbol{e}}wa_{1}
and

\Phi(u,v,a_{0})

u^{\prime}v^{\prime}a_{2}

.
Hence

and

. Thus there is

, and we have

\Phi(u^{\prime\prime},b,a_{3})

,

. Notice that
ba_{3}\equiv_{\boldsymbol{e}}v^{\prime}a_{2}\equiv_{\boldsymbol{e}}ba
, and hence again by (*), we have

. Therefore there is
k\in H
such that
k(b)=u^{\prime\prime}=h^{-1}(u^{\prime})
, and

. Hence
\Phi(u^{\prime},b,a)
, and

Thus

follows because

.
∎

Define

, so that

is a type-definable equivalence relation on

.
Notice that
F
is
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant. It remains to show

If
h\in H
, then
h(b)\in X
and
\Phi(h(b),b,a)
holds. Thus
F(h(b),b)
.
Conversely, assume

.
Then
F(h(b),b)
, so
F^{*}(h(b),b)
and
\Phi(h(b),b,a^{\prime})
hold where

.
Hence again by (*), we have

and there is

such that

.
Then

since

. Therefore

.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})
and let

.
Then
H^{\prime}
is closed in
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(T,\boldsymbol{e})

and

, if and only if

for some hyperimaginary
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}({\boldsymbol{e}})
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
:
Recall that (before Proposition
1.11
) we have fixed
M\models T
such that
a\in M
.
Note that

\operatorname{Aut}_{M}({\mathcal{M}})=\operatorname{Aut}_{M\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})\leq\operatorname{Autf}_{\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}})\leq H
, and since

is type-definable over

.
Thus by Proposition

for some

-type-definable equivalence relation

.

It remains to show that

Notice that due to Corollary

[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}}):H]=\lambda

(\Leftarrow)

-invariant bounded equivalence relation on

(2) with real

of arity

.
Thus
\pi^{-1}(H^{\prime})=H
.
Moreover,
H=\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}({\mathcal{M}}):f(a^{\prime})\models E^{\prime}(x^{\prime},a^{\prime})\}

, which is closed.

\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})
, and hence

is

\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant type-definable bounded equivalence relation.
Especially, if
H^{\prime}\unlhd\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(T,\boldsymbol{e})
, then
x_{F}\equiv^{H}_{\boldsymbol{e}}y_{F}
is
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant.

Proof.

Due to Proposition

Proposition 3.1

We now define and characterize the KP(Kim-Pillay)-type over a hyperimaginary.

Definition 3.3
.

(1)

Denote

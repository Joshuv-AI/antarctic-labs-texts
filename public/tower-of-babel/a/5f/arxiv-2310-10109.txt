Instability of conformally Kähler, Einstein metrics
Olivier Biquard and Tristan Ozuch

Abstract
. We prove the instability of conformally Kähler, compact or ALF Einstein 4 4 -manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature which are not half conformally flat. This applies to all the known examples of gravitational instantons which are not hyperKähler and to the Chen-Lebrun-Weber metric in particular.



Introduction

Einstein metrics are considered
optimal
in Riemannian geometry, especially in dimension
4
. Their
stability
in physics or as fixed points of Ricci flow is a crucial question: one should be able to perturb away from unstable metrics. In both contexts, the
stability
is defined as being a local maximum of the Hilbert-Einstein functional
\mathscr{S}:g\mapsto\int_{M}\Scal^{g}dv_{g}
, or Perelman’s functionals–which are equivalent at second perturbative order at Einstein metrics.

For Ricci-flat metrics, special holonomy implies stability, and an open question is that of the converse, which is sometimes formulated as a conjecture, see
[
1
]
under more assumptions.

Question 1

(Folklore)
.

Are Ricci-flat metrics with
generic
holonomy
unstable
?

Stability is a subtle question which has mostly been studied on the most symmetric Einstein metrics, and instabilities have been detected numerically on some of the simplest Einstein metrics,
[
19
,
18
]
.

In this article, we tackle the question for essentially all known examples of compact or complete Einstein
4
-manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature at once. This is because, all known examples of Einstein metrics with nonnegative scalar curvature and curvature decay at infinity have a specific structure: they are
conformally Kähler
, or
Hermitian
. This condition is also called
type
D^{+}
in the physics literature by analogy with the Petrov classification in Lorentzian geometry.

Theorem A
.

Let
(M^{4},g)
be an Einstein manifold which is conformal to a Kähler metric. Suppose

•

(M,g)
is compact with positive scalar curvature, or

•

(M,g)
is one of the known examples of ALF gravitational instantons (see Section
3
).

Then if
(M^{4},g)
is not half-conformally flat, then it is
unstable
.

Note that the method of proof is abstract and uniform: destabilizing symmetric 2-tensors are produced as compositions
\omega_{-}\,\circ\,\tau
, where
\omega_{-}
is a harmonic anti-selfdual 2-form and
\tau
is a Killing selfdual 2-form, see Section
2
.

Except for
\mathbb{S}^{4}
and
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, the theorem applies to all the known Einstein metrics with positive scalar curvature, i.e. the metrics of Page, Chen-LeBrun-Weber and the Kähler-Einstein Fano manifolds; the new case here is the Chen-LeBrun-Weber metric. It also applies to all of the known so-called gravitational instantons which are not hyperKähler: the Riemannian Kerr (including Schwarzschild), Taub-Bolt and Chen-Teo metrics. This is likely to be the full list, see
[
2
]
,
[
16
]
for the ALE case

Delay

was the first to realize that Einstein stability can be checked in the conformal class. Elaborating on this idea, our proof relies on three main ingredients: a Weitzenböck formula from

We treat the conformal invariance in Section

2
. We finally deal with the ALF setting in Section

.

1.
Conformal invariance

Let
(M^{4},g)
be an oriented Riemannian manifold. Suppose
g
is Einstein:
\Ric_{g}=\Lambda g
. The linearization of the Einstein operator
\Ric-\Lambda
, acting on symmetric 2-tensors, is given by the formula

(1)

d\Ric-\Lambda=\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{*}\nabla-\operatorname{Rm}-\delta^{*}B,

where
B=\delta+\frac{1}{2}d\tr
is the Bianchi operator,
\delta^{*}
is the adjoint of the divergence, and
\operatorname{Rm}
is the natural action of the Riemannian curvature on symmetric 2-tensors. The last term is a gauge term, so one considers often the linearization with gauge fixing

(2)

L:=\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{*}\nabla-\operatorname{Rm}.

In dimension four we have the bundles of selfdual or anti-selfdual 2-forms
\Omega_{\pm}
, and a special isomorphism
\Omega_{-}\otimes\Omega_{+}=\Sym^{2}_{0}T^{*}M
, which is given by

(3)

\alpha^{-}\otimes\alpha^{+}\longmapsto\alpha^{-}\circ\alpha^{+},

identified with the composition of the anti-symmetric endomorphisms associated to the
2
-forms
\alpha^{\pm}
. Observe that by
[
5
, (4.6)]
, for an Einstein metric we have on trace-free symmetric 2-tensors:

(4)

L=d_{-}d_{-}^{*}-W_{+}-\frac{\Scal}{12},

where
d_{-}:\Omega^{1}\otimes\Omega_{+}\rightarrow\Omega_{-}\otimes\Omega_{+}
is the composition of the covariant exterior derivative on
\Omega^{1}\otimes\Omega_{+}
and the projection
\Omega^{2}\otimes\Omega_{+}\to\Omega^{-}\otimes\Omega_{+}
on the anti-selfdual part. The action of
W_{+}
is given by
\alpha^{-}\circ\alpha^{+}\mapsto\alpha^{-}\circ W_{+}(\alpha^{+})
.

Let us use the spinor notation: the basic spinor bundles are
S_{\pm}
(they exist at least locally), and their symmetric powers are
S_{\pm}^{k}
. Then
TM=S_{+}\otimes S_{-}
and
\Omega_{\pm}=S_{\pm}^{2}
.

For simplicity of notation we will now drop the tensor product signs. We have the decomposition

(5)

\Omega^{1}\Omega_{+}=(S_{+}S_{-})S_{+}^{2}=S_{+}^{3}S_{-}\oplus S_{+}S_{-}.

We can identify the second summand with
\Omega^{3}
and we have the projection

(6)

\pi:\Omega^{1}\Omega_{+}\rightarrow\Omega^{3},\qquad\pi(\alpha\otimes\omega)=\alpha\wedge\omega.

Conversely we have the embedding

(7)

\sigma:\Omega^{3}\hookrightarrow\Omega^{1}\Omega_{+},\qquad\sigma(\varphi)=\sum_{1}^{4}e^{i}\otimes(e_{i}\lrcorner\varphi)_{+},

where
(e_{i})
is an orthonormal basis of
TM
. One calculates immediately

(8)

\sigma^{*}=\pi,\qquad\pi\circ\sigma=\frac{3}{2}.

Now decompose
d_{-}^{*}
on the sum (
5
): call
T
the projection on the first summand, we claim that the decomposition writes:

Proposition 2
.

We have the formulas:

(9)

\displaystyle d_{-}^{*}

\displaystyle=T+\frac{2}{3}\sigma*\delta,

and

(10)

\displaystyle d_{-}d_{-}^{*}

\displaystyle=T^{*}T+\frac{2}{3}\delta_{0}^{*}\delta,

where
\delta_{0}^{*}
is the trace-free part of
\delta^{*}
.

Proof.

The first formula is a consequence of (
8
) and of
\delta=-*\pi\circ d_{-}^{*}
, which is left to the reader.

The second formula is a consequence of the first formula and of (
8
).
∎

It follows that the operator
L
from (
4
) can be rewritten as

(11)

L=T^{*}T-W_{+}+\frac{2}{3}\delta_{0}^{*}\delta-\frac{\Scal}{12}=P+\frac{2}{3}\delta_{0}^{*}\delta-\frac{\Scal}{12},

where we define the operator
P
on
\Sym_{0}^{2}T^{*}M
by

(12)

P=T^{*}T-W_{+}.

The interest of the operator
P
comes from:

Proposition 3
.

The operator
P
acting on
\Sym^{2}_{0}T^{*}M
has the conformal invariance

(13)

P^{f^{2}g}h=f^{-1}P^{g}(f^{-1}h).

Proof.

The operator
T:S_{+}^{2}S_{-}^{2}\rightarrow S_{+}^{3}S_{-}
is a gradient operator, and it is known that such operators are always conformally invariant
[
10
]
. In our case we can apply the formula
[
17
, (3.22)]
to obtain

(14)

T^{f^{2}g}=\sigma\circ f^{-2}\circ T^{g}\circ f\circ\sigma^{-1},

where the identification
\sigma
between bundles for
g
and
f^{2}g
is chosen to be isometric. It follows that:

(15)

\displaystyle(T^{*})^{f^{2}g}

\displaystyle=\sigma\circ f^{-3}\circ(T^{*})^{g}\circ f\circ\sigma^{-1},

(16)

\displaystyle(T^{*}T)^{f^{2}g}

\displaystyle=\sigma\circ f^{-3}\circ(T^{*}T)^{g}\circ f\circ\sigma^{-1}.

For symmetric 2-tensors, the abstract identification
\sigma
between bundles for
g
and
f^{2}g
is the isometric map
\sigma(h)=f^{2}h
, so in terms of a fixed section of
h
of
\Sym^{2}_{0}T^{*}M
(independent of the metric), the formula (
16
) reads

(17)

(T^{*}T)^{f^{2}g}h=f^{-1}(T^{*}T)^{g}(f^{-1}h).

On the other hand, for the Weyl tensor seen as an endomorphism of
\Sym^{2}_{0}T^{*}M
we have

(18)

W_{+}^{f^{2}g}(h)=f^{-2}W_{+}^{g}(h).

The proposition follows.
∎

Remark 4
.

Delay
[
8
]
proved the conformal invariance of
L-\frac{2}{3}\delta_{0}^{*}\delta-\frac{\Scal}{12}
. Here we proved the conformal invariance of
T^{*}T-W_{+}
. A priori we have seen that the two operators coincide for Einstein metrics, see equation (
11
). It turns out that the coincidence is more general, but we do not need it here.

2.
Instability of compact conformally Kähler metrics

The Hilbert-Einstein functional is defined as
\mathscr{S}:g\mapsto\int_{M}\Scal^{g}dv_{g}
, and its first variation at a compactly supported variation of the metric
k
is
-\int_{M}\langle E_{g},k\rangle_{g}dv_{g}
, where
E_{g}=\Ric-\frac{\Scal^{g}}{2}g
. It is the second variation of this scalar curvature that determines the stability of an Einstein metric. From
[
3
, Proposition 4.55]
, for a
compactly supported
and
traceless
deformation
k
, this second variation at an Einstein metric is

(19)

\mathscr{S}^{(2)}_{g}(k,k)=-\int_{M}\big\langle L_{g}k-\delta^{*}_{g}\delta_{g}k\,,\,k\big\rangle_{g}dv_{g}.

Definition 5
.

An Einstein metric
g
is
stable
if the second variation of
\mathscr{S}
at
g
is negative for
all
compactly supported traceless deformations. It is
unstable
otherwise.

We will apply this definition to the following setting. Let us consider
g
an Einstein metric that is conformal to a Kähler metric
\tilde{g}
through the function
g=f^{2}\tilde{g}
. From
[
9
, Prop. 5]
one has necessarily

(20)

f^{-1}=\frac{\Scal^{\tilde{g}}}{6}.

Also remember that
\frac{1}{6}\Scal^{\tilde{g}}
is the eigenvalue of
W_{+}^{\tilde{g}}
on the Kähler form
\tilde{\omega}^{+}
of
\tilde{g}
, and therefore from (
18
) the eigenvalue of
W_{+}^{g}
on
\tilde{\omega}^{+}
is
f^{-3}
.

Note that both in the case of a compact Einstein
(M,g)
with
\Scal^{g}>0
and in the noncompact case with
\Scal^{g}=0
one has necessarily
\Scal^{\tilde{g}}>0
.

Suppose that we also have a closed anti-selfdual 2-form
\omega^{-}
.
Then we define (for the metric
g
):

(21)

h:=\omega^{-}\,\circ\,f^{3}\tilde{\omega}^{+}.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose
(M^{4},g)
is compact, Einstein, conformally Kähler, with nonnegative scalar curvature. Then for any closed anti-selfdual form
\omega^{-}
the tensor
h
defined by (
21
) destabilizes
g
, that is

(22)

\mathscr{S}^{(2)}_{g}(h,h)<0.

In particular, we have at least
b_{-}
unstable directions.

The version of this result for
(M,g)
complete noncompact will be stated in Section
3
.

Example 7
.

A trivial example is that of a Kähler-Einstein Fano manifold
(M^{4},g)
: in that case the instability is well-known, see formula
[
3
, (12.92’)]
.

Example 8
.

The 4-dimensional, non Kähler but conformally Kähler, Einstein metrics were classified by LeBrun
[
14
]
: one gets only the Page metric on
\mathbb{C}P^{2}\#\overline{\mathbb{C}P^{2}}
and the Chen-LeBrun-Weber metric
[
6
]
on
\mathbb{C}P^{2}\#2\overline{\mathbb{C}P^{2}}
. So the theorem says that these two metrics are unstable for the Ricci flow and provides destabilizing tensors. The instability of the Page was proven, and that of the Chen-LeBrun-Weber metric was conjectured in
[
11
]
. Further numerical evidence of the instability of the Chen-Lebrun-Weber metric and an easier proof of the instability of the Page metric as a Ricci shrinker were provided in
[
12
,
13
]
studying conformal deformations.

Remark 9
.

The 2-form
\tau=f^{3}\tilde{\omega}^{+}
has a nice conformal interpretation: it is a Killing 2-form for
g
, that is it satisfies the equation

(23)

\mathscr{T}^{g}(\tau)_{X}:=\nabla_{X}\tau-\frac{1}{3}(X\lrcorner d\tau-X\wedge\delta\tau)=0.

This is a conformally invariant equation:
f^{-3}\mathscr{T}^{f^{2}g}(f^{3}\tau)=\mathscr{T}^{g}(\tau)
and therefore the solution
f^{3}\tilde{\omega}^{+}
for
g
corresponds to the trivial solution
\tilde{\omega}^{+}
for
\tilde{g}
.

Proof.

We begin by considering
\tilde{h}=\omega^{-}\,\tilde{\circ}\,\tilde{\omega}^{+}
for the Kähler metric
\tilde{g}
: since
\tilde{\omega}^{+}
is parallel and
d\omega^{-}=0
, we have
d_{-}^{*}\tilde{h}=0
and therefore

(24)

P^{\tilde{g}}\tilde{h}=-\omega^{-}\,\tilde{\circ}\,W_{+}^{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{\omega}^{+})=-\frac{\Scal^{\tilde{g}}}{6}\tilde{h}=-f^{-1}\tilde{h}.

Now observe that
h=f\tilde{h}
because
\tilde{\circ}=f^{2}\circ
. By the conformal invariance (
13
) we obtain

(25)

P^{g}h=-f^{-3}h.

From (
19
) and (
11
) we have

(26)

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}^{(2)}_{g}(h,h)

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}\big\langle Ph-\frac{1}{3}\delta^{*}\delta h-\frac{\Scal^{g}}{12}h,h\big\rangle dv_{g}

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{M}\big\langle Ph,h\big\rangle dv_{g}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}f^{-3}|h|^{2}dv_{g}

\displaystyle>0.

The instability follows.
∎

3.
Instability of conformally Kähler ALF Ricci-flat metrics

We now extend Theorem
6
to noncompact complete Ricci flat metrics with quadratic decay of curvature, that is to
gravitational instantons
. The known non Kähler examples are all conformally Kähler, and ALF in the following sense
[
4
]
:

Definition 10
.

A Riemannian manifold
(M^{4},g)
is ALF if

•

it has one end diffeomorphic to
(A,+\infty)\times L
, where
L
is
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\mathbb{S}^{3}
, or a finite quotient;

•

there is a triple
(\eta,T,\gamma)
defined on
L
, where
\eta
is a 1-form,
T
a vector field such that
\eta(T)=1
and
T\lrcorner d\eta=0
, and
\gamma
is a
T
-invariant metric on the distribution
\ker\eta
;

•

the transverse metric
\gamma
has constant curvature
+1
;

•

the metric
g
has the behaviour

(27)

g=dr^{2}+r^{2}\gamma+\eta^{2}+h,\text{ with }|\nabla^{k}h|=\mathrm{O}\left(r^{-1-k}\right),

where
\gamma
is extended to the whole
TL
by deciding that
\ker\gamma
is generated by
T
, and the covariant derivative
\nabla
and the norm are with respect to the asymptotic model
dr^{2}+r^{2}\gamma+\eta^{2}
.

The definition covers all the known examples: the Riemannian Kerr metrics (a special case being the Schwarzschild metric), the Taub-bolt metric, the Chen-Teo metrics
[
7
]
. In addition, the Kerr and Chen-Teo metrics are AF, that is
L=\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, so that the end has the topology of
\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\mathrm{B}(R)\right)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
for some large
R
. It is expected that this list gives all examples of conformally Kähler, non Kähler, ALF gravitational instantons, see
[
2
]
.

To apply the technique of the previous section, we need to verify that our
2
-tensor
h=\omega_{-}\circ\tau
(with
\tau
the Killing 2-form and
\omega_{-}
harmonic) decays suitably at infinity. Our gravitational instanton is conformally Kähler with
g=f^{2}\tilde{g}
. From the definition of an ALF metric we have
|W_{+}|=O(r^{-3})
, and actually for all the examples we have exactly
|W_{+}|\sim\mathrm{cst.}\,r^{-3}
. Therefore the conformal factor
f
satisfies
f\sim\mathrm{cst.}\,r
, and the Killing 2-form
\tau=f^{3}\tilde{\omega}_{+}
satisfies

(28)

|\tau|=O(r).

Remark 11
.

This decay is true for all the anti-selfdual
2
-forms on our examples (which are supposed to give a complete list). It is proved in
[
4
]
to be true in general in the toric case. Actually one can prove the growth (
28
) in the general case, which is the basic ingredient needed to make all our arguments abstract, and not specific to the examples. Nevertheless we have written the proof that follows since it looks likely that the known examples already exhaust all conformally Kähler, non Kähler, gravitational instantons. This seems to be proved in the recently posted article
[
15
]
, as mentioned in the introduction. It in particular shows that there is always an anti-selfdual harmonic
2
-forms decaying on the manifolds we consider.

There is a concrete construction of an anti-selfdual harmonic form
\omega_{-}
. Denote by
J
the complex structure. The Kähler metric
\tilde{g}
is extremal, which implies that the vector field

(29)

X=J\nabla^{g}f

is a Killing vector field. Denoting
\alpha
the 1-form associated via the metric, Kostant’s formula (
32
) below implies that
\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha=\Ric\alpha=0
, therefore from the Bochner formula
\alpha
is harmonic, and
d_{+}^{*}d_{+}\alpha=d_{-}d_{-}^{*}\alpha=0
. So we obtain a closed anti-selfdual form defined by

(30)

\omega_{-}=d_{-}\alpha.

The asymptotics of the Killing form
\tau
determined in
[
4
]
implies that, up to a constant, one has
\alpha\sim\eta
, where
\eta
is the 1-form from the definition (
27
). Since
T\lrcorner d\eta=0
, one has
|d\eta|=O(r^{-2})
and therefore
|\omega_{-}|=O(r^{-2})
. So we obtain:

Proposition 12
.

For the Kerr, Taub-bolt and Chen-Teo metrics, the tensor
h=\omega_{-}\tau
, where
\tau
is the Killing 2-form coming from the conformally Kähler structure and
\omega_{-}
is the harmonic anti-selfdual form defined by (
29
), is nonzero and satisfies, for
l\in\mathbb{N}
and some
C_{l}>0
, the bounds

(31)

|\nabla_{g}^{l}h|\leqslant C_{l}r^{-1-l}.

Proof.

We already proved the
C^{0}
bound and the bounds on the derivatives are obtained in the same way. There remains to prove that
h
is nontrivial, that is
\omega_{-}
is nontrivial.

The proof does not refer to any specific metric: suppose
\omega_{-}=0
, then we will prove that
g
is hyperKähler for the opposite orientation. Indeed, if
\omega_{-}=0
then
A:=\nabla\alpha
is a selfdual 2-form (actually, up to a constant, it is
\tilde{\omega}_{+}
but we do not need this fact).

For the Killing field
X
in (
29
) we use Kostant’s formula: for a tangent vector
Y
,

(32)

\nabla_{Y}A=-R_{X,Y}.

Since
A
is a selfdual 2-form, we obtain that
R_{X,Y}
is selfdual for all tangent vectors
Y
. Since
\Ric(g)=0
the curvature operator
R
preserves
\Omega_{\pm}
, so for any
Y
we have
R_{X\wedge Y+*(X\wedge Y)}\in\Omega_{+}
and therefore
R_{*(X\wedge Y)}\in\Omega_{+}
. Since the collection of
\{X\wedge Y,*(X\wedge Y)\}
when
Y\in TM
generates all 2-forms, the image of the curvature operator is entirely contained in
\Omega_{+}
. Since
R
acts on
\Omega_{-}
by
W_{-}
we deduce that
W_{-}=0
, that is
g
is hyperKähler for the other orientation, which is contrary to the hypothesis that
g
is non Kähler.
∎

Remark 13
.

Conversely, in the case of a conformally Kähler metric which is hyperKähler for the other orientation, that is satisfying
W_{-}=0
(Eguchi-Hanson, Taub-NUT), the form
\omega_{-}
is trivial: one can reverse the above argument, or more directly observe that the Weitzenböck formula

(33)

d_{-}d_{-}^{*}\omega_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{*}\nabla\omega_{-}-W_{-}(\omega_{-})

implies
\nabla\omega_{-}=0
if
W_{-}=0
. This is consistent with the fact that hyperKähler metrics are stable.

Theorem 14
.

The Kerr, Taub-Bolt and Chen-Teo metrics are unstable.

Remind that this is conjectured to be the full list of conformally Kähler, non Kähler, gravitational instantons.

Proof.

Let
(M,g)
be one of these gravitational instantons. Using
P
the second variation (
19
) rewrites as follows for
k
traceless and compactly supported:

(34)

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}^{(2)}(k,k)

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}\big\langle Pk-\frac{1}{3}\delta^{*}\delta k\,,\,k\big\rangle dv_{g}

\displaystyle\geqslant-\int_{M}\langle Pk\,,\,k\rangle dv_{g}.

Therefore it is sufficient to show that
P
admits a compactly supported deformation for which
\int_{M}-\langle Pk\,,\,k\rangle dv_{g}>0
.

Consider the 2-tensor
h
coming from Proposition
12
. The situation is the same as in the proof of Theorem
6
, so we have
Ph=-f^{-3}h
. Observe that since
h=O(r^{-1})
we have

(35)

\int_{M}-\langle Ph,h\rangle dv_{g}=\int_{M}f^{-3}|h|^{2}dv_{g}<+\infty.

With the bounds (
31
) on the derivatives of
h
, a standard cutoff argument gives a compactly supported
k
with
\int_{M}-\langle Pk,k\rangle dv_{g}>0
, which proves instability.
∎

Example 15
.

On the (Euclidean) Schwarzschild metric:

g=\frac{dr^{2}}{1-\frac{2m}{r}}+\big(1-\frac{2m}{r}\big)dt^{2}+r^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}},
conformal to the Kähler metric
\tilde{g}=r^{-2}g
, one finds

(36)

h=-\frac{1}{r}\Big(\frac{dr^{2}}{1-\frac{2m}{r}}+\Big(1-\frac{2m}{r}\Big)dt^{2}-r^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Big),

and checks numerically that the associated second variation
\mathscr{S}_{g}^{(2)}(h,h)
is negative.

Ill-posedness for the two component Degasperis-Procesi equation in critical Besov space
Jinlu Li, Min Li, Weipeng Zhu

Abstract




Abstract:

In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem for the two component Degasperis-Procesi equation in critical Besov space
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
. By presenting a new construction of initial data, we proved the norm inflation of the corresponding solutions in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
and hence ill-posedness. This is quite different from the local well-posedness result
[
27
]
for the Degasperis–Procesi equation in critical Besov space
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
due to the coupled structure of density function.

Keywords:
two component Degasperis-Procesi equation; Ill-posedness; Critical Besov space.

MSC (2010):
35Q53, 37K10.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for the following two component Degasperis-Procesi equation
[
2
,
24
]

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}m+3mu_{x}+m_{x}u+k_{3}\rho\rho_{x}=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\partial_{t}\rho+k_{2}u\rho_{x}+(k_{1}+k_{2})u_{x}\rho=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
m=u-u_{xx},&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1.1)

here
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=(1,1,c)
or
(c,1,0)
and
c
is an arbitrary constant. System (
1.1
) first appeared in
[
2
]
as the Hamiltonian extension of the Degasperis–Procesi equation. It is worth mentioning that, in the case
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=(c,1,0),
system (
1.1
) is no more coupled and the equation on
\rho
becomes linear. Therefore, we only consider the first case
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=(1,1,c).
For simplicity, we might as well assume that
c=1
, which makes system (
1.1
) equivalent to the following quasi-linear evolution equation of hyperbolic type:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+uu_{x}=-\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(\frac{3}{2}u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}),&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\partial_{t}\rho+u\rho_{x}=-2u_{x}\rho,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

In particular, for
\rho_{0}\equiv 0,
system (
1.2
) reduces to the Degasperis–Procesi (DP) equation
[
22
]
. The DP equation can be regarded as a model for nonlinear shallow water dynamics and its asymptotic accuracy is the same as for the well known Camassa-Holm equation
[
3
]
. The DP equation was also proved formally integrable by constructing a Lax pair
[
13
]
and the direct and inverse scattering approach to pursue it can be seen in
[
23
]
. Moreover, they also presented
[
13
]
that the DP equation has a bi-Hamiltonian structure and an infinite number of conservation laws, and admits travelling wave and exact peakon solutions which are analogous to the Camassa–Holm peakons
[
4
,
6
]
.
The Cauchy problem of the DP equation is locally well-posed in certain Sobolev and Besov spaces
[
5
,
7
,
8
]
, also the non-uniform dependence and ill-posedness problem have recently been studied in
[
31
,
28
]
. It has global strong solutions
[
16
,
8
]
, the finite-time blow-up solutions
[
10
,
11
]
and global weak solutions
[
12
,
10
,
9
]
.
Different form the Camassa-Holm equation, the DP equation has not only peakon solutions
[
13
]
,
periodic peakon solutions
[
9
]
, but also shock peakons
[
15
]
and the periodic shock waves
[
11
]
.
Both Camassa-Holm and DP equation admit cusped travelling waves
[
17
,
4
]
. This type of weak solutions are known to arise as solutions for the governing equations for waves in a channel or along a sloped beach
[
18
,
19
]
, and also as solutions for the governing equations for equatorial ocean waves
[
20
,
21
]
.

Recently, a lot of literature was devoted to studying the well-posedness (especially the ill-posedness) problem of the Camassa-Holm type equations (CH, DP and Novikov equation etc.) in the critical Besov spaces. For example, Guo et al.
[
14
]
prove norm inflation and hence ill-posedness for the Camassa-Holm type equations in the critical Sobolev space
H^{\frac{3}{2}}
and even in the Besov space
B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})
with
p\in[1,\infty],r\in(1,\infty],
which implies
B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R})
is the critical Besov space for these Camassa-Holm type equations. Li et al.
[
29
,
30
]
demonstrated the non-continuity and then sharp ill-posedness of the Camassa-Holm type equations in
B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\sigma>\max\{\frac{3}{2},1+\frac{1}{p}\}
. Later, it has been proved the local well-posedness for the Cauchy problem of the Camassa-Holm type equations in
B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R})
with
p\in[1,\infty)
by adopt the compactness argument and Lagrangian coordinate transformation
[
25
]
. In the remaining case
p=\infty
, Guo et al.
[
26
]
prove the ill-posedness for the Camassa-Holm equation in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}.

Different from the CH equation, the authors in
[
27
]
have proved the well-posedness for the Cauchy problem of the DP equation in critical Besov space
B^{1}_{\infty,1}.
For the two component DP equation(
1.2
), the local well-posedness problem in certain Sobolev and Besov spaces has been studied by Yan et al.in
[
24
]
, here we mainly focus on the well-posedness problem in critical Besov space
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
. As the structure is changed from the DP equation due to the coupled density function
\rho
, here we have a different result for the two component DP equation (
1.2
) in this paper. Now, let us state our main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
large enough there exists
(u_{0},\rho_{0})
with

\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\|\rho_{0}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq\frac{1}{\log\log n},

such that if we denote by
(u,\rho)\in\mathcal{C}([0,1];H^{3}\times H^{2})
, the solution of the two component Degasperis-Procesi with initial data
(u_{0},\rho_{0})
, then

\|u(t_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\|\rho(t_{0})\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\geq{\log\log n},

with
t_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{\log n}]
.

Remark 1.1
.

This implies the ill-posedness of the two component DP equation (
1.2
) in Besov space
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
. In fact, by Theorem
1.1
we have construct the norm inflation in space
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
, thus the data-to-solution map is not continuous at origin in this space, in the sense of Hadamard this implies the ill-posedness.

2
Littlewood-Paley analysis

Next, we will recall some facts about the Littlewood-Paley decomposition, the nonhomogeneous Besov spaces and their some useful properties (see
[
1
]
for more details).

Let
\mathcal{B}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:|\xi|\leq 4/3\}
and
\mathcal{C}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:3/4\leq|\xi|\leq 8/3\}.

Choose a radial, non-negative, smooth function
\chi:\mathbb{R}\mapsto[0,1]
such that it is supported in
\mathcal{B}
and
\chi\equiv 1
for
|\xi|\leq 3/4
. Setting
\varphi(\xi):=\chi(\xi/2)-\chi(\xi)
, then we deduce that
\varphi
is supported in
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\chi(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\quad\mbox{ for any }\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

We should emphasize that the fact
\varphi(\xi)\equiv 1
for
4/3\leq|\xi|\leq 3/2
will be used in the sequel.

For every
u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
, the inhomogeneous dyadic blocks
{\Delta}_{j}
are defined as follows

{\Delta_{j}u=}\begin{cases}0,&if\quad j\leq-2;\\
\chi(D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi\mathcal{F}u),&if\quad j=-1;\\
\varphi(2^{-j}D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\mathcal{F}u\big),&if\quad j\geq 0.\end{cases}

In the inhomogeneous case, the following Littlewood-Paley decomposition makes sense

u=\sum_{j\geq-1}{\Delta}_{j}u\quad\text{for any}\;u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}).

Definition 2.1

(see
[
1
]
)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
. The nonhomogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

\displaystyle B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}):=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}):\;\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty\Big\},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{sjr}\|\Delta_{j}f\|^{r}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},

if
1\leq r<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}=

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq-1}2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})},

if
r=\infty
.

The following Bernstein’s inequalities will be used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1

(see Lemma 2.1 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Ball and
\mathcal{C}
be an annulus. There exist constants
C>0
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, any positive real number
\lambda
and any function
f\in L^{p}
with
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm{supp}}\hat{f}\subset\lambda\mathcal{B}\;\Rightarrow\;\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\|_{L^{q}}\leq C^{k+1}\lambda^{k+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\|f\|_{L^{p}},

\displaystyle{\rm{supp}}\hat{f}\subset\lambda\mathcal{C}\;\Rightarrow\;C^{-k-1}\lambda^{k}\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C^{k+1}\lambda^{k}\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

Lemma 2.2

(see Lemma 2.100 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
1\leq r\leq\infty
,
1\leq p\leq p_{1}\leq\infty
and
\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{1}}
. There exists a constant
C
depending continuously on
p,p_{1}
, such that

\left\|\left(2^{j}\left\|[\Delta_{j},v\partial_{x}]f\right\|_{L^{p}}\right)_{j}\right\|_{\ell^{r}}\leq C\left(\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{B_{p,r}^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\|\partial_{x}v\|_{B_{p_{1},r}^{0}}\right).

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

For localization, we first introduce the following bump function in the frequency space.
let
\widehat{\theta}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
be a smooth and even function with values in
[0,1]
which satisfies

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta}(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,\quad\mathrm{if}\ |\xi|\leq\frac{1}{200},\\
0,\quad\mathrm{if}\ |\xi|\geq\frac{1}{100}.\end{cases}

Then, shift the frequency of the above function by letting
\phi(x)=\theta(x)\sin(\frac{17}{24}x)
. For the convenience of notation, hereafter

\displaystyle n\in 16\mathbb{N}^{+}=\{16,32,48,\cdots\},~~~~\mathbb{N}(n)=\{k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}:\frac{n}{4}\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}\}.

and define the norm
B^{0}_{\infty,1}\left(\mathbb{N}(n)\right)
by

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left\|\Delta_{j}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Now, we construct the initial data
(u_{0},\rho_{0})
as following:

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=0,\qquad\rho_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n\cdot\sin(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x)\cdot\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\phi(2^{\ell}(x-2^{2n+\ell})).

3.1
Estimation of initial data

First of all, we give the estimates for
\rho_{0}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n,

\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n.

Proof.

By the definition of
\rho_{0}
and using convolution expressions, it’s easy to verify that the support set of
\rho_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\rho_{0}}\subset\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\ \frac{17}{12}2^{n}-\frac{17}{12}2^{\ell}-\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{17}{12}2^{n}+\frac{17}{12}2^{\ell}+\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

(3.3)

Since
\phi
and
\check{\chi}
are Schwartz functions, we have

\displaystyle|\phi(x)|+|\check{\chi}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-M},\qquad M\geq 100.

(3.4)

then, by a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n\left\|\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\phi(2^{\ell}(x-2^{2n+\ell}))\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n\left\|\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\frac{1}{(1+|2^{\ell}x-2^{2n+2\ell}|)^{M}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n.

Due to the fact of (
3.3
) and the definition of
\Delta_{j}
, it’s easy to check that
\Delta_{j}\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}
if
j=n
and
\Delta_{j}u_{0}=0
if
j\neq n
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

The following lower bound estimate of the squared term is crucial to our proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a positive constant
c
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\left\|(\rho_{0})^{2}\right\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}\left(\mathbb{N}(n)\right)}\geq c\log^{2}n,\qquad n\gg 1.

Proof.

It follows that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}(n)
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}[(\rho_{0})^{2}]=\frac{1}{2}n^{-1}\log^{2}n\cdot\Delta_{j}U,\qquad U:=\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\phi(2^{\ell}(x-2^{2n+\ell}))\right)^{2}.

(3.5)

We rewrite
U
as

\displaystyle U=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\phi^{2}(2^{\ell}(x-2^{2n+\ell}))+\sum_{\ell,m\in\mathbb{N}(n)\setminus\{\ell=m\}}\phi(2^{\ell}(x-2^{2n+\ell}))\phi(2^{m}(x-2^{2n+m})):=U_{1}+U_{2}.

Then, we have
\Delta_{j}U=\Delta_{j}U_{1}+\Delta_{j}U_{2}
. For
\Delta_{j}U_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}||\Delta_{j}U_{2}||_{L^{\infty}}&\leq C||U_{2}||_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq C\sum_{\ell,m\in\mathbb{N}(n)\setminus\{\ell=m\}}||\phi(2^{\ell}(x-2^{2n+\ell}))\phi(2^{m}(x-2^{2n+m}))||_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq C\sum_{\ell,m\in\mathbb{N}(n)\setminus\{\ell=m\}}||\frac{1}{(1+2^{\ell}|x-2^{2n+\ell}|)^{M}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1+2^{m}|x-2^{2n+m}|)^{M}}||_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq C\sum_{\ell,m\in\mathbb{N}(n)\setminus\{\ell=m\}}2^{-2nM}\leq Cn^{2}2^{-2nM}.\end{split}

(3.6)

By direct computations, for large enough
n
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}U_{1}=\Delta_{j}\phi^{2}(2^{j}(x-2^{2n+j}))+\Delta_{j}\sum_{j\neq\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\phi^{2}(2^{\ell}(x-2^{2n+\ell}))=:\Delta_{j}U_{1,1}+\Delta_{j}U_{1,2}.

Let us introduce the set
\mathbf{B}_{j}
defined by
\mathbf{B}_{j}=\{x:2^{j}|x-2^{2n+j}|\leq 1\}
.

We can show that

\displaystyle||\Delta_{j}U_{1}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}\geq||\Delta_{j}U_{1,1}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}-||\Delta_{j}U_{1,2}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}

Notice that

\displaystyle\phi^{2}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}(x)\cos(\frac{17}{12}x):=\Phi_{1}(x)+\Phi_{2}(x),

then we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}U_{1,1}=\Delta_{j}\Phi_{2}(2^{j}(x-2^{2n+j}))=\Phi_{2}(2^{j}(x-2^{2n+j})).

This implies

\displaystyle||\Delta_{j}U_{1,1}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}\geq\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}(0):=c.

(3.7)

By (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}U_{1,2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\neq\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left\|2^{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{\chi}(2^{j}(x-y))\phi^{2}(2^{\ell}(y-2^{2n+\ell}))\mathrm{d}y\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\neq\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left\|2^{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{(1+2^{j}|x-y|)^{M}}\frac{1}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-2^{2n+\ell}|)^{2M}}\mathrm{d}y\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}.

Dividing the integral region in terms of
y
into the following two parts to estimate:

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\left\{y:\;|y-2^{j+2n}|\leq 2^{2n}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\left\{y:\;|y-2^{j+2n}|\geq 2^{2n}\right\}.

For
x\in\mathbf{B}_{j}
and
y\in\mathbf{A}_{1}
, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\left|y-2^{\ell+2n}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|y-2^{j+2n}+2^{j+2n}-2^{\ell+2n}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|2^{\ell+2n}-2^{j+2n}\right|-\left|y-2^{j+2n}\right|\geq 2^{2n}.

For
x\in\mathbf{B}_{j}
and
y\in\mathbf{A}_{2}
, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle|x-y|

\displaystyle=\left|x-2^{j+2n}+2^{j+2n}-y\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|y-2^{j+2n}\right|-\left|x-2^{j+2n}\right|\geq 2^{2n}-2^{-j}\geq 2^{2n-1}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\left\|2^{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{(1+2^{j}|x-y|)^{M}}\frac{1}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-2^{2n+\ell}|)^{2M}}\mathrm{d}y\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}\leq C2^{j}2^{-2nM}\leq C2^{\frac{n}{2}}2^{-nM},

which implies

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}U_{1,2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-n}

(3.8)

Combining (
3.6
)-(
3.8
), we deduce that for
n\gg 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta_{j}[(\rho_{0})^{2}]\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\Delta_{j}[(\rho_{0})^{2}]\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\Delta_{j}U_{1,1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}-\left\|\Delta_{j}U_{1,2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}_{j})}-\left\|\Delta_{j}U_{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq cn^{-1}\log^{2}n.

Therefore, by the definition of the Besov norm, we have

\displaystyle\left\|(\rho_{0})^{2}\right\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left\|\Delta_{j}[(\rho_{0})^{2}]\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq c\log^{2}n.

This completes the proof of lemma
3.2
.
∎

3.2
Norm Inflation

Firstly, we define the Lagrangian flow-map
\psi
associated to
u
by solve the following ODE:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\psi(t,x)=u(t,\psi(t,x)),\\[4.30554pt]
\psi(0,x)=x.\end{array}\right.

(3.9)

Considering the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v+u\partial_{x}v=P,&t\in[0,T),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\\[4.30554pt]
v(0,x)=x,x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

(3.10)

Taking
\Delta_{j}
on (
3.10
), we get

\partial_{t}(\Delta_{j}v)+u\partial_{x}\Delta_{j}v=[u,\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}v+\Delta_{j}P:=R_{j}+\Delta_{j}P,

Due to (
3.9
), then

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big((\Delta_{j}v)\circ\psi\big)=R_{j}\circ\psi+\Delta_{j}P\circ\psi,

which is equivalent to the integral form

\displaystyle(\Delta_{j}v)\circ\psi

\displaystyle=\Delta_{j}v_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}R_{j}\circ\psi\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\Delta_{j}P\circ\psi\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.11)

Now we can go back to the proof of the Theorem
1.1
. For
n\gg 1
, it is easy to check that
(u,\rho)\in\mathcal{C}([0,1];H^{3}\times H^{2})
which satisfy for
t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+||\rho(t)||_{L^{\infty}}\leq(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+||\rho_{0}||_{L^{\infty}})\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{x}u(\tau),\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau\right).

For
n\gg 1
, we have for
t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n.

(3.12)

Moreover, we also have
t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int^{t}_{0}(\|u\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}+||\rho||^{2}_{L^{\infty}})\mathrm{d}\tau\leq Cn^{-1}\log^{2}n.

(3.13)

To prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that there exists
t_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{\log n}]
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\geq\log\log n.

(3.14)

We prove (
3.14
) by contraction. If (
3.14
) were not true, then

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,\frac{1}{\log n}]}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}<\log\log n.

(3.15)

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1: Lower bounds for
(\Delta_{j}u)\circ\psi

Now we consider the equation along the Lagrangian flow-map associated to
u
.
Utilizing (
3.11
) to (
1.2
) yields

\displaystyle(\Delta_{j}u)\circ\psi

\displaystyle=\Delta_{j}u_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}R^{1}_{j}\circ\psi\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\Delta_{j}F\circ\psi\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\big(\Delta_{j}E\circ\psi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}\big)\mathrm{d}\tau+t\Delta_{j}E_{0},

where

\displaystyle R^{1}_{j}=[u,\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}u,\quad F=-\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(u^{2}),\quad E=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(\rho^{2}).

Due to Lemma
3.2
, we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\approx\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\|\Delta_{j}\partial_{x}E_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq c\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\|\Delta_{j}(\rho_{0})^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq c\log^{2}n.

(3.16)

Notice that, for fixed
t
the Lagrangian flow-map
\psi(t,\cdot)
is a diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
, then we have for
t\in(0,\frac{1}{\log n}]
,

\displaystyle\|f(t,\psi(t,x))\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Then, using (
3.12
), (
3.15
) and the commutator estimate from Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|R^{1}_{j}\circ\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|R^{1}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n\cdot\log\log n.

(3.17)

Also, by (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}F\circ\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}F\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cn^{-2}\log^{4}n,

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}F\circ\psi\|_{L^{p}}\leq Cn^{-1}\log^{4}n.

(3.18)

Combining (
3.16
)-(
3.18
) and using Lemmas
3.1
-
3.2
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|(\Delta_{j}u)\circ\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq t\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E\circ\psi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}-Cn^{-1}\log^{4}n

\displaystyle\geq ct\log^{2}n-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E\circ\psi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}-Cn^{-1}\log^{4}n.

Step 2: Upper bounds for
\Delta_{j}E\circ\psi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}

By easy computations, we can see that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E+u\partial_{x}E

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(\partial_{t}\rho\rho)-\frac{1}{2}u\partial^{2}_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(\rho^{2})

(3.19)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(u\rho^{2})-\frac{3}{2}u_{x}\rho^{2})-\frac{1}{2}u(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(\rho^{2})+\frac{1}{2}u\rho^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(u\rho^{2})+\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(u_{x}\rho^{2})-\frac{1}{2}u(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(\rho^{2})+\frac{1}{2}u\rho^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(u\rho^{2})-\frac{1}{2}u\rho^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(u_{x}\rho^{2})-\frac{1}{2}u(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(\rho^{2})+\frac{1}{2}u\rho^{2}

\displaystyle=:G,

where

\displaystyle G=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(\rho^{2})-\frac{1}{2}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}\left(-u\rho^{2}-\partial_{x}(3u_{x}\rho^{2})\right).

Utilizing (
3.11
) to (
3.19
) yields

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}E\circ\psi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}=\int^{t}_{0}[u,\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}E\circ\psi\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\Delta_{j}G\circ\psi\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using the commutator estimate from Lemma
2.2
, one has

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|[u,\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}E\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|E\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}E\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,\infty}})\leq C(\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}})^{3}

and

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}G\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}})^{3}.

Then, we have from (
3.12
)

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E\circ\psi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}})^{3}\leq Cn^{-\frac{3}{2}}\log^{3}n,

which leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E\circ\psi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log^{3}n.

Combining Step 1 and Step 2, then for
t=\frac{1}{\log n}
, we obtain for
n\gg 1

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\geq\|u(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}\geq C\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|(\Delta_{j}u)\circ\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq ct\log^{2}n-Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log^{3}n\geq\log\log n,

which contradicts the hypothesis (
3.15
).

In conclusion, we obtain the norm inflation and hence the ill-posedness of the two component Degasperis-Procesi equation. Thus, Theorem
1.1
is proved.

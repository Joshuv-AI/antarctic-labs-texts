Multipolar nematic state of nonmagnetic FeSe based on the DFT + U U
Takemi Yamada, Takami Tohyama

Abstract
Clarifying the origin of nematic state in FeSe is one of urgent problems in the field of iron-based superconductivity. Motivated by the discovery of a nematic solution in the density-functional theory implemented by on-site Coulomb interaction (DFT + U U ) [npj Quantum Mater. 5 , 50 (2020)], we reexamine the U U dependence of electronic states in the nonmagnetic normal state of FeSe and perform full multipolar analyses for the nematic state. We find that with increasing U U the normal state experiences a topological change in the Fermi surfaces before the emergence of a nematic ground state. The resulting nematic ground state is a multipolar state having both antiferrohexadecapoles in the E E representation and ferromultipoles in the B 2 B_{2} representation on each Fe site. Cooperative coupling between the E E and the B 2 B_{2} multipoles in the local coordinate with the D 2 ​ d D_{2d} point group will play an important role in the formation of the d x ​ z , d y ​ z d_{xz},~d_{yz} orbital-splitting nematic state not only in FeSe, but also in other iron pnictides.

To summarize, we have studied the nonmagnetic normal and nematic states of the iron-based superconductor FeSe by using the DFT +
U
method with the multipole analyses.
The effect of
U
on the normal state generates a topological change in FSs from 3h-FSs to 2h-FSs,
leading to a change in the dominant orbital near
\varepsilon_{\rm F}
from
d_{xy}
to
d_{xz},~d_{yz}
.
As a result, the multipolar nematic state with the
E
antiferrohexadecapoles accompanying the
B_{2}
ferromultipoles
has been obtained without any assumption of the order parameters,
giving rise to both of the
d_{xz}
-
d_{yz}
orbital splitting at
\Gamma
and the
d_{xy}
-
(d_{xz},~d_{yz})
orbital mixing around M and A points.
From phenomenological analysis, we have found that the intermultipole coupling of
B_{2}
and
E
multipoles on each Fe site can explain the energy gain larger for the coexisting order than for the quadupole
O_{xy}
order alone.
This multipolar mechanism for the formation of nematic state will be applicable
not only to FeSe but also to other iron pnictides where the degenerated
d_{xz}
,
d_{yz}
orbitals play a crucial role.

Here we provide the details of the DFT calculation based on WIEN2k
Blaha et al. 2020
, which is the all-electron first-principles code,
where the basis functions are expanded by the relativistic full-potential augmented plane wave (FLAPW) and/or APW + local orbitals depending on the core and valence states of each atom in the unit-cell.
We employ the generalized gradient approximation for the exchange-correlation potential of the PBE-GGA potential
Perdew et al. 1996

and the DFT +
U
method within the self-interaction correction
Anisimov et al. 1993
;
Shick et al. 1999
.
The spin-orbit interaction (SOI) is neglected in this study to simplify the multipole analysis,
since its effect on the nematics state is weak as shown in Fig. 7 of Ref.
Long et al. 2020
and
does not give any serious problem on the main results in the present study.

The Coulomb interaction
U
for
d

R_{\rm MT}^{\nu}
with atomic sites
\nu
=Fe1,Fe2 in the unit cell is introduced, where the DFT +
U
correction energy is given by,

\displaystyle E_{U}=\frac{U}{2}\sum_{\nu}\left(n^{d,\nu}-\sum_{mm^{\prime}}\sum_{\sigma}n_{mm^{\prime}}^{\sigma,\nu}n_{m^{\prime}m}^{\sigma,\nu}\right),

(S1)

where

is total occupation number of
d

R_{\rm MT}^{\nu}
as

and the density matrix
n_{mm^{\prime}}^{\sigma,\nu}
. Hereafter, we drop the spin index

n_{mm^{\prime}}^{\uparrow,\nu}
=

In WIEN2k, the Kohn-Sham (KS) equation for single electron, depending on the core and valence states of each atom in the unit-cell, is explicitly written by

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\nabla}^{2}+v^{\rm KS}_{{\rm eff}}(\bm{r})+v_{U}(\bm{r})\right)\psi_{\bm{k}n}(\bm{r})=\varepsilon_{\bm{k}n}\psi_{\bm{k}n}(\bm{r}),

(S4)

\displaystyle v_{mm^{\prime}}^{\nu}=\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\frac{U}{2}(1-n_{m}^{\nu})+(1-\delta_{mm^{\prime}})(-Un_{mm^{\prime}}^{\nu}),

(S5)

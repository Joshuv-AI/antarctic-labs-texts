Pointwise decay for semilinear wave equations on Kerr spacetimes
Mihai Tohaneanu

Abstract
. In this article we prove pointwise bounds for solutions to the semilinear wave equation with integer powers p ≥ 3 p\geq 3 on Kerr backgrounds with small angular momentum and small initial data. We expect that the bounds proved in this paper are optimal.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Statement of the result

In this paper we establish pointwise bounds for solutions to the semilinear wave equations on Kerr backgrounds. We consider the equation

(1.1)

\Box_{K}\phi=\pm\phi^{p},\qquad\phi|_{{\tilde{t}}=0}=\phi_{0},\qquad\tilde{T}\phi|_{{\tilde{t}}=0}=\phi_{1}\ .

Here the coordinate
{\tilde{t}}
is chosen so that the slice
{\tilde{t}}=0
is space-like and so that
{\tilde{t}}=t

away from the black hole, and
\tilde{T}
is the future unit normal to
{\tilde{t}}=0
(see Section 2 for details). Moreover,
p\geq 3
is any integer,
\Box_{K}
denotes the d’Alembertian in the Kerr metric, and the initial data are smooth and supported in
\{|x|\leq R_{1}\}
for some fixed (but arbitrary)
R_{1}
. We also assume that, for some fixed
N\gg 1
, we have that

\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{N+1}}+\|\phi_{1}\|_{H^{N}}\leq\varepsilon.

Let
\kappa=\min\{2,p-2\}
. Let
{\tilde{r}}
equal to
r
on a compact region and to the Regge-Wheeler coordinate
r^{*}
near infinity (see Section 2 for details). We will show that, for small enough
\varepsilon
,

(1.2)

\phi\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\langle{\tilde{t}}\rangle\langle{\tilde{t}}-{\tilde{r}}\rangle^{\kappa}},

where
\langle x\rangle=\sqrt{2+|x|^{2}}
.

In future work, we will also show that this rate of decay is sharp.

Much less is known of the behavior of solutions of the semilinear wave equations with power nonlinearity on Schwarzschild and Kerr backgrounds. For small initial data, global existence and pointwise decay rates of

were shown for spherically symmetric solutions in Schwarzschild in the case

[

The goal of this paper is to establish sharp decay rates for solutions to (
1.1
) with small initial data. For simplicity, we also pick the initial data to be compactly supported, though one can do away with it assuming enough decay in weighted Sobolev spaces. The ideas of the proof also apply to other metrics and nonlinearities; in particular, see the upcoming result of Looi
[
20

, where a similar result for the quintic defocusing nonlinearity on perturbations of Minkowski (and large initial data) is shown.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the Kerr metric, our preferred coordinates, local energy estimates, and our main theorem. Section 3 is dedicated to proving local energy estimates for our nonlinear problem. These estimates imply an initial pointwise decay rate of
\frac{(t-r)^{1/2}}{t}
, which is insufficient as a starting point for
p\leq 5
. Section 4 supplements this with
r^{p}
estimates, which give better weighted
L^{2}
estimates. Section 5 rephrases the problem in a more convenient way. Section 6 contains the proof of the main lemma used to improve the pointwise decay. Section 7 uses the results of the previous sections to yield an initial pointwise decay of
\frac{1}{r(t-r)^{\delta}}
. Section 8 obtains improved decay rates for the solution in the interior, and for derivatives. Section 9 is the main bootstrap argument: starting from the decay estimate of Section 7, we use the results of Sections 6 and Section 8 to improve the decay rate to the optimal one.

2.
The Kerr metric and local energy norms

2.1.
The Kerr metric

The Kerr geometry in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates is given by

ds^{2}=g^{K}_{tt}dt^{2}+g_{t\phi}dtd\phi+g^{K}_{rr}dr^{2}+g^{K}_{\phi\phi}d\phi^{2},+g^{K}_{\theta\theta}d\theta^{2}

where
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
r>0
,
(\phi,\theta)
are the spherical coordinates
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
and

g^{K}_{tt}=-\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}},\qquad g^{K}_{t\phi}=-2a\frac{2Mr\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}},\qquad g^{K}_{rr}=\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta},

g^{K}_{\phi\phi}=\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta,\qquad g^{K}_{\theta\theta}={\rho^{2}},

with

\Delta=r^{2}-2Mr+a^{2},\qquad\rho^{2}=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta.

Here
M
represents the mass of the black hole, and
aM
its angular momentum.

A straightforward computation gives us the inverse of the metric:

g_{K}^{tt}=-\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}\Delta},\qquad g_{K}^{t\phi}=-a\frac{2Mr}{\rho^{2}\Delta},\qquad g_{K}^{rr}=\frac{\Delta}{\rho^{2}},

g_{K}^{\phi\phi}=\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta},\qquad g_{K}^{\theta\theta}=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}.

The case
a=0
corresponds to the Schwarzschild space-time. We shall
subsequently assume that
a
is small
0<a\ll M
, so that the Kerr
metric is a small perturbation of the Schwarzschild metric. Note also that the coefficients depend only
r
and
\theta
but are independent of

\phi
and
t
.

We denote the Kerr metric by
g_{K}
, and the Schwarzschild metric by
g_{S}
. Let

\Box_{K}
and
\Box_{S}
be the d’Alembertian associated to the Kerr and Schwarzschild metric, respectively. Similarly
dV_{K}
and
dV_{S}
are the volume forms, and
d\Sigma_{K}
and
d\Sigma_{S}
are the restrictions of the volume form to a hypersurface.

In the above coordinates the Kerr metric has singularities at
r=0
,
on the equator
\theta=\pi/2
, and at the roots of
\Delta
, namely

r_{\pm}=M\pm\sqrt{M^{2}-a^{2}}
. To remove the singularities at
r=r_{\pm}
we
introduce functions
r_{K}^{*}=r_{K}^{*}(r)
,
v_{+}=t+r_{K}^{*}
and
\phi_{+}=\phi_{+}(\phi,r)
so that (see

[
12
]
)

dr_{K}^{*}=(r^{2}+a^{2})\Delta^{-1}dr,\qquad dv_{+}=dt+dr_{K}^{*},\qquad d\phi_{+}=d\phi+a\Delta^{-1}dr.

Note that when
a=0
the
r_{K}^{*}
coordinate becomes the Schwarzschild Regge-Wheeler coordinate

r^{*}=r+2M\log(r-2M).

The Kerr metric can be written in the new coordinates
(v_{+},r,\phi_{+},\theta)

\begin{split}ds^{2}=&\ -(1-\frac{2Mr}{\rho^{2}})dv_{+}^{2}+2drdv_{+}-4a\rho^{-2}Mr\sin^{2}\theta dv_{+}d\phi_{+}-2a\sin^{2}\theta drd\phi_{+}+\rho^{2}d\theta^{2}\\
&\ +\rho^{-2}[(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-\Delta a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta]\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi_{+}^{2},\end{split}

which is smooth and nondegenerate across the event horizon up to but not including

r=0
. We introduce the function

\tilde{t}=v_{+}-\mu(r),

where
\mu
is a smooth function of
r
. In the
(\tilde{t},r,\phi_{+},\theta)
coordinates the metric has the form

\begin{split}ds^{2}=&\ (1-\frac{2Mr}{\rho^{2}})d\tilde{t}^{2}+2\left(1-(1-\frac{2Mr}{\rho^{2}})\mu^{\prime}(r)\right)d\tilde{t}dr\\
&\ -4a\rho^{-2}Mr\sin^{2}\theta d\tilde{t}d\phi_{+}+\Bigl(2\mu^{\prime}(r)-(1-\frac{2Mr}{\rho^{2}})(\mu^{\prime}(r))^{2}\Bigr)dr^{2}\\
&\ -2a(1+2\rho^{-2}Mr\mu^{\prime}(r))\sin^{2}\theta drd\phi_{+}+\rho^{2}d\theta^{2}\\
&\ +\rho^{-2}[(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-\Delta a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta]\sin^{2}\theta d\phi_{+}^{2}.\end{split}

On the function
\mu
we impose the following two conditions:

(i)
\mu(r)\geq r^{*}
for
r>2M
, with equality for
r>{5M}/2
.

(ii) The surfaces
\tilde{t}=const
are space-like, i.e.

\mu^{\prime}(r)>0,\qquad 2-(1-\frac{2Mr}{\rho^{2}})\mu^{\prime}(r)>0.

As long as
a
is small, we can use the same
function
\mu
as in the case of the Schwarzschild space-time in
[
22
]
.

Let
\tilde{r}
denote a smooth strictly increasing function (of
r
) that equals
r
for
r\leq R
and
r^{*}
for
r\geq 2R
for some large
R
. We will use the coordinates
(\tilde{t},x^{i})
, where
x^{i}={\tilde{r}}\omega
. We use Latin
indices
i,j=1,2,3
for spatial summation and Greek indices
\alpha,\beta=0,1,2,3
for space-time summation. By
\langle r\rangle
we denote a smooth radial function which agrees with
r
for
large
r
and satisfies
\langle r\rangle\geq 1
. Note that, since
r\approx{\tilde{r}}
, we can use
r^{k}
and
{\tilde{r}}^{k}
interchangeably when defining our spaces of functions.

We fix
r_{e}
satisfying
r_{-}<r_{e}<r_{+}
. The choice of
r_{e}
is unimportant,
and for convenience we may simply use
r_{e}=M
for all Kerr metrics
with
a/M\ll 1
. Let
\mathcal{M}=\{\tilde{t}\geq 0,\ r\geq r_{e}\}
,
\Sigma(T)=\mathcal{M}\cap\{\tilde{t}=T\}
, and
\Sigma^{-}=\Sigma(0)
.

A major difficulty in understanding dispersion properties for solutions to the linear wave equation on
\mathcal{M}
is the presence of trapped null geodesics (i.e. null geodesics which do not escape either to infinity or to the singularity). One family of such geodesics occurs at the event horizon; however, due to the red-shift effect, the energy decays exponentially (in the high-frequency approximation) along such rays. A second family of such geodesics occurs in the compact region
|r-3M|\lesssim|a|
. Here the energy does not decay exponentially, but due to the hyperbolic nature of the trapping, it disperses after a time proportional to the logarithm of the frequency, and thus space-time estimates can still be recovered, albeit with a loss of derivatives.

Our favorite sets of vector fields will be

\partial=\{\partial_{\tilde{t}},\partial_{i}\},\qquad\Omega=\{x^{i}\partial_{j}-x^{j}\partial_{i}\},\qquad S=\tilde{t}\partial_{\tilde{t}}+\tilde{r}\partial_{\tilde{r}},

namely the generators of translations, rotations and scaling. We set

Z=\{\partial,\Omega,S\}
.

For a triplet
\alpha=(i,j,k)
we define
|\alpha|=i+3j+9k
and

u_{\alpha}={\partial}^{i}\Omega^{j}S^{k}.

The numerology is borrowed from
[
23
]
, and takes into account the loss of derivatives that occurs when applying weak local energy estimates to vector fields.

We define the classes
S^{Z}(r^{k})
of
functions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
by

f\in S^{Z}(r^{k})\Longleftrightarrow|Z^{j}f(t,x)|\leq c_{j}\langle r\rangle^{k},\quad j\geq 0.

By
S^{Z}_{rad}(r^{k})
we denote spherically symmetric functions in
S^{Z}(r^{k})
, and by
S^{Z}_{der}(r^{k})
the space of functions so that

f\in S^{Z}(r^{k}),\quad{\partial}f\in S^{Z}(r^{k-1}).

Given a vector
g
, we will also use the notation

f\in S^{Z}(r^{k})g

to mean that

f=\sum h_{i}g_{i},\quad h_{i}\in S^{Z}(r^{k}),

and similarly for
S^{Z}_{rad}(r^{k})
,
S^{Z}_{der}(r^{k})
.

In particular, a quick computation yields that

(2.1)

g_{K}^{\alpha\beta}-g_{S}^{\alpha\beta}\in S^{Z}_{der}(r^{-2}).

Note that due to (
2.1
) we also have

(2.2)

\Box_{g_{K}}u-\Box_{g_{S}}u\lesssim r^{-2}(|{\partial}^{2}u|+|{\partial}u|).

The main theorem of the paper is the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p\geq 3
be an integer. Assume that
\phi
solves the wave equation

(2.3)

\Box_{K}\phi=\pm\phi^{p},\qquad\phi|_{\Sigma^{-}}=\phi_{0},\qquad\tilde{T}\phi|_{\Sigma^{-}}=\phi_{1}\ .

Let
\kappa=\min\{2,p-2\}
. Fix
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
R_{1}>r_{e}
. Then there are
N\gg m
and
\varepsilon>0
so that, for any initial data
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
supported in
\{r_{e}\leq r\leq R_{1}\}
with

\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{N+1}}+\|\phi_{1}\|_{H^{N}}\leq\varepsilon,

then
\phi
exists globally in
\mathcal{M}
and satisfies the pointwise bounds

(2.4)

\phi_{\leq m}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\langle{\tilde{t}}\rangle\langle{\tilde{t}}-{\tilde{r}}\rangle^{\kappa}}\ .

2.2.
Local energy norms

We consider a partition
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
into the dyadic sets
A_{R}=\{R\leq\langle r\rangle\leq 2R\}
for

R\geq 1
. We will use the notation
A\lesssim B
to mean that there is a constant
C
independent of
u
and
\varepsilon
so that
|A|\leq CB
; the value of
C
might change from line to line. Similarly,
A\ll B
means that
|A|\leq cB
for a small enough constant
c
.

We now introduce the local energy norm
LE

(2.5)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{LE}&=\sup_{R}\|\langle r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M}\cap{\mathbb{R}}\times A_{R})}\\
\|u\|_{LE[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}&=\sup_{R}\|\langle r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M}\cap[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]\times A_{R})},\end{split}

its
H^{1}
counterpart

(2.6)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{LE^{1}}&=\|\nabla u\|_{LE}+\|\langle r\rangle^{-1}u\|_{LE}\\
\|u\|_{LE^{1}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}&=\|\nabla u\|_{LE[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}+\|\langle r\rangle^{-1}u\|_{LE[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]},\end{split}

as well as the dual norm

(2.7)

\begin{split}\|f\|_{LE^{*}}&=\sum_{R}\|\langle r\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M}\cap{\mathbb{R}}\times A_{R})}\\
\|f\|_{LE^{*}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}&=\sum_{R}\|\langle r\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M}\cap[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]\times A_{R})}.\end{split}

We also define similar norms for higher Sobolev regularity

\begin{split}\|u_{\leq m}\|_{LE^{1}}&=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|u_{\alpha}\|_{LE^{1}}\\
\|u_{\leq m}\|_{LE^{1}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}&=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|u_{\alpha}\|_{LE^{1}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}\\
\|u_{\leq m}\|_{LE[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}&=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|u_{\alpha}\|_{LE[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]},\end{split}

respectively

\begin{split}\|f\|_{LE^{*,k}}&=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{LE^{*}}\\
\|f\|_{LE^{*,k}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}&=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{LE^{*}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}.\end{split}

Finally, we introduce a weaker version of the local energy
decay norm
1
1

1

In Kerr one can actually control a stronger norm, where the
r
-derivative does not degenerate at the trapped set. However, we do not need the stronger norm in this paper.

\begin{split}\|u\|_{LE^{1}_{w}}&=\|(1-\chi_{ps})\nabla u\|_{LE}+\|\langle r\rangle^{-1}u\|_{LE}\\
\|u\|_{LE^{1}_{w}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}&=\|(1-\chi_{ps})\nabla u\|_{LE[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}+\|\langle r\rangle^{-1}u\|_{LE[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]},\end{split}

as well as the dual norms

\begin{split}\|f\|_{LE^{*}_{w}}&=\|\chi_{ps}\nabla f\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}+\|f\|_{LE^{*}}\\
\|f\|_{LE^{*}_{w}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}&=\|\chi_{ps}\nabla f\|_{L^{2}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]L^{2}}+\|f\|_{LE^{*}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}.\end{split}

Here
\chi_{ps}
is a smooth, compactly
supported spatial cutoff function that equals
1
in a neighborhood of the trapped set. We also define the higher order weak norms as above.

We define the (nondegenerate) energy

E[u](\tilde{t})=\int_{\Sigma(\tilde{t})}|{\partial}u|^{2}d\Sigma_{K}(\tilde{t}).

We will need the following local energy estimates, which were proved in
[
39
]
(for derivatives) and
[
23
]
(for vector fields):

(2.8)

\|u_{\leq m}\|_{LE_{w}^{1}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}+\|{\partial}u_{\leq m}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\lesssim E[u_{\leq m}](\tilde{t}_{0})+\|(\Box_{K}u)_{\leq m}\|_{L^{1}L^{2}+LE^{*}_{w}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}.

We will also use a version that replaces the loss near the trapped set with a derivative loss:

(2.9)

\|u_{\leq m}\|_{LE^{1}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}+\|{\partial}u_{\leq m}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\lesssim E[u_{\leq m+1}](\tilde{t}_{0})+\|(\Box_{K}u)_{\leq m+1}\|_{L^{1}L^{2}+LE^{*}[\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}]}.

3.
Local energy decay for the nonlinear problem

In order to prove the main theorem from this section, we will use two results from
[
17
]
. We need the following weighted Sobolev
estimates, see Lemma 3.1 from
[
17
]
:

For
R\geq 10
,
2\leq q<\infty
, and any
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

(3.1)

\|r^{b}v\|_{L_{r}^{\frac{2q(q-1)}{q-2}}L_{\omega}^{\infty}(r\geq R+1)}\lesssim\|r^{b-\frac{1}{q-1}}v_{\leq 6}\|_{L_{r}^{q}L_{\omega}^{2}(r\geq R)},

(3.2)

\|r^{b}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(|x|\geq R+1)}\lesssim\|r^{b-\frac{2}{q}}v_{\leq 6}\|_{L^{q}_{r}L^{2}_{\omega}(r\geq R)}.

Moreover, we will use Theorem 3.2 from
[
17
]
:

Theorem 3.1
.

(Theorem 3.2,
[
17
]
)

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
. Suppose
w
solves

\Box_{K}w=G_{1}+G_{2},\qquad w(0,\cdot)={\partial}_{t}w(0,\cdot)=0,

Additionally, suppose that
w
vanishes in
[0,\infty]\times\{|x|\leq c\}
for some
c>0
.

Then for any
\delta_{1}>0
and
1/2-1/q<s<1/2

(3.3)

\|r^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{4}{q}-s}w\|_{L^{q}L^{q}L^{2}}\lesssim\|r^{-\frac{1}{2}-s}G_{1}\|_{L^{1}L^{1}L^{2}}+\|r^{\frac{3}{2}-s+\delta_{1}}G_{2}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}.

The goal of this section is to prove the following:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
m\geq 6
be a positive integer. Suppose that the initial data
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
satisfies

(3.4)

\|(\phi_{0})_{\leq m+6}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|(\phi_{1})_{\leq m+6}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2},

where
\varepsilon
is small enough. Then the equation (
2.3
) has a global solution that satisfies

(3.5)

\sup_{\tilde{t}}E[\phi_{\leq m}](\tilde{t})+\|\phi_{\leq m}\|_{LE^{1}}\leq c_{m}\varepsilon

(3.6)

\|\phi_{\leq m}\|_{L^{p}L^{2p}}\leq c_{m}\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfy
0\leq\chi(r)\leq 1
,
\chi(r)\equiv 0
for
r\leq R_{1}
, and
\chi(r)\equiv 1
for
r>R_{1}+1
. Pick any
1+\sqrt{2}<q<3
. For
\gamma=\frac{4}{q}-\frac{2}{q-1}=\frac{2(q-2)}{q(q-1)}
, we define

(3.7)

\|\phi\|_{X^{m}}=\|r^{-\gamma}\chi\phi_{\leq m}\|_{L^{q}L^{q}L^{2}}+\|{\partial}\phi_{\leq m}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|\phi_{\leq m-1}\|_{LE^{1}}

(3.8)

\|g\|_{N^{m}}=\|r^{-\gamma q}\chi^{q}g_{\leq m}\|_{L^{1}L^{1}L^{2}}+\|g_{\leq m}\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}.

We first prove the following linear estimate:

(3.9)

\|\phi\|_{X^{m}}\lesssim\|(\phi_{0})_{\leq m+6}\|_{H^{1}}+\|(\phi_{1})_{\leq m+6}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Box_{K}\phi\|_{N^{m}}\ .

Indeed, the last two terms in (
3.7
) can be estimated by using (
2.9
).

In order to estimate the first term, we apply (
3.3
) (with
s=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{2}{q-1}
and small
\delta_{1}
).

Pick any
|\alpha|\leq m
. Note first that, due to the support properties of
\chi
and the initial data, we have

\chi\phi_{\alpha}(0,\cdot)={\partial}_{t}\left(\chi\phi_{\alpha}(0,\cdot)\right)=0.

We have

\Box_{K}(\chi\phi_{\alpha})=\chi(\Box_{K}\phi)_{\alpha}+[\Box_{K},\chi]\phi_{\alpha}+\chi[\Box_{K},Z^{\alpha}]\phi

An easy computation gives

[\Box_{K},{\partial}]\phi\in S^{Z}(r^{-2}){\partial}{\partial}^{\leq 1}\phi,\quad[\Box_{K},\Omega]\phi\in S^{Z}(r^{-2}){\partial}{\partial}^{\leq 1}\phi,

[\Box_{K},S]\phi\in S^{Z}(1)\Box_{K}\phi+S^{Z}(r^{-2+}){\partial}\phi+S^{Z}(r^{-2+}){\partial}\Omega\phi+S^{Z}(r^{-2}){\partial}{\partial}^{\leq 1}\phi,

and thus by induction we obtain that

(3.10)

[\Box_{K},Z^{\alpha}]\phi=F_{1}+F_{2},\quad F_{1}\in S^{Z}(1)(\Box_{K}\phi)_{\leq|\alpha|},\quad F_{2}\in S^{Z}(r^{-2+}){\partial}\phi_{\leq|\alpha|}.

We now pick
G_{1}=\chi(\Box_{K}\phi)_{\alpha}+\chi F_{1}
and
G_{2}=[\Box_{K},\chi]\phi_{\alpha}+\chi F_{2}
.

Since
\chi-\chi^{q}
is supported in
[R,R+1]
, and
\frac{1}{2}+s=\gamma q
, we see that

\|r^{-\frac{1}{2}-s}G_{1}\|_{L^{1}L^{1}L^{2}}\lesssim\|r^{-\gamma q}\chi(\Box_{K}\phi)_{\leq|\alpha|}\|_{L^{1}L^{1}L^{2}}\lesssim\|r^{-\gamma q}\chi^{q}(\Box_{K}\phi)_{\leq|\alpha|}\|_{L^{1}L^{1}L^{2}}+\|(\Box_{K}\phi)_{\leq|\alpha|}\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}.

On the other hand, we see that for small enough
\delta_{1}
:

\|r^{\frac{3}{2}-s+\delta_{1}}\chi F_{2}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}\lesssim\|\chi r^{-\frac{1}{2}-s+\delta_{1}+}{\partial}\phi_{\leq|\alpha|}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}\lesssim\|\phi_{\leq|\alpha|}\|_{LE^{1}}.

We also have that

\|r^{\frac{3}{2}-s+\delta_{1}}[\Box_{K},\chi]\phi_{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}\lesssim\|{\partial}^{\leq 1}\phi_{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(R\leq|x|\leq R+1)}\lesssim\|\phi_{\leq|\alpha|}\|_{LE^{1}}.

Theorem
3.1
now implies (
3.9
).

We now finish the proof of Theorem
3.2
. We want to show that

(3.11)

\|\phi\|_{X^{m}}\leq C_{m}\varepsilon.

It is enough to show that, for any
\psi
,

(3.12)

\|\psi^{p}\|_{N^{m}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{X^{m}}^{p}.

Indeed, let
\psi_{0}\equiv 0
and recursively define
\psi_{k+1}

to be the solution to the linear equation

(3.13)

\Box_{K}\psi_{k+1}=\pm\psi_{k}^{p},\qquad\psi_{k+1}|_{\Sigma^{-}}=\phi_{0},\qquad\tilde{T}\psi_{k+1}|_{\Sigma^{-}}=\phi_{1}.

(
3.9
) and (
3.12
) imply

\|\psi_{k+1}\|_{X^{m}}\lesssim C(\epsilon+\|\psi_{k}\|_{X^{m}}^{p}),

and a continuity argument implies that for small enough
\epsilon
the sequence
\psi_{k}
is Cauchy in
X^{m}
and thus converges to a solution
\phi
that satisfies (
3.11
).

Let us now prove (
3.12
). We have for any
|\alpha|\leq m
:

(3.14)

Z^{\alpha}(\psi^{p})\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha_{1}|\leq\dots\leq|\alpha_{p}|\leq|\alpha|\\
\alpha_{1}+\dots\alpha_{p}=\alpha\end{subarray}}|Z^{\alpha_{1}}\psi|\dots|Z^{\alpha_{p}}\psi|.

In a compact region, we have by Hölder and Sobolev embeddings

\begin{split}&\Bigl\|Z^{\alpha_{1}}\psi\dots Z^{\alpha_{p}}\psi\Bigr\|_{L^{1}L^{2}(r\leq R+2)}\leq\|Z^{\alpha_{p-1}}\psi\|_{L^{2}L^{\infty}(r\leq R+2)}\|Z^{\alpha_{p}}\psi\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(r\leq R+2)}\prod_{i=1}^{p-2}\|Z^{\alpha_{i}}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(r\leq R+2)}\\
&\lesssim\|\psi_{\leq|\alpha_{p-1}|+2}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(r\leq R+3)}\|\psi_{\leq|\alpha_{p}|}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(r\leq R+2)}\|{\partial}\psi_{\leq|\alpha_{p-2}|+1}\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|\psi\|_{X^{\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor+2}}^{p-1}\|\psi\|_{X^{m}}\leq\|\psi\|^{p}_{X^{m}}.\end{split}

On the other hand, for
r\geq R+2
we have, using (
3.1
) (with
b=\frac{\gamma}{q-1}
), (
3.2
) (with
b=\gamma
) and the fact that

:

\begin{split}&\Bigl\|Z^{\alpha_{1}}u\dots Z^{\alpha_{p}}\psi\Bigr\|_{L^{1}L^{2}(r\geq R+2)}\lesssim\|r^{-\gamma}\chi Z^{\alpha_{p}}\psi\|_{L^{q}L^{q}L^{2}}\|r^{\gamma}Z^{\alpha_{1}}\psi\dots Z^{\alpha_{p-1}}\psi\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}L^{\infty}(r\geq R+2)}\\
&\lesssim\|r^{-\gamma}\chi Z^{\alpha_{p}}\psi\|_{L^{q}L^{q}L^{2}}\|\psi_{\leq|\alpha_{p-1}|}\|^{p-q}_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(r\geq R+2)}\|r^{\gamma}(\psi_{\leq|\alpha_{p-1}|})^{q-1}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}L^{\infty}(r\geq R+2)}\\
&\lesssim\|\psi\|_{X^{m}}\|\psi\|^{p-q}_{X^{\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor+6}}\|r^{\frac{\gamma}{q-1}}\psi_{\leq|\alpha_{p-1}|}\|^{q-1}_{L^{q}L^{\frac{2q(q-1)}{q-2}}L^{\infty}(r\geq R+2)}\lesssim\|\psi\|^{1+p-q}_{X^{m}}\|r^{\frac{\gamma-1}{q-1}}\psi_{\leq|\alpha_{p-1}|}\|^{q-1}_{L^{q}L^{q}L^{\infty}(r\geq R+1)}\\
&\lesssim\|\psi\|^{1+p-q}_{X^{m}}\|r^{-\gamma}\chi\psi_{\leq|\alpha_{p-1}|+6}\|^{q-1}_{L^{q}L^{q}L^{2}}\lesssim\|\psi\|^{1+p-q}_{X^{m}}\|\psi\|^{q-1}_{X^{\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor+2}}\lesssim\|\psi\|^{p}_{X^{m}}.\end{split}

Finally,

\begin{split}&\Bigl\|r^{-\gamma q}\chi^{q}Z^{\alpha_{1}}\psi\dots Z^{\alpha_{p}}\psi\Bigr\|_{L^{1}L^{1}L^{2}}\lesssim\|r^{-\gamma}\chi Z^{\alpha_{p}}\psi\|_{L^{q}L^{q}L^{2}}\|r^{-\gamma(q-1)}\chi^{q-1}Z^{\alpha_{1}}\psi\dots Z^{\alpha_{p-1}}\psi\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}L^{\infty}}\\
&\lesssim\|r^{-\gamma}\chi Z^{\alpha_{p}}\psi\|_{L^{q}L^{q}L^{2}}\|\psi_{\leq|\alpha_{p-1}|}\|^{p-q}_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\|r^{-\gamma(q-1)}\chi^{q-1}(\psi_{\leq|\alpha_{p-1}|})^{q-1}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}L^{\infty}}\\
&\lesssim\|\psi\|_{X^{m}}\|\psi\|^{p-q}_{X^{\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor+2}}\|r^{-\gamma}\chi\psi_{\leq|\alpha_{p-1}|}\|^{q-1}_{L^{q}L^{q}L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\psi\|^{p}_{X^{m}}.\end{split}

The proof of (
3.12
) is now complete.

The local energy spaces from the previous section are enough to obtain a weak decay estimate, see Theorem 6.1 from

Theorem 4.1
.

(Theorem 6.1,
[
18
]
)

Let
T
be a fixed time. We then have for

:

(4.1)

|\phi_{\leq_{|\alpha|}}|\leq C_{m}\langle\tilde{t}\rangle^{-1}\langle\tilde{t}-{\tilde{r}}\rangle^{1/2}\|\phi_{\leq|\alpha|+13}\|_{LE^{1}[T,2T]}.

In particular, this implies

(4.2)

|\phi_{\leq|\alpha|}|\lesssim\varepsilon\langle\tilde{t}\rangle^{-1}\langle\tilde{t}-{\tilde{r}}\rangle^{1/2}

for all

One idea to continue here is to rewrite the equation as

\Box\phi=(\Box_{\phi}-\Box_{K})\phi\pm\phi^{p},

and use the fundamental solution for the Minkowski, combined with (
4.2
), to improve the rate of decay. This works for

estimates inspired by the work of Dafermos-Rodnianski

Let
{\partial}_{v}={\partial}_{t}+{\partial}_{\tilde{r}}
, and
{\not\negmedspace\partial}
denote angular derivatives. We introduce the weighted local energy norm
LE_{\gamma}
for
\gamma>0
:

(4.3)

\|\phi\|_{LE_{\gamma}}=\|r^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})},

and its
H^{1}

(4.4)

\|\phi\|_{LE_{\gamma}^{1}}=\|{\partial}_{v}\phi\|_{LE_{\gamma}}+\|{\not\negmedspace\partial}\phi\|_{LE_{\gamma}}+\|r^{-1}\phi\|_{LE_{\gamma}}.

We also define the degenerate norm

\|\phi\|_{LE^{1}_{w,\gamma}}=\|(1-\chi_{ps}){\partial}_{v}\phi\|_{LE_{\gamma}}+\|(1-\chi_{ps}){\not\negmedspace\partial}\phi\|_{LE_{\gamma}}+\|r^{-1}\phi\|_{LE_{\gamma}},

and the weighted energy

E_{\gamma}[\phi](\tilde{t})=\int_{\Sigma(\tilde{t})}r^{\gamma}\left(|{\partial}_{v}\phi|^{2}+|{\not\negmedspace\partial}\phi|^{2}+r^{-2}\phi^{2}\right)d\Sigma_{K}(\tilde{t}).

For the dual norm, we define

(4.5)

\|f\|_{LE_{\gamma}^{*}}=\|r^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}f\|_{LE^{*}}.

We will prove the following linear estimate:

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
\phi
solves

\Box_{K}\phi=F,\qquad\phi|_{{\tilde{t}}=0}=\phi_{0},\qquad\tilde{T}\phi|_{{\tilde{t}}=0}=\phi_{1}\ .

Then for any
0<\gamma<2
and compactly supported
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
we have

(4.6)

\sup_{\tilde{t}}E_{\gamma}[\phi](\tilde{t})+\|\phi\|_{LE^{1}_{w,\gamma}}^{2}\lesssim E[\phi](0)+E[\phi](T)+\|\phi\|^{2}_{LE^{1}_{w}[0,T]}+\|F\|^{2}_{LE_{\gamma}^{*}}.

A similar result appears in a paper by Stogin
[
32
]
.

Proof.

Recall that the energy-momentum tensor is given by

Q_{\alpha\beta}[g]=\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial_{\mu}\phi.

Its contraction with respect to a vector field
X
is denoted by

P_{\alpha}[g,X]=Q_{\alpha\beta}[g]X^{\beta}.

Its divergence is given by

\nabla^{\alpha}P_{\alpha}[g,X]=\Box_{g}\phi\cdot X\phi+\frac{1}{2}Q[g,X],\qquad Q[g,X]=Q_{\alpha\beta}[g]\pi_{X}^{\alpha\beta},

where
\pi_{X}^{\alpha\beta}
is the deformation tensor of
X
, which is given in terms of the Lie derivative by

\pi_{\alpha\beta}^{X}=\nabla_{\alpha}X_{\beta}+\nabla_{\beta}X_{\alpha}=({\mathcal{L}}_{X}g)_{\alpha\beta}.

In coordinates, one can write

(4.7)

Q[g,X]=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}(X(\sqrt{|g|}g^{\alpha\beta}))\partial_{\alpha}\phi\,\partial_{\beta}\phi\\
+(g^{\alpha\gamma}\partial_{\gamma}X^{\beta}+g^{\beta\gamma}\partial_{\gamma}X^{\alpha})\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi-\partial_{\gamma}X^{\gamma}\,g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\,\partial_{\beta}\phi.

More generally, for a vector field

X
, a scalar function
q
and a one-form
m
, we define

P_{\alpha}[g,X,q,m]=P_{\alpha}[g,X]+q\phi\partial_{\alpha}\phi-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\alpha}q)\phi^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m_{\alpha}\phi^{2}.

The divergence of
P
is

(4.8)

\nabla^{\alpha}P_{\alpha}[g,X,q,m]=\Box_{g}\phi\Bigl(X\phi+qu\Bigr)+Q[g,X,q,m],

where

Q[g,X,q,m]=\frac{1}{2}Q[g,X]+q\partial^{\alpha}\phi\,\partial_{\alpha}\phi+m_{\alpha}\phi\,\partial^{\alpha}\phi+(\nabla^{\alpha}m_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}q)\,\phi^{2}.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{[0,T]}=\mathcal{M}\cap[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. The divergence theorem yields, assuming that
X
,
q
and
m
are supported in
\{r>4M\}
, that

(4.9)

\int_{\mathcal{M}_{[0,T]}}Q[g,X,q,m]dV_{g}=-\int_{\mathcal{M}_{[0,T]}}\Box_{K}\phi\left(X\phi+q\phi\right)dV_{g}+BDR^{g},

where
BDR^{g}
denotes the boundary terms

BDR^{g}=\int_{\Sigma(\tilde{t})}\langle d\tilde{t},P[g,X,q,m]\rangle d\Sigma\Big|_{0}^{T}.

Even though one can do the following computations in Kerr, it is easier to first do the Schwarzschild case, and treat Kerr perturbatively.

Fix
\gamma<2
and pick
0<\delta
small so that

(4.10)

(1-\delta)^{2}-2(1-\gamma\delta)<0.

Let

X=r^{\gamma}{\partial}_{v},\qquad q(r)=r^{\gamma-1}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)},\qquad m=\gamma(1-\delta)r^{\gamma-2}dv.

We compute, using the fact that
{\partial}_{{\tilde{r}}}={\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}{\partial}_{r}

\begin{split}&P_{0}[g_{S},X,q,m]=\frac{r^{\gamma}}{2}\left(|{\partial}_{t}\phi|^{2}+|{\partial}_{\tilde{r}}\phi|^{2}+{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}|{\not\negmedspace\partial}\phi|^{2}+2{\partial}_{t}\phi{\partial}_{\tilde{r}}\phi\right)+r^{\gamma-1}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}\phi{\partial}_{t}\phi+\frac{\gamma(1-\delta)r^{\gamma-2}}{2}\phi^{2}\\
&=\frac{r^{\gamma}}{2}\left(|{\partial}_{v}\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}\phi\right)^{2}+\frac{r^{\gamma}}{2}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}|{\not\negmedspace\partial}\phi|^{2}-r^{\gamma-1}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}^{2}\left(\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi+\frac{1}{2r}\phi^{2}\right)+\frac{\gamma(1-\delta)r^{\gamma-2}}{2}\phi^{2}.\end{split}

Recall that for the Schwarzschild metric
dV_{S}=r^{2}d\tilde{t}drd\omega
, and
d\Sigma_{S}=r^{2}drd\omega
. Let
R_{2}
be large enough, and
\chi_{R_{2}}(r)
be a smooth cutoff equal to
1
when
r\geq R_{2}
and supported in
\{r\geq\frac{R_{2}}{2}\}
. After integrating by parts we obtain

\begin{split}&-\int_{\Sigma(\tilde{t})}\langle d\tilde{t},P[g_{S},\chi_{R_{2}}X,\chi_{R_{2}}q,\chi_{R_{2}}m]\rangle d\Sigma_{S}=\int_{\Sigma(\tilde{t})}\chi_{R_{2}}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}^{-1}\left[\frac{r^{\gamma}}{2}\left({\partial}_{v}\phi+\frac{1}{r}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}\phi\right)^{2}\right.\\
&+\frac{r^{\gamma}}{2}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}|{\not\negmedspace\partial}\phi|^{2}-r^{\gamma-1}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}^{2}\left(\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi+\frac{1}{2r}\phi^{2}\right)+\frac{\gamma(1-\delta)r^{\gamma-2}}{2}\phi^{2}\Bigl.\Bigr]d\Sigma_{S}=\\
&\int_{\Sigma(\tilde{t})}\chi_{R_{2}}\frac{r^{\gamma}}{2}\left[{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}^{-1}\left({\partial}_{t}\phi+{\partial}_{\tilde{r}}\phi+\frac{1}{r}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}\phi\right)^{2}+|{\not\negmedspace\partial}\phi|^{2}+\left(\gamma(2-\delta)-\frac{2M\gamma}{r}\right)\frac{\phi^{2}}{r^{2}}\right]\\
&+\chi_{R_{2}}^{\prime}\frac{r^{\gamma-1}-2Mr^{\gamma-2}}{2}\phi^{2}d\Sigma_{S}.\end{split}

Since the support of
\chi_{R_{2}}^{\prime}
is contained in
\{\frac{R_{2}}{2}\leq r\leq R_{2}\}
, we have by Hardy’s inequality that

\int_{\Sigma(\tilde{t})}\chi_{R_{2}}^{\prime}\frac{r^{\gamma-1}-2Mr^{\gamma-2}}{2}\phi^{2}d\Sigma_{S}\lesssim E[\phi](\tilde{t}).

Moreover, due to (
2.1
) we see that

Q_{\alpha\beta}[g_{K}]-Q_{\alpha\beta}[g_{S}]\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2}}|{\partial}\phi|^{2},

which immediately implies, since
\gamma<2
, that

\langle d\tilde{t},P[g_{S},\chi_{R_{2}}X,\chi_{R_{2}}q,\chi_{R_{2}}m]\rangle-\langle d\tilde{t},P[g_{K},\chi_{R_{2}}X,\chi_{R_{2}}q,\chi_{R_{2}}m]\rangle\lesssim r^{\gamma-2}|{\partial}\phi|^{2}\lesssim|{\partial}\phi|^{2}.

Finally, we have that
\sqrt{|g_{K}|}\approx\sqrt{|g_{S}|}
.

We thus have

(4.11)

E_{\gamma}[\phi](\tilde{t})\lesssim-\int_{\Sigma(\tilde{t})}\langle d\tilde{t},P[g_{K},\chi_{R_{2}}X,\chi_{R_{2}}q,\chi_{R_{2}}m]\rangle d\Sigma_{K}+E[\phi](\tilde{t}).

Moreover, since the initial data has compact support, we clearly have that when
\tilde{t}=0
:

(4.12)

-\int_{\Sigma(0)}\langle d\tilde{t},P[g_{K},\chi_{R_{2}}X,\chi_{R_{2}}q,\chi_{R_{2}}m]\rangle d\Sigma_{K}\approx E[\phi](0).

We now compute the spacetime term. Using (
4.7
) we have for any
f(r)
:

Q[g_{S},f(r){\partial}_{t}]=2f^{\prime}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}{\partial}_{t}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi

\begin{split}Q\left[g_{S},{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}f(r){\partial}_{r}\right]=&f^{\prime}({\partial}_{t}\phi)^{2}+f^{\prime}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}^{2}({\partial}_{r}\phi)^{2}+\left[2f\left(1-\frac{3M}{r}\right)-{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}f^{\prime}\right]|{\not\negmedspace\partial}\phi|^{2}\\
&-\frac{1}{2r}{\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)}f{\partial}^{\gamma}\phi{\partial}_{\gamma}\phi\end{split}

and thus

Q[g_{S},X,q,0]=\frac{\gamma r^{\gamma-1}}{2}({\partial}_{v}\phi)^{2}+r^{\gamma-1}\left(\frac{2-\gamma}{2}+\frac{(\gamma-3)M}{r}\right)|{\not\negmedspace\partial}\phi|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\Box_{g_{S}}q.

We also compute

-\frac{1}{2}\Box_{g_{S}}q=\gamma(1-\gamma)r^{\gamma-3}\left(1+O(\frac{1}{r})\right),

which unfortunately has the wrong sign when
\gamma>1
. On the other hand, we have

\nabla^{\alpha}m_{\alpha}=(1-\delta)\gamma^{2}r^{\gamma-3}\left(1+O(\frac{1}{r})\right)

and thus

\nabla^{\alpha}m_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\Box_{g_{S}}q=\gamma(1-\delta\gamma)r^{\gamma-3}\left(1+O(\frac{1}{r})\right).

Due to (
4.10
) we see that

\frac{\gamma r^{\gamma-1}}{2}({\partial}_{v}\phi)^{2}+\gamma(1-\delta)r^{\gamma-2}\phi{\partial}_{v}\phi+\gamma(1-\delta\gamma)r^{\gamma-3}\phi^{2}\gtrsim r^{\gamma-1}({\partial}_{v}\phi)^{2}+r^{\gamma-3}\phi^{2}

and thus for
R_{2}
large enough and

we have

Q[g_{S},X,q,m]\gtrsim r^{\gamma-1}\left(|{\partial}_{v}\phi|^{2}+|{\not\negmedspace\partial}\phi|^{2}\right)+r^{\gamma-3}\phi^{2}.

Due to the support properties of
\chi_{R_{2}}
we get

(4.13)

\int_{\mathcal{M}_{[0,T]}}Q[g_{S},\chi_{R_{2}}X,\chi_{R_{2}}q,\chi_{R_{2}}m]dV_{K}\gtrsim\|\phi\|^{2}_{LE^{1}_{w,\gamma}[0,T]}-\|\phi\|^{2}_{LE^{1}_{w}[0,T]}.

Using (
2.1
) we see that

(4.14)

Q[g_{S},\chi_{R_{2}}X,\chi_{R_{2}}q,\chi_{R_{2}}m]-Q[g_{K},\chi_{R_{2}}X,\chi_{R_{2}}q,\chi_{R_{2}}m]\lesssim\chi_{R_{2}/2}\left(r^{\gamma-3}|{\partial}\phi|^{2}+r^{\gamma-4}|\phi|^{2}\right).

We thus obtain from (

), (
4.11
), (
4.12

E_{\gamma}[\phi](T)+\|\phi\|^{2}_{LE^{1}_{w,\gamma}[0,T]}\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{M}_{[0,T]}}\left|(\Box_{K}\phi)\chi_{R_{2}}\left(X\phi+q\phi\right)\right|dV_{K}+E[\phi](0)+E[\phi](T)+\|\phi\|^{2}_{LE^{1}_{w}[0,T]}.

|\alpha|\leq N

(5.7)

\|\phi_{0}^{\alpha}\|_{H^{1}}+\|\phi_{1}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon.

{\tilde{r}}

{\tilde{r}}

\Box

(5.10)

\Box\psi_{2}=F_{\alpha},\qquad\psi_{1}|_{{\tilde{t}}=0}=0,\qquad{\partial}_{t}\psi_{1}|_{{\tilde{t}}=0}=0.

By finite speed of propagation,

and

The goal of this section is to prove pointwise estimates for solutions to the inhomogeneous wave equation on Minkowski backgrounds.

We will first prove the following lemma, which gives pointwise bounds for the solution assuming the inhomogeneity lies in certain weighted

For any
\beta,\gamma,\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}

L^{\infty}

(6.1)

\|G\|_{L_{\beta,\gamma,\eta}^{\infty}}=\|\langle r\rangle^{\beta}\langle t\rangle^{\gamma}\langle t-r\rangle^{\eta}H(t,r)\|_{L_{t,r}^{\infty}},\quad H(t,r)=\sum_{0}^{2}\|\Omega^{i}G(t,r\omega)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})}.

Lemma 6.1
.

i) Let
\psi

(6.2)

\Box\psi=G,\qquad\psi(0)=0,\quad{\partial}_{t}\psi(0)=0,

where

Proof.

We use the ideas from

Atiyah classes of Lie algebroid homotopy modules
Panagiotis Batakidis batakidis@math.auth.gr, Sylvain Lavau lavau@math.univ-lyon1.fr

Abstract
For a Lie algebroid pair A ↪ L A\hookrightarrow L we study cocycles constructed from the extension to L L of the higher connection forms of a representation up to homotopy E E of the Lie algebroid A A . We show that there exists a cohomology class with values in the endomorphism bundle of E E that is independent of the extension above and vanishes whenever a homotopy A A -compatible extension exists. Whenever the representation up to homotopy E E is the resolution of a Lie algebroid representation K K of A A , it is shown that there exists a quasi-isomorphism sending the new Atiyah class to the classical one, associated to extensions to L L of the Lie algebroid representation K K .



Keywords:
Lie pairs; representations up to homotopy; Atiyah class; graded geometry.

MSC (2020): 53C05, 58J10, 58A15, 16S32, 17B35, 17B55, 70G45.

Notation:
Throughout this paper, the base manifold (body) is a smooth manifold
M
. A Lie pair will always be denoted as
A\hookrightarrow L
, so that
A
is a Lie subalgebroid of
L
. Graded vector bundles, and more specifically representations up to homotopy of
A
, are denoted by
E
. A
(split) graded vector bundle
over
M
is a family
E=(E_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
of vector bundles over
M
, labelled over
\mathbb{Z}
. We will assume that the label
i
– that we call the
degree of
E_{i}
– is bounded above and below. Following
[
Kotov and Salnikov, 2024
]
, we say that such a graded vector bundle

is of

, i.e. that all but a finite number of

are zero, and those which are not, are finite dimensional. The main consequence of this assumption is that tensor product stabilizes the class of split finite dimensional graded vector bundles over

, and that the graded vector bundle
\mathrm{End}(E)
of endomorphisms of

is of finite dimension as well. In this paper we always consider (split) graded vector bundles of finite dimension.

Atiyah classes of representations up to homotopy of Lie algebroid pairs

2.1
Superconnections and Lie algebroid homotopy modules

Let
M
be a smooth manifold,
(A,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebroid over
M
.
The sheaf
\Omega(A)
of forms on
A
is equipped with the Lie algebroid differential defined by the formula

\displaystyle d_{A}(u)(a_{0},\ldots,a_{p})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\,(-1)^{i}\rho(a_{i})\big(u(a_{0},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,a_{p})\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq p}\,(-1)^{i+j}u\big([a_{i},a_{j}],a_{0},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{a_{j}},\ldots,a_{p}\big),

for
u\in\Omega^{p}(A)
and
a_{0},\ldots,a_{p}\in\Gamma(A)
. In other words, the Lie algebroid differential is the pullback through
\rho^{*}
of the de Rham derivative,
d_{A}=\rho^{*}d_{\mathrm{dR}}
.

Let
E
be a (split, finite dimensional) graded vector bundle over
M
, that is to say: a family of vector bundles
(E_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
indexed over
\mathbb{Z}
, such that all but a finite number of
E_{i}
are zero, and those which are not are finite dimensional. We say that
E
is positively (resp. negatively) graded if the index
i
is concentrated in positive (resp. negative) integers. Let us define
(m,M)
to be the unique pair of integers satisfying the two conditions:

•

E_{m}\neq 0
, and
E_{i}=0
for any
i\leq m-1
;

•

E_{M}\neq 0
, and
E_{i}=0
for any
i\geq M+1
.

In particular,
m\leq M
, and the positive integer
M_{E}=M-m+1
is called the
amplitude of
E
.
Let
\mathrm{End}_{i}(E)
be the vector bundle of endomorphisms of
E
of degree
i
– i.e. those (degree 0) vector bundle morphisms
E_{\bullet}\rightarrow E_{\bullet+i}
. Denote by
\mathrm{End}_{\bullet}(E)=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{End}_{j}(E)
the graded vector bundle of graded endomorphisms of
E
. It is well defined as we have assumed
E
to be of finite dimension. The direct sum is actually bounded above by
M_{E}-1
and below by
-M_{E}+1
.

The sheaf
\Omega(A,E)
of differential forms on
A
valued in the graded vector bundle
E
is bigraded: one grading is by form degree (or
arity
) on
A
and the other is by the total degree of the form, i.e. a
k
-form
\omega
on
A
valued in
E_{i}
is said to be of total degree
t=k+i
, and we write
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(A,E)_{t}=\Omega^{k}(A,E_{t-k})
. The total degree is bounded above and below since
A
is of finite rank and
E
is of finite dimension. We denote by
\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E)_{\blacktriangle}
the following direct sum of sheaves:

\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E)_{\blacktriangle}=\bigoplus_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\mathrm{rk}(A)}\Omega^{k}(A,E)_{t}.

The sum is finite as we assumed that the graded vector bundle is of finite dimension. Indeed, the lower bound on
t
is
m
, while the upper bound is
\mathrm{rk}(A)+M
.
When we want to emphasize the role of the total degree
t
, we omit the
\bullet
exponent and only write
\Omega(A,E)_{\blacktriangle}
.
The Lie algebroid differential
d_{A}
can be straightfowardly extended to
\Omega(A,E)
or
\Omega(A,\mathrm{End}(E))
, by acting on the
A
-form part only.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\eta\in\Omega(A,\mathrm{End}(E))
be of total degree
t_{\eta}
. By definition of the amplitude of the (split, finite dimensional) graded vector bundle
E
, the total degree of
\eta
is bounded above and below:
-M_{E}+1\leq t_{\eta}\leq\mathrm{rk}(A)+M_{E}-1
.
Then
\eta

splits into a finite sum

\eta=\sum_{k=0}^{\mathrm{rk}(A)}\eta^{(k)},

(1)

where each component
\eta^{(k)}\colon\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E)_{\blacktriangle}\to\Omega^{\bullet+k}(A,E)_{\blacktriangle+t_{\eta}}
is a
k
-form on
A
valued in
\mathrm{End(E)}_{t_{\eta}-k}
. We see that whenever
t_{\eta}=-M_{E}+1
only the component
\eta^{(0)}
contributes to the sum, while whenever
t_{\eta}=\mathrm{rk}(A)+M_{E}-1
only the component
\eta^{(\mathrm{rk}(A))}
does.

Moreover, if the amplitude of
E
is higher than
1
, then whenever
t_{\eta}=0
(resp.
t_{\eta}=1
), the sum (
1
) is bounded above by
\mathrm{min}(\mathrm{rk}(A),M_{E}-1)
(resp.
\mathrm{min}(\mathrm{rk}(A),M_{E})
).
In both cases, we can however adopt the convention that the sum ranges from 0 to
\mathrm{rk}(A)
, keeping in mind that that some of its components identically vanish if
\mathrm{rk(A)}\geq M_{E}
(resp.
\mathrm{rk(A)}\geq M_{E}+1
) – see the examples in Section
3
where this phenomenon occurs.

Let
E=E_{0}
be a vector bundle over
M
. A (linear)
A
-connection on
E
is an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear map
\nabla:\;\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
such that
\nabla_{fa}e=f\nabla_{a}e
and
\nabla_{a}fe=f\nabla_{a}e+\rho(a)(f)e
, for every smooth function
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
. The
A
-connection
\nabla
on
E
can be equivalently described by an exterior covariant derivative
d^{\nabla}_{A}:\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E)\to\Omega^{\bullet+1}(A,E)
such that

d^{\nabla}_{A}(\eta\otimes e)=d_{A}(\eta)\otimes e+(-1)^{m}\eta\otimes\nabla(e),

(2)

for every
\eta\in\Omega^{m}(A)
and
e\in\Gamma(E)
. It extends to an
\Omega(A)
-derivation on
\Omega(A,E)
by the graded Leibniz rule

d^{\nabla}_{A}(\eta\wedge u)=d_{A}(\eta)\wedge u+(-1)^{m}\eta\wedge d^{\nabla}_{A}(u),

for every
\eta\in\Omega(A),\;u\in\Omega(A,E)
, and in local sections is defined by the formula

\displaystyle d^{\nabla}_{A}(u)(a_{0},\ldots,a_{p})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\,(-1)^{i}\nabla_{a_{i}}\big(u(a_{0},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,a_{p})\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq p}\,(-1)^{i+j}u\big([a_{i},a_{j}],a_{0},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{a_{j}},\ldots,a_{p}\big).

The curvature associated to the connection
\nabla
is a
\mathrm{End}(E)
-valued 2-form on
A
, defined by the usual formula:
R_{\nabla}(a,b)=[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}]-\nabla_{[a,b]}
. The connection
\nabla
is
flat
when the associated curvature
R_{\nabla}
vanishes, or, equivalently, when
(d^{\nabla}_{A})^{2}=0
. In this case,
d^{\nabla}_{A}
defines a chain complex structure on
\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E)
, whose associated (Chevalley-Eilenberg) cohomology is denoted by
H^{\bullet}(A,E)
, and

is said to be a

A
, or an
A
-module
.

Flat Lie algebroid connections on vector bundles
E=E_{0}
characterize representations of Lie algebroids. Passing to the graded context, flatness of a Lie algebroid connection makes sense only
up to homotopy
, justifying the introduction of the following notion.

Definition 2.2
.

[
Quillen, 1985
]

Let
A
be a Lie algebroid over
M
and
E=(E_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a (split, finite dimensional) graded vector bundle over
M
. We call
A
-superconnection
any differential operator
D_{A}:\Omega(A,E)_{\blacktriangle}\to\Omega(A,E)_{\blacktriangle+1}
satisfying the Leibniz rule

D_{A}(\eta\otimes u)=d_{A}(\eta)\otimes u+(-1)^{m}\eta\otimes D_{A}(u),

for every
\eta\in\Omega(A),\;u\in\Omega(A,E)
.

Any
A
-superconnection
D_{A}
splits into a finite sum

D_{A}=\sum_{k=0}^{\mathrm{rk}(A)}D_{A}^{(k)},

(3)

where we used the convention on the sum introduced in Remark
2.1
.
For
k=1
, the operator
D_{A}^{(1)}
is the exterior covariant derivative
d_{A}^{\nabla}
associated to some
A
-connection
\nabla
.
Given a choice of open covering
(U_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of
M
trivializing both
A
and
E
(this is possible since
E
is split of finite dimension), there exists a family
(\omega^{(1)}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of locally defined 1-forms
\omega^{(1)}_{\alpha}\in\Omega^{1}\big(A\big|_{U_{\alpha}},\mathrm{End}_{0}(E)\big|_{U_{\alpha}}\big)
such that on the overlap
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
they satisfiy the conditions

\omega^{(1)}_{\beta}=g_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}d_{A}g_{\alpha\beta}+g_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}\omega^{(1)}_{\alpha}g_{\alpha\beta}.

(4)

Here
g_{\alpha\beta}
represents the transition map of
E
over
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
.
Such a family of locally defined 1-forms
(\omega^{(1)}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
satisfying Equation (
4
) on overlaps is abusively called a
connection 1-form
and is denoted by
\omega^{(1)}
in the rest of the text.

For every
k\neq 1
, the restriction of the action of
D_{A}^{(k)}
on
\Omega^{0}(A,E)=\Gamma(E_{\bullet})
canonically induces an
\mathrm{End}_{1-k}(E)
-valued

-form
\omega_{A}^{(k)}
on
A

D_{A}^{(k)}\colon\Gamma(E_{\bullet})\longrightarrow\Omega^{k}(A,E_{\bullet+1-k})\qquad\longleftrightarrow\qquad\omega_{A}^{(k)}\in\Omega^{k}\big(A,\mathrm{End}_{1-k}(E)\big),

by the relation
D_{A}^{(k)}(e)(a_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge a_{k})=\omega_{A}^{(k)}(a_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge a_{k})(e)
.
The correspondence between
D_{A}^{(k)}
and
\omega_{A}^{(k)}
is described in the following equation:

D_{A}^{(k)}(u)=\omega_{A}^{(k)}\wedge u,

(5)

for every
u\in\Omega(A,E)
. Here, the wedge product means that we apply a concatenation on the
A
-form part of

, and that
\omega^{(k)}_{A}
acts as an endomorphism on the part of

sitting in
E
. Namely, for
\eta\otimes\alpha\in\Omega^{p}(A,\mathrm{End}_{i}(E))
and

, one has:

(\eta\otimes\alpha)\wedge(\omega\otimes e)=(-1)^{iq}(\eta\wedge\omega)\otimes\alpha(e).

In particular, the 0-form

.

the

-valued

.

This discussion establishes that an

In analogy with Lie algebroid representations, whenever

(7)

Remark 2.4
.

The bracket in Equations (

[\omega,\omega^{\prime}]=\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime}-(-1)^{mn}\omega^{\prime}\wedge\omega.

The wedge symbol means that we take the wedge product on the form part and the endomorphism composition on the

In particular, for sections

and

Since

and

2.2

A

, or

Lemma 2.5

Let

on

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(L,E)_{\blacktriangle}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(L,E)_{\blacktriangle+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(A,E)_{\blacktriangle}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(A,E)_{\blacktriangle+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{L}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{A}}

The difference between two choices

and

∎

•

associated to this

•

As in the classical theory of linear connections,

For

•

For

.

Since

, there is a priori no reason whatsoever that

, namely, that

is a homotopy

Denote as

There is a total degree on

\displaystyle s^{(k)}(\varpi)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k+p};l)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!p!}\Big(\omega_{A}^{(k)}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\circ\varpi(a_{k+1},\ldots,a_{k+p};l)

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{p+q+1+k(p+1)}\varpi(a_{k+1},\ldots,a_{k+p};l)\circ\omega_{A}^{(k)}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})

We denote

\displaystyle d^{\widehat{\nabla}}_{A}\varpi(a_{0},\ldots,a_{p};l)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\,(-1)^{i}\big[\nabla_{a_{i}},\varpi(a_{0},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,a_{p};l)\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq p}\,(-1)^{i+j}\varpi\big([a_{i},a_{j}],a_{0},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{a_{j}},\ldots,a_{p};l\big)

Lemma 2.6

The differential operator

Lemma 2.7

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{p+q+1+k(p+1)}\omega(a_{k+1},\ldots,a_{k+p},\ell)\circ\omega_{L}^{(k)}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})

\displaystyle\varphi^{(1)}\wedge\omega(a_{0},\ldots,a_{p},\ell)=\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\Big((-1)^{p+1}

The dual morphism

From this, we deduce that

are isomorphic as differential graded

-modules, through a

The first page of the spectral sequence associated to the filtered chain complex
(\widehat{\Omega}(E),s)

Remark 3.5
.

When
A=F,L=TM
is the Lie pair associated to an involutive subbundle corresponding to a regular foliation by Frobenius’ Theorem, the Atiyah class
\mathrm{at}^{Bott}
recovers the
Molino class

[
Chen et al., 2016
]
. Denote
\mathcal{L}
to be the leaf space of this foliation. This topological space is not necessarily a smooth manifold. The Bott connection is then a prescription of how to identify fibers of the normal bundle
\nu(F)=TM/F
over each leaf. The topological space of equivalence classes of elements of
\nu(F)
with respect to the Bott connection is a fiber bundle over the leaf space – denoted
\nu_{\mathcal{L}}
, which can be understood as the ‘tangent bundle’ of
\mathcal{L}
since, whenever
\mathcal{L}
is a smooth manifold, the former is canonically isomorphic to the latter.

Now, assume that the leaf space is a smooth manifold, and thus admits a tangent bundle.
The Molino class of the Bott connection being zero then means that the (extended) Bott
TM
-connection
\nabla
on the normal bundle
\nu(F)
is ‘projectable’ to a
T\mathcal{L}
-connection on
\nu_{\mathcal{L}}\simeq T\mathcal{L}
(not necessarily a representation).
If the regular foliation is
stricly simple
– i.e. if the leaf space
\mathcal{L}
is smooth and comes from a submersion
M\to\mathcal{L}
with connected fibers – then every
T\mathcal{L}
-connection on
T\mathcal{L}\simeq\nu_{\mathcal{L}}
can be lifted to a
TM
-connection on
\nu(F)
extending the Bott connection. In other words, it is always possible to find a projectable connection so in that case the Molino class is always zero.

and connection 2-form

\displaystyle=[\nabla^{\prime E}_{\ell},\partial]=0,

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime(1)}_{E}(a;l)

\displaystyle=R_{\nabla^{\prime E}}(a,\ell)+[\partial,\omega^{\prime(2)}_{L}](a,\ell)=0,

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime(2)}_{E}(a,b;l)

\displaystyle=d_{L}^{\nabla^{\prime E}}\omega^{\prime(2)}_{L}(a,b,\ell)+[\partial,\omega^{\prime(3)}_{L}](a,b,\ell)=0,

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime(3)}_{E}(a,b,c;l)

\displaystyle=d^{\nabla^{\prime E}}\omega_{L}^{\prime(3)}(a,b,c,\ell)+\omega_{L}^{\prime(2)}\wedge\omega_{L}^{\prime(2)}(a,b,c,\ell)=0,

4.1
Generalities about graded geometry and
Q
-connections

A graded manifold
\mathcal{M}
is a locally ringed space whose structure sheaf
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is locally of the form
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(U)\otimes\widehat{S}(V^{*})
, where
U\subset M
is an open subset of a given smooth manifold
M
– called the
base
or
body
– and
\widehat{S}(V^{*})
stands for the formal power series over the dual of a graded vector space
V=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{i}
. We refer to
[
Fairon, 2017
,
Kotov and Salnikov, 2024
]
for an explanation of why one should use formal power series instead of mere polynomials (which are not local rings). We say that
\mathcal{M}
is positively (resp. negatively) graded if the formal power series over
V^{*}
are positively (resp. negatively) graded, namely, if
V
is a negatively (resp. positively) graded vector space. If the graded manifold is (non-) positively or (non-) negatively graded, which will be the case in the present section, using formal power series is equivalent to using polynomials.

Following
[
Mehta, 2006
, Remark 2.2.1]
, one should mainly consider graded vector spaces of finite dimension, such that all but a finite number of the
V_{i}
are zero, and those which are not are finite dimensional. This choice allows performing tensor product operations in the category of graded vector spaces. This assumption is in line with the choice of graded vector bundles made in Section
2
.
Batchelor’s theorem establishes that every positively (resp. negatively) graded manifold is locally isomorphic to a split negatively (resp. positively) graded vector bundle over a smooth manifold. For a generalization of Batchelor’s statement to gradings over the integers, we refer to
[
Kotov and Salnikov, 2024
]
.
While in Section
2
we worked with (split, finite dimensional) graded vector bundles over smooth manifolds, we refer to
[
Mehta, 2009
,
Mehta et al., 2015
]
for details about defining graded vector bundles over a graded manifold
\mathcal{M}
in full generality. We will not need those apart from settling the conventions.

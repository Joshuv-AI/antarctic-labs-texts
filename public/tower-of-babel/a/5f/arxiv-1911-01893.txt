Abstract

This thesis concerns the study of the Bredon cohomological and geometric
dimensions of a discrete group
G
with respect to a family
\mathfrak{F}
of subgroups of
G
.
With that purpose, we focus on building finite-dimensional models for
\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}\left(G\right)
.
The cases of the family
\mathfrak{Fin}
of finite subgroups of a group and the family
\mathfrak{VC}
of virtually
cyclic subgroups of a group have been widely studied and many tools have been developed
to relate the classifying spaces for
\mathfrak{VC}
with those for
\mathfrak{Fin}
.

Given a discrete group
G
and an ascending chain

\mathfrak{F}_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{n}\subseteq\ldots
of families of subgroups
of
G
, we provide a recursive methodology to build models for
\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{F}_{r}}\left(G\right)
and give
certain conditions under which the models obtained are finite-dimensional. We provide
upper bounds for both the Bredon cohomological and geometric dimensions of
G
with
respect to the families
\left(\mathfrak{F}_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
utilising the classifying spaces
obtained.

We consider then the families
\mathfrak{H}_{r}
of virtually polycyclic subgroups of Hirsch length
less than or equal to
r
, for
r\in\mathbb{N}
. We apply the results obtained for chains of families
of subgroups to the chain
\mathfrak{H}_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{1}\subseteq\ldots
for an arbitrary virtually
polycyclic group
G
, proving that the corresponding Bredon dimensions are both bounded above
by
h(G)+r
, where
h(G)
is the Hirsch length of
G
.

Finally, we give similar results for the same chain of families of subgroups and an arbitrary locally
virtually polycyclic group as the ambient group, obtaining in this case the upper bound
h(G)+r+1
.

Chapter 0
Introduction

Given a group
G
, we say that a non-empty collection
\mathfrak{F}
of subgroups of
G
is a
family
if it
is closed under conjugation and taking subgroups. In this configuration, a
G
-CW-complex
X
is a model
for
E_{\mathfrak{F}}G
or a classifying space for the family
\mathfrak{F}
if for each subgroup
H\leq G
, the set of
points of
X
that
H
fixes is contractible if
H\in\mathfrak{F}
and empty otherwise.

A universal property holds for such spaces, meaning that if
X
is a model for
E_{\mathfrak{F}}G
and
Y

is any
G
-CW-complex with stabilizers in
\mathfrak{F}
, there is a
G
-map
f:Y\to X
unique up to
homotopy. In other words, a model for
E_{\mathfrak{F}}G
is a terminal object in the homotopy category of

G
-CW-complexes with stabilizers in
\mathfrak{F}
. As such, their existence is guaranteed for any
group and family of subgroups
[
luecksurvey
]
. Given their universal existence, the interest
lies in determining the least possible dimension of a model for
E_{\mathfrak{F}}G
, i.e.
the
Bredon geometric dimension
of
G
with respect to the family
\mathfrak{F}
, denoted by
\gd_{\mathfrak{F}}G
.

Homological methods facilitate the study of such dimensions. In the case of
G
-CW-complexes
with stabilisers in a family
\mathfrak{F}
, the Bredon cohomology of groups is the most suitable tool. Glen Bredon
introduced this homology theory in
[
bredon_equiv
]
for finite groups and
Wolfgang Lück extended it for arbitrary groups and families of subgroups in
[
luecktransformation
]
.

The spaces
\underline{E}G=E_{\mathfrak{F}}G
, known as universal space for proper actions, for
the family
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{Fin}
of finite subgroups and

\underline{\underline{E}}G=E_{\mathfrak{F}}G
for the family
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{VC}
of virtually
cyclic subgroups have been widely studied given their appearance as the geometric objects in the Baum-Connes
and Farrell-Jones conjectures, respectively. For a first introduction into the subject see, for example,
the survey
[
luecksurvey
]
.

In the case of
\mathfrak{Fin}
, explicit constructions of the respective models arise in a natural way
from the geometrical origin, interpretation or properties of many classes of groups. For
\mathfrak{VC}
,
however, building such spaces has proven more challenging. As such, methodologies
that could help obtain the desired models using known classifying spaces for the family of finite
subgroups and other related families have been developed.

Such methodologies made the construction of classifying spaces for families of subgroups
other than
\mathfrak{Fin}
and
\mathfrak{VC}
reachable. For example, in
[
nucinkispetrosyan_hier
]
, the authors build

3
-dimensional classifying spaces for the family of virtually nilpotent subgroups of any
abelian-by-infinite cyclic groups. Another example more aligned with
\mathfrak{Fin}
and
\mathfrak{VC}
can be
found in
[
nucinkisetal
]
, where
(n+r)
-dimensional models for
E_{\mathfrak{F}_{r}}G
, where
G

is finitely generated abelian and
\mathfrak{F}_{r}
is the family of subgroups of torsion-free rank less
than or equal to
r
, are constructed in a recursive manner.

Let
\mathfrak{F}_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{n}\subseteq\ldots
be an ascending
chain of families of subgroups of a discrete group
G
. Under certain conditions, it is possible
to build classifying spaces for all the families in the chain recursively, utilising those
for
\mathfrak{F}_{0}
and other families that will be introduced throughout the process. The aim of this
thesis is to provide such methodology and use it to give upper bounds for the respective Bredon dimensions.
We then apply this construction process to families of virtually polycyclic subgroups.

Structure of the thesis

In Chapter
1
, we present CW-complexes and their equivariant counterparts:

G
-CW-complexes. We also introduce some operations with such spaces that will be used throughout
the thesis and the conditions under which the resulting spaces are also (
G
-)CW-complexes. These
operations are quotients, products, joins, push-outs and, in particular, mapping cones and
mapping and double mapping cylinders. Finally, we give the definition and some basic properties
and examples of families of subgroups and classifying spaces.

In Chapter
2
, we introduce the orbit category
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{F}}G
and Bredon modules
as functors from said category to the category of abelian groups. We also give a basic overview
of free and projective objects of the category of Bredon modules, as they are key for defining
Bredon Cohomology (Chapter
2
) and describing its relation to
classifying spaces (Chapter
3
).

In Chapter
3
, apart from specifying some results about the aforementioned
relation, we define the Bredon cohomological and geometric dimensions of a group
G
with
respect to a family of subgroups
\mathfrak{F}
,
\cd_{\mathfrak{F}}G
and
\gd_{\mathfrak{F}}G
, respectively.

In Chapter
4
, we compile and extend a list of results that, given related
families and groups, connect their respective classifying spaces, Bredon cohomology groups and
Bredon dimensions. The particular cases we look into are: a family and its restriction to a
subgroup of the ambient group, families that are related by a functor, unions of families,
pairs of families
\mathfrak{F}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
such that the set
\mathfrak{G}\backslash\mathfrak{F}
admits certain structure and
families of subgroups of a direct union of groups and their restrictions to the groups appearing
in the direct union.

The main contribution of this thesis is Chapter
5
. Given an ascending chain

\left(\mathfrak{F}_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
of families of subgroups of a discrete group and provided that
the chain has certain properties, we develop a methodology
based on the results in Chapter
4
to build models for

\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{F}_{r}}\left(G\right)
recursively. We find upper bounds for the Bredon dimensions with respect to

\mathfrak{F}_{r}
depending on those with respect to
\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
and other related families.
In the case that there are finite-dimensional classifying spaces
for the family
\mathfrak{F}_{0}
for the ambient group and some of its subgroups, we list some further
conditions that will ensure that the models we build
have also finite dimension.

Finally, in Chapter
6
, we apply the results in the previous chapter to the
chain
\left(\mathfrak{F}_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
of families of subgroups, where
H\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}
if and only if

H
is virtually polycyclic and its Hirsch length is smaller than or equal to
r
. We consider
two different classes of groups for the ambient group: virtually polycyclic groups and
locally virtually polycyclic groups.

Chapter 1
Classifying Spaces for families of subgroups

1
CW-complexes

In
[
whitehead_combhomotopy1
]
, J.H.C. Whitehead introduced CW-complexes as a class of
topological spaces that could play the role of simplicial complexes in Homotopy
Theory and would allow the field to be studied from a different perspective.
The results we will present in this section can be found, in most of the cases,
in the aformentioned publication. Given the nature of their first appearance,
their description and study in
[
whitehead_combhomotopy1
]
is based on their desired properties.
For a more constructive view of CW-complexes,
we introduce them as in modern publications such as
[
Hatcher_algebraictop
]
and relate those results and definitions to the ones found in the original source.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be topological spaces. Let
f:A\to Y
be a continuous map,
where
A\subset X
is a subspace. Then, the
attaching space
or
adjunction space

for
f
is

X\cup_{f}Y=(X\sqcup Y)/\sim,

where
\sim
is the equivalence relation generated by
f(a)\sim a
for all
a\in A
.

Definition 1.2
.

A non-empty topological space
X
is a
CW-complex
if it admits a filtration
X^{0}\subseteq X^{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq X^{n}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq X
such that:

(i)

X^{0}
is a non-empty discrete set of points (
0
-cells).

(ii)

For each
n\geq 1
,
X^{n}
can be obtained by attaching
n
-cells
e^{n}_{\alpha}
to
X^{n-1}
via maps
\phi_{\alpha}:S^{n-1}\to X^{n-1}
.

(iii)

X=\bigcup_{n}X^{n}
and the topology in
X
coincides with the weak topology associated to such filtration.

Under these conditions,
X^{n}
will be called
n
-
skeleton
of
X
.

According to Definition
1.1
, the condition
(ii)
in the definition of CW-complex
means that
X^{n}
is the quotient space of the disjoint union of

X^{n-1}
with a collection of
n
-discs under the identification
x\sim\phi_{\alpha}(x)
for
x\in\partial D^{n}_{\alpha}
, i.e.

X^{n}=\left(X^{n-1}\bigsqcup_{\alpha}D^{n}_{\alpha}\right)/\sim
where
\sim
is the equivalence relation generated by
x\sim\phi_{\alpha}(x)
for
x\in\partial D^{n}_{\alpha}
. The
n
-cell
e^{n}_{\alpha}
is the homeomorphic image of
D^{n}_{\alpha}\setminus\partial D^{n}_{\alpha}
.

Definition 1.3
.

If
X=X^{n}
for some
n
, then
X
is
finite-dimensional
, in which case we will say that its
dimension
is

\dim(X)=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}\,|\,X=X^{n}\}
. If there is no such
n
, then
\dim(X)=\infty
.

Definition 1.4
.

A CW-complex
X
is called
finite
if it has only finitely many cells.

Example 1.5
.

The
n
-sphere is a CW-complex. For example, if we take one
0
-cell
e^{0}
and one
n
-cell
e^{n}
with constant attaching map
S^{n-1}\to e^{0}
, then
S^{n}=X^{n}
.

Example 1.6
.

\mathbb{R}
is a CW-complex. Let
X^{0}=\mathbb{Z}
and for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
attach to
X^{0}
a
1
-cell
e_{i}^{1}
via the map
\phi_{i}:S^{0}=\{0,1\}\to\mathbb{Z}
defined by
\phi_{i}(0)=i
and
\phi_{i}(1)=i+1
to obtain
X^{1}
. Then,
X^{1}=\mathbb{R}
.

More generally,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a CW-complex.

Example 1.7
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a graph with vertices
V
and edges
E
. Take as the
0
-cells the vertices,
X^{0}=\bigcup_{v\in V}e_{v}^{0}
, and for each edge
\{u,v\}\in E
attach to
X^{0}
a
1
-cell
e_{\{u,v\}}^{1}
via the map
\phi_{\{u,v\}}:S^{0}=\{0,1\}\to X^{0}
defined by
\phi_{\{u,v\}}(0)=u
and
\phi_{\{u,v\}}(1)=v
to obtain
X^{1}
. Then,
\Gamma=X^{1}
is a CW-complex.

Example 1.8
.

The torus
\mathbb{T}
is a CW-complex. A filtration fitting the definition would be the following:

Take
X^{0}=\{*\}
. Attach to it two
1
-cells
e_{1}^{1},e_{2}^{1}
via the constant maps
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}:S^{0}\to\{*\}
to obtain
X^{1}
. Take
e^{2}
a single
2
-cell and consider the map
\phi:S^{1}\to X^{1}
such that it sends each pair of opposite quarters of the
S^{1}
to a different
1
-cell. Then,
\mathbb{T}=X^{2}
.

These examples help us to get an idea of how important to the CW-complex structure of a topological space
X
is the way the discs
D^{n}_{\alpha}
are incorporated into
X
. That leads to the following definition, that we will use further in this section to clarify what
CW
in CW-complexes stand for:

Definition 1.9
.

For each
n
-cell
e^{n}_{\alpha}
of a CW-complex
X
we define its
characteristic map

\Phi^{n}_{\alpha}:D^{n}_{\alpha}\to X
by the composition

D^{n}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow X^{n-1}\bigsqcup_{\alpha}D^{n}_{\alpha}\twoheadrightarrow X^{n}\hookrightarrow X.

Remark 1.10
.

If
X
is a CW-complex and
e^{n}_{\alpha}
is any of its
n
-cells, then
\Phi^{n}_{\alpha}
is continuous and its restriction to the interior of
D^{n}_{\alpha}
is a homeomorphism onto
e^{n}_{\alpha}
.

Remark 1.11
.

If
A\subseteq X
is an open (closed) subset of a CW-complex
X
, then a map
f:A\to Y
, where
Y
is any topological space, is continuous if and only if its restriction
f\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\big|}_{A\cap\bar{e}}
is continuous for all cells of
X
.

And it is always important when we define a mathematical object (or structure) to define and study its sub-objects (sub-structures):

Definition 1.12
.

Given a CW-complex
X
, a subspace
A\subset X
is a
subcomplex
of
X
if for every cell
e
of
X
, if there is
p\in e
such that
p\in A
, then
\bar{e}\subset A
, where
\bar{e}
is the closure of
{e}
.

Equivalently, a subspace
A
of a CW-complex
X
is a subcomplex if it is a closed subset that is the
union of a set of cells of
X
.

Definition 1.13
.

Given a CW-complex
X
and a set of points
P\subseteq X
, the
closure
of
P
in
X
, denoted by
X(P)
, is the smallest subcomplex of
X
containing
P
, i.e., the intersection of all subcomplexes of
X
that contain
P
.

Definition
1.2
introduces CW-complexes in a constructive way, making it easier to work with such spaces. At this point we are able to relate this definition with the original one that J. H. C. Whitehead formulated in 1949 in
[
whitehead_combhomotopy1
]
. Whitehead’s definition, even if less practical than the one commonly used nowadays, gives a better insight in why such spaces are called CW-complexes and which topological necessities they were defined to cover in Homotopy Theory.

Definition 1.14
.

[
whitehead_combhomotopy1
, Section 4]

A Hausdorff space
X
is called a
cell complex
if it is the union of disjoint open cells
e^{n}_{\alpha}
subject to the following condition: the closure
\bar{e}^{n}_{\alpha}
of each
n
-cell
e^{n}_{\alpha}\in X
shall be the image of a fixed
n
-simplex
\sigma^{n}_{\alpha}
by a map
f:\sigma^{n}_{\alpha}\to\bar{e}^{n}_{\alpha}
such that

(i)

f\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\big|}_{(\sigma^{n}_{\alpha}\backslash\partial\sigma^{n}_{\alpha})}
is a homeomorphism onto
e^{n}_{\alpha}

(ii)

\partial e^{n}_{\alpha}\subset X^{n-1}
, where
\partial e^{n}_{\alpha}=f\partial\sigma^{b}_{\alpha}=\bar{e}^{n}_{\alpha}\backslash e^{n}_{\alpha}
and
X^{n-1}
is the
(n-1)
-skeleton of
X
, consisting of all the cells whose dimensionalities do not exceed
n-1
.

Definition 1.15
.

[
whitehead_combhomotopy1
, Section 5]

A cell complex
X
is said to be
closure finite
if
X(e)
is a finite subcomplex of
X
for every cell.

Note that the notion of
subcomplex
refers to cell complexes and not to CW-complexes. A subspace
A
of a cell complex
X
is a subcomplex if it is the union of a subset of
X
’s cells such that
e\subseteq L
implies
\bar{e}\subseteq L
for all cells of
X
.

Definition 1.16
.

[
whitehead_combhomotopy1
, Section 5]

A cell complex
X
has the
weak topology
if a subset
U\subseteq X
is closed provided
U\cap\bar{e}
is closed for each cell
e\in X
.

And finally, Whitehead’s definition of CW-complex:

Definition 1.17
.

[
whitehead_combhomotopy1
, Section 5]

A cell complex
X
is a
CW-complex
if it is closure finite and has the weak topology.

Now we can see clearly that
C
stands for
closure finite
and
W
for
weak topology
.

A proof of the equivalence between Definition
1.2
and Definition
1.17
can be found, for example, in the Appendix of
[
Hatcher_algebraictop
]
:

Proposition 1.18
.

[
Hatcher_algebraictop
, Proposition A.2.]

Given a Hausdorff space
X
and a family of maps
\Phi^{n}_{\alpha}:D^{n}_{\alpha}\to X
,
then these maps are the characteristic maps of a CW-complex (as in Definition
1.2
)
structure on
X
if and only if:

(i)

each
\Phi^{n}_{\alpha}
restricts to a homeomorphism from
\mathring{D}^{n}_{\alpha}=D^{n}_{\alpha}\backslash\partial D^{n}_{\alpha}
onto its image, a cell
e^{n}_{\alpha}\subseteq X
;

(ii)

for each cell
e^{n}_{\alpha}
,
\Phi^{n}_{\alpha}(\partial D^{n}_{\alpha})
is contained in a finite subcomplex whose cells have dimension strictly less than

; and

(iii)

a subset of

is closed if and only if it meets the topological closure of each cell of

in a closed set.

As one may note, previous proposition alone does not prove the equivalence of the two definitions. For its completion, CW-complexes defined as in Definition
1.2
have to be Hausdorff. A stronger property is true:

Proposition 1.19
.

[
Hatcher_algebraictop
, Proposition A.3.]

CW-complexes (as in Definition
1.2
) are normal and, in particular, Hausdorff.

We shall now introduce some constructions preserving the structure of CW-complexes that will be used directly or indirectly for the results on this thesis.

Let

be a CW-complex and

In a more general case that will be useful when considering mapping cones and joins,

.

[

, (F) in Section 5]

If

1.3
Join

for all

and

. Let
{\tilde{\pi}_{Y}:C_{X}\to X*Y}
be the corresponding quotient

to

we have that

∎

1.4
Attaching spaces along maps

Some important examples of attaching spaces that we will use throughout this thesis are the following:

Definition 1.25
.

Given
f:X\to Y
a continous map between topological spaces, the
mapping cylinder of
f

is

\operatorname{Cyl}\left(f\right)=\left(X\times[0,1]\right)\cup_{g}Y,

where
g:X\times\{1\}\subset X\times[0,1]\to Y
is defined by
g(x,1)=f(x)
.

The
mapping cone of

f
is

\operatorname{C}\left(f\right)=\operatorname{Cyl}\left(f\right)/\sim,

where
\sim
is the equivalence relation generated by
(x,0)\sim(x^{\prime},0)
for all

x,x^{\prime}\in X
.

Definition 1.26
.

Given
f:X\to Y
and
g:X\to Z
continuous maps between topological spaces,
the
double mapping cylinder of

Y\xleftarrow{f}X\xrightarrow{g}Z
is

\operatorname{Cyl}\left(f,g\right)=\left(X\times[0,1]\right)\cup_{k}\left(Y\sqcup Z\right),

where
k:X\times\{0,1\}\subset X\times[0,1]\to\left(Y\sqcup Z\right)
is
defined by
k(x,0)=f(x)\in Y
and
k(x,1)=g(x)\in Z
.

Definition 1.27
.

Let
X
and
Y
be CW-complexes. Then a continuous map
f:X\to Y

is called
cellular
if
f(X^{n})\subseteq Y^{n}
for all

.

A cellular map sends
0
-cells to
0
-cells but the same is not necessarily true
for
n
-cells with

. For example, take
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
f(x)=x^{2}
,
where
\mathbb{R}
has the CW-complex structure given in Example
1.6
.

is
a cellular map since
X^{0}=\mathbb{Z}
,

and

.
However, if we take
e^{1}=(1,2)
,
f(e^{1})=(1,4)
, and
(1,4)
is not a
1
-cell
but the union of two
1
-cells and one
0
-cell.

Note that the maps
g
and
h
in Definition
1.25
are cellular if
f

is, given that
\{0\}
and
\{1\}
are the
0
-cells of
[0,1]
. Analogously,
the map
k
in Definition
1.26
is cellular if
f
and
g
are.

The following theorem gives us some conditions under which an adjunction space
is a CW-complex:

Theorem 1.28
.

[
fritsch_cellularstr
, Theorem 2.3.1.]
[
lueck_alg_top
, Lemma 3.10]

Let

and
Y
be CW-complexes and

a subcomplex of

. Let

be a cellular map. Then, if we take

to be the topological

Moreover, if

and

are the maps that complete the push-out diagram

are the sets of open cells of

the
n
-skeleton

and

.

Corollary 1.29
.

Let
X
,
Y
and
Z
be CW-complexes and let
f:X\to Y
and

be

and

(and

Consequence of Proposition
1.20
and Theorem

.
∎

The condition of

being a cellular map is not as big a restriction as it may seem, given
the following result, that can be found for example in
[
tDieck_algebraic_top
, Theorem 8.5.4.]
,

:

Theorem 1.30

(Cellular Approximation Theorem)
.

Every continuous map

between CW-complexes is homotopic to a cellular
map
{g:X\to Y}
. If

is already cellular on a subcomplex

, the

In
[

, Theorem 2.1]
, W. Lück provides a version of the theorem for

2
G
-CW-Complexes

Definition 1.31
.

Let
G
be a discrete group and
X
a topological space such that
G
acts continuously on
X
. A
G
-CW-complex structure on
X
consists of

(i)

a filtration
\emptyset=X_{-1}\subseteq X^{0}\subseteq X^{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq X^{n}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq X
such that
\bigcup_{n\geq 0}X^{n}=X
;

(ii)

a collection
\{e^{n}_{\alpha}\,|\,\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{n}\}
of
G
-subspaces
e^{n}_{\alpha}\subseteq X^{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

such that

(a)

X
has the weak topology with respect to the filtration
\{X^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

(b)

for each
n\geq 0

X^{n}
can be obtained by attaching the
G
-subspaces
e^{n}_{\alpha}
to
X^{n-1}
via continuous
G
-maps
q^{n}_{\alpha}:G/H_{\alpha}\times S^{n-1}\to X^{n-1}
, where
H_{\alpha}
are subgroups of
G
. That is,
X^{n}
is the push-out of the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underset{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}{\bigsqcup}G/H_{\alpha}\times S^{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underset{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}{\bigsqcup}G/H_{\alpha}\times D^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underset{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}{\bigsqcup}q^{n}_{\alpha}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underset{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}{\bigsqcup}Q^{n}_{\alpha}}

In this case the
n
-skeleton is

again and
e^{n}_{\alpha}
are the (open) equivariant
n
-cells and
\bar{e}^{n}_{\alpha}
are their topological closure (we may refer to them as closed equivariant
n
-cells).

We may assume that

is a Hausdorff space. In fact, in some of their first appearances,

-CW-complexes were defined to be Hausdorff spaces (
[
matumoto_GCW
]
,
[
illman_GCW
]
), as the original CW-complexes were all normal (and hence Hausdorff). Also, in
[
tDieck_transformation
]
it is shown that if

is obtained from

as in the push-out above and

is Hausdorff, then
X^{n}
is also Hausdorff.

G
-CW-complexes can be defined more generally for topological groups (see
[
luecksurvey
]
, for example).

As for any
G
-space, the
isotropy groups
of a
G
-CW-complex

play an essential role when studying the relation between

and

.
If we take the isotropy group of

,
G_{x}=\{g\in G\,|\,gx=x\}
, we can see that it is nothing than the preimage of
\{x\}
by the action of

on

, which is a continuous map. In the case that we focus our interest, groups are discrete, and so equipped with the discrete topology. In that case, of course, isotropy groups are open and closed.

Proposition 1.32
.

[
luecksurvey
, Remark 1.3]

Let
X
be a
G
-space with
G
-invariant filtration

\emptyset=X_{-1}\subseteq X^{0}\subseteq X^{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq X^{n}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq X=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}X^{n}.

Then the following assertions are equivalent:

i)

Every isotropy group of
X
is open and the filtration above yields a
G
-CW-structure on
X
.

ii)

The filtration above yields a CW-structure on
X
such that for each open cell
e\subseteq X
and each
g\in G
, if
ge\cap e\neq\emptyset
then
g
fixes
e
point-wise.

The case of the proposition above holds for discrete groups, but it is not the general case if one takes in consideration any topological group. In Section 2 of
[
illman_rest_transf
]
there is an example of non-discrete equivariant (for the circle group

) CW-complex

such that

does not admit a CW structure compatible with the equivariant CW structure given.

Definition 1.33
.

Let
G
be a topological group acting on a CW-complex

. We say that the

-action on

is

(or that

acts

(i)

if

is an
n
-cell of

and

, then

is also an

-cell of

and

(ii)

if

is a cell of

and

is such that

then

for all

.

As a particular case of Proposition

, we have the following characterization of equivariant CW-complexes for discrete groups:

Corollary 1.34
.

Let
G
be a discrete group and

a topological space. Then

is a
G
-CW-complex if and only if

Example 1.35

(continues = RasCWcompl)
.

In Section

Corollary 1.43
.

Given a

-CW-complex

and
\pi:X\twoheadrightarrow L
a surjective map
as in Proposition

. Assume moreover that

for all

Proof.

Given

, where
\pi(x)=l
.
This action is cellular as both

and the action of

on

are cellular.
∎

Corollary 1.44
.

Let

and

be

-CW-complexes. Then, under any of the conditions stated in

,

is a

-CW-complex.

Proof.

By Theorem

,

with the product cellular structure

-complex.

Define the action of

on

by

(i)

If

is an

-cell of

and

, then

is also
an
n
-cell of
X\times Y
:

By definition of the product cellular structure on

a

Theorem 1.48

All results produced in this thesis will refer to full families. Therefore, if not indicated otherwise, we will assume that families are full families.

The following are full families of subgroups:

, the family of all

;

Example 1.51
.

Let

be a non-empty class of groups closed under taking subgroups and let

Remark 1.53

If the families

Classifying spaces

In this section, we present some basic results about classifying spaces for families of subgroups. More information can be
found in
[
luecksurvey
]
, for example.

Definition 1.54
.

Let

be a topological group and let
\mathfrak{F}
be a semi-full family of subgroups of

. A
G
-CW-complex

is a

for the family
\mathfrak{F}
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

belong to
\mathfrak{F}
.

(ii)

is a
G
-CW-complex with isotropy groups in
\mathfrak{F}
, then there exists

-map

, unique up to
G
-homotopy.

Equivalently, we may say

is a

\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}\left(G\right)
when

is a classifying space for the family

\mathfrak{F}
of subgroups of

.

We will refer to condition

as

, and it is equivalent to

being a terminal object in the
G
-homotopy category of
G
-CW-complexes with isotropy groups in the family
\mathfrak{F}
.

One of the first questions that arises is whether the existence of such spaces is conditional or universal. The following theorem shows that their existence is universal:

Theorem 1.55

(Existence of models for
\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}\left(G\right)
)
.

[

, Proposition 2.3]

Let

be a topological group and
\mathfrak{F}
a semi-full family of (closed) subgroups of

. Then, there is a model for
\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}\left(G\right)
.

And as a consequence of Whitehead Theorem for Families (

), we can characterize classifying spaces homotopically as follows:

Theorem 1.56

(Homotopy characterization of
\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}\left(G\right)
)
.

[
luecksurvey
, Theorem 1.9]

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a semi-full family of subgroups of a topological group

. Then, a
G
-CW-complex

is a model for

if and only if all its isotropy groups belong to

and for each

In the case of a discrete group

Proposition 1.57
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a semi-full family of subgroups of a discrete group

. Then, a

-CW-complex

is a model for
\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}\left(G\right)
if and only if all its isotropy groups belong to

and for each

.

[

.

Then,

and

5.1

We will firstly describe Farrell’s construction, which produces a classifying space

found in
[

and in
[

Studying these classical constructions of classifying spaces, we can perceive a hint of
what nowadays represents the most broadly used procedure to build such spaces for a family
\mathfrak{G}

of subgroups of a group

given known models for
\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}\left(G\right)
and other classifying spaces for families
related with
\mathfrak{F}

\mathfrak{F}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}

the Lück-Weiermann method.

Let then

and
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{VC}
be the family of (virtually) cyclic subgroups of

, which
in this case coincides with the family of the subgroups of

.

Note that for every

there is a unique

such that

and
\bar{H}
is maximal among those subgroups with the same properties, i.e., if

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the set of the maximals subgroups of
\mathfrak{F}
we just described. If

are different, then their intersection is necessarily trivial, as
a proper non-trivial intersection between two maximals would contradict the fact that they are maximals.
Moreover,
\mathbb{Z}^{2}/H\cong\mathbb{Z}
for any

.

The set
\mathcal{H}
can be indexed by
\mathbb{Z}
and we write

to denote the

-th maximal subgroup.
As we saw in Example

Chapter 5

The results presented in Chapter
4

[
lueckweiermann
]

have been used fruitfully to build the classifying spaces for the family
of virtually cyclic subgroups of a wide variety of groups from those for the family of finite subgroups.

In

The objective of this chapter is to widen the results presented in the aforementioned sources, to

Definition 5.1
.

A chain of families of subgroups

L\cap H\in\mathfrak{F}_{i}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{i-1}
if and only if
L\cap K\in\mathfrak{F}_{i}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{i-1}.

We will use the abbreviation “SSACFS” for “strongly structured ascending chains of families of subgroups”.

is a full family of subgroups of

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

And by construction we have
\mathfrak{X}_{k}(G)\subseteq\mathfrak{X}_{n}(G)
for all

k\leq n
. So
\left(\mathfrak{X}_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an ascending chain of full families
of subgroups of
G
.

It only remains to prove that for all
r,i\in\mathbb{N}
with
i\leq r
, if

H,K\in\mathfrak{X}_{r}(G)\backslash\mathfrak{X}_{r-1}(G)

are such that
H\cap K\in\mathfrak{X}_{r}(G)\backslash\mathfrak{X}_{r-1}(G)
and

L\in\mathfrak{X}_{i}(G)\backslash\mathfrak{X}_{i-1}(G)
, then

L\cap H\in\mathfrak{X}_{i}(G)\backslash\mathfrak{X}_{i-1}(G)
if and only if

L\cap K\in\mathfrak{X}_{i}(G)\backslash\mathfrak{X}_{i-1}(G).

Observe that for
H\leq G
,
H\in\mathfrak{X}_{r}(G)\backslash\mathfrak{X}_{r-1}(G)

if and only if
r(H)=r
. Therefore, given

0\leq i\leq r
and given
H,K,L\leq G
such that
h(H)=h(K)=h(H\cap K)=r

and
h(L)=i
, we need to prove that
h(H\cap L)=i
if and only if
h(K\cap L)=i
.
By hypothesis
(ii)
, since
L\cap K\leq L
and
L\cap H\leq L
, it is equivalent to
prove that
|L:L\cap H|<\infty
if and only if
|L:L\cap K|<\infty

given that
|H:H\cap K|<\infty
and
|K:H\cap K|<\infty
.

Assume then that
|H:H\cap K|<\infty
,
|K:H\cap K|<\infty
and
|L:L\cap H|<\infty
.
Since
|H:H\cap K|<\infty
, intersecting with
L
, we obtain

|L\cap H:L\cap H\cap K|<\infty
. Hence, since
|L:L\cap H|<\infty

and index is multiplicative,
|L:L\cap H\cap K|<\infty
. Finally, since

L\cap H\cap K\leq L\cap K\leq L
, we can conclude
|L:L\cap K|<\infty
,
as we wanted to show.

The converse implication is symmetrical.
∎

We will use the results in
[
lueckweiermann
]
summarised in section
5
of
Chapter
4

for each of the inclusions in the chain of families, so we need to start by showing
that we can use such results:

Definition 5.5
.

Given a chain
\left(\mathfrak{F}_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
of full families of subgroups of a group
G
,
for each
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\sim_{r}
denote the following relation on
\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}:

H\sim_{r}K\iff H\cap K\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}

Lemma 5.6
.

If
\left(\mathfrak{F}_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
is a strongly structured ascending chain
of families of subgroups of a group
G
, then
\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{r}
is a strongly
structured inclusion of families of subgroups of
G
with respect to
\sim_{r}
for
every
r>0
, i.e.,
\sim_{r}
is a strong equivalence relation
(in the sense of
[
lueckweiermann
]
) in
\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
for every
r>0
.

Proof.

\sim_{r}
is clearly reflexive and symmetric. As for transitivity,
given
H,K,L\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
such that
H\sim_{r}K
and
K\sim_{r}L
,
we need to see that
H\sim_{r}L
. By definition of SSACFS, in the particular
case that
i=r
, since
H\cap K\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
and
K\cap L\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
,
then
H\cap L\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
. Hence,
\sim_{r}
is an equivalence relation in
\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
.

To prove that it is strong in the sense of Lück-Weiermann, first let
H,K\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}

with
H\leq K
. Then,
H\cap K=H\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
, so
H\sim_{r}K
.
Finally, let
g\in G
and
H,K\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
. We need to see
H\sim_{r}K

if and only if
H^{g}\sim_{r}K^{g}
.
And for that it suffices to prove that
(H\cap K)^{g}=H^{g}\cap K^{g}
,
since
\mathfrak{F}_{r}
and
\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
are families of subgroups of
G
and, therefore,
closed under conjugation. An element
l\in G
belongs to
H^{g}\cap K^{g}
if and only
if there are
h\in H
and
k\in K
such that
l=ghg^{-1}=gkg^{-1}
.
But in that case
h=k
and so
l\in(H\cap K)^{g}
. The other implication follows trivially.
∎

And not only the equivalence relations
\sim_{r}
are strong in the sense of
[
lueckweiermann
]
,
but they are the finest of all possible strong equivalence relations at each inclusion.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\left(\mathfrak{F}_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
be a strongly structured ascending chain of families
of subgroups of a group
G
, let
r>0
and let
\sim
be any equivalence relation in
\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}

that is strong in the sense of
[
lueckweiermann
]
. Then
\sim_{r}
is finer than
\sim
.

Proof.

Let
H,K\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
such that
H\sim_{r}K
. Then,
H\cap K\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
.
By
(i)
in Definition
4.21
,
H\cap K\sim H
and
H\cap K\sim K

given
H\cap K\leq H
and
H\cap K\leq K
respectively. Finally, by transitivity
of
\sim
, that implies
H\sim K
, as we wanted to see.
∎

Following the example in Lemma
5.4
, we have:

Lemma 5.8
.

Given a group
G
and
\left(\mathfrak{X}_{n}(G)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as in
Lemma
5.4
, for every
r>0
and

,

H\sim_{r}K
if and only if
H
and
K
are commensurable.

Proof.

Given
H,K\in\mathfrak{X}_{r}(G)\backslash\mathfrak{X}_{r-1}(G)
, by definition of

\sim_{r}
,
H\sim_{r}K
if and only if
r(H\cap K)=r(H)=r(K)=r
. Since
commensurability is transitive,
H\cap K\leq H
and
H\cap K\leq K
, by hypothesis

(ii)
,
r(H\cap K)=r(H)=r(K)=r
if and only if
H
and
K
are commensurable,
as we needed to see.
∎

2

Our objective is to be able to give a bound on the Bredon cohomological and geometric
dimensions of a group over the families in a strongly structured ascending chain
of families of subgroups
\left(\mathfrak{F}_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}}

section we are going to build classifying spaces for the mentioned families using recursion
over
r

By Theorem
1.55

\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{F}_{r}}\left(G\right)

As the first example of the aforementioned basic spaces (i.e. spaces used but not being built
in the recursive process), a finite-dimensional model for

will be necessary. But let us start the construction process and discover

M\cap K\cap H\in\mathfrak{F}_{i}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{i-1}
.
We need to see

.

Since

The chain
(\mathfrak{F}_{r})_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
is a SSACFS,

and

M,M\cap L\in\mathfrak{F}_{i}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{i-1}

.

Analogously, since

as we needed to see.

.

The restriction of

\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{G}\left(H\right)\setminus\mathfrak{R}_{i-1}^{G}\left(H\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{i}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{i-1}

Proof.

From Corollaries
5.25
and
5.23
.

3.2
Bredon cohomological dimensions

Analogous results to those in the previous section can be proven for the Bredon
cohomological dimensions. We also present Mayer-Vietoris long exact sequences that
can help improve the upper bounds or provide lower bounds for the Bredon dimensions
when applied to particular groups and chains of families of subgroups.

Proposition 5.34
.

Let

and let

and

F_{\cap}:\mathfrak{R}_{i-1}^{N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}}\left(K\right)\to\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{G}\left(H\right)[K]
be the
inclusion functors.
Then, the following sequence in Bredon cohomology is exact

\cdots\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n$}}}_{\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{G}\left(H\right)[K]}\big(N_{N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}}\left[K\right]_{i};M\big)\rightarrow\\
\operatorname{H}^{{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n$}}}_{\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}}\left(K\right)}\big(N_{N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}}\left[K\right]_{i};\operatorname{res}_{F_{1}}M\big)\oplus\operatorname{H}^{{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n$}}}_{\mathfrak{R}_{i-1}^{N_{G}\left[K\right]_{i}}\left(H\right)}\big(N_{N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}}\left[K\right]_{i};\operatorname{res}_{F_{2}}M\big)\rightarrow\\

and hence

\displaystyle\cd_{\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{G}\left(H\right)[K]}(N_{N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}}\left[K\right]_{i})\leq\operatorname{max}\{

\displaystyle\cd_{\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}}\left(K\right)}(N_{N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}}\left[K\right]_{i})\,,

\displaystyle\cd_{\mathfrak{R}_{i-1}^{N_{G}\left[K\right]_{i}}\left(H\right)}(N_{N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}}\left[K\right]_{i})\,,

\displaystyle\cd_{\mathfrak{R}_{i-1}^{N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}}\left(K\right)}(N_{N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}}\left[K\right]_{i})+1\}.

Proof.

Consequence of Lemmas
4.20
,
5.21
and
5.22
.
∎

and

.
∎

.

be a group and

a strongly structured

G
. Let

. Let

4
Classifying spaces for the families
\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)

In this section, we give some additional conditions to the initial set-up that will
guarantee a finite-dimensional model for
\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)}\left(N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}\right)
. From this point
on, we adopt the commonly used notation
\ucd G,\ugd G,{\underline{E}G}
to represent
\cd_{\mathfrak{Fin}(G)}G
,

\gd_{\mathfrak{Fin}(G)}G
and
\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{Fin}(G)}\left(G\right)
, respectively.

Theorem 5.38
.

Let
H\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
be such that
N_{G}H=N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}
. Then,

\bar{\mathfrak{H}}=\{LH/H\,|\,L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)\}
is a full family of subgroups of

N_{G}H/H
. Moreover, if there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\cd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)\cap LH}LH\leq n

for all
L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
, then

\cd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)}N_{G}H\leq\cd_{\bar{\mathfrak{H}}}\left(N_{G}H/H\right)+n.

Proof.

First of all, if
L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
, since
N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}=N_{G}H
,
L\leq N_{G}H
, so

H\lhd LH
and
LH/H
is a subgroup of
N_{G}H/H
.

To see that
\bar{\mathfrak{H}}
is closed under
conjugation, we need to prove that
\left(LH/H\right)^{gH}
belongs to
\bar{\mathfrak{H}}
for
gH\in N_{G}H/H

and
L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
. Take
kH\in LH/H
. Then
(gH)(kH)(gH)^{-1}=gkg^{-1}H\in L^{g}H/H
,
given the fact that
gN_{G}H
and hence
(LH)^{g}=L^{g}H
. And since
L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
,

g\in N_{G}H
and
\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
is closed under conjugation,
L^{g}H/H\in\bar{\mathfrak{H}}
.

Let
L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
and
S/H\leq LH/H
. We need to find
S^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
such that

S^{\prime}H/H=S/H
. Take
S^{\prime}=S\cap L
. Since
S\leq LH
, then
S\cap L\leq L
, so

S^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}
. We only need to see that
S^{\prime}\cap H\sim_{j}S^{\prime}
for some
j\leq r
.
But
S^{\prime}\cap H=S\cap L\cap H=S\cap H\cap L=S\cap L=S^{\prime}
, since
H\leq S
.
In particular,
S^{\prime}\cap H\sim_{j}S
for some
j\leq r
, so
\bar{\mathfrak{H}}
is closed under taking subgroups.

Now that we proved that
\bar{\mathfrak{H}}
is a full family of subgroups of
N_{G}H/H
, as
direct consequence of Corollary
4.15
, we obtain the rest
of the theorem.
∎

Theorem 5.39
.

Let
H\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
be such that
N_{G}H=N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}
. Then, if there is
n\in\mathbb{N}

such that
\gd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)\cap LH}LH\leq n

for all
L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
, then

\gd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)}N_{G}H\leq\gd_{\bar{\mathfrak{H}}}\left(N_{G}H/H\right)+n.

Proof.

As we saw in Theorem
5.38
,
\bar{\mathfrak{H}}
is a full family of subgroups of
N_{G}H/H
.
Since
\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
is a full family of subgroups of
N_{G}H
and
LH/H\in\bar{\mathfrak{H}}
for every
L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
,
Theorem
4.17
yields the inequality we wanted to show.
∎

Corollary 5.40
.

Let
H\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
be such that
N_{G}H=N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}
and
LH\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)

for all
L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
. Then,

\cd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)}N_{G}H\leq\cd_{\bar{\mathfrak{H}}}\left(N_{G}H/H\right).

Proof.

By Theorem
5.38
, we know
\bar{\mathfrak{H}}
is a full family
of subgroups of
N_{G}H
and hence we can apply Corollary
4.15
.
Let
\mathfrak{H}=\{S\leq G\,|\,N\leq S\text{ and }S/N\in\bar{\mathfrak{H}}\}
and let
S\in\mathfrak{H}
.
By definition of
\bar{\mathfrak{H}}
and
\mathfrak{H}
, we know that
S=LH
for some
L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
.
Then,
S\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
, by hypothesis, so
\cd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)\cap S}S=0
, as we needed
to see.
∎

Corollary 5.41
.

Let
H\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
be such that
N_{G}H=N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}
and
LH\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)

for all
L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
. Then,

\gd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)}N_{G}H\leq\gd_{\bar{\mathfrak{H}}}\left(N_{G}H/H\right).

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{H}=\{S\leq G\,|\,N\leq S\text{ and }S/N\in\bar{\mathfrak{H}}\}
.
By Theorem
4.17
, we only need to see that
\gd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)\cap S}S=0

for all
S\in\mathfrak{H}
. And that is true given that
S\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
for all
S\in\mathfrak{H}
, as we
saw in the proof of Corollary
5.40
.
∎

Corollary 5.42
.

Let
\left(\mathfrak{F}_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a strongly structured ascending chain of families
of subgroups of
G
such that the equivalence relation
\sim_{r}
in
\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
is
commensurability, i.e., if
H,K\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
then
H\cap K\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}

if and only if
|H:H\cap K|<\infty
and
|K:H\cap K|<\infty
.
Then, if
[H]_{r}\in[\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}]_{r}
is such that
N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}=N_{G}H
, we have

\cd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)}N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}\leq\ucd\left(N_{G}H/H\right).

Proof.

First, we need to see that the family
\bar{\mathfrak{H}}
defined in Theorem
5.38

is the family of finite subgroups of
N_{G}H/H
.

For a subgroup of
L\leq N_{G}H
,
LH/H\in\mathfrak{Fin}(N_{G}H/H)
if and only if
|LH:H|<\infty
.
Also, as
\sim_{r}
is commensurability,
L\leq N_{G}H
belongs to
\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
if and only if
|H:H\cap L|<\infty
.
By the Second Isomorphism Theorem, we know
|LH:H|=|H:H\cap L|
, so
LH/H\in\mathfrak{Fin}(N_{G}H/H)
if and only if

LH/H\in\bar{\mathfrak{H}}
, as we wanted to see.

By Corollary
5.40
, it only remains to prove that given

H\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
such that
N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}=N_{G}H
and
L\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\cap N_{G}H
such that

|L:L\cap H|<\infty
, then
|LH:H|<\infty
. But that is consequence of
L\leq N_{G}H
and
the Second Isomorphism Theorem.
∎

Corollary 5.43
.

Let
\left(\mathfrak{F}_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a strongly structured ascending chain of families
of subgroups of
G
such that the equivalence relation
\sim_{r}
in
\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
is
commensurability. Assume that
[H]_{r}\in[\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}]_{r}
is such that
N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}=N_{G}H

and let
X
be a model for
{\underline{E}}\left(N_{G}H/H\right)
. Then,
X
is a model for
\operatorname{E}_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)}\left(N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}\right)

and

\gd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)}N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}\leq\ugd\left(N_{G}H/H\right).

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{H}=\{L\leq G\,|\,LH/H\in\mathfrak{Fin}(N_{G}H/H)\}
. If we show that
\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
, by
Lemma
4.16
, we will reach the desired conclusions.

But by the same argumentation we used to prove
\bar{\mathfrak{H}}=\mathfrak{Fin}(N_{G}H/H)
in Corollary
5.42
,

L\in\mathfrak{H}
if and only if
L\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
.

The inequality of Bredon geometric dimensions can be also proven as in the proof of Corollary
5.42
,
using Corollary
5.41
.
∎

We would like to relax the condition of
N_{G}H=N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}
in order to provide
upper bounds for the Bredon dimension with respect to the families
\mathfrak{R}_{r}^{G}\left(H\right)
of subgroups
of
N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}
in a more general set-up. For that, the following
equivalence will be helpful:

Lemma 5.44
.

Let
H\in\mathfrak{F}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{F}_{r-1}
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

there is
H^{\prime}\in[H]_{r}

and
H\leq G
, respectively,
given that if
A\leq B
then
h(A)\leq h(B)
for all
A,B
virtually polycyclic.

The proof for the Bredon cohomological dimension is the same as the one for the Bredon geometric
dimension, but using Corollary
6.10
instead of
6.9
.
∎

We can now derive similar results for the families
\mathfrak{H}_{r}[H]
and
\mathfrak{H}_{r}
of subgroups of
N_{G}H

and
G
, respectively.

Corollary 6.12
.

Let
G
be a virtually polycyclic group and for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathfrak{H}_{r}
be the family
of subgroups of
G
of Hirsch length smaller than or equal to
r
. Then,

\displaystyle\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}[H]}N_{G}H\leq\underset{H\in\mathcal{H}_{r}}{\operatorname{max}}\{

\displaystyle\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r-1}\cap N_{G}H}N_{G}H\,,\,h(N_{G}H)-h(H)\,,

\displaystyle\gd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r-1}^{G}\left(H\right)}N_{G}H+1\}.

and hence

\displaystyle\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\leq\underset{H\in\mathcal{H}_{r}}{\operatorname{max}}\{

\displaystyle\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r-1}\cap N_{G}H}(N_{G}H)+1\,,

\displaystyle h(N_{G}H)-h(H)\,,

\displaystyle\gd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r-1}^{G}\left(H\right)}(N_{G}H)+1\,,

\displaystyle\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r-1}}G\}.

Proof.

We get the first inequality from Corollaries
5.28
and
6.7
.
For the second inequality, we use the first and Proposition
5.32
.
∎

Corollary 6.13
.

Let
G
and
\mathfrak{H}_{r}
as in the previous result. Then,

\displaystyle\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}[H]}N_{G}H\leq\underset{H\in\mathcal{H}_{r}}{\operatorname{max}}\{

\displaystyle\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r-1}\cap N_{G}H}N_{G}H\,,\,h(N_{G}H)-h(H)\,,

\displaystyle\cd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r-1}^{G}\left(H\right)}N_{G}H+1\}.

and hence

\displaystyle\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\leq\underset{H\in\mathcal{H}_{r}}{\operatorname{max}}\{

\displaystyle\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r-1}\cap N_{G}H}(N_{G}H)+1\,,

\displaystyle h(N_{G}H)-h(H)\,,

\displaystyle\cd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r-1}^{G}\left(H\right)}(N_{G}H)+1\,,

\displaystyle\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r-1}}G\}.

Proof.

Using Proposition
5.36
, Corollary
6.8
and Theorem
5.37
.
∎

Finally, we get the following upper bounds for the Bredon dimensions of
G
with respect to
the family
\mathfrak{H}_{r}
, for
r\leq h(G)
:

Theorem 6.14
.

Let
G
be a virtually polycyclic group and, for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathfrak{H}_{r}
be the family
of subgroups of
G
of Hirsch length smaller than or equal to
r
. Then,

\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\leq h(G)+r

and

\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\leq h(G)+r.

Proof.

We proceed, as in the proof of Proposition
6.11
, by induction
over
r
. For the base case, since
\mathfrak{H}_{0}
is the family of finite subgroups of
G
, we know that

\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{0}}G=h(G)
.

For the inductive step, let
H\in\mathfrak{H}_{r}\setminus\mathfrak{H}_{r-1}
be such that
N_{G}\left[H\right]_{r}=N_{G}H
and
assume that for all
L
virtually polycyclic we have

\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r-1}\cap L}L\leq h(L)+i
.
By hypothesis of induction, we have:

(i)

\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r-1}\cap N_{G}H}N_{G}H\leq h(N_{G}H)+r-1\leq h(G)+r-1
;

(ii)

\gd_{\mathfrak{R}_{r-1}^{G}\left(H\right)}(N_{G}H)\leq h(N_{G}H)+r-1
and

(iii)

\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r-1}}G\leq h(G)+r-1
.

By Corollary
6.12
, we have
\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\leq h(G)+r
, as we
needed.

The proof for the Bredon cohomological dimension is the same as the one for the Bredon geometric
dimension, using Corollary
6.13
instead of
6.12
.
∎

We can also give lower bounds for the Bredon dimensions of
G
with respect to
\mathfrak{H}_{r}
that will
be useful in the next section.

Corollary 6.15
.

Let
G
be a virtually polycyclic group and, for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathfrak{H}_{r}
be the family
of subgroups
H\leq G
such that
h(H)\leq r
. Then,

\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\geq h(G)-r

and

\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\geq h(G)-r.

Proof.

Consider the families
\mathfrak{H}_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{r}
and
\pi:\mathfrak{H}_{0}\to\mathfrak{H}_{r}
to be the inclusion.
Given
H\in\mathfrak{H}_{r}
, we know that
\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{0}\cap H}H=\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{0}\cap H}H=h(H)\leq r
.
By Corollary
4.13
and Proposition
4.14
,
since
\mathfrak{H}_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{r}
are full families, we can conclude that

h(G)=\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{0}}G\leq\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G+r
and
h(G)=\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{0}}G\leq\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G+r
.
∎

2
Locally virtually polycyclic groups

In this section we use Theorems
4.33
and
4.32
to widen
the class of groups to which the ambient group belongs in the results in the previous section.

Definition 6.16
.

Let
G
be a locally virtually polycyclic group. Then, we define its Hirsch length
as
h(G)=\operatorname{sup}\{h(H)\,|\,H\leq G\emph{finitelygenerated}\}
.

Note that this extension of the definition of the Hirsch length is consistent with that for
virtually polycyclic groups given in the previous section and also with that for elementary amenable groups
given in
[
hillman1991elementary
]
.

Theorem 6.17
.

Let
G
be a locally virtually polycyclic countable group such that
h(G)<\infty
. Then,

\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\leq h(G)+r+1

and

\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\leq h(G)+r+1,

for
0\leq r<h(G)
and
\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\leq 1
and
\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\leq 1
for
r\geq h(G)
.

Proof.

Let
\{G_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\in\Lambda\}
be the set of finitely generated subgroups of
G
.
Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\mathfrak{H}_{r}
is a full family of finitely generated subgroups of
G
, by
Proposition
4.30
, the families
\mathfrak{H}_{r}
and

\{\mathfrak{H}_{r}\cap G_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\in\Lambda\}

are compatible with the direct union. Given that
G
is a countable group,
\Lambda
is
also countable. Hence, by Theorems
4.32
and
4.33
, we have

\underset{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\operatorname{sup}}\{\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}}G_{\lambda}\}\leq\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\leq\underset{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\operatorname{sup}}\{\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}}G_{\lambda}\}+1

and

\underset{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\operatorname{sup}}\{\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}}G_{\lambda}\}\leq\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\leq\underset{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\operatorname{sup}}\{\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}}G_{\lambda}\}+1,

where
\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}=\mathfrak{H}_{r}\cap G_{\lambda}
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
G
is locally virtually polycyclic and

G_{\lambda}
is finitely generated,
G_{\lambda}
is virtually polycyclic.
Therefore, by Theorem
6.14
,

\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}}G_{\lambda}\leq h(G_{\lambda})+r
and

\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}}G_{\lambda}\leq h(G_{\lambda})+r
, respectively.
Note that if
r\geq h(G_{\lambda})
,
G_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}
, so

\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}}G_{\lambda}=\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}}G_{\lambda}=0
, which concludes the
proof, since
h(G)=\underset{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\operatorname{sup}}\{h(G_{\lambda})\}
.
∎

Theorem 6.18
.

Let
G
be a locally virtually polycyclic countable group and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G<\infty
if and only if
h(G)<\infty
.

Proof.

We only need to prove the left-to-right implication, as the other implication is proven
in Theorem
6.17
. We want to see that
h(G)<\infty
assuming that

\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G<\infty
. In order to achieve that, we will proceed by contrapositive, i.e.,
we assume that
h(G)=\infty
and see that then
\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G
can not be finite.

Note that since
G
is countable,
G
is the direct union of
\{G_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\in\Lambda\}
,
where
G_{\lambda}
is finitely generated (and hence virtually polycyclic). Proceeding as we did in
in proof of Theorem
6.17
, we get that

\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G\geq\underset{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\operatorname{sup}}\{\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}}G_{\lambda}\}.

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
. We want to find
\lambda\in\Lambda
such that
\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}}G_{\lambda}>M
.
Since
G_{\lambda}
is virtually polycyclic for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, by
Corollary
6.15
,
\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}\cap G_{\lambda}}G_{\lambda}\geq h(G_{\lambda})-r
.
As
h(G)=\infty
and
h(G)=\underset{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\operatorname{sup}}\{h(G_{\lambda})\}
,
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is
\lambda(n)\in\Lambda
such that
h(G_{\lambda(n)})>n
. If we
take
\lambda=\lambda(M+r)
, we get that
\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r,\lambda}}G_{\lambda}>M+r-r=M
.
Therefore,
\cd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G
can not be finite, as we wanted to see.
∎

Analogously, in the case of the Bredon geometric dimension:

Theorem 6.19
.

Let
G
be a locally virtually polycyclic countable group and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\gd_{\mathfrak{H}_{r}}G<\infty
if and only if
h(G)<\infty
.

Proof.

Exchanging
\cd
by
\gd
in the previous theorem’s proof yields the desired result.
∎

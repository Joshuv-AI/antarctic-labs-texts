Abstract
. Let R R be a ring. In this paper, we study the characterization of cosilting modules and establish a relation between cosilting modules and cotilting objects in a Grothendieck category. We proved that each cosilting right R R -module T T can be described as a cotilting object in σ ⁡ [ R / I ] \sigma[R/I] , where I I is a right ideal of R R determined by T T and σ ⁡ [ R / I ] \sigma[R/I] is the full subcategory of right R R -modules, consisting of submodules of R / I R/I -generated modules. Conversely, under some suitable conditions, if T T is a cotilting object in σ ⁡ [ R / I ] \sigma[R/I] , then T T is cosilting.

1.
Introduction

In order to study
t
-structures in the bounded derived category of representations of Dynkin quivers, Keller and Vossieck in
[
7
]
introduced the notion of silting objects in triangulated categories. In
[
8
]
, Koenig and Yang established bijective correspondences between other important concepts such as simple-minded collections, bounded
t
-structures with length heart and bounded co-
t
-structures. So, silting theory plays an important role in the study of homotopy or derived categories.

Later, Angeleri-Hügel, Marks, and Vitória

[
2
]
introduced the notion of silting module, which generalizes the notion of tilting module over an arbitrary ring as well as the notion of support
\tau
-tilting module over a finite dimensional algebra (introduced by Adachi, etc. see
[
1
]
). Furthermore, they proved that there are bijections between silting
modules, 2-term silting complexes, certain
t
-structures
and co-
t
-structures in the derived module category. And all silting
modules are quasi-tilting modules, see
[
2
]
.

Recently, in
[
4
,
9
]
, Breaz, Pop, Zhang and Wei introduced the dual notion of silting modules, called cosilting modules. Zhang and Wei
[
9
]
proved that the three concepts AIR-cotilting modules, cosilting modules and quasi-cotilting modules (
[
10
]
) coincide with each other. Moreover, Breaz and Pop
[
4
]
gave several characterizations of cosilting modules. In particular, a finitely generated module
M
over an artin algebra is silting if and only if
M^{d}
is cosilting, where
(-)^{d}
is the standard duality. However, in the general case, the above statement is not valid and although
N
is cosilting,
N^{d}
is not necessarily silting, see the counterexamples
[
4
, Example 3.10 and 3.11]
.

Let
R
be a ring. From
[
6
]
, Wisbauer introduced the notion of
\sigma[M]
the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
formed by the
modules subgenerated by
M
. It was shown that a good
homology theory is possible in
\sigma[M]
. For example, it is a Grothendieck category with enough injective modules and arbitrary products exists.

In this short paper, we study the cotilting theory in
\sigma[M]
. Inspired by the framework of Breaz and Pop in
[
4
]
, we describe the connection between cosilting modules and cotilting objects in the subgenerated category
\sigma[M]
. Now, we present our main result as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R
be a ring and
\zeta:Q_{0}\rightarrow Q_{1}
is an
R
-homomorphism between injective modules with
T=\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\zeta
. Then
T
is a cosliting module in
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
if and only if there exists a right ideal
I
of
R
such that
T
and
I
satisfy the following conditions:

(1)

T
is a cotilting object in
\sigma[R/I]
with the injective coresolving

0\longrightarrow T\longrightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})\xrightarrow{\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\zeta)}\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{1})\longrightarrow 0.

(2)

\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\sigma[R/I]}(R/I,T)=0
.

(3)

I\in\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\mbox{Hom}_{R}(-,\mathcal{B}_{\zeta})
.

In particular, if
T
is a cosilting module over a commutative ring
R
or
T
is a finitely generated cosilting module over an artin algebra
R
, then
T
is a cotilting module over a factor algebra of
R
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some basic notions and results. In Section 3, we proved our main result and give an example to explain it.

For some unexplain notions, the readers refer to the references

,
3
,
6

.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
R
be a ring with unit element. Denote by
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
the category of right
R
-modules. If
\zeta:Q_{0}\rightarrow Q_{1}
is an
R
-homomorphism, then class
\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
is defined as

\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}=\{X\in\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR~|~\mbox{Hom}_{R}(X,\zeta)~\textrm{is an epimorphism}\}.

Definition 2.1
.

[
4
, Definition 3.1]

We say that an
R
-module
T
is:

(1)

partical cosilting (with respect to
\zeta
), if there exists an injective copresentation of
T

0\rightarrow T\xrightarrow{f}Q_{0}\xrightarrow{\zeta}Q_{1}

such that:

(a)

T\in\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
, and

(b)

the class
\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
is closed under direct products;

(2)

cosilting (with respect to
\zeta
), if there exists an injective copresentation

0\rightarrow T\xrightarrow{f}Q_{0}\xrightarrow{\zeta}Q_{1}

of

such that
\mathsf{Cogen}\hskip 0.72229ptT=\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
.

From
[
4
, Example 3.3]
, we can see that every (partial) cotilting module is (partial)
cosilting and, for every ring
R
, the trivial module
0
is cosilting. Moreover, even for some
hereditary rings, there exist non-zero cosilting modules which are not cotilting.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a non-empty set (class) of objects in a
subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
. Recall that an object
A
in
\mathcal{C}
is said to be generated by
\mathcal{U}
or
\mathcal{U}
-generated if, for every pair of distinct morphisms
f,g:A\rightarrow B
in
\mathcal{A}
, there is a morphism
h:U\rightarrow A
with
U\in\mathcal{U}
and
fh\neq gh
. In this case,
\mathcal{U}
is called a set (class) of
generators for
\mathcal{C}
. Dually, one can define the notions of cogenerated and cogenerators. Assume that the coproduct and product of
U
in
\mathcal{A}
exist.
\mathsf{Gen}\hskip 0.72229pt_{\mathcal{A}}U
denotes the objects class of
\mathcal{A}
which each object is an image of a coproduct of
U
in
\mathcal{A}
.
\mathsf{Cogen}\hskip 0.72229pt_{\mathcal{A}}U
denotes the objects class of
\mathcal{A}
which each object can be embedded into a product of
U
in
\mathcal{A}
.

For an
R
-module
L
of
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
, the submodule

\mathsf{Tr}_{\mathcal{U}}(L)=\sum\{\mathsf{Im}\hskip 0.72229ptf~|~f\in\mbox{Hom}_{R}(U,L),~U\in\mathcal{U}\}\subseteq L

is called the trace of
\mathcal{U}
in
L
, and

\mathsf{Rej}_{\mathcal{U}}(L)=\bigcap\{\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229ptf~|~f\in\mbox{Hom}_{R}(L,U),~U\in\mathcal{U}\}\subseteq L

is called the reject of
\mathcal{U}
in
L
.
It is easy to see that
\mathsf{Tr}_{\mathcal{U}}(L)
is the maximal submodule of
L
generated by
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathsf{Rej}_{\mathcal{U}}(L)
is the minimal submodule of
L
such that
L/\mathsf{Rej}_{\mathcal{U}}(L)
is cogenerated by
\mathcal{U}
. If an
R
-module
L
is generated by
\mathcal{U}
, then
\mathsf{Tr}_{\mathcal{U}}(L)=L
.

If
\mathcal{U}=\{U\}
, then

\mathsf{Tr}_{U}(L)=\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(u_{i})~|~u_{i}\in U,f_{i}\in\mbox{Hom}_{R}(U,L),k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

It is well-known that
\mathsf{Tr}_{U}(-)
defines a functor
from
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
into
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
. For any
R
-homomorphism
\zeta:L\rightarrow X
,
\mathsf{Tr}_{U}(\zeta):\mathsf{Tr}_{U}(L)\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{U}(X)
is defined by
\mathsf{Tr}_{U}(\zeta)(l)=\zeta(l)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\zeta f_{i}(u_{i})
for any
l=\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(u_{i})\in\mathsf{Tr}_{U}(L)
.
L
is (co)generated by
\mathcal{U}
if and only if
\mathsf{Tr}_{U}(L)=L
(
\mathsf{Rej}_{\mathcal{U}}(L)=0
). In particular,
\mathsf{Tr}_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathsf{Tr}_{\mathcal{U}}(L))=\mathsf{Tr}_{\mathcal{U}}(L)
and
\mathsf{Rej}_{\mathcal{U}}(L/\mathsf{Rej}_{\mathcal{U}}(L))=0
. From
[
3
]
, if
I
is a right ideal of
R
and
M
is an
R
-module, then
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(M)=\mathsf{Rej}_{I}(M)
. For more consequences about trace and reject, we refer to
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
.

\mathcal{A}
is called a Grothendieck category if direct limits are exact in
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}
has a generator. Let
M
be an

-module. Denote by

of modules in

, the product in

\mathsf{Tr}_{M}(W)
is an injective module in

.

3.
Main result

Recall that for an object
M
of
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
, the perpendicular category
{}^{\perp}M
of
M
in
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
is defined as follows

{}^{\perp}M=\{X\in\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR~|~\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{R}(X,M)=0\}.

Let
\zeta:Q_{0}\rightarrow Q_{1}
be an
R
-homomorphism in
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
. It has been shown that if
Q_{0}
is injective and
T=\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\zeta
, then
\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}\subseteq{{}^{\perp}T}
, see
[
4

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module. If for any set
\Lambda
,
T^{\Lambda}\in\sigma[M]
where
T^{\Lambda}
is the product of
T
in
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
, then the product
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{M}T_{\lambda}
of
T
in
\sigma[M]
is just the product of
T
in
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
, that is
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{M}T_{\lambda}=T^{\Lambda}
.

Proof
.

Assume that
\mathcal{M}_{f}=\{U\subset M^{\mathbb{N}}~|U~\text{finitely generated}\}
. From
[
6
, 15.1(4)]
,
U_{f}=\bigoplus\{U~|~U\in\mathcal{M}_{f}\}
is a generator of
\sigma[M]
. Since
T^{\Lambda}\in\sigma[M]
,
T^{\Lambda}
is generated by
U_{f}
. From
[
6
, 15.1(6)]
, we have that
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{M}T_{\lambda}=\mathsf{Tr}_{U_{f}}(T^{\Lambda})=T^{\Lambda}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category. For any integer
i\geq 1
,
\mathsf{Ext}^{i}_{\mathcal{A}}(A,B)
denotes the extension group, which
is defined via its Yoneda description as certain equivalent classes of
i
-fold extensions. Assume that the product of
T
in
\mathcal{A}
exists. Recall that
T
is said to be partial cotilting in an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
if
\mathsf{Cogen}\hskip 0.72229pt_{\mathcal{A}}T\subseteq\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(-,T)
in
\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, if
\mathsf{Cogen}\hskip 0.72229pt_{\mathcal{A}}T=\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(-,T)
in
\mathcal{A}
, then
T
is called cotilting.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
I
be a right ideal of a ring
R
and
\zeta:Q_{0}\rightarrow Q_{1}
is an
R
-homomorphism between injective modules with
T=\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\zeta
. If
T
is a cotilting object in
\sigma[R/I]
with the injective coresolving

(3.1)

0\longrightarrow T\longrightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})\xrightarrow{\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\zeta)}\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{1})\longrightarrow 0

in
\sigma[R/I]
and
\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\sigma[R/I]}(R/I,T)=0
, then

R/I\in\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
,
\mathsf{Cogen}\hskip 0.72229ptT\subseteq\sigma[R/I]
and
\mathsf{Cogen}\hskip 0.72229ptT\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
.

Proof
.

First, we prove that
R/I\in\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
. Applying the functor
\mbox{Hom}_{\sigma[R/I]}(R/I,-)
to the short exact sequence (
3.1
),
we have the following exact sequence

\mbox{Hom}_{\sigma[R/I]}(R/I,\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0}))\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}_{\sigma[R/I]}(R/I,\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{1}))\rightarrow\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\sigma[R/I]}(R/I,T)

From
[
3
, Exercises 8 (7)]
, we have isomorphisms

\mbox{Hom}_{R}(R/I,\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{i}))\cong\mbox{Hom}_{R}(R/I,Q_{i})
, for
i=1,2
. Since
\sigma[R/I]
is a full subcategory of
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
, we know that
\mbox{Hom}_{\sigma[R/I]}(R/I,\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0}))=\mbox{Hom}_{R}(R/I,\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{i}))
, for
i=1,2
.
Note that
\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\sigma[R/I]}(R/I,T)=0
. Thus, we have that
R/I\in\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
.

Assume that
T
is not zero. It remains to show that
T^{\mu}
is generated by
R/I
and
T^{\mu}\in\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
for all sets
\mu
since
\sigma[R/I]
and
\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
are closed under submodules.
We divided the proof into several steps.

Step 1
. We prove that
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(T^{\mu})
is a nonzero submodule of
T^{\mu}
.

If
\mbox{Hom}_{R}(R/I,T)=0
, then
R/I\in\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\mbox{Hom}_{\sigma[R/I]}(-,T)
. Note that
R/I\in\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}\subseteq{{}^{\perp}T}
. Then
R/I\in\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\sigma[R/I]}(-,T)
since
\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\sigma[R/I]}(R/I,T)\subseteq\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{R}(R/I,T)
. Since
T
is a cotilting object in
\sigma[R/I]
,
\mathsf{Cogen}\hskip 0.72229pt_{\sigma[R/I]}T=\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\sigma[R/I]}(-,T)
. Thus,
R/I
is cogenerated by
T
in
\sigma[R/I]
. Then there exist a monomorphism
R/I\rightarrow\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}^{R/I}T_{\theta}
, where
\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}^{R/I}T_{\theta}
is the product of
T
in
\sigma[R/I]
. By
[
6
, 15.1(6)]
, there exists a monomorphism
\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}^{R/I}T_{\theta}\rightarrow T^{\Theta}
, where
T^{\Theta}
is the product of
T
in
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
. Thus, there is a monomorphism
R/I\rightarrow T^{\Theta}
. It implies that
\mbox{Hom}_{R}(R/I,R/I)=0
. Thus,
R/I=0
and so
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})=0
. Form the exact sequence (
3.1
), we know that
T=0
. It is a contradiction. Hence,
\mbox{Hom}_{R}(R/I,T)\neq 0
and so
\mbox{Hom}_{R}(R/I,T^{\mu})\neq 0
. Therefore,
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(T^{\mu})
is a nonzero module.

Step 2
. We prove that
T^{\mu}
is generated by
R/I
.

From
[
3
, Exercises 8 (7)]
, we have an isomorphism

\mbox{Hom}_{R}(R/I,\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(T^{\mu}))\cong\mbox{Hom}_{R}(R/I,T^{\mu}).

Since
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(T^{\mu})
is generated by
R/I
, there exists a nonzero epimorphism of
R
-modules
f:{R/I}^{(\lambda)}\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(T^{\mu})
where
\lambda
is a set. For the set
\lambda
, there exists isomorphism

\mbox{Hom}_{R}({R/I}^{(\lambda)},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(T^{\mu}))\cong\mbox{Hom}_{R}({R/I}^{(\lambda)},T^{\mu}).

Then there exists an epimorphism
f^{\prime}:{R/I}^{(\lambda)}\rightarrow T^{\mu}
. Thus,
T^{\mu}
is generated by
R/I
. Thus,
\mathsf{Cogen}\hskip 0.72229ptT\subseteq\sigma[R/I]
since
\sigma[R/I]
is closed under submodules.

Step 3.
We claim that
\mbox{Hom}_{R}(T^{\mu},M/\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(M))=0
for any
M\in\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
.

We set
h\in\mbox{Hom}_{R}(T^{\mu},M/\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(M))
. For the epimorphism
f^{\prime}:{R/I}^{(\lambda)}\rightarrow T^{\mu}
, let
\varepsilon_{\lambda_{i}}:(R/I)_{\lambda_{i}}\rightarrow{R/I}^{(\lambda)}
be the
\lambda_{i}
-th canonical embedding with
\lambda_{i}\in\lambda
. Then for any
t\in T^{\mu}
, there exists a family
\{\overline{r_{i}}\}^{n}_{i=1}\in R/I
such that
\sum_{i}^{n}f^{\prime}\varepsilon_{\lambda_{i}}(\overline{r_{i}})=t
. Hence,
h(t)=\sum_{i}^{n}hf^{\prime}\varepsilon_{\lambda_{i}}(\overline{r_{i}})\in\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(M/\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(M))
. Note that
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(M/\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(M))=\mathsf{Rej}_{I}(M/\mathsf{Rej}_{I}(M))=0
. Thus, we know that
\mbox{Hom}_{R}(T^{\mu},M/\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(M))=0
.

Step 4.
We prove that
T^{\mu}\in\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
.

Since
T^{\mu}
is generated by
R/I
,
T^{\mu}\in\sigma[R/I]
. By Lemma
3.1
,
T^{\mu}=\prod_{\kappa\in\mu}^{R/I}T_{\kappa}
in
\sigma[R/I]
. Applying the functor
\mbox{Hom}_{\sigma[R/I]}(T^{\mu},-)
to the short exact sequence (
3.1
), we have the following exact sequence

\mbox{Hom}_{\sigma[R/I]}(T^{\mu},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0}))\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}_{\sigma[R/I]}(T^{\mu},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{1}))\rightarrow\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\sigma[R/I]}(T^{\mu},T)

Since
T
is a partial cotilting object in
\sigma[R/I]
and
\sigma[R/I]
is a full subcategory of
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
, we have
\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\sigma[R/I]}(T^{\mu},T)=\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{\sigma[R/I]}(\prod_{\kappa\in\mu}^{R/I}T_{\kappa},T)=0
and so,

\mbox{Hom}_{R}(T^{\mu},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\zeta)):\mbox{Hom}_{R}(T^{\mu},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0}))\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}_{R}(T^{\mu},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{1}))

is surjective. Applying
\mbox{Hom}_{R}(T^{\mu},-)
to the short exact sequence (
3.1
), we have the following exact sequence

(3.2)

\mbox{Hom}_{R}(T^{\mu},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0}))\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}_{R}(T^{\mu},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{1}))\rightarrow\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{R}(T^{\mu},T)\rightarrow\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{R}(T^{\mu},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})).

Now, we shall prove that
\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{R}(T^{\mu},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0}))=0
.
Applying
\mbox{Hom}_{R}(T,-)
to the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})\rightarrow Q_{0}\rightarrow Q_{0}/\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})\rightarrow 0,

we have the following exact sequence

(3.3)

\mbox{Hom}_{R}(T^{\mu},Q_{0}/\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0}))\rightarrow\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{R}(T^{\mu},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0}))\rightarrow\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{R}(T^{\mu},Q_{0}).

From the injectivity of
Q_{0}
in
\mathsf{Mod}\hskip 0.72229ptR
and Step 3, we have that
\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{R}(T^{\mu},\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0}))=0
.

From the exact sequence (
3.2
), we obtain that
\mathsf{Ext}^{1}_{R}(T^{\mu},T)=0
. That is,
T^{\mu}\in{{}^{\perp}T}
.

Now, we have a short exact sequence
0\rightarrow K\rightarrow{R/I}^{(\lambda)}\xrightarrow{f^{\prime}}T^{\mu}\rightarrow 0
with
{R/I}^{(\lambda)}\in\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
. Since
\mathcal{B}_{\zeta}
is closed under submodules,

is an inclusion map. Then we claim that we have the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(T)\xrightarrow{\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\iota)}\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})\xrightarrow{\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\zeta)}\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{1})\longrightarrow 0

First, we prove that
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\zeta):\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{1})
is surjective. We set

. Then,

can be written as
\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}(\overline{r_{i}})
, where

and
\overline{r_{i}}\in R/I
. Note that
\zeta^{\ast}
is surjective. That is, the induced map

is surjective. Then there exists a collection

of

-homomorphisms such that

for
1\leq i\leq k
. Hence,

, where
\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}(\overline{r_{i}})\in\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})
. Hence,
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\zeta):\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{1})
is surjective.

Second, we prove that
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\iota):\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(T)\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})
is injective. For any
x\in\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\iota)
, we write
x=\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(\overline{r_{i}})
. Then
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\iota)(x)=\iota(\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(\overline{r_{i}}))=0
. Since
\iota
is injective,

. Hence,
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\iota):\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(T)\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(Q_{0})
is injective.

Third, we prove that
\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\zeta)=\mathsf{Im}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\iota)
. For any
x=\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(\overline{r_{i}})\in\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(T)
, we have that
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\zeta)\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\iota)(\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(\overline{r_{i}}))=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\zeta\iota f_{i}(\overline{r_{i}})=0
since

. Thus,
\mathsf{Im}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\iota)\subseteq\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\zeta)
. On the other hand, for any

,
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\zeta)(\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}(\overline{r_{i}}))=\zeta(\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}(\overline{r_{i}}))=0
. Then,
y\in\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\zeta
. Note that
\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\zeta=\mathsf{Im}\hskip 0.72229pt\iota
. Thus, there exists
x\in T
such that
\iota(x)=y
. By the assumption, we know that
\mbox{Hom}_{R}(\pi,T):\mbox{Hom}_{R}(R/I,T)\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}_{R}(R,T)
is an isomorphism, where
\pi:R\rightarrow R/I
is a projection. Then,
T
is generated by
R/I
and for
x\in T
, there exists an
R
-homomorphism

such that
\mbox{Hom}_{R}(\pi,T)(h)(1)=h\pi(1)=x
where

is the unit of
R
. Thus,
y=\iota(h\pi(1))\in\mathsf{Im}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\iota)
and
\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\zeta)\subseteq\mathsf{Im}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(\iota)
.

We get the desired sequence from the fact
\mathsf{Tr}_{R/I}(T)=T
which has been proved above.
∎

Supersymmetric Shimura operators and interpolation polynomials
Siddhartha SAHI, Songhao ZHU

Abstract
. The Shimura operators are a certain distinguished basis for invariant differential operators on a Hermitian symmetric space. Answering a question of Shimura, Sahi–Zhang showed that the Harish-Chandra images of these operators are specializations of certain B ​ C BC -symmetric interpolation polynomials that were defined by Okounkov. We consider the analogs of Shimura operators for the Hermitian symmetric superpair ( 𝔤 , 𝔨 ) (\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k}) where 𝔤 = 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ​ p | 2 ​ q ) \mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(2p|2q) and 𝔨 = 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( p | q ) ⊕ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( p | q ) \mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{gl}(p|q)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(p|q) and we prove their Harish-Chandra images are specializations of certain B ​ C BC -supersymmetric interpolation polynomials introduced by Sergeev–Veselov.



1.
Introduction

The Harish-Chandra homomorphism for a symmetric space gives an explicit isomorphism of the algebra of invariant differential operators with a certain polynomial algebra, and knowing the Harish-Chandra image of an operator allows one to determine its spectrum. In
[
Shi90
]
Shimura introduced a basis for the algebra of invariant differential operators on a
Hermitian
symmetric space, and formulated the problem of determining their eigenvalues. Shimura’s problem was solved in
[
SZ19
]
where it was shown that the Harish-Chandra images of Shimura operators are specializations of certain interpolation polynomials of Type
BC
introduced by Okounkov
[
Oko98
]
.

Our main result, Theorem A below, solves the analogous problem for the Hermitian symmetric superpair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
with
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(2p|2q)
and
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{gl}(p|q)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(p|q)
.

Let
\mathfrak{U}
be the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, then the algebra of invariant differential operators is the quotient of
\mathfrak{k}
-invariants
\mathfrak{D}:=\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{k}}/(\mathfrak{U}\mathfrak{k})^{\mathfrak{k}}
. In Section
3.3
we describe a basis
\mathscr{D}_{\mu}
of
\mathfrak{D}
indexed by the set
\mathscr{H}(p,q)
of
(p,q)
-hook partitions, which are partitions
\mu
that satisfy
\mu_{p+1}\leq q
.
These
\mathscr{D}_{\mu}
are the super analogs of Shimura operators, and their definition involves the Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{-}
, and the multiplicity-free
\mathfrak{k}
-decompositions of
\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
and
\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
, which have
\mathfrak{k}
-summands
W_{\mu}
and
W_{\mu}^{*}
, naturally indexed by
\mu\in\mathscr{H}(p,q)

[
CW01
]
.

The Harish-Chandra homomorphism (Section
2
) is an algebra map
\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}:\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{P}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})
, where
\mathfrak{a}
is an even Cartan subspace of
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{P}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})
is the algebra of polynomials on
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. For our pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
the image of
\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}
can be identified with the ring
\mathscr{S}^{\mathtt{0}}_{p,q}
of even supersymmetric polynomials in
p+q
variables.
In Section
6
we introduce a basis
\{J_{\mu}:\mu\in\mathscr{H}(p,q)\}
of
\mathscr{S}^{\mathtt{0}}_{p,q}
. These
J_{\mu}
are suitable specializations of the supersymmetric interpolation polynomials of Type
BC
introduced by Sergeev–Veselov
[
SV09
]
, which are characterized up to multiple by certain vanishing properties.

Theorem A
.

The Harish-Chandra image of
\mathscr{D}_{\mu}
is
k_{\mu}J_{\mu}
where
k_{\mu}
is explicitly given in Eq. (
55
).

We will deduce this from two other results. First, let
\mathfrak{Z}
be the center of
\mathfrak{U}
, then we have a natural map
\pi:\mathfrak{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{k}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{D}
, and we prove the following result.

Theorem B
.

The map
\pi
is surjective. In particular, there exist
Z_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{Z}
such that
\pi(Z_{\mu})=\mathscr{D}_{\mu}
.

For
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(p,q)
let
I_{\lambda}
be the generalized Verma module for
\mathfrak{g}
obtained by parabolic induction from
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}
of the
\mathfrak{k}
-summand
W_{\lambda}
of
\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
(Section
5
).

Theorem C
.

The central element
Z_{\mu}
acts on
I_{\lambda}
by
0
unless
\lambda_{i}\geq\mu_{i}
for all
i
.

The special case
p=q=1
of Theorem
A
was proved previously in
[
Zhu22
]
, where it was also shown that the general case would follow if one knew that certain irreducible finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-modules are
\mathfrak{k}
-spherical
[
Zhu22
, Conjecture 1, Theorem A]
. For ordinary Lie algebras, the Cartan–Helgason Theorem
[

]
provides necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mathfrak{k}
-sphericity. However its precise analog for Lie superalgebras remains open and seems quite hard. Therefore in this paper we give a different argument for Theorem
A
, which does not rely on the
\mathfrak{k}
-sphericity conjecture.

The Shimura problem discussed in this paper is closely related to the Capelli problem studied in
[
KS93
,
Sah94
]
for usual Lie algebras, in
[
SS16
,
ASS18
]
, and
[
SSS20
]
for Lie superalgebra, and more recently
[

,

,

]
for quantum groups. Thus it is possible that the results of this paper can be generalized to many of the Hermitian symmetric superpairs constructed using Jordan superalgebras, e.g. in

The structure of the paper is as follows.
In Section

, we review some known results related to the pair

-modules. We also present the Sergeev–Veselov supersymmetric interpolation polynomials of Type

In Section

In Section

(Proposition
4.1

in Subsection

.
In Section

Finally in Section

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we assume that
\mathbb{N}
, the set of natural numbers, includes 0.
For a finite dimensional vector space
V
, we identify the polynomial algebra on
V
,
\mathfrak{P}(V)
, with the symmetric algebra
\mathfrak{S}(V^{*})
on the dual of
V
.
We denote the
\mathbb{C}
-vector superspace of superdimension
m|n
by
\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
where the even subspace is
\mathbb{C}^{m}
and the odd subspace is
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
A
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded algebra is called a superalgebra. For any superalgebra
A
, we denote its even subspace as
A_{\overline{0}}
and the odd subspace as
A_{\overline{1}}
. For

, we write
|x|=i
for its parity

.
The space of linear endomorphisms
\End(\mathbb{C}^{m|n})
is naturally
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded and identified with the space of
(m+n)\times(m+n)
matrices.
We denote the Lie superalgebra structure defined on
\End(\mathbb{C}^{m|n})
as
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
.
In this subsection, we denote the standard diagonal Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
as
\mathfrak{t}
, that is,
\mathfrak{t}=\Spn\{E_{i,i}:1\leq i\leq m+n\}
where
E_{i,j}
denotes the matrix with 1 in the
(i,j)
-th entry and 0 elsewhere.
We also let
\epsilon_{i}
and
\delta_{j}
be the coordinate functions of
E_{i,i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and of
E_{m+j,m+j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
respectively.

2.1.
Symmetric superpairs

In this subsection, we let

We assume the chosen nondegenerate invariant form

As in Subsection

.

The Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem applied to Eq. (

.

Next, we introduce Alldridge’s result on spherical representations.
Suppose

is a

-invariant Cartan subalgebra extended from

. If

-

Define

Then by

The following Lemma is

2.2.
The Harish-Chandra homomorphism for
\mathfrak{Z}(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))

Let

, and

be the standard Cartan subalgebra. We denote the root system of

2.3.
Cheng–Wang decomposition

Let
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
.
We recall a multiplicity-free
\mathfrak{k}
-module decomposition, known as Howe duality in
[

[

,

]

It will allow us to define the supersymmetric Shimura operators in Section

We first introduce some notation. A
partition

\lambda
is a sequence of non-negative integers
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
with only finitely many non-zero terms and
\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+1}
(c.f.
[
Mac95
]

Let
|\lambda|:=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}
denote the
size
of
\lambda
,
\ell(\lambda):=\max\{i:\lambda_{i}>0\}
the
length
of
\lambda
,
and
\lambda^{\prime}
for which
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}:=|\{j:\lambda_{j}\geq i\}|
the
transpose
of
\lambda
. When viewed as the corresponding Young diagram,
\lambda
is the collection of “boxes”
(i,j)

\{(i,j):1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda),1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\}.

A
(m,n)
-hook partition
is a partition
\lambda
such that
\lambda_{m+1}\leq n
. We define

\mathscr{H}(m,n):=\left\{\lambda:\lambda_{m+1}\leq n\right\},\quad\mathscr{H}^{d}(m,n):=\left\{\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m,n):|\lambda|=d\right\}.

For
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m,n)
, we define a
(m+n)
-tuple

(9)

\lambda^{\natural}:=\left(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m},\left\langle\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-m\right\rangle,\dots,\left\langle\lambda_{n}^{\prime}-m\right\rangle\right)

where
\langle x\rangle:=\max\{x,0\}
for

.
The last
n
coordinates can be viewed as the lengths of the remaining columns after discarding the first
m
rows of
\lambda
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be any Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
containing a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)_{\overline{0}}
. Then
\mathfrak{B}
can be described by an

Step 2.

Furthermore, we see that

Step 3.

5.
Generalized Verma Modules

In this section, we study certain generalized Verma modules, denoted as
I_{\lambda}
. These are the
\mathfrak{g}
-modules induced on
W_{\lambda}
(
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(p,q)
) from a parabolic subalgebra containing
\mathfrak{k}
to
\mathfrak{g}
. If we choose a Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
as the parabolic subalgebra, then we obtain the usual Verma module, which explains the name.
We show that
I_{\lambda}
is spherical.
We investigate a natural grading on
I_{\lambda}
, and use it to show that certain central elements
Z_{\mu}
act trivially on
I_{\lambda}
trivially whenever
|\lambda|\leq|\mu|
.
Further strengthening is also obtained, asserting the same vanishing action of
Z_{\mu}
for
\lambda\nsupseteq\mu
. This is Theorem
C
.

5.1.
Basics

Consider the parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{q}:=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}
in
\mathfrak{g}
. The associated set of roots is given by
\Sigma(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\cup\Sigma(\mathfrak{p}^{+},\mathfrak{t})
.
Let
W_{\lambda}
be as in the Cheng–Wang decomposition. We extend the
\mathfrak{k}
-action trivially to
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
to obtain a
\mathfrak{q}
-module structure on
W_{\lambda}
.
We define the
generalized Verma module
as

I_{\lambda}:=\Ind_{\mathfrak{k}+\mathfrak{p}^{+}}^{\mathfrak{g}}W_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{U}\otimes_{\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{q})}W_{\lambda}.

By the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem, we see that
\mathfrak{U}=\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{k})\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
, and we have

I_{\lambda}\cong\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})\otimes W_{\lambda}

both as super vector spaces and as
\mathfrak{k}
-modules. Clearly,
I_{\lambda}
is a weight module and the highest weight is Eq. (
28
), given with respect to
\mathfrak{b}^{\natural}
(see Eq. (
33
)).
Then
V(\lambda^{\natural})
is the irreducible quotient of
I_{\lambda}
.

Next, we introduce a grading on
I_{\lambda}
. Recall that in the one-dimensional center of
\mathfrak{k}
, there is a special element
J
(see Section
3
) such that the Harish-Chandra decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}

corresponds to
(-1,0,1)
-eigenspaces of
\ad J
, and gives rise to the short grading.
We may extend the action of
\ad J
to
\mathfrak{U}=\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{k})\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
.
Let
\{\xi_{i}\},\{x_{i}\}
and
\{\eta_{i}\}
be homogeneous bases for
\mathfrak{p}^{-}
,
\mathfrak{k}
, and
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
respectively.
Then the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt basis is:

\left\{\xi_{i_{1}}\cdots\xi_{i_{m}}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{l}}\eta_{k_{1}}\cdots\eta_{k_{n}}:i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{m},j_{1}\leq\cdots\leq j_{l},k_{1}\leq\cdots\leq k_{n}\right\}.

where a strict inequality of subindices occurs when the corresponding basis vectors are odd.
We let

\mathfrak{U}^{d}:=\Spn\{\xi_{i_{1}}\cdots\xi_{i_{m}}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{l}}\eta_{k_{1}}\cdots\eta_{k_{n}}:n-m=d\}.

Lemma 5.1
.

The universal enveloping algebra
\mathfrak{U}
is graded by
\ad J
, and
\mathfrak{U}=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{U}^{d}
.

Proof.

The action of
J
on the basis
\xi_{i_{1}}\cdots\xi_{i_{m}}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{l}}\eta_{k_{1}}\cdots\eta_{k_{n}}
is given by the scalar
n-m=d
in
\mathfrak{U}^{d}
. The bases for
\mathfrak{U}^{d}
for all
d\geq 0
give a partition of the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt basis, and the sum is hence direct.
Furthermore, the multiplication in
\mathfrak{U}
respects the Lie superbracket, which in turn respects the short grading on
\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore the grading is well-defined.
∎

Let
l=|\lambda|
. Then
I_{\lambda}
is also endowed with the
\ad J
grading. Explicitly,

I_{\lambda}^{l-d}:=\mathfrak{S}^{d}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})\otimes W_{\lambda}

has degree
l-d
for
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
In particular, its top homogeneous component is
\mathbb{C}\otimes W_{\lambda}
of degree
l
, isomorphic to
W_{\lambda}
.
By Lemma
5.1
, since the
\mathfrak{g}
acts by left multiplication on
I_{\lambda}
, we have
\mathfrak{U}^{d}.I_{\lambda}^{m}\subseteq I_{\lambda}^{d+m}
. This leads to the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
u\in\mathfrak{S}^{k}(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
. Then
u.I_{\lambda}^{m}\subseteq I_{\lambda}^{k+m}
.

Next, we show that
I_{\lambda}
is in fact spherical.

Proposition 5.3
.

The module
I_{\lambda}
is spherical. Furthermore,
\dim I_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{k}}=1
and
I_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{k}}\subseteq I_{\lambda}^{0}
.

Proof.

As a
\mathfrak{k}
-module, we have

I_{\lambda}\cong\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathscr{H}(p,q)}W_{\nu}^{*}\otimes W_{\lambda}
and each component can be identified as
\Hom(W_{\nu},W_{\lambda})
.
Only the degree 0 component
W_{\lambda}^{*}\otimes W_{\lambda}\cong\End\left(W_{\lambda}\right)
has a one-dimensional
\mathfrak{k}
-invariant subspace by Schur’s Lemma for Type
M
modules.
Hence
I_{\lambda}
is spherical with a unique up to constant spherical vector.
∎

By the above Proposition,
\omega\neq 0
in
I_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{k}}
is unique up to constant. Let us fix such
\omega\in I_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{k}}
in the following discussion.

5.2.
Vanishing actions

Recall that the supersymmetric Shimura operator
D_{\mu}
in
\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{k}}
is defined as the image of
1_{\mu}
under the following composition of maps (Definition
3.2
)

\displaystyle\left(W_{\mu}^{*}\right.\otimes\left.W_{\mu}\right)^{\mathfrak{k}}\hookrightarrow\left(\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})\otimes\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{+})\right)^{\mathfrak{k}}\rightarrow

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{k}}

\displaystyle 1_{\mu}\xmapsto{\hphantom{\left(W_{\mu}^{*}\right.\otimes\left.W_{\mu}\right)^{\mathfrak{k}}\hookrightarrow\left(\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})\otimes\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{+})\right)^{\mathfrak{k}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{k}}}}

\displaystyle D_{\mu}

In particular
D_{\mu}\in W_{\mu}^{*}W_{\mu}
.
If we take a homogeneous basis
\{\eta^{\mu}_{i}\}
for
W_{\mu}

and its dual basis
\{\xi^{-\mu}_{i}\}
for
W_{\mu}^{*}
, then
D_{\mu}
can be written as

(49)

D_{\mu}=\sum\xi^{-\mu}_{i}\eta^{\mu}_{i}.

Let
m=|\mu|
and
l=|\lambda|
. We have the following “vanishing” actions (Propositions
5.4
and
5.6
).

Proposition 5.4
.

If
l<m
, then
D_{\mu}.v^{0}=0
for any
v^{0}\in I_{\lambda}^{0}
.

Proof.

In
D_{\mu}
, every
\eta^{\mu}_{i}
in Eq. (
49
) has degree
m
as an element in
\mathfrak{S}^{m}(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
. Hence
\eta^{\mu}_{i}.I_{\lambda}^{0}\subseteq I_{\lambda}^{m}
by Lemma
5.2
. The top degree of
I_{\lambda}
is
l
. But
l<m
. Thus
D_{\mu}.I_{\lambda}^{0}=I_{\lambda}^{m}=\{0\}
.
∎

Viewing
W_{\mu}\subseteq\mathfrak{U}
, we have the representation
R:W_{\mu}\otimes I_{\lambda}\rightarrow I_{\lambda}
given by
\eta\otimes v\mapsto\eta.v
for
\eta\in W_{\mu}
and
v\in I_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

The image of
R
restricted to
W_{\mu}\otimes I_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{k}}
is homomorphic to
W_{\mu}
.

Proof.

Let
m=|\mu|
.
For each
\eta^{\mu}_{i}.\omega\in I_{\lambda}^{m}
, if we apply a homogeneous

, we get

(50)

X.(\eta^{\mu}_{i}.\omega)=[X,\eta^{\mu}_{i}].\omega+(-1)^{|X||\eta^{\mu}_{i}|}\eta^{\mu}_{i}.X.\omega=(\ad(X)(\eta^{\mu}_{i})).\omega

as
X.\omega=0
. Thus, the map
\eta^{\mu}_{i}\mapsto\eta^{\mu}_{i}.\omega
intertwines with the actions of
\mathfrak{k}
on
W_{\mu}
and
I_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{k}}
.

Let
S
be the subspace of
I_{\lambda}^{m}
spanned by
\{\eta^{\mu}_{i}.\omega\}
. Then
S
is a homomorphic image of
W_{\mu}
in
I_{\lambda}
by Eq. (
50
).
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

If
l=m
but
\lambda\neq\mu
, then
D_{\mu}.\omega=0
.

Proof.

Since
W_{\mu}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}^{l}(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
and
I_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{k}}\subseteq I_{\lambda}^{0}
, by Lemma
5.2
, we see that

W_{\mu}\otimes I_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{k}}\rightarrow I_{\lambda}^{l}=\mathbb{C}\otimes W_{\lambda}.

By Lemma
5.5
,
W_{\mu}
is homomorphic to some
S
in
I_{\lambda}^{l}
.
Since
\lambda\neq\mu
,
I_{\lambda}^{l}=\{0\}
. Hence,
W_{\mu}
acts on
I_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{k}}
by 0, and so does
D_{\mu}
.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(p,q)
such that
|\lambda|\leq|\mu|
,
\lambda\neq\mu
, we have
D_{\mu}.\omega=0
in
I_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

It follows from Propositions
5.3
,
5.4
for the case
|\lambda|<|\mu|
, and
5.6
for the case
|\lambda|=|\mu|
.
∎

Recall
\pi(D_{\mu})=\mathscr{D}_{\mu}
.
By Theorem
B
,
\pi
is surjective.
Then it is possible to find
Z_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{Z}
such that
\pi(Z_{\mu})=\mathscr{D}_{\mu}
.
We show that
Z_{\mu}
acts by 0 on
I_{\lambda}
when
\lambda
satisfies certain condition.

Proposition 5.8
.

The central element
Z_{\mu}
acts on
I_{\lambda}
by
0
when
|\lambda|\leq|\mu|
, and
\lambda\neq\mu
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
,
D_{\mu}
acts on
\omega\in I_{\lambda}
by a scalar
c_{\mu}(\lambda)
for any
\lambda
.
On the other hand, the action of
\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{k}}
on a spherical vector
v^{\mathfrak{k}}
in a
\mathfrak{g}
-module descends to an action of
\mathfrak{D}=\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{k}}/(\mathfrak{U}\mathfrak{k})^{\mathfrak{k}}
by setting

.
Hence, in

, there is no ambiguity of writing

(51)

Therefore, by Corollary

,

(52)

c_{\mu}(\lambda)=0,\;\text{if }|\lambda|\leq|\mu|,\lambda\neq\mu.

Since

acts on the entirety of a highest weight module by a scalar and

is a highest weight module, such scalar has to be

which vanishes according to Eq. (

).
∎

5.3.
A stronger result

What is proved in the previous subsection turns out to be enough for the purposes of this paper (Theorem
A
). Nonetheless, we give a better description of such vanishing actions, proving Theorem
C
.

First, we recall the following “weight decomposition” proposition from
[
Zhu22
]
, parallel to Proposition 3.2 in
[
Kum10
]
, and is first due to Kostant
[
Kos59
]
. We let
\mathfrak{L}
be a Lie (super)algebra,
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{L}
be a Borel subalgebra.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be two finite dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{L}
-modules with highest weight
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
, and
V
be a weight module of
\mathfrak{L}
. If
\Hom_{\mathfrak{L}}\left(L_{1},L_{2}\otimes V\right)\neq\{0\}
, then
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}+\nu
for some weight
\nu
of
V
.

We would like to show the following branching statement, i.e.,
W_{\mu}
occurs in
I_{\lambda}
as a homomorphic image if
\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
.
This result is similar to the one regarding the irreducible representation
V(\lambda^{\natural})
in
[
Zhu22
]
.

Proposition 5.10
.

If
\Hom_{\mathfrak{k}}\left(W_{\mu},I_{\lambda}\right)\neq\{0\}
, then
\lambda\supseteq\mu
.

Proof.

The
\mathfrak{k}
-module structure on
I_{\lambda}
is given by
\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})\otimes W_{\lambda}
.
Let us denote the representation map on
\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})\otimes W_{\lambda}
(respectively
W_{\lambda}\otimes\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
) as
\pi
(respectively
\pi^{\prime}
).
Then the braiding map
s:\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})\otimes W_{\lambda}\rightarrow W_{\lambda}\otimes\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
gives a canonical module isomorphism, meaning that if
W_{\mu}
occurs in
I_{\lambda}
, then it also occurs in
W_{\lambda}\otimes\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
.

By Proposition
5.9
,
\nu=\mu^{\natural}_{\mathfrak{t}}-\lambda^{\natural}_{\mathfrak{t}}
is a weight of
\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
. Here,
\mu^{\natural}_{\mathfrak{t}}
and
\lambda^{\natural}_{\mathfrak{t}}
are weights in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. As both
\mu^{\natural}_{\mathfrak{t}}
and
\lambda^{\natural}_{\mathfrak{t}}
are integral combinations of
\gamma\in\Sigma_{\perp}
, so is
\nu
. Since
\Sigma_{\perp}\subseteq\Sigma(\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\overline{0}},\mathfrak{t})
, such integral coefficients in
\nu
must be non-positive. Equivalently,
\lambda\supseteq\mu
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
C
.

As in the proof of Proposition
5.8
,
c_{\mu}(\lambda)
is the scalar by which
D_{\mu}
acts on
I_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{k}}
. Again by Eq. (
51
) and the fact that
Z_{\mu}
has to act uniformly by a scalar on
I_{\lambda}
,
c_{\mu}(\lambda)
is the scalar by which
D_{\mu}
acts on the entirety of
I_{\lambda}
.
By Lemma
5.5
, the representation of
W_{\mu}
on

always gives a homomorphic image of itself in

. If
\lambda\nsupseteq\mu
, then this image is zero by Proposition

.
Spelling out

as in Eq. (

), we see that

.
Thus,

(53)

c_{\mu}(\lambda)=0,\;\text{if }\lambda\nsupseteq\mu,

and this proves that

acts on

by 0 unless
\lambda_{i}\geq\mu_{i}
for all

.
∎

Eq. (

) gives more vanishing points of

than we actually need for proving Theorem

in Section

which concerns only zeros at lower degrees.
In fact, this is much closer to the original formulation of the vanishing properties, introduced in Theorem

, c.f. Proposition

.

We show that

\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathscr{D}_{\mu})=k_{\mu}J_{\mu}.

Step 1.

From the definition of
{\Gamma}
and
\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}
, we see that
\deg\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathscr{D}_{\mu})\leq 2|\mu|
.
By (
1
), we have
\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\pi(Z_{\mu}))=\Res(\gamma(Z_{\mu}))
.
The left side is just
\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathscr{D}_{\mu})
.
The right side, when applied to
2\overline{\lambda^{\natural}}+\rho
is just
\gamma(Z_{\mu})(2\lambda^{\natural}_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho_{\mathfrak{h}})

by Eq. (
42
) (c.f. Eq. (
46
)). Hence

(56)

\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathscr{D}_{\mu})(2\overline{\lambda^{\natural}}+\rho)=\gamma(Z_{\mu})(2\lambda^{\natural}_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho).

By Theorem
C
,
Z_{\mu}
acts by 0 on
I_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\nsupseteq\mu
,
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}
, which exactly gives
\gamma(Z_{\mu})(2\lambda^{\natural}_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho)=0
. Thus

\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathscr{D}_{\mu})(2\overline{\lambda^{\natural}}+\rho)=0

for all
\lambda\nsupseteq\mu
,
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}
, proving the vanishing properties.

Alternatively, by Proposition
5.8
, we have the “relaxed” vanishing properties
\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathscr{D}_{\mu})(2\overline{\lambda^{\natural}}+\rho)=0
when
\lambda\neq\mu
,
|\lambda|\leq|\mu|
.
By Proposition
6.2
, this also implies the full “extra” vanishing properties.

Therefore,
\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathscr{D}_{\mu})
is proportional to
J_{\mu}
by its vanishing properties.

Step 2.

We first pick a basis for
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
extended from

\{\mathsf{i}E_{i,p+i},\mathsf{i}D_{j,q+j}\},

and a dual basis for
\mathfrak{p}^{-}\cong(\mathfrak{p}^{+})^{*}
(identified via the bilinear form
b
) extended from

\{-2\mathsf{i}E_{i,p+i},2\mathsf{i}D_{j,q+j}\}.

Then the identity map
1_{\mathfrak{p}^{+}}\in\End_{\mathfrak{k}}(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
corresponds to

(57)

\sum_{i=1}^{p}\mathsf{i}E_{i,p+i}\otimes(-2\mathsf{i}E_{i,p+i})+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\mathsf{i}D_{j,p+j}\otimes(2\mathsf{i}D_{j,p+j})+\text{other terms}

in
\mathfrak{p}^{-}\otimes\mathfrak{p}^{+}
.
On the other hand, we may extend the basis
\{x_{i},y_{j}\}
for
\mathfrak{a}
(see Subsection
3.1
) to a basis for
\mathfrak{g}
according to
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{k}
. This is used to define
{\Gamma}
and
\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}
.
Then it is a direct computation to see that the top homogeneous degree of

{\Gamma}(D_{(1)})=\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathscr{D}_{(1)})

is precisely

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{q}y_{j}^{2}.

Note the
\rho
shift does not change this top degree.

Now we let
\mu\in\mathscr{H}^{m}
. The identity map
1_{\mu}
on
W_{\mu}
corresponds to an element in
W_{\mu}^{*}\otimes W_{\mu}
.
Thus the sum
\sum_{\mu\in\mathscr{H}^{m}}1_{\mu}
corresponds to an element in
\mathfrak{S}^{m}(\mathfrak{p}^{-})\otimes\mathfrak{S}^{m}(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
by the decomposition in Proposition
3.1
.
On
\mathfrak{S}^{m}(\mathfrak{p})\subseteq T^{m}(\mathfrak{p})
, the bilinear form is induced from
b
on
\mathfrak{p}
by normalizing
b^{\otimes m}
by
1/m!
.
Then similarly, we see that the top homogeneous degree of
\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}\left(\sum_{\mu\in\mathscr{H}^{m}}\mathscr{D}_{\mu}\right)
is given by

(58)

\frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{q}y_{j}^{2}\right)^{m}=\frac{1}{2^{m}m!}\left(p_{2}^{(m,n)}(x_{i},y_{j})\right)^{m}.

Note
d_{\lambda}
(Eq. (
12
)) specializes to
(-1)^{|\lambda|}
.
From Eq. (
11
), Theorems
2.6
and
2.7
, we see that

I^{\textup{SV}}_{\mu^{\prime}}\left(x_{i},y_{j};-1,q-p+\frac{1}{2}\right)=(-1)^{|\mu|}\hat{I}_{\mu}\left(x_{i},y_{j};-1,\frac{1}{2}-(q-p)\right).

and the top homogeneous degree of is
(-1)^{|\mu|}SP_{\mu}(x_{i}^{2},y_{j}^{2};1)
.
For
J_{\mu}
, this becomes

(-1/2)^{|\mu|}SP_{\mu}(x_{i}^{2},y_{j}^{2};1).

Thus by setting
\theta=1
in Eq. (
16
),

(59)

\frac{1}{2^{m}m!}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{q}y_{j}^{2}\right)^{m}=\sum_{\mu\in\mathscr{H}^{m}}2^{-m}C_{\mu}^{-}(1;-1)(-2)^{|\mu|}\left((-1/2)^{|\mu|}SP_{\mu}(x_{i}^{2},y_{j}^{2};1)\right).

Comparing the coefficients, we have

(60)

\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathscr{D}_{\mu})=(-1)^{|\mu|}C_{\mu}^{-}(1;-1)J_{\mu}.

Therefore the coefficient is precisely
k_{\mu}
given by Eq. (
55
).
∎

Additionally, we can answer the interesting question of by which scalar
D_{\mu}
acts on the spherical vector
\omega\in I_{\mu}^{\mathfrak{k}}
, without any direct consideration on the module
I_{\mu}
itself!

Corollary 6.3
.

The operator
D_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{k}}
acts on
I_{\mu}^{\mathfrak{k}}
by
(-1)^{|\mu|}C_{\mu}^{-}(1;-1)^{2}C_{\mu}^{+}(2q-2p;-1)
.

Proof.

This scalar is the value of
{\Gamma}(D_{\mu})=\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathscr{D}_{\mu})
at
2\overline{\mu^{\natural}}+\rho
which equals the value of
k_{\mu}I^{\textup{SV}}_{\mu^{\prime}}
at
(z(\mu^{\prime}),w(\mu^{\prime}))
:

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathscr{D}_{\mu})(2\overline{\mu^{\natural}}+\rho)

\displaystyle=k_{\mu}C_{\mu^{\prime}}^{-}(1;-1)C_{\mu^{\prime}}^{+}(2q-2p;-1)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\mu|}C_{\mu}^{-}(1;-1)^{2}C_{\mu}^{+}(2q-2p;-1).

∎

Growth in the universal cover under large simplicial volume
Hannah Alpert

Abstract
. Consider a closed manifold M M with two Riemannian metrics: one hyperbolic metric, and one other metric g g . What hypotheses on g g guarantee that for a given radius r r , there are balls of radius r r in the universal cover of ( M , g ) (M,g) with greather-than-hyperbolic volumes? We show that this conclusion holds for all r ≥ 1 r\geq 1 if ( Vol ⁡ ( M , g ) ) 2 (\Vol(M,g))^{2} is less than a small constant times the hyperbolic volume of M M . This strengthens a theorem of Sabourau and is partial progress toward a conjecture of Guth.



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian manifold. For each
r>0
we let
V_{r}(M)
denote the supremal volume of a ball of radius
r
in
M
. We let
\|M\|_{\Delta}
denote the Gromov simplicial volume of
M
; the definition appears in Section
3
. The relevant properties are that
\|M\|_{\Delta}
depends only on the fundamental group of
M
, and if
M
is hyperbolic then
\|M\|_{\Delta}
is equal to the hyperbolic volume of
M
times a constant depending only on the dimension of
n
. The purpose of this paper is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(Main theorem)
.

For every
n\geq 2
, there exists
\delta_{n}>0
such that if
M
is a closed, oriented, connected
n
-dimensional manifold admitting a hyperbolic metric, and
g
is another Riemannian metric on
M
with
\frac{(\Vol(M,g))^{2}}{\|M\|_{\Delta}}<\delta_{n}
, then for all
r\geq 1
we have

V_{r}(\widetilde{M})\geq V_{r}(\mathbb{H}^{n}),

where
\widetilde{M}
denotes the universal cover of
(M,g)
, and
\mathbb{H}^{n}
denotes the hyperbolic
n
-space.

Theorem
1
strengthens the following theorem of Sabourau and also uses a large part of its proof.

Theorem 2

(
[
Sab22
]
)
.

For every
n\geq 2
, there exists
\delta_{n}>0
such that if
M
is a closed, oriented, connected
n
-dimensional manifold admitting a hyperbolic metric, and
g
is another Riemannian metric on
M
with
\Vol(M,g)<\delta_{n}
, then for all
r\geq 1
we have

V_{r}(\widetilde{M})\geq V_{r}(\mathbb{H}^{n}).

Sabourau’s proof uses Papasoglu’s method of area-minimizing separating sets, introduced in
[
Pap20
]
. The part of the proof of Theorem
1
that is different from the proof of Theorem
2
is contained mainly in
[
Alp22
, Theorem 5]
, which combines Papasoglu’s method with simplicial volume to prove the following theorem, originally due to Guth.

Theorem 3

(Theorem 2 of
[
Gut11
]
)
.

For every
n\geq 2
, there exists
\delta_{n}>0
such that if
M
is a closed, oriented, connected
n
-dimensional manifold admitting a hyperbolic metric, and
g
is another Riemannian metric on
M
with
\frac{\Vol(M,g)}{\|M\|_{\Delta}}<\delta_{n}
, then we have

V_{1}(\widetilde{M})\geq V_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n}).

The most ambitious conjecture along these lines is
[
Gut11
, Conjecture 2]
, which says that if
\Vol(M,g)<\Vol(M,\mathrm{hyp})
, then
V_{r}(\widetilde{M})>V_{r}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
for all
r
. This statement strengthens the theorem of Besson, Courtois, and Gallot which proves the conclusion for all sufficiently large
r

[
BCG95
]
. A more feasible-sounding next step, suggested by Guth, would be to combine the conclusion of Theorems
1
and
2
with the hypothesis of Theorem
3
.

Conjecture 4

(
[
Gut11
]
)
.

For every
n\geq 2
, there exists
\delta_{n}>0
such that if
M
is a closed, oriented, connected
n
-dimensional manifold admitting a hyperbolic metric, and
g
is another Riemannian metric on
M
with
\frac{\Vol(M,g)}{\|M\|_{\Delta}}<\delta_{n}
, then for all
r\geq 1

V_{r}(\widetilde{M})\geq V_{r}(\mathbb{H}^{n}).

Karam has proved the

in
[

]
. In Section

we speculate on what would be needed to prove Conjecture

if combining Karam’s methods with those of the present paper.

Section

summarizes the proof of Theorem

and explains which parts are taken from
[

]

2.
Relationship with Sabourau’s proof

In this section we give an overview of the ingredients needed for the proof of Theorem
1
. This includes stating the parts of
[
Sab22
]
that we use unaltered, and commenting on the changes in the remainder of the proof.

To get the comparison
V_{r}(\widetilde{M})\geq V_{r}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
for all
r\geq 1
, we need to show that
V_{r}(\widetilde{M})
has at least the same exponential growth rate as
V_{r}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. Sabourau’s argument relies on the following theorem.

Theorem 5

(
[
DKL19
]
)
.

For each
n
, there exists
\kappa
such that the fundamental group
G
of every closed, connected, hyperbolic
n
-dimensional manifold satisfies the
\kappa
-Tits alternative: for every symmetric subset
S
of
G
containing the identity, either
S
generates a group of subexponential growth, or there exist two elements in
S^{\kappa}
generating a nonabelian free subgroup.

On any Riemannian manifold
M
, the
Margulis function

\mu\colon\thinspace M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
expresses the threshold between subexponential growth and exponential growth. At each point
x\in M
, the function value
\mu(x)
is defined to be the supremal value
\mu
such that the subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M,x)
generated by the loops based at
x
with length at most
\mu
is a group of subexponential growth. The function
\mu(x)
is greater than or equal to the
systole
of
M
, denoted by
\sys M
, which is the infimal length of a homotopically nontrivial loop in
M
. For
\rho<\frac{1}{2}\mu(x)
, the image in
\pi_{1}(M)
of the ball
B(x,\rho)
has subexponential growth and thus is amenable; for
\rho>\frac{1}{2}\mu(x)
, Theorem
5
exhibits two loops at
x
of length at most
2\rho\kappa
that generate a nonabelian free subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M)
, and so the volumes of balls in
\widetilde{M}
have at least a certain exponential growth rate. Sabourau uses this idea to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 6

(
[
Sab22
]
)
.

For every
n\geq 2
, there exists a small number
\alpha_{n}>0
such that the following holds. Let
M
be a closed, oriented, connected
n
-dimensional manifold admitting a hyperbolic metric, and let
g
be another Riemannian metric on
M
. Suppose that there exists
x_{0}\in M
such that
\frac{1}{2}\mu(x_{0})<\alpha_{n}
and
\Vol B\left(x_{0},\frac{1}{2}\mu(x_{0})\right)\geq\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\mu(x_{0})\right)^{n}}{n!}
. Then for all
r\geq 1
we have
V_{r}(\widetilde{M})\geq V_{r}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
.

Because Sabourau does not state it in this way, we include the following notes on how to deduce this statement from the proofs of
[
Sab22
, Theorem 3.7, Lemma 3.8, Corollaries 3.9 and 3.10]
.

Proof summary.

As in
[
Sab22
, Corollary 3.10]
, Theorem
5
implies that
M
satisfies the
\kappa
-Tits alternative for some
\kappa
depending only on
n
. Let
\mu_{0}=\mu(x_{0})
, and let
\widetilde{x}_{0}
denote any lift of
x_{0}
to
\widetilde{M}
. In our notation,
[
Sab22
, Lemma 3.8]
says that because of our hypothesis
\Vol B\left(x_{0},\frac{1}{2}\mu_{0}\right)\geq\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\mu_{0}\right)^{n}}{n!}
we have

\Vol B_{\widetilde{M}}(\widetilde{x}_{0},r)\geq\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\mu_{0}\right)^{n}}{n!}\cdot\left(3^{\left\lfloor\frac{r-\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}}{2\kappa\mu_{0}}\right\rfloor}-1\right)

for all
r\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu_{0}
. The proof is unchanged, depending only on the definition of
\kappa
-Tits alternative. For sufficiently small
\alpha_{n}
we have
\mu_{0}<5\kappa\mu_{0}<1
, so the proof of Case 1 of
[
Sab22
, Theorem 3.7]
gives a constant
C_{n,\kappa}
such that

\Vol B_{\widetilde{M}}(\widetilde{x}_{0},r)\geq\Vol B_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(C_{n,\kappa}r)

for all
r\geq 1
. Then as in
[
Sab22
, Corollary 3.9]
we can use
C_{n,\kappa}
to find a large constant
\lambda_{n,\kappa}
such that applying the above inequality to
\lambda_{n,\kappa}M
implies the desired conclusion

\Vol B_{\widetilde{M}}(\widetilde{x}_{0},r)\geq\Vol B_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(r)

for all
r\geq 1
, using the value of
\lambda_{n,\kappa}
to select
\alpha_{n}
sufficiently small.
∎

The remainder of Theorem
2
is proved by showing in
[
Sab22
, Theorem 2.7 and Proposition 3.3]
that the assumption
\Vol(M,g)<\delta_{n}
implies the existence of a point
x_{0}
with the two specified properties. We prove Theorem
1
by showing that the assumption
\frac{(\Vol(M,g))^{2}}{\|M\|_{\Delta}}<\delta_{n}
is sufficient to guarantee such a point
x_{0}
.

We informally summarize the proof of Theorem
1
as follows. Instead of producing only one point
x_{0}
, for each
\varepsilon>0
we produce a finite set of points
Z_{0}(\varepsilon)\subseteq M
with the following properties:

(1)

\Vol B(x,\frac{1}{8}\mu(x))\gtrsim\mu(x)^{n}-\varepsilon
for each
x\in Z_{0}(\varepsilon)
, by Lemma
9
. This plays the role of the second property of
x_{0}
in Lemma
6
, and the proof is the same as Sabourau uses.

(2)

\Vol M\geq\Vol B\left(x,\frac{1}{2}\mu(x)\right)\gtrsim\mu(x)^{n}\cdot\#\left(Z_{0}\cap B\left(x,\frac{1}{4}\mu(x)\right)\right)-\varepsilon
for each
x\in Z_{0}(\varepsilon)
, by Lemma
9
. This property compensates for the fact that the balls around various points of
Z_{0}(\varepsilon)
may overlap.

(3)

The number of points in
Z_{0}(\varepsilon)
is at least
\frac{1}{2^{n}}\cdot\|M\|_{\Delta}
, by Theorem
7
and Lemma
8
. In contrast, Sabourau’s proof only guarantees that the number of points in
Z_{0}(\varepsilon)
is nonzero. This statement that a large value of
\|M\|_{\Delta}
implies a large number of points in
Z_{0}(\varepsilon)
, and not just one point, is the main new idea in our proof.

The proof of Theorem
1
, given in Section
5
, consists of summing the volume estimates over a suitable subset of
Z_{0}(\varepsilon)
and taking the limit as
\varepsilon
approaches zero. To get the idea, the reader may safely ignore all instances of
\varepsilon
on a first read.

3.
Amenable Reduction Lemma

The simplicial norm was introduced by Gromov in
[
Gro82
]
. Let
z=\sum_{i}a_{i}\sigma_{i}
be a singular
d
-cycle on a space
P
, with real coefficients
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and simplices
\sigma_{i}\colon\thinspace\Delta^{d}\rightarrow P
. The
\ell^{1}
norm of
z
, denoted by
\left\lvert z\right\rvert_{1}
, is
\sum_{i}\left\lvert a_{i}\right\rvert
, and the
simplicial norm
of a given homology class is the infimum of
\left\lvert z\right\rvert_{1}
over all cycles
z
representing the homology class. The simplicial norm of the class
[z]
is denoted by
\|[z]\|_{\Delta}
. The
simplicial volume
of a closed, oriented, connected manifold
M
, denoted by
\|M\|_{\Delta}
, is the simplicial norm of the fundamental homology class of
M
with
\mathbb{R}
-coefficients.

The property of simplicial norm that we use in this paper is Theorem
7
, Gromov’s Amenable Reduction Lemma. To understand the relationship between simplicial norm and amenable groups, it may be helpful to start with Gromov’s Vanishing Theorem, found in
[
Gro82
, Section 3.1]
or
[
Iva87
, Corollary 6.3]
. The Vanishing Theorem can be viewed as a special case of the Amenable Reduction Lemma.

To state Theorem
7
, we need to define what it means to color the vertices of a singular cycle. Given an arbitrary singular
d
-cycle
z=\sum_{i}a_{i}\sigma_{i}
in a space
P
, we form a
\Delta
-complex in the following way. We take one Euclidean
d
-simplex for each
\sigma_{i}
, and identify the
(d-1)
-dimensional faces of these simplices whenever the corresponding singular
(d-1)
-simplices are equal. Because
\partial z=0
, every
(d-1)
-dimensional face is identified with at least one other face. This complex has a simplicial
d
-cycle in which the coefficient of each simplex equals the coefficient in
z
of the corresponding singular simplex. We can think of
z
as the image of this simplicial cycle under the map from the
\Delta
-complex into
P
, and we can talk about the vertices and edges of
z
, meaning the vertices and edges of the
\Delta
-complex, equipped with their maps into
P
.

If
P
is path-connected, we define a
amenable vertex coloring
of
z
to be a way to assign colors to the vertices of
z
such that for each color, if we take the union of all edges of
z
for which both vertices are that color, then the subgroup of
\pi_{1}(P)
generated by this
1
-complex is amenable. In an amenable vertex coloring, we also require that
z
does not contain any edges from a vertex to itself. (In this case, coloring every vertex a different color gives, trivially, an amenable vertex coloring.) We define a
rainbow simplex
of such a coloring to be any simplex in
z
for which all
d+1
vertices are different colors. The following theorem is from
[
Gro09
, Section 3.2]
(or see
[
AK16
]
).

Theorem 7

(Amenable Reduction Lemma)
.

Let
z=\sum_{i}a_{i}\sigma_{i}
be a singular cycle on a path-connected cell complex
P
, with an amenable vertex coloring. Then the simplicial norm of the homology class of
z
satisfies

\|[z]\|_{\Delta}\leq\sum_{\mathrm{rainbow}\ \sigma_{i}}\left\lvert a_{i}\right\rvert.

4.
Separating filtrations

This section contains lemmas from
[
Alp22
]
that are based on Papasoglu’s method of area-minimizing separating sets, which was introduced in
[
Pap20
]
. To learn the method, another useful exposition is by Nabutovsky in
[
Nab22
, Theorem 1.1]
, which has as a corollary Gromov’s Systolic Inequality, first proven in
[
Gro83
, Theorem 0.1.A]

Let
M
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, and let
\rho\colon\thinspace M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a positive, bounded function. A
\rho
-separating filtration
of
M
consists of sets

M=Z_{n}\supseteq Z_{n-1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Z_{1}\supseteq Z_{0}\supseteq Z_{-1}=\emptyset,

such that for each
i=0,1,\ldots,n
, the set
Z_{i-1}
is a
\rho
-separating subset
of
Z_{i}
; that is, for each connected component of
Z_{i}\setminus Z_{i-1}
, there is some
x\in M
such that the ball
B(x,\rho(x))
contains this connected component. (Henceforth we do not include
Z_{-1}
in the notation.) We also require smoothness and transversality conditions, given in
[
Alp22
]
, which guarantee in particular that
M
admits a triangulation in which each
Z_{i}
is an
i
-dimensional subcomplex. These conditions ensure the following lemma.

Lemma 8

(Lemma 4 of
[
Alp22
]
)
.

Let
M
be a closed, connected
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, and let
\rho\colon\thinspace M\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a bounded function with the property that for every ball
B(x,\rho(x))
in
M
, its image in
\pi_{1}(M)
is amenable. Then for every
\rho
-separating filtration

M=Z_{n}\supseteq Z_{n-1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Z_{1}\supseteq Z_{0}

there is a triangulation of
M
with an amenable vertex coloring, such that the number of rainbow simplices is
2^{n}\cdot\#Z_{0}
.

We omit the proof, which can be obtained from the proof in
[
Alp22
]
by replacing the constant radius
R
by the varying radius
\rho
and by requiring the contributions to
\pi_{1}(M)
to be amenable instead of trivial. The process for constructing the triangulation from the filtration is the same in both proofs.

The following lemma shows that it is possible to choose a
\rho
-separating filtration such that the balls around the points of
Z_{0}
satisfy lower bounds on their volumes.

Lemma 9

(Lemma 7 of
[
Alp22
]
)
.

Let
M
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. For every positive, bounded function
\rho\colon\thinspace M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\rho
-separating filtration

M=Z_{n}\supseteq Z_{n-1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Z_{1}\supseteq Z_{0},

such that for all
x\in M
and all
r_{1},r_{2}
with
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<\rho(x)
we have

\#(Z_{0}\cap B(x,r_{1}))\cdot\frac{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{n}}{n!}\leq\Vol B(x,r_{2})+\varepsilon.

We omit the proof, which can be obtained from the proof in
[
Alp22
]
by replacing the constant radius
R
by the varying radius
\rho
, both in this lemma and in the preceding Lemma 6 of
[
Alp22
]
. When computing the choice of how close each
Z_{i}
should be to the infimal possible area, we use
\sup\rho
instead of
R
.

5.
Main proof

The proof of Theorem
1
is obtained by combining Lemma
6
with the following Theorem
10
, which is similar both in statement and in proof to
[
Alp22
, Theorem 5]
, but where the role of
\frac{1}{2}\sys M
is replaced by
r_{0}(M)
, defined as follows. Given a closed, connected
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
M
, we define the ball growth threshold radius
r_{0}(M)
, or
growth radius
for short, to be the infimum of
\frac{1}{2}\mu(x)
, taken over only those
x\in M
that satisfy the property that for all
r\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(x)
, we have
\Vol B(x,r)\geq\frac{r^{n}}{n!}
. Here
\mu
denotes the Margulis function as defined in Section
2
. Sabourau’s proof of Theorem
2
shows that if
\|M\|_{\Delta}>0
, then
r_{0}(M)
is finite.

Theorem 10
.

Let
M
be a closed, oriented, connected
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Then we have

\|M\|_{\Delta}\leq 16^{n}(n!)^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{\Vol M}{r_{0}(M)^{n}}\right)^{2}.

Assuming Theorem
10
, we can quickly finish the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Using the
\alpha_{n}
guaranteed by Lemma
6
, we choose
\delta_{n}
to be

\delta_{n}=\frac{(\alpha_{n})^{2n}}{16^{n}(n!)^{2}}.

Then if
\displaystyle\frac{(\Vol M)^{2}}{\|M\|_{\Delta}}<\delta_{n}
, using Theorem
10
we have

\frac{r_{0}(M)^{2n}}{16^{n}(n!)^{2}}\leq\frac{(\Vol M)^{2}}{\|M\|_{\Delta}}<\delta_{n}=\frac{(\alpha_{n})^{2n}}{16^{n}(n!)^{2}},

and thus
r_{0}(M)<\alpha_{n}
. Applying Lemma
6
completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
10
.

We select a sequence
\varepsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0
and apply Lemma
9
with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{k}
and
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\mu
. In the resulting
\rho
-separating filtration, we define
\rho_{k}
by

\rho_{k}=\min_{x\in Z_{0}}\rho(x).

We select a subset
S
of
Z_{0}
in the following way. We consider the points of
Z_{0}
in order from greatest to least value of
\rho
, and for each point
x\in Z_{0}
, we include it in
S
if the ball
B(x,\frac{1}{4}\rho(x))
is disjoint from all balls
B(s,\frac{1}{4}\rho(s))
as
s
ranges over the points already selected to be in
S
. Then the balls
\{B(s,\frac{1}{4}\rho(s)):s\in S\}
are disjoint and the balls
\{B(s,\frac{1}{2}\rho(s)):s\in S\}
cover
Z_{0}
.

Applying Lemma
9
with
r_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\rho
and
r_{2}\rightarrow\rho
, for each
s\in S
we have

\#\left(Z_{0}\cap B\left(s,\frac{1}{2}\rho(s)\right)\right)\leq(\Vol B(s,\rho(s))+\varepsilon_{k})\cdot\frac{n!}{\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho(s)\right)^{n}}\leq(\Vol M+\varepsilon_{k})\cdot\frac{n!}{(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{k})^{n}}.

Applying Lemma
9
with
r_{1}\rightarrow 0
and
r_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\rho
, for each
s\in S
we have

1\leq\frac{\Vol B\left(s,\frac{1}{4}\rho(s)\right)}{\left(\frac{\left(\frac{1}{4}\rho(s)\right)^{n}}{n!}-\varepsilon_{k}\right)}\leq\frac{\Vol B\left(s,\frac{1}{4}\rho(s)\right)}{\left(\frac{\left(\frac{1}{4}\rho_{k}\right)^{n}}{n!}-\varepsilon_{k}\right)}.

Combining these inequalities with Lemma
8
and Theorem
7
, we have

\displaystyle\|M\|_{\Delta}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\cdot\#Z_{0}\leq

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\cdot\sum_{s\in S}\#\left(Z_{0}\cap B\left(s,\frac{1}{2}\rho(s)\right)\right)\leq

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\cdot\sum_{s\in S}(\Vol M+\varepsilon_{k})\cdot\frac{n!}{(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{k})^{n}}\leq

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\cdot(\Vol M+\varepsilon_{k})\cdot\frac{n!}{(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{k})^{n}}\cdot\sum_{s\in S}\frac{\Vol B\left(s,\frac{1}{4}\rho(s)\right)}{\left(\frac{\left(\frac{1}{4}\rho_{k}\right)^{n}}{n!}-\varepsilon_{k}\right)}\leq

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\cdot(\Vol M+\varepsilon_{k})\cdot\frac{n!}{(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{k})^{n}}\cdot\frac{\Vol M}{\left(\frac{\left(\frac{1}{4}\rho_{k}\right)^{n}}{n!}-\varepsilon_{k}\right)}.

Let
x_{k}
be a point in
Z_{0}
with
\rho(x_{k})=\rho_{k}
. Because
M
is compact, by passing to a subsequence we may assume that the sequence
x_{k}
converges to a point
x\in M
. The function
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\mu
is continuous; in fact it is
1
-Lipschitz. Thus
\rho_{k}\rightarrow\rho(x)
and taking the limit we have

\|M\|_{\Delta}\leq 16^{n}(n!)^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{\Vol M}{(\rho(x))^{n}}\right)^{2}.

What remains is to show that
\rho(x)\geq r_{0}(M)
. For each
x_{k}
, for all
r\leq\rho(x_{k})
, by Lemma
9
we have

\frac{r^{n}}{n!}\leq\Vol B(x_{k},r)+\varepsilon_{k}.

We need to show that for all
r\leq\rho(x)
, we have

\frac{r^{n}}{n!}\leq\Vol B(x,r).

If so, then
\rho(x)\geq r_{0}(M)
by definition.

For all
r
, we have

B(x_{k},r-d(x,x_{k}))\subseteq B(x,r).

By the
1
-Lipschitz property of
\rho
, if
r\leq\rho(x)
, then
r-d(x,x_{k})\leq\rho(x_{k})
. Thus we have

\frac{(r-d(x,x_{k}))^{n}}{n!}\leq\Vol B(x_{k},r-d(x,x_{k}))+\varepsilon_{k}\leq\Vol B(x,r)+\varepsilon_{k},

and taking the limit gives

\frac{r^{n}}{n!}\leq\Vol B(x,r),

as desired.
∎

6.
Open questions

In this open questions section, first we propose some possible strengthenings of Theorem
10
, and then we propose some possible statements that could prove Conjecture
4
.

Theorem
10
is stronger than the same statement with
r_{0}(M)
replaced by
\frac{1}{2}\sys M
. A different (asymptotic) strengthening of that statement about
\sys M
is the following theorem of Gromov.

Theorem 11

(Theorem 6.4.D’ of
[
Gro83
]
)
.

There exists
C_{n}>0

We can ask whether there is an analogous further strengthening of Theorem

.

Question 12
.

Does Theorem
11
remain true if
\sys M
is replaced by
r_{0}(M)

The following further strengthening of Theorem
11
would imply Conjecture
4
.

Question 13
.

Is the following statement true? There exists
C_{n}>0
such that if
M
is a closed, oriented, connected
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, then for all
r\leq\frac{1}{2}\sys M
, we have

\|M\|_{\Delta}\leq C_{n}\frac{\Vol M}{r^{n}}\cdot\left(\log\left(C_{n}\frac{V_{r}(M)}{r^{n}}\right)\right)^{n}.

The proof of Theorem
11
constructs a metric minimizing the value of
\frac{\Vol M}{(\sys M)^{n}}
and then proves the statement above for this special metric. Thus, it is reasonable to hope that the same statement may be true of an arbitrary metric. If so, then we would have

C_{n}\frac{V_{r}(M)}{r^{n}}\geq\exp\left(r\left(\frac{\|M\|_{\Delta}}{C_{n}\Vol M}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right),

meaning that if
\frac{\Vol M}{\|M\|_{\Delta}}
is small, then
V_{r}(M)
has a high exponential growth rate as long as
r\leq\frac{1}{2}\sys M
. Taking a covering space in which
\sys M
is arbitrarily large, this statement would imply Conjecture
4
.

A second potential approach to Conjecture
4
is to try to extend Karam’s proof of the
n=2
case to higher dimensions, as follows.

Question 14
.

Is the following statement true? There exist
C_{n},\varepsilon_{n}>0
such that if
M
is a closed, oriented, connected
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, then
M
has a finite-sheeted covering space
\widehat{M}
such that the following holds. Suppose that in the
(\frac{1}{2}\sys\widehat{M})
-separating filtration

\widehat{M}=Z_{n}\supseteq Z_{n-1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Z_{1}\supseteq Z_{0},

for every
i=0,\ldots,n-1
, the set
Z_{i}
is within
\varepsilon_{n}
of the infimal
i
-dimensional area of
(\frac{1}{2}\sys\widehat{M})
-separating subsets of
Z_{i+1}
. Then

\Length Z_{1}\leq C_{n}\cdot\left(\Vol\widehat{M}+\|\widehat{M}\|_{\Delta}\right).

We quickly sketch a proof, using the method of
[
Kar15
]
, that an affirmative answer to this question would imply Conjecture
4
. By a rescaling argument similar to
[
Sab22
, Corollary 3.9]
, it suffices to show that there are constants
a_{n},R_{n},\delta^{\prime}_{n}>0
such that if
\frac{\Vol M}{\|M\|_{\Delta}}<\delta^{\prime}_{n}
, then
V_{r}(\widetilde{M})\geq a_{n}\cdot 2^{r}
for all
r\geq R_{n}
. To do this, in a cover
\widehat{M}
with
\frac{1}{2}\sys\widehat{M}>r
we find a subset
Z^{\prime}_{1}
of
Z_{1}
that, when viewed as a graph, has minimum vertex degree at least
3
, has no null-homotopic cycles, and has at least
\frac{\|\widehat{M}\|_{\Delta}}{2^{n}}
edges. Thus, if
\frac{\Vol M}{\|M\|_{\Delta}}\leq\left(\frac{1}{12\cdot 2^{n}C_{n}}\right)^{n}
, then the average edge length in
Z^{\prime}_{1}
is at most
\frac{1}{6}
. Then
[
Kar15
, Theorem C]
implies that there is a point
x_{0}
in
Z^{\prime}_{1}
such that for all
r
with
1\leq r\leq\frac{1}{2}\sys\widehat{M}
, we have

\Length Z^{\prime}_{1}\cap B(x_{0},r)\geq\frac{3}{4}\cdot 2^{r}.

Using the near-minimizing property of each
Z_{i}
as in
[
Alp22
, Lemma 6]
, this implies by induction that the
i
-dimensional area of
Z_{i}\cap B(x_{0},r)
is at least
\frac{3}{4}2^{r-(i-1)}-(i-1)\varepsilon_{n}
if
i\leq r\leq\frac{1}{2}\sys\widehat{M}
, and in particular, that the volume of
B(x_{0},r)
is at least
\frac{3}{2^{n+1}}2^{r}-(n-1)\varepsilon_{n}
if
n\leq r\leq\frac{1}{2}\sys\widehat{M}
. Taking
\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0
gives the desired lower bound for
V_{r}(\widetilde{M})
.

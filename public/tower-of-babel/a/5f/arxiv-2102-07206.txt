Sample Efficient Subspace-based Representations for Nonlinear Meta-Learning
Halil Ibrahim Gulluk, Yue Sun, Samet Oymak, Maryam Fazel

Abstract
Constructing good representations is critical for learning complex tasks in a sample efficient manner. In the context of meta-learning, representations can be constructed from common patterns of previously seen tasks so that a future task can be learned quickly. While recent works show the benefit of subspace-based representations, such results are limited to linear-regression tasks. This work explores a more general class of nonlinear tasks with applications ranging from binary classification, generalized linear models and neural nets. We prove that subspace-based representations can be learned in a sample-efficient manner and provably benefit future tasks in terms of sample complexity. Numerical results verify the theoretical predictions in classification and neural-network regression tasks.

We specialize our results to practical settings with tasks involving binary classification and neural nets. We theoretically and empirically show that subspace-based representation can greatly improve sample efficiency of future tasks.

Problem Formulation

The meta-learning setup that will be considered in this work consists of two phases: (i) meta-training: prior tasks are used to learn a good representation and (ii) few-shot learning: the new task is learned with few samples.
In the meta-training phase, we learn the low dimensional space spanned by parameters. In the few-shot learning phase, we use the subspace to learn the model of a new task ideally with few samples.

In the first phase, there are multiple task vectors to infer from.We consider a realizable model where the input and label is associated via a labeling function. One accesses batches of data, each of whom is collected from a task.

y{\mathbf{x}}
in Lemma
A.2
. That leads to the proof of Lemma
3.1
. Then in Lemma
A.4
we bound the difference between sum of
y{\mathbf{x}}
and its mean using concentration. The concentration of covariance estimator is studied later in Lemma

A.0.1
Proof of Lemma
3.1

We first state Lemma
3.1
below.

Lemma A.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{M}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\scalebox{1.8}{$\sum$}}_{j=1}^{k}\frac{2}{n_{j}^{2}}\left[(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}/2}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}})(\sum_{i=n_{j}/2+1}^{n_{j}}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}})^{\top}\right.

(A.1a)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.+(\sum_{i=n_{j}/2+1}^{n_{j}}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}})(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}/2}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}})^{\top}\right].

And

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{j}(\bm{W}):\mathbb{R}^{r\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bm{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}}[f^{j}(\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}}){\mathbf{x}}]

\displaystyle\bm{M}:=\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bm{h}^{j}(\bm{W})(\bm{h}^{j}(\bm{W}))^{\top}\right)\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}.

Then
\bm{M},\hat{\bm{M}}
satisfies the following. (a)
\text{rank}(\bm{M})\leq r
. (b)
\text{range-space}(\bm{M})\subset\text{row-space}(\bm{W})
. (c)
\bm{E}[\hat{{\bm{M}}}]=\bm{M}
.

Proof.

Part (a) and (b) are easy to see.
\bm{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d}
so that
\text{rank}(\bm{M})\leq r
, and since
\bm{M}
is the product of
\bm{W}^{\top}
and another matrix, then
\text{range-space}(\bm{M})\subset\text{row-space}(\bm{W})
.
Now we will prove Part (c). We first give the lemma for the mean of the random vector
y{\mathbf{x}}
.

Lemma A.2
.

We assume that the data is generated as in Def.
2.1
, and we study the
j
-th task whose activation function is
f^{j}
.
Define

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{j}(\bm{W}):\mathbb{R}^{r\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bm{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}f^{j}(\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}}){\mathbf{x}}

Denote the joint distribution of
({\mathbf{x}},y)
as
\mathscr{P}_{x,y}
.
Then
\bm{E}_{({\mathbf{x}},y)\sim\mathscr{P}_{x,y}}(y{\mathbf{x}})=\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}\bm{h}^{j}(\bm{W})
.

Proof of Lemma
A.2
.
The expectation can be expanded as

\displaystyle\bm{E}_{({\mathbf{x}},y)\sim\mathscr{P}_{x,y}}(y{\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\bm{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}f^{j}(\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}}){\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\bm{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}\left(f^{j}(\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}})\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}}+f^{j}(\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}})(I-\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}){\mathbf{x}}\right).

Note that, because
{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)
so
\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}}
and
(I-\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}){\mathbf{x}}
are Gaussian.
\bm{W}\bm{W}^{\top}=I
implies

\displaystyle\quad{\bm{E}}(\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}}){\mathbf{x}}^{\top}((I-\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}))

\displaystyle=\bm{W}(I-\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W})=0.

So
\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}}
and
(I-\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}){\mathbf{x}}
are independent, so

\displaystyle\quad\bm{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}f^{j}(\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}})(I-\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}){\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=(\bm{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}f^{j}(\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}}))(\bm{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}(I-\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}){\mathbf{x}})=0

Then

\displaystyle\bm{E}_{({\mathbf{x}},y)\sim\mathscr{P}_{x,y}}(y{\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}(\bm{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}f^{j}(\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}}){\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}\bm{h}_{j}(\bm{W}).

Denote

\displaystyle{\bm{H}}^{j}(\bm{W})=\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}\bm{h}^{j}(\bm{W})

(A.2)

Because
\frac{2}{n_{j}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}/2}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}}

and

\frac{2}{n_{j}}\sum_{i={n_{j}/2}+1}^{n_{j}}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}}

are independent, and their expectation are both
{\bm{H}}^{j}(\bm{W})
, so that

\displaystyle\quad\bm{E}(\frac{2}{n_{j}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}/2}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}})(\frac{2}{n_{j}}\sum_{i={n_{j}/2}+1}^{n_{j}}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}})^{\top}

\displaystyle={\bm{H}}^{j}(\bm{W})({\bm{H}}^{j}(\bm{W}))^{\top}.

At then end

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{M}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bm{E}(\frac{2}{n_{j}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}/2}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}})(\frac{2}{n_{j}}\sum_{i={n_{j}/2}+1}^{n_{j}}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}})^{\top},

\displaystyle\bm{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\bm{H}}^{j}(\bm{W})({\bm{H}}^{j}(\bm{W}))^{\top},

so we can prove Part (c) that
\bm{E}(\hat{\bm{M}})=\bm{M}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

We restate Theorem
A.3
below.

Theorem A.3
.

Suppose the data is generated as Def.
2.1
. Let
\delta\in(0,1)
,
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. Suppose
y{\mathbf{x}}
is a subGaussian random vector with covariance upper bounded by

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{Cov}(y{\mathbf{x}})\|\leq\sigma^{2}.

(A.3)

Define

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{j}(\bm{W}):\mathbb{R}^{r\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bm{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}f^{j}(\bm{W}{\mathbf{x}}){\mathbf{x}}

And let

\displaystyle\bm{M}=\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bm{h}^{j}(\bm{W})(\bm{h}^{j}(\bm{W}))^{\top}\right)\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}

Then there exists a constant
c
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, let

\displaystyle k=\frac{cd}{n}\log^{2}(\frac{kd}{\delta})\max\{\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}},\frac{1}{\epsilon}\log(\frac{kd}{\delta})\}

we have

\displaystyle\|\hat{\bm{M}}-\bm{M}\|\leq\epsilon\sigma^{2}.

Proof.

The following lemmas are similar to
[
20
]
Section A.1. We extend the concentration inequalities from bounded random vectors to Gaussian random vectors. For completeness we place the lemmas here.

Lemma A.4
.

(
[
27
]
Cor. 7)
Let
\|\mathbf{Cov}(y{\mathbf{x}})\|\leq\sigma^{2}
,
\delta\in(0,1)
,
t>0
,
{\bm{H}}^{j}(\bm{W})
be defined in (
A.2
). With probability
1-\delta
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that for every
j
,

\displaystyle\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}}-{\bm{H}}^{j}(\bm{W})\|\leq c\sigma\sqrt{\frac{d}{t}\log(\frac{kd}{\delta})}

(A.4)

Denote this event as
\mathcal{E}
.

With the covariance of
y{\mathbf{x}}
being bounded by
\sigma^{2}
, the following inequalities are true
[
20
]
.

\displaystyle{\bm{E}}\left((\bm{v}^{T}\cdot(\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}}-\theta^{j}))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{2}/t,\mbox{~for~}\|\bm{v}\|=1.

\displaystyle{\bm{E}}\left(\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}}-\theta^{j}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{2}d/t.

Lemma A.5
.

Define

\displaystyle\bm{Z}_{j}

\displaystyle=(\frac{2}{n_{j}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}/2}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}})(\frac{2}{n_{j}}\sum_{i={n_{j}/2}+1}^{n_{j}}{y_{i,j}}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i,j}})^{\top}

\displaystyle\quad-{\bm{H}}^{j}(\bm{W})({\bm{H}}^{j}(\bm{W}))^{\top},

Then there exists a constant
c
such that on the event
\mathcal{E}
(thus with probability
1-\delta
), for all
j=1,...,k
,

\displaystyle\|\bm{Z}_{j}\|\leq\frac{c\sigma^{2}d}{n_{j}}\log(\frac{kd}{\delta}).

(A.5)

The proof is almost same as
[

]
, the only difference is that we replace the bound in (
[

A.4
). With the similar replacement, we propose the following lemma.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
. There exists a constant
c
, such that for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, and

\displaystyle k=\frac{cd}{n_{j}}\log^{2}(\frac{kd}{\delta})\max\{\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}},\frac{1}{\epsilon}\log(\frac{kd}{\delta})\},

(A.6)

with probability
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bm{Z}_{j}\|\leq\epsilon\sigma^{2}.

This means that when (

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{\bm{M}}-\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bm{h}^{j}(\bm{W})(\bm{h}^{j}(\bm{W}))^{\top}\right)\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}\right\|\leq\epsilon\sigma^{2}.

Note that

Proof of Theorem
3.4

Now we will prove Theorem
3.4
. We first review the notations and assumptions mentioned in the theorem.

A.1.1
Review of Notations and Assumptions

We define the SVD of
\hat{\bm{M}}
as
\hat{\bm{U}}\hat{\bm{{\Lambda}}}\hat{\bm{V}}^{\top}
. Let the first
r
columns of
\hat{\bm{U}}
be
{\bm{U}}_{r}
.

Let
\hat{\bm{W}}
be defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{W}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}\hat{\bm{Q}},

(A.7)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{Q}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{{\bm{Q}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r},{\bm{Q}}{\bm{Q}}^{\top}=I}\ \|\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}{\bm{Q}}-\bm{W}\|.

(A.8)

The population cross entropy loss is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f;{\bm{P}}):\mathcal{F}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}=

\displaystyle-\bm{E}_{{\mathbf{x}},y\sim\mathscr{P}_{x,y}}(y\log f(\hat{\bm{P}}{\mathbf{x}})+(1-y)\log(1-f(\hat{\bm{P}}{\mathbf{x}}))).

We will search for a function
f^{*}\in\mathcal{F}
.

Assumption 3
.

\mathcal{F}
is a set of functions satisfying: For any function
f\in\mathcal{F}
, any orthonormal matrix
{\bm{Q}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
and any matrix
{\bm{P}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d}
, there exists
g\in\mathcal{F}
such that
f({\bm{P}}{\mathbf{x}})=g({\bm{Q}}{\bm{P}}{\mathbf{x}})
.

Now we define a general loss function
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}_{e}
.

Assumption 4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}
satisfies the following assumptions:

1.

\mathcal{L}
has the form
\mathcal{L}(f;{\bm{P}})=\bm{E}_{\mathscr{P}_{x,y}}{\cal{L}}^{\mathrm{single}}(f({\bm{P}}{\mathbf{x}}),y)
.
\mathcal{L}_{e}
has the form
\mathcal{L}_{e}(f;{\bm{P}})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\cal{L}}^{\mathrm{single}}(f({\bm{P}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}),y_{i})
.

2.

{\cal{L}}^{\mathrm{single}}
is
L
Lipschitz in
{\bm{P}}{\mathbf{x}}
.

3.

\min_{{\bm{P}}}\mathcal{L}(f;{\bm{P}})=\mathcal{L}(f;\bm{W})
.

We solve for
\hat{f}
, defined as

\displaystyle\hat{f}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\ \mathcal{L}(f;\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top}).

(A.9)

A.1.2
Proof of Theorem
3.4

Now we are ready to restate Theorem
3.4
and prove it.

Theorem A.7
.

With Assumptions
3
and
4
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\hat{f};\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top})-\mathcal{L}(f^{*};{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top})\lesssim L\sqrt{r}\|\hat{\bm{W}}-\bm{W}\|.

Proof.

We learn the model from the following optimization algorithm.

\displaystyle\hat{f}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\ \mathcal{L}(f;\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top})

Denote
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{F}
as the function such that
\tilde{f}(\hat{\bm{W}}{\mathbf{x}})=\hat{f}(\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}})
. So that
\mathcal{L}(\hat{f};\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top})=\mathcal{L}(\tilde{f};\hat{\bm{W}})
. Since
\hat{f}
minimizes the cross entropy loss, we have that
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{f};\hat{\bm{W}})\leq\mathcal{L}(f^{*};\hat{\bm{W}})
.

Now we have

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{L}(\tilde{f};\hat{\bm{W}})-\mathcal{L}(f^{*};\bm{W})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}(f^{*};\hat{\bm{W}})-\mathcal{L}(f^{*};\bm{W})

\displaystyle\leq\bm{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}L\|(\hat{\bm{W}}-\bm{W})x\|

\displaystyle\lesssim L\sqrt{r}\|\hat{\bm{W}}-\bm{W}\|.

The last step uses the fact that
\hat{\bm{W}}
and
\bm{W}
are rank
r
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

We first state Theorem
3.5
below.

Theorem A.8
.

Suppose we generate
n
sample data for few shot learning solve for
\hat{\theta}_{e}
from

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{e}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\bm{\theta}}\ \mathcal{L}_{e}(\bm{\theta};{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top})\quad\text{such~that}\quad\|\bm{\theta}\|_{2}\leq a.

(A.10)

Suppose Assumptions
3
and
4
hold.
There exist constants
c>0
,
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-n^{-c+1}-\delta
, the solution
\hat{\theta}_{e}
and
\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}
satisfy

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{L}(\hat{\theta}_{e};\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top})-\mathcal{L}(\theta^{*};\bm{W})

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{caL(\sqrt{r}+\log(n))(1+\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)})}{\sqrt{n}}+L\sqrt{r}\|\hat{\bm{W}}-\bm{W}\|.

Proof.

We sketch the proof of Theorem
A.8
. After reviewing the notations, we quote a standard generalization bound via Rademacher complexity in Lemma
A.9
. Then we use the smoothness of the loss function to link to the generalization error of linear functions (
A.15
), and then use Lemma
A.10
to compute (
A.15
) and finish the proof.

In few-shot learning, the true parameter is
\theta^{*}
, and the empirical loss function with finite data is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{e}(\theta;{\bm{P}}):\mathcal{F}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\cal{L}}^{\mathrm{single}}(f({\bm{P}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}),y_{i})

(A.11)

Denote each term in the summation as

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{L}_{e}^{i}(\theta;{\bm{P}}):\mathcal{F}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}={\cal{L}}^{\mathrm{single}}(f({\bm{P}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}),y_{i}).

We search for the solution by

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{e}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\theta}\ \mathcal{L}_{e}(\theta;{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top}),\mbox{~such~that~}\|\theta\|\leq a.

(A.12)

In Theorem
A.7
, we know that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\hat{\theta};\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top})-\mathcal{L}(\theta^{*};{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top})\lesssim L\sqrt{r}\|\hat{\bm{W}}-\bm{W}\|.

Denote

\displaystyle M=\max_{\|\theta\|\leq a}|\theta^{\top}\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}|\leq a\max\|\hat{\bm{U}}_{r}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\|.

\mathcal{L}

.

[
28

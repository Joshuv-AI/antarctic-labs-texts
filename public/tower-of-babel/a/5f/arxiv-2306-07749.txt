Provably Learning Nash Policies in Constrained Markov Potential Games
Pragnya Alatur, Giorgia Ramponi, Niao He, Andreas Krause

Abstract
Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) addresses sequential decision-making problems with multiple agents, where each agent optimizes its own objective. In many real-world instances, the agents may not only want to optimize their objectives, but also ensure safe behavior. For example, in traffic routing, each car (agent) aims to reach its destination quickly (objective) while avoiding collisions (safety). Constrained Markov Games (CMGs) are a natural formalism for safe MARL problems, though generally intractable. In this work, we introduce and study Constrained Markov Potential Games (CMPGs), an important class of CMGs. We first show that a Nash policy for CMPGs can be found via constrained optimization. One tempting approach is to solve it by Lagrangian-based primal-dual methods. As we show, in contrast to the single-agent setting, however, CMPGs do not satisfy strong duality, rendering such approaches inapplicable and potentially unsafe. To solve the CMPG problem, we propose our algorithm C oordinate- A scent for CMPG s (CA-CMPG), which provably converges to a Nash policy in tabular, finite-horizon CMPGs. Furthermore, we provide the first sample complexity bounds for learning Nash policies in unknown CMPGs, and, which under additional assumptions, guarantee safe exploration.

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) addresses sequential decision-making problems with

Markov Potential Games

An

(\mathcal{S},\{\mathcal{A}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},H,\left\{{\mathcal{P}_{h}}\right\}_{h=1}^{H},

Policies:

For every agent

\Pi^{i}\triangleq\left\{{\{\pi_{i,h}\}_{h=1}^{H}\;|\;\pi_{i,h}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\Delta_{\mathcal{A}_{i}},\forall h\in[H]}\right\}

We denote by
\Pi\triangleq\left\{{\bm{\pi}=\left({\pi_{1},...,\pi_{n}}\right)|\pi_{i}\in\Pi^{i},\forall i\in[n]}\right\}
the set of
joint
policies. For any policy
\bm{\pi}\in\Pi

i\in[n]
, we denote by

the policy of the
other

n-1
agents.

Value Function:

For any policy
\bm{\pi}\in\Pi
and agent
i\in[n]
, the value function
V^{r_{i}}(\bm{\pi})
measures the expected, cumulative reward of agent

, and is defined as follows:

V^{r_{i}}(\bm{\pi})\triangleq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\sim\mu,\\
a_{h}\sim\bm{\pi}_{h}(\cdot|s_{h}),\\
s_{h+1}\sim\mathcal{P}_{h}(\cdot|s_{h},a_{h})\end{subarray}}\big[\sum_{h=1}^{H}r_{i,h}(s_{h},a_{h})|s_{0}=s\big].

(1)

Potential Function:
An MPG possesses an underlying potential function
\Phi:\Pi\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that:

\begin{gathered}V^{r_{i}}(\pi_{i},\bm{\pi}_{-i})-V^{r_{i}}(\pi^{\prime}_{i},\bm{\pi}_{-i})=\Phi(\pi_{i},\bm{\pi}_{-i})-\Phi(\pi^{\prime}_{i},\bm{\pi}_{-i})\qquad\forall\pi^{\prime}_{i}\in\Pi^{i},\forall\bm{\pi}\in\Pi,\forall i\in[n].\end{gathered}

(2)

This is an adaptation of the potential function defined in
Leonardos et al. 2022
to the finite-horizon setting. Instead of defining a per-state potential function, we directly consider the potential function with respect to the initial distribution

Remark:
Note that the potential function is a property of the MPG and is typically not known to the agents. In a cooperative game, the agents have one shared reward function

such that

,
\forall i\in[n]
. In this case, the potential function is simply the value function of the agents, i.e.,

. Note, however, that cooperative games are a

7
, we describe different instances in detail.

We call a policy

V^{c_{j}}_{\mu}(\bm{\pi})\triangleq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\sim\mu,\\

Constrained Markov Decision Processes

A

Conclusion

In this paper, we proved that strong duality does not hold always hold in CMPGs, making primal-dual approaches inapplicable. An interesting future question could be to understand under which conditions primal-dual methods may work for CMPGs. To tackle CMPGs, we presented our algorithm CA-CMPG, which provably converges to an
\varepsilon
-Nash policy. Note that while this paper focuses on the finite-horizon setting, our algorithm CA-CMPG can be adapted to the discounted, infinite-horizon setting by using an appropriate CMDP solver as a sub-routine. Furthermore, we established the first sample complexity bound for learning in CMPGs. In CA-CMPG, exploration happens only within the CMDP sub-routines. It would be interesting to understand whether the sample complexity bound for the generative model setting (
Section

6.1
) can be made tighter if we move the exploration outside the CMDP sub-routines.

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Pr\left[{\left|{\hat{V}^{r_{i}}(\bm{\pi}^{t-1})-V^{r_{i}}(\bm{\pi}^{t-1})}\right|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{8}}\right]+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Pr\left[{\left|{\hat{V}^{r_{i}}(\hat{\pi}_{i}^{t},\bm{\pi}_{-i}^{t-1})-V^{r_{i}}(\hat{\pi}_{i}^{t},\bm{\pi}_{-i}^{t-1})}\right|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{8}}\right]

(Union bound.)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8n^{2}H}{\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon\delta}{16n^{2}H}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{2}.

\displaystyle\Rightarrow Pr\left[{\mathcal{G}_{estimate}\cap\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{estimate}}\right]

\displaystyle=1-Pr\left[{\mathcal{G}_{estimate}^{C}\cup\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{estimate}^{C}}\right]\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{2}.

To obtain

5. Conclusion:

Combining 3. and 4., we obtain

∎

D.1
Convert mixture to a single policy

Value functions are typically not linear in the policy, i.e.,
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\hat{V}^{l}(\hat{\pi}_{t})
is, in general, not equal to
\hat{V}^{l}\left({\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\hat{\pi}_{t}}\right)
, for
l=r,c
. State-action occupancy measures

(
Borkar 1988
)
, however, allow us to reformulate the value function in a
linear
way. For brevity, we will refer to state-action occupancy measures as "occupancy measures" in the rest of this section.

Definition 2

(Occupancy Measure)
.

For every policy
\pi
, we define its
occupancy measure
\left\{{\hat{\rho}^{\pi}_{h}}\right\}_{h=1}^{H}
with respect to the transition model
\left\{{\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}}\right\}_{h=1}^{H}
as follows:

\hat{\rho}^{\pi}_{h}(s,a)\triangleq\sum_{(s,a)}Pr^{\pi}(s_{h}=s,a_{h}=a),\quad\forall(s,a,h)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times[H].

Alternatively, the occupancy measure can be expressed recursively as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{\pi}_{1}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\mu(s)\cdot\pi_{1}(a|s),

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{\pi}_{h}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})}\hat{\rho}^{\pi}_{h-1}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\cdot\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}(s|s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\cdot\pi_{h}(a|s),\forall h\in[H]\setminus\left\{{1}\right\},

for all state-action pairs
(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
. The following lemma establishes how we can construct a policy
\bar{\pi}
from the primal-dual policies
\left\{{\hat{\pi}_{t}}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
s.t.
\hat{V}^{l}(\bar{\pi})=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\hat{V}^{l}(\hat{\pi}_{t})
, for
l=r,c
.

Lemma 3
.

Given policies
\left\{{\hat{\pi}_{t}}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
, consider the averaged occupancy measure
\bar{\rho}\triangleq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\hat{\rho}^{\hat{\pi}_{t}}
. Define the policy
\bar{\pi}
as follows:

\bar{\pi}_{h}(a|s)\triangleq\begin{cases}\frac{\bar{\rho}_{h}(s,a)}{\sum_{a^{\prime}}\bar{\rho}_{h}(s,a^{\prime})}&\sum_{a^{\prime}}\bar{\rho}_{h}(s,a^{\prime})>0\\
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(18)

\forall h\in[H]
and
\forall(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
. Then,
\bar{\pi}
satisfies the following property:

\hat{V}^{l}(\bar{\pi})=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\hat{V}^{l}(\hat{\pi}_{t}),.

(for
l=r,c
.)

Proof.

Since the set of occupation measures is convex (
Lemma

\bar{\rho}
is a valid occupation measure, i.e., there exists a policy
\pi

\bar{\pi}

\hat{\rho}^{\bar{\pi}}\equiv\bar{\rho}

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{l}(\bar{\pi})

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{(s,a)}\hat{\rho}^{\bar{\pi}}_{h}(s,a)\cdot l_{h}(s,a)

(By
Lemma

4

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{(s,a)}\left({\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\hat{\rho}^{\hat{\pi}_{t}}_{h}(s,a)}\right)\cdot l_{h}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\left({\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\underbrace{\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{(s,a)}\hat{\rho}^{\hat{\pi}_{t}}_{h}(s,a)\cdot l_{h}(s,a)}_{=\hat{V}^{l}(\hat{\pi}_{t}),\text{ \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:value-function-linear}}}}\right)

, we obtain a bound on the dual regret:

\displaystyle R^{d}(\lambda,T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left({\lambda_{t}-\lambda}\right)\left({\alpha^{\prime}-\hat{V}^{c}(\hat{\pi}_{t})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left({\left|{\lambda_{t}-\lambda}\right|^{2}-\left|{\lambda_{t+1}-\lambda}\right|^{2}}\right)+\frac{\eta TH^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overbrace{\left|{\lambda_{0}-\lambda}\right|^{2}}^{\leq U^{2}}-\overbrace{\left|{\lambda_{T}-\lambda}\right|^{2}}^{\geq 0}}{2\eta}+\frac{\eta TH^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq U\sqrt{T}H.

(Setting
\eta=\frac{U}{\sqrt{T}H}
.)

∎

Lemma 9
.

For any
C>\lambda^{*}
and any policy
\widetilde{\pi}
s.t.
\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})-\hat{V}^{r}(\widetilde{\pi})+C\left[{\hat{V}^{c}(\widetilde{\pi})-\alpha^{\prime}}\right]_{+}\leq\beta
, we have:

\left[{\hat{V}^{c}(\widetilde{\pi})-\alpha^{\prime}}\right]_{+}\leq\frac{\beta}{C-\lambda^{*}}.

Proof.

We define the function
\nu(\tau)\triangleq\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left\{{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi)\Big|\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)\leq\alpha^{\prime}-\tau}\right\}
for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Strong duality for CMDPs
(
Paternain et al. 2019
)
gives us the following inequality:

\nu(0)=\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left[{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi)+\lambda^{*}\left({\alpha^{\prime}-\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)}\right)}\right].

Next, consider an arbitrary
\tau
and any policy
\pi^{\prime}
s.t.
\hat{V}^{c}(\pi^{\prime})\leq\alpha^{\prime}-\tau
. Subtracting
\tau\lambda^{*}
from the inequality above, we obtain:

\displaystyle\nu(0)-\tau\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left[{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi)+\lambda^{*}\left({\alpha^{\prime}-\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)}\right)}\right]-\tau\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle\geq\hat{V}^{r}(\pi^{\prime})+\lambda^{*}\left({\alpha^{\prime}-\hat{V}^{c}(\pi^{\prime})}\right)-\tau\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle=\hat{V}^{r}(\pi^{\prime})+\lambda^{*}\underbrace{\left({\alpha^{\prime}-\tau-\hat{V}^{c}(\pi^{\prime})}\right)}_{\geq 0}

\displaystyle\geq\hat{V}^{r}(\pi^{\prime}).

Since this inequality holds for any
\pi^{\prime}
with
\hat{V}^{c}(\pi^{\prime})\leq\alpha^{\prime}-\tau
, it also holds for
\pi^{\prime*}\in\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left\{{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi)\Big|\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)\leq\alpha^{\prime}-\tau}\right\}
. Plugging this into the inequality above, we obtain:

\displaystyle\nu(0)-\tau\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle\geq\underbrace{\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left\{{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi)\Big|\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)\leq\alpha^{\prime}-\tau}\right\}}_{=\nu(\tau)}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\tau\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\nu(0)-\nu(\tau).

Now, we select
\widetilde{\tau}=-\left({\hat{V}^{c}(\widetilde{\pi})-\alpha^{\prime}}\right)
and bound the following expression:

\displaystyle\left({C-\lambda^{*}}\right)\left|{\widetilde{\tau}}\right|

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\tau}\lambda^{*}+C\left|{\widetilde{\tau}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\nu(0)-\nu(\widetilde{\tau})+C\left|{\widetilde{\tau}}\right|

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})-\hat{V}^{r}(\widetilde{\pi})+C\left|{\widetilde{\pi}}\right|}_{=\beta}+\underbrace{\hat{V}^{r}(\widetilde{\pi})-\nu(\widetilde{\tau})}_{\leq 0}

(Addition and subtraction of common terms.)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left|{\widetilde{\tau}}\right|=\left[{\hat{V}^{c}(\widetilde{\pi})-\alpha^{\prime}}\right]_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta}{C-\lambda^{*}}.

∎

Lemma 10

(Sensitivity Error)
.

Let
\Delta>0
and define
\hat{\pi}^{*},\widetilde{\pi}^{*}
as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}^{*}

\displaystyle\in\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left[{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi),\text{ subject to: }\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)\leq\alpha-\Delta}\right]

(25)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\pi}^{*}

\displaystyle\in\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left[{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi),\text{ subject to: }\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)\leq\alpha+\Delta}\right]

Then, the sensitivity error
\left|{\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})-\hat{V}^{r}(\widetilde{\pi}^{*})}\right|
can be bounded as follows:

\left|{\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})-\hat{V}^{r}(\widetilde{\pi}^{*})}\right|\leq 2\Delta\lambda^{*},

where
\lambda^{*}
is the optimal dual variable for
Eq.

25
, i.e.:

\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left[{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi)+\lambda^{*}\left({\alpha-\Delta-\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)}\right)}\right],

which holds due to the strong duality property for CMDPs
(
Paternain et al. 2019
)
.

Proof.

We start with the strong duality property for
\hat{\pi}^{*}
:

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})

\displaystyle=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left[{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi)+\lambda^{*}\left({\alpha-\Delta-\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\hat{V}^{r}(\widetilde{\pi}^{*})+\lambda^{*}\left({\alpha-\Delta-\underbrace{\hat{V}^{c}(\widetilde{\pi}^{*})}_{\leq\alpha+\Delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\hat{V}^{r}(\widetilde{\pi}^{*})-2\Delta\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\hat{V}^{r}(\widetilde{\pi}^{*})-\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})\leq 2\Delta\lambda^{*}.

Note that the constraint set considered for
\widetilde{\pi}^{*}
is larger than the one for
\hat{\pi}^{*}
. Therefore,
\hat{V}^{r}(\widetilde{\pi}^{*})\geq\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})
holds and this implies:

\left|{\hat{V}^{r}(\widetilde{\pi}^{*})-\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})}\right|=\hat{V}^{r}(\widetilde{\pi}^{*})-\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})\leq 2\Delta\lambda^{*}.

(26)

∎

Lemma 11

(Bound on the dual variable)
.

Define
\pi_{c}^{*}\triangleq\arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi}V^{c}(\pi)
and
\zeta\triangleq\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left\{{\alpha-V^{c}(\pi)}\right\}
. Let
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha-\Delta
, for
\Delta\in\left({0,\frac{\zeta}{2}}\right)
and assume that
\left|{\hat{V}^{c}(\pi_{c}^{*})-V^{c}(\pi_{c}^{*})}\right|\leq\frac{\zeta}{2}-\Delta
holds. Furthermore, let
\hat{\pi}^{*}\in\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left[{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi)\text{ subject to: }\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)\leq\alpha^{\prime}}\right]
denote the optimal policy to the empirical CMDP, and let
\lambda^{*}
denote the corresponding optimal dual variable, i.e.:

\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left[{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi)+\lambda^{*}\left({\alpha^{\prime}-\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)}\right)}\right]

Then, the dual variable
\lambda^{*}
can be bounded as follows:

\lambda^{*}\leq\frac{2H}{\zeta}.

Proof.

First, we define the policy
\hat{\pi}_{c}^{*}\in\arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)
. Next, we use the strong duality property
(
Paternain et al. 2019
)
for
\hat{\pi}^{*}
:

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})

\displaystyle=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left[{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi)+\lambda^{*}\left({\alpha^{\prime}-\hat{V}^{c}(\pi)}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}_{c}^{*})+\lambda^{*}\left({\alpha^{\prime}-\hat{V}^{c}(\hat{\pi}_{c}^{*})}\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}_{c}^{*})+\lambda^{*}\left({\underbrace{\alpha-V^{c}(\pi_{c}^{*})}_{=\zeta}-\Delta+V^{c}(\pi_{c}^{*})-\hat{V}^{c}(\hat{\pi}_{c}^{*})}\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}_{c}^{*})+\lambda^{*}\left({\zeta-\Delta+\underbrace{\hat{V}^{c}(\pi_{c}^{*})-\hat{V}^{c}(\hat{\pi}_{c}^{*})}_{\geq 0}+\underbrace{V^{c}(\pi_{c}^{*})-\hat{V}^{c}(\pi_{c}^{*})}_{\geq-\left|{V^{c}(\pi_{c}^{*})-\hat{V}^{c}(\pi_{c}^{*})}\right|}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}_{c}^{*})+\lambda^{*}\left({\zeta-\Delta-\underbrace{\left|{\hat{V}^{c}(\pi_{c}^{*})-V^{c}(\pi_{c}^{*})}\right|}_{\leq\frac{\zeta}{2}-\Delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}_{c}^{*})+\frac{\lambda^{*}\zeta}{2}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\left({\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}^{*})-\hat{V}^{r}(\hat{\pi}_{c}^{*})}\right)}{\zeta}\leq\frac{2H}{\zeta}.

Appendix E
Learning in Unknown Constrained Markov Potential Games - Safe Exploration Without a Generative Model (
Section

6.2
)

In this section, we consider the setting, where the agents want to explore safely, but they do not have access to a generative model anymore. Existing algorithms with safe exploration
(
Bura et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2021b
)
have guarantees on the
regret
, but no sample complexity guarantees. First, we define a no-regret algorithm with safe exploration guarantees as follows:

Definition 3

(No-regret algorithm with safe exploration)
.

Consider a fixed number of
T>0
rounds and an algorithm
\mathfrak{A}
, which selects a policy
\pi_{t}\in\Pi
in every round
t\in[T]
.
\mathfrak{A}
’s regret after
T
rounds is defined as follows:

R(T)\triangleq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max_{\pi\in\Pi_{C}}V^{r}(\pi)-V^{r}(\pi_{t}).

We call
\mathfrak{A}
is called a no-regret algorithm with safe exploration, if
R(T)\in o(T)
and if
\pi_{t}\in\Pi_{C},\forall t\in[T]
. Examples of such algorithms are
Liu et al. 2021b
and
Bura et al. 2022
.

Next, we discuss how we can use the notion of regret to derive a sample complexity bound. For
unconstrained
MDPs, sub-linear regret bounds can be converted to a sample complexity bound by applying the well-known
online-to-batch
conversion trick
(
Jin et al. 2018
)
. Applying the same trick to the DOPE algorithm
Bura et al. 2022
, we derive a sample-efficient algorithm in
Algorithm

4
and prove its sample complexity in the following lemma.

Lemma 14
.

Consider a CMDP
\mathcal{M}=\left({\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},H,\left\{{r_{h}}\right\}_{h\in[H]},\left\{{\mathcal{P}_{h}}\right\}_{h\in[H]},\left\{{c_{h}}\right\}_{h\in[H]},\alpha}\right)

\pi^{S}
and its constraint value

T=\frac{4C^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}
. Thus, there exists
t^{*}\in\arg\min_{t\in[T]}\left\{{V^{r}(\pi^{*})-V^{r}(\pi_{t})}\right\}
s.t.
V^{r}(\pi^{*})-V^{r}(\pi_{t^{*}})\leq\frac{R(T)}{T}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
holds. We do a case analysis on
V^{r}(\pi_{t^{*}})-V^{r}(\pi)\geq 0
:

Case 1:

V^{r}(\pi_{t^{*}})-V^{r}(\pi)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Then, we can bound
V^{r}(\pi^{*})-V^{r}(\pi)
as follows:

\displaystyle V^{r}(\pi^{*})-V^{r}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\left[{V^{r}(\pi^{*})-V^{r}(\pi_{t^{*}})}\right]+\left[{V^{r}(\pi_{t^{*}})-V^{r}(\pi)}\right]

(Addition/subtraction of the common term.)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon.

Case 2:

V^{r}(\pi_{t^{*}})-V^{r}(\pi)>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. In this case, note that the following property must hold for the estimated value functions:

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{r}(\pi_{t^{*}})

\displaystyle\geq V^{r}(\pi_{t^{*}})-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}

\displaystyle>V^{r}(\pi)+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}

\displaystyle\geq\hat{V}^{r}(\pi).

Thus, in this case,
\pi
cannot be the maximizer of
\left\{{\hat{V}^{r}(\pi_{t})}\right\}_{t\in[T]}
.

Overall number of episodes required for this technique:
TN=\frac{64C^{2}H^{2}}{\varepsilon^{4}}\log\left({\frac{2C}{\varepsilon\delta}}\right)
.
∎

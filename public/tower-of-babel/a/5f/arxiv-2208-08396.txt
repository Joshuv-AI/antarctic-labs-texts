Abstract
. For the quiver Hecke algebra R R associated with a simple Lie algebra, let R R - gmod be the category of finite-dimensional graded R R -modules. It is well-known that it categorifies the unipotent quantum coordinate ring 𝒜 q ​ ( 𝔫 ) {\mathcal{A}}_{q}(\mathfrak{n}) . The localization of R R - gmod has been defined in [ 12 ] , which will be denoted by R ~ ​ -gmod \widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod} . Its Grothendieck ring 𝒦 ⁡ ( R ~ ​ -gmod ) {\mathcal{K}}(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}) defines the localized (unipotent) quantum coordinate ring 𝒜 ~ q ​ ( 𝔫 ) \widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(\mathfrak{n}) . We shall give a certain crystal structure on the localized quantum coordinate ring by regarding the set of self-dual simple objects 𝔹 ⁡ ( R ~ ​ -gmod ) \mathbb{B}(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}) in R ~ ​ -gmod \widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod} . We also give the isomorphism of crystals from 𝔹 ⁡ ( R ~ ​ -gmod ) \mathbb{B}(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}) to the cellular crystal 𝔹 𝐢 \mathbb{B}_{\mathbf{i}} for an arbitrary reduced word 𝐢 {\mathbf{i}} of the longest Weyl group element. This result can be seen as a localized version for the categorification of the crystal B ⁡ ( ∞ ) B(\infty) by Lauda-Vazirani [ 19 ] since the crystal B ⁡ ( ∞ ) B(\infty) is realized in the cellular crystal 𝔹 𝐢 \mathbb{B}_{\mathbf{i}} .

Introduction

The quiver Hecke algebra has been introduced by

[

[

{\mathcal{K}}(R\hbox{\rm-proj})
(resp.

(resp.
R
-
gmod

R
-modules, which is equipped with a multiplication by
the convolution product on modules. In particular,
in
[

30

this isomorphism yields one to one correspondence between
the set of indecomposable projective
R
-modules
(resp. finite-dimensional simple
R
-modules) and

(resp. upper global bases/dual canonical bases) (

Those were first introduced in

The theory of Kashiwara’s crystal bases

The categorification as above also played a significant role

Theory of crystal bases would be integrated into
crystals
,
which is a certain combinatorial object holding similar properties of crystal bases and

Indeed, in

, which is defined as follows:

(

The localized category

2.
Preliminaries

\mathfrak{g}
and

\langle\quad,\quad\rangle
a pairing on
\mathfrak{t}\times\mathfrak{t}^{*}

satisfying

(\,,\,)
on

P_{+}:=\{\lambda\in P\mid\langle h_{i},\lambda\rangle\geq 0\hbox{ for any }i\in I\}
the set of dominant weights.
Set

(resp.

.

,

.

We denote the dual weight lattice of

8

Definition 4.1

\displaystyle e(\nu)e(\nu^{\prime})=\delta_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}e(\nu),\quad\sum_{\nu\in I^{\beta}}e(\nu)=1,\quad e(\nu)x_{k}=x_{k}e(\nu),\quad x_{k}x_{l}=x_{l}x_{k},

\displaystyle\tau_{l}e(\nu)=e(s_{l}(\nu))\tau_{l},\quad\tau_{k}\tau_{l}=\tau_{l}\tau_{k}\,\,\hbox{ if }|k-l|>1,

\displaystyle\tau_{k}^{2}e(\nu)={\mathscr{Q}}_{\nu_{k},\nu_{k+1}}(x_{k},x_{k+1})e(\nu),

\displaystyle(\tau_{k}x_{l}-x_{s_{k}(l)}\tau_{k})e(\nu)=\begin{cases}-e(\nu)&\hbox{ if }l=k,\,\,\nu_{k}=\nu_{k+1},\\

{\mathbf{i}}=i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}
,
there exists an isomorphism of crystals:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Psi}:\mathbb{B}(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod})

\displaystyle\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{\mathbf{i}}=\bigcup_{h\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{i}}}B^{h}(\infty)

\displaystyle C_{\Lambda}\circ S

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle{\mathbf{h}}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(S)\in B^{{\mathbf{h}}_{\Lambda}}(\infty),

where
\Psi:\mathbb{B}(\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}B(\infty)
is the isomorphism
of crystals given in Theorem
4.4
,

S
is simple in
\mathbb{B}(\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}})
and
for
\Lambda=\sum_{i}a_{i}\Lambda_{i}
set

{\mathbf{h}}_{\Lambda}=\sum_{i}a_{i}{\mathbf{h}}_{i}
.

Proof.

We shall show:

(1)

Well-definedness of
\widetilde{\Psi}
, that is,
if
C_{\Lambda}\circ S\cong C_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\circ S^{\prime}
, then

{\mathbf{h}}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(S)={\mathbf{h}}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}+\Psi(S^{\prime})
.

(2)

Bijectivity of
\widetilde{\Psi}
.

(3)

\mathrm{wt}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S)=\mathrm{wt}({\mathbf{h}}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(S))
.

(4)

\varepsilon_{i}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S)=\varepsilon_{i}({\mathbf{h}}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(S))
.

(5)

\widetilde{\Psi}\circ\widetilde{F}_{i}=\tilde{f}_{i}\,\circ\widetilde{\Psi}
and

\widetilde{\Psi}\circ\widetilde{E}_{i}=\tilde{e}_{i}\,\circ\widetilde{\Psi}
.

First, let us see (1). We may show that

{\bf h}_{\Lambda+\mu}+\Psi(S)={\bf h}_{\Lambda^{\prime}+\mu}+\Psi(S^{\prime})

in case of
C_{\Lambda+\mu}\circ S\cong C_{\Lambda^{\prime}+\mu}\circ S^{\prime}
for

\mu\gg 0
satisfying
\Lambda+\mu,\Lambda^{\prime}+\mu\in P_{+}
. Thus,
we shall show if
S^{\prime}\cong C_{\Lambda}\circ S
for simple modules
S,S^{\prime}\in\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}}

and
\Lambda\in P_{+}
, then
\Psi(S^{\prime})=\Psi(S)+{\bf h}_{\Lambda}
.
Here we denote
(0)_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes(0)_{i_{N}}
by
\bf 0_{\mathbf{i}}
and identify

u_{\infty}
with
\bf 0_{\mathbf{i}}
.
By the definition of the determinantial module

C_{\Lambda}={\bf M}(w_{0}\Lambda,\Lambda)
and the isomorphism of crystals

\Psi:\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}}\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}B(\infty)
,
we know that there exists a sequence of indices

j_{1},\cdots,j_{l},j_{l+1},\cdots,j_{l+m}
such that

\widetilde{E}_{j_{l+m}}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{j_{l+1}}C_{\Lambda}={\bf 1}
and

\widetilde{E}_{j_{l}}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{j_{1}}S={\bf 1}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{j_{l+m}}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{j_{1}}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{j_{l+m}}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{j_{l+1}}(C_{\Lambda}\circ\widetilde{E}_{j_{l}}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{j_{1}}S)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{j_{l+m}}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{j_{l+1}}(C_{\Lambda}\circ{\bf 1})\cong\widetilde{E}_{j_{l+m}}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{j_{l+1}}(C_{\Lambda})\cong{\bf 1},

which also means
\widetilde{E}_{j_{l+m}}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{j_{1}}S^{\prime}={\bf 1}
.
Then, since
\Psi:\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}}\to B(\infty)
is an isomorphism of crystals,
we find that
\Psi(S^{\prime})=\tilde{f}_{j_{1}}\cdots\tilde{f}_{j_{l+m}}{\bf 0}_{\mathbf{i}}
.
It follows from Proposition
3.12
(1) and Proposition
6.2

that

\tilde{f}_{j_{1}}\cdots\tilde{f}_{j_{l+m}}{\bf 0}_{\mathbf{i}}=\tilde{f}_{j_{1}}\cdots\tilde{f}_{j_{l}}(\tilde{f}_{j_{l+1}}\cdots\tilde{f}_{j_{l+m}}{\bf 0}_{\mathbf{i}})=\tilde{f}_{j_{1}}\cdots\tilde{f}_{j_{l}}({\bf h}_{\Lambda})=\tilde{f}_{j_{1}}\cdots\tilde{f}_{j_{l}}({\bf 0}_{\mathbf{i}}+{\bf h}_{\Lambda})=\Psi(S)+{\bf h}_{\Lambda}.

Hence, we obtain
\Psi(S^{\prime})=\Psi(S)+{\bf h}_{\Lambda}
and then (1).

Next, let us show (2) the bijectivity of
\widetilde{\Psi}
.
For any
x\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathbf{i}}
by Proposition
3.12
, there exists

\Lambda\in P
and
b\in B(\infty)
such that
x={\bf h}_{\Lambda}+b\in B^{{\bf h}_{\Lambda}}(\infty)
.
Then, the fact
\widetilde{\Psi}(C_{\Lambda}\circ\Psi^{-1}(b))={\bf h}_{\Lambda}+b
shows the
surjectivity of
\widetilde{\Psi}
.
The following lemma is almost evident by the property of the localization method:

Lemma 8.2
.

For any simple modules
M,M^{\prime}\in\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}
, there exist
\xi\in P
and simple
modules
L,L^{\prime}\in\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}}
such that

M=C_{\xi}\circ L
and
M^{\prime}=C_{\xi}\circ L^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For
M,\,M^{\prime}
there exist
\Lambda,\,\Lambda^{\prime}\in P
and
S,\,S^{\prime}\in\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}}
such that

M\cong C_{\Lambda}\circ S
and
M^{\prime}\cong C_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\circ S^{\prime}
.
Then, there exists a dominant weight
\mu\gg 0
such that

\Lambda+\mu,\,\Lambda^{\prime}+\mu\in P_{+}
and

L:=C_{\mu}\circ M\cong C_{\Lambda+\mu}\circ S,\,L^{\prime}:=C_{\Lambda^{\prime}+\mu}\circ S^{\prime}\cong C_{\mu}\circ M^{\prime}\in\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}}
up to grading shift. Note that both
L,\,L^{\prime}
are simple.
Thus, setting
\xi=-\mu
, since

C_{\mu}
is invertible, we get
the desired result.

For any
M,M^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod})
, by the above lemma we can set

M=C_{\mu}\circ L
and
M^{\prime}=C_{\mu}\circ L^{\prime}
for some

\mu\in P
,
L,L^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}})
.
Here we assume
\widetilde{\Psi}(C_{\mu}\circ L)=\widetilde{\Psi}(C_{\mu}\circ L^{\prime})
.
Then we obtain
{\bf h}_{\mu}+\Psi(L)={\bf h}_{\mu}+\Psi(L^{\prime})
,
which implies
\Psi(L)=\Psi(L^{\prime})
and then
L\cong L^{\prime}
since
\Psi

is bijective.
Then, one has
M\cong M^{\prime}
, which means the injectivity of
\widetilde{\Psi}
.

Let us see (3).
By the definition of
\mathrm{wt}
in (
7.7
) and Proposition
6.2
,
it is immediate that

\mathrm{wt}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S)=w_{0}\Lambda-\Lambda+\mathrm{wt}(\Psi(S))=\mathrm{wt}({\bf h}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(S))
.

As for (4), first by the definition of
\varepsilon_{i}
on
\mathbb{B}(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod})

in (
7.7
)
one has

\varepsilon_{i}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S)=\varepsilon_{i}(\Psi(S))-\langle h_{i},w_{0}\Lambda\rangle
.
Now, set
{\bf h}_{\Lambda}=(m_{1},\cdots,m_{N})\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{i}}
and
\Psi(S)=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in B(\infty)
, where

m_{k}:=\langle h_{i_{k}},s_{i_{k+1}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\Lambda\rangle
.
Let
\{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots\}=\{k\mid i=i_{k}\}
where
k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots
.
By the definition of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{i}}
, one has
\sigma_{k_{1}}({\bf h}_{\Lambda})=\sigma_{k_{2}}({\bf h}_{\Lambda})=\cdots
and then

\varepsilon_{i}({\bf h}_{\Lambda})=\sigma_{k_{1}}({\bf h}_{\Lambda})=m_{k_{1}}+\sum_{j<k_{1}}a_{i,i_{j}}m_{j}.

Using this formula, one gets

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}({\bf h}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(S))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm max}\{\sigma_{k}({\bf h}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(S))\mid i=i_{k}\}={\rm max}\{x_{k}+m_{k}+\sum_{j<k}a_{i,i_{j}}(x_{j}+m_{j})\mid i=i_{k}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm max}\{x_{k}+\sum_{j<k}a_{i,i_{j}}x_{j}\mid i=i_{k}\}+m_{k_{1}}+\sum_{j<k_{1}}a_{i,i_{j}}m_{j}=\varepsilon_{i}(\Psi(S))+\varepsilon_{i}({\bf h}_{\Lambda}).

It yields that

\displaystyle\langle h_{i},w_{0}\Lambda\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle h_{i},s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\Lambda\rangle=\langle h_{i},s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\Lambda-\langle h_{i_{1}},s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\Lambda\rangle\alpha_{i_{1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle h_{i},s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\Lambda\rangle-\langle h_{i_{1}},\alpha_{i_{2}}\rangle m_{1}=\langle h_{i},s_{i_{3}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\Lambda-\langle h_{i_{2}},s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\Lambda\rangle\alpha_{i_{2}}\rangle-\langle h_{i},\alpha_{i_{1}}\rangle m_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle h_{i},s_{i_{3}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\Lambda\rangle-\langle h_{i},\alpha_{i_{2}}\rangle m_{2}-\langle h_{i},\alpha_{i_{1}}\rangle m_{1}=\cdots=\langle h_{i},s_{i_{k_{1}+1}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\Lambda\rangle-\sum_{j\leq k_{1}}\langle h_{i},\alpha_{i_{j}}\rangle m_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{k_{1}}-2m_{k_{1}}-\sum_{j<k_{1}}\langle h_{i},\alpha_{i_{j}}\rangle m_{j}=-m_{k_{1}}-\sum_{j<k_{1}}\langle h_{i},\alpha_{i_{j}}\rangle m_{j}=-\varepsilon_{i}({\bf h}_{\Lambda}),

which shows (4), that is,

\varepsilon_{i}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S)=\varepsilon_{i}(\Psi(S))-\langle h_{i},w_{0}\Lambda\rangle=\varepsilon_{i}(\Psi(S))+\varepsilon_{i}({\bf h}_{\Lambda})=\varepsilon_{i}({\bf h}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(S)).

Let us investigate (5). For a simple
C_{\Lambda}\circ S\in\mathbb{B}(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod})
, it yields

\widetilde{\Psi}\widetilde{F}_{i}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S)=\widetilde{\Psi}(C_{\Lambda}\circ\widetilde{F}_{i}S)={\bf h}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(\widetilde{F}_{i}S)={\bf h}_{\Lambda}+\tilde{f}_{i}\Psi(S),

and by (
3.35
) in Proposition
3.12
, one has

\tilde{f}_{i}\widetilde{\Psi}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S)=\tilde{f}_{i}({\bf h}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(S))={\bf h}_{\Lambda}+\tilde{f}_{i}\Psi(S).

It shows
\widetilde{\Psi}\circ\widetilde{F}_{i}=\tilde{f}_{i}\,\circ\widetilde{\Psi}
.
By use of the formula
\widetilde{\Psi}\circ\widetilde{F}_{i}=\tilde{f}_{i}\,\circ\widetilde{\Psi}
and

\widetilde{F}_{i}\widetilde{E}_{i}=\widetilde{E}_{i}\widetilde{F}_{i}={\rm id}_{\mathbb{B}(\widetilde{R}{\rm-gmod})}
,
it is clear that

{\bf h}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(S)=\widetilde{\Psi}(\widetilde{F}_{i}\widetilde{E}_{i}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S))=\tilde{f}_{i}\,\widetilde{\Psi}(\widetilde{E}_{i}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S))

and then applying
\tilde{e}_{i}
on the both sides, it
follows from
\tilde{f}_{i}\tilde{e}_{i}=\tilde{e}_{i}\tilde{f}_{i}={\rm id}_{\mathbb{B}_{\mathbf{i}}}
that

\tilde{e}_{i}({\bf h}_{\Lambda}+\Psi(S))=\widetilde{\Psi}(\widetilde{E}_{i}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S)),

which implies
\tilde{e}_{i}\widetilde{\Psi}=\widetilde{\Psi}\widetilde{E}_{i}
.
Now, we have completed the proof of the theorem.

9.1.
Operator
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}

Define the
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-linear anti-automorphism
\star
of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
by

(q^{h})^{\star}=q^{-h},\quad e_{i}^{\star}=e_{i},\quad f_{i}^{\star}=f_{i}.

Theorem 9.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

Set
L^{\star}(\infty):=\{u^{\star}\,|\,u\in L(\infty)\}
,

B^{\star}(\infty):=\{b^{\star}\,|\,b\in B(\infty)\}
. Then we have

\displaystyle L^{\star}(\infty)=L(\infty),

\displaystyle B^{\star}(\infty)=B(\infty).

From the proof of Theorem 5.13 in
[
12
]
we get

Proposition 9.2

(
[
12
]
)
.

For
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\cdots,\nu_{m-1},\nu_{m})\in I^{\beta}
(
m:=|\beta|
)
set
\overline{\nu}=(\nu_{m},\nu_{m-1},\cdots,\nu_{2},\nu_{1})
.
Define the automorphism
{\mathfrak{a}}
on
R(\beta)
by

{\mathfrak{a}}(e(\nu))=e(\overline{\nu}),\quad{\mathfrak{a}}(x_{i}e(\nu))=x_{m-i+1}e(\overline{\nu}),\quad{\mathfrak{a}}(\tau_{j}e(\nu))=-\tau_{m-j}e(\overline{\nu}).

Then, there exists the functor
\mathfrak{a}:\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}}\to\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}}
such that

\mathfrak{a}(C_{i})=C_{i^{*}}
(
\forall i\in I
),
\mathfrak{a}^{2}\cong{\rm id}

and
\mathfrak{a}(X\circ Y)\cong\mathfrak{a}(Y)\circ\mathfrak{a}(X)
for
X,Y\in\hbox{$R$-\hbox{gmod}}
. Furthermore,
it is extended to the functor
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}:\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}\to\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}
which satisfies

(9.1)

\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}\cong{\rm id},\quad\hbox{and}\quad\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(X\circ Y)\cong\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(Y)\circ\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(X)\quad\hbox{ for }X,Y\in\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}.

Note that
\mathfrak{a}
(resp.
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}
) induces the operation
\star

on
{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(\mathfrak{n})
(resp.
\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}_{q}}(\mathfrak{n})
) since

\mathfrak{a}(L(i))=L(i)
and then one has
\mathfrak{a}(f_{i})=f_{i}
(resp.
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(f_{i})=f_{i}
)
on
{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(\mathfrak{n})
(resp.
\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}_{q}}(\mathfrak{n})
).
Now, we obtain the following:

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}:\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}\to\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}
be the functor as above.
It yields

(9.2)

\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathbb{B}(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}))=\mathbb{B}(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}).

Proof.

It is evident that
for any simple module
L\in\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}
,
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L)
is also simple.
Therefore, it suffices to show that
if a simple module
L
satisfies
L\cong L^{*}
, then
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L)\cong\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L)^{*}
.
To do that, we show the following lemma:

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
\psi
be the anti-automorphism on
R(\beta)
as in
4.1
. We obtain

(9.3)

\psi\circ\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}\circ\psi.

Proof.
For
\beta
with
|\beta|=m
, it is trivial that for the generators
x_{i},e(\nu)
of
R(\beta)
,

\psi\circ\mathfrak{a}(e(\nu))=e(\overline{\nu})=\mathfrak{a}\circ\psi(e(\nu)),\qquad\psi\circ\mathfrak{a}(x_{i}e(\nu))=e(\overline{\nu})x_{m+1-i}=x_{m+1-i}e(\overline{\nu})=\mathfrak{a}\circ\psi(x_{i}(e(\nu))

One also gets

\displaystyle\psi\circ\mathfrak{a}(\tau_{j}e(\nu))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\psi(\tau_{m-j}e(\overline{\nu}))=-e(\overline{\nu})\tau_{m-j}=-\tau_{m-j}e(s_{m-j}(\overline{\nu}))=-\tau_{m-j}e(\overline{s_{j}\nu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}(\tau_{j}e(s_{j}\nu))=\mathfrak{a}(e(\nu)\tau_{j})=\mathfrak{a}\circ\psi(\tau_{j}e(\nu)),

which completes the proof.

By this lemma, we find that

(9.4)

\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L)^{*}\cong\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L^{*}).

Since
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L)
is simple, by Lemma
7.3
there exists a unique
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(q^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L))^{*}\cong q^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L)\cong\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(q^{n}L)
and then

q^{-n}(\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L))^{*}\cong(q^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L))^{*}\cong\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(q^{n}L)
.
Therefore, owing to (
9.4
) we get

\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(q^{n}L)\cong q^{-n}\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L^{*})\cong\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(q^{-n}L^{*})\quad\hbox{ and then }\quad q^{2n}L\cong L^{*},

which means
n=0
and then we obtain
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L)\cong\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}(L)^{*}
.

Here note that Proposition
9.3
can be seen as a generalization of
Theorem
9.1
.

Since as crystals
\mathbb{B}(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod})\cong\mathbb{B}_{\mathbf{i}}
for any reduced longest word
{\mathbf{i}}
,
the proposition above gives rise to the following problem.

Problem 1.

Can we describe
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}
-operation on

.

9.4.
Rigidity

Definition 9.7
.

Let
X,Y
be objects in a monoidal category
{\mathcal{T}}
, and
\varepsilon:X\otimes Y\to 1

and
\eta:1\to Y\otimes X
morphisms in
{\mathcal{T}}
.
We say that a pair
(X,Y)
is
dual pair
or

X
is a
left dual
to
Y
or

Y
is a
right dual
to
X
if
the following compositions are identities:

X\simeq X\otimes 1\,\,\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{\rm id}\otimes\eta}}\,\,X\otimes Y\otimes X\,\,\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\varepsilon\otimes{\rm id}}}\,\,1\otimes X\simeq X,\,\,Y\simeq 1\otimes Y\,\,\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\eta\otimes{\rm id}}}\,\,Y\otimes X\otimes Y\,\,\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{\rm id}\otimes\varepsilon}}\,\,Y\otimes 1\simeq Y

We denote a right dual to
X
by
{\mathcal{D}}(X)
and a
left dual to
X
by
{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}(X)
.

Theorem 9.8

(
[
12
]
)
.

For any finite type
R
,

\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}
is rigid, i.e.,
every object in
\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}
has left and right duals.

Note that in
[
13
]
, it is shown that for any symmetrizable Kac-Moody setting
the localized category
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{w}
is rigid.

For a category
C
, let us denote the opposite category of
C
by
C^{\rm op}
and
for a monoidal category
T=(T,\otimes)
, the reversed monoidal category

T^{\rm rev}=(T,\otimes^{\rm rev})
is defined by
X\otimes^{\rm rev}Y:=Y\otimes X
and

f\otimes^{\rm rev}g:=g\otimes f
for any objects
X,Y\in T
and any morphisms
f,g
.
By the results in
[
12
,
13
]
, we obtain

Proposition 9.9
.

The left (resp. right) dual
{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}
(resp.
{\mathcal{D}}
) defines the following equivalence of
categories

\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}\longleftrightarrow(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod}^{\rm op})^{\rm rev}.

Then by this proposition,
one finds that for a simple module
S
, both
{\mathcal{D}}(S)
and
{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}(S)

are simple. Then, we can consider the following problem.

Problem 4.

For a simple object
C_{\Lambda}\circ S\in\mathbb{B}(\widetilde{R}\hbox{-gmod})
, describe the right and
left duals explicitly:

\widetilde{\Psi}({{\mathcal{D}}}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S)),\quad\widetilde{\Psi}({{\mathcal{D}}}^{-1}(C_{\Lambda}\circ S))\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathbf{i}}.

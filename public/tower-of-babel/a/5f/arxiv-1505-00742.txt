Nevanlinna extremal measures for polynomials related to q − 1 q^{-1} -Fibonacci polynomials
F. Štampach

Abstract
The aim of this paper is the study of q − 1 q^{-1} -Fibonacci polynomials with 0 < q < 1 0<q<1 . First, the q − 1 q^{-1} -Fibonacci polynomials are related to a q q -exponential function which allows an asymptotic analysis to be worked out. Second, related basic orthogonal polynomials are investigated with the emphasis on their orthogonality properties. In particular, a compact formula for the reproducing kernel is obtained that allows to describe all the N-extremal measures of orthogonality in terms of basic hypergeometric functions and their zeros. Two special cases involving q q -sine and q q -cosine are discussed in more detail.

Introduction

In
[
13
]
, Carlitz introduced the
q
-Fibonacci polynomials
\varphi_{n}(x;q)
by

\varphi_{n}(x;q)=\sum_{2k<n}{n-k-1\brack k}_{q}q^{k^{2}}x^{n-2k-1},

(1)

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
(nonnegative integers). The
q
-binomial coefficient is defined by

{n\brack k}_{q}=\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{k}(q;q)_{n-k}},

for
k=0,1,\dots,n
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where

(a;q)_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(1-aq^{k}\right)

is the standard notation for the
q
-Pochhammer symbol.
In this paper, we intensively use the theory of basic hypergeometric series and, in the notation, we will strictly follow Gasper and Rahman’s book
[
18
]
.
In addition, if it is not stated otherwise, we always assume
0<q<1
.

The polynomials
\varphi_{n}(x;q)
satisfy the second-order recurrence

\varphi_{n+1}(x;q)=x\varphi_{n}(x;q)+q^{n-1}\varphi_{n-1}(x;q),\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(2)

with the initial conditions
\varphi_{0}(x;q)=0
and
\varphi_{1}(x;q)=1
. Let us mention that
\varphi_{n}(1;q)
are polynomials in
q
first considered by I. Schur in conjunction with his proof of the Rogers-Ramanujan
identities
[
31
]
. It can be referred as the
q
-analogue to the Fibonacci numbers
F_{n}
since clearly
\varphi_{n}(1;1)=F_{n}
, see
[
12
]
. For a different definition of
q
-Fibonacci numbers, see
also
[
21
]
.

One of our primary intention is the study of orthogonality of related polynomials

T_{n}(x;q)=(-{\rm i})^{n}q^{-n/2}\varphi_{n+1}({\rm i}q^{1/2}x;q),\quad n=-1,0,1,2,\dots.

(3)

They fulfill the second-order difference equation

T_{n+1}(x;q)=xT_{n}(x;q)-q^{n-1}T_{n-1}(x;q),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(4)

with the initial conditions
T_{-1}(x;q)=0
and
T_{0}(x;q)=1
. Identity (
1
) together with (
3
) yield the explicit expression

T_{n}(x;q)=\sum_{2k\leq n}{n-k\brack k}_{q}(-1)^{k}q^{k(k-1)}x^{n-2k},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

The polynomials
\varphi_{n}(x;q)
as well as
T_{n}(x;q)
attract attention of mathematicians for their beautiful properties. Let us mention at least their intriguing
relation to the Rogers-Ramanujan function, associated continued fraction, and the celebrated Rogers-Ramanujan identities
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
17
,
19
]
.
Some algebraic and combinatorial properties of these polynomials have also been studied in
[
13
,
16
]
.

By the spectral theorem for orthogonal polynomials, the sequence
\{T_{n}(x;q)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
obeying the recurrence rule (
4
),
constitutes a system of polynomials orthogonal with respect to a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
for any
q>0
.
In the case
0<q<1
, polynomials
T_{n}(x;q)
are a particular case of orthogonal polynomials studied by Al-Salam and Ismail
in
[
2
]
, consult also
[
19
, Sec. 13.6]
. The respective measure of orthogonality
\mu_{q}
is unique and its Stieltjes transform can be
expressed as a generalized Rogers-Ramanujan continued fraction.

Concerning the asymptotic behavior of
T_{n}(x;q)
, for
n\rightarrow\infty
and
0<q<1
, one has

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}x^{-n}T_{n}(x;q)=A_{q}\left(q^{-1}x^{2}\right)\quad\forall x\neq 0,

where

A_{q}(z)=\,_{0}\phi_{1}\!\left(\,;0;q,-qz\right)

is the Ramanujan’s entire function.
With the aid of Markov’s theorem, one can express the Stieltjes transform of
\mu_{q}
in the form

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mbox{d}\mu_{q}(x)}{1-xz}=\frac{A_{q}\left(z^{2}\right)}{A_{q}\left(q^{-1}z^{2}\right)},

(5)

see
[
2
, Thm. 3.2, 3.4]
. Equality (
5
)
holds for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
for which
A_{q}\left(q^{-1}z^{2}\right)\neq 0
. Recall that the set of zeros of the
Ramanujan’s entire function
A_{q}
is composed of denumerable number of positive isolated points accumulating at
\infty
,
[
2
,
20
]
.
Let us denote these zeros as
z_{j}=z_{j}(q)
, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
. The orthogonality relation for
\{T_{n}(x;q)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
reads

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{q}(qz_{j})}{z_{j}A_{q}^{\prime}(z_{j})}T_{n}\left(\pm q^{-1/2}z_{j}^{-1/2}\right)T_{m}\left(\pm q^{-1/2}z_{j}^{-1/2}\right)=-2q^{n(n-1)/2}\delta_{mn},

where
T_{n}(\pm y)T_{m}(\pm y)
is a shorthand for the expression

T_{n}\left(y;q\right)T_{m}\left(y;q\right)+T_{n}\left(-y;q\right)T_{m}\left(-y;q\right)\!,

see
[
2
, Thm. 3.3, Cor. 4.5]
.

In the case
q=1
, the polynomials
T_{n}(x;1)
are essentially the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind
U_{n}
. More precisely, one has
T_{n}(x;1)=U_{n}\left(x/2\right)
.
This family of polynomials is deeply investigated and very well known
[
15
]
.

In this paper we focus on the case
q>1
. This case is particularly interesting for there are infinitely many measures
of orthogonality. In other words, the corresponding Hamburger moment problem is in the indeterminate case if
q>1
.
Solutions of the indeterminate moment problem are characterized with the aid of four entire functions used in the Nevanlinna parametrization.
In the case under investigation, these functions are expressed as certain
q
-extensions
of sine
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and cosine
\mathcal{C}_{q}
, for the functions
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and

\mathcal{C}_{q}
naturally arise in the asymptotic formulas for polynomials
T_{n}(x;q)
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall certain version of
q
-exponential function
\mathcal{E}_{q}
, associated
q
-trigonometric functions

\mathcal{S}_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}
, and their hyperbolic counterparts
\mathcal{S}h_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}h_{q}
. These functions appeared at many various places and have been
studied extensively in past, though not in the context of
q
-Fibonacci polynomials. Section
2
recalls their basic properties and above all an addition
formula for the product of two
q
-exponential functions which is an essential identity needed for derivation of main results of this paper.

Section
3
is devoted to the study of relations between the
q
-exponential
\mathcal{E}_{q}
and the
q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials
\varphi_{n}(x;q^{-1})
.
We consider
q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials with
0<q<1
which is the same, though in some cases more convenient, as considering
q
-Fibonacci polynomials with
q>1
.
First of all, we derive a formula for
q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials in terms of the
q
-exponential
\mathcal{E}_{q}
. As a direct consequence, we obtain asymptotic formulas
for
\varphi_{n}(x;q^{-1})
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Depending on the parity of the index
n
, the functions
\mathcal{S}h_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}h_{q}
arise as the leading
term in the asymptotic formulas. Further, we prove a
q
-version of the Euler–Cassini formula for
q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials. We use a method based on a very general identity
which could be used analogously in numerous similar situations. As an application, we obtain several more subtle identities between

q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials and the functions
\mathcal{E}_{q}
,
\mathcal{S}h_{q}
, and
\mathcal{C}h_{q}
.

In Section
4
, we introduce a family of orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}(x;q)
related to
q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials.
We derive limit relations for
P_{n}(x;q)
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, as a straightforward consequence of the knowledge of the asymptotic behavior of
q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials.
This provides an alternative way of asymptotic analysis of
P_{n}(x;q)
to the usual method based on an appropriate generating
function formula for
P_{n}(x;q)
and Darboux’s theorem as it was originally done by Chen and Ismail in
[
14
, Sec. 3]
.

The second part of Section
4
contains the most important results concerning orthogonality of the polynomials
P_{n}(x;q)
. First, we recall that functions
from the Nevanlinna parametrization can be expressed in terms of the functions
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}
.
Next, with the aid of the addition formula from Section
2
, we find a compact expression
for the reproducing kernel for the polynomials
P_{n}(x;q)
. These two ingredients allow us to describe all N-extremal measures in a closed form and write down the corresponding orthogonality relations for
P_{n}(x;q)
.
In addition, we point out two special cases of orthogonality relations written in terms of the
q
-sine
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and its zeros only, and similar relation with the
q
-cosine
\mathcal{C}_{q}
and its zeros.

2
Functions
\mathcal{E}_{q}
,
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}

There are several known
q
-deformations of the exponential
function. Two commonly known are due to Jackson:

E_{q}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n-1)/2}}{(q;q)_{n}}z^{n}\quad(z\in\mathbb{C})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad e_{q}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}\quad(|z|<1),

see
[
18
, §1.3]
.

The one-parameter generalization

E_{q}^{(\alpha)}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\alpha n^{2}/2}}{(q;q)_{n}}z^{n}

where
\alpha\geq 0
, was studied by Atakishieyev in
[
6
]
, see also references therein.
Obviously, one has
E_{q}^{(0)}(z)=e_{q}(z)
and
E_{q}^{(1)}(z)=E_{q}(q^{1/2}z)
.
Ismail shows in
[
19
, Sec. 14.1]
that
E_{q}^{(\alpha)}
are entire functions of order zero,
hence they have infinitely many zeros. The particular case corresponding to the value
\alpha=1/2
plays a crucial
role in asymptotic formulas for
q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials, as
n\rightarrow\infty
. An asymptotic and numerical
study of zeros of
E_{q}^{(1/2)}
has been made in
[
28
]
.
For the sake of simplicity, we use the notation
\mathcal{E}_{q}(z)
for
E_{q}^{(1/2)}(z)
. Thus, we consider the function

\mathcal{E}_{q}(z)=E_{q}^{(1/2)}(z)={}_{1}\phi_{1}\left(0;-q^{1/2};q^{1/2},-q^{1/4}z\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}/4}}{(q;q)_{n}}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Let us also remark that this function can be obtained as a rescaled limit of yet another more general family of
q
-exponential functions introduced by Ismail and Zhang in
[
24
]

and later extensively studied mainly by Suslov, see
[
36
]
. Particularly, the function
\mathcal{E}_{q}
as well as the corresponding trigonometric functions appears in Suslov’s book
in
[
36
, Sec. 2.5]
. The function
\mathcal{E}_{q}
fulfills the second order
q
-difference equation

\mathcal{E}_{q}(z)-\mathcal{E}_{q}(qz)=zq^{1/4}\mathcal{E}_{q}(q^{1/2}z).

(6)

Corresponding
q
-sine and
q
-cosine functions can be introduced by formulas

\mathcal{S}_{q}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n(n+1)}}{(q;q)_{2n+1}}z^{2n+1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathcal{C}_{q}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{2n}}z^{2n},

(7)

for
z\in\mathbb{C}
. With this choice, the
q
-version of Euler’s identity

\mathcal{E}_{q}(\mathrm{i}z)=\mathcal{C}_{q}(z)+\mathrm{i}q^{1/4}\mathcal{S}_{q}(z)

(8)

holds. Clearly,

\lim_{q\rightarrow 1-}\mathcal{S}_{q}((1-q)z)=\sin z\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lim_{q\rightarrow 1-}\mathcal{C}_{q}((1-q)z)=\cos z.

Alternatively, functions
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}
can be written as the
{}_{1}\phi_{1}
function with the base
q^{2}
,

\mathcal{S}_{q}(z)=\frac{z}{1-q}\,_{1}\phi_{1}(0;q^{3};q^{2},q^{2}z^{2})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{C}_{q}(z)=\,_{1}\phi_{1}(0;q;q^{2},qz^{2})

(9)

and in this form, they were introduced by Koornwinder and Swarttouw in
[
26
, Sec. 5]
.
The formulas in (
9
) allow to express the functions
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}
in terms of the third Jackson (or Hahn-Exton or
{}_{1}\phi_{1}
)
q
-Bessel function

J_{\nu}^{(3)}(z;q)=\frac{(q^{\nu+1};q){}_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\,z^{\nu}\,_{1}\phi_{1}(0;q^{\nu+1};q,qz^{2}),

see, for example,
[
25
,
26
]
. Namely, one has

\mathcal{S}_{q}(z)=\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}z^{1/2}J_{1/2}^{(3)}(z;q^{2})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathcal{C}_{q}(z)=\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}z^{1/2}J_{-1/2}^{(3)}(q^{-1/2}z;q^{2})

(10)

which, if compared with the classical formulas

\sin z=\sqrt{\frac{\pi z}{2}}J_{1/2}(z)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\cos z=\sqrt{\frac{\pi z}{2}}J_{-1/2}(z),

gives another reason to refer to
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}
as
q
-sine and
q
-cosine, respectively.
For some additional properties concerning
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}
, consult also paper of Bustoz and Cardoso

[
11
]
.

Recall that the functions
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}
have infinite number of real interlacing zeros which are all simple.
This may be deduced from results of
[
25
, Sec. 3]
for
J_{\nu}^{(3)}
applying the relations (
10
).
Further, it is straightforward to verify that

\mathcal{S}_{q}(z)-\mathcal{S}_{q}(qz)=z\mathcal{C}_{q}(q^{1/2}z)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathcal{C}_{q}(z)-\mathcal{C}_{q}(qz)=-zq^{1/2}\mathcal{S}_{q}(q^{1/2}z).

(11)

Consequently, functions
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}
are linearly independent solutions to the second-order
q
-difference equation

u(q^{2}z)+\left(q^{2}z^{2}-(1+q)\right)u(qz)+qu(z)=0.

It is also remarkable that there is a symmetry while interchanging
q
and
q^{-1}
, namely

\mathcal{E}_{q^{-1}}(q^{-1/4}z)=\mathcal{E}_{q}(-q^{1/4}z)

and

q^{-1/4}\mathcal{S}_{q^{-1}}(q^{-1/4}z)=-q^{1/4}\mathcal{S}_{q}(q^{1/4}z)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathcal{C}_{q^{-1}}(q^{-1/4}z)=\mathcal{C}_{q}(q^{1/4}z).

At last, let us define the corresponding
q
-analogue to the hyperbolic sine and cosine by formulas

\mathcal{S}h_{q}(z)=-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{S}_{q}(\mathrm{i}z)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathcal{C}h_{q}(z)=\mathcal{C}_{q}(\mathrm{i}z),

(12)

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Finally, we recall an addition formula for
\mathcal{E}_{q}
. It has been originally derived by Rahman
[
30
]
and reproved by Suslov,
see
[
36
, Thm. 3.6]
. Since several important results stated below are a consequence of this formula we provide an independent
simple proof.

Proposition 1
.

For
u,v\in\mathbb{C}
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{q}(u)\mathcal{E}_{q}(-v)={}_{3}\phi_{3}\left[\begin{matrix}0,&u^{-1}vq^{1/2},&uv^{-1}q^{1/2}\\
q^{1/2},&-q^{1/2},&-q\end{matrix}\ ;q,uvq^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 66.0pt+q^{1/4}\frac{u-v}{1-q}\,_{3}\phi_{3}\left[\begin{matrix}0,&u^{-1}vq,&uv^{-1}q\\
q^{3/2},&-q^{3/2},&-q\end{matrix}\ ;q,uvq\right]\!.

(13)

Proof.

By multiplication, we obtain

\mathcal{E}_{q}(u)\mathcal{E}_{q}(-v)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}/4}}{(q;q)_{n}}v^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}_{q}q^{k(k-1)/2}\left(-q^{\frac{1-n}{2}}\frac{u}{v}\right)^{k}.

The inner sum equals
(q^{(1-n)/2}uv^{-1};q)_{n},
as it follows from the formula
[
18
, Exer. 1.2]

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}_{q}q^{k(k-1)/2}(-z)^{k}=(z;q)_{n}\,.

Now, it suffices to write separately the series with odd and even summation index
n
and take into account that

(q^{(1-n)/2}uv^{-1};q)_{n}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{k}u^{k}v^{-k}q^{-k^{2}/2}(u^{-1}vq^{1/2};q)_{k}(uv^{-1}q^{1/2};q)_{k},&\mbox{if }n=2k,\\
(-1)^{k}u^{k}v^{-k}q^{-k(k+1)/2}(u^{-1}vq;q)_{k}(uv^{-1};q)_{k+1},&\mbox{if }n=2k+1.\end{cases}

In this way, one arrives at the expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{q}(u)\mathcal{E}_{q}(-v)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}/2}}{(q;q)_{2n}}(u^{-1}vq^{1/2};q)_{n}(uv^{-1}q^{1/2};q)_{n}\ u^{n}v^{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 66.0pt+q^{1/4}(u-v)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n(n+1)/2}}{(q;q)_{2n+1}}(u^{-1}vq;q)_{n}(uv^{-1}q;q)_{n}\ u^{n}v^{n}.

∎

As an immediate consequence of (
13
) and (
8
), one has the following statement, cf. also
[
36
, Thm. 3.8]
.

Corollary 2
.

For
u,v\in\mathbb{C}
, one has

\mathcal{C}_{q}(u)\mathcal{C}_{q}(v)+q^{1/2}\mathcal{S}_{q}(u)\mathcal{S}_{q}(v)={}_{3}\phi_{3}\left[\begin{matrix}0,&u^{-1}vq^{1/2},&uv^{-1}q^{1/2}\\
q^{1/2},&-q^{1/2},&-q\end{matrix}\ ;q,-uvq^{1/2}\right]

(14)

and

\mathcal{S}_{q}(u)\mathcal{C}_{q}(v)-\mathcal{C}_{q}(u)\mathcal{S}_{q}(v)=\frac{u-v}{1-q}\,_{3}\phi_{3}\left[\begin{matrix}0,&u^{-1}vq,&uv^{-1}q\\
q^{3/2},&-q^{3/2},&-q\end{matrix}\ ;q,-uvq\right]\!.

(15)

Note that by setting
u=q^{1/2}v
in (
14
) one gets

\mathcal{C}_{q}(q^{1/2}v)\mathcal{C}_{q}(v)+q^{1/2}\mathcal{S}_{q}(q^{1/2}v)\mathcal{S}_{q}(v)=1.

(16)

In addition, from (
8
) and (
14
), it readily follows

|\mathcal{E}_{q}(ix)|^{2}=\mathcal{C}_{q}^{2}(x)+q^{1/2}\mathcal{S}_{q}^{2}(x)={}_{2}\phi_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}0,&q^{1/2}\\
-q^{1/2},&-q\end{matrix}\ ;q,-x^{2}q^{1/2}\right],\quad\mbox{for }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(17)

3
Relations between
\mathcal{E}_{q}
and
q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials

Recall that rather than assuming
q>1
, it is more convenient to write
q^{-1}
instead of
q
in (
3
) and still suppose
0<q<1
.
Then one arrives at the
q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials with the explicit expression:

\varphi_{n}(x;q^{-1})=\sum_{2k<n}{n-k-1\brack k}_{q}q^{k(k+1-n)}x^{n-2k-1},\quad\mbox{ for }n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(18)

Note the sequence

\left\{q^{(n-1)^{2}/2}\varphi_{n}\left(q^{-(n-1)/2}x;q^{-1}\right)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

satisfies the same recurrence (
2
) and initial conditions as
\{\varphi_{n}(x;q)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

Hence, we have the following relation between
q
-Fibonacci and
q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials,

\varphi_{n}(x;q)=q^{(n-1)^{2}/2}\varphi_{n}\left(q^{-(n-1)/2}x;q^{-1}\right),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

cf.
[
13
, Eq. (2.6)]
.

First, we derive an expression for the
q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials
\varphi_{n}(x;q^{-1})
in terms of the
q
-exponential function
\mathcal{E}_{q}
.
A limit relation for
\varphi_{n}(x;q^{-1})
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, then readily follows.

Proposition 3
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, one has

\varphi_{n}(x;q^{-1})=\frac{1}{2}q^{-(n-1)^{2}/4}\left(\mathcal{E}_{q}(x)\mathcal{E}_{q}(-q^{n/2}x)-(-1)^{n}\mathcal{E}_{q}(-x)\mathcal{E}_{q}(q^{n/2}x)\right).

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, put

\chi_{n}^{\pm}=(\mp 1)^{n}q^{-n(n-2)/4}\mathcal{E}_{q}(\pm q^{n/2}x).

By using (
6
), one readily verifies that the sequences
\{\chi_{n}^{+}\}
and
\{\chi_{n}^{-}\}
fulfill the recurrence (
2
) with
q
replaced by
q^{-1}
.
Next, with the aid of the identity (
13
), one obtains

\chi_{0}^{+}\chi_{1}^{-}-\chi_{0}^{-}\chi_{1}^{+}=q^{1/4}\left(\mathcal{E}_{q}(x)\mathcal{E}_{q}(-q^{1/2}x)+\mathcal{E}_{q}(-x)\mathcal{E}_{q}(q^{1/2}x)\right)=2q^{1/4}.

Finally, the sequence

\left\{\frac{\chi_{0}^{+}\chi_{n}^{-}-\chi_{0}^{-}\chi_{n}^{+}}{\chi_{0}^{+}\chi_{1}^{-}-\chi_{0}^{-}\chi_{1}^{+}}\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

coincides with
\{\varphi_{n}(x;q^{-1})\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
since it satisfies the same recurrence with the same initial conditions. The statement now follows.
∎

The following corollary readily follows from (
8
), (
12
), and Proposition
3
.

Corollary 4
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

Here, it suffices to recall the definition of

4
Related orthogonal polynomials, reproducing kernel, and N-extremal measures of orthogonality

In this section, we draw our attention to a family of orthogonal polynomials associated to

q^{-1}
-Fibonacci polynomials. The main goal is the description of all their measures of orthogonality
which are N-extremal solutions of the corresponding Hamburger moment problem. Main references concerning
the Hamburger moment problem are classical treatises
[
1
]
and
[
32
]
, for a brief
summary, see also
[
19
, Sec. 21.1]
.

Recall that polynomials
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, are orthogonal polynomials
of the first and second kind, respectively, if they are solutions of the recurrence relation

\beta_{n-1}u_{n-1}+(\alpha_{n}-z)u_{n}+\beta_{n}u_{n+1}=0,\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

satisfying initial conditions
P_{0}(z)=1
,
P_{1}(z)=(z-\alpha_{0})/\beta_{0}
,
Q_{0}(z)=0
and
Q_{1}(z)=1/\beta_{0}
,
see
[
1
, Chp. 1]
. It is to be assumed that
\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta_{n}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

For
n=-1,0,1,2,\dots
, define

P_{n}(x;q)=(-{\rm i})^{n}q^{n(n+1)/4}\varphi_{n+1}({\rm i}q^{-1/2}x;q^{-1}).

(26)

The polynomials
P_{n}(x;q)
form the family of orthogonal polynomials of the first kind
with
\alpha_{n}=0
and
\beta_{n}=q^{-n/2}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. In addition, they are
the symmetric counterpart to the polynomials
T_{n}(x;q^{-1})
since their monic version coincides with

T_{n}(x;q^{-1})
, namely

q^{-n(n-1)/4}P_{n}(x;q)=T_{n}(x;q^{-1}).

For the corresponding orthogonal polynomials of the second kind
Q_{n}(x;q)
, one has

Q_{n}(x;q)=P_{n-1}(q^{1/2}x;q)=(-{\rm i})^{n-1}q^{n(n-1)/4}\varphi_{n}({\rm i}x;q^{-1}),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(27)

Taking into account the equation (
18
), one easily verifies that

P_{2n-1}(0;q)=Q_{2n}(0;q)=0\;\mbox{ and }\;P_{2n}(0;q)=Q_{2n+1}(0;q)=(-1)^{n}q^{n/2}

(28)

where
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Recall that the Hamburger moment problem associated with a family of orthogonal polynomials
of the first kind
P_{n}
(and of the second kind
Q_{n}
) is in the indeterminate case if and only if

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(P_{n}^{2}(0)+Q_{n}^{2}(0)\right)<\infty,

see, for example,
[
1
, Chp. 2]
. For polynomials
P_{n}(0;q)
and
Q_{n}(0;q)
, this condition is clearly
true for all
q
such that
0<q<1
, as it readily follows from (
28
).

Further recall that, by the Nevanlinna theorem, all measures of orthogonality
\mu_{\varphi}
of polynomials
P_{n}

are in one-to-one correspondence with functions
\varphi
belonging to the one-point compactification
of the space of Pick functions. Recall that Pick functions are defined and holomorphic on the open complex halfplane
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Im}\nolimits z>0
,
with values in the closed halfplane
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Im}\nolimits z\geq 0
.
The correspondence is established by identifying the Stieltjes transform of the measure
\mu_{\varphi}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mbox{d}\mu_{\varphi}(x)}{z-x}=\frac{A(z)\varphi(z)-C(z)}{B(z)\varphi(z)-D(z)}\,,\ \ z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}.

Entire functions
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
are local uniform limits of polynomials
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
,
D_{n}
, respectively, where

\displaystyle A_{n+1}(z)=\beta_{n}\left(Q_{n+1}(z)Q_{n}(0)-Q_{n+1}(0)Q_{n}(z)\right),

(29)

\displaystyle B_{n+1}(z)=\beta_{n}\left(P_{n+1}(z)Q_{n}(0)-Q_{n+1}(0)P_{n}(z)\right),

(30)

\displaystyle C_{n+1}(z)=\beta_{n}\left(Q_{n+1}(z)P_{n}(0)-P_{n+1}(0)Q_{n}(z)\right),

(31)

\displaystyle D_{n+1}(z)=\beta_{n}\left(P_{n+1}(z)P_{n}(0)-P_{n+1}(0)P_{n}(z)\right),

(32)

see
[
19
, Sec. 21.1]
.

The particular class of measures of orthogonality is composed by measures
\mu_{t}
associated with the Pick function

\varphi(z)=t\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
. Measures
\mu_{t}
are called N-extremal and are purely discrete. The support
of
\mu_{t}
is an unbounded set of isolated points which is known to be equal to the zero set

\mathfrak{Z}_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid B(x)t-D(x)=0\}.

(33)

Hence

\mu_{t}=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{Z}_{t}}\rho(x)\delta_{x}

(34)

where

\rho(x)=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Res}\nolimits_{z=x}\frac{A(z)t-C(z)}{B(z)t-D(z)}=\frac{A(x)t-C(x)}{B^{\prime}(x)t-D^{\prime}(x)}=\frac{1}{B^{\prime}(x)D(x)-B(x)D^{\prime}(x)}

(35)

since, for
x\in\mathfrak{Z}_{t}
,
t=D(x)/B(x)
and the general identity

A(z)D(z)-B(z)C(z)=1,

(36)

holds true for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
. The orthogonality relation for polynomials of the first kind then reads

\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{Z}_{t}}\rho(x)P_{n}(x)P_{m}(x)=\delta_{mn},\quad\forall m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(37)

Thus, the determination of the Nevanlinna functions,
B
and
D
in particular, is essential for the description
of N-extremal measures of orthogonality. Formulas for
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
can be, in turn, established if the asymptotic
behavior of
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
is known for
n\rightarrow\infty
, as one observes from formulas (
29
)-(
32
).
In the case of
P_{n}(x;q)
and
Q_{n}(x;q)
, the asymptotic formulas follow from Proposition
6

and the relations (
26
) and (
27
). We arrive at the following limits:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{-n/2}P_{2n}(x;q)=\mathcal{C}_{q}(q^{-1/2}x)

(38)

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{-n/2}P_{2n+1}(x;q)=q^{1/2}\mathcal{S}_{q}(q^{-1/2}x).

(39)

An alternative way of derivation of the last two limit relations was presented
by Chen and Ismail
[
14
]
. It is based on Darboux’s method applied to a suitable generating function
formula for
P_{n}(x;q)
. According to this method, the leading asymptotic term of

is determined by the
singularity of the generating function which is located most closely to the origin, cf.
[

The explicit form of the generating function for

). Thus, we get the description of all the N-extremal measures
\mu_{u}(q)
in terms of
{}_{3}\phi_{3}
in the form stated
as the second part of the statement. The orthogonality relation then follows from (
37

∎

Remark 15
.

The general theory on the Nevanlinna parametrization provides us with the following generalization of Proposition
11
:

For
u\in\mathbb{R}
, all the zeros
\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u),\dots
of function (
46
)
are real and simple. In addition, if we denote the function from (
46
) by
f_{u}(\,\cdot\,;q)
, then, for any
u_{1},u_{2}\in[0,s_{1}(q))
such that
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
,
zeros of functions
f_{u_{1}}(\,\cdot\,;q)
and
f_{u_{2}}(\,\cdot\,;q)
interlace.

Let us draw our attention to two special cases. Let the sequences

0<s_{1}(q)<s_{2}(q)<s_{3}(q)<\dots\;\mbox{ and }\quad 0<c_{1}(q)<c_{2}(q)<c_{3}(q)<\dots

denote all positive zeros of
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}
, respectively.
Then there is an orthogonality relation for
P_{n}(x;q)
in terms of the function
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and its zeros only.

Proposition 16
.

The orthogonality relation

(1-q)P_{n}(0;q)P_{m}(0;q)-\!\!\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathcal{S}_{q}(qs_{k}(q))}{s_{k}(q)\mathcal{S}_{q}^{\prime}(s_{k}(q))}P_{n}\left(q^{1/2}s_{k}(q);q\right)P_{m}\left(q^{1/2}s_{k}(q);q\right)=\delta_{mn}

holds for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
s_{-k}(q)=-s_{k}(q)
.

Proof.

Consider the case
t=0
in the original parametrization of N-extremal measures (
33
), (
34
), and (
35
).
The corresponding measure is supported by the set of zeros of
D(z;q)=q^{1/2}\mathcal{S}_{q}(q^{-1/2}z)
, i.e., on the set

\{0\}\cup\left\{\pm q^{1/2}s_{k}(q)\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Further, for the function
\rho
, one has

\rho\left(q^{1/2}s_{k}(q)\right)=\frac{C(q^{1/2}s_{k}(q);q)}{D^{\prime}(q^{1/2}s_{k}(q);q)}=\frac{\mathcal{C}_{q}(q^{1/2}s_{k}(q))}{\mathcal{S}_{q}^{\prime}(s_{k}(q))}=-\frac{\mathcal{S}_{q}(qs_{k}(q))}{s_{k}(q)\mathcal{S}_{q}^{\prime}(s_{k}(q))}

where the last equality holds true due to the first relation of (
11
). Clearly, the expression for
\rho\left(q^{1/2}s_{k}(q)\right)
is valid for all nonzero
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, with the convention

s_{-k}(q)=-s_{k}(q)
. Further, one has

\rho(0)=\frac{\mathcal{C}_{q}(0)}{\mathcal{S}_{q}^{\prime}(0)}=1-q,

as it follows from the very definition of
\mathcal{S}_{q}
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}
(
7
). The rest then follows from the general relation (
37
).
∎

One more orthogonality relation for
P_{n}(x;q)
, this time in terms of the function
\mathcal{C}_{q}
and its zeros
only, is as follows.

Proposition 17
.

The orthogonality relation

-\!\!\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{q}(qc_{k}(q))}{c_{k}(q)\mathcal{C}_{q}^{\prime}(c_{k}(q))}P_{n}\left(q^{1/2}c_{k}(q);q\right)P_{m}\left(q^{1/2}c_{k}(q);q\right)=\delta_{mn}

holds for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
c_{-k}(q)=-c_{k}(q)
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in the same way as the proof of Proposition
16
. Nevertheless, we provide the details for the sake of completeness.

Consider the case
t=\infty
in the original parametrization of N-extremal measures (
33
), (
34
), and (
35
).
The corresponding measure is supported by the set of zeros of
B(z;q)=-\mathcal{C}_{q}(q^{-1/2}z)
, i.e., on the set

\left\{\pm q^{1/2}c_{k}(q)\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

For the function
\rho
, one has

\rho\left(q^{1/2}c_{k}(q)\right)=\frac{A(q^{1/2}c_{k}(q);q)}{B^{\prime}(q^{1/2}c_{k}(q);q)}=\frac{-q^{1/2}\mathcal{S}_{q}(q^{1/2}c_{k}(q))}{\mathcal{C}_{q}^{\prime}(c_{k}(q))}=-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{q}(qc_{k}(q))}{c_{k}(q)\mathcal{C}_{q}^{\prime}(c_{k}(q))}

where the last equality holds true due to the second relation of (
11
). Clearly, the expression for
\rho\left(q^{1/2}c_{k}(q)\right)
is valid for all nonzero
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, with the convention

c_{-k}(q)=-c_{k}(q)
. The orthogonality relation is then a consequence of (
37
).
∎

Remark 18
.

42
) and (
8

14

Generalized derivations of BiHom-supertrialgebras
Nil MANSUROĞLU 1 , Bouzid Mosbahi 2

Abstract
. In this note, our goal is to describe the concept of generalized derivations in the context of BiHom-supertrialgebras. We provide a comprehensive analysis of the properties and applications of these generalized derivations, including their relationship with other algebraic structures. We also explore various examples and applications of BiHom-supertrialgebras in different fields of mathematics and physics. Our findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the algebraic properties and applications of BiHom-supertrialgebras, and pave the way for further research in this area.

2.
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

Given a superspace
S
. If three even bilinear maps
\dashv,\vdash,\bot:S\times S\longrightarrow S
and an even superspace homomorphism
\gamma:S\longrightarrow S
satisfy the following axioms

\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma(d\dashv q)=\gamma(d)\dashv\gamma(q),\\
\gamma(d\bot q)=\gamma(d)\bot\gamma(q),\\
(d\dashv q)\dashv\gamma(y)=\gamma(d)\dashv(q\vdash y),\\
(d\dashv q)\vdash\gamma(y)=\gamma(d)\vdash(q\vdash y),\\
(d\dashv q)\dashv\gamma(y)=\gamma(d)\dashv(q\bot y),\\
(d\dashv q)\bot\gamma(y)=\gamma(d)\bot(q\vdash y),\\
(d\bot q)\vdash\gamma(y)=\gamma(d)\vdash(q\vdash y),\\
\end{array}\quad\quad\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma(d\vdash q)=\gamma(d)\vdash\gamma(q),\\
\quad(d\dashv q)\dashv\gamma(y)=\gamma(d)\dashv(q\dashv y),\\
\quad(d\vdash q)\dashv\gamma(y)=\gamma(d)\vdash(q\dashv y),\\
\quad(d\vdash q)\vdash\gamma(y)=\gamma(d)\vdash(q\vdash y),\\
\quad(d\bot q)\dashv\gamma(y)=\gamma(d)\bot(q\dashv y),\\
\quad(d\vdash q)\bot\gamma(y)=\gamma(d)\vdash(q\bot y),\\
\quad(d\bot q)\bot\gamma(y)=\gamma(d)\bot(q\bot y)\end{array}

for every
d,q,y\in S
, then a quintuple
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma)
is called a Hom-associative supertrialgebra.

Remark 2.1
.

By taking
\gamma=id
, we obtain the associative supertrialgebra. Any Hom-associative supertrialgebra becomes a Hom-associative superdialgebra without the operation
\bot
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
S
be a superspace. If three even bilinear mappings
\dashv,\vdash,\bot:S\times S\longrightarrow S
and two even superspace homomorphisms
\gamma,\xi:S\longrightarrow S
satisfy the next axioms

\displaystyle\text{(i)}\quad\gamma\circ\xi=\xi\circ\gamma,

\displaystyle\text{(ii)}\quad(d\dashv q)\dashv\xi(y)=\gamma(d)\dashv(q\vdash r)=\gamma(d)\dashv(q\bot y),

\displaystyle\text{(iii)}\quad(d\dashv q)\dashv\xi(y)=\gamma(d)\vdash(q\dashv y),

\displaystyle\text{(iv)}\quad(d\dashv q)\vdash\gamma(y)=\xi(d)\vdash(q\vdash y)=(d\bot q)\vdash\xi(y),

\displaystyle\text{(v)}\quad(d\bot q)\dashv\xi(y)=\gamma(d)\bot(q\dashv y),

\displaystyle\text{(vi)}\quad(d\dashv q)\bot\xi(y)=\gamma(d)\bot(q\vdash y),

\displaystyle\text{(vii)}\quad(d\vdash q)\bot\xi(y)=\gamma(d)\vdash(q\bot y)

for every
d,q,y\in S
, then a 6-truple
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)
is called a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra.

Remark 2.2
.

\gamma
and
\xi
are endomorphisms with
\dashv,\vdash
and
\bot
, namely,

\displaystyle\gamma(d\dashv q)=\gamma(d)\dashv\gamma(q),\quad\xi(d\dashv q)=\xi(d)\dashv\xi(q),

\displaystyle\gamma(d\vdash q)=\gamma(d)\vdash\gamma(q),\quad\xi(d\vdash q)=\xi(d)\vdash\xi(q),

\displaystyle\gamma(d\bot q)=\gamma(d)\bot\gamma(q),\quad\xi(d\bot q)=\xi(d)\bot\xi(q)

for each
d,q\in S
. Then,
we say
S
is a multiplicative BiHom-associative supertrialgebra.

Definition 2.3
.

A linear mapping

\pi:(S_{1},\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)\rightarrow(S_{2},\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime},\bot^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})

with

\gamma^{\prime}\circ\pi=\pi\circ\gamma,\;\;\xi^{\prime}\circ\pi=\pi\circ\xi

and

\displaystyle\pi(d\dashv q)

\displaystyle=\pi(d)\dashv^{\prime}\pi(q),

\displaystyle\pi(d\vdash q)

\displaystyle=\pi(d)\vdash^{\prime}\pi(q),

\displaystyle\pi(d\bot q)

\displaystyle=\pi(d)\bot^{\prime}\pi(q)

for every
d,q\in S_{1}
is said to be a morphism of BiHom-associative supertrialgebras.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)
be a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra of dimension
n
. Given an invertible linear map
l:S\rightarrow S
.
Then, there exists an isomorphism with a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra
(S,\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime},\bot^{\prime},l\gamma\xi^{-1},l\xi l^{-1})
, where

(\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime},\bot^{\prime})=l\circ(\dashv,\vdash,\bot)\circ(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1}
). Furthermore, if
the structure constants of
(\dashv,\vdash\bot)
with the basis

\left\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\right\}
are
\left\{\phi^{k}_{ij},\psi^{k}_{ij},\delta^{k}_{ij}\right\}
, then the structure constants of
(\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime},\bot^{\prime})

with the basis
\left\{l(u_{1}),\dots,l(u_{n})\right\}
are same.

Proof.

Here, one axiom only will be proved.
For any invertible linear mapping
l:S\rightarrow S
,
(S,\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime},\bot^{\prime},l\gamma l^{-1},l\xi l^{-1})
is a
BiHom-associative supertrialgebra.

\displaystyle(u\dashv^{\prime}v)\dashv^{\prime}l\xi l^{-1}(w)

\displaystyle=l\dashv(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(u,v)\dashv^{\prime}l\xi l^{-1}(w)

\displaystyle=(l(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(u\dashv v))\dashv^{\prime}l\xi l^{-1}(w)

\displaystyle=l(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(l(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(u\dashv v)\dashv l^{-1}\xi l(w)

\displaystyle=l(l^{-1}(u)\dashv l^{-1}(v))\dashv l^{-1}\xi l(w)

\displaystyle=l(\gamma l^{-1}(u)\dashv(l^{-1}(v)\vdash l^{-1}(w)))

\displaystyle=l(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(l\otimes l)\gamma l^{-1}(u)\dashv(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1}(v\vdash w))

\displaystyle=l(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(l\gamma l^{-1}(u)\dashv(l(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(v\vdash w)))

\displaystyle=l\gamma l^{-1}(u)\dashv^{\prime}(v\vdash^{\prime}w)=l\gamma l^{-1}(u)\dashv^{\prime}(v\bot^{\prime}w).

Thus,
(S,\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime},\bot^{\prime},l\gamma l^{-1},l\xi l^{-1})
becomes a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra.

Moreover, for
\gamma

\displaystyle l\gamma l^{-1}(u\dashv^{\prime}v)

\displaystyle=l\gamma l^{-1}l\dashv(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(u,v)

\displaystyle=l\gamma\dashv(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(u,v)

\displaystyle=l(\gamma l^{-1}(u)\dashv\gamma l^{-1}(v))

\displaystyle=l((l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(l\otimes l)\gamma l^{-1}(u)\dashv\gamma l^{-1}(v))=(l\gamma l^{-1}(u)\dashv^{\prime}l\gamma l^{-1}(v))

and for
\xi

\displaystyle l\xi l^{-1}(u\dashv^{\prime}v)

\displaystyle=l\xi l^{-1}l\dashv(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(u,v)

\displaystyle=l\xi\dashv(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(u,v)

\displaystyle=l(\xi l^{-1}(u)\dashv\xi l^{-1}(v))

\displaystyle=l((l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})(l\otimes l)\xi l^{-1}(u)\dashv\xi l^{-1}(v))=(l\xi l^{-1}(u)\dashv^{\prime}l\xi l^{-1}(v)).

Therefore,
l:(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)\rightarrow(S,\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime},\bot^{\prime},l\gamma l^{-1},l\xi l^{-1})
is a
BiHom-associative supertrialgebra morphism, as

l\circ\dashv=l\circ\dashv\circ(l^{-1}\otimes l^{-1})\circ(l\otimes l)=l^{\prime}\circ(l\otimes l)

and

(l\gamma l^{-1})\circ l=l\circ\gamma,\quad(l\xi l^{-1})\circ l=l\circ\xi.

It is obvious to see that

\left\{l(u_{i}),\dots,l(u_{n})\right\}
is a basis of
S
. For each
i,j=1,\dots,n
, we have

\displaystyle(l(u_{i})\dashv l(u_{j}))

\displaystyle=l(l^{-1}(u_{i})\dashv l^{-1}(u_{j}))=l(u_{i}\dashv u_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta^{k}_{ij}l(u_{k}).

∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)
be a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra and
(S,\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime},\bot^{\prime},l\gamma l^{-1},l\xi l^{-1})
be as described in Proposition
2.4
. If
\phi
is an automorphism of
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)
, then

l\phi l^{-1}
is an automorphism of
(S,\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime},\bot^{\prime},l\gamma l^{-1},l\xi l^{-1})
.

Proof.

By taking
\alpha=l\gamma l^{-1}
, we have

l\phi l^{-1}\alpha=l\phi l^{-1}l\gamma l^{-1}=l\phi\gamma l^{-1}=l\gamma\phi l^{-1}=l\gamma l^{-1}l\phi l^{-1}=\gamma l\phi l^{-1}.

For
\xi
, we take
\sigma=l\xi l^{-1}
. Consequently, we get

l\phi l^{-1}\sigma=l\phi l^{-1}l\xi l^{-1}=l\phi\xi l^{-1}=l\xi\phi l^{-1}=l\xi l^{-1}l\phi l^{-1}=\sigma l\phi l^{-1}.

For every
p,v\in S,

\displaystyle l\phi l^{-1}(l(p)\dashv^{\prime}l(v))

\displaystyle=l\phi l^{-1}l(p\dashv v)=l\phi(p\dashv v)=l(\phi(p)\dashv\phi(v))

\displaystyle=(l\phi(p)\dashv^{\prime}l\phi(v))=(l\phi l^{-1}(l(p)\dashv^{\prime}\phi l\phi^{-1}(\phi(v))).

By Definition
2.3
,
l\phi l^{-1}
becomes an automorphism of
(S,\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime},\bot^{\prime},\gamma l^{-1},l\xi l^{-1})
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Given two
BiHom-associative supertrialgebras
(\mathcal{N},\dashv_{\mathcal{N}},\vdash_{\mathcal{N}},\bot_{\mathcal{N}},\gamma_{\mathcal{N}},\xi_{\mathcal{N}})
and

(\mathcal{M},\dashv_{\mathcal{M}},\vdash_{\mathcal{M}},\bot_{\mathcal{M}},\gamma_{\mathcal{M}},\xi_{\mathcal{M}})
. Then, a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra structure becomes
on
\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathcal{M}
with the bilinear mappings
\triangleleft,\triangleright,\ast:(\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathcal{M})^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathcal{M}

defined by

(n_{1}+m_{1})\dashv(n_{2}+m_{2}):=n_{1}\dashv_{\mathcal{N}}n_{2}+m_{2}\dashv_{\mathcal{M}}m_{2},

(n_{1}+m_{1})\vdash(n_{2}+m_{2}):=n_{1}\vdash_{\mathcal{N}}n_{2}+m_{1}\vdash_{\mathcal{M}}m_{2},

(n_{1}+m_{1})\bot(n_{2}+m_{2}):=n_{1}\bot_{\mathcal{N}}n_{2}+m_{1}\bot_{\mathcal{M}}m_{2}

and the linear
mappings
\alpha=\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}+\alpha_{\mathcal{M}},\,\beta=\beta_{\mathcal{N}}+\beta_{\mathcal{M}}:\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathcal{M}
given by

(\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}+\alpha_{\mathcal{M}})(x+y):=\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}(x)+\alpha_{\mathcal{M}}(y),\,(\beta_{\mathcal{N}}+\beta_{\mathcal{M}})(x+y):=\beta_{\mathcal{N}}(x)+\beta_{\mathcal{M}}(y),\,\forall(x,y)\in\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{M}.

Furthermore, if
\xi:\mathcal{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is a linear mapping,
then,

\xi:(\mathcal{N},\dashv_{\mathcal{N}},\vdash_{\mathcal{N}},\bot_{\mathcal{N}},\alpha_{\mathcal{N}},\beta_{\mathcal{N}})\rightarrow(\mathcal{M},\dashv_{\mathcal{M}},\vdash_{\mathcal{M}},\bot_{\mathcal{M}},\alpha_{\mathcal{M}},\beta_{\mathcal{M}})

is a morphism iff its graph
\Gamma_{\xi}=\{(x,\xi(x)),x\in\mathcal{N}\}
is a BiHom-subalgebra of
(\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathcal{M},\triangleleft,\triangleright,\ast,\alpha,\beta)
.

Proof.

First we
suppose that

\xi:(\mathcal{N},\dashv_{\mathcal{N}},\vdash_{\mathcal{N}},\bot_{\mathcal{N}},\alpha_{\mathcal{N}},\beta_{\mathcal{N}})\rightarrow(\mathcal{M},\dashv_{\mathcal{M}},\vdash_{\mathcal{M}},\bot_{\mathcal{M}},\alpha_{\mathcal{M}},\beta_{\mathcal{M}})

is a morphism.
As a result,

(n+\xi(n))\dashv(m+\xi(m))=(n\dashv_{\mathcal{N}}m+\xi(n)\dashv_{\mathcal{M}}\xi(m))=n\dashv_{\mathcal{N}}m+\xi(n\dashv_{\mathcal{N}}m),

(n+\xi(n))\vdash(m+\xi(m))=(n\vdash_{\mathcal{N}}m+\xi(n)\vdash_{\mathcal{M}}\xi(m))=n\vdash_{\mathcal{N}}m+\xi(n\vdash_{\mathcal{N}}m),

(n+\xi(n))\bot(m+\xi(m))=(n\bot_{\mathcal{N}}m+\xi(n)\bot_{\mathcal{M}}\xi(m))=n\bot_{\mathcal{N}}m+\xi(n\bot_{\mathcal{N}}m).

Moreover, as
\xi\circ\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}=\alpha_{\mathcal{M}}\circ\xi,
and
\xi\circ\beta_{\mathcal{N}}=\beta_{\mathcal{M}}\circ\xi,
we have

(\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}\oplus\alpha_{\mathcal{M}})(n,\xi(n))=(\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}(n),\alpha_{\mathcal{M}}\circ\xi(n))=(\alpha_{\mathcal{M}}(n),\xi\circ\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}(n))

and

(\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\oplus\beta_{\mathcal{M}})(n,\xi(n))=(\beta_{\mathcal{N}}(n),\beta_{\mathcal{M}}\circ\xi(n))=(\beta_{\mathcal{N}}(n),\xi\circ\beta_{\mathcal{N}}(n)).

This implies that
\Gamma_{\xi}
is closed
\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}\oplus\alpha_{\mathcal{M}}
and
\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\oplus\beta_{\mathcal{M}}.

Therefore,
\Gamma_{\xi}
is a subalgebra of
(\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathcal{M},\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\alpha,\beta).

Now, let the graph
\Gamma_{\xi}\subset\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathcal{M}
be a subalgebra of

(\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathcal{M},\dashv,\vdash,\alpha,\beta)
, then we get

(n+\xi(n))\dashv(m+\xi(m))=(n\dashv_{\mathcal{N}}m+\xi(n)\dashv_{\mathcal{M}}\xi(m))\in\Gamma_{\xi},

(n+\xi(n))\vdash(m+\xi(m))=(n\vdash_{\mathcal{N}}m+\xi(n)\vdash_{\mathcal{M}}\xi(m))\in\Gamma_{\xi},

(n+\xi(n))\bot(m+\xi(m))=(n\bot_{\mathcal{N}}m+\xi(n)\bot_{\mathcal{M}}\xi(m))\in\Gamma_{\xi}.

Furthermore,
(\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}\oplus\alpha_{\mathcal{M}})(\Gamma_{\xi})\subset\Gamma_{\xi}
and

(\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\oplus\beta_{\mathcal{M}})(\Gamma_{\xi})\subset\Gamma_{\xi}
show that

(\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}\oplus\alpha_{\mathcal{M}})(n,\xi(n))=(\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}(n),\alpha_{\mathcal{M}}\circ\xi(n))\in\Gamma_{\xi},\,(\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\oplus\beta_{\mathcal{M}})(n,\xi(n))=(\beta_{\mathcal{N}}(n),\beta_{\mathcal{M}}\circ\xi(n))\in\Gamma_{\xi}.

This is equivalent to the condition
\alpha_{\mathcal{M}}\circ\xi(n)=\xi\circ\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}(k),
i.e.,
\alpha_{\mathcal{M}}\circ\xi=\xi\circ\alpha_{\mathcal{N}}.

Similarly,
\beta_{\mathcal{M}}\circ\xi=\xi\circ\beta_{\mathcal{N}}
. Hence,
\xi
is a morphism of BiHom-associative trialgebras.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

Given a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)
with a linear mapping
\lambda:S\longrightarrow S
with

\gamma\circ\lambda=\lambda\circ\gamma
,
\xi\circ\lambda=\lambda\circ\xi

\displaystyle\lambda(d)\vdash\lambda(v)

\displaystyle\lambda(d)\dashv\lambda\lambda(v)

\displaystyle=\lambda(\lambda(d)\vdash v+d\vdash\lambda(v)+c(d\vdash v)),

\displaystyle\lambda(d)\bot\lambda(v)

\displaystyle=\lambda(\lambda(d)\bot v+d\bot\lambda(v)+c(d\bot v)),

with

,

, we say

Proposition 2.8
.

Given a Rota-Baxter BiHom-associative supertrialgebra
(S,\ast,\gamma,\xi,\nu)
and the new operations
\dashv,\vdash
and
\bot
on
S
are described by

d\dashv v=d\ast\lambda(v),\,d\vdash v=\lambda(d)\ast v
and
d\bot v=cd\ast v
. Then,
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)
is a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra.

Proof.

Here, one axiom is only proved and the other axioms are showed in similar way. Indeed, for any
p,v,r\in S,

\displaystyle(d\dashv v)\vdash\xi(r)

\displaystyle=(d\ast\lambda(v))\vdash\xi(r)

\displaystyle=\lambda(d\ast\lambda(v))\ast\xi(r)

\displaystyle=\gamma\lambda(d)\ast(\lambda(v)\ast r)

\displaystyle=\gamma\lambda(d)\ast(v\vdash r)

\displaystyle=\gamma((d)\vdash(v\vdash r)=(d\bot v)\vdash\xi(r).

This ends the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)
be a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra such that
\gamma^{2}=\xi^{2}\gamma\circ\xi=\xi\circ\gamma=id.

Then,
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)\cong(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\xi,\gamma).

Proof.

Here, the proof of only one axiom will be done. For any
d,v,r\in S,

\displaystyle\gamma(d)\dashv(v\vdash r)

\displaystyle=\gamma(d)\dashv(v\bot r)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\gamma(\gamma\xi(d))\dashv(v\vdash r)=\gamma(\gamma\xi(d))\dashv(v\bot r)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\gamma^{2}\xi(d)\dashv(v\vdash r)=\gamma^{2}\xi(d)\dashv(v\bot r)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\xi(d)\dashv(v\vdash r)=\xi(d)\dashv(v\bot r).

Hence,
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)\cong(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\xi,\gamma).

∎

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)
be a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra. Then,
(S,\dashv,\bot,\ast,\gamma,\xi)
is a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra
where
d\ast v=d\vdash v+d\bot v
for any
d,v,r\in S.

Proof.

One axiom only will be proved. For any
d,v,r\in S,

\displaystyle(d\ast v)\dashv\xi(r)

\displaystyle=(d\vdash v+d\bot v)\dashv\xi(r)

\displaystyle=(d\vdash v)\dashv\xi(r)+(d\bot v)\dashv\xi(r)

\displaystyle=\gamma(d)\vdash(v\dashv r)+\gamma(d)\bot(v\dashv r)=\gamma(d)\ast(v\dashv r).

This ends the proof.
∎

2.2
, for each

\displaystyle\left[d\ast r,\beta(v)\right]+\left[\alpha(d),v\ast r\right]

Proposition 2.12
.

Given a BiHom-associative supertrialgebra
(S,\dashv,\vdash,\bot,\gamma,\xi)
and
d\ast v=d\vdash v+d\dashv v+d\bot v
for any
d,v\in S
. Then,
(S,\ast,\gamma,\xi)
is a BiHom-associative superalgebra.

Proof.

For any
d,v,r\in S,

\displaystyle(d\ast v)\ast\xi(r)

\displaystyle-\gamma(d)\ast(v\ast r)

\displaystyle=(d\vdash v+d\dashv v+d\bot v)\ast\xi(r)-\gamma(d)\ast(v\vdash r+v\dashv r+v\bot w)

\displaystyle=(d\vdash v)\ast\xi(r)+(d\dashv v)\ast\xi(r)+(d\bot v)\ast\xi(r)-\gamma(d)\ast(v\vdash r)-\gamma(d)\ast(v\dashv r)-\gamma(d)\ast(v\bot r)

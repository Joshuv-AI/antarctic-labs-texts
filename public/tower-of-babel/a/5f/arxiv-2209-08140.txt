Notation and Preliminaries

We first recall some definitions from general topology. A Polish space
X
, is a topological space that is separable and where the topology is metrisable with a complete metric. In this paper we will always use
X
for a fixed Polish space. No extra assumptions will be put on
X
. There are no local compactness assumptions as we believe that the most interesting applications are when
X
is for instance an infinite dimensional Banach space. The space of real valued bounded continuous functions defined on
X
is denoted by
C_{b}(X)
, the space of all continuous functions is denoted
C(X)
. The aim of the paper is to find a representation theorem for convex, monotonic functions defined on
C_{b}(X)
. We say that a function
F\colon C_{b}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is nondecreasing if for elements in
C_{b}(X)
,
f\leq g
we always have
F(f)\leq F(g)
. Such an assumption immediately shows that
F
is continuous for the sup norm on
C_{b}(X)
. Indeed if
\|f-g\|\leq\epsilon
(where
\epsilon>0
and
\|.\|
denotes the sup norm), then monotonicity implies

\displaystyle F(f-\epsilon)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F(g)\leq F(f+\epsilon)

\displaystyle F(f-\epsilon)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F(f)\leq F(f+\epsilon)

hence

\displaystyle|F(f)-F(g)|\leq F(f+\epsilon)-F(f-\epsilon)

Since the function
{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}};a\rightarrow F(f+a)
is convex, it is continuous, so we find
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}F(f+\epsilon)-F(f-\epsilon)=0
.

Definition 1
.

We will say that a nondecreasing function

is upward continuous if

Cheridito, Kupper and Tangpi,

from which it follows that downward continuity implies upward continuity. In this paper

will always be a real valued nondecreasing upward continuous convex function defined on the whole space

. To simplify the statements, we will also assume that

. This is no restriction as we can always replace

by a the new function

. It avoids that the value taken at

3.
The Representation Theorem

The formula proved using elements
\mu\in M_{+}(K)
, cannot be used to represent all functions of
C_{b}(X)
. There are two shortcomings. The first is that it is only valid for elements of the strictly smaller space
C(K)
, second it uses nonnegative measures that might charge the set
K\setminus X
. The proof of the main result addresses these two shortcomings.

Theorem 1
.

For a nondecreasing upward continuous convex function

F\colon C_{b}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\quad F(0)=0,

the conjugate function, defined on
M_{+}(X)
, satisfies

\displaystyle F^{*}

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle M_{+}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\}

\displaystyle F^{*}(\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{\mu(f)-F(f)\mid f\in C(K)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{\mu(f)-F(f)\mid f\in C_{b}(X)\},

and we have for all
f\in C_{b}(X)
:

F(f)=\sup\{\mu(f)-F^{*}(\mu)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(X)\}.

Proof
The approximation Lemma,
2
shows that the calculation of
F^{*}
can be done using all elements of
C_{b}(X)
and not just the elements of
C(K)
. In what follows we first work with the conjugate function defined on
M_{+}(K)
. We now show that the convex set

E=\{(\mu,t)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(X),+\infty>t\geq F^{*}(\mu)\}

is weak
∗
dense in the epigraph

Ep=\{(\mu,t)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(K),F^{*}(\mu)\leq t<+\infty\}.

For each
\epsilon>0
and each

we use the convex sets

W_{n,\epsilon}=\{(\mu,t)\mid(\mu,t)\in Ep,\mu(L_{n})<\epsilon\},

where

refer to Lemma

. The definition of the weak
∗

is an open set in

. We will show that it is weak
∗

Ep
. Suppose on the contrary that there is an element
(\mu_{0},t_{0})\in Ep\setminus\overline{W_{n,\epsilon}}
. According to the Hahn Banach theorem we can find an element

such that

\mu_{0}(f)+\alpha t_{0}<\inf\{\mu(f)+\alpha t\mid(\mu,t)\in W_{n,\epsilon}\}.

In case
W_{n,\epsilon}
is empty the right side is

and we can take
(f,\alpha)=(1,1)
. In case the set is nonempty, the number
\alpha\geq 0
since otherwise the right side would
-\infty
. The inequality then changes into

\mu_{0}(f)+\alpha F^{*}(\mu_{0})<\inf\{\mu(f)+\alpha F^{*}(\mu)\mid F^{*}(\mu)<+\infty,\mu(L_{n})<\epsilon\}.

Since
0\leq F^{*}(\mu_{0})<\infty
we can increase
\alpha
a little bit and still have the same inequality. In other words we may suppose that
\alpha>0
. Then we can divide by
\alpha

\mu_{0}(g)+F^{*}(\mu_{0})<\inf\{\mu(g)+F^{*}(\mu)\mid F^{*}(\mu)<+\infty,\mu(L_{n})<\epsilon\}.

Changing signs we get numbers
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}},\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(-g)-F^{*}(\mu_{0})>\gamma+\delta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\gamma=\sup\{\mu(-g)-F^{*}(\mu)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(K),\mu(L_{n})<\epsilon\}.

Let us now fix a number

k\epsilon>\delta
. Looking at functions

we get for
\mu(L_{n})\geq\epsilon

\mu(-g-k\phi_{n}^{m})-F^{*}(\mu)=\mu(-g)-F^{*}(\mu)-k\mu(\phi_{n}^{m})\leq F(-g)-\delta
whereas for
\mu(L_{n})<\epsilon
we have

\displaystyle\mu(-g-k\phi_{n}^{m})-F^{*}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu(-g)-F^{*}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\leq\mu_{0}(-g)-F^{*}(\mu_{0})-\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F(-g)-\delta.

Summarizing we get for all
\mu
that
\mu(-g-k\phi_{n}^{m})-F^{*}(\mu)\leq F(-g)-\delta
. Since
-g-k\phi_{n}^{m}\in C(K)
, we get that
F(-g-k\phi_{n}^{m})\leq F(-g)-\delta
. By passing to

and using upward continuity

, a contradiction. This shows the density of

. Since

is a Polish space the countable intersection of dense

sets is still dense and so we find that

we have

F(f)=\sup\{\mu(f)-F^{*}(\mu)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(X)\}.

The approximation Lemma
2

F(f)=\sup\{\mu(f)-F^{*}(\mu)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(X)\}.

∎

\mu_{n}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
, a sequence
(h_{n})_{n}\in C_{b}(X)
with
h_{n}\downarrow 0
such that
\mu_{n}(h_{n})\geq\delta
. Take now
k
such that
k\delta-m\geq 1
. Then
k\,h_{n}\downarrow 0
but

F(k\,h_{n})\geq\mu_{n}(k\,h_{n})-F^{*}(\mu_{n})\geq k\delta-m\geq 1,

A contradiction to the downward continuity. Suppose now conversely that
{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
is weak
∗
compact. Take a sequence
h_{n}\downarrow 0
in
C_{b}(X)
. Take a sequence
1\geq\epsilon_{n}>0
such that
\epsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0
. For each
n
take
\mu_{n}
such that
F(h_{n})\leq\mu_{n}(h_{n})-F^{*}(\mu_{n})+\epsilon_{n}
. We already know by Proposition
4
that for all
n
,
F^{*}(\mu_{n})\leq\psi(\|h_{1}\|)
and hence the sequence
\mu_{n}
is uniformly tight. We then have
\mu_{n}(h_{n})\rightarrow 0
. This shows that
F(h_{n})\rightarrow 0
(and at the same time
F^{*}(\mu_{n})\rightarrow 0
).∎

Theorem 3
.

If
F
is a nondecreasing convex function
F\colon C_{b}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
with
F(0)=0
, if moreover
F
is downward continuous then for each
f
there is a
\mu\in M_{+}(X)
such that
\mu(f)=F(f)+F^{*}(\mu)
. Consequently the epigraph of
F
has a supporting functional at each point
(f,F(f))
.

Proof
Take
f\in C_{b}(X)
. For each
\epsilon>0
, there is an
\epsilon-
supporting functional, given by a measure
\mu_{\epsilon}
. But from the previous theorem we know that this family of measures is uniformly tight since we have the bound
\sup_{1\geq\epsilon>0}F^{*}(\mu_{\epsilon})\leq\psi(\|f\|)<\infty
. Hence there is a sequence
\epsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0
such that the sequence
\mu_{\epsilon_{n}}
converges weak
∗
to, say, a measure
\mu
. Clearly the inequalities

F(f+g)\geq\mu_{\epsilon_{n}}(g)+F(f)-\epsilon_{n},

imply that
F(f+g)\geq\mu(g)+F(f)
, valid for all
g\in C_{b}(X)
. This is equivalent to
\mu(f)=F(f)+F^{*}(\mu)
.∎

We will now prove the converse of the previous theorem. The proof uses a result which is called the sup-limsup theorem. The version we need was proved by Galán and Simons,
[
3
]
. Such results are used to prove the James’s weak compactness theorem and variants of it. Because we are dealing with the special case of measures and continuous functions, we do not need to adapt the technique of the “undetermined” functions, see
[
3
]
for more information and other examples of the use of this technique. Let us first recall Theorem 7 of Galán and Simons.

Theorem 4
.

Let
E
be an arbitrary set and let
G\subset E
be a nonempty subset. Let
(f_{k})_{k}
be a uniformly bounded sequence of functions
f_{k}\colon E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
. By
\operatorname{conv}_{\sigma}(f_{k};k\geq 1)
we mean the set:

\operatorname{conv}_{\sigma}(f_{k};k\geq 1)=\left\{\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}f_{k}\mid\lambda_{k}\geq 0,\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}=1\right\}.

Let
G
satisfy the following peak condition:

\forall f\in\operatorname{conv}_{\sigma}(f_{k};k\geq 1)\,\,\exists x\in G\text{ such that }f(x)=\sup_{y\in E}f(y).

Then the function
\limsup_{k}f_{k}
satisfies:

\sup\{\limsup_{k}f_{k}(x);x\in G\}=\sup\{\limsup_{k}f_{k}(x);x\in E\}.

Theorem 5
.

Let
F\colon C_{b}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a nondecreasing convex function,
F(0)=0
, that satisfies the upward continuity condition. Suppose that for each
f\in C_{b}(X)
there is
\mu\in M_{+}(X)
such that
F(f)=\mu(f)-F^{*}(\mu)
. Then
F
satisfies the downward continuity condition or equivalent to it, by Theorem
2
, for each
m\geq 0
, the set
{\mathcal{S}}_{m}=\{\mu\mid F^{*}(\mu)\leq m\}
is weak
∗
compact.

Proof
Since
F
satisfies the upward continuity condition we already know that

F(f)=\sup\{\mu(f)-F^{*}(\mu)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(X)\}.

The hypothesis says that this supremum is a maximum. As before we will use a compact metric space
K
in which
X
is dense. We have already seen that when
f\in C(K)
:

\sup\{\mu(f)-F^{*}(\mu)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(X)\}=\sup\{\mu(f)-F^{*}(\mu)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(K)\}.

We will now suppose that
F
does not satisfy the downward continuity condition and we hope that this leads to a contradiction. So we suppose that there is a sequence of functions
h_{k}\in C(K)
and a
\delta>0
such that
h_{k}\downarrow 0
on
X
but
F(h_{k})\geq\delta
for each
k
. To keep the notation simple we use the same notation
h_{k}
for the extension of
h_{k}
to the compact space
K
. Also on
K
we have that
h_{k}
is a nonincreasing sequence but
g=\lim_{k}h_{k}
is not necessarily
0
on
K\setminus X
! Take now
m\geq\psi(\|h_{1}\|)
. To apply the sup-limsup theorem we use the following “translation”:

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mu\in M_{+}(K)\mid F^{*}(\mu)\leq m\}

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mu\in M_{+}(X)\mid F^{*}(\mu)\leq m\}={\mathcal{S}}_{m}

\displaystyle f_{k}

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\text{ is defined as }f_{k}(\mu)=\mu(h_{k})-F^{*}(\mu).

\displaystyle\tilde{g}

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\text{ is defined as }\tilde{g}(\mu)=\lim_{k}f_{k}(\mu).

The sequence
f_{k}
is uniformly bounded since – by the same argument as in Proposition
4
–
E
is a norm bounded set and for all
k
,
\|h_{k}\|\leq\|h_{1}\|
. Also
\operatorname{conv}_{\sigma}\{f_{k}\mid k\geq 1\}\subset C(K)
. It is easily seen that the hypothesis says that
G
satisfies the peak condition.
By the monotone convergence theorem we have
\tilde{g}(\mu)=\mu(g)-F^{*}(\mu)
for each
\mu\in E
. For each
k
the set
L_{k}\subset E
with
L_{k}=\{\mu\in E\mid f_{k}(\mu)\geq\delta\}
is convex and compact since
f_{k}
is concave and upper semi continuous for the weak
∗
topology. Furthermore each
L_{k}
is nonempty since
F(h_{k})\geq\delta
. The set
L=\cap_{k}L_{k}
is nonempty since the sequence
(L_{k})_{k}
is also nonincreasing. This means that there is
\nu\in E
with
f_{k}(\nu)\geq\delta
for each
k
. The monotone convergence theorem again says that
\nu(g)-F^{*}(\nu)\geq\delta>0
. This shows that

\sup\{\limsup_{k}f_{k}(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in E\}\geq\delta>0.

When we restrict to
X
the situation is easier. On
G
we have that
\mu(g)-F^{*}(\mu)\leq 0
and hence

\sup\{\limsup_{k}f_{k}(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in G\}\leq 0.

This is a contradiction to the sup-limsup theorem. Until now we only showed that downward continuity holds for sequences in
C(K)
. We will now use the equivalence with weak
∗
compactness as in Theorem
2
and show that for each
m
the set
{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
is uniformly tight or what is the same, weak
∗
compact. We will actually show that

\{\mu\in M_{+}(K)\mid F^{*}(\mu)\leq m\}=\{\mu\in M_{+}(X)\mid F^{*}(\mu)\leq m\}.

Suppose on the contrary that

\mu_{0}\in\{\mu\in M_{+}(K)\mid F^{*}(\mu)\leq m\}\setminus\{\mu\in M_{+}(X)\mid F^{*}(\mu)\leq m\}.

Then there is a compact set
L\subset K\setminus X
with
\mu_{0}(L)>0
. Let now
h\in C(K)
be such that
h=1
on
L
and
0\leq h<1
on
K\setminus L
. Take now
k\geq 1
so that
k\mu_{0}(L)-m\geq 1
. The sequence
k\,h^{n}
converges downward to
0
on
X
. According to the first part of the proof we have
F(k\,h^{n})\rightarrow 0
. But this is in contradiction with
F(k\,h^{n})\geq\mu_{0}(k\,h^{n})-F^{*}(\mu_{0})\geq k\mu_{0}(L)-m\geq 1
.∎

Remark 4
.

If
F
is nondecreasing and satisfies the downward continuity property then for measures
\mu\in M(K)\setminus M(X)
we have
F^{*}(\mu)=+\infty
.

Remark 5
.

It is easily seen that the downward continuity property cannot be extended to a continuity for uniformly bounded pointwise converging sequences, the so called Lebesgue property. We just take a compact metric space
K
and define
F(f)=\max_{x\in K}f(x)
which can be written as
F(f)=\sup\{\mu(f)\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(K)\}
, i.e. the sup over all probabilities on
K
. Without giving details we mention that a necessary and sufficient condition for this Lebesgue property is that for each
m
, the set
{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
is weakly compact, meaning it is compact for the topology
\sigma(M(X),M^{*}(X))
.

5.
The case of all continuous functions

The result in this section is a generalisation of the result for coherent utility functions,
[
2
]
. It follows the same lines but the use of the monetary property will be replaced by another argument. Also the theorem is formulated for convex functions and not just for positively homogeneous ones. For completeness we give the adapted proof. The symbol
M^{c}(X)
stands for measures on
X
having a compact support, this is also the dual space of the space
C(X)
with the usual topology of uniform convergence on compact sets. We also observe that for
\mu\in M^{c}(X)
:

\sup\{\mu(f)-F(f)\mid f\in C_{b}(X)\}=\sup\{\mu(f)-F(f)\mid f\in C(X)\}=F^{*}(\mu).

Theorem 6
.

Let
F\colon C(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a nondecreasing convex function with
F(0)=0
. Then

(1)

F
satisfies an upward continuity property, i.e. for a sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
in
C(X)
such that
f_{n}\uparrow f\in C(X)
we have
F(f_{n})\uparrow F(f)
.

(2)

There is a compact set
L\subset X
such that the function
F
factorises over the restriction map
C(X)\rightarrow C(L)
. In other words the conjugate function

\displaystyle F^{*}

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle M^{c}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\}

\displaystyle\mu\rightarrow F^{*}(\mu)=\sup\{\mu(f)-F(f)\mid f\in C(X)\}

can only take finite values on
M_{+}(L)
and

F(f)=\sup\{\mu(f)-F^{*}(\mu)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(L)\}.

(3)

The function
F
satisfies the downward continuity property since it factorises over the restriction map
C(X)\rightarrow C(L)
.

Proof
Let a sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
in
C(X)
be such that
f_{n}\uparrow f\in C(X)
and
F(f)\geq 2\epsilon+F(f_{n})
where
\epsilon>0
. Since the function
{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}};a\rightarrow F(f+a)
is convex, it is continuous. Therefore there is
\delta>0
such that for
|a|\leq\delta
,
|F(f+a)-F(f)|\leq\epsilon
. We replace the given sequence by
g=(f-\delta),g_{n}=\min(f-\delta,f_{n})
. We still have
F(g)\geq F(g_{n})+\epsilon
. But this time
g_{n}\uparrow g
in a stationary way, i.e. for each
x\in X
there is
n_{0}
such that for
n\geq n_{0}
,
g_{n}(x)=g(x)
. The sets
G_{n}=\{x\mid g_{n}(x)<g(x)\}
form a decreasing sequence of open sets and their closures satisfy
\overline{G_{n}}\subset\{x\mid f_{n}(x)\leq f(x)-\delta\}
. Their intersection is empty:
\cap_{n}\overline{G_{n}}=\emptyset
. We now define
h_{n}=g+n(g-g_{n})=(n+1)g-n\,g_{n}
and observe that convexity implies

F(g_{n})+\epsilon\leq F(g)\leq\frac{n}{n+1}F(g_{n})+\frac{1}{n+1}F(h_{n}).

This simplifies to
F(h_{n})\geq(n+1)\epsilon-F(g_{n})\geq(n+1)\epsilon-F(g_{1})
. We now remark that
h_{n}\rightarrow g
and that
h=\max(h_{n};n\geq 1)
is a real valued function. We now show that
h
is continuous. To do this, take a sequence
x_{m}\rightarrow x
forming the compact set
C=\{x_{m};m\geq 1\}\cup\{x\}
. The intersection
C\cap\cap_{n}\overline{G_{n}}=\emptyset
and compactness implies that there is
n_{0}
such that
\overline{G_{n_{0}}}\cap C=\emptyset
. This means that for all
x_{m}
and for
x
we have

\displaystyle h(x_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max(h_{n}(x_{m});n<n_{0})

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max(h_{n}(x);n<n_{0}).

Since the maximum is taken over a finite set we obtain convergence
h(x_{m})\rightarrow h(x)
. Obviously
F(h)\geq F(h_{n})\geq(n+1)\epsilon-F(g_{1})
which tends to infinity with
n
, violating that
F(h)
must be finite. This shows the first part of the theorem. For the second part we start by defining the conjugate only using bounded functions. So for the moment we put

F^{*}(\mu)=\sup\{\mu(f)-F(f)\mid f\in C_{b}(X)\}.

We already know that
F^{*}
can only take finite values for nonnegative measures. Because of upward continuity we may recall from the previous section, that for
f\in C_{b}(X)
:

F(f)=\sup\{\mu(f)-F^{*}(\mu)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(X),F^{*}(\mu)<\infty\}.

The upward continuity and the monotone convergence theorem from measure theory then show that for elements
f\in C(X)
that are bounded below, we get the same formula:
F(f)=\sup\{\mu(f)-F^{*}(\mu)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(X),F^{*}(\mu)<\infty\}.
The next step is to show that everything happens on a compact subset of
X
. For
\mu\in M_{+}(X)
, let
S_{\mu}
be the support of the measure
\mu
. We claim that
\cup_{F^{*}(\mu)<\infty}S_{\mu}
is a relatively compact set. If not there would exist a sequence of measures
\mu_{n}
with
F^{*}(\mu_{n})<\infty
as well as a sequence of different points
x_{n}\in S_{\mu_{n}}
together with open balls
B(x_{n},\eta_{n})
such that
\eta_{n}\rightarrow 0
and such that for each subset
J\subset{\mathbb{N}}
,
\cup_{j\in J}\overline{B(x_{j},\eta_{j})}
is closed and the closures
\overline{B(x_{n},\eta_{n})}
are disjoint. For each
n
we define
\psi_{n}(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,X\setminus B(x_{n},\eta_{n}))
. Observe that
\psi_{n}(x_{n})>0
. Because
x_{n}\in S_{\mu_{n}}
we have that
\mu_{n}(\psi_{n})>0
. Consequently there are numbers
a_{n}
such that
a_{n}\mu_{n}(\psi_{n})-F^{*}(\mu_{n})\geq n
. The function
h=\sum_{n}a_{n}\psi_{n}=\max(a_{n}\psi_{n};n\geq 1)
is still continuous (because of the choice of the numbers
\eta_{n}
). Now we have for each
n
:

F(h)\geq F(a_{n}\psi_{n})\geq a_{n}\mu_{n}(\psi_{n})-F^{*}(\mu_{n})\geq n

This is a contradiction to the finiteness of the function
F
, showing that all the measures
\mu
with
F^{*}(\mu)<\infty
are supported by the same compact set, say
L
. This changes the dual representation of
F
– at least for functions bounded below – into

F(f)=\sup\{\mu(f\,{\rm\bf 1}_{L})-F^{*}(\mu)\mid F^{*}(\mu)<\infty\}.

We must still show that the same expression holds for functions not necessarily bounded below. Take an arbitrary function
f\in C(X)
and for
m\leq\inf_{x\in L}f(x)=m_{0}
, observe that all the expressions
F(f\vee m)
are the same, say
\alpha
. Suppose now that
F(f)<\alpha-\delta
where
\delta>0
. The function
f^{\prime}=2(f\vee m_{0})-f=f\vee m_{0}+(f\vee m_{0}-f)
is bounded below and on
L
it equals
f
. Convexity then gives

\alpha=F(f\vee m_{0})\leq{1\over 2}F(f^{\prime})+{1\over 2}F(f)\leq{1\over 2}(\alpha+\alpha-\delta)=\alpha-\frac{\delta}{2},

a contradiction. Hence for all
f
we get:

F(f)=\sup\{\mu(f\,{\rm\bf 1}_{L})-F^{*}(\mu)\mid\mu\in M_{+}(L)\},

and
F
factorises over the restriction
C(X)\rightarrow C(L)
. The last statement in the theorem is now straightforward. ∎

Limit Law for the Maximum Interpoint Distance of High Dimensional Dependent Variables
Guowei Yan, Long Feng

Abstract
In this paper, we considier the limiting distribution of the maximum interpoint Euclidean distance M n = max 1 ≤ i < j ≤ n ⁡ ‖ 𝑿 i − 𝑿 j ‖ M_{n}=\max_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left\|\bm{X}_{i}-\bm{X}_{j}\right\| , where 𝑿 1 , 𝑿 2 , … , 𝑿 n \bm{X}_{1},\bm{X}_{2},\ldots,\bm{X}_{n} be a random sample coming from a p p -dimensional population with dependent sub-gaussian components. When the dimension tends to infinity with the sample size, we proves that M n 2 M_{n}^{2} under a suitable normalization asymptotically obeys a Gumbel type distribution. The proofs mainly depend on the Stein-Chen Poisson approximation method and high dimensional Gaussian approximation.



Keywords:
Maximum interpoint distance, Gaussian apprximation, Gumbel distribution, Stein–Chen Poisson approximation.

1
Introduction

Let
\bm{X}_{1},\dots,\bm{X}_{n}
be identically distributed copies of
p
-dimensional random vector
\bm{X}
with mean
\bm{\mu}
and covariance matrix
\mathbf{R}
. Write
\bm{X}_{i}=\left(x_{i,1},x_{i,2},\dots,x_{i,p}\right)^{T}
for each
i=1,2,\dots,n
, and
\bm{X}^{\left(j\right)}=\left(x_{1,j},x_{2,j},\dots,x_{n,j}\right)^{T}
for each
j=1,2,\dots,p
. In this paper, we are concerned the asymptotic distribution of the maximum interpoint distance

M_{n}=\max\left\{\left|\bm{X}_{i}-\bm{X}_{j}\right|;1\leq i<j\leq n\right\},

(1)

where
\left|\cdot\right|
is the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. It’s clear that the greatest distance is achieved when two vectors,
\bm{X}_{i}
and
\bm{X}_{j}
, which are nearly the longest in length, are almost diametrically opposed. Furthermore, the statistic
M_{n}
has potential uses in outlier detection, as discussed by
Matthews and Rukhin, 1993
.

In recent literature, it has been demonstrated by various researchers that the asymptotic distribution of
M_{n}
primarily falls into one of three distinct extreme-value distributions, namely the Gumbel, Weibull, and Fréchet distributions.
For the univariate case, the asymptotic distribution of
M_{n}
has been well established, see
David, 1981
for more information. Specially,
Jammalamadaka and Janson, 2015
show that the asymptotic distribution of
M_{n}
are sum of two independent Gumble distributions under the assumption that
\bm{X_{1}},\cdots,\bm{X_{n}}
are i.i.d symmetric real-valued random variables.
For the multivariate case, many literature investigated the limiting behavior of
M_{n}
under different distribution assumptions. For the standard multivariate normal distribution,
Matthews and Rukhin, 1993
showed that

\displaystyle P\left(\sqrt{2\log n}\left[M_{n}-2\sqrt{2\log n}-\frac{(p-3)/2\log\log n+\log\log\log n+a}{\sqrt{2\log n}}\right]\leq x\right)\to e^{-e^{-x}},

where
a=\log\frac{(p-1)2^{(p-7)/2}}{\Gamma(p/2)\sqrt{\pi}}
.
Henze and Klein, 1996
,
Jammalamadaka and Janson, 2015
extended the above result to the symmetric Kotz population, spherically symmetric type distribution, respectively. A multitude of findings regarding the maximum interpoint Euclidean distance have been derived when the population
\bm{X}
is associated with different kinds of multivariate distributions. These findings can be broadly classified into two groups based on whether the distribution of
\bm{X}
has a bounded or unbounded support. For the unbounded support case,
Henze, 2010
showed that the limiting distribution of
M_{n}
is not of the Gumble type if the distribution of
\bm{X}
is power-tailed spherically decomposable.
Demichel et al., 2015
consider the elliptical distributions with unbounded support. In the case of bounded support,
Tang et al., 2022
provide a comprehensive review of the current body of literature.

All the aforementioned results have been determined for a population with a fixed dimension. Therefore, it would be intriguing to explore a similar geometric phenomenon in a high-dimensional context. When the population
\bm{X}
have independent components and each component obeys the sub-exponential distribution,
Tang et al., 2022
showed that the limiting distribution of
M_{n}
is

\displaystyle 4\sqrt{\log n}\left(\frac{M_{n}^{2}-2p}{\sqrt{2\left(\kappa_{4}+1\right)p}}-2\sqrt{\log n}+\frac{\log\log n}{4\sqrt{\log n}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\xi,

(2)

where
\xi
is a random variable whose distribution is
F_{\xi}(x)=e^{-Ke^{-x/2}}
with
K=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2\pi}}
and
\kappa_{4}=E(X_{ij}^{4})
. In practice, the independent assumption is too restrictive. So we will consider the limiting distribution of
M_{n}
under a special dependent structure in high dimensional settings.

In this paper, we will consider
\bm{X}=\bm{\mu}+\mathbf{R}^{1/2}\bm{\epsilon}
where
\bm{\epsilon}
are i.i.d sub-gaussian variables and
\mathbf{R}
is a Toeplitz matrix with a sequence
\left\{r_{k}\right\}^{\infty}_{k=1}
, that is,

\begin{gathered}\mathbf{R}=\left(r_{\left|i-j\right|}\right)_{p\times p}=\begin{pmatrix}1&r_{1}&\dots&r_{p-1}\\
r_{1}&1&\dots&r_{p-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\;&\vdots\\
r_{p-1}&r_{p-2}&\dots&1\end{pmatrix},\end{gathered}

(3)

where
r_{k}>0
for any
k=1,2,\dots
. According to the Chen–Stein Poisson approximation method and high dimensional Gaussian approximation, we also show that the limiting distribution of
M_{n}
still is Gumble type.

Main results

Definition 2.1

(mixing coefficient)
Let
\left\{\bm{X}_{t}\right\}
be a random sequence. Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{-\infty}^{u}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{u}
the
\sigma
-fields generated respectively by
\left\{\bm{X}_{t}\right\}_{t\leq u}
and
\left\{\bm{X}_{t}\right\}_{t\geq u}
. The
\alpha
-mixing coefficient and
\rho
-mixing coefficient at lag
k
of the sequence
\left\{\bm{X}_{t}\right\}
is defined as

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}\left(k\right)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t}\sup_{A\in\mathcal{F}_{-\infty}^{t},B\in\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{t+k}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(AB\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(A\right)\mathbb{P}\left(B\right)\right|

\displaystyle\rho_{n}\left(k\right)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t}\sup_{f\in L^{2}\left(\mathcal{F}_{-\infty}^{t}\right),g\in L^{2}\left(\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{t+k}\right)}\left|\mathrm{corr}\left(f,g\right)\right|

We say the sequence
\left\{\bm{X}_{t}\right\}
is
\alpha
-mixing or
\rho
-mixing if
\alpha_{n}\left(k\right)\rightarrow 0
or
\rho_{n}\left(k\right)\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Clearly
\alpha_{n}\left(k\right)\leq\rho_{n}\left(k\right)
for any
k
.

Next, we present the conditions required by our theorem.

(C1)

Suppose the stationary sequence
X_{i1},\cdots,X_{ip}
for any
i=1,\dots,n
satisfies the strong mixing condition and the corresponding
\rho
-mixing coefficient
\rho_{n}\left(k\right)
satisfies
\rho_{n}\left(k\right)\leq K_{1}\exp\left(-K_{2}k^{\gamma}\right)
for any
k\geq 1
for some universal constants
K_{1}>1
,
K_{2}>0
and
\gamma>0
.

(C2)

Let
\nu=\frac{2+4\gamma}{3\gamma}\vee\frac{7}{3}
we assume
\left(\log n\right)^{\nu}=o\left(p^{1/9}\right)
where
\gamma
has been defined in Condition (C1).

Remark 1

According to
Kolmogorov and Rozanov, 1960
, we know that, for integer time

\rho\left(k\right)=\inf_{\phi}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\lambda}\left[\left|f\left(\lambda\right)-e^{i\lambda k}\phi\left(e^{-i\lambda}\right)\right|\frac{1}{f\left(\lambda\right)}\right],

where
\inf_{\phi}
is taken over all functions
\phi\left(z\right)
that can by analytically continued inside the unit disc and the spectral density function

is

f\left(\lambda\right)=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}r_{k}e^{-ik\lambda},

(4)

where
r_{0}=1
. So we know that
\rho_{n}
can be bounded by
\left\{r_{k}\right\}^{\infty}_{k=1}
.

Remark 2

There are two simple examples of
\mathbf{R}
satisfying Condition (C1).

\mathbf{R}
is the covariance matrix of the auto-regressive model
AR\left(1\right)
, that is,

\mathbf{R}=\left(r^{\left|i-j\right|}\right)_{p\times p}=\begin{pmatrix}1&r&\dots&r^{p-1}\\
r&1&\dots&r^{p-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
r^{p-1}&r^{p-2}&\dots&1\end{pmatrix},

where
r
is fixed(i.e.
r
do not change with
n
), and
0\leq r<1
.

\mathbf{R}
is the covariance matrix of the stationary
m
-dependent process, that is,

\mathbf{R}=\left(r_{\left|i-j\right|}\right)_{p\times p}=\begin{pmatrix}1&r_{1}&\dots&r_{p-1}\\
r_{1}&1&\dots&r_{p-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
r_{p-1}&r_{p-2}&\dots&1\end{pmatrix},

where
\left\{r_{k}\right\}^{\infty}_{k=1}
satisfies that there exist
m
such that
r_{k}=0
for all
k>m
.

Let the distribution of
\xi
be a Gumbel distribution with function

F_{\xi}\left(x\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x/2}\right),x\in\mathbb{R}.

Define

\mu=2\sqrt{\log n}-\frac{\log\log n}{4\sqrt{\log n}},

and

a_{p}=8+\frac{16}{p}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}r_{k}^{2}\left(p-k\right).

First, we state he asymptotical distribution of the maximum interpoint Euclidean distance of high-dimensional normal distributions.

Theorem 2.1

Under Condition (C1)-(C2) ,if
\bm{X}_{i}\overset{i.i.d.}{\sim}N\left(\bm{\mu},\mathbf{R}\right)
,
i=1,\cdots,n
, then, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

4\sqrt{\log n}\left(\frac{M_{n}^{2}-2p}{\sqrt{pa_{p}}}-\mu\right)\xrightarrow{d}\xi.

In a more general case, we will consider the random vector
\bm{X}
has dependent sub-gaussian components. Specifically,
\bm{X}_{i}=\mathbf{R}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\epsilon}_{i}
, for any
i=1,2,\dots,n
, where
\bm{\epsilon}_{i}
’s are
p
-dimensional independently and identically distributed random vectors whose components are also independently and identically distributed with a sub-gaussian distribution. The definition of
\mathbf{R}
is the same as that of formula (2).

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a
n\times n
-dimension matrix whose element is write as
(\mathbf{A})_{ij}
,
\left\|\mathbf{A}\right\|:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|(\mathbf{A})_{ij}\right|
.
For two matrices
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
of the same dimension
m\times n
, the Hadamard product
{\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{B}}
is a matrix of the same dimension as the operands, with elements given by

\left(\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{B}\right)_{ij}=\left(\mathbf{A}\right)_{ij}\left(\mathbf{B}\right)_{ij}

Furthermore, we write
\mathbf{A}^{\odot 2}:=\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}
.

Theorem 2.2

Assume
\bm{X}_{i}{=}\mathbf{R}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\epsilon}_{i}
, for any
i=1,2,\dots,n
, where
\bm{X}_{i}
’s are
p
-dimensional i.i.d. random vectors. The components of
\bm{\epsilon}_{i}=(\epsilon_{i1},\cdots,\epsilon_{ip})
are i.i.d. in a sub-gaussian distribution with
E(\epsilon_{ij})=0,var(\epsilon_{ij})=1,E(\epsilon_{ij}^{4})=\kappa_{4}<5
. Suppose Condition (C1)-(C2) hold. Set

b_{p}=p^{-1}\left(8\left\|\left(\mathbf{T}^{2}\right)^{\odot 2}\right\|+2\left(\kappa_{4}-3\right)\left\|\left(\mathbf{T}^{\odot 2}\right)^{2}\right\|\right).

Then, we have, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

4\sqrt{\log n}\left(\frac{M_{n}^{2}-2p}{\sqrt{pb_{p}}}-\mu\right)\xrightarrow{d}\xi,

(5)

where
\mathbf{T}:=\mathbf{R}^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Note that if
\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{I}_{p}
, i.e.
r_{k}=0,k=1,\cdots,p-1

5

).

We will list two applications of the result to high-dimensional data analysis.

1.
Test of covariance matrix.
Consider
\bm{X}_{1},\cdots,\bm{X}_{n}\overset{i.i.d.}{\sim}N\left(\bm{\mu},\mathbf{\Sigma}\right)
. We want to test the following hypothesis:

\displaystyle H_{0}:\mathbf{\Sigma}=\mathbf{I}_{p}\penalty\ v.s.\penalty\ H_{1}:\mathbf{\Sigma}\not=\mathbf{I}_{p}.

(6)

We could adopt
M_{n}
to test the above problem and reject the null hypothesis at the significant level
\alpha
if

M_{n}^{2}\geq 2p+\sqrt{p}\cdot\frac{q_{\alpha}+8\log n-\log\log n}{\sqrt{2\log n}}

where
q_{\alpha}=-\log(32\pi)-2\log\log(1-\alpha)^{-1}
. In addition, if
\mathbf{\Sigma}=\mathbf{R}
under the alternative hypothesis, the power function
\beta_{M_{n}}(r_{n})
is

\displaystyle\beta_{M_{n}}(r_{n})=1-F_{\xi}\left(\sqrt{\frac{8}{a_{p}}}q_{\alpha}+\left(\sqrt{\frac{8}{a_{p}}}-1\right)(8\log n-\log\log n)\right).

If
\frac{\log n}{p}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}r_{k}^{2}(p-k)\to\infty
, we have
\beta_{M_{n}}(r_{n})\to 1
.
Chen et al., 2010
proposed a test procedure based on a consistent estimator of
{\rm tr}[(\mathbf{\Sigma}-\mathbf{I}_{p})^{2}]
and show that it is consistent if
\frac{n}{p}{\rm tr}[(\mathbf{\Sigma}-\mathbf{I}_{p})^{2}]\to\infty
. Note that if
\mathbf{\Sigma}=\mathbf{R}
,

{\rm tr}[(\mathbf{\Sigma}-\mathbf{I}_{p})^{2}]=2\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}r_{k}^{2}(p-k)
. So the power of
Chen et al., 2010
’s test would converge to one if
\frac{n}{p}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}r_{k}^{2}(p-k)\to\infty
, which indicates that
Chen et al., 2010
’s test is more efficient than
M_{n}
.

2.
outlier detection
. Suppose
\bm{X}_{1},\cdots,\bm{X}_{n}\overset{i.i.d.}{\sim}N\left(\bm{\mu},\mathbf{R}\right)
. We want to test the following hypothesis:

\bm{H}_{0}\text{ : There is no typical outlier point among }\bm{X}_{1},\bm{X}_{2},\ldots,\bm{X}_{n}\text{. }

According to Theorem
2.1
, we will reject the null hypothesis if

M_{n}^{2}\geq 2p+\sqrt{\frac{pa_{p}}{8}}\cdot\frac{q_{\alpha}+8\log n-\log\log n}{\sqrt{2\log n}}.

Note that
a_{p}=\frac{8}{p}{\rm tr(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{2})}
under the covariance assumption and
T_{2,n}
is a consistent estimator of
{\rm tr(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{2})}
in
Chen et al., 2010
. So we can estimate
a_{p}
by
\frac{8}{p}T_{2,n}
.

Proof of main result

Throughout the paper,
\bm{X}_{1},\dots,\bm{X}_{n}
be identically distributed copies of
p
-dimensional random vector
\bm{X}
with mean
\bm{\mu}
and covariance matrix
\mathbf{R}
. Write
\bm{X}_{i}=\left(x_{i,1},x_{i,2},\dots,x_{i,p}\right)^{T}
for each

, and
\bm{X}^{\left(j\right)}=\left(x_{1,j},x_{2,j},\dots,x_{n,j}\right)^{T}
for each
j=1,2,\dots,p

y_{k,(i,j)}=x_{i,k}^{2}+x_{j,k}^{2}-2x_{i,k}x_{j,k}
Thus,

M_{n}^{2}=\max_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\left(x_{i,k}^{2}+x_{j,k}^{2}-2x_{i,k}x_{j,k}\right)=\max_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\sum_{k=1}^{p}y_{k,(i,j)}.

We state the following three important lemmas here.

Lemma 3.1
(
Chang et al., 2024
, Theorem 1)

Let
\bm{Y}_{1},\dots,\bm{Y}_{n}
be a sequence of p-dimensional dependent random vectors with mean zero, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}\left(\bm{Y}_{t}\right)=0
Write
\bm{S}_{n}=n^{-1/2}{\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{n}}\bm{Y}_{t}
and
\mathbf{\Xi}=\mathrm{Cov}\left(S_{n}\right)
.

Condition a: There exist a sequence of constants
B_{n}\geq 1
and a universal constant
\gamma_{1}\geq 1
such that
\left\|Y_{t,j}\right\|_{\psi_{\gamma_{1}}}\leq B_{n}
for all
t\in\left\{1,2,\dots,n\right\}
and
j\in\left\{1,2,\dots,p\right\}
.

Condition b: There exist some universal constants
K_{1}>1
,
K_{2}>0
and
\gamma_{2}>0
such that
\alpha_{n}\left(k\right)\leq K_{1}\exp\left(-K_{2}k^{\gamma_{2}}\right)
for any
k\geq 1
.

Condition c: There exist a universal constant
K_{3}
such that
\min\text{diag}\left(\mathbf{\Xi}\right)\geq K_{3}
.

Assume
\{\bm{Y}_{t}\}
is an
\alpha
-mixing sequence with
p\geq n^{\kappa}
for some universal constant
\kappa>0
. Under Condition a,b,c, it holds that

\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{S}_{n}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{G}\in A\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{B_{n}^{2/3}\left(\log p\right)^{\left(1+2\gamma_{2}\right)/\left(3\gamma_{2}\right)}}{n^{1/9}}+\frac{B_{n}\left(\log p\right)^{7/6}}{n^{1/9}},

where
G\sim N\left(0,\Xi\right)
and
\mathcal{A}
is a class of Borel subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

Lemma 3.2
(
Arratia et al., 1989
, Theorem 1)

Let
\left\{\eta_{\alpha},\alpha\in I\right\}
be random variables on an index set

and
\left\{B_{\alpha},\alpha\in I\right\}
be a set of subsets of
I
, that is, for each
\alpha\in I
,
B_{\alpha}\subset I
. for any

, set
\lambda_{p}=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\mathbb{P}\left(\eta_{\alpha}>t\right)
, Then we have

\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{\alpha\in I}\eta_{\alpha}\leq t\right)-e^{-\lambda_{p}}\right|\leq\left(1\wedge\lambda_{p}^{-1}\right)\left(b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}\right),

where

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{\beta\in B_{\alpha}}\mathbb{P}\left(\eta_{\alpha}>s_{n}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\eta_{\beta}>s_{n}\right),

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta\in B_{\alpha}}\mathbb{P}\left(\eta_{\alpha}>s_{n},\eta_{\beta}>s_{n}\right),

\displaystyle b_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\left|\mathbb{P}\left\{\eta_{\alpha}>t|\sigma\left(\eta_{\beta},\beta\notin B_{\alpha}\right)\right\}-\mathbb{P}\left(\eta_{\alpha}>t\right)\right|,

and
\sigma\left(\eta_{\beta},\beta\notin B_{\alpha}\right)

\eta_{\alpha}

\left(\eta_{\beta},\beta\notin B_{\alpha}\right)

Lemma 3.3

Let
A_{n}
be a random variable for each

satisfying

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(A_{n}\leq\sqrt{4\log n-\log\log n+x}\right)=F\left(x\right)

3.1
Proof of Theorem 2.1

Step 1: find the covariance structure of

\left(\sigma_{l,k}\right)_{\frac{n\left(n-1\right)}{2}\times\frac{n\left(n-1\right)}{2}}=\mathbf{\Sigma}:=\mathrm{Cov}\left(\bm{Y}_{1}\right)/8

With the function

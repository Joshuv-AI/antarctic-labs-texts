Bubbling solutions for mean field equations with variable intensities on compact Riemann surfaces
Pablo Figueroa

Abstract
For an asymmetric sinh-Poisson problem arising as a mean field equation of equilibrium turbulence vortices with variable intensities of interest in hydrodynamic turbulence, we address the existence of bubbling solutions on compact Riemann surfaces. By using a Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction, we find sufficient conditions under which there exist bubbling solutions blowing up at m m different points of S S : positively at m 1 m_{1} points and negatively at m − m 1 m-m_{1} points with m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 and m 1 ∈ { 0 , 1 , … , m } m_{1}\in\{0,1,...,m\} . Several examples in different situations illustrate our results in the sphere 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} and flat two-torus 𝕋 \mathbb{T} including non negative potentials with zero set non empty.



Keywords
: mean field equation, exponential nonlinearity, blow up solutions, Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction

Introduction

Let

be a compact Riemann surface and consider the problem

-\Delta_{g}u=\lambda_{1}\left({V_{1}(x)e^{u}\over\int_{S}V_{1}e^{u}dv_{g}}-\frac{1}{|S|}\right)-\lambda_{2}\tau\left({V_{2}(x)e^{-\tau u}\over\int_{S}V_{2}e^{-\tau u}dv_{g}}-\frac{1}{|S|}\right),

(1.1)

where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0
,
\tau>0
,

and

are smooth nonnegative potentials in
S

is the area of
S
. Here,

is the Laplace-Beltrami operator and

is the area element in

. This equation have attracted a lot of attention in recent years due to its relevance in the statistical mechanics description of 2D-turbulence, as initiated by Onsager
[
49

assumption on the distribution of the vortex circulations, Sawada, Suzuki
[

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}u=\lambda\int\limits_{[-1,1]}\alpha\bigg({e^{\alpha u}\over\int_{S}e^{\alpha u}dv_{g}}-{1\over|S|}\bigg)d\mathcal{P}(\alpha)&\hbox{in}\ S\end{array}

is a physical constant related to the inverse temperature and

Equation (

3
Approximation of the solution

The main idea to construct approximating solutions of (
1.1
), as in
[
23
]
, is to use as “basic cells” the functions

u_{\delta,\xi}(x)=u_{0}\Big(\frac{|x-\xi|}{\delta}\Big)-2\log\delta,\hskip 20.00003pt\delta>0,\>\xi\in\text{I\!R}^{2},

where
\displaystyle u_{0}(r)=\log\frac{8}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}.
They are all the solutions of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u+e^{u}=0&\text{in $\text{I\!R}^{2}$}\\
\int_{\text{I\!R}^{2}}e^{u}<\infty,&\end{array}\right.

and do satisfy the following concentration property:
e^{u_{\delta,\xi}}\rightharpoonup 8\pi\delta_{\xi}

in measure sense as
\delta\to 0
. We will use now
isothermal coordinates to pull-back
u_{\delta,\xi}
in
S
. Let us recall that every Riemann surface
(S,g)
is locally
conformally flat, and the local coordinates in which
g
is
conformal to the Euclidean metric are referred to as isothermal
coordinates (see for example the simple existence proof provided
by Chern
[
16
]
). For every
\xi\in S
it amounts to find a
local chart
y_{\xi}
, with
y_{\xi}(\xi)=0
, from a neighborhood of

\xi
onto
B_{2r_{0}}(0)
(the choice of
r_{0}
is independent of

\xi
) in which
g=e^{\hat{\varphi}_{\xi}(y_{\xi}(x))}dx
, where
\hat{\varphi}_{\xi}\in C^{\infty}(B_{2r_{0}}(0),\mathbb{R})
. In particular,

\hat{\varphi}_{\xi}
relates with the Gaussian curvature
K
of

(S,g)
through the relation:

\Delta\hat{\varphi}_{\xi}(y)=-2K(y_{\xi}^{-1}(y))e^{\hat{\varphi}_{\xi}(y)}\hskip 20.00003pt\hbox{ for }y\in B_{2r_{0}}(0).

(3.1)

We can also assume that
y_{\xi}
,
\hat{\varphi}_{\xi}
depends
smoothly in
\xi
and that
\hat{\varphi}_{\xi}(0)=0
,
\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\xi}(0)=0
. We now pull-back
u_{\delta,0}
in
\xi\in S
, for
\delta>0
, by simply setting
\displaystyle U_{\delta,\xi}(x)=u_{\delta,0}(y_{\xi}(x))=\log\frac{8\delta^{2}}{(\delta^{2}+|y_{\xi}(x)|^{2})^{2}}
for
x\in y_{\xi}^{-1}(B_{2r_{0}}(0))
. Letting
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2r_{0}}(0))
be a radial cut-off function so that

0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\chi\equiv 1
in
B_{r_{0}}(0)
, we introduce the
function
PU_{\delta,\xi}
as the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{g}PU_{\delta,\xi}(x)=\chi_{\xi}(x)e^{-\varphi_{\xi}(x)}e^{U_{\delta,\xi}(x)}-\frac{1}{|S|}\int_{S}\chi_{\xi}e^{-\varphi_{\xi}}e^{U_{\delta,\xi}}dv_{g}&\text{in }S\\
\int_{S}PU_{\delta,\xi}dv_{g}=0,\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

where
\chi_{\xi}(x)=\chi(|y_{\xi}(x)|)
and
\varphi_{\xi}(x)=\hat{\varphi}_{\xi}(y_{\xi}(x))
. Notice that the R.H.S. in (
3.2
) has
zero average and smoothly depends in
x
, and then (
3.2
) is
uniquely solvable by a smooth solution
PU_{\delta,\xi}
.

Let us recall the transformation law for
\Delta_{g}
under
conformal changes: if
\tilde{g}=e^{\varphi}g
, then

\Delta_{\tilde{g}}=e^{-\varphi}\Delta_{g}.

(3.3)

Decompose now the Green function
G(x,\xi)
,
\xi\in S
, as
\displaystyle G(x,\xi)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\chi_{\xi}(x)\log|y_{\xi}(x)|+H(x,\xi),
and by (
1.6
) then deduce that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{g}H=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{g}\chi_{\xi}\,\log|y_{\xi}(x)|-\frac{1}{\pi}\langle\nabla\chi_{\xi},\nabla\log|y_{\xi}(x)|\rangle_{g}-\frac{1}{|S|}&\text{in $S$}\\
\int_{S}H(\cdot,\xi)\,dv_{g}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S}\chi_{\xi}\log|y_{\xi}(\cdot)|dv_{g}.&\end{array}\right.

We have used that
\displaystyle\Delta_{g}\log|y_{\xi}(x)|=e^{-\hat{\varphi}_{\xi}(y)}\Delta\log|y|\Big|_{y=y_{\xi}(x)}=2\pi\delta_{\xi}
in view of
(
3.3
). For
r\leq 2r_{0}
define

B_{r}(\xi)=y_{\xi}^{-1}(B_{r}(0))
,
A_{r}(\xi)=B_{r}(\xi)\setminus B_{r/2}(\xi)
, and set

f_{\xi}={\Delta_{g}\chi_{\xi}\over|y_{\xi}(x)|^{2}}+2\Big\langle\nabla\chi_{\xi},\nabla|y_{\xi}(x)|^{-2}\Big\rangle_{g}+{2\over|S|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\chi^{\prime}(|y|)\over|y|^{3}}\,dy.

Setting
\Psi_{\delta,\xi}(x)=PU_{\delta,\xi}(x)-\chi_{\xi}[U_{\delta,\xi}-\log(8\delta^{2})]-8\pi H(x,\xi),
by the definition of
f_{\xi}
we then have that
-\Delta_{g}\Psi_{\delta,\xi}=-2\delta^{2}f_{\xi}+O(\delta^{4})
in
S
so that

\int_{S}f_{\xi}dv_{g}=\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{S}\Delta_{g}\Psi_{\delta,\xi}dv_{g}+O(\delta^{2})=O(\delta^{2})

for all
\delta>0
, and hence
\int_{S}f_{\xi}dv_{g}=0
. Therefore,

F_{\xi}
is well defined as the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{g}F_{\xi}=f_{\xi}&\text{in }S\\
\int_{S}F_{\xi}dv_{g}=0.&\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

We have the following asymptotic expansion of
PU_{\delta,\xi}
as

\delta\to 0
, as shown in
[
23
]
:

Lemma 3.1
.

The function
PU_{\delta,\xi}
satisfies

PU_{\delta,\xi}=\chi_{\xi}\left[U_{\delta,\xi}-\log(8\delta^{2})\right]+8\pi H(x,\xi)+\alpha_{\delta,\xi}-2\delta^{2}F_{\xi}+O(\delta^{4}|\log\delta|)

uniformly in
S
, where
F_{\xi}
is given in (
3.4
) and

\alpha_{\delta,\xi}=-{4\pi\over|S|}\delta^{2}\log\delta+2{\delta^{2}\over|S|}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi(|y|)\frac{e^{\hat{\varphi}_{\xi}(y)}-1}{|y|^{2}}dy+\pi-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\chi^{\prime}(|y|)\log|y|\over|y|}dy\right).

In
particular, there holds

PU_{\delta,\xi}=8\pi G(x,\xi)-2{\delta^{2}\chi_{\xi}\over|y_{\xi}(x)|^{2}}+\alpha_{\delta,\xi}-2\delta^{2}F_{\xi}+O(\delta^{4}|\log\delta|)

locally uniformly in
S\setminus\{\xi\}
.

The ansatz will be constructed as follows. Given

m\in\mathbb{N}
, let us consider distinct points

\xi_{j}\in S
and
\delta_{j}>0
,
j=1,\dots,m
. In order to have a good
approximation, we will assume that
\exists\,C_{0}>1\,:

\delta_{j}^{2}=\begin{cases}\mu_{1}^{2}\delta^{2}\rho_{j}(\xi_{j})&\text{ for $j\in\{1,\dots,m_{1}\}$}\\
\mu_{2}^{2}\delta^{2}\rho_{j}(\xi_{j})&\text{ for $j\in\{m_{1}+1,\dots,m\}$}\end{cases},\ \ \ \text{with }\ 0<\mu_{i}\leq C_{0},\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2

(3.5)

|\lambda_{1}-8\pi m_{1}|\leq C_{0}\delta^{2}|\log\delta|\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|\lambda_{2}\tau^{2}-8\pi m_{2}|\leq C_{0}\delta^{2}|\log\delta|,

(3.6)

where
\delta>0
,
m_{1}\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}
,
m_{2}=m-m_{1}
and
\rho_{j}
is given by (
1.8
)-(
1.9
). Up to take
r_{0}
smaller, we assume
that the points
\xi_{j}
’s are well separated and
V_{1}(\xi_{j})
,
V_{2}(\xi_{j})
are uniformly away from zero, namely, we choose

\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})\in\Xi
,
where

\Xi=\{(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})\in S^{m}\mid d_{g}(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})\geq 4r_{0}\>\text{ and }\>V_{1}(\xi_{j}),\>V_{2}(\xi_{j})\geq r_{0}\>\>\forall\>i,j=1,\dots,m,\>i\not=j\}.

Denote
U_{j}:=U_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}
and

W_{j}=PU_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,m
, where
P
is the projection
operator defined by (
3.2
). Thus, our approximating solution
is
\displaystyle W(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}W_{j}(x)-{1\over\tau}\sum_{j=m_{1}+1}^{m}W_{j}(x)
, parametrized by
(\mu,\xi)\in\mathcal{M}\times\Xi
, with
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
and
\mathcal{M}=(0,C_{0}]\times(0,C_{0}]
. Notice that for
r_{0}
small enough we have
that
\mathcal{D}\subset\Xi\subset\tilde{S}^{m}\setminus\Delta
. We
will look for a solution
u
of (
1.1
) in the form

u=W+\phi
, for some small remainder term
\phi
. In terms of

\phi
, the problem (
1.1
) is equivalent to find
\phi\in\bar{H}
so that

L(\phi)=-[R+N(\phi)]\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ in $S$},

(3.7)

where the linear operator
L
is defined as

L(\phi)=\Delta_{g}\phi+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\lambda_{i}\tau^{2(i-1)}{V_{i}(x)e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}W}\over\int_{S}V_{i}e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}W}dv_{g}}\left(\phi-{\int_{S}V_{i}e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}W}\phi dv_{g}\over\int_{S}V_{i}e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}W}dv_{g}}\right),

(3.8)

the nonlinear part
N
is given by

N(\phi)=N_{1}(\phi)-N_{2}(\phi)

(3.9)

with

\begin{split}N_{i}(\phi)=&\,\lambda_{i}\tau^{i-1}\bigg({V_{i}e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}(W+\phi)}\over\int_{S}V_{i}e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}(W+\phi)}dv_{g}}-{(-\tau)^{i-1}V_{i}e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}W}\over\int_{S}V_{i}e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}W}dv_{g}}\left[\phi-\frac{\int_{S}V_{i}e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}W}\phi dv_{g}}{\int_{S}V_{i}e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}W}dv_{g}}\right]\\
&\,-{V_{i}e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}W}\over\int_{S}V_{i}e^{(-\tau)^{i-1}W}dv_{g}}\bigg)\end{split}

(3.10)

for
i=1,2
and the approximation rate of
W
is encoded in

R=\Delta_{g}W+\lambda_{1}\left({V_{1}(x)e^{W}\over\int_{S}V_{1}e^{W}dv_{g}}-{1\over|S|}\right)-\lambda_{2}\tau\left({V_{2}(x)e^{-\tau W}\over\int_{S}V_{2}e^{-\tau W}dv_{g}}-{1\over|S|}\right).

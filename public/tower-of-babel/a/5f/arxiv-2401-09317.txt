From Zero-Freeness to Strong Spatial Mixing via a Christoffel-Darboux Type Identity
Shuai Shao, Xiaowei Ye

Abstract
We present a unifying proof to derive the strong spatial mixing (SSM) property for the general 2-spin system from zero-free regions of its partition function. Our proof works for the multivariate partition function over all three complex parameters ( β , γ , λ ) (\beta,\gamma,\lambda) , and we allow the zero-free regions of β , γ \beta,\gamma or λ \lambda to be of arbitrary shapes. Our main technical contribution is to establish a Christoffel-Darboux type identity for the 2-spin system on trees so that we are able to handle zero-free regions of the three different parameters β , γ \beta,\gamma or λ \lambda in a unified way. We use Riemann mapping theorem to deal with zere-free regions of arbitrary shapes. Our result comprehensively turns all existing zero-free regions (to our best knowledge) of the partition function of the 2-spin system where pinned vertices are allowed into the SSM property. As a consequence, we obtain new SSM properties for the 2-spin system beyond the direct argument for SSM based on tree recurrence. Moreover, we extend our result to handle the 2-spin system with non-uniform external fields. As an application, we obtain a new SSM property and two new forms of spatial mixing property, namely plus and minus spatial mixing for the non-uniform ferromagnetic Ising model from the celebrated Lee-Yang circle theorem.

Introduction

Spin systems arise from statistical physics to model interactions between
neighbors on graphs.
In this paper, we consider the 2-spin system.
Such a system is defined on a simple finite undirected graph
G=(V,E)
and in this way the individual entities comprising the system correspond to the
vertices
V
and their pairwise interactions correspond to the edges
E
.
The system is associated with three parameters including two edge activities
\beta
and
\gamma
that model the tendency of vertices to agree and disagree with their neighbors, and a vertex activity
\lambda
that models a uniform external field which determines the propensity of a vertex to be the spin
+
.
A partial configuration of this system refers to a mapping
\sigma:\Lambda\to\{+,-\}
for some
\Lambda\subseteq V
which may be empty. It assigns one of the two spins
+
and
-
to each vertex in
\Lambda
.
When
\Lambda=V
, it is a configuration and its weight denoted by
w(\sigma)
is
\beta^{m_{+}(\sigma)}\gamma^{m_{-}(\sigma)}\lambda^{n_{+}(\sigma)}
, where
m_{+}(\sigma),m_{-}(\sigma)
and
n_{+}(\sigma)
denote respectively the number of
(+,+)
edges,
(-,-)
edges and vertices with the spin
+
.
The
partition function
of a 2-spin system is defined to be

Z_{G}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda):=\sum\limits_{\sigma:V\to\{+,-\}}w(\sigma).

We also define the partition function conditioning on a pre-described partial configuration
\sigma_{\Lambda}
(i.e., each vertex in
\Lambda
, called pinned vertex is pinned to be the spin
+
or
-

Z^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}_{G}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)
to be
\sum_{\sigma:V\to\{+,-\}\atop\sigma|_{\Lambda}=\sigma_{\Lambda}}w(\sigma)
where
\sigma|_{\Lambda}
denotes the restriction of the configuration
\sigma
on
\Lambda
.

Computing the partition function of the 2-spin system given an input graph
G
is a very basic counting problem, and it is known to be #P-hard for all complex valued parameters
(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)

but a few very restricted settings such as
\beta\gamma=1
or
\lambda=0

[

,

,

Many natural combinatorial problems can be formulated as computing the partition function of the 2-spin system.
For example, when
\beta=0
and
\gamma=1

G

(also known as the

in statistical physics); it counts the number of independent

G

\lambda=1

And when
\beta=\gamma

In classical statistical mechanics the parameters
(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)
are usually non-negative real numbers and
(\beta,\gamma)\neq(0,0)

Such 2-spin systems are divided into the

For non-negative

which is the distribution where a configuration

However, it is meaningful to consider parameters of complex values.
First, the parameters are generally complex valued for quantum computation.
For instance, the partition function of 2-spin systems with complex parameters is closely related to the output probability amplitudes of quantum circuits

Moreover, even in classical theory, the study of the location of

One of the first and also the best known results regarding the zeros of the partition function is the Lee-Yang theorem

Remarkably, the two notions of phase transitions zero-freeness and correlation decay show some inherent connections.
In seminal works
[
DS85
,
DS87
]
, Dobrushin and Shlosman proved that the correlation decay property is actually equivalent to the analyticity of the free energy
and zero-freeness of the partition function for the special case of lattices.
Recently, some more refined connections between them have been observed in the hard-core model
[
PR19
]
, the Ising model
[
LSS19
,
PR20
,
LSS22
]
, and the general 2-spin system
[
SS21
]
on graphs of bounded degree.
In these results, one can extend real parameters
(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)
in which correlation decay exists to their complex neighborhoods where the partition function is zero-free.
However, it is not known whether correlation decay or SSM directly implies zero-freeness of the partition function.
On the other hand, Gamarnik
[
Gam23
]
showed that zero-freeness of the partition function
directly implies a weak form of SSM for the hard-core model and other
graph homomorphism models on graphs with certain properties.
Later, Regts
[
Reg23
]
showed that zero-freeness in fact implies SSM for the hard-core model near
\lambda=0
and other
graph homomorphism models on all graphs of bounded degree.
When restricted to the 2-spin system, the graph homomorphism model is
the 2-spin system without external field i.e.,
\lambda=1
, and
the implication from zero-freeness to SSM holds for a complex neighborhood of
(\beta,\gamma)=(1,1)
.

Our contributions

In this paper, we further study the direction from zero-freeness of the partition function to obtain SSM for the general 2-spin system.
We show that zero-freeness of the partition function implies SSM for 2-spin systems on arbitrary graphs.
Unlike the result for the hard-core model
[
Reg23
]
where the partition function is a univariate function on
\lambda
and the zero-free region of
\lambda
is in a strip shape, in this paper we view the partition function
Z_{G}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)
of the 2-spin system, as a
multivariate
function over three
complex
parameters
(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)
, and we allow the zero-free regions of
\beta,\gamma
or
\lambda
to be of arbitrary shapes as long as they contain some particular points.
For such zero-free regions, we are able to show SSM holds.
The underlying graphs of the 2-spin system are
not
necessarily of bounded degree, while are required to include graphs with pinned vertices.

Thus, Theorem

gives a powerful tool to handle the multivariate partition function of 2-spin systems and derive SSM from zero-freeness uniformly for all the three parameters
(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)
under different zero-free conditions.

Furthermore, the application of Theorem
7
has another potential advantage. It may answer a very interesting question:
if the partition function is zero-free on a complex neighborhood of some points besides
\lambda=0
and
\beta\gamma=1
, can we still derive SSM from such zero-free regions?
The answer would be true if we could factor out some
(\lambda-\lambda_{0})^{d(u,v)}
or
(\beta\gamma-z_{0})^{d(u,v)}
from
P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)-P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},u^{+}}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)
. However, such a factorization is false by Theorem
7
.
Otherwise, there would exist some
\lambda_{0}\neq 0
or
\beta_{0}\gamma_{0}=z_{0}\neq 1
such that
P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda_{0})\equiv P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},u^{+}}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda_{0})
or
P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(\beta_{0},\gamma_{0},\lambda)\equiv P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},u^{+}}(\beta_{0},\gamma_{0},\lambda)
for all graphs
G
.
Thus, it is highly possible that by using Theorem
7
, we have indeed carved out all zero-free regions from which SSM can be derived (at least via the approach of establishing LDC).
In addition, we use Riemann mapping theorem to deal with zero-free regions of arbitrary shapes, instead of a strip-shaped neighborhood of a real interval.

In order to obtain an FPTAS for non-disk zero-free regions via Barvinok’s algorithm, such regions should be able to be mapped to a disk by polynomials
[

]
. While by using Riemann

2.4
Regts’ approach

The following lemma is a key ingredient in Regts’ approach to derive SSM from zero-freeness.

Lemma 17

(
[
Reg23
]
)
.

Let
P(z)
and

be two analytic functions on some complex neighborhood

of
z_{0}
.
Suppose that the Taylor series
\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}(z-z_{0})^{k}
of
P(z)
and
\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}(z-z_{0})^{k}
of

near
z_{0}

satisfy
a_{k}=b_{k}
for
k=0,1,\cdots,n
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Also, suppose that there exists an
M>0
such that both
|P(z)|\leq M
and
|Q(z)|\leq M
on some circle
\partial\mathbb{D}_{\rho}(z_{0})\subseteq U

(\rho>0)
.
Then for every

, we have

|P(z)-Q(z)|\leq\frac{2M}{\rho(r-1)r^{n}},\quad\text{with}\quad r=\dfrac{\rho}{|z-z_{0}|}.

Proof.

Using Cauchy integral formula, we have

\displaystyle a_{k}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{\rho}(z_{0})}\dfrac{P(w)}{(w-z_{0})^{k+1}}\mathrm{d}w,\text{ \quad and \quad}b_{k}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{\rho}(z_{0})}\dfrac{Q(w)}{(w-z_{0})^{k+1}}\mathrm{d}w.

Note that the series
\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}(z-z_{0})^{k}
converges to
P(z)
and
\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}(z-z_{0})^{k}
converges to
Q(z)
on the closed disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}_{\rho}}(z_{0})
by Theorem

.
Thus, for every
z\in\mathbb{D}_{\rho}(z_{0}),
we have

\displaystyle|P(z)-Q(z)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum\limits_{k\geq n+1}(a_{k}-b_{k})(z-z_{0})^{k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k\geq n+1}\dfrac{|z-z_{0}|^{k}}{2\pi}\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{\rho}(z_{0})}\left|\dfrac{P(w)-Q(w)}{(w-z_{0})^{k+1}}\right|\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2M}{\rho}\sum\limits_{k\geq n+1}r^{-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2M}{\rho(r-1)r^{n}}

which completes the proof.

\sigma_{\Lambda}\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}
on some
\Lambda\subseteq V
, and any two vertices

, the functions

.

Lemma 21
.

If a 2-spin system satisfies point-to-point LDC, then it also satisfies LDC.

Proof.

When point-to-point LDC holds, we prove LDC by induction of
|\{\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}\neq\tau_{\Lambda_{2}}\}|.

When
|\{\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}\neq\tau_{\Lambda_{2}}\}|=0,
the assertion holds immediately. Suppose now that the assertion holds when
|\{\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}\neq\tau_{\Lambda_{2}}\}|=k\geq 0,
then when
|\{\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}\neq\tau_{\Lambda_{2}}\}|=k+1
, take
u\in\{\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}\neq\tau_{\Lambda_{2}}\},
by point-to-point LDC, the functions

P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}}(\lambda)
and

P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\setminus\{u\}}}(\lambda)
are analytic on
U
, and
their corresponding Taylor series
\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}a_{i}\lambda^{i}
and

\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}b_{i}\lambda^{i}
near
\lambda=0
satisfy
a_{i}=b_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq d(v,u)-1
; similar equality holds for
\tau_{\Lambda_{2}}
and

\tau_{\Lambda_{2}\setminus\{u\}}
.

Let
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\setminus\{u\}},\tau^{\prime}=\tau_{\Lambda_{2}\setminus\{u\}}
(recall that
\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\setminus\{u\}}
means the restriction of
\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}
on

then the two partial configurations are the same. ), and then we have

∎

Lemma 22
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a set of graphs, and

.
Fix complex parameters

and
\gamma
, and a neighborhood

of

.
If the 2-spin system defined on

satisfies point-to-point LDC, then the 2-spin system defined on
\mathcal{G}
also satisfies point-to-point LDC.

Proof.

Given
a graph

, a feasible configuration
\sigma_{\Lambda}\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}
on some
\Lambda\subseteq V
which maybe empty, and two vertices

proper to
\sigma_{\Lambda}
, let

and
(u)
denote the set of copies of

in

By Theorem

,
we have

,

P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},u^{+}}(\lambda)=P_{T,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda_{\rm{SAW}},(u)^{+}}}(\lambda)
, and
P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},u^{-}}(\lambda)=P_{T,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda_{\rm{SAW},(u)^{-}}}}(\lambda)
, where
\sigma_{\Lambda_{\rm SAW},(u)^{+}}

\sigma_{\Lambda_{\rm SAW},(u)^{-}}

Thus, it suffices to prove that point-to-point LDC derives from zero-freeness for trees.
We prove this by establishing a Christoffel-Darboux type identity for 2-spin systems on trees.

Before we dig into the general 2-spin system, we first consider a simple case, the hard-core model, the partition function of which is the independence polynomial. This model is the 2-spin system where
\beta=0
and
\gamma=1.
For the hard-core model,
[
Reg23
]
proved that LDC holds by using a tool called cluster expansion from statistic physics.
Here, we show that it can also be proved by the following Christoffel-Darboux type identity by Gutman
[
Gut91
]
.
For a graph
G=(V,E)
and a subset
U\subseteq V
, we use
G\backslash U
to denote the graph obtained from
G
by deleting all vertices in
U
and their adjacent edges (i.e., the subgraph of
G
induced by the vertex set
V\backslash U
).

Theorem 23
.

Let
T=(V,E)
be a tree and
u,v
be two distinct vertices in
V
. Then, for the partition function of the hard-core model on
G
, we have

Z_{T}(\lambda)Z_{T\setminus\{u,v\}}(\lambda)-Z_{T\setminus\{u\}}(\lambda)Z_{T\setminus\{v\}}(\lambda)=-(-\lambda)^{d(u,v)+1}Z_{T\setminus p_{uv}}(\lambda)Z_{T\setminus N[p_{uv}]}(\lambda)

where
p_{uv}
is the unique path connecting
u
and
v
and
N[p_{uv}]
is the set of neighbors of points in
p_{uv}
.

This identity was extended to general graphs in
[
Ben18
]
(Theorem 1.3).
By Lemma
22
,
the identity for trees is already enough for our proof.

Lemma 24
.

For the hard-core model defined on a family
\mathcal{G}
of trees closed under
taking subgraphs, if the partition function
Z_{G}(\lambda)
is zero-free on a neighborhood
U
of
0
for any
G\in\mathcal{G}
, then it satisfies point-to-point LDC on
U
.

Proof.

The case
d_{G}(u,v)=1
is easy to check. Here, we assume that
d_{G}(u,v)\geq 2
. For the hard-core model,
Z^{+}_{G,v}(\lambda)=\lambda Z_{G\backslash N[v]}(\lambda)
and
Z^{-}_{G,v}(\lambda)=Z_{G\backslash v}(\lambda)
.
Then,

P^{u^{-}}_{G,v}(\lambda)=\frac{Z^{-,+}_{G,u,v}(\lambda)}{Z^{-}_{G,u}(\lambda)}=\frac{Z^{+}_{G\backslash\{u\},v}}{Z_{G\backslash\{u\}}}=P_{G\backslash\{u\},v}(\lambda)

and

P^{u^{+}}_{G,v}(\lambda)=\frac{Z^{+,+}_{G,u,v}(\lambda)}{Z^{+}_{G,u}(\lambda)}=\frac{\lambda Z^{+}_{G\backslash N[u],v}}{\lambda Z_{G\backslash N[u]}}=P_{G\backslash N[u],v}(\lambda).

In fact, it is easy to see that for any graph
G
and any feasible partial configuration
\sigma_{\Lambda}
on some
\Lambda\subseteq U
, there exists a subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
such that
P^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}_{G,v}(\lambda)=P_{G^{\prime},v}(\lambda)
. Since the family
\mathcal{G}
is closed under taking subgraphs,
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
. Thus, in order to establish point-to-point LDC, it suffices to prove it for a tree
G\in\mathcal{G}
where no vertices are pinned.
We observe that

\displaystyle Z_{G}Z_{G\setminus\{u,v\}}-Z_{G\setminus\{u\}}Z_{G\setminus\{v\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(Z^{-,-}_{G,u,v}+Z^{+,+}_{G,u,v}+Z^{-,+}_{G,u,v}+Z^{+,-}_{G,u,v})Z^{-,-}_{G,u,v}-(Z^{-,-}_{G,u,v}+Z^{-,+}_{G,u,v})(Z^{-,-}_{G,u,v}+Z^{+,-}_{G,u,v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{G,u,v}^{+,+}Z_{G,u,v}^{-,-}-Z_{G,u,v}^{+,-}Z_{G,u,v}^{-,+}.

Here we omit the variable
\lambda
for simplicity. Then, we have

\displaystyle P_{G,v}(\lambda)-P^{u^{-}}_{G,v}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{Z^{+}_{G,v}(\lambda)}{Z_{G}(\lambda)}-\dfrac{Z^{+,-}_{G,v,u}(\lambda)}{Z^{-}_{G,u}(\lambda)}

(2)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{Z_{G,u,v}^{+,+}(\lambda)Z_{G,u,v}^{-,-}(\lambda)-Z_{G,u,v}^{+,-}(\lambda)Z_{G,u,v}^{-,+}(\lambda)}{Z_{G}(\lambda)Z^{-}_{G,u}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-(-\lambda)^{d(u,v)+1}Z_{G\setminus p_{uv}}(\lambda)Z_{G\setminus N[p_{uv}]}(\lambda)}{Z_{G}(\lambda)Z_{G\setminus\{u\}}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{d(u,v)}H_{1}(\lambda)

where
H_{1}(\lambda)
is a rational function on
U
which is analytic near
0
. Thus, the coefficients of the term
\lambda^{k}
for
0\leq k\leq d_{G}(u,v)-1
in the Taylor series of them are the same. Also, we have

\displaystyle P_{G,v}(\lambda)-P^{u^{+}}_{G,v}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{Z^{+}_{G,v}(\lambda)}{Z_{G}(\lambda)}-\dfrac{Z^{+,+}_{G,v,u}(\lambda)}{Z^{+}_{G,u}(\lambda)}

(3)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{-(Z_{G,u,v}^{+,+}(\lambda)Z_{G,u,v}^{-,-}(\lambda)-Z_{G,u,v}^{+,-}(\lambda)Z_{G,u,v}^{-,+}(\lambda))}{Z_{G}(\lambda)Z^{+}_{G,u}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-\lambda)^{d(u,v)+1}Z_{G\setminus p_{uv}}(\lambda)Z_{G\setminus N[p_{uv}]}(\lambda)}{\lambda Z_{G}(\lambda)Z_{G\setminus N[u]}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{d(u,v)}H_{2}(\lambda)

where
H_{2}(\lambda)
is a rational function on
U
which is analytic near
0
. Thus, the coefficients of the term
\lambda^{k}
for
0\leq k\leq d_{G}(u,v)-1
in the Taylor series of them are the same.
∎

Remark 4
.

The above proof of LDC for the hard-core model using the Christoffel-Darboux type identity may somewhat answer the following interesting question:
if the partition function of the hard-core model is zero-free on a complex neighborhood of some point
\lambda_{0}\neq 0
, can we get SSM for this zero-free region?

The answer would be true if the LDC property could hold for rational functions
P_{G}^{v}(\lambda)
expanded as Taylor series near
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
instead of
\lambda=0
.
However, the Christoffel-Darboux type identity implies that such a LDC property does not hold. Otherwise, for any graph
G
and any vertex
u
, we would have
P_{G,v}(\lambda)-P^{u^{+}}_{G,v}(\lambda)=(\lambda-\lambda_{0})^{d(u,v)}H(\lambda)
for some function
H(\lambda)
analytic near
\lambda_{0}
.
One can easily check that this is false.
Thus, it is highly possible that a zero-free region not containing
0
cannot imply SSM (at least not by our approach).

Now, we extend the above Christoffel-Darboux type identity to the general 2-spin system on trees. Actually, we can extend it to the more general
q
-spins system on trees (see Appendix
A
).

Theorem 25

(Theorem
7
restated)
.

Suppose that
T
is a tree,
\sigma_{\Lambda}
is a partial configuration on some
\Lambda\subseteq V
which maybe empty, and
u
and
v
are two distinct vertices in
V\backslash\Lambda
.
Let
p_{uv}
denote the unique path in
T
connecting
u
and
v
,
V(p_{uv})
denote the set of points in
p_{uv}
,
N[p_{uv}]
denote the set of neighbors of points in
p_{uv}
,
N[p_{uv}]\setminus V(p_{uv})=\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\}
, and
T_{i}
denote the component of
v_{i}
in

.
Then for the partition function of the 2-spin system defined on
T
conditioning on
\sigma_{\Lambda}
, we have (we omit the argument
(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)
in the second line for simplicity)

\displaystyle Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,+}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,-}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)-Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,-}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,+}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\beta\gamma-1)^{d(u,v)}\lambda^{d(u,v)+1}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\beta Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-})(Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+\gamma Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-})&,\text{ if }V(p_{uv})\cap\Lambda=\emptyset\\
0&,\text{ if }V(p_{uv})\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\end{cases}.

Proof.

In the following proof and Remark
5
, we omit the argument
(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)
of the partition functions for simplicity.
We prove the equality by induction on
k=d(u,v)
when
V(p_{uv})\cap\Lambda=\emptyset
. When
k=1,

u
and
v
are adjacent, Denote
u,v_{1},\cdots,v_{l}
neighbors of
v
and
v,v_{l+1},\cdots,v_{n}
neighbors of

, as illustrated in the following figure.

Then, we have

Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,+}=\lambda^{2}\beta\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\beta Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-});

Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,-}=\gamma\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+\gamma Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-});

Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,+}=\lambda\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}(\beta Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-})\prod\limits_{i=l+1}^{n}(Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+\gamma Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-});

Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,-}=\lambda\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}(Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+\gamma Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-})\prod\limits_{i=l+1}^{n}(\beta Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-}).

Thus, we deduce that

Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,+}Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,-}-Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,-}Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,+}=(\beta\gamma-1)\lambda^{2}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\beta Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-})(Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+\gamma Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-}).

Suppose now that
k\geq 2
and the assertion is true for

. Suppose that
v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{l}
are neighbors of
v
with
v_{0}
on

Let
T_{0}
denote the component of

containing

. Then

Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,+}=\lambda(\beta Z_{T_{0},v_{0},u}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,+}+Z_{T_{0},v_{0},u}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,+})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}(\beta Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-});

Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,+}=\lambda(\beta Z_{T_{0},v_{0},u}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,-}+Z_{T_{0},v_{0},u}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,-})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}(\beta Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-});

Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,-}=(Z_{T_{0},v_{0},u}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,+}+\gamma Z_{T_{0},v_{0},u}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,+})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}(Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+\gamma Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-}),

and hence,

\displaystyle Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,+}Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,-}-Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,-}Z_{T,u,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\gamma-1)\lambda(Z_{T_{0},u,v_{0}}^{,\sigma_{\Lambda},+,+}Z_{T_{0},u,v_{0}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,-}-Z_{T_{0},u,v_{0}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,-}Z_{T_{0},u,v_{0}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-,+})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}(\beta Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-})(Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}+\gamma Z_{T_{i},v_{i}}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},-}).

Remark 5
.

Since

4
Zero-freeness implies SSM

Now, we are ready to show that zero-freeness implies SSM.
We first apply Montel theorem to get a uniform bound for the multivariate function

[

]
.

Lemma 31
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be
a family of graphs, and
U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be quasi-neighborhoods of some
\beta_{0},\gamma_{0},\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
where
U_{1}\times U_{2}\times U_{3}\neq\{(\beta_{0},\gamma_{0},\lambda_{0})\}
(i.e., at least one of
U_{1},U_{2}
, and
U_{3}
is a complex region).
Denote the set
((U_{1}\times U_{2})\backslash\{(0,0)\})\times(U_{3}\backslash\{0\})
by
\mathbf{U}
.
Suppose that the partition function of the 2-spin system defined on
\mathcal{G}
is zero-free on
\mathbf{U}
, and one of the following conditions holds.

1.

\beta_{0},\gamma_{0},\lambda_{0}
are non-negative real numbers that are not all zeros;

2.

\mathcal{G}
is
a family of graphs of bounded degree and
\beta_{0}\gamma_{0}=1
.

Then for any compact set
S\subseteq\mathbf{U}
, there exists an
M>0
such that for every
G\in\mathcal{G}
, any feasible partial configuration
\sigma_{\Lambda}
, any vertex

proper to
\sigma_{\Lambda}
, we have

for all
(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)\in S

Proof.

We give the proof when
U_{1},U_{2}
are neighborhoods of some points.
For the case that some of them are singletons, the proof is identical using Montel theorem with fewer variables.

Suppose to the contrary that there exists a sequence of tuples
(G_{n}\in\mathcal{G},v_{n},\Lambda_{n},\sigma^{(n)}_{\Lambda_{n}},(\beta_{n},\gamma_{n},\lambda_{n})\in S)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\left|P_{G_{n},v_{n}}^{\sigma^{(n)}_{\Lambda_{n}}}(\beta_{n},\gamma_{n},\lambda_{n})\right|\geq n.

We denote by
\{f_{n}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
the sequence of functions
\{P_{G_{n},v_{n}}^{\sigma^{(n)}_{\Lambda_{n}}}(\beta,\gamma,\lambda)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Since the partition function of the 2-spin system defined on
\mathcal{G}
is zero-free on
\mathbf{U}
, for any
G=(V,E)\in\mathcal{G}
, any feasible partial configuration
\sigma_{\Lambda}
on some
\Lambda\subseteq V

\sigma_{\Lambda}

\bf U
(which is connected since

and
1

15

Now we consider the two conditions. If
\beta_{0},\gamma_{0},\lambda_{0}\geq 0
and they are not all zeros, then for any rational function

(\beta_{0},\gamma_{0},\lambda_{0})
is a probability which lies in the interval
(0,1)
.
Otherwise,
\mathcal{G}
is a family of graphs the degrees of which are bounded by some

.
Then, one can check that for any rational function

, its value given the input

is
\frac{\lambda_{0}\beta_{0}^{d_{v}}}{\lambda_{0}\beta_{0}^{d_{v}}+1}

is the degree of

in
G

Note that

since the partition function is nonzero on
\mathbb{U}
.
Since

is bounded by the maximum degree
\Delta
,
for all rational functions

, we have
|P^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}_{G,v}(\beta_{0},\gamma_{0},\gamma_{0})|\leq\max_{0\leq d_{v}\leq\Delta}\left|\frac{\lambda_{0}\beta_{0}^{d_{v}}}{\lambda_{0}\beta_{0}^{d_{v}}+1}\right|<\infty
.
Thus, in both cases, any sequence in the family of rational functions

can not converge locally uniformly to
\infty
. Then by Definition
14

Thus, the above sequence

has a sub-sequence
\{f_{n_{i}}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
converging locally uniformly to some holomorphic function

on

is a compact subset of

, and also the function

Then, there exists some

such that for all

on

.
Clearly, this contradicts that for all

where
(\beta_{n},\gamma_{n},\lambda_{n})\in S
.
∎

In particular, when the set

Theorem 33

\mathcal{G}
be

5
The implication for non-uniform external fields

In this section,
we extend our result to handle the 2-spin system with non-uniform external fields, and apply it to get new spatial mixing results for the non-uniform ferromagnetic Ising model from corollaries of Lee-Yang circle theorem on graphs with pinned vertices.

Definition 37
.

A 2-spin system defined on a graph
G=(V,E)
is said to has non-uniform external fields if vertices
v\in V
may have different external fields
\lambda_{v}
.
The partition function of such a system is defined to be

Z_{G}(\beta,\gamma,\boldsymbol{\lambda}):=\sum\limits_{\sigma:V\to\{+,-\}}w(\sigma)

with
w(\sigma)=\beta^{m_{+}(\sigma)}\gamma^{m_{-}(\sigma)}\prod\limits_{v\in V,\sigma(v)=+}\lambda_{v}
.

The notations and definitions for the 2-spin system with a uniform external field can be extended to the setting with non-uniform external fields as above. In particular, the Christoffel-Darboux type identity (Theorem
25
) is also valid for the 2-spin system with non-uniform external fields by replacing
\lambda^{d(u,v)+1}
with
\prod_{w\in p_{uv}}\lambda_{w}
where
p_{uv}
is the unique path connecting
u
and
v
in a tree.
The SAW tree construction also works where all vertices in
V_{\text{SAW}}
mapped from a single vertex
v\in V
have the same external field as the external field
\lambda_{v}
of the original
v
, and
Theorem
11
still holds for the multivariate rational functions
P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(\beta,\gamma,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
.
For non-uniform external fields
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{v})_{v\in V}
, we say they have the same argument
\theta
if
\arg(\lambda_{v})=\theta
for all
v\in V
.

Theorem 38

(Theorem
4
restated)
.

Fix
\beta,\gamma,\lambda^{\ast}>0
and a family of graphs
\mathcal{G}
closed under
SAW
tree constructions.
Suppose that for any
G\in\mathcal{G}
and any feasible partial configuration
\sigma_{\Lambda}
, the partition function
Z^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}_{G,\beta,\gamma}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
of the 2-spin system with non-uniform external fields
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
having the same argument
\theta
and satisfying
0<|\lambda_{v}|<\lambda^{*}
for all
v\in V(G)
is always non-zero.
Then, the corresponding 2-spin system
specified by
\beta,\gamma
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
exhibits SSM.

Proof.

Since the SAW tree construction still hold for the 2-spin system with non-uniform external fields, similar to Lemma
22
, we only need to consider the 2-spin system on trees.

For a graph
G
, a feasible partial configuration
\sigma_{\Lambda}
together with a vertex
v
proper to it, and some fixed values
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
of external fields satisfying
\arg(\lambda_{v})=\theta
for some
\theta
and
0<|\lambda_{v}|<\lambda^{*}
for all
v\in V(G)
and
\sup_{v\in V(G)}|\lambda_{v}|<\lambda^{\ast}
, we define the function
p_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(z)=P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(z\boldsymbol{\lambda})
for
z\in\mathbb{D}_{\rho}
where
\rho=\frac{\lambda^{*}}{\sup|\lambda_{v}|}>1.

Note that
p_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(z)
is a univariate function on the complex variable
z
.
Since the partition function

Z^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}_{G,\beta,\gamma}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
is zero-free, the function
p_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(z)
avoids
0
and
1
on
\mathbb{D}_{\rho}
.

Fix the values of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.
Still by Montel theorem, for any graph
G
, any feasible partial configuration
\sigma_{\Lambda}
, and any vertex
v
proper to it, the functions
p_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(z)
form a normal family.
Also, all functions in the family take a value in the interval
(0,1)
when
z=e^{-i\theta}
.
Still similar to the proof Lemma
31
, we can show that for any
0<\rho_{0}<\rho
, there exists a uniform bound for the family of functions
p_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(z)
on the circle
\partial\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}}
.

Also, by the Christoffel-Darboux type identity (Theorem
25
) for the 2-spin system with non-uniform external fields in which
\lambda^{d(u,v)+1}
is replace by
\prod_{w\in p_{uv}}\lambda_{w}
where
p_{uv}
is the unique path connecting
u
and
v
, we have

\displaystyle p_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(z)-p_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},u^{+}}(z)

\displaystyle=P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(z\boldsymbol{\lambda})-P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},u^{+}}(z\boldsymbol{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\frac{Z_{G,u}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}Z_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}-Z_{G,v,u}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+,+}Z_{G}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}}{Z_{G}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}Z_{G,u}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},+}}(z\boldsymbol{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\prod_{w\in p_{uv}}(z\lambda_{w})H(z)

\displaystyle=z^{d_{G}(u,v)+1}H^{\prime}(z),

for some functions
H(z)
and
H^{\prime}(z)
analytic near
0
.
The same form of equation holds for
P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(z)-P_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda},u^{-}}(z)
.
Similarly, we can define point-to-point LDC and LDC for functions
p_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(z)
near
z=0
, and show that point-to-point LDC implies LDC.
Thus, the functions
p_{G,v}^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}(z)
satisfy LDC.
Combine the uniform bound on a circle and LDC, and we get SSM by Lemma
17
.
∎

The following is the celebrated Lee-Yang circle theorem.

Theorem 39

(Lee-Yang circle theorem)
.

Fix
\beta>1.

For any graph
G=(V,E)
, the partition function
Z_{G}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
of the ferromagnetic Ising model specified by
\beta
and non-uniform external fields
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{v})_{v\in V}
is nonzero if for all
v\in V
we have
|\lambda_{v}|>1
or
|\lambda_{v}|<1
.

Note that Lee-Yang circle theorem gives zero-freeness for the partition function of the Ising model regarding the external fields

Concluding remarks and questions

We prove that if the partition function of the 2-spin system is zero-free in a complex neighbourhood of
\lambda=0
or
\beta\gamma=1
containing a positive point, then the corresponding 2-spin system exhibits SSM.
By a Christoffel-Darboux type identity, it seems like that the implication from zero-free regions to SSM
only works for complex neighbourhoods of
\lambda=0
or
\beta\gamma=1
.
A natural question is
what is the magic of these two special settings of parameters
\lambda=0
and
\beta\gamma=1
for which the exact computation of the partition function is polynomial-time solvable?

In fact, so far all currently known zero-free regions (and even all SSM results and all FPTAS results) for the 2-spin system lie in some complex neighborhoods of
\lambda=0
(up to a switch of the spins
+
and
-
) or
\beta\gamma=1
.

and

.

.

.

.
Suppose that the partition function

.

q

In this section, we extend Christoffel-Darboux type identity to
q
-spin systems.

Such a system is defined on a simple undirected graph
G=(V,E)
and in this way the individual entities comprising the system correspond to the
vertices
V
and their pairwise interactions correspond to the edges
E
.

The system is associated with some parameters including a
q\times q
symmetric matrix of edge activities
A=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq q}\in\mathcal{M}_{q}(\mathbb{C})
that models the tendency of vertices to agree and disagree with their neighbors, and a uniform vertex activity vector
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{q})\in\mathbb{C}^{q}
that models an external field which determines the propensities of a vertex.

A partial configuration of this system refers to a mapping
\sigma:\Lambda\to[q]=\{1,2,\cdots,q\}
for some
\Lambda\subseteq V
which may be empty. It assigns one of the
q
spins to each vertex in
\Lambda
.
When
\Lambda=V
, it is called a configuration and its weight is

\omega(\sigma)=\prod_{v\in V}\lambda_{\sigma(v)}\prod_{(u,v)\in E}a_{\sigma(u),\sigma(v)}.

The
partition function
of a 2-spin system is defined to be

Z_{G}(A,\boldsymbol{\lambda}):=\sum\limits_{\sigma:V\to[q]}w(\sigma).

We also define the partition function conditioning on a pre-described partial configuration
\sigma_{\Lambda}
by

Z^{\sigma_{\Lambda}}_{G}(A,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\sum_{\tau:V\to[q],\tau|_{\Lambda}=\sigma_{\Lambda}}w(\sigma)

where
\tau|_{\Lambda}
denotes the restriction of the configuration
\tau
on
\Lambda
. For
u,v\in V(G),
we define

If
v\in\Lambda
is already pinned to some

, then automatically

and

Given a partial configuration
\sigma_{\Lambda}
, for any
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda,
we denote
\sigma_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
the restriction of
\sigma_{\Lambda}
on
\Lambda^{\prime}

\sigma_{\Lambda}
. In the rest of this paper, we omit the argument
(A,\lambda)
of the partition functions for simplicity.

Abstract
. We discuss a product formula for F F -polynomials in cluster algebras, and provide two proofs. One proof is inductive and uses only the mutation rule for F F -polynomials. The other is based on the Fock-Goncharov decomposition of mutations. We conclude by expanding this product formula as a sum and illustrate applications. This expansion provides an explicit combinatorial computation of F F -polynomials in a given seed that depends only on the 𝐜 {\bf c} -vectors and 𝐠 {\bf g} -vectors along a finite sequence of mutations from the initial seed to the given seed.



1.
Introduction

Cluster algebras are certain commutative algebras with a rich combinatorial
structure, first introduced in
[
FZ02
]
. Their inception
was motivated by the study of semicanonical bases of Lie algebras; but
since then, researchers have made deep connections between cluster algebras
and many other areas of math and physics, including discrete dynamical
systems, Poisson geometry, higher Teichmüller spaces, commutative and
non-commutative algebraic geometry, string theory, and quiver representation
theory; see
[
Kel13
]
for specific references.

The generators of a cluster algebra, called cluster variables, can be naturally described using two pieces of data which were introduced in
[
FZ07
]
, namely
F
-polynomials
and
{\bf g}
-vectors
. More detail is given in Section
2
. Thus,
F
-polynomials play a key part in the study and applications of cluster patterns. In this paper, we draw attention to a less-known product formula for
F
-polynomials applicable to all skew-symmetrizable cluster algebras (Theorem
3.1
) that we refer to as
Gupta’s formula
. Gupta’s formula can also be expanded using the generalized binomial theorem into a summation formula indexed over integer tuples, giving an explicit formula for the coefficients of
F
-polynomials (Theorem
6.2
).

We begin this paper with some basics on cluster algebras,
F
-polynomials,
{\bf c}
-vectors, and
{\bf g}
-vectors in Sections
2.1
and
2.2
. We then recall for the reader the Fock-Goncharov decomposition of mutations
[
FG09
]
in Section
2.3
and reformulate mutations in terms of two automorphisms of the field of rational functions in the initial cluster variables in Section
2.4
. The main theorem follows in Section
3
with two proofs in Sections
4
and
5
. These results hold for an arbitrary initial skew-symmetrizable matrix
B_{t_{0}}
. Finally, we conclude with a second manifestation of our main theorem in Section
6
, this time as a formula involving an infinite sum, rather than as a product formula.

Let us give a brief account of the short history of Gupta’s formula, justifying its name.
As part of her REU project
[
Gup18
]
advised by the second author, Meghal Gupta discovered both the product and summation versions of this
F
-polynomial formula, and gave an elementary proof for it, up to the use of sign-coherence. She then used these two formulas to obtain explicit formulas for
F
-polynomials for cluster patterns associated to the
r
-Kronecker quiver, the affine
\mathcal{A}_{n,1}
quiver and Gale Robinson quivers, and resolved Eager and Franco’s stabilization conjecture
[
EF12
, Sec. 9.5]
for several special cases. Comparing with
[
Gup18
]
, the reader will notice that our current formulation of Gupta’s formula is very different from the original formulation. For example, instead of her usage of
a_{i,j}
,
b_{i,j}
, and
W(n,w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{k})
in
[
Gup18
, Def. 2.15 and Def. 2.16]
, we use dot products and sums involving more familiar cluster algebraic objects such as
{\bf c}
-vectors and
{\bf g}
-vectors.
Gupta’s proof for the product version of her formula (Theorem
3.1
) is embedded in her proof towards the summation version (Theorem
6.2
), but it proceeds by induction in essentially the same manner as the first proof we give, utilizing the same recurrence relation for
F
-polynomials as we do. In particular, Equation (2) from
[
Gup18
, Sec. 3.2]
translates to Theorem
6.2
herein. Our second proof is dissimilar to her approach, relying on the Fock-Goncharov decomposition of mutations into a tropical and a non-tropical part.

Background

Before delving into the statement of Gupta’s formula and its proofs, we will establish essential notation and provide some background on the Fock-Goncharov decomposition of mutations and related automorphisms, closely following
[
Nak23
]
. We assume that the reader is familiar with the basics of cluster algebras, such as the definitions of
F
-polynomials,
{\bf c}
-vectors,
{\bf g}
-vectors in
[
FZ07
]
, but begin by reviewing some of the salient points.

2.1.
The basics (Part 1)

Let
\mathbb{T}_{n}
be the
n
-regular tree graph where the
n
edges attached to each vertex are distinctly labeled by
1,\dots,n
.
We say that a pair of vertices
t
and
t^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
are
k
-adjacent
if they are connected with an edge labeled by
k
.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\mathrm{Trop}(\mathbf{y})
be the tropical semifield generated by
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
. The tropical semifield comes equipped with two binary operations, namely
tropical addition
which is denoted as
\oplus
, and ordinary multiplication of Laurent monomials. The tropical addition of two Laurent monomials in
\mathbb{P}
is defined as

y_{1}^{d_{1}}y_{2}^{d_{2}}\cdots y_{n}^{d_{n}}\oplus y_{1}^{e_{1}}y_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots y_{n}^{e_{n}}=y_{1}^{\min(d_{1},e_{1})}y_{2}^{\min(d_{2},e_{2})}\cdots y_{n}^{\min(d_{n},e_{n})}

for all integers
d_{i}
and
e_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
It is straightforward to show that these two operations turn
\mathbb{P}
into a semifield, meaning that the commutative, associative, and distributive laws are satisfied. Let
\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{P}
be the group ring of the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{P}
over
\mathbb{Z}
,
and let
\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}
be its fraction field.
In this paper, we consider cluster algebras of geometric type, meaning that their coefficients lie in
\mathbb{P}
.

A cluster algebra (of rank
n
) is defined by an initial
seed
with principal coefficients, which is a tuple
\Sigma_{t_{0}}=({\bf x}_{t_{0}},{\bf y}_{t_{0}},B_{t_{0}})
where
t_{0}
is some vertex in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
,
{\bf x}_{t_{0}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is an
n
-tuple of algebraically independent elements in the rational field
\mathbb{QP}({\bf x}_{t_{0}})
,
{\bf y}_{t_{0}}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
is the
n
-tuple of tropical generators of
\mathbb{P}
, and
B_{t_{0}}
is the initial exchange matrix, which is an
n
-by-
n
skew-symmetrizable integer matrix, meaning that there exists a diagonal matrix
D
with positive entries such that
DB_{t_{0}}=-B_{t_{0}}^{T}D
. The
principal extension
of the initial exchange matrix
B_{t_{0}}
is denoted as
\widetilde{B_{t_{0}}}
and it is the
2n
-by-
n
matrix whose top
n
rows is
B_{t_{0}}
and whose bottom
n
rows is an
n
-by-
n
identity matrix.

From the initial seed
\Sigma_{t_{0}}
, we obtain seeds
\Sigma_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
by an iterative process known as
mutation
: given two
k
-adjacent vertices
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, and a seed
\Sigma_{t}=({\bf x}_{t},{\bf y}_{t},B_{t})
with extended exchange matrix
\widetilde{B_{t}}
, by
mutating
\Sigma_{t}
in direction
k
, we obtain a seed
\Sigma_{t^{\prime}}=({\bf x}_{t^{\prime}},{\bf y}_{t^{\prime}},B_{t^{\prime}})
and an extended exchange matrix
\widetilde{B_{t^{\prime}}}
.
This assignment of a seed to each
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
is a
cluster pattern
, written
\mathbf{\Sigma}=\{\Sigma_{t}=({\bf x}_{t},{\bf y}_{t},B_{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}
.
In this process, we also obtain two more families of matrices
C_{t}
and
G_{t}
, known as
C
-,
G
-matrices, as well as a collection of polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
, known as
F
-polynomials. Write
A=(\mathbf{a}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
to mean that
\mathbf{a}_{i}
is the
i
-th column of the
n
-by-
n
matrix
A
. Then given a seed
\Sigma_{t}=({\bf x}_{t},{\bf y}_{t},B_{t})\in\mathbf{\Sigma}
, we write

\displaystyle{\bf x}_{t}

\displaystyle=(x_{1;t},\dots,x_{n;t}),

\displaystyle{\bf y}_{t}

\displaystyle=(y_{1;t},\dots,y_{n;t}),

\displaystyle B_{t}

\displaystyle=({\bf b}_{i;t})_{i=1}^{n}=(b_{ij;t})_{i,j=1}^{n},

\displaystyle C_{t}

\displaystyle=({\bf c}_{i;t})_{i=1}^{n}=(c_{ij;t})_{i,j=1}^{n},

\displaystyle G_{t}

\displaystyle=({\bf g}_{i;t})_{i=1}^{n}=(g_{ij;t})_{i,j=1}^{n}.

For the initial seed
\Sigma_{t_{0}}=({\bf x}_{t_{0}},{\bf y}_{t_{0}},B_{t_{0}})
, we often drop the index
t_{0}
:

\displaystyle{\bf x}_{t_{0}}

\displaystyle={\bf x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

\displaystyle{\bf y}_{t_{0}}

\displaystyle={\bf y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}),

\displaystyle B_{t_{0}}

\displaystyle=B=(b_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}.

Let us say a bit more about how
{\bf x}_{t}
,
{\bf y}_{t}
,
B_{t}
,
C_{t}
,
G_{t}
and
F
-polynomials are obtained via mutations. Let
\mu_{k;t}
denote the mutation map applied to the seed
\Sigma_{t}
in direction
k
. To mutate the exchange matrix
B_{t}=(b_{ij;t})
, we write
[n]_{+}=\max(n,0)
and define
B_{t^{\prime}}=(b_{ij;t^{\prime}})
as follows:

b_{ij;t^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}-b_{ij;t}&\mathrm{~if~}i=k\mathrm{~or~}j=k\\
b_{ij;t}+b_{ik;t}[b_{kj;t}]_{+}+[-b_{ik;t}]_{+}b_{kj;t}&\mathrm{~otherwise.}\end{cases}

By applying mutation to
\widetilde{B_{t}}
, we obtain
\widetilde{B_{t^{\prime}}}
whose the top
n
rows yield
B_{t^{\prime}}
, and the bottom
n
rows yield
C_{t^{\prime}}
, the
C
-matrix associated to
\Sigma_{t^{\prime}}
. Like our initial choice of
B_{t_{0}}
, each exchange matrix
B_{t}
is skew-symmetrizable. We may take a common skew-symmetrizer
D=(d_{1}^{-1},\dots,d_{n}^{-1})
, meaning that
DB_{t}=-B_{t}^{T}D
for all
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, and such that
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
are positive integers. We fix such a
D
from now on.
Note that the integers
d_{i}
’s match the ones in
[
Gro+18
]
in the scattering diagram formalism.

The mutation of
{\bf y}_{t}=(y_{1;t},\dots,y_{n;t})
, as elements of
\mathbb{P}
, is defined as

y_{i;t^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}y_{k;t}^{-1}&\mathrm{~if~}i=k\\
y_{i;t}y_{k;t}^{[b_{ki;t}]_{+}}(1\oplus y_{k;t})^{-b_{ki;t}}&\mathrm{~if~}i\not=k.\end{cases}

A related set of auxiliary variables, the
\widehat{y}
-variables are defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{y}_{i;t}=y_{i;t}\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j;t}^{b_{ji;t}}.

Again for the initial seed, we often drop the index
t_{0}
and write

\widehat{y}_{i;t_{0}}=\widehat{y}_{i}=y_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{b_{ji}}.

The effect of
\mu_{k;t}
on
{\bf x}_{t}
can be understood as an isomorphism of fields of rational functions. If we fix a choice of
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}
,
then the map
\mu_{k;t}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mu_{k;t}:\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}({\bf x}_{t^{\prime}})\quad

\displaystyle\to\quad\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}({\bf x}_{t})

(1)

\displaystyle x_{i;t^{\prime}}\quad

\displaystyle\mapsto\quad\begin{cases}\frac{x_{k;t}^{-1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j;t}^{[-\varepsilon b_{jk;t}]_{+}}\right)~\left(1~+~\widehat{y}_{k;t}^{\varepsilon}\right)}{1~\oplus~y_{k;t}^{\varepsilon}}&i=k,\\
x_{i;t}&i\neq k.\end{cases}

It is easy to see that the definition is independent of the choice of
\varepsilon
. This map allows us to understand each
x_{i;t^{\prime}}
as an element of
\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}({\bf x}_{t})
. Repeated mutations give rise to a map
\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}({\bf x}_{t})\to\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}({\bf x}_{t_{0}})
, which allows us to understand each
x_{i;t}
as an element of
\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}({\bf x}_{t_{0}})
. Since
\mathbb{P}=\mathrm{Trop}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
, we may also view
x_{i;t}
as a rational function (in fact a Laurent polynomial) in
\mathbb{Q}({\bf x}_{t_{0}},{\bf y}_{t_{0}})
such that only monomials in
{\bf x}_{t_{0}}
appear in the denominator.
Note that when iterating mutations, the order of the composition of the maps is opposite to the order of the mutations due to the domain and image of the map in (
1
).

We define the associated
F
-polynomials
F_{i;t}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\bf y}_{t_{0}}]
as the specialization of
x_{i;t}|_{{\bf x}_{t_{0}}=1}
. Based on equation (
1
), i.e. see Prop. 5.1 of
[
FZ07
]
, the
F
-polynomials satisfy the recurrence

(2)

\displaystyle F_{k;t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=F_{k;t}^{-1}\left(\frac{y_{k;t}}{1\oplus y_{k;t}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}F_{j;t}^{[b_{jk;t}]_{+}}+\frac{1}{1\oplus y_{k;t}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}F_{j;t}^{[-b_{jk;t}]_{+}}\right).

Furthermore, we obtain the
g
-vector
\mathbf{g}_{i;t}
as the exponent vectors of the Laurent monomial arising from the specialization
x_{i;t}|_{{\bf y}_{t_{0}}=0}
.
The
G
-matrix
G_{t}
is defined by concatenating the
{\bf g}
-vectors
\mathbf{g}_{1;t}
through
\mathbf{g}_{n;t}
together so that they form its columns.

2.2.
The basics (Part 2)

We remind the reader of some well-known properties that will be useful later in our proofs.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
FZ07
]
, Prop. 6.6)
.

Let
t^{\prime},t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
be
k
-adjacent. Then
{\bf g}_{i;t^{\prime}}={\bf g}_{i;t}
if

and

\displaystyle{\bf g}_{k;t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-{\bf g}_{k;t}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}[c_{jk;t}]_{+}{\bf b}_{j;t_{0}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}[b_{jk;t}]_{+}{\bf g}_{j;t}

\displaystyle=-{\bf g}_{k;t}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}[-c_{jk;t}]_{+}{\bf b}_{j;t_{0}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}[-b_{jk;t}]_{+}{\bf g}_{j;t}.

Theorem 2.2
.

The following holds for any
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

(First duality.
[
FZ07
]
, Eq. (6.14))

G_{t}B_{t}=B_{t_{0}}C_{t};

(Second duality.
[
NZ12
]
, Eq. (3.11).)

D^{-1}G^{T}_{t}DC_{t}=I;

(Sign-coherence.
[
Gro+18
]
, Cor. 5.5) for all
1\leq k\leq n
, either
c_{ik;t}\geq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, or
c_{ik;t}\leq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. In other words, there exists
\varepsilon_{k;t}\in\{-1,1\}
such that
{\bf c}_{k;t}^{+}=\varepsilon_{k;t}{\bf c}_{k;t}
consists of only non-negative entries.

The first duality motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\widehat{C}_{t}=G_{t}B_{t}=B_{t_{0}}C_{t}
, and let
\widehat{C}_{t}=(\widehat{{\bf c}}_{j;t})_{j=1}^{n}
. Let
\widehat{{\bf c}}_{j;t}^{+}=\varepsilon_{j;t}\widehat{{\bf c}}_{j;t}
.

Contrary to the case of the
{\bf c}^{+}
-vectors, as we will see in Example
3.3
, we do not expect the entries of
\widehat{{\bf c}}_{j}^{+}
to be all non-negative.

Additionally, the presence of the skew-symmetrizer

in the second duality motivates us to consider the inner product
({\bf u},{\bf v})_{D}={\bf u}^{T}D{\bf v}
. We observe that
({\bf u},{\bf v})_{D}=({\bf v},{\bf u})_{D}
, and

(3)

({\bf u},B_{t}{\bf v})_{D}={\bf u}^{T}DB_{t}{\bf v}=-{\bf u}^{T}B_{t}^{T}D{\bf v}=-(B_{t}{\bf u},{\bf v})_{D}

for all
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

Using this inner product, the second duality can be equivalently stated as follows in terms of individual
{\bf g}
-vectors and
{\bf c}
-vectors: for
1\leq i,j\leq n
and any
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
,

(4)

\displaystyle({\bf g}_{i;t},d_{j}{\bf c}_{j;t})_{D}=\delta_{i,j}.

Sign-Coherence allows us to rewrite Proposition
2.1
in a simpler form.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
t^{\prime},t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
be
k
-adjacent. Then
{\bf g}_{i;t^{\prime}}={\bf g}_{i;t}
if

and

\displaystyle{\bf g}_{k;t^{\prime}}=-{\bf g}_{k;t}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}[-\varepsilon_{k;t}b_{jk;t}]_{+}{\bf g}_{j;t}.

2.3.
Fock-Goncharov decomposition for principal coeffcients

Following
[
Nak23
]
, which builds off
[
FG09
]
in the
\varepsilon=1
case,
we consider the following decomposition of the map
\mu_{k;t}
:

(5)

\displaystyle\mu_{k;t}=\rho_{k;t}\circ\tau_{k;t},

where the map
\tau_{k;t}
is the following isomorphism,

\displaystyle\tau_{k;t}:\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{x}_{t^{\prime}})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{x}_{t}),

\displaystyle\tau_{k;t}(x_{i;t^{\prime}})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle x_{k;t}^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j;t}^{[-\varepsilon_{k;t}b_{jk;t}]_{+}}&i=k,\\
x_{i;t}&i\neq k,\end{cases}

while the map
\rho_{k;t}
is the following automorphism,

\displaystyle\rho_{k;t}:\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{x}_{t}),

\displaystyle\rho_{k;t}(x_{i;t})=x_{i;t}(1+\widehat{y}_{k;t}^{\varepsilon_{k;t}})^{-\delta_{i,k}}.

We call the decomposition (
5
) the
Fock-Goncharov decomposition

of a mutation
{\mu}_{k;t}
(
with tropical sign
) with respect to the initial vertex
t_{0}
.
We also call
\tau_{k;t}
and
\rho_{k;t}
the
tropical part

and the

of

, respectively. See Section 4 of
[
Nak23
]
for more information. Comparing this decomposition with our definition of
\mu_{k;t}
in (

to be the tropical sign

and hence the denominator

The involution property of the mutations of cluster variables
{\bf x}_{t}
and
{\bf g}
-vectors

is restated as follows.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

be vertices which are
k
-adjacent.
Then, the following relations hold.

\displaystyle\mu_{k;t^{\prime}}\circ\mu_{k;t}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id},

\displaystyle\tau_{k;t^{\prime}}\circ\tau_{k;t}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id}.

Proof.

Here, the first equality
is nothing but the involution of the mutations of
x
-variables.
The second equality
simply relies on the two identities

\varepsilon_{k;t^{\prime}}=-\varepsilon_{k;t}\mathrm{~and~}b_{jk;t^{\prime}}=-b_{jk;t}
.
∎

For any

,
consider a sequence of vertices

,

, …,

\displaystyle:=\mu_{k_{0};t_{0}}\circ\mu_{k_{1};t_{1}}\circ\dots\circ\mu_{k_{r};t_{r}}:\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{x}),

2.4.
Nontropical parts and

Observing Proposition

Let us make this statement more manifest, following

Let us introduce the following automorphisms

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}_{k;t}:\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle{\bf x}^{\mathbf{m}}

\displaystyle\mapsto{\bf x}^{\mathbf{m}}(1+\widehat{{\bf y}}^{\mathbf{c}_{k;t}^{+}})^{-(\mathbf{m},d_{k}\mathbf{c}_{k;t})_{D}},\text{ $\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}$},

3.

We are now ready to state the main theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(Gupta’s Formula)
.

Given a mutation sequence
\mu_{i_{1}}\mu_{i_{2}}\dots
, let

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{q}^{t_{0}}_{t_{j-1}}\left((1+z_{j})^{({\bf g}_{(\ell)},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}\right)

\displaystyle=(1+\mathfrak{q}^{t_{0}}_{t_{j-1}}(z_{j}))^{({\bf g}_{(\ell)},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}

\displaystyle=\left(1+z_{j}\prod_{k=1}^{j-1}L_{k}^{(\widehat{{\bf c}}_{(j)}^{+},d_{(k)}{\bf c}_{(k)})_{D}}\right)^{({\bf g}_{(\ell)},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}

\displaystyle=L_{j}^{({\bf g}_{(\ell)},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{t_{0}}_{t_{\ell}}({\bf x}^{{\bf g}_{(\ell)}})}{{\bf x}^{{\bf g}_{(\ell)}}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{t_{0}}_{t_{j}}({\bf x}^{{\bf g}_{(\ell)}})}{\mathfrak{q}^{t_{0}}_{t_{j-1}}({\bf x}^{{\bf g}_{(\ell)}})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}L_{j}^{({\bf g}_{(\ell)},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}

as desired.

∎

6.
Expansion of Gupta’s Formula

One may wish to expand the product formula given in Theorem
3.1
into a sum. To do so, we first prove a lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Given integers
h_{1},\dots,h_{\ell}
and the same setup as Theorem
3.1
,

\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}L_{j}^{h_{j}}=\sum_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{h_{j}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{\ell}m_{k}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}{m_{j}}\widehat{{\bf y}}^{\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}m_{j}{\bf c}_{(j)}^{+}}.

Proof.

We prove the following claim by induction: for all
1\leq p\leq\ell
,

(14)

\prod_{j=p}^{\ell}L_{j}^{h_{j}}=\sum_{(m_{p},\dots,m_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}L_{j}^{\sum_{k=p}^{\ell}m_{k}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}\prod_{j=p}^{\ell}\binom{h_{j}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{\ell}m_{k}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}{m_{j}}\widehat{{\bf y}}^{\sum_{j=p}^{\ell}m_{j}{\bf c}_{(j)}^{+}}.

When
p=1
, this claim specializes to our lemma.

First note that by the Generalized Binomial Theorem, for any
h\in\mathbb{Z}
, we can write

\displaystyle L_{k}^{h}

\displaystyle=\left(1+z_{k}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}L_{j}^{(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}\right)^{h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\binom{h}{m}z_{k}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}L_{j}^{m(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}

(15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}L_{j}^{m(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}\binom{h}{m}\widehat{{\bf y}}^{m{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+}}.

If we let
k=\ell
and
h=h_{\ell}
, the above is precisely the base case
p=\ell
of our claim.

Now suppose that (
14
) is true for
p+1
. Multiplying both sides by
L_{p}^{h_{p}}
, we get

\displaystyle\prod_{j=p}^{\ell}L_{j}^{h_{j}}=\sum_{(m_{p+1},\dots,m_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\

\displaystyle\bigg(\ \prod_{j=1}^{p-1}L_{j}^{\sum_{k=p+1}^{\ell}m_{k}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}L_{p}^{h_{p}+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\ell}m_{k}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(p)}{\bf c}_{(p)})_{D}}

(16)

\displaystyle\prod_{j=p+1}^{\ell}\binom{h_{j}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{\ell}m_{k}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}{m_{j}}\widehat{{\bf y}}^{\sum_{j=p+1}^{\ell}m_{j}{\bf c}_{(j)}^{+}}\bigg).

Letting
k=p
and
h=h_{p}+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\ell}m_{k}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(p)}{\bf c}_{(p)})_{D}
, our computation in Eq. (
15
) allows us to expand the middle term of
16
:

L_{p}^{h_{p}+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\ell}m_{k}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{{(p)}}{\bf c}_{(p)})_{D}}=\sum_{m_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}L_{j}^{m_{p}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(p)}^{+},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}\binom{h_{p}+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\ell}m_{k}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{{(p)}}{\bf c}_{(p)})_{D}}{m_{p}}\widehat{{\bf y}}^{m_{p}{\bf c}_{(p)}^{+}}.

Therefore, Eq. (
16
) reorganizes into

\prod_{j=p}^{\ell}L_{j}^{h_{j}}=\sum_{(m_{p},\dots,m_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}L_{j}^{\sum_{k=p}^{\ell}m_{k}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}\prod_{j=p}^{\ell}\binom{h_{j}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{\ell}m_{k}(\widehat{\bf c}_{(k)}^{+},d_{(j)}{\bf c}_{(j)})_{D}}{m_{j}}\widehat{{\bf y}}^{\sum_{j=p}^{\ell}m_{j}{\bf c}_{(j)}^{+}}

as desired. This completes our proof.
∎

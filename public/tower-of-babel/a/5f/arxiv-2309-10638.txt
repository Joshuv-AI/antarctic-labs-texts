Partial triangulations of surfaces with girth constraints Thanks: MSC2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 05C10, 52C25 August 9, 2026
Stephen C. Power

Abstract
. Barnette and Edelson have shown that there are finitely many minimal triangulations of a connected compact 2-manifold ℳ {\mathcal{M}} . Similar finiteness results are obtained for cellular partial triangulations that satisfy various girth inequality constraints for embedded cycles. A characterisation of various ℳ {\mathcal{M}} -embedded sparse graphs is given in terms of the satisfaction of higher genus girth inequalities. With this it is shown that there are finitely many contraction-minimal ℳ {\mathcal{M}} -embedded graphs that are ( 3 , 6 ) (3,6) -tight or ( 3 , 3 ) (3,3) -tight.

A compact 2-manifold

Two triangulations are regarded as the same if their embeddings are unique up to a homeomorphism.
The embedded graph definition of a triangulation does not require the stricter condition that each face is uniquely associated with its boundary 3-cycle. Thus the unique minimal triangulation of the sphere in our sense is

We also note that Barnette

One can view the

An embedded graph in the sphere is well-known to be

2.
The Barnette-Edelson theorem.

We give a proof of Theorem
1.1
that follows the elegant proof in
[
3
]

The induction scheme of the proof is outlined in the next paragraph and we use a similar inductions for Proposition

Proof scheme.
A 3-cycle of
T
is
planar
if it is the boundary of an embedded open disc in
{\mathcal{M}}
and is
nonplanar
otherwise.
The surface
{\mathcal{M}}
is cut by a nonplanar 3-cycle of the triangulation
T
. If this 3-cycle has a small closed neighbourhood that is an annulus then the resulting 1 or 2 open manifolds,
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
, or
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\prime}
, determine compact surfaces with boundary, each component of the boundary having an embedded 3-cycle.
If the cutting 3-cycle has a Möbius strip neighbourhood in
{\mathcal{M}}
then the resulting compact surfaces with boundary have an embedded 6-cycle in each boundary component.
In both cases, by capping the boundary components with a
topological disc one obtains 1 or 2 compact surfaces. These lower genus surfaces, say
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
, or
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
, have triangulations,
T_{1}
, or
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
, that are derived from

and from small triangulations of the capping discs.
A capping disc for the cutting cycle has the form of an attached triangle or an attached triangulated 6-cycle. For a minimal triangulation
T
, the derived triangulations need not be minimal and the crux of the proof is to show that they are close to minimal in the following sense. The number of edges of
T_{1}
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
that do not lie on an essential 3-cycle in
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
is bounded (with bound depending only on the genus of
{\mathcal{M}}
).
These constraints ensure that the derived triangulations are close to minimal, in the sense of Lemma
2.4
, where

has a genus bound.

The following lemma is Lemma 6 of
[
2
]
. See also Lemma 6 of
[
4
]
. A
shrinkable

is one that does not lie on a 3-cycle of edges other than the two facial 3-cycles that contain the edge. In this case there is a natural contraction
G/e
of

, obtained by contracting

to a single vertex and contracting each incident face to an edge.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
e
is an edge in a triangulation
T
of a compact 2-manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
and if

lies
on four distinct nonplanar 3-cycles, all homotopic to each other, then

has a shrinkable edge.

The idea of the proof given in

is that the hypotheses imply there is a triangulated planar patch of

with an edge that is sufficiently interior to this patch that it cannot lie on a nonfacial 3-cycle.
In Lemma

It is well-known that a nonfacial planar 3-cycle

, implies the existence of a contractible edge that is inside

The next theorem gives a key topological fact. See also Malnic̆ and Mohar

Theorem 2.2

If

is not the sphere or projective plane then any family

of homotopically nontrivial simple pairwise nonhomotopic curves, meeting only at a common point in

The theorem is Corollary 1 of Barnette and Edleson

Recall that the genus

of

is the maximum number of disjoint loops in

whose union has a connected complement in
{\mathcal{M}}
. We say that a set associated with an embedded graph in a family of

-embedded graphs, has a

With the following corollary
we may bypass some lemmas from
[

]
. It plays a key role in the induction step of the proof scheme.

Corollary 2.3
.

There is a constant

such that the degree of a vertex in a minimal triangulation

of the surface
{\mathcal{M}}
is no greater than
cg
where
g
is the genus of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that

has degree

corresponding to the union of the triangles of

By Lemma

there must be at least

of these 3-cycles that are pairwise nonhomotopic.
By shrinking

to

, by a path of homeomorphisms of

, we see that the peripheral embedded edges of these 3-cycles determine a set of loops at

∎

We next give a proof of Theorem
1.1
assuming Theorem
2.2
and follow this with a proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
T^{\prime}
be a triangulation obtained from the triangulation
T
by shrinking a single edge. Then the reverse operation,

, is referred to as a
vertex-splitting move
, or, more precisely, a
planar vertex-splitting move
. The following simple lemma plays a role in the induction argument of the proof.
It also features in Theorem

.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
T
be a triangulation of a compact manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
for which there are exactly
n
edges that do not lie on a nonfacial 3-cycle. Then
T
is obtained from a minimal triangulation by a sequence of at most
n
vertex-splitting moves.

Proof.

Observe first that if
T\to T^{\prime}
is an edge shrinking move between triangulations and some edge

in
T
lies on a nonfacial 3-cycle of
T
, then the corresponding edge in
T^{\prime}
once again lies on nonfacial 3-cycle. Thus shrinking an edge that does not lie on a nonfacial 3-cycle strictly decreases the number of edges failing to lie on a nonfacial 3-cycle. The edge shrinking move and the planar vertex-splitting moves are inverse operations on the set of triangulations of
{\mathcal{M}}
and so the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be orientable with genus

and suppose that the number of minimal triangulations of a compact 2-manifold with lower genus
is finite.
Cutting
{\mathcal{M}}
with a 3-cycle that is not homotopic in

to a point
we obtain 1 or 2 manifolds with boundary. Capping boundaries, as indicated above, yields a compact 2-manifold

and

, each of which has genus smaller than

Suppose first that there is one derived manifold

. It carries an inherited triangulation

which need not be minimal. A shrinkable edge,

has the property that it lies on a 3-cycle in

which passes through a vertex of the cutting 3-cycle.
By Lemma

the degrees of these vertices,

and

for some constant

It follows that the number of such 3-cycles, and therefore the number of shrinkable edges

is finite. By

the number of triangulations

of

This argument establishes the induction step in the orientable case with cut cycle giving a connected manifold

and so, since there is one minimal triangulation for the sphere the theorem follows for orientable 2-manifolds.

In the nonorientable case the capping of the boundaries of

(or

The proof of Theorem
2.2
depends on the following extension theorem which shows that we may assume that
the (nonsimple) embedded graph associated with
{\mathcal{E}}
is
cellular
.

We make use of the following terms. An embedded graph
G
in a compact 2-manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
is
cellular
if each of its faces is homeomorphic to an open disc. The
genus

g(U)
of a general face

is the maximum number of disjoint loops in
U
that do not disconnect
U
.
A loop edge
e
in a surface is

if an associated simple closed curve for it, say
\alpha(t),0\leq t\leq 1
, is not null-homotopic. Also, a pair of loops
e_{1},e_{2}
with curves
\alpha,\beta
with
\alpha(0)=\beta(0)=v
is a homotopic pair of loops if
\{\alpha,\beta\}
or
\{\alpha,-\beta\}
is a homotopic pair.
Loops in a surface are locally 2-sided. If both sides of a loop edge

belong to the same face we say that

is a 1-sided edge, otherwise

is called a 2-sided edge. By the number of edges of a face

we mean the number of 2-sided edges in

U
plus twice the number of 1-sided edges in the boundary.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{M}}
is not the sphere or the projective plane. Then any family
{\mathcal{E}}=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{r}\}
of pairwise nonhomotopic essential loops, meeting only at a common point
v
, extends to a similar family
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
whose embedded graph
G(\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}})

is cellular with each face having at least 3 edges.

Proof sketch.

Let
U
be a face of the embedded multigraph
G({\mathcal{E}})
of
{\mathcal{E}}
.
The idea of the proof is to introduce a loop in
U\cup\{v\}
in order to extend
{\mathcal{E}}
to a similar such family where
U
has been replaced by 1 or 2 faces that have lower genus.

If
g(U)
is positive then
viewing

as an open surface of finite genus, there exists a loop in
U
that passes through either
a handle or a cross cap of
U
.
This loop may modified to give a loop,

say, in
\{v\}\cup U
, with the relative topology, that passes through
v
and the handle or cross cap.
It follows that
e_{*}
is an essential loop in
{\mathcal{M}}
. Also it is not homotopic to any loop of
{\mathcal{E}}
that is in the closure of
U
. This follows formally on considering the fundamental group of the closure of

in
{\mathcal{M}}
. Further considerations shows that
e_{*}
is not homotopic to any loop in
{\mathcal{E}}
.
For the extended set
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{E}}\cup\{e_{*}\}
with embedded graph
G({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})
the face

has been replaced by 1 or 2 faces and it follows from the definition of

that they have strictly lower genus.

Repeating this argument sufficiently often leads to an extended set
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
for which the associated embedded graph has all faces
of genus 0. Further examination, together with the assumption that

is not the projective plane, shows that each face has at least 3 edges.
∎

The general Euler formulae for an embedded graph

are

v-e+f=2-2g\quad\mbox{and}\quad v-e+f=2-g,

for, respectively, the case of orientable and nonorientable

, where

denote the number of vertices, edges and faces of

is defined to be the maximum number of disjoint loops whose union has a connected complement in

. Moreover, it can be defined in terms of the standard models for

as the number of attached handles to a sphere when

is orientable, and as the number of attached cross caps to a sphere when

is not orientable.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
|{\mathcal{E}}|=n
. By the previous theorem we may assume that the embedded graph determined by
{\mathcal{E}}
, with a single vertex and

embedded edges, is cellular and the boundary of each face is the union of at least 3 embedded edges.
By Euler’s formula, for orientable

, we have
1-e+f=2-2g
. Also,
2e=\sum_{k}kf_{k}
where

is the number of faces with

edges. Thus
2e\geq 3f,

e=1+f+2g-2\leq 2e/3+2g-1

and so

. The genus bound in the nonorientable case is obtained in the same way.
∎

3.
Sparse graphs and girth inequalities

Our main interest concerns various families of embedded sparse graphs that satisfy the Maxwell count
3v-e=6
. However we also consider here families with
3v-e=\alpha
for general values of
\alpha
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a cellular embedded graph in a compact 2-manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
that satisfies the global count
3v-e=\alpha
.
Then

\sum(k-3)f_{k}=\alpha+3\mu g-6

where
\mu=2
if
{\mathcal{M}}
is orientable and
\mu=1
otherwise.

Proof.

We have
2e=\sum_{k}kf_{k}
and
3(2-\mu g)=3v-3e+3f
and so

6-3\mu g=e+\alpha-3e+3\sum f_{k}=\alpha-\sum kf_{k}+3\sum f_{k},

and the identity follows.
∎

In particular, for such cellular graphs there is a bound on both the size and number of faces with more than 3 edges and this bound depends only on
\alpha
and the genus of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}})
be a family of simple embedded graphs for the compact 2-manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
.
Then
G
in
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}})
is said to be
contraction-minimal
if for every edge
e
belonging to two facial 3-cycles, and no other 3-cycles, the contracted embedded graph
G/e
does not belong to
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}})
.

The Barnette-Edelson theorem shows that there are finitely many contraction-minimal embedded graphs in the family
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}})
of triangulations of
{\mathcal{M}}
. For a simple corollary, consider the family
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}},n_{4},\dots,n_{r})
of
partial triangulations
that are cellular embedded simple graphs
G
in
{\mathcal{M}}
that have
n_{k}
faces with closed boundary walks of length
k
, for
4\leq k\leq r
.
We extend earlier terminology by defining a 3-cycle of
G
to be
planar
if it is the boundary cycle of an open disc in
{\mathcal{M}}
that contains no nontriangular faces of
G
.
In particular, a 3-cycle that is not planar is an essential 3-cycle.

Theorem 3.2
.

There are finitely many contraction-minimal embedded graphs in the family

{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}},n_{4},\dots,n_{r})
.

Proof.

Let
G
be contraction-minimal in
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}},n_{4},\dots,n_{r})
. Then every edge on 2 facial 3-cycles of
G
lies on a nonfacial 3-cycle of
G
. Add a vertex and
k
edges to each face of
G
with a closed boundary walk of length
k\geq 4
to obtain an embedded simple graph
G^{+}
in
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}})
. Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be the set of these added edges together with the set of edges that belongs to these boundary walks of
G
.
Then
|{\mathcal{S}}|
is no greater than
N=2n_{4}+\dots+2n_{r}
. Any other edge of
G^{+}
is an edge of
G
in the boundaries of 2 adjacent facial 3-cycles and so must lie on a nonfacial 3-cycle of
G
.
It follows from Lemma
2.4
that
G^{+}
is obtained from a minimal triangulation of
{\mathcal{M}}
by at most
N
vertex-splitting moves and so, by the Barnette-Edelson Theorem, the embedded graphs
G^{+}
, and hence the embedded graphs
G
, are finite in number.
∎

3.1.
Girth inequalities.

Let
c
be a closed walk in an
{\mathcal{M}}
-embedded simple graph
G
. Then

c
is a
planar type closed walk
if, as an embedded graph,
c
has a face
U
, homeomorphic to the open disc, for which

is the boundary walk. We refer to

as a

.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a simple embedded cellular graph for the compact 2-manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
with nontriangular faces

.
Then a planar type closed walk

in
G
with cellular face

containing a subset of the nontriangular faces
satisfies the girth inequality
if

|c|-3\geq\sum_{k\in I(U)}(|c_{k}|-3),

where
I(U)
is the index set for the boundary walks

of the faces

that are contained in
U
. When equality holds

is said to be a
critical planar type closed walk for
G
.

Definition 3.4
.

A cellular embedded graph
G
in a compact 2-manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies the
planar girth inequalities
if every planar type closed walk
c
in
G
with cellular face

satisfies the girth inequality.

Write
{\mathcal{G}}_{\rm pl}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)
for the family of simple cellular embedded graphs
G
in
{\mathcal{M}}
that satisfy the planar girth inequalities and the freedom count
f(G)=3v-e=\alpha
.
Lemma

implies that for values

this family can be empty for low genus surfaces

.

For a planar type walk

Lemma 3.7

, for

. Note especially the fact that
every
set
W
in
{\mathcal{U}}
with
W\backslash U_{0}
nonempty now has at least one fewer uncoloured edges.

Since
r_{1}
has an independent bound there is an independent bound for number of edges in the boundary of the closed union,
K
say, of the sets
U_{i}
, for
0\leq i\leq r_{1}
.
Consider now the triangular faces, denoted
T_{r_{1}+1},\dots,T_{r_{2}}
, that are incident to the boundary edges of
K
and are uncoloured and lie in some set in
{\mathcal{U}}
. If there are no such faces then, by the previous lemma
{\mathcal{U}}
has an independent bound, otherwise choose sets
U_{j}
that contain
T_{j}
, for
r_{1}+1,\dots,r_{2}
, where
r_{2}
has an independent bound. Colour all the triangular faces contained in the new sets
U_{j}
.
Following this colouring, every set
U
in
{\mathcal{U}}
is either fully coloured or has fewer uncoloured triangular faces than it had before this colouring.

It follows from Lemma
4.4
that after a finite number of repetitions of this selection and colouring process stops, with an independent bound on the number of repetitions. Thus every set of
{\mathcal{U}}
is contained in the union of the resulting sets
U_{i}
and the size of this union has an independent bound. Since there are finitely many subsets of nontriangular faces, the lemma follows.
∎

The proof of the next proposition follows the proof scheme in Section
2
of the Barnette-Edelson Theorem.

Proposition 4.6
.

There is an independent bound for the number of essential 3-cycles in a contraction-minimal graph in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\rm pl}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)
.

Proof.

Assume that
{\mathcal{M}}
has genus
g
and is not the sphere or the projective plane and that
G
is a contraction minimal graph in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\rm pl}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)
. By Lemmas
4.4
,
4.5

we may assume that there exists an essential 3-cycle
c
and that no vertex of
c
lies on the boundary walk of a nontriangular face.
For the induction step of the proof assume that for all compact 2-manifolds
{\mathcal{N}}
of genus less than
g
there is an independent bound for the number of vertices of a contraction-minimal embedded graph in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\rm pl}({\mathcal{N}},\alpha)
.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, cut and cap
{\mathcal{M}}
by
c
to create the surface
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
(or pair of surfaces
{\mathcal{M}}_{1},{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
) and the embedded graph
G_{1}
(or pair
G_{1},G_{2}
). Suppose first that there is a single connected surface
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
with embedded graph
G_{1}
and that
G
is orientable. We have
f(G)=\alpha
and so
f(G_{1})=\alpha+6
.
The embedded graph
G_{1}
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
is cellular and it satisfies the planar girth inequalities since any critical walk
d
for
G_{1}
with open disc face
U
containing nontriangular faces of
G_{1}
is also a critical walk for
G
for the same nontriangular faces as they appear in
G
. Thus
G_{1}
is in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\rm pl}({\mathcal{M}}_{1},\alpha+6)
.
If
e
is an edge of
G_{1}
that is not an edge of the capping 3-cycle and if
e
is contractible, with
G_{1}/e
in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\rm pl}({\mathcal{M}}_{1},\alpha+6)
, then, as an edge of the contraction minimal graph
G
it must lie on an essential 3-cycle or critical walk that passes through a vertex of
c
. Since the degrees of the vertices of
G
have an independent bound, by Lemma
4.3
, it follows that the number of such edges has a uniform bound. The theorem follows in this case, as in the proof of Lemma
2.4
for almost minimal triangulations.

The same argument holds for the case that
{\mathcal{M}}
is orientable and the cut leads to 2 surfaces
{\mathcal{M}}_{1},{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
, and the nonorientable cases are entirely similar.
∎

We may now complete the proof of Theorem
4.1
, that there are finitely many contraction-minimal embedded graphs
G
, with
f(G)=\alpha
, that satisfy the planar type girth inequalities.
Indeed, there is an independent bound for the number of edges that are boundary edges for a nontriangular face, and every other edge of
G
lies on an essential 3-cycle or a critical walk and Lemma
4.5
and Proposition
4.6
provide independent bounds for these.

5.
Higher genus girth conditions and (3,6)-tight graphs

In this section we define and discuss higher genus girth conditions and characterise cellular
(3,6)
-tight embedded graphs in these terms.

Let
G\subset{\mathcal{M}}
be an embedded graph in a compact connected 2-manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
. A
superface

U
of
G
is a face of a subgraph
K
of
G
where
U
is not dense and
K
has no vertices of degree 0 or 1. The nondensity condition holds if and only if the complement of
U
contains a face of
G
.

Let
G_{U}
be the embedded subgraph given by the vertices and edges of
G
that lie in the closure
U^{-}
of the superface
U
. Also, let
G_{U}^{c}
denote the embedded graph
determined by the edges of
G
that are disjoint from
U
.
In particular,
G=G_{U}\cup G_{U}^{c}
and
G_{U}\cap G_{U}^{c}
is the embedded graph
\partial U
whose edges are the boundary edges of
U
. We assume that
\alpha\geq 3
.

A
balanced
superface of
G
is a superface
U
for which the complement of the closure of
U
is nonempty and connected. A
simple
superface
U
is a superface whose boundary consists of disjoint cycles.
For a simple superface
U
with disjoint cycles
d_{1},\dots,d_{s}
forming the boundary, the closure of
U
is a surface with boundary, and so the genus of
U
, denoted
g(U)
, is well-defined as the genus of this closure,
U^{-}
. Alternatively,
g(U)
is the genus of the surface
{\mathcal{M}}_{U}
obtained by attaching a disc to each boundary cycle.

The
reduced genus

g_{r}({\mathcal{M}})
is defined to be
g({\mathcal{M}})
if
{\mathcal{M}}
is oriented and to be
g({\mathcal{M}})/2
if
{\mathcal{M}}
is not oriented. If
U
is a balanced simple superface with complementary superface
W
, and boundary cycles
d_{1},\dots,d_{s}
then we have the following addition formula.

g_{r}({\mathcal{M}})=g_{r}({\mathcal{M}}_{U})+g_{r}({\mathcal{M}}_{W})+(s-1).

This may be verified by considering a triangulation of
{\mathcal{M}}
incorporating the edges of the boundary cycles of
U
and the associated equation for the Euler characteristic, namely

\chi=v-e+f=2-2g_{r}({\mathcal{M}}).

It follows from Lemma
3.1
that if
G
is a cellularly embedded graph with
f(G)=3v-e=\alpha
and nontriangular faces,
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}
, with closed boundary walks
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
, then

\sum_{k}(|c_{k}|-3)=6g_{r}({\mathcal{M}})+f(G)-6.

We refer to this as the
face walk equation
for
G
. It shows that there is an independent bound for the lengths
|c_{k}|
and for
n
, depending only on the genus of
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\alpha
.

If
U
is a balanced and simple superface of
G
with complementary superface
W
(that is, the complement of the closure of
U
) then we may consider the embedded subgraph
G_{W}
also as an embedded graph in the 2-manifold
{\mathcal{M}}_{W}
.
Then the face walk equation for
G_{W}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{W}
takes the form

\sum_{k=1}^{s}(|d_{k}|-3)+\sum_{k\in I(W)}(|c_{k}|-3)=6g_{r}({\mathcal{M}}_{W})+f(G_{W})-6.

where
I(W)
is the set of indices
k
for which
U_{k}
is contained in
W
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be a cellular
{\mathcal{M}}
-embedded graph with
f(G)=\alpha
, let
U
be a balanced simple superface of
G
with complementary superface
W
, and let
d_{1},\dots,d_{s}
be the common boundary cycles of
U
and
W
. Then the following inequalities are equivalent.

(i)
f(G_{W})\geq\alpha
.

(ii)

\sum_{k=1}^{s}(|d_{k}|-3)\geq 6g_{r}(W)-\sum_{k\in I(W)}(|c_{k}|-3)+(\alpha-6).

(iii)

\sum_{k=1}^{s}(|d_{k}|-3)\geq\sum_{k\in I(U)}(|c_{k}|-3)-6(g_{r}(U)+s-1)-(\alpha-6).

Proof.

That (i) is equivalent to (ii) follows from the face walk equation for
G_{W}
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{W}
. The equivalence of (ii) and (iii) follows from the face walk equation for
G
together with the addition formula for the reduced genus.
∎

A balanced simple superface
U
in
G
with boundary cycle set
\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
is said to satisfy the
higher genus girth inequality
if the inequality in Lemma
5.1
(iii) holds.
The lemma ensures that this is equivalent to the
\alpha
-sparsity of the complementary graph
G_{W}
.
Also
U
is a
critical superface
of
G
if this inequality is an equality. In this case we also say that the cycle set is a
critical cycle set
for
U
. We now define these properties for a general superface using an appropriate definition of reduced genus.

A general superface
U^{\prime}
of an embedded graph
G\subset{\mathcal{M}}
has a set of closed walks, say
\{d_{1}^{\prime},\dots,d_{s}^{\prime}\}
.
Also
U^{\prime}
is the face of a subgraph
H
with no vertices of degree 0 or 1, and so consecutive edges of the boundary walk, say
xv,vy
, are distinct with
x\neq y
. We may therefore effect a vertex-splitting move at
v
that creates a new vertex
w
located in
U^{\prime}
together with embedded edges
xw,vw,yw
and the facial 3-cycles
xvw,vwy
.
This move creates a new
{\mathcal{M}}
-embedded graph
G_{1}
containing
G
together with a subgraph
H_{1}
and face
U^{\prime}_{1}\subset U^{\prime}
. Moreover
w
appears once only on the boundary walk for
U_{1}^{\prime}

and appears on no other boundary walk of
H_{1}
. Repeating
such vertex splitting operations leads to a simple superface
U
, with
U\subset U^{\prime}
, that is the face of an embedded graph
H_{n}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
whose closed boundary walks are cycles.
Define
U^{\prime}
and
\{d_{1}^{\prime},\dots,d_{s}^{\prime}\}
to be
critical

if
U
or, equivalently, its set of boundary cycles, say
\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
, is critical.

Noting that in general the closure of a superface
U^{\prime}
is not a surface with disjoint boundary cycles we
define
the reduced genus
g_{r}(U^{\prime})
to be equal to
g_{r}(U)
.

In view of the Lemma
5.1
and noting that the lengths of
d_{i}
and
d_{i}^{\prime}
agree for each
i
, we now give the definition of a general critical superface and the associated notion of a
critical edge
of
G
.

Definition 5.2
.

(i) A superface
U
of the (3,6)-tight embedded graph
G
, with boundary walks
\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
, satisfies the
higher genus girth inequality
if

\sum_{k=1}^{s}(|d_{k}|-3)\geq\sum_{k\in I(U)}(|c_{k}|-3)-6(g_{r}(U)+s-1)-(\alpha-6)

and is a
critical superface
if equality holds.

(ii) An edge
e
of
G
is a
critical edge
if it is incident to 2 triangular faces and at least one of the following holds:
(a)
e
lies on an essential 3-cycle, that is, one whose curve is not null-homotopic.
(b)
e
lies on one of the boundary walks of a critical superface.

Suppose now that
U
is a superface that is not balanced, so that the complementary open set
W={\mathcal{M}}\backslash U^{-}
has components
W_{1},\dots,W_{\kappa}
, with
\kappa\geq 2
. Then, with the same proof we have the following generalisation of Lemma
5.1
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G
be a cellular
{\mathcal{M}}
-embedded graph with
f(G)=\alpha
and let
U
be a superface of
G
with boundary walks
d_{1},\dots,d_{s}
and where the complement
W
of
U^{-}
has connected components
W_{1},\dots,W_{\kappa}
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)
\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}f(G_{W_{i}})\geq\kappa\alpha
.

(ii)
U
satisfies the higher genus girth inequality.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
G
be a cellular
{\mathcal{M}}
-embedded graph with
f(G)=\alpha
and suppose that
f(G_{W})\geq\alpha
for all balanced superfaces of
G
. Then
f(G_{U})\geq\alpha
for each superface
U
of
G
.

Proof.

To prove the equivalence we may assume that the boundary of
U
consists of disjoint cycles.
To see this note that the superface
U^{\prime}
for the embedded graph
G^{\prime}\supset G
obtained by vertex splitting on the boundary
\partial U
satisfies

f(G_{U^{\prime}})=f(G_{U})
.

Let

W_{1},\dots,W_{\kappa}
be the connected components of the complement of
U^{-}
and let

U_{i}^{\prime}=U\cup\bigcup_{j\neq i}W_{j}.

Then
U_{i}
is a balanced superface and
f(G_{U_{i}})\geq\alpha
by assumption.
Let
a=f(G_{U})
and
a_{i}=f(G_{W_{i}})-f(\partial W_{i})
, for
1\leq i\leq\kappa
. Then

a+\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}a_{i}=f(G)=\alpha\quad\mbox{and}\quad a+\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}=f(G_{U_{i}})\geq\alpha.

Adding the inequalities and substituting
\alpha-a
for the sum
\sum_{j}a_{j}
gives
a\geq\alpha
as desired.
∎

Let
\gamma
be the genus of
{\mathcal{M}}
and define the families

{\mathcal{G}}_{0}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)\supseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{1}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)\supseteq\dots\supseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)

where
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)
consists of the
{\mathcal{M}}
-embedded cellular graphs with
f(G)=\alpha
that satisfy the girth inequalities for balanced superfaces
U
with
g(U)\leq k.

5.1.
Girth conditions and
(3,6)
-sparsity

We now obtains a girth inequality characterisation of cellularly embedded
(3,6)
-tight graphs.

Definition 5.5
.

An
{\mathcal{M}}
-embedded simple graph
G
with the sparsity count
f(G)=\alpha
satisfies the
superface sparsity inequalities
if
f(G_{U})\geq\alpha
for all superfaces
U
.

The following theorem is due to Shakir
[
23
]
and it gives an insight into the
(3,6)
-tightness of
G
. In fact it is the “no blocks case” of a more general equivalence for the higher genus analogues of the block and hole graphs described in the introduction.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
G
be a cellularly embedded graph in the connected compact 2-manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)
G
is (3,6)-tight.

(ii)
G
satisfies the sparsity count
f(G)=6
and the associated superface sparsity inequalities,
f(G_{U})\geq 6
for all superfaces
U
of
G
.

Proof.

That (i) implies (ii) is immediate. Suppose that (ii) holds and (i) is not true. Then there is a maximal subgraph
K
of
G
, with at least 3 vertices and
f(K)\leq 5
.
Let
U
be a face of
K
and suppose first that the boundary walks of
U
consist of disjoint cycles. Then the complement of the closure of
U
is the union of components, say
W_{1},\dots,W_{r}
, that have disjoint closures. In particular,
G_{U}^{c}
is the union of the disjoint subgraphs,

G_{W_{1}},\dots,G_{W_{r}}
and
f(G_{U}^{c})\geq 6r
, by the superface sparsity inequalities.

In the general case we perform, once again, vertex splitting moves on the vertices of each boundary walk of
W_{1},\dots,W_{r}
, creating new vertices and edges in each
W_{i}
, and superfaces
W_{i}^{\prime}\subset W_{i}
for the embedded graph
K^{\prime}
determined by
K
and the vertex-splitting moves. We can also view these moves as defined on
G_{U}^{c}
, determining an embedded graph
(G_{U}^{c})^{\prime}
. Since

f((G_{U}^{c})^{\prime})
is sum of
f(G_{W_{i}^{\prime}}^{c})
, for
1\leq i\leq r
, we have
f((G_{U}^{c})^{\prime})\geq 6r
. Also
f((G_{U}^{c})^{\prime})=f(G_{U}^{c})
and so in all cases we have
f(G_{U}^{c})\geq 6
.

Since
f(G)=f(G_{U})+f(G_{U}^{c})-f(G_{U}\cap G_{U}^{c})
it follows that

f(G_{U})-f(G_{U}\cap G_{U}^{c})\leq 0
.
Also,
\partial U=K\cap G_{U}=G_{U}\cap G_{U}^{c}
and

f(K\cup G_{U})=f(K)+(f(G_{U})-f(K\cap G_{U})),

and so
f(K\cup G_{U})\leq 5
. Thus
K=K\cup G_{U}
by the maximality of
K
. Since this is true for all faces
U
of
K
it follows that
K=G
, a contradiction, as required. Indeed, simple counting shows that
K
is not a tree and so has at least 2 faces
U_{1},U_{2}
, and
G
is the union of
G_{U_{1}}^{c}
and
G_{U_{2}}^{c}
.
∎

An
{\mathcal{M}}
-embedded graph
G
is
(3,3)
-tight
if the sparsity count
f(G)=3
holds and
f(G^{\prime})\geq 3
for all subgraphs
G^{\prime}
. We write
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}},3)
for the family of cellularly embedded
(3,3)
-tight graphs.
The argument of the previous theorem shows similarly that
G
is in
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}},3)
if and only if

f(G)=3
and
f(G_{U})\geq 3
for all superfaces
U
of
G
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a connected compact 2-manifold with genus
\gamma
. Then
{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},6)={\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}},6)
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},3)={\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}},3)
.

Proof.

Let
U
be a superface of
G
where the complement of the closure of
U
has components
W_{1},\dots,W_{\kappa}
. If
G
is in
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}},6)
then
f(G_{W_{i}})\geq 6
for each
i
and so the inequality of Lemma
5.3
(i) holds. By the equivalences given in the lemma
U
satisfies the higher genus girth inequalities and so
G
is in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},6)
.

For the converse let
G
belong to
{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},6)
and let
W
be a balanced superface of
G
. If
U
is the complementary superface then
U
satisfies the higher rank girth inequality and so Lemma
5.3
(ii) holds for
U
. By the equivalence of the lemma, with
W_{1}=W,\kappa=1
, we have
f(G_{W})\geq 6
. Now, by Lemma
5.4
for
\alpha=6
,
G
satisfies the superface sparsity inequalities and so, since
f(G)=6
, it follows from Theorem
5.6
that
G
is
(3,6)
-tight.
The same argument, with trivial adjustments, shows that
{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},3)={\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}},3)
.
∎

6.
Contraction-minimality and sparse graphs.

We now obtain analogues of the Barnette-Edelson finiteness theorem for partial triangulations of a 2-manifold satisfying higher genus girth conditions. This leads to the finiteness of contraction-minimal graphs in sparse graph families, such as
(3,6)
-tight graphs and
(3,3)
-tight graphs that feature in the rigidity theory of bar-joint frameworks in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
We assume that
\alpha\geq 3
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
G
be a contraction-minimal embedded graph in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)
. Then there is an independent bound for the degree of a vertex.

Proof.

The proof of Lemma
4.3
holds without change with the understanding that critical walks
c_{e}
are general genus critical walks.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
G
be a contraction-minimal embedded graph in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)
and let
U_{0}
be a critical superface of genus
0
. Then there is an independent bound for
|V\cap U_{0}|
.

Proof.

The argument in the proof of Lemma
4.4
applies directly.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
G
be a contraction-minimal embedded graph in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)
and let
U
be a critical superface of general genus. Then there is an independent bound for
|V\cap U|
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on the genus. Noting the previous lemma for genus
0
, let
G
be contraction-minimal and assume that an independent bound exists for the number of edges in the closure of a superface with genus less than
g
. We show that there is a such a bound for a superface of genus
g
. We may assume that one of the following is true.

(i) There is a boundary walk of a critical superface that is essential, that is, that is not null-homotopic in
{\mathcal{M}}
.

(ii) There is an essential 3-cycle.

To see this suppose that (ii) does not hold. Then, by contraction-minimality, every nonboundary edge of
G
is on a boundary walk for a critical superface. If every such boundary walk is null-homotopic then a general genus critical superface has all its boundary walks null-homotopic. Thus the components of the complement of
U^{-}
are genus 0 superfaces, and there is an independent bound for these, by Lemma
6.2
. Thus there is an independent bound for the number of critical edges in this case. So we can assume that (i) or (ii) holds and we let
c_{\rm ess}
be the essential walk or 3-cycle.

We now consider the division of
{\mathcal{M}}
by
c_{\rm ess}
that is analogous to the division in the induction scheme in Section
2
.

Consider first the case that
{\mathcal{M}}
is orientable. Assume moreover that the complement
{\mathcal{M}}\backslash c_{\rm ess}
is not connected and so has 2 components, namely
U_{0}
, the superface for
c_{\rm ess}
, and
W
say. Since
c_{\rm ess}
is essential we have
g(U_{0})<g
and
g(W)<g
.
Let
e
be an edge in a critical superface
U
of genus
g
. Then
e
lies on a closed walk
c
that is either an essential 3-cycle or a boundary walk of a critical superface
U_{c}
.
If
c
happens to lie in
G_{U}
or
G_{W}
then, by the induction hypothesis,
U_{c}
has an independent bound for the edges in its closure. The only other possibility requires that
c
contains a vertex of
c_{\rm ess}
. By Lemma
6.1
, and the bounded lengths of critical walks, it follows that there is an independent bound for the number of such critical walks. Combining these facts it follows that there is an independent bound for the number of edges
e
. This completes the induction step and so the proof is complete in this case. The case that
{\mathcal{M}}\backslash c_{\rm ess}
is connected is similar, as are the nonorientable cases.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
G
be a contraction-minimal embedded graph in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)
. Then there is an independent bound for the set of edges that lie on the boundary of a critical superface.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
6.3
and the exhaustive colouring argument in the proof of Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a compact connected 2-manifold. Then there are finitely many contraction-minimal graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)
.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
4.6
and Lemma
6.4
since every edge of a contraction minimal graph in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\gamma}({\mathcal{M}},\alpha)
is either a critical edge or the edge of the boundary of a nontriangular face.
∎

6.1.
Contraction minimal graphs and 3-rigidity

The following theorem is an immediate corollary of Theorems
6.5
,
5.7
.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a compact 2-manifold. Then there are finitely many contraction-minimal graphs in
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}},6)
and
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}},3)
.

Recall that a
bar-joint framework
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is a pair
(G,p)
where
G=(V,E)
is a simple graph and
p:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is a spatial realisation of its vertices, giving a spatial realisation of
G
. See
[
12
]
for example. Also,
(G,p)
is a
generic framework
if the set of coordinates of
p(v)
, for
v
in
V
, is an algebraically independent set, and
G
is a
3-rigid

G

6.2.
NonEuclidean minimal rigidity.

One can also consider the minimal 3-rigidity of bar-joint frameworks in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with respect to non-Euclidean norms, such as the norms
\|\cdot\|_{q}
, for
1<q\neq 2<\infty
. See Kitson and Power
[
18
]
and Dewar, Kitson and Nixon
[
8
]
for example. The necessary condition for such minimal
3
-rigidity of a framework
(G,p)
is that the simple graph
G
is
(3,3)
-tight. It remains an open problem from
[
18
]
whether this condition is also sufficient in general.

For the sphere, in view of Lemma
3.1
there are no cellularly embedded
(3,3)
-tight graphs. For the projective plane they are precisely the graphs of triangulations, and it is shown in
[
8
]
that these are the minimally 3-rigid projective plane graphs for
\|\cdot\|_{q}
. For the genus 1 torus
{\mathcal{M}}
, to show the sufficiency of
(3,3)
-tightness for minimal 3-rigidity for
\|\cdot\|_{q}
it would be sufficient, by Theorem
6.6
, to identify the contraction-minimal graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{M}},3)
and to determine their
3
-rigidity.

Abstract
. Our purpose in this paper is to construct new examples of twisted Brill-Noether loci on curves of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 . Many of these examples have negative expected dimension. We deduce also the existence of a new region in the Brill-Noether map, whose points support non-empty standard Brill-Noether loci.

1.
Introduction

Our object in this paper and its successor
[
9
]
is to construct twisted Brill-Noether loci different from those constructed in
[
15
]
. In particular, the relevance of many of these examples is that they have negative Brill-Noether numbers, whereas the examples constructed in
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
all have positive Brill-Noether numbers. We deduce also the existence of a new region in the Brill-Noether map (see below for the definition), the points of which support non-empty Brill-Noether loci, whose existence was not previously known. This implies that our new examples also differ from those constructed in
[
15
, Section 9]
(see Remark
4.10
(iii)). In this paper, we consider applications of the results of
[
6
,
8
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
25
]
together with Butler’s Theorem
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
. In
[
9
]
, we will, in particular, use known cases of Butler’s Conjecture
[
12
, Conjecture 2]
(see
[
3
,
4
,
7
]
) to obtain further examples. Our methods will continue to apply as further cases of Butler’s Conjecture are proved. In what follows, we shall usually abbreviate Brill-Noether to BN.

Let
C
be a smooth irreducible projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
defined over the complex numbers. For
i=1,2
, let
M_{i}:=M(n_{i},d_{i})
denote the moduli space of stable bundles of rank
n_{i}
and degree
d_{i}
. Similarly, we write
\widetilde{M}_{i}
for the corresponding moduli space of S-equivalence classes of semistable bundles.

Let
E_{2}
be any vector bundle on
C
of rank
n_{2}
and degree
d_{2}
. For any integers
n_{1}
,
d_{1}
,
k
with
n_{1}\geq 1
, one can define the
twisted BN locus

(1.1)

B(n_{1},d_{1},k)(E_{2}):=\{E_{1}\in M_{1}\,|\,h^{0}(E_{1}\otimes E_{2})\geq k\}.

The locus
B(n_{1},d_{1},k)(E_{2})
is a closed subscheme of
M_{1}
. In fact, it is a determinantal locus, being the pullback of a universal determinantal variety of codimension
k(k-\chi)
,
where

(1.2)

\chi:=n_{2}d_{1}+n_{1}d_{2}-n_{1}n_{2}(g-1).

It follows that, if
\beta(n_{1},d_{1},k)(E_{2})\leq n_{1}^{2}(g-1)+1
, then every irreducible component of
B(n_{1},d_{1},k)(E_{2})
has dimension greater than or equal to the
BN number

(1.3)

\beta(n_{1},d_{1},k)(E_{2}):=n_{1}^{2}(g-1)+1-k(k-\chi),

This number is often referred to as the
expected dimension
.

We define also

\widetilde{B}(n_{1},d_{1},k)(E_{2}):=\{[E_{1}]\in\widetilde{M}_{1}\,|\,h^{0}(\operatorname{gr}E_{1}\otimes E_{2})\geq k\},

where
[E_{1}]
denotes the S-equivalence class of
E_{1}
and
\text{gr}E_{1}
is the corresponding graded bundle.
This is a closed subscheme of
\widetilde{M}_{1}
, but can have components of dimension less than the BN number.

In the special case
E_{2}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
, we obtain the standard (untwisted) higher rank BN locus, denoted by
B(n_{1},d_{1},k)
, with expected dimension

(1.4)

\beta(n_{1},d_{1},k):=n_{1}^{2}(g-1)+1-k(k-d_{1}+n_{1}(g-1)).

In particular,
B(1,d_{1},k)
is the classical BN locus
W^{k-1}_{d_{1}}
.

In the case
n_{1}=1
, twisted BN loci have been studied for some time starting with
[
13
,
19
]
(see
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
for full details and further references). These loci include the special case of maximal line subbundles. A systematic study for arbitrary
n_{1}
was started in
[
15
]
, where, in particular, examples of twisted BN loci were constructed
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
using the technique of
[
22
]
. As we shall see in Theorem
3.1
, these have the important feature that the BN number (
1.3
) is positive. Many of them (maybe, all) have the expected dimension
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
.

We can now introduce the loci which we are going to study in this paper. This is a special case of a more general construction introduced in
[
15
]
, where it was used primarily to obtain results for
B(n_{1},d_{1},k)(E_{2})
. If
\gcd(n_{i},d_{i})=1
for
i=1,2
, then there exist universal bundles
\mathcal{U}_{i}
on
M_{i}\times C
and, for any integer
k
, we define the
universal twisted BN locus
as follows:

(1.5)

B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2}):=\{(E_{1},E_{2})\in M_{1}\times M_{2}\,|\,h^{0}(E_{1}\otimes E_{2})\geq k\}.

In the non-coprime case, we need to lift to étale coverings of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
in order to construct “universal” bundles
[
24
, Proposition 2.4]
. However (
1.5
) still makes sense and we shall use the same notation. The locus
B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
is a closed subscheme of
M_{1}\times M_{2}
; if
k\leq 0
, then
B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})=M_{1}\times M_{2}
. The structure of
B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
as a determinantal locus allows us to define a BN number (see
[
15
, (3.1)]

(1.6)

\beta^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2}):=\dim M_{1}+\dim M_{2}-k(k-\chi),

which may again be referred to as the
expected dimension
. Note that
\chi=\chi(E_{1}\otimes E_{2})
for all
(E_{1},E_{2})\in M_{1}\times M_{2}
. If
\beta^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\leq\dim M_{1}+\dim M_{2}
, then every irreducible component of
B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
has dimension greater than or equal to
\beta^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
. We define similarly the corresponding semistable locus

\widetilde{B}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2}):=\{([E_{1}],[E_{2}])\in\widetilde{M}_{1}\times\widetilde{M}_{2}\,|\,h^{0}(\text{gr}E_{1}\otimes\text{gr}E_{2})\geq k\}.

Note that the definition is symmetric and
B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\cong B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{U}_{1})
. Moreover, by Serre duality,
B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\cong B^{k_{1}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}^{*},\mathcal{U}_{2}^{*}\otimes p_{C}^{*}K_{C})
, where
k_{1}=k-\chi
and
K_{C}
denotes the canonical line bundle on
C
. Note also that
B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\cong B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes p_{C}^{*}L^{*},\mathcal{U}_{2}\otimes p_{C}^{*}L)
for any line bundle
L
. We take account of these possibilities in Theorem
3.1
(ii) and Remark
3.3
(iii) (see also Remark
5.4
(i)).

Remark 1.1
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

•

B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\neq\emptyset
;

•

B(n_{1},d_{1},k)(E_{2})\neq\emptyset
for some
E_{2}\in M_{2}
;

•

B(n_{2},d_{2},k)(E_{1})\neq\emptyset
for some
E_{1}\in M_{1}
.

Many of our results are valid for any smooth curve. For some, however, we require the concept of a Petri curve. The curve
C
is said to be
Petri
if the classical Petri map

H^{0}(L)\otimes H^{0}(K_{C}\otimes L^{*})\longrightarrow H^{0}(K_{C})

is injective for every line bundle
L
on
C
. The general curve is Petri in the sense that Petri curves of genus
g
form a non-empty open subset of the moduli space of curves of genus
g

[
14
,
20
]
. By classical BN theory, if
C
is Petri, the BN locus
B(1,d_{1},k)
has dimension precisely
\beta(1,d_{1},k)
whenever
0\leq\beta(1,d_{1},k)\leq g
and is empty when
\beta(1,d_{1},k)<0
. Sometimes we will not be able to obtain results for arbitrary Petri curves but only for curves which are
general
in the sense that they live in an unspecified open subset of the moduli space of curves of genus
g
.

The BN loci
B(1,d_{1},k)(E_{2})
, with
E_{2}
general, behave in a similar way to the classical BN loci
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
. By Remark
1.1
, this implies non-emptiness results for

B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
when
n_{1}=1
or
n_{2}=1
. Moreover, when
n_{2}=1
, we have an isomorphism

B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\longrightarrow B(n_{1},d_{1}+n_{1}d_{2},k)\times\mbox{Pic}^{d_{2}}(C):\ (E_{1},L)\mapsto(E_{1}\otimes L,L),

giving a description of
B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
in terms of an untwisted BN locus. We know many cases in which such loci are non-empty; see, in particular, Section
2
. In this paper, we shall assume that
n_{i}\geq 2
for
i=1,2
.

We can now state our first main theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
C
is a smooth curve of genus
g\geq 2
and that
d_{i}
,
k_{i}

(i=1,2)
are positive integers. Let
B(n_{i},d_{i},k_{i})
be non-empty BN loci with
n_{i}\geq 2
and suppose that
k=k_{1}k_{2}
.

(i)

If
d_{1}<2n_{1}
and
d_{2}\leq 2gn_{2}
, then
B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\neq\emptyset
.

(ii)

If, in addition,
d_{i}/n_{i}=\mu_{i}
,
k_{i}/n_{i}=\lambda_{i}
and
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}<\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+g-1
, then, for fixed
\mu_{i}
,
\lambda_{i}
and sufficiently large
n_{1}
,
n_{2}
,
\beta^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})<0
.

Part (i) of this theorem will be stated and proved in a more detailed version as Theorem
4.3
; (ii) is Lemma
4.2
. Using the results of Section
2
, it is easy to find many cases where the hypotheses of the theorem are satisfied, thus obtaining the examples we are seeking (see Example
4.5
). There exist examples even when
n_{1}=n_{2}=2
(see Example
4.6
).

Our second main theorem (Theorem
4.8
) is an important application to BN theory which shows that this construction gives a new region in the BN map (for the definition, see Subsection 2.1).

Returning to twisted BN loci, our third main theorem is of a rather different nature, in that
d_{2}
is negative and so
h^{0}(E_{2})=0
for all
E_{2}\in M_{2}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
C
is a smooth curve of genus
g\geq 2
,
n_{1}\geq 2
,
k_{1}>n_{1}
,
n
a positive integer and either
d>2ng
or
d=2ng
and
C
is non-hyperelliptic. Suppose further that
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})\neq\emptyset
and that

k\leq(d-n(g-1))(k_{1}-n_{1})-nd_{1}.

Let
(n_{2},d_{2})=(d-ng,-d)
. Then

B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\neq\emptyset
.
If, in addition,
k=d(k_{1}-n_{1})-e
, where
e\geq n(g-1)(k_{1}-n_{1})+nd_{1}
, and

d_{1}<k_{1}+n_{1}(g-1)-\frac{g-1}{k_{1}-n_{1}},

then, for any fixed values of
n_{1}
,
d_{1}
,
k_{1}
,
n
and
e
, and
d\gg 0
,

\beta^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})<0
.

A more detailed version of this theorem will be stated and proved as Theorem
5.3
. In Corollary
5.5
and the related Example
5.6
, we show that the requirement that
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})\neq\emptyset
is compatible with the condition
d_{1}<k_{1}+n_{1}(g-1)-\frac{g-1}{k_{1}-n_{1}}
. Hence Theorem
5.3
does lead to examples of twisted BN loci with negative expected dimension.

Section
2
is concerned with the non-emptiness of
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
. In subsection
2.1
, we introduce the BN map and describe some sufficient conditions for non-emptiness. Subsection
2.2
contains a description of kernel bundles and the dual span construction. We include also a description of BN loci for bundles of slope
\leq 2
(subsection
2.3
). In section

, we obtain an extended version of the main existence theorem of
[
15
]
. Sections
4
and

The dual span construction works also when we consider pairs
(E,V)
where

is a subspace of

which generates

. In the case where

is a line bundle, there are then further stability results, which have consequences for the non-emptiness of

[
9
]
.

2.
Non-emptiness of BN loci

In this section, we will recall and extend some of the principal known results on the non-emptiness of the BN loci
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
. The answer is completely known for
k_{1}\leq n_{1}
, but the problem is much more complicated (and still not completely solved) for
k_{1}>n_{1}
(except for
g\leq 3
and (almost) for hyperelliptic curves) (see
[
8
]
). For Petri curves, the naïve conjecture is that
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
is non-empty if and only if
\beta(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})\geq 0
. From (
1.4
), this is equivalent to

(2.1)

d_{1}\geq k_{1}+n_{1}(g-1)-\frac{n_{1}(g-1)}{k_{1}/n_{1}}-\frac{1}{k_{1}}.

In fact, numerous examples show that (
2.1
) is neither necessary nor sufficient for non-emptiness of
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
(see, for example,
[
6
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
18
, Remark 5.5]
,
[
1
]
).

2.1.
The BN map

Following
[
8
]
, the non-emptiness of
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
can be understood in terms of the
BN map
. For this, we map any non-empty BN locus
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
(or
\widetilde{B}(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
) with
0\leq d_{1}\leq n_{1}(2g-2)
to the point in the plane

(2.2)

(\mu,\lambda)=(d_{1}/n_{1},k_{1}/n_{1}).

We shall say that
(\mu,\lambda)

supports

B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
(resp.,
\widetilde{B}(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
) if (
2.2
) holds and
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})\neq\emptyset
(resp.,
\widetilde{B}(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})\neq\emptyset
). We are interested here in regions in the
(\mu,\lambda)
-plane for which it is known that all points with rational coordinates support infinitely many BN loci.

The first examples of such regions were determined by Teixidor i Bigas
[
25
]
and Mercat
[
22
]
.
To describe these in terms of the BN map, we follow
[
8
]
. For
s\geq 1
, write
\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(s):=s+g-2-\left\lfloor\frac{g-1}{s}\right\rfloor
; note that
d\geq\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(s)
if and only if
\beta(1,d+1,s)\geq 1
, and that
\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(1)=0
,
\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(g)=2g-2
. Now define a function
t_{g}
for
0\leq\mu\leq 2g-2
as follows:

(2.3)

t_{g}(\mu):=\begin{cases}0,&\mu=0;\\
\mu-\lceil\mu\rceil+s,&\mu\in]\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(s),\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(s)+1];\\
s,&\mu\in]\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(s)+1,\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(s+1)].\end{cases}

We define T to be the region in the
(\mu,\lambda)
-plane given by

(2.4)

\mu,\lambda\in{\mathbb{Q}},\ 0\leq\mu\leq 2g-2,\ 0<\lambda\leq t_{g}(\mu)

(see Figure 1). Note that Clifford’s Theorem can be stated in the form

\lambda\leq\frac{\mu}{2}+1

and the boundary line for this inequality (the
Clifford line
) is shown in the figure. Moreover, the condition
\beta(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})=1
becomes

\lambda(\lambda-\mu+g-1)=g-1

(see (
1.4
)), which defines a branch of a hyperbola with asymptotes
\lambda=0
and
\lambda=\mu-g+1
(the
BN curve
), and is also shown in the figure.

The following lemma summarises the results of Teixidor
[
25
]
and Mercat
[
22
]
. Teixidor’s proof is for general
C
; for
\widetilde{B}(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
, this is sufficient to prove it for any smooth curve. Mercat’s result
[
22
, Théorème A-5]
is valid for any smooth curve and implies our lemma. For the version stated below, see
[
8
, Section 5]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
g\geq 2
and suppose that
n_{1}\geq 2
. If
(\mu,\lambda)\in\operatorname{T}
, then
(\mu,\lambda)
supports
\widetilde{B}(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
for all values of
n_{1}
for which
n_{1}\mu
and
n_{1}\lambda
are both integers. The same is true for
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
, provided that the points

(2.5)

(\mu,\lambda)=(\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(s)+1,\lambda),\ s-1<\lambda\leq s,\ \widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(s)+1\neq\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(s+1).

are excluded.

Remark 2.2
.

Lemma
2.1
is equivalent to the statement that
\widetilde{B}(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})\neq\emptyset
if

(2.6)

d_{1}\geq k_{1}+n_{1}(g-1)-n_{1}\left\lfloor\frac{g-1}{\lceil k_{1}/n_{1}\rceil}\right\rfloor.

This can also be stated as

(2.7)

\mu\geq\lambda+g-1-\left\lfloor\frac{g-1}{\left\lceil\lambda\right\rceil}\right\rfloor.

For
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
, the case

(2.8)

d_{1}=n_{1}\left(\left\lceil\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}}\right\rceil+g-1-\left\lfloor\frac{g-1}{\lceil k_{1}/n_{1}\rceil}\right\rfloor\right)

must be excluded when
\beta(1,d_{1}/n_{1}+1,\left\lceil k_{1}/n_{1}\right\rceil+1)\leq 0
. (
2.6
) and (
2.8
) are essentially a restatement of Teixidor’s conditions
[
25
]
and relate closely to Mercat’s construction
[
22
]
.

Another method of constructing non-empty BN loci on a smooth curve
C
is described in
[
8
]
. This starts from the results of
[
6
,
21
,
23
]
, which give precise conditions for non-emptiness when
d_{1}\leq 2n_{1}
, and proceeds by tensoring by line bundles
L
with
h^{0}(L)=s\geq 1
. Serre duality extends the region further and we denote the result by BMNO. To describe BMNO in the BN map, we write

\hat{\eta}(s):=s+g-1-\left\lfloor\frac{g}{s}\right\rfloor

for
s\geq 1
.
The condition
d\geq\hat{\eta}(s)
is then equivalent to
\beta(1,d,s)\geq 0
. On a Petri curve, this is equivalent by classical BN theory to the assertion that
B(1,d,s)\neq\emptyset
. Note that
\hat{\eta}(1)=0
and, on a Petri curve,
\hat{\eta}(2)
is the gonality of
C
. We now define a function
f_{g}(\mu)
for
0\leq\mu\leq g-1
as follows:

(2.9)

f_{g}(\mu):=\begin{cases}\frac{s}{g}(\mu-\left\lceil\mu\right\rceil)+s,&\mu\in[\hat{\eta}(s),\hat{\eta}(s)+1];\\
\frac{s}{g}(\mu-\left\lceil\mu\right\rceil+1)+s,&\mu\in]\hat{\eta}(s)+1,\hat{\eta}(s+1)-1];\\
\frac{\hat{\eta}(s+1)-s}{g}(\mu-\left\lceil\mu\right\rceil+1)+s,&\mu\in]\hat{\eta}(s+1)-1,\hat{\eta}(s+1)[.\end{cases}

We extend the definition of
f_{g}
, which is modified from that in
[
8
]
to allow
\mu=0
, to the whole interval
[0,2g-2]
by Serre duality. The region BMNO in the
(\mu,\lambda)
-plane is then defined by the conditions

0\leq\mu\leq 2g-2,\ 0<\lambda\leq f_{g}(\mu).

For
g=10
, this region is illustrated in Figure 1, which also includes the top boundary of T, the Clifford line, the BN curve and an indication of the excluded points from Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3
and Remark
2.4
(ii). This is a copy of
[

The following lemma summarises the principal result of
[

]
, taking account of the results of

]
(see

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
g\geq 2
and
n_{1}\geq 2
. If
(\mu,\lambda)\in\operatorname{BMNO}
, then
(\mu,\lambda)
supports
\widetilde{B}(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
for infinitely many values of

. If

is non-hyperelliptic, the same holds for

, provided that points of the form
(\mu,\lambda)
such that

with
\mu\leq g-1
and
\lambda>\frac{s-1}{g}+s-1
, together with their Serre duals and the points
((\widehat{\eta}(s)+1,s)
, are excluded.

Remark 2.4
.

(i)

with

(ii) Some points excluded in Lemma

are included in Lemma

. In fact, if

is a multiple of

, then
\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}(s)=\widehat{\eta}(s)
, and the points
(\widehat{\eta}(s)+1,\lambda)
for

are excluded by (

), but included by Lemma

.

(iii) For
g\geq 4
, there are many non-empty

corresponding to points outside the union of T and BMNO; see, for example, the figures in
[
16
,
17

2.2.
Kernel and dual span bundles

Let
E
be a generated bundle of rank
n
and degree
d

(2.10)

0\longrightarrow D_{E}^{*}\longrightarrow H^{0}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow 0.

The bundle

(or

and degree

Following
[
12

a

.

The following theorem addresses the stability of

and summarises results of Butler and Mercat (see also
[

). We write

(2.12)

S:=\{E\in M(n,d)|D_{E}\text{ is stable}\}.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
g\geq 2
. Suppose that either
d>2ng
and
E\in M(n,d)
or
d=2ng
,

is non-hyperelliptic and

is generated and

is stable of slope
\leq 2
. In particular,

is a non-empty open subset of

and

when

. Moreover, the morphism

(2.13)

S\longrightarrow B(d-ng,d,d-n(g-1)):E\mapsto D_{E}

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Any stable bundle of slope
>2g-1
is generated with

. Hence
h^{0}(E)=d-n(g-1)
. It follows from (

) that

. Moreover, under the given hypotheses,
D_{E}
is stable by
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
. It is easy to check that

.
The fact that (

[
21
, Théorème 2-B-1]
and
[

.
∎

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
g\geq 2
and
E_{2}
any vector bundle of rank
n_{2}
and degree
d_{2}
on

and

\beta(n_{1},d_{1},k)(E_{2})>(\text{resp.},\geq)\,1

(ii)

-d_{1}\geq n_{1}d_{0}+1\ (\text{resp.},-d_{1}\geq n_{1}d_{0})

(i) If

and the result holda. Otherwise, the non-emptiness of

[

4.1.
The construction

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
g
. Suppose that

h^{0}(D_{E}\otimes E_{2})\geq k_{1}k_{2}\ (\text{resp.},h^{0}(D_{E}\otimes\text{gr}E_{2})\geq k_{1}k_{2})

Consider the sequence (
2.11

The semistable case proceeds similarly.

Note that, by (

.

Let

,

be positive rational numbers for

5.
Construction using kernel bundles

5.1.
The construction

The essential ingredient in proving Theorem
3.1
is Mercat’s construction
[
22
]
, while Theorem
4.3
depends on the constructions of
[
6
,
21
]
. Another way of constructing non-empty twisted BN loci is described in
[
10
]
and uses kernel bundles. The following proposition is contained in
[
10
, Remark 5.5]
and plays an essential rôle in the proof of
[
10
, Theorems 5.6 and 5.7]
. We include a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that
E_{1}
has rank
n_{1}
and degree
d_{1}
with
h^{0}(E_{1})\geq k_{1}
and that
E
is as in (
2.10
).
If
h^{1}(E_{1}\otimes E)=0
and

(5.1)

k\leq(d-n(g-1))(k_{1}-n_{1})-nd_{1},

then
h^{0}(E_{1}\otimes D_{E}^{*})\geq k
.

Proof.

We have
h^{0}(E_{1}\otimes E)=nd_{1}+n_{1}d-nn_{1}(g-1)
and
h^{0}(E)\geq d-n(g-1)
. So, tensoring (
2.10
) by
E_{1}
and taking global sections,

h^{0}(E_{1}\otimes D_{E}^{*})\geq(d-n(g-1))k_{1}-nd_{1}-n_{1}d+nn_{1}(g-1).

Simplifying and comparing with (
5.1
), this gives the result.
∎

We have the following immediate corollary (see Remark
1.1
).

Corollary 5.2
.

Suppose that the hypotheses of Proposition
5.1
are satisfied.

(i)

If
E_{1}
is stable
(\text{resp., semistable})
, then
B(n_{1},d_{1},k)(D_{E}^{*})\neq\emptyset

(\text{resp.},\widetilde{B}(n_{1},d_{1},k)(D_{E}^{*})\neq\emptyset)
.

(ii)

If
D_{E}^{*}
is stable
(\text{resp., semistable})
, then
B(h^{0}(E)-n,-d,k)(E_{1})\neq\emptyset\ (\text{resp.},\widetilde{B}(h^{0}(E)-n,-d,k)(E_{1})\neq\emptyset)
.

(iii)

If both
E_{1}
and
D_{E}^{*}
are stable
(\text{resp., semistable})
and
(n_{2},d_{2})=(h^{0}(E)-n,-d)
, then
B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\neq\emptyset\ (\text{resp.},\widetilde{B}^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\neq\emptyset)
.

Our object here is to use Corollary
5.2
(iii) to obtain new non-empty examples of twisted BN loci. We assume that
n_{1}\geq 2
. In order for (
5.1
) to yield positive values of
k
when

, which will always be the case, we clearly require

.

The key point we require in order to apply Corollary

and

are stable. We have already discussed the existence of stable

in subsection
2.1
and the stability of

in subsection

.

We can now state and prove a general theorem, which includes Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose that
C
is a smooth curve of genus
g\geq 2
,
n_{1}\geq 2
,
k_{1}>n_{1}
and either
d>2ng
or

and
C
is non-hyperelliptic. Suppose further that
B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})\neq\emptyset
and that (
5.1
) holds. Let
(n_{2},d_{2})=(d-ng,-d)
and suppose that
S
is defined as in (
2.12
). Then
S\neq\emptyset
and the morphism

(5.2)

B(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})\times S\longrightarrow M_{1}\times M_{2}:(E_{1},E)\mapsto(E_{1},D_{E}^{*})

is injective and has image contained in
B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
; in particular,

B^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\neq\emptyset.

If, in addition,
k=d(k_{1}-n_{1})-e
, where

(5.3)

e\geq n(g-1)(k_{1}-n_{1})+nd_{1},

and

(5.4)

d_{1}<k_{1}+n_{1}(g-1)-\frac{g-1}{k_{1}-n_{1}},

then, for any fixed values of

,
d_{1}
,
k_{1}
,
n
and

5.3
), and

(5.5)

\beta^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})<0.

Proof.

The first part follows from Theorem

5.1

(iii).
We need to note that

For the second part, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\beta^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{1}^{2}(g-1)+1+(d-ng)^{2}(g-1)+1

\displaystyle-(d(k_{1}-n_{1})-e)(d(k_{1}-n_{1})-e-\chi),

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=

n_{1}<k_{1}\leq n_{1}(g-1)

Suppose that

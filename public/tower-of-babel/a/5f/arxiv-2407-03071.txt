Birth, Death, and Horizontal Flight: Malthusian flocks with an easy plane in three dimensions
John Toner

Abstract
I formulate the theory of three dimensional “Malthusian flocks” – i.e., coherently moving collections of self-propelled entities (such as living creatures) which are being “born” and “dying” during their motion – whose constituents all have a preference for having their velocity vectors lie parallel to the same two-dimensional plane. I determine the universal scaling exponents characterizing such systems exactly, finding that the dynamical exponent z = 3 / 2 z=3/2 , the “anisotropy” exponent ζ = 3 / 4 \zeta=3/4 , and the “roughness” exponent χ = − 1 / 2 \chi=-1/2 . I also give the scaling laws implied by these exponents.

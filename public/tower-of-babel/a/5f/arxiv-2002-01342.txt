PROPERTIES OF CHEBYSHEV POLYNOMIALS
Natanael Karjanto

Abstract
Ordinary differential equations and boundary value problems arise in many aspects of mathematical physics. Chebyshev differential equation is one special case of the Sturm-Liouville boundary value problem. Generating function, recursive formula, orthogonality, and Parseval’s identity are some important properties of Chebyshev polynomials. Compared with a Fourier series, an interpolation function using Chebyshev polynomials is more accurate in approximating polynomial functions. Keywords : Sturm-Liouville boundary value problem, Chebyshev differential equation, Chebyshev polynomials, generating function, recursive formula, orthogonality, Parseval’s identity.



INTRODUCTION

Based on its name, these polynomials were first investigated by a Russian mathematician Pafnuty Lvovich Chebyshev (1821-1894). In addition to orthogonal functions, Chebyshev also studied inequalities, prime numbers, probability theory, quadratic forms, integral theory, geographical map, geometrical volume formulas, and mechanics as well as problems in changing circular motion to straight motion with mechanical coupling
[
1
]
. In this paper, we will observe the appearance of Chebyshev polynomials as solutions of one particular case from a Sturm-Liouville boundary value problem. We discuss and prove several essential properties, such as the generating formula, the recursive relation, and Parseval’s identity. We cover but do not prove Rodrigues’ formula, even and odd functions, as well as orthogonality. Depending on the type of functions, an interpolation function using Chebyshev polynomials has a faster rate of convergence and better accuracy than a Fourier series when approximating a polynomial. On the other hand, a Fourier series converges faster and better when interpolating non-polynomial functions.

PROPERTIES OF THE CHEBYSHEV POLYNOMIALS

Property 4
(Rodrigues’ formula).

The Chebyshev polynomials
T_{n}(x)
can be expressed as Rodrigues’ formula in terms of derivatives

T_{n}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}{(-1)^{n}\,(2n-1)(2n-3)\dots 1}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(1-x^{2})^{n-\frac{1}{2}}

(7)

with
n=0,1,2,3,\dots
.

Property 5
(Generating function).

The generating function for the Chebyshev polynomials
T_{n}(x)
reads

\frac{1-z\,x}{1-2\,z\,x+z^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,T_{n}(x)\,z^{n}.

(8)

Proof.

By letting
x=\cos\theta
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,T_{n}(x)\,z^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,T_{n}(\cos\theta)\,z^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,\cos(n\,\theta)\,z^{n}=1+\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{n=-\infty,\,n\neq 0}^{\infty}\,z^{|n|}\,e^{\,i\,n\,\theta}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,z^{n}\,e^{\,i\,n\,\theta}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,z^{n}\,e^{-i\,n\,\theta}\right)=1+\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\frac{z\,e^{\,i\,\theta}}{1-z\,e^{\,i\,\theta}}+\frac{z\,e^{-i\,\theta}}{1-z\,e^{-i\,\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\frac{z\,e^{\,i\,\theta}-z^{2}+z\,e^{-i\,\theta}-z^{2}}{1-z\,e^{\,i\,\theta}-z\,e^{-i\,\theta}+z^{2}}\right)=1+\frac{z\,\cos\theta-z^{2}}{1-2\,z\,\cos\theta+z^{2}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{x\,z}{1-2\,x\,z+z^{2}}=\frac{1-x\,z}{1-2\,x\,z+z^{2}}.

Hence, we have shown the generating function formula for the Chebyshev polynomials.

Property 6
(Even and odd functions).

Based on their degrees, the Chebyshev polynomials
T_{n}(x)
admit two possibilities as either even or odd functions.

•

For
n
even, then
T_{n}(x)
are even functions.

•

For
n
odd, then
T_{n}(x)
are odd functions.

Property 7
(Recursive relationship).

One Chebyshev polynomial
T_{n}(x)
at a particular point can be expressed in terms of its neighboring Chebyshev polynomials at the identical point

T_{n+1}(x)=2\,x\,T_{n}(x)-T_{n-1}(x).

(9)

We refer (
9
) as a three-term recursive relationship since the formula forms a relationship among three terms of successive Chebyshev polynomial.

Proof.

From the definition of the Chebyshev polynomials, we have
T_{n}(\cos\theta)=\cos(n\theta)
.
We also have

T_{n+1}(x)=T_{n+1}(\cos\theta)=\cos(n+1)\,\theta=\cos n\theta\,\cos\theta-\sin n\theta\,\sin\theta

and

T_{n-1}(x)=T_{n-1}(\cos\theta)=\cos(n-1)\,\theta=\cos n\theta\,\cos\theta+\sin n\theta\,\sin\theta.

Next, by adding these two expressions, we obtain

T_{n+1}(x)+T_{n-1}(x)=2\,\cos n\theta\,\cos\theta=2\,T_{n}(x)\,x.

Finally, we attain the recursive formula
T_{n+1}(x)=2\,x\,T_{n}(x)-T_{n-1}(x)
.

This recursive relationship can also be derived from the generating function for the Chebyshev polynomials (Property
5
) by differentiating it twice with respect to
z
and equating the coefficients for
z^{n}
on both sides of the equation. We will obtain the desired result accordingly.

Property 8
(Orthogonality).

The Chebyshev polynomials
T_{n}(x)
form a complete orthogonal set on the interval
-1\leq x\leq 1
with a weighted function
1/\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
.

This means

\int_{-1}^{1}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,T_{m}(x)\,T_{n}(x)\,dx=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{*{20}c}0,\\
\pi,\\
\frac{\pi}{2},\\
\end{array}}\right.\qquad\left.{\begin{array}[]{l}m\neq n\\
m=n=0\\
m=n=1,2,3,\dots\end{array}}\right.

(10)

Using this orthogonality property, a piecewise continuous function on interval
-1\leq x\leq 1
can be expressed in terms of the Chebyshev polynomials

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,C_{n}\,T_{n}(x)=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{*{20}c}f(x),\\
\frac{1}{2}\,[f(x^{-})+f(x^{+})],\\
\end{array}}\right.\qquad\left.{\begin{array}[]{l}\textmd{for continuous $f(x)$ }\\
\textmd{at discontinuous points}\end{array}}\right.

(11)

where

C_{0}=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\int_{-1}^{1}\,\frac{f(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,dx\qquad\textmd{and}\qquad C_{n}=\frac{2}{\pi}\,\int_{-1}^{1}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,f(x)\,T_{n}(x)\,dx,\qquad\textmd{for}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(12)

Property 9
(Parseval’s identity).

Parseval’s identity related to the series expansion
{\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,C_{n}\,T_{n}(x)}
with the Chebyshev polynomials
T_{n}(x)
is given by

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,[f(x)]^{2}\,dx=C_{0}^{2}\,\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,C_{n}^{2}.

(13)

Proof.

The first step is by taking the square of both sides of the series expansion
{\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,C_{n}\,T_{n}(x)}
to yield

[f(x)]^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\,C_{n}\,C_{m}\,T_{n}(x)\,T_{m}(x).

(14)

We then multiply (
14
) with the weighted function
1/\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
and integrate it with respect to the variable
x
from
-\infty
to
\infty
to obtain

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,[f(x)]^{2}\,dx=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\,C_{n}\,C_{m}\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}T_{n}(x)\,T_{m}(x)\,dx.

(15)

Using Property
8
, we can write (
15
) as follows:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,[f(x)]^{2}\,dx=C_{0}^{2}\,\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,C_{n}^{2}

which gives Parseval’s identity for the Chebyshev polynomials.

APPLICATION

The Chebyshev polynomials play an important role in function approximation, integral approximation, and polynomial interpolation problem. For a periodic function
f(x)
on a particular interval, a Fourier series is more superior in approximating it with a relatively tiny error. However, in applications, we often encounter non-periodic functions which are only defined in a limited interval. The Chebyshev polynomials have a substantial contribution to these cases
[
5
,
6
]
. By approximating the function
f(x)
with the Chebyshev polynomials
T_{n}(x)
, we obtain the following interpolation function
P_{N}(x)

f(x)\approx P_{N}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\,C_{n}\,T_{n}(x).

(16)

We must select the coefficients
C_{n}
such that the norm
\|f(x)-P_{n}(x)\|_{2}
is minimum.
By seeking extreme values (in this case minimum values) of the
L^{2}
-norm, we obtain the coefficients
C_{n}
as in (
12
).

CONCLUSION

In this paper, we have observed that the Chebyshev differential equation is a special case of the Sturm-Liouville boundary value problem. Its solutions, the Chebyshev polynomials, have properties which are also found in other polynomials. One of these properties is orthogonoality, which plays an important role in approximating functions, similar to the well-known Fourier series. Similar to the case in the Fourier series, approximating piecewise continuous functions using the Chebyshev polynomials also produces the Gibbs phenomenon. When approximating polynomials, the Chebyshev polynomials exhibit a superiority in comparison to the Fourier series due to its accuracy. However, the Fourier series seems to converge faster in interpolating non-polynomial functions than the Chebyshev polynomials.

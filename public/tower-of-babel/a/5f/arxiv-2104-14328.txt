Entropy production estimate for the ES-BGK model with the correct Prandtl number
Doheon Kim, Myeong-Su Lee, SEOK-BAE YUN

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish the entropy-entropy production estimate for the ES-BGK model, a generalized version of the BGK model of the Boltzmann equation introduced for better approximation in the Navier-Stokes limit. Our result improves the previous entropy production estimate [ 39 ] in that (1) the full range of Prandtl parameters − 1 / 2 ≤ ν < 1 -1/2\leq\nu<1 including the critical case ν = − 1 / 2 \nu=-1/2 is covered, and (2) a sharper entropy production bound is obtained. An explicit characterization of the coefficient of the entropy-entropy production estimate is also presented.



1.
Introduction

The BGK model
[
6
]
has been widely used as a model equation of the Boltzmann equation in various practical flow problems since the BGK model produces quantitatively reliable results at a much lower computational cost. The Navier-Stokes limit of the BGK model, however, shows
a slight inconsistency with the one derived from the Boltzmann equation or experimental data
in that the Prandtl number - the ratio between the viscosity and the heat conductivity - computed using the BGK model is not correct.
Halway
[
19
]
introduced the ellipsoidal BGK model to overcome this drawback by introducing a parameter
\nu
and generalizing the local Maxwellian in the original BGK model into an ellipsoidal Gaussian parametrized by
\nu
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=A_{\nu}(\mathcal{M}_{\nu}(f)-f),\\
&\hskip 34.14322ptf(x,v,0)=f_{0}(x,v).\end{split}

The non-negative function
f(x,v,t)
is the velocity distribution function representing the mass density on the position
x\in\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
at time
t\geq 0
.
A_{\nu}
is the collision frequency defined by
A_{\nu}=\rho^{\alpha}T^{\beta}/(1-\nu)
for some
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
. Throughout this paper, we fix the spatial domain to be
\Omega=

\mathbb{R}^{3}
or
\mathbb{T}^{3}
to avoid boundary issues.

The ellipsoidal Gaussian (or non-isotropic Gaussian) is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\nu}(f):=\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{\mathrm{det}(2\pi\mathcal{T}_{\nu})}}\exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2}(v-U)^{\top}\mathcal{T}^{-1}_{\nu}(v-U)\right)},

where the local density
\rho
, the local bulk velocity
U
and the local temperature
T
are defined by

\displaystyle\rho(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x,v,t)dv

\displaystyle U(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}vf(x,v,t)dv

\displaystyle T(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3\rho}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v-U|^{2}f(x,v,t)dv,

and the temperature tensor
\mathcal{T}_{\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\nu}=(1-\nu)TI+\nu\Theta.\qquad(-1/2\leq\nu<1)

Here,
I
denotes the 3-by-3 identity matrix, and
\Theta
denotes the stress tensor:

\displaystyle\Theta(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x,v,t)(v-U)\otimes(v-U)dv.

For later use, we observe that

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{M}_{0}(f)=\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{(2\pi T)^{3}}}e^{-\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{2T}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}(f)=\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{\mathrm{det}(2\pi\Theta)}}\exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2}(v-U)^{\top}\Theta^{-1}(v-U)\right)}.

Under the assumption of
\mathcal{T}_{\nu}>0
, it can be shown by the change of variable

X=\mathcal{T}^{-1/2}_{\nu}(v-U)
that the following cancellation holds:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\{\mathcal{M}_{\nu}(f)-f\right\}(1,v,|v|^{2})dv=0\qquad(-1/2\leq\nu<1),

which leads to the conservation of mass, momentum and energy. It was shown in
[
2
]
that the
relaxation operator also satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\{\mathcal{M}_{\nu}(f)-f\right\}\ln fdv\leq 0

from which the celebrated H-theorem can be derived:

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\ln fdxdv\leq 0.

1.1.
Main result: Cercignani type entropy production estimate

Cercignani conjectured in
[
13
]
that the entropy production term of the Boltzmann equation is controlled by the relative entropy between the distribution function and the corresponding Maxwellian through the so-called entropy-entropy production estimate, which would lead to the exponentially fast
convergence of the distribution function to the global Maxwellian, at least in the homogeneous case. Although the conjecture was disproved in
[
7
,
35
]
, Villani showed that a nonlinear variant of it can be secured
[
34
]
.
In the case of the original BGK model, such Cercignani type entropy production estimate can be derived directly from the convexity property of the
H
-functional. The same convexity argument, however, does not lead to the desired result for the ES-BGK model
since the entropy production functional of the ES-BGK model is bounded only by the relative entropy between the distribution function and the ellipsoidal Gaussian.
This discrepancy was resolved in the case
-1/2<\nu<1
in
[
39
]
, leaving the critical case
\nu=-1/2
unanswered. In this paper, we extend the result to cover the critical case
\nu=-1/2
, at which the correct Prandtl number is obtained.

To state our main result, we define the H-functional
H(f)
, the relative entropy
H(f|g)
, and the entropy production functional
D_{\nu}(f)
:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&H(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\ln fdv,\qquad H(f|g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\ln(f/g)dv,\\
&\hskip 28.45274ptD_{\nu}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}A_{\nu}(\mathcal{M}_{\nu}-f)\ln fdv.\end{split}

Throughout this paper, we define
C_{\nu}
for
-1/2\leq\nu<1
by

(1.3)

\displaystyle C_{\nu}=\sup_{x>0}\frac{\displaystyle 3\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)-\ln\left(1+\frac{1+2\nu}{3}x\right)-2\ln\left(1+\frac{1-\nu}{3}x\right)}{\displaystyle 3\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)-\ln(1+x)}.

Then our main result is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
-1/2\leq\nu<1
. Assume that the solution
f
to (
1.1
) is regular enough so that the functionals in (
1.2
) are well-defined, and satisfies
\rho(x,t)>0
for a.e.
x,t
. Then, we have

(1)

C_{\nu}
satisfies the following bound:

C_{\nu}\leq\frac{1}{3}\,\nu^{2}(5-2\nu)

on
-1/2\leq\nu<1
.

(2)

For such choice of
C_{\nu}
, Cercignani-type entropy-entropy production estimate holds:

\displaystyle D_{\nu}(f)\leq-(1-C_{\nu})A_{\nu}H(f|\mathcal{M}_{0})

on
-1/2\leq\nu<1
.

Remark 1.2
.

(1) We note that
\nu^{2}(5-2\nu)/3<1
in the given range of
\nu
. (2)
For example,
f(1+|v|^{2}+|\ln f|)\in L^{1}([0,\infty)_{t}\times\Omega_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})
and
f\geq ae^{-b|v|^{2}}
for some positive constants
a
and
b
, is sufficient for the assumption of the theorem to be satisfied.

Theorem
1.1
improves the previous results on the entropy-entropy production estimate obtained in
[
39
]
in the following senses: First, it covers the whole range of the Prandtl parameters:
-1/2\leq\nu<1
including the critical case
-1/2
, while the estimate in
[
39
]
is valid only in
-1/2<\nu<1
.
Secondly, the explicit bound
\nu^{2}(5-2\nu)/3
on
C_{\nu}
is less than the corresponding coefficient
\max\{2\nu,-\nu\}
obtained in
[
39
]
, with the equality holding only at
\nu=0
(See Figure 1):

\frac{1}{3}\nu^{2}(5-2\nu)\leq\max\{-2\nu,\nu\},

which leads to a sharper entropy-entropy production estimate.
We also note in the figure 1 below that there is a strict gap between
C_{\nu}
and
\max\{-2\nu,\nu\}
at
\nu=-1/2
.
With the choice of
\max\{2\nu,-\nu\}
in place of
C_{\nu}
, the entropy production estimate given in Theorem
1.1
(2) become degenerate at the critical case
\nu=-1/2
since
1-\max\{2\nu,-\nu\}=\min\{1+2\nu,1-\nu\}=0
at
\nu=-1/2
.

Figure 1.
Comparison of coefficients

The starting point of the proof is the observation that the establishment of the following inequality with
0\leq C_{\nu}<1
is enough to derive the desired result (Lemma
2.2
):

(1.4)

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{M}_{\nu})-H(\mathcal{M}_{0})\leq C_{\nu}\{H(\mathcal{M}_{1})-H(\mathcal{M}_{0})\}.

We prove this inequality by showing that the range of the following quantity

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{H(\mathcal{M}_{\nu})-H(\mathcal{M}_{0})}{H(\mathcal{M}_{1})-H(\mathcal{M}_{0})}

lies in the interval
\big[0,C_{\nu}\big)
under reasonable assumptions.
For this, we first rewrite (
1.5
) as

(1.6)

\displaystyle F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}):=\ln\left(\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{3}\{(1-\nu)\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3}+\nu\theta_{i}\}}{(\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3})^{3}}\right)\bigg/\ln\left(\frac{\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\theta_{3}}{(\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3})^{3}}\right),

using the eigenfunctions
\theta_{i}

(i=1,2,3)
of the stress tensor
\Theta
, and formulate the problem of proving the inequality (
1.4
) into the problem of solving the maximization problem of this three-variable function:

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}>0\\
\exists~i,j:~\theta_{i}\neq\theta_{j}\end{subarray}}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}).

We then
reduce this three-variable optimization problem into a two-variable problem:

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>0\\
\theta_{1}>\theta_{2}\end{subarray}}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{2})

by observing a hidden relation of this problem with the following elementary question (See Lemma
3.1
.):

“When does
xy+yz+zx
attains its minimum with
x+y+z
and
xyz
fixed
\,?
”

Finally, the following simple scaling property of the
functional (
1.6
):

F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})=F_{\nu}(k\theta_{1},k\theta_{2},k\theta_{3}),\quad\forall~k>0,

together with a proper choice of
k
enable one to reduce the optimization problem further into a much manageable problem involving only one variable given in (
1.3
), from which
various useful information on the entropy-entropy production estimate can be derived.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
(1)

For simplicity, we denote

\widetilde{C}_{\nu}=\frac{1}{3}\nu^{2}(5-2\nu).

Then, it suffices to prove that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 3\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)-\ln\left(1+\frac{1+2\nu}{3}x\right)-2\ln\left(1+\frac{1-\nu}{3}x\right)}{\displaystyle 3\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)-\ln(1+x)}\leq\widetilde{C}_{\nu}\quad(\forall~x>0),

or equivalently,

(2.2)

\left(1+\frac{1+2\nu}{3}x\right)\left(1+\frac{1-\nu}{3}x\right)^{2}\geq(1+x)^{\widetilde{C}_{\nu}}\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)^{3(1-\widetilde{C}_{\nu})}\quad(\forall~x>0).

For this, we compute

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(1+\frac{1+2\nu}{3}x\right)\left(1+\frac{1-\nu}{3}x\right)^{2}\\
&\qquad=1+x+\frac{1-\nu^{2}}{3}x^{2}+\left(\frac{1+2\nu}{3}\right)\left(\frac{1-\nu}{3}\right)^{2}x^{3}\\
&\qquad=(3\nu^{2}-2\nu^{3})+(3\nu^{2}-2\nu^{3})x+\frac{1}{3}(2\nu^{2}-2\nu^{3})x^{2}+(1+2\nu)(1-\nu)^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)^{3}\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{3}\nu^{2}\left((9-6\nu)(1+x)+(2-2\nu)x^{2}\right)+(1+2\nu)(1-\nu)^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)^{3}\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{3}\nu^{2}(1+2\nu)(1+x)+\frac{8}{3}\nu^{2}(1-\nu)\left(1+x+\frac{1}{4}x^{2}\right)+(1+2\nu)(1-\nu)^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)^{3}.\end{split}

We then observe

\left(1+x+\frac{1}{4}x^{2}\right)^{2}-(1+x)\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)^{3}=\frac{1}{6}x^{2}+\frac{7}{54}x^{3}+\frac{11}{432}x^{4}>0

to bound the last line of (
2.3
) from below by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\nu^{2}(1+2\nu)(1+x)+\frac{8}{3}\nu^{2}(1-\nu)(1+x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}+(1+2\nu)(1-\nu)^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)^{3},

which, with the help of the weighted AM-GM inequality, can be bounded further from below by

\displaystyle(1+x)^{\frac{1}{3}\nu^{2}(1+2\nu)}\left[(1+x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right]^{\frac{8}{3}\nu^{2}(1-\nu)}\left[\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)^{3}\right]^{(1+2\nu)(1-\nu)^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad=(1+x)^{\frac{1}{3}\nu^{2}(5-2\nu)}\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)^{3-5\nu^{2}+2\nu^{3}}

\displaystyle\qquad=(1+x)^{\widetilde{C}_{\nu}}\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)^{3(1-\widetilde{C}_{\nu})},

which gives (
2.2
).

Remark 2.1
.

We mention that the equality holds when
\nu=0
, since both sides of the inequality (
2.1
) reduces to 0 at
\nu=0
.
In Section 4, we will prove that the equality holds also in the case
\nu=-1/2
.

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
(2)

We now move on to the proof of the entropy-entropy production estimate. The following lemma that compares the differences of H-functionals of various Maxwellians, is the key element
in the proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

With the
C_{\nu}
defined in Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{M}_{\nu})-H(\mathcal{M}_{0})\leq C_{\nu}\{H(\mathcal{M}_{1})-H(\mathcal{M}_{0})\},

for
-1/2\leq\nu<1
.

Remark 2.3
.

This improves the corresponding estimate in
[
39
]
in that (1) the critical case
\nu=-1/2
is covered, and (2)
C_{\nu}
is sharper than the one obtained in
[
39
]
.

Assuming the validity of the Lemma
2.2
, we can prove the main theorem as follows.
First we use the convexity of
x\ln x
and
\frac{d}{dx}(x\ln x)=1+\ln x
to get

\displaystyle D_{\nu}(f)=\int A_{\nu}(1+\ln f)(\mathcal{M}_{\nu}-f)\leq A_{\nu}\{H(\mathcal{M}_{\nu})-H(f)\}.

Thanks to Lemma
2.2
, the R.H.S. can be treated as

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}H\big(\mathcal{M}_{\nu}\big)-H(f)&=-\{H(f)-H(\mathcal{M}_{0})\}+\{H(\mathcal{M}_{\nu})-H(\mathcal{M}_{0}))\}\cr&\leq-H(f|\mathcal{M}_{0})+C_{\nu}\left\{H(\mathcal{M}_{1})-H(\mathcal{M}_{0})\right\}.\end{split}

Then we recall the following inequality from
[
2
,
39
]

H(\mathcal{M}_{0})\leq H(\mathcal{M}_{1})\leq H(f).

to bound the last line of (
2.4
) by

\displaystyle-H(f|\mathcal{M}_{0})+C_{\nu}\left\{H(f)-H(\mathcal{M}_{0})\right\}=-(1-C_{\nu})H(f|\mathcal{M}_{0}),

which completes the proof.
The proof of Lemma
2.2
is considered in the next section.

3.
Proof of Lemma
2.2

The proof of Lemma
2.2
is obtained by first formulating it as a three-variable optimization problem and then reducing it into a one-variable problem. As such, we divide this section into several steps.

3.1.
Step 1: Reformulation as a maximization problem

Let
\theta_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
) be three eigenfunctions of the symmetric matrix
\Theta
.

\bullet
Case i)
\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}=\theta_{3}
:
In this case,
\theta_{i}=T
, so that the temperature tensor
\mathcal{T}_{\nu}
reduces to
TI
and
\mathcal{M}_{\nu}
reduces to
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, from which the desired inequality follows trivially.

\bullet
Case ii) At least one of
\theta_{i}
takes a different value: The assumption
\rho>0
implies that

\kappa^{\top}\Theta\kappa=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x,v,t)\{(v-U)\cdot\kappa\}^{2}dv>0\qquad\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

This gives
\theta_{i}>0
.
First, we recall from Lemma 2.2 in
[
39
]
that

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{M}_{0})-H(\mathcal{M}_{\nu})

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho}{2}\ln{\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{3}\{(1-\nu)\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3}+\nu\theta_{i}\}}{(\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3})^{3}}},

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{M}_{0})-H(\mathcal{M}_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho}{2}\ln{\frac{\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\theta_{3}}{(\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3})^{3}}}.

Therefore, the inequality in Lemma
2.2
:

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{M}_{0})-H(\mathcal{M}_{\nu})\geq C_{\nu}\{H(\mathcal{M}_{0})-H(\mathcal{M}_{1})\},

can be rewriten as

(3.1)

\ln{\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{3}\{(1-\nu)\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3}+\nu\theta_{i}\}}{(\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3})^{3}}}\geq C_{\nu}\ln{\frac{\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\theta_{3}}{(\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3})^{3}}}.

Here we divided both sides by
\rho
using the assumption
\rho\neq 0
.
From our assumption on
\theta_{i}
that at least one pair of
\theta_{i}
takes a different value, and the
fact that the equality in the Inequality of arithmetic and geometric means holds only when all the variables take the same value, the R.H.S. of (
3.1
) is strictly less than zero.
Therefore (
3.1
) is equivalent to

\ln{\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{3}\{(1-\nu)\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3}+\nu\theta_{i}\}}{(\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3})^{3}}}\bigg/\ln{\frac{\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\theta_{3}}{(\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3})^{3}}}\leq C_{\nu}.

From this, we conclude that, to derive the desired inequality, it is enough to study the range of the functional
F_{\nu}
defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{2})=\frac{3\ln\left(\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3}\right)-\ln\left\{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{3}\big((1-\nu)\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3}+\nu\theta_{i}\big)\right\}}{3\ln\left(\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3}\right)-\ln(\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\theta_{3})}.\end{split}

More precisely, it is sufficient to prove that the following identity holds:

(3.2)

C_{\nu}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}>0\\
\exists~i,j:~\theta_{i}\neq\theta_{j}\end{subarray}}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})

for each fixed
\nu\in[-1/2,1)
, where
C_{\nu}
is the coefficient defined in (
1.3
). The rest of this section is devoted to proof of (
3.2
).

3.2.
Step II: Reduction into a two-variable problem

We first show that the optimization problem (
3.2
) can be reduced into an optimization problem of two-variable problem. We need the following lemma on the range of
xy+yz+zx
when the value of
x+y+z
and

Lemma 3.1
.

[
5
]

Fix
P,S>0
such that
P<S^{3}/27
.
Let
x,y,z>0

Set

(

\frac{1}{4}S^{2}(4\alpha-3\alpha^{2})\leq xy+yz+zx\leq\frac{1}{4}S^{2}(4\beta-3\beta^{2}),

(0,2/3)

(2/3,1)

\frac{1}{2}S\alpha,~\frac{1}{2}S\alpha,~S(1-\alpha)

\frac{1}{2}S\beta,~\frac{1}{2}S\beta,~S(1-\beta)

Proof.

See
[
5

.

Remark 3.2
.

In Lemma
3.1
, we have

\frac{1}{2}S\alpha<S(1-\alpha)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{2}S\beta>S(1-\beta).

We are now ready to derive our first reduction.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
\nu\in[-1/2,1)
,
F_{\nu}
satisfies

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}>0\\
\exists~i,j:~\theta_{i}\neq\theta_{j}\end{subarray}}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}>0\\
\theta_{1}>\theta_{2}=\theta_{3}\end{subarray}}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}).

Proof.

From the obvious relation

\big\{(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}:\exists~i,j:\theta_{i}\neq\theta_{j}\big\}\supset\big\{(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}:\theta_{1}>\theta_{2}=\theta_{3}\big\},

we have

(3.3)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}>0\\
\exists~i,j:~\theta_{i}\neq\theta_{j}\end{subarray}}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}>0\\
\theta_{1}>\theta_{2}=\theta_{3}\end{subarray}}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}).

Therefore, it suffices to prove the reverse inequality. For this, we fix
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}>0
satisfying
\theta_{i}\neq\theta_{j}
for some
i,j
and set

S:=\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3},\quad\Delta:=\theta_{1}\theta_{2}+\theta_{2}\theta_{3}+\theta_{3}\theta_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad P:=\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\theta_{3}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we can choose
\tilde{\theta}_{1},\tilde{\theta}_{2},\tilde{\theta}_{3}>0
such that
\tilde{\theta}_{1}>\tilde{\theta}_{2}=\tilde{\theta}_{3}
and

S=\tilde{\theta}_{1}+\tilde{\theta}_{2}+\tilde{\theta}_{3},\quad\Delta\geq\tilde{\theta}_{1}\tilde{\theta}_{2}+\tilde{\theta}_{2}\tilde{\theta}_{3}+\tilde{\theta}_{3}\tilde{\theta}_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad P=\tilde{\theta}_{1}\tilde{\theta}_{2}\tilde{\theta}_{3}.

For such choice of
\tilde{\theta}_{i}
, we observe

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{3}\left\{(1-\nu)\bigg(\frac{S}{3}\bigg)+\nu\theta_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=(1-\nu)^{3}\bigg(\frac{S}{3}\bigg)^{3}+\nu(1-\nu)^{2}(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3})\bigg(\frac{S}{3}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu^{2}(1-\nu)(\theta_{1}\theta_{2}+\theta_{2}\theta_{3}+\theta_{3}\theta_{1})\bigg(\frac{S}{3}\bigg)+\nu^{3}P

\displaystyle\geq(1-\nu)^{3}\bigg(\frac{S}{3}\bigg)^{3}+\nu(1-\nu)^{2}(\tilde{\theta}_{1}+\tilde{\theta}_{2}+\tilde{\theta}_{3})\bigg(\frac{S}{3}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu^{2}(1-\nu)(\tilde{\theta}_{1}\tilde{\theta}_{2}+\tilde{\theta}_{2}\tilde{\theta}_{3}+\tilde{\theta}_{3}\tilde{\theta}_{1})\bigg(\frac{S}{3}\bigg)+\nu^{3}P

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{3}\left\{(1-\nu)\bigg(\frac{S}{3}\bigg)+\nu\tilde{\theta}_{i}\right\}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{3\ln\frac{S}{3}-\ln\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{3}\left\{(1-\nu)\frac{S}{3}+\nu\theta_{i}\right\}\right]}{3\ln\frac{S}{3}-\ln P}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\ln\frac{S}{3}-\ln\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{3}\left\{(1-\nu)\frac{S}{3}+\nu\tilde{\theta}_{i}\right\}\right]}{3\ln\frac{S}{3}-\ln P}

\displaystyle=F_{\nu}(\tilde{\theta}_{1},\tilde{\theta}_{2},\tilde{\theta}_{3}),

which gives

(3.4)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}>0\\
\exists~i,j:~\theta_{i}\neq\theta_{j}\end{subarray}}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}>0\\
\theta_{1}>\theta_{2}=\theta_{3}\end{subarray}}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}).

Combination of (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) establishes the desired result.
∎

3.3.
Step III: Reduction into a one-variable problem

In the following lemma, we reduce the problem further into a one-variable optimization problem to complete
the proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,1)
, then we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}>0\\
\exists~i,j:~\theta_{i}\neq\theta_{j}\end{subarray}}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})

\displaystyle=C_{\nu}.

for
C_{\nu}
defined in (
1.3
) as the maximum value of an one-variable optimization problem.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
, it is enough to consider

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{2})=\frac{3\ln\left(\frac{\theta_{1}+2\theta_{2}}{3}\right)-\ln\left\{(1-\nu)\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}}{3}+\nu\theta_{1}\right\}\left\{(1-\nu)\frac{\theta_{1}+2\theta_{2}}{3}+\nu\theta_{2}\right\}^{2}}{3\ln\left(\frac{\theta_{1}+2\theta_{2}}{3}\right)-\ln(\theta_{1}\theta^{2}_{2})}.\end{split}

for
\theta_{1}>\theta_{2}
without loss of generality.

If we denote by
F_{\nu,1}
,
F_{\nu,2}
the numerator and the denominator of
F_{\nu}
respectively, we observe the following scaling property:

F_{\nu,i}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})=F_{\nu,i}(k\theta_{1},k\theta_{2},k\theta_{3}),\quad\forall~k>0.

Therefore, taking
k=\theta^{-1}_{2}
, we get

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&F_{\nu,1}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{2})\\
&\qquad=3\ln\left(\frac{\theta_{1}+2\theta_{2}}{3}\right)-\ln\left[(1-\nu)\frac{\theta_{1}+2\theta_{2}}{3}+\nu\theta_{1}\right]\left[(1-\nu)\frac{\theta_{1}+2\theta_{2}}{3}+\nu\theta_{2}\right]^{2}\cr&\qquad=3\ln\left(\frac{\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{2}}+2}{3}\right)-\ln\left[(1-\nu)\frac{\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{2}}+2}{3}+\nu\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{2}}\right]\left[(1-\nu)\frac{\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{2}}+2}{3}+\nu\right]^{2}\cr&\qquad=3\ln\left[1+\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{2}}-1\right)\right]-\ln\left[1+\frac{1+2\nu}{2}\left(\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{2}}-1\right)\right]\left[1+\frac{1-\nu}{3}\left(\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{2}}-1\right)\right]^{2}.\end{split}

We then set

x=\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{2}}-1

to obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle F_{\nu,1}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{2})=3\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)-\ln\left(1+\frac{1+2\nu}{2}x\right)-2\ln\left(1+\frac{1-\nu}{3}x\right).

Similarly, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle F_{\nu,2}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{2})=3\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)-\ln(1+x).

This gives the desired result.
∎

4.
Further consideration of
C_{\nu}

In this section, we characterize the value at which the optimal value of
C_{\nu}
is attained. For this, we define

G_{\nu}(x):=\frac{\displaystyle 3\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)-\ln\left(1+\frac{1+2\nu}{3}x\right)-2\ln\left(1+\frac{1-\nu}{3}x\right)}{\displaystyle 3\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)-\ln(1+x)}

and let
G_{\nu,1}
and
G_{\nu,2}
denote the numerator and the denominator of
G_{\nu}
respectively.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
\nu\in[-1/2,1)
, let
C_{\nu}
be given by the relation (
3.2
). Then we have

(1)
C_{\nu}=0
if
\nu=0.

(2)
C_{\nu}=1/2
if
\nu=-1/2

(3) If
\nu\in(-1/2,0)\cup(0,1)
, then
C_{\nu}
is characterized by

C_{\nu}=\frac{G_{\nu,1}(x_{\nu})}{G_{\nu,2}(x_{\nu})},

where

0<x_{\nu}<\infty
is the unique solution to

\frac{G_{\nu,1}(x_{\nu})}{G_{\nu,2}(x_{\nu})}=\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x_{\nu})}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x_{\nu})}.

Proof.

(1) When
\nu=0
,
G_{\nu,1}(x)\equiv 0
, Therefore, by Lemma
3.4
, we have
C_{\nu}=0
.

(2) The case
\nu=-\frac{1}{2}
:
Note that

\displaystyle G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{3}x}-\frac{1+2\nu}{3}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1+2\nu}{3}x}-2\frac{1-\nu}{3}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1-\nu}{3}x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{2}{3}\nu^{2}x}{(1+\frac{1}{3}x)(1+\frac{1+2\nu}{3}x)(1+\frac{1-\nu}{3}x)},

\displaystyle G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{3}x}-\frac{1}{1+x}=\frac{\frac{2}{3}x}{(1+\frac{1}{3}x)(1+x)},

so that

\displaystyle\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu^{2}(1+x)}{\left(1+\frac{1+2\nu}{3}x\right)\left(1+\frac{1-\nu}{3}x\right)},

and

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu^{2}(\frac{1-\nu}{3})\left\{\frac{4+2\nu}{3}-(\frac{1+2\nu}{3})(1+x)^{2}\right\}}{\left(1+\frac{1+2\nu}{3}x\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{1-\nu}{3}x\right)^{2}}.

Therefore, when
\nu=-1/2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}\right)=\frac{1}{8(1+\frac{1}{2}x)^{2}}>0.

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}-G_{\nu,1}(x)\right)=G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}\right)>0,\quad\forall~x>0.

This implies that
G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}-G_{\nu,1}(x)
is a strictly increasing function, so that

\displaystyle G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}-G_{\nu,1}(x)>\lim_{x\searrow 0}\left(G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}-G_{\nu,1}(x)\right)=0\cdot\frac{1}{4}-0=0,\quad\forall~x>0.

Hence we have

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{G_{\nu,1}(x)}{G_{\nu,2}(x)}\right)=\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}{(G_{\nu,2}(x))^{2}}\left(G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}-G_{\nu,1}(x)\right)>0,\quad\forall~x>0.\end{split}

This shows that
G_{\nu,1}(x)/G_{\nu,2}(x)
also is a strictly increasing function. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle C_{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{G_{\nu,1}(x)}{G_{\nu,2}(x)},

and the use of L’Hospital’s rule gives the desired result:

\displaystyle C_{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1/\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)-1/\left(1+\frac{1}{2}x\right)}{1/\left(1+\frac{1}{3}x\right)-1/(1+x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}.

(3)
\nu\in\left(-\frac{1}{2},0\right)\cup(0,1)
: Set
x_{*}:=\sqrt{\frac{4+2\nu}{1+2\nu}}-1
. Then we observe from (
4.1
) that

\frac{d}{dx}\left(G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}-G_{\nu,1}(x)\right)=G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}\right)\begin{cases}\displaystyle>0\quad\mbox{if}\quad 0<x<x_{*},\\
\displaystyle<0\quad\mbox{if}\quad x_{*}<x<\infty.\end{cases}

Hence

G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}-G_{\nu,1}(x)>\lim_{x\searrow 0}\left(G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}-G_{\nu,1}(x)\right)=0\cdot\nu^{2}-0=0,\quad\forall~x\in(0,x_{*}].

Note that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}-G_{\nu,1}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=0-\left[3\ln\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)-\ln\left(\frac{1+2\nu}{3}\right)-2\ln\left(\frac{1-\nu}{3}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\ln\{1-\nu^{2}(3-2\nu)\}<0.

By the intermediate value theorem, there exists
x_{\nu}\in(x_{*},\infty)
satisfying

G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}-G_{\nu,1}(x)\begin{cases}\displaystyle>0\quad\mbox{if}\quad 0<x<x_{\nu},\\
\displaystyle=0\quad\mbox{if}\quad x=x_{\nu},\\
\displaystyle<0\quad\mbox{if}\quad x_{\nu}<x<\infty.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{G_{\nu,1}(x)}{G_{\nu,2}(x)}\right)=\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}{(G_{\nu,2}(x))^{2}}\left(G_{\nu,2}(x)\frac{G^{\prime}_{\nu,1}(x)}{G^{\prime}_{\nu,2}(x)}-G_{\nu,1}(x)\right)\begin{cases}\displaystyle>0\quad\mbox{if}\quad 0<x<x_{\nu},\\
\displaystyle=0\quad\mbox{if}\quad x=x_{\nu},\\
\displaystyle<0\quad\mbox{if}\quad x_{\nu}<x<\infty,\end{cases}

from which we find

C_{\nu}=\sup_{0<x<\infty}\frac{G_{\nu,1}(x)}{G_{\nu,2}(x)}=\frac{G_{\nu,1}(x_{\nu})}{G_{\nu,2}(x_{\nu})}.

∎

Abstract.

Our purpose of this paper is to study the isolated singularities of positive solutions to Choquard equation in the sublinear case
q\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\ \ -\Delta u+u=I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\;\;{\rm in}\;\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},\;\;\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0,

where
p>0,N\geq 3,\alpha\in(0,N)
and

I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{p}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy
is the Riesz potential, which appears as a nonlocal term in the equation.
We investigate the nonexistence and existence of isolated singular solutions of
Choquard equation under different range of the pair of exponent
(p,q)
.
Furthermore, we obtain qualitative properties for the minimal singular solutions of the equation.

1.
Introduction

This is a continuation of the work
[
6
]
on the study of the isolated singularities of positive solutions to Choquard equation

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\ \quad-\Delta u+u=I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\;\;\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0,\end{array}

(1.1)

where
u
is a classical solution in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
,
p>0,\,q>0
,
N\geq 3
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
and

I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{p}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}\,dy.

We note that it is natural to assume that
\alpha>0
, otherwise
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]
will be infinite in whole
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and here
I_{\alpha}(x)=|x|^{\alpha-N}
is the Riesz potential with the order
\alpha-N<0
, which is related to the fractional Laplacian when
\alpha\in(0,2)
and it is a nonlocal operator.
Here
u
is said to be a classical solution of (
1.1
) if
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
,
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]
is well-defined in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
u
satisfies (
1.1
) pointwisely. We have developed in
[
6
]
the method of Lions
[
15
]
(e.g.
[
4
]
) to classify the isolated singularities of the Choquard equation (
1.1
) for the case of
q\geq 1
.
The essential point to set
q\geq 1
in
[
6
]
is due to the fact that the positive solutions of (
1.1
) decays exponentially and
this fact guarantees the existence of isolated singular solutions. When
0<q<1
, the situation of the nonlinearity becomes
subtle, because the decay at infinity is no longer exponential and this causes many difficulties for the classification of the isolated singularities and for the construction of singular solutions of the equation.

The Choquard equation is considered as an approximation to Hartree-Fock theory for a one component plasma, which is a semilinear Schrödinger-Newton type equation proposed by P. Choquard (for
N=3
,
\alpha=p=2
and
q=1
) and explained in Lieb and Lieb-Simon’s papers
[
14
,
17
]
respectively.
The Choquard type equations also arise in the physics of multiple-particle systems (
[
12
]
). Furthermore, the Choquard type equations appear to a prototype of the nonlocal problems, which play a fundamental role in some Quantum-mechanical and non-linear optics (see e.g.
[
11
,
23
]
). As far as we know, the most mathematical results about the Choquard equations are known for the case of
q=p-1
.
More precisely, we consider the problem in the variational setting:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u+u=I_{\alpha}[|u|^{p}]|u|^{p-2}u\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{----}u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{array}

that is, for the solutions which correspond to the critical points of a functional defined on the Sobolev space

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
satisfies

\frac{N-2}{N+\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{N}{N+\alpha}.

(1.2)

Then the equation has a groundstate solution
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if
p
satisfies strictly the inequality in (
1.2
) (see
[
20
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
14
]
and in particular
[
22
]
which is a survey for the Choquard type equations, the related problems and the related references therein).

Our aim in this article focuses on the study of the Choqurad equation
(
1.1
) when
q\in(0,1)
.
Without special explanation, we always assume in the sequel that

{\bf N\geq 3,\ \,\alpha\in(0,N),\ \,p>0\,\ {\rm and}\ \,q\in(0,1).}

We emphasize that the problem we treated is in general non-variational and the solutions are considered in the distributional sense.
Our first result states the nonexistence of positive solution of (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that

(1-\frac{\alpha}{N})p+q<1\quad{\rm and}\quad p+q<1+\frac{\alpha}{N-2}.

(1.3)

Then problem (
1.1
) has no any nonnegative nontrivial solution.

Notice that in

[
20
]
, the authors showed an elegant results on the groundstates for the case
q=p-1
and gave the optimal range of
p
for the existence of the groundstates solution. In fact, they proved that (
1.1
)
has no nontrivial solution when
p\geq\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}
or
p\leq 1+\frac{\alpha}{N}
, at least for groundstates solutions by applying Pohožaev identity. In our case, the distributional solution are weaker than the variational solution, and we consider the pair exponent
(p,q)

in the planar domain, so the range of
(p,q)
for the existence or nonexistence of distributional solution are delicate. It would be larger than the one in variational sense.

Next we present the classification of singularities of (
1.1
), inspired by
[
6
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u
be a nonnegative classical solution of (
1.1
) and

(1-\frac{\alpha}{N})p+q\geq 1\qquad{\rm or}\qquad p+q\geq 1+\frac{\alpha}{N-2}.

(1.4)

We assume that

\phantom{}\;\;\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)=0,\end{array}

(1.5)

in the sense of distribution,
that is,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(u(-\Delta\xi)+u\xi-I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\xi\right)\,dx=k\xi(0),\quad\forall\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(ii)

if

, then
u
satisfies that

\lim_{|x|\to 0^{+}}u(x)|x|^{N-2}=c_{N}k,

We remark that Theorem
1.2

shows that in the case (

linear part of the first equation and fractional Laplacian in the second one. We remark that the Dirac mass only appears
in the first equation for the system. This is different for considering the system directly. As in
[
6

to connect the singular solutions of
elliptic equation in punctured domain and the solutions of corresponding elliptic equation in the distributional

It is well known that the study of singularities of semilinear elliptic equations is a major subject in PDEs and they have been investigated widely for decades (see for example

Note that in

2.
Nonexistence

We prove the nonexistence of weak solution of (
1.1
) by contradiction. Assume that problem (
1.1
)
admits a nonnegative nontrivial solution
u
, and then we will obtain a contradiction by the the blowing up phenomena derived from its decay at infinity.
Let

\tau_{0}=-\max\{\frac{N-\alpha}{1-q},N-2\}

(2.1)

and denote by
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j}
the sequence

\tau_{j}=\frac{\alpha}{1-q}+\frac{p}{1-q}\tau_{j-1}\quad{\rm for}\quad j=1,2,3\cdots.

(2.2)

We start with the following

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
(p,\,q)
satisfies (
1.3
),
then
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j}
is an increasing sequence and
there exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\tau_{j_{0}-1}<-\frac{\alpha}{p}\quad{\rm and}\quad\tau_{j_{0}}\geq-\frac{\alpha}{p}.

(2.3)

Proof.

Since
q\in(0,1)
, we have that

\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}>0
is equivalent to

\tau_{0}(1-p-q)<\alpha.

(2.4)

We observe that (
2.4
) is obvious for
p+q\leq 1
,
and when
p+q>1
, (
2.4
) holds if

\frac{N-\alpha}{1-q}<\frac{\alpha}{p+q-1}\quad{\rm and}\quad N-2<\frac{\alpha}{p+q-1},

which is exactly equivalent to condition (
1.3
).
Notice also that

\tau_{j}-\tau_{j-1}=\frac{p}{1-q}(\tau_{j-1}-\tau_{j-2})=\left(\frac{p}{1-q}\right)^{j-1}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}),

(2.5)

which implies that the sequence
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j}
is increasing under the condition (
1.3
).
If
\frac{p}{1-q}\geq 1
, the conclusion is clear. If
\frac{p}{1-q}\in(0,1)
,
it deduces from (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle\tau_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-\left(\frac{p}{1-q}\right)^{j}}{1-\frac{p}{1-q}}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{0})+\tau_{0}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\frac{1-q}{1-p-q}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{0})+\tau_{0}=\frac{\alpha}{1-p-q}>0,\quad{\rm as}\quad j\to+\infty,

then there exists
j_{0}>0
satisfying (
2.3
).
∎

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
u
be a nonnegative and nontrivial classical solution of (
1.1
),
then there exists
b_{0}>0
such that

u(x)\geq b_{0}|x|^{\tau_{0}}\quad{\rm for}\quad|x|\geq 1,

(2.6)

where
\tau_{0}
is given by (
2.1
).

Proof.

Since
u
is nonnegative, then
I_{\alpha}[u^{p}]u^{q}\geq 0
and by the Strong Maximum Principle we have that

u>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
since
u
is a nontrivial solution.

Step 1.
We claim that there exists
c_{2}>0
such that

I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)\geq c_{2}\min\{1,\,|x|^{\alpha-N}\},\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

(2.7)

In fact, fix a point
x_{0}
with
|x_{0}|=1
, there exists
c_{3}>0
such that

u^{p}\geq c_{3}\quad{\rm in}\quad\overline{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0})},

then for
|x|\geq 2
and
|y|<\frac{3}{2}
, we have that
|x-y|\leq|x|+|y|\leq 2|x|
and then

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{3}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{3}(2|x|)^{\alpha-N}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0})}dy\geq c_{4}|x|^{\alpha-N}.

For
|x|\leq 2
and
|y|<\frac{3}{2}
, we have that
|x-y|\leq 4
and

I_{\alpha}[u^{p}](x)\geq c_{3}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy\geq 4^{\alpha-N}c_{3}|B_{1/2}(0)|.

(2.8)

Therefore, (
2.7
) holds.

Let

v_{1}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle c_{4}|x|^{\tau_{0}}&{\rm if}\quad|x|\geq 1,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}0&{\rm if}\quad|x|<1,\end{array}\right.

where

c_{4}=\min\left\{(c_{2}q)^{\frac{1}{1-q}},\,\min_{x\in\partial B_{1}(0)}u(x)\right\}.

Step 2.
We show that
v_{1}
satisfies

\displaystyle-\Delta v_{1}\leq c_{2}|x|^{\alpha-N}v_{1}^{q}-v_{1}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B_{1}(0)}.

(2.9)

Since
\tau_{0}\leq 2-N
, then for
|x|>1
,

-\Delta v_{1}\leq 0

and

\displaystyle c_{2}|x|^{\alpha-N}v_{1}^{q}-v_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{1}^{q}[c_{2}|x|^{\alpha-N}-v_{1}^{1-q}(x)]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle v_{1}^{q}\left[c_{2}|x|^{\alpha-N}-c_{2}q|x|^{\tau_{0}(1-q)}\right]\geq 0.

Step 3.
We prove finally that

u\geq v_{1}\quad{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B_{1}(0)}.

(2.10)

Let
f(r,t)=c_{2}r^{\alpha-N}t^{q}-t
, then

t\mapsto f(r,t)
is decreasing in

(0,\,(c_{2}q)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}r^{\tau_{0}})
.
In fact,

\partial_{t}f(r,t)=c_{2}qr^{\alpha-N}t^{q-1}-1<0\quad{\rm for}\quad t\in(0,\,(c_{2}q)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}r^{\tau_{0}}).

If (
2.10
) fails, since
u\geq v_{1}
on
\partial B_{1}(0)
, we may assume that there exists some point
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B_{1}(0)}
such that

u(x)-v_{1}(x)<0
, denoting

l_{0}=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0)}(u-v_{1})(x)<0

and the set

A_{0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0):\ u(x)-v_{1}(x)<\frac{l_{0}}{2}\}

is a nonempty,
C^{2}
- bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B_{1}(0)}
.
We see that for any
x\in A_{0}
,
0<u(x)<v_{1}(x)\leq(c_{2}q)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}|x|^{\tau_{0}}
,
so by the fact that
t\mapsto f(r,t)
is decreasing in

(0,\,(c_{2}q)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}r^{\tau_{0}})
, we obtain that

c_{2}|x|^{\alpha-N}u^{q}(x)-u(x)\geq c_{2}|x|^{\alpha-N}v_{1}^{q}(x)-v_{1}(x)

and

-\Delta(u-v_{1}-\frac{l_{0}}{2})\geq 0\quad{\rm in}\ \ A_{0},\quad u-v_{1}-\frac{l_{0}}{2}=0\quad{\rm on}\ \ \partial A_{0}.

By Maximum Principle, we have that
u\geq v_{1}+\frac{l_{0}}{2}
in
A_{0}
, which is impossible with the definition of
A_{0}
.
This ends the proof of the lemma.
∎

\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(e_{x})}\frac{1}{|e_{x}-z|^{N-\alpha}}dz

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy\leq c_{14}f_{\infty}\frac{1}{|x|^{\beta-\alpha}}\left(\frac{R}{|x|}\right)^{N-\beta}=\frac{c_{14}f_{\infty}}{|x|^{N-\alpha+\gamma}},

(3.19)

since
\beta-\alpha+\gamma(N-\beta)=N-\alpha+\gamma
. Therefore
the estimates (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) imply (
3.16
).
∎

Before to complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we need also the following

Lemma 3.3
.

[
19
, Lemma 6.7]

Assume that
u
is a solution of the equation

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\ \ -\Delta u+\mu u=\frac{\nu}{|x|^{\sigma}}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(0),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0,\end{array}

where parameters
\mu,\,\nu,\,\sigma,\,r
are positive.
Then we have

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)|x|^{\sigma}=\frac{\nu}{\mu}.

Proof of (
1.12
) and (
1.14
) in Theorem
1.3
.

(
1.14
) follows by (
3.12
). Next we prove (
1.12
).

Step 1.
From Lemma
3.2
, we have that

u_{k}(x)\leq k|x|^{-\frac{N-\alpha}{1-q}}\quad{\rm for}\quad|x|>r_{0}.

From
(1-\frac{\alpha}{N})p+q>1
, we have that

\frac{N-\alpha}{1-q}p>N
and

u_{k}^{p}(x)\leq k^{p}|x|^{-\frac{N-\alpha}{1-q}p}\leq k^{p}|x|^{-N}\quad{\rm for}\quad|x|>r_{0}.

Moreover,
u_{k}^{p}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by Lemma
3.2
.
From Proposition
3.1
with
\gamma=\frac{\frac{N-\alpha}{1-q}p-N}{1+\frac{N-\alpha}{1-q}p-N}
, there exists
c_{15}>0
such that

\lVert u_{k}^{p}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}I_{\alpha}(x)-\frac{c_{15}}{|x|^{N-\alpha+\gamma}}\leq I_{\alpha}[u_{k}^{p}](x)\leq\lVert u_{k}^{p}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}I_{\alpha}(x)+\frac{c_{15}}{|x|^{N-\alpha+\gamma}},\quad|x|>r.

(3.20)

Let
w=u_{k}^{1-q}
, then

-\Delta w+(1-q)w\geq(1-q)\lVert u_{k}^{p}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}I_{\alpha}(x)-c_{16}\frac{1}{|x|^{N-\alpha+\gamma}},

Then by Lemma
3.3
and Comparison Principle for
-\Delta+(1-q)
, we obtain that

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}w(x)|x|^{N-\alpha}\geq\lVert u_{k}^{p}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Then

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}u_{k}(x)|x|^{\frac{N-\alpha}{1-q}}\geq\lVert u_{k}^{p}\rVert^{\frac{1}{1-q}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Step 2.
From the Young’s inequality, we have that

I[u_{k}^{p}]u_{k}^{q}\leq(1-q)I[u_{k}^{p}]^{\frac{1}{1-q}}+qu_{k}

and then

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{k}+(1-q)u_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-q)I[u_{k}^{p}]^{\frac{1}{1-q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-q)\lVert u_{k}^{p}\rVert^{\frac{1}{1-q}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}|x|^{-\frac{N-\alpha}{1-q}}(x)+c_{17}|x|^{-\frac{N-\alpha+\gamma}{1-q}},

then by Lemma
3.3
and comparison principle for
-\Delta+(1-q)
, we have that

\limsup_{|x|\to+\infty}u_{k}(x)|x|^{N-\alpha}\leq\lVert u_{k}^{p}\rVert^{\frac{1}{1-q}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u_{k}(x)|x|^{N-\alpha}=\lVert u_{k}^{p}\rVert^{\frac{1}{1-q}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

and this completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

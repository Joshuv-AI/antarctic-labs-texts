On the mod p p unramified cohomology of varieties having universally trivial Chow group of zero-cycles
Shusuke Otabe

Abstract
. Auel–Bigazzi–Böhning–Graf von Bothmer proved that if a proper smooth variety X X over a field k k of characteristic p > 0 p>0 has universally trivial Chow group of 0 0 -cycles, the cohomological Brauer group of X X is universally trivial as well. In this paper, we generalize their argument to arbitrary unramified mod p p étale motivic cohomology groups. We also see that the properness assumption on the variety X X can be dropped off by using the Suslin homology together with a certain tame subgroup of the unramified cohomology group.

1.
Introduction

The goal of the present paper is to give a positive answer (see Corollary
1.3
below) to the following problem posed by Auel et al.

Problem 1.1
.

(cf.
[
1
, Problem 1.2]
)
Let
X
be a proper smooth variety over a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. Suppose that
X
has universally trivial Chow group of
0
-cycles, i.e. the degree map of the Chow group of zero-cycles is an isomorphism
{\rm deg}\colon\mathrm{CH}_{0}(X_{K})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{Z}
for any field extension
K/k
. Then, is the natural homomorphism
H^{i}(k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(j))\to H^{i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(k(X)/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(j))
an isomorphism for any integers
i,j\geq 0
?

Note that the formulation is slightly different from theirs. Here, the cohomology group
H^{i}(k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(j))
is the mod
p
étale motivic cohomology group of weight
j
, i.e.
H^{i}(k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(j))=H^{i-j}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(k,\Omega^{j}_{\log})
, and the group
H^{i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(k(X)/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(j))
is the
unramified cohomology group
of the function field
k(X)
, which is defined as the subgroup of the group
H^{i}(k(X),\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(j))
consisting of cohomology classes which are unramified at every geometric rank one discrete valuation on
k(X)/k
(cf.
[
4
, §5]
). As the
p
-cohomological dimension of a field of characteristic
p>0
is less than or equal to one, we have
H^{i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(k(X)/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(j))=0
for
i-j\neq 0,1
. Therefore, the problem is nontrivial only in the case when
i=j
or
i=j+1
. In the former case, the groups
H^{i}(K,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))
are naturally isomorphic to the mod
p
Milnor K-groups
K_{i}^{\rm M}(K)/p
, i.e.

H^{i}(K,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))\simeq K^{\rm M}_{i}(K)/p

for all field extensions
K/k
and for all integers
i\geq 0
(cf.
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
), and they form a cycle module in the sense of Rost
[
22
]
. Therefore, by Merkurjev’s theorem
[
20
, Theorem 2.11]
, Problem
1.1
has an affirmative answer in that case.
The remaining case is when
i=j+1
. In
[
1
]
, Auel et al. solved the problem affirmatively for
(i,j)=(2,1)
(cf.
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
), in which case the unramified cohomology
H^{2}_{\mathrm{ur}}(k(X)/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(1))
can be identified with the
p
-torsion subgroup of the Brauer group
{\rm Br}(X)=H^{2}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
.

In the present paper, we will extend their argument to the unramified cohomology group
H^{i+1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(k(X)/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))
, where
i
is an arbitrary non-negative integer. As the main result, we will prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

(cf. Corollary
5.5
)
Let
X
be a smooth geometrically connected variety over a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. Suppose that the degree map
{\rm deg}\colon H^{S}_{0}(X_{K})\to\mathbb{Z}
is an isomorphism for any finitely generated field extension
K/k
. Then for any
i\geq 0
, we have a natural isomorphism
H^{i+1}(k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{i+1}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))
.

Here,
H^{S}_{0}(X_{K})
stands for the
0
-th Suslin homology group (cf.
[
15
, §3]
) and the group
H^{i+1}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))
is a certain subgroup of the unramified cohomology group
H^{i+1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))
, which we call the
unramified curve-tame cohomology group
(cf. Definition
4.9
). In the case where
X
is proper over
k
, the unramified curve-tame cohomology recovers the unramified cohomology of the function field
k(X)
, i.e.
H^{i+1}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))=H^{i+1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(k(X)/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))

, and the natural quotient map
H^{S}_{0}(X_{K})\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{CH}_{0}(X_{K})
is an isomorphism. Therefore, as a corollary of the theorem, we obtain the following result, which gives a positive answer to Problem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.3
.

(cf. Corollary
5.6
)
Let
X
be a proper smooth variety over a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. Suppose that the degree map
{\rm deg}\colon\mathrm{CH}_{0}(X_{K})\to\mathbb{Z}
is an isomorphism for any field extension
K/k
. Then for any
i\geq 0
, we have a natural isomorphism
H^{i+1}(k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{i+1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(k(X)/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))
.

After writing up the first version of the present paper, the author learned the paper
[
3
]
by Binda–Rülling–Saito, in which Corollary
1.3
is obtained as a general fact on
reciprocity sheaves
. On the other hand, our proof is independent of new framework developed there. In
[
16
]
, a further different type of approach is discussed.

We explain the organization of the present paper.
In §
2
, we recall general facts on the logarithmic Hodge–Witt sheaves. We recall the statement of the Gersten-type conjecture established by Gros–Suwa
[
11
]
and Shiho
[
23
]
(cf. Theorem
2.1
). We also recall basic properties of corestriction map on the mod
p
étale motivic cohomology, which was defined by Kato (cf.
[
17
]
).
In §
3
, we recall the notion of unramified cohomology and discuss some properties of it.

In §
4
, we introduce two kinds of tame subgroups of the mod
p
unramified cohomology, namely the
naïve unramified tame cohomology

H^{i+1}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))
(cf. Definition
4.7
) and the
unramified curve-tame cohomology

H^{i+1}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))
(cf. Definition
4.9
). We see the former one admits a corestriction map for any finite surjective morphism of normal varieties (cf. Proposition
4.14
). However, we cannot see that it has enough functoriality property. For that reason, we consider the latter tame subgroup, which is respected by morphisms between regular varieties. In the case where
X=C
is a normal curve, these tame subgroups coincide with each other (cf. Proposition
4.12
(1)). The idea of considering tame subgroups goes back to the works due to Kato
[
18
]
, Izhboldin
[
14
]
, Garibaldi–Merkurjev–Serre
[
9
]
, Auel et al.
[
2
]
and Totaro
[
25
]
.

In §
5
, we prove Theorem
1.2
. The technical issue is the same as in
[
1
]
. Namely, for a smooth variety over a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
, we construct a family of pairings

\left\{H^{S}_{0}(X_{K})\times H^{i+1}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X_{K}/K,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))\to H^{i+1}(K,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))\right\}_{K}

which fulfills a satisfactory compatibility condition, where
K
is taken over all finitely generated field extensions of
k
(cf. Theorem
5.2
). To this end, we follow the argument in
[
1
, §3]
. The idea of dropping off the properness assumption from the original problem (cf. Problem
1.1
) goes back to the work of Bruno Kahn
[
15
]
. He generalized Merkurjev’s theorem
[
20
, Theorem 2.11]
to an open variety by replacing the Chow group with the Suslin homology group (cf.
[
15
, Corollary 4.7]
).

Notation

For an equidimensional scheme
X
and any integer
i\geq 0
, we denote by
X^{(i)}
(respectively
X_{(i)}
) the set of points of
X
of codimension
i
(respectively of dimension
i
).

Let
k
be a field. A
variety
over
k
is an integral separated scheme of finite type over
k
. A
curve
over
k
(or
k
-
curve
) is a variety over
k
of dimension one. Let
K
be a finitely generated field over
k
. A
model
of
K/k
is a proper variety over
k
together with an isomorphism
k(X)\xrightarrow{\simeq}K
of fields over
k
.

Let
K/k
be a finitely generated field extension of
k
. A valuation
v
on
K
over
k
is a valuation on
K
such that the associated valuation ring
\mathscr{O}_{v}
contains
k
as a subalgebra. A discrete rank one valuation
v
on
K
over
k
is said to be
geometric
if it satisfies the condition that

{\rm tr.deg}_{k}(K)={\rm tr.deg}_{k}(k(v))+1,

where
k(v)
is the residue field of
v
and
{\rm tr.deg}_{k}(L)
means the transcendental degree over
k
for any field extension
L/k
. According to
[
20
, Proposition 1.7]
, a discrete rank one valuation
v
on
K
over
k
is geometric if and only if there exists a normal model
X
of
K/k
such that the point
x
dominated by
v
is of codimension one and
\mathscr{O}_{v}=\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
. A geometric discrete rank one valuation on
K/k
is also called a
divisorial valuation
(cf.
[
1
, Definition 2.4]
).

2.
The logarithmic Hodge–Witt sheaves

Let
X
be a scheme over the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of positive characteristic
p>0
. For any integer
n\geq 1
, let
W_{n}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}
denote the de Rham–Witt complex of
X/\mathbb{F}_{p}
(cf.
[
13
, I, 1.3]
). Recall that for any morphism of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-schemes
f\colon Y\to X
, we have a canonical morphism of complexes of
W_{n}(\mathscr{O}_{Y})
-modules,

(2.1)

f^{-1}W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}\to W_{n}\Omega_{Y}^{\bullet}

(cf.
[
13
, I, (1.12.3)]
), which is an isomorphism if
f
is étale (cf.
[
13
, I, Proposition 1.14]
).

For any
i\geq 0
, we denote by
W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X,\log}
the logarithmic Hodge–Witt sheaf of
X
in the sense of
[
23
, Definition 2.6]
. Namely it is the étale sheaf on
X
defined as the image

W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X,\log}\overset{{\rm def}}{=}{\rm Im}\bigl((\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\times})^{\otimes i}\to W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X}\bigl),

of the map
(\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\times})^{\otimes i}\to W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X}\,;\,x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i}\mapsto\mathrm{dlog}[x_{1}]\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{dlog}[x_{i}]
, where
[x_{i}]\in W_{n}\mathscr{O}_{X}
is the Teichmüller representative of
x_{i}
.
If
f\colon Y\to X
is a morphism of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-schemes, by the functoriality of the de Rham–Witt complexes (
2.1
), there exists a natural morphism of étale sheaves on
Y
,

(2.2)

f^{-1}W_{n}\Omega_{X,\log}^{i}\to W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{Y,\log}.

In particular, we have the restriction maps

H^{j}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(X,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X,\log})\to H^{j}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(Y,W_{n}\Omega_{Y,\log}^{i})
.

According to
[
23
, Proposition 2.8]
, for a regular scheme
X
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we have the exact sequence on
X_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}
,

(2.3)

0\to\Omega^{i}_{X,\log}\to\Omega^{i}_{X}\xrightarrow{1-F}\Omega^{i}_{X}/d\Omega^{i-1}_{X}\to 0,

where
F\colon\Omega^{i}_{X}\to\Omega^{i}_{X}/d\Omega_{X}^{i-1}
is the map induced by the Frobenius
F\colon W_{2}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}\to W_{1}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}=\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}
of the de Rham–Witt complexes (cf.
[
23
, Lemma 2.7]
).

For an equidimensional scheme
X
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we have the coniveau spectral sequence

(2.4)

E_{1}^{s,t}=\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(s)}}H^{s+t}_{x}(X,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X,\log})\Rightarrow E^{s+t}=H^{s+t}(X,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X,\log})

(cf.
[
23
, §4]
). We set
B_{n}^{t,i}(X)^{\bullet}\overset{{\rm def}}{=}E_{1}^{\bullet,t}
. Then the following Gersten-type conjecture is established by Gros–Suwa for localizations of smooth algebras of finite type over a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
and by Shiho in the arbitrary case.

Theorem 2.1
.

(cf.
[
11
]
[
23
, Theorem 4.1]
)
Let
X=\operatorname{Spec}A
be the spectrum of an equidimensional regular local ring
A
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then we have

H^{m}(B_{n}^{q,i}(X)^{\bullet})=\begin{cases}H^{q}(X,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X,\log})&m=0,\\
0&m>0.\end{cases}

As a consequence, we have the following. For smooth varieties, see also
[
4
, Proposition A.10]
.

Corollary 2.2
.

(Gersten Type Conjecture)
Let
X
be an equidimensional regular scheme over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{q,i}(n)_{X}\overset{{\rm def}}{=}R^{q}\varepsilon_{*}W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X,\log}
, where
\varepsilon\colon X_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}\to X_{{\rm Zar}}
is the natural map of sites. Then we have an exact sequence

0\to H^{0}(X_{{\rm Zar}},\mathcal{H}^{q,i}(n)_{X})\to\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(0)}}H^{q}_{x}(X,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X,\log})\to\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(1)}}H^{q+1}_{x}(X,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X,\log}).

As a consequence, the cohomology functor
H^{q}(-,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{\log})
has the injectivity property and the codimension one purity property in
[
7
, Definition 2.1.4]
as follows.

Corollary 2.3
.

(1)

(Injectivity Property) If
R
is a regular local ring over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with the field of fractions
K
, then the restriction map

H^{q}(R,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{R,\log})\to H^{q}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{K,\log})

is injective.

(2)

(Codimension One Purity) If
R
is a regular local ring over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with the field of fractions
K
, then

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Im}\left(H^{q}(R,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{R,\log})\to H^{q}(K,W_{n}\Omega_{K,\log}^{i})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{{\rm ht}\,\mathfrak{p}=1}{\rm Im}\left(H^{q}(R_{\mathfrak{p}},W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}},\log})\to H^{q}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{K,\log})\right)

as subgroups of
H^{q}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{K,\log})
.

Next we will recall some basic properties of the corestriction map

\mathrm{Cor}_{L/K}\colon H^{i}(L,W_{n}\Omega^{j}_{L,\log})\to H^{i}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{j}_{K,\log})

defined by Kato (cf.
[
17
]
), where
L/K
is a finite extension of fields of characteristic
p>0
. Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. Then as
K
has
p
-cohomological dimension
\leq 1
, the group
H^{q}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{K,\log})
is zero unless
q=0,1
. If
q=0
, there exists a natural isomorphism

H^{0}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{K,\log})\simeq K^{\rm M}_{i}(K)/p^{n}

(cf.
[
6
]
). Thus, for any finite extension
L/K
of fields of characteristic
p>0
, the norm map
N_{L/K}\colon K^{\rm M}_{i}(L)\to K^{\rm M}_{i}(K)
of the Milnor K-groups induces the corestriction map

H^{0}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(L,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{L,\log})\to H^{0}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{K,\log})
.

Let us consider the case
q=1
. In
[
17
, p. 658]
, Kato defined the corestriction map

\mathrm{Cor}_{L/K}\colon H^{1}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(L,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{L,\log})\to H^{1}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{K,\log})

for any finite extension
L/K
of fields of characteristic
p>0
. The map
\mathrm{Cor}_{L/K}
is defined by using the norm maps of Quillen’s K-groups
K_{*}(L)\to K_{*}(K)
(cf.
[
21
]
). Recall also that the graded abelian group
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{1}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{K,\log})
has a natural right
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}K^{\rm M}_{i}(K)/p^{n}
-module structure (cf.
[
17
, p. 658]
). We denote by

[-,-\}\colon H^{1}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{K,\log})\times K^{\rm M}_{j}(K)/p^{n}\to H^{1}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{i+j}_{K,\log})

the corresponding multiplication.

The following are some properties of the map
\mathrm{Cor}_{L/K}
which we will use later as
n=1
.

(C1)

(cf.
[
23
, Remark 5.1(2)]
) For any finite extensions
K^{\prime\prime}/K^{\prime}/K
of fields of characteristic
p>0
, one has

\mathrm{Cor}_{K^{\prime}/K}\circ\mathrm{Cor}_{K^{\prime\prime}/K^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Cor}_{K^{\prime\prime}/K}.

(C2)

(cf.
[
17
, p. 658, Lemma 1(1)]
) Let
L/K
be a finite extension of fields of characteristic
p>0
. Then for any
w\in\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{1}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(L,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{L,\log})
and any
a\in\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}K^{\rm M}_{i}(K)/p^{n}
, one has

\mathrm{Cor}_{L/K}([w,a_{L}\})=[\mathrm{Cor}_{L/K}(w),a\}.

(C3)

(cf.
[
17
, p. 658, Lemma 1(2)]
). Let
L/K
be a finite extension of fields of characteristic
p>0
. Then for any
w\in\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{1}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(K,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{K,\log})
and any
a\in\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}K^{\rm M}_{i}(L)/p^{n}
, one has

\mathrm{Cor}_{L/K}([w_{L},a\})=[w,N_{L/K}(a)\}.

In particular, one has

(2.5)

\mathrm{Cor}_{L/K}\circ\mathrm{Res}_{L/K}=[L:K].

(C4)

Let
L/K
be a finite extension of fields of characteristic
p>0
. Let
K^{\prime}/K
be an arbitrary field extension. Suppose that
L\otimes_{K}K^{\prime}\simeq\prod_{i}L^{\prime}_{i}
a finite product of fields
L^{\prime}_{i}
. Then we have

\mathrm{Res}_{K^{\prime}/K}\circ\mathrm{Cor}_{L/K}=\sum_{i}\mathrm{Cor}_{L^{\prime}_{i}/K^{\prime}}\circ\mathrm{Res}_{L_{i}^{\prime}/L}.

By definition, this can be deduced from the corresponding property of the norm maps of Quillen’s K-groups (cf.
[
5
, Lemma 2.4]
).

Remark 2.4
.

The condition that
L\otimes_{K}K^{\prime}\simeq\prod_{i}L_{i}^{\prime}
in the last property holds if
L/K
or
K^{\prime}/K
is a separable extension. For example, if
K=k(X)
is the function field of a normal variety
X
over a field
k
and if
x\in X^{(1)}
, then the local ring
\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
is an excellent discrete valuation ring (cf.
[
24
, Lemma 07QU]
) and hence the completion
K\hookrightarrow K_{x}\overset{{\rm def}}{=}\operatorname{Frac}(\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{X,x})
is a separable extension. Therefore, for any finite extension
L/K
, the tensor product
L\otimes_{K}K_{x}
is isomorphic to a direct product of fields.

3.
The unramified cohomology

In this section, following
[
7
, §2–§4]
, we recall the notion of
unramified cohomology
and discuss several properties of it. Fix a base field
k
with characteristic
p\geq 0
, a prime number
\ell
(possibly
\ell=p
) and integers
n>0
and
j
. Let us consider the étale sheaf (complex) on
k
-schemes

\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}(j)=\begin{cases}\mu_{\ell^{n}}^{\otimes j},&\text{$\ell\neq p$},\\
W_{n}\Omega_{\log}^{j}[-j],&\text{$\ell=p$},\end{cases}

where the integer
j
is assumed to be non-negative when
\ell=p
. For any
k
-scheme
X
and any non-negative integer
i
, we denote by
H^{i,j}(X)
the
i
-th étale cohomology group with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}(j)
, i.e.

H^{i,j}(X)\overset{{\rm def}}{=}H^{i}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}(j)).

Note that when
\ell=p
, we have

H^{i,j}(X)=H^{i-j}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(X,W_{n}\Omega^{j}_{X,\log})
.

Now we define three types of unramified cohomology associated with the cohomology functor
H^{i,j}(-)
(see Definitions
3.2
,
3.5
and
3.7
). It turns out that their value at an integral
k
-variety
X
coincide with each other when
X
is proper and smooth over
k
(see Proposition
3.9
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
K/k
a finitely generated field extension of
k
. We denote by
\mathrm{Val}^{div}(K/k)
the set of geometric discrete rank one valuations on
K
over
k
.

Definition 3.2
.

(cf.
[
4
, §5]
)
For a finitely generated field extension
K/k
, we define the
unramified cohomology

H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(K/k)
to be

H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(K/k)\overset{{\rm def}}{=}\bigcap_{v\in\mathrm{Val}^{div}(K/k)}{\rm Im}\left(H^{i,j}(\mathscr{O}_{v})\to H^{i,j}(K)\right),

where
\mathscr{O}_{v}
is the valuation ring associated with each
v
.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that for any
v\in\mathrm{Val}^{div}(K/k)
, the valuation ring
\mathscr{O}_{v}
is a discrete valuation ring with the fraction field
K
. Thus, when
\ell=p
, by Corollary
2.3
(1), the map
H^{i,j}(\mathscr{O}_{v})\to H^{i,j}(K)
is injective and the group
H^{i,j}(\mathscr{O}_{v})
can be naturally viewed as a subgroup of
H^{i,j}(K)
, in which case we have the equality

H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(K/k)=\bigcap_{v\in\mathrm{Val}^{div}(K/k)}H^{i,j}(\mathscr{O}_{v}).

as subgroups of
H^{i,j}(K)
.

Remark 3.4
.

(Functriality Property for field extensions)
Let
L/K
be a field extension of finitely generated fields over
k
. Then for any
w\in\mathrm{Val}^{div}(K/k)
, the restriction
v\overset{{\rm def}}{=}w|_{K}
is trivial or it belongs to
\mathrm{Val}^{div}(K/k)
(cf.
[
20
, Proposition 1.4]
). This implies that the natural restriction map
H^{i,j}(K)\to H^{i,j}(L)
induces a map between the unramified cohomology groups
H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(K/k)\to H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(L/k)
and the correspondence
K\mapsto H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(K/k)
is a covariant functor of the category of finitely generated field extensions of
k
into the category of abelian groups.

The second kind unramified cohomology is defined as below.

Definition 3.5
.

For a normal variety
X
over a field
k
, we define the
unramified cohomology

H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)
to be

H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)\overset{{\rm def}}{=}\bigcap_{x\in X^{(1)}}{\rm Im}\left(H^{i,j}(\mathscr{O}_{X,x})\to H^{i,j}(k(X))\right).

Remark 3.6
.

If
X
is normal, then as the local ring
\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
is a discrete valuation ring with
k\subset\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
and with fraction field
k(X)
for any codimension one point
x\in X^{(1)}
, we have an obvious inclusion

H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(k(X)/k)\subseteq H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X).

as subgroups of
H^{i,j}(k(X))
.

Finally let us introduce the third version of the unramified cohomology associated with the cohomology functor
H^{i,j}(-)
.

Definition 3.7
.

For a
k
-scheme
X
, let
\varepsilon\colon X_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}\to X_{{\rm Zar}}
be the natural map of sites. Then for any
i,j\geq 0
, we set
\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{X}\overset{{\rm def}}{=}R^{i}\varepsilon_{*}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}(j)_{X})
and consider the group

H^{0}_{{\rm Zar}}(X,\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{X})

of global sections of the Zariski sheaf
\mathcal{H}_{X}^{i,j}
as a version of unramified cohomology attached to the cohomology group
H^{i,j}(-)
.

Remark 3.8
.

(Full functoriality property, cf.
[
7
, Remark 4.1.2]
)
For any morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of schemes over
k
, there exists a natural map of étale sheaves
f^{-1}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}(j)_{Y})\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}(j)_{X}
,
which induces a morphism of Zariski sheaves

f^{*}\colon f^{-1}\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{Y}\to\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{X}

in a canonical way. In particular, we have a natural restriction map

(3.1)

f^{*}\colon H^{0}_{{\rm Zar}}(Y,\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{Y})\to H^{0}_{{\rm Zar}}(X,\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{X})

and the correspondence
X\mapsto H^{0}_{{\rm Zar}}(X,\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{X})
is a contravariant functor of the category of all
k
-varieties to the category of abelian groups.

Let
X
be a normal variety over
k
.
The restriction map

H^{0}_{{\rm Zar}}(X,\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{X})\to H^{0}_{{\rm Zar}}(k(X),\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{k(X)})=H^{i,j}(k(X))

factors through the second type unramified cohomology group of
X
(cf. Definition
3.5
), i.e.

H^{0}_{{\rm Zar}}(X,\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{X})\to H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)\to H^{i,j}(k(X)).

Now we can compare the three types of unramified cohomology in the following way.

Proposition 3.9
.

(cf.
[
7
, Theorem 4.1.1]
)
Let
X
be a smooth variety over
k
. Then there exists a natural isomorphism of abelian groups

H^{0}_{{\rm Zar}}(X,\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{X})\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X).

If
X
is proper over
k
in addition, then we have

H^{0}_{{\rm Zar}}(X,\mathcal{H}^{i,j}_{X})\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)=H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(k(X)/k).

Furthermore, in the case when
\ell=p
, the same conclusions hold for regular varieties over
k
.

Proof.

In the case when
\ell\neq p
, all the statements are included in
[
7
, Theorem 4.1.1]
. In the case when
\ell=p
, the same proof as there works by replacing the codimension one purity theorem
[
7
, Theorem 3.8.2]
with our version for logarithmic Hodge-Witt cohomology (see Corollary
2.3
(2)) and by applying the argument given in
[
7
, Proposition 2.1.8]
. The last statement is thanks to the fact that the codimension one purity theorem for
\ell=p
holds for arbitrary regular local rings over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
∎

As a consequence, we have the following.

Corollary 3.10
.

(1)

(Birational Invariance) With the above notation, the unramified cohomology
H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)
is a
k
-birational invariant for proper smooth
k
-varieties.

(2)

(Functoriality Property) The correspondence
X\mapsto H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)
is a contravariant functor of the category of smooth
k
-varieties into the category of abelian groups. Furthermore, in the case when
\ell=p
, the same functoriality property can be extended to the category of regular integral schemes over
k
.

Proof.

(1)

As mentioned in
[
7
, Proposition 2.1.8 (e)]
, the claim is immediate from the equivalence between Definitions
3.2
and
3.5
for proper smooth (or regular)
k
-varieties in Proposition
3.9
.

(2)

As discussed in
[
7
, Remark 4.1.2]
, the claim follows from the equivalence between Definitions
3.5
and
3.7
for smooth (or regular)
k
-varieties in Proposition
3.9
together with Remark
3.8
. In the case when
\ell=p
, this follows also from the injectivity property (Corollary
2.3
(1)) by applying the argument in
[
7
, Proposition 2.1.10]
.

∎

Remark 3.11
.

Let us assume that
\ell=p
. Let
C
be a normal
k
-curve. Then for any
i,j\geq 0
, the restriction map
H^{i,j}(C)\to H^{i,j}(k(C))
induces a surjective homomorphism

H^{i,j}(C)\twoheadrightarrow H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(C)
.
Indeed, as
C
is of dimension one, this follows from the coniveau spectral sequence

E_{1}^{s,t}=\bigoplus_{x\in C^{(s)}}H^{s+t,,j}_{x}(C)\Rightarrow E^{s+t}=H^{s+t,j}(C)

together with Proposition
3.9
.
Therefore, if the restriction map
H^{i,j}(C)\to H^{i,j}(k(C))
is injective, we have an isomorphism
H^{i,j}(C)\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(C)
.

Remark 3.12
.

If
\ell\neq p
, then the cohomology group
H^{i,j}(-)=H^{i}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(-,\mu_{\ell^{n}}^{\otimes j})
gives rise to a Rost’s cycle module
M_{*}
over
k
as below

M_{*}(-)\overset{{\rm def}}{=}\bigoplus_{*\geq-i}H^{i+*}(-,\mu_{\ell^{n}}^{\otimes j+*})

(see
[
22
, Remarks 1.11 and 2.5]
), and for any smooth variety
X
over
k
, the unramified cohomology
H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)
is canonically identified with the Chow group
A^{0}(X,M_{0})
with coefficients in
M_{0}
in the sense of Rost (cf.
[
22
, §5]
), i.e.

H^{i,j}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)\simeq A^{0}(X,M_{0}).

In the case where
\ell=p
, the same description of unramified cohomology in terms of cycle modules still holds when
i=j
. Namely, for the cohomology group
H^{i,i}(-)=H^{0}(-,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{\log})
, we have

H^{i,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)\simeq A^{0}(X,K_{i}^{\rm M}/p^{n}).

However, as remarked in the first paragraph in the next section, Rost’s theory of cycle modules cannot be directly adapted for the unramified cohomology group
H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)
.

4.
The unramified curve-tame cohomology

Let us restrict our attention to the mod
p
étale motivic cohomology group

H^{i+1,i}(X)=H^{i+1}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))=H^{1}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(X,\Omega^{i}_{X,\log}).

In the case
i=0
, we have
H^{1,0}(X)=H^{1}_{\text{{\rm\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
. If
i=1
, then the group
H^{2,1}(X)
can be identified with
H^{2}_{{\rm fppf}}(X,\mu_{p})
. Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and let us consider the affine line
X=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
. For these cases
i=0,1
, by purity, we obtain

H^{i+1,i}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k})\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k})

(Remark
3.11
). As is well-known, the natural map
H^{i+1,i}(k)\to H^{i+1,i}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k})
is far from isomorphic (in general) in both the cases
i=0,1
. Therefore, the unramified cohomology
H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)
is not
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy invariant. Hence, the collection of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded abelian groups
\left\{\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{i+1,i}(K)\right\}_{K}
does not form a cycle module in the sense of Rost
[
22
]
. To remedy the situation, we will consider
tame
subgroups of
H^{i+1,i}(K)
(cf.
[
18
]
[
14
]
[
9
]
[
2
]
[
25
]
).

We begin with the local case. For a complete discrete valuation field
K
of characteristic
p>0
, we define the
tame cohomology group

H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(K)
to be the kernel of the restriction map
H^{i+1,i}(K)\to H^{i+1,i}(K^{t})
, where
K^{t}
is a maximal tamely ramified extension of
K
(cf.
[
25
, §4]
). Note that as
H^{i+1,i}(K)
is
p
-torsion, the tame cohomology group
H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(K)
can be written also as the kernel of the restriction homomorphism
H^{i+1,i}(K)\to H^{i+1,i}(K^{ur})
, where
K^{ur}
is the maximal unramified extension of
K
(cf.
[
2
, Remark 3.7]
).

For a field
K
of characteristic
p>0
with a geometric discrete rank one valuation
v
, we define the
tame cohomology group

H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},v}(K)\subset H^{i+1,i}(K)
to be the inverse image of the tame cohomology
H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(K_{v})
via the restriction map
H^{i+1,i}(K)\to H^{i+1,i}(K_{v})
, where
K_{v}
is the completion of
K
with respect to the valuation
v
. Then, according to
[
25
, Theorem 4.3]
(see also
[
14
, §2, Corollary 2.7]
for complete discrete valuation fields), there exists a homomorphism
\partial_{v}\colon H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},v}(K)\to H^{i,i-1}(k(v))
called the
residue map
at
v
which fits into the short exact sequence

(4.1)

0\to H^{i+1,i}(\mathscr{O}_{v})\to H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},v}(K)\xrightarrow{\partial_{v}}H^{i,i-1}(k(v))\to 0,

where
\mathscr{O}_{v}
is the valuation ring of
v
.

The residue map
\partial_{v}
can be described as follows. Recall that we have an exact sequence of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector spaces (cf. (
2.3
)),

0\to H^{i,i}(K)\to\Omega^{i}_{K}\xrightarrow{F-1}\Omega^{i}_{K}/d\Omega_{K}^{i-1}\to H^{i+1,i}(K)\to 0.

We denote by
[f,g_{1},\dots,g_{i}\}
the image in
H^{i+1,i}(K)
of the differential form

f\,\dfrac{dg_{1}}{g_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dfrac{dg_{i}}{g_{i}}~\in~\Omega^{i}_{K}.

Then the tame cohomology group
H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},v}(K)
is generated by the elements of the form

[f,g_{1},\dots,g_{i}\}

where
f\in K,g_{1},\dots,g_{i}\in K^{*}
satisfying
v(f)\geq 0
(cf.
[
25
, Theorem 4.3]
). The residue map
\partial_{v}\colon H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},v}(K)\to H^{i,i-1}(k(v))
is now uniquely characterized by the following formula

\partial_{v}([f,g_{1},\dots,g_{i}\})=\begin{cases}[\overline{f},\overline{g}_{2},\dots,\overline{g}_{i}\}&\text{if $v(g_{1})=1$ and $v(g_{i})=0$ for $i\neq 1$},\\
0&\text{if $v(g_{i})=0$ for any $i$},\end{cases}

where
\overline{f}
and
\overline{g_{i}}
mean the images of
f
and
g_{i}
respectively in the residue field
k(v)
. It follows immediately from this characterization that for any generator
[f,g_{1},\dots,g_{i}\}\in H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},v}(K)
, we have

(4.2)

\partial_{v}([f,g_{1},\dots,g_{i}\})=[\overline{f},\partial_{v}^{\rm M}(\{g_{1},\dots,g_{i}\})\},

where
\partial_{v}^{\rm M}
is the tame symbol of the Milnor
K
-group (cf.
[
12
, Proposition 7.1.4]
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
K
be a field of positive characteristic
p>0
and
v
a geometric discrete rank one valuation on
K
. Let
\mathscr{O}_{v}
be the valuation ring and
k(v)
the residue field. Fix a uniformizer
\pi\in\mathscr{O}_{v}
. Then the natural reduction map of cohomology groups
H^{i+1,i}(\mathscr{O}_{v})\to H^{i+1,i}(k(v))
coincides with the composition of maps

H^{i+1,i}(\mathscr{O}_{v})\xrightarrow{[-,\pi\}}H^{i+2,i+1}_{{\rm tame},v}(K)\xrightarrow{\partial_{v}}H^{i+1,i}(k(v))

up to multiplication by
(-1)^{i}
.

Proof.

Noticing that the subgroup
H^{i+1,i}(\mathscr{O}_{v})
is generated by the elements
[f,g_{1},\dots,g_{i}\}
with
f\in\mathscr{O}_{v}
and
g_{1},\dots,g_{i}\in\mathscr{O}_{v}^{*}
,
the lemma is immediate from the description of the residue map (
4.2
).
∎

Definition 4.2
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and
K
a finitely generated field extension of
k
. We define the
tame cohomology

H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(K/k)
to be

H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(K/k)\overset{{\rm def}}{=}\bigcap_{v\in\mathrm{Val}^{div}(K/k)}H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},v}(K)\subset H^{i+1,i}(K).

Note that for any
v\in\mathrm{Val}^{div}(K/k)
, the graded subspace
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},v}(K)\subset\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{i+1,i}(K)
is stable under the multiplication map

[-,-\}\colon H^{i+1,i}(K)\times K_{j}^{\rm M}(K)/p\to H^{i+j+1,i+j}(K)

(cf.
[
9
, p. 153]
), and hence
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},v}(K)
is a graded
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}K^{\rm M}_{i}(K)/p
-submodule of
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{i+1,i}(K)
. Therefore, the graded submodule
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(K/k)
is also a graded
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}K^{\rm M}_{i}(K)/p
-submodule of the group
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{i+1,i}(K)
.

It is immediate from the exact sequence (
4.1
) that there exists a short exact sequence

(4.3)

0\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(K/k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(K/k)\xrightarrow{(\partial_{v})}\bigoplus_{v\in\mathrm{Val}^{div}(K/k)}H^{i,i-1}(k(v)).

Moreover, we have the following.

Theorem 4.3
.

(cf.
[
25
, Theorem 4.4]
)
Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. For any
i\geq 0
, there exists an exact sequence of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector spaces,

0\to H^{i+1,i}(k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(k(t)/k)\xrightarrow{(\partial_{x})_{x}}\bigoplus_{x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k(0)}}H^{i,i-1}(k(x))\xrightarrow{\sum_{x}\mathrm{Cor}_{k(x)/k}}H^{i,i-1}(k)\to 0.

In other words, the tame cohomology satisfies the the
homotopy property
for
\mathbb{A}^{1}
and the
reciprocity
for the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in
[
22
, §2]
.

Remark 4.4
.

(1)

(Homotopy Property for
\mathbb{A}^{1}
) The short exact sequence

0\to H^{i+1,i}(k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(k(t)/k)\xrightarrow{(\partial_{x})_{x}}\bigoplus_{x\in\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k(0)}}H^{i,i-1}(k(x))\to 0,

is exact.

(2)

(Reciprocity for
\mathbb{P}^{1}
) The sequence

H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(k(t)/k)\xrightarrow{(\partial_{x})_{x}}\bigoplus_{x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k(0)}}H^{i,i-1}(k(x))\xrightarrow{\sum_{x}\mathrm{Cor}_{k(x)/k}}H^{i,i-1}(k)

is a complex.

Remark 4.5
.

(Reciprocity for Curves) More generally, for any
k
-curve
C
, it turns out that the sequence

H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(k(C)/k)\xrightarrow{(\partial_{x})_{x}}\bigoplus_{x\in C_{(0)}}H^{i,i-1}(k(x))\xrightarrow{\sum_{x}\mathrm{Cor}_{k(x)/k}}H^{i,i-1}(k)

becomes a complex. Indeed, by taking a finite morphism
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
and by making use of corestriction maps on tame cohomology which we discuss later (see (
4.4
)), one can deduce the claim from Remark
4.4
(2).

Lemma 4.6
.

(Functoriality Property for field extensions)
Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and
L/K
an extension of finitely generated fields over
k
. Then the restriction map
H^{i+1,i}(K)\to H^{i+1,i}(L)
induces a map
H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(K/k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(L/k)
and the correspondence
K\mapsto H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(K/k)
is a covariant functor of the category of finitely generated field extensions over
k
into the category of abelian groups.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(K/k)
be an element. Suppose that
\alpha_{L}\not\in H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(L/k)
. Then by definition, there exists a geometric discrete rank one valuation
w
on
L
such that
\alpha_{L_{w}}\not\in H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(L_{w})
. As
H^{i+1,i}(\mathscr{O}_{w})\subset H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(L_{w})
, the restriction
v\overset{{\rm def}}{=}w|_{K}
must be nontrivial, and hence it gives a geometric discrete rank one valuation on
K
over
k
(cf.
[
20
, Proposition 1.4]
). Let us consider the commutative diagram of field extensions

\textstyle{L\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L_{w}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L_{w}^{t}}
\textstyle{K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{v}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{v}^{t}.\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Then the condition that
\alpha_{L_{w}}\not\in H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(L_{w})
implies that
\alpha_{K_{v}}\not\in H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(K_{v})
, which is a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

Next we introduce two types of tame subgroup (see Definitions
4.7
and
4.9
below) of the unramified cohomology
H_{\mathrm{ur}}^{i+1,i}(X)
(cf. Definition
3.5
).

Definition 4.7
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and
X
a normal variety over
k
. We define the
naïve unramified tame cohomology

H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
to be

H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)\overset{{\rm def}}{=}H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)\cap H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(k(X)/k)\subset H^{i+1,i}(k(X)).

Remark 4.8
.

(Functoriality Property for dominant morphisms)
Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a morphism between regular varieties over
k
. Then, by Corollary
3.10
(2), we get a natural restriction map between the unramified cohomology groups
f^{*}\colon H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(Y)
. If in addition
f
is dominant, by Lemma
4.6
, we also find that the map
H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(k(X)/k)\subset H^{i+1,i}(k(X))\xrightarrow{f^{*}}H^{i+1,i}(k(Y))
factors through the tame cohomology
H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame}}(k(Y)/k)
. By putting together these facts, we obtain a natural restriction map between the naïve unramified tame cohomology groups

f^{*}\colon H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(Y/k).

Moreover, the same argument as above implies that for any smooth geometrically connected variety
X
over
k
and any finitely generated field extension
K/k
such that the base change
X_{K}
of
X
along the extension
K/k
is regular and integral, the projection map
X_{K}\to X
induces a map
H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(X_{K}/K)
.

The following is another type of tame subgroup, whose definition is motivated by the work of Kerz–Schmidt
[
19
]
.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and
X
a regular variety over
k
. We define the
unramified curve-tame cohomology

H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
to be the subgroup of the unramified cohomology group
H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)
which consists of elements
\alpha\in H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)
such that for any finitely generated field extension
K/k
and for any
k
-morphism
C\to X
from any normal
K
-curve
C
, the restriction
\alpha|_{C}\in H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(C)
belongs to the subgroup
H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(C/K)
.

Remark 4.10
.

In the above definition, as
X
is regular, thanks to Corollary
3.10
(2), the restriction map

H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(C)\,;\,\alpha\mapsto\alpha|_{C}

of the unramified cohomology groups is well-defined. Hence, Definition
4.9
makes sense.

By definition, the unramified curve-tame cohomology has full functoriality as follows.

Proposition 4.11
.

(Functoriality Property)
Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and
K/k
a finitely generated field extension. Let
X
be a regular variety over
k
and
Y
a regular variety over

. Let

be a morphism over
k
, where

is viewed as a

-scheme via the composition

. Then the natural restriction map
f^{*}\colon H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(Y)
induces the map
H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(Y/K)
between the unramified curve-tame cohomology groups.

Proof.

This is immediate from Definition
4.9
.
∎

For later use, we collect some basic facts on the tame subgroups in the next proposition.

5.

Let

\displaystyle Z_{0}(X)\times H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)\subset

\displaystyle Z_{0}(X)\times H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(x^{*})_{x\in X_{(0)}}}

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{x\in X_{(0)}}H^{i+1,i}(k(x))\xrightarrow{\sum_{x\in X_{(0)}}\mathrm{Cor}_{k(x)/k}}H^{i+1,i}(k).

Here, for each closed point
x\in X_{(0)}
, thanks to Corollary
3.10
(2), the restriction map

x^{*}\colon H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(\operatorname{Spec}k(x))=H^{i+1,i}(k(x))

is well-defined. For any finitely generated field extension
K/k
, this construction similarly gives a pairing

Z_{0}(X_{K})\times H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X_{K}/K)\to H^{i+1,i}(K).

Then the pairing satisfies the following partial compatibility conditions, which are enough for our later application.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
k,X
be as above.
Let
\langle-,-\rangle
be the pairing given in (
5.2
).

(1)

Let
K\overset{{\rm def}}{=}k(X)
be the function field. Let
\eta\in(X_{K})_{(0)}
be the closed point associated with the generic point of
X
. Then the composite

H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X_{K}/K)\xrightarrow{\langle\eta,-\rangle}H^{i+1,i}(K)

gives the natural inclusion map
H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)\subset H^{i+1,i}(K)
.

(2)

Let
x\in X_{(0)}
be a closed point whose residue extension
k(x)/k
is separable. Let
\alpha\in H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
be an arbitrary element. Then we have
\langle x_{K},\alpha_{K}\rangle=\langle x,\alpha\rangle_{K}
in
H^{i+1,i}(K)
for any finitely generated field extension
K/k
.

Proof.

(1) By Proposition
4.11
, we have the commutative diagram

\textstyle{H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X_{K}/K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ur}}(X_{K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\eta^{*}}
\textstyle{H^{i+1,i}(K).}

Thus, the assertion holds.

(2) Let
f\colon X_{K}\to X
be the natural projection map. Then the fiber is given by
f^{-1}(x)=\operatorname{Spec}(K\otimes_{k}k(x))
. As
k(x)/k
is a finite separable extension, the fiber
f^{-1}(x)
is reduced and hence
x_{K}=\sum_{y\mid x}y
in
Z_{0}(X_{K})
. Therefore, by the equation (C
4
), we have

\displaystyle\langle x,\alpha\rangle_{K}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{K/k}\circ\mathrm{Cor}_{k(x)/k}(x^{*}\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\mid x}\mathrm{Cor}_{K(y)/K}\circ\mathrm{Res}_{K(y)/k(x)}(x^{*}(\alpha))

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\mid x}\mathrm{Cor}_{K(y)/K}(y^{*}(\alpha_{K}))=\langle x_{K},\alpha_{K}\rangle.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we prove the key result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth geometrically connected variety over a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. Let
i\geq 0
be an integer. For any finitely generated field extension
K/k
, there exists a pairing

H^{S}_{0}(X_{K})\times H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X_{K}/K)\to H^{i+1,i}(K)

which satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

If
\eta\in(X_{k(X)})_{(0)}
is the closed point associated with the generic point
\operatorname{Spec}k(X)\to X
, then the composite
H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X_{k(X)}/k(X))\xrightarrow{\langle\eta,-\rangle}H^{i+1,i}(k(X))

is given by the natural inclusion map
H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)\subset H^{i+1,i}(k(X))
.

(2)

If
x\in X_{(0)}
is a closed point such that the residue extension
k(x)/k
is separable, then for any element
\alpha\in H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
and for any finitely generated field extension
K/k
, we have
\langle x_{K},\alpha_{K}\rangle=\langle x,\alpha\rangle_{K}
in
H^{i+1,i}(K)
.

For the proof, we need two lemmas.

Lemma 5.3
.

With the same notation as in Theorem
5.2
, let
\phi\colon C\to X
be a morphism from a normal
k
-curve
C
into
X
. Then for any
0
-cycle
z\in Z_{0}(C)
and any element
\alpha\in H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
, we have

\langle\phi_{*}(z),\alpha\rangle=\langle z,\phi^{*}(\alpha)\rangle,

where
\langle-,-\rangle
is the pairing given by (
5.2
).

Proof.

We adapt the same argument as in the proof of
[
1
, Lemma 3.2]
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
z
is a closed point of
C
. Recall that
\phi_{*}(z)=[k(z):k(x)]x
, where we put
x\overset{{\rm def}}{=}\phi(z)
. Since the diagram

\textstyle{\operatorname{Spec}k(z)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{~~z}
\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Spec}k(x)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{~~x}
\textstyle{X}

is commutative, by the transitivity of corestriction maps (C
1
) together with the equality
\mathrm{Cor}_{k(z)/k(x)}\circ\mathrm{Res}_{k(z)/k(x)}=[k(z):k(x)]
(cf. (
2.5
)), we have

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{*}(z),\alpha\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cor}_{k(x)/k}(\mathrm{Cor}_{k(z)/k(x)}(\mathrm{Res}_{k(z)/k(x)}(x^{*}\alpha)))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cor}_{k(z)/k}(\mathrm{Res}_{k(z)/k(x)}(x^{*}\alpha))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cor}_{k(z)/k}(z^{*}\phi^{*}\alpha)=\langle z,\phi^{*}\alpha\rangle.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

With the same notation as in Theorem
5.2
, let
\phi\colon\Gamma\to C
be a finite surjective morphism between normal
k
-curves. Let
x\colon\operatorname{Spec}k(x)\to C
be a closed point and
\Gamma_{x}\overset{{\rm def}}{=}\phi^{-1}(x)
the scheme-theoretic fiber of
x
. Then for any
\alpha\in H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(\Gamma/k)
, we have

\langle\Gamma_{x},\alpha\rangle=\langle x,\mathrm{Cor}^{{\rm t}}_{\Gamma/C}(\alpha)\rangle,

where
\langle-,-\rangle
is the pairing given by (
5.2
).

Proof.

Fix a uniformizer
\pi_{x}\in\mathscr{O}_{C,x}
at
x
. Moreover, for each point
y\in\Gamma
lying above
x
, fix a uniformizer
\pi_{y}\in\mathscr{O}_{\Gamma,y}
and put
e_{y}\overset{{\rm def}}{=}v_{y}(\pi_{x})
. By using Lemmas
4.1
and
4.16
together with the formula (C
2
), one can compute

\displaystyle\langle x,\mathrm{Cor}^{{\rm t}}_{\Gamma/C}(\alpha)\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cor}_{k(x)/k}(x^{*}\mathrm{Cor}^{{\rm t}}_{\Gamma/C}(\alpha))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\mathrm{Cor}_{k(x)/k}(\partial_{x}([\mathrm{Cor}^{{\rm t}}_{\Gamma/C}(\alpha),\pi_{x}\}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\mathrm{Cor}_{k(x)/k}(\partial_{x}(\mathrm{Cor}^{{\rm t}}_{k(\Gamma)/k(C)}([\alpha,\phi^{*}(\pi_{x})\})))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\mathrm{Cor}_{k(x)/k}(\sum_{y\mid x}\mathrm{Cor}_{k(y)/k(x)}(\partial_{y}([\alpha,\phi^{*}(\pi_{x})\})))

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\mid x}e_{y}\mathrm{Cor}_{k(y)/k}((-1)^{i}\partial_{y}([\alpha,\pi_{y}\}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\mid x}e_{y}\mathrm{Cor}_{k(y)/k}(y^{*}\alpha)=\sum_{y\mid x}e_{y}\langle y,\alpha\rangle=\langle\Gamma_{x},\alpha\rangle.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

If one proves the pairing (
5.2
) factors through
H^{S}_{0}(X)\times H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
, then the required conditions (i) and (ii) are immediate from Lemma
5.1
. Let
\alpha\in H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
be an arbitrary element. Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\times X
be an integral closed subscheme such that the projection
\phi\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
is finite and surjective. Let
\Gamma_{0}\overset{{\rm def}}{=}\phi^{-1}(0)
and
\Gamma_{1}\overset{{\rm def}}{=}\phi^{-1}(1)
be the scheme-theoretic fibers of
0
and
1
respectively. We have to show that

\langle{\rm pr}_{X*}(\Gamma_{0}-\Gamma_{1}),\alpha\rangle=0.

First note that

\Gamma_{0}-\Gamma_{1}=\phi^{*}{\rm div}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}}\Bigl(\frac{t}{t-1}\Bigl)={\rm div}_{\Gamma}\Bigl(\frac{\phi^{*}(t)}{\phi^{*}(t)-1}\Bigl)=\pi_{*}{\rm div}_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\Bigl(\frac{\phi^{*}(t)}{\phi^{*}(t)-1}\Bigl)=\pi_{*}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}),

where
\pi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
is the normalization of the curve
\Gamma
. Thus by applying Lemma
5.3
to the composition
\widetilde{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{\pi}\Gamma\xrightarrow{{\rm pr}_{X}}X
, we are reduced to prove that for any finite surjective morphism
\phi\colon C\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
from a normal connected
k
-curve and any element
\alpha\in H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(C/k)
, we have
\langle C_{0},\alpha\rangle=\langle C_{1},\alpha\rangle
. However, according to Lemma
5.4
, we have

\langle C_{i},\alpha\rangle=s_{i}^{*}\mathrm{Cor}^{{\rm t}}_{C/\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}}(\alpha)

for
i=0,1
. On the other hand, by Proposition
4.12
(3), we have the natural isomorphism
H^{i+1,i}(k)\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}/k)
. Therefore,
s_{0}^{*}=s_{1}^{*}
on
H^{i+1,i}_{{\rm tame},\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}/k)
, which implies the desired equality
\langle C_{0},\alpha\rangle=\langle C_{1},\alpha\rangle
. This completes the proof.
∎

As an application of the theorem, we have the following.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth geometrically connected variety over a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. Suppose that for any finitely generated field extension
K/k
, the degree map induces an isomorphism
{\rm deg}\colon H^{S}_{0}(X_{K})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{Z}
. Then for any integer
i\geq 0
, we have the natural isomorphism
H^{i+1,i}(k)\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
.

Proof.

Let us prove the injectivity of the map
H^{i+1,i}(k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
. As the map
{\rm deg}\colon H^{S}_{0}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
is surjective, there exists a
0
-cycle
z\in Z_{0}(X)
such that
{\rm deg}(z)=1
. It suffices to notice that the composition

H^{i+1,i}(k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)\xrightarrow{\langle z,-\rangle}H^{i+1,i}(k)

is the identity map on
H^{i+1,i}(k)
. This follows from the equation (
2.5
) together with the condition that
{\rm deg}(z)=1
. Thus, the natural map
H^{i+1,i}(k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
is injective.

Let us prove the surjectivity of the map
H^{i+1,i}(k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
.
Let
\alpha\in H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
be an arbitrary element. It suffices to show that
\alpha
is in the image of the map
H^{i+1,i}(k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
. We follow the argument in
[
1
, §4]
. Let
\eta\in(X_{k(X)})_{(0)}
be the generic point of
X
.
As the degree map
{\rm deg}\colon H^{S}_{0}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
is surjective, by
[
8
, Theorem 9.2]
, there exists a
0
-cycle
z\in Z_{0}(X)
such that
{\rm deg}(z)=1
and
z
is supported on closed points having separable residue extension. Therefore, Theorem
5.2
(2) implies that

\langle z_{k(X)},\alpha_{k(X)}\rangle=\langle z,\alpha\rangle_{k(X)}.

On the other hand, as
{\rm deg}(z_{k(X)})={\rm deg}(\eta)
, the injectivity of the map
{\rm deg}\colon H^{S}_{0}(X_{k(X)})\to\mathbb{Z}
implies that

\langle z_{k(X)},\alpha_{k(X)}\rangle=\langle\eta,\alpha_{k(X)}\rangle=\alpha,

where the last equality follows from Theorem
5.2
(1). Thus
\alpha=\langle z,\alpha\rangle_{k(X)}
belongs to the image of the natural map
H^{i+1,i}(k)\to H^{i+1,i}_{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{ur}}(X/k)
. This completes the proof.
∎

By applying Corollary
5.5
to proper smooth varieties, we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
X
be a proper smooth variety over a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. Suppose that the degree map
{\rm deg}\colon\mathrm{CH}_{0}(X_{K})\to\mathbb{Z}
is an isomorphism for any field extension
K/k
. Then for any
i\geq 0
, we have a natural isomorphism
H^{i+1}(k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{i+1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(k(X)/k,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}(i))
.

Proof.

Noticing that for a proper smooth variety over a field
k
, there exists a natural isomorphism
H_{0}^{S}(X_{K})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{CH}_{0}(X_{K})

of abelian groups for any field extension
K/k
, the claim immediately follows from Corollary
5.5
together with Proposition
4.12
(2).
∎

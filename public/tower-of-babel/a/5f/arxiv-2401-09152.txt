On Cohen-Macaulay non-prime collections of cells
CARMELO CISTO, RIZWAN JAHANGIR, FRANCESCO NAVARRA

Abstract
. In this paper we investigate Cohen-Macaulayness, Gorensteinness and the Hilbert-Poincaré series for some classes of non-prime collections of cells. In particular, we show that all closed path polyominoes are Cohen-Macaulay and we characterize those that are Gorenstein.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
05B50, 05E40

Introduction

Combinatorial Commutative Algebra has been an intensive area of research since the pioneering work of R. Stanley (
[
34
]
). In 2012 a new topic emerged in this field due to the work of A. A. Qureshi (
[
29
]
). She established a connection between collections of cells and Commutative Algebra, assigning to every collection
\mathcal{P}
of cells the ideal of the inner 2-minors of
\mathcal{P}
in a suitable polynomial ring
S_{\mathcal{P}}
. This ideal
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is called the
inner 2-minor
ideal of
\mathcal{P}
and
K[\mathcal{P}]=S_{\mathcal{P}}/I_{\mathcal{P}}
is said to be the
coordinate ring
of
\mathcal{P}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{P}
is a polyomino, that is a collection of cells where the cells are joined edge by edge, then
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is called the
polyomino ideal
of
\mathcal{P}
. The aim of the research is to study the main algebraic properties of
K[\mathcal{P}]
depending on the geometry of
\mathcal{P}
. The most investigated include primality, Cohen-Macaulayness and Gorensteinness. As usual, we say that a collection of cells is prime (respectively, Cohen-Macaulay or Gorenstein) if the related binomial ideal is prime (respectively, Cohen-Macaulay or Gorenstein).
Regarding the classification of the primality for polyominoes, it is proven in
[
19
,
20
,
31
]
that if
\mathcal{P}
is simple, which means that
\mathcal{P}
has no hole, then
K[\mathcal{P}]
is a normal Cohen-Macaulay domain. Nowadays, the study is devoted to non-simple polyominoes but a complete characterization of primality, Cohen-Macaulay and Gorenstein properties is still unknown, despite the efforts of many mathematicians (see
[
2
,
4
,
6
,
8
,
19
,
20
,
22
,
26
,
27
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
). Other very interesting combinatorial problems on polyomino ideals have been investigated as shown in
[
9
,
11
,
14
,
16
,
24
]
.

Cohen-Macaulayness and Goresteiness have so far been discussed only for prime collections of cells. In all the papers where Cohen-Macaulayness is studied (
[
7
,
22
,
25
,
27
,
31
]
), the demonstrative strategy is based on two well-known theorems of Sturmfels (
[
36
, Chapter 13]
) and Hochster (
[
3
, Theorem 6.3.5]
), which state that a toric ring whose defining ideal admits a squarefree initial ideal is a normal Cohen-Macaulay ring. For Goresteiness, a well-known result of Stanley (
[
35
]
) allows to characterize the prime polyominoes which are Gorenstein, by checking if the
h
-polynomial is palindromic. In such a context, the so-called rook polynomial plays a crucial role. The rook polynomial is a polynomial whose
k
-th coefficient is the number of ways of placing
k
non-attacking rooks in a polyomino. The maximum number of non-attacking rooks is called the rook number of
\mathcal{P}
. The connection of the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of
K[\mathcal{P}]
with the rook number was shown in
[
15
]
for the first time. So far, the study of the
h
-polynomial in terms of the rook polynomial has produced a relevant number of articles, including
[
8
,
12
,
23
,
25
,
30
,
32
]
, and it could help to characterize the prime polyominoes that are Gorenstein. Indeed, if the
h
-polyonomial of a prime polyomino
\mathcal{P}
is the rook polynomial, then we can check the palindromicity counting the number of suitable configurations of non-attacking rooks in
\mathcal{P}
. On the other hand, discussing non-prime polyominoes which are Gorenstein seems very hard and such classes have not appeared so far.

In this context a natural question arises: what can we say about the Cohen-Macaulay and the Gorenstein properties for non-prime collections of cells? Motivated by this question, we begin to investigate these properties for non-prime collections of cells. From
[
26
, Corollary 3.6]
we know that if a collection of cells contains the so-called zig-zag walks (see
[
26
, Definition 3.2]
), then the related binomial ideal is not prime. This suggests to consider as a first step a class of non-prime collections of cells made up of zig-zag walks, which we called
zig-zag collections
. As conjectured in
[
26
, Conjecture 4.6]
, every non-prime collection of cells should contain a zig-zag collection, so the latter could be useful for gaining information on non-prime collections of cells, by applying a decomposition of them into suitable zig-zag collections. This method is used to study the before mentioned properties for the
closed paths
, a kind of non-simple polyominoes introduced for the first time in
[
4
]
, in the non-prime case. In particular, for this class of polyominoes, we state the following result at the end of this paper:

Theorem
4.18

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a closed path polyomino. Then:

(1)

K[\mathcal{P}]
is Cohen-Macaulay with Krull dimension
|V(\mathcal{P})|-|\mathcal{P}|
; moreover, if
\mathcal{P}
does not contain any zig-zag walk, then
K[\mathcal{P}]
is also a normal domain.

(2)

The
h
-polyonomial of
K[\mathcal{P}]
is the rook polynomial of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathrm{reg}(K[\mathcal{P}])=r(\mathcal{P})
.

(3)

K[\mathcal{P}]
is Gorenstein if and only if
\mathcal{P}
consists of maximal blocks of rank three.

The article is structured as follows. Section 1 is devoted to provide some basic definitions and properties of collections of cells and the associated coordinate rings. In Section 2 we prove that the
h
-polynomial of a simple collection of cells is equal to the product of the
h
-polynomial of each connected component (Theorem
2.2
), exploiting the monomial order in
[
28
]
and
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
. As a direct consequence, we can generalize
[
26
, Theorem 3.13]
, obtaining in Corollary
2.5
that the
h
-polynomial of a simple thin collection of cells coincides with the rook-polynomial. In Section 3 we introduce a new class of non-prime collections of cells, named
zig-zag collections
. Taking advantage of some recent results shown in
[
10
]
, we show that their coordinate rings are Cohen-Macaulay (Theorem
3.7
), we compute the Hilbert-Poincaré series in terms of the switching rook polynomial (Corollary
3.8
) and we eventually discuss the Gorenstein property (Corollary
3.9
). The results achieved in the previous sections are collected in Section 4 to prove Theorem
4.18
, which is our main result.

Let

Lemma 1.1
.

Let

and
\mathcal{P}_{2}

-rooks in
\mathcal{P}_{1}

-rooks in
\mathcal{P}_{2}
, for all non-negative integers

. Therefore

\mathcal{P}

is a normal Cohen–Macaulay domain if and only if

is a normal Cohen–Macaulay domain for all

. Hence it is not restrictive to consider just weakly connected collections of cells. For this reason, whenever the term “collection of cells” is introduced in the paper, we mean a weakly connected collection of cells.

For utility, we state the following result about the Cohen-Macaulay property of the simple collection of cells, that in the literature we found stated in this form only in the case
\mathcal{P}
is polyomino (see
[

, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
20
, Corollary 3.3]
). For the sake of completeness, we also provide its proof.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a simple collection of cells. Then
K[\mathcal{P}]
is a normal Cohen-Macaulay domain of dimension
|V(\mathcal{P})|-|\mathcal{P}|
.

Proof.

By
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
we know that
I_{\mathcal{P}}=J_{\mathcal{P}}
where
J_{\mathcal{P}}
is the toric ring of a weakly chordal bipartite graph. In particular
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is prime and, by
[
28
]
,
I_{\mathcal{P}}
has a squarefree quadratic Gröbner basis. A toric ring whose toric ideal admits a squarefree initial ideal is normal, see
[
17
, Corollary 4.26]
and by a theorem of Hochster (see
[
3
, Theorem 6.3.5]
), a normal toric ring is Cohen–Macaulay. Finally, by
[
29
, Theorem 3.5]
and
[
29
, Corollary 3.6]
we know that
I_{\mathcal{P}}=I_{\Lambda}
for a saturated lattice
\Lambda
such that
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda)=|\mathcal{P}|
. Hence
\operatorname{height}(I_{\mathcal{P}})=|\mathcal{P}|
(see
[
17
, Problem 3.12]
) and so
\dim K[\mathcal{P}]=|V(\mathcal{P})|-|\mathcal{P}|
(see also
[
37
, Corollary 3.1.7]
).
∎

Let

K

be a homogeneous ideal of

. The formal power series

is called the

of

. It is known by Hilbert-Serre theorem that there exists a polynomial

with

such that
\mathrm{HP}_{R/I}(t)=\frac{h(t)}{(1-t)^{d}}
, where

is the Krull dimension of
R/I
. The polynomial

is called

of
R/I
. Moreover, if
R/I
is Cohen-Macaulay then
\mathrm{reg}(R/I)=\deg h(t)
. Recall also that if
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
then
\mathrm{HP}_{S}(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{n}}
. In some parts of the paper, if
K[\mathcal{P}]
is the coordinate ring of a collection of cells, we use the notation
h_{K[\mathcal{P}]}
for the
h
-polyonimial of the Hilbert-Poincaré series of
K[\mathcal{P}]
. Once we set the previous notation, let us prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a simple collection of cells consisting of
n
connected components
\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{n}
. Let
h_{K[\mathcal{P}_{i}]}(t)
be the
h
-polynomial of
K[\mathcal{P}_{i}]
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, then
\prod_{i=1}^{n}h_{K[\mathcal{P}_{i}]}(t)
is the
h
-polynomial of
K[\mathcal{P}]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a simple collection of cells. According to references
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
and
[
28
]
,
I_{\mathcal{P}}
possesses a quadratic Gröbner basis with respect to a certain monomial order. We fix such a monomial order, then for any pair of inner 2-minors
f
and
g
, their
S
-polynomial
S(f,g)
reduces to 0.
Suppose that
f
and
g
are inner 2-minors of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
and
\mathcal{P}_{j}
respectively, where
i\neq j
. Now we have two cases: when
f
and
g
are binomials associated with inner intervals that are disjoint or they share a vertex. For the first, we have
\gcd(\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(f),\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(g)=1
trivially. For the second case, since the inner 2-minors
f
and
g
are of the form as shown in Figure
4
and
S(f,g)
reduces to
0
, so by Lemma
2.1
we obtain
\gcd(\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(f),\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(g))=1
as well. So, for all
i,j\in[n]
with
i\neq j
and for all
f,g
inner 2-minors of
I_{\mathcal{P}}
such that
f
is related to an inner interval of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
and
g
is related to an inner interval of
\mathcal{P}_{j}
, we have
\gcd(\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(f),\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(g))=1
. As a consequence, if
v\in V(\mathcal{P}_{i})\cap V(\mathcal{P}_{j})
for some
i,j\in[n]
with
i\neq j
, and
x_{v}\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(f))
, for some inner 2-minor
f
related to an inner interval of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
, then
x_{v}\notin\mathrm{supp}(\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(g))
, for all inner 2-minors
g
related to the inner intervals of
\mathcal{P}_{j}
.
Let
A_{\mathcal{P}}=\{v\in V(\mathcal{P})\mid v\in V(\mathcal{P}_{i})\cap V(\mathcal{P}_{j}),\text{ for some }i,j\in[n],i\neq j\}
. Let
C_{\mathcal{P}}
be the set of vertices
v\in A_{\mathcal{P}}
such that
x_{v}\notin\mathrm{supp}(\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(f))
, for all inner 2-minor
f
related to an inner interval of
\mathcal{P}
. For each
i\in[n]
, define
D_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}
the set of vertices
v\in V(\mathcal{P}_{i})\cap A_{\mathcal{P}}
such that
x_{v}\notin\mathrm{supp}(\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(f)),\text{ for all inner 2-minor }f\text{ related to an inner interval of }\mathcal{P}_{i}
. Denote
d_{i}=|D_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}|
. Set, for all
i\in[n]
,
S^{\prime}_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}=K[x_{a}:a\in V(\mathcal{P}_{i})\setminus D_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}]
. Then we have:

S_{\mathcal{P}}/\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(I_{\mathcal{P}})=K[x_{v}\mid v\in C_{\mathcal{P}}]\otimes_{K}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}S^{\prime}_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}/\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(I_{\mathcal{P}_{i}})\right).

Observe that for all
i\in[n]
we have
S_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}/\mathrm{in}(I_{\mathcal{P}_{i}})=S^{\prime}_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}/\mathrm{in}(I_{\mathcal{P}_{i}})\otimes_{K}K[x_{v}\mid v\in D_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}]
. In particular, by
[
37
, Lemma 5.1.11]
,
\mathrm{HP}_{S^{\prime}_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}/\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(I_{\mathcal{P}_{i}})}(t)=(1-t)^{d_{i}}\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{P}_{i}]}(t)
, and since
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is a simple polyomino, by Proposition
1.3
we have
\mathrm{HP}_{S^{\prime}_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}/\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits(I_{\mathcal{P}_{i}})}(t)=\frac{h_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}(t)}{(1-t)^{|V(\mathcal{P}_{i})|-|\mathcal{P}_{i}|-d_{i}}}
. Note that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|V(\mathcal{P}_{i})|=|V(\mathcal{P})|+|A_{\mathcal{P}}|
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathcal{P}_{i}|=|\mathcal{P}|
. Therefore, by
[
37
, Lemma 5.1.11]
, we have:

\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}h_{K[\mathcal{P}_{i}]}}{(1-t)^{|V(\mathcal{P})|-|\mathcal{P}|+|A_{\mathcal{P}}|+|C_{\mathcal{P}}|-\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}}}

Observe that
|A_{\mathcal{P}}|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}-|C_{\mathcal{P}}|
. In fact, let
v\in A_{\mathcal{P}}
. Then there exist
j_{1},j_{2}\in[n]
such that
v\in V(\mathcal{P}_{j_{1}})\cap V(\mathcal{P}_{j_{2}})
. Furthermore, if
v\in C_{\mathcal{P}}
, then
v\in D_{\mathcal{P}_{j_{1}}}\cap D_{\mathcal{P}_{j_{2}}}
, and if
v\notin C_{\mathcal{P}}
, then either
v\in D_{\mathcal{P}_{j_{1}}}
or
v\in D_{\mathcal{P}_{j_{2}}}
. By this observation, we easily argue the previous equality. So,
\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}h_{K[\mathcal{P}_{i}]}}{(1-t)^{|V(\mathcal{P})|-|\mathcal{P}|}}
. By Proposition
1.3
we have
\dim K[\mathcal{P}]=|V(\mathcal{P})|-|\mathcal{P}|
, hence
\prod_{i=1}^{n}h_{K[\mathcal{P}_{i}]}(t)
is the
h
-polynomial of
K[\mathcal{P}]
.
∎

By the previous result, we argue that, if

is equal to the switching rook polynomial of
\mathcal{P}
.

It is a consequence of Lemma
1.1
and Theorem
2.2
.

is a Cohen-Macaulay ring. Now we prove the result about Gorensteinness. Let

that

consists of

3.
Zig-zag collections

In this section we discuss the algebraic properties of the coordinate ring of a new family of collections of cells called
zig-zag collections
. Roughly speaking, a zig-zag collection is a collection of cells with just one hole that comes from the intervals related to a zig zag-walk. We start by pointing out that definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{\ell}
be a sequence of distinct intervals in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. For all
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
denote by
\mathcal{P}_{i}
the collection of cells related to
I_{i}
and
\mathcal{P}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{i}
.
\mathcal{P}
is a
zig-zag collection
if satisfies:

(1)

I_{1}\cap I_{\ell}=\{v_{1}=v_{\ell+1}\}
and
I_{i}\cap I_{i+1}=\{v_{i+1}\}
, for all
i=1,\dots,\ell-1
;

(2)

v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
are on the same edge interval of
\mathcal{P}
, for all
i=1,\dots,\ell
.

(3)

I_{i}\cap I_{j}=\emptyset
for all
\{i,j\}\subseteq[\ell]
with
i<j
, such that
j\neq i+1
and
(i,j)\neq(1,\ell)
.

In such a case, we say that
\mathcal{P}
is supported by
I_{1},\dots,I_{\ell}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a zig-zag collection. From Definition

it follows that
\mathcal{P}
is a non-simple weakly connected collection of cells having just one hole and
\ell
is an even number.

Moreover,
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is not prime because
I_{1},\dots,I_{\ell}
is a zig-zag walk of
\mathcal{P}
. For more details we refer to
[
26
, Section 3]
, where the arguments given for polyominoes can be extended easily to collections of cells.

Our first step is to study the Gröbner basis of the inner 2-minor ideal of a zig-zag collection. For this aim, we introduce four total orders on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Let
(i,j),(k,l)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

•

(k,l)<^{(1)}(i,j)
, if
l<j
, or
l=j
and
i<k
;

•

(k,l)<^{(2)}(i,j)
, if
l<j
, or
l=j
and
k<i
;

•

(k,l)<^{(3)}(i,j)
, if
l>j
, or
l=j
and
k<i
;

•

(k,l)<^{(4)}(i,j)
, if
l>j

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be closed path polyomino. In the framework of Set-up
4.2
we have:

\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t)=\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{Q}_{r}]}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{t}{1-t}\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{i}]}(t)=\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{Q}_{r}]}(t)+\frac{t}{(1-t)^{|V(\mathcal{P})|-|\mathcal{P}|}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}r_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{i}}(t)\right)

Moreover
\mathrm{depth}(K[\mathcal{P}])\geq\min\{\mathrm{depth}(K[\mathcal{Q}_{r}]),|V(\mathcal{P})|-|\mathcal{P}|\}
.

Proof.

Let
\prec
be the monomial order in
S_{\mathcal{P}}
defined by
[
7
, Algorithm 4.1]
, that provides a quadratic Gröbner basis for
I_{\mathcal{P}}
. For all
i\in[r]
, we focus on the initial ideals of
I_{\mathcal{Q}_{i}}
and
I_{\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{\prime}}
as explained in Remark
4.3
. In particular, let
i\in[r]
and consider the following exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\frac{S_{\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}}}{(\mathrm{in}(I_{\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}}):x_{m_{i}})}\longrightarrow\frac{S_{\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}}}{\mathrm{in}(I_{\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}})}\longrightarrow\frac{S_{\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}}}{(\mathrm{in}(I_{\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}}),x_{m_{i}})}\longrightarrow 0

Arguing as in the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 4.3]
, we can prove that:

•

\frac{S_{\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}}}{(\mathrm{in}(I_{\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}}),x_{m_{i}})}\cong\frac{S_{\mathcal{Q}_{i}}}{\mathrm{in}(I_{\mathcal{Q}_{i}})}

•

\frac{S_{\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}}}{(\mathrm{in}(I_{\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}}):x_{m_{i}})}\cong\frac{S_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{i}}}{\mathrm{in}(I_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{i}})}\otimes K[x_{m_{i}}]
.

So by
[
37
, Corollary 3.3.15]
and
[
37
, Lemmas 5.1.2 and 5.1.3]
it follows that for all
i\in\{1\,\ldots,r\}
it is verified:

\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}]}(t)=\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{Q}_{i}]}(t)+\frac{t}{1-t}\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{i}]}(t)

and also, by Propositions
3.6
,
[
37
, Lemma 3.1.34]
and
[
3
, Proposition 1.2.9]
, we obtain
\mathrm{depth}(K[\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}])\geq\min\{\mathrm{depth}(K[\mathcal{Q}_{i}]),\mathrm{depth}(K[\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{\prime}])+1\}
. In particular, we obtain the first equality of
\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t)
.

Moreover, in the framework of Set-up
4.2
, we have
|V(\mathcal{Q}_{i-1})|-|\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}|=|V(\mathcal{Q}_{i})|-|\mathcal{Q}_{i}|
and
|V(\mathcal{Q}_{i-1})|-|\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}|=|V(\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{i})|-|\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{i}|+1

|V(\mathcal{P})|-|\mathcal{P}|=|V(\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{i})|-|\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{i}|+1

∎

, that is a contradiction. Hence,

for every maximal interval

, as claimed.

(2)

Now, suppose that

In the following result we gather, as a summary, the main algebraic properties of

when

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a closed path polyomino. Then:

(1)

K[\mathcal{P}]
is Cohen-Macaulay with Krull dimension
|V(\mathcal{P})|-|\mathcal{P}|
; moreover, if
\mathcal{P}
does not contain any zig-zag walk, then
K[\mathcal{P}]
is also a normal domain.

(2)

The
h
-polyonomial of
K[\mathcal{P}]
is the rook polynomial of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathrm{reg}(K[\mathcal{P}])=r(\mathcal{P})
.

(3)

K[\mathcal{P}]
is Gorenstein if and only if
\mathcal{P}
consists of maximal blocks of rank three.

Proof.

We need to discuss just two cases. In the first one assume that
\mathcal{P}
has a zig-zag walk. Then (1) and (2) follow from Theorems
4.13
and
4.15
. Concerning (3), we know that
K[\mathcal{P}]
is not Gorenstein from Proposition
4.17
. Moreover, by

\mathcal{P}
does not contain any
L

\mathcal{P}
intersect themselves in the cells displayed in Figure
10a
or
10b
(up to reflections or rotations). Hence, we argue that if
\mathcal{P}
has a zig-zag walk then it contains maximal blocks of length two. This means that (3) holds if
\mathcal{P}
has zig-zag walks. Now, in the second case suppose that
\mathcal{P}
does not contain a zig-zag walk. The claims (1) and (2) are already proved in

and (3) in

, Theorem 5.7]

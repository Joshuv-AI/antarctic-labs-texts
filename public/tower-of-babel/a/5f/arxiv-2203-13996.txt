Parametric Euler T T -sums of odd harmonic numbers
Ce Xu and Lu Yan[1mm] a. School of Mathematics and Statistics,Anhui Normal University, Wuhu 241002, Chinab. School of Mathematical Sciences,Tongji University, Shanghai 200092, China

Abstract
In this paper, we define a parametric variant of generalized Euler sums and call them the (alternating) parametric Euler T T -sums. By using the contour integration method and residue theorem, we establish several explicit formulae for the linear parametric Euler T T -sums. Furthermore, by applying the results, we obtain explicit formulae for the Hoffman’s (alternating) double t t -values and Kaneko-Tsumura’s (alternating) double T T -values.

Introduction

We begin with some basic notations. Let
\mathbb{C},\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{N}^{-}
be the sets of complex numbers, integers, positive integers and negative integers, respectively. We also denote by
\mathbb{N}_{0}

For

n

p

\displaystyle H_{n}^{(p)}:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{p}}.

(1.1)

Then
H_{n}^{(1)}\equiv H_{n}
is the
n
-th harmonic number. For any
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
H_{0}^{(p)}:=0
. If
p>1

H_{n}^{(p)}
converges to the Riemann zeta value
\zeta(p)
when
n
tends to infinity, that is

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{n}^{(p)}=\zeta(p).

Here when
\operatorname{Re}{(z)}>1
, the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(z)
is defined by

\zeta(z):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{z}}.

The linear Euler sum is defined by

\displaystyle S_{p,q}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{(p)}}{n^{q}},

(1.2)

where
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
with
q\geq 2
.

p+q

[

\displaystyle S_{\boldsymbol{p},q}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{(p_{1})}H_{n}^{(p_{2})}\cdots H_{n}^{(p_{r})}}{n^{q}},

(1.3)

where
\boldsymbol{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
with
p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r}
and

. In
[
10
]
, Flajolet and Salvy used the method of contour integration to evaluate the linear and generalized Euler sums. In
[
23

The generalized Euler sums are in contrast to the multiple zeta values (abbr. MZVs) defined as

\displaystyle T_{\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q}}^{\sigma}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_{n}^{(p_{1})}h_{n}^{(p_{2})}\cdots h_{n}^{(p_{r})}\sigma^{n}}{\left(n+a_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{q_{1}}\left(n+a_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{q_{2}}\cdots\left(n+a_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{q_{k}}},

2
Several Lemmas

A complex function
\xi(z)
is called a kernel function if

(i)

\xi(z)
is meromorphic in the whole complex plane,

(ii)

\xi(z)
satisfies
\xi(z)=o(z)
over an infinite collection of circles
|z|=\rho_{k}
with
\rho_{k}\to\infty
.

Applying these two conditions, Flajolet and Salvy
[
10
]
discovered the following residue lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\xi(z)
be a kernel function and let
r(z)
be a rational function which is
O(z^{-2})
at infinity. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in O}{\rm Res}(r(z)\xi(z),\alpha)+\sum_{\beta\in S}{\rm Res}(r(z)\xi(z),\beta)=0,

where
S
is the set of poles of
r(z)
and
O
is the set of poles of
\xi(z)
that are not poles of
r(z)
. Here
{\rm Res}(f(z),\lambda)
denotes the residue of
f(z)
at
z=\lambda
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
26
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

If a meromorphic function
f(z)
has a pole of order
m
at
z=\lambda
, then

{\rm Res}\left(f(z),\lambda\right)=\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\lambda}\frac{1}{(m-1)!}\frac{d^{m-1}}{ds^{m-1}}\left[(z-\lambda)^{m}f(z)\right]=\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\lambda}\frac{1}{m!}\frac{d^{m}}{ds^{m}}\left[(z-\lambda)^{m+1}f(z)\right].

The parametric digamma function, denoted by
\boldsymbol{\Psi}(-z;a)
in
[
24
]
, is defined by

\boldsymbol{\Psi}(-z;a)+\gamma=\frac{1}{z-a}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k+a}-\frac{1}{k+a-z}\right),

where
a\notin\mathbb{N}^{-}
and
\gamma
denotes the Euler-Mascheroni constant. The function
\boldsymbol{\Psi}(-z;a)
is meromorphic in the entire complex plane with a simple pole at
z=n+a
for each negative integer
n
. For simplicity, we let

\boldsymbol{\Psi}(-z)=\boldsymbol{\Psi}\left(-z;-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\gamma=\frac{1}{z+\frac{1}{2}}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k-\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{k-\frac{1}{2}-z}\right).

Obviously, the
(p-1)
-th derivative of the function
\boldsymbol{\Psi}(-z)
is

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{(p-1)}(-z)=(-1)^{p}(p-1)!\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(k-\frac{1}{2}-z\right)^{p}}.

(2.1)

To introduce the following lemmas more concisely, we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{t}(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k})

\displaystyle:=2^{s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{k}}t(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geq 1}{\frac{1}{\left(n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s_{1}}\left(n_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s_{2}}\cdots\left(n_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s_{k}}}},

(2.2)

and call them multiple
\widetilde{t}
-values. Similarly, the corresponding alternating multiple
\widetilde{t}
-values are defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{t}(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k};\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{k})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geq 1}\frac{\sigma_{1}^{n_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots\sigma_{k}^{n_{k}}}{(n_{1}-1/2)^{s_{1}}(n_{2}-1/2)^{s_{2}}\cdots(n_{k}-1/2)^{s_{k}}}

\displaystyle=2^{s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{k}}t(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k};\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{k}).

(2.3)

Similarly, we put a bar on the top of
s_{j}
if
\sigma_{j}=-1
. For example, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{t}(\overline{s})=\widetilde{t}(s;-1)\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{t}(\overline{s_{1}},s_{2})=\widetilde{t}(s_{1},s_{2};-1,1)

Lemma 2.3

For

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{(p-1)}\left(\frac{1}{2}-z\right)}{(p-1)!}\overset{z\rightarrow n}{=}\frac{1}{(z-n)^{p}}+(-1)^{p}\sum\limits_{i=p}^{\infty}\dbinom{i-1}{p-1}\left((-1)^{i}H_{n}^{(i)}+\zeta(i)\right)(z-n)^{i-p},\end{aligned}

(2.4)

3
Parametric Euler
T
-sums

In this section, we apply the method of contour integration to study the (alternating) parametric linear Euler
T
-sums

T_{p,1^{2}}(a,b)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_{n}^{(p)}}{\left(n+a-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n+b-\frac{1}{2}\right)}

and

T^{-1}_{p,1^{2}}(a,b)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{h_{n}^{(p)}}{\left(n+a-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n+b-\frac{1}{2}\right)}.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
p\in\mathbb{N},a,b\in\mathbb{C}
with
a\neq b,a,b\notin\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}
and
a+\frac{1}{2},b+\frac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_{n}^{(p)}}{\left(n+a-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n+b-\frac{1}{2}\right)}-(-1)^{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_{n-1}^{(p)}}{\left(n-a-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n-b-\frac{1}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{(-1)^{p}}{b-a}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\left[\frac{p}{2}\right]}\widetilde{t}(2k)\left\{\zeta(p-2k+1;a)-\zeta(p-2k+1;b)\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{(-1)^{p}}{b-a}\left\{\pi\tan(\pi b)\left(\zeta(p;b)-\widetilde{t}(p)\right)-\pi\tan(\pi a)\left(\zeta(p;a)-\widetilde{t}(p)\right)\right\},

(3.1)

where
[x]
is the integer part of
x
, and the Hurwitz zeta function
\zeta\left(z;a\right)
is defined for
\operatorname{Re}\left(z\right)>1
and

\zeta\left(z;a\right):=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{\left(n+a\right)^{z}}}.

We also set
\zeta(1;a):=-\boldsymbol{\Psi}(1/2+a)=\psi(1/2)-\psi(a)
.

Proof.
Let

F(z)=\frac{\pi\tan(\pi z)\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{(p-1)}\left(\frac{1}{2}-z\right)}{(z+a)(z+b)(p-1)!}.

This function has poles at
z=-a,-b,n(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0})

\pm\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)(n\in\mathbb{N})
.
Obviously,
\frac{\pi\tan(\pi z)\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{(p-1)}\left(\frac{1}{2}-z\right)}{(p-1)!}
is a kernel function and
\oint_{(\infty)}F(z)\mathrm{d}z=0
, where
\oint_{\left(\infty\right)}
denotes the integral along large circles, that is, the limit of integrals
\oint_{\left|z\right|=R}
as
R\to\infty
. Hence, using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm Res}(F(z),n)+{\rm Res}(F(z),-a)+{\rm Res}(F(z),-b)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\{{\rm Res}\left(F(z),n-\frac{1}{2}\right)+{\rm Res}\left(F(z),\frac{1}{2}-n\right)\right\}=0.

(3.2)

Applying (
2.4
) and the fact that
z=n
is a simple zero of
\tan(\pi z)
, we note that the pole of
F(z)
at
z=n
is of order
p-1
for each nonnegative integer
n
. From Eqs. (
2.4
), (
2.9
) and Lemma

, the residue is

\displaystyle{\rm Res}(F(z),n)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p-1)!}\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow n}\frac{d^{p-1}}{dz^{p-1}}\left\{(z-n)^{p}F(s)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p-1)!}\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow n}\frac{d^{p-1}}{dz^{p-1}}\frac{\pi\tan(\pi z)}{(z+a)(z+b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{(-1)^{p}}{b-a}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\left[\frac{p}{2}\right]}\widetilde{t}(2k)\left(\frac{1}{(n+a)^{p-2k+1}}-\frac{1}{(n+b)^{p-2k+1}}\right).

For a positive integer
n
, the poles of

at
z=\pm\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right),-a,-b
are simple and by using Eqs. (
2.5
), (
2.6

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\rm Res}(F(z),-a)=-\frac{\pi\tan(\pi a)\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{(p-1)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+a\right)}{(b-a)(p-1)!},\end{aligned}

Then noting (

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{b-a}\left(\tan(\pi b)-\tan(\pi a)\right)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(n+a+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n+b+\frac{1}{2}\right)}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(n-a-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n-b-\frac{1}{2}\right)},

Theorem 4.1
.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle t(2j,2m+1)=

\displaystyle t(2j)t(2m+1)-\frac{1}{2}t(2j+2m+1)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\dbinom{2j+2m-2k}{2j-1}\frac{\zeta(2j+2m-2k+1)}{2^{2j+2m-2k+1}}t(2k)

\displaystyle-\sum_{l=1}^{j}\dbinom{2j+2m-2l}{2m}\frac{\zeta(2j+2m-2l+1)}{2^{2j+2m-2l+1}}t(2l).

(4.1)

Proof.
By calculations, for
|a|<\frac{1}{2}
, we can easily deduce the following expansions

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_{n-1}^{(2m+1)}}{\left(n+a-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n-a-\frac{1}{2}\right)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{t}(2i,2m+1)a^{2i-2},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2m+1}\left(\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-a^{2}\right)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{t}(2i+2m+1)a^{2i-2},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\zeta(2m-2k+2;a)-\zeta(2m-2k+2;-a)\\
=&-2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dbinom{2i+2m-2k}{2i-1}\zeta(2i+2m+1-2k)a^{2i-1},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \zeta(2m+1;a)+\zeta(2m+1;-a)=2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\dbinom{2i+2m}{2i}\zeta(2i+2m+1)a^{2i}.\end{aligned}

Substituting these identities and (
2.8
) into (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{t}(2i,2m+1)a^{2i-2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{t}(2i+2m+1)a^{2i-2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\dbinom{2i+2m-2k}{2i-1}\zeta(2i+2m+1-2k)\widetilde{t}(2k)a^{2i-2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{t}(2m+1)\widetilde{t}(2i)a^{2i-2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{i}\dbinom{2i+2m-2l}{2m}\zeta(2i+2m+1-2l)\widetilde{t}(2l)a^{2i-2}.

By comparing the coefficients of
a^{2j-2}
in the above equation and using (
2
), we obtain (
4.1
). This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.2

For

\displaystyle t(2j+1,2m)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}t(2j+2m+1)

|a|<\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}h_{n-1}^{(2m)}}{\left(n+a-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n-a-\frac{1}{2}\right)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{t}(\overline{2i},2m)a^{2i-2},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2m}\left(\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-a^{2}\right)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{t}(\overline{2i+2m})a^{2i-2},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\overline{\zeta}(2m-2k;a)-\overline{\zeta}(2m-2k;-a)\\
=&2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dbinom{2i+2m-2k-2}{2i-1}\overline{\zeta}(2i+2m-2k-1)a^{2i-1},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \zeta(2m;a)-\zeta(2m;-a)=-2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dbinom{2i+2m-2}{2i-1}\zeta(2i+2m-1)a^{2i-1}.\end{aligned}

Substituting these identities and (
2.11
) into (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{t}(\overline{2i},2m)a^{2i-2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{t}(\overline{2i+2m})a^{2i-2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\dbinom{2i+2m-2k-2}{2i-1}\overline{\zeta}(2i+2m-2k-1)\widetilde{t}(\overline{2k+1})a^{2i-2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{i-1}\dbinom{2i+2m-2l-2}{2m-1}\zeta(2i+2m-2l-1)\widetilde{t}(\overline{2l+1})a^{2i-2}.

By comparing the coefficients of
a^{2j-2}
in the above equation and using (
2
), we derive the desired result. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.3
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

For
j,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the following identity holds:

\displaystyle t(\overline{2j+1},2m+1)=

\displaystyle t(\overline{2j+1})t(2m+1)-\frac{1}{2}t(\overline{2j+2m+2})

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=0}^{m}\dbinom{2j+2m-2k}{2j}\frac{\overline{\zeta}(2j+2m-2k+1)}{2^{2j+2m-2k+1}}t(\overline{2k+1})

\displaystyle-\sum_{l=0}^{j}\dbinom{2j+2m-2l}{2m}\frac{\zeta(2j+2m-2l+1)}{2^{2j+2m-2l+1}}t(\overline{2l+1}).

(4.7)

Proof.
By taking the first derivative of both sides of (

) with respect to
a

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}h_{n}^{(2m+1)}}{\left(n+a-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\left(n-a-\frac{1}{2}\right)}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}h_{n-1}^{(2m+1)}}{\left(n+a-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n-a-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{a}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\widetilde{t}(\overline{2k+1})(2m-2k+1)\overline{\zeta}(2m-2k+2;a)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2a^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\widetilde{t}(\overline{2k+1})\left(\overline{\zeta}(2m-2k+1;a)-\overline{\zeta}(2m-2k+1;-a)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4a^{2}}\frac{\pi}{\cos(\pi a)}\left(\zeta(2m+1;-a)-\zeta(2m+1;a)\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2a}\left\{\frac{\pi}{\cos(\pi a)}(2m+1)\zeta(2m+2;a)-(\zeta(2m+1;a)-\widetilde{t}(2m+1))\frac{d}{da}\left(\frac{\pi}{\cos(\pi a)}\right)\right\}.

(4.8)

By direct calculations, for
|a|<\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}h_{n-1}^{(2m+1)}}{\left(n+a-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\left(n-a-\frac{1}{2}\right)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\left[\frac{i}{2}\right]\widetilde{t}(\overline{i+1},2m+1)a^{i-2},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}h_{n-1}^{(2m+1)}}{\left(n+a-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n-a-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{i}{2}\right]\widetilde{t}(\overline{i+1},2m+1)a^{i-2},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2m+1}\left(n+a-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\left(n-a-\frac{1}{2}\right)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\left[\frac{i}{2}\right]\widetilde{t}(\overline{i+2m+2})a^{i-2},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\overline{\zeta}(2m-2k+1;a)-\overline{\zeta}(2m-2k+1;-a)\\
=&\frac{2}{a^{2m-2k+1}}+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dbinom{2i+2m-2k-1}{2m-2k}\overline{\zeta}(2i+2m-2k)a^{2i-1},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \zeta(2m+1;-a)-\zeta(2m+1;a)=-\frac{2}{a^{2m+1}}+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dbinom{2i+2m-1}{2m}\zeta(2i+2m)a^{2i-1},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \overline{\zeta}(2m-2k+2;a)=\frac{1}{a^{2m-2k+2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\dbinom{i+2m-2k}{i-1}\overline{\zeta}(i+2m-2k+1)a^{i-1}.\end{aligned}

Then by substituting the above formulae and Eqs. (

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(1+(-1)^{i})\left[\frac{i}{2}\right]\widetilde{t}(\overline{i+1},2m+1)a^{i-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i-1}\left[\frac{i}{2}\right]\widetilde{t}(\overline{i+2m+2})a^{i-2}-\sum_{k=0}^{m}(2m-2k+2)\widetilde{t}(\overline{2k+1})a^{2k-2m-3}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\dbinom{i+2m-2k}{i-1}(2m-2k+1)\overline{\zeta}(i+2m-2k+1)\widetilde{t}(\overline{2k+1})a^{i-2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\dbinom{2i+2m-2k-1}{2m-2k}\overline{\zeta}(2i+2m-2k)\widetilde{t}(\overline{2k+1})a^{2i-3}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(2i)\widetilde{t}(2m+1)\widetilde{t}(\overline{2i+1})a^{2i-2}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(2m-2i+2)\widetilde{t}(\overline{2i+1})a^{2i-2m-3}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{i-1}\dbinom{2i+2m-2l-1}{2m}\zeta(2i+2m-2l)\widetilde{t}(\overline{2l+1})a^{2i-3}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\left[\frac{i-1}{2}\right]}(-1)^{i}\dbinom{i+2m-2l}{2m+1}(2m+1)\zeta(i+2m-2l+1)\widetilde{t}(\overline{2l+1})a^{i-2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\left[\frac{i-1}{2}\right]}(-1)^{i}\dbinom{i+2m-2l-1}{2m}(2l)\zeta(i+2m-2l)\widetilde{t}(\overline{2l+1})a^{i-3}.

(4.9)

Thus, comparing the coefficients of

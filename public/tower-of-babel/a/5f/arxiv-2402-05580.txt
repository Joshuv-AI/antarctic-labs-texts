Improved long time existence for the Willmore flow of surfaces of revolution with Dirichlet data.
Sascha Eichmann

Abstract
To avoid possible singularities in the Willmore flow, one usually works under an energy threshold provided by the Li-Yau inequality. Here we improve this threshold by also considering parts outside of a possible singularity together with Dirichlet boundary data. We work in the class of surfaces of revolution.



Keywords.
Willmore flow, surfaces of revolution, Dirichlet boundary conditions

MSC.
35K35, 49Q10, 34B60, 58E15

Introduction

For a smooth two dimensional oriented immersion
f:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
with pull back measure
\mu_{f}
and mean curvature
H
(i.e. the sum of the principal curvatures) the Willmore energy is defined by

W_{e}(f)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma}H^{2}\,d\mu_{f}.

(1.1)

Thomsen gave a first analysis in
[
37
]
of this energy. Further mathematical insight was not possible, because the necessary tools were not developed yet.
Willmore later revived the discussion in
[
38
]
and formulated his famous conjecture for the optimal torus.
Work on this conjecture essentially began in
[
36
]
and the result was finally proven in
[
27
]
.
This mainly concernes closed Willmore surfaces, i.e. critical points of
W_{e}
.
Here we are examining the corresponding
L^{2}
-flow, which is given by

\partial_{t}f=-(\Delta_{g_{f}}H+2H(H^{2}-K))N.

(1.2)

N
is the unit normal, such that the vector valued mean curvature
\vec{H}
satisfies
H=\vec{H}\cdot N
.
Furthermore
K
is the Gauss curvature and
\Delta_{g_{f}}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator of
f
.
In the series of papers
[
19
]
[
22
]
[
21
]
[
23
]
Kuwert&Schätzle worked out a lot of the analytical properties of this flow for closed immersions, which are also summarized in
[
20
]
.
Furthermore properties of the limit of the flow for approaching the maximal time of existence were found in
[
5
]
for general closed immersions.
Contrasting this development we are interested in the
L^{2}
-flow with Dirichlet boundary data here (results for other boundary data can be found in e.g.
[
28
]
for the Navier case).
To be precise let
f_{0}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
be given.
Let
\nu_{f_{0}}:\partial\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the inner conormal of
f_{0}
.
Then we examine the following initial/boundary value problem for a family of immersions
f:[0,T)\times\Sigma
with
T\in[0,\infty]
.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}f=-(\Delta_{g_{f}}H+2H(H^{2}-K))N&\mbox{ in }[0,\infty)\times\Sigma\\
f(0,p)=f_{0}(p)&\mbox{ for all }p\in\Sigma\\
f(t,p)=f_{0}(p)&\mbox{ for }t\geq 0\mbox{ and }p\in\partial\Sigma\\
\nu_{f(t,p)}=\nu_{f_{0}(p)}&\mbox{ for }t\geq 0\mbox{ and }p\in\partial\Sigma.\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

To extract more analytical properties,
we additionally assume, that the initial data is given by a surface of revolution.
They are defined by a smooth curve (also called profile curve)

\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)=:\mathbb{H}
, which induces such a surface of revolution
S(\gamma)
by the parametrisation
f_{\gamma}
:

[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\ni(s,\varphi)\mapsto f_{\gamma}(s,\varphi)=(\gamma^{1}(s),\gamma^{2}(s)\cos\varphi,\gamma^{2}(s)\sin\varphi).

(1.4)

Existence for minimisers under Dirichlet boundary data without rotational symmetry was discussed in
[
32
]
.
To obtain more analytic properties and finer results one can additionally impose the aforementioned rotational symmetry.
This was done in e.g.

[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
(see also the references therein).
For the flow
of rotational symmetric tori long time existence results have been achieved in e.g.
[
10
]
.
In this the central observation is, that if the hyperbolic length of the profil curve (see (
2.8
)) stays bounded during the evolution, the flow exists for all times and converges to a Willmore surface.
This is closely related to the flow of elastic curves (cf. (
2.7
)). We mention here some results in hyperbolic space

[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
33
]

and in euclidean space
[
31
]
,
[
9
]
(please see also the references therein).

Schlierf showed in
[
35
]
that this idea of bounded hyperbolic length transfers to Dirichlet boundary data as well.
He also gave a sufficient condition for this
hyperbolic length to stay bounded.
To be precise this is an energy bound for the initial datum on the Willmore energy.
For the readers convenience we cite the precise result in Theorem
A.2
.
Let us make the notion of boundary values for rotational surfaces more precise, i.e. let

x_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha_{\pm}>0
and
\beta_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Now we fixate the Dirichlet boundary data for a profile curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
and set (cf.
[
16
, Eq. (1.6)]
)

\begin{array}[]{cc}(x_{-},\alpha_{-}):=\gamma(0),&\ (x_{+},\alpha_{+})=\gamma(1),\\
(\cos\beta_{-},\sin\beta_{-})=\frac{d_{s}\gamma(0)}{|d_{s}\gamma(0)|},&\ -(\cos\beta_{+},\sin\beta_{+})=\frac{d_{s}\gamma(1)}{|d_{s}\gamma(1)|}\end{array}

(1.5)

The energy bound by Schlierf in
[
35
]
can also be interpreted as follows:

There are uniquely defined circles or vertical lines
\gamma_{circ}(x_{\pm},\alpha_{\pm},\beta_{\pm})
(see (
2.9
) and (
2.10
)), such that the surface of revolution w.r.t. to the profile curve

\gamma_{closed}=\gamma_{circ}(x_{-},\alpha_{-},\beta_{-})\oplus\gamma\oplus\gamma_{circ}(x_{+},\alpha_{+},\beta_{+}),

(1.6)

i.e.
S(\gamma_{closed})
, is a close surface, see Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
Boundary data and added sphere caps.

Furthermore we call the resulting sphere caps

\operatorname{cap}_{\pm}:=S(\gamma_{circ}(x_{\pm},\alpha_{\pm},\beta_{\pm})).

Now we define the closed Willmore energy (cf.
[
16
, Eq. (2.12)]
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}W_{closed}^{e}(S(\gamma)):=&W_{e}(S(\gamma))+W_{e}(\operatorname{cap}_{-})+W_{e}(\operatorname{cap}_{+})+4\pi\theta^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{2}\lfloor S(\gamma),\infty)\\
&+4\pi\theta^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{2}\lfloor\operatorname{cap}_{-},\infty)+4\pi\theta^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{2}\lfloor\operatorname{cap}_{+},\infty).\end{split}

(1.7)

Here
\theta^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{2}\lfloor\operatorname{cap}_{\pm},\infty)
is the
2
-density of
\operatorname{cap}_{\pm}
at infinity. The densities at
\infty
are added, so that this energy becomes invariant for all inversions at points in
\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
, see the argument in
[
16
, p. 6]
, i.e.
[
4
]
.
Now Schlierfs energy bound (see Theorem
A.2
) is equivalent to the condition

W^{e}_{closed}(S(\gamma_{0}))\leq 8\pi

(1.8)

for the initial curve
\gamma_{0}
(combine (
2.7
) with Lemma
4.3
). This is the same bound given by the Li-Yau inequality (see
[
26
]
) for having selfintersections.
In other words Schlierfs bound mitigates a self intersection but does not incorporate the parts outside of this singularity.
Here we are going to exploit exactly these parts and improve the energy condition by Schlierf.
These conditions cannot be improved arbitrarily. Counterexamples were provided by Blatt in
[
1
]
for closed surfaces and Schlierf in
[
35
]
under Dirichlet boundary data.

Let us formulate our new energy condition. For this let
x\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be arbitrary but fixated. Let
c_{\pm}^{x}:[0,1]\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{H}}
be smooth and satisfy the boundary conditions at their respective starting points, i.e.

\begin{array}[]{cc}(x_{-},\alpha_{-}):=c_{-}^{x}(0),&\ (x_{+},\alpha_{+})=c_{+}^{x}(0),\\
(\cos\beta_{-},\sin\beta_{-})=\frac{{d_{s}c_{-}^{x}}(0)}{|{d_{s}c_{-}^{x}}(0)|},&\ (\cos\beta_{+},\sin\beta_{+})=\frac{{d_{s}c_{+}^{x}}(0)}{|{d_{s}c_{+}^{x}}(0)|}\end{array}

(1.9)

such that

c_{\pm}(1)=(x,0).

(1.10)

Furthermore let
c_{\pm}^{x}
be part of Moebius transformed catenoids or half circles with centre on the
x
-axis.
This is well defined for all
x\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
by the Lemmata
3.1
and
3.2
.
Then we define the union
c^{x}:[0,2]\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{H}}
of these two curves as (see Figure
2
)

Figure 2:
Sketch of
c^{x}
consisting of one Moebius transformed catenoid and one sphere.

c^{x}(s):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{-}^{x}(s),&\mbox{ for }s\in[0,1]\\
c_{+}^{x}(2-s),&\mbox{ for }s\in[1,2]\end{array}\right.

(1.11)

Similar pictures to
c^{x}
also appear when the flow for the elastic energy (see (
2.6
)) blows up, see
[
33
]
.

The main result of this paper is now as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\gamma_{0}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
be a regular smooth curve satisfying the boundary data (
1.5
), such that

W^{e}_{closed}(S({\gamma_{0}}))\leq\inf\{W^{e}_{closed}(S({c^{x}}))|\ x\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}\}.

Then there is a global, rotational symmetric solution
f:[0,\infty)\times\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
to (
1.4
), with initial data
\gamma_{0}
and satisfying the boundary data (
1.5
).
Moreover
f(t,\cdot)
converges up to reparametrization smoothly to a Willmore immersion
f_{\infty}
for
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Independent and at the same time as our result Schlierf also published an improvement of the energy condition in
[
34
]
. He used a Li-Yau inequality tailored to boundary data.
Therefore this author conjectures that Schlierfs method can be used in a more general context, e.g. without rotational symmetry.
Surprisingly the result in
[
34
, Thm 1.3]
and ours are exactly the same.
Schlierf discusses this in detail in
[
34
, § D]
.

The idea of the proof of Thm.
1.1
is as follows: We will apply Schlierfs results from
[
35
]
, which are summarized in Theorem
A.3
.
We proceed by contradiction and assume the hyperbolic length (
2.8
) of the profile curves diverges to
\infty
.
Then by some geometric measure theory developed in
[
6
]
we obtain a limit with a singularity on the
x
-axis and still obeying the boundary data.
Theorem
4.7
essentially yields, that energy minimizers with boundary data and one singularity have to be Moebius transformed catenoids or circles. This is a contradiction to our energy assumption.
The proof is given in detail in section
5
.

Let us compare the energy condition by Schlierf (
1.8
) to our condition given in Theorem
1.1
.
Since any curve
c^{x}
has exactly one singularity in the inner part, Lemma
A.1
yields

W^{e}_{closed}(S({c^{x}}))\geq 8\pi.

Therefore our energy condition is always an improvement over
[
35
, Theorem 1.1]
, i.e. Theorem
A.2
, if
c^{x}
does not consists of two Moebius transformed circles.
In the circles case, the conditions are the same.
Furthermore the arguments in the proof of
[
16
, Prop. 3.9]
together with our result guarantee the existence of admissible initial curves for almost all boundary data.
For the remaining cases
[
16
, Prop. 4.1]
shows non-existence of energy minimising solutions, hence finding admissible initial curves is harder there (see also the discussion in
[
35
, § 5]
).

On the other hand, if the added sphere caps
\operatorname{cap}_{\pm}
have a nontrivial intersection, the Li-Yau inequality (see
[
26
]
) yields

W^{e}_{closed}(S({\gamma_{0}}))\geq 8\pi,

which greatly impedes the applicability of
[
35
, Theorem 1.1]
.

In the case of horizontal clamping, i.e.
\beta_{+}-\pi=\beta_{-}=0
, we calculate
W^{e}_{closed}(S({c^{x}}))
in section

.
The following formula follows from combining (

) with (

).

.

Let
\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-}>0
be given. If
\beta_{-}=0
,

and

Furthermore we have the following asymptotic for fixated

The asymptotic behaviour directly follows from (

Geometric background

We collect some necessary geometric formulae for our calculations. These are from
[
3
]
(cf. also
[
14
, § 2.1]
):

Let
\gamma\in W^{2,2}([0,1],\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty))
. We define the corresponding surface of revolution
S(\gamma)
by (
1.4
). Then the metric tensor of this surface is given by

(g_{ij})_{i,j=t,\varphi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(d_{s}\gamma^{1})^{2}+(d_{s}\gamma^{2})^{2}&0\\
0&(\gamma^{2})^{2}\end{array}\right).

(2.1)

We introduce the Radon measure
\mu_{\gamma}
on
\mathbb{R}
(cf.
[
6
, Eq. 2.2]
)

\mu_{\gamma}:=2\pi\gamma^{2}|d_{s}\gamma|\mathcal{L}^{1}\lfloor[0,1],

(2.2)

which represents the integration on the surface
S(\gamma)
in coordinates.
We call the principal curvatures of
S(\gamma)
by
k_{1}^{\gamma}
,
k_{2}^{\gamma}
. Usually one would have to provide an orientation to calculate these object, but since all of our formula are independent of this choice, we can neglect this. For example the square of the scalar mean curvature is given as

H^{2}=(k_{1}^{\gamma}+k_{2}^{\gamma})^{2}.

(2.3)

Next we introduce the hyperbolic half plane
\mathbb{H}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|\ y>0\}
equipped with the metric

ds^{2}=\frac{dx^{2}+dy^{2}}{y^{2}}.

(2.4)

With respect to this metric (which we call
g_{h}
) we can define the geodesic curvature
\kappa_{h}[\gamma]
(the
h
stands for hyperbolic) of
\gamma
by

\kappa_{h}[\gamma]=\frac{g_{h}(\nabla_{d_{s}\gamma}d_{s}\gamma,N_{\gamma})}{g_{h}(d_{s}\gamma,d_{s}\gamma)}.

(2.5)

Here
N_{\gamma}
is a choosen unit normal of
\gamma
w.r.t. to
g_{h}
and
\nabla_{\cdot}\cdot
is the covariant derivative w.r.t.
g_{h}
. Then the (hyperbolic) elastic energy (see e.g.
[
25
]
for a historical overview of elastic curves) is defined by

W_{h}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}(\kappa_{h}[\gamma](t))^{2}\,ds(t).

(2.6)

The Willmore energy and the elastic energy are connected by the following formula, which was first discovered in
[
2
]
.

\frac{2}{\pi}W_{e}(S(\gamma))=W_{h}(\gamma)-4\left[\frac{d_{s}\gamma^{2}}{\sqrt{(d_{s}\gamma^{1})^{2}+(d_{s}\gamma^{2})^{2}}}\right]_{0}^{1}.

(2.7)

If a curve
\gamma
is critical w.r.t. to
W_{h}
, then
S(\gamma)
also critical w.r.t. to
W_{e}
,
see e.g.
[
17
, Lemma 8.2]
.

Furthermore we call

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{H}}(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{1}|d_{s}\gamma(\ell)|\,ds(\ell)

(2.8)

the hyperbolic length of the curve
\gamma
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha_{0}>0
and
\beta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. We define sphere caps
\operatorname{cap}(x_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
(see Figure
1
) by rotating part of a circle (cf.
[
16
, Eq. 2.4]
)
given by

\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma_{circ}(x_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}):=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{H}|\ |x-x_{0}-\alpha_{0}\tan\beta_{0}|^{2}+y^{2}=\frac{\alpha_{0}^{2}}{\cos^{2}\beta_{0}},\\
\cos\beta_{0}(x-x_{0})\leq 0\}\end{array}

(2.9)

in case
\cos\beta_{0}\neq 0
. For
\cos\beta_{0}=0
we put

\gamma_{circ}(x_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}):=\{(x_{0},y)|\ y>0,\ (\alpha_{0}-y)\sin\beta_{0}>0\},

(2.10)

i.e.
\gamma_{closed}
is a vertical line.

3
Asymptotic geodesic elastica

Here we examine Moebius transformed catenoids
\gamma
. The classification in
[
24
, table 2.7 c)]
yields these to have hyperbolic curvature of the form

\kappa_{h}[\gamma](s)=\pm\frac{2}{\cosh(s+s_{0})}

(3.1)

for some
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Such curves are also called asymptotic geodesic (see e.g.
[
24
]
). Furthermore they have finite elastic energy
W_{h}
, they approach the
x
-axis or
\infty
and they are critical w.r.t.
W_{h}
. Hence they are prime candidates for rotational symmetric Willmore surfaces with a singularity.

Given an
s_{0}
and an
\alpha>0
we will solve the following Frenet equation in
\mathbb{H}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla_{d_{s}\gamma}d_{s}\gamma(s)=\pm\frac{2}{\cosh(s+s_{0})}N_{\gamma}\\
\gamma(0)=(0,\alpha),\ d_{s}\gamma(0)=(\alpha,0)\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

By Lemma
3.1
this will exhaust all possibilities for Moebius transformed catenoids.
Further the solution
\gamma
is parametrised by hyperbolic arclength.

The idea now is to use Moebius transformations of the upper half plane to find an explicit solution. Since we have two explicit solutions for
s_{0}=0
and
\alpha=1
, we can find any solution. These special curves are given by

s\mapsto(s,\cosh(s)),\ s\mapsto\frac{1}{s^{2}+\cosh^{2}(s)}(s,\cosh(s)).

(3.3)

This can be seen by direct computation as in e.g.
[
13
, § 7.4]
.

Any orientation preserving Moebius transformation of the upper half plane is given in complex coordinates
z\in\mathbb{C}
by

\varphi(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}

(3.4)

with

a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R},\ ad-bc>0.

(3.5)

It is also an isometry of the hyperbolic half plane (see e.g.
[
30
, Thm 4.6.2 and Thm. 4.6.7]
).
Now let
p\in\mathbb{H}
and
V\in T_{p}\mathbb{H}
with
|V|_{g}=1
. We now seek a Moebius Transformation
\varphi
with

\varphi(p)=(0,\alpha),\ \varphi^{\prime}(p)(V)=(\alpha,0).

(3.6)

If we have achived this, we will have solved (
3.2
) by using (
3.3
) to define
p
as the point of the curve in
s_{0}
and
V
as the derivative of that curve in
s_{0}
. These type of computations have been done in e.g.
[
29
, Lemma 2.9]
, but we need an explicit version here. Hence we repeat them for the readers convenience. (see e.g.
[
13
, Thm. 6.7]
for a similar result by using inversions on spheres).

We write
p
and
V
in complex numbers as

p=p_{1}+ip_{2},\ V=V_{1}+iV_{2}.

The above requirements now mean

p_{2}>0\mbox{ and }V_{1}^{2}+V_{2}^{2}=p_{2}^{2}.

(3.7)

To ease the calculations, we assume by composing with

z\mapsto z-p_{1},

(3.8)

that
p_{1}=0
.
If
c\neq 0
, we can assume without loss of generality that
c=1
. Since
c=0
corresponds to a linear transformation and since
a\in\mathbb{R}
we would not loose any significant solutions apart from the ones already given in (
3.3
).
We now calculate

\varphi^{\prime}(z)=\frac{a}{z+d}-\frac{az+b}{(z+d)^{2}}=\frac{az+ad-az-b}{(z+d)^{2}}=\frac{ad-b}{(z+d)^{2}}

(3.9)

Now we have to satisfy the following condition

\alpha=\varphi^{\prime}(ip_{2})(V_{1}+iV_{2}).

(3.10)

Seperating the right hand side in real and imaginary part yields

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(ip_{2})(V_{1}+iV_{2})=(ad-b)\frac{V_{1}+iV_{2}}{(ip_{2}+d)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ad-b)\frac{V_{1}+iV_{2}}{d^{2}+2ip_{2}d-p_{2}^{2}}=(ad-b)(V_{1}+iV_{2})\frac{d^{2}-p_{2}^{2}-2ip_{2}d}{(d^{2}-p_{2}^{2})^{2}+4p_{2}^{2}d^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ad-b}{(d^{2}-p_{2}^{2})^{2}+4p_{2}^{2}d^{2}}(V_{1}d^{2}-V_{1}p_{2}^{2}+2V_{2}p_{2}d+i(-2V_{1}p_{2}d-V_{2}p_{2}^{2}+V_{2}d^{2}))

Since the prefactor is real and the imaginary part should vanish, we obtain by a solution formula for zeros of second order polynomials

\displaystyle 0=-2V_{1}p_{2}d-V_{2}p_{2}^{2}+V_{2}d^{2}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle d^{2}-2\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}p_{2}d-p_{2}^{2}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle d_{1/2}=\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}p_{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{V_{1}^{2}}{V_{2}^{2}}p_{2}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}=\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}p_{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{V_{1}^{2}p_{2}^{2}+V_{2}^{2}p_{2}^{2}}{V_{2}^{2}}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:3_6}}{=}\frac{V_{1}p_{2}}{V_{2}}\pm\sqrt{\frac{p_{2}^{4}}{V_{2}^{2}}}=\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}p_{2}\pm\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{|V_{2}|}.

W.l.o.g. we can assume
V_{2}\neq 0
. Otherwise by scaling (
3.3
) would already yield our desired solution.

Now we have to decide a sign for this
\pm
: The real part of (
3.10
) is supposed to be positive. Since the prefactor in the above calculation is supposed to be positive as well, we can employ the equation for
d
and obtain

\displaystyle 0<

\displaystyle V_{1}d^{2}-V_{1}p_{2}^{2}+2V_{2}p_{2}d=V_{1}\left(d^{2}-p_{2}^{2}+2\frac{V_{2}}{V_{1}}p_{2}d\right)=V_{1}\left(2\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}p_{2}d+2\frac{V_{2}}{V_{1}}p_{2}d\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p_{2}V_{1}d\left(\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}+\frac{V_{2}}{V_{1}}\right)=2p_{2}^{2}V_{1}\left(\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}\pm\frac{p_{2}}{|V_{2}|}\right)\left(\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}+\frac{V_{2}}{V_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p_{2}^{2}V_{1}\frac{V_{1}^{2}+V_{2}^{2}}{V_{2}V_{1}}\frac{V_{1}|V_{2}|\pm p_{2}V_{2}}{V_{2}|V_{2}|}=2p_{2}^{2}(V_{1}^{2}+V_{2}^{2})\frac{V_{1}|V_{2}|\pm p_{2}V_{2}}{V_{2}^{2}|V_{2}|}

By (
3.7
) we have
|V_{1}V_{2}|\leq p_{2}|V_{2}|
. Hence we have to choose the sign as follows:

d=\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}p_{2}+\operatorname{sgn}(V_{2})\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{|V_{2}|}=\frac{V_{1}p_{2}+p_{2}^{2}}{V_{2}}

(3.11)

The condition on
p
yields

\displaystyle i\alpha=

\displaystyle\varphi(ip_{2})=\frac{aip_{2}+b}{ip_{2}+d}=\frac{(aip_{2}+b)(d-ip_{2})}{d^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}\left(aip_{2}d+ap_{2}^{2}+bd-ibp_{2}\right).

This yields the following two equations:

\displaystyle\alpha(d^{2}+p_{2}^{2})=

\displaystyle ap_{2}d-bp_{2}

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle ap_{2}^{2}+bd.

The second equation yields
a=-\frac{bd}{p_{2}^{2}}
(please note
p_{2}>0
). By inserting this into the other equation, we obtain

\alpha(d^{2}+p_{2}^{2})=-\frac{bd^{2}}{p_{2}}-bp_{2},

which in turn gives us

b=-\left(\frac{d^{2}}{p_{2}}+p_{2}\right)^{-1}\alpha(d^{2}+p_{2}^{2})=-\frac{p_{2}\alpha}{d^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}(d^{2}+p_{2}^{2})=-p_{2}\alpha.

Since

\displaystyle ad-b=-b\left(\frac{d^{2}}{p_{2}^{2}}+1\right)>0,

these
a,b,d
satisfy (
3.5
) and therefore we have found an eligible Moebius transformation.

Collecting all of our results and considering (
3.8
), we have

d=\frac{V_{1}p_{2}+p_{2}^{2}}{V_{2}}-p_{1},\ b=-\alpha p_{2}-\frac{V_{1}+p_{2}}{V_{2}}p_{1}\alpha,\ a=\frac{V_{1}+p_{2}}{V_{2}}\alpha.

(3.12)

Applying these results twice also yields the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1

(cf.
[
13
]
Thm. 6.7, cf.
[
29
]
Lemma 2.9)
.

Let
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{H}
and
W_{1}\in T_{q_{1}}\mathbb{H},W_{2}\in T_{q_{2}}\mathbb{H}
with
|W_{1/2}|_{g}=1
. Then there exists an orientation preserving Moebius transformation
\Phi:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
, i.e. an isometry of
\mathbb{H}
, such that

\Phi(q_{1})=q_{2},\ d\Phi(q_{1}).W_{1}=W_{2}.

Now we set

p=(s_{0},\cosh s_{0}),\ V=(1,\sinh(s_{0}))

(3.13)

and calculate
\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\varphi\circ(s,\cosh(s))
, which will be the singularity on the
x
-axis we are looking for:

\displaystyle\varphi(s,\cosh s)=\frac{a(s+i\cosh s)+b}{s+i\cosh s+d}\rightarrow a\mbox{ for }s\rightarrow\infty.

This defines a function for the singularity
x(s_{0})
:

x(s_{0}):=\frac{1+\cosh(s_{0})}{\sinh(s_{0})}\alpha.

(3.14)

(Please note, that
s_{0}=0
, i.e.
V_{2}=0
, will be taken into account later).
Calculating the derivative yields

\displaystyle d_{s_{0}}x(s_{0})=

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\frac{\sinh(s_{0})}{\sinh(s_{0})}-(1+\cosh(s_{0}))\frac{\cosh(s_{0})}{\sinh^{2}(s_{0})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\frac{\sinh^{2}(s_{0})-\cosh(s_{0})-\cosh^{2}(s_{0})}{\sinh^{2}(s_{0})}=-\alpha\frac{1+\cosh(s_{0})}{\sinh^{2}(s_{0})}<0.

Hence it is strictly monotone and therefore invertible. Furthermore

\lim_{s_{0}\rightarrow\pm 0}x(s_{0})=\pm\infty,\ \lim_{s_{0}\rightarrow\pm\infty}x(s_{0})=\pm\alpha.

(3.15)

Hence we can reach any singularity in
(-\infty,-\alpha)\cup(\alpha,\infty)
.
Now applying an inversion at the sphere with center

and radius

to

yields another elastica (see e.g. Figure

Inverting

on sphere with center zero and radius

.

The only change will be a change of sign in the geodesic curvature (cf. (

)). Hence these elastica enable us to reach the interval
(-\alpha,\alpha)
. The points
\pm\alpha
can be reached by half circles, which are geodesics and therefore elastica as well. Together with the uniqueness of solutions to the initial value problem for the Frenet equations and
[
24
, table 2.7 c)]
this yields

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
\alpha>0
and every
x\in\mathbb{R}-\{\pm\alpha\}
exists exactly one elastic curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
with finite elastic energy, such that

\gamma(0)=(0,\alpha),\ d_{s}\gamma(0)=(\alpha,0),\ \lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\gamma(s)=(x,0).

(3.16)

This elastica is of asymptotic geodesic type, i.e. its curvature satisfies (
3.1
).

Furthermore if
x=\pm\alpha
, there exists exactly one half circle connecting
(0,\alpha)
with
(\pm\alpha,0)
, which is also an elastica.

Now we like to calculate the actual elastic energy of such asymptotic geodesics. Therefore we have to explictly compute
x^{-1}
(Existence is guaranteed by
d_{s_{0}}x<0
, see above). Afterwards we will also obtain a formula for the inverted object, see Figure
6
.
To this end (
3.14
) yields

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\alpha(1+\cosh(s_{0}))-x(s_{0})\sinh(s_{0})=\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(e^{s_{0}}+e^{-s_{0}}\right)-x(s_{0})\frac{e^{s_{0}}-e^{-s_{0}}}{2}.

Multiplying by
e^{s_{0}}
yields

\alpha e^{s_{0}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(e^{s_{0}}\right)^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{x(s_{0})}{2}\left(e^{s_{0}}\right)^{2}+\frac{x(s_{0})}{2}=0.

By rearranging we obtain

\frac{\alpha-x(s_{0})}{2}\left(e^{s_{0}}\right)^{2}+\alpha e^{s_{0}}+\frac{\alpha+x(s_{0})}{2}=0.

By (
3.15
) we can assume
\alpha\neq x(s_{0})
(This would be the case for the half sphere anyway) and then

\left(e^{s_{0}}\right)^{2}+\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-x(s_{0})}e^{s_{0}}+\frac{\alpha+x(s_{0})}{\alpha-x(s_{0})}=0.

Completing the square then yields

\displaystyle\left(e^{s_{0}}\right)_{\pm}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-x(s_{0})}\pm\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(\alpha-x(s_{0}))^{2}}-\frac{\alpha+x(s_{0})}{\alpha-x(s_{0})}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-x(s_{0})}\pm\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}-\alpha^{2}+x(s_{0})^{2}}{(\alpha-x(s_{0}))^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-x(s_{0})}\pm\frac{|x(s_{0})|}{|\alpha-x(s_{0})|}.

Since
|x(s_{0})|>\alpha
and we also have to perform
\log
on both sides of the equation, the positive solution is the only viable one. Hence

e^{s_{0}}=-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-x(s_{0})}+\frac{|x(s_{0})|}{|\alpha-x(s_{0})|}.

(3.17)

If
x(s_{0})>0
, then

e^{s_{0}}=-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-x(s_{0})}+\frac{x(s_{0})}{x(s_{0})-\alpha}=-\frac{\alpha+x(s_{0})}{\alpha-x(s_{0})}.

On the other hand if
x(s_{0})<0

e^{s_{0}}=-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-x(s_{0})}+\frac{-x(s_{0})}{-x(s_{0})+\alpha}=-\frac{\alpha+x(s_{0})}{\alpha-x(s_{0})}.

Hence all in all we obtain

s_{0}(x)=\log\left(-\frac{\alpha+x}{\alpha-x}\right).

(3.18)

Let us now turn to the inversion at a sphere with radius
\alpha
and centre
0
. It is given by (see e.g.
[
30
, Eq. 4.1.2]
)

z\mapsto\frac{\alpha^{2}}{|z|^{2}}z.

For any point on the
x
-axis (

) with
|x|>\alpha
this yields

x_{inv}:=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{x}.

Hence the formula for the inverted asymptotic geodesic is

s_{0}^{inv}:=\log\left(-\frac{\alpha+x_{inv}}{\alpha-x_{inv}}\right)=\log\left(-\frac{\alpha+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{x}}{\alpha-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{x}}\right)=\log\left(-\frac{x+\alpha}{x-\alpha}\right).

(3.19)

In both cases, the square of the geodesic curvature function is of the form

\kappa(s)^{2}=\frac{4}{\cosh(s)^{2}},

hence the energies are

E_{+}(\alpha):=\int_{-\infty}^{s_{0}}\frac{4}{\cosh(s)^{2}}\,ds\mbox{ and }E_{-}(\alpha):=\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{4}{\cosh(s)^{2}}\,ds

depending on the direction we start at the initial value. Evaluating the first integral yields

E_{+}(\alpha)=4\left[\frac{\sinh s}{\cosh s}\right]_{-\infty}^{s_{0}}=4\frac{\sinh s_{0}}{\cosh s_{0}}+4

(3.20)

and similarly the second integral is

E_{-}(\alpha)=4\left[\frac{\sinh s}{\cosh s}\right]_{s_{0}}^{\infty}=4-4\frac{\sinh s_{0}}{\cosh s_{0}}.

(3.21)

Now we turn to the following boundary value problem for a given
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha_{\pm}>0
, cf. Theorem
1.2
.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\mbox{Find two asymptotic geodesics }c^{x}_{\pm}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}\mbox{ such that }\\
c^{x}_{-}(0)=(-1,\alpha_{-}),\ d_{s}c^{x}_{-}(0)=(\alpha_{-},0),\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}c^{x}_{-}(s)=(x,0)\\
c^{x}_{+}(0)=(1,\alpha_{+}),\ d_{s}c^{x}_{+}(0)=(\alpha_{+},0),\lim_{s\rightarrow-\infty}c_{+}^{x}(s)=(x,0).\end{array}\right.

(3.22)

In our formulas above we will have to replace
x
by
x\mp 1
depending on whether we start at the right or the left side. Instead of
s_{0}
we call the starting point
s_{\pm}
. Then the elastic energy
W^{h}_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}(x):=W_{h}(c^{x}_{-}\oplus c^{x}_{+})
is given with the help of (
3.20
) and (
3.21
) by

\displaystyle W^{h}_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}(x)=

\displaystyle 4\frac{\sinh s_{+}}{\cosh s_{+}}+4+4-4\frac{\sinh s_{-}}{\cosh s_{-}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:3_17}/\eqref{eq:3_18}}{=}

\displaystyle 8+4\frac{\left|\frac{\alpha_{+}+(x-1)}{\alpha_{+}-(x-1)}\right|-\left|\frac{\alpha_{+}-(x-1)}{\alpha_{+}+(x-1)}\right|}{\left|\frac{\alpha_{+}+(x-1)}{\alpha_{+}-(x-1)}\right|+\left|\frac{\alpha_{+}-(x-1)}{\alpha_{+}+(x-1)}\right|}-4\frac{\left|\frac{\alpha_{-}+(x+1)}{\alpha_{-}-(x+1)}\right|-\left|\frac{\alpha_{-}-(x-1)}{\alpha_{-}+(x+1)}\right|}{\left|\frac{\alpha_{-}+(x+1)}{\alpha_{-}-(x+1)}\right|+\left|\frac{\alpha_{-}-(x-1)}{\alpha_{-}+(x+1)}\right|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8+4\frac{(\alpha_{+}+(x-1))^{2}-(\alpha_{+}-(x-1))^{2}}{(\alpha_{+}+(x-1))^{2}+(\alpha_{+}-(x-1))^{2}}

\displaystyle-4\frac{(\alpha_{-}+(x+1))^{2}-(\alpha_{-}-(x-1))^{2}}{(\alpha_{-}+(x+1))^{2}+(\alpha_{-}-(x+1))^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8+4\left(\frac{4\alpha_{+}(x-1)}{2\alpha_{+}^{2}+2(x-1)^{2}}-\frac{4\alpha_{-}(x+1)}{2\alpha_{-}^{2}+2(x+1)^{2}}\right).

Hence the elastic energy is given by

W^{h}_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}(x)=8+8\left(\frac{\alpha_{+}(x-1)}{\alpha_{+}^{2}+(x-1)^{2}}-\frac{\alpha_{-}(x+1)}{\alpha_{-}^{2}+(x+1)^{2}}\right).

(3.23)

By inspection this formula still holds true, if we use a half circle instead of an asymptotic geodesic, i.e. if
x=\pm 1\mp\alpha_{\pm}
.
Hence by Lemma
3.2
the elastic energy of a solution to (
3.22
) has to be given by (
3.23
).

We define the infimum to be

W^{hmin}_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}:=\inf\{W^{h}_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}(x)|\ x\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}\}.

(3.24)

Please note, that the case
x=\infty
implies here
c^{x}_{\pm}
to be of the form
s\mapsto\alpha_{\pm}\cosh(s/\alpha_{\pm})
.

Now we also prove the claim for the asymptotic in Theorem
1.2
, i.e. for
\alpha_{-}>0
fixated we have

\liminf_{\alpha_{+}\rightarrow\infty}W^{hmin}_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}\geq 4.

(3.25)

Proof.

Using a half circle and one asymptotic geodesic, we obtain by Lemma

W^{hmin}_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}<8.

This and

\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}W^{h}_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}(x)=8

yields that there exists an
x_{\alpha_{+}}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

W^{hmin}_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}=W_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}(x_{\alpha_{+}}).

We distinguish two cases. The first is

\limsup_{\alpha_{+}\rightarrow\infty}|x_{\alpha_{+}}|=\infty.

(3.26)

After extracting a subsequence, we can actually assume to have convergence to
\infty
in (
3.26
). Then Youngs inequality yields

\displaystyle W_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}(x_{\alpha_{+}})=

\displaystyle 8+8\left(\frac{\alpha_{+}(x_{\alpha_{+}}-1)}{\alpha_{+}^{2}+(x_{\alpha_{+}}-1)^{2}}-\frac{\alpha_{-}(x_{\alpha_{+}}+1)}{\alpha_{-}^{2}+(x_{\alpha_{+}}+1)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 8-4-8\frac{\alpha_{-}(x_{\alpha_{+}}+1)}{\alpha_{-}^{2}-(x_{\alpha_{+}}+1)^{2}}\rightarrow 4\mbox{ für }\alpha_{+}\rightarrow\infty,

which handles the first case. The other case is

\limsup_{\alpha_{+}\rightarrow\infty}|x_{\alpha_{+}}|<\infty.

(3.27)

Hence we find a constant
C>0
, such that

x_{\alpha_{+}}\in[-C,C].

Hence we only need to examine
x\in[-C,C]
. Since we locally have uniform convergence, i.e.

W_{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}}\rightarrow E_{\infty}

with

E_{\infty}(x):=8-8\frac{\alpha_{-}(x+1)}{\alpha_{-}^{2}+(x+1)^{2}},

it is sufficient to find the minimisers of
E_{\infty}
:
Again by Youngs inequality, we have

E_{\infty}(x)\geq 8-4\frac{\alpha_{-}^{2}+(x+1)^{2}}{\alpha_{-}^{2}+(x+1)^{2}}=4.

All in all this yields (
3.25
).

(4.11)

In a first step we assume
\operatorname{diam}(S(\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell}))\leq C<\infty
, i.e. bounded independent of
\ell
.
By
[
36
, Lemma 1]
and the bound on the Willmore energy (
4.11
), we obtain

\mu_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell}}(\mathbb{R})=\operatorname{area}(S(\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell}))\leq C<\infty.

Since the corresponding Willmore energy and the area are bounded, we can apply Theorem
4.6
after a possible reparametrization.
Hence after choosing a suitable subsequence, we obtain a generalized generator
\tilde{\gamma}_{min}:[0,1]\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{H}}
(which is not a point on the
x
-axis due to the boundary data), such that

\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell}\rightarrow\tilde{\gamma}_{min}\mbox{ as generators.}

Next we show that

\forall\ t\in]0,1[\mbox{ we have }\tilde{\gamma}_{min}^{2}(t)>0.

(4.12)

We proceed by contradiction and assume there is a
t\in(0,1)
, such that
\tilde{\gamma}_{min}^{2}(t)=0
.
Then Lemma
A.1
yields

W_{e}(S(\tilde{\gamma}_{min}))=W_{e}(S(\tilde{\gamma}_{min}|_{[0,t]}))+W_{e}(S(\tilde{\gamma}_{min}|_{[t,1]}))\geq 4\pi+4\pi=8\pi.

(4.13)

On the other hand (
4.11
) and the lower semicontinuity Theorem
4.5
yield

W_{e}(S(\tilde{\gamma}_{min}))\leq\liminf_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}W_{e}(S(\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell}))<8\pi,

which is a contradiction. Hence there is no such
t
.

By the uniform convergence (
4.2
), we still have that
\tilde{\gamma}_{min}
consists of the boundary circle
\gamma_{circle}(p,\beta)
and some other part
\gamma_{min}:[0,1]\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{H}}
i.e. we have after a possible reparametrization

\tilde{\gamma}_{min}=\gamma_{circ}(p,\beta)\oplus\gamma_{min}.

By the Sobolov embedding and Lemma
4.2

\gamma_{min}
satisfies the boundary data, hence

\gamma_{min}|_{(0,1)}\in M_{p,\beta,x}.

Finally the lower semicontinuity Theorem
4.5
yields

\displaystyle W_{e}(S(\gamma_{circ}))+\liminf_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}W_{e}(S(\gamma_{\ell}))=\liminf_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}W_{e}(S(\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle W_{e}(S(\tilde{\gamma}_{min})=W_{e}(S(\gamma_{circ}))+W_{e}(S(\gamma_{min})).

Hence by (
2.7
), i.e. (
4.10
) we obtain

W_{h}(\gamma_{min})\leq\liminf_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}W_{h}(\gamma_{\ell}),

hence
\gamma_{min}
is a minimiser.

Let us now consider the case, that for a subsequence

\operatorname{diam}(S(\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell}))\rightarrow\infty.

By an inversion at a suitable circle with center
(x_{inv},0)
on the
x
-axis (avoiding the intersection of
\gamma_{circ}
with the
x
-axis and
x
itself), we obtain a sequence of generalized generators obeying new boundary values.
By the conformal invariance of the Willmore energy and (
4.10
) the new sequence is minimising for the new boundary data as well. Furthermore the new curves have bounded diameter.
Then the same arguments as above apply (especially (
4.13
), see also
[
15
, Figure 5]
for a similar argument) and we obtain a minimiser, which does not intersect with the inversion point
(x_{inv},0)
by (
4.12
).
Therefore going back to the original sequence yields by the uniform convergence (
4.2
), that the diameter is bounded. This is a contradiction.

All in all we found a minimiser
\gamma_{min}\in M_{p,\beta,x}
. It is obviously critical w.r.t.
W_{h}
and by e.g.
[
15
, §5]
it is smooth in
[0,1)
.
Moreover it has finite elastic energy
W_{h}
and the discussion in
[
24
, table 2c]
yields
\gamma_{min}
to be part of an asymptotic geodesic curve or a hyperbolic geodesic.
Asymptotic geodesic curves are Moebius transformed catenoids (cf. the discussion in section
3
) and therefore the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Lemma
3.2
together with Lemma
3.1
yield the minimiser in Theorem
4.7
to be unique.

5
Proof of main Theorem
1.1

We start with Theorem
1.1
. Our argument relies on
A.3
, i.e. the paper
[
35
]
. Furthermore our argument is strongly inspired by
[
16
, Prop. 3.7 and Prop. 3.9]
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma:[0,T)\times[0,1]
be the family of profile curves corresponding to a short time solution of the rotational symmetric Willmore flow. Here
T\in[0,\infty]
is the maximal time of existence.
Existence and uniqueness is discussed in
[
35
, Appendix C]
, see also the references therein.
We proceed by contradiction and assume the hyperbolic length diverges, i.e. that there exists a sequence
t_{\ell}\uparrow T
with

\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{H}}(\gamma(t_{\ell},\cdot))=\infty.

(5.1)

As in the proof of Theorem
4.7
or as in (
1.6
) (see also Figure
1
) we complement the boundary conditions by adding sphere caps
\operatorname{cap}_{-}=S(\gamma_{circ}(x_{-},\alpha_{-},\beta_{-}))
and
\operatorname{cap}_{+}=S(\gamma_{circ}(x_{+},\alpha_{+},\beta_{+})),
to
\gamma(t_{\ell},\cdot)
, which are independent of
\ell
.
We call the resulting curves
\tilde{\gamma}(t_{\ell},\cdot)
.
Since
W^{e}_{closed}
is invariant under inversions, we assume the sphere caps to be bounded and spherical. We can that, because in the end we will just compare energies.

Hence we are able to choose
x\in\mathbb{R}
, such that
c^{x}
(see (
1.11
)) consists of at least one part of a circle. Then (
3.20
)/(
3.21
) together with the Lemmata
3.1
and
3.2
yield

W_{h}(c^{x})<8.

Combining (
2.7
) with Lemma
4.3
gives us for this
x\in\mathbb{R}
(cf. (
4.10
))

\displaystyle\begin{split}W^{e}_{closed}(S({c^{x}}))=&\frac{\pi}{2}(W_{h}(\gamma_{circ}(x_{-},\alpha_{-},\beta_{-}))+W_{h}(\gamma_{circ}(x_{+},\alpha_{+},\beta_{+}))\\
&+W_{h}(c^{x})+8+8)<12\pi.\end{split}

(5.2)

The assumptions on the initial data in Theorem
1.1
therefore yields

W^{e}_{closed}(S({\gamma_{0}}))<12\pi.

(5.3)

First we assume the diameter to be bounded, i.e.

\sup_{\ell}\operatorname{diam}S({\tilde{\gamma}(t_{\ell},\cdot)})<\infty.

Again using
[
36
, Lemma 1]
and the bound on the Willmore energy (
5.3
), we obtain

\mu_{\tilde{\gamma}(t_{\ell},\cdot)}(\mathbb{R})=\operatorname{area}(S(\tilde{\gamma}(t_{\ell},\cdot)))\leq C<\infty.

As in the proof of Theorem
4.7
the
\tilde{\gamma}(t_{\ell},\cdot)
are generalized generators.
Hence we can apply Theorem
4.6
and choose a subsequence with

\tilde{\gamma}(t_{\ell},\cdot)\rightarrow\tilde{\gamma}_{sing}\mbox{ as generators for }\ell\rightarrow\infty

and
\tilde{\gamma}:[0,1]\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{H}}
is a generalized generator.
Since the sphere caps
\operatorname{cap}_{\pm}
remain unchanged in the limit by the uniform convergence, (
4.2
), the inner regularity Lemma
4.2
together with Sobolev embedding shows
\tilde{\gamma}_{sing}\in C^{1}_{loc}((0,1)\cap\tilde{\gamma}_{sing}^{-1}(\mathbb{H}))
.
Hence the restriction of
\tilde{\gamma}_{sing}
to the part without the sphere caps, which we call after reparametrization
\gamma_{sing}:[0,1]\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{H}}
, still satisfies the boundary values (
1.5
).
Then by (
5.2
) and Lemma
A.1
we find at most one
s_{0}\in(0,1)
with

\gamma^{2}_{sing}(s_{0})=0.

Since the hyperbolic length of
\gamma(t_{\ell},\cdot)
diverges to
\infty
and since the diameter is bounded, by uniform convergence, this
s_{0}
has to exist.
Hence
\gamma_{sing}|_{[0,s_{0}]}
and
\gamma_{sing}|_{[s_{0},1]}
respectively satisfy a boundary value problem as in Theorem
4.7
with
(\gamma_{sing}^{1}(s_{0}),0)
as singular boundary value. Therefore Theorem
4.7
yields

W^{e}_{closed}(S(\gamma_{sing}))\geq\inf\{W^{e}_{closed}(S({c^{x}}))|\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}.

The lower semi-continuity Theorem
4.5
, the energy assumption of Theorem
1.1
and the strict monotonicity of
t\mapsto W^{e}_{closed}(S({\gamma(t,\cdot)}))
outside of critical points (see e.g.
[
35
, Eq. (2.3)]
) yields

\displaystyle W^{e}_{closed}(S(\gamma_{sing}))\leq\liminf_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}W^{e}_{closed}(S({\gamma(t_{\ell},\cdot)}))<W^{e}_{closed}(S({\gamma(0,\cdot)}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf\{W^{e}_{closed}(S({c^{x}}))|\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}\leq W^{e}_{closed}(S(\gamma_{sing})),

which is a contradiction.

Let us now turn to the other case, i.e.

\operatorname{diam}(\tilde{\gamma}(t_{\ell},\cdot))\rightarrow\infty\mbox{ for }\ell\rightarrow\infty.

By an inversion at an appropriate fixated point
(x_{inv},0)
on the
x
-axis (cf. proof of Theorem
4.7
), we obtain a sequence of curves with bounded diameter, different but fixated boundary values and the same Willmore energy.
Hence we can apply the arguments of the first case and arrive at a contradiction there as well.
This is possible because
W^{e}_{closed}
is invariant w.r.t. to Moebius transformations of the upper half plane and therefore the energy assumptions of Theorem
1.1
carry over.

All in all we obtain a contradiction and the hyperbolic length stays bounded. Then Theorem
A.3
yields the desired result.
∎

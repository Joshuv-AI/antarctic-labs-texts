t-structures on a local 4-Calabi-Yau variety
Yirui Xiong

Abstract
. Let X = Tot ⁡ Ω ℙ 2 X=\Tot\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{2}} denote the total space of cotangent bundle of ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} . This is a non-compact Calabi-Yau 4 4 -fold (also called local Calabi-Yau variety in the physics literature). The aim of this paper is to use the tilting objects to characterize a large class of t-structures in the derived category of X X and study the combinatorics of their simple tilts.



1.
Introduction

The conception of the t-structure in the derived category (more generally triangulated category) first appeared in the paper of Beilinson, Bernstein and Deligne
[
15
]
as a powerful tool to study the perverse sheaves of a stratified topological space. The key feature of a t-structure is giving a formal construction of abelian subcategories (called hearts) other than the standard one, and this becomes an important ingredient in the definition of stability conditions in triangulated categories by Bridgeland
[
12
]
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category, a stability condition on
\mathcal{D}
is a pair of
\sigma=(\mathcal{A},Z)
where
\mathcal{A}
is a heart of some bounded t-structure in
\mathcal{D}
and a group homomorphism
Z:K(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying Harder-Narasimhan property
[
12
, Proposition 5.3]
. One of the most important properties is that the space of stability conditions
\Stab\mathcal{D}
(satisfying the local-finite conditions) will form a complex manifold, perhaps infinite dimensional
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
. In particular when the heart
\mathcal{A}
is of finite length, then we can get an open subset
U(\mathcal{A})\subset\Stab\mathcal{D}
which consists of stability conditions with fixed bounded hearts
\mathcal{A}
.
U(\mathcal{A})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}^{n}
(
\mathbb{H}
is the upper half plane) where
n
is the number of simple objects in
\mathcal{A}
. And two such open subsets are glued along 1-codimensional subset if the corresponding hearts are related by a simple tilt (see Section
2.1
).

The t-structures of
3
-Calabi-Yau categories have been studied a lot, and the space of stability conditions have been calculated in many cases
[
10
,
11
,
24
,
13
]
. For example, Bridgeland in
[
10
]
studied the case of total space of
\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
the canonical bundle where the t-structures are given by full exceptional collections on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and simple tilts are related to mutations of exceptional collections on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. In this paper, we are focusing on
X=\Tot\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
the total space of cotangent bundle, which is a non-compact Calabi-Yau
4
-fold. It is worth studying
X
since it is the Springer resolution of the nilpotent orbit in
sl(3)
. The t-structures of the derived category of
X
(more generally the cotagent bundle of flag varieties) were first studied in the work
[
2
,
4
,
3
]
, where the so-called exotic t-structures were found. In our paper, we constructed a large class of t-structures by finding the tilting objects in
D^{b}(\Coh X)
.

We firstly explain how to construct a t-structure by using tilting objects:

1.1.

Let
\mathcal{D}=D^{b}(\Coh V)
where
V
is a variety over
\mathbb{C}
,
T
be an object in
\mathcal{D}
satisfying

(1)

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}\left(T,T[n]\right)=0
, unless
n=0
;

(2)

T
is a classical generator of
\mathcal{D}
, i.e., the smallest triangulated subcategory containing
T
is
\mathcal{D}
,

then
T
is called a tilting object. By Rickard’s
[
28
,
22
]
general theory of derived Morita equivalence, we have an equivalence between derived categories:

(1.1)

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}\left(T,-\right):\mathcal{D}\longrightarrow D^{b}(\text{mod-}B)

where
B:=\End_{V}T
. What we are mostly interested is that the above equivalence will give us a bounded t-structure
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
by pulling back the standard one on the right side:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{F\in\mathcal{D}:\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(T,F[i])=0,\ i>0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{F\in\mathcal{D}:\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(T,F[i])=0,\ i<0\right\}.

We are characterizing the bounded t-structures of the above form in our case.

1.2.

We denote the projection map
\pi:X=\Tot\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
. The usual strategy is to find tilting objects in
D^{b}(\Coh X)
via pulling back the tilting objects from
\mathbb{P}^{2}
by
\pi
. More precisely, we consider the full exceptional collection
\mathbb{E}=(E_{0},E_{1},E_{2})
(see definition

) consisting of sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
thus
\oplus_{i}E_{i}
is a tilting object on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, then we try to show that
\pi^{*}\left(\oplus_{i}E_{i}\right)
is a tilting object for

. However, this is not always possible. We need to use the helix theory: for any full exceptional collection
\mathbb{E}=(E_{0},\cdots,E_{n})
, one can associate
\mathbb{H}=(\cdots,E_{-1},E_{0},E_{1},\cdots,E_{n},\cdots)
a collection of exceptional objects which is periodic under the Serre functor, and any successive
n+1
objects in
\mathbb{H}
(called a thread) will be a full exceptional collection. Now our first result on tilting objects in
D^{b}(\Coh X)
is stated as the following:

Proposition 1.1

(= Proposition
3.7
)
.

For any full and strong exceptional collection (see Definition
2.5
)
\mathbb{E}=(E_{0},E_{1},E_{2})
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, one can always find a thread
(E_{i},E_{i+1},E_{i+2})

in the corresponding helix

, such that the pulling back
\bigoplus_{i}^{i+2}\pi^{*}E_{i}
is a tilting object in
D^{b}(\Coh X)
.

Then we introduce a quiver associated with
\mathbb{E}
which encodes the non-tilting information of
\mathbb{E}
in the above, and is called the secondary quiver of
\mathbb{E}
. We denote it by

from mod-

(=Theorem

)
.

For any full and strong exceptional collection
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}

Here tilting objects in mod-

means they are tilting objects when viewed as objects in

Let

be the full subcategory of

consisting of objects with support on the zero section

We remark that the combinatorics of simple tilts are complicated, and not controlled entirely by the mutations of exceptional collections.

2.2.
Exceptional collections

Definition 2.5

(Exceptional collections)
.

An object
E
in
\mathcal{D}
is said to be exceptional if

\Hom^{k}_{\mathcal{D}}(E,E)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{C},&\ \text{if }k=0,\\
0,&\ \text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

An exceptional collection
{\mathbb{E}}\subset\mathcal{D}
is a sequence of exceptional objects

{\mathbb{E}}=(E_{0},\cdots,E_{n})

such that for all
0\leq i<j\leq n
, we have
\Hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{j},E_{i})=0
.

An exceptional collection
\mathbb{E}=(E_{0},\cdots,E_{n})
is said to be strong if for all
i,\ j

\Hom^{k}_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{i},E_{j})=0,\ \ \text{unless }k=0.

We write
\langle\mathbb{E}\rangle\subset\mathcal{D}
for the smallest full triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
containing the elements of an exceptional collection
\mathbb{E}\subset\mathcal{D}
. An exceptional collection
\mathbb{E}
is said to be full if
\langle\mathbb{E}\rangle=\mathcal{D}
.

From the definitions above, we have that for a full and strong exceptional collection
\mathbb{E}
, then
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}E_{i}
is a tilting object in
\mathcal{D}
.
Given an exceptional collection
\mathbb{E}
in
\mathcal{D}
, the right orthogonal subcategory to
\mathbb{E}
is the full triangulated subcategory

\mathbb{E}^{\bot}=\left\{X\in\mathcal{D}:\ \Hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(E,X)=0\ \text{for }E\in\mathbb{E}\right\}.

Similarly, the left orthogonal subcategory to
\mathbb{E}
is

{}^{\bot}\mathbb{E}=\left\{X\in\mathcal{D}:\ \Hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,E)=0\ \text{for }E\in\mathbb{E}\right\}.

\langle\mathbb{E}\rangle
is admissible due to
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
, i.e. the inclusion functor

has left and right adjoint functors. Thus the fullness of
\mathbb{E}
is equivalent to
\mathbb{E}^{\bot}=0
or
{}^{\bot}\mathbb{E}=0
.

2.4.
Mutation of Helices

Under our assumptions for category

Definition 2.8

(Helix)
.

A sequence of objects
\mathbb{H}=(E_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
\mathcal{D}
is a helix if there exist positive integers

such that

(1)

for each

the corresponding thread
(E_{i},\cdots,E_{i+n})
is a full exceptional collection,

(2)

for each

one has
E_{i-n-1}=S_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{i})[-d]
.

Given a full exceptional
\mathbb{E}
, we can generate a helix using the Serre functor

in the way by defining
E_{i-m(n+1)}:=(S_{\mathcal{D}})^{m}(E_{i})[-dm]
for integer

. Similarly we have the mutation for helices: given a helix
\mathbb{H}=(E_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and an integer

, then we can get a new helix
\sigma_{i}(\mathbb{H})=\mathbb{H}^{\prime}=(E^{\prime}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}

E^{\prime}_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}E_{j-1}&\text{if }j=i\text{ mod }n+1\\

E_{j}&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

We want to ask which property will be preserved under mutation, for example, strong exceptional collection will not be preserved under mutation.

\mathbb{P}^{n}
is called stable (resp. semi-stable), if for any subsheaf
\mathcal{E}
with strictly smaller rank than
\mathcal{F}
, then we have

\mu(\mathcal{E})<\mu(\mathcal{F})\ \text{(resp. }\mu(\mathcal{E})\leq\mu(\mathcal{F})\text{)}

The following useful lemma is proved in
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
25
, Proposition 2.9]
.

Lemma 2.12
.

If
E
is an exceptional object on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, then
E
is a shift of a locally free and stable sheaf.

A good proof of the following result is given by Bondal and Polishchuk
[
6
, Example 3.2]
.

Lemma 2.13

(Gorodentsev, Rudakov)
.

If
(E_{0},E_{1},E_{2})
is a full and strong exceptional collection in
D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
, then the positive integers
(a,b,c)
defined by
a=\dim\Hom_{D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2})}(E_{0},E_{1})
,
b=\dim\Hom_{D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2})}(E_{1},E_{2})
and
c=\dim\Hom_{D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2})}(E_{0},E_{2})
satisfy the Markov equation:

a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=abc.

Using the above lemmas, we can compare the slopes between bundles in an exceptional collection in
D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
:

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\mathbb{E}=(E_{0},E_{1},E_{2})
be a full and strong exceptional collection consisting of sheaves in
D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
, then for
j>i
, we have
\mu(E_{j})>\mu(E_{i})
.

Proof.

If not, we suppose
\mu(E_{j})\leq\mu(E_{i})
for some
j>i
. Then we claim that
\Hom_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(E_{i},E_{j})=0
since

(1)

if
\mu(E_{j})=\mu(E_{i})
. Any non-trivial homomorphism between stable bundles of equal slopes must be an isomorphism. However, since
\Hom_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(E_{j},E_{i})=0
by definition of an exceptional sequence, then we could only have
\Hom(E_{i},E_{j})=0
;

(2)

if
\mu(E_{j})<\mu(E_{i})
, then
\Hom_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(E_{i},E_{j})=0
since there is no map between stable bundles from the one with bigger slope to the smaller slope.

Then by Lemma
2.13
, we have
a=b=c=0
. Thus there is no map among
E_{0}
,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
.

Since the exceptional collection is full, we see that
D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
can be decomposed into two subcategories
\langle E_{0}\rangle
and
\langle E_{1},\ E_{2}\rangle
. But by
[
9
, Example 3.2]
, this would imply that
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is not connected, which is absurd.
∎

The following known result will be useful to us:

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
\mathbb{E}=(E_{0},E_{1},E_{2})
be a full exceptional collection which consists of sheaves in
D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
. Then
\mathbb{E}
is strong.

Proof.

By
[
25
, Corollary 2.11]
, for
i<j
there is at most one
\Ext^{k}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(E_{i},E_{j})
group which doesn’t vanish, and such
k\neq 2
. So either
\Hom_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(E_{i},E_{j})\neq 0
or
\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(E_{i},E_{j})\neq 0
. By using Riemann-Roch theorem for exceptional sheaves, we have

\chi(E_{i},E_{j})=\frac{1}{2}r(E_{i})r(E_{j})\left((\mu(E_{i})-\mu(E_{j}))^{2}+3(\mu(E_{j})-\mu(E_{i}))+\frac{1}{r^{2}(E_{i})}+\frac{1}{r^{2}(E_{j})}\right).

By Lemma
2.14
,
\mu(E_{j})>\mu(E_{i})
when

. Thus
\chi(E_{i},E_{j})>0
, which implies
\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(E_{i},E_{j})=0
.
∎

Proof of Main theorem I

First we show for an object which classically generates the derived category of

Lemma 3.1

Let

Let

Applying the same argument in reverse this means that

∎

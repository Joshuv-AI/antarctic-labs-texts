Residue of special functions of Anderson A A -modules at the characteristic graph
Quentin Gazda, and Andreas Maurischat

Abstract




1
Introduction

Context and motivations

Let
(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
be a geometrically irreducible smooth projective curve over a finite field
\mathbb{F}
and let
\infty
be a closed point on it. Let
A=\mathcal{O}_{C}(C\setminus\{\infty\})
be the
\mathbb{F}
-algebra of rational functions on
C
that are regular away from
\infty
. Let
K
be the function field of
C
. We denote by
K_{\infty}
the completed local field of
C
at the point
\infty
and we let
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
be the completion of an algebraic closure of
K_{\infty}
.

This paper deals with the following objects introduced in the pioneer work of Anderson
[
And86
]
.

Definition 1.1

(Anderson
A
-module)
.

An
A
-module scheme
E
over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
is called an
Anderson
A
-module of dimension
d
if the following holds:

1.

As an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space scheme over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
,
E
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}
and,

2.

The
L
-linear map
\partial a
, induced by the functorial action of
a\in A
on the
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-vector space
\operatorname{Lie}_{E}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
, satisfies
(\partial a-a\cdot\operatorname{id})^{d}=0
.

We refer to Section
2
below for the details.

If
C
is the projective line over
\mathbb{F}
and
\infty
is the point
[0:1]
, then
A
is identified with the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}[t]
. In this situation, the
Carlitz module
is a certain Anderson
A
-module scheme over
K
playing a role similar to that of the multiplicative group scheme in the arithmetic of function fields (
cf

[
GJ23
]
). It consists of the
\mathbb{F}[t]
-module scheme
\mathsf{C}
over
K=\mathbb{F}(\theta)
which, as an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space scheme, is
\mathbb{G}_{a}
and whose
\mathbb{F}[t]
-module action is given by
t\cdot x:=\theta x+x^{q}
for
x\in\mathbb{G}_{a}(-)
. It is an Anderson
\mathbb{F}[t]
-module of dimension one.

The Carlitz module is the simplest example of a
Drinfeld module
, themselves encompassed in the definition of Anderson
A
-modules.

The arithmetic of Anderson modules is in many aspects comparable to that of abelian varieties, but with the fundamental difference that their coefficients are function rings or fields. Following this analogy, one can study their geometric features. For instance, one may define their corresponding “cohomological realizations” such as the analogue of the Betti (
i.e.
singular) and that of the de Rham realization (see
e.g.

[
HJ20
]
).

In
[
GM21
]
, we constructed an
A
-module
\mathfrak{sf}(E)
of
special functions
attached to an Anderson
A
-module
E
, assembling prior existing construction in the literature. Roughly speaking (see Definition
4.10
for details),
\mathfrak{sf}(E)
is of the sub-
A
-module of a certain completed tensor product

A\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}}E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty}),

(1.1)

relative to a certain metric on
E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
, consisting of elements
\psi
for which
(1\otimes a)\psi=(a\otimes 1)\psi
for all
a\in A
. Although the counterpart of the construction (
1.1
) does not make sense for abelian varieties (due to the lack of a tensor product over
\mathbb{F}
), the resulting module
\mathfrak{sf}(E)
is–whenever
E
is uniformizable–canonically isomorphic to the dual of the
Betti realization
of
E
(
[
GM21
, Thm. 3.3]
).

The prototype of such special functions is the
Anderson-Thakur generating function

\omega
, first introduced by Anderson–Thakur in
[
AT90
]
in the situation of the Carlitz module. It is given by the infinite product

\omega(t)=\lambda_{\theta}\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}{\left(1-\frac{t}{\theta^{q^{i}}}\right)^{-1}}

(1.2)

which converges in the Tate algebra
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle t\rangle
, where
\lambda_{\theta}
is a fixed
(q-1)
th-root of
-\theta
in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. The element
\omega(t)
generates the free
\mathbb{F}[t]
-module
\mathfrak{sf}(\mathsf{C})
of special functions attached to
\mathsf{C}
.

The purpose of the elements of
\mathfrak{sf}(E)
is to play the same role for
E
as that played by
\omega
for the Carlitz module. Some fascinating features of
\mathfrak{sf}(E)
are its relation to periods, function fields Gauss sums (known as
Gauss-Thakur sums
) and, by the very recent work of Hermes Ferraro
[
Fer22
,
Fer23
]
, to
Pellarin type
L
-series
. Special functions permit to extend results known for
\omega
(universal Gauss-Thakur sums
[
AP15
]
and Pellarin’s identity
[
Pel12
]
) from
\mathsf{C}
to more general Anderson modules.

In
[
GM21
, Thm. 3.11]
we also described a relation among
\mathfrak{sf}(E)
and the
period lattice

\Lambda_{E}
of
E
which we now recall. Given
u\in A
a non constant element such that
K/\mathbb{F}(u)
is a (finite) separable field extension–we say that
u
is
separating
–there is an isomorphism of
A
-modules:

\delta_{u}:\mathfrak{d}_{A/\mathbb{F}[u]}\otimes_{A}\Lambda_{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{sf}(E)

(1.3)

where
\mathfrak{d}_{A/\mathbb{F}[u]}
is the different ideal relative to the extension
K/\mathbb{F}(u)
. In the situation of
A=\mathbb{F}[t]
, the ideal
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathbb{F}[t]/\mathbb{F}[u(t)]}
is principally generated by the derivative
u^{\prime}
of
u
, and the corresponding isomorphism (
1.3
) maps the generator
u^{\prime}(t)\otimes\tilde{\pi}
to
\omega(t)
. Here, we wrote
\tilde{\pi}
for the
Carlitz period
, the function field counterpart of
2\pi i
.

Although (
1.3
) is functorial in
E
, it is non canonical as it depends on the choice of
u
. However, one can make the following observation: the class of
\mathfrak{d}_{A/\mathbb{F}[u]}
and that of
\Omega^{1}_{A/\mathbb{F}}
are mutual inverses to each other in the group
\operatorname{Pic}(A)
. It then becomes natural to ask whether (
1.3
) admits a “canonical inverse”, taking the form

\Omega^{1}_{A/\mathbb{F}}\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{sf}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Lambda_{E}.

(1.4)

That the above does not depend on
u
was already noticed by the authors in the situation of
A=\mathbb{F}[t]
(Proposition 3.13 in
loc. cit.
).

The purpose of this text is to provide a positive answer to the above question, and to interpret (
1.4
) as a residue map. In this respect, we extend the result of the second author in
[
Mau18a
, Prop. 3.8]
to the general coefficient ring
A
.

Constructions and results

The main technicality of this note rests in the signification of
what we mean
by a residue map. Let us fix some notations in order to be more precise: let
E
be an Anderson
A
-module of dimension
d
over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. To pursue the presentation, we shall state precisely the construction of the completed module (
1.1
): once an isomorphism
\kappa:E\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}
of
\mathbb{F}
-vector spaces
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-schemes is fixed–called
a choice of coordinates
–we obtain a non-canonical identification among
A\otimes E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
and
(A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty})^{d}
. The Gauss norm on the latter induces a metric on the former, and one shows that this metric is independent of the choice of coordinates
\kappa
. Taking the completion with respect to this metric, we obtain (
1.1
), interpreting it thusly as a module of “
E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
-valued functions on the affinoid disk
\operatorname{Sp}(A\hat{\otimes}\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
”.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be the rigid analytic variety
(\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty})^{\text{rig}}
; we shall review its construction, as well as sheaves of meromorphic functions on it, in Section
3
below. If one wishes to extend (
1.1
) to more general admissible opens
\mathfrak{U}
of
\mathfrak{X}
, one encounters the following issue: surely, one wants “
E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
-valued functions on
\mathfrak{U}
” to be isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U})^{d}
after the choice of
\kappa
. Yet, this is not a reasonable wish in case the dimension is bigger than one. Indeed, assume
d>1
and suppose we have two choices of coordinates
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}:E\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}
which differ from one another by the automorphism

\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathbb{F}}\\
&1&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}).

Then, the module of “
E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
-valued functions on
\mathfrak{U}
” will also be identified with
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U}\cap\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathfrak{U}))^{d}
, preventing the notion of “
E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
-valued functions on
\mathfrak{U}
” to be well-defined.

To make things independent on the choice of coordinates, we are required to work with admissible opens
\mathfrak{U}
that we call
\tau
-costable
, meaning that
\mathfrak{U}\subseteq\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathfrak{U})
. If
\underline{\mathfrak{X}}=(\operatorname{Cat}\mathfrak{X},\operatorname{Cov}\mathfrak{X})
denotes the site on
\mathfrak{X}
corresponding to the
strong Grothendieck topology
, we denote by
\,{}^{\tau}\underline{\mathfrak{X}}=(\operatorname{Cat}^{\tau}\mathfrak{X},\operatorname{Cov}^{\tau}\mathfrak{X})
the induced site compound of
\tau
-costable admissible opens and coverings of
\underline{\mathfrak{X}}
(we refer the reader to
e.g.

[
Bos14
, §5.1]
for notions on Grothendieck topologies, which only appear at a basic level in this text). The
\tau
-costable site

\,{}^{\tau}\underline{\mathfrak{X}}
happens to be enough for our purpose (see Section
4
):

Proposition 1.2
.

Up to canonical isomorphisms, there exists a unique pair
(\mathcal{E},\iota)
consisting of a sheaf
\mathcal{E}
of
A\otimes A
-modules on
\,{}^{\tau}\underline{\mathfrak{X}}
and a family
\iota=(\iota(\mathfrak{U}):A\otimes E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\to\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{U}))_{\mathfrak{U}}
of
A\otimes A
-linear morphisms indexed by objects
\mathfrak{U}
of
\operatorname{Cat}^{\tau}\mathfrak{X}
such that, for any choice of coordinates
\kappa
and any such
\mathfrak{U}
, there exists an
A
-linear isomorphism
\kappa(\mathfrak{U}):\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{U})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U})^{d}
inserting in a pushout square of
A
-modules:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty})^{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\otimes E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U})^{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{U})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota(\mathfrak{U})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa(\mathfrak{U})^{-1}}

(1.5)

We call
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{U})
the
A\otimes A
-module of
E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
-valued holomorphic functions on
\mathfrak{U}
. More generally, if
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X}}
denotes the sheaf of meromorphic functions on

and

is a

-costable

verifying Proposition

with

.

If
\mathfrak{U}
is the affinoid disk
\mathfrak{D}:=\operatorname{Sp}(A\hat{\otimes}\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
, then
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{D})
is canonically isomorphic to
A\hat{\otimes}E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
(Lemma

). Therefore, the sheaf
\mathcal{E}
naturally extends our construction (
1.1
) to the whole
\,{}^{\tau}\underline{\mathfrak{X}}
. We subsequently generalize the definition of the module of special functions (Definition 3.1 in
loc. cit.
) as follows (Definition

).

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{U}
be an object of
\operatorname{Cat}^{\tau}\mathfrak{X}
. Given
\mathcal{L}
a
\tau
-costable sub-
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-module sheaf of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X}}
, we let
\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(\mathcal{L})(\mathfrak{U})
be the sub-
A
-module of
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L})(\mathfrak{U})
consisting of elements
\omega
satisfying
(a\otimes 1)\omega=(1\otimes a)\omega
for all
a\in A
.

One verifies that
\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(\mathcal{L})
is a sheaf of
A
-modules on
\,{}^{\tau}\underline{\mathfrak{X}}
(Proposition
4.11
) which extends
\mathfrak{sf}(E)
on the disk
\mathfrak{D}
(Lemma
4.9
).

Let
\mathfrak{j}\in\operatorname{Sp}(A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
be the prime ideal given by the kernel of the multiplication map
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. It has been observed in several situations that special functions not only live in the Tate algebra, but can also be analytically continued to meromorphic functions on the full
\mathfrak{X}
with only poles at
\mathfrak{j}
and its iterates by
\tau
(denoted
\tau\mathfrak{j}
,
\tau^{2}\mathfrak{j}
, etc.) and of order at most
d=\dim E
. For instance, the inverse of the Anderson-Thakur function
\omega(t)
has infinite radius of convergence and vanishes only at
t=\theta
,
\theta^{q}
, … In much greater generality, this also appears in the work of Hartl–Juschka
[
HJ20
, §2.3.4]
.

In our setting, the statement takes the following form (Proposition
5.5
):

Theorem 1.4

(Meromorphic continuation of special functions)
.

Let
J
be the rigid analytic divisor
\mathfrak{j}+\tau\mathfrak{j}+\tau^{2}\mathfrak{j}+...
on
\mathfrak{X}
. Then, the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(d\cdot J)
is
\tau
-costable and the map

\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(d\cdot J))(\mathfrak{X})\to\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(d\cdot J))(\mathfrak{D})=\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}})(\mathfrak{D})=\mathfrak{sf}(E),

where the first map is given by restriction, is an
A
-linear isomorphism.

To state the main theorem of this note, we require the notion of
residue map at
\mathfrak{j}
for
E(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
-valued meromorphic functions. Denote by
\mathcal{J}
the sheaf of meromorphic functions on
\underline{\mathfrak{X}}
that are holomorphic away from the support of
J
. Let
\kappa:E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}
be a choice of coordinates; note that
\operatorname{Lie}_{\kappa}
induces coordinates on the tangent space
\operatorname{Lie}_{E}\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}
. The residue map at
\mathfrak{j}
is defined as follows: it is the unique map
\operatorname{res}_{\mathfrak{j}}
of
A
-modules making the following diagram commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega^{1}_{A/\mathbb{F}}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{J})(\mathfrak{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Lie}_{E}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{A/\mathbb{F}}^{1}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{X})^{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{d}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa(\mathfrak{X})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{res}_{\mathfrak{j}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Lie}_{\kappa}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{residue}_{\mathfrak{j}}}

(1.6)

The map denoted
\operatorname{residue}_{\mathfrak{j}}
above denotes the residue map for rigid meromorphic functions; its definition is recalled below in Section
5
.

Our main result is as follows:

Theorem 1.5
.

The morphism
\operatorname{res}_{\mathfrak{j}}:\Omega^{1}_{A/\mathbb{F}}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{J})(\mathfrak{X})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Lie}_{E}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
is independent on the choice of coordinates
\kappa
. Further, through
\mathfrak{sf}(E)=\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(d\cdot J))(\mathfrak{X})\subseteq\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{J})(\mathfrak{X})
, the map
\operatorname{res}_{\mathfrak{j}}
induces an isomorphism
\Omega^{1}_{A/\mathbb{F}}\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{sf}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\Lambda_{E}
.

Remark 1.6
.

Surprisingly, we shall never use in this note the assumption that
E
is uniformizable nor abelian. Theorem
1.5
hence provides a general method to reconstruct the period lattice of
E
without any reference to its exponential function.

An immediate consequence of Theorem
1.5
is that there are no non-zero special functions that are holomorphic on the whole
\mathfrak{X}
:

Corollary 1.7
.

The module
\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}})(\mathfrak{X})
is trivial.

Compiling the results of this note, we notice that the
A
-module
\mathfrak{sf}(E)
carries a natural increasing finite filtration indexed by the order of poles along the divisor
J
:

(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Cor~}\ref{cor:no-pole-no-function}}}{{=}}\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}})(\mathfrak{X})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(J))(\mathfrak{X})\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(d\cdot J))(\mathfrak{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Thm~}\ref{thm:intro-continuation}}}{{=}}\mathfrak{sf}(E).

(1.7)

The above has by design at most
d
jumps. We computed some examples of this filtration in Subsection
5
. We leave the following question open for future research:

Question
.

To what filtration on
\Lambda_{E}
does (
1.7
) correspond?

i.e.
the kernel of the multiplication map:

\mathfrak{j}:=\ker(A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\xrightarrow{\ell\otimes\operatorname{id}}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}).

It is a maximal ideal of
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
whose support
V(\mathfrak{j})
is the graph of
\ell
. We denote by
\tau^{i}\mathfrak{j}
the
i
th iterate of
\mathfrak{j}
by
\tau
; namely
\tau^{i}\mathfrak{j}
is the kernel of the map
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
,
a\otimes b\mapsto\ell(a)b^{q^{i}}
. In this section we recall some classical facts about
\mathfrak{j}
.

We call
separating
any element
u
in
A
such that the extension
K/\mathbb{F}(u)
is finite and separable.

Lemma 2.1
.

Any non separating element in
A
is the
p
-th power of an element in
A
. In particular, for any
a\in A\setminus\mathbb{F}
, there exists a separating element
u\in A
and a non-negative integer
k
such that
a=u^{p^{k}}
.

Proof.

Let
a\in A
be non separating. If
a
is constant, it clearly is a
p
-th power, hence we assume that the extension
K/\mathbb{F}(a)
is finite. There is an intermediate extension
L
such that
K/L
is purely inseparable of degree
p^{k}
for some
k\geq 1
, and
L/\mathbb{F}(a)
is separable. As
K^{p^{k}}\subseteq L
, it is in fact an equality by comparing degrees. Hence,
\mathbb{F}(a)\subset K^{p}
and there exists
b\in K
such that
b^{p}=a
. As the divisor of
a
is the the
p
-th power of the divisor of
b
, we obtain that
b\in A
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The ideal
\mathfrak{j}
is generated by the differences
u\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(u)
where
u
runs over separating elements in
A
.

Proof.

We first claim that
\mathfrak{j}
is generated by the set
\{a\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(a)|a\in A\}
. It is clear that
a\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(a)\in\mathfrak{j}
for all
a\in A
. Conversely, let
\mathfrak{g}
be the ideal of
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
generated by
\{a\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(a)|a\in A\}
. Every element
x\in A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
can be written as a sum of elementary tensors
a_{i}\otimes c_{i}
for some
a_{i}\in A
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. We have

x=\sum_{i}{a_{i}\otimes c_{i}}\equiv\sum_{i}{1\otimes\ell(a_{i})c_{i}}\pmod{\mathfrak{g}}.

If
x\in\mathfrak{j}
, the right-hand sum is zero, hence
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Now, by Lemma
2.1
, for any
a\in A\setminus\mathbb{F}
, there exists a separating element
u
and a positive integer
k
such that
a\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(a)=(u\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(u))^{p^{k}}
. Thus
\mathfrak{j}\subset\langle\{u\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(u)~|~u~\text{separating}\}\rangle
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
u
is a separating element, then
u\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(u)
is a uniformizing parameter for
\mathfrak{j}
.

Proof.

Recall by definition of the module of Kähler differentials that we have an isomorphism
\Omega^{1}_{A/\mathbb{F}}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{j}/\mathfrak{j}^{2}
,
da\mapsto a\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(a)
. Thus, to prove the lemma, it suffices to show that
du\neq 0
for
u
separating. Yet, applying the relative cotangent sequence to
\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{F}(u)\to K
(
e.g.

[
Mat80
, II.10.(26.H)]
), we obtain an exact sequence

Kdu\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{K/\mathbb{F}}\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{K/\mathbb{F}(u)}

whose right-hand side term is zero as
K/\mathbb{F}(u)
is finite separable. Hence, the first map is surjective and the image of
du
is nonzero.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal ideal of
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
different from
\mathfrak{j}
,
\tau\mathfrak{j}
,
\tau^{2}\mathfrak{j}
, … There exists a separating element
u
such that, for all non-negative integer
i
,
u\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(u)^{q^{i}}
does not belong to
\mathfrak{m}
.

Proof.

Let
t
be a separating element. We first compute the prime ideal decomposition of
(t\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(t))
in the Dedekind domain
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. The inclusion of Dedekind rings
\mathbb{F}[t]\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\subset A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
makes
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
a free
\mathbb{F}[t]\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-module of rank
[K:\mathbb{F}(t)]
. In particular, there are at most
[K:\mathbb{F}(t)]
prime divisors of
(t\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(t))
. For
\sigma:\ell(K)\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
an
\mathbb{F}(t)
-algebra morphism, the ideal
\mathfrak{j}^{\sigma}
of
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
generated by the set
\{a\otimes 1-1\otimes\sigma(\ell(a))|a\in A\}
is maximal and divides the principal ideal
(t\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(t))
. There are
\#\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}(t)}(\ell(K),\mathbb{C}_{\infty})=[K:\mathbb{F}(t)]
such ideals, hence

(t\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(t))=\prod_{\sigma}\mathfrak{j}^{\sigma}

where the product runs over
\sigma\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}(t)}(\ell(K),\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
.

We turn to the proof of the lemma. Assume the converse, that is, for all separating elements
v
there exists
j\geq 0
such that
v\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(v)^{q^{j}}\in\mathfrak{m}
. This means that there exists a non-negative integer
i
for which
\mathfrak{m}\supset(t\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(t)^{q^{i}})=\tau^{i}\left(\prod_{\sigma}{\mathfrak{j}^{\sigma}}\right)
. By uniqueness of the prime ideal decomposition, there exists
\sigma\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\ell(\mathbb{F}(t))}(\ell(K),\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
such that
\mathfrak{m}=\tau^{i}(\mathfrak{j}^{\sigma})
. Because
\mathfrak{m}
is distinct from
\mathfrak{j}
,
\tau\mathfrak{j}
,
\tau^{2}\mathfrak{j}
, …, the morphism
\sigma
is not the inclusion
\ell(K)\subset\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. Because
K
is generated by separating elements over
\mathbb{F}
, there exists a separating element
u
such that
\sigma(\ell(u))\neq\ell(u)
. From our converse assumption, there exists a non-negative integer
j
such that
u\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(u)^{q^{j}}\in\mathfrak{m}=\tau^{i}(\mathfrak{j}^{\sigma})
. Hence, both
u\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(u)^{q^{j}}
and
u\otimes 1-1\otimes\sigma(\ell(u))^{q^{i}}
are in
\mathfrak{m}
. Since
\mathfrak{m}\neq A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, this implies
\sigma(\ell(u))^{q^{i}}=\ell(u)^{q^{j}}
.

This is a contradiction. Indeed,
\ell(u)^{q^{i}}
and
\sigma(\ell(u))^{q^{i}}=\ell(u)^{q^{j}}
have the same minimal polynomial over
\ell(\mathbb{F}(t))
so that either the latter polynomial has coefficients in
\mathbb{F}
or
i=j
. The first option is impossible as it would imply
\ell(u)\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}\cap\ell(A)=\mathbb{F}
. The second option is also impossible as we chose
u
such that
\sigma(\ell(u))\neq\ell(u)
.
∎

3
Objects from rigid analytic geometry

Let
X:=\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. In this section, we develop in more details the definition of the rigid analytic variety
\mathfrak{X}:=X^{\text{rig}}
, obtained by applying the rigid analytic GAGA on
X
(see
[
Bos14
, §5.4]
).

The rigid analytic space
\mathfrak{X}

To begin with, we define Gauss norms on
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. Let
c\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\times}
and let
\rho:=|c|>0
. For
f\in A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, we set:

\|f\|_{\rho}:=\inf\left(\max_{i}\{|c_{i}|\rho^{\deg a_{i}}\}\right)

where the infimum is taken over all representations of
f
as finite sums
\sum_{i}{a_{i}\otimes c_{i}}
in
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
.

Remark 3.1
.

For
\rho=1
,
\|\cdot\|_{1}
was denoted by
\|\cdot\|
in
[
GM21
]
and the completed ring denoted by
\mathbb{T}
. We will rather use the notation
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle
instead of
\mathbb{T}
below.

We prove next that
\|\cdot\|_{\rho}
is indeed a Gauss norm. For this, we construct a presentation of
A
as follows. First observe that
A
has a canonical filtration by finite dimensional sub-
\mathbb{F}
-vectors spaces
D_{n}
of elements of degree
\leq n
. Clearly
D_{0}=\mathbb{F}
and the union of all
D_{n}
is
A
. Let
n_{1}
be the least positive integer such that the inclusion
D_{0}\subset D_{n_{1}}
is strict, and let
(a_{1},...,a_{s_{1}})
be a basis of
D_{n_{1}}
. We let
A_{n_{1}}
be the sub-
\mathbb{F}
-algebra of
A
generated by
(a_{1},...,a_{s_{1}})
. Proceed by induction to define
n_{i}
as the least integer
>n_{i-1}
such that
D_{n_{i}}
is not contained in
A_{n_{i-1}}
, let
(a_{s_{1}+1},...,a_{s_{i}})
be a basis of a supplementary subspace of
(A_{n_{i-1}}\cap D_{n_{i}})
in
D_{n_{i}}
, and defined
A_{n_{i}}
as the sub-
\mathbb{F}
-algebra of
A
generated by
(a_{1},...,a_{s_{i}})
.

This procedure eventually terminates by the Riemann-Roch Theorem, and we obtain a finite generating family
(a_{1},...,a_{s})
of
A
. We obtain a morphism

\mathbb{F}[t_{1},...,t_{s}]\longrightarrow A,\quad t_{i}\longmapsto a_{i}

(3.1)

and a new degree
d
given as
d(t_{i}):=\deg(a_{i})
on monomials of
\mathbb{F}[t_{1},...,t_{s}]
. By design, (
3.1
) bears the property that
\deg P(a_{1},...,a_{s})=d(P(t_{1},...,t_{s}))
for any
P(t_{1},...,t_{s})\in\mathbb{F}[t_{1},...,t_{s}]
.
We denote by
\mathfrak{a}
the kernel of (
3.1
), and we let
(f_{1}(t_{1},...,t_{s}),...,f_{t}(t_{1},...,t_{s}))
be a family of generators of
\mathfrak{a}
. We denote by
\mathfrak{a}_{c}\subset\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t_{1},...,t_{s}]
the
rescaled
ideal

\left(f_{1}\left(c^{\deg t_{1}}t_{1},\cdots,c^{\deg t_{s}}t_{s}\right),...,f_{g}\left(c^{\deg t_{1}}t_{1},\cdots,c^{\deg t_{1}}t_{s}\right)\right)\subset\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t_{1},...,t_{s}].

Proposition 3.2
.

Through the above presentation of
A
, the ring homomorphism

\iota:\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t_{1},...,t_{s}]/\mathfrak{a}_{c}\to A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty},\quad t_{i}\mapsto a_{i}\otimes c^{-\deg a_{i}}

is an isomorphism which makes the Gauss norm on the left-hand side coincide with
\|\cdot\|_{\rho}
on the right.

Remark 3.3
.

Recall that the Gauss norm on the quotient
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t_{1},...,t_{s}]/\mathfrak{a}_{c}
is defined as

f\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t_{1},...,t_{s}],\quad\|f\!\pmod{\mathfrak{a}_{c}}\|:=\inf_{a\in\mathfrak{a}_{c}}\|f-a\|

where
\|\cdot\|
is the classical Gauss norm on
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t_{1},...,t_{s}]
;
i.e.
given by the maximum norm of coefficients.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

That
\iota
is bijective is clear, so we prove the statement on the norms. Let
x\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t_{1},...,t_{s}]/\mathfrak{a}_{c}
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\tilde{x}=\sum{\alpha_{i_{1},...,i_{s}}t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t^{i_{s}}_{s}}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t_{1},...,t_{s}]
lifting
x
such that
\|x\|_{c}\geq\|\tilde{x}\|-\varepsilon
. Hence,

\displaystyle\|\iota(x)\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i_{1},...,i_{s}}{a_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots a_{s}^{i_{s}}\otimes\alpha_{i_{1},...,i_{s}}c^{-\deg(a_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots a_{s}^{i_{s}})}}\right\|_{\rho}\leq\max_{i_{1},...,i_{s}}|\alpha_{i_{1},...,i_{s}}|=\|\tilde{x}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|_{c}+\varepsilon.

Being true for all
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain
\|\iota(x)\|_{\rho}\leq\|x\|_{c}
. Conversely, let
\iota(x)\in A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. Similarly, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a representation of
\iota(x)
as
\sum_{i}{b_{i}\otimes c_{i}}\in A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
for which

\|\iota(x)\|_{\rho}\geq\max_{i}\{|c_{i}|c^{\deg b_{i}}\}-\varepsilon.

Let us write
b_{i}
as
P_{i}(a_{1},...,a_{s})
for a polynomial
P_{i}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}
. Then,

x=\sum_{i}{c_{i}P(c^{\deg a_{1}}t_{1},...,c^{\deg a_{s}}t_{s})}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t_{1},...,t_{s}]/\mathfrak{a}_{c}

and
\|x\|_{c}
is therefore bounded by
\max_{i}\{|c_{i}|\|P_{i}(c^{\deg a_{1}}t_{1},...,c^{\deg a_{s}}t_{s})\|\}
. Now, by design of the chosen presentation, one has

\|P_{i}(c^{\deg a_{1}}t_{1},...,c^{\deg a_{s}}t_{s})\|=|c|^{\deg P_{i}(a_{1},...,a_{s})},

and we conclude that
\|x\|_{c}\leq\|\iota(x)\|_{\rho}+\varepsilon
.
∎

In virtue of Proposition
3.2
, we deduce that
\|\cdot\|_{\rho}
is a norm, and we let
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho}
denote the completion of
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
with respect to it. Also from Proposition
3.2
,
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho}
is an affinoid
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-algebra, hence
\mathfrak{X}_{\rho}:=\operatorname{Sp}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho}
is a
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-affinoid variety. If
\rho<\rho^{\prime}
, there is a canonical ring homomorphism
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho}
, and it induces a morphism of affinoid varieties denoted
t_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}:\mathfrak{X}_{\rho}\to\mathfrak{X}_{\rho^{\prime}}
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be the rigid analytic variety over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
resulting as the glueing of
(\mathfrak{X}_{\rho})_{\rho}
along the morphisms
(t_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}})_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}
(for
\rho
,
\rho^{\prime}
running over
|\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\times}|
).

The next lemma is immediate (
e.g.

[
Bos14
, §5.3 Prop. 5]
).

Lemma 3.5
.

The family
(\mathfrak{X}_{\rho}\to\mathfrak{X})_{\rho}
is an admissible covering of
\mathfrak{X}
.

By definition, we have
\mathfrak{X}:=\varinjlim_{\rho}\mathfrak{X}_{\rho}
as ringed spaces. In particular, the ring of global sections of
\mathfrak{X}
is

\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle\!\langle A\rangle\!\rangle:=\varprojlim_{\rho}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho}.

For
f\in A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, observe that we have
\|f^{(1)}\|_{\rho}=\|f\|_{\rho^{1/q}}^{q}
, so that twisting induces an isomorphism of rings and spaces:

\tau:\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho^{1/q}},\quad\tau:\mathfrak{X}_{\rho^{1/q}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{X}_{\rho}.

(3.2)

Compiling these for all
\rho>0
, we obtain isomorphisms

\tau:\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle\!\langle A\rangle\!\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle\!\langle A\rangle\!\rangle,\quad\tau:\mathfrak{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{X}.

(3.3)

Remark 3.6
.

We refer to
[
Bos14
, §5.4]
for the fact that
\mathfrak{X}
is isomorphic to
(\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty})^{\operatorname{rig}}
as rigid analytic spaces.

Example 3.7
.

Let
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}
and let
\infty
be the point
[0:1]
. We identify
A
with
\mathbb{F}[t]
and
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
with the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]
. Denoting
\ell(t)
by
\theta
, we have
\mathfrak{j}=(t-\theta)
and, more generally,
\tau^{i}\mathfrak{j}=(t-\theta^{q^{i}})
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}t^{n}}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![t]\!]~\bigg|~\lim_{n\to\infty}|a_{n}|\rho^{n}\to 0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle\!\langle A\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle\cong\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}t^{n}}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![t]\!]~\bigg|~\forall\rho>0:~\lim_{n\to\infty}|a_{n}|\rho^{n}\to 0\right\},

where
\tau
acts as
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}t^{n}}\mapsto\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}^{q}t^{n}}

.

Sheaves and divisors on
\mathfrak{X}

Given a commutative ring
R
with unit, we denote by
\operatorname{Q}(R)
its total ring of fractions;
i.e.
the localization of
R
by the set of non-zero divisors. Given an admissible open
\mathfrak{U}
in
\mathfrak{X}
, we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U})
the total ring of fractions
\operatorname{Q}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U}))
. According to Bosch
[
Bos82
, §2]
, the assignment

\mathfrak{U}\longmapsto\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U})

is a sheaf on
\underline{\mathfrak{X}}
; i.e with respect to the strong Grothendieck topology. We call
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X}}
the sheaf of meromorphic functions. A
fractional ideal sheaf
on
\underline{\mathfrak{X}}
is an invertible sub-
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-module sheaf of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X}}
. Lacking references, we recall briefly the dictionary among fractional ideal sheaves and rigid analytic divisors.

For any
x\in|\mathfrak{X}|=|X|
, we have
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X},x}=\operatorname{Q}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X},x})
where
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X},x}
is a discrete valuation ring with residue field
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
which coincides with
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
. Given
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U})
, a non zero meromorphic function on an admissible open
\mathfrak{U}
, we define its divisor as follows. For
x\in\mathfrak{U}
, we let
\operatorname{ord}_{x}f
be the order of
f
in the discrete valued field
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X},x}
. The
divisor of
f
on
\mathfrak{U}
is the formal (possibly infinite) sum:

\operatorname{div}f:=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{U}}{(\operatorname{ord}_{x}f)\cdot x}.

Let
D=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{U}}{n_{x}\cdot x}
be a formal
\mathbb{Z}
-sum of points of
\mathfrak{X}
. We say that
D
is a
rigid analytic divisor
if there exists an admissible open cover
(\mathfrak{U}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{i}}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{U})_{i\in I}
such that, for each
i\in I
, there exists
f_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})
for which

D_{|\mathfrak{U}_{i}}:=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{U}_{i}}{n_{x}\cdot x}=\operatorname{div}f_{i}.

More generally, we call
divisor
on
\mathfrak{X}
any formal
\mathbb{Z}
-sum of points of
\mathfrak{X}
. To any divisor
D
on
\mathfrak{X}
, one associates a presheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(D)
given on an admissible open
\mathfrak{U}
as

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(D)(\mathfrak{U}):=\{g\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U})~|~\operatorname{div}g+D\geq 0\}.

Whenever
D
is rigid analytic,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(D)
is further a fractional ideal sheaf on
\underline{\mathfrak{X}}
. Conversely, to any fractional ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
, there is a rigid analytic divisor
D
such that
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(D)
. Observe in addition that given
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})
non zero, we have

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\operatorname{div}f)(\mathfrak{U})=\{g\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U})~|~g\cdot f_{|\mathfrak{U}}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U})\}.

Lemma
2.4
has the following counterpart:

Proposition 3.8
.

For
u\in A
a non constant element, let
\mathfrak{j}_{u}
denote the principal ideal
(u\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(u))
of
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. Also, consider the divisor
J:=\mathfrak{j}+\tau\mathfrak{j}+\tau^{2}\mathfrak{j}+...
and
J_{u}:=\mathfrak{j}_{u}+\tau\mathfrak{j}_{u}+\tau^{2}\mathfrak{j}_{u}+...
on
\mathfrak{X}
. Then, both
J
and
J_{u}
are rigid analytic and, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathfrak{U}
an affinoid subdomain of
\mathfrak{X}
, we have the equality:

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(n\cdot J)(\mathfrak{U})=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in A\\
\text{separating}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(n\cdot J_{u})(\mathfrak{U})

where the intersection is taken over separating elements
u\in A
. In particular,
\bigcap_{u}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(n\cdot J_{u})
is a sheaf.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\rho\in|\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\times}|
and let
t=\lfloor\log_{q}\rho\rfloor
. Let
a\in A
be non constant. Then
a\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(a)^{q^{j}}
is invertible in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho}
for all
j>t
. In particular,
\tau^{j}\mathfrak{j}
is invertible in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho}
for all
j>t
.

Proof.

It suffices to note that, for
j>t
, the series

\frac{-1}{1\otimes\ell(a)^{q^{j}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\left(\frac{a\otimes 1}{1\otimes\ell(a)^{q^{j}}}\right)^{n}}

converges in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle_{\rho}
to an inverse of
a\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(a)^{q^{j}}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{U}
be an affinoid subdomain of
\mathfrak{X}
. By Lemma
3.5
, there exists
\rho\in|\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\times}|
such that
\mathfrak{U}\to\mathfrak{X}
factors through
\mathfrak{X}_{\rho}\to\mathfrak{X}
. Let
t=\lfloor\log_{q}\rho\rfloor
.

On one hand, we have by Lemma
3.9
,

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(n\cdot J)(\mathfrak{U})=\{f\in\operatorname{Q}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U}))~|~\forall h\in(\mathfrak{j}\cdot\tau\mathfrak{j}\cdots\tau^{t}\mathfrak{j})^{n}:~hf\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U})\}.

(3.4)

To prove that
J
is rigid analytic, observe that the ideal
\mathfrak{j}\cdot\tau\mathfrak{j}\cdots\tau^{t}\mathfrak{j}
of

is principal on each admissible open. Hence,

is rigid analytic.

On the other hand,

\bigcap_{u}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(n\cdot J_{u})(\mathfrak{U})=\left\{f\in\operatorname{Q}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U}))~\bigg|~\forall u:\left(\prod_{j=0}^{t}{(u\otimes 1-1\otimes\ell(u)^{q^{j}})}\right)^{\!n}\!f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U})\right\}.

(3.5)

Hence

is also a rigid analytic divisor.

We now prove equality among (

) and (
3.5
). Clearly, the former is included in the latter. Conversely, let

be an element of (

be a maximal ideal in

but

arises from a maximal ideal of

(which we still denote by

). By assumption, for all

separating,

Since
\mathfrak{m}
is prime and

, there exists

Filtration on
\mathfrak{sf}(E)

As already mentioned in the introduction, the
A
-module
\mathfrak{sf}(E)
carries a natural increasing finite filtration indexed by the order of poles along the divisor
J
:

(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Cor~}\ref{cor:no-pole-no-sf}}}{{=}}\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}})(\mathfrak{X})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(J)(\mathfrak{X})\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(d\cdot J)(\mathfrak{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Thm~}\ref{thm:continuation-sf}}}{{=}}\mathfrak{sf}(E).

(5.7)

We conclude this text by giving a few examples of the above filtration built out from the Carlitz
t
-module. These examples show that the filtration steps can be quite arbitrary.

Carlitz tensor powers

Let
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}
and let
\infty
be the point
[0:1]
. We identify
A
with
\mathbb{F}[t]
and
A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
with the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]
. We also denote
\ell(t)
by
\theta
. Let
n>0
. The
n
th tensor power of the Carlitz
t
-module over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
is the unique
A
-module
\mathsf{C}^{\otimes n}
which equals
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
as an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space scheme, and for which

\varphi(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\theta&1&&\\
&\theta&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&1\\
\tau&&&\theta\end{pmatrix}\in\mathsf{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\{\tau\})\cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}-\text{vs}/\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}).

As sub-
\mathbb{F}[t]
-modules of
A\hat{\otimes}\mathsf{C}^{\otimes n}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle t\rangle^{n}
, we have

\mathfrak{sf}(\mathsf{C}^{\otimes n})=\mathbb{F}[t]\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1\\
(t-\theta)\\
\vdots\\
(t-\theta)^{n-1}\end{pmatrix}\omega(t)^{n},

where
\omega
is the Anderson-Thakur function as in (
1.2
). Since
\omega(t)^{n}
has a pole of order
n
at
\mathfrak{j}=(t-\theta)
, the filtration (
5.7
) has only one jump at the
n
th degree:

(0)=\mathfrak{sf}_{\mathsf{C}^{\otimes n}}((n-1)\cdot J)(\mathfrak{X})\subsetneq\mathfrak{sf}_{\mathsf{C}^{\otimes n}}(n\cdot J)(\mathfrak{X})=\mathfrak{sf}(\mathsf{C}^{\otimes n}).

Sum of Carlitz tensor powers

Let
E
be the product of
m
copies of
\mathsf{C}^{\otimes n}
. Then,
E
has dimension
mn
, and
\mathfrak{sf}(E)=\mathfrak{sf}(\mathsf{C}^{\otimes n})^{\oplus m}
. In particular,
the filtration (
5.7
) has only one jump at the
n
th degree:

(0)=\mathfrak{sf}_{E}((n-1)\cdot J)(\mathfrak{X})\subsetneq\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(n\cdot J)(\mathfrak{X})=\mathfrak{sf}_{E}(mn\cdot J)(\mathfrak{X})=\mathfrak{sf}(E).

Prolongations of the Carlitz module

For
k\geq 0
, let
\rho_{k}\mathsf{C}
be the
k
th prolongation of
\mathsf{C}
as defined in
[
Mau18b
]
. It is an Anderson
A
-module which equals
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k+1}
as an
\mathbb{F}
-module scheme. The module
\mathfrak{sf}(\rho_{k}\mathsf{C})
is free rank
k+1
over
\mathbb{F}[t]
, having the elements

\omega_{1}:=\begin{pmatrix}\omega(t)\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad\omega_{2}:=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}^{(1)}\omega(t)\\
\omega(t)\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix},~\cdots~,\omega_{k+1}:=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}^{(k)}\omega(t)\\
\partial_{t}^{(k-1)}\omega(t)\\

for basis (here

) has exactly

\mathfrak{sf}_{\rho_{k}\mathsf{C}}(d\cdot J)(\mathfrak{X})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{F}[t]\cdot\omega_{i}.

Summary

For appropriate choices of
n
,
m
, and
k

in these three cases, we obtain
t
-modules of dimension
d
with different filtration steps.

•

For the
d
th Carlitz tensor power
\mathsf{C}^{\otimes d}
, there is one step and this one is at the
d
th degree.

•

For the product of
d
copies of
\mathsf{C}
,
i.e.

\mathsf{C}^{\oplus d}
, there is also one step, but this time at degree
1
.

•

For the
(d-1)

Of course, these examples can easily be extended,
e.g.
to products of different tensor powers of the Carlitz module, to obtain even more variation in the steps.
But this is not in the scope of this manuscript.

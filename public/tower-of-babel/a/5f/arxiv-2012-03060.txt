Equivalent generating vectors of finitely generated modules over commutative rings
Luc Guyot

Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative ring with identity and let M M be an R R -module which is generated by μ \mu elements but not fewer. We denote by SL n ​ ( R ) \textnormal{SL}_{n}(R) the group of the n × n n\times n matrices over R R with determinant 1 1 . We denote by E n ​ ( R ) \textnormal{E}_{n}(R) the subgroup of SL n ​ ( R ) \textnormal{SL}_{n}(R) generated by the the matrices which differ from the identity by a single off-diagonal coefficient. Given n ≥ μ n\geq\mu and G ∈ { SL n ​ ( R ) , E n ​ ( R ) } G\in\left\{\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R),\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)\right\} , we study the action of G G by matrix right-multiplication on V n ⁡ ( M ) \operatorname{V}_{n}(M) , the set of elements of M n M^{n} whose components generate M M . Assuming that M M is finitely presented and that R R is an elementary divisor ring or an almost local-global coherent Prüfer ring, we obtain a description of V n ⁡ ( M ) / G \operatorname{V}_{n}(M)/G which extends the author’s earlier result on finitely generated modules over quasi-Euclidean rings [ Guy17 ] .

1.
Introduction

Rings are supposed unital and commutative.
The unit group of a ring
R
is denoted by
R^{\times}
. Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.
We denote by
\mu(M)
the minimal number of generators of
M
.
For
n\geq\mu(M)
, we denote by
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
the set of
generating vectors of
M
of length
n
, i.e.,
the set of
n
-tuples in
M^{n}
whose components generate
M
.
We consider the action of
\textnormal{GL}_{n}(R)
on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
by matrix right-multiplication.
Let
\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)
be the subgroup of
\textnormal{GL}_{n}(R)
of the matrices with determinant
1
.
Let
\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
be the subgroup of
\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)
generated by the
elementary matrices
, i.e.,
the matrices which differ from the identity by a single off-diagonal element.
Let
G
be a subgroup of
\textnormal{GL}_{n}(R)
. Two generating vectors
\mathbf{m}
,
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
are said to be
G
-equivalent
,
which we also denote by
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\sim_{G}\mathbf{m}
, if there exists
g\in G
such that

\mathbf{m}^{\prime}=\mathbf{m}g
.
Our chief concern is the description of the quotient
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)/G
with
G\in\left\{\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R),\textnormal{E}_{n}(R),\textnormal{GL}_{n}(R)\right\}
for
R
in a class of rings comprising the Dedekind rings.

Let us highlight earlier results pertaining to this topic.
When
M=R
, the elements of
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
are usually called the unimodular rows of size
n

and the orbit set
\textnormal{W}_{n}(R)\Doteq\operatorname{V}_{n}(R)/\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
has been extensively studied
[
Lam06
, Section VIII.5]
.
Suslin and Vaserstein
[
Bas75
, Théorème 4]

[
VS76
, Corollary 7.4]
discovered that
\textnormal{W}_{3}(R)
can be identified with
the elementary symplectic Witt group of
R
if
\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
acts transitively on

\operatorname{V}_{n}(R)
for every
n>3
.
In
[
vdK83
, Theorem 3.6]
, van der Kallen used the former structure inductively to define a structure of Abelian group on

\textnormal{W}_{n}(R)
when
R
has finite stable rank and
n
is large enough.
The interested reader is referred to
[
DTZ18
]
for the latest developments on the van der Kallen group structure of
\textnormal{W}_{n}(R)
for an affine domain
R
.
In the latter article, Das, Tikader and Zinna describe moreover the Abelian group structure of
\operatorname{V}_{n+1}(R)/\textnormal{SL}_{n+1}(R)
induced by
\textnormal{W}_{n+1}(R)
for

R
a smooth affine real algebra of dimension
n\geq 2

[
DTZ18
, Theorem 1.2]
; see also
[
Fas11
]
for seminal results of this flavor.

For
R=S[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
, the ring of polynomials in
m
indeterminates over a ring
S
,
Murthy determined conditions under which
\textnormal{GL}_{n}(R)
acts transitively on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(\sum_{k=1}^{r}Rx_{i})
for
r\leq n\leq m

[
Mur03
]
.

To introduce further known results, let us define the
determinant

\det(M)
of a finitely generated
R
-module

M
as the exterior product
\bigwedge^{\mu}M
where
\mu=\mu(M)
.
The module
\det(M)
is a cyclic
R
-module whose annihilator is
\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)
, the
\mu
-th Fitting ideal of
M
(see Section
2.1
for definition).
The
determinant map

\det:M^{n}\rightarrow\bigwedge^{n}M
is defined by
\det(m_{1},\dots,m_{\mu})=m_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge m_{n}
.
It is immediate to check that
\det(\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M))\subseteq\operatorname{V}_{1}(\det(M))
.
For
I
an ideal of a ring
R
, we showed that
\operatorname{V}_{2}(I)/\textnormal{SL}_{2}(R)

is equipotent with a subset of
\operatorname{V}_{1}(\det(I))

[
Guy20
, Theorem A]
if
I
can be generated by two elements which are not zero-divisors.
For
R
a quasi-Euclidean ring and
M
an arbitrary finitely generated
R
-module,
we proved that the determinant map induces a bijection from

\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)/\textnormal{E}_{\mu}(R)
onto
\operatorname{V}_{1}(\det(M))
and that
\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
acts
transitively on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
for every
n>\mu

[
Guy17
, Theorem A and Corollary B]
.
In this article we generalize
[
Guy17
, Theorem A and Corollary B]

to two classes of rings: the elementary divisor rings and the rings considered by Couchot in
[
Cou07
, Theorems 1 and 2]
.
For the time being, we denote by
\mathcal{D}
the union of these two classes. Their definitions are postponed to Section
2.3
.
The reader should only bear in mind that
\mathcal{D}
contains the Dedekind rings and some natural generalizations.

The rings in
\mathcal{D}
share three features which make them particularly amenable
to the study of the generating vectors of their finitely presented modules.
For any such ring
R
, the following hold (see Proposition
2.15
and Section
2.3
’s theorems):

(1)

Every finitely generated ideal of
R
can be generated by two elements.

(2)

The stable rank of
R
is at most
2
.

(3)

Finitely presented
R
-modules have an
invariant decomposition
.

Our first result relates
\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)
-orbits of generating vectors to the image of the determinant map.

Theorem A
.

Let
R
be a ring in
\mathcal{D}
.
Let
M
be a finitely presented
R
-module and set
\mu=\mu(M)
.
Then the determinant map induces a bijection from
\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)/\textnormal{SL}_{\mu}(R)
onto
\operatorname{V}_{1}(\det(M))
.
In addition, the group
\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)
acts transitively on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
for every
n>\mu
.

In order to generalize Theorem
A
to direct products with factors in
\mathcal{D}
, we introduce the following definition.

Definition B
.

A finitely generated
R
-module
M
is said to satisfy the property
\Delta_{\textnormal{SL}}
if the determinant map
induces a bijection from
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)/\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)
to
\operatorname{V}_{1}(\bigwedge^{n}M)
for every
n\geq\mu(M)
.
The ring
R
is said to satisfy the property
\Delta_{\textnormal{SL}}
if every finitely presented
R
-module satisfies
\Delta_{\textnormal{SL}}
.

We define likewise the property
\Delta_{\textnormal{E}}
for which an analog of Proposition
C

below holds (see Propositions
5.3
and
5.4
).

Proposition C

(Propositions
5.3
and
5.4
)
.

The two following hold.

(i)

Let
\{R_{x}\}_{x\in X}
be a family of rings satisfying
\Delta_{\textnormal{SL}}
and let
R=\prod_{x}R_{x}
.
Then
R
satisfies
\Delta_{\textnormal{SL}}
.

(ii)

Let
R
be a ring which satisfies
\Delta_{\textnormal{SL}}
. Let
\overline{R}
be a quotient of
R
such that the natural map
\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)\rightarrow\textnormal{SL}_{n}(\overline{R})

is surjective for every
n
. Then
\overline{R}
satisfies
\Delta_{\textnormal{SL}}
.

Proposition
C
implies that a direct product of rings,
each factor of which is in
\mathcal{D}
or is the quotient of a ring in
\mathcal{D}
, has property
\Delta_{\textnormal{SL}}
.

Let us now outline a class of rings
R
for which the action of
\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)
on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
is always transitive.
We say that a ring
R
has property
T_{\textnormal{SL}}
if for every finitely presented
R
-module
M
, the group
\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)
acts transitively on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
for every
n\geq\mu(M)
.
We define likewise the property
T_{G}
for
G\in\{\textnormal{E},\textnormal{GL}\}
.
If
R
satisfies
T_{\textnormal{SL}}
, then the only unit of
R
is
1
, its identity element.
Commutative rings with no units other than
1
have been studied by Cohn
[
Coh58
, Theorem 3]
.
For every Boolean ring
B
, both
B
and
B[x]
have no units other than
1
. Since
B
and
B[x]
are elementary divisor rings

[
Sho74
, Example 2]
, both enjoy property
T_{\textnormal{SL}}
by Theorem
A
.

Regarding the action of
\textnormal{GL}_{n}(R)
on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(R)
, we observe that it is essentially determined
by the action of
\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)
. Indeed, decomposing
\textnormal{GL}_{n}(R)\simeq\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)\rtimes R^{\times}
, we easily see that

\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)/\textnormal{GL}_{n}(R)=(R^{\times})^{n}\backslash\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)/\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)
where
(R^{\times})^{n}
acts on the left via componentwise multiplication.
If for instance an
R
-module
M
has property
\Delta_{\textnormal{SL}}
, then
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)/\textnormal{GL}_{n}(R)
identifies with

R^{\times}\backslash\operatorname{V}_{1}(\det(M))\simeq(R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M))^{\times}/(R^{\times}+\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)).

If a ring
R
has the property
T_{\textnormal{GL}}
, then obviously every surjective ring homomorphism
R\twoheadrightarrow\overline{R}

must induce a surjective homomorphism
R^{\times}\twoheadrightarrow(\overline{R})^{\times}

on unit groups. The latter condition is equivalent to say that
R
has stable rank
1
, see
[
EO67
, Lemma 6.1]
or
[
Guy20
, Proposition 4.2]
.
Suppose
R
has property
\Delta_{\textnormal{SL}}
, e.g.,
R
lies in
\mathcal{D}
.
Then it is also clear that
R
has property
T_{\textnormal{GL}}
if
R
has moreover stable rank
1
.
For instance, the ring in
[
Che17
, Example 7]
is a Jacobson principal ideal domain of stable rank
1
and thus enjoys property
T_{\textnormal{GL}}
.

Besides the action of a subgroup
G\subseteq\textnormal{GL}_{n}(R)
on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
, it is also natural to consider the action of

\textnormal{Aut}_{R}(M)
, the group of the
R
-automorphisms of
M
, acting diagonally on
M^{n}
from the left.
This action commutes with the action of
G
from the right, so that
\textnormal{Aut}_{R}(M)\times G
acts also on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
from the left.
The following is an immediate consequence of Theorem
A
and of the invariant decompositions of the modules at play
(see Theorems
2.9
and
2.12
below):

Corollary D
.

Let
R
be a ring in
\mathcal{D}
. Let
M
be a finitely presented
R
-module.
Then
\textnormal{Aut}_{R}(M)\times\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)
acts transitively on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
for every
n\geq\mu(M)
.

We finally consider the action of
\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
on generating vectors.
Evidently, Theorem
A
provides us with a description of
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)/\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
for
n\geq\mu(M)
if
\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)=\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
.
If the latter identity holds, the ring
R
is termed a
\textnormal{GE}_{n}
-ring
. If
R
is
\textnormal{GE}_{n}
ring for every
n\geq 2
, then
R
is termed a
GE
-ring
.
The class of
GE
-rings includes quasi-Euclidean rings, rings of stable rank
1
and
Dedekind domains of arithmetical type with infinitely many units
[
Vas72
]
.
By taking direct products of
GE
-rings, we can obtain non-
GE
-rings for
which
\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
can replace
\textnormal{SL}_{n}(R)
in Theorem
A
.
It is well-known that the class of
\textnormal{GE}_{n}
-ring is stable under the formation of direct products with finitely many factors
[
Coh66
, Theorem 3.1]
, but not for infinitely many ones.
A typical counter-example is
\prod_{x}\mathbb{Z}
where
x
ranges in an infinite set (use, e.g.,
[
Coh66
, Lemma 5.1]
). Although the latter ring is not
\textnormal{GE}_{2}
, it is a
\textnormal{GE}_{n}
-ring for every
n>2
.
This follows from
[
CK83
, Main Theorem]
which implies more generally that an arbitrary direct product of rings of integers or their localizations, is a
\textnormal{GE}_{n}
-ring for every
n>2
.
For such products, combining Theorem
A
with Proposition
C
yields a description of
\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)/\textnormal{E}_{\mu}(R)
provided that
\mu=\mu(M)>2
.

If
R
is not a
\textnormal{GE}_{n}
-ring, describing
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)/\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
is already challenging when we restrict to
M=R
and
n=2
. In this case,

\operatorname{V}_{2}(R)/\textnormal{E}_{2}(R)
identifies with
\textnormal{E}_{12}(R)\backslash\textnormal{SL}_{2}(R)/\textnormal{E}_{2}(R)
where

\textnormal{E}_{12}(R)
is the group of matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}1&r\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

with
r\in R
(see Lemma
4.4
).
If for instance
R
is the ring of integers of an imaginary quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
that is not
\textnormal{GE}_{2}
,
then it follows from
[
FF88
, Theorem 2.4]
together with
the Normal Form Theorem for Free Products with Amalgamation
[
LS77
, Theorem IV.2.6]
that
\operatorname{V}_{2}(R)/\textnormal{E}_{2}(R)
is infinite;
see
[
Nic11
, Theorem 1.5]
and
[
She16
]
for an elementary proof of this fact.
The quotient
\operatorname{V}_{2}(R)/\textnormal{E}_{2}(R)
clearly surjects on the quotient of
\textnormal{SL}_{2}(R)
by the subgroup of unipotent matrices. For infinitely
many
d>0
, this quotient surjects in turn on a non-Abelian free group
[
GMV94
, Proof of Theorem 1.2]
.
With our last result we depart from such intricate situations, assuming that
\mu(M)>2
and that
M
has a non-trivial torsion submodule.
Under these assumptions, we can describe
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)/\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
by means of the determinant map and the special Whitehead group
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R)
of
R

(see Section
5
for the definition of
\operatorname{SK}_{1}
).
Let us denote by
\tau(M)
the
torsion submodule
of
M
, that is,

\tau(M)\Doteq\{m\in M\,|\,rm=0\text{ for some }r\in R\text{ which is not a zero divisor}\}.

A ring
R
is a
Bézout ring
if its finitely generated ideals are principal.

Theorem E
.

Assume that
R
is a Bézout ring whose proper quotients have stable rank
1
or
that
R
is an almost local-global coherent Prüfer ring (see Section
2.3
for definitions).
Let
M
be a finitely presented
R
-module.
Let
e
be an idempotent of
R
such that
\mu(eM)=\mu(M)
and set
\mu^{\prime}=\mu(\tau(M))
,
\mu=\mu(M)
.
Then the following hold.

(i)

If
\mu^{\prime}>1
or
\mu=1
, then the determinant map induces a bijection from from
\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)/\textnormal{E}_{\mu}(R)
onto
\operatorname{V}_{1}(\det(M))
.

(ii)

If
\mu^{\prime}=1
and
\mu>2
, then
\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)/\textnormal{E}_{\mu}(R)
is equipotent with
\operatorname{V}_{1}(\det(M))\times\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R)
.

(iii)

If either
\mu^{\prime}\neq 0
, or
\mu^{\prime}=0
and
eM
surjects onto a non-principal invertible ideal of
Re
, then
\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
acts transitively on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
for every
n>\mu
.
This holds in particular if
R
is an almost local-global Prüfer domain and
M
is torsion-free but not free.

(iv)

The group
\textnormal{E}_{n}(R)
acts transitively on
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
for every
n>\mu+1
.

A Bézout ring
R
such that the proper quotients of
R/\mathcal{J}(R)
have stable rank
1
is an elementary divisor ring
[
McG08
, Proposition 3.5 and Theorem 3.7]
.
To the best of our knowledge, all known examples of elementary divisor rings satisfy this property (this is corroborated by
[
McG08
, Remark 4.7]
).
Combining Theorem
E
with Proposition
2.7
below, we gain thus insight on all such examples.

Final Remarks

The proof Theorem
A
hinges on the fact that ideals are two-generated for rings in
\mathcal{D}
(see specifically the results of Section

The class of Bass rings is another well-studied class of rings whose ideals are two-generated

and whose finitely generated torsion-free modules are well-understood
[

]
.
The Bass rings constitute therefore a class of choice to pursue
the study of equivalent generating vectors in the spirit of Theorems
A
and
E
.

2.
Notation and background

We assume throughout that
R
is a commutative unital ring and
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module.
We denote by
\mathcal{J}(R)
the
Jacobson radical
of
R
, that is, the intersection of the maximal ideals of
R
.
If
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal of
R
, we denote by
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
the localization of
R
at
\mathfrak{p}
.
Similarly, we denote by
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
its localization at
\mathfrak{p}
, which we identify with
M\otimes_{R}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Given an element
m
in
M
, we abuse notation in denoting also by
m
its image
m\otimes_{R}1\in M_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
If
M
and
N
are two submodules of a given
R
-module, we denote by
(M:N)
the ideal consisting of all elements
r\in R
such that
rN\subseteq M
.
We define the
annihilator
\operatorname{ann}_{R}(M)
of
M
as
(0:M)
.
If
Rm
and
Rm^{\prime}
are two cyclic submodules of
M
, we simply write
(m:m^{\prime})
for
(Rm:Rm^{\prime})
.

Regular elements

An element
r
of a ring
R
is a
zero divisor
if
\operatorname{ann}_{R}(r)\Doteq(0:r)\neq\{0\}
. An element of
R
is
regular
if it is not a zero divisor.
An ideal of
R
is
regular
if it contains a regular element.
The
total ring of quotients
K(R)
of
R
is the localization
R[S^{-1}]
where
S
is the set of regular elements of
R
.
The above definition of a regular element should not be confused with the definition of a
von Neumann regular element
of
R
, that is an element for which there exists

a\in R
satisfying
r=ara
. A ring whose elements are von Neumann regular is called a
von Neumann regular ring
.

Matrices over
R
and
M

Given two
1\times n
row vectors
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in R^{n}
and
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in M^{n}
,
we denote by
\mathbf{r}^{\top}
and
\mathbf{m}^{\top}
the
n\times 1
column vectors obtained by transposition and
we define their product
\mathbf{r}\mathbf{m}^{\top}=\mathbf{m}\mathbf{r}^{\top}\Doteq\sum_{i}r_{i}m_{i}
.
Based on these identities, the product of any two matrices over
R
and
M
, with compatible numbers of rows and columns, is uniquely defined.
We denote respectively by
0_{m\times n}
and by
{1_{n}}
the
m\times n
zero matrix and the
n\times n
identity matrix over
R
.

2.1.
Fitting ideals and the determinant module

The invariant on which our study of the action of
\textnormal{SL}_{\mu}(R)
on
\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)
hinges is the determinant map.
This map can be effectively computed thanks to the
\mu
th-Fitting ideal.

Fitting ideals

Let

F\xrightarrow{\varphi}G\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

be an exact sequence where
F
and
G
are free
R
-modules and
G
is finitely generated. Let
I_{i}(\varphi)

the ideal of
R
generated by the
i\times i
minors of the matrix of
\varphi:F\rightarrow G
, agreeing that
I_{0}(\varphi)=R
.
Then the
(i+1)
-th Fitting ideal of
M
is
\operatorname{Fitt}_{i}(M)\Doteq I_{\mu(G)-i}(\varphi)
.
The Fitting ideals are independent of
\varphi
by Fitting’s lemma
[
Eis95
, Corollary 20.4]
.
They commute with
base change
, i.e,
\operatorname{Fitt}_{i}(M\otimes_{R}S)=\operatorname{Fitt}_{i}(M)\otimes_{R}S
for any
R
-algebra
S

[
Eis95
, Corollary 20.5]
, and hence with localization.

The determinant of a finitely generated module

Let
\mu=\mu(M)
.
Recall that the determinant
\det(M)
of
M
is the exterior product
\bigwedge^{\mu}M
.
This is a cyclic
R
-module whose annihilator is
\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)

[
Eis95
, Exercise 20.9.i]
.
Given
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{\mu})\in\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)
, we denote by
\phi_{\mathbf{m}}:\det(M)\rightarrow R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)

the isomorphism induced by the map
m_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge m_{\mu}\rightarrow 1+\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)
.
Given another generating pair
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}=(m_{1}^{\prime},\dots,m_{\mu}^{\prime})\in M^{\mu}
, we define

\text{det}_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{m}^{\prime})\Doteq\phi_{\mathbf{m}}(m_{1}^{\prime}\wedge\dots\wedge m_{\mu}^{\prime}).

It is easy to check that
\det_{\mathbf{m}}(\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M))
is a subgroup of
(R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M))^{\times}

2.3.
Elementary divisor rings and coherent Prüfer rings

In this section, we underscore the properties of the rings in
\mathcal{D}
which
are instrumental in the proofs of Theorems
A
and
E
. We detail in particular the structure of finitely presented modules over these rings
by stating the corresponding invariant decomposition theorems.
Complete definitions are provided and some remarkable examples and results are briefly outlined.
Recall that the class
\mathcal{D}
is the union of two classes: the elementary divisor rings and the almost local-global coherent Prüfer rings
; the latter are the almost local-global semihereditary rings of Couchot
[
Cou07
]
.

A ring
R
is an
elementary divisor ring
if every matrix over
R
with finitely many rows and columns
admits a Smith Normal Form, that is, for every
m\times n
matrix
A
over
R
, we can find

B\in\textnormal{GL}_{m}(R),C\in\textnormal{GL}_{n}(R)
such that
BAC
is a diagonal matrix whose diagonal coefficients
d_{1},\dots,d_{k}

satisfy the divisibility condition
d_{i+1}\,|\,d_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq k-1
.

The class of elementary divisor rings is easily seen to be stable under the formation of direct products and quotients.
An elementary divisor ring is clearly a K-Hermite ring (see definition right before Proposition
2.8
), hence a Hermite ring.
A finitely presented module over an elementary divisor ring decomposes into a direct sum of finitely many cyclic modules whose annihilator forms a descending chain.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
Kap49
, Theorem 9.1]

Let
R
be an elementary divisor ring. Then
R
satisfies the Invariant Factor Theorem,
that is, every finitely presented
R
-module
M
decomposes as

R/\mathfrak{a}_{1}\times\cdots\times R/\mathfrak{a}_{k}\,(k\geq 0)

where the ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{i}\subseteq R
form a descending chain
R\supsetneq\mathfrak{a}_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak{a}_{k}
.

Conversely, a ring whose finitely presented modules have a decomposition into a direct sum of cyclic modules is an elementary divisor rings
[
LLS74
, Theorem 3.8]
.

Examples 2.10
.

Principal ideal rings, i.e., rings whose ideals are principal, are elementary divisor rings
[
Kap49
, Theorem 12.3]
.
The ring of entire functions and the ring of algebraic integers are two emblematic non-Noetherian elementary divisor domains
[
DB72
, Examples 1 and 2 of Section 4]
.
A Boolean ring and more generally a von Neumann ring
R

[
GH56
, Remark 12]
as well as
R[X]

[
Sho74
, Example 2]
are elementary divisor rings.

We turn now to the definition of an
almost local-global coherent Prüfer ring
.

A ring is
reduced
if it has no non-zero nilpotent element.
A ring is
arithmetic
if its finitely generated ideals are locally principal.
A
Prüfer
ring in the sense of Hermida and Sànchez-Giralda
[
HSG86
, Definition 4]
is a reduced arithmetical ring
[
LQ15
, Proposition and definition VIII.4.4]
.
A ring is
coherent
if its finitely generated ideals are finitely presented.
A Prüfer coherent ring is characterized by the property that its finitely generated ideals are projective
[
LQ15
, Theorem XII.4.1]
.
A Prüfer coherent ring is often called a
semihereditary
ring, a terminology we shall discontinue (following Lombardi and Quitté
[
LQ15
]
) as we find it not suggestive enough.

A ring
R
is
local-global
, or

for short, if every (possibly multivariate) polynomial over
R
whose values generate
R
,
represents a unit.
The LG rings
[
MW81
,
EG82
]
are a natural generalization of the rings satisfying
van der Kallen’s primitive criterion
[
vdK77
, Definition 1.10]
, see
[
MW81
, Proposition on page 456]
.
Let
f(X)=aX+b
with
(a,b)\in\operatorname{V}_{2}(R)
.
Clearly, the ideal generated by the values
f(R)
of

is
R
. If

is LG, then there is

such that

is a unit. Therefore LG rings have stable rank
1
.
A ring
R
is
almost local-global
in the sense of Couchot
[
Cou07
]
, or

.

Examples 2.11
.

Semi-local rings and rings of Krull dimension

[

The Nagata ring

A

Dedekind domains are one-dimensional

The classical structure theorem of Steinitz for finitely generated modules over Dedekind rings

.

[

Let

where

are invertible ideals of

.

Using Examples

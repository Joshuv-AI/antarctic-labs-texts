Mc’Kean’s transformation for 3-rd order operators
Andrey Badanin, Evgeny L. Korotyaev

Abstract
. We consider a non-self-adjoint third order operator ( y ′′ + p ​ y ) ′ + p ​ y ′ + q ​ y (y^{\prime\prime}+py)^{\prime}+py^{\prime}+qy with 1-periodic coefficients p , q p,q . This operator is the L-operator in the Lax pair for the good Boussinesq equation on the circle. In 1981, McKean introduced a transformation that reduces the spectral problem for this operator to a spectral problem for the Hill operator with a potential that depends analytically on the energy. In the present paper we are studying this transformation.

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Introduction

We consider a non-self-adjoint operator
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{L}}(\psi)
acting on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and
given by

{\mathcal{L}}y=(y^{\prime\prime}+py)^{\prime}+py^{\prime}+qy,

(1.1)

where the 1-periodic coefficients
p,q
satisfy

\psi=(p,q)\in{\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}},

the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}=L_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}({\mathbb{T}}),{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, equipped
with the norm
\|f\|^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}|f(x)|^{2}dx
.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
be the complexification of the real space
{\mathcal{H}}
.
The operator is defined on the domain

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Dom}}\nolimits({\mathcal{L}})=\Big\{y\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}):(y^{\prime\prime}+py)^{\prime}+py^{\prime}+qy\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),y^{\prime\prime},(y^{\prime\prime}+py)^{\prime}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\Big\}.

(1.2)

The operator
{\mathcal{L}}
is the
L
-operator in the Lax pair
for the good Boussinesq equation on the circle.
The inverse problem for this operator was studied by McKean’s
[
McK81
]
.
The solution of the inverse problem uses a transformation that reduces the operator
{\mathcal{L}}

to the Schrodinger operator with a periodic energy-dependent potential.
Studying this transformation is the main goal of the present work.

Recall that in order to recover the potential of the Schrödinger operator
with a periodic potential, three sequences are required:

1) Sequence of eigenvalues of a 2-periodic problem.

2) Sequence of eigenvalues of the Dirichlet problem.

3) Sequence of +/- signs.

We showed in
[
BK24
]
that in order to recover the coefficients of the operator
{\mathcal{L}}
,
three sequences are also required:

1) Sequence of branch points of the Riemann surface of the operator.

2) Sequence of eigenvalues of the 3-point Dirichlet problem.

3) Sequence of +/- signs.

We prove in the present paper that in the case of small coefficients
McKean’s transformation establishes
a correspondence between these three sequences for the operator
{\mathcal{L}}

and the Schrödinger operator with an energy-dependent potential.

Note that since our operator is non-self-adjoint, the branch points
of the Riemann surface and the spectrum of the 3-point problem
can be non-real. The spectra of the 2-periodic problem and
the Dirichlet problem for the Schrodinger operator
with an energy-dependent potential can also be complex.
In our article
[
BK21
]
we proved that in the case
of a sufficiently small potential the spectrum of the Schrödinger
operator is real. Using this fact, in the present paper we prove
that for small coefficients the branch points of the Riemann surface
and the spectrum of the 3-point problem of the operator
{\mathcal{L}}
are real.

The inverse spectral problem for the Schrödinger operator with the periodic
potential in the class of potentials from
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})

is proved by Marchenko and Ostrovskii
[
MO75
]
and
Korotyaev
[
K99
]
.
Some inverse results for the 3-order operator
{\mathcal{L}}
are obtained by
McKean
[
McK81
]
. There, for the case of small smooth coefficients,
a uniqueness theorem
for recovering the potential from spectral data was proved.
The problem in the certain classes of non-smooth coefficients
is more difficult. Some results in this direction are presented in our article

[
BK24
]
.

1.3.
McKean’s transformation

Consider the differential equation

(y^{\prime\prime}+py)^{\prime}+py^{\prime}+qy=\lambda y,\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}},

(1.8)

in the class

C^{[2]}({\mathbb{R}})=\{y:y,y^{\prime},y^{[2]}\in AC_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})\},

where

y^{[2]}=y^{\prime\prime}+py.

(1.9)

McKean’s transformation is based on the following simple result.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
p,q\in L_{loc}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Assume that Eq. (
1.8
)
has a solution
\eta\in C^{[2]}({\mathbb{R}})

such that
\eta(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let, in addition,
y\in C^{[2]}({\mathbb{R}})
be a solution to Eq. (
1.8
).
Let the function
f
has the form

f=\eta^{3\over 2}\Big({y\over\eta}\Big)^{\prime}={y^{\prime}\eta-y\eta^{\prime}\over\sqrt{\eta}}.

(1.10)

Then
f,f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}+{p\over 2}f\in AC_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
f
satisfies the equation

-f^{\prime\prime}+Vf=Ef,

(1.11)

where

E={3z^{2}\over 4}={3\lambda^{2\over 3}\over 4},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda=\Big({4\over 3}E\Big)^{3\over 2},\hskip 10.00002ptz=\lambda^{1\over 3},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathop{\mathrm{arg}}\nolimits z\in(-{\pi\over 3},{\pi\over 3}],\hskip 10.00002pt\mathop{\mathrm{arg}}\nolimits\lambda\in(-\pi,\pi],

(1.12)

the energy-dependent potential

V
has the form

V=E-{p\over 2}-{3\over 2}{\eta^{[2]}\over\eta}+{3\over 4}\Big({\eta^{\prime}\over\eta}\Big)^{2}.

(1.13)

Moreover,
V+{p\over 2}\in AC_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Remark.

1) In the unperturbed case
\psi=0
we have
V=0
.

2) If the coefficients
p,q
are 1-periodic and
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}
, then
the potential
V
is also 1-periodic with respect to the variable
x
,
and
V+{p\over 2}\in AC({\mathbb{T}})
.

3) Eq. (
1.11
) was the subject of our article
[
BK21
]
.

1.4.
Ramifications

Now we consider the periodic coefficients.
Let
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}
.
Introduce the
fundamental solutions

\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3}
of Eq. (
1.8
)
satisfying the conditions
\varphi_{j}^{[k-1]}|_{x=0}=\delta_{jk}
,
j,k=1,2,3
,
where
y^{[0]}=y
,
y^{[1]}=y^{\prime}
,
y^{[2]}=y^{\prime\prime}+py
.
Each of the functions
\varphi_{j}(x,\cdot),j=1,2,3,x\in[0,1]
is entire
and real on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
We define
the
monodromy matrix
by

M(\lambda)=\big(\varphi_{j}^{[k-1]}(1,\lambda)\big)_{j,k=1}^{3}.

(1.14)

The matrix-valued function
M
is entire.
The characteristic polynomial
D
of the monodromy matrix is given by

D(\tau,\lambda)=\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits(M(\lambda)-\tau 1\!\!1_{3}),\hskip 10.00002pt(\tau,\lambda)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2},

(1.15)

here and below
1\!\!1_{N}
is the
N\times N
identity matrix.
An eigenvalue of
M
is called a
multiplier
, it is a
zero of the polynomial
D(\cdot,\lambda)
.
Each
M
has exactly
3
(counting with
multiplicities) multipliers
\tau_{j},j=1,2,3
, which satisfy

\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\tau_{3}=1.

In particular, each
\tau_{j}\neq 0
for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

Introduce the Floquet solutions
\mathfrak{f}_{k},k=1,2,3
,
of Eq. (
1.8
) satisfying the conditions

\mathfrak{f}_{k}(x+1,\lambda)=\tau_{k}(\lambda)\mathfrak{f}_{k}(x,\lambda),\hskip 20.00003pt\mathfrak{f}_{k}(0,\lambda)=1.

(1.16)

Three roots
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
and
\tau_{3}
of the equation
D(\cdot,\lambda)=0

constitute three distinct branches of some analytic function
\tau

of the variable
\lambda

that has only algebraic singularities in
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Thus the function
\tau
is
analytic on a 3-sheeted Riemann surface
{\mathcal{R}}
, Fig.
3
c),
see
[
BK24xxx
]
.
Moreover, for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}

the functions
\mathfrak{f}_{1}(x,\cdot),\mathfrak{f}_{2}(x,\cdot),\mathfrak{f}_{3}(x,\cdot)

constitute three branches of a function
meromorphic on the surface
{\mathcal{R}}
.

There are only a finite number of the algebraic singularities
(ramifications) in
any bounded domain. In order to describe these points we
introduce
the
discriminant

\rho

of the polynomial

D(\cdot,\lambda)
by

\rho=(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})^{2}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{3})^{2}(\tau_{2}-\tau_{3})^{2}.

(1.17)

The function
\rho(\lambda)
is entire.
A zero of
\rho
is a
ramification

of the Riemann surface
{\mathcal{R}}
.

If
\psi=0
, then the function
\rho
and its zeros
r_{n}^{0,\pm}

have the form

\rho_{0}=64\sin^{2}{\sqrt{3}z\over 2}\sin^{2}{\sqrt{3}\omega z\over 2}\sin^{2}{\sqrt{3}\omega^{2}z\over 2},\hskip 10.00002ptr_{n}^{0,+}=r_{n}^{0,-}=\Big({2\pi n\over\sqrt{3}}\Big)^{3},\ \ n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\hskip 10.00002pt\omega=e^{i{2\pi\over 3}}.

(1.18)

If the coefficients
p,q
are 1-periodic and
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}
, then
the potential
V
, given by (
1.13
), is also 1-periodic with respect to the variable
x
,
and
V+{p\over 2}\in AC({\mathbb{T}})
.
Consider the 2-periodic problem

f(2)=f(0),\hskip 20.00003ptf^{\prime}(2)=f^{\prime}(0),

(1.19)

for Eq. (
1.11
). Let each of the potentials
V(x,\cdot),x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, be analytic on
some neighborhood of
E_{o}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and let
the problem (
1.11
), (
1.19
) with
E=E_{o}
has a non-trivial solution.
Then
E_{o}
is an eigenvalue of this problem, see
[
BK21
]
.

We prove the following result about the ramification.

1.6.
Localization of the ramifications

Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
prove
that McKean’s transformation introduced in Lemma
1.1

transforms the ramification to the eigenvalue of the 2-periodic problem,
and the 3-point eigenvalue to the eigenvalue of the Dirichlet problem
for the Schrödinger operator.
Next we consider the case of small coefficients.
In this case, we prove a global one-to-one correspondence
between the set of positive (excluding
r_{0}^{\pm}

and the set of 2-periodic eigenvalues of the Schrödinger operator,
and a similar correspondence between the set of positive 3-point eigenvalues

ramifications (except

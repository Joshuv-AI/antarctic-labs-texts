Quadratic Forms, K K -groups and L L -values of elliptic curves
Li-Tong Deng, Yong-Xiong Li and Shuai Zhai

Abstract
. Let f f be a positive definite integral quadratic form in d d variables. In the present paper, we establish a direct link between the genus representation number of f f and the order of higher even K K -groups of the ring of integers of real quadratic fields, provided f f is diagonal and d ≡ 1 ​ mod ​ 4 d\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4 , by applying the Siegel mass formula. When d = 3 d=3 , we derive an explicit formula of r f ​ ( n ) r_{f}(n) in terms of the class number of the corresponding imaginary quadratic field and the central algebraic values of L L -functions of quadratic twists of elliptic curves, by exploring a theorem of Waldspurger. Moreover, by the 2 2 -divisibility results on the algebraic L L -values of quadratic twist of elliptic curves, we obtain a lower bound for the 2 2 -adic valuation of r f ​ ( n ) r_{f}(n) for some odd integer n n . The numerical results show our lower bound is optimal for certain cases. We also apply our main result to the quadratic form f = x 1 2 + ⋯ + x d 2 f=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x^{2}_{d} to determine the order of the higher K K -groups numerically.

1.
Introduction

Let
f=f(x_{1},...,x_{d})
be a positive definite integral quadratic form in
d\geq 2
variables. Within the arithmetic theory of quadratic forms, a fundamental challenge is to determine the possible values that the form
f
can take on when its variables
x_{1},...,x_{d}
are integers. Furthermore, a key objective is to establish the number of representations of a positive integer
n
by
f
, denoted as
r_{f}(n)
and defined by the expression:

r_{f}(n)=\#\{(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:f(x_{1},...,x_{d})=n\}.

To discover a precise and relatively straightforward formula for
r_{f}(n)
has consistently motivated the historical exploration of quadratic forms. In certain special cases, we can explicitly calculate
r_{f}(n)
, such as the well-known results of Gauss and Dirichlet when
d=2
. However, in general, providing a formula for a single
r_{f}(n)
is challenging. Perhaps the most notable general result in this context is Siegel’s well-known result, recognised as the Siegel mass formula, which links the genus representation number of
f
to the
L
-value of certain quadratic field. While in this paper, we will explore the link between the genus representation number involving
r_{f}(n)
and
K
-groups; and the connection among
r_{f}(n)
,
L
-values of elliptic curves and class numbers.

Throughout the paper, we assume that

f(x_{1},...,x_{d})=b_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+b_{d}x_{d}^{2}

is a diagonal quadratic form with
d
odd satisfying
b_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}

(1\leq i\leq d)
and
\gcd(b_{1},\cdots,b_{d})=1
. The matrix associated to
f
is defined by the diagonal matrix

(1.1)

A_{f}:=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right)_{d\times d}=2\cdot{\rm diag}\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{d}\}.

Denote the group of automorphisms of
f
by

\mathcal{O}(f)=\{\alpha\in GL_{d}(\mathbb{Z}):\alpha^{t}A_{f}\alpha=A_{f}\}.

It is clearly that
\mathcal{O}(f)
is finite, and set
\mu_{f}=|{\mathcal{O}}(f)|
. Here for a finite set
{\mathfrak{W}}
, we denote by
|{\mathfrak{W}}|
the cardinality of
{\mathfrak{W}}
. Let
\mathfrak{G}_{f}
be the genus of
f
. It is well known that
\mathfrak{G}_{f}
is a finite set with cardinality
h_{f}
, which we also call the class number of
f
. For each
h\in{\mathfrak{G}}_{f}
, denote

(1.2)

\xi_{h}=\left(\mu_{h}\sum\limits_{g\in\mathfrak{G}_{f}}\frac{1}{\mu_{g}}\right)^{-1}.

We then define the genus representation number of
f
to be

G_{f}(n)=\sum\limits_{g\in\mathfrak{G}_{f}}\xi_{g}r_{g}(n).

For each prime
p
(including the infinite prime
p=\infty
), we can view the equation

f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})=n

as a map from
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}_{p}
to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
(when
p=\infty
we view the map from
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to
{\mathbb{R}}
). Taking a Haar measure on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, we obtain a Haar measure on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}_{p}
. The density
\delta_{p}(n,f)
of solutions
f(x)=n
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is defined to be

(1.3)

\delta_{p}(n,f)=\lim_{U\to n}\frac{vol(f^{-1}(U))}{vol(U)},

where
U
runs through the neighborhoods of
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. One can show that when
U
is small enough, the ratio is stable (see
[
4
]
). Define
\varepsilon_{2}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\varepsilon_{d}=1
if
d>2
. One has the following mass formula of Siegel.

Theorem 1.1

(Siegel,
[
4
]
)
.

We have

(1.4)

G_{f}(n)=\varepsilon_{d}\prod\limits_{p\leq\infty}\delta_{p}(n,f).

Siegel’s formula gives a connection between coefficients of weighted average of theta series and the product of local representation densities of positive definite quadratic forms over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. The above formula is a special case of the Siegel–Weil formula, which establishes an identity between theta integrals and special values of Siegel Eisenstein series.We will apply the above formula to establish a direct link between the genus representation number
G_{f}
and the order of higher even
K
-groups of the ring of integers of real quadratic fields.

Main results on higher even

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that

. Then

The rational number

can be explicitly determined in (
3.4
) and has finitely many possibilities (depending on

we can find a finite set

We remark

is also zero.

Example 1.3
.

Let

-groups by studying the representation number of quadratic forms, and conversely, using the theory of

-groups to study quadratic forms. In the final section of the paper, we take

By using the formula (

for

.

Recall that

. For a positive integer

the class number of the imaginary quadratic field

.
Using the same method in the proof of Theorem

, when

to the class number of certain imaginary quadratic fields.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let

with
\gcd(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=1
. For any positive square free integer

1.2.
Main results on quadratic twists of elliptic curves

Recall that the theta series related to
f
is defined by

\theta_{f}=\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q^{f(x)}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}r_{f}(n)q^{n}.

If
g
lies in the same genus of
f
, then a classical result by Eichler
[
3
]
states that
\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}
is a cusp form of weight
\frac{d}{2}
. Moreover, if
d
is odd and
\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}
is an eigenform, by Shimura lift, the coefficients of
\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}
is related to the coefficients of some modular forms of integral weight. We define the class number
h_{f}
of
f
to be the number of the genus of
f
.

We assume that
d=3
and
h_{f}=2
. Thus we can write
{\mathfrak{G}}_{f}=\{f,g\}
. Recall that
A_{f}
is the matrix associated to
f
, we define the level of
f
to be the smallest positive integer
N=N_{f}
such that
NA^{-1}_{f}
has integral coefficients and even diagonal entries. It easily follows that
N
is divisible by
4
in view of (
1.1
). Recall that
D=b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}
. By a theorem of Eichler
[
3
]
,
\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}
is an eigen cusp form with weight
\frac{3}{2}
, level
N
and character
\chi
given by the Jacobian symbol
\left(\frac{4D}{\cdot}\right)
(see
[
10
]
). Namely,
\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}\in S_{\frac{3}{2}}(N,\chi)
. Let us write

\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}a_{n}q^{n}.

By a fundamental theorem of Shimura
[
11
]
and a remark made by Niwa
[
6
]
,
\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}
corresponds to a modular form of weight
2
, level
\frac{N}{2}
and character
\chi^{2}

\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}
. By the modularity theorem due to Wiles et. al., we put the following assumption:

—

(
Sh-E
) The Shimura lift of
\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}
corresponds to an elliptic curve
{\mathcal{E}}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

For an elliptic curve
{\mathcal{A}}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}

and a square free integer

the quadratic twist of

by the extension

Let

be the complex

{\mathcal{A}}^{(m)}

\Omega_{{\mathcal{A}}^{(m)}}=\Omega^{+}_{{\mathcal{A}}^{(m)}}

A well-known theorem of Birch shows that

is always a rational number. For each finite prime
\ell
, we denote by

the Tamagawa number of
{\mathcal{A}}
at
\ell
. For any square free integer

and
(M,C)=1
, we define

t_{\mathcal{A}}(M)=\sum_{\ell\mid M}t(\ell),

where

(respectively,

To state our main result, we have to introduce some notations. For each prime

be the field of

\xi_{g}=\frac{\mu_{f}}{\mu_{f}+\mu_{g}}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\xi_{f}=1-\xi_{g},

for

.

1.3.
Idea for the proofs.

The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.4
is Siegel’s mass formula, which says the genus representation number
G_{f}(n)
is a product of the local representation densities over all primes. We then make a very delicate study on the diagonal equations over finite fields, based on this, we determine the local representation densities up to a finite set of primes. As a result, the number
G_{f}(n)
is related to the Dirichlet
L
-values at non-positive integers. Since the Iwasawa main conjecture holds for abelian extensions over
{\mathbb{Q}}
for all primes, we know the Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture holds. Thus Theorem
1.2
follows.

For the proof of Theorem
1.5
, besides the above Siegel’s formula, we also need two more key ingredients. The first is a special value formula of Waldspurger. Under the condition (
Sh-E
), we know
\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}=\sum_{n}a_{n}q^{n}
corresponds to elliptic curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}
via Shimura lift. Then Waldspurger formula gives a precise relation between the
L
-values of elliptic curves with the coefficients of
\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}
. Thus, under the condition (
In-Nv
), we can relate
r_{f}(n)
to the algebraic
L
-values of quadratic twist of elliptic curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Here comes our second key input: by an induction argument by using modular symbols, one of us
[
19
]
proved a very general lower bound for the
2
-adic valuations for the algebraic
L
-values of the quadratic twist of optimal elliptic curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Based on this bound, we get a
2
-adic lower bound for
r_{f}(n)
and prove Theorem
1.5
.

In the final part of the introduction, we give the structure of the paper. In the next section §
2
, we determinate the local representation densities in the mass formula of Siegel. In section §
3
, we use Siegel’s formula to prove Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.4
. In section §
4
, we give the definition of Shimura lift and the formula of Waldspurger, then we prove Theorem
1.5
using the
2
-adic divisibility result. In section §
5
, we give several numerical examples related to Theorem
1.5
, in particular, some numerical data shows our lower bound is optimal. In the final section §
6
, we apply the formula in Theorem
1.2
to the quadratic form
f=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x^{2}_{d}
with
d=5,9,13
, we then get numerical data for the
K
-groups
K_{m}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
for
m=2,6,10
.

2.
Local representation densities

In this section, we determinate the local representation densities in the mass formula of Siegel. We now fix a diagonal quadratic form

f=b_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+b_{d}x_{d}^{2}.

Besides Lemma
2.4
, we always assume
d
is an odd positive integer. When
d=3
, we may also write
f
as
f=b_{1}x^{2}+b_{2}y^{2}+b_{3}z^{2}
. We set
D=b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{d}
. We remark that the level
N_{f}
is always divisible by
4
. Recall that the density
\delta_{p}(n,f)
of solutions
f(x)=n
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is defined to be

\delta_{p}(n,f)=\lim_{U\to n}\frac{vol(f^{-1}(U))}{vol(U)},

where
U
runs through the neighborhoods of

.

We first deal with the finite prime
p
, in this case,
there exists an integer
\beta_{p}(n,f)
such that for any integer
\beta\geq\beta_{p}(n,f)
,

(2.1)

\delta_{p}(n,f)=p^{-\beta(d-1)}\cdot|\{x\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{\beta}{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}:f(x)\equiv n\ (mod\ p^{\beta})\}|.

We first determine
\beta_{p}(n,f)
using the following version of Hensel’s Lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(Hensel)
.

Let
F(x)
be a polynomial with integer coefficients. If
r
is an integer such that
F(r)\equiv 0\ (mod\ p^{k})
and
F(r)\not\equiv 0\ (mod\ p^{k+1})
for some positive integer
k
, and
F^{\prime}(r)\equiv 0\ (mod\ p^{\ell})
,
F^{\prime}(r)\not\equiv 0\ (mod\ p^{\ell+1})
for some integer
\ell<\frac{k}{2}
, then for every integer
\tau>k-\ell
, there is a unique integer
s_{\tau}
such that

f(s_{\tau})\equiv 0\ (mod\ p^{\tau})
with
s_{\tau}\equiv r\ (mod\ p^{k-\ell})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
n
be a square free positive integer which is coprime to
2D
. Then we can take

\beta_{p}(n,f)=\begin{cases}1,\quad&\textrm{if $p\nmid 2nD$}\\
2,\quad&\textrm{if $p\mid n$.}\end{cases}

Assume that
d=3
,
\gcd(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=1
and
n
is coprime to
2D
, we can take
\beta_{2}(n,f)=3
.

Proof.

For the first assertion, we only prove the case when
p\nmid 2nD
, the other case is similar. Note the equation

(2.2)

b_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+b_{d}x^{2}_{d}\equiv n\ \mathrm{mod}\ p.

Under our assumption
p\nmid 2Dn
, if there is a
b_{i}
, say
b_{1}
, which is a quadratic residue modulo
p
,
by taking
x_{2}=\cdots=x_{d}=0
and
F(x_{1})=b_{1}x^{2}_{1}-n
in Lemma
2.1
, we can let
k=1,\ell=0
in Lemma
2.1
; if all
b_{i}
’s are quadratic non residue modulo
p
, via considering an equivalence equation to (
2.2
)

x^{2}_{1}+\frac{b_{2}}{b_{1}}x_{2}^{2}+\cdots+\frac{b_{d}}{b_{1}}x^{2}_{d}\equiv\frac{n}{b_{1}}\ \mathrm{mod}\ p,

we reduce to the previous case, thus we can also let
k=1,\ell=0
in Lemma
2.1
. Now we show that
\beta_{p}(n,f)=1
. In fact, for integer
\beta>1
and
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}(1\leq i\leq d)
, we
let

x_{i}=y_{i}+p^{\beta-1}z_{i},\quad n=n_{0}+p^{\beta-1}n_{1},

where
n_{1},z_{i}(1\leq i\leq d)
are integers and
y_{i},n_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[0,p^{\beta-1}-1]
.
Then the equation

b_{1}x_{1}^{2}+...+b_{d}x_{d}^{2}\equiv n\ mod\ p^{\beta}

implies

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &b_{1}y_{1}^{2}+..+cy_{d}^{2}\equiv n_{0}\ (mod\ p^{\beta-1})\\
&2b_{1}y_{1}z_{1}+...+2b_{d}y_{d}z_{d}\equiv\frac{n_{0}-(b_{1}y_{1}^{2}+..+cy_{d}^{2})}{p^{\beta-1}}+n_{1}\ (mod\ p)\end{aligned}\right.

Note that
n
is square free, the
y_{i}
’s cannot be divisible by
p
at the same time. Thus for a fixed non-zero root
(y_{1},...,y_{d})
of
b_{1}y_{1}^{2}+...+b_{d}y_{d}^{2}\equiv n_{0}\ (mod\ p^{\beta-1})
, we can lift it to
(x_{1},...,x_{d})
via the second congruence in (
2.3
), and there are
p^{d-1}
of such lift.
Thus for
\beta>1
, the number in the right hand of the equality (
2.1
) is stable, which must be
\delta_{p}(f,n)
. Therefore

\beta_{p}(f)=1
.

When
d=3
, by our assumption, we can take
\ell=1,k=3
in Lemma
2.1
. By the following modification to the method on the lifting solutions given above,
i.e., for
\beta>1
, we let

n=n_{0}+4p^{\beta-1}n_{1}\quad x_{i}=y_{i}+2p^{\beta-1}z_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq 3.

Here, all
n_{0},n_{1},y_{i},z_{i}
are integers with
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[0,2^{\beta+1}-1]
and
y_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[0,2^{\beta}]
. We consider the congruences via modulo
2^{\beta+2}
and
2^{\beta+1}
, and the second congruence in (
2.3
) becomes

b_{1}y_{1}z_{1}+b_{2}y_{2}z_{2}+b_{3}y_{3}z_{3}\equiv n_{1}+\frac{n_{0}-(b_{1}y^{2}_{1}+b_{2}y^{2}_{2}+b_{3}y^{2}_{3})}{2^{\beta+1}}\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2.

Then by the same method as above
we can take
\beta_{p}(f,n)=3
.

∎

To determine the local densities
\delta_{p}(f,n)
, we need a simple result on the sum of quadratic characters.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime, then

\sum\limits_{x=1}^{p}\left(\frac{x^{2}+a}{p}\right)=\begin{cases}-1&\textrm{if $p\nmid a$}\\
p-1&\textrm{if $p|a$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that
y^{2}-x^{2}\equiv a\ (mod\ p)
has
p-1
solutions
(x,y)
if
p\nmid a
and has
2p-1
solutions if
p|a
. Thus the result follows from that the cardinality of the set

\{x\ \mathrm{mod}\ p:x^{2}\equiv a\ \mathrm{mod}\ p\}
is
1+\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)
.
∎

Now we can determine the
\delta_{p}(f,n)
in the following lemmas. As remarked before, this lemma is the only place in the paper where we may assume
d
is even.

Lemma 2.4
.

Recall that
D=b_{1}...b_{d}
. Let
n
be a square free positive integer prime to
2D
, then for all primes
p\nmid 2nD
, we have

\delta_{p}(n,f)=\begin{cases}1-p^{-\frac{d}{2}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}D}{p}\right)&\textrm{if $2|d$,}\\
1+p^{\frac{1-d}{2}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}nD}{p}\right)&\textrm{if $2\nmid d$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, we know
\beta_{p}(n,f)=1
. We only prove the case for
d
odd, the other case can be done in the same way. The main idea is by induction on
d
.
For
d=1
, since
p
is coprime to
D=b_{1}
, we have

\delta_{p}(n,f)=|\{x\in\mathbb{Z}/p{\mathbb{Z}}:x^{2}\equiv b_{1}n\ \mathrm{mod}\ p)\}|=1+\left(\frac{b_{1}n}{p}\right).

We assume the lemma holds for
1,3,...,d-2
. For
d
, recall that

\delta_{p}(n,f)=p^{1-d}\cdot|\{x\in(\mathbb{Z}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}:f(x)\equiv n\ \mathrm{mod}\ p)\}|.

Since
p
is coprime to
2D
, the congruence

b_{1}x^{2}_{1}+\cdots+b_{d}x^{2}_{d}\equiv n\ \mathrm{mod}\ p

is equivalent to

x^{2}_{1}\equiv\frac{n}{b_{1}}-\frac{b_{2}}{b_{1}}x^{2}_{2}-\cdots-\frac{b_{d}}{b_{1}}x^{2}_{d}\ \mathrm{mod}\ p.

Set
{\mathfrak{E}}=\frac{n}{b_{1}}-\frac{b_{2}}{b_{1}}x^{2}_{2}-\cdots-\frac{b_{d}}{b_{1}}x^{2}_{d}
. Note that the number of solutions
x^{2}_{1}\equiv{\mathfrak{E}}\ \mathrm{mod}\ p
for any
(x_{2},\cdots,x_{d})\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{d-1}
is equal to
1+\left(\frac{{\mathfrak{E}}}{p}\right)
. Thus we obtain

(2.4)

\displaystyle\delta_{p}(n,f)=

\displaystyle p^{1-d}\sum^{p}_{x_{2},\cdots,x_{d}=1}\left(1+\left(\frac{{\mathfrak{E}}}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+p^{1-d}\cdot\left(\frac{-b_{1}b_{2}}{p}\right)\sum^{p}_{x_{2},\cdots,x_{d}=1}\left(\frac{x^{2}_{2}+{\mathfrak{D}}}{p}\right).

Here we denote
{\mathfrak{D}}=\frac{b_{3}}{b_{2}}x^{2}_{3}+\cdots+\frac{b_{d}}{b_{2}}x^{2}_{d}-\frac{n}{b_{2}}
and use the relation
{\mathfrak{E}}=-\frac{b_{2}}{b_{1}}(x^{2}_{2}+{\mathfrak{D}})
.
Now we apply Lemma
2.3
to the last sum in (
2.4
), we get

(2.5)

\sum^{p}_{x_{2},\cdots,x_{d}=1}\left(\frac{x^{2}_{2}+{\mathfrak{D}}}{p}\right)=\sum^{p}_{x_{3},\cdots,x_{d}=1}\sum^{p}_{x_{2}=1}\left(\frac{x^{2}_{2}+{\mathfrak{D}}}{p}\right)=-p^{d-2}+\sum^{p}_{x_{3},\cdots,x_{d}=1,p\mid{\mathfrak{D}}}p.

Equivalently, we have

\displaystyle\sum^{p}_{x_{2},\cdots,x_{d}=1}\left(\frac{x^{2}_{2}+{\mathfrak{D}}}{p}\right)+p^{d-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\cdot|\{(x_{3},\cdots,x_{d})\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{d-2}:b_{3}x^{2}_{3}+\cdots+b_{d}x^{2}_{d}\equiv n\ \mathrm{mod}\ p\}|.

Now applying the induction hypothesis to the above set
and put the above formula back into (
2.5
), we obtain the required result for
\delta_{p}(n,f)
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume
d
is odd and
n
is a square free positive integer with
(n,2D)=1
. For any odd prime
p
satisfying
p|n
, we have

\delta_{p}(n,f)=1-\frac{1}{p^{d-1}}

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, we know
\beta_{p}(n,f)=2
. We consider the congruence

(2.6)

b_{1}x^{2}_{1}+\cdots+b_{d}x^{2}_{d}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p^{2}.

Here we note that
p\mid n
. Write
x_{i}=y_{i}+pz_{i}(1\leq i\leq d)
with
y_{i},z_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
y_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[0,p-1]
. By the same lifting method as in Lemma
2.2
, we know the solution of the congruence (
2.6
) is equivalent to the following two congruences

(2.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &b_{1}y_{1}^{2}+..+b_{d}y_{d}^{2}\equiv 0\ (mod\ p)\\
&2b_{1}y_{1}z_{1}+...+2b_{d}y_{d}z_{d}\equiv\frac{-(b_{1}y_{1}^{2}+..+cy_{d}^{2})}{p}\ \mathrm{mod}\ p\end{aligned}\right.

Note that
n
is square free, so all the
y_{i}(1\leq i\leq d)
’s can not be divisible by
p
.
Thus the number of solutions in
({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{2}{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}
of the congruence (
2.6
) is
p^{d-1}
times the number of non-zero solutions in
({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}
of the congruence

b_{1}y_{1}^{2}+..+b_{d}y_{d}^{2}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p.

Now applying the same method in Lemma
2.4
, we have

|\{(y_{1},\cdots,y_{d})\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}:b_{1}y_{1}^{2}+..+b_{d}y_{d}^{2}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p\}|=p^{d-1}.

Therefore we get

|\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{2}{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}:b_{1}x_{1}^{2}+..+b_{d}x_{d}^{2}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p^{2})\}|=p^{d-1}(p^{d-1}-1),

and
\delta_{p}(n,f)=1-\frac{1}{p^{d-1}}
.

∎

It remains to consider the infinite prime case, i.e.,
p=\infty
. In this case, we use the usual Lebesgue measure.
Recall that
D=b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{d}
. Write
\Gamma(s)
for the Gamma function.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
p=\infty
,
\delta_{\infty}(n,f)=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}n^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})\sqrt{D}}
.

Proof.

Let
U
be open interval in
{\mathbb{R}}
centered at
n
with radius
R<n
.
Then

\displaystyle vol(f^{-1}(U))=

\displaystyle\int_{|b_{1}x_{1}^{2}+...+b_{d}x_{d}^{2}-n|<R}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\int_{|x_{1}^{2}+...+x_{d}^{2}-n|<R}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)\sqrt{D}}((n+R)^{\frac{d}{2}}-(n-R)^{\frac{d}{2}}).

Here the second equality follows by the change of variable formula and the third equality follows from the volume of ball with radius
R
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is equal to

\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}R^{d}.

Note that
vol(U)=2R
and
\Gamma(s+1)=s\Gamma(s)
, thus we have

\delta_{\infty}(n,f)=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)\sqrt{D}}\lim\limits_{R\to 0}\frac{(n+R)^{\frac{d}{2}}-(n-R)^{\frac{d}{2}}}{2R}=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}n^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})\sqrt{D}}.

∎

We will now clarify the case
d=3
, and we can calculate
\delta_{p}(n,f)
for all
p\leq\infty
explicitly. In this case

and

.

Corollary 2.7
.

When
d=3
and

with

has the following explicit form:

(1)

For

(2)

For

and

\delta_{p}(n,f)=\begin{cases}1+\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{-nD}{p}\right)&\textrm{if $p\nmid D$,}\\

For

For

.

Assertions (1), (2) and (3) directly follow from Lemmas

,

,

and

∎

3.
Siegel’s mass formula and applications

In this section, we use Siegel’s mass formula to prove Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.4
.

3.1.
Siegel’s Mass formula

We shall fix the quadratic form
f=b_{1}x^{2}_{1}+\cdots+b_{d}x^{2}_{d}
.
Define
\varepsilon_{2}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\varepsilon_{d}=1
if
d>2
. We have the following mass formula of Siegel:

G_{f}(n)=\varepsilon_{d}\prod\limits_{p\leq\infty}\delta_{p}(n,f)

3.2.
Application to the case
d=3
.

In view of the above Siegel’s formula, the product equals to certain Fourier coefficients of a normalized Eisenstein series, which is related to
L
-values. When
d=3
we can express the product as class number
h(-b)
of an imaginary quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-b})
for some
b>0
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f(x,y,z)=b_{1}x^{2}+b_{2}y^{2}+b_{3}z^{2}
with
\gcd(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=1
and
D=b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}
. Then for any positive square free integer
n\geq 1
coprime to
2D
, we can find a non-negative rational number
\lambda_{f}(n)
such that

(3.1)

G_{f}(n)=\lambda_{f}(n)h(-Dn),

where the number
\lambda_{f}(n)
is given as

(3.2)

\lambda_{f}(n)=\frac{24}{w_{K}}\sqrt{\frac{n}{D|D_{K}|}}\prod_{p|2D}\delta_{p}(n,f)\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)^{-1}.

Here
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-Dn})
,
D_{K}
is the discriminant of
K
,
w_{K}
is the number of roots of unity in
K
, and
\chi
is the Dirichlet character associated to
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Note that the square root part in
\lambda_{f}(n)
is independent of
n
. Let
D^{\circ}
denote the square free part of
D
. Thus by
D/D^{\circ}
is a square in
{\mathbb{Q}}
, we know
\lambda_{f}(n)
is indeed a rational number.

Proof.

The key idea is that the local representation densities are essential local Euler factors of
L
-functions.
From Corollary
2.7
, for all
p\nmid 2Dn
, we have

\delta_{p}(n,f)=1+\frac{\chi(p)}{p}=\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}.

From this relation and Siegel’s formula in Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle G_{f}(n)=

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{p\leq\infty}\delta_{p}(n,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\infty}(n,f)\frac{L(1,\chi)}{\zeta(2)}\times\prod_{p|2Dn}\left(\delta_{p}(n,f)(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p})(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}})^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{D}}\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\frac{2\pi h(-nD)}{w_{K}\sqrt{|D_{K}|}}\prod_{p|2Dn}\delta_{p}(n,f)(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p})(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}})^{-1},

where we have used the following standard facts on a zeta value and the class number formula

\zeta(2)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\quad\textrm{and}\quad L(1,\chi)=\frac{2\pi h(-nD)}{w_{K}\sqrt{|D_{K}|}}.

Recall (3) in Corollary
2.7
, we know for any
p\mid n
,
\delta_{p}(n,f)=1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}
. Therefore, we get

G_{f}(n)=\lambda_{f}(n)h(-nD)

and the number
\lambda_{f}(n)
is as given in (
3.2
).
∎

For an integer
M
, We recall the
M
-square-linked
relation defined in the introduction.
Two square free positive integers
n_{1},n_{2}
are
M
-square-linked
if
n_{1}/n_{2}
is a square in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}_{\ell}
for each prime
\ell
dividing
M
.

Corollary 3.2
.

There is a finite set
{\mathfrak{S}}(3)
of positive square free integers such that

\lambda_{f}(n)\in\{\lambda_{f}(s)\}_{s\in{\mathfrak{S}}(3)}

for all positive square free integer

.

Proof.

Put
1,3
in
{\mathfrak{S}}(3)
, we may assume
n>3
.
Recall the formula in (
3.2
), we know

(1)

|D_{K}|
depends on
n\ (mod\ 4)
.

(2)

\delta_{2}(n,f)
depends on

by Corollary

.

(3)

For any odd prime

,
\delta_{p}(n,f)
and
\chi(p)

depend on

.

Thus the cardinality of
{\mathfrak{S}}(3)
can be bounded by the number of the finite set

{\mathbb{Z}}/8{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/4D{\mathbb{Z}}\times\{1,3\}.

Notice the dependence of
\lambda_{f}(n)
on

in (1), (2) and (3) are mainly related to quadratic residues. Thus
if an integer

is a square in all
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
for

, from formula (

) and Corollary

, we know

.
Therefore we can let

containing a set of representatives of

-square-linked

∎

3.3.
Application to the case
d\geq 5

We now assume
d\geq 5
to be an odd integer. Assume that
f=b_{1}x^{2}_{1}+\cdots+b_{d}x^{2}_{d}
satisfies
\gcd(b_{1},\cdots,b_{d})=1
.
Recall that
D=b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{d}
and
D^{\circ}
the square free part of

. Put
{\mathfrak{d}}=\frac{d-1}{2}
and let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{(-1)^{{\mathfrak{d}}}Dn})
. Define
\chi
to be the Dirichlet character associated to
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
L(s,\chi)
be the Dirichlet

-series associated to
\chi
, since
\chi
is non-principal, the series

can be extended to a holomorphic function on the whole complex plane. We denote by
\varepsilon_{K}\in\{1,2\}

satisfying the discriminant

.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any positive square free integer
n
which is coprime to
2D
, there exists a rational number
\rho_{f}(n)
such that

(3.3)

G_{f}(n)=\rho_{f}(n)L\left(1-{\mathfrak{d}},\chi\right).

Moreover, the rational number
\rho_{f}(n)
is given as

(3.4)

\rho_{f}(n)=\frac{2^{{\mathfrak{d}}-1}(-1)^{[\frac{{\mathfrak{d}}}{2}]}\sqrt{\frac{D^{\circ}}{D}}}{(D^{\circ})^{\mathfrak{d}}\varepsilon_{K}^{2{\mathfrak{d}}-1}\zeta(1-2{\mathfrak{d}})}\cdot\prod_{p|2D}\delta_{p}(n,f)\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{\mathfrak{d}}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2{\mathfrak{d}}}}\right)^{-1}.

Here we denote by
[{\mathfrak{t}}]
the maximal integer no greater than
{\mathfrak{t}}
.

We remark since
D^{\circ}
is the square free part of

, thus
\sqrt{D^{\circ}/D}
is a rational number.

Proof.

The main idea is similar as Proposition
3.1
, i.e., the local representation densities provide the local Euler factor for
L
-functions. In contrast to the case

, we make essentially use of the functional equations of both the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet

-function.

From Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, we know that

\delta_{p}(n,f)=\begin{cases}(1-p^{-2{\mathfrak{d}}})(1-p^{-{\mathfrak{d}}}\chi(p))^{-1}&\textrm{if $p\nmid 2nD$,}\\
(1-p^{-2{\mathfrak{d}}})&\textrm{if $p|n,p\nmid 2D$,}\end{cases}

and
\delta_{\infty}(n,f)=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}n^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})\sqrt{D}}
.

By Siegel’s formula in Theorem

, we have

(3.5)

G_{f}(n)=\delta_{\infty}(n,f)\frac{L({\mathfrak{d}},\chi)}{\zeta(2{\mathfrak{d}})}\cdot\prod_{p|2Dn}\delta_{p}(n,f)\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{\mathfrak{d}}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2{\mathfrak{d}}}}\right)^{-1}.

Recalling that

\delta_{\infty}(n,f)=\frac{\pi^{{\mathfrak{d}}+\frac{1}{2}}n^{{\mathfrak{d}}-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma({\mathfrak{d}}+\frac{1}{2})\cdot\sqrt{D}},

and applying the functional equations of both zeta and

(3.6)

\delta_{\infty}(n,f)\frac{L({\mathfrak{d}},\chi)}{\zeta(2{\mathfrak{d}})}=\frac{2^{{\mathfrak{d}}-1}(-1)^{[\frac{{\mathfrak{d}}}{2}]}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{D}{D^{\circ}}}}{(D^{\circ})^{\mathfrak{d}}\varepsilon^{2{\mathfrak{d}}-1}_{K}}\cdot\frac{L(1-{\mathfrak{d}},\chi)}{\zeta(1-2{\mathfrak{d}})}.

By Lemma
2.5
, for any prime
p\mid n
but

, we have
\delta_{p}(n,f)=1-\frac{1}{p^{2{\mathfrak{d}}}}
. Note also that
\chi(p)=0
for

. Then put (
3.6
) into the equality (
3.5
), we see that both (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) follow.
∎

By the same method as in Corollary
3.2
and Lemma
2.4
, we have

Corollary 3.4
.

There is a finite set
{\mathfrak{S}}(d)
of positive square free integers such that

\rho_{f}(n)\in\{\rho_{f}(s)\}_{s\in{\mathfrak{S}}(d)}

for all positive square free integer
n
coprime to
2D
.

In particular, when
{\mathfrak{d}}
is even, which is equivalent to
d\geq 5
and
d\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
,
the field
K
is a real quadratic field. The number
{\mathfrak{d}}
is even.
Let
w_{{\mathfrak{d}}}(K)
be the largest integer
N
such that all the elements in
Gal(K(\zeta_{N})/N)
can be annihilated by
{\mathfrak{d}}
, where
\zeta_{N}
is a primitive
N^{th}
root of unity. Let
K_{d-3}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
be the
(d-3)^{th}

K
-group of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
defined by Quillen, then we have the following Quillen-Lichtenbaum conjecture
[
5
]
:

|K_{d-3}(\mathcal{O}_{K})|=\begin{cases}w_{{\mathfrak{d}}}(K)\zeta_{K}(1-{\mathfrak{d}}),\quad&\textrm{if }d\equiv 5\ \mathrm{mod}\ 8\\
\frac{w_{\mathfrak{d}}(K)}{4}\zeta_{K}(1-{\mathfrak{d}}),\quad&\textrm{if }d\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 8.\end{cases}

Here
\zeta_{K}(s)
is the Dedekind zeta function associated to
K
. By Wiles’s theorem on Iwasawa main conjecture
[
18
]
and
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
is abelian, we know the Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture is true for
K
by the fundamental work of Rost and Voevodsky.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Since
d\geq 5
and
d\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
,
{\mathfrak{d}}
is even. Keeping the same notations as above, by Proposition
3.3
, we have

G_{f}(n)=\rho_{f}(n)L(1-{\mathfrak{d}},\chi).

From the above remark on Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture and the identity

L(1-{\mathfrak{d}},\chi)\zeta(1-{\mathfrak{d}})=\zeta_{K}(1-{\mathfrak{d}}),

we know the result follows.
∎

4.
Waldspurger’s formula and applications to elliptic curves

In this section, we keep the assumption that
d
is odd. We first give some basic knowledge on Shimura lift and Waldspurger formula (see also
[
7
]
). We then study the case
d=3
and prove Theorem
1.5
.

4.1.
Modular Forms

Let
M_{k}(N,\chi)
be the space of modular forms of weight
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, level
N\in\mathbb{N}
and character
\chi
,
S_{k}(N,\chi)
be the subspace of cusp forms,
E_{k}(N,\chi)
be the orthogonal complement of
S_{k}(N,\chi)
with respect to the Petersson inner product.

First we assume
k
is not an integer. For any
h=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}\in M_{k}(N,\chi)
, the Hecke operator
T_{p^{2}}
with
p\nmid N
is given by
T_{p^{2}}h=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}q^{n}
where

(4.1)

b_{n}=a_{p^{2}n}+\chi(p)\left(\frac{(-1)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}n}{p}\right)p^{k-\frac{3}{2}}a_{n}+\chi(p^{2})p^{2k-2}a_{\frac{n}{p^{2}}}.

Here
a_{\frac{n}{p^{2}}}=0
if
p^{2}\nmid n
.

Note that when
h
is an eigenform with eigenvalue
\lambda_{p}
and
a_{1}=1
, then

(4.2)

\lambda_{p}=a_{p^{2}}+\chi(p)\left(\frac{(-1)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{p}\right)p^{k-\frac{3}{2}}

Next, we consider the weight
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. The Hecke operator
T_{p}
with
p\nmid N
is given by
T_{p}h=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}b_{n}q^{n}
where

(4.3)

b_{n}=a_{pn}+\chi(p)p^{k-1}a_{\frac{n}{p}}.

Here
a_{\frac{n}{p}}=0
if
p\nmid n
.
When
h
is an eigenform with eigenvalue
\mu_{p}
and
a_{1}=1
, then

(4.4)

\mu_{p}=a_{p}.

Recall that the theta series related to
f
is defined to be

\theta_{f}=\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q^{f(x)}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}r_{f}(n)q^{n}.

And the theta series related to the genus
{\mathfrak{G}}_{f}
of
f
is

\theta_{{\mathfrak{G}}_{f}}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}G_{f}(n)q^{n}.

Recall that
h=h_{f}
is the class number of
f
and
N_{f}
the level of
f
.
Write
{\mathfrak{G}}_{f}=\{f_{1}=f,...,f_{h}\}
,
\vartheta_{i}=\theta_{f}-\theta_{f_{i}}
for
i=2,...h
, and
V_{f}=\mathbb{C}\theta_{f_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}\theta_{f_{h}}
. Let
\chi_{f}=\left(\frac{4D}{.}\right)
, then we have the following decomposition:

Lemma 4.1
.

V_{f}
can be decomposed into

V_{f}\cap E_{\frac{d}{2}}(N_{f},\chi_{f})=\mathbb{C}\theta_{{\mathfrak{G}}_{f}}

V_{f}\cap S_{\frac{d}{2}}(N_{f},\chi_{f})=\mathbb{C}\vartheta_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}\vartheta_{h}

which are preserved by Hecke operators.

Proof.

This is a well-known result, see also
[
7
]
.
From
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
, we know
V_{f}
is preserved by Hecke operators.
By a result of Schulze-Pillot
[
10
, Korollar 1]
,
T_{p^{2}}\theta_{{\mathfrak{G}}_{f}}=(p^{d-2}+1)\theta_{{\mathfrak{G}}_{f}}
and
\theta_{{\mathfrak{G}}_{f}}\in E_{\frac{d}{2}}(N_{f},\chi_{f})
. Now a theorem of Eichler (see
[
15
, Theorem on Page 245]
) shows that
\vartheta_{j}(2\leq j\leq h)
are cusp forms. Therefore the result follows.
∎

We will consider the case when
d=3
and
h_{f}=2
. The Lemma
4.1
implies that
\vartheta_{2}
is an eigenform. So we can apply the Shimura lift to
\vartheta_{2}
. For this purpose, we give the following theorem:

Theorem 4.2

(Shimura
[
11
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 3
be an odd integer,
\lambda=\frac{k-1}{2}
,
4|N
,
\chi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
. Let
\theta\in S_{\frac{k}{2}}(N,\chi)
be an eigenform for
T_{p^{2}}
for all primes
p\nmid N
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{p}
. Define
\Theta=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}q^{n}
by

(4.5)

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}}=\prod\limits_{p}\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{p}p^{-s}+\chi(p^{2})p^{k-2-2s}}

Then
\Theta\in M_{k-1}(N^{\prime},\chi^{2})
for some integer
N^{\prime}
which is divisible by the conductor of
\chi^{2}
. If
k\geq 5
,
\Theta
is a cusp form. Moreover, by a remark made by Niwa
[
6
]
, we can choose
N^{\prime}=\frac{N}{2}
.

For a eigencusp new form
h
with integral weight and a Dirichlet character
\eta
, we denote by
L(h,\eta,s)
the
L
-functions associated to
h
twisted by
\eta
. For a square free integer
n
, we denote
\chi_{n}=(\frac{n}{\cdot})
.
Using Shimura lift, one can establish an relation between the coefficient of
\theta
and the values of the twisted
L
-functions of
\Theta
.

Theorem 4.3

(Waldspurger
[
14
]
)
.

Given
\theta=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{\frac{k}{2}}(N,\chi)
satisfying
T_{p^{2}}\theta=\lambda_{p}\theta
for all
p\nmid N
. If there is
\Theta\in S_{k-1}(\frac{N}{2},\chi^{2})
such that
T_{p}\Theta=\lambda_{p}\Theta
for all
p\nmid N
, then

(4.6)

a^{2}_{n_{0}}L(\Theta,\chi_{-1}^{\lambda}\chi^{-1}\chi_{n},\lambda)n^{\frac{k}{2}-1}=a^{2}_{n}L(\Theta,\chi_{-1}^{\lambda}\chi^{-1}\chi_{n_{0}},\lambda)n_{0}^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\chi\left(\frac{n_{0}}{n}\right)

for any positive square free integers
n,n_{0}
with
\frac{n_{0}}{n}\in(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
for all
p|N
.

If we can find
n_{0}
with
L(\Theta,\chi_{-1}^{\lambda}\chi^{-1}\chi_{n_{0}},\lambda)\neq 0
, then we can get an expression of
a_{n}
with respect to
L(\Theta,\chi_{-1}^{\lambda}\chi^{-1}\chi_{n},\lambda)
. In particular, if
\chi
is a quadratic character, then

(4.7)

a_{n}=\pm a_{n_{0}}\sqrt{\frac{L(\Theta,\chi_{-1}\chi_{f}^{-1}\chi_{n},1)n^{\frac{k}{2}-1}}{L(\Theta,\chi_{-1}\chi_{f}^{-1}\chi_{n_{0}},1)n_{0}^{\frac{k}{2}-1}}}.

Here we recall
\chi_{f}=\left(\frac{4D}{.}\right)
.

4.2.
Application to
d=3
and
h_{f}=2

Let
d=3
and
h_{f}=2
. In this case we write
{\mathfrak{G}}_{f}=\{f,g\}
, by Lemma
4.1
,
\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}
is an eigenform. We can give an explicit formula of
r_{f}(n)
applying both Theorems
4.2
and
4.6
. For this purpose, let us recall the following condition:

—

(
Sh-E
) The Shimura lift of
\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}
corresponds to an elliptic curve
{\mathcal{E}}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

For any square free integer
m
, we set
{\mathcal{E}}^{(m)}
to be the quadratic twist of
{\mathcal{E}}
by the extension
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{m})/{\mathbb{Q}}
. If
m=1
,
{\mathcal{E}}^{(m)}
is equal to
{\mathcal{E}}
. Let
L({\mathcal{E}}^{(m)},s)
be the
L
-function of
{\mathcal{E}}^{(m)}
. It is a holomorphic function on the whole complex plane. Let
\Omega_{{\mathcal{E}}^{(m)}}
be the real period of
{\mathcal{E}}^{(m)}
defined in the introduction. Recall that
D=b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}
and
D^{\circ}
is the square free part of
D
. We define the algebraic
L
-values of
{\mathcal{E}}^{(-mD^{\circ})}
to be

{\mathcal{L}}(m)=\frac{L({\mathcal{E}}^{(-mD^{\circ})})}{\Omega_{{\mathcal{E}}^{(-mD^{\circ})}}}.

Let
\xi=\frac{\mu_{f}}{\mu_{f}+\mu_{g}}
where
\mu_{f}=|{\mathcal{O}}(A_{f})|
. For any positive square free integer
b
, we denote by
h(-b)
the class number of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-b})
. Recall the rational number
\lambda_{f}(n)
given in Proposition
1.4
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let

\theta_{f}-\theta_{g}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}a_{n}q^{n}.

Assume the condition (
Sh-E
) holds. For any positive square free integer
n
coprime to
2D
, we have

(4.8)

r_{f}(n)=\xi a_{n}+\lambda_{f}(n)h(-nD^{\circ}).

Proof.

Note that
\chi_{f}=\left(\frac{4D}{.}\right)
. From Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
4.3
, we get a system of linear equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(1-\xi)r_{f}(n)+\xi r_{g}(n)=\lambda_{f}(n)h(-D^{\circ}n)\\
&r_{f}(n)-r_{g}(n)=a_{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Since the determinant of the coefficients of the linear equation is non-zero, which is in fact equal to
-1
. The result follows by solving the linear equation via Cramer’s rule.
∎

We recall the
N_{f}
-square-linked
relation between
two square free positive integers defined in the introduction. We say the two such integers
n_{1},n_{2}
are
N_{f}
-square-linked
if
n_{1}/n_{2}
is a square in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}_{\ell}
for each prime
\ell
dividing
N_{f}
.

Let
{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}
be a set of positive square free integers which consists of representatives of the equivalence classes of the
N_{f}
-square-linked

relation. Note that
N_{f}
and
2D
have the same prime divisors. From the proof of Corollary
3.2
, we can take
{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}
as a subset of
{\mathfrak{S}}(3)
. we now make the following assumption on
{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}
:

—

(
In-Nv
)There exists a positive square free integer
n_{0}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}
such that

L({\mathcal{E}}^{(-D^{\circ}n_{0})},1)\neq 0.

Proposition 4.5
.

Keep the notation as in Proposition
4.4
. Under the condition (
In-Nv
), if
n
and
n_{0}
are

N_{f}
-square-linked, then we have

(4.9)

r_{f}(n)=\pm\xi a_{n_{0}}\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{L}(n)}{\mathcal{L}(n_{0})}}+\lambda_{f}(n)h(-nD^{\circ}).

We remark from (
4.7
),
\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{L}(n)}{\mathcal{L}(n_{0})}}
is a rational number.

Proof.

The result directly follows from Proposition
4.4
and (
4.7
).
∎

From the formula (
4.9
), we know
r_{f}(n)
is related to the algebraic
L
-values of elliptic curves. Now we can apply a
2
-divisibility result on algebraic
L
-values of the quadratic twists of elliptic curves by one of us, see
[
19
]
. We fix a normalised additive
2
-adic valuation
v_{2}
on
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{2}
such that
v_{2}(2)=1
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an optimal eliptic curve with conductor
C
. Then for all square free integer
M\equiv 1\ (mod\ 4)
with
(M,C)=1
, we have

(4.10)

v_{2}(L(E^{(M)},1)/\Omega_{E^{(M)}})\geq t_{E}(M)-1-v_{2}(\nu_{E}),

where
\nu_{E}
is the Manin constant of
E
and
t_{E}(M)=\sum\limits_{q|M}t(q)
, with

t(q)=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if $2|c_{q}(E^{(M)})$,}\\
0&\textrm{if $2\nmid c_{q}(\mathcal{E}^{(M)})$.}\end{cases}

Here
c_{p}(E)
is the Tamagawa number of
E/\mathbb{Q}
at a finite prime
p
.

Now, we can easily see the following theorem completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Keep the conditions of Propositions
4.4
and
4.5
. Let
n>3
be a square free integer coprime to
2D
. Define
E={\mathcal{E}}^{(-D^{\circ})}
if
n\equiv 1\ (mod\ 4)
and
E={\mathcal{E}}^{(D^{\circ})}
if
n\equiv 3\ (mod\ 4)
. Assume that
E
is optimal. Let
n_{0}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}
satisfy (
In-Nv
). If
n
and
n_{0}
are
N_{f}
-square-linked,
then

(4.11)

v_{2}(r_{f}(n))\geq\min\left\{A_{n_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}t_{E}\left((\frac{-1}{n})n\right),B_{n_{1}}+v_{2}(h(-nD^{\circ}))\right\},

with
n_{1}\in{\mathfrak{S}}(3)
satisfying
\lambda_{f}(n_{1})=\lambda_{f}(n)
, where

\left\{\begin{aligned} &A_{n_{0}}=v_{2}\left(\frac{\xi a_{n_{0}}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}(n_{0})}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}(1+v_{2}(\nu_{\mathcal{E}}))\\
&B_{n_{1}}=v_{2}(\lambda_{f}(n_{1}))\end{aligned}\right.

are constants depend only on
n_{0}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}
satisfying (
In-Nv
) and
n_{1}\in{\mathfrak{S}}(3)
.

Furthermore, let
\mu(n)
be the number of distinct odd prime divisors of
n
. Then there exists constants
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
and
A\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
such that

(4.12)

v_{2}(r_{f}(n))\geq A+\frac{1}{2}t_{E}\left((\frac{-1}{n})n\right)

holds for all
n
satisfying
\mu(n)\geq\kappa
.

Proof.

Equation (
4.11
) is a direct corollary of Propositions
4.5
and
4.6
. By our choice, the set
{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}
is a finite subset of
{\mathfrak{S}}(3)
, we can define
A
to be the minimal value of
A_{n_{0}}
such that
n_{0}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}\textrm{satisfying (({In-Nv}))}
, and
B=\min\{B_{n_{1}}\}_{n_{1}\in{\mathfrak{S}}(3)}
.
Then equation (
4.11
) implies

(4.13)

v_{2}(r_{f}(n))\geq\min\left\{A+\frac{1}{2}t_{E}\left((\frac{-1}{n})n\right),B+v_{2}(h(-nD^{\circ}))\right\}.

By Gauss’s genus theory, we have
v_{2}(h(-D^{\circ}n))\geq\mu(n)+\mu(D^{\circ})-1
. Let
\kappa=\max\{2(A-B+1-\mu(D^{\circ})),1\}
, then for any

satisfying
\mu(n)\geq\kappa

\frac{1}{2}\mu(n)\geq A-B+1-\mu(D^{\circ}),

and

,
we get

Thus (
4.12
) follows from (
4.13
).
∎

Abstract
. We study metric properties of maximal framed representations of fundamental groups of surfaces in symplectic groups over real closed fields, interpreted as actions on Bruhat–Tits buildings endowed with adapted Finsler norms. We prove that the translation length can be computed as intersection with a geodesic current, give sufficient conditions guaranteeing that such a current is a multicurve, and, if the current is a measured lamination, construct an isometric embedding of the associated tree in the building. These results are obtained as application of more general results of independent interest on positive crossratios and actions with compatible barycenters.

Currents associated to positive crossratios

The proof of Theorem
1.2
relies on an abstract framework
that is applicable to more general situations and that we shortly describe here.
Let
X\subset\partial{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
be a
\Gamma
-invariant non-empty subset, such as, for example the set
{H_{\Gamma}}
of fixed points
of hyperbolic elements in
\Gamma

and let
X^{[4]}
denote the set of positively ordered quadruples in
X
. A
positive crossratio
is a
\Gamma
-invariant function

[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]\colon X^{[4]}\longrightarrow[0,\infty),

that
is flip-invariant

[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]=[x_{3},x_{4},x_{1},x_{2}]

and satisfies the property

[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4},x_{5}]=[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5}]+[x_{1},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]\,

whenever defined. Our definition is considerably more general than others existing in the literature (see Remark
3.2
for a comparison).
First we only require our crossratio to be defined on a dense subset
X
of the boundary of the hyperbolic plane:
this is important for some of the applications. For example, if the field
\mathbf{F}
is countable, also
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{F}^{n})
is countable,
and thus a maximal framing associated to a representation
\rho\colon\Gamma\to\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
,
as well as the induced crossratio, can only be defined on a countable set. Furthermore some representations,
as for example those defined via Fock–Goncharov or shear coordinates,
only have a framing defined on a countable set not including the fixed points of hyperbolic elements.
Second we do not require any continuity on our crossratio.
In many interesting examples, the crossratios arising from representations over non-Archimedean real closed fields are integer valued,
and thus cannot be continuous.
Dropping the continuity assumption on the crossratio also allows us to us encompass the theory of crossratios arising from actions on trees (see Example
3.5
).

If
\gamma\in\Gamma
is hyperbolic and
\{\gamma_{-},\gamma_{+}\}\subset X

\mu(\{g\})\geq 1

or

(2)

there exists
n_{0}
such that
\mu(R_{n})=0
for all

.

The second case cannot happen since

is in the support of
\mu

\mu
is purely atomic with
\mathbf{N}
-valued atoms.

We now show that all geodesics in the support of
\mu
are either closed or connect two cusps.
Let
(a,b)\in(\partial{\mathcal{H}}^{2})^{(2)}
be such an atom. Then
\Gamma\cdot(a,b)
meets every compact subset of
(\partial{\mathcal{H}}^{2})^{(2)}
in only finitely many points.
As a result, if

is the geodesic connecting

is a closed subset
where, as always,

denotes the universal covering map.
Thus either

corresponds to a periodic geodesic, or
{\rm pr}(g)
is a geodesic connecting two cusps.

There is a compact subset

such that every biinfinite geodesic,
as well as every closed geodesic, meets

.
Thus if
\mathcal{A}
denotes the set of atoms of
\mu
there is a compact subset

such that for all

,

\Gamma\cdot a\cap C\neq\emptyset
. This implies that

\mu=\sum_{c\in F}n_{c}\delta_{c}

where

is a finite set of geodesics either periodic or connecting to cusps,

\delta_{c}
is the geodesic current corresponding to

,
and
n_{c}\in\mathbf{N}^{*}
.
∎

Since

Let

We split the proof in three easy steps.

6.
The geometry of the Siegel spaces over real closed fields

The goal of this section is to recall facts about the geometry of Siegel spaces over real closed fields
needed to show that maximal framed actions give rise to a positive crossratio and admit a compatible barycenter
(Lemma
6.6
and Proposition
6.7
).

6.1.
Real closed fields

Recall that an ordered field is a field
\mathbf{F}
endowed with a total order relation
\leq
satisfying:

(1)

if
x\leq y
then
x+z\leq y+z
for all
z\in\mathbf{F}
;

(2)

if
0\leq x
and
0\leq y
, then
0\leq xy
.

The fields
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{R}
with their usual order are examples;
while some fields admit no ordering, like
\mathbf{C}
, others admit many, like
\mathbf{R}(X)
.

Example 6.1
.

The orders on
\mathbf{R}(X)
admit the following description for
\epsilon\in\{-,+\}
:

(1)

>_{\epsilon\infty}
: if
f\in\mathbf{R}(X)
,
f>_{\epsilon\infty}0

if
f(t)>0
for
t\to\epsilon\infty
.

(2)

>_{a\epsilon}
for
a\in\mathbf{R}
: we say that
f>_{a+}0
if there exists
\eta=\eta(f)\in\mathbf{R}
,
\eta>0
, with
f(t)>0
on the interval
(a,a+\eta)
,
and
f>_{a-}0
if
f(t)>0
on the interval
(a-\eta,a)
.

A basic fact is that every ordered field
\mathbf{F}
admits a real closure
\overline{\mathbf{F}}^{\mathrm{r}}
,
that is a maximal algebraic extension of
\mathbf{F}
to which the order extends.
Such a real closure is then unique up to a unique
\mathbf{F}
-isomorphism.
An ordered field
\mathbf{F}
is then
real closed
if the ordering does not extend to any proper algebraic extension.
Two useful characterizations are the following:

(1)

the field
\mathbf{F}
is ordered and
\mathbf{F}(\imath)
is algebraically closed, with
\imath=\sqrt{-1}
;

(2)

the field
\mathbf{F}
is ordered, every positive element is a square and any odd degree polynomial has a root.

Real closed fields have the same first order logic as the field
\mathbf{R}
of the reals.
An important consequence that is implicit in most of the geometric properties of the Siegel space we use,
is that any symmetric matrix with coefficient in a real closed field is orthogonally similar to a diagonal one.

An
\mathbf{R}
-valued valuation
is a map

v\colon\mathbf{F}\to\mathbf{R}\cup\{\infty\},

where
v\colon(\mathbf{F}^{\times},\cdot)\to(\mathbf{R},+)
is a group homomorphism,
v(0)=\infty
, and,
if we define the
norm
of
x\in\mathbf{F}
by
\|x\|_{v}:=e^{-v(x)}
if

x\neq 0
and
\|0\|_{v}:=0
, then

\|x+y\|_{v}\leq\|x\|_{v}+\|y\|_{v}.

An important valuation to keep in mind is
-\ln(|\cdot|)\colon\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Indeed valuations offer a replacement for the logarithm in more general real closed fields.
The valuation is
order compatible
if
|x|\leq|y|
implies that
v(x)\geq v(y)
,
and it is
non-Archimedean
if
v(x+y)\geq\min\{v(x),v(y)\}
,
that is if
\|x+y\|_{v}\leq\max\{\|x\|_{v},\|y\|_{v}\}
.

Example 6.2
.

The following are examples of ordered fields:

(1)

The field
\mathbf{R}
of real numbers and the field
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{r}
of real algebraic numbers;
both are real closed.

(2)

Let
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbf{N}
. The
quotient
\mathbf{R}_{\omega}
of the ring
\mathbf{R}^{\mathbf{N}}
by the
equivalence relation
(x_{n})\sim(y_{n})
if
\omega(\{n:\,x_{n}=y_{n}\})=1
,
ordered in such a way that positive elements are the classes of the sequences

(x_{n})
such that
\omega(\{n:\,x_{n}>0\})=1
, is a real closed field called the field of the
hyperreals
. It does not admit any order compatible
\mathbf{R}
-valued valuation.

(3)

Let
\sigma\in\mathbf{R}_{\omega}
be a positive infinitesimal, that is

\sigma
can be represented by a sequence
(\sigma_{n})_{n\geq 0}

with
\lim\sigma_{n}=0
and
\sigma_{n}>0
. Then

\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}:=\left\{x\in\mathbf{R}_{\omega}:\,|x|<\sigma^{-k}\text{ for some }k\in\mathbf{Z}\right\}

is a valuation ring with maximal ideal

\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}=\left\{x\in\mathbf{R}_{\omega}:\,|x|<\sigma^{k}\text{ for all }k\in\mathbf{Z}\right\}\,.

The quotient
\mathbf{R}_{\omega,\sigma}:=\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}/\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
is a real closed field, called the
Robinson field
.
It admits an order compatible valuation

v(x)=\lim_{\omega}\frac{\ln|x_{n}|}{\ln\sigma_{n}}\,,

where
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
represents
x
,
that leads to a non-Archimedean norm

\|x\|_{v}:=e^{-v(x)}\,.

(4)

Let
G
be a totally ordered Abelian group and let

\mathcal{H}(G):=\left\{f=\sum_{\sigma\in G}a_{\sigma}X^{\sigma}:\,a_{\sigma}\in\mathbf{R}\text{ and }\mathrm{supp}(f)\subset G\text{ is well ordered}\right\}

be the set of formal power series with exponents in
G
and coefficients in
\mathbf{R}
,
where
\mathrm{supp}(f):=\{\sigma\in G:\,a_{\sigma}\neq 0\}
.
This is an
\mathbf{R}
-vector space and the restriction on supports allows one to define
a ring structure that extends the ordinary multiplication on the group ring
\mathbf{R}[G]
of
G
.
In fact
\mathcal{H}(G)
turns out to be a field, called the
Hahn field with exponents
G
.
It is ordered by setting
f>0
if
a_{\sigma_{0}}>0
, where
\sigma_{0}=\min(\mathrm{supp}(f))

and it admits a
G
-valued compatible valuation
v(f)=\sigma_{0}
.
Moreover
\mathcal{H}(G)
is real closed if
G
is divisible.
For all of the above statements, see
[
DW96
, Theorem 2.15]
.

(5)

(Compare with §
8.3
) If
\alpha\in\mathbf{R}\smallsetminus\mathbf{Q}
, then the ring morphism

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}[x,y]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\,\,\,\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{R})

\displaystyle\longmapsto P(x,x^{\alpha})

extends to
\mathbf{R}(x,y)
. In this way we obtain an order on
\mathbf{R}(x,y)
for which

6.2.
The Siegel upper half space and the space
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\mathbf{F}}

Let
\mathbf{F}
be a real closed field, and
\imath
be a square root of
-1
.
Endow
V=\mathbf{F}^{2n}
with the standard symplectic form

\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
y_{1}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}x_{2}\\
y_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle:={}^{t}x_{1}y_{2}-{}^{t}y_{1}x_{2}\,,

where
x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbf{F}^{n}
.
The vector space
\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(\mathbf{F})
of symmetric matrices
admits a partial order defined by setting

X\ll Y\quad\text{ if }Y-X\text{ is positive definite.}

The
Siegel upper half space
is the semialgebraic set

\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}:=\{Z=X+\imath Y:\,X,Y\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(\mathbf{F})\text{ and }Y\gg 0\}

on which

\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbf{F}):=\left\{g=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}:\,\begin{array}[]{l}{{}^{t}\!A}D-{}^{t}CB=\mathrm{Id},\\
{}^{t}\!AC={}^{t}CA,\\
{}^{t}BD={}^{t}DB\end{array}\right\}

(8)

g_{\ast}Z:=(AZ+B)(CZ+D)^{-1}\,,

transitively. Of course this action descends to an action of
\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
.
The stabilizer of
\imath\mathrm{Id}_{n}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}
in
\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbf{F})

K=\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbf{F})\cap\operatorname{O}(2n)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\hphantom{-}A&B\\
-B&A\end{pmatrix}:\,{}^{t}AA+{}^{t}BB=\mathrm{Id}_{n},\,{}^{t}AB\text{ is symmetric}\right\}\,.

If
\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{R}
, then
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbf{R}}
is the symmetric space associated to
\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{R})
.
If, instead, the real closed field
\mathbf{F}
is endowed with an order compatible non-Archimedean valuation
v
,
then
\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
acts by isometries on a
v(\mathbf{F})
-metric space

\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}
whose construction we now recall. See
[
BIPP21c
,
BIPP21a
]
for generalizations of this construction.

On
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}
we define an multiplicative
\mathbf{F}
-valued distance function as follows.
Since
\mathbf{F}
is real closed, any pair
(Z_{1},Z_{2})
with
Z_{i}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}
, for
i=1,2
, is
\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
-congruent to a unique pair

(\imath\,\mathrm{Id}_{n},\imath D)
, where
D=\mathrm{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
,

d_{1}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}\geq 1
in
\mathbf{F}
.
We then set

D(Z_{1},Z_{2}):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}.

Proposition 6.3
.

D
is a
\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
-invariant
multiplicative distance function on
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}
, namely, for all
Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}
,

(MD1):

D(Z_{1},Z_{2})\in\mathbf{F}_{\geq 1}
, with equality of and
only if
Z_{1}=Z_{2}
;

(MD2):

D(Z_{1},Z_{2})=D(Z_{2},Z_{1})
;

(MD3):

D(Z_{1},Z_{2})\leq D(Z_{1},Z_{3})D(Z_{3},Z_{2})
.

Proof.

(MD1) and (MD2) are clear.
We consider the standard action of
\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
on
W=\wedge^{n}(\mathbf{F}^{2n})
,
endowed with the standard scalar product,
which is
K
-invariant as
K\subset\operatorname{O}(2n)
.
For

a=\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},a_{1}^{-1},\dots,a_{n}^{-1})
with

a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}\geq 1
in
\mathbf{F}
, we easily see that

\max_{w\in W,w\neq 0}\frac{||aw||}{||w||}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}

(the biggest eigenvalue of
a
in
W
).
For
g\in\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbf{F})

the operator norm of
g
on
W
is given by

|||g|||:=\max_{w\in W,w\neq 0}\frac{||gw||}{||w||}\,.

Since
g=kak^{\prime}
for some
k,k^{\prime}
in
K
and
a
as before (by the Cartan
decomposition), and

D(\imath\mathrm{Id}_{n},g_{\ast}\imath\mathrm{Id}_{n})=D(\imath\mathrm{Id}_{n},a_{\ast}\imath\mathrm{Id}_{n})=2|||a|||=2|||g|||\,,

(MD3) follows from submultiplicativity of the operator norm and transitivity of the action of
\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
.
∎

On
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}
we define an
associated pseudo-distance

d^{1}
as follows.

(9)

d^{1}(Z_{1},Z_{2}):=-v(D(Z_{1},Z_{2})=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}v(d_{i}).

The triangle inequality for
d^{1}
comes from (MD3).
We denote by
{\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\mathbf{F}}}
the metric quotient of
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}
with respect to the pseudo-distance
d^{1}
.
We will not need it, but note in passing that
{\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\mathbf{F}}}
can be identified with the quotient

\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{F})/\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{U})
, where
\mathbf{U}:=\{x\in\mathbf{F}:\,\|x\|_{v}\leq 1\}
.

Embedding in

6.4.
Maximal triples and intervals

We associate to a triple
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})
of pairwise
transverse Lagrangians in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{F}^{2n})

the quadratic form
Q_{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})}
on
\ell_{1}

defined by

Q_{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})}(v):=\langle v,v^{\prime}\rangle\,,

where
v^{\prime}\in\ell_{3}
is the unique vector such that
v+v^{\prime}\in\ell_{2}
.
If
\ell=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}X\\
\mathrm{Id}_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle
and

\ell^{\prime}=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}X^{\prime}\\
\mathrm{Id}_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle

are pairwise transverse,
then in the coordinates

(10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}^{n}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\ell

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}X\\
\mathrm{Id}_{n}\end{pmatrix}w

the quadratic form
Q_{(\ell,\ell^{\prime},\ell_{\infty})}
is represented by
X^{\prime}-X
.

Two triples of pairwise transverse Lagrangians
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})
and
(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})
are

\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
-congruent if and only if the quadratic spaces

(\ell_{1},Q_{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})})
and
(m_{1},Q_{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})})

are isomorphic or equivalently if and only if

Q_{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})}
and
Q_{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})}
have the same signature
[
BP17
, Proposition 2.5]
.
The value of the
Maslov cocycle
on
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})
is the signature of
Q_{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})}

\tau(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}):=\operatorname{sign}Q_{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})}\,.

The triple
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})
is
maximal
if
\tau(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})=n
,
the maximal value the Maslov cocycle can take. Similarly we say that a triple
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})
is
minimal
if
\tau(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})=-n
; it is easy to verify that
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})
is maximal if and only if
(\ell_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{3})
is minimal.
The group
\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
acts transitively on pairs of transverse Lagrangians in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{F}^{2n})

and on maximal triples.

Given
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{F}^{2n})
, we define the
interval

I_{(\ell,\ell^{\prime})}:=\{m\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{F}^{2n}):\,(\ell,m,\ell^{\prime})\text{ is maximal}\}\,.

Lemma 6.4

(
[
BP17
, Lemma 2.10]
)
.

Let
\ell=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}X\\
\mathrm{Id}_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle
and

\mathrm{Id}_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle
.
If the triple
(\ell,\ell^{\prime},\ell_{\infty})

then

with

,
we will also denote by

the set

and set

6.5.
Crossratios

In this subsection we recall the endomorphism valued
crossratio from
[

on quadruples of Lagrangians.
This, together with a maximal framing, will allow us in §
7.1
to associate to any maximal framed representation

Given a quadruple of Lagrangians

where

\mathbf{F}^{2n}
to
\ell_{j}
parallel to the complementary space
\ell_{i}
.
One verifies that for all

and hence

is
\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
-invariant.
If, for

, then
\ell_{j}=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}X_{j}\\
\mathrm{Id}_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle

for some
X_{j}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(\mathbf{F})
. By
[
BP17
, Lemma 4.2]
, the matrix representing
R(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4})
in the basis of
\ell_{1}
given by (
10
) is

(11)

(X_{1}-X_{2})^{-1}(X_{2}-X_{4})(X_{3}-X_{4})^{-1}(X_{1}-X_{3})\,.

We will denote such matrix
R(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4})
, with an abuse of notation.

Proposition 6.5

(
[
BP17
, Lemma 4.4]
)
.

Let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4},\ell_{5}
be pairwise transverse Lagrangians.

(2)

Lemma 6.6
.

(see (

6.7.
Barycenters

Assume that
\mathbf{F}
is non-Archimedean. If the triple of Lagrangians

(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})
is either maximal or minimal,
then
\ell_{2}\in I_{(\ell_{1},\ell_{3})}\cup I_{(\ell_{3},\ell_{1})}
.
We define

b(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}):={\rm pr}_{{\mathcal{Y}}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{3}}}(\ell_{2})\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}.

The
barycenter
of the triple
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})
is the point

B(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})=\pi(b(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}))\,,

where

is the metric quotient introduced in §

.
Our goal is to show the following (cfr.
[
BP17
, Lemma 7.5]
):

Proposition 6.7
.

B(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})
is invariant under permutation of the arguments.

First we need to establish a formula for the (pseudo-)distance in
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathbf{F}}
:

Lemma 6.8
.

If
z_{1}=x_{1}+\imath y_{1},\;z_{2}=x_{2}+\imath y_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathbf{F}}

d(z_{1},z_{2})=\max\left\{-v\left(\frac{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}{y_{1}y_{2}}\right),-v\left(\frac{y_{1}}{y_{2}}\right),-v\left(\frac{y_{2}}{y_{1}}\right)\right\}\,,

Proof.

Recall that for a general real closed field
\mathbf{F}

,

d(z_{1},z_{2})=\ln\|T+\sqrt{T^{2}-1}\|_{v}\,,

where
T=1+\frac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}{2y_{1}y_{2}}
.
Since

is non-Archimedean, then for

,
a\geq 0

\|a+b\|_{v}=\|\max\{a,b\}\|_{v}\,.

Hence

\|T+\sqrt{T^{2}-1}\|_{v}=\|T\|_{v}

and

Since

T=1+\frac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}{2y_{1}y_{2}}=\frac{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}{2y_{1}y_{2}}+\frac{y_{1}}{2y_{2}}+\frac{y_{2}}{2y_{1}}\,,

then

\|T\|_{v}=\max\left\{\left\|\frac{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}{y_{1}y_{2}}\right\|_{v},\left\|\frac{y_{1}}{y_{2}}\right\|_{v},\left\|\frac{y_{2}}{y_{1}}\right\|_{v}\right\}\,,

where we took into account that
\|n\|_{v}=1
for any

.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.7
.

Since
\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
acts transitively on maximal and minimal triples,
we may assume that
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}):=\left(\ell_{0},\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}Y\\
\mathrm{Id}_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle,\ell_{\infty}\right)
,
where

, depending on whether

is maximal or minimal.
A computation then gives:

\displaystyle{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{Y}_{0,\infty}}(\pm\mathrm{Id}_{n})

\displaystyle=\imath\mathrm{Id}_{n}

\displaystyle{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{\infty},\ell_{2}}}(0)

\displaystyle=(\pm 1+\imath)\mathrm{Id}_{n}

\displaystyle{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{2},\ell_{0}}}(\infty)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pm 1+\imath}{2}\mathrm{Id}_{n}\,.

Observe that if

Thus in order to compute the distances between various projections we just have to compute

\displaystyle d(\imath,\imath\pm 1)

\displaystyle=\ln\max\{\|1\|_{v},\|1\|_{v},\|1\|_{v}\}=0

\displaystyle d\left(\imath,\left(\frac{1+\imath}{2}\right)\right)

7.1.
The geodesic current

and Theorem
1.2

Let
\rho\colon\Gamma\to\operatorname{PSp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
be a maximal framed representation
with maximal framing

.
Define then for every

[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]_{\rho}:=-v(\det R(\varphi(x_{1}),\varphi(x_{2}),\varphi(x_{3}),\varphi(x_{4})))\,.

It follows from Proposition
6.5
that
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\rho}

is a positive crossratio on
{H_{\Gamma}}
,
and hence (Proposition
4.3
and
4.8
)
there is a geodesic current
\mu_{\rho}
such that

\mathrm{per}(\gamma)=i(\mu_{\rho},\delta_{c})

for every closed geodesic
c
represented by a hyperbolic element
\gamma\in\Gamma

Next, for the computation of
\mathrm{per}(\gamma)
,
recall that

is called
Shilov hyperbolic
if
there is a
g
-invariant decomposition
\mathbf{F}^{2n}=\ell_{+}\oplus\ell_{-}
into Lagrangians
such that all eigenvalues of
g|_{\ell_{-}}
have absolute value strictly smaller than one
(and thus all eigenvalues of
g|_{\ell_{+}}
have absolute value strictly larger than one),

[
BP17
, Definition 2.12]
. An element

is
Shilov hyperbolic
if any of its lifts to
\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
is. Of course the decomposition is uniquely determined by
g
and doesn’t depend on the lift.
We will need the following:

Lemma 7.1

(
[
BP17
, Lemma 7.9]
)
.

Let
g\in\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
be Shilov hyperbolic and
let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
g|_{\ell_{+}}
.
Then for any
\ell\in I_{(\ell_{-},\ell_{+})}
we have

\det R(\ell_{-},\ell,g\ell,\ell_{+})=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\,.

Proof.

We may assume by transitivity of the action of
\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbf{F})
on pairs of transverse Lagrangians, that
(\ell_{-},\ell_{+})=(\ell_{0},\ell_{\infty})
.
Furthermore
g=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&{}^{t}A^{-1}\end{pmatrix}
,
where
A
is the matrix of
g|_{\ell_{+}}
.
Then
\ell
corresponds to a matrix
X\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(\mathbf{F})
with
X\gg 0

and (
11
) gives

This implies that

\det R(\ell_{-},\ell,g\ell,\ell_{+})=(\det A)^{2},

hence the lemma.
∎

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
be hyperbolic with attractive and repulsive fixed points
\gamma_{+}
and
\gamma_{-}
.
It follows from
[
BP17
, Theorem 1.9]
that
\rho(\gamma)
is Shilov hyperbolic with corresponding decomposition

\mathbf{F}^{2n}=\varphi(\gamma_{-})\oplus\varphi(\gamma_{+})

\rho
.
Furthermore, if
\lambda_{1}(\gamma),\dots,\lambda_{n}(\gamma)
are the eigenvalues of
\rho(\gamma)

with
|\lambda_{1}(\gamma)|\geq\dots|\lambda_{n}(\gamma)|\geq 1
,
then Lemma
7.1
implies that if

\displaystyle\mathrm{per}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=-v(\det R(\varphi(\gamma_{-}),\varphi(x),\rho(\gamma)\varphi(x),\varphi(\gamma_{+})))

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{n}-v(\lambda_{i}(\gamma))=L(\rho(\gamma))\,.

If there is an element
\gamma\in\Gamma
with
-v(\mathrm{tr}(\rho(\gamma)))>0
, then necessarily
\rho(\gamma)
has an eigenvalue with the same property and thus
\mathrm{per}(\gamma)>0
. Viceversa if
\mu_{\rho}
is non-zero, there exists a proper closed rectangle

with
\mu_{\rho}(R)>0

\Gamma
is a lattice, we find

8.1.
Ultralimits of representations and asymptotic cones

Let

This gives rise to a representation

where the infinitesimal
\sigma
is defined below.
Denoting by
\mathbf{R}_{\omega,\sigma}
is the Robinson field, the representation
\rho_{\omega,\sigma}
obtained by composing
\rho_{\omega}

\Gamma

This construction is closely related to asymptotic cones, as we now recall.
Denoting by

0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}-3+y&-x\\
\frac{(y-2)^{2}}{x}&1-y\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1+y&\frac{y^{2}}{x}\\
-x&1-y\end{pmatrix},x>0,y\in\mathbf{R}\right\}\,.

The corresponding matrices are then

\displaystyle\rho_{X}(c_{1})=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}&0\\
X_{1}+X_{2}^{-1}{}^{t}X_{3}&{}^{t}X_{1}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{4}{x}&0&0\\
0&1&0&0\\
2&\frac{4}{x}&1&0\\
-\frac{4}{x}&2&-\frac{4}{x}&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\rho_{X}(c_{3})=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{}^{t}X_{3}^{-1}&-{}^{t}X_{3}^{-1}-X_{1}^{-1}{}^{t}X_{2}\\
0&X_{3}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1-y&x&-2&-x-\frac{y^{2}}{x}\\
-\frac{y^{2}}{x}&1+y&x+\frac{y^{2}}{x}&-2\\
0&0&1+y&\frac{y^{2}}{x}\\
0&0&-x&1-y\end{pmatrix}\,.

The above formulas allow us to consider
\rho_{X}
as a representation of
\Gamma
with coefficients
in the ring
\mathbf{R}[x,\frac{1}{x},y]
.
Now for every
\alpha\in\mathbf{R}
we consider the ring morphism of
\mathbf{R}[x,\frac{1}{x},y]
into the Hahn field
\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{R})

defined by sending
x
to
x
and
y
to
x^{\alpha}
. In this way we obtain for every
\alpha\in\mathbf{R}

a representation
\rho_{\alpha}\colon\Gamma\to\operatorname{Sp}(4,\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{R}))
.
It is easy to verify that the triple
X=(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})
with
y=x^{\alpha}
is in
R_{\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{R})}

and it follows from Proposition
8.2
that
\rho_{\alpha}
is maximal framed.
For the computation of the length function
L

it is not difficult to see that if
g\in\operatorname{Sp}(4,\mathbf{F})
, where
\mathbf{F}
is real closed non-Archimedean with an order compatible valuation, then

L(g)=-v(T(g))\,.

where
T(g)=(\mathrm{tr}g)^{2}-\mathrm{tr}g^{2}-4
.
In our case we obtain

T(\rho_{\alpha}(c_{1}^{-1}c_{3}))=4(4x^{2}+32x^{-4+4\alpha}+(18+8x^{2\alpha})+4x^{-2}(16+12x^{2\alpha}+x^{4\alpha}))\,

and

T(\rho_{\alpha}(c_{1}^{-1}c_{2}))=4(50+4x^{2}+8x^{2\alpha}+4x^{-2}(16+12x^{2\alpha}+x^{4\alpha}))

so

v(T(\rho_{\alpha}(c_{1}^{-1}c_{3})))=\min(-2,-4+4\alpha,-2+2\alpha)=\begin{cases}-2&\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}\\
-4+4\alpha&\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}

and

v(T(\rho_{\alpha}(c_{1}^{-1}c_{2})))=\min(-2,-2+4\alpha,-2+2\alpha)=\begin{cases}-2&\alpha\geq 0\\
-2+4\alpha&\alpha\leq 0\,.\end{cases}\,

We deduce that for
\alpha\leq 1/2
the length functions
\gamma\mapsto L(\rho_{\alpha}(\gamma))
are distinct
even when considered up to positive scaling. It is easy to verify that

\mathbf{Q}(\rho_{\alpha})=\begin{cases}\mathbf{Q}(x^{\alpha})&\text{ if }\alpha\in\mathbf{Q}\smallsetminus\{0\}\\
\mathbf{Q}(x)&\text{ if }\alpha=0\\
\mathbf{Q}(x,x^{\alpha})&\text{ if }\alpha\notin\mathbf{Q}\,.\\
\end{cases}

As a result, the image of the valuation is

v(\mathbf{Q}(\rho_{\alpha}))=\begin{cases}\alpha\mathbf{Z}&\text{ if }\alpha\in\mathbf{Q}\smallsetminus\{0\}\\
\mathbf{Z}&\text{ if }\alpha=0\\
\mathbf{Z}+\alpha\mathbf{Z}&\text{ if }\alpha\notin\mathbf{Q}\,,\\
\end{cases}

which implies by Theorem
1.4
that the geodesic current corresponding to
\rho_{\alpha}
is a multicurve if
\alpha\in\mathbf{Q}
.

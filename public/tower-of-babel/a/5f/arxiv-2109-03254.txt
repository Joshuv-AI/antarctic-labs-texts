Supergravity Black Holes, Love Numbers and Harmonic Coordinates
M. Cvetič

Abstract
To perform realistic tests of theories of gravity, we need to be able to look beyond general relativity and evaluate the consistency of alternative theories with observational data from, especially, gravitational wave detections using, for example, an agnostic Bayesian approach. In this paper we further examine properties of one class of such viable, alternative theories, based on metrics arising from ungauged supergravity. In particular, we examine the massless, neutral, minimally coupled scalar wave equation in a general stationary, axisymmetric background metric such as that of a charged rotating black hole, when the scalar field is either time independent or in the low-frequency, near-zone limit, with a view to calculating the Love numbers of tidal perturbations, and of obtaining harmonic coordinates for the background metric. For a four-parameter family of charged asymptotically flat rotating black hole solutions of ungauged supergravity theory known as STU black holes, which includes Kaluza-Klein black holes and the Kerr-Sen black hole as special cases, we find that all time-independent solutions, and hence the harmonic coordinates of the metrics, are identical to those of the Kerr solution. In the low-frequency limit we find the scalar fields exhibit the same S ​ L ​ ( 2 , R ) SL(2,R) symmetry as holds in the case of the Kerr solution. We point out extensions of our results to a wider class of metrics, which includes solutions of Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton theory.

The paper is organised as follows. In section 2, we show by considering

(r-r_{+})
factor
coming from
\Delta=(r-r_{+})(r-r_{-})
in the denominator, is cancelled
by the factor in the numerator), could just as well be assigned to

V_{1}
. It is included in
V_{0}
in order to give a precise
formulation of the near-zone equation that exhibits the
SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})

symmetry, in eqn (
4.11
) as seen below.

Following
[
26
]
,
and making the appropriate changes for our
case, we now define
SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})
generators as follows:

\displaystyle L_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa^{-1}\,{\partial}_{t}\,,

\displaystyle L_{\pm 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\pm\kappa t}\,\Big(\mp\Delta^{1/2}\,{\partial}_{r}+\kappa^{-1}\,{\partial}_{r}(\Delta^{1/2})\,{\partial}_{t}+{\frac{a}{\Delta^{1/2}}}\,{\partial}_{\phi}\Big)\,.

(4.8)

These obey the
SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})
algebra

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)\,L_{m+n}\,,\qquad-1\leq m\leq 1\,,\quad-1\leq n\leq 1\,.

(4.9)

Defining the quadratic
SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})
Casimir operator
{\cal C}_{2}\equiv L_{0}^{2}-{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 2}}}(L_{-1}\,L_{1}+L_{1}\,L_{-1})
, the scalar wave equation in the
near zone can (​ i.e. with the
V_{1}
term suppressed) be written as

\displaystyle{\cal C}_{2}\,\Phi=\lambda\,\Phi\,,

(4.10)

with the radial function
R
satisfying the near-zone equation

\displaystyle{\partial}_{r}(\Delta{\partial}_{r}R)+V_{0}\,R=\lambda R\,,

(4.11)

Thus, the near-zone massless scalar wave equation exhibits an
SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})

symmetry in the 4-charge rotating black hole background, extending the
previous findings for the Schwarzschild
[
44
]
and Kerr

[
26
]
black holes.

5
Time-independent Scalar Fields in Static Black Hole Backgrounds

It was observed in
[
27
]
that in the case of the
Schwarzschild black hole background, the symmetries of the
time-independent scalar wavefunctions, reflected in the tower of
ladder operators that related the the solutions with different
values of
\ell
in the decomposition in spherical harmonics

Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi)
, were related to the fact that the
massless time-independent wave operator was conformally related to
that on three-dimensional hyperbolic space. In this section, we demonstrate
that this feature extends to two broad classes of static black hole
solutions, namely those in a general Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton
(EMD) theory, and to the static 4-charge black holes of STU supergravity.

5.1
Static Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton black holes

These black holes are solutions of the theory described by the
Lagrangian

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\sqrt{-g}\,\Big[R-2({\partial}\phi)^{2}-e^{-2a\phi}\,F^{2}\Big]\,,

(5.1)

where the dimensionless constant,
a
, can be arbitrary.
The static black hole solutions were obtained in
[
45
]
. In a
convenient parameterisation described in
[
10
]
, the metrics
are given in terms of an isotropic radial coordinate
\rho
by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{2U}\,dt^{2}+\Phi^{4}\,(d\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}\,d\Omega^{2})\,,

(5.2)

where

\displaystyle e^{2U}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V^{2}\,W^{{\frac{2(1-a^{2})}{1+a^{2}}}}\,(CD)^{-{\frac{2}{1+a^{2}}}}\,,\qquad\Phi^{2}=(CD)^{{\frac{1}{1+a^{2}}}}\,W^{{\frac{2a^{2}}{1+a^{2}}}}\,,

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+{\frac{u^{2}}{\rho}}\,,\qquad D=1+{\frac{v^{2}}{\rho}}\,,\qquad W=1+{\frac{uv}{\rho}}\,,\qquad V=1-{\frac{uv}{\rho}}\,,

(5.3)

where
u
and
v
are constants parameterising the mass and the charge.

It can be seen that

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\rho\rho}=\rho^{2}\,VW\,\sin\theta\,,\qquad\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\theta\theta}=VW\,\sin\theta\,,\qquad\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\varphi\varphi}={\frac{VW}{\sin\theta}}\,,

(5.4)

and therefore a time-independent solution
\Psi
of the massless scalar wave
equation obeys

\displaystyle{\partial}_{\rho}(\rho^{2}\,VW\,{\partial}_{\rho}\Psi)+VW\,\nabla_{(\theta,\varphi)}^{2}\Psi=0\,,

(5.5)

where
\nabla_{(\theta,\varphi)}^{2}=\csc\theta\,{\partial}_{\theta}(\sin\theta\,{\partial}_{\theta})+\csc^{2}\theta\,{\partial}_{\varphi}^{2}
is the scalar Laplacian on the unit sphere.
In terms of the standard radial coordinate
r
defined by

r=\rho CD=\rho+u^{2}+v^{2}+{\frac{u^{2}v^{2}}{\rho}}\,,

(5.6)

the outer and inner horizons are located at
r_{\pm}=(u\pm v)^{2}
, and
defining
\bar{\Delta}=(r-r_{+})(r-r_{-})
we have

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}=\rho^{2}\,V^{2}\,W^{2}\,.

(5.7)

The time-independent wave equation (
5.5
) becomes

\displaystyle{\partial}_{r}(\bar{\Delta}\,{\partial}_{r}\Psi)+\nabla_{(\theta,\varphi)}^{2}\Psi=0\,,

(5.8)

and this can be seen to be the Laplace equation in the 3-metric

\displaystyle ds_{3}^{2}=dr^{2}+\bar{\Delta}\,d\Omega^{2}\,.

(5.9)

Following the same steps as were described in
[
27
]
in the
case of the Schwarzschild metric, we find here for the static EMD
black holes that the conformally-rescaled metric
d\tilde{s}_{3}^{2}=\Omega^{2}\,ds_{3}^{2}
, with
\Omega=L^{2}/\bar{\Delta}
, is given in terms of the isotropic
radial coordinate
\rho
by

d\tilde{s}_{3}^{2}={\frac{L^{4}}{(\rho^{2}-u^{2}\,v^{2})^{2}}}\,(d\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}\,d\Omega^{2})\,.

(5.10)

This can be recognised as the homogeneous metric on three-dimensional
hyperbolic space.

5.2
Static 4-charge STU supergravity black holes

These can be conveniently written, in terms of an isotropic radial
coordinate, in the form
[
10
]

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Pi^{-1/2}\,f_{+}^{2}\,f_{-}^{2}\,dt^{2}+\Pi^{1/2}\,(d\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}\,d\Omega^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\Pi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{I=1}^{4}C_{I}\,D_{I}\,,\quad f_{\pm}=1\pm{\frac{m}{2\rho}}\,,\quad C_{I}=1+{\frac{me^{2\delta_{I}}}{2\rho}}\,,\quad D_{I}=1+{\frac{me^{-2\delta_{I}}}{2\rho}}\,,

(5.11)

where the constants
m
and
\delta_{I}
parameterise the mass and the four
charges. The massless wave equation for a time-independent function

\Psi
is therefore given by

\displaystyle{\partial}_{\rho}(\rho^{2}\,f_{+}\,f_{-}\,{\partial}_{\rho}\Psi)+f_{+}\,f_{-}\nabla_{(\theta,\varphi)}^{2}\Psi=0\,.

(5.12)

In terms of the radial coordinate
r
defined by

r=\rho+m+{\frac{m^{2}}{4\rho}}\,,

(5.13)

and defining
\bar{\Delta}\equiv r(r-2m)=\rho^{2}\,f_{+}^{2}\,f_{-}^{2}
, eqn
(
5.12
) becomes

\displaystyle{\partial}_{r}(\bar{\Delta}\,{\partial}_{r}\Psi)+\nabla_{(\theta,\varphi)}^{2}\Psi=0\,.

(5.14)

This is the Laplace equation in the metric
ds_{3}^{2}=dr^{2}+\bar{\Delta}\,d\Omega^{2}
. The conformally-rescaled metric
d\tilde{s}_{3}^{2}=\Omega^{2}\,ds_{3}^{2}
, with
\Omega=L^{2}/\bar{\Delta}
, is recognisable as
the metric

\displaystyle d\tilde{s}_{3}^{2}={\frac{L^{4}}{(\rho^{2}-{\frac{m^{2}}{4}})^{2}}}\,(d\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}\,d\Omega^{2})\,,

(5.15)

on three-dimensional hyperbolic space,
when written in terms of the isotropic coordinate

\rho
.

In summary, we have seen that both in the case of the static EMD black
holes and the static 4-charge STU black holes, the massless time-independent
scalar wave equation reduces to the same form as was found in

[
27
]
in the case of the Schwarzschild metric. Thus the
construction of the time-independent wavefunctions in terms of
ladder operators proceeds in the same way as was seen in

[
27
]
, with the underlying conformally-related hyperbolic
metric providing a geometric understanding for the ladder structure.

In all the static backgrounds considered, the time-independent solutions
of the massless scalar wave equation obey the Laplace equation

g^{ij}\,\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\,\Psi\equiv\nabla^{2}\,\Psi=0\,,

(5.16)

in a 3-metric of the form (
5.9
) with
\bar{\Delta}
given by

\bar{\Delta}=(r-r_{+})(r-r_{-})\,.

(5.17)

In the conformally-rescaled metric
d\tilde{s}_{3}^{2}=\Omega^{2}\,ds_{3}^{2}

with
\Omega=L^{2}/\bar{\Delta}
, the conformally-invariant three-dimensional
scalar operator
(\widetilde{\nabla}^{2}-{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 8}}}\widetilde{R})
is related to
the corresponding operator in the untilded metric by

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\nabla}^{2}-{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 8}}}\widetilde{R})\,\widetilde{\Psi}=\Omega^{-5/2}\,(\nabla^{2}-{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 8}}}R)\,\Psi\,,

(5.18)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Psi}=\Omega^{-1/2}\,\Psi\,.

(5.19)

As can be easily verified, when
\bar{\Delta}
is given by (
5.17
)
the Ricci scalar in the untilded metric
(
5.9
) is given by

R={\frac{(r_{+}-r_{-})^{2}}{2\bar{\Delta}^{2}}}\,,

(5.20)

while the Ricci scalar of the tilded metric

d\tilde{s}_{3}^{2}=\Omega^{2}\,ds_{3}^{2}=(L^{4}/\bar{\Delta}^{2})\,ds_{3}^{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}=-{\frac{3(r_{+}-r_{-})^{2}}{2L^{4}}}\,.

(5.21)

Thus it follows that
-{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 8}}}\widetilde{R}+{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 8}}}\Omega^{-2}\,R=-{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 6}}}\widetilde{R}
, and so from eqn (
5.18
) we see that if

\Psi
obeys
\nabla^{2}\Psi=0
then
\widetilde{\Psi}
obeys

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\nabla}^{2}-{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 6}}}\widetilde{R})\,\widetilde{\Psi}=0\,.

(5.22)

In other words, in all the static metrics we
are considering here the time-independent solutions of the
massless scalar wave equation in the four-dimensional static black hole
background are conformally related to solutions of
eqn (
5.22
) in three-dimensional hyperbolic space. Note that,
as was observed in
[
27
]
in the case of the Schwarzschild
metric,
the coefficient of the Ricci scalar in this equation is the one associated
with the conformally-invariant scalar wave equation of four dimensions,
not three. As was discussed in
[
27
]
, the fact that the
metric
ds_{3}^{2}
whose Laplacian gives the time-independent scalar
wavefunctions is conformal to the
d\tilde{s}_{3}^{2}
hyperbolic
metric, which has
SO(3,1)
as its symmetry group, provides one
way to understand the

SL(2,R)
symmetry and ladder structure of the wavefunctions. In the
conformal frame of the hyperbolic metric the conformally-rescaled
wavefunctions
\widetilde{\Psi}
are eigenfunctions of the Laplace
operator
\widetilde{\nabla}^{2}
shifted by the constant
-{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 6}}}\widetilde{R}
.

6
Harmonic Coordinates

We turn, in this section, to an application of our previous results, enabling the
construction of harmonic coordinates, also known as wave coordinates.
Let

\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}=\sqrt{-\det g_{\alpha\beta}}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,.

(6.1)

Then, by definition, a harmonic coordinate chart,

x^{\mu}=(x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})=(x^{0},x^{i})
,
is one in which the De Donder gauge condition

\partial_{\mu}\,\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}=0\,,

(6.2)

holds. It follows that in harmonic coordinates the D’Alembertian
of any scalar function
f
is given by

\Box f{\,\,\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle*}$}}}{{=}}\,\,\,}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}f\,

(6.3)

where
\!\!\!{\,\,\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle*}$}}}{{=}}\,\,\,}\!\!\!
means “equal in harmonic coordinates”. In particular, harmonic coordinates satisfy

\Box\,x^{\alpha}=0\,.

(6.4)

The main interest of harmonic coordinates is that in
these coordinates the Einstein equations
become a semi-linear symmetric hyperbolic system,
since the highest derivative term has the same
form as the right-hand side of (
6.3
), with
f
representing
each component of the metric
g_{\alpha\beta}

[
46
]
. They also permit a reformulation of
the Einstein equations and the definition of total energy and momentum
in terms of the Landau-Lifshitz energy momentum pseudo-tensor
[
34
]
.

It is a standard result that for the Schwarzschild metric,
a set of harmonic coordinates is provided by
[
47
,
34
]

x^{\alpha}=\Big(t,(r-M)\sin\theta\cos\phi,(r-M)\sin\theta\sin\phi,(r-M)\cos\theta\Big)\,.

(6.5)

This result has been extended to the case of the Reissner-Nordström
black hole in
[
48
]
, the Kerr black hole in
[
49
]
, and the Kerr-Newman black hole in
[
50
]
.
In view of the universal form of the scalar D’Alembertian (
2.4
)
for the STU charged black holes we are considering in this paper, it
is evident that the procedure found in
[
49
]
for constructing
harmonic coordinates in the Kerr geometry can be immediately carried
over to the general case of the charged rotating STU black holes. Thus
we define a new azimuthal coordinate
\tilde{\phi}
by setting

\tilde{\phi}=\phi+\int^{r}{\frac{a\,dr^{\prime}}{\Delta(r^{\prime})}}\,,

(6.6)

and then it can be seen that the coordinates
x^{\alpha}
defined by

\displaystyle x^{\alpha}=\Big(t,[(r-M)\cos\tilde{\phi}-a\sin\tilde{\phi}]\,\sin\theta,[(r-M)\sin\tilde{\phi}+a\cos\tilde{\phi}]\,\sin\theta,(r-M)\,\cos\theta\Big)

(6.7)

are harmonic in the charged black hole metric (
2.1
), where
the base 3-metric
\gamma_{ij}
is given by that of the Kerr seed metric
in eqn (
2.8
).
In any stationary, axisymmetric metric for which
all metric components are independent of both
\phi
and
t
, and such that

g^{t\theta}
,
g^{tr}
,
g^{\phi\theta}
, and
g^{\phi r}

all vanish, so that
g^{\phi t}=g^{t\phi}
gives the only non-vanishing
cross term,
then
t
(and, in fact,
\phi
too) is a harmonic function, and we can seek the spatial harmonic coordinates by looking only at the time independent part of (
6.4
).
Thus, irrespective of
{\cal A}_{i}
and
U
, the coordinates
x^{\alpha}
given in eqn
(
6.7
) satisfy
\square\,x^{\alpha}=0
.
It follows that,
in complete generality, eqn (
6.7
) provides a set of
harmonic coordinates for all charged rotating black holes in the class
(
2.1
) with the Kerr seed 3-metric.

7
Conclusions

We have studied the charged, asymptotically-flat, rotating
black hole solutions of ungauged STU supergravity, which include
Kaluza-Klein black holes
and Kerr-Sen
black holes as special cases, and we find that the
time-independent solutions of the
massless scalar wave equation are identical to those of the Kerr solution.
This implies that the Love numbers for scalar perturbations, which
had previously been shown to vanish in the Kerr background, vanish also for
all these charged black holes. The harmonic coordinates for the
charged black holes also coincide with those for the Kerr metric.
In the low-frequency
limit, we find the scalar fields exhibit the same
SL(2,R)
symmetry as
holds in the case
of the Kerr solution. We have pointed out extensions of our results to
a wider class of black-hole metrics, including solutions of
Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton theory.

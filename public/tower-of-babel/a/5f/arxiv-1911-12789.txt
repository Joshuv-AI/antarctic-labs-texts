On the Penalty term for the Mixed Discontinuous Galerkin Finite Element Method for the Biharmonic Equation Thanks: Email: danu@goa.bits-pilani.ac.in
Balaje Kalyanaraman

Abstract
In this paper, we present a study on the effect of penalty term in the mixed Discontinuous Galerkin Finite Element Method for the biharmonic equation proposed by Gudi et al. 2008 . The proposed mixed Discontinuous Galerkin Method showed sub-optimal rates of convergence for piecewise quadratic elements and no significant convergence rates for piecewise linear elements. We show that by choosing the penalty term proportional to | e k | − 1 |e_{k}|^{-1} instead of | e k | − 3 |e_{k}|^{-3} , ensures an optimal rate of convergence for the approximation, including for piecewise linear elements. Finally, we present numerical experiments to validate our theoretical results. Keywords: Finite Elements; Discontinuous Galerkin Finite Element Method; Biharmonic problem; Optimal Error Estimates.



1
Introduction

Discontinuous Galerkin methods are popular finite element techniques
which use discontinuous polynomials to construct approximate
solutions. The local nature of approximation offers flexibility in
using higher order polynomials with non–uniformity in the degree of
approximation and in adaptive methods. For a review of the various
discontinuous Galerkin methods we refer the reader to

Arnold et al. 2002
;
Cockburn et al. 2012
;
Riviére 2008
. For fourth order problems, conforming
methods require imposition of
C^{1}
–continuity across the inter
element boundaries and are computationally expensive. Several finite
element methods have been proposed including the mixed finite element
method
(
Ciarlet & Raviart 1974
;
Monk 1987
;
Danumjaya & Pani 2012
)
,

C^{0}
–interior penalty methods
(
Brenner & Sung 2005
;
Engel et al. 2002
)
and so on to relax the continuity
requirements. The idea behind the mixed finite element method is to
split the fourth order problem into two second order problems by
introducing an auxiliary variable
v=-\Delta u
. The

C^{1}
–continuity requirement is relaxed and the system is then
approximated using a
C^{0}
-finite element method.

An
hp
mixed discontinuous Galerkin Finite Element
Method was proposed by
Gudi et al. 2008
. Using a
primal formulation leads to integrals involving higher order
derivatives which can be avoided by using a mixed
formulation. However, the method yielded sub-optimal convergence rates
for piecewise quadratic elements and no significant convergence rates
for piecewise linear elements. This is due to the choice of the
penalty parameter in the weak formulation which was taken proportional
to the inverse cube
(|e_{k}|^{-3})
of edge/face diameter
e_{k}
, a common choice in
super-penalization in non-symmetric interior penalty Galerkin
methods
(
Riviére 2008
)
. However, modifying the
penalty parameter by taking it proportional to the inverse of the edge
length
(|e_{k}|^{-1})
, essentially reducing the size of the penalty
term, yields optimal convergence.

In this paper, we consider the mixed formulation of the biharmonic
equation

\Delta^{2}u=f\quad\text{in}\;\;\Omega,

(1)

subject to the clamped boundary conditions

u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0\quad\text{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n=2,3
is a bounded and convex
domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
n
is the outward
unit normal to
\partial\Omega
. We assume that the data
f
is
sufficiently smooth so that there exists a unique solution to the
problem in
H^{4}(\Omega)
. We introduce a new variable
v=-\Delta u

and split the biharmonic equation (
1
) into two equations as

\displaystyle-\Delta v

\displaystyle=f\quad\text{in}\;\;\Omega,

(2)

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=v\quad\text{in}\;\;\Omega,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0\quad\text{on}\;\;\Gamma.

We then discretize the problem using the mixed discontiunous Galerkin
method and prove some error estimates for the finite element
solution to study the convergence of the method with respect to the
mesh size
h
. We then perform some numerical experiments to validate the
theoretical results.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we derive
the weak formulation of the biharmonic problem. The proof of
well–posedness of the problem is identical to
Gudi et al. 2008
and
hence we omit the same. In Section
3
, we discuss the error
estimates for
h-
refinement and we derive
L^{2}
and energy estimates
for the primal variable and an error estimate in the
L^{2}
-norm for
the auxiliary variable. Finally, in Section
4
we perform numerical
experiments to validate our theoretical results established in
Section
3
.

2
Weak Formulation

Let
\bar{\Omega}=\underset{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\cup}\bar{K}
be a
uniform partition of
\Omega
. Let us denote the edges of

\mathcal{T}_{h}
by
e_{k}
where
k=1,\cdots,M_{h}
. Let

\Gamma_{\text{I}}=\left\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots e_{P_{h}}\right\}
denote
the set of all interior edges and
\Gamma_{D}=\left\{e_{P_{h}+1},\cdots,e_{M_{h}}\right\}

\Gamma=\Gamma_{\text{I}}\cup\Gamma_{\text{D}}
. For each edge
e_{k}=K_{i}\cap K_{j}
, associate a unit normal
n_{k}
outward from
K_{i}
, so that

n|_{K_{i}}=-n|_{K_{j}}
. Let
h_{K}
denote the diameter of each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
h=\underset{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\max}\,h_{K}
. We define the broken Sobolev space

H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h})=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\;:\;v_{K_{i}}\in H^{s}(K)\;\,\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}.

(3)

where
H^{s}(K)
denotes the standard Sobolev space of order
s
. The
associated broken norm and semi–norm are defined respectively, by

\|v\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|v\|_{s,K}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad|v|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}|v|_{s,K}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

where
\|v\|_{s,K}
and
|v|_{s,K}
denotes the standard Sobolev norm
and seminorm on
K
, respectively. We denote the
L^{2}
norm by

\|\cdot\|
and its inner–product by
(\cdot,\cdot)
. We set
V=H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.

Let
u,v
be sufficiently smooth functions. We consider the first
equation in (
2
), multiply by some
\varphi\in V
, and
integrate over the domain to obtain

-\int_{\Omega}(\Delta v)\varphi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,dx

(4)

Applying integration by parts and using the fact that
[v]=0
in

\Gamma_{\text{I}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\nabla v\cdot n_{k}\right\}[\varphi]\,ds

\displaystyle-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\nabla\varphi\cdot n_{k}\right\}[v]\,ds

(5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}f\varphi\,dx.

Since
[u]=0
in
\Gamma
, we see that

J(u,\varphi)=\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\alpha_{k}[u][\varphi]\,ds=0

(6)

where
\alpha_{k}
is a positive real constant. Adding

J(u,\varphi)
to (
5
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\nabla v\cdot n_{k}\right\}[\varphi]\,ds-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\nabla\varphi\cdot n_{k}\right\}[v]\,ds

(7)

\displaystyle+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\alpha_{k}[u][\varphi]\,ds=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}f\varphi\,dx,

which yields the weak formulation of the first equation. Similarly for
the second equation, multiplying some
\chi\in V
, integrating over

\Omega
and using the fact that
\nabla u\cdot n=0
on
\Gamma_{\text{D}}
,

[u]=0
in
\Gamma
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\chi\,dx

\displaystyle-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\nabla u\cdot n_{k}\right\}[\chi]\,ds-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\nabla\chi\cdot n_{k}\right\}[u]\,ds=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}v\chi\,dx,

(8)

We define the bilinear forms

\displaystyle B(w,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla w\cdot\nabla z\,dx-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\nabla w\cdot n_{k}\right\}[z]\,ds-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\nabla z\cdot n_{k}\right\}[w]\,ds

\displaystyle B_{\text{I}}(w,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla w\cdot\nabla z\,dx-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\nabla w\cdot n_{k}\right\}[z]\,ds-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\nabla z\cdot n_{k}\right\}[w]\,ds

Now the weak formulation of the problem is as follows: Find
(u,v)\in V\times V
, such that

\displaystyle B(v,\varphi)+J(u,\varphi)

\displaystyle=(f,\varphi),

(9)

\displaystyle B(\chi,u)

\displaystyle=(v,\chi),

for all
(\varphi,\chi)\in V\times V
. Define a finite dimensional subspace

V_{h}
of
V
as

V_{h}=\left\{v\in H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\;:\;v|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(K)\;\;\text{for all}\;\;K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{p}(K)
denotes the space of polynomials of degree

\leq p
. Now the discontinuous Galerkin finite element weak
formulation reads: Find
(u_{h},v_{h})\in V_{h}\times V_{h}
such that

\displaystyle B(v_{h},\varphi)+J(u_{h},\varphi)

\displaystyle=(f,\varphi),

(10)

\displaystyle B(\chi,u_{h})

\displaystyle=(v_{h},\chi),

for all
(\varphi,\chi)\in V_{h}\times V_{h}
. For the purpose of error
analysis, we define the following mesh dependent energy norms,

\displaystyle|||w|||^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{|e_{k}|}{\sigma_{1}}\left\{\frac{\partial w}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}^{2}\,ds+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[w]^{2}\,ds\right),

\displaystyle|||w|||_{\text{I}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{|e_{k}|}{\sigma_{1}}\left\{\frac{\partial w}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}^{2}\,ds+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[w]^{2}\,ds\right).

Below, we state some properties of the bilinear form

B(\cdot,\cdot)
without proof.

Lemma 2.1
.

For sufficiently large constant
\sigma_{1}
, it can
be shown that for any
w_{h}\in V_{h}
,

C|||w_{h}|||_{\text{I}}^{2}\leq B_{\text{I}}(w_{h},w_{h})+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[w_{h}]^{2}\,ds,

(11)

where
C
is a constant independent of
h
and
p
. It can also be
shown that for all
w,q\in V
there exists a constant independent of

h
such that

|B(w,q)|\leq C|||w|||_{\text{I}}\;|||q|||.

(12)

The inequalities (
11
) and (
12
) refer to
the coercivity and the boundedness property of the bilinear forms. For
proofs, we refer the reader to

Riviére 2008
;
Gudi et al. 2008
and the references
therein. Below we define an interpolation operator for functions on
the Broken Sobolev space.

Definition 2.1
.

For given
\phi\in H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, define
I_{h}\phi\in V_{h}

be an interpolation operator satisfying optimal approximation
properties, i.e.,

|||\phi-I_{h}\phi|||\leq Ch^{\mu-1}\|\phi\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}

where
\mu=\min\{p+1,s\}
.

We recall the following trace inequality on the finite
element space.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
v_{h}\in V_{h}
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|\nabla^{l}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(e_{k})}\leq Ch_{K}^{-1/2}\|\nabla^{l}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)},\quad l=0,1.

(13)

Below, we state the inverse inequality without proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
v_{h}\in V_{h}
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that

|v_{h}|_{H^{1}(K)}\leq Ch_{K}^{-1}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)},

(14)

For proofs of the trace
inequality and the inverse inequality, we refer the reader to

Brenner & Scott 2007
and

Riviére 2008
. We state and prove the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
w_{h}\in V_{h}
, there exists a positive constant
C>0
, such that

|||w_{h}|||^{2}\leq C_{T}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla w_{h}|^{2}+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[w_{h}]^{2}\,ds\right).

Proof.

From the definition of the energy norm and the trace inequality,
we have for
w_{h}\in V_{h}

\displaystyle|||w_{h}|||^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla w_{h}|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{|e_{k}|}{\sigma_{1}}\left\{\frac{\partial w_{h}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}^{2}\,ds+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[w_{h}]^{2}\,ds\right),

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla w_{h}|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[w_{h}]^{2}\,ds\right),

which yields the desired result.
∎

We are interested to study the effect of the penalty parameter on the
convergence of the discrete solution. Let us set

\alpha_{k}=\sigma_{0}|e_{k}|^{-i}p^{2}.

(15)

where
p
is the degree of approximation of the polynomial,

\sigma_{0}
is a constant which will be defined later and
i
is
an integer which describes the degree of penalization. It
is well known in literature that for the case of the Non–symmetric
interior penalty Galerkin (NIPG) method for second order elliptic
problems, the choice of the penalty parameter plays a crucial role on
the convergence of the solution. If
i=1
, under normal penalization,
the convergence in the
L^{2}-
norm for the piecewise quadratic case is
suboptimal. However, for the case when
i=3
, under super
penalization, the convergence is optimal.
For the mixed Discontinuous Galerkin Method considered here, we observe that the
penalization term with
i=1
produces optimal convergence in all
cases, as opposed to
i=3
where optimal convergence rates are
observed only for piecewise cubic elements. The latter case was well
studied by
Gudi et al. 2008
. In the next section, we
derive optimal error estimates with
i=1
.

3
Error Analysis

Define the auxiliary projection
\Pi_{h}:H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\to V_{h}
by

\displaystyle B_{\text{I}}(\phi-\Pi_{h}\phi,\chi)

\displaystyle+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[\phi-\Pi_{h}\phi][\chi]\,ds=0,

(16)

\displaystyle\left(\phi-\Pi_{h}\phi,1\right)

\displaystyle=0.

The auxiliary projection can be shown to satisfy optimal error
estimates in the
L^{2}
and energy norms. We define

\mu=\min\{p+1,s\}
,
\bar{\mu}=\min\{p+1,\bar{s}\}
,

\theta=\min\{p+1,4\}
. We state and prove the
following Lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a given
\phi\in V
, there exists a unique
\Pi_{h}\phi\in V_{h}

satisfying (
16
). Moreover, there exists positive
constants independent of
h
such that

\displaystyle|||\phi-\Pi_{h}\phi|||

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\mu-1}\,\|\phi\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}

(17)

\displaystyle\|\phi-\Pi_{h}\phi\|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\mu}\,\|\phi\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

(18)

provided
\sigma_{1}
is a sufficiently large positive constant, i.e.,
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{1}^{*}
for some
\sigma_{1}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Proof.

For the proof, we refer the reader to Theorem 5.1 in
Gudi et al. 2008
.
∎

Subtracting (
10
) from
(
9
), we obtain the error equations

\displaystyle B(v-v_{h},\varphi)+J(u-u_{h},\varphi)

\displaystyle=0,

(19)

\displaystyle B(\chi,u-u_{h})-(v-v_{h},\chi)

\displaystyle=0.

(20)

for
(\varphi,\chi)\in V_{h}\times V_{h}
. Set
e_{u}:=u-u_{h}
and
e_{v}:=v-v_{h}
. The following Lemma is useful in proving the error estimates.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\xi_{v}=v_{h}-\Pi_{h}v
. Provided
\sigma_{0}>\sigma_{*}
, there
exists a positive constant

C_{*}
such that

\displaystyle C_{*}|||\xi_{v}|||^{2}\leq Ch^{2\mu-2}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+Ch^{2\bar{\mu}-2}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{0}\epsilon^{2}}{2C_{1}}J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u})

where
C_{1}
is a positive constant independent of
\sigma_{0}
and

.

Proof.

Let
\eta_{v}=v-\Pi_{h}v
. From equation (

B(\xi_{v},\varphi)=B(\eta_{v},\varphi)+J(\eta_{u},\varphi)-J(\xi_{u},\varphi),

where
\eta_{u}=u-I_{h}u
and
\xi_{u}=u_{h}-I_{h}u
. Setting

\varphi=\xi_{v}
and expanding the bilinear form

B(\cdot,\cdot)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\xi_{v}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{v}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}[\xi_{v}]\,ds+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{v}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}[\xi_{v}]\,ds

\displaystyle+B(\eta_{v},\xi_{v})+J(\eta_{u},\xi_{v})-J(\xi_{u},\xi_{v}).

(21)

Using Lemma
2.4
with
w_{h}=\xi_{v}
, and substituting the
result of equation (
21
) onto the Lemma, we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle c|||\xi_{v}|||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{v}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}[\xi_{v}]\,ds+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{v}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}[\xi_{v}]\,ds

\displaystyle+B(\eta_{v},\xi_{v})+J(\eta_{u},\xi_{v})-J(\xi_{u},\xi_{v})+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[\xi_{v}]^{2}\,ds,

with
c=\frac{1}{C_{T}}
being a constant which arises due to the trace inequality and is dependent on the triangulation. We observe that using the boundedness properties of the bilinear forms
together with the trace and Young’s inequalities, we have

\displaystyle B(\eta_{v},\xi_{v})

\displaystyle\leq C|||\eta_{v}|||\;|||\xi_{v}|||\leq Ch^{2\bar{\mu}-2}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}|||\xi_{v}|||^{2},

\displaystyle J(\eta_{u},\xi_{v})

\displaystyle\leq(J(\eta_{u},\eta_{u}))^{1/2}(J(\xi_{v},\xi_{v}))^{1/2}\leq Ch^{2\mu-2}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}|||\xi_{v}|||^{2},

subsequently the Young’s inequality, the trace inequality and the inverse
inequality, the term,

\displaystyle\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{v}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}[\xi_{v}]\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{|e_{k}|}{\sigma_{0}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{v}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}\,ds\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{0}}{|e_{k}|}[\xi_{v}]^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}|\nabla\xi_{v}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{0}}{|e_{k}|}[\xi_{v}]^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\sigma_{0}}|||\xi_{v}|||\;|||\xi_{v}|||,

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{2\sigma_{0}\epsilon^{2}}|||\xi_{v}|||^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|||\xi_{v}|||^{2},

\sigma_{0}

\displaystyle\left(c-\frac{C_{1}}{\sigma_{0}\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{0}}\right)|||\xi_{v}|||^{2}\leq Ch^{2\mu-2}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+Ch^{2\bar{\mu}-2}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\frac{\sigma_{0}\epsilon^{2}}{2C_{1}}J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u})

A condition that

\displaystyle C_{*}=\left(c-\frac{C_{1}}{\sigma_{0}\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{0}}\right)>0\quad\text{or}\quad\sigma_{0}>\frac{C_{1}+\sigma_{1}\epsilon^{2}}{c\epsilon^{2}},

yields a lower bound on

. Thus,

\displaystyle C_{*}|||\xi_{v}|||^{2}\leq Ch^{2\mu-2}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+Ch^{2\bar{\mu}-2}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\frac{\sigma_{0}\epsilon^{2}}{2C_{1}}J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u}),

which is the desired estimate with
\sigma_{*}=\frac{C_{1}+\sigma_{1}\epsilon^{2}}{c\epsilon^{2}}
for a sufficiently small
\epsilon
.

\displaystyle\|e_{v}\|^{2}+J(e_{u},e_{u})\leq C\left(h^{2\mu-2}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}}+h^{2\bar{\mu}-2}\|u\|_{\bar{s},\mathcal{T}}\right)

\displaystyle=(u-I_{h}u)-(u_{h}-I_{h}u):=\eta_{u}-\xi_{u},

\displaystyle v-v_{h}

Substituting the above definitions into the error equations

\displaystyle\|\xi_{v}\|^{2}+J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u})=B(\eta_{v},\xi_{u})+J(\eta_{u},\xi_{u})-B(\xi_{v},\eta_{u})+(\eta_{v},\xi_{v}).

Using the definition of the auxiliary projection we obtain,

\displaystyle B(\eta_{v},\xi_{u})

\displaystyle=B_{\text{I}}(\eta_{v},\xi_{u})-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{D}}}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\partial\eta_{v}}{\partial n_{k}}\xi_{u}\,ds,

\displaystyle=-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[\eta_{v}][\xi_{u}]\,ds-\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{D}}}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\partial\eta_{v}}{\partial n_{k}}\xi_{u}\,ds.

We observe that using the trace inequality, inverse inequality and
the Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle B(\eta_{v},\xi_{u})\leq Ch^{2\bar{\mu}-2}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u}).

for some
\delta\ll 1
. Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle J(\eta_{u},\xi_{u})\leq Ch^{2\mu-2}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u}).

We observe that using the Young’s inequality and the result from
Lemma
3.2
for the estimate of
|||\xi_{v}|||
we obtain,

\displaystyle B(\xi_{v},\eta_{u})

\displaystyle\leq C|||\xi_{v}|||\;|||\eta_{u}|||,

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2\mu-2}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{\delta^{2}C_{*}}{2}|||\xi_{v}|||^{2},

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2\mu-2}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+Ch^{2\bar{\mu}-2}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{0}\epsilon^{2}\delta^{2}}{4C_{1}}J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u}),

provided
\sigma_{0}>\sigma_{*}
. We note that the constant
C_{*}

which appears in front of
|||\eta_{v}|||
is absorbed into the
estimate involving
h
. Since
C_{*}

does not depend on
h
, the estimate is unaffected. Finally, we observe that

(\eta_{v},\xi_{v})\leq Ch^{2\bar{\mu}}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\|\xi_{v}\|^{2}.

Combining the bounds and
using the triangle inequality, we obtain

\|\xi_{v}\|^{2}+J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u})\leq Ch^{2\mu-2}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+Ch^{2\bar{\mu}-2}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\frac{\sigma_{0}\epsilon^{2}\delta^{2}}{4C_{1}}J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u}).

(22)

Setting

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\sigma_{0}\epsilon^{2}\delta^{2}}{4C_{1}}>0\quad\text{or}\quad\sigma_{0}<\frac{4C_{1}}{\epsilon^{2}\delta^{2}},

yields an upper bound for
\sigma_{0}
in terms of the constant

C_{1}
. The desired estimate follows from the triangle inequality and with
\sigma_{*}=\frac{C_{1}+\sigma_{1}\epsilon^{2}}{c\epsilon^{2}}
and

\sigma^{*}=\frac{4C_{1}}{\epsilon^{2}\delta^{2}}
for sufficiently small
\delta
.
∎

The consequence of having the condition that the constant
\sigma_{0}
to be bounded between

[\sigma_{*},\sigma^{*}]
may explain why the convergence rate
deteriorates for large values of penalty parameters. Choosing the value of
\sigma_{0}
too large tends the
value of the penalty terms to be proportional to
|e_{k}|^{-3}
and thusumay erode the estimates derived above. With the above conditions, the estimate in Theorem
3.1
predicts a higher rate of convergence,
\mu-1
for
\|e_{v}\|
than
\mu-2
which was predicted by
Gudi et al. 2008
. This will lead to better estimates for
|||e_{u}|||
and
\|e_{v}\|
. We state and prove the following theorems.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u_{h},v_{h}
satisfy (
10
). Then there exists some positive
constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|e_{u}\|\leq C\left(h|||e_{u}|||+h^{2}\|e_{v}\|+h^{\theta-1}|||v-\Pi_{h}v|||+h^{\theta-2}\|\Pi_{h}v-v_{h}\|+h^{\theta-1}(J(e_{u},e_{u}))^{1/2}\right).

Proof.

For the construction of the proof, we use the Aubin-Nitsche
duality argument. Consider the dual problem,

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi

\displaystyle=z\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

\displaystyle-\Delta\psi

\displaystyle=\phi\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial n}=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

where the functions
\phi
and
\psi
satisfy the regularity result

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{H^{4}}+\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|z\|.

Take
z=e_{u}
, multiply the first equation of the dual problem by

e_{u}
and integrate over the domain to obtain

B(\phi,e_{u})+J(e_{u},\psi)=\|e_{u}\|^{2}.

(23)

where we have used the fact that
[\psi]=0
on
\Gamma
and
[\phi]=0
on
\Gamma_{\text{I}}
. Next multiply the second equation of the dual problem by
e_{v}
and
integrate to obtain

B(e_{v},\psi)-(e_{v},\phi)=0.

(24)

where we have used
\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial n}=0
on
\Gamma_{\text{D}}
and
[\psi]=0
on
\Gamma
. Adding the equations (
23
) and (
24
) we obtain

\|e_{u}\|^{2}=B(\phi,e_{u})+J(e_{u},\psi)+B(e_{v},\psi)-(e_{v},\phi).

(25)

Denoting
\eta_{\phi}=\phi-I_{h}\phi
and
\eta_{\psi}=\psi-I_{h}\psi
, from the orthogonality result (
19
) and
(
20
) we have

\displaystyle B(e_{v},I_{h}\psi)+J(e_{u},I_{h}\psi)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle B(I_{h}\phi,e_{u})-(e_{v},I_{h}\phi)

\displaystyle=0.

Subtracting the above equations from (
25
), we obtain the following equation

\|e_{u}\|^{2}=B(\eta_{\phi},e_{u})+J(e_{u},\eta_{\psi})+B(e_{v},\eta_{\psi})-(e_{v},\eta_{\phi}).

(26)

Constructing the error bounds and using the regularity of the functions

\phi
and
\psi
we obtain,

\displaystyle B(\eta_{\phi},e_{u})

\displaystyle\leq C|||\eta_{\phi}|||\,|||e_{u}|||\leq Ch|||e_{u}|||\;\|\phi\|_{H^{2}},

\displaystyle(e_{v},\eta_{\phi})

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|e_{v}\|\;\|\phi\|_{H^{2}},

\displaystyle B(e_{v},\eta_{\psi})

\displaystyle\leq C|||e_{v}|||\;|||\eta_{\psi}|||\leq Ch^{\theta-1}|||e_{v}|||\;\|\psi\|_{H^{4}},

\displaystyle J(e_{u},\eta_{\psi})

\displaystyle\leq(J(\eta_{\psi},\eta_{\psi}))^{1/2}\,(J(e_{u},e_{u}))^{1/2}\leq Ch^{\theta-1}\;(J(e_{u},e_{u}))^{1/2}\|\psi\|_{H^{4}}.

Combining all the bounds,

\displaystyle\|e_{u}\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(h|||e_{u}|||+h^{2}\|e_{v}\|+h^{\theta-1}|||e_{v}|||+h^{\theta-1}(J(e_{u},e_{u}))^{1/2}\right),

\displaystyle\leq C\left(h|||e_{u}|||+h^{2}\|e_{v}\|+h^{\theta-1}|||v-\Pi_{h}v|||+h^{\theta-2}\|\Pi_{h}v-v_{h}\|+h^{\theta-1}(J(e_{u},e_{u}))^{1/2}\right),

(27)

which yields the desired estimate.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
u_{h},v_{h}
satisfy (
10
). There exists some positive
constant
C
such that

\displaystyle|||e_{u}|||

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\min\{\mu+\theta-3,\mu-1\}}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+Ch^{\min\{\bar{\mu}+\theta-3,\bar{\mu}-1\}}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}},

provided the conditions on
\sigma_{0}
in Theorem
3.1
holds.

Proof.

Let the constant
\epsilon\ll 1
and
\xi_{u}=u_{h}-I_{h}u
. We split the error
u-u_{h}=(u-I_{h}u)-(u_{h}-I_{h}u):=\eta_{u}-\xi_{u}
. From Lemma
2.4
, we note that

\displaystyle c|||\xi_{u}|||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\xi_{u}|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[\xi_{u}]^{2}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq B(\xi_{u},\xi_{u})+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{u}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}[\xi_{u}]\,ds+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{u}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}[\xi_{u}]\,ds+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[\xi_{u}]^{2}\,ds

From (
20
), we obtain

B(\chi,\xi_{u})=B(\chi,\eta_{u})-(e_{v},\chi).

Thus, with
\chi=\xi_{u}
we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle c|||\xi_{u}|||^{2}\leq B(\xi_{u},\eta_{u})-(e_{v},\xi_{u})

\displaystyle+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{u}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}[\xi_{u}]\,ds+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{I}}}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{u}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}[\xi_{u}]\,ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[\xi_{u}]^{2}\,ds.

We construct the following upper bounds for the terms on the right
hand side,

\displaystyle B(\xi_{u},\eta_{u})

\displaystyle\leq C|||\xi_{u}|||\;|||\eta_{u}||||\leq Ch^{2\mu-2}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|||\xi_{u}|||^{2},

\displaystyle(e_{v},\xi_{u})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{2\epsilon^{2}}\|e_{v}\|^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\|\xi_{u}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{u}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}[\xi_{u}]\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{|e_{k}|}{\sigma_{0}}\left\{\frac{\partial\xi_{u}}{\partial n_{k}}\right\}^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/2}\,\left(\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{0}}{|e_{k}|}[\xi_{u}]^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{2\epsilon^{2}}J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u})+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|||\xi_{u}|||^{2}.

Fixing
\sigma_{0}
and noting that
k_{1}\sigma_{0}\leq\sigma_{1}\leq k_{2}\sigma_{0}
, we have
the final upper bound,

\sum_{e_{k}\in\Gamma}\int_{e_{k}}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|e_{k}|}[\xi_{u}]^{2}\,ds\leq k_{2}J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u}).

Combining all the bounds and applying the triangle inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle c|||\xi_{u}|||^{2}\leq Ch^{2\mu-2}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+C\left(\|e_{v}\|^{2}+J(\xi_{u},\xi_{u})\right)+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}.

(28)

Writing

\displaystyle e_{v}=v-v_{h}=v-\Pi_{h}v-(v_{h}-\Pi_{h}v),

we obtain
\Pi_{h}v-v_{h}=(v-\Pi_{h}v)-e_{v}
. From estimate (
27
), we have

\displaystyle\epsilon\|e_{u}\|

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon\left(h|||e_{u}|||+h^{2}\|e_{v}\|+h^{\theta-1}|||\Pi_{h}v-v|||+h^{\theta-2}\|\Pi_{h}v-v_{h}\|+h^{\theta-1}(J(e_{u},e_{u}))^{1/2}\right),

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon\left(h|||e_{u}|||+h^{2}\|e_{v}\|+h^{\theta-1}|||v-\Pi_{h}v|||+h^{\theta-2}\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|+h^{\theta-2}\left(\|e_{v}\|+J(e_{u},e_{u})^{1/2}\right)\right).

Now we have from the results of Theorem
3.1
and
Lemma
3.1
the estimates,

\displaystyle h^{2}\|e_{v}\|+h^{\theta-2}\left(\|e_{v}\|+J(e_{u},e_{u})^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\min\{\theta-2,2\}}\left(h^{\mu-1}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+h^{\bar{\mu}-1}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\right),

\displaystyle h^{\theta-2}\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\bar{\mu}+\theta-2}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}},

\displaystyle h^{\theta-1}|||v-\Pi_{h}v|||

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\bar{\mu}+\theta-2}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}},

respectively. Thus the estimate for
e_{u}
reads,

\displaystyle\epsilon\|e_{u}\|\leq C\epsilon\left(h|||e_{u}|||+h^{\bar{\mu}+\theta-2}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+h^{\min\{\theta-2,2\}}\left[h^{\mu-1}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+h^{\bar{\mu}-1}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\right]\right).

(29)

Combining estimates (
28
) and (
29
) and using the triangle inequality, we obtain the desired result for
|||e_{u}|||
.
∎

Finally using Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
, we
can prove the following estimate for
\|e_{u}\|
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
u_{h},v_{h}
satisfy (
10
). There exists some positive
constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|e_{u}\|\leq Ch^{\min\{\mu+\theta-3,\mu\}}\|u\|_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+Ch^{\min\{\bar{\mu}+\theta-3,\bar{\mu}\}}\|u\|_{\bar{s}+2,\mathcal{T}_{h}},

provided the conditions on
\sigma_{0}
in Theorem
3.1
holds.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we have considered a mixed Discontinuous Galerkin Finite
Element Method to solve the biharmonic equation subject to clamped
boundary conditions. We have derived the weak formulation of the
problem and established error estimates for
h
-refinement. The
theoretical results predict optimal convergence rates in the energy
norm for the primal variable
u
, whereas optimal convergence is
predicted for the piecewise quadratic and cubic case in the

L^{2}
-norm. We performed a
series of numerical experiments using FreeFem++, and verified the
theoretical results. We observed that the choice of the penalty term
is crucial and must be chosen to be of the form
\alpha_{k}=\sigma_{0}|e_{k}|^{-1}p^{2}
to obtain optimal error estimates in

h
-refinement. Significant improvements in convergence rates for the
piecewise linear and quadratic elements were observed as a result.

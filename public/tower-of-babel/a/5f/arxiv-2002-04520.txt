A note on new type degenerate Bernoulli numbers
Taekyun Kim, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. Studying degenerate versions of various special polynomials have become an active area of research and yielded many interesting arithmetic and combinatorial results. Here we introduce a degenerate version of polylogarithm function, called the degenerate polylogarithm function. Then we construct new type degenerate Bernoulli polynomials and numbers, called degenerate poly-Bernoulli polynomials and numbers, by using the degenerate polylogarithm function and derive several properties on the degenerate poly-Bernoulli numbers.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11B83; 05A19

1.
Introduction

As is well known, for
s\in\mathbb{C}
, the polylogarithm function is defined by a power series in
z
, which is also a Dirichlet series in
s
:

(1)

\mathrm{Li}_{s}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{s}}=z+\frac{z^{2}}{2^{s}}+\frac{z^{3}}{3^{s}}+\cdots,\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6,9,22]).

This definition is valid for arbitrary complex order
s
and for all complex arguments
z
with
|z|<1
: it can be extended to
|z|\geq 1
by analytic continuation.

From (
1
), we note that

(2)

\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}=-\log(1-z).

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
(or
\mathbb{C}
), the degenerate exponential function is defined by

(3)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,14,15,17]).

By Taylor expansion, we get

(4)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13,14,15,16,17,18]),

where
\displaystyle(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\ (x)_{n,\lambda}=(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\ (n\geq 1)\displaystyle
.

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}t^{n}}{n!}=e^{xt}.

In [1,2], Carlitz considered the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials given by

(5)

\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\frac{t}{(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}-1}(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0
,
\beta_{n,\lambda}=\beta_{n,\lambda}(0)
are called the degenerate Bernoulli numbers. Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)=B_{n}(x),\quad(n\geq 0),

where
B_{n}(x)
are the ordinary Bernoulli polynomials given by

(6)

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-26]).

The Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined as

(7)

x^{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,l)(x)_{l},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,12,14,20,22]),

where
(x)_{0}=1,\ (x)_{n}=x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n+1),\ (n\geq 1)
.

Thus, we easily get

(8)

\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

In [11], Kim considered the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind which are defined as

(9)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=S_{2}(n,k).

The generating function of the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind is given by

(10)

\frac{1}{k!}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11]).

In this paper, we will introduce the degenerate polylogarithm function as a degenerate version of the polylogarithm function for
s=k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then we will construct new type degenerate Bernoulli polynomials and numbers, called degenerate poly-Bernoulli polynomials and numbers, by using the degenerate polylogarithm function and derive several properties on the degenerate poly-Bernoulli numbers.

2.
New type degenerate Bernoulli numbers and polynomials

We define the degenerate logarithm function
\log_{\lambda}(1+t)
, which is the inverse of the degenerate exponential function
e_{\lambda}(t)
and the motivation for the definition of degenerate polylogarithm function, as:

(11)

\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{n-1}(1)_{n,1/\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
4
) and (
11
), we note that

\displaystyle\log_{\lambda}(1+t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{n-1}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{2}{\lambda}\bigg)\cdots\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\lambda}(n-1)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\lambda)_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big((1+t)^{\lambda}-1\big).

Lemma 1
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big((1+t)^{\lambda}-1\big).

In addition,
e_{\lambda}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)=1+t
.

It is easy to show that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\frac{t^{n}}{n}=\log(1+t).

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the degenerate polylogarithm function as

(12)

l_{k,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n-1}(1)_{n,1/\lambda}}{(n-1)!n^{k}}x^{n},\quad(|x|<1).

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}l_{k,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n^{k}}=\mathrm{Li}_{k}(x),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,22]).

From (
12
), we note that

(13)

\frac{d}{dx}l_{k,\lambda}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n-1}(1)_{n,1/\lambda}}{(n-1)!n^{k}}x^{n}=\frac{1}{x}l_{k-1,\lambda}(x).

For
k\geq 2
, (
13
) can be written in the form of iterated integral which is given by

(14)

l_{k,\lambda}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\underbrace{\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t}\cdots\int_{0}^{t}}_{(k-2)-\mathrm{times}}\frac{1}{t}l_{1,\lambda}(t)dtdt\cdots dt

By (
11
) and (
12
), we get

(15)

l_{1,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n-1}(1)_{n,1/\lambda}}{n!}x^{n}=-\log_{\lambda}(1-x).

Thus, by (
14
) and (
15
), for
k\geq 2
we get

(16)

l_{k,\lambda}(x)=-\int_{0}^{x}\underbrace{\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t}\cdots\int_{0}^{t}}_{(k-2)-\mathrm{times}}\frac{1}{t}\log_{\lambda}(1-t)dtdt\cdots dt.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the new type degenerate Bernoulli numbers, which are called the degenerate poly-Bernoulli numbers, as

(17)

\frac{1}{x}l_{k,\lambda}(x)\bigg|_{x=1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}=\frac{1}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}l_{k,\lambda}(1-e_{\lambda}(-t))=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Note that

(18)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(1)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}l_{1,\lambda}(1-e_{\lambda}(-t))=\frac{-t}{e_{\lambda}(-t)-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}\beta_{n,\lambda}}{n!}t^{n}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
18
), we have

\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(1)}=(-1)^{n}\beta_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Now, we consider the new type degenerate Bernoulli polynomials which are called the degenerate poly-Bernoulli polynomials and given by

(19)

\frac{l_{k,\lambda}(1-e_{\lambda}(-t))}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}e_{\lambda}^{x}(-t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Note here that
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}=\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}(0)
.
From (
17
) and (
19
), we note that

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{l_{k,\lambda}(1-e_{\lambda}(-t))}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}e_{\lambda}^{x}(-t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\beta_{l,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(x)_{m,\lambda}}{m!}t^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\beta_{l,\lambda}^{(k)}(-1)^{n-l}(x)_{n-l,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

Comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
20
), we have

(21)

\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\beta_{l,\lambda}^{(k)}(-1)^{n-l}(x)_{n-l,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Now, we observe that

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}e_{\lambda}(-x)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{l}(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}x^{l}=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{l}(1)_{l,\lambda}}{(l-1)!}x^{l-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{l}(1)_{l+1,\lambda}}{l!}x^{l}=-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{l}(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}x^{l}(1-l\lambda)

\displaystyle=-e_{\lambda}(-x)+\lambda\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{l}(1)_{l,\lambda}}{(l-1)!}x^{l}=-e_{\lambda}(-x)+\lambda x\frac{d}{dx}e_{\lambda}(-x).

Thus, by (
22
), we get

(23)

(1-\lambda x)\frac{d}{dx}e_{\lambda}(-x)=-e_{\lambda}(-x).

Therefore, by (
23
), we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\frac{d}{dx}e_{\lambda}(-x)=-\frac{1}{1-\lambda x}e_{\lambda}(-x)=-e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-x).

By Lemma 2, we easily get

(24)

\frac{d}{dx}(1-e_{\lambda}(-x))=\frac{1}{1-\lambda x}e_{\lambda}(-x)=e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-x)

From (
13
), (
15
), (
17
) and (
24
), for
k\geq 2
we have

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{1-e_{\lambda}(-x)}l_{k,\lambda}(1-e_{\lambda}(-x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-e_{\lambda}(-x)}\int_{0}^{x}\underbrace{\frac{e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-t)}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-t)}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}\cdots\int_{0}^{t}}_{(k-2)-\mathrm{times}}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-t)}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}t\,\,dtdt\cdots dt.

Therefore, by (
25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

For
k\geq 2
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{1-e_{\lambda}(-x)}\int_{0}^{x}\underbrace{\frac{e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-t)}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-t)}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}\cdots\int_{0}^{t}}_{(k-2)-\mathrm{times}}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-t)}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}t\,\,dtdt\cdots dt.

From (
17
), we can derive the following equation:

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n-1}(1)_{n,1/\lambda}}{(n-1)!n^{k}}\big(1-e_{\lambda}(-t)\big)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{m}(1)_{m+1,1/\lambda}}{m!(m+1)^{k}}(1-e_{\lambda}(-t))^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{m}}{(m+1)^{k}}(1)_{m+1,1/\lambda}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}(-1)^{m-n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg((-1)^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{\lambda^{m}(1)_{m+1,1/\lambda}}{(m+1)^{k}}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{\lambda^{m}(1)_{m+1,1/\lambda}}{(m+1)^{k}}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m).

Note that

(-1)^{n}B_{n}=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(1)}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{m!}{m+1}(-1)^{m}S_{2}(n,m),\quad(n\geq 0).

For
k=2
, by Theorem 3, we get

(27)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(2)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-e_{\lambda}(-x)}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{t}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-t)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-e_{\lambda}(-x)}\int_{0}^{x}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}(1-\lambda)(-1)^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{1-e_{\lambda}(-x)}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{l,\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{l+1}\frac{(-1)^{l}x^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\beta_{m,\lambda}\frac{x^{m}}{m!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\frac{\beta_{l,\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{l+1}\frac{x^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg((-1)^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\beta_{m,\lambda}\frac{\beta_{n-m,\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{n-m+1}\bigg)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
27
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(2)}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\beta_{m,\lambda}\frac{\beta_{n-m,\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{n-m+1}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\beta_{n-m,\lambda}\frac{\beta_{m,\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{m+1}.

In general, from (25), we note that

(28)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{1-e_{\lambda}(-x)}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-t)}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-t)}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}\cdots\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(-t)}{1-e_{\lambda}(-t)}t\,\,dtdt\cdots dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{k-1}=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n_{1}!n_{2}!\cdots n_{k-1}!}\frac{\beta_{n_{1},\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{n_{1}+1}\frac{\beta_{n_{2},\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{n_{1}+n_{2}+1}\cdots\frac{\beta_{n_{k-1},\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k-1}+1}(-x)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k-1}}\frac{x}{1-e_{\lambda}(-x)}

\displaystyle=\quad\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\sum_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=n}\binom{n}{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}}\frac{\beta_{n_{1},\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{n_{1}+1}\frac{\beta_{n_{2},\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{n_{1}+n_{2}+1}\cdots\frac{\beta_{n_{k-1},\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k-1}+1}\beta_{n_{k},\lambda}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
28
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\geq 0
, we have

\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=n}\binom{n}{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}}\frac{\beta_{n_{1},\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{n_{1}+1}\frac{\beta_{n_{2},\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{n_{1}+n_{2}+1}\cdots\frac{\beta_{n_{k-1},\lambda}(1-\lambda)}{n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k-1}+1}\beta_{n_{k},\lambda}.

From (
17
), we observe that

(29)

\displaystyle l_{k,\lambda}\big(1-e_{\lambda}(-t)\big)

\displaystyle=\big(1-e_{\lambda}(-t)\big)\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\beta_{l,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(1)_{m,\lambda}}{m!}t^{m}\bigg)\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\beta_{l,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}-\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\beta_{l,\lambda}^{(k)}(-1)^{n-l}(1)_{n-l,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big(\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}-\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}(1)\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand,

(30)

\displaystyle l_{k,\lambda}\big(1-e_{\lambda}(-t)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{m,1/\lambda}(-\lambda)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!m^{k}}\big(1-e_{\lambda}(-t)\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{m,1/\lambda}}{m^{k-1}}(-\lambda)^{m-1}\frac{1}{m!}\big(1-e_{\lambda}(-t)\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{m,1/\lambda}(-\lambda)^{m-1}}{m^{k-1}}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)(-1)^{n-m}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{(1)_{m,1/\lambda}(-1)^{n-1}}{m^{k-1}}\lambda^{m-1}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
29
) and (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 7
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\quad\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}(1)-\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{(1)_{m,1/\lambda}}{m^{k-1}}\lambda^{m-1}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m),\quad(n\geq 1).

From (
15
), we note that

\displaystyle t=l_{1,\lambda}\big(1-e_{\lambda}(-t)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{m,1/\lambda}(-\lambda)^{m-1}}{m!}\big(1-e_{\lambda}(-t)\big)^{m}

(31)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(1)_{m,1/\lambda}(-\lambda)^{m-1}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)(-1)^{n-m}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{n}(1)_{m,1/\lambda}\lambda^{m-1}(-1)^{n-1}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
31
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 8
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{m=1}^{n}(1)_{m,1/\lambda}\lambda^{m-1}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)=\delta_{n,1},

where
\delta_{n,k}
is Kronecker’s symbol.

Remark.
Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(1)}=(-1)^{n}B_{n},\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(1)}(x)=(-1)^{n}B_{n}(x).

From Theorem 8 and Theorem 9, we note that

B_{0}=1,\quad B_{n}(1)-B_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&\textrm{if $n=1$},\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Corollary 9
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum_{m=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-m}(m-1)!S_{2}(n,m)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&\textrm{if $n=1$},\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

It is well known that the Stirling numbers of the first kind are given by

(32)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,12,22]),

where
k
is a nonnegative integer.

In view of (
32
), we may consider the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind which are given by

(33)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

From (
33
), we note that

(34)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x)_{k,\lambda}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand,

(35)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{x}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)=(1+t)^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By (
34
) and (
35
), we see that the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are also given by

(36)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)=S_{1}(n,k),\quad(n,k\geq 0).

Now, we observe that

\displaystyle(x)_{n+1}

\displaystyle=(x)_{n}(x-n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda}(x-k\lambda+k\lambda)-n\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda}

(37)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k+1,\lambda}+\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{n}kS_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda}-n\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k-1)(x)_{k,\lambda}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}(\lambda k-n)S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda}.

On the other hand,

(38)

(x)_{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}S_{1,\lambda}(n+1,k)(x)_{k,\lambda}.

Therefore, by (
37
) and (
38
) and with the usual convention that
S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)=0
, for
k>n
or
k<0
, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 10
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
, and
0\leq k\leq n+1
, we have

S_{1,\lambda}(n+1,k)=S_{1,\lambda}(n,k-1)+(\lambda k-n)S_{1,\lambda}(n,k).

From (
17
), we note that

(39)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n}(1)_{n+1,1/\lambda}}{n!(n+1)^{k}}\big(1-e_{\lambda}(-t)\big)^{n}.

Thus, by replacing
t
by
-\log_{\lambda}(1-t)
, we get

(40)

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\beta_{m,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{m}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n}(1)_{n+1,1/\lambda}}{n!(n+1)^{k}}t^{n}.

On the other hand,

(41)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\beta_{m,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{m}\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\beta_{m,\lambda}^{(k)}(-1)^{m}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,m)(-1)^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-m}\beta_{m,\lambda}^{(k)}S_{1,\lambda}(n,m)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
40
) and (
41
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 11
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\geq 0
, we have

\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k}}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{n}(1)_{n+1,1/\lambda}}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}\beta_{m,\lambda}^{(k)}S_{1,\lambda}(n,m).

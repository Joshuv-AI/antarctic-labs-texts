1.
Introduction

It is well-known that
the Schreier theorem does not hold for free profinite groups,
i.e.,
a subgroup of a free profinite group need not be free.
Subgroups of free profinite groups are groups of cohomological dimension
\leq 1
;
they are called projective,
as they satisfy the same universal property as projective modules.

Similarly, the Kurosh Subgroup Theorem
does not hold for free products of profinite groups.
Subgroups of free profinite product are relatively projective that gave motivation to study relatively projective groups in the context of profinite groups and Galois theory (see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
]
).

Following
[
6
]
a profinite group
G
acting continuously on a profinite space
T
will be called a
profinite pile
(or a pair if one follows
[
1
, Chapter 7]
) and denoted by
(G,T)
. We say that
(G,T)
is projective relatively to its point stabilizers if for given equivariant morphisms
(\varphi,\alpha)

\textstyle{(G,T)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{(B,Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{(A,X)}

(1.1)

such that
\alpha
is an epimorphism injective on point stabilizers that induces a homeomorphism on the spaces of orbits, there exists an equivariant map
\psi:(G,T)\rightarrow(B,Y)
such that
\alpha\psi=\varphi
. If
G
is a pro-
\mathcal{C}
group (where
\mathcal{C}
is a class of finite groups closed for subgroups, quotients and abelian extensions) then the whole diagram is restricted to pro-
\mathcal{C}
groups and the notion is the relative
\mathcal{C}
-projectivity. This definition is slightly differs from the definition in
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
12
]
; in
[
12
]
the definition is more restrictive for profinite groups of big cardinality as the author assumes the existence of a continuous section
\sigma:T/G\longrightarrow T
; in
[
4
]
the term is strongly projective; we refer the reader to
[
6
, Proposition 5.7]
for the interrelation of our definition and the one in
[
4
]
.

The objective of this note is to give a homological characterization of relatively prosolvable projective groups in the spirit of Ribes’ cohomological characterization of free (amalgamated) products
[
9
]
. Let
\pi
be a set of primes. We say that a pile
(G,T)
is
\pi
-projective if the embedding problem (
1.1
) with
\Ker(\alpha)
being an elementary abelian
p
-group is solvable for any
p\in\pi
. If
G
is prosolvable,
\pi
-projectivity coincides with
\mathcal{C}
-projectivity, where
\mathcal{C}
is the class of all solvable
\pi
-groups.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(G,T)
be a profinite pile.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

(G,T)
is
\pi
-projective;

(ii)

For each prime
p\in\pi
and every simple
p
-primary
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module
M

the map

H_{i}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}[[T]]\otimes M)\to H_{i}(G,M)
,
induced from
t\mapsto 1
for every
t\in\dot{t}\in T/G
,
is surjective for
i=2
and injective for
i=1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(G,T)
be a prosolvable pile and
\mathcal{C}
the class of all finite solvable groups.
Then
(G,T)
is
\mathcal{C}
-projective if and only if for each prime
p
and every simple
p
-primary
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module
M

the map

H_{i}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}[[T]]\otimes M)\to H_{i}(G,M)
,
induced from
t\mapsto 1
for every
t\in\dot{t}
,
is surjective for
i=2
and injective for
i=1
.

The structure of the paper is the following. Section 2 is dedicated to auxiliary results on relatively projective groups. Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
are proved in Section 3. We list open questions about relatively projective groups in Section 4.

2.
Relatively projective groups

We shall consider here a continuous action on the right of a profinite group
G
on a profinite space
T
and use the notation
t^{g}
for the translation.

Definition 2.1
.

A
pile

\mathbf{G}=(G,T)

consists of a profinite group
G
,
a profinite space
T
,
and a continuous action of
G
on
T
(from the right).

A pile

\mathbf{G}=(G,T)

is
finite
if
both
G
and
T
are finite.

A
morphism
of group piles

\alpha\colon\mathbf{B}=(B,Y)\to\mathbf{A}=(A,X)

consists of
a group homomorphism

\alpha\colon B\to A

and a continuous map

\alpha\colon Y\to X

such that

\alpha(y^{b})=\alpha(y)^{\alpha(b)}

for all
y\in Y
and
b\in B
.

The
kernel
\Ker\alpha
of
\alpha

is the kernel of the group homomorphism
\alpha\colon B\to A
.

The above morphism
\alpha
is an
epimorphism
if

\alpha(B)=A
,
\alpha(Y)=X
,
and for every
x\in X
there is
y\in Y
such that

\alpha(y)=x
and
\alpha(B_{y})=A_{x}
.
It is
rigid
, if

\alpha
maps
B_{y}
isomorphically onto
A_{\alpha(y)}
,
for all
y\in Y
,
and the induced map of the orbit spaces
Y/B\to X/A
is a homeomorphism.

Definition 2.2
.

An
embedding problem
for a pile
\mathbf{G}
is a pair

(\varphi\colon\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{A},\ \alpha\colon\mathbf{B}\to\mathbf{A})

(2.1)

of morphisms of group piles
such that
\alpha
is an epimorphism.
It is
finite
, if
\mathbf{B}
is finite and
rigid
if
\alpha
is rigid. It is a
\mathcal{C}
-embedding problem if
G,B,A
are pro-
\mathcal{C}
. It is a
\pi
-embedding problem if
\Ker\alpha
is an abelian
\pi
-group, where
\pi
is a set of primes.

A
solution
of (
1.1
) is a
morphism
\gamma\colon\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{B}

such that
\alpha\circ\gamma=\varphi
.

A pile
\mathbf{G}
is
\mathcal{C}
-projective (resp.
\pi
-
projective
),
if every finite rigid
\mathcal{C}
-embedding problem for
\mathbf{G}
(resp. every
\pi
-embedding problem)
has a solution.

Definition 2.3
.

A group homomorphism (resp. epimorphism)
\gamma:G\rightarrow B
such that
\alpha\circ\gamma=\varphi
will be called a weak solution (resp. proper weak solution) of (
1.1
).

Following
[
4
]
we shall say that a pro-
\mathcal{C}
group
G
is
\mathcal{G}
-projective (resp.properly
\mathcal{G}
-projective) if every finite
\mathcal{C}
-embedding problem has a weak solution (resp. proper weak solution). If every finite rigid
\pi
-embedding problem has a weak solution (resp. proper weak solution) then
G
shall be called
\mathcal{G}
-projective (resp.properly
\mathcal{G}
-projective) over
\pi
.

Remark 2.4
.

(a)
Of course if
\mathbf{G}
is projective then
G
is
\mathcal{G}
-projective. Moreover, the projectivity of
\mathbf{G}
implies strongly
\mathcal{G}
-projectivity of
G
in the sense of
[
4
]
which is called
\mathcal{G}
-projectivity in
[
6
]
. Conversely, if
G
is strongly
\mathcal{G}
-projective and
G_{t}\neq G_{s}
for any pair

then
\mathbf{G}
is projective (see
[
6

).

(b)

\mathbf{G}

,

has a solution. So we can always assume that

.

By the induction hypothesis

(c) A profinite group

is properly

We finish the section with proposition that will be used in Section 4.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class of finite groups closed for subgroups, quotients and extensions. Let
(G,T)
be a
\mathcal{C}
-projective pile such that there exists a continuous section
\sigma:T/G\longrightarrow T
. Then
G
embeds into a free pro-
\mathcal{C}
-product
H=\coprod_{s\in Im(\sigma)}G_{s}\amalg F
, where
F
is a free pro-
\mathcal{C}
group of rank
d(G)
, the minimal number of generators of
G
. Moreover,
\{G_{t}\mid t\in T\}=\{H_{s}^{h}\cap G\mid h\in H,s\in Im(\sigma)\}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:H\longrightarrow G
be an epimorphism that sends
H_{s}
on their isomorphic copies in
G
and
F
onto
G
. Put
S=\bigcup_{s\in Im(\sigma)}H_{s}\backslash H
. By
[
6
, Constriction 4.3]

(H,S)
is a pile and so extending
\alpha
to
S\rightarrow T
by
\alpha(H_{s}h)=s^{\alpha(h)}
one gets a rigid epimorphism
\alpha:(H,S)\longrightarrow(G,T)
. Since
(G,T)
is
\mathcal{C}
-projective the embedding problem
(id,\alpha)
has a solution which is clearly an embedding.

∎

3.
Homological characterization

Let
G
be a profinite group and

\{G_{t}\mathop{|\,}t\in T\}

be a family of subgroups indexed by a profinite space
T
.
Following
[
10
, Section 5.2]
we say that
it is
continuous

if for any open subgroup
U
of
G

the subset
\{t\in T\mathop{|\,}G_{x}\leq U\}
is open.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
10
, Lemma 5.2.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a profinite group and
let
\{G_{t}\mathop{|\,}t\in T\}
be a collection of subgroups
indexed by a profinite space
T
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\{G_{t}\mathop{|\,}t\in T\}
is continuous;

(b)

The set
\hat{\mathcal{G}}=\{(g,t)\in G\times T\mathop{|\,}t\in T,g\in G_{t}\}
is closed in
G\times T
;

(c)

The map
\varphi\colon T\to\Subgr(G)
, given by
\varphi(t)=G_{t}
, is continuous, where
\Subgr(G)
is endowed with the étale topology;

(d)

\bigcup_{t\in T}G_{t}
is closed in
G
.

If
(G,T)
is a pile then denoting by
G_{t}
the
G
-stabilizer of
t
,
for every
t\in T
,
we note that

\mathcal{G}=\{G_{t}\}_{t\in T}

is a continuous family of closed subgroups of
G

(
[
10
, Lemma 5.2.2]
)
closed under the conjugation in
G
,
such that

G_{t^{g}}=G_{t}^{g}
for all
t\in T
and
g\in G
.

Having a continuous family
\{G_{t}\mid t\in T\}
of subgroups of a pro-
\mathcal{C}
group
G
one can define a free pro-
\mathcal{C}
product
\coprod_{t\in T}G_{t}
(see
[
10
, Chapter 5]
). If
\mathcal{C}
consists of abelian groups, then the free pro-
\mathcal{C}
product becomes a profinite direct sum
\bigoplus_{t\in T}G_{t}
. This also extends to the case of pro-
\mathcal{C}
modules.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{G}=(G,T)
be a pile
and let

\mathcal{G}=\{G_{t}\}_{t\in T}
be the family of
G
-stabilizers.
Consider the following rigid embedding problem

\big(\id\colon\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G},\alpha\colon\mathbf{B}=(B,Y)\to\mathbf{G}\big)

(3.1)

with
M=\Ker(\alpha)
finite minimal abelian normal subgroup (i.e. an elementary abelian of order
p^{n}
). Let

\kappa_{i}\colon H_{i}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}[[T]]\otimes M^{*})\to H_{i}(G,M^{*})

be the homology maps induced from
T\mapsto 1
,
for
i=1,2
, where
M^{*}
is dual of
M
.

(a)

\kappa_{2}
is surjective if and only if for any embedding problem (
3.1
)
the group homomorphism
\alpha\colon B\to G

splits
and hence
B\cong M\rtimes G
.

(b)

\kappa_{1}
is injective if and only if every split embedding problem
(
3.1
) has a solution.

Proof.

(a)
We just need to show that the extension

\alpha\colon B\to G
,
as an element
\beta
of

,
is trivial if and only if
\kappa_{2}
is surjective.

Since
\alpha
is rigid,
\alpha_{|G_{y}}
is injective, for all

which means that
for every
G_{t}

there exists
y\in Y
and an isomorphism
\gamma_{t}:G_{t}\longrightarrow B_{y}
such that

\alpha\circ\gamma_{t}=\id
.
Note that the
G

\dot{t}
of

is homeomorphic to
G_{t}\backslash G
,
hence

\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\dot{t}]]\cong F_{p}[[G_{t}\backslash G]]
. Observe also that
Tor_{n}^{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}(M^{*},\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\dot{t}]])
is naturally isomorphic to
H_{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}[[T]]\otimes M^{*})
.
Therefore,
by
[
11
, Lemma 6.10.10]
for each
\dot{t}\in T/G

one has the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{H_{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\dot{t}]]\otimes M^{*})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\epsilon_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{n}}
\textstyle{H_{n}(G,M^{*})}
\textstyle{H_{n}(G_{t},M^{*})\hbox to0.0pt{,\hss}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

By

Thus by (

is equivalent to generation of

by

\Cor_{t}(H_{2}(G_{t},M^{*}))
,

Dually this means that

\beta_{t}
as a homomorphism

\delta_{\dot{t}}:H_{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\dot{t}]]\otimes M^{*})\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}.

Besides since

Claim. The maps

For each

choose a homomorphism
\gamma_{t_{i}}:{G_{i}}_{t_{i}}\longrightarrow B_{i}
with

for all

that represents

. Define

. Then we have a morphism of piles

\alpha_{i}:\mathbf{B}_{i}=(B_{i},Y_{i})\longrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{i}=(G_{i},T_{i})

and the embedding problem

High-order reliable numerical methods for epidemic models with non-constant recruitment rate
Bálint Máté Takács 1,2, Gabriella Svantnerné Sebestyén 1, István Faragó 1,2

Abstract
The mathematical modeling of the propagation of illnesses has an important role from both mathematical and biological points of view. In this article, we observe an SEIR-type model with a general incidence rate and a non-constant recruitment rate function. First, we observe the qualitative properties of different methods: first-order and higher-order strong stability preserving Runge-Kutta methods shu . We give different conditions under which the numerical schemes behave as expected. Then, the theoretical results are demonstrated by some numerical experiments.

Introduction

In recent years it turned out that the mathematical modeling of the propagation of illnesses is not only useful and important from a scientific point of view, but the outbreak of COVID-19 made the role of its real-life applications apparent: it showed the importance of adequate modeling of the illnesses not only from a clinical but also from an economical standpoint.

One of the key tools to observe such phenomena is mathematical models: in this paper, we consider differential equations. These models can be used to observe the rate of the spread of the illness and can also give an approximation of the number of infected people. Kermack and McKendrick
sir1927
used compartmental models to describe these phenomena. In this case, the members of the population are divided into compartments e.g. suspected or infected, and the members of the population can move between these compartments. In this paper we use the following common compartments:

•

S(t)
is the number of susceptible people who are not yet infected at time
t
,

•

E(t)
is the number of exposed individuals, who have been infected but are not yet infectious at time
t
,

•

I(t)
is the number of infectious people who can spread infectious diseases at time
t
,

•

R(t)
is the number of removed or deceased individuals, who have been infected and then removed from the disease meaning that they can no longer infect others at time
t
.

In the last decades numerous authors expanded the models of Kermack and McKendrick
matbio
,
capasso
,
sir_bev
. In recent years, the increasing rate of migration in Europe also influenced the rise of models incorporating the role of the movement of a considerable amount of people into the observed region
migration2
,
migration1
. In this paper, we also investigate an epidemic model which can be used to describe such phenomena. Throughout the article, we will assume that the recruitment rate function describing such migration (and also births in the system) is non-constant.

In Section
2
we propose the continuous model and show that the solution of this differential equation attains solutions that behave in a biologically reasonable way (e.g. they are non-negative). Then, in Section
3
we observe appropriate numerical schemes and show that by using a sufficiently small time step, the schemes preserve the qualitative properties of the continuous model such as positivity, boundedness, and conservation laws. Later in Section
3.1
similar theorems are also proved for higher-order methods. Finally, in Section
4
the previous results are demonstrated by some numerical experiments.

Model formulation and preliminaries

Let us consider the following generalization of the well-known SEIR type epidemics model:

\displaystyle\frac{dS(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=\Pi(t)-\mu S(t)-f(I(t))\cdot S(t),

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{dE(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=f(I(t))\cdot S(t)-(\mu+\sigma)E(t),

\displaystyle\frac{dI(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=\sigma E(t)-(\mu+\gamma)I(t)+\delta R(t),

\displaystyle\frac{dR(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=\gamma I(t)-(\mu+\delta)R(t)

(t\geq 0)
with the following initial conditions

\displaystyle S(0)=S_{0}\geq 0,\quad E(0)=E_{0}\geq 0,\quad I(0)=I_{0}\geq 0,\quad R(0)=R_{0}\geq 0.

(2)

Moreover, let function
N(t)
denote the total number of the population, meaning that

\displaystyle N(t)=S(t)+E(t)+I(t)+R(t).

(3)

The assumptions of the model are the following:

•

There is vital dynamics in this model: the function
\Pi(t)
is the recruitment rate (incorporating the births and the migration of the model) and the natural deaths rate equals
\mu\geq 0
in every compartment. Later we will assume that
0\leq\Pi(t)\leq K
(
K\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
) is a continuous function.

•

The incidence function has the form
f(I)\cdot S
, which function describes the way of transmission of the disease between susceptible and infected individuals in the population.

•

The parameter
\gamma
denotes the recovery rate of the infected individuals.

•

The rate at which exposed individuals become infected is expressed by parameter
\sigma
.

•

The parameter
\delta
represents the rate at which an individual from the recovered compartments comes to the infected class.

The function
f(I)
describes the effect infected people have on healthy members of the population. In epidemiology, this function is called the force of infection, which expresses the rate at which susceptible individuals acquire an infectious disease
matbio
. From now on, we assume that the function

has the following properties:

(C1)

f(x)\geq 0
for

(C2)

f(0)=0
,

(C3)

f(x)
is bounded in the sense that there exists a constant
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
|f(x)|\leq\alpha|x|
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

(C4)

f(x)
is locally Lipschitz, meaning that every point
z\in\mathrm{Dom}(f)
has a neighborhood
U_{z}\in\mathrm{Dom}(f)
in which

is Lipschitz continuous, i.e. for every

,
|f(x)-f(y)|<L|x-y|
holds for some constant

Note that these assumptions hold for several different choices of function

. The simplest choice of the function
f
when

\displaystyle f(x)=x.

(4)

Then the interaction between the suspected and infected people can be described by the function
I\cdot S
, which was used in the model

is a Holling–type function and has the form

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{c_{1}x}{1+c_{2}x^{k}}

(5)

where the parameters show the effect of the infectious

\displaystyle f(x)=\nu e^{-\eta x}x.

(6)

a)

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(t)=F(t,u(t)),

Since

\displaystyle\|F(t,u_{1})-F(t,u_{2})\|=\|-\mu S_{1}-f(I_{1})\cdot S_{1}+\mu S_{2}+f(I_{2})\cdot S_{2}\|+

(13)

\displaystyle+\|f(I_{1})\cdot S_{1}-(\mu+\sigma)E_{1}-f(I_{2})\cdot S_{2}+(\mu+\sigma)E_{2}\|+

\displaystyle+\|\sigma E_{1}-(\mu+\gamma)I_{1}+\delta R_{1}-\sigma E_{2}+(\mu+\gamma)I_{2}-\delta R_{2}\|+

\displaystyle+\|\gamma I_{1}-(\mu+\delta)R_{1}-\gamma I_{2}+(\mu+\delta)R_{2}\|

\displaystyle\|-\mu S_{1}-f(I_{1})\cdot S_{1}+\mu S_{2}+f(I_{2})\cdot S_{2}\|\leq

(14)

\displaystyle\leq\mu\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|+\|f(I_{1})\cdot S_{1}-f(I_{1})\cdot S_{2}+f(I_{1}\cdot S_{2}-f(I_{2})\cdot S_{2}\|\leq

\displaystyle\leq\mu\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|+\|f(I_{1})\|\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|+\|f(I_{1})-f(I_{2})\|\|S_{2}\|\leq

Now we use conditions (C3) and (C4):

\leq\mu\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|+\alpha\|I_{1}\|\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|+L\|I_{1}-I_{2}\|\|S_{2}\|\leq

(15)

Since we consider the local Lipschitz property, we can assume that

\leq\mu\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|+\alpha M\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|+LM\|I_{1}-I_{2}\|.

(16)

The second term of (

\displaystyle\|f(I_{1})\cdot S_{1}-(\mu+\sigma)E_{1}-f(I_{2})\cdot S_{2}+(\mu+\sigma)E_{2}\|\leq

(17)

Similarly, for the third term we have

\displaystyle\|\sigma E_{1}-(\mu+\gamma)I_{1}+\delta R_{1}-\sigma E_{2}+(\mu+\gamma)I_{2}-\delta R_{2}\|\leq

(18)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma\|E_{1}-E_{2}\|+(\mu+\gamma)\|I_{1}-I_{2}\|+\delta\|R_{1}-R_{2}\|.

The last, fourth term of (

\displaystyle\|\gamma I_{1}-(\mu+\delta)R_{1}-\gamma I_{2}+(\mu+\delta)R_{2}\|\leq

(19)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\|I_{1}-I_{2}\|+(\mu+\delta)\|R_{1}-R_{2}\|.

Then, it is clear that the local Lipschitz property will hold.

The global existence will be proved after part c).

b)

If we add up the equations of (
1
), we get

\displaystyle N^{\prime}(t)=\Pi(t)-\mu N(t),

(20)

which has a solution in the form

N(t)=N(0)\cdot e^{-\mu t}+\int_{0}^{t}\Pi(s)\cdot e^{-\mu(t-s)}\ ds.

(21)

Then,

N(t)\leq N(0)+e^{-\mu t}\int_{0}^{t}\Pi(s)\cdot e^{\mu s}\ ds\leq N(0)+e^{-\mu t}K\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mu s}\ ds=

(22)

=N(0)+e^{-\mu t}K\left(\dfrac{e^{\mu t}}{\mu}-\dfrac{1}{\mu}\right)\leq N(0)+\dfrac{K}{\mu}.

(23)

For the second claim, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left|N(t)-\dfrac{\Pi(t)}{\mu}\right|\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left|N(0)\cdot e^{-\mu t}\right|+\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\Pi(s)\cdot e^{-\mu(t-s)}\ ds-\dfrac{\Pi(t)}{\mu}\right|=

(24)

=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left|\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Pi(s)\cdot e^{\mu s}\ ds}{e^{\mu t}}-\dfrac{\Pi(t)}{\mu}\right|.

(25)

If
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\Pi(s)\cdot e^{\mu s}\ ds<\infty
, then the first term goes to zero. Moreover,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\Pi(s)\cdot e^{\mu s}\ ds<\infty

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Pi(t)=0
, which gives the statement.

On the other hand, if
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\Pi(s)\cdot e^{\mu s}\ ds=\infty

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left|\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Pi(s)\cdot e^{\mu s}\ ds}{e^{\mu t}}-\dfrac{\Pi(t)}{\mu}\right|=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left|\dfrac{\Pi(t)e^{\mu t}}{\mu e^{\mu t}}-\dfrac{\Pi(t)}{\mu}\right|=0.

(26)

If

(27)

and

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}N(t)=N(0)+\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\Pi(s)ds=N(0)+\mathcal{P}.

(28)

where we used the fact that

. Note that in this case

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dE}{dt}\right|_{E=0}

Then, since all the right-hand sides of the equations are non-negative for non-negative

(Cont’d) By part c) it is easy to see that the solutions cannot tend to infinity at a finite value of

3
Discrete models and their properties

Since the analytic solution of model (
1
) is not known, we are going to approximate these solutions by a numerical scheme.

The application of the explicit Euler method to equation (
1
) with timestep
\Delta t
results in the scheme

\displaystyle\frac{S^{n+1}-S^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{n}-\mu S^{n}-f(I^{n})\cdot S^{n},

(30)

\displaystyle\frac{E^{n+1}-E^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=f(I^{n})\cdot S^{n}-(\mu+\sigma)E^{n},

\displaystyle\frac{I^{n+1}-I^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\sigma E^{n}-(\mu+\gamma)I^{n}+\delta R^{n},

\displaystyle\frac{R^{n+1}-R^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\gamma I^{n}-(\mu+\delta)R^{n}

with the initial conditions
S^{0}=S(0)
,
E^{0}=E(0)
,
I^{0}=I(0)
,
R^{0}=R(0)\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 3.1

Assume that we apply scheme (
30
) to solve system (
1
). Then, the following statements hold:

a)

If

\displaystyle\Delta t\leq\Delta t^{\star}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{\mu+f(I^{n})},\frac{1}{\mu+\sigma},\frac{1}{\mu+\gamma},\frac{1}{\mu+\delta}\right\},

(31)

holds for every
n=0,1,\dots
, then the explicit Euler method applied to the problem (
1
) preserves the non-negativity property.

b)

Let
N^{k}=S^{k}+E^{k}+I^{k}+R^{k}
(
k=0,1,\dots
). If
\mu>0
and
\Delta t\leq\dfrac{1}{\mu}
, then the estimate

N^{k}\leq\mathcal{N}:=N^{0}+\frac{K}{\mu}

(32)

also holds for every value of
k=0,1,\dots
. Moreover, if
\Pi^{k}\rightarrow P
for some
P\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}N^{k}=\dfrac{P}{\mu}
.

If
\mu=0
, then we get
N^{k}\leq N^{0}+k\Delta tK
and if

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Pi^{k}=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathbb{R},

(33)

then
N^{k}\rightarrow N^{0}+\Delta t\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof

a)

The first equation in the system (
30
) can be written as

S^{n+1}=S^{n}+\Delta t\left(\Pi^{n}-\mu S^{n}-f(I^{n})\cdot S^{n}\right)=S^{n}(1-\Delta t\cdot\mu-\Delta t\cdot f(I^{n}))+\Delta t\cdot\Pi^{n}.

(34)

That means
S^{n+1}
is non-negative if the condition

\displaystyle 1-\Delta t\cdot\mu-\Delta t\cdot f(I^{n})\geq 0

(35)

is satisfied and we get the following upper bound for the step-size

\displaystyle\Delta t\leq\frac{1}{\mu+f(I^{n})}.

(36)

Similarly, by using the second equation in the system (
30
)

\displaystyle E^{n+1}=E^{n}+\Delta t(f(I^{n})\cdot S^{n}-(\mu+\sigma)E^{n})=\Delta t\cdot f(I^{n})\cdot S^{n}+E^{n}(1-\Delta t(\mu+\sigma))

(37)

which is non-negative if the condition

\displaystyle\Delta t\leq\frac{1}{\mu+\sigma}

(38)

holds. From the third equation

\displaystyle I^{n+1}=I^{n}+\Delta t(\sigma E^{n}-(\mu+\gamma)I^{n}+\delta R^{n})=\Delta t((\sigma E^{n}+\delta R^{n})+I^{n}(1-\Delta t(\mu+\gamma))

(39)

we get the following condition for the non-negativity property

\displaystyle\Delta t\leq\frac{1}{\mu+\gamma}.

(40)

Similarly from the last equation, the condition for the non-negativity preservation has the form

\displaystyle\Delta t\leq\frac{1}{\mu+\delta}.

(41)

b)

Let us assume that
\mu>0
. By adding up the equations of the system (
30
), the sum of the four compartments has the form

\displaystyle N^{n+1}

\displaystyle=S^{n+1}+E^{n+1}+I^{n+1}+R^{n+1}=S^{n}+E^{n}+I^{n}+R^{n}+

(42)

\displaystyle+\Delta t(\Pi^{n}-\mu(S^{n}+E^{n}+I^{n}+R^{n}))=N^{n}(1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)+\Delta t\cdot\Pi^{n}.

Similarly we can express
N^{n+1}
by using
N^{n-1}
,
N^{n-2}
and so on:

\displaystyle N^{n+1}

\displaystyle=N^{n-1}(1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)^{2}+\Delta t\cdot\Pi^{n-1}(1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)+\Delta t\cdot\Pi^{n}=

(43)

\displaystyle=N^{n-2}(1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)^{3}+\Delta t\cdot((1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)^{2}\Pi^{n-2}+(1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)\Pi^{n-1}+\Pi^{n})=

\displaystyle=\ldots=N^{0}(1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)^{n+1}+\Delta t\cdot((1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)^{n}\Pi^{0}+(1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)^{n-1}\Pi^{1}+\ldots+\Pi^{n})\leq

\displaystyle\leq N^{0}(1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)^{n+1}+\Delta tK\sum_{k=0}^{n}(1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)^{k}\leq

\displaystyle\leq N^{0}+\Delta t\cdot K\cdot\frac{1-(1-\Delta t\cdot\mu)^{n}}{\Delta t\cdot\mu}\leq N^{0}+\frac{K}{\mu},

where we used that
\Delta t\leq\dfrac{1}{\mu}
and
\Pi^{j}\leq K
for every index
j
.

Now we show the second claim. Since
\Pi^{k}\rightarrow P
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, then for an arbitrary fixed
\varepsilon>0

for which for every

It is clear that

Note that in the continuous case, we had

From the construction of

\Pi^{n}=0

\Pi^{n+1}=2

3.1
Higher order methods: strong stability preserving Runge-Kutta methods

Since the Euler method is only first order accurate (see Section
4
), we would like to apply higher-order methods to our system (
1
).

Let us consider a Runge-Kutta method in the Butcher-form
butcher
with coefficients
a_{i,j}
and
b_{j}
(
i,j=1,\dots,m
). Let us also use the notations
A
and
\boldsymbol{b}
for the matrix and the vector containing the previous coefficients, respectively. Also, define the matrix
\mathcal{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
\boldsymbol{b}^{T}&0\end{array}\right].

Also, let us use the notation
\mathcal{I}
for the
(m+1)
-dimensional identity matrix. Then, if there is such a value of
r>0
for which
(I+r\mathcal{K})
is invertible, then the Runge-Kutta method applied to equation

\displaystyle U^{\prime}(t)=F(U(t))

(51)

can be expressed in the canonical Shu-Osher form
shu1
,
shu2
,
shu3

\displaystyle\begin{split}U_{n-1}^{(i)}&=v_{i}U^{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{ij}\left(U_{n-1}^{(j)}+\frac{\tau}{r}F\left(U_{n-1}^{(j)}\right)\right),\qquad 1\leq i\leq m+1,\\
U^{n}&=U_{n-1}^{(m+1)},\end{split}

(52)

where the coefficients
\alpha_{ij}
and
v_{i}
are non-negative. These methods are usually called strong-stability preserving (or SSP) Runge-Kutta methods.
Let us use the notations
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{r}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{r}
for the matrix and the vector collecting the aforementioned coefficients, where we emphasized the dependence on the parameter of
r
(since different choices of
r
might result in different schemes).
The Shu-Osher representation with the largest value of
r
for which
(I+r\mathcal{K})^{-1}
exists is called optimal and the corresponding SSP coefficient is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\max\left\{r\geq 0\;|\;\exists\;(I+r\mathcal{K})^{-1}\text{ and }\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{r}\geq 0,\boldsymbol{v}_{r}\geq 0\right\}.

(53)

Then, an explicit Runge-Kutta method given in the optimal Shu-Osher form applied to equation (
1
) has the form

\displaystyle S_{n-1}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=v_{i}S^{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\left(S_{n-1}^{(j)}+\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\left(\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}-\mu S_{n-1}^{(j)}-f(I_{n-1}^{(j)})\cdot S_{n-1}^{(j)}\right)\right),

(54a)

\displaystyle E_{n-1}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=v_{i}E^{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\left(E_{n-1}^{(j)}+\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\left(f(I_{n-1}^{(j)})\cdot S_{n-1}^{(j)}-(\mu+\sigma)E_{n-1}^{(j)}\right)\right),

(54b)

\displaystyle I_{n-1}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=v_{i}I^{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\left(I_{n-1}^{(j)}+\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\left(\sigma E_{n-1}^{(j)}-(\mu+\gamma)I_{n-1}^{(j)}+\delta R_{n-1}^{(j)}\right)\right),

(54c)

\displaystyle R_{n-1}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=v_{i}R^{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\left(R_{n-1}^{(j)}+\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\left(\gamma I_{n-1}^{(j)}-(\mu+\delta)R_{n-1}^{(j)}\right)\right),

(54d)

\displaystyle S^{n}

\displaystyle=S_{n-1}^{(m+1)},

(54e)

\displaystyle E^{n}

\displaystyle=E_{n-1}^{(m+1)},

(54f)

\displaystyle I^{n}

\displaystyle=I_{n-1}^{(m+1)},

(54g)

\displaystyle R^{n}

\displaystyle=R_{n-1}^{(m+1)},

(54h)

where
1\leq i\leq m+1
.

The next theorem gives a sufficient condition for the timesteps under which the non-negativity property holds.

Theorem 3.2

Let us apply an explicit Runge-Kutta scheme given in the form (
54
).

a)

If the time-step satisfies

\displaystyle 0<\tau\leq\mathcal{C}\Delta t^{*}

(55)

(where
\Delta t^{*}
is the same as defined in Theorem
3.1
), then the non-negativity property holds.

b)

Let
N^{k}=S^{k}+E^{k}+I^{k}+R^{k}
(
k=0,1,\dots
). If
\mu>0
and
\tau\leq\mathcal{C}\dfrac{1}{\mu}
, then the estimate

N^{k}\leq\mathcal{N}:=N^{0}+\frac{K}{\mu}

(56)

also holds for every value of
k=0,1,\dots
. Moreover, if
\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Pi^{k}=P
, then
N^{k}\rightarrow\dfrac{P}{\mu}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Additionally, in the case
\mu=0
we have
N^{n}\leq N^{0}+n\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}K
for every
n
. Furthermore, if
\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Pi_{n}^{(j)}<\infty
, then
N^{n}
converges to a finite positive value as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and if
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Pi^{n}=P>0
, then
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}N^{n}=\infty
.

Proof

We prove the statements by induction.

a)

Let us assume that the non-negative property holds for
S^{k}
(
k=1,2,\dots n-1
). Then, equation (
54a
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle S_{n-1}^{(i)}=v_{i}S^{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\left(\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}+S_{n-1}^{(j)}\left(1+-\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu-\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}f(I_{n-1}^{(j)})\right)\right)

(57)

Then
S_{n-1}^{(1)}\geq 0
holds, and if
\tau\leq\mathcal{C}\Delta t^{*}
, then since
\tau\leq\dfrac{\mathcal{C}}{\mu+B}\leq\dfrac{\mathcal{C}}{\mu+f(I_{n-1}^{(j)})}
, the non-negativity property holds also for
i=k
if it was true for
i=1,2,\dots k-1
. Moreover,
f(I_{n-1}^{(j)})\leq B
. Therefore, the non-negativity holds for
S
.

Now let us assume that the non-negative property holds for
E^{k}
(
k=1,2,\dots n-1
). We can rewrite equation (
54b
) similarly as (
54a
):

\displaystyle E_{n-1}^{(i)}=v_{i}E^{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\left(\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}f(I_{n-1}^{(j)})\cdot S_{n-1}^{(j)}+E_{n-1}^{(j)}\left(1-\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}(\mu+\sigma)\right)\right)

(58)

Therefore,
S_{n-1}^{(1)}\geq 0
holds. Also, it is clear that the term
f(I_{n-1}^{(j)})\cdot S_{n-1}^{(j)}
is non-negative, and if
\tau\leq\mathcal{C}\Delta t^{*}
, then since
\tau\leq\dfrac{\mathcal{C}}{\mu+\sigma}
, the non-negativity property holds also for
i=k
if it was true for
i=1,2,\dots k-1
. Therefore, the non-negativity holds for
E
.

Now we prove the property for
I
and
R
. Let us assume that the non-negative property holds for
I^{k}
and
R^{k}
(
k=1,2,\dots n-1
). We can rewrite equations (
54c
) and (
54d
) as

\displaystyle I_{n-1}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=v_{i}I^{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\left(\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sigma E_{n-1}^{(j)}+\left(1-\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}(\mu+\gamma)\right)I_{n-1}^{(j)}+\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\delta R_{n-1}^{(j)}\right),

(59)

\displaystyle R_{n-1}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=v_{i}R_{n-1}^{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\left(\gamma\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}I_{n-1}^{(j)}+\left(1-\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}(\mu+\delta)\right)R_{n-1}^{(j)}\right).

It is easy to see that
I_{n-1}^{(1)},R_{n-1}^{(1)}\geq 0
. Moreover, we know that
E_{n-1}^{(j)}\geq 0
(
j=1,2,\dots,m+1
) by the previous claims. Now let us assume that
I_{n-1}^{(k)}\geq 0
and
R_{n-1}^{(k)}\geq 0
for
k=1,\dots,i-1
. If
\tau\leq\mathcal{C}\Delta t^{*}
, then
\tau\leq\dfrac{\mathcal{C}}{\mu+\gamma}
and
\tau\leq\dfrac{\mathcal{C}}{\mu+\delta}
is also true, meaning that the non-negative property also holds for
I_{n-1}^{(i)}
and
R_{n-1}^{(i)}
. This concludes this part of the proof.

b)

Let us assume that
\mu>0
. If we add up equations (
54a
)–(
54d
), then we get

\displaystyle N_{n-1}^{(i)}=

\displaystyle S_{n-1}^{(i)}+E_{n-1}^{(i)}+I_{n-1}^{(i)}+R_{n-1}^{(i)}=

(60)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{i}(S^{n-1}+E^{n-1}+I^{n-1}+R^{n-1})+

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\left(S_{n-1}^{(j)}+E_{n-1}^{(j)}+I_{n-1}^{(j)}+R_{n-1}^{(j)}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu(S_{n-1}^{(j)}+E_{n-1}^{(j)}+I_{n-1}^{(j)}+R_{n-1}^{(j)})\right)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{i}N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\left(N_{n-1}^{(j)}-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu N_{n-1}^{(j)}\right)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{i}N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}+\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}N_{n-1}^{(j)}\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle v_{i}N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}K\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}+\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}N_{n-1}^{(j)}.

Consistency requires that
\displaystyle v_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}=1
, meaning that we have

\displaystyle N_{n-1}^{(i)}\leq v_{i}N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}K(1-v_{i})+\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}N_{n-1}^{(j)}.

(61)

Consistency also means that
v_{1}=1
,
0\leq v_{i}\leq 1
and
0\leq\alpha_{ij}\leq 1
.

For the proof, we will use the following two lemmas (their proofs can be found in the Appendix):

Lemma 1

If we have a method of
m
steps, then

N^{n}=N_{n-1}^{(m+1)}\leq N^{n-1}A_{m+1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}KB_{m+1},

where

A_{1}=1,\qquad A_{i}=v_{i}+\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}A_{j}\quad(i=2,3,\dots,m),

and

B_{1}=0,\qquad B_{i}=1-v_{i}+\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}B_{j}\quad(i=2,3,\dots,m),

where
0\leq A_{i}\leq 1
and
0\leq B_{i}\leq 1
for
i=1,2,\dots m
.

Lemma 2

\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu B_{i}=1-A_{i}
holds for every
i=1,2,\dots m
.

Then, if we have an
m
-step method, by Lemma
1
the steps are

N^{n+1}\leq A_{m+1}N^{n}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}KB_{m+1}\leq

\leq A_{m+1}^{n+1}N^{0}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}KB_{m}(\left(A_{m+1}\right)^{n}+\left(A_{m+1}\right)^{n-1}+\dots+\left(A_{m+1}\right)^{2}+A_{m+1}+1)=

=\left(A_{m+1}\right)^{n+1}N^{0}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}KB_{m+1}\dfrac{1-\left(A_{m+1}\right)^{n+1}}{1-A_{m+1}}=

By Lemma
2
we get

=\left(A_{m+1}\right)^{n+1}N^{0}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}KB_{m+1}\dfrac{1-\left(A_{m+1}\right)^{n+1}}{\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu B_{m+1}}=\left(A_{m+1}\right)^{n+1}N^{0}+\dfrac{K}{\mu}\left(1-\left(A_{m+1}\right)^{n+1}\right).

By Lemma
1
we have

N^{n+1}\leq N^{0}+\dfrac{K}{\mu}.

For the second claim, we can use similar arguments as the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Namely, if
\Pi^{k}\rightarrow P
, then for any arbitrary fixed value of
\varepsilon
there exists an
N_{1}(\varepsilon)
index for which for every
n\geq N_{1}(\varepsilon)
we have
P-\varepsilon\leq N^{n}\leq P+\varepsilon
.

Now let us consider the following numerical scheme:
\underline{N}^{n}=N^{n}
for
n\leq N_{1}(\varepsilon)
and for
n>N_{1}(\varepsilon)
we have

\underline{N}^{n}=\underline{N}^{n-1}A_{m+1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}(P-\varepsilon)B_{m+1},

where
A_{m+1}
,
B_{m+1}
are as defined before. It can be seen by induction that
\underline{N}^{n}\leq N^{n}
. Moreover, the sequence
\underline{N}^{n}
converges, since the function

h_{2}(x)=xA_{m+1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}(P-\varepsilon)B_{m+1}

is a contraction, and it maps the interval
\left[0,N^{0}+\dfrac{K}{\mu}\right]
onto itself, and then we can use Banach’s fixed point theorem. The fixed point of this function is

x_{0}=\dfrac{\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}(P-\varepsilon)B_{m+1}}{1-A_{m+1}}=\dfrac{P-\varepsilon}{\mu},

where we used Lemma
2
. This means that
\underline{N}^{n}\rightarrow\dfrac{P-\varepsilon}{\mu}
, meaning that for the previous fixed value of
\varepsilon
there exists an index
N_{2}(\varepsilon)
for which
\underline{N}^{n}\geq\dfrac{P}{\mu}-\varepsilon
. Thus,
N^{n}>\underline{N}^{n}>\dfrac{P}{\mu}-\varepsilon
holds for
n>\max\{N_{1}(\varepsilon),N_{2}(\varepsilon)\}
.

By a similar argument, if we consider the sequence
\overline{N}^{n}
for which we have
\overline{N}^{n}=N^{n}
for
n\leq N_{1}(\varepsilon)
and for
n>N_{1}(\varepsilon)

\overline{N}^{n}=\overline{N}^{n-1}A_{m+1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}(P+\varepsilon)B_{m+1},

then
\overline{N}^{n}\geq N^{n}
and since
\overline{N}^{n}\rightarrow\dfrac{P+\varepsilon}{\mu}
, then there exists an index
N_{3}(\varepsilon)
for which
N^{n}\leq\overline{N}^{n}\leq\dfrac{P}{\mu}+\varepsilon
. Then, since for
n>\max\{N_{1}(\varepsilon),N_{2}(\varepsilon),N_{3}(\varepsilon)\}
we have
\dfrac{P}{\mu}-\varepsilon<N^{n}<\dfrac{P}{\mu}+\varepsilon
, by definition
N^{n}\rightarrow\dfrac{P}{\mu}
.

In the
\mu=0
case, the result of Lemma
1
reduces to

\displaystyle N^{n}=N^{(m)}\leq N^{n-1}\widetilde{A}_{m+1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}K\widetilde{B}_{m+1},

(62)

where since
\widetilde{A}_{1}=1
and
\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{i}=v_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\widetilde{A}_{j}
, by using
\displaystyle v_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}=1
we get that
\widetilde{A}_{i}=1
for every
j
index. Similarly, we have the
\widetilde{B}_{1}=0
and
\displaystyle\widetilde{B}_{i}=1-v_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\widetilde{B}_{j}
, and
0\leq\widetilde{B}_{i}\leq 1
also holds for every value of
j
. Then,

N^{n}\leq N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}K\widetilde{B}_{m+1}\leq N^{0}+n\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}K\widetilde{B}_{m+1}\leq N^{0}+n\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}K.

Moreover, in this case

N^{n}=N_{n-1}^{(m+1)}=v_{m+1}N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{m+1,j}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{m+1,j}N_{n-1}^{(j)}

Lemma 3

N_{n-1}^{(i)}=N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\gamma_{ij}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)},

where

\gamma_{ij}=\alpha_{ij}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ik}\gamma_{kj}.

(The proof of this claim can be found in the Appendix.)

Therefore, if
\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}\Pi_{n}^{(j)}<\infty
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}N^{n}=N^{0}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}\gamma_{mj}\Pi_{n}^{(j)}\leq N^{0}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}\Pi_{n}^{(j)}<\infty.

Here we used that
0\leq\gamma_{ij}\leq 1
, which can be seen by induction: for the first few elements it is true, and then if
\gamma_{ij}\leq 1
holds for
i<k
,
j<\ell
, then (here
k<\ell
)

\gamma_{k\ell}=\alpha_{k\ell}+\sum_{p=\ell+1}^{k-1}\alpha_{kp}\gamma_{p\ell}\leq\alpha_{k\ell}+\sum_{p=\ell+1}^{k-1}\alpha_{kp}\leq 1-v_{k}\leq 1.

Furthermore, if
\Pi^{n}\rightarrow P>0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, then for a fixed value of
0<\varepsilon<P
there exists an index
N_{4}(\varepsilon)
for which for every
n>N_{4}(\varepsilon)
we have
\Pi^{n}>P-\varepsilon
. Then, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}N^{n}=N^{0}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\gamma_{mj}\Pi_{n}^{(j)}=

=N^{0}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{4}(\varepsilon)}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\gamma_{mj}\Pi_{n}^{(j)}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{n=N_{4}(\varepsilon)+1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\gamma_{mj}\Pi_{n}^{(j)}>

>N^{0}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{4}(\varepsilon)}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\gamma_{mj}\Pi_{n}^{(j)}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{n=N_{4}(\varepsilon)+1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\gamma_{mj}(P-\varepsilon)=\infty,

where in the last step we used the fact that
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\gamma_{mj}(P-\varepsilon)
is a positive constant.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Remark 4

If our Runge-Kutta method is explicit, then
v_{i}=1
holds, meaning that the first stage of the method is always
X^{(1)}=X^{n}

\widetilde{S^{(1)}}:=S^{(2)}
,
\widetilde{S^{(2)}}:=S^{(3)}
etc. From now on we will also omit the use of these tildes.

Proof
(of Lemma
2
)

We prove the statement by induction. If
i=1
, then the statement is trivial. Moreover, for
i=2
we have

\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu B_{2}=\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu(1-v_{2})=\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\alpha_{21}

and similarly

1-A_{2}=1-\left(v_{2}+\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\alpha_{21}\right)=\alpha_{21}-\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\alpha_{21}=\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\alpha_{21}.

Now let us assume that the statement holds for
j=1,2,\dots i-1
and we would like to prove it for
j=i
. Then,

\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu B_{i}=\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu(1-v_{i})+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}B_{j}=

=\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\right)+\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu B_{j}=

Now we use the assumption that the statement holds for
j=1,2,\dots i-1
meaning that
\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu B_{j}=1-A_{j}
for
j=1,2,\dots i-1
:

=\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\right)+\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}(1-A_{j})=

=\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\right)+\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}A_{j}\right)=

=\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}A_{j}-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}A_{j}=

=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}-\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}A_{j}.

Similarly, for the right-hand side we have

1-A_{i}=1-\left(v_{i}+\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}A_{j}\right)=1-v_{i}-\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}A_{j}=

=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}-\left(1-\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\mu\right)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\alpha_{ij}A_{j},

which gives the statement of the lemma.

Proof
(of Lemma
3
)

We prove the statement by induction in
i
. It is clear that
N_{n-1}^{(1)}=N^{n-1}
and

N_{n-1}^{(2)}=v_{2}N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\alpha_{21}\Pi_{n-1}^{(1)}+\alpha_{21}N_{n-1}^{(1)}=N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\gamma_{21}\Pi_{n-1}^{(1)}.

Then, if the statement holds for
i=1,2,\dots,\ell-1
, then we prove it for
i=\ell
:

N_{n-1}^{(\ell)}=v_{\ell}N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{\ell j}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{\ell j}N_{n-1}^{(j)}=

=v_{\ell}N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{\ell j}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{\ell j}\left(N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji}\Pi_{n-1}^{(i)}\right)=

=\left(v_{\ell}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{\ell j}\right)N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{\ell j}\left(\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji}\Pi_{n-1}^{(i)}\right)=

=N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{\ell j}\left(\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji}\Pi_{n-1}^{(i)}\right).

The last term can be rewritten as

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{\ell j}\left(\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji}\Pi_{n-1}^{(i)}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{\ell j}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}+\alpha_{\ell j}\left(\gamma_{j1}\Pi_{n-1}^{(1)}+\dots+\gamma_{j,j-1}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j-1)}\right)=

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\ell 1}\Pi_{n-1}^{(1)}+\alpha_{\ell 2}\Pi_{n-1}^{(2)}+\alpha_{\ell 2}\gamma_{21}\Pi_{n-1}^{(1)}+\alpha_{\ell 3}\Pi_{n-1}^{(3)}+\alpha_{\ell 3}\gamma_{31}\Pi_{n-1}^{(1)}+\alpha_{\ell 3}\gamma_{32}\Pi_{n-1}^{(2)}+\dots+

\displaystyle+\alpha_{\ell,\ell-1}\Pi_{n-1}^{(\ell-1)}+\alpha_{\ell,\ell-1}\left(\gamma_{\ell-1,1}\Pi_{n-1}^{(1)}+\dots+\gamma_{\ell-1,\ell-2}\Pi_{n-1}^{(\ell-2)}\right)=

\displaystyle=\Pi_{n-1}^{(1)}\left(\alpha_{\ell 1}+\alpha_{\ell 2}\gamma_{21}+\alpha_{\ell 3}\gamma_{31}+\dots+\alpha_{\ell,\ell-1}\gamma_{\ell-1,1}\right)+

\displaystyle+\Pi_{n-1}^{(2)}\left(\alpha_{\ell 2}+\alpha_{\ell 2}\gamma_{22}+\alpha_{\ell 3}\gamma_{32}+\dots+\alpha_{\ell,\ell-1}\gamma_{\ell-1,2}\right)+\dots+\alpha_{\ell,\ell-1}\Pi_{n-1}^{(\ell-1)}=

=\Pi_{n-1}^{(1)}\gamma_{\ell 1}+\Pi_{n-1}^{(2)}\gamma_{\ell 2}+\dots+\Pi_{n-1}^{(\ell-1)}\gamma_{\ell,\ell-1}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\gamma_{\ell j}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)}.

Then,

N^{(\ell)}=N^{n-1}+\dfrac{\tau}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\gamma_{\ell j}\Pi_{n-1}^{(j)},

which concludes the proof of the lemma.

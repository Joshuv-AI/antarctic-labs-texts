Gravitational effects on oscillon lifetimes
Hong-Yi Zhang

Abstract
Many scalar field theories with attractive self-interactions support exceptionally long-lived, spatially localized and time-periodic field configurations called oscillons. A detailed study of their longevity is important for understanding their applications in cosmology. In this paper, we study gravitational effects on the decay rate and lifetime of dense oscillons, where self-interactions are more or at least equally important compared with gravitational interactions. As examples, we consider the α \alpha -attractor T-model of inflation and the axion monodromy model, where the potentials become flatter than quadratic at large field values beyond some characteristic field distance F F from the minimum. For oscillons with field amplitudes of 𝒪 ⁡ ( F ) \mathcal{O}(F) and for F ≪ 0.1 ​ M pl F\ll 0.1M_{\mathrm{pl}} , we find that their evolution is almost identical to cases where gravity is ignored. For F ∼ 0.1 ​ M pl F\sim 0.1M_{\mathrm{pl}} , however, including gravitational interactions reduces the lifetime slightly.

2.2
A virial theorem and small parameters

Assume that oscillons are single-frequency objects like Q-balls and Boson stars
Lee:1991ax
, then one way to derive a virial theorem is to use the variational principle. The Legendre transformation

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{osc}}=\omega N_{\mathrm{osc}}-E_{\mathrm{osc}}

(20)

defines a functional of
\phi_{1}
and a function of
\omega
(one may recognize that
F_{\mathrm{osc}}
is just the Lagrangian). The variation of
F_{\mathrm{osc}}
in terms of
\phi_{1}
by keeping
\omega
fixed gives the profile equation of oscillons (
8
). A virial theorem can be obtained by considering the variation
\phi_{1}(r)\rightarrow\phi_{1}(\lambda r)
for an oscillons solution. By setting
\left(\partial F_{\mathrm{osc}}/\partial\lambda\right)_{\omega}=0

at
\lambda=1
, we find

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{S}}/3+E_{\mathrm{V}}=E_{\mathrm{K}}~,

(21)

where the surface energy, potential energy and kinetic energy are defined

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{S}}=\int\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{r}\phi_{1})^{2}d^{3}r~,\quad E_{\mathrm{V}}=\int\left(\frac{1}{4}m^{2}\phi_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}U_{0}\right)d^{3}r~,\quad E_{\mathrm{K}}=\int\frac{1}{4}\omega^{2}\phi_{1}^{2}~d^{3}r~.

(22)

For oscillons with
\omega\lesssim m
, we can identify three small parameters immediately

\displaystyle\epsilon_{r}\equiv 1-\omega^{2}/m^{2}~,\quad\epsilon_{V}\sim\frac{U_{0}}{m^{2}\phi_{1}^{2}/2}~,\quad\epsilon_{\xi}\sim\frac{\xi_{j}}{\phi_{1}}~.

(23)

The parameter
\epsilon_{r}
is a measure of how relativistic the particles inside the oscillon are. Equation (
8
) implies that at large radius
\phi_{1}(r)\propto r^{-1}\exp\left[-(m^{2}-\omega^{2})^{1/2}r\right]
, hence a typical spatial derivative brings a factor
\sqrt{\epsilon_{r}}m
, that is,
\partial_{r}\phi_{1}\sim-\sqrt{\epsilon_{r}}m\phi_{1}
, and

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\phi_{1}\ll\omega^{2}\phi_{1}~.

(24)

This means that the particles that make up the oscillon are
non-relativistic
. And from equations (
12
) and (
21
), we see
\epsilon_{\xi}\sim-\epsilon_{V}
and
\epsilon_{V}\sim-\epsilon_{r}
.

For oscillons with
\omega\ll m
, we may not regard surface energy as a small quantity anymore. Take the tanh potential in (
1
) for example and assume a Gaussian profile

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(r)=C~e^{-r^{2}/R^{2}}~,

(25)

where
C\gg F
. Then each energy component becomes

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{S}}\sim C^{2}R~,\quad E_{\mathrm{K}}\sim C^{2}R^{3}\omega^{2}\sim\frac{N_{\mathrm{osc}}^{2}}{C^{2}R^{3}}~,\quad E_{\mathrm{V}}\sim R_{\mathrm{V}}^{3}m^{2}F^{2}~,

(26)

where we have taken advantage of the flatness of the potential at large
\phi
and
R_{\mathrm{V}}
is the length scale satisfying
\phi_{1}(R_{\mathrm{V}})\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
, i.e.

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{V}}\sim R\log^{1/2}\left(\frac{C}{F}\right)\sim R~.

(27)

By setting
\partial E_{\mathrm{osc}}/\partial R=\partial E_{\mathrm{osc}}/\partial C=0
and keeping
N_{\mathrm{osc}}
fixed, we obtain

\displaystyle R\sim\omega^{-1}~,\quad C\sim\omega^{-1}~.

(28)

We see that three components of energy now are all comparable, and thus oscillon particles are relativistic. This phenomenon has been witnessed numerically in the context of dense axion stars
Visinelli:2017ooc
;
Eby:2019ntd

5.2
The axion monodromy model

In constrast with the
\alpha
-attractor T-model, the subleading channel of decay rates
\Gamma_{5}
is never comparable with the leading channel
\Gamma_{3}
in the axion monodromy model. The longevity of oscillons in this case (whose lifetime
\sim 10^{8}\,m^{-1}
in Minkowski spacetime) is due to the dramatic suppresion of
\Gamma_{3}
just before their final collapse at
\omega_{\mathrm{crit}}
. Consequently, there are two factors that determine the lifetime of oscillons, the value of
\omega_{\mathrm{crit}}
and the decay rate around
\omega_{\mathrm{crit}}
.

In figure
4
(left panel), we show that the values of
\omega_{\mathrm{crit}}
are inversely related to
F
. This seems an indication of shorter lifetimes for stronger gravitational effects because oscillons now spend less time around
\omega_{\mathrm{crit}}
. A good match between analytics and numerics implies that the stability condition (
65

Based on our semi-analytical framework, we calculate oscillon decay rates for
F=0.0001M_{\mathrm{pl}}
and
F=0.07M_{\mathrm{pl}}
in the right panel. Compared with the very weak-field gravity, it is shown that the decay rate around
\omega_{\mathrm{crit}}
is more suppressed for

18

J_{3}
at
\omega_{\mathrm{crit}}
typically has a Gaussian-like shape with a negative amplitude, the introduction of the positive term
2\omega_{2}\phi_{1}\Phi_{2}
diminishes the magnitude of the effective source, and thus tends to reduce the decay rate and increase the total lifetime.

To determine which factor dominates, we integrate out the decay rate and show the lifetime in an inset of the right panel of figure

Nevertheless, the oscillon is still too long-lived to be simulated with our current numerical algorithms. And we leave this as a testable prediction for a future numerical experiment.

6
Conclusions

In this paper we generalize the method developed in our previous work
Zhang:2020bec
to include gravitational interactions, by expanding equations in terms of five small parameters

\displaystyle\epsilon_{r}\sim\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}}{m^{2}}~,\quad\epsilon_{V}\sim\frac{V_{\mathrm{nl}}}{m^{2}\phi_{\mathrm{osc}}^{2}}~,\quad\epsilon_{\xi}\sim\frac{\xi}{\phi_{\mathrm{osc}}}~,\quad\epsilon_{\phi}\sim\frac{\phi_{\mathrm{osc}}}{M_{\mathrm{pl}}}~,\quad\epsilon_{g}\sim\Phi~,

(71)

where
\epsilon_{r}\sim-\epsilon_{V}\sim\epsilon_{\xi}
and
\epsilon_{g}\sim\epsilon_{\phi}^{2}/\epsilon_{r}
for dense oscillons. We have shown in
Zhang:2020bec
that keeping just the leading order of
\epsilon_{r},\epsilon_{V}
and
\epsilon_{\xi}
gives accurate predictions of oscillon decay rates and lifetimes in Minkowski spacetime. Thus in this paper, we explore the impacts of
\epsilon_{\phi}
and
\epsilon_{g}
by keeping all other parameters fixed. The application of our semi-analytical and perturbative framework is based on two assumptions, the non-relativistic limit of oscillons and the weak-field limit of gravity, namely
\epsilon_{r}\lesssim 0.1
and
\epsilon_{\phi}\lesssim 0.1
.

In order to explore the gravitational effects on oscillon lifetimes, we discuss two well-motivated and representative models in detail, the
\alpha
-attractor T-model of inflation and the axion monodromy model with potentials given by (
1
). As presented in figure
2
,
3
and
4
, our results for
F\sim 0.1M_{\mathrm{pl}}
show the following:

•

In the
\alpha
-attractor T-model, oscillon lifetimes are dominated by an exceptionally stable intermediate configuration, which is visualized as a dip structure in figure
1
. We show that the existence of gravity decreases the value of
\omega_{\rm dip}
and increases the decay rate at
\omega_{\rm dip}
. As a result, oscillon lifetimes are reduced when gravity becomes more important.

•

In the axion monodromy model, oscillons are the most stable just before their final collapse at
\omega_{\mathrm{crit}}
. It is shown that stronger gravitational effects suppress the decay rate around
\omega_{\mathrm{crit}}
, which tends to stablize oscillons, while diminishing the value of
\omega_{\mathrm{crit}}
, which tends to decrease the lifetime. By explicitly integrating out the decay rate, we find that the latter factor dominates and oscillon lifetimes are reduced slightly.

In both examples we have considered, the evolution of an oscillon is almost identical to that in Minkowski spacetime if
F\ll 0.1M_{\mathrm{pl}}
. Therefore, in such cases, one may study the decay rate and dynamics of at least single oscillons by ignoring gravity.

For stronger-field gravity, i.e.
\epsilon_{\phi}\gtrsim 0.1
or
F\gtrsim 0.1M_{\mathrm{pl}}
, our equations with leading-order corrections are no longer accurate. Also note that both
\epsilon_{\phi}
and
\epsilon_{g}
increase rapidly when we probe smaller frequences. Thus one should keep higher-order perturbations of
\epsilon_{\phi}
and
\epsilon_{g}
to obtain more reliable conclusions. But in this regime, exotic phenomena such as black hole formation
Helfer:2016ljl
;
Muia:2019coe
;
Nazari:2020fmk
might be more interesting than oscillon lifetimes. In future work, we will further show that a new phenomenon of migration from dense to dilute oscillons makes the notion of oscillon lifetimes less meaningful.

Appendix A
Fourier cosine coefficients of scalar potentials

In order to build some intuition of how
U_{j},J_{j}
and
M_{j}
behave, let us consider a symmetric polynomial potential of the general form

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{nl}}(\phi)=\sum_{n=4}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n}}{n!}\phi^{n}~,

(72)

where
n
is even. By using the oscillon profile
\phi_{\mathrm{osc}}=\phi_{1}\cos(\omega t)
and the identity

\displaystyle\cos^{n}(\omega t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\frac{n!}{(n-k)!k!}\cos[(n-2k)\omega t]~,

(73)

we find the potentials can be written in terms of Fourier cosine series

\displaystyle U=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=4}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n}}{2^{n}\left(\frac{n}{2}!\right)^{2}}\phi_{1}^{n}+\sum_{n=4}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n}}{2^{n-1}\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)!\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)!}\phi_{1}^{n}\cos(2\omega t)

(74)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=4}^{\infty}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n}}{2^{n-1}\left(\frac{n+j}{2}\right)!\left(\frac{n-j}{2}\right)!}\phi_{1}^{n}\cos(j\omega t)~,

(75)

\displaystyle M=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n+2}}{2^{n}\left(\frac{n}{2}!\right)^{2}}\phi_{1}^{n}+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n+2}}{2^{n-1}\left(\frac{n+j}{2}\right)!\left(\frac{n-j}{2}\right)!}\phi_{1}^{n}\cos(j\omega t)~,

(76)

where
j
and
n
are even, and

\displaystyle J=\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n+1}}{2^{n-1}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)!\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)!}\phi_{1}^{n}\cos(\omega t)+\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n+1}}{2^{n-1}\left(\frac{n+j}{2}\right)!\left(\frac{n-j}{2}\right)!}\phi_{1}^{n}\cos(j\omega t)~,

(77)

where
j
and
n
are odd.

Appendix B
Numerical algorithms

In this appendix we introduce briefly our numerical algorithm. For convenience we will set
M_{\mathrm{pl}}\equiv 1
so that all the mass is in unit of reduced Planck mass. And to make the equations more accessible to coding, define

\displaystyle A\equiv e^{-\Psi}~,\quad B\equiv e^{\Phi+\Psi}~,\quad v\equiv B^{-1}\phi_{,0}~,\quad u\equiv\phi_{,1}~.

(78)

Then the equation of motion of
\phi
becomes

\displaystyle\phi_{,0}=vB~,\quad u_{,0}=(vB)_{,1}~,\quad v_{,0}=r^{-2}(r^{2}Bu)_{,1}-A^{2}BV^{\prime}(\phi)~,

(79)

where the metric can be given by 00 and 11 components of Einstein equations, i.e.

\displaystyle A_{,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{A}{2}\left[\frac{1-A^{2}}{r}+\frac{r}{2}\left(v^{2}+u^{2}+2A^{2}V\right)\right]~,

(80)

\displaystyle B_{,1}

\displaystyle=B\left(\frac{A^{2}-1}{r}-rA^{2}V\right)~.

(81)

One is also recommended to rescale the fields in unit of
F
to reduce the roundoff errors brought by small numbers when
F\ll M_{\mathrm{pl}}
. The oscillon energy can be calculated by

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{osc}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}B(v^{2}+u^{2}+2A^{2}V)~4\pi r^{2}dr~.

(82)

To maintain the smoothness at the center
r=0
, we must require

\displaystyle u=v_{,1}=0~,\quad A=1~,\quad A_{,1}=B_{,1}=0~.

(83)

To appropriately account for the origin, we set a spatial grid
r_{n}=(n-1/2)dr
with a fictitious point
r_{0}=-dr/2
. The inner boundary conditions then become a parity condition:
\phi,v,A,B
are even and
u
is odd. For the outer boundary conditions we adopt the radiative boundary conditions
Alcubierre:2000xu
by assuming that the dynamical variables
\phi
and
v
behave like spherical waves
\phi(t,r)=f(r-t)/r
. In practice, we will use this in the differential form

\displaystyle\phi_{,0}+\phi_{,1}+\phi/r=0~,\quad v_{,0}+v_{,1}+v/r=0~,\quad v+u+\phi/r=0~,

(84)

for
\phi,v,u
repectively. Note that one actually does not need boundary conditions for
\phi
, but we find that integrating
\phi
all the way to the boundary point will inevitably generate instabilities in long-time simulations. Finally, we note that the Schwarzchild metric should be recovered at large
r
hence
A
and
B
satisfy

\displaystyle B(r\rightarrow\infty)=1/A^{2}(r\rightarrow\infty)~.

(85)

We adopt 4th-order Runge-Kutta method as the time integrator to evolve equations (
79
), while the spatial derivatives are discretized by the standard 4th-order centered difference
Zlochower:2005bj
. The 6th-order Kreiss-Oliger dissipation with strength parameter
\epsilon=0.001

alcubierre2008introduction
is added except boundary points to avoid shock waves while the accuracy is still remained. The boundary condition (
84
) is discretized by finite difference methods, specifically a 2nd-order upwind method
alcubierre2008introduction

\displaystyle v^{m+1}_{n}=\frac{1}{1+dt/dr}\left[\left(1-\frac{dt}{dr}-\frac{2dt}{r_{n}}\right)v^{m}_{n}+\left(1+\frac{dt}{dr}\right)v^{m}_{n-1}-\left(1-\frac{dt}{dr}\right)v^{m+1}_{n-1}\right]~,

(86)

where
m,n
are the temporal and spatial indices respectively. Once
\phi,u,v
have been advanced for one time level, we use the 4th-order Runge-Kutta method to integrate (
80
) outwards and (
81
) inwards to get
A,B
. The initial values of
B
are obtained by using the Schwarzchild condition (
85
).

We typically set the boundary at a finite value
r_{f}
, which should be significantly larger than the oscillon size, and
dr=2dt
to satisfy the Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy condition. All the physical quantities are averaged over a time window
T_{\mathrm{ave}}=300~\mathrm{m^{-1}}
, which is much larger than one period
2\pi/\omega
but smaller than oscillon lifetime, unless otherwise stated. We have checked that slight changes of parameters (i.e.
dt,dr,r_{f},\epsilon,T_{\mathrm{ave}}
) do not affect the results significantly.

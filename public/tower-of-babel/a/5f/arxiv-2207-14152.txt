Quantization for the mixtures of overlap probability distributions
1 Asha Barua, 2 Angelina Chavera, 3 Ivan Djordjevic, 4 Valerie Manzano, 5 Sergio Soto Quintero, 6 Mrinal Kanti Roychowdhury, 7 Hilda Tejeda

Abstract
. Mixtures of probability distributions, also known as mixed distributions, are an exciting new area for optimal quantization. In this paper, we have considered a mixed distribution which is generated by overlap uniform distributions. For this mixed distribution we determine the optimal sets of n n -means and the n n th quantization errors for all positive integers n n .

60Exx, 94A34.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space equipped with a metric
\|\cdot\|
compatible with the Euclidean topology. Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be two Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, a Borel probability measure
P
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called a
mixture
, or a
mixed distribution
generated by
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
associated with a probability vector
(p,1-p)
if
P:=pP_{1}+(1-p)P_{2}
, where
0<p<1
. The
n
th
quantization
error
for
P
, with respect to the squared Euclidean distance, is defined by

V_{n}:=V_{n}(P)=\inf\Big\{V(P;\alpha):\alpha\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},1\leq\text{ card}(\alpha)\leq n\Big\},

where
V(P;\alpha)=\int\min_{a\in\alpha}\|x-a\|^{2}dP(x)
represents the distortion error due to the set
\alpha
with respect to the probability distribution
P
.
A set
\alpha\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called an optimal set of
n
-means for
P
if
V_{n}(P)=V(P;\alpha)
. It is known that for a Borel probability measure
P
if its support contains infinitely many elements and
\int\|x\|^{2}dP(x)
is finite, then an optimal set of
n
-means always has exactly
n
-elements
[
AW
,
GKL
,
GL1
,
GL2
]
. The elements of an optimal set of
n
-means are called
optimal quantizers
. For some work in this direction, one can see
[
CR
,
DR1
,
DR2
,
GL3
,
L1
,
R1
,
R2
,
R3
,
R4
,
R5
,
R6
,
RR1
]
.

Let us now state the following proposition (see
[
GG
,
GL1
]
):

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\alpha
be an optimal set of
n
-means for
P
, and
a\in\alpha
. Then,

(i)

P(M(a|\alpha))>0
,
(ii)

P(\partial M(a|\alpha))=0
,
(iii)

a=E(X:X\in M(a|\alpha))
,
where
M(a|\alpha)
is the Voronoi region of
a\in\alpha,
i.e.,
M(a|\alpha)
is the set of all elements
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which are closest to
a
among all the elements in
\alpha
.

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be two uniform distributions, respectively, on the intervals given by

[0,1]
, and
[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
. Notice that
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
have supports overlapped over the interval
[\frac{1}{2},1]
. In this paper for the mixed distribution
P:=\frac{1}{2}P_{1}+\frac{1}{2}P_{2}
we determine the optimal sets of
n
-means and the
n
th quantization errors for all positive integers
n
. The main significance of this paper is that using the similar technique, given in this paper, one can investigate the optimal sets of
n
-means and the
n
th quantization errors for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
for any mixed distribution generated by overlap probability distributions.

2.
basic preliminaries

Let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be the respective density functions for the uniform distributions
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
defined on the closed intervals
[0,1]
and
[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
. Then,

f_{1}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\text{ if }x\in[0,1],\\
0&\text{otherwise};\end{array}\right.\text{ and }f_{2}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\text{ if }x\in[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}],\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Let us consider the mixed distribution
P:=pP_{1}+(1-p)P_{2}
, where
0<p<1
. Notice that
P
has support the closed interval
[0,\frac{3}{2}]
, and the component probabilities
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
have overlaps on the interval
[\frac{1}{2},1]
. Since
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are the density functions for the probability distributions
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, respectively, we have

dP_{1}(x)=P_{1}(dx)=f_{1}(x)dx=dx,\text{ and }dP_{2}(x)=P_{2}(dx)=f_{2}(x)dx=dx.

Notice that if
x\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
, then
dP(x)=p\,dP_{1}(x)=pdx
; if
x\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, then

dP(x)=pdP_{1}(x)+(1-p)dP_{2}(x)=pf_{1}(x)dx+(1-p)f_{2}(x)dx=dx;

on the other hand, if
x\in[1,\frac{3}{2}]
, then
dP(x)=(1-p)dP_{2}(x)=(1-p)dx
. Let us now define a function
f
on the real line
\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}p&\text{ if }x\in[0,\frac{1}{2}],\\
1&\text{ if }x\in[\frac{1}{2},1],\\
(1-p)&\text{ if }x\in[1,\frac{3}{2}],\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Notice that the function
f
satisfies the following properties to be a density function:

f(x)\geq 0\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R},\text{ and }\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)dx=1;

in fact, the mixed distribution
P
can now be identified as a probability distribution on
\mathbb{R}
with the density function
f
, i.e., for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
dP(x)=P(dx)=f(x)dx
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
P
be a probability distribution with the density function
f
, and
X
be a random variable with distribution
P
. Then, its expectation and variance are respectively given by
E(X)=\frac{2-p}{2}
and
V(X)=\frac{1}{12}(-3p^{2}+3p+1)
.

Proof.

We have

E(X)=\int xdP=p\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}x\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}x\,dx+(1-p)\int_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}x\,dx=\frac{2-p}{2},

and

\displaystyle V(X)=\int(x-E(X))^{2}dP

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x-\frac{2-p}{2})^{2}\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}(x-\frac{2-p}{2})^{2}\,dx+(1-p)\int_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}(x-\frac{2-p}{2})^{2}\,dx,

implying

V(X)=\frac{1}{12}(-3p^{2}+3p+1)
, and thus, the lemma is yielded.
∎

Note 2.2
.

Lemma
2.1
implies that the optimal set of one-mean is the set
\{\frac{2-p}{2}\}
, and the corresponding quantization error is the variance
V:=V(X)
of a random variable with distribution
P
. For a subset
J
of
\mathbb{R}
with
P(J)>0
,
by
P(\cdot|_{J})
, we denote the conditional probability given that
J
is occurred, i.e.,
P(\cdot|_{J})=P(\cdot\cap J)/P(J)
, in other words, for any Borel subset
B
of
\mathbb{R}
we have
P(B|_{J})=\frac{P(B\cap J)}{P(J)}
.

The following proposition that appears in
[
BFGLR
]
plays an important role in this paper. For the completeness of the paper we also give the proof of it.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
such that
P
is uniformly distributed over a closed interval
[a,b]
with a constant density function
f
such that
f(x)=t
for all
x\in[a,b]
, where
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the optimal set
\alpha_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))
of
n
-means and the corresponding quantization error
V_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))
of
n
-means for the probability distribution
P(\cdot|_{[a,b]})
are, respectively, given by

\alpha_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]})):=\Big\{a+\frac{(2j-1)(b-a)}{2n}:1\leq j\leq n\Big\},\text{ and }V_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))=\frac{(b-a)^{3}t}{12n^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{n}:=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{n}\}
be an optimal set of
n
-means for the probability distribution
P
with a constant density function
f
on
[a,b]
such that
f(x)=t
for all
x\in[a,b]
, where
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, proceedings analogously as
[
RR2
, Theorem 2.1.1]
, we can show that
a_{j}=a+\frac{(2j-1)(b-a)}{2n}
implying

\alpha_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))=\Big\{a+\frac{(2j-1)(b-a)}{2n}:1\leq j\leq n\Big\}.

Notice that the probability density function is constant, and the Voronoi regions of the points
a_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
are of equal lengths. This yields the fact that the distortion errors due to each
a_{j}
are equal. Hence, the
n
th quantization error is given by

\displaystyle V_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))

\displaystyle=\int\min_{a\in\alpha_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))}(x-a)^{2}dP=nt\int_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})}(x-a_{1})^{2}dx=nt\int_{a}^{a+\frac{b-a}{n}}(x-(a+\frac{b-a}{2n}))^{2}dx

implying

V_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))=\frac{(b-a)^{3}t}{12n^{2}}.

Thus, the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

The following proposition plays an important role in the paper.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
such that
P
is uniformly distributed over a closed interval
[a,b]
with a constant density function
f
such that
f(x)=t
for all
x\in[a,b]
, where
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\alpha_{n+1}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]})):=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{n}<b\}
be an optimal set of
(n+1)
-means such that it always contains the endpoint
b
of the interval
[a,b]
. Then,

a_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}a+\frac{(2j-1)(b-a_{1})}{2n}&\text{ for }1\leq j\leq n,\\
b&\text{ if }j=n+1,\end{array}\right.

and the corresponding quantization error is given by

V_{n+1}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))=\frac{t}{12n^{2}}(b-a_{1})^{3}+\frac{t}{3}(a_{1}-a)^{3}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{n+1}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]})):=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{n}<b\}
be an optimal set of
(n+1)
-means for the probability distribution
P(\cdot|_{[a,b]})
on the closed interval
[a,b]
. Let us first prove the following claim.

Claim.
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}
are uniformly distributed over the closed interval
[a,\frac{1}{2}(a_{n}+b)]
.

Recall that the points in an optimal set are the conditional expectations in their own Voronoi regions. Thus,

a_{1}=E(X:X\in[a,\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})])=\frac{1}{4}(2a+a_{1}+a_{2})\text{ implying }a_{2}-a_{1}=2(a_{1}-a).

Set
a_{m+1}:=b
. Then, for
2\leq i\leq m
,

a_{i}=E(X:X\in[\frac{1}{2}(a_{i-1}+a_{i}),\frac{1}{2}(a_{i}+a_{i+1})])=\frac{1}{4}(a_{i-1}+2a_{i}+a_{i+1}),

yielding
a_{i}-a_{i-1}=a_{i+1}-a_{i}
. Hence,

a_{2}-a_{1}=a_{3}-a_{2}=\cdots=b-a_{m}=2(a_{1}-a).

Notice that
b-a_{m}=2(a_{1}-a)
implies that
\frac{1}{2}(a_{m}+b)-a_{m}=a_{1}-a
.
Thus, we deduce that
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}
are uniformly distributed over the closed interval
[a,\frac{1}{2}(a_{n}+b)]
, which is the claim.
Due to the claim, using Proposition
2.3
, we have

a_{j}=\{a+\frac{(2j-1)}{2n}(\frac{1}{2}(a_{n}+b)-a)\text{ for }1\leq j\leq n\}

yielding

\displaystyle a_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}a+\frac{(2j-1)}{2n}(\frac{1}{2}(a_{n}+b)-a)&\text{ for }1\leq j\leq n,\\
b&\text{ if }j=n+1.\end{array}\right.

Notice that

b-\frac{1}{2}(a_{n}+b)=a_{1}-a
yielding

\frac{1}{2}(a_{n}+b)-a=b-a_{1}.

Hence, by (
2
), we have

a_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}a+\frac{(2j-1)(b-a_{1})}{2n}&\text{ for }1\leq j\leq n,\\
b&\text{ if }j=n+1.\end{array}\right.

To find the quantization error we proceed as follows: Since the points
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}
are uniformly distributed over the closed interval
[a,\frac{1}{2}(a_{n}+b)]
, by Proposition
2.3
, the quantization error contributed by the points
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}
over the closed interval
[a,\frac{1}{2}(a_{n}+b)]
is given by

(7)

\frac{(\frac{1}{2}(a_{n}+b)-a)^{3}t}{12n^{2}}=\frac{t}{12n^{2}}(b-a_{1})^{3}.

The quantization error contributed by
b
in the closed interval
[\frac{1}{2}(a_{n}+b),b]
is given by

(8)

\int_{\frac{1}{2}(a_{n}+b)}^{b}(x-b)^{2}dP=t\int_{b+a-a_{1}}^{b}(x-b)^{2}\,dx=\frac{t}{3}(a_{1}-a)^{3}.

By (
7
) and (
8
), we have

V_{n+1}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))=\frac{t}{12n^{2}}(b-a_{1})^{3}+\frac{t}{3}(a_{1}-a)^{3}.

Thus, the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

3.
Optimal quantization for the mixed distribution
P
when
p=\frac{1}{2}

Recall that the mixed distribution
P
is identified as a probability distribution with density function
f
. If
p=\frac{1}{2}
, then
E(X)=\frac{3}{4}
and
V(X)=\frac{7}{48}
, i.e., the optimal set of one-mean for
p=\frac{1}{2}
is
\{\frac{3}{4}\}
and the corresponding quantization error is
V(X)=\frac{7}{48}
. For
p=\frac{1}{2}
, the density function
f
for
P
represented by (
3
) reduces to

\displaystyle f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }x\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]\cup[1,\frac{3}{2}],\\
1&\text{ if }x\in[\frac{1}{2},1],\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Notice that the probability measure
P
is ‘symmetric’ about the point
\frac{3}{4}
, i.e., if two intervals of equal lengths are
equidistant from the point
\frac{3}{4}
, then they have the same
P
-measure (see Figure
1
).

Remark 3.1
.

Since the probability distribution is symmetric about the point
\frac{3}{4}
, without any loss of generality we can always assume that if
\alpha_{n}
is an optimal set of
n
-means, then for an odd positive integer
n
the point
\frac{3}{4}\in\alpha_{n}
and all other elements in
\alpha_{n}
are equally distributed on both sides of
\frac{3}{4}
; on the other hand, if
n
is an even positive integer, than all the elements in the optimal set will be equally distributed on both sides of
\frac{3}{4}
. Thus, we see that
n
is even or odd, in any case, an optimal set
\alpha_{n}
of
n
-means contains equal number of points from both sides of the point
\frac{3}{4}
(see Figure
2
).

f
for the nonuniform probability distribution
P
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The optimal set of two-means is
\{\frac{7}{16},\frac{17}{16}\}
with quantization error
V_{2}=\frac{37}{768}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:=\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
be an optimal set of two means. Since the points in an optimal set are the conditional expectations in their own Voronoi region, we can assume that
0<a_{1}<a_{2}<\frac{3}{2}
. Again, due to symmetry of the probability distribution
P
about the point
\frac{3}{4}
, we can assume that the boundary
\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})
of the Voronoi regions of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
passes through the midpoint
\frac{3}{4}
of the support of
P
. Thus, we have

a_{1}=E(X:X\in[0,\frac{3}{4}])=\frac{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}x\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}x\,dx}{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}1\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}1\,dx}=\frac{7}{16},

and since
\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})=\frac{3}{4}
, we have
a_{2}=\frac{17}{16}
. Again, due to symmetry, the quantization error for two-means is given by

V_{2}=2\Big(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x-\frac{7}{16})^{2}\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}(x-\frac{7}{16})^{2}\,dx\Big)=\frac{37}{768}.

Thus the proof of the proposition is complete (also see Figure
2
).
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

The optimal set of three-means is
\{\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{4}\}
with quantization error
V_{3}=\frac{1}{48}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be an optimal set of three-means.
As mentioned in Remark
3.1
, we can assume that
\frac{3}{4}\in\alpha
. Let the other two elements in
\alpha
be
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
such that
0<a_{1}<\frac{3}{4}<a_{2}<\frac{3}{2}
. Now, the boundary of the Voronoi regions of
a_{1}
and
\frac{3}{4}
is
\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+\frac{3}{4})
. The following two cases can arise:

Case 1.
\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+\frac{3}{4})\leq\frac{1}{2}.

In this case, due to symmetry the distortion error is given by

\displaystyle\int\min_{a\in\alpha}(x-a)^{2}dP=2\Big(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+\frac{3}{4})}(x-a_{1}){}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+\frac{3}{4})}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x-\frac{3}{4})^{2}\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}(x-\frac{3}{4})^{2}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{768}\Big(192a_{1}^{3}+144a_{1}^{2}-108a_{1}+31\Big),

the minimum value of which is
0.0208333
and it occurs when
a_{1}=\frac{1}{4}
.

Case 2.
\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+\frac{3}{4}).

In this case, due to symmetry the distortion error is given by

\displaystyle\int\min_{a\in\alpha}(x-a)^{2}dP=2\Big(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x-a_{1}){}^{2}\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+\frac{3}{4})}(x-a_{1}){}^{2}\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+\frac{3}{4})}^{\frac{3}{4}}(x-\frac{3}{4})^{2}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{384}\Big(192a_{1}^{3}-48a_{1}^{2}-12a_{1}+11\Big),

the minimum value of which is
0.0208333
and it occurs when
a_{1}=\frac{1}{4}
.

Thus, considering all the possible cases we see that the distortion error is smallest when
a_{1}=\frac{1}{4}
, and since
\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})=\frac{3}{4}
, we have
a_{2}=\frac{5}{4}
. Thus, the optimal set of three-means is
\{\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{4}\}
with quantization error
V_{3}=\frac{1}{48}
(also see Figure
2
).
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

The optimal set of four-means is
\{0.198223,0.59467,0.90533,1.30178\}
with quantization error
V_{4}=0.01057
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:=\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\}
be an optimal set of four-means. Due to symmetry of the probability measure we can say that the points in the optimal set will be symmetrically located on the line with respect to the point
\frac{3}{4}
, i.e.,
0<a_{1}<a_{2}<\frac{3}{4}<a_{3}<a_{4}<\frac{3}{2}
, and
\frac{3}{4}
is the midpoint of
a_{2}
and
a_{3}
. The following cases can arise:

Case 1.
0<a_{1}<a_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

In this case, due to symmetry the distortion error is given by

\displaystyle\int\min_{a\in\alpha}(x-a)^{2}dP=2\Big(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})}(x-a_{1}){}^{2}\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}(x-a_{2}){}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x-a_{2}){}^{2}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{96}\Big(24a_{1}^{3}+24a_{2}a_{1}^{2}-24a_{2}^{2}a_{1}-24a_{2}^{3}+96a_{2}^{2}-84a_{2}+23\Big),

the minimum value of which is
0.0150463
and it occurs when
a_{1}=0.166667
and
a_{2}=0.5
.

Case 2.
0<a_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}<a_{2}<\frac{3}{4}
.

In this case, the following two subcases can occur.

Subcase 1.
\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})\leq\frac{1}{2}.

In this subcase, due to symmetry the distortion error is given by

\displaystyle\int\min_{a\in\alpha}(x-a)^{2}dP=2\Big(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})}(x-a_{1}){}^{2}\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}(x-a_{2}){}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x-a_{2}){}^{2}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{96}\Big(24a_{1}^{3}+24a_{2}a_{1}^{2}-24a_{2}^{2}a_{1}-24a_{2}^{3}+96a_{2}^{2}-84a_{2}+23\Big),

the minimum value of which is
0.01057
and it occurs when
a_{1}=0.198223
and
a_{2}=0.59467
.

Subcase 2.
\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2}).

In this case, due to symmetry the distortion error is given by

\displaystyle\int\min_{a\in\alpha}(x-a)^{2}dP=2\Big(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x-a_{1}){}^{2}\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})}(x-a_{1}){}^{2}\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})}^{\frac{3}{4}}(x-a_{2}){}^{2}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{96}(48a_{1}^{3}+48(a_{2}-1)a_{1}^{2}+(24-48a_{2}^{2})a_{1}-48a_{2}^{3}+144a_{2}^{2}-108a_{2}+23),

the minimum value of which is
0.0169271
and it occurs when
a_{1}=0.3125
and
a_{2}=0.6875
.

Case 3.
\frac{1}{2}\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\frac{3}{4}
.

Lemma 4.4

Proof.

For any even positive integer
n\geq 4
, let
\text{card}(\alpha_{n}\cap[0,\frac{1}{2}])=k:=k(n)

\text{card}(\alpha_{n}\cap(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4}))=m:=m(n)

Bin Shang and Chao Zhang ∗

Abstract
. We study the mixed local and nonlocal double phase parabolic equation ∂ t u ( x , t ) − div ( a ( x , t ) | ∇ u | q − 2 ∇ u ) + ℒ u ( x , t ) = 0 \displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\mathrm{div}(a(x,t)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)+\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=0 in Q T = Ω × ( 0 , T ) Q_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T) , where ℒ \mathcal{L} is the nonlocal p p -Laplace type operator. The local boundedness of weak solutions is proved by means of the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser iteration with the nonnegative coefficient function a ⁡ ( x , t ) a(x,t) being bounded. In addition, when a ⁡ ( x , t ) a(x,t) is Hölder continuous, we discuss the pointwise behavior and lower semicontinuity of weak supersolutions based on energy estimates and De Giorgi type lemma. Analogously, the corresponding results are also valid for weak subsolutions.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate some regularity results of the following problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\mathrm{div}(a(x,t)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)+\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=0\text{ \ \ in }Q_{T},

(1.1)

where
Q_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
with
T>0
and
\Omega
being a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
The fractional
p
-Laplace operator
\mathcal{L}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x,y,t)|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}\left(u(x,t)-u(y,t)\right)\,dy,

where P.V. stands for the Cauchy principle value and
K(x,y,t)
is the symmetric Kernel function satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\leq K(x,y,t)=K(y,x,t)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\ \ \ \forall t\in(0,T),\ \forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

for some
\Lambda\geq 1
. Moreover, we suppose that

\displaystyle p,q\in(1,\infty),\quad sp<q,\quad s\in(0,1)

(1.2)

and the coefficient function
a:Q_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfies

\displaystyle 0\leq a(x,t)\leq\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}.

(1.3)

1.1.
Overview of related literature

Eq. (
1.1
) we study in this paper is characterized by different type of operators depending on the size of the coefficient function
a(x,t)
. Observe that
(
1.1
)
behaves as a purely nonlocal
p
-Laplace equation on
\{a(x,t)=0\}
while leading to the problem involving different orders of differentiation on
\{a(x,t)>0\}
. For the study on regularity theory of nonlocal equations, we refer the readers to
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
. The mixed local and nonlocal problem have been widely studied recently owing to its essential applications in plasma physics
[
21
]
and biology
[
4

. In the following, we will focus on introducing some known results for the mixed problems.

For the basic prototype of the mixed local and nonlocal equation

-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u=0,

(1.4)

the Harnack inequality and boundary Harnack principle were derived in
[
23
]
and
[
9
]
, respectively; see also
[
10
]
for the Harnack estimate of globally nonnegative solutions regarding the parabolic version of (

). Moreover, a weak Harnack inequality with a tail term to the parabolic setting of (

) can be found in
[

]
. Recently, Biagi-Dipierro-Valdinoci-Vecchi
[
5
,
6

developed systematic research on mixed problems and established several results involving radial symmetry, regularity, maximum principle, and other qualitative properties of weak solutions to (
1.4
).
For the nonlinear scenario of (
1.4
)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u=0,

(1.5)

the regularity theory involving Harnack inequality, local boundedness, and Hölder continuity along with semicontinuity representative of weak solutions were discussed by Garain-Kinnunen
[
25
]
. When it comes to the parabolic version of (

[

]
and Harnack inequality
[

via analytic approach. For what concerns existence, uniqueness, local boundedness and higher Hölder regularity for weak solutions to (

[
24
]
. In particular, De Filippis-Mingione
[
15
]
first proposed the functionals with nonstandard growth

\displaystyle u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\left(|Du|^{q}-fu\right)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdy,

(1.6)

and proved the maximal regularity of minimizers to (
1.6
) with

. The case
q<sp
can be seen as a natural extension of the above problem and the local behaviour including the boundedness, Hölder continuity and Harnack inequality of the minimizers of (

[
19

. Besides, Byun-Lee-Song
[
8

considered the regularity results for minimizers of functionals modeled after

\displaystyle u\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdy+\int_{\Omega}a(x)|Du|^{q}\,dx,

(1.7)

where
0\leq a(x)\leq\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
1<p\leq q
. Functional (

) is linked to the local or nonlocal double phase elliptic problems. We refer the readers to
[

,
11
,
14
,
3
,
22
,
16
]
and references therein for the regularity results. On the other hand, for the parabolic double phase problem, the gradient higher integrability, Lipschitz truncation method and the Calderón-Zygmund type estimates have been provided in
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
. Prasad-Tewary
[
34
]
proved the local boundedness of variational solutions for the nonlocal double phase parabolic equation by variational method.

As far as we know, there are no results concerning the mixed local and nonlocal double phase parabolic equation (
1.1
) for the case
p\neq q
, even the coefficient function
a(x,t)
is a constant. To this end, we are interested in proving the local boundedness by energy estimates and De Giorgi-Nash-Moser iteration. We would like to mention that our approach to establish energy estimates differs from
[
34
]
since they discussed the variational solutions that can be proved as parabolic minimizers. Furthermore, based on energy estimates and De Giorgi type lemma, we take into account the lower semicontinuity and pointwise behavior of weak supersolutions to (
1.1
), and the related results also hold true for subsolutions.

1.2.
Notations and Definitions

For the sake of simplicity, we give some notations that will be used later.

For radius
\rho>0
, let
B_{\rho}(x)
be the domain centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We denote the forward and backward space-time cylinders by

\displaystyle Q_{\rho,\theta}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})=B_{\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0},t_{0}+\theta)\ \ \text{and}\ \ Q_{\rho,\theta}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0})=B_{\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\theta,t_{0}).

Denote the centered cylinders as

\displaystyle Q_{\rho,\theta}(x_{0},t_{0})=Q_{\rho,\theta}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0})\cup Q_{\rho,\theta}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0}).

If not important, we will omit
(x_{0},t_{0})
in the above symbols for clearly.

For any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, define

\displaystyle J_{p}(a,b)=|a-b|^{p-2}(a-b).

Set

\displaystyle d\mu=d\mu(x,y,t)=K(x,y,t)\,dxdy,

and

data:=data(N,p,s,q,\Lambda).

We introduce the tail space as

\displaystyle L_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{v\in L_{\rm{loc}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)|^{p}}{1+|x|^{N+\alpha}}\,dx<+\infty\right\},\quad p>0\text{ and }\alpha>0.

The tail appearing in estimates is denoted by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},\rho,I)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},\rho,t_{0}-\theta,t_{0})

\displaystyle:=\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{t\in I}\left(\rho^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\frac{|v(x,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+sp}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

(1.8)

with
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)
and the interval
I=\left(t_{0}-\theta,t_{0}\right)\subseteq(0,T)
. By the definition, it is not hard to verify that
\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},\rho,I)<\infty
for any
v\in L^{\infty}(I;L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.

Now, we state the definition of weak solutions to (
1.1
) as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that

u\in L^{q}_{\rm{loc}}(0,T;W_{\rm{loc}}^{1,q}(\Omega))\cap C_{\rm{loc}}(0,T;L_{\rm{loc}}^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))

is a local weak sub(super-)solution to (
1.1
), if for every closed interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subseteq(0,T)
and any compact set
K\subset\Omega
, integral equality

\displaystyle\int_{K}u(x,t_{2})\varphi(x,t_{2})\,dx-\int_{K}u(x,t_{1})\varphi(x,t_{1})\,dx-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{K}u(x,t)\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{K}a(x,t)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi,t)\,dt\leq(\geq)0

(1.9)

holds for every nonnegative test function
\varphi\in L^{q}_{\rm{loc}}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,q}(K))\cap W_{\rm{loc}}^{1,2}(0,T;L^{2}(K))
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

\displaystyle\Big[|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))

\displaystyle\cdot(\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(y,t))K(x,y,t)\Big]\,dxdy.

A function
u
called a weak solution to (
1.1
) that is both a weak supersolution and a weak subsolution.

Let
u
be a locally essentially bounded below measure function in
Q_{T}
, the lower semicontinuous regularization of
u
is given by

\displaystyle u_{*}(x,t):=\operatorname*{ess\lim\inf}_{(y,\hat{t})\rightarrow(x,t)}u(y,\hat{t})=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\operatorname*{ess\inf}_{Q_{\rho,\theta}(x,t)}u\text{ \ \ for every
}(x,t)\in Q_{T}.

Likewise, for the measurable function
u
that is essentially bounded above in
Q_{T}
, we define the upper semicontinuous regularization of
u
as

\displaystyle u^{*}(x,t):=\operatorname*{ess\lim\sup}_{(y,\hat{t})\rightarrow(x,t)}u(y,\hat{t})=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{Q_{\rho,\theta}(x,t)}u\text{ \ \ for every
}(x,t)\in Q_{T}.

Let
u\in L_{\rm{loc}}^{1}(Q_{T})
, we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:=\left\{(x,t)\in Q_{T}:|u(x,t)|<\infty,\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{\rho,\theta}(x,t)}|u(x,t)-u(y,\hat{t})|\,dyd\hat{t}=0\right\},

then it follows from the Lebesgue differentiation theorem that
|\mathcal{F}|=|Q_{T}|
.

1.3.
Main results

Our results can be stated as follows. The first one we present is the local boundedness result in the supercritical case. Denote

\displaystyle a\vee b:=\max\{a,b\},\quad a_{+}:=\max\{a,0\},\quad a_{-}:=-\min\{a,0\}.

(Local boundedness)
.

Suppose that (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) hold. Let

,
\kappa:=1+2s/N
and

2.
Energy Estimates

In this section, a Caccioppoli type inequality is established. In general, we need to begin with time regularization since the time derivative of a weak solution to (
1.1
) does not exist in the Sobolev sense. The specific procedure can be performed as
[
29
,
30
]
, and thus we omit this step here.

Lemma 2.1

(Caccioppoli-type inequality)
.

Let
p,q>1
and every cylinder
Q^{-}_{\rho,\theta}\subset Q_{T}
. Suppose that
u
is a local weak subsolution to (
1.1
). There exists constants
C>0
depending on the data such that for every
k\in\mathbb{R}
, there holds

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{t_{0}-\theta<t<t_{0}}\int_{B_{\rho}}w_{+}^{2}(x,t)\psi^{m}(x,t)\,dx+\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{\rho}}a(x,t)|\nabla w_{+}(x,t)|^{q}\psi^{m}(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}(\min\{\psi(x,t),\psi(y,t)\})^{m}|w_{+}(x,t)-w_{+}(y,t)|^{p}\,d\mu dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{\rho}}w_{+}^{2}(x,t)|\partial_{t}\psi^{m}|\,dxdt+C\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{\rho}}a(x,t)w_{+}^{q}(x,t)|\nabla\psi(x,t)|^{q}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}(\max\{w_{+}(x,t),w_{+}(y,t)\})^{p}\left|\psi^{\frac{m}{p}}(x,t)-\psi^{\frac{m}{p}}(y,t)\right|^{p}\,d\mu dt

\displaystyle\quad+C\mathop{\mathrm{ess}\sup}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t_{0}-\theta<t<t_{0}}}{{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\psi(\cdot,t)}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\rho}}\frac{w_{+}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{\rho}}w_{+}(x,t)\psi^{m}(x,t)\,dxdt,

(2.1)

where
w_{+}(x,t):=\left(u(x,t)-k\right)_{+}
,
m=\max\{p,q\}
and
\psi(x,t)\in[0,1]
is a piecewise smooth cutoff function vanishing on
\partial B_{\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\theta,t_{0})
.

Proof.

Denote
w_{+}(x,t):=\left(u(x,t)-k\right)_{+}
and
m=\max\{p,q\}
. Choose the piecewise smooth cutoff function
\psi(\cdot,t)\in[0,1]
that is compactly supported in
B_{\rho}
for any
(t_{0}-\theta,t_{0})
. Testing the weak formulation (
1.1
) by
\varphi(x,t):=w_{+}(x,t)\psi^{m}(x,t)
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\partial_{t}u(x,t)w_{+}(x,t)\psi^{m}(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{\rho}}a(x,t)|\nabla u(x,t)|^{q-2}\nabla u(x,t)\nabla(w_{+}(x,t)\psi^{m}(x,t))\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}J_{p}(w(x,t),w(y,t))\left(w_{+}(x,t)\psi^{m}(x,t)-w_{+}(y,t)\psi^{m}(y,t)\right)\,d\mu dt

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}J_{p}\left(w(x,t),w(y,t)\right)w_{+}(x,t)\psi^{m}(x,t)\,d\mu dt

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

Then we estimate
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
and
I_{4}
separately. First, we evaluate

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\partial_{t}w_{+}^{2}(x,t)\psi^{m}(x,t)\,dxdt

In this section, we aim at investigating the local boundedness of weak solutions. Before doing this, we give some notations as below.

\displaystyle\int_{-\theta_{j}}^{0}\int_{B_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}(\max\{w_{j}(x,t),w_{j}(y,t)\})^{p}\left|\zeta^{\frac{m}{p}}(x,t)-\zeta^{\frac{m}{p}}(y,t)\right|^{p}\,d\mu dt\leq C\frac{2^{jm}}{\rho^{sp}}L^{p}|A_{j}|.

(4.6)

For
|y|\geq\rho_{j}
and
|x|\leq\hat{\rho}_{j}
, we can get

\displaystyle\frac{|y-x|}{|y|}\geq 1-\frac{\hat{\rho}_{j}}{\rho_{j}}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\rho_{j}-\rho_{j+1}}{\rho_{j}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2^{j+4}},

which along with (
4.2
) gives that

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ess}\sup}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-\theta_{j}<t<0}}{{x\in\hat{B}_{j}}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{j}}\frac{w_{j}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\int_{-\theta_{j}}^{0}\int_{B_{j}}w_{j}(x,t)\zeta^{m}(x,t)\,dxdt\leq C\frac{2^{j(N+sp)}}{\rho^{sp}}L^{p}|A_{j}|.

(4.7)

We deduce from (
4
)–(
4.7
) that

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{-\tilde{\theta}_{j}<t<0}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}}w_{j}^{2}(x,t)\,dx+\int_{-\tilde{\theta}_{j}}^{0}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}}\frac{\left|w_{j}(x,t)-w_{j}(y,t)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{j(N+2m)}\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\theta}+\frac{L^{p}}{\rho^{sp}}\right)|A_{j}|,

where
C
depends on
the data
,
[a]_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}
, and
M
.
Employing the Sobolev embedding Lemma
1.7
and recalling the fact
\zeta=1
in
\tilde{Q}_{j}
yields that

\displaystyle\iint_{\tilde{Q}_{j}}(w_{j}\zeta)^{p(1+\frac{2s}{N})}\,dxdt=\iint_{\tilde{Q}_{j}}w_{j}^{p(1+\frac{2s}{N})}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-\tilde{\theta}_{j}}^{0}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}}\frac{\left|w_{j}(x,t)-w_{j}(y,t)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt\times\left(\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{-\tilde{\theta}_{j}<t<0}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}}w_{j}^{2}(x,t)\,dx\right)^{\frac{sp}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[2^{j(N+2m)}\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\theta}+\frac{L^{p}}{\rho^{sp}}\right)\right]^{\frac{N+sp}{N}}|A_{j}|^{\frac{N+sp}{N}}.

(4.8)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\iint_{\tilde{Q}_{j}}(w_{j}\zeta)^{p(1+\frac{2s}{N})}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\geq\iint_{\tilde{Q}_{j}}w_{j}^{p(1+\frac{2s}{N})}\chi_{[u<k_{j+1}]}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\geq\iint_{Q_{j+1}\cap[u<k_{j+1}]}(k_{j}-k_{j+1})^{p(1+\frac{2s}{N})}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\geq\left[\frac{(1-b)L}{2^{j+1}}\right]^{p\frac{N+2s}{N}}|Q_{j+1}\cap[u<k_{j+1}]|.

(4.9)

Set

\displaystyle Y_{j}=\frac{|[u<k_{j}]\cap Q_{j}|}{|Q_{j}|},

then it follows from (
4
) and (
4
) that

\displaystyle Y_{j+1}\leq\frac{Ch^{j}}{(1-b)^{p\frac{N+2s}{N}}}\left(\frac{\theta}{L^{2-p}}\right)^{\frac{sp}{N}}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{sp}}+\frac{L^{2-p}}{\theta}\right)^{\frac{N+sp}{N}}\rho^{sp}|Y_{j}|^{\frac{N+sp}{N}},

where
h=h(N,s,p,q)>1
. Since
\theta=\eta\rho^{sp}
, we can obtain
Y_{j}\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
by Lemma
1.8
provided

\displaystyle Y_{0}\leq C^{-\frac{N}{sp}}h^{-\left(\frac{N}{sp}\right)^{2}}(1-b)^{N+2}\frac{L^{2-p}}{\eta}\left(1+\frac{L^{2-p}}{\eta}\right)^{-\frac{N+sp}{sp}}:=\nu.

For the case
\bar{a}_{\rho}\geq 2[a]_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rho^{\alpha}
, we can check that
\frac{2}{3}\bar{a}_{\rho}\leq a(x,t)\leq 2\bar{a}_{\rho}
in
Q_{\rho,\eta\rho^{q}}(x_{0},t_{0})
which leads to the nonstandard growth problem. In this case, we can use the method of proving Lemma 3.2 in our previous paper

]
to get this result. Similar to the proof above, employ Caccioppoli inequality (

and Lemma 2.1 in

where
\tilde{h}=\tilde{h}(N,s,p,q)>1
.
∎

We now prepare to prove Theorem

. From the definition of

\displaystyle u_{*}(x,t)=\operatorname*{ess\lim\inf}_{(y,\hat{t})\rightarrow(x,t)}u(y,\hat{t})\leq\inf_{\eta>0}\lim_{\varrho\rightarrow 0}\operatorname*{ess\inf}_{Q^{\prime}_{\rho,\theta}(x,t)}u\text{\ \ for every }(x,t)\in Q_{T}.

(4.10)

For the reverse inequality of (

Lemma 4.3
.

Let the assumptions (
1.2
) and (
1.12
) hold. Set
M:=\sup_{Q_{T}}|u|
and fix the radius
\rho\in(0,1]
, the constants
L\in(0,M)
,
b\in(0,1]
. Assume that
u
is a local weak supersolution to (
1.1
). Set the same time levels as (
4.2
).
Let the number
\mu^{-}
satisfy

\displaystyle\mu^{-}\leq\operatorname*{ess\inf}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)}u.

There exists a constant
\eta>0
depending on the data,
M,L,b,\mu^{-}
and
[a]_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}
such that if

\displaystyle u(\cdot,t_{0})\geq\mu^{-}+L\text{\ a.e. in }B_{\rho}(x_{0}),

then

\displaystyle u\geq\mu^{-}+bL\text{\ a.e. in }Q_{\frac{\rho}{2},\frac{\theta}{2}}(x_{0},t_{0}).

Proof.

We may assume
(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
. Let
k_{j},\rho_{j},\tilde{\rho}_{j},\hat{\rho}_{j},\theta_{j},\tilde{\theta}_{j},\hat{\theta}_{j},B_{j},\tilde{B}_{j},\hat{B}_{j}
be as in Lemma
4.2
. Define the forward cylinders

\displaystyle Q_{j}=B_{j}\times(0,\theta_{j}),\ \tilde{Q}_{j}=\tilde{B}_{j}\times(0,\tilde{\theta_{j}}),\ \hat{Q}_{j}=\hat{B}_{j}\times(0,\hat{\theta}_{j}).

Choose the piecewise cutoff function
\zeta(x,t)=\zeta(x)
vanishing outside
\hat{B}_{j}
such that

\displaystyle 0\leq\zeta\leq 1,\quad\zeta(x)=1\text{\ in }\tilde{B}_{j},\quad|\nabla\zeta|\leq\frac{2^{j}}{\rho}.

When
\bar{a}_{\rho}\leq 2[a]_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rho^{\alpha}
, a combination of energy estimates (
2.1
) in the cylinder
\tilde{Q}_{j}
and the fact
u(\cdot,0)\geq\mu^{-}+L
yield that

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{0<t<\theta_{j}}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}}w_{j}^{2}(x,t)\,dx+\int_{0}^{\theta_{j}}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}}\frac{\left|w_{j}(x,t)-w_{j}(y,t)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{j(N+2m)}\frac{L^{p}}{\rho^{sp}}|A_{j}|,

where
A_{j}:=[u<k_{j}]\cap Q_{j}
. Let
Y_{j}=\frac{|A_{j}|}{|Q_{j}|}
. Following the same steps as proving Lemma
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle Y_{j+1}\leq\frac{Ch^{j}}{(1-b)^{p\frac{N+2s}{N}}}\left(\frac{\eta}{L^{2-p}}\right)^{\frac{sp}{N}}|Y_{j}|^{\frac{N+sp}{N}}

with
h=h(N,p,s,q)>1
. By Lemma
1.8
, it shows that if

\displaystyle Y_{0}\leq C^{-\frac{N}{sp}}h^{-\frac{N^{2}}{s^{2}p^{2}}}(1-b)^{N+2}\frac{L^{2-p}}{\eta},

then
Y_{j}\rightarrow 0
. Thus, we can arrive at the claim by choosing

\displaystyle\eta=C^{-\frac{N}{sp}}(1-b)^{N+2s}L^{2-p}.

Proceeding similarly as above, we may choose

\eta=C^{-\frac{N}{q}}(1-b)^{N+2}L^{2-q}(1+L^{p-q})^{-\frac{N+q}{q}}

in the case
\bar{a}_{\rho}\geq 2[a]_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rho^{\alpha}
to finish the proof.
∎

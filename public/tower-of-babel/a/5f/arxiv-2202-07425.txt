Algebraic function based Banach space valued ordinary and fractional neural network approximations
George A. Anastassiou

Abstract
Here we research the univariate quantitative approximation, ordinary and fractional, of Banach space valued continuous functions on a compact interval or all the real line by quasi-interpolation Banach space valued neural network operators. These approximations are derived by establishing Jackson type inequalities involving the modulus of continuity of the engaged function or its Banach space valued high order derivative of fractional derivatives. Our operators are defined by using a density function generated by an algebraic sigmoid function. The approximations are pointwise and of the uniform norm. The related Banach space valued feed-forward neural networks are with one hidden layer.

2
Basics

We consider the generator algebraic function

\varphi\left(x\right)=\frac{x}{\sqrt[2m]{1+x^{2m}}},\text{ \ }m\in\mathbb{N}\text{, }x\in\mathbb{R}\text{,}

(1)

which is a sigmoidal type of function and is a strictly increasing function.

We see that
\varphi\left(-x\right)=-\varphi\left(x\right)
with
\varphi\left(0\right)=0
. We get that

\varphi^{\prime}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{\left(1+x^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}}>0\text{, \ }\forall\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}\text{,}

(2)

proving
\varphi
as strictly increasing over
\mathbb{R},\varphi^{\prime}\left(x\right)=\varphi^{\prime}\left(-x\right).
We easily find that
\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}\varphi\left(x\right)=1
,
\varphi\left(+\infty\right)=1
, and
\underset{x\rightarrow-\infty}{\lim}\varphi\left(x\right)=-1
,
\varphi\left(-\infty\right)=-1.

We consider the activation function

\Phi\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left[\varphi\left(x+1\right)-\varphi\left(x-1\right)\right].

(3)

Clearly it is
\Phi\left(x\right)=\Phi\left(-x\right),

\forall

x\in\mathbb{R}
, so that
\Phi
is an even function and symmetric with
respect to the
y
-axis.

Since
x+1>x-1
, we have
\varphi\left(x+1\right)>\varphi\left(x-1\right)
and
\Phi\left(x\right)>0
,
\forall

x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Also it is

\Phi\left(0\right)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt[2m]{2}}.

(4)

We observe that

\Phi^{\prime}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left(\varphi^{\prime}\left(x+1\right)-\varphi^{\prime}\left(x-1\right)\right)=

\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{\left(1+\left(x+1\right)^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}}-\frac{1}{\left(1+\left(x-1\right)^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}}\right),\text{ \ }\forall\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(5)

Let now
x>0
, then
x>-x
and
\left(x+1\right)^{2}>\left(x-1\right)^{2}\geq 0
, implying
\left(x+1\right)^{2m}>\left(x-1\right)^{2m}\geq 0
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
1+\left(x+1\right)^{2m}>1+\left(x-1\right)^{2m}>0
. Consequently it holds

\frac{1}{\left(1+\left(x-1\right)^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}}>\frac{1}{\left(1+\left(x+1\right)^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}},

(6)

proving
\Phi^{\prime}\left(x\right)<0
for
x>0
.

That is
\Phi
is strictly decreasing over
\left(0,+\infty\right)
.

Clearly,
\Phi
is strictly increasing over
\left(-\infty,0\right)
and

\Phi^{\prime}\left(0\right)=0
.

Furthermore we obtain that

\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}\Phi\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left[\varphi\left(+\infty\right)-\varphi\left(+\infty\right)\right]=0,

(7)

and

\underset{x\rightarrow-\infty}{\lim}\Phi\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left[\varphi\left(-\infty\right)-\varphi\left(-\infty\right)\right]=0.

(8)

That is the
x
-axis is the horizontal asymptote of
\Phi
.

Conclusion,
\Phi
is a bell symmetric function with maximum

\Phi\left(0\right)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt[2m]{2}},\text{ \ }m\in\mathbb{N}.

(9)

We need

Theorem 1

We have that

\sum\limits_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi\left(x-i\right)=1\text{, \ }\forall\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(10)

Proof.
We observe that

\sum\limits_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\varphi\left(x-i\right)-\varphi\left(x-1-i\right)\right)=

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\varphi\left(x-i\right)-\varphi\left(x-1-i\right)\right)+\sum\limits_{i=-\infty}^{-1}\left(\varphi\left(x-i\right)-\varphi\left(x-1-i\right)\right).

Furthermore (
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
)

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\varphi\left(x-i\right)-\varphi\left(x-1-i\right)\right)=

(11)

\underset{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\lambda}\left(\varphi\left(x-i\right)-\varphi\left(x-1-i\right)\right)\text{
\ (telescoping sum)}

=\underset{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\left(\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(x-\left(\lambda+1\right)\right)\right)=1+\varphi\left(x\right).

Similarly, it holds

\sum\limits_{i=-\infty}^{-1}\left(\varphi\left(x-i\right)-\varphi\left(x-1-i\right)\right)=\underset{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\sum\limits_{i=-\lambda}^{-1}\left(\varphi\left(x-i\right)-\varphi\left(x-1-i\right)\right)

(12)

=\underset{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\left(\varphi\left(x+\lambda\right)-\varphi\left(x\right)\right)=1-\varphi\left(x\right).

Therefore we derive

\sum\limits_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\varphi\left(x-i\right)-\varphi\left(x-1-i\right)\right)=2\text{, \ }\forall\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R},

(13)

and

\sum\limits_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\varphi\left(x+1-i\right)-\varphi\left(x-i\right)\right)=2\text{, \ }\forall\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(14)

Adding (
13
) and (
14
) we find

\sum\limits_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\varphi\left(x+1-i\right)-\varphi\left(x-1-i\right)\right)=4\text{, \ }\forall\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(15)

Clearly, then

\Phi\left(x-i\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left[\varphi\left(x+1-i\right)-\varphi\left(x-1-i\right)\right],

proving (
10
).

Because

\sum\limits_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi\left(i-x\right)=1\text{, \ }\forall\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}.

\sum\limits_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi\left(i+x\right)=1\text{, \ }\forall\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R},

Theorem 3

It holds

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi\left(x\right)dx=1.

(17)

Proof.
We observe that

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi\left(x\right)dx=\sum\limits_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{j}^{j+1}\Phi\left(x\right)dx=\sum\limits_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi\left(x+j\right)dx=

(18)

\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum\limits_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi\left(x+j\right)dx\right)=\int_{0}^{1}1dx=1.

So
\Phi\left(x\right)
is a density function on
\mathbb{R}.

Theorem 4

Let
0<\alpha<1
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n^{1-\alpha}>2
.
It holds

\sum\limits_{\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}k=-\infty\\
:\left|nx-k\right|\geq n^{1-\alpha}\end{array}\right.}^{\infty}\Phi\left(nx-k\right)<\frac{1}{4m\left(n^{1-\alpha}-2\right)^{2m}},\text{ \ }m\in\mathbb{N}.

(19)

Proof.
We have that

\Phi\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left[\varphi\left(x+1\right)-\varphi\left(x-1\right)\right],\text{ \ }\forall\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
x\geq 1
. That is
0\leq x-1<x+1
. Applying the mean value theorem we
get

\Phi\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{4}\cdot 2\cdot\varphi^{\prime}\left(\xi\right)=\frac{1}{2\left(1+\xi^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}}>0,

(20)

where
0\leq x-1<\xi<x+1
.

Then,

\left(x-1\right)^{2}<\xi^{2}<\left(x+1\right)^{2}

\left(x-1\right)^{2m}<\xi^{2m}<\left(x+1\right)^{2m}

1+\left(x-1\right)^{2m}<1+\xi^{2m}<1+\left(x+1\right)^{2m}

\left(1+\left(x-1\right)^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}<\left(1+\xi^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}<\left(1+\left(x+1\right)^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}

\frac{1}{2\left(1+\xi^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}}<\frac{1}{2\left(1+\left(x-1\right)^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}}.

(21)

Hence

\Phi\left(x\right)<\frac{1}{2\left(1+\left(x-1\right)^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}},\text{ \ }\forall\text{ }x\geq 1.

(22)

Thus, we have

\sum\limits_{\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}k=-\infty\\
:\left|nx-k\right|\geq n^{1-\alpha}\end{array}\right.}^{\infty}\Phi\left(nx-k\right)=\sum\limits_{\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}k=-\infty\\
:\left|nx-k\right|\geq n^{1-\alpha}\end{array}\right.}^{\infty}\Phi\left(\left|nx-k\right|\right)<

\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}k=-\infty\\
:\left|nx-k\right|\geq n^{1-\alpha}\end{array}\right.}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(1+\left(\left|nx-k\right|-1\right)^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}}\leq

(23)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\left(n^{1-\alpha}-1\right)}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(1+\left(x-1\right)^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}}dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{n^{1-\alpha}-2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(1+z^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}}dz=:\left(\ast\right).

We see that

1+z^{2m}>z^{2m}

\left(1+z^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}>z^{2m+1}

\frac{1}{z^{2m+1}}>\frac{1}{\left(1+z^{2m}\right)^{\frac{2m+1}{2m}}}.

(24)

Therefore it holds

\left(\ast\right)<\frac{1}{2}\int_{n^{1-\alpha}-2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z^{2m+1}}dz=\frac{1}{2}\int_{n^{1-\alpha}-2}^{\infty}z^{-\left(2m+1\right)}dz=

\frac{1}{2}\left.\left(\frac{z^{-\left(2m+1\right)+1}}{-\left(2m+1\right)+1}\right)\right|_{n^{1-\alpha}-2}^{\infty}=\frac{1}{2}\left.\left(-\frac{z^{-2m}}{2m}\right)\right|_{n^{1-\alpha}-2}^{\infty}=

(25)

\left.\frac{z^{-2m}}{4m}\right|_{\infty}^{n^{1-\alpha}-2}=\frac{\left(n^{1-\alpha}-2\right)^{-2m}}{4m}-\frac{\left(\infty\right)^{-2m}}{4m}=\frac{\left(n^{1-\alpha}-2\right)^{-2m}}{4m},

proving (
19
).

Theorem 5

Let
\left[a,b\right]\subset\mathbb{R}
and

Proof.
Let

1=\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi\left(nx-k\right)>\sum\limits_{k=\left\lceil na\right\rceil}^{\left\lfloor nb\right\rfloor}\Phi\left(nx-k\right)=

\sum\limits_{k=\left\lceil na\right\rceil}^{\left\lfloor nb\right\rfloor}\Phi\left(\left|nx-k\right|\right)>\Phi\left(\left|nx-k_{0}\right|\right),

(27)

\forall

We can choose

Therefore we get that

\Phi\left(\left|nx-k_{0}\right|\right)>\Phi\left(1\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt[2m]{1+2^{2m}}}\right)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt[2m]{1+2^{2m}}},

(28)

and

\sum\limits_{k=\left\lceil na\right\rceil}^{\left\lfloor nb\right\rfloor}\Phi\left(\left|nx-k\right|\right)>\frac{1}{2\sqrt[2m]{1+2^{2m}}}.

(29)

That is

\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{k=\left\lceil na\right\rceil}^{\left\lfloor nb\right\rfloor}\Phi\left(\left|nx-k\right|\right)}<2\sqrt[2m]{1+4^{m}},

Remark 6

We also notice that

1-\sum\limits_{k=\left\lceil na\right\rceil}^{\left\lfloor nb\right\rfloor}\Phi\left(nb-k\right)=\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{\left\lceil na\right\rceil-1}\Phi\left(nb-k\right)+\sum\limits_{k=\left\lfloor nb\right\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\Phi\left(nb-k\right)

=\Phi\left(\varepsilon-1\right)=\Phi\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\geq\Phi\left(1\right)>0.

Therefore

\underset{n\rightarrow\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\left(1-\sum\limits_{k=\left\lceil na\right\rceil}^{\left\lfloor nb\right\rfloor}\Phi\left(nb-k\right)\right)>0.

Similarly, it holds

(call

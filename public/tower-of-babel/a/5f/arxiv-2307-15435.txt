Minimal error momentum Bregman-Kaczmarz
Dirk A. Lorenz, Maximilian Winkler

Abstract
The Bregman-Kaczmarz method is an iterative method which can solve strongly convex problems with linear constraints and uses only one or a selected number of rows of the system matrix in each iteration, thereby making it amenable for large-scale systems. To speed up convergence, we investigate acceleration by heavy ball momentum in the so-called dual update. Heavy ball acceleration of the Kaczmarz method with constant parameters has turned out to be difficult to analyze, in particular no accelerated convergence for the ℒ 2 \mathcal{L}_{2} -error of the iterates has been proven to the best of our knowledge. Here we propose a way to adaptively choose the momentum parameter by a minimal-error principle similar to a recently proposed method for the standard randomized Kaczmarz method. The momentum parameter can be chosen to exactly minimize the error in the next iterate or to minimize a relaxed version of the minimal error principle. The former choice leads to a theoretically optimal step while the latter is cheaper to compute. We prove improved convergence results compared to the non-accelerated method. Numerical experiments show that the proposed methods can accelerate convergence in practice, also for matrices which arise from applications such as computational tomography.



1
Introduction

We consider the problem

\displaystyle\min\varphi(x)\qquad\text{s.t. }\mathbf{A}x=b,\qquad\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\ b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

(1)

with a
\sigma
-strongly convex function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
. To invoke stochastic optimization, we make use of the stochastic reformulation of the linear system given by

\displaystyle\min f(x):=\mathbb{E}[f_{S}(x)],

(2)

where the expectation is taken over so-called
sketching matrices

S\sim\mathcal{D}
which stem from a distribution
\mathcal{D}
on the set of real-valued matrices with
m
rows, and

\displaystyle f_{S}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\|S^{T}(\mathbf{A}x-b)\|_{2}^{2}.

The corresponding stochastic “sketched Bregman method” for problem (
1
) with stochastic representation (
2
) is given by the update

\displaystyle x_{k+1}^{*}

\displaystyle=x_{k}^{*}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\nabla\varphi^{*}(x_{k+1}^{*}).

(3)

Here,
\alpha_{k}>0
is a sequence of step sizes,
S_{k}
is drawn from the distribution
\mathcal{D}
and
\varphi^{*}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
the convex conjugate function, which is everywhere finite and differentiable with
\sigma^{-1}
-Lipschitz continuous gradient. Method (
3
) generalizes the
block Bregman-Kaczmarz
method, initially named
block sparse Kaczmarz

[
13
,
18
,
20
]
, which iterates

\displaystyle x_{k+1}^{*}

\displaystyle=x_{k}^{*}-\alpha_{k}\mathbf{A}_{(i_{k})}^{T}(\mathbf{A}_{(i_{k})}x_{k}-b_{(i_{k})}),

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\nabla\varphi^{*}(x_{k+1}^{*}).

(4)

Here, the matrix
\mathbf{A}
and the vector
b
are partitioned as

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}_{(1)}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{A}_{(c)}\end{pmatrix},\qquad b=\begin{pmatrix}b_{(1)}\\
\vdots\\
b_{(c)}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that, if
\mathbf{A}_{(i_{k})}
consists of rows
r,r+1,...,s
, we recover the update by setting

\displaystyle S_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}\ldots&e_{r}&e_{r+1}&\ldots&e_{s}&\ldots\end{pmatrix}

in (
3
), with the corresponding unit vectors.
The block Bregman-Kaczmarz method (
4
) uses only a selected number of rows of
\mathbf{A}
and if
x_{0}^{*}=0
and the step size is chosen as
\alpha_{k}=\sigma\|\mathbf{A}_{S_{i_{k}}}\|_{F}^{-2}
, it is known to converge linearly in expectation to the solution of problem (
1
), see
[
20
]
.
For instance, with the
1
-strongly convex function
\varphi(x)=\lambda\|x\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_{2}^{2}
, the map
\nabla\varphi^{*}
is the soft-shrinkage function

\displaystyle x\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(x)=\mathrm{sign}(x)\cdot\max(|x|-\lambda,0)

and the method converges to the solution of the
regularized basis pursuit problem

\displaystyle\min\varphi(x)=\lambda\|x\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_{2}^{2}\qquad\text{s.t. }\mathbf{A}x=b,

which is sparse for
\lambda>0
large enough, see
[
4
,
13
]
.
For the choice
\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_{2}^{2}
, we recover the
block Kaczmarz method

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha_{k}\mathbf{A}_{(i_{k})}^{T}(\mathbf{A}_{(i_{k})}x_{k}-b_{(i_{k})}).

1.1
Contribution and outline

As a first result we show in Section
3
that the iterates
x_{k}
of (
3
) converge linearly in expectation to the solution of (
1
), if the
S_{k}
are independent samples from an appropriate distribution
\mathcal{D}
and
\alpha_{k}
is a suitable sequence of step sizes.

Next, in Section
4
we enrich method (
3
) by heavy ball momentum following the
minimal error
strategy. That is, we consider a parameterized update

\displaystyle x_{k+1}^{*}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=x_{k}^{*}-\alpha\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})+\beta d_{k}^{*},

\displaystyle x_{k+1}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\nabla\varphi^{*}(x_{k+1}^{*}(\alpha,\beta)),

and actually would like to find
\alpha_{k}=\alpha
and
\beta_{k}=\beta
such that the Bregman distance

\displaystyle D_{\varphi}^{x_{k+1}^{*}(\alpha,\beta)}(x_{k+1}(\alpha,\beta),\hat{x})=\varphi(\hat{x})-\varphi(x_{k+1}(\alpha,\beta))-\langle x_{k+1}^{*}(\alpha,\beta),\hat{x}-x_{k+1}(\alpha,\beta)\rangle

to the solution
\hat{x}
of (
1
) is minimized over
\alpha
and
\beta
.
This idea is inspired by the
exact-step sparse
Kaczmarz method
[
13
]
, which for the special case
S_{k}=e_{i_{k}}
and
\beta=0
selects the step size
\alpha_{k}
with the least Bregman distance to the solution
\hat{x}
and has been demonstrated to significantly accelerate convergence in examples. However, we will argue that this problem can be computationally hard to solve. Therefore, in Section
4.1
we look at an easier problem, where we fix the step size
\alpha=\alpha_{k}
, which can be stochastic and dependent on expressions known at iteration
k
, and optimize just over
\beta
.

While our approach at first glance seems to require knowledge of the solution
\hat{x}
, we can circumvent this problem by introducing an auxiliary variable
s_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
, which is recursively updated together with
x_{k},x_{k}^{*}
and in each iteration equals
\langle\hat{x},x_{k}^{*}-x_{k-1}^{*}\rangle
. It results that the momentum parameter
\beta_{k}
is determined by the one-dimensional convex optimization problem

\displaystyle\beta_{k}\in\argmin_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{*}(x_{k}^{*}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})+\beta\big(x_{k}^{*}-x_{k-1}^{*})\big)-\beta s_{k}

(5)

and the next
s_{k}
can be found by setting
s_{k+1}=\beta_{k}s_{k}-\alpha_{k}b_{i_{k}}
. We propose the corresponding heavy ball update as
randomized Bregman-Kaczmarz with exact momentum
(BK-EM).

In addition, in Section
4.2
, since problem (
5
) is still costly to solve in general, we propose a relaxed approach by minimizing a quadratic upper bound of the Bregman distance
D_{\varphi}^{x_{k+1}^{*}(\alpha,\beta)}(x_{k+1}(\alpha,\beta),\hat{x})
jointly over
\alpha
and
\beta
. This approach gives “relaxed exact” step sizes
\alpha_{k}
and momentum parameters
\beta_{k}
and we introduce this method as
Bregman-Kaczmarz method with relaxed exact momentum
(BK-REM).

Finally, Section
5
shows numerical experiments to illustrate the speedup in number of iterations and computational time.

1.2
Related work

In this article, we use the general stochastic reformulation of the linear system through sketching matrices as in
[
24
]
. The framework of sketching matrices was first proposed by
[
6
]
.

The standard error to be considered in convergence analysis for methods of randomized Kaczmarz - type and its accelerations is the
\mathcal{L}_{2}
-error
\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k}-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}]
, where
\hat{x}
is the solution of (
1
) and the expectation is taken over all possible combinations of sketching matrices. The seminal paper by Strohmer and Vershynin
[
21
]
proved the rate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k}-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\big(1-\frac{\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{A})^{2}}{\|\mathbf{A}\|_{F}^{2}}\big)^{k}\|x_{0}-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}.

for the iterates
x_{k}
of the Kaczmarz method, where
S_{k}=\{e_{i_{k}}\}
and the
i
-th row
a_{i}
is selected with probability
\|a_{i}\|_{2}^{2}/|\mathbf{A}\|_{F}^{2}
. For both the Kaczmarz and the Bregman-Kaczmarz method, is has been demonstrated that update averaging with appropriate weights gives a better convergence rate
[
14
,
22
]
. Accelerated rates have been also established for enhanced multistep versions
[
8
,
17
]
and accelerations of the Kaczmarz method of Nesterov type
[
5
,
10
,
11
]
, see also
[
16
]
for an extension to systems of linear inequalities
\mathbf{A}x\leq b
. However, strict accelerated rates for the
\mathcal{L}_{2}
-error have not been shown for heavy ball momentum for the Kaczmarz method with a fixed momentum parameter. To the best of our knowledge, a convergence speedup has only been established for the quantity
\|\mathbb{E}[x_{k}-\hat{x}]\|_{2}^{2}
, see
[
12
, Theorem 4]
, which is the error of the expected iterates and different from the above
\mathcal{L}_{2}
-error. Moreover, linear convergence of the
\mathcal{L}_{2}
error has only been guaranteed for relatively restricted choices of the parameter, see
[
9
,
12
,
15
]
. In
[
2
]
, accelerated rates have been shown for heavy ball acceleration of minibatch Kaczmarz with sufficiently large batch sizes. Very recently, acceleration of Tseng type has been studied for the Bregman-Kaczmarz method via its interpretation as a dual coordinate descent method
[
23
]
. Also recently, in
[
24
]
, heavy ball acceleration with an adaptive momentum parameter has been derived for the Kaczmarz method. There, the authors propose to use the momentum parameter which moves the update closest to the exact solution, which is computable due to the linearity of the system. We call this idea the minimum error approach. It was shown that the method from
[
24
]
converges at least with the Strohmer-Vershynin rate. In our work, we transfer this approach to the Bregman-Kaczmarz method and obtain
exact momentum
(SRK-EM) and
relaxed exact momentum
(SRK-REM) in the minimum error sense in Bregman distance.

Notation

We set
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,...\}

By

,

2.2
The stochastic reformulation

In this section, we make assumptions on the distribution
\mathcal{D}
of the sketching matrices which ensure exactness of the stochastic reformulation (
2
) of the linear system
\mathbf{A}x=b
and give useful lemmas which deal with the sketching.

Assumption 1
.

The matrix
\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\mathcal{D}}[SS^{T}]
has only finite entries and is positive definite.

Under assumption (
1
), the reformulation is exact, as the following Lemma states.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
24
, Lemma 2.2]

Let Assumption
1
hold. Then, the minimizers of (
2
) are exactly the solutions of the linear system
\mathbf{A}x=b
.

A central object for the convergence analysis will be the matrix

\displaystyle M:=\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\mathcal{D}}\big[\frac{SS^{T}}{\|\mathbf{A}^{T}S\|_{2}^{2}}\big].

(6)

Assumption 2
.

The matrix
M
in (
6
) has only finite entries.

Note that Assumption
2
requires in particular that
\mathbf{A}^{T}S
is nonzero
\mathcal{D}
-almost surely. We discuss the definition of
M
and Assumptions
1
and
2
in two examples.

Example 2.2
.

(i)

Single row sketching.

If
S=e_{i}
is sampled with probability
p_{i}
such as in the randomized Kaczmarz method, we compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[SS^{T}]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}e_{i}e_{i}^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}&&&\\
&p_{2}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&p_{m}\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle M=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\frac{e_{i}e_{i}^{T}}{\|a_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{p_{1}}{\|a_{1}\|_{2}^{2}}&&&\\
&\frac{p_{2}}{\|a_{2}\|_{2}^{2}}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\frac{p_{m}}{\|a_{m}\|_{2}^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, Assumption
1
is equivalent to
p_{i}>0
for
i=1,...,m
and Assumption
2
is fulfilled exactly if it holds
\|a_{i}\|_{2}>0
and
p_{i}>0
for
i=1,...,m
.

(ii)

Block sketching.

If
S=\begin{pmatrix}\ldots&e_{r_{i}}&e_{r_{i}+1}&\ldots&e_{s_{i}}&\ldots\end{pmatrix}
is sampled with probability
p_{i}
such as in the case of block Kaczmarz, Assumption
1
is equivalent to
p_{i}>0
for all
i
and Assumption
2
is equivalent to the condition
\|A_{(i)}\|_{F}>0
and
p_{i}>0
for all
i
.

The proof of the next lemma is along the lines of the one of
[
24
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. Then, the matrix
M
is positive definite.

Proof.

Since the matrix
L:=\mathbb{E}[SS^{T}]
is positive definite, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|x\|_{2}=1
it holds that
x^{T}Lx>\epsilon
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
we set

\Lambda_{k,k+1}:=\{S:k\leq\|\mathbf{A}^{T}S\|_{2}^{2}<k+1\}.

By Levi’s theorem on monotone convergence, we have that

\displaystyle x^{T}Lx=\int{x^{T}SS^{T}x}\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{D}(S)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Lambda_{k,k+1}}x^{T}SS^{T}x\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{D}(S).

Since the left-hand side is finite and
x^{T}SS^{T}x\geq 0
for all
S
, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{\Lambda_{k,k+1}}x^{T}SS^{T}x\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{D}(S)<\frac{\epsilon}{2},

which means that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-1}\int_{\Lambda_{k,k+1}}x^{T}SS^{T}x\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{D}(S)=x^{T}Lx-\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{\Lambda_{k,k+1}}x^{T}SS^{T}x\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{D}(S)>\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Hence, using Levi’s theorem again we conclude that

\displaystyle x^{T}Mx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Lambda_{k,k+1}}\frac{x^{T}SS^{T}x}{\|\mathbf{A}^{T}S\|_{2}^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{D}(S)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-1}\int_{\Lambda_{k,k+1}}\frac{x^{T}SS^{T}x}{\|\mathbf{A}^{T}S\|_{2}^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{D}(S)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k_{0}}\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-1}\int_{\Lambda_{k,k+1}}x^{T}SS^{T}x\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{D}(S)

\displaystyle>\frac{\epsilon}{2k_{0}}.

∎

2.3
Convex analysis

We recall some concepts and properties of convex functions
[
1
]
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be convex. Since
\varphi
is convex and finite everywhere, it is continuous and the set

\displaystyle\partial\varphi(x)=\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\varphi(y)\geq\varphi(x)+\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle\ \text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\},

is nonempty, convex and compact for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Throughout this article, we further assume that
\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly convex for some
\sigma>0
, which means that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(x)
we have that

\displaystyle\varphi(y)\geq\varphi(x)+\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y-x\|_{2}^{2}.

The
Bregman distance
between
x
and
y
with respect to
x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(x)
is defined as

\displaystyle D_{\varphi}^{x^{*}}(x,y)=\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)-\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle.

(7)

The
convex conjugate
of
\varphi
is defined as

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(x^{*})=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle x^{*},x\rangle-\varphi(x),\qquad x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

One can prove that the
\sigma
-strong convexity of
\varphi
implies that the function
\varphi^{*}
is finite everywhere and differentiable with
\sigma^{-1}
-Lipschitz continuous gradient.
Moreover, for all
x,x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
it holds that
x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(x)
if and only if
x=\nabla\varphi^{*}(x^{*})
.
In this case, the
Fenchel equality

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(x^{*})=\langle x^{*},x\rangle-\varphi(x)

holds and the definition of the Bregman distance (
7
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle D_{\varphi}^{x^{*}}(x,y)=\varphi^{*}(x^{*})-\langle x^{*},y\rangle+\varphi(y).

(8)

Finally, we can lower bound the Bregman distance between
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for all
x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(x)
by

\displaystyle D_{\varphi}^{x^{*}}(x,y)\geq\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}.

(9)

3
The sketched Bregman-Kaczmarz method

In this section, we study convergence of the sketched Bregman-Kaczmarz method for solving problem (
1
) given by

\displaystyle\min\varphi(x)\qquad\text{s.t. }\mathbf{A}x=b,\qquad\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\ b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

We suppose that the system
\mathbf{A}x=b
is consistent, i.e. has a solution, and
b\neq 0
. By strong convexity of
\varphi
, there is exactly one solution
\hat{x}
to problem (
1
). For a distribution
\mathcal{D}
on the set of real-valued matrices with
m
rows, we consider the stochastic reformulation (
2
) given by

\displaystyle\min f(x):=\mathbb{E}[f_{S}(x)],\qquad f_{S}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|S^{T}(\mathbf{A}x-b)\|_{2}^{2}.

The stochastic reformulation is exact under Assumption
1
due to Lemma
2.1
.
The gradient of
f_{S}
can be easily calculated as

\displaystyle\nabla f_{S}(x)=\mathbf{A}^{T}SS^{T}(\mathbf{A}x-b)

and the sketched Bregman-Kaczmarz method is given by Algorithm
1
.

1:

Input:

x_{0}^{*}=0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, step sizes
\alpha_{k}>0
and a distribution
\mathcal{D}
on the set of matrices with
m
rows

2:

Initialization:

x_{0}=\nabla\varphi^{*}(x_{0}^{*})

3:

for

k=0,1,...

do

4:

sample a random sketching matrix
S_{k}\sim{\mathcal{D}}
(independent of
S_{0},...,S_{k-1}
)

5:

x_{k+1}^{*}=x_{k}^{*}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})=x_{k}^{*}-\alpha_{k}\mathbf{A}^{T}S_{k}S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)

6:

x_{k+1}=\nabla\varphi^{*}(x_{k+1}^{*})

Algorithm 1

Basic method: Randomized Bregman-Kaczmarz method (BK) with sketching matrices

Note that, if
x_{0}^{*}
is chosen from
\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{A}^{T})
, by induction it holds
x_{k}^{*}\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{A}^{T})
at every iteration
k
. Also, by subdifferential inversion we have
x_{k}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(x_{k})
. As in
[
13
]
we refer to
x_{k}
,
x_{k}^{*}
as the primal variable and the dual variable, respectively.

We now prove convergence of the
x_{k}
in Algorithm
1
to the solution
\hat{x}
for two different step size sequences.

To this end, we need to assume an error bound which relates the least-squares residual
\|\mathbf{A}x-b\|_{2}^{2}
to the Bregman distance to the exact solution
\hat{x}
for iterates of Algorithm
1
.

Assumption 3
.

Let
\hat{x}
be the solution of problem (
1
).
There exists
\gamma>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\partial\varphi(x)\cap\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{A}^{T})\neq\emptyset
and all
x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(x)\cap\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{A}^{T})
we have

\displaystyle D_{\varphi}^{x^{*}}(x,\hat{x})\leq\gamma\cdot\|\mathbf{A}x-b\|_{2}^{2}.

For the function
\varphi(x)=\lambda\|x\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_{2}^{2}
, it holds that
\mathrm{dom}\ \varphi=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and Assumption
3
holds with an explicit constant
\gamma
depending on
A
and
\hat{x}
, see
[
19
, Lemma 3.1]
. See also
[
20
, Theorem 3.9]
for a sufficient condition for Assumption
3
to hold.
The next lemma will be used to prove convergence of Algorithm
1
for suitable step sizes.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let Assumptions
1
-
2
be fulfilled. Then, the iterates
x_{k}
,
x_{k}^{*}
of Algorithm
1
fulfill that

\displaystyle D_{\varphi}^{x_{k+1}^{*}}(x_{k+1},\hat{x})\leq D_{\varphi}^{x_{k}^{*}}(x_{k},\hat{x})-\alpha_{k}\|S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma}\|\mathbf{A}^{T}S_{k}S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly convex,
\varphi^{*}
is
\sigma^{-1}
smooth. Hence, using (
8
) and the descent lemma
[
1
, Theorem 18.15(iii)]
give that

\displaystyle D_{\varphi}^{x_{k+1}^{*}}(x_{k+1},\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\varphi^{*}(x_{k+1}^{*})-\langle x_{k+1}^{*},\hat{x}\rangle+\varphi(\hat{x})

\displaystyle\leq\varphi^{*}(x_{k}^{*})+\langle\nabla\varphi^{*}(x_{k}^{*}),x_{k+1}^{*}-x_{k}^{*}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|x_{k+1}^{*}-x_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}-\langle x_{k+1}^{*},\hat{x}\rangle+\varphi(\hat{x}).

By inserting the update and using that
\nabla\varphi(x_{k}^{*})=x_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle D_{\varphi}^{x_{k+1}^{*}}(x_{k+1},\hat{x})

\displaystyle\leq\varphi^{*}(x_{k}^{*})+\langle x_{k},-\alpha_{k}\mathbf{A}^{T}S_{k}S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)\rangle+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma}\|\mathbf{A}^{T}S_{k}S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\langle x_{k}^{*},\hat{x}\rangle+\alpha_{k}\langle\mathbf{A}^{T}S_{k}S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b),\hat{x}\rangle+\varphi(\hat{x})

4
Adaptive Heavy Ball acceleration

We consider the parameterized update

\displaystyle x_{k+1}^{*}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=x_{k}^{*}-\alpha\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})+\beta(x_{k}^{*}-x_{k-1}^{*}),

\displaystyle x_{k+1}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\nabla\varphi^{*}(x_{k+1}^{*}),

(17)

that is, we incorporate heavy ball acceleration into the dual update.

In this section, we want to derive expressions for
\alpha=\alpha_{k}
and
\beta=\beta_{k}
which ensure convergence of the update. For notational purposes, we set

\displaystyle y_{k}^{*}=x_{k}^{*}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),\qquad y_{k}=\nabla\varphi^{*}(y_{k}^{*}),\qquad d_{k}^{*}=x_{k}^{*}-x_{k-1}^{*}.

(18)

By (
8
), the Bregman distance of the primal variable to the solution
\hat{x}
of problem (
1
) can be calculated as

\displaystyle D_{\varphi}^{x_{k+1}^{*}(\alpha,\beta)}(x_{k+1}(\alpha,\beta),\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\varphi^{*}(x_{k}^{*}-\alpha\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})+\beta d_{k}^{*})-\langle x_{k}^{*}-\alpha\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})+\beta d_{k}^{*},\hat{x}\rangle+\varphi(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\varphi^{*}(x_{k}^{*}-\alpha\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})+\beta d_{k}^{*})-\langle x_{k}^{*},\hat{x}\rangle+\alpha\langle S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b),S_{k}^{T}b\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\beta\langle d_{k}^{*},\hat{x}\rangle+\varphi(\hat{x}).

(19)

Hence, minimizing (

) over
\alpha
and
\beta
would give us momentum with minimal errors measured in Bregman distance, i.e. our update would fulfill the property

\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta)\in\argmin_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}}D_{\varphi}^{x_{k+1}^{*}(\alpha,\beta)}\big(x_{k+1}(\alpha,\beta),\hat{x}\big).

However, there are two problems occurring here. First, problem (
19
) depends on the exact solution
\hat{x}
, which is not known.
Second,
\varphi^{*}
is an arbitrary convex function which Lipschitz continuous gradient and hence, we expect minimization over two variables to be computationally expensive. For instance, the function
\varphi(x)=\lambda\|x\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_{2}^{2}
has the conjugate

) over
\alpha
and
\beta
.
We will resolve the first problem of the
\hat{x}
-dependence completely by introducing a new scalar variable

, which is updated simultaneously with

,

and in each iteration fulfills that

, as a first approach to address the second problem, we optimize only over
\beta
for a fixed step size

chosen as to Algorithm
1

[
13
]

Therefore, as a second approach, in Section

we optimize over an upper bound of (

and
\beta

\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})
and
d_{k}^{*}
are linearly independent. In case of linear dependence, we can set
\beta_{k}=0
and using (
27
), we obtain back
\alpha_{k}
from (
11
). In case of linear independence, the optimal solution
(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k})
is given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle=\sigma\frac{\|S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)\|_{2}^{2}\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}+\langle\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),d_{k}^{*}\rangle(s_{k}-\langle x_{k},d_{k}^{*}\rangle)}{\|\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}-\langle\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}}

\displaystyle\beta_{k}

\displaystyle=\sigma\frac{\|S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)\|_{2}^{2}\langle\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),d_{k}^{*}\rangle+\|\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}(s_{k}-\langle x_{k},d_{k}^{*}\rangle)}{\|\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}-\langle\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}}.

We revisit again the case of the Bregman-Kaczmarz method with single row sketching (Example

), that is, with
S_{k}=e_{i_{k}}
and
\alpha_{k}=\frac{\sigma}{\|a_{i_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}}
.
In view of the equations from (
15
), the steps of Algorithm
3
are given as follows:

\displaystyle d_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=x_{k}^{*}-x_{k-1}^{*},

\displaystyle t_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sigma\frac{(\langle a_{i_{k}},x_{k}\rangle-b_{i_{k}})\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}+\langle a_{i_{k}},d_{k}^{*}\rangle(s_{k}-\langle x_{k},d_{k}^{*}\rangle)}{\|a_{i_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}-\langle a_{i_{k}},d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}},&\|a_{i_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}-\langle a_{i_{k}},d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}>0,\\
\sigma\frac{(\langle a_{i_{k}},x_{k}\rangle-b_{i_{k}})}{\|a_{i_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\beta_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sigma\frac{(\langle a_{i_{k}},x_{k}\rangle-b_{i_{k}})\langle a_{i_{k}},d_{k}^{*}\rangle+\|a_{i_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}(s_{k}-\langle x_{k},d_{k}^{*}\rangle)}{\|a_{i_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}-\langle a_{i_{k}},d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}},&\|a_{i_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}-\langle a_{i_{k}},d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}>0,\\
\sigma\frac{\langle a_{i_{k}},x_{k}\rangle-b_{i_{k}}}{\|a_{i_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle x_{k+1}^{*}

\displaystyle=x_{k}^{*}-t_{k}a_{i_{k}}+\beta_{k}d_{k}^{*},

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\nabla\varphi^{*}(x_{k+1}^{*}),

\displaystyle s_{k+1}

\displaystyle=-b_{i_{k}}t_{k}+\beta_{k}s_{k}.

Note that we have
\alpha_{k}(\langle a_{i_{k}},x_{k}\rangle-b_{i_{k}})=t_{k}
and hence
\alpha_{k}\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})=t_{k}a_{i_{k}}.

We now investigate convergence of Algorithm
3
. The following lemma will be useful.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
(y,z)
is linearly independent. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\|(I-P_{\langle\{y,z\}\rangle})x\|_{2}^{2}=\|(I-P_{z})\circ(I-P_{y})x\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\langle(I-P_{y})x,\ P_{z}\rangle^{2}}{\|(I-P_{z})y\|_{2}^{2}}.

Proof.

For

u=(I-P_{\langle\{y,z\}\rangle})x,\qquad u^{\prime}=(I-P_{z})\circ(I-P_{y})x

and any
v\in\langle\{y,z\}\rangle^{\perp}
we note that

\displaystyle\langle u,y\rangle=\langle u,z\rangle=\langle u^{\prime},z\rangle=0

and

\displaystyle\langle u^{\prime},v\rangle=\langle x,v\rangle=\langle u,v\rangle.

Hence, we have that

\displaystyle P_{\langle\{y,z\}\rangle^{\perp}}u^{\prime}=P_{\langle\{y,z\}\rangle^{\perp}}x=P_{\langle\{y,z\}\rangle^{\perp}}u=u,

which shows that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{2}^{2}=\|P_{\langle\{y,z\}\rangle^{\perp}}u^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}=\|u^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}-\|P_{\langle\{y,z\}\rangle}u^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}.

Choosing the orthonormal basis
\big(z/\|z\|_{2},\ (I-P_{z})y/\|(I-P_{z})y)\|_{2}\big)
of the space
\langle\{y,z\}\rangle
, we further conclude that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\langle u^{\prime},z\rangle^{2}}{\|z\|_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\langle(I-P_{z})(I-P_{y})x,\ (I-P_{z})y\rangle^{2}}{\|(I-P_{z})y\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|u^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\langle(I-P_{y})x,\ (I-P_{z})y\rangle^{2}}{\|(I-P_{z})y\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|u^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\langle(I-P_{y})x,\ P_{z}y\rangle^{2}}{\|(I-P_{z})y\|_{2}^{2}}.

∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let Assumptions
1
-
3
be fulfilled. Let
x_{k}
,
x_{k}^{*}
be the iterates of Algorithm
3
and

\displaystyle\tilde{y}_{k}=x_{k}-\frac{\|S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)\|_{2}^{2}}{\|\mathbf{A}^{T}S_{k}S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)\|_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{A}^{T}S_{k}S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b).

(29)

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[D_{\varphi}^{x_{k+1}^{*}}(x_{k+1},\hat{x})\big]

\displaystyle\leq\big(1-\frac{\sigma\lambda_{\min}(M)}{2\gamma}\big)\mathbb{E}\big[D_{\varphi}^{x_{k}^{*}}(x_{k},\hat{x})\big]-\frac{\sigma}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\frac{\langle\tilde{y}_{k},d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{\sigma}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\frac{\langle\tilde{y}_{k},d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}\langle\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}\big(\|\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}-\langle\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}\big)}\big].

(30)

In particular,
x_{k}
converges in expectation to
\hat{x}
in
\mathcal{L}_{2}
-sense with the linear rate (
13
), that is,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k}-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\frac{2\varphi(\hat{x})}{\sigma}\Big(1-\frac{\sigma\lambda_{\min}(M)}{2\gamma}\Big)^{k}.

Proof.

By (
26
) and the minimizing property of
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
we conclude that

\displaystyle D_{\varphi}^{x_{k+1}^{*}}(x_{k+1},\hat{x})

\displaystyle\leq D_{\varphi}^{x_{k}^{*}}(x_{k},\hat{x})+\langle x_{k}-\hat{x},-\alpha\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})+\beta d_{k}^{*}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|-\alpha\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})+\beta d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=D_{\varphi}^{x_{k}^{*}}(x_{k},\hat{x})+\frac{\sigma}{2}\big(\|x_{k}-\hat{x}+\frac{1}{\sigma}(-\alpha_{k}\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})+\beta_{k}d_{k}^{*})\|_{2}^{2}-\|x_{k}-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq D_{\varphi}^{x_{k}^{*}}(x_{k},\hat{x})+\frac{\sigma}{2}\inf_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}}\big(\|x_{k}-\hat{x}+(-\alpha\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})+\beta d_{k}^{*})\|_{2}^{2}-\|x_{k}-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq D_{\varphi}^{x_{k}^{*}}(x_{k},\hat{x})+\frac{\sigma}{2}\big(\|(I-P_{\langle\{\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),d_{k}^{*}\}\rangle})(x_{k}-\hat{x})\|_{2}^{2}-\|x_{k}-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\big).

(31)

We can therefore apply Lemma
4.5
with
x_{k}-\hat{x}
instead of
x
,
\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})
instead of
y
and
d_{k}^{*}
instead of
z
. We calculate the expressions on the right-hand side in Lemma
4.5
as

\displaystyle(I-P_{y})x

\displaystyle=x_{k}-\hat{x}-\frac{\langle x_{k}-\hat{x},\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\rangle}{\|\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}}\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})

\displaystyle=x_{k}-\hat{x}-\frac{\|S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)\|_{2}^{2}}{\|\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}}\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),

\displaystyle\|(I-P_{z})\circ(I-P_{y})x\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{y}_{k}-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\langle\tilde{y}_{k},d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|x_{k}-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\|S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)\|_{2}^{4}}{\|\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\langle\tilde{y}_{k},d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}}

and as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
we can upper bound

\displaystyle\|(I-P_{z})\circ(I-P_{y})x\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|x_{k}-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\|S_{k}^{T}(\mathbf{A}x_{k}-b)\|_{2}^{2}}{\|\mathbf{A}^{T}S_{k}\|_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\langle\tilde{y}_{k},d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}}.

Finally, we compute the right term on the right-hand side in Lemma
4.5
as

\displaystyle\frac{\langle(I-P_{y})x,\ P_{z}y\rangle^{2}}{\|(I-P_{z})y\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\tilde{y}_{k},P_{d_{k}^{*}}\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})-P_{d_{k}^{*}}\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\tilde{y}_{k},\frac{\langle d_{k}^{*},\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\rangle}{\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}}d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})-\frac{\langle\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),d_{k}^{*}\rangle}{\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}}d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\tilde{y}_{k},d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}\langle\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}\big(\|\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}\|d_{k}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}-\langle\nabla f_{S_{k}}(x_{k}),d_{k}^{*}\rangle^{2}\big)}.

After inserting all expressions into (

), the assertion follows as in the proof of Theorem

.
∎

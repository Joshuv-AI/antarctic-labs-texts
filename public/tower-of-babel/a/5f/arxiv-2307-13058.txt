Abstract:

We prove that there is a constant
\overline{C}\in(0,\infty)
such that the effective mass
m(\alpha)
of the Fröhlich Polaron satisfies
m(\alpha)\geq\overline{C}\alpha^{4}
, which is sharp according to a long-standing prediction of Landau-Pekar
[
19
]
from 1948 and of Spohn
[
35
]
from 1987. The method of proof, which demonstrates how the
\alpha^{4}
divergence rate of
m(\alpha)
appears in a natural way, is based on analyzing the Gaussian representation of the Polaron measure and that of the associated tilted Poisson point process developed in
[
25
]
, together with an explicit identification of local interval process
in the strong coupling limit
\alpha\to\infty
in terms of functionals of the
Pekar variational formula
.

1.
Introduction and main result

The
Polaron problem
in quantum statistical mechanics is inspired by studying the slow movement
of a charged particle, e.g., an electron, in a crystal whose lattice sites are polarized by this slow motion. The electron then drags around it a cloud of polarized lattice
points which influences and determines the
effective behavior
of the electron.
A key quantity is given by the bottom of the spectrum

E_{\alpha}(P)=\inf\,\mathrm{spec}(H_{P})

of the (fiber) Hamiltonian of the Fröhlich Polaron.
It is known that
E_{\alpha}(\cdot)
is rotationally symmetric and is analytic when
P\approx 0
. Then the central objects of interest are the
ground state energy

g(\alpha)=-\min_{P}E_{\alpha}(P)

(1.1)

as well as the
effective mass

m(\alpha)
of the Fröhlich Polaron, defined as the inverse of the curvature:

m(\alpha)=\bigg[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial P^{2}}E_{\alpha}(P)\big|_{P=0}\bigg]^{-1}.

(1.2)

See
[
35
,
13
]
. Physically relevant questions concern the
strong-coupling behavior
of
g(\alpha)
and
m(\alpha)
. Indeed, the ground state energy in this regime was studied by Pekar
[
29
]
, who also conjectured that the limit

\displaystyle g_{0}:=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\frac{g(\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{exists, and }

(1.3)

\displaystyle g_{0}=\sup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\psi\in H^1(\R^3)}{|\psi|_2=1}}\bigg[\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{\psi^{2}(x)\psi^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\psi|_{2}^{2}\bigg].

By a well-known result of E. Lieb
[
20
]
, the above variational formula
g_{0}
admits a rotationally symmetric, smooth and centered maximizer
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
|\psi_{0}|_{2}=1
which is unique except for spatial translations. One can also obtain a probabilistic representation for
g(\alpha)
. Indeed, Feynman’s path integral formulation
[
14
]
leads to
g(\alpha)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\langle\Psi|{\rm e}^{-TH}|\Psi\rangle
with
\Psi
being chosen such that its spectral resolution contains the ground state energy or low energy spectrum of
H
, but is otherwise arbitrary. Then the Feynman-Kac formula for the semigroup
{\rm e}^{-TH}
implies that the last expression can be rewritten further as

g(\alpha)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[\exp\bigg\{\alpha\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}{\rm d}s{\rm d}t\,\,\frac{{\rm e}^{-{|t-s|}}}{|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|}\bigg\}\bigg],

(1.4)

with
\mathbb{E}_{0}
denoting expectation w.r.t. the law of a three-dimensional Brownian path starting at
0
. Starting with this expression and using large deviation theory from
[
11
]
, Pekar’s conjecture (
1.3
) was proved in
[
12
]
. Later, a different proof was given by E. Lieb and L. Thomas
[
22
]
using a functional analytic approach which also provided quantitative error bounds.

As for the effective mass defined in (
1.2
), according to a long-standing conjecture by Landau-Pekar
[
19
]
and by H. Spohn
[
35
]
,
m(\alpha)
should diverge like
\alpha^{4}
with a pre-factor given by the centered solution
\psi_{0}

of the Pekar variational problem (
1.3
)
in the strong coupling limit
\alpha\to\infty
. With this background, the main result of this article is to show the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a constant
\overline{C}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
\alpha>0
,

\frac{m(\alpha)}{\alpha^{4}}\geq\overline{C}.

(1.5)

As a consequence of the method used the prove Theorem
1.1
, we will also obtain auxiliary results including an explicit description of the Polaron measure and the point process method from
[
25
]
in terms of the
Pekar process
in strong coupling,
strict
monotonicity of the effective mass
m(\alpha)
for all
\alpha>0
and the above result for a generalized class of Polaron type interactions. These will be discussed in Section
1.2
. Before that, let us provide some background.

1.1.
Background: Polaron path measure.

In 1987, H. Spohn
[
35
]
established a link between the effective mass
m(\alpha)
and
the actual
path behavior
under the
Polaron measure
. Indeed, the exponential weight on the right hand side in (
1.4
) defines a tilted measure on the path space of the Brownian motion, or rather, on the space of increments of Brownian paths.
More precisely, let
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{T}
be the law of the Brownian increments
\{\omega(t)-\omega(s)\}_{-T\leq s<t\leq T}
for three dimensional Brownian motion. Then the
Polaron measure
is defined as the transformed measure

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,T}({\rm d}\omega)=\frac{1}{Z_{\alpha,T}}\exp\bigg(\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{{\rm e}^{-|t-s|}}{|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s\bigg)\mathbb{P}({\rm d}\omega),

(1.6)

where

Z_{\alpha,T}=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\bigg[\exp\bigg(\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{{\rm e}^{-|t-s|}}{|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s\bigg)\bigg]

is the total mass of the exponential weight, or the
partition function
.

It was conjectured by Spohn in
[
35
]
that for any fixed coupling
\alpha>0
and as
T\to\infty
,
the distribution of the diffusively rescaled Brownian path under the Polaron measure should be asymptotically Gaussian with zero mean and variance
\sigma^{2}(\alpha)>0
.
The following results were shown in
[
25
,
27
]
: for any
\alpha>0
: the infinite-volume Polaron measure

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,T}

exists, and it is an explicit mixture of Gaussian measures. Moreover, the distribution of the rescaled Brownian increments
\frac{\omega(T)-\omega(-T)}{\sqrt{2T}}
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,T}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
satisfies a central limit theorem. More precisely, for any
\alpha>0

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,T}\bigg[\frac{\omega(T)-\omega(-T)}{\sqrt{2T}}\in\cdot\bigg]

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}\bigg[\frac{\omega(T)-\omega(-T)}{\sqrt{2T}}\in\cdot\bigg]

(1.7)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{N}(0,\sigma^{2}(\alpha)\mathbf{I}_{3\times 3}),

where
\mathbf{N}(0,\sigma^{2}(\alpha)\mathbf{I}_{3\times 3})
is a three-dimensional Gaussian vector with mean zero and covariance matrix
\sigma^{2}(\alpha)\mathbf{I}_{3\times 3}
, which satisfies

\sigma^{2}(\alpha)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,T}}\big[\big|\omega(T)-\omega(-T)|^{2}\big]=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}}\big[\big|\omega(T)-\omega(-T)|^{2}\big]\in(0,1).

(1.8)

(see also
[
4
]
for an extension of these results to other polaron-type interactions). Note that the strict bound
\sigma^{2}(\alpha)<1
from (
1.8
) for any coupling
\alpha>0
reflects the attractive nature of the interaction defined in (
1.6
).
Assuming the validity of the above CLT (
1.7
), already in
[
35
]
Spohn proved a simple relation between the effective mass
m(\alpha)
and the CLT variance

\sigma^{2}(\alpha)
:

m(\alpha)^{-1}=\sigma^{2}(\alpha)\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{for any }\alpha>0,

(1.9)

see also Dybalski-Spohn
[
13
]
for a recent proof of the above relation using (
1.7
). In
[
35
]
, Spohn also conjectured that the
the strong coupling behavior of the infinite-volume limit
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\lim_{T\to\infty}\,\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,T}=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
, suitably rescaled,
should converge to the so-called
Pekar process
, which is a diffusion process with generator

\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\frac{\nabla\psi}{\psi}\cdot\nabla,

where
\psi
is any solution of the variational problem (
1.4
). This conjecture was proved in
[
26
]
showing that, after rescaling, the process
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
converges as
\alpha\to\infty
to a unique limit which is the
increments
of the Pekar process.

In
[
26
]
the distribution of the rescaled process

(\alpha|\omega(\frac{t}{\alpha^{2}})-\omega(\frac{s}{\alpha^{2}})|)_{s\in A,t\in B}
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
was shown to converge to the stationary version of the
increments of the Pekar process. This process was also shown in
[

,

]
to be the limiting object of the
mean-field
Polaron problem – convergence of the latter towards the Pekar process was also conjectured by Spohn in
[

]
.
Based on the path behavior of
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
, in
[
35
]
the decay rate of the CLT diffusion constant
\sigma^{2}(\alpha)\sim\alpha^{-4}
as
\alpha\to\infty
was also derived heuristically – note that, given the relation (
1.9
),
this decay rate would be equivalent to the divergence rate
m(\alpha)\sim\alpha^{4}
, conjectured by Landau and Pekar
[
19
]
.
Using a functional analytic route from
[
22
]
, it was shown in
[
21
]
that
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}m(\alpha)=\infty
.
By means of probabilistic techniques from
[
25
,
27
]
, it has been recently shown in
[
5
]
that
\sigma^{2}(\alpha)\leq c\alpha^{-2/5}
for some
c<\infty
. Very recently,
using the probabilistic representation of the Polaron measure (
1.6
) but invoking Gaussian correlation inequalities orthogonally to the current method,
it has been shown in
[
33
]
that
m(\alpha)\geq C\alpha^{4}/(\log\alpha)^{6}
.
For the corresponding upper bound
m(\alpha)\leq C^{*}\alpha^{4}
, we refer to the very recent article

[
8
]
that used a functional analytic route, combined with a concavity result from
[
30
]
which used the probabilistic route and point process method from
[
25
]
. The method currently developed for obtaining Theorem
1.1
for the Fröhlich Polaron is quite different from the ones found in the literature for showing previous bounds on
m(\alpha)
. We will outline this approach below and explain along the lines how the
\alpha^{4}
divergence rate of
m(\alpha)
appears in a natural way.

1.2.
An outline of the proof and constituent results.

The starting point is the method developed in
[
25
]
, where by writing the Coulomb potential
\frac{1}{|x|}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}}{\rm d}u
and expanding the exponential weight in (
1.6
) in a power series
for any
\alpha>0
and
T>0
, the Polaron measure

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,T}({\rm d}\omega)=\int\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\xi},\hat{u}}({\rm d}\omega)\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha,T}({\rm d}\hat{\xi}{\rm d}\hat{u})

(1.10)

was represented as a mixture of centered Gaussian measures

\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\xi},\hat{u}}
with variance

\mathrm{Var}^{\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\xi},\hat{u}}}\Big[\frac{\omega(T)-\omega(-T)}{\sqrt{2T}}\Big]=3\sup_{f\in H_{T}}\bigg[2~\frac{f(T)-f(-T)}{\sqrt{2T}}-\int_{-T}^{T}\dot{f}^{2}(t){\rm d}t-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{T}(\hat{\xi})}u_{i}^{2}|(f(t_{i})-f(s_{i}))|^{2}\bigg].

(1.11)

Here,
H_{T}
denotes all absolutely continuous functions on
[-T,T]
with square integrable derivatives (see
[
25
, Eq. (3.3)-(3.4)]
and Section
3.1
for a detailed review). In (
1.10
),
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha,T}({\rm d}\hat{\xi}{\rm d}\hat{u})
represents the law of a tilted Poisson point process taking values on the space of

(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
, with
\hat{\xi}=\{[s_{1},t_{1}],\dots,[s_{n},t_{n}]\}_{n\geq 0}
denoting a collection for (possibly overlapping) intervals
contained in
[-T,T]
and
\hat{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in(0,\infty)^{n}
denoting a string of positive numbers, with
each
u_{i}
being linked to the interval
[s_{i},t_{i}]
. For any fixed
\alpha>0
and as
T\to\infty
,
the limit
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha,T}
exists, can be identified explicitly and is stationary. Consequently, the infinite-volume limit
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,T}

also admits a Gaussian representation

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}(\cdot)=\int\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\xi},\hat{u}}(\cdot)\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}({\rm d}\hat{\xi}{\rm d}\hat{u})

(1.12)

analogous to (
1.10
), and
for any
\alpha>0
, the distributions of the rescaled increments
\frac{\omega(T)-\omega(-T)}{\sqrt{2T}}
, both under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,T}
and under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
, converge for any
\alpha>0
and as
T\to\infty

to a
3d
centered Gaussian law
N(0,\sigma^{2}(\alpha))
with variance given by the
L^{1}(\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha})
and
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
-a.s. limit

\sigma^{2}(\alpha)=\lim_{T\to\infty}3\sup_{f\in H_{T}}\bigg[2~\frac{f(T)-f(-T)}{\sqrt{2T}}-\int_{-T}^{T}\dot{f}^{2}(t){\rm d}t-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{T}(\hat{\xi})}u_{i}^{2}|(f(t_{i})-f(s_{i}))|^{2}\bigg].

(1.13)

We refer to Section
3.1
for a more detailed review of these arguments from
[
25
]
. To show Theorem
1.1
, we will show that as
\alpha\to\infty
the averaged supremum in (
1.13
) under
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
is bounded above by a constant times
\alpha^{-4}
. We will outline the proof of this bound now, along with a discussion of the auxiliary results which we will prove on the way. This task will be split now into four main steps.

Step 1 (Duality):
In the identity (
1.12
), given any realization of the point process
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
sampled according to
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
,
\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\xi},\hat{u}}
is a centered Gaussian measure. The first step is to develop this
duality
between
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
(resp.
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha,T}
) and
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}

(resp.
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,T}
) further. This was done in the follow up work
[
27
]
, where a simple but a very useful identity was introduced (see
[
27
, Eq. (1.11), p.1647]
) – namely, for any
\alpha>0
and any interval
[-A,A]
, conditional on the Brownian increments
\{\omega(t)-\omega(s)\}_{-A\leq s<t\leq A}
sampled according to
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
, the “quenched” law of the point process

\{(s_{i},t_{i},u_{i}):-A\leq s_{i}<t_{i}\leq A\}
under
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
, denoted by
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha,\omega}
, is a stationary ergodic Poisson point process with random intensity

\Lambda(\alpha,\omega,\,{\rm d}s{\rm d}t{\rm d}u):=\alpha\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}{\rm e}^{-\frac{u^{2}|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|^{2}}{2}}\,\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{-A\leq s<t\leq A}\,{\rm d}s{\rm d}t\,{\rm d}u.

(1.14)

Consequently, the quenched distribution of
any
function

\mathrm{f}(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})=\sum_{i}\mathrm{f}(s_{i},t_{i},u_{i})\hskip 20.00003pts_{i},t_{i}\in[-A,A]

under
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha,\omega}
is itself
Poisson distributed
with a
random
intensity determined by
\Lambda(\alpha,\omega)
. For instance,
the quenched distribution of the point process
\{(s_{i},t_{i},u_{i}):-A\leq s_{i}<t_{i}\leq A,u\geq 0\}
is Poissonian with random intensity (using again the identity
1/|x|=\sqrt{2/\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-u^{2}|x|^{2}}{\rm d}u
)

\alpha\frac{{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}}{|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{-A\leq s<t\leq A}{\rm d}s{\rm d}t=\alpha^{2}\frac{{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}}{\alpha|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{-A\leq s<t\leq A}{\rm d}s{\rm d}t

(1.15)

Likewise, for any
C,C^{\prime}>0
, the above conditional law of
\{(s_{i},t_{i},u_{i}):-A\leq s_{i}<t_{i}\leq A,C\alpha\leq u\leq C^{\prime}\alpha\}
is Poissonian with random intensity

\alpha^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}\bigg(\int_{C}^{C^{\prime}}{\rm d}u\,\,{\rm e}^{-\frac{u^{2}\alpha^{2}|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|^{2}}{2}}\bigg)\,\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{-A\leq s<t\leq A}\,{\rm d}s{\rm d}t

(1.16)

That is, the quenched intensity of the point process
\{(s_{i},t_{i},u_{i}):u_{i}\geq 0\}
under
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
shoots up to

\alpha^{2}\int_{-A\leq s<t\leq A}{\rm d}s{\rm d}t{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}V(\alpha|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|)

(1.17)

for a suitable function
V
defined on the
rescaled
increments
(\alpha|\omega(t)-\omega(s))_{-A\leq s<t\leq A}
sampled according to
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
.
The same statement holds for the distribution of the point process
\{(s_{i},t_{i},u_{i}):C\alpha\leq u_{i}\leq C^{\prime}\alpha\}
under
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}

with
rescaled

u_{i}\in[C\alpha,C^{\prime}\alpha]
. We refer to Theorem
3.2
in Section
3.2
for details.

Step 2 (Random intensities in strong coupling and Pekar process):
The next task is to determine the behavior of intensities
\Lambda(\alpha,\cdot)
of the above form under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
in the
strong coupling limit

\alpha\to\infty
. Indeed, in Theorem
2.1
we show that for a large class of functions
V
(including continuous bounded functions, and
V(x)=\frac{1}{|x|}
, and
V(|x|)=|x|
etc.),

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}}\bigg[\int\int_{-A\leq s<t\leq A}{\rm d}s{\rm d}t{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}V(\alpha|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|)\bigg]

(1.18)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{-A\leq s<t\leq A}{\rm d}s{\rm d}t{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}V(|x-y|)\psi_{0}^{2}(x)\psi_{0}^{2}(y)\bigg)\in(0,\infty),

where
\psi_{0}
denote the centered solution of the Pekar variational formula
g_{0}
(recall (
1.3
)) – we refer to Theorem
2.1
for details. Consequently, we have the convergence of the (averaged) distributions

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}\big[\alpha|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|\in\cdot\big]\Longrightarrow(\psi_{0}^{2}\otimes\psi_{0}^{2})\big[|x-y|\in\cdot\big]\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha\to\infty,\hskip 10.00002pts,t\in[-A,A],

see Corollary
2.2
for a precise statement.

We remark that, as mentioned previously, in
[
26
]
,
the distributions of the rescaled process

(\alpha|\omega(\frac{t}{\alpha^{2}})-\omega(\frac{s}{\alpha^{2}}|))_{s\in A,t\in B}
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
was shown to converge to the stationary version of the

\alpha|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
with

In particular, the integral on the LHS in (

) remain uniformly bounded away from zero under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
, and as an upshot, we get that the Poisson intensity in Step 1 remains for

.

The above weak convergence, in particular, yields tightness of the rescaled increments
\alpha|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}

). For technical reasons (to be explained in Step 5 below), we will also strengthen this tightness to a quantified version – namely, in Proposition

{\rm e}^{-\tilde{C}(\varepsilon)\alpha^{2}}\leq{\rm e}^{-\alpha}

Step 3: (Functionals of
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
under
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
and Pekar process):
We now apply the above duality to particular choices of
\mathrm{f}(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
and
combine Step 1 and Step 2 above to show that, with high probability and in average under
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
and as
T\to\infty
, followed by
\alpha\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{n_{T}(\hat{\xi})}{2\alpha^{2}T}\simeq 2g_{0}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla\psi_{0}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{\psi_{0}^{2}(x)\psi_{0}^{2}(y){\rm d}x{\rm d}y}{|x-y|},\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{where}\hskip 10.00002ptn_{T}(\hat{\xi}):=\#\big\{i:[s_{i},t_{i}]\subset[-T,T]\big\},

f(s,t,u)={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A\alpha\leq u\leq B\alpha}
.
∎

3.3.
FKG inequality on point processes

The purpose of this subsection is to develop an FKG correlation inequality for the space
\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
, equipped with its Poisson point process measure.
This inequality and its application in Section
3.4
will be used in Section
5
for the purpose of completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Additionally, it will also yield monotonicity and sub-additivity properties of independent interest (see Corollary
3.16
and Corollary
3.17
).

First recall the notation from (
3.3
) and that

\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}=\cup_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{n,T}
denotes the space of intervals
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
in
[-T,T]
. Also recall that
\Gamma_{\alpha,T}
denotes the law of the Poisson point process with intensity
\gamma_{\alpha}({\rm d}s{\rm d}t)\otimes{\rm d}u
,
where
\gamma_{\alpha}({\rm d}s{\rm d}t)={\rm e}^{-(t-s)}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{-T\leq s<t\leq T}{\rm d}s{\rm d}t
. The realizations of
\Gamma_{\alpha,T}
are denoted by
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})\in\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
. For notational simplicity, we will write
\Theta_{0}:=\Gamma_{\alpha,T}
.

In this section, we will need the following definitions.

Definition 3.7
.

(i)

We say a function
f:\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is coordinate-wise increasing if
f(\hat{\xi}\cup\{[s,t]\})\geq f(\hat{\xi})
for all
\hat{\xi}\in\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
and
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
(recall Remark
2
).

(ii)

We say that a probability measure
\Theta_{1}
on
\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
stochastically dominates another probability measure
\Theta_{2}
and write
\Theta_{1}\succeq\Theta_{2}
if there exists a coupling
(\hat{\xi}_{1},\hat{\xi}_{2})\in\Pi(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
such that
\hat{\xi}_{1}
contains
\hat{\xi}_{2}
almost surely.

(iii)

We say
f:\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is log-super-modular if

f(\hat{\xi}_{1}\vee\hat{\xi}_{2})f(\hat{\xi}_{1}\wedge\hat{\xi}_{2})\geq f(\hat{\xi}_{1})f(\hat{\xi}_{2}),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\hat{\xi}_{1},\hat{\xi}_{2}\in{\mathcal{J}}.

Here
\vee,\wedge
respectively denote union and intersection.

First, we recall the following characterization of stochastic domination, which is well-known in the discrete case.

Lemma 3.8

(
[
3
, Corollary 24.7]
)
.

Let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}
be probability measures on
\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
.
Then
\Theta_{1}\succeq\Theta_{2}
if and only if

{\mathbb{E}}^{\Theta_{1}}[f(\cdot)]\geq{\mathbb{E}}^{\Theta_{2}}[f(\cdot)]

for all coordinate-wise increasing functions
f:\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

We can now state the version of the FKG inequality we will use.

Proposition 3.9

(
[
15
, Corollary 1.2]
)
.

Let
F:\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
be log-super-modular and
\Theta_{0}
-integrable (recall
\Theta_{0}:=\Gamma_{\alpha,T}
).
Define a probability measure
\Theta_{F}
on
\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
by

{\rm d}\Theta_{F}:=\frac{1}{Z_{F}}F(\cdot){\rm d}\Theta_{0},\hskip 20.00003ptZ_{F}:=\mathbb{E}^{\Theta_{0}}[F].

Then
\Theta_{F}
is positively associated in the sense that for any coordinate-wise increasing
f,g:\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
,

{\mathbb{E}}^{\Theta_{F}}[fg]\geq{\mathbb{E}}^{\Theta_{F}}[f]{\mathbb{E}}^{\nu_{F}}[g].

(3.39)

Lemma 3.10
.

For
\Theta_{0}
defined previously, let

{\rm d}\Theta_{1}\propto f_{1}(\cdot){\rm d}\Theta_{0}
and

{\rm d}\Theta_{2}\propto f_{1}(\cdot)f_{2}(\cdot){\rm d}\Theta_{0}

where
f_{1}
is log-super-modular and
f_{2}
is increasing.
Then
\nu_{2}\succeq\nu_{1}
.

Proof.

For any bounded coordinate-wise increasing function
g:{\mathcal{J}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, Proposition
3.9
implies

{\mathbb{E}}^{\nu_{2}}[g]=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}^{\nu_{1}}[f_{2}g]}{{\mathbb{E}}^{\nu_{1}}[f_{2}]}\geq{\mathbb{E}}^{\nu_{1}}[g].

Then Lemma
3.8
implies the result.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
Q_{1},Q_{2}
be positive semi-definite finite rank quadratic forms on a separable Banach space equipped with a centered Gaussian measure
\mu
.
Then

{\mathbb{E}}^{\mu}[{\rm e}^{-Q_{1}-Q_{2}}]\geq{\mathbb{E}}^{\mu}[{\rm e}^{-Q_{1}}]{\mathbb{E}}^{\mu}[{\rm e}^{-Q_{2}}].

Proof.

This follows by direct covariance computation or by the functional Gaussian correlation inequality. (The finite rank condition immediately reduces us to the finite-dimensional setting.)
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

The function (recall Section
3.1
)

W(\hat{\xi},\hat{u}):=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[{\rm e}^{-Q_{\hat{\xi},\hat{u}}(\omega)}],\hskip 20.00003ptQ_{\hat{\xi},\hat{u}}(\omega):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\{[s,t],u\}\in(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})}u^{2}|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|^{2},

(3.40)

is log-super-modular.

Proof.

For convenience, we write
\hat{\xi}
for
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
. Then, given
\hat{\xi},\hat{\xi}^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
we let

Q_{0}=Q_{\hat{\xi}\cap\hat{\xi}^{\prime}},\hskip 10.00002ptQ_{1}=Q_{\hat{\xi}}-Q_{0}=Q_{\hat{\xi}\backslash\hat{\xi}^{\prime}},\hskip 10.00002ptQ_{2}=Q_{\hat{\xi}^{\prime}}-Q_{0}=Q_{\hat{\xi}^{\prime}\backslash\hat{\xi}}.

Then it suffices to show that
{\rm e}^{-Q_{1}},{\rm e}^{-Q_{2}}
are positively correlated under
{\rm e}^{-Q_{0}(\omega)}\mathbb{P}({\rm d}\omega)
.
This is a consequence of Lemma
3.11
since the latter measure is Gaussian.
∎

3.4.
Duality and stochastic domination of
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
by FKG comparison of potentials

We also recall from Section
3.1
that the key idea in
[
25
]
leading to the interval spaces introduced before was to expand
V(r)=1/r
as a positive combination of Gaussian densities:

V(x)=\frac{1}{x}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}x^{2}}{2}\right)~{\rm d}u.

(3.41)

This led to a coupling of the Polaron path measure to a tilted Poisson point process on intervals.
We will use an extension of this coupling which allows general positive combinations of Gaussian densities, and argue stochastic monotonicity by applying FKG to the interval process.

Let
\gamma(\cdot)
on
\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
be a positive Borel measure, and
\{\gamma_{[s,t]}(\cdot)\}_{0\leq s<t\leq T}
a choice of regular conditional distributions on
(0,\infty)
.
We will assume that the latter family is uniformly locally finite on
{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.

\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\gamma_{[s,t]}([-A,A])<\infty,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall A\in(0,\infty).

(3.42)

Then let

V_{[s,t],\gamma}:=\int\exp\bigg(-\frac{u^{2}x^{2}}{2}\bigg)~\gamma_{[s,t]}({\rm d}u).

(3.43)

We write simply
V_{\gamma}
if all the
\gamma_{[s,t]}
are identical.
We assume throughout that
\gamma
is such that the corresponding partition function

Z_{\gamma(\cdot),T}={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\bigg[\exp\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}{\rm e}^{-|s-t|}V_{[s,t],\gamma}(|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|)~{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}t\bigg)\bigg]

(3.44)

is finite.
Hence for finiteness of
Z_{\gamma(\cdot),T}
, it suffices for the measure
\gamma(\cdot)
to have uniformly bounded density.
Assuming this, we denote by
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\gamma(\cdot),T}
the associated Polaron path measure.
For example, the constant density
\gamma(\cdot)\equiv\alpha
recovers the Polaron measure
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,T}
.

Also, given the measure
\gamma(\cdot)
, there is a point process of weighted intervals
([s,t],u)
for
0\leq a<b\leq T
and
u\geq 0
. Let
\Theta_{\gamma(\cdot)}
be law the Poisson process on
\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
with intensity
\gamma(\cdot){\rm e}^{-(t-s)}
.
To a collection
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})=\{([s_{i},t_{i}],u_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
, using the same weight
W(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
from (
3.40
), we have, similar to (
3.9
):

\widehat{\Theta}_{\gamma(\cdot)}({\rm d}\hat{\xi}{\rm d}\hat{u})\propto W(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})\,\,\Theta_{\gamma(\cdot)}({\rm d}\hat{\xi}{\rm d}\hat{u}).

(3.45)

Lemma 3.13
.

There exists a coupling of the laws of the Brownian increments
\omega
and pairs
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
with:

(a)

Conditional on
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
sampled according to
\widehat{\Theta}_{\gamma(\cdot),T}
, the conditional distribution of the Brownian increments
\omega
is the corresponding Gaussian measure

\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\xi},\hat{u}}
.

(b)

Conditional on
\omega
sampled according to

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\gamma(\cdot),T}
,
the law of
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
under

\widehat{\Theta}_{\gamma(\cdot),T}
is Poissonian with intensity
\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|^{2}}{2}-|t-s|\right)\gamma(\cdot)
.

Proof.

This follows exactly the same proof as in
[
25
]
(recall Section
3.1
) and Theorem
3.2
).
∎

Lemma 3.14
.

If
\gamma_{1}(A)\leq\gamma_{2}(A)
for all Borel
A\subseteq\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
, then
\widehat{\Theta}_{\gamma_{1}(\cdot)}\preceq\widehat{\Theta}_{\gamma_{2}(\cdot)}
as measures on
\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
.

Proof.

The assumption implies the Radon–Nikodym derivative
\frac{{\rm d}\nu_{\gamma_{2}}}{{\rm d}\nu_{\gamma_{1}}}

is an increasing function on
\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
.
Combining Lemma
3.12
and Lemma
3.10
yields the result.
∎

Corollary 3.15
.

Suppose
\gamma_{1}(A)\leq\gamma_{2}(A)
for all Borel
A\subseteq(0,\infty)
, and let
F:C([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a symmetric quasi-convex function, such as

. Then

{\mathbb{E}}^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\gamma_{1}(\cdot),T}}[F]\geq{\mathbb{E}}^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\gamma_{2}(\cdot),T}}[F].

Proof.

By the definition of stochastic domination, it suffices to show that if
\hat{\xi}_{1}\subseteq\hat{\xi}_{2}
, then

{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\xi}_{1}}}[F]\geq{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\xi}_{2}}}[F].

This follows by the Gaussian correlation inequality.
∎

Recall from Lemma
3.1
the formula
\sigma^{2}(\alpha)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\sigma^{2}_{\alpha,T}(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
for the CLT variance from
[
25
]
and that the effective mass satisfies

. The above ideas then immediately give the monotonicity and sub-additivity we mentioned previously.

.

The effective mass

is strictly increasing in

.

Proof.

The uniform intensity
\gamma(\cdot)\equiv\alpha
on
\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}_{T}
is monotone in

, so
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
is strictly stochastically increasing by Lemma

.

3.15
implies the claim.
∎

.

Let

.
Since
\alpha\geq\alpha\cdot({\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{s,t\leq T_{1}}+{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{s,t\geq T_{1}})

.

4.
Estimating the CLT variance
\sigma^{2}(\alpha)
: proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Recall from (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\alpha,T}^{2}(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})=3\sup_{f\in H_{T}}\bigg[2~\frac{f(T)-f(-T)}{\sqrt{2T}}-\int_{-T}^{T}\dot{f}^{2}(t){\rm d}t-\sum_{-T\leq s_{i}<t_{i}\leq T}u_{i}^{2}|(f(t_{i})-f(s_{i}))|^{2}\bigg],

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\sigma^{2}(\alpha)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\sigma^{2}_{\alpha,T}(\cdot,\cdot)\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{a.s. and $L^{1}(\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha})$.}

Our goal is to show the following result, which will imply Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 4.1
.

There is a constant
\overline{C}\in(0,\infty)

such that for any
\alpha>0
,

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\alpha^{4}\sigma_{\alpha,T}^{2}(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})\leq\frac{1}{\overline{C}}\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}\text{-}a.s.

(4.2)

Consequently, for any
\alpha>0
,

\sigma^{2}(\alpha)\leq\frac{1}{\overline{C}\alpha^{4}},\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{and equivalently,}\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{m(\alpha)}{\alpha^{4}}\geq\overline{C}.

(4.3)

Our first step is the following observation, stated as

Lemma 4.2
.

If for every
f\in H_{T}
it holds that

\frac{f(T)-f(-T)}{\sqrt{2T}}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}\sqrt{\overline{C}}}\sqrt{\int_{-T}^{T}f^{\prime 2}(t){\rm d}t+\sum_{-T\leq s_{i}<t_{i}\leq T}u_{i}^{2}|f(t_{i})-f(s_{i})|^{2}}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{for some $K>0$,}

(4.4)

then

\sigma_{\alpha,T}^{2}(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})\leq\frac{1}{\overline{C}\alpha^{4}}.

Proof.

Let

Q_{T}(f):=Q_{T}(f,\hat{\xi},\hat{u})=\int_{-T}^{T}f^{\prime 2}(t){\rm d}t+\sum_{-T\leq s_{i}<t_{i}\leq T}u_{i}^{2}|f(t_{i})-f(s_{i})|^{2}.

If (
4.4
) holds, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{T}^{2}(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in H_{T}}\bigg[2~\frac{f(T)-f(-T)}{\sqrt{2T}}-\int_{-T}^{T}\dot{f}^{2}(t){\rm d}t-\sum_{-T\leq s_{i}<t_{i}\leq T}u_{i}^{2}|(f(t_{i})-f(s_{i}))|^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in H_{T}}\Big[\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}\sqrt{\overline{C}}}\sqrt{Q_{T}(f)}-Q_{T}(f))\Big]=\sup_{f\in H_{T}}\bigg[\frac{1}{\overline{C}\alpha^{4}}-\bigg(\sqrt{Q_{T}(f)}-\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}\sqrt{\overline{C}}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg]\leq\frac{1}{\overline{C}\alpha^{4}}.

∎

4.1.
A sufficient condition for (
4.4
) to hold.

We now provide a criterion for (
4.4
) to hold.
Let us first describe the main idea. For technical reasons, instead of working on
[-T,T]
, we will work on the time horizon
[0,T_{0}]
for some
T_{0}=T_{0}(\alpha)\gg\alpha
sufficiently large (see Remark
4
below for an explanation).

First, recall from Theorem
3.2
that given a realization of Brownian increments
\omega
, the conditional law
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha,\omega}
of the point process
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
is Poissonian with random intensity
\Lambda(\alpha,\omega)
. That is, if we fix a realization
\omega
, this determines a collection of intervals
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
.

Next, we recall from Corollary
3.6
that in the interval
[0,T_{0}]
, under
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
, on average there are
(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\psi_{0}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x)\alpha^{2}T_{0}
-many intervals
[s,t]
, the lengths of all these intervals are exponentially distributed (i.e., the lengths of all these intervals are of order one, i.e., these remain uniformly bounded in
\alpha
) and the corresponding
u
(attached to each interval
[s,t]
) satisfies
A\alpha\leq u\leq B\alpha
. We will therefore refer to these intervals as
standard
and restrict only to these standard intervals from our point process
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
realization.

Using these standard intervals, we will construct
\delta\alpha^{2}

runs
, or
paths
of intervals (for some absolute constant
\delta\in(0,1)
to be chosen later), where each run will consist of
T_{0}
many standard disjoint intervals. More concretely, the
L
-th round, or path, will be defined by a collection

\displaystyle\eta_{L}:=\bigg\{\big[s_{1}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}},t_{1}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}\big],\big[s_{2}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}},t_{2}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}\big],\dots,\big[s_{T_{0}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}},t_{T_{0}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}\big]:\forall k=0,\dots,T_{0},\,\,\,\,2(k-1)+\frac{1}{3}\leq s^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{k}\leq 2(k-1)+1\,\,\mbox{and}

(4.5)

\displaystyle 2k+\frac{1}{3}\leq t^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{k}\leq 2k+\frac{2}{3},\hskip 10.00002ptA\alpha\leq u^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{k}\leq B\alpha\bigg\}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{for}\hskip 20.00003ptL=1,\dots,\delta\alpha^{2}.

of
disjoint
intervals in
[0,T_{0}]
, and at each step
L
, we will work with a
new
set of intervals from
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
, i.e.,
\eta_{\ell}\cap\eta_{\ell^{\prime}}=\emptyset
for all
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
– we refer to Section
6.2
for details

The
vacant period

V_{L}
of the
L
-th round is denoted by the union of the “gaps”:

V_{L}=\big[0,s^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{1}\big)\,\bigcup\,\big(t^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{1},s^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{2}\big)\,\bigcup\,\big(t^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{2},s^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{3}\big)\,\bigcup\dots\bigcup\big(t^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{T_{0}},T_{0}\big],\hskip 20.00003ptL=1,\dots,\delta\alpha^{2}.

(4.6)

We choose a point
x\in[-T,T]
and define the random variable

U_{N}(x)=U_{N}(x,\hat{\xi},\alpha)=\sum_{L=1}^{N}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{x\in V_{L}}

(4.7)

That is,
U_{N}(x)
is the number of times the point
x\in[0,T_{0}]
is caught in a vacant period
V_{L}
if we consider
N
rounds. Note that, for any
J=1,\dots,\delta\alpha^{2}
, conditional on the event
\{U_{N}(x)=J\}
, the count
U_{N+1}(x)
at the immediate next step either goes up by one and becomes
J+1
, or the count remains the same at
J
. In the first part of Corollary
4.4
below, we will show that whenever
\sup_{\alpha>0}\frac{1}{T_{0}}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha,\omega}}\big[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}U_{\delta\alpha^{2}}(x)^{2}{\rm d}x\big]<\infty
, then the criterion (
4.4
) holds. In fact, in Lemma
6.4
, we will prove the following.

.

Let
T_{0}=T_{0}(\alpha)=\alpha^{10}
. Then there is an event
E_{\alpha}
of Brownian increments on
[0,T_{0}]
with
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}[E_{\alpha}]\leq{\rm e}^{-\alpha}
, such that for any

and some

) so that,

\limsup_{\alpha\to\infty}\frac{1}{T_{0}}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha,\omega}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}U_{\delta\alpha^{2}}(x)^{2}{\rm d}x\bigg]=:\overline{A}<\infty.

(4.8)

Proposition (
4.3
) is proved in Section
6.3
. Assuming this fact, we can now conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
:

Corollary 4.4
.

The validity of (
4.8
) implies the validity of (
4.4
) for
T=T_{0}=\alpha^{10}
(and
-T=0
). This, in turn, implies the validity of (

.

Proof.

With
T_{0}=\alpha^{10}
, fix

and a collection of intervals

). Then for any step

\displaystyle f(T_{0})-f(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{V_{L}}f^{\prime}(t){\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{T_{0}}(f(t^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{k})-f(s^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{k}))

\displaystyle\leq\int_{V_{L}}f^{\prime}(t){\rm d}t+\frac{1}{A\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{T_{0}}u_{k}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}|f(t^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{k})-f(s^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({L}})}}_{k})|.

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}|f^{\prime}(t)|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\Big(\sum_{L=1}^{\delta\alpha^{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{V_{L}}(t)\Big)^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\sum_{L=1}^{\delta\alpha^{2}}T_{0}}{A\alpha}\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{\delta\alpha^{2}}T_{0}}\sum_{(s_{i},t_{i})\in\bigcup_{L=1}^{\delta\alpha^{2}}\eta_{L}}u_{i}|f(t_{i})-f(s_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}|f^{\prime}(t)|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\Big(\sum_{L=1}^{\delta\alpha^{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{V_{L}}(t)\Big)^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{L=1}^{\delta\alpha^{2}}T_{0}}}{A\alpha}\sqrt{\sum_{(s_{i},t_{i})\in\bigcup_{L=1}^{\delta\alpha^{2}}\eta_{L}}u_{i}^{2}|f(t_{i})-f(s_{i})|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\overline{A}T_{0}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}|f^{\prime}(t)|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{T_{0}\delta\alpha^{2}}}{A\alpha}\sqrt{\sum_{0<s_{i}<t_{i}<T}u_{i}^{2}|f(t_{i})-f(s_{i})|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\overline{A}T_{0}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}|f^{\prime}(t)|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{T_{0}\delta}}{A}\sqrt{\sum_{0<s_{i}<t_{i}<T}u_{i}^{2}|f(t_{i})-f(s_{i})|^{2}}

In the third upper bound above, we applied Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the first term, while in the fourth inequality, we applied Jensen’s inequality to the second term. In the fifth inequality, we invoked the estimate from Proposition
4.3
(where
\overline{A}
comes from) for the first summand. We now use the bound
\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y}\leq 2\sqrt{x+y}
and define
\frac{1}{\sqrt{C}}:=2\max\{\frac{\sqrt{\overline{A}}}{\delta},\frac{1}{A\sqrt{\delta}}\}
to conclude the proof of (
4.4
) for
T=T_{0}
.

It remains to prove (
4.3
).
By subadditivity of variance (Corollary
3.17
), it suffices to prove a variance upper bound for
T_{0}:=\alpha^{10}
. Indeed, by Corollary
3.17
, together with stationarity,

\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}}[\sigma^{2}_{\alpha,T}]\leq\frac{T}{T_{0}}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}}[\sigma^{2}_{\alpha,T_{0}}].

Now we can construct the interval paths on
[0,\alpha^{10}]
using Proposition
4.3
. If we now apply Lemma
4.2
in the time interval
[0,T_{0}]
for
T_{0}=\alpha^{10}
, then for some
C\in(0,\infty)
, we have

\limsup_{\alpha\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}}[\sigma^{2}_{\alpha,T_{0}}]}{T_{0}}\leq C\alpha^{-4},\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{so that}\hskip 20.00003pt\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}}[\sigma^{2}_{\alpha,T}]}{T}\leq C\alpha^{-4},

as required. Recall that, for any
\alpha>0
, the CLT variance
\sigma^{2}(\alpha)
is the
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
-almost sure limit
\lim_{T\to\infty}\sigma^{2}_{\alpha,T}(\cdot,\cdot)/{T}
of the quenched variance and it is also the limit
\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}}[\sigma^{2}_{\alpha,T}(\cdot,\cdot)]/{T}
of the corresponding annealed version. Hence, we have shown that
\sigma^{2}(\alpha)\leq 1/(\overline{C}\alpha^{4})
for some absolute constant
\overline{C}\in(0,\infty)
, as required. This proves the corollary.
∎

Remark

)

5.
High interval density with very high probability

The main goal of this section is to prove Proposition
5.4
,
which quantifies the tightness statement from Corollary
2.2
of the rescaled increments
\alpha|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
in an averaged sense on order one time scale. From this result, we will deduce Corollary
5.5
, which will be subsequently used in Section
6
to complete the proof of Proposition
4.3
.

To derive Proposition
5.4
, we will first derive the quantitative tightness in Lemma
5.3
for a modified interaction, then use Lemma
3.14
and duality between
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
and

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
again to transfer the result to the Polaron interaction.

5.1.
Quantified tightness under a modified interaction.

We will apply the comparison results from Section
3.4
with

\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}({\rm d}s{\rm d}t{\rm d}u):=C_{1}\alpha\cdot{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{u\leq\alpha}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{0\leq s<t\leq 3}\,\,{\rm d}s{\rm d}t\,{\rm d}u.

(5.1)

Here
1\ll C_{1}\ll\alpha
, i.e.,
C_{1}
will be chosen to be a sufficiently large absolute constant. Since the density of
\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}
does not depend on
0\leq s<t\leq 3
, we write
V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}
instead of
V_{[s,t],\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}
, and implicitly restrict attention to
[0,3]
.
By Lemma
3.14
, this measure is dominated by the original point process
\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha}
(corresponding to Polaron) up to a constant factor (the
\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}
does not play a role here since the Polaron corresponds to
\gamma(\cdot)\equiv\alpha
):

\widehat{\Theta}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}\preceq\widehat{\Theta}_{C_{1}\alpha}.

(5.2)

It will be useful to define the smooth function

g(y)=\int_{0}^{1}{\rm e}^{-v^{2}y^{2}/2}~{\rm d}v,\hskip 10.00002pty\geq 0,\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{so that}\hskip 10.00002ptg(\cdot)\leq g(0)

and the explicit constant

A_{*}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{3}\int_{0}^{3}{\rm e}^{-|t-s|}~{\rm d}t~{\rm d}s=\int_{0}^{3}\int_{0}^{3}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{s<t}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}~{\rm d}t~{\rm d}s\in[4,4.1].

(5.3)

Lemma 5.1
.

For
\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}
as in (
5.1
), the associated
V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(x)=V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(|x|)
is given by

V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(x)=C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(\alpha x).

Proof.

Substituting
v=u/\alpha
, we have

V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(x)=C_{1}\alpha\int_{0}^{\alpha}{\rm e}^{-u^{2}x^{2}/2}~{\rm d}u=C_{1}\alpha^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm e}^{-v^{2}(\alpha x)^{2}/2}~{\rm d}v\equiv C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(\alpha x).\qed

We also let
\varepsilon,C_{0}
be such that
1\ll 1/\varepsilon\ll C_{0}\ll C_{1}\ll\alpha
(we think of everything except
\alpha
as an absolute constant, in particular, the other constants are not sent to infinity at any point).

Lemma 5.2
.

For
{\mathbb{P}}
Wiener measure and any
\eta>0
,
let
E_{\eta}
be the event that
\sup_{t\in[0,3]}|\omega_{t}|\leq\eta
. Then

{\mathbb{P}}[E_{\eta}]\geq{\rm e}^{-C_{0}\eta^{-2}}.

Proof.

The probability lower bound holds even if we additionally require that
|\omega_{j\eta^{2}}|\leq\eta/2
for all integers
0\leq j\leq\eta^{-2}
.
Indeed, splitting up time at each time in
\eta^{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
, we just need a constant probability event to happen
\eta^{-2}
consecutive times.
Namely if one conditions on some
\omega_{[0,j\eta^{2}]}
-dependent event satisfying
|\omega_{j\eta^{2}}|\leq\eta/2
, then with constant
{\mathbb{P}}
-conditional probability:

\displaystyle|\omega_{(j+1)\eta^{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq\eta/2,\hskip 20.00003pt\sup_{j\eta^{2}\leq t\leq(j+1)\eta^{2}}|\omega_{t}|\leq\eta.

(In particular, this probability does not depend on
\eta
by Brownian scaling.) This gives the bound.
∎

Since
C_{1}\gg C_{0}
and
g:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is just a fixed function, we may assume that

\displaystyle g(C_{1}^{-1/3})

\displaystyle\geq g(0)\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\right),

(5.4)

\displaystyle g(C_{1})

\displaystyle\leq g(0)/2.

(5.5)

Using free energy estimates, we now obtain a key confinement result.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
,
0\leq j\leq T
and
C_{1}>0
, we write

E_{\star}(\alpha):=\left\{\omega:\frac{1}{A_{\star}}\int_{j\leq s<t\leq j+3}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\alpha|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|\geq C_{1}}~{\rm d}t~{\rm d}s\geq\varepsilon\right\}.

(5.6)

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, and
C_{0}>\frac{4}{\varepsilon}
and
C_{1}\geq\frac{C_{0}}{g(0)A_{*}}
satisfying (
5.4
)-(
5.5
) and for any
\alpha>0
it holds that

p_{\star}(\alpha):=\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}[E_{\star}(\alpha)]\leq{\rm e}^{-\alpha^{2}}.

Proof.

We recall that

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}({\rm d}\omega)=\frac{1}{Z_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}}\exp\bigg(\int_{0}^{3}\int_{0}^{3}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{s<t}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|){\rm d}s{\rm d}t\bigg)\mathbb{P}({\rm d}\omega)

We start with a lower bound on the partition function:

\displaystyle Z_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{3}\int_{0}^{3}1_{s<t}e^{-|s-t|}V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|)~{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}t\right)

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\left[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{E_{\frac{1}{2\alpha C_{1}^{1/3}}}}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{3}\int_{0}^{3}1_{s<t}e^{-|s-t|}V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|)~{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}t\right)\right]

We now use the definition of event
E_{\eta}
for

\eta={\frac{1}{2\alpha C_{1}^{1/3}}}
(recall Lemma
5.2
)
to estimate that, on this event,

V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s})=C_{1}\alpha^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm e}^{-\frac{v^{2}(\alpha|\omega(t-s)-\omega(0)|)^{2}}{2}}{\rm d}v=C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(\alpha|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|)\geq C\alpha^{2}g(0)\big(1-\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\big).

In the last lower bound, we have used (
5.4
). Thus, applying Lemma
5.2
, we have the lower bound

\displaystyle Z_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\bigg(A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)\big(1-\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\big)\bigg)\,\,\mathbb{P}(E_{\frac{1}{2\alpha C_{1}^{1/3}}})

\displaystyle\geq\exp\bigg(A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)\big(1-\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\big)\bigg){\rm e}^{-C_{0}4\alpha^{2}C_{1}}

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)-\frac{A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)}{C_{0}}+\frac{A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)}{2C_{0}}-4\alpha^{2}C_{0}C^{2/3}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(A_{*}C_{1}g(0)\alpha^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{C_{0}}\right)\right).

where in the last lower bound, we used that, since
C_{1}\gg C_{0}
, we have
C_{1}^{\frac{1}{3}}\gg\frac{8C_{0}^{2}}{A_{\star}g(0)}
.
It then follows from the definition of
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}
and the above lower bound on
Z_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}
that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}\left[\int_{0\leq s<t\leq 3}e^{-(t-s)}V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|)~{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}t\leq A_{*}C_{1}g(0)\alpha^{2}\left(1-\frac{2}{C_{0}}\right)\right]

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{Z_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}}\,\,\exp\big(A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)\big)\exp\big(-A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)\frac{2}{C_{0}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\big(A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)\big)\exp\bigg(-A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)\frac{2}{C_{0}}\bigg)\,\,\exp\big(-A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)\big)\exp\bigg(\frac{A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)}{C_{0}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{-A_{*}C_{1}g(0)\alpha^{2}/C_{0}}\leq{\rm e}^{-\alpha^{2}}

if

.
On the other hand, from (

) and unimodality of

, we have the implication

|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|\geq C_{1}/\alpha\implies V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(\omega_{t}-\omega_{s})\leq Cg(C_{1})\alpha^{2}\leq C_{1}g(0)\alpha^{2}/2

for all
0\leq s<t\leq 3
. Using this pointwise bound for all
0\leq s<t\leq 3
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0\leq s<t\leq 3}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\alpha|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|>C_{1}}{\rm d}s{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)}\int_{0\leq s<t\leq 3}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}\big[C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)-V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(\omega_{t}-\omega_{s})\big]{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\alpha|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|>C_{1}}{\rm d}s{\rm d}t

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)}\int_{0\leq s<t\leq 3}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}\big[C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)-V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(\omega_{t}-\omega_{s})\big]

where in the last inequality, we again used the unimodality of
g(\cdot)
(which enforces that
V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(x)=C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(\alpha x)\geq C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)
, implying the non-negativity of the term inside the bracket in the integral above).
Using (
5.8
), we thus have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(E_{\star}(\alpha))

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}\bigg[\frac{1}{A_{\star}}\int_{j\leq s<t\leq j+3}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\alpha|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|\geq C_{1}}~{\rm d}t~{\rm d}s\geq\varepsilon\bigg]

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}\bigg[\frac{2}{A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)}\int_{0\leq s<t\leq 3}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}\big[C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)-V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(\omega_{t}-\omega_{s})\big]{\rm d}s{\rm d}t\geq\varepsilon\bigg]

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}\bigg[\int_{0\leq s<t\leq 3}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}V_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}){\rm d}s{\rm d}t\leq(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2})A_{\star}C_{1}\alpha^{2}g(0)\bigg]

Now, given
\varepsilon>0
we choose
C_{0}=C_{0}(\varepsilon)>\frac{4}{\varepsilon}
so that
1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<1-\frac{2}{C_{0}}
so that combining (
5.9
) and (
5.7
) we have that
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}(E_{\star}(\alpha))\leq{\rm e}^{-\alpha^{2}}
, as desired.
∎

5.2.
Quantified tightness under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
.

Next, we transfer this result to the original Polaron measure. A bit of care is needed here since we cannot directly compare the integrands in, e.g., (
5.10
), but have to compare the associated interval processes instead.
The idea of the proof below is to define a set of
standard
intervals in (
5.11
) below, the number
n_{\footnotesize\textbf{{std}}}
of which serves as a proxy for the scale of fluctuations of
\omega
. Stochastic domination of point processes lets us directly compare the number of standard intervals, allowing us to transfer, using duality again, the preceding result to
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
at the cost of some constant factors.

Proposition 5.4
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and integer
0\leq j\leq T
and
\tilde{C}>0
, let

\displaystyle E_{\star\star}(\alpha):=\bigg\{\omega\colon\frac{1}{A_{\star}}\int_{j\leq s<t\leq j+3}{\rm d}s{\rm d}t{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\alpha|\omega_{t}-\omega_{s}|\geq{\tilde{C}}}\geq\varepsilon\bigg\}.

Then for any
0<\varepsilon<1
, there are constants
\tilde{C}=\tilde{C}(\varepsilon)
and
\tilde{C}_{1}=\tilde{C}_{1}(\varepsilon)
such that for
\alpha>0
large enough,

p_{\star\star}(\alpha):=\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}[E_{\star\star}(\alpha)]\leq{\rm e}^{-\tilde{C}_{1}(\varepsilon)\alpha^{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{thus in particular}\hskip 10.00002ptp_{\star\star}(\alpha)\leq{\rm e}^{-2\alpha}.

Proof.

We fix
\varepsilon>0
. We choose
\tilde{C}=\frac{C_{1}^{3}}{\varepsilon}
, where
C_{1}=C_{1}(\varepsilon^{2})
is defined in Lemma
5.3
. By the comparison (
5.2
), we will analyse
E_{\star\star}(C_{1}\alpha)
under the measure
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{C_{1}\alpha}

Recall from Theorem
3.2
(and also Lemma
3.13
(b)
) that

\widehat{\Theta}_{\alpha,\omega}
(resp.
\widehat{\Theta}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha},\omega}
) denotes the law of
(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})
conditional on
\omega|_{[0,T]}

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
(resp.
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}
) – this law is Poissonian with
\omega

\omega|_{[0,T]}
. We recall Corollary
3.6
and set the

([s,t],u)\in(\hat{\xi},\hat{u})

j\leq s<t\leq j+3,\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002ptu\in[\alpha/C_{1}^{2},2\alpha/C_{1}^{2}].

(5.11)

We let
n_{\footnotesize\textbf{{std}}}
be the number of standard intervals, which is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{\widehat{\Theta}_{C_{1}\alpha,\omega}}[n_{\footnotesize\textbf{{std}}}~|~\omega_{[0,T]}]

\displaystyle=C_{1}\alpha\int_{0\leq s\leq t<3}\int_{\alpha/C_{1}^{2}}^{2\alpha/C_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}|\omega_{s}-\omega_{t}|^{2}}{2}-(t-s)\right){\rm d}u~{\rm d}s{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{C_{1}}\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s\leq t\leq 3\\

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{C_{1}}A_{*}\big(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big).

(5.12)

In particular combining with (
5.12

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}^{\widehat{\Theta}_{C_{1}\alpha,\omega}}\bigg[n_{\footnotesize\textbf{{std}}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{C_{1}}A_{*}\big(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\big)\Big)~\bigg|~E_{\star\star}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq 1/2

(5.13)

\displaystyle\implies{\mathbb{P}}^{\widehat{\Theta}_{C_{1}\alpha}}\bigg[n_{\footnotesize\textbf{{std}}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{C_{1}}A_{*}\big(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq p_{\star\star}(C_{1}\alpha)/2.

Next we return to
\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{\gamma}_{\alpha}}
. We can use Lemma
5.3
to similarly lower bound the number of intervals obeying (

).

p_{\star}(\alpha)\leq{\rm e}^{-\alpha^{2}}
.
Indeed if

Conditioned on the complementary event

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\log\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\big[\exp[\alpha F(T,\omega)\big]\big]\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\sup_{x}\log\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}_{x}}\left[\exp\left(\alpha c_{n}G_{n}(\omega)\right)\right].

It remains to show that the right hand side is finite. Indeed, recalling the definition of
G_{n}
and using Jensen’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}_{x}}\left[\exp\left(\alpha c_{n}G_{n}(\omega)\right)\right]\leq\int_{n}^{n+1}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}_{0}}\left[{\rm e}^{\alpha c_{n}|\omega(u)|}\right]{\rm d}u,

where we used that, for a fixed
u
,
\omega(u)-\omega(0)
under
\mathbb{P}_{x}
has the same distribution as
\omega(u)
under
\mathbb{P}_{0}
. Noting that
\omega(u)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\omega^{i}(u)|
and the independence of the coordinates, we deduce that

\int_{n}^{n+1}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}_{0}}\left[{\rm e}^{\alpha c_{n}|\omega(u)|}\right]{\rm d}u\leq\int_{n}^{n+1}\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm e}^{3\alpha c_{n}|X(u)|}\right]{\rm d}u,

where
X(u)\sim N(0,u)
. In particular,
\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm e}^{3\alpha c_{n}|X(u)|}\right]\leq 2{\rm e}^{9\alpha^{2}c_{n}^{2}u}
. A crude bound give us

\int_{n}^{n+1}\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm e}^{3\alpha c_{n}|X(u)|}\right]{\rm d}u\leq 2{\rm e}^{9\alpha^{2}c_{n}^{2}(n+1)},

so that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\sup_{x}\log\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}_{x}}\left[\exp\left(\alpha c_{n}G_{n}(\omega)\right)\right]\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\log(2)}{2^{n+1}}+9\frac{\alpha^{2}c_{n}^{2}(n+1)}{2^{n+1}}\right),

which is clearly summable since
c_{n}:=(n+1)2^{n+1}{\rm e}^{-n}
.

∎

Completing the proof of Proposition
A.1
:

From Lemma
A.3
it follows that, for any
\alpha,\lambda>0
,

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\log\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\bigg[\exp\bigg[\alpha\lambda\int\int_{-T\leq s<t\leq T}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}|(\omega(t)-\omega(s))|{\rm d}s{\rm d}t\bigg]\bigg]\leq C(\alpha,\lambda)<\infty.

Thus, with
V(x)=|x|
,
V_{M}=V\wedge M
and
Y_{M}=V-V_{M}
, we have for any
M>0
,

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\log\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[\exp[\alpha\lambda\int\int_{-T\leq s<t\leq T}{\rm e}^{-(t-s)}Y_{M}(|(\omega(t)-\omega(s))|){\rm d}s{\rm d}t]]\leq C_{M}(\alpha,\lambda),

so that for any
\alpha,\lambda>0
,

\lim_{M\uparrow\infty}C_{M}(\alpha,\lambda)=0,

which proves Proposition
A.1
.
∎

Appendix C
Stationary point processes, Palm measures, and random intensities.

In this section, we consider a generic simple point process
N
in
{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e., random measures supported on atoms, living in a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
such that
N(\{x\})\in\{0,1\}.
We will usually refer to it as a quadruple
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,N)
. The point process can be characterized by its support, namely,
N(\cdot)=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta_{r_{i}}(\cdot)
– more precisely, if
(r_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a sequence of ordered (random) real numbers with the convention that

\cdots<r_{-2}<r_{-1}<r_{0}\leq 0<r_{1}<r_{2}<\cdots,

(C.1)

then for every
\omega\in\Omega
and Borel set
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}
,

N(\omega,C)=\#\{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}:r_{i}(\omega)\in C\}
denotes the number of indices
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

r_{i}=r_{i}(\omega)\in C
. On
\Omega
, the shifts
(\theta_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
act via
N(\theta_{t}\omega,C):=N(\omega,C+t)
for a Borel set
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. We say that the point process is
stationary
if
P\circ\theta_{t}=P
for each
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We define a measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
by
the expected number of points

\lambda(C):=E^{P}[N(C)]
on Borel sets
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. If the point process is stationary, then
\lambda
is a multiple of the Lebesgue measure so that there is a constant
m>0
such that
\lambda(C)=m|C|
for each Borel set
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. We call
m
the
intensity
of
N
.

Definition C.1

(Palm measure)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,N)
be a stationary point process with positive intensity
m>0
. Let
C
be any Borel set of positive and finite Lebesgue measure
|C|
. Then we define the (normalized) Palm measure
P_{0}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
as

P_{0}(A):=\frac{1}{m|C|}E\Big[\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\theta_{r_{n}})\mathbbm{1}_{C}(r_{n})\Big],\hskip 20.00003ptA\in\mathcal{F}.

(C.2)

We take note of the following consequences of the above definition: First, since the point process
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,N)
is stationary, the above definition is independent of the set
C
. Moreover, from the definition we can see that
P_{0}
is concentrated on
\Omega_{0}:=\{\omega:r_{0}(\omega)=0\}
– indeed, for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\omega\in\Omega

{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{r_{0}=0\}}(\theta_{r_{n}}\omega)=1

, which is true by definition. Thus, under
P_{0}
,

is concentrated on the set of the point processes with an atom at the origin. The following lemma justifies our interest in the Palm measure since it allows us to see the point process from the “point of view of the particle”:

Lemma C.2
.

[
1

We will be interested in a particular class of stationary and ergodic point process on
{\mathbb{R}}
, the so-called
Poisson point process with random intensity
:

Definition C.8
.

Let
\mu
be a random measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e., given a probability space
(\widehat{\Omega},\widehat{\mathcal{F}},\widehat{P})
,
\mu:\widehat{\Omega}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}})
is a random variable taking values on the space of locally finite measures on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
A point process N on
{\mathbb{R}}
is called a Poisson process with random intensity
\mu
(or a Poisson process directed by a random measure
\mu
), if, conditionally on the random measure
\mu
,
N
is a Poisson point process with intensity measure
\mu
, that is,

P\big(N(C|\mu(\widehat{\omega},\cdot))=k\big)=\frac{\mu(\widehat{\omega},C)^{k}{\rm e}^{-\mu(\widehat{\omega},C)}}{k!},\hskip 20.00003ptk\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},C\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{R}}).

The Laplace functional of a Poisson process directed by random intensity
\mu
defined on a probability space
(\widehat{\Omega},\widehat{\mathcal{F}},\widehat{P})
is given by

L_{N}(u):=\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{P}}\bigg[\exp\Big(-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-{\rm e}^{u(x)})\mu(\cdot,{\rm d}x)\Big)\bigg]=\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\widehat{P}({\rm d}\widehat{\omega})\exp\Big(-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-{\rm e}^{u(x)})\mu(\widehat{\omega},{\rm d}x)\Big)

(C.11)

and it also uniquely characterizes its distribution. Stationarity and ergodicity of this point process can be determined by its directing measure.

Lemma C.9
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,N)
be a Poisson process directed by the random measure
\mu
on the probability space
(\widehat{\Omega},\widehat{\mathcal{F}},\widehat{P})
.

(i)

[
9
, Proposition 6.1.I]

N
is stationary if and only if its Laplace functional is stationary, i.e.,
L_{N}(\theta_{t}u)=L_{N}(u)
for each measurable
u:{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto[0,\infty)
.

(ii)

[
10
, Proposition 12.3.VII]
If
N
is stationary, then it is also ergodic if and only if the distribution
\widehat{P}[\mu\in\cdot]
of
\mu
under
\widehat{P}
is ergodic.

Integral Ricci curvature and the mass gap of Dirichlet Laplacians on domains Thanks: G.W. is partially supported by NSF DMS 1811558, 2104704
Xavier Ramos Olivé, Christian Rose, Lili Wang, Guofang Wei

Abstract
. We obtain a fundamental gap estimate for classes of bounded domains with quantitative control on the boundary in a complete manifold with integral bounds on the negative part of the Ricci curvature. This extends the result of [ 16 ] to L p L^{p} -Ricci curvature assumptions, p > n / 2 p>n/2 . To achieve our result, it is shown that the domains under consideration are John domains, what enables us to obtain an estimate on the first nonzero Neumann eigenvalue, which is of independent interest. Keywords. eigenvalue estimate, spectral gap, mass gap, integral Ricci curvature



1.
Introduction

Consider a complete Riemannian manifold
M
of dimension
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
, and
\Omega\subset M
open such that
\overline{\Omega}
is a smooth compact manifold of dimension
n
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. The Dirichlet Laplacian
-\Delta\geq 0
has discrete spectrum consisting of an unbounded increasing sequence of positive reals
\lambda_{i}(\Omega)
,
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which can be arranged as

0<\lambda_{1}(\Omega)<\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{3}(\Omega)\leq\ldots\to\infty

counting multiplicities. For fixed
\Omega\subset M
as above, the difference

\Gamma(\Omega):=\lambda_{2}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(\Omega)>0

is called the
mass gap
(or
fundamental gap
) of the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
. In quantum mechanics, the mass gap represents the energy needed to jump from the ground state to the next lowest energy state. The subject has a long history and has been a very active area of research recently, see e.g. the survey article
[
8
]
.

In the celebrated work
[
1
]
, Andrews and Clutterbuck proved the fundamental gap conjecture that for bounded and convex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

\Gamma(\Omega)\geq 3\pi^{2}\diam(\Omega)^{-2}.

This has been generalized to convex domains in
\mathbb{S}^{n}

in
[
23
,
13
,
7
]
, showing the same gap estimate. On the other hand, it was proven in

[
3
]
that given any diameter there are convex domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
with arbitrarily small fundamental gap.

For the lower bound estimate on the gap, the convexity condition is essential. In
[
6
]
a lower bound on
\Gamma(\Omega)
was derived for domains
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
assuming the so-called interior rolling
R
-ball condition for
\partial\Omega
, cf. Definition
2.4
, in terms of bounds on the second fundamental form and volume, where
\Omega
is not necessarily convex.
This is generalized to compact manifolds in
[
16
]
,
showing that the gap is bounded from below in terms of uniform lower bounds on the Ricci curvature, the diameter, sectional curvature near the boundary, and the interior rolling
R
-ball condition. The construction in
[
3
]
shows that the rolling
R
-ball condition is necessary there and for general manifolds the
R
-rolling ball condition is more suitable than the convexity condition in some sense.

The present paper provides a gap estimate for suitable subsets of manifolds assuming only integral bounds on the negative part of the Ricci curvature.
Such integral curvature conditions gathered a lot of attention during the last decades because in contrast to lower Ricci curvature pointwise bounds they are more stable under perturbations of the metric, see, e.g.,
[
2
,
5
,
4
,
17
,
18
,
9
,
11
,
21
,
22
,
19
,
24
,
25
]
and the references therein.

We fix some notation in order to state our main result.
Denote by
B_{r}(x)
the geodesic ball centered at
x\in M
with radius
r>0
. Furthermore, for
\Omega\subset M
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, we let
\II:=\II_{\partial\Omega}
be its second fundamental form with respect to the inward pointing normal. Moreover, we denote by
\Sec
the sectional curvatures.

Define

\rho\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad x\mapsto\min\spec(\Ric_{x}),

where
\Ric
is considered as pointwise endomorphism on the tangent bundle. For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
we denote
x_{-}:=\max\{0,-x\}
.
Let
p>n/2
and define for
x\in M
and
r>0

\kappa(x,p,r):=r^{2}\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x)}\rho_{-}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

measuring the
L^{p}
-mean of the negative part of the Ricci curvature in a ball with respect to the Riemannian volume form
\mathrm{dvol}
.
It is convenient to work with the scaling invariant curvature quantity introduced in
[
17
]

\displaystyle\kappa(p,r)=\sup\limits_{x\in M}\kappa(x,p,r).

In this paper we deal with the following class of subsets.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
, without boundary,
H,K>0
, and
R>0
. A subset
\Omega\subset M
is called
(R,H,K)
-regular
if

-

\Omega
is open, bounded, and connected,

-

\overline{\Omega}\neq M
is a smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
,

-

\partial\Omega
satisfies
\II_{\partial\Omega}\leq H
and the interior rolling
R
-ball condition, cf. Definition
2.4
,

-

|\Sec|\leq K
in the inner
R
-tubular neighborhood
T(\partial\Omega,R):=\{x\in\Omega|d(x,\partial\Omega)<R\}
.

Our main result can now be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
2p>n\geq 2
,

D>0
,
H,K>0
. There exists
R_{0}=R_{0}(K,H)>0
such that the following holds: for any
0<R\leq R_{0}
, there exist explicitly computable constants

C=C(n,p,D,R,H,K)>0
and
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,p)>0
such that if
M
is a complete manifold of dimension
n
with
\kappa(p,D)\leq\varepsilon
, then for any
(R,H,K)
-regular
\Omega\subset M
with
\diam\Omega\leq D

we have

\Gamma(\Omega)\geq C(n,p,D,R,H,K).

General criteria for a quantitative lower bound on the gap for compact manifolds have been provided in
[
16
]
, cf. Theorem
5.3
.
The key ingredients are volume doubling, a Harnack type inequality for the first Dirichlet eigenfunction, weak Neumann-Poincaré inequalities on interior balls, and Neumann eigenvalue estimates for certain subsets of the manifold. We will show that all of these conditions are satisfied for
(R,H,K)
-regular domains with integral Ricci curvature bounds, which in turn yields our result. The volume doubling condition follows directly from
[
18
,
20
]
, and the Harnack inequality for the first Dirichlet eigenfunction follows from a combination of the results in
[
9
,
16
,
17
]
, and
[
18
]
.
The crucial step to obtain the main result proved in the present article is the following new Neumann eigenvalue estimate for
(R,H,K)
-regular domains.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
2p>n\geq 2
,
D>0
, and
K,H>0
.
There exist explicitly computable
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,p)>0
,
R_{0}=R_{0}(K,H)>0

such that the following holds: for any
R\in(0,R_{0}]
there is an explicitly computable
C=C(n,R,D,H,K)>0
such that for any Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
with

\kappa_{M}(p,D)\leq\epsilon
,
any
(R,H,K)
-regular domain
\Omega\subset M
with
\diam\Omega\leq D
, the first (non-zero) Neumann eigenvalue
\eta_{1}(\Omega)
of
\Omega
satisfies

\eta_{1}(\Omega)\geq C.

To achieve this we prove that
(R,H,K)
-regular sets are John domains in the sense of

[
12
]
, cf. Definition
3.1
, which is of independent interest.
Based on techniques in
[
9
]
we derive weak Neumann-Poincaré inequalities for balls.
The desired Neumann eigenvalue estimate then follows from the main results in
[
12
]
.
Prop.
1.3
also generalizes the recently appeared eigenvalue estimate in
[
19
, Corollary 1.5]
obtained by completely different techniques.

The structure of this paper is as follows: in Section
2
we fix notation and recall the Laplace and volume comparison and Sobolev constant estimates for integral Ricci curvature.
The essential John domain property of
(R,H,K)
-regular domains will be proven in Section

as well as the weak Neumann-Poincaré inequalities in balls,
yielding Prop.

]

Preliminaries on integral Ricci curvature

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, a measurable function
f\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}
, and any geodesic ball
B\subset M

we denote as usual

\|f\|_{p,B}=\left(\int_{B}|f|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\ \ \|f\|_{p,B}^{*}=\left(\frac{1}{\vol\left(B\right)}\int_{B}|f|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{\infty,B}=\esssup_{B}f.

If not explicitly stated differently we integrate w.r.t. the Riemannian volume measure
\mathrm{dvol}
.
We will need the Laplacian and volume comparison estimate for integral curvature from
[
17
,
18
]
.
Given
x\in M
, let
d(y)=d(x,y)
be the distance function and

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi(y):=\left(\Delta d-\frac{n-1}{d}\right)_{+}.\end{split}

The classical Laplacian comparison states that
\Ric_{M}\geq 0
implies
\Delta d\leq\frac{n-1}{d}
. The integral curvature version of the Laplacian and volume comparison estimates we will use here are as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
17
]

Let
p>\frac{n}{2}
,
x\in M
,
r>0
. We have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\psi\|_{2p,B_{r}(x)}^{*}\leq C(n,p)\left(\|\rho_{-}\|_{p,B_{r}(x)}^{*}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(n,p)r^{-1}\kappa(p,r)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

where

C(n,p)=\left(\frac{(n-1)(2p-1)}{2p-n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
17
,
18
]
)
.

Let
x\in M
,
p>\frac{n}{2}
. There exists
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(n,p)>0
such that if
\kappa(x,p,r_{0})\leq\epsilon_{0}
we have for all
0<r\leq s\leq r_{0}

\displaystyle\frac{\vol(B_{s}(x))}{\vol(B_{r}(x))}\leq 2\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{n}.

Remark 2.3
.

(cf.
[
9
, Remark 2.2]

[
18
, Section 2.3]
)
If
\kappa(x,p,r_{2})\leq\varepsilon_{0}
for
\varepsilon_{0}
as above, Theorem
2.2
implies for all
for all
x\in M
and
0<r_{1}\leq r_{2}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa(x,p,r_{1})\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{2-\frac{n}{p}}\kappa(x,p,r_{2})\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\kappa(x,p,r_{2}).\end{split}

Hence,
\kappa(x,p,r_{1})\leq\varepsilon_{0}
if
r_{1}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{n-2p}}r_{2}
. If
\kappa(p,r_{1})\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, then for all

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa(p,r_{2})\leq 2^{\frac{n+1}{p}}\left(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\right)^{2}\kappa(p,r_{1}).\end{split}

Hence when
\kappa(p,r)
is small for some

\kappa(p,r)
for all

Note that for compact manifolds with diameter

3.
Neumann-Poincaré inequalities for John Domains

This section is devoted to the proof of Proposition
1.3
.
We show that
(R,H,K)-
regular domains defined in Definition
1.1
are John domains defined below.

Definition 3.1

(
[
12
, Page 39]
)
.

A bounded open subset
\Omega\subset M
is called a
John domain
if there exist
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
C_{J}>0
such that for every
x\in\Omega
there exists a curve
\gamma:[0,l]\rightarrow\Omega
parametrized by arclength with
\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma(l)=x_{0}
, and

{\rm dist}(\gamma(t),\partial\Omega)\geq C_{J}t.

Clearly, any ball of a geodesic metric space is a John domain with
C_{J}=1
by taking
x_{0}
to be the center of the ball.
John domains are a very general class of subsets of metric spaces possessing strong inclusion properties of function spaces.
A key property is that they satisfy the chain condition. Hence, to obtain a Sobolev inequality for the domain it is suffices to work
on balls inside the domain, see
[
12
, Chapter 9]
for details.

Here we prove that
(R,H,K)
-regular domains are John domains.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be an
(R,H,K)-
regular domain with
{\rm diam}(\Omega)\leq D
. Then
\Omega
is a John domain with
C_{J}=C(n,D,R,H,K)>0
.

Proof.

For
\delta>0
denote

\Omega_{\delta}=\{x\in\Omega\colon{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>\delta\}

and
\widetilde{D}_{\delta}={\rm diam}(\Omega_{\delta})
the intrinsic diameter of
\Omega_{\delta}
. Since
\Omega
is an
(R,H,K)-
regular domain, the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 3.2.7, Page 60]
gives for
\delta<R/2

(3.1)

\widetilde{D}_{\delta}=\diam(\Omega_{\delta})\leq(C_{K,H,\delta})^{n-1}D,

where

C_{K,H,\delta}:=2\left(\frac{K}{H}+\frac{H}{K}\right)\cosh(2\sqrt{K}\delta).

In particular, we have

\tilde{D}_{R/4}\leq(C_{K,H,R/4})^{n-1}D
.
Moreover,
\Omega_{R/4}\not=\varnothing
since
\Omega
satisfies the rolling
R-
ball condition. Pick any fixed
x_{0}\in\Omega_{R/4}
and
x\in\Omega
.
If
x\in\Omega_{R/4}
, let
\gamma:[0,l]\rightarrow\Omega_{R/4}
be a minimizing normal
geodesic in
\Omega_{R/4}
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(l)=x_{0}
. Then
l
, the intrinsic distance between
x_{0}
and
x
in
\Omega_{R/4}
, will satisfy
l\leq\widetilde{D}_{R/4}
. For
C=\frac{R}{4\widetilde{D}_{R/4}}
we have

{\rm dist}(\gamma(t),\partial\Omega)\geq\frac{R}{4}\geq C\widetilde{D}_{R/4}\geq Ct.

If
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{R/4}
, let
p\in\partial\Omega
be such that
{\rm dist}(x,p)={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
, let
q\in\Omega
be the center of a rolling
R
-ball
such that
\overline{B_{R}(q)}\cap\partial\Omega=\{p\}
, and let
\gamma_{1}
be a distance minimizing normal geodesic from
p
to
q
. Since
\gamma_{1}
goes through
x
, choose the parametrization so that
\gamma_{1}(0)=x
and
\gamma_{1}(dist(x,q))=q
. Notice that
q\in\overline{\Omega_{R/4}}
, since
{\rm dist}(q,\partial\Omega)=R
. Let
\gamma_{2}
be a minimizing
normal
geodesic in
\Omega_{R/4}
joining
q=\gamma_{2}(0)
and
x_{0}=\gamma_{2}(l)
. Then consider the curve
\gamma:[0,l+{\rm dist}(x,q)]\rightarrow\Omega
defined by

\gamma(t)=\begin{cases}\gamma_{1}(t)&\text{if }0\leq t\leq{\rm dist}(x,q),\\
\gamma_{2}(t-{\rm dist}(x,q))&\text{if }{\rm dist}(x,q)<t\leq l+{\rm dist}(x,q).\end{cases}

For the choice
C=\frac{R}{4\widetilde{D}_{R/4}+R}
we need to consider the following two cases.

Case 1:

t<{\rm dist}(x,q)

In this case, we have

{\rm dist}(\gamma(t),\partial\Omega)={\rm dist}(\gamma_{1}(t),\partial\Omega)={\rm dist}(\gamma(t),p)={\rm dist}(x,p)+t\geq t\geq Ct.

Case 2:

{\rm dist}(x,q)\leq t\leq{\rm dist}(x,q)+l

In this situation, we have
t\leq R/4+\widetilde{D}_{R/4}
. Since
\gamma_{2}
is a curve in
\Omega_{R/4}
, we have

{\rm dist}(\gamma(t),\partial\Omega)={\rm dist}(\gamma_{2}(t-{\rm dist}(x,q)),\partial\Omega)\geq\frac{R}{4}=C(\widetilde{D}_{R/4}+R/4)\geq Ct.

Hence, we have shown that
\Omega
is a
C
-John domain for
C=\frac{R}{4\widetilde{D}_{R/4}+R}
. We conclude the proof by noting
\widetilde{D}_{R/4}\leq C(n,D,R,H,K)
.

We observe that the set of radii on the right hand side is not empty. Indeed, for

, thus using Theorem
2.2

\Vol(B_{r/16}(y)\cap\Omega_{2})\geq\Vol(\Omega_{2}^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}\Vol(B_{r/32}(x))\geq\frac{1}{2^{2n+3}}\Vol(B_{r/8}(x))\geq\frac{1}{2^{2n+3}}\Vol(B_{r/16}(y)).

Notice also that
r_{y}>0
, because the ratio
\frac{\Vol(B_{s}(y)\cap\Omega_{2})}{\Vol(B_{s}(y)}
is continuous in
s
and becomes
0
for
s
small enough. Thus for any
y\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
we have that
0<r_{y}\leq\frac{r}{16}
.

The set
\{B_{2r_{y}}(y)\}_{y\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}
gives an open cover for
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
. Then, by the Vitali covering lemma, there exists a subfamily of disjoint balls
\displaystyle\{B_{2r_{i}}(y_{i})\}_{i\in I}
,
r_{i}=r_{y_{i}}
, such that

\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{10r_{i}}(y_{i}).

Notice that, since
r_{y}\leq\frac{r}{16}
and
y\in B_{r/32}(x)
, we have that
B_{10r_{i}}(y_{i})\subseteq B_{r}(x)
. In particular,
B_{10r_{i}}(y_{i})\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
and
10r_{i}\leq r
, so we can use Theorem
2.2
on these balls to get

\Vol(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})\leq\sum_{i\in I}\Vol(B_{10r_{i}}(y_{i}))\leq 2\cdot 10^{n}\sum_{i\in I}\Vol(B_{r_{i}}(y_{i})).

On the other hand, by definition
\Vol(B_{r_{i}}(y_{i})\cap\Omega_{2})=\frac{1}{2^{2n+3}}\vol(B_{r_{i}}(y_{i}))
, so
\Vol(B_{r_{i}}(y_{i})\cap\Omega_{1})=\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{2n+3}}\right)\vol(B_{r_{i}}(y_{i}))>\Vol(B_{r_{i}}(y_{i})\cap\Omega_{2})
. Thus, choosing
\epsilon(n,p)
small enough, we can use (
3.5
). Since the balls
\{B_{2r_{i}}(y_{i})\}_{i\in I}
are disjoint, we have

\mathcal{A}(H)\geq\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{A}(H\cap B_{2r_{i}}(y_{i}))\geq 2^{-(3n+5)}\sum_{i\in I}r_{i}^{-1}\Vol(B_{r_{i}}(y_{i})).

Combining the two estimates together, we get

\frac{\Vol(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})}{\mathcal{A}(H)}\leq 2^{3n+5}(2\cdot 10^{n})\frac{\sum_{i\in I}\Vol(B_{r_{i}}(y_{i}))}{\sum_{i\in I}r_{i}^{-1}\Vol(B_{r_{i}}(y_{i}))}\leq 2^{3n+6}10^{n}\sup_{i\in I}r_{i}\leq 2^{4n+2}5^{n}r.

∎

By the equivalence of the isoperimetric constant and the Sobolev constant (the same proof as
[
14
, Theorem 9.6]
applies to the weak version), we have

(3.6)

\|f-f_{B_{\frac{r}{32}}}\|_{1,B_{\frac{r}{32}}}\leq 2\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\|f-a\|_{1,B_{\frac{r}{32}}}\leq 2I_{R}(B_{r/32}(x),B_{r}(x))\|\nabla f\|_{1,B_{r}}.

Proposition
3.4
and volume doubling, i.e., Theorem
2.2
, give (
3.2
) when
\kappa(p,r)\leq\epsilon(n,p)
.

Finally, we are ready to prove Proposition
1.3
.

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

By (
2.2
), we have
\kappa(p,r)\leq 2^{1/p}\kappa(p,D)
. Choosing
\epsilon(n,p)
smaller we get (
3.2
) if
\kappa(p,D)\leq\epsilon(n,p)
.
Since we have volume doubling (
2.4
), the weak Neumann-Poincaré inequality (
3.2
), and Lemma
3.2
, we can apply
[
12
, Theorem 9.7]
(for
p=1,s=n
) to obtain

\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\|f-a\|^{*}_{\tfrac{n}{n-1},\Omega}\leq C(n,C_{0},C_{J})\diam(\Omega)\,\|\nabla f\|^{*}_{1,\Omega}\quad f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega),

where
C_{0},C_{J}
are constants from Lemmas
2.5
and
3.2
.
Hölder’s inequality yields

\|f-a\|^{*}_{1,\Omega}\leq\|f-a\|^{*}_{\tfrac{n}{n-1},\Omega}.

Hence

(3.7)

\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\|f-a\|^{*}_{1,\Omega}\leq C(n,C_{0},C_{J})\diam(\Omega)\,\|\nabla f\|^{*}_{1,\Omega}\quad f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega).

Applying Cheeger’s inequality (see e.g.
[
14
, Page 92]
) gives the Neumann eigenvalue estimate (
1.3
).
∎

Now when
\Omega
is a ball
B_{r}(x)
, then
C_{J}=1,R=r,D=2r
, so
C_{0}=2\cdot 4^{n}
. Therefore applying the estimate (

) to the ball, we have

\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\|f-a\|^{*}_{1,B_{r}}\leq C(n)r\|\nabla f\|^{*}_{1,B_{r}},\quad f\in W^{1,2}(B_{r}).

Again applying Cheeger’s inequality immediately gives the Neumann-Poincaré inequality for the ball, with the following explicit dependence which we will also need.

(3.8)

\|f-f_{B_{r}}\|_{2,B_{r}}\leq C(n)r\|\nabla f\|_{2,B_{r}},\quad f\in W^{1,2}(B_{r}).

In fact we only need the weak

version of the Neumann-Poincaré inequality. While it is automatic to get the

version from the

version for the usual Neumann-Poincaré inequality, this is not the case for the weak version. The estimate (

) has been pointed out in

, Remark 1.7]
.

4.
Local Harnack inequality for the first Dirichlet eigenfunction

In this section, we will prove a local Harnack inequality for the first Dirichlet eigenfunction via a gradient estimate. For pointwise curvature bounds, one can prove it using the maximum principle, see e.g.
[
14
, Theorem 6.1]
.
For integral curvature conditions, the gradient estimate can be established via the Nash-Moser iteration. The essential tools are the local Sobolev inequality in Theorem
2.6

and the Laplacian comparison estimate in Theorem
2.1
.

We have the following gradient estimate for the first eigenfunction of ball depending on integral Ricci curvature bounds.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
p>n/2
,
x\in M
,
r>0
, and
\lambda\geq 0
. If
u
is a positive solution of

\Delta u=-\lambda u

on
B_{r}(x)
, then

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)}|\nabla\ln u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)\left(r^{-2}\frac{\vol\left(B_{r}(x)\right)}{\vol\left(B_{\frac{7}{8}r}(x)\right)}+\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left[r^{-2}C_{s}^{2}\left(B_{r}(x)\right)\left(1+r^{-2}C_{s}^{2}\kappa(p,r)\right)+\left(r^{-2}C_{s}^{2}\left(B_{r}(x)\right)\kappa(p,r)\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-n}}\right]^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}.

The proof is a modification of the argument in
[
9
, Theorem 5.2]
.

Proof.

Let
h=\ln u
and
v=|\nabla h|^{2}+\lambda
, such that
\Delta h=-v
. By scaling we assume that
r=1
. We infer from the Bochner formula

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Delta v=\Delta|\nabla h|^{2}=2|\Hess h|^{2}+2\langle\nabla h,\nabla\Delta h\rangle+2\Ric(\nabla h,\nabla h)\geq\frac{2}{n}v^{2}-2\langle\nabla h,\nabla v\rangle-2\rho_{-}v.

We abbreviate
B_{r}:=B_{r}(x)
. All integrals below are on
B_{1}
which we omit.
Given
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{1})
and
l>1
, integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\int|\nabla(\eta v^{l})|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int\left(\eta v^{l}\right)\left[v^{l}\Delta\eta+2\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla v^{l}\rangle+\eta\Delta v^{l}\right]

\displaystyle=\int v^{2l}\left(-\eta\Delta\eta\right)-2\int v^{l}\langle\nabla\eta,\eta\nabla v^{l}\rangle-\int\eta^{2}v^{l}\left(lv^{l-1}\Delta v+l(l-1)v^{l-2}|\nabla v|^{2}\right)

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\int v^{2l}\left(-\eta\Delta\eta+2|\nabla\eta|^{2}\right)-2\int v^{l}\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla\left(\eta v^{l}\right)\rangle-l\int\eta^{2}v^{2l-1}\Delta v-\frac{l-1}{l}\int\eta^{2}|\nabla v^{l}|^{2}.

We have

\displaystyle-\frac{l-1}{l}\int\eta^{2}|\nabla v^{l}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{l-1}{l}\int|\nabla(\eta v^{l})-v^{l}\nabla\eta|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{l-1}{l}\int|\nabla(\eta v^{l})|^{2}+\left(2-\frac{2}{l}\right)\int v^{l}\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla(\eta v^{l})\rangle-\frac{l-1}{l}\int v^{2l}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Inserting the above equality into (
4
), using
-2\langle a\nabla\eta,b\nabla\left(\eta v^{l}\right)\rangle\leq a^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+b^{2}|\nabla\left(\eta v^{l}\right)|^{2}
with
a=\frac{v^{l}}{\sqrt{l(l-1)}}
and
b=\frac{\sqrt{l-1}}{\sqrt{l}}
, and (
4.1
) gives

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int|\nabla(\eta v^{l})|^{2}&=-\int v^{2l}\eta\Delta\eta+\frac{l+1}{l}\int v^{2l}|\nabla\eta|^{2}-\frac{2}{l}\int v^{l}\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla\left(\eta v^{l}\right)\rangle\\
&\ \ \ -l\int\eta^{2}v^{2l-1}\Delta v-\frac{l-1}{l}\int|\nabla(\eta v^{l})|^{2}\\
&\leq-\int v^{2l}\eta\Delta\eta+\frac{l+1}{l}\int v^{2l}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\frac{l-1}{l}\int|\nabla(\eta v^{l})|^{2}+\frac{1}{l(l-1)}\int v^{2l}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\\
&\ \ \ \ -\frac{2l}{n}\int\eta^{2}v^{2l+1}+2l\int\eta^{2}v^{2l-1}\langle\nabla h,\nabla v\rangle+2l\int\eta^{2}v^{2l}\rho_{-}-\frac{l-1}{l}\int|\nabla(\eta v^{l})|^{2}\\
&=-\int v^{2l}\eta\Delta\eta+\frac{l}{l-1}\int v^{2l}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{2l}{n}\int\eta^{2}v^{2l+1}+2l\int\eta^{2}v^{2l-1}\langle\nabla h,\nabla v\rangle+2l\int\eta^{2}v^{2l}\rho_{-}.\end{split}

We infer from
\Delta h=-v
and
|\nabla h|\leq v^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle 2l\int\eta^{2}v^{2l-1}\langle\nabla h,\nabla v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\langle\eta^{2}\nabla h,\nabla v^{2l}\rangle=-\int(\Delta h)\eta^{2}v^{2l}-2\int\langle\nabla h,\eta\nabla\eta\rangle v^{2l}

\displaystyle\leq\int\eta^{2}v^{2l+1}+\int\eta^{2}v^{2l+1}+\int v^{2l}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Combining (
4.3
) with above inequality leads to

\displaystyle\int|\nabla(\eta v^{l})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\int v^{2l}\eta\Delta\eta+\frac{2l-1}{l-1}\int v^{2l}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+(2-\frac{2l}{n})\int\eta^{2}v^{2l+1}+2l\int\eta^{2}v^{2l}\rho_{-}.

Let
d(y)=d(x,y)
be the distance function from
x
. We choose
\eta(y)=\varphi(d(y))
, where
\varphi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
satisfying

\varphi(t)\equiv 0
for
t\geq 1
and
\varphi(t)\equiv 1
for
t\in[0,t_{0}]
with
t_{0}\in(0,1)
, and
\varphi^{\prime}\leq 0
.
Thus,

\displaystyle|\nabla\eta|=|\varphi^{\prime}|\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta\eta=\varphi^{\prime\prime}+\varphi^{\prime}\Delta d\geq\varphi^{\prime\prime}+\varphi^{\prime}\left(\psi+\frac{n-1}{d}\right)\geq-|\varphi^{\prime\prime}|-|\varphi^{\prime}|\psi-\frac{n-1}{d}|\varphi^{\prime}|,

where
\psi=\left(\Delta d-\frac{n-1}{d}\right)_{+}
.
Hence, for
l\geq n
, we have

\displaystyle\int|\nabla(\eta v^{l})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)l\int\left[\left(|\varphi^{\prime\prime}|+|\varphi^{\prime}|\psi+\frac{|\varphi^{\prime}|}{d}\right)\eta v^{2l}+|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}v^{2l}+\eta^{2}v^{2l}\rho_{-}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(n)l\int\left[\left(|\varphi^{\prime\prime}|+\frac{|\varphi^{\prime}|}{d}\right)\eta v^{2l}+|\varphi^{\prime}|\psi\eta v^{2l}+|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}v^{2l}+\eta^{2}v^{2l}\rho_{-}\right].

Choose
\beta=\frac{n}{n-2}
. Apply the Sobolev inequality (
2.5
) to get

\displaystyle\left(\fint\left(\eta^{2}v^{2l}\right)^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\ \ C_{s}^{2}\left(B_{1}(x)\right)\fint\Big|\nabla\left(\eta v^{l}\right)\Big|^{2}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq\ \ C_{s}^{2}\left(B_{1}(x)\right)C(n)l\fint\Big[\left(|\varphi^{\prime\prime}|+\frac{|\varphi^{\prime}|}{d}\right)\eta v^{2l}+|\varphi^{\prime}|\psi\eta v^{2l}+|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}v^{2l}+\eta^{2}v^{2l}\rho_{-}\Big]

To control the
\psi
-term, applying Hölder’s inequality and the Laplacian comparison estimate Theorem
2.1
, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle C(n)lC^{2}_{s}\fint|\varphi^{\prime}|\psi\eta v^{2l}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)lC^{2}_{s}\|\psi\|^{*}_{2p}\|\eta\varphi^{\prime}v^{2l}\|^{*}_{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}\leq C(n)lC^{2}_{s}C(n,p)\left(\kappa(p,1)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta\varphi^{\prime}v^{2l}\|^{*}_{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}.

Set
\alpha=\frac{p(n-2)}{n(2p-1)}=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{p}{2p-1}<1
. Since
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{1})
we get

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\eta\varphi^{\prime}v^{2l}\|^{*}_{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}&=\left[\fint\left(\eta^{2}v^{2l}\right)^{\alpha\beta}\left(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}v^{2l}\right)^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\right]^{\frac{2p-1}{2p}}\\
&\leq\left[\left(\fint\left(\eta^{2}v^{2l}\right)^{\beta}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\fint\left(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}v^{2l}\right)^{\frac{np}{np+2p-n}}\right)^{\frac{np+2p-n}{n(2p-1)}}\right]^{\frac{2p-1}{2p}}\\
&\leq\left[\left(\fint\left(\eta^{2}v^{2l}\right)^{\beta}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\fint|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}v^{2l}\right)^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\right]^{\frac{2p-1}{2p}}\\
&\leq\epsilon\left(\fint\left(\eta^{2}v^{2l}\right)^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}+\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\fint|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}v^{2l},\end{split}

where we used Hölder inequality and
\frac{np}{np+2p-n}<1
due to
p>\frac{n}{2}
in the second inequality.
By setting
\varepsilon=\left(3C_{s}^{2}C(n)lC(n,p)\left(\kappa(p,1)\right)^{1/2}\right)^{-1}
and inserting (
4.6
) into (
4.5
) we obtain

(4.7)

C(n)lC^{2}_{s}\fint\psi\eta|\varphi^{\prime}|v^{2l}\leq\frac{1}{3}\left(\fint\left(\eta^{2}v^{2l}\right)^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}+C(n)l^{2}C_{s}^{4}C^{2}(n,p)\kappa(p,1)\fint|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}v^{2l}.

Setting
a=a(n,p)=\frac{2p-n}{2(p-1)}>0
and using Young’s inequality

\displaystyle xy\leq\varepsilon x^{b}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{b^{*}}{b}}y^{b^{*}},\forall x,y\geq 0,b>1,\frac{1}{b^{*}}+\frac{1}{b}=1,

where

b=\frac{p}{(1-a)(p-1)\beta},\ \ b^{*}=\frac{p}{(p-1)a}

we estimate the
\rho
-term by

\displaystyle\fint\eta^{2}v^{2l}\rho_{-}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho_{-}\|_{p}^{*}\left(\fint(\eta^{2}v^{2l})^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq\kappa(p,1)\left(\fint\eta^{2}v^{2l}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}a}\left(\fint(\eta^{2}v^{2l})^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}(1-a)}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa(p,1)\left[\varepsilon\left(\fint(\eta^{2}v^{2l})^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{(1-a)\beta}{a}}\left(\fint\eta^{2}v^{2l}\right)\right].

By choosing
\varepsilon=\left(3C(n)lC_{s}^{2}\kappa(p,1)\right)^{-1}
, we obtain

(4.8)

C_{s}^{2}C(n)l\fint\eta^{2}v^{2l}\rho_{-}\\
\leq\frac{1}{3}\left(\fint(\eta^{2}v^{2l})^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}+C(n,p)\left(lC_{s}^{2}\kappa(p,1)\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-n}}\left(\fint\eta^{2}v^{2l}\right).

Inserting (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) into (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\left(\fint\left(\eta^{2}v^{2l}\right)^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq 3C(n)lC_{s}^{2}\Big[\fint\left(|\varphi^{\prime\prime}|+\frac{|\varphi^{\prime}|}{d}\right)\eta v^{2l}+\left(1+C^{2}(n,p)lC_{s}^{2}\kappa(p,1)\right)\fint|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}v^{2l}\Big]

(4.9)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +C(n,p)\left(lC_{s}^{2}\kappa(p,1)\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-n}}\left(\fint\eta^{2}v^{2l}\right).

Define
l=\frac{\beta^{i}}{2}\geq n
,
r_{i}=\tau-\sum^{i}_{j=0}2^{-j-1}\delta
with
\tau\in[\frac{1}{2}+\delta,1]
and
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
B_{i}:=B_{r_{i}}(x)
. Choose cut-off functions
\eta_{i}=\varphi_{i}(d)\in C^{\infty}_{0}\left(B_{i}\right)
such that

\eta_{i}\equiv 1\ \ \ \text{on}\ B_{i+1};\ \ |\varphi^{\prime}_{i}|\leq 2^{i+1},\ \ |\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{i}|\leq 2^{2i+2}.

Substituting
\eta_{i}
into (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\|v\|^{*}_{\beta^{i+1},B_{i+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big[C(n)\beta^{i}C_{s}^{2}\left[2+\left(1+C^{2}(n,p)\beta^{i}C_{s}^{2}\kappa(p,1)\right)\right]4^{i+1}+C(n,p)\left(\beta^{i}C_{s}^{2}\kappa(p,1)\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-n}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\beta^{i}}}\|v\|^{*}_{\beta^{i},B_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[C(n,p)2^{2i}\left(\beta^{q}\right)^{i}\left(C_{s}^{2}\left(1+C_{s}^{2}\kappa(p,1)\right)+\left(C_{s}^{2}\kappa(p,1)\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-n}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{\beta^{i}}}\|v\|^{*}_{\beta^{i},B_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(4\cdot\beta^{q}\right)^{\frac{i}{\beta^{i}}}\left(C(n,p)A\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{i}}}\|v\|^{*}_{\beta^{i},B_{i}},

where

q=\max\left\{2,\frac{2p}{2p-n}\right\},\ \ \ A=C_{s}^{2}\left(1+C_{s}^{2}\kappa(p,1)\right)+\left(C_{s}^{2}\kappa(p,1)\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-n}}.

Iterating from
i_{0}
such that
n+1>\beta^{i_{0}}\geq n
to
\infty
, since
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta^{i}}=\frac{n}{2}
and
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{i}{\beta^{i}}
are finite, we obtain for
k<n

\displaystyle\|v\|^{*}_{\infty,B_{\tau-\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)A^{\frac{n}{2}}\|v\|^{*}_{n,B_{\tau}}\leq C(n,p)A^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\|v\|^{*}_{k,B_{\tau}}\right)^{\frac{k}{n}}\left(\|v\|^{*}_{\infty,B_{\tau}}\right)^{1-\frac{k}{n}}.

Let

\delta_{i}=2^{-i-3},\tau_{0}=\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{0},\quad\tau_{i+1}=\tau_{i}+\delta_{i},\quad i=0,1,2,\ldots.

Iterating from
0
to
i
, we have

\displaystyle\|v\|^{*}_{\infty,B_{\tau_{0}-\delta_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)A^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\|v\|^{*}_{k,B_{\tau_{0}}}\right)^{\frac{k}{n}}\left(\|v\|^{*}_{\infty,B_{\tau_{0}}}\right)^{1-\frac{k}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod^{i}_{j=0}\left[C(n,p)A^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\|v\|^{*}_{k,B_{\tau_{j}}}\right)^{\frac{k}{n}}\right]^{\left({1-\frac{k}{n}}\right)^{j}}\left(\|v\|^{*}_{\infty,B_{\tau_{i}}}\right)^{\left({1-\frac{k}{n}}\right)^{i+1}}.

Let
i\to\infty
, then
\left({1-\frac{k}{n}}\right)^{i+1}\to 0
and
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left({1-\frac{k}{n}}\right)^{j}=\frac{n}{k}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\|v\|^{*}_{\infty,B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\left(C(n,p)A^{\frac{n}{2}}\right)^{\frac{n}{k}}\|v\|^{*}_{k,B_{\tau_{\infty}}}\leq C^{\frac{n}{k}}(n,p)A^{\frac{n^{2}}{2k}}\|v\|^{*}_{k,B_{\frac{7}{8}}}.

Thus, we get

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup\limits_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}|\nabla h|^{2}\leq\sup\limits_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}v\leq C(n,p)A^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}\fint_{B_{\frac{7}{8}}}v\end{split}

by setting
k=1
and using
v=|\nabla h|^{2}+\lambda
.
Choosing
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1})
with
\eta\equiv 1
in

and

, we have

\displaystyle\fint\eta^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}=\fint\eta^{2}\left(-\Delta h-\lambda\right)\leq 2\fint\eta\langle\nabla h,\nabla\eta\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}\fint\eta^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}+2\fint|\nabla\eta|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\fint\eta^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}+128.

Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\fint\eta^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}\leq 256,\end{split}

and hence

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{\frac{7}{8}}}(v-\lambda)=\fint_{B_{\frac{7}{8}}}|\nabla h|^{2}\leq\frac{\vol\left(B_{1}\right)}{\vol\left(B_{\frac{7}{8}}\right)}\fint_{B_{1}}\eta^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}\leq 256\frac{\vol\left(B_{1}\right)}{\vol\left(B_{\frac{7}{8}}\right)}.

Inserting above inequality into (
4.10
) gives

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}|\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)\left(\frac{\vol\left(B_{1}\right)}{\vol\left(B_{\frac{7}{8}}\right)}+\lambda\right)\left[C_{s}^{2}\left(B_{1}\right)\left(1+C_{s}^{2}\left(B_{1}\right)\kappa(p,1)\right)+\left(C_{s}^{2}\left(B_{1}\right)\kappa(p,1)\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-n}}\right]^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}.

The desired result can be obtained by scaling.
∎

With volume doubling the following upper bound for the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the ball follows easily from a simple test-function argument.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold.
Given
p>\frac{n}{2},D>0
, there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,p)>0
and
C=C(n)>0
such that if
\kappa(p,D)<\epsilon
, then for any
x\in M,\ 0<r\leq D

\lambda_{1}(B_{r}(x))\leq C\,r^{-2}.

This is well known to the experts. For completeness we present a proof.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:[0,r]\rightarrow[0,1]
be the cut off function with
\varphi|_{[0,\tfrac{r}{4}]}=1,\varphi|_{[\tfrac{3}{4}r,r]}=0
, and
|\varphi^{\prime}|\leq\tfrac{2}{r}
. Let
d(y)=d(x,y)
be the distance function from
x
. Using the test function
f(y)=\varphi(d(y))
we have

\lambda_{1}(B_{r}(x))\leq\frac{\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla f|^{2}}{\int_{B_{r}(x)}f^{2}}\leq 4r^{-2}\frac{\vol(B_{r})}{\vol(B_{\tfrac{r}{4}})}\leq 2\cdot 4^{n+1}r^{-2}.

Here in the last step we use the volume doubling estimate Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Now using the local Sobolev estimate Theorem
2.6
, Theorem
4.1
gives the following Harnack estimate for the first eigenfunction of
\Omega
on a ball.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
p>\frac{n}{2}
and
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded with
\partial\Omega\neq\varnothing
. Assume that
u_{1}
is the positive first Dirichlet eigenfunction of
\Omega
with
\diam\Omega=D
.
For
B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega
satisfying
B_{r}(x)\cap\partial\Omega=\varnothing
, there exist
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,p)>0
and
C=C(n,p)>0
such that if
\kappa(p,D)\leq\varepsilon
, we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup\limits_{B_{r/2}(x)}u_{1}\leq C(n,p)\inf\limits_{B_{r/2}(x)}u_{1}.\end{split}

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1}>0
be the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
\Omega
. Then

\Delta u_{1}=-\lambda_{1}u_{1}.

By domain monotonicity of eigenvalues,
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{1}(B_{r}(x))
.

Combining Lemma
4.2
, volume doubling (cf. Theorem
2.2
), Theorem
4.1
, and Theorem
2.6
, we obtain

(4.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup\limits_{B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)}|\nabla\ln u_{1}|^{2}\leq C_{1}(n,p)r^{-2}.\end{split}

For any
y,z\in B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)
, let
\gamma(s):[0,l]\rightarrow\Omega
be a distance minimizing geodesic joint
y
and
z
parametrized by arc length. By (
4.12
), we have

\displaystyle\ln\frac{u_{1}(z)}{u_{1}(y)}

\displaystyle=\int^{l}_{0}\frac{d}{ds}\ln u_{1}\left(\gamma(s)\right)\mathrm{d}s=\int^{l}_{0}\langle\nabla\ln u_{1},\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rangle ds\leq\sqrt{C_{1}(n,p)}r^{-1}l\leq\sqrt{C_{1}(n,p)}.

where we used

. Hence,

(4.13)

u_{1}(z)\leq\exp\left(\sqrt{C_{1}(n,p)}\right)u_{1}(y).

According to the arbitrariness of
y
and
z
, we have

\sup\limits_{B_{r/2}(x)}u_{1}\leq C(n,p)\inf\limits_{B_{r/2}(x)}u_{1}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.
Global Harnack inequality and the Fundamental Gap Estimate

In this section we derive a global Harnack inequality for the first eigenfunction, therefore getting an estimate on the fundamental gap. The proofs are the same as in
[
16
]
, so we omit the proofs.

Let
d(x)=d(x,\partial\Omega)
and

With the assumption that

near the boundary, where

\epsilon(n,p)>0,\ \delta(n,H,K,R)>0

\displaystyle u_{1}(x)\geq C_{1}(n,p,H,K,R,D)\ \

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3\delta}C_{1}(n,p,H,K,R,D)\,d(x)\leq u_{1}(x)\leq C_{2}(n,H,K,R)\,d(x)\ \

]

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

,

,

-regular domain

The constant

Theorem 5.3
.

[

Let

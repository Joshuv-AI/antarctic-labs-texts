Symbolic-Numeric Computation of Integrals in Successive Galerkin Approximation of Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman Equation
Tomoyuki Iori

Abstract
This paper proposes an efficient symbolic-numeric method to compute the integrals in the successive Galerkin approximation (SGA) of the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation. A solution of the HJB equation is first approximated with a linear combination of the Hermite polynomials. The coefficients of the combination are then computed by iteratively solving a linear equation, which consists of the integrals of the Hermite polynomials multiplied by nonlinear functions. The recursive structure of the Hermite polynomials is inherited by the integrals, and their recurrence relations can be computed by using the symbolic computation of differential operators. By using the recurrence relations, all the integrals can be computed from a part of them that are numerically evaluated. A numerical example is provided to show the efficiency of the proposed method compared to a standard numerical integration method.



I
Introduction

For general nonlinear systems, it is well-known that the optimal feedback controller with infinite horizon is provided by a solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation
[
1
]
.
In the case of linear systems with a quadratic performance index, the HJB equation reduces to a matrix equation called the Riccati equation, which can be solved efficiently.
However, for nonlinear cases, the HJB equation is formulated as a nonlinear partial differential equation (PDE), which is extremely difficult to solve analytically.
Hence, there have been many studies on numerical approximation techniques of a solution of the HJB equation.

One possibility is to approximate a solution of the HJB equation by the Taylor-series approximation
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
By substituting the truncated Taylor-series expansion of a solution to the HJB equation, a finite number of algebraic equations for the coefficients of the truncated expansion can be obtained.
Moreover, in
[
4
]
, a recursive closed form procedure for computing the coefficients is proposed under several conditions.

The stable manifold method
[
5
]
is one of the most promising techniques.
In this method, a sequence of trajectories that converges to a trajectory on the stable manifold is iteratively computed.
After sufficient number of iterations, the trajectory can be viewed as an approximation of the Hamiltonian flow on the stable manifold, and thus, it approximately gives the optimal feedback controller.

Another approach is to solve a linear PDE called the generalized HJB (GHJB) equation iteratively, which is called a successive approximation approach (SAA)
[
6
]
.
As the GHJB equation is still difficult to solve, the GHJB equation and its solution is further approximated by using multiple basis functions, which is called the successive Galerkin approximation (SGA).
It is proved in
[
6
]
that the solutions of the SGA converges to a solution of the HJB as the number of basis functions and iterations go to infinity.
Several modified versions of the SAA have been studied
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.

One main issue of the SGA is the computation of integrals associated with the inner product of a function space.
Even if all the integrals can be computed offline, their computational cost may still be too high to complete within a reasonable amount of computing power and time.
Therefore, it is still important to seek an efficient method for computing the inner products or the integrals of nonlinear functions.

In recent years, the symbolic computation of differential operators has been intensively studied
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
, and its applications can be found in statistics
[
14
,
15
]
, moment problems
[
16
]
.
In the theory of D-modules
[
17
]
, the solutions of PDEs are characterized by an infinite set of differential operators called an ideal.
It can be proven that any ideal is represented by a finite number of differential operators, and thus, it can be computed symbolically by using the computer algebra systems (CASs) such as
Singular

[
18
]
,
Macaulay2

[
19
]
, and
Risa/Asir

[
20
]
.
In particular, a specific type of ideals called holonomic ideals and their solutions called holonomic functions are the main objects manipulated by the symbolic computation.
More specifically, for the integral of a holonomic function, a holonomic ideal that the integral satisfies can be computed exactly from a holonomic ideal that the integrand satisfies.
This provides a great advantage in the design of algorithms because the integral of a general nonlinear function cannot be computed exactly.
In addition to differential equations, recurrence equations can also be treated by the symbolic computation by converting them to differential equations via the Mellin transform
[
11
,
13
]
.

In this paper, we propose a method to compute recurrence relations satisfied by the integrals required for the SGA.
The expansion of a solution of the HJB equation with Hermite polynomials, which is holonomic as an infinite sequence of functions is considered.
In addition, the plant dynamics is assumed to be described by holonomic functions.
These problem settings allow us to compute a set of recurrence relations that are satisfied by the integrals for the SGA.
Once the recurrence equations are computed, any number of the integrals can be readily computed just by substituting several initial values to the recurrence equations.
Moreover, in contrast to the earlier methods
[
7
,
8
]
, the proposed method can consider the nonlinearities of the plant dynamics exactly in the computation of the integrals.

II
Problem Setting

Consider the optimal control problem of a nonlinear system:

\dot{x}=f(x)+g(x)u

(1)

with a performance index defined as

J(x,u)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}q(x(t))+\|u(t)\|^{2}_{R}dt,

(2)

where
x\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{n}
and
u\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{m}
are the state and input of the system, respectively.
The nonlinear functions
f\colon{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{n}\to{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{n}
,
g\colon{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{n}\to{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{n\times m}
, and
q\colon{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{n}\to{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}
are assumed to consists of holonomic functions, which will be defined in Section
III
.
It is also assumed that
q(x)\geq 0\ (x\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{n})
and
R\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{m\times m}
be positive definite.

For a given feedback law
u(x)
, the performance index for the closed loop trajectory
\phi(t)
is defined as

V(x_{0},u(\cdot))\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}q(\phi(t))+\|u(\phi(t))\|^{2}_{R}dt,

(3)

where
\phi(0)=x_{0}
.
The optimal feedback law
u^{*}(x)
is given as

u^{*}=-\frac{1}{2}R^{-1}g^{\top}{\frac{\partial{V^{*}}}{\partial{x}}}^{\top},

(4)

where
V^{*}\colon{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{n}\to{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}
is a solution of the HJB equation

\mathrm{HJB}(V^{*})\coloneqq{\frac{\partial{V^{*}}}{\partial{x}}}f+q-\frac{1}{4}{\frac{\partial{V^{*}}}{\partial{x}}}gR^{-1}g^{\top}{\frac{\partial{V^{*}}}{\partial{x}}}^{\top}=0,

(5)

such that
V^{*}(0)=0
.

In the SAA, a solution of the HJB equation is iteratively approximated by solving the GHJB equation defined as

\mathrm{GHJB}(V,u)\coloneqq{\frac{\partial{V}}{\partial{x}}}(f+gu)+q+\|u\|^{2}_{R}=0.

(6)

As the GHJB equation is still difficult to solve, the iteration
V^{(l)}
is further approximated by using a finite number of basis functions
\phi_{1}(x),\dots,\phi_{N}(x)
as

V^{(l)}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}^{(l)}\phi_{i}(x)=\Phi^{\top}(x)v^{(l)}\quad\left(v_{i}^{(l)}\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}\right),

(7)

where
\Phi(x)\coloneqq[\phi_{1}(x)\ \cdots\ \phi_{N}(x)]^{\top}
and
v^{(l)}\coloneqq[v_{1}^{(l)}\ \cdots\ \allowbreak v_{N}^{(l)}]^{\top}
.
This also yields a representation of
u^{(l+1)}
as

\displaystyle u^{(l+1)}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}R^{-1}g^{\top}(x){\frac{\partial{V^{(l)}}}{\partial{x}}}^{\top}(x)

(8)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}R^{-1}g^{\top}(x){\frac{\partial{\Phi}}{\partial{x}}}^{\top}(x)v^{(l)}.

(9)

The functional equation
\mathrm{GHJB}(V^{(l)},u^{(l)})=0
is then approximated with the following finite number of algebraic equations for
v_{i}^{(l)}
:

\left\langle\mathrm{GHJB}(V^{(l)},u^{(l)}),\phi_{k}\right\rangle=0\quad(p=1,\dots,N),

(10)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is an inner product of functions defined as

\langle f(x),g(x)\rangle\coloneqq\int_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{n}}w(x)f(x)g(x)dx

(11)

with a weighting function
w(x)>0\ (x\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}^{n})
.
By substituting (
7
) and (
9
) into (
10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{GHJB}(V^{(l)},u^{(l)}),\phi_{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}^{(l)}\left\langle{\frac{\partial{\phi_{i}}}{\partial{x}}}f,\phi_{k}\right\rangle

(12)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}^{(l)}\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{(l-1)}\left\langle{\frac{\partial{\phi_{i}}}{\partial{x}}}GR^{-1}G^{\top}{\frac{\partial{\phi_{j}}}{\partial{x}}}^{\top},\phi_{k}\right\rangle

(13)

\displaystyle+\left\langle q,\phi_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}^{(l-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{(l-1)}\left\langle{\frac{\partial{\phi_{i}}}{\partial{x}}}GR^{-1}G^{\top}{\frac{\partial{\phi_{j}}}{\partial{x}}}^{\top},\phi_{k}\right\rangle.

(14)

Now, let us define

\begin{gathered}a(i,j,k)\coloneqq\left\langle{\frac{\partial{\phi_{i}}}{\partial{x}}}GR^{-1}G^{\top}{\frac{\partial{\phi_{j}}}{\partial{x}}}^{\top},\phi_{k}\right\rangle,\\
b(i,k)\coloneqq\left\langle{\frac{\partial{\phi_{i}}}{\partial{x}}}f,\phi_{k}\right\rangle,\quad c(k)\coloneqq\left\langle q(x),\phi_{k}(x)\right\rangle.\end{gathered}

(15)

In addition, let
A_{k}
be the
N\times N
matrix having

as its
(i,j)
-component and
b_{k}
be the
N
-dimensional vector having
b(i,k)
as its
i
-th component.
The approximated GHJB equation (
10
) is then rewritten as the following linear equations for

:

\mathrm{LE}(v^{(l)},v^{(l-1)})\coloneqq\\
{v^{(l)}}^{\top}\left(b_{k}-\frac{1}{2}A_{k}v^{(l-1)}\right)+c(k)+\frac{1}{4}\left\|v^{(l-1)}\right\|_{A_{k}}^{2}=0\\
(k=1,\dots,N).

(16)

In what follows, for a given
N\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{N}}}
,
A_{N}
,
B_{N}
, and
C_{N}
denote the sets of the integrals required to define (
16
), that is,

\displaystyle A_{N}\coloneqq\{a(i,j,k)\mid i,j,k\in\{1,\dots,N\}\},

(17)

\displaystyle B_{N}\coloneqq\{b(i,k)\mid i,k\in\{1,\dots,N\}\},

(18)

\displaystyle C_{N}\coloneqq\{c(k)\mid k\in\{1,\dots,N\}\}.

(19)

Once the integrals

, and

are computed, the SGA procedure can be performed by solving the linear equation (

4:

Although

,

, and

Such recursive relations can be computed by using the symbolic computation of differential operators, which will be briefly introduced in the next section.

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}}[x]\langle\partial_{x}\rangle

, and

Consider a set of PDEs for an unknown function

In this paper, the adjective “left” is omitted because all ideals in this paper are left ideals.

Definition 1
(Holonomic ideal of

An ideal

is holonomic if the quotient
{\mathcal{D}}_{n}/I
, which can be viewed as a left

For the definition of the dimension of left

,
10

12

The notion of holonomic ideals plays an important role in the symbolic computation of differential operators because of its finiteness
[
10
]
.

The solutions of holonomic ideals are called holonomic functions and include most nonlinear functions appears in systems and control theory
[
22
,
23
]
.

Definition 2
(Holonomic function)

An analytic function

is said to be holonomic if

is annihilated by a holonomic ideal

, that is,
{\mathcal{P}}\bullet f=0
for all
{\mathcal{P}}\in I
.

The class of holonomic functions is closed under multiplication and integration; the product of two holonomic functions and the integral of a holonomic function are also holonomic
[
11
]
.

Lemma 1

For two holonomic functions
f(x)
and
g(x)
, their product
(f\cdot g)(x)
is holonomic.

Lemma 2

Suppose a holonomic function
f(x)
is rapidly decreasing with respect to

, that is,
\lim_{x_{1}\to\infty}x_{1}^{i}\partial_{x_{1}}^{j}f(x)=0
for any nonnegative integers

.
Then, the integral
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)dx_{1}
is holonomic as a function of
[x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]^{\top}
.

Recurrence relations of infinite sequences can be converted into PDEs and manipulated by the symbolic computation of differential operators through the Mellin transform.

Definition 3
(Mellin transform between
\partial
and
{\mathsf{S}}
)

A mapping
\mu\colon{\mathcal{D}}_{n+p}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{n}\langle i,{\mathsf{S}}_{i},{\mathsf{S}}_{i}^{-1}\rangle
defined as

\mu(s_{j})={\mathsf{S}}_{i_{j}},\quad\mu(\partial_{s_{j}})=-i_{j}{\mathsf{S}}_{i_{j}}^{-1}

(21)

is called the Mellin transform,
and a mapping
\hat{\mu}\colon{\mathcal{D}}_{n}\langle i,{\mathsf{S}}_{i}\rangle\allowbreak\to{\mathcal{D}}_{n+p}
defined as

\hat{\mu}(i_{j})=-\partial_{s_{j}}s_{j}=-s_{j}\partial{s_{j}}-1,\quad\hat{\mu}({\mathsf{S}}_{i_{j}})=s_{j}

The Mellin transform is related to the multivariate z-transformation

between a multivariate function and an infinite sequence of functions:

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}[f](s,x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{i_{p}=0}^{\infty}f(i;x)s_{1}^{-i_{1}-1}\cdots s_{p}^{-i_{p}-1},

(23)

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}^{-1}[g](i;x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{C}g(s,x)s_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots s_{p}^{i_{p}}ds,

(24)

where

is the product of unit circles centered at the origin of the complex plane.
It is readily seen that

\hat{\mu}(i)\bullet\mathscr{Z}[f]=\mathscr{Z}[if],\quad\hat{\mu}({\mathsf{S}}_{i})\bullet\mathscr{Z}[f]=\mathscr{Z}[{\mathsf{S}}_{i}f].

(25)

Recurrence Equations of Integrals

Hereafter, suppose

With the weighting function

, the z-transforms of integrals (

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}[a](s,t,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{R}\Big\{e^{-x^{2}}g^{2}(x)\mathscr{Z}[\partial_{x}H](s,x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mathscr{Z}[\partial_{x}H](t,x)\mathscr{Z}[H](u,x)\Big\}dx,

(27)

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}[b](s,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}}\mathscr{Z}[\partial_{x}H](s,x)f(x)\mathscr{Z}[H](u,x)dx,

(28)

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}[c](v)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}}q(x)\mathscr{Z}[H](u,x)dx.

To this end, we next show that the z-transforms of the Hermite polynomials and their derivatives are holonomic.
The Hermite polynomials satisfy the following difference-differential equations:

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}^{2}-2x\partial_{x}+2i)\bullet H(i;x)=0,

(30)

\displaystyle({\mathsf{S}}_{i}^{2}-2x{\mathsf{S}}_{i}+2(i+1))\bullet H(i;x)=0.

By the inverse Mellin transform (
22

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}^{2}-2x\partial_{x}-2s\partial_{s}-2)\bullet\mathscr{Z}[H](s,x)=0,

(32)

\displaystyle(s^{2}-2xs-2s\partial_{s})\bullet\mathscr{Z}[H](s,x)=0.

Lemma 3

The z-transform of the Hermite polynomials

\displaystyle=((i+1){\mathsf{S}}_{i}^{2}-2x(i+2){\mathsf{S}}_{i}+2(i+1)(i+2))

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(\partial_{x}^{2}-2x\partial_{x}+2(i-1))=\partial_{x}^{2}-2x\partial_{x}-2s\partial_{s}-4,

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, a symbolic-numeric computation method for the integrals in the successive approximation algorithm has been proposed.
The successive approximation algorithm approximates a solution of Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation by iteratively solving a linear equation, which is defined with multiple integrals of nonlinear functions.
By using the symbolic computation of differential operators and the Mellin transform of difference-differential operators, a set of recurrence relations satisfied by the integrals can be computed.
From a finite number of the integrals computed by numerical integration, the other integrals can be computed up to any number by using the recurrence relations.

Future work directions of this study includes the extension of the proposed method to the case of multi-dimensional systems.
Moreover, the approximation with another orthogonal polynomial such as the Chebyshev polynomials could be included.

Non-surjective Milnor patching diagrams
Alexandr Grebennikov

Abstract
. Milnor patching diagram is essentially the commutative square of rings, over which gluing of finitely generated projective modules is possible in the strongest sense. Necessary and sufficient conditions for a square to be Milnor patching diagram were studied by Milnor, Beauville-Laszlo and Landsburg. We relate this question to determinant-induced factorization in matrix rings to construct a series of non-surjective Milnor patching diagrams, settling the question of Landsburg, and make a step towards the classification of such examples. Also we consider a possible generalization of the notion of Milnor patching diagram to arbitrary subcategories of modules and obtain a classification result for this setting.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Rings with determinant-induced factorization

The work
[
6
]
of Estes and Matijevic we are following in this paragraph is devoted to the study of
Towber rings
(see
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
for more information on the subject and
[
11
]
for a survey on related questions). Theorem 1 of
[
6
]
provides a few equivalent definitions of them, the version (i) being the most convenient for our purposes.

Definition 2.1
.

A reduced Noetherian ring
R
is called
Towber
if for any
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\in\Mat_{n}(R)
and a non-zero-divisor
b\in R
, such that
b\mid\det(\sum_{i=1}^{k}X_{i}C_{i})
for any
X_{i}\in\Mat_{n}(R)
, there are
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k},B\in\Mat_{n}(R)
satisfying

\det(B)=b,\;A_{i}B=C_{i}.

Clearly, any Towber ring has determinant-induced factorization in the sense of definition
1.5
by taking
k=1
. Moreover, proposition 5 of
[
6
]
shows that the converse also holds when
R
is a direct product of Krull domains.

Corollary 2 of
[
6
, section 3]
says that a factorial Noetherian ring is Towber if and only if its global dimension is at most 2 and all finitely generated projective modules over it are free. So, polynomial ring
k[x,y]
for a field
k
and regular local rings of dimension 2 are the first non-trivial examples of Towber (and thus having determinant-induced factorization) rings.

2.2.
Properties of principal localization squares

Here we record two basic statements about principal localization squares. Their proofs are quite straightforward (and partially follow from Proposition 4.2 of

]

Proposition 2.2
.

For a principal localization square

the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{F}_{loc}
is cartesian;

(2)

the induced map
\bar{g}:R/(x)\to R^{\prime}/(g(x))

For a principal localization square

the following are equivalent:

(1)

is cartesian and surjective;

(2)

the induced map

(3)

2.4.
Strong Milnor patching diagrams

To check that a square is a strong Milnor patching diagram we always use the following (trivial) lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose a square
\mathcal{F}
is cartesian and for every
A\in\SL_{n}(S)
there are
A_{1}\in\GL_{n}(R_{1}),A_{2}\in\GL_{n}(R_{2})
such that
A=h_{1}(A_{1})h_{2}(A_{2})
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is a strong Milnor patching diagram.

The results of Milnor’s work
[
2
, Chapter 2]
(see also
[
8
]
or introduction of
[
1
]
for the notation more similar to the one we use here) imply that

•

strong Milnor patching diagrams are actually Milnor patching diagrams;

•

if a square
\mathcal{F}
is cartesian and one of
h_{1}:R_{1}\to S
and
h_{2}:R_{2}\to S
is surjective, then
\mathcal{F}
is a strong Milnor patching diagram (the assumption is stronger than just
\mathcal{F}
being surjective, but in comparison to Beauville-Laszlo theorem this result does not require it to be principal localization square).

In
[
8
]
Landsburg conjectures that strong Milnor patching is equivalent to the usual one. The next lemma asserts that in certain cases this conjecture is straightforward to verify.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a Milnor patching diagram, and every finitely generated projective module over
R
is free. Then
\mathcal{F}
is a strong Milnor patching diagram.

Proof.

For
A\in\SL_{n}(S)
consider
N=\GG_{\mathcal{F}}(R_{1}^{n},R_{2}^{n},A)
. By Milnor patching it is a finitely generated projective
R
-module, hence free. So,
N\simeq R^{m}
, and

(R_{1}^{m},R_{2}^{m},\id_{S^{m}})=\FF_{\mathcal{F}}(R^{m})\simeq\FF_{\mathcal{F}}(N)\simeq(R_{1}^{n},R_{2}^{n},A)

in the category
\PD(\mathcal{F})
of patching data, what means

and

A=h_{1}(A_{1})h_{2}(A_{2}),\text{ for some }A_{1}\in\GL_{n}(R_{1}),\;A_{2}\in\GL_{n}(R_{2}).

So, we are done by lemma
2.6
.
∎

Lemma 4.1

Before passing to the proof we record a version of Smith normal form for a matrix over Euclidean domain.

Proposition 4.2

Let

Proof of lemma
4.1
.

Let

and
p^{\prime}:R^{\prime}\to R^{\prime}/(g(x))
be the quotient maps. Since
\bar{g}
identifies

with some subring inside the fraction field

, taking

equal to the product of all denominators in

\bar{g}(T)p^{\prime}(X)=\bar{g}(Y)\text{ for some }Y\in\Mat_{n}(R/(x)).

By Smith normal form (

At the same time

thus

On the Stability of Cylindrical Singularities of the Mean Curvature Flow
Jingxuan Zhang

Abstract
We study the rescaled mean curvature flow (MCF) of hypersurfaces that are global graphs over a fixed cylinder of arbitrary dimensions. We construct an explicit stable manifold for the rescaled MCF of finite codimensions in a suitable configuration space. For any initial hypersurface from this stable manifold, we construct a unique global solution to the rescaled MCF, and derive precise asymptotics for these solutions that are valid for all time. Using these asymptotics, we prove asymptotic stability of cylindrical singularities of arbitrary dimensions under generic initial perturbations. As a by-product, for any flow of hypersurfaces evolving according to the MCF that enters this stable manifold at any time and first develops a singularity at a subsequent time, we give a simple proof of the uniqueness of tangent flow, first established by Colding and Minicozzi. Moreover, in this case we show the unique singularity profile is determined by the hypersurface profile when the flow enters the stable manifold. For all results in this paper, there is no symmetry or solitonic assumption.



1
Introduction

Consider the mean curvature flow (MCF) for a family of hypersurfaces given
by immersions
X(\cdot,t):\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, satisfying

\partial_{t}X=-H(X)\nu(X).

(1)

In this paper, we are interested in the dynamical behaviour
of a solution
X
to (
1
), which first develops a singularity
at
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\,t=T>0
.

Equation (
1
) is invariant under rotation, translation, and parabolic rescaling.
Motivated by these symmetries, we consider the following time-dependent rescaling for a solution to (
1
) as follows:

X(x,\omega,t)=\lambda(t)g(t)Y(y(x,t),\omega,\tau)+(0,z(t)),

(2)

where the immersion
Y
is defined through this relation, and

\displaystyle g(t)\in SO(n+1),\quad g(0,x^{\prime})=(0,x^{\prime})\text{ for every $x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}$},

(3)

\displaystyle z(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1},

(4)

\displaystyle\lambda(t):=\left(2\int_{t}^{T}a(t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\right)^{1/2},\quad a(t)>0,

(5)

\displaystyle y(x,t):=\lambda(t)^{-1}x,

(6)

\displaystyle\tau(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\lambda(t^{\prime})^{-2}\,dt^{\prime}.

(7)

Notice that we do not fix the parameter
\sigma:=(g,z,a)
, but rather regard
this as a path to be determined in the manifold

\Sigma:=SO(n+1)\times\mathbb{R}^{k+1}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

(8)

The condition (
5
) shows that
\lambda(t)
is uniquely
determined by the path
a(t)>0
. Indeed, this
\lambda(t)
is the unique solution to the
Cauchy problem

\dot{\lambda}(t)\lambda(t)=-a(t),\quad\lambda(T)=0.

Here and in the remaining of this section,
the dot denotes differentiation w.r.t. the fast time
t
-variable. By definition, the terminal condition on
\lambda
ensures the rescaling
(
2
) gives rise to a tangent flow
Y=Y(y,\omega,\tau)

in the microscopic variable
y
and slow time variable
\tau
.

Direct computation shows
X
of the form (
2
) solves (
1
) if and only if the pair

(\sigma,Y)
solves

\partial_{\tau}Y=-H(Y)\nu(Y)-a\left\langle y,\,\nabla_{y}\right\rangle Y+aY-g^{-1}\partial_{\tau}gY-\lambda^{-1}g^{-1}\partial_{\tau}z.

(9)

To get (
9
), one uses the relations
\lambda\dot{\lambda}=-a
,
\lambda^{2}\dot{y}=ay
, and
\dot{\tau}=\lambda^{-2}
, which follow from (
5
)-(
7
)
respectively.
We call (
9
)
the rescaled mean curvature flow.

The rescaled MCF (
9
) has the following family of stationary solutions:

\displaystyle Y_{a_{0}}\equiv\left(y,\sqrt{\frac{k}{a_{0}}}\omega\right),

(10)

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\equiv(g_{0},z_{0},a_{0})\in\Sigma.

(11)

Geometrically, the three components in
\sigma_{0}
consist of
a
rotation
g_{0}
of the cylindrical axis, a transversal translation
z_{0}
,
and a dilation by a factor of
\sqrt{k/a_{0}}
.
The pair
(\sigma,Y)
corresponds to a cylinder
with unit radius along the
y
axis, transformed
by the symmetry
\sigma_{0}
as in (
2
).

\displaystyle\sigma(0)=(\delta_{ij},0,a_{0})\in\Sigma,

(17)

where the vector fields

).

3.

(Dissipative estimates)
Moreover, the path
\sigma(\tau)
dissipates to
\sigma(0)
,

\left\lvert\sigma(\tau)-\sigma(0)\right\rvert\lesssim\delta\left\langle\tau\right\rangle^{-1},\quad\tau\geq 0.

(18)

Here and below, we write
\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle:=(1+\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert^{2})^{1/2}
.

Moreover, the function
\xi
in the decomposition (

)
is non-negative for all

, and dissipates to zero,
with the decay estimate

\left\lVert\xi(\cdot,\tau)\right\rVert_{X^{s}}\leq\delta\left\langle\tau\right\rangle^{-2},\quad\tau\geq 0.

(19)

The result
above are obtained by studying
a quasilinear PDE (
27
)
in
\xi
, coupled to a system of ODEs, (
43
)-(
45
)
in the parameter
\sigma
. Up to a rigid motion
and a dilation, initial configuration (
17
)
can be replaced with any
\sigma_{0}\in\Sigma
.

In
[
12
]
*Thm. 4.31, Colding and Minicozzi have shown
that the cylindrical singularity of the MCF is
F
-unstable.
In terms of the PDE (
27
),
this means that the static solution

\xi=0
is
linearly unstable.

In view of this,
Theorem
1
above states
that for a generic (i.e. finite codimensional)
class of initial perturbations,

\xi=0
is
asymptotically stable
under the evolution
(
27
).
This gives a justification of the
generality of cylindrical singularity
based on the PDE ground.

Indeed, at the intuitive level,
the fact that cylinders are
F
-unstable
has to do with the symmetry of the (rescaled) MCF.
The broken symmetries of the cylinder,
e.g. transversal translation to the axis,
generates some unstable modes
to the linearized operator
at the cylinder,

L(a):=-\Delta_{y}+a\left\langle y,\,\nabla_{y}(\cdot)\right\rangle-\frac{a}{k}\Delta_{\omega}-2a.

Here
a>0
corresponds to the cylindrical radius.
In view of this, the space
\mathcal{S}
in Theorem
1
,
defined in Definition
4
below,
removes all the zero-unstable modes of
L(a)

from the configuration space.

Then, as customary in the analysis of solitons,
one would like to write down a number of
ODEs,
which governs the motion
of a frame of symmetries
that eliminates the zero-unstable modes of
L(a)
for all subsequent time.
In our situation, this gives rise to the ODEs (
16
)-(
17
) above.
These ODEs are called the
modulation equations
,
and their use in the study of
solitary wave dynamics dates back to
[
32
,
33
]

The twist here is that not all of the zero-unstable modes
of
L(a)
can be eliminated by the modulation method.
First, there is the translation along the cylindrical axis,
which incurs unstable modes protected by the symmetry of the cylinder.
Secondly, as pointed out in
[
15
]
*Sect. 3.2,
there are certain zero modes that are not due to
any symmetries.

To eliminate
these,
following
[
31
]
,
we introduce
the second order correction
\Phi
, constructed explicitly in Definition
5
below.
In
[
31
]
, Schlag has
studied a
L^{2}
-supercritical
nonlinear Schrödinger equation,
where some unstable mode
arises which is not due to any symmetry.
Here we implement Schlag’s method,
which is essentially a renormalization technique,
to study the rescaled MCF (
27
).

Let
\Phi
be the map constructed in Theorem
5
.
In terms of invariant manifold theory,
the set

M=M_{\delta,a_{0}}=\left\{X:(x,\omega)\mapsto(x,(\sqrt{k/a_{0}}+\eta+\Phi(\eta))\omega)\mid\eta\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{S}\right\}

(20)

can be viewed as a stable manifold for (
1
),
or, more appropriately, for the rescaled MCF (
9
). We discuss the geometric property of
M
in Section
6
.
In this paper, our focus is on the “shadow”
of a flow generated by
an initial configuration in
M
under the rescaled
MCF, namely (
16
)-(
17
),
and dissipative estimates of the form (
18
)-(
19
).
Indeed,
M
is a stable manifold precisely in the sense that
the infinite dimensional flow of hypersurfaces is well-approximated by
the finite system of ODEs (
16
)-(
17
)
in the space
\Sigma
.

In physical terms, Theorem
1

is an adiabatic theory for MCF near a cylindrical singularity.
In the physics literature, especially in classical field theory,
the method of
adiabatic approximation
involves
the decomposition of a flow
into a slowly evolving main part,
together with a small fluctuation.
In our situation, for a rescaled flow starting
in
M
,
the adiabatic approximation are the ODEs (
16
),
which govern the motion of a main part (a transformed cylinder
by symmetry)
moving at the speed of
O(\delta)
.
The fluctuation is the
remainder
\xi
.

In view of the original MCF,
the adiabatic dynamics (
16
)-(
17
) of
\sigma
implies the following:

Corollary 1

(uniqueness of tangent flow, c.f.
[
15
]
*Thm. 0.2)
.

Fix
0<\delta\ll 1
and
a_{0}\geq 1/2+2\delta
.
Suppose
X(\cdot,t)
is
a maximal solution to (
1
) that first develops
a singularity at
(0,T)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Suppose up to a dilation by a positive factor,
at some
t_{0}<T

the hypersurface
X(\cdot,t_{0})

lies in the stable manifold
M_{\delta,a_{0}}
given in (
20
).
Then
the tangent flow of
X
is unique.

Moreover,
the limit cylinder is parametrized by

(y,\sqrt{k/a_{0}}\omega)
with
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{k}
.

The uniqueness of tangent flow is established in

[
13
,
15
,
11
]
under various assumptions.
In these papers, the authors show that
assuming

a flow develops a cylindrical singularity of a certain
profile, then the latter is independent of the tangent flow.

The point of Corollary
1
is that for a flow starting from
M
,
we have
explicit information of the limit of the tangent flow given the initial profile.
In fact, the uniqueness of tangent flow
is a by-product of the equations (
16
)-(
17
).
By assumption, Corollary
1
applies only to
surfaces that at some
t_{0}<T

that are
X^{s}(a_{0})
-close to a cylinder.
This is as expected for an adiabatic theory,
whose validity
relies on the fact that the evolving
configurations are near equilibira.

Theorem
1
and the corollary are proved in Section
3.3
.

1.2
Historical Remarks

In a series of papers
[
22
,
20
,
19
,
24
,
23
,
36
]
,
Zhou Gang and several co-authors have developed an adiabatic theory for
cylindrical singularity of certain dimensions.
Here, as remarked earlier, by
adiabatic theory
we mean
the method of slow motion approximation.
Indeed, the key technique in
those papers is the decomposition of a solution evolving
according to
(
1
) into a slowly evolving main part of the form

V_{a,B}=\sqrt{\frac{k+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle By,\,y\right\rangle}{a}},

(21)

together with a remainder. Here
a(t)>0
, and

B(t)
is a path of symmetric real matrix of size
n-k
to be determined.
(These play the role of
\sigma
in (
12
).)
Then one studies (
1
) in a suitable configuration space, and tries to derive
dissipative estimate for the remainder.
This line of thought is an essential motivation to the present paper.

Ultimately, the
adiabatic theory of
[
22
,
20
,
19
,
24
,
23
,
36
]

has to do with the zero-unstable modes of
the linearized operator at a cylinder of radius
a>0,

as mentioned earlier.
Indeed, consider the expression (
21
).
This
V
solves the static equation

-a\left\langle y,\,\nabla_{y}\right\rangle v+av-kv^{-1}=0,

which is obtained by ignoring all time derivatives
and all second order spatial derivatives in
the graphical form of the rescaled MCF, (
27
).
(In multi-scale analysis and classical field theory,
solution of such truncated equation is often called
the adiabatic solution.
)
For
\left\lvert B\right\rvert\ll 1
,
expanding
V
in
y
gives
V=\sqrt{k/a}(1+\tfrac{1}{4k}\left\langle By,\,y\right\rangle+O(\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{4}))
,
where the first term corresponds to the unstable modes with eigenvalue
-2a

of the operator
L(a)
, and the
second term, a homogeneous quadratic polynomial in
y
,
corresponds to the non-symmetry zero modes mentioned earlier.
This expansion of
V
is valid for
\left\lvert\left\langle By,\,y\right\rangle\right\rvert\ll 1
,
which holds on a bounded region for sufficiently small
\left\lvert B\right\rvert
.
On such a region, it is possible to write down a system of modulation equations
for
B
so as to eliminate the effect of the non-symmetry zero modes.
The analysis of the dynamics of the matrix
B

constitutes the most difficult part in
[
22
,
20
,
19
,
24
,
23
,
36
]
.
Then there is another modulation equation for
a
to eliminate
the scaling instability, together with some
center manifold analysis to control the rest of zero-unstable modes.
The authors of the above mentioned papers have developed an extension-improvement
method (what they call the “bootstrap machines”) to make this rigorous.
This method culminates in a proof of the mean convexity conjecture with
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
singularity in
[
24
]
.

In the past decade, in a series of papers
[
12
,
15
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
27
,

,
29
,
25
,
8
,
9
,

,

]
, a group of prominent geometers
have developed
some remarkable regularity results for cylindrical singularities,
using various methods of geometric analysis and geometric measure theory.
Here we single out
[
15
]
, in which Colding and Minicozzi
developed the Łojasiewicz inequalities for the
rescaled MCF. Some estimates from that paper are
used for the developments in Sects.
2
-
6
,
though the Łojasiewicz inequalities themselves are not used.

The parabolic rescaling (
5
)-(
7
) sends a fixed
spacetime neighbourhood on which the MCF (
1
) is posed
to a growing region in the blow-up
variables
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
and
\tau\geq 0
, on which (
9
) is posed.
This results in some unfavourable issues due to non-compactness
in the analysis of the latter equation.
To overcome these issues, many results in
[
12
,
15
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
25
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
10
]

are set in the Gaussian weighted Sobolev space (e.g.
[
12
,
15
]
).
This, together with the extensive use of geometric
measure theory, makes it difficult to apply
the regularity theory from these papers
to derive precise asymptotics of the form (
16
)-(
19
)
for the rescaled MCF,
which are explicit and valid uniformly in time.

In
[
22
,
20
,
19
,
24
,
23
,
36
]
,
in order to derive precise and uniform asymptotics,
Zhou Gang and various co-authors study the rescaled MCF
in the blow-up variables in
a weighted
C^{k}
-space, with a norm of the form

\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert=\left\lVert\left\langle y\right\rangle^{-l}\partial_{y}^{\alpha}\partial_{\omega}^{\beta}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

for a function
\phi
defined on a fixed cylinder,
multi-indices
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert+\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leq k
,
\left\langle y\right\rangle:=(1+\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2})^{1/2}
, and
some power
l\geq 1
.
Using this norm, these authors are able to obtain

C^{2}
-control over a frowing region of size roughly
\tau^{1/2}
at time
\tau

in the microscopic scale.
This amounts to
C^{2}
-control over a fixed spatial neighbourhood in
the original scale.
To get such control, the authors of the aforementioned papers
have developed several novel propagator estimates
in the polynomially weighted norm given above.
This kind of propagator estimates are used in
the present paper.

In contrast,
using the Łojasiewicz inequalities from
[
15
]
,
one has
C^{2}
-control only
over a region of size
\sqrt{\log\tau}
on the rescaled hypersurface,
which amounts to a shrinking region in the original, macroscopic scale.
This limitation is due to the fact that control
over Gaussian weighted Sobolev norm
is, in general, not sufficient to conclude any
uniform control, since one loses
almost all asymptotic data.
In particular, we lack embedding theorems
of Gaussian weighted Sobolev spaces into Hölder spaces.

On the other hand,
since the adiabatic solution

in (

)
is
not in fact a stationary solution to the rescaled MCF,
the regularity theory of Colding-Minicozzi,
based on the analysis of Huisken’s

[
22
,

,
19
,

,

,

]
.
Indeed, unlike the cylinder, this

from (

This fact results in considerable technical complications in those papers.

In the present paper, we are only interested in the asymptotic
singularity profile of (

all the analysis in the subsequent sections

By the first Łojasiewicz inequality from
[
15
]
*Thm. 2.54, Thm. 5.3,
it is not hard to see that the remainder estimate (

implies
C^{2}

on
the rescaled hypersurfaces,
but this does not correspond to a fixed region on the

in this paper, but leave this to some future work.

Recently, invariant manifold theory for MCF is studied in
[
35
,
34
]
. In these papers,
the authors have constructed stable/central/unstable manifolds

These works are along the line of
[

,

,
3
,

,

]

invariant manifold theory for semilinear parabolic

In this paper we use different methods and have rather different focus,

1.3
Organization of the Paper

The purpose of the present paper is to lay out
a framework in which one has
precise asymptotics and effective dynamics near a
cylindrical singularity of arbitrary dimensions.

Uniform control (in time) of this kind is achieved in the main result, Theorem
1
,
for a generic class
of initial configurations.
The latter is the
manifold
M
given in (
20
), constructed explicitly
in Definition
5
. This is generic in the sense that it has finite
codimensions in the configuration space.

In this paper we mostly consider the rescaled equation (
9
).
To keep track of the precise dynamics of the hypersurfaces,
we restrict ourselves to graphs over
cylinder.
To use some of the regularity theory of Colding-Minicozzi,
we study the rescaled MCF in the Gaussian weighted
Sobolev space
X^{s}(a)
.
The graphical equation thus obtained and the configuration spaces are
defined in Section
2
.

In Section
2.2
, we collect some known results regarding
the linearized operator
L(a)
at a cylinder.
In Section
2.3
,
we develop the first order correction that eliminates
the destabilizing effect of the zero-unstable modes arising
from broken symmetry. This is done by imposing a certain set
of modulation equations, namely (
43
)-(
45
).

In Section
3
, we define the key map
\Phi

in Theorem
1
. This is defined in
terms of the unique fixed points of a family of maps

\Psi=\Psi(\cdot,\eta_{0})
, also introduced in that section.
In Section
3.3
, we prove the main theorem and its corollaries,
assuming the key ingredients from Sects.
4
-
6
.

Indeed, Theorem
1
relies on a fixed point scheme
for the map
\Psi
.
In Section
4
, we show that this map
\Psi
goes from a suitable
configuration space
\mathcal{A}_{\delta}
into itself.
This section contains the heart of the matter, namely an
appropriate second order correction
that eliminates the effect of the remaining zero-unstable modes
that persist the modulation.
These remaining modes
correspond to translation symmetry
Y(y,\omega)\mapsto Y(y+y^{\prime},\omega)

along the cylindrical axis, which is not broken by the cylinder,
and the non-symmetry zero modes mentioned earlier.

In Section
5
, we show that
\Psi
is a contraction in
\mathcal{A}_{\delta}
.
In Section
6
, we prove the estimates (
13
)-(
14
),
which show that the set
M
in (
20
) forms a Lipschitz graph over
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{S}
of
size
\delta
, and therefore can be treated as a manifold.

In Appendix
A
, we prove some elementary results that are
nonetheless crucial for the developments in Sects.
4

In this section we prove some abstract and elementary lemmas.
Despite their simple appearcance, they play crucial roles in

Recall that the weighted Sobolev space

is defined in Section

As discussed in that section, for two numbers

In Sect. 4, we are concerned with the

-estimate
for a family of functions

knowing some estimate of the varying norm

Lemma

Definition 6

(pivot condition)
.

Fix some
0<\delta<1/8
.
A family of functions

independent of

s.th.

\int\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq s}\left\lvert\partial^{\alpha}\phi(t)\right\rvert^{2}\rho_{b}\leq c,\quad b:=\frac{1}{2}-4\delta>0.

(132)

Equivalently, this means
\phi(t)\in X^{s}(b)
and is uniformly bounded for all
t
in the
X^{s}(b)
-norm.

Lemma 4
.

Let
a(t)
be a function satisfying
0\leq a(t)-1/2\leq 2\delta
.
Suppose

satisfies the pivot condition (
132

\left\lVert\phi(t)\right\rVert_{s,a(t)}\leq g(t)
for some positive function

.
Then there holds

\left\lVert\phi(t)\right\rVert_{s}\leq c^{1/12}g(t)^{2/3}.

(133)

Proof.

Let
u:=\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq s}\left\lvert\partial^{\alpha}\phi\right\rvert^{2}
.
Then
u\rho_{1/2}=(u^{2/3}\rho_{2a/3})(u^{1/3}\rho_{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2a}{3}}).

By Hölder’s inequality with
p=3/2
and
q=3
, we find

\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{s}^{2}=\int u\rho_{1/2}\leq\left(\int u\rho_{a}\right)^{2/3}\left(\int u\rho_{\frac{3}{2}-2a}\right)^{1/3}=\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{s,a}^{4/3}\left(\int u\rho_{\frac{3}{2}-2a}\right)^{1/3}.

(134)

The assumption

implies

, and therefore
the second factor in the r.h.s. of (

by the l.h.s. of (

and taking square root on both sides of (

).
∎

Lemma 10

Proof.

Lemma 11
.

For
s\geq 2
, define

V(\delta_{1}):=\left\{(a,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times X^{s}:\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{s}\leq\delta_{1},0<a<1\right\}

Proof.

Consider the trivial inequality

\left\lVert N(a^{(1)},v^{(1)})-N(a^{(0)},v^{(0)})\right\rVert_{s-2}\\
\lesssim\left\lVert N(a^{(1)},v^{(1)})-N(a^{(1)},v^{(0)})\right\rVert_{s-2}+\left\lVert N(a^{(1)},v^{(0)})-N(a^{(0)},v^{(0)})\right\rVert_{s-2}.

To bound the first term, consider the explicit expression (

From here we see that for fixed

by the uniform bound (

The claim now is that

\left\lVert N(a^{(1)},v^{(1)})-N(a^{(1)},v^{(0)})\right\rVert_{s-2}\lesssim\delta_{1}\left\lVert\xi^{(1)}-\xi^{(0)}\right\rVert_{s},

Towards Understanding The Semidefinite Relaxations of Truncated Least-Squares in Robust Rotation Search Thanks: This work is an extension of our conference paper [ 46 ] .
Liangzu Peng, Mahyar Fazlyab, René Vidal

Abstract
The rotation search problem aims to find a 3D rotation that best aligns a given number of point pairs. To induce robustness against outliers for rotation search, prior work considers truncated least-squares (TLS), which is a non-convex optimization problem, and its semidefinite relaxation (SDR) as a tractable alternative. Whether this SDR is theoretically tight in the presence of noise, outliers, or both has remained largely unexplored. We derive conditions that characterize the tightness of this SDR, showing that the tightness depends on the noise level, the truncation parameters of TLS, and the outlier distribution (random or clustered). In particular, we give a short proof for the tightness in the noiseless and outlier-free case, as opposed to the lengthy analysis of prior work.

\bm{z}_{k^{*}+1}=\cdots=\bm{z}_{\ell}=\bm{z}_{0}=\bm{w}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}}
, (
O2
) becomes

\begin{split}&\ \ \sum_{i=1}^{k^{*}}c_{i}^{2}\Big(\big\|\bm{w}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\bm{w}_{0}^{*}\big\|_{2}^{2}-2(\bm{w}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}})^{\top}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}\Big)+\sum_{j=k^{*}+1}^{\ell}(\bm{w}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}})^{\top}\Big(\bm{Q}_{j}-c_{j}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}\Big)\bm{w}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}}\geq 0\\
\Leftrightarrow&\ \ 1-\frac{\sum_{j=k^{*}+1}^{\ell}c_{j}^{2}}{2\sum_{i=1}^{k^{*}}c_{i}^{2}}\geq(\bm{w}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}})^{\top}\bm{w}_{0}^{*},\end{split}

which violates assumption (
17
). This finishes the proof.

The
clustered
outliers of
theorem

9
defined in the sense of (
16
) mean that the outlier pairs
(\bm{y}_{j},\bm{x}_{j})
(
j>k^{*}
) are related by the same 3D rotation
\bm{R}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}}
that correspond to
\bm{w}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}}
, that is
\bm{y}_{j}=\bm{R}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}}\bm{x}_{j}
,
\forall j>k^{*}
(
lemma

1
). Clustered outliers can be thought of as a special type of
adversarial
outliers, the latter usually used to study the robustness of algorithms in the worse case; it should be distinguished from data clustering
[
33
,
23
]
.

To understand condition (
17
) of
theorem

9
, consider a situation where all truncation parameters are equal,
c_{1}^{2}=\cdots=c_{\ell}^{2}
. Then (
17
) simplifies to
1-(\ell-k^{*})/(2k^{*})<\big|(\bm{w}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}})^{\top}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}\big|
; also note that
\big|(\bm{w}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}})^{\top}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}\big|\in[0,1)
. Thus, if
\ell-k^{*}>2k^{*}
, then (
17
) always holds, and so
\mathpzc{W}^{*}
never globally minimizes (
SDR
), which is forgivable as in this case
\bm{w}_{0}^{*}
neither globally minimizes (
TLS-Q
). However, even if the number of outliers is only half the number of inliers, i.e.,
\ell-k^{*}=k^{*}/2
,
theorem

9
implies that
\mathpzc{W}^{*}
would still fail to globally minimize (
SDR
) as long as
\big|(\bm{w}_{0}^{\textnormal{cl}})^{\top}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}\big|>3/4
, but
\bm{w}_{0}^{*}
would
in general
globally minimize (
TLS-Q
) with suitable
c_{j}^{2}
(cf.
[
61
]
). In this sense, (
SDR
) is
strictly
less robust to outliers than (
TLS-Q
).

Finally, we note that
theorem

9
might be overly pessimistic. In fact, experiments show that (
SDR
) is robust to
40\%
-
50\%
outliers
(\bm{y}_{j},\bm{x}_{j})
’s, where
\bm{y}_{j}
and
\bm{x}_{j}
are sampled uniformly at random from
\mathbb{S}^{2}
(so
\lambda_{\textnormal{min}}(\bm{Q}_{j})=0
by
lemma

1
). Two factors account for this empirically better behavior: i) Such random outliers are less adversarial than clustered ones, ii) the extra projection step that converts the global minimizer of (
SDR
) to a unit quaternion alleviates to some extent the issue of
\mathpzc{W}^{*}
not minimizing (
SDR
). In retrospect, there are two downsides in our analysis of Theorems
7
and
9
: (1) We have not taken such extra projection step into account, and (2) we only showed that
\mathpzc{W}^{*}
might not minimize (
SDR
) but have not proved how far the global minimizers of (
SDR
) can be from
\mathpzc{W}^{*}
.

3.3
The Noisy + Outlier-Free Case

The noisy case, even without outliers, is more difficult to penetrate than previous cases. A general reason for this is that the global minimizers of (
QCQP
) and (
SDR
) are now complicated functions of noise. One might wonder whether
theorem

6
can be extended to the noisy + outlier-free case using some continuity argument. In fact,
[
21
]
shows that, under certain conditions, if the noiseless version of the Schor relaxation of some QCQP is tight, then its noisy version is also tight. While this result is quite general, its conditions are abstract and, at least for non-experts, hard to verify. To our case,
[
21
]
is not directly applicable, as in our problem the truncation parameters
c_{i}^{2}
also have impacts on the tightness, and the approach of
[
21
]
does not model, and thus could not control the values of
c_{i}^{2}
. Instead, our analysis must take both
c_{i}^{2}
and noise into account.

We begin our analysis by decomposing the corrupted data matrix
\bm{Q}_{i}
of (
TLS-Q
) into
the pure data part
\bm{P}_{i}
and noise part
\bm{E}_{i}+\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}
:

Lemma 10
.

Let
\bm{R}_{0}\in\SO(3)
. If
(\bm{y}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})
is an inlier that obeys (
1
), then we have

\big\|\bm{y}_{i}-\bm{R}_{0}\bm{x}_{i}\big\|_{2}^{2}=\bm{w}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\bm{w}_{0},\ \ \ \bm{Q}_{i}=\bm{P}_{i}+\bm{E}_{i}+\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\big\|_{2}^{2}\bm{I}_{4},

(18)

where
\bm{w}_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
is the unit quaternion representation of
\bm{R}_{0}
, and
\bm{P}_{i}
and
\bm{E}_{i}
are
4\times 4
symmetric matrices that repsectively satisfy the following properties:

•

\bm{P}_{i}
is positive semidefinite with its entries depending on
\bm{y}_{i}
and
\bm{x}_{i}
, and it has two different eigenvalues
4\|\bm{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}
and
0
, each of multiplicity
2
. The ground-truth unit quaternion
\bm{w}_{0}^{*}
is an eigenvector of
\bm{P}_{i}
corresponding to eigenvalue
0
, i.e.,
\bm{P}_{i}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}=0
. In particular, we have
\bm{Q}_{i}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}=\bm{P}_{i}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}=0
in the noiseless case.

•

\bm{E}_{i}
has entries depending on
\bm{y}_{i}
,
\bm{x}_{i}
, and noise
\bm{\epsilon}_{i}
, and has two different eigenvalues
2\bm{\epsilon}_{i}^{\top}\bm{R}_{0}^{*}\bm{x}_{i}+2\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\|_{2}\|\bm{x}_{i}\|_{2}
and
2\bm{\epsilon}_{i}^{\top}\bm{R}_{0}^{*}\bm{x}_{i}-2\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\|_{2}\|\bm{x}_{i}\|_{2}
, each of multiplicity
2
. We have
\bm{w}_{0}^{\top}\bm{E}_{i}\bm{w}_{0}=2\bm{\epsilon}_{i}^{\top}(\bm{R}_{0}^{*}\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{R}_{0}\bm{x}_{i})
and in particular
(\bm{w}_{0}^{*})^{\top}\bm{E}_{i}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}=0
.

Proof of
lemma

10
.

We start with the following equality:

\big\|\bm{y}_{i}-\bm{R}_{0}\bm{x}_{i}\big\|_{2}^{2}=\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}+\bm{R}_{0}^{*}\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{R}_{0}\bm{x}_{i}\big\|_{2}^{2}=\big\|\bm{R}_{0}^{*}\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{R}\bm{x}_{i}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\big\|_{2}^{2}+2\bm{\epsilon}_{i}^{\top}(\bm{R}_{0}^{*}\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{R}_{0}\bm{x}_{i})

From the proof of
lemma

1
and in particular (
5
), we see that the first term
\|\bm{R}_{0}^{*}\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{R}_{0}\bm{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}
is equal to
\bm{w}_{0}^{\top}\bm{P}_{i}\bm{w}_{0}
, where
\bm{P}_{i}:=Q(\bm{R}_{0}^{*}\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})
is a
4\times 4
positive semidefinite matrix with eigenvalues
4\|\bm{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{2},4\|\bm{x}_{i}\|^{2},0,0
, and
\bm{w}_{0}
is the unit quaternion that represents
\bm{R}_{0}
. In particular, we have
(\bm{w}_{0}^{*})^{\top}\bm{P}_{i}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}=\|\bm{R}_{0}^{*}\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{R}_{0}^{*}\bm{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}=0
, which implies
\bm{P}_{i}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}=0
.

On the other hand, from the proof of
lemma

1
and in particular (
6
), we see the equality
-2\bm{\epsilon}_{i}^{\top}\bm{R}_{0}\bm{x}_{i}=-2\bm{w}_{0}^{\top}\ U(\bm{\epsilon}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})\ \bm{w}_{0}
where
-2U(\bm{\epsilon}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})
is symmetric and has two different eigenvalues
2\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\|_{2}\|\bm{x}_{i}\|_{2}
and
-2\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\|_{2}\|\bm{x}_{i}\|_{2}
, each of multiplicity
2
. Then
\bm{E}_{i}:=2(\bm{\epsilon}_{i}^{\top}\bm{R}_{0}^{*}\bm{x}_{i})\bm{I}_{4}-2U(\bm{\epsilon}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})
satisfies the claimed properties.

Our investigation into the noisy case is tightly related to
the
eigengap

\zeta
, defined as the ratio between the second smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\textnormal{min2}}(\cdot)
of
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\bm{Q}_{i}
and its minimum eigenvalue:

\zeta:=\frac{\lambda_{\textnormal{min2}}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\bm{Q}_{i}\big)}{\lambda_{\textnormal{min}}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\bm{Q}_{i}\big)}

(19)

In analysis of eigenvalue algorithms (cf.
[
18
]
), the eigengap is typically defined as the
difference
between two consecutive eigenvalues of some matrix. Our eigengap (
19
) that takes the
division
of two smallest eigenvalues is not standard, but it will be convenient for our purpose. Also note that,
\bm{Q}_{i}
is a perturbed version of
\bm{P}_{i}
by noise
\bm{\epsilon}_{i}
(
lemma

10
), so
\lambda_{\textnormal{min}}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\bm{Q}_{i}\big)
is in general nonzero, while it indeed approaches zero if
\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\to 0
. Clearly
\zeta\geq 1
. Moreover, we have the following immediate observation:

Remark 11
.

If (
TLS-Q
) has a unique solution and if
c_{i}^{2}>\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}
(
\forall i
), then
\zeta>1
.

We are now ready to state the following result:

Theorem 12
(Noisy and Outlier-Free Point Sets).

Consider (
TLS-Q
) with noisy inliers
\{\bm{Q}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}
and
\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
its global minimizer. Let
\hat{\mathpzc{w}}:=[\hat{\bm{w}}_{0};\dots;\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}]\in\mathbb{R}^{4(\ell+1)}
. Suppose
\zeta\geq\ell/(\ell-1)
. (
SDR
) is tight as long as

\begin{split}&c_{i}^{2}>\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}+\big\|\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}\big\|_{2}+\frac{|d_{i}|+d_{i}}{2},\ \ \ \forall i=1,\dots,\ell\\
\textnormal{with \ }&d_{i}:=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}}{\ell}-\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}+\frac{\lambda_{\textnormal{max}}\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\big(\bm{Q}_{i}-\bm{Q}_{j}\big)\Big)}{\zeta(\ell-1)}.\end{split}

(20)

Moreover, the angle
\hat{\tau}^{*}_{0}
between
\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}
and the ground-truth unit quaternion
\bm{w}^{*}_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
grows proportionally with the magnitude of noise
\bm{\epsilon}_{i}
:

\sin^{2}(\hat{\tau}^{*}_{0})\leq\frac{4\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\big\|_{2}\big\|\bm{x}_{i}\big\|_{2}}{\lambda_{\textnormal{min2}}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\bm{P}_{i}\big)},\ \ \ \ \ \sin^{2}(\hat{\tau}^{*}_{0}):=1-(\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{w}_{0}^{*})^{2}

(21)

In (
21
), each
\bm{P}_{i}
corresponds to the “pure data” part of
\bm{Q}_{i}
that satisfies
\bm{P}_{i}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}=0,\bm{P}_{i}\succeq 0
(
lemma

10
), and
\lambda_{\textnormal{min2}}\big(\cdot)
denotes the second smallest eigenvalue of a matrix.

Proof of Theorem
12
.

Since (
20
) implies
c_{i}^{2}>\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}
, (
TLS-Q
) preserves all inliers with minimum
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}
. So
\hat{\mu}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\big(\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}-c_{i}^{2}\big)
satisfies (
O3
) of
proposition

5
.

Let
\widehat{\bm{V}}:=[\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0},\ \ \hat{\bm{w}}_{0}]\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}
form an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
;
\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times 3}
satisfies
\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{\top}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}=0
and
\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{\top}\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}=\bm{I}_{3}
. Let
\hat{\mathpzc{D}}
be of the form (
12
) with each
[\hat{\mathpzc{D}}]_{0i}
satisfying

[\hat{\mathpzc{D}}]_{0i}:=\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\bm{Q}_{i}-c_{i}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}\Big),\ \ \ \widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}:=\widehat{\bm{V}}\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{\bm{T}}_{i}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\widehat{\bm{V}}^{\top}

(22)

where
\widehat{\bm{T}}_{i}
is a
3\times 3
symmetric matrix defined as

\widehat{\bm{T}}_{i}:=\frac{\zeta-\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}}{\zeta}\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}+\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{j}\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}}{\zeta(\ell-1)}-\frac{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}\Big)\bm{I}_{3}}{\ell}.

(23)

Clearly
\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}=0
, with which one verifies that (
O1
) of
proposition

5
is fulfilled.

It remains to prove (
O2
) holds true. For this we need the following lemma.
lemma

13
is a bit technical, so we put its proof into the appendix.

Lemma 13
.

Let
[\hat{\mathpzc{D}}]_{0i},\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}
and
\widehat{\bm{T}}_{i}
be defined in (
22
) and (
23
) respectively. Suppose that the assumptions of
theorem

12
, namely
\zeta\geq\ell/(\ell-1)
and (
20
), hold. Then

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}&=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\big(\bm{Q}_{i}-\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}\bm{I}_{4}\big)\\
\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}&\succeq 0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall i=1,\dots,\ell\\
\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}+c_{i}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}-\bm{Q}_{i}&\succ 0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall i=1,\dots,\ell\end{split}

With
lemma

13
and definitions of
[\hat{\mathpzc{D}}]_{0i}
(
22
), we can now write (
O2
) as (
\forall\bm{z}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
)

\begin{split}&\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\Big(\bm{z}_{0}^{\top}\big(c_{i}^{2}-\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}\big)\bm{z}_{0}+\bm{z}_{i}^{\top}\big(2\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}+c_{i}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}-\bm{Q}_{i}\big)\bm{z}_{i}-2\bm{z}_{0}^{\top}\big(\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}+c_{i}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}-\bm{Q}_{i}\big)\bm{z}_{i}\Big)\geq 0\\
\Leftrightarrow&\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\Big(\bm{z}_{0}^{\top}\big(\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}+c_{i}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}-\bm{Q}_{i}\big)\bm{z}_{0}+\bm{z}_{i}^{\top}\big(2\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}+c_{i}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}-\bm{Q}_{i}\big)\bm{z}_{i}-2\bm{z}_{0}^{\top}\big(\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}+c_{i}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}-\bm{Q}_{i}\big)\bm{z}_{i}\Big)\geq 0\\
\Leftrightarrow&\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(\bm{z}_{i}-\bm{z}_{0})^{\top}\Big(\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}+c_{i}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}-\bm{Q}_{i}\Big)(\bm{z}_{i}-\bm{z}_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\bm{z}_{i}^{\top}\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}\bm{z}_{i}\geq 0\end{split}

which holds, as
\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}+c_{i}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}-\bm{Q}_{i}
and
\widehat{\bm{S}}_{i}
are both positive semidefinite for every
i=1,\dots,\ell
(
lemma

13
). We have thus verified all conditions of
proposition

5
, thus (
SDR
) is tight.

It now remains to prove the error bound (
21
). With
lemma

10
we write
\bm{Q}_{i}=\bm{P}_{i}+\bm{E}_{i}+\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\bm{I}_{4}
, and we know that
\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}
is an eigenvector of
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(\bm{P}_{i}+\bm{E}_{i})
corresponding to its minimum eigenvalue. Hence, with the optimality of
\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}
and
lemma

10
, we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}(\bm{P}_{i}+\bm{E}_{i})\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(\bm{w}_{0}^{*})^{\top}(\bm{P}_{i}+\bm{E}_{i})\bm{w}_{0}^{*}=0\\
\Rightarrow&\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{P}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}\leq-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{E}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}=-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}2\bm{\epsilon}_{i}^{\top}(\bm{R}_{0}^{*}-\widehat{\bm{R}}_{0})\bm{x}_{i}\leq 4\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\big\|_{2}\big\|\bm{x}_{i}\big\|_{2}\end{split}

Here we recall that
\widehat{\bm{R}}_{0}
is the global minimizer of (
TLS-R
) that corresponds to
\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}
, and used
lemma

10
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\bm{P}_{i}
is positive semidefinite with
\bm{P}_{i}\bm{w}_{0}^{*}=0
, from the eigen decomposition of
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\bm{P}_{i}
we can easily obtain the inequality

\big(1-(\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{w}_{0}^{*})^{2}\big)\lambda_{\textnormal{min2}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\bm{P}_{i}\Big)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}^{\top}\bm{P}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0},

where
\lambda_{\textnormal{min2}}(\cdot)
denotes the second smallest eigenvalue of a matrix. The proof is complete.

theorem

.

We then elaborate the more complicated condition (

). First we recall that

is essential for (

) to preserve all inliers. Second, we argue that the term

which holds in the noiseless case (where

) but gets violated (in general) in the presence of noise. Finally, (

defined in a sophisticated way (
20
). If

then this term is

. Thus it remains to understand the values of

). In fact, as noise approaches zero, we have
\bm{Q}_{i}\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}\to 0
and (in general)

While the term

is quite small (Figure

. However, proof under this alternative condition demands showing some matrix inequality that involves a sum of matrix inverses always holds; we were not able to prove it.

Finally, we discuss the error bound (

The Noisy + Outliers Case

With the proof ideas of Theorems
7

Theorem 14
(Noisy Point Sets with Outliers).

Let
\bm{Q}_{1},\dots,\bm{Q}_{k^{*}}
be inliers, the rest
\bm{Q}_{j}
’s outliers, and
\hat{\bm{w}}_{0}
a global minimizer of (

5
Discussion and Future Work

We have investigated the tightness of a semidefinite relaxation (
SDR
) of truncated least-squares for robust rotation search in four different cases, and in each case we either showed improvements over prior work or proved new theoretical results. Our investigation can potentially be borrowed to understand semidefinite relaxations of many other geometric vision tasks; see
[
60
]
for
6
examples of truncated least-squares and see also
[
21
,
3
,
11
]
.

As is common in the optimization literature, the relaxation we analyzed is at the first (i.e., lowest) relaxation order of the
Lasserre
hierarchy
[
37
]
, or otherwise known as the
Shor relaxation

[
51
]
. A tighter relaxation that has quadratically more constraints than (
SDR
) exists (cf.
[
59
]
). However, analyzing this tighter relaxation is significantly harder, as one needs to either (1) construct quadratically more dual certificates during the proof, or (2) use more abstract optimality conditions (cf.
[
20
,
21
]
). Therefore, we leave this challenging question to future work.

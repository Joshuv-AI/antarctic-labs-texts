Abstract
. In this paper we give necessary and sufficient conditions for a functor to be representable in a strongly generated triangulated category which has a linear action by a graded ring, and we discuss some applications and examples.

1.
Introduction

Every object
X
in a category induces a contravariant functor into the category of sets that sends an object
Y
to the set
\operatorname{Hom}(Y,X)
. Any functor that is naturally isomorphic to such a functor is called
representable
. There are a number of results in various settings, called Brown representability, when every ‘reasonable’ functor is representable. The first such result is due to Brown; see
[
11
]
.

The first Brown representability result for triangulated categories was established by Neeman
[
19
, Theorem 3.1]
. The work on hand was motivated by
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
and
[
21
, 4.3]
.

Theorem
2.7
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded graded-commutative noetherian ring and
{\mathsf{T}}
a graded
R
-linear triangulated category, that is strongly generated, Ext-finite and idempotent complete. Then a graded
R
-linear cohomological functor
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
is graded representable if and only if
\mathsf{f}
only takes values in
\operatorname{grmod}(R)
.

In contrast to the previous works, we characterize the
graded representable
functors; those are the functors naturally isomorphic to

\coprod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(-,\Sigma^{d}X)

for some object
X
. The result is proved in
Section

2
. Without the assumption that
R
is noetherian and
{\mathsf{T}}
Ext-finite, we obtain necessary, through not sufficient, conditions for a functor to be graded representable; see
Corollary

2.19
.

The study of representability is motivated by the fact, that the characterization of representable functors in a triangulated category
{\mathsf{T}}
yields the existence of a right adjoint functor to a functor
{\mathsf{S}}\to{\mathsf{T}}
. For a nice discussion on this see
[
20
, Introduction]
. In
Section

3
we show the same holds in the graded setting.

Finally we discuss some examples where
Theorem

2.7
yields new insight: When
G
is a finite group and
R
a commutative noetherian ring, then
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(RG))
is Ext-finite as a
\coh^{*}(G,R)
-linear category. In the second example we consider the action of Hochschild cohomology
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(R/Q)
on
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(R))
, when
Q
is a regular ring and
R=Q/(\bm{f})
a quotient by a regular sequence.

2.
Representable Functors in the Graded Setting

Let
{\mathsf{T}}
be a triangulated category with suspension functor
\Sigma
.

2.1
.

For objects
X
and
Y
in
{\mathsf{T}}
we write

(2.1.1)

\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(X,Y)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\coprod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(X,\Sigma^{d}Y)\,.

When
{\mathsf{T}}=\operatorname{D}(R)
, the derived category of modules over a ring
R
, and
X
and
Y
are
R
-modules viewed as objects in
\operatorname{D}(R)
via the natural embedding, then this coincides with the classical Ext-groups.

2.2
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded graded-commutative ring. This means
R
decomposes as

R=\coprod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}R_{d}\,,

and the Koszul sign rule holds

rs=(-1)^{de}sr\quad\text{for }r\in R_{d}\text{ and }s\in R_{e}\,.

We say
r\in R_{d}
is an homogeneous element of degree
d
.

2.3
.

A triangulated category
{\mathsf{T}}
is
graded
R
-linear
if

(1)

for any objects
X
and
Y
in
{\mathsf{T}}
, the abelian group
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(X,Y)
is a graded
R
-module with the grading given by the coproduct in
Eq.

2.1.1
, and

(2)

composition is
R
-bilinear.

This data is equivalent to a ring homomorphism
R\to\ctr({\mathsf{T}})
, where

\ctr({\mathsf{T}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\coprod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\eta\colon\id_{\mathsf{T}}\to\Sigma^{d}\,\middle|\,\eta\Sigma=(-1)^{d}\Sigma\eta\right\}

is the
graded center of
{\mathsf{T}}
. More precisely, a ring homomorphism
\varphi\colon R\to\ctr({\mathsf{T}})
yields an
R
-action on
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(X,Y)
via

\displaystyle r\cdot-\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(X,Y)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(X,\Sigma^{d}Y)=\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(X,Y)[d]\,,

\displaystyle f\mapsto(\Sigma^{d}f)\circ\varphi(r)_{X}=\varphi(r)_{Y}\circ f

for any homogeneous element
r\in R
. Conversely, any homogeneous element
r\in R
yields a natural transformation
\eta
given by

\eta_{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}r\cdot\id_{X}\colon X\to\Sigma^{|r|}X

for any
X\in{\mathsf{T}}
. It is straightforward to check, that these identifications are well-defined and mutually inverse. The graded center has been studied in a number of works; for example
[
8
,
13
]
.

2.4
.

We denote by
\operatorname{grMod}(R)
the category of graded
R
-modules, and by
\operatorname{grmod}(R)
its full subcategory of finitely generated
R
-modules. The
n
th shift
M[n]
of a graded
R
-module
M
is given by
(M[n])_{d}=M_{n+d}
.

The suspension functor of a graded
R
-linear category
{\mathsf{T}}
in the first component of
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(-,-)
corresponds to the negative shift in
\operatorname{grMod}(R)
:

\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(\Sigma^{n}X,Y)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(X,Y)[-n]\,.

2.5
.

A functor
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
is
graded
R
-linear
, if

(1)

the induced map
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(X,Y)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{R}(\mathsf{f}(Y),\mathsf{f}(X))
is a map of graded
R
-modules, and

(2)

the suspension becomes the negative shift under
\mathsf{f}
, that is

\mathsf{f}(\Sigma^{n}X)=\mathsf{f}(X)[-n]\,.

The functor
\mathsf{f}
is
cohomological
if
\mathsf{f}
applied to any exact triangle yields a long exact sequence of graded
R
-modules.

Without explicitly stating we always assume that a natural transformation between graded
R
-linear functors respects this structure.

Definition 2.6
.

A functor
{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
is
graded representable
if it is naturally isomorphic to

\mathsf{g}_{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(-,X)\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)

for some object
X
in
{\mathsf{T}}
.

When
{\mathsf{T}}
is graded
R
-linear, then any graded representable functor is graded
R
-linear.

A graded
R
-linear functor
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
is graded representable if and only if
\mathsf{f}_{d}\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{Mod}(R_{0})
is representable for an(y) arbitrary integer
d
. The functors
\mathsf{f}_{d}
are the degree
d
part of
\mathsf{f}
, that is
\mathsf{f}_{d}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathsf{f}(X)_{d}
. Since
\mathsf{f}
is graded
R
-linear, the degree
d
part
\mathsf{f}_{d}
for an integer
d
encodes all the information of
\mathsf{f}
, that is

\mathsf{f}_{d}(\Sigma^{e}X)=\mathsf{f}(\Sigma^{e}X)_{d}=\mathsf{f}(X)[-e]_{d}=\mathsf{f}(X)_{d-e}=\mathsf{f}_{d-e}(X)\,.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded graded-commutative noetherian ring and
{\mathsf{T}}
a graded
R
-linear triangulated category, that is strongly generated, Ext-finite and idempotent complete. Then a graded
R
-linear cohomological functor
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
is graded representable if and only if
\mathsf{f}
is locally finite.

Before we give a proof, we recall some definitions and properties:

2.8
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring
R
is noetherian, if and only if
R_{0}
is noetherian and
R
is finitely generated as an
R_{0}
-algebra; see for example
[
15
, Corollaire (2.1.5)]
or
[
12
, Theorem 1.5.5]
. In particular, such a ring is bounded below.

2.9
.

A graded
R
-linear triangulated category
{\mathsf{T}}
is
Ext-finite
if for all
X,Y\in{\mathsf{T}}
, the graded
R
-module
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(X,Y)
is finitely generated.

A triangulated category
{\mathsf{T}}
is
idempotent complete
if for every object
X
in
{\mathsf{T}}
and every idempotent
e\in\End_{\mathsf{T}}(X)
, that is
e^{2}=e
, there exists an object
Y
and maps

i\colon Y\to X\quad\text{and}\quad p\colon X\to Y

such that
p\circ i=\id_{Y}
and
i\circ p=e
.

2.10
.

A subcategory
{\mathsf{S}}\subseteq{\mathsf{T}}
is
thick
, if it is triangulated and closed under retracts. Since the intersection of thick subcategories is thick, there exists a smallest thick subcategory of
{\mathsf{T}}
containing an object
G
, which we denote by
\thick(G)
. We say
G

finitely builds
an object
X
in
{\mathsf{T}}
when
X\in\thick(G)
.

There is an exhaustive filtration of
\thick_{R}(G)
: Let
\thick^{1}(G)
be the smallest full subcategory containing
G
that is closed under finite coproducts, retracts and suspension. Then

\thick^{n}(G)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{X\in{\mathsf{T}}\,\middle|\,\begin{gathered}\text{there exists }X^{\prime}\in{\mathsf{T}}\text{ and an exact triangle }\\
Y\to X\oplus X^{\prime}\to Z\to\Sigma Y\\
\text{ such that }Y\in\thick^{n-1}(G)\text{ and }Z\in\thick^{1}(G)\end{gathered}\right\}\,.

These are full subcategories and form an exhaustive filtration of
\thick(G)
; cf.
[
10
,
3
]
. In particular, if
X
lies in
\thick(G)
, then there exists an integer
n
, such that
X\in\thick^{n}(G)
.

A triangulated category
{\mathsf{T}}
is
strongly generated
, if there exists an object
G
in
{\mathsf{T}}
and a non-negative integer
n
, such that
{\mathsf{T}}=\thick^{n}(G)
. The object
X
is a
strong generator
of
{\mathsf{T}}
; cf.
[
21
]
.

In the reminder of this section, we give a proof of
Theorem

2.7
. We fix a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded graded-commutative ring
R
, a graded
R
-linear triangulated category
{\mathsf{T}}
, and a graded
R
-linear cohomological functor
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
.

Lemma 2.11

(Graded version of Yoneda’s lemma)
.

For any
X\in{\mathsf{T}}
, the map

\Nat(\mathsf{g}_{X},\mathsf{f})\to\mathsf{f}_{0}(X)\quad\text{given by}\quad\eta\mapsto\eta(X)(\id_{X})

is an isomorphism of abelian groups.

Proof.

For
u\in\mathsf{f}_{0}(X)
, we define a natural transformation

\eta_{u}\colon\mathsf{g}_{X}\to\mathsf{f}\quad\text{as}\quad\eta_{u}(Y)(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathsf{f}(f)(u)

where
Y\in{\mathsf{T}}
and
f\in\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(Y,X)
. Since
u
is a degree zero element, the map
\eta_{u}(Y)
is homogeneous. It is straightforward to verify that this is the inverse of the map in the claim and both are maps of abelian groups.
∎

In particular, any morphism
f\colon X\to Y
corresponds to a natural transformation

f_{*}\colon\mathsf{g}_{X}\to\mathsf{g}_{Y}

given by post-composition.

2.12
.

Adapting the definitions in
[
21
, Section 4]
, a graded
R
-linear functor
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
is

•

locally finitely generated
, if for every
X
in
{\mathsf{T}}
there exists
Y
in
{\mathsf{T}}
and a natural transformation
\zeta\colon\mathsf{g}_{Y}\to\mathsf{f}
such that
\zeta(X)
is surjective,

•

locally finitely presented
, if it is locally finitely generated and the kernel of any natural transformation
\mathsf{g}_{Y}\to\mathsf{f}
is locally finitely generated, and

•

locally finite
, if
\mathsf{f}
only takes values in
\operatorname{grmod}(R)
.

When
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
is locally finitely generated or locally finitely presented, then
\mathsf{f}_{d}\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{Mod}(R_{0})
is locally finitely generated or locally finitely presented in the sense of
[
21
, Section 4]
, respectively. The same need not hold for locally finite, for examples see
Section

3
.

If
{\mathsf{T}}
is Ext-finite, then any graded representable functor is locally finite. Without the assumption that
{\mathsf{T}}
is Ext-finite, we can make the following statement:

Lemma 2.13
.

Any graded representable functor is locally finitely presented.

Proof.

It is clear that a graded representable functor is locally finitely generated. Let
\mathsf{g}_{X}
be a graded representable functor, and
\mathsf{g}_{Y}\to\mathsf{g}_{X}
a natural transformation. By Yoneda’s
Lemma

2.11
, this corresponds to a morphism
Y\to X
. If we complete this to an exact triangle
Z\to Y\to X\to\Sigma Z
, the sequence

\mathsf{g}_{Z}\to\mathsf{g}_{Y}\to\mathsf{g}_{X}

is exact on
{\mathsf{T}}
. In particular, the kernel of
\mathsf{g}_{Y}\to\mathsf{g}_{X}
is locally finitely generated.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

If
\mathsf{f}
is locally finite, then
\mathsf{f}
is locally finitely generated.

Proof.

Let
X
be an object in
{\mathsf{T}}
. Then the
R
-module
\mathsf{f}(X)
is finitely generated, and we can choose a finite set of homogeneous generators
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
of
\mathsf{f}(X)
in degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
. Set

Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\coprod_{j=1}^{n}\Sigma^{d_{j}}X\,.

For every generator
x_{j}
, we obtain canonical maps

\Sigma^{d_{j}}X\xrightarrow{i_{j}}Y\xrightarrow{p_{j}}\Sigma^{d_{j}}X

whose composition is the identity map on
\Sigma^{d_{j}}X
. Let
y\in\mathsf{f}(Y)
be the canonical element, for which

x_{j}=\mathsf{f}(i_{j})(y)\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq n\,.

Because of the suspensions introduced in the definition of
Y
, the element
y
is homogeneous of degree 0. By Yoneda’s lemma
2.11
, the element
y
corresponds to the natural transformation
\zeta\colon\mathsf{g}_{Y}\to\mathsf{f}
with
\zeta(Y)(\id_{Y})=y
. Then
\zeta(y)(i_{j})=x_{j}
, and
\zeta(X)
is surjective. That is
\mathsf{f}
is locally finitely generated.
∎

In general a locally finite functor need not be locally finitely presented. This requires further assumptions on
R
and
{\mathsf{T}}
:

Lemma 2.15
.

If
R
is noetherian and
{\mathsf{T}}
Ext-finite, then a locally finite functor
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
is locally finitely presented.

Proof.

By
Lemma

2.14
the functor
\mathsf{f}
is locally finitely generated. Let
\mathsf{g}_{Y}\to\mathsf{f}
be a natural transformation. We set

\mathsf{f}^{\prime}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\ker(\mathsf{g}_{Y}(X)\to\mathsf{f}(X))\,.

Since
{\mathsf{T}}
is Ext-finite, the
R
-module
\mathsf{g}_{Y}(X)
is finitely generated. By assumption on
\mathsf{f}
, so is
\mathsf{f}(X)
. Since
R
is noetherian, the kernel
\mathsf{f}^{\prime}(X)
is also finitely generated. Thus
\mathsf{f}^{\prime}
is a locally finite functor and by
Lemma

2.14
it is locally finitely generated. In particular,
\mathsf{f}
is locally finitely presented.
∎

2.16
.

Let
(\mathsf{f}_{i},\eta_{i})_{i>0}
be a direct system of cohomological functors
\mathsf{f}_{i}\colon{\mathsf{T}}\to{\mathsf{A}}
where
{\mathsf{A}}
an abelian category and natural transformations
\eta_{i}\colon\mathsf{f}_{i}\to\mathsf{f}_{i+1}
. Following
[
21
, 4.2.2]
, a direct system
(\mathsf{f}_{i},\eta_{i})_{i>0}
is
almost constant on
a subcategory
{\mathsf{S}}
of
{\mathsf{T}}
, if for every
X\in{\mathsf{S}}
the sequence

0\to\ker(\eta_{i}(X))\to\mathsf{f}_{i}(X)\to\colim_{j}\mathsf{f}_{j}(X)\to 0

is exact for all positive integers
i
.

A direct system
(X_{i},f_{i})_{i>0}
of objects
X_{i}
and morphisms
f_{i}\colon X_{i}\to X_{i+1}
in
{\mathsf{T}}
is
almost constant on
{\mathsf{S}}
, if the induced direct system of functors
(\mathsf{g}_{X_{i}},(f_{i})_{*})_{i>0}
is almost constant on
{\mathsf{S}}
.

For almost constant direct systems the following hold; see
[
21
, Proposition 4.13]
.

Facts 2.17
.

Let
{\mathsf{S}}\subseteq{\mathsf{T}}
be a subcategory closed under suspension, and
(\mathsf{f}_{i},\eta_{i})_{i>0}
a direct system that is almost constant on
{\mathsf{S}}
. Then

(1)

(\mathsf{f}_{ni+r})_{i\geqslant 0}
is almost constant on
\thick^{n}({\mathsf{S}})
for any
r>0
, and

(2)

\mathsf{f}_{n+1}\to\colim\mathsf{f}_{i}
is split surjective on
\thick^{n}({\mathsf{S}})
.

If the functors
\mathsf{f}_{i}
are graded
R
-linear, the assumption that
{\mathsf{S}}
is closed under suspension is redundant.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
{\mathsf{T}}
be a strongly generated, graded
R
-linear triangulated category and
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{T}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
a cohomological graded
R
-linear functor. Then
\mathsf{f}
is locally finitely presented if and only if
\mathsf{f}
is a retract of a graded representable functor.

Proof.

We assume
\mathsf{f}
is locally finitely presented. Let
G\in{\mathsf{T}}
be a strong generator of
{\mathsf{T}}
with
\thick^{d}(G)={\mathsf{T}}
. Then there exist
A_{1}\in{\mathsf{T}}
and a natural transformation
\zeta_{1}\colon\mathsf{g}_{A_{1}}\to\mathsf{f}
such that
\zeta_{1}(G)
is surjective. Inductively we construct a direct system

\mathsf{g}_{A_{1}}\to\mathsf{g}_{A_{2}}\to\cdots

with compatible natural transformations
\zeta_{i}\colon\mathsf{g}_{A_{i}}\to\mathsf{f}
: Assume we have constructed
A_{i}
and
\zeta_{i}
for
i\leq n
. Since
\mathsf{f}
is locally finitely presented, there exists

\mathsf{g}_{B}\to\ker(\mathsf{g}_{A_{n}}\to\mathsf{f})

that is surjective on
G
. This induces a natural transformation
\mathsf{g}_{B}\to\mathsf{g}_{A_{n}}
, which by the graded version of Yoneda’s Lemma
2.11
corresponds to a morphism
f\colon B\to A_{n}
. We complete this morphism to an exact triangle

B\to A_{n}\to A_{n+1}\to\Sigma B

and apply
\mathsf{f}_{0}
, the degree 0 part of
\mathsf{f}
. By the graded version of Yoneda’s Lemma
2.11
we obtain the exact sequence

\Nat(\mathsf{g}_{B},\mathsf{f})\leftarrow\Nat(\mathsf{g}_{A_{n}},\mathsf{f})\leftarrow\Nat(\mathsf{g}_{A_{n+1}},\mathsf{f})\,.

Thus by construction of
B
there exists a natural transformation
\zeta_{n+1}
whose image is
\zeta_{n}
.

By this construction we have

\ker(\mathsf{g}_{A_{n}}(G)\to\mathsf{f}(G))=\ker(\mathsf{g}_{A_{n}}(G)\to\mathsf{g}_{A_{n+1}}(G))\,.

Using this and that
\zeta_{1}(G)
is surjective, it is straightforward to verify that the direct system is almost constant on
G
. Then the induced natural transformation
\colim_{i}\mathsf{g}_{A_{i}}\to\mathsf{f}
is a natural isomorphism. By
2.17
, the natural transformation

\mathsf{g}_{A_{d+1}}\to\colim_{i}\mathsf{g}_{A_{i}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{f}

is split surjective, and thus
\mathsf{f}
is a retract of
\mathsf{g}_{A_{d+1}}
on
{\mathsf{T}}
.

For the converse direction, we assume
\mathsf{f}
is the retract of
\mathsf{g}_{X}
for some object
X
. Then we have a canonical projection and a canonical injection

\mathsf{g}_{X}\to\mathsf{f}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{f}\to\mathsf{g}_{X}\,,

respectively. The canonical projection is surjective on
{\mathsf{T}}
, the canonical injection is injective. In particular, the canonical projection yields that
\mathsf{f}
is locally finitely generated. Given a natural transformation
\mathsf{g}_{Y}\to\mathsf{f}
, its kernel coincides with the kernel of the composition
\mathsf{g}_{Y}\to\mathsf{f}\to\mathsf{g}_{X}
. By
Lemma

2.13
, any representable functor is locally finitely presented, and thus is
\mathsf{f}
.
∎

Corollary 2.19
.

If
{\mathsf{T}}
is additionally idempotent complete, then every locally finitely presented functor is graded representable.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{f}
be a locally finitely presented functor. By
Proposition

2.18
, it is a retract of a graded representable functor
\mathsf{g}_{X}
. Then the natural transformation

\mathsf{g}_{X}\to\mathsf{f}\to\mathsf{g}_{X}

corresponds to an idempotent
e\colon X\to X
. Since
{\mathsf{T}}
is idempotent complete, there exists a retract of
Y
of
X
, such that
e
decomposes as the natural inclusion and projection morphism. Then
\mathsf{f}\to\mathsf{g}_{X}\to\mathsf{g}_{Y}
is a natural isomorphism, and
\mathsf{f}
is graded representable.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

2.7
.

Since
{\mathsf{T}}
is Ext-finite, any graded representable functor is locally finite. For the converse, we assume
\mathsf{f}
is locally finite. Since
R
is noetherian and
{\mathsf{T}}
Ext-finite, we can apply
Lemma

2.15
to obtain that
\mathsf{f}
is locally finitely presented. Then
\mathsf{f}
is graded representable by
Corollary

2.19
.
∎

3.
Applications

Adjoint Functors

As explained in
[
20
, Introduction]
there is a connection between representable functors and adjoint functors. In our context we obtain the following:

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded graded-commutative ring. A functor
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{S}}\to{\mathsf{T}}
between
R
-linear graded triangulated categories is
graded
R
-linear
, if it is exact and the induced map

\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{S}}}(X,Y)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(\mathsf{f}(X),\mathsf{f}(Y))

is a map of graded
R
-modules.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded graded-commutative ring, and
{\mathsf{S}}
,
{\mathsf{T}}
graded
R
-linear triangulated categories. Suppose
{\mathsf{T}}
is Ext-finite and every cohomological graded
R
-linear functor
{\mathsf{S}}^{op}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
, that is locally finite, is graded representable. Then every graded
R
-linear functor
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{S}}\to{\mathsf{T}}
has a right adjoint.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
20
, Theorem 8.4.4]
. Given
Y\in{\mathsf{T}}
we define a functor
\mathsf{h}\colon{\mathsf{S}}\to\operatorname{grMod}(R)
by

\mathsf{h}(-)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(\mathsf{f}(-),Y)\,.

This is a graded
R
-linear functor. Since
{\mathsf{T}}
is Ext-finite, this functor is locally finite. So by assumption
{\mathsf{h}}
is graded representable, that is there exists an object
\mathsf{f}^{\prime}(Y)\in{\mathsf{S}}
, such that

\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(\mathsf{f}(-),Y)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{S}}}(-,\mathsf{f}^{\prime}(Y))\,.

It remains to verify that
\mathsf{f}^{\prime}
is a functor and this isomorphism is natural in both components. Let
f\colon Y\to Z
be a morphism in
{\mathsf{T}}
. Then the induced map

\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{S}}}(-,\mathsf{f}^{\prime}(Y))\to\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{\mathsf{S}}}(-,\mathsf{f}^{\prime}(Z))

corresponds to a morphism
\mathsf{f}^{\prime}(Y)\to\mathsf{f}^{\prime}(Z)
by Yoneda’s
Lemma

2.11
. Thus
\mathsf{f}^{\prime}
is a functor. The above isomorphism is natural by construction. So
\mathsf{f}^{\prime}
is a right adjoint of
\mathsf{f}
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded graded-commutative noetherian ring and
{\mathsf{S}}
,
{\mathsf{T}}
Ext-finite graded
R
-linear triangulated categories. Suppose
{\mathsf{S}}
is strongly generated and idempotent complete. Then every graded
R
-linear functor
\mathsf{f}\colon{\mathsf{S}}\to{\mathsf{T}}
has a right adjoint. ∎

Derived Category

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring and
A
an
R
-algebra that is finitely generated as an
R
-module. Then
A
is noetherian; see for example
[
18
, Theorem 3.7]
. The bounded derived category of finitely generated modules over
A
, denoted by
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A))
, has a canonical structure as an
R
-linear category, and the
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A))}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Ext}^{0}_{R}(X,Y)
is finitely generated for any
X,Y
. In general, the category
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A))
need not be Ext-finite as an
R
-linear category. By
[
6
, Corollary 2.10]
, the category
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A))
is idempotent complete.

3.3
.

In general the question whether
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A))
is strongly generated is rather difficult. When
A
is artinian, then
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A))
is strongly generated by
[
21
, Proposition 7.37]
. When
A=R
is a commutative notherian ring, then
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(R))
is strongly generated when
R
is either essentially of finite type over a field or over an equicharacteristic excellent local ring; see
[
1
, Main Theorem]
and
[
16
, Corollary 7.2]
.

In the following we discuss two examples in which
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A))
is Ext-finite for some cohomology ring connected to
A
.

Finite Group over a Commutative Ring

We consider
A=RG
, the group algebra of a finite group
G
.

3.4
.

The group cohomology of the group algebra
RG
with coefficients in an
RG
-complex
M
is

\coh^{*}(G,M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{RG}(R,M)\,.

When
M=R
this is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded graded-commutative ring, and every
\coh^{*}(G,M)
is a graded
\coh^{*}(G,R)
-module. In particular, for
RG
-complexes
X
,
Y
the identification

\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(RG))}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{RG}(X,Y)\cong\coh^{*}(G,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(X,Y))

holds and the cohomology ring
\coh^{*}(G,R)
acts on any Ext-module; see for example
[
9
, Proposition 3.1.8]
. So the bounded derived category of finitely generated
RG
-modules
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(RG))
is graded
\coh^{*}(G,R)
-linear.

3.5
.

By
[
22
,
14
]
, the group cohomology ring
\coh^{*}(G,R)
is noetherian, and
\coh^{*}(G,M)
is finitely generated over
\coh^{*}(G,R)
for every finitely generated
RG
-module
M
. In particular, the derived category
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(RG))
is Ext-finite as a graded
\coh^{*}(G,R)
-linear triangulated category.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
R
be a commutative notherian ring and
G
a finite group. If
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(RG))
is strongly generated, then a graded
\coh^{*}(G,R)
-linear functor

\mathsf{f}\colon\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(RG))\to\operatorname{grMod}(\coh^{*}(G,R))

is graded representable if and only if
\mathsf{f}
is locally finite. ∎

Regular ring modulo a regular sequence

We consider
R=A
a commutative noetherian ring.

3.7
.

The category
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(R))
is Ext-finite over
R
, if and only if the Ext-modules
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{R}(X,Y)
are bounded for all
X
and
Y
in
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(R))
. That is precisely when
R
is regular: When
R
is regular the Ext-modules are bounded by definition. For the converse, for every
X
in
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(R))
the Ext-module
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{R}(X,R/\mathfrak{p})
is bounded and
X_{\mathfrak{p}}
has finite projective dimension for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
. Then
X
has finite projective dimension; see
[
7
, Lemma 4.5]
for modules, and
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
17
, Theorem 3.6]
for complexes.

When
R
is regular, the bounded derived category
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(R))
is strongly generated if and only if
R
is a strong generator. The later holds precisely when
R
has finite global dimension, that is
R
has finite Krull dimension. Then Rouquier’s representability theorem
[
21
, Corollary 4.18]
applies.

3.8
.

Suppose
R=Q/(\bm{f})
is the quotient of a regular ring
Q
by a regular sequence
\bm{f}=f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}
. Then there exist cohomological operators
\bm{\chi}=\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{c}
in degree 2, such that for
X,Y
in
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(R))
the graded modules
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{R}(X,Y)
are finitely generated over the noetherian graded ring
R[\bm{\chi}]
; see
[
4
, Theorem (4.2)]
. In particular, the category
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(R))
is
R[\bm{\chi}]
-linear and Ext-finite.

The ring of cohomological operators coincides with the Hochschild cohomology

R[\bm{\chi}]\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(R/Q)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{R\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{Q}R}(R,R)\,;

see
[
2
, Section 3]
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
R=Q/(\bm{f})
be the quotient of a regular ring
Q
by a regular sequence
\bm{f}=f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}
with cohomological operators
\bm{\chi}
. If
\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(R))
is strongly generated, then any graded
R[\bm{\chi}]
-linear functor
\mathsf{f}\colon\operatorname{D}_{b}(\operatorname{mod}(R))\to\operatorname{grMod}(R[\bm{\chi}])
is graded representable if and only if
\mathsf{f}
is locally finite. ∎

3.10
.

For
Corollaries

3.6
and
3.9
it is crucial that the ring action on the derived category is graded, since the Ext-modules need not be not bounded. In particular,
Corollaries

3.6
and
3.9
are not consequences of
[
21
, 4.3]
, but require
Theorem

2.7
.

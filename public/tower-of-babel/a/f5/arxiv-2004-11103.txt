Separation of quantum, spatial quantum, and approximate quantum correlations
Salman Beigi

Abstract
Quantum nonlocal correlations are generated by implementation of local quantum measurements on spatially separated quantum subsystems. Depending on the underlying mathematical model, various notions of sets of quantum correlations can be defined. In this paper we prove separations of such sets of quantum correlations. In particular, we show that the set of bipartite quantum correlations with four binary measurements per party becomes strictly smaller once we restrict the local Hilbert spaces to be finite dimensional, i.e. , 𝒞 q ( 4 , 4 , 2 , 2 ) ≠ 𝒞 qs ( 4 , 4 , 2 , 2 ) \mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{q}}}^{(4,4,2,2)}\neq\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qs}}}^{(4,4,2,2)} . We also prove non-closure of the set of bipartite quantum correlations with four ternary measurements per party, i.e. , 𝒞 qs ( 4 , 4 , 3 , 3 ) ≠ 𝒞 qa ( 4 , 4 , 3 , 3 ) \mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qs}}}^{(4,4,3,3)}\neq\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qa}}}^{(4,4,3,3)} .

Our results:

In this paper we give new proofs for both the separations
\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{q}}}\neq\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qs}}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qs}}}\neq\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qa}}}

Theorem 1
.

\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{q}}}^{(4,4,2,2)}\neq\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qs}}}^{(4,4,2,2)}
.

This theorem is an improvement on the result of
[
7
]
that establishes this separation for
(n_{A},n_{B},m_{A},m_{B})=(4,5,3,3)
. We also, building on this result, show that

\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{q}}}^{(3,4,3,2)}\neq\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qs}}}^{(3,4,3,2)},

which again is an improvement on
[
7
]
, but is not comparable to Theorem
1
.

Similarly to
[
7
]
our proof of this theorem is based on the self-testing of tilted CHSH correlations. That is, we assume that the parties are playing two copies of the tilted CHSH game to self-test a certain entangled state. But these two tests are not independent, and there are certain correlations among their outputs. This forces a particular structure on the shared entangled state. Then by studying the ranks of measurement operators we conclude that they cannot be finite, so that
\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{q}}}\neq\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qs}}}
.

To emphasize on the main difference of our proof with that of
[
7
]
, we note that the shared entangled state
|\psi\rangle
in
[
7
]
is taken to be
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}|i\rangle|i\rangle
up to a normalization factor for some
0<\alpha<1
. Then this state can be written as

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}|i\rangle|i\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{2j}\Big(|2j\rangle|2j\rangle+\alpha|2j+1\rangle|2j+1\rangle\Big),

which up to local isometries equals
\big(|00\rangle+\alpha|11\rangle\big)\otimes|\psi^{\prime}\rangle
for some auxiliary state
|\psi^{\prime}\rangle
. So we may apply the tilted CHSH game to self-test the shared state
\big(|00\rangle+\alpha|11\rangle\big)
. In another decomposition we may write

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}|i\rangle|i\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{2j-1}\Big(|2j-1\rangle|2j-1\rangle+\alpha|2j\rangle|2j\rangle\Big)+|0\rangle|0\rangle,

which again up to local isometries equals
\big(|00\rangle+\alpha|11\rangle\big)\otimes|\psi^{\prime}\rangle\bigoplus|22\rangle
. Thus again we may apply the tilted CHSH game on the orthogonal subspace to
|2\rangle
to self-test
\big(|00\rangle+\alpha|11\rangle\big)
. Considering these two tilted CHSH games and the non-trivial correlations between them, the main result of
[
7
]
is derived. Here our proof strategy departs from
[
7
]
by considering the starting shared state

\sum_{i=-\infty}^{+\infty}\alpha^{|i|}|i\rangle|i\rangle.

This state can be written as
\big(|00\rangle+\alpha|11\rangle\big)\otimes|\psi^{\prime}\rangle
up to local isometries in two different ways
without any extra summand
. This would allow us to decrease the number of outputs from
m_{A}=m_{B}=3
to
m_{A}=m_{B}=2
. However, after this modification, the argument of
[
7
]
based on the analysis of Schmidt coefficients of the shared state does not directly work. To overcome this difficulty, we further use self-testing properties of tilted CHSH correlations and build our arguments based on the ranks of the measurement operators. This gives us our first result
\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{q}}}^{(4,4,2,2)}\neq\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qs}}}^{(4,4,2,2)}

We note that
\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{q}}}^{(2,n_{B},2,2)}=\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qs}}}^{(2,n_{B},2,2)}=\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qa}}}^{(2,n_{B},2,2)}

Self-testing protocols

Recall that the CHSH inequality states that for binary observables
A_{s},B_{t}
,
s,t\in\{0,1\}
, with
\pm 1
values we have

\langle A_{0}B_{0}\rangle+\langle A_{0}B_{1}\rangle+\langle A_{1}B_{0}\rangle-\langle A_{1}B_{1}\rangle\leq 2,

where
\langle AB\rangle
is the expectation value of the observable
AB
.
The CHSH inequality belongs to a family of such inequalities called the tilted CHSH inequalities
[
1
]
, which given a parameter
\beta\in[0,2)
states that

\beta\langle A_{0}\rangle+\langle A_{0}B_{0}\rangle+\langle A_{0}B_{1}\rangle+\langle A_{1}B_{0}\rangle-\langle A_{1}B_{1}\rangle\leq 2+\beta.

Observe that for
\beta=0
we recover the standard CHSH inequality. Moreover, similar to the CHSH inequality, the tilted CHSH inequality is violated in the quantum regime. Indeed, there are quantum binary observables
A_{s},B_{t}
,
s,t\in\{0,1\}
and an entangled state
|\phi\rangle
such that

\displaystyle\big\langle\phi\big|\big(\beta A_{0}\otimes I+A_{0}\otimes B_{0}+A_{0}\otimes B_{1}+A_{1}\otimes B_{0}-A_{1}\otimes B_{1}\big)\big|\phi\big\rangle=\sqrt{8+2\beta^{2}}.

(3)

Moreover,
\sqrt{8+2\beta^{2}}
is the maximum value that can be gained by a quantum strategy.

Let us describe the observables and the shared state that give (
3
). Given
\beta\in[0,2)
define
\theta,\mu\in(0,\pi/4]
, and
\alpha\in(0,1]
by

\tan(\mu)=\sin(2\theta)=\sqrt{\frac{4-\beta^{2}}{4+\beta^{2}}},\qquad\quad\alpha=\tan(\theta).

Let

\displaystyle|\Phi^{(\alpha)}\rangle=\cos\theta|00\rangle+\sin\theta|11\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}}(|00\rangle+\alpha|11\rangle),

(4)

be a two-qubit state. Also let
\sigma_{z}
and
\sigma_{x}
be the Pauli operators and define

\displaystyle\sigma_{z}^{(\alpha)}:=\cos\mu\sigma_{z}+\sin\mu\sigma_{x},\qquad\sigma_{x}^{(\alpha)}=\cos\mu\sigma_{z}-\sin\mu\sigma_{x}.

(5)

Observe that
\sigma_{z}^{(\alpha)},\sigma_{x}^{(\alpha)}
are also binary observables.
Next let

A_{0}=\sigma_{z},\qquad A_{1}=\sigma_{x},\qquad B_{0}=\sigma_{z}^{(\alpha)},\qquad B_{1}=\sigma_{x}^{(\alpha)}.

Then with the shared state
|\Phi^{(\alpha)}\rangle
in (
4
) and these four observables we obtain (
3
).

Surprisingly, the above shared state and observables represent essentially the unique strategy that achieves (
3
). This is called the self-testing property of the tilted CHSH inequality and is proven in

There are some remarks in line.

Remark 1
.

In the statement of the theorem we assume that
U,V
are not only isometries, but also invertible,
i.e.
, they are onto. Indeed, we claim that for such isometries
U,V
, their images
U\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathsf{A}}
and
V\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathsf{B}}
are always of the form
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}}
and
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{B}^{\prime}}
respectively, so that we may identify
\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{B}^{\prime}}
in the statement of the theorem by
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{B}^{\prime}}
respectively. To prove this claim, we compute

\displaystyle U\text{\rm tr}_{\mathsf{B}}\big(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\big)U^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\text{\rm tr}_{\mathsf{B}}\big(U\otimes I|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|U^{\dagger}\otimes I\big)

\displaystyle=\text{\rm tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathsf{B}^{\prime}}\big(U\otimes V|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|U^{\dagger}\otimes V^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle=\text{\rm tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathsf{B}^{\prime}}\big(|\Phi^{(\alpha)}\rangle\langle\Phi^{(\alpha)}|\otimes|\psi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\psi^{\prime}|\big)

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}\theta\big(|0\rangle\langle 0|+\alpha^{2}|1\rangle\langle 1|\big)\otimes\text{\rm tr}_{\mathsf{B}^{\prime}}\big(|\psi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\psi^{\prime}|\big).

where we used

,
i.e.
,

which of course is of the form
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}}
. The proof for

Remark 2
.

Suppose that

in the statement of the theorem has finite Schmidt rank. Then by definition
\text{\rm{Sch-rank}}(|\psi\rangle)=\dim\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathsf{A}}=\dim\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathsf{B}}

\text{\rm{Sch-rank}}(|\psi\rangle)=\text{\rm{Sch-rank}}(U\otimes V|\psi\rangle)=\text{\rm{Sch-rank}}(|\Phi_{\alpha}\rangle)\cdot\text{\rm{Sch-rank}}(|\psi^{\prime}\rangle).

Then using the fact that

\text{\rm{Sch-rank}}(|\psi^{\prime}\rangle)=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{B}^{\prime}}
and
\text{\rm tr}_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}}(|\psi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\psi^{\prime}|),\text{\rm tr}_{\mathsf{B}^{\prime}}(|\psi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\psi^{\prime}|)
are full-rank.

We note that the operators

that in fact any entangled state of finite Schmidt rank can be self-tested. In the special case of the maximally entangled state of Schmidt rank
d

[

. However, one would expect to get self-testing protocols for maximally entangled states with smaller question and answer sets, particularly with

and
m_{A}=m_{B}=d
. This problem was resolved recently in
[
17
]
. To state this result we need to develop some notation.

Binary observables are usually represented by unitary operators
A
with eigenvalues
\pm 1
(equivalently with
A^{2}=I
). Indeed, to a binary projective measurement
\{P_{0},P_{1}\}
one can associate the binary observable
A=P_{0}-P_{1}
. Conversely, having such
A
, the operators of the associated projective measurement are given by
P_{0}=(I+A)/2
and
P_{1}=(I-A)/2
. We denote the projection on the
(-1)^{a}
eigenspace of
A
by
A^{(a)}=P_{a}=(I+(-1)^{a}A)/2
for
a\in\{0,1\}
.
We can apply the same idea to represent
d
-valued measurements
,
i.e.
, measurements with outcomes in
\{0,1,\dots,d-1\}
.

Let
\{P_{0},\dots,P_{d-1}\}
be a
d
-valued projective measurement. Then define

A=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\omega^{k}P_{k},

where
\omega=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/d}
is a
d
-th root of unity. Observe that
A
is a unitary and
A^{d}=I
. Conversely, eigen-decomposition of any unitary
A
with
A^{d}=I
corresponds to a
d
-valued projective measurement with measurement operators:

P_{a}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}\omega^{-a\ell}A^{\ell},\quad\qquad 0\leq a\leq d-1.

As in the binary case, we denote by
A^{(a)}=P_{a}
, for
0\leq a\leq d-1
, the projection on the eigenspace of
A
with eigenvalue
\omega^{a}
.
As a result, similar to the binary case, any Bell inequality with
d
-valued measurements, can be written in terms of unitary operators
A_{i},B_{j}
with
A_{i}^{d}=B_{j}^{d}=1
, and their powers. We call such unitaries
d
-valued observables
.

We now state our desired Bell inequality which following
[
17
]
we call the
SATWAP Bell inequality

[
16
]
. Let
A_{0},A_{1}
be Alice’s observables with
A_{0}^{d}=A_{1}^{d}=I
and
B_{0},B_{1}
be Bob’s observables with
B^{d}_{0}=B^{d}_{1}=I
as above. Then the SATWAP Bell operator is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{d}=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\Big(r_{k}A_{0}^{k}\otimes B_{0}^{-k}+\bar{r}_{k}\omega^{k}A_{0}^{k}\otimes B_{1}^{-k}+\bar{r}_{k}A_{1}^{k}\otimes B_{0}^{-k}+r_{k}A_{1}^{k}\otimes B_{1}^{-k}\Big),

(6)

where as before
\omega=2^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/d}
,

r_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\omega^{\frac{2k-d}{8}}=\frac{1-\mathrm{i}}{2}\omega^{\frac{k}{4}},

and
\bar{r}_{k}
is the complex conjugate of
r_{k}
given by
\bar{r}_{k}=r_{d-k}
. It is known that the maximum of
\mathcal{O}_{d}
in the local hidden variable model,
i.e.
, in
\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{L}}}^{(2,2,d,d)}

\big[2\cot(\pi/4d)-\cot(3\pi/4d)-4\big]/2
. Also, for any entangled state
|\psi\rangle
we have

\langle\psi|\mathcal{O}_{d}|\psi\rangle\leq 2(d-1).

Moreover, there is essentially a unique strategy to saturate the above inequality.

.

(Self-testing of SATWAP

(ii)

4

\mathcal{C}_{\text{\rm{qa}}}^{(4,4,3,3)}

In constructing

Following

𝐜 \mathbf{c} axis electrical transport at the metamagnetic transition in the heavy-fermion superconductor UTe 2 under pressure
G. Knebel, A. Pourret, S. Rousseau, N. Marquardt, D. Braithwaite, F. Honda, D. Aoki, G. Lapertot, W. Knafo, G. Seyfarth, J-P. Brison, J. Flouquet

Abstract
The electrical resistivity of the unconventional superconductor UTe 2 shows very anisotropic behavior in the normal state depending on the current direction. In the present paper we show that the maximum in the resistivity ρ c \rho_{c} for current applied along the c c axis at T ρ c max ≈ 14.75 T^{\rm max}_{\rho_{c}}\approx 14.75 K follows the minimum in the thermal expansion T α ⋆ T_{\alpha}^{\star} along b b axis. Under a magnetic field applied along the b b axis, T ρ c max T^{\rm max}_{\rho_{c}} can be tracked up to the critical point of the first order metamagnetic transition, which is located near 6 K and 34.5 T. Surprisingly, at the metamagnetic field H m H_{m} the resistivity ρ c \rho_{c} shows a steplike decrease while the resistivities ρ a \rho_{a} and ρ b \rho_{b} , for current along the a a and b b axis, respectively, show a steplike increase. Under hydrostatic pressure T ρ c max T^{\rm max}_{\rho_{c}} and H m H_{m} decrease significantly up to the critical pressure p c p_{c} at which superconductivity is suppressed and a long range antiferromagnetic order appears. We show that the phase diagram at different pressures can be scaled by T ρ c max T^{\rm max}_{\rho_{c}} in field and temperature suggesting that this temperature scale is governing the main interactions in the normal state.

Introduction

The discovery of superconductivity in UTe
2
has raised an intense research effort due to the possible spin-triplet pairing
Ran et al. 2019a
;
Aoki et al. 2019
;
Aoki et al. 2022a
. Indication for this rare pairing state is the strong enhancement of the superconducting upper critical field
H_{c2}
, which exceeds by far the Pauli limit of superconductivity for all three crystallographic axes. Another one is the change of this paramgnetic limit in the different superconducting phases observed under pressure for field along the easy a-axis
Knebel et al. 2020
. Knight shift measurements in magnetic resonance experiments (NMR) suggest a possible spin-triplet superconducting state too
Nakamine et al. 2021
;
Fujibayashi et al. 2022
;
Matsumura et al. 2023
. Moreover, a quite singular property of superconductivity in UTe
2
is that the pairing strength itself is sensitive to the magnetic field for all crystal axes
Rosuel et al. 2023
. For a field applied along the
b
axis, superconductivity is enhanced above
H^{\star}\approx 17
T
Knebel et al. 2019
;
Ran et al. 2019b
, and a phase transition between two susperconducting phases, the low-field superconducting phase (lfSC) and a high-field phase (hfSC), have been evidenced by specific heat
Rosuel et al. 2023
, and independently by combined NMR and ac susceptibility measurements
Kinjo et al. 2023a
. This shows that the field-induced hfSC is a bulk superconducting phase.
It is limited by a metamagnetic transition at
\mu_{0}H_{m}\approx 35
T
Knebel et al. 2019
;
Ran et al. 2019b
. The first order metamagnetic transition is characterized by a large jump of the magnetization
Miyake et al. 2019
, a volume discontinuity
Miyake et al. 2022
, and a jump in the magnetoresistance with the current applied along the
a
axis
Knafo et al. 2019
. It is accompanied by a strong increase of the Sommerfeld coefficient
\gamma
of the specific heat
Imajo et al. 2019
;
Miyake et al. 2021
;
Rosuel et al. 2023
and of the
A
coefficent of the Fermi-liquid resistivity
Knafo et al. 2019
;
Thebault et al. 2022
. Recent NMR measurements show that critical longitudinal magnetic fluctuations starts to develop for
H>H^{\star}
and are diverging in the vicinity of
H_{m}

Tokunaga et al. 2023
.
An abrupt Fermi-surface change at
H_{m}
has been discussed from Hall effect measurements
Niu et al. 2020a
, but constitutes still an open question
Helm et al. 2024
. Superconductivity in UTe
2
was claimed to be topologically non-trivial following the detection of chiral-edge states in scanning-tunneling microscopy (STM)
Jiao et al. 2020
, and of broken time-reversal symmetry in Kerr effect studies
Hayes et al. 2021
. However, these results are still discussed controversally and may depend on the sample quality
Azari et al. 2023
;
Ajeesh et al. 2023
.

Under hydrostatic pressure, two superconducting phases occur above 0.3 GPa, indicating multiphase superconductivity at zero field
Braithwaite et al. 2019
;
Thomas et al. 2021
;
Lin et al. 2020
. Different superconducting phases have been confirmed by measurements under magnetic field applied along the
a
and
b

Aoki et al. 2020a
;
Lin et al. 2020
. The superconducting phases are suppressed at a critical pressure

, but recent neutron diffraction experiments under confirm an incommensurate antiferromagnetic order above

. The magnetic anisotropy is reversed at
p_{c}

Li et al. 2021
;
Thomas et al. 2024
;
Vališka et al. 2021
;
Kinjo et al. 2022
.
While the
a

b

p_{c}
, the upper critical field

is the highest for the

;
Ran et al. 2020
;
Aoki et al. 2021
;

To understand the occurrence of these various superconducting phases in UTe
2
the knowledge of the electronic structure and of the magnetic fluctuations is necessary. Various electronic band structure calculations indicate cylindrical Fermi surfaces along the
c
axis

;
Ishizuka et al. 2019
;
Fujimori et al. 2019
;
Miao et al. 2020
;
Shishidou et al. 2021
;
Choi et al. 2022
. Experimentally the Fermi surface of UTe

is not fully determined, but angle resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) experiments

;
Eaton et al. 2024
;
Broyles et al. 2023
;
Aoki et al. 2023
.

Resistivity measurements with different current directions suggest that UTe
2
is a three dimensional metal

indicating that either the cylinders are strongly corrugated or that a three-dimensional Fermi surface is missing in quantum oscillation experiments. For currents applied along the
a
and
b

Once a non-magnetic background of the scattering is subtracted, this maximum is shifted down to the temperature scale

;

;

. For a current applied along the

;
Thebault et al. 2022
;
Girod et al. 2022
;
Kim et al. 2023
. Near this characteristic temperature several other quantities show anomalies: the electronic specific heat has a broad maximum

, the thermal expansion coefficients along the

b

;

;
Butch et al. 2022
. Furthermore, inelastic neutron scattering experiments show the development of a magnetic excitation below

K at the incommensurate wavevector
\mathbf{k_{1}}=(0,0.57,0)

;
Knafo et al. 2021
;
Butch et al. 2022
. They also reveal that these fluctuations are low dimensional and antiferromagnetic, saturating below

;
Knafo et al. 2021

In the following Section

Electrical resistivity measurements have been performed with a standard four point lock-in technique with a maximal applied current of 0.5 mA for the measurements at ambient pressure and 1 mA in the high pressure cell. For all samples, electrical contacts to the samples have been performed by spot welding 15
\mu
m diameter Au wires on a freshly polished surface of the crystal as it is known that the surface of UTe
2
is very sensitive to air. To strengthen the electrical contacts mechanically, tiny drops of conducting silver paste have been added on top of the welded contacts. The contact resistance is typically of the order of several m
\Omega
, but this has not been further studied.

The high-field transport measurements have been performed at the high magnetic field laboratory LNCMI in Grenoble using the resistive 36 T magnet M9 using a
3
He cryostat with a base temperature near 400 mK. We used a MP35N piston cylinder pressure cell to apply pressures up to 1.61 GPa with Daphne 7373 oil as pressure medium for the high-pressure resistivity experiment. The outer diameter of the pressure cell is only 15 mm which fits very close to the inner diameter of the vaccuum can of the cryostat (16 mm). The high-pressure experiments have been performed up to a maximal field of 35 T. The temperature has been measured with a calibrated RuO
2
chip which is glued on the pressure cell. The lowest temperature in the pressure cell was
T_{\rm min}\approx 1.2
K.

Thermal expansion measurements at zero pressure have been performed using a high resolution capacitive dilatometer

in the LNCMI Grenoble on the magnet M10 with a maximal field of 30 T in the temperature range from 2 to 25 K. The CVT grown sample (S2) with

V
Conclusions

We have shown that the resistivity in UTe
2
depends strongly on the electrical current direction. The measurements with
J\parallel c
clearly reveal the important energy scales. In particular, at the metamagnetic transtion at
\mu_{0}H_{m}\approx 34.5
T the resistivity
\rho_{c}
strongly decreases for current applied along the
c
axis, while
\rho_{a}
and
\rho_{b}
strongly increase (current along
a
or
b
axis respectively).
The field dependence of the
A
coefficient for
J\parallel c
seems to replicate
\gamma(H)
better than for the other current directions.
The maximum
T^{\rm max}_{\rho_{c}}
in the temperature dependence of
\rho_{c}
decrease on approaching the critical point of the first order metamagnetic transition line.
T^{\rm max}_{\rho_{c}}
coincides with the temperature of the minimum observed in the thermal expansion
\alpha_{b}
along the
b
axis. Under hydrostatic pressure
T^{\rm max}_{\rho_{c}}
and
H_{m}
decreases up to the critical pressure
p_{c}
. The phase diagram in the normal state below
p_{c}
scales with
T^{\rm max}_{\rho_{c}}
, indicating the importance of this low energy scale: the coherence along the
c
axis governs the low temperature behavior. Superconductivity is observed for all pressures up to
H_{m}
, which collapses at
p_{c}
. At zero pressure the low field superconducting phase lfSC scales with
T_{sc}
independently of the sample quality, while the high field superconducting phase hfSC is strongly sample dependent. This indicated that these two phases may emerge from different pairing mechanisms
Rosuel et al. 2023
. Accurate determination of the phase diagrams under pressure by thermodynamic measurements under magnetic field along
b
are strongly required, in particular close to the pressure where the high-temperature superconducting phase occurs. This will elucidate whether the high temperature superconducting phase is the pressure evolution of the field-induced hfSC phase, or not. A challenge in future will be to precise the field and pressure dependence of the valence and spin correlations and their feedback on superconductivity under pressure.

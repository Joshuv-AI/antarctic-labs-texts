Confined H - ion within a density functional framework
Sangita Majumdar, Neetik Mukherjee, Amlan K. Roy

Abstract
Ground and excited states of a confined negative Hydrogen ion has been pursued under Kohn-Sham density functional approach by invoking a physically motivated work-function-based exchange potential. The exchange-only results are of near Hartree-Fock quality. Local parameterised Wigner-type, and gradient- and Laplacian-dependent non-local Lee-Yang-Parr functionals are chosen to investigate the electron correlation effects. Eigenfunctions and eigenvalues are extracted by using a generalized pseudospectral method obeying Dirichlet boundary condition. Energy values are reported for 1s 2 ( 1 S), 1s2s ( 3,1 S) and 1s2p ( 3,1 P) states. Performance of the correlation functionals in the context of confinement is examined critically. The present results are in excellent agreement with available literature. Additionally, Shannon entropy and Onicescu energy are offered for ground and low lying singly excited 1s2s ( 3 S) and 1s2p ( 3 P) states. The influence of electron correlation is more predominant in the weaker confinement limit and it decays with an increase in confinement strength. In essence, energy and some information measures are estimated using a newly formulated density functional strategy. Keywords: Shannon Entropy, Onicescu energy, quantum confinement, impenetrable boundary, excited states, hydride ion, exchange-correlation.

II
Methodology

Here we briefly outline the proposed density functional method for a particular state of an arbitrary
atom centered inside an impenetrable spherical cavity, followed by the GPS scheme for calculation of eigenvalues and
energies of KS equation. This has been very successful for ground and various states (such as singly,
doubly, triply excited states corresponding to low- and high-lying excitation, valence and core excitation,
autoionizing, hollow, doubly hollow, Rydberg and satellite states etc.) of
free or unconfined

neutral atoms as well as ions
roy97
;
roy97a
;
roy97b
;
roy02
;
roy04
;
roy05
;
roy07
. Very
recently, this has been extended to confinement situations
majumdar20
. Our focus remains on essential portions,
omitting the relevant details, which are available in above references.

Our starting point is the non-relativistic single-particle time-independent KS equation with imposed confinement, which
can be conveniently written as (atomic unit employed unless otherwise mentioned),

\left[-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+v_{eff}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})\right]\phi_{i}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})=\varepsilon_{i}\phi_{i}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}),

(1)

where the “effective” potential is constituted of following terms,

\displaystyle v_{eff}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{ne}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})+\int\frac{\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}{|\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}|}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}+\frac{\delta E_{xc}[\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})]}{\delta\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})}+v_{conf}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}).

(2)

In this equation, the first three terms in right-hand site correspond to usual electron-nuclear attraction,
classical Hartree repulsion and XC potentials respectively. The following perturbation accounts for the desired
confinement (
r_{c}
refers to the radius of spherical cage),

v_{conf}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})=\begin{cases}0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ r\leq r_{c}\\
+\infty,\ \ \ \ r>r_{c}.\\
\end{cases}

(3)

Despite the remarkable progress and success in ground-state electronic structure and properties of atoms/molecules,
in past five decades, excited state-DFT has faced difficulties and challenges. This is mainly due to
lack of (i) an analogous Hohenberg-Kohn theorem and (ii) an accurate, proper XC functional for a general excited state.
This work intends to employ an exchange potential
sahni90
;
sahni92
, which is derived
from physical grounds. Accordingly, one can interpret exchange energy as resulting from an interaction between
an electron at
\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}
and its Fermi-Coulomb hole charge density
\rho_{x}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})
at
\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}
.
Thus it is given by,

E_{x}[\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})]=\frac{1}{2}\int\int\frac{\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})\rho_{x}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}{|\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}|}\ \mathrm{d}\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}\ \mathrm{d}\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}.

(4)

The unique local exchange potential
v_{x}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})
for a given state, can then be defined as the work done in
bringing an electron to the point
\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}
against the electric field arising out of its Fermi-Coulomb hole density,
leading to the following form,

v_{x}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})=-\int_{\infty}^{r}\mathcal{E}_{x}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})\mathrm{d}l,

(5)

where the electric field may be defined as,

\mathcal{E}_{x}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})=\int\frac{\rho_{x}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}{|\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}|^{3}}\ \mathrm{d}{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{{}^{\prime}}}.

(6)

One can write the Fermi hole in terms of orbitals as,

\rho_{x}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})=-\frac{\left|\gamma(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})\right|^{2}}{2\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})},

(7)

where
\left|\gamma(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})\right|=\sum_{i}\phi_{i}^{*}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})\phi_{i}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})
is
single-particle density matrix, while
\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})
corresponds to electron density, expressed in terms of occupied
orbitals (
n_{i}
implies occupation number) as,

\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}|\phi_{i}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})|^{2}.

(8)

While
v_{x}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})
thus defined above, can be accurately calculated, one needs to approximate the unknown correlation
potential
v_{c}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})
for practical calculations. For this purpose, we employ two correlation functionals, namely,
a Wigner-type
brual78
and LYP
lee88
. They have been chosen on the basis of their success in the context
of excited states, which are recorded in the references
roy97
;
roy97a
;
roy97b
;
roy02
;
roy04
;
roy05
;
roy07
. This will
give the opportunity to examine and calibrate the performance of these functionals in current situation.

By taking the
v_{x}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})
and
v_{c}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})
as above, we proceed towards the solution of resulting KS equation
following the Dirichlet boundary condition. This is done here by adopting an accurate and efficient GPS prescription,
providing a non-uniform, optimal spatial discretization. It is a simple but effective method giving excellent results on
numerous physically and chemically relevant problems, such as
singular
and non-singular
roy02
;
roy04
;
roy05
;
roy07
;
roy04a
;
roy04b
;
roy05a
;
roy05b
, Coulomb, Húlthen, Yukawa, logarithmic, spiked oscillator, Hellmann potential,
etc., along with its recent extension to quantum confinement
sen06
;
roy15
;
roy16
. Since the details are
well established and documented, we skip these here and refer the interested reader to the references above.

The numerical
p
-space wave function is obtained by Fourier transforming of
r
-space counterpart, as follows,

\displaystyle\xi(\mathrm{\mathbf{p}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})\ e^{i{\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}}.{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}}\mathrm{d}{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}.

(9)

Here,
\xi(\mathrm{\mathbf{p}})
needs to be normalized. The normalized
r
- and
p
-space densities are then expressed in the forms as

\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}|\phi_{i}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})|^{2}
and
\Pi(\mathrm{\mathbf{p}})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}|\xi_{i}(\mathrm{\mathbf{p}})|^{2}

respectively, where
n_{i}
indicates the occupation number of the
i
th orbital.

Next,
S_{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},~S_{\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}}
and Shannon entropy sum
S_{t}
are defined as given below,

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathcal{R}}^{3}}\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})\ \ln[\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})]\ \mathrm{d}\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\ \ \ S_{\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}}=-\int_{{\mathcal{R}}^{3}}\Pi(\mathrm{\mathbf{p}})\ \ln[\Pi(\mathrm{\mathbf{p}})]\ \mathrm{d}\mathrm{\mathbf{p}},

(10)

\displaystyle S_{t}

\displaystyle=\left[S_{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}+S_{\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}}\right]\geq 3(1+\ln\pi),\ \ \ \ \ \text{in 3 dimension}.

Here both
\rho(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})
and
\Pi(\mathrm{\mathbf{p}})
are normalized to unity.

All the computations are done numerically. The convergence is ensured by carrying out calculations with respect to variation
in grid parameters, such as total number of radial points and maximum range of grid. It is generally observed that convergence
is achieved relatively easily in the lower
r_{c}
region compared to the
r_{c}\rightarrow\infty
limit. All the quantities given

Future and Outlook

An appropriate and effective KS DFT method is presented for calculation of H
-
ion trapped inside an
impenetrable spherical cavity of varying radius. The proposed recipe is computationally achievable and can easily be
applied to other atoms in both ground and excited states. Energies are reported for ground and selected singly

Wigner correlation energies show qualitative similar behavior with the high-quality result of Hylleraas method.
The results are generally in good agreement with the available literature. X-only results are very close to HF. A detailed investigation
involving the singly excited states has been done to understand the rearrangement of atomic orbitals in strong confinement region.

supramolacular cavity). Investigation of multipole polarisability, atomic avoided crossing, hyperpolarisability,
influence of electric and magnetic field through dynamical study is highly desirable, some of which may be undertaken later.

Mock theta functions and indefinite modular forms III

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we study modular transformation properties of
a certain class of functions with indefinite quadratic forms.

Let us begin this section with recalling a very simple formula:

Note 2.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
\omega
be an

n

for a polynomial function

in

and a non-negative integer

1)

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}\dfrac{a(\omega^{-j})}{1-\omega^{j}x}\,\ =\,\ \dfrac{n\,a(x)}{1-x^{n}}

2)

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}\dfrac{\omega^{-jk}}{1-\omega^{j}x}\,=\,\dfrac{n\,x^{k}}{1-x^{n}}

Proof.

1)   Since
\dfrac{1}{1-\omega^{j}x}\,=\,\dfrac{1}{1-x^{n}}(1-x)(1-\omega x)\cdots(\widehat{1-\omega^{j}x})\cdots(1-\omega^{n-1}x)

\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{a(\omega^{-j})}{1-\omega^{j}x}\,=\,\frac{1}{1-x^{n}}\underbrace{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a(\omega^{-j})\,(1-x)(1-\omega x)\cdots(\widehat{1-\omega^{j}x})\cdots(1-\omega^{n-1}x)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hskip 17.07164pt||\,\ put\\[2.84526pt]
{\displaystyle f(x)}\end{subarray}}

where the notation
\widehat{1-\omega^{j}x}
with the hat on the top
indicates to remove this factor

.
Then
f
is a polynomial of degree less than
n
and satisfies the following:

f(\omega^{-j})\,\ =\,\ a(\omega^{-j})\underbrace{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n-1}(1-\omega^{\ell})}_{\begin{subarray}{c}||\\[2.84526pt]
{\displaystyle n}\end{subarray}}=\,\ n\,a(\omega^{-j})\quad{\rm for}\,\ j=0,\,\cdots\,,\,n-1

Hence
f(x)\,=\,n\,a(x)
, proving 1).
2) is obtained by applying 1) to

.

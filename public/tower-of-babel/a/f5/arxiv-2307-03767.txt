Conformal blocks on smoothings via mode transition algebras
Chiara Damiolini, Angela Gibney, Daniel Krashen

Abstract
. Here we introduce a series of associative algebras attached to a vertex operator algebra V V , called mode transition algebras, and show they reflect both algebraic properties of V V and geometric constructions on moduli of curves. Pointed and coordinatized curves, labeled by modules over V V , give rise to sheaves of coinvariants. We show that if the mode transition algebras admit multiplicative identities satisfying certain natural properties (called strong identity elements), then these sheaves deform as wanted on families of curves with nodes. This provides new contexts in which coherent sheaves of coinvariants form vector bundles. We also show that mode transition algebras carry information about higher level Zhu algebras and generalized Verma modules. To illustrate, we explicitly describe the higher level Zhu algebras of the Heisenberg vertex operator algebra, proving a conjecture of Addabbo–Barron.

Mode transition algebras, introduced here, are a series of associative algebras that give insight into algebraic structures on moduli of stable pointed curves, and representations of the vertex operator algebras from which they are derived.

Modules over vertex operator algebras (VOAs for short) give rise to vector bundles of coinvariants on moduli of smooth, pointed, coordinatized curves
[

]

5.2.6
one obtains a potentially rich source of vector bundles, including as in

3.4.6
(
b
), the well-known class given by rational and

-cofinite VOAs
[

,
BFM91
,
NT05
,
DGT22a
]

Definition 1.1.1
.

A

(b)

, called the

. The series

assigned to

,

satisfying the following axioms:

(a)

(vertex operators are fields)
for all

,

a_{(m)}b=0
, for

;

(b)

(vertex operators of the vacuum)

, that is

{\bm{1}}_{(-1)}=\mathrm{id}_{V}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad{\bm{1}}_{(m)}=0,\quad\mbox{for $m\neq-1$,}

(d)

(conformal structure)
for

\left[\omega_{(p+1)},\omega_{(q+1)}\right]=(p-q)\,\omega_{(p+q+1)}+\frac{c}{12}\,\delta_{p+q,0}\,(p^{3}-p)\,\textrm{id}_{V}.

(z_{1}-z_{2})^{N}\left[Y^{W}(a,z_{1}),Y^{W}(b,z_{2})\right]u=0;

(d)

(weak associativity)
for all
a\in V
and
u\in W
, there exists an
N\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for all
b\in V
, one has

(z_{1}+z_{2})^{N}\left(Y^{W}(Y(a,z_{1})b,z_{2})-Y^{W}(a,z_{1}+z_{2})Y^{W}(b,z_{2})\right)u=0;

(e)

(conformal structure)
for
Y^{W}(\omega,z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{(m)}^{W}z^{-m-1}
, one has

\left[\omega^{W}_{(p+1)},\omega^{W}_{(q+1)}\right]=(p-q)\,\omega^{W}_{(p+q+1)}+\frac{c}{12}\,\delta_{p+q,0}\,(p^{3}-p)\,\textrm{id}_{W}.

where
c\in\mathbb{C}
is the central charge of
V
. Moreover
Y^{W}\left(L_{-1}a,z\right)=\frac{d}{dz}Y^{W}(a,z)
;

(f)

(
\mathbb{N}
-gradability)

W
admits a grading
W=\bigoplus\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}W_{n}
with
a^{W}_{(m)}W_{n}\subset W_{n+\deg(a)-m-1}
.

As one can see in the literature, e.g. by
[
DL93
,
FHL93
,
LL04
,
Li96
]
, weak associativity and weak commutativity together are equivalent to the
Jacobi identity
: for
\ell
,
m
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
a
,
b\in V

\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}{\binom{\ell}{i}}\left(a^{W}_{(m+\ell-i)}b^{W}_{(n+i)}-(-1)^{\ell}b^{W}_{(n+\ell-i)}a^{W}_{(m+i)}\right)=\sum_{i\geq 0}{\binom{m}{i}}(a_{(\ell+i)}(b))^{W}_{(m+n-i)}.

Moreover, by
[
DLM97
, Lemma 2.2]
, axiom (
e
) is redundant.

2.
The universal enveloping algebra of a VOA

Here we describe constructions of the universal enveloping algebra associated to a VOA
V
, as quotients of certain graded, as well as (left and right) filtered completions of the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra associated to
V
. Filtered completions are essential to our constructions, as they are compatible with crucial restriction maps from the Chiral Lie algebra to certain ancillary Lie algebras, allowing for the definition of the action of the Chiral Lie algebra on (tensor products of)
V
-modules. The graded completion, on the other hand, allows both for ease in computation, and simpler descriptions of induced modules, and bimodules, and in
Section

3.2
of the mode transition algebras. While these concepts are treated in one way or another throughout the VOA literature, for instance in
[
FZ92
,
FBZ04
,
Fre07
,
NT05
,
MNT10
]
, we provide here and in
Appendix

A
, a uniform description, where many details are given, clarifications are made, and the different constructions are compared to one another. We further remark that, although this section assumes that
V
is a vertex operator algebra, however all the arguments and construction here in
Section

2
hold assuming only that
V
is a graded vertex algebra since the conformal structure does not play a role (see also
Section

9.3
).

2.1.
Graded and filtered completions

We recall the constructions of the universal enveloping algebra
[
FZ92
]
and the current algebra
[
NT05
]
associated to a VOA
V
.

2.2.
Split filtrations

The underlying vector spaces of the objects we will need to consider will either be graded or filtered (sometimes both), and these filtrations and gradings will be related to each other. The basic example of this is the space of Laurent polynomials
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]
which we choose to grade by
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]_{n}=\mathbb{C}t^{-n-1}
, and the space of Laurent series
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
, which admits an increasing filtration by setting
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)_{\leq n}:=t^{-n-1}\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
. We will refer to this filtration as a
left filtration
of
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
(see
A.1.1
). In this situation, when we have a graded subspace of a filtered space
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\subset\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
which identifies the degree
n
part of the graded subspace with the degree
n
part of the associated graded space, we say that this pair gives a
split filtration
(see
A.1.6
). Similarly, we refer to the filtration of
\mathbb{C}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
given by
\mathbb{C}(\!(t^{-1})\!)_{\geq n}=t^{-n-1}\mathbb{C}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
as a
right filtration
, and the pair
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\subset\mathbb{C}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
is also a split filtration.

2.3.
Lie algebras

Let
V
be a VOA (or a graded vertex algebra). Since it is a graded vector space, it admits a trivial left (respectively right) split-filtration, given by
V_{\leq n}=\bigoplus_{d\leq n}V_{d}
(respectively
V_{\geq n}=\bigoplus_{d\geq n}V_{d}
). In view of
A.3.3
, tensor products of split-filtered modules are naturally split-filtered, and consequently

V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\subset V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)\quad\text{ and }\quad V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\subset V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}(\!(t^{-1})\!)

define splittings of their left and right filtrations.

Remark 2.3.1
.

Concretely, we can define the map
\mathsf{val}\colon V\otimes\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)\to\mathbb{Z}
by

\mathsf{val}(a\otimes f(t))=\deg(a)-N-1.

for a homogeneous element
a\in V
and
f(t)\in t^{N}\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]\setminus t^{N-1}\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
. The natural left filtration on
V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
is then given by
(V\otimes\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!))_{\leq n}:=\mathsf{val}^{-1}(-\infty,n]
.

The linear map
\nabla=L_{-1}\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{id}\otimes\frac{d}{dt}
is a linear endomorphism of each of these spaces of degree
1
(see
A.1.10
). We define

{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{L}}=\dfrac{V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)}{\mathrm{Im}(\nabla)},\quad{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{R}}=\dfrac{V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}(\!(t^{-1})\!)}{\mathrm{Im}(\nabla)},\ \mbox{ and }\ {\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}=\dfrac{V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]}{\mathrm{Im}(\nabla)}.

These have induced split-filtrations via
{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}\subset{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{L}},{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{R}}
by
A.1.14
.
These filtered and graded vector spaces admit (filtered and graded) Lie algebra structures, with Lie brackets defined by:

\left[a\otimes f(t),b\otimes g(t)\right]:=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}\left(a_{(k)}(b)\right)\otimes g(t)\dfrac{d^{k}(f(t))}{dt^{k}},

for all
a,b\in V
and
f(t),g(t)\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
or
f(t),g(t)\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
.

More concretely in the case of
{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}
, for
a\in V
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
we denote by
a_{[i]}
the class of the element
a\otimes t^{i}
in
{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}\subset{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{L}},{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{R}}
.
The restriction of the Lie bracket on
{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}
then is given by the following formula

\left[a_{[i]},b_{[j]}\right]:=\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{i}{k}\left(a_{(k)}(b)\right)_{[i+j-k]},

for all
a,b\in V
and
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Extending the notation introduced in
[
DGT22a
]
, we call
{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{L}}
and
{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{R}}
the left and right
ancillary Lie algebras
and
{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}
the
finite ancillary Lie algebra
. Note that
{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{L}}
is isomorphic to the
current Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}(V)
from
[
NT05
]
.

2.4.
Enveloping algebras

We now let
\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}},\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}},\mathit{U}
be the universal enveloping algebras of
{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{L}},{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{R}},{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}
respectively. These enveloping algebras are left filtered, right filtered, and graded respectively, and we note that
\mathit{U}\subset\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}},\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}
again give splittings to the respective filtrations (see
A.3.6
). In the language of
A.9.1
we will say that
(\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}},\mathit{U},\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}})
forms a good triple of associative algebras.

Example 2.4.1
.

These induced filtrations can be explicitly described. For instance we have that
\mathit{U}_{d}
is linearly spanned by elements
\ell^{1}\cdots\ell^{k}
such that
\ell^{i}\in{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}_{d_{i}}
and
\sum_{i}d_{i}=d
(and
k
possibly zero if
d=0
). Analogously
(\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}})_{\leq d}
is linearly spanned by elements
\ell^{1}\cdots\ell^{k}
such that
\ell^{i}\in{\mathfrak{L}}(V)_{\leq d_{i}}
and
\sum_{i}d_{i}=d
(and
k
possibly zero if
d\geq 0
).

These enveloping algebras have an additional structure of a topology induced by seminorms, which can be described in terms of systems of neighborhoods of
0
(as in
A.4.1
and
Remark

A.4.4
). These neighborhoods of the identity are given by left ideals
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}
of
\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}
and
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}
of
\mathit{U}
and right ideals
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}
of
\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}
and
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n}\mathit{U}
of
\mathit{U}
defined by:

\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}=\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq-n},\quad\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}=\mathit{U}\mathit{U}_{\leq-n},\quad\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}=\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}},\quad\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n}\mathit{U}=\mathit{U}_{\geq n}\mathit{U}.

This definition coincides with that of a canonical seminorm on a (split-)filtered algebra, as described in
A.6.1
, and in particular gives a good seminorm on the triple (see
A.9.3
and
Remark

A.9.7
). Most useful for us is that the category of good triples of algebras with good seminorms is closed under quotients and completions (
A.9.9
and
A.9.10
).

These neighborhoods can also be described alternately as follows:

Lemma 2.4.2
.

One has an identification of left ideals
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}=\mathit{U}\mathit{U}_{\leq-n}=\mathit{U}{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}_{\leq-n}
. Similarly, one has
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}=\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq-n}
,
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}={\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}
and
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n}\mathit{U}={\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}_{\geq n}\mathit{U}
.

We note that in
2.4.2
, we identify
{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}_{\leq-n}
with its image in
\mathit{U}
.

Proof.

We will prove only the first equality, the others following by similar methods. Clearly
\mathit{U}\mathit{U}_{\leq-n}
contains
\mathit{U}{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}_{\leq-n}
, so we need only prove the reverse inclusion, which amounts to showing
\mathit{U}_{-n}\subset\mathit{U}{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}_{\leq-n}
. For this, suppose we have an element
\alpha=a^{1}_{n_{1}}a^{2}_{n_{2}}\cdots a^{r}_{n_{r}}\in\mathit{U}_{-n}
. By inductively using
[
He17
, Lemma A.2.1]
, for
M>>0
, we may write

\alpha=a^{1}_{n_{1}}a^{2}_{n_{2}}\cdots a^{r}_{n_{r}}=\beta+\gamma
,
with
\beta\in{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}_{-n}
and
\gamma\in\mathit{U}{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}_{\leq-M}
. In particular, choosing
M\geq n
, we find
\gamma\in\mathit{U}{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}_{\leq-n}
, showing
\alpha\in\mathit{U}{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}_{\leq-n}
as desired.
∎

We can restrict these seminorms to various filtered and graded parts of these algebras as in
A.4.6
. For example, we write
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq p}
to denote
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}(\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}})\cap\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq p}
. Concretely we obtain systems of neighborhoods as follows (see
A.3.4
for more details):

\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq p}=(\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq-n})_{\leq p}=\sum_{j\leq-n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq p-j}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq j},\qquad\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq p}=(\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}})_{\geq p}=\sum_{i\geq n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq i}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq p-i}

\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}_{p}=(\mathit{U}\mathit{U}_{\leq-n})_{p}=\sum_{j\leq-n}\mathit{U}_{p-j}\mathit{U}_{j},\ \mbox{ and }\qquad\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n}\mathit{U}_{p}=(\mathit{U}_{\geq n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}})_{p}=\sum_{i\geq n}\mathit{U}_{i}\mathit{U}_{p-i}.

We note that in particular, we have
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n+p}\mathit{U}_{p}=\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}_{p}
. Through the restriction of the seminorm to these subspaces, we then define a filtered completions of
\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}
and
\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}
, both containing a graded completion of
\mathit{U}
(see
A.5.6
).
Specifically, we set

\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{L}}_{d}:=\varprojlim_{n}\dfrac{\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq d}}{\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq d}},\qquad\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{R}}_{d}:=\varprojlim_{n}\dfrac{\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq d}}{\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq d}},\qquad\widehat{\mathit{U}}_{d}:=\varprojlim_{n}\dfrac{\mathit{U}_{d}}{\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathit{U}_{d}}=\varprojlim_{n}\dfrac{\mathit{U}_{d}}{\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n+d}\mathit{U}_{d}},

And set

\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{L}}:=\bigcup_{d}\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{L}}_{d},\qquad\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{R}}:=\bigcup_{d}\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{R}}_{d},\qquad\widehat{\mathit{U}}:=\bigoplus_{d}\widehat{\mathit{U}}_{d}.

As previously mentioned, it follows from
A.9.10
that this will result in a good triple
(\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{L}},\widehat{\mathit{U}},\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{R}})
of associative algebras with good seminorms.

Finally, one may construct a graded ideal
J
of
\widehat{\mathit{U}}
generated by the Jacobi relations, namely
J
is generated by for
\ell
,
m
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
a
,
b\in V
, by

\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}{\binom{\ell}{i}}\left(a_{[m+\ell-i]}b_{[n+i]}-(-1)^{\ell}b_{[n+\ell-i]}a_{[m+i]}\right)=\sum_{i\geq 0}{\binom{m}{i}}(a_{(\ell+i)}(b))_{[m+n-i]}.

If we let
J^{\mathsf{R}}
and
J^{\mathsf{L}}
be the ideals of
\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{R}}
and
\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{L}}
generated by
J
, and we let
\overline{J},\overline{J}^{\mathsf{R}},\overline{J}^{\mathsf{L}}
be the respective closures (see
A.5.10
), then we find that
(\overline{J}^{\mathsf{L}},\overline{J},\overline{J}^{\mathsf{R}})
form a good triple (of nonunital algebras)
by
A.9.5
and
A.9.6
. Finally, by
A.9.9
,
we find that the resulting quotient algebras

\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{L}}=\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{L}}/\overline{J}^{\mathsf{L}},\quad\mathscr{U}=\widehat{\mathit{U}}/\overline{J},\quad\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{R}}=\widehat{\mathit{U}}^{\mathsf{R}}/\overline{J}^{\mathsf{R}},

form a good triple of associative algebras with good seminorms (actually norms).

Definition 2.4.3
.

We call
\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{L}},\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{R}},\mathscr{U}
the left, right and finite universal enveloping algebras of
V
, respectively.

We note that a
V
-module corresponds, in this language, to a
\mathscr{U}
-module
W
(or a
\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{L}}
-module) such that the action of this normed (and hence topological) algebra is continuous. That is, such that the multiplication map

\mathscr{U}\times W\to W\quad\text{ or equivalently, }\quad\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{L}}\times W\to W

is continuous, where
W
is given the discrete topology, and
\mathscr{U}
and
\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{L}}
are topologized according to their norms.

2.4.4.
Relation to the literature

We note that
\mathscr{U}
coincides with the universal enveloping algebra of
V
introduced in
[
FZ92
]
, while we can identify
\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{L}}
with the current algebra introduced in
[
NT05
]
or with the universal enveloping algebra
\widetilde{U}(V)
introduced in
[
FBZ04
]
, with a minor modification (see
[
Fre07
, footnote on p.74]
).

2.5.
Subalgebras and subquotient algebras

We describe here some algebras built from the algebras
\mathscr{U},\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{L}},\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{R}}
which will play a special role. By definition,
\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq-n}\triangleleft\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq 0}
and
\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq n}\triangleleft\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq 0}
are two-sided ideals when
n>0
with

\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq 0}/\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{L}}_{\leq-1}\cong\mathscr{U}_{0}\cong\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq 0}/\mathscr{U}^{\mathsf{R}}_{\geq 1},

by the fact that these algebras are part of a good triple. We now look more closely at
\mathscr{U}_{0}
, which forms a subring of
\mathscr{U}
. As our triple of algebras is good, the seminorms on our algebras are almost canonical (
A.6.8
), and in particular by
A.6.8
(
c
), it follows that
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}\mathscr{U}_{0}=\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n}\mathscr{U}_{0}
for every
n
, so that there is no ambiguity in denoting these neighborhoods by
\operatorname{N}\!^{r}\mathscr{U}_{0}
. We also see in the same way that
\operatorname{N}\!^{r}\mathscr{U}_{0}
is a two-sided ideal of
\mathscr{U}_{0}
.

Definition 2.5.1
.

The
d
th higher level Zhu algebra
of
V
is the quotient

\mathsf{A}_{d}(V)=\mathscr{U}_{0}/\operatorname{N}\!^{d+1}\mathscr{U}_{0}.

For an element
\alpha\in\mathscr{U}_{0}
, we will write
[\alpha]_{d}
for its image in
\mathsf{A}_{d}(V)
. When
V
is understood, we will denote
\mathsf{A}_{d}(V)
simply by
\mathsf{A}_{d}
.

2.5.2.
Relation to the literature

In
[
Zhu96
]
the author defines an associative algebra, now referred to as the (zeroth) Zhu algebra as the quotient of
V
by an appropriate subspace
O(V)
. In
[
FZ92
,

]
it is shown that this algebra is isomorphic to an appropriate quotient of the degree zero piece of the universal enveloping algebra of

(or of the current algebra of

). As mentioned in the introduction, higher level Zhu algebras

have been introduced in
[

, and proved to be realized as quotients of the degree zero piece of the universal enveloping algebra of

and

is explicitly realized by identifying

in

3.1.

As is the convention, throughout we denote

For a left module

(3)

Remark 3.1.3
.

If
W_{0}

3.4.1.
The involutions, left and right actions

As we explain here, there is an anti-Lie algebra isomorphism

The map

(5)

a\otimes\sum_{i\geq N}c_{i}t^{i}\mapsto(-1)^{\deg(a)}\sum_{j\geq 0}^{\deg(a)}\left(\dfrac{1}{j!}\left(L_{1}^{j}(a)\right)\otimes\sum_{i\geq N}c_{i}t^{2\deg(a)-i-j-2}\right),

The map

a\mapsto(-1)^{\deg a}\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{1}{i!}L_{1}^{i}(a),

This notation, used in

Lemma 3.4.2

For

Proof.

This follows by combining (

Lemma 3.4.3

The map

One can check that this restricts to an endomorphism of

, that is,

, and

. Moreover, it is easy to verify that

Remark 3.4.6
.

Let

be a rational VOA. Then the mode transition algebras

have strong identity elements. To see this, we note that the rationality of

implies that
\mathsf{A}
is finite and semi-simple, and thus has a bimodule decomposition

\mathsf{A}\cong\prod_{k=1}^{m}I^{k}=\prod_{k=1}^{m}W_{0}^{k}\otimes(W_{0}^{k})^{\vee},

where

is the set indexing the isomorphism classes of simple

runs over all isomorphism classes of simple left

-modules (so that

is the corresponding dual right

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}=\Phi(\mathsf{A})=\Phi\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}W_{0}^{k}\otimes(W_{0}^{k})^{\vee}\right)=\prod_{k=1}^{m}\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(W_{0}^{k})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Phi^{\mathsf{R}}(W_{0}^{k})^{\vee}.

Note that since

is rational, the functor

takes simple

-modules to simple
V
-modules. This follows from the fact that

preserves indecomposable modules

a simple

-module,

must be simple as well. Similarly, we have that also

-module.
We give some details about this: the involution

-modules allowing us to consider

,

and

Lemma 4.3.3
.

For

any module over

, let

be elements whose images generate

Proof.

We induct on

For the induction step, suppose

and consider the

\overline{M}=M\otimes_{R_{k}}R_{k-1}
. By the induction hypothesis, the elements

generate
\overline{M}
. Therefore we can find

so that

maps to

\overline{M}
.

Now consider the submodule

. As

we find that

, and therefore we can write

for some

. Writing

, we find

for some

. But now we have

as desired.
∎

. Define

For simplicity, denote

We say that an induced admissible module

has the LCW property if
L_{0}
acts on
W_{0}
as a scalar with eigenvalue
c_{W}\in\mathbb{C}
, and there is no proper submodule
Z\subset W
with
c_{Z}-c_{W}>0
for every eigenvalue
c_{Z}
of
L_{0}
on
Z
. Suppose for contradiction that
V
admits a module
W=\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(W_{0})
, and
W
does not have the LCW property. We will show that there must be a
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
is not unital, contradicting our assumptions.

By hypothesis,
W
has a proper submodule
Z
with
c_{Z}-c_{W}>0
for every eigenvalue
c_{Z}
of
L_{0}
on
Z
. In particular,
Z
is not induced in degree zero over
\mathsf{A}
. Let
z_{d}
be any homogeneous element in
Z
of smallest degree
d>0
, so that
z_{d}\in W_{d}
.
By assumption
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
is unital, with unity
\mathfrak{u}_{d}=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes 1\otimes\beta_{i}
, where each
\alpha_{i}
has degree
d
and each
\beta_{i}
has degree
-d
.
The action of
\mathfrak{A}
on
W
restricts to an action of
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
on
W_{d}
, and since
\mathfrak{u}_{d}
is the unity of
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
we have

\mathfrak{A}_{d}\times W_{d}\longrightarrow W_{d},\qquad(\mathfrak{u}_{d},z_{d})\mapsto\mathfrak{u}_{d}\star z_{d}=z_{d}.

Unraveling the definition of
\star
and its associativity properties we have
\mathfrak{u}_{d}\star z_{d}=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\cdot(\beta_{i}\cdot z_{d})
. But now since the degree of
\beta_{i}\cdot z_{d}
is zero and
Z
is a submodule, we have that
\beta_{i}\cdot z_{d}\in Z\cap W_{0}=0
, since
Z
does not have a degree zero component. It then follows that
z_{d}=\mathfrak{u}_{d}\star z_{d}=0
, giving a contradiction since we assumed
z_{d}\neq 0
.
∎

Remark 6.0.2
.

Although the eigenvalues
c_{Z}
and
c_{W}
are in general complex numbers, the difference
c_{Z}-c_{W}
is always an integer, hence it makes sense to require that this number be positive. In fact, every eigenvalue of the action of
L_{0}
on
W
will be obtained by shifting
c_{W}
by a non-negative integer. The condition
c_{Z}-c_{W}>0
coincides then with
c_{Z}\neq c_{W}
. We remark that when
V
is
C_{2}
-cofinite, then the eigenvalues of
L_{0}
are necessarily rational numbers
[
Miy04
]
.

7.2.
Mode transition algebras for the Heisenberg vertex algebra

We can now state and prove the main result of this section.

Proposition 7.2.1
.

There is a natural identification

Proof.

Denote by

and for some

the element in
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
given by

From the explicit description of

given above, and the fact that the Zhu algebra

at level zero is Abelian, we have that the set whose elements are
\varepsilon_{\bm{r},{\bm{s}}}
freely generates
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
as an
\mathsf{A}
-module. Moreover, using a computation similar to
3.2.3
, one may show that

Ht^{s_{m}}\circ\cdots\circ Ht^{s_{1}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.7}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.7}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.7}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}Ht^{-r_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ Ht^{-r_{m}}=\begin{cases}||\bm{r}||&\text{if }{\bm{s}}={\bm{r}}\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where

is a non-zero, positive integer entirely depending on

By identifying

with the element of

in the

-entry, and zero otherwise, the above description gives an isomorphism of rings between

and

, as is claimed.∎

Example 7.2.2

8.

For

, by

we mean the (not necessarily simple) Virasoro VOA of central charge

8.1.
\mathrm{Vir}_{c}

By
[
Wan93
]
, when
c\neq c_{p,q}=1-\frac{6(p-q)^{2}}{pq}
, then
\mathrm{Vir}_{c}
is a simple VOA, but it is not rational or
C_{2}
-cofinite. When

, the VOA
\mathrm{Vir}_{c}
is not simple, but its simple quotient

will be rational and

-cofinite, and therefore satisfy smoothing. We therefore only consider
\mathrm{Vir}_{c}
, for any values of
c
, and not
L_{c}
.

Proposition 8.1.1
.

Let
\mathrm{Vir}_{c}
be the Virasoro VOA.

(a)

The first mode transition algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
is not unital, and so
\mathrm{Vir}_{c}
does not satisfy smoothing.

(b)

The kernel of the canonical projection
\mathsf{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})\to\mathsf{A}_{0}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
.

Proof.

We first prove
(a)
.
By
[
Wan93
, Lemma 4.1]
, one has
\mathsf{A}_{0}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})\cong\mathbb{C}[t]
, where the class of
(L_{-2}1)_{[1]}
is mapped to the generator
t
.

Here, as in Heisenberg case,
{\mathfrak{L}}(V)^{\mathsf{f}}_{\pm 1}
is a one dimensional vector space, with generators denoted
u_{\pm 1}
, so that
\mathfrak{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})=u_{1}\mathsf{A}_{0}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})u_{-1}
. We can choose
u_{1}=(L_{-2}1)_{[0]}
and
u_{-1}=(L_{-2}1)_{[2]}
, and to understand the multiplicative structure of
\mathfrak{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
we are only left to compute
[u_{-1},u_{1}]
. Since
L_{-2}1
is the conformal vector of
\mathrm{Vir}_{c}
, we can identify
(L_{-2}1)_{[n]}
with the element
\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
of the Virasoro algebra, and the bracket of
L(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
coincides with the bracket in the Virasoro algebra. Hence we obtain

[u_{-1},u_{1}]=[(L_{-2}1)_{[2]},(L_{-2}1)_{[0]}]=[\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{-1}]=2\mathcal{L}_{0}=2(L_{-2}1)_{[1]}.

We then have an identification of
\mathfrak{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
with
(\mathbb{C}[t],+,\star)
, where
+
denotes the usual sum of polynomials, while
f(t)\star g(t)=2tf(t)g(t)
. In particular, this implies that
\mathfrak{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
is not unital.

We now show
(b)
. By
[
Wan93
]
,
{\mathsf{A}}_{0}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
is generated by
L_{-2}\textbf{1}+O_{0}(V)
and
L_{-2}^{2}\textbf{1}+O_{0}(V)
so that

{\mathsf{A}}_{0}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})\cong\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(y-x^{2}-2x)\cong\mathbb{C}[x],

L_{-2}\textbf{1}+O_{0}(V)\mapsto x+(q_{0}(x,y)),\qquad L_{-2}^{2}\textbf{1}+O_{0}(V)\mapsto y+(q_{0}(x,y)),

where
q_{0}(x,y)=y-x^{2}-2x
. By
[
BVWY20
, Theorem 4.7]
,

\mathsf{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})

is generated by
L_{-2}\textbf{1}+O_{1}(V)
and
L_{-2}^{2}\textbf{1}+O_{1}(V)
, and by
[
BVWY20
, Theorem 4.11]
on has that

{\mathsf{A}}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})\cong\mathbb{C}[x,y]/((y-x^{2}-2x)(y-x^{2}-6x+4)),

L_{-2}\textbf{1}+O_{1}(V)\mapsto x+(q_{0}(x,y)q_{1}(x,y)),\qquad L_{-2}^{2}\textbf{1}+O_{1}(V)\mapsto y+(q_{0}(x,y)q_{1}(x,y)),

where
q_{0}(x,y)=y-x^{2}-2x
and
q_{1}(x,y)=y-x^{2}-6x+4
(see also
[
BVWY20
, §5]
). With the change of variables
X=y-x^{2}-6x+4
and
Y=y-x^{2}-2x
, one has

\mathsf{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})=\dfrac{\mathbb{C}[X,Y]}{XY}\qquad\text{ and}\qquad\mathsf{A}_{0}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})=\mathbb{C}[X],

so that the kernel of the projection
\mathsf{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})\to\mathsf{A}_{0}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
is identified with the ideal
K_{1}
generated by
Y
inside
\mathsf{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
. Since
XY=0
, the ideal
K_{1}
is isomorphic to
(Y\mathbb{C}[Y],+,\cdot)
. Furthermore, this algebra is isomorphic to the algebra
(\mathbb{C}[t],+,\star)
through the assignment
Yf(Y)\mapsto f(2t)
. This shows that, abstractly,
\mathfrak{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
is identified with the kernel of
\mathsf{A}_{1}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})\to\mathsf{A}_{0}(\mathrm{Vir}_{c})
.

We now see directly that this identification is provided by the natural map
\mu_{1}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{1}\to\mathsf{A}_{1}(V)
, which is induced by
(L_{-2}1)_{[0]}\otimes 1\otimes(L_{-2}1)_{[2]}\mapsto[(L_{-2}1)_{[0]}(L_{-2}1)_{[2]}]
as in

. To check that indeed

naturally identifies with the kernel of

\tilde{Y}-2(L_{-2}1)_{[0]}(L_{-2}1)_{[2]}\in N^{2}\mathscr{U}_{0},

where
\tilde{Y}
is any lift of
Y
to
\mathscr{U}_{0}

\tilde{Y}=(L_{-2}L_{-2}1)_{[3]}-(L_{-2}1)_{[1]}(L_{-2}1)_{[1]}-2(L_{-2}1)_{[1]}.

To simplify the notation, we will now write

to denote

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n\geq 2}\mathcal{L}_{-n}\mathcal{L}_{n}+\mathcal{L}_{-1}\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{1}\mathcal{L}_{-1}+\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathcal{L}_{0}-\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathcal{L}_{0}-2\mathcal{L}_{0}

9.1.2.
Triplet VOAs

Let

denote the triplet vertex operator algebra. There are

\mathsf{A}_{0}(X_{s}^{\pm})={X_{s}^{\pm}}/{I_{0}(X_{s}^{\pm})}
which are simple modules over Zhu algebra
\mathsf{A}=\mathsf{A}_{0}(\mathcal{W}(p))

\Omega(X_{s}^{\pm})=(X_{s}^{\pm})_{0}=\overline{X}_{s}^{\pm}

In particular, since
\overline{X}_{s}^{\pm}
is an
\mathsf{A}

. Moreover, using for instance
[
TW13

on the indecomposable modules

, i.e. the conformal weights, satisfy

. The induced module
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})
can be identified with a quotient of the projective cover of

, as follows. By
[
NT11
, Proposition 4.5]
(see also
[
TW13
]
) the projective cover

of

has socle filtration of length three consisting of submodules

and

Claim 9.1.3
.

\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})\cong P^{+}_{s}/X_{s}^{+}

Proof.

The
\mathsf{A}
-module
\overline{X}_{s}^{+}
is indecomposable, and as
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}
takes indecomposable modules to indecomposable modules (eg.
[
DGK22
]
), one has that
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})
is an indecomposable admissible
\mathcal{W}(p)
module. It follows that
\overline{X}_{s}^{+}
will be the weight space of least conformal weight in
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})
, and as
X_{s}^{+}
is generated by its lowest weight space
\overline{X}_{s}^{+}
, we get a canonical surjective map
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})\to X_{s}^{+}
. By projectivity, the map from the projective cover
P_{s}^{+}\to X_{s}^{+}
lifts to a map
P_{s}^{+}\to\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})
. As this map is surjective on the least weight space, the weight of
X_{s}^{+}
, and
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})
is generated by this subspace by construction, the map
P_{s}^{+}\to\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})
is surjective and so
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})
is a quotient of
P_{s}^{+}
.

The kernel of this quotient must contain the socle (which isomorphic to
X_{s}^{+}
), otherwise
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})
would have two composition factors isomorphic to
X_{s}^{+}
contradicting the size of its lowest weight space. The kernel cannot be larger, otherwise
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})
would admit a nontrivial extension by
X^{-}_{p-s}
(which as noted, has greater conformal weight than
X_{s}^{+}
). Note that if we had such an extension
0\to X^{-}_{p-s}\to E\to\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})\to 0
, the lowest weight spaces of
E
and
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{S}_{s}^{+})
would be isomorphic, producing a universal map
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\overline{X}_{s}^{+})\to E
whose composition with the map in the above sequence would be the universal map from

Appendix A
Split filtrations

This appendix contains a number of details about graded and filtered completions, and their relationships to one another. These serve to provide simple definitions of the building blocks of our constructions and uniform proofs of their properties.

A.1.
Filtrations

The purpose of this first section is to provide a framework in which we can simultaneously discuss and compare filtered and graded versions of certain constructions. In particular, this will give us a language appropriate for dealing simultaneously with both graded and filtered versions of the universal enveloping algebra of a vertex operator algebra which we recall in
2.4.3
.

Definition A.1.1

(Left and right filtrations)
.

Let
X
be an Abelian group. A left filtration on
X
is a sequence of subgroups
X_{\leq n}\subset X_{\leq n+1}\subset X
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Similarly, a right filtration on
X
is a sequence of subgroups
X_{\geq n}\subset X_{\geq n-1}\subset X
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark A.1.2
.

If
X
has a left filtration of subgroups
X_{\leq n}
we may produce a right filtration by setting
X_{\geq n}=X_{\leq-n}
. Hence the concepts of left and right filtrations are essentially equivalent. We will work in this section exclusively with left filtrations, but will have use for both left and right filtrations eventually. The reader should therefore keep in mind that the results in this section all have their right counterparts. If
X
is a graded Abelian group, we can naturally regard it as filtered by setting
X_{\leq n}=\bigoplus_{i\leq n}X_{i}
.

Notation A.1.3
.

If
X
is a filtered Abelian group and
S\subset X
is a subset, we write
S_{\leq n}
to mean
S\cap X_{\leq n}
.

We will now introduce some concepts which we will use throughout.

Definition A.1.4

(Exhaustive filtration)
.

Let
X
be a (left) filtered Abelian group. We say that the filtration on
X
is exhaustive if
\bigcup_{n}X_{\leq n}=X
and separated if
\bigcap_{n}X_{\leq n}=0
.

Definition A.1.5

(Splittings of filtrations)
.

Given a (left) filtered Abelian group
X
, we define the associated graded group to be
\operatorname{gr}X=\bigoplus_{n}\left(X_{\leq n}/X_{\leq n-1}\right)
.
A splitting of
X
is defined to be a graded subgroup
X^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{n}X^{\prime}_{n}\subset X
with
X^{\prime}_{n}\subset X_{\leq n}
such that for each
n
, the induced map
X^{\prime}_{n}\to(\operatorname{gr}X)_{n}
is an isomorphism.

Definition A.1.6

(Split-filtered Abelian groups)
.

A split-filtered Abelian group is a filtered Abelian group
(X,\leq)
together with a graded Abelian group
X^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{n}X^{\prime}_{n}
, and an inclusion
X^{\prime}\subset X
which defines a splitting as in
A.1.5
.

Notation A.1.7
.

For
X
a split-filtered Abelian group, and
x\in X_{\leq n}
, we write
x_{n}\in X_{n}^{\prime}
and
x_{<n}\in X_{\leq n-1}
for the unique elements such that
x=x_{n}+x_{<n}
.

Example A.1.8

(Concentrated split-filtrations)
.

If
X
is an Abelian group, with no extra structure, we may define a split filtered structure on it,
X[d]
, which we refer to as “concentrated in degree
d
,” by:

X[d]_{\leq p}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }p<d,\\
X&\text{if }p\geq d.\end{cases}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ and }\hskip 28.45274ptX[d]^{\prime}_{p}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }p\neq d,\\
X&\text{if }p=d.\end{cases}

If
X
is an Abelian group, with no extra structure, we may define the trivial split-filtration on
X
to be
X[0]
.

Example A.1.9
.

If
X=\bigoplus_{n}X_{n}
is a graded Abelian group, we may also consider it as a split Abelian group with respect to the filtration
X_{\leq n}=\bigoplus_{p\leq n}X_{p}
. In this case the inclusion of
X
into itself provides the splitting.

Definition A.1.10

(Split-filtered maps)
.

If
X
and
Y
are split-filtered Abelian groups and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, we say that a group homomorphism
f\colon Y\to X
is a map of degree
d
if
f(Y_{\leq p})\subset X_{\leq p+d}
and
f(Y^{\prime}_{p})\subset X^{\prime}_{p+d}
for all
p
.

Definition A.1.11

(Split-filtered subgroups)
.

If
X
and
Y
are split filtered Abelian groups with
Y\subset X
, we say
Y
is a split-filtered subgroup of
X
is the inclusion is a degree
0
map of split-filtered Abelian groups.

Lemma A.1.12
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a degree
d
homomorphism of split-filtered Abelian groups. Then
\ker f
is a split-filtered subgroup of
X
.

Proof.

We verify that
(\ker f)_{\leq p}=(\ker f^{\prime})_{p}+(\ker f)_{\leq p-1}
, where
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}_{p}\to Y^{\prime}_{p+d}
is the restriction of
f
. For this, we simply note that by definition,
f
induces a map
X^{\prime}_{p}\oplus X_{\leq p-1}\to Y^{\prime}_{p+d}\oplus Y_{\leq p+d-1}
which preserves the decomposition.
∎

The following lemma is straightforward to verify.

Lemma A.1.13
.

Suppose
f\colon Y\to X
is a degree
d
map of split-filtered Abelian groups. Then restricting the filtration on
X
to the image of
f
, we find
(\operatorname{im}f)_{\leq p}=\operatorname{im}\left(f|_{Y_{\leq p-d}}\right)
. Further,
\operatorname{im}f^{\prime}\subset\operatorname{im}f
defines a splitting, giving
\operatorname{im}f
the structure of a split-filtered Abelian group.

Lemma A.1.14
.

Suppose
f\colon Y\to X
is a degree
d
map of split-filtered Abelian groups. Then
\operatorname{coker}(f^{\prime})\subset\operatorname{coker}(f)
defines a splitting, giving
\operatorname{coker}(f)
the structure of a split-filtered Abelian group.

Proof.

Via
A.1.13
, we know that

\operatorname{im}(f^{\prime})\subset\operatorname{im}(f)
defines a split-filtered structure on
\operatorname{im}(f)
. As
\operatorname{coker}(f)=\operatorname{coker}\left(\operatorname{im}(f)\to X\right)
, it therefore suffices to consider the case where
f
is injective.
We have a diagram of (split) short exact sequences:

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y_{p}^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y_{\leq p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y_{\leq p-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{p}^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{\leq p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{\leq p-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0,}

where the vertical maps are injections. By the snake lemma, this gives a split short exact sequence of cokernels
(X/Y)_{\leq p}=(X^{\prime}/Y^{\prime})_{p}\oplus(X/Y)_{\leq p}
. In particular, the inclusion
X_{p}\to X_{\leq p}
induces an inclusion
(X^{\prime}/Y^{\prime})_{p}\subset(X/Y)_{\leq p}
giving our desired splitting.
∎

Proposition A.1.15
.

The category of split-filtered Abelian groups is an Abelian category which is cocomplete, i.e. closed under colimits.

Proof.

The fact that we have an Abelian category is a consequence of
A.1.14
,
A.1.13
,
A.1.12
. By
[
Wei94
, Prop. 2.6.8]
cocompleteness follows from being closed under direct sums, which can be checked by noticing that

\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X^{\lambda}
is
split-filtered with respect to the graded subgroup
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(X^{\lambda})^{\prime}
.
∎

A.2.
Modules and tensors

Definition A.2.1
.

Let
R
be a ring and
M
a left (or right)
R
-module. We say that
M
is a split-filtered
R
module if it is a split filtered Abelian group such that
M_{\leq n}
,
M^{\prime}
, and
M^{\prime}_{n}
are
R
-submodules of
M
for all
n
.

Lemma A.2.2
.

Let
R
be a ring. Suppose
M
is a split filtered right
R
-module and
N
a left
R
-module. Then the natural maps
M_{\leq p}\otimes_{R}N\to M\otimes_{R}N
and
M_{p}^{\prime}\otimes_{R}N\to M\otimes_{R}N
are injective.

Proof.

As
M\otimes_{R}N
is a directed limit of
M_{\leq i}\otimes_{R}N
taken over all
i
, it follows that an element
x\in M_{\leq p}\otimes_{R}N
maps to
0
in
M\otimes_{R}N
if and only if it maps to
0
in
M_{\leq i}\otimes_{R}N
for some
i>p
. Consequently, by induction, it suffices to show that the map
M_{\leq i}\otimes_{R}N\to M_{\leq i+1}\otimes_{R}N
is injective for all
i
. But note that (for any
i
, not just
i>p
):

M_{\leq i+1}\otimes_{R}N=(M_{\leq i}\oplus M_{i+1}^{\prime})\otimes_{R}N\cong(M_{\leq i}\otimes_{R}N)\oplus(M_{i+1}^{\prime}\otimes_{R}N),

from which we see that
M_{\leq i}\otimes_{R}N\to M_{\leq i+1}\otimes_{R}N
is in fact split injective and therefore
M_{\leq p}\otimes_{R}N\to M\otimes_{R}N
is injective.

But by this same reasoning, we see (taking
i+1=p
) that
M_{p}^{\prime}\otimes_{R}N\to M_{\leq p}\otimes_{R}N
is split injective. Since
M_{\leq p}\otimes_{R}N\to M\otimes_{R}N
is injective by the previous paragraph, we find that
M_{p}^{\prime}\otimes_{R}N\to M\otimes_{R}N
is injective as well.
∎

Definition/Lemma A.2.3
.

Let
R
be a ring,
M
a split-filtered right
R
-module and
N
a split-filtered left
R
-module. Then
M\otimes_{R}N
is naturally a split-filtered
R
-module by defining
(M\otimes_{R}N)_{\leq n}
to be
\sum\limits_{p+q=n}M_{\leq p}\otimes_{R}N_{\leq q}
and
(M\otimes_{R}N)^{\prime}_{n}
to be
\bigoplus\limits_{p+q=n}M_{p}^{\prime}\otimes_{R}N_{q}^{\prime}
.

Note that it follows from
A.2.2
that these are in fact submodules of
M\otimes_{R}N
.

Proof.

We have:

M_{\leq p}\otimes_{R}N_{\leq q}=(M^{\prime}_{p}\oplus M_{\leq p-1})\otimes_{R}(N^{\prime}_{q}\oplus N_{\leq q-1})\\
=(M^{\prime}_{p}\otimes_{R}N^{\prime}_{q})\oplus(M_{\leq p-1}\otimes_{R}N^{\prime}_{q})\oplus(M^{\prime}_{p}\otimes_{R}N_{\leq q-1})\oplus(M_{\leq p-1}\otimes_{R}N_{\leq q-1})\\
\subseteq(M^{\prime}\otimes_{R}N^{\prime})_{n}\oplus(M\otimes_{R}N)_{\leq n-1}.

This shows
(M\otimes_{R}N)_{\leq n}\subseteq(M^{\prime}\otimes_{R}N^{\prime})_{n}\oplus(M\otimes_{R}N)_{\leq n-1}
. The other inclusion is straightforward.
∎

A.3.
Rings and ideals

Definition A.3.1
.

If
U
is a filtered Abelian group with a (not necessarily associative, not necessarily unital) ring structure, we say that it is a filtered ring if
U_{\leq p}U_{\leq q}\subset U_{\leq p+q}
. If
U
is a filtered ring and we are given
U^{\prime}
a graded subring providing a splitting, we say
U
is a split-filtered ring.

Definition A.3.2
.

Let
U
be a split-filtered ring and
M
a left
U
-module, split-filtered as an Abelian group.
We say that
M
is a split-filtered
U
-module if we have
U_{\leq p}M_{\leq q}\subset M_{\leq p+q}
and
U_{p}^{\prime}M_{q}^{\prime}\subset M_{p+q}^{\prime}
.
Equivalently,
M
is a split filtered
U
-module if the multiplication map
U\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M\to M
is a split filtered map (where the split-filtration on
U\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M
is described in
A.2.3
).

Definition/Lemma A.3.3
.

Let
U
be a split-filtered ring.
M
a split-filtered right
U
-module and
N
a split-filtered left
U
-module.
Then
M\otimes_{U}N
is naturally a split-filtered
U
-module by defining
(M\otimes_{U}N)_{\leq n}
and
(M\otimes_{U}N)^{\prime}_{n}
to be the images in

M\otimes_{U}N
of
\bigoplus\limits_{p+q=n}M_{\leq p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N_{\leq q}
and

\bigoplus\limits_{p+q=n}M_{p}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N_{q}^{\prime}
respectively.

Proof.

Consider the map

f\colon M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}U\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N\to M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N

given by
f(x\otimes u\otimes y)=xu\otimes y-x\otimes uy
. By definition,
M\otimes_{U}N
is defined as the cokernel of this map. Regarding the domain and codomain as split-filtered via
A.2.3
, we see that this is a degree
0
map of split-filtered Abelian groups. So by
A.1.14
, the cokernel is split filtered.
∎

Lemma A.3.4
.

Let
U
be a split-filtered ring and let
S,T\subset U
be arbitrary split-filtered additive subgroups. Then
ST
is split filtered with
(ST)_{\leq n}=\sum_{p+q=n}S_{\leq p}T_{\leq q}
and
(ST)^{\prime}_{n}=\sum_{p+q=n}S_{p}^{\prime}T_{q}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

If we consider the tensor product
S\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}T
, with its split-filtered structure of
A.3.3
, we see that the multiplication map
S\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}T\to ST\subset U
is a degree
0
map of split-filtered groups. The result now follows from
A.1.13
.
∎

Lemma A.3.5
.

Let
U
be a split-filtered associative, unital ring, and let
X\subset U
be a split-filtered additive subgroup. Then the ideal generated by
X
in
U
is also split-filtered with homogeneous part the ideal of
U^{\prime}
generated by
X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It follows from
A.2.3
that
U\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}X\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}U
is split filtered with homogeneous part
U^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}X^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}U^{\prime}
. As the multiplication map
U\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}X\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}U\to U
is a map of degree
0
, it follows that its image, the ideal generated by
X
is split-filtered.
∎

Lemma A.3.6
.

Suppose
L
is a split-filtered Lie algebra over a commutative (associative and unital) ring
R
. Then the universal enveloping algebra
U(L)
is a split-filtered algebra with respect to the graded subalgebra
U(L^{\prime})\subset U(L)
.

Proof.

It follows from
A.3.3
and
A.1.15
that the tensor algebra
T(L)
is split-filtered with respect to
T(L^{\prime})
. Let

be the image of the map

defined by

(note the tensor in the preimage is over

and in the image is over

A.4.
Seminorms

The algebraic structures which naturally arise in studying the universal enveloping algebras of a VOA come with additional topological structure in the form of a seminorm. In this section we will examine seminorms and their interactions with gradings, filtrations and split-filtrations.

Definition A.4.1
.

A system of neighborhoods of
0
in an Abelian group
X
is a collection of subgroups
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X\subset X
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, with
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{n-1}X
and
\bigcup_{n}\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X=X
.

Definition A.4.2
.

A (non-Archimedean) seminorm on an Abelian group
X
is a function
X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
x\mapsto|x|
such that
|0|=0
and
|x+y|\leq\max\{|x|,|y|\}.

Definition A.4.3
.

A psuedometric on a set
X
is a function
d:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
d(x,x)=0
,
d(x,y)=d(y,x)
and
d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)
for all
x,y,z\in X
.

Remark A.4.4
.

The notion of a system of neighborhoods is equivalent to the notion of an Abelian group seminorm (we always assume these to be non-Archimedean), where we would set
|x|=e^{-n}
if
x\in\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X\setminus\operatorname{N}\!^{n+1}X
or
|x|=0
if
x\in\bigcap_{n}\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X
. Such a seminorm also gives rise to a pseudometric by setting
d(x,y)=|x-y|
. Finally, these give rise to a topology on
X
whose basis is given by open balls with respect to this pseudometric. We see that addition is continuous with respect to this topology.

With this remark in mind, we will refer to systems of neighborhoods of
0
and seminorms interchangeably, and will often refer to an Abelian group with a system of neighborhoods of
0
as a seminormed Abelian group.

Remark A.4.5
.

It follows from the definition that a system of neighborhoods (and hence a seminorm) is precisely the same as an exhaustive right filtration. We may therefore consider the seminorm associate to either a right or left exhaustive filtration (in view of
Remark

A.1.2
).

Definition A.4.6

(Restriction of seminorms)
.

If
X
is a seminormed Abelian group and
Y\subset X
is a subgroup, we will consider
Y
a seminormed Abelian group via the restriction of the seminorm. That is, we set
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}Y=\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X\cap Y
.

Definition A.4.7

(Seminormed rings and modules)
.

Let
U
be a ring which is seminormed as an Abelian group. We say that
U
is a seminormed ring if
|xy|\leq|x||y|
, or, equivalently,
(\operatorname{N}\!^{p}U)(\operatorname{N}\!^{q}U)\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{p+q}U
. If
M
is a left
U
-module which is seminormed as an Abelian group, we say that it is a seminormed left module if
|xm|\leq|x||m|
for all
x\in U
,
m\in M
.

Remark A.4.8
.

It follows immediately from
A.3.1
that a filtered ring (not necessarily associative or unital) becomes a seminormed ring with respect to the seminorm induced by the filtration as in
Remark

A.4.5
, and that multiplication map is continuous with respect to the induced topology.

Warning A.4.9
.

We will often consider seminorms on rings which are not ring seminorms, but just Abelian group seminorms.

Definition A.4.10

(Split-filtered seminorms)
.

Let
X
be an split-filtered Abelian group. We say that a seminorm is split-filtered if each of its neighborhoods
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X
are split-filtered subgroups of
X
. In this case we will simply refer to
X
as a split-filtered seminormed Abelian group.

The following notion captures a property that we will often seek: that smaller filtered parts of a given filtered Abelian group lie in progressively smaller neighborhoods.

Definition A.4.11

(Tight seminorms)
.

Suppose
X
is a filtered seminormed Abelian group. We say that the
X
is tightly seminormed if
for all
m,p
there exists
d
such that
X_{\leq-d}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{m}X_{\leq p}
.

Lemma A.4.12
.

Suppose
X
is a split-filtered seminormed Abelian group whose seminorm is tight. Then
X^{\prime}
is dense in
X
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
. We can choose
n,p
with
x\in\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p}\subset X
. For any
m
, we need to show that there exists
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
with
x-x^{\prime}\in\operatorname{N}\!^{m}X
. We can write
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p}=\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X^{\prime}_{p}\oplus\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p-1}
and iterating this expression, we find
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p}=\bigoplus_{i=p-d+1}^{p}\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X^{\prime}_{i}\oplus\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p-d}
for any
d>0
. But by the tightness of the seminorm, choosing
d>\!\!>0
we can ensure
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p-d}\subset X_{\leq p-d}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{m}X_{\leq p}
. In particular, we may write
x=x^{\prime}+y
with
x^{\prime}\in\bigoplus_{i=p-d+1}^{p}\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X^{\prime}_{i}\subset X^{\prime}
and
y\in\operatorname{N}\!^{m}X_{\leq p}
as desired.
∎

A.5.
Graded and filtered completions

Definition A.5.1

(Graded-complete and filtered-complete Abelian groups)
.

Let
X
be a normed Abelian group. If
X
is graded, we say that it is graded-complete if each of the graded subspaces
X_{n}
is complete. If
X
is filtered, we say that is is filtered-complete if each subspace
X_{\leq n}
is complete.

Definition A.5.2

(Short homomorphisms)
.

Let
X,Y
be seminormed Abelian groups. A group homomomorphism
f\colon X\to Y
is called a short (or metric) homomorphism if
|f(x)|\leq|x|
for all
x\in X
.

Definition/Lemma A.5.3

(Separated completions)
.

Let
X
be a seminormed Abelian group. Then we may form the (separated) completion
\widehat{X}
of
X
, which a complete normed Abelian group equipped with a short map
\iota\colon X\to\widehat{X}
which is universal for short maps to complete normed Abelian groups. That is, for every complete normed Abelian group
Y
and short homomorphism
X\to Y
, there is a unique factorization of this map as
X\overset{\iota}{\to}\widehat{X}\to Y
.

This can be constructed in the usual way via equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences. The following Lemma is a consequence of the fact that a metric space maps injectively into its completion:

Lemma A.5.4
.

Let
X
be a seminormed Abelian group. Then the canonical map
X\to\widehat{X}
has kernel
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X
. In particular,
X\to\widehat{X}
is injective exactly when the seminorm on
X
is actually a norm.

Lemma A.5.5
.

Let
W\subset Z\subset X
be subgroups of a seminormed Abelian group
X
. Then in the induced seminorm on
Z/W
, the separated completion of
Z/W
can be identified with
\widehat{Z}/\widehat{W}
, and
\widehat{Z}
,
\widehat{W}
can be identified with the closures of the images of
Z
and
W
in
\widehat{X}
respectively.

Proof.

The latter identification of completions and closures is straightforward to check. We note that there is a universal map
\widehat{Z}\to\widehat{\left(Z/W\right)}
of separated completions with
W
in the kernel. But as the image is Hausdorff, it follows that
\overline{W}
must also be in the kernel. But now we see that the map
Z/W\to\widehat{Z}/\widehat{W}
is therefore universal giving us
\widehat{Z}/\widehat{W}\cong\widehat{\left(Z/W\right)}
as desired.
∎

We have various closely related universal constructions as follows.

Definition/Lemma A.5.6

(Filtered and graded completions)
.

Let
X
be a seminormed Abelian group. If
X
is graded, then we can construct a short homomorphism
X\to{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{g}}}
which is universal for short homomorphisms to graded-complete Abelian groups. If
X
is filtered, then we can construct a short homomorphism
X\to{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}
which is universal for short homomorphisms to filtered-complete Abelian groups.

Proof.

We set
{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{g}}}=\bigoplus_{n}{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{n}
where
{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{n}=\widehat{X_{n}}
, and
{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}=\bigcup_{n}{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq n}
where
{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq n}=\widehat{X_{\leq n}}
.
∎

Remark A.5.7
.

These are also described in
[
MNT10
]
as the degreewise completion and the filterwise completion respectively.

Lemma A.5.8
.

For
X
a split-filtered tightly seminormed Abelian group,

{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}
is a split-filtered tightly seminormed Abelian group with respect to the graded subgroup
{{\widehat{X^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}
.

Proof.

Let us note that the natural morphism
{{\widehat{X^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}\to{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}
is an inclusion. For this, suppose that we have a pair of Cauchy sequences
(a_{n}),(b_{n})
in
X^{\prime}_{p}
which have the same image in
{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}
. Without loss of generality, we may select subsequences and re-index (possibly after modifying our starting index so an appropriate integer), and assume
a_{n}-b_{n}\in\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p}
for all
n
. But as
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X^{\prime}_{p}=\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p}\cap X^{\prime}_{p}
tells us that these Cauchy sequences have the same limit in
{{\widehat{X^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}
as well, giving injectivity.

Next we check that
{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p-1}\cap{{\widehat{X^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{p}=0
. Suppose we have an equality of classes of Cauchy sequences
(x_{n})=(y_{n})
where
x_{n}\in X_{\leq p-1}
,
y_{n}\in X^{\prime}_{p}
and
x_{n}-y_{n}\in\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p}
.
We claim that we may replace
(x_{n})
by an equivalent Cauchy sequence
(x_{n}^{\prime})
with
x_{n}^{\prime}\in X_{\leq p-d}
for all
d>0
.
To see this, we argue by induction on
d
. Suppose
x_{n}\in X_{\leq p-(d-1)}
we use the fact that our seminorm is split filtered to write
x_{n}=x^{\prime}_{n}+x^{\prime\prime}_{n}
with
x^{\prime}_{n}\in X_{\leq p-d}
and
x^{\prime\prime}_{n}\in X_{p-(d-1)}
. As
y_{n}-x_{n}=y_{n}-x^{\prime}_{n}-x^{\prime\prime}_{n}\in\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p}
and

\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p}=\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{p}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p-1}=\cdots=\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{p}\oplus\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{p-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{p-d+1}\oplus\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p-d},

we see that an element of
X_{\leq p}
lies in the
n
’th neighborhood
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p}
if and only if each of its factors with respect to the decomposition

X_{\leq p}=X_{p}\oplus X_{p-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus X_{p-d+1}\oplus X_{\leq p-d},

lies in their corresponding
n
’th neighborhood.
We therefore find
x^{\prime\prime}_{n}\in\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{p-d+1}
for all
n
. Consequently
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}x^{\prime\prime}_{n}=0
, which says the Cauchy sequences
(x_{n})
and
(x^{\prime}_{n})
are equivalent. This verifies our claim.

Now, we claim that
(y_{n})=0
. By definition of the completion, this amounts to
(y_{n})\in\operatorname{N}\!^{m}X_{\leq p-1}
for all
m
. By our hypothesis, for any
m
there exists
d^{\prime}
such that
X_{\leq-d^{\prime}}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{m}X_{\leq p-1}
. In particular, choosing
d=d^{\prime}+p
in the prior argument, we find that we may choose a Cauchy sequence
(y^{\prime}_{n})
equivalent to the first, with
y^{\prime}_{n}\in X_{\leq-d^{\prime}}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{m}X_{\leq p-1}
, showing that
(y^{\prime}_{n})\in\operatorname{N}\!^{m}{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p-1}
for all
m
, verifying our claim.

Now we check
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p-1}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{X^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{p}
. For this, let
\sum x_{i}\in\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}
be a convergent infinite series. Without loss of generality, we may assume
x_{i}\in\operatorname{N}\!^{n+i}X_{\leq p}
for all
i
. As our seminorm is split-filtered, we can write
x_{i}=(x_{i})_{p}+(x_{i})_{<p}
as in
A.1.7
. But now we see that the sums
\sum_{i}(x_{i})_{p}
and
\sum_{i}(x_{i})_{<p}
both converge in
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{X^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{p}

and
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p-1}
respectively, showing that

\sum x_{i}\in\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p-1}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{X^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{p}
as desired.

As
{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}=\bigcup_{n}\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}
and

{{\widehat{X^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}=\bigcup_{n}\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{X^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}

we conclude that
X
is split-filtered seminormed by
A.1.15
.

To check that it is tightly seminormed, We notice that whenever
X_{\leq-d}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{m}X_{\leq p}
we find that, upon taking closures in
{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}
, that

{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq-d}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{m}{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}
, showing that
{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}
is also tightly seminormed (with the same choice of
d
for a given
m,p
).
∎

Remark A.5.9
.

In light of this result, it makes sense to refer to
{{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}
as the completion of
X
, when
X
is split-filtered, with the understanding that the graded subgroup is given by
{{\widehat{X^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}
. In the case
X={{\widehat{X}}^{\textsf{f}}}
, we say
X
is complete.

Definition/Lemma A.5.10
.

Suppose
X
is a split-filtered, tightly seminormed, complete Abelian group, and suppose
Y\subset X
is a split-filtered subgroup. Define the closure
\overline{Y}
of
Y
in
X
to be the filtered subgroup with
\overline{Y}_{\leq p}
the closure of the image of
Y_{\leq p}
in
X_{\leq p}
and
\overline{Y^{\prime}}_{p}
the closure of the image of
Y^{\prime}_{p}
in
X^{\prime}_{p}
. Then
\overline{Y}
is split-filtered with respect to the graded subgroup
\overline{Y}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It follows from the definition that the restriction of a tight seminorm is again a tight seminorm. As we can identify the closures with the completions by
A.5.5
, the result follows from
A.5.8
.
∎

A.6.
Canonical seminorms

The seminorms used in studying universal enveloping algebras of VOAs arise in a very specific way, as described in
[
TUY89
,
FZ92
,
FBZ04
,
Fre07
,
NT05
,
MNT10
]
. We will recall an generalized definition of these seminorms, as in
[
MNT10
]
, and then examine some abstract features in the context of split-filtrations, which will allow us to relate the filtered and graded versions.

Definition A.6.1

(The canonical seminorm)
.

Let
U
be a filtered ring. The canonical system of neighborhoods on
U
is defined by
\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U=UU_{\leq-n}
(a left ideal of
U
if
U
is associative). We will write
{}^{c}|\cdot|
for the corresponding canonical seminorm.

Lemma A.6.2
.

Suppose
U^{\prime}\subset U
is a split-filtered ring. Then the canonical seminorm is split-filtered and tight.

Proof.

Suppose
u\in\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}
.
By
A.3.4
, we can write
u
as a sum of elements of the form

\alpha\beta
with
\alpha\in U_{\leq a}
and
\beta\in U_{\leq b}
with

a+b=p
and
b\leq-n
.

Using our splitting we may write
\alpha=\overline{\alpha}+\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta=\overline{\beta}+\beta^{\prime}
with
\overline{\alpha}\in U^{\prime}_{a}
,
\alpha^{\prime}\in U_{\leq a-1}
,
\overline{\beta}\in U^{\prime}_{b}
,
\beta^{\prime}\in U_{\leq b-1}
, and so we have
\alpha^{\prime}\overline{\beta}\in\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p-1}
giving us:

\alpha\beta=\overline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}+\overline{\alpha}\beta^{\prime}+\alpha^{\prime}\overline{\beta}+\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}\in\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{p}+\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p-1}+\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n+1}U_{\leq p-1}=\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{p}+\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p-1}.

It follows that
\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}\subset\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p-1}+\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{p}
, and hence
\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}=\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p-1}+\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{p}
, showing the canonical seminorm is split filtered.

To check that it is tight, we simply notice that for any
m,p
, we have for
d\geq\max\{m,-p\}
,
U_{\leq-d}\subset U_{\leq p}\cap\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{m}U=\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{m}U_{\leq p}
.
∎

The following Lemmas are easily verified.

Lemma A.6.3
.

Let
U
be a filtered associative ring. Then for any
p,q,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\left(\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}\right)U_{\leq q}\subset\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n-q}U_{\leq p+q}\quad\text{ and }\quad U_{\leq p}\left(\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq q}\right)\subset\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p+q}.

Lemma A.6.4
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a surjective filtered homomorphism of filtered associative rings. Then
f(\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X)=\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}Y
.

By
A.6.5
, a useful property of the canonical topology is that multiplication is continuous with respect to it, at least when restricted to the various filtered parts.

Lemma A.6.5
.

Let
U
be a filtered associative ring equipped with a seminorm such that

\left(\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}\right)U_{\leq q}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{n-q}U_{\leq p+q}\quad\text{ and }\quad U_{\leq p}\left(\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq q}\right)\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p+q}.

Then for any
p,q
, the multiplication map
U_{\leq p}\times U_{\leq q}\to U_{\leq p+q}
is continuous with respect to the seminorm in both variables. Consequently, the completion
{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}
naturally has the structure of an associative ring.

Remark A.6.6
.

It follows that under these hypotheses, if
U
and its seminorm is split-filtered, then the multiplication map
U^{\prime}_{p}\times U^{\prime}_{q}\to U^{\prime}_{p+q}
is also continuous (being the restriction of a continuous map). Consequently, in this case, the completion
{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}
is also a split-filtered associative ring, which is tightly split-filtered if
U
is (by
A.5.8
).

Proof.

Let
u_{1}\in U_{\leq p},u_{2}\in U_{\leq q}
.
Then we must show that multiplication is continuous with respect to both variables at
(u_{1},u_{2})
. That is, given
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, we must show there exist
n_{1},n_{2}
such that
(u_{1}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n_{1}}U_{\leq p})u_{2}\subset u_{1}u_{2}+\operatorname{N}\!^{d}U_{\leq p+q}
and
u_{1}(u_{2}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n_{2}}U_{\leq q})\subset u_{1}u_{2}+\operatorname{N}\!^{d}U_{\leq p+q}
.
By our hypotheses, for
n_{2}\geq d
, we have
u_{1}(u_{2}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n_{2}}U_{\leq q})\subset u_{1}u_{2}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n_{2}}U_{\leq p+q}\subset u_{1}u_{2}+\operatorname{N}\!^{d}U_{\leq p+q}
.
On the other hand, for
n_{1}\geq q+d
, we find

(\operatorname{N}\!^{n_{1}}U_{\leq p})u_{2}\subset\left(\operatorname{N}\!^{n_{1}}U_{\leq p}\right)U_{\leq q}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{n_{1}-q}U_{\leq p+q}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{d}U_{\leq p+q},

as desired.
∎

Remark A.6.7
.

If
U
is a split-filtered seminormed ring with
\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}
then by
A.6.2
, it is tightly seminormed.

The canonical seminorm on a split-filtered associative ring has a number of useful properties which we would like to axiomatize. As we have seen, it is tight and split-filtered (
A.6.2
) and verifies the identities of
A.6.3
.

Definition A.6.8
.

Let
U
be a split-filtered seminormed associative ring. We say the seminorm is almost canonical if it verifies the following conditions:

(a)

the seminorm is split filtered,

(b)

\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}=\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n+1}U_{\leq p}
for all
n,p
,

(c)

\left(\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}\right)U_{\leq q}\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{n-q}U_{\leq p+q}
and
U_{\leq p}\left(\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq q}\right)\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p+q}
for all
p,q,n
.

Lemma A.6.9
.

Let
U
be a split-filtered almost canonically seminormed associative ring. Then

\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{p}=\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{p}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n+1}U^{\prime}_{p}
for all
n,p
.

Proof.

Using the fact that the seminorm is split filtered and
A.6.8
(
b
), we have

\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{p}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p-1}=\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{p}+\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p-1}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n+1}U^{\prime}_{p}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n+1}U_{\leq p-1}

from which the result follows looking modulo
U_{\leq p-1}
.
∎

Lemma A.6.10
.

Let
U
be a split-filtered seminormed associative ring. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}=\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n+1}U_{\leq p}
for all
n,p
,

(b)

\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}=\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}+\operatorname{N}\!^{n+d}U_{\leq p}
for all
n,p
and
d>0
,

(c)

\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}
is contained in and dense in
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}
.

Proof.

This follows by iterating the expression in part (
a
). ∎

Remark A.6.11
.

In particular, if
U
carries an almost canonical seminorm, then by
A.6.8
(
b
), it satisfies the equivalent conditions of
A.6.10
, and from
A.6.10
(
c
), it follows from
Remark

A.6.7
that its seminorm is also tight.

Lemma A.6.12
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a surjective map of split-filtered associative rings, and suppose
X
is endowed with an almost canonical seminorm. Then the system of neighborhoods
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}Y_{\leq p}=f(\operatorname{N}\!^{n}X_{\leq p})
defines an almost canonical seminorm on
Y
.

Proof.

By
A.6.4
the image of the canonical neighborhoods in
X
are canonical neighborhoods in
Y
, and by definition the images of neighborhoods in
X
are neighborhoods in
Y
. The result then follows directly by applying the homomorphism
f
to the properties of
A.6.8
(
b
) and (
c
).
∎

Lemma A.6.13
.

Suppose
U
is a split-filtered associative ring with an almost canonical seminorm. Then the induced norm on the filtered completion
{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}
is also almost canonical.

Proof.

By
Remark

A.6.7
and
A.5.8
,
{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}
is split-filtered and tightly seminormed, implying that
{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}
satisfies
A.6.8
(
a
). We proceed to
A.6.8
(
b
) using the equivalent conditions of
A.6.10
.
As the neighborhoods
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}
can be identified as the closure of the image of
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}
and
\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}
is dense in
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}
, it follows that the image of
\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}
is dense in
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}
. But as the image of
\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}
is contained in
\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}
, it follows that
\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}
is also dense in
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}
, verifying
A.6.8
(
b
).

We verify the first part of
A.6.8
(
c
) (the second part is analogous). The multiplication map
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}\times U_{\leq q}\to U_{\leq p+q}
is continuous by
A.6.5
and it factors through
\operatorname{N}\!^{n-q}U_{\leq p+q}
. By continuity, taking closures (of the images) in the completions
{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p},{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq q},{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p+q}
of
U_{\leq p},U_{\leq q},U_{\leq p+q}
respectively, we find that our map extends to a continuous map
\overline{\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}}\times\overline{U_{\leq q}}\to\overline{U_{\leq p+q}}
which factors through
\overline{\operatorname{N}\!^{n-q}U_{\leq p+q}}
. Since the closure of the image in a completion can be identified with the completion itself, and
\overline{\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}}=\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}
,
\overline{\operatorname{N}\!^{n-q}U_{\leq p+q}}=\operatorname{N}\!^{n-q}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p+q}
, we interpret our multiplication as a continuous map
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}\times{{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq q}}\to{{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p+q}}
which factors through
\operatorname{N}\!^{n-q}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p+q}
, as desired.
∎

A.7.
Completed tensors

Completed tensors, introduced here in
A.7.2
, make a number of arguments more natural.

Definition A.7.1

(Seminorm on tensors)
.

Let
R
be a seminormed ring,
M
a right seminormed
R
-module and
N
a left seminormed
R
-module. We define a seminorm on
M\otimes_{R}N
by the following neighborhoods of
0
:

\operatorname{N}\!^{n}(M\otimes_{R}N)=\sum_{p+q=n}\operatorname{im}\big((\operatorname{N}\!^{p}M\otimes_{\operatorname{N}\!^{0}R}\operatorname{N}\!^{q}N)\to M\otimes_{R}N\big).

Definition A.7.2
.

(Complete tensors)
Let
R
be a seminormed ring,
M
a right seminormed
R
-module, and
N
a left seminormed
R
-module. The complete tensor product
M\widehat{\otimes}_{R}N
is defined to be the completion of the seminormed Abelian group
M\otimes_{R}N
with seminorm as described in
A.7.1
.

Definition/Lemma A.7.3

(Complete tensors, filtered and graded)
.

Let
R
be a seminormed ring,
M
a right seminormed
R
-module and
N
a left seminormed
R
-module. If
R,M,N
are graded then we can construct a short homomorphism
M\times N\to M{{\widehat{\otimes}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{R}N
which is universal for
R
-bilinear maps to graded-complete Abelian groups. If
R,M,N
are filtered then we can construct a short homomorphism
M\times N\to M{{\widehat{\otimes}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{R}N
which is universal for
R
-bilinear maps to filtered-complete Abelian groups.

Proof.

These are
M{{\widehat{\otimes}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{R}N={{\widehat{M\otimes_{R}N}}^{\textsf{g}}}
and
M{{\widehat{\otimes}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{R}N={{\widehat{M\otimes_{R}N}}^{\textsf{f}}}
respectively.
∎

A.8.
Discrete quotients

This section will be particularly useful in construction of generalized Verma modules and new algebraic structures (the mode transition algebras of
Section

3.2
) which will play an important role for us.

If
U
is a filtered ring, then
U_{\leq 0}
is always a subring and
U_{\leq-n}
for
n>0
is a two-sided ideal of
U_{\leq 0}
. Moreover, for
n>0
, we have

U\otimes_{U_{\leq 0}}U_{\leq 0}/U_{\leq-n}=U/UU_{\leq-n}=U/\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U
.

Lemma A.8.1
.

Suppose
U
is a split-filtered almost canonically seminormed ring.
Then

U{{\widehat{\otimes}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{U_{\leq 0}}U_{\leq 0}/U_{\leq-n}\cong{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}/[\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}\cong{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}\cong U^{\prime}{{\widehat{\otimes}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{U^{\prime}_{\leq 0}}U^{\prime}_{\leq 0}/U^{\prime}_{\leq-n}

with isomorphism induced by the continuous map

U\otimes_{U_{\leq 0}}U_{\leq 0}/U_{\leq-n}\to U/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U
via
u\otimes\overline{a}\mapsto\overline{ua}
.

In particular, as a topological space, these have the discrete topology.

Proof.

It is immediate that, assuming the claimed equalities hold, the natural quotient topology on
{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}
is discrete.

As we have noticed,
U\otimes_{U_{\leq 0}}U_{\leq 0}/U_{\leq-n}\cong U/\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U
. We can therefore identify the separated completion of
U_{\leq p}\otimes_{U_{\leq 0}}U_{\leq 0}/U_{\leq-n}
with the separated completion of
U_{\leq p}/\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}
. But since
\overline{\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}}=\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U_{\leq p}
by
A.6.10
, the isomorphism
U{{\widehat{\otimes}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{U_{\leq 0}}U_{\leq 0}/U_{\leq-n}\cong{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}
follows by
A.5.5
.

Next, we note that the natural map
{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}\to{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}
has kernel
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}
. As
U^{\prime}_{\leq-m}\subset\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U\subset\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U
for
m\gg 0
it follows that our map
U^{\prime}\to{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}
, which has dense image by
A.4.12
factors through the surjection
U^{\prime}/U^{\prime}_{\leq-m}\to U^{\prime}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}
.
In particular, the restriction of this map to
U^{\prime}_{\leq p}/U^{\prime}_{\leq-m}
factors through
\widehat{U^{\prime}_{\leq p}/U^{\prime}_{\leq-m}}
and hence the image of this part coincides with the image of
{{\widehat{U^{\prime}_{\leq p}}}^{\textsf{g}}}
. But
\widehat{U^{\prime}_{\leq p}/U^{\prime}_{\leq-m}}=\bigoplus\limits_{-m<i\leq p}\widehat{U}^{\prime}_{i}=\bigoplus\limits_{-m<i\leq p}{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{i}
. We therefore find that the map
{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{\leq p}\to{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}
factors through
\bigoplus\limits_{-m<i\leq p}{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{i}
which is a complete space. As this map has dense image, it is surjective and from our prior description of the kernel, we see
{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{\leq p}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{\leq p}\cong{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}_{\leq p}
. Taking a union over all

gives the identification
{{\widehat{U}}^{\textsf{f}}}\cong{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}
.

Making the same observations as in the beginning of the proof with
U^{\prime}
instead of
U
, we may identify the separated completion of
U^{\prime}_{\leq p}\otimes_{U^{\prime}_{\leq 0}}U^{\prime}_{\leq 0}/U^{\prime}_{\leq-n}
with the separated completion of
U^{\prime}_{\leq p}/\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{\leq p}
. Choosing
m
as in the previous paragraph, we find that we have a surjective map
U^{\prime}/U^{\prime}_{\leq-m}\to U^{\prime}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}
which allows us to identify the separated completion of
U^{\prime}_{\leq p}/\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{\leq p}
with
{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{\leq p}/\operatorname{N}\!^{n}{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}}_{\leq p}
as desired.
∎

A.9.
Triples of associative algebras

In this section we collect some of our previous facts which will be useful for the construction of our universal enveloping algebras of a VOA. As we simultaneously construct and relate three versions of the enveloping algebra (left, right and finite
2.4.3
), we will therefore introduce notions for working with triples of associative algebras here.

Definition A.9.1
.

A good triple of associative algebras
(U^{\mathsf{L}},U^{\prime},U^{\mathsf{R}})
consists of the data of a left split-filtered associative algebra
U^{\mathsf{L}}
, a right split-filtered associative algebra
U^{\mathsf{R}}
, and a graded subalgebra
U^{\prime}
of both
U^{\mathsf{L}}
and
U^{\mathsf{R}}
.

Definition A.9.2
.

A morphism of good triples
(X^{\mathsf{L}},X^{\prime},X^{\mathsf{R}})\to(Y^{\mathsf{L}},Y^{\prime},Y^{\mathsf{R}})
is a pair of degree
0
maps of split-filtered associative algebras
X^{\mathsf{L}}\to Y^{\mathsf{L}}
and
X^{\mathsf{R}}\to Y^{\mathsf{R}}
which agree on
X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
.

Definition A.9.3
.

A good seminorm on a good triple of associative algebras
(U^{\mathsf{L}},U^{\prime},U^{\mathsf{R}})
consists of almost canonical split-filtered seminorms on
U^{\mathsf{L}}
and
U^{\mathsf{R}}
defined by neighborhoods
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}U^{\mathsf{L}}
and
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n}U^{\mathsf{R}}
respectively such that
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}U^{\prime}_{p}=\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n+p}U^{\prime}_{p}
.

Remark A.9.4
.

We note that in the case
p=0
we have
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{n}U^{\prime}_{0}=\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{n}U^{\prime}_{0}
, and in this case we can unambiguously write
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{0}
for each. Also in this case, it follows from
A.6.8
(
c
) that
\operatorname{N}\!^{n}U^{\prime}_{0}
is a two sided ideal of
U^{\prime}_{0}
.

Lemma A.9.5
.

Suppose
(U^{\mathsf{L}},U^{\prime},U^{\mathsf{R}})
is a good triple of associative unital algebras and
I\triangleleft U^{\prime}
is a homogeneous ideal. Let
I^{\mathsf{L}}=U^{\mathsf{L}}IU^{\mathsf{L}}
and
I^{\mathsf{R}}=U^{\mathsf{R}}IU^{\mathsf{R}}
be the ideals of
U^{\mathsf{L}}
and
U^{\mathsf{R}}
generated by
I
. Then
(I^{\mathsf{L}},I,I^{\mathsf{R}})
is a good triple (of ideals).

Proof.

We note that the triple
(I,I,I)
is good, where we regard
I
itself as left and right filtered as in
A.1.9
. The result now follows from
A.3.5
, in light of the observation that the ideal generated by
I
in
U^{\prime}
is
I
itself.
∎

The following Lemma is an immediate consequence of
A.5.10
(note that good seminorms give rise to almost canonical ones which are tight by
Remark

A.6.11
).

Lemma A.9.6
.

Let
(U^{\mathsf{L}},U^{\prime},U^{\mathsf{R}})
be a good triple of associative unital algebras and
(I^{\mathsf{L}},I,I^{\mathsf{R}})
a good triple of ideals. Then the closures
(\overline{I}^{\mathsf{L}},\overline{I},\overline{I}^{\mathsf{R}})
is a good triple of ideals.

Remark A.9.7
.

If our seminorms on a triple
(U^{\mathsf{L}},U^{\prime},U^{\mathsf{R}})
are canonical, they are easily verified to be good: it is split-filtered by
A.6.2
and satisfies the other conditions of
A.6.8
by definition of the canonical seminorm and by
A.6.3
.

Definition A.9.8
.

If
(U^{\mathsf{L}},U^{\prime},U^{\mathsf{R}})
is a good triple with a good seminorm, it’s completion is the triple
\left({{\widehat{U^{\mathsf{L}}}}^{\textsf{f}}},{{\widehat{U^{\prime}}}^{\textsf{g}}},{{\widehat{U^{\mathsf{R}}}}^{\textsf{f}}}\right)
. We say that triple is complete if these are the same seminormed triple under the canonical map.

The following two results show that, in the appropriate sense, the class of good triples with good seminorms are closed under completions and homomorphic images.

Corollary A.9.9
.

If
(X^{\mathsf{L}},X^{\prime},X^{\mathsf{R}})\to(Y^{\mathsf{L}},Y^{\prime},Y^{\mathsf{R}})
is a surjective map of good triples, and
(X^{\mathsf{L}},X^{\prime},X^{\mathsf{R}})
has a good seminorm, the induced seminorm on
(Y^{\mathsf{L}},Y^{\prime},Y^{\mathsf{R}})
is good.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of
A.1.14
and
A.6.12
. ∎

Corollary A.9.10
.

Good triples with good seminorms are closed under the operation of completion.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of
A.6.13
. ∎

Definition B.2.6
.

For a graded algebra with almost canonical seminorm
U
, we define
\mathfrak{A}(U)=\Phi(\mathsf{A}_{0}(U))
to be the
(generalized) mode transition algebra
, and we write
\mathfrak{A}(U)_{d}
for the
d
th mode transition subalgebra
\mathfrak{A}(U)_{d,-d}
.

In this case, it turns out that the action of
\mathfrak{A}(U)
extends to much more general modules than simply the Verma-type modules, as we now observe.

Lemma B.2.7
.

Suppose
U
is a graded algebra with a good seminorm, and let
W
be an
\mathbb{N}
-graded
U
-module endowed with the discrete topology, such that the action of
U\times W\to W
is continuous. Then for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{m+1}U_{m-d}W_{d}=0=\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{d+1}U_{m-d}W_{d}
.

Proof.

As a good seminorm is almost canonical, we may iteratively apply
A.6.9
, to see that
{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{m+1}U_{m-d}
is dense in
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{m+1}U_{m-d}
. Consequently, if
\alpha\in\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{m+1}U_{m-d}
and
w\in W_{d}
, then by continuity, we can find
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{m+1}U_{m-d}
such that
\alpha^{\prime}w=\alpha w
. But therefore
\alpha^{\prime}w\in UU_{-d-1}w=0
.
As the seminorm is good, we find
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{d+1}U_{m-d}=\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{d+1}U_{m-d}
, giving the final claim.
∎

Lemma B.2.8
.

Suppose
U
is a graded algebra with a good seminorm, and let
W
be an
\mathbb{N}
-graded
U
-module endowed with the discrete topology, such that the action of
U\times W\to W
is continuous. Then the left action of
U
on
W
induces well-defined maps for every
p
:

\displaystyle(U_{p}/\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{p+d+1}U_{p})\times W_{d}

\displaystyle\to W_{p+d},

\displaystyle([u],w)

\displaystyle\mapsto uw,

which we will simply write as
[u]w
.

Proof.

This follows from
B.2.7
, which shows
(\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{p+d+1}U_{p})W_{d}=0
.
∎

Proposition B.2.9
.

Let
U
be a graded algebra with an almost canonical seminorm, and let
W
be an
\mathbb{N}
-graded
U
-module endowed with the discrete topology, such that the action of
U\times W\to W
is continuous. Then we obtain an action of
\mathfrak{A}(U)
on
W

such that
\mathfrak{A}(U)_{d_{1},-d_{2}}W_{d_{2}}\subset W_{d_{1}}
and
\mathfrak{A}(U)_{d_{1},-d_{2}}W_{d_{3}}=0
when
d_{2}\neq d_{3}
which, on simple tensors, is given by the products defined in
B.2.8
:

(\alpha\otimes a\otimes\alpha^{\prime})\star w=\begin{cases}\alpha(a(\alpha^{\prime}w))&\text{if $d_{2}=d_{3}$}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Explicitly, if we write
\alpha=[u],a=[x],\alpha^{\prime}=[v]
for
u\in U_{d_{1}},v\in U_{-d_{2}},x\in U_{0}
, we have by definition (
B.2.8
),
\alpha(a(\alpha^{\prime}w))=u(x(v(w)))
. It follows from
B.2.8
that this is a well defined map. Further, associativity of these products follows from the associativity of the action of
U
on
W
.
∎

B.3.
Relationship with higher generalized Zhu algebras

Throughout this section, let us fix a graded algebra
U
complete with respect to an almost canonical seminorm. We will write
\mathsf{A}_{n}
in place of
\mathsf{A}_{n}(U)
and
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
in place of
\mathfrak{A}(U)_{n}
.

The action of
U_{0}
on
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
is continuous where
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
is defined as a discrete module.

Lemma B.3.1
.

For each
d\geq 0
, there is an exact sequence

(21)

\mathfrak{A}_{d}\overset{\mu_{d}}{\longrightarrow}\mathsf{A}_{d}\overset{\pi_{d}}{\longrightarrow}\mathsf{A}_{d-1}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mu_{d}(\overline{\alpha}\otimes[u]_{0}\otimes\overline{\beta})=[\alpha u\beta]_{d}
, for all
\alpha\in U_{d}
(respectively
\beta\in U_{-d}
) and where
\overline{\alpha}
(respectively
\overline{\beta}
) denotes its class in
U/\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{1}U
(respectively in
U/\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{1}U
).

Proof.

We first check that the map
\mu_{d}
is well defined. Notice
\mu_{d}
is independent on the lifts of
\overline{\alpha}
and
\overline{\beta}
to
U
, since
\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{L}}^{1}U\cdot U_{0}\cdot U_{d}\subseteq\operatorname{N}\!^{1}U_{0}
and similarly
U_{d}\cdot U_{0}\cdot\operatorname{N}\!_{\mathsf{R}}^{1}U_{-d}\subseteq\operatorname{N}\!^{1}U_{0}
. Analogously, since
U_{d}\cdot\operatorname{N}\!^{1}U_{0}\cdot U_{-d}\subseteq\operatorname{N}\!^{d}U_{0}
, the map
\mu_{d}
is independent of the lift of
[u]_{0}
to
u\in U_{0}
. Finally, we need to show that it respects the tensor products over
U_{0}
. For this we need to check that
\mu_{d}(\overline{\alpha v}\otimes[u]_{0}\otimes\overline{\beta})=\mu_{d}(\overline{\alpha}\otimes[vu]_{0}\otimes\overline{\beta})
for every
v\in U_{0}
. But by definition both are the class of the element
\alpha vu\beta
in
\mathsf{A}_{d}
.

We have identifications
\mathsf{A}_{d}=U_{0}/\operatorname{N}\!^{d+1}U_{0}
and
\mathsf{A}_{d-1}=U_{0}/\operatorname{N}\!^{d}U_{0}
. Consequently the kernel of the canonical projection
\pi_{d}
can be written as
\operatorname{N}\!^{d}U_{0}/\operatorname{N}\!^{d+1}U_{0}
. It follows from the definition of
\mu_{d}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
that the image of the
\mu_{d}
consists exactly of sums of element of the form
[\alpha\beta]_{d}
with
\deg\alpha=d
and
\deg\beta=-d
. Hence the image of
\mu_{d}
consists of the image of
\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{d}U_{0}
in
\mathsf{A}_{d}
. Since we have an almost canonical filtration, we have by
A.6.9
,

\operatorname{N}\!^{d}U_{0}=\mbox{}^{c}\!\operatorname{N}\!^{d}U_{0}+\operatorname{N}\!^{d+1}U_{0},

which shows that
\mu_{d}
is therefore surjective onto
\operatorname{N}\!^{d}U_{0}/\operatorname{N}\!^{d+1}U_{0}=\ker\pi_{d}
, showing right exactness.
∎

The following result is immediate from the definitions and from the associativity of the actions.

Lemma B.3.2
.

Let
W_{0}
be an
\mathsf{A}_{0}
-module. Then the action of
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
on
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(W_{0})_{d}
factors through the action of
\mathsf{A}_{d}
described in
B.1.7
via the map
\mu_{d}
.

We now are ready to state the principal result of this section.

Theorem B.3.3
.

If
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
admits an identity element,
then the map
\mu_{d}
in (
21
) is injective and the sequence splits, giving a ring product

\mathsf{A}_{d}\cong\mathfrak{A}_{d}\times\mathsf{A}_{d-1}.

Proof.

We first check that the map
\mu_{d}
is injective. Suppose we are given an element
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathfrak{A}_{d}
which is in the kernel of this map. By
B.3.2
, the action of
\mathfrak{a}
on
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(M)_{d}
for any
M
factors through the action of
\mathsf{A}_{d}
via
\mu_{d}
, so it should be
0
. If we consider the case of
M=\Phi^{\mathsf{R}}(\mathsf{A}_{0})
, this says that, in particular, the action of
\mathfrak{a}
on
\mathfrak{A}_{d}\subset\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{R}}(\mathsf{A}_{0}))_{d}
is
0
. This action is identified with the algebra product via
B.2.4
. It follows that since
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
has an identity element
\mathscr{I}_{d}
, we have
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}\star\mathscr{I}_{d}=0
as claimed.

Since
\mu_{d}
is injective we will omit it in the remainder of the proof and see
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
as naturally sitting inside
\mathsf{A}_{d}
. Denote the unity in the higher level Zhu algebras
\mathsf{A}_{d}
by
1
, and write
e=\mathscr{I}_{d}
. Let
f=1-e
so that
e
and
f
are orthogonal idempotents. Note that
e
generates the 2-sided ideal
\mathfrak{A}_{d}\triangleleft\mathsf{A}_{d}
. Furthermore, since e is the unity of
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
, for every
a\in\mathsf{A}_{d}
, we have
ae=eae=ea
and so
e\in Z(\mathsf{A}_{d})
. Consequently
f=1-e\in Z(\mathsf{A}_{d})
as well. It follows that
f
and
e
are orthogonal central idempotents, and therefore
\mathsf{A}_{d}=\mathsf{A}_{d}e\times\mathsf{A}_{d}f
as rings. But
\mathsf{A}_{d}e=\mathfrak{A}_{d}
and
\mathsf{A}_{d}f\cong\mathsf{A}_{d}/\mathfrak{A}_{d}\cong\mathsf{A}_{d-1}
completing our proof.
∎

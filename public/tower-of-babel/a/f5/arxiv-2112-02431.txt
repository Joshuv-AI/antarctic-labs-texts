Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field of characteristic not equal to
2
. Any associative algebra
A
over the field
\mathbb{F}
gives rise to the Lie algebra
A^{(-)}=(A,[a,b]=ab-ba)
. Let
V
be an infinite-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}
. In this paper, we consider the algebras
\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
of all linear transformations
V\to V
and
\mathfrak{gl}(V)=\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)^{(-)}
the general Lie algebra of
V.

For a cardinal
\alpha\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V
denote by
I_{\alpha}
the ideal of all linear transformations
\varphi:V\rightarrow V
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\varphi(V)
is less than
\alpha

\aleph_{0}
the ideal
I_{\aleph_{0}}
consists of all linear transformations of finite ranges. Let
\text{Id}_{V}
denote the identity transformation.

N. Jacobson
[
9
]
classified ideals of the associative ring
\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
of linear transformations of
V.
He proved that:

all ideals of the ring
\text{\emph{End}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
of linear transformations of an infinite-dimensional vector space
V
are:
(0),

I_{\alpha},
where
\aleph_{0}\leq\alpha\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V,
and
\text{\emph{End}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V).

The purpose of this paper is the classification of ideals of the algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(V).

Theorem 1
.

Let
V
be an infinite-dimensional vector space over a field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic not equal to
2.
Every ideal of
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
belongs to one of the following families:

(1)

\{0\},

\mathbb{F}\cdot\emph{Id}_{V},

\mathfrak{gl}(V);

(2)

I_{\alpha}
or
\mathbb{F}\cdot\emph{Id}_{V}+I_{\alpha},
where
\aleph_{0}\leq\alpha\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V;

(3)

any subspase

[I_{\aleph_{0}},\mathfrak{gl}(V)]\subseteq U\subseteq\mathbb{F}\cdot\emph{Id}_{V}+I_{\aleph_{0}}.

Moreover, the co-dimension of
[I_{\aleph_{0}},\mathfrak{gl}(V)]
in
\mathbb{F}\cdot\text{Id}_{V}+I_{\aleph_{0}}
is
2.
Therefore, if the field
\mathbb{F}
is infinite then the family

is an infinite family of ideals.

Proposition 1
.

The center
C
of the algebra
\text{\emph{End}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)/{I_{\alpha}}

Lemma 4
.

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\ker z<\alpha.

Proof.

The subspace
I=I_{\alpha}+\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)z
of
\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
is an ideal. The ideal
I
contains
z
and, therefore, is strictly larger then
I_{\alpha}
. By Jacobson‘s Theorem, all ideals of
\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
are of the types
(0),

I_{\beta}
, where
\aleph_{0}\leq\beta\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V
, and
\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
.

Case 1.

I_{\alpha}+\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)z=I_{\beta}
,
\alpha<\beta\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V
.

Suppose that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\ker z\geq\alpha
. Then there exists a subspace
V^{\prime}\subseteq\ker z
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V^{\prime}=\alpha
.

Let
p
be a projection
p:V\to V^{\prime},
that is
p(V)\subseteq V^{\prime}
and
p(v)=v
for every element
v\in V^{\prime}
. Then
p\in I_{\beta}
. Hence, there exist elements
\varphi\in I_{\alpha}
and
a\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
such that
p=\varphi+az.
For an arbitrary element
v\in V^{\prime}
we have

v=p(v)=\varphi(v)+a(z(v)).

Since
V^{\prime}\subseteq\ker z
, it follows that
z(v)=0
. Hence,
\varphi(v)=v
for all elements of
V^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\varphi(V)\geq\alpha
, which contradicts the inclusion
\varphi\in I_{\alpha}
.

Case 2.

I_{\alpha}+\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)z=\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V).

Recall that
\text{Id}_{V}
is the identity transformation on
V
. Again there exist elements
\varphi\in I_{\alpha}
and
a\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
such that

\varphi(v)+az=\text{Id}_{V}.

If
v\in\ker z
then

v=\varphi(v)+a(z(v))=\varphi(v),

hence
\ker z\subseteq\varphi(V)
. Since
\varphi\in I_{\alpha}
it implies that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\ker z<\alpha
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

If
W
is a subspace of
V
and

W\cap z(W)=(0)

then
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}W<\alpha.

Proof.

There exists a linear transformation
\varphi\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
such that
\varphi(W)=(0)
and
\varphi(v)=v
for an arbitrary element
v\in z(W)
.

By the assumption on
z
, the image of the linear transformation
z\varphi-\varphi z
has dimension less than
\alpha
.

For an arbitrary element
w\in W
we have
z(\varphi(w))=0
,
\varphi(z(w))=z(w)

z(w)=(\varphi z-z\varphi)(w)

\varphi z-z\varphi

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}z(W)<\alpha

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}z(W)<\alpha

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\ker z<\alpha

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Let
W
be a maximal subspace of
V
such that
W\cap z(W)=(0)
(it exists by Zorn‘s Lemma). Then
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}W<\alpha
.

Choose an element
v\in V\setminus(W\oplus z(W))
. Then by maximality of
W
, we have

(W+\mathbb{F}v)\cap z(W+\mathbb{F}v)\neq(0).

Choose an element
w\in W
and a scalar
\xi\in\mathbb{F}
such that

0\neq z(w+\xi v)\in W+\mathbb{F}v.

Case 1.

\xi=0
. Then
0\neq z(w)\in W+\mathbb{F}v
, which is impossible since
z(W)\cap W=(0)
and
v\notin W\oplus z(W)
.

Case 2.
If
\xi\neq 0
then
z(w)+\xi z(v)=w^{\prime}+\eta v
, where
w^{\prime}\in W
,
\eta\in\mathbb{F}
. So,

z(v)=\frac{\eta}{\xi}\,v\mod(W\oplus z(W)).

We proved that for an arbitrary element
v\in V\setminus(W\oplus z(W))
there exists a scalar
t_{v}\in\mathbb{F}
such that

z(v)=t_{v}v\mod(W\oplus z(W)).

Let
v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\in V
be linearly independent modulo
W\oplus z(W)
. Then

z(v^{\prime})=t_{v^{\prime}}v^{\prime}\mod(W\oplus z(W)),

(1)

z(v^{\prime\prime})=t_{v^{\prime\prime}}v^{\prime\prime}\mod(W\oplus z(W)),

(2)

z(v^{\prime}+v^{\prime\prime})=t_{v^{\prime}+v^{\prime\prime}}(v^{\prime}+v^{\prime\prime})\mod(W\oplus z(W)).

(3)

Subtracting the equalities (
1
) and (
2
) from the equality (
3
) we get

(t_{v^{\prime}+v^{\prime\prime}}-t_{v^{\prime}})v^{\prime}+(t_{v^{\prime}+v^{\prime\prime}}-t_{v^{\prime\prime}})v^{\prime\prime}\in(W\oplus z(W)).

In view of the linear independence of
v^{\prime}
,
v^{\prime\prime}
modulo
W\oplus z(W)
we get

t_{v^{\prime}}=t_{v^{\prime\prime}}=t_{v^{\prime}+v^{\prime\prime}}.

Hence there exists a scalar
t\in\mathbb{F}
such that

z(v)=tv\mod(W\oplus z(W))

for an arbitrary
v\in V
.
The image of the linear transformation
z-t\cdot\text{Id}_{V}
lies in
W\oplus z(W).
Hence

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(z-t\cdot\text{Id}_{V})<\alpha\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994ptz-t\cdot\text{Id}_{V}\in I_{\alpha}.

This completes the proof of the proposition. ∎

Lemma 6
.

(1)

Let
\aleph_{0}<\alpha\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V
. Then

I_{\alpha}=[I_{\alpha},I_{\alpha}].

(2)

[I_{\aleph_{0}},\mathfrak{gl}(V)]=[I_{\aleph_{0}},I_{\aleph_{0}}]
has co-dimension
1
in
I_{\aleph_{0}}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in I_{\alpha}
,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\varphi(V)<\alpha
. If
\aleph_{0}<\alpha
then there exists a subspace
V^{\prime}\subset V
such that

\varphi(V)\subseteq V^{\prime}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\aleph_{0}\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V^{\prime}<\alpha.

Choose a subspace
V^{\prime\prime}\subset V
such that
V=V^{\prime}\oplus V^{\prime\prime}
is a direct sum. The linear transformation
\varphi
can be decomposed as
\varphi=\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}
, where

\varphi_{1}:V^{\prime}\to V^{\prime},\ \varphi_{1}(V^{\prime\prime})=(0),\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\varphi_{2}:V^{\prime\prime}\to V^{\prime},\ \varphi_{2}(V^{\prime})=(0).

Let
p
be a projection from
V
to
V^{\prime}
, i.e.

p|_{V^{\prime}}=\text{Id}_{V^{\prime}},\hskip 9.24994ptp(V^{\prime\prime})=(0).

We notice that the images of
\varphi_{1}
,
\varphi_{2}
,
p
lie in
V^{\prime}.
Therefore,
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},p\in I_{\alpha}
.

By
[
5
]
, we have

\varphi_{1}|_{V^{\prime}}\in[\mathfrak{gl}(V^{\prime}),\mathfrak{gl}(V^{\prime})].

Hence
\varphi_{1}\in[I_{\alpha},I_{\alpha}].
Furthermore,

\varphi_{2}=[p,\varphi_{2}]\in[I_{\alpha},I_{\alpha}].

We proved that
\varphi\in[I_{\alpha},I_{\alpha}]
. This completes the proof of part (1) of the lemma.

Now, consider the ideal
I_{\aleph_{0}}
. A linear transformation
\varphi
lies in
I_{\aleph_{0}}
if and only if the subspace
V^{\prime}=\varphi(V)
is finite-dimensional.

There exists a finite-dimensional subspace
V^{\prime\prime}\subset V
such that
V^{\prime}\subseteq V^{\prime\prime}
and
\varphi(V^{\prime\prime})=V^{\prime}.
Let
\text{tr}(\varphi)
be the trace of the restriction

\varphi|_{V^{\prime\prime}}\in\mathfrak{gl}(V^{\prime\prime}).

It is easy to see that

(i)

\text{tr}(\varphi)
does not depend on a choice of the subspace
V^{\prime\prime},

(ii)

\text{tr}:I_{\aleph_{0}}\to\mathbb{F}
is a linear functional,

(iii)

\text{tr}(\varphi)=0
if and only if
\varphi\in[I_{\aleph_{0}},I_{\aleph_{0}}].

This implies that
[I_{\aleph_{0}},I_{\aleph_{0}}]
has co-dimension 1 in
I_{\aleph_{0}}
.

It remains to show that

[I_{\aleph_{0}},\mathfrak{gl}(V)]=[I_{\aleph_{0}},I_{\aleph_{0}}]

or equivalently

\text{tr}([I_{\aleph_{0}},\mathfrak{gl}(V)])=(0).

Choose
\varphi\in I_{\aleph_{0}}
and
\psi\in\mathfrak{gl}(V).
Since
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\varphi(V)<\infty
the subspace
\ker\varphi
has a finite co-dimension in
V.
Hence, there exists a finite-dimensional subspace
V_{1}\subset V
such that
\varphi(V)\subset V_{1},

\varphi(V_{1})=\varphi(V)
and
V=V_{1}+\ker\varphi.
Let
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V_{1}=n.
Choose a subspace
V_{2}\subset\ker\varphi
such that

\ker\varphi=(V_{1}\cap\ker\varphi)\oplus V_{2}

is a direct sum. Then
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
is a direct sum. Let
B_{1},

B_{2}
be bases of the subspaces
V_{1},

V_{2}
, respectively. In the basis
B=B_{1}\cup B_{2}
of the vector space
V
the linear transformations
\varphi,

\psi
have (infinite) matrices

\begin{pmatrix}a&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\mbox{0}\\
\hline\cr\mbox{0}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\mbox{0}\end{pmatrix},\hskip 9.24994pt\begin{pmatrix}b_{11}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&b_{12}\\
\hline\cr b_{21}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&b_{22}\end{pmatrix}

respectively, where the blocks
a,

b_{11}
are
n\times n
matrices. We have

\left[\begin{pmatrix}a&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\mbox{0}\\
\hline\cr\mbox{0}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\mbox{0}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}b_{11}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&b_{12}\\

The trace of this infinite matrix is equal to the trace of the

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
U
be a proper ideal of the Lie algebra

Let

Consider the ideal

\text{id}_{A}([U,U])
equals
(0),

for some

\aleph_{0}\leq\alpha\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V,
or
A.
We will consider each of these cases separately.

Case 1.
Suppose that
\text{id}_{A}([U,U])=A
. Then by Lemma
1
, we have

[\text{id}_{A}([U,U]),A]=[A,A]\subseteq U.

By
[
5
]
, we have
U=\mathfrak{gl}(V),
which contradicts the assumption that
U
is proper.

Case 2.
Suppose that
\text{id}_{A}([U,U])=(0)
. Then
[U,U]=(0)
. It is easy to see that the algebra
A
is prime. Indeed, let
\varphi,

\psi\in A
be nonzero linear transformations
\varphi(v)\neq 0,

\psi(w)\neq 0,
where

w\in V.
There exists a linear transformation
\chi:V\to V

Then

\psi\,\chi\,\varphi(v)=\psi(w)\neq 0,

\psi A\varphi\neq(0).
By Lemma
2
,
U
lies in the center of the algebra

. Hence

.

Case 3.
Now, let
\text{id}_{A}([U,U])=I_{\alpha}
,
\aleph_{0}\leq\alpha\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V
. The ideal

of the Lie algebra

Lemma
3

. Hence

On the other hand, by Lemma

[I_{\alpha},A]\subseteq U
. We proved that

[I_{\alpha},A]\subseteq U\subseteq\mathbb{F}\cdot\text{Id}_{V}+I_{\alpha}.

If

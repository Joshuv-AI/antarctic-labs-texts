Gaudin Algebras, RSK and Calogero-Moser Cells in Type A Thanks: I.G. has been supported by EPSRC grants EP/R034826/1 and EP/G007632/1.
Adrien Brochier, Iain Gordon, Noah White

Abstract
. We study the spectrum of a family of algebras, the inhomogeneous Gaudin algebras, acting on the n n -fold tensor representation ℂ ​ [ x 1 , … , x r ] ⊗ n \mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]^{\otimes n} of the Lie algebra 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 r \mathfrak{gl}_{r} . We use the work of Halacheva-Kamnitzer-Rybnikov-Weekes to demonstrate that the Robinson-Schensted-Knuth correspondence describes the behaviour of the spectrum as we move along special paths in the family. We apply the work of Mukhin-Tarasov-Varchenko, which proves that the rational Calogero-Moser phase space can be realised as a part of this spectrum, to relate this to behaviour at t = 0 t=0 of rational Cherednik algebras of 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} . As a result, we confirm for symmetric groups a conjecture of Bonnafé-Rouquier which proposes an equality between the Calogero-Moser cells they defined and the well-known Kazhdan-Lusztig cells.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

Let
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}={\rm Mat}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
be the general Lie algebra. Any tensor product of locally finite dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-representations,
M=V_{1}\otimes V_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{n}
, carries an action of an inhomogeneous Gaudin algebra,
\mathcal{A}(\underline{z};\underline{q})
. This algebra depends on two sets of parameters
\underline{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\underline{q}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
. It is commutative. Works of several authors prove that for many
M
and general values of the parameters
\underline{z}
and
\underline{q}
its action has a simple spectrum. We will denote its spectrum by
\mathcal{E}_{\underline{z},\underline{q}}(M)
.

1.2.

We are interested in the behaviour of the sets
\mathcal{E}_{\underline{z},\underline{q}}(M)
as we vary the parameters
\underline{z}
and
\underline{q}
. We shall investigate the case
M=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]^{\otimes n}
, where
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
acts on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
via differential operators
E_{ij}\mapsto x_{i}\partial_{j}
. For the rest of the introduction we fix
M
as this representation, and denote
\mathcal{E}_{\underline{z},\underline{q}}(M)
by
\mathcal{E}_{\underline{z},\underline{q}}
.

1.3.

We shall consider tuples
\underline{z}\in\mathbb{R}_{\text{{<}}}^{n}=\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{1}<z_{2}<\cdots<z_{n}\}
and
\underline{q}\in\mathbb{R}_{\text{{<}}}^{r}=\{(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:q_{1}<q_{2}<\cdots<q_{r}\}
. We will prove, with appropriate definitions for limits, that:

•

as
\underline{z}\rightarrow\underline{\infty}
, the set
\mathcal{E}_{\underline{\infty},\underline{q}}
identifies with
{\rm Mat}_{r\times n}(\mathbb{N})
, the set of
r
-by-
n
matrices whose entries are non-negative integers;

•

as
\underline{z}\rightarrow\underline{0}
and then
\underline{q}\rightarrow\underline{0}
, the set
\mathcal{E}_{\underline{0},\underline{0}}
identifies with
\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\mathtt{Part}}\mathtt{SSYT}_{n}(\lambda)\times\mathtt{SSYT}_{r}(\lambda)
where
\mathtt{SSYT}_{r}(\lambda)
is the set of semistandard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
with entries from
\{1,\ldots,r\}
and similarly for
\mathtt{SSYT}_{n}(\lambda)
;

•

tracking the sets
\mathcal{E}_{\underline{z},\underline{q}}
through the process of moving
(\underline{z},\underline{q})
in (an extension of)
\mathbb{R}_{\text{{<}}}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{\text{{<}}}^{r}
from the first limit to the second limit induces the Robinson-Schensted-Knuth correspondence

\Mat_{r\times n}(\mathbb{N})\longrightarrow\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\mathtt{Part}}\mathtt{SSYT}_{n}(\lambda)\times\mathtt{SSYT}_{r}(\lambda).

1.4.

Our proof of these results relies critically on
[
Hal+20
]
which endows the sets
\mathcal{E}_{\underline{z},\underline{q}}(M)
with the structure of a
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-crystal. That the RSK-correspondence appears is then a common theme in the theory of crystals.

1.5.

Our interest in the above result stems from an application of a special case to confirm conjectures of Bonnafé-Rouquier,
[
BR17
, Conjecture L and LR]
, in the theory of rational Cherednik algebras of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
which we now explain.
To each complex reflection group
(W,\mathfrak{h})
there is a family of rational Cherednik algebras associated,
[
EG02
]
. These algebras depend on a pair of parameters, usually denoted by
t
and
c
. When
t=0
, the rational Cherednik algebras have a large centre whose geometry controls much of their representation theory. The spectrum of this centre, a generalised Calogero-Moser space, depends on the parameter
c
and is a ramified covering of the affine space
\mathfrak{h}/W\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}/W
.
Bonnafé and Rouquier,
[
BR17
]
, have used the Galois theory of this covering to define partitions of the elements of
W
into (left, right, two-sided) Calogero-Moser “cells”, depending on
c
. When
(W,\mathfrak{h})
is a Coxeter group it is conjectured that with appropriate choices these cells agree with the Kazhdan-Lusztig cells of
W
, important objects in Lie theoretic representation theory and algebraic combinatorics. Thus, conjecturally, Calogero-Moser cells generalise the theory of Kazhdan-Lusztig cells from Coxeter groups to all complex reflection groups.
In
[
BR17
]
the Bonnafé-Rouquier conjecture is proved for rank 2 Coxeter groups.

1.6.

At
t=0
the spectrum of the centre of rational Cherednik algebra of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
(for
c\neq 0
) is isomorphic to classical rational Calegero-Moser phase space

\operatorname{CM}_{n}=\{(Z,Y)\in{\rm Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\times{\rm Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{C}):[Z,Y]+\id=\text{rank 1 matrix}\}/PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

Sending the pair of matrices
(Z,Y)
to their eigenvalues produces a ramified covering

\Upsilon:\operatorname{CM}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{n}.

Any
n
-tuple of pairwise distinct complex numbers,
\underline{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}_{\text{{reg}}}^{n}
and
n
-tuple
\underline{p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
give rise to a point in
\operatorname{CM}_{n}
:

Z=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&z_{2}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&z_{n}\end{pmatrix},\,Y=\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}&(z_{1}-z_{2})^{-1}&\cdots&(z_{1}-z_{n})^{-1}\\
(z_{2}-z_{1})^{-1}&p_{2}&\cdots&(z_{2}-z_{n})^{-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
(z_{n}-z_{1})^{-1}&(z_{n}-z_{2})^{-1}&\cdots&p_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

In this description
\Upsilon(Z,Y)=([\underline{z}],[\underline{q}])
where the unordered eigenvalues of the matrix
Y
depend on
\underline{p}
and
\underline{z}
and are denoted by
[\underline{q}]
.

1.7.

In this language the Bonnafé-Rouquier conjecture can be stated in terms of the behaviour of
\underline{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{\text{{<}}}^{n}
and
\underline{q}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{\text{{<}}}^{n}
. For appropriate large values of
\underline{z}
, the elements in the fibre of
\Upsilon
can be identified with the symmetric group, since the matrices
Y
above
[\underline{q}]
are close to diagonal matrices with distinct entries. The fibre above
[\underline{z}]=[\underline{0}]
can be identified with the standard Young tableaux of
n
,
[
MTV12
, Theorem 1.5]
. Tracking the elements in the fibre as
\underline{z}
moves from infinity to zero sees them coalesce to tableaux. The conjecture is that these collisions are determined by the Robinson-Schensted algorithm, and in particular that the element in the fibre corresponding to
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
tracks to the tableau given by the
P
-symbol of
w
.

1.8.

To confirm this we use the work
[
MTV14
]
which identifies the fibres of
\Upsilon
with a part of the spectrum
\mathcal{E}_{\underline{z},\underline{q}}(V_{\varpi_{1}}^{\otimes n})
of the inhomogeneous Gaudin algebras for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. Since
V_{\varpi_{1}}
is a summand of
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
V_{\varpi_{1}}^{\otimes n}
is a summand of
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{\otimes n}
and we are in the situation described above in the introduction, with
r=n
.
As a result we can use the limiting behaviour of the spectrum
\mathcal{E}_{\underline{z},\underline{q}}
to interpret the behaviour of the fibres of
\Upsilon
in terms of the Robinson-Schensted correspondence. This allows us to confirm Bonnafé-Rouquier conjecture for
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

1.9.

This paper is organised as follows. In the Sections 2 and 3 we recall a variety of constructions which we will require: parallel transport of representations of algebras, the RSK correspondence, inhomogeneous Gaudin algebras. We state our main theorem in Section 4. Sections 5–7 then prove the theorem. In Section 8 we apply this to confirm the conjecture of Bonnafé-Rouquier.

2.
Setup

In this section we introduce notation that we will use throughout the paper.

2.1.
Spectra of Commutative Algebras

Let
V
be a
k
-dimensional vector space. Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a commutative algebra that acts on
V
, meaning that we have a mapping
\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\End(V)
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is commutative there will exist a set of distinct algebraic characters
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{\ell}
such that for each
i
the generalised eigenspace

V_{i}=\left\{v\in V\;\middle|\;(a-\chi_{i}(a))^{k}\cdot v=0\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal{A}\right\}

is non-zero. It follows that
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\ell}
is an
\mathcal{A}
-stable decomposition. We denote the set of all generalised eigenspaces for
\mathcal{A}
acting on
V
by

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{A}}(V)=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}\}.

We say that
\mathcal{A}
acts with a simple spectrum on
V
if for each
i
the space
V_{i}
above is one-dimensional. In particular this means that
\ell
, the number of characters, equals
k
.

2.2.

Suppose
U
is an algebra and we have an algebra map
U\longrightarrow\End(V)
. A family of commutative subalgebras of
U
parametrized by a space
X
, denoted
\{\mathcal{A}(x):x\in X\}
, gives us a family of subalgebras acting on
V
. Typically the images of these subalgebras in
\End(V)
are not all of the same dimension nor do they all act semisimply. Nonetheless we will be interested in two different situations.

(1)

We will restrict
X
to some topological subspace
Y\subseteq X
such that for each
y\in Y
,
\mathcal{A}(y)
acts with simple spectrum on
V
. Then we get a covering space

\mathcal{E}(V)\longrightarrow Y

whose fibres
are
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{A}(y)}(V)
. In particular, if we have a path
\gamma:[0,1]\longrightarrow Y
we get a parallel transport
map
p:\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{A}(\gamma(0))}(V)\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{A}(\gamma(1))}(V)
.

(2)

We will restrict
X
to some topological subspace
Y\subseteq X
such that for each
y\in Y
,
\mathcal{A}(y)
acts with simple spectrum on
V
with the exception of a single distinguished point
\tilde{y}\in Y
where
\mathcal{A}(\tilde{y})
acts semisimply, but without a simple spectrum. Then we get a branched covering space

\mathcal{E}(V)\longrightarrow Y.

In this case, if we have a path
\gamma:[0,1]\longrightarrow Y
with
\gamma(t)=\tilde{y}
if and only if
t=1
, then we get a degenerated parallel transport
map
p:\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{A}(\gamma(0))}(V)\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{A}(\gamma(1))}(V)
.

2.3.
Tensor Embeddings

Let
U
be an algebra and
x\in U
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq a\leq n
. We have an embedding

\iota_{a}:U\longrightarrow U^{\otimes n},\quad x\mapsto\id^{\otimes a-1}\otimes x\otimes\id^{\otimes n-a}

which places
x
in the
a^{\text{th}}
tensorand. We use the notation
x^{(a)}=\iota_{a}(x)
for this. If
\underline{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
is a sequence of distinct integers between
1
and
n
, we use
\iota_{\underline{a}}:U^{\otimes k}\longrightarrow U^{\otimes n}
to denote the map that embeds the
i^{\text{th}}
factor into the
a_{i}^{\text{th}}
factor, i.e.
\iota_{\underline{a}}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{(a_{i})}
.

2.4.

If
U
is a bialgebra with coproduct
\Delta:U\longrightarrow U\otimes U
, we use
\Delta^{n}
to denote the map
U\longrightarrow U^{\otimes n}
defined inductively by
\Delta^{n}=(\Delta^{n-1}\otimes\id)\circ\Delta
with
\Delta^{1}=\id
. Note this means
\Delta^{2}=\Delta
. For
\underline{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
as above, let
\#\underline{a}=k
. If
\underline{a}^{1},\underline{a}^{2},\ldots,\underline{a}^{m}
are sequences of distinct integers between
1
and
n
, such that the underlying sets are disjoint, we let
\Delta^{\underline{a}^{1},\underline{a}^{2},\ldots,\underline{a}^{m}}:U^{\otimes m}\longrightarrow U^{\otimes n}
be the map defined by
\Delta^{\underline{a}^{1},\underline{a}^{2},\ldots,\underline{a}^{m}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\iota_{\underline{a}^{i}}\circ\Delta^{\#\underline{a}^{i}}
. If
A\subseteq U^{\otimes m}
is a subalgebra we also use the notation
A^{\underline{a}^{1},\underline{a}^{2},\ldots,\underline{a}^{m}}=\Delta^{\underline{a}^{1},\underline{a}^{2},\ldots,\underline{a}^{m}}(A)
. For example, if
A\subseteq U^{\otimes 2}
we think of
A^{(23)(1)}\subseteq U^{\otimes 3}
as the algebra obtained from
A
by spreading its first leg over the second and third tensor factors, and placing the second leg into the first tensor factor.

2.5.
The Robinson-Schensted-Knuth correspondence

A thorough account can be found in
[
Ful97
, Chapter 4]
. Let
\Mat_{r\times n}(\mathbb{N})
be the set of
r\times n
matrices with nonnegative integer entries. Let

. We set
\mathtt{Part}(k)
to be the set of partitions of
k
and the set of all partitions is denoted
\mathtt{Part}=\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\mathtt{Part}(k)

\mathtt{Part}_{\leq t}
be the set of partitions with at most
t

2.8.
Crystals

We let
\mathtt{SSYT}_{r}(\lambda)
be the set of semistandard tableaux of shape
\lambda\in\mathtt{Part}
with entries in
\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
. Recall that the set
\mathtt{SSYT}_{r}(\lambda)
carries a natural
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-crystal structure.
The set
\Mat_{r\times n}(\mathbb{N})
is given the structure of a
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-crystal as follows. Let
\mathbb{N}^{r}(k)
be the set of vectors in
\mathbb{N}^{r}
with the sum of entries equal to
k
. We can identify
\mathbb{N}^{r}(k)
with the natural basis of monomials in the irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-representation
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r}]_{k}
,
giving
\mathbb{N}^{r}(k)
the structure of a
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-crystal.
If
\underline{k}=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, then let
\Mat_{r\times n}(\mathbb{N},\underline{k})
be the set of matrices with column sums
\underline{k}
.
This identifies
\Mat_{r\times n}(\mathbb{N},\underline{k})
with
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{N}^{r}(k_{i})
, and so gives
\Mat_{r\times n}(\mathbb{N},\underline{k})
a
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-crystal structure as a tensor product of crystals.
Finally,
\Mat_{r\times n}(\mathbb{N})=\bigsqcup_{\underline{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\Mat_{r\times n}(\mathbb{N},\underline{k})
is the direct sum (i.e. disjoint union) of crystals.

Proposition

(
[
BS17
, Corollary 9.2]
)
.

The map
\mathtt{RSK}
is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-crystals, where
\mathtt{SSYT}_{n}(\lambda)\times\mathtt{SSYT}_{r}(\lambda)
is taken to be
\#\mathtt{SSYT}_{n}(\lambda)
many copies of the crystal
\mathtt{SSYT}_{r}(\lambda)
.

2.9.

The following is an important property of the RSK correspondence which we will exploit later. If
1\leq i\leq r
and
S\in\mathtt{SSYT}_{r}(\lambda)
we set
S|_{i}
to be the tableau obtained by removing all boxes containing numbers strictly larger than
i
.

Proposition
.

Suppose
f:\Mat_{r\times n}(\mathbb{N})\longrightarrow\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\mathtt{Part}}\mathtt{SSYT}_{n}(\lambda)\times\mathtt{SSYT}_{r}(\lambda)
,
f(A)=(S(A),T(A))
, is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-crystals. If, for every
A\in\Mat_{r\times n}(\mathbb{N})
,
f
has the property that
S(A)|_{n-1}=P(A)|_{n-1}
, then
f=\mathtt{RSK}
.

Proof.

The fact that
f
and
\mathtt{RSK}
are both crystal isomorphisms means that
Q(A)=T(A)
.
For
T\in\mathtt{SSYT}_{n}(\lambda)
, define
\mathtt{sh}(T)=\lambda
and observe therefore that

\mathtt{sh}(S(A))=\mathtt{sh}(T(A))=\mathtt{sh}(Q(A))=\mathtt{sh}(P(A)).

Thus we know that
S(A)
and
P(A)
are semistandard tableaux of the same shape. By hypothesis they are identical semistandard tableaux once all boxes containing
n
have been removed. This means
S(A)=P(A)
.
∎

3.
Inhomogeneous Gaudin Algebras

We will introduce the algebras whose spectrum we will be interested in studying and some of their limits.

3.1.
Definitions

Let
\hat{\mathfrak{gl}_{r}}_{-}=t^{-1}\mathfrak{gl}_{r}[t^{-1}]
. For any non-zero complex number
w
, there is an evaluation map
\hat{\mathfrak{gl}_{r}}_{-}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
which sends
g\otimes t^{-s}
to
gw^{-s}
. It is a Lie algebra homomorphism and induces a morphism of algebras
\phi_{w}:U(\hat{\mathfrak{gl}_{r}}_{-})\longrightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
. For any
\underline{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
, there is a Lie algebra homomorphism:

\hat{\mathfrak{gl}_{r}}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{r}^{\oplus n},\qquad g\otimes t^{-s}\mapsto(g(w-z_{1})^{-s},g(w-z_{2})^{-s},\ldots,g(w-z_{n})^{-s}).

This induces an algebra morphism

\phi_{w}(\underline{z})=\phi_{w-z_{1}}\otimes\phi_{w-z_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\phi_{w-z_{n}}\circ\Delta^{n}:U(\hat{\mathfrak{gl}_{r}}_{-})\longrightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{r}^{\oplus n})=U(\mathfrak{gl}_{r})^{\otimes n}.

There is also an evaluation at
\infty
mapping,
\hat{\mathfrak{gl}_{r}}_{-}\rightarrow(\mathfrak{gl}_{r})_{\sf ab}
, given by extracting the coefficient of
t^{-1}
. This Lie algebra homomorphism gives rise to an algebra morphism

Now fix

Proof.

Since

partitions

6.
Moduli of inhomogenous Gaudin algebras

For
(\underline{z},\underline{q})\in\mathbb{C}_{\text{{reg}}}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}_{\text{{reg}}}^{r}
, the algebras
\mathcal{A}(\underline{z};\underline{q})\subseteq U(\mathfrak{gl}_{r})^{\otimes n}
have constant Hilbert series with respect to the PBW filtration (see
[
Hal+20
, Section 9.4]
). Thus if we fix
\underline{q}\in\mathbb{C}_{\text{{reg}}}^{r}
, then
\mathbb{C}_{\text{{reg}}}^{n}
maps into the space of subalgebras of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{r})^{\otimes n}
with the same Hilbert series as
\mathcal{A}(\underline{z};\underline{q})
. Denote the closure of the image of
\mathbb{C}_{\text{{reg}}}^{n}
by
\mathfrak{X}_{\underline{q}}
, the compactified moduli space of inhomogeneous Gaudin algebras. If
x\in\mathfrak{X}_{\underline{q}}
, we denote the corresponding algebra by
\mathcal{A}(x;\underline{q})
. The purpose of this section is to present a homotopy of paths in the space of
\mathfrak{X}_{\underline{q}}
. This will be the main technical ingredient in the proof of Theorem
Theorem
.

6.1.

We call a path
\underline{z}(t):\mathbb{R}_{>0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\text{{reg}}}^{n}
a
collision path
if it satisfies the following four properties:

•

ordered
:
\underline{z}(t)\in\mathbb{R}_{\text{{<}}}^{n}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,

•

monotonicity
: the functions
z_{i}(t)
are monotonic for
1\leq i\leq n
,

•

limiting behaviour
:
\lim_{t\to 0}z_{i}(t)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}z_{i}(t)=\infty
for all
1\leq i\leq n
,

•

asymptotic ordering
:
\lim_{t\to 0}z_{i+1}(t)/z_{i}(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}z_{i+1}(t)-z_{i}(t)=\infty
for all
1\leq i<n
.

These conditions ensure that

•

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{A}(\underline{z}(t);\underline{q})=\mathcal{A}(\underline{q})^{\otimes n}
(see Section
3.7
),

•

\lim_{t\to 0}\mathcal{A}(\underline{z}(t);\underline{q})
is the algebra generated by
\Delta^{n}\mathcal{A}(\underline{q})
and
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{M}_{n}
(see Section
3.9
).

Example
.

An example of a collision path is
z_{i}(t)=t^{n-i+1}(1+t^{2})^{i-1}
. If we want this to pass through a specific point
(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})
, at say
t=1
for example, we can take the path
z_{i}(t)=2^{1-i}z_{i}t^{n-i+1}(1+t^{2})^{i-1}
.

Fix a choice of collision path. Let
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
. We can now define a path
\gamma:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{\underline{q}}
given by
\gamma(t)=\mathcal{A}(\underline{z}(t);\underline{q})
when
t\neq 0,\infty
, and given by the limiting algebras at the start and end points. This is the path whose parallel transport will be studied in Theorem
Theorem
.

6.2.
Remark

When
\underline{q}
is regular, the moduli space
\mathfrak{X}_{\underline{q}}
is expected to be isomorphic to the moduli space of framed, stable, genus zero curves with
n+1
marked points (see
[
Hal+20
, Remark 10.20]
). These are chains of genus zero curves, with points marked by
1,2,\ldots,n
and
\infty
, and a choice of nonzero tangent vector at
\infty
.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{X}_{\underline{q}}
is indeed isomorphic to this moduli space, then the path
\gamma
corresponds to a path starting at the curve
\gamma(0)
with
n-1
components arranged linearly, the labels
1,2
on the first component,
i+1
on the
i^{\text{th}}
component, and
\infty
on the final component. For
0<t<\infty
, the curve
\gamma(t)
is a single component with labels at the pointed determined by
z(t)
. For
t=\infty
, the curve
\gamma(\infty)
is again
n
components, this time, all sharing a single nodal point marked
\infty
.
Now we would like to apply induction on
n
to this path later. The moduli space of curves interpretation suggests how to do this. We use a different path
\gamma_{0}
. We start at the same stable curve
\gamma_{0}(0)=\gamma(0)
, but now move only the point marked by
n
. That is we move through stable curves
\gamma_{0}(t)
with
n-1
components, arranged linearly, the first marked by
1,2
and the
i^{\text{th}}
marked by
i+1
, but now the last component marked by
n
and
\infty
.
We ask that the
n^{\text{th}}
marked point collides with
\infty
when
t=1
, that means,
\gamma_{0}(1)
is the same stable curve as
\gamma_{0}(0)
except that
\infty
labels the node between the final two components, and
n
labels a point on the final component. For
1<t<\infty
we then inductively use a collision path to move the marked points
1,2,\ldots,n-1
to
\infty
to arrive at
\gamma_{0}(\infty)=\gamma(\infty)
.
In the moduli space of curves, it is clear these two paths are homotopy equivalent. However since we do not know that
\mathfrak{X}_{\underline{q}}
is indeed isomorphic to this space, we cannot use this fact. The following proves that the corresponding paths in
\mathfrak{X}_{\underline{q}}
are homotopy equivalent directly.

6.3.

The idea of a collision path is that it parametrises
n
particles that collide at zero and infinity in a specified order, simultaneously. Our aim now, is to present a new path in
\mathfrak{X}_{\underline{q}}
, where the
n^{\text{th}}
particle is sent from zero to infinity first, and then the remaining particles follow. This will allow us to use induction when calculating the parallel transport along a collision path. We show below that this new path is homotopy equivalent to
\gamma
. So the strategy can be summarised as writing down what would be an explicit homotopy in the moduli space of curves and showing explicitly that it is in fact a homotopy in
\mathfrak{X}_{\underline{q}}
.

6.4.

Define a deformation
\underline{z}:(0,1]\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\text{{reg}}}^{n}
of our fixed collision path. For
1\leq i\leq n-1
let

\displaystyle z_{i}(s,t)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}z_{i}(st)&\text{when }0\leq t\leq 1,\\
z_{i}(t-1+s)&\text{when }t>1,\end{cases}

and let

\displaystyle z_{n}(s,t)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{2-t}{1-t+s}z_{n}(t)&\text{when }0\leq t\leq 1,\\
s^{-1}z_{n}(t)&\text{when }t>1,\end{cases}

Lemma
.

Fix

. Then

is a collision path.

Proof.

We first observe that the path is continuous. Now we check the four properties defining collision paths.

To see the ordering property, clearly

for any fixed

where the first inequality follows by monotonicity, the second by the ordering, and the third since

Monotonicity follows from the observation that if

\underline{z}(t)

∎

∎

8.10.

We can also describe the left CM-cells and the two-sided cells.

Corollary
.

(1)

The left Calogero-Moser cells for
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
agree with the right Kazhdan–Lusztig cells for
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, both being described by
w\sim^{L}w^{\prime}
if and only if
Q(w)=Q(w^{\prime})
.

(2)

The two-sided Calogero-Moser cells for
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
agree with the two-sided Kazhdan–Lusztig cells for
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, both being described by
w\sim^{LR}w^{\prime}
if and only the partition underlying
P(w)
is the same as the partition underlying
P(w^{\prime})
.

Proof.

(1) For the left cells we use the path
\tilde{\lambda}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
given by
\tilde{\lambda}(t)=(t,\underline{z},(1-t)\underline{q})
, with
\underline{z}
and
\underline{q}
as before. We can then repeat the argument of Theorem
Theorem
.

Alternatively let the projection of
\tilde{\lambda}
to
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be denoted by
\lambda
. As explained in
[
BR17
, Proposition 5.6.1]
there is an automorphism
\sigma_{H}
of
R
which restricts to automorphisms of
P
and
Q
, which are induced from the mapping

(c,\underline{q},\underline{z})\mapsto(c,-\underline{q},\underline{z}).

Note that
-\underline{q}
is no longer an element of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{{<}}}^{n}
, rather
w_{0}(\-uq)
is, where
w_{0}=(1\,n)(2\,n-1)\cdots\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the longest word. The mapping
\sigma_{H}\circ\lambda
is just the mapping
\gamma
introduced in
8.8
.

We need to understand when the lifts of
\lambda
to
\operatorname{Spec}P
beginning at the points
(0,(w(\underline{z}),\underline{q})\Delta\mathfrak{S}_{n})
and
(0,(w^{\prime}(\underline{z}),\underline{q})\Delta\mathfrak{S}_{n})
collide at the endpoint. This happens if and only if it happens on applying
\sigma_{H}
. In other words, this happens if and only if the lifts of
\gamma
to
\operatorname{Spec}P
beginning at the points
\sigma_{H}((0,(w(\underline{z}),\underline{q})\Delta\mathfrak{S}_{n}))
and
\sigma_{H}(0,(w^{\prime}(\underline{z}),\underline{q})\Delta\mathfrak{S}_{n})
collide at the endpoint. But

\displaystyle\sigma_{H}((0,(w(\underline{z}),\underline{q})\Delta\mathfrak{S}_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,(-\underline{q},w(\underline{z}))\Delta\mathfrak{S}_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,(w^{-1}(-\underline{q}),\underline{z})\Delta\mathfrak{S}_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,(w^{-1}w_{0}(\underline{q}),\underline{z})\Delta\mathfrak{S}_{n})

where, up to homotopy, we can assume that
w_{0}(\underline{q})=-\underline{q}
. Thus
\lambda_{w}(1)=\lambda_{w^{\prime}}(1)
if and only if
\gamma_{w^{-1}w_{0}}(1)=\gamma_{w^{\prime-1}w_{0}}(1)
. By Theorem
Theorem
this happens if and only if
P(w^{-1}w_{0})=P(w^{\prime-1}w_{0})
. Equivalently
P((w_{0}w)^{-1})=P((w_{0}w^{\prime})^{-1})
. Since
P(\tau^{-1})=Q(\tau)
this is the same as
Q(w_{0}w)=Q(w_{0}w^{\prime})
. By
[
Sta99

Q(w_{0}w)=(\textsf{evac}Q(w))^{t}
where

is the evacuation procedure on tableaux, a bijection. It follows that
Q(w_{0}w)=Q(w_{0}w^{\prime})
if and only if

, as claimed.

(2) Choosing the path
\mu(t)=(t,(1-t)\underline{z},(1-t)\underline{q})
ensures that the two-sided cells are unions of left cells and also right cells. Thus if

and

give rise to the same partition, then we can find

such that

and

. It follows that

and

are in the same two-sided call. On the other hand, thanks to

, Theorem 14.4.1]

. Hence they cannot have any more elements in them, confirming the final claim.
∎

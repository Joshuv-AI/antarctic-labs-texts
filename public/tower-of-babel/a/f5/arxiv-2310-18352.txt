Theory of Rayleigh-Brillouin optical activity light scattering applicable to chiral liquids
Robert P. Cameron, Emmanouil I. Alexakis, Aidan S. Arnold, Duncan McArthur

Abstract
It has long been understood that dilute samples of chiral molecules such as rarefied gases should exhibit Rayleigh optical activity. We extend the existing theory by accounting for molecular dynamics and correlations, thus obtaining a more general theory of Rayleigh-Brillouin optical activity applicable to dense samples such as neat liquids.

It was predicted a little over fifty years ago by Barron and collaborators that chiral molecules should exhibit Rayleigh optical activity (RayOA): differential Rayleigh scattering with respect to left- and right-handed circular polarisations of light

;

;
Berne and Pecora 2000
;
Sobel’man 2002
;
Boyd 2003
;
Barron 2004

In this paper, we extend the existing theory of RayOA by accounting for the translational and rotational dynamics of the molecules and adding scattered fields due to different molecules coherently, thus obtaining a more general theory that describes Rayleigh-Brillouin optical activity (RayBOA). Our theory provides expressions for not only the total intensity of the analysed signal but also the underlying frequency spectrum. It is applicable to dilute samples such as rarefied gases as well as dense samples such as neat liquids in which correlations between molecules are important.

II.6
Dimensionless circular spectral and intensity differentials

RayOA (and by extension RayBOA) can manifest as an intensity difference with respect to left- and right-handed circular polarisation states in the scattered light (scattered circular polarisation or SCP)
Atkins and Barron 1969
, the incident light (incident circular polarisation or ICP)
Barron and Buckingham 1971
or both simultaneously (dual circular polarisation or DCP
I
)
Nafie and Freedman 1989
.

As convenient measures of RayBOA, we identify dimensionless circular spectral differentials
\bbDelta=\bbDelta(\Omega)
of the form

\displaystyle\bbDelta

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{I}^{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}-\mathbb{I}^{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{d}}}{\mathbb{I}^{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}+\mathbb{I}^{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{d}}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}\cos\theta+\mathbb{C}\cos^{2}\theta}{\mathbb{D}+\mathbb{E}\cos\theta+\mathbb{F}\cos^{2}\theta}

(18)

and circular intensity differentials
\Delta
of the form

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\frac{{I}^{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}-{I}^{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{d}}}{{I}^{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}+{I}^{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{d}}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{A}(\Omega)\mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{B}(\Omega)\mathrm{d}\Omega\cos\theta+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{C}(\Omega)\mathrm{d}\Omega\cos^{2}\theta}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{D}(\Omega)\mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\Omega)\mathrm{d}\Omega\cos\theta+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{F}(\Omega)\mathrm{d}\Omega\cos^{2}\theta},

(19)

where
\mathrm{a}
and
\mathrm{c}
refer to analysed polarisation states of the scattered light,
\mathrm{b}
and
\mathrm{d}
refer to polarisation states of the incident light and
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
,
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}(\Omega)
,
\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{C}(\Omega)
,
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}(\Omega)
,
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{E}(\Omega)
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}(\Omega)
are coefficients that depend on the specific configuration being considered. Substituting (
17
) into (
18
) and (

) and making use of basic symmetry arguments, we obtain the results listed in Table

with

\displaystyle\bbalpha^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle\frac{1}{9}\tilde{\alpha}^{\ast}_{\alpha\alpha}(t)\tilde{\alpha}_{\beta\beta}(t+\tau)\rangle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\Omega-\omega)\tau}\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\bbbeta^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle\frac{1}{2}[3\tilde{\alpha}^{\ast}_{\alpha\beta}(t)\tilde{\alpha}_{\alpha\beta}(t+\tau)-\tilde{\alpha}^{\ast}_{\alpha\alpha}(t)\tilde{\alpha}_{\beta\beta}(t+\tau)]\rangle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\Omega-\omega)\tau}\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\bbalpha\mathbb{G}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle\frac{1}{18}[\tilde{\alpha}^{\ast}_{\alpha\alpha}(t)\tilde{G}^{\prime}_{\beta\beta}(t+\tau)+\tilde{\alpha}_{\alpha\alpha}(t+\tau)\tilde{G}^{\prime\ast}_{\beta\beta}(t)]\rangle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\Omega-\omega)\tau}\mathrm{d}\tau,

where

For the sake of illustration, let us now evaluate the circular differentials
\bbDelta

\Delta

Considering molecules with quasi-cylindrical symmetry for the sake of simplicity, we take

\displaystyle\alpha^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\alpha\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\Delta\alpha\left(\hat{u}^{(n)}_{\alpha}\hat{u}^{(n)}_{\beta}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\right),

\displaystyle G^{\prime(n)}_{\alpha\beta}

where
\alpha
and
G^{\prime}
are isotropic polarisabilities;
\Delta\alpha
,

and
\Delta A
are polarisability anisotropies and

is a unit vector dictated by the orientation of the

IV
Conclusions

We have presented a theory of RayBOA applicable to dense samples such as neat liquids. There are many possible avenues for future research.

We have evaluated the circular differentials for a toy model of an enantiopure neat liquid. It remains for us to consider more realistic models for a variety of different samples. Molecular dynamics simulations have recently been applied with success to ROA
Brehm and Thomas 2017
;
Yang et al. 2022
;
Michal et al. 2022
and might be developed for RayBOA as well.

We have adopted a microscopic approach, facilitating comparison with the existing theory of RayOA
Atkins and Barron 1969
;
Barron and Buckingham 1971
;
Andrews 1980
;
Hecht and Barron 1994
;
Craig and Thirunamachandran 1998
;
Barron 2004
;
Zuber et al. 2008
;
Cameron and Barnett 2014
;
Cameron and Mackinnon 2018
;
Forbes 2019
;
Forbes and Andrews 2019
. A macroscopic approach is also possible
Smoluchowski 1908
;
Einstein 1910
;
Berne and Pecora 2000
;
Boyd 2003
;
Landau et al. 1984
and might yield new insights. Care will need to be taken with the choice of macroscopic constitutive relations
Barnett and Cameron 2016
;
Ossikovski et al. 2021
, which must include electric dipole-electric quadrupole contributions to describe RayBOA correctly even for isotropic samples.

We have focussed on SCP, ICP and DCP
I
RayBOA for off-resonant illumination of a fluid by planar light. Interesting new features should emerge for illumination near resonance
Barron and Escribano 1985
;
Nafie and Freedman 1989
;
Hecht and Nafie 1990
;
Hecht and Barron 1994
, anisotropic samples
Hecht and Barron 1994
and illumination by structured light
Cameron and Barnett 2014
;
Forbes 2019
;
Forbes and Andrews 2019
;
McArthur et al. 2020
. It should also prove fruitful to consider the influence of static magnetic fields
Barron and Buckingham 1972
, static electric fields
Buckingham and Raab 1975
;
Gassó and Knobelspiesse 2022
and higher-order multipolar contributions
Tinoco Jr. and Williams
Jr. 1984
;
Cameron and Mackinnon 2018
.

A particularly interesting question is whether Rayleigh-Brillouin optical activity can be used to detect chiral (acoustic) phonons in appropriate samples
Zhu et al. 2018
;
Choi et al. 2022
;
Michal et al. 2022
;
Ueda et al. 2023
.

Although our focus in this paper has been on small molecules, similar ideas can be developed for larger scatterers, including large biomolecules
Maestre et al. 1982
;
Tinoco Jr. and Williams
Jr. 1984
;
Gratiet et al. 2020
;
Pan et al. 2022
;
Gassó and Knobelspiesse 2022
.

We will return to these and related tasks elsewhere.

Jet mass distribution in Higgs/vector boson + jet events at hadron colliders with 𝒌 𝒕 {\boldsymbol{k}}_{\boldsymbol{t}} clustering
Naïma Ziani, Kamel Khelifa-Kerfa, Yazid Delenda

Abstract
We address the issues of clustering and non-global logarithms for jet shapes in the process of production of a Higgs/vector boson associated with a single hard jet at hadron colliders. We perform an analytical fixed-order calculation up to second order in the coupling as well as an all-orders estimation for the specific invariant mass distribution of the highest- p t p_{t} jet, for various jet algorithms. Our results are derived in the eikonal (soft) limit and are valid up to next-to-leading logarithmic accuracy. We perform a matching of the resummed distribution to next-to-leading order results from MCFM and compare our findings with the outputs of the Monte Carlo event generators Pythia 8 and Herwig 7 . After accounting for non-perturbative effects we compare our results with available experimental data from the CMS collaboration for the Z Z + jet production. We find good agreement over a wide range of the observable.

Introduction

The invariant mass of a jet is a typical example of a jet shape that plays an important role in the study of the substructure of jets, testing QCD, and identifying new-physics signals. Being sensitive to soft and/or collinear emissions from the parton initiating the jet and from the other incoming and outgoing partons, this observable provides an indispensable mean for probing various aspects that are relevant to achieving better accuracy in QCD calculations. Examples of such aspects include, on the non-perturbative side, hadronisation corrections, underlying event, pile-up interactions, and on the perturbative side, initial and final-state radiation, colour flow, resummation of large logarithms, etc. Analytical calculations for these aspects pave the way for a deeper insight into QCD processes, a better control of theoretical uncertainties, and a precise quantification of missing higher-order contributions and their significance, all of which are issues not very clear in Monte Carlo event generators.

In this paper we shed light on the resummation of large logarithms that arise due to a miscancellation of soft and collinear singularities between real emissions and their corresponding virtual corrections. The convergence of the perturbative series, in the invariant jet mass (
m_{j}
) distribution, is spoiled by the presence of large logarithms in the ratio of the jet mass and its transverse momentum
p_{t}
,
L=\ln(m_{j}/p_{t})
, at each order in perturbation theory. In the exponent of the integrated distribution, these logarithms take the form
\alpha_{s}^{n}L^{m}
, with
\alpha_{s}
being the strong coupling constant and
m\leq(n+1)
, and thus they require an all-orders resummation. A next-to-leading logarithmic (NLL) resummation ensures that all single logarithms of the form
\alpha_{s}^{n}L^{n}
are resummed, in addition to the leading (double) logarithms (LL)
\alpha_{s}^{n}L^{n+1}
.

The jet mass is a non-global observable, i.e., an exclusive observable that is sensitive only to gluon emissions which end up inside the jet. To ensure a proper NLL resummation then its distribution must carefully be treated for a class of large single logarithms known as non-global logarithms (NGLs), which are related to secondary non-Abelian emissions of soft gluons
Dasgupta:2001sh
;
Dasgupta:2002bw
. Furthermore, another type of large single logarithms known as clustering logarithms (CLs)
Banfi:2005gj
;
Delenda:2006nf
, related to primary gluon emissions off the hard Born configuration, needs to be resummed when the jets are reconstructed using jet algorithms such as
k_{t}

Catani:1993hr
;
Ellis:1993tq
and Cambridge-Aachen (C-A)
Dokshitzer:1997in
;
Wobisch:1998wt
. The anti-
k_{t}
clustering algorithm

is known to cause no CLs (see for instance refs.
Banfi:2010pa
;
Dasgupta:2012hg
). The full resummation of both NGLs and CLs has thus far proven to be a formidable challenge. The resummation of NGLs is usually achieved numerically via a Monte Carlo approach

;

Setup

2.1
Processes and kinematics

In this paper we are interested in the calculation of both CLs and NGLs at single logarithmic accuracy, for the jet mass distribution in the process of production of a single jet associated with a vector (
W/Z/\gamma
) or Higgs boson at hadron colliders. For this purpose, it suffices to consider the eikonal (soft) approximation in the squared matrix elements for the emission of gluons. The emitted gluons are assumed to be strongly ordered in transverse momenta, i.e.,
k_{tn}\ll\cdots\ll k_{t2}\ll k_{t1}\ll p_{t}
, where
k_{ti}
is the transverse momentum of the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
emission and
p_{t}
is that of the outgoing hard jet. The latter ordering simplifies the calculations of the emission amplitudes while being sufficient for capturing the single logarithmic CLs and NGLs.

For a vector boson + one jet production in hadron collisions, there are three partonic channels that contribute to the Born process, namely:
q\bar{q}\to g
,
qg\to q
, and
\bar{q}g\to\bar{q}
. For
W^{\pm}
production, flavour changing needs to be taken into account at the Born level, but this does not affect the QCD structure of initial and final-state radiation. As for the Higgs + one jet process there are four partonic channels to be considered. These are:
qg\to qH
,
\bar{q}g\to\bar{q}H
,
q\bar{q}\to Hg
, and
gg\to Hg
. As far as QCD calculations are concerned all mentioned channels, whether for Higgs or vector boson production, are in fact identical as they all involve three hard coloured (QCD) partons and a colour-neutral boson. This means that the resummation of the jet mass distribution is essentially identical in all of the said channels, with differences pertaining to just the Born cross-section and the associated colour factors for the various channels. We note that the relevant total cross-sections have been calculated up to next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO): Higgs + jet in refs.
Boughezal:2015dra
;
Boughezal:2015aha
;
Caola:2015wna
;
Chen:2016zka
,
Z
+ jet in refs.
Ridder:2015dxa
;
Boughezal:2015ded
,
W
+ jet in ref.
Boughezal:2015dva
, and
\gamma
+ jet in ref.
Campbell:2016lzl
.

2.2
Jet mass observable and jet algorithms

We study the normalised (squared) invariant mass of the outgoing hard jet
j
defined by

\begin{split}\varrho&\equiv\frac{1}{p_{t}^{2}}\left(p_{j}+\sum_{i\in\mathrm{jet}}k_{i}\right)^{2}\approx\frac{2}{p_{t}^{2}}\sum_{i\in\mathrm{jet}}k_{i}\cdot p_{j}=\sum_{i\in\mathrm{jet}}\varrho_{i}\,,\\
\varrho_{i}&\equiv\,\frac{2\,k_{i}\cdot p_{j}}{p_{t}^{2}}=2\,\frac{k_{ti}}{p_{t}}\left[\cosh(\eta_{i}-y)-\cos(\phi_{i}-\varphi)\right],\end{split}

(2)

where the sum is over all emitted soft gluons which end up inside the hard jet after the application of a jet algorithm on the final state partons. Notice that we are considering massless quarks and that the soft approximation has been assumed in the above equation whereby
p_{j}\cdot k_{i}\gg k_{\ell}\cdot k_{m}
.

The
k_{t}
, C-A and anti-
k_{t}
jet algorithms work as follows. For each pair
(im)
of hadrons in the final state one defines a distance

\displaystyle d_{im}=\min(k_{ti}^{2p},k_{tm}^{2p})\left[(\eta_{i}-\eta_{m})^{2}+(\phi_{i}-\phi_{m})^{2}\right],

(3a)

and for each single hadron a beam distance

\displaystyle d_{i}=k_{ti}^{2p}\,R^{2}\,,

(3b)

for some fixed jet radius parameter
R
. Here the parameter
p=1,0,-1
for
k_{t}
, C-A, and anti-
k_{t}
clustering, respectively. If the smallest of all of these distances is
d_{im}
, then particles
i
and
m
are combined into a single particle with four-momentum
p_{i}+p_{m}
, whereas if the smallest is
d_{i}
then particle
i
is considered as a jet and is removed from the list of particles. This procedure is iterated until one is left only with jets in the final state.

For the
k_{t}
algorithm, and in the regime of strongly-ordered emissions, the clustering of particles starts with the softest real gluon. Then, in a given event this softest gluon is dragged towards the next-to-softest real parton within a circle of radius
R
in the
(\eta,\phi)
plane. If no such harder parton exists then this softest gluon is considered as a jet and is removed from the list of partons. The process is then repeated until no particles are left. When clustering two partons together, the resulting pseudo-jet is essentially aligned along the direction of the harder, and its four-momentum is just that of the harder parton.

For the anti-
k_{t}
algorithm, on the other hand, clustering starts with the hardest particle, and hence it works in an apposite way to
k_{t}
clustering. For the C-A algorithm, only geometric distances between partons in the
(\eta,\phi)
plane decide how clustering happens. Particles which are closest to each other get clustered first.

2.3
Jet mass distribution

In what follows we calculate at NLL accuracy the jet mass distribution for a given channel
\delta
, defined by (following the notation of refs.
Dasgupta:2012hg
;
Banfi:2004yd
)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\Sigma_{\delta}(\rho)}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}}=\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma_{\delta}}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}\varrho}\,\mathrm{d}\varrho\,,

(4)

where
\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma_{\delta}/\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}\varrho
is the differential cross-section with respect to both the Born configuration
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
and the jet mass observable
\varrho
. Details of the differential Born configuration
\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
are discussed further in appendix
A
. The integrated jet mass distribution is obtained by integrating
\mathrm{d}\Sigma_{\delta}(\rho)/\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
over
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
with some chosen kinematical cuts (which we denote by
\Xi_{\mathcal{B}}
), and summing over all Born channels. That is

\displaystyle\Sigma(\rho)=\sum_{\delta}\int\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\Sigma_{\delta}(\rho)}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}}\,\Xi_{\mathcal{B}}\,.

(5)

Following ref.
Dasgupta:2012hg
, we write eq. (
4
) in the region
\rho\ll 1
in the factorised form

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Sigma_{\delta}(\rho)}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{0,\delta}}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}}\,f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}(\rho)\,C_{\mathcal{B},\delta}(\rho)\,,

(6)

where
\mathrm{d}\sigma_{0,\delta}/\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
is the differential partonic Born cross-section for channel
\delta
(see appendix
A
) and the factor
C_{\mathcal{B},\delta}(\rho)
depends on the Born kinematics and has the perturbative expansion

C_{\mathcal{B},\delta}(\rho)=1+\alpha_{s}\,C_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{(1)}(\rho)+\alpha_{s}^{2}\,C_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{(2)}(\rho)+\cdots\,,

(7)

where
C_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{(n)}(\rho)
are channel-dependent terms that correct the resummation for non-logarithmically-enhanced terms. The
\rho
-dependent function
f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}(\rho)
resums all the large logarithms. It has the form
Banfi:2004yd

f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}(\rho)=\exp\left[L\,\tilde{g}_{1}(\alpha_{s}L)+\tilde{g}_{2}(\alpha_{s}L)+\alpha_{s}\,\tilde{g}_{3}(\alpha_{s}L)+\cdots\right],

(8)

where the function
L\,\tilde{g}_{1}
resums the leading (double) logarithms (LL) of the form
\alpha_{s}^{n}L^{n+1}
,
\tilde{g}_{2}
resums next-to-leading (single) logarithms (NLL) of the form
\alpha_{s}^{n}L^{n}
, and
\alpha_{s}\,\tilde{g}_{3}
resums next-to-next-to-leading logarithms (NNLL) of the form
\alpha_{s}^{n}L^{n-1}
, and so on, with
L=\ln(R^{2}/\rho)
. The LL function
\tilde{g}_{1}
receives contributions from soft-collinear emissions from the parton initiating the jet and depends on its colour Casimir scalar. The NLL function
\tilde{g}_{2}
receives contributions from various sources:

(a)

hard-collinear emissions from the outgoing hard parton,

(b)

soft wide-angle emissions from all hard partons,

(c)

starting at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
, NGLs from soft wide-angle correlated secondary emissions, and

(d)

CLs, when jet algorithms other than anti-
k_{t}
are implemented for jet reconstruction, from soft wide-angle primary emissions off the hard partons. These again appear starting from
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
.

The whole function
\tilde{g}_{1}
and parts of
\tilde{g}_{2}
, namely contributions (a) and (b) stated above, have been determined in ref.
Dasgupta:2012hg
for the anti-
k_{t}
algorithm. The exact same result also applies for the case of
k_{t}
and C-A clustering as the effect of jet algorithms first appears at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
. Our task is to determine the other two contributions to
\tilde{g}_{2}
, namely (c) NGLs and (d) CLs, for
k_{t}
and C-A algorithms. Before doing so, we review in the next section the basic calculations that lead to the determination of
\tilde{g}_{1}
and contributions (a) and (b) of
\tilde{g}_{2}
.

One-gluon emission

3.1
Fixed-order calculation

In this section we compute the jet mass distribution at leading order in QCD and present the all-orders resummed result. Our calculations are valid in the eikonal approximation and accurate up to NLL accuracy. First, we define the following antenna functions relevant for the squared matrix elements for the emission of soft gluons

\displaystyle w_{\alpha\beta}^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{ti}^{2}}{2}\,\frac{p_{\alpha}\cdot p_{\beta}}{(p_{\alpha}\cdot k_{i})\,(k_{i}\cdot p_{\beta})}\,,

(9a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\alpha\beta}^{ik}

\displaystyle=w^{i}_{\alpha\beta}\left(w^{k}_{\alpha i}+w^{k}_{i\beta}-w^{k}_{\alpha\beta}\right).

\Theta\left(\xi_{1}\,R^{2}\,r_{1}^{2}-\rho\right)
. We now perform the integrations for each dipole.

•

The dipole
(ab)
:

The contribution of the in-in dipole
(ab)
to eq. (
14
) at single logarithmic accuracy may be written as follows

\displaystyle f_{\mathcal{B},(ab)}^{(1)}(\rho)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{C}_{ab}\,\bar{\alpha}_{s}\,\frac{R^{2}}{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi_{1}}{\xi_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}r_{1}^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}}{2\pi}\,\Theta\left(1-r_{1}^{2}\right)\times

\displaystyle\quad\times\Theta\left[\xi_{1}\,R^{2}\,r_{1}^{2}-\rho\right]

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{C}_{ab}\,\bar{\alpha}_{s}\,\frac{R^{2}}{2}\,L\,,

(18)

with
L=\ln(R^{2}/\rho)
being the large logarithm that we aim to resum. This contribution corresponds to soft wide-angle radiation from the in-in dipole into the interior of the measured outgoing jet, and is thus free from collinear logarithms.

•

The dipole
(aj)
:

For the in-jet dipole
(aj)
eq. (
14
) reads

\displaystyle f_{\mathcal{B},(aj)}^{(1)}=-\mathcal{C}_{aj}\,\bar{\alpha}_{s}\,\frac{R^{2}}{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi_{1}}{\xi_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}r_{1}^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}}{2\pi}\,\Theta\left[\xi_{1}\,R^{2}\,r_{1}^{2}-\rho\right]\times

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\exp(R\,r_{1}\cos\theta_{1})}{\cosh(R\,r_{1}\cos\theta_{1})-\cos(R\,r_{1}\sin\theta_{1})}\,\Theta\left(1-r_{1}^{2}\right).

(19)

Note here that the step function
\Theta\left[\xi_{1}\,R^{2}\,r_{1}^{2}-\rho\right]
which restricts
\xi_{1}>\rho/(R^{2}\,r_{1}^{2})
also implies that
R^{2}\,r_{1}^{2}>\rho
since
\xi_{1}<1
. This serves as a collinear regulator for the integral over
r_{1}
, which would otherwise diverge, resulting in an overall double logarithm as well as a single logarithm. Evaluating the
\xi_{1}
integration yields

\displaystyle f_{\mathcal{B},(aj)}^{(1)}(\rho)=-\mathcal{C}_{aj}\,\bar{\alpha}_{s}\,\frac{R^{2}}{2}\int_{\rho/R^{2}}^{1}\ln\frac{R^{2}\,r_{1}^{2}}{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}r_{1}^{2}\,\times

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}}{2\pi}\,\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\exp(R\,r_{1}\cos\theta_{1})}{\cosh(R\,r_{1}\cos\theta_{1})-\cos(R\,r_{1}\sin\theta_{1})}\,.

(20)

We perform the integration over
\theta_{1}
by expanding the integrand as a series in
R
and neglecting higher-order terms that have small coefficients. Thus we find

\displaystyle\frac{R^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}}{2\pi}\,\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\exp(R\,r_{1}\cos\theta_{1})}{\cosh(R\,r_{1}\cos\theta_{1})-\cos(R\,r_{1}\sin\theta_{1})}=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\,r_{1}^{2}}+\frac{R^{2}}{8}+\frac{r_{1}^{2}\,R^{4}}{288}+\mathcal{O}(R^{8})\,.

(21)

The first term in this expansion corresponds to soft
and
collinear emissions from the outgoing hard leg
(j)
into its own jet. It contributes at the double logarithmic level, giving the result

f_{\mathcal{B},(aj)}^{(1)\mathrm{DL}}=-\mathcal{C}_{aj}\,\bar{\alpha}_{s}\,\frac{L^{2}}{4}\,,

(22)

which is independent of the jet radius (other than in the argument of the logarithm). The other terms in the expansion (
21
) are purely soft wide-angle contributions, hence we can set
\rho\to 0
in the lower limit of integration over
r_{1}^{2}
, and throw away the sub-leading
\ln r_{1}^{2}
term in the integrand. Performing the integration we obtain

f_{\mathcal{B},(aj)}^{(1)\mathrm{SL}}=-\mathcal{C}_{aj}\,\bar{\alpha}_{s}\,L\left(\frac{1}{8}\,R^{2}+\frac{1}{576}\,R^{4}+\mathcal{O}(R^{8})\right).

(23)

We note that the coefficient of
R^{8}
in this expression is vanishingly small (
\mathcal{O}(10^{-7})
).

•

The dipole
(bj)
:

For the other in-jet dipole
(bj)
the only differences relative to the dipole
(aj)
are the colour factor
\mathcal{C}_{aj}\to\mathcal{C}_{bj}
and a minus sign to be inserted in the exponent of the exponential in the integrand of eq. (

3.1
), i.e.,
\exp(R\,r_{1}\cos\theta_{1})\to\exp(-R\,r_{1}\cos\theta_{1})
. This is equivalent to a change

(the rest of the integral is invariant under this change). This actually does not produce any differences in the integration since only even powers of

appear in the results (

We can therefore write the assembled soft-collinear double-logarithmic result as

f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{(1)\mathrm{DL}}=-\left(\mathcal{C}_{aj}+\mathcal{C}_{bj}\right)\bar{\alpha}_{s}\,\frac{L^{2}}{4}\,,

(24)

and the soft wide-angle single-logarithmic contribution as

f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{(1)\mathrm{SL}}=-\bar{\alpha}_{s}\,L\left[\mathcal{C}_{ab}\,\frac{R^{2}}{2}+\left(\mathcal{C}_{aj}+\mathcal{C}_{bj}\right)h(R)\right],

(25)

with

h(R)=\frac{1}{8}\,R^{2}+\frac{1}{576}\,R^{4}+\mathcal{O}(R^{8})\,.

(26)

This result was first derived in ref.
Dasgupta:2012hg
, and it actually exponentiates to all orders. However, the running coupling, whose argument is the invariant transverse momentum
\kappa_{t1,(i\ell)}^{2}=k_{t1}^{2}/w_{i\ell}^{1}
of the emission
k_{1}
with respect to the emitting dipole
(i\ell)

Catani:1999ss
, contributes at higher orders and modifies the single logarithmic contribution
f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{(1)\mathrm{SL}}
with a change

-\bar{\alpha}_{s}\,L\to\frac{1}{2\pi\beta_{0}}\ln\left(1-2\,\alpha_{s}\,\beta_{0}\,L\right),

(27)

where
\beta_{0}
is the one-loop coefficient of the QCD
\beta

is more subtle. In fact the running coupling introduces additional single logarithmic components which depend on the renormalisation scheme. We discuss the all-orders resummed result in the following subsection.

3.2
Resummed global result

The full NLL-resummed global form factor
f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{\mathrm{global}}(\rho)
has been computed in ref.
Dasgupta:2012hg
(eqs. (3.3), (3.11) and appendix C therein). The interested reader is referred to the latter reference, together with ref.
Banfi:2004yd
, for details. Here we only state its form, which is given by
Dasgupta:2012hg

\displaystyle f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{\mathrm{global}}(\rho)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left[1+\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\delta}(\rho)\right]}\exp\left[-\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(\rho)-\gamma_{E}\,\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(\rho)\right],

(28)

with
\gamma_{E}
the Euler-Mascheroni constant (
\gamma_{E}\approx 0.577
) and
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function. The radiator
\mathcal{R}
and its derivative with respect to
L
,
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
, are presented in appendix
B
. We note that the global form factor is identical for all jet algorithms. We also note that the expression of
f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{\mathrm{global}}(\rho)
may be deduced from the general form presented in ref.
Banfi:2004yd
as we show in appendix
B
.

In the next section we treat the case of two-gluon emission where clustering and non-global logarithms first pop-up.

4

In the eikonal approximation, the factorised squared amplitude for the emission of two real gluons

and

\mathcal{W}_{12,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}=\mathcal{W}_{1,\delta}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}\,\mathcal{W}_{2,\delta}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}+\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{12,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}\,,

(29)

where the one-loop amplitude squared
\mathcal{W}_{i,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}
, which builds up the
reducible
part of the above two-gluon squared amplitude (first term on the right-hand side), is given in eq. (
10
), and the
irreducible
contribution
\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{12,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}
reads

\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{12,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}=\mathrm{C_{A}}\sum_{(i\ell)\in\Delta_{\delta}}\mathcal{C}_{i\ell}\,\mathcal{A}_{i\ell}^{12}\,.

(30)

The virtual corrections at this order are

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{12,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{V}}}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{W}_{12,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}\,,

(31a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{12,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{V}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{W}_{1,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}\,\mathcal{W}_{2,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}\,,

(31b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{12,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{V}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{V}}}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{W}_{12,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{V}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}\,.

(31c)

Following ref.
Khelifa-Kerfa:2015mma
, and implementing the measurement-operator method, we write the jet mass distribution at this order as

\displaystyle f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{(2)}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\int_{\xi_{1}>\xi_{2}}\mathrm{d}\Pi_{12}\,\Xi^{\mathrm{p}}(k_{1},k_{2})\,\mathcal{W}_{1,\delta}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}\,\mathcal{W}_{2,\delta}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}-

\displaystyle-\int_{\xi_{1}>\xi_{2}}\mathrm{d}\Pi_{12}\,\Xi^{\mathrm{NG}}(k_{1},k_{2})\,\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{12,\delta}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}\,,

(32)

with phase-space factor
\mathrm{d}\Pi_{12}=\mathrm{d}\Phi_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\Phi_{2}\,\Theta(\varrho_{1}-\rho)\,\Theta(\varrho_{2}-\rho)
. Here, the first integral produces the primary-emission contribution, which contains CLs, and the second integral gives NGLs. The functions
\Xi^{\mathrm{p}}
, where
\mathrm{p}
stands for primary, and
\Xi^{\mathrm{NG}}
, where
\mathrm{NG}
stands for non-global, result from the application of the jet algorithm and restrict the angular integration regions for gluons
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
.

4.1
Clustering logarithms

In this subsection we focus on the primary-emission integral in eq. (
4
) and leave the treatment of the correlated-emission NGLs term to the next subsection. The primary-emission contribution may be split into two parts. The first is the global component which results from integrating both gluons within the measured jet region. This has, however, been accounted for by the all-orders resummed formula (
28
) discussed in the previous section, and will thus be skipped here. The second part is related to the way jet algorithms cluster gluons and results in large single logarithms that are referred to as clustering logarithms
Banfi:2005gj
;
Delenda:2006nf
;
Delenda:2012mm
. These logarithms are a result of miscancellation between real emissions and virtual corrections. The key point is that while real gluons may be dragged into/out of the jet by other real gluons and thus get clustered together, virtual gluons can neither drag nor get dragged. We note that CLs are totally absent when jets are reconstructed using the anti-
k_{t}
algorithm. At two loops, the C-A and
k_{t}
algorithms produce identical CLs, but they start to differ at higher orders as was shown in ref.
Delenda:2012mm
.

To perform the first integral in eq. (
4
) we begin by simplifying the clustering function
\Xi^{\mathrm{p}}(k_{1},k_{2})
. To this end, we introduce the same change of variables as in eq. (
16
) such that
(\eta_{1},\phi_{1})\to(r_{1},\theta_{1})
and
(\eta_{2},\phi_{2})\to(r_{2},\theta_{2})
. Note that the upper limit of
r_{i}
is
\pi/\left(R\,|\sin\theta_{i}|\right)
since we have
-\pi<\phi_{i}-\varphi<\pi
. We then have for the
k_{t}
clustering algorithm
Delenda:2006nf
;
Delenda:2012mm

\displaystyle\Xi^{\mathrm{p}}(k_{1},k_{2})=\Theta\left(R^{2}-d_{1j}\right)\Theta\left(R^{2}-d_{2j}\right)+

\displaystyle+\Theta\left(d_{1j}-R^{2}\right)\Theta\left(R^{2}-d_{2j}\right)\Theta\left(d_{2j}-d_{12}\right)

\displaystyle=\Theta\left(1-r_{1}^{2}\right)\Theta\left(1-r_{2}^{2}\right)+

\displaystyle+\Theta\left(r_{1}^{2}-1\right)\Theta\left(1-r_{2}^{2}\right)\Theta\left(2\,r_{2}\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})-r_{1}\right),

(33)

where the algorithm distances
d_{im}
have been defined in eq. (
3
). The first term exactly reproduces half the square of the one-loop result (
14
), i.e.

1/2!\,[f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{(1)}]^{2}
, and persists at higher orders as
1/n!\,[f_{\mathcal{B},\delta}^{(1)}]^{n}
. This signifies that the one-loop result simply exponentiates into the global form factor discussed before. It is the second term, the CLs term, that we shall focus on in the remainder of this subsection.

We write the CLs contribution at this order as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2,\delta}(\rho)=\bar{\alpha}_{s}^{2}\,R^{4}\int_{\xi_{1}>\xi_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi_{1}}{\xi_{1}}\,r_{1}\,\mathrm{d}r_{1}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}}{2\pi}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\,r_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r_{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\,\times

\displaystyle\times\Theta\left(r_{1}^{2}-1\right)\Theta\left(1-r_{2}^{2}\right)\Theta\left(2\,r_{2}\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})-r_{1}\right)\times

\displaystyle\Theta(\varrho_{1}-\rho)\Theta(\varrho_{2}-\rho)\left[\sum_{(ik)\in\Delta_{\delta}}\mathcal{C}_{ik}\,w_{ik}^{1}\right]\left[\sum_{(\ell m)\in\Delta_{\delta}}\mathcal{C}_{\ell m}w_{\ell m}^{2}\right].

(34)

The expressions inside the square brackets are the one-loop eikonal amplitudes squared (
10
) for gluons
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, respectively. To single logarithmic accuracy the
\xi
integrations factor out from the rest of the integrals yielding the result
L^{2}/2!
, and we are left with

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2,\delta}(\rho)=\frac{1}{2!}\,\bar{\alpha}_{s}^{2}\,L^{2}\,\mathcal{F}_{2}^{\delta}(R)\,,

(35)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}^{\delta}(R)=\sum_{(ik)\in\Delta_{\delta}}\sum_{(\ell m)\in\Delta_{\delta}}\mathcal{C}_{ik}\,\mathcal{C}_{\ell m}\,R^{4}\int_{0}^{1}r_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r_{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\,\times

\displaystyle\times\int_{1}^{2}r_{1}\,\mathrm{d}r_{1}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}}{2\pi}\,\Theta\left(2\,r_{2}\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})-r_{1}\right)\,w_{ik}^{1}\,w_{\ell\,m}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(ik)\in\Delta_{\delta}}\mathcal{C}_{ik}^{2}\,\mathcal{F}_{2,\mathrm{dip}}^{(ik)}+\sum_{(ik)\neq(\ell m)\in\Delta_{\delta}}\mathcal{C}_{ik}\,\mathcal{C}_{\ell m}\,\mathcal{F}_{2,\mathrm{int}}^{(ik,\ell m)}\,,

(36)

where the first term represents contributions from
independent
dipoles, that is, each dipole consecutively emits softer gluons at each order independently of the other dipoles. This situation is analogous to that in
e^{+}e^{-}

\delta

To carry out the integrations we expand the integrand as a power series in
R
and use the change of variable
\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\to\theta_{1}
for the angular integrations. We obtain the following result

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2,\mathrm{dip}}^{(ab)}

\displaystyle=0.046+0.047\,R^{2}+0.009\,R^{4}+

(37b)

for the independent-dipoles part, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2,\mathrm{int}}^{(aj,bj)}=\mathcal{F}_{2,\mathrm{int}}^{(bj,aj)}

\displaystyle=0.046+0.004\,R^{2}+0.0004\,R^{4}+

\displaystyle+0.00004\,R^{6}+\mathcal{O}(R^{8})\,,

(37c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2,\mathrm{int}}^{(aj,ab)}=\mathcal{F}_{2,\mathrm{int}}^{(bj,ab)}

\displaystyle=0.032\,R^{2}+0.013\,R^{4}+

\displaystyle+0.0006\,R^{6}+\mathcal{O}(R^{8})\,,

(37d)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2,\mathrm{int}}^{(ab,aj)}=\mathcal{F}_{2,\mathrm{int}}^{(ab,bj)}

\displaystyle=0.071\,R^{2}+0.013\,R^{4}+

\displaystyle+0.0003\,R^{6}+\mathcal{O}(R^{8})\,,

(37e)

for the dipole-interference part. Notice that the interference term

is not symmetric under the interchange of the dipoles
(aj)
and
(ab)

(bj)

(aj)

Including the resummation of NGLs and CLs together with the global form factor (

;

;
Hagiwara:2015bia

k_{t}

For the anti-
k_{t}
algorithm, the all-orders numerical resummation of NGLs may be obtained from the dipole-evolution Monte Carlo code of ref.

Before we end this section we discuss the matching of the resummed result with the NLO fixed-order distribution. In fact, including the constant term

in eq. (

where now both

Comparison to CMS data

In order to compare our results with the experimental data we first need to account for non-perturbative effects from hadronisation corrections and the underlying event. One commonly used numerical approach to extract these corrections is to compute the ratio of the results obtained from Monte Carlo event generators with non-perturbative effects switched on and off. In this paper we include these corrections analytically by considering the mean value of the change in the jet mass
\delta m_{j}^{2}
due to these non-perturbative effects. This change was computed in ref.
Dasgupta:2007wa

\langle\delta m_{j}^{2}\rangle=\sum_{(i\ell)\in\Delta_{\delta}}\mathcal{C}_{i\ell}\,\mathcal{A}(\mu_{I})\,p_{t}\,\mathcal{N}_{i\ell}(R)\,,

(56)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{ab}(R)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\,R^{4}+\frac{1}{4608}\,R^{8}+\cdots\,,

(57a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{aj}(R)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{bj}(R)=R+\frac{3}{16}\,R^{3}+\frac{125}{9216}\,R^{5}+\cdots\,,

(57b)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mu_{I})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{I}}{\pi}\bigg[\alpha_{0}(\mu_{I})-\alpha_{s}(p_{t})

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta_{0}}{2\pi}\left(\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\mu_{I}}+\frac{K}{\beta_{0}}+1\right)\alpha_{s}^{2}(p_{t})\bigg].

(58)

Here
\mu_{I}
is an arbitrary matching scale (chosen to be of order of a few GeV) and

is the averaged coupling over the non-perturbative low-

\alpha_{0}=1/\mu_{I}\int_{0}^{\mu_{I}}\alpha_{s}(k_{t})\,\mathrm{d}k_{t}

8
Conclusions

In this paper we have presented state-of-the-art detailed fixed-order calculations as well as all-orders estimates of distributions of important observables at the LHC. Specifically we have considered a typical jet-shape observable that has been studied quite substantially in the literature, namely the invariant jet mass. It is a member of a large class of observables known as non-global observables, that have so far proven to be quite delicate to treat. The subtleties in the analysis of such observables stem from the fact that they are defined for a restricted phase-space region. This is unlike global observables which are defined over the whole phase space. The former non-global observables receive contributions that are totally absent for their global counterparts. These contributions appear at each higher order in perturbation theory and have so far shown no pattern of iteration.

We have extended the work of ref.
Dasgupta:2012hg
from various angles: (a) we have implemented two clustering algorithms,
k_{t}
and C-A, instead of just the anti-
k_{t}
considered in the latter reference. Generally, computations in
k_{t}
and C-A algorithms are much more difficult to handle than in anti-
k_{t}
case; (b) we have computed CLs, which are completely absent for anti-
k_{t}
and thus not treated in
Dasgupta:2012hg
; (c) we have investigated the jet mass distribution in various different processes, namely
W/Z/\gamma/H
+ one jet, while only
Z
+ jet was considered in the said reference; and (d) we have provided analytical expressions for our results in the form of power-series expansions in the jet radius
R
. As the experimental data
Chatrchyan:2013rla
for the jet mass distribution in
Z
+ jet events at the LHC with anti-
k_{t}
clustering were available only after the publication of ref.
Dasgupta:2012hg
, we made the comparison of the resummed result with these experimental data herein.

We have confirmed previous results that were arrived at in studies of
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation processes. These include, for instance, the observation that NGLs are decreased by the application of jet clusterings other than anti-
k_{t}
. In other words, NGLs are more significant when anti-
k_{t}
is used. This may hint at the advantage of using other jet clustering algorithms in order to bypass the difficulties posed by NGLs. Additionally, we showed that in the limit of very small jet-radius parameter the NGLs and CLs at hadron colliders coincide with those at
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders. Moreover, we have been able to identify new features that are not present in the simple
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation case such as the significance of initial-state radiation and its impact on the jet mass distribution. The jet mass provides a tool to discriminate gluon and quark-initiated jets as their corresponding jet mass distributions were shown to be quite different.

It is worth, as a continuation to this project, investigating other crucial hadronic processes at the LHC such as di-jet production. The latter represents an important background for numerous potential new physics signals. Another issue that is also worth tackling is performing calculations beyond two-gluon emission. This will provide a deeper insight into the nature of QCD hadronic processes that have not been fully understood so far.

Appendix A
Born cross-section

In this section we present the structure of the differential partonic Born cross-section that is needed in the convolution with the resummed result to obtain the jet mass distribution (eq. (
6
)).

The differential partonic Born cross-section (for some fixed transverse momentum
p_{t}
and rapidity
y
of the outgoing hard parton) for channel
\delta
of the process of production of a Higgs (
H
) or a vector boson (
V
) in association with a jet in hadron collisions may be written as follows

\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{0,\delta}}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}}=\frac{1}{16\pi\,\hat{s}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{0,\delta}\right|^{2}\delta\left[(p_{a}+p_{b}-p_{j})^{2}-M^{2}\right],

(59)

where
|\mathcal{M}_{0,\delta}|^{2}
is the corresponding partonic Born squared amplitude, summed and averaged over spins and colours, and
M
is the mass of the boson. For the vector-boson processes, the differential partonic Born cross-section reduces to
Ellis:1981hk
;
Arnold:1988dp
;
Gonsalves:1989ar

\begin{split}&q_{i}\bar{q}_{j}\to gV:\mathcal{K}_{V}\,\frac{2\pi\alpha_{s}\mathrm{C_{F}}}{\mathrm{N_{c}}}\,\frac{1}{\hat{s}}\,T_{0}(\hat{s},\hat{u},\hat{t})\,\delta\left(\hat{s}+\hat{t}+\hat{u}-M^{2}\right),\\
&q_{i}g\to q_{j}V:-\mathcal{K}_{V}\,\frac{\pi\alpha_{s}}{\mathrm{N_{c}}}\,\frac{1}{\hat{s}}\,T_{0}(\hat{t},\hat{u},\hat{s})\,\delta\left(\hat{s}+\hat{t}+\hat{u}-M^{2}\right),\end{split}

(60)

where the couplings are

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{Z}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\,\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{G_{F}}\,M_{Z}^{2}}{4\pi}\left(g_{iV}^{2}+g_{iA}^{2}\right),

(61a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{W}

\displaystyle=\left|V_{ij}\right|^{2}\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{G_{F}}\,M_{W}^{2}}{4\pi}\,,

(61b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\,e_{i}^{2}\,\alpha\,,

(61c)

with
\mathrm{G_{F}}
the Fermi coupling, and
g_{iV}
and
g_{iA}
the vector and axial-vector couplings given by

\displaystyle g_{iV}

\displaystyle=T^{i}_{3}-2\,e_{i}\sin^{2}\theta_{w}\,,

(62a)

\displaystyle g_{iA}

\displaystyle=T^{i}_{3}\,,

(62b)

where
T^{i}_{3}
is the weak isospin of the quark
q_{i}
(
+\frac{1}{2}
for up-type and
-\frac{1}{2}
for down-type quarks),
e_{i}
is the fraction of electric charge carried by quark
q_{i}
, and
\theta_{w}
is the Weinberg angle. Moreover,
V_{ij}
are CKM matrix elements corresponding to flavours
i
and
j
, with
i
and
j
being of different magnitude of electric charge (we exclude the top quark). In the above,
\alpha
is the electromagnetic coupling. The kinematical factor
T_{0}
describing the matrix-element squared of the underlying Born scattering is given by

T_{0}(\hat{s},\hat{u},\hat{t})=\frac{\hat{t}^{2}+\hat{u}^{2}+2\,\hat{s}\left(\hat{s}+\hat{t}+\hat{u}\right)}{\hat{t}\,\hat{u}}\,,

(63)

with
\hat{s}
,
\hat{t}
and
\hat{u}
the partonic Mandelstam variables

\displaystyle\hat{s}

\displaystyle=\left(p_{a}+p_{b}\right)^{2}=x_{a}\,x_{b}\,s\,,

(64a)

\displaystyle\hat{t}

\displaystyle=\left(p_{a}-p_{j}\right)^{2}=-x_{a}\,\sqrt{s}\,p_{t}\,e^{-y}\,,

(64b)

\displaystyle\hat{u}

\displaystyle=\left(p_{b}-p_{j}\right)^{2}=-x_{b}\,\sqrt{s}\,p_{t}\,e^{y}\,.

(64c)

For the process
gg\to Hg
(i.e. channel
(\delta_{3})
) we have
Ravindran:2002dc

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{0,\delta_{3}}}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta_{3}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{G_{F}}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\tau^{2}\,F^{2}\,\frac{\pi}{\mathrm{C_{F}}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{3}\frac{1}{\hat{s}}\,\times

\displaystyle\times\frac{\hat{s}^{4}+\hat{t}^{4}+\hat{u}^{4}+m^{8}}{\hat{s}\,\hat{t}\,\hat{u}}\,\delta\left(\hat{s}+\hat{t}+\hat{u}-M_{H}^{2}\right),

(65)

where
\tau=7.65
and
F=0.09
.

The total Born cross-section
\sigma_{0}
is simply the integral of
\mathrm{d}\sigma_{0,\delta}/\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
(including the kinematical-cuts function
\Xi_{\mathcal{B}}
) over
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
, summed over possible
\delta
, where we have

\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}=\mathrm{d}x_{a}\,\mathrm{d}x_{b}\,f_{a}(x_{a},\mu_{\mathrm{F}}^{2})\,f_{b}(x_{b},\mu_{\mathrm{F}}^{2})\,\mathrm{d}p_{t}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,,

(66)

where
f_{i}
denotes the parton density function for the corresponding incoming parton
(i)
evaluated at a factorisation scale
\mu_{\mathrm{F}}
.

Substituting the Mandelstam variables into the delta function in the integrand we obtain

\delta\left[x_{a}\,x_{b}\,s-x_{a}\,\sqrt{s}\,p_{t}\,e^{-y}-x_{b}\,\sqrt{s}\,p_{t}\,e^{y}-M^{2}\right].

(67)

This delta function can be used to perform the integration over one of the
x
’s, say
x_{b}
, and thus we set

x_{b}=\frac{x_{a}\,e^{-y}\,p_{t}/\sqrt{s}+M^{2}/s}{x_{a}-e^{y}\,p_{t}/\sqrt{s}}\,,

(68)

and multiply the integrand by

\Theta(1-x_{b})\,\Theta(x_{b})/\left(x_{a}\,s-\sqrt{s}\,p_{t}\,e^{y}\right).

(69)

Since
x_{b}>0
then
x_{a}>e^{y}\,p_{t}/\sqrt{s}
and
y<\ln(\sqrt{s}/p_{t})
. Additionally, since
x_{b}<1
then
y>-\ln(\sqrt{s}/p_{t})
and

x_{a}>\frac{e^{y}\,p_{t}/\sqrt{s}+M^{2}/s}{1-e^{-y}\,p_{t}/\sqrt{s}}\,.

(70)

The latter inequality overrules
x_{a}>e^{y}\,p_{t}/\sqrt{s}
, and furthermore, since
x_{a}<1
we deduce that

\cosh y<\left(1-\frac{M^{2}}{s}\right)\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\sqrt{s}}{p_{t}}\,,

(71)

which also overrules the condition
|y|<\ln\sqrt{s}/p_{t}
.

We perform the integration over
p_{t}
,
y
and
x_{a}
, either in the Born cross-section or in the jet mass distribution, numerically via Monte Carlo method.

Appendix B
Resummed Global form factor

The Sudakov global form factor that resums global logarithms is given in eq. (
28
). The radiator
\mathcal{R}
is composed of contributions from double-logarithmic soft-collinear and single-logarithmic hard-collinear emissions from the outgoing leg
(j)
, and from single-logarithmic soft wide-angle emissions from all legs. The leading-order soft wide-angle contribution that we calculated in section
3.1
simply exponentiates to all orders. Additionally, the non-global and clustering corrections appear as a factorised part that multiplies the resummed global form factor. The remaining soft-collinear and hard-collinear contributions may be obtained using the general formalism of resummation introduced in ref.
Banfi:2004yd
as we show below.

First we write the definition of the observable
\varrho
in terms of the transverse momentum
k_{t}^{(\ell)}
, rapidity
\eta^{(\ell)}
, and azimuth
\phi^{(\ell)}
of a single soft-collinear emission with respect to the direction of the hard leg
(\ell)
. Emissions that are collinear to the incoming legs
(a)
and
(b)
do not end up inside the jet, so they do not contribute to its mass, hence
\varrho^{(a)}=\varrho^{(b)}=0
. For emissions that are collinear to the outgoing leg
(j)
, we introduce a coordinate rotation that takes the momentum of leg
(j)
to the
z
axis, where the momenta of the jet and the soft emission become

\displaystyle p_{j}^{(j)}

\displaystyle=p_{t}\cosh y\left(1,0,0,1\right),

(72a)

\displaystyle k^{(j)}

\displaystyle=k_{t}^{(j)}\left(\cosh\eta^{(j)},\cos\phi^{(j)},\sin\phi^{(j)},\sinh\eta^{(j)}\right).

(72b)

The jet mass observable (being invariant under rotations) is then given by

\varrho^{(j)}=2\,\frac{k^{(j)}\cdot p_{j}^{(j)}}{p_{t}^{2}}=2\,\frac{k_{t}^{(j)}}{p_{t}}\,e^{-\eta^{(j)}}\,\cosh y\,.

(73)

Comparing the definition of the normalised invariant jet mass (
73
) to the general parametrisation of observables from ref.
Banfi:2004yd

V=d_{\ell}\left(\frac{k_{t}^{(\ell)}}{Q}\right)^{a_{\ell}}e^{-b_{\ell}\,\eta^{(\ell)}}\,g_{\ell}(\phi^{(\ell)})\,,

(74)

where
Q=p_{t}
is the hard scale, we see that
a_{j}=b_{j}=g_{j}=1
and
d_{j}=2\cosh y
.

Employing the master formula for resummation (eq. (3.6) from ref.
Banfi:2004yd
) we obtain the expression of the radiator
\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(\rho)
, for a given Born channel
\delta
, in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalisation scheme

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(\rho)

\displaystyle=C_{j}\left[L\,g_{1}(\alpha_{s}L)+g_{2}(\alpha_{s}L)+g_{2,\mathrm{coll}}(\alpha_{s}L)\right]+

\displaystyle+g_{2,\mathrm{wide}}(\alpha_{s}L)\left[\mathcal{C}_{ab}\,\frac{R^{2}}{2}+\left(\mathcal{C}_{aj}+\mathcal{C}_{bj}\right)h(R)\right],

(75)

with
h(R)
being given in eq. (
26
). Here
C_{j}
is the colour factor associated with leg
j
,
C_{j}=\mathrm{C_{F}}
for outgoing (anti-) quark jet and
C_{j}=\mathrm{C_{A}}
for outgoing gluon jet, and
\mathcal{C}_{i\ell}
is the colour factor for dipole
(i\ell)
introduced in the main text. We have

\displaystyle g_{1}=\frac{1}{2\pi\beta_{0}\lambda}\left[(1-2\lambda)\ln(1-2\lambda)-2(1-\lambda)\ln(1-\lambda)\right],

(76a)

\displaystyle g_{2}=\frac{\mathrm{K}}{4\pi^{2}\beta_{0}^{2}}\left[2\ln(1-\lambda)-\ln(1-2\lambda)\right]+\frac{\beta_{1}}{2\pi\beta_{0}^{3}}\times

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{2}\ln^{2}(1-2\lambda)-\ln^{2}(1-\lambda)+\ln(1-2\lambda)-2\ln(1-\lambda)\right],

(76b)

\displaystyle g_{2,\mathrm{coll}}=-B_{j}\,\frac{1}{\pi\beta_{0}}\,\ln(1-\lambda)\,,

(76c)

\displaystyle g_{2,\mathrm{wide}}=-\frac{1}{2\pi\beta_{0}}\ln(1-2\lambda)\,,

(76d)

with
\lambda=\alpha_{s}(R\,p_{t})\,\beta_{0}\,\ln(R^{2}/\rho)
. The factor
B_{j}
accounts for corrections due to hard-collinear emissions to the outgoing jet
j
and is given by

\displaystyle B_{q}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{4}

\displaystyle\text{for quark jets}\,,

(77)

\displaystyle B_{g}

\displaystyle=-\frac{11\,\mathrm{C_{A}}-4\,\mathrm{T_{R}}\,\mathrm{n_{f}}}{12\,\mathrm{C_{A}}}=-\frac{\pi\beta_{0}}{\mathrm{C_{A}}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{for gluons jets}\,,

with
\mathrm{T_{R}}
the Dynkin index (normalisation constant) for the
\mathrm{SU}(\mathrm{N_{c}})
generators,
\mathrm{T_{R}}=1/2
, and
\mathrm{n_{f}}=5
the number of active quark flavours. Additionally we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{K}&=\mathrm{C_{A}}\left(\frac{67}{18}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)-\frac{5}{9}\,\mathrm{n_{f}}\,,\\
\beta_{0}&=\frac{11\,\mathrm{C_{A}}-2\,\mathrm{n_{f}}}{12\,\pi}\,,\\
\beta_{1}&=\frac{17\,\mathrm{C_{A}^{2}}-5\,\mathrm{C_{A}}\,\mathrm{n_{f}}-3\,\mathrm{C_{F}}\,\mathrm{n_{f}}}{24\pi^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(78)

In the master formula we excluded the single-logarithmic soft wide-angle term referred to in ref.
Banfi:2004yd
as
\ln S(T)

. It appears as the last term in the radiator (

The derivative of the radiator

Fixed-order expansion

For the sake of matching we need the fixed-order expansion of the resummed form factor (
49
). We can cast the latter in the form

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Sigma_{\delta}(\rho)}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{0,\delta}}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}}\,C_{\delta}(\rho)\,\exp\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{n+1}G_{nm}\,\bar{\alpha}_{s}^{n}\,L^{m}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{0,\delta}}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}_{\delta}}\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{2n}H_{nm}\,\bar{\alpha}_{s}^{n}\,L^{m},

(80)

where the expansion coefficients in the exponent,
G_{nm}
, up to
\mathcal{O}(\bar{\alpha}_{s}^{2})
, are

\displaystyle G_{12}

\displaystyle=-\frac{C_{j}}{2}\,,

\displaystyle G_{11}

\displaystyle=-B_{j}\,C_{j}-\mathcal{C}_{ab}\,\frac{R^{2}}{2}-\left(\mathcal{C}_{aj}+\mathcal{C}_{bj}\right)h(R)\,,

\displaystyle G_{23}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\beta_{0}}{2}\,C_{j}\,,

\displaystyle G_{22}

\displaystyle=-\frac{C_{j}}{4}\left(K+2\pi\beta_{0}\,B_{j}\right)-\pi\beta_{0}\left(\mathcal{C}_{aj}+\mathcal{C}_{bj}\right)h(R)+

\displaystyle-\pi\beta_{0}\,\mathcal{C}_{ab}\,\frac{R^{2}}{2}+\frac{\mathcal{F}_{2}^{\delta}}{2}-\frac{\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\delta}}{2}-\frac{\zeta_{2}}{2}\,.

(81)

and the expansion coefficients in the series,

\displaystyle H_{12}=G_{12}\,,\qquad H_{11}=G_{11}\,,\qquad H_{10}=C^{(1)}_{\delta}\,,

\displaystyle H_{24}=\frac{G_{12}^{2}}{2},\qquad H_{23}=G_{23}+G_{12}\,G_{11},

\displaystyle H_{22}=G_{22}+\frac{G_{11}^{2}}{2}+\left(C^{(1)}_{\delta}\right)G_{12}.

(82)

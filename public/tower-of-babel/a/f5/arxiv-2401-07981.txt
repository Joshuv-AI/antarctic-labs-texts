Probability Mass Function, Moments and Factorial Moments of the Negative Binomial Distribution NB ( k , r ) (k,r) Journal: (internal report CC24-1)
S. R. Mane

Abstract
The negative binomial distribution NB ( k , r ) (k,r) of Type I is the probability distribution for a sequence of independent Bernoulli trials (with success parameter p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) ) with r r nonoverlapping success runs of length ≥ k \geq k . We present a new, more concise, expression for its probability mass function. We show it can also be succinctly written using hypergeometric functions. We also present new expressions (combinatorial sums) for its moments and factorial moments, as opposed to only the mean and variance (which are already known). Next, we present an alternative non-combinatorial viewpoint, which yields expressions for the factorial moments not only for nonoverlapping success runs, but also for runs with an overlap of ℓ \ell , where ℓ ∈ [ 0 , k − 1 ] \ell\in[0,k-1] . The case ℓ = k − 1 \ell=k-1 is the negative binomial distribution NB ( k , r ) (k,r) of Type III. The results also yield the solution for the negative binomial distribution NB ( k , r ) (k,r) with a minimum gap between the success runs (explained in the text). Introduction

The negative binomial distribution NB
(k,r)
(or NB
k,r
) of Type I is the probability distribution for a sequence of
n
independent Bernoulli trials
(all with success parameter
p\in(0,1)
)
with
r
nonoverlapping success runs of length
\geq k
.
The term “nonoverlapping” follows the definition in the classic text by Feller
[
1
]
.
This means that the counting for a new success run begins as soon as a success run of length
k
is attained.
Hence a run of six consecutive successes
SSSSSS
signifies
(i) six consecutive success runs if
k=1
,
(ii) three consecutive success runs if
k=2
,
(iii) two consecutive success runs if
k=3
,
(iv) one success run if
k=6

and for
k=4
and
k=5
it contains one success run, with the beginnings of another success run.
This is called the negative binomial distribution of Type I.
The text by Balakrishnan and Koutras
[
2
]
gives an excellent exposition on the subject,
with formulas for the negative binomial distribution of Types I, II and III.

1.

The negative binomial distribution of Type II requires at least one failure between success runs.
In the above example, the sequence
SSSSSS
counts as only one success run, for any value
k=1,\dots,6
.
We do not treat Type II in this note.

2.

The negative binomial distribution of Type III permits
k-1
overlaps between consecutive success runs of length
k
.
In the above example, the sequence
SSSSSS
contains
(i) two success runs of length 5 if
k=5
,
(ii) three success runs of length 4 if
k=4
,
(iii) four success runs of length 3 if
k=3
,
(iv) five success runs of length 2 if
k=2
.
We discuss Type III briefly in Sec.
7
.

Note that
[
2
]
is a reference text:
our principal references for the original formulas are
Charalambides
[
3
]
and Philippou and Georghiou
[
4
]
.
Both sets of authors published expressions for the probability mass function as nested combinatorial sums.
Charalambides
[
3
]
also published a recurrence for the factorial moments.

This note presents a new, more concise, expression for the probability mass function (pmf) of the negative binomial distribution NB
k,r
of Type I.
The pmf can also be succinctly written using hypergeometric functions (which is computationally faster to evaluate).
We also present new expressions (combinatorial sums) for its moments and factorial moments, as opposed to only the mean and variance. Next, we present an alternative non-combinatorial viewpoint to derive the factorial moments.
It applies also for runs with an overlap of
\ell
, where
\ell\in[0,k-1]
.
Finally, the results also yield the solution for the negative binomial distribution NB
(k,r)
with a minimum gap between the success runs (see Sec.
8
).

The structure of this paper is as follows.
Sec.
2
presents the basic definitions and notation employed in this note.
Secs.
3
,
4
and
5
present our results for the probability mass function, factorial moments and moments, respectively.
Sec.
6
gives a brief discussion of the central moments,
i.e. moments centered on the mean, in particular the skewness and kurtosis.
Sec.
7
presents an alternative non-combinatorial expression for the factorial moments.
Sec.
8
presents the application of the above results to the case of the negative binomial distribution NB
(k,r)
with a minimum gap between the success runs.
Sec.
9
concludes.

General remarks

4.2
C

We begin with

The equation to solve is eq. (

It is the

\begin{split}\mu_{(j)}&=\frac{q}{1-p^{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(i)_{j}p^{i-1}\\

&\equiv\frac{C_{j}p^{k}}{1-p^{k}}\,.\end{split}

(4.1)

Some algebra yields

C_{j}=\frac{j!}{q^{j}p^{k}}\biggl[p^{j-1}(1-p^{k})-p^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\binom{k}{i}p^{j-i-1}q^{i}\biggr]-(k)_{j}\,.

(4.2)

Here
(k)_{j}=k(k-1)\dots(k-j+1)

Substitute into eq. (
2.3
) to obtain

M_{(n)}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\min(n,k)}\binom{n}{j}(n+rj-j)C_{j}M_{(n-j)}\,.

(4.3)

Following the ideas in
[

Define

. Then

M_{(n)}=n!\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\dots+kn_{k}=n}(r+s_{n}-1)_{s_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{n_{j}!}\Bigl(\frac{C_{j}}{j!}\Bigr)^{n_{j}}\,.

(4.4)

\begin{split}Y_{(j)}&=(Z+a)_{(j)}\\

(4.19a)

\displaystyle F_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{j!}{q^{j}p^{k}}\biggl[-p^{k+j-1}+(-1)^{j-1}\binom{k+j-2}{j-1}q^{j-1}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(1-j,1,2-j-k;-p/q)\biggr]\,.

(4.19b)

5
Raw (power) moments

5.1
General remarks

Denote the raw (or power) moment by
M_{n}
for
C
indexing and by
\hat{M}_{n}
for
F
indexing.
The subscript is
n
not
(n)
.
Again, let
Y\sim\mathrm{NB}_{k,r}
with
F
indexing and
Z\sim\mathrm{NB}_{k,r}
with
C
indexing.
Then by definition

\hat{M}_{n}=\mathbb{E}[Y^{n}]
and
M_{n}=\mathbb{E}[Z^{n}]
and it is also evident that

Y^{n}=(Z+rk)^{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}Z^{n-i}(rk)^{i}\,.

(5.1)

Hence

\hat{M}_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}M_{n-i}(rk)^{i}\,.

(5.2)

We follow the ideas in
[
15
]
.
First define functions
\tilde{C}_{j}
for
C
indexing and
\tilde{F}_{j}
for
F
indexing.
The expressions for the raw moments
M_{n}
and
\hat{M}_{n}
are formally similar to those for the factorial moments in eq. (
5.3
).
We substitute
\tilde{C}_{j}
in place of
C_{j}
or
\tilde{F}_{j}
in place of
F_{j}
to obtain the sum for the raw moments.
Recall
s_{n}=n_{1}+\dots+n_{k}
.

\displaystyle M_{n}=n!\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\dots+kn_{k}=n}(r+s_{n}-1)_{s_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{n_{j}!}\Bigl(\frac{\tilde{C}_{j}}{j!}\Bigr)^{n_{j}}\,,

(5.3a)

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{n}=n!\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\dots+kn_{k}=n}(r+s_{n}-1)_{s_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{n_{j}!}\Bigl(\frac{\tilde{F}_{j}}{j!}\Bigr)^{n_{j}}\,.

(5.3b)

It remains to write explicit expressions for
\tilde{C}_{j}
and
\tilde{F}_{j}
.
They have a pattern, but it is more complicated than for the factorial moments.
They involve the Eulerian polynomials, as will be explained below.

The Eulerian polynomials
A_{i}(t)
are defined as the coefficients of
x^{i}
as follows

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}A_{i}(t)\frac{x^{i}}{i!}=\frac{t-1}{t-e^{(t-1)x}}\,.

(5.4)

Here
A_{0}(t)=1
and for
i\geq 1
,
A_{i}(t)
is a polynomial in
t
of degree
i-1
.
Also
A_{i}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}A_{ij}t^{j}
and

A_{ij}
is an element in the triangle of Eulerian Numbers, given by

A_{ij}=\sum_{s=0}^{j}(-1)^{s}\binom{i+1}{s}(j+1-s)^{i}\,.

(5.5)

The first few Eulerian polynomials are

\displaystyle A_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=1\,,

(5.6a)

\displaystyle A_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=1\,,

(5.6b)

\displaystyle A_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=1+t\,,

(5.6c)

\displaystyle A_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=1+4t+t^{2}\,,

(5.6d)

\displaystyle A_{4}(t)

\displaystyle=1+11t+11t^{2}+t^{3}\,.

(5.6e)

5.2
C
indexing

We begin with
C
indexing.
Define
\xi_{j}
via

\tilde{C}_{j}=\frac{\xi_{j}}{q^{j}p^{k}}\,.

(5.7)

Then we obtain the following expressions for
\xi_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,4
.

\displaystyle\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=(1-p^{k})A_{1}(p)-kp^{k}\,,

(5.8a)

\displaystyle\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=(1-p^{k})A_{2}(p)-p^{k}\Bigl[2kqA_{1}(p)+(kq)^{2}\Bigr]\,,

(5.8b)

\displaystyle\xi_{3}

\displaystyle=(1-p^{k})A_{3}(p)-p^{k}\Bigl[3kqA_{2}(p)+3(kq)^{2}A_{1}(p)+(kq)^{3}\Bigr]\,,

(5.8c)

\displaystyle\xi_{4}

\displaystyle=(1-p^{k})A_{4}(p)-p^{k}\Bigl[4kqA_{3}(p)+6(kq)^{2}A_{2}(p)+4(kq)^{3}A_{1}(p)+(kq)^{4}\Bigr]\,.

(5.8d)

In general, the expression is

\xi_{j}=(1-p^{k})A_{j}(p)-p^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{j}\binom{j}{i}(kq)^{i}A_{j-i}(p)\,.

(5.9)

5.3
F
indexing

The pattern for
F
indexing is similar, but the signs alternate and the last term
(highest power of
kq
) is absent and there is no factor of
p^{k}
, as will be explained below.
Define
\chi_{j}
via

\tilde{F}_{j}=\frac{\chi_{j}}{q^{j}p^{k}}\,.

(5.10)

Then we obtain the following expressions for
\chi_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,4
.

\displaystyle\chi_{1}

\displaystyle=(1-p^{k})A_{1}(p)\,,

(5.11a)

\displaystyle\chi_{2}

\displaystyle=(1-p^{k})A_{2}(p)-2kqA_{1}(p)\,,

(5.11b)

\displaystyle\chi_{3}

\displaystyle=(1-p^{k})A_{3}(p)-3kqA_{2}(p)+3(kq)^{2}A_{1}(p)\,,

(5.11c)

\displaystyle\chi_{4}

\displaystyle=(1-p^{k})A_{4}(p)-4kqA_{3}(p)+6(kq)^{2}A_{2}(p)-4(kq)^{3}A_{1}(p)\,.

(5.11d)

In general, the expression is

\chi_{j}=(1-p^{k})A_{j}(p)+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(-1)^{i}\binom{j}{i}(kq)^{i}A_{j-i}(p)\,.

(5.12)

6
Central moments

6.1
General remarks

The term “central moments” refers to moments centered on the mean.
The central moments do not depend on the indexing scheme.
Let
Y\sim\mathrm{NB}_{k,r}
with
F
indexing and
Z\sim\mathrm{NB}_{k,r}
with
C
indexing.
Denote their respective means by
\mu_{F}
and
\mu_{C}
and recall
\mu_{F}=\mu_{C}+rk
.
Then

\mathbb{E}[(Y-\mu_{F})^{n}]=\mathbb{E}[(Z+rk-(\mu_{C}+rk))^{n}]=\mathbb{E}[(Z-\mu_{C})^{n}]\,.

(6.1)

Denote the central moments by
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. By definition
\mathcal{M}_{1}=0
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
is the variance
\sigma^{2}
.

\begin{split}\sigma^{2}=\mathcal{M}_{2}&=M_{2}-\mu_{C}^{2}\\
&=r(r+1)\tilde{C}_{1}^{2}+r\tilde{C}_{2}-r^{2}\tilde{C}_{1}^{2}\\
&=r(\tilde{C}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{C}_{2})\\
&=r(\tilde{F}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{F}_{2})\,.\end{split}

(6.2)

As already noted, the variance is proportional to
r
.

6.2
Third central moment

The third central moment is given as follows.

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{3}&=\mathbb{E}[(Z-\mu_{C})^{3}]\\
&=M_{3}-3\mu_{C}M_{2}+3\mu_{C}^{2}M_{1}-\mu_{C}^{3}\\
&=r(r+1)(r+2)\tilde{C}_{1}^{3}+3r(r+1)\tilde{C}_{1}\tilde{C}_{2}+r\tilde{C}_{3}-3r\tilde{C}_{1}\Bigl[r(r+1)\tilde{C}_{1}^{2}+r\tilde{C}_{2}\Bigr]+3r^{3}\tilde{C}_{1}^{3}-r^{3}\tilde{C}_{1}^{3}\\
&=r(2\tilde{C}_{1}^{3}+3\tilde{C}_{1}\tilde{C}_{2}+\tilde{C}_{3})\\
&=r(2\tilde{F}_{1}^{3}+3\tilde{F}_{1}\tilde{F}_{2}+\tilde{F}_{3})\,.\end{split}

(6.3)

The third central moment is proportional to
r
.
The skewness
\gamma
is the normalized third central moment

\gamma=\frac{\mathcal{M}_{3}}{\sigma^{3}}\propto r^{-1/2}\,.

(6.4)

For fixed
k
and
p
, the skewness
decreases
as
r
increases.

6.3
Fourth central moment

The fourth central moment is given as follows.

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{4}&=\mathbb{E}[(Z-\mu_{C})^{4}]\\
&=M_{4}-4\mu_{C}M_{3}+6\mu_{C}^{2}M_{2}-4\mu_{C}^{3}M_{1}+\mu_{C}^{4}\\
&=r(r+1)(r+2)(r+3)\tilde{C}_{1}^{4}+6r(r+1)(r+2)\tilde{C}_{1}^{2}\tilde{C}_{2}+r(r+1)(4\tilde{C}_{1}\tilde{C}_{3}+3\tilde{C}_{2}^{2})+r\tilde{C}_{4}\\
&\qquad-4r\tilde{C}_{1}\Bigl[r(r+1)(r+2)\tilde{C}_{1}^{3}+3r(r+1)\tilde{C}_{1}\tilde{C}_{2}+r\tilde{C}_{3}\Bigr]\\
&\qquad+6r^{2}\tilde{C}_{1}^{2}\Bigl[r(r+1)\tilde{C}_{1}^{2}+r\tilde{C}_{2}\Bigr]-4r^{4}\tilde{C}_{1}^{4}+r^{4}\tilde{C}_{1}^{4}\\
&=3r^{2}(\tilde{C}^{4}+2\tilde{C}_{1}^{2}\tilde{C}_{2}+\tilde{C}_{2}^{2})+r(6\tilde{C}^{4}+12\tilde{C}_{1}^{2}\tilde{C}_{2}+3\tilde{C}_{2}^{2}+4\tilde{C}_{1}\tilde{C}_{3}+\tilde{C}_{4})\\
&=3\sigma^{4}+r(6\tilde{C}^{4}+12\tilde{C}_{1}^{2}\tilde{C}_{2}+3\tilde{C}_{2}^{2}+4\tilde{C}_{1}\tilde{C}_{3}+\tilde{C}_{4})\\
&=3\sigma^{4}+r(6\tilde{F}^{4}+12\tilde{F}_{1}^{2}\tilde{F}_{2}+3\tilde{F}_{2}^{2}+4\tilde{F}_{1}\tilde{F}_{3}+\tilde{F}_{4})\,.\end{split}

(6.5)

The fourth central moment has terms of both
O(r^{2})
and
O(r)
.
However, for the
excess kurtosis
we subtract
3\sigma^{4}
, which leaves only terms proportional to
r
.

\frac{\mathcal{M}_{4}-3\sigma^{4}}{\sigma^{4}}\propto r^{-1}\,.

(6.6)

For fixed
k
and
p
, the excess kurtosis
decreases
as
r
increases.

7
Alternative (non-combinatorial) viewpoint

We employ
F
indexing exclusively in this section.
For a sequence of independent Bernoulli trials with probability
p\in(0,1)
of success,
let
N_{k}
be the waiting time for the first run of
k
consecutive successes.
Then
N_{k}
is said to have the
geometric distribution of order
k
.
For
k=1
it is the geometric distribution.
Its probability generating function (pgf) is
[
1
]

\phi(s,k)=\frac{p^{k}s^{k}(1-ps)}{1-s+qp^{k}s^{k+1}}=\frac{p^{k}s^{k}}{1-qs(1+pk+\dots+k^{k-1}s^{k-1})}\,.

(7.1)

The pmf
f_{k}(n)
satisfies the recurrence
[
12
]

f_{k}(n)=qf_{k}(n-1)+pqf_{k}(n-2)+p^{2}qf_{k}(n-3)+\dots+p^{k-1}qf_{k}(n-k)\,.

(7.2)

The initial conditions are
f_{k}(n)=0
for
n\in[1,k-1]
and
f_{k}(k)=p^{k}
.
The auxiliary polynomial is

\mathscr{A}(z)=z^{k}-qz^{k-1}-qpz^{k-2}-\dots-qp^{k-1}\,.

(7.3)

Here
z=1/s
(see eq. (
7.1
)).
Feller
[
1
]
proved the roots of the auxiliary polynomial are distinct and have magnitude less than unity.
We denote the roots by
\lambda_{j}(p,k)
, where
j=0,\dots,k-1
.
An important fact is that for any root,
\lambda_{j}^{k}(1-\lambda_{j})=p^{k}q
(see eq. (
7.3
) or
[
13
]
).
Dilworth and Mane
[
13
]
derived expressions for the roots in terms of Fuss-Catalan numbers.
The details can be found in
[
13
]
and will not be repeated here.

In a second paper, Dilworth and Mane
[
16
]
applied the formalism in
[
13
]

to treat the negative binomial distribution NB
(k,r)
of Type I with
\ell
-overlapping, say NB
(k,r,\ell)
.
Up to now, we have considered only the case
\ell=0
of nonoverlapping success runs.
The results below apply for all
\ell\in[0,k-1]
.
The pgf for NB
(k,r,\ell)
is (eq. (24) in
[
16
]
)

\phi_{r,k,\ell}(s)=\frac{\phi(s,k)^{r}}{\phi(s,\ell)^{r-1}}\,.

(7.4)

By definition, the pgf
\phi_{r,k,\ell}(s)
is related to the pmf
f_{r,k,\ell}(n)
via

\phi_{r,k,\ell}(s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}s^{n}f_{r,k,\ell}(n)\,.

(7.5)

Then the pmf is given as a sum of the powers of the roots
\lambda_{j}
as follows (eq. (52) in
[
16
]
)

f_{r,k,\ell}(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^{r}\binom{n-1}{m-1}a_{jm}\lambda_{j}(p,k)^{n-m}\,.

(7.6)

The details of the coefficients
a_{jm}
were given in
[
16
]
and their explicit values are not required below.

To avoid confusion with too many uses of
n
, we denote the
i^{th}
factorial moment by
\mathscr{M}_{(i)}
.
Then

\begin{split}\mathscr{M}_{(i)}&=i!\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^{r}a_{jm}\lambda_{j}^{-m}\biggl[\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}\binom{n}{i}\binom{n-1}{m-1}\lambda_{j}^{n}\biggr]\\
&=i!\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^{r}a_{jm}\lambda_{j}^{-m}\biggl[\frac{\lambda_{j}^{i}}{(1-\lambda_{j})^{i+m}}\,\sum_{b=1}^{m}\binom{m}{b}\binom{i-1}{b-1}\lambda_{j}^{m-b}\biggr]\qquad\textrm{(because $|\lambda_{j}|<1$)}\\
&=i!\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^{r}a_{jm}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{i}}{(1-\lambda_{j})^{i+m}}\,\sum_{b=1}^{m}\binom{m}{b}\binom{i-1}{b-1}\lambda_{j}^{-b}\\
&=i!\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^{r}a_{jm}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{(i+m)k+i}}{\lambda_{j}^{(i+m)k}(1-\lambda_{j})^{i+m}}\,\sum_{b=1}^{m}\binom{m}{b}\binom{i-1}{b-1}\lambda_{j}^{-b}\\
&=i!\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^{r}a_{jm}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{(i+m)k+i}}{(qp^{k})^{i+m}}\,\sum_{b=1}^{m}\binom{m}{b}\binom{i-1}{b-1}\lambda_{j}^{-b}\qquad\qquad\qquad\textrm{(because $\lambda_{j}^{k}(1-\lambda_{j})=p^{k}q$)}\\
&=\frac{i!}{(qp^{k})^{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^{r}a_{jm}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{(i+m)k+i}}{(qp^{k})^{m}}\,\sum_{b=1}^{m}\binom{m}{b}\binom{i-1}{b-1}\lambda_{j}^{-b}\\
&=\frac{i!}{(qp^{k})^{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^{r}a_{jm}\frac{m\lambda_{j}^{(i+m)k+i-1}}{(qp^{k})^{m}}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(1-m,1-i,2;1/\lambda_{j})\,.\end{split}

(7.7)

In the special case
r=1
, then
m=1
hence
b=1
(or
{}_{2}F_{1}(0,\dots)=1
) and the above simplifies to the expression derived in
[
17
]

\begin{split}\mathscr{M}_{(i)}&=\frac{i!}{(qp^{k})^{i+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{j1}\lambda_{j}^{(i+1)k+i}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\\
&=\frac{i!}{(qp^{k})^{i+1}}\,f_{k}((i+1)k+i)\,.\end{split}

(7.8)

Note the following.

1.

First, eq. (
7.7
) furnishes a non-combinatorial expression for the factorial moments.

2.

Moreover, it does so not only for nonoverlapping success runs but for
all

\ell\in[0,k-1]
.

3.

Note that the sum in eq. (
7.7
) is
independent of
n
.
It contains
rk
summations (and the computation of the hypergeometric function).

4.

Of course, one must calculate the roots
\lambda_{j}
and the coefficients
a_{jm}
, which may require a numerical algorithm.

Finally, note that the case
\ell=k-1
is the negative binomial distribution of Type III.
Hence eq. (
7.7
) yields the factorial moments for the negative binomial distribution of Type III.

8
Gaps between success runs

Most of the notation and formalism below is based on the exposition in Sec.
7
.
It was noted in
[
16
]
that the analysis therein also applies to the case where there is a minimum gap between the success runs.
This can be modelled as a “negative overlap” which means
\ell<0
(see the presentation in Sec.
7
above).
We must clarify what “gap” means in this context.
Denote the gap by
g
, where
g>0
.
Then after a success run of length
k
is attained,
the outcomes of the subsequent
g
trials are ignored
.
The count for the next success runs begins only after those
g
trials have been completed.
To return to the example in Sec.
1
, suppose
k=2
and
g=2
.
Then the sequence
SSSSSS
contains two success runs of length 2, because the outcomes of the third and fourth trials are ignored.
Similarly, the sequences
SSFFSS
,
SSSFSS
and
SSFSSS
all contain two success runs of length 2.
In all four cases, the two success runs are the first two and last two trials.
This is
not
the same as the negative binomial distribution of Type II.
The latter requires at least one failure between success runs. The number of trials between success runs is arbitrary (but at least one).

We employ
F
indexing exclusively in this section.
With respect to the formalism in
[
16
]
and the exposition in Sec.
7
,
g=-\ell
(and
g>0
).
Then it was noted in
[
16
]
that the pmf for
\ell<0
is given by that for
\ell=0
via the relation (Prop. 19 in
[
16
]
)

f_{r,k,\ell}(n)=f_{r,k,0}(n+(r-1)\ell)=f_{r,k,0}(n-(r-1)g)\,.

(8.1)

Hence the pgf is given by

\phi_{r,k,-g}(s)=\phi_{r,k,0}(s)s^{(r-1)g}\,.

(8.2)

The mean and variance are

\displaystyle\mu_{r,k,-g}

\displaystyle=\mu_{r,k,0}+(r-1)g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;r\mu_{1,k,0}+(r-1)g\,,

(8.3a)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{r,k,-g}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2}_{r,k,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;r\sigma^{2}_{1,k,0}\,.

(8.3b)

The expressions for the pgf and mean correct misprints in
[
16
]
.
Next, eq. (
8.1
) furnishes the key to computing the moments and factorial moments for
G
.
We reuse the notation
\mathscr{M}_{(i)}
from Sec.
7
but attach arguments
(r,k,\ell)=(r,k,-g)
to clarify the dependency on its arguments.
We obtain, analogous to eqs. (
4.6
) and eq. (
5.2
) for the factorial moments and raw moments respectively,

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{(n)}(r,k,-g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\hat{M}_{(n-i)}((r-1)g)_{(i)}\,,

(8.4a)

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{n}(r,k,-g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\hat{M}_{n-i}((r-1)g)^{i}\,.

(8.4b)

The central moments are the same as those for a gap of zero, as was noted above for the variance.

9
Conclusion

Most of this note was devoted to the negative binomial distribution NB
(k,r)
of Type I,
which is the probability distribution for a sequence of independent Bernoulli trials
(with success parameter
p\in(0,1)
) with
r
nonoverlapping success runs of length
\geq k
.
We presented a new, more concise, expression for its probability mass function,
and also noted that our expression can be succinctly written using hypergeometric functions.
In this context, the published literature employs two different indexing schemes to count the trials,
hence care was taken to distinguish between them and present expressions for both schemes.
We also presented new expressions (as combinatorial sums) for the moments and factorial moments, as opposed to only the mean and variance (which are already known).
We also briefly discussed the central moments, displaying in particular expressions for the skewness and kurtosis.
Next, we presented an alternative non-combinatorial viewpoint, where the probability mass function was expressed as
a sum of powers of the roots of the auxiliary equation associated to the distribution.
This non-combinatorial formalism allowed us to derive expressions for the factorial moments not only for nonoverlapping success runs,
but also for runs with an overlap of
\ell
, where
\ell\in[0,k-1]
.
The case
\ell=k-1
is the negative binomial distribution NB
(k,r)
of Type III.
The non-combinatorial formalism also treats the scenario of a minimum gap between success runs, effectively a ‘negative overlap’
g=-\ell>0
.
Expressions for the moments and factorial moments were presented.

We can derive the probability mass function directly from the probability generating function in eq. (
7.1
).
We employ
F
indexing.
We use the binomial theorem to expand the pgf in powers of
s
and temporarily set the upper limits on the sums to
\infty
and determine their exact values later.
The pgf in this case is

\begin{split}\psi(s,k)=\phi(s,k)^{r}&=\frac{p^{rk}s^{rk}(1-ps)^{r}}{(1-s)^{r}}\frac{1}{(1+qp^{k}s^{k+1}/(1-s))^{r}}\\
&=p^{rk}s^{rk}(1-ps)^{r}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\binom{r+i-1}{r-1}\frac{q^{i}p^{ik}s^{i(k+1)}}{(1-s)^{r+i}}\\
&=p^{rk}s^{rk}(1-ps)^{r}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\binom{r+i-1}{r-1}q^{i}p^{ik}s^{i(k+1)}\sum_{u=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+i+u-1}{r+i-1}s^{u}\\
&=\dots\\
&=p^{rk}s^{rk}\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}s^{v}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{v-j}p^{v-j}\binom{r}{v-j}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}q^{i}p^{ik}\binom{r+i-1}{r-1}\binom{r+j-ik-1}{r+i-1}\,.\end{split}

(10.1)

The probability
P_{n}
is the coefficient of
s^{n}
, hence set
n=v+rk
or
v=n-rk
.

P_{n}=p^{rk}\sum_{j=0}^{j_{\max}}(-1)^{n-rk-j}p^{n-rk-j}\binom{r}{n-rk-j}\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\max}}(-1)^{i}q^{i}p^{ik}\binom{r+i-1}{r-1}\binom{r+j-ik-1}{r+i-1}\,.

(10.2)

The value of
i_{\max}
is given by
j-ik=i
hence
i\leq\lfloor j/(k+1)\rfloor
.
The value of
j_{\max}
is given by
0=n-rk-j
hence
j\leq n-rk
.
Then

P_{n}=p^{rk}\sum_{j=0}^{n-rk}(-1)^{n-rk-j}p^{n-rk-j}\binom{r}{n-rk-j}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor j/(k+1)\rfloor}(-1)^{i}q^{i}p^{ik}\binom{r+i-1}{r-1}\binom{r+j-ik}{r+i}\,.

(10.3)

The sums in eq. (
10.3
) are a double nested sum,
as opposed to the multinomial sum derived by
Philippou, Georghiou, and Philippou (Theorem 3.1 in
[
6
]
),
who employed the multinomial theorem to expand the pgf in eq. (
7.1
).
The sums in eq. (
10.3
) are not as concise but are equivalent to eq. (
3.4
).
The factorial moment
\hat{M}_{(n)}
is given by the
n^{th}
partial derivative of the pgf via

\begin{split}\hat{M}_{(n)}&=\biggl[\frac{\partial^{n}\psi(s,k)}{\partial s^{n}}\biggr]_{s=1}\\
&=n!p^{rk}\sum_{v=n-rk}^{\infty}\binom{v+rk}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{v-rk}(-1)^{v-j}p^{v-j}\binom{r}{v-j}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor j/(k+1)\rfloor}(-1)^{i}q^{i}p^{ik}\binom{r+i-1}{r-1}\binom{r+j-ik-1}{r+i-1}\,.\end{split}

(10.4)

This does not look as elegant as eq. (
4.8

as opposed to the more complicated

-fold nesting in eq. (
4.8
).
However, the sum over

1.

(11.1)

1.

\begin{split}P(M_{n}^{(k)}=r)&=\sum_{m=r}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{k+1}\right\rfloor}(-1)^{m-r}\binom{m}{r}p^{mk}q^{m-1}\biggl[\binom{n-mk+1}{m}-\binom{n-mk}{m}+q\binom{n-mk}{m}\biggr]\\

(11.4)

\begin{split}P(\textrm{NB}_{k,r}=n)&=\sum_{m=r}^{\lfloor\frac{n+1}{k+1}\rfloor}(-1)^{m-r}\binom{m-1}{r-1}p^{mk}q^{m-1}\biggl[\binom{n-mk}{m-1}-\binom{n-mk-1}{m-1}+q\binom{n-mk-1}{m-1}\biggr]\\

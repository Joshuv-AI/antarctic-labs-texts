. Let f f be a primitive form with respect to SL 2 ​ ( ℤ ) {\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}) . Then, we propose a conjecture on the congruence between the Klingen-Eisenstein lift of the Duke-Imamoglu-Ikeda lift of f f and a certain lift of a vector valued Hecke eigenform with respect to Sp 2 ​ ( ℤ ) \mathrm{Sp}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}) . 1.
Introduction

Harder’s conjecture is one of the most fascinating conjectures in the arithmetic of automorphic forms. It plays an important role in constructing nontrivial elements of the Bloch-Kato Selmer group attached to a modular form (cf.
[
15
]
). Harder’s conjecture predicts that the Hecke eigenvalues of a primitive form for
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
are related with those of a certain Hecke eigenform for
\mathrm{Sp}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
modulo some prime ideal. We explain it more precisely. For a non-increasing sequence
{\bf k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
of non-negative integers we denote by
M_{\bf k}(\mathrm{Sp}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}))
and
S_{\bf k}(\mathrm{Sp}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}))
the spaces of modular forms and cusp forms of weight
{\bf k}
(or, weight
k
, if
{\bf k}=(\overbrace{k,\ldots,k}^{n})
) for
\mathrm{Sp}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
, respectively. (For the definition of modular forms, see Section 2). Let
f(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a(m,f)\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}mz)
be a primitive form in
S_{2k+j-2}({\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
, and suppose that a ‘big prime’
{\mathfrak{p}}
divides the algebraic part of
L(k+j,f)
. Then, Harder
[
17
]
conjectured that there exists a Hecke eigenform
F
in
S_{(k+j,k)}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
such that

\lambda_{F}(T(p))\equiv a(p,f)+p^{k-2}+p^{j+k-1}\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}}

for any prime number
p
, where
\lambda_{F}(T(p))
is the eigenvalue of the Hecke operator
T(p)
on
F
, and
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
is a prime ideal of
{\mathbb{Q}}(f)\cdot{\mathbb{Q}}(F)
lying above
{\mathfrak{p}}
. One of main difficulties in treating this congruence arises from the fact that this is not concerning the congruence between Hecke eigenvalues of two Hecke eigenforms of the same weight. Indeed, the right-hand side of the above is not the Hecke eigenvalue of a Hecke eigenform if
j>0
. Several attempts have been made to overcome this obstacle. Ibukiyama
[
21
]
,
[
23
]
proposed a half-integral weight version of Harder’s conjecture given as
congruences of Hecke eigenforms and related it to the original Harder’s conjecture
through his conjectural Shimura type correspondence for vector valued Siegel modular forms of degree two
(and this Shimura type conjecture was now proved by H. Ishimoto
[
34
]
).
This explains the Harder conjecture for odd
k
(
[
26
]
) and
the proved example of
congruence in
[
23
]
means the Harder conjecture for
(k,j)=(5,18)
.
In
[
5
]
, Bergström and Dummigan, among other things, reformulated Harder’s conjecture as congruence between a certain induced representation of
\pi_{f}
and
a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GSp}(2)
.
In
[
12
]
, Chenevier and Lannes gave several congruences between theta series of even unimodular lattices, and using Arthur’s endoscopic classification and Galois representation theoretic method, they, among other things, proved Harder’s conjecture for
(k,j)=(10,4)
.
In this paper we consider a conjecture concerning the congruence between two liftings to higher degree
of Hecke eigenforms (of integral weight) of degree two. More precisely, for the
f
above, let
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(f)
be the Duke-Imamoglu-Ikeda lift of
f
to the space of cusp forms of weight
{j\over 2}+k+{n\over 2}-1
for
\mathrm{Sp}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
with

n
even. For a sequence

\displaystyle{\bf k}=\Bigl(\overbrace{{j\over 2}+k+{n\over 2}-1,\ldots,{j\over 2}+k+{n\over 2}-1}^{n},\overbrace{{j\over 2}+{3n\over 2}+1,\ldots,{j\over 2}+{3n\over 2}+1}^{n}\Bigr)

with
k\geq n+2
, let
[{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(f)]^{{\bf k}}
be the Klingen-Eisenstein lift of
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(f)
to
M_{{\bf k}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}}))
.
Then, we propose the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1
.

(Conjecture
4.5
)
Let
k,j
and
{\bf k}
be as above. Let
f(z)\in S_{2k+j-2}({\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
be a primitive form and
{\mathfrak{p}}
a prime ideal of
{\mathbb{Q}}(f)
. Then under certain assumptions,
there exists a Hecke eigenform
F
in
S_{(k+j,k)}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
such that

\lambda_{{\mathscr{A}}^{(I)}_{2n}(F)}(T)\equiv\lambda_{[{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(f)]^{{\bf k}}}(T)\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}}

for any integral Hecke operator
T
. Here,
{\mathscr{A}}_{2n}^{(I)}(F)
is the lift of
F
to
S_{\bf k}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}}))
, called the lift of type
{\mathscr{A}}^{(I)}
, which will be defined in Theorem
4.2
. (As for the definition of integral Hecke operators, see Section 3.)

This conjecture implies Harder’s conjecture (cf. Theorem
4.8
).

The advantage of this formulation is that one can compare the Hecke eigenvalues of two Hecke eigenforms. Indeed, by using the same argument as in Katsurada-Mizumoto
[
39
]
, under the above assumption, we can prove that there exists a Hecke eigenform
G\in M_{{\bf k}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}}))
such that
G
is not a constant multiple of
[{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(f)]^{{\bf k}}
and

\lambda_{G}(T)\equiv\lambda_{[{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(f)]^{{\bf k}}}(T)\ \pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}}

for any integral Hecke operator
T
. Therefore, to prove the above conjecture, it suffices to show that
G
is a lift of type
{\mathscr{A}}^{(I)}
.
Here we expect that
G
can be taken as
{\mathscr{A}}^{(I)}
and indeed we will see that
in the cases
(k,j)=(10,4),(14,4)
and
(4,24)
using the dimension formula due to Taibi
https://otaibi.perso.math.cnrs.fr/dimtrace
and the numerical tables of Hecke eigenvalues due to Poor-Ryan-Yuen
[
50
]
and Ibukiyama-Katsurada-Poor-Yuen
[
30
]
. As a result, we prove Conjecture
4.5
and so Harder’s conjecture in those cases.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give a brief summary of Siegel modular forms, especially about their
{\mathbb{Q}}
-structures or
{\mathbb{Z}}
-structures. In Section 3, after giving a summary of several
L
-values, we state Harder’s conjecture. In Section 4, we introduce several lifts, and among other things define the lift of
{\mathscr{A}}^{(I)}
-type of a vector valued modular form in
S_{(k+j,k)}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
, and propose a conjecture and explain how this conjecture implies Harder’s conjecture.
In Section 5, we consider the pullback formula of the Siegel Eisenstein series with differential operators. In Section 6, we consider the congruence for vector valued Klingen-Eisenstein series, which is a generalization of
[
39
]
, and explain how the assumption that
{\mathfrak{p}}
divides the algebraic part of
L(k+j,f)
for
f\in S_{2k+j-2}({\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
gives the congruence between
[{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(f)]^{{\bf k}}
and another Hecke eigenform in
M_{{\bf k}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}}))
. In Section 7, we give a formula for the Fourier coefficients of the Klingen-Eisenstein series, from which we can confirm some assumption
in our main results. In Section 9, we state our main results, which confirm our conjecture, and so Harder’s.

In a subsequent paper, we will prove Conjecture
4.5
and so Harder’s in more general setting, that is, in the case
k
is even and
j\equiv 0\text{ mod }4
, that is, we will prove these conjectures without using the dimension formula or the computation of Hecke eigenvalues of Siegel modular forms
(cf.
[
4
]
).

\displaystyle L_{p}(X,F,{\rm Sp})\equiv L_{p}(X,f)(1-p^{k-2}X)(1-p^{j+k-1}X)\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}}.

In particular,

\lambda_{F}(T(p))\equiv p^{k-2}+p^{j+k-1}+a(p,f)\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}}.

Remark 3.8
.

(1)

The original version of Harder’s conjecture did not mention what “largeness” of
{\mathfrak{p}}
means.

(2)

To formulate Harder’s conjecture we must choose the periods
c_{s}(f)
in an appropriate way. The original
version of Harder’s conjecture did not specify them. After that, Harder suggested assuming another type of divisibility condition instead of the divisibility of
L(k+j,f;c_{s}(f))
in his conjecture (cf.
[
18
]
). However, it does not seem so easy to confirm such a condition.

(3)

The original version of Harder’s conjecture, which states only the last congruence on
\lambda_{F}(T(p))
, is naturally included in the above Euler factor
version since we have

\displaystyle L_{p}(X,F,\mathrm{Sp})=

\displaystyle 1-\lambda_{F}(T(p))X+\lambda_{F}(\widetilde{\rho}_{2,1}(p))X^{2}

\displaystyle-\lambda_{F}(T(p))p^{2k+j-3}X^{3}+p^{4k+2j-6}X^{4},

and

L_{p}(X,f)=1-a(f,p)X+p^{2k-j-3}X^{2}.

(4)

The above congruence is trivial in the case
k
is even and
j=0
. Indeed, for the Saito-Kurokawa lift
F
of
f
,
we have

L_{p}(X,F,{\rm Sp})=L_{p}(X,f)(1-p^{k-2}X)(1-p^{k-1}X),

so we have equality, not only congruence.

To avoid the ambiguity in (1) and (2) of Remark
3.8
, we propose the following conjecture, which we also call Harder’s conjecture.

Let

and

be as that in Conjecture

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
n_{1}
,
m
be integers such that
1\leq m\leq n_{1}
. Let
U
be an
m\times n_{1}
matrix of variables.
Let
Q(U)
be a (scalar valued) polynomial in the components of
U
such that

Q(BU)=\det(B)^{l}Q(U)
for any
B\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
. Then

Q(U)
is a linear combination of products
\prod_{i=1}^{l}U_{I_{i}}
, where

I_{i}\subset\{1,\ldots,n_{1}\}
with
|I_{i}|=m
and
U_{I_{i}}
is the
m\times m
minor consisting of

p_{\nu}
-th columns
for
p_{\nu}\in I_{i}
.

Proof.

We regard
B=(b_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq m}
as a matrix of variables and define a matrix of operators by

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}B}=\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}b_{ij}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq m}.

We consider

\det\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}B}\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{m}}sgn(\sigma)\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}b_{1\sigma(1)}}\cdots\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}b_{m\sigma(m)}},

where
S_{m}
is the permutation group on
m
letters.
By Cayley type identity (
[
11
]
), we have

\det\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}B}\right)\det(B)^{l}=(l)_{m}\det(B)^{l-1},

where
(x)_{m}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+m-1)
is the ascending Pochhammer symbol,
so by the assumption
Q(BU)=\det(B)^{l}Q(U)
, we have

\det\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}B}\right)Q(BU)=(l)_{m}\det(B)^{l-1}Q(U).

Repeating this process, we have

\det\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}B}\right)^{l}Q(UB)=(l)_{m}(l-1)_{m}\cdots(1)_{m}Q(U).

On the other hand, writing
U=(u_{i\nu})
and
BU=(v_{i\nu})
. we have

v_{i\nu}=\sum_{p=1}^{m}b_{ip}u_{p\nu}
and

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}b_{i\sigma(i)}}(Q(BU))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{\nu=1}^{n_{1}}\frac{{\partial}v_{j\nu}}{{\partial}b_{i\sigma(i)}}\frac{{\partial}Q}{{\partial}v_{j\nu}}(BU)=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n_{1}}u_{\sigma(i)\nu}\frac{{\partial}Q}{{\partial}v_{i\nu}}(BU).

So we have

\det\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}B}\right)Q(BU)=\sum_{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{m}=1}^{n_{1}}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{m}}sgn(\sigma)u_{\sigma(1)\nu_{1}}\cdots u_{\sigma(m)\nu_{m}}\right)\frac{{\partial}^{m}Q}{{\partial}v_{1\nu_{1}}\cdots{\partial}v_{m\nu_{m}}}(BU).

Here if we fix
\nu_{1}
, …,
\nu_{m}
, then we have

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{m}}sgn(\sigma)u_{\sigma(1)\nu_{1}}\cdots u_{\sigma(m)\nu_{m}}=\begin{vmatrix}u_{1\nu_{1}}&\cdots&u_{1\nu_{m}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
u_{m\nu_{1}}&\cdots&u_{m\nu_{m}}\end{vmatrix}.

If
\nu_{i}=\nu_{j}
for some
i\neq j
, then of course this is
0
and
if the cardinality
|I|
of
I=\{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{m}\}
is
m
, then this is

U_{I}
up to sign.
By taking
B
to be scalar, we see that
Q(U)
is a homogeneous polynomial of
the total degree
ml
. so the
ml
-th derivatives of
Q(U)
is a constant.
So we see that

\det\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}B}\right)^{l}Q(BU)

is a linear combination of
l
products of

m\times m
minors of
U
.
Since this is equal to
(l)_{m}(l-1)_{m-1}\cdots(1)_{m}Q(U)
,
we see that
Q(U)
is a linear combination of
l
products of minors of degree
m

of
U
.
∎

Theorem A.1

(Explicit multiplicity formula for
S_{{\bf k}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}))
)
.

Let
{\bf k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
be a sequence of positive integers such that

k_{1}\geq\dots\geq k_{n}\geq n+1
.

(1)

We can associate each Hecke eigenform
G
in
S_{{\bf k}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}))

with its

-parameter
\psi_{G}
which is a formal sum

where
\pi

satisfy the following conditions (a) to (f):

(a)

\pi_{i}=\otimes_{v}\pi_{i,v}

is an irreducible unitary cuspidal automorphic self-dual representation of
\mathrm{PGL}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})

(b)

d_{i}
is a positive integer such that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}d_{i}=2n+1

(c)

\pi_{i,p}
is unramified for any prime

(d)

if we denote the infinitesimal character of
\pi_{i,\infty}
by

which is a semisimple conjugacy class of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n_{i}}({\mathbb{R}})
,
and if we set

then the eigenvalues of the semisimple conjugacy class

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}c_{i,\infty}\otimes e_{d_{i}}

in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2n+1}({\mathbb{R}})
are the distinct integers

k_{1}-1>\dots>k_{n}-n>0>-(k_{n}-n)>\dots>-(k_{1}-1);

(e)

there exists
1\leq i_{0}\leq t
such that

d_{i_{0}}=1
,
n_{i_{0}}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
, and

n_{i}d_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{4}
for any

;

(f)

the sign condition

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq t\\
j\not=i\end{subarray}}\varepsilon(\pi_{i}\times\pi_{j})^{\min\{d_{i},d_{j}\}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-1)^{\frac{n_{i}d_{i}}{4}}&&\text{if $d_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{2}$},\\
&(-1)^{|K_{i}|}&&\text{if $d_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{2}$}\end{aligned}\right.

,
where

is the set of odd indices

.

The
A
-parameter

is characterized by

where the right-hand side is a product of the finite parts of the Godement–Jacquet

-functions.

(2)

Conversely, for any formal sum
\psi=\boxplus_{i=1}^{k}\pi_{i}[d_{i}]
satisfying (a)–(f) above,
there exists a Hecke eigenform

such that

.

(3)

For two Hecke eigenforms

in

,
the following are equivalent:

•

Here, a formal sum means that it is an equivalence class defined so that

\boxplus_{i=1}^{t}\tau_{i}[d_{i}]\sim\boxplus_{i=1}^{t^{\prime}}\tau^{\prime}_{i}[d^{\prime}_{i}]

Standing waves for the NLS equation with competing nonlocal and local nonlinearities: the double L 2 L^{2} -supercritical case
Shuai Yao, Hichem Hajaiej, Juntao Sun, Tsung-fang Wu

Abstract
We investigate the NLS equation with competing Hartree-type and power-type nonlinearities i ​ ∂ t ψ + Δ ​ ψ + γ ⁡ ( I α ∗ | ψ | p ) ​ | ψ | p − 2 ​ ψ + μ ​ | ψ | q − 2 ​ ψ = 0 , ∀ ( t , x ) ∈ ℝ × ℝ N , \begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi+\gamma(I_{\alpha}\ast|\psi|^{p})|\psi|^{p-2}\psi+\mu|\psi|^{q-2}\psi=0,&\text{ }\forall(t,x)\in\mathbb{R\times R}^{N},\end{array} where γ ​ μ < 0 \gamma\mu<0 . We establish conditions for the local well-posedness in the energy space. Under the double L 2 L^{2} -supercritical case, we prove the existence and multiplicity of standing waves with prescribed mass by developing a constraint method when γ ​ < 0 , μ > ​ 0 \gamma<0,\mu>0 and γ > 0 , μ < 0 , \gamma>0,\mu<0, respectively. Moreover, we prove weak orbital stablility and strong instability of standing waves by considering a suitable local minimization problem and by analyzing the fibering mapping, respectively. A new analysis of the fibering mapping is performed in this work. We believe that it is innovative as it was not discussed at all in any previous results. The lower bound rate of blow-up solutions for the Cauchy problem is given as well. Due to the different “strength” of the two types of nonlinearities, we find some essential differences in our results between these two competing cases. We will be dealing with two major scenarios that are totally different from each other due to their diverse geometric structure. This leads to surprising findings. Additionally, the competing pure power-type nonlinearities case can be derived from our study thanks to a good choice of the kernel of the Hartree term.



Keywords:
NLS equations; competing nonlinearities; variational
methods; dynamics.

Introduction

Consider the Cauchy problem for the NLS equation with combined Hartree-type
and power-type nonlinearities:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi+\gamma(I_{\alpha}\ast|\psi|^{p})|\psi|^{p-2}\psi+\mu|\psi|^{q-2}\psi=0,\\
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\psi(t,x)
is the complex valued function in the spacetime
\mathbb{R\times R}^{N}
(
N\geq 1
),
2_{\alpha}\leq p\leq 2_{\alpha}^{\ast}
(
2_{\alpha}=\frac{N+\alpha}{N},

2_{\alpha}^{\ast}=\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}

if
N\geq 3
and
2_{\alpha}^{\ast}=\infty
if
N=1,2
),
2<q\leq 2^{\ast}
(
2^{\ast}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3
and
2^{\ast}=\infty
if
N=1,2
), the parameters
\gamma,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
I_{\alpha}
is
the Riesz potential of order
\alpha\in(0,N)
defined by

I_{\alpha}=\frac{A(N,\alpha)}{|x|^{N-\alpha}}\quad\text{with}\quad A(N,\alpha)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-\alpha}{2})}{\pi^{N/2}2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}\text{ for each }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\},

and
\ast
the convolution product on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The exponent
2_{\alpha}
(
2_{\alpha}^{\ast}
) is the lower (upper) critical exponent
in the sense of Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, and
2^{\ast}
is the
Sobolev critical exponent in the sense of Sobolev inequality.

Equation (
1
) appears in the context of various physical models. It
seems to be first introduced in the pioneering work of Fröhlich and
Pekar’s to describe the model of the polaron, where free electrons in an
ionic lattice interact with phonons associated with deformations of the
lattice or with the polarisation that it creates in the medium (interaction
of an electron with its own hole)
[
18
,
19
]
. It is a large system model
of non-relativistic bosonic atoms and molecules under an attractive
interaction that is weaker and has a longer range than the one of the
nonlinear Schrödinger equation, where the interaction potential
I_{\alpha}
is formally Dirac’s delta at the origin
[
20
]
. Moreover, (
1
) also arises as a mean-field limit of a bosonic system with
attractive two-body interactions which can be taken rigorously in many cases

[
20
,
34
]
.

For the Cauchy problem (
1
), when the initial data
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we can study the finite Hamiltonian or the finite
energy solutions, which satisfy energy (Hamiltonian), mass and momentum
conservations:

E_{\gamma,\mu}[\psi(t)]:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}dx-\frac{\gamma}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|\psi|^{p})|\psi|^{p}dx-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\psi|^{q}dx=E_{\gamma,\mu}[\psi_{0}],

M[\psi(t)]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\psi|^{2}dx=M[\psi_{0}],

P[\psi(t)]:=\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{\psi}\nabla\psi dx=P[\psi_{0}].

An important feature related to the nonlinear evolution equations such as (
1
) is the study of standing waves. A standing wave of (
1
)
is a solution of the form

\psi(t,x)=e^{i\lambda t}u(x),

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
u
satisfies the stationary equation

-\Delta u+\lambda u=\gamma(I_{\alpha}\ast\left|u\right|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(2)

At this point, there are two different ways to deal with (
2
)
according to the role of the frequency
\lambda
:

(i)
the frequency
\lambda
is a fixed and assigned parameter;

(ii)
the frequency
\lambda
is an unknown of the problem.

For case
(i),
one can see that solutions of (
2
) can be obtained
as critical points of the functional defined in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by

I(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx-\frac{\gamma}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{p}dx-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx.

This case has been extensively studied by many authors in the last years,
see for example
[
2
,
21
,
42
,
45
,
50
,
51
,
52
]
and the references therein.

Alternatively one can look for solutions of (
2
) with
\lambda

unknown. In this case
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
appears as a Lagrange
multiplier and
L^{2}
-norms of solutions are prescribed, which are usually
called normalized solutions. This study seems particularly meaningful from
the physical point of view, since standing waves of (
1
) conserve
their mass along time, and physicists are very interested in the stability.
Here we focus on this issue. For
c>0
given, we consider the problem of
finding solutions to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\lambda u=\gamma(I_{\alpha}\ast\left|u\right|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=c.&\end{array}\right.

(
P_{c}
)

Solutions of
(P_{c})
can be obtained as critical points of the energy
functional
E_{\gamma,\mu}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

given by

E_{\gamma,\mu}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\gamma}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{p}dx-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx

on the constraint

S(c):=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ }|\text{ }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=c\right\}.

It is easy to show that
E_{\gamma,\mu}
is a well-defined and
C^{1}

functional on
S(c)
for
2_{\alpha}\leq p\leq 2_{\alpha}^{\ast}
and
2<q\leq 2^{\ast}.

As we will see, the number and properties of normalized solutions to
(P_{c})
are strongly affected by further assumptions on the exponents
p,q
, the parameters
\gamma,\mu
and the mass
c.
When the problem involves
pure power-type nonlinearity, i.e.
\gamma=0
and
\mu>0,
from the
variational point of view,
E_{0,\mu}
is bounded from below on
S\left(c\right)
for
2<q<\overline{q}:=2+4/N,
here
\overline{q}
is called the
L^{2}
-critical exponent for the power nonlinearity. Thus, for each
c>0
, a
ground state can be found as a global minimizer of
E_{0,\mu}
on
S\left(c\right)
, see
[
41
,
46
,
47
]
. Moreover, the set of ground states is
orbitally stable
[
14
,
25
,
46
]
. For
\overline{q}<q<2^{\ast}
, on the
contrary,
E_{0,\mu}
is unbounded from below on
S\left(c\right)
.
Jeanjean
[
27
]
has developed an approach to show that we can still define
normalized ground state and they do exist for
c>0
also in this case.
Recently, Hajaiej and Song
[
26
]
provided a general approach to prove
the existence of normalized solutions for this kind of problem.

When the problem only involves the Hartree-type nonlinearity, i.e.
\gamma>0
and
\mu=0,
(
2
) is usually called the nonlinear Choquard or
Choquard–Pekar equation. It has several physical origins. In the case where

N=3
and
\alpha=p=2,
the problem

-\Delta u+\lambda u=\gamma\left(I_{2}\ast u^{2}\right)u\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}

(3)

appears as a model in quantum theory of a polaron at rest
[
43
]
. The
time-dependent form of (
3
) also describes the self-gravitational
collapse of a quantum mechanical wave-function
[
44
]
. Lieb
[
39
]

proved the existence and uniqueness of normalized solutions for (
3
)
on
S\left(c\right)
by using symmetrization techniques, and Lions
[
40
]

studied the existence and stability issues of normalized solutions for (
3
) on
S\left(c\right)
. Recently, for (
2
) with
\gamma>0

and
\mu=0,
Li and Ye
[
37
]
showed that if
N\geq 3
and
\overline{p}:=\frac{N+\alpha+2}{N}<p<2_{\alpha}^{\ast},
then
E_{\gamma,0}
has a
MP geometry on
S(c)
and there exists a normalized solution for each
c>0.

Usually,
\overline{p}
is called the
L^{2}
-critical exponent for the
Hartree-type nonlinearity.

When the problem involves both Hartree-type and a power-type nonlinearities,
the situation seems to become more complicated. We summarize two different
cases as follows.

\bullet
The mixed attractive case (
\gamma>0,\mu>0
).
Li
[
35
,
36
]
studied the upper critical case of
(P_{c}),
i.e.
p=2_{\alpha}^{\ast}
and
2<q<2^{\ast}.
She concluded that
(P_{c})
has one radial
normalized solution when
\overline{q}<q<2^{\ast}
while two radial
normalized solutions when
2<q<\overline{q}.
Moreover, qualitative
properties and stability of normalized solutions are described as well. For
the lower critical case of
(P_{c}),
i.e.
p=2_{\alpha}
and
2<q\leq 2^{\ast},
the first and third authors of the current paper proved several
nonexistence and existence results by introducing a method of adding mass
term and Sobolev subcritical approximation in
[
58
]
.

\bullet
The mixed competing case (
\gamma\mu<0
).
In the
specific case of
N=3,\gamma<0,\mu>0
and
\alpha=p=2,
(
1
) is
reduced to the well-known Schrödinger–Poisson equations, appearing in
the physical literature as an approximation of the Hartree–Fock model of a
quantum many–body system of electrons, see
[
33
]
for a mathematical
introduction to Hartree–Fock method. Clearly, the Hartree-type nonlinearity
in Schrödinger–Poisson equations belongs to the
L^{2}
-subcritical
case, since
2=p<\overline{p}=7/3.
Bellazzini and Siciliano
[
8
]
and
Jeanjean and Luo
[
29
]
obtained a ground state as a global minimizer of
E_{\gamma,\mu}
on
S(c)
for
2<q<3
and
c>0
sufficiently small, and
for
3<q<10/3
and
c>0
sufficiently large, respectively. Bellazzini et al.

[
7
]
found a mountain-pass normalized solution for
10/3<q<6
and
c>0

sufficiently small. Moreover, the strong instability of standing waves at
the mountain pass energy level was established. Later, Feng et al.
[
17
]

proved that the standing waves are strongly unstable by blow up for
q=10/3.

The study of standing waves with prescribed mass for NLS equation with
competing Hartree-type and power-type nonlinearities has attracted many
mathematicians. However, so far only a very few results exist in our
context,
[
7
,
8
,
17
,
29
]
, and many interesting cases are still open,
such as the double
L^{2}
-supercritical case, since its geometric structure
is totally different from others, leading us to some surprising results.

In this paper, our aim is to develop a method that will enable us to
establish optimal results on the existence, multiplicity and dynamics of
standing waves with a prescribed mass for (
1
) with
\gamma\mu<0

and
p,q
being both
L^{2}
-supercritical exponents. More precisely, we
firstly establish the local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1
) in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
Secondly, the existence and
multiplicity of normalized solutions of
(P_{c})
are studied under the
cases of
\gamma<0,\mu>0
, and
\gamma>0,\mu<0
. As a result, the
corresponding standing waves with prescribed mass of (
1
) are
obtained. We note that there are some essential differences in the geometry
of the corresponding functionals
E_{\gamma,\mu}
between the two above
competing cases, since “the strength” of Hartree-type and
power-type nonlinearities are different. Thirdly, we study the stability and
instability of standing waves with prescribed mass for (
1
).
Finally, we compute the lower bound rate of blow-up solutions for the Cauchy
problem (
1
). It is worth emphasizing that one has to face more
challenges due to the interaction of the double
L^{2}
-supercritical
nonlinearities. In order to overcome these considerable difficulties, new
ideas and techniques have been explored. More details will be discussed in
the next subsection.

1.1
Main results

Before stating our main results, we agree that when
p=2_{\alpha}^{\ast}

or
q=2^{\ast}
is involved, we always assume that
N\geq 3.
Firstly, we
deal with the local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1
) in the
energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with standard contraction mapping
argument.

Theorem 1.1

Let
N\geq 1,

\gamma
and
\mu
be nonzero real constants and
the initial data
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
If
\alpha\in(\max\{0,N-4\},N)
,
2\leq p\leq 2_{\alpha}^{\ast}
and
2<q\leq 2^{\ast}
(with additional assumption of smallness of
\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}
for
p=2_{\alpha}^{\ast}
or
q=2^{\ast}
), then
there exists
T_{max}
and a unique solution
\psi(t,x)\in C([0,T_{\max});H^{1})
of the Cauchy problem (
1
) such that either
T_{\max}=\infty
(
global existence
) or
T_{\max}<\infty
and
\|\psi(t,x)\|_{H^{1}}\rightarrow\infty
as
t\uparrow T_{\max}
(
blow up
). Moreover, if
|x|\psi_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
then the function
V(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2}|\psi(t,x)|^{2}dx

belongs to
C^{2}([0,T_{\max}))
, and the following Virial identities

V^{\prime}(t)=2\mathrm{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2}\bar{\psi}\partial_{t}\psi dx=4\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{\psi}x\cdot\nabla\psi dx

and

V^{\prime\prime}(t)=8\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}dx-\frac{\gamma(Np-N-\alpha)}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|\psi|^{p})|\psi|^{p}dx-\frac{\mu N(q-2)}{2q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\psi|^{q}dx\right)

hold for all
0\leq t<T_{\max}
.

Remark 1.2

For the non-perturbation case
\mu=0
and
p\geq 2
, the local
well-posedness of
H^{1}
-solutions of (
1
) was established in
[
3
,
23
]
. For the perturbation case
\mu\neq 0
and
p=2
, Fang and Han
obtained the local well-posedness in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(see
[
16
, Propositions 3.1 and 3.2]
). Theorem
1.1
generalizes the
results in
[
3
,
16
,
23
]
, in particular, where the double energy-critical
case is involved.

Next, we focus on the existence and multiplicity of standing waves with
prescribed mass.

Theorem 1.3

Let
N\geq 2,\gamma<0
and
\mu>0
. Assume that one of the two
following conditions holds:

(a)

\overline{p}<p<2_{\alpha}^{\ast},\overline{q}<q<2^{\ast}
with
2Np-Nq-2\alpha\leq 0
and
|\gamma|c^{\frac{N+\alpha-(N-2)p}{2}}
is
sufficiently small;

(b)

\overline{p}<p\leq 2_{\alpha}^{\ast},\overline{q}<q<2^{\ast}
with

2Np-Nq-2\alpha>0
and
|\gamma|
is sufficiently small.

Then
(P_{c})
has a radially symmetric solution
(\lambda^{-},u^{-})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
E_{\gamma,\mu}(u^{-})>0.
Moreover,
u^{-}
has exponential decay at infinity:

|u^{-}|\leq Ce^{-\delta|x|},\quad\text{for }|x|\geq R,

for some
C>0
,
\delta>0
and
R>0
.

We note that under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, the functional
E_{\gamma,\mu}
is unbounded from below on
S(c).
Then it will not be
possible to find a global minimizer. In order to seek for critical points of

E_{\gamma,\mu}
restricted to
S\left(c\right),
we shall use the
Pohozaev manifold
\mathcal{M}\left(c\right)
that contains all the
critical points of
E_{\gamma,\mu}
restricted to
S(c).
It is given by

\mathcal{M}(c):=\left\{u\in S(c)\text{ }|\text{ }Q(u)=0\right\},

(4)

where
Q(u)=0
is the Pohozaev type identity corresponding to (
2
).
For more details, we refer the reader to Section 2. As we shall see, for
Theorem
1.3

(a)
, the functional
E_{\gamma,\mu}
is bounded from
below on
\mathcal{M}\left(c\right),
a natural constraint of
E_{\gamma,\mu}
. We expect to find critical points with positive level by applying
the minimax method to
E_{\gamma,\mu}
restricted to
\mathcal{M}(c)\cap H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),
introduced by Bartsch and Soave
[
6
]
.
However, it is unclear if the solution
u^{-}
obtained by us is a ground
state of
(P_{c})
since it is difficult to verify that
\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}(c)}E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}(c)\cap H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}E_{\gamma,\mu}(u).

For Theorem
1.3

(b),
due to the complicated competing effect between
Hartree type nonlinearity and the power one, we can not guarantee that an
arbitrary
u\in S(c)
can always be projected onto
\mathcal{M}(c)
for
c>0
. Furthermore, it seems difficult to prove that the functional
E_{\gamma,\mu}
is bounded from below on
\mathcal{M}(c)
. For these reasons,
inspired by
[
15
]
, we shall introduce a submanifold of codimension
1

in
S(c)
given by

\Lambda(c):=V\cap\mathcal{M}(c),

where
V
is an open subset of
S(c)
defined as (
32
) in Section 4
satisfying the dilation
u_{s}:=s^{N/2}u(sx)\in V
for any
u\in V
and
s>0
, and then apply the minimax method to
E_{\gamma,\mu}
restricted to
\Lambda(c)\cap H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
Since
V
has a boundary, we
need to make sure that our deformation arguments take place inside
V
by
imposing on the parameter
\gamma
.

Remark 1.4

In recent years, there has been much attention on the existence of
normalized solution for the following Schrödinger equations with van der
Waals type potentials:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\lambda u=(|x|^{-\beta_{1}}\ast\left|u\right|^{2})u+\gamma(|x|^{-\beta_{2}}\ast\left|u\right|^{2})u&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=c,&\end{array}\right.

where
\beta_{1}\neq\beta_{2}
denotes the two-body potentials with
different width. We refer the reader to
[
12
,
31
]
for
\gamma>0
and
to
[
10
]
for
\gamma<0.
In particular, Bhimani et al.
[
10
]

established the existence of ground states for the above problem when
\gamma<0
and
0<\beta_{2}<\beta_{1}<\min\{N,4\}
. However, there is an
unresolved case, i.e.,
\gamma<0
and
2<\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}<\min\{N,4\}
. The method of Theorem
1.3

(b)
can be applied to address this case,
which can be regarded as a supplement to the previous works.

Theorem 1.5

Let
N\geq 2,\gamma>0
and
\mu<0.
Then the following
statements are true.

(a)

Assume that one of the four following conditions holds:

(i)

\overline{p}<p<2_{\alpha}^{\ast},\overline{q}<q<2^{\ast}
with
2Np-Nq-\alpha q>0
and
|\mu|c^{\frac{2N-(N-2)q}{4}}
is sufficiently small;

(ii)

\overline{p}<p<2_{\alpha}^{\ast},\overline{q}<q\leq 2^{\ast}

with
2Np-Nq-\alpha q\leq 0\leq 2Np-Nq-2\alpha.

Then
(P_{c})
has a ground state
(\lambda^{-},u^{-})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
E_{\gamma,\mu}(u^{-})>0.
In
particular, such solution
u^{-}
is radially symmetric.

(b)

If
\overline{p}<p<2_{\alpha}^{\ast}
and
\overline{q}<q\leq 2^{\ast}
with
2Np-Nq-2\alpha<0,
then for
c>0
sufficiently small,
(P_{c})
has two positive radially symmetric solutions
(\lambda^{\pm},u^{\pm})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
E_{\gamma,\mu}(u^{+})<0<E_{\gamma,\mu}(u^{-})
and
\|\nabla u^{-}\|_{2}<\|\nabla u^{+}\|_{2}.

Moreover,
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
have exponential decay at infinity.

(5)

Contrary to Theorem

2.3

Definition 2.5

.

[

5.1
Stability/Strong instability of standing waves

Firstly, we give the proof of Theorem
1.7
:
Following the
classical arguments of Cazenave and Lions
[
14
]
. Assume that there exist
an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, a sequence of initial data
\left\{u_{n}^{0}\right\}\subset U_{r}
and a time sequence
\left\{t_{n}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that the unique solution
\psi_{n}
of the
Cauchy problem (
1
) with initial data
u_{n}^{0}=\psi_{n}(0,\cdot)

satisfies

\text{dist}_{H^{1}}(u_{n}^{0},\mathcal{M}_{c}^{r_{0}})<\frac{1}{n}\text{ and
dist}_{H^{1}}(\psi_{n}(t_{n},\cdot),\mathcal{M}_{c}^{r_{0}})\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\left\{u_{n}^{0}\right\}\subset U_{r}
. Since
\text{dist}_{H^{1}}(u_{n}^{0},\mathcal{M}_{c}^{r_{0}})\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, the conservation laws
of the energy and mass imply that
\psi_{n}(t_{n},\cdot)
is a minimizing
sequence for
d^{+}(c)
provided
\psi_{n}(t_{n},\cdot)\subset\mathbf{A}_{r_{0}}
. Indeed, if
\psi_{n}(t_{n},\cdot)\subset(H^{1}\backslash\mathbf{A}_{r_{0}})
, then by the continuity there exists
\bar{t}_{n}\in[0,t_{n})
such that
\left\{\psi_{n}(\bar{t}_{n},\cdot)\right\}\subset\partial\mathbf{A}_{r_{0}}
. Hence, by Theorem
4.31
one has

E_{\gamma,\mu}(\psi_{n}(\bar{t}_{n},\cdot))\geq\inf_{u\in\partial_{\mathbf{A}_{r_{0}}}}E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)>\inf_{u\in\mathbf{A}_{r_{0}}}E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)=d^{+}(c),

which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\left\{\psi_{n}(t_{n},\cdot)\right\}
is a minimizing sequence for
d^{+}(c)
. Then there exists
v_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{r_{0}}
such that
\psi_{n}(t_{n},\cdot)\rightarrow v_{0}
in
H^{1}
, which contradicts with dist
{}_{H^{1}}(\psi_{n}(t_{n},\cdot),\mathcal{M}_{c}^{r_{0}})\geq\varepsilon_{0}.
This
completes the proof.

Define

\mathcal{G}_{1}:=\left\{u\in V\text{ }|\text{\ }E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)<\inf_{\Lambda^{-}(c)}E_{\gamma,\mu}\right\},\quad\mathcal{G}_{2}:=\left\{u\in U\text{ }|\text{\ }E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)<\inf_{\Phi^{-}(c)}E_{\gamma,\mu}\right\},

and

\mathcal{G}_{3}:=\left\{u\in S(c)\text{ }|\text{\ }E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)<\inf_{\mathcal{M}(c)}E_{\gamma,\mu}\right\}.

Proposition 5.1

Assume that the assumptions of Theorem
1.3

(b)
are
satisfied. Let
u\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
, then
g_{u}(s)
has a local maximum
point
s_{\ast}^{-}(u)
. If
0<s_{\ast}^{-}(u)<\min\{s_{u}^{\ast},1\}<\max\{s_{u}^{\ast},1\}<s_{\ast}^{+}(u)
and
|x|u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then the solution
\psi
of the Cauchy problem (
1
) with
initial datum
u
blows-up in finite time.

Proof.
Following the classical arguments of Glassey
[
24
]
and Berestycki and
Cazenave
[
9
]
. For every
u\in V
, the
g_{u}(s)
has a unique local
maximum point
s_{\ast}^{-}(u)
on
(0,s_{u}^{\ast})
and
g_{u}(s)
is
strict decreasing and concave on
(s_{\ast}^{-}(u),s_{u}^{\ast})
. We
claim that if
u\in V
, then

Q(u)\leq E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)-\inf_{u\in\Lambda^{-}(c)}E_{\gamma,\mu}.

(53)

By the fact that
0<s_{\ast}^{-}(u)<\min\{s_{u}^{\ast},1\}<\max\{s_{u}^{\ast},1\}<s_{\ast}^{+}(u)
, we have
Q(u)<0
, and

\displaystyle E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)=g_{u}(1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle g_{u}(s_{\ast}^{-}(u))-(s_{\ast}^{-}(u)-1)g_{u}^{\prime}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\gamma,\mu}(u_{s_{\ast}^{-}(u)})-|Q(u)|(1-s_{\ast}^{-}(u))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\Lambda^{-}(c)}E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)-|Q(u)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\Lambda^{-}(c)}E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)+Q(u),

which proves the claim.

Now let us consider the solution
\psi
with initial datum
u
. Since by
the assumption that
0<s_{\ast}^{-}(u)<\min\{s_{u}^{\ast},1\}<\max\{s_{u}^{\ast},1\}<s_{\ast}^{+}(u)
, and that the map
u\mapsto s_{\ast}^{-}(u)
is continuous, we also deduce that
0<s_{\ast}^{-}(\psi(\tau))<\min\{s_{\psi(\tau)}^{\ast},1\}<\max\{s_{\psi(\tau)}^{\ast},1\}<s_{\ast}^{+}(\psi(\tau))
for every
|\tau|
small, say
|\tau|<\overline{\tau}
. By (
53
) and recalling the assumption
E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)<\inf_{u\in\Lambda^{-}(c)}E_{\gamma,\mu}
, we have

\displaystyle Q(\psi(\tau))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E_{\gamma,\mu}(\psi(\tau))-\inf_{u\in\Lambda^{-}(c)}E_{\gamma,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\gamma,\mu}(u)-\inf_{u\in\Lambda^{-}(c)}E_{\gamma,\mu}:=-\delta<0,

for every such
\tau
, and hence
0<s_{\ast}^{-}(\psi(\overline{\tau}))<\min\{s_{\psi(\overline{\tau})}^{\ast},1\}<\max\{s_{\psi(\overline{\tau})}^{\ast},1\}<s_{\ast}^{+}(\psi(\overline{\tau}))
. By using the
continuity argument one has

Q(\psi(t))\leq-\delta\text{ for }t\in(T_{\min},T_{\max}).

Since
|x|u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it follows from the Virial identity

[
13
, Proposition 6.5.1]
that the function

f(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2}|\psi(t,x)|^{2}dx

is of class
C^{2}
with
f^{\prime\prime}(t)=8Q(\psi(t))\leq-8\delta<0

for
t\in(T_{\min},T_{\max})
. Integrating twice in time gives

0\leq f(t)\leq f(0)+f^{\prime}(0)t-4\delta t^{2}.

Since the right hand side becomes negative for
t
sufficiently large, it is
necessary that both
T_{\min}
and
T_{\max}
are bounded, which in turn
implies final time blow-up. The proof is complete.

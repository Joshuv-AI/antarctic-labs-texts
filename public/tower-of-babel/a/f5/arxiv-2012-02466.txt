RIS-Assisted Secure Transmission Exploiting Statistical CSI of Eavesdropper
Cen Liu, Chang Tian, and Peixi Liu

Abstract
We investigate the reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) assisted downlink secure transmission where only the statistical channel of eavesdropper is available. To handle the stochastic ergodic secrecy rate (ESR) maximization problem, a deterministic lower bound of ESR (LESR) is derived. We aim to maximize the LESR by jointly designing the transmit beamforming at the access point (AP) and reflect beamforming by the phase shifts at the RIS. To solve the non-convex LESR maximization problem, we develop a novel penalty dual convex approximation (PDCA) algorithm based on the penalty dual decomposition (PDD) optimization framework, where the exacting constraints are penalized and dualized into the objective function as augmented Lagrangian components. The proposed PDCA algorithm performs double-loop iterations, i.e., the inner loop resorts to the block successive convex approximation (BSCA) to update the optimization variables; while the outer loop adjusts the Lagrange multipliers and penalty parameter of the augmented Lagrangian cost function. The convergence to a Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) solution is theoretically guaranteed with low computational complexity. Simulation results show that the proposed PDCA scheme is better than the commonly adopted alternating optimization (AO) scheme with the knowledge of statistical channel of eavesdropper.

Reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS), which has given rise to the brand-new concept of “smart radio environment”, is regarded as a promising technology in the future 5G beyond and 6G cellular network

It is compatible with existing wireless communication systems and can bring many distinctive advantages, such as low power consumption, noise-free and self-interference-free under full-duplex mode. Therefore, RIS-assisted wireless transmission systems are getting growing interest from both academia and industry.

Physical layer security, as a key technology aiming at providing confidential message transmission and solving the privacy protection problem in the physical layer, has received considerable attention in the past decade

In an RIS-assisted wireless system, the received signal can be suppressed at the Eve while being boosted at the user. Thus, the deployment of RIS can bring a new degree of freedom (DoF) in the space domain, and the security performance can be further improved.

III
Problem Formulation

III-A

Ergodic Secrecy Rate Maximization

The secrecy rate from AP to user has the following expression

\displaystyle R_{\rm S}=\left(R_{\rm U}-R_{\rm E}\right)^{+}

(4)

where
\left(\cdot\right)^{+}\triangleq\max\{\cdot,0\}
is the non-negative operator, and

\displaystyle R_{\rm U}=\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{\left|\left(\bm{\phi}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{\rm U}+\mathbf{h}_{\rm AU}^{\mathsf{H}}\right)\mathbf{w}\right|^{2}}{\sigma_{\rm U}^{2}}\right)

(5)

\displaystyle R_{\rm E}=\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{\left|\left(\bm{\phi}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{\rm E}+\mathbf{h}_{\rm AE}^{\mathsf{H}}\right)\mathbf{w}\right|^{2}}{\sigma_{\rm E}^{2}}\right)

(6)

are the achievable rates at the user and Eve, respectively. Taking expectation over Eve’s statistical channels, we get the ergodic secrecy rate (ESR) as follows

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\rm S}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}_{\rm E}}\left[\left(R_{\rm U}-R_{\rm E}\right)^{+}\right]

(7)

where
\mathcal{H}_{\rm E}\triangleq\{\mathbf{h}_{\rm AE},\mathbf{h}_{\rm IE}\}
. Then the ESR maximization problem can be formulated as

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{\phi},\mathbf{w}}\

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\rm S}

(8a)

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}\

\displaystyle|\phi_{i}|=1,\ i=1,2,\ldots,N,

(8b)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}\|^{2}\leq P_{\rm max}.

(8c)

where (8b) and (8c) are unit-modulus constraint of RIS and maximum transmit power constraint at the AP, respectively.

IV
The Proposed Penalty Dual Convex Approximation Algorithm

Intuitively, AO can be applied to solve problem (12) as in
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
. But to ensure the convergence of the block coordinate descent (BCD) method (AO is a special case of BCD since there are only a pair of optimization variables), the subproblem of each block variable need to be constrained in a convex set and also to be solved to its unique global optimal
[
21
]
. As to primal problem (12), the former requirement can not be satisfied due to the non-convex constraint (8b) while the latter is hard to meet since the objective of each subproblem is non-convex, so the global optimality of the solutions can not be guaranteed. Specifically, semidefinite relaxation (SDR) as adopted in
[
9
,
12
]
and
[
13
]
can only provide an approximate local optimal solution.

To address above issues, in this section we propose a penalty dual convex approximation (PDCA) algorithm by employing the penalty dual decomposition (PDD) optimization framework in which the sequence of solution are guaranteed to converge to a KKT point
[
19
,
20
]
. By applying the PDD technique, we first obtain an
alternative formulation to (12) where an augmented
Lagrangian term is incorporated into the objective in order
to handle the unit-modulus constraint. We then develop an block successive convex approximation (BSCA) algorithm to solve the augmented Lagrangian problem in the inner loop of the PDD framework. Finally, we summarize the PDCA
joint beamforming algorithm and analyze its
computational complexity.

At the beginning, we drop the non-negative operator in the objective of (12) since zero rate can be achieved at least by adopting
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{0}
. Also, the outer logarithmic function is omitted because of its monotonicity.

IV-A

Augmented Lagrangian Problem

To start with, an augmented Lagrangian (AL) function with penalty parameter
\varrho
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\bm{\phi},\mathbf{w};\bm{\lambda})
while the dual variable
\bm{\lambda}
is introduced to deal with the constraint (8b). Further, let us define an
AL problem
(
P_{\varrho,\bm{\lambda}}
) as follows

\displaystyle(P_{\varrho,\bm{\lambda}})\ \max_{\bm{\phi},\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}\ \bigg\{

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\bm{\phi},\mathbf{w};\bm{\lambda})\triangleq\bar{R}_{\rm S}^{\rm lb}(\bm{\phi},\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\varrho}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[(|\phi_{i}|-1-\varrho\lambda_{i})^{2}-(\varrho\lambda_{i})^{2}\right]\bigg\}

(13)

where
\mathcal{W}=\left\{\mathbf{w}\ |\ \|\mathbf{w}\|^{2}\leq P_{\rm max}\right\}
. The proposed PDCA algorithm exhibits a double-loop
structure, where the outer loop updates the dual variable and
the penalty parameter while the inner loop seeks to optimize
the primal variables by solving problem (
P_{\varrho,\bm{\lambda}}
). The key to the
PDD method is the inner iterations for solving augmented
Lagrangian problems.

IV-B

Proposed BSCA Algorithm for Solving AL Problem

In the following, we develop an BSCA algorithm
to solve (
P_{\varrho,\bm{\lambda}}
) in the inner loop of the PDD optimization
framework. With only the convex constraint (8c) left, BCD-type algorithms can be used to solve problem (
P_{\varrho,\bm{\lambda}}
) to ensure the accessibility of a KKT solution of the primal problem (12) as suggested in
[
19
]
. Here, an inexact variant of BCD named block successive convex approximation (BSCA)
[
21
]
is adopted to solve above AL problem. Following a simple cyclic rule, convex approximation of the objective of each block variable is updated successively and in each iteration a sufficient decrease of the objective value can be yielded. By fixing the transmit beamformer in (
P_{\varrho,\bm{\lambda}}
), the RIS phase shifts design problem is stated as

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\phi}}\ \bigg\{h(\bm{\phi})\triangleq

\displaystyle-\frac{\bm{\phi}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{C}\bm{\phi}+2\Re\left[\bm{\phi}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{c}_{1}\right]+c_{2}}{\bm{\phi}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{D}\bm{\phi}+2\Re\left[\bm{\phi}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{d}_{1}\right]+d_{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\varrho}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(|\phi_{i}|-1-\varrho\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\bigg\}

(14)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm U}^{2}}\mathbf{H}_{\rm U}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{\rm U}^{\mathsf{T}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm E}^{2}}{\rm diag}(\mathbf{H}_{\rm AI}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{*})\mathbf{G}_{\rm I}{\rm diag}(\mathbf{H}_{\rm AI}\mathbf{w}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm U}^{2}}\mathbf{H}_{\rm U}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{h}_{\rm AU}^{*},\ \mathbf{d}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm E}^{2}}{\rm diag}(\mathbf{H}_{\rm AI}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{*})\mathbf{G}_{\rm AI}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{w},

\displaystyle c_{2}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm U}^{2}}\left|\mathbf{h}_{\rm AU}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{w}\right|^{2}+1,\ d_{2}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm E}^{2}}\|\mathbf{G}_{\rm A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+1.

(15)

A strongly convex quadratic function
\hat{h}(\bm{\phi},\bm{\phi}^{k})
is selected as a surrogate function in the neighborhood of the
k
th iteration point
\bm{\phi}^{k}

\hat{h}(\bm{\phi},\bm{\phi}^{k})\triangleq h(\bm{\phi}^{k})+\left<h^{\prime}(\bm{\phi}^{k}),\bm{\phi}-\bm{\phi}^{k}\right>+\frac{1}{2\alpha^{k+1}}\|\bm{\phi}-\bm{\phi}^{k}\|^{2}

who satisfies
\hat{h}^{\prime}(\bm{\phi}^{k},\bm{\phi}^{k})=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\bm{\phi}^{k},\mathbf{w};\bm{\lambda})}{\partial\bm{\phi}}
(Assumption (6.1) in
[
21
]
). The next iteration is generated by

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\phi}}^{k+1}=\arg\min_{\bm{\phi}}\hat{h}(\bm{\phi},\bm{\phi}^{k})=\bm{\phi}^{k}-\alpha^{k+1}\nabla h(\bm{\phi}^{k})

(16)

which is exactly the gradient descent step. Nevertheless,
\hat{\bm{\phi}}^{k+1}
may not be a point of sufficient decrease, which differs from that in block successive upper-bound minimization (BSUM)
[
22
]
since
\hat{h}
is only a local approximation of
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}
in BSCA. For this reason, backtracking line search approach with the
Armijo
step size selection rule
[
23
]
is exploited to search for a point of sufficient decrease, denoted by
\bm{\phi}^{k+1}
.

Fixing the RIS phase shifts in (
P_{\varrho,\bm{\lambda}}
) results in following transmit beamforming design problem at the AP

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}\ \bigg\{g(\mathbf{w})\triangleq-\frac{\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w}+1}{\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}+1}\bigg\}

(17)

where
\mathbf{A}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm U}^{2}}\mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{H}}
with
\mathbf{a}=(\bm{\phi}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{\rm U}+\mathbf{h}_{\rm AU}^{\mathsf{H}})^{\mathsf{H}}
and
\mathbf{B}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm E}^{2}}(\mathbf{G}_{\rm A}+\mathbf{B}_{1}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{G}_{\rm I}\mathbf{B}_{1}+\mathbf{B}_{2}+\mathbf{B}_{2}^{\mathsf{H}})
with
\mathbf{B}_{1}=\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{H}_{\rm AI}
,
\mathbf{B}_{2}=\mathbf{G}_{\rm AI}\mathbf{B}_{1}
. The problem (17) can be addressed by the same approach as stated above, with the only difference is that gradient projection, rather than gradient descent as (16), is performed as follows

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{k+1}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\mathbf{w}^{k}-\alpha^{k+1}\nabla g(\mathbf{w}^{k})\right]

(18)

where
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\cdot\right]
denotes the projection onto the convex set
\mathcal{W}
.

VI
Conclusion

We studied the RIS-assisted secure transmission with the knowledge of statistical CSI of eavesdropper. A lower bound of ESR is derived. Therefore, the stochastic ESR maximization problem is converted into a deterministic LESR maximization problem. By leveraging the proposed PDCA algorithm, the solution of this non-convex optimization problem is guaranteed to converge to a KKT point. Simulation results demonstrate that the security performance achieved by the proposed PDCA scheme is better than the commonly adopted AO scheme. The performance under perfect CSI settings are also given for comparison to acquire more useful insights.

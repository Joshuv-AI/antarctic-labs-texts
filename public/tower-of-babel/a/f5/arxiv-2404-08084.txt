Diagramatics for cyclic pointed fusion categories
Agustina Czenky

Abstract
. We give a parametrization of cyclic pointed categories associated to the cyclic group of order n n in terms of n n -th roots of unity. We also provide a diagramatic description of these categories by generators and relations, and use it to characterize their 2 2 -group of automorphisms.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbf{k}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. A
fusion category
over
\mathbf{k}
is a semisimple rigid tensor category over
\mathbf{k}
with finitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects, finite dimensional hom spaces, and such that the unit object is simple
[
3
]
. They are connected to many areas of mathematics and mathematical physics, which makes the problem of classifying them both an important yet difficult task. At the moment, a complete classification of fusion categories seems out of reach as it would yield a classification of finite groups.
Some progress has been made towards the classification of fusion categories of certain classes; this work concerns pointed fusion categories associated to finite cyclic groups.

An object
X
in a fusion category
\C
is called
invertible
if its evaluation and coevaluation morphisms are isomorphisms. A fusion category
\C
is called
pointed
if every simple object is invertible. Pointed fusion categories are well-understood in terms of finite group data: any pointed fusion category is equivalent to the category
\Vect_{G}^{\omega}
of finite dimensional
G
-graded vector spaces, where
G
is a finite group and
\omega
is a 3-cocycle on
G
with coefficients in
\mathbf{k}
codifying the associativity isomorphism. Monoidal functors between these categories are also classified: a functor
\Vect_{G_{1}}^{\omega_{1}}\to\Vect_{G_{2}}^{\omega_{2}}
is determined by a group homomorphism
f:G_{1}\to G_{2}
satisfying that
\omega_{1}
and
f^{*}\omega_{2}
are cohomologous, along with the data of a 2-cocycle on
G_{1}

[
2
, Section 2.6]
.

In this work, the hope is to provide a more efficient way of working with pointed fusion categories associated to finite cyclic groups. To this end, we show in Section
4
that for the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
of order
n
, pointed fusion categories associated to it have a classification which depends on a unique parameter: an
n
-th root of unity
\zeta\in\mathbf{k}
. We refer to the corresponding pointed category by
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
. We later use this parametrization in Section
5.1
to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

The category
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
has a description by generators and relations with diagramatics given in Definition
5.1
.

With this description, we get the following universal property:

Theorem 2
.

Tensor functors
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}\to\mathcal{C}
are in bijection with pairs
(X,\lambda)
, where
X\in\C
and
\lambda:X^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{1}
satisfies
\text{id}\otimes\lambda=\zeta\lambda\otimes\text{id}
.

For a proof of this theorem, see Section
5.2
. This gives a way of measuring when two cyclic pointed fusion associated to
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
are equivalent, and we give an answer for the number of equivalence classes for a fixed
n
in Section
6
. Lastly, we provide a description of the automorphism 2-group of
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
in Section
6
, summarized in the following theorem.

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, denote by
\delta_{j}
the simple object in
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
defined by
(\delta_{j})_{k}=0
if
k\neq j
, and
(\delta_{j})_{k}=\mathbf{k}.

Theorem
6.7
.

The 2-group
\Aut_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta})
has:

(1)

Objects: monoidal automorphisms
F_{X,\lambda}:\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
, where

•

X\simeq\delta_{j}
for some
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\times}
such that
\zeta^{j^{2}}=\zeta
,

•

\lambda
is an isomorphism
\delta_{j}^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{1},

•

F_{X,\lambda}
is the tensor functor obtained from mapping the generator
\delta_{1}\mapsto X
and the canonical isomorphism
\delta_{1}^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{1}
to
\lambda
.

(2)

Morphisms: Given two automorphisms
F_{X,\lambda},F_{Y,\lambda^{\prime}}:\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
, where
X\simeq\delta_{j}
and
Y\simeq\delta_{k}
for some
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\times}
, we have that

\displaystyle\Hom_{\Aut_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta})}(F_{X,\lambda},F_{Y,\lambda^{\prime}})\simeq\begin{cases}\Theta&\text{if }j=k,\\
0&\text{if }j\neq k,\end{cases}

where
\Theta
denotes the set of
n
-th roots of unity. Moreover,

\displaystyle\Aut_{\Aut_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta})}(F_{X,\lambda})\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{n},

for all
F_{X,\lambda}\in\Aut_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta})
.

2.
Preliminaries

We work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbf{k}
of characteristic zero.
We denote the ring of non-negative integers by
\mathbb{N}
, and by
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\times}
the rings of integers mod
n
and its units, respectively. For a category
\C
,
\Hom_{\C}(X,Y)
denotes the hom space from
X
to
Y
.

We recall some useful definitions regarding monoidal and tensor
categories. We refer the reader to
[
3
,
2
]
for a more detailed study of these topics.

2.1.
Monoidal categories

A
monoidal category
is a collection
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,a,\textbf{1},\emph{l},\emph{r})
where
\mathcal{C}
is a category,
\otimes:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
is a bifunctor, called
tensor product
,
\textbf{1}\in\mathcal{C}
is the
unit object
, and
a_{X,Y,Z}:(X\otimes Y)\otimes Z\xrightarrow{\sim}X\otimes(Y\otimes Z),\ r_{X}:X\otimes\textbf{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}X,\ \text{and}\ l_{X}:\textbf{1}\otimes X\xrightarrow{\sim}X
are natural isomorphisms, for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathcal{C}
, called
associativity
and
unit isomorphisms
, respectively, such that the diagrams

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\otimes(Y\otimes(Z\otimes W))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X\otimes(Y\otimes Z))\otimes W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\otimes((Y\otimes Z)\otimes W),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{X\otimes Y,Z,W}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{X,Y,Z}\otimes id_{W}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{X,Y,Z\otimes W}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces id_{X}\otimes a_{Y,Z,W}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{X,Y\otimes Z,W}}

and

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces id_{X}\otimes l_{Y}}

commute for all
X,Y,Z,W\in\C
.

A
monoidal subcategory
of
\C
is a monoidal category
(\mathcal{D},\otimes,a,\textbf{1},\emph{l},\emph{r})
, where
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{C}
is a full subcategory such that
\textbf{1}\in\mathcal{D}
, and
\mathcal{D}
is closed by tensor product. For any
X
in a monoidal category
\C
, we can consider the
monoidal subcategory generated by
X
, which we will denote by
\langle X\rangle
, with objects
X^{\otimes n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Given two monoidal categories
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,a,\textbf{1},\emph{l},\emph{r})
and
(\mathcal{C^{\prime}},\otimes^{\prime},a^{\prime},\textbf{1}^{\prime},\emph{l}\ ^{\prime},\emph{r}^{\prime})
, a
monoidal functor

\C\to\C^{\prime}
is a triple
(F,J,u),
where
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C^{\prime}}
is a functor,
u:\textbf{1}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\sim}F(\textbf{1})
is an isomorphism, and
J:\otimes^{\prime}\circ(F\times F)\to F\circ\otimes
is a natural isomorphism satisfying certain commutative diagrams, see for example
[
2
, Definition 2.4.1]
.
A monoidal functor
F:\C\to\mathcal{D}
is said to be
full
(respectively,
faithful
) if
\Hom_{\C}(X,Y)\xrightarrow{F}\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(Y))
is surjective (respectively, injective) for all
X,Y\in\C.
We say
F
is
essentially surjective
if for all
Y\in\mathcal{D}
there exists
X\in\C
such that
F(X)\simeq Y
;
F
is an
equivalence
if it is both essentially surjective and fully-faithful.

We call the morphisms
\operatorname{ev}_{X}
and
\operatorname{coev}_{X}

evaluation
and
co-evaluation
, respectively. The definition of a
right dual
for
X
is analogous. A monoidal category is said to be
rigid
if every object has a left and right dual.

An object
X
in a rigid category
\C
is said to be
invertible
if its evaluation and coevaluation morphisms are isomorphisms. A fusion category is said to be
pointed
if all simple objects are invertible.

A
2-group
is a rigid monoidal category in which every object is invertible and all morphisms are isomorphisms. A
2
-group
\mathbf{G}
is determined up to monoidal equivalence by a triple
(\pi_{0}(\mathbf{G}),\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G}),a),
where
\pi_{0}(\mathbf{G})
is the group of isomorphism classes of objects in
\mathbf{G}
,
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G})
is the group of automorphisms of the unit object, and
a\in H^{3}(\pi_{0}(\mathbf{G}),\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G}))

[
1
]
.

Given a monoidal category
\C
, let
\Aut_{\otimes}(\C)
be the monoidal category with objects the tensor autoequivalences of
\C
, morphisms monoidal natural isomorphisms, and tensor product given by composition. This makes
\Aut_{\otimes}(\C)
a
2
-group, with invariants
\pi_{0}(\Aut_{\otimes}(\C))
the group of isomorphism classes of automorphisms of
\C
and
\pi_{1}(\Aut_{\otimes}(\C))
the group of monoidal natural isomorphisms of the identity functor.

2.1.2.
Additive envelope

We call a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}

\mathbf{k}
-linear
if for each pair of objects
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
the set
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
has a
k
-module structure such that composition of morphisms is
k
-bilinear. We say
\mathcal{C}
is
additive
if it is
k
-linear and for every finite sequence of objects
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
in
\mathcal{C}
, there exists their direct sum
X_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus X_{n}
in
\mathcal{C}
.

Given a monoidal category
\C
, we can construct its
additive envelope

\mathcal{A(C)}
which has objects
finite formal sums
X_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus X_{m}
of objects in
\mathcal{C}
, and morphisms defined as follows. For every
X_{1}\oplus\dots X_{m},Y_{1}\oplus\dots Y_{n}
in
\mathcal{A(C)}
,

\Hom_{\mathcal{A(C)}}(X_{1}\oplus\dots X_{m},Y_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus Y_{n}):=\bigoplus\limits_{i,j}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i},Y_{j}).

Composition of morphisms is given by matrix multiplication.

2.2.
Tensor categories

A
tensor category

\mathcal{C}
is a
k
-linear locally finite rigid abelian monoidal category, such that
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(\textbf{1})\simeq\textbf{k}
, see e.g.
[
4
]
or
[
2
]
. For tensor categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, a
tensor functor

F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is a
k
-linear exact and faithful monoidal functor.

A
fusion category
is a tensor category which is semisimple and has a finite number of isomorphism classes of simple objects.

2.3.
Pointed categories

For a group
G
, we can consider the category
\operatorname{\text{Vec}_{G}}
of finite-dimensional
G
-graded vector spaces. That is, objects are vector spaces
V
with a decomposition
V=\bigoplus\limits_{g\in G}V_{g}
and morphisms are linear transformations that preserve the grading. Then
\operatorname{\text{Vec}_{G}}
is a monoidal category with tensor product given by

(V\otimes W)_{g}=\bigoplus_{x,y\in G\ :\ xy=g}V_{x}\otimes W_{y},

unit object
\delta_{1}
defined by
(\delta_{1})_{1}=\textbf{k}
and
(\delta_{1})_{g}=0
for
g\neq 1
, and associativity given by the identity.

More generally, given a 3-cocycle
\omega:G\times G\times G\to\textbf{k}^{\times}
, we have the monoidal category
\operatorname{\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}
which modifies the associativity isomorphism in
\operatorname{\text{Vec}_{G}}
in the following way.
For
U,V,W\in\operatorname{\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}},
define
a_{U,V,W}^{\omega}:(U\otimes V)\otimes W\xrightarrow{\sim}U\otimes(V\otimes W)
as the linear extension of

a_{U_{g},V_{h},W_{l}}^{\omega}:=\omega(g,h,l)\text{ id}_{U_{g}\otimes V_{h}\otimes W_{l}}:(U_{g}\otimes V_{h})\otimes W_{l}\xrightarrow{\sim}U_{g}\otimes(V_{h}\otimes W_{l}),

for all
g,h,l\in G.

When
G
is a finite group,
\operatorname{\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}
is a rigid fusion category. Here the simple objects are given by
\delta_{g}
defined by
(\delta_{g})_{h}:=0
if
h\neq g
, and
(\delta_{g})_{g}:=\mathbf{k},
for all
g\in G
. The dual of a simple object
\delta_{g}
is
(\delta_{g})^{*}=\delta_{g^{-1}}
, for all
g\in G
. In particular, all the simple objects in
\operatorname{\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}
are invertible, and so
\operatorname{\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}
is pointed.

It is well known that any pointed fusion category is equivalent to
\operatorname{\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}
for some finite group
G
and 3-cocycle
\omega

[
3
]
; cohomologically equivalent
\omega
’s give rise to equivalent categories
\operatorname{\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}
. Hence pointed fusion categories are classified in terms of finite group data. We will assume without loss of generality that the cocycle
\omega
is normalized, i.e.,
\omega(g,1,h)=1
, since any 3-cocycle
\omega
is cohomologous to a normalized one. This will help with computations, as it implies that
l_{\delta_{g}}=r_{\delta_{g}}=\text{id}_{\delta_{g}}
, for all
g\in G
.

5.2.
Universal property of
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}

Let
\C
be a tensor category. Now that we have a description of
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
by generators and relations (see Definition
5.1
), to define a tensor functor
F:\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}\to\C
it is enough to make a choice of:

(1)

an object
X\in\C
, and

(2)

an isomorphism
\lambda:X^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{1}
such that
\text{id}\otimes\lambda=\zeta\lambda\otimes\text{id}
.

We refer to the corresponding functor by
F_{X,\lambda}
. That is,
F_{X,\lambda}:\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}\to\C
is the tensor functor obtained from mapping
\delta_{1}\mapsto X
and the canonical isomorphism
\delta_{1}^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{1}
to
\lambda
. We then have a bijection:

\displaystyle\begin{Bmatrix}\text{Tensor functors}\\
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}\to\mathcal{C}\end{Bmatrix}\longleftrightarrow\begin{Bmatrix}\text{pairs }(X,\lambda),\text{ where }X\in\C\text{ and }\lambda:X^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{1}\\
\ \text{is such that }\text{id}\otimes\lambda=\zeta\lambda\otimes\text{id}\end{Bmatrix}.

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188ptF_{X,\lambda}\longleftrightarrow(X,\lambda)

Formally, we can consider the category
\operatorname{Fun}_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta},\C)
with objects tensor functors
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}\to\C
and morphisms monoidal natural transformations between functors. Then the bijection above can be promoted to an equivalence of categories in the following way. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\zeta}
denote the category with:

•

Objects are pairs
(X,\lambda),
where
X
is an object in
\C
and
\lambda:X^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{1}
satisfies
\text{id}\otimes\lambda=\zeta\lambda\otimes\text{id}
.

•

Maps
(X,\lambda)\to(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
are morphisms
f:X\to X^{\prime}
in
\C
such that
\lambda^{\prime}f^{\otimes n}=\lambda
.

Then we have an equivalence of categories

\displaystyle\Phi:\operatorname{Fun}_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta},\C)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\zeta}

\displaystyle F_{X,\lambda}

\displaystyle\mapsto(X,\lambda)

\displaystyle(\tau:F_{X,\lambda}\to F_{X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\mapsto(\tau_{\delta_{1}}:X\to X^{\prime}).

Proposition 5.10
.

The map defined above is in fact an equivalence of categories.

Proof.

To show that
\Phi
is well defined, we need to prove that any monoidal natural transformation
\tau:F_{X,\lambda}\to F_{X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}
satisfies
\lambda^{\prime}\tau_{\delta_{1}}^{\otimes n}=\lambda
. In fact, this follows from naturality of
\tau
, which gives the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\otimes n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime\otimes n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{1}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\delta_{1}}^{\otimes n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\mathbf{1}}=\text{id}}

It is clear that
\Phi
is surjective on objects. It remains to show that
\Phi
is fully-faithful. For this, we prove that

(11)

\displaystyle\Hom_{\operatorname{Fun}_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta},\C)}(F_{X,\lambda},F_{X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}})\xrightarrow{\Phi}\Hom_{\C_{\zeta}}((X,\lambda),(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}))

is bijective for every suitable pair
(X,\lambda)
. Let
f:(X,\lambda)\to(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
be a morphism in
\C_{\zeta}
, and define a monoidal natural transformation
\tau:F_{X,\lambda}\to F_{X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}
by
\tau_{\delta_{1}}=f
and extend monoidally everywhere else. It is a quick check that this gives a well defined inverse
\Hom_{\C_{\zeta}}((X,\lambda),(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}))\to\Hom_{\operatorname{Fun}_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta},\C)}(F_{X,\lambda},F_{X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}})
for

, and so the statement follows.
∎

6.
Automorphism group of
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}

In this section, we give a description of the 2-group
\Aut_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta})
of tensor automorphisms of
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
.
We start by describing when two such categories are equivalent.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\zeta
and
\xi
be
n
-th roots of unity.
Then

\displaystyle\Vect^{\zeta}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\simeq\Vect^{\xi}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}

if and only if there exists
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\times}
such that
\xi^{j^{2}}=\zeta
.

Proof.

Recall from Proposition
5.10
that to define a functor
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}\to\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\xi}
it is enough to make a choice of an object
X\in\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\xi}
and an isomorphism
\lambda:X^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{1}
such that
\text{id}\otimes\lambda=\zeta\lambda\otimes\text{id}
. In particular, this implies that
X
is invertible, hence
X\simeq\delta_{j}
for some
0\leq j<n.
Moreover, if we want to have an equivalence, the functor should be surjective and thus
X\simeq\delta_{j}
should be a generator.
The statement then follows from Remark
4.2
.
∎

Now that we have a way of measuring when two cyclic pointed fusion associated to
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
are equivalent, we give an answer for the number of equivalence classes for a fixed
n
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
p_{1},\cdots,p_{l}
be distinct odd primes such that
n=2^{k_{0}}p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{l}^{k_{l}}
for some
k_{0},\cdots,k_{l}\in\mathbb{N}
. There are
c(n)
categories of the form
\Vect^{\zeta}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
up to equivalence, where

\displaystyle c(n)=\begin{cases}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}(2k_{i}+1)&\text{if}\ k_{0}=0,\\
2\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}(2k_{i}+1)&\text{if}\ k_{0}=1,\\
4\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}(2k_{i}+1)&\text{if}\ k_{0}=2,\\
4(k_{0}-1)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}(2k_{i}+1)&\text{if}\ k_{0}\geq 3.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\theta
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity. Then any pointed fusion category associated to
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is of the form
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\theta^{a}}
for some
a\in\mathbb{N}
. By Corollary
6.1
, we know that any two pointed categories
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\theta^{a}}
and
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\theta^{b}}
are equivalent if and only if there exists
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\times}
such that
\theta^{aj^{2}}=\theta^{b}
, or equivalentely, such that
j^{2}a\equiv b\mod n
. Consider the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\times}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\times}

\displaystyle\to\End(\mathbb{Z}_{n})

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle\mapsto(a\mapsto j^{2}a).

That is, the action of left multiplication by squares.
Then counting equivalence classes of pointed fusion categories associated to
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
reduces to computing the number of orbits of this action. The rest of the proof is dedicated to this computation.

Assume for now that
n=p^{k}
for some prime
p
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
. Then one can get from
x
to
y
multiplying by a unit if and only if
x
and
y
are divisible by the same power of
p
. So if we are looking at the action of multiplication by squares of units, it is enough to compute the orbits of the action restricted to
\mathcal{H}_{i}:=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}/x\text{ is divisible by }p^{i}\text{ but not }p^{i+1}\}
for all
i=0,\dots,k-1
.

Let
x,y\in\mathcal{H}_{i}
. Then there exist
x_{1},y_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k-i}}^{\times}
such that
x=p^{i}x_{1}
and
y=p^{i}y_{1}
. So
x,y
are in the same orbit if and only if there exists
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}^{\times}
such that

(12)

\displaystyle p^{i}x_{1}\equiv j^{2}p^{i}y_{1}\mod{p^{k}}.

That is,

\displaystyle x_{1}\equiv j^{2}y_{1}\mod{p^{k-i}}

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle y_{1}y_{2}^{-1}\equiv j^{2}\mod{p^{k-i}}.

So if
\mathcal{R}_{k-i}
denotes the subgroup of quadratic residues of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k-i}}
, the number of orbits of the action restricted to
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is exactly

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k-i}}/\mathcal{R}_{k-i}\Big|

for all
0\leq i<k.
When
p
is odd,
\Big|\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k-i}}/\mathcal{R}_{k-i}\Big|=2
for all
0\leq i<k
, and this together with the fact that
\mathcal{H}_{k}=\{0\}
gives a total number of
2k+1
orbits.
If
p=2,
then

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k-i}}/\mathcal{R}_{k-i}\Big|=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ i=k-1\\
2&\text{if}\ i=k-2\\
4&\text{if}\ 0\leq i\leq k-3.\end{cases}

Hence if
k=1
the action has exactly two orbits, if
k=2
the action has four orbits and if
k\geq 3
the action has
4(k-1)
orbits.

Lastly, note that for the general case
n=2^{k_{0}}p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{l}^{k_{l}}
we have identifications

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k_{0}}}^{\times}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{k_{1}}}^{\times}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p_{l}^{k_{l}}}^{\times}

and

\displaystyle\End(\mathbb{Z}_{m})\simeq\End(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k_{0}}})\oplus\End(\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{k_{1}}})\oplus\cdots\oplus\End(\mathbb{Z}_{p_{l}^{k_{l}}}).

Thus if we consider the action of multiplication by a square, the orbit of an element
x=(x_{0},\dots,x_{l})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
is identified with the product of the orbits of each
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{n_{i}}}
.
Hence the number of orbits of the action on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
is the product of the number of orbits restricted to each of the terms
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{n_{i}}}
which we computed previously, and the result follows.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

The first 10 terms of the sequence

are

We now wish to give a description of the 2-group
\Aut_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta})
, with objects monoidal automorphisms of
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
and morphisms their monoidal natural isomorphisms.

It follows from the previous results in this section that monoidal automorphisms of
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
are of the form
F_{X,\lambda}
, where
X\simeq\delta_{j}
for some
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\times}
such that
\zeta^{j^{2}}=\zeta
,
\lambda
is any isomorphism
\delta_{j}^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{1}
, and
F_{X,\lambda}
is the tensor functor obtained from mapping
\delta_{1}\mapsto X
and the canonical isomorphism
\delta_{1}^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{1}
to
\lambda
, see Section
5.2
.

For the rest of this section, let
X\simeq\delta_{j}
and
Y\simeq\delta_{k}
in
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta},
for
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\times}
satisfying
\zeta^{j^{2}}=\zeta=\zeta^{k^{2}}
, and let
F_{X,\lambda},F_{Y,\lambda^{\prime}}
be two automorphisms
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}\to\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
.

Proposition 6.4
.

If
j\neq k
, then
\Hom_{\Aut_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta})}(F_{X,\lambda},F_{Y,\lambda^{\prime}})=0.

Proof.

To define a natural transformation
F_{j,\lambda}\to F_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}
we need to choose a map
\tau_{\delta_{1}}:X\to Y
in
\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta}
. If
j\not\equiv k\mod n,
the only possible choice is zero.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

If
j=k
, then
\Hom_{\Aut_{\otimes}(\Vect_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}^{\zeta})}(F_{X,\lambda},F_{Y,\lambda^{\prime}})\simeq\Theta
as sets, where
\Theta
denotes the set of
n
-th roots of unity.

Proof.

Since
j=k
, we have that
X\simeq Y
. Fix an isomorphism
\Gamma:X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
. By Proposition
5.10
, defining a monoidal transformation
\tau:F_{X,\lambda}\xrightarrow{\sim}F_{Y,\lambda^{\prime}}
is equivalent to choosing a map
\tau_{\delta_{1}}:X\to Y
such that
\lambda^{\prime}\tau_{\delta_{1}}^{\otimes n}=\lambda
. Now, since
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\Gamma^{\otimes n}\in\Hom(X^{\otimes n},\mathbf{1})\simeq\mathbf{k},
there exists
\alpha\in\mathbf{k}^{\times}
such that
\lambda=\alpha\lambda^{\prime}\Gamma^{\otimes n}
. Hence if
\lambda^{\prime}\tau_{\delta_{1}}^{\otimes n}=\lambda
, we get that

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}\tau_{\delta_{1}}^{\otimes n}=\alpha\lambda^{\prime}\Gamma^{\otimes n},

and so

(13)

\displaystyle\tau_{\delta_{1}}^{\otimes n}=\alpha\Gamma^{\otimes n}.

Since
\Gamma^{-1}\tau_{\delta_{1}}\in\Hom(X,X)\simeq\mathbf{k}
, then
\Gamma^{-1}\tau_{\delta_{1}}
is a multiple of the identity, and so it follows from Equation (
13
) that

\tau_{\delta_{1}}=\alpha_{n}\Gamma,

where
\alpha_{n}
is some
n
-th root of
\alpha
.

Note that this does not depend on the choice of isomorphism
\Gamma:X\to Y
. In fact, if
\Gamma^{\prime}:X\to Y
is another isomorphism, then since
\Hom(X,Y)\simeq\mathbf{k}
there exists
a\in\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
such that
\Gamma=a\Gamma^{\prime}
. Following the same computations for
\Gamma^{\prime}
, we get that
\tau_{\delta_{1}}=\beta\Gamma^{\prime}
for
\beta
and
n
-th root of
\alpha a^{n}
, and so again
\tau_{\delta_{1}}=\alpha_{n}\Gamma
for
\alpha_{n}
an
n
-th root of
\alpha
.

Since we now know that our computations do not depend on the choice of
\Gamma
, we may choose
\Gamma
so that
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}\Gamma^{\otimes n}.
Hence

\displaystyle\tau_{\delta_{1}}=\theta_{n}^{k}\Gamma,\text{ for }k=0,1\dots,n-1,

are all the possible choices for
\tau_{\delta_{1}}
, where
\theta
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

It follows from the proof that
\Aut_{\otimes}(F_{X,\lambda})\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
as groups.

An explicit representation for the axisymmetric solutions of the free Maxwell equations
Mayeul Arminjon

Abstract
Garay-Avendaño & Zamboni-Rached (2014) defined two classes of axisymmetric solutions of the free Maxwell equations. We prove that the linear combinations of these two classes of solutions cover all totally propagating time-harmonic axisymmetric free Maxwell fields — and hence, by summation on frequencies, all totally propagating axisymmetric free Maxwell fields. It provides an explicit representation for these fields. This will be important, e.g., to have the interstellar radiation field in a disc galaxy modelled as an exact solution of the free Maxwell equations. Keywords: Maxwell equations; axial symmetry; exact solutions; electromagnetic duality.

2.3
Time-harmonic axisymmetric fields: from a scalar wave to a Maxwell field

To any solution

Thus, in the way recalled in the foregoing subsection, they define a unique solution of the free Maxwell equations, which is time-harmonic. Their equations for the different components of this solution

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}B_{\phi}}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}E_{\rho}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathrm{i}\frac{c^{2}}{\omega}\frac{\partial^{2}A_{z}}{\partial\rho\,\partial z}},\qquad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}B_{\rho}=0},

(12)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}E_{z}}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathrm{i}\frac{c^{2}}{\omega}\frac{\partial^{2}A_{z}}{\partial z^{2}}+\mathrm{i}\omega A_{z}},\qquad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}B_{z}=0}.

3
From Maxwell fields to scalar waves

Now an important question arises: Do the GAZR solutions generate all axisymmetric time-harmonic solutions of the Maxwell equations (in which case, by summation on frequencies, they would generate all axisymmetric solutions of the Maxwell equations)? That is:
let
\ ({\bf A},{\bf E},{\bf B})\
be any time-harmonic axisymmetric solution of the free Maxwell equations. Can one find a GAZR1 solution and a GAZR2 solution, whose sum give just that starting solution?

Note from Eqs. (
11
)–(
13
) and (
14
)
that
the GAZR1 solution and the GAZR2 solution are complementary:
in cylindrical coordinates, the GAZR1 solution provides non-zero components
B_{\phi},E_{\rho}
,
E_{z}
, the other components
E_{\phi}
,
B_{\rho}
,
B_{z}
being zero — and the exact opposite is true for the GAZR2 solution. In view of this complementarity, we can consider separately the two sets of components:
B_{\phi},E_{\rho}
,
E_{z}
on one side, and
E_{\phi}
,
B_{\rho}
,
B_{z}
on the other side.

3.1
Sufficient conditions for the existence of the decomposition

For the “GAZR1” solution, which gives non-zero values to the first among the two sets of components just mentioned, we have the following result:

Proposition 1.

Let
({\bf A},{\bf E},{\bf B})
be any time-harmonic axisymmetric solution of the free Maxwell equations. In order that a time-harmonic axisymmetric solution
(A_{1z},B_{1\phi},E_{1\rho},E_{1z};E_{1\phi}=B_{1\rho}=B_{1z}=0)
, of the form (
11
)–(
13
), and having the same frequency
\omega
as the starting solution
({\bf A},{\bf E},{\bf B})
, be such that
B_{1\phi}=B_{\phi},\ E_{1\rho}=E_{\rho},\ E_{1z}=E_{z}
, it is sufficient that we have just

B_{1\phi}=B_{\phi}.

(21)

Proof.
Let
A_{1z}(t,\rho,z)
be a time-harmonic axisymmetric solution of the wave equation, with frequency
\omega
, and assume that
B_{1\phi}
as defined by Eq. (
11
) [with
A_{1z}
in the place of
A_{z}
] is equal to
B_{\phi}
, where
{\bf B}
is defined by Eq. (
4
). I.e., assume that

-\frac{\partial A_{1z}}{\partial\rho}=\frac{\partial A_{\rho}}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial A_{z}}{\partial\rho}.

(22)

Denoting by
{\bf A}_{1}:=A_{1z}{\bf e}_{z}
the vector potential that provides the GAZR1 solution
(B_{1\phi},E_{1\rho},E_{1z};E_{1\phi}=B_{1\rho}=B_{1z}=0)
, let us compute
E_{\rho}-E_{1\rho}
and
E_{z}-E_{1z}
. We have by Eq. (
9
):

\frac{\omega}{\mathrm{i}c^{2}}\left({\bf E}-{\bf E}_{1}\right)=\nabla\left(\mathrm{div}{\bf A}^{\prime}\right)+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}{\bf A}^{\prime},

(23)

where

\qquad{\bf A}^{\prime}:={\bf A}-{\bf A}_{1}:={\bf A}-A_{1z}{\bf e}_{z}.

(24)

In order that the vector potential
{\bf A}
of the
a priori
given solution
({\bf A},{\bf E},{\bf B})
be axisymmetric, its components
A_{\rho},\,A_{\phi},\,A_{z}
must depend only on
t,\rho,z
, i.e., be independent of
\phi
. Therefore:

\mathrm{div}{\bf A}=\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial(\rho A_{\rho})}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\partial A_{z}}{\partial z},

(25)

and, using this and (
24
):

\mathrm{div}{\bf A}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial(\rho A_{\rho})}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\partial A^{\prime}_{z}}{\partial z}.

(26)

Hence, in (
23
), we have

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\mathrm{div}{\bf A}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\frac{\partial A_{\rho}}{\partial\rho}+\frac{A_{\rho}}{\rho}+\frac{\partial A^{\prime}_{z}}{\partial z}\right)

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{2}A_{\rho}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial A_{\rho}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}A_{\rho}+\frac{\partial^{2}A^{\prime}_{z}}{\partial\rho\partial z}\right){\bf e}_{\rho}+\left(\frac{\partial^{2}A_{\rho}}{\partial z\partial\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial A_{\rho}}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial^{2}A^{\prime}_{z}}{\partial z^{2}}\right){\bf e}_{z}.

The radial component of the vector (
23
) is thus:

\frac{\omega}{\mathrm{i}c^{2}}\left({\bf E}-{\bf E}_{1}\right)_{\rho}=\frac{\partial^{2}A_{\rho}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial A_{\rho}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{A_{\rho}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}A_{z}}{\partial\rho\partial z}-\frac{\partial^{2}A_{1z}}{\partial\rho\partial z}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}A_{\rho}.

(28)

However, the vector potential
{\bf A}
obeys the Helmholtz equation (
7
), that is for the radial component (using the fact that
\frac{\partial A_{\rho}}{\partial\phi}=\frac{\partial A_{\phi}}{\partial\phi}\equiv 0
):

\displaystyle\left(\Delta{\bf A}\right)_{\rho}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}A_{\rho}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Delta A_{\rho}-\frac{A_{\rho}}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial A_{\phi}}{\partial\phi}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}A_{\rho}

(29)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}A_{\rho}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial A_{\rho}}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\partial^{2}A_{\rho}}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{A_{\rho}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}A_{\rho}=0.

Inserting (
29

) gives us:

\frac{\omega}{\mathrm{i}c^{2}}\left({\bf E}-{\bf E}_{1}\right)_{\rho}=-\frac{\partial^{2}A_{\rho}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}A_{z}}{\partial\rho\partial z}-\frac{\partial^{2}A_{1z}}{\partial\rho\partial z}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\frac{\partial A_{\rho}}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial A_{z}}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\partial A_{1z}}{\partial\rho}\right).

(30)

Therefore, if Eq. (
22
) is satisfied, then we have
E_{1\rho}=E_{\rho}
.

Similarly, from (
27
), the axial component of the vector (
23
) is

\frac{\omega}{\mathrm{i}c^{2}}\left({\bf E}-{\bf E}_{1}\right)_{z}=\frac{\partial^{2}A_{\rho}}{\partial z\partial\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial A_{\rho}}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial^{2}A_{z}}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}A_{1z}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\left(A_{z}-A_{1z}\right).

(31)

On the other hand, the axial component of the Helmholtz equation (
7
) is

\displaystyle\left(\Delta{\bf A}\right)_{z}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}A_{z}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Delta A_{z}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}A_{z}

(32)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}A_{z}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial A_{z}}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\partial^{2}A_{z}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}A_{z}=0.

If Eq. (
22

\frac{\partial^{2}A_{z}}{\partial\rho^{2}}=\frac{\partial^{2}A_{\rho}}{\partial\rho\partial z}+\frac{\partial^{2}A_{1z}}{\partial\rho^{2}}.

(33)

In Eq. (

\frac{\partial^{2}A_{\rho}}{\partial\rho\partial z}+\frac{\partial^{2}A_{1z}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial A_{\rho}}{\partial z}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial A_{1z}}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\partial^{2}A_{z}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}A_{z}=0.

(34)

Using this equation in Eq. (

\frac{\omega}{\mathrm{i}c^{2}}\left({\bf E}-{\bf E}_{1}\right)_{z}=-\frac{\partial^{2}A_{1z}}{\partial\rho^{2}}-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial A_{1z}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{\partial^{2}A_{1z}}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\,A_{1z}.

(35)

(36)

But this is zero, since

3.2
Generality of the decomposition

Proposition 2.

Let
({\bf A},{\bf E},{\bf B})
be any time-harmonic axisymmetric solution of the free Maxwell equations. There exists a time-harmonic axisymmetric solution
A_{2z}
of the wave equation, with the same frequency, such that the associated GAZR2 solution
({\bf E}^{\prime}_{2},{\bf B}^{\prime}_{2})
, deduced from
A_{2z}
by Eqs. (
11
)–(
13
) followed by the duality transformation (
14
), satisfy

E^{\prime}_{2\phi}=E_{\phi},\quad B^{\prime}_{2\rho}=B_{\rho},\quad B^{\prime}_{2z}=B_{z}.

(43)

Proof.
In view of
Corollary 1
, we merely have to prove that there exists a time-harmonic axisymmetric solution
A_{2z}
of the wave equation, such that Eq. (
37
) is satisfied. From Eqs. (
9
) and (
25
), we have

E_{\phi}=\mathrm{i}\omega A_{\phi}.

(44)

Using this with Eqs. (
11
) and (
14
)
1
, we may rewrite the sought-for relation (
37
) as:

-\frac{\partial A_{2z}}{\partial\rho}=\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}A_{\phi}.

(45)

This equation can be solved by a quadrature:

A_{2z}(t,\rho,z)=h(t,z)+\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}A_{\phi}(t,\rho^{\prime},z)\,\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime}.

(46)

We thus have to find out if it is possible to determine the function
h
so that
A_{2z}
given by (
46
) obey the wave equation. Moreover, the unknown function
A_{2z}
must have a harmonic time dependence with frequency
\omega
as has
A_{\phi}
, i.e.,

A_{2z}(t,\rho,z)=\psi(\rho,z)\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t},\quad A_{\phi}(t,\rho,z)=\hat{A}_{\phi}(\rho,z)\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t},

(47)

so we must have
h(t,z)=e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}g(z)
, too. Hence we may rewrite (
46
) as

\psi(\rho,z)=g(z)+\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\hat{A}_{\phi}(\rho^{\prime},z)\,\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime},

(48)

and now the question is to know if
g
can be determined so that
\psi
obey the scalar Helmholtz equation, i.e. [cf. Eq. (
32
)]:

\mathcal{H}\psi:=\Delta\psi+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\psi\equiv\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\psi=0

(49)

— knowing that
A_{\phi}
or
\hat{A}_{\phi}
does obey the
\phi
component of the vector Helmholtz equation (
7
), i.e.:

\displaystyle\left(\Delta{\bf\hat{A}}\right)_{\phi}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\hat{A}_{\phi}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{A}_{\phi}-\frac{\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{\rho}}{\partial\phi}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\hat{A}_{\phi}

(50)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\hat{A}_{\phi}=0.

We have from Eqs. (
45
) and (
47
):

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\rho}=-\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\hat{A}_{\phi},

(51)

hence

\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial\rho^{2}}=-\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho}.

(52)

And we get from (
48
):

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial z}=\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{\mathrm{d}z}-\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial z}(\rho^{\prime},z)\,\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime},

(53)

whence

\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial z^{2}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}g}{\mathrm{d}z^{2}}-\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial z^{2}}(\rho^{\prime},z)\,\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime}.

(54)

Entering Eqs. (
51
), (
52
) and (
54
) into (
49
)
1
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\frac{\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\rho}+\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}g}{\mathrm{d}z^{2}}-\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial z^{2}}(\rho^{\prime},z)\,\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime}

(55)

\displaystyle+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\left(g-\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}\hat{A}_{\phi}(\rho^{\prime},z)\,\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime}\right).

Therefore, the scalar Helmholtz equation (
49
)
2
rewrites as

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}g}{\mathrm{d}z^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}g=\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\left[\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\rho}+\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\hat{A}_{\phi}\right)\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime}\right],

(56)

or, using (
50
):

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}g}{\mathrm{d}z^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}g=\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\left[\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\rho}+\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho^{\prime 2}}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho^{\prime}}+\frac{\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\rho^{\prime 2}}\right)\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime}\right].

(57)

An integration by parts gives us:

\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho^{\prime 2}}+\frac{\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\rho^{\prime 2}}\right)\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime}=-\left[\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho^{\prime}}+\frac{\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\rho^{\prime}}\right]_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}+\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime},

(58)

so Eq. (
57
) rewrites as

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}g}{\mathrm{d}z^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}g=\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{c}\left[\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{\phi}}{\partial\rho}(\rho_{0},z)+\frac{\hat{A}_{\phi}(\rho_{0},z)}{\rho_{0}}\right].

(59)

As is well known and easy to check, this very ordinary differential equation can be solved explicitly by the method of variation of constants. (The general solution
g
of (
59
) depends linearly on two arbitrary constants.) And by construction, any among the solutions
g
of (
59
) is such that, with that
g
, the function
\psi
in Eq. (
48
) obeys the scalar Helmholtz equation (
49
). This proves
Proposition 2
.
\square

Corollary 2.

Let
({\bf A},{\bf E},{\bf B})
be any time-harmonic axisymmetric solution of the free Maxwell equations. There exists a time-harmonic axisymmetric solution
A_{1z}
of the wave equation, with the same frequency, such that the associated GAZR1 solution
({\bf E}_{1},{\bf B}_{1})
, deduced from
A_{1z}
by Eqs. (
11
)–(
13
), satisfy

B_{1\phi}=B_{\phi},\quad E_{1\rho}=E_{\rho},\quad E_{1z}=E_{z}.

(60)

Proof.
Let
A_{1z}
be a time-harmonic axisymmetric solution of the wave equation, and consider:

(i) the GAZR1 solution defined from
A_{1z}
by Eqs. (
11
)–(
13
) (thus with
A_{1z},\ B_{1\phi},\ E_{1\rho},\ E_{1z},...
instead of
A_{z},\ B_{\phi},\ E_{\rho},\ E_{z},...
respectively).

(ii) the GAZR2 solution defined from the same
A_{1z}
by applying the duality (
14
) to the said GAZR1 solution:

E^{\prime}_{1\phi}:=cB_{1\phi},\quad B^{\prime}_{1\rho}:=-\frac{1}{c}E_{1\rho},\quad B^{\prime}_{1z}:=-\frac{1}{c}E_{1z},

(61)

E^{\prime}_{1\rho}:=cB_{1\rho}=0,\quad E^{\prime}_{1z}:=cB_{1z}=0,\quad B^{\prime}_{1\phi}:=-\frac{1}{c}E_{1\phi}=0.

(62)

On the other hand, consider the free Maxwell field
({\bf E}^{\prime},{\bf B}^{\prime})
deduced from the given time-harmonic axisymmetric solution
({\bf E},{\bf B})
of the free Maxwell equations by the same duality relation:

{\bf E}^{\prime}:=c{\bf B},\quad{\bf B}^{\prime}:=-{\bf E}/c.

(63)

Just in the same way as it was shown in Note
3
, we know that a vector potential
{\bf A}^{\prime}
such that
{\bf B}^{\prime}=\mathrm{rot}{\bf A}^{\prime}
does exist and can be chosen to be axisymmetric (and is indeed chosen so) — as are
{\bf E}
and
{\bf B}
, and hence
{\bf E}^{\prime}
and
{\bf B}^{\prime}
. Clearly, the sought-for relation (
60
) is equivalent to

E^{\prime}_{1\phi}=E^{\prime}_{\phi},\quad B^{\prime}_{1\rho}=B^{\prime}_{\rho},\quad B^{\prime}_{1z}=B^{\prime}_{z}.

(64)

Therefore, the existence of
A_{1z}
as in the statement of
Corollary 2
is ensured by
Proposition 2
.
\square

Accounting for
Proposition 2
and for
Corollary 2
, and remembering the
“complementarity”
of the GAZR1 and GAZR2 solutions, we thus can answer positively to the question asked at the beginning of
this section
:

Theorem.

Let
({\bf A},{\bf E},{\bf B})
be any time-harmonic axisymmetric solution of the free Maxwell equations. There exist a unique
GAZR1
solution
({\bf E}_{1},{\bf B}_{1})
and a unique
GAZR2
solution
({\bf E}^{\prime}_{2},{\bf B}^{\prime}_{2})
, both with the same frequency as has
({\bf A},{\bf E},{\bf B})
, and whose sum gives just that solution:

{\bf E}={\bf E}_{1}+{\bf E}^{\prime}_{2},\qquad{\bf B}={\bf B}_{1}+{\bf B}^{\prime}_{2}.

(65)

Remark.
Thus, the uniqueness of the representation concerns the electric and magnetic fields. It of course does not concern the potentials
{\bf A}_{1}=A_{1z}{\bf e}_{z}
and
{\bf A}_{2}=A_{2z}{\bf e}_{z}
that generate respectively
({\bf E}_{1},{\bf B}_{1})
and
({\bf E}^{\prime}_{2},{\bf B}^{\prime}_{2})
.

4
Discussion and conclusion

The authors of Ref.
[
10
]
introduced two classes of axisymmetric solutions of the free Maxwell equations, and they showed that these two classes allow one to obtain in explicit form nonparaxial EM beams. It has been proved here that, by combining these two classes, one can define a method that allows one to get
all
totally propagating, time-harmonic, axisymmetric free Maxwell fields — and thus, by the appropriate summation on frequencies, all totally propagating axisymmetric free Maxwell fields. This method results immediately from the
Theorem
just above, and from the general form (
1
) of a totally propagating, time-harmonic, axisymmetric solution of the scalar wave equation. However, that theorem is not an obviously expected result, and its proof is not immediate. We thus have now a constructive method to obtain all totally propagating axisymmetric free Maxwell fields. Namely, considering a discrete frequency spectrum
(\omega_{j})_{j=1,...,N_{\omega}}
for simplicity: there are
2\,N_{\omega}
functions,
k\mapsto S_{j}(k)
, and
k\mapsto S^{\prime}_{j}(k)\ \ (j=1,...,N_{\omega})
, such that the components
B_{\phi},E_{\rho},E_{z}
of the field are given by Eqs. (
18
), (
19
), (
20
) respectively — while the components
E_{\phi},B_{\rho},B_{z}
are given by these same equations applied with the primed spectra
S^{\prime}_{j}
, followed by the duality transformation (
14
).

In a forthcoming work, we shall apply this to model the interstellar radiation field in a disc galaxy as an (axisymmetric) exact solution of the free Maxwell equations. In this application, it is very important that, due to the present work, one knows that any (totally propagating) axisymmetric free Maxwell field can be got in this way.

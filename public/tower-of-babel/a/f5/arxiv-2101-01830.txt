A Hamiltonian Approach for Obtaining Irreducible Projective Representations and the k ⋅ p k\cdot p Perturbation for Anti-unitary Symmetry Groups
Zhen-Yuan Yang, Jian Yang, Chen Fang, Zheng-Xin Liu

Abstract
As is known, the irreducible projective representations (Reps) of anti-unitary groups contain three different situations, namely, the real, the complex and quaternion types with torsion number 1,2,4 respectively. This subtlety increases the complexity in obtaining irreducible projective Reps of anti-unitary groups. In the present work, a physical approach is introduced to derive the condition of irreducibility for projective Reps of anti-unitary groups. Then a practical procedure is provided to reduce an arbitrary projective Rep into direct sum of irreducible ones. The central idea is to construct a hermitian Hamiltonian matrix which commutes with the representation of every group element g ∈ G g\in G , such that each of its eigenspaces forms an irreducible representation space of the group G G . Thus the Rep is completely reduced in the eigenspaces of the Hamiltonian. This approach is applied in the k ⋅ p k\cdot p effective theory at the high symmetry points (HSPs) of the Brillouin zone for quasi-particle excitations in magnetic materials. After giving the criterion to judge the power of single-particle dispersion around a HSP, we then provide a systematic procedure to construct the k ⋅ p k\cdot p effective model.

The advantage of the method is that no information of the irreducible Reps of the groups need to be known beforehand. We further generalize this approach to judge the power of the quasi-particle dispersions in magnetic semimetals, and then to obtain the

III
Hamiltonian approach for the Reduction of Projective Reps

The criterion of judging the irreducibility actually provides a practical procedure to reduce reducible projective Reps of finite groups. In the following, we discuss unitary groups and anti-unitary groups separately.

IV
Application of the Hamiltonian approach in perturbation theory

The Hamiltonian approach can be generalized to obtain the response of the system to symmetry breaking probe fields if the low-energy physics is dominated by particle-like excitations, such as the electron-like quasiparticles in metals, Bogoliubov quasi-particles in superconductors or the magnon excitations in the spin sector. We restrict our discussion to irreducible projective Reps of anti-unitary groups.

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Gamma}^{m})^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M(T_{0})(\Gamma^{m})^{T}

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M(T_{0})\sum_{n}D^{(v)}_{mn}(T_{0})M^{*}(T_{0})\Gamma^{n}M^{T}(T_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{0}M(\sigma)\sum_{n}D^{(v)}_{mn}(T_{0})\tilde{\Gamma}^{n},

where we have used the transpose of (

Taking the transpose of

Noticing

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{0}}

\displaystyle\eta_{0}(I\otimes\mathscr{T})\Big(D^{(v)}(T_{0})\otimes M(\sigma)\otimes I\Big)

\displaystyle D^{(v)}(T_{0})\otimes\mathscr{T}_{\eta_{0}},

\displaystyle[W_{\eta_{0}}(h)]_{mkl,nij}\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!{1\over 2}\Big[D_{mn}^{(v)}(h)M_{ki}(h)F_{lj}(h)

\displaystyle\!\!\eta_{0}\sum_{a}D_{mn}^{(v)}(hT_{0})M_{kj}(h)F_{la}(h)M_{ai}(\sigma)\Big],

IV.1.2
Procedure of obtaining the
\Gamma^{1,2,3}

As
a_{I}^{H}\geq 1

\Gamma^{1,2,3}

Firstly, obtain the eigenspace of

Thirdly, from (

\tilde{\Delta}_{i}={1\over\sqrt{3}}e_{1}\otimes\tilde{\gamma}_{i}^{1}+{1\over\sqrt{3}}e_{2}\otimes\tilde{\gamma}_{i}^{2}+{1\over\sqrt{3}}e_{3}\otimes\tilde{\gamma}_{i}^{3},

IV.1.3
Higher Order Dispersions and Nodal Lines

The discussion of linear dispersion can be straightforwardly generalized to higher order dispersions. Suppose that a set of order-
N

carry a linear Rep
(\bar{\mu})
of the group
G
, the existence of the dispersion

H_{\boldsymbol{k}+\delta\boldsymbol{k}}=\sum_{i}P^{(N)}_{i}(\delta\boldsymbol{k})\psi_{\boldsymbol{k}+\delta\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}\Gamma^{(N)}_{i}\psi_{\boldsymbol{k}+\delta\boldsymbol{k}}

42

(v)
replaced by the linear Rep
(\mu)
(see Appendix
C
for an example). The method of obtaining the corresponding matrices
\Gamma^{(N)}_{i}
is also similar.

If the vector Rep of

is reducible, it is possible that the degeneracy is lifted along some directions (such as the

direction) to form a nodal line. The little co-group on the line is generally smaller than the one on the conner of the BZ. If the IPRep of the little co-group at the conner of the BZ is still irreducible along a certain line, then this line is a nodal line. Therefore, the existence of the nodal line can be judge from the formula (

Yang et al. 2021

IV.2
Response to External Probe Fields

The IPRep
M(g)K_{s(g)},g\in G
of anti-unitary symmetry group
G
results in energy degeneracy in single-particle spectrum. Here we discuss the possible lifting of the degeneracy under external probe fields, such as
\boldsymbol{E}
and
\boldsymbol{B}
, stain, or temperature gradience, etc. We assume that the probe fields carry irreducible linear Reps of the group
G
. For instance, electric fields
\boldsymbol{E}
or magnetic fields
\boldsymbol{B}
carry vector Reps of the unitary subgroup
H
, but they vary differently under the anti-unitary element
T_{0}
since
\boldsymbol{E}
is invariant under time reversal while
\boldsymbol{B}
reverse its sign under time reversal.

There are two possible consequences under external probes. The first possible result is that the degeneracy guaranteed by the IPRep
M(g)K_{s(g)}
is preserved. The other possibility is that the degeneracy is lifted in linear or higher order terms of the probe fields.

To judge if the probe fields can lift the degeneracy or not (summing over all orders of perturbation), we need to know the remaining symmetry group with the presence of the perturbation, and then judge if the restrict Rep is reducible or not. Suppose the probe field reduces the symmetry group from
G=H+T_{0}H
to
G^{\prime}=H^{\prime}+T^{\prime}_{0}H^{\prime}
where
T_{0}^{\prime}
is anti-unitary. If the irreducible Rep
M(g)K_{s(g)}
of
G
remains irreducible for
G^{\prime}
, namely if

\displaystyle{1\over|H^{\prime}|}\sum_{h\in H^{\prime}}{1\over 2}\left[\chi(h)\chi^{*}(h)+\omega_{2}(T^{\prime}_{0}h,T^{\prime}_{0}h)\chi((T^{\prime}_{0}h)^{2})\right]=1.

holds for the group
G^{\prime}
, then the degeneracy is robust against this perturbation.

If the left hand side of above equation is not equal to 1, then the restricted Rep is reducible and the degeneracy can be lifted at certain order. In the following we only discuss the linear splitting by external fields, such as
\boldsymbol{E}
and
\boldsymbol{B}
. The linear response is given by the perturbed Hamiltonian in form of

\displaystyle H=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\psi^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{E}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}+\boldsymbol{B}\cdot\boldsymbol{M})\psi_{\boldsymbol{k}}

(44)

where
P^{m},M^{m}
are CG matrices similar to the
\Gamma^{m}
matrices discussed before. The existence of linear coupling terms
\boldsymbol{P}
(or
\boldsymbol{M}
) can be checked using the criterion (
42
) with
M^{(v)}(T_{0})
the transformation matrix of
\boldsymbol{E}
(or
\boldsymbol{B}
) under the action of
T_{0}
.

V
Conclusions and Discussions

In summary, from a physical approach, we derived the condition (
1
) for the irreducible projective representations of anti-unitary groups. This approach provides a practical method to reduce an arbitrary projective Rep into a direct sum of irreducible ones, which is applicable for either unitary or anti-unitary groups.

As a physical application of this approach, for single particle systems with magnetic space group symmetry, we provide the method to construct the
k\cdot p
perturbation theory at the high symmetry point of the Brillouin zone. We provide the criterion (
42
) to judge if the dispersion is linear or of higher order, and then provide the method to obtain the corresponding
k\cdot p
Hamiltonian up to a few non-universal constants.

In the present work, we assume that the quasiparticles vary under linear representations of the magnetic space groups. However, in strongly interacting systems, projective representations of the magnetic space groups can emerge in the fractionalized low-energy quasiparticle excitations for systems with intrinsic topological order. We leave the discussion of this situation for future study.

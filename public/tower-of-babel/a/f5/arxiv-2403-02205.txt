Perfect codes in circulant graphs of degree p l − 1 p^{l}-1
Xiaomeng Wang, Oriol Serra, Shou-Jun Xu, Sanming Zhou

Abstract
A perfect code in a graph is an independent set of the graph such that every vertex outside the set is adjacent to exactly one vertex in the set. A circulant graph is a Cayley graph of a cyclic group. In this paper we study perfect codes in circulant graphs of degree p l − 1 p^{l}-1 , where p p is a prime and l ≥ 1 l\geq 1 . We obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for such a circulant graph to admit perfect codes, give a construction of all such circulant graphs which admit perfect codes, and prove a lower bound on the number of distinct perfect codes in such a circulant graph. This extends known results for the case l = 1 l=1 and provides insight on the general problem on the existence and structure of perfect codes in circulant graphs. Key words: perfect code, efficient dominating set, Cayley graph, circulant graph, tiling AMS subject classifications (2020): 05C25, 05C69

Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are finite, undirected and simple, and all groups considered are finite. Let
\Gamma=(V(\Gamma),E(\Gamma))
be a graph and
e\geq 1
an integer. A subset
D
of
V(\Gamma)
is called
[
1
,
22
]
a
perfect
e
-code
in
\Gamma
if every vertex of
\Gamma
is at distance no more than

to exactly one vertex in
D
. In what follows a perfect
1
-code is simply called a

. So a subset
D
of
V(\Gamma)
is a perfect code in
\Gamma
if and only if
D
is an independent set of
\Gamma
and every vertex in
V(\Gamma)\setminus D
has exactly one neighbour in
D
, which holds if and only if
\{\Gamma[x]:x\in D\}
is a partition of
V(\Gamma)
, where
\Gamma[x]
is the closed neighbourhood of
x
in
\Gamma
. A perfect code in a graph is also called an
efficient dominating set

[
6
]
or
independent perfect dominating set

[
11
]
. A graph is distance-transitive
[
1
]
if any pair of vertices can be permuted to any other pair of vertices at the same distance by an automorphism of the graph. Given a group
G
and an inverse-closed subset
S
of
G
excluding the identity element, the
Cayley graph

\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
of
G
with
connection set

S
is the group with vertex set
G
such that
x,y
are adjacent if and only if
xy^{-1}\in S
. Cayley graphs of cyclic groups are widely known as
circulant graphs
.

The study of perfect
e
-codes in graphs began with N. Biggs
[
1
]
who among other things generalized the celebrated Lloyd’s theorem in coding theory to all distance-transitive graphs. Since the Hamming graph
H(d,q)
is distance-transitive, perfect
e
-codes in distance-transitive graphs can be considered as a generalization of perfect
e
-codes in classical coding theory. On the other hand, since
H(d,q)
is the Cayley graph of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{d}
with respect to the set of elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{d}
with exactly one nonzero coordinate, perfect
e
-codes in Cayley graphs can be viewed as another generalization of perfect
e
-codes in coding theory. Also, perfect codes in Cayley graphs are essentially factorizations and tilings of the underlying group
[
8
,
15
,
16
]
. Because of these connections, perfect codes in Cayley graphs have attracted considerable attention in recent years. See
[
7
, Section 1]
and
[
8
, Section 1]
for short surveys of related results and background information. See also
[
3
,
9
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
for recent results on subgroup perfect codes in Cayley graphs, where a perfect code in a Cayley graph is a
subgroup perfect code
if it is a subgroup of the underlying group.

In this paper we study perfect codes in circulant graphs of degree
p^{l}-1
, where
p
is a prime and
l\geq 1
. We obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for such a circulant graph to admit perfect codes, give a construction of all such circulant graphs admitting perfect codes, and obtain a lower bound on the number of distinct perfect codes in such a circulant graph. The characterization extends to the case
l\geq 1
the known one for the case
l=1
and provides a relatively rich set of perfect codes in circulant graphs. A particular motivation of this paper is also to provide a deeper insight on the existence and structure of perfect codes. We note that a subset
D
of an abelian group
G
containing
0
is a perfect code in
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
if and only if
(S_{0},D)
is a tiling of
G
, that is, every element of
G
can be expressed in a unique way as

and

\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

1.1
Some history

Before presenting our results, let us give a brief account of known results on perfect codes in circulant graphs. It was observed in
[

(see also
[
7
, Lemma 2.3]
) that a connected circulant graph

(mod

) for distinct

, and in this case the unique subgroup of

with order

is a perfect code in

. This sufficient condition has been proved to be necessary in the following cases:

(i)

for an odd prime

(
[

, Theorem 3.1]
and
[

, Theorem 1.1]
);

(ii)

a prime and

in

(
[
5
, Theorem 3.4]
and
[

, Theorem 1.2]
);

(iii)

for some (not necessarily distinct) prime divisors

of

such that

and

are coprime and

is periodic (
[

, Theorem 3.2]

(iv)

is aperiodic (
[

, Theorem 3.2]

In
[
13
]
, Obradović, Peters and Ružić gave a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of perfect codes in a circulant graph of degree
3
or
4
, and showed that under this condition all perfect codes are equally spaced (that is, they are cosets of some subgroup perfect codes). In a recent paper, Kwon, Lee and Sohn
[

gave a classification of all perfect codes in any circulant graph of degree

[

, Tamizh Chelvam and Mutharasu proved that a proper subgroup

of

is a perfect code in some connected Cayley graph of

if and only if either

is odd. A few years later Huang, Xia and Zhou proved that this result is a special case (
[

, Corollary 2.8]
) of a general result
[
8
, Theorem 2.7]
in the framework of studying when a given subgroup is a perfect code in some Cayley graph of the group.

In
[
14
]
, Kumar and MacGillivray constructed infinitely many examples of circulant graphs which admit non-equally spaced perfect codes, and they gave a characterization of circulant graphs which admit perfect codes of size
2
or
3
using the wreath product of a circulant graph and a complete graph. Their research prompted the following question
[
14
]
: Is it true that in any circulant graph admitting perfect codes either all perfect codes are equally spaced or the circulant graph is the wreath product of a smaller circulant graph admitting perfect codes and a complete graph on at least two vertices? Our work in this paper shows that the answer to this question is negative; a counterexample will be given in Example
3.1
.

In
[
22
]
, the fourth author studied two families of cyclotomic graphs and perfect
e
-codes in them; they are Cayley graphs on the additive group of
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m}]/A
with respect to
\{\pm(\zeta_{m}^{i}+A):0\leq i\leq m-1\}
and
\{\pm(\zeta_{m}^{i}+A):0\leq i\leq\phi(m)-1\}

(

) is an

th primitive root of unity,

is a nonzero ideal of

, and

[

(see also
[

) as certain Cayley graphs associated with finite Frobenius groups. In the special case when

in conjunction with

.

1.2
Periodic sets

We will only consider Cayley graphs of abelian groups. We will use additive notation for abelian groups and denote their identity elements by
0
. Thus, for an abelian group
G
, a subset
S
of
G\setminus\{0\}
is inverse-closed if
S=-S
, where
-S=\{-x:x\in S\}
, and for this connection set
S
,
x,y\in G
are adjacent in
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
if and only if
x-y\in S
. Write

S_{0}=S\cup\{0\}

and call it the
extended connection set
of
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
. It is readily seen that
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
is a regular graph with degree
|S|
, and
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
is connected if and only if
\langle S\rangle=G
, where for any subset
X\subseteq G
,
\langle X\rangle
denotes the subgroup of
G
generated by
X
. It can be verified that a subset
D
of
G
is a perfect code in
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
if and only if
D+g
is a perfect code in
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
for every
g\in G
. Thus, in studying perfect codes in Cayley graphs of abelian groups, we may assume without loss of generality that
0
is in the perfect code under consideration. Given a subgroup
H
of
G
, let
\pi_{H}:G\to G/H,g\mapsto g+H
denote the canonical projection. Slightly abusing notation, for
X\subseteq G
we use
X/H
to denote the image of
X
under
\pi_{H}
. Any non-zero element
a\in G
satisfying
X+a=X
is called a
period
of
X
. The periods of
X
together with
0
form a subgroup of
G
, denoted by
G_{X}
, which is called
[
16
]
the
subgroup of periods
of
X
. Note that
X
is the union of some cosets of
G_{X}
in
G
. If
G_{X}
is nontrivial, then
X
is
periodic
(or
G_{X}
-periodic
in order to emphasize the subgroup
G_{X}
); otherwise,
X
is
aperiodic
.

Organisation of the paper

Let

G/H=(S_{0}/H)\oplus(D/H)
and
(D-D)\cap H=\{0\}
. So, by the induction hypothesis,
S_{0}/H
is a pyramidal set of
G/H
. Therefore,
S_{0}
is a pyramidal set of
G
by definition.

Case 2.

S_{0}
is aperiodic.

In this case, by Lemma
2.7
,

must be periodic, say,

for some subgroup

with order

dividing

. By Theorem

,
G/K=(S_{0}/K)\oplus(D/K)
and
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap K=\{0\}
. The second condition implies that
|S_{0}/K|=p^{\ell}
. By a further application of Lemma

, we see that
S_{0}/K
is periodic, say, with subgroup of periods

. Thus, by what we have proved in Case 1,
S_{0}/K
is a pyramidal set of
G/K
. Hence, if
H/K
is the subgroup of periods of
S_{0}/K
, where
H
is a subgroup containing

, then
S_{0}/H
is an aperiodic pyramidal set of
G/H
. Let
\{0\}<H_{1}/H<\cdots<H_{2t-1}/H<H_{2t}/H=G/H
be an admissible subgroup series of
G/H
associated with
S_{0}/H
. Then
\{0\}<K<H<H_{1}<\cdots<H_{2t-1}\leq H_{2t}=G
is an admissible subgroup series of
G
associated with
S_{0}
and therefore

is a pyramidal set of
G
.

Sufficiency.
Suppose that
p^{\ell}
divides
n
and
S_{0}
is pyramidal. Then
n=p^{\ell}m
for some integer
m\geq 2
. (Note that
m\neq 1
for otherwise
\Gamma
would be a complete graph, which contradicts our assumption.) Let
H_{0}<H_{1}<\cdots<H_{2t-1}\leq H_{2t}=G
be an admissible subgroup series of
G
associated with
S_{0}
. To prove that
\Gamma
admits a perfect code, it suffices to prove the existence of a subset
D
of
G
such that
G=S_{0}\oplus D
.

Suppose first that
S_{0}
is aperiodic. Then
H_{0}=\{0\}
. If
t=1
, then, by (T1),
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap H_{1}=0
. In particular,
|S_{0}/H_{1}|=|S_{0}|
and, by (T3),
n=|S_{0}/H_{1}|\cdot|H_{1}|=|S_{0}|\cdot|H_{1}|
. Therefore, the sum
S_{0}\oplus H_{1}
is direct and is equal to
G
, completing the proof in this case. Assume
t>1
. By (T3),
|G/H_{2t-1}|=|S_{0}/H_{2t-1}|
. So
|(G/H_{2t-2})/(H_{2t-1}/H_{2t-2})|=|G/H_{2t-1}|=|S_{0}/H_{2t-1}|
.
Again, by (T3),
|G/H_{2t-2}|=|S_{0}/H_{2t-1}||H_{2t-1}/H_{2t-2}|=|S_{0}/H_{2t-2}||H_{2t-1}/H_{2t-2}|
. Combining (T1) and Lemma
2.6
, we obtain that
G/H_{2t-2}=(S_{0}/H_{2t-2})\oplus(H_{2t-1}/H_{2t-2})
. Using Theorem
2.10
and (T2), we see that there exists a subset
D\subset G
such that
G/H_{2t-3}=(S_{0}/H_{2t-3})\oplus(D/H_{2t-3})
. Moreover,

(G/H_{2t-4})/(H_{2t-3}/H_{2t-4})=\left((S_{0}/H_{2t-4})/(H_{2t-3}/H_{2t-4})\right)\oplus\left((D/H_{2t-4})/(H_{2t-3}/H_{2t-4})\right).

Again, using Lemma
2.6
and (T1), we obtain that
G/H_{2t-4}=(S_{0}/H_{2t-4})\oplus((D/H_{2t-4})+(H_{2t-3}/H_{2t-4}))
. Since
t
is finite, repeating this process we can eventually obtain a subset
D^{\prime}\subset G
containing
0
such that
G/H_{1}=(S_{0}/H_{1})\oplus(D^{\prime}/H_{1})
, and a further application of Theorem
2.1
using (T1) with

gives

as desired.

In the case when
S_{0}
is period, the proof is essentially the same as above with
G/H_{0},S_{0}/H_{0}
playing the role of
G,S_{0}
, respectively, where
H_{0}
is the subgroup of periods of
S_{0}
in
G
.

Up to now we have proved the necessary and sufficient condition in Theorem
1.2
.

We now prove (a) and (b). Suppose that
p^{\ell}
divides
n
,
S_{0}
is pyramidal and aperiodic, and the longest admissible subgroup series associated with
S_{0}
is
H_{0}<H_{1}<H_{2}<\dots<H_{2t-1}\leq H_{2t}=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Then
n=p^{\ell}m
, where
m\geq 2
as
\Gamma
is non-complete, and
H_{0}=\{0\}
as
S_{0}
is aperiodic. By what we have proved above,
\Gamma
admits perfect codes.

(a) Assume
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap\langle p^{\ell}\rangle=\{0\}
. Consider the case
t=1
first. In this case we have
H_{1}=\langle p^{\ell}\rangle
and
\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}/H_{1},(S_{0}/H_{1})\setminus\{H_{1}\})
is a complete graph with
0+H_{1}
as the unique perfect code containing the identity element of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}/H_{1}
. By Lemma
2.4
, we have
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=S_{0}\oplus H_{1}
. Hence
H_{1}
is the only perfect code of
\Gamma
containing
0
.

Now consider the case
t>1
. In this case
S_{0}/H_{1}
is periodic and its subgroup of periods has order
p^{\ell_{1}}
for some integer
0<\ell_{1}<\ell
. Thus,
\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}/H_{1},(S_{0}/H_{1})\setminus\{H_{1}\})
admits a perfect code
D/H_{1}
which is not a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}/H_{1}
. So, by Theorem
2.1
and the definition of a pyramidal set,
\Gamma
admits
D+H_{1}
as a perfect code which is not a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. On the other hand, since
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap\langle p^{\ell}\rangle=\langle 0\rangle
and
|S_{0}|\cdot|\langle p^{\ell}\rangle|=p^{\ell}m
,
S_{0}+\langle p^{\ell}\rangle
is a direct sum and
S_{0}\oplus\langle p^{\ell}\rangle=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
by Lemma

(c), and so

is a perfect code in
\Gamma
. Thus the subgroup
\langle p^{\ell}\rangle
of

with order

is a perfect code in
\Gamma
.

(b) Assume
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap\langle p^{\ell}\rangle\neq\{0\}
. Then by Theorem
2.1
,
\langle p^{\ell}\rangle
is not a perfect code in
\Gamma
. Since any perfect code in
\Gamma
has size

and
\langle p^{\ell}\rangle
is the only subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
with order

, it follows that
\Gamma
admits no subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
as a perfect code. However,

admits perfect codes as mentioned earlier. Hence there are perfect codes in

but none of them is
\langle p^{\ell}\rangle
.

\Box

A graph
\Sigma

such that for each

\Sigma

\rho(v)
in
\Gamma
. Call

a

from
\Sigma
to
\Gamma

have the same size, say,

is a

of
\Gamma
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Sigma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
be a circulant graph. Let

be a divisor of

and

with order

if

H

Remark 4.2
.

(i) If
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap H=\{0\}

Let

Lemma 4.9

\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
are distinct circulant graphs of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we have

. Thus one of the following cases happens.

Case 1.

\{x\pmod{|R_{0}|}:x\in R_{0}\}\neq\{x\pmod{|S_{0}|}:x\in S_{0}\}

4.7
, for any

and

\bar{f}_{m}(R_{0})\cap\bar{f}_{m}(S_{0})=\emptyset

\{x\pmod{|R_{0}|}:x\in R_{0}\}=\{x\pmod{|S_{0}|}:x\in S_{0}\}

(s-r)+(\tau(s)-\sigma(r))n=0

, where

We use the notation in Construction
5.3

, for

We prove the first statements in (a) and (b) by induction on
t
. Consider the base case

is aperiodic, then

and by (T3),
\Gamma_{1}
is a complete graph and

\Gamma_{1}

is the unique perfect code in
\Gamma
containing

. Now assume
S_{0}
is
H_{0}
-periodic. Then
H_{0}\neq\{0\}
and
\Gamma_{1}
is a circulant graph whose extended connection set
S_{0}/H_{1}
is an aperiodic pyramidal set of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}/H_{1}
. Applying what we just proved to
\Gamma_{1}
, we obtain that
D_{0}
is the unique perfect code in
\Gamma_{1}
containing
0
. Moreover,
D_{0}=\{0,p^{h_{0}},\ldots,(m-1)p^{h_{0}}\}
. Since
\Gamma\cong\Gamma_{0}\otimes\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell-h_{0}}},\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell-h_{0}}}\setminus\{0\})
, by the definition of the wreath product we see that
D_{1}=\{x+\tau_{1}(x):x\in D_{0}\}
is a perfect code in
\Gamma
containing
0
, where
\tau_{1}
is a map from
D_{0}
to
p^{h_{0}}k_{0}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell-h_{0}}}
satisfying
\tau_{1}(0)=0
. Since
D_{0}
is the unique perfect code in
\Gamma_{0}
containing
0
, by the definition of the wreath product, all perfect codes in
\Gamma
containing
0
can be obtained by different choices of
\tau_{1}
. So each perfect code in
\Gamma
containing

can be constructed from

. This establishes the result in the base case

Suppose that the result is true when

for some

. Consider the case when

. Assume

is aperiodic first. Then

is a circulant graph whose extended connection set

. It can be verified that the longest admissible subgroup series associated with

\mathbf{h}(T_{0})=(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{l-1})
and
\mathbf{k}(T_{0})=(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{l-1})
, where

for

. By our hypothesis,
D_{2l-4}
is a perfect code in
\Gamma_{2}
containing
0
. Since
\Gamma_{1}\cong\Gamma_{2}\otimes\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{h_{0}-h_{1}}},\mathbb{Z}_{p^{h_{0}-h_{1}}}\setminus\{0\})
,
D_{2l-3}=\{x+\tau_{l-1}(x):x\in D_{2l-4}\}
(see (
10
)) is a perfect code in
\Gamma_{1}
containing
0
, where
\tau_{l-1}
is a map from

to

to

S_{0}
is aperiodic. Let

\displaystyle=p^{\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}(a_{i-1}-a_{i})b_{i}+(\ell-h_{0})k_{0}+a_{l-1}-a_{0}-\ell+h_{0}}\hskip 10.00002pt(\text{as $a_{-1}=a_{0}=h_{0}$})

\displaystyle=p^{\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}(h_{i-1}-h_{i})k_{i}+(\ell-h_{0})k_{0}+h_{l-1}-\ell}\hskip 10.00002pt(\text{as $a_{i}=h_{i}$ and $b_{i}=k_{i}$ for $0\leq i\leq l$})

\displaystyle=p^{\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}(h_{i-1}-h_{i})k_{i}+(h_{-1}-h_{0})k_{0}+h_{l-1}-\ell}\hskip 10.00002pt(\text{taking $h_{-1}=\ell$})

\displaystyle=p^{\left(\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}(h_{i-1}-h_{i})k_{i}\right)+h_{l-1}-\ell}.

So the result holds for
\Gamma
.

Combining the two cases above, we have proved that the result is true when
t=l
. This completes the proof by mathematical induction.

\Box

6
Final remarks

The main result of this paper gives a complete explicit description of the family of circulant graphs admitting a perfect code with extended connection set of cardinality a prime power (Theorem
1.3
) as well as a structural description of such circulant graphs (Theorem
1.2
). The characterization also leads to a counting result (Theorem
1.4
) which shows that the universe of perfect codes in circulant graphs is relatively rich.

The characterization can be extended by a similar approach to the case of perfect codes in the directed case, namely when the connection set
S
of
\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
is not symmetric, although the corresponding results are more involved and we have not attempted to report them here. In another direction, a result analogous to Theorem
1.2
can be obtained for the closely related family of Cayley sum graphs
\mathrm{Cay}^{+}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
of cyclic groups, where two vertices
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
are adjacent if and only if
x+y\in S
.

The main limitation of our approach is the restriction to prime powers for the cardinality of the extended connection sets. This is due to the use of the key Lemma
2.7
, which provides the strategy to reach the pyramidal structure in Theorem
1.2
and then to the recursive description of the family of circulant graphs admitting perfect codes by lifts of the complete graph given in Construction
4.6
. It is still an open problem to determine the extent to which the statement of Lemma
2.7
can be generalized. In view of the results in the present paper it appears that a complete characterization of perfect codes, even in the case of circulant graphs, beyond the prime power case may become a project of high complexity. In any case, in absence of the result in Lemma
2.7
, new ideas and techniques will be required.

Appendix A
A formula

In this appendix we give a formula for the number of connected circulant graphs
\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
with order
n
and degree
p^{\ell}-1
such that
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap\langle p^{\ell}\rangle=\{0\}
. Note that, by Theorem
1.2
, such a graph
\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
admits
\langle p^{\ell}\rangle
as a perfect code if in addition
S_{0}
is pyramidal and aperiodic. To obtain our formula we need the following two lemmas.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
n=p^{\ell}m
be an integer, where
p
is a prime and
\ell\geq 1,m\geq 2
are integers. Let
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
be a circulant graph with order
n
and degree
p^{\ell}-1
such that
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap\langle p^{\ell}\rangle=\{0\}
. If
\Gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{l},\mathbf{m}}(n,p^{\ell})
for some sequences
\mathbf{l}=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{t}),\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{t})
of positive integers satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\ell_{i}=\ell
and
\prod_{i=1}^{t}m_{i}=m
, then
p
is not a divisor of
m_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq t
.

Proof.

Let
r=\ell_{t}
. Suppose, for a contradiction, that
p
is a divisor of
m_{i}
for some
i
between
2
and
t
. Choose
k
to be the largest subscript such that
p
divides
m_{k}
. Then
2\leq k\leq t
and
p
is not a divisor of
m_{i}
for
k<i\leq t
. For
1\leq i\leq t
, let
\mathbf{l}_{i}=(\ell_{i},\ldots,\ell_{t})
,
\mathbf{m}_{i}=(m_{i},\ldots,m_{t})
,
\ell(i)=\ell_{i}+\cdots+\ell_{t}
, and
n(i)=p^{\ell_{i}+\cdots+\ell_{t}}m_{i}\cdots m_{t}=p^{\ell(i)}m_{i}\cdots m_{t}
. Let
\Gamma_{i}=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n(i)},S(i))
be a circulant graph in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{l}_{i},\mathbf{m}_{i}}(n(i),p^{\ell(i)})
. In particular,
\Gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{l}_{k},\mathbf{m}_{k}}(n(k),p^{\ell(k)})
. By part (c) of Lemma
4.10
, we have

\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{l}_{k},\mathbf{m}_{k}}(n(k),p^{\ell(k)})\subseteq(\bar{f}_{m_{k}}\cdots\bar{f}_{m_{t}})(\bar{g}_{p^{\ell_{k}}}\cdots\bar{g}_{p^{\ell_{t-1}}}(K_{p^{\ell_{t}}})).

Moreover, by part (b) of Lemma
4.10
, we have
(\bar{g}_{p^{\ell_{k}}}\cdots\bar{g}_{p^{\ell_{t}-1}})(K_{p^{\ell_{t}}})=K_{p^{\ell(k)}}
. So, by part (a) of Lemma
4.10
,
\Gamma_{k}\in(\bar{f}_{m_{k}}\cdots\bar{f}_{m_{t}})(K_{p^{\ell(k)}})=\bar{f}_{m_{k}\cdots m_{t}}(K_{p^{\ell(k)}})
. Note that
K_{p^{\ell(k)}}=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell(k)}},\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell(k)}}\setminus\{0\})
and
x-y\not\equiv 0\pmod{p^{\ell(k)}}
for distinct
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell(k)}}
. By part (a) of Lemma
4.7
, we have
x-y\not\equiv 0\pmod{p^{\ell(k)}}
for distinct
x,y\in S(k)_{0}
. Let
T(\sigma)_{0}\in\bar{g}_{p^{\ell_{k-1}}}(S(k)_{0})
. Then
T(\sigma)_{0}=\{s+in(k):s\in S(k)_{0},0\leq i\leq p^{\ell_{k-1}}-1\}
, where
n(k)
is as above. Since
p
is a divisor of
m_{k}
and
\ell_{k-1}+\ell(k)=\ell(k-1)
,
p^{\ell_{k-1}-1}n(k)
is divisible by
p^{\ell(k-1)}
. Thus, by part (c) of Lemma
4.7
, there are distinct elements
x,y\in S_{0}
such that
x-y\equiv 0\pmod{p^{\ell}}
, that is,
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap\langle p^{\ell}\rangle\neq\{0\}
, which contradicts our assumption.

\Box

For positive integers
d,n
and prime
p
, let
\nu(d)
be the number of prime divisors of
d
and
D_{n}^{p}
the set of square-free divisors of
n
greater than
1
and coprime to
p
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
n=p^{\ell}m
, where
p
is a prime and
\ell>1,m\geq 2
are integers such that
\ell+p\geq 4
.
If
D_{n}^{p}\neq\emptyset
, then the number of elements
T(\sigma)\in\bar{f}_{m}(K_{p^{\ell}})
satisfying
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))\neq 1
are exactly
\sum\limits_{d\in D_{n}^{p}}(-1)^{\nu(d)-1}(m/d)^{\frac{p^{\ell}-1}{2}}
.

Proof.

For each integer
1\leq i\leq p^{\ell}-1
, set

P_{i}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}:x\equiv i\pmod{p^{\ell}}\}=\{i,i+p^{\ell},\ldots,i+(m-1)p^{\ell}\}.

Note that for any feasible map
\sigma
from
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell}}\setminus\{0\}
to
\{0,1,\ldots,(n/p^{\ell})-1\}
,
T(\sigma)=\{x+\sigma(x)p^{\ell}:x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell}}\setminus\{0\}\}
. Thus, for each integer
1\leq i\leq p^{\ell}-1
,
|T(\sigma)\cap P_{i}|=1
.
View
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell}}
as a set.
Since
p
is not a divisor of
{\rm gcd}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell}}\setminus\{0\})
,
p
is not a divisor of
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))
.

For any integer
d\in D_{n}^{p}
, if
{\rm gcd}(p,d)=1
, then
{\rm gcd}(p^{\ell},d)=1
. There are two integers
a,b
such that
ad+bp^{\ell}=1
, and so
ad=-bp^{\ell}+1
. Then for any integer
1\leq i\leq p^{\ell}-1
, there is an integer
\alpha
between
0
and
d-1
such that
d
is a divisor of
i+\alpha p^{\ell}
and for any integer
0\leq\beta\leq\alpha-1
,
d
is not a divisor of
i+\beta p^{\ell}
.
Clearly, for each integer
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,(m/d)-1\}
,
d
is also a divisor of
i+(jd+\alpha)p^{\ell}
.
Thus for each integer
1\leq i\leq p^{\ell}-1
there are exactly
m/d
elements in
P_{i}
such that
d
is a divisor of these
m
elements. Moreover, since
T(\sigma)
is inverse-closed in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, if
s
is fixed, then
-s
is also fixed. Hence for each divisor
d\in D_{n}^{p}
there are exactly
(m/d)^{\frac{p^{\ell}-1}{2}}
connection sets
T(\sigma)
in
\bar{f}_{m}(K_{p^{\ell}})
such that
d
is a divisor of
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))
.

Suppose
m=p^{a(0)}p^{a(1)}_{1}p^{a(2)}_{2}\cdots p^{a(b)}_{b}
, where
a(0)\geq 0
,
p,p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{b}
are different primes and for any integer
1\leq i\leq b
,
a(i)\geq 1
. For any integer
1\leq i\leq b
, define
W(i)=\{p_{1(1)}p_{1(2)}\cdots p_{1(i)}:p_{1(1)},p_{1(2)},\ldots,p_{1(i)}\text{ are different elements of }\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{b}\}\}
.
For each integer
1\leq i\leq p^{\ell}-1
, define

U(i)=\{T(\sigma)_{0}\in\bar{f}_{m}(K_{p^{\ell}}):\text{there exists $d\in W(i)$ such that $d$ is a divisor of ${\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))$}\}.

Let
F(i)=\sum_{d\in W(i)}(m/d)^{\frac{p^{\ell}-1}{2}}
. Note that
U(b)\subseteq U(b-1)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U(1)
. Thus each extended connection set
T(\sigma)_{0}
in
\bar{f}_{m}(K_{p^{\ell}})
satisfying
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))\neq 1
is counted by
F(1)
. Moreover, it is known that for any integer
1\leq i<j\leq b
,
F(i)
counts extended connection sets
T(\sigma)_{0}
in
\bar{f}_{m}(K_{p^{\ell}})
satisfying that there is an integer
d\in W(j)
such that
d
is a divisor of
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))
exactly
\binom{j}{i}
times. Thus, in order to count the number of elements
T(\sigma)_{0}\in\bar{f}_{m}(K_{p^{\ell}})
satisfying
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))\neq 1
, we only need to consider the number of extended connection sets
T(\sigma)_{0}\in\bar{f}_{m}(K_{p^{\ell}})
satisfying
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))\in D_{n}^{p}
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{a}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{a}{i}=1
for any integer
a\geq 1
, for any
1\leq j\leq b
,
\sum_{i=1}^{j}(-1)^{j-1}F(j)
counts each extended connection set
T(\sigma)_{0}
in
\bar{f}_{m}(K_{p^{\ell}})
satisfying
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))\in\cup_{i=1}^{j}W(j)
exactly once. In particular, taking
j=b
, we obtain that the number of elements
T(\sigma)\in\bar{f}_{m}(K_{p^{\ell}})
satisfying
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))\neq 1
is exactly
\sum\limits_{d\in D_{n}^{p}}(-1)^{\nu(d)-1}(m/d)^{\frac{p^{\ell}-1}{2}}
.

\Box

Theorem A.3
.

Let
n=p^{\ell}m
, where
p
is a prime and
\ell\geq 1,m\geq 2
are integers such that
\ell+p\geq 4
. Then the number of connected circulant graphs
\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
with order
n
and degree
p^{\ell}-1
such that
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap\langle p^{\ell}\rangle=\{0\}
is equal to

\left(\frac{n}{p^{\ell}}\right)^{r_{\ell}}+\sum_{d\in D_{n}^{p}}(-1)^{\nu(d)}\left(\frac{n}{p^{\ell}d}\right)^{r_{\ell}},

(11)

where
r_{\ell}=(p^{\ell}-1)/2
if
p
is odd and
r_{\ell}=2^{\ell-2}
if
p=2
, with the understanding that the second term vanishes when
D_{n}^{p}=\emptyset
.

Denote by
N(n,p^{\ell})
the said number in the theorem. The condition
\ell+p\geq 4
ensures
\ell\geq 2
when
p=2
. (If
\ell=1
and
p=2
, then
N(n,p^{\ell})=0
as there is no connected circulant graph with order
n\geq 4
and degree
1
.) Note that when
D_{n}^{2}=\emptyset
we have
n=2^{\ell}
and (
11
) yields
N(n,2^{\ell})=1
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.3
, a connected circulant graph
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
with order
n
and degree
p^{\ell}-1
admits a perfect code if and only if
\Gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{l},\mathbf{m}}(n,p^{\ell})
for some
\mathbf{l}=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{t})
and
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{t})
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\ell_{i}=\ell
and
\prod_{i=1}^{t}m_{i}=m
. If in addition
\Gamma
satisfies
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap\langle|S_{0}|\rangle=\{0\}
, then by Lemma
A.1
we have
{\rm gcd}(m_{i},p)=1
for
2\leq i\leq t
. By parts (a)–(c) of Lemma
4.10
, we have
\Gamma\in\bar{f}_{n/p^{\ell}}(K_{p^{\ell}})
. Note that any element of
\bar{f}_{n/p^{\ell}}(K_{p^{\ell}})
is of the form
T(\sigma)=\{x+\sigma(x)p^{\ell}:x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell}}\setminus\{0\}\}
, where
\sigma
is a feasible map from
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell}}
to
\{0,1,\ldots,(n/p^{\ell})-1\}
(so that
\sigma(0)=0
and
-T(\sigma)=T(\sigma)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
).

Assume
p
is odd. Since for each
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell}}\setminus\{0\}
,
\sigma(-x)
is determined by
\sigma(x)
, each
\sigma\in\bar{f}_{n/p^{\ell}}(K_{p^{\ell}})
is determined by
\sigma(x)
for
x\in\{1,2,\ldots,(p^{\ell}-1)/2\}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell}}\setminus\{0\}
. Since each
\sigma(x)
can independently take any value from
\{0,1,\ldots,(n/p^{\ell})-1\}
, it follows that the cardinality of
\bar{f}_{n/p^{\ell}}(K_{p^{\ell}})
is exactly
(n/p^{\ell})^{\frac{p^{\ell}-1}{2}}
. Since
\Gamma
satisfies
(S_{0}-S_{0})\cap\langle|S_{0}|\rangle=\{0\}
and
|S_{0}|=p^{\ell}
, for any
T(\sigma)\in\bar{f}_{n/p^{\ell}}(K_{p^{\ell}})
and
y\in T(\sigma)
, we have
y\not\equiv 0\pmod{p^{\ell}}
. By the definition of
T(\sigma)
,
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))
is not divisible by
p
. If
D_{n}^{p}=\emptyset
, then
n/p^{\ell}=p^{r}
for some integer
r\geq 0
. Since
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))
is a divisor of
n=p^{\ell+r}
, by the definition of
T(\sigma)
again we see that
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))=1
and hence all circulant graphs in
\bar{f}_{n/p^{\ell}}(K_{p^{\ell}})
are connected. Hence, if
D_{n}^{p}=\emptyset
, then
N(n,p^{\ell})=(n/p^{\ell})^{\frac{p^{\ell}-1}{2}}
. If
D_{n}^{p}\neq\emptyset
, then by Lemma
A.2
the number of elements
T(\sigma)\in\bar{f}_{n/p^{\ell}}(K_{p^{\ell}})
satisfying
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))\neq 1
is exactly
\sum\limits_{d\in D_{n}^{p}}(-1)^{\nu(d)-1}(n/(p^{\ell}d))^{\frac{p^{\ell}-1}{2}}
, and therefore
N(n,p^{\ell})
is given by (
11
).

Now assume
p=2
. Then
\ell\geq 2
by our assumption, and
n/2
belongs to every
T(\sigma)\in\bar{f}_{n/2^{\ell}}(K_{2^{\ell}})
. So there are exactly
(n/2^{\ell})^{2^{\ell-2}}
different elements
T(\sigma)\in\bar{f}_{n/2^{\ell}}(K_{2^{\ell}})
, all of which are such that
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))
is odd. By Remark
4.5
,
{\rm gcd}(n/2^{\ell},2)=1
. Thus, if
D_{n}^{2}=\emptyset
, then
n=2^{\ell}
and hence
\bar{f}_{n/2^{\ell}}(K_{2^{\ell}})
contains only one graph, yielding
N(n,2^{\ell})=1
. If
D_{n}^{2}\neq\emptyset
, then there are exactly
\sum\limits_{d\in D_{n}^{2}}(-1)^{\nu(d)-1}(n/(2^{\ell}d))^{2^{\ell-2}}
different elements
T(\sigma)\in\bar{f}_{n/2^{\ell}}(K_{2^{\ell}})
such that
{\rm gcd}(T(\sigma))\neq 1
, and hence
N(n,2^{\ell})
is given by (
11
).

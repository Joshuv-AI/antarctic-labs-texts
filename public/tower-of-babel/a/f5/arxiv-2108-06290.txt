Sklyanin algebras and a cubic root of 1
Natalia Iyudu, Stanislav Shkarin

Abstract
We consider Sklyanin algebras S S with 3 generators, which are quadratic algebras over a field 𝕂 {\mathbb{K}} with 3 3 generators x , y , z x,y,z given by 3 3 relations p ​ x ​ y + q ​ y ​ x + r ​ z ​ z = 0 pxy+qyx+rzz=0 , p ​ y ​ z + q ​ z ​ y + r ​ x ​ x = 0 pyz+qzy+rxx=0 and p ​ z ​ x + q ​ x ​ z + r ​ y ​ y = 0 pzx+qxz+ryy=0 , where p , q , r ∈ 𝕂 p,q,r\in{\mathbb{K}} . This class of algebras has enjoyed much attention. In particular, using tools from algebraic geometry Artin, Tate and Van Den Berg [ 4 ] showed that if at least two of the parameters p p , q q and r r are non-zero and at least two of three numbers p 3 p^{3} , q 3 q^{3} and r 3 r^{3} are distinct, then S S is Artin–Schelter regular. More specifically, S S is Koszul and has the same Hilbert series as the algebra of commutative polynomials in 3 indeterminates. It has became commonly accepted that it is impossible to achieve the same objective by purely algebraic and combinatorial means like the Gröbner basis technique. The authors have previously dispelled this belief. However our previous proof was no less complicated than the one based on algebraic geometry. It used a construcion of a Gröbner basis in a suitable one-sided module over S S and had quite a number of cases to consider. In this paper we exhibit a linear substitution after which it becomes possible to determine the leading monomials of a reduced Gröbner basis for the ideal of relations of S S itself (without passing to a module). We also find out explicitly (in terms of parameters) which Sklyanin algebras are isomorphic. The only drawback of the new technique is that it fails if the characteristic of the ground field equals 3.

Another concept playing an important role in this paper is Koszulity. For a quadratic algebra

equips

with respect to some free bases consist of elements of

(=are homogeneous of degree

The notion of a
noncommutative potential
was introduced in
[
15
]

[
5
]

for every

\frac{\delta}{\delta x_{j}}:{\mathbb{K}}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle\to{\mathbb{K}}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle

\frac{\delta u}{\delta x_{j}}=0

and
\frac{\delta u}{\delta x_{j}}=v
if

. A potential algebra

defined by a potential

is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra given by the generators
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and the relations
\frac{\delta F}{\delta x_{j}}=0
for

. For the sake of convenience, we consider the onto linear map
G\mapsto G^{\rcirclearrowleft}
from

onto
{\mathbb{K}}^{\rm cyc}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
defined by its action on homogeneous elements by

, where

Prime examples of a potential algebras are Sklyanin algebras. Recall that if
(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{3}

Sklyanin algebra

Q^{p,q,r}
is the quadratic algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
with generators

\text{$pyz+qzy+rxx=0$},\quad\text{$pzx+qxz+ryy=0$},\quad\text{$pxy+qyx+rzz=0$}.

It is easy to observe that

is a potential algebra given by the potential
r(x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3})+pxyz^{\rcirclearrowleft}+qxzy^{\rcirclearrowleft}
.

It is worth mentioning that our use of potentiality of Sklyanin algebras is just a matter of convenience. Namely, it makes the job of parforming a linear substitution much easier. Instead of making the sub in each of the three relations, one can perform it with the potential and then compute the ’derivatives’
\frac{\delta}{\delta x_{j}}
yielding the same result.

Odesskii
[
17
]
proved that in the case
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
, a generic Sklyanin algebra is a PHS-algebra. That is,

\textstyle H_{Q^{p,q,r}}(t)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(j+2)(j+1)}{2}\,t^{j}\ \ \text{for generic $(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{3}$,}

where generic means outside the union of countably many algebraic varieties in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
(different from
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
). In particular, the equality above holds for almost all
(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Polishchuk and Positselski
[
19
]
showed in the same setting and with the same meaning of the word ’generic’, that for generic
(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, the algebra
Q^{p,q,r}
is Koszul but is not a PBW-algebra. The same results are implicitly contained in the Artin, Shelter paper
[
2
]
.

Artin, Tate and Van den Berg
[
4
,
3
]
, and Feigin, Odesskii
[
18
]
, considered certain family of infinite dimensional representations of Sklyanin algebra. Namely, they used representations, where variables are represented by infinite matrices with one nonzero upper diagonal. Equivalently, they considered graded modules with all graded components being one-dimensional. The geometric interpretation of the space of such modules is in the core for most of their arguments. Artin, Tate and Van den Berg showed that if at least two of the parameters

,

and

are non-zero and the equality

is Artin–Shelter regular. More specifically,

is Koszul and has the same Hilbert series as the algebra of commutative polynomials in three variables.

It became commonly accepted that it is impossible to obtain the same results by purely algebraic and combinatorial means like the Gröbner basis technique, see, for instance, comments in
[
17
,
26
]
. We have dispelled this notion in
[
13
]
. However our previous proof is rather complicated. It uses a construcion of a Gröbner basis in a suitable one-sided module over

and has quite a number of cases to consider. In this paper we exhibit a linear substitution after which it becomes possible to determine the leading monomials of a reduced Gröbner basis for the ideal of relations of
S
itself (without passing to a module). We also find out explicitly (in terms of parameters) which Sklyanin algebras are isomorphic. The only drawback of the new technique is that it fails if the characteristic of the ground field equals 3.

Theorem 1.1
.

The algebra
Q^{p,q,r}
is Koszul for any
(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{3}
. The algebra
Q^{p,q,r}
is PHS
if and only if at least two of

,
q
and
r
are non-zero and the equality

fails.

We stress again that the above theorem is essentially one of the main results in
[
4
]
with a different proof provided in
[
13
]
. Our new proof here is however much easier and shorter, since we found clever change of variables which makes the Gröbner basis regular (which is a generalisation of finiteness).

General background

We shall use the following well-known facts, all of which can be found in
[
19
]
. Every monomial quadratic algebra
A=A(V,R)
(=there are linear bases
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}
in
V
and
R
respectively, such that each

is a monomial in

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in

defined by
b(u,v)=\delta_{u,v}
for every monomials

and

in the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. The algebra
A^{!}=A(V,R^{\perp})
, where
R^{\perp}=\{u\in V^{2}:b(r,u)=0\ \text{for each}\ r\in R\}
, is called the
dual algebra
of

. Clearly,

is a quadratic algebra in its own right. Recall also that there is a specific complex of free right
A
-modules, called the Koszul complex, whose exactness is equivalent to the Koszulity of

:

\dots\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{d_{k+1}}(A^{!}_{k})^{*}\otimes A\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{d_{k}}(A^{!}_{k-1})^{*}\otimes A\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{d_{k-1}}\dots\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{d_{1}}(A^{!}_{0})^{*}\otimes A=A\longrightarrow{\mathbb{K}}\to 0,

(2.1)

where the tensor products are over
{\mathbb{K}}
, the second last arrow is the augmentation map, each tensor product carries the natural structure of a free right

-module and
d_{k}

d_{k}(\varphi\otimes u)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\varphi_{j}\otimes x_{j}u
, where
\varphi_{j}\in(A^{!}_{k-1})^{*}

\varphi_{j}(v)=\varphi(x_{j}v)
. Although

and the Koszul complex seem to depend on the choice of a basis in

, it is not really the case up to the natural equivalence
[
19
]
. We recall that

every PBW-algebra is Koszul;

\displaystyle\text{$A$ is Koszul $\iff$ $A^{!}$ is Koszul};

\displaystyle\text{if $A$ is Koszul, then $H_{A}(-t)H_{A^{!}}(t)=1$}.

(2.2)

Note that the Koszul complex (
2.1
) of any quadratic algebra is exact at its last
3
terms:
{\mathbb{K}}
,
(A^{!}_{0})^{*}\otimes A=A
and
(A^{!}_{1})^{*}\otimes A
. The following lemma proved in
[
13
]
allows us to prove Koszulity of Sklyain algebras once we have computed their Hilbert series. We say that

is a

if
Vu=\{0\}
in

. A right annihilator

if

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A=A(V,R)
be a quadratic algebra such that
A^{!}_{4}=\{0\}
,
A^{!}_{3}
is one-dimensional and
wA_{2}^{!}\neq\{0\}
for every non-zero
w\in A_{1}^{!}
. Then the following statements are equivalent

(
2.1
.1)

is Koszul
;

(
2.1
.2)

has no non-trivial right annihilators and
H_{A}(-t)H_{A^{!}}(t)=1
.

Our next observation is that neither Koszulity nor the Hilbert series of a quadratic algebra

is sensitive to changing the ground field.

Remark 2.2
.

Fix the bases
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
r_{1},\dots,r_{m}
in
V
and
R
respectively. Then
A=A(V,R)
is given by the generators
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and the relations
r_{1},\dots,r_{m}
. Let
{\mathbb{K}}_{0}
be the subfield of
{\mathbb{K}}
generated by the coefficients in the relations
r_{1},\dots,r_{m}
and

be the
{\mathbb{K}}_{0}
-algebra defined by the exact same generators
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and the exact same relations
r_{1},\dots,r_{m}
. Then
A
is Koszul if and only if
B
is Koszul (see, for instance,
[
19
]
) and the Hilbert series of
A
and of
B
coincide. The latter follows from the fact that the Hilbert series depends only on the set of leading monomials of the Gröbner basis. Now the Gröbner basis construction algorithm for
A
and for

produces exactly the same result. Thus if a quadratic algebra given by generators and relations makes sense over 2 fields of the same characteristic, then the choice of the field does not effect its Hilbert series or its Koszulity. In particular, replacing the original field
{\mathbb{K}}
by its algebraic closure or by an even bigger field does not change the Hilbert series or Koszulity of
A
. On the other hand, the PBW-property is sensitive to changing the ground field
[
19
]
.

3
Elementary observations

The following elementary facts are proved in
[
13
]
. The next lemma is proved by a direct computation of the reduced Gröbner basis for the ideal of relations of the duals of Sklyanin algebras (using the usual left-to-right degree lexicographical ordering on monomials assuming
x>y>z
. The last statement is verified directly since knowing the Gröner basis we know the multiplication table in the relevant finite dimensional (in this case) algebra
A^{!}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{3}
and
A=Q^{p,q,r}
. Then the Hilbert series of
A^{!}
is given by

H_{A^{!}}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1+3t&\text{if $p=q=r=0$;}\\
\textstyle\frac{1+2t}{1-t}&\text{if $p^{3}=q^{3}=r^{3}\neq 0$ or exactly two of $p$, $q$ and $r$ equal $0$;}\\
(1+t)^{3}&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

Moreover,
A_{2}^{!}w\neq\{0\}
for each non-zero
w\in A_{1}^{!}
provided
H_{A^{!}}(t)=(1+t)^{3}
.

Note
[
19
]
that for every quadratic algebra
A=A(V,R)
(Koszul or otherwise), the power series
H_{A}(t)H_{A^{!}}(-t)-1
starts with

with

. This allows to determine
{\rm dim}\,A_{3}
provided we know
{\rm dim}\,A^{!}_{j}
for

. Applying this observation together with (

), we immediately obtain the following fact.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{3}
and

{\rm dim}\,A_{3}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}27&\text{if $p=q=r=0;$}\\
12&\text{if $p^{3}=q^{3}=r^{3}\neq 0$ or exactly two of $p$, $q$ and $r$ equal $0;$}\\
10&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

Obviously, multiplying
(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{3}
by a non-zero scalar does not change the algebra
Q^{p,q,r}
. It turns out that there are non-proportional triples of parameters, which lead to isomorphic (as graded algebras) Sklyanin algebras. The following three lemmas can be found in

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{3}
and
\theta\in{\mathbb{K}}
is such that
\theta^{3}=1
and
\theta\neq 1
. Then the graded algebras

and

are isomorphic.

Proof.

The relations of
Q^{p,q,r}
in the variables

,
v
,
w
given by

,

and

read

puv+qvu+\theta rww=0
,
pwu+quw+\theta rvv=0
and
pvw+qwv+\theta ruu=0
. Thus this change of variables provides an isomorphism between

and

.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{3}
and
\theta\in{\mathbb{K}}

. Then the graded algebras

.

Proof.

A direct computation shows that the space of the quadratic relations of

in the variables

,
v
,

p^{\prime}wu+q^{\prime}uw+r^{\prime}vv=0

and

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

The Sklyanin algebra
A=Q^{p,q,r}
is PBW and therefore is Koszul if
r=0
, or if
p=q=0
, or if
p^{3}=q^{3}=r^{3}
. Moreover,
H_{A}(t)=(1-3t)^{-1}
if
p=q=r=0
,
H_{A}(t)=\frac{1+t}{1-2t}
if exactly two of
p
,
q
and
r
are
0

p^{3}=q^{3}=r^{3}\neq 0
and
H_{A}=(1-t)^{-3}

and

. Furthermore,

First, if
p=q=r=0
, then

is the free algebra and therefore

. If exactly two of

Lemma 3.6

The Sklyanin algebra

Isomorphic Sklyanin algebras

The question when two different Sklyanin algebras are isomorphic as graded algebras is touched in

4
]

The authors associate to each Sklyanin algebra some geometric data (an elliptic curve and its automorphism) and observe that Sklyanin algeras are isomorphic precisely, when the same happens to geometric data associated to them. This certainly may be useful on many occasions, however this characterization of isomorphic Sklyanin algebras is hard to use when the only data we have are just the triples of parameters (possible, of course, but rather inconvenient). In this section we find out explicitly in terms of just the values of parameters which Sklyanin algebras are isomorphic to each other.

One may wonder whether the answer depends on whether we allow all algebra isomorphisms instead of just graded (=linear in our case) ones. Well, it does not according to the following very general observation. It must be somewhere in the literature, however we could not come up with a reference. For this reason we include its proof. We banish the said proof to the next section devoted to remarks in order to keep the flow of arguments intact.

and
Q^{0,0,1}
are non-isomorphic since the only one-dimensional representation of
Q^{0,0,1}
is the augmentation map, while the map sending
x
to
1
and
y
and
z
to
0
extends to a one-dimensional representation of
Q^{1,0,0}
(
Q^{0,0,1}
has only one one-dimensional representation, while
Q^{1,0,0}
has more than one; as a matter of fact it has infinitely many of those if
{\mathbb{K}}
is infinite).

Finally, by Theorem
1.1
and Lemma

, every degenerate Sklyanin algebra has exponential growth, while every non-degenerate Sklyanin algebra has polynomial growth. Hence a degenerate Sklyanin algebra can not be
\cal A
-isomorphic to a non-degenerate one.
∎

Note that monomiality of degenerate Sklyanin algebras was first noticed by Smith
[
21
]
. Lemma

effectively takes degenerate algebras out of the picture, so we can concentrate on the non-degenerate ones. That is we have to deal with

for

(p,q,r)\in M_{0}=\{(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{3}:(pq,pr,qr)\neq(0,0,0)\ \ \text{and}\ \ (p_{3}-q^{3},p^{3}-r^{3},q^{3}-r^{3})\neq(0,0,0)\}.

We split
M_{0}
into two disjoint subsets
M_{0}=M_{1}\cup M_{2}
, where

M_{1}=\{(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{3}:r\neq 0,\ (p,q)\neq(0,0),\ (p+q)^{3}+r^{3}\neq 0\ \ \text{and}\ \ (p_{3}-q^{3},p^{3}-r^{3},q^{3}-r^{3})\neq(0,0,0)\}

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(p,q,r)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{3}
be such that
r\neq 0
,
(p_{3}-q^{3},p^{3}-r^{3},q^{3}-r^{3})\neq(0,0,0)
and
(p+q)^{3}+r^{3}=0
. Then
Q^{p,q,r}
is isomorphic to the quantum polynomial algebra
Q^{1,-\alpha,0}
given by the relations
xy=\alpha yx
,
zx=\alpha xz
and
yz=\alpha zy
, where
\alpha=\theta\frac{p-\theta^{2}q}{p-\theta q}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
Q^{p,q,r}=Q^{s,t,1}
, where
s=\frac{p}{r}
and
t=\frac{q}{r}
. Since
(p+q)^{3}+r^{3}=0
, we have
(-s-t)^{3}=1
. By Lemma
3.3
,
Q^{s,t,1}
is isomorphic to
Q^{s,t,-s-t}
. By Lemma
3.4
,
Q^{s,t,-s-t}
is isomorphic to
Q^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}
, where
p^{\prime}=(\theta^{2}-1)s+(\theta-1)t
,
q^{\prime}=(\theta-1)s+(\theta^{2}-1)t
and
r^{\prime}=0
. Note that the assumptions yield that
Q^{p,q,r}
is non-degenerate and therefore so is
Q^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}
. Hence
p^{\prime}q^{\prime}\neq 0
. Then
Q^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}=Q^{1,-\alpha,0}
with
\alpha=-\frac{(\theta-1)s+(\theta^{2}-1)t}{(\theta^{2}-1)s+(\theta-1)t}
. After easy simplifications, one gets
\alpha=\theta\frac{p-\theta^{2}q}{p-\theta q}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

For every
(p,q,r)\in M_{2}
, the only one-dimensional representation of
Q^{p,q,r}
is the augmentation map.

Proof.

Let
A=Q^{p,q,r}
with
(p,q,r)\in M_{1}
. We shall verify that
A
has no one-dimensional representations other than the augmentation map. Let
\varphi:A\to{\mathbb{K}}
be a representaion (=an algebra homomorphism) and let
a=\varphi(x)
,
b=\varphi(y)
and
c=\varphi(z)
. We have to prove that
a=b=c=0
. From the defining relations of
A
it follows that
(p+q)ab=-rc^{2}
,
(p+q)bc=-ra^{2}
and
(p+q)ac=-rb^{2}
. Multiplying these equalities, we get
a^{2}b^{2}c^{2}((p+q)^{3}+r^{3})=0
. Since
(p,q,r)\in M_{1}
,
(p+q)^{3}+r^{3}\neq 0
. Hence

. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
a=0
. From
(p+q)ab=-rc^{2}
and
(p+q)ac=-rb^{2}
we now have
rb=rc=0
. Since
(p,q,r)\in M_{1}
,
r\neq 0
and therefore
a=b=c=0
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For every
(p,q,r)\in M_{2}
,

If
(p,q,r)\in M_{2}
, from definition of

or

(p_{3}-q^{3},p^{3}-r^{3},q^{3}-r^{3})\neq(0,0,0)

If

\alpha=\beta
of

,

Next lemma clinches description of isomorphic Sklyanin algebras. Since every
(p,q,r)\in M_{1}
satisfies

can be turned into a unique triple

Since scaling the triple of parameters does not change the Sklyanin algebra, in order to describe which Sklyanins with

Lemma 5.6
.

If both
(a,b)
and
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})

and
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
are in the same orbit of the group action on

generated by two maps
(a,b)\mapsto(\theta a,\theta b)
and
(a,b)\mapsto\left(\frac{\theta a+\theta^{2}b+1}{a+b+1},\frac{\theta^{2}a+\theta b+1}{a+b+1}\right)
. This group is finite, consists of
24
elements
(
thus if
{\mathbb{K}}
is infinite, for generic
(a,b)\in M
, there are exactly
23
other elements of
M
giving rise to an isomorphic Sklyanin algebra
)
and is isomorphic to
SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{3})
.

The complete list of pairs
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in M
such that for a given
(a,b)\in M
,
Q^{a,b,1}
and
Q^{a^{\prime},b^{\prime},1}
are isomorphic is as follows
:

•

(\theta^{j}a,\theta^{j}b)
with
j\in\{0,1,2\};

•

(\theta^{j}b,\theta^{j}a)
with
j\in\{0,1,2\};

•

\left(\frac{\theta^{j}a+\theta^{k}b+\theta^{m}}{a+b+\theta^{n}},\frac{\theta^{k}a+\theta^{j}b+\theta^{m}}{a+b+\theta^{n}}\right)
with
n\in\{0,1,2\}
and
\{j,k,m\}=\{0,1,2\}.

The last condition means that
(j,k,m)
is a permutation of
(0,1,2)
. Each of the first two lines in the above list provide
3
pairs, while the last line yields
18
.

Proof.

Assume that
(a,b)
and
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
belong to
M
, then the Sklyanin algebras
A=Q^{a,b,1}=A(V,R)
and
B=Q^{a^{\prime},b^{\prime},1}=A(V,R^{\prime})
are isomorphic. By Theorem
1.1
,
{\rm dim}\,A_{3}={\rm dim}\,B_{3}=10
. Since
{\rm dim}\,V^{3}=27
, we have
{\rm dim}\,(VR+RV)={\rm dim}\,(VR^{\prime}+R^{\prime}V)=27-10=17
. Since
{\rm dim}\,VR={\rm dim}\,RV={\rm dim}\,VR^{\prime}={\rm dim}\,R^{\prime}V=9
, it follows that
{\rm dim}\,(RV\cap VR)={\rm dim}\,(R^{\prime}V\cap V^{\prime}R)=1
. On the other hand, obviously, the potentials
F=x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}+axyz^{\rcirclearrowleft}+bxzy^{\rcirclearrowleft}
and
F^{\prime}=x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}+a^{\prime}xyz^{\rcirclearrowleft}+b^{\prime}xzy^{\rcirclearrowleft}
for
A
and
B
respectively satisfy
F\in RV\cap VR
and
F^{\prime}\in R^{\prime}V\cap VR^{\prime}
. Since a linear substitution

facilitating the graded isomorphism of
A
and
B
must send
RV\cap VR
to
R^{\prime}V\cap VR^{\prime}
, it transforms
F
to
F^{\prime}
up to a scalar multiple. Hence
T
must transform the abelianization of
F

G=x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}+3(a+b)xyz\in{\mathbb{K}}[x,y,z]
(the image of
F
under the canonical map from
{\mathbb{K}}\langle x,y,z\rangle
to
{\mathbb{K}}[x,y,z]
) into the abelianization
G^{\prime}=x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}+3(a^{\prime}+b^{\prime})xyz\in{\mathbb{K}}[x,y,z]
of

up to a scalar multiple. Hence,
T
provides an isomorphism between the elliptic (projective) curves
C
given by
G=0
and
C^{\prime}
given by
G^{\prime}=0
.

Since
(a,b)
and
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
belong to
M
, we have
(a+b)^{3}+1\neq 0
and
(a^{\prime}+b^{\prime})^{3}+1\neq 0
and therefore the curves
C
and
C^{\prime}
are regular. Note that the each of the curves
C
and
C^{\prime}
have the same exactly collection of nine inflection points which are the nine lines

for

spanned by
(1,-1,0)
,
(1,-\theta,0)
,
(1,-\theta^{2},0)
,
(1,0,-1)
,
(1,0,-\theta)
,
(1,0,-\theta^{2})
,
(0,1,-1)
,
(0,1,-\theta)
and
(0,1,-\theta^{2})
respectively. Since
T
is an isomorphism between

and
C^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
,

must leave the union of
L_{j}
invariant. It is a routine exercise to verify that the subgroup
G
of
GL_{3}({\mathbb{K}})
leaving the union of

invariant is generated by

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\lambda&0&0\\
0&\lambda&0\\
0&0&\lambda\end{array}\right)\ \ (\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}),\ \ \left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
1&0&0\end{array}\right),\ \ \left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&\theta\end{array}\right)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\theta&\theta^{2}&1\\
\theta^{2}&\theta&1\\
1&1&1\end{array}\right).

(5.1)

One way to see it is to notice that, first of all, each linear map in the above display leaves the union of

invariant. Next, one easily checks that the group

generated by matrices in the above display acts transitively on the set of triples of

that span

. Thus

.

Now the linear transformations given by the first two matrices in the above display provide automorphisms of each

, while the linear transformation given by the third matrix in the above display facilitates an isomorphism between

and

for each

. Finally, a direct computation shows that the linear transformations given by the last matrix in the above display provides an isomorphism of

and

for every

and

being in the same orbit of the group action on

generated by two maps

A direct computation shows that this group consists of the maps

with

must generate

\sum\limits_{1\leqslant k,p\leqslant n}a^{(j)}_{k,p}u^{(k)}u^{(p)}=0\ \ \text{in $B$ for $1\leqslant j\leqslant s$.}

The zero component of the above equation reads

\sum\limits_{1\leqslant k,p\leqslant n}a^{(j)}_{k,p}u_{0}^{(k)}u_{0}^{(p)}=0\ \ \text{in ${\mathbb{K}}$ for $1\leqslant j\leqslant s$.}

The second degree component of (

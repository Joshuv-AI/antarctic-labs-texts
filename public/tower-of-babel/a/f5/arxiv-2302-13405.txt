Strategic (Timed) Computation Tree Logic
Jaime Arias, Wojciech Jamroga, Wojciech Penczek, Laure Petrucci, Teofil Sidoruk

Abstract
. We define extensions of 𝐂𝐓𝐋 \mathbf{CTL} and 𝐓𝐂𝐓𝐋 \mathbf{TCTL_{\mathrm{}}} with strategic operators, called Strategic 𝐂𝐓𝐋 \mathbf{CTL} ( 𝐒𝐂𝐓𝐋 \mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}} ) and Strategic 𝐓𝐂𝐓𝐋 \mathbf{TCTL_{\mathrm{}}} ( 𝐒𝐓𝐂𝐓𝐋 \mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}} ), respectively. For each of the above logics we give a synchronous and asynchronous semantics, i.e. 𝐒𝐓𝐂𝐓𝐋 \mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}} is interpreted over networks of extended Timed Automata (TA) that either make synchronous moves or synchronise via joint actions. We consider several semantics regarding information: imperfect (i) and perfect (I), and recall: imperfect (r) and perfect (R). We prove that 𝐒𝐂𝐓𝐋 \mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}} is more expressive than 𝐀𝐓𝐋 \mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}} for all semantics, and this holds for the timed versions as well. Moreover, the model checking problem for 𝐒𝐂𝐓𝐋 ir \mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}} is of the same complexity as for 𝐀𝐓𝐋 ir \mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}} , the model checking problem for 𝐒𝐓𝐂𝐓𝐋 ir \mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}} is of the same complexity as for 𝐓𝐂𝐓𝐋 \mathbf{TCTL_{\mathrm{}}} , while for 𝐒𝐓𝐂𝐓𝐋 iR \mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{iR}}}} it is undecidable as for 𝐀𝐓𝐋 iR \mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{iR}}}} . The above results suggest to use 𝐒𝐂𝐓𝐋 ir \mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}} and 𝐒𝐓𝐂𝐓𝐋 ir \mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}} in practical applications. Therefore, we use the tool IMITATOR to support model checking of 𝐒𝐓𝐂𝐓𝐋 ir \mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}} .



1.
Introduction

Alternating-time temporal logics
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}^{*}}
and
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}}

(
Alur
et al. 1997
;
Alur
et al. 2002
)

extend the temporal logic
\mathbf{CTL^{*}}
and
\mathbf{CTL}
, resp., with the notion of
strategic ability
.
These logics allow for expressing properties of agents (or groups of agents) referring
to what they can achieve.
Such properties can be useful for specification, verification, and reasoning about interaction
in multi-agent systems
(
Kacprzak and
Penczek 2004
;
Kacprzak and
Penczek 2005
;
Lomuscio
et al. 2015
;
Jamroga
et al. 2016
;
Huang and van der
Meyden 2014
)
.

In this paper we investigate timed extensions of strategy logics,
these already known as well as newly introduced ones.
One of our main aims is to identify the most expressive logics for which the model checking problem
is not only decidable, but also of complexity acceptable in practice.
We start with recalling the syntax of
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}}

(
Alur
et al. 1997
;
Alur
et al. 2002
)

and
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}

(
Laroussinie
et al. 2006
)
.
Then, we put forward definitions of two new logics:
Strategic CTL (
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
),
and its timed extension, Strategic Timed CTL (
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
).
For each (timed) strategy logic we consider two types of interpretations, over
models of synchronous (Time) Multi-Agent Systems MAS and asynchronous (Time) Multi-Agent Systems AMAS.
In addition, Time MAS and Time AMAS can be either discrete (D), or continuous (C).
We investigate the model checking problem for
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
and
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
for
all the semantics, and compare their complexity with other strategy logics.
Notably, we prove that
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
is more expressive than
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}}
for all semantics,
and this holds for the timed versions as well.
Moreover,
the model checking problem for
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
is of the same complexity as for
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
,
the model checking problem for
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
is of the same complexity as for
\mathbf{TCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
,
while for
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{iR}}}}
it is undecidable as for
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{iR}}}}
.
These results suggest to use
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
and
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
in practical applications.
Therefore, we demonstrate the feasibility of
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
model checking
on a small scalable example using IMITATOR.

Related Work.

\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}

(
Laroussinie
et al. 2006
)
is a discrete-time extension of
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}}

(
Alur
et al. 1997
;
Alur
et al. 2002
)
,
the subset of
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}^{*}}
where each strategic modality is immediately followed by a single temporal operator.
A hierarchy of semantic variants of
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
was established and studied in
(
Knapik et al. 2019
)
,
including counting strategies.
Game Logic (
\mathbf{GL}
)
(
Alur
et al. 2002
)
, similarly to
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
,
combines path quantifiers with the notion of strategic ability.

\mathbf{GL}
is a generalisation of
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}^{*}}
over perfect information,
where quantification is possible separately over paths within a strategy outcome.

\mathbf{CTL}
timed games
(
Faella
et al. 2014
)
are defined over timed automata with continuous time,
but with specifications given using
\mathbf{CTL}
and
\mathbf{LTL_{\mathrm{}}}
,
placing them somewhere between untimed
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{IR}}}^{U}}
and
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{IR}}}^{C}}
considered here.
They are shown to be
\mathbf{EXPTIME}
-complete,
demonstrating that model checking of
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{IR}}}}
over continuous time models retains the same complexity as over untimed ones (cf.
Table

1
).
Analogously to
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}^{*}}
, the logic
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}^{*}}
over continuous time semantics,
call it
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}^{*C}}
, would be a natural counterpart to the discrete-time
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}^{*}}
.
However, even without the strategic modality, model checking is
undecidable for continuous time extensions of
\mathbf{LTL_{\mathrm{}}}
(
\mathbf{MTL}
and
\mathbf{TPTL}
)
(
Bouyer 2009
)
.
This has motivated our choice of
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
,
which is applicable where discrete time is insufficient,
more expressive than
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
,
and yet its model checking is decidable for
\mathrm{r}
-strategies.

2.
Reasoning about Strategies and Time

In this section, we define the logical framework to reason about strategic abilities in timed synchronous multi-agent systems.
Our definitions are based on
(
Alur and Dill 1990
;
Lomuscio and Ryan 1997
;
Alur
et al. 2002
;
Schobbens 2004
;
Laroussinie
et al. 2006
;
Knapik et al. 2019
)
.

2.1.
Syntax of
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
and its Fragments

We begin by introducing the logical formulas of interest.
Assume a countable set
\mathit{PV}
of atomic propositions, and a finite set
\mathcal{A}
of agents.
The syntax of
Strategic Timed Computation Tree Logic
(
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
) resembles that of Game Logic
(
Alur
et al. 2002
)
,
and can be defined by the grammar:

\varphi::=\mathsf{{p}}\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\wedge\varphi\mid\langle\!\langle{A}\rangle\!\rangle\gamma
,

\gamma::=\varphi\mid\neg\gamma\mid\gamma\wedge\gamma\mid\mathsf{\forall}\mathtt{X}\,\gamma\mid\mathsf{\forall}\gamma\,\mathtt{U}\,_{I}\gamma\mid\mathsf{\exists}\gamma\,\mathtt{U}\,_{I}\gamma\mid\mathsf{\forall}\gamma\,\mathtt{R}\,_{I}\gamma\mid\mathsf{\exists}\gamma\,\mathtt{R}\,_{I}\gamma
,

where
\mathsf{{p}}\in\mathit{PV}
is an atomic proposition,
A\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is a subset of agents, and
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{0+}
is
an interval with bounds of the form
[n,n^{\prime}]
,
[n,n^{\prime})
,
(n,n^{\prime}]
,
(n,n^{\prime})
,
(n,\infty)
,
[n,\infty)
, for
n,n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
.

\langle\!\langle{A}\rangle\!\rangle
is the strategic operator expressing that the agents in
A
have a strategy to enforce the temporal property that follows after it.

\mathsf{\forall}
(“for all paths") and
\mathsf{\exists}
(“there exists a path") are the usual path quantifiers of
\mathbf{CTL}
.
The temporal operators
\mathtt{X}\,,\,\mathtt{U}\,,\,\mathtt{R}\,
stand for “next”, “strong until”, and “release,” respectively.
Boolean connectives and the remaining operators
\mathtt{F}\,
(“eventually”),
\mathtt{G}\,
(“always”) can be derived as usual.
Notice that we added the next step operator
\mathtt{X}\,
to the syntax of
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
(and
\mathbf{TCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
) in order to be able to define
other logics as syntactic fragments of
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
, as follows:

\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
::

untimed Strategic
\mathbf{CTL}
, obtained from
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
by restricting the time intervals
I
in temporal operators

\,\mathtt{U}\,_{I},\,\mathtt{R}\,_{I}
to
I=[0,\infty)
. So, they are removed from the syntax of
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
;

\mathbf{TCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
::

Timed
\mathbf{CTL}
, obtained from
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
by removing the strategic modality
\langle\!\langle{A}\rangle\!\rangle
from the syntax;

\mathbf{CTL}
::

“vanilla”
\mathbf{CTL}
, obtained from
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
by removing
\langle\!\langle{A}\rangle\!\rangle
;

\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
::

Timed Alternating-time Temporal Logic, the fragment of
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
where each instance of

\langle\!\langle{A}\rangle\!\rangle
is immediately followed by
\forall\mathtt{X}\,
,
\forall\,\mathtt{U}\,_{I}
, or
\forall\,\mathtt{R}\,_{I}
,
then
\forall
is removed from the syntax;

\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}}
::

“vanilla”
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}}
, obtained from
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
by restricting the time intervals to
I=[0,\infty)
,
thus removing them from the syntax;

We can now introduce the syntax and semantics of synchronous MAS
with continuous and discrete time, as well as untimed.

2.2.
Continuous Time Synchronous MAS

In
synchronous continuous-time multi-agent systems
, all agents have an associated
set of clocks. All clocks evolve at the same rate (across agents), thus allowing for
delays and instantaneous actions.
We first recall the formal definitions for key notions of
timed systems

(
Alur and Dill 1990
)
.
Then, we combine them with the concept of
interpreted systems

(
Lomuscio and Ryan 1997
)

which has been successful in modelling synchronous MAS.

Clocks
are non-negative, real-valued variables;
we denote a finite set of clocks by
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n_{\mathcal{X}}}\}

(with a fixed ordering assumed for simplicity).
A
clock valuation
on
\mathcal{X}
is a
n_{\mathcal{X}}
-tuple
v
.
We denote:

•

by
v(x_{i})
or
v(i)
, the value of clock
x_{i}
in
v
;

•

by
v+\delta
, where
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{0+}
,
v^{\prime}
s.t.
v^{\prime}(x)=v(x)+\delta
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
;

•

by v[X := 0], where
X\subseteq\mathcal{X}
,
v^{\prime}
s.t.
v^{\prime}(x)=0
for all
x\in X
, and
v^{\prime}(x)=v(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus X
.

The
clock constraints
over
\mathcal{X}
are defined by the following grammar:

\mathfrak{cc}:=true\mid x_{i}\sim c\mid x_{i}-x_{j}\sim c\mid\mathfrak{cc}\land\mathfrak{cc}
,
where
x_{i},x_{j}\in\mathcal{X}
,
c\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\sim\in\{\leq,<,=,>,\geq\}
.
The set
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{X}}
collects all constraints over
\mathcal{X}
.
For
\mathfrak{cc}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{X}}
, the satisfaction relation
\models
is inductively defined as:

v\models true
,

v\models(x_{i}\sim c)
iff
v(x_{i})\sim c
,

v\models(x_{i}-x_{j}\sim c)
iff
v(x_{i})-v(x_{j})\sim c
, and

v\models(\mathfrak{cc}\land\mathfrak{cc}^{\prime})
iff
v\models\mathfrak{cc}
and
v\models\mathfrak{cc}^{\prime}
.

The set of all valuations satisfying
\mathfrak{cc}
is denoted by
\llbracket\mathfrak{cc}\rrbracket
.

Definition 2.1 (CMAS).

A
continuous-time multi-agent system (CMAS)
consists of
n
agents

\mathcal{A}=\{{1,\dots,n}\}
, each associated with a 9-tuple

AG_{i}=\left(L_{i},\iota_{i},Act_{i},P_{i},\mathcal{X}_{i},\mathcal{I}_{i},T_{i},\mathit{PV}_{i},V_{i}\right)

including:

•

a finite non-empty set of
local states

L_{i}=\{l_{i}^{1},l_{i}^{2},\dots,l_{i}^{n_{i}}\}
;

•

an
initial local state

\iota_{i}\in L_{i}
;

•

a finite non-empty set of
local actions

Act_{i}\!=\!\{a_{i}^{1},a_{i}^{2},\ldots,a_{i}^{m_{i}}\}
;

•

a
local protocol

P_{i}:L_{i}\to 2^{Act_{i}}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
;

•

a set of
clocks

\mathcal{X}_{i}
;

•

an
invariant

\mathcal{I}_{i}\colon L_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{X}_{i}}

specifying a condition for the CMAS to stay in a given local state;

•

a (partial)
local transition function

T_{i}:L_{i}\times\mathit{JAct}\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{X}_{i}}\times 2^{\mathcal{X}_{i}}\rightharpoonup L_{i}
, where

\mathit{JAct}\triangleq\prod_{i\in\mathcal{A}}Act_{i}
is the set of
joint (global) actions
of all agents,
is s.t.
T_{i}(l_{i},\alpha,\mathfrak{cc},X)=l^{\prime}_{i}
for some
l^{\prime}_{i}\in L_{i}
iff
a^{i}\in P_{i}(l_{i})
,

\mathfrak{cc}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{X}_{i}}
, and
X\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{i}
;

•

a finite non-empty set of
local propositions

\mathit{PV}_{i}=\{\mathsf{{p_{i}^{1}}},\ldots,\mathsf{{p_{i}^{r_{i}}}}\}
;

•

a
local valuation function

V_{i}:L_{i}\rightarrow 2^{\mathit{PV}_{i}}
.

For a local transition
t:=l\xrightarrow{\alpha,\mathfrak{cc},X}l^{\prime}
in a CMAS,

l
and
l^{\prime}
are the
source
and
target
states,

\alpha
is the executed
action
,
clock condition
\mathfrak{cc}
is called a
guard
,
and
X
is the set of clocks to be
reset
.

Definition 2.2 (Model of CMAS).

The
model
of CMAS is a 7-tuple
\mathit{M}=(\mathcal{A},S,\iota,\mathcal{X},\mathcal{I},T,V)
, where:

•

\mathcal{A}=\{{1,\dots,n}\}
is the set of
agents
;

•

S=\prod_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}
is the set of
global states
;

•

\iota=(\iota_{1},\dots,\iota_{n})\in S
is the
initial global state
;

•

\mathcal{X}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{X}_{i}
is the set of
clocks
;

•

\mathcal{I}(s)=\bigwedge_{i\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{I}_{i}(s^{i})
is the

global invariant
,
where
s^{i}
is the
i
-th local state of
s
;

•

T:S\times\mathit{JAct}\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{X}}\times 2^{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow S
s.t.
T(s,\alpha,\bigwedge_{i}\mathfrak{cc}_{i},\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i})=s^{\prime}
iff

T_{i}(s^{i},\alpha,\mathfrak{cc}_{i},X_{i})=s^{\prime i}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
;

•

a
valuation function

V\colon S\to 2^{\mathit{PV}}
, where
\mathit{PV}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathit{PV}_{i}
.

The continuous (dense) semantics of time defines

concrete states
as tuples of global states and non-negative real clock valuations.

Definition 2.3 (CTS).

The
concrete model
of a CMAS model
\mathit{M}=(\mathcal{A},S,\iota,\mathcal{X},\mathcal{I},T,V)

is given by its
Continuous Transition System
(CTS)
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{CS},q_{\iota},\to_{c},V_{c})
, where:

•

\mathcal{A}=\{{1,\dots,n}\}
is the set of
agents
;

•

\mathcal{CS}=S\times\mathbb{R}_{0+}^{n_{\mathcal{X}}}
is the set of
concrete states
;

•

q_{\iota}=(\iota,v)\in\mathcal{CS}
, such that
v(x_{i})=0
for each
x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
, is the
concrete initial state
;

•

\to_{c}\subseteq\mathcal{CS}\times(\mathit{JAct}\cup\mathbb{R}_{0+})\times\mathcal{CS}
is the
transition relation
,
defined by time- and action successors as follows:

(s,v)\xrightarrow{\delta}_{c}(s,v+\delta)

for
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{0+}
and
v,v+\delta\in\llbracket\mathcal{I}(s)\rrbracket
,

(s,v)\xrightarrow{\alpha}_{c}(s^{\prime},v^{\prime})

iff there are
\alpha\in\mathit{JAct}
,
\mathfrak{cc}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{X}}
,
X\subseteq\mathcal{X}
s.t.:

s\xrightarrow{\alpha,\mathfrak{cc},X}s^{\prime}\in T
,

v\in\llbracket\mathfrak{cc}\rrbracket
,

v\in\llbracket\mathcal{I}(s)\rrbracket
,

v^{\prime}=v[X:=0]
,

v^{\prime}~\in~\llbracket\mathcal{I}(s^{\prime})\rrbracket
,

•

V_{c}(s,v)=V(s)
is the
valuation function
.

Intuitively, there are two types of transitions:
delay transitions
\xrightarrow{\delta}_{c}
,
which increase the clock valuation(s) by a given
\delta
but do not change the global state,
and action transitions
\xrightarrow{\alpha}_{c}
which correspond to executing
an enabled action in the CMAS and move the latter to a successor state,
possibly resetting some clocks.
Note that if the set of clocks is empty, the concrete model contains only action transitions,
and thus it is identical with the model itself.

2.3.
Discrete Time Synchronous MAS

Synchronous models with discrete time were considered for reasoning
in
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
in
(
Laroussinie
et al. 2006
;
Knapik et al. 2019
)
using Tight Durational Concurrent Game Structures (TDCGS),
which is a flat model, as opposed to a network of synchronising models, used in this paper.
This gives an equivalent model whose definition is consistent with MAS.
Indeed,
synchronous discrete time
MAS extend MAS with a constant duration associated with each individual transition.

Definition 2.4 (DMAS).

A
discrete-time multi-agent system (DMAS)
consists of
n
agents

\mathcal{A}=\{{1,\dots,n}\}
, where each agent
i\in\mathcal{A}
is associated with a 7-tuple

AG_{i}=\left(L_{i},\iota_{i},Act_{i},P_{i},T_{i},\mathit{PV}_{i},V_{i}\right)
including:

•

a finite non-empty set of
local states

L_{i}=\{l_{i}^{1},l_{i}^{2},\dots,l_{i}^{n_{i}}\}
;

•

an
initial local state

\iota_{i}\in L_{i}
;

•

a finite non-empty set of
local actions

Act_{i}\!=\!\{a_{i}^{1},a_{i}^{2},\ldots,a_{i}^{m_{i}}\}
;

•

a
local protocol

P_{i}:L_{i}\to 2^{Act_{i}}\setminus\{\emptyset\}

selecting the actions available at each local state;

•

a (partial)
local transition function

T_{i}:L_{i}\times\mathit{JAct}\rightarrow L_{i}\times\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that
T_{i}(l_{i},\alpha)
is defined iff

\alpha^{i}\in P_{i}(l_{i})
, where

\alpha^{i}
is the action of agent
i\in\mathcal{A}
in the
joint action

\alpha\in\mathit{JAct}
;

T_{i}(l_{i},\alpha)=(l^{\prime}_{i},\delta_{i})
where
\delta_{i}
is the
duration
of the transition;

•

a finite non-empty set of
local propositions

\mathit{PV}_{i}=\{\mathsf{{p_{i}^{1}}},\ldots,\mathsf{{p_{i}^{r_{i}}}}\}
;

•

a
local valuation function

V_{i}:L_{i}\rightarrow 2^{\mathit{PV}_{i}}
.

This definition is similar to
definition

2.7
with a duration associated with each
transition.
As for CMAS, the model of a DMAS is defined, that describes its behaviour.

Definition 2.5 (Model of DMAS).

The
model
of DMAS is a 5-tuple
\mathit{M}=(\mathcal{A},S,\iota,T,V)
, where:

•

\mathcal{A}=\{{1,\dots,n}\}
is the set of
agents
;

•

S=\prod_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}
is the set of
global states
;

•

\iota=(\iota_{1},\dots,\iota_{n})\in S
is the
initial global state
;

•

T:S\times\mathit{JAct}\rightarrow S\times\mathbb{N}_{+}
is the partial
global
transition function
, such that
T(s,\alpha)=(s^{\prime},\delta)
iff
T_{i}(s^{i},\alpha)=(s^{\prime i},\delta)
for all
i\in\mathcal{A}
,
where
s^{i}
is the
i
-th local state of
s
;

•

V:S\rightarrow 2^{\mathit{PV}}
is the
valuation function
such that

V((l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}))=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}(l_{i})
, where
\mathit{PV}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathit{PV}_{i}
.

This definition enforces all synchronising actions to have the same
duration in their respective local components.
Another possibility could be to have individual durations
\delta_{i}
for each component,
and the longest duration
\max_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{i}
for the synchronised transition.
This would mimic actions of the different components
taking place together, with the longest slowing down the whole execution.

Definition 2.6 (DTS).

The concrete model of a DMAS is given by its
Duration Transition System

(DTS)
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{TS},q_{\iota},\mathcal{E},V_{d})
, where:

•

\mathcal{A}=\{{1,\dots,n}\}
is the set of
agents
;

•

\mathcal{TS}=S\times\mathbb{N}
is a set of
timed states
;

•

q_{\iota}=(\iota,0)\in\mathcal{TS}
is the
initial timed state
;

•

\mathcal{E}\colon\mathcal{TS}\times\mathit{JAct}\to\mathcal{TS}
is a (partial)

transition function
such that

\mathcal{E}((s,d),\alpha)=(s^{\prime},d+\delta)\text{ iff }T(s,\alpha)=(s^{\prime},\delta)
,
for
s,s^{\prime}\in S
,
\alpha\in\mathit{JAct}
,

d\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\delta\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
;

•

V_{d}(s,d)=V(s)
is the
valuation function
.

It is straightforward to see that these compositional definitions correspond to those of
the flat structures (TDCGS and DTS) in
(
Knapik et al. 2019
)
.

2.4.
Untimed MAS

Untimed MAS can be defined as Timed MAS with no clocks, see below.
Note that the definition is essentially equivalent to the concept of
an

in
(

)
.

Definition 2.7 (MAS).

An
untimed multi-agent system
, simply MAS, is a CMAS with every
\mathcal{X}_{i}=\emptyset
.
The model and concrete model of MAS are equal and defined as in Definition

3.
Semantics of Logics

We start with defining strategies and their outcomes.

3.1.
Strategies

The taxonomy proposed by Schobbens
(
Schobbens 2004
)

defines four strategy types based on agents’
state information
:
perfect (
\mathrm{I}
) vs. imperfect (
\mathrm{i}
),
and their
recall of state history
:
perfect (
\mathrm{R}
) vs. imperfect (
\mathrm{r}
).

Intuitively, a strategy can be seen as a conditional plan that dictates the choice of an agent in each possible situation.
In
perfect information
strategies, agents have complete knowledge about global states of the model and thus can make
different choices in each one.
Under
imperfect information
,
decisions can only be made based on local states.

Perfect recall
assumes that agents have access to a full history of previously visited states,
whereas under
imperfect recall
only the current state is explicitly known.
Formally:

•

A
memoryless imperfect information
(\mathrm{ir})
strategy
for
i\in\mathcal{A}
is a function

\sigma_{i}\colon L_{i}\to Act_{i}
such that
\sigma_{i}(l)\in P_{i}(l)

for each
l\in L_{i}
.

•

A
memoryless perfect information
(\mathrm{Ir})
strategy
for
i\in\mathcal{A}
is a function

\sigma_{i}\colon S\to Act_{i}
such that
\sigma_{i}(s)\in P_{i}(s^{i})

for each
s\in S
.

•

A
perfect recall, imperfect information
(\mathrm{iR})
strategy
for
i\in\mathcal{A}
is a function

\sigma_{i}\colon L_{i}^{+}\to Act_{i}
s.t.
\sigma_{i}(\mathit{h})\in P_{i}(last(\mathit{h}))

for each
l\in L_{i}
.

•

A
perfect recall, perfect information
(\mathrm{IR})
strategy
for
i\in\mathcal{A}
is a function

\sigma_{i}\colon S^{+}\to Act_{i}
s.t.
\sigma_{i}(\mathit{H})\in P_{i}(last(\mathit{H})^{i})

for each
s\in S
.

By
\mathit{H}\in S^{+}
(resp.
\mathit{h}\in L_{i}^{+}
),
we denote a history of global (resp.
i
’s local) states,
and
last(H)
(resp.
last(h)
) refers to its last state.
The notion of a strategy can be generalized to an agent coalition
A\subseteq\mathcal{A}
,
whose
joint strategy

\sigma_{A}
is a tuple of strategies, one for each
i\in A
.

We now formally define executions in concrete models of MAS.

Let

An execution of a DTS

from

\pi=q_{0},\delta_{0},\alpha_{0},

q_{1},\delta_{1},\alpha_{1},\dots

\alpha_{k}\in\mathit{JAct}

\alpha_{k}\in\mathit{JAct}

for each

Note that if the set of clocks is empty in CMAS, then the executions of CTS contain only action transitions,
as in an untimed MAS.

The outcome of a strategy
\sigma_{A}
represents executions where the agents in
A
adopt
\sigma_{A}
,
i.e. it is the set of all paths in the model that may occur when the coalition strictly follows the strategy,
while opponents freely choose from actions permitted by their protocols.

Definition 3.3 (
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
Semantics).

Let
\mathit{M}=(S,\iota,T,V)
be a DMAS model,

be its DTS,

s=s_{0}\in S

•

\mathit{M},s\not\models\varphi

\mathit{M},s\models\varphi

\mathit{M},s\models\psi

•

iff there exists a joint
Y
-strategy
\sigma_{A}

\pi\in\mathit{out}_{\mathit{M}}^{Y}(s,\sigma_{A})

we have
\mathit{M},s_{1}\models\varphi
,

•

\mathit{M},s\models\langle\!\langle{A}\rangle\!\rangle\varphi U_{I}\psi

Y
-strategy
\sigma_{A}

(

).
Then, in

,

,
we provide new results for
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}}

.
It is important to note that complexity results are given wrt. the

as defined in

4.1.
Model Checking
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}}

The standard fixpoint algorithm for model checking
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}}
under perfect information was presented in the original
paper by Alur, Henzinger and Kupferman
(
Alur
et al. 2002
)
.
In a nutshell, to verify a formula
\langle\!\langle{A}\rangle\!\rangle\varphi
, it starts with a candidate set of states
(chosen appropriately depending on
\varphi
) and then iterates backwards
over the abilities of coalition
A
at each step
(
Jamroga 2015
)
.
Model checking of
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}}
is
\mathbf{PTIME}
-complete
(
Alur
et al. 2002
)
,
for both memoryless and perfect recall strategies,
since the satisfaction semantics for
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{Ir}}}}
and
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{IR}}}}
coincide
(
Jamroga 2015
)
.

On the other hand, the fixpoint-based approach cannot be adapted to imperfect information

(
Ågotnes et al. 2015
;
Bulling
et al. 2015
)
, making model checking significantly more complex
in this setting:

\mathbf{\Delta^{P}_{2}}
for
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{ir}}}
and NP-hard for simple instances
of
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{ir}}}

(
Jamroga 2015
)
,
and undecidable for
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{iR}}}

(
Dima and Tiplea 2011
)
.

4.2.
Model Checking
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}

Algorithms and complexity results are given in
(
Laroussinie
et al. 2006
)
for model checking
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
over TDCGS,
which are analogous to DMAS.
The strategies considered are defined on histories of concrete states, i.e. of type
\mathrm{IRT}
,
where
T
refers to the timed strategy.
Furthermore, analogously to
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}^{*}}
, the semantics of
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{IrT}}}}
and
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{IRT}}}}
coincide
(
Knapik et al. 2019
)
.
Model checking
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{IrT}}}}
and
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{IRT}}}}
is
\mathbf{EXPTIME}
-complete in the general case
(
Laroussinie
et al. 2006
, Theorem 13)
,
and
\mathbf{PTIME}
-complete for the subset that excludes equality in time constraints
(
Laroussinie
et al. 2006
, Theorem 14)
.

It is important to note, though, that these results are given wrt. the formula size;
in particular, the exponential blowup in the general case is attributed
solely to the binary encoding of constraints in
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
formulas,
while the algorithm is actually in
\mathbf{PTIME}
wrt. the model size
(
Laroussinie
et al. 2006
, Theorem 12)
.
Hence, we put the latter in
Table

1
,
as it cannot be otherwise compared with our results for
\mathbf{S(T)CTL}
.

We are not aware of any works investigating
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
under imperfect information thus far.
However, for
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
we can at least establish upper and lower bounds of
\mathbf{PSPACE}
(since
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}\subset\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
)
and
\mathbf{\Delta^{P}_{2}}
(since
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}}\supset\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
), respectively.
Furthermore,
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{iR}}}}
model checking is undecidable since it is already the case for
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{iR}}}}
.

4.3.
Model Checking
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
with r-Strategies

Under the memoryless semantics of strategic ability,
the complexity of model checking
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
can be established analogously to the way it was done for
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{}}}

(
Jamroga 2015
)
.
We begin with
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{ir}}}
.

•

Guess a strategy
\sigma_{A}
.

•

Prune
\mathit{M}
by removing transitions not consistent with
\sigma_{A}
.

•

If
\langle\!\langle{A}\rangle\!\rangle\gamma

\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}

run
\mathbf{TCTL_{\mathrm{}}}

\gamma
, else run
\mathbf{CTL}

\gamma
.

Algorithm 1

\textsc{mcheck{$\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}$}}_{\mathrm{ir,Ir}}({\mathit{M},s,\langle\!\langle{A}\rangle\!\rangle\gamma})

Theorem 4.1.

Model checking
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
is
\mathbf{\Delta^{P}_{2}}
-complete.

Proof.

Let
\varphi=\langle\!\langle{A}\rangle\!\rangle\gamma
be an
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
formula without nested strategic modalities.
Consider the procedure in
Algorithm

1
.
It runs in
\mathbf{NP}
for input
\varphi
,
since
\sigma_{A}
can be guessed in non-deterministic polynomial time,
while pruning transitions and model checking of
\mathbf{CTL}
formula
\gamma

requires deterministic polynomial time.
Note that for an arbitrary
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
formula,
nested strategic modalities can be replaced with fresh propositions
by calling
Algorithm

1
recursively, bottom up.
This requires polynomially many calls (wrt. the formula size)
to an
\mathbf{NP}
oracle executing
Algorithm

1
,
hence the upper bound of
\mathbf{P}^{\mathbf{NP}}=\mathbf{\Delta^{P}_{2}}
.

The lower bound follows from the fact that
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
subsumes
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
whose model checking complexity
is
\mathbf{\Delta^{P}_{2}}

(
Jamroga 2015
, Th. 34)
.
∎

For
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{Ir}}}
, i.e., strategies with perfect information, a more involved construction is required to establish the lower bound.

Theorem 4.2.

Model checking
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{Ir}}}}
is
\mathbf{\Delta^{P}_{2}}
-complete.

Proof.

The upper bound follows exactly as for
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}
(Th.

The lower bound is obtained by a reduction from the model checking problem for
\mathbf{ATL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{ir}}}}

which is

Let

The reduction proceeds via the following steps:

(1)

Reduction of model checking for

\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{}}}

(2)

Reduction of model checking for
\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{Ir}}}K}

\mathbf{SCTL_{\mathrm{\mathrm{Ir}}}}

Model reconstruction
.
First, we reconstruct the model

by cloning its states so that they record the latest

Jamroga 2004

That is, for each state

we create a new state
(q,a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

We can see in Section

We start by recalling the formal definitions of expressive and distinguishing power.

Definition 5.1 (Expressive power and distinguishing power
(
Wang and Dechesne 2009
)
).

Consider two logical systems
{L}_{1}
and
{L}_{2}
, with their semantics defined
over the same class of models
\mathcal{M}
.

{L}_{1}
is
at least as expressive as
{L}_{2}
(written
{L}_{2}\preceq_{e}{L}_{1}
) if,
for every formula
\varphi_{2}
of
{L}_{2}

\varphi_{1}
of
{L}_{1}

such that
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are satisfied in the same models from
\mathcal{M}
.

Moreover,
{L}_{1}
is
at least as distinguishing as
{L}_{2}
(
{L}_{2}\preceq_{d}{L}_{1}
)
if every pair of models

that can be
distinguished by a formula of
{L}_{2}
can also be distinguished by some formula of
{L}_{1}
.

It is easy to see that
{L}_{2}\preceq_{e}{L}_{1}
implies
{L}_{2}\preceq_{d}{L}_{1}
.
By transposition, we also have that
{L}_{2}\not\preceq_{d}{L}_{1}
implies
{L}_{2}\not\preceq_{e}{L}_{1}
.
The following is straightforward.

Follows as

is a syntactic restriction of
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
.

6.
The Asynchronous Case

This section considers the case of asynchronous multi-agent systems (AMAS),
providing the syntax and semantics of continuous time, discrete time,
and untimed AMAS.

6.1.
Asynchronous MAS

Asynchronous Multi-Agent Systems
(AMAS
(
Jamroga et al. 2018
)
) are
a modern semantic model for the study of agents’ strategies in asynchronous systems.
Technically, AMAS are similar to networks of automata that synchronise on
shared actions, and interleave local transitions to execute asynchronously

(
Fagin
et al. 1995
;
Lomuscio
et al. 2010a
;
Jamroga et al. 2018
)
.
However, to deal with agents coalitions, automata semantics (e.g. for Timed Automata) must
resort to algorithms and additional attributes.
In contrast, by linking protocols to agents, AMAS are a natural compositional formalism
to analyse multi-agent systems.

However, when agents share an action, the time the action takes is not enforced to be the
same for all participants.
Instead, the duration of the global action is the maximum of the participating agents’ durations.
Thus, the slowest agent slows down its partners.

Definition 6.6 (Model of DAMAS).

The
model
of a DAMAS is defined as the model of a DMAS except for the component:

•

is the partial

These changes to local and global transitions are incorporated in the concrete DAMAS model,
otherwise identical to that of a DMAS.

for

6.5.
Model Checking in AMAS

The semantics of
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
(
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
) is the same as in the synchronous case
except for each
\alpha
to be replaced by
a
in the paths.
In principle, the model checking procedures and complexity results for
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
and
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
and their untimed variants given in
Section

4
also apply to asynchronous models.
Note, however, that complexity is specified wrt. the model size,
which in AMAS is significantly larger due to asynchronous interleaving of agents’ actions.
On the other hand, the associated blow-up of state- and transition-space can be alleviated
via techniques such as partial order reductions
(
Jamroga et al. 2020
)
.

8.
Conclusions and Future Work

This paper shows that
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
, being a syntactic extension of
\mathbf{TATL_{\mathrm{}}}
,
but interpreted over timed models with continuous semantics, in both synchronous and asynchronous settings,
is of theoretical and practical interest in model checking with ir- and Ir-strategies.
Our plans for future research include:
investigating also counting and timed strategies,
a finer tuning of a model checking practical approach to easily
capture all
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
properties, and
extending
\mathbf{STCTL_{\mathrm{}}}
to
\mathbf{STCTL^{*}}
.
Moreover, since we have observed that synthesis of all strategies is too time consuming,
but feasible even with the existing tool, we plan to implement a smarter, dedicated algorithm.

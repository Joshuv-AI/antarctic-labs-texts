Divisibility of the Sums of the Power of Consecutive Integers
Tian-Xiao He, Peter J.-S. Shiue

Abstract
We study the divisibility of the sums of the odd power of consecutive integers, S ⁡ ( m , k ) = 1 m ​ k + 2 m ​ k + ⋯ + k m ​ k S(m,k)=1^{mk}+2^{mk}+\cdots+k^{mk} and 1 k + 2 k + ⋯ + n k 1^{k}+2^{k}+\cdots+n^{k} for odd integers m m and k k , by using the Girard-Waring identity. Key Words and Phrases: Divisibility, sum of powers of consecutive integers, Girard-Waring identity, recursive sequence, arithmetic series, Faulhaber’s theorem.

It is well known that the sum of cubes of three consecutive integers is always divisible by

consecutive integers is divisible for

the situation is quite different. In Ho, Mellblom, and Frodyma
[
14

(mk)^{th}

(8)

of any

the above sum
\pmod{k^{2}}

k
consecutive terms beginning with the integer

S(m,k):=1^{mk}+2^{mk}+\cdots+k^{mk}.

(9)

Proposition 2.1

Let
x,y\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, and let
p\geq 3
be a prime number. If
x+y=p
, then
x^{p}+y^{p}\equiv 0\,(mod\,p^{2})
. Furthermore, for any odd positive integer
m
, there holds

x^{pm}+y^{pm}\equiv 0\,(mod\,p^{2}).

(10)

Proof.

Substituting
x+y=z=p
into Proposition
1.1

p^{2}xy|(x^{p}+y^{p}-p^{p})

which implies
x^{p}+y^{p}\equiv 0\,(mod\,p^{2})

x^{p}\equiv-y^{p}\,(mod\,p^{2})

m

(x^{p})^{m}\equiv(-y^{p})^{m}\,(mod\,p^{2}).

Hence, we obtain (
10

Proposition 2.6

Let

Proof.

4
Faulhaber’s approach for the divisibility of sums of powers of integers

Faulhaber’s formula, named after Johann Faulhaber, expresses the sum of the
k
-th powers of the first
n
positive integers
S_{k}(n)
as a
(k+1)
th degree polynomial function of
n
, the coefficients involving Bernoulli numbers
B_{j}
, in the form

S_{k}(n)=\sum^{n}_{\ell=1}\ell^{k}=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum^{k}_{\ell=0}\binom{k+1}{\ell}B_{\ell}n^{k+1-\ell},

(30)

where we use the Bernoulli number of the second kind
B_{1}=1/2
. If we use the Bernoulli number of the first kind
B_{1}=-1/2
and noting all Bernoulli numbers with odd index
k\geq 3
are zero, then formula (
30
) becomes (
1
), i.e.,

S_{k}(n)=\sum^{n}_{\ell=1}\ell^{k}=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum^{k}_{\ell=0}(-1)^{\ell}B_{\ell}\binom{k+1}{\ell}n^{k+1-\ell},

(31)

where
B_{1}=-1/2
.

We first give a corollary of Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
.

Corollary 4.1

Let
S_{k}(n)
be the sum defined by (
19
) with odd integers
n
and
k\geq 3
. Then if either (i)
k\equiv 0\,(mod\,n)
or (ii)
n=p^{\alpha}
, a positive integer power of a prime
p
, and
p-1\not|(k-1)
implies

\frac{1}{k+1}\sum^{k}_{\ell=0}\binom{k+1}{\ell}B_{\ell}n^{k-\ell-1}\in{{\mathbb{N}}},

(32)

where
B_{1}=1/2
, and

\frac{1}{k+1}\sum^{k}_{\ell=0}(-1)^{\ell}B_{\ell}\binom{k+1}{\ell}n^{k-\ell-1}\in{{\mathbb{N}}},

(33)

where
B_{1}=-1/2
.

Proof.
In case (i), by using Proposition
3.1
we know
\left.n^{2}\right|S_{k}(n)
. Thus, from Faulhaber’s formulas (
30
) and (
31
), we obtain (
32
) and (
33
).

In case (ii), by using Theorem
3.3
we know
\left.(p^{\alpha})^{2}\right|S_{k}(p^{\alpha})
. Thus, from
Faulhaber’s formulas (
30
) and (
31
), we get (
32
) and (
33
).

We now consider the divisibility of the sums of powers of arithmetic sequence
\{a+(i-1)d\}_{1\leq i\leq k}
, where
(d,k)=1
, denoted by
S(a,d;k)
and defined by

S(a,d;k):=\sum^{k}_{i=1}(a+(i-1)d)^{k}=a^{k}+(a+d)^{k}+\cdots+(a+(k-1)d)^{k}.

(34)

Theorem 4.2

Let
a,d,
and
k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, where
k
is an odd number with
(d,k)=1
, and let
S(a,d;k)
be the series defined by (
34
). Then

S(a,d;k)\equiv 0\,(mod\,k^{2})
.

Proof.

For
i=1,2,\ldots,k
we have

(a+(i-1)d)\equiv j_{i}\,(mod\,k),

(35)

where
j_{i}\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
. Since
(d,k)=1
,
j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,
and
j_{k}
are distinct, otherwise
j_{i}=j_{m}
for some
i>m
implies

(a+(i-1)d)-(a+(m-1)d)=(i-m)d\equiv 0\,(mod\,k),

From
(i-m)d\equiv 0\,(mod\,k)
, we have
k|d(i-m)
. Under the condition
(d,k)=1
, we infer that
k|(i-m)
, which is contrary to the fact that
k\nmid(i-m)
for any
1\leq m<i\leq k
. Therefore,
j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,
and
j_{k}
are distinct.

From (
35
), for some
\ell
we have

\displaystyle(a+(i-1)d)^{k}-j_{i}^{k}=(j_{i}+\ell k)^{k}-j_{i}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j_{i}^{k-1}\binom{k}{1}(\ell k)+j_{i}^{k-2}\binom{k}{2}(\ell k)^{2}+\cdots+\binom{k}{k}(\ell k)^{k}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 0\,(mod\,k^{2}).

Thus

\sum^{k}_{i=1}(a+(i-1)d)^{k}\equiv\sum^{k}_{i=1}j_{i}^{k}=\sum^{k}_{j=1}j^{k}\equiv 0\,(mod\,k^{2}),

completing the proof of the theorem.

Remark 4.3

If
a=d=1
, then
S(1,1;k)=S(1,k)
and the result of Theorem
4.2
reduces to Proposition
2.7
.

Because of the relation
(a+bi)^{m}=b^{m}(a/b+i)^{m}
, there is no loss of generality to consider the sum of the powers of
x+i
, namely,
\sum^{n}_{i=1}(x+i)^{m}
. If
x
is a positive integer, then the last sum can be written as

\sum^{n}_{i=1}(x+i)^{m}=\sum^{n+x}_{i=1}i^{m}-\sum^{x}_{i=1}i^{m}.

By Faulhaber’s theorem, the two sums on the right-hand side are polynomials in
(n+x)(n+x+1)
and
x(x+1)
, respectively. Using the relation

(n+x)(n+x+1)=n(n+2x+1)+x(x+1),

we see that

[(n+x)(n+x+1)]^{i}-[x(x+1)]^{i}=\sum^{i}_{k=1}\binom{i}{k}[n(n+2x+1)]^{k}[x(x+1)]^{i-k},

which is a polynomial in
n(n+2x+1)
. Hence, we have the following result.

Proposition 4.4

Let
p\geq 3
be a prime number, and let
n
be a positive integer with
p
as a factor. If
x
is a positive integer satisfying
p|(2x+1)
, then

\sum^{n}_{i=1}(x+i)^{m}\equiv 0\,(mod\,p^{2})

for all
m=0,1,2,\ldots.

Proof.

It is sufficient to notice

p^{2}\left|\sum^{i}_{k=1}\binom{i}{k}[n(n+2x+1)]^{k}[x(x+1)]^{i-k}\right.

5
Power sum and Stirling numbers of the second kind

The Stirling numbers of the second kind, denoted by
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}
, count the number of ways to partition a set of
n
labelled objects into
k
nonempty unlabelled subsets. The Stirling numbers of the second kind may also be characterized as the coefficients of the expansion of powers of an indeterminate
x
in terms of the falling factorials
(x)_{n}=x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n+1).
In particular,
(x)_{0}=1
because it is an empty product. Hence,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}(x)_{k}=x^{n}.

(36)

Substituting
n\to n+1
into (
36
) and noting
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+1}{0}=0
for all
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+1}{k}(x-1)_{k-1}=x^{n}.

(37)

Expression of
x^{k}
shown in (
36
) may help us to write the power sum
S_{k}(n)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}j^{k}
as

\displaystyle S_{k}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}\sum^{k}_{i=0}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i}(j)_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}\sum^{k}_{i=0}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i}i!\left({j\atop i}\right)

Since
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{0}=0
for all
k>1
, by interchanging the sums of the rightmost side of the above equation and noting

\displaystyle\left({n+1\atop i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({n\atop i}\right)+\left({n-1\atop i}\right)+\cdots+\left({i+1\atop i}\right)+\left({i+1\atop i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({n\atop i}\right)+\left({n-1\atop i}\right)+\cdots+\left({i+1\atop i}\right)+\left({i\atop i}\right),

we obtain

\displaystyle S_{k}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i}i!\left(\sum^{n}_{j=i}\left({j\atop i}\right)\right)=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i}i!\left({n+1\atop i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i}\frac{1}{i+1}(n+1)_{i+1}=(n+1)\sum^{k}_{i=1}\frac{1}{i+1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i}(n)_{i}.

Substituting (
37
) into
S_{k}(n)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}j^{k}
and noting
(j-1)_{i-1}=(i-1)!\left({j-1\atop i-1}\right)
and

\left({n\atop i}\right)=\left({n-1\atop i-1}\right)+\left({n-2\atop i-1}\right)+\cdots+\left({i-1\atop i-1}\right),

we have

\displaystyle S_{k}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}\sum^{k+1}_{i=1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{i}(j-1)_{i-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}\sum^{k+1}_{i=1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{i}(i-1)!\left({j-1\atop i-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{k+1}_{i=1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{i}\left(\sum^{n}_{j=i}(i-1)!\left({j-1\atop i-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{k+1}_{i=1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{i}(i-1)!\left({n\atop i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{k+1}_{i=1}\frac{1}{i}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{i}(n)_{i}.

Hence, we obtain the following results.

Proposition 5.1

Denote
S_{k}(n)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}j^{k}
and let
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}
be the Stirling numbers of the second kind. Then

\displaystyle S_{k}(n)=(n+1)\sum^{k}_{i=1}\frac{1}{i+1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i}(n)_{i}

(38)

\displaystyle S_{k}(n)=\sum^{k+1}_{i=1}\frac{1}{i}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{i}(n)_{i}.

(39)

Remark 5.2

Formula (
38
) is familiar, for instance, see
(4)
in
[
15
]
.

Since an odd prime
p|\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p}{i}
for all
2\leq i\leq p-1
and
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p}{1}=\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p}{p}=1
, if

, from (
38
) we know that

\frac{n+1}{i+1}(n)_{i}=\frac{p+1}{i+1}i!\left({p\atop i}\right)

is divisible by

when

and

Corollary 5.3

Denote

. Then

Finite torsors over strongly F F -regular singularities
Javier A. Carvajal-Rojas

Abstract




Abstract.
We investigate finite torsors over big opens of
spectra of strongly
F
-regular germs that do not extend to torsors
over the whole spectrum. Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},\mathcal{k},K)
be a strongly
F
-regular

\mathcal{k}
-germ where
\mathcal{k}
is an algebraically closed field of
characteristic
p>0
. We prove the existence of a finite local cover

R\subset R^{\star}
so that
R^{\star}
is a strongly
F
-regular

\mathcal{k}
-germ and: for all finite algebraic groups
G/\mathcal{k}
with
solvable neutral component, every
G
-torsor over a big open of

\Spec R^{\star}
extends to a
G
-torsor everywhere. To achieve
this, we obtain a generalized transformation rule for the

F
-signature under finite local extensions. Such formula is used to
show that the torsion of
\Cl R
is bounded by
1/s(R)
. By
taking cones, we conclude that the Picard group of globally

F
-regular varieties is torsion-free. Likewise, this shows that
canonical covers of
\mathbb Q
-Gorenstein strongly
F
-regular singularities
are strongly
F
-regular.

Keywords.

F
-regularity;
F
-signature; finite torsors; local Nori
fundamental group-scheme

1. Introduction

Inspired by the relationship between strongly
F
-regular
singularities in characteristic
p>0
and KLT singularities in
characteristic zero, K. Schwede, K. Tucker, and the author obtained in

[
C-RST18
, Theorem A]
an analog
of
[
Xu14
, Theorem 1]
. Concretely, they proved
that the order of the étale fundamental group of a big open of the
spectrum of a strongly
F
-regular germ
(R,\mathfrak{m},\mathcal{k},K)
is at
most
1/s(R)
and prime-to-
p
(where
s(R)
is the
F
-signature of

R
). It is worth noting that B. Bhatt, O. Gabber, and M. Olsson
introduced a spreading out technique to give a proof of Xu’s result
from ours
[
BGO17
]
. See

Subsection 2.1
for more on strong
F
-regularity and

F
-signatures.

The aforementioned result gives a satisfactory answer to what extent
there are nontrivial finite étale torsors over big opens
U\subset X\coloneqq\Spec R
(with
R
as above). We use the word torsor as in

[
Mil80
, III, §4]
(
i.e.
principal homogeneous
spaces in the fppf topology). Let us refer to a finite torsor over a
big open as a quasitorsor. Indeed,
[
C-RST18
, Theorem
A]
establishes the existence
of a generically Galois finite extension
(R,\mathfrak{m},\mathcal{k},K)\subset(R^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{k},K^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})
that restricts to a

\textnormal{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}/K)
-torsor over

X_{\mathrm{reg}}
and:

\bullet

(R^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{k},K^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})
is a strongly
F
-regular germ,

\bullet

every étale quasitorsor over
X^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\coloneqq\Spec R^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
extends to a torsor over

X^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
, and

\bullet

its generic degree (
i.e.
the order of

\textnormal{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}/K)
) is at most
1/s(R)
and
prime-to-
p
.

Cartier’s theorem establishes that, in characteristic zero, all affine (resp. finite) algebraic groups are smooth (resp. étale); see
[
Mil17
, 3.23, 3.24]
,
[
DG70
, II, §6.1]
. This result fails in positive characteristics, thereby showing that the local étale fundamental group is not suited to classify quasitorsors over a singularity (as it does in characteristic zero). This had been observed in the failure in positive characteristics of the celebrated Mumford–Flenner theorem characterizing the regularity of normal surface singularities by the triviality of their local fundamental group
[
Mum61
,
Fle75
]
. Remarkably, H. Esnault and E. Viehweg gave an explanation for this failure via their local Nori fundamental group-scheme. Their central idea was incorporating general quasitorsors into the picture; see
[
EV10
]
. This raises the question of whether there is a
maximal cover
as above for general finite torsors.

Question 1.1
.

Further assume
\mathcal{k}\subset R
. Does there exist a
nice
finite cover
R\subset R^{\star}
by a strongly
F
-regular germ so that the restriction map of (isomorphism classes of) torsors

\varrho^{1}_{X^{\star},U^{\star}}(G)\mathrel{:}\check{H}^{1}(X_{\mathrm{fl}}^{\star},G)\to\check{H}^{1}(U_{\mathrm{fl}}^{\star},G)

is surjective for all big opens
U^{\star}\subset X^{\star}=\Spec R^{\star}
and all finite algebraic groups
G/\mathcal{k}
?

By nice in
Section 1
, we seek for a cover described in terms of quasitorsors. There is a purity theorem for finite torsors
[
Nor82
, II, Proposition 7]
,
[
M-B85
]
; see
[
Mar16
]
for an account. That is,
Section 1
has an affirmative answer if
R
is regular by taking
R^{\star}=R
. Thus, the abundance of quasitorsors over a (normal) germ that are not restrictions of a torsor over the whole spectrum is a measurement of the severity of the singularity. See
[
EV10
]
.

Note that
\check{H}^{1}(X_{\mathrm{fl}},G)\neq 0
unless
G/\mathcal{k}
is étale. See
[
EV10
, §2]
for a glimpse into the difficulties this introduces. We may then rephrase
[
C-RST18
, Theorem A]
by saying that
Section 1
has an affirmative answer for all étale algebraic groups
G/\mathcal{k}
. In particular, we may replace
R
by
R^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
and study
Section 1
for infinitesimal algebraic groups. In such a case, we answer
Section 1
affirmatively for all
solvable

G/\mathcal{k}
and
R\subset R^{\star}
can be obtained as finite chain of purely inseparable cyclic covers. In the process, we will solve a fundamental problem raised by K. Watanabe
[
Wat91
]
on the perseverance of strong
F
-regularity under canonical covers and prove that the Picard group of a globally
F
-regular variety is torsion-free. To explain this in more detail, let us consider the following setup.

Setup 1.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},\mathcal{k},K)
be an
F
-finite strongly
F
-regular germ defined over an algebraically closed field
\mathcal{k}
of characteristic

. In particular,

is an excellent henselian local normal domain with maximal ideal

, residue field

, and field of fractions

. Fix

Let

Letting

(

)
.

Work in
Section 1
. There is a finite chain of finite local extensions of strongly

-regular

-germs

In the rest of this introduction, we explain the main steps in achieving Theorem A, whereby outlining the paper and pinpointing some important results obtained in its afterglow.

In studying
Section 1
, it is natural to stick as much as possible to the strategy that was so successful in
[
C-RST18
]
for the étale case. That strategy was based on bounding above the degree of a connected element of
\check{H}^{1}(U_{\mathrm{fl}},G)
(for any étale
G/\mathcal{k}
). This was done via a novel transformation rule for the
F
-signature under local étale quasitorsors; see
[
C-RST18
, Theorem B]
. Thus, one may hope to get an analogous transformation rule for general local quasitorsors. Under closer inspection, one sees that such transformation was built upon the
trace map
of such local étale quasitorsors and exploiting its most basic properties. Thus, we are left facing two questions. Do such trace maps exist for general quasitorsors? If so, do they have the same or analogous properties to those of the trace map in the étale case? We dedicate
Section 3
entirely to these questions. Fortunately, the former question will turn out to have a quite satisfactory answer whereas the latter will end up making things very interesting. In
Section 3
, we adapt to our purposes the theory of
integrals and traces
found in the Hopf algebras literature. We further prove the properties we need from these traces but could not find a reference for. The following theorem summarizes the section. We denote by
o(G)
the order of a finite algebraic group
G/\mathcal{k}
.

Theorem A

(
Subsection 3.1
,

Theorem 3.9
,
Subsection 3.2
)
.

Let
q\mathrel{:}Y\to X
be a quotient by a finite algebraic group

G/\mathcal{k}
; see
subsubsection 2.3.3
. There exists
\Tr_{Y/X}\in\Hom_{X}(q_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{X})
such that:
q
is
G
-torsor at
x\in X
if
and only if
(q_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y})_{x}
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
-module of rank

o(G)
and
\Tr_{Y/X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
freely generates

\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}((q_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y})_{x},\mathcal{O}_{X,x})
as a

(q_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y})_{x}
-module. In particular, assuming that

,

are

\mathbf{S}_{2}
schemes and

is a
G
-quasitorsor, the

\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-module

.

Using this, we are able to obtain the following transformation rule.

Theorem B

(

,

)

Let

This formula can be used along the same lines of
[

]
to investigate

modulo these two hypotheses on

. Just as in

2. Notations, conventions, and preliminaries

Convention 2.1
.

All schemes are defined over an algebraically closed field
\mathcal{k}
of
characteristic
p>0
. We let
F\mathrel{:}X\to X
denote the Frobenius endomorphism of
a scheme
X
. For instance, if
R
is a ring then
F^{e}_{*}R
denotes
the
R
-module obtained by restriction of scalars under
R\to R
,
r\mapsto r^{p^{e}}
. Let us recall that a scheme
X
is said to be
F
-finite if its Frobenius endomorphism is finite. We assume all our schemes to be
F
-finite and noetherian, and so
excellent. Fibered/tensor products are defined over

unless otherwise explicitly stated.

2.1.
F
-signatures and strong

F
-regularity

In view of Kunz’s theorem

[
Kun69
]
, it is natural to use
Frobenius splittings to measure singularities in positive
characteristic. Strongly
F
-regular singularities were introduced by
M. Hochster and C. Huneke

[
HH89
]
. Let

(R,\mathfrak{m},\mathcal{k},K)
be a local ring. The
e^{\mathrm{th}}

F
-splitting
number of
R
is defined by

a_{e}(R)\coloneqq\lambda_{R}\big(\Hom_{R}(F^{e}_{*}R,R)\big/\Hom_{R}(F^{e}_{*}R,\mathfrak{m})\big)\in\mathbb N

where
\lambda_{R}(M)
denotes the length of an
R
-module
M
. This
number turns out to be the maximum of the set

\{a\mid\/\exists\,F^{e}_{*}R\to R^{\oplus a}\textnormal{ surjective}\}\subset\mathbb Z
;
see
[
BST12
]
. In particular, setting

\delta\coloneqq\dim R+\log_{p}[\mathcal{k}^{1/p}:\mathcal{k}]
, we have
0\leq a_{e}(R)\leq p^{e\delta}
where
p^{e\delta}
is the (generic) rank
of
F^{e}_{*}R
and at the same time the free rank of
F^{e}_{*}R
if
R
were
regular. For details, see

[
BST12
,
PT18
]
.

In this way, we may reinterpret Kunz’s theorem by saying that:
R
is regular if and only if
a_{e}(R)=p^{e\delta}
for some (equivalently all)
e\geq 1
. Moreover, one says that
R
is
F
-pure
if
a_{e}\neq 0
for some (equivalently all)
e\geq 0
. To define
strong
F
-regularity
, one considers the asymptotic behavior of the splitting numbers as
e\rightarrow\infty
. To be precise, after
[
HL02
,
SdB97
]
, one defines the
F
-signature of
R
as the limit

s(R)\coloneqq\lim_{e\rightarrow\infty}\frac{a_{e}(R)}{p^{e\delta}}\in[0,1],

whose existence was established in
[
Tuc12
]
. Then,
R
is
strongly
F
-regular
if and only if
s(R)>0
; see
[
AL03
, Theorem 0.2]
. On the other hand, it turns out that
s(R)=1
if and only if
R
is regular; see
[
HL02
, Corollary 16]
.

Intuitively, one often thinks of
s(R)
as a volume attached to the singularity
R
,
cf.

[
Von12
]
. Furthermore,
F
-signatures have been linked (conjecturally) to normalized volumes; see
[
LLX20
, §6.3.1]
. For surveys on the subject of strong
F
-regularity, we recommend
[
SZ15
,
ST12
]
. See
[
PT18
]
for more on
F
-signatures.

2.2. Linear and bilinear forms

Let
M
be a module over a commutative ring
R
. We denote its dual
\Hom_{R}(M,R)
by
M^{\vee}
. A
bilinear form
on
M
over
R
is the same as an element of
(M\otimes_{R}M)^{\vee}\eqqcolon M^{\vee 2}
. By
\otimes
-
\Hom
adjointness, there are two natural isomorphisms
\upsilon_{i}\mathrel{:}M^{\vee 2}\to\Hom_{R}(M,M^{\vee})
. A bilinear form
\Theta
is
symmetric
if
\upsilon_{1}(\Theta)=\upsilon_{2}(\Theta)
, and we write
\upsilon(\Theta)
for either one. It is said to be
nondegenerate
(resp.
nonsingular
) if
\upsilon(\Theta)
is injective (resp. an isomorphism). If
M
is free of finite rank, there is a determinant function
\det\mathrel{:}M^{\vee 2}\to R
. Note that
\Theta
is nondegenerate (resp. nonsingular) if and only if
\det\Theta
is a nonzerodivisor (resp. a unit). If
M
is locally free of finite rank, we associate to
\Theta
a locally principal ideal
\det\Theta
of
R
, for naturally
(M^{\vee 2})_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong(M_{\mathfrak{p}})^{\vee 2}
. Say
S=M
is an
R
-algebra,
i.e.
there is a diagonal morphism
\Delta_{S/R}\mathrel{:}S\otimes_{R}S\to S
. Its dual
\Delta_{S/R}^{\vee}\mathrel{:}S^{\vee}\to(S\otimes_{R}S)^{\vee}
gives a canonical way to define a bilinear form from a linear one. We refer to
\theta\in S^{\vee}
as nondegenerate or nonsingular if so is
\Delta_{S/R}^{\vee}(\theta)
. If
S/R
is finite and flat, one defines the
discriminant
of
\theta
as
\disc\theta\coloneqq\det\Delta_{S/R}^{\vee}(\theta)
.

2.3. Group-schemes and their actions on schemes

For the most part, we follow

[
Mil17
,
Tat97
,
Mon93
]
. Let

G/\mathcal{k}
be an affine group-scheme. We denote by
\mathcal{O}(G)
its
corresponding (commutative) Hopf algebra. Given a Hopf algebra

H/\mathcal{k}
, we denote by
u\mathrel{:}\mathcal{k}\to H
and

\Delta\mathrel{:}H\otimes H\to H
the algebra morphisms of

unit
and
product
whereas the coalgebra morphisms

counit
and
\emph{coproduct}
are denoted by
e\mathrel{:}H\to\mathcal{k}
and
\nabla\mathrel{:}H\to H\otimes H
. For the

antipode
, we use
\iota\mathrel{:}H\to H
. The trivial
group-scheme is denoted by
\ast
. If
G/\mathcal{k}
is finite, its

order
is
o(G)\coloneqq\dim_{\mathcal{k}}\mathcal{O}(G)
. If
G
is
commutative, its
Cartier dual
is denoted by
G^{\vee}
; see

[
Mil17
, 11.c]
. The dual of a finite Hopf algebra

H
is denoted by
H^{\vee}
; see

[
Mon93
, §1.2]
. In particular

\mathcal{O}(G^{\vee})=\mathcal{O}(G)^{\vee}
. The connected-étale exact sequence

[
Mil17
, 5.h]
is denoted by

\ast\to G^{\circ}\to G\to\pi_{0}(G)\to\ast,

which splits as
\mathcal{k}
is perfect
[
Mil17
, 11.3]
. In other words,
G=G^{\circ}\rtimes\pi_{0}(G)
. In fact,
G_{\mathrm{red}}\cong\pi_{0}(G)
and the splitting is (uniquely) given by the canonical closed embedding
G_{\mathrm{red}}\to G
.

2.3.1. Examples of affine algebraic groups

Finite
constant group-schemes
are examples of finite étale algebraic groups and (since
\mathcal{k}
is separably closed) these are the only ones
[
Mil17
, 2.b]
. If
G
is a finite discrete group, the Hopf algebra of the constant group-scheme it defines (also denoted by
G
) is
\mathcal{O}(G)=\Hom_{\mathsf{Set}}(G,\mathcal{k})

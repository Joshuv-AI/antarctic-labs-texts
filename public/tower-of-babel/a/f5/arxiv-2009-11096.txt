The controlling L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra, cohomology and homotopy of embedding tensors and Lie-Leibniz triples
Yunhe Sheng, Rong Tang, Chenchang Zhu

Abstract
. In this paper, we first construct the controlling algebras of embedding tensors and Lie-Leibniz triples, which turn out to be a graded Lie algebra and an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra respectively. Then we introduce representations and cohomologies of embedding tensors and Lie-Leibniz triples, and show that there is a long exact sequence connecting various cohomologies. As applications, we classify infinitesimal deformations and central extensions using the second cohomology groups. Finally, we introduce the notion of a homotopy embedding tensor which will induce a Leibniz ∞ -algebra. We realize Kotov and Strobl’s construction of an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra from an embedding tensor, as a functor from the category of homotopy embedding tensors to that of Leibniz ∞ -algebras, and a functor further to that of L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras.

1.
Introduction

An embedding tensor on a Lie algebra representation
(\mathfrak{g},V)
is a
linear map
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
satisfying a quadratic equivariancy constraint (see Definition
2.2
). Leibniz algebras, embedding tensors and their associated tensor hierarchies provide a
nice and efficient way to construct supergravity theories
and further to higher gauge theories (see e.g.
[
6
,
7
,
18
,
24
,
51
]
and
references therein
for a rich physics literature on this subject, and see
[
27
,
28
]

for a math-friendly introduction on this
subject). Recently, this topic has attracted much attention of
the mathematical physics world. First of all, a sharp and beautiful observation by Kotov and Strobl in a
recent article
[
24
]
demonstrates for us a possible mathematical nature
behind the various calculations from embedding tensors to their
associated tensor hierarchies in the physics literature. An embedding
tensor gives rise to a Leibniz algebra, which further gives rise to an

L_{\infty}
-algebra, and this corresponds to tensor hierarchies in physics
literature. We see later that both procedures are
functorial (the first one in Section
2
, and the second one in Section

6
), moreover the functoriality can be extended with

homotopy
added in for all objects. In
fact, the second procedure is a composition of several very classic results

[
35
,
37
]
. This therefore guarantees us, from a category viewpoint, that
the process from embedding tensors to tensor hierarchies, and its
corresponding transition from supergravity theories to higher gauge
theories, is natural. Then, almost at the same time, appeared several other approaches to
encode this process to tensor hierarchies, involving also Leibniz
field theory:
[
50
,
52
]
are from
the point of view of enhanced Leibniz structures;
[
29
]
builds an

L_{\infty}
-algebra extension from a Leibniz algebra, which is
apparently different from the second functor described above. The
functoriality was shown in both procedures.

In our setting, we further conjecture that the above two functors are functorial
in an
\infty
-category sense. We will explore this direction in a future work

[
45
]
. Notice that the homotopy nature of
L_{\infty}
-algebras
suggests homotopy also in the category hosting these objects.

In this article, we provide a rich math tool box for embedding tensors
and Lie-Leibniz triples, which seem not yet existing in the mathematical
literature. Indeed, as a sort of algebra (or operad), embedding tensors involve not only binary but also unary operations. We develop the theory of controlling algebras, thus further the theory of cohomology and homotopy for embedding tensors and Lie-Leibniz triples.

To establish a good cohomology theory for an object, besides the
standard homological algebraic method of projective resolutions, there is
also another shorter way through its “controlling
algebraic object”. Let us explain this idea in the case of a Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
. We start with a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
, then the graded vector space

\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
equipped with the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}}
becomes a
graded Lie algebra (g.l.a.), or a differential graded Lie algebra
(d.g.l.a.) with 0 differential
[
38
]
. Then a Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}

corresponds
exactly
to a Maurer-Cartan element
\pi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. We call this g.l.a.
(\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}})
the
controlling algebra of Lie algebra structures
on
\mathfrak{g}
. Furthermore,
since
[\pi,\pi]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0
,
d_{\pi}:=[\pi,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}}
satisfies

d^{2}_{\pi}=0
, thus
d_{\pi}
is a differential. The controlling
g.l.a.
\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
together with

d_{\pi}
becomes exactly the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex to
calculate the cohomology of
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in its adjoint
representation. This is a general phenomenon and works not only for
Lie algebras, but also for associative algebras, Leibniz algebras,

n
-Lie algebras, and pre-Lie algebras. See the review
[
10
,
17
]

for more details. Thus, we use this principal as a guide to
develop cohomology theories for embedding tensors (Section
3
) and
Lie-Leibniz triples (Section
4
). Here a Lie-Leibniz triple
[
28
]
consists
of a Lie algebra representation
(\mathfrak{g},V)
, and an embedding tensor

T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
. The subtle difference between these two concepts shows
up, e.g. in deformation theory. To deform an
embedding tensor, we fix
(\mathfrak{g},V)
and deform only the operator

T
, but to deform a Lie-Leibniz triple, we are allowed to deform also
the Lie algebra and its representation
(\mathfrak{g},V)
simultaneously. It turns out that the controlling algebraic structure for embedding tensors is a g.l.a. and that for Lie-Leibniz triples is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Thus indeed the theory of Lie-Leibniz triples is more involved.

We give some immediate applications (also as verifications) of this cohomology theory of Lie-Leibniz triples in Section
5
. It does behave as it should: Given a Lie-Leibniz triple,

(1)

its second cohomology classes in
H^{2}
with coefficients in the adjoint representation correspond exactly to the equivalence classes of its infinitesimal deformations;

(2)

its second cohomology classes in
H^{2}
with coefficients in the trivial representation correspond exactly to the equivalence classes of central extensions.

Here, we actually need a bit of additional luck in the second
application: We need to develop a cohomology theory for Lie-Leibniz
triples with arbitrary coefficients, but not just the one from adjoint
representation. For this, we find that there is a natural
Lie-Leibniz triple structure on the endomorphisms of a 2-term complex of vector
spaces. This natural structure comes from the strict Lie 2-algebra
structure on them
[
46
]
, and a strict Lie 2-algebra is a
special Lie-Leibniz triple (see Example
2.10
and Example

2.13
).

Finally, in Section
6
, we study how embedding tensors cooperate with
homotopy. That is, what a homotopy embedding tensor should be, and
how Kotov-Strobl’s functor
\mathsf{KS}
behaves with respect to homotopy. Will
\mathsf{KS}

still produces an
L_{\infty}
-algebra or something involving even more
homotopy? This will test how stable the concept of embedding tensors
and the procedure to topological hierarchies are. We still use the tool of controlling algebras to develop the homotopy theory. A
standard approach
[
35
,
54
]
to give a homotopy
\mathcal{P}
-algebraic structure is
to construct a minimal model
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
of the operad

\mathcal{P}
. Along this approach,
L_{\infty}
-algebras and

A_{\infty}
-algebras are well developed. Moreover,
Leibniz
∞
-algebras are defined as the algebras over the
minimal model
[
2
,
31
]
of the
\mathcal{L}eibniz
operad. However, apart from this approach, we can also use Maurer-Cartan
elements of the aforementioned controlling algebra on a graded vector
space to define a homotopy algebraic structure. For example, to define
an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, one can start with a graded vector space

\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}
and define an
L_{\infty}
-algebra to be a Maurer-Cartan
element of the g.l.a.
(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathsf{S}(\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}),\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}})
. Using this method, we define a homotopy embedding
tensor to be a Maurer-Cartan element of a graded
version of the controlling
algebra that we develop in Section
3
. Then we show that a homotopy embedding tensor gives rise to a Leibniz
∞
-algebra, and a
Leibniz
∞
-algebra further gives rise to an

L_{\infty}
-algebra. We further prove that these two processes are
functorial. Thus the functor
\mathsf{KS}
extends to a homotopic version.

We want to emphasis that embedding tensors and Lie-Leibniz triples
have been already known in mathematics literature under the name of
averaging operators and averaging algebras respectively for a long time. In the last century, many studies on averaging operators were done for various special algebras, such as function spaces, Banach algebras, and the topics and methods were largely
analytic
[
5
,
9
,
19
,
41
]
. See
the well-written introduction in
[
40
]
for more details. More
recently, people have begun to study averaging operators in double
algebras, classical Yang-Baxter equation, conformal algebras, and the
procedure of replication in the operad theory
[
1
,
15
,
21
,
39
]
. It is not yet clear to us how these aspects of embedding tensors and Lie-Leibniz triples are related. But we wish our article makes some first steps to understand these concepts more deeply.

2.
Embedding tensors, omni-Lie algebras and Leibniz algebras

In this section, first we establish relations between embedding tensors, omni-Lie algebras and Leibniz algebras. Then we give some interesting examples of embedding tensors.

Definition 2.1
.

A
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair consists of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and a representation
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
of
\mathfrak{g}
on a vector space
V
.

We denote a
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair by
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V;\rho))
, or simply by
(\mathfrak{g},V)
if there is no confusion.

Definition 2.2
.

(i)

An
embedding tensor
on a
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V;\rho))
is a linear map
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the following quadratic constraint:

(1)

[Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\big(\rho(Tu)(v)\big),\quad\forall u,v\in V.

(ii)

A
Lie-Leibniz triple
is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},V,T)
, where
(\mathfrak{g},V)
is a
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair and
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is an embedding tensor on the
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair
(\mathfrak{g},V)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V;\rho),T)
and
((\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}),(V^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}),T^{\prime})
be two Lie-Leibniz triples. A
homomorphism
from
((\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}),(V^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}),T^{\prime})
to
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V;\rho),T)
consists of
a Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and a linear map
\varphi:V^{\prime}\longrightarrow V
such that

(2)

\displaystyle T\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi\circ T^{\prime},

(3)

\displaystyle\varphi\rho^{\prime}(x)(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\phi(x))(\varphi(u)),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},u\in V^{\prime}.

In particular, if
\phi
and
\varphi
are invertible, then
(\phi,\varphi)
is called an
isomorphism
.

The algebraic structure underlying an embedding tensor is a
Leibniz algebra
, which is a vector space
\mathfrak{G}
together with a bilinear operation
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{G}}:\mathfrak{G}\otimes\mathfrak{G}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{G}
such that

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{G}}]_{\mathfrak{G}}=[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}},z]_{\mathfrak{G}}+[y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{G}}]_{\mathfrak{G}},\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{G}.

Proposition 2.4
.

(
[
1
]
)

Let
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
be an embedding tensor on a
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V;\rho))
. Then there exists a Leibniz algebra structure
[\cdot,\cdot]_{T}
on
V
given by

(4)

\displaystyle[u,v]_{T}:=\rho(Tu)v,\,\,\,\,\forall u,v\in V.

Remark 2.5
.

This association gives rise to a functor
F:\mathsf{ET}\to\mathsf{Leibniz}\mbox{-}\mathsf{Alg}
from the
category of embedding tensors to that of Leibniz algebras. The direction from Leibniz algebras to embedding tensors is less well
behaved. It is easy to check that the association in
[
24
]

gives rise to a functor
G:\mathsf{Leibniz}\mbox{-}\mathsf{Alg}\to\mathsf{ET}
, and
F\circ G={\rm{Id}}
as also noticed therein. But
G\circ F\neq{\rm{Id}}
, and these two functors do not
differ even by a natural transformation. There is
another natural association given in
[
28
]
, namely for a Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{G}})
, the left multiplication
L:\mathfrak{G}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{G})
given by

(5)

L_{x}y=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{G},

is an embedding tensor on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{G})
with
respect to the natural representation on the vector space

\mathfrak{G}
. Even though with this method, it is more likely to create a
natural transformation, it does not give rise to even a functor
\mathsf{Leibniz}\mbox{-}\mathsf{Alg}\to\mathsf{ET}
in the first place.

In the sequel, we give an alternative explanation of Proposition
2.4
using integrable subspaces of omni-Lie algebras. For this purpose, we give an interesting example of embedding tensors.

Example 2.6
.

Let
V
be a vector space. Then the general linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
represents on the direct sum
\mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus V
naturally via:

\rho(A)(B+v)=[A,B]+Av,\quad\forall A,B\in\mathfrak{gl}(V),v\in V.

Let
P:\mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be the projection to the first summand. Then we have

P\Big(\rho(P(A+u))(B+v)\Big)=P(\rho(A)(B+v))=[A,B]=[P(A+u),P(B+v)],

for all
A,B\in\mathfrak{gl}(V),u,v\in V.
Thus,
P
is an embedding tensor on
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
with respect to the representation
(\mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus V;\rho)
.

By Proposition
2.4
, there is an induced Leibniz algebra structure on
\mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus V
given by

(6)

[A+u,B+v]_{\mathfrak{ol}}=\rho(P(A+u))(B+v)=[A,B]+Av.

The above bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{ol}}
is exactly the omni-Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus V
introduced by Weinstein in
[
56
]
. Recall that an
omni-Lie algebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{ol}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{+})
, where the omni-Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{ol}}
is given by (
6
), and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{+}
is a symmetric
V
-valued pairing given by

(7)

(A+u,B+v)_{+}=Av+Bu.

Definition 2.7
.

A subspace
H\subset\mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus V
is said to be
integrable
if
[H,H]_{\mathfrak{ol}}\subset H
.

Now we are ready to characterize embedding tensors using integrable subspaces of the omni-Lie algebra.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be a linear map. Then
T
is an embedding tensor on the general linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
with respect to the natural representation on
V
if and only if the graph of
T
, denoted by
G_{T}
, is an integrable subspace of the omni-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{ol}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{+})
.

Proof.

For all
u,v\in V
, we have

\displaystyle[Tu+u,Tv+v]_{\mathfrak{ol}}=[Tu,Tv]+(Tu)v,

which implies that the graph of
T
is integrable if and only if
[Tu,Tv]=T((Tu)v)
, i.e.
T
is an embedding tensor.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

Since the omni-Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{ol}}
is a Leibniz algebra
structure, it follows that an integrable subspace is also a Leibniz
algebra. Thus, if
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is an embedding tensor, then
G_{T}

is a Leibniz algebra. Since
G_{T}
and
V
are isomorphic, so there
is an induced Leibniz algebra structure on
V
. This Leibniz algebra
structure on
V
is exactly the one given in Proposition

2.4
.

In the rest of this section, we give various interesting examples.

Example 2.10

(differential Lie algebras)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},d)
be a differential Lie algebra, that is a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with a derivation
d

such that
d\circ d=0
. Then we have

d[dx,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[d^{2}x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[dx,dy]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[dx,dy]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Thus,
d
is an embedding tensor on the
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad}))
.

Example 2.11

(an example from supergravity)
.

This example is taken from physics literature
[
24
,
42
]
on supergravity in space-time dimension 4, which is
one of the origins where the concept of embedding tensors
appear. The vector space
V
is taken as the fundamental
representation
{\bf 56}=(\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{8})\oplus(\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{8})^{*}
,
of
E_{7(7)}
, the non-compact real form of
E_{7}
. We take

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(8)
, the Lie algebra of real skew-symmetric matrices. In fact

{\rm SO}(8)\subset E_{7(7)}
and the naturally induced representation
\rho
of

\mathfrak{so}(8)
on
V
is simply the sum of a wedge product of the
fundamental representation of
\mathfrak{so}(8)
and its dual. More precisely,
\mathfrak{so}(8)
naturally represents on
W:=\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{8}
via

\bar{\rho}(A)(u\wedge v)=(Au)\wedge v+u\wedge Av,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{8}.

Let
\bar{\rho}^{*}
be the dual representation of
\mathfrak{so}(8)
on
W^{*}
. Then
\rho=\bar{\rho}+\bar{\rho}^{*}
is a representation of
\mathfrak{so}(8)
on
V.

Let
E_{ij}=R_{ij}-R_{ji}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
, where
R_{ij}
denotes the
8\times 8
matrix with the
(i,j)
-position being 1 and elsewhere being 0. Then we have

\displaystyle[E_{ij},E_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}E_{il}-\delta_{ik}E_{jl}+\delta_{jl}E_{ki}-\delta_{il}E_{kj}.

Let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{8}\}
be the basis of
\mathbb{R}^{8}
where
e_{i}
is the vector with the
i
-th position being
1
and elsewhere being
0
. Then
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}_{i<j}
forms a basis of
W
. Let
\{e_{1}^{*},\cdots,e_{8}^{*}\}
be the dual basis. So
\{e_{i}^{*}\wedge e_{j}^{*}\}_{i<j}
forms a basis of
W^{*}.

Define
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{so}(8)
by

T(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=E_{ij},\quad T(e_{i}^{*}\wedge e_{j}^{*})=0.

Then we have

\displaystyle T(\rho(T(e_{i}\wedge e_{j}))(e_{k}\wedge e_{l}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T((E_{ij}e_{k})\wedge e_{l}+e_{k}\wedge E_{ij}e_{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(\delta_{jk}e_{i}\wedge e_{l}-\delta_{ik}e_{j}\wedge e_{l}+\delta_{jl}e_{k}\wedge e_{i}-\delta_{il}e_{k}\wedge e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{jk}E_{il}-\delta_{ik}E_{jl}+\delta_{jl}E_{ki}-\delta_{il}E_{kj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[E_{ij},E_{kl}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[T(e_{i}\wedge e_{j}),T(e_{k}\wedge e_{l})],

which implies that
T
is an embedding tensor on the
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair
(\mathfrak{so}(8),V)
.

The induced Leibniz algebra structure on
V
is given by

\displaystyle[e_{i}\wedge e_{j}+e_{p}^{*}\wedge e_{q}^{*},e_{k}\wedge e_{l}+e_{m}^{*}\wedge e_{n}^{*}]_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(T(e_{i}\wedge e_{j}+e_{p}^{*}\wedge e_{q}^{*}))(e_{k}\wedge e_{l}+e_{m}^{*}\wedge e_{n}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}(E_{ij})(e_{k}\wedge e_{l})+\bar{\rho}^{*}(E_{ij})(e_{m}^{*}\wedge e_{n}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{jk}e_{i}\wedge e_{l}-\delta_{ik}e_{j}\wedge e_{l}+\delta_{jl}e_{k}\wedge e_{i}-\delta_{il}e_{k}\wedge e_{j}

\displaystyle+\delta_{jm}e_{i}^{*}\wedge e_{n}^{*}-\delta_{im}e_{j}^{*}\wedge e_{n}^{*}+\delta_{jn}e_{m}^{*}\wedge e_{i}^{*}-\delta_{in}e_{m}^{*}\wedge e_{j}^{*}.

Notice that even though the first term
\bar{\rho}(E_{ij})(e_{k}\wedge e_{l})=[E_{ij},E_{kl}]=-\bar{\rho}(E_{kj})(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})
has
antisymmetric property, the second term make the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{T}
not
antisymmetric. Thus we end up really with a Leibniz algebra, not a
Lie algebra. Mathematically, this example can be generalized to all
Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
acts on
V=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
via its adjoint and coadjoint representation.
That is, the natural
projection to the first factor
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
is an embedding tensor on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the action on
V=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Example 2.12

(endomorphism algebra of a 2-term complex)
.

Given a 2-term complex of vector spaces
W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{T}}}{{\,\rightarrow\,}}\mathfrak{h}
, we
define
\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{T}}}{{\,\rightarrow\,}}\mathfrak{h})
by

\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{T}}}{{\,\rightarrow\,}}\mathfrak{h})\triangleq\{(A_{0},A_{1})\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(W)|A_{0}\circ\mathfrak{T}=\mathfrak{T}\circ A_{1}\}.

It is obvious that
\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{T}}}{{\,\rightarrow\,}}\mathfrak{h})
with the commutator bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{C}
is a Lie algebra. Moreover it represents on
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},W)
via

\rho(A_{0},A_{1})(\Phi)=[(A_{0},A_{1}),\Phi]_{C}\triangleq A_{1}\circ\Phi-\Phi\circ A_{0},\quad\forall(A_{0},A_{1})\in\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{T}}}{{\,\rightarrow\,}}\mathfrak{h}),~\Phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},W).

Define
\overline{\mathfrak{T}}:\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},W)\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{T}}}{{\,\rightarrow\,}}\mathfrak{h})
by

\overline{\mathfrak{T}}(\Phi)\triangleq(\Phi\circ\mathfrak{T},\mathfrak{T}\circ\Phi),\quad\forall~\Phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},W).

Then it is straightforward to deduce that
(\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{T}}}{{\,\rightarrow\,}}\mathfrak{h}),\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},W),\overline{\mathfrak{T}})
is a Lie-Leibniz triple.

This Lie-Leibniz triple plays an important role in the representation
theory of Lie-Leibniz triples. See Definition
5.11
for
more details. In fact, this embedding tensor comes from a strict 2-Lie
algebra structure on the endomorphisms of a 2-term complex
described in

[
46
]
. This can be generalized to any strict Lie 2-algebra as follows.

Example 2.13

(strict Lie 2-algebras)
.

A strict Lie
2
-algebra is a
2
-term graded vector spaces
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}
equipped with a linear map
\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and a skew-symmetric bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}_{i}\wedge\mathfrak{g}_{j}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{i+j},0\leq i+j\leq 1
, such that for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},~a,b\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
the following equalities are satisfied:

(a)

\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}[x,a]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[x,\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}a]_{\mathfrak{g}}
,
[\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[a,\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}b]_{\mathfrak{g}}
,

(b)

[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},x]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[[z,x]_{\mathfrak{g}},y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0
,
[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},a]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[[y,a]_{\mathfrak{g}},x]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[[a,x]_{\mathfrak{g}},y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0
.

Define
\rho
from
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
to
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})
by

\rho(x)(a)=[x,a]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Then,
(\mathfrak{g}_{1};\rho)
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{0},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is an embedding tensor on the
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair
((\mathfrak{g}_{0},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(\mathfrak{g}_{1};\rho))
.

As strict Lie 2-algebras are equivalent to crossed modules of Lie algebras,
we naturally have the following example.

Example 2.14

(crossed modules of Lie algebras)
.

A crossed module of Lie algebras is a quadruple
(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathrm{d},\rho)
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{1}
are Lie algebras,
\mathrm{d}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
\rho:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})
are homomorphisms of Lie algebras such that for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
m,n\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, we have

\mathrm{d}(\rho(x)m)=[x,\mathrm{d}(m)]_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}},\quad\rho(\mathrm{d}(m))(n)=[m,n]_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}.

Then
d
is an embedding tensor on the
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair
((\mathfrak{g}_{0},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}),(\mathfrak{g}_{1};\rho))
.

Example
2.13
and Example
2.14
can be generalized to the following more general case.

Example 2.15

(Lie objects in the infinitesimal tensor category of linear maps)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
a representation. If a linear map
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is
\mathfrak{g}
-equivariant, that is,

\displaystyle T(\rho(x)v)=[x,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},~v\in V,

then
T
is an embedding tensor on the
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V;\rho))
. In fact
V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\,\rightarrow\,}}\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie object in the infinitesimal tensor category of linear maps if and only if
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is
\mathfrak{g}
-equivariant. Let
(\mathfrak{G},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{G}})
be a Leibniz algebra and
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathsf{ann}}
be the two-sided ideal of
\mathfrak{G}
generated by
[x,x]_{\mathfrak{G}}
for all
x\in\mathfrak{G}
. Then the natural projection
\pi
from
\mathfrak{G}
to
\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{G}^{\mathsf{ann}}
gives a Lie object in the infinitesimal tensor category of linear maps and
\pi
is an embedding tensor. See
[
24
,
33
]
for more details.

Example 2.16
.

The
Heisenberg algebra

H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
is a
three-dimensional complex Lie algebra generated by
elements
e_{1},e_{2}
and
e_{3}
with Lie brackets

[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{3},~[e_{1},e_{3}]=0,~[e_{2},e_{3}]=0.

T=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}r_{11}&r_{12}&r_{13}\\
r_{21}&r_{22}&r_{23}\\
r_{31}&r_{32}&r_{33}\end{array}\right)
is an embedding tensor on
H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
with respect to the adjoint representation if and only if

[Te_{i},Te_{j}]=T[Te_{i},e_{j}],~~i,j\in\{1,2,3\}.

First by

\displaystyle 0=[Te_{1},Te_{1}]=T[Te_{1},e_{1}]=T[r_{11}e_{1}+r_{21}e_{2}+r_{31}e_{3},e_{1}]=-r_{21}r_{13}e_{1}-r_{21}r_{23}e_{2}-r_{21}r_{33}e_{3},

we obtain

r_{21}r_{13}=0,~r_{21}r_{23}=0,~r_{21}r_{33}=0.

Similarly, we can obtain

\begin{array}[]{rcl rcl rcl}r_{12}r_{13}&=&0,&r_{12}r_{23}&=&0,&r_{12}r_{33}&=&0,\\
r_{11}r_{13}&=&r_{22}r_{13}=0,&r_{11}r_{23}&=&r_{22}r_{23}=0,&r_{11}r_{22}-r_{12}r_{21}&=&r_{11}r_{33}=r_{22}r_{33},\\
r_{23}r_{13}&=&0,&r_{23}^{2}&=&0,&r_{23}r_{33}&=&r_{11}r_{23}-r_{13}r_{21}=0,\\
r_{13}^{2}&=&0,&r_{13}r_{23}&=&0,&-r_{13}r_{33}&=&r_{12}r_{23}-r_{13}r_{22}=0.\end{array}

Therefore, we have

(i)

If
r_{13}=r_{23}=r_{33}=0
, then
T=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}r_{11}&r_{12}&0\\
r_{21}&r_{22}&0\\
r_{31}&r_{32}&0\end{array}\right)
is an embedding tensor on
H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
if and only if
r_{11}r_{22}=r_{12}r_{21}
.

(ii)

If
r_{13}=r_{23}=0
and
r_{33}\not=0
, then
r_{12}=r_{21}=0
and
T=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}r_{11}&0&0\\
0&r_{22}&0\\
r_{31}&r_{32}&r_{33}\end{array}\right)
is an embedding tensor on
H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
if and only if
r_{11}r_{22}=r_{22}r_{33}=r_{11}r_{33}
.

These two cases exhaust all the possibilities of embedding tensors on

H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
with respect to the adjoint representation.

3.
The controlling graded Lie algebra and cohomology of embedding tensors

In this section first we recall the controlling g.l.a. that characterize Leibniz algebras as Maurer-Cartan elements and the g.l.a. governing a
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair that was originally given in
[
3
]
. Then we construct the controlling g.l.a. of embedding tensors. Finally we introduce the cohomologies of embedding tensors.

3.1.
The controlling graded Lie algebra of
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pairs

A permutation
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
is called an
(i,n-i)
-shuffle
if
\sigma(1)<\cdots<\sigma(i)
and
\sigma(i+1)<\cdots<\sigma(n)
. If
i=0
or
n
, we assume
\sigma={\rm{Id}}
. The set of all
(i,n-i)
-shuffles will be denoted by
\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i)}
. The notion of an
(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k})
-shuffle and the set
\mathbb{S}_{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k})}
are defined analogously.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space. We consider the graded vector space

C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

It is known that
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
equipped with the
Balavoine bracket

[
4
]
:

(8)

\displaystyle[P,Q]_{\mathsf{B}}=P\bar{\circ}Q-(-1)^{pq}Q\bar{\circ}P,\,\,\,\,\forall P\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),Q\in C^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),

is a g.l.a. where
P\bar{\circ}Q\in C^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

P\bar{\circ}Q=\sum_{k=1}^{p+1}P\circ_{k}Q,

and
\circ_{k}
is defined by

\displaystyle(P\circ_{k}Q)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,q)}}(-1)^{(k-1)q}(-1)^{\sigma}P(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k-1)},Q(x_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k+q-1)},x_{k+q}),x_{k+q+1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1}).

Remark 3.1
.

In fact, the Balavoine bracket is the commutator of coderivations on the cofree conilpotent coZinbiel coalgebra
\bar{\mathsf{T}}(\mathfrak{g})
. See
[
2
,
53
]
for more details. Note that on the same graded vector space
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
there is the Gerstenhaber bracket
[
49
]
which is the commutator of coderivations on the cofree conilpotent coassociative coalgebra
\bar{\mathsf{T}}(\mathfrak{g})
.

The following conclusion is straightforward.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
\Omega\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\Omega,\Omega]_{\mathsf{B}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\Omega\bar{\circ}\Omega(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\Omega(\Omega(x_{1},x_{2}),x_{3})-\Omega(x_{1},\Omega(x_{2},x_{3}))+\Omega(x_{2},\Omega(x_{1},x_{3})).

Thus,
\Omega
defines a Leibniz algebra structure if and only if
[\Omega,\Omega]_{\mathsf{B}}=0
, i.e.
\Omega
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the g.l.a.
(C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be vector spaces and elements in
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
will be denoted by
x,y,z,x_{i}
and elements in
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
will be denoted by
u,v,v_{i}
.
Let
c:\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\otimes n}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{1}
be a linear map. Define a linear map
\hat{c}\in C^{n-1}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
by

\displaystyle\hat{c}\big((x_{1},v_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes(x_{n},v_{n})\big):=(c(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}),0).

In general, for a given linear map
f:\mathfrak{g}_{i(1)}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{i(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{i(n)}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{j}
,
i(1),\cdots,i(n),j\in\{1,2\}
, we define a linear map
\hat{f}\in C^{n-1}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
by

\hat{f}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f&\mbox{on $\mathfrak{g}_{i(1)}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{i(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{i(n)}$, }\\
0&\mbox{all other cases.}\end{array}\right.

We call the linear map
\hat{f}
a
horizontal lift
of
f
.

Let us write
\hat{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V):=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}\otimes V,V)
. Using the horizontal lift, we can regard
\hat{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)
as a subspace of
{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

The above defined
\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\hat{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)
is a subalgebra of the g.l.a.
({C}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
. Moreover, a Maurer-Cartan element of the g.l.a.
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\hat{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
is exactly a
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair.

Proof.

It is straightforward to deduce that
\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\hat{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)
is a subalgebra of the graded Lie algebra
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
.
Moreover, let
(\mu,\rho)\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes V,V)
be a Maurer-Cartan element. Then
[\hat{\mu}+\hat{\rho},\hat{\mu}+\hat{\rho}]_{\mathsf{B}}=0
implies that
\mu
defines a Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\rho
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu)
on
V.

∎

3.2.
The controlling graded Lie algebra of embedding tensors

Let
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V;\rho))
be a
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair. Usually we will also use
\mu
to indicate the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}
. We have a Leibniz algebra structure
\mu\boxplus\rho
on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
, which is given by

(9)

\displaystyle(\mu\boxplus\rho)\big((x,u),(y,v)\big)=([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x)v).

This Leibniz algebra is called the
hemisemidirect product
of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and the representation
(V;\rho)
. It first appeared in
[
20
]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V;\rho))
be a
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair. Then

(\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)

is a graded Lie algebra, where the graded Lie bracket

\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket
is given in a derived fashion

(10)

\left\llbracket\theta,\phi\right\rrbracket=(-1)^{m-1}[[\mu\boxplus\rho,\theta]_{\mathsf{B}},\phi]_{\mathsf{B}},\quad\forall\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{m}V,\mathfrak{g}),~\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n}V,\mathfrak{g}).

More precisely, it is given by

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\theta,\phi\right\rrbracket(v_{1},\cdots,v_{m+n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,n)}}(-1)^{(k-1)n+1}(-1)^{\sigma}\theta(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k-1)},\rho(\phi(v_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k+n-1)}))v_{k+n},v_{k+n+1},\cdots,v_{m+n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,n)}}(-1)^{mn+1}(-1)^{\sigma}[\theta(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(m)}),\phi(v_{\sigma(m+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(m+n-1)},v_{\sigma(m+n)})]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,m)}}(-1)^{m(k+n-1)}(-1)^{\sigma}\phi(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k-1)},\rho(\theta(v_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k+m-1)}))v_{k+m},v_{k+m+1},\cdots,v_{m+n}).

Moreover, its Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely embedding tensors.

Proof.

In short, the graded Lie algebra
(\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is obtained via the derived bracket
[
22
,
23
,
55
]
. In fact, the Balavoine bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}}
associated to the direct sum vector space
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
gives rise to a graded Lie algebra
(\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
. Since
\mu:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra structure and
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\otimes V\longrightarrow V
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
, therefore the hemisemidirect product Leibniz algebra structure
\mu\boxplus\rho
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
(\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
, defining a differential
d_{\mu\boxplus\rho}
on
(\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
via

d_{\mu\boxplus\rho}=[\mu\boxplus\rho,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}}.

Since the subspace
\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g})
is
abelian under
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}}
by degree reasons, the differential
d_{\mu\boxplus\rho}
gives rise to a graded Lie algebra structure on the graded vector space
\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g})
via the derived bracket
(
10
).

For
T\in\mathrm{Hom}(V,\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\llbracket T,T\right\rrbracket(v_{1},v_{2})=[Tv_{1},Tv_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-T(\rho(Tv_{1})v_{2}),\quad\forall v_{1},v_{2}\in V,

which implies that Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely embedding tensors.
∎

There is a close relationship between the graded Lie algebra
(\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
and the graded Lie algebra
(C^{\bullet}(V,V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
, where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}}
is the Balavoine bracket defined by (
8
).

Define a linear map
\Phi:\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k+1}V,V)
for
k=1,2,\cdots,
by

(11)

\displaystyle\qquad\Phi(f)(u_{1},\cdots,u_{k},u_{k+1})=-\rho(f(u_{1},\cdots,u_{k}))u_{k+1},\,\,\,\,\forall f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g}),~u_{1},\cdots,u_{k+1}\in V.

Proposition 3.5
.

The linear map
\Phi
, defined by (
11
), is a
homomorphism of graded Lie algebras from

(\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
to

(C^{\bullet}(V,V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
.

Proof.

For
\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{m}V,\mathfrak{g})
and
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n}V,\mathfrak{g})
, we have
[\Phi(\theta),\Phi(\phi)]_{\mathsf{B}}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{m+n+1}V,V)
. More precisely, for all
u_{1},\cdots,u_{m+n+1}\in V
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(\theta)\bar{\circ}\Phi(\phi)(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m+1}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,n)}}(-1)^{(k-1)n}(-1)^{\sigma}\Phi(\theta)(u_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(k-1)},\Phi(\phi)(u_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(k+n-1)},u_{k+n}),u_{k+n+1},\cdots,u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,n)}}(-1)^{(k-1)n}(-1)^{\sigma}\rho(\theta(u_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(k-1)},\rho(\phi(u_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(k+n-1)}))u_{k+n},u_{k+n+1},\cdots,u_{m+n}))u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,n)}}(-1)^{mn}(-1)^{\sigma}\rho(\theta(u_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(m)}))\rho(\phi(u_{\sigma(m+1)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(m+n)}))u_{m+n+1}.

For any
\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{(n,m)}
, we can define
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,n)}
by

\sigma(i)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tau(n+i),&1\leq i\leq m;\\
\tau(i-m),&m+1\leq i\leq m+n.\end{array}\right.

Thus, we have
(-1)^{\sigma}=(-1)^{mn}(-1)^{\tau}
.
In fact, the elements of
\mathbb{S}_{(n,m)}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the elements of
\mathbb{S}_{(m,n)}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\Phi(\phi)\bar{\circ}\Phi(\theta)(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,m)}}(-1)^{(k-1)m}(-1)^{\tau}\rho(\phi(u_{\tau(1)},\cdots,u_{\tau(k-1)},\rho(\theta(u_{\tau(k)},\cdots,u_{\tau(k+m-1)}))u_{k+m},u_{k+m+1},\cdots,u_{m+n}))u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{(n,m)}}(-1)^{mn}(-1)^{\tau}\rho(\phi(u_{\tau(1)},\cdots,u_{\tau(n)}))\rho(\theta(u_{\tau(n+1)},\cdots,u_{\tau(m+n)}))u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,m)}}(-1)^{(k-1)m}(-1)^{\sigma}\rho(\phi(u_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(k-1)},\rho(\theta(u_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(k+m-1)}))u_{k+m},u_{k+m+1},\cdots,u_{m+n}))u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,n)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\rho(\phi(u_{\sigma(m+1)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(m+n)}))\rho(\theta(u_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(m)}))u_{m+n+1}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle[\Phi(\theta),\Phi(\phi)]_{\mathsf{B}}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,n)}}(-1)^{(k-1)n}(-1)^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\rho(\theta(u_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(k-1)},\rho(\phi(u_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(k+n-1)}))u_{k+n},u_{k+n+1},\cdots,u_{m+n}))u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle-(-1)^{mn}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,m)}}(-1)^{(k-1)m}(-1)^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\rho(\phi(u_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(k-1)},\rho(\theta(u_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(k+m-1)}))u_{k+m},u_{k+m+1},\cdots,u_{m+n}))u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{mn}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,n)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\rho([\theta(u_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(m)}),\phi(u_{\sigma(m+1)},\cdots,u_{\sigma(m+n)})]_{\mathfrak{g}})u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(\left\llbracket\theta,\phi\right\rrbracket)(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m+n+1}).

Thus,
\Phi
is a homomorphism from
(\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
to

(C^{\bullet}(V,V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The above result gives another intrinsic explanation of Proposition
2.4
. By Lemma
3.2
, Maurer-Cartan elements of the g.l.a

(C^{\bullet}(V,V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
are Leibniz algebra structures on
V
. By Theorem
3.4
, Maurer-Cartan elements of the g.l.a
(\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
are embedding tensors. By Proposition
3.5
,
\Phi
sends Maurer-Cartan elements to Maurer-Cartan elements. Thus, an embedding tensor
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
induces a Leibniz algebra structure on
V
.

3.3.
Cohomology of embedding tensors

First we review representations and cohomologies of Leibniz algebras.
A
representation
of a Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{G}})
is a triple
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
, where
V
is a vector space,
\rho^{L},\rho^{R}:\mathfrak{G}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(V)
are linear maps such that for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{G}
,

\displaystyle\rho^{L}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}})=[\rho^{L}(x),\rho^{L}(y)],\quad\rho^{R}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}})=[\rho^{L}(x),\rho^{R}(y)],\quad\rho^{R}(y)\circ\rho^{L}(x)=-\rho^{R}(y)\circ\rho^{R}(x).

Here
[\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{gl}(V)\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is the commutator Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
.
It is straightforward to see that

4.
The controlling
L_{\infty}
-algebra of Lie-Leibniz triples

In this section, we apply T. Voronov’s higher derived bracket to construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra that characterizes Lie-Leibniz triples as Maurer-Cartan elements.

4.1.
L_{\infty}
-algebras and higher derived brackets

Let
V^{\bullet}=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}V^{k}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space.
We will denote by
\mathsf{S}(V^{\bullet})
the symmetric algebra of
V^{\bullet}
. That is,

\mathsf{S}(V^{\bullet}):=\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})/I,

where
\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})
is the tensor algebra and
I
is the
2
-sided ideal of
\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})
generated by all homogeneous elements of the form

x\otimes y-(-1)^{xy}y\otimes x.

We will write
\mathsf{S}(V^{\bullet})=\oplus_{i=0}^{+\infty}\mathsf{S}^{i}(V)
.
Moreover, we denote the reduced tensor algebra and reduced symmetric algebra by
\bar{\mathsf{T}}V^{\bullet}:=\oplus_{i=1}^{+\infty}\mathsf{T}^{i}(V^{\bullet})
and
\bar{\mathsf{S}}(V^{\bullet}):=\oplus_{i=1}^{+\infty}\mathsf{S}^{i}(V^{\bullet})
respectively. Denote the product of homogeneous elements
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\in V^{\bullet}
in
\mathsf{S}^{n}(V^{\bullet})
by
v_{1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{n}
. The degree of
v_{1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{n}
is by definition the sum of the degrees of
v_{i}
. For a permutation
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\in V^{\bullet}
, the Koszul sign
\varepsilon(\sigma)=\varepsilon(\sigma;v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})\in\{-1,1\}
is defined by

\displaystyle v_{1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{n}=\varepsilon(\sigma;v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})v_{\sigma(1)}\odot\cdots\odot v_{\sigma(n)}.

The desuspension operator
s^{-1}
changes the grading of
V^{\bullet}
according to the rule
(s^{-1}V^{\bullet})^{i}:=V^{i+1}
. The degree
-1
map
s^{-1}:V^{\bullet}\,\rightarrow\,s^{-1}V^{\bullet}
is defined by sending
v\in V^{\bullet}
to its copy
s^{-1}v\in s^{-1}V^{\bullet}
.

The notion of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra was introduced by Stasheff in
[
48
]
. See
[
25
,
26
]
for more details.

Definition 4.1
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space

\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}^{k}
equipped with a collection

(k\geq 1)
of linear maps
l_{k}:\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}
of degree
1
with the property that, for any homogeneous elements
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}
, we have

(i)

(graded symmetry)
for every
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
,

l_{n}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})=\varepsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})l_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}).

(ii)

(generalized Jacobi identity)
for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i)}}\varepsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})l_{n-i+1}(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})=0.

Remark 4.2
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on a graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}
is equivalent to a codifferential on the cofree conilpotent
cocommutative coalgebra
\bar{\mathsf{S}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet})
.

Definition 4.3
.

The set of
Maurer-Cartan elements
of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet},\{l_{i}\}_{i=1}^{+\infty})
is the set of those
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{0}
satisfying the Maurer-Cartan equation

\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k}(\alpha,\cdots,\alpha)=0.

Let
\alpha
be a Maurer-Cartan element. Define
l_{k}^{\alpha}:\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}

(k\geq 1)
by

(15)

\displaystyle l_{k}^{\alpha}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{n!}l_{k+n}(\underbrace{\alpha,\cdots,\alpha}_{n},x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}).

Proposition 4.4
.

(
[
16
]
)

With the above notation,
(\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet},\{l_{k}^{\alpha}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})

L_{\infty}
-algebra. The
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet},\{l_{k}^{\alpha}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
is called the
twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra
.

In the sequel, we recall T. Voronov’s derived brackets
[

, which is a useful tool to construct explicit
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Definition 4.5
.

V
-data consist of a quadruple
(L,\mathfrak{h},P,\Delta)
where

\bullet

(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a graded Lie algebra,

\bullet

is an abelian graded Lie subalgebra of
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])

\bullet

and kernel is a graded Lie subalgebra of

\bullet

(

Let

\displaystyle l_{1}(s^{-1}x,a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-s^{-1}[\Delta,x],P(x+[\Delta,a])),

\displaystyle P[\cdots[[\Delta,a_{1}],a_{2}],\cdots,a_{k}],\quad k\geq 2.

\mathfrak{h}
and

are homogeneous elements of

\displaystyle l_{k}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})=P\underbrace{[\cdots[[}_{k}\Delta,a_{1}],a_{2}],\cdots,a_{k}],\quad\mbox{for }~a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}\in\mathfrak{h}.

-algebras were constructed using the above method to study simultaneous deformations of morphisms between Lie algebras in

4.2.
The controlling

-algebra of Lie-Leibniz triples

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and

be two vector spaces. Then we have a graded Lie

Proposition 4.7
.

We have
V
-data
(L,\mathfrak{h},P,\Delta)
as follows:

\bullet

(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
is given by
\big(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}}\big)

\bullet

the abelian graded Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
is given by

(17)

\mathfrak{h}:=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n+1}V,\mathfrak{g});

\bullet

is the projection onto the subspace
\mathfrak{h}
;

\bullet

\Delta=0
.

Consequently, we obtain an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}L\oplus\mathfrak{h},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})

are given by

\displaystyle l_{1}(s^{-1}Q,\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(Q),

\displaystyle l_{2}(s^{-1}Q,s^{-1}Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{Q}s^{-1}[Q,Q^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{B}},

\displaystyle l_{k}(s^{-1}Q,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k-1})

\displaystyle P[\cdots[Q,\theta_{1}]_{\mathsf{B}},\cdots,\theta_{k-1}]_{\mathsf{B}},

(L,[\cdot,\cdot])

is the projection onto
\mathfrak{h}

. It is also straightforward to see that the kernel of

is a graded Lie subalgebra of
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])

The other conclusions follows immediately from Theorem

By Theorem

.

With above notations,

-algebra, where
l_{i}
are given by

\displaystyle l_{2}(s^{-1}Q,s^{-1}Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{Q}s^{-1}[Q,Q^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{B}},

\displaystyle l_{k}(s^{-1}Q,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P[\cdots[Q,\theta_{1}]_{\mathsf{B}},\cdots,\theta_{k-1}]_{\mathsf{B}},

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and

be two vector spaces,
\mu\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes V,V)
and

((\mathfrak{g},\mu),(V;\rho),T)
is a Lie-Leibniz triple if and only if
(s^{-1}(\mu\boxplus\rho),T)
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}L^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{h},\{l_{i}\}_{i=1}^{+\infty})
given in Proposition

.

Proof.

Let
(s^{-1}(\mu\boxplus\rho),T)
be a Maurer-Cartan element of
(s^{-1}L^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{h},\{l_{i}\}_{i=1}^{+\infty})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k}\Big((s^{-1}(\mu\boxplus\rho),T),\cdots,(s^{-1}(\mu\boxplus\rho),T)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2!}l_{2}\Big((s^{-1}(\mu\boxplus\rho),T),(s^{-1}(\mu\boxplus\rho),T)\Big)+\frac{1}{3!}l_{3}\Big((s^{-1}(\mu\boxplus\rho),T),(s^{-1}(\mu\boxplus\rho),T),(s^{-1}(\mu\boxplus\rho),T)\Big)

\displaystyle(0,0).

[\mu\boxplus\rho,\mu\boxplus\rho]_{\mathsf{B}}=0,\quad[[\mu\boxplus\rho,T]_{\mathsf{B}},T]_{\mathsf{B}}=0.

5.
Cohomology of Lie-Leibniz triples and applications

In this section, we introduce a cohomology theory of Lie-Leibniz
triples and justify it by using it to classify infinitesimal
deformations and central extensions.

5.1.
Regular cohomology of Lie-Leibniz triples and infinitesimal deformations

In this subsection, first we recall the cohomology of a
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair, and then we introduce a regular cohomology of a Lie-Leibniz triple. Finally, we use the second cohomology group to characterize infinitesimal deformations of a Lie-Leibniz triple.

Let
((\mathfrak{g},\mu),(V;\rho))
be a
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair. Define the set of
0
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},\rho)
to be
0
. For
n\geq 1
, we define the set of
n
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho)
by

\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho)=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes V,V).

Define the coboundary operator
\delta:\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho)\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{C}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},\rho)
by

(19)

\delta f=(-1)^{n-1}[\mu\boxplus\rho,{f}]_{\mathsf{B}}.

By Theorem
3.3
,
\delta^{2}=0
. Thus we obtain a cochain complex
(\oplus_{n}\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho),\delta)
.

Definition 5.1
.

The cohomology of the cochain complex
(\oplus_{n}\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho),\delta)
is called the
cohomology of the
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair
.

Now we give the precise formula of the coboundary operator
\delta
. Write
f=(f_{\mathfrak{g}},f_{V})
and
\delta f=\Big((\delta f)_{\mathfrak{g}},(\delta f)_{V}\Big)
, where
f_{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
,
f_{V}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes V,V)
,
(\delta f)_{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and
(\delta f)_{V}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}\otimes V,V)
. Then we have

(\delta f)_{\mathfrak{g}}=(-1)^{n-1}[{\mu},{f}_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathsf{B}}=\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}f_{\mathfrak{g}},

where
\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is the
Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator
of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
, and
(\delta f)_{V}
is given by

\displaystyle(\delta f)_{V}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},v)=(-1)^{n-1}[\mu\boxplus\rho,f]_{\mathsf{B}}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\Big((\mu\boxplus\rho)\bar{\circ}f-(-1)^{n-1}f\bar{\circ}(\mu\boxplus\rho)\Big)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{i}f_{V}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{j-1},[x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{n},v)+(-1)^{n-1}\rho(f_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}))v

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\Big(\rho(x_{i})f_{V}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x}_{i},\cdots,x_{n},v)-f_{V}\big(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x}_{i},\cdots,x_{n},\rho(x_{i})v\big)\Big),

for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
v\in V.

Now we are ready to define the cohomology of a Lie-Leibniz triple. Let
((\mathfrak{g},\mu),(V;\rho),T)
be a Lie-Leibniz triple, i.e.
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu)
and
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is an embedding tensor. Define the set of
0
-cochains

6.3.
Leibniz
∞
-algebras and
L_{\infty}
-algebras

There is a procedure to associate an

L_{\infty}
-algebra to a Leibniz algebra
[
24
]
. In this section, we extend this
construction to a functor from the category of
Leibniz
∞
-algebras to that of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. Thus
we arrive at a functor from the category of homotopy embedding tensors
to that of
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Let
V^{\bullet}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space. Then the tensor
algebra
(\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet}),\otimes)
is a free graded unital associative algebra.
The freeness implies the uniqueness of the graded unital algebra morphism
\triangle^{\mathsf{cosh}}:\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})\,\rightarrow\,\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})\otimes\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})
such that

\triangle^{\mathsf{cosh}}(x)=1\otimes x+x\otimes 1,\quad\forall x\in V^{\bullet}.

More precisely,
\triangle^{\mathsf{cosh}}
is given by

\displaystyle\bigtriangleup^{\mathsf{cosh}}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i)}}\varepsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\big(x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(i)}\big)\otimes\big(x_{\sigma(i+1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(n)}\big).

It is immediate to check that it is coassociative
and counital. Hence
(\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet}),\otimes,\triangle^{\mathsf{cosh}})
is a
graded bialgebra. We call
(\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet}),\otimes,\triangle^{\mathsf{cosh}})

the (graded) coshuffle bialgebra.

Let
\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet})
be the free graded Lie algebra generated by the graded
vector space
V^{\bullet}
. In fact,
\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet})
is the
intersection of all the Lie subalgebras of the (graded) commutator Lie algebra

\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})_{\mathsf{Lie}}=(\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{C})
containing
V^{\bullet}
. Note that the space of
primitive elements of
(\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet}),\otimes,\triangle^{\mathsf{cosh}})
is

\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet})
. Thus the grading on
\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})
induces a natural grading on

\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet})
making it a graded Lie algebra. We have an embedding
V^{\bullet}\subset\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet})
of
graded vector spaces. This induces
\mathfrak{i}:\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})\,\rightarrow\,\mathsf{T}(\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet}))
a grading preserving inclusion of graded
coshuffle bialgebras. Recall that the universal enveloping algebra

\mathrm{U}(\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet}))=\mathsf{T}(\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet}))/I_{\mathrm{U}}
, where the two sided ideal
I_{\mathrm{U}}
is generated
by
a\otimes b-b\otimes a-[a,b]
. Moreover, we have

\triangle^{\mathsf{cosh}}(I_{\mathrm{U}})\subset I_{\mathrm{U}}\otimes\mathsf{T}(\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet}))+\mathsf{T}(\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet}))\otimes I_{\mathrm{U}}.

Thus
I_{\mathrm{U}}
is a homogeneous ideal of the bialgebra
(\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet}),\otimes,\triangle^{\mathsf{cosh}})
.
Then the natural projection
\mathfrak{p}:\mathsf{T}(\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet}))\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{U}(\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet}))
is a surjective homomorphism of graded
bialgebras.

Lemma 6.13
.

With the above notations,
\Phi=\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathfrak{i}:\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{U}(\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet}))
is an isomorphism of graded bialgebras. More precisely, the isomorphism
\Phi
is given by

\displaystyle\Phi(1)=1,\quad\Phi(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})=v_{1}*\cdots*v_{n},~\forall v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\in V^{\bullet},

where
*
denotes the multiplication in

\mathrm{U}(\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet}))
.

Proof.

Since
\Phi
is a homomorphism of graded bialgebras, we only need to prove that
\Phi
is an isomorphism. Let
A
be a unital associative algebra,
A_{\mathsf{Lie}}
the commutator Lie algebra of
A
and
f:\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet})\,\rightarrow\,A_{\mathsf{Lie}}
a Lie algebra homomorphism. Since
\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})
is a free graded unital associative algebra, we have a unique associative algebra homomorphism
\bar{f}:\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})\,\rightarrow\,A
such that
f=\bar{f}\circ i
, where
i:\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet})\,\rightarrow\,\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})_{\mathsf{Lie}}
is the inclusion of graded Lie algebras. Thus
\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})
satisfies the universal property of the universal enveloping algebra of the free graded Lie algebra
\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet})
. Set
A=\mathrm{U}(\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet}))
, by the universal property, we deduce that
\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathfrak{i}:\mathsf{T}(V^{\bullet})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{U}(\mathsf{Lie}(V^{\bullet}))
is an isomorphism of graded associative algebras. Thus,

\Phi
is an isomorphism of bialgebras.
∎

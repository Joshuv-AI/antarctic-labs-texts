Transcendental solution to linear coefficient non-homogeneous second order recurrence relation with constant non-homogenity.
Jens Walter Fischer ♠♣

Abstract
. Second order recurrence relations of real numbers arise form various applications in discrete time dynamical systems as well as in the context on Markov chains. Solutions to the recurrence relations are fully defined by the first two initial values as well as the recurrence formula. We calculate in this work explicitly as a function of a 1 a_{1} the minimal positive solution ( a i ) i ∈ ℕ (a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}} to non-homogeneous second order recurrence relation with affine coefficients when the non-homogeneity is constant and negative, and the first initial value equals a 0 = 0 a_{0}=0 . We show that rational coefficients lead to a sequence of transcendental numbers. Additionally, we prove that this sequence is the only bounded solution when varying a 1 a_{1} , converges to 0 0 and obtain the convergence speed in O ⁡ ( i − 1 ) O(i^{-1}) . We comment in the last section further on the choice of rational parameters in the recurrence relation and we make a link to the impossibility of obtaining computer based visualizations of the minimal positive solution ( a i ) i ∈ ℕ (a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}} .

1.
Introduction

Non-homogeneous second order recurrence relations with constant non-homogenity of sequences of real numbers
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
have the general form

\begin{cases}(a_{0},\,a_{1})=(q,a)\\
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}a_{i+1}=\alpha+\tilde{\omega}_{i}a_{i}-\tilde{\mu}_{i}a_{i-1},&i\geq 1\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
q,a,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
(\tilde{\lambda}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(\tilde{\mu}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(\tilde{\omega}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
sequences of real numbers. Minimal positive solutions to second order recurrence relations come into play, when considering a family of sequences
\mathcal{A}=\left\{\left(a_{i}^{(a)}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|a\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\right\}
defined by the same recurrence relation (
1.1
) but with only the first initial value
a_{0}^{(a)}\geq 0
being fixed and the second one
a_{1}^{(a)}=a
being variable but positive. A minimal positive solution
(\hat{a}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of the recurrence relation (
1.1
) then satisfies
\hat{a}_{i}>0
for all
i\geq 1
and for any other sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{A}
with
a_{i}>0
for all
i\geq 1
also
a_{i}\geq\hat{a}_{i}
is satisfied for all
i\geq 1
. In particular, we find a link to transcendental numbers.

Definition 1.1

(Algebraic and Transcendent Numbers,
[
Ono90
]
)
.

A number
z\in\mathbb{C}
is called algebraic, if it is a non-zero root of some rational polynomial
p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}q_{n}z^{n}
, i.e.,
q_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
n=0,\ldots,N
. Otherwise, the number
z
is called a transcendental number.

In what follows, we specify the recurrence relation of interest for us in this work. We investigate the behavior of the sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by the recurrence relation (
1.1
) assuming
q=0
,
a>0
,
\alpha\leq 0
and write
\alpha=-\xi
for some
\xi\geq 0
and
\tilde{\omega}_{i}>\tilde{\lambda}_{i}+\tilde{\mu}_{i}
. Due to this assumption we can rewrite
\tilde{\omega}_{i}=\tilde{\gamma}_{i}+\tilde{\lambda}_{i}+\tilde{\mu}_{i}
for some positive
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
and for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. From this we obtain the following form of the recurrence relation (
1.1
)

\begin{cases}(a_{0},\,a_{1})=(0,a)\\
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}a_{i+1}=-\xi+(\tilde{\lambda}_{i}+\tilde{\mu}_{i}+\tilde{\gamma}_{i})a_{i}-\tilde{\mu}_{i}a_{i-1},&i\geq 1.\end{cases}

(1.2)

For all quantitative results in this work, in particular, the rates of convergences for the the sequences resulting from (
1.2
) we assume the following form of the concerned sequences
(\tilde{\lambda}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(\tilde{\mu}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(\tilde{\omega}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Assumption 1.2
.

Let

(1)

\mathfrak{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
,
\gamma,\lambda,\mu>0
,
\xi\geq 0
,

(2)

\tilde{\gamma}_{i}=\gamma\,(i+\mathfrak{n}),\tilde{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda\,(i+\mathfrak{n})
and
\tilde{\mu}_{i}=\mu\,(i+\mathfrak{n})
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

We discussed generalizations under reduced assumptions on the coefficients in the Outlook.

2.
Results on the minimal positive solution

We make a distinction between two cases, which behave qualitatively completely differently, the case
\xi=0
and the case
\xi>0
. The following Theorem considers the case
\xi=0
and describes fully the sequence defined in (
1.2
). The case
\xi=0
corresponds effectively the homogeneous case and is, therefore, easily treatable and can be covered with straight forward arguments. We need them as preliminary results in the following section. The case
\xi>0
, which we consider in this work, is more intricate but we obtain, nonetheless, the explicit form of all solutions. In particular, we obtain the following result on transcendental solutions which correspond to minimal positive solutions of Equation (
1.2
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Denote by
\hat{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
the initial value
a_{1}
, such that the resulting solution is the positive minimal solution to (
1.2
).
Suppose
\gamma,\lambda,\mu>0
and let
\underset{\bar{}}{a}<\bar{a}
be the distinct real zeros of the polynomial

X\mapsto\mu X^{2}-(\lambda+\mu+\gamma)X+\lambda.

Under assumption
1.2
, either
\gamma,\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Q}
or
\hat{a}\in\mathbb{Q}
but not both.

Theorem
2.1
is based on our complete analysis of the solutions to (
1.2
), in which we find the necessary and sufficient condition for the minimal positive solution as well as a complete description of the limit behavior of the minimal positive solution as
i\to\infty
as well as for all other solutions.

Theorem 2.2
.

Denote by
\underset{\bar{}}{a},\bar{a}\in(0,1)
with
\underset{\bar{}}{a}<\bar{a}
the real zeros of the polynomial

X\mapsto\mu X^{2}-(\lambda+\mu+\gamma)X+\lambda.

Under Assumption
1.2
any solution
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
to equation (
1.2
) with initial value
(0,x)
where
x>0
has the form

a_{i}=\lambda\,x\,c\left(\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\dfrac{c\xi}{k+\mathfrak{n}}\left(-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i-k}}+\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i-k}}\right),

(2.1)

with
c=(\sqrt{(\lambda+\mu+\gamma)^{2}-4\lambda\mu})^{-1}
.

Since a sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by a recurrence relation of order
2
is fully defined by its initial values
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
and having fixed
a_{0}=0
, the minimality of the solution to (
1.2
) is solely dependents on
a_{1}
. The following Theorem
2.3
gives explicitly the form of said initial value and shows the behavior of
a_{i}
as
i\to\infty
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Under Assumption
1.2
and defining

\hat{a}:=\dfrac{\xi}{\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{k}}{\mathfrak{n}+k}-\mathfrak{n}^{-1}\right)

(2.2)

the sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
1.2
) with initial value
(a_{0},a_{1})=(0,\hat{a})
is the minimal positive solution to (
1.2
) and
a_{i}=\mathcal{O}(i^{-1})
as
i\to\infty
.

Indeed, the proof of this result leads to the conclusion that the only initial value
a_{1}
for which the solution to (
1.2
) is bounded. The value
\hat{a}
separates the real line into initial values
a_{1}^{\prime}<\hat{a}
for which
a_{i}^{\prime}\to-\infty
as
i\to\infty
and values
a_{1}^{\prime}>\hat{a}
for which
a_{i}^{\prime}\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
.

We discuss, in the following section, the proofs of the Theorems
3.1
to
2.3
as well as additional properties and supplementary results.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under Assumption
1.2

\xi=0

the sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by the recurrence relation (
1.2
) with
\xi=0
.

\begin{cases}(a_{0},\,a_{1})=(0,a)\\
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}a_{i+1}=(\tilde{\lambda}_{i}+\tilde{\mu}_{i}+\tilde{\gamma}_{i})a_{i}-\tilde{\mu}_{i}a_{i-1},&i\geq 1.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Then
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is non-decreasing and
\underset{i\to\infty}{\lim}a_{i}=\infty
.

Indeed, we can quantify the speed of divergence for the sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
defined in Proposition
3.1
by the following Proposition
3.2
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Under Assumption
1.2
with
\xi=0
consider for
a>0
the solution
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
to the recurrence relation (
3.1
) with initial condition
(0,a)
. Then the sequence
(a_{i}a_{i+1}^{-1})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to the smaller real zero
\underset{\bar{}}{a}\in(0,1)
of the polynomial

X\mapsto\mu X^{2}-(\lambda+\mu+\gamma)X+\lambda.

We start with the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Note that (
3.1
) is equivalent to

\tilde{\lambda}_{i}(a_{i+1}-a_{i})=\tilde{\mu}_{i}(a_{i}-a_{i-1})+\tilde{\gamma}_{i}(a_{i}-a_{0}).

First,
a_{1}-a_{0}=a>0
by assumption. Fix
i\geq 1
and suppose that for
j\leq i-1
the inequality
a_{j+1}-a_{j}\geq 0
holds. Hence, in particular,
a_{i}\geq 0
. Moreover

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{i}(a_{i+1}-a_{i})=\tilde{\mu}_{i}(a_{i}-a_{i-1})+\tilde{\gamma}_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}(a_{j}-a_{j-1})\geq 0.

By induction
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is non-decreasing and, thus,
a_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, knowing now that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
it holds
a_{i}\geq a_{i-1}\geq 0
we obtain
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}a_{i+1}\geq(\tilde{\lambda}_{i}+\tilde{\gamma}_{i})a_{i}
. Hence, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\displaystyle a_{i+1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}+\tilde{\gamma}_{i}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}a_{i}=\left(1+\dfrac{\gamma}{\lambda}\right)a_{i}\geq\ldots\geq\left(1+\dfrac{\gamma}{\lambda}\right)^{i}a

and, thus,
\underset{i\to\infty}{\lim}a_{i}=\infty
with at least a geometric rate.
∎

Indeed, the proof can be extend to any case where
\inf_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}\,\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{-1}>C
for some positive constant
C>0
. The assumption on the explicit form of the rates can, therefore, be loosened.

Next, we consider the proof of Proposition
3.2
, exploiting the form of the coefficients given by Assumption
1.2
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

It holds for
i\geq 2
under Assumption
1.2

\displaystyle a_{i-1}a_{i}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\tilde{\lambda}_{i-1}a_{i-1}}{(\tilde{\lambda}_{i-1}+\tilde{\gamma}_{i-1}+\tilde{\mu}_{i-1})a_{i-1}-\tilde{\mu}_{i-1}a_{i-2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda a_{i-1}}{(\lambda+\gamma+\mu)a_{i-1}-\mu\,a_{i-2}}.

Set
z_{i}=a_{i}a_{i+1}^{-1}
for
i\geq 1
we therefore have

\displaystyle z_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda+\gamma+\mu},\qquad z_{i}=\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda+\gamma+\mu-\mu z_{i-1}}.

Consider the map
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
defined by
\phi(x)=\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda+\gamma+\mu-\mu x}
. Since
0<\phi(0)<\phi(1)<1
and
\phi
is strictly increasing
\phi
has a unique fixed point
\underset{\bar{}}{a}\in(0,1)
such that
z_{i}\to\underset{\bar{}}{a}
as
i\to\infty
and we obtain
\lambda=(\lambda+\mu+\gamma)\underset{\bar{}}{a}-\mu\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{2}
. The value
\underset{\bar{}}{a}<1
is thus given by

\underset{\bar{}}{a}=\dfrac{\lambda+\mu+\gamma-\sqrt{(\lambda+\mu+\gamma)^{2}-4\lambda\mu}}{2\mu}.

∎

let
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a solution to (

). Then there is at most one initial value
(0,a)

Proof.

Consider two solutions
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

1.2
) for initial conditions
(0,a)
and

The following lemmas yield step by step an initial value

Lemma 3.4
.

Under Assumption
1.2
consider the solution
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
to the recurrence relation (
1.2
). There exists a value
\hat{a}>0
such that,

(1)

if
a_{1}<\hat{a}
,
\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}=-\infty
,

(2)

if
a_{1}>\hat{a}
,
\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}=+\infty
.

Proof.

Consider all unbounded solutions
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
to (
1.2
). Recall that for solutions
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(a_{i}^{\prime})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of (
1.2
) with initial values
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

we define the sequence
(\Delta_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}=(a_{i}-a_{i}^{\prime})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. Since
(\Delta_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is by Proposition
3.2
non-decreasing we obtain that if for two initial values

the inequality
a_{1}=x>x^{\prime}=a_{1}^{\prime}
holds then
a_{i}>a_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i\geq 1
.

Consider the case where there is a

such that
a_{i_{0}}<0
and
a_{i_{0}-1}\geq 0
. Note that if there is a
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(a_{i_{0}},a_{i_{0}-1})
may always be found since
a_{0}=0
. Then, because
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}>0
for all

, we have

\tilde{\lambda}_{i_{0}}(a_{i_{0}+1}-a_{i_{0}})=-\xi+\tilde{\gamma}_{i_{0}}a_{i_{0}}+\tilde{\mu}_{i_{0}}(a_{i_{0}}-a_{i_{0}-1})<0.

(3.2)

Thus,
a_{i_{0}+1}<a_{i_{0}}<0
and, because
\tilde{\gamma}_{i_{0}}<\tilde{\gamma}_{i_{0}+1}
, we have

Hence, inductively we see that starting from

the sequence
(a_{i})_{i\geq{i_{0}}}
is decreasing and negative. In particular, multiplying the recurrence relation (
3.2
) with

we obtain that for all

-a_{i+1}>\dfrac{\xi}{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}+\dfrac{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}+\tilde{\gamma}_{i}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}(-a_{i})=\dfrac{\xi}{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}+\left(1+\dfrac{\gamma}{\lambda}\right)(-a_{i})

(3.3)

such that

diverges to
-\infty
as
i\to\infty
. In particular, once the sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

Secondly, consider a positive solution
(a_{i}^{\prime})_{i\geq 0}
of (

). Then, by the unboundedness assumption, it holds that for all

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{I}(a_{I+1}^{\prime}-a_{I}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{I}(a_{I}^{\prime}-a_{I-1}^{\prime})+\tilde{\gamma}_{I}a_{I}^{\prime}-\xi>\tilde{\gamma}_{I}C-\xi>0.

Hence,

and, thus,
\tilde{\gamma}_{I+1}a_{I+1}^{\prime}>\tilde{\gamma}_{I}a_{I}^{\prime}>1
. Furthermore, there is a

a_{I}^{\prime}<C+\varepsilon

such that the resulting solution to (

Lemma 3.5
.

Under Assumption
1.2
and with
q=\lambda+\mu+\gamma
consider the solution
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
to the recurrence relation (
1.2
) with initial value
(0,a)
for
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Its generating function
\mathcal{E}(z):=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}
satisfies

\mathcal{E}(z)=\dfrac{z\lambda x-z\xi\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}}{\lambda+\mu z^{2}-qz}

(3.4)

within its radius of convergence
R\in[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Set
q=\lambda+\gamma+\mu
and
\tilde{q}_{i}=q(i+\mathfrak{n})
such that (
1.2
) becomes for
i\geq 1
,

\tilde{q}_{i}a_{i}=\tilde{\lambda}_{i}a_{i+1}+\tilde{\mu}_{i}a_{i-1}+\xi.

By exploiting this form of the recurrence relation (
1.2
) for
i\geq 1
we obtain the following.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\tilde{q}_{i}a_{i}\dfrac{z^{i}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\lambda}_{i}a_{i+1}+\tilde{\mu}_{i}a_{i-1}+\xi\right)\dfrac{z^{i}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda}{q}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i+1}z^{i}+\dfrac{\mu}{q}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i-1}z^{i}+\xi\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{z^{i}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda}{qz}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i+1}z^{i+1}+\dfrac{\mu}{q}z\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}+\xi\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{z^{i}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda}{qz}\left(\mathcal{E}(z)-xz\right)+\dfrac{\mu}{q}z\mathcal{E}(z)+\xi\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{z^{i}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}.

Equivalently, we have

\mathcal{E}(z)=\dfrac{z\lambda x-z\xi\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}}{\lambda+\mu z^{2}-qz}.

∎

Lemma 3.6

Under Assumption

and

as well as

Having established a positive radius of convergence we proceed with a direct calculation of the coefficients. Recall that
\underset{\bar{}}{a}<\bar{a}
are the distinct real zeros of the polynomial

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

According to
3.5
and
3.6
the introduced generating function
\mathcal{E}
has a positive radius of convergence and the general form

\mathcal{E}(z)=z\dfrac{f(z)}{g(z)}.

Note first, that by that form we have

\partial_{z}^{i}\mathcal{E}(z)\bigg|_{z=0}=i\,\partial_{z}^{i-1}\left(\dfrac{f(z)}{g(z)}\right)\bigg|_{z=0}=i\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\binom{i-1}{k}\left(\partial_{z}^{k}f(z)\partial_{z}^{i-1-k}(g(z)^{-1})\right)\bigg|_{z=0}.

Set
c=(\sqrt{(\lambda+\mu+\gamma)^{2}-4\lambda\mu})^{-1}
. Then we have for
g(z)=\lambda-qz+\mu z^{2}
the expression

g(z)^{-1}=\dfrac{c}{z-\bar{a}}-\dfrac{c}{z-\underset{\bar{}}{a}},

which gives canonically any derivative of order
n
of
g(z)^{-1}
at
0
, namely,

\partial_{z}^{i}g(z)^{-1}\bigg|_{z=0}=ci!\left(-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i+1}}+\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i+1}}\right).

(3.5)

Considering the numerator
f(z)=\lambda x-\xi\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}
, we find that for
i\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{z}^{i}f(z)\bigg|_{z=0}=-\xi\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\dfrac{z^{k-i}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}k\cdot(k-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(k-i+1)\bigg|_{z=0}=-\xi\dfrac{i!}{i+\mathfrak{n}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\partial_{z}^{i}\mathcal{E}(z)\bigg|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\,\partial_{z}^{i-1}\left(\dfrac{f(z)}{g(z)}\right)\bigg|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\left(\lambda\,x\,(i-1)!c\left(\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.-\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\binom{i-1}{k}\dfrac{k!\,\xi}{k+\mathfrak{n}}c(i-1-k)!\left(-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i-k}}+\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i-k}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i!\left(\lambda\,x\,c\left(\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\dfrac{c\,\xi}{k+\mathfrak{n}}\left(-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i-k}}+\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i-k}}\right)\right)

Since
a_{i}=i!^{-1}\partial_{z}^{i}\mathcal{E}(z)\bigg|_{z=0}
, we find that for any
i\geq 1

a_{i}=\lambda\,x\,c\left(\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\dfrac{c\,\xi}{k+\mathfrak{n}}\left(-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i-k}}+\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i-k}}\right).

∎

To improve readability, we use the following notation. For
z\in\mathbb{R}
with
|z|<1
and
n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 1
we use the notation

\Phi_{n}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{z^{k}}{n+k},

(3.6)

leaning on the Lerch transcendent
\Phi(n,1,z)
, see
[
Ler00
]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Under Assumption
1.2
any sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
1.2
) with initial values
x_{0}=0
and
a_{1}<\hat{a}
where

\hat{a}:=\dfrac{\xi}{\lambda}\left(\Phi_{\mathfrak{n}}(\underset{\bar{}}{a})-\mathfrak{n}^{-1}\right).

(3.7)

tends to
-\infty
as
i\to\infty
.

Proof.

Assume that
a_{1}<\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\left(\Phi_{\mathfrak{n}}(\underset{\bar{}}{a})-\mathfrak{n}^{-1}\right)
, i.e., there is a
\delta>0
such that

a_{1}=\dfrac{\xi}{\lambda}\left(\Phi_{\mathfrak{n}}(\underset{\bar{}}{a})-\mathfrak{n}^{-1}\right)-\delta.

(3.8)

Since
\Phi_{\mathfrak{n}}(\cdot)
is continuous and
\underset{\bar{}}{a}<1
, we find that its left limit for
z\nearrow\underset{\bar{}}{a}
exists such that

\forall\varepsilon>0\,\exists\,\bar{\delta}>0\,\forall z:\;\underset{\bar{}}{a}-z<\bar{\delta}\Rightarrow\Phi_{\mathfrak{n}}(\underset{\bar{}}{a})-\Phi_{\mathfrak{n}}(z)<\varepsilon.

Note that
\mu z^{2}-qz+\lambda>0
for all
z<\underset{\bar{}}{a}
. Choose
\varepsilon=\dfrac{\delta\lambda}{2\xi}
such that for all
z\in[0,\underset{\bar{}}{a})\cap(\underset{\bar{}}{a}-\bar{\delta},\underset{\bar{}}{a})
we obtain by (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{z\left(\xi\Phi_{\mathfrak{n}}(\underset{\bar{}}{a})-\xi\mathfrak{n}^{-1}-\lambda\delta-\xi\Phi_{\mathfrak{n}}(z)+\xi\mathfrak{n}^{-1}\right)}{\lambda+\mu z^{2}-qz}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\dfrac{z\left(\xi\varepsilon-\lambda\delta\right)}{\lambda+\mu z^{2}-qz}=-\dfrac{z\lambda\delta}{2(\lambda+\mu z^{2}-qz)}<0.

Since positivity of the sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
would imply that
\mathcal{E}(z)>0
for all
z>0
we find that for
a_{1}<\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\left(\Phi_{\mathfrak{n}}(\underset{\bar{}}{a})-\mathfrak{n}^{-1}\right)
there is an index
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{i_{0}}<0
. Consequently,
a_{i}
tends to
-\infty
as
i\to\infty
by Lemma
3.4
.
∎

The necessity of
\xi>0
becomes evident in the choice of
\varepsilon
in the proof. In fact, it turns out that the value
\hat{a}
yields the minimal positive solution of (
1.2
) because any other smaller initial value leads to a sequence diverging to
-\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Take
(a_{0},a_{1})=(0,\hat{a})
. In Theorem
2.2
the following identity is given.

\displaystyle a_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\,\hat{a}\,c\left(\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\dfrac{c\xi}{k+\mathfrak{n}}\left(-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i-k}}+\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i-k}}\right)

(3.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{k}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}\,c\xi\left(\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\dfrac{c\xi}{k+\mathfrak{n}}\left(-\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i-k}}+\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i-k}}\right).

Define for
i\geq 1
the sequence
(b_{i})_{i\geq 1}
with
b_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\dfrac{\bar{a}^{k}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\dfrac{1}{k+\mathfrak{n}}\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i-k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\dfrac{\bar{a}^{k}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}=\dfrac{b_{i}}{\bar{a}^{i}}.

Furthermore, it holds

\displaystyle\dfrac{b_{i+1}-b_{i}}{\bar{a}^{i+1}-\bar{a}^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i}(\bar{a}-1)}\dfrac{\bar{a}^{i}}{i+\mathfrak{n}}=\dfrac{1}{(\bar{a}-1)(i+\mathfrak{n})}\to 0,\qquad i\to\infty.

(3.10)

Using
\bar{a}>1
and employing the Stolz-Césaro theorem we find that

\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}^{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\dfrac{\bar{a}^{k}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}\to 0,\;\text{as }i\to\infty,

as well. Thus, the limiting behavior of
(a_{i})_{i\geq 0}
is the same as of

\dfrac{c}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}\left(\lambda\,\hat{a}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\xi\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{k}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}\right)=\dfrac{c}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\dfrac{\xi\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{k}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}\geq\dfrac{c\xi}{i+\mathfrak{n}}.

(3.11)

Secondly, using the integral comparison, it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{k}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}{i+\mathfrak{n}}+\int_{i}^{\infty}\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{s}}{s+\mathfrak{n}}ds

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}{i+\mathfrak{n}}+\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{-\mathfrak{n}}}{i+\mathfrak{n}}\int_{i+\mathfrak{n}}^{\infty}\mathrm{exp}(\log\underset{\bar{}}{a}{s})ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}{i+\mathfrak{n}}+\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}{(i+\mathfrak{n})(-\log\underset{\bar{}}{a})}.

Hence,
\dfrac{c\xi}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{i}}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}^{k}}{k+\mathfrak{n}}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
such that
a_{i}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
and moreover
a_{i}>0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, since otherwise
a_{i}\to-\infty
as
i\to\infty
by Lemma
3.7
. Moreover, by the lower bound from (
3.11
) and the upper bound of order
\mathcal{O}(i^{-1})
it follows that
a_{i}=\mathcal{O}(i^{-1})
as
i\to\infty
. Finally by Lemma
3.4
, every solution starting from a smaller
a_{1}
tends to
-\infty
, such that
(a_{i})_{i\geq 0}
with initial value
(0,\hat{a})
is the minimal positive solution to (
1.2
).
∎

4.
Issues with simulating the minimal positive solution

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
2.1
and to the stability analysis of said recurrence relation with respect to perturbations of the value
\hat{a}
and its implication on simulation results.
To make our point, we recall a preliminary lemma and an important theorem on the nature of algebraic and transcendent numbers. They can be found in
[
Ono90
]
together with the respective proofs.

Lemma 4.1

(Properties Algebraic & Transcendent Numbers,
[
Ono90
]
)
.

The algebraic and transcendental numbers satisfy the

a)

Any
p\in\mathbb{Q}
is algebraic.

b)

The sum of a transcendental number and an algebraic number is transcendental.

c)

The product of a transcendental number by a nonzero algebraic number is transcendental.

Additionally, we employ a theorem on the relation of algebraic and transcendental numbers via the logarithm. It can be found including the proof in
[
Küh15
]
.

Theorem 4.2

(Logarithm of Algebraic Numbers,
[
Küh15
]
)
.

Let
z\neq 1
be an algebraic complex number. Then
\log(z)
is transcendental.

The combination of both, together with the explicit form of solutions, which we obtained previously, leads to the following proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Proof.

Assume
\hat{a}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\gamma,\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Q}
. Thus,
\underset{\bar{}}{a}
is algebraic and, therefore, also
1-\underset{\bar{}}{a}
is algebraic by Lemma
4.1
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\underset{\bar{}}{a})^{i}}{i+\mathfrak{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\underset{\bar{}}{a})^{i+1}}{i+\mathfrak{n}}=\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\underset{\bar{}}{a})^{i}}{i-1+\mathfrak{n}}-\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}{\mathfrak{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\underset{\bar{}}{a})^{i}}{i-2+\mathfrak{n}}-\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}{\mathfrak{n}-1}\right)-\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}{\mathfrak{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}\left(\ldots\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\underset{\bar{}}{a})^{i}}{i+1}-\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}{2}\right)\ldots\right)-\dfrac{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}{\mathfrak{n}}\right).

Consider the term

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\underset{\bar{}}{a})^{i}}{i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\underset{\bar{}}{a})^{i+1}}{i+1}=\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}\int_{0}^{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}\dfrac{1}{1-x}-1dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\underset{\bar{}}{a}}(-\ln(1-\underset{\bar{}}{a}))-1.

By Theorem
4.2
, since
1-\underset{\bar{}}{a}\neq 1
, we obtain that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\underset{\bar{}}{a})^{i}}{i+1}
is transcendent. Due to
\underset{\bar{}}{a}
being algebraic, the term
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\underset{\bar{}}{a})^{i}}{i+\mathfrak{n}}
is transcendent by the previous reasoning and lemma
4.1
. Thus,

\lambda\,\hat{a}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\underset{\bar{}}{a})^{i}}{i+\mathfrak{n}}

is transcendent, but by assumption
\lambda\hat{a}\in\mathbb{Q}
and, therefore, algebraic, such that we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

5.
Outlook

Having found explicit results under Assumption
1.2
nautral extensions come to mind by generalizing the form of the parameters. Indeed, they are twofold. Firstly, considering the most general form of the non-homogenity
\xi
as a function of
i\in\mathbb{N}
, which could be called
\xi_{i}
, give identical qualitative results on the form of the minimal solution by changing the terms
\xi\Phi_{\mathfrak{n}}(z)
to

\Psi_{\mathfrak{n};(\xi_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\xi_{i}z^{i}}{i+\mathfrak{n}}.

This yields identical results as in Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
for the form of the resulting sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. Nonetheless, the quantitative result on the convergence speed cannot be proven in the same way and will depend on the behavior of
(\xi_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. In particular, the link to the natural logarithm used in Equation (
3.12
) is no longer valid. Methods from Analytic Combinatorics as discussed, for example, in
[
FS09
]
might shed light on the question of the link between the limit behaviors of
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\xi_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
when keeping the remaining assumptions in Assumption
1.2
.

On the other hand, using more general forms for
(\tilde{\lambda}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(\tilde{\mu}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\tilde{\gamma}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
will render the calculations more difficult or even unfeasible, as soon as their quotients are not independent of
i
. The particular affine form, which we chose in Assumption
1.2
, is only a first step and a useful when looking for explicit forms of the solution.

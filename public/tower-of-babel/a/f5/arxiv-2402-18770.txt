Rational Cherednik Algebras and Torus Knot Invariants
Xinchun Ma

Abstract
In [ GORS14 ] , the HOMFLY polynomial of the ( m , n ) (m,n) torus knot T m , n T_{m,n} is extracted from the doubly graded character of the finite-dimensional representation L m n \mathrm{L}_{\frac{m}{n}} of the type A n − 1 A_{n-1} rational Cherednik algebra. It is furthermore conjectured in loc. cit. that one can obtain the triply-graded Khovanov-Rozansky homology of T m , n T_{m,n} by considering a certain filtration on L m n \mathrm{L}_{\frac{m}{n}} . In this paper, we show that two of the proposed candidates, the algebraic filtration and the inductive filtration, are equal.

Introduction

1.1
The rational Cherednik algebra and link homology

The HOMFLY polynomial is a link invariant defined by the skein relation in the form of a two-variable polynomial, generalizing the one-variable Alexander polynomial and the Jones polynomial. In the early 2000s, these link polynomials were realized as Euler characteristics of various link homology theories. This process was inspired by the ideas of “categorification”, for a survey, see
[
Kho06
]
. Remarkably, the HOMFLY polynomial was categorified by the Khovanov–Rozansky (KhR) link homology using matrix factorization
[
KR08
]
. Soon after, the construction of the KhR homology was modified using Hochschild homology of Soergel bimodules (hence the notation
\mathrm{H}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{H}
), which revealed the connection between link invariants and Kazhdan-Lusztig theory
[
Kho07
]
. The past twenty years have seen a large variety of incarnations of KhR homology, to name a few: the (co)homology of braid/positroid/Richardson varieties, affine/global Springer theory, coherent sheaves on the Hilbert scheme of points and representation theory of double affine Hecke algebras (for a survey, see
[
GKS23
]
).

This paper will focus on the representation theoretic model of the KhR homology. Let
m,n
be a coprime positive integer pair and let
\mathfrak{h}
denote the
(n-1)
-dimensional standard representation of the symmetric group
S_{n}
. The rational Cherednik algebra
\mathrm{H}_{\frac{m}{n}}
(also called rational DAHA) is a deformation of
D(\mathfrak{h})\ltimes S_{n}
at the parameter
{\frac{m}{n}}
where
D(\mathfrak{h})
is the ring of differential operators on
\mathfrak{h}
. The algebra
\mathrm{H}_{\frac{m}{n}}
has a unique finite-dimensional irreducible representation which we denote by
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{m}{n}}
. Under the action of the Euler field,
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{m}{n}}
decomposes into a direct sum of eigenspaces, allowing us to define the associated
q
-graded Euler characterstic
\mathrm{ch}_{q}(\mathrm{L}_{\frac{m}{n}})=\sum_{i}\dim(\mathrm{L}_{\frac{m}{n}}(i))q^{i}
. In
[
GORS14
]
, the authors recover the HOMFLY polynomials of the
(m,n)
torus knot
T_{m,n}
from the graded character of (the hook
S_{n}
-isotypic components of)
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{m}{n}}
by comparing explicit computational results of both sides:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
GORS14
, Theorem 1.1]

\displaystyle\mathrm{HOMFLY}_{a,q}(T_{m,n})=a^{(n-1)(m-1)}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a^{2i}\mathrm{ch}_{q}\big(\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{n}}(\wedge^{i}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathrm{L}_{c})\big).

(1)

As mentioned above, the HOMFLY polynomial is the doubly graded Euler characteristic of the KhR homology.
In fact, the KhR homology is a triply graded knot invariant whose full characteristic is the so-called “link superpolynomial”. As seen in Theorem
1.1
, the internal
q
-grading of
\mathrm{H}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{H}
corresponds to the weight space decomposition of
\mathrm{L}_{c}
; the Hochschild homological
a
-grading corresponds to the (hook-)isotypic components of
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{m}{n}}
.
The main conjecture of
[
GORS14
]
is that the third usual homological
t
-grading on the KhR homology should be given by a filtration on
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{m}{n}}
:

Conjecture 1.2
.

[
GORS14
, Conjecture 1.2]
There exists a filtration on
\mathrm{L}_{c}
whose associated
t
-grading in (
1
) yields the refined identity

\mathrm{ch}_{a,q,t}(\mathrm{H}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{H}(T_{m,n}))=a^{(n-1)(m-1)}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a^{2i}\mathrm{ch}_{q,t}\big(\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{n}}(\wedge^{i}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathrm{L}_{c})\big).

In
loc. cit.
, three potential candidates for this
t
-grading are proposed and conjectured to coincide:

•

The inductive filtration
F^{\mathrm{ind}}
is defined inductively using the shift functor from
\mathrm{H}_{\frac{m}{n}}
-modules to
\mathrm{H}_{\frac{m}{n}+1}
-modules and the “flipping” isomorphism
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{m}{n}}^{S_{n}}\cong\mathrm{L}_{\frac{n}{m}}^{S_{m}}
.

•

The algebraic filtration
F^{\mathrm{alg}}
is defined by reshuffling weight spaces under the Euler field and orthogonal complements of powers of the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
generated by non-constant symmetric polynomials under the Dunkl form.

•

The geometric filtration
F^{\text{geom}}
is defined as the perverse filtration on the cohomology of a Hitchin fiber isomorphic to the compactified Jacobian of the planar singular curve
y^{m}=x^{n}
.

3.1
The algebraic filtration and the power filtration

3.1.1
The power filtration

Let
p_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{i}
be the power sum symmetric polynomial of degree
i
. Then
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]^{W}=\mathbb{C}[p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{n}]/(p_{1})
.

The following three elements in
\mathrm{H}_{c}
form an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple

\mathbf{e}=\frac{1}{2}p_{2}-\frac{1}{2n}p_{1}^{2}=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2},\ \mathbf{f}=-\Phi_{c}(\mathbf{e})=-\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i<j}(y_{i}-y_{j})^{2},

\mathbf{h}=\frac{1}{2}\sum(x_{i}y_{i}+y_{i}x_{i})-\frac{1}{2n}\left(p_{1}\Phi_{c}(p_{1})+\Phi_{c}(p_{1})p_{1}\right)=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i<j}\bigg((x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})+(y_{i}-y_{j})(x_{i}-x_{j})\bigg)

[\mathbf{h},\mathbf{e}]=2\mathbf{e},\quad[\mathbf{h},\mathbf{f}]=-2\mathbf{f},\quad[\mathbf{e},\mathbf{f}]=\mathbf{h}

Lemma 3.1
.

On
\mathrm{L}_{c}
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{h}
acts by
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})-\mu
where
\mu=\frac{(m-1)(n-1)}{2}
.

Remark 3.2
.

\mu
is the Milnor number of the singular curve
\{x^{m}=y^{n}\}
.

Proof.

We compute that

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i<j}\bigg((x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})-(x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i<j}\bigg(2(x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})+2|S|-\sum_{i<j}\sum_{s\in S}c\langle\alpha_{s},x_{i}-x_{j}\rangle\langle y_{i}-y_{j},\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\rangle s\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i<j}\bigg(2(x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})+\bigg(2|S|-c\bigg({n\choose 2}+{n-2\choose 2}-3\bigg)\sum_{s\in S}s\bigg)\bigg).

Here
S
is the set consisting of all reflections with
|S|={n\choose 2}
and
{n\choose 2}-{n-2\choose 2}=2n-3
is the number of
\alpha_{s}
such that
\langle\alpha_{s},x_{i}-x_{j}\rangle\neq 0
. Moreover,
\langle\alpha_{s},x_{i}-x_{j}\rangle
equals to
2
when
\alpha_{s}=x_{i}-x_{j}
and equals to
\pm 1
when
s=(ik)
or
(jk)
for some
k\neq i,j
. Therefore

\mathbf{h}=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i<j}\bigg(2(x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})+(2|S|-2nc\sum_{s\in S}s)\bigg).

Since
\sum_{s\in S}s
is in the center of the group algebra of
W
, it acts by a scalar on
\mathrm{L}_{c}
, which is
|S|
. So finally

\mathbf{h}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i<j}\bigg((x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})+(1-nc)|S|\bigg)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i<j}\bigg((y_{i}-y_{j})(x_{i}-x_{j})-(1-nc)|S|\bigg).

We conclude the lemma by

\frac{1}{n}(nc-1)|S|=\frac{(m-1)(n-1)}{2}=\mu.\qed

The left action of
\mathbf{h}
gives a decomposition of
\mathrm{L}_{c}
into weight spaces
\bigoplus_{k}\mathrm{L}_{c}(k)
where
\mathrm{L}_{c}(k)=\{v\in\mathrm{L}_{c}|\mathbf{h}v=kv\}
. As a corollary of Lemma
3.1
,
\mathbf{h}\cdot 1=-\mu
. Therefore
1
is a eigenvector vector of minimal lowest weight
-\mu
and
\mathbf{e}^{\mu}
is a eigenvector of maximal highest weight
\mu
.

Note that
[\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{f},x]=[-\mathbf{f},x]=y
, and
[\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{f},y]=-x
. Also, because the action of
\langle\mathrm{ad}(\mathbf{e}),\mathrm{ad}(\mathbf{f}),\mathrm{ad}(\mathbf{h}\rangle)
on
\mathrm{H}_{c}
is locally finite and integrable,
[
BEG03a
, Remark after 3.8]
tells us that the Fourier transform (
6
) can also be expressed by

\displaystyle\Phi_{c}=\mathrm{Ad}(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{i\pi}{2}(\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{f})}):\mathrm{H}_{c}\to\mathrm{H}_{c}.

(13)

This allows us to define

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi}_{c}:\mathrm{L}_{c}\to\mathrm{L}_{c},\quad\phi\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\frac{i\pi}{2}(\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{f})}\phi

(14)

where
\mathrm{e}^{\frac{i\pi}{2}(\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{f})}
acts by left action.
The lemma below gives a concrete characterization of
\overline{\Phi}_{c}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
0\neq\phi\in\mathrm{L}_{c}(k)
. If
k<0
, then
\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\phi)
is a nonzero multiple of
\mathbf{e}^{-k}\phi
; if
k>0
, then
\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\phi)
is a nonzero multiple of
\mathbf{f}^{k}\phi
. In particular, we have that
\overline{\Phi}_{c}(1)
is a nonzero multiple of
\mathbf{e}^{\mu}
.

Proof.

Since
\Phi_{c}(\mathbf{e})=-\mathbf{f}
and
\Phi_{c}(\mathbf{f})=-\mathbf{e}
, we know that
\overline{\Phi}_{c}
preserves
\langle\mathbf{e},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{h}\rangle
-subrepresentations of
\mathrm{L}_{c}
. On the other hand, because
\Phi_{c}(\mathbf{h})=-\mathbf{h}
, for any
\phi\in\mathrm{L}_{c}
we have
\mathbf{h}\Phi_{c}(\phi)=-\Phi_{c}\mathbf{h}(\phi)
.
As a result

\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\mathrm{L}_{c}(k))=\mathrm{L}_{c}(-k)
. Finally, notice that when
\phi\in\mathrm{L}_{c}(k)
with
k<0
, it follows that
0\neq\mathbf{e}^{-k}\phi\in\mathrm{L}_{c}(-k)\cap\langle\mathbf{e},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{h}\rangle\phi
, which is one-dimensional. Hence
\mathbf{e}^{-k}\phi=C\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\phi)
for some nonzero constant
C
. The argument is similar for the case when
k>0
.
∎

Lemma 3.5

\phi\in(\mathfrak{a}^{i})^{\perp_{c}}\subset\mathrm{L}_{c}
if and only if for all

“

Lemma 3.6
.

For
\phi\in[(\mathfrak{a}^{i+1})^{\perp_{c}}]^{W}

3.1.2
The Kazhdan filtration

Definition 3.7
.

[
Gin18
, 3.2]

The Kazhdan filtration
K^{F}
on a vector space

associated to an ascending
\mathbb{Z}
-filtration

\mathbb{Z}
-grading
V=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}V(k)

Definition 3.8
.

Define the algebraic filtration
F^{\mathrm{alg}}
on
\mathrm{L}_{c}
to be the Kazhdan filtration associated to
F^{\mathfrak{a}}
and the
\mathbf{h}
-grading, i.e.

Lemma 3.9
.

F^{\mathrm{alg}}=F^{\mathrm{alg}^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

For fixed
i
and
k
we have

F_{i}^{\mathrm{alg}}(k)=\sum_{2j+k\leq i}\overline{\Phi}_{c}((\mathfrak{a}^{j+1})^{\perp_{c}})(k)=\overline{\Phi}_{c}((\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil\frac{i-k+1}{2}\rceil})^{\perp_{c}})(k),

F_{i}^{\mathrm{alg}^{\prime}}(k)=(\sum_{k<2j-i}(\mathfrak{a}^{j})(k))^{\perp_{c}}=(\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil\frac{i+k+1}{2}\rceil})^{\perp_{c}}(k).

Take
P\in\Phi_{c}[(\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil\frac{i-k+1}{2}\rceil})^{\perp_{c}}]
a polynomial in
y_{1}-y_{2},\dots,y_{n-1}-y_{n}
of degree

. Take
\phi\in\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil\frac{i+k+1}{2}\rceil}
of degree
\mu+k
so that
\phi\in\mathrm{L}_{c}(k)
. Then

(\phi,P\mathbf{e}^{\mu})_{c}=(\Phi_{c}(P),\Phi_{c}(\phi)\mathbf{e}^{\mu})_{c}.

As a result
F_{i}^{\mathrm{alg}}(k)=F_{i}^{\mathrm{alg}^{\prime}}(k)
if and only if
\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil\frac{i+k+1}{2}\rceil})(-k)=\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil\frac{i-k+1}{2}\rceil}(-k)
. After change of variables, this latter condition is equivalent to
\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}^{u})(v)=\mathfrak{a}^{u+v}(v)
.

Now by Lemma
3.3
,
\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}^{u})(v)=\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}^{u}(-v))
, which equals to
\mathbf{e}^{v}(\mathfrak{a}^{u}(-v))=(\mathbf{e}^{v}\mathfrak{a}^{u})(v)
when

and equals to
\mathbf{f}^{-v}(\mathfrak{a}^{u}(-v))=(\mathbf{f}^{-v}\mathfrak{a}^{u})(v)
when

. Since
(\mathbf{e}^{v}\mathfrak{a}^{u})(v)
and

\Phi_{c}(\mathfrak{a}^{u})(v)\subset\mathfrak{a}^{u+v}(k),

from which we also have

\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}^{u+v}(v))=(\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}^{u+v}))(-v)\subset\mathfrak{a}^{u}(-v).

Now applying
\overline{\Phi}_{c}
gives us
\mathfrak{a}^{u+v}(v)\subset\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}^{u}(-v))=\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}^{u})(v)
. Therefore
\overline{\Phi}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}^{u})(v)=\mathfrak{a}^{u+v}(v)
and
F^{\mathrm{alg}}=F^{\mathrm{alg}^{\prime}}
.
∎

i\neq j
,
\dim(V_{i}\cap V_{j})=\dim\mathrm{Ker}\begin{pmatrix}J_{i}&J_{j}.\end{pmatrix}

Using elementary matrix transformations,
\begin{pmatrix}J_{i}&J_{j}\end{pmatrix}
can be transformed into

\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1&\cdots&0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
&&\cdots&&\cdots&&\cdots\\
0&0&\cdots&x_{i}^{2n-m}&0&\cdots&x_{i}-x_{j}&x_{j}^{2n-m}\end{pmatrix}

Therefore,
\dim\mathrm{Ker}\begin{pmatrix}J_{i}&J_{j}\end{pmatrix}=\dim\mathrm{Ker}(x_{i}^{2n-m}\ \ x_{i}-x_{j}\ \ \ x_{j}^{2n-m})
.

Since
(x_{i}-x_{j})|(x_{i}^{2n-m}-x_{j}^{2n-m})
, we get

\mathrm{Ker}(x_{i}^{2n-m}\ \ x_{i}-x_{j}\ \ x_{j}^{2n-m})=\mathrm{Ker}(x_{i}^{2n-m}\ \ x_{i}-x_{j}).

Without loss of generality, we may assume
i=1
and
j=2
.

By Lemma
4.9
below, one has
\mathrm{Ker}(x_{1}-x_{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\psi_{j}\mathcal{R}_{n-2}
where

\psi_{j}:=x_{1}^{j}x_{2}^{n-2}+2x_{1}^{j+1}x_{2}^{n-3}+\cdots+(n-j)x_{1}^{n-1}x_{2}^{j-1}\in\mathrm{Ker}(x_{1}-x_{2}).

In particular,
\psi_{m-n},\psi_{m-n+1},\cdots,\psi_{n-1}\in\mathrm{Ker}(x_{1}^{2n-m})
. Since multiplication by
x_{1}^{2n-m}
preserves the basis

x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{a_{n-1}}
(
0\leq a_{i}\leq n-i
) (possibly sending some elements to
0
), we conclude that

\psi_{m-n}\mathcal{R}_{n-2}+\cdots+\psi_{n-1}\mathcal{R}_{n-2}=\mathrm{Ker}(x_{1}^{2n-m})\cap\mathrm{Ker}(x_{1}-x_{2}),

and so
\dim(V_{1}\cap V_{2})=\dim\mathrm{Ker}(J_{1}\ \ J_{2})=\dim(\mathrm{Ker}(x_{1}^{2n-m})\cap\mathrm{Ker}(x_{1}-x_{2}))=(2n-m)(n-2)!
.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{n-2}\subset\mathcal{R}_{n}
be generated by
x_{3}^{a_{3}}x_{4}^{a_{4}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{a_{n-1}}
,
0\leq a_{i}\leq n-i
. For
1\leq j\leq n-1
, write

\psi_{j}:=x_{1}^{j}x_{2}^{n-2}+2x_{1}^{j+1}x_{2}^{n-3}+\cdots+(n-j)x_{1}^{n-1}x_{2}^{j-1}

Then
\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\psi_{j}\mathcal{R}_{n-2}=\mathrm{Ker}(x_{1}-x_{2},\mathcal{R}_{n}\to\mathcal{R}_{n})
.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle(x_{1}-x_{2})(x_{1}^{j}x_{2}^{n-2}+2x_{1}^{j+1}x_{2}^{n-3}+\cdots+(n-j)x_{1}^{n-1}x_{2}^{j-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-x_{1}^{j}x_{2}^{n-1}+x_{1}^{j+1}x_{2}^{n-2}-2x_{1}^{j+1}x_{2}^{n-2}+2x_{1}^{j+2}x_{2}^{n-3}+\cdots-(n-j)x_{1}^{n-1}x_{2}^{j}+(n-j)x_{1}^{n}x_{2}^{j-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-x_{1}^{j}x_{2}^{n-1}-x_{1}^{j+1}x_{2}^{n-2}-\cdots-x_{1}^{n-1}x_{2}^{j}.

The element in the last line belongs to
\mathfrak{a}
by Corollary
4.6
.

Next, consider the basis
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{a_{n-1}}
ordered by lexicographic order on the powers. With respect to this basis, multiplication by
x_{1}
can be expressed by

N\otimes I_{(n-1)!}

where
I_{k}
stands for the
k\times k
identity matrix and

N:=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
&&\cdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0\end{pmatrix}_{n\times n}

Multiplication by
x_{2}
can be expressed by

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&-N^{n-1}\\
I&0&\cdots&0&-N^{n-2}\\
0&I&\cdots&0&-N^{n-3}\\
&&\cdots&&\\
0&0&\cdots&I&-N\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{(n-2)!}

Observe that, using elementary row operations, the following matrix that represents multiplication by
x_{2}-x_{1}

\begin{pmatrix}-N&0&\cdots&0&-N^{n-1}\\
I&-N&\cdots&0&-N^{n-2}\\
0&I&\cdots&0&-N^{n-3}\\
&&\cdots&&\\
0&0&\cdots&I&-2N\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{(n-2)!}

can be transformed to

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&-nN^{n-1}\\
I&0&\cdots&0&-(n-1)N^{n-2}\\
0&I&\cdots&0&-(n-2)N^{n-3}\\
&&\cdots&&\\
0&0&\cdots&I&-2N\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{(n-2)!}

By column operations we transform it to

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&-nN^{n-1}\\
I&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&I&\cdots&0&0\\
&&\cdots&&\\
0&0&\cdots&I&0\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{(n-2)!}

Since rank
(N^{n-1})=1
, we see
\dim(\mathrm{Ker}(x_{1}-x_{2}))=(n-1)(n-2)!
. The lemma follows.

.

\dim(V_{1}\cap(V_{2}+\cdots+V_{i}))\leq(i-1)(2n-m)(n-2)!

Appendix A
Distributive lattices

One can use the language of distributive lattice to further describe the structure captured by (
32
) in Proposition
4.11
.

Following
[
BGS88
]

[
Bez95
]
, we define the following notions:

•

A set of subspaces of vector space
U
is called a lattice if it is closed under taking sum and intersections.

•

A lattice is called distributive if
U_{1}\cap(U_{2}+U_{3})=(U_{1}\cap U_{2})+(U_{1}\cap U_{3})
for any elements
U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}
in the lattice.

•

A
n
-tuple
(U_{1},\cdots,U_{n})
of subspaces in
U
is called distributive if the lattice generated by
U_{1},\cdots,U_{n}
is distributive.

•

A
n
-tuple is called predistributive if all the
(n-1)
-tuples
(U_{1},\dots,\hat{U_{i}},\cdots,U_{n})
,
i=1,\cdots,n
, are distributive.

•

A
n
-tuple is called acyclic if all the
3
-triples
(U_{1}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i},U_{i+1},U_{i+2}+\cdots+U_{n})
,
i=1,\cdots,n-2
, are distributive.

Lemma A.1
.

[
BGS88
, Lemma 4.5.2]

A
n
-tuple
(U_{1},\cdots,U_{n})
of subspaces in
U
is distributive if and only if it is both predistributive and acyclic.

Write
V_{i}=\{(x_{i}^{n-1}\phi,\cdots x_{i}^{m-n}\phi)|\phi\in\mathcal{R}\}\subset\mathcal{R}_{n}^{2n-m}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
.
We will finally show that

Proposition A.2
.

The
n
-tuple
(V_{1},\cdots,V_{n})
is distributive.

To prove this, we first establish the following lemma:

Lemma A.3
.

For any
1\leq j<i
, we have

V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i})=(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap V_{j+1})+(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap V_{j+2})+\cdots+(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap V_{i})

Proof.

Consider the short exact sequences

V_{1}\cap V_{2}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap V_{j+1}\to V_{1}\cap V_{2}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i})\xrightarrow{J_{j+1}}(V_{1}\cap V_{2}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i}))J_{j+1}

\displaystyle[(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i}))J_{j+1}]\cap V_{j+2}\to

\displaystyle(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i}))J_{j+1}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{J_{j+2}}(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i}))J_{j+1}J_{j+2}

\cdots\cdots

[(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i}))J_{j+1}\cdots J_{i-2}]\cap V_{i}\to(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i}))J_{j+1}\cdots J_{i-2}

\xrightarrow{J_{i-1}}(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i}))J_{j+1}\cdots J_{i-1},

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\dim V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i})=

\displaystyle\dim V_{1}\cap V_{2}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap V_{j+1}

\displaystyle+\dim[(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i}))J_{j+1}]\cap V_{j+2}+\cdots

\displaystyle+\dim(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i}))J_{j+1}\cdots J_{i-1}.

On the other hand, for
1\leq j<1
and
1\leq k\leq i-j
,
there are inclusions

\displaystyle[(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i}))J_{j+1}\cdots J_{j+k-1}]\cap V_{j+k}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle[V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap\mathrm{Ker}(J_{j+1}\cdots J_{j+k})]J_{j+1}\cdots J_{j+k-1}\subset V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap V_{j+k}.

As a consequence

\dim V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{i-j}\dim V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap V_{j+k}.

Therefore the inclusion

V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap(V_{j+1}+\cdots+V_{i})\supset(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap V_{j+1})+(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap V_{j+2})+\cdots+(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{j}\cap V_{i})

has to be an equality.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Proposition
A.2

Proof of Proposition
A.2

We show the lemma by induction. The cases of

2

(
[
BGS88

). Our

satisfies this property.

Now assume any
(n-1)
-subtuple of
(V_{1},\cdots,V_{n})
is distributive. In other words,
(V_{1},\cdots,V_{n})
is predistributive. By Lemma
A.1
, it only remains to show that

is acyclic, i.e. all the
3

(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{i},V_{i+1},V_{i+2}+\cdots+V_{n})

\displaystyle V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{i}\cap(V_{i+1}+(V_{i+2}+\cdots+V_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{i}\cap V_{i+1})+(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{i}\cap V_{i+2})+\cdots+(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{i}\cap V_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{i}\cap V_{i+1})+(V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{i}\cap(V_{i+2}+\cdots+V_{n})))

using Lemma

twice.

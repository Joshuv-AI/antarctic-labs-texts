Abstract
. Recently, the visibility property of Kobayashi (almost) geodesics has been used to provide localizations of the Kobayashi distance. In this note, we provide sufficient growth conditions for the Kobayashi distance to obtain new strong multiplicative and additive localization results. Curiously, the conditions we provide are deeply related to the behaviour of the Kobayashi geodesics.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
32F45

1.
Introduction

The Kobayashi distance is a celebrated tool in complex analysis, therefore it is often of interest to study its behaviour. In many cases one may observe localizations of the Kobayashi distance, that is to say, in many occasions the Kobayashi distance of a domain and its subdomains are comparable.

In particular, the visibility property of Kobayashi geodesics (with respect to Euclidean boundary) is first exploited in
[
BNT
]
to give an additive localization result in the case of convexity. Thanks to some important observations, Sarkar
[
Sar
]
removed the convexity assumption from the aforementioned result. To localize the notion of visibility itself,
[
NÖT
]
presented a local version of the additive localization result given in
[
Sar
]
. The papers
[
Sar
,
NÖT
]
also provide multiplicative localization results in the same spirit.

In this article, we introduce the notion of
v
-points and
w
-points in terms of metric properties of the Kobayashi distance arising from the behaviour of geodesics. Our goal is to present strong multiplicative and additive localization results near such points, namely Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
. More explicitly, under our assumptions, the ratio of the Kobayashi distances in a domain and in its suitable subdomains is arbitrarily close to one and their difference is arbitrarily close to zero. We also conclude that due to
[
BNT
, Section 6]
, our results cover various interesting cases including strong pseudoconvexity and more generally
\mathbb{C}
-strictly convexifiability.

2.
Results

Before we state our results, let us recall some terminology.

Unless stated otherwise, in what follows,
\Omega
denotes a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Let
z,w\in\Omega
. The Kobayashi distance
k_{\Omega}
is the largest pseudodistance
not exceeding the Lempert function
l_{\Omega}(z,w):=\tanh^{-1}\tilde{l}_{\Omega}(z,w),

where
\Delta
is the
unit disc and

\tilde{l}_{\Omega}(z,w):=\inf_{\alpha\in\Delta}\{|\alpha|:\exists\varphi\in\mathcal{O}(\Delta,\Omega)\hbox{ with }\varphi(0)=z,\varphi(\alpha)=w\}.

The Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric is defined as

\kappa_{\Omega}(z;v)=\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}\{|\lambda|:\exists\varphi\in\Omega(\Delta,\Omega)\hbox{ with }\varphi(0)=z,\lambda\varphi^{\prime}(0)=v\},

and the Kobayashi-Royden length of an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:I\rightarrow\Omega
is given as

l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma):=\int_{I}\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma(t);\gamma^{\prime}(t))dt.

The Kobayashi pseudodistance is the integrated form of the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric (see
[
Ven2
, Theorem 1.3]
), that is

k_{\Omega}(z,w)=\inf l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma),

where the infimum is taken over all absolutely continuous curves joining
z
to
w
.

Recall that
\Omega
is said to be
hyperbolic
if
k_{\Omega}
is a distance. When studying local properties, global hyperbolicity is often not necessary. This leads us to say that
\Omega
is
hyperbolic near

p\in\partial\Omega
if there exists a neighbourhood
U
of
p
such that

z,w\in\Omega\cap U
,
z\neq w
implies

(1)

k_{\Omega}(z,w)>0.

Another related notion is hyperbolicity at
p\in\partial\Omega
. Recall the definition given in
[
NÖT
]
. If
\Omega
is bounded
\Omega
is

hyperbolic at

p
for any
p\in\partial\Omega
. If
\Omega
is unbounded, then
\Omega
is
hyperbolic at

p\in\partial\Omega
if we have

(2)

\liminf_{z\to p,w\to\infty}l_{\Omega}(z,w)>0.

For
A,B\subset\Omega
denote
k_{\Omega}(A,B):=\inf_{a\in A,b\in B}k_{\Omega}(a,b)
.
If
\Omega
is an unbounded domain, by
[
NÖT
, Proposition 2]
, (
2
) holds if and only if there exists a bounded neighbourhood
U
of
p
, such that for any other two neighbourhoods of
p
satisfying
V^{\prime}\subset\subset U^{\prime}\subset U
we have

(3)

k_{\Omega}(\Omega\cap V^{\prime},\Omega\setminus U^{\prime})>0.

It is worth noting that (
1
) and (
3
) hold for any bounded domain.

Let
\epsilon>0
. In general, geodesics for the Kobayashi distance need not to exist. This motivates us to recall that an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:I\rightarrow\Omega
joining
z
to
w
is said to be an
\epsilon
-geodesic
if we have

(4)

l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma)\leq k_{\Omega}(z,w)+\epsilon.

The existence of
\epsilon
-geodesics for any
\epsilon>0
is an immediate consequence of the fact that the Kobayashi distance is the integrated form of the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric.

Recall now the definition of Gromov product:

(z|w)^{\Omega}_{o}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(k_{\Omega}(z,o)+k_{\Omega}(w,o)-k_{\Omega}(z,w)\right),\quad z,w,o\in\Omega.

We say that
\Omega
satisfies the
Gromov property
at
p\in\partial\Omega
(
[
NÖT
, Definition 5]
)
if for some (hence all)
o\in\Omega
we have

(5)

\limsup_{z\rightarrow p,w\rightarrow q}(z|w)^{\Omega}_{o}<\infty,

and
\Omega
satisfies the
weak Gromov property
at
p\in\partial\Omega
if

(6)

\liminf_{z\rightarrow p,w\rightarrow q}\left(k_{\Omega}(z,w)-k_{\Omega}(z,o)\right)>-\infty\>\>\>\forall o\in\Omega,\>\forall q\neq p.

Obviously, if
\Omega
satisfies the Gromov property at
p,
then it satisfies the weak Gromov property at
p
.

Further, note that geodesics for the Kobayashi distance might not exist in general. On the other, it is clear
that any
\epsilon>0
there exists an
\epsilon
-geodesic
, that is
an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:I\rightarrow\Omega
joining
z
to
w
such that

(7)

l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma)\leq k_{\Omega}(z,w)+\epsilon.

Let us recall that
p\in\partial\Omega
(see
[
NÖT
, Definition 4]
) is said to be a
weakly visible
point if for any bounded neighbourhood
U
of
p
, there exists
V\subset\subset U
such that if
\gamma:I\rightarrow\Omega
is an
\epsilon
-geodesic joining a point in
\Omega\cap V
to a point in
\Omega\setminus U
then
\gamma(I)
intersects a compact set (depending on
\epsilon
)
K_{\epsilon}\subset\subset\Omega
. As a consequence of
[
BNT
, Proposition 2.5]
one can see that satisfying the Gromov property is equivalent to being a weakly visible point.

Definition 1
.

We say that
p\in\partial\Omega
is a
v
-point
if
\Omega
satisfies the weak Gromov property at
p
.

We say that
p\in\partial\Omega
is a
w
-point
if for any neighbourhood
U
of
p
such that
\Omega\setminus U
is non-empty we have

(8)

\lim_{z,w\rightarrow p}\left(\inf_{o\in\Omega\setminus U}(z|w)^{\Omega}_{o}\right)=\infty.

Notice that (
8
) is stronger than the following notion introduced in
[
Nik
]
:

(9)

\liminf_{z,w\rightarrow p}(z|w)^{\Omega}_{o}=\infty\>\>\>\>\>\forall o\in\Omega.

It is easy to see that under the assumption of (
6
), (
8
) and (
9
) are equivalent.

We extend the notion of well-behaved geodesics given in
[
BZ2
]
, saying that
p\in\partial\Omega
has
well-behaved
\epsilon
-geodesics
if for some (hence any)
o\in\Omega
, any
z_{n},w_{n}\rightarrow p
and any
\epsilon
-geodesics
\gamma_{n}:I_{n}\rightarrow\Omega
joining
z_{n}
to
w_{n}
we have
k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(I_{n}),o)\rightarrow\infty
.

Proposition 2
.

The following are equivalent for
p\in\partial\Omega:

(a)
p
has well-behaved
\epsilon
-geodesics for any
\epsilon>0
;

(b) (
9
) holds.

We say that
\Omega
satisfies weak multiplicative, resp. additive localization at
p\in\partial\Omega
if for any neighbourhood
U
of
p
one has that

\limsup_{z,w\rightarrow p}\dfrac{k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,w)}{k_{\Omega}(z,w)}<\infty,

\mbox{resp. }\limsup_{z,w\rightarrow p}\left(k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,w)-k_{\Omega}(z,w)\right)<\infty.

Here and below we assume that
z,w
belong to the same connected component of
\Omega\cap U
. We would like to mention the weak multiplicative localization
results in
[
NÖT
, Theorem 14]
and
[
Sar
, Theorem 1.6]
, and the weak additive localization results in

[
BNT
, Theorem 1.4]
,
[
NÖT
, Theorem 9]
, and
[
Sar
, Theorem 1.3]
.

We present our main results which can be considered as
strong
multiplicative, resp. additive localization. They extend, for instance,
[
Ven1
, Proposition 3]
and
[
BNT
, Proposition 6.5]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
p\in\partial\Omega
be a
w
-point. Then for any neighbourhood
U
of
p
we have

(10)

\lim_{z,w\rightarrow p}\dfrac{k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,w)}{k_{\Omega}(z,w)}=1.

Note that Proposition
11
below implies that under the hypothesis of Theorem
3
,

k_{\Omega}(z,w)>0
for
z,w\in\Omega
near
p.
Therefore the limit in (
10
) is well defined.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
p\in\partial\Omega
be a
v
-point and a
w
-point. Then for any neighbourhood
U
of
p
, we have

(11)

\lim_{z,w\rightarrow p}\left(k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,w)-k_{\Omega}(z,w)\right)=0.

We note that our results are indeed ”strong” localizations because the constants given in limits (
10
) and (
11
) are the best constants one can get.

We defer the proofs of Theorems
3
and
4
to Section
3
.

Remark 5
.

The assumptions of Theorems
3
and
4
may be replaced with conditions depending on the behaviour of (
\epsilon
-)geodesics, which are reminiscents of hyperbolic geometry.

To be more explicit, observe that our previous discussion and Proposition
2
imply that in Theorem
4
we may replace
p\in\partial\Omega
being a
v
-point with being a weakly visible point and
p
being a
w
-point with
p
having well-behaved
\epsilon
-geodesics for any
\epsilon>0
. On the other hand, in Theorem
3
in order to replace
p\in\partial\Omega
being a
w
-point with
p
having well-behaved
\epsilon
-geodesics for any
\epsilon>0
, we need to assume that (
6
) holds for the pair
\{\Omega,p\}
, that is
p\in\partial\Omega
is a
v
-point.

As they are seemingly independent of (
\epsilon
-)geodesics, we prefer to state our theorems under the
v
-point and
w
-point conditions.

To get corollaries of Theorems
3
and
4
, we continue the discussion.

Let
\delta_{\Omega}(x):=\inf\{\|x-y\|:y\in\partial\Omega\}
.
Recall
[
BNT
, Definition 6.1]
.
p\in\partial\Omega
is said to be a
k
-point
(of
\Omega
) if for any neighbourhood
U
of
p
one has that

(12)

\liminf_{z\rightarrow p}\left(k_{\Omega}(z,\Omega\setminus U)-\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{1}{\delta_{\Omega}(z)}\right)\right)>-\infty.

Dini-smoothness is a property stronger than
\mathcal{C}^{1}
smoothness and weaker than
\mathcal{C}^{1,\epsilon}
smoothness.
By
[
NA
, Theorem 7]
, if
p
is a Dini-smooth boundary point if a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n},
then (see
[
NA
, Theorem 7]
)

(13)

k_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq\log\left(1+\dfrac{2\|z-w\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(z)\delta_{\Omega}(w)}}\right),\>\>\>\>\>z,w\in\Omega\mbox{ near }p.

Note also that the proof of
[
BNT
, Proposition 6.15]
implies that:

Proposition 6
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(not necessarily bounded) and assume that
p\in\partial\Omega
is a
k
-point and
\partial\Omega
is Dini-smooth near
p
. Then for any neighbourhood
U
of
p
we have

\lim_{z,w\rightarrow p}\left(k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,w)-k_{\Omega}(z,w)\right)=0.

We claim the following:

Proposition 7
.

Let
p\in\partial\Omega
be a
k
-point and assume that
\partial\Omega
is Dini-smooth in a neighbourhood of
p
. Then
p
is a
v
-point and a
w
-point.

We also defer the proof of Proposition
7
to Section
3
.

By Proposition
7
, Theorem
4
generalizes Proposition
6
. Moreover, by Theorem
3
and Proposition
7
we get:

Corollary 8
.

Let
p\in\partial\Omega
be a
k
-point and assume that
\partial\Omega
is Dini-smooth in a neighbourhood of
p
. Then for any neighbourhood
U
of
p
, the Kobayashi distance satisfies

\lim_{z,w\rightarrow p}\dfrac{k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,w)}{k_{\Omega}(z,w)}=1.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a convex domain,
p\in\partial\Omega
is said to be a
\mathbb{C}
-strictly convex boundary point if
\partial\Omega\cap T^{\mathbb{C}}_{p}\partial\Omega=\{p\}
. Convexity leads to more precise estimates of the Kobayashi distance. In particular, it is not hard to see that any
\mathbb{C}
-strictly convex boundary point of a convex domain is a
k
-point. However, there is more to say on that. We say
p\in\partial\Omega
is a
\mathbb{C}
-strictly convexifiable boundary point if there exists a neighbourhood
U
of
p
and a holomorphic embedding
\Psi:U\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\Psi(\Omega\cap U)
is a convex domain and
\Psi(p)
is a
\mathbb{C}
-strictly convex boundary point.

Proposition 9
.

[
BNT
, Theorem 6.13]

Let
p\in\partial\Omega
be a
\mathbb{C}
-strictly convexifiable boundary point. Then
p
is a
k
-point.

As a corollary of Theorem
3
, Theorem
4
, Proposition
7
and Proposition
9
we obtain:

Corollary 10
.

Let
p\in\partial\Omega
be a
\mathbb{C}
-strictly convexifiable boundary point and assume that
\partial\Omega
is Dini-smooth near
p
. Then for any neighbourhood
U
of
p
we have:

\lim_{z,w\rightarrow p}\dfrac{k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,w)}{k_{\Omega}(z,w)}=1,

\lim_{z,w\rightarrow p}\left(k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,w)-k_{\Omega}(z,w)\right)=0.

It is well-known that
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth strongly pseudoconvex boundary points are
\mathbb{C}
-strictly convex boundary points. In particular, Corollary
10
applies to such points. This covers
[
Ven1
, Proposition 3]
.

Moreover, under global assumptions such as strong pseudoconvexity or convexity and visibility, one may find quantitative strong localization results in
[

]
. The reader may also consult to
[

for weaker versions of these results in the case of

and the so-called Carathéodory distance

(recall that

). Our results can be also applied to such a study.

k_{\Omega}(z,w)+\frac{1}{n}\geq l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma)\geq\tanh(c^{\prime})l^{\kappa}_{\Omega\cap U}(\gamma)\geq\tanh(c^{\prime})k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,w)>0.

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, the claim follows. Now, we consider the other case. We assume that for large
n
, each
\gamma_{n}
above leaves
\Omega\cap V
. Set
t_{n}:=\inf\{t\in I_{n}:\gamma_{n}(t)\in\partial V\cap\Omega\}
and set
\gamma^{\prime}_{n}=\gamma_{n}|_{I^{\prime}_{n}}
, where
I^{\prime}_{n}:=\{t\in I_{n}:t\leq t_{n}\}
.
Then, by (
16
), we obtain

k_{\Omega}(z,w)+\frac{1}{n}\geq l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n})\geq l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma^{\prime}_{n})\geq\tanh(c^{\prime})l^{\kappa}_{\Omega\cap U}(\gamma^{\prime}_{n})\geq\tanh(c^{\prime})k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,\gamma_{n}(t_{n})).

\Omega\cap U
is bounded so
\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,\gamma_{n}(t_{n}))=:c^{\prime\prime}>0
. Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
we see that also in this case
k_{\Omega}(z,w)>0
. Hence, the proposition follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

We will first show the following:

of

\tilde{l}_{\Omega}(z,w)>1-\epsilon,\>\>\>\>\>z\in\Omega\cap V,\>w\in\Omega\setminus U.

Claim 2.

,

of

Taking

We continue with the proof of Theorem

.
Let

be any neighbourhood of

.

, we can find a neighbourhood

To prove Theorem

, we will follow the proof of

Proof of Theorem
4
.

We fix a neighbourhood
U
of
p
such that
\Omega\setminus U\neq\emptyset
. (otherwise there is nothing to prove) Since
p
is a
w
-point by (
8
) we can find neighbourhoods of
p
, say
V_{n}\subset\subset U
, such that

(18)

k_{\Omega}(z,o)+k_{\Omega}(w,o)-k_{\Omega}(z,w)>n,\quad z,w\in\Omega\cap V_{n},\>o\in\Omega\setminus U.

Notice that the conditions of Theorem
4
is stronger than the conditions of Theorem
3
. So, the Claim 2 given in the proof of Theorem
3
remains true. Therefore by Claim 2, for each
V_{n}
we can find another neighbourhood
W_{n}\subset\subset V_{n}
of
p
such that whenever
z,w\in\Omega\cap W_{n}
and
\gamma:I\rightarrow\Omega
is a
\epsilon_{n}
-geodesic joining
z
to
w
we have
\gamma(I)\subset\Omega\cap V_{n}
. Let
z_{n},w_{n}\rightarrow p
be arbitrary. By passing to a subsequence if necessary we assume that
z_{n},w_{n}\in\Omega\cap W_{n}
. Choose a sequence of
\epsilon_{n}
-geodesics as above with
\epsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0
, say
\gamma_{n}:I_{n}\rightarrow\Omega
, joining
z_{n}
to
w_{n}
. By construction, we can assume that
\gamma_{n}(I_{n})\subset\Omega\cap V_{n}
.

As

is a

-point Proposition
11
shows that
\Omega
is hyperbolic at

. Using the important estimate given in
[
Sar
, Lemma 3.1]
we get that

k_{\Omega\cap U}(z_{n},w_{n})\leq l^{\kappa}_{\Omega\cap U}(\gamma_{n})=\int_{I_{n}}\kappa_{\Omega\cap U}(\gamma(t);\gamma^{\prime}(t))dt\leq\int_{I_{n}}(1+Ce^{-k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t),\Omega\setminus U)})\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(t)))dt.

Since each
\gamma_{n}
is an
\epsilon_{n}
-geodesic, by the above we get

E_{n}:=k_{\Omega\cap U}(z_{n},w_{n})-k_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\leq C\int_{I_{n}}e^{-k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t),\Omega\setminus V)}\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(t))dt-\epsilon_{n}.

We will show that
E_{n}\rightarrow 0
. To do so, we note that since
\Omega
is hyperbolic at
p
by Proposition
11
it is hyperbolic near
p
. By passing to the limit if necessary,
[
BNT
, Lemma 6.10]
(see also
[
BZ1
, Proposition 4.4]
) allows us to parametrize each

k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t_{1}),\gamma_{n}(t_{2}))\leq|t_{1}-t_{2}|=l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]})\leq k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t_{1}),\gamma_{n}(t_{2}))+\epsilon_{n},\>\>\>\>\>t_{1}\leq t_{2}\in I_{n}

and
\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(t))=1
almost everywhere on

.

With these parameterizations we set

t_{n}:=\inf\{t\in I_{n}:\>\forall s\in I_{n},\>k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t),\Omega\setminus U)\leq k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(s),\Omega\setminus U)\}.

Let
o\in\Omega
be arbitrary, since
p
is a

-point we can apply the arguments given in the

to get

k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t),\Omega\setminus V)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t),o)+k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t_{n}),o)\right)-C^{\prime}\geq\dfrac{1}{2}|t-t_{n}|+\frac{n}{2}-C^{\prime\prime}

so

E_{n}\leq C^{\prime\prime\prime}e^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{I_{n}}e^{-\frac{|t-t_{n}|}{2}}dt-\epsilon_{n}.

Letting

,
E_{n}\rightarrow 0
. As
\{z_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\{w_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
was arbitrary, Theorem

4
follows.∎

Remark 12
.

The conditions given in Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
can be localized.

To see that the
v
-point condition can be localized, suppose that
p\in\partial\Omega
is a
w
-point of
\Omega
and
v
-point of
\Omega\cap U
, where
U
is a bounded neighbourhood of
p
. By Proposition
11
,
\Omega
is hyperbolic at
p
.
Therefore due to the discussion after its proof
[
NÖT
, Theorem 9]
applies to the pair

. Then one may conclude that

is indeed a
v
-point of
\Omega
. This observation extends Theorem
4

On the other hand, it is not clear that being a local

.

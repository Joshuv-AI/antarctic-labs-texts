Abstract
. This is largely an exposition article that expands the author’s talk at the Xiamen International Conference on Geometric Analysis in June 2021. We first survey the author’s joint work with Jiyuan Han on the Yau-Tian-Donaldson (YTD) conjecture for g g -weighted Kähler-Ricci solitons (or g g -solitons). We then review recent works of Apostolov-Canderbank-Jubert-Lahdili which establish a connection between a particular g g -soliton equation with Ricci-flat Kähler cone metrics (or equivalently Sasaki-Einstein metrics). The main interest in this connection is the transformation of a possibly irregular Sasaki-Einstein metric to a particular g g -soliton equation on any quasi-regular quotient. We will revisit this transformation by understanding how the corresponding transversal complex Monge-Ampère equations are transformed under the deformation of Reeb vector fields. Finally we explain how this PDE/pluripotential point of view allows one to combine the version of YTD conjecture for g g -solitons on log Fano pairs proved in [ 48 ] , the algebraic results from [ 16 , 73 ] and the discovery in [ 1 , 2 ] to prove the YTD conjecture for general Fano cones.

In the first part, we first review the pluripotential theory for

1.1.
g
-soliton equations

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional Fano manifold. In other words,
X
is a projective manifold satisfying that
-K_{X}:=\wedge^{n}T^{hol}X
is an ample line bundle.
In particular,
X
is a Kähler manifold.
For simplicity of notation, we also denote
-K_{X}
by
L
. Fix a reference Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
L
such that its Chern curvature
-{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\log h_{0}=\omega_{0}
is a Kähler form. Any other Hermitian metric metric on
L
is of the form
h_{\varphi}:=h_{0}e^{-\varphi}
for some function
\varphi
on
X
. Its Chern curvature is equal to
-{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\log h_{\varphi}=\omega_{0}+{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\varphi=:\omega_{\varphi}
.

\omega_{\varphi_{1}}=\omega_{\varphi_{2}}
iff
{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}(\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1})=0
iff
\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1}
is a real constant.

A function
\varphi
is called
\omega_{0}
-plurisubharmonic if
\varphi
is upper semicontinuous and
\varphi+\psi
is plurisubharmonic
for any local potential
\psi
of
\omega_{0}
. The set of all
\omega_{0}
-psh functions will be denoted by
{\rm PSH}(\omega_{0})
.
The space of smooth strictly
\omega_{0}
-psh functions (or called Kähler potentials) will be denoted by

(1)

{\mathcal{H}}:={\mathcal{H}}(L)={\mathcal{H}}(X,L)=\left\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X);\omega_{0}+{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\varphi>0\right\}.

Any Hermitian metric
h_{\varphi}
can be considered as a volume form as follows. Choose any local holomorphic coordinate chart
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\}
. Set
s=dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}
,
s^{*}=\partial_{z_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\partial_{z_{n}}\in-K_{X}
. It is easy to verify the following volume does not depend on the choice of holomorphic coordinates:

(2)

\Omega_{\varphi}:=|s^{*}|^{2}_{h_{\varphi}}(\sqrt{-1})^{n^{2}}s\wedge\bar{s}=\Omega_{0}e^{-\varphi}.

For any holomorphic vector field
v
on
X
, denote by
\mathfrak{L}_{v}
the Lie derivative with respect to
v
and set

(3)

\theta_{v}(\varphi):=-\frac{\mathfrak{L}_{{v}}\Omega_{\varphi}}{\Omega_{\varphi}}=-\frac{\mathfrak{L}_{v}\Omega_{0}}{\Omega_{0}}+v(\varphi)=:\theta_{v,0}+v(\varphi)

where
\theta_{v,0}=\theta_{v}(0)
.
Then
\theta_{v}=\theta_{v}(\varphi)
satisfies the identity

(4)

\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}\theta_{v}(\varphi)=\iota_{v}(\omega_{0}+{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\varphi)=\iota_{v}\omega_{\varphi}

where
\iota_{v}
is the contraction with respect to
v
.
In local coordinates this is saying that
v^{i}(\omega_{\varphi})_{i\bar{j}}=\partial_{\bar{z}_{j}}\theta_{v}
.
If
Im(v)
is furthermore a Killing vector field with respect to
\omega=\omega_{\varphi}
, then
\theta_{v}
is a real valued function and the identity (
4
) is equivalent to the identity

(5)

\iota_{2Im(v)}\omega=-\iota_{\xi}\omega=d\theta_{v}

where
\xi=-2Im(v)
. Sometimes we will also write
\theta_{v}
as
\theta_{\xi}
.

Let
T
denote the
r
-dimensional real torus
(S^{1})^{r}
and let
\mathbb{T}\cong({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{r}
be its complexification.
Assume that
\mathbb{T}\cong({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{r}
acts on
X
biholomorphically. There is a canonical action of
\mathbb{T}
on
-K_{X}
induced by the pushforward of tangent vectors.
Denote by
{\mathcal{H}}(-K_{X})^{T}
the set of
T
-invariant metrics in
{\mathcal{H}}(-K_{X})
. Fix
h_{0}e^{-\varphi}\in{\mathcal{H}}(-K_{X})^{T}
.

Denote by
N_{\mathbb{R}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
the Lie algebra of
(S^{1})^{r}
, and by
M_{\mathbb{R}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
the dual of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
For any
\kappa\in\{1,\dots,r\}
, let
\xi_{\kappa}\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
be the standard generator of the
\kappa
-th factor of
(S^{1})^{r}
. Then
\xi_{\kappa}
is a Killing vector field. Denote
v_{\kappa}=\frac{1}{2}(-J\xi_{\kappa}-\sqrt{-1}\xi_{\kappa})
. By the above discussion, there exists real valued function
\theta_{\kappa}
satisfying
\iota_{\xi_{\kappa}}\omega_{\varphi}=d\theta_{\kappa}(\varphi)
.
If we set
\theta_{\kappa}=\theta_{v_{\kappa},0}
, then

(6)

\theta_{\kappa}(\varphi)=\theta_{\kappa,0}+v_{\kappa}(\varphi)=\theta_{\kappa,0}-\frac{1}{2}d\varphi(J\xi_{k}).

The action of
(S^{1})^{r}
on
(X,\omega_{\varphi})
becomes Hamiltonian and the associated moment map is given by:

\displaystyle{\bf m}_{\varphi}:X

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle M_{\mathbb{R}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{r}

\displaystyle{\bf m}_{\varphi}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta_{1}(\varphi),\cdots,\theta_{r}(\varphi))=\left(\theta_{\kappa}(\varphi)\right)_{\kappa\in\{1,\dots,r\}}.

We will denote by
P
the image of
{\bf m}_{\varphi}
. By Atiyah-Guillemin-Sternberg theorem,
P
is a convex polytope which does not depend on the choice of
\omega_{\varphi}=\omega_{0}+{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\varphi>0
. Moreover, the pushforward measure
({\bf m}_{\varphi})_{*}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}
is a Radon measure that does not depend on the choice of
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}(L)
. We will call it the Duistermaat-Heckmann measure and denote it by
{\rm DH}_{T}(L)
.

Let
g:P\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth
positive
function which we can assume to the restriction of a smooth function on
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. We will be interested in the following equation for
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}(-K_{X})^{T}
which we call the
g
-weighted Kähler-Ricci (KR) soliton
1
1

1

It was called generalized Kähler-Ricci soliton in
[
48
]
, or just
g
-soliton equation:

(7)

g({\bf m}_{\varphi})(\omega_{0}+{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\varphi)^{n}=e^{-\varphi}\Omega_{0}.

The integral of the left-hand-side does not depend on
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}(L)
:

(8)

{\bf V}_{g}:=\int_{X}g({\bf m}_{\varphi})(\omega_{0}+{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\varphi)^{n}=\int_{P}g(x){\rm DH}_{T}(L).

For simplicity of notation, we will write
g_{\varphi}=g({\bf m}_{\varphi})=g(\theta_{1}(\varphi),\cdots,\theta_{r}(\varphi))
. It is also convenient to introduce

(9)

f_{\varphi}=\log g_{\varphi},\quad H_{\varphi}=\log\frac{\Omega_{\varphi}}{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}.

Then (
7
) can be re-written as the equation:

(10)

\log\frac{\Omega_{\varphi}}{(\omega_{0}+{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\varphi)^{n}}-\log g_{\varphi}=\text{ constant},\quad\text{i.e.}\quad H_{\varphi}-f_{\varphi}=\text{ constant }.

It will be useful to consider the following equivalent form of the equation (
10
):

(11)

(\Delta_{\varphi}+v_{f,\varphi})(H_{\varphi}-f_{\varphi})=0

where the
\Delta_{\varphi}=\omega_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\partial_{i}\partial_{\bar{j}}
is the Laplace operator of
\omega_{\varphi}
and
v_{f,\varphi}
is given by:

(12)

\displaystyle v_{f,\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{\varphi}}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}\omega_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\partial_{i}=\sum_{\kappa}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{\kappa}}\frac{\partial\theta_{\kappa}(\varphi)}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}\omega_{\varphi}^{i\bar{j}}\partial_{i}=\sum_{\kappa}f_{\kappa}({\bf m}_{\varphi})v_{\kappa}.

Note also that the equation (
7
) is equivalent to the tensorial equation:

(13)

Ric(\omega_{\varphi})-\omega_{\varphi}={\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\log g_{\varphi}.

The
g
-soliton equation, or more generally, the weighted extremal metrics in an equivariant setting, seems to first appear in Tian’s work in
[
86
]
generalizing the Kähler-Ricci soliton equation. There are many following works in related equations, see for example
[
79
,
57
]
). Our work, which deals with the general
g
-soliton equation, is partly inspired by the work of Berman-Witt-Nyström in
[
13
]
which studies the Kähler-Ricci soliton equation from the variational point of view.

Remark 1.1
.

The study of

(1)

(
Kähler-Einstein metrics
)
T=\{e\}
and
g=1
. This is the Kähler-Einstein case and is well-understood thanks to the works of Tian, Berman, Chen-Donaldson-Sun and others (see

(2)

(
Kähler-Ricci soliton
)

\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}(-K_{X})
is a (shrinking) Kähler-Ricci soliton on
(X,\mathbb{T})
if there exists a holomorphic vector field
v=v_{\xi}
with
\xi\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\omega_{\varphi}
satisfies

\displaystyle Ric(\omega_{\varphi})-\omega_{\varphi}=\mathfrak{L}_{v}\omega_{\varphi}.

It is well-known that KR solitons give rise to self-similar solutions to the Kähler-Ricci flow.
By using
\mathfrak{L}_{v}\omega_{\varphi}=d\iota_{v}\omega_{\varphi}={\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\theta_{v}(\varphi)
, the above equation is equivalent to
the following equation

(14)

(\omega_{0}+{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\varphi)^{n}=e^{\theta_{v}(\varphi)-\varphi}\Omega_{0}.

This is a special case of (
7
) when
g(x)=e^{\langle x,\xi\rangle}=e^{\ell_{\xi}(x)}
where
v=v_{\xi}
and for simplicity of notation, we set:

(15)

\ell_{\xi}(x)=\langle x,\xi\rangle.

The Kähler-Ricci soliton equation was studied extensively in works of Tian-Zhu and others (see
[
89
,
90
,
25
]
).

(3)

(
Mabuchi soliton
)

\varphi
is called a Mabuchi soliton if there exists a holomorphic vector field

with
\xi\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle Ric(\omega_{\varphi})-\omega_{\varphi}={\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\log(1+\theta_{v}(\varphi)-\underline{\theta}_{v})

(16)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\Longleftrightarrow\left(1+\theta_{v}(\varphi)-\underline{\theta}_{v}\right)(\omega_{0}+{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\varphi)^{n}=e^{-\varphi}\Omega_{0}.

where
\underline{\theta}_{v}=\frac{1}{\int_{X}\omega^{n}}\int_{X}\theta_{v}\omega^{n}
. It is known that solution
\varphi
to this equation is the critical point to the Ricci-Calabi functional (see

]
)

\varphi\mapsto\int_{X}(e^{\bar{H}_{\varphi}}-1)^{2}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}

where
\bar{H}_{\varphi}=H_{\varphi}-\log(\frac{1}{{\bf V}_{1}}\int_{X}e^{H_{\varphi}}\omega_{\varphi}^{n})
. The equation (

) is a special case of (
7
) when

g(x)=1+\langle x-\underline{x},\xi\rangle
where
\underline{x}=\frac{1}{{\bf V}_{1}}\int_{P}x\,{\rm DH}_{T}(-K_{X})
is the barycenter of the Duistermaat-Heckman measure

over

.

The Mabuchi soliton equation was introduced in

(4)

(
Ricci-flat Kähler cone metric
) This quite un-expected example is due to the works in

Because
-K_{X}
is ample, there is an affine cone over

. We will explain in section

the existence of a Ricci-flat Kähler cone metric on the cone

with the Reeb vector field induced by

is equivalent to the solvability to the following equation:

\displaystyle Ric(\omega_{\varphi})-\omega_{\varphi}=-(n+2){\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\log(n+1+\theta_{v}(\varphi))

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\Longleftrightarrow\frac{1}{(n+1+\theta_{v}(\varphi))^{n+2}}(\omega_{0}+{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\varphi)^{n}=e^{-\varphi}\Omega_{0}

where

.
This is (

g(x)=\frac{1}{(n+1+\langle x,\xi\rangle)^{n+2}}=\frac{1}{(n+1+\ell_{\xi}(x))^{n+2}}.

The constant

1.2.
Generalized Futaki invariant and Matsushima type result

Fox any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}(-K_{X})^{T}
, set
H_{\varphi}=\log\frac{\Omega_{\varphi}}{(\omega_{0}+{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\varphi)^{n}}
which satisfies the identity
Ric(\omega_{\varphi})-\omega_{\varphi}={\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}H_{\varphi}
. For any holomorphic vector field
v
, define the
g
-weighted Futaki invariant as:

(18)

\displaystyle{\rm Fut}_{g}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}v(H_{\varphi}-\log g_{\varphi})g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}.

Note that in the case of Kähler-Ricci solitons this is nothing but Tian-Zhu’s modified Futaki invariant.
The basic result is then:

Theorem 1.2
.

{\rm Fut}_{g}
does not depend on the choice of
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}(-K_{X})^{T}
. Moreover, if there is a
g
-soliton, then
{\rm Fut}_{g}(v)=0
for any holomorphic vector field
v
.

By appropriate integration by parts, Futaki invariant can written into two useful forms:

(1):
Recall that
\mathfrak{L}_{v}e^{-\varphi}
is the Lie derivative of the volume form
e^{-\varphi}
with respect to
v
. We have:

(19)

\displaystyle{\rm Fut}_{g}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}v\left(\log\frac{\Omega_{\varphi}}{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}-\log g_{\varphi}\right)g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\frac{\mathfrak{L}_{{v}}\Omega_{\varphi}}{\Omega_{\varphi}}g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}-\mathfrak{L}_{{v}}(g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n})=-\int_{X}\theta_{v}(\varphi)g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}.

It is straightforward to verify that the last integral does not depend on the choice of
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}^{T}
.

Assume that
v=v_{\zeta}=\frac{1}{2}(-J\zeta-\sqrt{-1}\zeta)
such that
[\zeta,\xi]=0
for any
\xi\in T
. Then
\zeta
and
T
together generates a possibly bigger torus
T^{\prime}
. We can choose a
T^{\prime}
-invariant metric
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}(L)
and get a moment map from
X
to
\mathfrak{t}^{\prime*}
(the dual of the Lie algebra of
T^{\prime}
). Then the Futaki invariant can then be expressed as an integral with respect to the associated Duistermaat-Heckman measure on
\mathfrak{t}^{\prime*}
. In particular, if we choose
v=v_{\kappa}
, then we get the necessary vanishing condition for the existence of
g
-solitons:

(20)

\displaystyle\int_{X}\theta_{v}(\varphi)g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}=\int_{P}x_{\kappa}g(x){\rm DH}_{T}(-K_{X})=0.

A special class of
g
-soliton satisfies
g(x)=b(\ell_{\xi})
where
b
is a smooth function over
{\mathbb{R}}
and
\ell_{\xi}=\langle x,\xi\rangle
. In this situation, let
a
be a primitive function of
b
, i.e.
a^{\prime}=b
and define a function on
N_{\mathbb{R}}

(21)

\xi\mapsto\int_{P}a(x){\rm DH}_{T}(-K_{X})=:\mathfrak{V}(\xi)

where
a(x)=a(\ell_{\xi}(x))
.
The vanishing condition (
20
) becomes the condition that
\xi
is the critical point of
\mathfrak{V}(\xi)
. If we assume that
a
is moreover strictly convex (resp. strictly concave), or equivalently that
b
is strictly increasing (resp. strictly decreasing), then

\mathfrak{V}(\xi)
is also strictly convex (resp. strictly concave) over
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. So the critical point of
\mathfrak{V}(\xi)
is unique if it exists.

(2):
The following form is useful later for integration to get
g
-Mabuchi functional. Set
f_{\varphi}=\log g_{\varphi}
and

(22)

\displaystyle{\rm Fut}_{g}(v)=-\int_{X}\theta_{v}(\Delta_{\varphi}+f^{i}\partial_{i})(H_{\varphi}-f_{\varphi})g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}.

We have the following Matsushima type result (which can also be proved by using the second uniqueness statement of Theorem
1.10
).
We should point out that there is such type of result in
[
59
]
in the more general setting of weighted extremal metrics.

Theorem 1.3
.

If we set

(23)

{\rm Aut}(X,\mathbb{T})=\{\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(X);\sigma\cdot t=t\cdot\sigma\text{ for any }t\in\mathbb{T}\},

then its identity component
{\rm Aut}_{0}(X,\mathbb{T})
is reductive.

Proof.

Let
{\rm aut}(X,\mathbb{T})
be the Lie algebra of
{\rm Aut}(X,\mathbb{T})
.
Assume that
\omega=\omega_{\varphi}
is a
g
-soliton. We just need to prove the following identity: under the correspondence
v\mapsto\theta_{v}=-\frac{\mathfrak{L}_{v}\Omega_{\varphi}}{\Omega_{\varphi}}
,

(24)

{\rm aut}(X,\mathbb{T})\cong\{\theta\in C^{\infty}(X,{\mathbb{C}})^{T};(\Delta+v_{f}+1)\theta=0\}

where
v_{f}=v_{f,\varphi}=\sum_{\kappa}f_{\kappa}v_{\kappa}
(see (
12
)). Indeed, note that when
\theta
is
T
-invariant,
v_{f}(\theta)=Re(v_{f})(\theta)
and hence
\Delta+v_{f}+1
is a real
operator over
C^{\infty}(X,{\mathbb{C}})^{T}
, whose kernel is the complexification of the real subspace which corresponds to Killing vector fields that commute with
\mathbb{T}
-action. To see the identity (
24
) we can first calculate by using the
g
-soliton equation
R_{k\bar{i}}=\omega_{k\bar{i}}+f_{k\bar{i}}
to get:

\displaystyle((\Delta+v_{f}+1)\theta)_{\bar{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta+\sum_{j}(\theta_{j\bar{j}}+f_{j}\theta_{\bar{j}}))_{\bar{i}}=\theta_{\bar{i}}+\sum_{j}(\theta_{\bar{j}\bar{i},j}-\theta_{\bar{j}}R_{j\bar{i}}+f_{j\bar{i}}\theta_{\bar{j}}+f_{j}\theta_{\bar{j}\bar{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}(\theta_{\bar{j}\bar{i},j}+f_{j}\theta_{\bar{j}\bar{i}}).

Now multiplying both sides by
\bar{\theta}_{i}
and integrating by parts with respect to the measure
e^{f}\omega^{n}
, we get:

\displaystyle\int_{X}\sum_{i}((\Delta+v_{f}+1)\theta)_{\bar{i}}\bar{\theta}_{i}e^{f}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\sum_{i,j}\theta_{\bar{j}\bar{i}}\bar{\theta}_{ij}e^{f}\omega^{n}.

We get
\theta_{\bar{j}\bar{i}}=0
if and only if
(\Delta+v_{f}+1)(\theta)
is a constant. This happens if and only if
(\Delta+v_{f}+1)(\theta)=0
because:

\int_{X}(\Delta+v_{f}+1)\theta e^{f}\omega^{n}=\int_{X}\theta e^{f}\omega^{n}=0

by the vanishing of
g
-weighted Futaki invariant.
∎

1.3.
Energy functionals

We first define functionals on
{\mathcal{H}}(\omega_{0})^{T}
. For any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}(\omega_{0})^{T}
, define:

\displaystyle{\bf E}_{g}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{{\bf V}_{g}}\int_{0}^{1}dt\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}=\frac{1}{{\bf V}_{g}}\int_{0}^{1}dt\int_{X}\varphi g_{t\varphi}\omega_{t\varphi}^{n}

where
\varphi(t)
is a smooth path connecting
0
and
\varphi
in
{\mathcal{H}}(\omega_{0})^{T}
, hence the second identity. Note that
{\bf E}_{g}
satisfies the monotonicity:

(25)

\varphi_{1}\leq\varphi_{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{\bf E}_{g}(\varphi_{1})\leq{\bf E}_{g}(\varphi_{2}).

One can show that
{\bf E}_{g}
is well defined: it does not depend on the path.
Moreover we define the following functionals:

\displaystyle{\bf I}_{g}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\bf V}_{g}}\int_{X}\varphi(g_{0}\omega_{0}^{n}-g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n})

\displaystyle{\bf\Lambda}_{g}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\bf V}_{g}}\int_{X}\varphi g_{0}\omega_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle{\bf J}_{g}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\Lambda}_{g}(\varphi)-{\bf E}_{g}(\varphi)

\displaystyle({\bf I}_{g}-{\bf J}_{g})(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf E}_{g}(\varphi)-\frac{1}{{\bf V}_{g}}\int_{X}\varphi g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}.

We will denote by
{\bf E},{\bf I},{\bf J}
the above functional when
g=1
.

Lemma 1.4
.

(i)

There exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,g)>0
such that
C_{1}^{-1}{\bf F}\leq{\bf F}_{g}\leq C_{1}{\bf F}
for any
{\bf F}\in\{{\bf I},{\bf J},{\bf I}-{\bf J}\}
.

(ii)

{\bf I}_{g}(\varphi)\geq 0
. Moreover
{\bf I}_{g}(\varphi)=0
if and only if
\varphi-\varphi_{0}
is a constant.

(iii)

There exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(n,g)>0
such that
C_{2}^{-1}{\bf J}_{g}\leq{\bf I}_{g}-{\bf J}_{g}\leq C_{2}{\bf J}_{g}
.

(iv)

For any
t\in[0,1]
, we have the inequality:

(26)

{\bf J}_{g}(tu)\leq t^{1+C_{2}^{-1}}{\bf J}_{g}(u).

(v)

There exists
C=C(X,L)
such that

(27)

{\bf\Lambda}_{g}(\varphi)\leq\sup\varphi\leq{\bf\Lambda}_{g}(\varphi)+C.

One can prove (i) first and then deduce (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) by using the well-known property for
{\bf I},{\bf J}
. With (ii) proved, (iii) follows by integrating the differential inequality:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\bf J}_{g}(tu)=\frac{1}{{\bf V}_{g}}\int_{X}u(\omega_{0}^{n}-\omega_{tu}^{n})=\frac{1}{t}{\bf I}_{g}(tu)\geq\frac{1}{C_{2}t}{\bf J}_{g}(tu).

The first inequality in (v) is obviously true. The second one can be proved using Hartogs’ theorem for
\omega_{0}

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}(\Delta_{\varphi}+v_{f,\varphi})(H_{\varphi}-f_{\varphi})g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}

\displaystyle-\int_{X}(H_{\varphi}-f_{\varphi})((\Delta_{\varphi}+v_{f,\varphi})\dot{\varphi})g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}

We will identify equivalent test configurations. By resolution of singularities, any test configuration is equivalent to a dominant test configuration so that without loss of generality, we can always assume that a given test configuration is dominant.

Sketch of proof.

This is proved by using the Minimal Model Program (MMP) and calculate the variation of

({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}})

({\mathcal{X}}^{s},{\mathcal{L}}^{s})

Then we can run the MMP with rescaling by setting
{\mathcal{L}}_{t}:=\frac{K_{{\mathcal{X}}}+t{\mathcal{L}}}{t-1}

[

-K_{{\mathcal{X}}}-{\mathcal{L}}=\sum_{i}e_{i}E_{i}
with

\displaystyle-K_{{\mathcal{X}}}-{\mathcal{L}}_{t}=\frac{t}{t-1}(-K_{{\mathcal{X}}}-{\mathcal{L}})=\frac{t}{t-1}\sum_{i}e_{i}E_{i}

\displaystyle{\bf L}^{\rm NA}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}}_{t})=-\frac{t}{t-1}e_{1}-1,\quad\frac{d}{dt}{\bf L}^{\rm NA}=\frac{1}{(t-1)^{2}}e_{1}.

\displaystyle\dot{{\mathcal{L}}}_{t}=\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{L}}_{t}=-\frac{K_{{\mathcal{X}}}+{\mathcal{L}}}{(t-1)^{2}}=\frac{1}{(t-1)^{2}}\sum_{i}e_{i}E_{i}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\bf E}^{\rm NA}_{g}=\frac{1}{(t-1)^{2}}\sum_{i}e_{i}\int_{E_{i}}g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\bf\Lambda}^{\rm NA}_{g}=\frac{1}{(t-1)^{2}}\sum_{i}e_{i}\int_{\rho^{*}E_{i}}g_{\psi}({\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\psi)^{n}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}({\bf D}^{\rm NA}_{g}-\epsilon{\bf J}^{\rm NA}_{g})=\frac{d}{dt}\left({\bf L}^{\rm NA}-(1-\epsilon){\bf E}^{\rm NA}_{g}-\epsilon{\bf\Lambda}^{\rm NA}_{g}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt=\frac{1}{(t-1)^{2}}\sum_{i}(e_{1}-e_{i})\left((1-\epsilon)\int_{E_{i}}g_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}+\epsilon\int_{\rho^{*}E_{i}}g_{\psi}({\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}}\psi)^{n}\right)\geq 0.

In the algebraic study of K-stability, one needs to consider more general data than test configurations. One such generalization is filtrations of section rings of
-K_{X}
. Such a filtration gives rise to a sequence of test configurations. For Fano varieties, it turns out that it suffices to consider filtrations associated valuations. We will only use divisorial valuations in this note although more general quasi-monomial valuations do play an important role in the recent study. A divisor over
X
is an ordinary irreducible divisor
E
on a projective variety
X^{\prime}
that has a birational morphism to
X
. By resolution of singularities, we can assume that
X^{\prime}
is smooth. Then the order of vanishing along
E
defines a functional
{\rm ord}_{E}
on the field
{\mathbb{C}}(X)
of rational functions on
X
. Any functional on
{\mathbb{C}}(X)
of the form
c\cdot{\rm ord}_{E}
with
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
is called a divisorial valuation. The set of divisorial valuations will be denoted by
X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

There are two invariants of a divisorial valuation which play important roles in the study of K-stability. The first one is the log discrepancy defined by the formula
A_{E}(c\cdot{\rm ord}_{E})=c\cdot({\rm ord}_{X}(K_{X^{\prime}/X})+1)
. The second one is defined as (see (
141
), (
145
))

(40)

S_{g}(v)=\frac{1}{{\bf V}_{g}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\rm vol}_{g}(\mu^{*}L-tE)dt.

We will not give detailed discussion of the use of valuations, but just give some hint of its importance.
There is a compactification of
X^{\rm div}
called the Berkovich space and denoted by
X^{\rm an}
. It is a non-Archimedean analytic space and is a compactification of
X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}
which is in turn dense in
X^{\rm an}
. One also has a non-Archimedean line bundle
L^{\rm an}
. Now any test configuration defines a non-Archimedean metric on
L^{\rm an}
.
Equivalent test configurations define the same metric. For us it suffices to know that such non-Archimedean metric can be represented by a function on
X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}
which is the relative non-Archimedean potential with respect to the trivial metric (defined by the trivial test configuration) on
L^{\rm an}
. More precisely, for any test configuration
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}})
which dominates the trivial test configuration by
\rho:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow X\times{\mathbb{C}}=:X_{\mathbb{C}}
, we set:

(41)

\phi(v)=\phi_{({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}})}=G(v)({\mathcal{L}}-\rho^{*}L_{\mathbb{C}}).

One important consequence of this point of view is the following valuative formula for the
{\bf L}^{\rm NA}
invariant:

(42)

{\bf L}^{\rm NA}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}})=\inf_{v\in X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(A_{X}(v)+\phi(v)).

This is a variation (or a global analogue) of the well-known valuative formula for log canonical thresholds.
We can state a valuative criterion for the reduced uniform
g
-weighted stability.

Theorem 1.16

(
[
64
,
48
]
)
.

(X,\mathbb{T})
is reduced uniformly
g
-weighted stable if and only if there exists
\delta>1
such that for any
\tilde{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant divisorial valuation
v
, there exists
\xi\in\tilde{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that

(43)

A_{X}(v_{\xi})-\delta\cdot S_{g}(v_{\xi})\geq 0

where
v_{\xi}
is defined in (

Proof.

Assume that
X
is reduced uniformly
g
-weighted stable. Then
{\rm Fut}_{g}\equiv 0
on
\tilde{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Choose any
v\in X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and let
\mathcal{F}_{v}
be the associated filtration. Then

\displaystyle\inf_{\xi\in\tilde{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}}\left[{\bf D}^{\rm NA}_{g}-\gamma{\bf J}^{\rm NA}_{g})((\mathcal{F}_{v})_{\xi})\right]=\inf_{\xi\in\tilde{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}}\left[({\bf D}^{\rm NA}_{g}-\gamma{\bf J}^{\rm NA}_{g})(\mathcal{F}_{v_{\xi}})\right]\geq 0.

Now we have
A_{X}(v_{\xi})\geq{\bf L}^{\rm NA}(\mathcal{F}_{v_{\xi}})
,
{\bf E}_{g}^{\rm NA}(\mathcal{F}_{v_{\xi}})=S_{g}(v_{\xi})
and

(44)

{\bf J}_{g}^{\rm NA}(\mathcal{F}_{v_{\xi}})\sim{\bf J}^{\rm NA}(\mathcal{F}_{v_{\xi}})\sim S(v_{\xi})\sim S_{g}(v_{\xi})

where

means the ratio of two quantities are uniformly bounded (independent of

and
\xi
).
The second inequality is proved by K. Fujita. So there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\inf_{\xi\in N_{\mathbb{R}}}\left[A_{X}(v_{\xi})-(1+C\gamma)S_{g}(v_{\xi})\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{\xi\in\tilde{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}}\left[({\bf D}_{g}^{\rm NA}-\gamma{\bf J}^{\rm NA}_{g})((\mathcal{F}_{v})_{\xi})\right]\geq 0.

For the other direction, there are two approaches. The first approach is by using the above theorem
1.15
. For a special test configuration
({\mathcal{X}},-K_{{\mathcal{X}}})
,
let
v=r({\rm ord}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{0}})\in X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the restriction of
{\rm ord}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{0}}
to
{\mathbb{C}}(X)
. Then
\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{F}_{({\mathcal{X}},-K_{{\mathcal{X}}})}=\mathcal{F}_{v}(-A_{X}(v))
. Then

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{g}^{\rm NA}({\mathcal{X}},-K_{{\mathcal{X}}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\bf E}_{g}^{\rm NA}(\mathcal{F})=-{\bf E}_{g}^{\rm NA}(\mathcal{F}_{v}(-A_{X}(v)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{X}(v)-{\bf E}_{g}^{\rm NA}(\mathcal{F}_{v})=A_{X}(v)-S_{g}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{X}(v_{\xi})-S_{g}(v_{\xi})+{\rm Fut}_{g}(\xi).

The theorem then follows easily. The second approach uses the inequality (

1.24

1.6.
Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture

A psh ray
\Phi=\{\varphi(s)\}_{[0,+\infty)}
is a geodesic ray if for any

,
\Phi|_{[s_{1},s_{2}]}
is a geodesic segment between
\varphi(s_{1})
and
\varphi(s_{2})

\sup(\varphi(s)-\varphi_{0})
and

is affine with respect to
s
. Any geodesic ray defines a non-Archimedean metric on
L^{\rm NA}
which is represented by a function on

defined as:

(45)

\Phi_{\rm NA}(v)=-G(v)(\Phi),\quad\text{ for any }v\in X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}

where
G(v)
is the Gauss extension of
v
and
G(v)(\Phi)
is the generic Lelong number with respect to
G(v)
(see the paragraph after Theorem
D.2
for more details).

Because
{\mathcal{L}}
is relatively ample, there always exists locally bounded psh Hermitian metrics on
{\mathcal{L}}
which we call the psh ray.

Theorem 1.18

(
[
82
]
)
.

Fix a reference metric
\varphi_{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}(L)
.
For any test configuration
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}})
, there exists a unique geodesic ray emanating from
\varphi_{0}
.

The following result connects the Archimedean and non-Archimedean functionals.

Theorem 1.19
.

For any test configuration
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}})
, let
\Phi=\{\varphi(s)\}
be any locally bounded psh ray induced by a locally bounded psh Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{L}
. Then we have the following slope formula:
for any
{\bf F}\in\{{\bf E}_{g},{\bf I}_{g},{\bf J}_{g},{\bf\Lambda}_{g},{\bf L}\}

{\bf F}^{\prime\infty}(\Phi)=\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{\bf F}(\varphi(s))}{s}={\bf F}^{\rm NA}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}}).

Applying this theorem to both sides of the inequality (
26

Let
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}})
be any test configuration.
For any

, we have the inequality:

{\bf J}^{\rm NA}_{g}({\mathcal{X}},t{\mathcal{L}}+(1-t)L_{\mathbb{C}})\leq t^{-C_{3}}{\bf J}^{\rm NA}_{g}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}}).

The following is a version of the Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture.

Theorem 1.21

(
[
48
]
, see also
[
51
]
)

(X,\mathbb{T})
admits a
g

Sketch of proof.

We just need to show that
{\bf M}_{g}
is
\tilde{\mathbb{T}}
-coercive. Assuming not, then for any fixed
\epsilon>0
there exists a sequence of
\varphi_{j}
satisfying:

\sup\varphi_{j}=0,\quad{\bf J}_{g}(\varphi_{j})=\inf_{\sigma\in\tilde{\mathbb{T}}}{\bf J}_{g}(\sigma^{*}\omega_{\varphi_{j}}),\quad{\bf M}_{g}(\varphi_{j})\leq\epsilon{\bf J}_{g}(\varphi_{j})-j.

Because
{\bf M}_{g}={\bf H}_{g}-({\bf I}_{g}-{\bf J}_{g})\geq-C_{2}{\bf J}_{g}
(since
{\bf H}_{g}(\varphi)\geq 0
), we get:

{\bf J}_{g}(\varphi_{j})=-{\bf E}_{g}(\varphi_{j})+O(1)\rightarrow+\infty.

Set
S_{j}=-{\bf E}_{g}(\varphi_{j})\rightarrow+\infty
and let
\{\varphi_{j}(s)\}_{s\in[0,S_{j}]}
be geodesic segment connecting
\varphi_{0}
to
\varphi_{j}

1.9
, we can extract strongly convergent sequence and construct a geodesic ray

\inf_{\xi\in N_{\mathbb{R}}}{\bf J}^{\prime\infty}_{g}(\Phi_{\xi})=1,\quad{\bf M}^{\prime\infty}_{g}(\Phi)\leq\epsilon

where
\Phi_{\xi}=\{\varphi_{\xi}(s)\}_{s\in[0,+\infty)}
with

.
This implies

(48)

{\bf L}^{\prime\infty}(\Phi)\leq{\bf D}^{\prime\infty}_{g}(\Phi)+{\bf E}^{\prime\infty}_{g}(\Phi)\leq{\bf M}^{\prime\infty}_{g}(\Phi)-1\leq-1+\epsilon.

The idea due to Berman-Bouksom-Jonsson is now to approximate the geodesic ray by a sequence of test configurations by blowing up
multiplier ideal sheaves.
Let
\mathcal{J}(m\Phi)
be the multiplier ideal sheaf. Let
\mu_{m}:{\mathcal{X}}_{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{X}}
be the blow up of
\mathcal{J}(m\Phi_{m})
with exceptional divisor

. Set
{\mathcal{L}}_{m}=\mu_{m}^{*}L_{\mathbb{C}}-\frac{1}{m+m_{0}}E_{m}

{\mathcal{L}}_{m}
is globally generated. So
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}}_{m})
is a test configuration of
(X,L)
. Moreover

(49)

\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\phi_{m}(v)=-G(v)(\Phi)=\Phi_{\rm NA}(v),\quad\text{ for any }v\in X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Next we want to approximate the slopes by non-Archimedean quantities of test configurations.
First by Demailly’s result (Theorem
D.1
),
\Phi_{m}\geq\Phi
, where
\Phi_{m}
is the geodesic rays associated to
({\mathcal{X}}_{m},{\mathcal{L}}_{m})
. This implies
(\Phi_{m})_{\xi}\geq\Phi_{\xi}
for any
\xi\in\tilde{N}_{\mathbb{R}}
where
\Phi_{\xi}=\{\sigma_{\xi}(s)^{*}\varphi(s)\}
, and

(50)

{\bf E}^{\rm NA}_{g}((\phi_{m})_{\xi})={\bf E}^{\prime\infty}_{g}((\Phi_{m})_{\xi})\geq{\bf E}^{\prime\infty}_{g}(\Phi_{\xi}),\quad{\bf\Lambda}^{\rm NA}_{g}((\phi_{m})_{\xi})={\bf\Lambda}^{\prime\infty}_{g}((\Phi_{m})_{\xi})\geq{\bf\Lambda}^{\prime\infty}_{g}(\Phi_{\xi})

represents (the non-Archimedean metric associated to) the test configuration

.

42
) (see (

(whose proof uses the strong openness conjecture proved by Guan-Zhou).

(51)

{\bf L}^{\prime\infty}(\Phi)=\inf_{v\in X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(A_{X}(v)+\Phi_{\rm NA}(v)).

Moreover by using the valuative description of multiplier ideals it is easy to prove:

(52)

\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}{\bf L}^{\rm NA}({\mathcal{X}}_{m},{\mathcal{L}}_{m})={\bf L}^{\prime\infty}(\Phi).

By reduced uniform stability, we know that there exists

such that

So we can estimate as follows:

\displaystyle{\bf L}^{\rm NA}(\phi_{m})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\gamma{\bf\Lambda}^{\rm NA}_{g}((\phi_{m})_{\xi_{m}})+(1-\gamma){\bf E}^{\rm NA}_{g}((\phi_{m})_{\xi_{m}})

\displaystyle\gamma{\bf\Lambda}^{\prime\infty}_{g}(\Phi_{\xi_{m}})+(1-\gamma){\bf E}^{\prime\infty}_{g}(\Phi_{\xi_{m}})

\displaystyle\gamma-1.

By letting

Examples of (local) klt singularities include orbifold singularities and log Fano cones.

2.
Application of
g
-soliton equations to Kähler Ricci-flat cone metrics

In this section, we first revisit the recent works of Apostolov et al. in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. They discovered a connection between a particular
g
-soliton
equation and Sasaki-Einstein metrics. This connection goes roughly as follows. Each Sasaki manifold has a CR and contact structure, and an associated Tanaka-Webster connections which coincides with the Levi-Civita connection.
The change of Reeb of vector field in the Reeb cone changes the associated Tanaka-Webster connection. The transformation formula for the Ricci curvature of the Tanaka-Webster connection, which was well-known in the CR literature (for example from
[
55
, 2]
), implies that Sasaki-Einstein metrics can be described as a
g
-soliton in the tensorial form on a quasi-regular quotient. Here we will understand their work via a different point of view by considering (transversal) Monge-Ampère equation that is equivalent to the Ricci-flat Kähler cone equation on affine varieties. We will see that this approach is in some way simpler and shows immediately that, more generally,
g
-soliton equations for different Reeb vector fields are related to each other. Finally we discuss how to combine this transformation and the Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture for
g
-solitons on general log Fano pairs to derive the Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture for polarized Fano cones.

2.1.
Kähler Ricci-flat cone metric

We will first review the Ricci-flat Kähler cone metric following the work of Martelli-Sparks-Yau in
[
74
]
.
Assume that
Y
is an affine variety with an isolated singularity

. Assume that there exists an effective torus action by

Set

The
\hat{\mathbb{T}}

\hat{{\mathbb{N}}}_{\mathbb{Z}}

(59)

\hat{N}_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}=\{\hat{\xi}\in\hat{N}_{\mathbb{R}};\langle\hat{\alpha},\hat{\xi}\rangle>0\text{ for any }\hat{\alpha}\neq 0\text{ with }R_{\hat{\alpha}}\neq 0\}.

Any

For any quasi-regular

(X,D)
equipped with an orbifold line bundle

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a

Proof.

Let

Set

∎

Appendix D
Multiplier ideal sheaf

Let
\varphi
be a psh function on a domain
U\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Then define the
m
-th multiplier ideal sheaf:

(155)

\mathcal{J}(m\varphi)(U)=\{f\in{\mathcal{O}}(U);\int_{U}|f|^{2}e^{-m\varphi}dV_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}<+\infty\}.

Take an orthonormal basis
\{f^{(m)}_{i};i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
of
\mathcal{J}(m\varphi)
and set:

(156)

\varphi_{m}=\frac{1}{m}\log\sum_{i}|f^{(m)}_{i}|^{2}.

Then by using Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem, Demailly proved

Theorem D.1
.

There exists constants
C_{1}>0
such that for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and any
z\in U

(157)

\varphi(z)-\frac{C_{1}}{m}\leq\varphi_{m}(z).

Moreover
\varphi_{m}
converges to
\varphi
pointwise and in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
topology on
U
as
m\rightarrow+\infty
.

More generally, on a projective manifold
X
for any psh Hermitian metric
h_{0}e^{-\varphi}
on a line bundle
L
, one can define multiplier ideal sheaf.
Boucksom-Favre-Jonsson gave a valuative description of multiplier ideals, which can be strengthened by using Demailly’s strong openness conjecture proved by Guan-Zhou:

Theorem D.2
.

f\in\mathcal{J}(m\varphi)
if and only if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
A_{X}(v)+v(f)-(1+\epsilon)v(\varphi)>0
for any
v\in X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Here
v(\varphi)
is called the generic Lelong number of
\varphi
with respect to
v
: if
v={\rm ord}_{E}
for a smooth divisor
E
on
Y\rightarrow X
. Then

v(\varphi)=\sup\{C>0;\varphi\leq C\log|z_{1}|^{2}+O(1)\}
where
z_{1}
is any local coordinate satisfying
E=\{z_{1}=0\}
.

Now let
\Phi
be a psh ray which is a psh Hermitian metric on
p_{1}^{*}(-K_{X})
. Let
v\in X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}
,
G(v)
be the Gauss extension which is the unique
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant valuation on
X\times{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying
G(v)(t)=1
and
G(v)|_{{\mathbb{C}}(X)}=v
.
By using this valuative description, Berman-Boucksom-Jonsson derives a valuative formula for
{\bf L}^{\prime\infty}(\Phi)
:

(158)

{\bf L}^{\prime\infty}(\Phi)=\inf_{v\in X^{\rm div}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(A_{X}(v)-G(v)(\Phi)).

Multiplier ideals satisfy the important a (Nadel) vanishing and a global generation property. The version we need is the following

be the multiplier ideal sheaf of

such that for any

,
\mathcal{O}_{X\times{\mathbb{C}}}((m+m_{0})p_{1}^{*}L)\otimes\mathcal{J}(m\Phi)

[

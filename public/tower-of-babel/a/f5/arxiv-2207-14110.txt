2.1.
Operators defined by discrete convolution

We consider as framework of this study the commutative
Banach Algebra with unit

\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}):=\{f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}:\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(n)|<\infty\},

equipped with the product the
discrete convolution
defined by

(f\ast g)(n):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n-j)g(j),\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

The unit for the product is given by
\delta
-Kronecker measure
concentrated at
0
, denoted by
\delta_{0}
. This element satifies: for all
a\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}),\ a\ast\delta_{0}=a
. It is convenient recall that the
\delta
-Kronecker measure
\delta_{n}
is defined by

\delta_{n}(m):=\begin{cases}1,&\quad m=n,\\
0,&\quad m\neq n.\end{cases}

Furthermore, we remark that
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
is semi-simple and regular Algebra (see
[
24
, Theorem 4.7.4]
) and
[
24
, Corolary 7.2.3]
respectively).

Given
a=(a(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
, we define the following discrete operator by convolution

Af(n):=(a\ast f)(n),\ {n\in\mathbb{Z}},\ f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}).

We say that
A
is a
discrete convolution operator
, with
kernel

a
. We note that
A\in{\mathcal{B}}(\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})):=\{B:\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})\to\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}):B\ \mbox{is linear and bounded}\}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
, since from Young’s inequality

\|Af(n)\|_{p}=\|(a\ast f)(n)\|_{p}\leq\|a\|_{1}\|f\|_{p}.

Moreover, the operator
A
satisfies

(2.1)

\|A\|_{1}=\|a\|_{1}.

Here, we understand the powers for
a\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
through discrete convolution, given by

a^{j}:=a^{j}(n):=(\underbrace{a\ast...\ast a}_{j-times})(n).

Obviously, we have
a^{0}=\delta_{0}
,
a^{1}=a
.

In the Banach Algebra
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
, given
\alpha,\beta>0,
we define the vector-valued
Mittag-Leffler function

E_{\alpha,\beta}:\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})\to\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
as follows

E_{\alpha,\beta}(a):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{j}}{\Gamma(\alpha j+\beta)},\quad a\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}).

We define
e^{at}

the exponential of
a
as follows

e^{ta}:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{t^{n}a^{n}\over n!},\ t\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}.

Note that
\displaystyle E_{1,1}(ta)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(ta)^{j}}{j!}=e^{ta}.

We denote
\{e^{tA}\}_{t\geq 0}
to the
family semigroup of operators
generated by the operator

. In the case of discrete convolution operators,
Af=a\ast f
, this family acts in fact by discrete convolution over
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
. Moreover, we have the explicit representation

e^{tA}f(n)=(e^{at}\ast f)(n),\ n\in\mathbb{Z},\ f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}),

and, for each

, from the equality (

(2.2)

\|e^{tA}\|_{1}=\|e^{ta}\|_{1}.

See

2.3.
Markovian semigroups

The concept of Markovian semigroups is the key for the achievement of this work.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space. We denote
M\subseteq\ell^{1}(X):=\ell^{1}(X,\Sigma,\mu)
as the subset of all density functions over
X

M=\{f\in\ell^{1}(X):f\geq 0,\|f\|_{1}=1\}.

We call
Markov Operator
to all linear mapping
F:\ell^{1}(X)\to\ell^{1}(X)
satisfying
F(M)\subset M
.

Definition 2.3
.

A family
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
of Markov operators is called
Markovian semigroup
if furthermore it is a
C_{0}
-semigroup family of operators.

Remark 2.4
.

We note that if
A
is a convolution operator with kernel
a\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
, with
e^{tA}
a Markovian semigroup, then
e^{ta}
satisfies
e^{ta}\geq 0
and
\|e^{ta}\|_{1}=1
. Reciprocally, the assertion is true using (
2.2
).

Remark 2.5
.

Let
\textbf{1}(n)
the sequence constantly equals to
1
. Let
A
be a convolution operator with kernel
a\in\ell^{1}(Z)
be a generator of a Markovian semigroup
e^{tA}
. Then the following equality holds

(2.3)

e^{tA}\textbf{1}(n)=\textbf{1}(n).

Proof.

We remark that
\textbf{1}(n)=\textbf{1}(n-m)
, for all
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
e^{tA}
is a Markovian semigroup, we have

\displaystyle e^{tA}\textbf{1}(n)=(e^{ta}\ast\textbf{1})(n)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ta}(m)\textbf{1}(n-m)=\textbf{1}(n)\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ta}(m)=\textbf{1}(n).

∎

Definition 2.6
.

Let

A family

(1)

is strongly continuous on

and

(2)

. That means that
S(t){D}(B)\subset{D}(B)
and
BS(t)x=S(t)Bx
, for all

and

;

(3)

The resolvent equation holds:

S(t)x=x+\int_{0}^{t}a(t-s)BS(s)xds,\quad\mbox{for all}\ x\in{D}(B),\,\,t\geq 0.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
B
be a closed linear operator with domain

and
a\in C(\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}).
A strongly continuous family
\{P(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
of bounded linear operators in

is called an

with generator

if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

P(0)=a(0)I;

(2)

P(t)
commutes with
B;

(3)

The integral resolvent equation holds:

P(t)x=a(t)x+\int_{0}^{t}a(t-s)BP(s)xds,\quad\mbox{for all}\ x\in{D}(B),t\geq 0.

Remark 2.8
.

It is well known the fact for the case

the resolvent family corresponds to the

is the fractional Caputo derivative,

,

some positive constant, the discrete convolution operator

In order to prove the main result of this work, for each

\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}:=\{u\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}):0\leq u(n)\leq\gamma,\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Let

This function satisfies properties presented in the following Lemma. The proof can be found in

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\gamma>0
. Suppose that

is measurable, the restriction of

to
[0,\gamma]

Then, the function
F
defined in (
3.1
) is no decreasing in the second variable and no negative for

.

We get the expression of the solution for the equation (
1.1
) employing the resolvent operator theory and the subordination principle on Wright function (

) presented in
[
6
,
21
]
. Let us define the following operators

called the

for (

for (

). Then, the mild solution

We consider the perturbed operator

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
S_{\beta}(t)
and
P_{\beta}
be the resolvent and integral resolvent respectively generated by the operator
B
. Let
S_{\beta}^{\rho}(t)
and

be the resolvent and integral resolvent respectively generated by the operator
-\rho I
. Then, we have the inequalities

(3.8)

S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)\leq S_{\beta}^{\rho}(t)\varphi(n)

and

(3.9)

P_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)\leq P_{\beta}^{\rho}(t)\varphi(n).

Proof.

We use the representation (
3.6
) (
3.7
) and the fact that

is the infinitesimal generator of a Markovian semigroup
e^{tB}

\displaystyle S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}t^{-\beta}\Phi_{\beta}(\tau t^{-\beta})e^{\tau B_{\rho}}\varphi(n)d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}t^{-\beta}\Phi_{\beta}(\tau t^{-\beta})e^{\tau B}e^{-t\rho}\varphi(n)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}t^{-\beta}\Phi_{\beta}(\tau t^{-\beta})e^{-t\rho}\varphi(n)d\tau

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}^{\rho}(t)\varphi(n),

Similarly for the integral resolvent, we consider any
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
, we get

\displaystyle P_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\beta\frac{\tau}{t^{1+\beta}}\Phi_{\beta}(\tau t^{-\beta})e^{\tau B_{\rho}}\varphi(n)d\tau

∎

Next, we present the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\gamma>0
. Suppose the hypotheses of Lemma
3.1
holds and
f(x,\gamma)\leq 0
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore, suppose that
\partial_{s}f(x,0)
is a non-increasing function of
x
. Let the initial value
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
. Then, there exists a unique mild solution
u\in C(\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+},\mathcal{L}_{\gamma})
to the equation (
3.5
) and hence a unique solution for nonlinear system (
1.1
).

Proof.

We used a iterative method. We define the following operator

(3.10)

\mathcal{K}_{\beta}u(n,t):=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,u(n,s))\ ds,

where
F(x,y)=\rho y+f(x,y)
and inequality (
3.2
) holds. Note that
F(x,0)=f(x,0)=0
. Furthermore, its follows from Dominated convergence Lebesgue Theorem that the operator
\mathcal{K}_{\beta}
is continuous over
C(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{L}_{\gamma})
.

We construct the sequence

v_{k}(n,t)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{K}_{\beta}v_{k-1}(n,t),&\ \mbox{if}\ k\geq 1,\\
0,&\ \mbox{if}\ k=0.\end{cases}

We computing
v_{1}(n,t)
, obtaining

v_{1}(n,t)=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,0)\ ds=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)\geq 0,

since
S_{\beta}(t)
is a positive operator. Using the fact that
F(x,y)
is no negative for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
0\leq y\leq\gamma
, we get

\displaystyle v_{1}(n,t)

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)

\displaystyle\leq S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,v_{1}(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle=v_{2}(n,t).

We remark that
F(x,y)
is no decreasing in the second variable by Lemma (
3.1
). Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We shall prove that
v_{k}(n,t)\leq v_{k+1}(n,t)
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle v_{k}(n,t)

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,v_{k}(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle\leq S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,v_{k+1}(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle=v_{k+1}(n,t).

Therefore, we get the inequalities
v_{0}\leq v_{1}\leq\ldots\leq v_{k}\leq v_{k+1}\leq\ldots
.

On the other hand, we consider the sequence

w_{k}(n,t)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{K}_{\beta}w_{k-1}(n,t),&\ \mbox{if}\ k\geq 1,\\
\gamma,&\ \mbox{if}\ k=0.\end{cases}

Then, using that
F(x,y)
is no decreasing in the second variable, we get

\displaystyle w_{1}(n,t)

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,w_{0}(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,\gamma)\ ds.

Note the fact that
0\leq F(x,y)\leq\rho\gamma
since
f(x,\gamma)\leq 0
. Using this fact and the inequalities (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) we obtain

\displaystyle w_{1}(n,t)

\displaystyle\leq S_{\beta}^{\rho}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}^{\rho}(t-s)F(n-cs,\gamma)\ ds

\displaystyle\leq S_{\beta}^{\rho}(t)\varphi(n)+\rho\gamma\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}^{\rho}(t-s)\ ds.

We recall that
0\leq\varphi(n)\leq\gamma
and the fact that
S_{\beta}^{\rho}(0)=1
. Furthermore, we recall that

\frac{d}{dt}S_{\beta}^{\rho}(t)=-\rho P_{\beta}^{\rho}(t).

Therefore, integrating the above equality and computing, we have

\displaystyle w_{1}(n,t)

\displaystyle\leq S_{\beta}^{\rho}(t)\varphi(n)+\rho\gamma\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}^{\rho}(t-s)\ ds

\displaystyle\leq S_{\beta}^{\rho}(t)\gamma-S_{\beta}^{\rho}(t)\gamma+\gamma

\displaystyle=\gamma.

We use above inequality and Lemma (
3.1
) for to obtain

F(n-cs,w_{1}(n,t))\leq F(n-cs,\gamma).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle w_{1}(n,t)

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,\gamma)\ ds

\displaystyle\leq S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,w_{1})\ ds

\displaystyle=w_{2}(n,t).

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We shall prove that
w_{k+1}(n,t)\leq w_{k}(n,t)
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle w_{k+1}(n,t)

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,w_{k}(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle\leq S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,w_{k-1})\ ds

\displaystyle=w_{k}(n,t).

Therefore, we get

\gamma\geq w_{1}(n,t)\geq w_{2}(n,t)\geq\ldots\geq w_{k}(n,t)\geq w_{k+1}(n,t)\geq\ldots.

Assertion
: For any
j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

0\leq v_{1}(n,t)\leq\ldots\leq v_{k}(n,t)\leq\ldots\leq w_{j}(n,t)\leq\ldots\leq\omega_{1}(n,t)\leq\gamma.

We suppose that there exists
j,k
such that
w_{j}(n,t)\leq v_{k}(n,t)
. Then, we can find the first entire number
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
w_{m}(n,t)>v_{m}(n,t)
. Using lemma (
3.1
) we get

\displaystyle 0<(v_{m}-w_{m})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)[F(n-cs,v_{m-1})-F(n-cs,w_{m-1})]\leq 0,

which is a contradiction.

Therefore, it is clear that
\{v_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{w_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are convergent sequences. Let us define
\displaystyle v:=\lim_{k\to\infty}v_{k}
and
\displaystyle w:=\lim_{k\to\infty}w_{k}
. It is clear from assertion that
v(n,t)\leq w(n,t)
. Moreover, we have that
v,w\in C(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{L}_{\gamma})
. Since the operator
K_{\beta}
is continuous over
C(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{L}_{\gamma})
, we have that
v
and
w
are solution for (
3.5
).

We shall prove uniqueness of solution. Indeed, the application the Mean Value Theorem yields

\displaystyle 0\leq(w-v)(n,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)[F(n-cs,w(n,t))-F(n-cs,v(n,t))]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)\partial_{s}F(n-cs,\theta)[(w-v)(n,t)],

where
v(n,t)\leq\theta\leq w(n,t)
. Since
\partial_{s}F(n-cs,\theta)=\rho+\partial_{s}f(n-cs,\theta)
, the Lemma (
3.1
) yields
\partial_{s}f(n-cs,\theta)
is non-increasing and hence
\partial_{s}f(n-cs,\gamma)\leq\partial_{s}f(n-cs,\theta)\leq\partial_{s}f(n-cs,0)
. Consequently, we have
\leq\partial_{s}F(n-cs,\theta)\leq\partial_{s}F(n-cs,0)
.

Observe that, for
y
fixed,
\partial_{s}F(\cdot,y)
is no decreasing. Then, from above inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq(w-v)(n,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)[F(n-cs,w(n,t))-F(n-cs,v(n,t))]

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}F(n,0)\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)[(w-v)(n,t)].

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}[w(n,t)-v(n,t)]

\displaystyle\leq\partial_{s}F(n,0)\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}[(w-v)(n,t)]

\displaystyle\leq\partial_{s}F(n,0)\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}^{\rho}(t-s)\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}[(w-v)(n,t)],

where we used that (
3.9
). Finally, from Gronwall inequality (see e.g
[
45
]
) for integral inequalities, we obtain
v\equiv w
, getting the desired result.
∎

Next, we present two consequences of the Theorem (
3.3
) concerning to a comparison principle between solution with respective initial values.

Corollary 3.4
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem (
3.3
). Let
u_{1}(n,t)
and
u_{2}(n,t)
the solutions for equation (
3.5
) with initial condition
\varphi
and
\psi
respectively. If
\varphi(n)\leq\psi(n)
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
u_{1}(n,t)\leq u_{2}(n,t)
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

Analogously to proof of the Theorem (
3.3
), we consider the sequence
\{u_{1,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{u_{2,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, defined according to iterative definition (
3
). By uniqueness of solution for the system (
3.5
), we have
u_{1,k}\to u_{1}
and
u_{2,k}\to u_{2}
when
n\to\infty
. Since
u_{1,0}=u_{2,0}=0
and
F(x,0)=0
, we have

\displaystyle u_{1,1}(n,t)

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,u_{1,0}(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)

and

\displaystyle u_{2,1}(n,t)

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,u_{2,0}(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\psi(n).

Since
S_{\beta}(t)
is a positive operator, we have
u_{1,1}(n,t)\leq u_{2,1}(n,t)
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\geq 0
.

Using Lemma (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle u_{1,2}(n,t)

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,u_{1,1}(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,u_{2,1}(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle=u_{2,2}(n,t).

By induction principle, we obtain
u_{1,k}(n,t)\leq u_{2,k}(n,t)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and therefore,
u_{1}(n,t)\leq u_{2}(n,t)
. The proof is finished.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem (
3.3
). Let
v,w\in C(\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+},\mathcal{L}_{\gamma})
such that
\mathcal{K}_{\beta}v(n,t)\geq v(n,t)
and
\mathcal{K}_{\beta}w(n,t)\leq w(n,t)
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
. Suppose that
v(n,0)\leq w(n,0)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the inequality
v(n,t)\leq w(n,t)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
holds.

Proof.

We use the Lemma (
3.1
) in order to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\beta}^{2}v(n,t)

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,\mathcal{K}_{\beta}v(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle\geq S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,v(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}_{\beta}v(n,t).

Applying recursively the above process, we get

v(n,t)\leq\mathcal{K}_{\beta}v(n,t)\leq\mathcal{K}_{\beta}^{2}v(n,t)\leq\ldots\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{K}_{\beta}^{k}v(n,t):=v^{*}(n,t).

Analogously, from Lemma (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\beta}^{2}w(n,t)

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,\mathcal{K}_{\beta}w(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle\leq S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(n)+\int_{0}^{t}P_{\beta}(t-s)F(n-cs,w(n,t))\ ds

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}_{\beta}w(n,t).

and proceeding inductively, we get

w(n,t)\geq\mathcal{K}_{\beta}w(n,t)\geq\mathcal{K}_{\beta}^{2}w(n,t)\geq\ldots\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{K}_{\beta}^{k}w(n,t):=w^{*}(n,t).

Note that
v^{*}
and
w^{*}
there exists, since the operator
\mathcal{K}_{\beta}
is continuous.
Furthermore, we remark that
v^{*}
and

are solution for the equation (

) with initial values

and

respectively. Then, the Corollary (

) yields

v(n,t)\leq v^{*}(n,t)\leq w^{*}(n,t)\leq w(n,t),

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and

. The proof is finished.
∎

4.1.
Convolution Operators

We present a sufficient condition in order to guarantee the Markov properties for the
C_{0}
-semigroup generated by the fractional powers of a discrete convolution operator generating a Markovian
C_{0}
-semigroup.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}))
be an operator by discrete convolution, namely
Af(n)=(a\ast f)(n)
, which is the infinitesimal generator of Markovian semigroup
e^{tA}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then, the semigroup generated for the fractional powers
-(-A)^{\alpha}
, namely
e^{-t(-A)^{\alpha}}
is a Markovian semigroup.

Proof.

The key of the proof is the Lévy subordination principle and its properties.
Let
\phi\geq 0
be a sequence in
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Since
\alpha\in(0,1)
, from remark (
5.2
) property i) we have

e^{-t(-A)^{\alpha}}\phi(n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\lambda A}\phi(n)f_{t,\alpha}(\lambda)d\lambda,\quad t>0.

The positivity of Lévy process (see remark (
5.2
), property ii)) and
e^{tA}
(Markovian property) yield

e^{-t(-A)^{\alpha}}\phi(n)\geq 0.

Let us choose
\phi(n)=\delta_{0}(n)
. A straightforward computation yield

0\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\lambda A}\delta_{0}(n)f_{t,\alpha}(\lambda)d\lambda=e^{-t(-A)^{\alpha}}\delta_{0}(n)=e^{-t(-a)^{\alpha}}.

Let
\textbf{1}(n)
be the sequence constantly equals to
1
considered in (
2.3
). We use again the integral representation of Lévy subordination principle, and (
2.3
) in order to obtain

e^{-t(-A)^{\alpha}}\textbf{1}(n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\lambda A}\textbf{1}(n)f_{t,\alpha}(\lambda)d\lambda=\textbf{1}(n)\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{t,\alpha}(\lambda)d\lambda=\textbf{1}(n).

On the other hand, since
e^{-t(-A)^{\alpha}}
is defined by discrete convolution, we have

e^{-t(-A)^{\alpha}}\textbf{1}(n)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-t(-a)^{\alpha}}(m)\textbf{1}(n-m)=\textbf{1}(n)\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-t(-a)^{\alpha}}(m).

Therefore, from above equalities, we have
\displaystyle\|e^{-(-a)^{\alpha}}\|_{1}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-t(-a)^{\alpha}}(n)=1
. From (
2.2
), we obtain
\|e^{-(-A)^{\alpha}}\|_{1}=1
. The proof is finished.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. The semigroups
e^{-t(-\Delta_{d})^{\alpha}}
,
e^{-t(\Delta/-\nabla)^{\alpha}}
and
e^{-t(-\Delta_{dd})^{\alpha}}
are Markovian.

Proof.

A straightforward computation yields that
e^{t\Delta_{d}}
and
e^{t(-\Delta/\nabla)}
are Markovian semigroups. Indeed, from generatrix formula for
I_{\nu}
Bessel function, we have

\|e^{t\Delta_{d}}\|_{1}=e^{-2t}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{n}(2t)=1

and

\|e^{t\Delta_{dd}}\|_{1}=e^{-2t}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{\frac{n}{2}}\chi_{2\mathbb{Z}}(2t)=1.

Furthermore

\|e^{t(-\Delta/\nabla)}\|_{1}=e^{-t}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{t^{(\pm n)}}{(\pm n)!}\chi_{\mathbb{Z}_{\pm}}(n)=1.

The positive condition is immediate from positivity of
I_{n}(2t),e^{-2t}
and
t^{n}
, since
t\geq 0
.

Therefore, from the above proposition, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Remark 4.3

[

]
, Theorem 1.3, v) in the study of the discrete fractional Laplacian and the properties of the generated semigroup.

) satisfies all the hypotheses of the Theorem (
3.3
).

(3)

We can generalize the above function. Let
r:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous, no decreasing, bounded and piecewise continuously differentiable satisfying
0<r(\infty)<\infty
. In fact, we define
r(\infty)=\gamma^{\frac{1}{p}}
.
Let
p\geq 1
. We define the nonlinear function
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
as follows

(4.3)

f(x,s)=s(r(x)-s^{p}).

It is clear that
f
is a measurable function, the restriction
f(x,\cdot)
belongs to
C^{1}(0,\gamma)
and
f(x,0)=0
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Its partial derivatives
\partial_{s}f(x,s)=r(x)-(p+1)s^{p}
and
\partial_{s}^{2}f(x,s)=-p(p+1)s^{p-1}<0
gives us the concavity of
f
on
[0,\gamma]
. Let us choose
\rho=(p+2)\gamma^{p}
. With this choice, we ensure that the inequality (
3.2
) holds. Furthermore, its clear that
\partial_{s}f(x,0)
is a no decreasing function, and
f(x,\gamma)=\gamma(r(x)-\gamma^{p})=\gamma^{\frac{1}{p}}(r(x)-\gamma)\leq 0
. Therefore, the function
f
defined in (
4.3
) satisfies all the hypotheses of the Theorem (
3.3
).

Remark 4.4
.

The model (
1.1
), considering the fractional Laplacian and the nonlinear function (
4.1
) is the well-known
discrete fractional generalized Fisher-KPP equation
. This includes the discrete generalized Fisher-KPP equation with delay since
f(n-ct,s)=s(r(n-ct)-s)
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and some constant
c>0
. The classical Fisher-KPP equation corresponds to the case where
r(x)\equiv\gamma
.

When we considerate
r\equiv 1
and
p=2
, we recover the
Newell-Whitehead-Segel equation
, which describes the so called Rayleigh-Benard convection. The Newell-Whitehead-Segel equation is a well-known universal equation to govern evolution of nearly one-dimensional nonlinear patterns produced by a finite-wavelength instability in isotropic two-dimensional media. More references for these applications, see
[
28
]
and
[
33
]
.

5.
Appendix

In this appendix we give the basic concepts of the special functions involved in the development of this study.

5.1.
Wright function
\Phi_{\gamma}

The Wright type function with one parameter from
[
16
, Formula (28)]
(see also
[
30
,
32
,
44
]
) is given by

(5.1)

\Phi_{\alpha}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-z)^{n}}{n!\Gamma(-\alpha n+1-\alpha)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\mu^{\alpha-1}e^{\mu-z\mu^{\alpha}}d\mu,\quad 0<\alpha<1,

where
\gamma
is a contour which starts and ends
at
-\infty
and encircles the origin once counterclockwise. This special case has sometimes been called
Mainardi
function.

Remark 5.1
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
t>0
and
0<\alpha,\gamma<1
. Then the following properties hold:

(i)

\displaystyle E_{\gamma,1}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{\gamma}(t)e^{zt}dt
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha}(t)\geq 0
.

(iii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{\alpha}(t)dt=1
.

It follows from (ii) and (iii) that
\Phi_{\alpha}
is a probability density function on
\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
.
Actually, the Wright function has been used for models in stochastic processes
[
16
,
17
]
.

5.2.
Lévy process
f_{t,\alpha}

We present the following function, called stable Lévy process, defined for
0<\alpha<1
by

(5.2)

f_{t,\alpha}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}e^{z\lambda-tz^{\alpha}}dz,\;\;\;\;\sigma>0,\quad t>0,&\lambda\geq 0,\\
0\quad&\lambda<0,\end{cases}

where the branch of
z^{\alpha}
is taken so that
\mbox{Re}(z^{\alpha})>0
for
\mbox{Re}(z)>0.
This branch is single-valued in the

z
-plane cut along the negative real axis.
These functions were introduced by S. Bochner
[
8
]
in the study of certain stochastic processes. K. Yosida
[
46
]
used them systematically in the study of
C_{0}
-semigroups generated by fractional powers of uniformly bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups of linear operators.
The Lévy functions are the density functions associated with the stable Lévy processes in
the rotational invariant case, and are related to the fractional Brownian motion.

Remark 5.2
.

The following properties hold:

(i)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda a}f_{t,\alpha}(\lambda)d\lambda=e^{-ta^{\alpha}},\quad t>0,\quad a>0,\quad 0<\alpha<1.

(ii)

\displaystyle f_{t,\alpha}(\lambda)\geq 0,\quad\lambda>0,\,\,t>0,\quad 0<\alpha<1.

(iii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{t,\alpha}(\lambda)d\lambda=1,\quad t>0,\quad 0<\alpha<1.

For a proof of (i)-(iii), see
[
46
, p.260-262]
.

5.3.
Bessel function
I_{\nu}

For
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, the
Modified Bessel functions of the first kind
is defined by

(5.3)

I_{\nu}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\Gamma(n+\nu+1)n!}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2n+\nu}.

It is direct from the definition (
5.3
) that
I_{n}(x)\geq 0,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},\,\,x\geq 0
,
Following propierties can be founded in
[
18
, Formula 8.511]
,
[
4
]
.

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{n}(x)z^{n}=\displaystyle e^{\frac{x}{2}(z+\frac{1}{z})},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

(2)

I_{-n}(x)=I_{n}(x)=(-1)^{n}I_{n}(-x)
.

(3)

\displaystyle I_{n}(x+y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{n-k}(x)I_{k}(y)
.

On the distribution of heat in fibered magnetic fields
Theodore D. Drivas, Daniel Ginsberg, Hezekiah Grayer II

Abstract
. We study the equilibrium temperature distribution in a model for strongly magnetized plasmas in dimension two and higher. Provided the magnetic field is sufficiently structured (integrable in the sense that it is fibered by co-dimension one invariant tori, on most of which the field lines ergodically wander) and the effective thermal diffusivity transverse to the tori is small, it is proved that the temperature distribution is well approximated by a function that only varies across the invariant surfaces. The same result holds for “nearly integrable” magnetic fields up to a “critical” size. In this case, a volume of non-integrability is defined in terms of the temperature defect distribution and related the non-integrable structure of the magnetic field, confirming a physical conjecture of Paul–Hudson–Helander [ 6 ] . Our proof crucially uses a certain quantitative ergodicity condition for the magnetic field lines on full measure set of invariant tori, which is automatic in two dimensions for magnetic fields without null points and, in higher dimensions, is guaranteed by a Diophantine condition on the rotational transform of the magnetic field.

) defines the anisotropic spaces

tailored to the tori. The result is then

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that

for some
\gamma\geq 0

and that

satisfies

(2.10)

\int_{S_{\psi}}\frac{F}{|\nabla\psi|}\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}=0,

for all
\psi\in[\psi_{-},\psi_{+}]
. There is a constant
C
depending
only on

so that

we have

(2.11)

\left|\int_{D}Fu\,{\rm d}\mu\right|\leq CM\|F\|_{H^{(0,\gamma)}}\|\nabla_{b}u\|_{L^{2}}+C\mu(N(\gamma,M))^{1/2}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

By the co-area formula (
A.1
), we have

(2.12)

\int_{D}Fu\,{\rm d}\mu=\int_{\psi_{-}}^{\psi_{+}}\int_{S_{\psi}}\frac{F}{|\nabla\psi|}u\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}{\rm d}\psi.

Now, for each
\psi
, we write

(2.13)

\int_{S_{\psi}}\frac{F}{|\nabla\psi|}u\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{d-1}}G(\psi,\theta_{1},...,\theta_{d-1})u(\psi,\theta_{1},...,\theta_{d-1}){\rm d}\theta_{1}\cdots{\rm d}\theta_{d-1},

where
G=\frac{F}{|\nabla\psi|}|h|^{1/2}
,
where we are writing the metric on

as
h=h_{\alpha\beta}{\rm d}\theta^{\alpha}{\rm d}\theta^{\beta}

and
|h|=\det h_{\alpha\beta}
. By Parseval’s theorem,

\int_{[0,2\pi]^{d-1}}G(\psi,\theta_{1},...,\theta_{d-1})u(\psi,\theta_{1},...,\theta_{d-1}){\rm d}\theta_{1}\cdots{\rm d}\theta_{d-1}=(2\pi)^{d-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}}\widehat{G}(\psi,k)\widehat{u}(\psi,-k),

where
\widehat{F},\widehat{u}
are defined as in (
2.8
). Now we note that

(2.15)

\widehat{G}(\psi,0)=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{d-1}}\frac{F}{|\nabla\psi|}|h|^{1/2}{\rm d}\theta_{1}\cdots{\rm d}\theta_{d-1}=\int_{S_{\psi}}\frac{F}{|\nabla\psi|}\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}=0,

by assumption. We therefore have

(2.16)

\int_{S_{\psi}}\frac{F}{|\nabla\psi|}u\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}=(2\pi)^{d-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\setminus 0}\widehat{G}(\psi,k)\widehat{u}(\psi,-k),

and it follows that

(2.17)

\left|\int_{D}Fu\,{\rm d}\mu\right|\leq C\int_{\psi_{-}}^{\psi_{+}}\sum_{|k|\not=0}|\widehat{G}(\psi,k)\widehat{u}(\psi,-k)|\,{\rm d}\psi.

Now we split
[\psi_{-},\psi_{+}]=E(\gamma,M)\cup N(\gamma,M)
and bound

(2.18)

\int_{\psi\in E(\gamma,M)}\sum_{|k|\not=0}|\widehat{G}(\psi,k)\widehat{u}(\psi,-k)|\,\,{\rm d}\psi\\
\leq\int_{\psi\in E(\gamma,M)}\|G\|_{H^{\gamma}(S_{\psi})}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}(S_{\psi})}\,{\rm d}\psi\leq M\int_{\psi\in E(\gamma,M)}\|G\|_{H^{\gamma}(S_{\psi})}\|\nabla_{B}u\|_{L^{2}(S_{\psi})}\,{\rm d}\psi\\
\leq M\|G\|_{H^{(0,\gamma)}(D)}\|\nabla_{B}u\|_{L^{2}(D)},

by the definition of
E(\gamma,M)

and

(2.19)

\int_{\psi\in N(\gamma,M)}\sum_{|k|\not=0}|\widehat{G}(\psi,k)\widehat{u}(\psi,-k)|\,\,{\rm d}\psi\leq\|G\|_{L^{2}(N(\gamma,M))}\|u\|_{L^{2}(D)}\\
\leq\mu(N(\gamma,M))^{1/2}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(D)}.

This gives the result.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
: The 3d integrable case

We first show that if

1.2

) holds
with

Let

. Fix
\iota=\iota(\psi)
with
\iota\in L^{\infty}(I)

Let
|(m,n)|=\sqrt{m^{2}+n^{2}}
. We define

(4.1)

D(\gamma,M)=\left\{\psi\in I:|m+\iota(\psi)n|\geq\frac{1}{M|(m,n)|^{\gamma}}\quad\text{for all}\quad(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\right\}.

If
\psi\in D(\gamma,M)
for some
\gamma,M
, we will say that
\psi
is
“Diophantine” and that the surface
S_{\psi}
is a “Diophantine surface”.
With

replaced by
\omega\cdot(m,n)
for
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
these sets play a fundamental role in the proof of the celebrated KAM theorem

[
19
]
.

At least when
\iota(\psi)=\psi
, these sets are empty if
\gamma<2

(see
[
18
]
),
but it turns out
that if
\iota
is bi-Lipschitz and
\gamma>2
they have positive measure;
in fact the complement of
\cup_{M>0}D(\gamma,M)
has zero measure,
as the next result shows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\iota:I\to\mathbb{R}
be an invertible function
and suppose there is
L>0
so that

(4.2)

\frac{1}{L}|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}|\leq|\iota(\psi_{1})-\iota(\psi_{2})|\leq L|\psi_{1}-\psi_{1}|.

Define
D(\gamma,M)
as in (
4.1
) and let
\mu
denote the
one-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
If
\gamma>2
and
M>0
, there is a constant
K
depending only on

\mu(I)
,
L
, and
1/(\gamma-2)
so that

(4.3)

\mu(I\setminus D(\gamma,M))\leq K\left(\frac{1}{M^{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}}+\frac{1}{M}\right).

Taking
M\to\infty
in (
4.3
) shows that the set of
\psi

which fails the condition
(
4.1
) for
all

M>0
has zero measure. Equivalently, the set of
\psi
the condition in (
4.1
) for
some

M
has full measure, though
in general the complement may be nonempty.

Proof.

This result follows from a straightforward modification
of the argument from e.g.
[
18
]
. We include the details
here for the convenience of the reader.
For
(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus(0,0)
, we define

(4.4)

\Pi_{(m,n)}(\gamma,M)=\left\{\psi\in I:|m+\iota(\psi)n|<\frac{1}{M|(m,n)|^{\gamma}}\right\},

so that

(4.5)

I\setminus D(\gamma,M)\subseteq\bigcup_{(m,n)\not=(0,0)}\Pi_{(m,n)}(\gamma,M).

If
n\not=0
,
\Pi_{(m,n)}(\gamma,M)
is contained in the interval

[\psi_{1},\psi_{2}]\subset I
where
\psi_{1}
are such that
\iota(\psi_{1})=\frac{M}{n}\frac{1}{|(m,n)|^{\gamma}}+\frac{m}{n}
and

\iota(\psi_{2})=-\iota(\psi_{1})

(such
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
exist and are unique since by (
4.2
)
\iota
is invertible). These are the maximal and minimal values of
\psi
such that (
4.4
) holds for a given
(m,n)
.
Therefore

(4.6)

\mu(\Pi_{(m,n)}(\gamma,M))\leq|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}|\leq\frac{L}{2M|n||(m,n)|^{\gamma}}.

If
n=0
,
\Pi_{(m,0)}(\gamma,M)=I

when
|m|<M^{-1/(1+\gamma)}
and it is empty otherwise. Therefore

(4.7)

\sum_{(m,n)\not=(0,0)}\mu(\Pi_{(m,n)}(\gamma,M))\leq\sum_{m\not=0}\mu(\Pi_{(m,0)}(\gamma,M))+\sum_{(m,n)\not=(0,0),n\not=0}\mu(\Pi_{(m,n)}(\gamma,M))\\
\leq|I|\frac{1}{M^{1/(1+\gamma)}}+\frac{L}{M}+\frac{L}{2M}\sum_{|m|,|n|\geq 1}\frac{1}{|n||(m,n)|^{\gamma}},

since there are two terms in the second sum on the first line with

.
The last term here is bounded by

(4.8)

2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{\gamma/2}}\leq 2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z(m^{2}+z^{2})^{\gamma/2}}=2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{\gamma}}\int_{1/m}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}w}{w(1+w^{2})^{\gamma/2}}\\
\leq 2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{\gamma-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}w}{(1+w^{2})^{\gamma/2}}

which is finite if
\gamma>2
. In this case we therefore have

(4.9)

\mu\left(I\setminus D(\gamma,M)\right)\leq\mu\left({\bigcup}_{(m,n)\not=(0,0)}\Pi_{m,n}(\gamma,M)\right)\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{1}{M^{1/(1+\gamma)}}+\frac{1}{M}\right),

for a constant
C_{1}
depending only on
|I|
,
L
and

,
which proves (
1.26
).
∎

(5.11)

\|\nabla_{b}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{b}^{\perp}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2a}}{2\delta}\|\partial_{\theta}\chi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|T_{0}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\|\nabla_{b}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\left|\int_{\psi_{-}}^{\psi_{+}}\int_{S_{\psi}}\frac{\div\nabla T_{0}}{|\nabla\psi|}\rho\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}{\rm d}\psi\right|.

Arguing as in (
2.24
)-(
2.26
)
and using (
5.3
), this implies that there is a constant
C>0
so that
for any
M>0
,

(5.12)

\|\nabla_{b_{0}}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{b_{0}}^{\perp}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\leq C\varepsilon^{2a}\|\partial_{\theta}\chi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|T_{0}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(M^{2}\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon\mu(N(\gamma,M))\right)\left(\|\Delta T_{0}\|_{H^{(0,\gamma)}}^{2}+\|\Delta T_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right).

If we take
M=\varepsilon^{-1/3}
and use the estimate (
4.12
)
for
\mu(N(\gamma,M))
, we find

(5.13)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{b_{0}}^{\perp}\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2a-1}\|\partial_{\theta}\chi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\|\Delta T_{0}\|_{H^{(0,\gamma)}}^{2}+\|\Delta T_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right),

(5.14)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{b_{0}}\rho\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2a}\|\partial_{\theta}\chi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}\left(\|\Delta T_{0}\|_{H^{(0,\gamma)}}^{2}+\|\Delta T_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right).

∎

5.2.
Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Before proving Corollary
1.3
, we collect some preliminary results.
First, from the uniform bounds (
1.21
) and (
1.22
),
it follows that that the sequence
T_{\varepsilon}-T_{0}
has weak limit

\rho^{*}
in
H^{1}
and that
\nabla_{b}(T_{\varepsilon}-T_{0})
converges strongly to

0
in
L^{2}
.

If we knew that
\nabla_{B}\rho^{*}
was smooth, it would follow that

\nabla_{B}\rho^{*}=0
everywhere. We however only know that
\nabla_{B}\rho^{*}

is in
L^{2}
and in particular the restriction
\nabla_{B}\rho^{*}|_{S_{\psi}}

need not be defined. The following result shows that
\nabla_{B}\rho^{*}|_{S_{\psi}}=0

in a weak sense.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\rho^{*}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}T_{\varepsilon}-T_{0}
. For any

\psi
and any
v\in C^{2}(S_{\psi})
, with
B^{\prime}=B|\nabla\psi|^{-1}
,

(5.15)

\int_{S_{\psi}}\rho^{*}\div_{S_{\psi}}(B^{\prime}v)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}=0.

Remark
.

The statement of (
5.15
) holds with
B^{\prime}
replaced by
B

(or indeed any multiple of
B
) but it is more convenient for our purposes
to use
B^{\prime}
since
\div_{S_{\psi}}(B^{\prime}v)=|\nabla\psi|\div(Bv)=|\nabla\psi|\nabla_{B}v
,
by (
A.6
) and the fact that
\div B=0
. See Lemma
5.3
.

Proof.

The idea is to use Stokes’ theorem to write (
5.15
) in
terms of an integral in the interior of
D
and to integrate by parts
in the interior to exploit the fact that
\nabla_{B}\rho^{*}=0
almost everywhere.

Fix a surface
S_{\psi^{\prime}}
.
Define a cutoff function

Q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
Q(z)=1
when
|z|\leq 1
and
Q(z)=0

when
|z|>2
. Define
Q_{\delta}(\psi)=Q((\psi-\psi^{\prime})/\delta)
, so that

Q_{\delta}(\psi)
vanishes when
|\psi-\psi^{\prime}|<2\delta
and

Q_{\delta}(\psi)\equiv 1
when
|\psi-\psi^{\prime}|\leq\delta
. Let

D_{-}(\psi^{\prime})=D\cap\{\psi\leq\psi^{\prime}\}
. Then the boundary of
D_{-}(\psi^{\prime})

is
S_{\psi^{\prime}}\cup S_{\psi_{-}}
, and if
\delta
is sufficiently small,
Q_{\delta}

vanishes identically on
S_{\psi_{-}}
.

Let
V
denote the constant extension of
v
to
D_{-}(\psi^{\prime})
,

V(\psi,\theta,\phi)=v(\psi^{\prime},\theta,\phi)
for all
(\psi,\theta,\phi)\in D_{-}(\psi^{\prime})
.
By Stokes’ theorem, since the outer unit normal to
S_{\psi^{\prime}}
is

n=\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|}
, we have

(5.16)

\int_{S_{\psi^{\prime}}}\rho^{*}\div_{S_{\psi^{\prime}}}(B^{\prime}u)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}=\int_{S_{\psi^{\prime}}}(n\cdot n)\,\rho^{*}\div_{S_{\psi^{\prime}}}(B^{\prime}V)Q_{\delta}\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}\\
=\int_{D_{-}}Q_{\delta}\div\left(\rho^{*}\div_{S_{\psi^{\prime}}}(B^{\prime}V)\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|}\right){\rm d}\mu+\int_{D_{-}}\rho^{*}\div_{S_{\psi^{\prime}}}(B^{\prime}V)\,\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|}\cdot(\nabla Q_{\delta})\,{\rm d}\mu.

The first term here is bounded by

(5.17)

\|\div_{S_{\psi^{\prime}}}(B^{\prime}V)\|_{H^{1}(|\psi-\psi^{\prime}|\leq\delta)}\|\rho^{*}\|_{H^{1}(|\psi-\psi^{\prime}|\leq\delta)}\leq C\delta^{1/2}\|v\|_{C^{2}(S_{\psi^{\prime}})}\|\rho^{*}\|_{H^{1}(D)}.

As for the second term, we use the identity (
A.6
) to write

(5.18)

\div_{S_{\psi}}(B^{\prime}V)=\frac{1}{|\nabla\psi|}\div(BV),

and since
\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|}\cdot(\nabla Q_{\delta})=\frac{1}{\delta}|\nabla\psi|Q_{\delta}^{\prime}

and
b
is tangent to
S_{\psi^{\prime}}
,
the second term in (
5.16
) is

(5.19)

\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{D_{-}}\rho^{*}\div_{S_{\psi^{\prime}}}(B^{\prime}V)\,|\nabla\psi|Q_{\delta}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}\mu=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{D_{-}}\rho^{*}\div(BV)Q_{\delta}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}\mu=-\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{D_{-}}\nabla_{B}(\rho^{*}Q_{\delta}^{\prime})V\,{\rm d}\mu,

after integrating by parts.
We have therefore shown that for any
\delta>0
,

(5.20)

\left|\int_{S_{\psi^{\prime}}}\rho^{*}\div_{S_{\psi}}(B^{\prime}V)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}\right|\leq C\delta^{1/2}\|v\|_{C^{2}(S_{\psi^{\prime}})}\|\rho^{*}\|_{H^{1}(D)}+\frac{1}{\delta}C\left(\|\nabla_{B}\rho^{*}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla_{B}Q_{\delta}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|V\|_{L^{2}}\\
=C\delta^{1/2}\|v\|_{C^{2}(S_{\psi^{\prime}})}\|\rho^{*}\|_{H^{1}(D)},

where we used that
\nabla_{B}u=0
whenever
u=u(\psi)
and that
\nabla_{B}\rho^{*}=0

in
L^{2}
. Taking
\delta\to 0
gives the claim.
∎

The condition (
5.15
) nearly says that
\rho^{*}=0
on each
S_{\psi}

in the sense of distributions, but in order to conclude this we would need to
know that any test function
v
can be written in the form

v=\div_{S_{\psi}}(Bw)
for some test function
w
. This need not be possible
on an arbitrary surface
S_{\psi}
, but by the next Lemma it is possible
provided
S_{\psi}
is a Diophantine surface. We set

(5.21)

D(\gamma)=\bigcup_{M>0}D(\gamma,M).

Note that by (
4.11
),
D(\gamma)\subseteq E(\gamma)
where

E(\gamma)=\cup_{M>0}E(\gamma,M)
denotes the collection of all ergodic
values of
\psi
. Note also that by Lemma
4.1
, the complement

I\setminus D(\gamma)
has zero measure when
\gamma>2
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Fix
\gamma>2
and define
D(\gamma)
as in (
5.21
).
If
\psi\in D(\gamma)
and
v\in H^{s+\gamma}(S_{\psi})
for some

s\geq 0
, there is

w\in H^{s}(S_{\psi})
satisfying

(5.22)

\div_{S_{\psi}}(B^{\prime}w)=v,\qquad B^{\prime}=B|\nabla\psi|^{-1}.

Remark
.

The equation (
5.22
) is sometimes known as the “magnetic
differential equation”. Lemma
5.3
simply says that you can solve this equation on good (sufficiently ergodic) flux surfaces.

Proof.

We start by using (
A.6
) to write

(5.23)

\div_{S_{\psi}}(B^{\prime}w)=\frac{1}{|\nabla\psi|}\div(Bw)=\frac{1}{|\nabla\psi|}\nabla_{B}w,

and so (
5.22
) takes the form

(5.24)

\nabla_{B/J}w=V,\qquad V=J|\nabla\psi|v,

where recall
J=|g|^{-1/2}=|(\nabla\psi\times\nabla\theta)\cdot\nabla\phi|
. We now define

(5.25)

\widehat{w}(m,n)=\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{m+\iota(\psi)n}\widehat{V}(m,n).

Because
\psi\in D(\gamma)
, for some
M>0
, we have the bound

(5.26)

|\widehat{w}(m,n)|^{2}\leq M^{2}\left(m^{2}+n^{2}\right)^{\gamma}|\widehat{V}(m,n)|^{2},

so that in particular
w\in H^{s}(S_{\psi})
whenever
v=V/(J|\nabla\psi|)\in H^{s+\gamma}(S_{\psi})
. By the identity (
4.14
), it follows that

w
satisfies (
5.22
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Fix
\gamma>2
and define
D(\gamma)
as in (
5.21
).
It follows from Lemma
5.3
that given
v\in C^{\infty}(S_{\psi})
,
there is
w\in C^{\infty}(S_{\psi})
so that
with
\div_{S_{\psi}}(B|\nabla\psi|w)=v
. It then follows from
Lemma
5.15
that

(5.27)

\int_{S_{\psi}}\rho^{*}v\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}=\int_{S_{\psi}}\rho^{*}\div_{S_{\psi}}(B|\nabla\psi|w)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}=0,

as required.
∎

5.3.
Proof of Proposition
1.1
: effective volume of non-integrability

It follows immediately from the equation (
1.9
)
(or, equivalently, (
2.6
)) for
T_{0}=\Theta(\psi)
that either

T_{0}
is constant or else
T_{0}^{\prime}
is nonvanishing.
Since
T_{0}|_{S_{+}}=T_{+}\not=T_{-}=T_{0}|_{S_{-}}
we
have that
T_{0}^{\prime}
is nonvanishing and in particular
T_{0}^{\prime}
is bounded below.
With
\lambda=\min_{D}\frac{1}{|T_{0}^{\prime}|}
,
for any set
N\subseteq D
we therefore have

(5.28)

\mu(N)\leq\lambda^{2}\int_{N}|T_{0}^{\prime}|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mu\leq C\left(\lambda^{2}\int_{N}|\nabla_{b}^{\perp}T|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mu+\lambda^{2}\int_{N}|\nabla_{b}^{\perp}\rho|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mu\right),

where we used that
T_{0}^{\prime}=|\nabla\psi|^{-2}\nabla T_{0}\cdot\nabla\psi

and that
|\nabla T_{0}|\leq|\nabla_{b}^{\perp}T|\leq|\nabla_{b}^{\perp}T|+|\nabla_{b}^{\perp}\rho|^{2}
.
In particular, with
N=N(\varepsilon)
,
since
\|\nabla_{b}^{\perp}T\|_{L^{2}(N(\varepsilon))}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\|\nabla_{b}T\|_{L^{2}(N(\varepsilon))}^{2}
,

(5.29)

\mu(N(\varepsilon))\leq C\lambda^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{N(\varepsilon)}|\nabla_{b}T|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mu+\int_{N(\varepsilon)}|\nabla_{b}^{\perp}\rho|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mu\right)\\
\leq C\lambda^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{N(\varepsilon)}|\nabla_{b}T_{0}|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mu+\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{b}\rho|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mu+\int_{D}|\nabla_{b}^{\perp}\rho|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mu\right)\\
\leq C\lambda^{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\left(\int_{N(\varepsilon)}|\nabla_{b}T_{0}|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mu+\int_{D}|\nabla_{b}\rho|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mu+\varepsilon\int_{D}|\nabla_{b}^{\perp}\rho|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mu\right).

Since
b=b_{0}+\varepsilon^{a}b_{1}
and
\nabla_{b_{0}}T_{0}=0
, we have

(5.30)

\|\nabla_{b}T_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2a}\|\nabla_{b_{1}}T_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2a}\|\partial_{\theta}\chi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\|T_{0}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}^{2}.

To handle the second and third terms in (
5.29
), we
use (
1.21
)-(
1.22
) combined with (
5.3
). By (
5.30
)
we therefore have

(5.31)

\mu(N(\varepsilon))\leq C\lambda^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2a-1}\|\partial_{\theta}\chi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\|T_{0}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}^{2}+\varepsilon^{1/3}\|\Delta T_{0}\|_{H^{(0,\gamma)}(D)}^{2}\right).

Appendix A
Geometric identities from the co-area formula

In this section we collect some geometric formulas that we will use repeatedly. In what follows, we fix
\psi:D\to\mathbb{R}
such that
|\nabla\psi|\neq 0
on
D
and so that the level surfaces

S_{\psi}
are codimension one manifolds which foliate
D
. Let
\psi_{-}=\inf_{D}\psi
and
\psi_{+}=\sup_{D}\psi
.
We will use the co-area formula

(A.1)

\int_{D}u\,{\rm d}\mu=\int_{\psi_{-}}^{\psi_{+}}\int_{S_{\psi}}\frac{u}{|\nabla\psi|}\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}{\rm d}\psi,

see e.g.
[
22
]
.
We start with a simple result that generalizes Lemma E.2 from
[
8
]
.

Lemma A.1
.

If
F\in H^{2}(D)
, we have

(A.2)

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\psi}\int_{S_{\psi}}F\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}=\int_{S_{\psi}}\div\left(\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|}F\right)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}.

Proof.

We start with the observation that

(A.3)

\int_{S_{\psi_{2}}}F\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}-\int_{S_{\psi_{1}}}F\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}=\int_{D_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}}\div\left(\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|}F\right)\,{\rm d}\mu,

where
D_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}=\cup_{\psi_{1}\leq\psi^{\prime}\leq\psi_{2}}S_{\psi^{\prime}}

denotes the region bounded by the surfaces
S_{\psi_{1}},S_{\psi_{2}}
. Indeed,
by the divergence theorem,

(A.4)

\int_{D_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}}\div\left(\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|}F\right)\,{\rm d}\mu=\int_{S_{\psi_{2}}}n^{S_{\psi_{2}}}\cdot\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|}F\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}+\int_{S_{\psi_{1}}}n^{S_{\psi_{1}}}\cdot\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|}F\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)},

where
n^{S_{\psi}}
denotes the outward-pointing unit normal
to
S_{\psi}
. Then

n^{S_{\psi_{2}}}=\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|}\big|_{S_{\psi_{2}}}

and

n^{S_{\psi_{1}}}=-\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|}\big|_{S_{\psi_{1}}}
, so
(
A.4
) gives (
A.3
).
Dividing (
A.3
) by
\psi_{2}-\psi_{1}
and taking the limit
gives (
A.2
).
∎

In particular, if
F=F(\psi)
is constant on
S_{\psi}
,
writing

\Delta F=\div(\nabla F)=\div(\nabla\psi F^{\prime})
we have

(A.5)

\int_{S_{\psi}}\Delta F{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\psi}\left(\int_{S_{\psi}}|\nabla\psi|F^{\prime}{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}\right)=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\psi}\left(\left[\int_{S_{\psi}}|\nabla\psi|{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}\right]F^{\prime}\right).

Another consequence of the formula (
A.1
) is the following

Lemma A.2
.

Let
X
be a vector field defined
in
D
with the property that
X|_{S_{\psi}}
is tangent to

S_{\psi}
. Then the divergence
\div X
in
D
is related to the divergence
operator
\div_{S_{\psi}}
on
S_{\psi}
by

(A.6)

\frac{\div X}{|\nabla\psi|}\Big|_{S_{\psi}}=\div_{S_{\psi}}\left(\left[\frac{X}{|\nabla\psi|}\right]\Big|_{S_{\psi}}\right).

In particular, if
X
is divergence-free in
D
, then
\varrho:=\frac{1}{|\nabla\psi|}\big|_{S_{\psi}}
is a density conserved by
X
on
S_{\psi}
.

Proof.

This can be seen by working in local coordinates but it is simpler to use (
A.1
)
and note that if
u\in C^{\infty}(D)
is any test function then

\displaystyle-\int_{D}\div Xu\,{\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{D}(X\cdot\nabla)u\,{\rm d}\mu=\int_{\psi_{-}}^{\psi_{+}}\int_{S_{\psi}}(X\cdot\nabla)u\,\frac{{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}}{|\nabla\psi|}

\displaystyle=\int_{\psi_{-}}^{\psi_{+}}\int_{S_{\psi}}(X\cdot\nabla^{T})u\frac{{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}}{|\nabla\psi|}=-\int_{\psi_{-}}^{\psi_{+}}\int_{S_{\psi}}\div_{S_{\psi}}\left(\frac{X}{|\nabla\psi|}\right)u\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)},

where we used that
X\cdot\nabla=X\cdot\nabla^{T}
on
S_{\psi}
,
where
\nabla^{T}
denotes the tangential gradient on
S_{\psi}
,

\nabla^{T}U=(\nabla-\frac{\nabla\psi}{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla)u

whenever
u
is an extension of
U
from
S_{\psi}
to a neighborhood of
S_{\psi}
.
By (
A.1
), the left-hand side is

-\int_{\psi_{-}}^{\psi_{+}}\int_{S_{\psi}}\frac{\div X}{|\nabla\psi|}u\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{(d-1)}
. Then (
A.6
) follows since
u
is arbitrary.
∎

Quantum Noise as a Symmetry-Breaking Field
Beatriz C. Dias, Domagoj Perković, Masudul Haque, Pedro Ribeiro, Paul A. McClarty

Abstract
We investigate the effect of quantum noise on the measurement-induced quantum phase transition in monitored random quantum circuits. Using the efficient simulability of random Clifford circuits, we find that the transition is broadened into a crossover and that the phase diagram as a function of projective measurements and noise exhibits several distinct regimes. We show that a mapping to a classical statistical mechanics problem accounts for the main features of the random circuit phase diagram. The bulk noise maps to an explicit permutation symmetry breaking coupling; this symmetry is spontaneously broken when the noise is switched off. These results have implications for the realization of entanglement transitions in noisy quantum circuits.

Circuit and Observables –
We consider a circuit model

We note that

-
The smearing of the phase transition into a crossover at finite
q

q
explicitly breaks the symmetry that is spontaneously broken by tuning
p

Comparison of classical picture and quantum model

-
We can now interpret the random circuit results from the point of view of the classical model. The primary effect of introducing quantum noise is an explicit breaking of the symmetry of the model down to
S_{Q}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
to which, we recall, it is spontaneously broken when
q=0
. This can be seen more transparently by inspecting the large
d
, small
q
limit. In this limit, the
q=0
model reduces to a
Q!
state Potts model. Expanding Eq.
4

q
and collecting the largest powers of

we obtain
SM

W_{KM}(g_{i},g_{j})=d^{Q}\Big((1-p)^{Q}\big[(1-q)+q|g_{j}|_{1}\big]\delta_{g_{i},g_{j}}+p^{Q}\Big)

(5)

where

denotes the number of 1-cycles in

is to pick out the permutation with the largest number of cycles – the trivial permutation from all
Q!
states equivalent to applying directly a symmetry-breaking field that biases the system towards the

(Fig.

Thus, for finite
p
, quantum noise drives the system to the infinite temperature fixed point of the classical model. This accounts for the most prominent feature of the phase diagram: the smearing of the transition into a crossover for
q\neq 0
. For
p=0
, the system instead flows to the infinite-field fixed point corresponding to the maximally mixed state.

Turning to the observables, we note that
S_{AB}
vanishes for
q=0
but exhibits a volume law for
q\neq 0
. From the point of view of the classical model, when
q=0
,
S_{AB}
is the free energy difference of a system with boundary degrees of freedom pinned to a nontrivial permutation across the entire system and a system with a boundary pinned to the trivial permutation (Fig.
3
(d)). But, for
q=0
, the free energy is indifferent to the precise pinning field so the free energy difference must vanish. This ceases to be the case when
q\neq 0
but
q
distinguishes the trivial permutation leading to an
O(L)
free energy difference as observed in the quantum model. A similar argument allows us to understand why
I_{AB}
has an area law for
q\neq 0
: the domain wall cost is a constant
\alpha

L
in
S_{AB}
which is cancelled off by the two
\alpha L/2
contributions in
S_{A}
and
S_{B}
.

Finally the classical model provides some insight into the

q=1

. Since

is close to

, the appropriate expansion parameter is

. Then for

, the only further scale that enters the problem is

\log q

Discussion and Conclusion

-
We have introduced and studied a random circuit model subjected to both projective and un-monitored measurements. We have shown that, at finite
q
, the transition known for projective-only measurements (
q=0
) is washed out and the phase diagram is reminiscent of that of a finite temperature ferromagnet with
q
playing the role of an applied field. We have made this connection precise through a mapping to a classical statistical mechanics model with finite replica index
Q
(the quantum model corresponding to replica index
Q\rightarrow 1
). In the classical model,
q
explicitly breaks down the global
S_{Q}\times S_{Q}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
permutation symmetry of the model to
S_{Q}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Thus, quantum noise (originating, e.g., from coupling to a bath) is a relevant operator driving the system to the infinite temperature fixed point. We have seen that various features of the quantum model follow from the classical statistical mechanics analogue. It is amusing to note that the passage from pure to mixed states by switching on
q
is reflected in the classical model as a field that affects the response of the system but not the state space. Indeed, in the classical model the difference between the pure and mixed states is only apparent through boundary conditions.

All quantum systems are subject to quantum noise coming from the environment. Our result suggests that, in a realistic setting, the entanglement transition driven by monitored measurements will be smeared out by the environment. In addition, our work shows that nonequilibrium mixed state dynamics in Hilbert space in the presence of a quantum bath can be viewed as breaking a delicate symmetry that is only present for pure states. It would be of considerable interest to devise a model with an entanglement transition in the presence of bulk noise
Choi et al. 2020
. Whether this is possible or not has a bearing on the experimental simulability of these entanglement transitions as well as implications for error corrected quantum computers
Aharonov 2000
.

S.II
Classical Mapping for the Mixed State Circuit

As discussed in the main text, Ref.
Jian et al. 2020
(building on work from Ref.
Zhou and Nahum 2019
) presents a mapping of the suitably replicated quantum random circuit model with projective measurements to a classical lattice model at finite temperature. Within the classical statistical mechanics model the volume to area entanglement transition is an order-disorder transition involving a broken permutation symmetry in the volume law phase. The entanglement scaling follows from the free energy cost of a domain wall.

Here we review this work and generalize it to the case with both projective measurements and un-monitored measurements.

S.II.A
Replicas

We wish to compute the n
th
Rényi entropy

\displaystyle S_{A}^{\left(n\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\ln\left[\tr_{A}\left(\rho_{A}^{n}\right)\right]

(S.1)

for the circuit model with Haar-random unitaries acting on
d
dimensional local degrees of freedom (qudits), projective measurements,
M
, and un-monitored measurements,
K
. This is to be done by averaging over circuit disorder and measurement outcomes as follows:

\displaystyle S_{A}^{\left(n\right)}

\displaystyle=\int dU\sum_{m}\ \frac{1}{1-n}\ln\left[\frac{\tr_{A}\left(\rho_{A,m}^{n}\right)}{\tr\left(\rho_{m}\right)^{n}}\right]\tr\left(\rho_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\int dU\sum_{m}\ \frac{1}{1-n}\left\{\ln\left[\tr_{A}\left(\rho_{A,m}^{n}\right)\right]-\ln\left[\tr\left(\rho_{m}\right)^{n}\right]\right\}\tr\left(\rho_{m}\right).

(S.2)

Here the density matrix for some choice of measurements (indicated in the subscript) is

\displaystyle\rho_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}K_{\mu_{T}}M_{m_{T}}U_{T}...K_{\mu_{1}}M_{m_{1}}U_{1}\rho_{0}U_{1}^{\dagger}M_{m_{1}}^{\dagger}K_{\mu_{1}}^{\dagger}\ldots U_{T}^{\dagger}M_{m_{T}}^{\dagger}K_{\mu_{T}}^{\dagger},

(S.3)

where
T
is the circuit depth,
L
the circuit length and

\displaystyle M_{0}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-p},

(S.4)

\displaystyle M_{i=1,\ldots,d}

\displaystyle=p\left|i\right\rangle\left\langle i\right|,

(S.5)

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-q},

(S.6)

\displaystyle K_{i=1,\ldots,d}

\displaystyle=q\left|i\right\rangle\left\langle i\right|.

(S.7)

The integral is performed with the standard Haar measure.
Using
\ln x=\lim_{k\to 0}\frac{x^{k}-1}{k}
, we get a replica index
k

\displaystyle S_{A}^{\left(n\right)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to 0}S_{A}^{\left(n,k\right)}=\lim_{k\to 0}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{1}{k}\int dU\sum_{m}\left\{\left[\tr_{A}\left(\rho_{A,m}^{n}\right)\right]^{k}-\left[\tr\left(\rho_{m}\right)\right]^{nk}\right\}\tr\left(\rho_{m}\right).

(S.9)

Defining the swap operator

\displaystyle\Sigma_{A}^{\left(n\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[i\right]}\left|i_{g^{n}_{\text{cyc}}\left(1\right)}^{A}i_{1}^{B},\ldots,i_{g^{n}_{\text{cyc}}\left(n\right)}^{A}i_{n}^{B}\right\rangle\left\langle i_{1}^{A}i_{1}^{B},\ldots,i_{n}^{A}i_{n}^{B}\right|,

(S.10)

where
g^{n}_{\text{cyc}}
is a cyclic permutation of
n
replicas, and
i_{r}^{A(B)}

runs over a complete basis of replica
r
of subsystem
A
(
B
),
we can write

\displaystyle S_{A}^{\left(n,k\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{1}{k}\tr\left\{\left[\Sigma_{A}^{\left(n\right)}{}^{\otimes k}\otimes 1-1^{\otimes\left(nk+1\right)}\right]\left[\int dU\sum_{m}\rho_{m}^{\otimes\left(nk+1\right)}\right]\right\}.

(S.11)

Or, in vectorial notation and defining
Q\equiv nk+1
,

\displaystyle S_{A}^{\left(n,k\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{1}{k}\left[\left\langle\left\langle\Sigma_{A}^{\left(n,k\right)}\right|\right|-\left\langle\left\langle 1\right|\right|\right]\left|\left|\bar{\rho}_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle,

(S.12)

with

\displaystyle\left|\left|\bar{\rho}_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int dU\sum_{m}\left|\left|\rho_{m}\right\rangle\right\rangle^{\otimes Q},

(S.13)

\displaystyle\left|\left|\Sigma_{A}^{\left(n\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\left[i\right]}\left|i_{g^{n}_{\text{cyc}}\left(1\right)}^{A}i_{1}^{B},\ldots,i_{g^{n}_{\text{cyc}}\left(n\right)}^{A}i_{n}^{B}\right\rangle\otimes\left|i_{1}^{A}i_{1}^{B},\ldots,i_{n}^{A}i_{n}^{B}\right\rangle\right)^{\otimes k}\otimes\left(\sum_{\left[i_{Q}\right]}\left|i_{Q}^{A}i_{Q}^{B}\right\rangle\otimes\left|i_{Q}^{A}i_{Q}^{B}\right\rangle\right),

(S.14)

\displaystyle\left|\left|1_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i}\left|i^{A}i^{B}\right\rangle\otimes\left|i^{A}i^{B}\right\rangle\right)^{\otimes Q}.

(S.15)

We use Eq.
S.12
to introduce a classical partition function as indicated in the main text. In particular, we define

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{A}^{\left(n,k\right)}=\frac{n}{1-n}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{A}-\mathcal{Z}_{\emptyset}}{Q-1}

(S.16)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{A}=\left\langle\left\langle\Sigma_{A}^{\left(n\right)}\middle\|\bar{\rho}_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle

(S.17)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\emptyset}=\left\langle\left\langle 1\middle\|\bar{\rho}_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle.

(S.18)

S.II.B
Averaging Unitaries

The averaged replicated density matrix is given by

\displaystyle\left|\left|\bar{\rho}_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\int dU\sum_{m}\left|\left|\rho_{m}\right\rangle\right\rangle^{\otimes Q},

(S.19)

where

\displaystyle\left|\left|\rho_{m}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\mu_{T}}K_{\mu_{T}}\otimes K_{\mu_{T}}^{*}\right)\left(M_{m_{T}}\otimes M_{m_{T}}^{*}\right)\left(U_{T}\otimes U_{T}^{*}\right)...\left(\sum_{\mu_{1}}K_{\mu_{1}}\otimes K_{\mu_{1}}^{*}\right)\left(M_{m_{1}}\otimes M_{m_{1}}^{*}\right)\left(U_{1}\otimes U_{1}^{*}\right)\left|\left|\rho_{0}\right\rangle\right\rangle.

(S.20)

Let us analyze the operators averaged over replica space:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle=\int dU\left(U\otimes U^{*}\right)^{\otimes Q},

(S.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\left(M_{m}\otimes M_{m}^{*}\right)^{\otimes Q},

(S.22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\mu}K_{\mu}\otimes K_{\mu}^{*}\right)^{\otimes Q}.

(S.23)

For
U
acting on two qubits we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{12}

\displaystyle=\int dU\left(U_{12}\otimes U_{12}^{*}\right)^{\otimes Q}=\sum_{g_{1}g_{2}\in S_{Q}}W\left(g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}\right)\left|\left|g_{1}\right\rangle\right\rangle\left|\left|g_{1}\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle g_{2}\right|\right|\left\langle\left\langle g_{2}\right|\right|

(S.24)

where
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are permutations of
Q
elements, and
\left|\left|g\right\rangle\right\rangle
is a state on the replica-copy
space of one of the qubits, given by

\displaystyle\left|\left|g\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[i\right]}\left|\left|i_{g\left(1\right)},\ldots,i_{g\left(Q\right)};i_{1},\ldots,i_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle.

(S.25)

A schematic representation of this relation is given in Fig.
S2
The coefficient
W(g)
is a Weingarten function on permutation
g
. In short, the average over unitaries leads to the emergence of classical permutation variables - each pairwise unitary operator gives two permutation degrees of freedom. Thus, on the lattice, the brick wall pattern of unitaries maps to a lattice model with permutation degrees of freedom on two sites for each unitary.

. The integration over the Haar distributed unities,
U_{1,2}
, acting individual on

, acting on copy-space on

are defined in Eq.

.

We turn now to the averaged monitored measurements. For a single qubit the average is
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\left(M_{m}\otimes M_{m}^{*}\right)^{\otimes Q}=\left(1-p\right)^{Q}+p^{Q}\sum_{i}\left|\left|i,\ldots,i;i,\ldots,i\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i,\ldots,i;i,\ldots,i\right|\right|.

(S.26)

Finally, for the un-monitored measurements on a single qubit we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\mu}K_{\mu}\otimes K_{\mu}^{*}\right)^{\otimes Q}=\left[\left(1-q\right)+q\sum_{i}\left|\left|i;i\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i;i\right|\right|\right]^{\otimes Q}=\left[\sum_{ij}\left[\left(1-q\right)+q\delta_{i,j}\right]\left|\left|i;j\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i;j\right|\right|\right]^{\otimes Q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[i\right]\left[j\right]}\prod_{k=1}^{Q}\left[\left(1-q\right)+q\delta_{i_{k},j_{k}}\right]\left|\left|i_{1},\ldots,i_{Q};j_{1},\ldots,j_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i_{1},\ldots,i_{Q};j_{1},\ldots,j_{Q}\right|\right|

(S.27)

S.II.C
Matrix Elements and Symmetries

The matrix element between two
g
states is given by

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle g\middle\|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[i\right]}\left\langle\left\langle i_{g\left(1\right)},\ldots,i_{g\left(Q\right)}\middle\|i_{g^{\prime}\left(1\right)},\ldots,i_{g^{\prime}\left(Q\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle

(S.28)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[i\right]}\left\langle\left\langle i_{g^{\prime-1}g\left(1\right)},\ldots,i_{g^{\prime-1}g\left(Q\right)}\middle\|i_{1},\ldots,i_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle=d^{\left|gg^{\prime-1}\right|}

(S.29)

and

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle g\middle\|i,\ldots,i;i,\ldots,i\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=1.

(S.30)

One can further show that

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle h_{L}gh_{R}^{-1}\middle\|h_{L}g^{\prime}h_{R}^{-1}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left\langle g\middle\|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle

(S.31)

so there is a double (left and right) permutation invariance for this matrix element that is of great importance for this discussion.

It is useful to define operators
\mathcal{P}
that implements the permutations
h_{L}
and
h_{R}
and another operator
\mathcal{S}
that swaps bras and kets (or left and right indices in the vectorized notation)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{h_{L}h_{R}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[i\right]\left[j\right]}\left|\left|i_{h_{R}^{-1}\left(1\right)},\ldots,i_{h_{R}^{-1}\left(Q\right)};j_{h_{L}^{-1}\left(1\right)},\ldots,j_{h_{L}^{-1}\left(Q\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i_{1},\ldots,i_{Q};j_{1},\ldots,j_{Q}\right|\right|

(S.32)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[i\right]\left[j\right]}\left|\left|j_{1},\ldots,j_{Q};i_{1},\ldots,i_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i_{1},\ldots,i_{Q};j_{1},\ldots,j_{Q}\right|\right|

(S.33)

One may check that
\mathcal{P}
acting on state
\left|\left|g\right\rangle\right\rangle
leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{h_{L}h_{R}}\left|\left|g\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[i\right]\left[i^{\prime}\right]}\left|\left|i_{h_{R}^{-1}\left(1\right)},\ldots,i_{h_{R}^{-1}\left(Q\right)};i^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{-1}\left(1\right)},\ldots,i^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{-1}\left(Q\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i_{1},\ldots,i_{Q}\middle\|i^{\prime}_{g\left(1\right)},\ldots,i^{\prime}_{g\left(Q\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[i^{\prime}\right]}\left|\left|i^{\prime}_{gh_{R}^{-1}\left(1\right)},\ldots,i^{\prime}_{gh_{R}^{-1}\left(Q\right)};i^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{-1}\left(1\right)},\ldots,i^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{-1}\left(Q\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[i\right]}\left|\left|i_{h_{L}gh_{R}^{-1}\left(1\right)},\ldots,i_{h_{L}gh_{R}^{-1}\left(Q\right)};i{}_{1},\ldots,i{}_{1}\right\rangle\right\rangle=\left|\left|h_{L}gh_{R}^{-1}\right\rangle\right\rangle,

(S.34)

where in the last equality we perform the change of variables
i^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{-1}\left(k\right)}=i_{k},i^{\prime}_{k}=i_{h_{L}\left(k\right)}
.

Similarly, one may look at the action of
\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\left|\left|g\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[i\right]}\left|\left|i_{1},\ldots,i_{Q},i_{g\left(1\right)},\ldots,i_{g\left(Q\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle=\sum_{\left[i\right]}\left|\left|i_{g^{-1}\left(1\right)},\ldots,i_{g^{-1}\left(Q\right)},i_{1},\ldots,i_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle=\left|\left|g^{-1}\right\rangle\right\rangle.

(S.35)

The matrix element of the projective measurement operator is

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\mathcal{M}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(1-p\right)^{Q}+p^{Q}d.

(S.36)

This operator is also left-/right- permutation symmetric and also symmetric under the swap
\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{h_{L}h_{R}}^{-1}\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}_{h_{L}h_{R}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M},

(S.37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{-1}\mathcal{M}\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}.

(S.38)

Taken together with the average over unitaries it follows that the model in the permutation variables has global symmetries
S_{Q}\times S_{Q}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

However, when we consider the un-monitored measurements we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{h_{L}h_{R}}^{-1}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{h_{L}h_{R}}

\displaystyle\neq\mathcal{K}\text{ in general for }\ h_{L}\neq h_{R},

(S.39)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{hh}^{-1}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{hh}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K},

(S.40)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{-1}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}

(S.41)

so there is only one surviving permutation invariance
-
the one where left- and right- are locked to one another.

S.II.D
The Un-Monitored Measurements

It remains to evaluate the matrix elements for the combined monitored and un-monitored measurements.

The action of
\mathcal{M}
on a permutation degree of freedom is
g^{\prime}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left[\left(1-p\right)^{Q}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle+p^{Q}\sum_{i}\left|\left|i,\ldots,i;i,\ldots,i\right\rangle\right\rangle\right].

(S.42)

We note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\left|\left|i,\ldots,i;i,\ldots,i\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|\left|i,\ldots,i;i,\ldots,i\right\rangle\right\rangle.

(S.43)

Now acting with
\mathcal{K}
leads to

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\mathcal{M}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left[\left(1-p\right)^{Q}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle+p^{Q}\sum_{i}\left|\left|i,\ldots,i;i,\ldots,i\right\rangle\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\left(1-p\right)^{Q}\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle+p^{Q}\sum_{i}\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left|\left|i,\ldots,i;i,\ldots,i\right\rangle\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\left(1-p\right)^{Q}\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle+p^{Q}d.

(S.44)

Our goal now is to compute the matrix elements
\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle
appearing in (
S.44
). The operator
\mathcal{K}
is given by (
S.27
).

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\left(\sum_{\left[i\right]\left[j\right]}\prod_{k=1}^{Q}\left[\left(1-q\right)+q\delta_{i_{k},j_{k}}\right]\left|\left|i_{1}...i_{Q};j_{1}...j_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i_{1}...i_{Q};j_{1}...j_{Q}\right|\right|\right)\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle

(S.45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\left[i\right]\left[j\right]}\prod_{k=1}^{Q}\left[\left(1-q\right)+q\delta_{i_{k},j_{k}}\right]\left\langle\left\langle j_{g\left(1\right)},...,j_{g\left(Q\right)}\middle\|i_{1}...i_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i_{1}...i_{Q}\middle\|j_{g^{\prime}\left(1\right)},...,j_{g^{\prime}\left(Q\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle

(S.46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((1-q)^{Q}\sum_{\left[i\right]\left[j\right]}+\sum_{l=1}^{Q}q^{l}(1-q)^{Q-l}\sum_{\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{l}\}\in B_{l,Q}}\sum_{\left[i\right]\left[j\right]}\delta_{i_{r_{1}},j_{r_{1}}}\ldots\delta_{i_{r_{l}},j_{r_{l}}}\right)

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle j_{g\left(1\right)},...,j_{g\left(Q\right)}\middle\|i_{1}...i_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i_{1}...i_{Q}\middle\|j_{g^{\prime}\left(1\right)},...,j_{g^{\prime}\left(Q\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle

(S.47)

where
B_{l,Q}
is the set of subsets of
\{1,2,\ldots,Q-1,Q\}
with

l
elements.

The first term corresponds to performing no un-monitored measurements.
As seen in (
S.29
), this yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\left[i\right]\left[j\right]}\left\langle\left\langle j_{g\left(1\right)},...,j_{g\left(Q\right)}\middle\|i_{1}...i_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i_{1}...i_{Q}\middle\|j_{g^{\prime}\left(1\right)},...,j_{g^{\prime}\left(Q\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left[j\right]}\left\langle\left\langle j_{g\left(1\right)},...,j_{g\left(Q\right)}\middle\|j_{g^{\prime}\left(1\right)},...,j_{g^{\prime}\left(Q\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle=\left\langle\left\langle g\middle\|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle=d^{\left|gg^{\prime-1}\right|}.

(S.48)

The second term corresponds to measuring
l=1,\ldots,Q
out of the

Q
replicas. For a fixed
l
, we sum over the
\binom{Q}{l}
ways
of choosing which
l
copies to measure. The coefficient of the
q^{l}(1-q)^{Q-l}

term is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\left[i\right]\left[j\right]}\sum_{\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{l}\}\in B_{l,Q}}\delta_{i_{r_{1}},j_{r_{1}}}\ldots\delta_{i_{r_{l}},j_{r_{l}}}\left\langle\left\langle j_{g\left(1\right)},...,j_{g\left(Q\right)}\middle\|i_{1}...i_{Q}\right\rangle\right\rangle\left\langle\left\langle i_{1}...i_{Q}\middle\|j_{g^{\prime}\left(1\right)},...,j_{g^{\prime}\left(Q\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle=d^{|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{1}}^{t_{1}}\ldots h_{r_{l}}^{t_{l}}|}

(S.49)

where
B_{l,Q}
is the set of subsets of
{1,2,\ldots,Q}
with
l
elements,

\displaystyle h_{r}=(g^{-1}(r)\ r)

(S.50)

is a two-cycle that permutes replica index
r
and
g^{-1}(r)
and

\displaystyle t_{k}=\frac{1+\sign(|gg^{\prime-1}|-|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}|)}{2}

(S.51)

is either 0 or 1. If in the permutation

gg^{\prime-1}
, the elements
g^{-1}(r)
and
r
belong to different
cycles, then
gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}^{t_{k}}
joins the cycles to which

g^{-1}(r)
and
r
belong and
\left|gg^{\prime-1}\right|-|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}|=1
.
If the elements
g^{-1}(r)
and
r
already belong to the same cycle,
then
gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}
separates it such that
g^{-1}(k)

and
k
belong to different cycles and
\left|gg^{\prime-1}\right|-|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}|=-1
. In this case,
t_{k}=0
and
gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}^{t_{k}}=gg^{\prime-1}
.
Thus, expression
|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}^{t_{k}}|
selects the permutation

gg^{\prime-1}
or
gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}
with fewer cycles, i.e.

\displaystyle|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}^{t_{k}}|=\begin{cases}\left|gg^{\prime-1}\right|&{\rm if}\ \left|gg^{\prime-1}\right|=|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}|-1\\
|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}|&{\rm if}\ \left|gg^{\prime-1}\right|=|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}|+1\end{cases},

(S.52)

where
\left|gg^{\prime-1}\right|=|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{k}}|\pm 1
always. We consider
the null power of a cycle to be the identity cycle.

Finally,

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle=(1-q)^{Q}d^{\left|gg^{\prime-1}\right|}+\sum_{l=1}^{Q}q^{l}(1-q)^{Q-l}\sum_{\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{l}\}\in B_{l,Q}}d^{|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r_{1}}^{t_{1}}\ldots h_{r_{l}}^{t_{l}}|}.

(S.53)

Notice that, setting
q=0
, we recover the expression for monitored measurements alone, given by Eq. 13 in
Jian et al. 2020
.

S.II.E
Bulk and Boundary contributions

The entanglement entropy is obtained as the analytic continuation of the difference of factors
\mathcal{Z}_{A}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\emptyset}
in Eq. 7 in the main text. For a set of sites in region
A
,
\mathcal{Z}_{A}
can be interpreted as a classical partition function with boundary conditions imposed on the set of sites with
t=0
,
\partial_{0}
, and on the set of sites with
t=T
,
\partial_{T}
. After applying the rules derived in the last sections, the bulk degrees of freedom are found to be distributed on the anisotropic honeycomb lattice depicted in Fig.
S3
. Vertical bonds (red),
\mathcal{E}_{\rm V}
, correspond to Weingarten weights and the zigzag bonds (blue),
\mathcal{E}_{\rm ZZ}
, to the matrix elements in Eq.
S.44
.

Assuming the starting state is the high-temperature state, i.e. proportional to the identity, the contribution from the
t=0
boundary is simply given by
\prod_{i\in\partial_{0}}\left\langle\left\langle g_{i}\middle\|1\right\rangle\right\rangle/d^{Q}
. As the protocol assumes a steady-state is reached much before any of the observables are considered, this boundary condition does not influence any of the following results and may be safely ignored.

On the contrary, the contributions from the
t=T
boundary are nontrivial and differ for sites belonging to the set
A
or to its complement,
B
. This boundary contribution, given by Eq.
S.17
can be most conveniently obtained by rewriting Eq.
S.14
as
\left|\left|\Sigma_{{A}}^{\left(n,k\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle=\otimes_{i\in B}\left|\left|1\right\rangle\right\rangle\otimes_{i\in A}\left|\left|g(n,k,1)\right\rangle\right\rangle
, with
\left|\left|g(n,k,1)\right\rangle\right\rangle
a double-ket state of the from (
S.25
). Thus, sites in
\bar{A}
, contribute with
\prod_{i\in\partial_{T}\cap B}\left\langle\left\langle 1\middle\|g_{i}\right\rangle\right\rangle
. A depiction,
g(n,k,1)
, acting on
Q=kn+1

S.II.F
Classical Statistical Model

So far we have evaluated matrix elements for the measurements on a single qubit and the average over unitaries on a pair of sites. We now use these results to write down the partition function in Eq.
S.14
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{A}=\sum_{\left\{g_{i}\in S_{Q}\right\}}\left[\prod_{\langle i,j\rangle\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm V}}W(g_{i}g_{j}^{-1})\times\prod_{\langle i,j\rangle\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm ZZ}}W_{KM}(g_{i},g_{j})\times\prod_{i\in\partial_{0}}d^{\left|g_{i}\right|}\times\prod_{i\in\partial_{T}\cap B}d^{\left|g_{i}\right|}\times\prod_{i\in\partial_{T}\cap A}d^{\left|g(n,k,1)^{-1}g_{i}\right|}\right]

(S.54)

where we reproduce here for convenience the weights computed above

\displaystyle W_{KM}(g_{i},g_{j})=\left(1-p\right)^{Q}\left\langle\left\langle g_{i}\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left|\left|g_{j}\right\rangle\right\rangle+p^{Q}d,

(S.55)

and

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle g_{i}\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left|\left|g_{j}\right\rangle\right\rangle=(1-q)^{Q}d^{\left|g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}\right|}+\sum_{l=1}^{Q}q^{l}(1-q)^{Q-l}\sum_{\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{l}\}\in B_{l,Q}}d^{|g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}h_{r_{1}}^{t_{1}}\ldots h_{r_{l}}^{t_{l}}|}.

(S.56)

This reduces to the expression in Ref.
Jian et al. 2020
for
q=0
whereupon
\left\langle\left\langle g_{i}\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left|\left|g_{j}\right\rangle\right\rangle=d^{\left|g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}\right|}
. In that case the weights may be interpreted as follows: when
p
is large the weight is nearly invariant under changes to the states corresponding to the paramagnetic regime and, for small
p
, the system tries to maximize the number of cycles in
g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}
corresponding to an identity permutation where the permutation degrees of freedom are ferromagnetic spontaneously breaking one permutation symmetry.

In the presence of
q\neq 0
, the symmetry that is spontaneously broken for small
p
is explicitly broken by
q
analogous to a field applied to a classical ferromagnet. This is consistent with the crossover observed numerically.

S.II.G
Limiting cases

We consider limiting cases that serve to illustrate some features of the model. We first linearize in
q
:

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle=(1-q)d^{\left|gg^{\prime-1}\right|}+q\sum_{r=1}^{Q}d^{|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r}^{t}|}+\mathcal{O}(q^{2}).

(S.57)

We then take the
d\rightarrow\infty
limit. For that, let us take (
S.57
) up to
\mathcal{O}(d^{Q-2})
.
We have

\displaystyle d^{\left|gg^{\prime-1}\right|}=\sum_{k=1}^{Q}d^{k}\delta_{|gg^{\prime-1}|,k}=d^{Q}\delta_{g^{-1},g^{\prime-1}}+d^{Q-1}\delta_{|gg^{\prime-1}|,Q-1}+\mathcal{O}(d^{Q-2})

(S.58)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{Q}d^{|gg^{\prime-1}h_{r}^{t}|}

\displaystyle=|g|_{1}\delta_{g^{-1},g^{\prime-1}}d^{Q}+\left(\max(|g|_{1},|g^{\prime-1}|_{1})\,\delta_{|gg^{\prime-1}|,Q-1}+Q\delta_{|gg^{\prime-1}|,Q}\,\delta_{|g|_{1},0}\right)d^{Q-1}+\mathcal{O}(d^{Q-2})

\displaystyle=|g|_{1}\delta_{g^{-1},g^{\prime-1}}d^{Q}+\left(\max(|g|_{1},|g^{\prime-1}|_{1})\,\delta_{|gg^{\prime-1}|,Q-1}+Q\delta_{g^{-1},g^{\prime-1}}\,\delta_{|g|_{1},0}\right)d^{Q-1}+\mathcal{O}(d^{Q-2})

(S.59)

where we used
\delta_{|gg^{\prime-1}|,Q}=\delta_{g^{-1},g^{\prime-1}}
and where
|g|_{1}
denotes the number of 1-cycles in the permutation
g
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\langle g\right|\right|\mathcal{K}\left|\left|g^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left((1-q)+q|g|_{1}\right)\delta_{g^{-1},g^{\prime-1}}d^{Q}

\displaystyle+\left([1+q\max(|g|_{1},|g^{\prime-1}|_{1})]\delta_{|gg^{\prime-1}|,Q-1}+qQ\delta_{g^{-1},g^{\prime-1}}\,\delta_{|g|_{1},0}\right)d^{Q-1}+\mathcal{O}(d^{Q-2})

(S.60)

The leading order in
d
result in (
S.60
) reveals that the full
S_{Q}\times S_{Q}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry of the
q=0
model is broken down to
S_{Q}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

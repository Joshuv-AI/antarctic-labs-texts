Abstract
. We study the large-time behavior of the 1D barotropic Navier-Stokes flow perturbed from Riemann data generating a composition of two shock waves with small amplitudes. We prove that the perturbed Navier-Stokes flow converges, uniformly in space, towards a composition of two viscous shock waves as time goes to infinity, up to dynamical shifts. Especially, the strengths of the two waves can be chosen independently. This is the first result for the convergence to a composite wave of two viscous shocks with independently small amplitudes.



1.
Introduction

We consider the one-dimensional barotropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations in the Lagrangian mass coordinates:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
&u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=\left(\mu\frac{u_{x}}{v}\right)_{x},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad t>0\end{aligned}

where
v=v(t,x)>0
and
u=u(t,x)
denote the specific volume and the velocity of the fluid, respectively. The pressure
p=p(v)
is given by the
\gamma
-law
p(v)=bv^{-\gamma}
, with
b>0
and
\gamma>1
and
\mu>0
denotes the viscosity coefficient of the fluid. For simplicity, we normalize the coefficients as
b=1
and
\mu=1
.

Consider initial data of the system (
1.1
) given by
(v_{0},u_{0})
, which connects prescribed far-field constant states:

(1.2)

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}(v_{0}(x),u_{0}(x))=(v_{\pm},u_{\pm}).

A heuristic argument (see e.g.
[
16
]
) describes that large-time behavior of solutions
(v,u)
to the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) has a close relationship with the Riemann problem of the associated Euler equations:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
&u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad t>0,\end{aligned}

subject to the Riemann initial data

(1.4)

(v(t,x),u(t,x))|_{t=0}=\begin{cases}(v_{-},u_{-}),\quad x<0,\\
(v_{+},u_{+}),\quad x>0.\end{cases}

We consider the end states
(v_{\pm},u_{\pm})
such that there exists a unique intermediate state
(v_{m},u_{m})
which is connected with
(v_{-},u_{-})
by 1-shock curve and with
(v_{+},u_{+})
by 2-shock curve. That is, there exists a unique
(v_{m},u_{m})
such that the following Rankine-Hugoniot condition and Lax entropy condition hold:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\begin{cases}-\sigma_{1}(v_{m}-v_{-})-(u_{m}-u_{-})=0,\\
-\sigma_{1}(u_{m}-u_{-})+(p(v_{m})-p(v_{-}))=0,\end{cases}\quad\sigma_{1}:=-\sqrt{-\frac{p(v_{m})-p(v_{-})}{v_{m}-v_{-}}},\quad v_{-}>v_{m},\quad u_{-}>u_{m};\\
&\begin{cases}-\sigma_{2}(v_{+}-v_{m})-(u_{+}-u_{m})=0,\\
-\sigma_{2}(u_{+}-u_{m})+(p(v_{+})-p(v_{m}))=0,\end{cases}\quad\sigma_{2}:=\sqrt{-\frac{p(v_{+})-p(v_{m})}{v_{+}-v_{m}}},\quad v_{m}<v_{+},\quad u_{m}>u_{+}.\end{aligned}

Then, the Euler equations (
1.3
) with (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) admit a unique self-similar solution, the so-called Riemann solution
(\bar{v},\bar{u})
, represented by the composition
(\bar{v},\bar{u})=(v^{s}_{1},u^{s}_{1})+(v^{s}_{2},u^{s}_{2})-(v_{m},u_{m})
of 1-shock wave
(v^{s}_{1},u^{s}_{1})
and 2-shock wave
(v^{s}_{2},u^{s}_{2})
defined as (see e.g.
[
23
]
)

(v^{s}_{1},u^{s}_{1})(t,x)=\begin{cases}(v_{-},u_{-}),\quad x<\sigma_{1}t,\\
(v_{m},u_{m}),\quad x>\sigma_{1}t,\end{cases},\quad(v^{s}_{2},u^{s}_{2})(t,x)=\begin{cases}(v_{m},u_{m}),\quad x<\sigma_{2}t,\\
(v_{+},u_{+}),\quad x>\sigma_{2}t.\end{cases}

The viscous counterpart of the Riemann solution
(\bar{v},\bar{u})
is given by the composite wave:

(1.6)

(\widetilde{v}(t,x),\widetilde{u}(t,x)):=\Big(\widetilde{v}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t),\widetilde{u}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t)\Big)+\Big(\widetilde{v}_{2}(x-\sigma_{2}t),\widetilde{u}_{2}(x-\sigma_{2}t)\Big)-(v_{m},u_{m}),

which is composed of 1-viscous shock
(\widetilde{v}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{1})(x-\sigma_{1}t)
and 2-viscous shock
(\widetilde{v}_{2},\widetilde{u}_{2})(x-\sigma_{2}t)
satisfying: for each
i=1,2,

(1.7)

\begin{cases}-\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{\prime}-(\widetilde{u}_{i})^{\prime}=0,\\
-\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{u}_{i})^{\prime}+p(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{\prime}=\left(\frac{(\widetilde{u}_{i})^{\prime}}{\widetilde{v}_{i}}\right)^{\prime},\\
(\widetilde{v}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{1})(-\infty)=(v_{-},u_{-}),\quad(\widetilde{v}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{1})(+\infty)=(v_{m},u_{m}),\\
(\widetilde{v}_{2},\widetilde{u}_{2})(-\infty)=(v_{m},u_{m}),\quad(\widetilde{v}_{2},\widetilde{u}_{2})(+\infty)=(v_{+},u_{+}).\end{cases}

Notice that each viscous shock
(\widetilde{v}_{i}(x-\sigma_{i}t),\widetilde{u}_{i}(x-\sigma_{i}t))
is a traveling wave solution to (
1.1
).

In this paper, we aims to prove that solutions to the Navier-Stokes system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with (
1.5
) converge to the composite wave
(\bar{v},\bar{u})
up to shifts, uniformly in
x
as
t\to\infty
.

As previous results on time-asymptotic stability for (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) when the end states are connected by a single shock, Matsumura and Nishihara
[
17
]
first proved the convergence of solutions toward a single viscous shock with small amplitude, uniformly in
x
, using the anti-derivative method under the zero-mass condition. Later on, this zero-mass condition is removed by introducing a constant shift with diffusion wave by Liu
[
13
]
, Liu-Zeng
[
15
]
and Szepessy-Xin
[
25
]
. On the other hand, Masica and Zumbrun
[
21
]
showed the spectral stability of viscous shock wave under the weaker condition compared to the zero-mass condition, called a spectral condition. Recently, Wang-Wang
[
28
]
studied on a planar shock wave for the three-dimensional barotropic Navier-Stokes equations, by utilizing a new method called “
a
-contraction with shifts”.

For the composition of two shocks as in our setting, Huang-Matsumura
[
3
]
showed the convergence toward a composite wave composed of two viscous shocks for the Navier-Stokes-Fourier system when the strengths of two viscous shocks are small with the same order. As mentioned in
[
16
]
, the same result also could be obtained for the barotropic case (
1.1
), using the parallel argument as in
[
3
,
17
]
.

In this paper, however, we do not assume the same order smallness, that is, our result provides the uniform convergence toward a composition of two viscous shocks with independently small amplitudes.

On the other hand, when the initial data (
1.4
) generates rarefaction waves, the time-asymptotic stability of the rarefaction wave has been proven by Matsumura-Nishihara
[
18
,
19
]
. Similar stability results are also shown for the Navier-Stokes-Fourier system in
[
14
,
22
]
.

However, all the mentioned literature treated either shocks or rarefaction waves, but not the composition of them. Indeed, the time-asymptotic stability of the composition of shock and rarefaction waves is a challenging problem
[
17
]
. This open problem is recently solved by the second author, Vasseur and Wang
[
11
]
, using the method of
a
-contraction with shift for the shock wave, combining with the energy method for the rarefaction wave.

We will apply the method of
a
-contraction with shifts to prove the time-asymptotic behavior as a composition of two viscous shock waves in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a given constant state
(v_{+},u_{+})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, there exist positive constants
\delta_{0},\varepsilon_{0}
such that the following holds.

For any constant states
(v_{m},u_{m})
and
(v_{-},u_{-})
satisfying (
1.5
) with

(1.8)

|v_{+}-v_{m}|+|v_{m}-v_{-}|<\delta_{0},

let
(\widetilde{v}_{i},\widetilde{u}_{i})(x-\sigma_{i}t)
be the
i
-viscous shock wave satisfying (
1.7
). In addition, let
(v_{0},u_{0})
be any initial data satisfying

\sum_{\pm}\left(\|v_{0}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|u_{0}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)+\|\partial_{x}v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\varepsilon_{0},

where
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=(0,+\infty)
and
\mathbb{R}_{-}:=(-\infty,0)
. Then, the compressible Navier-Stokes system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with (
1.5
) admits a unique global-in-time solution
(v,u)
in the following sense: there exist absolutely continuous shift functions
X_{1}(t),X_{2}(t)
such that

\displaystyle v(t,x)-(\widetilde{v}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t-X_{1}(t))+\widetilde{v}_{2}(x-\sigma_{2}t-X_{2}(t))-v_{m})\in C(0,+\infty;H^{1}(\mathbb{R})),

\displaystyle u(t,x)-(\widetilde{u}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t-X_{2}(t))+\widetilde{u}_{2}(x-\sigma_{2}t-X_{2}(t))-u_{m})\in C(0,+\infty;H^{1}(\mathbb{R})).

Moreover, we have the large-time behavior:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|v(t,x)-\big(\widetilde{v}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t-X_{1}(t))+\widetilde{v}_{2}(x-\sigma_{2}t-X_{2}(t))-v_{m}\big)\Big|=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|u(t,x)-\big(\widetilde{u}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t-X_{1}(t))+\widetilde{u}_{2}(x-\sigma_{2}t-X_{2}(t))-u_{m}\big)\Big|=0,

where

(1.9)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}|\dot{X}_{i}(t)|=0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad i=1,2.

Especially, the shifts are well-separated in the following sense:

(1.10)

X_{1}(t)+\sigma_{1}t\leq\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}t<0<\frac{\sigma_{2}}{2}t\leq X_{2}(t)+\sigma_{2}t,\quad t>0.

Remark 1.1
.

1. It follows from the Rankine-Hugoniot condition (
1.5
) that the strength of 1-shock (resp. 2-shock) can be measured by
|v_{-}-v_{m}|
(resp.
|v_{m}-v_{+}|
). Thus, by the smallness condition (
1.8
), the strengths of the two shocks can be chosen independently. This removes the same order assumption, i.e.
|v_{-}-v_{m}|+|v_{-}-v_{m}|\lesssim\min(|v_{-}-v_{m}|,|v_{-}-v_{m}|)\ll 1
that is a crucial assumption in
[
3
]
.

2.
The property (
1.9
) implies that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{X_{i}(t)}{t}=0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad i=1,2.

Thus, the shift functions
X_{i}(t)
grow at most sub-linearly w.r.t.
t
, by which the shifted composite wave

\big(\widetilde{v}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{1}\big)\big(x-\sigma_{1}t-X_{1}(t)\big)+\big(\widetilde{v}_{2},\widetilde{u}_{2}\big)\big(x-\sigma_{2}t-X_{2}(t)\big)-(v_{m},u_{m})

time-asymptotically keeps the original composite wave.

The remaining part of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present a main idea of the proof, and useful estimates for the shock waves. Elementary estimates for the pressure and relative entropy are also presented. In Section
3
, we present the proof of the main theorem under the a priori estimates. The a priori estimates will be proved in the following two sections. In Section
4
, we use the method of
a
-contraction with shifts to estimate the
L^{2}
-perturbation. Then, the remaining estimates will be obtained in Section
5
.

2.
Main ideas of the proof and useful lemmas

In this section, we present a main idea of the proof, and useful estimates for the shock waves and relative quantities.

2.1.
Main ideas and the method of
a
-contraction with shifts

The main tool to obtain the desired nonlinear stability is the method of
a
-contraction with shifts, which was introduced by the second author and Vasseur in
[
5
]
(see also
[
27
]
) to study the stability of extremal shocks for the hyperbolic system of conservation laws such as the Euler system (
1.3
).

This method has been extended to studying viscous models as follows. For the one-dimensional barotropic Navier-Stokes system, the contraction property of any large perturbations for a single viscous shock is proved in
[
8
]
, and for a composite wave of two shocks in
[
7
]
. Those results play a crucial role to prove the stability of entropy Riemann shocks of the isentropic Euler system in the class of inviscid limits from the Navier-Stokes system in
[
8
,
9
]
. As mentioned in Introduction, this method was also used in
[
11
,
28
]
to show the long-time behavior of the barotropic Navier-Stokes system for the composition of shock and rarefaction in 1D, and for a single shock in multi-D.
As applications of the method to other viscous hyperbolic systems, we also refer to
[
4
,
6
,
10
]
for viscous scalar conservation laws,
[
12
]
for the stability of a planar contact discontinuity of the 3D full Euler system in the class of zero dissipation limits, and
[
1
,
2
]
for the viscous hyperbolic system arising from a chemotaxis model.

To illustrate a key idea of the method for the viscous system (
1.1
), consider the entropy
\eta
of the Euler system (
1.3
) defined as

\eta(U):=\frac{u^{2}}{2}+Q(v),\quad Q(v):=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)v^{\gamma-1}},

where
U=(v,u)
. Then, the relative entropy
\eta(U|\overline{U})
between two states
U
and
\overline{U}
is defined as

\eta(U|\overline{U}):=\eta(U)-\eta(\overline{U})-D\eta(\overline{U})(U-\overline{U})=\frac{|u-\overline{u}|^{2}}{2}+Q(v|\overline{v}),

where
Q(v|\overline{v}):=Q(v)-Q(\overline{v})-Q^{\prime}(\overline{v})(v-\overline{v})
. Since
Q(v)
is a strictly convex function in
v
,
Q(\cdot|\cdot)
is locally quadratic in the sense that for any
0<a<b
, there exists
C>0
such that

C^{-1}|v_{1}-v_{2}|^{2}\leq Q(v_{1}|v_{2})\leq C|v_{1}-v_{2}|^{2},\qquad\forall v_{1},v_{2}\in[a,b].

When
\overline{U}(x-\sigma t)
is a single viscous shock, the method is to find a weight function
a:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and a time-dependent shift function
X:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
such that the weighted relative entropy with shift is not increasing in time:

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(x-\sigma t-{X}(t))\eta(U(t,x)|\overline{U}(x-\sigma t-{X}(t)))\,dx\leq 0.

First, by a standard computation based on the relative entropy method, the left-hand side can be decomposed into three parts (as in Lemma
4.2
for example):

\mbox{LHS}=X^{\prime}(t)Y(U)+\mathcal{J}^{\rm bad}(U)-\mathcal{J}^{\rm good}(U),

where
\mathcal{J}^{\rm bad}(U)
and
\mathcal{J}^{\rm good}(U)
consist of the all bad terms and all good terms respectively.
To make the right-hand side non-positive, we might use the typical energy method for parabolic equations. However, since the barotropic Naiver-Stokes system has the dissipation in one variable only (more precisely, in the
u
variable for (
1.1
)), the weight function
a
would be found to provide an additional good term in terms of the
v
variable, by which the bad terms could be represented only by the
u
variables. Indeed, since
\sigma
is a non-zero constant,
constructing a monotone function
a
satisfying
\sigma a^{\prime}>0
, we have a good term

-\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{\prime}(x-\sigma t-{X}(t))\eta(U(t,x)|\overline{U}(x-\sigma t-{X}(t)))\,dx.

In fact, the weight function
a
will be defined by the first component
\overline{v}
of the viscous shock such that
a^{\prime}
localizes the perturbation in space as done by
\overline{v}^{\prime}
, and the image of
a
is a bounded open interval.
Using the above term, we maximize all bad terms in terms of the
v
variable, from which the remaining bad terms are related to the
u
variable only, and localized by
a^{\prime}
or
\overline{v}^{\prime}
.
To absorb the remaining bad terms by the diffusion term, we may use the following Poincaré-type inequality.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
8
, Lemma 2.9]

For any
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|f^{\prime}|^{2}dy<\infty
,

(2.1)

\int_{0}^{1}\left|f-\int_{0}^{1}fdy\right|^{2}dy\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|f^{\prime}|^{2}dy.

However, to apply Lemma
2.1
, we may need the other good term as an average of linear perturbation on
u
-variable, which together with the bad terms would give a variance of the perturbation that could be absorbed by the dissipation as in the Poincaré-type inequality. Here, the desired good term would be extracted from the shift part
X^{\prime}(t)Y(U)
by a sophisticated construction of the shift
X(t)
. This gives the desired contraction estimate.

However, the composition (
1.6
) of two viscous shocks leads to several problematic issues. Since the composition of two viscous shocks is not an exact solution to the Navier-Stokes equations, unlike the case of a single viscous shock, due to the nonlinear terms in the equations, we need to control some error terms on the nonlinear interactions between two shock waves. Second, as we consider two shock waves, we should construct two shift functions, say
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, and two weight functions to control perturbation near each shock wave, based on the above method for a single wave. On top of that, since all the bad terms are localized by the derivative of each weight or shock, whereas the diffusion term is not localized, we need to localize the diffusion term by introducing some auxiliary localization functions. The localization functions will be defined by two shift functions as in (
3.11
), by constructing the two shift functions to be well-separated like (
1.10
).

2.2.
Viscous shock waves

We review the useful estimates of viscous shocks and their derivatives that will be used throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
7
, Lemma 5.1]

For a given constant
U_{*}:=(v_{*},u_{*})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, there exist positive constants
\delta_{0}
,
C
,
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that the following holds. Let
U_{-}:=(v_{-},u_{-})
,
U_{m}:=(v_{m},u_{m})
, and
U_{+}:=(v_{+},u_{+})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
be any constants such that
U_{-},U_{m},U_{+}\in B_{\delta_{0}}(U_{*})
, and
|p(v_{-})-p(v_{m})|=:\delta_{1}<\delta_{0}
and
|p(v_{m})-p(v_{+})|=:\delta_{2}<\delta_{0}
. Let
\widetilde{U}_{1}=(\widetilde{v}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{1})
and
\widetilde{U}_{2}=(\widetilde{v}_{2},\widetilde{u}_{2})
be the 1- and 2-shocks connecting from
U_{-}
to
U_{m}
and from
U_{m}
to
U_{+}
respectively, satisfying
\widetilde{v}_{1}(0)=\frac{v_{-}+v_{m}}{2}
and
\widetilde{v}_{2}(0)=\frac{v_{m}+v_{+}}{2}
without loss of generality. Then, the following estimates hold: for each
i=1,2
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &v_{i}^{\prime}\sim u_{i}^{\prime}\quad\mbox{i.e.,}\quad C^{-1}\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\prime}(x-\sigma_{i}t)\leq\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\prime}(x-\sigma_{i}t)\leq C\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\prime}(x-\sigma_{i}t),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\,t>0,\\
&C^{-1}\delta_{i}e^{-C_{1}\delta_{i}|x-\sigma_{i}t|}\leq\widetilde{v}_{i}(x-\sigma_{i}t)-v_{m}\leq C\delta_{i}e^{-C_{2}\delta_{i}|x-\sigma_{i}t|},\quad(-1)^{i}(x-\sigma_{i}t)\leq 0,\\
&-C^{-1}\delta_{i}^{2}e^{-C_{1}\delta_{i}|x-\sigma_{i}t|}\leq\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\prime}(x-\sigma_{i}t)\leq-C\delta_{i}^{2}e^{-C_{2}\delta_{i}|x-\sigma_{i}t|},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad t>0,\\
\end{aligned}

in addition,

(2.3)

|(\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\prime\prime}(x-\sigma_{i}t),\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\prime\prime}(x-\sigma_{i}t))|\leq C\delta_{i}|(\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\prime}(x-\sigma_{i}t),\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\prime}(x-\sigma_{i}t))|.

Remark 2.1
.

Throughout the paper, we will use Lemma
2.2
with
U_{*}=U_{+}
. Thus, the constants
\delta_{0}
,
C
,
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
depend only on
U_{+}
.

2.3.
Estimate on the relative quantities

As our method is based on the estimate of relative entropy, we need to control the relative quantities, such as the relative pressure
p(v|w)
or the relative internal energy
Q(v|w)
. Recall that the relative quantities are defined as

p(v|w)=p(v)-p(w)-p^{\prime}(w)(v-w),\quad Q(v|w)=Q(v)-Q(w)-Q^{\prime}(w)(v-w).

Considering the Taylor expansion, it is expected that they are almost quadratic quantities at least locally. The following estimates show the exact estimate on this locally quadratic behavior of the relative quantities. The proof of the lemma can be found in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\gamma>1
and
v_{+}
be given constants. Then, there exist constants
C,\delta_{*}>0
such that the following assertions hold:

(1)

For any
v,w
satisfying
0<w<2v_{+}
and
0<v<3v_{+}
,

(2.4)

|v-w|^{2}\leq CQ(v|w),\quad|v-w|^{2}\leq Cp(v|w).

(2)

For any
v,w
satisfying
v,w>v_{+}/2
,

(2.5)

|p(v)-p(w)|\leq C|v-w|.

(3)

For any
0<\delta<\delta_{*}
and any
(v,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
satisfying
|p(v)-p(w)|<\delta
and
|p(w)-p(v_{+})|<\delta
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &p(v|w)\leq\left(\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma}\frac{1}{p(w)}+C\delta\right)|p(v)-p(w)|^{2},\\
&Q(v|w)\geq\frac{p(w)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{2\gamma}|p(v)-p(w)|^{2}-\frac{1+\gamma}{3\gamma^{2}}p(w)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-2}(p(v)-p(w))^{3},\\
&Q(v|w)\leq\left(\frac{p(w)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{2\gamma}+C\delta\right)|p(v)-p(w)|^{2}.\end{aligned}

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we present a main part of proof for Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Local existence

In order to get the desired result on time-asymptotic stability of
H^{1}
-perturbations of a composite wave connecting two different constant states at far-fields, we need to recall the well-known result on local-in-time existence of solutions to (
1.1
) connecting the two different constant states in
H^{1}
-norm.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
20
,
24
]

Let
\underline{v}
and
\underline{u}
be smooth monotone functions such that

\underline{v}(x)=v_{\pm},\quad\underline{u}(x)=u_{\pm},\quad\mbox{for}\quad\pm x\geq 1.

Then, for any constants
M_{0}
,
M_{1}
,
\underline{\kappa}_{0}
,
\overline{\kappa}_{0}
,
\underline{\kappa}_{1}
,
\overline{\kappa}_{1}
with

0<M_{0}<M_{1},\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0<\underline{\kappa}_{1}<\underline{\kappa}_{0}<\overline{\kappa}_{0}<\overline{\kappa}_{1},

there exists a constant
T_{0}>0
such that if the initial data
(v_{0},u_{0})
satisfy

\|v_{0}-\underline{v}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{0}-\underline{u}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq M_{0},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{\kappa}_{0}\leq v_{0}(x)\leq\overline{\kappa}_{0},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},

then the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) admit a unique solution
(v,u)
on
[0,T_{0}]
satisfying

\displaystyle v-\underline{v}\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R})),

\displaystyle u-\underline{u}\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{2}([0,T_{0}];H^{2}(\mathbb{R})),

together with

\|v-\underline{v}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{0}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|u-\underline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{0}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq M_{1},

and

(3.1)

\underline{\kappa}_{1}\leq v(t,x)\leq\overline{\kappa}_{1},\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{R}.

3.2.
Construction of shifts

As desired, we should show that a small
H^{1}
-perturbation of a composite wave of two viscous shocks is orbitally stable for a large time, more precisely, the perturbation uniformly converges to the composite wave up to shifts where each shock is shifted by
X_{i}(t)
:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}},\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}})(t,x)\\
&\qquad:=\left(\widetilde{v}_{1}^{X_{1}}(x-\sigma_{1}t)+\widetilde{v}_{2}^{X_{2}}(x-\sigma_{2}t)-v_{m},\widetilde{u}_{1}^{X_{1}}(x-\sigma_{1}t)+\widetilde{u}_{2}^{X_{2}}(x-\sigma_{2}t)-u_{m}\right),\end{aligned}

where
F^{X_{i}}
denotes a function
F
shifted by
X_{i}
, that is,
F^{X_{i}}(x):=F(x-X_{i}(t))
for any function
F
. This notation will be used throughout the paper.

We here introduce the shift functions explicitly, from which we could obtain a bound of derivative of shifts (at least locally-in-time) in Lemma
3.1
, and obtain the desired a priori estimates in Proposition
3.2
. Those will be used for the continuation argument in Section
3.4
.
We define a pair of shifts
(X_{1},X_{2})
as a solution to the system of ODEs:

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\dot{X}_{1}(t)=-\frac{M}{\delta_{1}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{a^{X_{1},X_{2}}}{\sigma_{1}}(\widetilde{h}^{X_{1}}_{1})_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}))\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X_{1},X_{2}}(p(\widetilde{v}^{X_{1}}_{1}))_{x}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}})\,dx\bigg],\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle\dot{X}_{2}(t)=-\frac{M}{\delta_{2}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{a^{X_{1},X_{2}}}{\sigma_{2}}(\widetilde{h}^{X_{2}}_{2})_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}))\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X_{1},X_{2}}(p(\widetilde{v}^{X_{2}}_{2}))_{x}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}})\,dx\bigg],\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle X_{1}(0)=X_{2}(0)=0,\end{array}\right.

where
a^{X_{1},X_{2}}
is the shifted weight function defined in (
4.7
),
\widetilde{h}_{i}:=\widetilde{u}_{i}-(\ln\widetilde{v}_{i})_{x}
, and
M
is the specific constant chosen as
M:=\frac{5(\gamma+1)}{8\gamma p(v_{m})}\big(-p^{\prime}(v_{m})\big)^{\frac{3}{2}}
, which will be used in the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
However, for well-posedness of the above ODEs in Lemma
3.1
, we only need the following assumption for the shifted weight function at this point:
a^{X_{1},X_{2}}
is a
C^{1}
-function of
(x,X_{1},X_{2})
with finite
C^{1}
-norm. This is verified by the explicit one defined in (
4.7
).

The following lemma ensures that (
3.3
) has a unique absolutely continuous solution at least for the lifespan
[0,T_{0}]
of solution
v
satisfying (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that the following is true. For any
T>0
, and any function
v\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})
verifying

(3.4)

c_{1}\leq v(t,x)\leq c_{2},\qquad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R},

the system (
3.3
) has a unique absolutely continuous solution
(X_{1},X_{2})
on
[0,T]
. Moreover,

(3.5)

|X_{1}(t)|+|X_{2}(t)|\leq Ct,\quad\forall t\leq T.

Proof.

Let
F(t,X_{1},X_{2})
denote the right-hand side of the system (
3.3
).

Using (
3.4
) and the facts that the
C^{1}
-norm of
a^{X_{1},X_{2}}
w.r.t.
(x,X_{1},X_{2})
is finite, and

\|\widetilde{h}_{i}\|_{C^{2}}<\infty,\quad\|\widetilde{v}_{i}\|_{C^{2}}<\infty,\quad\|(\widetilde{h}_{i})_{x}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\delta_{i},\quad\|(\widetilde{v}_{i})_{x}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\delta_{i},

we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sup_{X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathbb{R}}|F(t,X_{1},X_{2})|\\
&\quad\leq C\|a\|_{C^{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{1}{\delta_{i}}\Big(\|(p(\widetilde{v}_{i})\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|p(v)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\widetilde{v}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)(\|(\widetilde{h}_{i})_{x}\|_{L^{1}}+\|(\widetilde{v}_{i})_{x}\|_{L^{1}})\leq C,\end{aligned}

where the constant
C
is independent of
t
.
Likewise, we have

\sup_{X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathbb{R}}|\nabla_{X_{1},X_{2}}F(t,X_{1},X_{2})|\leq C.

Therefore, (
3.3
) has a unique absolutely continuous solution by a simple adaptation of Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem (for example, by
[
1
, Lemma A.1]
and
[
7
, Lemma C.1]
).

Since
|\dot{X_{1}}(t)|+|\dot{X_{2}}(t)|\leq C
by (
3.6
), we have (
3.5
).
∎

3.3.
Main proposition for a priori estimates

Based on the previous setting, we present the main proposition for a priori estimate.

Proposition 3.2
.

For a given constant
U_{+}:=(v_{+},u_{+})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, there exist positive constants
\delta_{0}
,
C_{0}
, and
\varepsilon_{1}
such that the following holds:

For any constant states
U_{m}:=(v_{m},u_{m})\in S_{2}(v_{+},u_{+})
and
U_{+}:=(v_{-},u_{-})\in S_{1}(v_{m},u_{m})
satisfying

|p(v_{-})-p(v_{m})|=:\delta_{1}<\delta_{0}
and
|p(v_{m})-p(v_{+})|=:\delta_{2}<\delta_{0}
, let
(\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}},\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}})
denote the composite wave of two shifted shocks as in (
3.2
), where
(X_{1},X_{2})
solves (
3.3
).
Suppose that
(v,u)
is the solution to
(
1.1
)
on
[0,T]
for some
T>0
, and satisfy

\displaystyle v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{R})),

\displaystyle u-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R})),

and

(3.7)

\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq\varepsilon_{1}.

Then, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sup_{t\in[0,T]}&\left(\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\right)+\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\delta_{i}|X_{i}|^{2}\,ds}\\
&\quad+\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathcal{G}^{S}(U)+{D}(U)+D_{1}(U)+D_{2}(U)\right)\,ds}\\
&\leq C_{0}\left(\|v_{0}-\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\right)+C_{0}\delta_{0}^{1/4}.\end{aligned}

In particular,

(3.9)

|\dot{X}_{1}(t)|+|\dot{X}_{2}(t)|\leq C_{0}\|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\qquad t\leq T.

Here, the constant
C_{0}
is independent of
T
and

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{S}(U)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}||\phi_{i}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}})|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle D(U)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}))|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle D_{1}(U)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}})_{x}|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle D_{2}(U)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}})_{xx}|^{2}\,dx,

where
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are cutoff functions defined by

(3.11)

\phi_{1}(t,x):=\begin{cases}1&\quad\mbox{if}\quad x<\frac{X_{1}(t)+\sigma_{1}t}{2},\\
0&\quad\mbox{if}\quad x>\frac{X_{2}(t)+\sigma_{2}t}{2},\\
\mbox{linearly decreasing $1$ to $0$}&\quad\mbox{if}\quad\frac{X_{1}+\sigma_{1}t}{2}\leq x\leq\frac{X_{2}+\sigma_{2}t}{2},\end{cases}\quad\phi_{2}(t,x):=1-\phi_{1}(t,x).

Remark 3.1
.

Since the (shifted) composite wave
(\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}},\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}})
is not a solution to the Navier-Stokes equation as in (
3.25
), we need to control the interaction term of the 1- and 2-waves to get the desired results of Proposition
3.2
. For that, we localize the perturbation near each wave by using the above cutoff functions. More specifically,
\phi_{1}
(resp.
\phi_{2}
) localizes the perturbation near the 1-wave (resp. 2-wave) shifted by
X_{1}
(resp.
X_{2}
). Notice from (
3.22
) that

X_{2}(t)+\sigma_{2}t\geq\frac{\sigma_{2}}{2}t>0>\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}t\geq X_{1}(t)+\sigma_{1}t,\qquad t>0,

and so the functions
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are well-separated as time goes.

3.4.
Global-in-time existence for perturbation

We here prove the global existence of solution by using the continuation argument based on Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
. We first assume that the constants
\delta_{0},C
, and
\varepsilon_{1}
are given by Proposition
3.2
, and consider the end states
(v_{-},u_{-})
and
(v_{+},u_{+})
satisfying the conditions in Proposition
3.2
. First of all, the smooth monotone functions
\underline{v}(x)
and
\underline{u}(x)
given in Proposition
3.1
satisfy

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{\pm}&\left(\|\underline{v}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|\underline{u}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)+\|\partial_{x}\underline{v}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}\underline{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&\leq C(|v_{+}-v_{-}|+|u_{+}-u_{-}|)\leq C_{1}(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}).\end{aligned}

Then, we estimate the
H^{1}
-perturbation between the monotone functions
\underline{v},\underline{u}
and the initial composite waves
\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot),\widetilde{u}(0,\cdot)
in (
1.6
) as

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\underline{v}(\cdot)-\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\underline{u}(\cdot)-\widetilde{u}(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&\leq\|\underline{v}-\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}(\underline{v}-\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\underline{u}-\widetilde{u}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}(\underline{u}-\widetilde{u}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&\leq\sum_{\pm}\left(\|\underline{v}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|\underline{u}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)\\
&\quad+\|\widetilde{v}_{1}-v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\widetilde{v}_{1}-v_{-}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{-})}+\|\widetilde{v}_{2}-v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\widetilde{v}_{2}-v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{-})}\\
&\quad+\|\partial_{x}\underline{v}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}\widetilde{v}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}\widetilde{v}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&\quad+\|\widetilde{u}_{1}-u_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\widetilde{u}_{1}-u_{-}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{-})}+\|\widetilde{u}_{2}-u_{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\widetilde{u}_{2}-u_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{-})}\\
&\quad+\|\partial_{x}\underline{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&\leq C_{2}(\sqrt{\delta_{1}}+\sqrt{\delta_{2}}),\end{aligned}

where we used the shock estimates (
2.2
) and (
3.12
) in the last inequality. Now, for the constant
\varepsilon_{1}
, we choose
\delta_{0}
small enough so that the following condition holds for any
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}<\delta_{0}
:

\frac{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{4}-C_{0}\delta_{0}^{1/4}}{C_{0}+1}-C_{1}(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})-C_{2}(\sqrt{\delta_{1}}+\sqrt{\delta_{2}})\geq 0.

Consider the two positive constants
\varepsilon_{*}
and
\varepsilon_{0}
defined as

\varepsilon_{*}:=\frac{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}-C_{0}\delta_{0}^{1/4}}{C_{0}+1}-C_{2}(\sqrt{\delta_{1}}+\sqrt{\delta_{2}}),\qquad\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{4(C_{0}+1)}.

We now consider any initial data
(v_{0},u_{0})
satisfying

\sum_{\pm}(\|v_{0}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|u_{0}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})})+\|\partial_{x}v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\varepsilon_{0},

which implies that the initial
H^{1}
-perturbation from the monotone functions
\underline{v},\underline{u}
is small:

(3.14)

\|v_{0}-\underline{v}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{0}-\underline{u}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\varepsilon_{0}+C_{1}(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})\leq\varepsilon_{*}.

In particular, Sobolev embedding implies
\|v_{0}-\underline{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\varepsilon_{*}
, and therefore,

\frac{v_{-}}{2}<v_{0}(x)<2v_{+},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
0<\varepsilon_{*}<\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}
, we use Proposition
3.1
to guarantee the local existence of the unique solution
(v,u)
on
[0,T_{0}]
satisfying

(3.15)

\|v-\underline{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|u-\underline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2},

and

\frac{v_{-}}{3}<v(t,x)<3v_{+},\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{R}.

We now use a similar argument as in (
3.13
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|\underline{v}-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\underline{u}-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\pm}\left(\|\underline{v}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|\underline{u}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\|\widetilde{v}_{1}^{X_{1}}-v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\widetilde{v}_{1}^{X_{1}}-v_{-}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{-})}+\|\widetilde{v}_{2}^{X_{2}}-v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\widetilde{v}_{2}^{X_{2}}-v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{-})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\partial_{x}\underline{v}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}\widetilde{v}_{1}^{X_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}\widetilde{v}_{2}^{X_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\widetilde{u}_{1}^{X_{1}}-u_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\widetilde{u}_{1}^{X_{1}}-u_{-}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{-})}+\|\widetilde{u}_{2}^{X_{2}}-u_{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\widetilde{u}_{2}^{X_{2}}-u_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{-})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\partial_{x}\underline{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}_{1}^{X_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}_{2}^{X_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\delta_{1}}(1+\sqrt{X_{1}(t)})+C\sqrt{\delta_{2}}(1+\sqrt{X_{2}(t)}),

where we used

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle|\widetilde{v}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t-X_{1}(t))-v_{m}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\widetilde{v}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t)-v_{m}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{-X_{1}(t)}^{0}|\widetilde{v}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t)-v_{m}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{1}(1+|X_{1}(t)|),

and a similar estimates for
\widetilde{v}_{2}
. Then, using Lemma
3.1
, we have

\|\underline{v}-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\underline{u}-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\sqrt{\delta_{0}}(1+\sqrt{t}).

Therefore, taking
0<T_{1}<T_{0}
small enough such that
C\sqrt{\delta_{0}}(1+\sqrt{T_{1}})<\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}
, we obtain

(3.16)

\|\underline{v}-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\underline{u}-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}.

We now combine (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) to obtain the following
H^{1}
-perturbation estimate

(3.17)

\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}<\varepsilon_{1}.

In particular, since the shifts
X_{i}(t)
are absolutely continuous, we have

v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}},u-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\in C([0,T_{1}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R})).

In order to extend the solution globally-in-time, we consider the maximal time
T_{*}
defined as

T_{*}:=\sup\left\{t>0~\Bigg|~\sup_{[0,t]}\left(\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\leq\varepsilon_{1}\right\}.

Suppose
T_{*}<+\infty
. Then, by the continuation argument,

(3.18)

\sup_{[0,T_{*}]}\left(\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\right)=\varepsilon_{1}.

However, by (
3.13
) and (
3.14
),

\|v_{0}-\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}-C_{0}\delta_{0}^{1/4}}{C_{0}+1},

which together with Proposition
3.2
, implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{[0,T_{*}]}

\displaystyle\left(\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\frac{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}-C_{0}\delta_{0}^{1/4}}{C_{0}+1}+C_{0}\delta_{0}^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}-C_{0}\delta_{0}^{1/4}\right)+C_{0}\delta_{0}^{1/4}=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}.

This contradicts to (
3.18
) and therefore, we conclude that
T_{*}=+\infty
. Then, it again follows from Proposition
3.2
that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert u-\widetilde{u}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\right)+\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\delta_{i}|X_{i}|^{2}\,ds}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{G}^{S}(U)+D(U)+D_{1}(U)+D_{2}(U)\right)\,ds}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\|v_{0}-\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\right)+C_{0}\delta_{0}^{1/4},

and

(3.20)

|\dot{X}_{1}(t)|+|\dot{X}_{2}(t)|\leq C_{0}\|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\qquad t>0.

Especially, since the right-hand side of (
3.19
) is small enough, we find that

(3.21)

\frac{v_{-}}{3}<v(t,x)<3v_{+},\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}.

3.5.
Time-asymptotic behavior

Based on the global estimates (
3.19
)-(
3.21
), we will show the time-asymptotic behavior in Theorem
1.1
.
The proof mainly follows the typical argument, but we will crucially use the following two lemmas based on the wave separation by shifts, since the energy functional
\mathcal{G}^{S}
is localized by the cutoff functions
\phi_{i}
.

First, the estimate (
3.19
) with the Sobolev embedding implies

\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})}\leq C(\varepsilon_{0}+\delta_{0}^{1/4}).

Thus, (
3.20
) and the smallness of
\delta_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{0}
imply

X_{1}(t)\leq-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}t,\quad X_{2}(t)\geq-\frac{\sigma_{2}}{2}t,\quad t>0,

or equivalently,

(3.22)

X_{1}(t)+\sigma_{1}t\leq\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}t,\quad X_{2}(t)+\sigma_{2}t\geq\frac{\sigma_{2}}{2}t,\quad t>0,

which proves (
1.10
).
Thus, the waves
\widetilde{v}_{1}^{X_{1}}
and
\widetilde{v}_{2}^{X_{2}}
are well-separated, and so are
\widetilde{v}_{1}^{X_{1}}
and
\phi_{2}
. Using this property, we have the following lemmas on the wave separation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume (
3.22
). Given
v_{+}>0
, there exist positive constants
\delta_{0},C
such that for any
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in(0,\delta_{0})
, the following estimates hold. For each
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle|(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}||\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}-\widetilde{v}^{X_{i}}_{i}|\leq C\delta_{i}\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\exp\left(-C\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}t\right),\quad t>0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}||\widetilde{v}^{X_{1},X_{2}}-\widetilde{v}^{X_{i}}_{i}|\,dx\leq C\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\exp\left(-C\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}t\right),\quad t>0,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}||(\widetilde{v}_{2})^{X_{2}}_{x}|\,dx\leq C\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\exp\left(-C\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}t\right),\quad t>0.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume (
3.22
). Let
\phi_{i}
be the functions defined in (
3.11
). Given
v_{+}>0
, there exist positive constants
\delta_{0},C
such that for any
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in(0,\delta_{0})
, the following estimates hold.

4.
Estimates on weighted relative entropy with shifts

We will use the method of

-contraction with shifts to obtain the

-bounds of perturbations in (

h:=u-(\ln v)_{x}
as in
[

]
(see also
[
8
,
9
]
) to transform the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) to the system:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &v_{t}-h_{x}=\left(\ln v\right)_{xx},\\
&h_{t}+p(v)_{x}=0.\end{aligned}

Then, it follows from (
1.7
) that the associated viscous shocks
\widetilde{v}_{i}
and
\widetilde{h}_{i}:=\widetilde{u}_{i}-(\ln\widetilde{v}_{i})_{x}
satisfy the following ODEs:

(4.2)

\begin{cases}-\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{\prime}-(\widetilde{h}_{i})^{\prime}=(\ln\widetilde{v}_{i})^{\prime\prime},\\
-\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{h}_{i})^{\prime}+(p(\widetilde{v}_{i}))^{\prime}=0,\\
(\widetilde{v}_{1},\widetilde{h}_{1})(-\infty)=(v_{-},u_{-}),\quad(\widetilde{v}_{1},\widetilde{h}_{1})(+\infty)=(v_{m},u_{m}),\\
(\widetilde{v}_{2},\widetilde{h}_{2})(-\infty)=(v_{m},u_{m}),\quad(\widetilde{v}_{2},\widetilde{h}_{2})(+\infty)=(v_{+},u_{+}).\end{cases}

Let
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{h})
denote the shifted composite wave as

(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{h})(t,x):=\left(\widetilde{v}_{1}^{X_{1}}(x-\sigma_{1}t)+\widetilde{v}_{2}^{X_{2}}(x-\sigma_{2}t)-v_{m},\widetilde{h}_{1}^{X_{1}}(x-\sigma_{1}t)+\widetilde{h}_{2}^{X_{2}}(x-\sigma_{2}t)-u_{m}\right),

which satisfies

(4.3)

\begin{cases}\widetilde{v}_{t}-\widetilde{h}_{x}=-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\dot{X}_{i}(t)(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}+E_{1}\\
\widetilde{h}_{t}+p(\widetilde{v})_{x}=-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\dot{X_{i}}(\widetilde{h}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}+E_{2},\end{cases}

where

and

are the interaction terms defined as

Remark 4.1

The goal of this section is to obtain the following control on the relative entropy.

Lemma 4.1

There exists a positive constant

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{|h-\widetilde{h}|^{2}}{2}+Q(v|\widetilde{v})\right)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\delta_{i}|\dot{X}_{i}|^{2}+{G}_{1}+\mathcal{G}^{S}+D\right)\,ds\\

4.1.

In order to deal with the two shock waves, we first introduce two weight functions

associated with 1-shock and 2-shock respectively: for each

where

Notice that

from which we have

To handle the shifted composite wave, we consider the following composition of shifted weight functions as

For notational simplicity as before, we will omit the dependence on shifts:

a(t,x):=a^{X_{1},X_{2}}(t,x).

4.3.
Relative entropy method

First of all, we rewrite the system (
4.1
) in the abstract form:

(4.11)

\partial_{t}U+\partial_{x}A(U)=\partial_{x}\Big(M(U)\partial_{x}D\eta(U)\Big),

where

U:={v\choose h},\quad A(U):={-h\choose p(v)},\quad M(U):={\frac{1}{\gamma p(v)}\quad 0\choose\quad\ 0\quad\ 0},

and

(4.12)

D\eta(U)={-p(v)\choose h}.

Indeed, by
-p^{\prime}(v)v=\gamma p(v)
, one has

\big(\ln v\big)_{xx}=\left(\frac{(-p(v))_{x}}{\gamma p(v)}\right)_{x}.

Then, using the nonnegative matrix
M(U)
and the gradient (
4.12
) of the entropy
\eta(U):=\frac{h^{2}}{2}+Q(v)
of (
4.1
), where
Q(v)=\frac{v^{-\gamma+1}}{\gamma-1}
, i.e.,
Q^{\prime}(v)=-p(v)
,
we have

{\big(\ln v\big)_{xx}\choose 0}=\partial_{x}\Big(M(U)\partial_{x}D\eta(U)\Big).

Let
\widetilde{U}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t)
and
\widetilde{U}_{2}(x-\sigma_{2}t)
be the viscous 1-shock and 2-shock of the system (
4.11
).
Then, the system (
4.2
) can be cast in the following form: for each
i=1,2,

(4.13)

-\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{U}_{i})_{x}+A(\widetilde{U}_{i})_{x}=\partial_{x}\left(M(\widetilde{U}_{i})\partial_{x}D\eta(\widetilde{U}_{i})\right).

Then, from (
4.3
), the shifted composite wave
\widetilde{U}:=(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{h})
satisfies

(4.14)

\partial_{t}\widetilde{U}+A(\widetilde{U})_{x}=\partial_{x}(M(\widetilde{U})\partial_{x}D\eta(\widetilde{U}))-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\dot{X}_{i}(\widetilde{U}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}+\begin{pmatrix}E_{1}\\
E_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

For the relative entropy method, we use the relative entropy functional
\eta(U|V)
and relative flux
A(U|V)
defined as

\eta(U|V):=\eta(U)-\eta(V)-D\eta(V)(U-V)

and

A(U|V):=A(U)-A(V)-DA(V)(U-V).

We also define the relative entropy flux
G(U;V)
as

G(U;V):=G(U)-G(V)-D\eta(V)(A(U)-A(V)),

where
G
is the entropy flux for
\eta
satisfying
\partial_{i}G(U)=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\partial_{k}\eta(U)+\partial_{i}A_{k}(U)
for
i=1,2
. The relative quantities for the system (
4.1
) can be explicitly written as follows:

\displaystyle\eta(U|\widetilde{U})=\frac{|h-\widetilde{h}|^{2}}{2}+Q(v|\widetilde{v}),

\displaystyle A(U|\widetilde{U})=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
p(v|\widetilde{v})\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle G(U;\widetilde{U})=(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))(h-\widetilde{h}).

Here, the relative internal energy
Q(v|\widetilde{v})
and the relative pressure
p(v|\widetilde{v})
are defined as

Q(v|\widetilde{v})=Q(v)-Q(\widetilde{v})-Q^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})(v-\widetilde{v}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad p(v|\widetilde{v})=p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})-p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})(v-\widetilde{v}).

In order to obtain the desired estimate in Lemma
4.1
, we need to control the relative entropy between the solution
U
to (
4.11
) and the shifted composite wave
\widetilde{U}
. However, as we mentioned, the direct estimate of the relative entropy would fail to control it, and therefore, we instead estimate the following weighted relative entropy:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(t,x)\eta\left(U(t,x)\Big|\widetilde{U}(t,x)\right)\,dx.

It follows from the choice of the weight function that the global weight
a(t,x)
satisfies
1/2\leq a\leq 1
by the smallness of
\lambda
, which recovers the control of the relative entropy. Following the estimate in
[
7
, Lemma 5.2]
(see also
[
11
, Lemma 4.3]
and
[
26
, Lemma 4]
), the time-derivative of the weighted relative entropy can be computed as follows.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
a:=a^{X_{1},X_{2}}
be the weight function defined in (
4.7
) and
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
be any absolutely continuous function. Let
U
be a solution to system (
4.11
) and
\widetilde{U}
be the shifted composition wave satisfying (
4.14
). Then, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(t,x)\eta\left(U(t,x)\Big|\widetilde{U}(t,x)\right)\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\dot{X}_{i}(t)Y_{i}(U))+\mathcal{J}^{\rm bad}(U)-\mathcal{J}^{\rm good}(U),

where

(4.15)

Y_{i}(U):=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(\widetilde{U}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}D^{2}\eta(\widetilde{U})(U-\widetilde{U})\,dx,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\rm bad}(U):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))(h-\widetilde{h})\,dx+\sigma_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}p(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\gamma p(v)}\partial_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))^{2}\frac{\partial_{x}p(\widetilde{v})}{\gamma p(v)p(\widetilde{v})}\,dx\Bigg]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\partial_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\gamma p(v)p(\widetilde{v})}\partial_{x}p(\widetilde{v})\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))E_{1}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(h-\widetilde{h})E_{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\rm good}(U):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left[\frac{\sigma_{i}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}\left|h-\widetilde{h}\right|^{2}\,dx+\sigma_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}Q(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{a}{\gamma p(v)}|\partial_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))|^{2}\,dx.

Remark 4.2
.

We note that the definition of the weight
a_{i}
implies

\sigma_{i}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}=-\frac{\lambda\sigma_{i}}{\delta_{i}}(p(\widetilde{v_{i}}^{X_{i}}))_{x}=\frac{\gamma\lambda\sigma_{i}}{\delta_{i}}(\widetilde{v_{i}}^{X_{i}})^{-\gamma-1}(\widetilde{v}_{i}^{X_{i}})_{x}>0,

from which we observe that
\mathcal{J}^{\rm good}
consists of good terms.

4.4.
Maximization on
h-\widetilde{h}

Among the terms in
\mathcal{J}^{\rm bad}(U)
, a primary bad term is

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))(h-\widetilde{h})\,dx

where the perturbations for
p(v)
and
h
are coupled. In order to exploit the parabolic term on
v
-variable and hence use the Poincaré-type inequality, we separate
h-\widetilde{h}
from
p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})
by using the quadratic structure of
h-\widetilde{h}
. Precisely, since

\displaystyle(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}

\displaystyle(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))(h-\widetilde{h})-\frac{\sigma_{i}}{2}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}|h-\widetilde{h}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sigma_{i}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}}{2}\left|h-\widetilde{h}-\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\sigma_{i}}\right|^{2}+\frac{(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}}{2\sigma_{i}}|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|^{2},

we rewrite the terms
\mathcal{J}^{\rm bad}(U)-\mathcal{J}^{\rm good}(U)
in the above lemma as

\mathcal{J}^{\text{bad}}(U)-\mathcal{J}^{\text{good}}(U)=\mathcal{B}(U)-\mathcal{G}(U),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(U)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Bigg[\frac{1}{2\sigma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|^{2}\,dx+\sigma_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}p(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\qquad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\gamma p(v)}\partial_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\qquad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))^{2}\frac{\partial_{x}p(\widetilde{v})}{\gamma p(v)p(\widetilde{v})}\,dx\Bigg]

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\partial_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\gamma p(v)p(\widetilde{v})}\partial_{x}p(\widetilde{v})\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))E_{1}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(h-\widetilde{h})E_{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(U)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left[\frac{\sigma_{i}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}\left|h-\widetilde{h}-\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\sigma_{i}}\right|^{2}\,dx+\sigma_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}Q(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{a}{\gamma p(v)}|\partial_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))|^{2}\,dx.

Therefore, we will estimate the right-hand side of the following equation:

(4.16)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\dot{X}_{i}Y_{i}(U))+\mathcal{B}(U)-\mathcal{G}(U).

4.5.
Decompositions

We first name each term of
\mathcal{B}(U)
and
\mathcal{G}(U)
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(U)=\sum_{i=1}^{5}\mathcal{B}_{i}(U)+\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(U)=\mathcal{G}_{1}(U)+\mathcal{G}_{2}(U)+\mathcal{D}(U),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}(U):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{1}{2\sigma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}(U):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sigma_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}p(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{3}(U):=-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\gamma p(v)}\partial_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{4}(U):=-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))^{2}\frac{\partial_{x}p(\widetilde{v})}{\gamma p(v)p(\widetilde{v})}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{5}(U):=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\partial_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\gamma p(v)p(\widetilde{v})}\partial_{x}p(\widetilde{v})\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))E_{1}\,dx,\quad\mathcal{S}_{2}:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(h-\widetilde{h})E_{2}\,dx,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}(U):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\sigma_{i}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}\left|h-\widetilde{h}-\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\sigma_{i}}\right|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}(U):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sigma_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}Q(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(U):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{a}{\gamma p(v)}|\partial_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))|^{2}\,dx.

For each
Y_{i}
in (
4.15
), we first write
Y_{i}
more explicitly as follows:

\displaystyle Y_{i}(U)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}\left(\frac{|h-\widetilde{h}|^{2}}{2}+Q(v|\widetilde{v})\right)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(\widetilde{h}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(h-\widetilde{h})\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}ap^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(v-\widetilde{v})\,dx.

Since a key idea in our analysis is to apply Poincaré-type inequality of Lemma
2.1
by extracting a good term on an average of perturbation
p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})
from the shift part
\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\dot{X}_{i}Y_{i}(U))
, we decompose
Y_{i}
as follows: for each
i
,

Y_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{6}Y_{ij},

where

\displaystyle Y_{i1}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{a}{\sigma_{i}}(\widetilde{h}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{i2}:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}ap^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{X_{i}}_{i})(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(v-\widetilde{v})\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(p(\widetilde{v}^{X_{i}}_{i}))_{x}(v-\widetilde{v})\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{i3}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(\widetilde{h}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}\left(h-\widetilde{h}-\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{i4}:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})-p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{X_{i}}_{i}))(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(v-\widetilde{v})\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{i5}:=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}\left(h-\widetilde{h}-\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\left(h-\widetilde{h}+\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{i6}:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}Q(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(a_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}}{2\sigma_{i}^{2}}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))^{2}\,dx.

Observe that it follows from our construction (
3.3
) on shifts
X_{i}
that

\dot{X}_{i}=-\frac{M}{\delta_{i}}(Y_{i1}+Y_{i2}),

which yields

(4.17)

\dot{X}_{i}Y_{i}(U)=-\frac{\delta_{i}}{M}|\dot{X_{i}}|^{2}+\dot{X}_{i}\sum_{j=3}^{6}Y_{ij}.

Here, the good term
-\frac{\delta_{i}}{M}|\dot{X_{i}}|^{2}
would give an average of linear perturbation on
v
-variable as mentioned above, while the remaining part would be controlled by the good terms in
\mathcal{G}(U)
. To show it, we combine (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) to have

(4.18)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx=\mathcal{R},\quad\mbox{where}\\
&\mathcal{R}:=-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\delta_{i}}{M}|\dot{X}_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(\dot{X_{i}}\sum_{j=3}^{6}Y_{ij}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{5}\mathcal{B}_{i}+\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2}-\mathcal{G}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}-\mathcal{D}.\\
&\quad=\underbrace{-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\delta_{i}}{2M}|\dot{X}_{i}|^{2}+\mathcal{B}_{1}+\mathcal{B}_{2}-\mathcal{G}_{2}-\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{D}}_{=:\mathcal{R}_{1}}\\
&\quad\quad\underbrace{-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\delta_{i}}{2M}|\dot{X}_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(\dot{X_{i}}\sum_{j=3}^{6}Y_{ij}\right)+\sum_{i=3}^{5}\mathcal{B}_{i}+\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2}-\mathcal{G}_{1}-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}}_{=:\mathcal{R}_{2}}.\end{aligned}

A motivation of the decomposition (
4.18
) is that the two bad terms
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}
are the main bad terms, which should be controlled by using the sharp Poincaré-type inequality of Lemma
2.1
. The remaining terms in
\mathcal{R}_{2}
can be controlled in a rather rough way.
We first focus on the estimate of
\mathcal{R}_{1}
.

4.6.
Estimate of the main part
\mathcal{R}_{1}

A key idea for estimates of
\mathcal{R}_{1}
is to use the Poincaré-type inequality (
2.1
) in Lemma
2.1
. To apply the Poincaré-type inequality, we need to localize the perturbation near each wave by using the cutoff functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
defined in (
3.11
) (see Remark
3.1
), and then change of variables from whole space
\mathbb{R}
to a bounded interval
(0,1)
for each wave.
For that, we will consider the following change of variables in space:

y_{1}:=1-\frac{p(v_{m})-p(\widetilde{v}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t))}{\delta_{1}},\qquad y_{2}:=\frac{p(v_{m})-p(\widetilde{v}_{2}(x-\sigma_{2}t))}{\delta_{2}}.

Indeed, for each
i
,
y_{i}:\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)
is monotone in
\xi_{i}:=x-\sigma_{i}t
, since

\frac{dy_{1}}{d\xi_{1}}=\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}p(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{\prime}>0,\quad\frac{dy_{2}}{d\xi_{2}}=-\frac{1}{\delta_{2}}p(\widetilde{v}_{2})^{\prime}>0,

and

\lim_{\xi_{i}\to-\infty}y_{i}=0,\quad\lim_{\xi_{i}\to+\infty}y_{i}=1.

In terms of the new variables, we will apply the Poincaré-type inequality to each perturbation
w_{i}
localized by
\phi_{i}
respectively:

\displaystyle w_{1}:=\phi_{1}(x+X_{1}(t))\Bigg(p(v(t,x+X_{1}(t)))-p\Big(\widetilde{v}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t)+\widetilde{v}_{2}(x-\sigma_{2}t-X_{2}(t)+X_{1}(t))-v_{m}\Big)\Bigg)\circ y_{1}^{-1},

\displaystyle w_{2}:=\phi_{2}(x+X_{2}(t))\Bigg(p(v(t,x+X_{2}(t)))-p\Big(\widetilde{v}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t-X_{1}(t)+X_{2}(t))+\widetilde{v}_{2}(x-\sigma_{2}t)-v_{m}\Big)\Bigg)\circ y_{2}^{-1}.

In what follows, for simplicity, we use the following notations to denote constants of
O(1)
-scale:

\sigma_{m}:=\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v_{m})},\quad\alpha_{m}:=\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma\sigma_{m}p(v_{m})},

which are indeed independent of the small constants
\delta_{i}
since
v_{+}/2\leq v_{m}\leq v_{+}
.

Since the shock strengths
\delta_{i}
are bounded by
\delta_{0}
respectively, the following estimates on the
O(1)
-constants hold:

(4.19)

|\sigma_{1}-(-\sigma_{m})|\leq C\delta_{1},\quad|\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{m}|\leq C\delta_{2},

and

(4.20)

\|\sigma_{m}^{2}-|p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}_{i})|\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta_{i},\quad\left\|\frac{1}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}-\frac{p(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{\gamma}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta_{i},\quad\left\|\frac{1}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}-\frac{p(\widetilde{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{\gamma}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta_{0}.

We are now ready to estimate the terms in
\mathcal{R}_{1}
.
As mentioned, we need to extract a good term on an average of the perturbation
w_{i}
from the shift part
\frac{\delta_{i}}{2M}|\dot{X}_{i}|^{2}
as follows, so that we could apply Lemma
2.1
.

\bullet

(Estimate of shift part
\frac{\delta_{i}}{2M}|\dot{X}_{i}|^{2}
):

We will show that: for each
i
,

(4.21)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\frac{\delta_{i}}{2M}|\dot{X}_{i}|^{2}&\leq-\frac{M\delta_{i}}{\sigma_{m}^{4}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}w_{i}\,dy_{i}\right)^{2}+C\delta_{i}(\lambda+\delta_{0}+\varepsilon_{1})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|w_{i}|^{2}\,dy_{i}\\
&\quad+C\delta_{i}\exp(-C\delta_{i}t)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx.\end{aligned}

As the estimates of
\frac{\delta_{1}}{2M}|\dot{X}_{1}|^{2}
and
\frac{\delta_{2}}{2M}|\dot{X}_{2}|^{2}
are the same, we only handle the case of
X_{1}
. Since
\dot{X}_{1}=-\frac{M}{\delta_{1}}(Y_{11}+Y_{12})
, we first estimate
Y_{11}
and
Y_{12}
.

Using the relation
\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}=1
, we have

\displaystyle Y_{11}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{a}{\sigma_{1}}(\widetilde{h}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}\phi_{1}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{a}{\sigma_{1}}(\widetilde{h}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}\phi_{2}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ap(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}\phi_{1}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{a}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}p(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}\phi_{2}(t,x)(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\,dx.

The first term is a main part for applying the Poincaré inequality. We use change of variable
x\to x+X_{1}(t)
and apply the change of variables for
y_{1},w_{1}
to observe that

\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ap(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}\phi_{1}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\,dx=\frac{\delta_{1}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}a(t,x+X_{1}(t))w_{1}\,dy_{1}.

Then, using (
4.19
) and
\|a-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})}\leq\lambda
, we have

\left|Y_{11}-\frac{\delta_{1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{1}\,dy_{1}\right|\leq C\delta_{1}(\lambda+\delta_{0})\int_{0}^{1}|w_{1}|\,dy_{1}+C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}|\phi_{2}|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|\,dx.

To estimate
Y_{12}
, we first use the equation of state
v=p(v)^{-1/\gamma}
and Taylor expansion to have

\left|v-\widetilde{v}-\left(-\frac{p(\widetilde{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{\gamma}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\right)\right|\leq C|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|^{2},

which together with the estimates (
4.20
) and (
4.8
) yields

\left|v-\widetilde{v}-\left(-\frac{1}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))\right)\right|\leq C(\delta_{0}+\varepsilon_{1})|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|.

This with the same argument as above implies

\left|Y_{12}-\frac{\delta_{1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{1}\,dy_{1}\right|\leq C\delta_{1}(\lambda+\delta_{0}+\varepsilon_{1})\int_{0}^{1}|w_{1}|\,dy_{1}+C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}|\phi_{2}|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|\,dx.

We combine the estimates for
Y_{11}
and
Y_{12}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left|\dot{X}_{1}+\frac{2M}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{1}\,dy_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{\delta_{1}}\left(\left|Y_{11}-\frac{\delta_{1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{1}\,dy_{1}\right|+\left|Y_{12}-\frac{\delta_{1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{1}\,dy_{1}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\lambda+\delta_{0}+\varepsilon_{1})\int_{0}^{1}|w_{1}|\,dy_{1}+\frac{C}{\delta_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}|\phi_{2}|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|dx,

which implies

\left(\left|\frac{2M}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{1}\,dy_{1}\right|-|\dot{X}_{1}|\right)^{2}\leq C(\lambda+\delta_{0}+\varepsilon_{1})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|w_{1}|^{2}\,dy_{1}+\frac{C}{\delta_{1}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}|\phi_{2}|(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))|dx\right)^{2}.

We use an elementary inequality
\frac{x^{2}}{2}-y^{2}\leq(x-y)^{2}
for
x,y\geq 0
to obtain

(4.22)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{2M^{2}}{\sigma_{m}^{4}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}w_{1}\,dy_{1}\right)^{2}-|\dot{X}_{1}|^{2}&\leq C(\lambda+\delta_{0}+\varepsilon_{1})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|w_{1}|^{2}\,dy_{1}\\
&\quad+\frac{C}{\delta_{1}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}|\phi_{2}|(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))|dx\right)^{2}.\end{aligned}

The last term can be estimated as follows: by using Lemmas
2.2
,
2.3
,
3.3
,

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\delta_{1}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}|\phi_{2}|(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))|\,dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{1}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}|\phi_{2})^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{1}^{2}}\sup_{t,x}\big(\phi_{2}^{2}|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}|\big)\|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{1}\exp(-C\delta_{1}t)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx.

Therefore, we substitute the above estimate to (
4.22
) to derive the following estimate on
\dot{X}_{1}
:

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\widetilde{U}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}D^{2}\eta(\widetilde{U})(U-\widetilde{U})\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})(\widetilde{v}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(v-\widetilde{v})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\widetilde{u}_{i})^{X_{i}}_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})\,dx=:\mathcal{Y}_{i1}+\mathcal{Y}_{i2},

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\psi_{xx}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert(|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{xx}|+|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}|^{2})|\widetilde{v}-\widetilde{v}_{1}^{X_{1}}|+(|(\widetilde{v}_{2})^{X_{2}}_{xx}|+|(\widetilde{v}_{2})^{X_{2}}_{x}|^{2})|\widetilde{v}-\widetilde{v}_{2}^{X_{2}}|+|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}||(\widetilde{v}_{2})^{X_{2}}_{x}|\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\mathbf{D}_{2}+C\left\lVert|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}||\widetilde{v}-\widetilde{v}_{1}^{X_{1}}|+|(\widetilde{v}_{2})^{X_{2}}_{x}||\widetilde{v}-\widetilde{v}_{2}^{X_{2}}|+|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}||(\widetilde{v}_{2})^{X_{2}}_{x}|\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, combining the estimates for
J_{i}
, there exists a positive constant
c_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\psi_{x}|^{2}}{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}\mathbf{D}_{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\delta_{i}}{2}|\dot{X}_{i}|^{2}+c_{2}D+C(\varepsilon_{1}+\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})(\mathcal{G}^{S}+D_{1})+C\varepsilon_{1}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\delta_{i}^{5/2}\exp(-C\delta_{i}t)

\displaystyle\quad+C\left\lVert|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}||\widetilde{v}-\widetilde{v}_{1}^{X_{1}}|+|(\widetilde{v}_{2})^{X_{2}}_{x}||\widetilde{v}-\widetilde{v}_{2}^{X_{2}}|+|(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{X_{1}}_{x}||(\widetilde{v}_{2})^{X_{2}}_{x}|\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Integrating the above estimate over
[0,t]
for any
t\leq T
, and using (
5.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|(u-\widetilde{u})_{x}|^{2}}{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|(u_{0}-\widetilde{u}(0,x))_{x}|^{2}}{2}\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\left(-\frac{1}{4}\mathbf{D}_{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}|\dot{X}_{i}|^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left(c_{2}D+C(\varepsilon_{1}+\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})(\mathcal{G}^{S}+D_{1})\right)ds+C\varepsilon_{1}\max(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})^{1/2}.

Multiplying the above inequality by the constant
\frac{1}{2\max(1,c_{2})}
and then adding the result (
5.1
), together with the smallness of
\varepsilon_{1},\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert v-\widetilde{v}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert u-\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\delta_{i}|\dot{X}_{i}|^{2}+\mathcal{G}^{S}+D+\mathcal{D}_{1}+\mathbf{D}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert v_{0}-\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot)\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert u_{0}-\widetilde{u}(0,\cdot)\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right)+C\delta_{0}^{1/2},

which is the desired estimate (
3.8
). This completes the proof of Proposition
3.2
.

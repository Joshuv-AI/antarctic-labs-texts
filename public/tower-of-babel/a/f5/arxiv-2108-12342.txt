A moving black hole in TEGR as a moving matter ball
E. D. Emtsova, A. N. Petrov

Abstract
Possibilities of the covariant with respect to both coordinate and local Lorentz transformations formalism developed earlier in the framework of Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR) are studied. The formalism is applied to a solution for a moving with constant velocity (with respect to distant static observers) Schwarzschild black hole. Coordinate and Lorentz invariant global conserved mass and momentum are constructed. The acceptable results are obtained in spite of the solution under consideration has no, at least, Killing vectors of space displacements. Calculations are quite analogous to calculating the mass and momentum of a moving matter ball in Minkowski space, and this analogy is used essentially.

A moving matter ball in Minkowski space

From the start, because the model of moving black hole is similar to moving spherically distributed matter in Minkowski space, it is instructive to consider such a matter in a flat spacetime with metric:

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}.

(8)

Here, we denote these coordinates as
(t,x,y,z)=(x^{0},x^{i})=(x^{\alpha})

\xi^{\alpha}=(-1,0,0,0)

\Theta^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}

\partial_{\alpha}\Theta^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}=0

{{\cal J}}{}^{\alpha}=\Theta^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\xi^{\beta}
is conserved,
\partial_{\alpha}{{\cal J}}{}^{\alpha}=0
, as well, and its components present the energy density
{{\cal J}}{}^{0}=\Theta^{0}{}_{0}\xi^{0}
and the momentum density
{{\cal J}}{}^{i}=\Theta^{i}{}_{0}\xi^{0}
measured by the introduced above observers. One recognizes that the definition of the current
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)
in (
4

5

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)
have the analogous interpretation for observers with proper vectors
\xi

Assuming the static spherically symmetric distribution of matter, one has for the current

{{\cal J}}{}^{\alpha}_{s}=\left[\rho(r),~0,~0,~0\right],

(9)

where
\rho(r)={{\cal J}}{}^{0}_{s}(r)=\Theta^{0}{}_{0}(r)\xi^{0}
is just the energy density,
r^{2}\equiv x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}

x=r\sin\theta\cos\phi;~~y=r\sin\theta\sin\phi;~~z=r\cos\phi.

(10)

\Sigma

To illustrate the below calculations related to a moving matter ball we consider a simpler case. Let the moving sphere be filled by

\{\overline{x}^{\alpha}\}

\gamma

\gamma m

\gamma vm

\gamma

4
Conclusion

Let us discuss the results. First, the transformation (
12
) applied to (
17
) presents the global Lorentz boost for distant observers. Thus, the results (
34
), presenting the global (at infinity) conserved quantities, can be interpreted as quite acceptable ones.

Second, the result (
30
) was obtained by choosing (
17
), or (
21
), with observers (
27
). The
same result could be obtained with the use of the Killing vector
\xi^{\alpha}=(-1,~0,~0,~0)
instead
of (
27
). However, following this logic, to calculate the momentum density, a space-like
displacement Killing vector would be needed, but it does not exist for the solution under
consideration. Here, following the analogies in Minkowski space we interpret components of
the conserved current for a static black hole as components of the energy-momentum vector
when energy density and momentum density are measured by local observers. Components
of the current for a moving black hole are obtained by related transformation (
12
) with the
following integration and final results (
34
).

Third, in
[
17
]
it was found that for the same solution, the “turning-off gravity” in different frames can lead to different ISCs, including those which give unacceptable results. Here
we have used the only gauge in which tetrad (
18

vanishing ISC, and this gauge leads to physically meaningful results (
30
) and (
34
). Nevertheless, the problem of determining different gauges for the solution (
31
) is interesting, and
its study is carried over to the future.

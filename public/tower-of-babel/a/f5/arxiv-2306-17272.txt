Recognizing 𝐖 𝟐 \mathbf{W_{2}} Graphs
Vadim E. Levit

Abstract
Let G G be a graph. A set S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) is independent if its elements are pairwise nonadjacent. A vertex v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) is shedding if for every independent set S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) ∖ N ⁡ [ v ] S\subseteq V(G)\setminus N[v] there exists u ∈ N ⁡ ( v ) u\in N(v) such that S ∪ { u } S\cup\{u\} is independent. An independent set S S is maximal if it is not contained in another independent set. An independent set S S is maximum if the size of every independent set of G G is not bigger than | S | |S| . The size of a maximum independent set of G G is denoted α ⁡ ( G ) \alpha(G) . A graph G G is well-covered if all its maximal independent sets are maximum, i.e. the size of every maximal independent set is α ⁡ ( G ) \alpha(G) . The graph G G belongs to class 𝐖 𝟐 \mathbf{W_{2}} if every two pairwise disjoint independent sets in G G are included in two pairwise disjoint maximum independent sets. If a graph belongs to the class 𝐖 𝟐 \mathbf{W_{2}} then it is well-covered. Finding a maximum independent set in an input graph is an NP-complete problem. Recognizing well-covered graphs is co-NP-complete. The complexity status of deciding whether an input graph belongs to the 𝐖 𝟐 \mathbf{W_{2}} class is not known. Even when the input is restricted to well-covered graphs, the complexity status of recognizing graphs in 𝐖 𝟐 \mathbf{W_{2}} is not known. In this article, we investigate the connection between shedding vertices and 𝐖 𝟐 \mathbf{W_{2}} graphs. On the one hand, we prove that recognizing shedding vertices is co-NP-complete. On the other hand, we find polynomial solutions for restricted cases of the problem. We also supply polynomial characterizations of several families of 𝐖 𝟐 \mathbf{W_{2}} graphs.

Theorem 5.8

Let
G
be a connected graph containing neither
C_{4}
nor
C_{5}
as a subgraph. The following conditions are equivalent.

1.

G\in\mathbf{W_{2}}

2.

G\approx K_{2}
or there exists a partition
\{T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{k}\}
of
V(G)
, such that

is a triangle with at least 2 simplicial vertices, for each

Proof.

1\implies 2
Suppose that
G\in\mathbf{W_{2}}
. By Theorem
1.7
,
V(G)=Shed(G)
. Therefore,
G
is isomorphic neither to
K_{1}
nor to
C_{7}
nor to
T_{10}
. By Theorem
5.7
,

. Hence, there exists
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)
, where for each
1\leq i\leq k
it holds that
x_{i}
is simplicial,
|N[x_{i}]|\leq 3
, and
\{N[x_{i}]:i=1,2,\ldots,k\}
is a partition of

.

If
|N[x_{i}]|=1
then
x_{i}
is an isolated vertex, which is a contradiction. If
|N[x_{i}]|=2
then
x_{i}
is a leaf, and therefore

. In the remaining case
|N[x_{i}]|=3
for every
1\leq i\leq k
. Let
y_{i}
and
z_{i}
be the neighbors of
x_{i}
, and assume on the contrary that both are not simplicial. There exists
y^{\prime}_{i}\in N(y_{i})\setminus\{x_{i},z_{i}\}
and
z^{\prime}_{i}\in N(z_{i})\setminus\{x_{i},y_{i}\}
. if
y^{\prime}_{i}=z^{\prime}_{i}
then
(y^{\prime}_{i},y_{i},x_{i},z_{i})
is a

, which is a contradiction. If

then
(z^{\prime}_{i},y^{\prime}_{i},y_{i},x_{i},z_{i})
is a

and

are distinct and non-adjacent.

Define
A=\{x_{i}\}
and

. Clearly,

and
B
are independent and disjoint. Therefore, they are contained in two disjoint maximal independent sets,

and
B^{*}
, respectively. However,

since

. Moreover,
y_{i}
and
z_{i}

. Therefore

and

Suppose that Condition 2 holds and

|S\cap T_{i}|\leq 1
for every independent set

and for every

. Moreover, every dominating independent set contains exactly

vertices. If

and

are disjoint independent sets of

and

such that

and

are disjoint maximal independent sets of

. Therefore,

.

if

can be partitioned into two subsets,

, such that the following holds.

•

contains the vertices adjacent to the pendant edges.

•

The pendant edges are a perfect matching of

.

•

contains the vertices of the basic 5-cycles.

•

The basic 5-cycles are a partition of
C
.

Theorem 6.1

]

2.

\mathbf{PC}

Theorem 6.2

The only graphs with girth 5 or more in the class
\mathbf{W_{2}}
are

and

Proof.
Let

be a graph with girth 5 or more in the class
\mathbf{W_{2}}
. Obviously,

is well-covered. By Theorem

. By Theorem

\mathbf{PC}
. The only graph in
\mathbf{W_{2}}
which contains leaves is

. Suppose that

does not contain leaves and belongs to
\mathbf{W_{2}}
. Then

.

Let

be a basic cycle in

, and assume on the contrary that
d(v_{1})\geq 3
. Then
d(v_{2})=d(v_{5})=2
. Let
v_{1}^{\prime}\in N(v_{1})\setminus\{v_{2},v_{5}\}
. Define

and

. Since

and

are disjoint and independent, they are contained in two disjoint maximal independent sets,

and

, of

and

Theorem 6.3

There exists an
O(n)
algorithm which solves the following problem.

G

v

Let

Case 2:

Case 3:

Theorem 7.1

The following problem can be solved in

time.

Input: A graph
G
without cycles of lengths 4 and 6, and a vertex

.

Question: Is

shedding ?

Proof.
Every vertex of

is adjacent to
exactly one vertex of

, or otherwise

contains a

.
Every component of
N_{2}(v)
contains at most

contains either a

or a

be the components of

The following algorithm decides whether

is shedding. It constructs a flow network
F_{v}=\{G_{F}=(V_{F},E_{F}),s\in V_{F},t\in V_{F},c:E_{F}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\}
. A similar construction was used in
[
10
]
.

Define

, where

are new vertices,

and

are the source and sink of
the network, respectively. Denote

.

The directed edges

are:

•

the directed edges from

.

•

all directed edges
v_{1}v_{2}

•

.

•

the directed edges

Let

. Invoke Ford and Fulkerson’s algorithm for finding a maximum flow

in the network. The flow in a vertex

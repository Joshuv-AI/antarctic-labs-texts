Remarks on undecidability of indecomposable polynomial rings
Marco Barone, Nicolás Caro-Montoya, Eudes Naziazeno

Abstract
. We prove that arithmetic is interpretable in any indecomposable polynomial ring (in any set of variables), and in addition we provide an alternative uniform proof of undecidability for all members in this class of rings.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this note is twofold: first, after some preliminary definitions and results (
Section

1.1
), we provide two interpretations of arithmetic in polynomial rings in one variable over a field.

The first interpretation (
Section

2.1
) works uniformly in the case of positive characteristic; in particular, it does not depend on such value of the characteristic. The bulk of required results are consequence of the far more general treatment from
[
BCN-main
]
; however, the construction in this particular case can be carried out in a direct and elementary way. Moreover, though this result is apparently well-known, we were not able to find a specific source containing a full proof of it, and so this presentation is desirable for future reference.

The second interpretation already works uniformly for polynomial rings (in any set of variables) over reduced indecomposable coefficient rings that are not fields. In order to extend its scope (
Section

2.2
), we prove a technical result for polynomial rings in one variable over a field having elements of infinite multiplicative order; in the course of the proof we characterize such fields. In this way, the second interpretation also works for the referred class of coefficient fields (
Section

2.2
). Since this subclass includes the characteristic zero case, the two constructed interpretations deal with the entire class of univariate polynomial rings over a field.

Our second aim (
Section

3
) is to extend, to the larger class of indecomposable polynomial rings, our previous interpretability/undecidability results appearing in
[
BCN-main
]
, valid only for
reduced
indecomposable polynomial rings. To this end, we use the fact that the nilradical
N_{S}
of a polynomial ring
S
is definable and that
S/N_{S}
is isomorphic to a reduced indecomposable polynomial ring whenever
S
is indecomposable.

{proposition}

Arithmetic is interpretable in the structures
(\mathbb{Z}^{+},+,\mid\,)
and
(\mathbb{Z}^{+},\leq,\mid\,)

Proof.

\bm{\forall}a\,\Lparen\,t\mid a\,\Rparen

]
*p. 146

\displaystyle\bullet\hskip 0.0pt

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle=k(k+1)

\displaystyle\iff\ j=\operatorname{lcm}(k,k+1)\,;

\displaystyle\bullet\hskip 0.0pt

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle=km

\displaystyle\iff\ (k+m)(k+m+1)=k(k+1)+m(m+1)+2j\,,

hence

\displaystyle\bullet\hskip 0.0pt

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle=km

\displaystyle\iff\ \operatorname{lcm}(k+m,k+m+1)=\operatorname{lcm}(k,k+1)+\operatorname{lcm}(m,m+1)+2j\,,

One might expect that, given the infinitary definition of the nilradical, there would be plenty of examples of rings lacking a definable nilradical. Surprisingly, this is not the case, and the construction of the cited textbook counterexample seems to involve some form of choice, so perhaps it cannot be carried out directly in
ZF
.

Thus, if
\mathcal{H}
is the class of indecomposable polynomial rings, then there is a formula

{lemma}

Let

Proof.

Immediate from the equality

{remark}

The set
J_{S}=\{f\in S\colon 1+fg\in S^{*}\ \textnormal{for all}\ g\in S\}
appearing in the statement of
\thepropositionc
is known, in the literature, as the
Jacobson
radical
(of the ring
S
), and it is equal to the intersection of all the maximal ideals in
S
, provided every nonunit in
S
belongs to at least one such ideal. As the reader may check, our proof of
\thepropositionc
does not rely at all on such characterization, and in particular we do not need to resort to these ideals (whose existence, in arbitrary nonzero commutative unital rings, is known to be equivalent to the axiom of choice
[
Hod79
]
). Similarly we do not use, in any part of our work, the characterization of the nilradical of a ring as the intersection of its prime ideals, which is known to be equivalent to
BPI
, the Boolean prime ideal theorem (this result is independent of
ZF
; see
[
HR98
]
*Forms 14 AL and 14 AN).

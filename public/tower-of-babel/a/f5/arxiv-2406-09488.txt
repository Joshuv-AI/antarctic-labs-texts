Abstract
. This note explores the theoretical justification for some approximations of arithmetic forwards ( F a F_{a} ) with weighted averages of overnight (ON) forwards ( F k F_{k} ). The central equation presented in this analysis is: F a ​ ( 0 , T s , T e ) = 1 τ ⁡ ( T s , T e ) ​ ∑ k = 1 K τ k ​ 𝒜 k ​ F k , F_{a}(0;T_{s},T_{e})=\frac{1}{\tau(T_{s},T_{e})}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tau_{k}\mathcal{A}_{k}F_{k}\,, with 𝒜 k \mathcal{A}_{k} being explicit model-dependent quantities, numerically stable and close to one under certain market scenarios. We will present computationally cheaper methods that approximate F a F_{a} , i.e., we will define some { 𝒜 ~ k } k = 1 K \{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K} such that F a ​ ( 0 , T s , T e ) ≈ 1 τ ⁡ ( T s , T e ) ​ ∑ k = 1 K τ k ​ 𝒜 ~ k ​ F k , F_{a}(0;T_{s},T_{e})\approx\frac{1}{\tau(T_{s},T_{e})}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tau_{k}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{k}F_{k}\,, thereby gaining some intuition about the arithmetic factors 𝒜 k \mathcal{A}_{k} . Additionally, theoretical bounds and closed-form expressions for the arithmetic factors 𝒜 k \mathcal{A}_{k} in the context of Gaussian HJM models are explored. Finally, we demonstrate that one of these forms can be closely aligned with an approximation suggested by Katsumi Takada in his work on the valuation of arithmetic averages of Fed Funds rates.

1.
Introduction

In the realm of financial markets, various interest rate products are actively traded, such as interest rate swaps, basis swaps, and cross-currency swaps. Among these, the valuation of forward rates and their respective structures play a crucial role in pricing and risk management. One notable aspect of this valuation process involves the arithmetic average of overnight (ON) forward rates and its approximation.

Arithmetic averages of ON rates are particularly relevant in contexts where the Fed Funds (FF) rate is used, as seen in some swaps, liquid or OTC, with a floating leg based on FF or in Fed Fund Futures. While ON rates are often compounded daily in financial instruments like overnight index swaps, the arithmetic average of these rates offers a different perspective that requires careful consideration and adjustment for accurate valuation. This note aims to demonstrate the conditions under which arithmetic forwards can be closely approximated by a weighted average of the ON forwards. In particular, this aligns with an approximation given by some platforms, such as Murex, which provides the expression:

F_{a}^{\text{Murex}}(0;T_{s},T_{e})\coloneqq\frac{1}{\tau(T_{s},T_{e})}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tau_{k}F_{k}\,.

Based on exact theoretical expressions for the arithmetic factors, we will provide a theoretical and numerical discussion under which market scenarios this approximation can be considered accurate and consider alternative and more accurate approximations. Additionally, analytical bounds and closed-form expressions for the arithmetic factors
\mathcal{A}_{k}
in the context of Gaussian HJM models are explored. We also demonstrate that one of these forms can be closely aligned with an approximation suggested by Katsumi Takada in his work on the valuation of arithmetic averages of Fed Funds rates. See also
[
Sko24
]
for a new approach in computing the convexity adjustment particularized for the SABR model.

V(t,T)=\tilde{V}(T-t)
with
\tilde{V}(0)=0
. By Taylor’s Theorem,
\tilde{V}(T)=\tilde{V}(t)+O(T-t)
with the constant depending only on the model parameters. Therefore, we can arrive at:

\displaystyle\frac{P(T_{k},T_{e})\cdot P(T_{k})}{P(T_{e})}

\displaystyle=1+O(T_{e}-T_{k})-(x_{T_{k}}+y_{T_{k}})(T_{e}-T_{k})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(a\cdot x_{T_{k}}^{2}+b\cdot y_{T_{k}}^{2})(T_{e}-T_{k})^{2}+o_{x,y}\left((T_{e}-T_{k})^{2}\right)\,,

where the little-o term depends on the stochastic factors at time
T_{k}
, not just the model parameters.
Thus, heuristically, given this expression, (
7
), and the fact that if
T_{K}=T_{e}
,
\mathcal{A}_{k}=1
, we can propose the simple linear approximation:

\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\text{lin}}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{1}+(T_{k}-T_{1})\frac{1-\mathcal{A}_{1}}{T_{e}-T_{1}}\,.

The obvious advantage of this formulation is that, while taking into account the “convexity” adjustment of arithmetic forwards, we just need to compute the expectation in (
4
), not for every
k
, reducing the computational cost or increasing the analytical tractability. From the expression above, we can see that the quadratic term of the stochastic factors and, in general, the convexity of the bond price function, will generally make this approximation an upper bound, cf. Proposition
5.1
. Obviously, we can refine this approximation by taking a midpoint and performing a piecewise linear interpolation, so we can account for the curvature of the arithmetic factors with respect to the tenor, see Figure
1
. That is,

\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\text{pw}}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}_{1}+(T_{k}-T_{1})\frac{\mathcal{A}_{m}-\mathcal{A}_{1}}{T_{m}-T_{1}},&\text{for }k\leq m\,,\\
\mathcal{A}_{m}+(T_{k}-T_{m})\frac{1-\mathcal{A}_{m}}{T_{e}-T_{m}},&\text{for }k>m\,,\end{cases}

where
T_{m}
is the time at the midpoint.

5.
Theoretical bounds and closed formula for the Gaussian HJM model

As we can see from the plots, it seems to be the case that
\mathcal{A}_{1}\leq 1
. Let us prove this for one-factor model (that is, Hull-White or Hagan’s LGM model).

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume the short rate follows (
12
) with
n=1
, then

\mathcal{A}_{k}\leq 1\quad\text{for all }k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\,.

Proof.

To simplify the proof, let us work in the HJM framework
7
7

7

We will explore more on this, although using a slightly different approach, below.
, following Andersen and Piterbarg’s book. It is easier if we work with the expression,

\mathcal{A}_{k}=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}\left(R_{k}\right)}{\mathbb{E}^{T_{k}}\left(R_{k}\right)}\,.

By
[
AP10a
, Remark 10.1.8]
, the discount bond dynamics for
P(t,T)
are given by

dP(t,T)/P(t,T)=r(t)dt-\sigma_{P}(t,T)dW(t),\quad\sigma_{P}(t,T)=\sigma_{r}(t)G(t,T)\,,

with
G(t,T)\coloneqq\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\int_{t}^{u}\varkappa(s)ds}du
,
\sigma_{r}
the short rate volatility (now a deterministic function), and
\varkappa
the mean reversion speed. That is, if
x(t)\coloneqq r(t)-f(0,t)
by
[
AP10a
, Proposition 10.1.7]
,

dx(t)=(y(t)-\varkappa(t)r(t))dt+\sigma_{r}(t)dW(t)\,.

Thus, as the SDE with deterministic functions as coefficients,

dx(t)=(A(t)x(t)+\tilde{B}(t))dt+C(t)dW(t)\,,

admits the unique solution
[
Arn74
, (8.2.5) Corollary]
given by the fundamental “matrix”,

\Phi(t)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}A(s){d}s\right)\,,

i.e.,

x(t)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}A(s){d}s\right)\left(x(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{s}A(u){d}u\right)\left(\tilde{B}(s){d}s+C(s){d}W_{s}\right)\right).

we can solve our equation. Using
[
AP10
, Equation (4.34)]
, Girsanov’s Theorem reads as

dW^{T}(t)=dW(t)+\sigma_{P}(t,T)dt\,,

where
W^{T}
is a
Q^{T}
-Brownian motion. This introduces a new term in the drift, in particular in
\tilde{B}
, of the form
-\sigma_{r}^{2}(t)G(t,T)
. For
T^{\prime}\geq T
,
\sigma_{r}^{2}(t)G(t,T^{\prime})\geq\sigma_{r}^{2}(t)G(t,T)
. Thus, if
Z\mid_{Q}
denotes the distribution of any random variable
Z
under the measure
Q
, and
X^{T^{\prime}}_{t}\sim x(t)\mid_{Q^{T^{\prime}}}
, then

X^{T}_{t}:=X^{T^{\prime}}_{t}+\beta(t)\sim x(t)\mid_{Q^{T}}\,,

where

\beta(t)\coloneqq\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}-\kappa(s){d}s\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{s}\kappa(u){d}u\right)\left(\sigma_{r}^{2}(s)G(s,T^{\prime})-\sigma_{r}^{2}(s)G(s,T)\right){d}s\right)\geq 0

is a deterministic function. As
[
AP10a
, Proposition 10.1.7]
,

P(t,T,x(t))=\frac{P(0,T)}{P(0,t)}\exp\left(-x(t)G(t,T)-\frac{1}{2}y(t)G(t,T)^{2}\right)\,,

thus
8
8

8

Using the aforementioned law of the unconscious statistician.
,

\mathbb{E}^{T_{k}}\left(\frac{1}{P(T_{k-1},T_{k},x(T_{k-1}))}\right)=\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}\left(\frac{1}{P(T_{k-1},T_{k},x(T_{k-1})+\beta(T_{k}))}\right)\geq\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}\left(\frac{1}{P(T_{k-1},T_{k},x(T_{k-1}))}\right)\,,

as
G(T_{k-1},T_{k})>0.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

It is reasonable to assume that we are going to have
A_{k}\leq 1
, but not necessarily for
\mathcal{A}_{k}
. Indeed, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left(e^{-\int_{0}^{T_{k}}r(s)\,ds}R_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=P(0,T_{k})\cdot\mathbb{E}^{T_{k}}(R_{k})=P(0,T_{k})\cdot F_{k}\,.

By the law of iterated expectations,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left(e^{-\int_{0}^{T_{e}}r(s)\,ds}R_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left(e^{-\int_{0}^{T_{k}}r(s)\,ds}\,P(T_{k},T_{e})R_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left(e^{-\int_{0}^{T_{k}}r(s)\,ds}R_{k}\right)=P(0,T_{k})\cdot F_{k}\,,

where we have assumed that
P(T_{k},T_{e})\leq 1
and
R_{k}\geq 0
almost surely, a sufficient condition being
r(t)
non-negative. Note that for the equality to hold, it is sufficient that the measure of the subset where
P(T_{k},T_{e})R_{k}>R_{k}
is small enough
9
9

9

Indeed, let
\Omega_{0}
be the subset where
P(T_{k},T_{e})R_{k}<R_{k}
and
\Omega_{0}^{c}
the subset where
P(T_{k},T_{e})R_{k}\geq R_{k}
. Then,

\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left(e^{-\int_{0}^{T_{k}}r(s)\,ds}\,(P(T_{k},T_{e})-1)R_{k}\right)=\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\Omega_{0}}e^{-\int_{0}^{T_{k}}r(s)\,ds}\,(P(T_{k},T_{e})-1)R_{k}\right)+\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\Omega_{0}^{c}}e^{-\int_{0}^{T_{k}}r(s)\,ds}\,(P(T_{k},T_{e})-1)R_{k}\right)\,,

but if
H_{k}\coloneqq\exp\left({-\int_{0}^{T_{k}}r}\right)\left(P(T_{k},T_{e})-1\right)R_{k}
is in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for some
p>1
, then the integral satisfies

\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\Omega_{0}^{c}}H_{k}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left[H_{k}^{p}\right]^{1/p}Q(\Omega_{0}^{c})^{1/q}\to 0\text{ as }\,Q(\Omega_{0}^{c})\to 0\,.

where
1/p+1/q=1
and we have used Hölder’s inequality. Alternatively, the Dominated Convergence Theorem can be used to show that the limit goes to zero in the case of
p=1
taking a decreasing sequence of
\Omega_{0}^{c}
. Note that the fact of the measure being small enough depends on the equivalent measure used.
, depending on the values of
R_{k}
and the discount on that region. Therefore,
A_{k}\leq 1
. However, the sign of
\mathcal{A}_{k}-1
is undetermined a priori since

\mathcal{A}_{k}=A_{k}\cdot\frac{P(0,T_{k})}{P(0,T_{e})}\,\text{ with, generally, }\frac{P(0,T_{k})}{P(0,T_{e})}\geq 1\,.

∎

Gaussian HJM models

Following
[
AP10a
, Chapter 12]
(see also Chapter 22 of
[
Bjo20
]
or Appendix A of
[
Hen14
]
), let us consider a general

-factor Gaussian model that can be written as
[
AP10
, (4.31)]
,

(18)

\frac{dP(t,T)}{P(t,T)}=r(t)dt-\sigma_{P}(t,T)^{\top}d\tilde{W}(t),

where
\sigma_{P}(t,T)
is a bounded
n
-dimensional function of time, and
\tilde{W}(t)
a
n
-dimensional (uncorrelated) Brownian motion in the risk-neutral measure
{Q}
. Then, the instantaneous forward rates,
f(t,T)=:f^{T}(t)
, are given by
[
AP10
, (4.33), the HJM condition]
,

df(t,T):=df^{T}(t)=\sigma_{f}(t,T)^{\top}\sigma_{P}(t,T)dt+\sigma_{f}(t,T)^{\top}d\tilde{W}(t)

(19)

=\sigma_{f}(t,T)^{\top}\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{f}(t,u)du\,dt+\sigma_{f}(t,T)^{\top}d\tilde{W}(t).

This model is generally not Markovian
[
AP10
, p.184]
, so we impose the following condition.

Assumption 1

(Separability condition)
.

Assume that
\sigma_{f}(t,T)
is
separable
, i.e.,

(20)

\sigma_{f}(t,T)=g(t)h(T),

where
g
is a
n\times n
deterministic matrix-valued function, and
h
is a
n
-dimensional deterministic vector.

The full theory for these models is developed in Chapter 12 of
[
AP10a
]
, but from here we can compute explicitly
\mathcal{A}_{k}
. See also
[
Hen14
, Theorem 6.4]
for a different proof of this result

Some minor typos are present in that formula.
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume (
18
) holds. Then,

\mathcal{A}_{k}=\gamma_{k}+\frac{\gamma_{k}-1}{\tau_{k}F_{k}}\,,

where the convexity adjustment for deferred payment is given by

\gamma_{k}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}(\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k-1})-\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k}))^{\top}\cdot(\sigma_{P}(s,T_{e})-\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k}))\,ds\right)\,.

Note that if
T_{e}=T_{k}
, i.e.,
k=K
, then
\gamma_{K}=1
and
\mathcal{A}_{K}=1
, as expected.

Proof.

For the sake of simplicity in our computations, let us define:

\sigma_{k}\coloneqq\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k})^{\top}\,,\quad\sigma_{e}\coloneqq\sigma_{P}(s,T_{e})^{\top}\,,\quad\sigma_{k}^{2}\coloneqq\sigma_{k}\cdot\sigma_{k}^{\top}\,.

It is standard that, see for instance Appendix A of
[
Hen14
]
,

P(T_{k-1},T_{k})=P(0,T_{k-1},T_{k})\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}(\sigma_{k}-\sigma_{k-1})\,dW_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}(\sigma_{k}^{2}-\sigma_{k-1}^{2})\,ds\right)\,,

being
P^{M}(0;T_{k-1},T_{k})
the forward bond price at time
0
, so obtained from market prices, for the period between
T_{k-1}
and
T_{k}
. Now, considering the conditional expectation under the measure
\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}({R_{k}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}\left(\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\left(\frac{1}{P(T_{k-1},T_{k})}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\cdot\frac{1}{P^{M}(0,T_{k-1},T_{k})}\,\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}\left(\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}(\sigma_{k}-\sigma_{k-1})\,dW_{s}^{T_{e}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\times\left.\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}(\sigma_{k}^{2}-\sigma_{k-1}^{2})\,ds+\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}(\sigma_{k}-\sigma_{k-1})\sigma_{e}^{\top}\,ds\right)\right)-\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}.

where we have used the following Girsanov’s transformation, see
[
AP10
, (4.34)]
,

(21)

dW_{s}=dW^{T_{e}}_{s}+\sigma_{e}\,ds\,.

As
\sigma
is deterministic, from the moment generating function of a normal variable and Itô’s isometry, we obtain

\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}\left(\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}(\sigma_{k}-\sigma_{k-1})\,dW_{s}^{T_{e}}\right)\right)=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}(\sigma_{k}-\sigma_{k-1})^{2}\,ds\right)\,.

Therefore:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}(R_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\cdot\frac{1}{P^{M}(0,T_{k-1},T_{k})}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}\sigma_{k}^{2}-\sigma_{k-1}\cdot\sigma_{k}^{T}-(\sigma_{k}-\sigma_{k-1})\sigma_{e}^{\top}\,ds\right)-\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\cdot\frac{1}{P^{M}(0,T_{k-1},T_{k})}\gamma_{k}-\frac{1}{\tau_{k}},

where the convexity adjustment for deferred payment is given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}(\sigma_{k}-\sigma_{k-1})(\sigma_{e}-\sigma_{k})^{\top}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}(\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k})-\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k-1}))^{\top}\cdot(\sigma_{P}(s,T_{e})-\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k}))\,ds\right)\,.

Finally, we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}(R_{k})}{\mathbb{E}^{T_{k}}(R_{k})}=\left(\frac{F_{k}+1/\tau_{k}}{F_{k}}\right)\gamma_{k}-\frac{1}{\tau_{k}F_{k}}=\gamma_{k}+\frac{\gamma_{k}-1}{\tau_{k}F_{k}}\,.

∎

Remark 5.4
.

Let us explore the sign of
\mathcal{A}_{k}-1
; see Proposition
5.1
. It is useful to work with the LGM (Linear Gaussian Markov) with
n
factors; see
[
Rom24
]
for details. That is,

H(T)=\int_{0}^{T}h(s)^{\top}ds,\quad g(t)=C^{\top}A(t),

where
\rho=CC^{\top}
is the correlation matrix of
W
, and
A_{ij}(t)=\alpha_{i}(t)\delta_{ij}
. Here,
dZ_{i}(t)=\alpha_{i}(t)\,dW_{i}^{N}(t)
is the
i
-th factor in the measure
Q^{N}
; see the aforementioned reference for more details. Then, the convexity adjustment reads as

(22)

\log\gamma_{k}=-\left(H(T_{k})-H(T_{k-1})\right)\zeta(T_{k-1})\left(H(T_{e})-H(T_{k})\right)^{\top},

with
\zeta(T_{k})
being the covariance matrix of the factors at time
T_{k}
. It is standard to assume that the components of
h(s)
are non-negative and non-increasing, so we have a mean-reverting short rate rather than a mean-fleeing one; see Equation (12.8) in
[
AP10a
]
. Therefore, the components of
H(\cdot)
are non-decreasing since its derivative is non-negative.
In the case of
n=1
, this is enough to ensure that
\gamma_{k}\leq 1
, but this is not sufficient when
n>1
, as one can easily construct two componentwise positive vectors which, when multiplied through a positive definite matrix, return a negative value.
∎

5.1.1.
Example: the Hull-White model with constant coefficients

Given the Hull-White model with volatility function, see Remark 10.1.8 of
[
AP10a
]
,
\sigma_{P}(s,t)=\frac{\eta}{a}\left(1-e^{-a(t-s)}\right)
, where both
\eta
and
a
are constants, we can develop the particular expressions for this case.
First, recall the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{k}
:

\mathcal{A}_{k}=\gamma_{k}+\frac{\gamma_{k}-1}{\tau_{k}F_{k}}\,,

where the convexity adjustment
\gamma_{k}
for deferred payment is given by

\gamma_{k}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T_{k-1}}\left[\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k-1})-\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k})\right]\left[\sigma_{P}(s,T_{e})-\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k})\right]\,ds\right)\,.

Substituting the given
\sigma_{P}(s,t)
into the expression for
\gamma_{k}
, we compute the integrand:

\displaystyle\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k-1})-\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta}{a}\left(e^{-a(T_{k}-s)}-e^{-a(T_{k-1}-s)}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{P}(s,T_{e})-\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta}{a}\left(e^{-a(T_{k}-s)}-e^{-a(T_{e}-s)}\right)\,.

The product of these differences is:

\displaystyle\left[\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k-1})-\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k})\right]

\displaystyle\left[\sigma_{P}(s,T_{e})-\sigma_{P}(s,T_{k})\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\eta}{a}\right)^{2}\left(e^{-a(T_{k}-s)}-e^{-a(T_{k-1}-s)}\right)\left(e^{-a(T_{k}-s)}-e^{-a(T_{e}-s)}\right)\,.

Expanding the product:

\displaystyle\left(e^{-a(T_{k}-s)}-e^{-a(T_{k-1}-s)}\right)\left(e^{-a(T_{k}-s)}-e^{-a(T_{e}-s)}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-a(T_{k}+T_{k}-2s)}-e^{-a(T_{k}+T_{e}-2s)}-e^{-a(T_{k-1}+T_{k}-s)}+e^{-a(T_{k-1}+T_{e}-2s)}\,.

Integrate this expression with respect to
s
from
0
to
T_{e}
:

6.
Connection with Takada’s approximation

In this section, we explore the relationship between the approximation developed in the previous sections and an approximation suggested by Katsumi Takada in
[
Tak11
]
. We start with the following trivial expression:

(23)

\sum\tau_{k}\cdot F_{k}=\log\left(e^{\sum\tau_{k}F_{k}}\right)\,.

Given that
e^{x}=1+x+o(x)
for small
x
. In this case,
o(x)>0
for positive values of
x
. Therefore, using the definition of little-o,

e^{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tau_{k}F_{k}}=\prod_{k=1}^{K}e^{\tau_{k}F_{k}}=\prod_{k=1}^{K}\left(1+\tau_{k}F_{k}+o\left(\tau_{k}F_{k}\right)\right)=\prod_{k=1}^{K}\left(1+\tau_{k}F_{k}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{K}o\left(\mu^{k}\right)\,,

being
\mu\coloneqq\max_{k}{|\tau_{k}F_{k}|}
. Thus,

e^{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tau_{k}F_{k}}=\prod_{k=1}^{K}\left(1+\tau_{k}F_{k}\right)+o\left(\mu\right)\,,

being the error term strictly positive. Expanding the leading term, this is equal to:

\displaystyle\log\left(\prod_{k=1}^{K}(1+\tau_{k}F_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\prod_{k=1}^{K}\frac{P(T_{k-1})}{P(T_{k})}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{P(T_{s})}{P(T_{e})}\right)\equiv\log\left(1+\tau(T_{s},T_{e})F_{g}(0;T_{s},T_{e})\right)\,,

where the last equality follows by the definition of continuously compounded or geometric forwards and a telescopic cancellation for the product. Therefore, we obtain, cf. (7) and (8) of
[
Tak11
]
or (145) of
[
AB13
]
,

(24)

\sum\tau_{k}\cdot F_{k}\approx\log\left(1+\tau(T_{s},T_{e})F_{g}(0;T_{s},T_{e})\right)\eqqcolon\tau(T_{s},T_{e})\cdot O^{\text{det}}_{a}(0;T_{s},T_{e})\,,

following (13) of
[
Tak11
]
, a deterministic version of Takada’s forward definition, which he denotes by
O_{a}(0;T_{s},T_{e})
. That is,

{O}_{a}(0;T_{s},T_{e})\coloneqq\frac{1}{\tau(T_{s},T_{e})}\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}\left(\int_{T_{s}}^{T_{e}}r\right)\,

and for deterministic rates
O^{\text{det}}_{a}(0;T_{s},T_{e})=O_{a}(0;T_{s},T_{e})
.
Thus, if
\mathcal{A}_{k}\approx 1
, the forward rate approximation
F_{a}(0,T_{s},T_{e})
is given by:

(25)

F_{a}(0,T_{s},T_{e})\approx O^{\text{det}}_{a}(0;T_{s},T_{e})>O_{a}(0,T_{s},T_{e})\,,

by (15) of
[
Tak11
]
.

Remark 6.1
.

Note that (15) of
[
Tak11
]
or the last inequality of (
25
) is proven using a no-arbitrage argument. Nevertheless, a “pure ” mathematical argument can be given too. Indeed, using Jensen’s inequality, assuming that the integral is strictly positive,

\displaystyle\delta(T_{s},T_{e}){O}_{a}(0;T_{s},T_{e})=\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}\left(\int_{T_{s}}^{T_{e}}r\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Jensen's ineq.}}{<}\log\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}\left(e^{\int_{T_{s}}^{T_{e}}r}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{1}{P(T_{e})}\cdot\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left(e^{-\int_{0}^{T_{s}}r}\right)\right)\,,

where we have used that for any derivative with value
V
, the change of numeraire formula reads as,

P(t,T_{e})\mathbb{E}^{T_{e}}\left({V_{T_{e}}}\right)=V_{t}=B(t)\cdot\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left(e^{-\int_{0}^{T_{e}}r}{V_{T_{e}}}\right)\,,

Thus, we conclude the proof noting that
\mathbb{E}^{Q}\left(e^{-\int_{0}^{T_{s}}r}\right)=P(T_{s})
.

1.
Introduction

Morrison’s cone conjecture is stated as follows:

Conjecture 1.1
.

(The Morrison cone conjecture)
Let
X
be a Calabi-Yau 3-fold. Then,

(1)

The automorphism group of
X
acts on the nef cone of
X
with a rational polyhedral fundamental domain; and

(2)

The pseudoautomorphism group of
X
(see Definition
1.7
below) acts on the movable cone of
X
with a rational polyhedral fundamental domain.

In particular, Morrison’s conjecture implies the following:

(1)

The automorphism group of
X
acts on the faces of the nef cone of
X
with finitely many orbits; and

(2)

The pseudoautomorphism group of
X
acts on the faces of the movable cone of
X
with finitely many orbits.

The Morrison cone conjecture was generalized by Totaro to klt pairs (see
[
T10
]
). In this paper, we prove a version of the cone conjecture for certain types of log Calabi-Yau 3-folds, namely, for smooth projective 3-folds
Y
that admit a
K
3 fibration
f:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
-K_{Y}=f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Remark 1.2
.

If
f:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a K3 fibration such that
K_{Y}
is relatively nef,
-K_{Y}
is effective and nonzero,
f
is not isotrivial, and
f
has no multiple fibers, then
-K_{Y}=f^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)
. This follows from positivity of the pushforward of the relative canonical bundle, see e.g.
[
F78
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

If
D
is any smooth (or reduced normal crossing) fiber of
f
, then
(Y,D)
is log Calabi-Yau.

Definition 1.4
.

The
cone of curves

\operatorname{\text{$\overline{\mbox{Curv}\raisebox{8.53581pt}{}}$}}(Y)
is defined as:

\operatorname{\text{$\overline{\mbox{Curv}\raisebox{8.53581pt}{}}$}}(Y)=\mathop{\overline{\{\sum a_{i}[C_{i}]\;|\;a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\text{ and }C_{i}\subset Y\text{ is a curve }\}}}
.

Definition 1.5
.

The
nef cone

\operatorname{Nef}(Y)
is the dual cone of the cone of curves. The
nef effective cone
is defined as
\operatorname{\operatorname{Nef}^{\;e}}(Y):=\operatorname{Nef}(Y)\cap\operatorname{Eff}(Y)
.

Definition 1.6
.

The
movable cone
is defined as:

\operatorname{Mov}(Y):=\mathop{\overline{\langle B\;|\;B\text{ is a divisor with }|B|\text{ having no fixed part}\rangle}}_{\mathbb{R}\geq 0}
.

The
movable effective cone
is defined as
\operatorname{\operatorname{Mov}^{\;e}}(Y):=\operatorname{Mov}(Y)\cap\operatorname{Eff}(Y)
.

Definition 1.7
.

A pseudoautomorphism of a variety
Y
is a birational map
\theta:Y\dashrightarrow Y
that is an isomorphism outside of codimension 2 subsets of the domain and codomain.

Definition 1.8
.

A
K
3 fibration is a morphism
f:Y\rightarrow B
such that the general fiber
F\subset Y
of
f
is a
K
3 surface.

Here we briefly lay out some key results used in this paper. First of all, we note that our setup is a special case of the Kawamata-Morrison-Totaro cone conjecture (see Theorem
2.1
). This allows us to conclude that
\operatorname{Mov}(Y)
is the union of the nef cones of all flops of
Y
(see Theorem
2.2
). Two important results that we will use next are Mori’s cone theorem (
[
KM98
]
, Theorem 1.24) and Mori’s classification of extremal rays of the cone of curves for smooth 3-folds (
[
M82
]
). Given a smooth projective variety
Y
, Mori’s cone theorem describes the cone of curves of
Y
in the region
K_{Y}<0
. Mori gives a classification of the contractions
\mathrm{cont}_{R}:Y\rightarrow X
associated to extremal rays
R\subset\operatorname{\text{$\overline{\mbox{Curv}\raisebox{8.53581pt}{}}$}}(Y)
in the region
K_{Y}<0
when
Y
is a smooth projective 3-fold over
\mathbb{C}
. There are a total of eight possibilities (see
[
M82
]
or
[
KM98
]
).

Now suppose that
Y
is a smooth projective 3-fold and
f:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a
K
3 fibration such that
-K_{Y}=f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(1)
. In this setting, we show (Proposition
2.5
) that for any
Z
obtained by a sequence of flops of
Y
, six of the eight possible types of extremal rays of
\operatorname{\text{$\overline{\mbox{Curv}\raisebox{8.53581pt}{}}$}}(Z)
in the region
K_{Z}<0
can be eliminated, leaving two remaining types:

(1)

Type (1): the blowup of a smooth curve
\Gamma
; or

(2)

Type (6): a conic bundle.

We prove the following main results.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective 3-fold and
f:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
a K3 fibration such that
-K_{Y}=f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(1)
. Then the pseudoautomorphism group
\operatorname{PsAut}(Y)
of
Y
acts on

(1)

the Type (6) faces of
\operatorname{\operatorname{Mov}^{\;e}}(Y)
with finitely many orbits;

(2)

the Type (1) faces of
\operatorname{\operatorname{Mov}^{\;e}}(Y)
with finitely many orbits if
H^{3}(Y,\mathbb{C})=0
.

Remark 1.10
.

If
H^{3}(Y,\mathbb{C})=0
, then any contraction of Type (1) has genus
g=0
.

By a result of Kawamata, we can also take care of all curves on

:

Theorem 1.11
.

(Kawamata
[
K97
]
)

\operatorname{PsAut}(Y)
acts on the faces of
\operatorname{\operatorname{Mov}^{\;e}}(Y)
containing
-K_{Y}
with finitely many orbits.

Remark 1.12
.

Because
-K_{Y}
(a fiber) is nef, there are no curves in the region
K>0
.

Theorem 1.13
.

\operatorname{PsAut}(Y)
acts on the codimension 1 faces of
\operatorname{\operatorname{Mov}^{\;e}}(Y)
with finitely many orbits if
H^{3}(Y,\mathbb{C})=0
.

Conjecture 1.14
.

\operatorname{PsAut}(Y)
acts with finitely many orbits on the faces of
\operatorname{\operatorname{Mov}^{\;e}}(Y)
of Type (1) for any genus
g\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.15
.

Assume that Conjecture
1.14
holds. Then
\operatorname{PsAut}(Y)
acts with finitely many orbits on the codimension 1 faces of
\operatorname{\operatorname{Mov}^{\;e}}(Y)
.

In Section

admitting a

such that

Proof of main theorem

Here we prove Theorem
1.9
. Let
Y
be a smooth projective 3-fold admitting a
K

-K_{Y}=f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(1)

where

is klt log Calabi-Yau, since the coefficients of

[

confirms the special case of the Kawamata-Morrison-Totaro cone conjecture.

Theorem 2.1
.

(The Kawamata-Morrison-Totaro cone conjecture,
[

]
, Conjecture 2.1). Let
(Y,\Delta)

(1)

\operatorname{\text{Aut}}(Y,\Delta)

\operatorname{\operatorname{Nef}^{\;e}}(Y)
with a rational polyhedral fundamental domain; and

(2)

\operatorname{PsAut}(Y,\Delta)
acts on
\operatorname{\operatorname{Mov}^{\;e}}(Y)
with a rational polyhedral fundamental domain.

Because we are in the klt log Calabi-Yau setting, we may use a result implied by Birkar-Cascini-Hacon-McKernan
[
BCHM10
]
, stated as Theorem

as the union of the nef cones of
Z

Y\dashrightarrow Z
an SQM (small

-factorial modification).

Theorem 2.2
.

(cf.
[
BCHM10
]
, Corollary 1.1.5)

Let
(Y,\Delta)
be klt where
\Delta
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on

and
K_{Y}+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
. Then we have a decomposition

\operatorname{\operatorname{Mov}^{\;e}}(Y)=\bigcup_{Y\dashrightarrow Z}\operatorname{\operatorname{Nef}^{\;e}}(Z)

that is locally rational polyhedral in the interior of

, where the union is taken over SQMs

.

In our setting, the only type of SQM that appears is a flop (or a composition of finitely many flops). The reason is because
-K_{Y}=f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(1)
, a fiber, and thus
-K_{Y}
is nef, that is,
-K_{Y}\cdot C\geq 0
for any curve

. If

is an exceptional curve on an SQM, then

because a smooth 3-fold has no flips (

). So

[

]
).

Let

denote the following set:

S:=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{Y\dashrightarrow Z}}\{\text{codimension 1 faces of }\operatorname{Nef}(Z)\text{ in the boundary of }\operatorname{Mov}(Y)\}
,

where the union is taken over all flops

. There is a surjection from

to the collection of all codimension 1 faces of

. Moreover, the elements of

\displaystyle{\bigcup_{Y\dashrightarrow Z}}\{\text{extremal rays of }\operatorname{\text{$\overline{\mbox{Curv}\raisebox{8.53581pt}{}}$}}(Z)=\operatorname{Nef}(Z)^{\ast}\text{ not corresponding to small contractions}\}

(1)

Mori’s cone theorem (

]

Remark 2.3

Because

Proposition 2.5
.

Of the eight possible types of extremal rays of
\operatorname{\text{$\overline{\mbox{Curv}\raisebox{8.53581pt}{}}$}}(Y)

The main idea of the proof of the Theorem

is as follows. We start with a smooth projective 3-fold

such that

are related by a flop (or by a composition of flops). First, we show that the

that are of Type (1) or Type (6) (this is Proposition

\operatorname{\text{$\overline{\mbox{Curv}\raisebox{8.53581pt}{}}$}}(Z)

Proof.

(Proof of Proposition
2.5
). Let
f:Y\rightarrow X
be the contraction of a divisor

to a point

(so this covers cases (2), (3), (4), and (5)). If we have any curve

, then by definition of the contraction

of an extremal ray

(8) We cannot have

, a point, and

is not ample. This leaves (1) and (6) as the only two possibilities, completing the proof.

Proof.

(Proof of (2) of Theorem

). Suppose that the contraction is the blowup of a smooth curve, which may be considered as the contraction of a ruled surface

, and we claim that it is a trivial
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle in our case. Let

\displaystyle l\cdot(-K_{Y})

\displaystyle=l\cdot f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(1)\text{ \; by our assumptions}

\displaystyle=\deg(f|_{l}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(1)),

. Now we assume that

K
3 cover, the complete linear system
|-2K_{\bar{S}}|
does not have a multiple fixed component, and thus
n\leq 4
.

We show this in more detail here. Suppose that
n>4
. The diagram below shows the relevant maps.

We write
\bar{B}=Im(B)\subset\bar{S}
. On
\bar{S}=\mathbb{F}_{n}
, let
f
denote a fiber and
C_{1}
the positive section and
C_{0}
the negative section. Observe that
C_{1}=C_{0}+nf
. We show that
|-2K_{\bar{S}}|
contains
2C_{0}
. Since
-K_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}=D
, the toric boundary, we can write

\displaystyle-K_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}

\displaystyle=C_{0}+f+C_{1}+f

\displaystyle=2C_{0}+(n+2)f,

using the observation above. Then,

-K_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}\cdot C_{0}=-(n-2)<0\text{ for }n>2
.

Thus
C_{0}
is fixed in
|-2K_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}|
for
n>4
and
C_{0}\subset\bar{B}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle(|-2K_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}-C_{0}|)\cdot C_{0}

\displaystyle=-2(K_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}\cdot C_{0})-C_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2(n-2)+n

\displaystyle=-2n+4+n

\displaystyle-(n-4)

\displaystyle<0\text{ for }n>4.

Then for
n>4
, the curve
C_{0}
is fixed in
|-2K_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}-C_{0}|
. Therefore
2C_{0}\subset\bar{B}
. But this is a contradiction because we see that the double cover is singular along the curve
\bar{h}^{-1}(C_{0})
, but the cover should be smooth there. Therefore
n\leq 4
.

Next, we construct a birational morphism
\phi:\bar{S}\rightarrow S
by taking
\phi
to be the identity if
\bar{S}=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, or in the latter case (that is if
\bar{S}=\mathbb{F}_{n}
with
0\leq n\leq 4
and
n\neq 1
), by letting
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(1,1,n)
be the contraction of the negative section of
\mathbb{F}_{n}
. Now we have the sequence of maps

F\xrightarrow{h}S\xrightarrow{\pi}\bar{S}\xrightarrow{\phi}\hat{S}
.

Let
\theta=(\pi\circ h):F\rightarrow\bar{S}
. If
\bar{S}=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, take
L=\theta^{\ast}M
, where
M=H
is a hyperplane class. Then
M^{2}=1
and
L^{2}=2
. Suppose that
\bar{S}=\mathbb{F}_{n}
(with
0\leq n\leq 4
and
n\neq 1
). Take
L=\theta^{\ast}M
, where
M
is the positive section of
\mathbb{F}_{n}
. Then
M^{2}=n
and therefore
L^{2}=2n\leq 8
(because
n\leq 4
). This proves that in any case (if
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
or
\mathbb{F}_{n}
, where
n\leq 4
and
n\neq 1
), there is a line bundle
L
where
L^{2}
is bounded, and thus we may apply Theorem
2.8
to conclude that
\operatorname{\text{Aut}}(F)
acts with finitely many orbits on such
L
.

Now we have
\theta:F\rightarrow\bar{S}
where
\theta=\pi\circ h
and
L=\theta^{\ast}M
. We want to show that we can recover the 2-to-1 map
F\rightarrow S
from
L
. It suffices to show that
L
defines the morphism
F\rightarrow\hat{S}
. First of all, there is always an injection

H^{0}(F,L)\hookleftarrow H^{0}(\hat{S},M)
,

given by
\theta^{\ast}
. We need to show that this map is also surjective. To do this, it suffices to show that the dimensions of the left and right sides are equal, that is,

\dim H^{0}(F,L)=\dim(H^{0}(\hat{S},M))
.

Recall that
L
and
M
are nef and big. Moreover, recall that
F
is smooth and
\bar{S}
is log terminal. First of all,

\displaystyle h^{0}(L)

\displaystyle=\chi(F,L)\text{ by Kawamata Viehweg vanishing}

\displaystyle=\chi(\mathcal{O})+\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}L\cdot(L-K_{F})\text{ by Riemann-Roch}

\displaystyle=2+\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}L^{2}

\displaystyle=2+\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}(2\cdot M^{2})

\displaystyle=2+M^{2}

\displaystyle=2+n,

where
\bar{S}=\mathbb{P}(1,1,n)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then on
\bar{S}
, we can compute directly that
h^{0}(M)=n+2
. Therefore
\dim H^{0}(\bar{D},L)=\dim(H^{0}(\bar{S},M))
.
∎

What remains is to prove Theorem
1.9
for the extremal rays on
-K_{Z}^{\bot}
. We use the following theorem of Kawamata:

Theorem 2.9
.

(Kawamata,
[
K97
]
).

Let
Y
be a 3-fold and
f:Y\rightarrow S
a K3 fibration. Then
\operatorname{PsAut}(Y/S)
acts on
\operatorname{Mov}^{e}(Y/S)
with finitely many orbits on faces.

Kawamata’s cone lives in
N^{1}(Y/S)=N^{1}(Y)\times\mathbb{R}/f^{\ast}N^{1}(S)\otimes\mathbb{R}
, which for us is

N^{1}(Y)\otimes\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{R}\cdot[-K_{Y}]

(this is because in our setting,
S=\mathbb{P}^{1}
so that
N^{1}(S)=\mathbb{Z}\cdot[-K_{Y}]
). We know that
\operatorname{PsAut}(Y/\mathbb{P}^{1})
acts on faces of the chamber decomposition containing
[-K_{Y}]
with finitely many orbits. In our picture, every chamber
\operatorname{Nef}(Z)
contains
[-K_{Y}]
because every model has this fibration (the models are related by flops). Then
\operatorname{PsAut}(Y)
acts on the codimension 1 faces of

with finitely many orbits, taking care of the extremal rays on

and completing the proof of Theorem

.

, Theorem

Let

be a
K
3 surface over a field

K
is trivial (Tsen’s theorem). Then, writing

for the absolute Galois group of

\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\operatorname{Pic}(S\otimes_{K}\bar{K})^{G}

[

, Chapter 17 §2.2. Assume in addition that

\operatorname{Pic}(S\otimes_{K}\bar{K})

\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\operatorname{Pic}(S\otimes_{K}\bar{K})

be the Weyl group generated by the reflections

for
\alpha\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)
such that
\alpha^{2}=-2
. Then
\operatorname{\text{Aut}}(S)
is commensurable with
\operatorname{\text{Aut}}(\operatorname{Pic}(S))/W

(Theorem

[

Proposition 3.1
.

With notation as above,
\operatorname{\text{Aut}}(S)
is finite if and only if

has finite index. Then the lattice
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
is called 2-reflective in

D83
]
. ∎

Example 3.6
.

Here we describe all examples of Type (6) with base
S
an Enriques surface. Recall that in this case, we have a conic bundle
g:Y\rightarrow S
. The finite 2-to-1 morphism
(g,f):Y\rightarrow S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
has branch locus
\mathcal{B}\sim\mathrm{pr}^{\ast}_{1}(-2K_{S})+\mathrm{pr}^{\ast}_{2}\mathcal{O}(2)
by Riemann-Hurwitz and our assumption that
-K_{Y}=f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(1)
. Since
S
is an Enriques surface, we have
2K_{S}\sim 0
and
\mathcal{B}\sim\mathrm{pr}^{\ast}_{2}\mathcal{O}(2)
. So the double cover
(g,f)
is branched over two fibers of the trivial family
S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
and restricts to the universal cover
F\rightarrow S
of
S
on the remaining fibers. It follows that

Y=(F\times\mathbb{P}^{1})/(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
,

where the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
action is given by the involution
i\times j
, where
i
is the fixed point free involution on
F
with quotient
S
and
j:\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
j(t)=-t
. We consider the conic bundle
g:Y\rightarrow S=F/\langle i\rangle
as the first projection map and the
K
3 fibration
f:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}/\langle j\rangle\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
as the second projection.

In general if
G
is a finite group acting on
X
and
Y=X/G
, then the nef cone of
Y
may be described as

\operatorname{Nef}(Y)=\operatorname{Nef}(X)\cap N^{1}(Y)
,

where
N^{1}(Y)=N^{1}(X)^{G}\subset N^{1}(X)
. Therefore, to understand
\operatorname{Nef}(Y)
, it is enough to understand
\operatorname{Nef}(X)
.

In our case, we have
X=F\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. So

\operatorname{Nef}(X)=\langle\mathrm{pr}^{\ast}_{1}\operatorname{Nef}(F),\mathrm{pr}^{\ast}_{2}\operatorname{Nef}\mathcal{O}(1)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}\geq 0}
.

Now,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Nef}(Y)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Nef}((F\times\mathbb{P}^{1})/(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Nef}(F\times\mathbb{P}^{1})\cap N^{1}(F\times\mathbb{P}^{1})^{G}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathrm{pr}^{\ast}_{1}(\operatorname{Nef}(F)),\mathrm{pr}^{\ast}_{2}(\mathcal{O}(1))\rangle\cap N^{1}(F\times\mathbb{P}^{1})^{G}

\displaystyle=\langle g^{\ast}\operatorname{Nef}(S),f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(1)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}\geq 0}.

In particular, there are no faces of
\operatorname{Nef}(Y)
corresponding to small contractions, so
\operatorname{Mov}(Y)=\operatorname{Nef}(Y)
. Finally,
\operatorname{PsAut}(Y)=\operatorname{\text{Aut}}(Y)
acts on
N^{1}(Y)=N^{1}(S)\times N^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
via the surjection
\operatorname{\text{Aut}}(Y)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{\text{Aut}}(S)
. Now the cone conjecture for Enriques surfaces (see
[
K97
]
, Theorem 2.1) implies the cone conjecture for
Y
. In particular, Theorem 1.8 holds.

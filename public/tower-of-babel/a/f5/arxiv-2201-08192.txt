Self-adjointness for the MIT bag model on an unbounded cone
Biagio Cassano, Vladimir Lotoreichik

Abstract
. We consider the massless Dirac operator with the MIT bag boundary conditions on an unbounded three-dimensional circular cone. For convex cones, we prove that this operator is self-adjoint defined on four-component H 1 H^{1} –functions satisfying the MIT bag boundary conditions. The proof of this result relies on separation of variables and spectral estimates for one-dimensional fiber Dirac-type operators. Furthermore, we provide a numerical evidence for the self-adjointness on the same domain also for non-convex cones. Moreover, we prove a Hardy-type inequality for such a Dirac operator on convex cones, which, in particular, yields stability of self-adjointness under perturbations by a class of unbounded potentials. Further extensions of our results to Dirac operators with quantum dot boundary conditions are also discussed.

1.
Introduction

In the present paper, we consider the three-dimensional massless
Dirac operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
on an unbounded Euclidean circular cone of half-aperture
\omega\in(0,\pi)

(1.1)

{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}:=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\colon x_{3}>\cot\omega\,(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}

with the MIT bag boundary conditions. These boundary conditions are
related to the celebrated MIT bag model
[
C75
,
CJJT74
,
CJJ+74
,
DJJK75
,
J75
]
, which describes the confinement of quarks in hadrons. The cone
{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}
on which the Dirac operator is defined can be viewed
as a Lipschitz hypograph in the sense of
[
McL
, Eq. (3.26)]
and clearly
{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}
is non-smooth due to the conical point at the origin
\mathbf{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
The operator domain of
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}

consists of functions in the space
C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\setminus\mathbf{0};{\mathbb{C}}^{4}):=\{u|_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\colon u\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\mathbf{0};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\}

satisfying the MIT bag boundary conditions on the boundary of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}
. This densely defined operator can be shown to be symmetric. The natural question that arises whether this operator is essentially self-adjoint
or whether it has non-zero deficiency indices.

In the existing literature self-adjointness of the
three-dimensional Dirac operators
with the MIT bag boundary
conditions was first shown for
C^{2}
-smooth domains with
compact boundaries
[
OV18
,
BHM20
]
; see also
[
ALR17
,
ALR20
]

for related approaches.
It is possible to regard Dirac operators with MIT bag boundary
conditions in the framework of
Dirac operators with Lorentz-scalar
\delta
-shell interactions; see
e.g.

[
AMV14
,
AMV15
,
AMV16
,
BEHL16
,
BHSS21
,
HOP18
,
OV18
,
CLMT21
,
B22b
,
R22
]
, the review papers
[
BEHL19
,
OP21
]
, and the references
therein. In particular, in
[
B22b
]

self-adjointness for interactions with non-compact supports less regular than

C^{2}
-smooth is shown, but the regularity assumptions there still
exclude the conical surface.
On the other hand, in
[
BHSS21
]
self-adjointness for interactions supported on boundaries of general bounded Lipschitz domains that can have conical points is proved: a difference with the approach of
[
BHSS21
]
is that they define the Dirac operator with the operator domain contained in the Sobolev space
H^{1/2}
whereas in our construction we
verify (essential) self-adjointness for a more regular
operator domain, contained in the Sobolev space
H^{1}
. We remark that the unboundedness of the conical domain is not a real issue: away from the vertex singularity the boundary of the domain is smooth, so it can be handled as in
[
B22b
,
R20
]
.
Considering a more regular operator domain can in many cases lead to non-zero deficiency indices and, in fact, this happens
in a related setting of two-dimensional polygons analysed in
[
LO18
,
PV19
]
.

We prove that the Dirac operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
is unitarily equivalent to an infinite orthogonal sum of Dirac operators on the half-line with off-diagonal Coulomb-type potentials, whose coefficients
are given by the eigenvalues of an effective essentially self-adjoint spin-orbit operator
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}
on the spherical cap
{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, where
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is the two-dimensional unit sphere centred at the origin. We
characterise the eigenvalues of
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}

in terms of solutions of transcendental equations and show that for
convex cones
\omega<\pi/2
all the eigenvalues
of
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}
escape the critical interval
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
.
This construction implies that
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
is
essentially self-adjoint in this case. Moreover, we show that for
\omega<\pi/2
the domain of the self-adjoint closure
\overline{{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}}
of
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}

consists of four-component
H^{1}
-functions satisfying the MIT bag boundary conditions in the sense of traces.
We complement our
analysis by numerical results describing the eigenvalue distribution
of
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}
for
\pi/2<\omega<\pi
, supporting the claim that

{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
is essentially self-adjoint for any
\omega\in(0,\pi)
: we remark that
the numerical approach is only used to find the roots of explicit transcendental equations associated to the problem.
Finally, we study for
\omega<\pi/2
the stability of
the self-adjointness
\overline{{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}}
under perturbations
by Hermitian matrix-valued potentials that can have Coulombic singularity at the origin.

Our analysis is inspired by the closely related
considerations for the Laplace operator on planar domains with corners and three-dimensional domains with conical points
and for the Dirac operator on planar domains with corners. Let us briefly review the existing literature on the subject. The two-dimensional Laplace operator
with Dirichlet boundary conditions on a curvilinear polygon
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
was first considered in
[

]
and then further analysed in
[

,
[

[

]
and more recently in
[

]

In particular, it is proved that the deficiency indices of such a Laplace operator with the domain

are
(n,n)

is the number of non-convex corners of
{\mathcal{P}}
;
i.e.
corners with the opening angle larger than
\pi
.
A similar effect occurs for the Robin Laplacian, where the coefficient in the boundary conditions having a specific singularity at a boundary point
creates deficiency indices
[
ES88
,
MR09
,
NP18
]
. The three-dimensional Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with a conical point is analysed first in
[
HS67
,
K67
]
, see also
[
G85
, §8.2.2]
and
[
BDY99
,
D88
]
.
The appearance of deficiency indices here is more subtle and we discuss it in detail in Remark

on a domain that has a conical point and which is smooth outside it, the transition between self-adjointness and existence of non-trivial deficiency indices indeed occurs for some critical opening angle of the cone. In this perspective the phenomenon for the Dirac operator is substantially different.
As for Dirac operators, analysis of deficiency indices for boundaries with singular points has only been carried out
in two dimensions
[
FL23
,
LO18
,
PV19
,
CL20
]
so far. The deficiency indices on a curvilinear polygon here are characterised in the same way as for the Dirichlet Laplacian
[
LO18
,
PV19
]
.
For the discontinuous infinite mass boundary conditions on a sector the deficiency indices can be unequal
[
CL20
]

while for Lorentz-scalar
\delta
-shell interactions supported on star-graphs
[

various scenarios are possible and, in particular, the deficiency indices depend on the strength of the interaction.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we rigorously define the Dirac operator on an unbounded cone with MIT bag boundary conditions and formulate all the main results of the paper. Section
3
contains preliminary material used throughout the paper. In this section we recall the concept of boundary triples, analyse self-adjointness of a model Dirac operator on the half-line, and find the form of the spin-orbit differential expression in the spherical coordinates. Further, in Section

(0,\omega)
. Relying on this spectral analysis we decompose the operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
in Section
5
in spherical coordinates and using this decomposition we prove all the main results of the paper. Finally, the paper is complemented by
Appendix
A
with the derivation of expressions for partial derivatives in spherical coordinates, Appendix
B
on Dirac systems, and Appendix
C
with numerical results for non-convex cones.

2.
Setting and the main results

In this section, we will first rigorously define the operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
clarifying the MIT bag boundary conditions.
Then we will formulate all the main results of the present paper.

2.1.
Definition of the operator

The problem is better described in spherical coordinates
(r,\theta,\phi)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, which are connected with the Cartesian coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
by the well-known formulae

(2.1)

\begin{cases}x_{1}=r\cos\phi\sin\theta,\\
x_{2}=r\sin\phi\sin\theta,\\
x_{3}=r\cos\theta.\end{cases}

In this convention the north pole of the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
has coordinates

r=1,\theta=0
, and
\phi=0
.
The cone with the half-aperture
\omega\in(0,\pi)
defined in (
1.1
) can be alternatively characterised in the spherical coordinates as

(2.2)

{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}=\big\{(r\sin\theta\cos\phi,r\sin\theta\sin\phi,r\cos\theta)\colon r\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\theta\in[0,\omega),\phi\in[0,2\pi)\big\}.

The unit normal vector to the boundary
\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}
of the cone

{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}
pointing outwards is explicitly given as

\nu_{\omega}(r,\phi)=(\cos\omega\cos\phi,\cos\omega\sin\phi,-\sin\omega),\qquad r\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\phi\in[0,2\pi).

After having described the geometric setting, we can
proceed to the definition of the Dirac operator on
{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}
. To this aim we recall the expressions for the
{\mathbb{C}}^{2\times 2}
Pauli matrices

\sigma_{1}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\sigma_{2}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-{\mathsf{i}}\\
{\mathsf{i}}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\sigma_{3}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},

and for the
{\mathbb{C}}^{4\times 4}
Dirac matrices
\alpha_{j}
,
j=1,2,3
, and
\beta

\alpha_{j}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma_{j}\\
\sigma_{j}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad j=1,2,3,\qquad\quad\beta=\begin{pmatrix}I_{2}&0\\
0&-I_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
I_{2}
is the identity matrix in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. The Dirac matrices are Hermitian and they satisfy the anti-commutation relations

\alpha_{k}\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k}=2\delta_{kj}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha_{k}\beta+\beta\alpha_{k}=0,\quad k,j\in\{1,2,3\},

where
\delta_{kj}
is the Kronecker symbol.
We define the triple of the Dirac matrices
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
and define
\alpha\cdot b
with
b\in{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
as

\alpha\cdot b=\alpha_{1}b_{1}+\alpha_{2}b_{2}+\alpha_{3}b_{3}
.
With all the above preparations, the Dirac differential expression in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is given by

{\mathcal{D}}:=-{\mathsf{i}}\alpha\cdot\nabla=-{\mathsf{i}}(\alpha_{1}\partial_{1}+\alpha_{2}\partial_{2}+\alpha_{3}\partial_{3}).

We consider the following Dirac operator in the Hilbert space
(L^{2}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4}),(\cdot,\cdot)_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}})
(here and in the following all the inner products are linear in the first entry)

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}u

\displaystyle:={\mathcal{D}}u,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}

\displaystyle:=\big\{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\setminus\mathbf{0};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}+{\mathsf{i}}\beta(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}=0\big\},

where
C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\setminus\mathbf{0};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})=\{u|_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\colon u\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\mathbf{0};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\}
is the space of smooth four-component functions on
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}
, the support of which is a compact subset of the closure of the cone with the removed tip
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\setminus\mathbf{0}
.
The operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
is symmetric.
Indeed, integrating by parts we find for
u,v\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}

\displaystyle(-{\mathsf{i}}\alpha\cdot\nabla u,v)_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}-(u,-{\mathsf{i}}\alpha\cdot\nabla v)_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle=\!\int_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\langle-{\mathsf{i}}(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}},v|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}{\mathsf{d}}\sigma

\displaystyle=\!\int_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\langle(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})(\beta(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega}))u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}},{\mathsf{i}}\beta(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})v|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}{\mathsf{d}}\sigma

\displaystyle=\!\int_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\langle{\mathsf{i}}(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})^{2}(\beta^{2}(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega}))u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}},v|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}{\mathsf{d}}\sigma

\displaystyle=\!\int_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\langle{\mathsf{i}}(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}},v|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}{\mathsf{d}}\sigma=0,

where we used that
\beta^{2}=(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})^{2}=1
in the penultimate step.

It can be shown by the same integration by parts that the extension

(2.4)

\{u\in H^{1}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}+{\mathsf{i}}\beta(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}=0\}\ni u\mapsto{\mathcal{D}}u

of the Dirac operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
is symmetric, where the boundary conditions are understood in the sense of traces in the Sobolev space
H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
.

The MIT bag boundary condition

u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}+{\mathsf{i}}\beta(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}=0
can be written in terms of components
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})^{\top}
of the vector-valued function
u

\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega&{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega e^{-{\mathsf{i}}\phi}\\
0&1&{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega e^{{\mathsf{i}}\phi}&{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega\\
{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega&-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega e^{-{\mathsf{i}}\phi}&1&0\\
-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega e^{{\mathsf{i}}\phi}&-{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}(r,\omega,\phi)\\
u_{2}(r,\omega,\phi)\\
u_{3}(r,\omega,\phi)\\
u_{4}(r,\omega,\phi)\end{pmatrix}=0,

where
r\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
.
This condition is equivalent to

\begin{cases}u_{1}(r,\omega,\phi)-{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega u_{3}(r,\omega,\phi)+{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega e^{-{\mathsf{i}}\phi}u_{4}(r,\omega,\phi)=0,\\
u_{2}(r,\omega,\phi)+{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega e^{{\mathsf{i}}\phi}u_{3}(r,\omega,\phi)+{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega u_{4}(r,\omega,\phi)=0.\end{cases}

2.2.
Main results

In order to formulate our first main result
we need some preparation. We introduce for
\omega\in(0,\pi)\setminus\{\frac{\pi}{2}\}
the following matrix differential expressions on
the interval
{\mathcal{I}}_{\omega}:=(0,\omega)

\tau_{k}:=\begin{pmatrix}k+1&-\frac{{\mathsf{d}}}{{\mathsf{d}}\theta}-\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\cot\theta\\
\frac{{\mathsf{d}}}{{\mathsf{d}}\theta}-\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\cot\theta&-k\end{pmatrix},\qquad{\mathcal{T}}_{k}:=\begin{pmatrix}\tau_{k}&0\\
0&\tau_{k}\end{pmatrix},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}},

and
associate to them the differential operators
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, in the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})

defined by

(2.5)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{T}}_{k}\psi

\displaystyle:={\mathcal{T}}_{k}\psi,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{\psi\in{\rm AC}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon\psi,{\mathcal{T}}_{k}\psi\in L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4}),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\psi_{1}(\omega)\\
\psi_{2}(\omega)\end{smallmatrix}\right)={\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\psi_{3}(\omega)\\
\psi_{4}(\omega)\end{smallmatrix}\right)\bigg\},

where the matrix
{\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}
is given by

{\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}:=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega&-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega\\
-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega&-{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega\end{pmatrix}.

We check that the operators
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, are self-adjoint, that their spectra are discrete, simple and symmetric about the origin and that zero is not an eigenvalue of any of these operators. Moreover, it turns out that
\sigma({\mathsf{T}}_{k})={\mathcal{Z}}_{k}\cup(-{\mathcal{Z}}_{k})
, where
the discrete subsets
{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, of the real axis are defined by

(2.6)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\big\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\colon(\lambda+k+1){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)={\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k}(\cos\omega)\big\},

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\big\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\colon(\lambda+k){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k}(\cos\omega)=-{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k+1}(\cos\omega)\big\},

\displaystyle-k

\displaystyle\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
{\mathsf{P}}_{\nu}^{\mu}(x)
,
x\in(-1,1)
, is the Ferrers function of the first kind
[
O97
, §5.15]
and
[
GR07
, §8.7 and 8.8]
. Here, we drop the dependence on
\omega
in the notation

{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}
for the sake of brevity.

For
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we introduce the auxiliary one-dimensional Dirac operator in the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
by

(2.7)

\mathbf{d}_{\lambda}\psi:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\frac{{\mathsf{d}}}{{\mathsf{d}}x}-\frac{\lambda}{x}\\
\frac{{\mathsf{d}}}{{\mathsf{d}}x}-\frac{\lambda}{x}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,\mathbf{d}_{\lambda}:=C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbb{C}}^{2}).

In the first main result we decompose
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
into an orthogonal sum of the fiber operators.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\omega\in(0,\pi)\setminus\{\frac{\pi}{2}\}
and let the Dirac operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
be as in (
2.3
).
Let the operators
{\mathsf{T}}_{k},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, be as in (
2.5
), the discrete sets
{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}
be as in (
2.6
) and let the fiber operators
\mathbf{d}_{\lambda}
be as in (

). Then the following unitary equivalences hold

(2.8)

{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}\simeq\bigoplus_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\sigma({\mathsf{T}}_{k})\cap(0,\infty)}\mathbf{d}_{\lambda}\simeq\bigoplus_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}}\mathbf{d}_{\lambda}.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
relies on the representation of

{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
in spherical coordinates, in which the spin-orbit operator
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}

acting in the Hilbert space
L^{2}([0,\omega)\times[0,2\pi);\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta{\mathsf{d}}\phi)
arises. The spin-orbit operator
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}
is defined in (
5.4
) below. The operators
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
appear naturally in the analysis
upon separation of variables for the spin-orbit operator
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}
.
We decompose the
Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
into an orthogonal sum of
subspaces, whose construction is based on the eigenfunctions of the aforementioned spin-orbit operator
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}
. This orthogonal decomposition of
L^{2}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
enables to characterise the reducing subspaces for
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
. It turns out that the compressions of

to these reducing subspaces are unitarily equivalent to the one-dimensional fiber operators in (

) with specific

Remark 2.2
.

The case
\omega=\frac{\pi}{2}
does not fit to our general scheme of reduction and requires a separate consideration within our approach.
However, in this case the cone
{\mathcal{C}}_{\pi/2}
is the half-space and the theory is already established. The reader is referred to
[
ALR20
,
B22a
]
, where it is shown that the Dirac operator on
{\mathcal{C}}_{\pi/2}
is self-adjoint defined on functions in the Sobolev space
H^{1}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};\mathbb{C}^{4})
satisfying the MIT bag boundary conditions.
For this reason, we exclude the case
\omega=\frac{\pi}{2}
from our results.

.

Let
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
be defined as
in (
2.3
). If
\omega\in(0,\pi/2)
then
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
is essentially

(2.9)

\mathrm{dom}\,\overline{{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}}:=\big\{u\in H^{1}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}+{\mathsf{i}}\beta(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}=0\big\},

Remark 2.4
.

We conjecture that Theorem
2.3
is true for all the values
\omega\in(0,\pi)
of the aperture angle. In Appendix
C
we provide numerical evidence to support our conjecture,
showing that for
\pi/2<\omega<\pi
all the eigenvalues

Remark 2.5
.

The operator
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}
can be viewed as a Dirac-type operator on the manifold
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}
. Geometric spectral bounds in the spirit of
[
HMZ01
]
can also be useful to show that there are no eigenvalues of

in the interval
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
. This approach is also suitable to deal with
general non-circular cones, for which separation of variables can not be used any more.

Remark 2.6
.

In the literature, sometimes also the following operator

is called Dirac operator with MIT bag boundary conditions:

(2.10)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{-}u

\displaystyle:={\mathcal{D}}u,

\displaystyle:=\big\{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\setminus\mathbf{0};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}-{\mathsf{i}}\beta(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}=0\big\},

and

The operator

is unitarily equivalent to

through the unitary and self-adjoint matrix

Remark 2.7
.

It is interesting to compare Theorem
2.3
with related results for the Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary condition.
Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be a bounded domain such that
\mathbf{0}\in\partial\Omega
, that
\partial\Omega\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
is
C^{2}
-smooth and that there exists a neighbourhood of
{\mathcal{V}}
of
\mathbf{0}
such that in
{\mathcal{V}}
the domain
\Omega
coincides with an unbounded circular cone
{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}
with half-aperture
\omega\in(0,\pi)
. We consider the densely defined symmetric Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega

{\mathsf{H}}^{\Omega}_{\rm D}u:=-\Delta u,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{H}}^{\Omega}_{\rm D}:=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Let
t_{0}
be the zero of
{\mathsf{P}}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{0}
on the interval
(-1,1)
, and set
\omega_{\rm cr}:=\arccos(t_{0})\approx 0.726\pi
.
The operator
{\mathsf{H}}_{\rm D}^{\Omega}
is closed by
[
G85
, Thm. 8.2.2.1]
and
[
HS67
, Lem. 4.1]
for all
\omega\neq\omega_{\rm cr}
.
Combining
[
G85
, Thm. 8.2.2.6]
and
[
HS67
, Sec. 4]
one gets that if the angle
\omega>\omega_{\rm cr}
then the deficiency indices of
{\mathsf{H}}_{\rm D}^{\Omega}
are
(1,1)
while if
\omega<\omega_{\rm cr}
the operator
{\mathsf{H}}_{\rm D}^{\Omega}
is self-adjoint.
Similar results are expected to hold also for the Dirichlet Laplacian on
{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}
, but to the best of our knowledge they are not stated in the existing literature in terms of deficiency indices. In this perspective the result for the Dirac operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
is qualitatively different. The transition to non-trivial deficiency indices for large half-aperture does not happen for
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
as suggested by our numerical analysis of the problem.

It is possible to consider more general boundary conditions for the Dirac operator. For
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
, the Dirac operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{\theta}
with
quantum dot
bondary conditions is defined as follows:

(2.11)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{\theta}u

\displaystyle:={\mathcal{D}}u,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{\theta}

\displaystyle:=\big\{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\setminus\mathbf{0};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}=[(\cos\theta){\mathsf{i}}(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})\beta+(\sin\theta)\beta]u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\big\}.

One sees that
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{0}={\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
and
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{\pi}={\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{-}
are the Dirac operators with MIT bag boundary conditions defined in (
2.3
) and (
2.10
) respectively; the operators
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}
and
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}}
are called Dirac operators with
zig-zag
boundary conditions. From Theorem
2.3
it is immediate to give a result on essential self-adjointness and self-adjointness for Dirac operators with quantum dot boundary conditions, except for the case of zig-zag boundary conditions.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\theta\in[0,2\pi)\setminus\{\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{2}\}
and let
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{\theta}
be defined as
in (
2.11
). If
\omega\in(0,\pi/2)
then
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{\theta}
is essentially
self-adjoint and

(2.12)

\mathrm{dom}\,\overline{{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{\theta}}:=\big\{u\in H^{1}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}=[(\cos\theta){\mathsf{i}}(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})\beta+(\sin\theta)\beta]u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\big\},

where the trace
u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}
is well defined as a function in the Sobolev space
H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
.

Remark 2.9
.

The Dirac operators
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}
and
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}}
with zig-zag boundary conditions represent different phenomena and are not considered in this work. We refer to
[
H21
]
and references therein.

We complete our manuscript with a result on stability of essential self-adjointness and self-adjointness under perturbations of
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
by regular potentials.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\omega\in(0,\pi/2)
and
\mathbb{V}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{4\times 4}
such that
\mathbb{V}(x)
is Hermitian for all
x\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}
and

(2.13)

\sup_{x\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}|x||\mathbb{V}(x)|=\nu.

If
\nu\leq\frac{\pi}{4\omega}
,
then the Dirac operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}+\mathbb{V}
is essentially self-adjoint, with

\begin{split}&({\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}+\mathbb{V})u=({\mathcal{D}}+\mathbb{V})u,\\
&\mathrm{dom}\,({\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}+\mathbb{V})=\mathrm{dom}\,({\mathsf{D}}_{\omega})=\big\{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\setminus\mathbf{0};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}+{\mathsf{i}}\beta(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}=0\big\}.\end{split}

Moreover, if

\nu<\frac{\pi}{4\omega}
, then
\overline{{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}+\mathbb{V}}=\overline{{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}}+\mathbb{V}
is self-adjoint, with

\begin{split}&(\overline{{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}+\mathbb{V}})u=({\mathcal{D}}+\mathbb{V})u,\\
&\mathrm{dom}\,(\overline{{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}+\mathbb{V}})=\mathrm{dom}\,\overline{{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}}=\big\{u\in H^{1}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}+{\mathsf{i}}\beta(\alpha\cdot\nu_{\omega})u|_{\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}=0\big\}.\end{split}

Theorem
2.10
is worth to compare with the perturbation results for the Dirac operator in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. By
[
K95
, Thm. V 5.10]
the operator

H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\ni u\mapsto-{\mathsf{i}}(\alpha\cdot\nabla)u+{\mathbb{V}}u

is self-adjoint in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
if the Hermitian matrix-valued function
{\mathbb{V}}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{4\times 4}
satisfies the condition
\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x||{\mathbb{V}}(x)|<\frac{1}{2}
. For the Coulomb potential
{\mathbb{V}}_{\rm C}(x)=\frac{\gamma}{|x|}I_{4}
finer results are available, and, in particular, by
[
GR73
, Thm. 2.1]
the above Dirac operator with
{\mathbb{V}}={\mathbb{V}}_{\rm C}
is self-adjoint provided that
|\gamma|<\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
. More detailed discussion and further references can be found in
[
CP18
]
. According to Theorem
2.10
, self-adjointness of the Dirac operator on convex cones with MIT bag boundary conditions is stable under adding
general matrix-valued potentials with stronger singularities at the origin, than in the case of the full space. In particular, we can add Coloumb potentials
(having singularity at the tip of the cone)
with arbitrary large coefficient
\gamma
provided that the opening angle of the cone is sufficiently small.

Theorem
2.10
is a consequence of the Hardy inequality in the following proposition, that we state because it has an independent interest.
Thanks to it, the proof of Theorem
2.10
descends immediately
from the Kato-Rellich theorem and the Wüst theorem.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
be defined as
in (
2.3
). If
\omega\in(0,\pi/2)
then

(2.14)

\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}|({\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}u)(x)|^{2}{\mathsf{d}}x\geq\left(\frac{\pi}{4\omega}\right)^{2}\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}{\mathsf{d}}x,\qquad\text{for all}\,u\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}.

Remark 2.12
.

The constant in the inequality (
2.14
) follows from the estimate (
4.40
). Numerical evidence in Appendix
C
shows that this constant is not sharp. However,
it can also be seen from numerics that the difference between the sharp constant and
\left(\frac{\pi}{4\omega}\right)^{2}
tends to zero as
\omega\rightarrow\frac{\pi}{2}
.

3.
Preliminaries

In this section we provide preliminary material that will be used in the proofs of our main results. In Subsection
3.1
we recall the concept of boundary triples and provide some basic facts related to it. Further, in Subsection
3.2
we study
self-adjointness of a class of one-dimensional Dirac operators. Finally, in Subsection
3.3
we represent the spin-orbit operator on the spherical cap in the spherical coordinates.

3.1.
Boundary triples for adjoints of symmetric operators

In this subsection we recall the concept of ordinary boundary triples. This concept is introduced in
[
Bru76
,
Ko75
]
, further developed in
e.g.

[
DM91
,
DM95
]
and presented in detail in the monographs
[
BHdS20
,
S12
]
.
Throughout this subsection
{\mathsf{A}}
denotes a densely defined symmetric operator in a Hilbert space
({\mathcal{H}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{H}})
.

First, we define the notions of deficiency subspaces and deficiency indices of a symmetric operator.

Definition 3.1
.

The deficiency subspaces of the symmetric operator
{\mathsf{A}}
are defined as

{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathsf{i}}({\mathsf{A}}):=\ker({\mathsf{A}}^{*}-{\mathsf{i}}){\mathcal{N}}_{-{\mathsf{i}}}({\mathsf{A}}):=\ker({\mathsf{A}}^{*}+{\mathsf{i}}).

The deficiency indices of
{\mathsf{A}}
are given by
n_{\pm}({\mathsf{A}}):=\dim{\mathcal{N}}_{\pm{\mathsf{i}}}({\mathsf{A}})
.

Next, we recall the definition of the concept of a boundary triple for the adjoint of a symmetric operator.

Definition 3.2
.

A boundary triple for
{\mathsf{A}}^{*}
is a triple

\{{\mathcal{G}},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
of a Hilbert space
({\mathcal{G}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{G}})
and linear mappings

\Gamma_{j}\colon\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{A}}^{*}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
,
j=0,1
such that

(i)

({\mathsf{A}}^{*}f,g)_{\mathcal{H}}-(f,{\mathsf{A}}^{*}g)_{\mathcal{H}}=(\Gamma_{1}f,\Gamma_{0}g)_{\mathcal{G}}-(\Gamma_{0}f,\Gamma_{1}g)_{\mathcal{G}}

for all
f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{A}}^{*}
;

(ii)

the mapping
\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{A}}^{*}\ni f\mapsto(\Gamma_{0}f,\Gamma_{1}f)\in{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
is surjective.

In the next proposition we provide a necessary and sufficient condition for the boundary triple for the adjoint of a symmetric operator to exist.

Proposition 3.3
.

[
S12
, Prop. 14.5]

There exists a boundary triple
\{{\mathcal{G}},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
for
{\mathsf{A}}^{*}
if and only if the symmetric operator has equal deficiency indices. We then have
n_{+}({\mathsf{A}})=n_{-}({\mathsf{A}})=\dim{\mathcal{G}}
.

For
{\mathsf{A}}
a densely defined symmetric operator in a Hilbert
space
{\mathcal{H}}
, the knowledge of a boundary triple for the operator
{\mathsf{A}}^{*}
allows to parametrize
its self-adjoint restrictions.
Let
\Theta
be a linear operator in
{\mathcal{G}}
.
We define the operator
{\mathsf{A}}_{\Theta}:={\mathsf{A}}^{*}\upharpoonright\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{A}}_{\Theta}
, where

\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{A}}_{\Theta}:=\{f\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{A}}^{*}\colon\Gamma_{0}f\in\mathrm{dom}\,\Theta,\Gamma_{1}f=\Theta\Gamma_{0}f\}.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
S12
, Prop. 14.7 (v)]
)
.

The operator
{\mathsf{A}}_{\Theta}
is self-adjoint in
{\mathcal{H}}

if and only if
\Theta
is self-adjoint in
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Remark 3.5
.

One can not parametrize all self-adjoint extensions of
{\mathsf{A}}
by self-adjoint linear operators
\Theta
as described above. In order to cover all self-adjoint extensions self-adjoint linear relations in the boundary conditions should be considered;
cf.

[
S12
, Thm. 14.10]
. This most general construction is not necessary for our purposes.

3.2.
Deficiency indices and self-adjoint extensions for a class of 1-D Dirac operators

In this subsection we consider a model one-dimensional Dirac operator on the interval
(0,b)
with
b\in(0,\infty]
.
We find the deficiency indices and characterise the self-adjoint extensions. In the analysis we rely on the general approach to one-dimensional Dirac operators briefly outlined in Appendix
B
.

Let the parameter
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
be fixed. We consider the following Dirac differential expression on the interval
{\mathcal{I}}:=(0,b)
with
b\in(0,\infty]

(3.1)

\tau_{\alpha}f:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}f^{\prime}+\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{\alpha}{x}\\
\frac{\alpha}{x}&0\end{pmatrix}f,

where
f=(f_{1},f_{2})^{\top}\colon{\mathcal{I}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
The differential expression
\tau_{\alpha}
is of the type (
B.1
)
with the potential

(3.2)

q(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{\alpha}{x}\\
\frac{\alpha}{x}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Next we classify the endpoints of the interval
{\mathcal{I}}
for
\tau_{\alpha}
in the sense of Definition
B.3
.
The differential expression
\tau_{\alpha}
is singular at
x=0
for
\alpha\neq 0
, because the matrix norm of the potential
q
in (
3.2
) has a non-integrable singularity at the origin, while
\tau_{0}
is regular at
x=0
. The differential expression
\tau_{\alpha}
is singular at the right endpoint
x=b
if
b=\infty
and regular at
x=b
if
b<\infty
.

In view of (
B.2
), the maximal operator associated with
\tau_{\alpha}
is given by

(3.3)

\begin{split}{\mathsf{T}}_{\alpha}f&:=\tau_{\alpha}f,\\
\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{\alpha}&:=\left\{f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})\colon f\in{\rm AC}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2}),\tau_{\alpha}f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})\right\}.\end{split}

For
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the deficiency subspaces by

(3.4)

{\mathcal{N}}_{\alpha,z}:=\ker({\mathsf{T}}_{\alpha}-z).

According to (
B.3
) the densely defined preminimal operator is given by

(3.5)

{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}f:=\tau_{\alpha}f,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}:=C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2}).

By Proposition
B.2
the closed minimal symmetric operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}:=\overline{{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}}
is characterised by

(3.6)

\begin{split}{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}f&=\tau_{\alpha}f,\\
\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}&=\{f\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{\alpha}\colon[g,f]_{0}=[g,f]_{b}=0,\,\,\forall\,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{\alpha}\}\\
&=\{f\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{\alpha}\colon[g,f]_{0}=[g,f]_{b}=0,\,\,\forall\,g\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\alpha,{\mathsf{i}}}+{\mathcal{N}}_{\alpha,-{\mathsf{i}}}\},\end{split}

where the boundary values of Lagrange brackets
[g,f]_{0}
and
[g,f]_{b}
are defined via (
B.5
) and Proposition
B.1
.
By Proposition
B.5
we have

(3.7)

({\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime})^{*}=({\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha})^{*}={\mathsf{T}}_{\alpha}.

Further, we characterise the deficiency indices and the self-adjoint
extensions of
{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}
under different assumptions on
b
and

\alpha
. This analysis is reminiscent of the one
in
[
LO18
, Lem. 2.5]
and
[
CP18
, Thms. 1.1 and 1.2]
,
[
CP19
]
. We provide it in full detail for convenience of the reader and also because we need to cover some additional aspects.
We remark that item (iii) of the below proposition will not be used in the proofs of our main results and is provided only for completeness.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let the maximal operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{\alpha}
, the preminimal operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}

and the minimal operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}
be as in (
3.3
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), respectively.

(i)

For
|\alpha|\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
b<\infty
the preminimal operator has deficiency indices
(1,1)
and the triple
\{{\mathbb{C}},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
with the well-defined mappings

(3.8)

\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\colon\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{\alpha}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}},\qquad\Gamma_{0}f:=f_{1}(b),\quad\Gamma_{1}f:=f_{2}(b)

is a boundary triple for
{\mathsf{T}}_{\alpha}=({\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime})^{*}
. Moreover, any self-adjoint extension of
{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}
has purely discrete spectrum.

(ii)

For
|\alpha|\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
b=\infty
the preminimal operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}
is essentially self-adjoint.
If, moreover,
|\alpha|>\frac{1}{2}
, then

\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}=H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

(iii)

For
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
and
b=\infty
the preminimal operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}
has deficiency indices
(1,1)
and the deficiency subspaces are
given by

{\mathcal{N}}_{\alpha,\pm{\mathsf{i}}}=\ker({\mathsf{T}}_{\alpha}\mp{\mathsf{i}})={\rm span}\,\left\{f_{\alpha}^{\pm}\right\},\qquad f_{\alpha}^{\pm}(x):=\begin{pmatrix}x^{1/2}K_{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}(x)\\
\mp{\mathsf{i}}x^{1/2}K_{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}(x)\end{pmatrix},

where
K_{\nu}
stands for the modified Bessel function of the second kind and order
\nu
.
All self-adjoint extensions of
{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}
are
characterised by

(3.9)

{\mathsf{T}}_{\gamma,\alpha}f=\tau_{\alpha}f,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{\gamma,\alpha}=\left\{f+c(f_{\alpha}^{+}+\gamma f_{\alpha}^{-})\colon f\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha},c\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\},

for
\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
|\gamma|=1
.

Proof.

It is clear from (
3.3
), (
3.4
), and (
3.7
) that the deficiency indices of
{\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}
are given by

n_{\pm}({\mathsf{T}}_{0,\alpha}^{\prime\prime})=\dim{\mathcal{N}}_{\alpha,\pm{\mathsf{i}}}.

In order to characterise
{\mathcal{N}}_{\alpha,\pm{\mathsf{i}}}
we need to find all square-integrable solutions of the differential equations
\tau_{\alpha}f=\pm{\mathsf{i}}f
.

The differential equation

\tau_{\alpha}f=\pm{\mathsf{i}}f
is equivalent to the system

(3.10)

\begin{cases}-f_{1}^{\prime}(x)+\frac{\alpha}{x}f_{1}(x)=\pm{\mathsf{i}}f_{2}(x),\\
+f_{2}^{\prime}(x)+\frac{\alpha}{x}f_{2}(x)=\pm{\mathsf{i}}f_{1}(x).\end{cases}

We express
f_{2}
through
f_{1}
from the first equation in (
3.10
) and substitute it into the second equation

(3.11)

\begin{cases}f_{2}(x)=\pm\left({\mathsf{i}}f_{1}^{\prime}(x)-{\mathsf{i}}\frac{\alpha}{x}f_{1}(x)\right),\\
{\mathsf{i}}f_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x)-{\mathsf{i}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{x}f_{1}(x)\right)^{\prime}+\frac{\alpha}{x}\left({\mathsf{i}}f_{1}^{\prime}(x)-{\mathsf{i}}\frac{\alpha}{x}f_{1}(x)\right)-{\mathsf{i}}f_{1}(x)=0.\end{cases}

The second equation in the above system can be transformed into

f_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x)+\left(\frac{\alpha}{x^{2}}f_{1}(x)-\frac{\alpha}{x}f_{1}^{\prime}(x)\right)+\frac{\alpha}{x}\left(f_{1}^{\prime}(x)-\frac{\alpha}{x}f_{1}(x)\right)-f_{1}(x)=0

and then simplified as

(3.12)

f_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x)-\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{x^{2}}f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x)=0.

By making the substitution

f_{1}(x)=g(x)x^{1/2}
in (
3.12
) we find that
g
satisfies
the ordinary differential equation

(3.13)

x^{2}g^{\prime\prime}(x)+xg^{\prime}(x)-\left(\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+x^{2}\right)g(x)=0.

According to
[
AS64
, §9.6]
there
are exactly
two linearly independent solutions of the above equation given by the modified Bessel functions
K_{\nu}(x)
and
I_{\nu}(x)
of the order

\nu=\left|\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right|,

and any solution of (
3.13
) is a linear combination of them.
Recall also that the modified Bessel functions are smooth on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Hence, the two linearly independent solutions of (
3.12
) are given by

(3.14)

f^{+}_{1}(x)=x^{1/2}I_{\nu}(x)f^{-}_{1}(x)=x^{1/2}K_{\nu}(x).

Using the first equation in (
3.11
) we also find the second component of the solution to the system (
3.10
) associated with
f^{+}_{1}

(3.15)

\begin{split}f_{2}^{+}(x)&=\pm{\mathsf{i}}\left((x^{1/2}I_{\nu}(x))^{\prime}-\frac{\alpha}{x^{1/2}}I_{\nu}(x)\right)\\
&=\pm{\mathsf{i}}\left(x^{1/2}I_{\nu-1}(x)-\frac{\nu+\alpha-1/2}{x^{1/2}}I_{\nu}(x)\right),\end{split}

where we used that
I_{\nu}^{\prime}(x)=I_{\nu-1}(x)-\frac{\nu}{x}I_{\nu}(x)
;
cf.

[
AS64
, 9.6.26]
.
The second component associated with
f_{1}^{-}
can be recovered from the first formula in (
3.11
)

(3.16)

\begin{split}f_{2}^{-}(x)&=\pm{\mathsf{i}}\left((x^{1/2}K_{\nu}(x))^{\prime}-\frac{\alpha}{x^{1/2}}K_{\nu}(x)\right)\\
&=\pm{\mathsf{i}}\left(-x^{1/2}K_{\nu-1}(x)-\frac{\nu+\alpha-1/2}{x^{1/2}}K_{\nu}(x)\right),\end{split}

where we used that
K_{\nu}^{\prime}(x)=-K_{\nu-1}(x)-\frac{\nu}{x}K_{\nu}(x)
;
cf.

[
AS64
, 9.6.26]
.
We have found that the system of differential equations
\tau_{\alpha}f=\pm{\mathsf{i}}f
has two linearly independent solutions

f^{+}=(f_{1}^{+},f_{2}^{+})
and
f^{-}=(f_{1}^{-},f_{2}^{-})
with respective components given by (
3.14
), (
3.15
), and (
3.16
).

Now, we develop the asymptotic expansions
of the components
f_{2}^{+}
,
f_{1}^{-}
and
f_{2}^{-}
in the limit
x\rightarrow 0^{+}
.
For the second component of the solution
f^{+}
we get using
[
AS64
, Eq. 9.6.7 and Eq. 9.6.10]

and taking that
I_{-1}(x)=I_{1}(x)
into account for
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}

(3.17)

f_{2}^{+}(x)\sim_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}\begin{cases}\pm\frac{{\mathsf{i}}x^{\nu+3/2}}{\nu(\nu+1)2^{\nu+1}\Gamma(\nu)},&\alpha>\frac{1}{2},\\
\pm\frac{{\mathsf{i}}x^{3/2}}{2},&\alpha=\frac{1}{2},\\
\pm\frac{{\mathsf{i}}x^{\nu-1/2}}{2^{\nu-1}\Gamma(\nu)},&\alpha<\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

For the first component of the solution
f^{-}
we find using the asymptotics
K_{\nu}
as
x\rightarrow 0^{+}

(see
[
AS64
, 9.6.8, 9.6.9]
) we find that

(3.18)

f_{1}^{-}(x)\sim_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}\begin{cases}-x^{1/2}\ln(x),&\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2},\\
2^{\nu-1}\Gamma(\nu)x^{1/2-\nu},\quad&\alpha\neq\tfrac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Finally, for the second component of the solution
f^{-}
we find again using
the asymptotics
K_{\nu}
as
x\rightarrow 0^{+}

that

(3.19)

f_{2}^{-}(x)\sim_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}\begin{cases}\pm(-{\mathsf{i}})2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)x^{-\nu-\frac{1}{2}},&\alpha>\tfrac{1}{2},\\
\pm(-{\mathsf{i}})2^{-\nu}\Gamma(1-\nu)x^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}},&\alpha\in\big(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\big],\\
\pm{\mathsf{i}}x^{1/2}\ln(x),&\alpha=-\tfrac{1}{2},\\
\pm(-{\mathsf{i}})2^{\nu-2}\Gamma(\nu-1)x^{3/2-\nu},&\alpha<-\tfrac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

The remaining part of the proof is split into the analysis of the three cases

(i)

b<\infty
,
|\alpha|\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

(ii)

3.3.
The spin-orbit differential expression in spherical coordinates

The main goal of this subsection is to compute the representation of the spin-orbit differential expression in the natural coordinates on the unit sphere. The spin-orbit differential expression appears in the representation of the Dirac differential expression
{\mathcal{D}}
in the spherical coordinates; see
[
W03
, §20.3]
and
[
T92
, §4.6]
.

To this aim we first find that the components of the orbital angular momentum differential expression

{\mathcal{L}}=-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf x}\times\nabla=({\mathcal{L}}_{1},{\mathcal{L}}_{2},{\mathcal{L}}_{3})

are given by

{\mathcal{L}}_{1}=-{\mathsf{i}}(x_{2}\partial_{3}-x_{3}\partial_{2}),\qquad{\mathcal{L}}_{2}=-{\mathsf{i}}(x_{3}\partial_{1}-x_{1}\partial_{3}),\qquad{\mathcal{L}}_{3}=-{\mathsf{i}}(x_{1}\partial_{2}-x_{2}\partial_{1}).

According to Appendix
A
the expressions for

in terms of

are given by

(3.23)

\begin{cases}\partial_{1}=\cos\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\cos\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}-\frac{\sin\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r},\\
\partial_{2}=\sin\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\sin\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}+\frac{\cos\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r},\\
\partial_{3}=\cos\theta\partial_{r}-\frac{\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}.\end{cases}

The next step is to express
{\mathcal{L}}_{1},{\mathcal{L}}_{2},{\mathcal{L}}_{3}
in terms of
\partial_{\theta}

and
\partial_{\phi}
. In order to compute
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
we combine the expression
for
x_{2}
,
x_{3}
in (
2.1
) with the formulae for
\partial_{2},\partial_{3}

in (
3.23
)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{1}

\displaystyle=-{\mathsf{i}}r\sin\phi\sin\theta\left(\cos\theta\partial_{r}-\frac{\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle+{\mathsf{i}}r\cos\theta\left(\sin\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\sin\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}+\frac{\cos\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathsf{i}}\left(\sin\phi\sin^{2}\theta+\sin\phi\cos^{2}\theta\right)\partial_{\theta}+{\mathsf{i}}\cot\theta\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}={\mathsf{i}}\sin\phi\partial_{\theta}+{\mathsf{i}}\cot\theta\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}.

To compute
{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
we combine the expression for
x_{1}
,
x_{3}
in (
2.1
) with the formulae for
\partial_{1},\partial_{3}
in (
3.23
)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{2}

\displaystyle=-{\mathsf{i}}r\cos\theta\left(\cos\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\cos\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}-\frac{\sin\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle+{\mathsf{i}}r\cos\phi\sin\theta\left(\cos\theta\partial_{r}-\frac{\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=-{\mathsf{i}}\left(\cos\phi\cos^{2}\theta+\cos\phi\sin^{2}\theta\right)\partial_{\theta}+{\mathsf{i}}\cot\theta\sin\phi\partial_{\phi}=-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\phi\partial_{\theta}+{\mathsf{i}}\cot\theta\sin\phi\partial_{\phi}.

For the derivation of
{\mathcal{L}}_{3}
we combine the expression for
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
in (
2.1
) with the formulae for
\partial_{1},\partial_{2}
in (

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{3}

\displaystyle=-{\mathsf{i}}(x_{1}\partial_{2}-x_{2}\partial_{1})=-{\mathsf{i}}r\cos\phi\sin\theta\left(\sin\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\sin\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}+\frac{\cos\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle+{\mathsf{i}}r\sin\phi\sin\theta\left(\cos\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\cos\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}-\frac{\sin\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathsf{i}}\left(-\sin\phi\cos\phi\sin\theta\cos\theta+\sin\phi\cos\phi\sin\theta\cos\theta\right)\partial_{\theta}+{\mathsf{i}}\left(-\cos^{2}\phi-\sin^{2}\phi\right)\partial_{\phi}=-{\mathsf{i}}\partial_{\phi}.

Using the expressions for

,

we compute the auxiliary differential expression

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}:=\sigma_{1}{\mathcal{L}}_{1}+\sigma_{2}{\mathcal{L}}_{2}+\sigma_{3}{\mathcal{L}}_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}-{\mathsf{i}}\partial_{\phi}&-e^{-{\mathsf{i}}\phi}\partial_{\theta}+{\mathsf{i}}\cot\theta e^{-{\mathsf{i}}\phi}\partial_{\phi}\\

4.
Spectral analysis of the one-dimensional model Dirac-type operator

In this section we consider a family of one-dimensional Dirac operators on the interval
{\mathcal{I}}_{\omega}=(0,\omega)
,
\omega\in(0,\pi)\setminus\{\frac{\pi}{2}\}
. These operators arise in the orthogonal decomposition of the spin-orbit operator on the spherical cap. We show that these Dirac-type operators are self-adjoint in
the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
and that their spectrum is discrete. Furthermore, we explicitly find their eigenvalues as solutions of certain transcendental equations and characterise
the respective associated eigenfuctions. Finally, we obtain estimates on the size of the spectral gaps of these model Dirac-type operators.

First, we recall the definitions of the

2\times 2
matrix differential expression on the interval
{\mathcal{I}}_{\omega}

(4.1)

\tau_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}k+1&-\frac{{\mathsf{d}}}{{\mathsf{d}}\theta}-\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\cot\theta\\
\frac{{\mathsf{d}}}{{\mathsf{d}}\theta}-\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\cot\theta&-k\end{pmatrix},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}},

and the associated
4\times 4
matrix differential expression on
{\mathcal{I}}_{\omega}

{\mathcal{T}}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}\tau_{k}&0\\
0&\tau_{k}\end{pmatrix}.

Recall also that
2\times 2
unitary matrix

{\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}
is defined by

(4.2)

{\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega&-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega\\
-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega&-{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega\end{pmatrix},

and that the coupling of two Dirac operators on the interval
{\mathcal{I}}_{\omega}

is introduced as

(4.3)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{T}}_{k}\psi

\displaystyle={\mathcal{T}}_{k}\psi,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\!\left\{\psi\!\in\!{\rm AC}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon\!\psi,{\mathcal{T}}_{k}\psi\in L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4}),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\psi_{1}(\omega)\\
\psi_{2}(\omega)\end{smallmatrix}\right)\!=\!{\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\psi_{3}(\omega)\\
\psi_{4}(\omega)\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\}\!.

In the first proposition of this section we establish the self-adjointness of
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}

and show that the spectrum of
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
is purely discrete.

Proposition 4.1
.

The operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, in (
4.3
) is self-adjoint in
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
, and the restriction of
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
to the subspace of
\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{k}

(4.4)

{\mathcal{M}}:=\left\{\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,\omega];{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\psi_{1}(\omega)\\
\psi_{2}(\omega)\end{smallmatrix}\right)\!=\!{\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\psi_{3}(\omega)\\
\psi_{4}(\omega)\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\}

is essentially self-adjoint. Finally, the spectrum of
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}

is purely discrete.

Proof.

The proof of self-adjointness of
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
is based on a perturbation argument. We decompose
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
into a sum of the Dirac-type operator whose self-adjointness is established by the methods of Subsection
3.2
and a bounded self-adjoint perturbation.

To this aim, we consider the auxiliary differential expression

\check{\tau}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{{\mathsf{d}}}{{\mathsf{d}}\theta}+\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\theta}\\
-\frac{{\mathsf{d}}}{{\mathsf{d}}\theta}+\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\theta}&0\end{pmatrix}.

This differential expression is of the type (
3.1
) with
\alpha=k+\frac{1}{2}
. In particular, we immediately observe that
|\alpha|\geq\frac{1}{2}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Let us associate the symmetric preminimal operator to
\check{\tau}_{k}
as in (
3.5
)

\check{{\mathsf{T}}}_{0,k}^{\prime\prime}\psi:=\check{\tau}_{k}\psi,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,\check{{\mathsf{T}}}_{0,k}^{\prime\prime}:=C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{2}).

According to Subsection
3.2
the adjoint of
\check{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}:=(\check{{\mathsf{T}}}_{0,k}^{\prime\prime})^{*}
is given by

(4.5)

\check{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}\psi:=\check{\tau}_{k}\psi,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,\check{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}:=\big\{\psi\colon\psi,\check{\tau}_{k}\psi\in L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{2}),\psi\in{\rm AC}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})\big\}.

By Proposition
3.6
(i), the deficiency indices of
\check{{\mathsf{T}}}_{0,k}^{\prime\prime}
are
(1,1)
and
\{{\mathbb{C}},\check{\Gamma}_{0},\check{\Gamma}_{1}\}
with
\check{\Gamma}_{0},\check{\Gamma}_{1}\colon\mathrm{dom}\,\check{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
,
\check{\Gamma}_{0}\psi:=\psi_{1}(\omega)
,
\check{\Gamma}_{1}\psi:=\psi_{2}(\omega)
is a boundary triple for its adjoint
\check{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
.

Further, consider the auxiliary symmetric operator

(4.6)

\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}_{0,k}^{\prime\prime}=\check{{\mathsf{T}}}_{0,k}^{\prime\prime}\oplus\check{{\mathsf{T}}}_{0,k}^{\prime\prime}

\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
of

It is easy to see that the deficiency indices
of
\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}_{0,k}^{\prime\prime}

and that

\{{\mathbb{C}}^{2},\widehat{\Gamma}_{0},\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\}

\psi_{3}(\omega)\end{pmatrix}\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\psi:=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{2}(\omega)\\

in the sense of Definition

Let the Hermitian matrix

be defined by

\widehat{{\mathsf{A}}}_{\omega}:=\begin{pmatrix}\tan\omega&-\frac{{\mathsf{i}}}{\cos\omega}\\
\frac{{\mathsf{i}}}{\cos\omega}&\tan\omega\end{pmatrix}.

By Proposition

\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}\psi:=\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}\psi,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}:=\big\{\psi\in\mathrm{dom}\,\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}\colon\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\psi=\widehat{{\mathsf{A}}}_{\omega}\widehat{\Gamma}_{0}\psi\big\},

is self-adjoint in the Hilbert space

Let us define the multiplication operator
by a matrix-valued function in the Hilbert space

-\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\cot\theta-\frac{1}{\theta}\right)&-k\end{pmatrix}\psi.

It remains to notice that

The operator

can be viewed as a finite-rank perturbation in the sense of resolvent differences of an orthogonal sum of two self-adjoint extensions of the symmetric operator

In view of the decomposition (

\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
.
Moreover, for any

we find via integration by parts that

\begin{split}&({\mathsf{S}}\psi,\phi)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})}-(\psi,\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}\phi)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})}\\
&\qquad=\int_{0}^{\omega}\left(\psi_{2}^{\prime}\overline{\phi_{1}}-\psi_{1}^{\prime}\overline{\phi_{2}}+\psi_{4}^{\prime}\overline{\phi_{3}}-\psi_{3}^{\prime}\overline{\phi_{4}}+\psi_{2}\overline{\phi_{1}^{\prime}}-\psi_{1}\overline{\phi_{2}^{\prime}}+\psi_{4}\overline{\phi_{3}^{\prime}}-\psi_{3}\overline{\phi_{4}^{\prime}}\right){\mathsf{d}}\theta\\
&\qquad=\psi_{2}(\omega)\overline{\phi_{1}(\omega)}-\psi_{1}(\omega)\overline{\phi_{2}(\omega)}+\psi_{4}(\omega)\overline{\phi_{3}(\omega)}-\psi_{3}(\omega)\overline{\phi_{4}(\omega)}\\
&\qquad=-\left(\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}(\omega)\\
\psi_{3}(\omega)\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{2}(\omega)\\
\phi_{4}(\omega)\end{pmatrix}-\widehat{\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}(\omega)\\
\phi_{3}(\omega)\end{pmatrix}\right)_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}.\end{split}

From the above computation we conclude that the adjoint of
{\mathsf{S}}

is characterised by

{\mathsf{S}}^{*}\psi=\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}\psi,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{S}}^{*}=\big\{\psi\in\mathrm{dom}\,\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}\colon\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\psi=\widehat{{\mathsf{A}}}_{\omega}\widehat{\Gamma}_{0}\psi\big\}.

Hence,
{\mathsf{S}}^{*}
coincides with
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
and it is thus self-adjoint.
Therefore, the symmetric operator
{\mathsf{S}}
is essentially self-adjoint.
∎

Further we make use of the following Hardy inequality.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\omega\in(0,\pi/2]
.
For all
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\omega])
the following inequality
holds true:

(4.8)

\int_{0}^{\omega}|f^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{|f(\theta)|^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta+\frac{\pi^{2}}{16\omega^{2}}\int_{0}^{\omega}|f(\theta)|^{2}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta.

Proof.

To show (
4.8
) we exploit
the following Hardy inequality: for all
g\in H_{0}^{1}((0,\pi))

(4.9)

\int_{0}^{\pi}|g^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{|g(\theta)|^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\pi}|g(\theta)|^{2}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta.

Such inequality can be derived from the inequality

0\leq\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big|g^{\prime}(\theta)-\frac{\cos\theta}{2\sin{\theta}}g(\theta)\Big|^{2}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta

expanding the square and integrating by parts: for a detailed proof
and interesting details on how this inequality is related to
Bessel-type operators we refer to
[
GPS21
]
.
Let
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\omega])
and define

\widetilde{f}\in H_{0}^{1}((0,\pi)),\qquad\widetilde{f}(\theta):=\begin{cases}f(\frac{2\omega}{\pi}\theta),\quad&\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}],\\
f(\frac{2\omega}{\pi}(\pi-\theta)),\quad&\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi).\end{cases}

Since
\widetilde{f}
is symmetric in
(0,\pi)
with respect to the
point
\pi/2
, from (
4.9
) we have that

(4.10)

\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}|\widetilde{f}^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{|\widetilde{f}(\theta)|^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}|\widetilde{f}(\theta)|^{2}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta.

After a change of variables, (
4.10
) gives

(4.11)

\int_{0}^{\omega}|f^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{(\frac{\pi}{2\omega})^{2}}{\sin^{2}{\frac{\pi\theta}{2\omega}}}|f(\theta)|^{2}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\pi}{2\omega}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{\omega}|f(\theta)|^{2}\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta.

In order to conclude (
4.8
) from
(
4.11
)
we show that

(4.12)

\frac{(\frac{\pi}{2\omega})^{2}}{\sin^{2}{\frac{\pi\theta}{2\omega}}}\geq\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta},\quad\text{ for all }\theta\in(0,\omega).

Since
\theta\in(0,\omega)\subset(0,\pi/2)
, this is equivalent to
show that the functions

(4.13)

c_{\theta}(\omega):=\sin\theta-\frac{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2\omega}\theta)}{\frac{\pi}{2\omega}},\quad\quad\text{ for }\omega\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right]

are non-negative for all
\theta\in(0,\omega)
. We have that
c_{\theta}(\pi/2)=0
and

\frac{{\mathsf{d}}}{{\mathsf{d}}\omega}c_{\theta}(\omega)\leq 0
if and only if

\frac{\pi}{2\omega}\theta\leq\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2\omega}\theta\right),\quad\text{ for }\omega\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right],\,\theta\in(0,\omega),

that is true since
\tan 0=0
and
\frac{{\mathsf{d}}}{{\mathsf{d}}\varphi}(\tan\varphi)\geq 1
for
\varphi\in(0,\pi/2)
.
This shows (
4.12
) and completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\omega<\pi/2

(4.14)

\left\lVert\left({\mathsf{T}}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})}\geq\frac{\pi}{4\omega}\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})},\quad\text{ for all }\psi\in{\mathcal{M}}.

Proof.

Let
\psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4})^{\top}\in{\mathcal{M}}

and for convenience let us denote
\alpha:=k+1/2\in(\mathbb{Z}+1/2)
.
From (
4.3
) we have that

(4.15)

\begin{split}&\left\lVert\left({\mathsf{T}}_{k}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})}^{2}\!=\!\left\lVert\left(\tau_{k}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\!+\!\left\lVert\left(\tau_{k}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{3}\\
\psi_{4}\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}.\end{split}

Performing a long but elementary computation and thanks to integration by parts,
we have that for any
f,g\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\omega])

(4.16)

\begin{split}&\left\|\left(\tau_{k}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\begin{pmatrix}f\\
g\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\\
&\qquad=\int_{0}^{\omega}\left(|\alpha f(\theta)-g^{\prime}(\theta)-\alpha\cot\theta g(\theta)|^{2}+|f^{\prime}(\theta)-\alpha\cot\theta f(\theta)-\alpha g(\theta)|^{2}\right){\mathsf{d}}\theta\\
&\qquad=\int_{0}^{\omega}\left(|f^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}+|g^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}+\alpha^{2}(1+\cot^{2}\theta)\big(|f(\theta)|^{2}+|g(\theta)|^{2}\big)\right){\mathsf{d}}\theta\\
&\qquad\qquad-2\alpha{\rm Re}\,\int_{0}^{\omega}\left(f(\theta)\overline{g^{\prime}(\theta)}+f^{\prime}(\theta)\overline{g(\theta)}-\cot\theta\big(g^{\prime}(\theta)\overline{g(\theta)}-f^{\prime}(\theta)\overline{f(\theta)}\big)\right){\mathsf{d}}\theta\\
&\qquad=\int_{0}^{\omega}\left[|f^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}+|g^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}\right]\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{\omega}\left[\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\sin^{2}\theta}|f(\theta)|^{2}+\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{\sin^{2}\theta}|g(\theta)|^{2}\right]\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta-\alpha B(f,g)(\omega),\end{split}

with

B(f,g)(\omega):={\rm Re}\,\left[2f(\omega)\overline{g(\omega)}+\cot\omega|f(\omega)|^{2}-\cot\omega|g(\omega)|^{2}\right].

We underline that, performing the integration by parts, the boundary term
B(f,g)(\omega)

only has a contribution in
\omega
, since the functions
f
and

g
are
supported outside the origin.
From the boundary conditions in (
4.2
) and
(
4.4
), we get that

(4.17)

\displaystyle B(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})(\omega)+B(\psi_{3},\psi_{4})(\omega)

\displaystyle=2{\rm Re}\,\left[\psi_{1}(\omega)\overline{\psi_{2}(\omega)}+\psi_{3}(\omega)\overline{\psi_{4}(\omega)}\right]

\displaystyle+\cot\omega\left[|\psi_{1}(\omega)|^{2}+|\psi_{3}(\omega)|^{2}-|\psi_{2}(\omega)|^{2}-|\psi_{4}(\omega)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2{\rm Re}\,\left[-\left(\sin\omega\psi_{3}(\omega)-\cos\omega\psi_{4}(\omega)\right)\left(\cos\omega\overline{\psi_{3}(\omega)}+\sin\omega\overline{\psi_{4}(\omega)}\right)+\psi_{3}(\omega)\overline{\psi_{4}(\omega)}\right]

\displaystyle+\!\cot\omega\left[|\sin\omega\psi_{3}(\omega)-\cos\omega\psi_{4}(\omega)|^{2}\!+\!|\psi_{3}(\omega)|^{2}\!-\!|\cos\omega\psi_{3}(\omega)+\sin\omega\psi_{4}(\omega)|^{2}\!-\!|\psi_{4}(\omega)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2\sin\omega\cos\omega\left[|\psi_{4}(\omega)|^{2}-|\psi_{3}(\omega)|^{2}\right]+4\cos^{2}\omega{\rm Re}\,[\psi_{3}(\omega)\overline{\psi_{4}(\omega)}]

\displaystyle+\cot\omega\left[2\sin^{2}\omega\big(|\psi_{3}(\omega)|^{2}-|\psi_{4}(\omega)|^{2}\big)-4\sin\omega\cos\omega{\rm Re}\,[\psi_{3}(\omega)\overline{\psi_{4}(\omega)}]\right]=0,

so from (
4.15
), (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) we
conclude that

(4.18)

\begin{split}\lVert({\mathsf{T}}_{k}-\tfrac{1}{2})\psi\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})}^{2}=&\int_{0}^{\omega}\left[|\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}+|\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}+|\psi_{3}^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}+|\psi_{4}^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}\right]\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta\\
&+\int_{0}^{\omega}\left[\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\sin^{2}\theta}\left(|\psi_{1}(\theta)|^{2}+|\psi_{3}(\theta)|^{2}\right)\right]\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta\\
&+\int_{0}^{\omega}\left[\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{\sin^{2}\theta}\left(|\psi_{2}(\theta)|^{2}+|\psi_{4}(\theta)|^{2}\right)\right]\,{\mathsf{d}}\theta.\end{split}

We have that
\min[\alpha(\alpha-1),\alpha(\alpha+1)]\geq-1/4
: thanks to Lemma
4.2
, from (
4.18
)
we conclude that for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and

(4.19)

\left\lVert\left({\mathsf{T}}_{k}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})}\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{16\omega^{2}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})}^{2},\quad\text{ for all }\psi\in{\mathcal{M}}.

From (
4.19
) it is immediate to conclude (
4.14
).

(ii)

(4.24b)

\displaystyle(\lambda+k){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k}(\cos\omega)

\lambda

). It is straightforward to verify that

{\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}
has two eigenvalues
\lambda=\pm{\mathsf{i}}
and that the respective eigenvectors are given by

\xi_{\mathsf{i}}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\frac{\sin\omega-1}{\cos\omega}\end{pmatrix}\xi_{-{\mathsf{i}}}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\frac{\sin\omega+1}{\cos\omega}\end{pmatrix}.

Using these properties of the matrix
{\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}
, the boundary condition (
4.31
) and the
representations (
4.29
), (
4.30
) for
k\geq 0
we conclude that
if
k\geq 0
then
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
is an eigenvalue of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
if and only if it is the root of at least of one of the following equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(k-\lambda+1){\mathsf{P}}^{-k-1}_{\lambda-1}(\cos\omega)&=\frac{\sin\omega-1}{\cos\omega}{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k}(\cos\omega),\\
(k-\lambda+1){\mathsf{P}}^{-k-1}_{\lambda-1}(\cos\omega)&=\frac{\sin\omega+1}{\cos\omega}{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k}(\cos\omega).\end{cases}

Applying
[
GR07
, 8.735 (1)]
we simplify the above equations as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\lambda+k+1){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)={\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k}(\cos\omega),\\
(\lambda+k+1){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)=-{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k}(\cos\omega).\end{cases}

It follows from the first equations in (
4.29
) and (
4.30
) that the respective eigenfunctions are given as in item (i).

Again using these properties of the matrix
{\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}
, the boundary condition (
4.31
) and the
representations (
4.29
), (
4.30
) for
k\leq-1
we conclude that
if
k\leq-1
then
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
is an eigenvalue of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
if it is the root of at least one of the following equations

\begin{cases}(\lambda+k){\mathsf{P}}^{k}_{\lambda-1}(\cos\omega)=\frac{\cos\omega}{\sin\omega-1}{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k+1}(\cos\omega),\\
(\lambda+k){\mathsf{P}}^{k}_{\lambda-1}(\cos\omega)=\frac{\cos\omega}{\sin\omega+1}{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k+1}(\cos\omega).\end{cases}

Applying
[
GR07
, 8.735 (4)]
we simplify the above equations as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\lambda+k){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k}(\cos\omega)=-{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k+1}(\cos\omega),\\
(\lambda+k){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k}(\cos\omega)={\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k+1}(\cos\omega).\end{cases}

It follows from the second equations in (
4.29
) and (
4.30
) that the respective eigenfunctions are given as in item (ii).

It remains to show that the spectrum of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
is simple.
We will consider the case
k\geq 0
only, because the case
k\leq-1
can be analysed analogously. Since the eigenfunctions are characterized via the first equations in (
4.29
), (
4.30
) by two constants
c_{12}
and
c_{34}
leaving two degrees of freedom the multiplicity is bounded from above by
2
. Suppose that
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
of multiplicity
2
. Then
\lambda
is the root of both equations in (
4.22
)
and the respective linear independent
eigenfunctions are given by
(\pm{\mathsf{i}}\varphi)\oplus\varphi
with
\varphi
as in (
4.23
).
Hence, we conclude that
0\oplus\varphi
is also an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda
and from the boundary conditions in (
4.21
) we get that
\varphi(\omega)=(0,0)^{\top}
. In view of
\tau_{k}((\sin\theta)^{1/2}\varphi(\theta))=\lambda(\sin\theta)^{1/2}\varphi(\theta)
,
\theta\in(0,\omega)
we get by the unique solvability result
[
W03
, Satz. 15.4 (b)]
that
\varphi\equiv 0
,
which leads to a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\omega\in(0,\pi)\setminus\{\frac{\pi}{2}\}
.
The spectrum of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, is symmetric with respect to the origin. In particular,

(i)

For
k\geq 0
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a root of (
4.22a
) if and only if
-\lambda
is a root of (
4.22b
).

(ii)

For
k\leq-1
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a root of (
4.24a
) if and only if
-\lambda
is a root of (
4.24b
).

In particular,
0
is not an eigenvalue of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
.

Proof.

First of all, we remark that in view of (i) and (ii) the point
\lambda=0
can not be an eigenvalue of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
as otherwise it would be a double eigenvalue, which contradicts the simplicity of the spectrum shown in Proposition
4.5
.
Now we pass to the proofs of the items (i) and (ii).

(i) Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
be an eigenvalue of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
,
k\geq 0
. Then
\lambda
is a root either of (
4.22a
) or of (
4.22b
). Assume for definiteness that
\lambda
is a root of (
4.22a
);
i.e.

(4.32)

(\lambda+k+1){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)={\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k}(\cos\omega).

Our aim is to show that
-\lambda
is a root of the complementary equation (
4.22b
);
i.e.

(-\lambda+k+1){\mathsf{P}}_{-\lambda}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)=-{\mathsf{P}}_{-\lambda-1}^{-k}(\cos\omega).

Transforming the above equation using
[
GR07
, 8.733 (5)]
we need to show that

(4.33)

(\lambda-k-1){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)={\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k}(\cos\omega).

In view of
[
GR07
, 8.735 (2)]
and using (
4.32
) we get

(4.34)

\begin{split}(\lambda-k-1){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)&\!=\!(\lambda+k+1)\cos\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)-\sin\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k}(\cos\omega)\\
&\!=\!\cos\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k}(\cos\omega)-\sin\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k}(\cos\omega).\end{split}

Applying further the identity
[
GR07
, 8.735 (4)]

we find

(4.35)

{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k}(\cos\omega)=\cos\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k}(\cos\omega)-(\lambda-k-1)\sin\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)

Taking the difference of (
4.34
) and (
4.35
) we get

(\lambda-k-1){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)-{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k}(\cos\omega)=\sin\omega\left[(\lambda-k-1){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)-{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k}(\cos\omega)\right].

Hence, we conclude that (
4.33
) holds.

(ii)
Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
be an eigenvalue of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
,
k\leq-1
. Then
\lambda
is a root either of (
4.24a
) or of (
4.24b
). Assume for definiteness that
\lambda
is a root of (
4.24a
);

(4.36)

(\lambda+k){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k}(\cos\omega)=-{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k+1}(\cos\omega).

Our aim is to show that
-\lambda
is a root of the complementary equation (
4.24b
);
i.e.

(-\lambda+k){\mathsf{P}}_{-\lambda-1}^{k}(\cos\omega)={\mathsf{P}}_{-\lambda}^{k+1}(\cos\omega).

Transforming the above equation using
[
GR07
, 8.733 (5)]
we need to show that

(4.37)

(k-\lambda){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k}(\cos\omega)={\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k+1}(\cos\omega).

In view of
[
GR07
, 8.735 (1)]
and using (
4.36
) we get

(4.38)

\begin{split}(k-\lambda){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k}(\cos\omega)&\!=\!-(\lambda+k)\cos\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k}(\cos\omega)-\sin\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k+1}(\cos\omega)\\
&\!=\!\cos\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k+1}(\cos\omega)-\sin\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k+1}(\cos\omega).\end{split}

Applying further the identity
[
GR07
, 8.735 (3)]

we find

{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k+1}(\cos\omega)=\cos\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k+1}(\cos\omega)+(\lambda-k)\sin\omega{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k}(\cos\omega)

Taking the difference of (
4.38
) and (
4.39

(k-\lambda){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k}(\cos\omega)-{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k+1}(\cos\omega)=\sin\omega\left[(k-\lambda){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k}(\cos\omega)-{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k+1}(\cos\omega)\right].

Hence, we conclude that (

∎

Remark 4.7
.

The symmetry of the spectrum of
\widetilde{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, with respect to the origin can be alternatively shown via an anti-commutation relation. In this argument it is more convenient to work with the unitarily equivalent operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
.
Consider the matrix-valued function
M\colon[0,\omega]\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{2\times 2}

M=M(\theta):=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&-\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}

and define the unitary and self-adjoint operator

{\mathcal{U}}\colon L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\rightarrow L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4}),\qquad({\mathcal{U}}\psi)(\theta):=\big(M(\theta)\oplus(-M(\theta)\big)\psi(\theta).

It is straightforward to check the following anti-commutation relations
\tau_{k}M=-M\tau_{k}
and
{\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}M(\omega)=-M(\omega){\mathsf{A}}_{\omega}
. Hence, we conclude that
\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{k}=\mathrm{dom}\,({\mathsf{T}}_{k}{\mathcal{U}})
and that

{\mathsf{T}}_{k}{\mathcal{U}}=-{\mathcal{U}}{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
.
Thus, for any
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
the mapping
{\mathcal{U}}
is a bijection between
\ker({\mathsf{T}}_{k}-\lambda)
and
\ker({\mathsf{T}}_{k}+\lambda)
and therefore the spectrum of
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
is symmetric about the origin.

Now we can combine the symmetry of the spectrum shown above with the estimate in Proposition
4.3
in order to get a lower bound on the spectral gap for
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\omega<\pi/2
,

(4.40)

\|{\mathsf{T}}_{k}\psi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})}\geq\left(\frac{\pi}{4\omega}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\|\psi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})},\qquad\text{for all}\,\psi\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{k}.

In particular,
\sigma({\mathsf{T}}_{k})\cap[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Recall that
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
is unitarily equivalent to
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
.
Hence, by Corollary
4.6
the spectrum of
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
is symmetric with respect to the origin
and
0
is not an eigenvalue of
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
. Let
\lambda>0
be the smallest positive eigenvalue of
{\mathsf{T}}_{k}
. According to the bound in Proposition
4.3

we conclude that
|\lambda-\frac{1}{2}|\geq\frac{\pi}{4\omega}
. This condition implies that
\lambda\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4\omega}
and the inequality in the formulation of the proposition follows by the min-max principle.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

Depending on

, we can provide better estimates
allowing us to show that

.

5.
Decomposition of
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
in spherical coordinates

In this section we decompose the Dirac operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}
into the orthogonal sum of one-dimensional Dirac operators on the half-line and using this decomposition we prove the main results of the paper formulated in Theorems
2.1
,
2.3
, and
2.10
.

First of all, we observe that the cone can be expressed in the spherical coordinates as
{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}={\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega}
where
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega}:=[0,\omega)\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
is the spherical cap.
We introduce the spherical
L^{2}
-space on
{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}

L^{2}_{\rm sph}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega};r^{2}\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta{\mathsf{d}}\phi).

The Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\rm sph}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
can be decomposed into the tensor product of weighted
L^{2}
-spaces

(5.1)

L^{2}_{\rm sph}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};r^{2}{\mathsf{d}}r)\otimes L^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta{\mathsf{d}}\phi;{\mathbb{C}}^{4}).

Along with the spherical
L^{2}
-spaces we define the spherical first-order Sobolev space

(5.2)

H^{1}_{\rm sph}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})=\big\{u\in L^{2}_{\rm sph}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon\partial_{r}u,r^{-1}\nabla_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}u\in L^{2}_{\rm sph}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\big\},

where
\nabla_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}
is the gradient on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
.
Next, we introduce the unitary map

{\mathsf{V}}\colon L^{2}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\rm sph}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4}),\qquad({\mathsf{V}}u)(r,\theta,\phi):=u(r\sin\theta\cos\phi,r\sin\theta\sin\phi,r\cos\theta).

In particular, we have
{\mathsf{V}}(H^{1}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4}))=H^{1}_{\rm sph}({\mathcal{C}}_{\omega};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
.
By means of the map
{\mathsf{V}}
we define the operator which is unitarily equivalent to
{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}

\widetilde{{\mathsf{D}}}_{\omega}:={\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}{\mathsf{V}}^{-1},\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,\widetilde{{\mathsf{D}}}_{\omega}:={\mathsf{V}}(\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}).

According to
[
W03
, Satz 20.6]
the Dirac differential expression
{\mathcal{D}}
can be written in the spherical coordinates as follows

{\mathcal{D}}={\mathsf{i}}\alpha_{r}\left(-\partial_{r}-\frac{1}{r}+\frac{{\mathcal{K}}}{r}\right),

where
\alpha_{r}
is given by (
3.25
)
and where
{\mathcal{K}}
is the spin-orbit differential expression in (
3.24
).
With respect to the tensor product representation (
5.2
) we can decompose the Dirac operator
\widetilde{{\mathsf{D}}}_{\omega}
as

(5.3)

\widetilde{{\mathsf{D}}}_{\omega}u={\mathsf{i}}\alpha_{r}\left(\left(-\partial_{r}-\frac{1}{r}\right)\otimes{\mathsf{I}}+\frac{1}{r}\otimes{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}\right)u,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,\widetilde{{\mathsf{D}}}_{\omega}={\mathsf{V}}\big(\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{D}}_{\omega}\big),

where the spin-orbit operator
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}
acts in the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta{\mathsf{d}}\phi;{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
and is defined as

(5.4)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}f

\displaystyle=\!{\mathcal{K}}f,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\!\left\{f\!\in\!C^{\infty}(\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega}};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})\colon\!\left(\begin{smallmatrix}f_{1}(\omega,\phi)\\
f_{2}(\omega,\phi)\end{smallmatrix}\right)\!=\!\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega&-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega e^{-{\mathsf{i}}\phi}\\
-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega e^{{\mathsf{i}}\phi}&-{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}f_{3}(\omega,\phi)\\
f_{4}(\omega,\phi)\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\}.

In view of the above definition the domain of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{D}}}_{\omega}
can be characterised more explicitly as

(5.5)

\mathrm{dom}\,\widetilde{{\mathsf{D}}}_{\omega}=\{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,\infty)\times\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\omega})\colon u(r,\cdot,\cdot)\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega},\,\forall r>0\}.

Our next goal is to show that
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}
has an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions that correspond to real eigenvalues. The latter will also imply as a by-product that the operator
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}
is essentially self-adjoint in the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta{\mathsf{d}}\phi;{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
.
The Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta{\mathsf{d}}\phi;{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
can be further decomposed as the tensor product

L^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta{\mathsf{d}}\phi;{\mathbb{C}}^{4})=L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta)\otimes L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1};{\mathbb{C}}^{4}).

We consider the orthonormal basis
\{h_{k1},h_{k2},h_{k3},h_{k4}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})

given by

\displaystyle h_{k1}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\begin{pmatrix}e^{{\mathsf{i}}k\phi}\\
0\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad

\displaystyle h_{k2}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
e^{{\mathsf{i}}(k+1)\phi}\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle h_{k3}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
e^{{\mathsf{i}}k\phi}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad

\displaystyle h_{k4}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
0\\
e^{{\mathsf{i}}(k+1)\phi}\end{pmatrix}.

Consider the subspace
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}={\rm span}\,\{h_{k1},h_{k2},h_{k3},h_{k4}\}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, of
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
. Now, we have the decomposition

(5.6)

L^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta{\mathsf{d}}\phi;{\mathbb{C}}^{4})=\bigoplus_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big(L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta)\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\big)

and the isomorphism

(5.7)

L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta)\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\simeq L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta;{\mathbb{C}}^{4}).

By the spectral theorem the family of eigenfunctions of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
constructed in Proposition
4.5
upon normalization constitutes an orthonormal basis in the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta;{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
.
In view of the orthogonal decomposition (
5.6
)
and the isomorphism (
5.7
) we can construct an orthonormal basis in the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta{\mathsf{d}}\phi;{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
by transplanting the basis of normalized eigenfunctions of
\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
into the respective fiber
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta)\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
in the decomposition (
5.6
).
To this aim recall the definition of the following discrete subsets of the real axis:

(5.8)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\big\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\colon(\lambda+k+1){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{-k-1}(\cos\omega)={\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{-k}(\cos\omega)\big\},

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\geq 0,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\big\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\colon(\lambda+k){\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda-1}^{k}(\cos\omega)=-{\mathsf{P}}_{\lambda}^{k+1}(\cos\omega)\big\},

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq-1.

Let us introduce the following two-component functions
\{\Phi_{k,\lambda}^{\pm}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}}
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega}

\displaystyle\Phi_{k,\lambda}^{\pm}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathsf{P}}^{-k}_{\pm\lambda-1}(\cos\theta)e^{{\mathsf{i}}k\phi}\\
(k\mp\lambda+1){\mathsf{P}}^{-k-1}_{\pm\lambda-1}(\cos\theta)e^{{\mathsf{i}}(k+1)\phi}\end{pmatrix},\qquad

\displaystyle k\geq 0,\,\lambda\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{k},

\displaystyle\Phi_{k,\lambda}^{\pm}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}(\lambda\pm k){\mathsf{P}}^{k}_{\pm\lambda-1}(\cos\theta)e^{{\mathsf{i}}k\phi}\\
\pm{\mathsf{P}}^{k+1}_{\pm\lambda-1}(\cos\theta)e^{{\mathsf{i}}(k+1)\phi}\end{pmatrix},\qquad

\displaystyle k\leq-1,\,\lambda\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}.

From Proposition
4.5
and Corollary
4.6

we get that the following family
\{\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{+},\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{-}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}}
of vector-valued functions on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega}
:

\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{\pm}=c_{k,\lambda}(\pm{\mathsf{i}}\Phi_{k,\lambda}^{\pm})\oplus\Phi_{k,\lambda}^{\pm},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\,\lambda\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{k},

is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{\omega};\sin\theta{\mathsf{d}}\theta{\mathsf{d}}\phi;{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
, where
\{c_{k,\lambda}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}}
are just normalizing constants.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and let
\lambda\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}
be fixed. Then the following hold:

(i)

\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{\pm}\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}
;

(ii)

{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{\pm}=\pm\lambda\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{\pm}
;

(iii)

{\mathsf{i}}\alpha_{r}\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{+}=-\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{-}
and

{\mathsf{i}}\alpha_{r}\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{-}=\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{+}
with
\alpha_{r}
as in (
3.25
).

Proof.

(i) The fact that
\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{\pm}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega}})
follows from the

C^{\infty}
-smoothness of the Ferrers functions and the behaviour of the Ferrers functions as
x\rightarrow 0^{+}
(
[
DLMF
, Eqs. (14.3.1) and (15.2.1)]
. Thus, it only remains to check the boundary condition.
Let use the shorthand
\Psi:=\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{\pm}
. In view of (
5.4
) we need first to check that
\Psi
satisfies

\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_{1}(\omega,\phi)\\
\Psi_{2}(\omega,\phi)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega&-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega e^{-{\mathsf{i}}\phi}\\
-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega e^{{\mathsf{i}}\phi}&-{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_{3}(\omega,\phi)\\
\Psi_{4}(\omega,\phi)\end{pmatrix}.

Taking the structure of
\Psi
into account, the latter is equivalent to

(5.9)

\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_{1}(\omega,0)\\
\Psi_{2}(\omega,0)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega&-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega\\
-{\mathsf{i}}\cos\omega&-{\mathsf{i}}\sin\omega\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_{3}(\omega,0)\\
\Psi_{4}(\omega,0)\end{pmatrix}.

By its construction
\Psi(\cdot,0)\in\mathrm{dom}\,\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
with
\mathrm{dom}\,\widetilde{{\mathsf{T}}}_{k}
specified in (
4.21
). Hence, it follows from (
4.21
) that (
5.9
) holds.

(ii)
Let
\Psi=\Psi_{k,\lambda}^{+}

Combining the expression for
\partial_{r}
in (
A.1
) and the expression for

Hence, we get the expression for

\sin\theta

\displaystyle\partial_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin\phi\sin\theta}\left(-\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}+\cos\phi\sin\theta\partial_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sin\phi\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}+\frac{\cos\phi}{\sin\phi}\left(\sin\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\sin\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}+\frac{\cos\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sin\phi\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}+\cos\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\cos\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}+\frac{\cos^{2}\phi}{\sin\phi\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}

\displaystyle=\cos\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\cos\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}-\frac{\sin\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}.

Finally, we obtain a formula for
\partial_{3}
substituting (
A.5
) and (
A.4
) into the expression for
\partial_{r}
in (
A.1
)

\displaystyle\partial_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\partial_{r}-\frac{\cos\phi\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\left(\cos\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\cos\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}-\frac{\sin\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\sin\phi\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\left(\sin\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\sin\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}+\frac{\cos\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\cos\theta}-\frac{\cos^{2}\phi\sin^{2}\theta}{\cos\theta}-\frac{\sin^{2}\phi\sin^{2}\theta}{\cos\theta}\right)\partial_{r}-\left(\cos^{2}\phi\sin\theta+\sin^{2}\phi\sin\theta\right)\frac{\partial_{\theta}}{r}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\cos\phi\sin\phi}{\cos\theta}-\frac{\sin\phi\cos\phi}{\cos\theta}\right)\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r}

\displaystyle=\cos\theta\partial_{r}-\frac{\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}.

Now we summarize the expressions that we obtained

(A.6)

\begin{cases}\partial_{1}=\cos\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\cos\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}-\frac{\sin\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r},\\
\partial_{2}=\sin\phi\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\frac{\sin\phi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}+\frac{\cos\phi}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial_{\phi}}{r},\\
\partial_{3}=\cos\theta\partial_{r}-\frac{\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r}.\end{cases}

Appendix B
Dirac systems

In this appendix we provide basic facts on deficiency indices of one-dimensional Dirac operators on intervals. We follow the constructions given in
[
W87
]
and
[
W03
, Chap. 15]
.

First, we introduce on the interval
{\mathcal{I}}:=(0,b)
with
b\in(0,\infty]
a class of one-dimensional first-order Dirac differential expressions

(B.1)

\tau f(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}f^{\prime}(x)+q(x)f(x),

where
q\colon{\mathcal{I}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2\times 2}
is measurable with
q(x)
symmetric almost everywhere in
{\mathcal{I}}
and
\|q(\cdot)\|\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}})
. Throughout this section we denote the inner product in
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

We associate with the differential expression
\tau
the maximal operator in the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})

(B.2)

{\mathsf{T}}f:=\tau f,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}=\{f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})\colon f\in{\rm AC}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2}),\tau f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})\},

where
{\rm AC}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
stands for the space of locally absolutely continuous two-component functions on the interval
{\mathcal{I}}
.

We define the preminimal operator in
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
by

(B.3)

{\mathsf{T}}_{0}^{\prime\prime}f:=\tau f,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{0}^{\prime\prime}=C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2}).

According to
[
W87
, Chap. 3]
the preminimal operator is closable and its closure

(B.4)

{\mathsf{T}}_{0}:=\overline{{\mathsf{T}}_{0}^{\prime\prime}}

is the minimal symmetric operator in
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
.

Let us define the Lagrange brackets for (locally) absolutely continuous functions
f,g\colon{\mathcal{I}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
by

(B.5)

[f,g]_{x}:=f_{2}(x)\overline{g_{1}(x)}-f_{1}(x)\overline{g_{2}(x)}.

Using the concept of the Lagrange bracket we can provide the Green’s identity for
{\mathsf{T}}
.

Proposition B.1

(Green’s formula,
[
W03
, Satz 15.3]
)
.

Let the maximal operator
{\mathsf{T}}
be as in (
B.2
). Let the Lagrange bracket
[f,g]_{x}
be as in (
B.5
).
Then for any
f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}
there exist the limits

[f,g]_{0}:=\lim_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}[f,g]_{x},\qquad[f,g]_{b}:=\lim_{x\rightarrow b^{-}}[f,g]_{x},

and it holds that

\langle{\mathsf{T}}f,g\rangle-\langle f,{\mathsf{T}}g\rangle=[f,g]_{b}-[f,g]_{0}.

The domain of the closed symmetric operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{0}
can be explicitly characterised.

Proposition B.2
.

[
W87
, Thm. 3.11]

For
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}
we set
{\mathcal{N}}_{z}:=\ker({\mathsf{T}}-z)
, then

\begin{split}\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}_{0}&=\left\{f\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}\colon[g,f]_{0}=[g,f]_{b}=0\,\,\forall\,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}\right\}\\
&=\left\{f\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}\colon[g,f]_{0}=[g,f]_{b}=0\,\,\forall\,g\in{\mathcal{N}}_{z}+{\mathcal{N}}_{\overline{z}}\right\}.\end{split}

The endpoints of the interval
{\mathcal{I}}
admit some classifications. First, one can classify whether the endpoints are regular or singular.

Definition B.3
.

Let the differential
\tau
be as in (
B.1
).
Then
\tau
is called
regular
at
x=0
if there exists
c\in{\mathcal{I}}
so that
\|q(\cdot)\|
is integrable on
[0,c]
.
The expression
\tau
is called
regular
at
x=b
if
b<\infty
and there exists
c\in{\mathcal{I}}
so that
\|q(\cdot)\|
is integrable on
[c,b]
.
If
\tau
is not regular, respectively, at
x=0
and/or at
x=b
, then it is called
singular
at
x=0
and/or
x=b
, respectively.

The functions in
\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}
have certain regularity in the neighbourhood of a regular endpoint. This is clarified in the next proposition.

Proposition B.4
.

[
W03
, Satz 15.4]

Let the maximal operator
{\mathsf{T}}
be as in (
B.2
).
Assume that
\tau
is regular at
x=b
. Then the following hold.

(i)

For any
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}
there exists a finite
limit
f(b):=\lim_{x\rightarrow b^{-}}f(x)

(ii)

The mapping
\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}\ni f\mapsto f(b)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is surjective.

Analogous statements hold if
\tau
is regular at
x=0
.

The connection between the maximal operator and the minimal and preminimal operators is revealed in the next proposition.

Proposition B.5
.

Let the maximal operator
{\mathsf{T}}
, the preminimal operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{0}^{\prime\prime}
and the minimal operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{0}
be as in (
B.2
), (
B.3
), and (
B.4
), respectively. Then

({\mathsf{T}}_{0}^{\prime\prime})^{*}={\mathsf{T}}_{0}^{*}={\mathsf{T}}.

Next we define the concepts of being right or left in
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
.

Definition B.6
.

Let
f\colon{\mathcal{I}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
be measurable. One says that
f
lies "left in
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
" if there exists
c\in{\mathcal{I}}
such that
f|_{(0,c)}\in L^{2}((0,c);{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
. One says that
f
lies "right in
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
" if there exists
c\in{\mathcal{I}}
such that
f|_{(c,b)}\in L^{2}((c,b);{\mathbb{C}}^{2})

The solutions of the differential equation
(\tau-z)f=0
obey a dichotomy with respect to the property stated in Definition
B.6
.

Proposition B.7

(Weyl’s alternative)
.

For any Dirac differential expression
\tau
as in (
B.1
) on the interval
{\mathcal{I}}
one of the following two alternatives holds.

(i)

For all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
every solution of
(\tau-z)f=0
lies left in
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
.

(ii)

For all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
there is at least one solution of
(\tau-z)f=0
that does not lie left in
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
. In this case there exists for any
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}
exactly one solution of
(\tau-z)f=0
(up to a constant factor) that lies "left in
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
".

The same alternative holds for “right in
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
”.

The Weyl’s alternative stated above yields yet another classification of the endpoints of the interval
{\mathcal{I}}
with respect to the differential expression
\tau
.

Definition B.8
.

If the first alternative in Proposition
B.7
holds, then one says that
\tau
is limit-circle at
x=0
(respectively, at
x=b
). If the second alternative in Proposition
B.7
holds, the one says that
\tau
is limit-point at
x=0
(respectively, at
x=b
).

The limit-point/limit-circle classification provides extra properties of functions in
\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}
at the endpoints and allows to compute the deficiency indices of the closed symmetric operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{0}
.

Proposition B.9
.

Let
\tau
be the Dirac differential expression as in (
B.1
) on the interval
{\mathcal{I}}
. Let the maximal operator
{\mathsf{T}}
and the minimal operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{0}
be as in (
B.2
) and (
B.4
), respectively.

(i)

If
\tau
is limit-point at
x=0
(respectively, at
x=b
) then
[f,g]_{0}=0
(respectively,
[f,g]_{b}=0
) for all
f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{T}}
.

(ii)

The deficiency indices of
{\mathsf{T}}_{0}
are

-

(2,2)
if
\tau
is limit-circle at
x=0
and
x=b
.

-

(1,1)
if
\tau
is limit-circle at either
x=0
or at
x=b
and limit-point at the other endpoint of
{\mathcal{I}}
.

-

(0,0)
if
\tau
is limit-point at both endpoints of
{\mathcal{I}}
.

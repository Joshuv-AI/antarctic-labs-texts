On A Class of Greedy Sparse Recovery Algorithms
Gang Li, Qiuwei Li, Shuang Li, Wu Angela Li

Abstract
Sparse signal recovery deals with finding the sparest solution of an under-determined linear system 𝒙 = 𝑸 ​ 𝒔 \bm{x}=\bm{Q}\bm{s} . In this paper, we propose a novel greedy approach to addressing the challenges from such a problem. Such an approach is based on a characterization of solutions to the system, which allows us to work on the sparse recovery in the 𝒔 \bm{s} -space directly with a given measure. With l 2 l_{2} -based measure, an orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP)-type algorithm is proposed, which significantly outperforms the classical OMP algorithm in terms of recovery accuracy while maintaining comparable computational complexity. An l 1 l_{1} -based algorithm, denoted as Alg G ​ L ​ 1 \text{Alg}_{GL1} , is derived. Such an algorithm significantly outperforms the classical basis pursuit (BP) algorithm. Combining with the CoSaMP-strategy for selecting atoms, a class of high performance greedy algorithms is also derived. Extensive numerical simulations on both synthetic and image data are carried out, with which the superior performance of our proposed algorithms is demonstrated in terms of sparse recovery accuracy and robustness against numerical instability of the system matrix 𝑸 \bm{Q} and disturbance in the measurement 𝒙 \bm{x} .



Keywords:
Sparse signal recovery; Greedy methods; Basis pursuit methods; Under-determined linear system; Low-rank interference; Compressed sensing.

1.1
Related work

To accurately solve the sparse recovery problem (
2

\bm{s}^{\star}
? Let
\text{spark}[\bm{Q}]
denote the smallest number of columns of
\bm{Q}
that are linearly dependent. It is well known that the following result
[
30

plays a key role in sparse recovery: Let
\bm{x}
be any
\kappa

\bm{Q}
, that is there exists a
\tilde{\bm{s}}
with
\|\tilde{\bm{s}}\|_{0}\leq\kappa
such that
\bm{x}=\bm{Q}\tilde{\bm{s}}

2

\bm{s}^{\star}=\tilde{\bm{s}}
, if and only if
\bm{Q}
satisfies

\text{spark}[\bm{Q}]>2\kappa.

(3)

Let
\kappa_{\bm{Q}}
be the largest integer that is not bigger than
(\text{spark}[\bm{Q}]-1)/2
. Then, any
\kappa
-sparse solution
\tilde{\bm{s}}
of
\bm{x}=\bm{Q}\bm{s}=\bm{Q}\tilde{\bm{s}}
is the unique solution of (
2

\kappa\leq\kappa_{\bm{Q}}
, which is assumed in the sequel.

Another very important concept is the

(RIP), introduced in
[
31
]

a matrix
\bm{Q}
is said to satisfy the
(\kappa,\delta)
-RIP with

(1-\delta)\|\bm{s}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\|\bm{Q}\bm{s}\|^{2}_{2}\leq(1+\delta)\|\bm{s}\|^{2}_{2},~~~\forall~\bm{s}\in\Sigma_{\kappa},

(4)

where
\Sigma_{\kappa}
denotes the set of all
\kappa
-sparse signals in
\bm{Q}
. It can be seen that if
\bm{Q}
satisfies the
(2\kappa,\delta)
-RIP, then any
2\kappa
columns of
\bm{Q}
are linearly independent, and hence condition (
3

\bm{x}=\bm{Q}\bm{s}^{\star}
with
\|\bm{s}^{\star}\|_{0}\leq\kappa
, then, the underdetermined system
\bm{x}=\bm{Q}\bm{s}
has a unique
\kappa
-sparse solution, which is
\bm{s}^{\star}
.

As an NP-hard problem, (

Iteratively re-weighted approaches

This class includes basis pursuit (BP)-based algorithms, which rely on replacing the

\displaystyle\bm{s}^{\ast}\triangleq\arg\min_{\bm{s}}\|\bm{s}\|_{1}~~~\text{s.t}~~~\bm{x}=\bm{Q}\bm{s}.

\displaystyle\bm{s}^{\ast}\triangleq\arg\min_{\bm{s}}\|\bm{s}\|_{\chi}~~\text{s.t.}~~\bm{x}=\bm{Q}\bm{s}.

1.1.2
Greedy methods

By nature, sparse recovery aims to identify the support
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
of the
\kappa
-sparse vector
\bm{s}^{\star}
underlying the measurement
\bm{x}
. Thus, one sparse algorithm differs from another in how it detects the support. The iteratively re-weighted methods estimate the support
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
through a global search in the
\bm{s}
-space and then select the indices of the
\kappa
largest entries in magnitude from their minimizer
\bm{s}^{\ast}
.

The greedy algorithms, as described in
[
44
,
45
]
, intends to address the sparse recovery problem (
2
) by solving
the following alternative problem:

\displaystyle\bm{s}^{\#}\triangleq\min_{\bm{s}}\|\bm{x}-\bm{Q}\bm{s}\|^{2}_{2}~~~\text{s.t.}~~~\|\bm{s}\|_{0}\leq\kappa,

where
\kappa
is the given sparsity level. The
\kappa
non-zero entries of
\bm{s}^{\#}
are identified sequentially through a procedure that minimizes residual.
Such a method is known as the orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP) method.

The OMP algorithm extracts the
\kappa
indices one by one iteratively. The
k
th atom, indexed by
i_{k}
, is identified as follows:

i_{k}\triangleq\arg\min_{i,\beta}\|\bm{r}_{k-1}-\beta\bm{Q}(:,i)\|^{2}_{2}.

(8)

Here,
\bm{r}_{k-1}
is the residual obtained at the
(k-1)
th iteration:

\bm{v}

\bm{r}_{k-1}=\bm{x}-\bm{Q}(:,\mathcal{I}_{k-1})\bm{\beta}_{k-1},

1.2
Problem formulation and contribution

It should be pointed out that
the iteratively re-weighted algorithms provide
state-of-the-art
sparse recovery performance. However, they are computationally demanding. In particular, IRLS-based algorithms, though much faster than the IRBP-based ones, can encounter numerical issues when the
\bm{Q}
matrix is ill-conditioned (See (
7
)). By contrast, OMP is significantly more efficient.
The most computationally demanding stage involves solving the least squares problem to update the non-zero entries. A class of iterative methods, such as gradient pursuit (GP) and conjugate gradient pursuit (CGP), can be used to address this issue, as described in
[
46
]
. To improve the recovery accuracy and update the index set of the
\kappa
non-zero entries more efficiently, one approach is to select multiple entries at once, rather than just one as in OMP. This leads to the development of stage-wise OMP (StOMP)
[
47
]
, compressive sampling match pursuit (CoSaMP)
[
48
]
, and stagewise weak GP (StWGP)
[
49
]
. A comprehensive survey of these issues as well as performance guarantees can be found in
[
50
]
.

As well-known, the greedy methods can not yield a comparable sparse recovery performance to the iteratively re-weighted ones. Suppose that
\mathcal{I}_{k-1}
has its
k-1
indices that all fall within the support
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
. Then,
\bm{r}_{k-1}=\bm{Q}\bm{s}-\bm{Q}(:,\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}})\bm{\beta}_{k-1}\triangleq\bm{Q}\tilde{\bm{s}}
, and this new system has a unique sparsest
solution, denoted as
\tilde{\bm{s}}^{\star}
, with
\|\tilde{\bm{s}}^{\star}\|_{0}\leq\kappa
. The
l_{2}
-norm based minimization (
8
) can not ensure that the obtained index
i_{k}
falls into
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
because the cost function is a complicated function of the true indices to be detected and such a function changes with
\bm{\beta}_{k-1}
. This is the main reason why the OMP algorithm cannot generally yield a sparse recovery as accurate as the iteratively re-weighted algorithms, prompting the proposal of its variants such as CoSaMP and StOMP.

The question we ask ourselves: Can we transform the system
\bm{r}_{k-1}=\bm{Q}\tilde{\bm{s}}
into
\bm{s}
domain by characterizing its set of solutions and then work directly on the sparse recovery problem? If an estimate of
\tilde{\bm{s}}^{\star}
is achieved, the next index
i_{k}
can be selected as the one that corresponds to the largest entry in magnitude of this estimate. Intuitively, by doing so the accuracy of index detection can be improved. This motivates us to deal with the sparse recovery problem on the
\bm{s}
-space,
instead of the measurement
\bm{x}
-space.

The main objective of this paper is to derive a class of greedy algorithms that operate directly in the
\bm{s}
-domain and outperform classical greedy algorithms (i.e., OMP, CoSaMP) in terms of sparse recovery accuracy while maintaining similar implementation complexity. Additionally, these algorithms offer greater robustness against the numerical instability caused by
\bm{Q}
and interferences in the signal
\bm{x}
compared to the classical algorithms, especially the IRLS-based ones. Our contributions are summarized as follows.

•

Based on a characterization of solutions to equation (
1
), we propose a class of greedy sparse recovery approaches for a given sparsity measure. Such an approach allows us to work directly in the
\bm{s}
-space;

•

Two algorithms, denoted as
\text{Alg}_{GL2}

and
\text{Alg}_{GL1}
,
are derived based on
l_{2}
and
l_{1}
, respectively. Despite having nearly the same computational complexity,
\text{Alg}_{GL2}
significantly outperforms the classical OMP algorithm and is comparable to the classical BP algorithm in terms of recovery accuracy. Meanwhile, the
\text{Alg}_{GL1}
algorithm significantly surpasses the classical OMP, BP
C
, and CoSaMP algorithms in terms of sparse recovery accuracy;

•

An improved version of
\text{Alg}_{GL1}
has been derived, denoted as the
\text{Alg}_{GLQ}
algorithm, in which
l_{q}
-minimization is used to enhance the sparsity. Fast variants of the algorithms
\text{Alg}_{GL1}
and
\text{Alg}_{GLQ}
, denoted as
\text{Alg}^{F}_{GL1}
and
\text{Alg}^{F}_{GLQ}
, are developed to accelerate
\text{Alg}_{GL1}
and
\text{Alg}_{GLQ}
by selecting multiple atoms per iteration;

•

It is shown that the proposed algorithms such as
\text{Alg}_{GL1}
,
\text{Alg}_{GLQ}
and their fast variants can be used to deal with low-rank interferences in the measurements effectively, which owes to the proposed characterization and is a significant advantage over the classical algorithms.

All claims have been validated through extensive numerical simulations on both synthetic and image data.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

2.1
The proposed greedy approach

Suppose that

\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}

First of all, it follows from
\bm{x}=\bm{Q}\bm{s}
that the residual is given by

\bm{x}-\beta\bm{Q}(:,i_{1})=\bm{Q}\bm{s}-\beta\bm{Q}(:,i_{1})

\bm{x}-\beta\bm{Q}(:,i_{1})=\bm{Q}\bm{s}_{1}

\bm{s}_{1}
, according to the proposed characterization (

\displaystyle\bm{s}_{1}

\displaystyle=\bm{V}_{1}\bm{T}_{0}(\bm{x}-\beta\bm{Q}(:,i_{1}))+\bm{W}\bm{z}=\bm{s}_{0}+\bm{W}\bm{z}-\beta\bm{V}_{1}\bm{T}_{0}\bm{Q}(:,i_{1})=\bm{s}(\bm{z})-\beta\bm{V}_{1}\bm{T}_{0}\bm{Q}(:,i_{1}),

where
\bm{s}(\bm{z})=\bm{s}_{0}+\bm{W}\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}

Recall that

\displaystyle\bm{s}_{0}=\bm{V}_{1}\bm{V}^{\top}_{1}\bm{s}^{\star}\triangleq\bar{\bm{Q}}\bm{s}^{\star},\quad\bm{V}_{1}\bm{T}_{0}\bm{Q}(:,i_{1})=\bar{\bm{Q}}(:,i_{1}),

Performance Analysis of the Proposed Greedy Approach

From the numerical example presented above, we observe that the proposed
\text{Alg}_{GL2}
algorithm significantly outperforms the classical

and even surpasses
\text{BP}_{C}
. Meanwhile, as expected, the proposed

3.1
Approach analysis

As understood, greedy methods, including our proposed approach (
2.1
), are designed based on minimizing a residual. Consequently, the generated sequence of residuals is non-increasing.
Although the residuals converge, most greedy algorithms are expected to yield the unique solution
\bm{s}^{\star}
within a finite number of iterations.

First of all, we present the following result associated with the proposed greedy approach (
2.1
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{k}
denote the set of indices detected, and let
(\tilde{\bm{\beta}}_{k},\tilde{\bm{z}}_{k})
be obtained using the proposed greedy approach (
2.1
). Furthermore, let
\tilde{\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{s}(\tilde{\bm{z}}_{k})-\bar{\bm{Q}}_{k}\tilde{\bm{\beta}}_{k}
be the residual, and denote
\hat{\bm{s}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times 1}
as the vector satisfying

\hat{\bm{s}}_{k}(\mathcal{I}^{c}_{k})=\bm{0},~~\hat{\bm{s}}_{k}(\mathcal{I}_{k})=\tilde{\bm{\beta}}_{k}.

Then, when
\tilde{\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{0}
for
k\leq 2\kappa
, we have

\hat{\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{s}^{\star}

as long as
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{k}
.

Proof
: First of all, we note that
\tilde{\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{0}
, that is

\bm{s}_{0}+\bm{W}\tilde{\bm{z}}_{k}-\bar{\bm{Q}}(:,\mathcal{I}_{k})\tilde{\bm{\beta}}_{k}=\bm{0},

which is equivalent to
\bar{\bm{Q}}(\bm{s}^{\star}-\hat{\bm{s}}_{k})+\bm{W}\tilde{\bm{z}}_{k}=\bm{0}
. It then follows from
\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{V}_{1}={\bf 0}
and
\bar{\bm{Q}}=\bm{V}_{1}\bm{V}^{\top}_{1}
that

\tilde{\bm{z}}_{k}=\bm{0}~\text{and}~\bm{V}^{\top}_{1}(\bm{s}^{\star}-\hat{\bm{s}}_{k})=\bm{0}.

We note that
\bm{s}^{\star}-\hat{\bm{s}}_{k}
is
2\kappa
-sparse when the condition given in the theorem holds and
\text{spark}[\bm{V}^{\top}_{1}]=\text{spark}[\bm{Q}]>2\kappa
due to the assumption of uniqueness on
\bm{s}^{\star}
. Thus,
\bm{V}^{\top}_{1}(:,\mathcal{I}_{k})
is of full (column) rank, and hence the above equation leads to
\bm{s}^{\star}-\hat{\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{0}
. This completes the proof.

In fact, the claims in Theorem
1
also hold for the classical
\text{OMP}_{C}
algorithm, in which the upper bound for the number of iterations is set to
\kappa
. This means that
\text{OMP}_{C}
must select an index that falls within
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
, which precludes the possibility of
\text{OMP}_{C}
making some mistakes in selecting indices. However, these mistakes can be rectified by taking more than
\kappa
iterations
[
50
]
. In general, for
\mathcal{I}_{k}
with
\kappa<k\leq 2\kappa
, nothing can guarantee whether
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{k}
holds unless
\|\tilde{\bm{\beta}}_{k}\|_{0}=\kappa
is verified.

The primary advantage of the proposed greedy approach over classical methods, such as
\text{OMP}_{C}
and
CoSaMP
, lies in its index selection strategy. In the proposed greedy approach (
2.1
), the next index
i_{k+1}
is chosen as the one corresponding to the largest absolute entry of the residual
\tilde{\bm{s}}_{k}
. As demonstrated in Remark
2.1
and the surrounding discussion, this selection ensures that
i_{k+1}
belongs to
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
.
In contrast,
\text{OMP}_{C}
determines
i_{k+1}
by minimizing
\|\bm{r}_{k}-\beta\bm{Q}(:,i)\|^{2}_{2}
with respect to
\beta
for
i\in\mathcal{I}^{c}_{k}
. However, such a selection does not necessarily guarantee that
i_{k+1}
belongs to
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
, even if
\mathcal{I}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
, as the minimizer of
\|\bm{r}_{k}-\beta\bm{Q}(:,i)\|^{2}_{2}
may not correspond to an index within
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
.

Furthermore, let
\mathcal{I}_{k}
be the detected indices and
\mathcal{I}^{\ast}_{k}
be a subset of
\mathcal{I}_{k}
, which contains
k^{\star}
elements with
\mathcal{I}^{\ast}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
. Using similar arguments to those in Remark
2.1
, we have

\{\tilde{\bm{\beta}}_{k},\tilde{\bm{z}}_{k}\}\triangleq\arg\min_{\bm{\beta},\bm{z}}\|\bm{s}(\bm{z})-\bar{\bm{Q}}(:,\mathcal{I}_{k})\bm{\beta}\|_{0},

for which the residual is

\tilde{\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{s}(\tilde{\bm{z}}_{k})-\bar{\bm{Q}}(:,\mathcal{I}_{k})\tilde{\bm{\beta}}_{k}=\bm{s}^{\star}_{k^{\star}}

as long as

k^{\star}>k-k^{\star}~~\Rightarrow~~k^{\star}>k/2

holds, where
\bm{s}^{\star}_{k^{\star}}(l)=0,~l\in\mathcal{I}^{\ast}_{k}
and otherwise,
\bm{s}^{\star}_{k^{\star}}(l)=\bm{s}^{\star}(l)
. This implies that with
\chi
taking
l_{0}
, the index
i_{k+1}
of the absolutely largest entry of the residual
\bm{s}^{\star}_{k}
should fall within
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
, provided that more than half of the
k
detected indices belong to
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
. In other words, when the first few indices are detected correctly, the proposed greed approach tolerates a small number of mistakes in index detection. This is indeed a remarkable property.

Remark 3.1
.

The conclusion drawn above is based on the use of the ideal sparsity measure

is used. Indeed, the excellent performance of
\text{Alg}_{GL1}

The condition (

\bm{s}^{\star}
. As shown in
[

[

]
and
[
54

{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}\end{array}\right]\bm{V}^{\top},

where
\bm{V}
is orthonormal. As ETFs exist only for some pairs

, tight-frames provide an alternative for designing sparse recovery systems
[
55
,
56
,
57
]

Our proposed
\text{Alg}_{GL2}

\bm{s}_{0}(\bm{x})=\bar{\bm{Q}}\bm{s}

•

\mu[\bm{Q}]

•

The classical
\text{OMP}_{C}

\bm{r}_{k}=\bm{Q}(\bm{s}^{\star}-\tilde{\bm{s}}_{k})\triangleq\bm{Q}\Delta\bm{s}

is uncorrelated with a zero-mean. Thus,

\bm{R}(l,l)=\sigma^{2}_{l},~\forall~l

The proposed
\text{Alg}_{GL1}

\text{Alg}_{GL1}
is
designed to solve the minimization problems of the form (

In this section, we derive a class of efficient algorithms that adopt an atom update scheme similar to that of CoSaMP and explore strategies for handling cases where the measurements are contaminated by noise.

4.1
Iteratively re-weighted approach to (

Note that the cost function

\bm{\beta}\end{array}\right],

\chi

Thus, the key to (

(30)

Here, we consider two approximating functions defined before:

\phi_{1}(t)=|t|,~~\phi_{3}(t)=(|t|^{2}+\epsilon)^{q/2},

where

When

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{z}}^{(k+1)}\triangleq

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\bar{\bm{z}}}\|\bar{\bm{s}}_{0}+\bar{\bm{\Omega}}\bar{\bm{z}}\|^{2}_{2},\quad\bm{s}^{(k+1)}=\bm{s}_{0}+\bm{\Omega}\bar{\bm{z}}^{(k+1)},

\bm{D}^{(k)}_{2}(l,l)=\sqrt{[|\bm{s}^{(k)}(l)|^{2}+\epsilon]^{q/2-1}},~\forall~l.

\bar{\bm{z}}^{(k+1)}=-(\bar{\bm{\Omega}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{\Omega}})^{-1}\bar{\bm{\Omega}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{s}}_{0}.

(33)

4.3
Robust sparse recovery algorithms against low-rank disturbance

So far, our discussion has been limited to the case where the measurement signal
\bm{x}
is strictly sparse. Now, we consider a more practical scenario where the signal
\bm{x}
is of the following form

\bm{x}=\bm{Q}\bm{s}+\bm{e}\triangleq\bm{x}_{0}+\bm{e},

(34)

where
\bm{e}
represents a disturbance that affects the recovery of the true sparse vector
\bm{x}_{0}
from
\bm{x}
.

The objective here is to separate
\bm{x}_{0}
and
\bm{e}
from a
\bm{x}
given. A traditional approach to address this problem is to first obtain a primary estimate
\bm{s}^{\ast}
of the true
\bm{s}^{\star}
underlying
\bm{x}_{0}
by applying a sparse recovery algorithm to
\bm{x}
. Then, defining
\mathcal{I}
as the set of indices corresponding to the
\kappa
largest entries of
\bm{s}^{\ast}
in magnitude, an estimate
\hat{\bm{x}}_{0}
of the clean
\bm{x}_{0}
is given by

\hat{\bm{x}}_{0}=\bm{Q}\hat{\bm{s}}^{\star},

(35)

where
\hat{\bm{s}}^{\star}
is
\kappa
-sparse and satisfies
\hat{\bm{s}}^{\star}(\mathcal{I})=\bm{\beta}^{\ast}
, with

\bm{\beta}^{\ast}\triangleq\arg\min_{\bm{\beta}}||\bm{x}-\bm{Q}(:,\mathcal{I})\bm{\beta}||^{2}_{2}.

This estimation procedure corresponds to the classical Oracle estimator
[
31
]
when
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
. In general, the classical sparse recovery algorithms are highly sensitive to noise
\bm{e}
. Even when
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
, the obtained estimate
\hat{\bm{x}}_{0}
does not necessarily equal
\bm{x}_{0}
unless
\bm{e}=\bm{0}
.

There are scenarios, in which
\bm{e}
is often modeled as a low-rank signal
[
59
,
60
]
:

\bm{e}=\bm{\Gamma}\bm{\alpha}^{\star},

(36)

where
\bm{\Gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N_{e}}
with
N_{e}<<N
is a matrix satisfying
\bm{\Gamma}^{\top}\bm{\Gamma}=\bm{I}_{N_{e}}
, and
\bm{\alpha}^{\star}
is an unknown vector. In multiple measurement vector models, (
34
) transforms into a low-rank and sparse decomposition problem, a framework that has been extensively studied in the literature
[
61
,
62
,
63
,
64
,
65
]
. Similar to the Robust PCA (RPCA)-based approach introduced in
[
61
]
, most of these works focus on the special case where
\bm{Q}=\bm{I}_{N}
, investigating various methods for separating the low-rank and sparse components. In this paper, we address the problem of recovering the clean signal
\bm{x}_{0}
from
\bm{x}
as given in (
34
), with
\bm{e}
modeled by (
36
). We consider the scenario where both
\bm{Q}
and
\bm{\Gamma}
are known, with
\bm{Q}
having dimensions
N\times L
and assuming
N_{e}<<N\leq L
.

Note
\bm{x}-\bm{e}=\bm{Q}\bm{s}
. Under our proposed characterization, we have
\bm{s}=\bm{T}_{0}(\bm{x}-\bm{e})+\bm{W}\bm{z}
, and hence

\bm{s}=\bm{V}_{1}\bm{T}_{0}\bm{x}+\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{W}&-\bm{V}_{1}\bm{T}_{0}\bm{\Gamma}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{z}\\
\bm{\alpha}\end{array}\right]\triangleq\bm{s}_{0}+\bm{W}_{c}\bm{z}_{c}.

Based on this formulation, we propose the following robust approach to sparse recovery

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\star}_{c}

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\bm{z}_{c}}\|\bm{s}_{0}+\bm{W}_{c}\bm{z}_{c}\|_{0}\quad\mapsto~~~~\bm{s}^{\star}=\bm{s}_{0}+\bm{W}_{c}\bm{z}^{\star}_{c}.

(37)

Let
\bm{W}_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times L_{c}}
. It was shown in
[
58
]
that (
37
) has a unique
\kappa
-solution
\bm{s}^{\star}
if and only if all the
(L-2\kappa)\times L_{c}
submatrices of
\bm{W}_{c}
are of rank
L_{c}
, which holds when
\bm{W}_{c}=\bm{W}
. Since, in general,
L_{c}=L-N+N_{e}
, ensuring this condition requires that

L-2\kappa\geq L_{c}
, which simplifies to

2\kappa+N_{e}\leq N.

As observed, (
37
) has the same form as (
10
). Consequently, it can be addressed using classical methods such as
\text{BP}_{alt}
and the just derived
\text{IRLS}_{LQ-alt}
. More interestingly, with
(\bm{W},\bm{z})
replaced by
(\bm{W}_{c},\bm{z}_{c})
, our proposed greedy algorithms such as
\text{Alg}_{GL1}
,
\text{Alg}_{GLQ}
,
\text{Alg}^{F}_{GL1}
and
\text{Alg}^{F}_{GLQ}
, can also be applied to solve (
37
).

Remark 4.3
.

It is important to highlight the following key points:

•

The results obtained above indicate that the true support
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
can be recovered with high probability using our proposed algorithms, even in the presence of disturbance
\bm{e}
. This demonstrates a significant advantage of our proposed sparse model
\bm{s}=\bm{s}_{0}(\bm{x})+\bm{W}\bm{z}
over the classical formulation
\bm{x}=\bm{Q}\bm{s}
;

•

Let
\bm{s}^{\ast}
be the primary estimate of
\bm{s}^{\star}
obtained using one of our proposed algorithms. Suppose that

- the set of indices corresponding to the
\kappa

\bm{s}^{\ast}
, is equal to
\mathcal{I}_{\bm{s}^{\star}}
. In this case, the estimate

can be computed using the classical Oracle estimator (

\bm{s}^{\star}=\bm{s}_{0}+\bm{W}_{c}\bm{z}^{\star}_{c}
and
\bm{s}^{\star}(\mathcal{I})=\bm{0}

\bm{0}=\bm{s}_{0}(\mathcal{I})+\bm{W}_{c}(\mathcal{I},:)\bm{z}^{\star}_{c}.

This result implies that the clean signal

•

Note

\tilde{\bm{s}}^{\star}\triangleq\arg\min_{\tilde{\bm{s}}}||\bm{D}_{0}\tilde{\bm{s}}||_{0}~~~\text{s.t.}~~\bm{x}=\tilde{\bm{Q}}\tilde{\bm{s}},

cannot be recovered using the classical Oracle estimator, as previously discussed.

Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have investigated the sparse signal recovery problems. Our study has led to several key findings and contributions that enhance the field of sparse signal recovery.
Based on a proposed system model, we have developed a novel approach leading to a class of greedy algorithms, including

•

three innovative greedy algorithms,
\text{Alg}_{GL2}
,
\text{Alg}_{GL1}
and
\text{Alg}_{GLQ}
, which significantly improve the accuracy of classical methods such as OMP and BP;

•

two fast algorithms,
\text{Alg}^{F}_{GL1}
and
\text{Alg}^{F}_{GLQ}
, designed to accelerate
\text{Alg}_{GL1}
and
\text{Alg}_{GLQ}
, achieving performance that even surpasses
state-of-the-art
sparse recovery methods.

Furthermore, the five proposed algorithms demonstrate excellent robustness against instability caused by the poor numerical properties of the system matrix
\bm{Q}
and measurement interferences – issues that significantly affect classical sparse recovery algorithms.

Future work will focus on further enhancing the algorithms’ efficiency and exploring their applications in more complex high-dimensional data analysis tasks, such as hyperspectral imaging, seismic data processing, and wireless communication. In conclusion, the proposed greedy algorithms represent a significant advancement in sparse recovery techniques. By combining theoretical rigor with practical efficiency, our work paves the way for future innovations and applications in the field of signal processing and beyond.

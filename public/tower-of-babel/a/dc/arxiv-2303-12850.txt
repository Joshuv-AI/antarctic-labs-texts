Karthekeyan Chandrasekaran, Chandra Chekuri, Samuel Fiorini, Shubhang Kulkarni, Stefan Weltge

Abstract
We consider the feedback vertex set problem in undirected graphs (FVS). The input to FVS is an undirected graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) with non-negative vertex costs. The goal is to find a minimum cost subset of vertices S ⊆ V S\subseteq V such that G − S G-S is acyclic. FVS is a well-known NP-hard problem and does not admit a ( 2 − ϵ ) (2-\epsilon) -approximation for any fixed ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 assuming the Unique Games Conjecture. There are combinatorial 2 2 -approximation algorithms [ BBF95 , BG96 ] and also primal-dual based 2 2 -approximations [ CGHW98 , Fuj99 ] . Despite the existence of these algorithms for several decades, there is no known polynomial-time solvable LP relaxation for FVS with a provable integrality gap of at most 2 2 . More recent work [ CM16 ] developed a polynomial-sized LP relaxation for a more general problem, namely Subset FVS, and showed that its integrality gap is at most 13 13 for Subset FVS, and hence also for FVS. Motivated by this gap in our knowledge, we undertake a polyhedral study of FVS and related problems. In this work, we formulate new integer linear programs (ILPs) for FVS whose LP-relaxation can be solved in polynomial time, and whose integrality gap is at most 2 2 . The new insights in this process also enable us to prove that the formulation in [ CM16 ] has an integrality gap of at most 2 2 for FVS. Our results for FVS are inspired by new formulations and polyhedral results for the closely-related pseudoforest deletion set problem (PFDS). Our formulations for PFDS are in turn inspired by a connection to the densest subgraph problem. We also conjecture an extreme point property for a LP-relaxation for FVS, and give evidence for the conjecture via a corresponding result for PFDS.

Pseudoforest Deletion Set Problem (PFDS).

A connected graph is a
pseudotree
if
it has exactly one cycle; in other words there is an edge whose removal
results in a spanning tree. A graph is a
pseudoforest
if every connected component is either acyclic or a pseudotree. For a graph
G=(V,E)
, a
subset
U\subseteq V
is a
pseudoforest deletion set
if
G-U

is a pseudoforest. In the pseudoforest deletion set problem (PFDS),
the input is an undirected graph
G=(V,E)
with non-negative vertex
costs
c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and the goal is to find a
pseudoforest deletion set of minimum cost, i.e.,
\min\left\{\sum_{u\in U}c(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}U\subseteq V\ \text{and $U$ is a pseudoforest deletion set for
$G$}\right\}
. Intuitively, one can see that PFDS is closely related to FVS:
we note that a feasible solution for FVS in a given graph
G
is a feasible
solution to the PFDS instance on
G
. Also, finding an FVS in a graph that is
a pseudoforest is easy: for each connected component that is a pseudotree, we remove
the cheapest vertex in its unique cycle.
PFDS and FVS are special cases of the more general

[

[

\ell
-pseudoforest deletion problem.

-approximation

1.1
Results

We begin with our results for PFDS. These results influence our results for FVS which will be discussed subsequently.

PFDS: ILP formulations and LP integrality gap.

We answer Question
2
affirmatively via a new ILP for PFDS.
Our ILP for PFDS is based on Charikar’s LP for the densest
subgraph problem (DSG)
[
Cha00
]
. In DSG, the input is
an undirected graph
G=(V,E)
and the goal is to find an induced subgraph
G[S]
of
maximum density where the density of
S\subseteq V
is defined
as
{|E(S)|}/{|S|}
. We define the density of
G
to be
\max\{|E[S]|/|S|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq V\}

its density is at most one. Thus, PFDS can equivalently be phrased as
the problem of finding a minimum cost subset of vertices to delete so
that the remaining graph has density at most one. Charikar formulated
an LP to compute the density of a graph. The dual of Charikar’s LP can
be interpreted as a fractional orientation problem. Using that dual,
we obtain an ILP for PFDS. We describe the details now. Via Charikar’s LP
and previous results, one can show that an unweighted graph
G
has density at most
\lambda

iff the edges of
G
can be fractionally oriented such that the total fractional in-degree at
every vertex is at most
\lambda

y_{e,u}

and
y_{e,v}
to denote the fractional amount of
e
that is oriented towards
u
and
v

respectively. We recall that in PFDS the goal is to remove vertices such that
the residual graph has density at most
1

A set function

We will rely on the following property:

Proposition 1

Let

w\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

for all
S\subseteq V

Two sets
A
and
B
are said to

if they have a non-empty intersection and neither set is contained in the other, i.e.

For a ground set

. We need the following proposition on chain families. This is well-known but we give
a proof for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 2

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a chain family and
A
be a subset that crosses some set
B\in{\mathcal{C}}
. Then, the number of sets in
{\mathcal{C}}
crossed by
A\cup B
and
A\cap B
is strictly less than the number of sets in
{\mathcal{C}}
crossed by
A
.

Proof.

Our strategy will be to pick an arbitrary set in
{\mathcal{C}}
that crosses the set
A\cup B
(resp.
A\cap B
) and show that this picked set also crosses the set
A
. The claim then follows since the set
B
crosses the set
A
but not the set
A\cup B
(resp
A\cap B
).

Let
P\in{\mathcal{C}}
be an arbitrary set that crosses the set
A\cup B
. Since both

, it must be that either
P\subset B
or
B\subset P
. First, we consider the case where
P\subset B
. Then

and thus does not cross the set
A\cup B
as it is contained in it. This contradicts the choice of
P
. Next, we consider the case where
B\subset P
. If
A\subseteq P
then
A\cup B\subseteq P
, contradicting choice of
P
crossing
A\cup B
. Furthermore, if
P\subseteq A
, then
B\subset P\subseteq A
, contradicting the hypothesis that the set
B
crosses the set
A
. Finally,
A\cap P\not=\emptyset
as
A\cap B\not=0
and
B\subset P
. Thus the set
P
crosses the set
A
.

Let
P\in{\mathcal{C}}
be an arbitrary set that crosses the set
A\cap B
. Since the sets
P,B\in{\mathcal{C}}
are part of a chain family, it must be that either
P\subseteq B
or
B\subseteq P
. If
B\subseteq P
, then
A\cap B\subseteq P
, contradicting the choice of set
P
. Thus
P\subseteq B
. If
P\subseteq A
, then
P\subseteq A\cap B
, contradicting the choice of set
P
. Moreover, if
A\subseteq P
, then we have that
A\subseteq B
, contradicting that
A
and
B
cross. Thus the set
P
crosses the set
A
.
∎

3.2.2
Conditional supermodularity of Weak Density constraints

We now show that the function
f_{x}
is supermodular if
x_{u}<\frac{1}{2}

\forall u\in V
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
. If
x_{u}<\frac{1}{2}\ \forall u\in V
, then the function
f_{x}
is supermodular.

Proof.

Let
S\subseteq V
. We have

\displaystyle f_{x}(S)

\displaystyle=|E[S]|-|S|-\sum_{u\in S}(d_{S}(u)-1)x_{u}

\displaystyle=|E[S]|-\sum_{u\in S}d_{S}(u)x_{u}-|S|+\sum_{u\in S}x_{u}

\displaystyle=|E[S]|-\sum_{u\in S}\sum_{v\in\delta(u)}x_{u}-\sum_{u\in S}(1-x_{u})

\displaystyle=|E[S]|-\sum_{uv\in E[S]}(x_{u}+x_{v})-\sum_{u\in S}(1-x_{u})

\displaystyle=\sum_{uv\in E[S]}\left(1-(x_{u}+x_{v})\right)-\sum_{u\in S}\left(1-x_{u}\right).

Thus, the function
f_{x}
can be expressed as
f_{x}=p_{x}-q_{x}
for functions
p_{x},q_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by
p_{x}(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{uv\in E[S]}(1-(x_{u}+x_{v}))
and
q_{x}(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{u\in S}(1-x_{u})
.
Since
x_{u}<1/2
for all
u\in V
, we have that
p_{x}(S)\geq 0
and
q_{x}(S)\geq 0
for all
S\subseteq V
.

Now, let
H=(V,E)
be a graph with edge weights
w\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by
w(uv)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1-(x_{u}+x_{v})
.
We note that the edge weights are non-negative since
x_{a}<1/2
for all
a\in V
.
By definition,
p_{x}(S)=\sum_{uv\in E[S]}w(uv)
for all
S\subseteq V
. By
Proposition

1
, the function
p_{x}
is supermodular. Moreover, the function
q_{x}
is modular. Thus, the function
f_{x}
is the sum of a supermodular function and a modular function. Consequently,
f_{x}
is a supermodular function.
∎

3.2.3
Conditional properties of tight sets

Next, we prove certain properties of tight sets that help us to prove the existence of a well-structured basis for the extreme point
x
under the condition that
x_{u}<1/2
for every
u\in V
.

Lemma 2

(Conditional Uncrossing Properties)
.

Let
x
be an extreme point of
P_{\text{WD}}(G)
such that
x_{u}<\frac{1}{2}
for all
u\in V
and the family of tight sets for
x
be
{\mathcal{T}}_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{S\subseteq V\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f_{x}(S)=0\}
.
Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{T}}
. Then,

1.

A\cap B\neq\emptyset
, i.e., tight sets overlap,

2.

A\cap B,A\cup B\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
, i.e. tight sets form a lattice family,

3.

\delta(A-B,B-A)=\emptyset
, i.e. tight sets admit no crossing edges, and

4.

\mathsf{row}(A)+\mathsf{row}(B)=\mathsf{row}(A\cap B)+\mathsf{row}(A\cup B)
.

Proof.

We recall that for a set
S\subseteq V
, we denote
b(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|E[S]|-|S|
. Moreover, for the point
x\in P_{WD}(G)
, we denote
g_{x}(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{u\in S}(d_{S}(u)-1)x_{u}
. We prove each property separately below.

1.

By way of contradiction, assume that
A\cap B=\emptyset
. We have that

\displaystyle b(A\cup B)

\displaystyle\leq g_{x}(A\cup B)

\displaystyle=g_{x}(A)+g_{x}(B)+\sum_{ab\in\delta(A,B)}(x_{a}+x_{b})

\displaystyle=|E[A]-|A|+|E[B]-|B|+\sum_{ab\in\delta(A,B)}(x_{a}+x_{b})

\displaystyle=|E[A\cup B]|-|A\cup B|-|\delta(A,B)|+\sum_{ab\in\delta(A,B)}(x_{a}+x_{b})

\displaystyle=b(A\cup B)-|\delta(A,B)|+\sum_{ab\in\delta(A,B)}(x_{a}+x_{b})

\displaystyle<b(A\cup B)-|\delta(A,B)|+|\delta(A,B)|,

a contradiction. Here, the first inequality is by the weak density constraint for the set
A\cup B
. The
first and third equalities are by the hypothesis that
A\cap B=\emptyset
. The final inequality is because
x_{u}<\frac{1}{2}

\forall u\in V
.

2.

We have the following:

0=f_{x}(A)+f_{x}(B)\leq f_{x}(A\cup B)+f_{x}(A\cap B)\leq 0.

Here, the first equality is due to the sets
A,B\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
, and thus
f_{x}(A)=f_{x}(B)=0
. The first inequality follows from supermodularity of the function
f_{x}
shown in
Lemma

1
. The final inequality follows from weak density constraints for the sets
A\cup B
and
A\cap B
.
Thus, all inequalities are equalities, and we have that
f_{x}(A\cap B)=f_{x}(A\cup B)=0
using weak density constraints on the respective sets.

3.

By way of contradiction, assume that
\delta(A-B,B-A)\not=\emptyset
.
Since
A,B\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
, we have that
A\cap B
,
A\cup B\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
by the previously shown property that tight sets uncross. Thus,
f_{x}(A)=f_{x}(B)=f_{x}(A\cap B)=f_{x}(A\cup B)=0
.
We have that

\displaystyle 0=f_{x}(A)+f_{x}(B)-f_{x}(A\cap B)-f_{x}(A\cup B)=\sum_{uv\in\delta(A-B,B-A)}\left(1-(x_{u}+x_{v})\right)>0,

a contradiction. Here, the second equality is by definition of
f_{x}
, and the final inequality is because
x_{u}<\frac{1}{2}

\forall u\in V
.

4.

Let
u\in V
be an arbitrary vertex. It suffices to show that
(d_{A}(u)-1)+(d_{B}(u)-1)=(d_{A\cup B}(u)-1)+(d_{A\cap B}(u)-1)
. We consider four cases:

First, we consider the case
u\in A-B
. Then, we have that (1)
d_{A}(u)=d_{A-B}(u)+\delta(u,A\cap B)
, (2)
d_{B}(u)=0
(3)
d_{A\cap B}(u)=0
and (4)
d_{A\cup B}(u)=d_{A-B}(u)+\delta(u,A\cap B)
. Here, (4) follows from
\delta(u,B-A)=\emptyset
by the previous part. Thus, the claimed equality follows. We note that the argument for the case where
u\in B-A
is similar to this case.

Next, we consider the case
u\in A\cap B
. Then, we have that (1)
d_{A}(u)=d_{A\cap B}(u)+\delta(u,A-B)
, (2)
d_{B}(u)=d_{A\cap B}(u)+\delta(u,B-A)
, and (3)
d_{A\cup B}(u)=d_{A\cap B}(u)+\delta(u,A-B)+\delta(u,B-A)
. Thus, the claimed equality follows.

Finally, we consider the case
u\in V-(A\cup B)
. Then, we have that
d_{A}(u)=d_{B}(u)=d_{A\cap B}(u)=d_{A\cup B}(u)=0
and the claimed equality follows.

∎

Next, we show that every tight set is of size at least
2
and the graph induced over the tight set is connected.

Lemma 3
.

Let
x
be an extreme point of
P_{\text{WD}}(G)
such that
x_{u}<\frac{1}{2}
for all
u\in V
and the family of tight sets for
x
be
{\mathcal{T}}_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{S\subseteq V\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f_{x}(S)=0\}
. For every
A\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
, we have that
|A|\geq 2
and the graph
G[A]
is connected.

Proof.

Let
A\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
.
Suppose that
|A|=1
. Let
A=\{u\}
. Then,
A\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
implies that
f_{x}(\{u\})=0
. Equivalently,
-x_{u}=-1
and hence,
x_{u}=1
, a contradiction. Hence,
|A|\geq 2
. Next, we show that
G[A]
is connected. By way of contradiction, let
A=A_{1}\uplus A_{2}
such that
G[A_{1}],G[A_{2}]
are disconnected components of
G[A]
. Then, we have the following:

\displaystyle g_{x}(A_{1})+g_{x}(A_{2})

\displaystyle=g_{x}(A)

\displaystyle=b(A)

\displaystyle=|E[A]|-|A|

\displaystyle=|E[A_{1}]|-|A_{1}|+|E[A_{2}]|-|A_{2}|

\displaystyle=b(A_{1})+b(A_{2})

\displaystyle\leq g_{x}(A_{1})+g_{x}(A_{2}).

Here, the first and fourth equalities are because
A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\emptyset
. The second equality holds because
A\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
. The final inequality holds by the weak density constraints on
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
.

The chain of inequalities implies that the final inequality is an equality. By weak density constraints for
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, we also have that
b(A_{1})\leq g_{x}(A_{1})
and
b(A_{2})\leq g_{x}(A_{2})
and consequently,
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are tight sets, i.e.,
A_{1},A_{2}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
. However,
A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\emptyset
by assumption, contradicting
Lemma

2
that tight sets must overlap.
∎

3.2.4
Conditional basis structure for extreme points

In this section, we use the conditional structural properties of tight sets proved in
Section

3.2.3
to show that every extreme point
x
for the weak density polyhedron for which
x_{u}<1/2
for all
u\in V
has a well-structured basis.
We recall that a
2
-pseudotree is a connected graph that has one more edge than the number of vertices.
The following lemma is the main result of this section.

Lemma 4
.

Let
x
be an extreme point of
P_{\text{WD}}(G)
such that
x_{u}<\frac{1}{2}

\forall u\in V
, the family of tight sets for
x
be
{\mathcal{T}}_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{S\subseteq V\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f_{x}(S)=0\}
, and let
{\mathcal{Z}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{u\in V\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x_{u}=0\right\}
.
Then, there exists a family
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
such that

(1)

the family
{\mathcal{C}}
is a chain family,

(2)

the set of vectors
\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}\cup{\mathcal{Z}})
is linearly independent,

(3)

\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}\cup{\mathcal{Z}}))=\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{T}}_{x}\cup{\mathcal{Z}}))
,

(4)

For each
S\in{\mathcal{C}}
, the subgraph
G[S]
contains a
2
-psuedotree,

(5)

For each
S\in{\mathcal{C}}
and each vertex
u\in S
, we have that
d_{S}(u)\geq 2
, and

(6)

For every
A,B\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
A\subset B
, there exists a vertex
v\in B-A
such that
x_{v}>0
.

Proof.

We first show that there exists a family satisfying properties (1)-(4). Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
be an inclusion-wise maximal chain family.
2
below shows that
\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}))=\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{T}}_{x}))
.

Claim 2
.

\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}))=\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{T}}_{x})).

Proof.

By way of contradiction assume false. Let
A\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
such that
\mathsf{row}(A)\not\in\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}))
and
A
crosses the fewest number of sets in
{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}
. Consider a set
B\in{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}
such that
B
crosses
A
. We note that such a set
B
exists since otherwise the family
{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}\cup\{A\}
contradicts the inclusion-wsie maximality of
{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}
. Recall that by
Lemma

2
, the sets
A\cap B,A\cup B\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
are also tight. We note that by
Proposition

2
, the sets
A\cap B
and
A\cup B
cross fewer sets in
{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}
than the number of sets in
{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}
crossed by
A
. We consider two cases based on whether
\mathsf{row}(A\cup B),\mathsf{row}(A\cap B)\in\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}))
. First, consider the case where
\mathsf{row}(A\cup B)\not\in\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}))
without loss of generality. Since
A\cup B
crosses fewer sets in
{\mathcal{C}}
, the set
A\cup B
contradicts the choice of
A
.
Next, consider the case where
\mathsf{row}(A\cup B),\mathsf{row}(A\cap B)\in\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}))
.
By
Lemma

2
,
we have that
\mathsf{row}(A)+\mathsf{row}(B)=\mathsf{row}(A\cup B)+\mathsf{row}(A\cap B)
. Thus
\mathsf{row}(A)\in\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}(B,A\cap B,A\cup B))
, contradicting choice of
A
.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}
be an inclusion-wise maximal family such that the set
\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}\cup{\mathcal{Z}})
is linearly independent. We note that

\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}\cup{\mathcal{Z}}))=\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}\cup{\mathcal{Z}}))=\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{T}}_{x}\cup{\mathcal{Z}}))
, where the first equality is because the family
{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}
is inclusion-wise maximal. In particular, we have that the family
{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}
satisfies properties (1)-(3).

3
below shows that the family
{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}
also satisfies property (4).

Claim 3
.

For every
S\in{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}
, the subgraph
G[S]
contains a
2
-pseudotree.

Proof.

By way of contradiction, let
S\in{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}
such that
G[S]
does not contain a
2
-pseudotree. Let
S^{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{u\in S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}d_{S}(u)=i\}
and
S^{\geq i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{u\in S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}d_{S}(u)\geq i\}
.
We note that
b(S)\leq 0
since
G[S]
does not contain a
2
-pseudotree. Hence, we have that

\displaystyle 0\ \geq\ b(S)\

\displaystyle=g(S)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in S}(d_{S}(u)-1)x_{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in S^{0}}(d_{S}(u)-1)x_{u}+\sum_{u\in S^{1}}(d_{S}(u)-1)x_{u}+\sum_{u\in S^{\geq 2}}(d_{S}(u)-1)x_{u}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{u\in S^{\geq 2}}x_{u}

\displaystyle\geq 0

Here, the first equality holds because
S\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
. The final inequality holds since
|S|\geq 2
and
G[S]
has no isolated vertices by
Lemma

3
, and by the non-negativity constraints on vertex variables of
P_{\text{WD}}(G)
.

Thus, all inequalities above are equalities, and consequently, we have that
\sum_{u\in S^{\geq 2}}x_{u}=0

This, coupled with the non-negativity constraints on vertex variables, implies that
x_{u}=0
for each
u\in S^{\geq 2}
. We also note that
\mathsf{row}(S)_{u}=0
for each
u\in S^{1}\cup S^{0}
as
S^{0}=\emptyset
and
d_{S}(u)-1=0
for
u\in S^{1}
. Thus, we have that
\mathsf{row}(S)\in\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{Z}}))
, contradicting linear independence of
\mathsf{Rows}({\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}\cup{\mathcal{Z}})
. ∎

We now show the existence of a family satisfying properties (1)-(5). Let
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
be a family satisfying properties (1)-(4) which minimizes
\sum_{S\in{\mathcal{C}}}|S|
. We will show that this
{\mathcal{C}}
also satisfies property (5).
By way of contradiction, suppose there exists
S\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
u\in S
such that
d_{S}(u)<2
. Since
S\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
, we have that
|S|\geq 2
and
d_{S}(u)\geq 1
since tight sets cannot have isolated vertices by
Lemma

3
. Thus,
d_{S}(u)=1
. Let
uv\in E[S]
be the unique edge incident to
u
in
G[S]
.
We note that
E[S]
should contain at least one more edge apart from the edge
uv
as otherwise
\mathsf{row}(S)=0
. Furthermore, by
Lemma

3
, the subgraph
G[S]
must be connected. Thus,
d_{S}(v)\geq 2
and we have the following:

\displaystyle g_{x}(S-u)

\displaystyle=g_{x}(S)-x_{v}

\displaystyle=b(S)-x_{v}

\displaystyle=|E[S]|-|S|-x_{v}

\displaystyle=(|E[S-u]|+1)-(|S-u|+1)-x_{v}

\displaystyle=b(S-u)-x_{v}

\displaystyle\leq g_{x}(S-u)-x_{v}

The above chain of inequalities implies that
x_{v}\leq 0
. By the non-negativity constraints on
x_{v}
, we have that
x_{v}=0
. Thus, the final inequality in the above chain is in fact an equality, and hence, the set
S-u
is in
{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
.

We now make three observations:

1.

\mathsf{row}(S)-\chi_{v}=\mathsf{row}(S-u)
(we note that the LHS subtracts the indicator vector of the vertex
v
while the RHS considers the row vector of the set
S-u
, where
u
is the vertex with
d_{S}(u)=1
),

2.

Every
X\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
X\subset S
either has
d_{X}(u)=1
or
X
does not contain
u
, and

3.

For every
X\subset S
such that
X\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
u,v\in X
,
\mathsf{row}(X)-\chi_{v}=\mathsf{row}(X-u)
.

Here, the first observation is due to
d_{S}(u)=1
and thus
\mathsf{row}(S)_{u}=0
. The second observation follows because of monotonicity of the induced-degree function and the fact that tight sets cannot have isolated vertices by
Lemma

3
. The third observation follows by writing down the corresponding chain of inequalities as above for each such set
X
.
Then, by the three observations, we can remove the vertex
u
from every set in
{\mathcal{C}}
that contains
u
resulting in a set family
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\not={\mathcal{C}}
that still satisfies properties (1)-(4). However, we have that
\sum_{S\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}|S|<\sum_{S\in{\mathcal{C}}}|S|
, contradicting our choice of family
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Finally, we show that
{\mathcal{C}}
also satisfies property (6).
By way of contradiction, assume that there exists
A,B\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
A\subset B
and
x_{u}=0
for each
u\in B-A
. We first show a lower bound on
|\delta(A,B-A)|
.

Claim 4
.

\frac{|\delta(A,B-A)|}{2}\geq-b(B-A)
.

Proof.

First, we argue that the subgraph
G[B-A]
is a forest. By way of contradiction, suppose that the subgraph
G[B-A]
contains a cycle. Let
C
be a minimal cycle in the subgraph
G[B-A]
. Then,
g_{x}(C)=0
as
x_{u}=0
for each
u\in B-A
by our hypothesis. Furthermore,
b(C)=0
as
C
is a cycle. Thus,
C\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
is a tight set. However, since
C\subseteq B-A
, we have that
C\cap A=\emptyset
. This contradicts the fact that all tight sets must overlap (as shown by
Lemma

2
).

Let
k
be the number of disconnected acyclic components of the forest
G[B-A]
. Then, we have the following two observations:

1.

b(B-A)=-k
and

2.

|\delta(A,B-A)|\geq 2k
.

We note that the claim follows from above observations. We justify these observations next.

The first observation follows from the fact that the subgraph
G[B-A]
is a forest. In particular, we have that

b(B-A)=\left|E[B-A]\right|-\left|B-A\right|=\left(\left(\left|B-A\right|-1\right)-(k-1)\right)-|B-A|=-k.

We now prove the second observation. Each of the
k
acyclic components of the subgraph
G[B-A]
is either a singleton or has at least two leaves. If the component is a singleton vertex
v
, then we have that
|\delta(v,A)|=d_{B}(v)\geq 2
, where the inequality is because
B\in{\mathcal{C}}
and the family
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies property (5). Alternatively, suppose that the component has two leaves. Let
v
be an arbitrary leaf of the component. We note that the induced degree
d_{B-A}(v)=1
. Thus,
|\delta(v,A)|=d_{B}(v)-d_{B-A}(v)\geq 1
, where the inequality is once again because
B\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies property (5).
∎

With the above lower bound on the cut size
|\delta(A,B-A)|
, we get the required contradiction as follows:

\displaystyle g_{x}(A)+\frac{|\delta(A,B-A)|}{2}\

\displaystyle>g_{x}(A)+\sum_{uv\in\delta(A,B-A)}x_{u}

\displaystyle=g_{x}(A)+g_{x}(B-A)+\sum_{uv\in\delta(A,B-A)}(x_{u}+x_{v})

\displaystyle=g_{x}(B)

\displaystyle=b(B)

\displaystyle=|E[B]|-|B|

\displaystyle=\left(|E[A]|+|E[B-A]|+|\delta(A,B-A)|\right)-\left(|A|+|B-A|\right)

\displaystyle=b(A)+b(B-A)+|\delta(A,B-A)|

\displaystyle\geq b(A)+\frac{|\delta(A,B-A)|}{2}

\displaystyle=g_{x}(A)+\frac{|\delta(A,B-A)|}{2}.

The first inequality follows from
x_{u}<1/2

\forall u\in V
. The first equality follows from the assumption that
x_{u}=0
for each
u\in B-A
. The penultimate inequality follows from
4
, and the final equality holds since the set
A\in{\mathcal{T}}_{x}
is a tight set.
∎

3.2.5
Proof of Theorem
2

We now complete the proof of
Theorem

2
.
In the next lemma, we use a stronger hypothesis that
x_{u}<1/3
for all
u\in V
to
show that there exist at least two vertices
u,v
which take non-zero values. We emphasize that the lemma does not rely on extreme point properties and holds for every feasible point
x
satisfying the hypothesis.

Lemma 5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected graph with minimum degree at least
2
such that
G
contains a

Lemma 13

Claim 5

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in F}(d(v)-1)

\displaystyle=2|E|-|F|-\left(|\delta(F,V-F)|+2|E[V-F]|\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2|E|-|F|-\left(2|V|-|F|\right)

\displaystyle=2b(V).

Furthermore, we define the weight of an edge

. We note that
|K|=t
.

Since

for some

in the original graph

is at least

.

For this, we claim that each vertex

2

Next, consider the case where

Proof of integrality gap

In this section, we prove

We now observe that the algorithm terminates in polynomial time to return a feasible FVS.

Proof.

Each step of the while loop and reverse-delete procedure can be implemented to run in polynomial time.

4.3
Alternative formulations

In Section
4.2
, we described an ILP formulation based on orientation and cycle cover polyhedra and showed that its LP-relaxation is at least as strong as (
FVS-LP: WD-and-cycle-cover
). We briefly discuss two more polynomial-sized LP-relaxations for FVS with integrality gap at most

. We give full details in the appendix for the sake of completeness.

We recall that Chekuri and Madan
[

]

\overline{x}\in P_{\text{cycle-cover}}(G)
owing to inequality (4). We now show that
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in P_{\text{orient}}(G)
.
We have that
\overline{x}_{u}\geq 0
for all

and
\overline{y}_{e,u}\geq 0
for all
e\in\delta_{G}(u),u\in V_{G}
. We now show that
\overline{x}_{u}+\sum_{e\in\delta_{G}(u)}\overline{y}_{e,u}\leq 1
for every
u\in V_{G}
. Let
u\in V

\overline{x}_{u}+\sum_{e\in\delta_{G}(u)}\overline{y}_{e,u}=\overline{x}_{u}+\sum_{e\in\ell_{H}(u)}\overline{z}_{u,e}-\overline{z}_{u,e_{r}}=1-\overline{z}_{u,e_{r}}\leq 1,

where the last inequality holds since
\overline{z}_{u,e_{r}}\geq 0
.

Finally, we show that
\overline{x}_{u}+\overline{x}_{v}+\overline{y}_{e,u}+\overline{y}_{e,v}\geq 1
for every

. Let
e=uv\in E_{G}
. Let
u,a_{e},s_{e},b_{e},v
be the path in the graph

\displaystyle\overline{x}_{u}+\overline{x}_{v}+\overline{y}_{e,u}+\overline{y}_{e,v}

\displaystyle=\overline{x}_{u}+\overline{x}_{v}+\overline{z}_{u,e}+\overline{z}_{v,e}

\displaystyle=(\overline{x}_{u}+\overline{x}_{v}+\overline{z}_{u,e}+\overline{z}_{v,e})+(\overline{z}_{a_{e},e}+\overline{z}_{b_{e},e})-(\overline{z}_{a_{e},e}+\overline{z}_{b_{e},e})

\displaystyle=1+(\overline{x}_{u}+\overline{z}_{u,e}-\overline{z}_{a_{e},e})+(\overline{x}_{v}+\overline{z}_{v,e}-\overline{z}_{b_{e},e})

\displaystyle\geq 1.

Here, the third equality follows from constraint (2) and the final inequality follows from constraint (3) of (
FVS-LP: CM
) in

.
∎

Lemma

In this section, we give another polynomial-sized ILP for FVS whose LP-relaxation has integrality gap at most

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in V}c_{u}x_{u}

\displaystyle x_{v}+x_{w}+y^{f}_{e,v}+y^{f}_{e,w}

\displaystyle\geq 1\ \forall\ e=vw\in E,f\in E

\displaystyle x_{v}+\sum_{e=vw\in E}y^{f}_{e,w}

\displaystyle\geq 1\ \forall\ v\in V,f\in E

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V-\{a,b\}}x_{v}+\sum_{e=vw\in E-\{f\}}(y^{f}_{e,w}+y^{f}_{e,v})

\displaystyle\leq|V|-2\ \forall\ f=ab\in E

(11)

\displaystyle x_{u}

\displaystyle\geq 0\ \forall u\in V

(12)

\displaystyle y^{f}_{e,v}

\displaystyle\geq 0\ \forall e\in\delta(v),v\in V,f\in E

\displaystyle x_{u}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}\ \forall u\in V

\displaystyle y^{f}_{e,v}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}\ \forall e\in\delta(v),v\in V,f\in E.

Theorem

5
follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 20
.

For an input graph
G=(V,E)
with non-negative vertex costs
c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
(
FVS-IP: Complete-Orient
) is an integer linear programming formulation for FVS. Moreover, its LP-relaxation has integrality gap at most
2
.

Proof.

Let
x
be the indicator vector of a feedback vertex set
S
of
G
.
We show that there exists a binary vector
y
satisfying the constraints. Let
T
be a spanning tree of
G
containing all edges of
G
that are not incident to vertices in
S
.
Fix an edge
f=ab\in E
.
For each vertex
v\in V\setminus(S\cup\{a,b\})
, set
y^{f}_{e,w}=1
where
e=vw
is the first edge on the unique path from
v
to
a
in
T
. Moreover, set
y^{f}_{f,a}=y^{f}_{f,b}=1
and all other
y^{f}
variables to
0
. We prove that this choice of
y
satisfies all constraints.

For an edge
e=vw\in E
, if
|S\cap\{v,w\}|\geq 1
, then
x_{v}+x_{w}\geq 1
and if
v,w\not\in S
, then
e\in T
and consequently, either
y_{e,v}^{f}=1
or
y^{f}_{e,w}=1
and hence, the constraint
x_{v}+x_{w}+y^{f}_{e,v}+y^{f}_{e,w}\geq 1
holds. For a vertex
v\in V
, if
v\in S
, then
x_{v}\geq 1
and if
v\in V-S
, then there exists an edge
e=vw\in T
and hence,
y^{f}_{e,w}=1
and consequently, the constraint
x_{v}+\sum_{e=vw\in E}y^{f}_{e,w}\geq 1
holds. It remains to show that the constraint
\sum_{v\in V-\{a,b\}}x_{v}+\sum_{e=vw\in E-\{f\}}(y^{f}_{e,w}+y^{f}_{e,v})\leq|V|-2
holds. By the choice of
x
, it suffices to show that
|S\setminus\{a,b\}|+\sum_{e=vw\in T-\{f\}}(y^{f}_{e,w}+y^{f}_{e,v})\leq|V|-2
holds. By the choice of
y^{f}
, it suffices to show that
|S\setminus\{a,b\}|+\sum_{e=vw\in T}(y^{f}_{e,w}+y^{f}_{e,v})\leq|V|
. Simplifying this further, it suffices to show that

\sum_{e=vw\in T}(y^{f}_{e,w}+y^{f}_{e,v})\leq|(V-S)\cup\{a,b\}|.

We note that
\sum_{e=vw\in T}(y^{f}_{e,w}+y^{f}_{e,v})=\sum_{v\in V}(\sum_{e=vw\in T}y^{f}_{e,w})
. Hence, it suffices to show that

\sum_{v\in V}\left(\sum_{e=vw\in T}y^{f}_{e,w}\right)\leq|(V-S)\cup\{a,b\}|.

We have the following four inequalities:

\displaystyle\sum_{e=vw\in T}y^{f}_{e,w}

\displaystyle\leq 1\ \forall v\in V-(S\cup\{a,b\}),

\displaystyle\sum_{e=vw\in T}y^{f}_{e,w}

\displaystyle=0\ \forall v\in S-\{a,b\},

\displaystyle\sum_{e=aw\in T}y^{f}_{e,w}

\displaystyle=y^{f}_{e,b}=1,\text{ and}

\displaystyle\sum_{e=bw\in T}y^{f}_{e,w}

\displaystyle=y^{f}_{e,a}=1.

Hence,
\sum_{v\in V}\left(\sum_{e=vw\in T}y^{f}_{e,w}\right)\leq|(V-S)\cup\{a,b\}|
.

Next, we show that an integral solution to the system of inequalities (
9
), (
10
), (
11
), (
12
), and (
13
) is the indicator vector of a feedback vertex set.
We will use the following claim.

Claim 6
.

Constraints (
9
), (
10
), (
11
), (
12
), and (
13
) imply the strong density constraints.

Proof.

Let
(x,y)
be a solution satisfying constraints (
9
), (
10
), (
11
), (
12
), and (
13
). Let
S\subseteq V
such that
E[S]\neq\emptyset
. We will show that

\sum_{u\in S}(d_{S}(u)-1)x_{u}\geq|E[S]|-|S|+1.

Equivalently, we will show that

|E[S]|-|S|-\sum_{u\in S}(d_{S}(u)-1)x_{u}\leq-1.

Let us rewrite the LHS as

|E[S]|-|S|-\sum_{u\in S}(d_{S}(u)-1)x_{u}=\sum_{vw\in E[S]}(1-x_{v}-x_{w})+\sum_{v\in S}(x_{v}-1).

Let us inspect each sum separately. Fix an arbitrary
f=ab\in E[S]
. We have that

\displaystyle\sum_{vw\in E[S]}(1-x_{v}-x_{w})

\displaystyle=\sum_{vw\in E[S]}\left(1-x_{v}-x_{w}-y^{f}_{e,w}-y^{f}_{e,v}\right)+\sum_{vw\in E[S]}\left(y^{f}_{e,v}+y^{f}_{e,w}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1-x_{a}-x_{b}-y^{f}_{f,a}-y^{f}_{f,b}+\sum_{vw\in E[S]}\left(y^{f}_{e,v}+y^{f}_{e,w}\right)

where the inequality follows from (
9
) and the fact that
f=ab\in E[S]
. We also have that

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}(x_{v}-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in S}\left(x_{v}-1+\sum_{e=vw\in E}y^{f}_{e,w}\right)-\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{e=vw\in E}y^{f}_{e,w}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V}\left(x_{v}-1+\sum_{e=vw\in E}y^{f}_{e,w}\right)-\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{e=vw\in E}y^{f}_{e,w}

where the last inequality follows from (

). Before adding the two upper bounds, we also note that

\sum_{vw\in E[S]}\left(y^{f}_{e,v}+y^{f}_{e,w}\right)-\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{e=vw\in E}y^{f}_{e,w}\leq 0

by (
13
).
Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{vw\in E[S]}(1-x_{v}-x_{w})+\sum_{v\in S}(x_{v}-1)

\displaystyle\leq 1-x_{a}-x_{b}-y^{f}_{f,a}-y^{f}_{f,b}+\sum_{v\in V}\left(x_{v}-1+\sum_{e=vw\in E}y^{f}_{e,w}\right)

\displaystyle=1-|V|+\sum_{v\in V-\{a,b\}}x_{v}+\sum_{vw\in E-\{f\}}(y^{f}_{e,v}+y^{f}_{e,w})

\displaystyle\leq-1

where the last inequality follows from (
11
).
∎

]

We have already shown that the ILP in the lemma statement is a valid formulation for FVS.
By Claim

.

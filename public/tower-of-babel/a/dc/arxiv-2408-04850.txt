Irreducibility of polynomials defining parabolic parameters of period 3 3
Junnosuke Koizumi, Yuya Murakami, Kaoru Sano, Kohei Takehira

Abstract
. Morton and Vivaldi defined the polynomials whose roots are parabolic parameters for a one-parameter family of polynomial maps. We call these polynomials delta factors. They conjectured that delta factors are irreducible for the family z ↦ z 2 + c z\mapsto z^{2}+c . One can easily show the irreducibility for periods 1 1 and 2 2 by reducing it to the irreducibility of cyclotomic polynomials. However, for periods 3 3 and beyond, this becomes a challenging problem. This paper proves the irreducibility of delta factors for the period 3 3 and demonstrates the existence of infinitely many irreducible delta factors for periods greater than 3 3 .



1.
Introduction

This paper discusses the irreducibility of polynomials that appear in connection with discrete dynamical systems of a one-parameter family of polynomial maps.

Consider the polynomial
f_{c}(z)=z^{2}+c\in\mathbb{Z}[c][z]
parametrized by a parameter
c
.
The periodic points of the dynamical system
z\mapsto f_{c}(z)
have long been a subject of interest.
For a positive integer
n
, the
n
-th
dynatomic polynomial

\Phi_{n}^{*}
is defined by

(1.1)

\Phi_{n}^{*}(z,c)\coloneqq\prod_{k\mid n}(f_{c}^{\circ k}(z)-z)^{\mu(n/k)},

where
f_{c}^{\circ k}
is recursively defined by
f_{c}^{\circ 0}(z)=z
and
f_{c}^{\circ k}=f_{c}\circ f_{c}^{\circ(k-1)}
.
The polynomial
\Phi_{n}^{*}({-},c)
has periodic points of period
n
as its roots.
For general properties of the dynatomic polynomials, see
[
Sil07
]
.

One important quantity associated with periodic points is the multiplier.
For a root
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}(c)}
of
\Phi_{n}^{*}({-},c)
, the
multiplier
at
\alpha
is defined by
\omega_{n}(\alpha)=(f_{c}^{\circ n})^{\prime}(\alpha).

Since the chain rule shows the equality

(1.2)

(f_{c}^{\circ n})^{\prime}(\alpha)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}f_{c}^{\prime}(f_{c}^{\circ k}(\alpha)),

the multiplier is constant on the orbit, that is, the equality
\omega_{n}(\alpha)=\omega_{n}(f_{c}(\alpha))
holds.
The
n
-th
multiplier polynomial

\delta_{n}
is defined to be the monic polynomial satisfying

(1.3)

\delta_{n}(x,c)^{n}\coloneqq\Resultant_{z}(\Phi_{n}^{*}(z,c),x-(f_{c}^{\circ n})^{\prime}(z))=\prod_{\alpha\colon\Phi_{n}^{*}(\alpha,c)=0}(x-\omega_{n}(\alpha)),

where
\Resultant_{z}
is the resultant with respect to the variable
z
.
The multiplier polynomial
\delta_{n}
is indeed a polynomial with integer coefficients (cf
[
VH92
]
).
Here, we remark that Huguin
[
Hug21
]
and Murakami–Sano–Takehira
[
MST24
]
independently proved that
\delta_{n}
is in
\mathbb{Z}[4c,x]
and is monic in
4c
.

[
MST24
]
discussed generalizations of this fact for other one-parameter families.

An
n
-periodic point
\alpha
whose multiplier
\omega_{n}(\alpha)
is a root of unity is said to be
parabolic
.
We say that a parameter
\gamma
is
parabolic
if
f_{\gamma}
has a parabolic periodic point.
Parabolic parameters are precisely the roots of the polynomials
\Delta_{n,m}\;(m,n>0,\;m\mid n)
defined in the following way.
Let
\Phi^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{k}(x)
denote the
k
-th cyclotomic polynomial.
For positive integers
m,n
which satisfy
m\mid n
and
m<n
, the
delta factor

\Delta_{n,m}
is defined by the equation

(1.4)

\Delta_{n,m}(c)\coloneqq\Resultant_{x}(\Phi^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{n/m}(x),\delta_{m}(x,c)).

In the case where
m=n
, the delta factor
\Delta_{n,n}(c)
is defined by

(1.5)

\delta_{n}(1,c)=\Delta_{n,n}(c)\prod_{m\mid n,m\neq n}\Delta_{n,m}(c).

The list of
\Delta_{n,m}
can be found in
[
MV95
, Section 3, Table 1]
or
[
MST24
, Appendix B]
.
Since the parabolic parameters are algebraic numbers, looking at their number-theoretic properties is natural.
For example, Buff–Koch determined all totally real parabolic parameters in
[
BK22
]
, and Murakami–Sano–Takehira determined all quadratic parabolic parameters in
[
MST24
]
.

Morton–Vivaldi conjectured the irreducibility of
\Delta_{n,m}
over
\mathbb{Q}
in
[
MV95
]
.
This conjecture is also cited as an open problem in
[
Sil07
, Exercise 4.12 (e)**]
.

(The irreducibility conjecture
[

]
)

For a family of the polynomial maps
f_{c}(z)=z^{2}+c
, the polynomials
\Delta_{n,m}
for positive integers

are all irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
.

One can easily show the irreducibility of
\Delta_{n,m}
for

by reducing it to the irreducibility of cyclotomic polynomials; see
[

, Corollary 3.7]
.
However, for

Our first main theorem is the irreducibility of delta factors defining parabolic parameters of period

Theorem 1.2
.

For any positive integer

.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Basic facts on dynamics and delta factors

This subsection recalls some basic facts on holomorphic dynamics and delta factors.
First, we review a well-known relationship between parabolic periodic points and critical points.

Theorem 2.1

(cf
[
Mil06
, Theorem 10.15]
)
.

Let
f
be a rational map on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
d\geq 2
.
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
be a parabolic
n
-periodic point of
f
.
Then, there is a critical point
\beta
of
f
satisfying

(2.1)

\lim_{m\to\infty}f^{\circ(nm+i)}(\beta)=\alpha

for some
0\leq i\leq n-1
.

Since a critical point of
f_{c}(z)=z^{2}+c
is either
0
or
\infty
, we can easily deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 2.2
.

For a complex number
c\in\mathbb{C}
, the polynomial
f_{c}(z)=z^{2}+c
has at most one parabolic periodic orbit.

Recall that the multiplier polynomial
\delta_{n}
is an element of
\mathbb{Z}[x,4c]
and is monic in
4c
.
We define a polynomial
\widetilde{\delta}_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[x,C]
by
\widetilde{\delta}_{n}(x,C)=\delta_{n}(x,C/4)
.
For
m,n>0
with
m\mid n
, we define a polynomial
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,m}
by
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,m}(C)=\Delta_{n,m}(C/4)
.
It follows that
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,m}
is a monic polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[C]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

For any
m,n>0
with
m\mid n
, the polynomial
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,m}
is separable.

Proof.

By
[
MV95
, Proposition 3.2]
, the polynomial
\widetilde{\delta}_{n}(1,C)
is separable.
Since we have

\widetilde{\delta}_{n}(1,C)=\prod_{m\mid n}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,m}(C),

the polynomials
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,m}
are also separable.
∎

2.2.
Multiplier curves

Throughout this section, for a polynomial
F
of complex coefficients, we write
Z(F)
for the set of complex roots of
F
without counting multiplicity.

To study parabolic parameters, we introduce the following geometric object.

Definition 2.4
.

We define the
multiplier curve

X_{m}
to be the curve in
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
defined by
\widetilde{\delta}_{m}(x,C)=0
.

It is known that the curve
X_{m}
is geometrically integral
[
Mor96
, Corollary 1]
.
Let
k\geq 2
be an integer.
By definition, the roots of
\widetilde{\Delta}_{mk,m}
are the numbers
\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\widetilde{\delta}_{m}(\zeta,\gamma)=0
for some primitive
k
-th root of unity
\zeta
.
Since we have the uniqueness of parabolic periodic orbits by
2.2
, such
\zeta
is uniquely determined by
\gamma
.
In other words, we have a Galois-equivariant bijection

(2.2)

\displaystyle\{(\zeta,\gamma)\in X_{m}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\mid\Phi^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{k}(\zeta)=0\}\xlongrightarrow{\sim}Z(\widetilde{\Delta}_{mk,m});\quad(\zeta,\gamma)\mapsto\gamma.

In particular, the polynomial
\widetilde{\Delta}_{mk,m}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
acts transitively on the set
\{(\zeta,\gamma)\in X_{m}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\mid\Phi^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{k}(\zeta)=0\}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer.
For any
\gamma\in Z(\widetilde{\Delta}_{mk,m})
, the field
\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)
contains the cyclotomic field
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})
.
In particular, the degree of an irreducible factor of
\widetilde{\Delta}_{mk,m}
over
\mathbb{Q}
is divisible by
\varphi(k)
.

Proof.

By the existence of the bijection
Equation

2.2
, there is a unique primitive
k
-th root of unity
\zeta
such that
(\zeta,\gamma)\in X_{m}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.
If
\sigma\in\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\gamma))
, then
(\sigma(\zeta),\sigma(\gamma))=(\sigma(\zeta),\gamma)
is also an element of
X_{m}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.
By the uniqueness of
\zeta
, we have
\zeta=\sigma(\zeta)
.
This implies
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)\subset\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)
by Galois theory.
∎

Now, we focus on the case of
m=3
.
The multiplier curve
X_{3}
is given by

(2.3)

\widetilde{\delta}_{3}(x,C)=x^{2}-(2C+16)x+(C^{3}+8C^{2}+16C+64)=0.

It is rational since this is a cubic curve with a nodal singular point
(x,C)=(8,0)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

The normalization of
X_{3}
is given by

(2.4)

\varphi\colon\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow X_{3};\quad t\mapsto(t^{3}-t^{2}+7t+1,-t^{2}-7).

Proof.

A direct computation shows that
\varphi
is well-defined.
For each
(x,C)\in X_{3}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, we have

(2.5)

\#\varphi^{-1}(x,C)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }(x,C)\neq(8,0),\\
2&\text{if }(x,C)=(8,0).\end{cases}

This observation shows that
\varphi
is finite and birational.
Since
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is normal, this map gives the normalization of
X_{3}
.
∎

.

Definition 2.8
.

Let

2.2
.
∎

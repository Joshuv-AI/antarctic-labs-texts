The Dijkgraaf-Witten invariants from second Chern classes


Abstract
Given a 3-cocycle ψ \psi in the cohomology of a finite group G G , we can define the Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant of closed 3-manifolds. In this paper, we focus on the case where ψ \psi is a 3-cocycle systematically transferred from the second Chern class of a complex representation of G G , and show some procedures for computing the invariant, and clarify its topological interpretation under some conditions.

Introduction

Let
M
be a connected oriented closed 3-manifold with orientation 3-class
[M]\in H_{3}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Let
G
be a group, and
BG
be an Eilenberg-MacLane space of type
(G,1)
. If
G=\pi_{1}(X)
, we can choose a classifying map
\iota:M\hookrightarrow B\pi_{1}(M)
, uniquely up to homotopy. Fix a group 3-cocycle
\psi\in H^{3}(BG;A)=H^{3}(G;A)
with trivial coefficients. Then, for a group homomorphism
f:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow G
, we can consider the pairing
\langle f^{*}(\psi),\iota_{*}[M]\rangle\in A
.
As an important example, if
G
is a Lie group with discrete topology and
\psi
is a Chern-Simons 3-class, the pairing
is called
the Chern-Simons invariant

[
CS
]
(see §
A
for the definition).
Meanwhile, as another example, the Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant
[
DW
, §6]
is a toy model of the Chern-Simons invariant when
G
is of finite order (See Appendix
A
for details).
Precisely, the invariant is defined as the following formal sum in the group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[A]
:

\mathrm{DW}_{\psi}(M):=\sum_{f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(\pi_{1}(M),G)}1_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\langle f^{*}(\psi),\iota_{*}[M]\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}[A].

(1)

Here,
1_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
is the unit of
{\mathbb{Z}}.

The DW invariant seems simple; however, there are few examples of the resulting computations and studies of the invariant, unless
G
is abelian or nilpotent.

To study such pairings and the invariant (
1
) it is a problem to examine what kind of
\psi
we should choose, and how we find an explicit expression of
\psi
when
G
is not abelian. Since the Chern-Simons 3-class is, roughly speaking, considered to be an inverse of a transgression of the second Chern classes of principle
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
-bundles (see, e.g.,
[
CS
]
or
[
DW
, §§3–5]
), it is sensible to focus on a class of group 3-cocycles, which are like such inverses from second Chern classes of
G
with
|G|<\infty
.
For this, if
|G|<\infty,
notice the following isomorphisms obtained from the cohomology long exact sequence and the universal coefficient theorem:

H^{n+1}(G;{\mathbb{Z}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta^{-1}}}{{\cong}}H^{n}(G;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_{n}(G;{\mathbb{Z}}),

(2)

where
n\geq 1
and the first isomorphism is the inverse of the Bockstein map induced from

0\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{\ \textrm{inclusion}\ }{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow 0\hskip 11.74988pt\mathrm{(exact)}.

(3)

In addition, as seen in
[
Ev2
,
Th
]
, any complex representation
\rho:G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
uniquely admits the Chern class
c_{i}(\rho)\in H^{2i}(G;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Thus, we have a cohomology 3-class of the form

\beta^{-1}(c_{2}(\rho))\in H^{3}(G;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}).

(4)

In this paper, as a finite-order model of the Chern-Simons 3-class, we consider the Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant (
1
) with
\psi=12\beta^{-1}(c_{2}(\rho))
.
First, as a result of a Riemann-Roch type theorem, we develop a formula for describing
12\beta^{-1}(c_{2}(\rho))
(see Proposition
2.2
),
and show (Section
3
) that, under a certain condition, the twelvefold invariant can be computed from the cohomology rings of some covering spaces of
M
.
For example, the condition holds if
G={\rm SL}_{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\nolimits)
with
q>10
; see Corollary
3.6
(see also Remark
3.12
for other groups).
In Section
A
, from a viewpoint of the Chern-Simons invariant, we suggest a procedure for computing the Dijkgraaf-Witten invariants of some Seifert 3-manifolds. Finally, in Section
4
we observe other approaches to the invariants with
\psi=\beta^{-1}(c_{2}(\rho))
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
M

[M]\in H_{3}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
.
By
BG
we mean an Eilenberg-MacLane space of type

Twelvefold second Chern classes

Let
\rho:G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be any complex representation,
and
c_{i}(\rho)\in H^{2i}(G;{\mathbb{Z}})
be the Chern class of
\rho
; see, e.g.,
[
Ev2
,
Th
]
for the definition.
The purpose of this section is to develop an algorithm to describe the twelvefold 3-class
12\beta^{-1}(c_{2}(\rho)).

Let
c(\rho)\in H^{*}(G;{\mathbb{Z}})
be the total Chern class, that is,
c(\rho)=1+c_{1}(\rho)+c_{2}(\rho)+\cdots+c_{n}(\rho).

Let
R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
be the complex representation ring of
G
.
As is known as Brauer theorem, there are finitely many
n_{i}(\rho)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and subgroups
H_{i}\subset G
with one–dimensional representation

\phi_{i}:H_{i}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{1}({\mathbb{C}})
such that

\rho=\sum_{i}n_{i}(\rho)\mathrm{Ind}_{H_{i}}^{G}(\phi_{i})\in R_{{\mathbb{C}}}(G).

(5)

Here,
\mathrm{Ind}_{H_{i}}^{G}(\phi_{i})
is the induced representation.
By the Whitney sum formula of
c(\rho)
, we have

c(\rho)=\prod(1+c_{1}(\mathrm{Ind}_{H_{i}}^{G}(\phi_{i}))+c_{2}(\mathrm{Ind}_{H_{i}}^{G}(\phi_{i}))+\cdots)^{n_{i}(\rho)}\in H^{*}(G;{\mathbb{Z}})

(6)

To summarize, to compute
c_{2}(\rho)
, we may focus only on
c_{1}(\mathrm{Ind}_{H_{i}}^{G}(\phi_{i}))
and
c_{2}(\mathrm{Ind}_{H_{i}}^{G}(\phi_{i}))
.

Thus, we shall restrict ourselves to a subgroup
H\subset G
and a one–dimensional representation

\phi:H\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{1}({\mathbb{C}})={\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, and show Lemma
2.1
below.
Identify
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
with the multiplicative subgroup
\{\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}r)\mid r\in{\mathbb{Q}}\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
.
Since
|H|<\infty
, the image of
\phi
is contained in
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
; we may regard
\phi
as a 1-cocycle of
H
with trivial coefficients.
Denote the transfer map by
\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}:H^{n}(H;A)\rightarrow H^{n}(G;A)
; see, e.g.,
[
Br
, §III.9]
for the definition.
Then, the following claims that the cohomology 3-class
12\beta^{-1}\bigl(c_{2}(\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\phi))\bigr)
can be computed from
\beta
and the transfers.

Lemma 2.1
.

Take another subgroup
H^{\prime}\subset G
with a representation
\phi^{\prime}:H^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{1}({\mathbb{C}})
.
Then, the following two identities hold:

2\beta^{-1}\bigl(c_{1}(\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\phi))\smile c_{1}(\mathrm{Ind}_{H^{\prime}}^{G}(\phi^{\prime}))\bigr)=2\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}(\phi)\smile_{{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\beta\circ\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H^{\prime}}(\phi^{\prime}),

(7)

12\beta^{-1}\bigl(c_{2}(\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\phi))\bigr)=6\bigl(\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}(\phi)\smile_{{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\beta\circ\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}(\phi)-\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}(\phi\smile\beta(\phi))\bigr)\in H^{3}(G;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}).

(8)

Here,
\smile_{{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}
means the cup product with coefficients induced from the biadditive map
{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
that sends
(n,[a])
to
[an]
.
Furthermore, if
\phi
extends to a 1-cocycle
G\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, then (
8
) vanishes.

Proof.

Recall the integral Riemann-Roch theorem in group cohomology (see, e.g.,

Remark 2.3
.

Let us make a remark on a topological meaning of the first term in (
12
).
In general, for any
1

\bar{\phi}:G\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
as in
\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H_{j}}(\phi_{j})
and any homomorphism

, we regard
\bar{\phi}\circ f
as first cohomology classes of

via
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(\pi_{1}(M),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Hence, the pairing
\langle f^{*}(\psi),[M]\rangle
with
\psi=\phi_{j}\smile\beta(\phi_{k})
can be computed from the cohomology ring
H^{*}(M;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
and the Bockstein maps (i.e., the linking form) of
M
.
In addition, the pairing can be computed in several ways: for example, see
[
MOO
]
for such computations from some quasitriangular Hopf algebras, branched coverings, or Kirby diagrams.
Meanwhile, the second term in (
12
) will be topologically examined in Section

.

By Lemma
2.1
, it is reasonable to describe explicit formula of
\beta
and
\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}

in the non-homogeneous complex of
G
.
First, from the definition of the Bockstein map,
\beta(\phi):H\times H\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
is represented by

\beta(\phi)(g,h):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\mathrm{if}\hskip 11.74988pt\overline{\phi(g)}+\overline{\phi(h)}\geq 1,\\
0,&\mathrm{if}\hskip 11.74988pt\overline{\phi(g)}+\overline{\phi(h)}<1.\end{array}\right.

(13)

Here, for
r\in{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, we uniquely choose the representative
\overline{r}\in{\mathbb{Q}}\cap[0,1)
. Next, let us choose a complete set of representatives of the left cosets
G/H
,
T:=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|G/H|}\subset G
, such that
T\cap H=\{1_{G}\}.

For
\sigma\in G
, we fix notation

\bar{\sigma}:=H\sigma\cap T,\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988pt\widetilde{\sigma}:=\sigma(\bar{\sigma})\in H.

Then,
according to
[
Br
, Exercise II.10.2]
,
\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}(\psi):G\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
is represented by a correspondence

\sigma\longmapsto\sum_{i:1\leq i\leq|G/H|}\phi(g_{i}\sigma\overline{g_{i}\sigma}^{-1})/|G|.

(14)

In addition, for a 3-cocycle
\psi:H^{3}\rightarrow A
, the transfer
{\mathrm{Tr}}_{H}^{G}(\psi)
is known to be represented as a map
G^{3}\rightarrow A
defined by setting

(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\longmapsto\sum_{i:1\leq i\leq|G/H|}\psi(\widetilde{\bar{g_{i}}\sigma_{1}},\widetilde{\overline{g_{i}\sigma_{1}}\sigma_{2}},\widetilde{\overline{g_{i}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\sigma_{3}}).

(15)

In summary, combing the above formulae (
13
)–(
15
) with Lemma
2.1
, we can concretely obtain a representative 3-cocycle of
12\beta^{-1}(c_{2}({\mathrm{Ind}}_{H}^{G}\phi))
as a map
G^{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.
In particular, if every 4-cocycle
C\in H^{4}(G;{\mathbb{Z}})
admits a representation
\rho:G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
such that
12C=12c_{2}(\rho)
, then we can obtain a representative
G^{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
of twelvefold every 3-cocycle of
G
.
For example, if
G
has a periodic cohomology,
G
satisfies this condition;
see
[
Th
]
for the detail and other examples, in comparison with studies of the Chern subring
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(G)\subset H^{\rm even}(G;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
where
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(G)
is defined to be the subring generated by the Chern classes of all complex representations of
G
.

3
Finite groups of type
C_{m}
and covering spaces

In this section, we will introduce a class of finite groups (Definition
3.3
), and show (Theorem
3.4
) that,
if
G
lies in the class, some multiples of the Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant of
M
can be computed from
the cohomology rings of some finite covering spaces of
M
.
To this end, let us prepare the following lemmas:

Lemma 3.1
.

For a surjective homomorphism
f:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow G
and a subgroup
j:H\subset G
,
let
\pi:M_{f,H}\rightarrow M
be the finite covering associated with the subgroup
f^{-1}(H)\subset\pi_{1}(M)
.
Let
m
be the greatest common divisor of
|H|
and
|G/H|
, i.e.,
m=\mathrm{g.c.d}(|H|,|G/H|)
.
Suppose
\bar{\psi}\in H^{3}(G;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
and
\psi\in H^{3}(H;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
j^{*}(\bar{\psi})=m\psi
.
Then,

m^{2}\langle\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}(\psi),f_{*}\circ\iota_{*}([M])\rangle=m^{2}\langle\psi,\mathrm{res}\circ\iota_{*}(f)_{*}([M_{f,H}])\rangle\in{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}.

(16)

Here, the fundamental 3-class
[M_{f,H}]
is canonically defined from
[M]
via the covering
M_{f,H}\rightarrow M
.
In particular, if
\psi=\phi_{k}\smile\beta(\phi_{k})
as in the second term in (
12
), then the pairing (
16
) is computed from the cup product of the space
M_{f,H}
.

Proof.

Choose
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
|G/H|=ms
and
(s,|H|)=1
. Since
H^{*}(H;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
is annihilated by
|H|
,
s
is invertible in computing the pairing on the cohomology of
H
. Since
\pi_{*}([M_{f,H}])=|G/H|[M]
by the definition of
[M_{f,H}]
, we have

m^{2}\langle\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}(\psi),f_{*}\circ\iota_{*}([M])\rangle=s^{-1}\langle\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}(m\psi),f_{*}\circ\iota_{*}(|G/H|[M])\rangle=s^{-1}\langle\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}(j^{*}(\bar{\psi})),f_{*}\circ\pi_{*}\circ\iota_{*}([M_{f,H}])\rangle

(17)

Let
\mathrm{res}(f)
be the restriction of
f
on
\pi_{1}(M_{f,H})
.
By
f\circ\pi_{*}=j_{*}\circ\mathrm{res}(f)
and the familiar identity
\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}(j^{*}(\bar{\psi}))=|G/H|\bar{\psi}
, (
17
) is reduced to

m\langle\bar{\psi},j_{*}\circ\mathrm{res}(f)_{*}\circ\iota_{*}([M_{f,H}])\rangle=m\langle j^{*}(\bar{\psi}),\mathrm{res}(f)_{*}\circ\iota_{*}([M_{f,H}])\rangle

=m^{2}\langle\psi,\mathrm{res}(f)_{*}\circ\iota_{*}([M_{f,H}])\rangle\in{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}.

(18)

This computation means (
16
) exactly.
∎

We remark that the surjectivity of
f
is not so important; indeed, if
f
is not surjective, we may replace
H\subset G
as
H\cap\mathrm{Im}(f)\subset G\cap\mathrm{Im}(f)
.

We also give a sufficient condition for the assumption in Lemma
3.1
:

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose an inclusion
j:H\hookrightarrow G
, and let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
be
\mathrm{g.c.d.}(|H|,|G/H|)
as above. Then, for any
\psi\in H^{n}(H;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
, there exists
\bar{\psi}\in H^{n}(G;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
j^{*}(\bar{\psi})-m\psi\in{\rm Ker}(\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H})
. In particular, if
\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}
is injective,
j^{*}(\bar{\psi})=m\psi
.

Proof.

Choose
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
|G/H|=ms
and
(s,|H|)=1
.
Since
H^{n}(H;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
is annihilated by
|H|
, we can define
\bar{\psi}
to be
s^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}_{H}^{G}(\psi)
. By the familiar equality
|G/H|x=\mathrm{Tr}_{H}^{G}(j^{*}(x))
for any
x\in H^{n}(G;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
,
we have
\mathrm{Tr}_{H}^{G}(j^{*}(\bar{\psi})-m\psi)=0
as required.
∎

In general,
\mathrm{Tr}^{G}_{H}
is far from being injective; to solve non-injectivity, it is sensible to consider a class of finite groups satisfying the condition in Lemma

as follows:

Definition 3.3
.

For
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we say a finite group

to be
of type

, if there are finitely many subgroups

with one–dimensional representation

\phi_{i}:H_{i}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{1}({\mathbb{C}})

(i)

The subring of
H^{*}(G;{\mathbb{Z}})
generated by the total Chern classes

.

(ii)

For any

, g.c.d(
|H_{i}|
,

) is divisible by

\kappa_{i}\in H^{3}(G;{\mathbb{Z}})

Remark 3.5
.

The assumption of type
C_{m}
seems necessary because of unnaturality of the transfer.
To be precise, for any homomorphism
f:K\rightarrow G
and a subgroup
H\subset G
of finite index,

\mathrm{res}(f)^{*}\circ{\mathrm{Tr}}_{H}^{G}
and
{\mathrm{Tr}}_{f^{-1}(H)}^{K}\circ f^{*}
are not always equal. For instance,
for any odd prime
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}

if
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}=:H=K\subset G:=\mathfrak{S}_{p}
and
f
is the inclusion
K={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{p}
, then
f^{*}\circ{\mathrm{Tr}}_{H}^{G}=0
and
{\mathrm{Tr}}_{f^{-1}(H)}^{K}\circ f^{*}
is the identity on
H^{1}(\bullet;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}).

Thus, in general, (

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(\pi_{1}(M),G)

Example 3.7

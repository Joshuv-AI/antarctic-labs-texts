Weighted uniform convergence of entire Grünwald operators on the real line
Friedrich Littmann, Mark Spanier

Abstract
. We consider weighted uniform convergence of entire analogues of the Grünwald operator on the real line. The main result deals with convergence of entire interpolations of exponential type τ > 0 \tau>0 at zeros of Bessel functions in spaces with homogeneous weights. We discuss extensions to Grünwald operators from de Branges spaces.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 41A05; Secondary 41A17, 30E05

1.
Introduction and Results

An entire function
F
, not identically zero, has exponential type if
\tau(F)
defined by

\displaystyle\tau(F)=\limsup_{|z|\to\infty}|z|^{-1}\log|F(z)|

(1)

is finite. The nonnegative number
\tau(F)
is called the exponential type of
F
.

Let
w
be a measurable, nonnegative function on
\mathbb{R}
; we call
w
a weight. We denote by
\mathcal{B}_{p}(\tau,w)
the space of entire functions
F
of exponential type
\tau\geq 0
with
Fw\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
. For functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
and a weight
w
, we seek discrete sets
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and
G_{\tau}f\in\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\tau,w)
with
G_{\tau}f(t)=f(t)
for
t\in\mathcal{T}
and

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\|(G_{\tau}f-f)w\|_{\infty}=0.

(2)

By way of motivation we review known results from polynomial interpolation. Fejér discovered the following property of interpolation at the zeros of the Chebyshev polynomials: denoting by
x_{n,k}
the
k
th zero of the
n
th Chebyshev polynomial, there exists a polynomial
H_{2n-1}
of degree
2n-1
with
H_{2n-1}(x_{n,k})=f(x_{n,k})
and
H_{2n-1}^{\prime}(x_{n,k})=0
such that for continuous
f
the statement
\|f-H_{2n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}[-1,1]}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
holds.

There has been a large amount of research into analogous statements for weighted polynomial spaces where the interpolation points are chosen to be zeros of certain associated orthogonal polynomials, cf. Horváth
[
7
]
, Lubinsky
[
8
]
, Szabados
[
9
,
10
]
, Szabó
[
12
]
, and the references therein. For earlier work we refer to the book by Szabados and Vértesi
[
11
]
. There are different generalizations of Fejér’s result; the so called Fejér-Hermite interpolation has derivative zero at the interpolation nodes, while the Grünwald operator assigns a derivative value at each node that depends on the function and the location of the nodes. Concretely, the polynomial Grünwald operator is given by

z\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(y_{n,k})\ell_{n,k}^{2}(z)

where
(y_{n,k})
is a given set of nodes and
\ell_{n,k}
is the
k
th Lagrange interpolating polynomial of degree
\leq n
for
(y_{n,k})
. As we indicate below, the corresponding operator for functions of exponential type is in some sense the most natural generalization of Fejér’s result to weighted spaces on the real line. It was pointed out in Gervais, Rahman, and Schmeisser
[
4
]
that the series

\displaystyle F_{\tau}f(z)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f(\tau^{-1}k)\frac{\sin^{2}(\tau z)}{(\tau z-k)^{2}}

(3)

has convergence properties entirely analogous to the Fejér result but no other entire Grünwald operators seem to have been investigated.

Let
\nu>-1
. Our first results deals with homogeneous weights

w_{\nu}(x)=|x|^{2\nu+1}.

Let
J_{\nu}
be the Bessel function of order
\nu
of the first kind. We define entire functions
A_{\nu}
and
B_{\nu}
by

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{\nu}(z)&=\Gamma(\nu+1)(z/2)^{-\nu}J_{\nu}(z),\\
B_{\nu}(z)&=\Gamma(\nu+1)(z/2)^{-\nu}J_{\nu+1}(z),\end{split}

(4)

and for
\tau>0
we define the formal series
G_{\nu,\tau}f,H_{\nu,\tau}f
by

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{\nu,\tau}f(z)&=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\nu}}f(\tau^{-1}t)\frac{A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau z)}{A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(\tau z-t)^{2}}\\
H_{\nu,\tau}f(z)&=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\nu+1}}f(\tau^{-1}t)\frac{B_{\nu}^{2}(\tau z)}{B_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(\tau z-t)^{2}}\end{split}

(5)

where
\mathcal{T}_{\nu}=\{\pm\xi:J_{\nu}(\xi)=0,\xi>0\}
.

Let
f
be continuous on
\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
and assume that
fw_{\nu}
has a limit at the origin. We say that
fw_{\nu}
has a uniformly continuous extension to
\mathbb{R}
if, after defining the value of
fw_{\nu}
at the origin to be this limit, the resulting function is uniformly continuous on
\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\nu>-1
with
\nu\neq-\frac{1}{2}
and
\tau>0
. If
f\in C(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
with
fw_{\nu}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, then
G_{\nu,\tau}f
and
H_{\nu,\tau}f
define entire functions of exponential type
2\tau
. If in addition
fw_{\nu}
has a uniformly continuous extension to
\mathbb{R}
with

\lim_{x\to 0}f(x)w_{\nu}(x)=0,

then (a) for
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}

\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\|(G_{\nu,\tau}f-f)w_{\nu}\|_{\infty}=0,

and (b) for
-1<\nu<-\frac{1}{2}

\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\|(H_{\nu,\tau}f-f)w_{\nu}\|_{\infty}=0.

The usual approach to polynomial analogues of (
2
) consists in requiring a condition of the form
fv\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with a different weight
v
which is usually more restrictive than the target weight (but not always, see Szabó
[
12
, Corollary 2]
), prove (
2
) for a dense set, and extend to the smaller space. In this paper we follow a different approach that is modeled after Fejér’s original proof. We construct approximations
L_{\nu,\tau}
to
1/w_{\nu}
and use the identity

\displaystyle(G_{\nu,\tau}f-f)w_{\nu}=(G_{\nu,\tau}f-fL_{\nu,\tau}w_{\nu})w_{\nu}+fw_{\nu}(L_{\nu,\tau}w_{\nu}-1).

(6)

A good candidate for
L_{\nu,\tau}
is the extremal minorant of
1/w_{\nu}
among functions of exponential type
2\tau
with respect to
L^{1}(w_{\nu})
norm (cf.
[
2
]
). We show in Lemmas
3
and
6
that
L_{\nu,\tau}=G_{\nu,\tau}w_{\nu}^{-1}
for
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
and
L_{\nu,\tau}=H_{\nu,\tau}w_{\nu}^{-1}
for
-1<\nu<-\frac{1}{2}
which allows estimation of the first summand on the right. The difference
L_{\nu,\tau}-1/w_{\nu}
has a representation in terms of Laplace transforms that gives
L^{\infty}
bounds to control the second summand.

Remarks.

(a)

Uniform convergence fails for
f=1/w_{\nu}
if
\nu\neq\frac{1}{2}
, i.e., the condition
f(x)w_{\nu}(x)\to 0
as
x\to 0
is necessary. (The case
\nu=-\frac{1}{2}
is the unweighted case where (
3
) is used.)

(b)

To obtain uniform convergence of
(G_{\nu,\tau}f-f)w_{\nu}
for
-1<\nu<-\frac{1}{2}
requires considerably more restrictive conditions on
f
, and the same remark holds for uniform convergence of
(H_{\nu,\tau}f-f)w_{\nu}
for
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
. This can be traced back to the fact that the series
G_{\nu,\tau}(1/w_{\nu})
and
H_{\nu,\tau}(1/w_{\nu})
do not minorize
1/w_{\nu}
for these choices of
\nu
.

A second candidate for
L_{\nu,\tau}
comes from the observation that the space of entire functions
F
of exponential type
\tau
with
\|F\|_{L^{2}(w_{\nu})}<\infty
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. Denoting by
K_{\nu,\tau}(w,z)
the reproducing kernel, it follows from de Branges
[
3
, Theorem 22]
that

\displaystyle K_{\nu,\tau}(\bar{z},z)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\nu}}K_{\nu,\tau}(\tau^{-1}t,\tau^{-1}t)\frac{A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau z)}{A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(\tau z-t)^{2}},

(7)

leading to a version of Theorem
1
with less general assumptions.

An identity analogous to (
7
) holds for every weight
w
with the property that evaluation functionals are bounded on
\mathcal{B}_{2}(\tau,w)
. In particular, reproducing kernels enable us to deal with weights of the form
w(x)=|W(x)|^{-2}
where
W
is a Hermite-Biehler entire function of exponential type. Since the corresponding statements require some notation from the theory of de Branges spaces, we give it as Theorem
2
in Section
5
.

2.
Notation and Bessel function estimates

Throughout this article
c_{\nu},C_{\nu}
denote unspecified positive constants depending only on
\nu
. (Their value may change between lines.) We use the notation
f(x,\tau)\simeq_{\nu}g(x,\tau)
to mean that
f(x,\tau)\leq c_{\nu}g(x,\tau)
and
g(x,\tau)\leq C_{\nu}f(x,\tau)
for all
x
and
\tau
.

For
\lambda\geq 0
and complex
z
we denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
the Gaussian

\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}(z)=e^{-\pi\lambda z^{2}}.

Bessel functions satisfy
J_{\nu}^{2}(x)+J_{\nu+1}^{2}(x)\simeq_{\nu}x^{-1}
for
|x|\geq 1
. Hence for real
x

A_{\nu}^{2}(x)+B_{\nu}^{2}(x)\simeq_{\nu}\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }|x|\leq 1,\\
|x|^{-2\nu-1}&\text{ if }|x|\geq 1.\end{cases}

A direct calculation gives

\displaystyle A_{\nu}^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=-\Gamma(\nu+1)(z/2)^{-\nu}J_{\nu+1}(z)

\displaystyle A_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)}{z^{2+\nu}}\left((2\nu+4\nu^{2}-z^{2})J_{\nu}(z)-z(1+2\nu)J_{\nu-1}(z)\right)

and this gives
|A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)|\simeq_{\nu}|t|^{-\nu-\frac{1}{2}}
and
|A_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(t)|\simeq_{\nu}|t|^{-\nu-\frac{3}{2}}
for
t\in\mathcal{T}_{\nu}
. Analogous statements hold for
B_{\nu}
and its derivatives, and they lead to the same bounds for
B_{\nu}^{\prime}(u)
and
B_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(u)
when
u\in\mathcal{T}_{\nu+1}
. These estimates are used repeatedly in the calculations below.

3.
The case
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}

3.1.
Extremal minorants

We review the construction of extremal minorants from
[
2
]
. In view of (
12
) below, we start by constructing entire minorants of exponential type
\tau
of the Gaussian
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
with nodes at the zeros of
A_{\nu}
. It was observed in
[
2
]
that this can be achieved by first constructing a minorant of type zero of the exponential
e^{-\lambda z}
at zeros of
A_{\nu}(\sqrt{z})
(see (
10
) below) and then substituting
z\mapsto z^{2}
. In
[
2
]
the integration in
\lambda
is performed on the Fourier transform side to get maximum generality, but since we require
L^{\infty}
estimates, we integrate the minorants directly. (The error estimates in Lemma
2
are not contained in
[
2
]
.)

Since the derivative of a minorant of
1/w_{\nu}
must interpolate the derivative of
1/w_{\nu}
at any interpolation point, we consider
A_{\nu}^{2}
in the following. We have

A_{\nu}^{2}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{z^{2}}{\eta_{\nu,j}^{2}}\right)^{2}

where
0<\eta_{\nu,1}<\eta_{\nu,2}<...
are the positive zeros of
J_{\nu}
. It is known that
\sum\eta_{\nu,j}^{-2}<\infty
. Since
A_{\nu}^{2}
is even, the function
F_{\nu}
defined by
A_{\nu}^{2}(z)=F_{\nu}(z^{2})
is entire, nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
, and has only positive (double) zeros
z_{j}=\eta_{\nu,j}^{2}
. We have
F_{\nu}(0)=1
and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z_{j}^{-1}<\infty.

It follows from the theory of Polya-Laguerre entire functions (e.g.,
[
6
, Ch. III Corollary 5.4 and Ch. V Corollary 3.1]
) that
g_{\nu}
defined for real
t
by

\displaystyle g_{\nu}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty}\frac{e^{tz}}{F_{\nu}(z)}dz

(8)

is nonnegative, equal to zero on
[0,\infty)
, and satisfies for
\Re z<z_{1}
the inversion formula

\frac{1}{F_{\nu}(z)}=\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-zt}g_{\nu}(t)dt.

The inversion formula can be put in the form

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda z}=F_{\nu}(z)\int_{-\infty}^{\lambda}e^{-zt}g_{\nu}(t-\lambda)dt

(9)

in
\Re z<z_{1}
, and since
g_{\nu}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}
it follows that the entire function
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\nu}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\nu}(z)=e^{-\lambda z}-F_{\nu}(z)\int_{0}^{\lambda}e^{-wz}g_{\nu}(w-\lambda)dw

(10)

satisfies
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)\leq e^{-\lambda x}
for all real
x
. Evidently
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=e^{-\lambda x}
at the zeros of
F
. Hence,
z\mapsto\mathcal{A}_{\pi\lambda,\nu}(z^{2})
is a minorant of
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{R}
that interpolates
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
at the points of
\mathcal{T}_{\nu}
. To simplify notation in the following we set

\xi_{1}=\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\nu,1}^{2},\qquad\xi_{2}=\frac{3}{4}\eta_{\nu,1}^{2}.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
. There exists
c_{\nu}>0
with the following property.

(a)

For all complex
z

\left|e^{-\lambda z}-\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\nu}(z)\right|\leq c_{\nu}|F_{\nu}(z)|\frac{e^{-\lambda\xi_{1}}-e^{-\lambda\Re z}}{\Re z-\xi_{1}}.

(b)

In the half plane
\Re z\leq\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\nu,1}^{2}

|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\nu}(z)|\leq c_{\nu}|F_{\nu}(z)|e^{-\lambda\xi_{2}}.

Moreover, the function
z\mapsto\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\nu}(z^{2})
has exponential type
2
.

Proof.

Combining the properties of
g_{\nu}
above with
[
6
, Ch. V Theorem 2.1]
implies that for every
\xi\in(0,\eta_{\nu,1}^{2})
there exists
c_{\xi}>0
with

\displaystyle\begin{cases}g_{\nu}(t)=0\text{ for }t>0,\\
0\leq g_{\nu}(t)\leq c_{\xi}\exp(\xi t)\text{ for }t<0.\end{cases}

(11)

Applying (
11
) with
\xi=\xi_{1}
in (
10
) gives for every complex
z

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\nu}(z)-e^{-\lambda z}\right|\leq c_{\xi_{1}}|F_{\nu}(z)|\int_{0}^{\lambda}e^{-w\Re z+\xi_{1}(w-\lambda)}dw

and (a) follows.

Let now
\Re z<\xi_{2}
. We replace the term
e^{-\lambda z}
in (
10
) by (
9
), combine the integrals, apply (
11
) with
\xi=\xi_{2}
, and obtain

|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\nu}(z)|\leq c_{\xi_{2}}|F_{\nu}(z)|\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{(w-\lambda)(\xi_{2}-\Re z)}dw.

After evaluation of the integration we further restrict to
\Re z\leq\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\nu,1}^{2}
which leads to (b). Moreover, since
e^{-\lambda z}
is bounded in
\Re z\geq\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\nu,1}^{2}
, combining (a) with (b) shows that
|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\nu}|/(1+|F_{\nu}|)
is bounded in
\mathbb{C}
by a constant (which may depend on
\lambda
and
\nu
). After substituting
z\mapsto z^{2}
the final statement of the lemma follows since
A_{\nu}
has exponential type
1
.
∎

For functions that are subordinated to the Gaussian
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}(z)=e^{-\pi\lambda z^{2}}
, minorants can now be obtained by integrating an appropriate measure in
\lambda
. We use this to construct an approximation to
1/w_{\nu}
. The measure is obtained by noting that for
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
and (real)
x\neq 0

\displaystyle|x|^{-2\nu-1}=\pi^{\nu+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\nu+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}(x)\lambda^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}d\lambda.

(12)

It follows from Lemma
1
that in the range
\Re(z^{2})\leq\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\nu,1}^{2}
the integral (in
\lambda
) of
\mathcal{A}_{\pi\lambda,\nu}(z^{2})
with respect to the measure from (
12
) is convergent, while in the range
\Re(z^{2})\geq\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\nu,1}^{2}
the same is true for the integral of
\mathcal{A}_{\pi\lambda,\nu}(z^{2})-\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}(z)
. Since
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
is also integrable in the latter region with respect to this measure, the function
z\mapsto L_{\nu}(z)
defined by

L_{\nu}(z)=\pi^{\nu+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\nu+\tfrac{1}{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{\pi\lambda,\nu}(z^{2})\lambda^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}d\lambda

is entire and a minorant of
|x|^{-2\nu-1}
that interpolates this function at the zeros of
A_{\nu}
. Furthermore, investigating the bounds obtained from Lemma
1
(a) in
\Re z\geq\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\nu,1}^{2}
and Lemma
1
(b) in
\Re z\leq\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\nu,1}^{2}
shows that
L_{\nu}
has exponential type
2
. To obtain a minorant of type
2\tau
, we define

L_{\nu,\tau}(z)=\tau^{2\nu+1}L_{\nu}(\tau z).

Lemma 2
.

Let
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
and
\tau>0
. There exists
c_{\nu}>0
so that for real
x

0\leq|x|^{-2\nu-1}-L_{\nu,\tau}(x)\leq c_{\nu}A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau x)\frac{|x|^{-2\nu-1}-\left|\frac{1}{2}\tau^{-1}\eta_{\nu,1}\right|^{-2\nu-1}}{\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\nu,1}^{2}-\tau^{2}x^{2}}.

For (real)
|x|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\tau^{-1}\eta_{\nu,1}
we also have

0\leq L_{\nu,\tau}(x)\leq c_{\nu}A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau x)\left(\tfrac{\eta_{\nu,1}}{2\tau}\right)^{-2\nu-1}.

Proof.

These estimates follow by setting
z=x^{2}
in Lemma
1
, integrate with respect to
\lambda
against the measure from (
12
) to get the bounds for
\tau=1
, multiply by
\tau^{2\nu+1}
and substitute
x\mapsto\tau x
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
and
\tau>0
. The identity

L_{\nu,\tau}(z)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\nu}}|\tau^{-1}t|^{-2\nu-1}\frac{A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau z)}{A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(\tau z-t)^{2}}

holds for all complex
z
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
1
(a) that
x\mapsto\mathcal{A}_{\pi\lambda,\nu}(x^{2})
is in
L^{1}(w_{\nu})
. The entire function
E_{\nu}=A_{\nu}-iB_{\nu}
is Hermite-Biehler, that is, the inequality
|E(z)|>|E(\bar{z})|
holds for all
\Re z>0
(cf. de Branges
[
3
, Section 50]
). It follows that
z\mapsto\mathcal{A}_{\pi\lambda,\nu}(z^{2})
satisfies the assumptions of Gonçalves
[
5
, Theorem 1]
, see in particular the discussion of homogeneous spaces in
[
5
, Section 4.1]
. In place of
E_{\nu}
we use
iE_{\nu}
(which is also Hermite-Biehler) and obtain an interpolation series at the zeros of
A_{\nu}
.

The minorant property implies that
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}(z)-\mathcal{A}_{\pi\lambda,\nu}(z^{2})
must have derivative equal to zero at
z=t\in\mathcal{T}_{\nu}
. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{\pi\lambda,\nu}(z^{2})&=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\nu}}\Bigg(\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}(t)\frac{A_{\nu}^{2}(z)}{A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(z-t)^{2}}\left(1-\frac{A_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(t)}{A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)}(z-t)\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)\frac{A_{\nu}^{2}(z)}{A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(z-t)}\Bigg).\end{split}

(13)

Combining estimates from Section
2
with the classical fact that the zeros of
J_{\nu}
grow at the same rate as the positive integers allows application of Fubini to interchange summation in
t
and integration in
\lambda
. It follows that
L_{\nu}
has a representation obtained from (
13
) by replacing
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}(t)
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)
with
w_{\nu}(t)^{-1}

and
\partial/\partial t[w_{\nu}(t)^{-1}]
, respectively. The differential equation of the Bessel function gives

(2\nu+1)A_{\nu}^{\prime}(z)+zA_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(z)=-zA_{\nu}(z),

which implies that
A_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(t)/A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)=-(2\nu+1)/t
for
t\in\mathcal{T}_{\nu}
. It follows that the series for
L_{\nu}
simplifies to the right hand side of the claimed identity for
\tau=1
, and scaling in
\tau
gives the general result.
∎

3.2.
Grünwald operator

We give the proof of Theorem
1
(a). Let
\tau>0
and
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
. Since
fw_{\nu}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, it follows that the series defining
G_{\nu,\tau}f
converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
and thus defines an entire function. Since the difference of consecutive zeros of
A_{\nu}
is
\simeq_{\nu}1
we see that
|G_{\nu,\tau}f(z)|\leq c_{\nu}\tau^{2}|A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau z)|
and hence
G_{\nu,\tau}f
has exponential type
2\tau
. (For
|\Im z|\leq 1
we use a contour integral of
(u-z)^{-1}G_{\nu,\tau}f(u)
over a rectangle with vertical sides through zeros of
A_{\nu}
that are at least distance
1
away from
z
.)

Let
\varepsilon>0
. By assumption there exists
\delta>0
so that for
|x-y|<\delta
and
|u|<\delta

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|f(y)w_{\nu}(y)-f(x)w_{\nu}(x)\right|&<\varepsilon\\
|f(u)w_{\nu}(u)|&<\varepsilon.\end{split}

(14)

The identity (
6
) takes the form

\displaystyle\big(

\displaystyle G_{\nu,\tau}(x)-f(x)\big)w_{\nu}(x)

(15)

\displaystyle=w_{\nu}(x)\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\nu}}\left(f(\tau^{-1}t)w_{\nu}(\tau^{-1}t)-f(x)w_{\nu}(x)\right)\frac{w_{\nu}(\tau^{-1}t)^{-1}A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau x)}{A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(\tau x-t)^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+f(x)w_{\nu}(x)(L_{\nu,\tau}(x)w_{\nu}(x)-1).

We consider the sum first and partition
\mathcal{T}_{\nu}
into
t
with
|\tau^{-1}t-x|<\delta
and with
|\tau^{-1}t-x|\geq\delta
. We observe for all
\tau>0

\displaystyle w_{\nu}(x)\sum_{|\tau^{-1}t-x|<\delta}

\displaystyle\left(f(\tau^{-1}t)w_{\nu}(\tau^{-1}t)-f(x)w_{\nu}(x)\right)\frac{|\tau^{-1}t|^{-2\nu-1}A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau x)}{A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(\tau x-t)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\,w_{\nu}(x)L_{\nu,\tau}(x)\leq\varepsilon.

(16)

For the second sum we use that
fw_{\nu}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}
(by
M
, say), and we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{\nu}(x)\sum_{|\tau^{-1}t-x|\geq\delta}\big(&f(\tau^{-1}t)w_{\nu}(\tau^{-1}t)-f(x)w_{\nu}(x)\big)\frac{|\tau^{-1}t|^{-2\nu-1}A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau x)}{A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(\tau x-t)^{2}}\\
&\leq 2Mw_{\nu}(x)A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau x)\sum_{|\tau^{-1}t-x|\geq\delta}\frac{|\tau^{-1}t|^{-2\nu-1}}{A_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(\tau x-t)^{2}}\end{split}

(17)

Combining the estimates of Section

with
w_{\nu}(x)\leq w_{\nu}(1/\tau)
for

gives
w_{\nu}(x)A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau x)\leq c_{\nu}w_{\nu}(1/\tau)
for all

. Denote by

greater (smaller) than

\displaystyle\leq c_{\nu}M\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathcal{T}_{\nu}\\
|\tau^{-1}t-x|\geq\delta\end{subarray}}\frac{|\tau^{-1}t-\tau^{-1}t_{+}|}{(x-\tau^{-1}t)^{2}}\leq\frac{c_{\nu}M}{\tau\delta}

where the last inequality above follows by recognizing the series as a Riemann sum for the integral of

on
\mathbb{R}\backslash[x-\delta,x+\delta]
.

It remains to analyze

\displaystyle|L_{\nu,\tau}(x)w_{\nu}(x)-1|\leq c_{\nu}A_{\nu}^{2}(\tau x)w_{\nu}(x)\frac{\left|w_{\nu}(x)^{-1}-w_{\nu}(\frac{\eta_{\nu,1}}{2\tau})^{-1}\right|}{\frac{\eta_{\nu,1}^{2}}{4}-\tau^{2}x^{2}}

Lemma 4

Lemma 5

Lemma 7

5.
De Branges spaces

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
we denote by
H^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
the space of analytic functions
F
in the upper half plane
\mathbb{C}^{+}
for which
\sup_{y>0}\|F(.+iy)\|_{p}
is finite. We require a few facts from the theory of de Branges spaces. For a more complete picture we refer to de Branges
[
3
]
for
p=2
and Baranov
[
1
]
for arbitrary
p
.

An entire function
E
satisfying

\displaystyle|E(z)|>|E(\bar{z})|

(22)

for
\Im z>0
will be called a Hermite-Biehler function. Throughout this article we assume that
E
has no real zeros. We set

\mathcal{H}^{p}(E)=\{F\text{ entire}:F/E,F^{*}/E\in H^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{+})\},

where
F^{*}
is the entire function
F^{*}(z)=\overline{F(\bar{z})}
, and the norm is given by
F\mapsto\|F/E\|_{p}
. We write
E=A-iB
with real entire
A=2^{-1}(E+E^{*})
and
B=2^{-1}i(E-E^{*})
, and we denote by
\mathcal{T}_{A}
the set of zeros of
A
. These are necessarily real by (
22
). We denote by
K_{E}
the function

\displaystyle K_{E}(w,z)=\frac{A(\bar{w})B(z)-A(z)B(\bar{w})}{\pi(z-\bar{w})},

(23)

and we observe that
z\mapsto K_{E}(w,z)
is an entire function in
\mathcal{H}^{p}(E)
for all
w\in\mathbb{C}
and
p\in[0,\infty]
. The Cauchy integral formula for the upper half plane and the alternative representation
K_{E}(w,z)=[2\pi i(z-\bar{w})]^{-1}(E(\bar{w})E^{*}(z)-E(z)E^{*}(\bar{w}))
may be used to check that
K_{E}(w,z)
is the reproducing kernel for
\mathcal{H}^{2}(E)
. A function
\varphi_{E}
with the property
E(x)e^{i\varphi_{E}(x)}\in\mathbb{R}
for all real
x
is called a
phase
of
E
. As a consequence of (
22
) it may be chosen to be analytic on an open set containing the real line.

If
\mathcal{B}_{2}(\tau,w)
has bounded evaluation functionals, then by
[
3
, Theorem 23]
it is isometrically equal to a space
\mathcal{H}^{2}(E)
.

Let
f
be continuous and
f|E|^{-2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. We define the formal series
G_{E}f
by

G_{E}f(z)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{A}}f(t)\frac{A^{2}(z)}{A^{\prime}(t)^{2}(z-t)^{2}}.

In the following
\{E_{\tau}:\tau>0\}
is a collection of Hermite-Biehler functions
E_{\tau}
of exponential type
\tau
. We set
G_{\tau}f=G_{E_{\tau}}f
,
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}=\mathcal{T}_{A_{\tau}}
, and
\varphi_{\tau}=\varphi_{E_{\tau}}
if the choice of
E_{\tau}
is clear from the context. We consider
f
with the following properties.

(a)

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
and
\tau_{0}>0
such that for all
x,t\in\mathbb{R}
and all
\tau\geq\tau_{0}

\displaystyle|x-t|<\delta\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\left|\frac{f(x)}{|E_{\tau}(x)|^{2}}-\frac{f(t)}{|E_{\tau}(t)|^{2}}\right|<\varepsilon

(24)

(b)

There exists
M>0
so that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and positive
\tau

\displaystyle\frac{f(x)}{|E_{\tau}(x)|^{2}}\leq M

(25)

If
|E_{\tau}|^{-2}
converges to
w
uniformly in
x
as
\tau\to\infty
, then these conditions may be reformulated in terms of uniform continuity and uniform boundedness of
fw
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
E_{\tau}
,
\tau>0
, be a Hermite-Biehler entire function of exponential type
\tau
without real zeros, and such that there exists
C>0
so that
|\varphi_{\tau}^{\prime}(x)-\tau|\leq C
for all
x
and
\tau
.

(a)

If
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies (
24
) and (
25
), then
G_{\tau}
converges uniformly on compact sets,
G_{\tau}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(E_{\tau}^{2})
and

\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\|(G_{\tau}f-f)E_{\tau}^{-2}\|_{\infty}=0.

(b)

If in addition there exists constants
c>0,d>0
with

c\leq|E_{\tau}(x)|^{2}w(x)\leq d

for all real
x
and
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
, then

\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\|(G_{\tau}f-f)w\|_{\infty}=0.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0,\delta>0,\tau_{0}>0
as in (
24
). We apply
[
3
, Theorem 22]
with
iE
in place of
E
to
z\mapsto K_{\tau}(x,z)
with

to obtain

K_{\tau}(x,x)=\|K_{\tau}(x,.)/E\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\tau}}\frac{|K_{\tau}(x,t)|^{2}}{K_{\tau}(t,t)}.

Equation (
23
) leads to

\displaystyle K_{\tau}(x,x)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\tau}}K_{\tau}(t,t)\frac{A_{\tau}^{2}(x)}{A_{\tau}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(x-t)^{2}}.

(26)

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{f(x)-G_{\tau}(x)}{K_{\tau}(x,x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K_{\tau}(x,x)}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\tau}}\left(\frac{f(x)}{K_{\tau}(x,x)}-\frac{f(t)}{K_{\tau}(t,t)}\right)\frac{K_{\tau}(t,t)A_{\tau}^{2}(x)}{A_{\tau}^{\prime}(t)^{2}(x-t)^{2}}.

We recall the identity
\pi K_{\tau}(x,x)=\varphi_{\tau}^{\prime}(x)|E_{\tau}(x)|^{2}
from
[
3
, Problem 48]
. In the range

we use
\pi K_{\tau}(t,t)A_{\tau}^{\prime}(t)^{-2}=(\pi\varphi_{\tau}^{\prime}(t))^{-1}\leq c\tau^{-1}
. In the range
|x-t|<\delta
we add and subtract
f(t)(\varphi_{\tau}^{\prime}(x)|E_{\tau}(t)|^{2})^{-1}
and use

with

independent of

and

. Since the values in
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}
are the points where

is purely imaginary we have

for consecutive

. With these estimates (a) can be proved analogous to (

17
) and (

∎

Let
\tau\geq\tau_{0}\geq 0
, and assume that

where
\mathcal{T}_{\tau,\alpha}
is the set of (real) zeros of

. A direct consequence of Theorem
2
is the following statement.

Corollary 1
.

Let
f|W|^{-2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be uniformly continuous. Then

defines an entire function of exponential type

, and

\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\|(G_{\tau,\alpha}f-f)|W|^{-2}\|_{\infty}=0.

This corollary includes for example the Poisson measure

.

We finally describe some open questions. It would be of interest to have a characterization of those entire functions that may be used in place of
A_{\tau}
and give uniform convergence of the corresponding Grünwald operator, but even for simple measures this seems out of reach. We consider the concrete example
w(x)=x^{2}+1
. First, for the Hermite-Biehler entire function

\displaystyle E_{\tau}(z)=\left(\frac{2}{\sinh(2\tau)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\sin(\tau(z+i))}{z+i}

(27)

we observe that
\tanh(\tau)\leq(x^{2}+1)|E_{\tau}(x)|^{2}\leq\coth(\tau)
. Since

K_{\tau}(x,x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{\tau}{(x^{2}+1)|E_{\tau}(x)|^{2}}-\frac{1}{x^{2}+1}\right)|E_{\tau}(x)|^{2},

we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\tau\left(\frac{\text{sinh}(2\tau)}{\text{cosh}(2\tau)-\cos(2\tau x)}\right)-\frac{1}{x^{2}+1},

\displaystyle A_{\tau}(z)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\text{sinh}(2\tau)}}\frac{z\text{cosh}(\tau)\sin(\tau z)+\text{sinh}(\tau)\cos(\tau z)}{z^{2}+1}.

Hence for

satisfies

. This is true in particular for

.

On the other hand, it can be shown that starting with an even Polya-Laguerre function

of exponential type

and

as

. In particular, using the dilation

in place of (

In contrast,

has the property that

as
\tau\to\infty
for

with

that is uniformly continuous on

; the series

has a closed form that may be used to show

, which allows an argument analogous to the proofs of Theorems

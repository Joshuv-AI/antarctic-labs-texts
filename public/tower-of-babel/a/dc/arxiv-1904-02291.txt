Finite-Sample Concentration of the Multinomial in Relative Entropy Author’s version of paper published in the IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, DOI:10.1109/TIT.2020.2996134 .
Rohit Agrawal

Abstract
We show that the moment generating function of the Kullback–Leibler divergence (relative entropy) between the empirical distribution of n n independent samples from a distribution P P over a finite alphabet of size k k (i.e. a multinomial distribution) and P P itself is no more than that of a gamma distribution with shape k − 1 k-1 and rate n n . The resulting exponential concentration inequality becomes meaningful (less than 1) when the divergence ε \varepsilon is larger than ( k − 1 ) / n (k-1)/n , whereas the standard method of types bound requires ε > 1 n ⋅ log ⁡ ( n + k − 1 k − 1 ) ≥ ( k − 1 ) / n ⋅ log ⁡ ( 1 + n / ( k − 1 ) ) \varepsilon>\frac{1}{n}\cdot\log{\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}}\geq(k-1)/n\cdot\log(1+n/(k-1)) , thus saving a factor of order log ⁡ ( n / k ) \log(n/k) in the standard regime of parameters where n ≫ k n\gg k . As a consequence, we also obtain finite-sample bounds on all the moments of the empirical divergence (equivalently, the discrete likelihood-ratio statistic), which are within constant factors (depending on the moment) of their asymptotic values. Our proof proceeds via a simple reduction to the case k = 2 k=2 of a binary alphabet (i.e. a binomial distribution), and has the property that improvements in the case of k = 2 k=2 directly translate to improvements for general k k . In particular, we conjecture a bound on the binomial moment generating function that would almost close the quadratic gap between our finite-sample bound and the asymptotic moment generating function bound from Wilks’ theorem (which does not hold for finite samples). Keywords: Concentration inequalities, empirical distributions, Kullback–Leibler divergence, likelihood-ratio test, binomial tail bounds



I
Introduction

A key problem in statistics is to understand the rate of convergence of
an empirical distribution of independent samples to the true underlying
distribution. Indeed, this convergence is the basis of hypothesis
testing and statistical inference in general
[
1
]
.
For the case of discrete distributions over a finite alphabet, the
Neyman–Pearson lemma
[
2
]
shows that for optimal
hypothesis testing it is important to consider the

likelihood-ratio statistic
, or equivalently

[
3
]
, the Kullback–Leibler divergence
(relative entropy) from the true distribution to the empirical
distribution, as formally defined in
I.1
:

Definition I.1
.

Let
X=(X_{1},\dotsc,X_{k})
be distributed according to a
multinomial distribution with
n
samples and probabilities
P=(p_{1},\dotsc,p_{k})
, and define

V_{n,k,P}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny def}}}{=}}\KL\Big(\left(X_{1}/n,\dotsc,X_{k}/n\right)\;\Big\|\;\mathopen{}\left(p_{1},\dotsc,p_{k}\right)\Big)

where

\displaystyle\KL\Big(\left(q_{1},\dotsc,q_{k}\right)\;\Big\|\;\mathopen{}\left(p_{1},\dotsc,p_{k}\right)\Big)\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny def}}}{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}q_{i}\log\frac{q_{i}}{p_{i}}

is the Kullback–Leibler divergence between two probability
distributions on a finite set
\left\{1,\dotsc,k\right\}
(represented as
probability mass functions), and
\log
is in the natural base (as are
all logarithms and exponentials in this work). The
likelihood-ratio statistic is
2nV_{n,k,P}

[
3
]
.

In this language, the Neyman–Pearson lemma states that the uniformly most powerful
hypothesis test for significance
\alpha
rejects a hypothesis

P=(p_{1},\dots,p_{k})
if and only if
V_{n,k,P}
is at least

\varepsilon_{\alpha}
, where
\varepsilon_{\alpha}
is such that

\Pr\left[V_{n,k,P}\geq\varepsilon_{\alpha}\right]\leq\alpha
. To apply this test in
practice an upper bound on
\varepsilon_{\alpha}
is needed, so to maximize the
power of a provably correct finite-sample test we seek upper bounds on

\Pr\left[V\geq\varepsilon\right]
which are meaningful (less than
1
) for
\varepsilon
as
small as possible. Equivalently, tight control on
\varepsilon
reduces the
number of samples needed to obtain a given level of significance, which
is of importance in areas as disparate as high-dimensional statistics

[
4
]
, combinatorial constructions in
complexity theory
[
5
]
, and private
machine learning
[
6
]
.

In this work, we focus on tail bounds for
\Pr\left[V_{n,k,P}\geq\varepsilon\right]
which decay
exponentially for small
\varepsilon
, ideally when
\varepsilon\approx\Exp\left[V_{n,k,P}\right]
. Paninski
[
7
]
showed that

\Exp\left[V_{n,k,P}\right]\leq\log\left(1+\frac{k-1}{n}\right)\leq\frac{k-1}{n}
, and
conversely Jiao et al.
[
8
]
showed that
for
P
the uniform distribution and large enough
n
that

\Exp\left[V_{n,k,U_{k}}\right]\geq\frac{k-1}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{2}
, so in general the
smallest
\varepsilon
for which one can expect a meaningful bound is of order

(k-1)/n
. In this work, we derive the first tail bound decaying
exponentially in
\varepsilon
for
\varepsilon
as small as
(k-1)/n
, whereas existing
bounds either require
\varepsilon
to be at least order
(k-1)/n\cdot\log(n/k)
when
k<n

(
[
9
,
10
]
) or
work only for the uniform distribution and
decay exponentially in
\varepsilon^{2}
(
[
11
]
), which
when
\varepsilon<1
is significantly weaker than decay in
\varepsilon^{1}
. Formally,
our result is as follows:

Theorem I.2
.

Let
V_{n,k,P}
be as in
I.1
. Then for all
\varepsilon>\frac{k-1}{n}
, it holds that

\Pr\left[V_{n,k,P}\geq\varepsilon\right]\leq e^{-n\varepsilon}\cdot\left(\frac{e\varepsilon n}{k-1}\right)^{k-1}.

I.2
is in fact an immediate corollary of our main
technical result, which is a bound on the moment generating function of

V_{n,k,P}
.

Theorem I.3
.

Let
V_{n,k,P}
be as in
I.1
. Then for all

0\leq t<n
it holds that

\Exp\left[\exp\big(t\cdot V_{n,k,P}\big)\right]\leq\left(\frac{1}{1-t/n}\right)^{k-1}.

Note that this is also the moment generating function of a gamma
distribution with shape
k-1
and rate
n
. Bounding the moment
generating function is a standard technique to obtain concentration
bounds (see e.g.
[
12
]
), but to the best
of our knowledge
I.3
is the first to give a finite
bound on
\Exp\left[\exp\left(s\cdot 2nV_{n,k,P}\right)\right]
independent of
n
for any
constant
s>0
. As a consequence, we are able to give the first (to
the best of our knowledge) upper bounds on the
m
’th moments of

2nV_{n,k,P}
which do not depend on
n
for all
m>2
.
Using Wilks’ theorem
[
13
]
on the asymptotic
distribution of the likelihood-ratio statistic, we are then able to
compute the asymptotic moments of
2nV_{n,k,P}
for fixed
k
and
P
as

n
goes to infinity. Furthermore, our finite sample bounds on the

m
’th non-central moment are within constant factors (with the constant
depending on
m
) of the asymptotic value.

The rest of this work is organized as follows. In

Section

II
we prove

I.3
and
I.2
, with the proof divided into two
parts: in
Section

II.A
we show
I.3
can be
derived from bounds for the special case of a binary alphabet (
k=2
),
e.g. a binomial distribution, and in
Section

II.B
we give a bound
for this simpler case. In
Section

III
we use

I.3
to derive moment bounds and asymptotic results.
Finally, in
Section

IV
we compare our bounds to
existing results in the literature and suggest possible directions
for future research, and in particular conjecture an improvement to

I.3
which would nearly close the quadratic gap between
our finite-sample bound and the bound of Wilks’ theorem on the
asymptotic distribution of likelihood-ratio statistic (which does not
hold in general for finite
n
).

II
Proof of Finite-Sample Bounds

In this section we prove our main technical result, the moment
generating function bound of
I.3
, and use it
to derive our new tail bound
I.2
.

II.A
Reducing the Multinomial to the Binomial

We first show that the moment generating function of the
empirical relative entropy for arbitrary finite alphabets of size
k

can be bounded in terms of the special case
k=2
. Formally, this
requires the bound to be of a particular form:

Definition II.1
.

A function
f:[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is a
sample-independent MGF
bound for the binomial KL
if for every positive integer
n
, real

t\in[0,n)
, and
p\in[0,1]
it holds that

\Exp\left[\exp\left(t\cdot V_{n,2,(p,1-p)}\right)\right]\leq f(t/n).

Remark 1
.

Recalling that
2nV_{n,k,P}
is the likelihood-ratio statistic,

II.1
is equivalent to requiring bounds on the
moment generating function
\Exp\left[\exp\left(s\cdot 2nV_{n,2,(p,1-p)}\right)\right]
for

0\leq s<1/2
which do not depend on
n
or
p
.

We can now state our reduction.

Proposition II.2
.

Let
P=(p_{1},\dotsc,p_{k})
be a distribution on a set of size
k
for

k\geq 2
. Then for every sample-independent MGF bound for the
binomial KL
f:[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
and
0\leq t<n
, the moment
generating function of
V_{n,k,P}
satisfies

\Exp\left[\exp\left(t\cdot V_{n,k,P}\right)\right]\leq f(t/n)^{k-1}.

Proof.

This is a simple induction on
k
. The base case
k=2
holds
by definition of sample-independent MGF bound for the binomial KL.

For the inductive step, we compute conditioned on the value of

X_{k}
. Note that if
p_{k}=1
then the inductive step is trivial
since
V_{n,k,P}=0
with probability
1
, so assume that
p_{k}<1
.
For each
i\in\left\{1,\dotsc,k-1\right\}
define
p^{\prime}_{i}=p_{i}/(1-p_{k})
,
so that conditioned on
X_{k}=m
, the variables
(X_{1},\dotsc,X_{k-1})
are distributed multinomially with
n-m
samples
and probabilities
P^{\prime}=(p^{\prime}_{1},\dotsc,p^{\prime}_{k-1})
. Simple rearranging
(using the chain rule) implies that

\displaystyle V_{n,k,P}

\displaystyle=\KL\left(\left(X_{1}/n,\dotsc,X_{k}/n\right)\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}\left(p_{1},\dotsc,p_{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\KL\big(\left(X_{k}/n,1-X_{k}/n\right)\;\big\|\;\mathopen{}\left(p_{k},1-p_{k}\right)\big)+\frac{n-X_{k}}{n}\cdot V_{n-X_{k},k-1,P^{\prime}}

(II.1)

where

V_{n-X_{k},k-1,P^{\prime}}=\KL\left(\left(\frac{X_{1}}{n-X_{k}},\dotsc,\frac{X_{k-1}}{n-X_{k}}\right)\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}\left(p^{\prime}_{1},\dotsc,p^{\prime}_{k-1}\right)\right)

and where we treat the second term of
Eq.

II.1

as
0
if
X_{k}=n
. Now for every
0\leq t<n
we have

\displaystyle\Exp\bigg[

\displaystyle\exp\big(t\cdot V_{n,k,P}\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\Exp\bigg[\Exp\Big[\exp\big(t\cdot V_{n,k,P}\big)\,\Big|\,\mathopen{}X_{k}\Big]\bigg]

\displaystyle=\Exp\Bigg[\exp\Big(t\cdot\KL\big(\left(X_{k}/n,1-X_{k}/n\right)\;\big\|\;\mathopen{}\left(p_{k},1-p_{k}\right)\big)\Big)\cdot\Exp\left[\exp\left(t\cdot\frac{n-X_{k}}{n}\cdot V_{n-X_{k},k-1,P^{\prime}}\right)\,\middle|\,\mathopen{}X_{k}\right]\Bigg].

Since
0\leq t\cdot\frac{n-X_{k}}{n}<n-X_{k}
, the inductive hypothesis for

V_{n-X_{k},k-1,P^{\prime}}
implies the upper bound

\displaystyle\leq\Exp\left[\exp\Big(t\cdot\KL\big(\left(X_{k}/n,1-X_{k}/n\right)\;\big\|\;\mathopen{}\left(p_{k},1-p_{k}\right)\big)\Big)\cdot f\left(\frac{t(n-X_{k})/n}{n-X_{k}}\right)^{k-2}\right]

\displaystyle=f(t/n)^{k-2}\cdot\Exp\left[\exp\Big(t\cdot\KL\big(\left(X_{k}/n,1-X_{k}/n\right)\;\big\|\;\mathopen{}\left(p_{k},1-p_{k}\right)\big)\Big)\right].

By definition of a sample-independent MGF bound for the binomial KL,
the second term is at most
f(t/n)
, so we get a bound of

f(t/n)^{k-1}
as desired.
∎

Remark 2
.

Mardia et al.
[
10
]
use the
same chain rule decomposition of the multinomial KL to inductively
bound the (non-exponential) moments.

II.B
Bounding the Binomial

It remains to give a sample-independent MGF bound for the binomial KL:

Proposition II.3
.

The function

f(x)=\frac{1}{1-x}

is a sample-independent MGF bound for the binomial KL.

Remark 3
.

Hoeffding’s inequality
[
14
]
can be used
to give a simple proof of the weaker claim that
2^{x}/(1-x)
is a
sample-independent MGF bound for the binomial KL.

Proof.

Let
B_{n,p}
denote a random variable with
\Binom(n,p)
distribution.
Using the fact that

\exp{\Big(n\cdot\KL\big(\left(i/n,1-i/n\right)\;\big\|\;\mathopen{}\left(p,1-p\right)\big)\Big)}=\frac{\Pr\left[B_{n,i/n}=i\right]}{\Pr\left[B_{n,p}=i\right]}

for any integers
0\leq i\leq n
, we can expand the moment generating
function as

\displaystyle\Exp\left[\exp\left(nx\cdot\KL\left(\left(\frac{B_{n,p}}{n},1-\frac{B_{n,p}}{n}\right)\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}\left(p,1-p\right)\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Pr\left[B_{n,p}=i\right]^{1-x}\Pr\left[B_{n,i/n}=i\right]^{x}.

For every
n
and
i
, the function
q\mapsto\Pr\left[B_{n,q}=i\right]=\binom{n}{i}q^{i}(1-q)^{n-i}
is
easily seen to be log-concave over
[0,1]
, so we can upper bound the moment generating function by

\displaystyle G_{n}(p,x)\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny def}}}{=}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Pr\left[B_{n,(1-x)p+ix/n}=i\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\big((1-x)p+ix/n\big)^{i}\big(1-\big((1-x)p+ix/n\big)\big)^{n-i}

It turns out
G_{n}
does not depend on
p
and can be simplified
significantly, which we prove in the following two lemmas.

Lemma II.4
.

For all non-negative integers
n
and real numbers
x
and
p
we have

G_{n}(p,x)=G_{n}(0,x)
.

Proof.

Define
R_{n}(q,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\left(q+ix/n\right)^{i}\left(1-q-ix/n\right)^{n-i}
(where when
i=n=0
we treat

0/0=1
) so that
G_{n}(p,x)=R_{n}\left((1-x)p,x\right)

and it suffices to prove that
R_{n}(q,x)=R_{n}(0,x)
.
We prove this by induction on
n
: the
base case of
n=0
holds since
R_{n}(q,x)=1
always, and
for the inductive step we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial q}R_{n}(q,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\left((q+ix/n)^{i}(1-q-ix/n)^{n-i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\left(i(q+ix/n)^{i-1}(1-q-ix/n)^{n-i}-(n-i)(q+ix/n)^{i}(1-q-ix/n)^{n-i-1}\right)

\displaystyle=n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{i-1}\left(q+x/n+\frac{i-1}{n-1}\cdot\frac{x(n-1)}{n}\right)^{i-1}\left(1-q-x/n-\frac{i-1}{n-1}\cdot\frac{x(n-1)}{n}\right)^{n-1-(i-1)}

\displaystyle\qquad-n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{i}\left(q+\frac{i}{n-1}\cdot\frac{x(n-1)}{n}\right)^{i}\left(1-q-\frac{i}{n-1}\cdot\frac{x(n-1)}{n}\right)^{n-1-i}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{i}\left(q+x/n+\frac{i}{n-1}\cdot\frac{x(n-1)}{n}\right)^{i}\left(1-q-x/n-\frac{i}{n-1}\cdot\frac{x(n-1)}{n}\right)^{n-1-i}

\displaystyle\qquad-n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{i}\left(q+\frac{i}{n-1}\cdot\frac{x(n-1)}{n}\right)^{i}\left(1-q-\frac{i}{n-1}\cdot\frac{x(n-1)}{n}\right)^{n-1-i}

\displaystyle=n\left(R_{n-1}\left(q+\frac{x}{n},\frac{x(n-1)}{n}\right)-R_{n-1}\left(q,\frac{x(n-1)}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=n\big(R_{n-1}\left(0,x(n-1)/n\right)-R_{n-1}\left(0,x(n-1)/n\right)\big)=0

where the last line is by the inductive hypothesis.
∎

Lemma II.5
.

For all non-negative integers
n
we have

G_{n}(p,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{n!}{n^{i}(n-i)!}\cdot x^{i}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
II.4
we have that

G_{n}(p,x)=G_{n}(0,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(\frac{ix}{n}\right)^{i}\left(1-\frac{ix}{n}\right)^{n-i}

is a polynomial in
x
of degree at most
n
. For any non-negative
integer
i\leq n
we can compute the coefficient of
x^{i}
in

G_{n}(0,x)
by summing over the power of
x
contributed by the
(jx/n)^{j}
term
for each
j
:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{n}{j}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)^{j}\cdot\binom{n-j}{i-j}\left(-\frac{j}{n}\right)^{i-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{n!}{j!(n-j)!}\cdot\frac{(n-j)!}{(i-j)!(n-i)!}\cdot\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)^{i}(-1)^{i-j}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{n^{i}(n-i)!}\cdot\frac{1}{i!}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i}{j}j^{i}(-1)^{i-j}

where
\frac{1}{i!}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i}{j}j^{i}(-1)^{i-j}

is by definition the Stirling number of the second kind
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{i}{i}

and is equal to
1
(see e.g.
[
15
, Chapter 6.1]
),
so that we can simplify this to

\frac{n!}{n^{i}(n-i)!}

as desired.
∎

Putting together Lemma
II.4
and
Lemma
II.5
, we have that the moment generating
function is at most

G_{n}(p,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{n!}{n^{i}(n-i)!}x^{i}
, where

\frac{n!}{n^{i}(n-i)!}=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\left(1-j/n\right)\leq 1

and thus for each
x\in[0,1)
we have
G_{n}(p,x)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}x^{i}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x^{i}=1/(1-x)
.
∎

Together,
II.2
and
II.3
imply our
moment generating function bound (
I.3
), and thus a
Chernoff bound implies our tail bound:

Proof of
I.2
.

By
I.3
, we know for every
t\in[0,n)
that

\Exp\left[\exp\left(t\cdot V_{n,k,P}\right)\right]\leq\left(\frac{1}{1-t/n}\right)^{k-1}
, so by a
Chernoff bound

\displaystyle\Pr\left[V_{n,k,P}\geq\varepsilon\right]

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{t\in[0,n)}\exp\left(-t\varepsilon\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{1-t/n}\right)^{k-1}.

The result follows by making the optimal choice
t/n=1-(k-1)/(\varepsilon n)
when
\varepsilon>\left(k-1\right)/{n}
.
∎

III
Moment and Asymptotic Bounds

In this section we use
I.3
to give finite-sample
and asymptotic bounds on the moments of
V_{n,k,P}
. We will need
some basic facts about subexponential random variables, for which we
follow the textbook of Vershynin
[
16
]
.

Lemma III.1

(
[
16
, Definition 2.7.5, Proposition 2.7.1]
)
.

There is a universal constant
C>0
such that every real-valued
random variable
X
with finite
subexponential norm

\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\psi_{1}}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny def}}}{=}}\inf\left\{t>0:\Exp\left[\exp\left(\left\lvert X\right\rvert/t\right)\right]\leq 2\right\}

satisfies
\Exp\left[\left\lvert X\right\rvert^{m}\right]^{1/m}\leq Cm\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\psi_{1}}
for all
m\geq 1
.

III.1
allows us to bound the moments of
2nV_{n,k,P}

uniformly for all
n
.

Theorem III.2
.

For every
n
,
k
, and
P
, it holds that

\left\lVert 2nV_{n,k,P}\right\rVert_{\psi_{1}}\!\leq 4(k-1)
and

\left\lVert 2nV_{n,k,P}-\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]\right\rVert_{\psi_{1}}\allowbreak\leq 8(k-1)
.
In particular, there exist universal constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for all

n
,
k
,
P
and
m\geq 1

\displaystyle\Exp\left[\left(2nV_{n,k,P}\right)^{m}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(C_{1}m(k-1))^{m}

\displaystyle\Exp\left[\left(2nV_{n,k,P}-\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]\right)^{m}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(C_{2}m(k-1))^{m}

Proof.

I.3
implies for all
n
,
k
, and
P
that

\Exp\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{4(k-1)}\cdot 2nV_{n,k,P}\right)\right]\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2(k-1)}\right)^{-(k-1)}\leq 2
, so by
III.1
we have that

\left\lVert 2nV_{n,k,P}\right\rVert_{\psi_{1}}\leq 4(k-1)
.
By the triangle inequality and convexity of norms, this lets us bound
the norm of the centered random variable as

\left\lVert 2nV_{n,k,P}-\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]\right\rVert_{\psi_{1}}\leq 2\left\lVert 2nV_{n,k,P}\right\rVert_{\psi_{1}}\leq 8(k-1)
.
∎

Our asymptotic results rely on Wilks’ theorem
[
13
]

on the asymptotic behavior of the likelihood ratio test, which for fixed
k

and
P
implies that the random variable
2nV_{n,k,P}
converges in distribution to the
chi-squared distribution with
k-1
degrees of freedom as
n
goes to
infinity (see also
[
17
, Theorem 4.2]
).
Though in general convergence in distribution does not imply
convergence of moments or of the moment generating function

[
18
]
, it turns out that the bounds
from
III.2
are strong enough for convergence in distribution to
imply convergence of the moments.

Theorem III.3
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer and
P=(p_{1},\dots,p_{k})
be a probability
distribution over a finite alphabet of size
k
with
p_{i}\neq 0
for
every
i\in\left\{1,\dots,k\right\}
. Then for every
m\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Exp\left[(2nV_{n,k,P})^{m}\right]

\displaystyle=\Exp\left[\left(\chi^{2}_{k-1}\right)^{m}\right]=2^{m}\frac{\Gamma\left(m+\frac{k-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{k-1}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Exp\left[\left(2nV_{n,k,P}-\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]\right)^{m}\right]

\displaystyle=\Exp\left[\left(\chi^{2}_{k-1}-\Exp\left[\chi^{2}_{k-1}\right]\right)^{m}\right]

and for every
s\in[0,1/2)
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Exp\left[\exp\left(s\cdot 2nV_{n,k,P}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\Exp\left[\exp\left(s\cdot\chi^{2}_{k-1}\right)\right]=\left(1-2s\right)^{-(k-1)/2}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Exp\left[\exp\left(s\cdot\left(2nV_{n,k,P}-\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\Exp\left[\exp\left(s\cdot\left(\chi^{2}_{k-1}-\Exp\left[\chi^{2}_{k-1}\right]\right)\right)\right]=e^{-(k-1)s}(1-2s)^{-(k-1)/2}

Remark 4
.

[
10
]
prove the one-sided
lower bound that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Var\left(2nV_{n,k,P}\right)\geq\Var\left(\chi^{2}_{k-1}\right)
, which is a special case of the
second equality above.

Proof.

Given a sequence of random variables
\left(X_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

which convergence in distribution to a random variable
X
,
a sufficient condition for
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Exp\left[X_{n}\right]=\Exp\left[X\right]

is that
\sup_{n}\Exp\left[\left\lvert X_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\alpha}\right]<\infty
for some

\alpha>0
(see e.g.
[
18
]
).

Wilks’ theorem
[
13
]
shows that

2nV_{n,k,P}
converges in distribution to
\chi^{2}_{k-1}
,
and thus the continuous mapping theorem implies that

\left(2nV_{n,k,P}\right)^{m}
converges in distribution to

\left(\chi^{2}_{k-1}\right)^{m}
for every
m\geq 1
.

III.2
implies

\sup_{n}\Exp\left[\left\lvert\left(2nV_{n,k,P}\right)^{m}\right\rvert^{2}\right]\leq(Cm(k-1))^{2m}<\infty
,
which establishes the first claim.
In particular, for
m=1
we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]=\Exp\left[\chi^{2}_{k-1}\right]
, so Slutsky’s theorem implies that

2nV_{n,k,P}-\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]
converges in distribution to

\chi^{2}_{k-1}-\Exp\left[\chi^{2}_{k-1}\right]
.
Again by the continuous mapping theorem we thus have that

\left(2nV_{n,k,P}-\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]\right)^{m}
converges in distribution
to
\left(\chi^{2}_{k-1}-\Exp\left[\chi^{2}_{k-1}\right]\right)^{m}
, so since

III.2
implies

\sup_{n}\Exp\left[\left\lvert\left(2nV_{n,k,P}-\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]\right)^{m}\right\rvert^{2}\right]\leq(Cm(k-1))^{2m}<\infty
, we also get the second claim.

For the moment generating function claims, first note that
they are trivial for
s=0
, as both sides are always
1
,
and for
s\in(0,1/2)
we have
1/2>1/4+s/2>s
. Now, since
the continuous mapping theorem implies
\exp(s\cdot 2nV_{n,k,P})

converges in distribution to
\exp\left(s\cdot\chi^{2}_{k-1}\right)
,
and
I.3
implies

\sup_{n}\Exp\left[\left\lvert\exp\left((1/4+s/2)\cdot 2nV_{n,k,P}\right)\right\rvert\right]\leq\left(1/2-s\right)^{k-1}<\infty
, we get the third claim.
Finally, for the last claim, we again have that

\exp\left(s\cdot\left(2nV_{n,k,P}-\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]\right)\right)
converges in
distribution to
\exp\left(s\cdot\left(\chi^{2}_{k-1}-\Exp\left[\chi^{2}_{k-1}\right]\right)\right)

by the continuous mapping theorem, and since
V_{n,k,P}\geq 0

we have

\exp\left((1/4+s/2)\cdot\left(2nV_{n,k,P}-\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]\right)\right)\leq\exp\left((1/4+s/2)\cdot 2nV_{n,k,P}\right)
and we conclude
as for the third claim.
∎

IV
Discussion

In this section we compare our bounds to existing results in the
literature and discuss possible directions for future work.

IV.A
Moment generating function bounds

To the best of our knowledge, this work is the first to explicitly
consider the moment generating function of the empirical divergence, and
existing tail bounds do not give finite bounds on
\sup_{n}\Exp\left[\exp\left(x\cdot nV_{n,k,P}\right)\right]=\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Pr\left[nV_{n,k,P}>\frac{\log t}{x}\right]\,dt
for any
k\geq 3
or constant
x>0
.
Thus, we focus on comparing our finite sample bound (
I.3
)
to the asymptotic one (
III.3
).

In
III.3
we showed for all
x\in[0,1)
that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Exp\left[\exp\left(x\cdot nV_{n,k,P}\right)\right]=\left(1-x\right)^{-(k-1)/2}
,
whereas our finite sample bound of
I.3
instead gave the
upper bound
\Exp\left[\exp\left(x\cdot nV_{n,k,P}\right)\right]\leq\left(1-x\right)^{-(k-1)}
, which is
quadratically worse. This loss arises from our binomial bound from

II.3
of
(1-x)^{-1}
for the case
k=2
, where the correct
asymptotic bound is
\left(1-x\right)^{-1/2}
. Unfortunately, it is
not

the case that this latter asymptotic bound holds for all
n
,
p
, and

0\leq x<1
: indeed, this is violated even for
(n,p,x)=(2,1/2,1/2)
.
Nevertheless, we conjecture that
II.3
can be improved to
something closer to the asymptotic bound:

Conjecture IV.1
.

The function

f(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{1-x}}-1

is a sample-independent MGF bound for the binomial KL.

Remark 5
.

1/\sqrt{1-x}\leq 2/\sqrt{1-x}-1\leq 1/(1-x)

for all
x\in[0,1)
.

IV.1
would follow from the following more natural
conjecture, which looks at a single branch of the KL divergence and is
supported by numerical evidence:

Conjecture IV.2
.

Letting

\BKL_{>}\left(p\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}q\right)\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny def}}}{=}}\begin{cases}0&p\leq q\\
\KL\Big(\left(p,1-p\right)\;\Big\|\;\mathopen{}\left(q,1-q\right)\Big)&p>q\end{cases}

it holds for every positive integer
n
, real
t\in[0,n)
, and
p\in[0,1]
that

\Exp\left[\exp\Big(t\cdot\BKL_{>}\left(B/n\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}p\right)\Big)\right]\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t/n}}

where
B\sim\Binom(n,p)
.

Remark 6
.

We believe the results (or techniques) of Zubkov and Serov

[
19
]
and Harremoës

[
20
]
strengthening Hoeffding’s inequality may
be of use in proving these conjectures.

Proof of
IV.1
given
IV.2
.

We have that

\KL\Big((p,1-p)\;\Big\|\;\mathopen{}(q,1-q)\Big)=\BKL_{>}\left(p\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}q\right)+\BKL_{>}\left(1-p\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}1-q\right)

so for every
i\in\left\{0,1,\dotsc,n\right\}

\exp\Big(t\cdot\KL\Big((i/n,1-i/n)\;\Big\|\;\mathopen{}(p,1-p)\Big)\Big)=\exp\Big(t\cdot\BKL_{>}\left(i/n\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}p\right)\Big)\cdot\exp\Big(t\cdot\BKL_{>}\left(1-i/n\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}1-p\right)\Big).

Letting
x=\exp\Big(t\cdot\BKL_{>}\left(i/n\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}p\right)\Big)
and

y=\exp\Big(t\cdot\BKL_{>}\left(1-i/n\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}1-p\right)\Big)
, we have
that at least one of
x
and
y
is equal to
1
, so that

xy=\big(1+(x-1)\big)\big(1+(y-1)\big)=1+(x-1)+(y-1)+(x-1)(y-1)=x+y-1,

and thus by taking expectations over
i=B
for
B\sim\Binom(n,p)
, we get

\Exp\left[\exp\left(t\cdot\BKL\left(B/n\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}p\right)\right)\right]=\Exp\left[\exp\left(t\cdot\BKL_{>}\left(B/n\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}p\right)\right)\right]+\Exp\left[\exp\left(t\cdot\BKL_{>}\left(1-B/n\;\middle\|\;\mathopen{}1-p\right)\right)\right]-1.

We conclude by bounding both terms using
IV.2
,
since
n-B
is distributed as
\Binom(n,1-p)
.
∎

IV.B
Moment bounds

The moments of
V_{n,k,P}
have seen some study in the literature. Most
notably, Paninski
[
7
]
showed by comparison
to the
\chi^{2}
-statistic that
\Exp\left[V_{n,k,P}\right]\leq\log\left(1+\frac{k-1}{n}\right)\leq\frac{k-1}{n}
. In the reverse direction,

[
8
]
showed that if
n\geq 15k
then for
the uniform distribution it holds that
\Exp\left[V_{n,k,U_{k}}\right]\geq\frac{k-1}{2n}
, complementing the asymptotic result that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Exp\left[nV_{n,k,U_{k}}\right]=\frac{k-1}{2}
, which follows from

III.3
(and can also be derived from

[
10
]
). For higher moments,

[
10
]
showed that

\Var(V_{n,k,P})\leq Ck/n^{2}
for some constant
C
, and asymptotically
that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Var(2nV_{n,k,P})\geq\Var(\chi^{2}_{k-1})=2(k-1)
. To the best of our knowledge, no bounds on the higher
moments have appeared in the literature.

In
III.2
we showed for every
m\geq 1
that

\Exp\left[\left(2nV_{n,k,P}\right)^{m}\right]\leq\left(Cm(k-1)\right)^{m}
for some universal constant

C>0
, and we showed in
III.3
the asymptotic equality

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Exp\left[\left(2nV_{n,k,P}\right)^{m}\right]=2^{m}\frac{\Gamma\left(m+\frac{k-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{k-1}{2}\right)}=\left(C^{\prime}m(k-1)\right)^{m}
where
C^{\prime}
is bounded in a constant range. Thus, our
finite-sample bound is asymptotically optimal up to the universal
constant
C
.

However, the situation is quite different for the central moments

\Exp\left[\left(2nV_{n,k,P}-\Exp\left[2nV_{n,k,P}\right]\right)^{m}\right]
, where we again showed the
finite sample bound
\left(Cm(k-1)\right)^{m}
, but asymptotically from

III.3
the bound is
\left(C^{\prime}m(k-1)\right)^{\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor}
for

m\geq 2
and some
C^{\prime}
in a constant range. For
m=2
,

[
10
]
were able to achieve this
bound up to constant factors, but it is an intriguing open question to
get finite sample central moment bounds with the asymptotically correct
power for
m>2
.

IV.C
Tail bound

To understand our tail bound (
I.2
), we compare our
result to existing bounds in the literature. Antos and Kontoyiannis

[
11
]
used McDiarmid’s bounded differences
inequality
[
21
]
to give a concentration bound
for the empirical entropy, which in the case of the uniform distribution
implies the bound

\Pr\left[\left\lvert V_{n,k,U_{k}}-\Exp\left[V_{n,k,U_{k}}\right]\right\rvert\geq\varepsilon\right]\leq 2e^{-n\varepsilon^{2}/(2\log^{2}n)}.

This bound has the advantage of providing subgaussian concentration
around the expectation, but for the case of small
\varepsilon<1
it is
preferable to have a bound with linear dependence on
\varepsilon
.
Unfortunately, existing tail bounds which decay like
e^{-n\varepsilon}
are
not, in the common regime of parameters where
n\gg k
, meaningful for
\varepsilon

close to
\Exp\left[V_{n,k,P}\right]\leq(k-1)/n
. For example, the method of types

[
9
]
is used to prove the standard bound

\Pr\left[V_{n,k,P}>\varepsilon\right]\leq e^{-n\varepsilon}\cdot\binom{n+k-1}{k-1},

(IV.1)

which is commonly used in proofs of Sanov’s theorem (see
e.g.
[
22
]
).
However, this bound is meaningful only for
\varepsilon>\frac{1}{n}\cdot\log\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}\geq\frac{k-1}{n}\cdot\log\left(1+\frac{n}{k-1}\right)
, which is off by a factor of order

\log\left(1+\frac{n}{k-1}\right)
. A recent bound due to Mardia et al.
[
10
]
improved on the method of
types bound for all settings of
k
and
n
, but for
3\leq k\leq\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi}\cdot n
still requires
\varepsilon>\frac{k}{n}\cdot\log\left(\sqrt{\frac{e^{3}n}{2\pi k}}\right)>\frac{k-1}{n}\cdot\log\left(1+\frac{n-1}{k}\right)/2
, which again has dependence on
\log\left(1+\frac{n-1}{k}\right)
.

Thus, if
k\leq n
, then our bound is meaningful for
\varepsilon

smaller than what is needed for the method of types bound or the bound
of
[
10
]
by a factor of order

\log(n/k)
, which for
k
as large as
n^{0.99}
is still
\log(n)
,
and for
k
as large as
n/\log n
is of order
\log\log n
. However,

I.2
has slightly worse dependence on
\varepsilon
than the
other bounds, so for example it is better than the method of types bound
if and only if

\frac{k-1}{n}<\varepsilon<\frac{k-1}{n}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{e}\sqrt[k-1]{\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}}\right).

(IV.2)

In particular, when
n\geq e(k-1)
, our bound is better for
\varepsilon
up
to order
\frac{n}{k-1}
times larger than
\frac{k-1}{n}
.
However, we can also see that our bound can be better only when

\sqrt[k-1]{\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}}\geq e
, which asymptotically is
equivalent to
k-1\leq Cn
, where
C\approx 1.84
is the solution
to the equation
(1+C)/C\cdot H(C/(1+C))=1
for
H
the binary entropy
function in nats. From a finite-sample perspective, note that the condition
is always satisfied in the
standard setting of parameters where
n\geq k
, that is, the number
of samples is larger than the size of the alphabet. In this regime, we
can also compare to the “interpretable” upper bound of
[
10
, Theorem
3]
, to see that

I.2
is better if

\frac{k-1}{n}<\varepsilon<\frac{k-1}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{e}\left(\frac{6e^{2}}{\pi^{3/2}}\sqrt{\frac{e^{3}n}{2\pi k}}^{k}\right)^{1/(k-1)},

so that in particular our bound is better for
\varepsilon
up to order

\sqrt{\frac{n}{k}}^{1+1/(k-1)}\geq\sqrt{\frac{n}{k}}
times larger than

\frac{k-1}{n}
.

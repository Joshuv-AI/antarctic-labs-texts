Second order phase dispersion by optimised rotation pulses
David L. Goodwin, Martin R. M. Koos, Burkhard Luy

Abstract
We show that the duration of broadband universal control pulses can be halved by choosing control targets with a quadratic function of phase dispersion. This class of control pulses perform a broadband universal rotation around an axis, in the Bloch sphere representation of two-level systems, given by this phase dispersion function. We present an effective optimal control method to avoid the problem of convergence to local extrema traps.

I
Introduction

The critically important milestones on the road-map of quantum technology development are expected to include quantum optimal control
Glaser et al. 2015
;
Acín et al. 2018
. The main result of this communication is to reduce the time and energy required to control a quantum system. As an application of optimal control theory, quantum optimal control can be tasked with finding control pulses, usually in the form of electromagnetic radiation, to perform a desired operation on an arbitrary quantum state. Already, this method has been applied successfully to a wide range of experiments including magnetic resonance spectroscopy
Khaneja et al. 2005
;
Tošner et al. 2006
, error-correction for quantum computing
Zhang et al. 2012
, quantum information registers
Dolde et al. 2014
, robust atom interferometry
Saywell et al. 2018
;
Saywell et al. 2020
, and high-resolution medical imaging satisfying legal irradiation constraints
Vinding et al. 2017
.

The growing number of reported applications of quantum optimal control are expanded by the concurrent development of optimal control algorithms. There are a number of approaches to optimal control, such as Lagrangian methods
Somlói et al. 1993
, time optimal control
Khaneja et al. 2001
, annealing quantum systems to a desired effective Hamiltonian
Tošner et al. 2006
, sophisticated gradient-free searches
Doria et al. 2011
, rapidly converging Krylov-Newton methods
Ciaramella et al. 2015
, and optimal control using analytic controls
Machnes et al. 2018
. This development is essential as desired solutions push to the limit of what is physically possible and control problems become computationally, and numerically challenging
Pechen and Tannor 2011
.

Quantum optimal control problems can be solved numerically by using a piecewise-constant control pulse approximation
Conolly et al. 1986
;
Mao et al. 1986
;
Viola and Lloyd 1998
;
Viola et al. 1999a
, and the trajectory gradient can be calculated and followed using the
gradient ascent pulse engineering
(
grape
) method
Khaneja et al. 2005
. The initial
grape
method showed linear convergence to a desired optimum destination, and was later extended to give superlinear convergence
de Fouquieres et al. 2011
, then quadratic convergence
de Fouquieres 2012
;
Goodwin and Kuprov 2016
. This communication introduces an ordered optimal control method, based on the
grape
method, to avoid local convergence traps encountered in the optimal control problem set out below. This method is termed
morphic
-
grape
by the authors.

In addition, and vital to effective experimental applications, optimal control has advanced theoretical studies calculating universal gates for quantum computing
Viola et al. 1999b
;
Schulte-Herbrüggen et al. 2005
;
Grace et al. 2007
;
Goerz et al. 2014
;
Zahedinejad et al. 2015
. A subset of universal quantum gates include control targets designed as an effective rotation in the Bloch sphere representation of a two-level system, with a defined rotation axis and rotation angle, for any initial basis-state vector
Kobzar et al. 2012
;
Dolde et al. 2014
. The developments reported here will be concerned with this
universal rotation
class of control pulses.

One of the drawbacks associated with universal rotation solutions is the long duration of control pulses when compared with the easier control problem of optimising
state-to-state
problems
Kobzar et al. 2012
;
Glaser et al. 2015
. The optimal control method presented here will show that the previously required pulse duration can be halved by defining target propagators as a function of phase dispersion.

III
Rotation axes with quadratic phase dispersion

Derived from Landau-Zener-Stückelberg-Majorana theory
Jeschke et al. 2015
, the ensemble of Eq. (
1
) can be controlled with adiabatic evolution

;
Leghtas et al. 2011
;
Augier et al. 2018
and is described as a linear frequency sweep over the two-level systems. The phase dispersion resulting from a linear frequency sweep is quadratic, and can be used to define the local targets of an optimal control method. Previous work on this theme has optimised state-to-state problems with linear phase dispersion
Gershenzon et al. 2008
;
Koos et al. 2015
;
Koos et al. 2017
and quadratic phase dispersion

;
Meister et al. 2014
;
Brif et al. 2014
;
Altenhof et al. 2019
.

Although state-to-state solutions are useful, they are only functionally defined for a specified state preparation: the desired control is only effective for a given initial state of the system. A
universal

;

In the authors’ experience, the standard procedure of starting

an adiabatic passage pulse, produces a fidelity profile periodically reducing to zero over the bandwidth, particularly when setting

in Eq. (
11

;
Goodwin and Kuprov 2016
. This can make the process of finding the desired optimal solutions set out in Eq. (
9
) a difficult task. Furthermore, a high average fidelity over the bandwidth is not sufficient for acceptable pulse performance – the pulse should uniformly manipulate the entire bandwidth.

An ordered optimisation procedure of
morphic
-
grape
is set out below to avoid these traps. This method is effective, yet easy to use, and proceeds by morphing one optimisation problem into an incrementally modified optimisation problem; using the solution of one as the new starting point of the next.

A grid of coefficients,
Q\in[0,1]

Q

+\Delta Q

to

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}}{\mathrm{d}Q}

1.

VII
Conclusions

Broadband universal rotation pulses with second order phase dispersion define a new class of pulses, named
sordor
pulses by the authors, with the universality of rotation pulses and an evolution character of adiabatic pulses. The difficulty in producing universal rotation pulses with second order phase dispersion, similar to adiabatic passage pulses, poses an interesting question regarding methods of optimal control.

A simple but effective
morphic
-
grape
optimal control method has been designed to avoid local extrema traps and find shorter duration control pulses when compared with the current best universal rotation (
burbop
) pulses. The trend of this comparison shows that
sordor
pulses are approximately
50\%
of
burbop
durations: a significant improvement.

The advance in pulse performance, compared with
burbop
, derives from a phase dispersion function used to define target unitary propagators. The particular phase dispersion function used in this work consists of a constant term, a quadratic term, and a variable multiplier. The exact form of this function is not derived within this work and is used in this form simply because the function produces high fidelity pulses. Studies using other phase dispersion functions,
e.g.
of higher or arbitrary order, are left to future investigations.

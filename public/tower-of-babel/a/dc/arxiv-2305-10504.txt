Model-Free Robust Average-Reward Reinforcement Learning
Yue Wang, Alvaro Velasquez, George Atia, Ashley Prater-Bennette, Shaofeng Zou

Abstract
Robust Markov decision processes (MDPs) address the challenge of model uncertainty by optimizing the worst-case performance over an uncertainty set of MDPs. In this paper, we focus on the robust average-reward MDPs under the model-free setting. We first theoretically characterize the structure of solutions to the robust average-reward Bellman equation, which is essential for our later convergence analysis. We then design two model-free algorithms, robust relative value iteration (RVI) TD and robust RVI Q-learning, and theoretically prove their convergence to the optimal solution. We provide several widely used uncertainty sets as examples, including those defined by the contamination model, total variation, Chi-squared divergence, Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence and Wasserstein distance.



1
Introduction

Reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated remarkable success in applications like robotics, finance, and games. However, RL often suffers from a severe performance degradation when deployed in real-world environments, which is often due to the model mismatch between the training and testing environments. Such a model mismatch could be a result of non-stationary conditions, modeling errors between simulated and real systems, external perturbations and adversarial attacks.
To address this challenge, a framework of robust MDPs and robust RL was developed in
(
Nilim & El Ghaoui 2004
;
Iyengar 2005
;
Bagnell et al. 2001
)
, where an uncertainty set of MDPs is constructed, and a pessimistic approach is adopted to optimize the worst-case performance over the uncertainty set. Such a minimax approach guarantees performance for all MDPs within the uncertainty set, making it robust to model mismatch.

Two performance criteria are commonly used for infinite-horizon MDPs: 1) the discounted-reward setting, where the reward is discounted exponentially with time; and 2) the average-reward setting, where the long-term average-reward over time is of interest. For systems that operate for an extended period of time, e.g., queue control, inventory management in supply chains, or communication networks, it is more important to optimize the average-reward since policies obtained from the discounted-reward setting may be myopic and have poor long-term performance
(
Kazemi et al. 2022
)
.
However, much of the work on robust MDPs has focused on the discounted-reward setting, and robust average-reward MDPs remain largely unexplored.

Robust MDPs under the two criteria are fundamentally different.
Compared to the discounted-reward setting, the average-reward setting places equal weight on immediate and future rewards, thus depends on the limiting state-action frequencies of the underlying MDPs. This makes it more challenging to investigate than the discounted-reward setting. For example, to establish a contraction in the discounted-reward setting, it generally suffices to have a discount factor strictly less than one, whereas in the average-reward setting, convergence depends on the structure of the MDP. Studies on robust average-reward MDPs are quite limited in the literature, e.g.,
(
Tewari & Bartlett 2007
;
Lim et al. 2013
;
Wang et al. 2023
)
, and are model-based, where the uncertainty set is fully known to the learner. However, the more practical model-free setting, where only samples from the nominal MDP (the centroid of the uncertainty set) can be observed, has yet to be explored. Algorithms and fundamental
convergence guarantees in this setting have not been established yet, which is the focus of this paper.

1.1
Challenges and Contributions

In this paper, we develop a fundamental characterization of solutions to the robust average-reward Bellman equation, design model-free algorithms with provable optimality guarantees, and construct an
unbiased
estimator of the robust average-reward Bellman operator for various types of uncertainty sets. Our results fill the gap in model-free algorithms for robust average-reward RL, and are fundamentally different from existing studies on robust discounted RL and robust and non-robust average-reward MDPs.
In the following, we summarize the major challenges and our key contributions.

We characterize the fundamental structure of solutions to the robust average-reward Bellman equation.

Value-based approaches typically converge to a solution to the (robust) Bellman equation. For example, TD and Q-learning converge to the
unique
fixed-point that yields an optimal solution to the Bellman equation in the discounted setting
(
Sutton & Barto 2018
)
. However, in the (robust) average-reward setting, solutions to the (robust) average-reward Bellman equation may not be unique
(
Puterman 1994
;
Wang et al. 2023
)
, and the fundamental structure of the solution space usually plays an important role in proving convergence (e.g.,
(
Wan et al. 2021
;
Abounadi et al. 2001
;
Tsitsiklis & Van Roy 1999
)
). In the non-robust average-reward setting, the solution to the average-reward Bellman equation is unique up to an additive constant
(
Puterman 1994
)
. This result is largely due to the nice linear structure of the non-robust average-reward Bellman equation. However, in the robust setting, where the worst-case performance is of interest, the robust average-reward Bellman equation is non-linear, which poses a significant challenge in characterizing its solution. We demonstrate that the worst-case transition kernel may not be unique and then show that any solution to the robust average-reward Bellman equation is the relative value function for some worst-case transition kernel (up to an additive constant). We note that this structure is of key importance later in our convergence analysis.

We develop model-free approaches for robust policy evaluation and optimal control.
We then design model-free algorithms for robust average-reward RL. To ensure stability of the iterates, we adopt a similar idea as in the non-robust average-reward setting
(
Puterman 1994
;
Bertsekas 2011
)
, which is to subtract an offset function and design robust RVI TD and Q-learning algorithms.
Unlike the convergence proof for model-free algorithms for non-robust average-reward MDPs, e.g.,
(
Wan et al. 2021
;
Abounadi et al. 2001
)
and robust discounted MDPs, e.g.,
(
Wang & Zou 2021
;
Liu et al. 2022
)
, where the Bellman operator is either linear or a contraction, our robust average-reward Bellman operator is neither linear nor a contraction, which makes the convergence analysis challenging. Nevertheless, based on the solution structure of the robust average-reward Bellman equation discussed above, we prove that our algorithms converge to solutions to the robust average-reward Bellman equations. Specifically, for robust RVI TD, its output converges to the worst-case average-reward; and for the robust RVI Q-learning, the greedy policy w.r.t. the Q-function converges to an optimal robust policy.

We construct unbiased estimators with bounded variance for robust Bellman operators under various uncertainty set models.
One popular approach in RL is bootstrapping, i.e., use
\mathbf{H}Q
as an estimate of the true
Q
-function, where
\mathbf{H}
is the (robust) Bellman operator. In the non-robust setting, an unbiased estimate of
\mathbf{H}
can be easily derived because
\mathbf{H}
is linear in the transition kernel. This linearity, however, does not hold in the robust setting since we minimize over the transition kernel to account for the worst-case and, therefore, directly plugging in the empirical transition kernel results in a biased estimate.
In this paper, we employ the multi-level Monte-Carlo method
(
Blanchet & Glynn 2015
)
and construct unbiased estimators for five popular uncertainty models, including those defined by contamination models, total variation, Chi-squared divergence, Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, and Wasserstein distance.
We then prove that our estimator is unbiased and has a bounded variance. These properties play an important role in establishing the convergence of our algorithms.

1.2
Related Work

Robust average-reward MDPs.
Studies on robust average-reward MDPs are quite limited in the literature. Model-based robust average-reward MDPs were first studied in
(
Tewari & Bartlett 2007
)
, where the authors showed the existence of the Blackwell optimal policy for a specific uncertainty set of bounded parameters. Results for general uncertainty sets were developed in recent work
(
Wang et al. 2023
)
, following a fundamentally different approach from
(
Tewari & Bartlett 2007
)
. However, these two methods are model-based. The work of
(
Lim et al. 2013
)
designed a model-free algorithm for the uncertainty set defined by total variation and characterized its regret bound. However, their method cannot be generalized to other uncertainty sets. In this paper, we design the first model-free method for general uncertainty sets and provide fundamental insights into the model-free robust average-reward RL problem.

Robust discounted-reward MDPs.

Model-based methods for robust discounted MDPs were studied in
(
Iyengar 2005
;
Nilim & El Ghaoui 2004
;
Bagnell et al. 2001
;
Satia & Lave Jr 1973
;
Wiesemann et al. 2013
;
Lim & Autef 2019
;
Xu & Mannor 2010
;
Yu & Xu 2015
;
Lim et al. 2013
;
Tamar et al. 2014
;
Neufeld & Sester 2022
)
, where the uncertainty set is assumed to be known, and the problem can be solved using robust dynamic programming. Later, the studies were generalized to the model-free setting
(
Roy et al. 2017
;
Badrinath & Kalathil 2021
;
Wang & Zou 2021
;
Wang & Zou 2022
;
Tessler et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2022
;
Zhou et al. 2021
;
Yang et al. 2021
;
Panaganti & Kalathil 2021
;
Goyal & Grand-Clement 2018
;
Kaufman & Schaefer 2013
;
Ho et al. 2018
;
Ho et al. 2021
)
. Our work focuses on the average-reward setting. First, the robust average-reward Bellman operator does not come with a simple contraction property like the one for the discounted setting, and the average-reward depends on the limiting behavior of the underlying MDP. Moreover, the average-reward Bellman function is a function of two variables: the average-reward and the relative value function, whereas its discounted-reward counterpart is a function of only the value function. These challenges make the robust average-reward problem more intricate. Furthermore, existing studies mostly focus on a certain type of uncertainty set, e.g., contamination
(
Wang & Zou 2021
)
and total variation
(
Panaganti et al. 2022
)
; in this paper, our method can be used to solve a wide range of uncertainty sets.

Non-robust average-reward MDPs.
Early contributions to non-robust average-reward MDPs include a fundamental characterization of the problem and model-based methods
(
Puterman 1994
;
Bertsekas 2011
)
. Model-free methods in the tabular setting, e.g., RVI Q-learning
(
Abounadi et al. 2001
)
and differential Q-learning
(
Wan et al. 2021
;
Wan & Sutton 2022
)
, were developed recently and are both shown to converge to the optimal average-reward. There is also work on average-reward RL with function approximation, e.g.,
(
Zhang et al. 2021b
;
Tsitsiklis & Van Roy 1999
;
Zhang et al. 2021a
;
Yu & Bertsekas 2009
)
. In this paper, we focus on the robust setting, where the key challenge lies in the non-linearity of the robust average-reward Bellman equation, whereas it is linear in the non-robust setting.

Preliminaries and Problem Formulation

Average-reward MDPs.

An MDP
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathsf{P},r)
is specified by: a state space
\mathcal{S}
, an action space
\mathcal{A}
, a transition kernel
\mathsf{P}=\left\{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}\in\Delta(\mathcal{S}),a\in\mathcal{A},s\in\mathcal{S}\right\}

1

\Delta(\mathcal{S})
denotes the
(|\mathcal{S}|-1)
-dimensional probability simplex on
\mathcal{S}
.
, where
\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}
is the distribution of the next state over
\mathcal{S}
upon taking action
a
in state
s
, and a reward function
r:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
. At each time step
t
, the agent at state
s_{t}
takes an action
a_{t}
, the environment then transitions to the next state
s_{t+1}\sim\mathsf{P}^{a_{t}}_{s_{t}}
, and provides a reward signal
r_{t}\in[0,1]
.

A stationary policy
\pi:\mathcal{S}\to\Delta(\mathcal{A})
maps a state to a distribution over
\mathcal{A}
, following which the agent takes action
a
at state
s
with probability
\pi(a|s)
.
Under a transition kernel
\mathsf{P}
, the average-reward of
\pi
starting from
s\in\mathcal{S}
is defined as

\displaystyle g_{\mathsf{P}}^{\pi}(s)\triangleq\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\pi,\mathsf{P}}\bigg[\frac{1}{T}\sum^{T-1}_{n=0}r_{t}|S_{0}=s\bigg].

(1)

The relative value function is defined to measure the cumulative difference between the reward and
g^{\pi}_{\mathsf{P}}
:

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{\mathsf{P}}(s)\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\pi,\mathsf{P}}\bigg[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}(r_{t}-g^{\pi}_{\mathsf{P}})|S_{0}=s\bigg].

(2)

Then
(g^{\pi}_{\mathsf{P}},V^{\pi}_{\mathsf{P}})
satisfies the following Bellman equation
(
Puterman 1994
)
:

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{\mathsf{P}}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi,\mathsf{P}}\bigg[r(s,A)-g^{\pi}_{\mathsf{P}}(s)+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{A}_{s,s^{\prime}}V^{\pi}_{\mathsf{P}}(s^{\prime})\bigg].

(3)

Robust average-reward MDPs.

For robust MDPs, the transition kernel is assumed to be in some uncertainty set
\mathcal{P}
. At each time step, the environment transits to the next state according to an arbitrary transition kernel
\mathsf{P}\in\mathcal{P}
. In this paper, we focus on the
(s,a)
-rectangular uncertainty set
(
Nilim & El Ghaoui 2004
;
Iyengar 2005
)
, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}=\bigotimes_{s,a}\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}
, where
\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}\subseteq\Delta(\mathcal{S})
. Popular uncertainty sets include those defined by the contamination model
(
Huber 1965
;
Wang & Zou 2022
)
, total variation
(
Lim et al. 2013
)
, Chi-squared divergence
(
Iyengar 2005
)
, Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence

(
Hu & Hong 2013
)
and Wasserstein distance
(
Gao & Kleywegt 2022
)
. We will investigate these uncertainty sets in detail in Section
5
.

We investigate the worst-case average-reward over the uncertainty set of MDPs. Specifically, define the robust average-reward of a policy
\pi
as

\displaystyle g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}(s)\triangleq\min_{\kappa\in\bigotimes_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{P}}\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\pi,\kappa}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}r_{t}|S_{0}=s\right],

(4)

where
\kappa=(\mathsf{P}_{0},\mathsf{P}_{1}...)\in\bigotimes_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{P}
. It was shown in
(
Wang et al. 2023
)
that the worst case under the time-varying model is equivalent to the one under the stationary model:

\displaystyle g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}(s)=\min_{\mathsf{P}\in\mathcal{P}}\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\pi,\mathsf{P}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}r_{t}|S_{0}=s\right].

(5)

Therefore, we limit our focus to the stationary model. We refer to the minimizers of (
5
) as the worst-case transition kernels for the policy
\pi
, and denote the set of all possible worst-case transition kernels by
\Omega^{\pi}_{g}
, i.e.,
\Omega^{\pi}_{g}\triangleq\{\mathsf{P}\in\mathcal{P}:g^{\pi}_{\mathsf{P}}=g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}\}
. As shown in
Example

A.1
in the appendix, the worst-case transition kernel may not be unique.

We focus on the model-free setting, where only samples from the nominal MDP (centroid of the uncertainty set) are available. We investigate two problems: 1) given a policy
\pi
, estimate its robust average-reward
g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}
, and 2) find an optimal robust policy that optimizes the robust average-reward:

\displaystyle\max_{\pi}g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}(s),\text{ for any }s\in\mathcal{S}.

(6)

We denote
by
g^{*}_{\mathcal{P}}(s)\triangleq\max_{\pi}g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}(s)
the optimal robust average-reward.

Robust RVI TD for Policy Evaluation

In this section, we study the problem of policy evaluation, which aims to estimate the robust average-reward
g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}
for a fixed policy
\pi

For technical convenience, we make the following assumption to guarantee that the average-reward is independent of the initial state

Wan et al. 2021

Zhang et al. 2021a

Assumption 1
.

The Markov chain induced by
\pi

\mathsf{P}\in\mathcal{P}

In general, the average-reward depends on the initial state. For example, imagine a policy that induces a multichain in an MDP with two closed communicating classes. A learning algorithm would be able to learn the average-reward for each communicating class; however, the average-rewards for the two classes may be different. To remove this complexity, it is common and convenient to rule out this possibility.
Under Assumption
1

g^{\pi}_{\mathsf{P}}(s)=g^{\pi}_{\mathsf{P}}(s^{\prime}),\forall s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}

4
Robust RVI Q-Learning for Control

In this section, we study the problem of optimal control, which aims to find a policy that optimizes the robust average-reward:
\pi^{*}=\arg\max_{\pi}g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}.

Similar to Assumption
1
, we make the following assumption to guarantee that the average-reward is independent of the initial state
(
Abounadi et al. 2001
;
Li et al. 2022
)
.

Assumption 4
.

The Markov chain induced by any
\mathsf{P}\in\mathcal{P}
and any
\pi
is a unichain.

We first revisit the optimal robust Bellman equation in
(
Wang et al. 2023
)
and further present a characterization of its solutions. Consider a
Q
-function
Q:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and define
V_{Q}(s)=\max_{a}Q(s,a),\forall s\in\mathcal{S}
.

Lemma 4.1

(Optimal robust Bellman equation)
.

If

\displaystyle Q(s,a)=r(s,a)-g+\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V_{Q}),\forall s,a,

(

(

;

for some

We note that a complete characterization of the solution set to (

5
Case Studies

In the previous two sections, we showed that if an unbiased estimator with bounded variance is available for the robust Bellman operator, then both robust algorithms proposed converge to the optimum. In this section, we present the design of these estimators for various uncertainty set models.

The major challenge in designing the estimated operators satisfying (
9
) and (
11
) lies in estimating the support function
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}_{s}^{a}}(V)
unbiasedly with bounded variance using samples from the nominal transition kernel.
However, the nominal transition kernel
\mathsf{P}
in general is different from the worst-case transition kernel, and the straightforward estimator is shown to be biased. Specifically, if we centered at the empirical transition kernel
\hat{\mathsf{P}}
and construct the uncertainty set
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
, then the estimator is biased:

\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}_{s}^{a}}(V)]\neq\sigma_{\mathcal{P}_{s}^{a}}(V).

We consider several widely-used uncertainty models including the contamination model, the total variation model, the Chi-square model, the KL-divergence model, and the Wasserstein distance model.
We show that our estimators are unbiased and have bounded variance in the following theorem. We will present the design in later sections.

Theorem 5.1
.

For each uncertainty set, denote its corresponding estimators by
\hat{\mathbf{T}}
and
\hat{\mathbf{H}}
as in
Sections

5.1
and
5.2
. Then, there exists some constant
C
, such that (
9
) and (
11
) hold.

In the following sections, we construct an operator
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}
to estimate the support function
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}
,
\forall s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}
for each uncertainty set. We further define the estimated robust Bellman operators as

\hat{\mathbf{T}}V(s)\triangleq\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(r(s,a)+\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V))

and

\hat{\mathbf{H}}Q(s,a)\triangleq r(s,a)+\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V_{Q}).

5.1
Linear Model: Contamination Uncertainty Set

The
\delta
-contamination uncertainty set is

\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}=\{(1-\delta)\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}+\delta q:q\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})\},

where
0<\delta<1
is the radius.
Under this uncertainty set, the support function can be computed as

\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)=(1-\delta)\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}V+\delta\min_{s}V(s),

and this is linear in the nominal transition kernel
\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}
. We hence use the transition to the subsequent state to construct our estimator:

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)\triangleq(1-\delta)\gamma V(s^{\prime})+\delta\min_{x}V(x),

(12)

where
s^{\prime}
is a subsequent state sample after
(s,a)
.

5.2
Non-Linear Models

Unlike the
\delta
-contamination model, most uncertainty sets result in a non-linear support function of the nominal transition kernel. We will employ the approach of multi-level Monte-Carlo which is widely used in quantile estimation under stochastic environments
(
Blanchet & Glynn 2015
;
Blanchet et al. 2019
;
Wang & Wang 2022
)
to construct an unbiased estimator with bounded variance.

For any
s,a
, we first generate
N
according to a geometric distribution with parameter
\Psi\in(0,1)
. Then, we take action
a
at state
s
for
2^{N+1}
times, and observe
r(s,a)
and the subsequent state
\{s_{i}^{\prime}\},i=1,...,2^{N+1}
. We divide these
2^{N+1}
samples into two groups: samples with odd indices, and samples with even indices. We then individually calculate the empirical distribution of
s^{\prime}
using the even-index samples, odd-index ones, all the samples, and the first sample:

\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a,E}_{s,N+1}=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{N}}\mathbbm{1}_{s_{2i}^{\prime}},\quad\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a,O}_{s,N+1}=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{N}}\mathbbm{1}_{s_{2i-1}^{\prime}},

\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,N+1}=\frac{1}{2^{N+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{N+1}}\mathbbm{1}_{s_{i}^{\prime}},\quad\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a,1}_{s,N+1}=\mathbbm{1}_{s_{1}^{\prime}}.

Then, we use these estimated transition kernels as nominal kernels to construct four estimated uncertainty sets
\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{a,E}_{s,N+1},\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{a,O}_{s,N+1},\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{a}_{s,N+1},\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{a,1}_{s,N+1}
. The multi-level estimator is then defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)\triangleq\sigma_{\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{a,1}_{s,N+1}}(V)+\frac{\Delta_{N}(V)}{p_{N}},

(13)

where
p_{N}=\Psi(1-\Psi)^{N}
and

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}(V)\triangleq\sigma_{\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{a}_{s,N+1}}(V)-\frac{\sigma_{\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{a,E}_{s,N+1}}(V)+\sigma_{\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{a,O}_{s,N+1}}(V)}{2}.

We note that in previous results of the multi-level Monte-Carlo estimator
(
Blanchet & Glynn 2015
;
Blanchet et al. 2019
;
Wang & Wang 2022
)
, several assumptions are needed to show that the estimator is unbiased. These assumptions, however, do not hold in our cases. For example, the function
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}(V)
is not continuously differentiable. Hence, their analysis cannot be directly applied here.

We then present four examples of non-linear uncertainty sets. Under each example, a solution to the support function
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}(V)
is given, and by plugging it into (
13
) the unbiased estimator can then be constructed. More details can be found in
Appendix

D
and
Appendix

E
.

Total Variation Uncertainty Set.

The total variation uncertainty set is

\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}=\{q\in\Delta(|\mathcal{S}|):\frac{1}{2}\|q-\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}\|_{1}\leq\delta\},

and the support function can be computed using its dual function
(
Iyengar 2005
)
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)

\displaystyle=\max_{\mu\geq 0}\big(\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(V-\mu)-\delta\text{Span}(V-\mu)\big).

(14)

Chi-square Uncertainty Set.

The Chi-square uncertainty set is

\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}=\{q\in\Delta(|\mathcal{S}|):d_{c}(\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s},q)\leq\delta\},

where
d_{c}(q,p)=\sum_{s}\frac{(p(s)-q(s))^{2}}{p(s)}
.
Its support function can be computed using its dual function
(
Iyengar 2005
)
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)=\max_{\mu\geq 0}\big(\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(V-\mu)-\sqrt{\delta\text{Var}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}(V-\mu)}\big).

(15)

Kullback–Leibler (KL) Divergence Uncertainty Set.

The KL-divergence between two distributions
p,q
is defined as

D_{KL}(q||p)=\sum_{s}q(s)\log\frac{q(s)}{p(s)},

and the uncertainty set defined via KL divergence is

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{s}^{a}=\left\{q:D_{KL}(q||\mathsf{P}_{s}^{a})\leq\delta\right\},\forall s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}.

(16)

Its support function can be efficiently solved using the duality result in
(
Hu & Hong 2013
)
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)=-\min_{\alpha\geq 0}\left(\delta\alpha+\alpha\log\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}\big[e^{\frac{-V}{\alpha}}\big]\right)\right).

(17)

The above estimator for the KL-divergence uncertainty set has also been developed in
(
Liu et al. 2022
)
for robust discounted MDPs. Its extension to our average-reward setting is similar.

Wasserstein Distance Uncertainty Sets.

Consider the metric space
(\mathcal{S},d)
by defining some distance metric
d
. For some parameter
l\in[1,\infty)
and two distributions
p,q\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
, define the
l
-Wasserstein distance between them as

W_{l}(q,p)=\inf_{\mu\in\Gamma(p,q)}\|d\|_{\mu,l}
, where
\Gamma(p,q)
denotes the distributions over
\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}
with marginal distributions
p,q
, and
\|d\|_{\mu,l}=\big(\mathbb{E}_{(X,Y)\sim\mu}\big[d(X,Y)^{l}\big]\big)^{1/l}
. The Wasserstein distance uncertainty set is then defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}=\left\{q\in\Delta(|\mathcal{S}|):W_{l}(\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s},q)\leq\delta\right\}.

(18)

To solve the support function w.r.t. the Wasserstein distance set, we first prove the following duality lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)=\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\left(-\lambda\delta^{l}+\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}\big[\inf_{y}\big(V(y)+\lambda d(S,y)^{l}\big)\big]\right).

(19)

Thus, the support function can be solved using its dual form, and the estimator can then be constructed following (
13
).

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we developed the first model-free algorithms with provable convergence and optimality guarantee for robust average-reward RL under a broad range of uncertainty set models. We characterized the fundamental structure of solutions to the robust average-reward Bellman equation, which is crucial for the convergence analysis. We designed model-free robust algorithms based on the ideas of relative value iteration for non-robust average-reward MDPs and the robust average-reward Bellman equation.
We developed concrete solutions to five popular uncertainty sets, where we generalized the idea of multi-level Monte-Carlo and constructed an unbiased estimate of the non-linear robust average-reward Bellman operator.

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}^{\pi}ge=ge

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{P}^{\pi}r_{\pi}+\mathsf{P}^{\pi}(\mathsf{P}^{\pi}-I)V,

\displaystyle ge

\displaystyle\leq(\mathsf{P}^{\pi})^{2}r_{\pi}+(\mathsf{P}^{\pi})^{2}(\mathsf{P}^{\pi}-I)V,

(104)

Lemma

∎

Appendix D
Case Studies for Robust RVI TD

In this section, we provide the proof of the first part of
Theorem

5.1
, i.e., that
\hat{\mathbf{T}}
is unbiased and has bounded variance under each uncertainty model.

We first show a lemma, by which the problem can be reduced to investigating whether
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}
is unbiased and has bounded variance.

Lemma D.1
.

If

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V]=\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V),\forall s,a,

(119)

and moreover, there exists a constant
C
, such that

\displaystyle\text{Var}(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V)\leq C(1+\|V\|^{2}),\forall s,a,

(120)

then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mathbf{T}}V(s)]=\mathbf{T}V(s),\forall s,

(121)

and

\displaystyle\text{Var}(\hat{\mathbf{T}}V(s))\leq|\mathcal{A}|C(1+\|V\|^{2}),\forall s.

(122)

Proof.

From the definition,
\hat{\mathbf{T}}V(s)=\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(r(s,a)+\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mathbf{T}}V(s)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(r(s,a)+\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(r(s,a)+\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V])

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(r(s,a)+\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V))=\mathbf{T}V(s),

(123)

which shows that
\hat{\mathbf{T}}
is unbiased. On the other hand, we have that

\displaystyle\text{Var}(\hat{\mathbf{T}}V(s))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(r(s,a)+\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V)-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(r(s,a)+\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V)\bigg]\bigg)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(r(s,a)+\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V)-\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(r(s,a)+\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V\big]\bigg)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V)-\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V\big]\bigg)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V-\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V\big])^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)\mathbb{E}\bigg[(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V-\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V^{2}\big])^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle{\leq}\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)\text{Var}(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{A}|C(1+\|V\|^{2}),

(124)

where
(a)
is because
(\mathbb{E}[X])^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]
, which completes the proof.
∎

This lemma implies that to prove
Theorem

5.1
, it suffices to show that
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}
is unbiased and has bounded variance.

Theorem D.1

\hat{\mathbf{T}}

Proof.

First, note that

\displaystyle V_{n+1}(s)

\displaystyle=V_{n}(s)+\alpha_{n}(r(s,a)+((1-\delta)V_{n}(s^{\prime})+\delta\min_{x}V_{n}(x)-f(V_{n})-V_{n}(s))

The estimator under the total variation uncertainty set can be written as

Theorem D.2
.

The estimated operator
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}
defined in (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V]=\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V).

Proof.

\displaystyle g_{s,a}^{V}(\mu)=\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(V-\mu)-\delta\text{Span}(V-\mu),

(133)

and denote its optimal solution by

\displaystyle\mu^{V}_{s,a}=\arg\max_{\mu\geq 0}\big(\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(V-\mu)-\delta\text{Span}(V-\mu)\big).

(134)

Then, the support function
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)=g_{s,a}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a})

Similarly, define the empirical function

\displaystyle{g}_{s,a,N+1}^{V}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,N+1}(V-\mu)-\delta\text{Span}(V-\mu),

(135)

\displaystyle{g}_{s,a,N+1,O}^{V}(\mu)

and their optimal solutions are denoted by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{\mu\geq 0}\big(\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a,1}_{s,N+1}(V-\mu)-\delta\text{Span}(V-\mu)\big)+\frac{\Delta_{N}(V)}{p_{N}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[g^{V}_{s,a,0}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,0})]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{\Delta_{N}(V)}{p_{N}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[g^{V}_{s,a,0}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,0})]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}p(N=n)\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{\Delta_{N}(V)}{p_{N}}|N=n\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[g^{V}_{s,a,0}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,0})]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{n}(V)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[g^{V}_{s,a,0}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,0})]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}\bigg[g^{V}_{s,a,n+1}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n+1})-\frac{g^{V}_{s,a,n+1,O}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n+1,O})+g^{V}_{s,a,n+1,E}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n+1,E})}{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[g^{V}_{s,a,0}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,0})]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}\bigg[g^{V}_{s,a,n+1}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n+1})-g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n})\bigg],

(138)

where the last inequality is from Lemma
F.2

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V]

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg[g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n})\bigg]=g^{V}_{s,a}(\mu^{V}_{s,a}).

\{X_{i}\}

F.3

and their optimal solutions are denoted by
\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,O},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,E}
. We have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[g^{V}_{s,a,0}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,0})]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{\Delta_{N}(V)}{p_{N}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[g^{V}_{s,a,0}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,0})]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}p(N=n)\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{\Delta_{N}(V)}{p_{N}}|N=n\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[g^{V}_{s,a,0}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,0})]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{n}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[g^{V}_{s,a,0}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,0})]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}\bigg[g^{V}_{s,a,n+1}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n+1})-\frac{g^{V}_{s,a,n+1,O}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n+1,O})+g^{V}_{s,a,n+1,E}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n+1,E})}{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[g^{V}_{s,a,0}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,0})]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}\bigg[g^{V}_{s,a,n+1}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n+1})-g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n})\bigg],

(156)

where the last inequality is from Lemma
F.2
. The last equation can be further rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[g^{V}_{s,a,0}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,0})]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}\bigg[g^{V}_{s,a,n+1}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n+1})-g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n})\bigg]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg[g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n})\bigg].

(157)

To show that
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}
is unbiased, it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg[g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n})\bigg]=g^{V}_{s,a}(\mu^{V}_{s,a}).

(158)

For any arbitrary i.i.d. samples
\{X_{i}\}
and its corresponding function
g^{V}_{s,a,n}
, together with Lemma
F.4
, we have that

\displaystyle|g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n})-g^{V}_{s,a}(\mu^{V}_{s,a})|

\displaystyle=|\max_{0\leq\mu\leq V+\|V\|e}g^{V}_{s,a}(\mu)-\max_{0\leq\mu\leq V+\|V\|e}g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\mu)|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{0\leq\mu\leq V+\|V\|e}|g^{V}_{s,a}(\mu)-g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\mu)|

\displaystyle=\max_{0\leq\mu\leq V+\|V\|e}\bigg|\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(V-\mu)-\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,n}(V-\mu)-\bigg(\sqrt{\delta\text{Var}_{{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}}(V-\mu)}-\sqrt{\delta\text{Var}_{\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,n}}(V-\mu)}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{0\leq\mu\leq V+\|V\|e}|\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(V-\mu)-\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,n}(V-\mu)|+\max_{0\leq\mu\leq V+\|V\|e}\bigg|\bigg(\sqrt{\delta\text{Var}_{{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}}(V-\mu)}-\sqrt{\delta\text{Var}_{\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,n}}(V-\mu)}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\max_{0\leq\mu\leq V+\|V\|e}\|V-\mu\|\|\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}-\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,n}\|_{1}+\max_{0\leq\mu\leq V+\|V\|e}\sqrt{|\delta\text{Var}_{{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}}(V-\mu)-\delta\text{Var}_{\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,n}}(V-\mu)|},

(159)

where
(a)
is due to
|\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y}|\leq\sqrt{|x-y|}
.
Note that for any distribution
p,q\in\Delta(|\mathcal{S}|)
and any random variable
X
,

\displaystyle|\text{Var}_{p}[X]-\text{Var}_{q}[X]|

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{E}_{p}[X^{2}]-\mathbb{E}_{p}[X]^{2}-\mathbb{E}_{q}[X^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{q}[X]^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbb{E}_{p}[X^{2}]-\mathbb{E}_{q}[X^{2}]|+|(\mathbb{E}_{p}[X]+\mathbb{E}_{q}[X])(\mathbb{E}_{p}[X]-\mathbb{E}_{q}[X])|

\displaystyle\leq\sup|X^{2}|\|p-q\|_{1}+2(\sup|X|)^{2}\|p-q\|_{1}.

(160)

Hence,

\displaystyle\sqrt{|\delta\text{Var}_{{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}}(V-\mu)-\delta\text{Var}_{\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,n}}(V-\mu)|}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{3\delta\|V-\mu\|^{2}\|\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}-\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,n}\|_{1}}.

(161)

Thus, by Hoeffding’s inequality and Theorem 3.7 from
(
Liu et al. 2022
)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n})-g^{V}_{s,a}(\mu^{V}_{s,a})|]\leq 3\|V\|\left(\frac{|\mathcal{S}|^{2}\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}+\sqrt{\frac{3\delta|\mathcal{S}|^{2}\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}}\right),

(162)

which implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg[g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,n})\bigg]=g^{V}_{s,a}(\mu^{V}_{s,a}),

(163)

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem D.5
.

\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\text{Var}(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V)^{2}]-\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)^{2}

where the last inequality is from Lemma

.

\displaystyle\leq 18(1+\sqrt{3\delta})^{2}\|V\|^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}-\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,n}\|_{1}^{2}+\|\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}-\hat{\mathsf{P}}^{a}_{s,n}\|_{1}]

where

(168)

To study the support function w.r.t. this uncertainty model, we first introduce some notation.

Definition D.1
.

For any function
f:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda\geq 0
and
x\in\mathcal{Z}
, define the regularization operator

\displaystyle\Phi(\lambda,x)\triangleq\inf_{y\in\mathcal{Z}}(\lambda d(x,y)^{l}+f(y)).

(170)

The growth rate
\kappa
of function
f
and any distribution
q
over
\mathcal{Z}
is defined as

\displaystyle\kappa_{q}\triangleq\inf\bigg(\lambda\geq 0:\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Z}}q(x)\Phi(\lambda,x)>-\infty\bigg).

(171)

Lemma D.2
.

(
Gao & Kleywegt 2022
)

Consider the distributional robust optimization of a function
f
:

\displaystyle\inf_{W_{l}(q,p)\leq\delta}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim q}[f(x)],

(172)

and define its dual problem as

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}(-\lambda\delta^{l}+\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Z}}p(x)\inf_{y\in\mathcal{Z}}(f(y)+\lambda d(x,y)^{l})).

(173)

If
\kappa_{p}<\infty
, then the strong duality holds, i.e.,

\displaystyle\inf_{W_{l}(q,p)\leq\delta}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim q}[f(x)]=\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}(-\lambda\delta^{l}+\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Z}}p(x)\inf_{y\in\mathcal{Z}}(f(y)+\lambda d(x,y)^{l})).

(174)

We first verify that this strong duality holds for our support function.

Lemma D.3
.

(Restatement of
Equation

19
)
It holds that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)=\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\bigg(-\lambda\delta^{l}+\sum_{x}\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s,x}\inf_{y}(V(y)+\lambda d(x,y)^{l})\bigg).

(175)

Proof.

In our case, the regularization operator is

\displaystyle\Phi(\lambda,x)=\inf_{s\in\mathcal{S}}(\lambda d(s,x)^{l}+V(s)).

(176)

Note that for any
\lambda\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(x)\Phi(\lambda,x)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(x)\inf_{s\in\mathcal{S}}(\lambda d(s,x)^{l}+V(s))\geq-\|V\|>-\infty.

(177)

Hence, the growth rate
\kappa_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}=0<\infty
. Thus, the strong duality holds.
∎

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V

(178)

In this section, we provide the proof of the second part of

Lemma E.1
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\|V_{Q}\|\leq\|Q\|.

(193)

Proof.

From the definition of

\displaystyle\|V_{Q}\|=\max_{s}|V_{Q}(s)|=\max_{s}|\max_{a}Q(s,a)|\triangleq|Q(s^{*},a^{*})|.

(194)

\displaystyle\|V_{Q}\|\leq\|Q\|.

(195)

∎

Similar to
Appendix

D
, the propositions of
\hat{\mathbf{H}}
can be reduced to the ones of
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}
.

Lemma E.2
.

If

\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V]=\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)
,
and moreover there exists a constant
C
, such that for any
s,a
,

\text{Var}(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V)\leq C(1+\|V\|^{2})
,
then

\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mathbf{H}}Q(s,a)]=\mathbf{H}Q(s,a),

and
\text{Var}(\hat{\mathbf{H}}Q(s,a))\leq C(1+\|Q\|^{2}).

Proof.

First, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mathbf{H}}Q(s,a)]=\mathbb{E}[r(s,a)+\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V_{Q}(s)]=r(s,a)+\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V_{Q})=\mathbf{H}Q(s,a).

(196)

For boundedness, note that

\displaystyle\text{Var}(\hat{\mathbf{H}}Q(s,a))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[(\hat{\mathbf{H}}Q(s,a)-\mathbf{H}Q(s,a))^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}V_{Q}(s)-\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V_{Q})\big)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|V_{Q}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|Q\|^{2}),

(197)

where the last inequality is from Lemma
E.1
.
∎

This implies that the problem is reduced to verifying whether
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}
is unbiased and has bounded variance, which is identical to the results in
Appendix

D
. We thus omit the proofs for this part.

Appendix F

Lemma F.1
.

For a robust Bellman operator
\mathbf{T}
, define

\displaystyle{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}V

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathbf{T}}V-f(V)e,

(198)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{T}}V

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathbf{T}}V-g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}e.

(199)

Assume that
x(t),y(t)
are the solutions to equations

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}x-x,

(200)

\displaystyle\dot{y}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbf{T}}y-y,

(201)

with the same initial value
x(0)=y(0)=x_{0}
. Then,

\displaystyle x(t)=y(t)+r(t)e,

(202)

where
r(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\dot{r}(t)=-r(t)+g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}-f(y(t)).

(203)

Proof.

Note that
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}V=\tilde{\mathbf{T}}V+(g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}-f(V))e
, then from the variation of constants formula, we have that

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=x_{0}e^{-t}+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-s)}\tilde{\mathbf{T}}(x(s))ds+\left(\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-s)}(g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}-f(x(s)))ds\right)e,

(204)

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=x_{0}e^{-t}+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-s)}\tilde{\mathbf{T}}(y(s))ds.

(205)

Hence, the maximal and minimal components of
x(t)-y(t)
can be bounded as:

\displaystyle\max_{i}(x_{i}(t)-y_{i}(t))

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-s)}\max_{i}(\tilde{\mathbf{T}}_{i}(x(s))-\tilde{\mathbf{T}}_{i}(y(s)))ds+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-s)}(g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}-f(x(s)))ds,

\displaystyle\min_{i}(x_{i}(t)-y_{i}(t))

\displaystyle\geq\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-s)}\min_{i}(\tilde{\mathbf{T}}_{i}(x(s))-\tilde{\mathbf{T}}_{i}(y(s)))ds+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-s)}(g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}-f(x(s)))ds.

(206)

This hence implies that

\displaystyle\text{Span}(x(t)-y(t))

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-s)}\text{Span}(\tilde{\mathbf{T}}(x(s))-\tilde{\mathbf{T}}(y(s)))ds

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-s)}\text{Span}(x(s)-y(s))ds,

(207)

where the last inequality is because
\tilde{\mathbf{T}}
is non-expansive w.r.t. the span semi-norm
(
Wang et al. 2023
)
.

Gronwall’s inequality implies that
\text{Span}(x(t)-y(t))\leq 0\cdot\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-s)}ds=0
for any
t\geq 0
. However, since
Span
is non-negative, then
\text{Span}(x(t)-y(t))=0
. Hence, we have that
x(t)=y(t)+r(t)e
for some
r(t)
satisfying
r(0)=0
.

Also note that the differential of
r(t)
can be written as

\displaystyle\dot{r}(t)e

\displaystyle=\dot{x}(t)-\dot{y}(t)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbf{T}}x(t)+(g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}-f(x(t)))e-x(t)-\tilde{\mathbf{T}}y(t)+y(t)

\displaystyle=(-r(t)+g^{\pi}_{\mathcal{P}}-f(y(t)))e,

(208)

where the last equation is because

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{T}}x(t)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbf{T}}(y(t)+r(t)e)=\tilde{\mathbf{T}}(y(t))+r(t)e,

(209)

\displaystyle f(x(t))

\displaystyle=f(y(t)+r(t)e)=f(y(t))+r(t).

(210)

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma F.2
.

For any function
g:\Delta(|\mathcal{S}|)\to\mathbb{R}
, assume there are
2^{n+1}
i.i.d. samples
X_{i}\sim q
. Denote the empirical distributions from samples
\{X_{i}:i=1,...,2^{n+1}\},\{X_{2i-1}:i=1,...,2^{n}\},\{X_{2i}:i=1,...,2^{n}\}
by
\hat{q}_{n+1},\hat{q}_{n+1,O},\hat{q}_{n+1,E}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[g(\hat{q}_{n+1,O})]=\mathbb{E}[g(\hat{q}_{n+1,E})]=\mathbb{E}[g(\hat{q}_{n})].

(211)

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{n+1,O}(s)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{X_{2i-1}=s}}{2^{n}},

(212)

hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[g(\hat{q}_{n+1,O})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=(p_{1},...,p_{|\mathcal{S}|})\in\Delta(|\mathcal{S}|)}g(p)\mathbb{P}(\hat{q}_{n+1,O}=p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=(p_{1},...,p_{|\mathcal{S}|})\in\Delta(|\mathcal{S}|)}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\frac{\sum^{2^{n}}_{i=1}\mathbbm{1}_{X_{2i-1}=s_{1}}}{2^{n}}=p_{1},...,\frac{\sum^{2^{n}}_{i=1}\mathbbm{1}_{X_{2i-1}=s_{|\mathcal{S}|}}}{2^{n}}=p_{|\mathcal{S}|}\bigg|q\bigg)g(p),

(213)

where
2^{n}p_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{S}|}p_{i}=1
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[g(\hat{q}_{n})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=(p_{1},...,p_{|\mathcal{S}|})\in\Delta(|\mathcal{S}|)}g(p)\mathbb{P}(\hat{q}_{n}=p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=(p_{1},...,p_{|\mathcal{S}|})\in\Delta(|\mathcal{S}|)}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\frac{\sum^{2^{n}}_{i=1}\mathbbm{1}_{X_{i}=s_{1}}}{2^{n}}=p_{1},...,\frac{\sum^{2^{n}}_{i=1}\mathbbm{1}_{X_{i}=s_{|\mathcal{S}|}}}{2^{n}}=p_{|\mathcal{S}|}\bigg|q\bigg)g(p).

(214)

Note that
X_{i}
are i.i.d., hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\frac{\sum^{2^{n}}_{i=1}\mathbbm{1}_{X_{i}=s_{1}}}{2^{n}}=p_{1},...,\frac{\sum^{2^{n}}_{i=1}\mathbbm{1}_{X_{i}=s_{|\mathcal{S}|}}}{2^{n}}=p_{|\mathcal{S}|}\bigg|q\bigg)=\mathbb{P}\bigg(\frac{\sum^{2^{n}}_{i=1}\mathbbm{1}_{X_{2i-1}=s_{1}}}{2^{n}}=p_{1},...,\frac{\sum^{2^{n}}_{i=1}\mathbbm{1}_{X_{2i-1}=s_{|\mathcal{S}|}}}{2^{n}}=p_{|\mathcal{S}|}\bigg|q\bigg).

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[g(\hat{q}_{n+1,O})]=\mathbb{E}[g(\hat{q}_{n})].

(215)

Similarly,
\mathbb{E}[g(\hat{q}_{n+1,E})]=\mathbb{E}[g(\hat{q}_{n})]
and hence it completes the proof.
∎

Lemma F.3
.

Under the total variation uncertainty model, the optimal solution and optimal value for
g_{s,a}^{V},g_{s,a,N+1}^{V},g_{s,a,N+1,E}^{V},g_{s,a,N+1,O}^{V}
are bounded. Specifically,

\displaystyle\mu^{V}_{s,a},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,E},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,O}

\displaystyle\leq V+\|V\|e,

(216)

\displaystyle\|\mu^{V}_{s,a}\|,\|\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1}\|,\|\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,E}\|,\|\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,O}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|V\|,

(217)

\displaystyle|g_{s,a}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a})|,|g_{s,a,N+1}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1})|,|g_{s,a,N+1,O}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,O})|,|g_{s,a,N+1,E}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,E})|

\displaystyle\leq 3(1+2\delta)\|V\|.

(218)

Proof.

First we show the bounds on the optimal solutions.
If we denote the minimal entry of
V
by
w
:
w=\min_{s}V(s)
, then
W\triangleq V-we\geq 0
.
Note that,

\displaystyle\mu^{W}_{s,a}

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{\mu\geq 0}\big(\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(W-\mu)-\delta\text{Span}(W-\mu)\big)

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{\mu\geq 0}\big(-w+\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(V-\mu)-\delta\text{Span}(V-\mu)\big),

(219)

which is because
\text{Span}(V+ke)=\text{Span}(V)
and
\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(V+ke)=k+\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}V
. Hence,
\mu^{W}_{s,a}=\mu^{V}_{s,a}
. Moreover note that
W\geq 0
, hence
\mu^{W}_{s,a}
is bounded:
\mu^{W}_{s,a}\leq W
, this further implies that

\displaystyle\|\mu^{V}_{s,a}\|=\|\mu^{W}_{s,a}\|\leq\|W\|\leq 2\|V\|.

(220)

The bounds on
\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,O},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,E}
can be similarly derived.

We then consider the optimal value. Note that,

\displaystyle g_{s,a}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(V-\mu^{V}_{s,a})-\delta\text{Span}(V-\mu^{V}_{s,a})

\displaystyle\leq\|V\|+\|\mu^{V}_{s,a}\|+\delta|\max_{i}(V(i)-\mu^{V}_{s,a}(i))|+\delta|\min_{i}(V(i)-\mu^{V}_{s,a}(i))|

\displaystyle\leq 3\|V\|+2\delta(\|V\|+\|\mu^{V}_{s,a}\|)

\displaystyle\leq 3(1+2\delta)\|V\|.

(221)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle g_{s,a}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a})\geq g_{s,a}^{V}(0)=\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}V-\delta\text{Span}(V)=\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}V-\delta\max_{i}V(i)+\delta\min_{i}V(i).

(222)

Denote the maximal and minimal entries of
V
by
V(M)
and
V(m)
, then we have that

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}V-\delta\max_{i}V(i)+\delta\min_{i}V(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s,x}V(x)-\delta V(M)+\delta V(m)

\displaystyle\geq-\|V\|-2\delta\|V\|,

(223)

where the last inequality is from
\|V\|\geq V(i)\geq-\|V\|
for any entry
i
. Thus, combining (
222
) and (
F
) implies that

\displaystyle-(1+2\delta)\|V\|\leq g_{s,a}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a})\leq 3(1+2\delta)\|V\|.

(224)

Similarly, the bounds on
g_{s,a,N+1}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1}),g_{s,a,N+1,O}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,O}),g_{s,a,N+1,E}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,E})
can be derived.
∎

Lemma F.4
.

Under the chi-square uncertainty model, the optimal solution and optimal value for
g_{s,a}^{V},g_{s,a,N+1}^{V},g_{s,a,N+1,E}^{V},g_{s,a,N+1,O}^{V}
are bounded. Specifically,

\displaystyle\mu^{V}_{s,a},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,E},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,O}

\displaystyle\leq V+\|V\|e,

(225)

\displaystyle\|\mu^{V}_{s,a}\|,\|\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1}\|,\|\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,E}\|,\|\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,O}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|V\|,

(226)

\displaystyle|g_{s,a}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a})|,|g_{s,a,N+1}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1})|,|g_{s,a,N+1,O}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,O})|,|g_{s,a,N+1,E}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,E})|

\displaystyle\leq 3(1+\sqrt{2\delta})\|V\|.

(227)

Proof.

First, we show the bounds on the optimal solutions.
If we denote the minimal entry of
V
by
w
:
w=\min_{s}V(s)
, then
W\triangleq V-we\geq 0
.
Note that,

\displaystyle\mu^{W}_{s,a}

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{W\geq\mu\geq 0}\big(\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(W-\mu)-\sqrt{\delta\text{Var}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}(W-\mu)}\big)

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{W\geq\mu\geq 0}\big(-w+\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(V-\mu)-\sqrt{\delta\text{Var}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}(V-\mu)}\big),

(228)

which is because
\text{Var}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}(V-\mu-we)=\text{Var}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}(V-\mu)+\text{Var}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}(we)-2\text{Cov}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}(V-\mu,we)=\text{Var}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}(V-\mu)
. Hence
\mu^{W}_{s,a}=\mu^{V}_{s,a}
. Moreover note that
W\geq 0
, hence
\mu^{W}_{s,a}
is bounded:
\mu^{W}_{s,a}\leq W
, this further implies that

\displaystyle\|\mu^{V}_{s,a}\|=\|\mu^{W}_{s,a}\|\leq\|W\|\leq 2\|V\|.

(229)

The bounds on
\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,O},\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,E}

We then consider the optimal value. Note that,

\displaystyle|g_{s,a}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a})|

\displaystyle=|\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}(V-\mu^{V}_{s,a})-\sqrt{\delta\text{Var}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}(V-\mu^{V}_{s,a})}|

\displaystyle\leq\|V\|+\|\mu^{V}_{s,a}\|+\sqrt{2\delta\|V-\mu^{V}_{s,a}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\|V\|+\sqrt{2\delta}(\|V\|+\|\mu^{V}_{s,a}\|)

\displaystyle\leq 3(1+\sqrt{2\delta})\|V\|.

(230)

Similarly, the bounds on
g_{s,a,N+1}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1}),g_{s,a,N+1,O}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,O}),g_{s,a,N+1,E}^{V}(\mu^{V}_{s,a,N+1,E})
can be derived.
∎

Lemma F.5
.

Under the Wasserstein distance uncertainty model, the optimal solution and optimal value for
g_{s,a}^{V},g_{s,a,N+1}^{V},g_{s,a,N+1,E}^{V},g_{s,a,N+1,O}^{V}
are bounded. Specifically,

\displaystyle\lambda^{V}_{s,a},\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n},\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n,O},\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n,E}\leq\frac{2\|V\|}{\delta^{l}},

(231)

\displaystyle|g^{V}_{s,a}(\lambda^{V}_{s,a})|,|g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n})|,|g^{V}_{s,a,n,O}(\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n,O})|,|g^{V}_{s,a,n,E}(\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n,E})|\leq\|V\|.

(232)

Proof.

First, we show the bounds on the optimal solutions.
Denote the optimal solution to
\max_{\lambda\geq 0}g_{s,a}^{V}(\lambda)
by
\lambda^{V}_{s,a}
. Moreover, for each state
y\in\mathcal{S}
and any
\lambda\geq 0
, denote
s^{y}_{\lambda}\triangleq\arg\min_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\{\lambda d(x,y)^{l}+V(x)\}
. Hence,

\displaystyle g^{V}_{s,a}(\lambda)=-\lambda\delta^{l}+\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}[\lambda d(S,s^{S}_{\lambda})^{l}+V(s^{S}_{\lambda})].

(233)

Moreover, note that
g^{V}_{s,a}(\lambda^{V}_{s,a})=\max_{\lambda\geq 0}g^{V}_{s,a}(\lambda)
, hence,

\displaystyle-\lambda^{V}_{s,a}\delta^{l}+\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}[\lambda^{V}_{s,a}d(S,s^{S}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})^{l}+V(s^{S}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})]\geq g^{V}_{s,a}(0)=\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}[V(s^{S}_{0})]=\min_{x}V(x),

(234)

where the last equation is due to the fact that
s^{S}_{0}=\arg\min_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\{V(x)\}=\min_{x}V(x)
.
Now consider the inner problem
\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}[\lambda^{V}_{s,a}d(S,s^{S}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})^{l}+V(s^{S}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})]
. Note that,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}[\lambda^{V}_{s,a}d(S,s^{S}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})^{l}+V(s^{S}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s,x}\big(\lambda^{V}_{s,a}d(x,s^{x}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})^{l}+V(s^{x}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})\big)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\sum_{x}\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s,x}\big(\lambda^{V}_{s,a}d(x,x)^{l}+V(x)\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}[V(S)],

(235)

where
(a)
is because
s^{x}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}}=\arg\min_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}d(x,y)^{l}+V(y)\}
and hence
\lambda^{V}_{s,a}d(x,s^{x}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})^{l}+V(s^{x}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})\leq\lambda^{V}_{s,a}d(x,x)^{l}+V(x)
.

Combine (
234
) and (
F
), then we further have that

\displaystyle\min_{x}V(x)\leq-\lambda^{V}_{s,a}\delta^{l}+\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}[\lambda^{V}_{s,a}d(S,s^{S}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})^{l}+V(s^{S}_{\lambda^{V}_{s,a}})]\leq-\lambda^{V}_{s,a}\delta^{l}+\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}[V(S)].

(236)

This implies that

\displaystyle\lambda^{V}_{s,a}\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{P}^{a}_{s}}[V(S)]-\min_{x}V(x)}{\delta^{l}}\leq\frac{2\|V\|}{\delta^{l}},

(237)

and hence
\lambda^{V}_{s,a}
is bounded.

On the other hand, note that
g^{V}_{s,a}(\lambda^{V}_{s,a})=\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}[V(S)]
, hence,

\displaystyle\big|g^{V}_{s,a}(\lambda^{V}_{s,a})\big|\leq\|V\|.

(238)

Same bound can be similarly derived for
\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n},\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n,O},\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n,E},g^{V}_{s,a,n}(\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n}),g^{V}_{s,a,n,O}(\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n,O}),g^{V}_{s,a,n,E}(\lambda^{V}_{s,a,n,E})
.
∎

Lemma F.6
.

For an optimal robust Bellman operator:

{\mathbf{H}}Q(s,a)=r(s,a)+\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V_{Q}),

define

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}^{\prime}Q

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathbf{H}}Q-f(Q)e,

(239)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{H}}Q

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathbf{H}}Q-g^{*}_{\mathcal{P}}e.

(240)

Assume that
x(t),y(t)
are the solutions to equations

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{H}}^{\prime}x-x,

(241)

\displaystyle\dot{y}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbf{H}}y-y,

(242)

with the same initial value
x(0)=y(0)
. Then

x(t)=y(t)+r(t)e,

where
r(t)
satisfies

\dot{r}(t)=-r(t)+g^{*}_{\mathcal{P}}-f(y(t)).

Proof.

The proof follows exactly that of Lemma
F.1
if we show that
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}
is non-expansion w.r.t. the span semi-norm.

Following Theorem 17 of
(
Wang et al. 2023
)
, it can be shown that

\displaystyle\text{Span}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(Q_{1})-\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(Q_{2}))\leq\text{Span}(V_{Q_{1}}-V_{Q_{2}}).

(243)

Let

\displaystyle s=\arg\max_{i}\{\max_{a}Q_{1}(i,a)-\max_{a}Q_{2}(i,a)\},

(244)

\displaystyle t=\arg\min_{i}\{\max_{a}Q_{1}(i,a)-\max_{a}Q_{2}(i,a)\}.

(245)

Then,

\displaystyle\text{Span}(V_{Q_{1}}-V_{Q_{2}})

\displaystyle=(\max_{a}Q_{1}(s,a)-\max_{a}Q_{2}(s,a))-(\max_{a}Q_{1}(t,a)-\max_{a}Q_{2}(t,a))

\displaystyle\leq Q_{1}(s,a_{s})-Q_{2}(s,a_{s})-(Q_{1}(t,a_{t})-Q_{2}(t,a_{t}))

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x,b}(Q_{1}(x,b)-Q_{2}(x,b))-\min_{x,b}(Q_{1}(x,b)-Q_{2}(x,b))

\displaystyle=\text{Span}(Q_{1}-Q_{2}).

(246)

where
a_{s}=\arg\max_{a}Q_{1}(S,a)
and
a_{t}=\arg\max_{a}Q_{2}(t,a)
. This completes the proof.
∎

As the results show, our robust RVI Q-learning learns a more robust policy under the nominal environment, which obtains a higher reward in the perturbed environment; whereas the non-robust Q-learning learns a policy that is optimal w.r.t. the nominal environment, but less robust when the environment is perturbed. This verifies that our algorithm is more robust than the vanilla method.

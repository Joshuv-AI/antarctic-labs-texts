Prime subcomplexes
Ismael Akray

Abstract
. In 1859, Ernst E. Kummer [ 7 ] introduced the concept prime ideal and after that in 1983 this concept was generalized to prime submodules by R. McCasland [ 8 ] . In this article, by considering complexes as a generalization of modules, I introduce the concept prime subcomplex and study some properties analogous to that of prime submodules.

2.
Prime subcomplexes

In this section we introduce prime subcomplex and investigate some properties and results on such concept. We begin with their definition.

Definition 2.1
.

A proper subcomplex

Let

Proposition 2.3
.

Every direct summand subcomplex

Proof.

Assume

C=S\oplus T

rm\in S_{j}

Proposition 2.4

if and only if

. Hence

and we conclude from part (10) that

or

.

The remains are clear.
∎

Theorem 2.10
.

An

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
C
and
F
be
R-
complexes and
S
be a subcomplex of
C
. Then
F_{i}\otimes(S_{i}:r)\subseteq(F_{i}\otimes S_{i}:r)
, for any

. The equality holds if

is a flat complex.

Proof.

Let
f\otimes c\in F_{i}\otimes(S_{i}:r)
for

,
rc\in S_{i}
and
i\in I
. Then
r(f\otimes c)=f\otimes rc\in F_{i}\otimes S_{i}
. Now, suppose
F
is flat. Then clearly
F_{i}\otimes\frac{C_{i}}{S_{i}}\cong\frac{F_{i}\otimes C_{i}}{F_{i}\otimes S_{i}}
. Also, exactness of

0\to F_{i}\otimes(S_{i}:r)\to F_{i}\otimes C_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i\otimes f_{r}}}{{\to}}\frac{F_{i}\otimes C_{i}}{F_{i}\otimes S_{i}}

gives that
Im(i\otimes f_{r})=F_{i}\otimes(S_{i}:r)=(F_{i}\otimes S_{i}:r)
,
\forall i\in I
.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let

be a prime subcomplex of

. Then

Proof.

We prove this theorem by the equivalence (4) of prime subcomplexes in Theorem

. For this purpose, let

for

. Then the primeness of

(4) gives us
(S_{i}:r)=S_{i}
and we obtain from Lemma

again, we conclude that

is a prime subcomplex of

.

The converse of Theorem

Theorem 2.17
.

Let

Proof.

Since the exactness of the sequence

so we can assume that

We now generalize the prime avoidance theorem to prime subcomplexes.

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
T_{1},\cdots,T_{n}
be subcomplexes of an
R-
complex
C
and let
S
be a prime subcomplex of
C
with
T_{1}\cap T_{2}\cap\cdots\cap T_{n}\subseteq S
. Then there exists an
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
such that either
T_{i}\subseteq S
or
(T_{i}:C)\subseteq(S:C)
.

Proof.

Assume by contrary that
T_{1}\nsubseteq S
and
((T_{i})_{j}:C_{j})\nsubseteq(S_{j}:C_{j})
for some
j\in P_{I}
. So, there exist
m\in(T_{1})_{j}-S_{j}
and
r_{i}\in((T_{i})_{j}:C_{j})-(S_{j}:C_{j})
for all
i=1,\cdots,n
. Thus
r_{i}m\in(T_{1})_{j}\cap(T_{i})_{j}
for
i\in\{2,\cdots,n\}
, so
r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n}m\in(T_{1})_{j}\cap(T_{2})_{j}\cap\cdots\cap(T_{n})_{j}=(T_{1}\cap\cdots\cap T_{n})_{j}\subseteq S_{j}
. The primeness of
S
gives us
m\in S_{j}
or
r_{2}r_{3}\cdots r_{n}\in(S_{j}:C_{j})
which contradicts our assumption.
∎

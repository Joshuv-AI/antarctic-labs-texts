(1)

\displaystyle\conv(\phi(e_{1}))\cap\conv(\phi(e_{2}))=\conv(\phi(e_{1})\cap\phi(e_{2}))\textrm{ for every $e_{1},e_{2}\in E$}.

(2)

Note that the inclusion

Let us first introduce some conventions of basic convex geometry following Ziegler’s book on polytopes

Let

for every

.
A non-zero affine dependency
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

\bm{z}
of

\bm{z}^{\prime}
of

Proof.

Let
\bm{z}^{\prime}=(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{n}^{\prime})\neq 0

\sign(\bm{z}^{\prime})\leq\sign(\bm{z})
. We show that
|\supp(\bm{z}^{\prime})|=d+2

\bm{z}^{\prime\prime}=(z_{1}^{\prime\prime},\dots,z_{n}^{\prime\prime})

.

If

I=\{i\in\supp(\bm{z}^{\prime\prime}):z_{i}^{\prime}<0<z_{i}^{\prime\prime}\textrm{ or }z_{i}^{\prime\prime}<0<z_{i}^{\prime}\}

Let
\rho

\bm{z}^{(3)}=\bm{z}^{\prime}+\rho\bm{z}^{\prime\prime}
is an affine dependency of

. It is enough to show that

|z_{i}^{\prime\prime}|\rho\leq|z_{i}^{\prime\prime}||z_{i}^{\prime}|/|z_{i}^{\prime\prime}|=|z_{i}^{\prime}|,

Suppose that

S_{1},\dots,S_{l}
which are in skewed position is defined as their convex hull:

S_{1}*\cdots*S_{l}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda_{i}p_{i}:\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda_{i}=1,\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\textrm{ and }p_{i}\in S_{i}\textrm{ for every $i\in[l]$}\right\}.

Note that every
k
disjoint edges in the boundary of the cyclic polytope
C_{2k}(n)
are in skewed position,
and their images under the projection from
\mathbb{R}^{2k}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2k-1}
that forgets the last coordinate are in skewed position, with vertices on
\gamma_{2k-1}
, and with geometric join a
(2k-1)
-simplex.

4.1
Construction of
L_{d}

First, we construct
L_{d}
. Fix a positive integer
k\geq 2
and set
d=2k-1
.

(1)

For
i\in[d]=[2k-1]
, let

V_{i}=\{v_{1}^{i},\dots,v_{4k-2}^{i}\}

be a set of
4k-2
vertices. Let
P_{i}
be the edge set of the path on
V_{i}
where the vertices appear in order, that is,

P_{i}=\{\{v_{1}^{i},v_{2}^{i}\},\dots,\{v_{4k-3}^{i},v_{4k-2}^{i}\}\}.

(2)

Let

K_{1}=\bigcup_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq d}P_{i_{1}}*\cdots*P_{i_{k}},

where
*
denotes the abstract join operation.
Note that
K_{1}
is a
(d+1)
-uniform hypergraph.

(3)

We define
K_{2}
in a similar way. Let

\displaystyle W_{i}

\displaystyle=\{w_{1}^{i},\dots,w_{4k-2}^{i}\},\textrm{ and}

\displaystyle Q_{i}

\displaystyle=\{\{w_{1}^{i},w_{2}^{i}\},\dots,\{w_{4k-3}^{i},w_{4k-2}^{i}\}\}

for every
i\in[d]
. Then, let

K_{2}=\bigcup_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq d}Q_{i_{1}}*\cdots*Q_{i_{k}}.

(4)

Let

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=\{\{v_{1}^{i},v_{3}^{i},\dots,v_{2k-1}^{i}\},\{v_{2k}^{i},v_{2k+2}^{i},\dots,v_{4k-2}^{i}\}:i\in[d]=[2k-1]\},\textrm{ and}

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle=\{\{w_{1}^{i},w_{3}^{i},\dots,w_{2k-1}^{i}\},\{w_{2k}^{i},w_{2k+2}^{i},\dots,w_{4k-2}^{i}\}:i\in[d]=[2k-1]\}.

That is,
M_{1}
is the collection whose elements are the subset of
V_{i}
which consists of the vertices with the first
k
odd indices and the subset of
V_{i}
which consists of the vertices with the last
k
even indices for every
i\in[d]
.
M_{2}
is defined similarly. Note that
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are
k
-uniform hypergraphs.

(5)

Finally, we define

L_{d}=K_{1}\cup K_{2}\cup M_{1}*M_{2}.

Note that
L_{d}
is a
(d+1)
-uniform hypergraph.

4.2
Non-2-colorability of
L_{d}

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
be odd. Then
L_{d}
is not 2-colorable.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise and we color all vertices of
L_{d}
by RED and BLUE so that no hyperedge is monochromatic. In
K_{1}
, note that not all
2k-1
vertex sets
V_{i}
have consecutive vertices of the same color: Otherwise, without loss of generality, we can assume that RED was used in
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
in consecutive vertices. By definition of
K_{1}
, this gives a hyperedge of
K_{1}
which is monochromatic, a contradiction. So at least one
V_{i}
has an alternating coloring, say
V_{1}
does. The same also holds for
K_{2}
, so at least one
W_{j}
has an alternating coloring, say
W_{1}
does. By the alternating colorings given to
V_{1}
and
W_{1}
, at least one (in fact, there are exactly two) of the 4 hyperedges of
M_{1}*M_{2}
which are subsets of
V_{1}\cup W_{1}
should be monochromatic. ∎

Note that
L_{d}
is 3-colorable: For each
i\in[d]
, we color the vertices of
P_{i}
by RED, BLUE and GREEN alternatingly in order. We do the same for every
Q_{i}
. It is easy to see that each of the hyperedges in
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
, and
M_{1}*M_{2}
has at least two colors from this coloring.

4.3
Embeddability of
L_{d}

In this subsection we prove the following proposition. Recall that
L_{d}
is defined only for odd
d\geq 3
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For every odd
d\geq 3
,
L_{d}
is geometrically embeddable in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The following lemma is useful in the proof (see also
[
10
, Section 6.1]
).

Lemma 4.3

(
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
be the vertices of
C_{d+1}(n)
indexed by their order along the moment curve
\gamma_{d}
.
Let
\hat{0}_{n,d}
be the set of facets in the lower envelope of
C_{d+1}(n)
, namely the facets of the boundary of
C_{d+1}(n)
which are visible from any point on the
x_{d+1}
-axis with sufficiently small
(d+1)
-coordinate. Then,

\displaystyle\hat{0}_{n,d}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\{p_{1}\}*D(d/2,\{p_{2},\dots,p_{n}\})&\text{when $d$ is even,}\\
D((d+1)/2,\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\})&\text{when $d$ is odd,}\end{array}\right.

where

D(k,\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\})=\{\{a_{i_{1}},a_{i_{1}+1},\dots,a_{i_{k}},a_{i_{k}+1}\}:i_{j+1}\geq i_{j}+2\textrm{ for $j\in[k-1]$}\}

for an ordered set
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
. Furthermore,
\hat{0}_{n,d}
gives a triangulation of
C_{d}(n)
.

Figure 2:
Illustration of the geometric embedding of
L_{3}
. A small circle around each end vertex of
s_{i}
and
s_{i}^{\prime}
describes a small neighborhood where the other vertices are introduced. The blue lines indicate a thin tetrahedron in the realization of
M_{1}*M_{2}
. The other notations are introduced in the proof below.

We first overview how we geometrically embed
L_{d}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Recall
d=2k-1
. For
K_{1}
, we begin with the cyclic polytope
C_{d}(2d)
, denoted by
B_{1}
. The vertices of
B_{1}
are exactly the end vertices
v_{1}^{i}
and
v_{4k-2}^{i}
of the paths
P_{i}
for
i\in[d]
, ordered
v_{1}^{1},v_{4k-2}^{1},v_{1}^{2},v_{4k-2}^{2}\ldots,v_{1}^{d},v_{4k-2}^{d}
on
\gamma_{d}
; internal vertices of
P_{i}
are not introduced yet. Denote the line segment
[v_{1}^{i},v_{4k-2}^{i}]
by
s_{i}
.
It follows from Lemma
4.3
that the geometric joins, which equal the convex hulls, of
s_{i_{1}},\dots,s_{i_{k}}
, for all
k
-subsets
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\subseteq[d]=[2k-1]
form a subcollection of the facets in the triangulation
\hat{0}_{2d,d}
of
B_{1}
. Hyperedges of
K_{1}
will eventually appear in a subdivision of this triangulation after a perturbation of the vertices. In this way we will embed
K_{1}
.

Then
B_{2}
is defined as a reflected copy of
B_{1}
w.r.t. some hyperplane
H
. Denote by
s^{\prime}_{i}
the reflected image of
s_{i}
. A crucial assumption is that
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are far enough from each other so that there exist hyperplanes
H_{i}
(resp.
H^{\prime}_{i}
) which weakly separate
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, and
B_{1}\cap H_{i}=s_{i}
(resp.
B_{2}\cap H^{\prime}_{i}=s^{\prime}_{i}
) for every
i\in[d]
.
These hyperplanes will be used to separate hyperedges in
K_{1}
(resp.
K_{2}
) from hyperedges in
M_{1}*M_{2}
.

In
B_{1}
, we subdivide each
s_{i}
to introduce internal vertices of
P_{i}
, and similarly in
B_{2}
. We perturb these points so that hyperedges of
M_{1}*M_{2}
become full-dimensional and we can still use the hyperplane separators we previously constructed. Also, we assume that the first half (resp. last half) of the internal vertices of
P_{i}
were chosen very close to
v_{1}^{i}
(resp. to
v_{4k-2}^{i}
), and we do the same for
B_{2}
. This is to ensure that every two disjoint hyperedges of
M_{1}*M_{2}
do not intersect in the embedding. We give some additional assumptions for the other pairs of hyperedges in
M_{1}*M_{2}
.
Thus, these final positions will provide a geometric embedding of
L_{d}
. We now proceed to the details.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

The construction of the geometric embedding progresses through several steps. For notational simplicity, we use the same notation for the vertices of
L_{d}
and their embedded images in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
even when their locations are modified, for instance, by perturbing them.

Step 1. Constructing the
polytope
B_{1}
.
We first construct the polytope
B_{1}
for
K_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which has the combinatorial type of
C_{d}(2d)
. Let
\gamma_{d}
be the moment curve in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We choose
2d
vertices

v_{1}^{1},v_{4k-2}^{1},v_{1}^{2},v_{4k-2}^{2},\dots,v_{1}^{d},v_{4k-2}^{d}

in that order on
\gamma_{d}
. We denote the set of those vertices by
V^{\prime}
and let
B_{1}=\conv(V^{\prime})
. Let

H_{V^{\prime}}=\left\{\{v_{1}^{i_{1}},v_{4k-2}^{i_{1}},v_{1}^{i_{2}},v_{4k-2}^{i_{2}},\dots,v_{1}^{i_{k}},v_{4k-2}^{i_{k}}\}:1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq d\right\}.

One can easily see that
H_{V^{\prime}}\subseteq D((d+1)/2,V^{\prime})
, hence by Lemma
4.3
,

H_{V^{\prime}}
forms a part of this triangulation of
B_{1}
, and in particular,
H_{V^{\prime}}
is embedded on the moment curve.

Step 2. Making a reflected copy
B_{2}
of
B_{1}
and separating hyperplanes
H_{i}
and
H_{i}^{\prime}
.

Now, we want to construct the polytope
B_{2}
similarly for
K_{2}
and suitably locate
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
. Recall that we denote the segment
[v_{1}^{i},v_{4k-2}^{i}]
by
s_{i}
for every
i\in[d]
.

By Lemma
4.3

every
s_{i}
belongs to the lower envelope of
B_{1}
, that is,
s_{i}
is visible from the point

p_{i}=(0,\dots,0,z_{i})
for every negative
z_{i}
with large enough absolute value
|z_{i}|
.
For each
i\in[d]
we choose a facet

in the lower envelope of

which contains
s_{i}
. Then, by slightly rotating
\aff(f_{i})
while containing

, we obtain a supporting hyperplane

of

at

(so

) which satisfies the extra property that it separates
B_{1}
from
p_{i}
.

Let
B_{2}
be a reflected copy of
B_{1}
with respect to the hyperplane

P_{z}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{d-1},z):x_{1},\dots,x_{d-1}\in\mathbb{R}\}

for a fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}
. We denote the vertices of
B_{2}
with the same notation
of
K_{2}
. That is, the vertex set of
B_{2}
is

W^{\prime}=\{w_{1}^{1},w_{4k-2}^{1},w_{1}^{2},w_{4k-2}^{2},\dots,w_{1}^{d},w_{4k-2}^{d}\},

where each
w^{j}_{l}
is the reflection of
v^{j}_{l}
w.r.t.
P_{z}
.
Let
s_{i}^{\prime}=\conv(\{w_{1}^{i},w_{4k-2}^{i}\})
and

H_{W^{\prime}}=\left\{\{w_{1}^{i_{1}},w_{4k-2}^{i_{1}},w_{1}^{i_{2}},w_{4k-2}^{i_{2}},\dots,w_{1}^{i_{k}},w_{4k-2}^{i_{k}}\}:1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq d\right\}.

We denote the supporting hyperplane for
s_{i}^{\prime}
by
H_{i}^{\prime}
, which are chosen as the reflection of
H_{i}
by
P_{z}
. We assume that
z
is chosen to be negative with sufficiently large
|z|
so that every
H_{i}
separates
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
such that
B_{2}
is in an open halfsapce of
H_{i}
for every

. By symmetry,
H_{i}^{\prime}
also separtates
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
such that
B_{1}
is in an open halfspace of

for every
i\in[d]
.

Step 3. Introducing remaining vertices of
L_{d}
in the embedding via general position assumptions.

We subdivide each
s_{i}
by introducing
4k-4
new vertices
v_{1}^{i},v_{2}^{i},\dots,v_{4k-2}^{i}
in order.
We also subdivide
s_{i}^{\prime}
using
w_{1}^{i},\dots,w_{4k-2}^{i}
in order. This subdivision of line segments induces a subdivision of the simplices

s_{i_{1}}*\cdots*s_{i_{k}},\textrm{ and }s_{i_{1}}^{\prime}*\cdots*s_{i_{k}}^{\prime}

where
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq d
, which are exactly the simplices in the geometric realization of

of Step 1. Therefore, this gives a geometric realization of
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
. Denote the
\epsilon
-neighborhood of

by

. On

, we first locate the vertices
v_{2}^{i},v_{3}^{i},\dots,v_{2k-1}^{i}
in

and locate

in

, where

is chosen such that the neighborhoods

, over all vertices of

, are pairwise disjoint, and further, for every four distinct vertices

, the joins

are disjoint. (These joins are “thin thickening” of the segments

and

resp.)
This is possible after we slightly perturb the vertices of

so that they are in general position.

We also perturb the hyperplanes

and

for each

as well as the vertices of

if necessary to fix

-dimensional flats

and

such that

and

and

(resp.

) and every

(resp.

),

(resp.

) is in general position. By this, we can require

to be sufficiently small so that for every

(resp.
F_{j}^{\prime}
) and

(resp.

), there is a hyperplane which contains

(resp.

) and strictly separates

from

(resp.

). Clearly this is possible.

Now, for every

, we perturb the new vertices

within

and perturb

within

as follows:
For the odd-indexed vertices

, we additionally require that they are only perturbed within the flat

and are in general position, which implies that the resulting vertices affinely span

. We require the same for the even-indexed vertices

. Note that the sets

and

, for all

, are exactly the hyperedges of

. The other vertices on

are perturbed so that they are contained in

, the open halfspace which contains the interior of

. This perturbation should be small enough so that it also gives a geometric embedding of

. The vertices

are perturbed to satisfy the same properties w.r.t. the flats

and halfspaces

.
Finally, we add the simplices of

. This completes the construction.

Now we show that our construction gives an embedding. Condition (
1
) is easily satisfied: Hyperedges of
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are
in general position;
hyperedges of

are also in general position
by the skewedness of each pair
F_{i}
and
F^{\prime}_{j}
and as hyperedges in
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are
spanning their corresponding flats.

We now show that Condition (
2
) is satisfied. Let
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
be two distinct hyperedges of
L_{d}
. There are three cases to consider: (i)
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are from

. When they are from the same

we already showed that our construction gives an embedding of
K_{i}
. Else, without loss of generality

for

. Since

and
K_{2}
are separated by a hyperplane then the realizations of
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
do not intersect.

(ii)
e_{1}\in K_{i}
and
e_{2}\in M_{1}*M_{2}
; without loss of generality
i=1
. Then,
e_{2}=f_{1}\cup f_{2}
where
f_{i}\in M_{i}
for

. From the construction, we can find

which contains

and the end vertex

of the segment

such that

is contained in

.
Note that
f_{2}\subseteq H_{i}^{-}
, hence

is realized in the closed halfspace

.
On the other hand,

is realized in the closed halfspace

.
Thus,

\displaystyle\conv(e_{1})\cap\conv(e_{2})=(\conv(e_{1})\cap H_{i})\cap(\conv(e_{2})\cap H_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\conv(e_{1}\cap H_{i})\cap\conv(e_{2}\cap H_{i}).

But

consists of at most two vertices, consecutive on the path subdividing

, so at most one of them belong to

. It follows that
\conv(e_{1})\cap\conv(e_{2})=\conv(e_{1}\cap e_{2})
, which is either empty or equals a single vertex.

(iii) Finally,
e_{1},e_{2}\in M_{1}*M_{2}
.
There exist unique partitions
e_{1}=f_{1}^{1}\cup f_{2}^{1}
and
e_{2}=f_{1}^{2}\cup f_{2}^{2}
, where
f_{1}^{1},f_{1}^{2}\in M_{1}
and
f_{2}^{1},f_{2}^{2}\in M_{2}
.
If these four sets
f_{j}^{i}
are distinct, then each is contained in
N_{\epsilon}(v)
for a distinct vertex
v\in L_{d}
, and as the joins
N_{\epsilon}(u)*N_{\epsilon}(v)
and
N_{\epsilon}(w)*N_{\epsilon}(x)
are disjoint for every distinct vertices

we conclude that
\conv(e_{1})\cap\conv(e_{2})
is empty.

Else, without loss of generality,
f_{1}^{1}=f_{1}^{2}
; we find the flat
F_{i}
which contains
f_{1}^{1}
, and the vertices of

corresponding to

and
f_{2}^{2}
, say
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
.
Now, there exists a hyperplane
H^{\prime\prime}
containing

which separates

from

, hence
H^{\prime\prime}
separates

from
f_{2}^{2}
. If follows that
the intersection
\conv(e_{1})\cap\conv(e_{2})
is contained in

and equals
\conv(f_{1}^{1})=\conv(e_{1}\cap e_{2})
.

This completes the proof.
∎

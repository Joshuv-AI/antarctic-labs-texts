On Fatou sets containing Baker omitted value
Subhasis Ghora, Tarakanta Nayak, Satyajit Sahoo

Abstract
An omitted value of a transcendental meromorphic function f f is called a Baker omitted value, in short bov if there is a disk D D centered at the bov such that each component of the boundary of f − 1 ​ ( D ) f^{-1}(D) is bounded. Assuming that the bov is in the Fatou set of f f , this article investigates the dynamics of the function. Firstly, the connectivity of all the Fatou components are determined. If U U is the Fatou component containing the bov then it is proved that a Fatou component U ′ U^{\prime} is infinitely connected if and only if it lands on U U , i.e. f k ​ ( U ′ ) ⊂ U f^{k}(U^{\prime})\subset U for some k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . Every other Fatou component is either simply connected or lands on a Herman ring. Further, assuming that the number of critical points in the Fatou set whose forward orbits do not intersect U U is finite, we have shown that the connectivity of each Fatou component belongs to a finite set. This set is independent of the Fatou components. It is proved that the Fatou component containing the bov is completely invariant whenever it is forward invariant. Further, if the invariant Fatou component is an attracting domain and compactly contains all the critical values of the function then the Julia set is totally disconnected. Baker domains are shown to be non-existent whenever the bov is in the Fatou set. It is also proved that, if there is a 2 2 -periodic Baker domain (these are not ruled out when the bov is in the Julia set), or a 2 2 -periodic attracting or parabolic domain containing the bov then the function has no Herman ring. Some examples exhibiting different possibilities for the Fatou set are discussed. This includes the first example of a meromorphic function with an omitted value which has two infinitely connected Fatou components.



Keywords:

Baker omitted value, Fatou set, Herman rings and Transcendental meromorphic functions.

A critical value is the image of a critical point, that is,

The next two results deal with Baker domains and Herman rings.

In every cycle of Baker domains, there is always a Baker domain whose boundary contains an asymptotic value of the function (Theorem 13,
[

]

Theorem 1.4
.

Let a meromorphic function have a bov. Then it has no invariant Baker domain. Further, if the bov is stable then the function has no Baker domain of any period.

Invariant Baker domains are ruled out by Theorem

Theorem 1.5

Let

a Baker domain, or

Remark 2.1

In the proof of the previous theorem, the disk

An asymptotic value of a meromorphic function with finite order is either a limit point of its critical values or all the singularities lying over that asymptotic value are logarithmic (Corollary 1,
[

Lemma 2.5
.

If a meromorphic function has a bov then the bov is a limit point of its critical values.

Proof.

Let
b
be the bov of a meromorphic function

.
Suppose on the contrary that there is a neighbourhood

which does not contain any critical value of

. Since

is the only asymptotic value of

(by Lemma

is a covering. The fundamental group,

of a Riemann surface

is the group of all loops with the same starting and ending point

such that none of these is homotopically equivalent to the other.
Note that

is connected in this case. It is well-known that

is a subgroup of
\pi_{1}(N\backslash\{b\})
. Since
\pi_{1}(N\backslash\{b\})
is isomorphic to the additive group

|c(z_{n})^{d}|<k|x_{n}|^{d}

and

. In view of Case(1) above, we assume that

and
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=\infty
. Then
e^{x_{n}}-k(x_{n})^{d}
, the right hand side of this inequation goes to
\infty
as

. This is obvious as

for sufficiently large positive

is unbounded.

•

Case-3
: If
\gamma

\gamma_{\beta}
or
\overline{\gamma_{\beta}}
contains an unbounded curve, or

(also

as

does not satisfy the condition of Case (2). In the first two cases, the set

or

contains an unbounded interval whereas

takes all possible real values in the third case.

Either
\gamma_{\beta}
or
\overline{\gamma_{\beta}}
is unbounded. Assume that
\gamma_{\beta}
is unbounded. If
\overline{\gamma_{\beta}}
is unbounded the proof goes exactly in the same way. The image of
\gamma_{\beta}
under

(not

d|\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta|

. The points

is on the ray emanating from the origin, containing

and lying in

. Both the sequences

\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{z_{n}}=\infty
(as the condition of Case(1) is not satisfied). The number

Remark 2.3
.

The point at
\infty
is the bov for
f(z)=cz^{d}+e^{z}
and consequently,

is the bov of
\frac{1}{f(z)}
.
The critical points of
\frac{1}{f(z)}
are the roots of

Proofs of results

This section proves all the results of this article.

3.1
Topology of Fatou components

Recall that for a Fatou component
V
of
f
,
V_{n}
denotes the Fatou component containing
f^{n}(V)
. We say a Fatou component
V^{\prime}
lands on a Fatou component
V
if
V^{\prime}_{k}=V
for some
k\geq 1
. The following is a restatement of Theorem 3 proved in
[
12
]
. The omitted value mentioned in this lemma is not necessarily a bov.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
be a meromorphic function with at least one omitted value and such that all omitted values be contained in a Fatou component
U
of
f
. For each Fatou component
V
with
V_{n}\neq U
for any
n\geq 0
the following are true.

1.

If
U
is unbounded, then
c(V_{n})=1
for all
n\geq 0
.

2.

If
U
is bounded, then
c(V)=1
or
V
lands on a Herman ring.

3.

If
U
is wandering, then
c(U_{n})=1
for all
n\geq 0
.

4.

Let
U
be pre-periodic but not periodic.
If
U
is unbounded, then
c(U_{n})=1
for all
n\geq 0
. If
U
is bounded,
then
c(U)=1
or
U
lands on a Herman ring.

5.

If
U
is periodic, then
c(U_{n})
is either
1
or
\infty
for all
n\geq 0
.

Though the above result is silent on the connectivity of the Fatou components landing on
U
, it is the main ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
which determines the connectivity of every Fatou component whenever the function has a stable bov. We now present a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

1.

If
V\notin\mathcal{O}(U)
then
V_{n}\neq U
for any
n
and it follows from Lemma
3.1
(1-2) that
V
lands on a Herman ring whenever it is multiply connected. Since for each
V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(V)
,
V^{\prime}
is bounded, there is a
k
such that
V^{\prime}_{k}
is a Herman ring and
f^{k}:V^{\prime}\to V^{\prime}_{k}
is proper by Lemma
2.2
. Further,
c(V^{\prime})=2+N
by Lemma
2.1
, where
N
is the number of critical points of
f^{k}
in
V^{\prime}
counting multiplicity. In other words,
1<c(V^{\prime})<\infty
.

2.

Let
U
be periodic and
U^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(U)
. Then, the preimage
U_{-1}
of
U
is periodic and there is a
k\geq 0
such that
f^{k}(U^{\prime})\subseteq U_{-1}
. The map
f^{k}:U^{\prime}\to U_{-1}
is either proper or is an infinite-to-one map. Since
U_{-1}
is infinitely connected,
U^{\prime}
is infinitely connected by Lemma
2.1
in both the cases.

3.

If
U
is pre-periodic then each Fatou component landing on
U
is infinitely connected by Lemma
2.4
(2).

If a Fatou component
U^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{O}(U)
not landing on
U
, is multiply connected then it lands on a Herman ring
H
by Lemma
3.1
(2).
The map
f^{k}:U^{\prime}\to H
is proper for some
k
. Now it follows from Lemma
2.1
that
1<c(U^{\prime})<\infty
.

4.

If
U
is wandering then every Fatou component landing on
U
is infinitely connected by Lemma
2.4
(2). Let
U^{\prime}
be a Fatou component in
\mathcal{O}(U)
not landing on
U
. If

U^{\prime}
is multiply connected then it lands on a Herman ring by Lemma
3.1
(2), giving that
U^{\prime}
is periodic or pre-periodic, which is not true. Thus
U^{\prime}
is simply connected for all
U^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(U)
.

∎

The proof of Theorem
1.2
follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Recall that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{F}(f)\cap\{c:f^{\prime}(c)=0~\mbox{and}~f^{n}(c)\notin U~\mbox{for any}~n\}
. This set is finite, by assumption. Let
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\cdots c_{l}\}
and
d=\prod_{i=1}^{l}(d_{i}-1)
where
d_{i}
is the local degree of
f
at
c_{i}
. The desired finite set of connectivities is going to be
\mathcal{N}=\{1,2,3,\cdots,2+d\}
.

1.

Let
V\notin\mathcal{O}(U)
. If
V
is multiply connected then all the Fatou components in
\mathcal{O}(V)
are multiply connected, by Theorem
1.1
. Further, each Fatou component in
\mathcal{O}(V)
lands on a Herman ring. Clearly, this Herman ring is the same, say
H
for all Fatou components in
\mathcal{O}(V)
. Let
V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(V)
. Then
V^{\prime}_{k}=H
for some
k
. Since
H
and its forward iterated images cannot contain any critical point,
V^{\prime}_{n}
intersects
\mathcal{C}
for at most finitely many values of
n
and a single
V^{\prime}_{n}
cannot contain any critical point more than once. In other words,
f^{k}:V^{\prime}\to H
is a proper map with degree at most
d
and, by Lemma
2.1
,
c(V^{\prime})=2+j
where
j\leq d
. In other words,
c(V^{\prime})\in\{2,3,4,\cdots,2+d\}
.

If
V
is simply connected then
V^{\prime}
is simply connected for all
V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(V)
.

Thus
c(V^{\prime})\in\mathcal{N}
for all
V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(V)
.

2.

Let
U^{\prime}
be a Fatou component landing on
U_{k}
for some
k\geq 1
. Then it follows from Theorem
1.1
(3) that either
c(U^{\prime})=1
or
U^{\prime}
lands on a Herman ring. Following the same argument as in (1), it is concluded that
c(U^{\prime})\in\mathcal{N}
.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(U)
be the set of all elements of
\mathcal{O}(U)
which do not land on
U
. Then it follows from Theorem
1.1
(3) that if one Fatou component in

is multiply connected then so are all others.

For proving Theorem
1.3
, we need the following lemma, which is slightly more general than what is required.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
U
be an invariant Fatou component of a meromorphic function
f
. If
f^{-1}(D)
is connected, for some disk
D\subset U
then
U
is completely invariant. In particular, every invariant Fatou component
U
is completely invariant if it contains an omitted value over which there is only one singularity.

Proof.

Since
f^{-1}(D)
is connected,
f^{-1}(U)
is connected by Theorem 1 of
[
10
]
. The forward invariance of
U
gives that
U\cap f^{-1}(U)
is non-empty. As
f^{-1}(U)
is contained in a Fatou component,
f^{-1}(U)\subset U
. In other words,
U
is backward invariant. Therefore
U
is completely invariant.

If
U
contains an omitted value over which there is only one singularity then
f^{-1}(D)
is connected, for each sufficiently small ball
D
contained in
U
and centered at the omitted value. Therefore, every invariant Fatou component
U
is completely invariant if it contains an omitted value over which there is only one singularity.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
f
be a meromorphic function with a stable bov.
As evident from Lemma
2.3
, there is only one singularity of
f^{-1}
lying over a bov.
This along with Lemma
3.2
proves that the invariant Fatou component
U
containing the bov is completely invariant.

Consider the closure
K
of a simply connected subdomain of
U
such that
K
contains the attracting fixed point, the bov and all the singular values of
f
.
Let
D
be the closure of
\bigcup_{n\geq 0}f^{n}(K)
. Then
D
is a forward invariant closed and connected subset of
U
.
Now each Julia component other than the one containing
\infty
is contained in a component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus f^{-1}(D)
(See Remark
2.1
).
Let
J
be a Julia component of
f
and
z\in J
. If
\lim_{k\to\infty}f^{n_{k}}(z)=z^{*}\in\mathbb{C}
for some subsequence
n_{k}
and the Julia component containing

is
J^{*}
then
J^{*}\subset V
for some component
V
of
\mathbb{C}\setminus f^{-1}(D)
. Since
f^{-1}(D)
is connected,
V
is simply connected. Further, there is a
k_{0}
such that
f^{n_{k}}(J)\subset V
for all
k>k_{0}
. Since all the singular values and their forward orbits are in
D
,
V
does not intersect the forward orbit of any singular value, and hence each branch
f_{k}
of
f^{-n_{k}}
is a well-defined analytic map in
V
for every
k>k_{0}
. Note that each
f_{k}(V)
contains
J
. It is known that
\{f_{k}\}_{k>0}
is normal on
V
(for example, see Proposition 2
[
16
]
).
Let
f^{*}
be a limit function of a convergent subsequence of
\{f_{k}\}_{k>0}
on
V
. Then
f^{*}
is analytic by the Weirstrass Theorem. If
f^{*}
is non-constant then it is observed that
f^{*}(V)
is an open set intersecting
J
. Now a disk in
f^{*}(V)
can be found which intersects the Julia set. The sequence
\{f^{n_{k}}\}_{k>0}
is normal in this disk. However this cannot be true proving that
f^{*}
is constant. Since

is contained in the closure of
f^{*}(V)
,

is singleton. Using a conformal conjugate of

, it can be shown that
J
is singleton even if, for a

and a subsequence

,
\lim_{k\to\infty}f^{n_{k}}(z)=\infty
. Here, it is important to note that all the poles are simple and

is not a critical value, by the hypothesis of this theorem.
Thus, the Julia set of

is totally disconnected.

∎

Baker domains and Herman rings

We now provide the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

First we prove that if the bov is stable then there is no Baker domain of any period.
Let
U
be the Fatou component containing the bov
b
. Let

be a Baker domain of

for

. Then there exists

and

is unbounded. Without loss of generality assume that

. But it follows from Lemma
2.4
(2)
that the set

is the unique unbounded Fatou component of

and so

. Take

. Then

. For a simple curve
\gamma
in

joining

and

and containing these points,

is a curve in

joining

and

. Let
\gamma_{0}=\gamma\cup f^{p}(\gamma)\cup f^{2p}(\gamma)\dots,
and
\gamma_{i}=f^{i}(\gamma_{0})
for

.
We claim that

as

along
\gamma_{0}
. The claim will be proved by showing that
\lim_{k\to\infty}f^{p}(w_{k})=\infty

is a sequence on
\gamma_{0}
converging to
\infty
. Let
\Omega
be a ball around
\infty
with respect to the spherical metric. Since
\gamma
is a compact subset of

and

\gamma

such that

(1)

Each iterated image of
\gamma

is bounded and that is why contains at most finitely many

such that

for any

.

It follows from the previous paragraph that,

\mbox{ each}~B_{i}~\mbox{ contains}~\gamma_{i},f^{p}(\gamma_{i})\subset\gamma_{i}~\mbox{and}~f^{p}(z)\rightarrow l_{i}~\mbox{as}~z\rightarrow l_{i}~\mbox{along}~\gamma_{i}.

∎

For proving Theorem

Let

.

.

5
Concluding Remarks

We conclude with few remarks leading to some directions of research.

Lemma
2.5
proves that the bov is always a limit point of the critical values of the function. A limit point of critical values different from the bov seems to be impossible. Though all the examples discussed in this article confirm this, it remains to be proved.

If the bov is stable then the function has a unique unbounded Fatou component (Lemma
2.4
). It is yet to be known whether a function has an unbounded Fatou component when bov is unstable.

In presence of a stable bov, the Julia components not intersecting the backward orbit of
\infty
are all bounded and are mapped onto each other. Their topology can be investigated. Further, assuming that the bov is the only limit point of critical values, an upper bound for the number of periodic cycles of Fatou components may exist. In the absence of recurrent critical points, these issues seem to be tractable.

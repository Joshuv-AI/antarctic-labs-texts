A varying gravitational constant map in asymptotically AdS black hole thermodynamics Journal: Physics Letters B
Iarley P. Lobo, João Paulo Morais Graça, Eduardo Folco Capossoli, Henrique Boschi-Filho

Abstract
We propose a sequence of steps and a generic transformation for connecting common thermodynamic quantities considered in asymptotically anti-de Sitter black hole thermodynamics in the bulk and those that are appropriate for CFT thermodynamics in the boundary. We do this by constructing a “varying- G G map”, where G G is the gravitational constant, and demonstrate its usefulness by considering various examples.



1
Introduction

In 1997, Maldacena proposed a duality between string theories in an anti-de Sitter (AdS) spacetime (the bulk) and conformal field theories at its boundary
[
1
]
, which could give insights, for instance, about a theory of quantum gravity
[
2
]
. Based on this idea, Witten related phase transitions in the bulk with the confinement/deconfinement transition of the gauge fields at the boundary
[
3
]
. The phase transitions considered by Witten are the so-called Hawking-Page phase transitions, originally the transition between a black hole in an anti-de Sitter space and pure radiation in this same space
[
4
]
. In thermodynamics, we say this is a first-order transition.

Witten’s analysis is based on traditional black hole thermodynamics, where the black hole’s mass, for example, corresponds to its internal energy. More recently, however, Kastor, Ray and Traschen have proposed a modification to this framework, equating the black hole’s mass not with its internal energy, but with its enthalpy
[
5
]
. With that, they were able to introduce a notion of a mechanical pressure for the black hole, as well as a thermodynamic volume.

In this new framework, Kubiznak and Mann showed that anti-de Sitter charged black holes exhibit other types of phase transitions, such as second-order phase transitions, similar to the phase transitions of usual matter, which often can be described by equations of state such as that of Van der Waals
[
6
]
. Because the black hole’s mass is equaled to its enthalpy, such a framework was called black hole chemistry, to differentiate it from the usual thermodynamics of black holes (for a review on the subject, see
[
7
]
). This new framework, however, requires extensions to the dictionary proposed by Witten. The reason for this is multiple. For instance, the mechanical pressure of the black hole (the pressure in the bulk) is not the same as the boundary pressure
[
8
]
. But what does this pressure mean then? And how to calculate the boundary pressure from the bulk pressure?

Another key point is the relationship between the size of the anti-de Sitter space and the number of colors,
N
, of the dual Yang-Mills (Y-M) theory or degrees of freedom of the Conformal Field Theory (CFT) at the boundary. According to the usual dictionary, the latter is measured by the central charge,
C
, as
N^{2}\propto C\propto L^{D-2}/G
, where
L
is the anti-de Sitter radius,
G
is the gravitational constant in
D
bulk dimensions
[
9
,
10
]
. This means that, for a fixed
G
, a change in the size of the anti-de Sitter space leads to a variation in the number of colors, i.e., we modify the Y-M theory at the boundary or takes us from a CFT to another.

On the other hand, the radius of the AdS space is related to the volume of the CFT as
{\cal V}=L^{D-2}
. Therefore if one aims to vary, in an independent way, both the volume and the central charge (or degrees of freedom) of the CFT, it becomes necessary to allow the gravitational constant to vary. Or if one aims to consider modifications of the CFT volume, but keeping the same theory, it becomes necessary to also vary simultaneously the gravitational constant and the AdS radius in order to keep the central charge unmodified (for further details, please refer to
[
9
]
and references therein). It should be emphasized that had one not considered a variation of the AdS radius (or equivalently the cosmological constant), Witten’s dictionary would be perfectly fine, and the problematic behavior mentioned above emerges in the context of the extended black hole thermodynamics, that introduces a mechanical pressure due to a variation of the cosmological constant.

The compatibility between the black hole thermodynamics valid in the bulk and the CFT at the boundary requires that a dictionary is constructed in a way that relates the “hair” parameters that define the black hole and the CFT thermodynamic quantities. Usually, this is done by identifying a map between the boundary-defined quantities and the bulk black hole ones in a way that preserves the first law and Smarr relations in both representations. Recently, such an approach has been carried out for cases with rotation
[
9
,
10
]
, electric charge with different types of electrodynamics (reflected in the black hole solutions considered)
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
, and also for string theory corrections modeled by higher derivative terms
[
14
]
in a way that present, as a common point, the need for the aforementioned redefinition of variables for each case under scrutiny.

In this paper, we construct a general algorithm for identifying the necessary transformation between generic thermodynamic quantities that are considered in standard black hole thermodynamics in the bulk and those needed for making this thermodynamics compatible with the one carried out in the boundary CFT. The dictionary itself is written in definition
2.1
and the proof of theorem
2.1
(that states the compatibility between the black hole thermodynamics in the bulk and the one in the boundary) is done throughout section
2
. Using this algorithm (that is explicitly written in section
3
), we show its application by recovering various known results in the literature and we propose how it should be applied in a novel scenario. Finally, we draw our final remarks in section
4
.

Varying-
G

The starting point of this derivation consists in identifying how the gravitational constant
G
scales in
D

[G]=l^{D-2}

\tau_{i}

(no sum over indices assumed here).
The motivation for this definition stems from usual couplings of
\alpha_{i}
powers of
G
and
\tau_{i}
in the metric functions of black holes, which will serve as the starting point of the holographic map that we shall propose.

Definition 2.1
(Varying-
G
map)

Let
M
,
A
,
\kappa
and
L
be the black hole’s mass, area, surface gravity and anti-de Sitter radius in
D
spacetime dimensions. Consider a set of
n
thermodynamic quantities, called charges,
\tau_{i}
(where
n=1,...,n
), that scale with length dimensions
l^{\beta_{i}}
and that couple to the
\alpha_{i}
-th power of the gravitational constant (which presents length dimension
l^{D-2}
) as
\tau_{i}G^{\alpha_{i}}
, where
l
is fiducial length scale. The varying-
G
map consists in the transformation from the set of thermodynamic variables
(M,A,\kappa,L,\tau_{i},A_{i},G)
to the set
(E,S,T,{\cal V},p,\nu_{i},B_{i},C,\mu)
as follows

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=M\,,\quad S=\frac{A}{4G}\,,\quad T=\kappa/2\pi\,,

(11)

\displaystyle{\cal V}

\displaystyle=L^{D-2}\,,\quad p=\frac{E}{{(D-2)\cal V}}\,,

(12)

\displaystyle\nu_{i}

\displaystyle=\tau_{i}G^{\alpha_{i}-1}L^{(D-2)(1-\alpha_{i})-\beta_{i}}\,,

(13)

\displaystyle B_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{i}}{L^{(D-2)(1-\alpha_{i})-\beta_{i}}}\,

(14)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{16\pi}\frac{L^{D-2}}{G}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C}(E-TS-\sum_{i}B_{i}\nu_{i})\,,

(16)

where
k
is an arbitrary constant that labels different holographic systems.
The set
(E,S,T,{\cal V},p,C,\mu)
corresponds to variables known as internal energy, entropy, temperature, volume, pressure, central charge and chemical potential, respectively. The quantities
(\nu_{i},B_{i})
are the redefined charges and potentials.

Let

,

,
\kappa
,
\Lambda=-(D-1)(D-2)/(2L^{2})

\tau_{i}
(where

) that scale with length dimensions

and that couple to the
\alpha_{i}
-th power of the gravitational constant as

. If

is a variable gravitational constant (that presents length dimension

), such that the black hole’s first law and Smarr relation read

\displaystyle d(GM)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}dA+\frac{\Theta}{8\pi}d\Lambda+\sum_{i}A_{i}d(G^{\alpha_{i}}\tau_{i})\,,

(17)

\displaystyle(D-3)M

\displaystyle=(D-2)\frac{\kappa A}{8\pi G}-\frac{\Theta\Lambda}{4\pi G}+\sum_{i}[(D-2)\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}]G^{\alpha_{i}-1}\tau_{i}A_{i}\,,

(18)

then the varying-
G

\displaystyle dE

\displaystyle=TdS+\sum_{i}B_{i}d\nu_{i}-pd{\cal V}+\mu dC\,,

\displaystyle=TS+\sum_{i}B_{i}\nu_{i}+\mu C\,.

The substitution of Eqs.(

This way, from the holographic first law, one sees that the quantity

3
General algorithm and examples

A general algorithm for applying the varying-
G
map would be the following:

1.

Identify the relevant charges,
\tau_{i}
, in which the standard black hole thermodynamics would be formulated.

2.

Explicitly write
G
in the equations of the gravitational system (for example, in the metric), in order to identify the power,
\alpha_{i}
, in which it couples to
\tau_{i}
for finding the quantity
\eta_{i}=G^{\alpha_{i}}\tau_{i}
.

3.

Discover the power length dimensions,
\beta_{i}
, of
\tau_{i}
as
[\tau_{i}]=l^{\beta_{i}}
.

4.

Express the first law of black hole thermodynamics through variations of
GM
,
A
,
\Lambda
and
G^{\alpha_{i}}\tau_{i}
as

\displaystyle d(GM)=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}dA+\frac{\Theta}{8\pi}d\Lambda+\sum_{i}A_{i}d(G^{\alpha_{i}}\tau_{i})\,.

(21)

5.

From the expected form of the Smarr relation in standard black hole thermodynamics

(D-3)M=(D-2)\frac{\kappa A}{8\pi G}-\frac{\Theta\Lambda}{4\pi G}+\sum_{i}[(D-2)\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}]G^{\alpha_{i}-1}\tau_{i}A_{i}\,.

(22)

one can find the form of the potentials
A_{i}
as given by the product of
\gamma_{i}
-powers of
G
and the usual potentials
\Phi_{i}
that are conjugate to
\tau_{i}
in standard black hole thermodynamics (the one in which
G
was held fixed)

(D-3)M=(D-2)\frac{\kappa A}{8\pi G}-\frac{\Theta\Lambda}{4\pi G}+\sum_{i}[(D-2)\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}]G^{\gamma_{i}}\tau_{i}\Phi_{i}\,,

(23)

where
A_{i}=G^{\gamma_{i}-\alpha_{i}+1}\Phi_{i}
.

6.

Endowed with these quantities, one uses the varying-
G
map of
2.1
, in order to find the transformation to the holographic first law and Smarr relation given by Eqs.(
19
) and (
20
).

We shall employ this algorithm to the following examples.

3.5
Casimir energy and Kerr-AdS
5
black hole

Now, we analyze the specific role that Casimir energy plays in this context. We have been analyzing the case in which the energy considered in the first law is given by the one of a ground state, i.e., the vacuum energy of the CFT was effectively considered as null. However, there exists an energy for the ground state, or Casimir energy, for CFTs on a curved background, as argued in the Appendix E of Ref.
[
9
]

[
9
]

\Theta_{\text{ren}}=-\frac{\Omega_{D-2}}{D-1}\left(r_{+}^{D-1}-\frac{D-3}{D-2}L^{D-1}M_{\text{Cas}}\right)\,.

(50)

And the effect in the holographic approach described in this paper consists in a renormalization of the chemical potential
\mu
due to the renormalized Smarr relation as

E_{\text{ren}}=TS+\mu_{\text{ren}}C+\sum_{i}B_{i}\nu_{i}\,,

(51)

and of the holographic pressure
p
according to (
12

p_{\text{ren}}=\frac{E_{\text{ren}}}{(D-2){\cal V}}\,.

(52)

As an example of this subject, we consider the case of the Kerr-AdS
5
black hole, which as has been shown, for instance in
[
19
]
, to present such renormalized energy, with a first law given by Eqs.(6.55) of
[

dM_{\text{ren}}=TdS+\Omega_{a}dJ_{a}+\Omega_{b}dJ_{b}+VdP\,,

Since the angular momenta continue to have length dimensions

4
Concluding remarks

We defined a general sequence of steps with which one can translate quantities that are usually considered in standard asymptotically AdS black hole extended phase space thermodynamics, i.e., the one in which the cosmological constant is allowed to vary, to those that are considered in CFTs in the boundary of the AdS space. Such a construction aims to serve as a guide for future analyses carried out in different scenarios on the recently considered proposal, for instance described in
[
10
,
9
,
11
]
, to make compatible the black hole thermodynamics in AdS space and the thermodynamics defined in its boundary in light of the holographic principle. Our goal is that the algorithm proposed in this paper can be applied for a more general class of asymptotically anti-de Sitter black holes, thus working as a shortcut when one aims to investigate this subject in different scenarios.

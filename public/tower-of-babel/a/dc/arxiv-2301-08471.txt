A geometric characterization of range-kernel complementarity
Dimosthenis Drivaliaris, Nikos Yannakakis

Abstract
. We show that a bounded linear operator on a Banach space with closed range has range-kernel complementarity if and only if its generalized amplitude is less than π \pi . An application to the strong convergence of the iterations of a bounded linear operator is also given.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 47A05 Secondary 47A10, 46C50

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and
A:X\rightarrow X
be a bounded linear operator on
X
. If we denote by
R(A)
and
N(A)
the range and the kernel of
A
respectively and
R(A)
is closed, then range-kernel complementarity is the decomposition

(1.1)

X=R(A)\oplus N(A)\,.

In a sense, range-kernel complementarity is the closest thing to
A
being invertible, since it is equivalent to
A
being of the form “invertible
\,\oplus\,0
”. Our aim in this short paper is its characterization by a simple geometric condition.

Our approach combines two ingredients. The first is that (
1.1
) is possible if and only if
A
can be factorized as
TP
with
T
invertible and
P
a projection, see
[
11
, Theorem 3]
. The second is that (
1.1
) follows from a suitable angle of
A
being less than
\pi
, see
[
3
, Theorem 3.4]
.

The idea behind the possible connection between range-kernel complementarity and some geometric condition on
A
originates from the following question. If an angle condition is imposed on
A
(for example, the well-known
Re\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq c\|x\|^{2}
of the Lax-Milgram Theorem which implies that
A
is invertible) how far from invertible can
A
be? The answer is interesting and surprising;
A
cannot be too bad as a quite natural angle condition implies (
1.1
).

We conclude this paper with an application of our results to the problem of strong convergence of the iterates
T^{n}
of an asymptotically regular operator
T
, for which
R(I-T)
is closed.

For other characterizations of range-kernel complementarity we refer the interested reader to
[
11
]
and the references therein.

2.
Main result

In order to formulate our geometric condition we first define the angle of
A
with respect to a certain class of linear operators.
This class
\mathcal{E}_{A}
for
A
is defined as follows

\mathcal{E}_{A}=\left\{T\in B(X):T(N(A))=N(A)\text{ and }A+tT\text{ is invertible for some }t>0\right\}\,.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that in the terminology of
[
6
, p. 49]
if
T\in\mathcal{E}_{A}
, then
A+tT,\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{C}
, is a regular linear pencil.

If
[\cdot,\cdot]
is a semi-inner product in
X
compatible with its norm, then the cosine of
A
with respect to
T\in\mathcal{E}_{A}
is defined as

\cos_{T}(A)=\inf\left\{\frac{Re[Ax,Tx]}{\|Ax\|\,\|Tx\|}:x\notin N(A)\cup N(T)\right\}

and the angle of
A
with respect to
T
is defined as

\varphi_{T}(A)=\arccos\left(\cos_{T}(A)\right)\,.

For
T=e^{i\theta}I\,,\,\,\,\theta\in[0,2\pi]
, the above is nothing more than the angle of
A
along the ray

\rho_{\theta}=\left\{0\right\}\cup\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\arg z=\theta\right\}

which was defined by Gustafson and Krein in
[
7
]
and
[
10
]
respectively. For each
\theta
this angle
\varphi_{\theta}(A)
measures the maximum turning effect of
A
along the ray
\rho_{\theta}
.

The amplitude of
A
, also introduced by Krein in
[
10
]
, is then defined as

\am(A)=\min\left\{\phi_{T}(A):\,T=e^{i\theta}I,\,\,\,\theta\in[0,2\pi]\right\}\,.

The amplitude compares the maximum turning effect of
A
along every possible
\rho_{\theta}
and provides the smallest one.

In
[
3
]
we proved that if
A
has closed range,
R(A)+N(A)
is closed and
\am(A)<\pi
, then
A
has range-kernel complementarity. The converse of this is not true, since it may be proved that
\am(A)<\pi
implies that the spectrum of
A
is confined to a sector of the complex plane. In
[
4
]
the hypothesis that
R(A)+N(A)
is closed was dropped and a characterization of range-kernel complementarity was given, using the angle of
A
along a curve, for closed range operators whose spectrum is not confined to a sector of
\mathbb{C}
, but still does not separate
0
from
\infty
. Hence, again, no complete characterization of range-kernel complementarity was given.

Trying to amend these shortcomings and motivated by the amplitude of Krein we define the generalized amplitude of
A
with respect to
\mathcal{E}_{A}
to be

\Am(A)=\inf\left\{\varphi_{T}(A):T\in\mathcal{E}_{A}\right\}\,.

Remark 2.2
.

(a) Obviously
\Am(A)\leq\am(A)
, for all operators
A
.

(b) In the special case of an invertible operator
A
, we have that
A\in\mathcal{E}_{A}
and hence
\Am(A)=\varphi_{A}(A)=0
. This simple example encapsulates a crucial difference between the generalized amplitude and Krein’s one. The bilateral shift
A
on
l^{2}
is an invertible operator and so
\Am(A)=0
, whereas its Krein amplitude is equal to
\pi
, since its spectrum separates 0 from
\infty
. Note that by
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
the angle of
A
along any curve is also equal to
\pi
.

(c) More generally, if
A:X\rightarrow X
is invertible, then for the non-invertible operator
A\oplus 0:X\oplus X\rightarrow X\oplus X
we have that
A\oplus I\in\mathcal{E}_{A}
and hence
\Am(A\oplus 0)\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
. Taking
A
to be the bilateral shift we get an example of a non-invertible operator with
\Am(A)<\am(A)
.

Using this generalized amplitude we get the following characterization of range-kernel complementarity.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
A:X\rightarrow X
be a bounded linear operator with closed range. Then

X=R(A)\oplus N(A)

if and only if
\Am(A)<\pi
.

To get to the proof we start with five preparatory Lemmas.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\varphi_{T}(A)<\pi
, then there exists
c>0
such that
\|Ax+tTx\|\geq c\|Ax\|
, for all
x\notin N(A)
and all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

We have that
\varphi_{T}(A)<\pi
implies that there exists
\delta>0
such that

\frac{Re[Ax,Tx]}{\|Ax\|\,\|Tx\|}\geq-1+\delta\,,

for all
x\notin N(A)\cup N(T)
and hence

Re[Ax,Tx]+\|Ax\|\,\|Tx\|\geq\delta\,\|Ax\|\,\|Tx\|\,,

for all
x\notin N(A)\cup N(T)
.

So for
t\geq 0
and
x\notin N(A)\cup N(T)
we get that

\displaystyle Re[Ax+tTx,Tx]+\|Ax+tTx\|\,\|Tx\|\geq

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\geq Re[Ax,Tx]+t\|Tx\|^{2}+(\|Ax\|-t\|Tx\|)\|Tx\|

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,=Re[Ax,Tx]+\|Ax\|\,\|Tx\|

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\geq\delta\|Ax\|\,\|Tx\|\,.

Thus, for
c=\frac{\delta}{2}
, we get
\|Ax+tTx\|\geq c\|Ax\|
, for all
x\notin N(A)\cup N(T)
and
t\geq 0
. Since this inequality obviously holds if
x\in N(T)
the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume
A\in B(X)
has closed range and
\varphi_{T}(A)<\pi
, for some
T\in B(X)
. If
N(A)=\left\{0\right\}
and
A+t_{0}T
is invertible, for some
t_{0}>0
, then
A+tT
is invertible, for any
|t-t_{0}|<\mu
, where
\mu>0
is independent of
t_{0}
.

Proof.

Since the range of
A
is closed, there exists
k_{A}>0
such that

\|Ax\|\geq k_{A}\dist(x,N(A))

and thus, since
N(A)=\left\{0\right\}
,

\|Ax\|\geq k_{A}\|x\|\,,\text{ for all }x\in X\,.

Let
\mu=c\,k_{A}>0
, where
c
is the constant from Lemma
2.4
. Since
N(A)=\left\{0\right\}
, we get that

(2.1)

\|Ax+tTx\|\geq\mu\|x\|\,,\text{ for all }x\in X\text{ and }t\geq 0\,.

So
A+tT
is injective for all
t\geq 0
.

Hence it is enough to show that if
|t-t_{0}|<\mu
then
A+tT
is also onto. So fix such a
t
and let
L=\frac{|t-t_{0}|}{\mu}<1
. For
y
in
X
, define
F:X\rightarrow X
by

Fh=(A+t_{0}T)^{-1}\left(y+(t_{0}-t)h\right),\text{ for every }h\in X\,.

Then, using (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|Fh_{1}-Fh_{2}\|

\displaystyle=\|(A+t_{0}T)^{-1}\left(y+(t_{0}-t)h_{1}\right)-(A+t_{0}T)^{-1}\left(y+(t_{0}-t)h_{2}\right)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu}\,\|(t_{0}-t)(h_{1}-h_{2})\|

\displaystyle=L\,\|h_{1}-h_{2}\|\,.

Hence
F
is a contraction and thus there exists a unique
x\in X
such that
Fx=x
, which implies that
(A+t_{0}T)x=y+(t_{0}-t)x
. Hence
(A+tT)x=y
and so
A+tT
is onto as was required.
∎

Next we show that if some angle of
A
is less than
\pi
, then the sum of
R(A)
and
N(A)
is both closed and direct.

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume
\varphi_{T}(A)<\pi
, for some
T\in\mathcal{E}_{A}
. Then
R(A)+N(A)
is closed and
R(A)\cap N(A)=\left\{0\right\}
.

Proof.

If
x\notin N(A)
,
y\in N(A)
and
t>0
, from Lemma
2.4
we get that

\left\|A\left(x+\frac{1}{t}\,y\right)+tT\left(x+\frac{1}{t}\,y\right)\right\|\geq c\|Ax\|

and thus

\|Ax+tTx+Ty\|\geq c\|Ax\|\,.

Letting
t\rightarrow 0
we have that

\|Ax+Ty\|\geq c\|Ax\|\,.

Since
T(N(A))=N(A)
, we get that the sum
R(A)+N(A)
is closed.

To conclude note that if
Ax\in N(A)
, then from the above inequality
Ax=0
and so
R(A)\cap N(A)=\left\{0\right\}
.
∎

The final two results that we will need for the proof of Theorem
2.3
are the following:

Lemma 2.7
.

[
11
, Corollary p. 129]

If

R(A)\cap N(A)=\left\{0\right\}\,,

then
R(A)+N(A)
is closed if and only if
R(A^{2})
is closed.

Lemma 2.8
.

[
4
, Corollary 5.2]

If
P
is a projection, then
\varphi_{I}(P)<\pi
.

We may now proceed with the proof of our main result.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Assume that
X=R(A)\oplus N(A)
. Then, by
[
11
, Theorem 3]
, there exist an invertible operator
S
and a projection
P
such that
A
can be factorized as
A=SP
. In particular
S=A|_{R(A)}\oplus I_{N(A)}
with respect to
X=R(A)\oplus N(A)
and
P
is the projection onto
R(A)
parallel to
N(A)
. Hence
S(N(A))=N(A)
and
SP=PS
and so, by Lemma
2.8
, we get that

\varphi_{S}(A)=\varphi_{I}(P)<\pi\,.

Thus
\Am(A)<\pi
.

For the converse if
\Am(A)<\pi
, then there exists
T\in\mathcal{E}_{A}
such that
\varphi_{T}(A)<\pi
.

We first assume that
N(A)=\left\{0\right\}
. Since
T\in\mathcal{E}_{A}
we have that
A+t_{0}T
is invertible for some
t_{0}>0
and hence, by Lemma
2.5
, we get that so is
A+tT
, for
|t-t_{0}|<\mu
. Since
\mu>0
is independent of
t_{0}
, continuing this way we get that
A
is invertible and hence we have range-kernel complementarity.

If
N(A)\neq\left\{0\right\}
and since by Lemma
2.6
we have that
R(A)\cap N(A)=\left\{0\right\}
, in order to conclude (see
[
9
, Proposition 38.4]
) it is enough to show that
R(A^{2})=R(A)
. By Lemma
2.7
we have that
R(A^{2})
is closed and hence if

R\left(A|_{R(A)}\right)=R(A^{2})\neq R(A)\,,

then
A|_{R(A)}
has trivial kernel, closed range but is not invertible. Hence by the first part of the proof

\varphi_{T}(A|_{R(A)})=\pi\,.

But
\varphi_{T}(A|_{R(A)})\leq\varphi_{T}(A)
and thus we have a contradiction. Hence

R(A^{2})=R(A)
and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

If the operator
T
for which
\varphi_{T}(A)<\pi
is invertible, then things are much simpler. In particular then
\varphi(T^{-1}A)<\pi
and hence, by
[
3
, Theorem 3.4]
, we have that

X=R(T^{-1}A)\oplus N(T^{-1}A)\,.

Therefore by
[
11
, Theorem 3]
there exist an invertible operator
S
and a projection
P
such that
T^{-1}A=SP
and thus
A=TSP
which, again by
[
11
, Theorem 3]
, gives

X=R(A)\oplus N(A)

as required.

If
0
is an isolated point of the spectrum of
A
, then with respect to the Riesz decomposition
A
is of the form “invertible
\,\oplus
quasinilpotent” . Since range-kernel complementarity is equivalent to the “quasinilpotent part” of
A
being
0
it is quite expected that quasinilpotent operators with closed range should have amplitude equal to
\pi
.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
Q
be a quasinilpotent operator with closed range. Then
\Am(Q)=\pi
.

Proof.

If
\Am(Q)<\pi
then by Theorem
2.3

X=R(Q)\oplus N(Q)

which is impossible since by
[
5
]
the range chain of a quasinilpotent operator does not terminate.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

As
\Am(Q)\leq\am(Q)
, for quasinilpotent operators with closed range the generalized amplitude is equal to Krein’s. Moreover since
\sigma(Q)
does not separate 0 from
\infty
by
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
the angle of
Q
along any curve is also equal to
\pi
.

3.
An application

In this section we present a simple application of our main result to the problem of strong convergence of the iterates
T^{n}
of a bounded linear operator
T
defined on a Banach space
X
. Recall that
T
is called asymptotically regular if, for all
x\in X
,

(T^{n}-T^{n+1})x\rightarrow 0,\text{ as }n\rightarrow+\infty\,.

Remark 3.1
.

If
A
is nilpotent then it is asymptotically regular and hence the asymptotic regularity of
A
does not imply that
\Am(A)<\pi
.

The proof of our result follows the idea of
[
1
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and
T\in B(X)
be an asymptotically regular operator, such that
R(I-T)
is closed. The sequence of iterates
T^{n}x
converges, for all
x\in X
, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
if and only if
\Am(I-T)<\pi
.

Proof.

Since
R(I-T)
is closed if
\Am(I-T)<\pi
we have from Theorem
2.3
that

X=R(I-T)\oplus N(I-T)\,.

Moreover by the fact that
T
is asymptotically regular we have that
T^{n}z\rightarrow 0
, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, for all
z\in R(I-T)
. Hence if
x\in X
, then
x=y+z
with
y\in N(I-T)
and
z\in R(I-T)
and so

T^{n}x=T^{n}y+T^{n}z\rightarrow y\text{ as }n\rightarrow+\infty\,.

The converse follows again from Theorem
2.3
since the strong convergence of
T^{n}
implies by
[
8
, Theorem 3]
that
X=R(I-T)\oplus N(I-T)
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

(a) It may be easily seen from the above proof that
T^{n}
converges strongly to the projection onto
R(I-T)
parallel to
N(I-T)
.

(b) Note that, by
[
1
, Theorem 4.1]
,
R(I-T)
is closed if and only if the convergence of
T^{n}
is uniform.

(c) The connection between range-kernel complementarity and the strong convergence of the iterates
T^{n}
was used in the well-known paper of Halperin
[
8
]
in order to prove the convergence of the iterates of
T=P_{1}P_{2}...P_{n}
, for
P_{1},...,P_{n}
orthogonal projections on a Hilbert space. This was later generalized for norm-one projections on a Banach space by Bruck and Reich in
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
and by Badea and Lyubich in
[
1
, Main Theorem, p. 25]
, with suitable additional conditions .

Recall that we say that a contraction
T
is primitive if
T
satisfies the Katznelson-Tzafriri spectral condition, i.e. if

\sigma(T)\subseteq\left\{1\right\}\cup\left\{t\in\mathbb{C}:|t|<1\right\}\,.

We have the following Corollary of Theorem
3.2
for primitive contractions on a reflexive Banach space.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
X
is a reflexive Banach space and
T
is a primitive contraction with
R(I-T)
closed, then
\Am(I-T)<\pi
.

Proof.

To see this note that
T
is asymptotically regular and by
[
1
, Theorem 4.1]
its iterates
T^{n}
converge strongly. Hence the result follows by Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

(a) Since
T
in Corollary
3.4
is a contraction
\sigma(I-T)
does not separate 0 from
\infty
and hence, by
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
, we have that the Krein amplitude of
I-T
is also less than
\pi
.

(b) Corollary
3.4
combined with
[
1
, Main Theorem]
shows the following: If
T
is a convex combination of products of orthoprojections on a complex Banach space
X
,
R(I-T)
is closed,
X
is uniformly convex or uniformly smooth or reflexive, and the orthoprojections are of class (D) (for the definition see
[
1
]
), then
\Am(I-T)<\pi
. We would like to note here that since such a
T
may be quasinilpotent with closed range, the generalized amplitude of such a
T
, by Corollary
2.10
, may be equal to
\pi
.

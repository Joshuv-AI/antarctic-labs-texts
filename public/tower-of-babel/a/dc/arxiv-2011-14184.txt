Cohomology jump loci and absolute sets for singular varieties
Leonardo A. Lerer

Abstract
. We extend the notion of absolute subsets of Betti moduli spaces of smooth algebraic varieties to the case of normal varieties. As a consequence we prove that twisted cohomology jump loci in rank one over a normal variety are a finite union of translated subtori. We show that the same holds for jump loci twisted by a unitary local system in the case where the underlying variety X X is projective with H 1 ​ ( X , ℚ ) H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}) pure of weight one. Lastly, we study the interaction of these loci with Hodge theoretic data naturally associated to the representation variety of fundamental groups of smooth projective varieties.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. The set of semisimple local systems of rank
n
on
X
is parametrized by the
{\mathbb{C}}
-points of a scheme
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)
, called the Betti moduli space. The topological properties of the space
X
are reflected in the structure of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)
and of naturally defined subsets therein.
An example of the latter are the
cohomology jump loci
, namely the sets

\prescript{}{n}{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}({\mathcal{F}}):=\{L\in{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)({\mathbb{C}}):\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{i}(X,L\otimes{\mathcal{F}})\geq k\},

for
i,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
{\mathcal{F}}
is an object of
D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
, the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves on
X
. When
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathbb{C}}_{X}
, the constant sheaf of rank one, we simply write
\prescript{}{n}{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}:=\prescript{}{n}{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}({\mathbb{C}}_{X})
and, when
n=1
, we write
\Sigma^{i}_{k}({\mathcal{F}}):=\prescript{}{1}{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}({\mathcal{F}})
. These subsets are closed algebraic subvarieties of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)
and they have been the subject of numerous investigations, starting with Green-Lazarsfeld, Beauville and Catanese (see
[
20
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
12
]
).

When
n=1
the Betti moduli space is a finite union of complex algebraic tori and if
X
is smooth the cohomology jump loci have a simple form, namely they are translated subtori of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)
. When
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X,{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})
is of geometric origin or, more generally, if it defines an absolute
{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
-point (cf. Definition
2.1
) the translation is by torsion points. In this generality, the result was proved in
[
9
, Theorem 1.4.1]
, building on the previous work of many authors, see the chronology in
[
8
, Remark 6.13]
.

The main focus of this paper is the study of the structure of such subvarieties in the case where
X
is allowed to be singular. This has been the topic of some recent works, in which the torsion-translated-subtori property was extended to some classes of singular varieties. In
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
the property is proved for
\Sigma^{1}_{k}
when
X
is normal. If
X
is an algebraic variety whose mixed Hodge structure
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
satisfies
W_{0}H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=0
, it is proved in
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
that the irreducible components of
\Sigma^{i}_{k}
passing through the identity are subtori. The most general result is found in
[
16
, Theorem 1.6]
where the torsion-translated-subtori property is proved for
X
satisfying the same weight condition on
H^{1}
and for
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X,{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})
satisfying a certain arithmeticity condition, which includes objects coming from geometry (see the discussion at the end of Section 2.1 for a comparison between the notion of arithmeticity and the notion of absolute
{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
-point). Note that a normal variety satisfies
W_{0}H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=0
, since the pullback
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{1}(X^{\mathrm{reg}},{\mathbb{Q}})
is injective, where
X^{\mathrm{reg}}
denotes the smooth locus (see the related discussion in Section 2.3). We extend the results of
[
16
]
in the case of normal varieties and deal with arbitrary twisting objects
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
. More precisely, we prove:

Theorem 1.1

(
2.11
)
.

Let
X
be a complex quasi-projective normal variety and
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
. Then, for all
i,k\geq 0
,
\Sigma^{i}_{k}({\mathcal{F}})
is a finite union of translated subtori. If
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X,{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})
is of geometric origin, the translation is by torsion points.

The results of
[
6
]
and
[
16
]
mentioned above suggest that the most general varieties for which we can expect the torsion-translated-subtori property for twisted cohomology jump loci are those varieties
X
satisfying
W_{0}H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=0
(we also refer the reader to the article
[
30
]
by M. Saito for a study on the condition
W_{0}H^{1}=0
). In this direction, we prove that if
X
is a
projective
variety satisfying the above weight condition, i.e. whose
H^{1}
is pure of weight one, and
E
is a unitary local system on
X
, then
\Sigma^{i}_{k}(E)
is a finite union of translated subtori. In particular, we do not require
E
to possess any arithmeticity property. We remark, however, that we do not recover the fact that the translation is by torsion points when
E
comes from geometry, as proved in
[
16
]
. Our precise statement is as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(
3.1
)
.

Let
X
be a complex projective variety such that
W_{0}H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=0
. Let
E
be a semisimple local system on
X
such that for one (equivalently, any) resolution of singularities
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
,
p^{*}E
is semisimple. Then, for all
i,k\geq 0
, the varieties
\Sigma^{i}_{k}(E)
are a finite union of translated subtori.

We then study the interaction of cohomology jump loci with Hodge theoretic data which is naturally attached to the Betti moduli space or representation varieties. In rank one, the Zariski tangent space at the identity of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)
is canonically identified with
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
and in
[
16
]
it is proved that the tangent space of irreducible components of cohomology jump loci, translated back to the identity, is a sub-MHS of
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. A subtorus of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)
whose tangent space is a sub-MHS is called “motivic” in
loc. cit.
.
Denote by
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)^{0}
the connected component of the Betti moduli space containing the constant local system.
We prove the following:

Theorem 1.3

(
4.3
)
.

Let
X
be a complex quasi-projective normal variety and
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
. The subtori corresponding to the irreducible components of
\Sigma^{i}_{k}({\mathcal{F}})\cap{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)^{0}({\mathbb{C}})
are motivic.

To prove Theorem 1.1 and 1.3, we build on the work in
[
9
]
and extend the notion of absolute subsets of Betti moduli spaces to the case of normal varieties using resolutions of singularities. The idea of studying the topology of normal varieties by resolving singularities was already employed for instance in
[
2
]
. On the other hand, the proof of Theorem 1.2 follows an “ad-hoc” strategy involving simplicial resolutions. We also remark that our proofs use transcendental methods while the work of Esnault-Kerz uses
p
-adic geometry and Galois-theoretic arguments.

For local systems of higher rank, the picture is more complicated. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety,
x_{0}\in X({\mathbb{C}})
a point and denote by
{\mathbf{R}}(X,x_{0},n)
the representation variety of rank
n
, i.e. the fine moduli space parametrizing rank
n
representations of
\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})
. If
\rho:\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})\to{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
is a representation underlying a polarizable complex variation of Hodge structures (
{\mathbb{C}}
VHS), Eyssidieux and Simpson in
[
17
]
and later Lefèvre in
[
26
]
have constructed a functorial
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS on the completed local ring
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
, where
{\mathcal{O}}_{\rho}
is the local ring at
\rho
in
{\mathbf{R}}(X,x_{0},n)
. Note that such mixed Hodge structure is defined on an infinite dimensional vector space, cf. Definition
4.4
. On
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
there is also another functorial
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS, which is easier to describe and which is isomorphic to the
{\textnormal{Gr}}_{W}
of the previous
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS, where
W
denotes the weight filtration. It corresponds essentially to a bigrading on
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
as a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space. We shall refer to it as the
split

{\mathbb{C}}
MHS on
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
(see Section
4.2
for its construction). Define the cohomology jump loci in the representation variety by
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}:=\pi^{-1}(\Sigma^{i}_{k})
, where
\pi:{\mathbf{R}}(X,x_{0},n)\to{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)
is the GIT quotient map. We prove that, if
k:=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{i}(X,L_{\rho})
, then the ideal
I\subset\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
cutting the locus
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}
at
\rho
is a sub-MHS of the split MHS on
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
(see Theorem
4.7
). In other words, we prove that
I
is compatible with the bigrading corresponding to the split MHS introduced above. This can be seen as a higher rank analogue of the motivicity result of
[
16
]
.

In the last section, we complement some results of
[
11
]
concerning jump loci associated to the Hodge theory of
H^{\bullet}(X,L)
where
X
is a smooth algebraic variety and
L
is a unitary local system of rank one (thus underlying a polarizable
{\mathbb{C}}
VHS of type
(0,0)
). More precisely, we prove:

Theorem 1.4

(
5.1
)
.

Let
U
be a smooth complex algebraic variety and
{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)\subset{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(U,1)({\mathbb{C}})
the union of real subtori parametrizing rank one unitary local systems on
U
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the Boolean algebra generated by torsion-translated real subtori of
{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)
.

(1) For all
a,i,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, the set

W^{a,i,k}:=\{L\in{\mathbf{U}}(U,1):\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Gr_{W}^{a}H^{i}(U,L)\geq k\}

belongs to
{\mathcal{A}}
.

(2) Let
j:U\to X
be an open embedding into a smooth proper variety
X
such that
X\setminus U
is a simple normal crossing divisor. For all
p,q,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, the set

V^{p,q,k}:=\{L\in{\mathbf{U}}(U,1):\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Gr^{F}_{p}\mathit{IH}^{p+q}(X,L)\geq k\}

belongs to
{\mathcal{A}}
. Here

denotes intersection cohomology.

Notation

A complex algebraic variety is an integral, separated scheme of finite type over
{\rm Spec}({\mathbb{C}})
. Unless stated otherwise, a “variety” is a complex algebraic variety. If
X
is a complex algebraic variety, we write
LocSys(X)
for the category of local systems of finite dimensional complex vector spaces on the associated complex analytic space
X^{\mathrm{an}}
. If
K
is a field, then
D^{b}_{c}(X,K)
denotes the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves of
K
-vector spaces on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a category, then
\mathrm{Iso}({\mathcal{C}})
denotes the “collection” of isomorphism classes of objects of
{\mathcal{C}}
. We will consider isomorphism classes of objects only when
{\mathcal{C}}
is an essentially small category, therefore
\mathrm{Iso}({\mathcal{C}})
will be an honest set (in our case,
{\mathcal{C}}
will be one of the following, for a complex algebraic variety
X
:
D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
,
LocSys(X)
or the bounded derived category of regular holonomic
{\mathcal{D}}_{X}
-modules). The abbreviation “dg” (resp. “dga”, “dgla”) will mean “differential-graded” (resp. “differential-graded algebra”, “differential-graded Lie algebra”).

2.
Absolute sets for normal varieties

2.1.
Absolute
K
-constructible sets

In this section we recall the formalism of absolute constructible subsets on a smooth complex variety, introduced in
[
9
]
.

Let
X
be a smooth complex algebraic variety. We denote by
{\mathcal{D}}_{X}\subset{\mathcal{H}}om_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
the sheaf of differential operators on
X
. The Riemann-Hilbert correspondence is an equivalence of categories

RH:D^{b}_{rh}({\mathcal{D}}_{X})\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}})

between the bounded derived category of regular holonomic
{\mathcal{D}}_{X}
-modules and the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves on
X^{\textnormal{an}}
(see e.g.
[
24
]
).

Given an automorphism
\sigma\in\textnormal{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})
, let
X^{\sigma}\to X
be the base change of
X
via
\sigma
.
It is an isomorphism of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-schemes however the induced map on the analytifications is far from being continuous in general.
Consider the diagram

(1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}_{rh}({\mathcal{D}}_{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}_{rh}({\mathcal{D}}_{X^{\sigma}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}_{c}(X^{\sigma},{\mathbb{C}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces RH}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{\sigma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces RH}

where
p_{\sigma}
is the pullback of
{\mathcal{D}}_{X}
-modules under the base change over
\sigma
.
On the sets of isomorphism classes of objects, the maps induced by the diagram (
1
) are bijections.
Notice that there is no natural arrow
D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}})\to D^{b}_{c}(X^{\sigma},{\mathbb{C}})
making the diagram commute.

For a subset
T\subset\mathrm{Iso}(D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))
, define

(2)

T^{\sigma}:=RH\circ p_{\sigma}\circ RH^{-1}(T)\subset\mathrm{Iso}(D^{b}_{c}(X^{\sigma},{\mathbb{C}})).

If
n\geq 1
is an integer and
S\subset{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)({\mathbb{C}})
is a subset, then, regarding the elements of
S
as isomorphism classes of semisimple local systems,
S
determines a subset of
\mathrm{Iso}(D^{b}_{c}(X^{\sigma},{\mathbb{C}}))
.
We define
S^{\sigma}
as in (
2
).
Since Galois conjugation preserves flat connections and semisimplicity, we deduce that
S^{\sigma}
corresponds to a subset of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X^{\sigma},n)({\mathbb{C}})
. Recall that since
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)
is defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
(even over
{\mathbb{Z}}
), the notion of
K
-constructible subset of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)({\mathbb{C}})
, for a subfield
K\subset{\mathbb{C}}
, is well-defined.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be a subfield.

(1)

A subset
S\subset{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)({\mathbb{C}})
is absolute
K
-constructible (resp. absolute
K
-closed) if it is
K
-constructible (resp.
K
-closed) and if, for all
\sigma\in\textnormal{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})
, the subset
S^{\sigma}\subset{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X^{\sigma},n)({\mathbb{C}})
defined above is
K
-constructible (resp.
K
-closed).

(2)

An object
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X,K)
is an
absolute
K
-point
if for all
\sigma\in\textnormal{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})
, there exists an element
{\mathcal{G}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X^{\sigma},K)
such that

p_{\sigma}\circ RH^{-1}({\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbb{C}})\cong RH^{-1}({\mathcal{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}).

Remark 2.2
.

(1)

The above definition differs slightly from the one given in
[
9
, Definition 6.3.1]
but is seen to be equivalent, according to
[
5
, Proposition 4.6]
.
We chose to present it in this way to avoid introducing the language of unispaces which will not be needed in the sequel.

(2)

In the case of a smooth projective variety, the absolute constructible subsets were defined by Simpson in
[
32
, Section 6]
.

It is easy to see that if
{\mathcal{F}}
is of geometric origin in the sense of
[
4
, 6.2.4]
then
{\mathcal{F}}
is an absolute
{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
-point. Conversely, it is conjectured that if
{\mathcal{F}}
is a semisimple perverse sheaf over a smooth variety then
{\mathcal{F}}
being an absolute
{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
-point should imply that
{\mathcal{F}}
is of geometric origin, see
[
9
, Conjecture 10.2.2 (6)]
.

Remark 2.3
.

There is a more geometric version of the definition of absolute constructibility, called
moduli-absolute constructibility
in
[
9
]
.
Let
X
be a smooth
quasi-projective
variety,
\overline{X}
a smooth compactification with normal crossing divisor
D
.
Nitsure
[
29
]
and Simpson
[
33
]
constructed a scheme
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\overline{X}/D,n)
that is a coarse moduli space parametrizing Jordan equivalence classes of semistable logarithmic connections of rank
n
, with poles at most on
D
.
The Riemann-Hilbert correspondence induces an analytic morphism

(3)

RH^{\textnormal{an}}:{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\overline{X}/D,n)^{\textnormal{an}}\to{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)^{\textnormal{an}}.

A subset
S\subset{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)({\mathbb{C}})
is called
moduli-absolute
K
-constructible

[
9
, Definition 7.4.1]
if it is
K
-constructible and
RH^{\textnormal{an}}\circ p_{\sigma}\circ(RH^{\textnormal{an}})^{-1}(S)\subset{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X^{\sigma},n)
is
K
-constructible, for all
\sigma\in\textnormal{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})
. Here
p_{\sigma}:{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\overline{X}/D,n)\to{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\overline{X}^{\sigma}/D^{\sigma},n)
is defined by pullback of semistable logarithmic connections.

As pointed out in
[
5
, Section 5.6]
, it is not known in general whether
RH^{\textnormal{an}}
is surjective.
This leads to problems with the previous definition of moduli-absoluteness.
However, in rank one, it is certainly known that
RH^{\textnormal{an}}
is surjective and the two definitions of absoluteness coincide
[
9
, Proposition 7.4.4]
. This fact will be used below in the proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Arithmeticity.

Definition
2.1
(2) is reminiscent of the arithmeticity condition of Esnault-Kerz
[
16
, Definition 1.4]
, which applies more generally to non-smooth varieties. We recall it here for convenience. Let
F\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be a finitely generated field,
X
a geometrically connected algebraic variety over
F
and
p
a prime number. We denote by
X_{{\mathbb{C}},\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
the étale site of
X_{\mathbb{C}}
. An object
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X_{{\mathbb{C}},\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}_{p})
is
arithmetic
if there exists a finitely generated field extension
F\subset F^{\prime}
such that for all
\sigma\in\textnormal{Aut}({\mathbb{C}}/F^{\prime})
, the complex
\sigma({\mathcal{F}})
is isomorphic (in the derived category) to
{\mathcal{F}}
. An object
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
is
arithmetic
it there exists a number field
K
such that
{\mathcal{F}}
descends to
D^{b}_{c}(X,K)
and such that for infinitely many embeddings
K\hookrightarrow{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}_{p}
,
{\mathcal{F}}
induces an arithmetic object
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X_{{\mathbb{C}},\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}_{p})
.

Assume now that
X
is smooth and consider
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
: we do not know whether the assumption that
{\mathcal{F}}
is arithmetic implies that
{\mathcal{F}}
is an absolute
{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
-point, or vice versa. The two notions are equivalent if
{\mathcal{F}}
is a local system of rank one on
X
. Indeed, it follows from
[
16
, Theorem 5.2]
that in this case
{\mathcal{F}}
is arithmetic if and only if it is torsion. On the other hand, a local system of rank one defines an absolute
{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
-point if and only if it is torsion, by
[
9
, Theorem 9.1.2]
.

2.3.
Absoluteness on normal varieties

Let
X
be a normal complex algebraic variety (one could weaken this assumption by taking
X
to be unibranch: in that case, the normalization induces an homeomorphism on the underlying topological spaces. All functors considered in the sequel depend only on the homeomorphism type of
X^{\textnormal{an}}
).
Let
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a resolution of singularities which is an isomorphism over a Zariski open subset
U\subset X
.
By normality of
X
and
\widetilde{X}
, the maps
\pi_{1}(U)\to\pi_{1}(X)
and
\pi_{1}(U)\to\pi_{1}(\widetilde{X})
are surjective (the latter map is obtained by considering
U
as an open in
\widetilde{X}
).
This follows from
[
18
, 0.7 (B)]
.
The diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(\widetilde{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{*}}

shows that
p_{*}
is surjective.
This implies that the map
i:{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)\to{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(\widetilde{X},n)
obtained by functoriality is actually a closed embedding.
This is immediate at the level of representation varieties: if
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{X}({\mathbb{C}})
is a base point and
x=p(\widetilde{x})\in X({\mathbb{C}})
, then the representation variety
{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,x,n)
is cut scheme-theoretically inside
{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{B}}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{x},n)
by the equations

Theorem 2.10

Let

Let

be a closed embedding in a smooth variety

and apply the previous theorem to the map

Remark 2.12
.

The above arguments break down if we only assume that

is an algebraic variety with

3.
Projective varieties with

The aim of this section is to prove the following:

Theorem 3.1

Let

be a projective variety such that

The strategy of the proof follows the same idea of the proof of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{D}}A^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Simp}_{\bullet}X,L)^{\mathrm{const}}}

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

be a split simplicial manifold and

a local system of

-vector spaces (resp.

-algebras) on

. Let

be the usual de Rham complex of smooth forms with values in

, and

The argument of

and use

, 5.3.7]

The proof of Theorem

will be devoted to the construction of a certain dga

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(A,M)
be a dga pair. Then

has a natural structure of dga, functorial in the pair
(A,M)
. Moreover, a morphism
(A,M)\to(A^{\prime},M^{\prime})
is a quasi-isomorphism if and only if

is quasi-isomorphism of dga’s.

Proof.

The construction is well-known and appears in the context of Lie algebras and

-algebras in
[

]
and
[

, Theorem 2.5]
. The differential on

is given by

and multiplication is given by the rule
(a_{1},m_{1})(a_{2},m_{2})=(a_{1}a_{2},a_{1}m_{2}+a_{2}m_{1})

loc. cit.
translate easily in this situation.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
A
(resp.
A^{\prime}
) be a cosimplicial dga,

(resp.

(resp.

). Assume that

We use Lemma

to endow

with the structure of cosimplicial dga’s. Then

∎

4.
Motivicity of absolute sets

4.1.
Motivicity in rank one

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety. Recall that
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)
is a finite union of algebraic tori defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and the algebraic tangent space at the identity is canonically identified with
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. By work of Deligne, it is endowed with a functorial mixed Hodge structure. We recall the definition of motivicity from
[
16
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
{\mathbb{Q}}
-subtorus
M\subset{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)
is
motivic
if
T_{e}M\subset H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
is a sub-MHS, where
T_{e}M
is the algebraic tangent space of
M
at the identity.

In
loc. cit.
it is proved, using Faltings’ theorem on the Mordell conjecture, that the cohomology jump loci
\Sigma^{i}_{k}({\mathcal{F}})
in
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)
are finite unions of translated motivic subtori, where
X
is an algebraic variety satisfying
W_{0}H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=0
and
{\mathcal{F}}
is arithmetic. We prove this fact for certain components of absolute
{\mathbb{C}}
-constructible subsets for normal varieties. Denote by
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)^{0}
the connected component of the Betti moduli space containing the constant local system.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
X
be a normal quasi-projective variety and
S
an absolute
{\mathbb{C}}
-constructible subset contained in
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)^{0}({\mathbb{C}})
. Then the Zariski-closure of an irreducible component of
S
is a translated motivic subtorus.

Proof.

Consider first the case where
X
is smooth.
Let
\overline{X}
be a smooth projective compactification of
X
with normal crossing divisor
D
.
We can and will assume that
X,\overline{X},D
are defined over a common countable subfield
K\subset{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
Z
be an irreducible component of
S
.
By
[
9
, Proposition 10.4.3]
, irreducible components of absolute
{\mathbb{C}}
-constructible subsets in rank one are absolute
{\mathbb{C}}
-constructible.
By Remark
2.3
,
Z
is also moduli-absolute
{\mathbb{C}}
-constructible.
Let
RH^{\textnormal{an}}:{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\overline{X}/D,1)^{\textnormal{an}}\to{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)^{\textnormal{an}}
be the analytic Riemann-Hilbert correspondence and
W
be an analytic irreducible component of
(RH^{\textnormal{an}})^{-1}(Z)
.
Since
Z
is moduli-absolute
{\mathbb{C}}
-constructible, we have that for all
\sigma\in{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{C}}/K)
the set
\sigma(W)\subset{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\overline{X}/D,1)^{\textnormal{an}}
is analytically constructible (here we use that
X,\overline{X},D
are defined over
K
, hence
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\overline{X}/D,1)
is as well).
Up to enlarging the field
K
and preserving its countability, we can also assume that the irreducible component of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\overline{X}/D,1)
containing
W
is defined over
K
.
Then
[
9
, Theorem 7.4.6]
implies that the Euclidean closure of
W
is algebraic (and irreducible) in
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\overline{X}/D,1)
.
Let
{\mathbf{M}}
be the connected component of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\overline{X}/D,1)
containing
W
and
{\mathbf{M}}_{0}
be the connected component containing the trivial connection.
After choosing a point of
(RH^{\textnormal{an}})^{-1}(1)\cap{\mathbf{M}}
as origin, one constructs an algebraic isomorphism
{\mathbf{M}}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbf{M}}_{0}
, commuting with
RH^{\textnormal{an}}
.
Let
W^{\prime}
be the image of
W
in
{\mathbf{M}}_{0}
.
With these hypotheses in place, the Zariski closure
\overline{Z}
is an irreducible Betti-de Rham subset of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)^{0}({\mathbb{C}})

[
10
, Definition 3.1 and Section 4]
i.e.
\dim(\overline{W^{\prime}})=\dim(\overline{Z})
and
RH^{\textnormal{an}}(\overline{W^{\prime}})\subset\overline{Z}
.
Now [
loc. cit.
, Theorem 3.3] implies that
\overline{Z}
is a translate of a motivic subtorus.
We note that the case where
X
is smooth projective was already treated in
[
32
]
.

Assume now that
X
is only normal. Let
\widetilde{X}\to X
be a resolution of singularities,
i:{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(\widetilde{X},1)
the embedding and
S
an absolute
{\mathbb{C}}
-constructible subset contained in
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)^{0}({\mathbb{C}})
. By definition,
i(S)
is absolute
{\mathbb{C}}
-constructible in
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(\widetilde{X},1)({\mathbb{C}})
and it is contained in
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(\widetilde{X},1)^{0}({\mathbb{C}})
. By the smooth case treated above, the closure of an irreducible component is a translated motivic subtorus in
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(\widetilde{X},1)
. To conclude that the closure of an irreducible component of
S
is motivic in
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)
, it suffices to observe that
i({\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)^{0})
is itself a motivic subtorus of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(\widetilde{X},1)
since the pullback
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{1}(\widetilde{X},{\mathbb{Q}})
is a morphism of Hodge structures.
∎

By applying the previous proposition to the cohomology jump loci we obtain the motivicity of cohomology jump loci mentioned in the introduction:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
X
be a normal quasi-projective variety and
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}_{c}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
. The subtori corresponding to the irreducible components of
\Sigma^{i}_{k}({\mathcal{F}})\cap{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)^{0}({\mathbb{C}})

Proof.

If
S\subset{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)({\mathbb{C}})
is an absolute
{\mathbb{C}}
-closed subset then the intersection
S\cap{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)^{0}({\mathbb{C}})
is absolute
{\mathbb{C}}
-closed as well (to see this, embed
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,1)
into
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(\widetilde{X},1)
and apply the fact that taking irreducible or connected components preserves absolute constructibility
[
9
, Proposition 10.4.3]
). Now the result follows from Proposition
4.2
and the fact that
\Sigma^{i}_{k}({\mathcal{F}})
is absolute
{\mathbb{C}}
-closed.
∎

4.2.
Motivicity in higher rank

For the purpose of studying motivicity in higher rank, it is more convenient to work with the representation variety
{\mathbf{R}}(X,x_{0},n)
instead of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)
(note that they coincide in rank one). For
n\geq 2
, the space
{\mathbf{R}}(X,x_{0},n)
is no longer an algebraic group and the global geometry of “special subvarieties” such as the cohomology jump loci is not well understood. Nonetheless there is a good description of their formal geometry at a point. This is the essential ingredient in what follows.

We recall here some basic definitions of the objects we will deal with. A more detailed treatment can be found in
[
17
, §1]
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
w\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be an integer. A
{\mathbb{C}}
-Hodge structure of weight
w
is the datum of a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space
V
(allowed to be infinite dimensional) endowed with two decreasing filtrations
F^{\bullet},\overline{G}^{\bullet}
such that
Gr_{F}^{p}Gr_{\overline{G}}^{q}V=0
unless
p+q=w
. A
polarization

S
on a finite dimensional
{\mathbb{C}}
-Hodge structure
V
of weight
w
is an Hermitian form such that the Hodge decomposition of
V
is orthogonal with respect to
S
and
(-1)^{p+w}S|_{H^{p,q}}
is positive definite.

A
{\mathbb{C}}
-mixed Hodge structure
is the datum of
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space
V
endowed with two decreasing filtrations
F^{\bullet},\overline{G}^{\bullet}
and an increasing filtration
W_{\bullet}
such that
Gr_{W}^{w}V
, endowed with the induced filtrations
F^{\bullet}
,
\overline{G}^{\bullet}
is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-Hodge structure of weight
w
. A
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS
V
is
defined over

{\mathbb{R}}
if
V
is the complexification of a real vector space, the filtration
W_{\bullet}
is defined over
{\mathbb{R}}
and
\overline{G}^{\bullet}
is the complex conjugate of
F^{\bullet}
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold. A
{\mathbb{C}}
-variation of Hodge structures of weight
w
is the datum of a
C^{\infty}
complex vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
on
X
, a decomposition
{\mathcal{V}}=\oplus_{p+q=w}{\mathcal{V}}^{p,q}
of
C^{\infty}
bundles and a flat connection
\nabla:{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathcal{E}}^{1}\otimes{\mathcal{V}}
satisfying Griffiths’ transversality:

\nabla:{\mathcal{V}}^{p,q}\to{\mathcal{E}}^{1,0}({\mathcal{V}}^{p-1,q+1})\oplus{\mathcal{E}}^{1,0}({\mathcal{V}}^{p,q})\oplus{\mathcal{E}}^{0,1}({\mathcal{V}}^{q,p})\oplus{\mathcal{E}}^{0,1}({\mathcal{V}}^{q+1,p-1}),

where
{\mathcal{E}}^{n}
(resp.
{\mathcal{E}}^{a,b}
) denotes the sheaf of smooth
n
-forms (resp. smooth forms of type
(a,b)
). A
polarization
on a
{\mathbb{C}}
VHS
({\mathcal{V}},\nabla)
of weight
w
is a flat Hermitian form
S
such that
{\mathcal{V}}_{x}=\oplus{\mathcal{V}}_{x}^{p,q}
is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-Hodge structure of weight
w
polarized by
S
, for all
x\in X
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety,
x_{0}\in X({\mathbb{C}})
a base point and
\rho\in{\mathbf{R}}(X,x_{0},n)({\mathbb{C}})
a representation underlying a polarized
{\mathbb{C}}
VHS. Let
{\mathcal{O}}_{\rho}
be the local ring at
\rho
and let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal of its completion
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
. The ring
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
is endowed with a functorial
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS and if the representation
\rho
is defined over
{\mathbb{R}}
, the MHS is defined over
{\mathbb{R}}
and is split, i.e. it is isomorphic as
{\mathbb{R}}
MHS to a direct sum of pure Hodge structures defined over
{\mathbb{R}}
. Its construction is due implicitly to Goldman and Millson in
[
19
]
and spelled out in
[
17
, Section 2.3]
. We recall its definition here, following
loc. cit
.

Denote by
L_{\rho}
the local system on
X
defined by
\rho
and
\epsilon_{x_{0}}:H^{0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})\to(\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})_{x_{0}}
the evaluation at
x_{0}
. The
quadratic cone
at
\rho
is the algebraic variety given by

{\mathcal{Q}}(L_{\rho}):=\{\omega\in H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho}):[\omega,\omega]=0\}\subset H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho}).

The main result in
[
19
]
constructs an isomorphism between
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
and the completion of the local ring at the origin in the space

(6)

{\mathcal{Q}}(L_{\rho})\times(\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})_{x_{0}}/\mathrm{Im}\,\,\epsilon_{x_{0}}.

The completed local ring at the origin in
{\mathcal{Q}}(L_{\rho})
is given by

(7)

Sym^{\bullet}H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})^{\vee}/I

where
I
is the homogeneous ideal generated by
I_{2}:=\mathrm{Im}(H^{2}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})^{\vee}\to Sym^{2}H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})^{\vee})
and the latter map is the transpose of the cup product

[-,-]:H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})\otimes H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})\to H^{2}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho}).

For
n\geq 3
define the subspaces

I_{n}:=I_{2}\otimes Sym^{n-2}H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})^{\vee}\subset Sym^{n}H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})^{\vee}.

If we define
I_{0}=I_{1}=0
, then
I_{n}
corresponds to the degree
n
piece of
I
. Thus
\widehat{{\mathcal{Q}}(L_{\rho})}_{(0)}
is given by

(8)

\prod_{n\geq 0}Sym^{n}H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})^{\vee}/I_{n}.

By the generalized Kähler identities for polarized
{\mathbb{C}}
VHS (see
[
35
, Section 2]
for real VHS), the cohomology groups
H^{n}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})
carry a functorial
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS of weight
n
and the cup product is a morphism of MHS. The direct product in (
8
) then carries a (infinite-dimensional)
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS, and the direct factor in place
n
is pure of weight
-n
. A similar discussion applies to the second factor in (
6
): the completed local ring at
0
of
(\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})_{x_{0}}/\mathrm{Im}\,\,\epsilon_{x_{0}}
is naturally an infinite dimensional
{\mathbb{C}}
-Hodge structure of weight zero.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\rho\in{\mathbf{R}}(X,x_{0},n)({\mathbb{C}})
be a representation underlying a polarized
{\mathbb{C}}
VHS. The
canonical split

{\mathbb{C}}
MHS on
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
is the
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS constructed in the preceding paragraph.

Define the cohomology jump loci in
{\mathbf{R}}(X,x_{0},n)
with indices
i,k\geq 0
in the obvious way:

\widetilde{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}:=\{\rho\in{\mathbf{R}}(X,x_{0},n)({\mathbb{C}}):\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{i}(X,L_{\rho})\geq k\}.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. For every
i,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, the cohomology jump locus
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}
contains representations underlying polarized
{\mathbb{C}}
VHS.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that the image of
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}
in
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)
, which is
\Sigma^{i}_{k}
, contains
{\mathbb{C}}
VHS. We claim that
{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}
is invariant under the action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
. This follows from
[
31
, Lemma 2.2]
where it is proved that if
E
is a harmonic bundle then there are natural isomorphisms between the Dolbeault cohomology of
E
and the de Rham cohomology of
E
. Here the Dolbeault cohomology of
E
is the cohomology of the complex

0\to{\mathcal{E}}\xrightarrow{\theta}{\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\Omega^{1}\xrightarrow{\theta}{\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\Omega^{2}\to\ldots.

where
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is the Higgs bundle associated to
E
. Hence the jump locus on the Betti side corresponds to the jump locus on the Dolbeault side. Moreover, if
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is a Higgs bundle and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
then
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{i}_{\mathrm{Dol}}({\mathcal{E}},\lambda\theta)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{i}_{\mathrm{Dol}}({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
, which proves that
{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-invariant. To conclude observe that
{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}
is closed. This in turn implies that
\Sigma^{i}_{k}
contains
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-invariant points, i.e. polarized
{\mathbb{C}}
VHS. Indeed,
[
31
, Proposition 1.4]
implies that if
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is a Higgs bundle then
\lim_{t\to 0}({\mathcal{E}},t\theta)
exists in the Dolbeault moduli space and is
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-invariant.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety with a base point
x_{0}
,
\rho\in{\mathbf{R}}(X,x_{0},n)({\mathbb{C}})
a representation underlying a polarized
{\mathbb{C}}
VHS. Let
i\geq 0
and
k=\dim H^{i}(X,L_{\rho})
. Then the ideal cutting
\tilde{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}
in
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
is a sub-MHS of the canonical split
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS.

Proof.

By
[
7
, §7]
the infinitesimal structure of
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}
around
\rho
is governed by the deformation theory of the augmented dgla-pair
({\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho}),{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}(L_{\rho}),\epsilon_{x_{0}})
, where
{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}(-)
denotes the twisted de Rham complex of
C^{\infty}
forms and the augmentation
\epsilon_{x_{0}}:{\mathcal{A}}^{0}(\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})\to(\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})_{x_{0}}
is the evaluation at
x_{0}
. Let
R_{\rho}
be the local ring of
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{i}_{k}
at
\rho
. The resonance variety is defined by

{\mathcal{R}}^{i}_{k}(L_{\rho}):=\{\omega\in H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho}):[\omega,\omega]=0\,\,\text{and}\,\,\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{i}(H^{\bullet}(X,L_{\rho}),\omega\wedge)\geq k\},

where
(H^{\bullet}(L_{\rho}),\omega\wedge)
is the
Aomoto complex
:

\ldots\rightarrow H^{r}(X,L_{\rho})\xrightarrow{\omega\wedge}H^{r+1}(X,L_{\rho})\rightarrow\ldots\,\,.

Since
\rho
underlies a
{\mathbb{C}}
VHS it is semisimple and
[
7
, 7.8]
implies that the inclusion
\widehat{R}_{\rho}\subset\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
followed by the Goldman-Millson isomorphism, identifies
\widehat{R}_{\rho}
with the completed local ring at
0
of

{\mathcal{R}}^{i}_{k}(L_{\rho})\times(\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})_{x_{0}}/\mathrm{Im}\,\,\epsilon_{x_{0}}.

To prove the theorem it is enough to prove that the ideal cutting
\widehat{{\mathcal{R}}^{i}_{k}(L_{\rho})_{(0)}}
in
\widehat{{\mathcal{Q}}(L_{\rho})_{(0)}}
is a sub-MHS of the split MHS described above.

Let
Z
be the subset of
\omega\in H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})
such that the linear map

\omega\wedge(-)\oplus\omega\wedge(-):H^{i-1}(X,L_{\rho})\oplus H^{i}(X,L_{\rho})\to H^{i}(X,L_{\rho})\oplus H^{i+1}(X,L_{\rho})

has rank bigger than
\dim H^{i}(L_{\rho})-k+1=1
. It has a natural scheme structure given by the zero locus of a certain determinantal ideal. By
[
7
, Section 2]
, whenever
\omega
is a Maurer-Cartan element, then
\omega\in Z
if and only if
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{i}(H^{\bullet}(X,L_{\rho}),\omega\wedge)\geq k
. The scheme structure on the cohomology jump locus is then given by the scheme theoretic intersection of the quadratic cone with
Z
(see
[
7
, Definition 4.4]
). Let
E
be the vector space
H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,L_{\rho})
and
R=Sym^{\bullet}E^{\vee}
. We show that the ideal
J\subset\widehat{R}
that cuts (the completion of)
Z_{(0)}
is a sub-MHS, concluding the proof.

Let
A_{n}:=\widehat{R}/\mathfrak{m}^{n}
. The ring
A_{n}
is isomorphic as
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS to
\bigoplus_{r=0}^{n-1}\textnormal{Sym}^{r}E^{\vee}
and the algebra operation
A_{n}\otimes A_{n}\to A_{n}
is a morphism of
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS. Let

(9)

\phi_{n}:(H^{i-1}\oplus H^{i})\otimes A_{n}\xrightarrow{\omega_{t}\wedge\,\,\oplus\,\,\omega_{t}\wedge}(H^{i}\oplus H^{i+1})\otimes A_{n}

be the wedge with the tautological element
\omega_{t}\in E\otimes E^{\vee}\subset E\otimes A_{n}
. We show that the map
\phi_{n}
is a morphism of
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS. Let
V_{1}=H^{i-1}\oplus H^{i}
and
V_{2}=H^{i}\oplus H^{i+1}
. The cup-product corresponds to a morphism
\phi:E\otimes V_{1}\to V_{2}
of
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS. Tensoring
\phi
with
A_{n}
we get a morphism

(E\otimes A_{n})\otimes V_{1}\to V_{2}\otimes A_{n}.

Fixing the tautological element (which is of type
(0,0)
) in the summand
E\otimes E^{\vee}\subset E\otimes A_{n}
, we get a morphism of
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS

(10)

V_{1}\to V_{2}\otimes A_{n}

and
\phi_{n}
is obtained by extending
A_{n}
-linearly:

V_{1}\otimes A_{n}\to V_{2}\otimes A_{n}\otimes A_{n}\to V_{2}\otimes A_{n},

where the first map is (
10
) tensored with
A_{n}
and the second map is obtained by tensoring the identity on
V_{2}
with the multiplication map
A_{n}\otimes A_{n}\to A_{n}
. This shows that
\phi_{n}
is a morphism of
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS. In down to earth terms, this means that the map between the free
A_{n}
-modules in (
9
) can be given by a matrix with elements in
E^{\vee}
that are pure of some bidegree. By
[
7
, Corollary 2.5 and §7]
the ideal
J_{n}:=J\otimes\widehat{R}/\mathfrak{m}^{n}
, corresponding to the
n
-th infinitesimal neighborhood of
0
in
Z
, is the ideal generated by the coefficients of this matrix. Since the coefficients are pure with respect to the bigrading,
J_{n}
is compatible with the bigrading.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

As explained in
[
17
, Introduction]
, the split
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS on
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\rho}
is only an approximation of the “true”
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS, which is constructed in
loc. cit.
and in
[
26
]
. It is clear that the natural framework of the above theorem should be such a
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS. After a first version of this paper appeared, Louis-Clément Lefèvre proved in
[
27
, Theorem 1.1]
that Theorem
4.7
holds for this
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS and for arbitrary indices
i,k\geq 0
.

We record here that a similar calculation is carried out in
[
6
, Theorem 1.7]
, where the authors give a description of the tangent space to the cohomology jump locus associated to an
L_{\infty}
-pair. Namely, if
(C,M)
is a
L_{\infty}
pair and
h_{i}=\dim H^{i}M
then

T\text{Def}^{i}_{k}(C,M)=\begin{cases}H^{1}C&\text{if }\,\,k<h_{i},\\
\ker\left(H^{1}C\to\oplus_{j=i-1,i}{\rm Hom}(H^{j}M,H^{j+1}M)\right)&\text{if }\,\,k=h_{i},\\
0&\text{if }\,\,k>h_{i},\end{cases}

where, in the second case, the map is given by the module structure of
H^{\bullet}M
over
H^{\bullet}C
. It is easy to see that if
C
and
M
carry a weight filtration and Hodge filtrations compatible with the
L_{\infty}
operations then the tangent space to the cohomology jump loci inherits a
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS.

5.
Jump loci of Hodge theoretic data

In this section we complement some results of
[
11
]
about the structure of jump loci associated to Hodge theoretic numerical data.

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety. If
L
is a unitary local system, then Saito’s theory of mixed Hodge modules endows the cohomology groups
H^{i}(X,L)
with functorial
{\mathbb{C}}
MHS’s. If we fix
k,p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, one can ask about the structure of the loci over which one of the quantities

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Gr^{F}_{p}H^{k}(X,L),\,\,\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Gr_{W}^{p+q}H^{k}(X,L),\,\,\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Gr^{F}_{p}Gr_{W}^{p+q}H^{k}(X,L)

remains constant, as
L
varies in the real subvariety
{\mathbf{U}}(X,n)\subset{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(X,n)
of unitary local systems. The same question can be asked with ordinary cohomology replaced by intersection cohomology, or cohomology with compact supports. The most general framework for the problem would involve arbitrary operations in the category of mixed Hodge modules.

We prove the following result for rank one local systems on smooth varieties:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
U
be a smooth variety and let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the Boolean algebra generated by torsion-translated real subtori of
{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)
.

(1) For all
a,i,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, the set

W^{a,i,k}:=\{L\in{\mathbf{U}}(U,1):\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Gr_{W}^{a}H^{i}(U,L)\geq k\}

belongs to
{\mathcal{A}}
.

(2) Let
j:U\to X
be an open embedding into a smooth proper variety
X
such that
X\setminus U
is a simple normal crossing divisor. For all
p,q,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, the set

V^{p,q,k}:=\{L\in{\mathbf{U}}(U,1):\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Gr^{F}_{p}\mathit{IH}^{p+q}(X,L)\geq k\}

belongs to
{\mathcal{A}}
. Here
\mathit{IH}
denotes intersection cohomology.

Proof.

(1) Let
X
be a smooth compactification of
U
by a simple normal crossing divisor
D\subset X
. Up to a suitable renumbering of the indices the weight filtration on
H^{i}(X,L)
coincides with the Leray filtration associated to the embedding
j:U\hookrightarrow X
(see Remark to Theorem 7.1 in
[
34
]
). By
[
9
, Corollary 6.4.4]
, the jumping loci for the dimension of the graded pieces for Leray filtration induced on
H^{i}(X,L)
are absolute
{\mathbb{Q}}
-constructible, hence belong to the Boolean algebra generated by torsion-translated subtori. Intersecting with
{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)
we obtain the result.

(2) By Theorem
2.5
, for any
i,k\geq 0
, the set

S^{i}_{k}:=\{L\in{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(U,1)({\mathbb{C}}):\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathit{IH}^{i}(X,L)=k\}

belongs to the Boolean algebra generated by torsion-translated subtori. By the Hodge theory of unitary local systems
[
34
]
, the spectral sequence converging to
\mathit{IH}^{\bullet}(X,L)
associated to the Hodge filtration degenerates at the first page, whenever
L
is unitary. We will first partition
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(U,1)
into subvarieties belonging to the Boolean algebra of torsion-translated subtori and prove that the dimension of
\prescript{}{F}{E}_{1}^{p,q}
varies in an upper semi-continuous way as
L
varies in
V\cap{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)
, for all strata
V
of the partition. The argument used to conclude is then the same as in
[
32
]
: one writes
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathit{IH}^{i}=\sum_{a}\dim\prescript{}{F}{E}_{1}^{a,i-a}
and when restricted to a subset of
V\cap{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)
where
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathit{IH}^{i}
is constant, the terms of the sum have to stay constant, by upper-semicontinuity, therefore proving the claim on
V\cap{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)
, which in turn proves the theorem. To identify the
E_{1}
-page we will use the results in
[
34
]
. Note also that in the present case of the complement of a normal crossing divisor in a smooth variety, we have
\mathit{IH}^{\bullet}(X,L)\cong H^{\bullet}(X,j_{*}L)
(see e.g. [
loc. cit.
, Proposition 5.4]).

Let
D:=X\setminus U
and write
D=\cup_{i\in I}D_{i}
where
D_{i}
are the irreducible components. For all
i\in I
, let
D^{*}_{i}:=D_{i}\setminus\cup_{j\neq i}(D_{i}\cap D_{j})
and, for all subsets
J\subset I
, define
D_{J}:=\cup_{j\in J}D_{j}
and
U_{J}:=U\cup\bigcup_{j\in J}D^{*}_{j}
.
For each local system
L\in{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(U,1)({\mathbb{C}})
there corresponds a subset
J\subset I
such that
L
has trivial monodromy around
D_{j},j\in J
and non-trivial monodromy around
D_{i},i\notin J
. We can partition
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(U,1)
as

{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(U,1)=\bigsqcup_{J\subset I}V_{J},

where
V_{J}
is the set of local systems with trivial monodromy around
D_{j}
,
j\in J
and nontrivial monodromy around
D_{i},i\notin J
. Clearly
V_{J}
is an open (possibly empty) subset of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(U_{J},1)({\mathbb{C}})
and it belongs to the Boolean algebra generated by torsion-translated subtori of
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{B}}(U,1)
.
Fix
L\in V_{J}\cap{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)
. By
[
34
, Theorem 5.1.(a)]
, we have
\prescript{}{F}{E}_{1}^{p,q}=H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}(\log D_{I\setminus J})\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{>-1})
, where
{\mathcal{L}}^{>-1}
is the Deligne extension of
L
. (Note that in
loc. cit.
, certain sheaves denoted by
\widetilde{\Omega}^{p}_{X}({\mathcal{L}}^{>-1})
are introduced, but if the local system has no trivial monodromies around the boundary divisor then they reduce to the usual twisted sheaves of log-forms.)
In
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U_{J},1)
, choose the logarithmic connection
({\mathcal{L}}^{>-1},\nabla)
corresponding to the Deligne extension of
L
. There exists a neighborhood
{\mathcal{B}}
of
L
in
V_{J}^{\textnormal{an}}
and a neighborhood
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
of
({\mathcal{L}}^{>-1},\nabla)
in
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U_{J},1)^{\textnormal{an}}
with
RH^{\textnormal{an}}({\mathcal{B}}^{\prime})={\mathcal{B}}
and such that, if
(E,\nabla)\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
then
(E,\nabla)
is the Deligne extension of
RH^{\textnormal{an}}(E,\nabla)
. Indeed, as a logarithmic connection
(E,\nabla)
varies in
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U_{J},1)
, its vector of residues around the divisor
D_{I\setminus J}
varies continuously (in fact, algebraically).
As
(E,\nabla)
varies in
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
,
\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}(\log D_{I\setminus J})\otimes E)
varies upper-semicontinuously.
Therefore, as
L
varies in
{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)
the terms
\dim\prescript{}{F}{E}_{1}^{p,q}
vary upper-semicontinuously.
As explained in the first paragraph, this implies that as
L
varies in
{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)\cap S^{p+q}_{k}
, for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, the terms
\prescript{}{F}{E}_{1}^{p,q}
have constant dimension.
The partition of
V_{J}\cap{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)
defined by the dimensions of
Gr_{F}^{p}\mathit{IH}^{p+q}
consists of locally closed real-analytic subvarieties and the above argument proves that around each point there is a small neighborhood such that said partition coincides with the one induced by the dimensions of
\mathit{IH}^{p+q}
. The latter partition is contained in the Boolean algebra generated by torsion-translated real subtori, by Theorem
2.5
.
∎

In the notation of the previous theorem, the same arguments prove that the stratification of
{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)
given by
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\textnormal{Gr}}^{F}_{p}H^{p+q}(U,L)
belongs to
{\mathcal{A}}
. This implies for example
[

Remark 5.2
.

Let
j:U\hookrightarrow X
as in Theorem
5.1
(2). It is unclear to us whether the jumping loci of
mixed
Hodge numbers of
H^{\bullet}(U,L)
determine torsion-translated real subtori
{\mathbf{U}}(U,1)
. Let
h^{p,q,k}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Gr_{p}^{F}Gr_{W}^{p+q}H^{k}(U,L)
. The spectral sequence corresponding to the weight filtration degenerates at
E_{2}
and it is endowed with a Hodge filtration
F
. The number
h^{p,q,k}
is equal to the dimension of
Gr^{F}_{p}
applied to the middle cohomology of the complex

{}_{W}E_{1}^{-m-1,k+m}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}~_{W}E_{1}^{-m,k+m}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}~_{W}E_{1}^{-m+1,k+m},

where
m=p+q
. By strictness of the differentials with respect to
F
, one can invert the operations of taking cohomology and applying
Gr^{F}
. The terms of the spectral sequence are described in
[
34
, Proposition 1.7 (b)]
and
h^{p,q,k}
is the dimension of the middle cohomology of

\displaystyle Gr^{F}_{p}H^{k-m-2}(D^{(m+1)},\Omega_{D^{(m+1)}}^{\bullet}\otimes{\mathcal{M}}_{m+1})

\displaystyle\to\quad

\displaystyle Gr^{F}_{p}H^{k-m}(D^{(m)},\Omega_{D^{(m)}}^{\bullet}\otimes{\mathcal{M}}_{m})

\displaystyle\to\quad

\displaystyle Gr^{F}_{p}H^{k-m+2}(D^{(m-1)},\Omega_{D^{(m-1)}}^{\bullet}\otimes{\mathcal{M}}_{m-1})

as
L
varies: here
D^{(n)}
denotes the normalization of the
n
-fold intersections of the
D_{i}
’s and
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
is the canonical extension of
j_{*}L|_{D^{(n)}\setminus D^{(n+1)}}
. The graded pieces are in turn identified with the cohomology over
D^{(n)}
,
n=m-1,m,m+1
, of certain
coherent
sheaves depending on
L
: their dimension therefore varies in an upper-semicontinuous way but we are unable to control the dimension of the cohomology of the maps between them.

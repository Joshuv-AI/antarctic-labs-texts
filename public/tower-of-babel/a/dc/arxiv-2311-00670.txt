On convex integration solutions to the surface quasi-geostrophic equation driven by generic additive noise
Florian Bechtold, Theresa Lange, Jörn Wichmann

Abstract
. We study the surface quasi-geostrophic equation driven by a generic additive noise process W W . By means of convex integration techniques, we establish existence of weak solutions whenever the stochastic convolution z z associated with W W is well defined and fulfills certain regularity constraints. Quintessentially, we show that the so constructed solutions to the non-linear equation are controlled by z z in a linear fashion. This allows us to deduce further properties of the so constructed solutions, without relying on structural probabilistic properties such as Gaussianity, Markovianity or a martingale property of the underlying noise W W .

In this work, we are concerned with the construction of solutions to the surface quasi-geostrophic equation (SQG equation) on the two-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}

1.2.
Stochastic convex integration results

After the advent of deterministic convex integration results (for instance see
[
BV20
]
and references therein), it became natural to ask if the techniques developed in this context carry over to corresponding stochastic problems in order to establish non-uniqueness results. With this background, Hofmanová, Zhu and Zhu were able to adapt convex integration methods to hydrodynamic equations with noise. Their first papers
[
HZZ23c
,
HZZ23a
,
HZZ23b
]
did so by working with stopping time techniques, which has the advantage of avoiding moment estimates along the convex integration iteration. For a gentle overview of these results, refer also to
[
HB22
]
. In this context, let us also mention the works of Yamazaki
[
Yam22a
,
Yam22
,
Yam23
]
and Schenke and Rehmeier
[
RS23
]
where in particular
[
Yam22b
]
treats the case of SQG equations with additive noise deriving existence and non-uniqueness in law of solutions of regularity
C_{t}C_{x}^{1/2+\iota}\cap C_{t}^{\eta}C_{x},\iota\in\left(0,1/2-\gamma/3\right),\eta\in(0,1/3]
. Establishing moment estimates poses a problem in all these works, as convex integration schemes typically involve super-linear estimates that need to be controlled through careful parameter-tuning. In particular, as the estimate of a given moment in iteration
n+1
thus requires higher and higher moment estimates on the previous levels, one is unable to close moment estimates.

In
[
CDZ22
]
, this issue was for the first time overcome by a moment-dependent parameter-tuning that allows to control the blow-up of higher and higher moments along the iteration scheme, exploiting the quantified moment behaviour of the underlying Gaussian noise. While this opened the door for the establishment of stationary solutions through a Krylov-Boguliobov type argument in
[
HZZ22
]
, the drawback of the approach consists in the fact that one can not simultaneously study different moments of a specific convex integration, as the scheme itself depends on the moment one intends to inverstigate in the beginning. Also, as the tuning depends on the exact moment behaviour of the underlying noise, extensions to different noises are not obvious.

Inspired by
[
CKL21a
]
, Hofmanová et al. study moment-based convex integration in the context of the SQG equations: for the case of only space dependent noise
[
HZZ23
]
and recently space-time white noise
[
HLZZ23
]
, they obtain infinitely many non-Gaussian weak solutions (of regularity
L^{p}_{\rm loc}(0,\infty);B_{\infty,1}^{-1/2})\cap C_{b}([0,\infty);B_{\infty,1}^{-1/2-})\cap C_{b}^{1}([0,\infty);B_{\infty,1}^{-3/2-})
for all
p\in[1,\infty)

[
HZZ23
]
, and of regularity
L^{p}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);B_{\infty,1}^{-1/2})\cap C([0,\infty),B_{\infty,1}^{-1/2-})\cap C^{1/2-}([0,\infty),B_{\infty,1}^{-1-})
for all
p\in[1,\infty)

[
HLZZ23
]
, respectively) as well as infinitely many stationary (ergodic) solutions. While one main focus of
[
HLZZ23
]
consists in demonstrating that convex integration schemes can serve to establish solutions to supercritical SPDEs inaccessible by tools such as regularity structures or paracontrolled distributions, the authors were able to also show that the convex integration scheme can be set up independently of a given moment fixed in the beginning. This allows them to show that arbitrary moments of the constructed convex integration solutions exist, provided this is the case for the initial condition. Further quantified moment bounds, however, are lacking. In particular, the questions of existence of exponential moments is left open and the consideration of more general noises is lacking.

Structure of the paper

3.
Convex integration

We want to construct a solution as the limit of an iterative algorithm, i.e.,

\displaystyle g=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{\leq n},\hskip 30.00005ptg_{\leq n}=g_{\leq n-1}+\tilde{g}_{n},

where
\tilde{g}_{n}
are constructed in an explicit manner.

At first, the iterates
g_{\leq n}
do not need to satisfy (
1.2
) exactly, but are allowed to carry a residual
\tilde{q}_{n}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}g_{\leq n}+\nabla^{\perp}(g_{\leq n}+z)\Lambda(g_{\leq n}+z)-\nu\Lambda^{\gamma-1}\nabla g_{\leq n}\mathring{\approx}\nabla\tilde{q}_{n}.

The main difficulty (that enables convex integration) is to find an explicit update rule
\tilde{g}_{n}
that corrects
g_{\leq n}
in a way such that the magnitude of the correction is proportional to the residual. Ultimately, we hope for concentration of
g_{\leq N}
on

and a vanishing residual.

Crucially, Cheng, Kwon and Li
[

found suitable building blocks to correct the iterates adapted to the SQG equation. They were modified to the stochastic setting by Hofmanová, Zhu and Zhu in
[

]
(see also

]

We split our convex integration strategy into the following steps:

(a)

(b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j/2}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}g_{n}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}=\sum_{j:5\lambda_{n}-\mu_{n}\leq 2^{j}\leq 5\lambda_{n}+\mu_{n}}2^{j/2}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}g_{n}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

In order to simplify the estimate we restrict to

\displaystyle\mu_{n}\leq\lambda_{n}.

Then

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j:5\lambda_{n}-\mu_{n}\leq 2^{j}\leq 5\lambda_{n}+\mu_{n}}2^{j/2}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}g_{n}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq(6\lambda_{n})^{1/2}\sum_{j:4\lambda_{n}\leq 2^{j}\leq 6\lambda_{n}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}g_{n}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{1/2}\left\lVert g_{n}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

Here we used that

\displaystyle\sum_{j:4\lambda_{n}\leq 2^{j}\leq 6\lambda_{n}}1\lesssim 1,

since
\#\{j:4\lambda_{n}\leq 2^{j}\leq 6\lambda_{n}\}=\#\{j:2+\log_{2}(\lambda_{n})\leq j\leq\log_{2}(6)+\log_{2}(\lambda_{n})\}
and
C^{0}\hookrightarrow B^{0}_{\infty,\infty}
(see for example
[
Tri83
, p.89]
). Moreover, due to stability of the frequency truncation and
\left\lVert q_{\ell_{n}}\right\rVert_{X}\leq\chi_{\ell_{n}}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert g_{n}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\lVert a_{n,j}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(t)}{5\lambda_{n}}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\lVert C_{0}+\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}\frac{q_{\ell_{n}}(t)}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(t)}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(t)}{\lambda_{n}}}\left(C_{0}+\frac{\left\lVert q_{\ell_{n}}(t)\right\rVert_{X}}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(t)}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(t)}{\lambda_{n}}}\left(C_{0}+1\right)^{1/2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\lVert g_{n}(t)\right\rVert_{B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\sqrt{(C_{0}+1)\chi_{\ell_{n}}(t)}.

Take the supremum over
t\in[s,s+1]
,
L^{2m}_{\omega}
-norm and supremum in
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert g_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim(C_{0}+1)\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[s,s+1]}\chi_{\ell_{n}}(t)^{m}\right]\right)^{1/2m}.

We restrict our setting to

(R2)

\displaystyle\ell_{n}\leq 1.

Then
[s-\ell_{n},s+1+\ell_{n}]\subset[s-1,s+2]=\cup_{k=0}^{2}[s-1+k,s+k]
and thus,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[s,s+1]}\chi_{\ell_{n}}(t)\leq\sup_{t\in[s-1,s+2]}\left\lVert q_{n-1}(t)\right\rVert_{X}+r_{n-1}.

Therefore,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\lVert g_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\sqrt{(C_{0}+1)(\left\lVert q_{n-1}\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}X}+r_{n-1})}.

Similarly,

3.1.4.
Hölder scale

Let
s\neq s^{\prime}\in[t,t+1]
. Analogously to previous section, one finds for increments

\displaystyle\left\lVert g_{n}(s)-g_{n}(s^{\prime})\right\rVert_{B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\lVert a_{j,n}(s)-a_{j,n}(s^{\prime})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

Using
\left\lvert\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b}\right\rvert\leq\sqrt{\left\lvert a-b\right\rvert}
and the stability of frequency truncation

\displaystyle\left\lVert a_{j,n}(s)-a_{j,n}(s^{\prime})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{-1/2}\sqrt{\left\lvert\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s)-\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})\right\rvert}\left\lVert\sqrt{C_{0}+\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}\frac{q_{\ell_{n}}(s)}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s)}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{n}^{-1/2}\sqrt{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})}\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}\frac{q_{\ell_{n}}(s)}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s)}-\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}\frac{q_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}.

Notice that
\left\lVert\sqrt{C_{0}+\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}\frac{q_{\ell_{n}}(s)}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s)}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{C_{0}+1}
by definition of
\chi_{\ell_{n}}
. Moreover, recall (
R2
) and
\left\lvert s-s^{\prime}\right\rvert\leq 1
. Now, Young’s inequality and properties of convolution ensure

\displaystyle\left\lvert\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s)-\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})\right\rvert=\left\lvert\int\left\lVert q_{n-1}(\nu)\right\rVert_{X}(\psi_{\ell_{n}}(s-\nu)-\psi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime}-\nu))\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sup_{\nu\in[s-\ell_{n},s+\ell_{n}]\cup[s^{\prime}-\ell_{n},s^{\prime}+\ell_{n}]}\left\lVert q_{n-1}(\nu)\right\rVert_{X}\int\left\lvert\psi_{\ell_{n}}(s-\nu)-\psi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime}-\nu)\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sup_{\nu\in[t-1,t+3]}\left\lVert q_{n-1}(\nu)\right\rVert_{X}\left\lvert s-s^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2\alpha}\ell_{n}^{-2\alpha}.

Additionally,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}\frac{q_{\ell_{n}}(s)}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s)}-\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}\frac{q_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}=\frac{\left\lVert\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}q_{\ell_{n}}(s)-\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s)\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}q_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s)\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\frac{\left\lvert\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})-\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s)\right\rvert}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})}\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}q_{\ell_{n}}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s)}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}q_{\ell_{n}}(s)-\mathcal{R}_{j}^{o}q_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\frac{\left\lvert\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})-\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s)\right\rvert}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})}+\frac{\left\lVert q_{\ell_{n}}(s)-q_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})\right\rVert_{X}}{\chi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})}.

Similarly to the estimate for increments of
\chi_{\ell_{n}}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert q_{\ell_{n}}(s)-q_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime})\right\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\int q_{n-1}(\nu)(\psi_{\ell_{n}}(s-\nu)-\psi_{\ell_{n}}(s^{\prime}-\nu))\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\nu\in[t-1,t+3]}\left\lVert q_{n-1}(\nu)\right\rVert_{X}\left\lvert s-s^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2\alpha}\ell_{n}^{-2\alpha}.

Collecting all estimates shows

\displaystyle\left\lVert a_{j,n}(s)-a_{j,n}(s^{\prime})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left((C_{0}+1)\lambda_{n}^{-1}\ell_{n}^{-2\alpha}\left\lvert s-s^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2\alpha}\sup_{\nu\in[t-1,t+3]}\left\lVert q_{n-1}(\nu)\right\rVert_{X}\right)^{1/2}

and

(3.9)

\displaystyle{[{g_{n}}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\ell_{n}^{-\alpha}\sqrt{(C_{0}+1)\left\lVert q_{n-1}\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}X}}.

Similarly,

(3.10)

\displaystyle{[{g_{n}}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}C^{\beta}_{x}}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{\beta-1/2}\ell_{n}^{-\alpha}\sqrt{(C_{0}+1)\left\lVert q_{n-1}\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}X}}.

scale

Take the time derivative in (

Remark 6
.

We want to stress that Lemma
5
does not depend on the value
C_{\mathrm{start}}
nor the explicit value
C_{z}
. In particular, it is independent of
m\geq 1
as long as
C_{z}<\infty
.

Notice that the exponent
b
is purely determined by the
\alpha
-Hölder regularity in time of

, cf. (
3.23
). In other words, a time irregular stochastic term (
\alpha
small) forces us to introduce higher frequencies at a faster rate, i.e.,

increases rapidly.

We are ready to proof convergence of the iteration (

).

Theorem 7
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
\alpha>0
,
\kappa>1/2
,
\gamma<3/2
and
z\in L^{2m}_{\omega}\big(C_{\mathrm{loc}}C^{1+\kappa}_{x}\cap C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\alpha}C^{1+}_{x}\big)

Then there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta\in(0,1)
and

such that for all

(3.30)

\displaystyle\left\lVert q_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}X}

\displaystyle\leq r_{n},

(3.31)

\displaystyle\left\lVert g_{\leq n^{\prime}}-g_{\leq n}\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{r_{n}}.

In particular,
g=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{\leq n}\in L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}
exists, solves (
1.2
) and satisfies

(3.32)

\displaystyle\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{start}}}\left(\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}C^{1+\kappa}_{x}}+{[{z}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}C^{1+}_{x}}+1\right).

Proof.

We apply Lemma
5
for

from Proposition
4
and obtain a parameter configuration
(a,b,\beta)
such that (

), (
R2
), (
R3
) and (

) hold for all

. We can choose
(a,b,\beta)
such that (
3.22
) holds.

Now, we proceed via induction. First, we argue that it is possible to start the iteration. By (
3.1b
), the frequency locality of

and Lemma
23
, Poincaré’s and Hölder’s inequalities

\displaystyle\left\lVert q_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}X}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\Delta^{-1}\nabla\cdot[\nabla^{\perp}z_{0}\Lambda z_{0}]\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}X}

\displaystyle\lesssim\log(\lambda_{0})\left\lVert\Delta^{-1}\nabla\cdot[\nabla^{\perp}z_{0}\Lambda z_{0}]\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\log(\lambda_{0})\left\lVert\nabla^{\perp}z_{0}\Lambda z_{0}]\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\log(\lambda_{0})\left\lVert z_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}C^{1+}_{x}}^{2}

such that

.

Since Lemma
5
guarantees that the assumptions of Proposition
4
are satisfied, the induction step follows and we have shown (
3.30
).

Next, we verify (
3.31
). Notice that, due to (
3.2a
) for arbitrary
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert g_{\leq k+1}-g_{\leq k}\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\ell_{k+1}^{\alpha}{[{g_{\leq k}}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}+\left\lVert g_{k+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}.

Therefore, using (

\displaystyle\left\lVert g_{\leq k+1}-g_{\leq k}\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\ell_{k+1}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{S}_{k,\alpha}+\sqrt{r_{k}}.

Recall (
3.21
) and (
3.22
). This allows to bound

\displaystyle\ell_{k+1}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{S}_{k,\alpha}\leq\sqrt{r_{k+1}}a^{b^{k}(b\beta/2-\alpha(b-1))}4^{k+1}\leq\sqrt{r_{k+1}}\leq\sqrt{r_{k}}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\left\lVert g_{\leq n^{\prime}}-g_{\leq n}\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{r_{n}}\sum_{k=n}^{n^{\prime}-1}a^{-b^{n}\beta(b^{k-n}-1)}\lesssim\sqrt{r_{n}},

) and satisfies (

Multiply by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\langle-\nabla q_{n}-\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}g_{\leq n}-\nu\Lambda^{\gamma-1}\nabla g_{\leq n},\nabla\xi\rangle\xi_{\omega}\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{E}\left[\langle-\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}g-\nu\Lambda^{\gamma-1}\nabla g,\nabla\xi\rangle\xi_{\omega}\right].

Using Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\nabla^{\perp}(g_{\leq n}+z_{n})\Lambda(g_{\leq n}+z_{n})-\nabla^{\perp}(g+z)\Lambda(g+z),\nabla\xi\rangle\xi_{\omega}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert(g_{\leq n}+z_{n})-(g+z)\right\rVert_{C_{\mathrm{loc}}\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{t}\dot{H}^{4}}\left\lvert\xi_{\omega}\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\left\lVert(g_{\leq n}+z_{n})-(g+z)\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{t}\dot{H}^{4}}\left\lVert\xi_{\omega}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{\omega}}.

Thus, as
\xi_{\omega}
was arbitrary, we have verified that
g
satisfies (
1.3
).

The estimate (
3.32
) follows from (
3.31
) by noticing that
g_{\leq 0}=0
.

∎

In Theorem
7
we traced the concentration speed of
g_{\leq n}
in
L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}
as it is sufficient for the identification of the non-linearity. However, the explicit structure of the solution allows to read of improved regularity based on the parameter choice.

Corollary 8

(Regularity)
.

Let the framework of Theorem
7
be satisfied. Moreover, let
(a,b,\beta,C_{\mathrm{start}})
be given by Theorem
7
and
g
denote the corresponding solution.
Let
\vartheta_{t},\vartheta_{x}\in[0,1)
satisfy
\vartheta_{t}+\vartheta_{x}<1/2+\beta/(2b)
and either

(a)

(high spatial regularity)

(3.33)

\displaystyle\vartheta_{x}\geq\frac{1}{2}-\alpha+\frac{\beta}{2b}\quad\text{and}\quad\vartheta_{t}<\alpha-\frac{1}{b}\left(\vartheta_{x}-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha+\frac{\beta}{2b}\right)\right),

(b)

(high temporal regularity) or

(3.34)

\displaystyle\vartheta_{x}<\frac{1}{2}-\alpha+\frac{\beta}{2b}\quad\text{and}\quad\vartheta_{t}<\alpha.

Then
g\in L^{2m}_{\omega}C^{\vartheta_{t}}_{\mathrm{loc}}C^{\vartheta_{x}}
with

(3.35)

\displaystyle{[{g}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\vartheta_{t}}C^{\vartheta_{x}}}\lesssim\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{start}}}\left(\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}C^{1+\kappa}_{x}}+{[{z}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}C^{1+}_{x}}+1\right).

Proof.

Let
(a,b,\beta,C_{\mathrm{start}})
be a parameter configuration given by Theorem
7
and
g
its corresponding convex integration solution. In particular, it holds
\left\lVert q_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}X}\leq r_{n}
for all
n
. Then, using (
3.2a
) and (
3.10
)

\displaystyle{[{g_{\leq N+1}-g_{\leq N}}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\vartheta_{t}}C^{\vartheta_{x}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell_{N+1}^{\alpha-\vartheta_{t}}{[{g_{\leq N}}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\alpha}C^{\vartheta_{x}}}+{[{g_{N+1}}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\vartheta_{t}}C^{\vartheta_{x}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell_{N+1}^{\alpha-\vartheta_{t}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}4^{N-n}{[{g_{n}}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\alpha}C^{\vartheta_{x}}}+{[{g_{N+1}}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\vartheta_{t}}C^{\vartheta_{x}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell_{N+1}^{\alpha-\vartheta_{t}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}4^{N-n}\lambda_{n}^{\vartheta_{x}-1/2}\ell_{n}^{-\alpha}r_{n-1}^{1/2}+\lambda_{N+1}^{\vartheta_{x}-1/2}\ell_{N+1}^{-\vartheta_{t}}r_{N}^{1/2}.

Since
\vartheta_{t}+\vartheta_{x}<1/2+\beta/(2b)
the second term is controlled

\displaystyle\lambda_{N+1}^{\vartheta_{x}-1/2}\ell_{N+1}^{-\vartheta_{t}}r_{N}^{1/2}=a^{b^{N}(b(\vartheta_{t}+\vartheta_{x}-1/2)-\beta/2)}r_{0}^{1/2}.

We distinguish two cases for the estimate of the first term.

First let us consider
\vartheta_{x}\geq 1/2-\alpha+\beta/(2b)\Leftrightarrow b(\vartheta_{x}+\alpha-1/2)-\beta/2\geq 0
. Then estimating the super exponential by the last summand and bounding the geometric series

\displaystyle\ell_{N+1}^{\alpha-\vartheta_{t}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}4^{N-n}\lambda_{n}^{\vartheta_{x}-1/2}\ell_{n}^{-\alpha}r_{n-1}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=r_{0}^{1/2}a^{\beta/2}a^{b^{N+1}(\vartheta_{t}-\alpha)}\sum_{n=1}^{N}4^{N-n}a^{b^{n-1}(b(\vartheta_{x}+\alpha-1/2)-\beta/2)}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq r_{0}^{1/2}a^{\beta/2}a^{b^{N-1}(b^{2}(\vartheta_{t}-\alpha)+b(\vartheta_{x}+\alpha-1/2)-\beta/2)}4^{N}.

In order for the exponent to have a sign we need

\displaystyle b^{2}(\vartheta_{t}-\alpha)+b(\vartheta_{x}+\alpha-1/2)-\beta/2<0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\vartheta_{t}<\alpha+\frac{\beta-2b(\vartheta_{x}+\alpha-1/2)}{2b^{2}}.

Thus, if
\vartheta_{t}
and
\vartheta_{x}
satisfy (
3.33
) we can estimate

\displaystyle{[{g_{\leq N+1}-g_{\leq N}}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\vartheta_{t}}C^{\vartheta_{x}}}\lesssim r_{0}^{1/2}a^{-N\varsigma_{1}},

where
-\varsigma_{1}=\max\{b(\vartheta_{t}+\vartheta_{x}-1/2)-\beta/2,\,b^{2}(\vartheta_{t}-\alpha)+b(\vartheta_{x}+\alpha-1/2)-\beta/2\}
.

Now, let us consider the second case, i.e.,
\vartheta_{x}<1/2-\alpha+\beta/(2b)
. In this situation we can just neglect the super exponential in the sum and derive

\displaystyle\ell_{N+1}^{\alpha-\vartheta_{t}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}4^{N-n}\lambda_{n}^{\vartheta_{x}-1/2}\ell_{n}^{-\alpha}r_{n-1}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=r_{0}^{1/2}a^{\beta/2}a^{b^{N+1}(\vartheta_{t}-\alpha)}\sum_{n=1}^{N}4^{N-n}a^{b^{n-1}(b(\vartheta_{x}+\alpha-1/2)-\beta/2)}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq r_{0}^{1/2}a^{\beta/2}a^{b^{N+1}(\vartheta_{t}-\alpha)}4^{N}.

Using (
3.34
) it follows

\displaystyle{[{g_{\leq N+1}-g_{\leq N}}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\vartheta_{t}}C^{\vartheta_{x}}}\lesssim r_{0}^{1/2}a^{-N\varsigma_{2}},

where
-\varsigma_{2}=\max\{b(\vartheta_{t}+\vartheta_{x}-1/2)-\beta/2,\,\vartheta_{t}-\alpha\}
.

The assertion (
3.35
) follows, using the summability of
a^{-n\varsigma}
(applied for
\varsigma\in\{\varsigma_{1},\varsigma_{2}\}
) and
g_{\leq 0}=0
,

\displaystyle{[{g}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\vartheta_{t}}C^{\vartheta_{x}}}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{[{g_{\leq n+1}-g_{\leq n}}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\vartheta_{t}}C^{\vartheta_{x}}}\lesssim r_{0}^{1/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{-n\varsigma}\lesssim r_{0}^{1/2}.

∎

Remark 9
.

If we choose
\vartheta_{t}=0
, we can pick
\vartheta_{x}\approx 1/2+\beta/(2b)>1/2
. On the other hand, if we choose
\vartheta_{x}=1/2
, we can take
\vartheta_{t}\approx\min\{\alpha,\beta/(2b)\}>0
. Therefore,
g
enjoys improved regularity in the sense that

(3.36)

\displaystyle g\in L^{2m}_{\omega}\left(C_{\mathrm{loc}}C^{1/2+\beta/(2b)-}_{x}\cap C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\min\{\alpha,\beta/(2b)\}-}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}\right).

4.
Examples of noises
W

As seen in Theorem
7
and more precisely in (
3.32
), all our convex integration scheme requires is the finiteness of the term

(4.1)

\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\mathrm{loc}}C^{1+\kappa}_{x}}+{[{z}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}C^{1+}_{x}}

for some
\kappa>1/2
and
\gamma<3/2
and
\alpha>0
. Recalling that

z_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\nu(t-s)\Lambda^{\gamma}}\Lambda^{\delta-1}{\rm d}W_{s}

we provide some examples of noises
W
and according constraints on the parameter
\delta
which assure that (
4.1
) is finite. For the sake of conciseness, we set the viscosity
\nu=1
in this section.

4.1.
Cylindrical Wiener noise

The canonical example is to take
W
to be a cylindrical Wiener process. Let us stress that in our setting, the parameter
\delta
will always be negative. The process
z
should be accordingly thought of as the stochastic convolution of the colored noise
\tilde{W}:=\Lambda^{\delta-1}W
. We cite the following result from
[
HLZZ23
]
on the regularity of the stochastic convolution in this setting.

Lemma 10

(Regularity of stochastic convolution)
.

Let
W
be a cylindrical Wiener noise on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}).
Let
\gamma\in(0,3/2)
,
\kappa>1/2
and
\delta<(-1-\kappa+\gamma/2)\wedge-1
. Then for all
m\geq 1
and
\alpha<1/2
there exists
C>1
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle{[{z}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C^{\alpha}_{t}C^{1+}_{x}}+\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{t}C^{1+\kappa}_{x}}\leq C<\infty.

Proof.

Refer to
[
HLZZ23
, Proposition 4.1]
.
∎

Note that in this case,
z
is a Gaussian random variable. As a direct consequence, from (
3.32
) we conclude that the moments of our convex integration solution
g
are sub-Gaussian. Indeed, by (
3.32
) we obtain

\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{\rm loc}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim{[{z}]}_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C^{\alpha}_{t}C^{1+}_{x}}+\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{L^{2m}_{\omega}C_{t}C^{1+\kappa}_{x}}\lesssim C(2m)^{1/2}

where in the last step, we exploited that also the Hölder modulus of continuity of a Gaussian process admits sub-Gaussian moments (see for example
[
HV08
]
). In particular, this quantified moment bound in
m
implies that
g
admits square expontential moments, i.e. there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp{\varepsilon\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{C_{\rm loc}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}^{2}}\right]<\infty,

which is a result not available in the setting of
[
HLZZ23
]
.

4.2.
Noises constructed via Young integrals

In this section, we show how to construct the stochastic convolution
z
for a given noise
W
via sewing techniques. The latter constrains us to work on a compact time interval
[0,T]
, fixed throughout this subsection. We are basically able to cover the class of processes which admit some temporal Hölder regularity, provided the spatial coloring is strong enough, i.e.
\delta
sufficiently negative. Let
(\beta^{k})_{k}
be a sequence of independent copies of a stochastic process
\beta
such that

(4.3)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sup_{s\neq t\in[0,T]}\frac{|\beta_{t}-\beta_{s}|}{|t-s|^{H}}\right)^{2m}\right]<\infty.

For
(e_{k})_{k}
an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, let us consider a noise of the form

W_{t}=\sum_{|k|=0}^{\infty}e_{k}\beta^{k}_{t}.

We then have the following

Lemma 11
.

Let
W
be as above. Suppose
\gamma<3/2
and
\kappa>1/2
and
H\in(0,1)
. Suppose moreover
\delta<-2-(\gamma\vee\kappa+\gamma(1-H))
. Then

{[{z}]}_{L^{2}_{\omega}C^{H}_{[0,T]}C^{1+}_{x}}+\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\omega}C_{[0,T]}C^{1+\kappa}_{x}}\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[[\beta]^{2m}_{C^{H}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2m}}

Example 12
.

A canonical example for
\beta
in the above would be a
H+\varepsilon
-fractional Brownian for
H\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily small. Indeed, in this case (
4.3
) is finite and moreover also enjoys sub-Gaussian moment explosion. Similar to the above case of cylindrical Wiener noise, this implies that our constructed convex integration solution
g
enjoys square exponential moments in the sense that for some
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp{\varepsilon^{\prime}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{C_{[0,T]}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}}^{2}}\right]<\infty.

We stress that the construction is feasible for all
H\in(0,1)
, as the integrand of the stochastic convolution is smooth in time.

Remark 13
.

Note that in the case
H=1/2
, which recovers the case of cylindrical Wiener noise, the condition in the above lemma is worse than that given in Lemma
10
. Besides the fact that we loose optimality due to Sobolev embeddings, this would still be natural to expect, as the sewing arguments are entirely pathwise and thus do not capture any stochastic cancellations.

Proof of Lemma
11
.

Let
(e_{k})_{k}
be the sequence of eigenvectors of
\Lambda
and
(\lambda_{k})_{k}
be the corresponding sequence of eigenvalues. Note that
\lambda_{k}\simeq|k|
. We begin by showing that the expression

(4.4)

\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta_{r}

is well defined as the sewing of the germ
A^{t}_{s,r}=e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-s)}(\beta_{r}-\beta_{s})
. For the reader unfamiliar with sewing techniques and the corresponding terminologies, we refer to section
A.3
in the appendix. Let us point out that
A^{t}:\Delta_{2}([0,t))\to\mathbb{R}
is indeed a well defined object for any
t\in[0,T]
, since - contrary to Volterra settings - no singularities in
t\in[0,T]
are present. Using the Schauder estimate Lemma
27
, we note that
0<\varepsilon<H

|(\delta A^{t})_{s,u,r}|=|(\beta_{r}-\beta_{u})e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}t}(e^{\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}s}-e^{\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}u})|\lesssim[\beta]_{C^{H}}|r-u|^{H}(\lambda_{k}^{\gamma})^{1-H+\varepsilon}|u-s|^{1-H+\varepsilon}.

By the Sewing Lemma
26
, we therefore obtain the existence of an associated integral
(\mathcal{I}A^{t})
, which we use to define (
4.4
)

(\mathcal{I}A^{t})_{t}=:\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta_{r}.

Using the a priori bound (
A.4
) that comes with the Sewing Lemma
26
, we have

|(\mathcal{I}A^{t})_{t}-(\mathcal{I}A^{t})_{s}|\lesssim|t-s|^{H}[\beta]_{C^{H}}+|t-s|^{1+\varepsilon}[\beta]_{C^{H}}(\lambda_{k}^{\gamma})^{1-H+\varepsilon}.

With the definition of the one dimensional stochastic convolution at hand, we define

z_{t}:=\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{\delta-1}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-s)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{s}e_{k}

We note that

\begin{split}\left\lVert z_{t}-z_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}&=\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{2}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}\left(\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}(e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-s)}-1)^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{2}\end{split}

We can now use the above estimate to deduce that

\left|\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right|=|(\mathcal{I}A^{t})_{t}-(\mathcal{I}A^{t})_{s}|\lesssim|t-s|^{H}[\beta^{k}]_{C^{H}}+|t-s|^{1+\varepsilon}[\beta^{k}]_{C^{H}}(\lambda_{k}^{\gamma})^{1-H+\varepsilon}

and

\left|\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right|=|(\mathcal{I}A^{s})_{s}-(\mathcal{I}A^{s})_{0}|\lesssim T^{H}[\beta^{k}]_{C^{H}}+T^{1+\varepsilon}[\beta^{k}]_{C^{H}}(\lambda_{k}^{\gamma})^{1-H+\varepsilon}.

We therefore conclude that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}\left(\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[[\beta]_{C^{H}}^{2}\right]|t-s|^{2H}\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}(\lambda_{k}^{\gamma})^{2(1-H+\varepsilon)},\end{split}

where the above sum converges provided

(4.5)

2(\delta-1)+2\sigma+2\gamma(1-H+\varepsilon)<-2.

Concerning the second sum, we obtain again thanks to Schauder estimates
|e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-s)}-1|\lesssim\lambda_{k}^{\gamma H}|t-s|^{H}
(Lemma
27
) and thus

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}(e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-s)}-1)^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[[\beta]_{C^{H}}^{2}\right]\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}(\lambda_{k}^{\gamma})^{2H+2(1-H+\varepsilon)}|t-s|^{2H}\end{split}

where the above sum converges provided

(4.6)

2(\delta-1)+2\sigma+2\gamma(1+\varepsilon)<-2.

Noting that (
4.6
) implies (
4.5
), we can conclude that
z\in L^{2}_{\omega}C^{H}_{t}H^{\sigma}_{x}
provided (
4.6
) holds. Using the Sobolev embedding
H^{\sigma}\hookrightarrow C^{1+\varepsilon}
for
\sigma>2
and
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we have
z\in L^{2}_{\omega}C^{H}_{t}C^{1+\varepsilon}_{x}
. Concerning the other regularity claim on
z
, note that it suffices to ask for arbitrarily small Hölder regularity, meaning that in this case we may use the Schauder estimate
|e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-s)}-1|\lesssim\lambda_{k}^{\gamma\varepsilon}|t-s|^{\varepsilon}
, which lets (
4.6
) collapse to (
4.5
). We therefore obtain that
z\in L^{2}_{\omega}C^{\varepsilon}_{t}H^{\sigma}_{x}
for some
\varepsilon>0
, provided (
4.5
) holds. Using again the Sobolev embedding
H^{\sigma}\hookrightarrow C^{1+\kappa+\varepsilon}
for
\sigma>2+\kappa
, we obtain the claim.
∎

4.3.
Another class of general stochastic processes

We give another example of a class of stochastic processes
W
that assures the existence of a stochastic convolution
z
and that can assure the finiteness of (
4.1
). Contrary to the previous subsection, we will provide a construction in spirit of that of the Itô integral (i.e. in particular not a pathwise one), yet going beyond the martingale and Gaussian setting.

Let
\beta:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a stochastic process which starts in zero, is continuous and centered and has independent increments. We moreover assume that

(4.7)

\mbox{Var}(\beta_{t}-\beta_{s})=(t-s),\qquad\mathbb{E}[|\beta_{t}-\beta_{s}|^{4}]\leq C|t-s|^{1+\upsilon}

for some
\upsilon\in(0,1]
. Note that the above are obviously satisfied for
\beta
being a Brownian motion with
\upsilon=1
, however
\beta
need not be a Brownian motion or even a Gaussian process (if it was a Gaussian process, the above conditions would imply that
\beta
is a Brownian motion).
We consider again

W_{t}=\sum_{k}e_{k}\beta^{k}_{t}

where
(e_{k})_{k}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
(\beta^{k})_{k}
are independent copies of
\beta
, which we describe below. In the case of deterministic integrands which we are concerned with, the above conditions on
\beta
suffice to establish an analogue of the Paley-Wiener integral by means of an isometry. Time regularity of the so obtained integral can then be recovered by means of the Kolmogorov continuity theorem (which is why we require a fourth moment assumption).

Lemma 14
.

Let
f:[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}
be a deterministic elementary function meaning for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq t_{1}<\dots t_{N}\leq t
and
c_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}c_{k}\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}.

Suppose
\beta
as above, i.e. a continuous centered process starting in zero satisfying (
4.7
). Then we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}f_{s}{\rm d}\beta_{s}\right)^{2}=\int_{0}^{t}f_{s}^{2}{\rm d}s

Proof.

The proof follows from a direct computation as in the case of the Itô isometry, exploiting that the coefficients
(c_{k})_{k\leq N}
are deterministic.
∎

As the elementary functions are dense in
L^{2}([0,t])
, we can use the above isometry to define the Paley-Wiener integral for
f\in L^{2}([0,t])
. Indeed, if
f\in L^{2}([0,t])
, we find a sequence of elementary function
(f^{n})_{n}
such that
\left\lVert f^{n}-f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\to 0
. Thus

\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}f^{n}_{s}-f^{m}_{s}{\rm d}\beta_{s}\right)^{2}=\int_{0}^{t}(f^{n}_{s}-f^{m}_{s})^{2}{\rm d}s

i.e. the
\left(\int_{0}^{t}f^{n}_{s}{\rm d}\beta_{s}\right)
is Cauchy in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, meaning we obtain a limit object for which the above isometry carries over. In particular, we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}{\rm d}\beta_{s}\right)^{2}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\lambda(t-s)}{\rm d}s=\frac{1}{2\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda t}).

Moreover, we can use the estimate on the fourth moment in (
4.7
) to deduce the existence of a Hölder continuous modification of the integral process via Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem.

Lemma 15
.

Let
f:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded. We have

\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}{\rm d}\beta_{r}\right)^{4}\leq 6\left(\int_{s}^{t}f^{2}_{r}{\rm d}r\right)^{2}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}^{4-2(1+\upsilon)}\left(\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}^{2}{\rm d}r\right)^{1+\upsilon}.

Consequently, the process
X_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}f_{s}{\rm d}\beta_{s}
admits a
\frac{\upsilon}{4}
-Hölder continuous modification.

Proof.

By density, we may argue again through approximation via elementary functions: let

f^{N}_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}(t).

We have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{s}^{t}f^{N}_{r}{\rm d}\beta_{r}\right)^{4}\\
&=\sum_{k,j,n,m=1}^{N}c_{k}c_{j}c_{n}c_{m}\mathbb{E}(\beta_{t_{k+1}}-\beta_{t_{k}})(\beta_{t_{j+1}}-\beta_{t_{j}})(\beta_{t_{n+1}}-\beta_{t_{n}})(\beta_{t_{m+1}}-\beta_{t_{m}}).\end{split}

Observe that due to the independent increments and centeredness, the above expectation vanishes whenever all four running indices
k,j,n,m
are distinct and whenever three of them coincide but one is distinct. We are therefore left with

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{s}^{t}f^{N}_{r}{\rm d}\beta_{r}\right)^{4}&=6\sum_{k\neq j}^{N}c_{k}^{2}c_{j}^{2}\mathbb{E}(\beta_{t_{k+1}}-\beta_{t_{k}})^{2}(\beta_{t_{j+1}}-\beta_{t_{j}})^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}^{4}\mathbb{E}(\beta_{t_{k+1}}-\beta_{t_{k}})^{4}\\
&\leq 6\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}^{2}(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\sum_{j=1}^{N}c_{j}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})+\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}^{4}(t_{k+1}-t_{k})^{1+\upsilon}\\
&\leq 6\left(\int_{s}^{t}(f_{r}^{N})^{2}dr\right)^{2}+\left\lVert f^{N}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{4-2(1+\upsilon)}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(c_{k}^{2}(t_{k+1}-t_{k}))^{1+\upsilon}\\
&\leq 6\left(\int_{s}^{t}(f_{r}^{N})^{2}dr\right)^{2}+\left\lVert f^{N}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{4-2(1+\upsilon)}\left(\int_{s}^{t}(f_{r}^{N})^{2}dr\right)^{1+\upsilon}.\end{split}

We then conclude by density and Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem.
∎

From the above proof, it is clear that the statement extends to deterministic bounded functions depending also on the upper integration variable, that is for
f:\Delta_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
bounded deterministic, we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{s}^{t}f(t,r){\rm d}\beta_{r}\right)^{4}\leq 6\left(\int_{s}^{t}f^{2}(t,r){\rm d}r\right)^{2}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}^{4-2(1+\upsilon)}\left(\int_{s}^{t}f^{2}(t,r){\rm d}r\right)^{1+\upsilon}.

Consequently,
|\int_{s}^{t}f(t,r){\rm d}\beta_{r}|\lesssim|t-s|^{\upsilon/4}

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely. This essentially gives us Hölder regularity of the stochastic integral. To conclude that the stochastic convolution is Hölder, we need to also obtain some Hölder regularity from the semigroup, for which we use again the Schauder estimate of Lemma
27
. Indeed, for
z_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-a(t-s)}{\rm d}\beta_{s}
we have

z_{t}-z_{s}=\int_{s}^{t}e^{-a(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta_{r}+\int_{0}^{s}(e^{-a(t-r)}-e^{-a(s-r)}){\rm d}\beta_{s}

and therefore using Lemma
15
and the Schauder estimate
27
(using it once with
\theta=(1+\upsilon)/4
and once with
\theta=1/2
), we obtain

(4.8)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}|z_{t}-z_{s}|^{4}&\lesssim(t-s)^{2}+(t-s)^{1+\upsilon}\\
&+\left(\int_{0}^{s}(e^{-a(t-r)}-e^{-a(s-r)})^{2}{\rm d}r\right)^{2}+\left(\int_{0}^{s}(e^{-a(t-r)}-e^{-a(s-r)})^{2}{\rm d}r\right)^{1+\upsilon}\\
&\lesssim(t-s)^{2}+(t-s)^{1+\upsilon}+a^{(1+\upsilon)}(t-s)^{(1+\upsilon)}.\end{split}

Hence, we can apply the Kolmogorov continuity theorem to obtain the existence of a continuous modification. In the infinite dimensional setting, we need to again make sure that we stay summable along Fourier modes but the calculation is essentially similar.

Lemma 16
.

Let
\beta:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a centered, continuous stochastic process starting in zero satisfying (
4.7
). Consider as noise

W_{t}=\sum_{k}e_{k}\beta^{k}_{t}

where
(e_{k})
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
of eigenvectors of
\Lambda
and
(\beta^{k})_{k}
are independent copies of
\beta
. Then the process

(4.9)

z_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\Lambda^{\gamma}(t-s)}\Lambda^{\delta-1}{\rm d}W_{s}=\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-s)}\lambda_{k}^{\delta-1}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{s}e_{k}

is well defined and we have for any
\varepsilon>0

z\in L^{4}_{\omega}C^{\upsilon/4-\varepsilon}_{[0,T]}H^{\sigma}_{x}

provided the parameters satisfy

4(\delta-1)+4\sigma+(1+\upsilon)\gamma<-2.

Using again Sobolev embeddings, the above implies:

Corollary 17
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,3/2)
and
\kappa>1/2
. Suppose that
\upsilon
as above and

\delta<-3/2-(1+\upsilon)\gamma/4-\kappa.

Then the process
z
in (
4.9
) satisfies
z\in L^{4}_{\omega}C^{\upsilon/4-\varepsilon}_{[0,T]}C^{1+\kappa}_{x}
.

Proof of Lemma
16
.

Note that

\begin{split}\left\lVert z_{t}-z_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}=\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{2}\end{split}

and thus

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left\lVert z_{t}-z_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}^{4}\\
&=\sum_{k\neq j}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{2}\\
&\qquad\times\lambda_{j}^{2(\delta-1)}|j|^{2\sigma}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{j}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{j}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{j}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{j}_{r}\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{4(\delta-1)}|k|^{4\sigma}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{4}\\
&\lesssim\left(\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{2}\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{4(\delta-1)}|k|^{4\sigma}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{4}\end{split}

Using (
4.8
), we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{4(\delta-1)}|k|^{4\sigma}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{4}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{4(\delta-1)}|k|^{4\sigma}\left(|t-s|^{2}+\lambda_{k}^{\gamma(1+\upsilon)}(t-s)^{1+\upsilon}\right).\end{split}

The above sum converges if

(4.10)

4(\delta-1)+4\sigma+(1+\upsilon)\gamma<-2

in which case the above sum is bounded by

\left|\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{4(\delta-1)}|k|^{4\sigma}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{4}\right|\lesssim|t-s|^{1+\upsilon}.

Concerning the second sum, note that we can bound similarly using again (
4.8
)

\begin{split}&\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{2}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{4}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}\left(|t-s|^{2}+\lambda_{k}^{\gamma(1+\upsilon)}(t-s)^{1+\upsilon}\right)^{1/2}.\end{split}

Under condition (
4.10
), the above sum converges in which case we obtain

\begin{split}\left(\sum_{|k|=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\delta-1)}|k|^{2\sigma}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(t-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}(s-r)}{\rm d}\beta^{k}_{r}\right)^{2}\right)^{2}\lesssim|t-s|^{1+\upsilon}.\end{split}

Overall, this shows

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert z_{t}-z_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}^{4}\lesssim|t-s|^{1+\upsilon}

and thus, we may conclude by Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem.
∎

5.5.
Stationary (ergodic) solutions

Stationary solutions satisfy the equation on the full time horizon
\mathbb{R}
hence in order to study the existence of such solutions in the above setting, we must be able to extend the convex integration results to
\mathbb{R}
as well. Hence in this subsection we can only treat the case of Section

which allows for the same extension procedure as described in
[
HLZZ23
, Section 8]
. Analogously we use Theorem

to start in

and apply the convex integration mechanism of Section

z_{t}=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\Lambda^{\gamma}}\Lambda^{\delta-1}{\rm d}W_{s}

to construct infinitely many (ergodic) stationary solutions. Indeed: due to lack of uniqueness, we understand stationarity as invariance with respect to time shifts

S_{t}(\theta,W)(\cdot):=(\theta(\cdot+t),W(\cdot+t)-W(t)),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},(\theta,W)\in\mathcal{T},

on the trajectory space
\mathcal{T}:=C\left(\mathbb{R},B_{p,1}^{-1/2}\right)\times C\left(\mathbb{R},B_{p,p}^{-1-}\right)
for some

.

In analogy to
[

, Definition 8.1]
we hence define stationary solutions
((\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P}),\theta,W)
as analytically weak solutions of (
1.1
) in the sense of Lemma
25
on
(-\infty,\infty)
such that

{\rm Law}(S_{t}(\theta,W))={\rm Law}(\theta,W)\quad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Similarly we may define a stationary solution
((\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P}),\theta,W)
as ergodic (cf.
[
HLZZ23
, Definition 8.4]
) if

{\rm Law}(\theta,W)(A)\in\{0,1\}\quad\text{for all }A\subset\mathcal{T}\text{ Borel and shift invariant}.

In analogy to
[
HLZZ23
, Theorem 8.2, Corollary 8.3, Theorem 8.5]
and
[
HZZ23
, Theorem 4.2]
) we may therefore deduce

Theorem 22
.

(a)

Each convex integration solution
\theta
obtained in Theorem
19
with
\theta_{0}=0
and extended to
\mathbb{R}
gives rise to a stationary solution
((\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}),\tilde{\mathbb{P}}),\tilde{\theta},\tilde{W})

(b)

(c)

C\geq 1
such that for all
g,g^{\prime}\in H^{1/2}
and
\xi\in H^{4}
it holds

(A.2)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\langle\Lambda g,\nabla^{\perp}g^{\prime}\cdot\nabla\xi\rangle\right\rvert\leq C\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\left\lVert g^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{4}}.

Proof.

Notice that

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda g,\nabla^{\perp}g^{\prime}\cdot\nabla\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}\left\lvert k\right\rvert l\cdot k^{\perp}\widehat{g}(k)\widehat{g^{\prime}}(l)\widehat{\xi}(k-l)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq 0}\sum_{l\neq 0,k}\left\lvert k\right\rvert l\cdot k^{\perp}\widehat{g}(k)\widehat{g^{\prime}}(l)\widehat{\xi}(k-l).

Hölder’s inequality shows

\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq 0}\sum_{l\neq 0,k}\left\lvert k\right\rvert l\cdot k^{\perp}\widehat{g}(k)\widehat{g^{\prime}}(l)\widehat{\xi}(k-l)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\left(\sum_{k\neq 0}\left\lvert k\right\rvert\left\lvert\widehat{g}(k)\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{k\neq 0}\left\lvert\sum_{l\neq 0,k}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{1/2}l\cdot k^{\perp}\widehat{g^{\prime}}(l)\widehat{\xi}(k-l)\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Therefore it remains to verify

(A.3)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\neq 0}\left\lvert\sum_{l\neq 0,k}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{1/2}l\cdot k^{\perp}\widehat{g^{\prime}}(l)\widehat{\xi}(k-l)\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\left\lVert g^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{4}}.

We distinguish two cases.

(a)

If
\left\lvert l\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert
, then
\left\lvert k\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert
and

\displaystyle\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{1/2}l\cdot k^{\perp}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{3/2}\left\lvert l\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert l\right\rvert^{1/2}.

(b)

If
\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert\ll\left\lvert l\right\rvert
, then
\left\lvert k\right\rvert\sim\left\lvert l\right\rvert
and

\displaystyle\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{1/2}l\cdot k^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{1/2}(k-l)\cdot k^{\perp}\leq\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{3/2}\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert<\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{3/2}\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lvert l\right\rvert^{1/2}.

Split up the interior sum of the left hand side of (
A.3
) into the two cases and estimate separately. For fixed
k
by Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{l\neq 0,k\,\&\left\lvert l\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert}l\cdot k^{\perp}\widehat{g^{\prime}}(l)\widehat{\xi}(k-l)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\sum_{l\neq 0,k\&\left\lvert l\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert}\left\lvert l\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lvert\widehat{g^{\prime}}(l)\right\rvert\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\widehat{\xi}(k-l)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\left(\sum_{l\neq 0,k\&\left\lvert l\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert}\left\lvert l\right\rvert\left\lvert\widehat{g^{\prime}}(l)\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{l\neq 0,k\&\left\lvert l\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert}\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert\widehat{\xi}(k-l)\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The second case can be treated analogously,

\displaystyle\sum_{l\neq 0,k\,\&\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert\ll\left\lvert l\right\rvert}l\cdot k^{\perp}\widehat{g^{\prime}}(l)\widehat{\xi}(k-l)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\sum_{l\neq 0,k\,\&\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert\ll\left\lvert l\right\rvert}\left\lvert l\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lvert\widehat{g^{\prime}}(l)\right\rvert\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{3/2}\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lvert\widehat{\xi}(k-l)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\left(\sum_{l\neq 0,k\,\&\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert\ll\left\lvert l\right\rvert}\left\lvert l\right\rvert\left\lvert\widehat{g^{\prime}}(l)\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{l\neq 0,k\,\&\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert\ll\left\lvert l\right\rvert}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{3}\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert\left\lvert\widehat{\xi}(k-l)\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Lastly, we need to bound

\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq 0}\sum_{l\neq 0,k\&\left\lvert l\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert}\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert\widehat{\xi}(k-l)\right\rvert^{2}\lesssim\sum_{k\neq 0}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{8}\left\lvert\widehat{\xi}(k)\right\rvert^{2}=\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{4}}^{2}.

A change of variables
(k,l)\mapsto(k^{\prime},l^{\prime})=(k,k-l)
reveals a similar bound for the second case. Indeed,
\left\lvert l^{\prime}\right\rvert=\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert\ll\left\lvert l\right\rvert\sim\left\lvert k\right\rvert=\left\lvert k^{\prime}\right\rvert
and, therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq 0}\sum_{l\neq 0,k\,\&\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert\ll\left\lvert l\right\rvert}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{3}\left\lvert k-l\right\rvert\left\lvert\widehat{\xi}(k-l)\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq 0}\sum_{l^{\prime}\neq 0,k^{\prime}\,\&\left\lvert l^{\prime}\right\rvert\ll\left\lvert k^{\prime}\right\rvert}\left\lvert k^{\prime}\right\rvert^{3}\left\lvert l^{\prime}\right\rvert\left\lvert\widehat{\xi}(l^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\lesssim\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq 0}\sum_{l^{\prime}\neq 0,k^{\prime}\,\&\left\lvert l^{\prime}\right\rvert\ll\left\lvert k^{\prime}\right\rvert}\left\lvert k^{\prime}\right\rvert^{-4}\left\lvert l^{\prime}\right\rvert^{8}\left\lvert\widehat{\xi}(l^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{4}}^{2}.

∎

A.2.
Transformation of SQG equations

We have the following

Lemma 25
.

Let
g\in L^{2m}C_{\rm loc}B^{1/2}_{\infty,1}
be a weak solution to (
1.3
). Let

z_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\nu(t-s)\Lambda^{\gamma}}\Lambda^{\delta-1}{\rm d}W_{s}

be well defined and enjoy the regularity (
1.4
). Then
\theta:=\Lambda(g+z)
is a weak solution to (
1.1
) in the sense that

\langle\theta_{t},\xi\rangle-\langle\theta_{0},\xi\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{R}^{\perp}\theta_{s}\cdot\nabla\theta_{s},\xi\rangle ds=-\nu\int_{0}^{t}\langle\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta_{s},\xi\rangle ds+\langle\Lambda^{\delta}(W_{t}-W_{0}),\xi\rangle

for some
\theta_{0}\in H^{1/2}
and any
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
where the non-linearity is understood by means of the identity

\langle\mathcal{R}^{\perp}\theta\cdot\nabla\theta,\xi\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\theta,[\mathcal{R}^{\perp}\cdot,\nabla\xi]\theta\rangle

Proof.

As already mentioned prior to Lemma
24
it was already shown in
[
CKL21
, Proposition 5.1]
that the commutator
[\mathcal{R}^{\perp}\cdot,\nabla\xi]
extends to a linear map
[\mathcal{R}^{\perp}\cdot,\nabla\xi]:\dot{H}^{-1/2}\to\dot{H}^{1/2}
. Hence,
\theta:=\Lambda(g+z)

∎

A.3.
The Sewing Lemma

We recall the Sewing Lemma due to
[
Gub04
]
(see also
[
FH20
, Lemma 4.2]
). Let
E
be a Banach space,
[0,T]
a given interval. Let
\Delta_{n}
denote the
n
-th simplex of
[0,T]
, i.e.
\Delta_{n}:\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})|0\leq t_{1}\dots\leq t_{n}\leq T\}
. For a function
A:\Delta_{2}\to E
define the mapping
\delta A:\Delta_{3}\to E
via

(\delta A)_{s,u,t}:=A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t}

Provided
A_{t,t}=0
we say that for
\alpha,\beta>0
we have
A\in C^{\alpha,\beta}_{2}(E)
if
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha,\beta}<\infty
where

\displaystyle\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{(s,t)\in\Delta_{2}}\frac{\left\lVert A_{s,t}\right\rVert_{E}}{|t-s|^{\alpha}},\qquad\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\beta}:=\sup_{(s,u,t)\in\Delta_{3}}\frac{\left\lVert(\delta A)_{s,u,t}\right\rVert_{E}}{|t-s|^{\beta}},

\displaystyle\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle:=\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha}+\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\beta}.

For a function
f:[0,T]\to E
, we note
f_{s,t}:=f_{t}-f_{s}

Moreover, if for any sequence
(\mathcal{P}^{n}([s,t]))_{n}
of partitions of
[s,t]
whose mesh size goes to zero, the quantity

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}^{n}([s,t])}A_{u,v}

converges to the same limit, we note

(\mathcal{I}A)_{s,t}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}^{n}([s,t])}A_{u,v}.

Lemma 26

(Sewing)
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1<\beta
. Then for any
A\in C^{\alpha,\beta}_{2}(E)
,
(\mathcal{I}A)
is well defined. Moreover, denoting
(\mathcal{I}A)_{t}:=(\mathcal{I}A)_{0,t}
, we have
(\mathcal{I}A)\in C^{\alpha}([0,T],E)
and
(\mathcal{I}A)_{0}=0
and for some constant
c>0
depending only on
\beta
we have

(A.4)

\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A)_{t}-(\mathcal{I}A)_{s}-A_{s,t}\right\rVert_{E}\leq c\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\beta}|t-s|^{\beta}.

We say the germ
A
admits a sewing
(\mathcal{I}A)
and call
\mathcal{I}
the sewing operator.

Lemma 27

(’Schauder estimate’)
.

We have for
a\geq 0
and
\theta\in[0,1]
and
0\leq s\leq t

(e^{-at}-e^{-as})\lesssim a^{\theta}(t-s)^{\theta}.

Proof.

The proof follows from interpolating the bounds

|e^{-at}-e^{-as}|\leq 2,\qquad|e^{-at}-e^{-as}|=|\int_{as}^{at}-e^{-x}{\rm d}x|\leq a(t-s).

∎

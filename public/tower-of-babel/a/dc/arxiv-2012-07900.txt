On the number of generators of an algebra over a commutative ring Thanks: The second and third authors have been partially supported by Discovery Grants from the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada.
Uriya A. First, Zinovy Reichstein, Ben Williams

Abstract
. A theorem of O. Forster says that if R R is a noetherian ring of Krull dimension d d , then every projective R R -module of rank n n can be generated by d + n d+n elements. S. Chase and R. Swan subsequently showed that this bound is sharp: there exist examples that cannot be generated by fewer than d + n d+n elements. We view rank- n n projective R R -modules as R R -forms of the non-unital R R -algebra R n R^{n} where the product of any two elements is 0 0 . The first two authors generalized Forster’s theorem to forms of other algebras (not necessarily commutative, associative or unital); A. Shukla and the third author then showed that this generalized Forster bound is optimal for étale algebras. In this paper, we prove new upper and lower bounds on the number of generators of an R R -form of a k k -algebra, where k k is an infinite field and R R is a finitely generated k k -ring of Krull dimension d d . In particular, we show that, contrary to expectations, for most types of algebras, the generalized Forster bound is far from optimal. Our results are particularly detailed in the case of Azumaya algebras. Our proofs are based on reinterpreting the problem as a question about approximating the classifying stack B ​ G BG , where G G is the automorphism group of the algebra in question, by algebraic spaces of a certain type.

3.
Preliminaries on schemes of generators and non-generators

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional (multi)algebra over a field

. Recall from Section
2
that we denote the affine space of ordered
r
-tuples of elements in

by

, the closed subscheme of
r
-tuples not generating

by

and the open subscheme of
r
-tuples of generators by

. In this section, we will discuss some basic properties of these schemes. We write

for

.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a
k
-ring (not necessarily of finite type). Then

U_{r}(R)
is the set of
r
-tuples
(b_{1},\dots,b_{r})\in V_{r}(R)=A_{R}^{r}
that generate
A_{R}
as an
R
-algebra.

Proof.

Let
\overline{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{r})\in V_{r}(R)
and let
t:\operatorname{Spec}R\to V_{r}
be the corresponding morphism.

Suppose that
\overline{b}
generates
A_{R}
. Let
P\in\operatorname{Spec}R
, let
A(P)=A\otimes_{k}\mathrm{Frac}(R/P)

and let
b_{i}(P)
denote the image of
b_{i}
in
A(P)
. Then
\overline{b}(P):=(b_{1}(P),\dots,b_{r}(P))
generates
A(P)
. By
the construction of
Z_{r}
in Section
2
, there is
\overline{w}\in(W_{r})^{n}

such that
f_{\overline{w}}(\overline{b}(P))\neq 0
in
\mathrm{Frac}(R/P)
. This means that the image
of
t
does not meet the
P
-fibre of

. As this holds for all

, the image of

is contained

and

.

Conversely, assume
\overline{b}\in U_{r}(R)
. Then for every

, we have

. This means that there is
\overline{w}\in(W_{r})^{n}

such that
f_{\overline{w}}(\overline{b}(P))\neq 0
, hence
\overline{b}(P)
generates

. This holds for all

, Lemma 2.1]

generates

.
∎

Recall from Section
2
that the group scheme

acts on

from the left, and the action restricts to

and

.

Lemma 3.2
.

The action of

on

is scheme-theoretically free, i.e.,
the morphism

(3.1)

\Phi:G\times U_{r}\longrightarrow U_{r}\times U_{r},

given by
\Phi(g,(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}))=((g(a_{1}),\ldots,g(a_{r})),(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}))
is a closed embedding.

Proof.

By
[
Gro64
, Cor. 18.12.6]
,
showing that
\Phi
is a closed embedding is equivalent to showing that
\Phi
is a proper monomorphism.
To see that
\Phi
is a monomorphism, it suffices to test that

(3.2)

\Phi(R):G(R)\times U_{r}(R)\to U_{r}(R)\times U_{r}(R)

is injective for every
k
-ring

. Here, thanks to Lemma

,

is the set of all
r
-tuples
(b_{1},\dots,b_{r})
of generators for
A_{R}
.
The action of

on

is free because

fixes

if
and only if

fixes the

-algebra generated by the

, i.e., the entire algebra

.
The element

fixes the entire algebra

if and only if

is the identity automorphism.
It follows that for any

, the map

It remains to show that

is a proper map. We use the valuative criterion for properness. Suppose

is a
valuation ring over

with fraction field

, and let
\varphi
,
\psi
be morphisms as in the following
commutative diagram. We need to show the existence of the dotted arrow.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\times U_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{r}\times U_{r}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

We remark that

. The arrow

, with

be an algebraic closure of

(a)

\dim Z_{r}\leqslant n_{\max}r+n_{\max}(n-n_{\max})

9.
A new proof of the generalized Forster bound

As yet another application of Theorem
6.1
we will now
give a short proof of the following variant of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 9.1
.

Assume that
k
is infinite field,
A
is a finite-dimensional
k
-(multi)algebra,

R
is a finite type
k
-ring of Krull dimension
d
, and
B
is an
R
-form of
A
. Then

\operatorname{gen}_{R}(B)\leqslant d+\operatorname{gen}_{k}(A)
.

Our proof will rely on the following.

Proposition 9.2
.

c_{A}(r+1)\geqslant c_{A}(r)+1
whenever
r\geqslant\operatorname{gen}_{k}(A)-1
.

As a consequence of Proposition
9.2
, we see that
c_{A}(r)\geqslant r-\operatorname{gen}_{k}(A)+1
for any
r\geqslant\operatorname{gen}_{k}(A)
. Setting
r=d+\operatorname{gen}_{k}(A)
, we can rewrite this as
c_{A}(r)>d
.
Theorem
6.1
now tells us that
\operatorname{gen}_{R}(B)\leqslant r=d+\operatorname{gen}_{k}(A)
, and Theorem
9.1
follows.

In the course of proving Proposition
9.2
we may assume without loss of generality that

k
is algebraically closed. It is then harmless to replace
V_{r}
and
Z_{r}
by their sets of
k
-points endowed with the Zariski topology,
and this will be our convention in this section.

We say that an

-tuple
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\in A^{r}

almost generates

if there exists

a_{r+1}\in A
such that
a_{1},\dots,a_{r},a_{r+1}
generates
A
.
Denote the set of almost generating
r
-tuples in

by

.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
\psi:V_{r+1}\to V_{r}
denote projection to the first
r
coordinates.

(a) Let
\overline{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\in Z_{r}
. Then

\dim\big(\psi^{-1}(\overline{a})\cap Z_{r+1}\big)<\dim A

if and only if
\overline{a}\in U^{\prime}_{r}
.

(b)

U_{r}^{\prime}
is Zariski open in
V_{r}
.

(c) If
r\geqslant\operatorname{gen}_{k}(A)-1
, then
Z_{r}\cap U^{\prime}_{r}
is dense in
Z_{r}
.

Proof.

(a) is obvious from the definition of
U_{r}^{\prime}
.
(b) holds because
\psi
is flat and finitely presentated,
hence open, and
U^{\prime}_{r}=\psi(U_{r+1})
.

(c) It is enough to show that if there exists
\overline{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\in Z_{r}-\overline{Z_{r}\cap U^{\prime}_{r}}
,
then
r<\operatorname{gen}_{k}(A)-1
, or equivalently, that
U^{\prime}_{r}=\emptyset
. To prove this assertion, we will show by induction on
i=0,1,\dots,r
that the affine
space

(9.1)

L_{i}:=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{i},a_{i+1},\dots,a_{r})\,|\,x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1}\in A\}
is contained in
Z_{r}-U^{\prime}_{r}
.

For
i=r
, (
9.1
) tells us that
\emptyset=L_{r}\cap U_{r}^{\prime}=V_{r}\cap U_{r}^{\prime}=U_{r}^{\prime}
.
In other words,

, and part (c) follows.

The base case, where
i=0
, is clear. For the induction step, we assume
assume that
L_{i}\subseteq Z_{r}-U^{\prime}_{r}
for some
i\in\{0,\dots,r-1\}
.
This means that for every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in A
,
(x_{1},\dots,x_{i},a_{i+1},\dots,a_{r})\notin U^{\prime}_{r}
.
Consequently, for every
x_{i+1}\in A
, the
r+1
elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1},a_{i+1},\dots,a_{r}

do not generate
A
as an
k
-algebra, and, in particular,
(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1},a_{i+2},\dots,a_{r})\in Z_{r}
.
In other words,
L_{i+1}\subseteq Z_{r}
. Our goal is to show that
L_{i+1}\cap U^{\prime}_{r}=\emptyset
. Assume the contrary.
Then by part (b),
L_{i+1}\cap U^{\prime}_{r}
is Zariski dense in

. In particular,

\overline{a}
lies in the Zariski closure of
Z_{r}\cap U_{r}^{\prime}
, contradiction
our assumption that
\overline{a}\notin Z_{r}-\overline{Z_{r}\cap U^{\prime}_{r}}
. This contradiction tells us that

L_{i+1}\cap U^{\prime}_{r}=\emptyset
, completing the proof of (
9.1
) and thus of part (c).
∎

Proof of Proposition
9.2
.

By Lemma
9.3
the fibre of

\psi:Z_{r+1}\to Z_{r}
over every point of a dense open subset

of

is of dimension

.
Hence,

.
∎

.

Our proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
will rely on topological methods. These methods work best
if the base field
k
is equipped with an embedding
k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. To extend our arguments to an arbitrary base
field
k
of characteristic

, we will repeatedly use the following version of the Lefschetz principle.

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
R
be a
k
-ring, and
B
be an
R
-algebra. Suppose

is finitely generated as an
R
-module.
For any field extension
K/k
, set
R_{K}=R\otimes_{k}K
and
B_{K}=B\otimes_{R}R_{K}
. Then

(a)

\operatorname{gen}_{R}(B)\geqslant\operatorname{gen}_{R_{K}}(B_{K})
.

(b)

Moreover, there exists a subextension
k\subset F\subset K
such that
F/k
is finitely generated and

\operatorname{gen}_{R_{F}}(B_{F})=\operatorname{gen}_{R_{K}}(B_{K})
.

(c)

Suppose
k
is a finitely generated extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. If
\operatorname{gen}_{R_{K}}(B_{K})<r
for some field
K
containing
k
, then

\operatorname{gen}_{R_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{\mathbb{C}})<r.

Proof.

(a)
is obvious: if
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
generate
A
as an
R
-algebra, then
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
generate
B_{K}
as an
R_{K}
-algebra.

(b)
Suppose
\operatorname{gen}_{R_{K}}(B_{K})=r
. Choose
r

b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\in B_{K}
that generate
B_{K}
as
an
R_{K}
-algebra. We claim that these same elements will generate
B_{F}
as an
R_{F}
-algebra for some extension

such that
F
is finitely generated over
k
. In particular, the claim tells us that

\operatorname{gen}_{R_{F}}(B_{F})\leqslant r
. The opposite inequality is given by part
(a)
, so if we can prove this claim, then
part
(b)
will follow.

To prove the claim, choose elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}
that generate
B
as an
R
-module and
write each element

as

b_{i}=r_{i1}a_{1}+\ldots+r_{id}a_{d}.

for some coefficients
r_{ij}\in R_{K}
. Each
r_{ij}
lies in

for some intermediate subfield

such that

is finitely generated over

k
. After replacing

by the compositum of

in
K
(which is still finitely generated over
k
), we may
assume without loss of generality that
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}
lie in
B
.

Since
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}
generate
B_{K}
as an
R_{K}
-algebra, we can write each
a_{i}
as

for some monomials
M_{ij}
in
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}
and some
coefficients
s_{ij}\in R_{K}
. Once again, each
s_{ij}
lies in some intermediate subfield
E_{ij}
finitely generated
over
k
. Setting

to be the compositum of

in
K
, we see that the
R_{F}
-subalgebra of

generated by

contains
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}
. This shows that
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}
generate
B_{F}
as an

R_{F}
-algebra. Hence,
\operatorname{gen}_{R_{F}}(B_{F})\leqslant r
, as desired.

(c)
Choose
F
as in part
(b)
, so that
\operatorname{gen}_{R_{F}}(B_{F})<r
. Since
F
is a finitely generated extension of

\mathbb{Q}
, it is isomorphic (over
k
) to a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
. Thus we may assume without loss of generality
that

. By part
(a)
,

\operatorname{gen}_{R_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{\mathbb{C}})\leqslant\operatorname{gen}_{R_{F}}(B_{F})=\operatorname{gen}_{R_{K}}(B_{K})<r,

as desired.
∎

11.
Equivariant cohomology of
U_{r}

Suppose the field
k
is embedded in
\mathbb{C}
, so that a
k
-variety

gives rise to a complex analytic space

. The
purpose of this section is to calculate some of the
\operatorname{PGL}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant cohomology of the variety
U_{r}(\mathbb{C})
. We
begin with two short subsections that cover preliminary material.

We write
\mathrm{H}^{*}(X)
for the singular cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{*}(X(\mathbb{C});\mathbb{Z})

The Affine Lefschetz Theorem

We use singular cohomology, rather than some other cohomology theory of varieties, because of the following theorem.

Theorem 11.1

(Affine Lefschetz Hyperplane Theorem)
.

Let
k\subset\mathbb{C}
be a field with a chosen complex embedding, let
\mathbb{A}^{N}
be an affine space and

X\subset\mathbb{A}^{N}
be a smooth closed subvariety of dimension
d
. Then there exists a smooth affine hyperplane section
Y\subseteq X
defined over
k
such that
\dim Y=d-1
and
\mathrm{H}^{i}(X)\to\mathrm{H}^{i}(Y)
is an isomorphism if
i<d-1
and an injection if
i=d-1
.

Here, a hyperplane section is the scheme-theoretic intersection of
X
with a hyperplane in the ambient

\mathbb{A}^{N}
.

When
k=\mathbb{C}

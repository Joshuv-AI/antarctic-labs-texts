Generalised Hardy type and Rellich type inequalities on the Heisenberg group
Abimbola Abolarinwa, Michael Ruzhansky

Abstract
. This paper is primarily devoted to a class of interpolation inequalities of Hardy and Rellich types on the Heisenberg group ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} . Consequently, several weighted Hardy type, Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle and Hardy-Rellich type inqualities are established on ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} . Moreover, new weighted Sobolev type embeddings are derived. Finally, an integral inequality for vector fields in a domain of the Heisenberg group is obtained, leading to several specific weighted Hardy type inequalities by making careful choices of vector fields.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

1.1.
Introduction

Dated back to the works of G. H. Hardy (1877–1947), F. Rellich (1906–1955) and W. Heisenberg (1901–1976), Hardy type, Hardy-Rellich and uncertainty principle inequalities have found numerous applications in Mathematical analysis, theory of partial differential equations, physics, quantum mechanics, etc. See the books and monographs
[
22
,
29
,
31
,
33
]
for details, and also
[
5
,
10
,
12
,
19
]
and the references therein. Owing to their usefulness, these classes of inequalities have undergone series of extensions and refinements in the past decades. The interest of this present paper is on a class of interpolation inequalities of Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (CKN) type on the Heisenberg group. Explicit derivations of various forms of Hardy, Hardy-Rellich and uncertainty principle type inequalities follow consequently.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space and
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a smooth function. The CKN inequality
[
6
]
states that there exists a constant
C_{K}>0
such that

\displaystyle\||x|^{c}f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{K}\||x|^{a}\nabla f\|^{\delta}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\||x|^{b}f\|^{(1-\delta)}_{{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}},

(1.1)

where
|x|
is the Euclidean norm and
r>0
,
p,q\geq 1
,
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta\in(0,1]
satisfy

\frac{1}{r}+\frac{c}{n},\ \frac{1}{p}+\frac{a}{n},\ \frac{1}{q}+\frac{b}{n}>0,\ c=\delta\sigma+(1-\delta)b

if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}+\frac{c}{n}=\delta\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{a-1}{n}\right)+(1-\delta)\left(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{b}{n}\right)

with
0\leq\delta-\sigma\ \ \text{if}\ \delta>0
and

a-\sigma\leq 1\ \ \text{if}\ \frac{1}{r}+\frac{c}{n}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{a-1}{n}
.

If
a=0
,
\delta=1
and
r=p
are chosen, (
1.1
) is the Hardy inequality, while it is the
L^{2}
-Sobolev inequality if
a=0
,
\delta=1
,
p=2
and
r=2n/(n-2)
. Other interesting and useful inequalities contained in (
1.1
) are Gagliardo-Nirenberg, Hardy-Sobolev, Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl (HPW), Nash inequalities and so on.
The proof of (
1.1
) is more involved and has been generalised to some other settings such as Riemannian manifolds, homogeneous groups, hyperbolic spaces, geodesic spheres, see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
11
,
26
,
30
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
and the references therein for details. By the application of integration by parts and elementary algebraic inequality, D. G. Costa obtained new and short proofs for the
L^{2}
-version of CKN inequalities with sharp constants. In
[
8
]
, he proved (Hardy type) that

\displaystyle C_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(1.2)

for
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
,
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
C_{H}:=|n-(a+b+1)|/2
is sharp, and
\nabla
is the Euclidean gradient operator. The possible values of
a
and
b
which are required in determining the exact values of the optimal constants and the functions that achieve them are discussed in
[
7
]
. While in
[
9
]
, he proved (Hardy-Rellich type) that

\displaystyle C_{R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta f|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(1.3)

for
a+b+3\leq n
and
C_{R}:=|n+a+b-1|/2
is sharp and
\Delta
is the Euclidean Laplacian.

Note that (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) contain as special cases various forms of inequalities (Hardy, HPW, Rellich types). This can be seen by choosing different values for
a
and
b
. Later on Di, Jang, Shen and Jin
[
13
]
extended (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) to the general
L^{p}
case (
1<p<\infty
) by a more direct method and they obtained

\displaystyle C_{H,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f|^{p}}{|x|^{ap}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{|x|^{bq}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

(1.4)

where
1<p,q<\infty
,
1/p+1/q=1
and the constant
C_{H,p}=|n-(a+b+1)|/p
is sharp, and

\displaystyle C_{R,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f|^{p}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta_{p}f|^{p}}{|x|^{ap}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f|^{p}}{|x|^{bq}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(1.5)

with
\frac{p-n}{p-1}\leq\beta:=a+b+1
,
C_{R,p}:=(n+\beta(p-1)-p)/p
and
\Delta_{p}
is the usual
p
-Laplacian.

There have been few literature extending these results into domains where Euclidean distance is not available. For example, Abolarinwa, Rauf and Yin
[
3
]
recently obtained versions of (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) on the unit
n
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, using geodesic distance
r_{q}
from a fixed point
q\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, replacing
|x|
with
|\tan r_{q}|
. Their inequalities have sharp constants with remainder terms. Wei and Li
[
36
]
extended (
1.4
) into Riemannian manifolds using Hessian comparison theorem via construction of certain vector fields involving radial vector fields.

Let
L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
D^{1,2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
H^{1}_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
H^{2}_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the completions of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
respectively with the weighted Sobolev type norms

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{|x|^{2\alpha}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{1}_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}+\frac{|f|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}\right)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{D^{1,2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{|x|^{2\alpha}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
and
\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{2}_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{|\Delta f|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}+\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}\right)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Then another consequences of the above equations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are the following continuous weighted Sobolev type embeddings
[
8
,
9
]
:

\displaystyle H^{1}_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{2}_{(a+b+a)/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\displaystyle D^{2,2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset D^{1,2}_{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\displaystyle H^{2}_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset D^{1,2}_{(a+b+1)/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The main aim of this paper is to extend (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) (resp. (
1.4
) and (
1.5
)) to the general
L^{p}
-version (
1<p<\infty
) in the setting of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
using a more direct approach. Since
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a stratified nilpotent Lie group, some methods used in the Euclidean setting cannot be imported directly to derive interpolation inequalities. The approach adopted here involved horizontal (Heisenberg norm) distance function (see Section
2
). Moreover, new weighted continuous Sobolev type embeddings are constructed by using Costa’a approach (Section
3
).
In Section
4
, we obtain an integral inequality for vector fields in a domain of the Heisenberg group based on the integrability of vector fields and horizontal divergence theorem. Finally, some specific Hardy type inequalities are then rederived by making careful choices of vector fields.
The Heisenberg group is an important model to study many significant problems such as existence, continuation, eigenvalue problems, etc., so, obtaining vital integral inequalities such as those discussed in this paper become essential on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

1.2.
Basics of the Heisenberg group

Heisenberg group arises from many fields such as quantum physics, differential geometry, harmonic analysis. From the mathematical point of view, the Heisenberg group is the simplest and most important model in the theory of noncommutative vector fields satisfying Hörmander’s condition. Here below we give basics of the group but detailed description can be found in
[
16
,
33
]
.

Let
\xi=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
,
n\geq 1
. The set
\mathbb{H}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{2n}\oplus\mathbb{R}
equipped with the group law
\circ
,

\displaystyle\xi\circ\xi_{0}=\Big(x+x_{0},y+y_{0},t+t_{0}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{0j}\cdot y_{j}-x_{j}\cdot y_{0j})\Big),

(1.6)

where
x\cdot y
is the usual Euclidean inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is called the Heisenberg group of homogeneous dimension
Q=2n+2
. With the group law,
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a unimodular, connected and simply connected step two Lie group whose Haar measure is the Lebesgue measure,
d\xi=dxdydt
.
The distance between points
\xi
and
\xi_{0}
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(\xi,\xi_{0})=|\xi_{0}^{-1}\circ\xi|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}},

(1.7)

where
\xi_{0}^{-1}
is the inverse of
\xi_{0}
given by
\xi_{0}^{-1}=-\xi_{0}
with respect to the group law
\circ
. Let
z=(x,y)
,
\xi=(z,t)
and
\xi_{0}=(z_{0},t)
. By (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), one has

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(\xi,\xi_{0})=\left([(x-x_{0})^{2}+(y-y_{0})^{2}]^{2}+[t-t_{0}-2(x\cdot y_{0}-x_{0}\cdot y)]^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(1.8)

The Heisenberg distance function (also called the Heisenberg norm) is therefore defined by

d:=d_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(z,t)=(|z|^{4}+t^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}.

A basis for the Lie algebra of the left invariant vector fields on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is given by (
1\leq j\leq n
)

X_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+2y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \ Y_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}-2x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \ T=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

By the Lie bracket definition one can easily check that

[X_{j},X_{k}]=[Y_{j},Y_{k}]=[X_{j},T]=[Y_{j},T]=0,\ j,k=1,2\cdots n,

and

[X_{j},Y_{k}]=-4T\delta_{jk},

which constitute Heisenberg canonical commutation relation of quantum mechanics for position and momentum, whence the name Heisenberg group.

A family of dilations is defined by
\delta_{\lambda}(z,t)=(\lambda z,\lambda^{2}t),\lambda>0
. It is also easy to check that
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}},X_{j},Y_{j},(j=1,2\cdots n)
are homogeneous of degree
1
with respect to dilations.

The horizontal gradient, divergence and sub-Laplace operators on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are respectively defined by

\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f:=(X_{1}f,\cdots,X_{n}f,Y_{1}f,\cdots,Y_{n}f),\ \ \text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}w:=\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\cdot w\ \ \text{and}

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(X_{j}^{2}+Y_{j}^{2}\right)=\Delta_{z}+4|z|^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+4P\frac{\partial}{\partial t},

where
\Delta_{z}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y_{j}^{2}}\right)
,
|z|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}\right)
and
P:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\right)
.
For
p>1
, the
p
-sub-Laplacian is also defined on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
by

\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}f:=\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p-2}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f).

The Heisenberg group belongs to a class of stratified Lie groups (or homogeneous Carnot groups). It was shown by Folland
[
15
]
(see also
[
16
]
) that the sub-Laplacian on a general stratified Lie group has a unique fundamental solution
u_{\varepsilon}
in distributional sense, which in the Heisenberg setting means

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u_{\varepsilon}=\delta_{0},

where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac delta-distribution at the neutral element
0
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The function (called
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}
-gauge)

\displaystyle d(\xi):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{\frac{1}{2-Q}},&\text{for}\ \xi\neq 0,\\
\\
\displaystyle 0,&\text{for}\ \xi=0,\end{array}\right.

is a homogeneous quasi-norm on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, that is, it is a continuous function smooth away from the origin. The action of
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}
on
d
is given as

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}d=(Q-1)\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{2}}{d}\ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

We shall make use of the fact that Heisenberg is polarizable as a Carnot group
[
33
]
, such that the
\infty
-sup-Laplacian of
d
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{2},\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d\rangle=0\ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

(1.9)

A simple computation therefore gives the following identities in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
in explicit form:

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|

\displaystyle=|z|d^{-1},

(1.10)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}d

\displaystyle=(Q-1)|z|^{2}d^{-3},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}(|z|^{p-2})\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d

\displaystyle=(p-2)|z|^{p}d^{-3}.

(1.12)

Using these identities one can easily compute

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}\right)\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d=0.

(1.13)

Note that the standard Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the Haar measure for the homogeneous group, so we write
d\xi=dzdt
, the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

2.
Interpolation inequalities of Hardy and Rellich types

In this section we consider interpolation inequalities of Hardy and Rellich types.

2.1.
Sharp interpolation inequalities of Hardy type

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the Heisenberg group of homogeneous dimension
Q=2n+2
. Let
1<p<Q
and
\lambda=a+b
,
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that
Q\neq 1+\lambda
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{a,b,p}^{H}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d^{\lambda+1}}d\xi\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}}{d^{ap}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d^{bq}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(2.1)

for all
f\in D^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, with
D^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
being the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
with respect to the norm

\|f\|_{D^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}:=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}}{d^{ap}}d\xi,

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and
\mathscr{C}_{a,b,p}^{H}=|\frac{Q-1-\lambda}{p}|
is sharp.

Proof.

By a simple computation

\displaystyle\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}-\frac{\lambda|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{2}}{d^{\lambda+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(Q-1)}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{3}}-\frac{\lambda}{d^{\lambda+1}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}=\frac{(Q-1-\lambda)}{d^{\lambda+1}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}},

where we have used (
1.10
) and (
1.11
). In the case
Q=\lambda+1
, the inequality is trivial, so we assume that
Q\neq\lambda+1
. Applying the divergence theorem we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d^{\lambda+1}}d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Q-1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f|^{p}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\right)d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{Q-1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\left(|f|^{p}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}\right)\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}d\xi

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{-p}{Q-1-\lambda}\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f|^{p-2}f\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}d\xi,

where we have applied (
1.13
) into the second line to arrive at the last equality. On the other hand, application of Cauchy and Hölder inequalities gives

\displaystyle\frac{-p}{Q-1-\lambda}\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}

\displaystyle|f|^{p-2}f\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{p}{Q-1-\lambda}\right|\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f|^{p-1}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}d\xi

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{p}{Q-1-\lambda}\right|\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f|^{p-1}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{p-1}}{d^{p-1}}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{p}{Q-1-\lambda}\right|\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}}{d^{ap}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d^{(\lambda-a)\frac{p}{p-1}}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Inserting the last inequality into (
2.1
) leads to the required inequality (
2.1
).

To show the sharpness of the constant, we examine the equality condition in the Hölder inequality (see
[
30
]
). Define

\displaystyle g(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e^{-\frac{C}{\beta}d^{\beta}}:\hskip 28.45274pt\beta=a-\frac{b}{p-1}+1\neq 0\\
d^{-C}\ \ :\hskip 28.45274pt\beta=a-\frac{b}{p-1}+1=0,\end{array}\right.

where
C:=|\frac{Q-(\lambda+1)}{p}|
and
Q\neq\lambda+1
. Then it can be checked that

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{p}{Q-1-\lambda}\Big|^{p}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}g|^{p}}{d^{ap}}=\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|g|^{p}}{d^{b\frac{p}{p-1}}},

which satisfies the equality condition in the Hölder inequality. This shows that the constant
\mathscr{C}_{a,b,p}^{H}=|\frac{Q-1-\lambda}{p}|
is sharp.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the Heisenberg group of homogeneous dimension
Q=2n+2
. Let
1<p<Q
. Then for all
f\in D^{1,p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
the following weighted Hardy inequality

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Q-p}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d^{p}}d\xi\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi

(2.3)

holds with
(\frac{Q-p}{p})^{p}
being the best constant.

Furthermore, the following HPW uncertainty principle inequality

\displaystyle\frac{Q-p}{p}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d^{p}}d\xi\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{q}}{d^{q}}\frac{|f|^{q}}{d^{q}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(2.4)

holds for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.

Proof.

Setting
a=0
and
b=p-1
in (
2.1
) then
f\in D_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})=D^{1,p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and (
2.1
) yields (
2.3
).

Furthermore, by Hölder inequality (with conjugates
p
and
q
) and (
2.3
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d^{p}}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d^{p}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{q}}{d^{q}}\frac{|f|^{q}}{d^{q}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p}{Q-p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{q}}{d^{q}}\frac{|f|^{q}}{d^{q}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

yielding (
2.4
).
∎

In the Euclidean setting, the error term between left hand and right hand side of (
2.3
) was investigated by Ioku, Ishiwata and Ozawa
[
23
]
. See
[
33
]
for the case of general homogeneous group with radial operator and quasi-norms. Note also that (
2.3
) has been derived by Niu, Zhang and Wang
[
28
]
through the application of generalised Picone formula to the existence of subsolution of
p
-subLaplacian eigenvalue. A similar approach to
[
28
]
was adopted by Han and Niu
[
20
]
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d^{2p}}d\xi\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi,

where the constant
\mathscr{D}>0
,
\Omega=\mathbb{B}_{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
\mathbb{B}_{R}=\{(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:d(z,t)<R\}
.

The weighted Hardy inequality (
2.3
) also gives a special case of Han, Niu and Zhang
[
21
, Theorem 3.2]
with sharp constant derived via a representation formula for functions. The case
p=2
in (
2.3
) (that is when
a=0
,
b=0
and
p=2
for
f\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
) has been discussed by Garofalo and Lanconelli
[
18
]
in their Corollaries 2.1 and 2.2 using a different method which involves the use of the fundamental solution of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}
. They derived an uncertainty principle

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Q-2}{2}\right)^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}|f|^{2}d\xi\right)^{2}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|z|^{2}|f|^{2}d\xi\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f|^{2}d\xi\right)

and remarked that equality holds when
(Q-2)/2
is replaced by
Q/2
if and only if
f(z,t)=A\exp(-\alpha d^{2}(z,t))
(
A\in\mathbb{R}

2.2.
Rellich type interpolation inequalities

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the Heisenberg group of homogeneous dimension
Q=2n+2
. Let
1<p<Q
and
\lambda=a+b
be such that for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
\frac{p-Q}{p-1}\leq\lambda+1\leq 0
. Then for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{a,b,p}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}}{d^{\lambda+1}}d\xi\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}f|^{p}}{d^{ap}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{d^{b\frac{p}{p-1}}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}},

(2.5)

where the constant is
\mathscr{C}_{a,b,p}^{R}:=\frac{Q-p+(\lambda+1)(p-1)}{p}
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}
is the
p
-sub-Laplacian defined by
\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}f:=\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p-2}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f).

Remark 2.5
.

It is not clear whether the constant
\mathscr{C}_{a,b,p}^{R}:=\frac{Q-p+(\lambda+1)(p-1)}{p}
is optimal or not.

Proof.

Recall that a simple computation gives

\displaystyle\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\right)=\frac{(Q-1-\lambda)}{d^{\lambda+1}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}.

Then by the divergence theorem and (
1.13
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}}{d^{\lambda+1}}d\xi=\frac{-p}{Q-1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p-2}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2})\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}d\xi.

(2.6)

On the other hand, also by the divergence theorem and (
1.13
) we have

\displaystyle\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}f\frac{\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}d\xi

\displaystyle=-\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p-2}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\frac{\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}\right)d\xi

\displaystyle=-\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p-2}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\frac{\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\right)\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}d\xi

\displaystyle=-\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p-2}\Bigg(\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}(\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f})\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}+\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}(\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d)}{d^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374pt-\frac{\lambda|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{2}}{d^{\lambda+1}}\Bigg)\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}d\xi

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p-2}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2})\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}-\frac{\lambda|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2}}{d^{\lambda+1}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}\right)\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}d\xi,

where the middle term in the third equality vanishes in the sense of (
1.9
). That is,

\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}(\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d)\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}=\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}(\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d)|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{p-2}=\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{2},\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d\rangle|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{p-4}=0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p-2}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2})\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}d\xi=

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}}{d^{\lambda+1}}d\xi

\displaystyle-\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}f\frac{\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}d\xi.

(2.7)

Combining (
2.6
) and (
2.2
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}}{d^{\lambda+1}}d\xi=\frac{-p}{Q-1-\lambda}\Big(\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}}{d^{\lambda+1}}d\xi-\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}f\frac{\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}d\xi\Big)

which after rearrangement implies

\displaystyle\frac{Q-1+\lambda(p-1)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}}{d^{\lambda+1}}d\xi=\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}f\frac{\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}d\xi.

(2.8)

By the condition
\frac{p-Q}{p-1}\leq\lambda+1=a+b+1\leq 0
, we note that

\mathscr{C}_{a,b,p}^{R}:=\frac{Q-1+\lambda(p-1)}{p}=\frac{Q-p+(\lambda+1)(p-1)}{p}\geq 0.

Now by the Hölder inequality and (
1.10
) we get

\displaystyle\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}f\frac{\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{d^{p-2}}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}f|\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|}{d^{\lambda}}\frac{|z|^{p-1}}{d^{p-1}}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}f|^{p}}{d^{ap}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{q}}{d^{(\lambda-a)q}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Inserting the last inequality into (
2.8
) yields the required inequality.
∎

Definition 3.1

(Weighted Sobolev type spaces)

(b)

denotes the completion of

with the respect to the norm

\|f\|_{D^{1,2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2}}{d^{2\alpha}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(c)

D^{1,2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}})
denotes the completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
with the respect to the norm

\|f\|_{D^{1,2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2}}{d^{2\alpha}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(d)

D^{2,2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
denotes the completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
with the respect to the norm

\|f\|_{D^{2,2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2}}{d^{2\alpha}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(e)

H^{1}_{a,b}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}})
denotes the completion of

with the respect to the norm

\|f\|_{H^{1}_{a,b}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2}}{d^{2a}}d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{d^{2b}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(f)

Theorem 3.2
.

(Sobolev type embedding with horizontal weights)

Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the Heisenberg group of homogeneous dimension
Q=2n+2
and let

.

(a)

For
Q\neq a+b+1

(i)

.

Moreover,

(ii)

H^{1}_{b,a}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}})\subset L^{2}_{\frac{a+b+1}{2}}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}})

due to symmetry with respect to
a
and

.

(b)

For
Q\neq a+b-1
, the following continuous embeddings hold

(i)

H^{2}_{a,b}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}})\subset D^{1,2}_{\frac{a+b+1}{2}}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}})

and

(ii)

D^{2,2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\subset D^{1,2}_{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}})

for
\alpha\leq\frac{Q}{2}-2
and

.

3.1.
Proof of Part (a) in Theorem
3.2
and some consequences

To prove part (a) Theorem
3.2
, we start with the case
p=2
of Theorem
2.1
, that is, the inequality stated in the next Lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
Q\neq a+b+1
,
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{a,b}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{d^{a+b+1}}d\xi\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2}}{d^{2a}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{d^{2b}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

with sharp constant

Proof of Lemma
3.3

We discuss two approaches to this.

First Approach:
As stated above, setting
p=2
in Theorem (
2.1
) leads to (
3.1
).

Second Approach:
- It follows from Costa’s approach
[
8
]
. For all
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
and
a,b,s\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left|\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}{d^{a}}+s\frac{f}{d^{b}}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}d\right|^{2}d\xi\geq 0

(3.2)

implying that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2}}{d^{2a}}d\xi+s^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{d^{2b}}d\xi+2s\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{f}{d^{a+b}}\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}dd\xi\geq 0.

(3.3)

By the divergence theorem

\displaystyle\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{f}{d^{a+b}}\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}dd\xi=-\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\overline{f}\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\frac{f}{d^{a+b}}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d\right)d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{d^{a+b}}\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}dd\xi-\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{f}{d^{a+b}}\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}dd\xi+(a+b)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{d^{a+b+1}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(Q-(a+b+1))\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{d^{a+b+1}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}d\xi-\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{f}{d^{a+b}}\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}dd\xi,

where we have used (
1.10
) and (
1.11
). Thus,

\displaystyle 2\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{f}{d^{a+b}}\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}dd\xi=-(Q-(a+b+1))\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{d^{a+b+1}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}d\xi.

Furthermore, denote by

A:=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{d^{2b}}d\xi,\ \ B:=\Re{e}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{f}{d^{a+b}}\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}dd\xi,\ \ \text{and}\ \ \ C:=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2}}{d^{2a}}d\xi.

Then (
3.3
) takes the form
As^{2}+Bs+C\geq 0
which holds true if and only if

\displaystyle\left((Q-(a+b+1))\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{d^{a+b+1}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}d\xi\right)^{2}\leq 4\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{2}}{d^{2a}}d\xi\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{d^{2}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{d^{2b}}d\xi.

The proof is complete.

4.
Inequalities for vector fields in domains of
\mathbb{H}^{n}

In this section, for an open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
we let
D^{1,p}(\Omega)
denote the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|f\|_{D^{1,p}(\Omega)}=(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi)^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

Let
\mathscr{W}^{1}(\Omega)
be the space of locally integrable vector fields
V
on
\Omega
for which
V
has weak horizontal divergence. By the way of definition, the function
A_{V}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
is called the weak horizontal divergence of
V
if and only if
\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}V=A_{V}
in the sense of distribution, that is,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi A_{V}d\xi=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\phi\cdot Vd\xi

(4.1)

for every
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
. If we pick any vector field
V\in\mathscr{W}^{1}(\Omega)
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, then the vector field
|f|^{p}V
has compact support in
\Omega
and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}(|f|^{p}V)d\xi=0.

(4.2)

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. For
p>1
and any vector field
V\in\mathscr{W}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\geq\int_{\Omega}\left[\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}V-(p-1)|V|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]|f|^{p}d\xi.

(4.3)

This result can be extended to the whole of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as a result of the embedding
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\subset D^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any vector field
V\in\mathscr{W}^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
p>1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\geq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left[\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}V-(p-1)|V|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]|f|^{p}d\xi.

(4.4)

Proof.

For any vector field
V\in\mathscr{W}^{1}(\Omega)
and
f\in D^{1,p}(\Omega)
, it follows that
|f|^{p}V\in\mathscr{W}^{1}
and we have

\displaystyle\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}(|f|^{p}V)=\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}|f|^{p}V+|f|^{p}\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}V.

(4.5)

Combining (
4.5
) with the divergence theorem (
4.2
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}Vd\xi

\displaystyle=-p\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p-1}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}|f|Vd\xi

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p-1}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f||V|d\xi

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq p\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|V|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|f|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}},

where we used the fact that
|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}|f||\leq|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|
and the Hölder inequality.

Consideration of Young’s inequality of the form
\Phi\Psi\leq\frac{1}{p}\Phi^{p}+\frac{(p-1)}{p}\Psi^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
and setting
\Phi:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
and
\Psi:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|V|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|f|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}
leads to

\displaystyle p\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|V|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|f|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi+(p-1)\int_{\Omega}|V|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|f|^{p}d\xi.

(4.7)

Putting (
4
) and (
4
) together and rearranging gives

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\geq\int_{\Omega}\left[\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}V-(p-1)|V|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]|f|^{p}d\xi.

This completes the proof.
∎

In the next part we specialize
V
into various quantities and obtain specific weighted Hardy inequalities.

Example 4.3
.

Choose

V=\alpha\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{p-2}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{p-1}},

where
\alpha
is to be determined.
Clearly

|V|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}=|\alpha|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{p}}{d^{p}}=|\alpha|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{2p}}

and

\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}V=\alpha\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{p-2})\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{p-1}}+\alpha\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{p-2}\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}d}{d^{p-1}}-\alpha(p-1)\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{p}}{d^{p}}.

Using (
1.11
), (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) we obtain for
p\neq Q
that

\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}V=-\alpha(p-Q)\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{2p}}.

Thus

\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}V-(p-1)|V|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}=-(p-1)\left[\alpha\frac{p-Q}{p-1}+|\alpha|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{2p}}.

Therefore (
4.4
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\geq-(p-1)\left[\alpha\frac{p-Q}{p-1}+|\alpha|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{2p}}|f|^{p}d\xi.

(4.8)

Optimizing the quantity
\alpha\mapsto F(\alpha):=-(p-1)\left[\alpha\frac{p-Q}{p-1}+|\alpha|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]
, we obtain
\frac{p-Q}{p-1}+\frac{p}{p-1}|\alpha|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=0
giving the extremal value
\alpha_{\star}=-\left(\frac{p-Q}{p}\right)^{p-1}
and then

F(\alpha_{\star})=\left(\frac{Q-p}{p}\right)^{p}.

By this, (
4.8
) yields the weighted inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\geq\left(\frac{Q-p}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d^{p}}d\xi.

The above is another approach to proving (
2.3
). Generalised Picone identity has also been applied by Niu, Zhang and Wang
[
28
]
to derive this inequality.

Example 4.4
.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{+}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\langle\xi,\nu\rangle>\delta\}
be the half space of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, where
\nu=(\nu_{x},\nu_{y},\nu_{t})
with
\nu_{x},\nu_{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{+}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\geq\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{+}}\frac{W(\xi)^{p}}{\text{dist}(\xi,\partial\mathbb{H}^{+})^{p}}|f|^{p}d\xi

is the so-called angle function,
and
\text{dist}(\xi,\partial\mathbb{H}^{+}):=\langle\xi,\nu\rangle-d
is the Euclidean distance to the boundary
\partial\mathbb{H}^{+}
.

To obtain (

) we can apply (
4.3
) by choosing

as

V=\beta\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\widetilde{\delta}|^{p-2}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\widetilde{\delta}}{\widetilde{\delta}^{p-1}}

\widetilde{\delta}

\widetilde{\delta}:=\text{dist}(\xi,\partial\mathbb{H}^{+})
.

Note that inequality (

) was conjectured by Larson
[

[

[

]
by choosing

and

Example 4.6

\displaystyle\text{div}_{\mathbb{H}}V-(p-1)|V|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle=(p-1)\left[\gamma^{p-1}-|\gamma|^{p}\right]\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d|^{p}}{d^{p}(\ln R/d)^{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{1}{(d\ln R/d)^{p}}.

Hence, by (
4.3
) we obtain the following improved weighted Hardy inequality for
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
and
1<p=Q

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f|^{p}d\xi\geq\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|z|^{p}}{d^{p}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d^{p}(\ln R/d)^{p}}d\xi.

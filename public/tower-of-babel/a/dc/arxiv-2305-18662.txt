Entanglement partners and monogamy in de Sitter universes
Yasusada Nambu, Koji Yamaguchi

Abstract
We investigate entanglement of local spatial modes defined by a quantum field in a de Sitter universe. The introduced modes show disentanglement behavior when the separation between two regions where local modes are assigned becomes larger than the cosmological horizon. To understand the emergence of separability between these local modes, we apply the monogamy inequality proposed by S. Camalet. We embed the focusing bipartite mode defined by the quantum field in a pure four-mode Gaussian state, and identify its partner modes. Then applying a Gaussian version of the monogamy relation, we show that the external entanglement between the bipartite mode and its partner modes constrains the entanglement of the bipartite mode. Thus the emergence of separability of local modes in the de Sitter universe can be understood from the perspective of entanglement monogamy.



I
Introduction

Cosmic inflation explains the origin of structures in our Universe by
preparing seeds of primordial fluctuations as quantum origin, vacuum
fluctuations of a quantum scalar field called inflaton. The
contribution of this quantum field to energy density functions
as a cosmological constant, leading to the accelerated
expansion of the Universe. During the rapid expansion of the
Universe, vacuum fluctuations receive parametric amplification, and
the resulting fluctuations evolve to become “classical” seed
fluctuations causing gravitational instability and later
forming the large scale structures

Kiefer and Polarski 2008
. Although this is an accepted scenario of
structure formation based on cosmic inflation in standard
cosmology, the mechanism of “quantum to classical transition” of
primordial fluctuation has not been well understood yet.

Entanglement is a key concept to differentiate quantum systems
from classical ones and a crucial tool to investigate the quantum
nature of the initial stage of our universe. In our previous
studies

Nambu 2008
;
Nambu and Ohsumi 2009
;
Nambu and Ohsumi 2011
;
Nambu 2013
;
Matsumura and Nambu 2018
;
Nambu and Hotta 2023
,
local oscillator modes defined from the quantum scalar field in a de
Sitter universe were investigated, and it was found that the initial
entangled state becomes separable; that is, two local modes A and B, which are
assigned at two spatial regions, become separable when their
separation exceeds the Hubble horizon scale. This disentanglement
behavior can be explained as follows: the “thermal noise” with the
Gibbons-Hawking temperature associated with the de Sitter horizon
breaks quantum correlations between two spatial regions, and
therefore, the entangled bipartite state of modes A and B becomes
separable. After these two modes become separable, only classical
correlations survive between them.

The mechanism of disentanglement can also be studied from the
property of multipartite entanglement. The bipartite system AB is
defined as a subsystem of the total system, i.e., the field in the
entire universe. Although the total system is assumed to be in a pure
state, modes AB are in a mixed state since they are correlated with
its complement
\overline{\text{AB}}
. It is always possible to find
a subsystem in
\overline{\text{AB}}
that purifies AB, which is
called the partner mode of AB.
Then, we can understand the disentanglement of AB as a result of
entanglement sharing between these modes and their partners. More concretely, the disentanglement of AB can be analyzed from the perspective
of entanglement monogamy in multipartite quantum systems

Coffman et al. 2000
;
Osborne and Verstraete 2006
;
Hiroshima et al. 2007
;
Adesso and Illuminati 2007
;
Adesso et al. 2014
;
Dhar et al. 2016
. Monogamy
of entanglement is an intrinsic property of quantum correlations that
is not amenable to classical explanations. For the bipartite state AB
and its complement
\text{C}:=\overline{\text{AB}}
, the conventional
monogamy relation is expressed as the following inequality

E(\text{A:B})+E(\text{A:C})\leq E(\text{A:BC}),

(1)

where
E(\text{X:Y})
denotes a suitably chosen entanglement measure
for a bipartite system XY. The inequality restricts the
amount of the bipartite entanglement
E(\text{A:B})
as sharing of
correlations in the tripartite system ABC. However, this inequality
does not provide such a tight constraint as to derive a condition on
the separability
E(\text{A:B})=0
for multipartite Gaussian states

Matsumura and Nambu 2018
. See also Appendix
A
where we
review the monogamy property (
1
) for a pure
four-mode Gaussian state.

A slightly different form of the monogamy relation was proposed by
Camalet

Camalet 2017a
;
Camalet 2017b
;
Camalet 2018
;
Zhu et al. 2020
;
Zhu et al. 2021
, which
relates “internal” and “external” quantum correlations in
multipartite states. Here, for a bipartite system AB of interest,
the correlation between A and B is internal, while the one between AB
and another subsystem X in the complementary system

\overline{\text{AB}}
is external. Based on assumptions of general
correlation measures, a new kind of monogamy inequality was derived,
which states that internal entanglement and external entanglement
obey a trade-off relation. As a consequence, explicit forms of the
monogamy inequality are obtained in terms of entanglement measures
for finite-dimensional systems, such as qubits.

In this paper, we investigate the entanglement behavior of local
bipartite modes AB of a quantum field in the de Sitter universe from
the viewpoint of the monogamy of entanglement. For this purpose, we
identify partner modes that purify the bipartite modes AB by using
the formalism proposed in

Trevison et al. 2019
;
Tomitsuka et al. 2019
;
Yamaguchi 2020
;
Yamaguchi and Hotta 2020
;
Yamaguchi et al. 2020
. We
then prove a Camalet-type trade-off relation between internal and
external correlations for these modes, i.e., a monogamy relation on
the entanglement between A and B, and the entanglement between AB and
their partners. Based on these formalisms, we find that the emergence
of separability between local modes in the de Sitter universe can be
understood from the viewpoint of entanglement monogamy.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we introduce
a quantum scalar field in the de Sitter universe and show the
disentanglement behavior of local modes assigned at two spatial
points. In Sec.
III
, we review our method of
construction of the partner modes of the two local modes based on
the formulas in

Trevison et al. 2019
;
Tomitsuka et al. 2019
;
Yamaguchi 2020
;
Yamaguchi and Hotta 2020
;
Yamaguchi et al. 2020
. In Sec.
IV
, based on the result obtained in
Sec.
III
, we examine Camalet’s monogamy relation for Gaussian
modes with which the emergence of separability in the de Sitter
universe is
analyzed. Section
V
is devoted to summary and conclusion. We adopt
units of
c=\hbar=1
throughout this paper.

II
Scalar field and local modes

To comprehend the behavior of the entanglement of quantum fields in
the de Sitter universe, we consider a minimally coupled massless
scalar field
\hat{\phi}
in a (3+1)-dimensional flat
Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) universe. The scalar
field obeys the Klein-Gordon equation
\square\hat{\phi}=0
. The metric
of the FLRW universe with the conformal time
\eta
and the comoving
coordinate
\boldsymbol{x}=(x,y,z)
is

ds^{2}=a_{\text{sc}}^{2}(\eta)(-d\eta^{2}+d\boldsymbol{x}^{2}),

(2)

where
a_{\text{sc}}(\eta)
is the scale factor of the universe. We
will later fix its functional form as that which corresponds to the de
Sitter universe. The rescaled scalar field

\hat{\varphi}=a_{\text{sc}}\hat{\phi}
obeys the following field equation

\hat{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}-\left(\frac{a^{\prime\prime}_{\text{sc}}}{a_{\text{sc}}}+\delta^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\right)\hat{\varphi}=0,\quad i,j=x,y,z,

(3)

where
{}^{\prime}=d/d\eta
. We adopt this mode equation in a (3+1)-dimensional spacetime, but we assume that excitation propagates only in one spatial direction to simplify the analysis.
Then the field operators of the massless scalar field are expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\hat{\varphi}_{k}\,e^{ikx},\quad\hat{\varphi}_{k}=f_{k}(\eta)\hat{a}_{k}+f_{k}^{*}(\eta)\hat{a}_{-k}^{{\dagger}},

(4)

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\hat{\pi}_{k}\,e^{ikx},\quad\hat{\pi}_{k}=(-i)(g_{k}(\eta)\hat{a}_{k}-g_{k}(\eta)^{*}\hat{a}_{-k}^{\dagger}),

(5)

\displaystyle[\hat{a}_{k_{1}},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k_{2}}]=\delta(k_{1}-k_{2}),\quad f_{k}^{\prime\prime}+\left(k^{2}-\frac{a_{\text{sc}}^{\prime\prime}}{a_{\text{sc}}}\right)f_{k}=0,\quad g_{k}=i\left(f_{k}^{\prime}-\frac{a_{\text{sc}}^{\prime}}{a_{\text{sc}}}f_{k}\right),\quad f_{k}g_{k}^{*}+f_{k}^{*}g_{k}=1,

(6)

where
\hat{a}_{k}
and
\hat{a}_{k}^{\dagger}
are annihilation and creation
operators, and
\hat{\pi}(x)
is the conjugate momentum of the field
variable
\hat{\varphi}(x)
. In terms of the Fourier components of the field
operators, the creation and the annihilation operators can be
represented as

\hat{a}_{k}=g_{k}^{*}\hat{\varphi}_{k}+if_{k}^{*}\hat{\pi}_{k},\quad\hat{a}_{-k}^{\dagger}=g_{k}\hat{\varphi}_{k}-if_{k}\hat{\pi}_{k}.

(7)

We assume that the scalar field is in the vacuum state
\ket{\psi}
associated with the annihilation operator

\hat{a}_{k}
such that

\hat{a}_{k}\ket{\psi}=0.

(8)

The equal-time commutation relations for the field operators are given by

[\hat{\varphi}(\eta,x),\hat{\pi}(\eta,y)]=i\delta(x-y),\quad[\hat{\varphi}(\eta,x),\hat{\varphi}(\eta,y)]=[\hat{\pi}(\eta,x),\hat{\pi}(\eta,y)]=0.

(9)

Covariances of the field operators are calculated as

\displaystyle M_{11}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\expectationvalue{\{\hat\varphi(x),\hat\varphi(y)\}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk|f_{k}|^{2}\cos(k(x-y)),

(10)

\displaystyle M_{22}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\expectationvalue{\{\hat\pi(x),\hat\pi(y)\}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk|g_{k}|^{2}\cos(k(x-y)),

(11)

\displaystyle M_{12}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\expectationvalue{\{\hat\varphi(x),\hat\pi(y)\}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\,i(f_{k}g_{k}^{*}-f_{k}^{*}g_{k})\cos(k(x-y)),

(12)

where
\expectationvalue{\,}
denotes the expectation value with respect to the state
\ket{\psi}
.

II.1
Local modes

We consider measurement of the field operators
\hat{\varphi},\hat{\pi}
at spatial points
x_{\text{A}}
and
x_{\text{B}}
. The measurement process can be represented as
the interaction between the field operators and dynamical variables of
the measurement apparatus such as Unruh-DeWitt detectors
Birrell and Davies 1984
. In the present analysis, we do not specify
details of the apparatus but just assume the interaction Hamiltonian
between the field operators and the apparatus has the following form:

H_{\text{int}}=\sum_{j=\text{A,B}}\lambda_{j}(t)g_{j}(\hat{q}_{D},\hat{p}_{D})\otimes\int dx(w_{1j}(x)\hat{\varphi}(x)+w_{2j}(x)\hat{\pi}(x)),

(13)

where
g_{j}
is a function of canonical variables of the measurement
apparatus
(\hat{q}_{D},\hat{p}_{D})
,
w_{1j}(x),w_{2j}(x)
are window functions defining a spatial
local mode of the field at
x_{\text{A,B}}
, and
\lambda_{j}(t)
is a
switching function of the interaction. In the present analysis, we do
not treat details of measurement protocols but only pay attention
to the behavior of the local modes of the quantum field introduced by
the window functions.

Let us introduce local operators at
x_{j}
(
j=\text{A,B}
) using a
window function
w_{j}(x)=w(x-x_{j})
as

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{j}:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dxw_{j}(x)\hat{\varphi}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk\,\hat{\varphi}_{k}\,e^{ikx_{j}}w_{k},

(14)

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{j}:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dxw_{j}(x)\hat{\pi}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk\,\hat{\pi}_{k}\,e^{ikx_{j}}w_{k},

(15)

where the window function is assumed to be localized around
x_{j}
,
and
w_{k}
denotes the Fourier component of the window function:

w_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,w(x)e^{ikx},\quad w_{k}=w_{-k}^{*}.

(16)

We require that the window function is fixed so that the local operators
(\hat{q}_{j},\hat{p}_{j})
define independent modes. In other words, they satisfy the canonical commutation relations given by

\displaystyle[\hat{q}_{i},\hat{p}_{j}]=i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk\,e^{ik(x_{i}-x_{j})}|w_{k}|^{2}\equiv i\delta_{ij},

(17)

\displaystyle[\hat{q}_{i},\hat{q}_{j}]=[\hat{p}_{i},\hat{p}_{j}]=0.

(18)

Note that these commutators are independent of the state of the quantum field.
Covariances for the local operators are

\displaystyle c_{1}(i,j)

\displaystyle:=\expectationvalue{\{\hat q_i,\hat q_j\}}=4\int_{0}^{\infty}dk|w_{k}|^{2}|f_{k}|^{2}\cos k\Delta_{ij},

(19)

\displaystyle c_{2}(i,j)

\displaystyle:=\expectationvalue{\{\hat p_i,\hat p_j\}}=4\int_{0}^{\infty}dk|w_{k}|^{2}|g_{k}|^{2}\cos k\Delta_{ij},

(20)

\displaystyle c_{3}(i,j)

\displaystyle:=\expectationvalue{\{\hat q_i,\hat p_j\}}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}dk|w_{k}|^{2}\,i(f_{k}g_{k}^{*}-f_{k}^{*}g_{k})\cos(k\Delta_{ij}),

(21)

where
\Delta_{ij}:=x_{i}-x_{j}
.

Window function

We adopt a
k
-top hat window function in this study:

w_{k}=w_{0}\,\theta(k_{c}-|k|)\theta(|k|-k_{0}),~k_{c}\geq k_{0}
, where
k_{c}
is
the infrared (IR) cutoff corresponding to the total system size
(comoving size of the total universe) and
k_{c}
is the ultraviolet
(UV) cutoff defining the size of localized modes. This type of a
window function was adopted in the stochastic approach to inflation

Starobinsky 1988
, which is a phenomenological treatment of long
wavelength quantum fluctuations in the de Sitter universe, and this
method describes dynamics of the quantum inflaton field as a classical
stochastic variable obeying a Langevin equation. The normalization

w_{0}
is determined by (
17
):

\delta_{ij}=2w_{0}^{2}\int_{k_{0}}^{k_{c}}dk\cos(k\Delta_{ij})=2w_{0}^{2}\frac{\sin(k_c\Delta_{ij})-\sin(k_0\Delta_{ij})}{\Delta_{ij}}.

(22)

For
\Delta_{\text{AA}}=\Delta_{\text{BB}}=0
, Eq. (
22
)
provides the normalization of the window function that is determined as

w_{0}^{2}=1/(2(k_{c}-k_{0}))
.
For
\Delta_{\text{AB}}\neq 0
, Eq. (
22

\sin(k_c\Delta_\text{AB})-\sin(k_0\Delta_\text{AB})=0
which determines

\Delta_{\text{AB}}=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{k_{c}+k_{0}},\frac{(2n)\pi}{k_{c}-k_{0}},\quad n=1,2,\dots.

(23)

As a value of
\Delta_{\text{AB}}
, we adopt the following in our analysis:

|\Delta_{\text{AB}}|=\frac{\pi}{k_{c}+k_{0}}=:\Delta.

(24)

The quantity
\Delta
represents the distance between adjacent two local regions A and B
with
x_{\text{B}}-x_{\text{A}}=\Delta
(Fig.
1
). The

also represents the size of each local region.
The spatial profile of the window function is given by

w(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{k_{0}}^{k_{c}}dk\,w_{0}e^{-ikx}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi(k_{c}-k_{0}})}\frac{\sin(k_c x)-\sin(k_0x)}{x}.

(25)

Left panel: setup of spatial regions A and B. Right panel: the
window functions for A:
w(x+\Delta/2)

w(x-\Delta/2)

with
k_{0}/k_{c}=0.2

Covariances for the local operators are calculated as

\displaystyle c_{1}(\Delta)=\frac{2}{k_{c}-k_{0}}\int_{k_{0}}^{k_{c}}dk|f_{k}|^{2}\cos k\Delta=\frac{2}{1-\delta}\int_{\delta}^{1}dz|f_{k}|^{2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{1+\delta}\right),

(26)

\displaystyle c_{2}(\Delta)=\frac{2}{k_{c}-k_{0}}\int_{k_{0}}^{k_{c}}dk|g_{k}|^{2}\cos k\Delta=\frac{2}{1-\delta}\int_{\delta}^{1}dz|g_{k}|^{2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{1+\delta}\right),

(27)

\displaystyle c_{3}(\Delta)=\frac{1}{k_{c}-k_{0}}\int_{k_{0}}^{k_{c}}dk\,i(f_{k}g_{k}^{*}-f_{k}^{*}g_{k})\cos(k\Delta)=\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{\delta}^{1}dz\,i(f_{k}g_{k}^{*}-f_{k}^{*}g_{k})\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{1+\delta}\right),

(28)

where
z=k/k_{c},\delta=k_{0}/k_{c}
. The parameter
\delta
represents the size
of the local region normalized by the total system size:

\delta=(k_{0}\Delta/\pi)(1-k_{0}\Delta/\pi)^{-1}

The covariance matrix of the bipartite system AB defined by

(\hat{q}_{\text{A}},\hat{p}_{\text{A}},\hat{q}_{\text{B}},\hat{p}_{\text{B}})
is given by

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&a_{3}&c_{1}&c_{3}\\
a_{3}&a_{2}&c_{3}&c_{2}\\
c_{1}&c_{3}&a_{1}&a_{3}\\
c_{3}&c_{2}&a_{3}&a_{2}\end{bmatrix},

(29)

where
a_{i}:=c_{i}(\Delta=0)
for
i=1,2,3
. Owing to the homogeneity of
the universe represented by the metric (
2
), the
covariance matrices of each mode A and B have the same components;
i.e., the bipartite system AB is in a symmetric Gaussian
state. Symplectic eigenvalues of the covariance matrix

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}
are calculated as

\displaystyle(\nu_{1})^{2}=a_{1}a_{2}-a_{3}^{2}+c_{1}c_{2}-c_{3}^{2}+|a_{1}c_{2}+a_{2}c_{1}-2a_{3}c_{3}|,

(30)

\displaystyle(\nu_{2})^{2}=a_{1}a_{2}-a_{3}^{2}+c_{1}c_{2}-c_{3}^{2}-|a_{1}c_{2}+a_{2}c_{1}-2a_{3}c_{3}|.

(31)

The state represented by the covariance matrix (
29
) is
physical, i.e., positive-semidefinite, if and only if

1\leq\nu_{2}\leq\nu_{1}
.

The partially transposed covariance matrix, which is obtained by
reversing the sign of mode B’s momentum, has the following two
symplectic eigenvalues:

\displaystyle(\tilde{\nu}_{1})^{2}=a_{1}a_{2}-a_{3}^{2}-c_{1}c_{2}+c_{3}^{2}+|(a_{1}c_{2}-a_{2}c_{1})^{2}+4(a_{1}c_{3}-a_{3}c_{3})(a_{2}c_{3}-a_{3}c_{2})|^{1/2},

(32)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\nu}_{2})^{2}=a_{1}a_{2}-a_{3}^{2}-c_{1}c_{2}+c_{3}^{2}-|(a_{1}c_{2}-a_{2}c_{1})^{2}+4(a_{1}c_{3}-a_{3}c_{3})(a_{2}c_{3}-a_{3}c_{2})|^{1/2}.

(33)

Based on the positivity criterion of the partially transposed
covariance matrix for a bipartite Gaussian state

Peres 1996
;
Horodecki 1997
;
Simon 2000
, the negativity gives a
measure of entanglement between modes A and B, which is defined as

Vidal and Werner 2002
;
Plenio 2005

N_{\text{A:B}}:=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{max}\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{\nu}_{2}}-1,0\right).

(34)

The modes A and B are entangled if

, while the modes A
and B are separable if
N_{\text{A:B}}=0

III
Purification of local Gaussian modes in quantum fields

To understand the behavior of entanglement between
spatial local modes, we look for their partners, i.e., the modes that
purify them. In
Hotta et al. 2015
, a partner mode of a given
mode is calculated in specific examples, including a system with
Hawking radiation. A general partner formula that identifies a partner
mode for a single mode in any pure Gaussian state is proven in

Trevison et al. 2019
. Generalizing these results, a systematic method
to identify any number of modes in a pure state has been developed in

Yamaguchi et al. 2020
;
Yamaguchi and Hotta 2020
;
Yamaguchi 2020
. Such a subsystem
composed of modes in a pure state is called a quantum information
capsule (QIC). Although the QIC formula in

Yamaguchi et al. 2020
;
Yamaguchi and Hotta 2020
;
Yamaguchi 2020
provides an
algebraic way to identify modes in a pure state, it cannot be directly
used for our purpose of analyzing the disentanglement structure of two
local modes AB
Nambu 2008
;
Nambu and Ohsumi 2011
;
Nambu and Ohsumi 2009
;
Nambu 2013
from the
viewpoint of monogamy. In this section, we derive a more useful
formula to identify the partner modes that purify given two modes AB.

In Sec.
III.1
, we briefly review the QIC argument with which modes in a pure state are identified. In Sec.
III.2
, we derive a formula identifying the partner mode of a single mode, which reproduces the partner formula in
Trevison et al. 2019
. In Sec.
III.3
, we generalize the partner formula to identify the partner of a two-mode system.

The partners here are a special class of QIC. In

;

;

III.2
Purification of a single Gaussian mode

As a practical application of the map
f_{\psi}
, we look for a partner
mode that purifies a given single mode A [Fig.
3

(a)]. In particular, we apply the partner formula for a local mode

\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}}=(\hat{q}_{\text{A}},\hat{p}_{\text{A}})^{T}
at a
spatial point
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\text{A}}
defined in the previous section.
As we have seen in Sec.
III.1
, four operators

\hat{q}_{\text{A}},\hat{p}_{\text{A}},f_{\psi}(\hat{q}_{\text{A}}),f_{\psi}(\hat{p}_{\text{A}}),

(71)

define a two-mode system that is in a pure state, provided that the field is in a pure Gaussian state. To identify the partner mode of

\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}}
, we here construct a mode generated by the operators in Eq. (
71
), which is orthonormal to the mode A. The
covariance matrix for
\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}}
is

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}:=\expectationvalue{\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{A},\hat{\bs{\xi}}^T_\text{A}
\}}=\begin{bmatrix}2\expectationvalue{\hat q_\text{A}^2}&\expectationvalue{\hat q_\text{A}\hat p_\text{A}+\hat p_\text{A}\hat q_\text{A}}\\
\expectationvalue{\hat q_\text{A}\hat p_\text{A}+\hat p_\text{A}\hat q_\text{A}}&2\expectationvalue{\hat p_\text{A}^2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}&c_{3}\\
c_{3}&c_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

(72)

Commutators and covariances between these operators are given by

[\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{T}_{\text{A}}]=[f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}}),f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{T}_{\text{A}})]=i\boldsymbol{J},\quad[\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}},f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{T}_{\text{A}})]=i\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}},

(73)

and

\expectationvalue{\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{A},\hat{\bs{\xi}}^T_\text{A}
\}}=\expectationvalue{\{f_\psi(\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{A}),
f_\psi(\hat{\bs{\xi}}^T_\text{A}) \}}=\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}},\quad\expectationvalue{\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{A},
f_\psi(\hat{\bs{\xi}}^T_\text{A}) \}}=-\boldsymbol{J}.

(74)

To extract a mode orthogonal to the original mode

\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}}
from
f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}})
, we
define operators

\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}:=f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}})-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{J}\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}}.

(75)

They indeed commute with
\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}}
as

\displaystyle[\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{T}_{\text{A}}]

\displaystyle=[f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}}),\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{T}_{\text{A}}]-[\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{J}\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{T}_{\text{A}}]

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{J}[\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{T}_{\text{A}}]

\displaystyle=0.

(76)

Therefore, they define a mode
orthogonal to
\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}}
.

The commutator of
\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}
is calculated as

\displaystyle[\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{T}]

\displaystyle=i(\boldsymbol{J}-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}).

(77)

If the mode A is in a pure state, it holds

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}=\boldsymbol{J}
which corresponds to the
relation of the density operator

\hat{\rho}^{2}_{\text{A}}=\hat{\rho}_{\text{A}}
, implying that the commutator
of
\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}
is trivial. In this case, because

\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}}
is in a pure state, its partner mode does
not exist. If the mode A is not pure, its partner is characterized by

\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}
.
We normalize
\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}
to make it a canonical pair of
operators. For this purpose, we introduce
\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{C}}
by

\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}=\boldsymbol{A}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{C}}
with a matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
so that

[\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{C}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{C}}^{T}]=i\boldsymbol{J}
holds. The
condition on the matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
is given by

\boldsymbol{AJA}^{T}=\boldsymbol{J}-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}.

(78)

To obtain
\boldsymbol{A}
, we consider the standard form of the covariance
matrix
\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}
:

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}=\boldsymbol{S}\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{S}^{T}=a\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{S}^{T},\quad\boldsymbol{SJS^{T}}=\boldsymbol{J},

(79)

where
\boldsymbol{S}
represents a symplectic transformation to diagonalize

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}
and
a
is the symplectic eigenvalue of

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}
.

Although the partner mode C itself is unique,
the matrix
\boldsymbol{S}
satisfying Eq. (
78
) is
not uniquely determined because of the remaining freedom in fixing a
canonical set of operators for the mode C. In other words, when

\boldsymbol{A}
satisfies Eq. (
78
), so does

\boldsymbol{A}^{\prime}:=\boldsymbol{S}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{A}
, where
\boldsymbol{S}^{\prime}
is an arbitrary
2\times 2

symplectic matrix. Since Eq. (
78
) is recast
into
\boldsymbol{AJA}^{T}=(1-a^{2})\boldsymbol{J}
, we can choose

\boldsymbol{A}=\sqrt{a^{2}-1}\,\boldsymbol{X},

(80)

or equivalently

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}\boldsymbol{X},\quad

(81)

where
\boldsymbol{X}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}
.

In summary, the partner mode C of mode A is obtained as the following formula:

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{C}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}\boldsymbol{X}(f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}})-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{J}\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}\begin{bmatrix}c_{2}\hat{q}_{\text{A}}-c_{3}\hat{p}_{\text{A}}+f_{\psi}(\hat{p}_{\text{A}})\\
c_{3}\hat{q}_{\text{A}}-c_{1}\hat{p}_{\text{A}}+f_{\psi}(\hat{q}_{\text{A}})\end{bmatrix}=:\begin{bmatrix}\hat{q}_{\text{C}}\\
\hat{p}_{\text{C}}\end{bmatrix},

(82)

which is equivalent to the partner formula for a single mode
Trevison et al. 2019
in a Gaussian state.
Covariances of operators

(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{C}})
are given by

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{A},\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{A}^T\}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}},

(83)

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{C},\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{A}^T\}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{J}-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}})=\sqrt{a^{2}-1}\boldsymbol{Z},

(84)

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{C},\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{C}^T\}}

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}+\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}})(\boldsymbol{A}^{-1})^{T}=\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{X},

(85)

where
\boldsymbol{Z}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}
.
In a matrix form, they are summarized as

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AC}}:=\expectationvalue{\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_{\text{AC}},\hat{\bs{\xi}}_{\text{AC}}^T\}}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}&\sqrt{a^{2}-1}\boldsymbol{Z}\\
\sqrt{a^{2}-1}\boldsymbol{Z}&\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{A}}\boldsymbol{X}\end{bmatrix},\quad\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AC}}:=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}}\\
\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{C}}\end{bmatrix}.

(86)

One can explicitly confirm that this covariance matrix satisfies the following purity condition of the state AC:

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AC}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AC}}=\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2},\quad\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{2}\boldsymbol{J},

(87)

implying that it represents a pure two-mode squeezed state characterizing the pair of partners AC. The total state is decomposed as

\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi'}_{\text{AC}}\otimes\ket{\psi''}_{\overline{\text{AC}}},

(88)

where
\ket{\psi'}_{\text{AC}}
is the pure Gaussian state defined by

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AC}}
and has no correlation with its complement system
\overline{\text{AC}}
in another pure state
\ket{\psi''}_{\overline{\text{AC}}}
.

III.3
Purification of the bipartite Gaussian mode

In this subsection, we construct partner modes CD for the two-mode
system

\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}=(\hat{q}_{\text{A}},\hat{p}_{\text{A}},\hat{q}_{\text{B}},\hat{p}_{\text{B}})^{T}
. See Fig.
3
(b)
for the schematic picture of the setup.
To the authors’ knowledge, an explicit formula to obtain the partner
modes of a two-mode system has not yet appeared in the
literature. Therefore, we explain here the derivation, although it is
quite similar to the arguments in the previous subsection, i.e., the
derivation of the partner formula for a one-mode system.

From the arguments in Sec.
III.1
, the eight operators

\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}
and
f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}})
, i.e.,

\hat{q}_{\text{A}},\hat{p}_{\text{A}},\hat{q}_{\text{B}},\hat{p}_{\text{B}},f_{\psi}(\hat{q}_{\text{A}}),f_{\psi}(\hat{p}_{\text{A}}),f_{\psi}(\hat{q}_{\text{B}}),f_{\psi}(\hat{p}_{\text{B}})

(101)

define a system composed of four modes ABCD, which is in a pure state. We aim to construct two modes CD orthonormal to modes AB.
Commutators between these operators are given by

\displaystyle[\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}^{T}]=i\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2},\quad[\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}},f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}^{T})]=i\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}},\quad[f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}),\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}^{T}]=-i\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}},\quad[f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}),f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}^{T})]=i\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2},

(102)

where
\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}
denotes the covariance matrix for the two
mode system
\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}
:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}:=\expectationvalue{\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{AB},\hat{\bs{\xi}}^T_\text{AB}\}}=\expectationvalue{\{f_\psi(\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{AB}),f_\psi(\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{AB}^T)\}},\quad\expectationvalue{\left\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{AB},f_\psi(\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{AB}^T)\right\}}=-\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}.

(103)

To find modes orthogonal to the original mode

\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}
, we introduce operators

\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}:=f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}})-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}.

(104)

They satisfy
[\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}^{T}]=0
since

\displaystyle[\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}^{T}]

\displaystyle=[f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}),\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}^{T}]-[\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}]

\displaystyle=-i\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}[\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}^{T}]

\displaystyle=0

(105)

and therefore define modes orthogonal to the original modes AB.
The commutators and the covariances for
\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}
are calculated as

[\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{T}]=i(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}),\quad\expectationvalue{\left\{\hat{\bs{\zeta}},\hat{\bs{\zeta}}^T\right\}}=-\left(\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}+\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\right).

(106)

If
\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}=\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}
,
the two-mode system AB is in a pure state, implying their partners do
not exist. We therefore assume that

\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\neq\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}
.
We normalize
\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}
as

\hat{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}=\boldsymbol{A}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{CD}}
using a matrix

\boldsymbol{A}
so that the standard canonical commutation relation for modes
C and D

\quad[\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{CD}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{CD}}^{T}]=i\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}

(107)

is satisfied. This is equivalent to a constraint on the

4\times 4
matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
given by

\boldsymbol{A\Omega}_{2}\boldsymbol{A}^{T}=\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}.

(108)

By using matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
satisfying this condition, covariances of
normalized operators
\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{CD}}
are expressed as

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\left\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{CD},\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{CD}^T\right\}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}\expectationvalue{\left\{\hat{\bs{\zeta}},\hat{\bs{\zeta}}^T\right\}}(\boldsymbol{A}^{-1})^{T}

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}+\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}})(\boldsymbol{A}^{-1})^{T}=:\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{CD}},

(109)

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\left\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{CD},\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{AB}^T\right\}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}})=:\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB,CD}}^{T},

(110)

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\left\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{AB},\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{CD}^T\right\}}

\displaystyle=\left[\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}})\right]^{T}=:\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB,CD}},

(111)

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\left\{\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{AB},\hat{\bs{\xi}}_\text{AB}^T\right\}}

\displaystyle=:\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}.

(112)

These covariances define a state for the four-mode system ABCD as

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{ABCD}}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}&\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB,CD}}\\
\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB,CD}}^{T}&\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{CD}}\end{bmatrix}.

(113)

Since the four-mode system ABCD is in a pure state, the purity
condition is satisfied

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{ABCD}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{4}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{ABCD}}=\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{4},

(114)

where
\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{4}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{4}\boldsymbol{J}
. In this case, the state of the total system is decomposed as

\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi'}_{\text{ABCD}}\otimes\ket{\psi''}_{\overline{\text{ABCD}}},

(115)

where
\ket{\psi'}_{\text{ABCD}}
denotes a pure state of the four-mode
system ABCD defined by the covariance matrix
\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{ABCD}}
,
while
\ket{\psi}_{\overline{\text{ABCD}}}
is a pure state for its
complement system
\overline{\text{ABCD}}
. This decomposition implies
that there is no correlation between the four-mode system ABCD and its
complement
\overline{\text{ABCD}}
. Therefore, all the information on
the correlation between AB and its complement is confined to the
four-mode system ABCD.

We look for the matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
satisfying Eq. (
108
) by
using the standard form of the covariance matrix of a two-mode
Gaussian state
Duan et al. 2000
;
Serafini and Adesso 2007
. Our aim is
to find the partner modes of local modes A and B defined at spatial
points
x_{\text{A}}
and
x_{\text{B}}
. Because of the spatial translation
symmetry, the symplectic eigenvalue of the covariance matrix of mode A
is equal to that of B. Therefore, without loss of generality, we can
assume that the covariance matrix of the bipartite system AB is given
by the standard form of symmetric Gaussian state

\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&d_{1}&0\\
0&a&0&d_{2}\\
d_{1}&0&a&0\\
0&d_{2}&0&a\end{bmatrix},

(116)

after performing a local symplectic transformation on each mode.

Using the standard form of
\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}
,
the right-hand side of Eq. (
108
) is expressed as

\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}=\begin{bmatrix}(1-a^{2}-d_{1}d_{2})\boldsymbol{J}&-a(d_{1}+d_{2})\boldsymbol{J}\\
*&(1-a^{2}-d_{1}d_{2})\boldsymbol{J}\end{bmatrix}.

(117)

As noted in the previous section, the solution
\boldsymbol{A}
of
Eq. (
108
) is not uniquely determined as there is
remaining freedom for fixing canonical operators for CD. As an ansatz
for the matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
, we adopt

\boldsymbol{A}=\begin{bmatrix}g\boldsymbol{X}&h\boldsymbol{X}\\
h\boldsymbol{X}&g\boldsymbol{X}\end{bmatrix}.

(118)

Because

\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\boldsymbol{A}=-\begin{bmatrix}(g^{2}+h^{2})\boldsymbol{J}&2gh\boldsymbol{J}\\
2gh\boldsymbol{J}&(g^{2}+h^{2})\boldsymbol{J}\end{bmatrix},

(119)

the constraint in Eq. (
108
) is equivalent to

g^{2}+h^{2}=a^{2}+d_{1}d_{2}-1,\quad 2gh=a(d_{1}+d_{2}),

(120)

and the solution is

g=\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{x+y}+\sqrt{x-y}),\quad h=\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{x+y}-\sqrt{x-y}),

(121)

where we introduced

x=a^{2}+d_{1}d_{2}-1,\quad y=a(d_{1}+d_{2}).

(122)

Because the inverse of
\boldsymbol{A}
is given by

\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}=\frac{1}{g^{2}-h^{2}}\begin{bmatrix}g\boldsymbol{X}&-h\boldsymbol{X}\\
-h\boldsymbol{X}&g\boldsymbol{X}\end{bmatrix},

(123)

the covariance matrix of the pure state of the four-mode system ABCD
is obtained from (
109
)-(
112
) as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{ABCD}}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}&\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB,CD}}\\
\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB,CD}}^{T}&\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{CD}}\end{bmatrix},

(124)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&d_{1}&0\\
0&a&0&d_{2}\\
d_{1}&0&a&0\\
0&d_{2}&0&a\end{bmatrix},\quad\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{CD}}=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&d_{2}&0\\
0&a&0&d_{1}\\
d_{2}&0&a&0\\
0&d_{1}&0&a\end{bmatrix},\quad\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB,CD}}=\begin{bmatrix}g&0&h&0\\
0&-g&0&-h\\
h&0&g&0\\
0&-h&0&-g\end{bmatrix}.

(125)

The canonical operators describing partner modes CD are given by

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{CD}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}(f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}})-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g^{2}-h^{2}}\begin{bmatrix}g\boldsymbol{X}&-h\boldsymbol{X}\\
-h\boldsymbol{X}&g\boldsymbol{X}\end{bmatrix}(f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}})-\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}),

(126)

which establishes the partner formula for a two-mode symmetric
Gaussian state. Note that as
f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{B}})
is
obtained by replacing
x_{\text{A}}\rightarrow x_{\text{B}}
in

f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}})
, their window functions are
calculated by shifting the window functions of

f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{A}})
given in Eqs. (
91
)
and (
92
). Therefore, their behaviors are expressed by
Fig.
4
, except for the shift of the

126
) implies that the

given by
\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}}
and

f_{\psi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\text{AB}})
, we find that they are given by
linear combinations of functions which are localized around

x_{\text{A}}
and
x_{\text{B}}
, whose tails change depending on the

a_{\text{sc}}
.

IV
Monogamy and Separability

We regard the bipartite system AB as a subsystem embedded in the pure
four-mode state ABCD. Then an entanglement measure

\widetilde{E}(\text{A:B})
between A and B (internal entanglement) and
an entanglement measure
E(\text{AB:CD})
between AB and CD (external
entanglement) are expected to obey the following monogamy inequality

Camalet 2017a
;
Camalet 2017b
;
Camalet 2018
;
Zhu et al. 2020
;
Zhu et al. 2021
:

\widetilde{E}(\text{A:B})+E(\text{AB:CD})\leq\widetilde{E}_{\text{max}},

(127)

where
\widetilde{E}_{\text{max}}
is the maximum of

\widetilde{E}(\text{A:B})
. This inequality represents a trade-off
relation between internal and external entanglement and has been
proven to hold for finite-dimensional Hilbert space cases, including qubit
systems.
For qubit cases, explicit
forms of inequalities are presented in terms of various entanglement measures
(concurrence, entanglement of formation, and negativity).
In this paper, based on the specific representation of the four-mode Gaussian state (
124
) and (
125
) which
purifies AB, we show this type of monogamy inequality also holds for
Gaussian states.

IV.1
Parametrization of the bipartite Gaussian state

In the standard form, the covariance matrix (
116
) of
the bipartite symmetric Gaussian state AB includes three parameters

a,d_{1},d_{2}
. For later convenience, we parametrize it with
a,x,y

where
x,y
are defined by (
122
). By solving Eq. (
122
)
with respect to
d_{1},d_{2}
, we get

d_{1}=\frac{y}{2a}+\sqrt{\frac{y^{2}}{4a^{2}}-(x-a^{2}+1)},\quad d_{2}=\frac{y}{2a}-\sqrt{\frac{y^{2}}{4a^{2}}-(x-a^{2}+1)},

(128)

and
d_{1}-d_{2}=\sqrt{y^{2}/a^{2}-4(x-a^{2}+1)}\geq 0,\quad d_{1}d_{2}=x-a^{2}+1
.
Thus
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are expressed using
a,x,y
. Symplectic
eigenvalues of the covariance matrix
\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}
are given by

\displaystyle\nu_{1}^{2}=(a+d_{1})(a+d_{2})=x+y+1\geq 1,\quad\nu_{2}^{2}=(a-d_{1})(a-d_{2})=x-y+1\geq 1,

(129)

and

\mathrm{det}\,\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}=(a^{2}-d_{1}^{2})(a^{2}-d_{2}^{2})=\nu_{1}^{2}\nu_{2}^{2}=\tilde{\nu}_{1}^{2}\tilde{\nu}_{2}^{2}=(x+1)^{2}-y^{2}
.
Symplectic eigenvalues of the partially transposed covariance matrix

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{m}}_{\text{AB}}
are expressed as

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{1}^{2}=(a+d_{1})(a-d_{2})=2a^{2}-x-1+\sqrt{y^{2}-4a^{2}(x-a^{2}+1)},

(130)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{2}^{2}=(a-d_{1})(a+d_{2})=2a^{2}-x-1-\sqrt{y^{2}-4a^{2}(x-a^{2}+1)}.

(131)

The sum of these symplectic eigenvalues satisfies
\nu_{1}^{2}+\nu_{2}^{2}+\tilde{\nu}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{\nu}_{2}^{2}=4a^{2}
.
For real values of
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
, it holds

y^{2}\geq 4a^{2}(x-a^{2}+1).

(132)

The modes A and B are entangled if
0<\tilde{\nu}_{2}<1<\tilde{\nu}_{1}
, or equivalently,
(x+2)^{2}-y^{2}<4a^{2}
. The negativity of the state AB is given by

N_{\text{A:B}}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{max}\left[\frac{1}{\tilde{\nu}_{2}}-1,0\right].

(133)

For a fixed
a
, the minimum of
\tilde{\nu}_{2}
is attained at
x=y=0
and given by

\tilde{\nu}_{2}|_{\text{min}}=a-\sqrt{a^{2}-1}.

(134)

In this case, the bipartite state AB is a two-mode squeezed pure
state with a squeezing parameter
r=\cosh^{-1}a
, and its covariance
matrix is given by Eq. (
116
) with

d_{1}=-d_{2}=\sqrt{a^{2}-1}
. Thus the maximum of
N_{\text{A:B}}
is

N_{\text{A:B}}|_{\text{max}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(a-1+\sqrt{a^{2}-1}\right).

(135)

The bipartite state AB becomes separable at
\tilde{\nu}_{2}=1
, and this condition is equivalent to

(x+2)^{2}-y^{2}=4a^{2}.

(136)

With a fixed value of
a
, it is possible to draw a parameter region
in the
(x,y)
plane where
\boldsymbol{m}_{\text{AB}}
represents a physical
Gaussian state (Fig.
6
). The region is bounded by

x=|y|
corresponding to
\nu=1
(positivity of the state) and

y^{2}=4a^{2}(x-a^{2}+1)
corresponding to the reality condition of

d_{1,2}
. The region is divided into two regions: one corresponds to
entangled state, and the other corresponds to separable states. A pure
state is located at
x=y=0
, corresponding to a two-mode squeezed pure
state. The state becomes separable for
a=1
.

Symplectic eigenvalues of the four-mode state ABCD are given by

\displaystyle\sqrt{(a-d_{1})(a-d_{2})-(g-h)^{2}}=1,\quad\sqrt{(a+d_{1})(a+d_{2})-(g+h)^{2}}=1,

(137)

which implies the state ABCD is pure. Symplectic eigenvalues of the
partially transposed state with bipartition AB:CD are given by

\displaystyle(\tilde{\nu}_{2\pm})^{2}

\displaystyle=(a-d_{1})(a-d_{2})+(g-h)^{2}\pm 2|g-h|\sqrt{(a-d_{1})(a-d_{2})}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{x-y+1}\pm\sqrt{x-y})^{2},

(138)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\nu}_{1\pm})^{2}

\displaystyle=(a+d_{1})(a+d_{2})+(g+h)^{2}\pm 2|g+h|\sqrt{(a+d_{1})(a+d_{2})}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{x+y+1}\pm\sqrt{x+y})^{2}.

(139)

Therefore,
\tilde{\nu}_{2\pm}=\nu_{2}\pm\sqrt{\nu_{2}^{2}-1},\tilde{\nu}_{1\pm}=\nu_{1}\pm\sqrt{\nu_{1}^{2}-1}
,
and the negativity for the bipartition AB:CD is calculated as

\displaystyle N_{\text{AB:CD}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nu_{1}+\sqrt{\nu_{1}^{2}-1}\right)\left(\nu_{2}+\sqrt{\nu_{2}^{2}-1}\right)-\frac{1}{2}>0.

(140)

IV.2
Monogamy relation for Gaussian states

We examine the monogamy inequality (
127
) for
Gaussian states. For the qubit case treated in
Camalet 2017a
, as
the entanglement measure in this monogamy inequality,

\widetilde{E}(\text{A:B})
can be the negativity
N_{\text{A:B}}
.

E(\text{AB:CD})
is a decreasing function of the negativity

N_{\text{AB:CD}}
, and the explicit form of this function is presented
in
Camalet 2017a
. In the present analysis with Gaussian states,
we also adopt negativity as an entanglement measure to show a monogamy
inequality.

As we have already presented, negativities
N_{\text{A:B}}

V
Summary and conclusion

We investigated the emergence of separability for local bipartite
modes assigned to two spatial regions in the de Sitter universe. The
bipartite mode AB becomes separable after their separation exceeds the
Hubble horizon scale. To understand the emergence of this separability
from the viewpoint of entanglement monogamy, we considered
purification of the local mode AB and obtained the pure four-mode
state ABCD applying the partner formula. Then, we found the monogamy
inequality between the negativity
N(\text{A:B})
and

N(\text{AB:CD})
for the four-mode Gaussian state, which is an
extension of Camalet’s monogamy relation to continuous variable
systems. It is demonstrated that the separability of the mode AB can
be understood as the monogamy property between the internal and the
external entanglements, and the monogamy inequality provides a
sufficient condition for the separability of the local mode AB defined
from the quantum field. In the stochastic approach to inflation

Starobinsky 1988
, local oscillator modes are defined as long
wavelength components of the inflaton field. The introduced local
modes are treated as “classical” stochastic variables, and they obey
a Langevin equation with a stochastic noise originating from the short
wavelength quantum fluctuations. Although the stochastic approach to
inflation is a phenomenological treatment of quantum fields in the de
Sitter spacetime and is widely employed to investigate the physics
related to cosmic inflation, its justification is still missing. Our
investigation of this paper provides one reasoning to this method from
the viewpoint of quantum information; local modes in the de Sitter
universe lose quantum correlation when their separation exceeds the
cosmological horizon, and this behavior is related to delocalization
of partner modes.

The partner formula adopted in this study may provide a new
perspective on information sharing in multipartite quantum
systems. Indeed, as shown in Fig.
4
, the
spatial profiles of partner modes can be visualized and they are
helpful in capturing how the information of a system is shared with
its partners. The information stored in a system is lost but classical
properties of the system appear as a result of decoherence via
information sharing with its partners (environments). This direction
of investigation is closely related to the concept of “quantum
Darwinism”
Zurek 2009
which states that the
emergence of a classical
behavior of a quantum system, such as objectivity, is connected with
the amount of information of the system redundantly shared or stored
in the environment. Thus spatial profiles of partner modes of the
system may help to quantify this redundancy of the information
and to understand the quantum to classical transition in the early
universe.

(153)

\displaystyle N_{\text{A:D}}=\mathrm{max}\left[\frac{1}{2a-(\sqrt{x+y}-\sqrt{x-y})}-\frac{1}{2},0\right],

(154)

and
N_{\text{A:B}}
is given by (
133
). We can observe the
monogamy inequality (
151
) indeed holds for this
four-mode Gaussian state (Fig.
9
) because generated

of (
151
). However, Fig.
9
shows that

Quasisymmetric expansion of Hall-Littlewood symmetric functions
Darij Grinberg \addressmark 1, Ekaterina A. Vassilieva \addressmark 2

Abstract
In our previous works we introduced a q q -deformation of the generating functions for enriched P P -partitions. We call the evaluation of this generating functions on labelled chains, the q q -fundamental quasisymmetric functions. These functions interpolate between Gessel’s fundamental ( q = 0 q=0 ) and Stembridge’s peak ( q = 1 q=1 ) functions, the natural quasisymmetric expansions of Schur and Schur’s Q Q -symmetric functions. In this paper, we show that our q q -fundamental functions provide a quasisymmetric expansion of Hall-Littlewood S S -symmetric functions with parameter t = − q t=-q .

1.1
Integer partitions, Young tableaux and permutation statistics

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the set of positive integers and
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
be the set of positive and negative integers ordered by
-1<1<-2<2<-3<3<\dots
. (Our below results would actually be true for
any
total order on
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
, but we have chosen this one by force of habit.)
We embed
\mathbb{P}
into
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
and let
-\mathbb{P}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
be the set of all
-n
for
n\in\mathbb{P}
. For
n\in\mathbb{P}
write
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
the symmetric group on
[n]
.

A
partition

\lambda
of an integer
n
, denoted
\lambda\vdash n
, is a sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{p})
of
\ell(\lambda)=p
parts sorted in decreasing order such that
|\lambda|=\sum_{i}{\lambda_{i}}=n
. We denote the one-part partition
(n)
simply by
n
. A partition
\lambda
is represented as a Young diagram of
n=|\lambda|
boxes arranged in
\ell(\lambda)
left-justified rows so that the
i
-th row from the top contains
\lambda_{i}
boxes. Given a second partition
\mu
with
\ell(\mu)\leq\ell(\lambda)
such that
\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
(for all
i\leq\ell(\mu)
), delete the
\mu_{i}
leftmost boxes of the
i
-th row to get the diagram of
shape

\lambda/\mu
.

A Young diagram whose boxes are filled with positive integers such that the entries are weakly increasing along the rows and strictly increasing down the columns is called a
semistandard Young tableau
. If the entries are
1,2,\ldots,n
(each appearing exactly once), we call it a
standard Young tableau
. We denote
\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda/\mu)
(resp.
\operatorname{SSYT}(\lambda/\mu)
) the set of standard (resp. semistandard) Young tableaux of shape
\lambda/\mu
. A
marked semistandard Young tableau
is a Young diagram filled with integers in
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
such that the entries are weakly increasing along rows and columns and such that each row contains at most once each negative integer and that each column contains at most once each positive integer.

1.2
Hall-Littlewood symmetric functions

Consider the set of indeterminates
X=\left\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots\right\}
. Let
\Lambda
denote the ring of symmetric functions over

\mathbb{C}
. We use notations consistent with
[
7
]
. Namely, for
\lambda\vdash n
, let
m_{\lambda}(X)
,
h_{\lambda}(X)
,
e_{\lambda}(X)
,
p_{\lambda}(X)
and
s_{\lambda}(X)
denote the
monomial
,
complete homogeneous
,
elementary
,
power sum
and
Schur
symmetric functions over
X
indexed by
\lambda
. Fix a parameter
t\in\mathbb{C}
and define
q_{n}(X;t)\in\Lambda
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
as follows: Set
q_{0}(X;t)=1
and
q_{n}(X;t)=0
for all
n<0
. For all
n>0
, define
q_{n}(X;t)
in finitely many indeterminates by

q_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k};t)=\left(1-t\right)\sum_{i}x_{i}^{n}\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{x_{i}-tx_{j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}};

(1)

then define
q_{n}(X;t)\in\Lambda
by letting
k\to\infty
.
The generating function for the
q_{n}
is

\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{n}(X;t)u^{n}=\prod_{i}\frac{1-x_{i}tu}{1-x_{i}u}.

(2)

The family
(q_{n}(X;t))_{n}
generates a subalgebra of
\Lambda
that we denote
\Lambda_{t}
. In particular,
\Lambda_{t}
is a proper subalgebra of
\Lambda
when
t
is a root of unity.

Definition 1

(Hall-Littlewood
S
-symmetric functions)
.

For any skew shape
\lambda/\mu
, define the
Hall-Littlewood
S
-symmetric function
indexed by
\lambda/\mu
as

S_{\lambda/\mu}(X;t)=\det\left(q_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}(X;t)\right)_{i,j}.

(3)

Setting
t=0
in Definition
1
, we obtain
S_{\lambda/\mu}(X;0)=s_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
.
When
t=-1
,
S_{\lambda/\mu}(X;-1)
is a variant of
Schur’s
Q
-function
indexed by
\lambda/\mu
.

We end this section by defining a classical ring homomorphism.

Definition 2

(Homomorphism
\theta_{t}
)
.

Define a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism
\theta_{t}:\Lambda\longrightarrow\Lambda_{t}
by setting

\theta_{t}(h_{n})(X)=q_{n}(X;t)\qquad\text{for all }n\geq 0.

In particular, as a consequence of Definition
1
, we have

\theta_{t}(s_{\lambda/\mu})(X)=S_{\lambda/\mu}(X;t).

It is also easy to see that
\theta_{t}(p_{n})(X)=(1-t^{n})p_{n}(X)
.

2
Relating Hall-Littlewood and

-fundamentals functions

The ring of symmetric functions
\Lambda
is a subalgebra of
\operatorname{QSym}
and any symmetric function may be expanded in quasisymmetric bases. The relation between Schur functions (i.e Hall-Littlewood

-functions with parameter

be a skew shape. Gessel shows in

S_{\lambda/\mu}(X;0)=s_{\lambda/\mu}(X)=\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda/\mu)}L^{(0)}_{\operatorname{Des}(T)}(X).

(6)

On the other hand, Stembridge shows in
[

that

S_{\lambda/\mu}(X;-1)=\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda/\mu)}L^{(1)}_{\operatorname{Des}(T)}(X).

(7)

To understand how these relations generalise for general

seems to be a very legitimate question. We state our result and some significant consequences.

2.2
Cauchy-like formula for Hall-Littlewood symmetric functions

We use Theorem
2
to provide an alternative proof of a classical Cauchy-like formula for Hall-Littlewood
S
-symmetric functions. Denote
Y=\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots\}
an additional alphabet of commutating indeterminates independent of and commuting with
X
. Let
XY=\{x_{i}y_{j}\}_{i,j}
be the product alphabet. We first show the following proposition.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be a permutation. Extend the definition of
\Gamma^{(q)}
to the alphabet
XY
by considering
P_{\pi}
-partitions
(f,g):i\mapsto(f(i),g(i))
with values in the set
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
, which we equip with the lexicographic order. Declare a pair
(i,j)\in\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
to be negative if and only if
j
is negative. Then,

\Gamma^{(q)}(P_{\pi})(XY)=\sum_{(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{P}^{\pm}}([n],<_{\pi})}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}q^{[g(i)<0]}x_{f(i)}y_{|g(i)|}

The
q
-fundamental indexed by
\pi
on the product alphabet
XY
satisfies

L^{(q)}_{\pi}(XY)=\Gamma^{(q)}(P_{\pi})(XY)=\sum_{\sigma\circ\tau=\pi}L^{(0)}_{\sigma}(X)L^{(q)}_{\tau}(Y).

(10)

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one in
[
8
, thm 6.11]
and not detailed here.
∎

In
[
7
, III. 4. Eq. (4.7)]
, Macdonald provides a Cauchy-like formula for Hall-Littlewood symmetric functions:

q_{n}(XY;t)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}s_{\lambda}(X)S_{\lambda}(Y;t).

(11)

Proposition 5
.

Equation (
11
) is a direct consequence of Proposition
4
and Theorem
2
.

Proof.

Fix
q\in\mathbb{C}
and use Proposition
4
to write

q_{n}(XY;-q)=L_{\operatorname{id}_{n}}^{(q)}(XY)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}L_{\sigma^{-1}}^{(0)}(X)L_{\sigma\phantom{{}^{-1}}}^{(q)}\!\!(Y),

where
\operatorname{id}_{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the identity permutation. The RS correspondence allows to reindex the sum over standard Young tableaux.

\displaystyle q_{n}(XY;-q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\sum_{T,U\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda)}L_{n,\operatorname{Des}(T)}^{(0)}(X)L_{n,\operatorname{Des}(U)}^{(q)}(Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\left(\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda)}L_{n,\operatorname{Des}(T)}^{(0)}(X)\right)\left(\sum_{U\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda)}L_{n,\operatorname{Des}(U)}^{(q)}(Y)\right).

Applying Theorem
2
yields Equation (
11
).
∎

3
Proof of Theorem
1

We now approach the proof of Theorem
1
.
We generalize the setting somewhat:
Let
\prec
be a total order on
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}

(for example, our order
<
, or any other total order).
Define a further binary relation
R
on
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
as follows:
For any two elements
i,j\in\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
, set

\left(i\ R\ j\right)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \left(i\preccurlyeq j\text{
but not }i=j\in-\mathbb{P}\right).

Definition 7
.

For each non-negative integer
n
, define the formal power series

H_{n}:=H_{n}(X^{\pm}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{n}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{P}^{\pm}\right)^{n};\\
i_{1}\ R\ i_{2}\ R\ \cdots\ R\ i_{n}\end{subarray}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}

on the alphabet
X^{\pm}=\{x_{-1},x_{1},x_{-2},x_{2},\dots\}
.
Moreover, set
H_{n}=0
for all
n<0
.

Furthermore, for any labelled poset
([n],<_{P})
, define
the new generating function

\Gamma^{\pm}\left([n],<_{P}\right)\in\mathbb{C}\left[\left[X^{\pm}\right]\right]

of enriched
P
-partitions by

\Gamma^{\pm}([n],<_{P}):=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{P}^{\pm}}([n],<_{P})}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}x_{f(i)}.

This is a lift of our
q
-deformed generating function

\Gamma^{(q)}([n],<_{P})
.
Indeed, recall the homomorphism
\varpi:\mathbb{C}\left[\left[X^{\pm}\right]\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}\left[\left[X\right]\right]
, such that
\varpi(x_{i})=q^{[i<0]}x_{|i|}
for
x_{i}\in X^{\pm}
. Clearly

\varpi(\Gamma^{\pm}([n],<_{P}))=\Gamma^{(q)}([n],<_{P}).

Proposition 6
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be two partitions such that
\lambda/\mu
is a skew shape. We have

\Gamma^{\pm}([n],<_{\lambda/\mu})=\det\left(H_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}\right)_{i,j\in\left[k\right]}.

(12)

Proof.

We want to apply
[
2
, §7]
. To this end, we
introduce a new relation. Let
\overline{R}
be the complement of the binary relation
R
. (Thus,

\overline{R}
is the binary relation on
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
defined by

\left(i\ \overline{R}\ j\right)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \left(\text{not
}i\ R\ j\right)
.) It is easy to see that both relations
R
and

\overline{R}
are transitive. Hence, the relation
R
is semitransitive
(meaning that if
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
satisfy
a\ R\ b\ R\ c
, then
a\ R\ d
or
d\ R\ c
). Therefore,
[
2
, Theorem 11]
yields that the power series
s_{\lambda/\mu}^{R}
(defined in
[
2
, §7]
) counts
R
-tableaux of shape
\lambda/\mu
. But the
R
-tableaux of shape
\lambda/\mu
are precisely the enriched
P_{\lambda/\mu}
-partitions,
whereas the series
s_{\lambda/\mu}^{R}
is our
\det\left(H_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}\right)_{i,j\in\left[k\right]}
.
∎

In order to prove Theorem
1
from (
12
), we need to show that
\varpi(H_{n}(X^{\pm}))=q_{n}(X;-q)
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We proceed in three steps. First we have the following proposition, which follows easily from the definition of
H_{n}
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then,

H_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\text{ is a size-}k\\
\text{subset of
}-\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\text{ is a size-}\left(n-k\right)\\
\text{multisubset of }\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{u\in U}x_{u}\right)\left(\prod_{v\in V}x_{v}\right)

(where the product over
v\in V
takes each element with its multiplicity). In
particular,
H_{n}
does not depend on the order
\prec
.

Secondly, we express
q_{n}(X;-q)
in terms of elementary and complete homogeneous symmetric functions.

Lemma 1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
q\in\mathbb{C}
. Then,

q_{n}(X;-q)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{k}e_{k}h_{n-k}.

(13)

Proof.

From (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n}q_{n}(X;-q)u^{n}=\prod_{i\geq 1}\dfrac{1+qx_{i}u}{1-x_{i}u}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(\prod_{i\geq 1}\left(1+qx_{i}u\right)\right)}_{=\sum_{n}q^{n}e_{n}t^{n}}\underbrace{\left(\prod_{i\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{1-x_{i}u}\right)}_{=\sum_{n}h_{n}u^{n}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n}q^{n}e_{n}u^{n}\right)\left(\sum_{n}h_{n}u^{n}\right)=\sum_{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{k}e_{k}h_{n-k}\right)u^{n}.

Extracting coefficients in
u^{n}
on both sides yields the desired result.
∎

Finally, use Proposition
7
and Lemma
1
to relate
H_{n}
and
q_{n}
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then,

\varpi\left(H_{n}(X^{\pm})\right)=q_{n}(X;-q).

Proof.

Applying the map
\varpi
to both sides of Proposition
7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varpi\left(H_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\text{ is a size-}k\\
\text{subset of
}-\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\text{ is a size-}\left(n-k\right)\\
\text{multisubset of }\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{u\in U}\varpi\left(x_{u}\right)\right)\left(\prod_{v\in V}\varpi\left(x_{v}\right)\right)

(14)

(since
\varpi
is a continuous
\mathbf{k}
-algebra homomorphism).
However, each size-
k
subset
U
of
-\mathbb{P}
satisfies

\prod_{u\in U}\underbrace{\varpi\left(x_{u}\right)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}=qx_{-u}\\
\text{(since }u\in-\mathbb{P}\text{)}\end{subarray}}=\prod_{u\in U}\left(qx_{-u}\right)=q^{k}\prod_{u\in U}x_{-u}\qquad\text{(since $\left|U\right|=k$),}

whereas each size-
\left(n-k\right)
-multisubset
V
of
\mathbb{P}

satisfies

\prod_{v\in V}\underbrace{\varpi\left(x_{v}\right)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}=x_{v}\\
\text{(since }v\in\mathbb{P}\text{)}\end{subarray}}=\prod_{v\in V}x_{v}.

In light of these two equalities, we can rewrite
(
14
) as

\displaystyle\varpi\left(H_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\text{ is a size-}k\\
\text{subset of
}-\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\text{ is a size-}\left(n-k\right)\\
\text{multisubset of }\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}q^{k}\left(\prod_{u\in U}x_{-u}\right)\left(\prod_{v\in V}x_{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{k}\underbrace{\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\text{ is a
size-}k\\
\text{subset of }-\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\ \ \prod_{u\in U}x_{-u}\right)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\text{ is a size-}k\\
\text{subset of
}\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\ \ \prod_{u\in U}x_{u}\\
=e_{k}\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\text{ is a size-}\left(n-k\right)\\
\text{multisubset of
}\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\ \ \prod_{v\in V}x_{v}\right)}_{=h_{n-k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{k}e_{k}h_{n-k}.

By Equation (
13
), we can rewrite this
as
\varpi\left(H_{n}\right)=q_{n}(X;-q)
.
∎

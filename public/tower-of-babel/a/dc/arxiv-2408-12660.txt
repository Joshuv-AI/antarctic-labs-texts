Abstract
. Given a linear recurrence of the form c n = a 1 ​ c n − 1 + ⋯ + a j ​ c n − j c_{n}=a_{1}c_{n-1}+\cdots+a_{j}c_{n-j} , it is well-known that c n = ∑ r p r ​ ( n ) ​ r n c_{n}=\sum_{r}p_{r}(n)r^{n} , where the sum is taken over the set of characteristic roots and each p r ​ ( n ) p_{r}(n) is some polynomial. We give a closed formula for the coefficients of each polynomial p r ​ ( n ) p_{r}(n) for any linear recurrence of this form.

1.
Introduction

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{1\leq n\leq j}
be a sequence of complex numbers and
\{c_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be the sequence defined by the recurrence
c_{n}=a_{1}c_{n-1}+\cdots+a_{j}c_{n-j}
for all
n\geq j
, where the initial conditions
\{c_{k}\}_{0\leq k\leq j-1}
are fixed. Such a sequence
\{c_{n}\}
is called a
linear recurrence with constant coefficients
. The
characteristic polynomial
of
\{c_{n}\}
is the polynomial
p(z)=z^{j}-a_{1}z^{j-1}-a_{2}z^{j-2}-\cdots-a_{j-1}z-a_{j}
. A
characteristic root
of the recurrence satisfied by
\{c_{n}\}
is defined to be a root of
p
.

A classical result (e.g.,
[
8
, Theorem IV.9]
) in the theory of recurrence relations states that
\{c_{n}\}
admits a
generalized Binet formula
: for all
n\geq 0
, we have that
c_{n}=\sum_{r}p_{r}(n)r^{n}
, where the sum is taken over the set of characteristic roots and each
p_{r}(n)
is a polynomial depending on the initial conditions. We construct closed formulas for the coefficients of each
p_{r}(n)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Define the sequence
\{c_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
by the recurrence relation

(1)

\displaystyle c_{n}\ =\ a_{1}c_{n-1}+\cdots+a_{j}c_{n-j}

with
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{j-1}\in\mathbb{C}
. For each characteristic root
r
and nonnegative integer
k\leq j-1
, let

(i)

m_{r}
be the multiplicity of
r
,

(ii)

s_{r,k,h}\vcentcolon=\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}{k+\ell\choose h}a_{\ell}r^{-k-\ell}
, and

(iii)

{N}_{r}\vcentcolon=(-1)^{m_{r}-1}\left(\sum_{\ell\geq m_{r}}{\ell\choose m_{r}}a_{\ell}r^{-\ell}\right)^{-1}
.

Then
c_{n}=\sum_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{r}}\kappa_{r,i}n^{m_{r}-i}r^{n}
, where the outer sum is taken over the set of characteristic roots and

(2)

\displaystyle\kappa_{r,i}\ =\ \frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{(m_{r}-i)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varnothing\neq\{d_{1},\dots,d_{p}\}\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,i-1\}\\
d_{1}<\cdots<d_{p}=i-1\end{subarray}}{N}_{r}^{p}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{j-1}c_{k_{1}}s_{r,k_{1},d_{1}}\prod_{u=1}^{p-1}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{j-1}\frac{k_{2}^{m_{r}-1-d_{u}}r^{k_{2}}s_{r,k_{2},d_{u+1}}}{(-1)^{d_{u}-1}(m_{r}-1-d_{u})!}.

Computing
p_{r}(n)
is traditionally done by algorithmically computing the generating function for
\{c_{n}\}
and determining its partial fraction decomposition. Algorithms for computing partial fraction decompositions are well-studied, some of which can be found in
[
10
,
11
,
15
]
.

From Theorem
1.1
, one may obtain a closed formula for the partial fraction decomposition of a proper rational function, circumventing any algorithmic computations. A closed formula for such a partial fraction decomposition was also recently given by Chargeishvili, Fekésházy, Somogyi, and Van Thurenhout in
[
6
]
. Of course, from
[
6
, Equation 11]
, a closed formula for the linear recurrence may be obtained by applying Newton’s binomial expansion to each term in the composition. However, our formula contrasts favorably with this approach in two ways:

(i)

the formula given for each
\kappa_{r,i}
is not a function of any of the other characteristic roots, and

(ii)

our formula provides an exact formula for each coefficient of
p_{r}(n)
rather than representing
p_{r}(n)
as a sum of binomial coefficients.

Thus, if one wished to compute a particular
p_{r}(n)
, none of the roots other than
r
would need to be computed.

The primary application of Theorem
1.1
is toward an implementation to symbolically compute the generalized Binet formula of any linear recurrence with constant coefficients. Most of the difficulty lies in accounting for multiple roots, since each leading coefficient
\kappa_{r,1}
reduces to

(3)

\displaystyle\kappa_{r,1}\ =\ \frac{N_{r}}{(m_{r}-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}s_{r,k,0}.

Thus, Theorem
1.1
simplifies dramatically if all of the characteristic roots of the recurrence are of multiplicity one. A prototypical class of recurrences with this property are the
j
-nacci numbers, which are defined by the recurrence
F(n,j)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}F(n-\ell,j)
and initial conditions
F(0,j)=F(1,j)=\cdots=F(j-2,j)=0
and
F(j-1,j)=1
. Letting
j\geq 2
and
n\geq 0
, from Theorem
1.1
we have the formula

(4)

\displaystyle F(n,j)\ =\ \sum_{r}\frac{r^{n+1}(1-r^{-j})}{r^{j+1}-j},

where the sum is taken over the set of roots of the characteristic polynomial
x^{j}-x^{j-1}-\cdots-x-1
. This follows from Theorem
1.1
by a simplification of geometric series and the identity
\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}r^{k}=r^{j}
. This result is, of course, equivalent to the various closed forms for the
j
-nacci numbers given in the literature (e.g.,
[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
).

However, our formula permitting multiple roots allows us to consider more complicated recurrences. Exposited by Ahlgren in
[
2
]
, Ramanujan stated in his last notebook
[
14
, pg.54]
that the circular summation of the sixth powers of the symmetric theta function decomposes into a product of Ramanujan’s general theta function and another function
F_{6}
. By a formula for
F_{6}(q)
given in
[
2
, Theorem 1]
, Zabolotskiy notes in
[
13
]
that the exponents in the expansion of
F_{6}(q^{2})
are precisely the nonnegative integers congruent to
0,5,8,9

\mathrm{mod~}12
, which satisfy the recurrence
c_{n}=2c_{n-1}-2c_{n-2}+2c_{n-3}-c_{n-4}
. By definition,
c_{0}=0
,
c_{1}=5
,
c_{2}=8
, and
c_{3}=9
. The characteristic roots are
1,i,-i
, which have multiplicities
2,1,1
respectively. Applying Theorem
1.1
, we find that
\kappa_{1,1}=3,~\kappa_{1,2}=1,~\kappa_{i,1}=(-1-i)/2,~\kappa_{-i,1}=(-1+i)/2
, thus obtaining

(5)

\displaystyle c_{n}\ =\ 3n+1+\frac{1}{2}(-1-i)i^{n}+\frac{1}{2}(-1+i)(-i)^{n}\ =\ 3n+1+\sin\left(\frac{\pi n}{2}\right)-\cos\left(\frac{\pi n}{2}\right).

We now consider another example demonstrating the power of allowing for multiple roots: the
generalized pentagonal numbers
are the exponents in the expansion given by the celebrated pentagonal number theorem
[
4
]
, which asserts the
q
-series identity

(6)

\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})\ =\ \sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k}q^{k(3k-1)/2}.

That is,
n
is a generalized pentagonal number if and only if
n=k(3k-1)/2
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We aim to prove a closed formula for the generalized pentagonal numbers in increasing order, which in turn would provide a singly infinite series expansion of monomials. By
[
9
]
, this sequence satisfies the recurrence
c_{n}=c_{n-1}+2c_{n-2}-2c_{n-3}-c_{n-4}+c_{n-5}
with
c_{0}=0
,
c_{1}=1
,
c_{2}=2
,
c_{3}=5
, and
c_{4}=7
. The characteristic roots are
1
and
-1
, which have multiplicities
3
and
2
respectively. Applying Theorem
1.1
, we find that
\kappa_{1,1}=3/8,~\kappa_{1,2}=3/8,~\kappa_{1,3}=1/16,~\kappa_{-1,1}=-1/8,~\kappa_{-1,2}=-1/16
, thus obtaining

(7)

\displaystyle c_{n}\ =\ \frac{3n^{2}}{8}+\frac{3n}{8}+\frac{1}{16}+\left(-\frac{n}{8}-\frac{1}{16}\right)(-1)^{n},

which matches the formula given by Barry in
[
3
, Section 7]
.

The rest of the paper is dedicated to proving Theorem
1.1
, followed by a discussion on directions for future work.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we appeal to the fact that the coefficient of the asymptotically dominant term in the expansion formula for
\{c_{n}\}
is straightforward to compute by
[
8
, Note IV.26]
. In particular, to compute the coefficient of
n^{m_{r}-i}r^{n}
, we subtract all terms which asymptotically dominate
n^{m_{r}-i}r^{n}
, obtaining a new linear recurrence
\{c_{n}^{\prime}\}
satisfying
c^{\prime}_{n}=\kappa_{r,i}n^{m_{r}-i}r^{n}(1+O(1/n))
. To leverage
[
8
, Note IV.26]
, we first must compute the generating function of
\{c_{n}\}
in closed form. To do this, we break
\{c_{n}\}
into a linear combination of a simpler sequence, which we call the
(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})
-nacci sequence.

Definition 2.1

(
(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})
-nacci sequence)
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{1\leq n\leq j}
be a sequence of complex numbers. Define the sequence
\{F_{n}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}\}_{n\geq 0}
, which we call the
(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})
-nacci sequence, by the recurrence relation

(8)

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}\ =\ \sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}F_{n-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})},

with
F_{0}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}=F_{1}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}=\cdots=F_{j-2}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}=0
and
F_{j-1}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}=1
.

The upside of considering the simpler
(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})
-nacci sequence is that its generating function is easy to compute in closed form. Thus, once we precisely describe the linear combination yielding
\{c_{n}\}
, the desired generating function formula for
\{c_{n}\}
quickly follows.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{1\leq n\leq j}
be a sequence of complex numbers. Then

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}F_{n}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}z^{k}\ =\ \frac{z^{j-1}}{1-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}z^{\ell}}.

Proof.

To determine the ordinary generating function of
\{F^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}_{n}\}
, we use the standard ansatz (see
[
1
]
for example) that this function is rational with denominator
1-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}z^{\ell}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}z^{\ell}\right)\sum_{k\geq 0}F_{k}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}z^{k}\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k\geq 0}F_{k}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}z^{k}-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{\ell}F_{k}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}z^{k+\ell}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k\geq 0}F_{k}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}z^{k}-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq\ell}a_{\ell}F_{k-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}z^{k}

\displaystyle\overset{*}{=}\ F_{j-1}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}z^{j-1}+\sum_{k\geq j}\left(F_{k}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}F_{k-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}\right)z^{k}

(10)

\displaystyle=\ F_{j-1}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}z^{j-1}\ =\ z^{j-1},

where the starred equality follows since
F_{k}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}=0
if
k\leq j-2
. So

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}F_{k}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}z^{k}\ =\ \frac{z^{j-1}}{1-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}z^{\ell}},

as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Define the sequence
\{c_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
by the recurrence relation
c_{n}=a_{1}c_{n-1}+a_{2}c_{n-2}+\cdots+a_{j}c_{n-j}
with
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{j-1}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then for all
n\geq j
,

(12)

\displaystyle c_{n}\ =\ \sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}{\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-1-k-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}}.

In
[
12
, pg.6]
, Liu proves a result similar to Lemma
2.3
, using instead an explicit formula for
F_{n}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}
. As we are ultimately only concerned with the generating function of
F_{n}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}
, we need not consider its explicit form.

We now recall the definition of the Kronecker delta for use in the proof of Lemma
2.3
. For two numbers
k,k^{\prime}
, the Kronecker delta is defined by

(13)

\displaystyle\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}\ \vcentcolon=\ \begin{cases}1&\text{if~}k=k^{\prime},\\
0&\text{if~}k\neq k^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

We have that

(14)

\displaystyle Q(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})\ \vcentcolon=\ \begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{j-1}&a_{j}\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0\end{bmatrix}

is the
j\times j
companion matrix of the
(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})
-nacci sequence. This matrix encodes the recurrence relation for the
(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})
-nacci sequence in that

(15)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}F^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}_{n+1}\\
F^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}_{n}\\
\vdots\\
F^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}_{n-j+2}\end{bmatrix}\ =\ Q(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})\begin{bmatrix}F^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}_{n}\\
F^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}_{n-1}\\
\vdots\\
F^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}_{n-j+1}\end{bmatrix}.

For each
0\leq k\leq j-1
, let
e_{k,n}
be such that
c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}e_{k,n}
. From the linear recurrence satisfied by
\{c_{n}\}
, we deduce
e_{k,n}=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}e_{k,n-\ell}
. As this is the same recurrence satisfied by the
(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})
-nacci sequence,

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}e_{k,n+1}\\
e_{k,n}\\
\vdots\\
e_{k,n-j+2}\end{bmatrix}\ =\ Q(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})\begin{bmatrix}e_{k,n}\\
e_{k,n-1}\\
\vdots\\
e_{k,n-j+1}\end{bmatrix}.

Thus,

(17)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}e_{k,n+j-1}\\
e_{k,n+j-2}\\
\vdots\\
e_{k,n}\end{bmatrix}\ =\ \left(Q(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})\right)^{n}\begin{bmatrix}e_{k,j-1}\\
e_{k,j-2}\\
\vdots\\
e_{k,0}\end{bmatrix}\ =\ \left(Q(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})\right)^{n}\begin{bmatrix}\delta_{k,j-1}\\
\delta_{k,j-2}\\
\vdots\\
\delta_{k,0}\end{bmatrix}.

We claim that
\left(Q(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})\right)^{n}
equals

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-1-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\sum_{\ell\geq 2}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\cdots&\sum_{\ell\geq j-1}a_{\ell}F_{n+2j-3-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\sum_{\ell\geq j}a_{\ell}F_{n+2j-2-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}\\
\vskip 1.0pt&&&&\\
\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-2-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\sum_{\ell\geq 2}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-1-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\cdots&\sum_{\ell\geq j-1}a_{\ell}F_{n+2j-4-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\sum_{\ell\geq j}a_{\ell}F_{n+2j-3-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}F_{n+1-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\sum_{\ell\geq 2}a_{\ell}F_{n+2-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\cdots&\sum_{\ell\geq j-1}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-1-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\sum_{\ell\geq j}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}\\
\vskip 1.0pt&&&&\\
\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}F_{n-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\sum_{\ell\geq 2}a_{\ell}F_{n+1-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\cdots&\sum_{\ell\geq j-1}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-2-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}&\sum_{\ell\geq j}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-1-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}\end{bmatrix}

for any integer
n\geq j
. Indeed, let
q_{a,b}^{(n)}
denote the
(a,b)
th
entry of
(Q(a_{1},\dots,a_{j}))^{n}
. By
[
7
, Theorem 3.2]
,

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{a,b}^{(n)}z^{n}\ =\ \frac{z^{a-b}\left(1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{b-1}a_{\ell}z^{\ell}\right)}{1-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}z^{\ell}}

if
a\geq b
, and

(19)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{a,b}^{(n)}z^{n}\ =\ \frac{\sum_{\ell\geq b}a_{\ell}z^{a-b+\ell}}{1-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}z^{\ell}}

if
a<b
. Lemma
2.2
gives that

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}F_{n}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}z^{k}\ =\ \frac{z^{j-1}}{1-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}a_{\ell}z^{\ell}}.

From these formulas, we see that

(21)

\displaystyle q_{a,b}^{(n)}\ =\ F_{n+j-1+b-a}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{b-1}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-1+b-a-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}\ =\ \sum_{\ell\geq b}F_{n+j-1+b-a-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}

for any
a,b
, verifying the formula for
(Q(a_{1},\dots,a_{j}))^{n}
.

The last entry
e_{k,n}
, of the vector in (
17
) is obtained by taking the
(j-k)
th
entry (with entries numbered from left to right starting from 1) of the last row of
\left(Q(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})\right)^{n}
. Thus,

(22)

\displaystyle e_{k,n}\ =\ \sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-1-k-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})},

completing the proof.
∎

From Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, we obtain a closed formula for the generating function of an arbitrary linear recurrence with constant coefficients. We note that this formula is equivalent to other such generating function formulas in the literature, such as
[
16
, Theorem 5]
; however, writing the formula in this particular manner is conducive to ultimately simplifying the formula for
\kappa_{r,i}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Define the sequence
\{c_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
by the recurrence relation

(23)

\displaystyle c_{n}\ =\ a_{1}c_{n-1}+a_{2}c_{n-2}+\cdots+a_{j}c_{n-j}

with
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{j-1}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}z^{n}\ =\ \frac{\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}z^{k+\ell}+(1-a_{1}z-\cdots-a_{j}z^{j})\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}z^{k}}{1-a_{1}z-\cdots-a_{j}z^{j}}.

Proof.

By Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}z^{n}\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}z^{k}+\sum_{n\geq j}z^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}{\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}F_{n+j-1-k-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}z^{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}{\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}\sum_{n\geq j}F_{n+j-1-k-\ell}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}}z^{n}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}z^{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}{\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}\sum_{n\geq 2j-1-k-\ell}F_{n}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}}z^{n-j+1+k+\ell}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}z^{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}{\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}\sum_{n\geq j-1}F_{n}^{(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})}}z^{n-j+1+k+\ell}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}z^{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}\frac{z^{k+\ell}}{1-a_{1}z-\cdots-a_{j}z^{j}}

(25)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}z^{k+\ell}+(1-a_{1}z-\cdots-a_{j}z^{j})\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}z^{k}}{1-a_{1}z-\cdots-a_{j}z^{j}}.

∎

The final lemma before the proof of Theorem
1.1
, Lemma
2.5
, is crucial toward simplifying our formula for
\kappa_{r,i}
. In particular, it is due to this result that our formula for
\kappa_{r,i}
does not depend on any characteristic root other than
r
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
p(z)=z^{j}-a_{1}z^{j-1}-\cdots-a_{j}
. Then for any nonzero distinct roots
r_{1},r_{2}
of
p
,

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}k^{M_{1}}r_{1}^{k}\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}{k+\ell\choose M_{2}}a_{\ell}r_{2}^{-k-\ell}\ =\ 0

for any nonnegative integers
M_{1}\leq m_{r_{1}}-1
and
M_{2}\leq m_{r_{2}}-1
, where
m_{r_{1}}
and
m_{r_{2}}
are the multiplicities of
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
respectively.

Proof.

Rewriting the sum as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}k^{M_{1}}r_{1}^{k}\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}{k+\ell\choose M_{2}}a_{\ell}r_{2}^{-k-\ell}\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{j}a_{\ell}r_{2}^{-\ell}\sum_{k=j-\ell}^{j-1}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{k}{k+\ell\choose M_{2}}k^{M_{1}}

(27)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{j}a_{\ell}r_{2}^{-j}r_{1}^{-\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{k}{j+k\choose M_{2}}(j+k-\ell)^{M_{1}},

we see that it suffices to show that
b_{\ell}\vcentcolon=r_{2}^{-j}r_{1}^{-\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\left(r_{1}/r_{2}\right)^{k}{j+k\choose M_{2}}(j+k-\ell)^{M_{1}}
satisfies the linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial
z^{j}p(1/z)=a_{j}z^{j}+a_{j-1}z^{j-1}+\cdots+a_{1}z-1
for all
\ell\geq j
. We proceed by determining the generating function of
\{b_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 0}
. Splitting into two series, we observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 0}b_{\ell}z^{\ell}

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{\ell\geq 0}z^{\ell}\left(r_{2}^{-j}r_{1}^{-\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{k}{j+k\choose M_{2}}(j+k-\ell)^{M_{1}}\right)-\sum_{\ell\geq 0}z^{\ell}\left(r_{2}^{-j}r_{1}^{-\ell}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{\ell}{j+\ell\choose M_{2}}j^{M_{1}}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle\ =\ r_{2}^{-j}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\left(\frac{z}{r_{1}}\right)^{\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{k}{j+k\choose M_{2}}(j+k-\ell)^{M_{1}}-\frac{D(z)}{(1-z/r_{2})^{M_{2}+1}}

for some polynomial
D(z)
of degree at most
M_{2}
.
Recognizing that
\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\left(r_{1}/r_{2}\right)^{k}{j+k\choose M_{2}}(j+k-\ell)^{M_{1}}
is a Cauchy product, we see that

(29)

\displaystyle r_{2}^{-j}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\left(\frac{z}{r_{1}}\right)^{\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{k}{j+k\choose M_{2}}(j+k-\ell)^{M_{1}}\ =\ \frac{A(z)}{(1-z/r_{1})^{M_{1}+1}(1-z/r_{2})^{M_{2}+1}}

for some polynomial
A(z)
of degree at most
M_{1}+M_{2}
. Let
L(z)=(1-z/r_{1})^{M_{1}+1}(1-z/r_{2})^{M_{2}+1}
. Since
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are distinct,
\deg(A)\leq M_{1}+M_{2}<j
. So (
28
) and (
29
) show that
\{b_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 0}
is a linear combination of sequences which, by
[
8
, pg.255]
, all satisfy the linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial
z^{M_{1}+M_{2}+2}L(1/z)
for all
\ell\geq j
. As
r_{1},r_{2}
are distinct,
z^{M_{1}+M_{2}+2}L(1/z)
divides
z^{j}p(1/z)
, whence it follows that
b_{\ell}
satisfies the linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial
z^{j}p(1/z)
for all
\ell\geq j
.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathbf{u}=\{c_{n}\}
be as in Theorem
1.1
. For each characteristic root
r
, let
p^{\mathbf{u}}_{r}(n)
be the polynomial such that
c_{n}=\sum_{r}p^{\mathbf{u}}_{r}(n)r^{n}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By a standard result in the theory of recurrence relations (e.g.,
[
8
, Theorem IV.9]
), we know that
c_{n}=\sum_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{r}}\kappa_{r,i}n^{m_{r}-i}r^{n}

for some constants
\kappa_{r,i}
. So it suffices to show that formula (
2
) holds for all characteristic roots
r
and
1\leq i\leq m_{r}
.

Let
\mathbf{u}=\{c_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Let
r
be a characteristic root, let
1\leq i\leq m_{r}
, and let
S\not\ni r
be the set of characteristic roots greater than or equal to
r
in modulus. Define

(30)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\ =\ \left\{c_{n}-\sum_{R\in S}p^{\mathbf{u}}_{R}(n)R^{n}-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}n^{m_{r}-v}r^{n}\right\}_{n\geq 0}.

Noting that
\kappa_{r,i}=[z^{m_{r}-i}]~p_{r}^{\mathbf{u}}(z)=[z^{m_{r}-i}]~p_{r}^{\mathbf{u^{\prime}}}(z)
, we proceed by computing
[z^{m_{r}-i}]~p_{r}^{\mathbf{u^{\prime}}}(z)
. As
n^{M}R^{n}
satisfies recurrence (
1
) for any characteristic root
R
and
M\leq m_{R}-1
, it follows that
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}
also satisfies recurrence (
1
) with initial conditions
\{c_{k}-\sum_{R\in S}p^{\mathbf{u}}_{R}(k)R^{k}-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}\}_{0\leq k\leq j-1}
. Let
p(z)
be the characteristic polynomial of (
1
). Then by Proposition
2.4
, the generating function of
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}
is

(31)

\displaystyle f(z)\ \vcentcolon=\ \frac{1}{p(z)}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(c_{k}-\sum_{R\in S}p^{\mathbf{u}}_{R}(k)R^{k}-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}\right)\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}z^{k+\ell}+p(z)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}z^{k}\right).

Note that
f
has a single dominant pole
r^{-1}
with multiplicity
m_{r}-i+1
since the asymptotically dominant term of
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}
is
\kappa_{r,i}n^{m_{r}-i}r^{n}
. So by
[
8
, Note IV.26]
,
\kappa_{r,i}=C/(m_{r}-i)!
, where
C=\lim_{z\to r^{-1}}(1-rz)^{m_{r}-i+1}f(z)
. Since the numerator of
f
is divisible by
(1-rz)^{i-1}
, it follows that
C=C_{1}/C_{2}
, where
C_{2}=\lim_{z\to r^{-1}}p(z)/(1-rz)^{m_{r}}
and
C_{1}
equals

(32)

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to r^{-1}}\frac{1}{(1-rz)^{i-1}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(c_{k}-\sum_{R\in S}p^{\mathbf{u}}_{R}(k)R^{k}-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}\right)\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}z^{k+\ell}+p(z)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}z^{k}\right).

As
i-1<m_{r}
,

(33)

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to r^{-1}}\frac{p(z)}{(1-rz)^{i-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}z^{k}\ =\ 0;

hence, (
32
) reduces to

(34)

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to r^{-1}}\frac{1}{(1-rz)^{i-1}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(c_{k}-\sum_{R\in S}p^{\mathbf{u}}_{R}(k)R^{k}-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}\right)\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}a_{\ell}z^{k+\ell}\right).

Thus,

\displaystyle C_{1}\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{r^{i-1}(i-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(c_{k}-\sum_{R\in S}p^{\mathbf{u}}_{R}(k)R^{k}-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}\right)\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}(i-1)!{k+\ell\choose i-1}a_{\ell}r^{-k-\ell+i-1}

\displaystyle=\ (-1)^{i-1}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(c_{k}-\sum_{R\in S}p^{\mathbf{u}}_{R}(k)R^{k}-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}\right)\sum_{\ell\geq j-k}{k+\ell\choose i-1}a_{\ell}r^{-k-\ell}

\displaystyle=\ (-1)^{i-1}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(c_{k}-\sum_{R\in S}p^{\mathbf{u}}_{R}(k)R^{k}-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}\right)s_{r,k,i-1}

(35)

\displaystyle\overset{*}{=}\ (-1)^{i-1}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(c_{k}-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}\right)s_{r,k,i-1},

where the starred equality follows from Lemma
2.5
. Computing
C_{2}
, we obtain

(36)

\displaystyle C_{2}\ =\ \frac{-\sum_{\ell\geq m_{r}}m_{r}!{\ell\choose m_{r}}a_{\ell}r^{-\ell+m_{r}}}{(-1)^{m_{r}}r^{m_{r}}m_{r}!}\ =\ (-1)^{m_{r}-1}\sum_{\ell\geq m_{r}}{\ell\choose m_{r}}a_{\ell}r^{-\ell}=N_{r}^{-1}.

Therefore, we have the recurrence

(37)

\displaystyle\kappa_{r,i}\ =\ \frac{(-1)^{i-1}N_{r}}{(m_{r}-i)!}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(c_{k}-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}\right)s_{r,k,i-1}.

It now only remains to show that recurrence (
37
) admits the explicit formula

(38)

\displaystyle\kappa_{r,i}\ =\ \frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{(m_{r}-i)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varnothing\neq\{d_{1},\dots,d_{p}\}\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,i-1\}\\
d_{1}<\cdots<d_{p}=i-1\end{subarray}}{N}_{r}^{p}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{j-1}c_{k_{1}}s_{r,k_{1},d_{1}}\prod_{u=1}^{p-1}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{j-1}\frac{k_{2}^{m_{r}-1-d_{u}}r^{k_{2}}s_{r,k_{2},d_{u+1}}}{(-1)^{d_{u}-1}(m_{r}-1-d_{u})!}.

Let
\{\kappa_{r,i}^{\prime}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
be the sequence defined by the explicit formula given in (
38
). For abbreviation, let

(39)

\displaystyle\Pi(\{d_{1},\dots,d_{p}\})\ =\ \sum_{k_{1}=0}^{j-1}c_{k_{1}}s_{r,k_{1},d_{1}}\prod_{u=1}^{p-1}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{j-1}\frac{k_{2}^{m_{r}-1-d_{u}}r^{k_{2}}s_{r,k_{2},d_{u+1}}}{(-1)^{d_{u}-1}(m_{r}-1-d_{u})!}.

Towards proving that
\kappa_{r,i}\equiv\kappa_{r,i}^{\prime}
, it suffices to show that
\{\kappa_{r,i}^{\prime}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
satisfies the recurrence since
\{\kappa_{r,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
is completely determined by (
37
). Note

\displaystyle\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}^{\prime}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}{k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}s_{r,k,i-1}}\ =\

\displaystyle\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varnothing\neq\{d_{1},\dots,d_{p}\}\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,v-1\}\\
d_{1}<\cdots<d_{p}=v-1\end{subarray}}{N}_{r}^{p}~\Pi(\{d_{1},\dots,d_{p}\})\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\frac{k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}s_{r,k,i-1}}{(-1)^{v-1}(m_{r}-v)!}

(40)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varnothing\neq\{d_{1},\dots,d_{p+1}\}\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,i-1\}\\
d_{1}<\cdots<d_{p}=v-1,~d_{p+1}=i-1\end{subarray}}{N}_{r}^{p}~\Pi(\{d_{1},\dots,d_{p+1}\}).

Now with the aid of (
40
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i-1}N_{r}}{(m_{r}-i)!}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(c_{k}-\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa^{\prime}_{r,v}k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}\right)s_{r,k,i-1}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{(m_{r}-i)!}\Bigg(N_{r}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}s_{r,k,i-1}-N_{r}\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\kappa_{r,v}^{\prime}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}k^{m_{r}-v}r^{k}s_{r,k,i-1}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{(m_{r}-i)!}\left(N_{r}\Pi(\{i-1\})+\sum_{v=1}^{i-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varnothing\neq\{d_{1},\dots,d_{p+1}\}\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,i-1\}\\
d_{1}<\cdots<d_{p}=v-1,~d_{p+1}=i-1\end{subarray}}{N}_{r}^{p+1}~\Pi(\{d_{1},\dots,d_{p+1}\})\right)

(41)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{(m_{r}-i)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varnothing\neq\{d_{1},\dots,d_{p+1}\}\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,i-1\}\\
d_{1}<\cdots<d_{p+1}=i-1\end{subarray}}{N}_{r}^{p+1}~\Pi(\{d_{1},\dots,d_{p+1}\})\ =\ \kappa^{\prime}_{r,i}.

Thus,
\kappa_{r,i}\equiv\kappa_{r,i}^{\prime}
.
∎

3.
Directions for Future Work

A natural continuation of our work is to generalize Theorem
1.1
to a broader class of recurrences. A particularly natural generalization is to consider linear recurrences of the form
c_{n}=a_{1}c_{n-1}+\cdots+a_{j}a_{n-j}+d(n)
, where
d(n)
is some polynomial. These recurrences are classified as non-homogeneous linear recurrences: a linear recurrence
c_{n}-a_{1}c_{n-1}-\cdots-a_{j}a_{n-j}=f(n)
is
homogeneous
if
f(n)\equiv 0
, and
non-homogeneous
otherwise. This proposed generalization only encapsulates the case when
f
is a polynomial; however, this induces the generating function of
\{c_{n}\}
to remain rational, thereby permitting the techniques presented in this work to be readily generalized.

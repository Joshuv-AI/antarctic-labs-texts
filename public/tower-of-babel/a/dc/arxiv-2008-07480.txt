Pandemic-type Failures in Multivariate Brownian Risk Models
Krzysztof Dȩbicki, Enkelejd Hashorva, Nikolai Kriukov

Abstract




Abstract:
Modelling of multiple simultaneous failures in insurance, finance and other areas of applied probability
is important especially from the point of view of pandemic-type events.
A benchmark limiting model for the analysis of multiple failures is the classical
d
-dimensional Brownian risk model (Brm), see
[
1
]
.
From both theoretical and practical point of view, of interest is the calculation of
the probability of multiple simultaneous failures in a given time horizon.
The main findings of this contribution concern the approximation of the probability that at least
k
out of
d
components of
Brm fail simultaneously. We derive both sharp bounds and asymptotic approximations of the probability of interest
for the finite and the infinite time horizon. Our results extend previous findings of
[
2
,
3
]
.

Key Words:
Multivariate Brownian risk model; probability of multiple simultaneous failures; simultaneous ruin probability;
failure time; exact asymptotics; pandemic-type events.

AMS Classification:
Primary 60G15; secondary 60G70

1.
Introduction

In this paper we are interested in the probabilistic aspects of multiple simultaneous failures typically occurring due to pandemic-type events.
A key benchmark risk model considered here is the
d
-dimensional Brownian risk model (Brm)

\boldsymbol{R}(t,\boldsymbol{u}){{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=(R_{1}(t,u_{1}),\ldots,R_{d}(t,u_{d}))^{\top}}}=\boldsymbol{u}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+\boldsymbol{c}t-\boldsymbol{W}(t)}},\quad t\geq 0,

where

\boldsymbol{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})^{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\top}}},\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})^{\top}
are vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

and

\boldsymbol{W}(t)=\Gamma\boldsymbol{B}(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

with
\Gamma
a
d\times d
real-valued non-singular matrix and
\boldsymbol{B}(t)=(B_{1}(t),\ldots,B_{d}(t))^{\top},t\in\mathbb{R}
a
d
-dimensional
Brownian motion with independent components which are standard Brownian motions.

By bold symbols we denote column vectors, operations with vectors are meant component-wise and
a\boldsymbol{x}=(ax_{1},\ldots,ax_{d})^{\top}
for any scalar
a\in\mathbb{R}
and any
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Indeed, Brm is a natural limiting model in many statistical applications. Moreover, as shown in
[
1
]
such a risk
model appears naturally in insurance applications. Being a limiting model,
Brm can be used as a benchmark for various untractable models.
Given the fundamental role of Brownian motion in applied probability and statistics, it is also of theoretical interest to study failure events arising from this model. Specifically,
in this contribution we are interested in the behaviour of the probability of multiple simultaneous
failures occurring in a given time horizon
[S,T]\subset[0,\infty]
.

In our settings failures can be defined in various ways.
Let us consider first the failure of a given component of our risk model. Namely, we say that the
i
th component of our Brm has a failure (or ruin occurs)
if
R_{i}(t,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}u_{i}}}){{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=u_{i}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+c_{i}t-W_{i}(t)}}}}<0
for some
t\in[S,T]
.
The extreme case of a catastrophic event is when
d
multiple simultaneous failures occurs.
Typically, for pandemic-type events there are at least
k
components of the model with simultaneous failures and
k
is large with the extreme case
k=d
.
In mathematical notation, for given positive integer
k\leq d
of interest is the calculation of the following probability

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(S,T,\boldsymbol{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists t\in[S,T],\,\exists\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\},|\mathcal{I}|=k:\cap_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\{R_{i}(t,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}u_{i}}})<0\}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists t\in[S,T],\,\exists\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\},|\mathcal{I}|=k:{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\cap_{i\in\mathcal{I}}}}\,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\{}}W_{i}(t)-c_{i}t>u_{i}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\}}}\right\},

where
|\mathcal{I}|
denotes the cardinality of the set
\mathcal{I}
. If
T
is finite,
by the self-similarity
property of the Brownian motion
\psi_{k}(S,T,\boldsymbol{u})
can be derived from the case
T=1
,
whereas
T=\infty
has to be treated separately.

There are no results in the literature investigating
\psi_{k}(S,T,\boldsymbol{u})
for general
k
. The particular case
k=d
, for which
\psi_{d}(S,T,\boldsymbol{u})
coincides with
the simultaneous ruin probability has been studies in different contexts, see e.g.,
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
3
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
. The case
d=2
of Brm has been recently investigated in
[
2
]
.

Although the probability of multiple simultaneous failures seems very difficult to compute,
our first result below, motivated by
[
15
]
[Thm 1.1], shows that
\psi_{k}(S,T,\boldsymbol{u})
can be bounded by the multivariate Gaussian survival probability, namely by

p_{T}(\boldsymbol{u})=\mathbb{P}\left\{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(W_{1}(T)-c_{1}T,\ldots,W_{d}(T)-c_{d}T)}}\in\boldsymbol{E}(\boldsymbol{u})\right\},

where

(1)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{E}(\boldsymbol{u}){{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|\mathcal{I}|=k\end{subarray}}\boldsymbol{E_{\mathcal{I}}(\boldsymbol{u})}}}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|\mathcal{I}|=k\end{subarray}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\forall i\in\mathcal{I}:~\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\geq\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\}}}.

When
u\to\infty
we can approximate
p_{T}(\boldsymbol{u})
utilising Laplace asymptotic method, see e.g.,
[
16
]
,
whereas for small and moderate values of
u
it can be calculated or simulated with sufficient accuracy.
Our next result gives bounds for

\psi_{k}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}S}},T,\boldsymbol{u})
in terms of
p_{T}(\boldsymbol{u})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If the matrix
\Gamma
is non-singular, then for any positive integer
k\leq d
, all constants
0\leq S<T<\infty
and all
\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(2)

\displaystyle p_{T}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq\psi_{k}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}S}},T,\boldsymbol{u})\leq Kp_{T}(\boldsymbol{u}),

where
K=1/\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\},|\mathcal{I}|=k\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\forall_{i\in\mathcal{I}}:W_{i}(T)>\max(0,c_{i}T)\right\}>0.

The bounds in (
2
) indicate that it might be possible to derive an approximations of
\psi_{k}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}S}},T,\boldsymbol{u})
for large threshold
\boldsymbol{u}
, which has been already shown for
k=d=2
in
[
2
]
. In this paper we consider the general case
k\leq d,d>2
discussing both the finite time interval (i.e.,
T=1
)
and the infinite time horizon case with
T=\infty
extending the results of
[
3
]
where
d=k
is considered.

In Section 2 we explain the main ideas that lead to the approximation of
\psi_{k}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}S}},T,\boldsymbol{u})
.
Section 3 discusses some interesting special cases, whereas the proofs are postponed to Section 4. Some technical calculations are displayed in Section
5
.

2.
Main Results

In this section
\boldsymbol{W}(t),t\geq 0

where we fix

Let hereafter

\mathcal{I}
. Set next

\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(S,T,\boldsymbol{a}_{\mathcal{I}}u)=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists{t\in[S,T]}:A_{\mathcal{I}}(t)\right\},

(3)

\displaystyle A_{\mathcal{I}}(t)=\{\boldsymbol{W}(t)-\boldsymbol{c}t\in\boldsymbol{E}_{\mathcal{I}}(\boldsymbol{a}u)\}=\{\forall i\in\mathcal{I}:\ ~W_{i}(t)-c_{i}t\geq a_{i}u\},

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(S,T,\boldsymbol{a}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists t\in[S,T]:~\bigcup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|\mathcal{I}|=k\end{subarray}}A_{\mathcal{I}}(t)\right\}=\mathbb{P}\left\{\bigcup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\

The following lower bound (by Bonferroni inequality)

(4)

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(S,T,\boldsymbol{a}u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|\mathcal{I}|=k\end{subarray}}\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(S,T,\boldsymbol{a}_{\mathcal{I}}u)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{J}|=k\\
\mathcal{I}\not=\mathcal{J}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists t,s\in[S,T]:~A_{\mathcal{I}}(t)\cap A_{\mathcal{J}}(s)\right\}

together with the upper bound

(5)

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(S,T,\boldsymbol{a}u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|\mathcal{I}|=k\end{subarray}}\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(S,T,\boldsymbol{a}_{\mathcal{I}}u)

are crucial

\psi_{k}(S,T,\boldsymbol{a}u)
as
u\to\infty
.
As we shall show below, the upper bound (

u\to\infty
.
The following theorem constitutes the main finding of this contribution.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that the square
d\times d
real-valued matrix
\Gamma
is non-singular.
If
\boldsymbol{a}
has no more than
k-1
non-positive components,
where
k\leq d
is a positive integer,
then
for all
0\leq S<T<\infty,\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(6)

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(S,T,\boldsymbol{a}u)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|\mathcal{I}|=k\end{subarray}}\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(0,T,\boldsymbol{a}_{\mathcal{I}}u),\quad u\to\infty.

Moreover, (
6
) holds also if
T=\infty
, provided that

\boldsymbol{c}
and
\boldsymbol{a}+\boldsymbol{c}t

have no more than
k-1
non-positive components for all
t\geq 0
.

Essentially, the above result is the claim that
the second term in the Bonferroni
lower bound (
4
) is asymptotically negligible. In order to prove that, the asymptotics of

\psi_{\left\lvert\mathcal{I}\right\rvert}(S,T,\boldsymbol{a}_{\mathcal{I}}u)
has to be derived. For the special case that
\mathcal{I}
has only two elements and
S=0
,
its approximation has been obtained in
[
2
]
.
The assumption in Theorem
2.1
that
\boldsymbol{a}
has no more than
k-1
non-positive components excludes the case that there exists a set
\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\},\ |\mathcal{I}|=k

such that
\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(0,T,\boldsymbol{a}_{\mathcal{I}}u)
does not tend to
0
as
u\to\infty
,
which due to its non-rare event nature is out of interest in this contribution.

The next result extends the findings of
[
2
]
to the case

. For notational simplicity we consider the case
\mathcal{I}
has

Recall that in our model

Hereafter
\boldsymbol{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\text{minimise }\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{x},\text{ for all }\boldsymbol{x}\geq\boldsymbol{a}.

Its unique solution

is such that

(7)

\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}_{I}=\boldsymbol{a}_{I},\ \ (\Sigma_{II})^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_{I}>\boldsymbol{0}_{I},\ \ \tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}_{J}=\Sigma_{JI}(\Sigma_{II})^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_{I}\geq\boldsymbol{a}_{J},

where

The index set

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Sigma

\boldsymbol{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus(-\infty,0]^{d}

\Pi_{\Sigma}(\boldsymbol{a})
has a unique solution
\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}

(8)

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{x}\geq\boldsymbol{a}}\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{a}_{I}^{\top}(\Sigma_{II})^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_{I}>0,

(9)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}=\boldsymbol{x}_{F}^{\top}(\Sigma_{FF})^{-1}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}_{F}}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

. Further if

In the following we set

In view of the above lemma

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{I}=(\Sigma_{II})^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_{I}>\boldsymbol{0}_{I},\ \ \boldsymbol{\lambda}_{J}\geq\boldsymbol{0}_{J},

with the convention that when
J

3.
Examples

In order to illustrate our findings we shall consider three examples assuming that
\Gamma\Gamma^{\top}
is a positive definite correlation matrix. The first example is dedicated to the simplest case
k=1
. In the second one we discuss
k=2
restricting
\boldsymbol{a}
to have all components equal to 1 followed then by the last example where only the assumption
\Gamma\Gamma^{\top}
is an equi-correlated correlation matrix is imposed.
In order to avoid additional notation we shall consider for simplicity on
T=1
.
Below
S\in[0,1)
is fixed.

Example 1
(
k=1
):
Suppose that
\boldsymbol{a}
has all components positive. In view of Theorem
2.1
we have that

\psi_{k}(S,1,\boldsymbol{a}u)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{d}\psi_{\{i\}}(0,1,a_{i}u)

as
u\to\infty
. Note that for any positive integer
i\leq d

\psi_{\{i\}}(0,1,a_{i}u)=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\in[0,1]}:B(t)-c_{i}t>a_{i}u\right\},

where
B
is a standard Brownian motion. It follows easily that

\psi_{k}(S,1,\boldsymbol{a}u)\sim 2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{P}\left\{B(1)>a_{i}u+c_{i}\right\},\quad u\to\infty.

Example 2
(
k=2
and
\boldsymbol{a}=\boldsymbol{1}
): Suppose next
k=2
and
\boldsymbol{a}
has all components equal 1.
In view of Theorems
2.1
and
2.3
we have that

\psi_{k}(S,1,\boldsymbol{1}u)\sim\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}}C_{i,j}(\boldsymbol{1})\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{k\in\{i,j\}}(W_{k}(1)-c_{k})>u)\right\}

as
u\to\infty
, where
\boldsymbol{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
has all components equal to 1. By Remark
2.4
we obtain further

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{k\in\{i,j\}}(W_{k}(1)-c_{k})>u)\right\}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{u^{-2}}{(1-\rho_{i,j})^{2}\sqrt{2\pi(1-\rho_{i,j}^{2})}}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{1+\rho_{i,j}}-{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{(c_{i}+c_{j})u}{1+\rho_{i,j}}}}-\frac{c_{i}^{2}-2\rho_{i,j}c_{i}c_{j}+c_{j}^{2}}{2(1-\rho^{2}_{i,j})}},\quad u\to\infty.

Here we set
\rho_{i,j}=corr(W_{i}(1),W_{j}(1))
. Consequently, if
\rho_{i,j}>\rho_{i^{*},j^{*}}
, then as
u\to\infty

\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{k\in\{i^{*},j^{*}\}}(W_{k}(1)-c_{k}>u)\right\}=o\left(\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{k\in\{i,j\}}(W_{k}(1)-c_{k}>u)\right\}\right).

The same holds also if
\rho_{i,j}=\rho_{i^{*},j^{*}}
and
c_{i}+c_{j}>c_{i^{*}}+c_{j^{*}}
. If we denote by
\tau
the maximum of all
\rho_{i,j}
’s and by
c_{*}
the maximum of
c_{i}+c_{j}
for all
i,j
’s such that
\rho_{i,j}=\tau
, then we conclude that

\psi_{k}(S,1,\boldsymbol{a}u)\sim\newline
\sum_{i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\},\rho_{i,j}=\tau,~c_{i}+c_{j}=c_{*}}C_{i,j}(\boldsymbol{1})\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{k\in\{i,j\}}(W_{k}(1)-c_{k}>u)\right\}.

Note that in this case
C_{i,j}(\boldsymbol{1})
does not depend on
i
and
j
and is equals to

(1-\tau)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\geq 0}:B_{1}(t)-t>x,B_{2}(t)-t>y\right\}e^{(1-\rho^{2})(x+y)}dxdy,

where
(B_{1}(t),B_{2}(t)),t\geq 0
is a 2-dimensional Gaussian process with
B_{i}
’s being standard Brownian motions with constant correlation
\tau
. Consequently, as
u\to\infty

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(S,1,\boldsymbol{1}u)\sim C_{*}u^{-2}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{1+\tau}-\frac{c_{*}u}{2(1{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+}}\tau)}},

where

\displaystyle C_{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{c_{*}^{2}}{2(1-{\tau}^{2})}}}{\sqrt{2\pi(1-{\tau}^{2})}}\sum_{i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\},\rho_{i,j}=\tau,~c_{i}+c_{j}=c_{*}}e^{\frac{c_{i}c_{j}}{1-\tau}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\geq 0}:B_{1}(t)-t>x,B_{2}(t)-t>y\right\}e^{(1-{\tau}^{2})(x+y)}dxdy\in(0,\infty).

Example 3
(Equi-correlated risk model): We consider the matrix
\Gamma
such that
\Sigma=\Gamma\Gamma^{\top}
is an equi-correlated non-singular correlation matrix with off-diagonal entries equal to
\rho\in(-1/(d-1),1)
. Let
\boldsymbol{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with at least one positive component and assume for simplicity that its components are ordered, i.e.,
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{d}
and thus
a_{1}>0
.
The inverse of
\Sigma
equals

\left[I_{d}-\boldsymbol{1}\boldsymbol{1}^{\top}\frac{\rho}{1+\rho(d-1)}\right]\frac{1}{1-\rho},

where
I_{d}
is the identity matrix. First we determine the index set
I
corresponding to the unique solution of
\Pi_{\Sigma}(\boldsymbol{a})
. We have for this case that
I
with
m
elements is unique and in view of (
7
)

(15)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{I}=(\Sigma_{II})^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_{I}=\frac{1}{1-\rho}\left[\boldsymbol{a}_{I}-\rho\frac{\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}}{1+\rho(m-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{I}\right]>\boldsymbol{0}_{I},

with
\boldsymbol{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the origin. From the above

if and only if

which holds in the particular case that all

When the above does not hold, the second condition on the index set

\displaystyle\int_{{(A_{Z})}_{J}}e^{<\boldsymbol{c}_{J}^{*},\boldsymbol{x}_{J}>-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}||\boldsymbol{x}_{J}||^{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}_{J}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|J|}}e^{<\boldsymbol{c}_{J}^{*},\boldsymbol{x}_{J}>-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}||\boldsymbol{x}_{J}||^{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}_{J}<\infty,

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{S}_{Z}\setminus A_{Z})_{J}}e^{<\boldsymbol{c}_{J}^{*},\boldsymbol{x}_{J}>-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}||\boldsymbol{x}_{J}||^{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}_{J}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|J|}}e^{<\boldsymbol{c}_{J}^{*},\boldsymbol{x}_{J}>-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}||\boldsymbol{x}_{J}||^{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}_{J}<\infty,

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{S}_{Z}\setminus A_{Z})_{{K}}}e^{-\sum_{i\in{K}}(C^{*}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{2}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\boldsymbol{x}_{i})}d\boldsymbol{x}_{{K}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|{K}|}}e^{-\sum_{i\in{K}}(C^{*}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{2}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\boldsymbol{x}_{i})}d\boldsymbol{x}_{{K}}<\infty

and for the other two integrals (recall that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}_{I}
has positive components)

\displaystyle\int_{{(A_{Z})}_{I\setminus Z}}e^{\sum_{i\in I\setminus Z}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}}d\boldsymbol{x}_{{L}}\leq\int_{(-\infty,0)^{|{L}|}}e^{\sum_{i\in{L}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}}d\boldsymbol{x}_{{L}}<\infty,

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{S}_{Z}\setminus A_{Z})_{{L}}}e^{\sum_{i\in{L}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}}d\boldsymbol{x}_{{L}}\leq\int_{(-\infty,0)^{|{L}|}}e^{\sum_{i\in{L}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}}d\boldsymbol{x}_{L}<\infty,

where the last term is finite because the area
(A_{Z})_{{K}}
is finite.

Consequently, the dominated convergence theorem yields

\displaystyle M(u,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Lambda}})\sim F(u)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h([0,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Lambda}}],\boldsymbol{x})\theta(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x},\qquad u\to\infty.

Suppose for simplicity that the number of elements of

is

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\boldsymbol{c}}^{\top}(\Sigma^{-1})_{JJ}\tilde{\boldsymbol{c}}},\ \ \nu_{J}(\boldsymbol{x}_{J})=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{J}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{c}})^{\top}(\Sigma^{-1})_{JJ}(\boldsymbol{x}_{J}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{c}})},

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-m}}\mathbb{I}_{\{\boldsymbol{x}_{U}<\boldsymbol{0}_{U}\}}\nu_{J}(\boldsymbol{x}_{J})d\boldsymbol{x}_{J}\in(0,\infty),

\displaystyle E([0,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Lambda}}])

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\in[0,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Lambda}}]}:\boldsymbol{W}_{I}(t)-t\boldsymbol{a}_{I}>\boldsymbol{x}_{I}\right\}e^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{I}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}_{I}}d\boldsymbol{x}_{I}.

Then we can rewrite (

Since

For any

\displaystyle E([0,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Lambda}}])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\in[0,\infty]}:\boldsymbol{W}_{I}(t)-t\boldsymbol{a}_{I}>\boldsymbol{x}_{I}\right\}e^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{I}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}_{I}}d\boldsymbol{x}_{I}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\{e^{\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{I},\boldsymbol{M}_{i}\rangle}\right\},

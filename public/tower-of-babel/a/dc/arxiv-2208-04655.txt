Integral models of moduli spaces of shtukas with deep Bruhat-Tits level structures Thanks: This work was supported by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) via the CRC/TRR 326 Geometry and Arithmetic of Uniformized Structures , project number 444845124, and the CRC/TRR 358 Integral Structures in Geometry and Representation Theory , project number 491392403.
Patrick Bieker

Abstract
. We construct integral models for moduli spaces of shtukas with deeper Bruhat-Tits level structures. We embed the moduli space of global shtukas for a deeper Bruhat-Tits group scheme into the limit of the moduli spaces of shtukas for all associated parahoric group schemes. Its schematic image defines an integral model of the moduli space of shtukas with deeper Bruhat-Tits level with favourable properties: They admit proper, surjective and generically étale level maps as well as a natural Newton stratification. In the Drinfeld case, this general construction of integral models recovers the moduli space of Drinfeld shtukas with Drinfeld level structures.



1.
Introduction

Following Langlands’ philosophy, the Hasse-Weil
\zeta
-function of a Shimura variety should be computable in terms of automorphic
L
-functions.
The Langlands-Kottwitz method for computing the Hasse-Weil
\zeta
-function (or more generally to study the cohomology of Shimura varieties) relies on two geometric inputs.
On the one hand one needs a description of the mod-
p
points of the Shimura variety as conjectured by Langlands and Rapoport
[
LR87
]
and on the other hand one needs to identify a certain test function that serves as an input to the Arthur-Selberg trace formula.
For both steps the existence of good integral models of the Shimura variety is necessary.
For Shimura varieties with parahoric level structure there has been substantial progress towards both steps. The Langlands-Rapoport conjecture is proved under mild hypotheses in
[
Hof22
]
(and
[
AH16
]
in the function field setting), while the test function conjecture is shown in the series of papers
[
HR21
,
HR20
,
Ans+22
]
.
The goal of this work is to provide a first step towards generalising the results in the function field setting to more general (Bruhat-Tits-)level structures. Namely, we construct analogues of the integral models of Shimura varieties of
[
KP18
]
for these deeper level structures in the function field setting.

The analogues of Shimura varieties over function fields are moduli spaces of (global) shtukas.
They were first introduced in
[
Dri87a
]
for
\GL_{n}
and later generalised to arbitrary split reductive groups in
[
Var04
]
and further to flat affine group schemes of finite type in
[
AH21
]
. They are used to great success in establishing a Langlands correspondence over function fields in
[
Dri87
]
for
\GL_{2}
,
[
Laf02
]
for
\GL_{n}
and
[
Laf18
]
for arbitrary reductive groups.
Recently, a lot of progress has been made in studying the geometry of moduli spaces of shtukas with parahoric level, compare for example
[
AH14
]
,
[
AH19
]
,
[
Bre18
]
,
[
YZ19
]
and
[
Zhu14
]
. However, little is recorded for deeper level structures. A first result beyond the parahoric case is obtained in
[
Bie23
]
, where Drinfeld
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
-level structures for Drinfeld shtukas are defined (in the style of
[
Dri76
,
KM85
]
) and it is shown that their moduli spaces admit well-behaved (that is finite flat and generically étale) level maps as in the case of modular curves in
[
KM85
]
.
The goal of this work is to construct integral models of moduli spaces of shtukas with deep Bruhat-Tits level structures for general reductive groups that generalise both the parahoric case and in the Drinfeld case the moduli space of shtukas with Drinfeld
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
-level structures of
[
Bie23
]
, and to study properties of these integral models.

X
be a smooth, projective and geometrically connected curve over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.
Let

[

Let us fix a closed point

of

Let

, where

is the completion of

By Bruhat-Tits theory, we get a smooth affine

with connected fibres that we glue with

to obtain a (global) Bruhat-Tits group scheme

Without changing

Let

\underline{\mu}=(\mu_{i})_{i\in I}

of

A global

is a
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
-bundle

on

together with an isomorphism

We denote by

the moduli space of global
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
-shtukas whose zeroes and poles are bounded by

While for a subset
\Omega^{\prime}
of

there is still a natural map
\Sht^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}\to\Sht^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega^{\prime}},X}
by

, the level map
\Sht_{\GL_{2,[0,n]},X}^{(1,2),\leq((0,-1),(1,0))}\to\Sht_{\GL_{2},X}^{(1,2),\leq((0,-1),(1,0))}

In order to remedy this issue, we propose the following construction to relatively compactify
\Sht^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}
.

Definition 1.0.1

(compare Definition
5.2.4
)
.

In the situation above, that is, for a reductive group
G
over
F
, and a Bruhat-Tits group scheme
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}\to X
for a subset
\Omega
(assumed to be convex, closed and a union of facets) of the Bruhat-Tits building for

at the fixed point

of

\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
-level structure

\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}
is defined to be the schematic image in the sense of
[

]
of the map

\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}},

where the limit is taken over the set of all facets
\mathfrak{f}
contained in
\Omega

Clearly, in the parahoric case (that is, when
\Omega
is a facet) we have

\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X^{I}}^{\leq\underline{\mu}}=\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}},X^{I}}^{\leq\underline{\mu}}=\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X^{I}}^{\leq\underline{\mu}},

as the index set has
\Omega
as its final object. So our construction generalises the parahoric case.
Moreover, by
[
Bie23
, Theorem 6.7]
, our notion of integral models recovers in the Drinfeld case the moduli space of Drinfeld shtukas with Drinfeld
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
-level structure at
x_{0}
.

The main result of this work is to show that our integral models have good formal properties. Namely, that they admit proper, surjective and generically finite étale level maps (similar to the integral models of Shimura varieties with parahoric level structures):

Theorem 1.0.2

(compare Theorem
5.2.3
and Theorem
5.2.6
)
.

In the situation of Definition
1.0.1
, the map

\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}

is schematic and a quasi-compact locally closed immersion. It factors into an open immersion
\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}\to\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}
followed by the closed immersion
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}
.
The restriction of the inclusion

\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}|_{(X\setminus\{x_{0}\})^{I}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}|_{(X\setminus\{x_{0}\})^{I}}

away from
x_{0}
is an open and closed immersion.
Moreover, for a subset
\Omega^{\prime}\prec\Omega
, there is a natural level map

\bar{\rho}_{\Omega^{\prime},\Omega}\colon\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}\to\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega^{\prime}},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}

that is schematic, proper and surjective, and over
(X\setminus\{x_{0}\})^{I}
it is finite étale.

Remark 1.0.3
.

In principle, we expect our methods to apply also to even more general level structures, namely to stabilisers of bounded subsets
\Omega
of the Bruhat-Tits building that are not necessarily contained in a single apartment.
This includes in particular the case that
\Omega
is a ball of radius
r
around a point
x
in the Bruhat-Tits building, whose (connected) stabiliser should be closely connected (up to the centre) to the Moy-Prasad subgroup
G(F_{x_{0}})_{x,r}
.

However, the results from Bruhat-Tits theory we rely on in the proof of Theorem
1.0.2
do not seem to be available in this case.
Compare Section
5.3
for further discussion of this situation and the statements we can prove in the more general setting.

The construction of integral models is a first step towards generalising results used in the Langlands-Kottwitz method at parahoric level to the more general level structures considered here.
In particular, a fine analysis of the (étale-local) structure of their special fibres together with the calculation of the trace of Frobenius on the nearby cycles might yield significant progress towards a geometric construction of test functions for deeper Bruhat-Tits level structures generalising the results in the parahoric case of
[
HR21
,
HR20
,
Ans+22
]
(and
[
HR12
]
in the Drinfeld case with
\Gamma_{1}(p)
-level structures).
Note that the test functions for
\Gamma(p^{n})
-level (which are not Bruhat-Tits level structures in our sense) constructed in
[
Sch13
]
have no apparent connection to geometry.
A geometric construction of test functions in our setting might shed some more light onto this situation as well.

Let us briefly comment on the proof of Theorem
1.0.2
.
In the parahoric case, level maps on moduli spaces of shtukas are also studied in
[
Bre18
, Theorem 3.20]
.
However, the assumption on the bounds used there does not apply to the bounds given by cocharacters.
We provide the relevant background on bound for global (and local) shtukas and generalise the result of
[
Bre18
, Theorem 3.20]
to include bounds in this sense (compare Theorem
3.5.5
).
Using the assertion in the parahoric case, we are then able to deduce the result also for level maps at deeper level.

The first part of Theorem
1.0.2
is based on a corresponding result on the moduli space of
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
-bundles.

Theorem 1.0.4

(compare Theorem
4.3.3
)
.

In the situation of Definition
1.0.1
, the natural map

\Bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}

is schematic and representable by a quasi-compact open immersion.

As a first step in the proof of this theorem, we show in the local case (and hence also for the corresponding global Bruhat-Tits group schemes),
that the not necessarily parahoric Bruhat-Tits group scheme
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
is the limit of all its associated parahoric group schemes
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}\xrightarrow{\cong}\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}},

compare Theorem
4.2.1
.

Note that given a compatible system of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}
-torsors for all facets
\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega
, it is in general not true that their limit is a torsor for
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
, as it might be impossible to construct a compatible system of sections.
By controlling the deformation theory of torsors for the
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}
, we are able to show that the locus where the limit of a compatible system of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}
-bundles on
X
is already a
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
-bundle on
X
is open.

As a first step in studying the structure of the special fibre of
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}
, we show that the Newton stratification on the special fibre of the moduli space of shtukas in the parahoric case induces a well-defined Newton stratification on the special fibre in the case of deeper level.
For a reductive group
H
over a local field
E
we denote by
B(H)
the set of
\sigma
-conjugacy classes in
H(\breve{E})
, where
\breve{E}
is the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
k
.
Then
B(H)
classifies quasi-isogeny classes of local shtukas for (an integral model of)
H
.

We fix a tuple of pairwise distinct closed points
\underline{y}=(y_{i})_{i\in I}
in
X
and denote by
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X,\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}}}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}=\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}\times_{X}\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}}
the special fibre over
\underline{y}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}}
is the compositum of the residue fields of the points

of

.

Combining our compactification with the results of
[
HV11
, Theorem 7.11]
and
[
Bre18
, Section 5]
in the parahoric case, we get the following result on the Newton stratification for deep level.

Theorem 1.0.5

(compare Definition
5.4.3
and Corollary
5.4.4
)
.

Let
\ell
be an algebraically closed extension of
\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}}
.
There is a well-defined map

\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\colon\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X,\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}}}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}(\ell)\to\prod_{i\in I}B(G_{F_{y_{i}}})

that is compatible with the level maps in the sense that for
\Omega^{\prime}\prec\Omega
we have

\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}=\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega^{\prime}}}\circ\bar{\rho}_{\Omega^{\prime},\Omega}.

Moreover, for
\underline{b}=(b_{i})_{i\in I}\in B(G_{F_{y_{i}}})
the preimage of
\underline{b}
under
\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}
is the set of
\ell
-valued points of a locally closed substack

\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X,\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}}}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu},\underline{b}}
of
\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X,\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}}}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}
called the
Newton stratum
of
\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X,\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}}}^{I,\leq\underline{\mu}}
for
\underline{b}
.

In the parahoric case, the map
\bar{\delta}
is given by associating to a point in the special fibre over
\underline{y}
the quasi-isogeny classes of its local shtukas at the points
y_{i}
.
We use the compatibility of the Newton stratification with the level maps in the parahoric case to extend this result to the case of deep level.

Moreover, we show that in the hyperspecial case the Newton stratification satisfies the strong stratification property (as for Shimura varieties).
Recall that there is a natural order on
B(H)
induced by the dominance order on cocharacters.
It is well-known in the parahoric case that the closure of a Newton stratum

\overline{\Sht^{I,\leq\underline{\mu},\underline{b}}_{\mathcal{G},X,\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}}}}\subseteq\bigcup_{\underline{b^{\prime}}\leq\underline{b}}\Sht^{I,\leq\underline{\mu},\underline{b^{\prime}}}_{\mathcal{G},X,\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}}}

is contained in a union of Newton strata. Note that this also generalises to deeper level.
We say that the Newton stratification satisfies the strong stratification property when we even have equality.
However, the inclusion is strict in general.
For local shtukas for split reductive groups, the strong stratification property is due to
[
Vie13
]
. We use the Serre-Tate theorem for shtukas of
[
AH14
]
to deduce the corresponding result in the global case, compare Theorem
5.4.5
.

Organisation

This paper is organised as follows. Sections
2
and
3
provide background on the general theory of moduli spaces of shtukas.
Our construction of integral models for deeper level relies on functoriality results for moduli spaces of shtukas.
As the functoriality result of
[
Bre18
, Theorem 3.20]
is not applicable in our setting,
we first carefully set up (and slightly generalise) the theory of bounds for (iterated, global and local shtukas) in Section
2
. We show basic properties including local-global compatibility.
In Section
3
, we generalise various well-known results for moduli spaces of (bounded iterated) shtukas to our setting.
In particular, we show the relevant functoriality of moduli spaces of shtukas in Theorem
3.5.5
which also proves the analogue of Theorem
1.0.2
at parahoric level. In Section
4
, we study (torsors under) Bruhat-Tits group schemes as well as their deformation theory and show Theorem
1.0.4
.
In Section
5
, we give the construction of integral models at deeper Bruhat-Tits level and deduce our main results. We construct a Newton stratification on the integral models with deeper level.

2.
Bounds for (global and local) shtukas

Bounds for (global or local) shtukas usually are defined as certain (equivalence classes of) subschemes of affine Beilinson-Drinfeld Grassmannians, compare for example
[
Var04
,
Laf18
,
AH14
,
AH19
]
.
In this section we generalise the existing notion of bounds and provide a uniform treatment of global and local bounds.
An important class of bounds are generically defined bounds, which arise as the closure of their generic fibre.
In this case, the theory simplifies. For example, we show that a bound always admits a representative over its reflex field.
Moreover, generically defined bounds are the natural setting for our construction of integral models with deeper level structure in the following.

This includes in particular the bounds given by Schubert varieties inside the affine Grassmannian for parahoric group schemes that are often considered in applications, compare for example
[
Laf18
,
YZ19
,
FYZ24
]
.
We discuss the relation of our notion of bounds to various other notions of bounds in the literature.

The discussion of bounds is used to formulate and prove analogues of the functoriality results for moduli spaces of shtukas in the next section.
In particular, generically defined bounds provide the natural setting to formulate the functoriality of moduli spaces of shtukas under generic isomorphisms of group schemes in Theorem
3.5.5
.

2.1.
Beilinson-Drinfeld affine Grassmannians

We recall the definition of Beilinson-Drinfeld affine Grassmannians.
We work in the following setting. Let
k
be a field and let
X
be either a smooth geometrically connected curve over
k

Let

We denote by

\hat{\Gamma}_{x}^{\circ}
and
\Gamma_{x}^{(r)}
.

Remark 2.1.2
.

Using Beauville-Laszlo descent, compare
[
BL95
]
, a point of
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
is given by a tuple

((x_{i})_{i\in I},(\mathcal{E}_{j})_{j=0,\ldots,m},(\varphi_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,m},\varepsilon),

where the
\mathcal{E}_{j}
are now
\mathcal{G}
-torsors on
\hat{\Gamma}_{\underline{x}}
, the
\varphi_{j}\colon\mathcal{E}_{j}\to\mathcal{E}_{j-1}
are isomorphisms over
\hat{\Gamma}_{\underline{x}}\setminus\Gamma_{\underline{x}_{j}}
, and

is a trivialisation of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
defined over
\hat{\Gamma}_{\underline{x}}
.
This also shows that the natural map
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}|_{U}\xrightarrow{\cong}\left(\prod_{i\in I}\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}\right)|_{U}

We use the following global version of the (positive) loop group.

Definition 2.1.3
.

The
global loop group

\mathcal{L}_{X^{I}}\mathcal{G}
is the functor on the category of
k
-algebras

R\mapsto\left\{(\underline{x},g)\colon\underline{x}\in X^{I}(R),g\in\mathcal{G}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\underline{x}}^{0})\right\}.

The
positive global loop group

\mathcal{L}_{X^{I}}^{+}\mathcal{G}
is the functor on the category of
k
-algebras

R\mapsto\left\{(\underline{x},g)\colon\underline{x}\in X^{I}(R),g\in\mathcal{G}(\hat{\Gamma}_{\underline{x}})\right\}.

Moreover, for
r\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathcal{L}_{X^{I}}^{(r)}\mathcal{G}
be the functor on
k
-algebras

R\mapsto\left\{(\underline{x},g)\colon\underline{x}\in X^{I}(R),g\in\mathcal{G}(\Gamma_{\underline{x}}^{(r)})\right\}.

By
[
Hei10
, Proposition 2]
,
\mathcal{L}_{X^{I}}\mathcal{G}
is representable by an ind-affine group ind-scheme over
X^{I}
and
\mathcal{L}_{X^{I}}^{+}\mathcal{G}
is representable by a closed affine group subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{L}_{X^{I}}\mathcal{G}
over
X^{I}
.
Moreover by
[
Ric16
, Lemma 2.11]
, all
\mathcal{L}^{(r)}_{X^{I}}\mathcal{G}\to X^{I}
are representable by flat affine group schemes of finite type and the transition maps for varying

are affine.
The canonical map given by the projections

(1)

\mathcal{L}^{+}_{X^{I}}\mathcal{G}\xrightarrow{\cong}\varprojlim_{r}\mathcal{L}^{(r)}_{X^{I}}\mathcal{G}

is an isomorphism.
Assume moreover that
\mathcal{G}
is smooth. Then all
\mathcal{L}^{(r)}_{X^{I}}\mathcal{G}
are smooth and by (
1
) we have that
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{X^{I}}\mathcal{G}\to X^{I}
is flat.

There is a natural
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{X^{I}}\mathcal{G}
-action on
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
by changing the trivialisation
\varepsilon
.

2.2.
Definition of bounds

We define bounds inside the iterated affine Grassmannian by adapting the definition in the non-iterated setting of
[
AH19
, Definition 3.1.3]
.
We work in the setup of
[
HX23
, Section 2.6]
and allow different types of bounds (global/local/finite) at different legs. More precisely we define bounds in the following cases.

Definition 2.2.1
.

(1)

For each
i\in I
a ground field
F^{(i)}
is fixed, which can be either

(a)

of global type

F^{(i)}=F
(only in the
global setting
when
X
is a smooth geometrically connected curve over
k
),

(b)

of local type

F^{(i)}=F_{y}
, the completion of
F
at a closed point
y
of
X
,

(c)

or
of finite type

F^{(i)}=\mathbb{F}_{y}
, the residue field at
y
.

(2)

All finite extension of
F^{(i)}
are without further mention assumed to be separable and contained in a fixed separable closure
\widebar{F}^{(i)}
of
F^{(i)}
.
We set
\widetilde{X}_{F^{(i)}}
to be either
X
,
\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{y})
or
\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{y})
according to the type of
F^{(i)}
.
Moreover, let
\underline{F}=(F^{(i)})_{i\in I}
and
\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}}=\prod_{i\in I}\widetilde{X}_{F^{(i)}}
, where the product is taken over
k
.
For a finite extension
(F^{(i)})^{\prime}
of
F^{(i)}
we set
\widetilde{X}_{(F^{(i)})^{\prime}}
to be the normalisation of
\widetilde{X}_{F^{(i)}}
in
(F^{(i)})^{\prime}
. It is either a smooth curve in the global type, the (spectrum of the) valuation ring of
(F^{(i)})^{\prime}
in the local type or
\Spec(F^{(i)})^{\prime}
in the finite type.
In each case the natural map
\widetilde{X}_{(F^{(i)})^{\prime}}\to\widetilde{X}_{F^{(i)}}
is finite and faithfully flat.
For an
I
-tuple
\underline{F}^{\prime}=((F^{(i)})^{\prime})_{i\in I}
of componentwise finite extensions of
\underline{F}
we set
\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}=\prod_{i\in I}\widetilde{X}_{(F^{(i)})^{\prime}}
.

(3)

Let
\underline{F}_{1}
and
\underline{F}_{2}
be two
I
-tuples of finite extensions of
\underline{F}
.
Two quasi-compact closed subschemes
Z_{1}\subseteq\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}_{1}}^{I}
and
Z_{2}\subseteq\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}_{2}}^{I}
are called
equivalent
if there is an
I
-tuple of finite extensions
\underline{F}^{\prime}
of
F
such that componentwise
\underline{F}^{\prime}
is a finite extension of both
\underline{F}_{1}
and
\underline{F}_{2}
with
Z_{1}\times_{\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}_{1}}^{I}}\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}=Z_{2}\times_{\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}_{2}}^{I}}\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}
in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}
.

(4)

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be an equivalence class of quasi-compact closed subschemes
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}\subseteq\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}
and let us fix a representative
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
defined over some
\underline{F}^{\prime}
such that all components
(F^{(i)})^{\prime}
are Galois extensions of
F^{(i)}
.
We set

\Aut_{\mathcal{Z}}(\underline{F}^{\prime})=\{(g_{i})_{i\in I}\in\Gal((F^{(i)})^{\prime}/F^{(i)}):(g_{i})_{i\in I}^{\ast}(\mathcal{Z})=\mathcal{Z}\}
. The
reflex scheme

\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}}
of
\mathcal{Z}
is defined to be the quotient

\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}}=\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}/\Aut_{\mathcal{Z}}(\underline{F}^{\prime}).

(5)

By Lemma
2.2.3
the reflex scheme is independent of the choice of representative.
We say that
\mathcal{Z}

admits a representative over
\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}}
if
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
(or by Lemma
2.2.3
any other representative of
\mathcal{Z}
) descends to
\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}}
.

(6)

A
bound
is an equivalence class
\mathcal{Z}
of quasi-compact closed subschemes
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}\subset\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}
, such that all its representatives
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
are stable under the left
\mathcal{L}_{X^{I}}^{+}\mathcal{G}\times_{X^{I}}\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}
-action on
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}
.

Remark 2.2.2
.

The definition of the reflex scheme is due to
[
HX23
, Definition 2.6.3]

(1)

Later only global bounds (i.e. when

However, restricting to this situation does not significantly simplify the arguments in this section.
Bounds (in the global setting) with legs of different types are for example used in
[

]
.

(2)

In general, the reflex scheme can be complicated, but in all cases relevant later it will be a product

In particular, when

It is however not clear if there always exists a representative over the reflex scheme in general.

(3)

By construction, the reflex scheme comes equipped with maps

for any

-tuple
\underline{F}^{\prime}
of finite extensions of
\underline{F}
such that
\mathcal{Z}
admits a representative over
\underline{F}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be an equivalence class of closed subschemes of
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
and let
\underline{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}
be two
I
-tuples of finite extensions of
F
such that
\mathcal{Z}
admits representatives
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
and
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}
over
\underline{F}^{\prime}
and
\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}
, respectively. Then
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
is the scheme-theoretic image of the map
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}\to\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}\to\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
.
In particular, if
\mathcal{Z}
has a representative defined over some
\underline{F}^{\prime}
, it is unique.

Proof.

Let
\underline{F}^{\prime\prime\prime}
be componentwise a common extension of
\underline{F}^{\prime}
and
\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}
such that
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}\times_{\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}}\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime\prime}}=Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}\times_{\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}}\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime\prime}}
.
Then the projection
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\hookrightarrow\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\to\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
factors through
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
. But as the question is Zariski-local on
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
, we may assume that
\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}=\Spec(R)
and
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}=\Spec(A)
are affine.
Then both
\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime\prime}}=\Spec(R^{\prime})
and
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime\prime}}=\Spec(A\otimes_{R}R^{\prime})
are affine as well. The map

is a finite and faithfully flat and thus a universally injective map of
R
-modules.
Hence, the map
A\to A\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}
is injective. This shows that
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
is the scheme-theoretic image of
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\to Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
.
By the functoriality properties of scheme-theoretic images the projection
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}\to\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
factors through
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
and has
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
as its scheme-theoretic image.

Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}

g\times\mathrm{id}_{\tilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}^{I}}\colon S\times_{\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}}}\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}\to\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}

factors through
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}
for all finite extensions
\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}
such that
\mathcal{Z}
admits a representative
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}
over
\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof.

(1)

By construction,
g
factors through
S\to S\times_{Y}\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{g\times\mathrm{id}_{\tilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}}}\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
.

(2)

Assume that
g\colon S\to\Gr_{G,X^{I},I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}
factors through
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
.
By Lemma
2.2.3
, the induced map
g\colon S\to\Gr_{G,X^{I},I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}X_{\mathcal{Z}}^{I}
factors through the representative
Z_{\widetilde{X}_{\mathcal{Z}}}
over the reflex scheme. Hence,
g\times\mathrm{id}_{\tilde{X}_{\tilde{F}}^{I}}\colon S\times_{X^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}}}\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}\to\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}
factors through
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}
for all finite extensions
\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}
by the argument of Lemma
2.2.8
.

∎

Remark 2.2.10
.

We do not know whether (
2
) holds without the assumption that
\mathcal{Z}
admits a representative over the reflex field.
However, this assumption is satisfied for all examples of bounds considered in practice, in particular for all bounds considered in the following two subsections.

2.3.
Generically defined bounds

We now discuss a special class of bounds, namely those that are
generically defined
in the sense that they are given as the scheme-theoretic closure of their generic fibre.
In this case, the theory simplifies significantly, for example we show that in this case representatives always exist over the reflex field.
In this subsection we always assume without further mention that
\mathcal{L}_{X^{I}}^{+}\mathcal{G}
is flat over
X^{I}
, which is the case for example if
\mathcal{G}\to X
is smooth by
[
Ric16
, Lemma 2.11]
.

For this subsection we restrict ourselves to bounds of global type in the global setting or bounds of local type in the local setting.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a bound in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
. We say that
\mathcal{Z}
is
generically defined
if there is an
I
-tuple of finite extensions
\underline{F}^{\prime}
of
F
such that there exists a representative
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
over
\underline{F}^{\prime}
which is the scheme-theoretic closure of its generic fibre over every connected component of
\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
.

Remark 2.3.2
.

A bound with a representative
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
that is flat over
\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
is clearly generically defined, and all its representatives will then be flat.
When
I
has only one element, any representative of a generically defined bound in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}
is automatically flat over
\widetilde{X}_{F^{\prime}}
(as the base is a Dedekind scheme in this case).

Lemma 2.3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a generically defined bound in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
. Then every representative of
\mathcal{Z}
arises as the closure of its generic fibre.

Proof.

Let
\underline{F}^{\prime}
be such that the representative
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{Z}
is the closure of its generic fibre. As taking the scheme theoretic image commutes with flat base change, this property remains true for all representatives defined over some finite extension of
\underline{F}^{\prime}
.
Let
\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}
be a second
I
-tuple of field extensions over which a representative of
\mathcal{Z}
exists. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\underline{F}^{\prime}
is a componentwise extension of
\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}
.
Then the induced map of the generic fibre
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime},\eta_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}}\to Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}
factors through
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime},\eta_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}^{I}}
and both the maps
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime},\eta_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}}\to Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}\to Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}}
are scheme-theoretically surjective. This shows the claim.
∎

We show that Constructions
2.2.4
and
2.2.7
preserve the property of being generically defined.

Lemma 2.3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a bound in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
. Then
\mathcal{Z}
is generically defined if and only if there exist generically defined bounds
(\mathcal{Z}^{(i)})_{i\in I}
in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}
such that
\mathcal{Z}
arises from
(\mathcal{Z}^{(i)})_{i\in I}
as in Construction
2.2.4
.

The
\mathcal{Z}^{(i)}
are unique by the Lemma
2.2.5
. We call them the components of
\mathcal{Z}
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mathcal{Z}
arises from generically defined bounds
\mathcal{Z}^{(i)}
via Construction
2.2.4
.
Let
\underline{F}^{\prime}
be an
I
-tuple of finite extensions of
F
such that
\mathcal{Z}^{(i)}
admits a representative over
(F^{(i)})^{\prime}
for every
i\in I
.
As the generic point of
X^{I}
lies in the complement
U
of all diagonals and
\prod_{i\in I}Z^{(i)}_{(F^{(i)})^{\prime}}
is flat over
X^{I}
,

\left(\prod_{i\in I}Z^{(i)}_{F^{\prime}}\right)|_{U_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}}
is the closure of its generic fibre.

Conversely, assume that
\mathcal{Z}
is generically defined and let
(\mathcal{Z}^{(i)})_{i\in I}
be the components of
\mathcal{Z}
from Construction
2.2.7
. By construction, all
\mathcal{Z}^{(i)}
are generically defined and the generic fibre of any representative of
\mathcal{Z}
is the product of the corresponding representatives of the
\mathcal{Z}^{(i)}
.
This shows that
\mathcal{Z}
arises from the
\mathcal{Z}^{(i)}
via Construction
2.2.4
.
∎

Lemma 2.3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a generically defined bound. Its reflex scheme is given by
\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}}=\prod_{i\in I}\widetilde{X}_{\mathcal{Z}^{(i)}}
where the
\mathcal{Z}^{(i)}
are the components of
\mathcal{Z}
as in Lemma
2.3.4
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{Z}
admits a representative over its reflex scheme.

Proof.

The description of the reflex scheme follows from Lemma
2.2.5
. Moreover, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{Z}
admits a representative over its reflex scheme in the case
I=\{\ast\}
. In this case
\widetilde{X}_{\mathcal{Z}}=\widetilde{X}_{F_{\mathcal{Z}}}
for some finite extension
F_{\mathcal{Z}}/F
.
In particular there is a finite Galois extension
F^{\prime}/F_{\mathcal{Z}}
such that there exists a representative
Z_{F^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{Z}
over
F^{\prime}
.
By assumption it suffices to descend
Z_{F^{\prime},\eta_{F^{\prime}}}
to some subscheme
Z_{{F_{\mathcal{Z}}},\eta_{{F_{\mathcal{Z}}}}}
of the affine Grassmannian over
\eta_{F_{\mathcal{Z}}}
, the representative of
\mathcal{Z}
over
F_{\mathcal{Z}}
will then be given by the scheme-theoretic closure of
Z_{{F_{\mathcal{Z}}},\eta_{{F_{\mathcal{Z}}}}^{I}}
.
But
\eta_{F^{\prime}}\to\eta_{{F_{\mathcal{Z}}}}
is a finite étale Galois cover with Galois group
\Gal(F^{\prime}/{F_{\mathcal{Z}}})
and
Z_{F^{\prime},\eta_{F^{\prime}}}
is fixed by the Galois action by assumption. Thus,
Z_{F^{\prime},\eta_{F^{\prime}}}
descends to
{F_{\mathcal{Z}}}
.
∎

Remark 2.3.6
.

By Lemma
2.3.4
, a generically defined bound is uniquely determined by an
I
-tuple
(Z^{(i)})
of (equivalence classes of)
\mathcal{L}_{X}^{+}\mathcal{G}\times_{X}\eta
-stable closed subschemes of the generic fibre
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}\times_{X}\eta
of the Beilinson-Drinfeld affine Grassmannian.

If the generic fibre
G
of
\mathcal{G}
is reductive, the generic fibre of Beilinson-Drinfeld Grassmannian for
\mathcal{G}
can be identified (non canonically) with the (classical) affine Grassmannian
\Gr_{G}
for
G\times_{\Spec(F)}\Spec(F\left\llbracket\varpi\right\rrbracket)
by
[
Ric21
, Section 0.2]
.
Hence,
I
-tuples of equivalence classes of closed subschemes (that are stable under the loop group action) in
\Gr_{G}
determine bounds in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
for all smooth models
\mathcal{G}\to X
of
G
by taking closures.
Note that the bounds do not depend on the choice of isomorphism
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}\times_{X}\eta\cong\Gr_{G}
.

By a slight abuse of notation, we call such an equivalence class
\mathcal{Z}=(\mathcal{Z}^{(i)})_{i\in I}
in
\Gr_{G}
a
generically defined bound for
G
and denote the resulting bound in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
for any smooth model
\mathcal{G}
of
G
by
\mathcal{Z}
as well.

2.4.
Examples of bounds

2.4.1.
Beilinson-Drinfeld Schubert varieties

The bounds commonly used in practice are bounds given by Beilinson-Drinfeld Schubert varieties.
We recall and generalise their construction following
[
Laf18
, Définition 1.12]
.
We assume now that the generic fibre
G
of
\mathcal{G}
is reductive.
Let
\mu
be a conjugacy class of geometric cocharacters of
G
with reflex field
F_{\mu}
and let
F^{\prime}/F
be a finite separable extension that splits
G
.
By a slight abuse of notation, we denote by
\Gr_{G}
the (classical) affine Grassmannian
\Gr_{G\times_{F}\Spec(F\left\llbracket t\right\rrbracket)}
over
F
.
We denote by
\Gr_{G_{F^{\prime}}}^{\leq\mu}\subseteq\Gr_{G_{F^{\prime}}}=\Gr_{G}\times_{F}F^{\prime}
the Schubert variety corresponding to
\mu
, which descends to the reflex field
F_{\mu}
of
\mu
.
For an
I
-tuple of conjugacy classes of geometric cocharacters
\underline{\mu}=(\mu_{i})_{i\in I}
the (iterated) BD-Schubert variety
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet},\leq\underline{\mu}}
is the bound induced by the
\Gr_{G}^{\leq\mu_{i}}
as in the previous section, compare Remark
2.3.6
. More explicitly, it is defined as follows.

Definition 2.4.1

(compare
[
Zhu14
, Definition 3.1]
,
[
Laf18
, Définition 1.12]
)
.

(1)

Let
\mu
be a conjugacy class of cocharacters of
G
. Then

\Gr^{\leq\mu}_{\mathcal{G},X}
is defined as the scheme-theoretic image

\Gr^{\leq\mu}_{\mathcal{G},X}=\mathrm{image}\left(\Gr_{G}^{\leq\mu}\hookrightarrow\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}\times_{X}X_{\mu}\right)

where
X_{\mu}=\widetilde{X}_{F_{\mu}}
is the reflex curve of
\mu
.

(2)

Let
\underline{\mu}=(\mu_{i})_{i\in I}
be an
I
-tuple of conjugacy classes of geometric cocharacters of
G
and let
F_{\underline{\mu}}=(F_{\mu_{i}})_{i\in I}
be the
I
-tuple of reflex fields
F_{\mu_{i}}
of the
\mu_{i}
. Then
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet},\leq\underline{\mu}}
is defined from the
(\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{\leq\mu_{i}})_{i\in I}
as in Construction
2.2.4
. More precisely, it admits a representative defined over
F_{\underline{\mu}}
given by

\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet},\leq\underline{\mu}}=\mathrm{image}\left(\prod_{i\in I}\Gr^{\leq\mu_{i}}_{\mathcal{G},X}|_{U_{F_{\underline{\mu}}}}\hookrightarrow\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}X^{I}_{\underline{\mu}}\right),

where
U
denotes the complement of all diagonals in
X^{I}
,
U_{F_{\underline{\mu}}}=U\times_{X^{I}}X_{\underline{\mu}}^{I}
and the map
\prod_{i\in I}\Gr^{\leq\mu_{i}}_{\mathcal{G},X}|_{U_{F_{\underline{\mu}}}}\hookrightarrow\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}X^{I}_{\underline{\mu}}
is induced by the isomorphism of Remark
2.1.2
.

This construction generalises many of the boundedness conditions used in applications. When
\mathcal{G}
is constant split reductive, these bounds are constructed and used in
[
Laf18
]
. In particular, when
\mathcal{G}=\GL_{n}
,
I_{\bullet}=(\{1\},\{2\})
and
\underline{\mu}=((1,0,\ldots,0),(0,\ldots,0,-1))
this bound gives rise to the notion of Drinfeld shtuka as defined in
[
Dri87a
]
.
Further examples include the shtukas for unitary groups considered in
[
FYZ24
]
for
\mathcal{G}=U(n)
and
\underline{\mu}=((1,0,\ldots,0),\ldots,(1,0,\ldots,0))
.
Moreover, in the local setting (in the case that
\mathcal{G}
is constant split reductive these bounds are considered in
[
GL18
,
Li-23
]
.

Both
\Gr^{\leq\mu}_{\mathcal{G},X}
and
\Gr^{I_{\bullet},\leq\underline{\mu}}_{\mathcal{G},X}
are bounds in the sense of Definition
2.2.1
with reflex fields
F_{\mu}
and
F_{\underline{\mu}}
, respectively. In particular, they are generically defined. For
\Gr^{I_{\bullet},\leq\underline{\mu}}_{\mathcal{G},X}
this follows from Lemmas
2.2.5
and
2.3.4
.

2.4.2.
Further examples of global bounds

Let now
X
be a smooth projective and geometrically connected curve over
k
. We call bounds (in the sense of Definition
2.2.1
) defined in this situation also
global bounds
.
We now discuss various other constructions of global bounds used in the literature.

Example 2.4.2
.

(1)

Let
W
be an
E
-linear representation of
(^{L}G)^{I}
, where
{}^{L}G
denotes the
L
-group of
G
and where
E
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
such that all highest weights of the dual group of
G
are defined over
E
.
Then
\bigcup_{\underline{\mu}}\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet},\leq\underline{\mu}}
, where
\underline{\mu}
runs over all highest weights appearing in
W
, is defined over the compositum of all reflex fields of all the highest weights appearing and naturally descends to a closed subscheme
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet},W}
of
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
defined over
X^{I}
.

(2)

In the case that
\mathcal{G}
is constant split reductive,
[
Var04
]
defines bounds as follows. For a
\mathcal{G}
-torsor
\mathcal{E}
and a dominant coweight
\lambda
of
\mathcal{G}
we denote by
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{E}\times^{\mathcal{G}}V_{\lambda}
, where
V_{\lambda}
is the Weyl module for
\lambda
.
Then
\Gr_{G,X}^{I_{\bullet},\lesssim\underline{\mu}}
is defined to be the closed subscheme of
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
parametrising points
((x_{i})_{i\in I},(\mathcal{E}_{j})_{j=0,\ldots,m},(\varphi_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,m},\varepsilon)
such that

\varphi_{j,\lambda}(\mathcal{E}_{j,\lambda})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{j-1,\lambda}\left(\sum_{i\in I_{j}}\langle\lambda,\mu_{i}\rangle\Gamma_{x_{i}}\right)

for all
1\leq j\leq m
and all dominant weights
\lambda
of
\mathcal{G}
.

In contrast to the Beilinson-Drinfeld Schubert varieties defined above, these bounds admit a moduli description by definition. However,
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet},\lesssim\underline{\mu}}
might fail to be flat or reduced.

[
Neu16
, Definition 3.3.1]
gives a variant of this definition also for quasi-split reductive groups over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.

(3)

For general flat affine group schemes of finite type
\mathcal{G}\to X
,
[
AH21
]
give a boundedness condition by choosing a closed immersion
f\colon\mathcal{G}\hookrightarrow\SL(\mathcal{V})
for some vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
on
X
and an
I
-tuple of cocharacters
\underline{\mu}=(\mu_{i})_{i\in I}
of
\SL_{n}
, where
n
is the rank of
\mathcal{V}
.
The condition given in corresponds to the bound
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet},\lesssim\underline{\mu}}:=\Gr_{\SL(\mathcal{V}),X}^{I_{\bullet},\lesssim\underline{\mu}}\times_{\Gr_{\SL(\mathcal{V}),X}^{I_{\bullet}}}\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
defined via pullback of the corresponding bound for
\SL_{n}
along the map
f_{\ast}\colon\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\to\Gr_{\SL(\mathcal{V}),X}^{I_{\bullet}}
.

As noted in
[
AH21
, Remark 3.20]
, when
\mathcal{G}
is constant split reductive, this definition of bounds agrees with the bound
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet},\lesssim f\circ\underline{\mu}}
of
[
Var04
]
as in (
2
) for the
I
-tuple of cocharacters
f\circ\underline{\mu}=(f\circ\mu_{i})_{i\in I}
of
\mathcal{G}
.

All the bounds considered in this example clearly have reflex scheme
X^{I}
and a representative defined over
X^{I}
.
Moreover,
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet},W}
is generically defined by construction.

2.4.3.
Local bounds

By a
local bound
we mean a bound in the sense of Definition
2.2.1
when
X=\Spec(k\left\llbracket\varpi\right\rrbracket)
for a finite field
k
. Below, these are used as bounds for local shtukas.

[
HV11
, Definition 3.5]
give a definition of local bounds in the style of
[
Var04
]
for constant split reductive groups when
I=\{\ast\}
, compare also
[
AH14
, Example 4.11]
.

A different definition of local bounds is given in
[
AH14
, Definition 4.5]
, where bounds are not defined as subschemes of
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}
, but rather as formal subschemes of
\Gr_{\mathcal{G}}\hat{\times}_{\Spec k}\Spf(k\left\llbracket\varpi\right\rrbracket)
.
By
[
Li-23
, Lemma 1.4]
, we have

\Gr_{\mathcal{G}}\hat{\times}_{\mathbb{F}}\Spf(\mathcal{O})\cong\Gr_{\mathcal{G},k\left\llbracket\varpi\right\rrbracket}\hat{\times}_{\Spec k\left\llbracket\varpi\right\rrbracket}\Spf(k\left\llbracket\varpi\right\rrbracket),

so the local bounds in our setting (for

) define bounds in the sense of
[
AH14
]
by pullback under this isomorphism.

2.5.
Construction of local bounds from global bounds

We explain how to construct local bounds from global ones in our setting following the construction in
[
AH19
, Proposition 4.3.3]
.
We generalise their construction to the iterated setting and moreover also construct local bounds at points on the diagonal of
X^{I}
.

We consider the global setting. In other words,
X
denotes a smooth geometrically connected curve over
k
for the remainder of this section. Let
\mathcal{Z}
be an equivalence class of quasi-compact closed subschemes in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
.
We fix a closed point
y^{0}
of
X
and assume there is a closed point
\underline{y}
of the reflex scheme
\widetilde{X}_{\mathcal{Z}}^{I}
of
\mathcal{Z}
lying over
\Delta_{I}(y^{0})
, where
\Delta_{I}\colon X\to X^{I}
denotes the diagonal embedding.
This is the case if and only if all components of local or finite type in
\mathcal{Z}
are defined at
y^{0}
. This is satisfied in particular in the case that
\mathcal{Z}
only has components of global type that will be relevant later.

Let
\underline{F}^{\prime}
be an
I
-tuple of finite extensions of
\underline{F}
such that
\mathcal{Z}
admits a representative
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
over
\underline{F}^{\prime}
.
We fix a closed point
\underline{y}^{\prime}
of
\widetilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
over
\underline{y}
and let us denote by
y_{i}^{\prime}\in\widetilde{X}_{(F^{(i)})^{\prime}}
its
i
-th component.
We set
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime},\underline{y}^{\prime}}=Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}\times_{\tilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}}\Spec(\bigotimes_{\mathbb{F}_{y^{0}}}\mathcal{O}_{y^{\prime}_{i}})
where we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{y^{\prime}_{i}}
the completed local ring at
y_{i}^{\prime}
in
\widetilde{X}_{(F^{(i)})^{\prime}}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{Z}_{\underline{y}}
the equivalence class of
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime},\underline{y}^{\prime}}
in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{y^{0}})}^{I_{\bullet}}
(which will be of local type in the sense of Definition
2.2.1
).
Note that components of global type for
\mathcal{Z}
become components of local type for
\mathcal{Z}_{\underline{y}}
while components of local (respectively finite) type for
\mathcal{Z}
remain of local (respectively finite) type for
\mathcal{Z}_{\underline{y}}
.

Lemma 2.5.1

(compare
[
AH19
, Proposition 4.3.3]
)
.

The equivalence class
\mathcal{Z}_{\underline{y}}
does neither depend on the choice of representative
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
nor the choice of the point
\underline{y}^{\prime}
.
Moreover, when
\mathcal{Z}
is a (global) bound,
\mathcal{Z}_{\underline{y}}
is a (local) bound.

Proof.

We first assume that
\underline{F}^{\prime}
is a componentwise Galois extension of
\underline{F}
.
Let
\underline{y}^{\prime\prime}
be a second place of
\tilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
lying over
\underline{y}
.
As
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
is Galois-invariant,
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime},\underline{y}^{\prime}}
and
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime},\underline{y}^{\prime\prime}}
are equivalent.
Let now
\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}
be a componentwise finite extension of
\underline{F}^{\prime}
and let
\underline{y}^{\prime\prime}
be a closed point of
\underline{F}^{\prime\prime}
lying over
\underline{y}^{\prime}
. Then clearly
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime\prime},\underline{y}^{\prime\prime}}=Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime},\underline{y}^{\prime}}\times_{\Spec(\bigotimes_{\mathbb{F}_{y^{0}}}\mathcal{O}_{y^{\prime}_{i}})}\Spec(\bigotimes_{\mathbb{F}_{y^{0}}}\mathcal{O}_{y^{\prime\prime}_{i}})
.
This shows that
\mathcal{Z}_{\underline{y}}
is independent of the auxiliary choices.
Moreover, when
\mathcal{Z}
is stable under the loop group action so is
\mathcal{Z}_{\underline{y}}
.
∎

We slightly change notation and fix an
I
-tuple
\underline{y}^{0}=(y^{0}_{i})_{i\in I}
of closed points of
X
for the remainder of this subsection.
For a closed point
y^{0}
of
X
we write
y^{0}\in\underline{y}^{0}
if
y^{0}=y^{0}_{i}
for some

. We set
I^{y^{0}}=\{i\in I\colon y^{0}_{i}=y^{0}\}
and define

analogously.

Definition 2.5.2
.

(1)

An
I
-tuple of local bounds at
\underline{y}^{0}
is a tuple of (local) bounds
(\mathcal{Z}_{y^{0}})_{y^{0}\in\underline{y}^{0}}
, where
\mathcal{Z}_{y^{0}}
is defined in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{y^{0}})}^{I^{y^{0}}_{\bullet}}
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a global bound and assume that
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{X^{I}}\mathcal{G}\to X^{I}
is flat. For each
{y^{0}}\in\underline{y}^{0}
we fix a closed point
\underline{y}
on

lying over
\Delta_{I^{y^{0}}}(y^{0})
. Its associated

is
(\mathcal{Z}_{\underline{y}})_{y^{0}\in\underline{y}^{0}}
, where

is as constructed above (using Construction

).

Note that when all

are pairwise distinct, an

-tuple of bounds in the sense of the previous definition is an actual
I
-tuple of bounds
(\mathcal{Z}_{y^{0}_{i}})_{i\in I}
, where each
\mathcal{Z}_{y^{0}_{i}}
lives inside
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},\mathcal{O}_{y^{0}_{i}}}
. Our definition gives the natural generalisation to the case where

is also allowed to lie on a diagonal of

.

Example 2.5.3
.

By construction, the local bounds attached to a Schubert variety in the global setting is again a Schubert variety (in the local setting).
Compare also
[
Neu16
, Theorem 3.3.9]
for a local-global compatibility for global bounds in the style of
[
Var04
]
and local bounds in the style of
[
HV11
]
.

3.
Moduli spaces of bounded, iterated, global shtukas

Global shtukas for
\GL_{n}
were first introduced in
[
Dri87a
]
and generalised to split reductive groups (respectively to flat affine group schemes of finite type) by
[
Var04
]
and
[
AH21
]
, respectively.
In this section, we recall the definition and basic properties of moduli spaces of (bounded, iterated, global) shtukas.
We rely of the definition of bounds given in the previous section and generalise the Serre-Tate theorem, the local model theorem and functoriality results to this setting.
The functoriality results will be crucial to the construction of integral models with deeper level structure below.

3.1.
Global shtukas

We consider the “global setting” of the previous section:
Let
X
be a smooth projective and geometrically connected curve over
k={\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, let
\mathcal{G}\to X
be a smooth affine group scheme,
let

be a finite set, and let

be a partition of

We denote by

the stack of
\mathcal{G}
-bundles on

by
[

Definition 3.1.1
.

We denote by

{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
whose
S

•

are points on

(or

•

are
\mathcal{G}
-bundles on

for

, and

•

are isomorphisms of
\mathcal{G}
-bundles away from the graphs
\Gamma_{x_{i}}\subseteq X_{S}

As above, we use the shorthand notation

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(p_{m},p_{0})}

whose

((x_{i})_{i\in I},(\mathcal{E}_{j})_{j=0,\ldots,m},(\varphi_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,m},\theta),

are points on

called the

(or

) for

,

•

\mathcal{E}_{j}\in\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}(R)
are
\mathcal{G}
-bundles on

for

,

The projection to the characteristic sections defines a map

By

. By the previous observation, this stack does not depend on the choice of the partition

of

whenever the

are pairwise different.
Usually, when the index set

and the curve

Definition 3.1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a bound in the sense of Definition
2.2.1
. Let

\underline{\mathcal{E}}=((x_{i})_{i\in I},(\mathcal{E}_{j})_{j=0,\ldots,m},(\varphi_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,m},\theta)\in(\Sht_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\widetilde{X}_{\mathcal{Z}}^{I})(R).

By
[

, Lemma 3.4]
, there exists an étale cover
\Spec(R^{\prime})\to\Spec(R)
such that

\hat{\Gamma}_{\underline{x}_{R^{\prime}}}\to\hat{\Gamma}_{\underline{x}}
trivializes
\mathcal{E}_{0}|_{\hat{\Gamma}_{\underline{x}}}
.
Fixing a trivialisation
\alpha\colon\mathcal{E}_{0}|_{\hat{\Gamma}_{\underline{x}_{R^{\prime}}}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{G}|_{\hat{\Gamma}_{\underline{x}_{R^{\prime}}}}

defines a point

using the description of the Beilinson-Drinfeld affine Grassmannian of Remark

.
We say that
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
is
bounded by

\mathcal{Z}
if for some (or by Lemma
2.2.8
equivalently for all)

-tuples of finite extensions
\underline{F}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{Z}
admits a representative over
\underline{F}^{\prime}
, the map

g\times_{\widetilde{X}_{\mathcal{Z}}^{I}}\id_{\tilde{X}^{I}_{F^{\prime}}}\colon\Spec(R^{\prime})\times_{\widetilde{X}_{\mathcal{Z}}^{I}}\tilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}\to(\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\times_{X^{I}}\tilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I})

factors through
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
. As
\mathcal{Z}
is invariant under the left
\mathcal{L}_{X^{I}}^{+}\mathcal{G}
-action, the definition is independent of the choices of the étale cover

and the trivialisation

.

We denote the moduli stack of shtukas bounded by

by
\Sht_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathcal{Z}}=\Sht^{I_{\bullet},\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G},X}
. It is a Deligne-Mumford stack defined over

and a closed substack of

When the bound

3.2.
Local shtukas with multiple legs and local-global compatibility

We again consider the local setting.
Let
\mathcal{O}
be the ring of integers of a local field in characteristic
p
with residue field
k
a finite extension of
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.
The choice of a uniformiser
\varpi
of
\mathcal{O}
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}\cong k\left\llbracket\varpi\right\rrbracket
.
Let
\mathcal{G}\to\Spec\mathcal{O}
be a smooth affine
\mathcal{O}
-group scheme, let
G
be its generic fibre.
As before, let
I
be a finite set and let
I_{\bullet}=(I_{1},\ldots,I_{m})
be a partition of
I
.
We set
\mathcal{O}_{I}=\mathcal{O}\hat{\otimes}_{k}\ldots\hat{\otimes}_{k}\mathcal{O}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{O}_{I}\cong k\left\llbracket\varpi_{i}\right\rrbracket_{i\in I}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{N}ilp_{\mathcal{O}_{I}}
the category of
\mathcal{O}_{I}
-algebras
R
such that the images
\zeta_{i}
of
\varpi_{i}
in
R
under the structure map
\mathcal{O}_{I}\to R
are nilpotent.

Similarly to the global loop group defined in the previous section, the (local) loop group
LG
of
G
is defined as the functor on
k
-algebras

R\mapsto G(R\left(\!\left(\varpi\right)\!\right)).

Analogously, the positive loop group
L^{+}\mathcal{G}
is defined as the functor on
k
-algebras

R\mapsto\mathcal{G}(R\left\llbracket\varpi\right\rrbracket).

The functor
LG
is representable by an ind-affine group ind-scheme while
L^{+}\mathcal{G}
is representable by an affine group scheme over
k
.

By
[
AH14
, Proposition 2.4]
, the category of
L^{+}\mathcal{G}
-torsors over a
k
-algebra
R
is equivalent to the category of formal
\mathcal{G}
-torsors on
\Spf(R\left\llbracket\varpi\right\rrbracket)
. By a slight abuse of notation, we denote by
\mathcal{E}\left[\frac{1}{\varpi}\right]=\mathcal{E}\times^{L^{+}\mathcal{G}}LG
the associated
LG
-torsor of an
L^{+}\mathcal{G}
-torsor
\mathcal{E}
.
Note that for
R\in\mathcal{N}ilp_{\mathcal{O}_{I}}
, we have
R\left(\!\left(\varpi\right)\!\right)=R\left\llbracket\varpi\right\rrbracket\left[\frac{1}{\varpi-\zeta_{i}}\right]
for all
i\in I
as the
\zeta_{i}
are nilpotent.

Definition 3.2.1
.

A
local shtuka
(with
I
-legs) over
R\in\mathcal{N}ilp_{\mathcal{O}_{I}}
is given by a tuple

((\mathcal{E}_{j})_{j=0,\ldots,m},(\varphi_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,m},\theta),
where

•

the
\mathcal{E}_{j}
are
L^{+}\mathcal{G}
-torsors on
R
,

•

the
\varphi_{j}\colon\mathcal{E}_{j}[\frac{1}{\varpi}]\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{E}_{j-1}[\frac{1}{\varpi}]
are isomorphisms of the associated
L\mathcal{G}
-torsors,

•

and
\theta\colon\mathcal{E}_{0}\xrightarrow{\cong}\sigma^{\ast}\mathcal{E}_{m}
is an isomorphism of
L^{+}\mathcal{G}
-torsors on
R
.

In a similar fashion as in the global setting, we define bounded local shtukas.

Definition 3.2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a (local) bound in the sense of Definition
2.2.1
and let
\underline{y}
be a closed point of
\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}}
. Let
\underline{\mathcal{E}}=(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
be a local shtuka over
R\in\mathcal{N}ilp_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{y}}}
.
Let
R\to R^{\prime}
be an fppf-cover that trivialises
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and choose a trivialisation
\alpha
of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
over
R^{\prime}
.
Using the equivalence of
L^{+}\mathcal{G}
-torsors and formal
\mathcal{G}
-torsors of
[
AH14
, Proposition 2.4]
,

\left(\mathcal{E}_{m}\overset{\varphi_{m}}{\dashrightarrow}\mathcal{E}_{m-1}\overset{\varphi_{m-1}}{\dashrightarrow}\ldots\overset{\varphi_{1}}{\dashrightarrow}\mathcal{E}_{0}\overset{\alpha}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{G}\right)

defines a point
g\in\left(\Gr_{\mathcal{G},\mathcal{O}}^{I_{\bullet}}\hat{\times}_{\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{I})}\Spf(\mathcal{O}_{I})\right)(R^{\prime})
.
We say that
\underline{\mathcal{E}}

is bounded by
\mathcal{Z}
if for some (or equivalently for all) representatives
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{Z}
over an
I
-tuple of finite extensions
\underline{F}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{Z}
admits a representative over
\underline{F}^{\prime}
, the map

g\times\id_{\mathcal{O}_{I}^{\prime}}\colon\Spec(R^{\prime})\times_{\Spf(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{y}})}\Spf(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}})\to(\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}\hat{\times}_{\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{I})}\Spf(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}))

factors through
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}\times_{\tilde{X}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}^{I}}\Spf(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}})
for all closed points
\underline{y}^{\prime}
of
\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
lying over
\underline{y}
. As
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
is invariant under the left
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}}^{+}\mathcal{G}
-action, the definition is independent of the choice of the trivialisation
\alpha
.

Remark 3.2.3
.

In the literature on local shtukas the Frobenius-linear map is often defined to be an isomorphism
\theta^{\prime}\colon\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{0}\cong\mathcal{E}_{m}
, compare for example
[
HV11
,
AH14
]
. In particular, for a local shtuka
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
in our sense inverting all maps yields a local shtuka

\underline{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}=\left(\mathcal{E}_{m}\overset{\varphi^{-1}_{m}}{\dashleftarrow}\mathcal{E}_{m-1}\overset{\varphi^{-1}_{m-1}}{\dashleftarrow}\ldots\overset{\varphi^{-1}_{1}}{\dashleftarrow}\mathcal{E}_{0}\overset{\theta^{-1}}{\leftarrow}\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{m}\right)

in the more classical sense (and vice versa).
Moreover,
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
is bounded by some bound
\mathcal{Z}
if and only if
\underline{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}
is bounded by the inverse bound
\mathcal{Z}^{-1}
as defined in
[
HV23
, Lemma 2.12]
.

We explain how to associate to a global shtuka an
I
-tuple of local shtukas.
At points away from the diagonal, this is contained in
[
AH14
]
.
We extend their construction to also include points on the diagonal using local shtukas with multiple legs.

We consider the global setting from above.
Let
X
be a smooth, projective, and geometrically connected curve over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, and let
\mathcal{G}\to X
be a smooth affine group scheme.
We use the notation following
[
AH14
, Remark 5.2]
. Let

be a closed point. We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{y}
the completed local ring at

, and by

and

its maximal ideal with uniformiser

and residue field, respectively.
Moreover, let

.
Then the

-adic completion of

where the

Let us fix an
I

of

.
We use the notation of Section

for some

Let
\underline{\mathcal{E}}\in\Sht_{\mathcal{G},\underline{y}}(R)
.
By the observation above, the

-adic completion of

Hence, each component

-torsor

Then,

\widehat{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{y}}=\left(\left(\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{j,y}}^{(0)}\right)_{0\leq j\leq m},\left(\varphi_{j}|_{V(\mathfrak{a}_{0})}\right)_{1\leq j\leq m},\tilde{\theta}\right)
is a local

-shtuka over

Proposition 3.2.6
.

its associated local shtuka

at

Proof.

Let

be an étale cover trivialising

by assumption on

Restricting

yields trivialisations of

for all

and hence points

Let us fix an

of

such that

admits a representative over
\underline{F}^{\prime}

3.3.
A Serre-Tate theorem

The classical Serre-Tate theorem relates the deformation theory of an abelian variety in characteristic
p
to the deformation theory of its
p
-divisible group.
[
AH14
, Theorem 5.10]
proves an analogue of the Serre-Tate theorem for
\mathcal{G}
-shtukas.
We generalise the Serre-Tate theorem for shtukas to the iterated case using local shtukas with multiple legs.
The local-global compatibility then also gives a bounded version of the Serre-Tate theorem.

We follow
[
AH14
, Section 5]
and start by analysing quasi-isogenies of local and global shtukas.

Definition 3.3.1
.

(1)

Let
\underline{\mathcal{E}}

and
\underline{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}

be two global shtukas over an
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
-algebra
R
with the same characteristic sections.
A
quasi-isogeny

f\colon\underline{\mathcal{E}}\dashrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
is a tuple of isomorphisms
f_{j}\colon\mathcal{E}_{j}|_{X_{R}\setminus D_{R}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{j}|_{X_{R}\setminus D_{R}}
for all
0\leq j\leq m
away from some effective divisor

of

, such that

for all

and

.

(2)

Let
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
and
\underline{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
be two local shtukas over an
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
-algebra
R
.
A
quasi-isogeny

f\colon\underline{\mathcal{E}}\dashrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
is a tuple of isomorphisms
f_{j}\colon\mathcal{E}_{j}\left[\frac{1}{\varpi}\right]\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{j}\left[\frac{1}{\varpi}\right]
of the associated

-torsors for all

for all

In either case, we denote by
\mathrm{QIsog}(\underline{\mathcal{E}},\underline{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})
the set of quasi-isogenies between
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
and
\underline{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
.

Example 3.3.2
.

Let
\underline{y}
be an

-tuple of closed points of

Proposition 3.3.4

(Rigidity of quasi-isogenies)
.

Let

be a nilpotent ideal with quotient

Note that rigidity of quasi-isogenies for local shtukas is shown in
[
AH14
, Proposition 2.11]
and
[
Li-23
, Proposition 2.3]
in the iterated setting.

Proof.

It suffices to treat the case

from

(respectively

(respectively

and

\underline{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}
be a global shtuka over
\bar{R}

over

Proposition 3.3.5

(Serre-Tate Theorem for iterated (bounded) shtukas, compare

.

Let
\underline{y}

I

The second statement clearly follows from the first and Proposition
3.2.6
. We prove the first following the proof of
[
AH14
, Theorem 5.10]
by constructing an inverse functor.
It suffices to treat the case

. Then
\sigma

We set

\underline{\mathcal{E}}
over

along the

3.5.
Functoriality results for moduli spaces of shtukas

We study functoriality properties of moduli spaces of shtukas under homomorphisms of group schemes.
As the main result in this section we show that for a generic isomorphism
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
the induced map on the moduli stacks of (iterated, bounded) shtukas is schematic, separated and of finite type. Moreover, when
\mathcal{G}
is parahoric and the bound is generically defined, the map is proper and surjective.
The result is an extension of
[
Bre18
, Theorem 3.20]
to our setting.
In particular, the use of global bounds allows us to work on the whole curve
X
and we need not restrict the legs to a formal neighbourhood of a fixed point as in
[
Bre18
]
.
Moreover, we show that the level maps are generically finite étale, which is not part of

In our setting, the notion of a shtuka datum (respectively a map of shtuka data) in the sense of

Definition 3.5.1
.

A
shtuka datum

is a pair of a smooth affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}\to X
and a bound
\mathcal{Z}
in
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},X}^{I_{\bullet}}
.
Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
and

be two shtuka data such that for every

\mathcal{Z}
and

A

f\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
such that for all

-tuples of finite extensions
\underline{F}^{\prime}
such that both
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}

\underline{F}^{\prime}

Under mild hypotheses, a map of shtuka data

Let

Remark 3.5.3

We need the following lemma on twisted flag varieties in the local setting.

Lemma 3.5.4
.

Let
k\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
be the field of formal Laurent series over an arbitrary field
k
and let
\mathcal{O}=k\left\llbracket t\right\rrbracket

Let

be a smooth affine group scheme over

\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
be two smooth integral models of

with geometrically connected fibres.
Let

be a homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}
-group schemes that is the identity on
G
over

is a closed immersion.

(1)

The corresponding twisted flag variety

is representable by a separated scheme of finite type over

If

(2)

Then

are compact open subgroups of

In particular, the quotient
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\mathcal{O})/\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O})
is discrete and finite.

(3)

Let
S
be an

-scheme. Giving a

-torsor over

is equivalent to giving a
L^{+}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
-torsor
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
over
S
together with an isomorphism
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}/L^{+}\mathcal{G}\xrightarrow{\cong}L^{+}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}/L^{+}\mathcal{G}
.

Note that giving an isomorphism
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}/L^{+}\mathcal{G}\xrightarrow{\cong}L^{+}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}/L^{+}\mathcal{G}
in (3) is also clearly equivalent to giving a section in
\left(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}/L^{+}\mathcal{G}\right)(S)
.

Proof.

(1)

By the argument in the proof of
[
Bre18
, Lemma 3.17]
, the quotient stack
L^{+}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}/L^{+}\mathcal{G}
is representable by a separated scheme of finite type over

that is moreover a closed subscheme of the affine Grassmannian

.
For the second claim, it suffices to show that

is trivial by the moduli description of the quotient stack.
But this is shown in the proof of
[
Ric20
, Corollary 3.22]
.

(2)

Clearly, both
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\mathcal{O})
are compact open subgroups of

by construction. The existence of the map

then means that
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O})
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\mathcal{O})

The assertion on the quotient then directly follows from basic facts from topology.

(3)

Given a

-torsor

on

-torsor is given by

The map on sections given by

then induces an isomorphism

∎

We are now in a position to prove the main result in this section, an extension of the functoriality result of
[
Bre18
, Theorem 3.20]
to moduli spaces of shtukas with global and generically bounds.

Theorem 3.5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
be two smooth affine group schemes over
X
with geometrically connected fibres.
Let
f\colon(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})\to(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},\mathcal{Z}^{\prime})
be a map of shtuka data such that the map

is an isomorphism over

for a finite set of closed points
\left\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\right\}
of
X
. Assume that
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
admits a representative over its reflex scheme.

(1)

The induced map

f_{\ast}\colon\Sht^{I_{\bullet},\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G},X}\times_{\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}}}\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}.\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}\to\Sht^{I_{\bullet},\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime},X}\times_{\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}}\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}.\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}

is schematic, separated and of finite type.

(2)

Assume that
\mathcal{G}
is a parahoric Bruhat-Tits group scheme. Then the map
f_{\ast}
is proper.

(3)

Assume that
Z_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}\to Z^{\prime}_{\underline{F}^{\prime}}
is an isomorphism over

for some
\underline{F}^{\prime}
. Then over
U^{I}\times_{X^{I}}\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}.\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}
the map

is étale locally representable by the constant scheme

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\underline{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\mathcal{O}_{y_{i}})/\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{y_{i}})}.

In particular,
f_{\ast}
is
finite étale and surjective over

.

(4)

Under the assumptions of (2) and (3) assume additionally that
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
is generically defined. Then

is surjective.

Remark 3.5.6
.

The first two statements are direct analogues of the corresponding statements in
[
Bre18
, Theorem 3.20]
, while there is no analogue of the third assertion in
[
Bre18
, Theorem 3.20]
.
In order to get surjectivity of the map
f_{\ast}
, in
[
Bre18
]
it is assumed that the bound
\mathcal{Z}
arises as the base change of
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
under the map
f_{\ast}
on affine Grassmannians.
This assumption does not seem adequate in our setting, in particular, it is not satisfied for the bounds given by Schubert varieties.

We thus replace this assumption by the condition that the bounds are generically defined and that the map is a generic isomorphism, both of which are satisfied in the examples of interest.
Note that when
\mathcal{Z}
arises as a base change, the map
\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
is clearly an isomorphism over

Proof.

(1)

We proceed as in the proof of
[
Bre18

.
We consider the projection
\Sht_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathcal{Z}}\to\prod_{j=1,\ldots,m}\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}

Let us fix

\underline{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}=((x_{i})_{i\in I},(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{j})_{j=0,\ldots,m},(\varphi_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,m},\theta)\in\left(\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}\times_{\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}}\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}.\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}\right)(S).

We claim that the induced map

is a quasi-compact locally closed immersion. This shows the assertion (

.

4.
Torsors under Bruhat-Tits group schemes

We show that a Bruhat-Tits group scheme is the limit of all corresponding parahoric group schemes and use this observation to show that the induced map on the level of
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
is an open immersion.
We first discuss (pseudo-)torsors for limits of groups.

4.1.
Pseudo-torsors for limits of groups

We use the following result on pseudo-torsors under limits of groups.
For a sheaf of groups
\underline{G}
on a site
\mathcal{C}
we denote by
\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}}
the category of
\underline{G}
-pseudo-torsors

for
\underline{G}
with
\underline{G}
-equivariant maps.
In other words, an object of
\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}}
is given by a sheaf
E
on
\mathcal{C}
together with a (right) action
E\times\underline{G}\to E
of
\underline{G}
such that the induced map
E\times\underline{G}\to E\times E
given by
(e,g)\mapsto(e,eg)
is an isomorphism.
A map
f\colon\underline{G}\to\underline{G}^{\prime}
of sheaves of groups on
\mathcal{C}
induces a functor
f_{\ast}\colon\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}}\to\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}^{\prime}}
given by
E\mapsto E\times^{\underline{G}}\underline{G}^{\prime}
, where the action of
\underline{G}^{\prime}
is by right multiplication in the second factor. Moreover, the canonical map
(\id_{E},\mathbf{1}_{\underline{G}^{\prime}})\colon E\to E\times^{\underline{G}}\underline{G}^{\prime}
is
\underline{G}
-equivariant for the
\underline{G}
-action on
E\times^{\underline{G}}\underline{G}^{\prime}
via
f
on the second factor.

A
\underline{G}
-pseudo-torsor
E
is a
G
-torsor
if for every object
U
on
\mathcal{C}
there is a cover
\{U_{i}\to U\colon i\in I\}
of
U
in
\mathcal{C}
such that
\Gamma(U_{i},E)\neq\emptyset
. We denote by
\mathfrak{B}(\underline{G})
the full subcategory of
\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}}
of
\underline{G}
-torsors on
\mathcal{C}
. The map
f_{\ast}
for a map of sheaves of groups
f\colon\underline{G}\to\underline{G}^{\prime}
restricts to a map
f_{\ast}\colon\mathfrak{B}(\underline{G})\to\mathfrak{B}(\underline{G}^{\prime})
.

Lemma 4.1.1
.

Let
I
be a finite partially ordered set and let
(\underline{G}_{i})_{i\in I}
be a diagram of sheaves of groups over
I
.
Let
\underline{G}=\varprojlim_{i\in I}\underline{G}_{i}
. Then
\underline{G}
is a sheaf of groups on
\mathcal{C}
together with a compatible system of projection maps
f_{i}\colon\underline{G}\to\underline{G}_{i}
.
The functor

\varprojlim_{i\in I}f_{i,\ast}\colon\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}}\to\varprojlim_{i\in I}\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}_{i}},\qquad E\mapsto(E\times^{\underline{G}}\underline{G}_{i})_{i\in I}

has a right-adjoint given by

\lim\colon\left(\varprojlim_{i\in I}\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}_{i}}\right)\to\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}},\qquad(E_{i})_{i\in I}\mapsto\varprojlim_{i\in I}E_{i}.

Moreover, the restriction
\varprojlim_{i\in I}f_{i,\ast}\colon\mathfrak{B}(\underline{G})\to\varprojlim_{i\in I}\mathfrak{B}(\underline{G}_{i})
to the full subcategory of torsors is fully faithful.

Proof.

As a first step, we show that
\varprojlim_{i\in I}E_{i}
is indeed a pseudo-torsor for
\underline{G}
. The sheaf of groups
\underline{G}
acts on
E_{i}
by the action induced by
f_{i}
, and all these actions are compatible by the observation above that the reduction maps are equivariant. Hence,
\varprojlim_{i\in I}E_{i}
carries a canonical
\underline{G}
-action.
As all the
E_{i}
are pseudo-torsors under
\underline{G}_{i}
, the induced map

\displaystyle\left(\varprojlim_{i\in I}E_{i}\right)\times\underline{G}

\displaystyle\to\left(\varprojlim_{i\in I}E_{i}\right)\times\left(\varprojlim_{i\in I}E_{i}\right)

\displaystyle((e_{i})_{i\in I},g)

\displaystyle\mapsto((e_{i})_{i\in I},(e_{i}f_{i}(g))_{i\in I})

is an isomorphism, so
\varprojlim_{i\in I}E_{i}
is a
\underline{G}
-pseudo-torsor.

As a next step, we show that the limit is right adjoint to the family of projections.
Let
(F_{i})_{i\in I}\in\varprojlim_{i\in I}\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}_{i}}
.
A
\underline{G}
-equivariant map
E\to F_{i}
factors as
E\to E\times^{\underline{G}}\underline{G}_{i}\to F_{i}
for a unique
\underline{G}_{i}
-equivariant map
E\times^{\underline{G}}\underline{G}_{i}\to F_{i}
. Hence, we get

\Hom_{\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}}}(E,\varprojlim_{i\in I}F_{i})=\Hom_{\varprojlim_{i\in I}\mathrm{PTor}_{\underline{G}_{i}}}((E\times^{\underline{G}}\underline{G}_{i})_{i\in I},(F_{i})_{i\in I}).

In order to see that the restriction to
\mathfrak{B}(\underline{G})
is fully faithful, we check that the unit of the adjunction
E\mapsto\varprojlim_{i\in I}E\times^{\underline{G}}\underline{G}_{i}
is an isomorphism for
E\in\mathfrak{B}(\underline{G})
. We can do so locally, so we may assume that
E
is trivial. As all maps
E\to E\times^{\underline{G}}\underline{G}_{i}
are
\underline{G}
-equivariant, choosing a trivialisation of
E
induces a compatible choice of trivialisations of all
E\times^{\underline{G}}\underline{G}_{i}
. Hence, the map
E\to\varprojlim_{i\in I}E\times^{\underline{G}}\underline{G}_{i}
is given by
\underline{G}\to\varprojlim_{i\in I}\underline{G}_{i}
, which is an isomorphism by construction.
∎

Remark 4.1.2
.

Note that given a compatible family of
\underline{G}_{i}
-torsors
(E_{i})_{i\in I}\in\varprojlim_{i\in I}\mathfrak{B}(G_{i})
, their limit will in general not be a
\underline{G}
-torsor, as it might not be possible to produce a compatible system of sections for
(E_{i})_{i\in I}
.
For example, consider
G_{1}=G_{2}=\{e\}
the trivial group and
G_{3}=\mathbb{Z}/2
. Then
G_{1}\times_{G_{3}}G_{2}=\{e\}
is again the trivial group. Let us moreover consider the sets

and

is a trivial

-torsor for all

for

is empty, hence in particular not a torsor under the trivial group.

After fixing a trivialisation of

Let now

.

(2)

For each

Proof of Theorem

.

We use induction on

Corollary 4.2.6
.

The Bruhat-Tits group scheme

Proof.

The inclusion

∎

We consider torsors for the Bruhat-Tits group schemes above.
Recall that a limit of

Proposition 4.2.7
.

Let

-algebra.
Let

.

4.3.
Torsors for global Bruhat-Tits group schemes

The goal of this subsection is to show that the isomorphism of Bruhat-Tits group schemes of Theorem
4.2.1
induces an immersion on the level of the corresponding moduli stacks
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
.
Therefore, let us now change perspective and consider (global) Bruhat-Tits group schemes in the following sense.
Let again
X
be a smooth projective and geometrically connected curve over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
with function field
F
.

Definition 4.3.1
.

A smooth, affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}\to X
is called a
(global) Bruhat-Tits group scheme
if it has geometrically connected fibres, its generic fibre
\mathcal{G}_{F}=G
is a reductive group over
F
and if for all closed points
x
of
X
the pullback
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{O}_{x}}=\mathcal{G}\times_{X}\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{x})
is of the form
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
for some bounded subset
\Omega
contained in an apartment of the Bruhat-Tits building
B(G/F_{x})
.
The group scheme
\mathcal{G}
is called a
parahoric (Bruhat-Tits) group scheme
if moreover all
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{O}_{x}}
are parahoric group schemes.

Let
G
be a (connected) reductive group over the function field
F
of
X
. Bruhat-Tits group schemes with generic fibre
G
can be constructed as follows.

Construction 4.3.2
.

(1)

There exists a reductive model
G\to U
of
G
over some dense open subset
U\subseteq X
. For each of the finitely many points
x\in X\setminus U
in the complement of
U
we choose a parahoric group scheme
\mathcal{G}^{(x)}\to\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{x})
with generic fibre
\mathcal{G}^{(x)}_{F_{x}}=G_{F_{x}}
.
As
U\amalg\coprod_{x\in X\setminus U}\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{x})\to X
is an fpqc-cover, we can glue
G\to U
with all
\mathcal{G}^{(x)}
using fpqc-descent to obtain a smooth affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}\to X
, which is a parahoric group scheme by construction.

(2)

Let us now fix a parahoric model
\mathcal{G}\to X
and a closed point
x_{0}
of
X
.
For a connected bounded subset
\Omega
in an apartment of the Bruhat-Tits building of
G_{F_{x_{0}}}
as in the previous paragraph, we denote by
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}\to\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{x_{0}})
the corresponding (local) Bruhat-Tits group scheme.
We glue
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
with
\mathcal{G}
along the identity over
F_{x_{0}}
and denote the resulting smooth affine group scheme over
X
by a slight abuse of notation again by
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
.
Then
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
is a Bruhat-Tits group scheme in the sense of the previous definition and parahoric if and only if
\Omega
is contained in the closure of a facet.

The local homomorphisms
\rho_{\Omega^{\prime},\Omega}\colon\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}\to\mathcal{G}_{\Omega^{\prime}}
over
\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{x_{0}})
for
\Omega^{\prime}\prec\Omega
glue with the identity away from
x_{0}
to morphisms of group schemes
\rho_{\Omega^{\prime},\Omega}\colon\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}\to\mathcal{G}_{\Omega^{\prime}}
on
X
.

In particular, the isomorphism of Theorem
4.2.1
extends to an isomorphism

\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}\xrightarrow{\cong}\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}

of the corresponding global Bruhat-Tits group schemes.

For any smooth affine group scheme
\mathcal{H}
on
X
, we denote by
\Bun_{\mathcal{H}}
the moduli stack of
\mathcal{H}
-bundles on
X
.
By the functoriality of
\Bun
, the maps
\rho_{\mathfrak{f},\Omega}
induce maps
\rho_{\mathfrak{f},\Omega,\ast}\colon\Bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\Bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}
for all facets
\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega
.

Theorem 4.3.3
.

Let
G
be a reductive group over
F
, let
x_{0}
be a closed point of
X
and let
\Omega=\mathrm{cl}(\Omega)
be a bounded subset of an apartment in the Bruhat-Tits building
\mathcal{B}(G_{F_{x_{0}}},F_{x_{0}})
. Let
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}\to X
be the corresponding Bruhat-Tits group scheme from Construction
4.3.2
(
2
).
The map

\rho_{\Omega,\ast}:=\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\rho_{\mathfrak{f},\Omega,\ast}\colon\Bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}

induced by the
\rho_{\mathfrak{f},\Omega,\ast}
for facets
\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega
is schematic and a quasi-compact open immersion.

Proof.

By
[
Bre18
, Proposition 3.19]
, the maps
\rho_{\mathfrak{f},\Omega,\ast}
are schematic and quasi-projective for all facets
\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega
. By Lemma
5.1.2
below, the map
\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
is schematic, separated and of finite type.
By Lemma
4.1.1
, the map
\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
is a monomorphism.
We show that
\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
is formally étale.
As all
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}
are locally of finite type over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
by
[
Hei10
, Proposition 1]
, it suffices to check the infinitesimal lifting criterion for local Artin rings over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
by
[
EGA4
, Proposition 17.14.2]
.
Let
R
be a local Artin
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
-algebra and let
I\subseteq R
be an ideal of square zero. Let moreover
(\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{f}})_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\in\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}(R)
such that
\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{f}}
is a
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
-torsor over
X_{\overline{R}}
, where
\overline{R}=R/I
.
We claim that
\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{f}}
is already a
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
-torsor over
X_{R}
.
The map
\widehat{(X_{R})_{x_{0}}}\cup(X\setminus\{x_{0}\})_{R}\to X_{R}
is a fpqc-cover, where
\widehat{(X_{R})_{x_{0}}}=\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{x_{0}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}R)
, with
\mathcal{O}_{x_{0}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}V
being the underlying
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
-algebra of the completion of

along

.
As all maps
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}\to\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}
for
\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega

\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime},R}\times^{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}\to\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{f},R}

.
Using Proposition

, it remains to check that the pullback to

is surjective, but the underlying topological spaces of
\widehat{(X_{R})_{x_{0}}}

Hence,
\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
is formally étale and thus a quasi-compact open immersion being a flat monomorphism of finite presentation.
∎

5.
Level maps and integral models with deep Bruhat-Tits level

We construct integral models for moduli spaces of shtukas with deep Bruhat-Tits level structures and show that these integral models admit proper, surjective and generically étale level maps.

5.1.
Some lemmata on algebraic stacks

We collect some results on finite connected limits of algebraic stacks we use below for which we could not find a reference in the literature.

In this section,
I
will always denote a finite connected index category and
(\mathcal{X}_{i})_{i\in I}
denotes a diagram over
I
of (fppf-) Artin stacks over some base scheme
S
.

Lemma 5.1.1
.

Assume that all algebraic stacks
\mathcal{X}_{i}
have a diagonal that is schematic.
Let all transition maps in
(\mathcal{X}_{i})_{i\in I}
be schematic. Then the projections
\varprojlim_{i\in I}\mathcal{X}_{i}\to\mathcal{X}_{j}
are schematic for all
j\in I
.

Moreover, assume that all
\mathcal{X}_{i}
are separated over
S
and that all transition maps have a property
\mathbf{P}
of morphisms of schemes that is stable under base change and composition and is smooth local on the target such that all proper maps have
\mathbf{P}
. Then the projections
\varprojlim_{i\in I}\mathcal{X}_{i}\to\mathcal{X}_{j}
have property
\mathbf{P}
for all
j\in I
.

Proof.

It suffices to show the claim for fibre products and equalisers. For fibre products this is clear.
Let us thus consider the equaliser diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}_{2}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

The equaliser of this diagram is given by the fibre product
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{2}\times_{\Delta,\mathcal{X}_{2}\times_{S}\mathcal{X}_{2},(f,g)}\mathcal{X}_{1}
.
Thus, the projection
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{1}
arises as the base change of the diagonal of
\mathcal{X}_{1}
and is thus schematic in the first case and moreover proper in the second case (as we assumed
\mathcal{X}_{1}
to be separated).
The projection
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{2}
has the required properties as it is the composition
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{1}\to\mathcal{X}_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 5.1.2
.

Let
(f_{i}\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{i})_{i\in I}
be a cone over the diagram
(\mathcal{X}_{i})_{i\in I}
such that all maps
f_{i}
are schematic.
Then the limit
f\colon\mathcal{X}\to\varprojlim_{i\in I}\mathcal{X}_{i}
is schematic as well.

Assume moreover that all
f_{i}
are separated and have a property
\mathbf{P}
of morphisms of schemes that is stable under base change and composition and is smooth local on the target such that all closed immersions have
\mathbf{P}
. Then

has
\mathbf{P}
.

Proof.

Let
T
be an
S
-scheme. Let us fix a map
T\to\varprojlim_{i\in I}\mathcal{X}_{i}
. As different limits commute, we get that

T\times_{\varprojlim_{i\in I}\mathcal{X}_{i}}\mathcal{X}=\varprojlim_{i\in I}(T\times_{\mathcal{X}_{i}}\mathcal{X}),

which is representable by a scheme by assumption.
For the second part, let us denote by
T_{i}=T\times_{\mathcal{X}_{i}}\mathcal{X}
.
Then
T_{i}
is a separated
T
-scheme by assumption. As
I
is connected, we may take the limit on the right hand side in the category of
T
-schemes (as opposed to the category of
S
-schemes).
We represent the limit as an equaliser between products

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left.\prod_{i\in I}T_{i}\right),}

where the products are taken in the category of
T
-schemes.
As all

are separated over

, the inclusion of
\varprojlim_{i\in I}T_{i}\hookrightarrow\prod_{i\in I}T_{i}
is a closed immersion. Moreover, as all
T_{i}\to T
have property
\mathbf{P}
, so does their product. Hence,
\varprojlim_{i\in I}T_{i}\to T
has property
\mathbf{P}
.
∎

Lemma 5.1.3
.

Let

be a schematic map of algebraic stacks and let

and

5.2.
Integral models with deeper Bruhat-Tits level structure

In this section, we define the integral model of the moduli space of shtukas with deep Bruhat-Tits level structure as the schematic image of the moduli space of shtukas for the Bruhat-Tits group scheme inside the limit of all the corresponding spaces for parahoric level and show that these integral models admit proper surjective and generically étale level maps as in the parahoric case.

In this section we work in the following setup that we record for future reference.

Assumption 5.2.1
.

Let
x_{0}
be a fixed closed point of

. Let

be a reductive group over

\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}\to X

Theorem 5.2.3

In the situation of Assumption

is an open and closed immersion over

.
When

\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The assertion in the case that
\Omega
is a facet is clear because the index set
\{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega\}
has the final object
\Omega
.
By Corollary
3.5.7
and Lemma
5.1.2
, the map is schematic, separated and of finite type.
By Theorem
4.3.3
, the corresponding map on the unbounded moduli stacks of shtukas is representable by an open immersion.
Hence,
\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
is a locally closed immersion as being bounded by
\mathcal{Z}
is a closed condition.

Over
(X\setminus x_{0})^{I}
, an element of
\Sht_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}
is bounded by
\mathcal{Z}
if and only if its image under
\rho_{\mathfrak{f},\Omega,\ast}
for one (or equivalently all) facet
\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega
is bounded by
\mathcal{Z}
by the second part of Lemma
5.2.2
.
Thus,
\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
is an open immersion over
(X\setminus x_{0})^{I}
.
Moreover, the map
\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
is finite away from
x_{0}
by Lemma
5.1.2
, hence also a closed immersion.
∎

Definition 5.2.4
.

The integral model
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}=\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{I_{\bullet},\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},X}
of the moduli space of shtukas with
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
-level
is defined to be the schematic image in the sense of
[
EG21
]
of the map

\rho_{\Omega,\ast}\colon\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}.

By Theorem
5.2.3
, we have
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}=\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}
in the parahoric case. Moreover, the inclusion
\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}
is an isomorphism away from
x_{0}
by Theorem
5.2.3
together with the fact that the schematic closure commutes with flat base change.

Remark 5.2.5
.

As the map
\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
is schematic and the schematic image commutes with flat base change and is fpqc-local on the target (compare
[
EG21
, Remark 3.1.2]
), the schematic image of
\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
is given by descending the schematic image of the map of schemes
\rho_{\Omega,\ast,S}\colon S\times_{\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}}\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to S
for any smooth atlas
S\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}
.

By construction, we have level maps
\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{f},\Omega}\colon\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}
for all facets
\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega
.
In particular, for
\Omega^{\prime}\prec\Omega
we obtain a map
\overline{\rho}_{\Omega^{\prime},\Omega}\colon\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega^{\prime}}\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}
that factors through
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega^{\prime}}}
by construction.

Theorem 5.2.6
.

Let
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
be two bounded connected subsets of an apartment in the Bruhat-Tits building of
G_{K_{x_{0}}}
such that
\Omega^{\prime}\prec\Omega
. Then, the level map

\overline{\rho}_{\Omega^{\prime},\Omega}\colon\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega^{\prime}}}

is schematic, proper, surjective and finite étale away from
x_{0}
.

Proof.

As a first step, we show that
\overline{\rho}_{\Omega^{\prime},\Omega}
is schematic. By Lemmas
5.1.1
and
5.1.2
, the map

\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\prec\Omega^{\prime}}\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}}

is schematic. The claim for
\overline{\rho}_{\Omega^{\prime},\Omega}
then follows from Lemma
5.1.3
.

That the map is finite étale away from
x_{0}
follows from the fact that the map
\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}
is an isomorphism away from
x_{0}
by the observation above together with Corollary
3.5.7
.

Moreover, the map
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega^{\prime}}}\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega^{\prime}}\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}
is a closed immersion by definition. Thus, by Lemma
5.1.2
, it suffices to consider the level maps

\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}

for facets
\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega
to show the properness. Similarly, by construction of
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}
, it suffices to show the claim for the projections

\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}\to\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}.

But for the projections the claim follows from Lemma
5.1.1
.
The surjectivity follows as in the parahoric case in the proof of Theorem
3.5.5
.
∎

Remark 5.2.7
.

In a similar fashion we define an integral model of the moduli space of bounded
\mathcal{G}
-shtukas for
arbitrary
Bruhat-Tits group schemes

.

5.3.
The case that
\Omega
is not contained in a single apartment

In principle, our techniques should also extend to the more general situation where the set
\Omega
is not necessarily contained in a single apartment.
Generalising our results might yield further interesting applications.
For example, when
\Omega
is the ball of radius
r
around a point
x
in the building, its (connected, pointwise) stabiliser
G(\breve{F}_{x_{0}})^{0}_{\Omega}
should be closely related (up to the centre) to the Moy-Prasad subgroup
G(\breve{F}_{x_{0}})_{x,r}
.
However, in this situation analogues of the results from Bruhat-Tits theory that go into the proofs in Section
4
(in particular of Lemma
4.2.4
) do not seem to be available.
We present the extent to which we can generalise our main results.

In the situation of Assumption
5.2.1
, we drop the assumption that
\Omega
is contained in a single apartment. In this section, let
\Omega\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(G_{F_{x_{0}}},F_{x_{0}})
be a non-empty bounded subset of the Bruhat-Tits building of
G_{F_{x_{0}}}
.
Recall that the smoothening of the closure of

G\xrightarrow{\Delta}\prod_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}},

where
\Delta
denotes the diagonal embedding in the generic fibre, is by construction a smooth affine
\mathcal{O}_{x_{0}}
-group scheme
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
with generic fibre
G
and such that
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}(\breve{\mathcal{O}}_{x_{0}})=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}G(\breve{F}_{x_{0}})_{\mathfrak{f}}^{0}
.
As in the case where
\Omega
is contained in an apartment, we denote by
\mathrm{cl}(\Omega)
the maximal subset of
\mathfrak{B}(G,F_{x_{0}})
that is pointwise stabilised by
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}(\breve{\mathcal{O}}_{x_{0}})
. Then
\mathrm{cl}(\Omega)
is convex and closed.

We consider the following conditions.

Condition 5.3.1
.

(1)

The group scheme
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
is a smooth affine group scheme with geometrically connected fibres. Moreover, the natural map
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}\xrightarrow{\cong}\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}

is an isomorphism.

(2)

The map

\rho_{\Omega,\ast}\colon\Bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}

is schematic and representable by a quasi-compact immersion.

Recall that when
\Omega
is contained in a single apartment, the group schemes
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
satisfy the two conditions by Theorems
4.2.1
and
4.3.3
. We do not know if the properties remain satisfied in general.

Proposition 5.3.2
.

Let
\Omega=\mathrm{cl}(\Omega)
be a bounded subset of the Bruhat-Tits building (not necessarily contained in a single apartment).

(1)

The map

\rho_{\Omega,\ast}\colon\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{f}\prec\Omega}\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}}}

is schematic, separated and of finite type, and a monomorphism.
Moreover, it is an open and closed immersion over
(X\setminus\{x_{0}\})^{I}
.

(2)

Assume that Condition
5.3.1
holds for
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
. Then
\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
is a quasi-compact immersion and an isomorphism over
(X\setminus\{x_{0}\})^{I}
.

Proof.

We proceed as in the proof of Theorem
5.2.3
.
Assertion (
1
) follows from Lemma
4.1.1
, Corollary
3.5.7
, and Lemma
5.1.2
. Moreover, (
2
) follows from Condition
5.3.1
.
∎

Remark 5.3.3
.

In the situation of Proposition
5.3.2
, one could define an integral model
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}
again as the schematic image of the map
\rho_{\Omega,\ast}
. The argument in the proof of Theorem
5.2.6
clearly generalises to this case. Hence, the models
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}
defined in this way again admit proper, surjective and generically étale level maps.
Assuming Condition
5.3.1
,
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}
is again a relative compactification of
\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}}
as before by Proposition
5.3.2
(
2
).

5.4.
Newton stratification

We recall the Newton stratification on stacks of global shtukas and define a Newton stratification on our integral models with deep level.
We show that the expected closure relations of Newton strata are satisfied in the hyperspecial case.

Let
K\cong k\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
be a local field in characteristic
p
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}\cong k\left\llbracket t\right\rrbracket
and finite residue field
k
. We denote by
\bar{K}=K^{\mathrm{sep}}
a fixed separable closure and by
\breve{K}\cong k^{\mathrm{alg}}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
K
.
Let
G/K
be a reductive group and
let us fix
T\subseteq G
be a maximal torus defined over
K
.
Recall that
G_{\breve{K}}=G\times_{K}\breve{K}
is quasi-split by a theorem of Steinberg and Borel-Springer, compare
[
Ste65
, Theorem 1.9]

[
BS68
, Section 8]
. We choose a Borel
B\subseteq G_{\breve{K}}
containing
T_{\breve{K}}
.
We denote by
X_{\ast}(T)
its group of geometric cocharacters and by
\pi_{1}(G)
the algebraic fundamental group of
G
given by the quotient of the cocharacter lattice by the coroot lattice.

We denote by
B(G)
the set of
\sigma
-conjugacy classes in
G(\breve{K})=LG(k^{\mathrm{alg}})
.
By
[
Kot85
,
Kot97
,
RR96
]
, the elements of
B(G)
are classified by two invariants: the
Kottwitz map
denoted by

\kappa\colon B(G)\to\pi_{1}(G)_{\Gal({\bar{K}}/K)}

and the
Newton map
denoted by

\nu\colon B(G)\to(\Hom(\mathbb{D}_{\bar{K}},G_{\bar{K}})/G({\bar{K}}))^{\Gal({\bar{K}}/K)},

where
\mathbb{D}
denotes the pro-torus with character group
\mathbb{Q}
and
G({\bar{K}})
acts by conjugation. Note that we can identify

(\Hom(\mathbb{D}_{\bar{K}},G_{\bar{K}})/G({\bar{K}}))^{\Gal({\bar{K}}/K)}=X_{\ast}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{+,\Gal({\bar{K}}/K)}=X_{\ast}(T)_{\mathbb{Q},\Gal({\bar{K}}/K)}^{+}

with the set of rational dominant (with respect to the choice of
B
) Galois-invariant cocharacters, and that
\kappa(b)=\nu(b)
in
\pi_{1}(G)_{\mathbb{Q},{\Gal({\bar{K}}/K)}}
.

The choice of Borel determines a set of simple positive roots and consequently defines the dominance order on
X_{\ast}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}
by
\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}
if
\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of positive simple roots with non-negative coefficients.
Via
\kappa
and
\nu
the dominance order induces a partial order on
B(G)
by

b_{1}\leq b_{2}
if and only if
\kappa(b_{1})=\kappa(b_{2})
and
\nu(b_{1})\leq\nu(b_{2})
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\to\Spec(\mathcal{O})
be a smooth affine group scheme such that
\mathcal{G}_{K}=G
.
Note that for an algebraically closed extension
\ell
of
k
the set of
\sigma
-conjugacy classes in
LG(\ell)
does not depend on the choice of
\ell
by
[
RR96
, Lemma 1.3]
.
It classifies quasi-isogeny classes of local
\mathcal{G}
-shtukas by associating to
(L^{+}\mathcal{G},b^{-1})
the class
[b]\in B(G)
.
For a local
\mathcal{G}
-shtuka
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
over
S=\Spec(R)
and a point
s\in S
we denote by
[\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{s}]\in B(G)
the corresponding element. This does not depend on the choice of an algebraic closure of the residue field at
s
.

Let us shift perspective back to the global setting again and consider a smooth affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}\to X
with generic fibre
\mathcal{G}_{F}=G
a reductive group. Let us moreover fix a tuple
\underline{y}=(y_{i})_{i\in I}
of pairwise distinct closed points of
X
.
Let us fix a (global) bound
\mathcal{Z}
and points
\underline{y}^{\prime}=(y^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in I}\in X_{\mathcal{Z}}^{I}
lying over
\underline{y}
.
We denote by
\Sht^{I_{\bullet},\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G},X,\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}=\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}=\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G},\underline{y}^{\prime}}\times_{X_{\mathcal{Z}}^{I}}\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}})
the special fibre of the moduli space of shtukas at
\underline{y}^{\prime}
.

Definition 5.4.1

(
[
Bre18
, Definition 4.12]
)
.

Let
\ell
be an algebraically closed extension of
\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}
. The global-to-local functor induces maps

\delta_{\mathcal{G},y_{i},\ell}\colon\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}(\ell)\to B(G_{y_{i}}),\qquad{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}\mapsto[\widehat{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{y_{i}}}]

for all
i\in I
and

\delta_{\mathcal{G},\underline{y},\ell}=\prod_{i\in I}\delta_{\mathcal{G},y_{i},\ell}\colon\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}(\ell)\to\prod_{i\in I}B(G_{y_{i}}).

Let
\underline{b}=(b_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}B(G_{y_{i}})
. The locus in
\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}
where
\delta_{\mathcal{G},\underline{y}}
maps to
\underline{b}
is locally closed by
[
HV11
, Theorem 7.11]
, compare also
[
RR96
]
.
The reduced substack on this locally closed subset is denoted by
\Sht^{\mathcal{Z},\underline{b}}_{\mathcal{G},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}
and called the
Newton stratum
associated to
\underline{b}
.

The Newton map is compatible with changing the group scheme in the following sense.

Lemma 5.4.2

(compare
[
Bre18
, Section 5.2]
)
.

Let
G/F
be a reductive group and let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
be two smooth affine models of
G
over
X
.
Let
f\colon(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})\to(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},\mathcal{Z}^{\prime})
be a map of shtuka data such that
f\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is given by the identity on
G
in the generic fibre. Assume that
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
admits a representative over its reflex scheme.
The induced map

f_{\ast}\colon\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}\times_{\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}}}\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}.\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}\to\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}\times_{\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}}\widetilde{X}^{I}_{\mathcal{Z}.\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}

satisfies

\delta_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime},\underline{y}}\circ f_{\ast}=\delta_{\mathcal{G},\underline{y}}.

Proof.

The proof of
[
Bre18
, Section 5.2]
carries over to this situation.
∎

Using the compatibility of the Newton map with the change in level structures, we define a Newton stratification on our integral models for deeper level structures.
Let us now consider the situation of Assumption
5.2.1
. In particular, let
\Omega=\mathrm{cl}(\Omega)
be a bounded subset of an apartment of the Bruhat-Tits building of
G_{F_{x_{0}}}
for a fixed closed point
x_{0}
of
X
. Let
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}
be the corresponding Bruhat-Tits group scheme.
Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a generically defined bound for
G
. Let moreover
\underline{y}^{\prime}=(y^{\prime}_{i})
be a tuple of closed points of
X_{\mathcal{Z}}
lying over
\underline{y}
.
In order to define a Newton stratification on
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}
, we note that by construction and by the previous lemma the map

\delta_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}},\underline{y}}\circ\rho_{\mathfrak{f},\Omega}\colon\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}\to\Sht^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{f}},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}\to\prod_{i\in I}B(G_{y_{i}})

does not depend on the choice of the facet

. Hence, we obtain a well-defined map

\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},\underline{y}}\colon\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}\to\prod_{i\in I}B(G_{y_{i}}).

Let
\underline{b}=(b_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}B(G_{y_{i}})
. The locus in
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}
where
\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},\underline{y}}
maps to
\underline{b}
is again locally closed by the result in the parahoric case together with Lemma
5.4.2
.

Definition 5.4.3
.

Let
\underline{b}=(b_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}B(G_{y_{i}})
. The
Newton stratum
in
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}
associated to
\underline{b}
is the reduced locally closed substack on the set of points where
\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{G},\underline{y}}
maps to
\underline{b}
.
It is denoted by
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z},\underline{b}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}
.

We have the obvious analogue of Lemma
5.4.2
for deep level, in other words, the Newton stratification for deep levels is still compatible with the level maps.

Corollary 5.4.4
.

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\prec\Omega
be two connected bounded subsets of the Bruhat-Tits building. Then

\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega^{\prime}},\underline{y}}\circ\bar{\rho}_{\Omega^{\prime},\Omega}=\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},\underline{y}}.

In particular,
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z},\underline{b}^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}\cap\overline{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ht}^{\mathcal{Z},\underline{b}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Omega},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}}\neq\emptyset
only if
\underline{b}^{\prime}\leq\underline{b}
.

Proof.

This follows from the construction and Lemma
5.4.2
. The second statement then follows directly from the parahoric case in
[
Bre18
, Proposition 4.11, Section 5]
, compare also
[
HV11
, Theorem 7.11]
.
∎

We conclude by showing the strong stratification property of the Newton stratification in the hyperspecial case.

Theorem 5.4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\to X
be a parahoric group scheme that is hyperspecial at
y_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Let
\underline{\mu}=(\mu_{i})_{i\in I}
be an
I
-tuple of conjugacy classes of geometric cocharacters of
G
. Then the Newton stratification at
\underline{y}^{\prime}
satisfies the strong stratification property in the sense that

\overline{\Sht^{\leq\underline{\mu},\underline{b}}_{\mathcal{G},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}}=\bigcup_{\underline{b}^{\prime}\leq\underline{b}}\Sht^{\leq\underline{\mu},\underline{b^{\prime}}}_{\mathcal{G},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}

for all
\underline{b}\in\prod_{i\in I}B(G_{y_{i}})
.

Proof.

Let
\underline{b},\underline{b}^{\prime}\in\prod_{i\in I}B(G_{y_{i}})
with
\underline{b}^{\prime}\leq\underline{b}
. It suffices to show that every closed point
\bar{s}=\underline{\mathcal{E}}\in\Sht^{\leq\underline{\mu},\underline{b^{\prime}}}_{\mathcal{G},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}})
lies in the closure of
\Sht^{\leq\underline{\mu},\underline{b}}_{\mathcal{G},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}
. Let
R
be the
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}
-algebra pro-representing the deformation functor of
\bar{s}
.
Then
\bar{s}
lies in the closure of
\overline{\Sht^{\leq\underline{\mu},\underline{b}}_{\mathcal{G},\mathbb{F}_{\underline{y}^{\prime}}}}
if and only if the same is true in the Newton stratification on
\Spec R
.
By the bounded Serre-Tate Theorem (Proposition
3.3.5
) the universal deformation ring factors as
\Spec R=\prod_{i\in I}\Spec R_{i}
, where
R_{i}
is the universal deformation ring of the corresponding bounded local shtuka at
y_{i}
. Under this isomorphism we have
\Spec(R)_{\underline{b}}=\prod_{i\in I}\Spec(R_{i})_{b_{i}}
, where we denote by

Disjointness graphs of segments in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} are almost all hamiltonian
J. Leaños 1 and Christophe Ndjatchi 2 and L. M. Ríos-Castro 3

Abstract
. Let P P be a set of n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 points in general position in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} . The edge disjointness graph D ⁡ ( P ) D(P) of P P is the graph whose vertices are all the closed straight line segments with endpoints in P P , two of which are adjacent in D ⁡ ( P ) D(P) if and only if they are disjoint. In this note, we give a full characterization of all those edge disjointness graphs that are hamiltonian. More precisely, we shall show that (up to order type isomorphism) there are exactly 8 instances of P P for which D ⁡ ( P ) D(P) is not hamiltonian. Additionally, from one of these 8 instances, we derive a counterexample to a criterion for the existence of hamiltonian cycles due to A. D. Plotnikov in 1998.

Introduction

Let
P
be a set of
n\geq 2
points in general position in the plane, i.e., no three points in

are collinear.
A

of

is a closed straight line segment with its two endpoints being elements of

. In this note, we shall use
{\mathcal{P}}

to denote the set of all
\binom{n}{2}
segments of

. The

of

is the graph whose vertex set is

, and two segments of
{\mathcal{P}}
are adjacent in

if and only if
they are disjoint.

If

and

are distinct points of
P
, we shall use
xy
to denote the closed straight line segment whose endpoints are
x
and
y
.
We often make no distinction between an element of
{\mathcal{P}}
and its corresponding vertex in
D(P)
. As usual, we will denote by

the boundary of the
convex hull of
P
, and by
\overline{P}
to

. Then, if

is in convex position, we have

. See Figure
1
.

The edge disjointness graphs were introduced in 2005 by Araujo, Dumitrescu, Hurtado, Noy, and Urrutia
[
6
]

with

k
–subsets of

and in which two
k
-subsets form an edge if and only if they are disjoint. In
[
17
]
Kneser conjectured that the chromatic number
\chi(KG(m;k))
of

is equal to
m-2k+2
. This conjecture was proved by Lovász
[
20
]
in 1978 and, as the reader can check in
[
25
,
9
,
21
]
and the references therein,
the research on Kneser graphs is still of interest.

The chromatic number
\chi(D(P))
of

has been studied in
[
6
,
13
,
16
,
14
]
. As far as we know, the determination of the exact value of

remains open in general. In
[

]
a general lower bound for

was established. On the other hand, the exact value of

is known only for two families of point sets: when

is in convex position
[

]
, and when

is a double chain
[

In
[

Pach, Tardos, and Tóth studied the chromatic number and the clique number of

in the more general setting of

for

it was shown that the chromatic number of

We remark that the grounded

play the role of the straight line segments in

The exact determination of the connectivity number

Let

be a simple connected graph. We recall that a

•

•

•

The graph

To determine whether or not a given connected graph

[
12

for a good recent survey on this problem.
In particular, it is well-known that such a problem is NP-complete
[
11
]
. In view of this fact, many standard approaches have been developed for the study of
hamiltonicity of graphs. For instance, the determination of necessary and/or sufficient conditions under which a graph contains a spanning cycle is one of them.
In this note, we follow another common approach, which can be written as follows:
given a nontrivial family of graphs
{\mathcal{G}}
, provide a characterization of
all those elements of
{\mathcal{G}}
that are hamiltonian.
In this work, our interest lies in the following family.

{\mathcal{G}}=\{D(P)~:~P\mbox{ is a finite point set in general position of }{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\mbox{ with }|P|\geq 2\}.

Let

1&x_{2}&y_{2}\\

Let

and

if and only if there is a bijection

\gamma:P\to Q
such that each ordered triple

We write

Note that if

then (see for instance

are disjoint if and only if their corresponding segments

are disjoint, and so

and

are isomorphic. In view of this, we need only consider a representative

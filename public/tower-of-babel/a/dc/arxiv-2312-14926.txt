Topological Green’s function zeros in an exactly solved model and beyond
Steffen Bollmann, Chandan Setty, Urban F. P. Seifert, Elio J. König

Abstract
The interplay of topological electronic band structures and strong interparticle interactions provides a promising path towards the constructive design of robust, long-range entangled many-body systems. As a prototype for such systems, we here study an exactly integrable, local model for a fractionalized topological insulator. Using a controlled perturbation theory about this limit, we demonstrate the existence of topological bands of zeros in the exact fermionic Green’s function and show that in this model they do affect the topological invariant of the system, but not the quantized transport response. Close to (but prior to) the Higgs transition signaling the breakdown of fractionalization, the topological bands of zeros acquire a finite “lifetime”. We also discuss the appearance of edge states and edge zeros at real space domain walls separating different phases of the system. This model provides a fertile ground for controlled studies of the phenomenology of Green’s function zeros and the underlying exactly solvable lattice gauge theory illustrates the synergetic cross-pollination between solid-state theory, high-energy physics and quantum information science.

Discussion and Outlook.
In summary, we have studied an exactly soluble, local model of a fractionalized topological insulator
Zhong et al. 2013
;
Stern 2016
. Analytically controlled perturbation about the integrable limit reveals topological bands of GF zeros, which are sharp deep in the deconfining phase, but acquire a “lifetime” near the Higgs condensation.

As an outlook devoted to the experimental detection of (topological) GF zeros, we discuss momentum conserving tunneling which has previously served as a crucial tool in determining many-body Fermi and Luttinger liquid physics in 2D (“planar tunneling”
Murphy et al. 1995
;
Britnell et al. 2012
) and 1D
Barak et al. 2010
, respectively. In a device with momentum conservation such as Fig.
3
d), the decay rate
\Gamma(\epsilon^{\rm lead}_{\mathbf{p}},\mathbf{p})
of electrons in the upper lead determines the current across the junction. We use the notation
\epsilon^{\rm lead}_{\mathbf{p}},G^{R}_{\rm lead}
for dispersion and GF of lead electrons, respectively, consider identical top and bottom leads and, for simplicity, scalar Green’s functions. As we argue in the following
Sup
, the planar tunneling rate and, by consequence, the tunneling conductance
dI/dV
display characteristic dips in the presence of GF zeros.

To lowest order
\Gamma(\epsilon^{\rm lead}_{\mathbf{p}},\mathbf{p})=-t^{2}\text{Im}\mathbf{G}^{R}(\epsilon_{\mathbf{p}}^{\rm lead},\mathbf{p})
which vanishes for energies below the spectral gap of the central layer of the device. In this case, the leading elastic contribution is
\Gamma(\epsilon_{\mathbf{p}},\mathbf{p})=-t^{4}[\text{Re}\mathbf{G}^{R}(\epsilon_{\mathbf{p}}^{\rm lead},\mathbf{p})]^{2}\text{Im}G^{R}_{\rm lead}(\epsilon^{\rm lead}_{\mathbf{p}},\mathbf{p})
, which however also vanishes when the lead electron energy and momentum
(\epsilon^{\rm lead}_{\mathbf{p}},\mathbf{p})
coincide with energy and momentum of zeros in the central layer.
For 2D planar tunneling, these kinematic constraints might admittedly be challenging to fine-tune experimentally. However, 1D momentum conserving tunneling in a sandwich (chiral edge state – ZTI edge zero – chiral edge state) is practical, since it can be expected that TI edge state and ZTI edge zero dispersions cross each other at a given energy
E_{*}
. While momentum-conserving co-tunneling in the absence of edge zeros implies a finite differential conductance at
\mathcal{O}(t^{4})
, the same experiment shows a suppressed signal at the characteristic voltage
E_{*}/e
in the presence of topological edge zeros.

S1.1
The local lattice limit

We consider the thermodynamic limit and treat the lattice momentum

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\textbf{k}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\text{Vol B.Z.}}\int_{\text{B.Z.}}\text{d}\textbf{k}

(S1)

\textbf{n}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\sqrt{3}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\textbf{n}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-1\\
\sqrt{3}\end{pmatrix}

(S2)

\text{Vol B.Z.}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}(2\pi)^{2}

\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\int(\text{d}k)
where we use the shorthand notation
(\text{d}k)=\frac{\text{d}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}

In the main text, we use the notation

is a Bravais lattice vector and

c_{\textbf{r},A/B}=\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}\int(\text{d}k)\text{e}^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{r}}c_{A/B}(\textbf{k}).

(S3)

from which directly follows that

G_{s,s^{\prime}}(\tau,\textbf{r})=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\int(\text{d}k)\text{e}^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{r}}G_{s,s^{\prime}}(\tau,\textbf{k}),

(S4)

where
G_{s,s^{\prime}}(\tau,\textbf{r})=-\langle\mathcal{T}\left[c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\textbf{r},s}(\tau)c^{\dagger}_{0,s^{\prime}}(\tau)\right]\rangle
is the imaginary time ordered GF and
s,s^{\prime}\in\{A,B\}

S1.3
Ground state in the exactly solvable limit

As shown in

To explicitly construct the Fermi sea, we need to switch to Fourier space. Starting from the gauge
Z_{\mathbf{b}}=1

\displaystyle H_{\rm f}=\int_{\rm BZ}(\text{d}k)\left(c^{\dagger}_{A}(\mathbf{k}),c_{B}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k})\right)h(\mathbf{k})\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{A}(\mathbf{k})\\

. The single-particle Hamiltonian has the form

h(\mathbf{k})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}w_{2}m(\mathbf{k})+w_{0}&-w_{1}s(\mathbf{k})\\

(S6c)

with

\displaystyle s(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=[e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{1}}+e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{2}}+1]

(S6d)

\displaystyle m(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\left[\sin(\v k \cdot\v n_{1-2})-\sin(\v k \cdot\v n_{1})+\sin(\v k \cdot\v n_{2})\right]

(S6e)

We will also need the energy

\epsilon(\mathbf{k})=\sqrt{w_{1}^{2}|s(\mathbf{k})|^{2}+(w_{2}m(\mathbf{k})+w_{0})^{2}}.

(S7)

S2
Fermionic Green’s Function in the presence of topological order

According to the discussion in the main text and a comprehensive discussion in Ref.
König et al. 2020
, the full GF for fermionic excitations in the OTI and ZTI is given as the component-wise product of the fermionic GF
G_{s,s^{\prime}}(\tau,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
and the
e
-particle GF
D_{s,s^{\prime}}(\tau,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
, that is

\displaystyle\textbf{G}_{s,s^{\prime}}(\tau,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=G_{s,s^{\prime}}(\tau,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})D_{s,s^{\prime}}(\tau,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

(S8)

which translates to the convolution

\textbf{G}_{s,s^{\prime}}(i\epsilon,\textbf{k})=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\int(\text{d}Q)D_{s,s^{\prime}}(i\omega,q)G_{s,s^{\prime}}(i(\epsilon-\omega),\textbf{k}-\textbf{q})

(S9)

in momentum/Matsubara frequency space, and
(dQ)=d\omega d^{2}q/(2\pi)^{3}
.

S2.1
Propagator of orthogonal fermions

As explained in the main text, the GF of the orthogonal fermions in momentum space is given as

G(i\epsilon,\mathbf{k})=[i\epsilon-h(\mathbf{k})]^{-1}=-\frac{i\epsilon+h(\mathbf{k})}{\epsilon^{2}+\epsilon(\mathbf{k})^{2}}.

(S10)

S2.2
Propagator of
e
-particles

In the limit of vanishing
J
, the
e
-particle can not move over the lattice, and the GF becomes ultra-local and takes the simple shape

D^{(0)}_{s,s^{\prime}}(\tau,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=\text{e}^{-2h|\tau|}\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\delta_{\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}

(S11)

or

D^{(0)}_{s,s^{\prime}}(i\omega,\mathbf{q})=\frac{4h}{\omega^{2}+4h^{2}}.

(S12)

If non-Gaussian fluctuations of the gauge field are subleading (an estimate is given in Sec.
S2.4
, below), the
e
-particle propagator can be approximated by the recursion formula

\displaystyle D_{s_{f},s_{i}}\left(\mathbf{r}_{f},i\omega\right)

\displaystyle=D^{(0)}(i\omega)\delta_{s_{f},s_{i}}\delta_{\mathbf{r}_{f},\mathbf{r}_{i}}

\displaystyle+J\sum_{\langle(\mathbf{r},s),(\mathbf{r}_{f},s_{F})\rangle}D_{s,s_{i}}(\mathbf{r},i\omega)D^{(0)}(i\omega),

(S13)

where the brackets
\langle...\rangle
stand for nearest neighbors.
It incorporates all possible paths the particle can take from its initial position
(r_{i},s_{i})
to the final position
(r_{f},s_{f})
that do not intersect themselves. A more detailed discussion can be found in Ref.
König et al. 2020
;
Roy and König 2023
.

In momentum space, the recursion equation (
S13
) takes the shape

D(i\omega,\mathbf{q})=D^{(0)}\left[\bm{1}+J\begin{pmatrix}0&s(\mathbf{q})\\
s^{*}(\mathbf{q})&0\end{pmatrix}D(i\omega,\mathbf{q})\right]

(S14)

and we obtain a formal solution

\begin{split}\,&D(i\omega,\mathbf{q})=4h\left[\omega^{2}+4h^{2}-4hJ\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&s(\mathbf{q})\\
s^{*}(\mathbf{q})&0\end{array}\right)\right]^{-1}\\
&=\frac{4h}{\omega^{2}+4h^{2}-(4hJ)^{2}|s(\mathbf{q})|^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\omega^{2}+4h^{2}&4hJs(\mathbf{q})\\
4hJs^{*}(\mathbf{q})&(\omega^{2}+4h^{2})\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(S15)

for the
e
-particle propagator. The matrices act in sublattice space.

In the following, we present the calculation of
\mathbf{G}
, first, in a perturbative expansion for
h/J\gg 1
and, second, in a continuum limit when
J\sim h
where we, however, stay far enough from the Higgs transition where non-Gaussian gauge field fluctuations become important.

S2.3
Pertuabative GF for
h/J\gg 1

We now expand Eq. (
S15
) up to the second order in
J
and insert it order by order in the convolution (
S9
).

For
w_{0}=0
, the dispersion relation
\epsilon(\mathbf{k})
would be
C_{6}
symmetric, simplifying further calculations. Any finite
w_{0}
reduces the symmetry to
C_{3}
. However, if we stay within the hierarchy of limits
h\gg w_{1}\gg w_{2}\gg w_{0}
(in the top left of the phase diagram
1
b) and
h\gg\epsilon
we can expand the dispersion relation

\epsilon(\mathbf{k})=\sqrt{\underbrace{w_{1}^{2}|s(\mathbf{k})|^{2}+w_{2}^{2}m(\mathbf{k})^{2}+w_{0}^{2}}_{\xi^{2}(\mathbf{k})}+2w_{0}w_{2}m(\mathbf{k})}.

(S16)

Note that the term
2w_{0}w_{2}m(k)
is, for any
\mathbf{k}
, small compared to
\xi^{2}(\mathbf{k})
. Thus, we can expand
\epsilon(\mathbf{k})
in powers of
w_{0}w_{2}m(\mathbf{k})
and obtain up to first order

\epsilon(\mathbf{k})\simeq\xi(\mathbf{k})+\frac{w_{0}w_{2}m(\mathbf{k})}{\xi(\mathbf{k})}.

(S17)

We split
\epsilon(\mathbf{k})
into a dominating
C_{6}
symmetric and a subleading
C_{6}
anti-symmetric part.

It will be useful to introduce the function

g(x,y)=\frac{1}{x^{2}+y^{2}}.

(S18)

S2.3.1
GF to order
J^{0}

The integral

\begin{split}\mathbf{G}(i\epsilon)|_{J^{0}}&=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\int(\text{d}q)(\text{d}\omega)D^{(0)}(i\omega)\\
&\times\frac{i(\epsilon-\omega)+(w_{2}m(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})+w_{0})\sigma_{z}}{(\epsilon-\omega)^{2}+\epsilon^{2}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})}\end{split}

(S19)

gives the zeroth order contribution to the GF. Performing the
\omega
integration and applying the shift
\mathbf{q}\rightarrow-\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{k}
yields

\begin{split}\mathbf{G}(i\epsilon)|_{J^{0}}&=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\int(\text{d}q)\Bigg(i\epsilon g(\epsilon,\epsilon(\mathbf{q})+2h)\\
&+\left.\left(1+\frac{2h}{\epsilon(\mathbf{q})}\right)g(\epsilon,\epsilon(\mathbf{q})+2h)(w_{2}m(\mathbf{q})+w_{0})\sigma_{z}\right).\end{split}

(S20)

Now, we use that (in the assumed limit)
h
is the dominant energy scale, and approximate the function
g(\epsilon,\epsilon(\mathbf{k}))\rightarrow\frac{1}{4h^{2}}
. This approximation will be used throughout the rest of this section.
Thus, the first part simply contributes
\frac{-i\epsilon}{4h^{2}}
. In the second part, we only keep the term in leading order in
1/h
.

Also, we expand the function
g(\epsilon,\epsilon(\mathbf{q})+2h)/\epsilon(\mathbf{q})
in the subleading
C_{6}
anti-symmetric part, according to the procedure outlined in the last section. We obtain

\begin{split}\frac{g(\epsilon,\epsilon(\mathbf{q}+2h))}{\epsilon(\mathbf{q})}&\approx\frac{g(\epsilon,\xi(\mathbf{q})+2h)}{\xi(\mathbf{q})}\\
&-\frac{w_{0}w_{2}g(\epsilon,\xi(\mathbf{q}))}{\xi^{3}(\mathbf{q})}m(\mathbf{q})\\
&-2\frac{w_{0}w_{2}g^{2}(\epsilon,\xi(\mathbf{q})+2h)}{\xi^{2}(\mathbf{q})}\left(1+\frac{2h}{\xi(\mathbf{q})}\right)m(\mathbf{q}).\end{split}

(S21)

All anti-symmetric terms (i.e., those are proportional to
m(\mathbf{q})
) are either of higher order in
1/h
or are suppressed by additional factors
w_{0}/w_{1}
or
w_{2}/w_{1}
compared to the symmetric part. Therefore, we also ignore these terms. Applying the approximations yields

\begin{split}\mathbf{G}|_{J^{0}}(i\epsilon)&=-\frac{i\epsilon}{4h^{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\int(\text{d}q)\frac{2hg(\epsilon,\xi(\mathbf{q})+2h)}{\xi(\mathbf{q})}w_{0}\sigma_{z}\\
&\approx-\frac{i\epsilon}{4h^{2}}-\frac{1}{hw_{1}}\underbrace{\sqrt{3}\int(\text{d}q)\frac{1}{|s(\mathbf{q})|}}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\sigma_{z}\\
&=-\frac{1}{4h^{2}}(i\epsilon+\frac{h}{w_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{0}\,w_{0}\sigma_{z}),\end{split}

(S22)

where
\mathcal{C}_{0}\approx 1.8
. Here, we first used that, due to symmetry,

\int(\text{d}q)g(\epsilon,\xi(\mathbf{q})+2h)m(\mathbf{q})=0

(S23)

and, second, that in the limit
w_{1}\gg w_{2}\gg w_{0}
the scaling of
\xi(\mathbf{q})
is essentially given by
w_{1}
. Therefore, we can approximate
\xi(\mathbf{q})\approx w_{1}|s(\mathbf{q})|
.

S2.3.2
GF to order
J^{1}

The integral

\begin{split}\mathbf{G}(i\epsilon,\mathbf{k})|_{J^{1}}&=-J\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\int(\text{d}q)(\text{d}\omega)(D^{(0)}(i\omega))^{2}\\
&\times\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{-w_{1}s(\mathbf{q})s(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})}{(\epsilon-\omega)^{2}+\epsilon^{2}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})}\\
\frac{s^{*}(\mathbf{q})s^{*}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})}{{(\epsilon-\omega)^{2}+\epsilon^{2}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})}}&0\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(S24)

gives the first order contribution to the GF. Using the identity

(D^{(0)}(i\omega))^{2}=-\frac{h}{2}\partial_{h}\frac{1}{h}D^{(0)}(i\omega)

(S25)

the frequency integration is analogous to the one for the zeroth order. Performing the integration and only keeping terms that are leading in
1/h
we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbf{G}(i\epsilon,\mathbf{k})|_{J^{1}}&=Jh\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\partial_{h}\int(\text{d}q)\frac{g(\epsilon,\xi(\mathbf{q})+2h)}{\xi(\mathbf{q})}\\
&\times\begin{pmatrix}0&-w_{1}s(\mathbf{q})s(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})\\
-w_{1}s^{*}(\mathbf{q})s^{*}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})&0\end{pmatrix}\\
&=Jh\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&-w_{1}s(\mathbf{k})\\
-w_{1}s^{*}(\mathbf{k})\end{pmatrix}\\
&\times\partial_{h}\int(\text{d}q)\frac{g(\epsilon,\xi(\mathbf{q})+2h)}{\xi(\mathbf{q})}(1+2\cos(\v q\cdot\v n_{1/2}))\\
&\approx-\frac{1}{4h^{2}}J\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathcal{C}_{1}\begin{pmatrix}0&-s(\mathbf{k})\\
-s^{*}(\mathbf{k}),\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(S26)

where

\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathcal{C}_{1}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\int(\text{d}q)\frac{1+2\cos(\v q \cdot\v n_{1/2})}{|s(\mathbf{q})|}\approx 0.26.

(S27)

To achieve the second equality in Eq. (
S26
), we expand the product
s(\mathbf{q})s(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})
and use the
C_{6}
symmetry of the integrand.
\mathbf{n}_{1/2}
denotes that both
\mathbf{n}_{1}
and
\mathbf{n}_{2}
can be used since they yield the same numerical value (again due to the
C_{6}
symmetry).

S2.3.3
GF to order
J^{2}

The integral

\begin{split}\mathbf{G}(i\epsilon,\mathbf{k})|_{J^{2}}&=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}J^{2}\int(\text{d}q)(\text{d}\omega)(D^{(0)}(i\omega))^{3}\\
&\times|s(q)|^{2}\frac{w_{2}m(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})+w_{0}}{(\epsilon-\omega)^{2}+\epsilon(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})}\end{split}

(S28)

gives the second-order contribution to the GF. Using a similar trick as for the first-order, that is

(D^{0}(i\omega))^{3}=\underbrace{\frac{1}{8}\left(h\partial_{h}^{2}-\partial_{h}\right)}_{\mathcal{D}_{h}}\frac{1}{h}D^{(0)}(i\omega)

(S29)

enables a straightforward frequency integration. We obtain

\begin{split}\mathbf{G}(i\epsilon,\mathbf{k})|_{J^{2}}&=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathcal{D}_{h}\frac{1}{h}\int(\text{d}q)|s(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})|^{2}m(\mathbf{q})\\
&\times\frac{g(\epsilon,\xi(\mathbf{q})+2h)}{\xi(\mathbf{q})}\sigma_{z}\\
&\approx-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{{2}}\mathcal{D}_{h}\frac{m(\mathbf{k})}{2h^{2}}\int(\text{d}q)\\
&\times\frac{2\sin^{2}(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{1/2})\sin^{2}(\frac{\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{2/1}}{2})}{w_{1}|s(\mathbf{q})|}\sigma_{z}\\
&=-\frac{1}{4h^{2}}\frac{J^{2}}{hw_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathcal{C}_{2}m(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{z},\end{split}

where

\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathcal{C}_{2}=4\sqrt{3}\int(\text{d}q)\frac{\sin^{2}(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{1/2})}{|s(\mathbf{q})|}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{2/1}}{2}\right)\approx 2.4.

(S30)

Here, we used trigonometric identities again to expand

S2.3.4
Full Result

Adding all calculated orders yields the final expression for the GF:

\begin{split}\mathbf{G}(i\epsilon,\mathbf{k})&=\mathbf{G}(i\epsilon,\mathbf{k})|_{J^{0}}+\mathbf{G}(i\epsilon,\mathbf{k})|_{J^{1}}+\mathbf{G}(i\epsilon,\mathbf{k})|_{J^{2}}\\
&=\frac{-1}{4h}\left(i\epsilon+\mathcal{C}_{0}\frac{h}{w_{1}}h_{\emptyset}\right),\end{split}

(S31a)

where

h_{\emptyset}=\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}+\frac{J^{2}}{h^{2}}w_{2}\mathcal{C}_{2}m(\mathbf{k})&-\frac{J}{h}w_{1}\mathcal{C}_{1}s(\mathbf{k})\\
-\frac{J}{h}w_{1}\mathcal{C}_{1}s^{*}(\mathbf{k})&-w_{0}-\frac{J^{2}}{h^{2}}\mathcal{C}_{2}w_{2}m(\mathbf{k})\end{pmatrix}.

(S31b)

This is the origin of equation (

).

S2.4
Estimate of Ginzburg-Levanyuk number

For the next section, in which we employ a continuum approximation of both e-particle and fermionic propagators it is useful to discuss the limits of applicability of the present theories without non-Gaussian fluctuaions.

The confinement transition is described by an effective
\phi^{4}
theory

\mathcal{L}=\phi[-\partial_{\tau}^{2}-\nabla^{2}+r^{2}]\phi+\lambda\phi^{4}

(S32)

Note that
[r]=E,[\phi]=E^{-1+D/2}
,
[\lambda]=E^{4-D}

One may estimate the regime of negligible non-Gaussian fluctuations as

r>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{D-4}}.

(S33)

Our bare estimates of coupling constants are
König et al. 2020

are
r=\sqrt{h^{2}-3hJ}
,
\lambda=J^{3}/h^{2}
, leading to

\sqrt{h^{2}-3hJ}>J^{3}/h^{2}.

(S34)

S2.5
e
-particle and orthogonal fermion GFs in continuum limit

In the case where
J\sim h
we can find an exact expression of
\textbf{G}_{s,s^{\prime}}(i\epsilon,\mathbf{k})
for the low energy/momentum limit.
First, we diagonalize the

-particle propagator (
S15

\begin{split}D(i\omega,\mathbf{q})&=\frac{4h}{\omega^{2}+4h^{2}-4hJ|s(q)|}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\\
&+\frac{4h}{\omega^{2}+4h^{2}+4hJ|s(q)|}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\\
&\approx\frac{4h}{\omega^{2}+4h^{2}-4hJ|s(q)|}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\

Overcoming Output Constraints in Iterative Learning Control Systems by Reference Adaptation We acknowledge support of the Deutsche Förderungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) under Germany’s Excellence Strategy – EXC-2002/1 – Projectnumber 390523135
Michael Meindl, Fabio Molinari, Jörg Raisch, Thomas Seel

Abstract
Iterative Learning Control (ILC) schemes can guarantee properties such as asymptotic stability and monotonic error convergence, but do not, in general, ensure adherence to output constraints. The topic of this paper is the design of a reference-adapting ILC (RAILC) scheme, extending an existing ILC system and capable of complying with output constraints. The underlying idea is to scale the reference at every trial by using a conservative estimate of the output’s progression. Properties as the monotonic convergence above a threshold and the respect of output constraints are formally proven. Numerical simulations and experimental results reinforce our theoretical results.

1
Introduction

Iterative Learning Control (ILC) is a control scheme
suitable
for systems operating in a repetitive manner.
ILC tracks a desired reference
and aims at improving its accuracy
from repetition to repetition by exploiting
the information of previous trials,
see
Bristow et al. 2006
for a survey.
ILC can achieve better performance than conventional feedback systems as the latter does not exploit available information from previous trials, see, e.g.,
Moore et al. 1992
. ILC finds much appeal in fields such as robotics, manufacturing, and biomedical applications
due to the possibility of repeating trials; such fields
have greatly benefited from the
implementation of ILC systems, see, e.g.
Elci et al. 2002
,
Seel et al. 2011
,
Rogers and Tutty 2016
,
and
Pandit and Buchheit 1999
.

A relevant
class of systems
for ILC applications are those subject to output constraints.
A robotic manipulator, for example, is commonly restricted when it comes to possible positions and paths; violating these constraints may potentially damage the system.
In general, ILC systems can guarantee
monotonic error convergence, meaning
that the difference between
the output and the desired trajectory
decreases in a suitable norm at every trial.
However, compliance with
output constraints is, in general, not guaranteed.
For example, Fig.
1

depicts
an ILC system learning the desired trajectory

\mathbf{r}
while being constrained by the maximum value
y_{\mathrm{max}}
.

Figure 1:
Illustration of the output-constrained ILC problem and a robotic manipulator restricted by output constraints

Although the system is monotonically convergent,
the output constraints are violated during the second trial.
This paper addresses this problem
and proposes a modular solution extending
the ILC system, thus ensuring
compliance with the output constraints
while maintaining monotonic error convergence.

Output-constrained ILC systems
have already been investigated elsewhere
and can be classified in two different categories.
In the first class,
novel update laws based on constrained optimization have been introduced. For example,
Jin et al. 2014
propose an update-law based on a high-dimensional
and constrained optimization problem.
This requires accurate knowledge of the plant dynamics
to ensure compliance with the output constraints in time-varying ILC systems.
In the second class,
methods like the one proposed by

Sebastian et al. 2018a

combine ILC with additional feedback
control to handle linear,
time-varying, multi-input multi-output
systems with input and output constraints.
Sebastian et al. 2018b
validate this
approach for a robotic manipulator where an underlying feedback controller
is implemented to avoid the violation of constraints.

The approach in this paper is to update the reference trajectory for each trial
based on a low-dimensional optimization problem.
It does not require accurate knowledge
of the plant dynamics
and ensures compliance with the output constraints
while maintaining the existing ILC system’s monotonic
error convergence.
In contrast to previous work,
the proposed RAILC scheme is a modular extension
requiring neither high-dimensional optimization
nor the design of an underlying feedback controller.
The proposed method is validated
using a two-wheeled inverted pendulum robot (TWIPR),
whose task is
to learn a highly accurate motion
in the presence of output constraints.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section 2 introduces notation. Section 3 briefly summarizes the structure of conventional ILC system consisting of a linear plant, learning function, and Q-filter. Section 4 formulates the problem. Section 5 introduces a RAILC algorithm and discusses its properties formally. Section 6 presents a TWIPR and validates the RAILC scheme in both simulation and experiment. The simulation results of the RAILC algorithm are also compared to those of the conventional ILC.
Finally, Section 7 gives concluding remarks.

3
Lifted-System ILC in a Nutshell

Consider the discrete-time, linear, time-invariant SISO system
at its
j^{\mathrm{th}}
trial,
j\in\rm I\!N_{\mathrm{0}}
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathbf{x}_{j}(n+1)&=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{j}(n)+\mathbf{B}u_{j}(n)+d(n)\\
y_{j}(n)&=\mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}_{j}(n)\end{array}\right.\,,

(1)

where
n\in\left\{1,...,N\right\}
is the sample index
and
N\in\rm I\!N
is the numbers of samples in each trial. Let,
\forall n\in\left\{1,...,N\right\}
,
\mathbf{x}(n)\in\rm I\!R^{k}
denote the
k
dimensional state vector,
u(n)\in\rm I\!R
the input variable,
y(n)\in\rm I\!R
the output variable and
d(n)\in\rm I\!R
an unknown disturbance, which is the same for all trials. Let
\mathbf{A}\in\rm I\!R^{k\times k}
denote the system matrix,
\mathbf{B}\in\rm I\!R^{k\times 1}
the input matrix, and
\mathbf{C}\in\rm I\!R^{1\times k}
the output matrix.

System (
1
) can be rewritten in its so-called lifted form, i.e.,

\mathbf{y}_{j}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{u}_{j}+\mathbf{d}\,,

(2)

where, at each iteration
j\in\rm I\!N_{\mathrm{0}}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{j}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}u_{j}(0)&u_{j}(1)&\dots&u_{j}(N-1)\end{bmatrix}^{T}

(3a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{j}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}y_{j}(m)&y_{j}(m+1)&\dots&y_{j}(m+N-1)\end{bmatrix}^{T}

(3b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}d(m)&d(m+1)&\dots&d(m+N-1)\end{bmatrix}^{T}

(3c)

and
m\in\rm I\!N_{\mathrm{0}}
denotes the system’s relative degree, see
Bristow et al. 2006
. For system (
1
) the parameters
p_{ij}\in\rm I\!R
of the plant matrix
\mathbf{P}
are given by

\forall i,j\in\rm I\!N\qquad p_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{i-j+m-1}\mathbf{B}&\forall i\geq j\\
0&\forall i<j\end{array}\right.\,.

(4)

The goal of an ILC system is to have output
\mathbf{y}_{j}
follow a desired trajectory
\mathbf{r}\in\rm I\!R^{N}
.
To this end,
a learning matrix, namely
\mathbf{L}\in\rm I\!R^{N\times N}
,
and
a Q-filter, namely
\mathbf{Q}\in\rm I\!R^{N\times N}
,
need to be designed (see, e.g.
Bristow et al. 2006
),
thus leading to the update law

\forall j\in\rm I\!N,\qquad\mathbf{u}_{j+1}=\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{u}_{j}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{e}_{j}\right)\,,

(5)

where
\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\rm I\!R^{N}
denotes the error trajectory defined as

\forall j\in\rm I\!N,\qquad\mathbf{e}_{j}:=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{y}_{j}\,.

(6)

Note that the matrices
\mathbf{P}
,
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{Q}
are regular.

Definition 1

(
Asymptotic Stability
,
see
Bristow et al. 2006
):
The ILC system with dynamics (
2
) and update law (
5
) is asymptotically stable if the
limit

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{\infty}:

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{e}_{j}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\left[\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\right]^{-1}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{L}\right]\left(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{d}\right)

(7)

uniquely exists. In what follows, let

\mathbf{e}_{\infty}
be named
residual error
.

Note that,
if
\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{I}
,
by (
7
),

\mathbf{e}_{\infty}=0
.

Proposition 1

(
Bristow et al. 2006
, Theorem 1)
s
The ILC system with dynamics (
2
) and update law (
5
) is asymptotically stable if and only if

\rho\left(\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\right)<1\,.

(8)

{pf}

See
(
Bristow et al. 2006
, Theorem 1)
.

Despite asymptotic stability,
the error trajectory
can take arbitrarily large values,
thus generating large learning transients.
The concept of
monotonic convergence

narrows the set of possible error trajectories.

Definition 2

(
Monotonic Convergence
, see
Bristow et al. 2006
):
The system composed of
plant (
2
)
and
update law (
5
)
is

under a given norm
\left|\left|{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}\right|\right|

if

\forall j\in\rm I\!N_{\mathrm{0}},\quad\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{\infty}-\mathbf{e}_{j+1}}\right|\right|\leq\gamma\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{\infty}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right|\right|\,,

(9)

where
\gamma\in[0,1)
is the convergence rate.

Proposition 2

The system (
2
)-(
5
)
is monotonically convergent under a given norm
\left|\left|{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}\right|\right|

if

\gamma:=\left|\left|{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\mathbf{P}^{-1}}\right|\right|<1\,.

(10)

{pf}

See
Bristow et al. 2006
.

Monotonic convergence is traditionally
verified for the spectral and the infinite norms, i.e.,
respectively,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathrm{2}}

\displaystyle:=\overline{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\mathbf{P}^{-1}\right)

(11)

and

\displaystyle\gamma_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=\left|\left|{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\mathbf{P}^{-1}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\,.

(12)

2
)-(
5
)
is monotonically convergent above a threshold
\kappa\in\rm I\!R_{\geq 0}
under a given norm
\left|\left|{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}\right|\right|

if

\forall j\in\rm I\!N_{\mathrm{0}}:\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right|\right|\geq\kappa\quad\implies\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j+1}}\right|\right|\leq\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right|\right|\,.

(13)

Proposition 3

(
Seel et al. 2017
, Theorem 1)

System (
2
)-(
5
)
is monotonically convergent above the threshold
\epsilon=\left|\left|{\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}\right)\left(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{d}\right)}\right|\right|
under a given norm
\left|\left|{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}\right|\right|
if

\gamma<1\,.

(14)

{pf}

See
(
Seel et al. 2017
, Theorem 1)
.

By Propositions 1-3,
to ensure properties as
asymptotic stability and
monotonic convergence,
standard design
methods, see, e.g.,
Bristow et al. 2006
, can be employed.
Commonly, the Q-filter is chosen as a
low-pass filter so that
frequencies above its bandwidth
are cut-off from the learning,
thus increasing robustness.
The learning matrix
\mathbf{L}
is
typically designed by either tuning
the parameters of a PD-function,
applying
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
optimization,
or by solving a quadratic optimal problem.

4
Problem Formulation

Consider system (
2
)-(
5
)
with a desired trajectory
\mathbf{r}
.
Both the Q-filter and the learning matrix are
designed such that asymptotic stability
and monotonic error
convergence are guaranteed.

Additionally, the output
\mathbf{y}_{j}
is constrained by an upper bound
y_{\mathrm{max}}\in\rm I\!R_{>0}
, which must not be violated on any trial, i.e.,

\forall j\in\rm I\!N_{\mathrm{0}},\qquad\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}<y_{\mathrm{max}}\,.

(15)

Despite monotonic convergence,
the conventional ILC system does not necessarily enforce
output constraints (see Fig. 1).
We propose to extend the designed ILC in a modular fashion,
so that output constraints are satisfied
and monotonic error convergence maintained.

To ensure the problem being well-posed, some assumptions are needed.

Assumption 1

There is a known initial input trajectory
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{0}}
leading to an initial output trajectory
\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{0}}
such that

\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{0}}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq y_{\mathrm{max}}\,.

(16)

Assumption 2

The reference trajectory
\mathbf{r}
is chosen such that

\left|\left|{\mathbf{r}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq y_{\mathrm{max}}\,.

(17)

5
Reference-Adapting ILC

The basic idea of this reference-adapting iterative learning control (RAILC) scheme is to adapt the reference trajectory
\mathbf{r}
at each ILC trial to ensure that the output trajectory
\mathbf{y}_{j}
does not exceed the maximum value
y_{\mathrm{max}}
. To this end, let

\forall j\in\rm I\!N_{\mathrm{0}},\qquad{\mathbf{r}}_{j}\in\rm I\!R^{N}

(18)

denote the adapted reference trajectory at the
j^{\mathrm{th}}
trial leading to the adapted update law

\forall j\in\rm I\!N_{\mathrm{0}},\qquad\mathbf{u}_{j+1}=\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{u}_{j}+\mathbf{L}\left({\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{j}\right)\right)\,.

(19)

Figure 2:
Block diagram of the RAILC system

Let
\overline{\epsilon}\in\rm I\!R
denote an upper bound such that

\overline{\epsilon}\geq\max_{\forall j\in\rm I\!N}{\left|\left|{\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}\right)\left({\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{d}\right)}\right|\right|_{\infty}}\,.

(20)

Algorithm 1

The adapted reference trajectory
{\mathbf{r}}_{j}
is

\forall j\in\rm I\!N_{\mathrm{0}},\qquad{\mathbf{r}}_{j}:=\mathbf{y}_{j}+a_{j}\left(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{y}_{j}\right)

(21)

with

\displaystyle a_{j}=\max\tilde{a}_{j}

(22a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{j}\in[0,1]

(22b)

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{j}\leq\frac{y_{\mathrm{max}}-\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j}+\tilde{a}_{j}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{y}_{j})}\right|\right|_{\infty}-\overline{\epsilon}}{\gamma_{\infty}\left|\left|{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}}

(22c)

Note that the optimization problem (
22
) can be efficiently solved using bisection, which makes the algorithm applicable to embedded systems with low computational power.

Consider the upper bound
\overline{\epsilon}
required to solve the optimization problem (
22
). In the trivial case of
\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{I}
,
\overline{\epsilon}
can be set to zero. In the case of
\mathbf{Q}\neq\mathbf{I}
an argument similar to the one in
Seel et al. 2017
can be applied. In fact,
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}
commonly takes the form of a high-pass filter. Under the assumption that
\mathbf{r}
,
\mathbf{d}
,
\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{0}}
and therefore
{\mathbf{r}}_{j}

have spectra well
below the high-pass filter’s
cutoff frequency, the upper bound

\overline{\epsilon}
can be
assumed to be a small positive number.

Next, conditions for the existence of a solution to the optimization problem (
22
) are investigated.

Proposition 4

For a given
\overline{\epsilon}
and
\mathbf{y}_{j}
, there is a solution to (
22
) if

y_{\mathrm{max}}-\overline{\epsilon}\geq\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\,.

(23)

{pf}

\tilde{a}_{j}=0
clearly is a feasible solution of (
22
) if
y_{\mathrm{max}}-\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}-\overline{\epsilon}\geq 0
; i.e., if (
23
) holds.

Proposition 4 implies that a solution to the optimization problem (
22
) is guaranteed to exist if

y_{\mathrm{max}}-\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\geq\overline{\epsilon}.

As previously discussed,
\overline{\epsilon}
can be assumed to be a small number. Hence, if
\mathbf{y}_{j}
is not “too close” to
y_{\mathrm{max}}
, a solution to (
22
) exists.

Under this assumption,
we can now focus on the output constraints.

Proposition 5

If there is a solution to the optimization problem (
22
),
the output constraint (
15
) is guaranteed to hold in the next trial.

{pf}

If a solution to (
22
) exists, the following is true

a_{j}\gamma_{\infty}\left|\left|{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq y_{\mathrm{max}}-\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}-\overline{\epsilon}\,.

(24)

Furthermore, (
21
) gives

a_{j}\left|\left|{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}=\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\,,

(25)

which, incorporated into (
24
), yields

\gamma_{\infty}\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}+\overline{\epsilon}\leq y_{\mathrm{max}}-\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\,.

(26)

By combining (
19
)
and (
2
),
one obtains

\mathbf{u}_{j+1}=\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\mathbf{u}_{j}+\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{L}\left({\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{d}\right)

(27)

and

\mathbf{y}_{j+1}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\mathbf{P}^{-1}\mathbf{y}_{j}+\\
\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{L}{\mathbf{r}}_{j}\,,

(28)

which leads to

{\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{j+1}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\mathbf{P}^{-1}\left({\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{j}\right)+\\
\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}\right)\left({\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{d}\right)\,.

(29)

By applying the inequalities of norms,
and by incorporating (
20
) and (
12
), one obtains

\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{j+1}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq\gamma_{\infty}\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}+\overline{\epsilon}\,.

(30)

Combining this with (
26
) yields

\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{j+1}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq y_{\mathrm{max}}-\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\,,

(31)

which, by adding
\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j+1}}\right|\right|_{\infty}
, equals

\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j+1}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq y_{\mathrm{max}}+\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j+1}}\right|\right|_{\infty}-\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}-\\
\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{j+1}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\,.

(32)

Next, consider the norm inequality

\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{j+1}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\geq\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}-\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty},

(33)

equivalently,

0\geq\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j+1}}\right|\right|_{\infty}-\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}-\left|\left|{{\mathbf{r}}_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{j+1}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\,.

(34)

Combining the latter with (
32
) yields

\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j+1}}\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq y_{\mathrm{max}}\,,

(35)

which concludes the proof.

Also, the monotonic convergence properties
of the RAILC system are investigated.

Proposition 6

System (
2
)-(
22
) is monotonically convergent above a threshold
\hat{\kappa}=\frac{\hat{\epsilon}}{1-\hat{\gamma}}
, with

\hat{\epsilon}:=\left|\left|{\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}\right)\left(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{d}\right)}\right|\right|\,,

(36)

under a given norm
\left|\left|{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}\right|\right|

if

\hat{\gamma}:=\max_{j\in\rm I\!N_{\mathrm{0}}}\left\{\left|\left|{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-a_{j}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\mathbf{P}^{-1}}\right|\right|\right\}<1\,.

(37)

{pf}

By combining (
19
) and (
21
), one obtains

\mathbf{u}_{j+1}=\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-a_{j}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\mathbf{u}_{j}+a_{j}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{L}\left(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{d}\right)\,,

(38)

which substituted into (
2
) gives

\mathbf{y}_{j+1}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-a_{j}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\mathbf{P}^{-1}\mathbf{y}_{j}+\\
\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{d}+a_{j}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{r}\,.

(39)

Working the latter into (
6
) leads to

\mathbf{e}_{j+1}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-a_{j}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\mathbf{P}^{-1}\mathbf{e}_{j}+\\
\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}\right)\left(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{d}\right)\,.

(40)

Taking the norm, inserting (
36
) and (
37
), and applying the inequalities of norms leads to

\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j+1}}\right|\right|\leq\hat{\gamma}\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right|\right|+\hat{\epsilon}\,,

(41)

which, by subtracting
\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right|\right|
, gives

\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j+1}}\right|\right|-\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right|\right|\leq\left(\hat{\gamma}-1\right)\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right|\right|+\hat{\epsilon}\,.

(42)

Furthermore, the monotonic convergence of the form

\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j+1}}\right|\right|\leq\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right|\right|

(43)

equals

\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j+1}}\right|\right|-\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right|\right|\leq 0\,,

which, by (
42
), is guaranteed if

\left(\hat{\gamma}-1\right)\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right|\right|+\hat{\epsilon}\leq 0\,.

(44)

The latter is equivalent to

\left|\left|{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right|\right|\geq\frac{\hat{\epsilon}}{1-\hat{\gamma}},

(45)

thus concluding the proof.

Recall the argument
that
\overline{\epsilon}
can be set to a small number,
if
\mathbf{r}
,
\mathbf{d}
, and
\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{0}}
have spectra
below the cutoff frequency of
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}
. By (
36
), the same argument applies to
\hat{\epsilon}
. Regarding (
37
), consider the following proposition.

Proposition 7

System (
2
)-(
22
) fulfills

\hat{\gamma}_{\mathrm{2}}:=\max_{j\in\rm I\!N}\left\{\left|\left|{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{I}-a_{j}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}\right)\mathbf{P}^{-1}}\right|\right|_{2}\right\}<1

(46)

if

\gamma_{\mathrm{2}}<1\quad\textit{and}\quad\left|\left|{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}}\right|\right|_{2}\leq 1\,.

(47)

{pf}

The proof follows directly from Proposition 8 (see App. B).

Therefore, the RAILC system is monotonically convergent above a threshold under the Euclidean norm, if the conventional ILC system is monotonically convergent under the Euclidean norm and the mild requirement of
\left|\left|{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}}\right|\right|_{2}\leq 1
.

Summarizing the above results, the following statements can be given for the proposed RAILC scheme.

•

Under mild assumptions on the spectra of
\mathbf{r}
,
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{0}}
, the upper bound
\overline{\epsilon}
can be assumed to be a small number.

•

If
y_{\mathrm{max}}-\overline{\epsilon}\geq\left|\left|{\mathbf{y}_{j}}\right|\right|_{\infty}
, a solution to the optimization problem (
22
) exists.

•

If a solution to the optimization problem (
22
) exists, the reference adaption scheme ensures that the output trajectory
\mathbf{y}_{j+1}
of the next trial satisfies the output constraints (
15
).

•

If the conventional ILC system is monotonically convergent under the Euclidean norm and
\left|\left|{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}^{-1}}\right|\right|_{2}\leq 1
, the RAILC system is monotonically convergent above a threshold under the Euclidean norm.

•

The low-dimensional nature of the optimization problem (
22
) makes the RAILC scheme applicable to systems with low computing power.

•

The above results were derived under the assumption of
\mathbf{P}
,
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{Q}
being regular. Subsequently, the RAILC scheme can also be applied to time-varying, non-causal systems.

Experimental Results

As a demonstrator for the proposed RAILC, we consider a TWIPR, supposed to perform complex and repeated maneuvers. The robot consists of the pendulum body housing the main electronics, i.e., a microcomputer, inertial measurement units, motors and accumulator. Wheels are mounted onto the motors, which combined with the robot’s body create an inverted pendulum. Aiming at keeping the vehicle in an upright position, the system is clearly unstable, thus requiring feedback control.
Two aspects encourage the use of ILC:

7
Conclusion

We have extended standard
ILC schemes in a modular fashion to cope with output-constrained systems.
By adapting the reference trajectory
based on a conservative estimate of the
output progression, the violation of output constraints is avoided.
Under mild assumptions on disturbances, reference trajectory
and the output trajectory, the existence of a solution
is guaranteed.
A condition
for the RAILC error to monotonically converge
above a threshold was given.
It has been shown that this threshold can be expected to be close to zero.
The RAILC approach has been applied to a TWIPR, and both simulation and experimental results have been shown.
While the conventional ILC system violates output constraints,
the RAILC system complies with them.
Furthermore, the RAILC system exhibits monotonic convergence above a small threshold
in both simulation and experiment.
Finally, simulation results show that applying RAILC does not slow down the decline of the error norm when compared to the conventional ILC.

Ongoing work investigates conditions for the existence of a solution to the optimization problem (
22
). Furthermore, we will
study the possibility of using RAILC to increase performance.

Lemma 1

Given a matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\rm I\!R^{N\times N}
, a matrix
\mathbf{B}\in\rm I\!R^{N\times N}
, and a vector
\mathbf{v}\in\rm I\!R^{N\times N}
with

\left|\left|{\mathbf{v}}\right|\right|_{2}=1\,,

(59)

if (sufficient condition)

\left|\left|{\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{A}}\right|\right|_{2}<1\,,

(60)

then

\mathbf{v}^{T}\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{B}\right)\mathbf{v}>\mathbf{v}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{T}\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}-1\,.

(61)

{pf}

Combining (
60
), (
59
), and the submultiplicativity of norms gives

1>\left|\left|{\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{A}}\right|\right|_{2}=\left|\left|{\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{A}}\right|\right|_{2}\left|\left|{\mathbf{v}}\right|\right|_{2}\\
\geq\left|\left|{\mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}}\right|\right|_{2}=\sqrt{{\left(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right)}^{T}\left(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right)}\,,

(62)

which, by taking the squares and expanding, leads to (
61
), thus, concluding the proof.

Proposition 8

Given a matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\rm I\!R^{N\times N}
, a matrix
\mathbf{B}\in\rm I\!R^{N\times N}
,
and a scalar
a\in(0,1]
, if (sufficient condition)

\left|\left|{\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{A}}\right|\right|_{2}<1\quad\mathit{and}\quad\left|\left|{\mathbf{B}}\right|\right|_{2}\leq 1\,,

(63)

then

\left|\left|{\mathbf{B}-a\mathbf{A}}\right|\right|_{2}<1\,.

(64)

{pf}

First, let
\mathbf{w}\in\rm I\!R^{N}
denote a vector such that

\mathbf{w}=\underset{\left|\left|{\mathbf{x}}\right|\right|_{2}=1}{\mathrm{argmax}}\left\{\left|\left|{\left(\mathbf{B}-a\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{x}}\right|\right|_{2}\right\}\,,

(65)

which, according to the definition of induced matrix norms, implies

\left|\left|{\mathbf{B}-a\mathbf{A}}\right|\right|_{2}=\left|\left|{\left(\mathbf{B}-a\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{w}}\right|\right|_{2}=\\
\sqrt{\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}-a\mathbf{w}^{T}\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{B}\right)\mathbf{w}+a^{2}\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w}}\,.

(66)

Lemma 1 further gives

\mathbf{w}^{T}\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{B}\right)\mathbf{w}>\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w}+\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}-1\,,

(67)

which combined with (
66
) leads to

\left|\left|{\mathbf{B}-a\mathbf{A}}\right|\right|_{2}<\\
\sqrt{(1-a)\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}+a+(a^{2}-a)\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w}}\,.

(68)

Submultiplicativity of norms, (
63
), and (
65
) give

\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}\leq 1\,,

(69)

which combined with

a\in(0,1]~\implies~a^{2}\leq a~\implies~a^{2}-a\leq 0

(70)

and (
68
) leads to

\left|\left|{\mathbf{B}-a\mathbf{A}}\right|\right|_{2}<\sqrt{1-a+a}=1\,.

(71)

This concludes the proof.

On Drinfeld modular forms of higher rank VI: The simplicial complex associated with a coefficient form
Ernst-Ulrich Gekeler

Abstract
. The coefficient forms ℓ k a {}_{a}\ell_{k} and the para-Eisenstein series α k \alpha_{k} are simplicial Drinfeld modular forms. We study the attached simplicial complexes ℬ ​ 𝒯 r ​ ( ℓ k a ) \mathcal{BT}^{r}({}_{a}\ell_{k}) and ℬ ​ 𝒯 r ​ ( α k ) \mathcal{BT}^{r}(\alpha_{k}) , which are full subcomplexes of the Bruhat-Tits building ℬ ​ 𝒯 r \mathcal{BT}^{r} of PGL ⁡ ( r , K ∞ ) \PGL(r,K_{\infty}) . They are connected (if the rank r r is larger than 2), strongly equidimensional of codimension 1 in ℬ ​ 𝒯 r \mathcal{BT}^{r} , boundaryless, and satisfy a symmetry property under the non-trivial involution of the Dynkin diagram A r − 1 A_{r-1} .

2.1.

As it is important for the sequel, we break down the latter statement.

2.2.

For

be the

Its

{}_{a}\phi^{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\vcentcolon=\{z\in C_{\infty}\mid\phi_{a}^{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(z)=0\}.

This is an

-vector space of dimension

where

(2.2.3)

Suppose that
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbf{F}
, and arrange the elements of
{}_{a}B_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
in increasing order
\lvert\lambda_{1}\rvert\leq\lvert\lambda_{2}\rvert\leq\dots\leq\lvert\lambda_{rd}\rvert

where, if
\lvert e_{s,i}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\rvert=\lvert e_{s^{\prime},i^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\rvert

is decreed to come before
e_{s^{\prime},i^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})
if and only if

. Then, e.g.,

\lambda_{1}=e_{0,r}(\boldsymbol{\omega})
and
\lambda_{rd}=e_{d-1,1}(\boldsymbol{\omega})

is

-

Proposition 2.3

Let
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbf{F}
. The map

\displaystyle\{T^{s}\omega_{i}\mid 0\leq s<d,1\leq i\leq r\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{}_{a}\phi^{\boldsymbol{\omega}}

\displaystyle T^{s}\omega_{i}

\displaystyle\longmapsto e_{s,i}(\boldsymbol{\omega})

is strictly monotonically increasing in the sense that
\lvert T^{s}\omega_{i}\rvert\leq\lvert T^{s^{\prime}}\omega_{i^{\prime}}\rvert
implies
\lvert e_{s,i}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\rvert\leq\lvert e_{s^{\prime},i^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\rvert
, with
(in-)equality on the left hand side implying (in-)equality in
\lvert e_{s,i}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\rvert\leq\lvert e_{s^{\prime},i^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\rvert

Proof.

With notations as before,

(2.3.1)

e_{s,i}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\frac{T^{s}\omega_{i}}{a}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{\mathbf{a}\in A^{r}}\left(1-\frac{T^{s}\omega_{i}/a}{\mathbf{a}\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right).

For one of the factors of
\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}
, the absolute value is

(2.3.2)

\left\lvert 1-\frac{T^{s}\omega_{i}/a}{\mathbf{a}\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right\rvert=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\lvert\mathbf{a}\boldsymbol{\omega}\geq\lvert T^{s}\omega_{i}/a\rvert,\\
\left\lvert\frac{T^{s}\omega_{i}/a}{\mathbf{a}\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right\rvert,&\text{if }\lvert\mathbf{a}\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert<\lvert T^{s}\omega_{i}/a\rvert.\end{cases}

Here the value 1 in case
\lvert\mathbf{a}\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert=\lvert T^{s}\omega_{i}/a\rvert
results from
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbf{F}
and the orthogonality of the set

\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}\}
, see, e.g.,
[
9
]
Proposition 3.1. If we put for

:

(2.3.3)

\varphi(x)\vcentcolon=x\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{a}\in A^{r}\\
\lvert\mathbf{a}\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert<x\end{subarray}}\frac{x}{\lvert\mathbf{a}\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert},

then by the above,
\lvert e_{s,i}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\varphi(\lvert T^{s}\omega_{i}/a\rvert)

is certainly monotonically increasing, whence the result.
∎

m_{1}^{(j)}\geq\dots\geq m_{r-1}^{(j)}\geq m_{r}^{(j)}=0
. For
\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\Phi
, its restriction to
\sigma(\mathds{R})
satisfies

(3.7.1)

\boldsymbol{\beta}(\sigma(\mathds{R}))=\{b\}\text{ or }[b,b+1]

with some
b\in\mathds{Z}
. Now consider the functions
\mathbf{x}\mapsto x_{i}+s
(resp.
\mathbf{x}\mapsto x_{i^{\prime}}+s^{\prime}
) on
\sigma(\mathds{R})
, where
1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq r
,
0\leq s,s^{\prime}<d
. If

(3.7.2)

x_{i}+s<x_{i^{\prime}}+s^{\prime}

for one
\mathbf{x}\in\overset{\circ}{\sigma}(\mathds{R})
, then by (
3.7.1
) this relation will persist all over
\overset{\circ}{\sigma}(\mathds{R})
. Similarly, equality in
(
3.7.2
) holds or doesn’t hold for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overset{\circ}{\sigma}(\mathds{R})
simultaneously, but (
3.7.2
) may degenerate to equality
if
\mathbf{x}
approaches a boundary point
\mathbf{y}
of
\sigma(\mathds{R})
. Letting
(v_{k}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x}))_{1\leq k\leq rd}
be the
d
-characteristic sequence of
\mathbf{x}\in\sigma(\mathds{R})
, it follows that

(3.7.3)

\lim_{\mathbf{x}\to\mathbf{y}}v_{k}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})=v_{k}^{(d)}(\mathbf{y}).

In particular, if
\mathbf{x}\in\overset{\circ}{\sigma}(\mathds{R})
belongs to
\mathcal{W}(d,k)=\{x\in\mathcal{W}(\mathds{R})\mid v_{k}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})=v_{k+1}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})\}
, then all the boundary points of

\sigma(\mathds{R})
, and in particular the vertices
\mathbf{m}^{(j)}
belong to
\mathcal{W}(d,k)
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathbf{m}^{(0)},\dots,\mathbf{m}^{(\ell)}
belong to
\mathcal{W}(d,k)
, and let
\mathbf{x}\in\overset{\circ}{\sigma}(\mathds{R})
,

(3.7.4)

\mathbf{x}=\sum_{0\leq j\leq\ell}t_{j}\mathbf{m}^{(j)}

with barycentric coordinates
t_{j}
,
0\leq t_{j}\leq 1
,
\sum t_{j}=1
. Write
v_{k}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})=x_{i}+s
,
v_{k+1}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})=x_{i^{\prime}}+s^{\prime}
with suitable data
(i,s)
and
(i^{\prime},s^{\prime})
. Then

x_{i^{\prime}}-x_{i}=\sum_{0\leq j\leq\ell}t_{j}(m_{i^{\prime}}^{(j)}-m_{i}^{(j)})

and

v_{k+1}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})-v_{k}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{j}t_{j}(m_{i^{\prime}}^{(j)}-m_{i}^{(j)})+s^{\prime}-s.

Letting
\mathbf{x}
tend to
\mathbf{m}^{(j)}
, we find for each

that is,

. Inserting in (

) yields

Therefore,
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{W}(d,k)
. As this holds for each
\mathbf{x}\in\overset{\circ}{\sigma}(\mathds{R})
, we have verified criterion (b) of
1.6

, i.e.,

Proposition 3.8

\mathcal{W}(d,k)
(resp.

(resp.
\mathcal{A}

Proposition 4.5
:

Let

\mathbf{n}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{n}_{i}
with some

\mathrm{(i)}

, then
\mathbf{n}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}(k)\Leftrightarrow\rho_{k}(\mathbf{n})\neq i

\mathrm{(ii)}

. Then

(i)

Let
i<\rho_{k}(\mathbf{n})
and
H\vcentcolon=\{j\mid 1\leq j\leq i\text{ and }n_{j}<v_{k}(\mathbf{n})\},
of cardinality
h
. Under
\mathbf{n}\leadsto\mathbf{n}^{\prime}
(which on
\diag
shifts the first

i

become larger than

, viz, those of shape

with

. Therefore,

B_{k-h}(\mathbf{n}^{\prime})=B_{k}(\mathbf{n})\quad\text{and}\quad B_{k+1-h}(\mathbf{n}^{\prime})=B_{k+1}(\mathbf{n}).

Since the column of

contains at least the further

and

.

and

) still holds, but there are only

.

be the boxes

and

). Then there are precisely

(ii)

8.
The involution
(\,.\,)\hat{}

We study the involution on
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{BT}
which corresponds to the non-trivial automorphism of the Dynkin diagram.

8.1.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be the group scheme
\PGL(r)
with its subgroups
\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{T}
, the standard Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices and the standard torus of diagonal matrices. For
simplicity, we write matrices where classes of matrices modulo scalar matrices are meant.

For
g\in\mathbf{G}(K_{\infty})=\PGL(r,K_{\infty})
, we let
g^{t}
be its transpose, and

(8.1.1)

(\,.\,)^{*}\colon G(K_{\infty})\longrightarrow G(K_{\infty})

is the automorphism
g\mapsto g^{*}\vcentcolon=(g^{t})^{-1}=(g^{-1})^{t}
. It is well-known that
(\,.\,)^{*}
is not inner. (Recall that we always assume
r\geq 3
. For
r=2
,
(\,.\,)^{*}
is the inner automorphism induced by the element

(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{smallmatrix})
.) We have
\mathbf{T}^{*}=\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{B}^{*}=\mathbf{B}^{-}
, the group of lower triangular matrices. Let
w
be the well-defined maximal element
of the Weyl group
W=W(\mathbf{T},\Phi)
with respect to the Borel subgroup
\mathbf{B}
. Then

(8.1.2)

w=\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&0&1\\
\vdots&&1&0\\
0&&&\vdots\\
1&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}\text{ with zeroes off the anti-diagonal},w^{2}=1,w\mathbf{B}w^{-1}=\mathbf{B}^{-}.

Further, the two involutions
(\,.\,)^{w}\colon g\mapsto wgw^{-1}
and
(\,.\,)^{*}\colon g\mapsto g^{*}
commute. Therefore

(8.1.3)

(\,.\,)\hat{}\colon g\longmapsto g\hat{}\vcentcolon=wg^{*}w^{-1}=(wgw^{-1})^{*}

is an involution of
\mathbf{G}
, too. As the set of vertices
\mathcal{BT}(\mathds{Z})
of
\mathcal{BT}
is given by
\mathbf{G}(K_{\infty})/\mathbf{G}(O_{\infty})
and
\mathbf{G}(O_{\infty})\hat{}=\mathbf{G}(O_{\infty})
,
we finally get a simplicial involution on
\mathcal{BT}
, also termed
(~)\hat{}
. It stabilizes the data
\mathbf{T}
,
\mathbf{B}
and
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{W}
, and its effect on
\mathbf{T}
is

(8.1.4)

\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&t_{r}\end{pmatrix}\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}t_{r}^{-1}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&t_{1}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Correspondingly, its effect on
\mathcal{A}(\mathds{Z})\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\{[L_{\mathbf{n}}]\mid\mathbf{n}\in\mathds{Z}^{r}\}
(recall the notation from (
1.3.5
) and (
1.3.6
)) is

(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})\longmapsto({-}n_{r},\dots,{-}n_{1}),

which in normalized coordinates is

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r-1},0)

\displaystyle\mapsto\text{class of }(n_{1}-n_{r},n_{1}-n_{r-1},\dots,n_{1}-n_{1})

(8.1.5)

\displaystyle=(n_{1},n_{1}-n_{r-1},\dots,n_{1}-n_{2},0)=\vcentcolon\mathbf{n}\hat{}.

One easily checks that for the simple roots
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}
of
\Phi
the rule
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}(\mathbf{n}\hat{})=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{r-i}(\mathbf{n})
holds, or briefly

(8.1.6)

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}\hat{}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{r-i}.

That is,
(\,.\,)\hat{}
corresponds to the inversion of the Dynkin diagram
\cdots
of type
A_{r-1}
of
\Phi
.

Now we can state and prove the symmetry property of
\mathcal{W}(d,k)
with respect to
(\,.\,)\hat{}
.

Theorem 8.2
:

Let
d
be a natural number and

. For
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{W}(\mathds{Z})

(8.2.1)

\mathcal{W}(d,k)\hat{}=\mathcal{W}(d,rd-k)
and, since
(\,.\,)\hat{}

\mathcal{BT}

\mathcal{BT}(d,k)\hat{}=\mathcal{BT}(d,rd-k)

\mathcal{A}(d,k)\hat{}=\mathcal{A}(d,rd-k)

(i)

If

(ii)

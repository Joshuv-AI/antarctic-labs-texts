Learning With Errors based Dynamic Encryption that Discloses Residue Signal for Anomaly Detection
Yeongjun Jang, Joowon Lee, Junsoo Kim, Hyungbo Shim

Abstract
Anomaly detection is a protocol that detects integrity attacks on control systems by comparing the residue signal with a threshold. Implementing anomaly detection on encrypted control systems has been a challenge because it is hard to detect an anomaly from the encrypted residue signal without the secret key. In this paper, we propose a dynamic encryption scheme for a linear system that automatically discloses the residue signal. The initial state and the input are encrypted based on the zero-dynamics of the system, so that the effect of encryption on the residue signal remains identically zero. The proposed scheme is shown to be secure in the sense that no other information than the residue signal is disclosed. Furthermore, we demonstrate a method of utilizing the disclosed residue signal to operate an observer-based controller over encrypted data for an infinite time horizon without re-encryption.

II-A

Learning With Errors (LWE) based Encryption

We make use of the encryption scheme of
[
13
]
, where the additively homomorphic property is simply exploited.
The cryptosystem parameters and the secret key are first chosen;
the set
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}=\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\}
is the space of messages with some modulus
q
, the secret key is denoted by a column vector
\mathrm{sk}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{N}
of length

determining the security level, and
\sigma
is the standard deviation for the (discrete) Gaussian distribution
N(0,\sigma)
.
For a (column) vector
\mathrm{v}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{n}
of an
n
-dimensional message, the encryption
\mathsf{Enc}
is defined by

\mathsf{Enc}(\mathrm{v}):=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{v}+\mathrm{A}\cdot\mathrm{sk}+\mathrm{e},&\!\!\mathrm{A}\end{bmatrix}\!\!\mod q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{{n\times(N+1)}},

(1)

where
\mathrm{A}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{n\times N}
is a random matrix consisting of entries sampled from the uniform distribution over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
, and
\mathrm{e}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is an error vector
whose elements are sampled from
N(0,\sigma)
.
The decryption of a ciphertext
\mathbf{c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{{n\times(N+1)}}
is defined by

\mathsf{Dec}(\mathbf{c}):=\mathbf{c}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-\mathrm{sk}\end{bmatrix}\!\!\mod q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{n},

thus recovering an
n
-dimensional message
\mathrm{v}
as

\displaystyle\mathsf{Dec}(\mathsf{Enc}(\mathrm{v}))

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{v}+\mathrm{A}\cdot\mathrm{sk}+\mathrm{e},&\!\!\mathrm{A}\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-\mathrm{sk}\end{bmatrix}\!\!\mod q

(2)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{v}+\mathrm{e}\!\!\mod q,

along with the error.
In practice, the message is scaled by a large number, so that the effect of the error
\mathrm{e}
is made far smaller than the message.

The encryption is additively homomorphic.
By construction, a matrix
\mathrm{K}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{m\times n}
can be multiplied with
\mathsf{Enc}(\mathrm{v})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{{n\times(N+1)}}
, resulting in the
m
-dimensional ciphertext

\mathrm{K}\cdot\mathsf{Enc}(\mathrm{v})\!\!\mod q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{{m\times(N+1)}}.

Then, it is easily verified that

\mathsf{Dec}(\mathrm{K}\cdot\mathsf{Enc}(\mathrm{v}))=\mathrm{K}\cdot(\mathrm{v}+\mathrm{e})\!\!\mod q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{m},

where the multiplication by
\mathrm{K}
is applied to both the message
\mathrm{v}
and the error
\mathrm{e}
.

Finally, the LWE problem
[
13
]
ensures security given that the secret key dimension
N
is chosen sufficiently large.
Roughly, it is “hard” to distinguish the encryption of a given message from that of a random number in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
, without knowing the secret key.

Thus, (

∎

III-B

Proposed Encryption Scheme

Finally, we propose the encryption scheme for the system (
II-B
) by making use of its zero-dynamics.
Recall that (

\mathrm{x}_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{n}
and
\mathrm{y}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
. With respect to the conventional encryption (
1

\displaystyle\mathsf{Enc}(\mathrm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{x}_{0}+\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}},&\!\!\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{x}}\end{bmatrix}\!\!\mod q,

(24a)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Enc}(\mathrm{y}(t))

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{y}(t)+\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(t),&\!\!\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)\end{bmatrix}\!\!\mod q,\quad\forall t\geq 0,

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}&:=\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{x}}\cdot\mathrm{sk}+\mathrm{e}_{\mathrm{x}}\!\!\mod q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{n},\\

(24b)

where each element of

and

are sampled respectively from the uniform distribution over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
and
N(0,\sigma)
.
Likewise, at each
t\geq 0
, the elements of
\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{1\times N}
are sampled from the uniform distribution over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
, and
\mathrm{e}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
is from
N(0,\sigma)
.

Thanks to linearity, the first columns of the encrypted messages in (
II-B
) separately obey the dynamics of (
6
), with their respective initial state and input.
In other words, one can observe that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{x}_{0}+\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}},\{\mathrm{y}(\tau)+\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(\tau)\}_{\tau=0}^{t})

(25)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{x}_{0},\{\mathrm{y}(\tau)\}_{\tau=0}^{t})+\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}},\{\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(\tau)\}_{\tau=0}^{t})\!\!\mod q

is the first element of the encrypted output
\mathbf{r}(t)
,
which contains the information of the residue
\mathrm{r}(t)
in (
7
).

We have investigated in Section
III-A
that disclosing the information of
\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}},\{\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)\}_{\tau=0}^{t})
is equivalent to disclosing
the portion of the initial state
\mathrm{T}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}\!\!\mod q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{\nu}
and
the output
\mathrm{B}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
of the system

\displaystyle\mathrm{z}(t+1)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{F}_{1}\mathrm{z}(t)\!\!\mod q,\quad\mathrm{z}(0)=\mathrm{T}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}\!\!\mod q,

(26)

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)+\mathrm{g}^{-1}\psi\mathrm{z}(t)\!\!\mod q,

which stems from the zero-dynamics (
21
) and (
22
), in case of
\nu\geq 1
.
When
\nu=0
, only the output
\mathrm{B}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)
is disclosed.

Based on this observation, we modify (
24
) as

\displaystyle\mathsf{Enc}_{0}^{\mathrm{x}}(\mathrm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle:=\left[\mathrm{x}_{0}+\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}-\mathrm{V}_{2}\mathrm{T}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}},\,\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{x}},\,\mathrm{V}_{2}\mathrm{T}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}\right]\!\!\mod q

\displaystyle\qquad\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{n\times(N+2)},

(27)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Enc}_{t}^{\mathrm{y}}\!(\mathrm{y}(t))

\displaystyle:=\left[\mathrm{y}(t)+\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)-\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}^{\prime}(t),\,\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{y}},\,\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}^{\prime}(t)\right]\!\!\mod q

\displaystyle\qquad\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{1\times(N+2)},\qquad\forall t\geq 0,

where
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}^{\prime}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
is obtained according to (
26
) from
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}
and
\{\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(\tau)\}_{\tau=0}^{t}
.
The proposed scheme (
III-B
) discloses
\mathrm{V}_{2}\mathrm{T}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}
and
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}^{\prime}(t)
as the last columns of the encrypted messages.
However, the message parts
\mathrm{x}_{0}
and
\mathrm{y}(t)
are concealed by
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}-\mathrm{V}_{2}\mathrm{T}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}
and
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)-\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}^{\prime}(t)
, respectively.
The rationale behind this modification is to decompose
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}
and
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)
in (
24

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}-\mathrm{V}_{2}\mathrm{T}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}\right)+\mathrm{V}_{2}\mathrm{T}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)-\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}^{\prime}(t)\right)+\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}^{\prime}(t).

Since the encrypted messages have
N+2
columns, the decryption
\mathsf{Dec}
is also modified with respect to a ciphertext
\mathbf{c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{m\times(N+2)}
, by

\mathsf{Dec}^{\prime}(\mathbf{c}):=\mathbf{c}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}1,&\!\!-\mathrm{sk}^{\top},&\!\!1\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\!\!\mod q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{m}.

(28)

By definition, the modified encryptions (
III-B
) share the correctness and the additively homomorphic property of LWE based encryption, as shown in the following proposition.

Proposition 3
.

The proposed encryption scheme (
III-B
) and (
28
) satisfy the following:

1.

Given the encrypted messages (
24
),

\displaystyle\mathsf{Dec}^{\prime}(\mathsf{Enc}_{0}^{\mathrm{x}}(\mathrm{x}_{0}))

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Dec}(\mathsf{Enc}(\mathrm{x}_{0})),

\displaystyle\mathsf{Dec}^{\prime}(\mathsf{Enc}_{t}^{\mathrm{y}}(\mathrm{y}(t)))

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Dec}(\mathsf{Enc}(\mathrm{y}(t))),\quad\forall t\geq 0.

2.

Given
\mathbf{c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{m\times(N+1)}
and
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{m\times(N+2)}
,

\displaystyle\text{if }\mathsf{Dec}^{\prime}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})=\mathsf{Dec}(\mathbf{c})\text{, then }\mathsf{Dec}^{\prime}(\mathrm{K}\cdot\mathbf{c}^{\prime})=\mathsf{Dec}(\mathrm{K}\cdot\mathbf{c})

for any
\mathrm{K}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{l\times m}
.

\square

Now, let
\mathsf{Enc}(\mathrm{x}_{0})
and
\mathsf{Enc}(\mathrm{y}(t))
of the system (
II-B
) be replaced with the modified encrypted messages

and

in (

As in (

), the first element of the encrypted residue output

is obtained as

\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{x}_{0}\!+\!(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}\!-\!\mathrm{V}_{2}\mathrm{T}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}),\{\mathrm{y}(\tau)\!+\!(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(\tau)\!-\!\mathrm{B}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{y}}(\tau))\}_{\tau=0}^{t})\!\!\!

(29)

in this case.

) remains identical to
\mathrm{r}(t)
for all

, thus disclosing the residue signal.
It is implied that an adversary collecting the modified encrypted messages (
III-B
) learns nothing but the information of the disclosed residue signal, in terms of security.

Theorem 1
.

Given the encrypted messages (
24
), there exist
\mathrm{v}_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{\nu}

\mathrm{y}^{\prime}(\cdot):{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}

(30)

for all

, which are uniquely determined by

\mathrm{v}_{0}=\mathrm{T}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}\!\!\mod q,\quad\mathrm{y}^{\prime}(t)=\mathrm{B}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{y}}(t),\quad\forall t\geq 0.

(31)

\square

Proof.

By the linearity of
\mathcal{S}
,
(
30
) is equivalent to

\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}-\mathrm{V}_{2}\mathrm{v}_{0},\{\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}(\tau)-\mathrm{y}^{\prime}(\tau)\}_{\tau=0}^{t})\equiv 0.

(32)

By Lemmas
1
and

, (

), which concludes the proof.
∎

The implication of Theorem

is provided;

), the residue
\mathrm{r}(t)
is obtained by means of (

and

Conversely, given the residue

and the conventional encrypted messages (

.
Therefore, despite the disclosure of the residue, the proposed encryption scheme is secure in the sense that an adversary collecting the modified encrypted messages cannot learn any information other than the disclosed residue signal, as long as the conventional encryption scheme is secure.

Remark 2
.

In terms of computational effort,

Running the system (

The order

\square

IV
Application to Observer-based Controller

In this section, the proposed encryption scheme is applied to an observer-based controller over
{\mathbb{R}}
,
thereby disclosing the residue signal.
To encrypt a dynamic controller,
it is well known that the state matrix of the controller needs to be an integer matrix
[
16
]
.
Thus, we propose a method to convert the state matrix of a given observer-based controller into an integer matrix
by utilizing the disclosed residue signal.
Then, the performance of the encrypted controller is discussed.

Consider a linear discrete-time single-input single-output plant written by

\begin{split}x(t+1)&=Ax(t)+Bu(t),\quad x(0)=x_{0},\\
y(t)&=Cx(t),\end{split}

(33)

where
x(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
u(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
y(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are the state, the input, the output, and the initial state, respectively.
It is assumed that the plant (
33
) is controllable and observable.
Suppose an observer-based controller that stabilizes the plant (
33
) has been designed as

\displaystyle\hat{x}(t+1)

\displaystyle=\left(A+BK-LC\right)\hat{x}(t)+Ly(t),\quad\hat{x}(0)=\hat{x}_{0},

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=K\hat{x}(t),

(34)

\displaystyle r(t)

\displaystyle=y(t)-C\hat{x}(t),

where
\hat{x}(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the estimate of
x(t)
with the initial value
\hat{x}_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
r(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
is the residue signal, and
K\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1\times n}
and
L\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times 1}
are the gains such that
the closed-loop system of (
33
) and (
IV
) is stable.

We first present a method of converting the controller (
IV
) to have an integer state matrix.
Let us rewrite the state dynamics of the controller as

\hat{x}(t+1)=F\hat{x}(t)+\left(L-R\right)y(t)+Rr(t),

(35)

where
F:=A+BK-LC+RC
can be considered as the state matrix regarding
r(t)
as a fed-back input with an input matrix
R\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Motivated by the results of
[
14
]
, we aim to transform
F
to an integer matrix by assigning the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of
F
to be integers.
To do so, the following assumption is made.

Assumption 1
.

The pair
(A+BK,C)
is observable.

\square

By Assumption
1
and
[
14
, Lemma 1]
, there exist
R\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times 1}
and an inveritble matrix
T\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
such that

TFT^{-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n\times n}
.
Therefore, the coordinate transformation by the matrix
T
yields a state matrix consisting of integers.

Next, the parameters of the transformed controller are converted to integer matrices over the space of messages
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
.
To this end, a scaling parameter
1/\mathsf{s}\in{\mathbb{N}}
is introduced to convert the control parameters other than the state matrix.
Then, all the integer matrices are mapped to the following matrices over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}

by taking the modulo operation:

\displaystyle\mathrm{F}

\displaystyle:=TFT^{-1}\!\!\mod q,

\displaystyle\mathrm{G}

\displaystyle:=\left\lceil\frac{T(L\!-\!R)}{\mathsf{s}}\right\rfloor\!\!\!\mod q,

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}

\displaystyle:=\left\lceil\frac{-CT^{-1}}{\mathsf{s}}\right\rfloor\!\!\mod q,

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\mathsf{s}^{2}}\!\!\mod q,

(36)

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}

\displaystyle:=\left\lceil\frac{TR}{\mathsf{s}}\right\rfloor\!\!\mod q,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}

\displaystyle:=\left\lceil\frac{KT^{-1}}{\mathsf{s}}\right\rfloor\!\!\mod q.

Now, we encrypt the given controller (
IV
) as follows;
the transformed initial state
T\hat{x}_{0}
is scaled up and quantized with a step size
\mathsf{r}>0
,
and then preprocessed for encryption as

\mathrm{x}_{0}:=\frac{1}{\mathsf{L}}\cdot\left\lceil\frac{T\hat{x}_{0}}{\mathsf{r}\mathsf{s}}\right\rfloor\mod q,

where
1/\mathsf{L}\in{\mathbb{N}}
is a scaling factor that diminishes the effect of the error
\mathrm{e}
in (
2
).
Subsequently, it is encrypted offline by the proposed scheme (
III-B
).
While online,
the plant output
y(t)
is quantized and scaled up as

\mathrm{y}(t):=\frac{1}{\mathsf{L}}\cdot\left\lceil\frac{y(t)}{\mathsf{r}}\right\rfloor\!\!\mod q,

and encrypted as in (
III-B
).
It is then sent to the encrypted controller, written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}(t+1)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{F}\cdot\mathbf{x}(t)+\mathrm{G}\cdot\mathsf{Enc}_{t}^{\mathrm{y}}\left(\mathrm{y}(t)\right)+\mathrm{R}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{1}(t)\!\!\mod q,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{P}\cdot\mathbf{x}(t)\!\!\mod q,

(37)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{H}\cdot\mathbf{x}(t)+\mathrm{J}\cdot\mathsf{Enc}_{t}^{\mathrm{y}}\left(\mathrm{y}(t)\right)\!\!\mod q,

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}(0)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Enc}_{0}^{\mathrm{x}}\left(\mathrm{x}_{0}\right),

where

\mathbf{x}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{n\times(N+2)}
is the state,

\mathbf{u}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{1\times(N+2)}
is the output,

\mathbf{r}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{1\times(N+2)}
is the residue signal,
and
\mathbf{r}_{1}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{1\times(N+2)}
is the fed-back input as in (
35
) that is defined from the first element
\mathrm{r}_{1}(t)
of the row vector
\mathbf{r}(t)
, as

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{1}(t):=\begin{bmatrix}{\left\lceil\mathsf{s}^{2}\cdot\mathrm{r}_{1}(t)\right\rfloor},&\!\!0_{1\times(N+1)}\end{bmatrix}\!\!\mod q.

Implementing an anomaly detector to the proposed encrypted controller (
IV
)
is efficient in the sense that
the residue signal can be restored from
\mathrm{r}_{1}(t)
without decryption, as

r_{1}(t):=\mathsf{L}\mathsf{r}\mathsf{s}^{2}\cdot\left(\mathrm{r}_{1}(t)-\left\lfloor\frac{\mathrm{r}_{1}(t)+\frac{q}{2}}{q}\right\rfloor q\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(38)

Hence, the comparison of the residue signal with a threshold need not be carried out over encrypted data.
Finally, the output
\mathbf{u}(t)
of the encrypted controller is transmitted to the plant, decrypted, and then scaled down to obtain the plant input
u(t)
, as

u(t):=\mathsf{L}\mathsf{r}\mathsf{s}^{2}\cdot\left(\mathsf{Dec}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u}(t))-\left\lfloor\frac{\mathsf{Dec}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u}(t))+\frac{q}{2}}{q}\right\rfloor q\right).

(39)

It is worth noting that although the encrypted controller (
IV
) has an additional term
\mathrm{R}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{1}(t)
in the state dynamics,
the portions of
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}-\mathrm{V}_{2}\mathrm{T}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}
and
\mathrm{B}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{y}}(t)
of the modified encryptions (
III-B
) obey the dynamics of (
6
),
with the matrices
\mathrm{F}
,
\mathrm{G}
,
\mathrm{H}
and
\mathrm{J}
defined as in (
IV
).
Thus, the modified encryptor computes
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{y}}^{\prime}(t)
as the output of the system (
26
), where the matrices are defined as in (
23
) because
\mathrm{J}\neq 0
. Since
\mathrm{T}_{1}=I_{n}
in this case, the last column
\mathrm{V}_{2}\mathrm{T}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{x}}
of
\mathsf{Enc}_{0}^{\mathrm{x}}(\mathrm{x}_{0})
is a zero vector.

The following theorem states that the difference between the residue signals from the encrypted and the given controller can be made arbitrarily small, with an appropriate choice of the parameters
1/\mathsf{r}
,
1/\mathsf{s}
, and
1/\mathsf{L}
, and
q
.

Theorem 2
.

Under Assumption
1
,
there exist parameters
\mathsf{r}>0
,
1/\mathsf{s}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
1/\mathsf{L}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and a prime number
q
such that
for given
\epsilon>0
,
the residue signal (
38
) in the encrypted controller (
IV
) satisfies

|r(t)-r_{1}(t)|\leq\epsilon

for all
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.

\square

Proof.

It can be derived from Theorem

Remark 3
.

The proposed encrypted controller is capable of operating for an infinite time horizon without re-encryption, unlike the previous result

.
The re-encryption requires an additional communication link between the actuator and the encrypted controller, since the controller output is decrypted, re-scaled, encrypted, and then transmitted back to the controller.
Instead, we utilized the disclosed residue signal to convert the state matrix of the given controller into an integer matrix,

Therefore, the encrypted control system is implemented without the additional communication link and the decryption device at the actuator,

V
Conclusion

We have proposed a dynamic encryption scheme that automatically discloses the residue signal, thus enabling a computing device without the secret key to directly and efficiently detect an anomaly.
To maintain the effect of encryption on the residue signal to remain identically zero, the proposed scheme exploits the zero-dynamics of the system to generate the encryptions of the initial state and the input.
Specific to an observer-based controller, we have demonstrated a method of utilizing the disclosed residue signal to operate the system over encrypted data for an infinite time horizon without re-encryption.

Computing H 2 H^{2} -conforming finite element approximations without having to implement C 1 C^{1} -elements Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Mark Ainsworth, Charles Parker

Abstract
We develop a method to compute the H 2 H^{2} -conforming finite element approximation to planar fourth order elliptic problems without having to implement C 1 C^{1} elements. The algorithm consists of replacing the original H 2 H^{2} -conforming scheme with pre-processing and post-processing steps that require only an H 1 H^{1} -conforming Poisson type solve and an inner Stokes-like problem that again only requires at most H 1 H^{1} -conformity. We then demonstrate the method applied to the Morgan-Scott elements with three numerical examples.

Introduction

Conforming Galerkin finite element schemes inherit the stability properties of the underlying continuous problem. As a consequence, they provide stable approximations to a range of problem, such as structural mechanics applications, and deliver optimal approximations measured in an energy norm. Classical finite element texts
[
11
]
abound with examples of elements that provide
H^{1}
-conforming schemes (for second order problems such as the Lamé-Navier equations of linear elasticity) and, to a lesser extent,
H^{2}
-conforming schemes (for fourth order problems such as the Kirchhoff plate).

Nevertheless, while
H^{1}
-conforming schemes are routinely implemented in finite element packages,
H^{2}
-conforming schemes are a comparative rarity. For example, Firedrake
[
22
]
and scikit-fem
[
21
]
only offer the lowest order (degree five) Argyris element and FreeFEM
[
23
]
provides only the Hsieh-Clough-Tocher (HCT) element, while other major packages offer no capability for
H^{2}
-conforming approximation at all. Part of the reason for the
H^{1}
-conforming case discrepancy stems from the fact that
H^{2}
-conforming elements require derivative degrees of freedom that have more complicated transformation properties compared with pointwise values needed for the
H^{1}
-conforming case. This also means that
H^{2}
-conforming elements require higher order basis functions than their
H^{1}
-conforming counterparts.

On the theoretical side too, there seems to be a desire to avoid
C^{1}
-conforming elements through the use of mixed, discontinuous Galerkin, or non-conforming finite element schemes, etc. that can be implemented using more readily available elements. Although such schemes can often be shown to be effective, they come at a price such as sacrificing the stability of the original problem in favor of an indefinite mixed finite element scheme or require the selection of appropriate penalty parameters. Either way, one ends up with a non-conforming approximation of the original
H^{2}
-conforming problem. While such artifacts need not present a problem
per se
in the context of approximating a given equation, issues can arise when the schemes form part of larger multi-physics applications where coupling arises through terms that involve post-processing of what should be
H^{2}
-conforming variables or where the stability of the continuous problem is lost through the use of a mixed or non-conforming scheme.

The principled approach to these issues is to insist on using
H^{2}
-conforming schemes that preserve the conformity, stability, and structure of the original problem. However, such a position fails to recognize that there are non-trivial difficulties in implementing such schemes that has fueled the large body of research on avoiding
H^{2}
-conformity, quite apart from the fact that few codes cater for higher order smoothness.

The current work seeks the best of both worlds. Namely,
we show how to compute the actual
H^{2}
-conforming approximation itself without having to implement
C^{1}
-conforming elements
using only approximation schemes that are routinely available in existing finite element packages. The attractions of such an approach are clear: one preserves the stability and conformity of the original formulation while enjoying the possibility to utilize existing software packages. Of course, there is no free lunch: our scheme replaces the original
H^{2}
-conforming scheme by a sequence of pre-processing and
post-processing steps that require only an
H^{1}
-conforming Poisson type solve, combined with an inner Stokes-like problem that again only requires at most
H^{1}
-conformity.

After describing the problem setting in
section

2
, we explain the method first in a simplified setting in
section

3
before turning to the general case in
section

4
. Implementation aspects of the Stokes-like solve are addressed in
section

5
where we present several numerical examples. Concluding remarks appear in
section

6
.

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=\frac{12z}{\tau^{3}}\bm{M}(\bm{\theta})=\frac{12z}{\tau^{3}}\bm{M}(\grad w).

The transverse displacement of the plate is governed by a problem of the form

1

\displaystyle a(\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi})=D\left[(1-\nu)(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{\theta}),\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{\psi}))+\nu(\dive\bm{\theta},\dive\bm{\psi})\right]\qquad\forall\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega).

\displaystyle a(\grad w,\grad w)\geq C\|\bm{\varepsilon}(\grad w)\|^{2}\geq C\|\grad w\|_{1}^{2}\geq C\|w\|_{2}^{2}\qquad\forall w\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

\displaystyle w_{X}\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}:\qquad a(\grad w_{X},\grad v)=F(v)\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}.

(9)

The use of a Galerkin scheme based on a

-conforming finite element space

means problem
eq.

\displaystyle\|w-w_{X}\|_{2}\leq\frac{M}{\alpha}\inf_{v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}}\|w-v\|_{2}.

However, the spaces

-continuous spaces, even for lower polynomial orders

Our objective here is to seek an implementation scheme to compute

which avoids the need to construct a
C^{1}
-conforming basis for the space
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
and which leads itself to implementation using standard packages.

3
The case
\bar{\Gamma}_{c}\cup\bar{\Gamma}_{s}
is connected

In order to illustrate the basic idea, we first present a simple algorithm to compute the solution of
eq.

9
in the simplified setting when the portion of the boundary
\Gamma_{cs}:=\bar{\Gamma}_{c}\cup\bar{\Gamma}_{s}
on which
u|_{\Gamma_{cs}}=0
is connected. For definiteness, the space
\mathbb{W}=W^{p}
is taken to be the Morgan-Scott space
[
24
]
of degree
p
:

\displaystyle W^{p}

\displaystyle:=\{v\in C^{1}(\Omega):v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\}\subset H^{2}(\Omega),

(11)

where the degree
p
is usually taken to be at least fifth order (although lower orders can also be considered). Later, we consider other choices for
\mathbb{W}\subset H^{2}(\Omega)
, but we start with the Morgan-Scott space which is generally regarded as particularly challenging to implement since, in addition to the
H^{2}
-conformity condition, difficulties can also arise from the topology of the mesh
[
24
]
.

We present a finite element scheme that still produces the unique
C^{1}
conforming approximation defined by
eq.

9
that one could obtain by implementing the space
W^{p}
, yet avoids both the need to generate a basis for the
C^{1}
space and complications arising from mesh topology. The key idea is to view the Morgan-Scott space as an
H^{2}
-conforming subspace of a corresponding
H^{1}
-conforming space
\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}
defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}:=\{v\in C(\Omega):v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\text{ and }v|_{\Gamma_{cs}}=0\}.

(12)

The fact that
\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}
is only
H^{1}(\Omega)
-conforming means that standard packages can be used to compute the solution
\tilde{z}_{p}\in\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}
of the problem:

\displaystyle(\grad\tilde{z}_{p},\grad\tilde{v})=F(\tilde{v})\qquad\forall\tilde{v}\in\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p},

(13)

where for simplicity henceforth, we assume that
F
is well-defined on
\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}
. Thanks to the inclusion
\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}\subset W_{\Gamma}^{p}:=W^{p}\cap H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
,
eq.

9
may then be rewritten in the form:

\displaystyle a(\grad w_{p},\grad v)=(\grad\tilde{z}_{p},\grad v)\qquad\forall v\in W_{\Gamma}^{p},

(14)

where we use the notation
w_{p}:=w_{X}
to highlight the dependence on
p
. Observing that
eq.

14
only involves gradients of functions in
W_{\Gamma}^{p}
, it is tempting to instead pose
eq.

14
over the image space
\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}
and seek
\bm{\theta}_{p-1}=\grad w_{p}
which, in view of
eq.

14
, must satisfy

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}_{p-1}\in\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}:\qquad a(\bm{\theta}_{p-1},\bm{\psi})=(\grad\tilde{z}_{p},\bm{\psi})\qquad\forall\bm{\psi}\in\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}.

(15)

An immediate benefit of this approach is that
\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}
is a conforming subspace of

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{\bm{\theta}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega):\bm{\theta}|_{\Gamma_{c}}=\bm{0}\text{ and }\bm{t}\cdot\bm{\theta}|_{\Gamma_{s}}=0\},

(16)

for which only
\bm{H}^{1}
-conformity is required. The snag in working with the space
\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}
is that, while the continuity requirements are weakened, the structure of the space means that one has to find a basis consisting of curl-free functions. Such a basis can be constructed by taking the gradients of a basis for the Morgan-Scott space which, of course, defeats the object. Instead, we relax the curl-free constraint and seek
\bm{\theta}_{p-1}
in the larger subspace

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}:=\{\bm{\theta}\in\bm{\Theta}_{\Gamma}(\Omega):\bm{\theta}|_{K}\in[\mathcal{P}_{p-1}(K)]^{2}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\}

(17)

for which it is straightforward to construct a basis: only
\bm{C}^{0}
-continuity is required. However, relaxing the curl-free constraint comes at the expense of introducing an indeterminancy in
\bm{\theta}_{p-1}
stemming from the fact that
\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}\neq\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}
along with the fact that
eq.

15
does not determine the components of
\bm{\theta}_{p-1}\in\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}/\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}
. Still undeterred, we seek additional conditions on
\bm{\theta}_{p-1}
that remove this non-uniqueness.

To this end, we observe that the spaces
W^{p}_{\Gamma}
and
\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}
form part of an
exact sequence
(see
lemma

4
):

\displaystyle 0\xrightarrow{\ \ \ \subset\ \ \ }W^{p}_{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{\ \ \ \grad\ \ \ }\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{\ \ \rot\ \ }\rot\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{\ \ \ 0\ \ \ }0,

(18)

where
\rot\bm{\theta}=\partial_{x}\theta_{2}-\partial_{y}\theta_{1}
. While
\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}
is a proper subspace of
\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}
, the sequence
eq.

18
is
exact
in the sense the kernel of each operator is the range of the preceding operator; i.e. if
\bm{\theta}\in\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}
satisfies
\rot\bm{\theta}\equiv 0
, then
\bm{\theta}=\grad w
for some
w\in W^{p}_{\Gamma}
. In other words, the following characterization holds:

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}_{p-1}\in\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}\iff\rot\bm{\theta}_{p-1}\equiv 0.

(19)

The significance of
eq.

19
is that the condition
\bm{\theta}_{p-1}\in\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}
can be enforced by augmenting
eq.

15
with the additional, independent, condition that
\rot\bm{\theta}_{p-1}\equiv 0
. This is most conveniently achieved through the use of a Lagrange multiplier
r_{p-2}\in\rot\bm{\Theta}_{\Gamma}^{p-1}
, which gives rise to the following variational problem: Find
(\bm{\theta}_{p-1},r_{p-2})\in\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}\times\rot\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}
such that

\displaystyle a(\bm{\theta}_{p-1},\bm{\psi})+(\rot\bm{\psi},r_{p-2})

\displaystyle=(\grad\tilde{z}_{p},\bm{\psi})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\psi}\in\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma},

(20a)

\displaystyle(\rot\bm{\theta}_{p-1},s)

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall s\in\rot\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}.

(20b)

Lemma

1
below states that the system
eq.

20
uniquely determines
\bm{\theta}_{p-1}
, and guarantees that
\bm{\theta}_{p-1}=\grad w_{p}
while only requiring the use of
\bm{C}^{0}
-continuous finite element spaces. Of course, the reader may quite reasonably object that
eq.

20
only enables us to obtain the
gradient
of the Morgan-Scott approximation
w_{p}
of the displacement.

Remarkably, the
H^{2}
-conforming approximation to the actual displacement
w_{p}\in W_{\Gamma}^{p}\subset\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}
can be recovered via a post-processing step that again only involves the
H^{1}
-conforming subspace

\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}
(rather than the
H^{2}
-conforming space
W_{\Gamma}^{p}
) as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{p}\in\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}:\qquad(\grad\tilde{w}_{p},\grad v)=(\bm{\theta}_{p-1},\grad v)\qquad\forall v\in\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}.

(21)

The following result confirms that the displacement
\tilde{w}_{p}\in\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}
computed from the
H^{1}
-conforming scheme
eq.

21
is well-defined and
coincides with the
H^{2}
-conforming Morgan-Scott displacement
w_{p}
defined by
eq.

9
; i.e.
w_{p}=\tilde{w}_{p}
:

Lemma 1
.

Let
W_{\Gamma}^{p}
,
\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}
, and
\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}
be given by
eqs.

11
,
12
, and
17
, respectively, and let
\Gamma_{cs}
be connected. Then, there exists unique
(\tilde{z}_{p},\bm{\theta}_{p-1},r_{p-2},\tilde{w}_{p})\in\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}\times\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}\times\rot\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}\times\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}
satisfying
eqs.

13
,
20
, and
21
. Moreover,
\grad\tilde{w}_{p}=\bm{\theta}_{p-1}
and
\tilde{w}_{p}=w_{p}
, where
w_{p}
is defined by
eq.

9
.

Lemma

1
is a consequence of
theorem

3
and
lemma

4
which we discuss in the next section.

In summary, when
\Gamma_{cs}
is connected, the
H^{2}
-conforming Morgan-Scott finite element approximation
eq.

9
can be computed by solving a sequence of three standard finite element problems:

1.

Pre-process the data
F
by computing the
H^{1}
-conforming Riesz representer of the source data on
\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}

eq.

13
.

2.

Solve a Stokes-like problem using the mixed Scott-Vogelius element
[
27
,
28
]

\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}\times\rot\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}

eq.

20
.

3.

Post-process the gradient
\bm{\theta}_{p-1}
by computing an
H^{1}
-conforming elliptic projection on
\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}^{p}

eq.

21
.

Crucially, steps 1-3 only require
H^{1}
-conforming finite element spaces that are commonly available in software packages. The limitation of the analysis to
\Gamma_{cs}
connected here is unduly restrictive. In the next section, we modify the basic approach in this section to accommodate more general boundary conditions.

4
General boundary conditions

The foregoing discussion dealt with the case when
\Gamma_{cs}=\bar{\Gamma}_{c}\cup\bar{\Gamma}_{s}
is simply-connected for reasons that only become in the general case in which
\Gamma_{cs}=\cup_{i=1}^{N}\Gamma_{cs}^{(i)}
consists of
N\geq 1
connected components. If
\mathbb{W}
is again taken to be the Morgan-Scott space
W^{p}
as in the previous section, then the same arguments of the previous section again show that the quantity
\bm{\theta}_{p-1}\in\bm{G}^{p-1}_{\Gamma}
must satisfy

\displaystyle\rot\bm{\theta}_{p-1}\equiv 0\quad\text{and}\quad a(\bm{\theta}_{p-1},\bm{\psi})=(\grad\tilde{z}_{p},\bm{\psi})\qquad\forall\bm{\psi}\in\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}.

(22)

When
\Gamma_{cs}
consists of a single connected component (
N=1
), the exactness of the sequence
eq.

18
meant that the reverse implication in
eq.

19
holds so that conditions
eq.

22
uniquely determine
\bm{\theta}_{p-1}
. However, when
\Gamma_{cs}
is not connected (
N>1
), the corresponding implication is no longer valid:

\displaystyle\rot\bm{\theta}_{p-1}\equiv 0\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\implies}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\implies}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\implies}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\implies}}}\bm{\theta}_{p-1}\in\grad W_{\Gamma}^{p}.

In other words,
\bm{\Theta}_{\Gamma}^{p-1}
contains rot-free fields that cannot be written as the gradient of a potential in
W_{\Gamma}^{p}
and, as a consequence,
lemma

1
does not extend to the case when
\Gamma_{cs}
is not connected. We justify this claim below, but it is important to note that this issue is not specific to the Morgan-Scott space. In this section, we aim to extend the basic idea used in
section

3
where (a)
\Gamma_{cs}
need not be simply-connected and (b)
\mathbb{W}
can be chosen to be a general
H^{2}
-conforming finite element space.

4.1
General Theory

Let
\mathbb{W}\subseteq H^{2}(\Omega)
be any
H^{2}
-conforming subspace and set
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}:=\mathbb{W}\cap H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
. Our objective is again to compute the
H^{2}
-conforming approximation
w_{X}
defined in
eq.

9
while avoiding having to implement the space
\mathbb{W}
directly. Let
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\subseteq\bm{\Theta}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
denote any conforming subspace such that
\grad\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}\subseteq\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
and which satisfies the following property:

(A1)

Given
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}
, there exists
v\in\mathbb{W}
satisfying

\displaystyle v|_{\Gamma_{cs}^{(i)}}=\mu_{i},\qquad 1\leq i\leq N,\qquad\partial_{n}v|_{\Gamma}=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\grad v\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}.

(23)

With this notation, the generalized version of
eq.

13
reads

\displaystyle\tilde{z}_{X}\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}:\qquad(\grad\tilde{z}_{X},\grad\tilde{v})=F(\tilde{v})\qquad\forall\tilde{v}\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma},

(24)

where
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
is chosen to be any space satisfying the condition

(A2)

\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}\subseteq\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\subset H^{1}(\Omega).

As in the previous section, we will again assume that
F
is well-defined on
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
. Assumption
(A2)
offers the flexibility to choose

Let

35

[
1
,
3

28
]

An alternative is to use the iterated penalty method
[
8
,
16
,
19
]
which enables one to compute the solution to
eq.

36
without the need for a basis for the space
\rot\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
. Specifically we define the following bilinear forms on
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
for a given
\lambda\geq 0
:

\displaystyle b(\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi})

\displaystyle:=(\rot\bm{\theta},\rot\bm{\psi})+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(\bm{t}\cdot\bm{\theta},1)_{\Gamma_{f}^{(i)}}(\bm{t}\cdot\bm{\psi},1)_{\Gamma_{f}^{(i)}},

(41a)

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi})

\displaystyle:=a(\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi})+\lambda b(\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi}).

(41b)

Given
\lambda\geq 0
and
\bm{\phi}^{0}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
, the iterated penalty method for
eq.

36
is as follows: For
n=0,1,\ldots
, define
\bm{\theta}^{n},\bm{\phi}^{n+1}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
by

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(\bm{\theta}^{n},\bm{\psi})

\displaystyle=(\grad\tilde{z}_{X},\bm{\psi})-b(\bm{\phi}^{n},\bm{\psi})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\psi}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma},

(42a)

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{\phi}^{n}+\lambda\bm{\theta}^{n}.\qquad

(42b)

Under appropriate conditions, given below, the iterates converge to the discrete solution to
eq.

36
as
n\to\infty
:

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}^{n}\to\bm{\theta}_{X},\quad\rot\bm{\phi}^{n}\to r_{X},\quad\text{and}\quad(\bm{t}\cdot\bm{\phi}^{n},1)_{\Gamma_{f}^{(i)}}\to\kappa_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq N-1.

To quantify the rate of convergence, we let
B
denote a positive constant satisfying

\displaystyle|b(\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi})|\leq B\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}\|\bm{\psi}\|_{1}\qquad\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi}\in\bm{\Theta}_{\Gamma}.

The existence of such constant follows from the trace theorem. The effect of the choice of finite elements spaces on the rate of convergence is measured by a constant
\beta_{X}

Conclusion

Algorithm

1
enables one to compute the

-conforming approximation of

9
without having to implement

-conforming finite elements provided that three basic conditions
(A1)
,
(A2)
, and
(A3)
are satisfied. It was shown that all common triangular
C^{1}
-finite elements satisfy these conditions.
Algorithm

1
consists of (i) a pre-processing
H^{1}
-conforming elliptic projection, (ii) a Stokes-like solve, and (iii) a post-processing
H^{1}
-conforming elliptic projection, all of which only require finite element spaces and routines that are routinely available in software packages.

Our approach has similarities to
[
17
]
in the special case where
\Gamma_{cs}
is connected although, as shown here,
[
17
]
will only recover the
C^{1}
-conforming finite element approximation of the original problem if conditions

,
(A2)
, and
(A3)
happen to be satisfied.
Section

4
deals with the case when
\Gamma_{cs}
is not connected (
N>1
), which brings a number of fresh difficulties that seem to have not been addressed previously. Nevertheless, the basis idea of
section

3
is extended to the non-connected case in
algorithm

1
.

While there are many other works that seek to relax the
C^{1}
-continuity required by a conforming method by rewriting the biharmonic and/or Kirchhoff plate problem as a mixed formulation (see e.g.
[

,
7
,
9
,

,
13
,

26

is the first that is able to deliver the actual

-conforming approximation defined in
eq.

9
.

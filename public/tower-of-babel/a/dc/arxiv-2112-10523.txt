The plurigenera of nondegenerate minimal toric hypersurfaces
Julius Giesler

Abstract
In this article we present a formula for the plurigenera of minimal models of nondegenerate toric hypersurfaces, which is valid in arbitrary dimension and which expresses these invariants through lattice points on the Fine interior. From this formula we derive a formula for the volume of the canonical divisor of the toric hypersurface. We also study the pluricanonical mappings and show under some restrictions how to construct a canonical model of the toric hypersurface.

Introduction

In (
[
15
, (4.12),(4.13)]
) it was formulated that one might use a special polyotpe, called the
Fine interior
, to compute the plurigenera of nondegenerate toric hypersurfaces. This idea goes back to the doctoral thesis of J. Fine (
[
9
]
). But the article of M. Reid lacks an explicit formula. In this article we provide such a formula for the plurigenera of minimal models of nondegenerate toric hypersurfaces:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Delta
be an
n
-dimensional lattice polytope, where
n\geq 2
, with
k:=\dim\,F(\Delta)\geq 0
and let
Y
be a minimal model of
Z_{f}
. Then for
m\geq 1
the plurigenera
P_{m}(Y):=h^{0}(Y,mK_{Y})
are given by

P_{m}(Y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}l(m\cdot F(\Delta))-l^{*}((m-1)\cdot F(\Delta)),&k=n\\
l(m\cdot F(\Delta))+l^{*}((m-1)\cdot F(\Delta)),&k=n-1\\
l(m\cdot F(\Delta))&k<n-1,\par\end{array}\right.

Here for
F\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
a rational polytope
l(F):=\text{\textbar}F\cap M\text{\textbar}
is the number of lattice point of
F
and
l^{*}(F)
the number of interior lattice points of
F
.

In the article (
[
3
]
) it is shown how to construct minimal models and other birational models with mild singularities of nondegenerate toric hypersurfaces. That is we start with a nondegenerate Laurent polynomial
f
with
n
-dimensional Newton polytope
\Delta\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
, consider the zero set
Z_{f}:=\{f=0\}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
and ask for a projective toric variety
\mathbb{P}
such that the closure
Y=Y_{f}
of
Z_{f}
in
\mathbb{P}
has at most terminal singularities and nef canonical divisor
K_{Y}
. In this case we call
Y
a
minimal model
of
Z_{f}
.

\mathbb{P}
exists if
F(\Delta)\neq\emptyset
and in (
[
3
]
) it was shown how to construct
\mathbb{P}
combinatorially. To compute the plurigenera of
Y
in section
4
we use an exact sequence arising from the adjunction formula

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}((m-1)(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)+K_{\mathbb{P}})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(m(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y))\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y}(mK_{Y})\rightarrow 0.

We exploit that the toric divisor
m(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and nef, and the polytope associated to it is
m\cdot F(\Delta)
. Then we apply Serre duality on
\mathbb{P}
and a vanishing Theorem to deduce the formula on the plurigenera.

In (
[
7
]
) and (
[
15
]
) the Plurigenera are computed for
3
-folds, which are not necessarily toric hypersurfaces: If
Y
is a projective
3
-fold with at most canonical singularities and with

ample, then we have for

(
[

\displaystyle P_{m}(Y)

\displaystyle=\chi(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(mK_{Y}))

\displaystyle=\frac{(2m-1)m(m-1)}{12r}K_{Y}^{3}-(2m-1)\chi(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})+l(n).

Here the numbers

and
l(n)
depend on the singularities of
Y
and a computation of them is more involved. In (
[
6
]
) it is shown that if
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial the first plurigenus of the closure
Z_{\Delta}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
is given by
l^{*}(\Delta)
, which equals
l(F(\Delta))
. Our formula in Theorem
1.1
is valid in aribtrary dimensions

and should be much more useful for computations. As an illustration of our formula we deal with smooth curves in toric surfaces.

In Corollary
5.1
we derive from Theorem
1.1
a formula for the volume
K_{Y}^{n-1}
of
K_{Y}
, which though was first and independently found by V. Batyrev. If
\dim\,F(\Delta)\in\{n-1,n\}
then
Y
is a variety of general type. In Corollary

we show how to construct a
canonical model
of

at least if
\dim\,F(\Delta)=n

\psi_{mK_{Y}}
on

and give criteria when they are morphisms or birational, where for the latter we assume
\dim\,F(\Delta)=n
.

Background on toric varieties

In this article
M
always denotes an
n
-dimensional lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with dual lattice

n
-dimensional torus. By
\Delta

and think of it as the Newton polytope of some Laurent polynomial
f

\displaystyle f=\sum\limits_{m\in M\cap\Delta}a_{m}x^{m},\quad a_{m}\in\mathbb{C},

(1)

where
a_{m}\neq 0
for

a vertex of
\Delta
. Here

By a rational polytpe

F=\{x\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle\geq-a_{i},\,i=1,...,r\},

where

. We associate to

\Sigma_{F}
which has as rays
\Sigma_{F}[1]=\{\nu_{1},...,\nu_{r}\}
. For
\Delta

n
-dimensional lattice polytope or more generally a rational polytope let

\ord_{\Delta}(\nu):=\min\limits_{m\in\Delta}\langle m,\nu\rangle\quad\nu\in N,

such that
\ord_{\Delta}(\nu_{i})=-a_{i}
. We denote the projective toric variety associated to the normal fan
\Sigma_{F}
of

by
\mathbb{P}_{F}
. We denote the toric divisor associated to

.

For an
n
-dimensional lattice polytope

and

and

Remark 2.1
.

We write
D\sim_{lin}D^{\prime}
for the linear equivalence of Weil divisors. To a Cartier (Weil) divisor

is isomorphic to

if and only if

Proposition 2.2
.

A projective toric variety
V
to a fan
\Sigma
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial if and only if each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
is simplicial, that is the generators
\nu_{i}\in\sigma[1]
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Given two
n
-dimensional complete fans
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
with associated toric varieties
V
and
V^{\prime}
, such that
\Sigma[1]
and
\Sigma^{\prime}[1]

\Sigma^{\prime}
refines
\Sigma

.

To every Weil divisor

D=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}D_{i},\quad a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}

on a toric variety

is associated a polytope

\displaystyle P_{D}:=\{x\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle\geq-a_{i},\quad\nu_{i}\in\Sigma[1]\},

which is a rational polytope, and which computes the global sections of

2.1
Nondegenerate hypersurfaces in toric varieties

Given a Laurent polynomial
f
with Newton polytope
\Delta
, we denote the zero set in the torus
T
by
Z_{f}
. For
F\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
a rational polytope we denote the closure of
Z_{f}
in
\mathbb{P}_{F}
by
Z_{F}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Given a Laurent polynomial
f
with Newton polytope
\Delta
we call

nondegenerate with respect to
\Delta
(or
\Delta

Z_{f}
is smooth and

intersects the toric strata of
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}

Remark 2.5
.

By Bertini’s Theorem the condition for
f\in L(\Delta)
to be
\Delta
-regular is a Zariski open condition on the coefficients
(a_{m})_{m\in M\cap\Delta}
.

Construction 2.6
.

By (
[
3
, Prop.7.1]
) for
f\in L(\Delta)
the closure
Z
in the toric variety
V
to an
n
-dimensional complete fan
\Sigma
is as Weil divisor linear equivalent to

\displaystyle Z\sim_{lin}-\sum\limits_{\nu_{i}\in\Sigma[1]}\ord_{\Delta}(\nu_{i})D_{i}.

(3)

In particular the linear equivalence class of

is independent of

. This justifies our notation without mentioning

.

Z
is a Cartier divisor if and only if
\ord_{\Delta}
is a

, that is

\ord_{\Delta}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is linear on each cone of

and
\ord_{\Delta}(N)\subset\mathbb{Z}
. Similarly

is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier if we just have

4
The plurigenera of minimal models

First the Kodaira dimension of
Y
, which by definition is the Kodaira dimension of a resolution of singularities of
Y
, has already been computed: For
\Delta
an
n
-dimensional lattice polytope with
F(\Delta)\neq\emptyset
and
k:=\dim\,F(\Delta)
if
k=n
then
\kappa(Y)=n-1
, else
\kappa(Y)=k
(
[
3
, Thm.9.2]
). In the following Theorem we also compute the plurigenera of
Y
:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Delta
be an
n
-dimensional lattice polytope, where
n\geq 2
, with
k:=\dim\,F(\Delta)\geq 0
. Then for
m\geq 1
the plurigenera
P_{m}(Y):=h^{0}(Y,mK_{Y})
are given by

P_{m}(Y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}l(m\cdot F(\Delta))-l^{*}((m-1)\cdot F(\Delta)),&k=n\\
l(m\cdot F(\Delta))+l^{*}((m-1)\cdot F(\Delta)),&k=n-1\\
l(m\cdot F(\Delta))&k<n-1,\par\end{array}\right.

Proof.

If we take cohomology groups of a divisor
D
we always mean the cohomology groups of the sheaf
\mathcal{O}(D)
associated to
D
. Since
\mathbb{P}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial every Weil divisor on
\mathbb{P}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. By Remark
3.12
we have

H^{0}(\mathbb{P},m(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y))\cong L(m\cdot F(\Delta))\quad m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}.

We use an ideal sheaf sequence for
Y\subset\mathbb{P}
and apply the adjunction formula

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathbb{P},(m-1)(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)+K_{\mathbb{P}})\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathbb{P},m(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y))

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{0}(Y,mK_{Y})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{P},(m-1)(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)+K_{\mathbb{P}})\rightarrow 0

where

H^{1}(\mathbb{P},m(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y))=0

by Demazure’s vanishing (
[
5
, Thm.9.2.3]
) for the divisor
K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y
, which is nef by Theorem
3.8
. By (
[
5
, Thm.9.2.10a)]
) we may apply Serre-duality to the divisor
K_{\mathbb{P}}
. Thus for
m=1
we have
h^{0}(\mathbb{P},K_{\mathbb{P}})=0
and

h^{1}(\mathbb{P},K_{\mathbb{P}})=h^{n-1}(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}})=0

by Demazure’s vanishing Theorem again. Thus
P_{1}(Y)
is given by

P_{1}(Y)=l(F(\Delta))=l^{*}(\Delta).

For
m\geq 2
we apply Serre duality to the divisor
(m-1)(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)
: We have splittings

\displaystyle H^{n-p}(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y))\cong\bigoplus\limits_{m\in M}H^{n-p}(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y))_{m}\cdot\chi^{m}

\displaystyle H^{p}(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(-Y))\cong\bigoplus\limits_{m\in M}H^{p}(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(-Y))_{m}\cdot\chi^{m}

and by (
[
5
, Ex.9.12 formula (9.2.9)]
) Serre duality restricts to a duality

\big(H^{n-p}(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y))_{m}\big)^{*}\cong H^{p}(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(-Y))_{-m}.

Thus for
D:=K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y

H^{n-p}(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(D))\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&p\neq\dim\,P_{D}\\
\bigoplus\limits_{m\in L^{*}(P_{D})}\mathbb{C}\cdot\chi^{m}&p=\dim\,P_{D}\end{array}\right.,

by the Batyrev-Borisov vanishing Theorem (
[
5
, Thm.9.2.7]
), where
P_{D}
denotes the polytope associated to
D
. We get

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathbb{P},(m-1)(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)+

\displaystyle K_{\mathbb{P}})\cong H^{n}(\mathbb{P},-(m-1)(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y))^{*}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\dim\,F(\Delta)\leq n-1\\
L^{*}((m-1)F(\Delta)),&\dim\,F(\Delta)=n\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbb{P},(m-1)(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)+

\displaystyle K_{\mathbb{P}})\cong H^{n-1}(\mathbb{P},-(m-1)(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y))^{*}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}L^{*}((m-1)F(\Delta)),&\dim\,F(\Delta)=n-1\\
0,&\dim\,F(\Delta)\neq n-1\end{array}\right.

The result follows by adding the dimensions in the exact sequence.

5
The volume of
K_{Y}

The lattice normalized volume
\Vol_{\mathbb{Z}}(F)
of a rational polytope
F
may be defined by

\displaystyle\Vol_{\mathbb{Z}}(F)=\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{l(m\cdot F)\cdot(\dim\,F)!}{m^{\dim\,F}}.

(5)

This is a standard fact (
[
4
, Lemma 3.19]
) and normalized means that the standard
n
-simplex
\Delta_{n}
has
\Vol_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Delta_{n})=1
. We refer to (
[
4
]
) for details. The following formula follows easily from the formula for the plurigenera. It was first and independently found by V. Batyrev in (
[
3
, Thm.9.4]
).

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
\Delta
be an
n
-dimensional lattice polytope with
F(\Delta)\neq\emptyset
. Then

K_{Y}^{n-1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Vol_{\mathbb{Z}}(F(\Delta))+\sum\limits_{Q\textrm{ facet of }F(\Delta)}\Vol_{\mathbb{Z}}(Q),&\dim\,F(\Delta)=n\\
2\cdot\Vol_{\mathbb{Z}}(F(\Delta))&\dim\,F(\Delta)=n-1\\
0&\dim\,F(\Delta)<n-1\par\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By (
[
11
, Remark after Def. 2.2.31]
) we have

K_{Y}^{n-1}=\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(n-1)!\cdot h^{0}(Y,mK_{Y})}{m^{n-1}}.

It follows that
K_{Y}^{n-1}=0
if
\dim\,F(\Delta)<n-1
. If
\dim\,F(\Delta)=n-1
then by (
[
4
, Thm.4.1]
) we have

\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(n-1)!\cdot l^{*}((m-1)F(\Delta)}{m^{n-1}}=\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(n-1)!\cdot l(m\cdot F(\Delta))}{m^{n-1}}=\Vol_{\mathbb{Z}}(F(\Delta))

and the result follows.

If
\dim\,F(\Delta)=n
then write

P_{m}(Y)=l(mF(\Delta))-l((m-1)F(\Delta))+l((m-1)F(\Delta))-l^{*}((m-1)F(\Delta)).

By formula
5
we have

\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{l(mF(\Delta))-l((m-1)F(\Delta))}{m^{n-1}/(n-1)!}=\Vol_{\mathbb{Z}}(F(\Delta)).

Finally by (
[
4
, Thm.5.6]
) we have

\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{l((m-1)F(\Delta))-l^{*}((m-1)F(\Delta))}{m^{n-1}/(n-1)!}=\sum\limits_{Q\textrm{ facet of }F(\Delta)}\Vol_{\mathbb{Z}}(Q).

∎

6
The pluricanonical mappings

By Remark
3.12
for

\displaystyle m:=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\mid n\cdot F(\Delta)\textrm{ is a lattice polytope}\},

(6)

the divisor
m(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)
is Cartier and nef and thus basepointfree. Thus so is the restriction
mK_{Y}
. Let
D_{F(\Delta)}
denote the ample
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
\mathbb{P}_{F(\Delta)}
associated to
F(\Delta)
. By (
[
5
, Thm.6.2.8]
) and Remark
3.12
we have an equality

\displaystyle m(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)=(\theta\circ\pi)^{*}(mD_{F(\Delta)}).

(7)

Thus
m(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)
induces the morphism
\theta\circ\pi:\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{F(\Delta)}
.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
\Delta
be an
n
-dimensional lattice polytope such that

\dim\,F(\Delta)=n
. Then
Z_{F(\Delta)}
gets a canonical model of
Y
.

Proof.

The morphism
\theta\circ\pi:\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{F(\Delta)}
is birational and equal to the identity on the torus. Thus also
(\theta\circ\pi)_{|{Y}}
defines a birational morphism
Y\rightarrow Z_{F(\Delta)}
.
Z_{F(\Delta)}
is normal since else it would contain an
(n-2)
-dimensional torus orbit of
\mathbb{P}_{F(\Delta)}
. But then also
Y
would contain an
(n-2)
-dimensional torus orbit of
\mathbb{P}
contradiciting Remark
3.9
. By the adjunction formula we get for any curve
C\subset Y

(\theta\circ\pi)(C)=pt\Leftrightarrow K_{Y}.C=0.

Thus
Z_{F(\Delta)}
equals the canonical model of
Y
(
[
12
, Thm.3-3-6]
).
∎

At least if
C(\Delta)=\Delta
there is a useful criterion for
k\cdot K_{Y}
to be basepointfree for a given
k\geq 1
:

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a
n
-dimensional lattice polytope with
F(\Delta)\neq\emptyset
and
C(\Delta)=\Delta
. Assume that
k\geq 1
and every edge of
k\cdot F(\Delta)
contains a lattice point. Then
k\cdot K_{Y}
is basepointfree.

Proof.

Denote for
w=(w_{1},...,w_{n})\in M
by
\chi^{w}
the character

t=(t_{1},...,t_{n})\mapsto t_{1}^{w_{1}}...t_{n}^{w_{n}}

corresponding to
w
. Then

H^{0}(\mathbb{P},k(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y))\cong\bigoplus\limits_{w\in M\cap k\cdot F(\Delta)}\chi^{w}\cdot\mathbb{C}.

Let
M\cap k\cdot F(\Delta)=\{w_{1},...,w_{l}\}
and let
\phi_{k}
denote the map associated to the linear system
\text{\textbar}k(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)\text{\textbar}
. Then on the torus
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
the map
\phi_{k}
is given by

t\mapsto(\chi^{w_{1}}(t),...,\chi^{w_{l}}(t)).

By (
[
5
, Prop.4.1.2]
) the divisor of
\chi^{w}
on
\mathbb{P}
is

div(\chi^{w})=\sum\limits_{\nu_{i}\in\Sigma[1]}\langle w,\nu_{i}\rangle D_{i}.

Let
F
be an
r
-dimensional face of
k\cdot F(\Delta)
, where
r\geq 1
, and let
\Gamma_{1},...,\Gamma_{l}
denote all facets of
k\cdot F(\Delta)
containing
F
. Then by assumption we find an
w\in M\cap F
. Let
div_{0}(\chi^{w})
denote the zero divisor of
\chi^{w}
, then

div_{0}(\chi^{w})=k(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)+div(\chi^{w})=\sum\limits_{\nu_{i}\in\Sigma[1]}(-k\ord_{F(\Delta)}(\nu_{i})+\langle w,\nu_{i}\rangle)D_{i}.

Say that
\nu_{i}
is a normal vector to
\Gamma_{i}
, then we have

\langle w,\nu_{i}\rangle=k\ord_{F(\Delta)}(\nu_{i}),\quad i=1,...,l.

Thus
div_{0}(\chi^{w})
does not meet the torus orbit to
F
. Thus the only basepoints of
\phi_{k}
might be torus fixed points. But since
C(\Delta)=\Delta
the hypersurface does not pass through these torus fixed points. Thus
k(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)
is basepointfree on
Y
and so is
kK_{Y}
.
∎

If
\dim\,F(\Delta)=\dim\,\Delta
then we have for
1\leq k\leq m
the following criterion for the morphism
\phi_{k}
associated to
\text{\textbar}k(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)\text{\textbar}
to be birational:

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\dim\,F(\Delta)=\dim\,\Delta=n
and
m
be as in (
6
). Given
1\leq k\leq m
assume that every lattice point in
m\cdot F(\Delta)
may be written as
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of lattice points of
k\cdot F(\Delta)
. Then
\text{\textbar}k(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)\text{\textbar}
and
\text{\textbar}kK_{Y}\text{\textbar}
define birational maps.

Proof.

The assumptions imply that there are
n+1
affine linear independent points in
L(kF(\Delta))
. Thus the image of
\phi_{k}
has dimension
n
. We may restrict global sections of
k(K_{\mathbb{P}}+Y)
to
Y
and restrict the maps to the torus and
Z_{f}
. But on the torus
\phi_{k}
is a finite
morphism
. Thus it is enough to show that
\phi_{k}
is birational. Let
L(kF(\Delta))=\{w_{1},...,w_{l}\}
and
L(mF(\Delta))=\{w_{1},...,w_{s}\}
where
s\geq l
. Restricted to the torus
\phi_{k}
factors

\displaystyle t\mapsto(\chi^{w_{1}}(t),...,\chi^{w_{s}}(t))\rightarrow(\chi^{w_{1}}(t),...,\chi^{w_{l}}(t))

with the second map a projection and the first birational onto its image. Under the assumption of the Proposition the projection map, restricted to the image of the first map, has degree one, since for
w\in L(mF(\Delta))
we find
a_{1},...,a_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\chi^{w}(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{l}(\chi^{w_{i}}(t))^{a_{i}}.

∎

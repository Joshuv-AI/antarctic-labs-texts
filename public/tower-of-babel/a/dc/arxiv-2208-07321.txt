Azimuthally-correlated contributions to QCD transverse-momentum resummation at 𝒪 ⁡ ( α S 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\alpha_{\rm S}^{2})
Prasanna K. Dhani

Abstract
Singular factors originating from the QCD factorisation of scattering amplitudes in soft and collinear limits play a prominent role in both organising and computing high-order perturbative contributions to hard-scattering cross sections. In this talk, we start from the factorisation structure of scattering amplitudes in the collinear limit, and we introduce collinear functions that have a process-independent structure. These collinear functions, which are defined at the fully-differential level, can then be integrated over the appropriate observable-dependent phase space to compute logarithmically-enhanced contributions to the corresponding observable. For transverse-momentum dependent observables, we show how the collinear functions can be defined without introducing what is known as rapidity divergences in the literature. We present the results of explicit computations of the collinear functions up to next-to-next-to-leading order in the QCD perturbation theory.

Introduction

Transverse-momentum (
\mathbf{q_{T}}
) resummation is fully developed for the inclusive-production processes of high-mass colourless systems in hadron–hadron collisions. In the kinematical region where the
q_{T}

M

q_{T}

\ln^{n}(M^{2}/q_{T}^{2})
. The QCD resummation procedure organises and systematically sums these large contributions to all perturbative orders in the strong coupling
\alpha_{\rm S}
. In this talk we refer to the

resummation formalism of Refs.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

Transverse-momentum resummation is conveniently carried out in impact parameter (
\mathbf{b}
) space, Fourier conjugate to
\mathbf{q_{T}}
space. We directly consider and refer to the notation in Ref.
[
4
]
. The
\mathbf{b}
space cross section at

bM\gg 1
is expressed in terms of the parton distribution functions (PDFs) of the colliding hadrons and of perturbatively calculable factors. In this talk

C_{ca}
(see Eqs. (11) and (14) in Ref.
[
4

(

(

[

6

The quark collinear function

\displaystyle C_{qa}\left(z;\alpha_{\rm S}\right)=\delta_{qa}\delta(1-z)+\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\,C_{qa}^{(1)}\left(z\right)+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{m}C_{qa}^{(m)}\left(z\right)\,.

(1)

The gluon collinear function

\displaystyle C_{ga}^{\mu\nu}\left(z;p_{1},p_{2},\mathbf{b};\alpha_{\rm S}\right)=d^{\mu\nu}(p_{1},p_{2})\,C_{ga}(z;\alpha_{\rm S})+D^{\mu\nu}(p_{1},p_{2};\mathbf{b})\,G_{ga}(z;\alpha_{\rm S})\,,

\displaystyle C_{ga}\left(z;\alpha_{\rm S}\right)

(3)

\displaystyle G_{ga}\left(z;\alpha_{\rm S}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\,G_{ga}^{(1)}\left(z\right)+\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{2}\,G_{ga}^{(2)}\left(z\right)+\sum_{m=3}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{m}\,G_{ga}^{(m)}\left(z\right)\,.

2
Details of our computational method: differential and TMD collinear functions

In this talk, we compute the collinear functions introduced in Sec.
1
starting from the evaluation of QCD scattering amplitudes.
At the bare level, the computation exhibits ultraviolet (UV) and infrared (IR) divergences. We regularise both the divergences by working in

d=4-2\epsilon
space-time dimensions. In particular, we use the conventional dimensional regularisation (CDR) scheme,
in which on-shell gluons have
d-2
physical states of spin polarisations and on-shell massless quarks (or antiquarks) have 2 spin polarisation states. The dimensional regularisation scale is denoted by
\mu_{0}
.

QCD scattering amplitudes are singular in the kinematical configurations in which two or more momenta of their external massless
partons become collinear. The singular behaviour in the collinear limit is described by a factorisation formula that has a process-independent structure. Following Ref.
[
12
]
, we write the collinear factorisation formula in its most general form as follows

|{\cal M}\left(\{q_{i}\};k_{1},\dots,k_{N}\right)|^{2}=\langle{\cal M}\bigl(\{q_{i}\};{\tilde{k}}\bigr)|\;{\boldsymbol{\cal P}}\bigl(\{q_{i}\};k_{1},\dots,k_{N};n\bigr)\;|{\cal M}\bigl(\{q_{i}\};{\tilde{k}}\bigr)\rangle+\dots\;,

(5)

where the dots on the right-hand side denote non-singular terms in the collinear limit.
Here,
\cal M
denotes the on-shell scattering amplitude in colour+spin space notation of a generic hard-scattering process, and

|{\cal M}|^{2}
is the corresponding squared amplitude.
In Eq. (
5
) we are considering the limit in which the momenta
k_{1},\dots,k_{N}
of
N
external massless QCD partons become collinear. The momenta of the other external partons of
\cal M

are
q_{1},q_{2},\dots
and their dependence through momenta and other quantum numbers is denoted by
\{q_{i}\}
. The singular behaviour in the collinear limit is embodied in the factor

{\boldsymbol{\cal P}}
, while
{\cal M}\bigl(\{q_{i}\};{\tilde{k}}\bigr)
denotes the reduced scattering amplitude that is obtained
from
{\cal M}\left(\{q_{i}\};k_{1},\dots,k_{N}\right)
by replacing the
N
collinear partons with a single parent parton with momentum

{\tilde{k}}
.

In Eq. (
5
),
\tilde{k}
and
k_{1},\dots,k_{N}
are the
outgoing

momenta of the corresponding external partons.
The scattering amplitude
\cal M
(and the kernel
{\boldsymbol{\cal P}}
) is evaluated in different physical kinematical regions depending on the sign
of the ‘energies’ of the outgoing momenta. If the energies of
k_{1},\dots,k_{N}
are all positive, we are dealing
with the TL collinear region. Otherwise, we are considering the space-like (SL) collinear region. The distinction between TL and SL collinear regions is,
in general, very relevant. Indeed, in the case of the TL collinear region the splitting kernel
{\boldsymbol{\cal P}}
has the relevant property of being completely
process independent: it does not depend on the momenta and quantum numbers of the non-collinear partons in
\cal M
.
This property of strict collinear factorisation is instead violated in the SL collinear regions
[
12
]
. The collinear splitting kernels
{\boldsymbol{\cal P}}
at
{\cal O}(\alpha_{\rm S})
are well known and at
{\cal O}(\alpha_{\rm S}^{2})
, they are fully known

[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]

for both the TL and SL collinear regions
[
12
]
.

Considering the TL region, the splitting kernels
{\boldsymbol{\cal P}}
are process independent and proportional to the unit matrix in colour space. However, the dependence of
{\boldsymbol{\cal P}}_{c\rightarrow a_{1}\dots a_{N}}
(where
a_{i}
denotes the flavour of the collinear parton with momentum
k_{i}
, and
c
is the flavour of the parent parton) on the spin of the parent collinear parton
c
can be instead non-trivial
[
14
]
and it is different for the cases
c=q,{\bar{q}}
and
c=g
. In the case of collinear splitting of a quark or antiquark, spin correlations are completely absent while they are present in the collinear splitting of a gluon through a rank-2 tensor.

The tensor dependence of
{\cal P}^{\mu\nu}_{g\rightarrow a_{1}\cdots a_{N}}

is due to terms that are proportional to either the metric tensor
g^{\mu\nu}

or to quadratic terms of the type
k_{i\,T}^{\mu}k_{j\,T}^{\nu}
, where

k_{i\,T}^{\mu}
is the transverse momentum of the
i
-th collinear parton with respect to the collinear direction.
The remaining dependence of
{\cal P}_{c\rightarrow a_{1}\cdots a_{N}}
(
c=q,\bar{q},g
)
is due to scalar functions of the collinear momenta
k_{1},\dots,k_{N}
. These functions are the sub-energies
s_{ij}=2k_{i}k_{j}
and the ratios
x_{i}/x_{j}

of the longitudinal-momentum fractions
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
of the momenta
k_{i}
and

k_{j}
. The most general definition
[
14
]
of the longitudinal-momentum fractions is obtained by introducing an auxiliary reference
vector
n^{\mu}
that is far away from the collinear direction. Then the collinear splitting kernels depend on the ratios
x_{i}/x_{j}
that are defined as
x_{i}/x_{j}=nk_{i}/nk_{j}
. In the literature the reference vector
n^{\mu}
is usually chosen to be a
light-like
(i.e.,
n^{2}=0
)
vector.
However, we emphasise that one can also set
n^{2}\neq 0
. In this talk we introduce and use a
time-like
(i.e.,
n^{2}>0
) auxiliary vector
n^{\mu}
. Note that we do not modify any formal expression of the splitting kernels
{\boldsymbol{\cal P}}
in the literature. Simply, we use the freedom of arbitrarily choosing
n^{2}\geq 0
in the collinear limit.

After our discussion of the structure of Eq. (
5
) in the TL collinear region, we define differential TL collinear functions
{{\cal F}}^{\rm TL}_{ca}

(
c,a=g,q,\bar{q}
) as follows
[
18
]
. We consider the production of a parton of flavour
a
and momentum
p^{\mu}
in the physical final state and we fully integrate over the accompanying collinear radiation by keeping its total momentum
k
fixed. If the parent collinear parton
c
is a gluon, we have to take into account
the spin correlations, and the precise definition of the collinear function
{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{ga}
is

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{ga}(p,k;n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=2}^{+\infty}\left[\prod_{m=1}^{N-1}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{m}}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\delta_{+}\left(k_{m}^{2}\right)\right]\;\delta^{(d)}\!\!\left(k-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}k_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{N-1}}\frac{{\widetilde{\cal P}}_{g\rightarrow a_{1}\dots a_{N}}^{\mu\nu}\left(k_{1},\dots,k_{N};n\right)}{{\rm SF}\left(a_{1},\dots,a_{N-1}\right)}{\Bigg|}_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{N}=\,p\\
a_{N}=\,a\end{subarray}}\;\;,

(6)

where
{\rm SF}\left(a_{1},\dots,a_{N-1}\right)
is the Bose symmetry factor for the identical particles in the final state. The parton momentum
p^{\mu}
precisely specifies the collinear direction, and we can use a light-cone reference frame where
p^{\mu}=(p^{+},\mathbf{0_{T}},0)
with

p^{+}>0
. In this frame we have
k_{i}^{\mu}=(k_{i}^{+},\mathbf{k_{i\,T}},k_{i}^{-})

and
k^{\mu}=(k^{+},\mathbf{k_{T}},k^{-})
. The auxiliary TL vector
n
has coordinates
n^{\mu}=(n^{+},\mathbf{0_{T}},n^{-})
, with
n^{2}=2n^{+}n^{-}>0
. The case of a light-like vector
n^{\mu}
is obtained by setting
n^{+}=0
. The gluonic kernel
{\widetilde{\cal P}}^{\mu\nu}
in Eq. (
2
) is given by
{\widetilde{\cal P}}_{g\rightarrow a_{1}\dots a_{N}}^{\mu\nu}\left(k_{1},\dots,k_{N};n\right)=d^{\mu}_{\;\;\mu^{\prime}}(p;n)\;{{\cal P}}_{g\rightarrow a_{1}\dots a_{N}}^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\left(k_{1},\dots,k_{N};n\right)\;d_{\nu^{\prime}}^{\;\;\;\nu}(p;n),

where the spin polarization tensor,
d^{\mu\nu}(p;n)=-g^{\mu\nu}+(p^{\mu}n^{\nu}+n^{\mu}p^{\nu})/(np)-(n^{2}p^{\mu}p^{\nu})/(np)^{2}\;.

The use of
{\widetilde{\cal P}}^{\mu\nu}
in Eq. (
2
) removes purely longitudinal terms, proportional to
p^{\mu}
or
p^{\nu}
, from

{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{ga}
.
The function
{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}\,\mu\nu}
depends on the vectors
p,k,n
and it is orthogonal to both
p
and
n
. Therefore it has the following decomposition in tensor structures:

{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{ga}(p,k;n)=d^{\mu\nu}(p;n)\;{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}}_{ga,\,{\rm az.in.}}(p,k;n)+D^{\mu\nu}(p,n;\mathbf{k_{T}},\epsilon)\;{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}}_{ga,\,{\rm corr.}}(p,k;n)\;\;,

(7)

where
D^{\mu\nu}(p,n;\mathbf{k_{T}},\epsilon)=d^{\mu\nu}(p;n)-(d-2)k_{T}^{\mu}k_{T}^{\nu}/\mathbf{k_{T}}^{2}
. The tensor
D^{\mu\nu}
in Eq. (
7
) leads to correlations with respect to the azimuthal angle of the transverse-momentum vector
\mathbf{k_{T}}
. The scalar functions
{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}}_{ga,\,{\rm az.in.}}
and
{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}}_{ga,\,{\rm corr.}}
control the size of the azimuthal un-correlated and correlated contributions to
{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{ga}
, respectively. The tensors in Eq. (
7
) are the
d
-dimensional generalisation of those in Eq. (
2
) and they fulfil following relations:
d_{\mu\nu}(p;n)D^{\mu\nu}(p,n;\mathbf{k_{T}},\epsilon)=0,\,d_{\mu\nu}(p;n)\,d^{\mu\nu}(p;n)=d-2,\,D_{\mu\nu}(p,n;\mathbf{k_{T}},\epsilon)D^{\mu\nu}(p,n;\mathbf{k_{T}},\epsilon)=(d-2)(d-3),\,
with
d-2=2-2\epsilon
and
d-3=1-2\epsilon
. The scalar functions
{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}}_{ga,\,{\rm az.in.}}
and
{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}}_{ga,\,{\rm corr.}}
can be easily and directly expressed in terms of the collinear splitting kernels
{{\cal P}}_{g\rightarrow a_{1}\dots a_{N}}^{\mu\nu}
using the above relations. The TL collinear function
{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}}_{ca}(p,k;n)

(c=q,\bar{q})
of a parent collinear fermion
c
is defined analogously to the gluon collinear function
{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}}_{ga}(p,k;n)
by simply performing the replacements

{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{ga}\rightarrow{\cal F}^{\rm{TL}}_{ca}
and

{\widetilde{\cal P}}_{g\rightarrow a_{1}\dots a_{N}}\rightarrow{{\cal P}}_{c\rightarrow a_{1}\dots a_{N}}
in Eq. (
2
).

The SL collinear splitting kernels
{\boldsymbol{\cal P}}
in the factorisation formula (
5
)
are, in general, process dependent and, in particular, they can depend on the colour indices and momenta of the non-collinear partons and on the colour indices of the parent collinear parton
c

in the hard-scattering process. The general extension
{\boldsymbol{\cal F}}_{ca}
of the TL collinear function in
Eqs. (
2
) to the SL collinear regions
is as follows

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\cal F}}_{ca}(\{q_{i}\};p,k;n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=2}^{+\infty}\left[\prod_{m=1}^{N-1}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{m}}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\delta_{+}\left(k_{m}^{2}\right)\right]\;\delta^{(d)}\!\!\left(k-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}k_{i}\right)

(8)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{N-1}}\frac{{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\cal P}}}_{{\bar{c}}\rightarrow a_{1}\dots a_{N}}\left(\{q_{i}\};k_{1},\dots,k_{N};n\right)}{{\rm SF}\left(a_{1},\dots,a_{N-1}\right)}{\Bigg|}_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{N}=\,-p\\
a_{N}=\,{\bar{a}}\end{subarray}}\;\;\frac{\mathcal{N}_{c}(\epsilon)}{\mathcal{N}_{a}(\epsilon)}\;\;,\quad\;\;\;c=g,q,{\bar{q}}\;,

where
a
denotes the parton with momentum
p^{\mu}
that collides in the physical initial state and

refers to the incoming parton of the hard-scattering process
after the radiation of the final-state collinear partons with total momentum

k^{\mu}
. The function
{\mathcal{N}_{a}(\epsilon)}
in
Eq. (
8
) is given by
\mathcal{N}_{a}(\epsilon)=(-1)^{2S_{a}}n_{s}(a,\epsilon)n_{c}(a)
,
where
S_{a}
denotes the spin,

denotes the number of spin polarisation states, and

n_{c}(a)
denotes the number of colours of the parton

. Therefore, we have

\mathcal{N}_{q}(\epsilon)=\mathcal{N}_{{\bar{q}}}(\epsilon)=-2N_{c}

and
\mathcal{N}_{g}(\epsilon)=2(1-\epsilon)(N_{c}^{2}-1)
.

Considering perturbative contributions at
{\cal O}(\alpha_{\rm S})
and
{\cal O}(\alpha_{\rm S}^{2})
,
the SL collinear kernels

and functions

are process independent
and proportional to the unit matrix in the colour space of the hard-scattering partons.
Therefore, we can factorise such overall (and trivial) colour space dependence
in both sides of Eq. (

), and we can simply deal with

-number
SL collinear functions, analogously to the TL collinear case. Such SL collinear

{\cal F}^{\,\mu\nu}_{ga}(p,k;n)
,
{\cal F}_{ga,\,{\rm az.in.}}(p,k;n)
,

, and

\displaystyle{\bf F}_{ca}(\{q_{i}\};z;zp,\mathbf{q_{T}};n)

3
Perturbative results

In this section we discuss the perturbative calculation of the SL collinear functions introduced in Sec.
2
.
We consider explicit computations up to
{\cal O}(\alpha_{\rm S}^{2})
and, hence, we simply refer to the process-independent
c
-number functions. We left the computation of TL collinear functions to our original article. The perturbative expansion of the collinear functions can be written as follows

{\cal F}(p,k;n)={\cal F}^{(1R)}(p,k;n)+\left[\;{\cal F}^{(2R)}(p,k;n)+{\cal F}^{(1R1V)}(p,k;n)\;\right]+{\cal O}(\alpha_{\rm S}^{3})\;\;.

(11)

The notation in Eq. (
11
) applies to any specific collinear function and, therefore, we have not explicitly denoted the corresponding subscripts and superscripts in
{\cal F}
. The contribution to
{\cal F}
at
{\cal O}(\alpha_{\rm S})
is due to

{\cal F}^{(1R)}
, which corresponds to single real emission in the final state at the tree-level. The contributions to
{\cal F}
at

{\cal O}(\alpha_{\rm S}^{2})
are
{\cal F}^{(2R)}
(double real emission at the tree level)
and
{\cal F}^{(1R1V)}
(single real emission with one-loop virtual corrections).

We express the contributions in Eq. (
11
) in terms of the unrenormalised (bare) QCD coupling
\alpha^{u}_{\rm S}
, which is related to the renormalised coupling
\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2})
in the

\overline{{\rm MS}}
renormalisation scheme as follows:

\displaystyle\alpha^{u}_{\rm S}\,\mu_{0}^{2\epsilon}\,S_{\epsilon}=\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2})\,\mu_{R}^{2\epsilon}\left[1-\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2})}{\pi}\frac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}+\mathcal{O}\left(\alpha_{\rm S}^{2}(\mu_{R}^{2})\right)\right]\,,

(12)

where
\beta_{0}=(11C_{A}-2N_{f})/12
and
N_{f}
is the number of massless-quark flavours.
The
d
-dimensional spherical factor
S_{\epsilon}
is

S_{\epsilon}=\left(4\pi\,e^{-\gamma_{E}}\right)^{\epsilon}
and
\gamma_{E}
is the Euler number
(
\gamma_{E}=0.5772\dots
).

In the case of the azimuthally-independent functions

{\cal F}^{(1R)}_{ca,\,\text{az.in.}}
, the collinear kernels are proportional to
{\widehat{P}}_{ca}(x;\epsilon)

[
14
]
, which are the
d
-dimensional real emission contributions to the Altarelli–Parisi splitting functions for the leading order evolution of the PDFs. We have

{\cal F}^{(1R)}_{ca,\,\text{az.in.}}(p,k;n)=\frac{\alpha^{u}_{\rm S}\,\mu_{0}^{2\epsilon}\,S_{\epsilon}}{\pi}\;\frac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\pi^{1-\epsilon}}\;\frac{\delta_{+}(k^{2})}{pk}\;\frac{1}{z_{n}}\;{\widehat{P}}_{ca}(z_{n};\epsilon)\;\;,\;\;\;\quad c=g,q,{\bar{q}}\;\;,

(13)

where we have introduced the notation

{\cal F}^{(1R)}_{ca}\equiv{\cal F}^{(1R)}_{ca,\,\text{az.in.}}
(
c=q,{\bar{q}}
) for the
collinear functions in the quark and antiquark partonic channels.
The azimuthal-correlation contribution in the gluon channel
is

\mathcal{F}^{(1R)}_{ga,\,\text{corr.}}(p,k;n)=-\;\frac{\alpha^{u}_{\rm S}\,\mu_{0}^{2\epsilon}\,S_{\epsilon}}{\pi}\;\frac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\pi^{1-\epsilon}}\;\frac{\delta_{+}(k^{2})}{pk}\;C_{a}\;\frac{1-z_{n}}{z_{n}^{2}}\;\;,

(14)

where
C_{a}
is the Casimir colour coefficient of the parton
a=q,{\bar{q}},g
.
The expressions of
\mathcal{F}^{(1R)}
in Eqs. (
13
) and (
14
)
depend on the auxiliary vector
n^{\mu}
through the variable
z_{n}
,
z_{n}=n(p-k)/np
.
In the exact collinear limit (i.e.,
k^{-}=0
) the parent hard-scattering parton
c
in
\mathcal{F}^{(1R)}_{ca}
carries the momentum
z_{n}p^{\mu}
, independently of the value of
n^{2}
.

The SL TMD functions
F_{ca}
are obtained from the differential collinear functions

{\cal F}_{ca}
by using Eq. (
2
). The terms
{\cal F}_{ca}^{(1R)}
,

{\cal F}_{ca}^{(2R)}
, and
{\cal F}_{ca}^{(1R1V)}
in Eq. (
11

F_{ca}^{(1R)}
,
F_{ca}^{(2R)}
, and
F_{ca}^{(1R1V)}
, respectively.

We immediately discuss the dependence on the auxiliary vector
n^{\mu}
, which affects

F_{ca}^{(1R)}
through the variable
z_{n}
. The key point regards the effect of the singular contribution of
{\widehat{P}}_{ca}(z_{n};\epsilon)
to
{\cal F}_{ca}^{(1R)}
and, hence, to
F_{ca}^{(1R)}
.
Such contribution is proportional to the following factor:

\frac{1}{1-z_{n}}=\frac{np}{nk}=\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}+\frac{n^{2}}{2np}\frac{k^{-}}{n^{-}}p^{+}}=\frac{1}{1-z+\frac{n^{2}\mathbf{q_{T}}^{2}}{(1-z)(2np)^{2}}}\;\;,

(15)

where in the last equality we have implemented the kinematics of the TMD collinear function at
{\cal O}(\alpha_{\rm S})
(i.e.,
k^{2}=0,\,k^{+}=(1-z)p^{+},\,\mathbf{k_{T}}=-\mathbf{q_{T}}
).
Setting
n^{2}=0
, the factor in Eq. (
15
) becomes
(1-z)^{-1}
and, therefore, it is divergent (and not integrable over
z
) at
z=1
. Correspondingly, the first-order contributions
F_{ca}^{(1R)}
to the TMD collinear functions are divergent. Such divergences, which are known as
rapidity divergences in the literature, are a general feature of SCET formulations of TMD functions, and they can be treated by introducing appropriate regularisation
procedures. In our computations
of the TMD functions we use
n^{2}>0
, thus
avoiding rapidity divergences. Indeed,
setting
\lambda=n^{2}\mathbf{q_{T}}^{2}/{(2pn)^{2}}
in Eq. (
15
), we can use the following relation:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-z+\frac{\lambda}{1-z}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1-z}{(1-z)^{2}+\lambda}\right)_{+}+\delta(1-z)\int_{0}^{1}dz^{\prime}\frac{1-z^{\prime}}{(1-z^{\prime})^{2}+\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\;\ln\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\;\delta(1-z)+\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)_{+}+{\cal O}({\sqrt{\lambda}})\;\;,

(16)

where the symbol
\bigl(f(z)\bigr)_{+}
denotes the customary ‘plus-distribution’
of the function
f(z)
with respect to the variable
z
.
The term of
{\cal O}({\sqrt{\lambda}})\sim{\cal O}(\mathbf{q_{T}})
in Eq. (
3
)
smoothly vanishes in the limit
\mathbf{q_{T}}\rightarrow 0
and, therefore, it can be neglected in the computation of
F_{ca}^{(1R)}
. We can similarly neglect other smooth terms
in the limit
\mathbf{q_{T}}\rightarrow 0
by using
z_{n}=z+{\cal O}(\mathbf{q_{T}}^{2})
in the remaining

z_{n}
dependence of
F_{ca}^{(1R)}
.

We introduce the Fourier transformation of the TMD collinear function
F_{ca}
to the purpose of having a more direct relation with the discussion in Sec.
1
.
The Fourier transformation
{\widetilde{F}}_{ca}
in
\mathbf{b}
space of the TMD collinear function
F_{ca}
for the quark and antiquark partonic channels is

{\widetilde{F}}_{ca}\!\left(z;\frac{\mathbf{b}^{2}}{b_{0}^{2}},\frac{n^{2}b_{0}^{2}}{(2zpn)^{2}\,\mathbf{b}^{2}}\right)\equiv\int d^{d-2}\mathbf{q_{T}}\;e^{-i\mathbf{b}.\mathbf{q_{T}}}\;{F}_{ca}\!\left(z;\mathbf{q_{T}}^{2},\frac{n^{2}\mathbf{q_{T}}^{2}}{(2zpn)^{2}}\right)\;,

(17)

where the impact parameter
\mathbf{b}
is a
(d-2)
-dimensional vector. The numerical coefficient,
b_{0}=2e^{-\gamma_{E}}
.
We note that
n^{\mu}
dependence of
{\widetilde{F}}_{ca}
occurs through
the variable
\tilde{\lambda}=n^{2}(b_{0}^{2}/\mathbf{b}^{2})/(2zpn)^{2}

In general, the perturbative computation at
{\cal O}(\alpha_{\rm S}^{n})

of the TMD functions in
\mathbf{b}
space leads to divergent pole terms

with

1\leq m\leq 2n

\mathbf{b}

18

\displaystyle{\widetilde{F}}_{ca,\,{\rm az.in.}}\!\!\left(z;\frac{\mathbf{b}^{2}}{b_{0}^{2}},\frac{n^{2}b_{0}^{2}}{(2zpn)^{2}\,\mathbf{b}^{2}}\right)=Z_{c}\!\left(\alpha_{\rm S}(b_{0}^{2}/\mathbf{b}^{2}),\frac{n^{2}b_{0}^{2}}{(2zpn)^{2}\,\mathbf{b}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\sum_{b}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{dx}{x}\;{\widetilde{C}}_{cb}\!\left(x;\alpha_{\rm S}(b_{0}^{2}/\mathbf{b}^{2}),\epsilon,\frac{n^{2}b_{0}^{2}}{(2zpn)^{2}\,\mathbf{b}^{2}}\right)\;\;{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{ba}(z/x;b_{0}^{2}/\mathbf{b}^{2})\;\;,

4
Conclusion

In this talk we have considered the computation of collinear contributions to the transverse momentum resummation up to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{\rm S}^{2})
. Starting from the factorisation structure of scattering amplitudes in the collinear limit, we have introduced differential collinear functions that have a process independent structure. These collinear functions upon integration over the appropriate observable-dependent phase space gives the logarithmically-enhanced contributions to the corresponding observable. Through our formalism we have shown how these collinear functions can be defined without introducing what is known as rapidity divergences in the literature and presented explicit results for the azimuthally-correlated contributions to the TMD collinear function at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{\rm S}^{2})
.

Lagrangian solutions to the Vlasov-Poisson system with L 1 L^{1} density
Anna Bohun, François Bouchut, Gianluca Crippa

Abstract
. The recently developed theory of Lagrangian flows for transport equations with low regularity coefficients enables to consider non BV vector fields. We apply this theory to prove existence and stability of global Lagrangian solutions to the repulsive Vlasov-Poisson system with only integrable initial distribution function with finite energy. These solutions have a well-defined Lagrangian flow. An a priori estimate on the smallness of the superlevels of the flow in three dimensions is established in order to control the characteristics.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem for the classical Vlasov-Poisson system

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+E\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,

(1.1)

f(0,x,v)=f^{0}(x,v)\,,

(1.2)

where
f(t,x,v)\geq 0
is the distribution function,
t\geq 0
,
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and

E(t,x)=-\nabla_{x}U(t,x)

(1.3)

is the force field. The potential
U
satisfies the Poisson equation

-\Delta_{x}U=\omega(\rho(t,x)-\rho_{b}(x)),

(1.4)

with
\omega=+1
for the electrostatic (repulsive) case,
\omega=-1
for the gravitational (attractive) case,
and where the density
\rho
of particles is defined through

\rho(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(t,x,v)dv,

(1.5)

and
\rho_{b}\geq 0
,
\rho_{b}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is an autonomous background density.
Since we are in the whole space, the relation (
1.3
) together with the Poisson equation (
1.4
)
yield the equivalent relation

E(t,x)=\frac{\omega}{|S^{N-1}|}\frac{x}{|x|^{N}}*(\rho(t,x)-\rho_{b}(x)),

(1.6)

where the convolution is in the space variable.

The Vlasov-Poisson system has been studied for long.
Existence of local in time smooth solutions in dimension
N=3
has been obtained
in
[
15
]
after the results of
[
7
]
. Global smooth solutions have been
proved to exist in
[
21
]
(and simultaneously in
[
17
]
with a different method),
with improvements on the growth in time in
[
22
,
23
]
.
These solutions need a sufficiently smooth initial datum
f^{0}
.
In particular, the following theorem is a classical result due to Pfaffelmoser
[
21
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
N=3
, let
f^{0}
be a non-negative
C^{1}
function of compact support defined on
\mathbb{R}^{6}
.
Then there are a non-negative
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{7})
and
U\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
, which tends to zero at infinity
for every fixed
t
, satisfying equations (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) with
\rho_{b}=0
.
For each fixed
t
, the function
f(t,x,v)
has compact support. The solution is determined uniquely
by the initial datum
f^{0}
.

In a different spirit, global weak solutions were proved to exist in
[
6
,
12
,
14
]
, with only

f^{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
,
f^{0}\log^{+}f^{0}\in L^{1}
,
|v|^{2}f^{0}\in L^{1}
,
E^{0}\in L^{2}
(and
\rho_{b}=0
,
\omega=+1
).
Related results with
weak
initial data have been obtained in
[
20
,
16
,
24
]
.

In this paper we would like mainly to extend the existence result of
[
12
]
to initial data in
L^{1}

with finite energy (in the repulsive case
\omega=+1
), avoiding the
L\log^{+}\!L
assumption.
Our existence result is Theorem
8.4
. It involves a well-defined flow.
Even weaker solutions were considered in
[
25
,
18
,
19
]
,
where the distribution function is a measure. However, these solutions do not have well-defined characteristics.

Our approach uses the theory of Lagrangian flows for transport equations with vector fields having
weak regularity, developed in
[
13
,
1
,
4
,
11
,
5
]
, and recently in
[
10
,
2
,
8
]
. It enables to consider force fields that
are not in
W^{1,1}_{loc}
, nor in
BV_{loc}
. In this context we prove stability results
with strongly or weakly convergent initial distribution function. The flow is proved to converge strongly anyway.
Our main results were announced in
[
9
]
. Related results can be found in
[
3
]
.

2.
Conservation of mass and energy

We would like here to recall some basic identities related to the VP system.
Integrating (
1.1
) with respect to
v
and noting that the last term is in
v
-divergence form we obtain the
local conservation of mass

\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)+{\rm{div}}_{x}({J}(t,x))=0,

(2.1)

where the current
{J}
is defined by

{J}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}vf(t,x,v)\,dv.

(2.2)

Integrating again with respect to
x
, we obtain the global conservation of mass

\frac{d}{dt}\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(t,x,v)dxdv=\frac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho(t,x)dx=0.

(2.3)

Multiplying (
1.1
) by
\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}
, integrating in
x
and
v
, we get after integration by parts in
v

\frac{d}{dt}\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}f(t,x,v)dxdv-\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}E\cdot vf\,dxdv=0.

(2.4)

Using (
1.6
) and (
2.1
), one has

\partial_{t}E+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(\frac{\omega}{|S^{N-1}|}\frac{x}{|x|^{N}}*{J}_{k}\right)=0,

(2.5)

or in other words

\partial_{t}E=\omega\nabla_{x}(-\Delta_{x})^{-1}{\rm{div}}_{x}{J},

(2.6)

which means that
\partial_{t}E
is the the gradient component of
-\omega{J}
(Helmholtz projection).
We deduce that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}E\cdot\partial_{t}E\,dx=-\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}E\cdot{J}\,dx.

(2.7)

Using (
2.2
) in (
2.4
), we obtain the
conservation of energy

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}f(t,x,v)dxdv+\frac{\omega}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|E(t,x)|^{2}dx\right]=0\,.

(2.8)

The total conserved energy is the sum of the kinetic energy and of the potential energy multiplied by the factor
\omega=\pm 1
.
In particular, in the electrostatic case
\omega=+1
we deduce from (
2.8
) a uniform bound in time on both the kinetic and the potential energy,
assuming that they are finite initially.
In the gravitational case
\omega=-1
it is not possible to exclude that the individual terms of the kinetic and potential energy become unbounded in finite time,
while the sum remains constant. Indeed it is known that it does not happen in three dimensions as soon as

f^{0}
is sufficiently integrable, but we cannot exclude this a priori for only
L^{1}
solutions.

Note that the assumption
E^{0}\in L^{2}
is satisfied in 3 dimensions as soon as

\rho^{0}-\rho_{b}\in L^{6/5}
. However, in one or two dimensions, for
E^{0}
to be in
L^{2}
it is necessary
that
\int(\rho^{0}-\rho_{b})dx=0
, as is easily seen in Fourier variable.
It is also necessary that
\rho^{0}-\rho_{b}
has enough decay at infinity.
Thus in one or two dimensions, in order to have finite energy,
\rho_{b}
cannot be zero identically.

3.
Regularity of the force field for
L^{1}
densities

3.1.
Singular integrals

Definition 3.1
.

A function
K
is a
singular kernel of fundamental type
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
if the following properties hold:

(1)

K|_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
.

(2)

There exists a constant
C_{0}\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccc}|K(x)|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{|x|^{N}},&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.\end{array}

(3.1)

(3)

There exists a constant
C_{1}\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccc}|\nabla K(x)|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{|x|^{N+1}},&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.\end{array}

(3.2)

(4)

There exists a constant
A_{2}\geq 0
such that

\left|\int_{R_{1}<|x|<R_{2}}K(x)dx\right|\leq A_{2},

(3.3)

for every
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<\infty
.

Theorem 3.2

(Calderón-Zygmund)
.

A singular kernel of fundamental type
K
has an extension as a distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{N}

(still denoted by
K
), unique up to a constant times Dirac delta at the origin, such that

\hat{K}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Define

Su=K*u,\quad\textrm{for }u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(3.4)

in the sense of multiplication in the Fourier variable. Then we have the estimates for
1<p<\infty

\begin{array}[]{cc}||Su||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{N,p}(C_{0}+C_{1}+||\hat{K}||_{L^{\infty}})||u||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},&u\in L^{p}\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).\end{array}

(3.5)

If
K
is a singular kernel of fundamental type, we call the associated operator
S
a singular integral operator on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We define then the Fréchet space
\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\cap_{m\in{\mathbb{N}},\,1<p<\infty}W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and its dual
\mathscr{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the space of tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Since all singular integral operators are bounded on
\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by duality we can define the operator

S
also
\mathscr{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathscr{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
In particular it enables to define
Su
for
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
or for
u
a measure.
The result
Su
is in
\mathscr{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

3.2.
The split vector field

Let
\rho(t,x)\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
. We denote by

b(t,x,v)=(b_{1},b_{2})(t,x,v)=(v,E(t,x))=\Bigl(v,-\omega\nabla_{x}(-\Delta_{x})^{-1}(\rho(t,x)-\rho_{b}(x))\Bigr)

(3.6)

the associated vector field on
(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then the Vlasov equation can be written in the form of the transport equation
\partial_{t}f+b\cdot\nabla_{x,v}f=0
.
In the following subsections we establish bounds on the vector field
b
.

3.3.
Local integrability

For
L^{1}
densities, we have the weak estimates from the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\hphantom{=\ }\left|\left|\left|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(\rho(t,x)-\rho_{b}(x))\right|\right|\right|_{M^{\frac{N}{N-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
\displaystyle\leq\left|\left|\left|{\frac{1}{|S^{N-1}|}|x|^{1-N}}*|\rho(t,x)-\rho_{b}(x)|\right|\right|\right|_{M^{\frac{N}{N-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
\displaystyle\leq c_{N}||\rho(t,x)-\rho_{b}(x)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\end{array}

(3.7)

where
|||u|||_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\equiv\sup_{\gamma>0}\gamma\mathop{\mathscr{L}^{N}}(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,s.t.\,|u(x)|>\gamma\})^{1/p}
.
It follows that

|||E|||_{L^{\infty}((0,T);M^{\frac{N}{N-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\leq c_{N}||\rho-\rho_{b}||_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))},

(3.8)

and using the inclusion
M^{\frac{N}{N-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{p}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
1\leq p<\frac{N}{N-1}
we conclude that

b\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{p}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v}))
for any
1\leq p<\frac{N}{N-1}
, since
v\in L^{p}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v})
for any
p
.

3.4.
Spatial regularity

Since
b_{1}=v
is smooth, the only non-trivial gradient is the one of
b_{2}=E
,
indeed the differential matrix of the vector field is given by

Db=\left(\begin{matrix}D_{x}b_{1}&D_{v}b_{1}\\
D_{x}b_{2}&D_{v}b_{2}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&{\rm Id}\\
D_{x}E&0\end{matrix}\right).

(3.9)

We have by (
3.6
)

(D_{x}E)_{ij}\equiv\partial_{x_{j}}E_{i}=-\omega\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}((-\Delta_{x})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{b}))\quad\mbox{for }1\leq i,j\leq N.

(3.10)

It is well-known that the operator
\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}(-\Delta_{x})^{-1}
is a singular integral operator.
Its kernel is

K_{ij}(x)=-\frac{1}{|S^{N-1}|}\frac{\partial}{\partial_{x_{j}}}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{|x|^{N}}\right),

(3.11)

it is given outside of the origin by

\displaystyle K_{ij}(x)=\frac{1}{|S^{N-1}|}\left(N\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{N+2}}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{|x|^{N}}\right),\quad\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}.

(3.12)

The kernel satisfies the conditions of Subsection
3.1
,
and
\hat{K}_{ij}(\xi)=-\xi_{i}\xi_{j}/|\xi|^{2}
.
Thus (each component of)
D_{x}E
is a singular integral of an
L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
function.

3.5.
Time regularity

According to (
2.6
),
\partial_{t}E
is a singular integral of the current
{J}

defined by (
2.2
).
Using the bounds available for solutions with finite mass and energy

||f(t,\cdot)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v})},\iint|v|^{2}f(t,x,v)dxdv\leq C,

(3.13)

and since
|v|\leq 1+|v|^{2}
, we get that
{J}\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}))
.
Hence
\partial_{t}E
is a singular integral of an
L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}))
function.
In particular,

\partial_{t}E\in L^{\infty}((0,T);\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})).

(3.14)

4.
Lagrangian flows

Suppose that
f
is a smooth solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) with
f(0,x,v)=f^{0}
. Then
f
is constant along the characteristics
(X(s,t,x,v),V(s,t,x,v))
, which solve the system of equations

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{dX}{ds}(s,t,x,v)=V(s,t,x,v),\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{dV}{ds}(s,t,x,v)=E(s,X(s,t,x,v),V(s,t,x,v)),\end{cases}

(4.1)

with initial data
X(t,t,x,v)=x
and
V(t,t,x,v)=v
.
Thus the solution can be expressed as
f(t,x,v)=f^{0}(X(0,t,x,v),V(0,t,x,v))
.

In order to extend this notion of characteristics to non-smooth solutions, we define regular Lagrangian flows, which are defined in an almost everywhere sense.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
b\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2N};\mathbb{R}^{2N})
, and
t\in[0,T)
.
A map
Z:[t,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2N}
is a regular Lagrangian flow
starting at time
t
for the vector field
b
if

(1)

for a.e.
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}
the map
s\mapsto Z(s,z)
is an absolutely continuous integral solution of
\dot{\beta}(s)=b(s,\beta(s))
for
s\in[t,T]
with
\beta(t)=z
.

(2)

There exists a constant
L
independent of
s
such that

\mathscr{L}^{2N}(Z(s,\cdot)^{-1}(A))\leq L\mathscr{L}^{2N}(A)

for every Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2N}
.

The constant
L
is called the compressibility constant of
Z
.

Definition 4.2
.

Define the sublevel of the flow as the set

G_{\lambda}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}:|Z(s,z)|\leq\lambda\text{ for almost all }s\in[t,T]\}.

(4.2)

5.
Stability estimate for Lagrangian flows

We summarize the main result from
[
10
]
in the following regularity setting of the vector field in arbitrary dimension.
We say that a vector field
b
satisfies
(R1)
if
b
can be decomposed as

\frac{b(t,z)}{1+|z|}=\tilde{b}_{1}(t,z)+\tilde{b}_{2}(t,z)

(5.1)

where
\tilde{b}_{1}\in L^{1}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2N}))
,
\tilde{b}_{2}\in L^{1}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2N}))

We assume also that

satisfies
(R2)
: for every

\partial_{z_{j}}b=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}S_{jk}g_{jk}

(5.2)

where
S_{jk}
are singular integrals of fundamental type on
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
and
g_{jk}\in L^{1}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2N}))
.
Moreover, we assume condition
(R3)

(5.3)

We recall the following stability theorem from
[
10
]
, where we denote by
B_{r}

the ball with center

and radius

in

.

,

•

from

,

•

Proof.

We summarize the modifications of the proof of Theorem
5.1
from
[
10
]
.
The main added difficulty is the singular integral operators on
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}
instead of
\mathbb{R}^{2N}

More general situations with singular integrals of measures instead of

are considered in
[
8
]
.
Let
z=(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We estimate the quantity

\Phi_{\delta}(s)=\int\limits_{B_{r}\cap G_{\lambda}\cap\widebar{G}_{\lambda}}\log\left(1+\frac{|Z(s,z)-\widebar{Z}(s,z)|}{\delta}\right)dz\,.

(5.8)

Differentiating with respect to time, we get

\displaystyle\Phi_{\delta}^{\prime}(s)\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{r}\cap G_{\lambda}\cap\widebar{G}_{\lambda}}\frac{|b(s,Z(s,z))-\widebar{b}(s,\widebar{Z}(s,z))|}{\delta+|Z(s,z)-\widebar{Z}(s,z)|}dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\widebar{L}}{\delta}||b(s,\cdot)-\widebar{b}(s,\cdot)||_{L^{1}(B_{\lambda})}

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{B_{r}\cap G_{\lambda}\cap\widebar{G}_{\lambda}}\min\biggl\{\frac{|b(s,Z(s,z))|+|b(s,\widebar{Z}(s,z))|}{\delta},

\displaystyle\frac{|b_{1}(s,Z(s,z))-b_{1}(s,\widebar{Z}(s,z))|}{|Z(s,z)-\widebar{Z}(s,z)|}+\frac{|b_{2}(s,Z(s,z))-b_{2}(s,\widebar{Z}(s,z))|}{|Z(s,z)-\widebar{Z}(s,z)|}\biggr\}dz.

Using the special form of

, we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi_{\delta}^{\prime}(s)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\widebar{L}}{\delta}||b(s,\cdot)-\widebar{b}(s,\cdot)||_{L^{1}(B_{\lambda})}

(5.9)

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{B_{r}\cap G_{\lambda}\cap\widebar{G}_{\lambda}}\min\biggl\{\frac{|b(s,Z(s,z))|+|b(s,\widebar{Z}(s,z))|}{\delta},

\displaystyle\frac{|b_{1}(V(s,z))-b_{1}(\widebar{V}(s,z))|}{|V(s,z)-\widebar{V}(s,z)|}+\frac{|b_{2}(s,X(s,z))-b_{2}(s,\widebar{X}(s,z))|}{|X(s,z)-\widebar{X}(s,z)|}\biggr\}dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\widebar{L}}{\delta}||b(s,\cdot)-\widebar{b}(s,\cdot)||_{L^{1}(B_{\lambda})}+\textrm{Lip}(b_{1})\mathscr{L}^{2N}(B_{r})

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{B_{r}\cap G_{\lambda}\cap\widebar{G}_{\lambda}}\min\biggl\{\frac{|b(s,Z(s,z))|+|b(s,\widebar{Z}(s,z))|}{\delta},

\displaystyle\frac{|b_{2}(s,X(s,z))-b_{2}(s,\widebar{X}(s,z))|}{|X(s,z)-\widebar{X}(s,z)|}\biggr\}dz.

Using assumption
(R2a)
, we can use the estimate of
[
10
]
on the difference quotients of

,

\frac{|b_{2}(s,X(s,z))-b_{2}(s,\widebar{X}(s,z))|}{|X(s,z)-\widebar{X}(s,z)|}\leq\mathscr{U}(s,X(s,z))+\mathscr{U}(s,\widebar{X}(s,z)),

(5.10)

where
\mathscr{U}(s,.)\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for fixed

\mathscr{U}(s,\cdot)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{m}M_{j}(S_{jk}g_{jk}(s,.)),

(5.11)

with

a smooth maximal operator on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Next, we can define the function

\mathscr{Z}(s,x,v)=\mathscr{U}(s,x)\mathbbm{1}_{(x,v)\in\widebar{B_{\lambda}}},

(5.12)

and we notice that since the above integrals are over
G_{\lambda}\cap\widebar{G}_{\lambda}
, we can
replace the right-hand side of (
5.10
) by
\mathscr{Z}(s,Z(s,z))+\mathscr{Z}(s,\widebar{Z}(s,z))
.
Then, for given
\varepsilon>0
, we can decompose

g_{jk}=g_{jk}^{1}+g_{jk}^{2}
, with
\|g_{jk}^{1}\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\varepsilon
and

\|g_{jk}^{2}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}
,

having support in a

of finite measure.
This gives rise to two type of terms
\mathscr{Z}^{1}
and
\mathscr{Z}^{2}
.
Since all the
\mathscr{Z}
terms have compact support in

, this allows to perform the same
estimates as in
[
10
]
. We finally get

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{2N}(B_{r}\cap\{|Z(\tau,\cdot)-\widebar{Z}(\tau,\cdot)|>\gamma\})

(5.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log(1+\gamma/\delta)}\int_{t}^{\tau}\Phi_{\delta}^{\prime}(s)\,ds+\mathscr{L}^{2N}(B_{r}\setminus G_{\lambda})+\mathscr{L}^{2N}(B_{r}\setminus\widebar{G}_{\lambda}).

By choosing

\varepsilon

we conclude the proof as in
[
10
]
.
∎

The case of low space dimension

We first recall the following lemma from

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

This lemma allows us to control the superlevels of

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
b
be the vector field in (
3.6
), with

For

Proof.

It is clear that

\frac{v}{1+|x|+|v|}\in L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{\infty}_{x,v}),

(6.1)

and

\frac{E(t,x)}{1+|x|+|v|}=\frac{E(t,x)}{1+|x|+|v|}\mathbbm{1}_{|v|\leq|E(t,x)|}+\frac{E(t,x)}{1+|x|+|v|}\mathbbm{1}_{|v|>|E(t,x)|}\equiv\tilde{E_{1}}+\tilde{E_{2}}.

Clearly
\tilde{E_{2}}\in L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{\infty}_{x,v})
, and if

\tilde{E_{1}}\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}_{x,v})

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|E(t,x)|}{1+|x|+|v|}\mathbbm{1}_{|v|\leq|E(t,x)|}dxdv\\
\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|E(t,x)|\biggl(\int\limits_{|v|\leq|E(t,x)|}\frac{1}{|v|}dv\biggr)dx=2\pi\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|E(t,x)|^{2}dx.\end{array}

(6.3)

In the case

.
∎

6.2.
The case of three space dimensions

The condition

being not satisfied in

in order to control the superlevels.
For getting this we integrate in space a function growing slower at infinity than
\log(1+|Z|)

Proposition 6.3
.

Let

be as in (

) with

,

)
with

\omega=+1
,

, and
\rho_{b}\in L^{1}\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

Then

on
L
,

,
||E||_{L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{2}_{x})}

||{J}||_{L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{1}_{x})}

and one has

Proof.

Step 1.) Let
Z:[t,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a regular Lagrangian flow relative to

starting at time
t

L
and sublevel
G_{\lambda}
.
Denoting

, we have the ODEs

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\dot{X}(s,x,v)=V(s,x,v),\\
&\dot{V}(s,x,v)=E(s,X(s,x,v)).\end{cases}

(6.4)

Recalling that

, one has

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\frac{|V(s,x,v)|^{2}}{2}=V(s,x,v)\cdot\partial_{s}V(s,x,v)=E(s,X(s,x,v))\cdot\partial_{s}X(s,x,v)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{s}[U(s,X(s,x,v))]+\partial_{t}U(s,X(s,x,v)).

(6.5)

This computation is indeed related to the form of the Hamiltonian for (
1.1
),
{\mathscr{H}}=|v|^{2}/2+U(t,x)
.

We are going to bound the superlevels of
V(s,x,v)
. We claim that

\iint_{B_{r}}\sup_{s\in[t,T]}\left(1+\log\Bigl(1+|V(s,x,v)|^{2}/2\Bigr)\right)^{\alpha}dxdv\leq A,

(6.6)

where

, and for some constant

depending on

,
T
,
r
,
\alpha
, and on the norms

||E||_{L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{2}_{x})}
,
||{J}||_{L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{1}_{x})}
,
\|(-\Delta)^{-1}\rho_{b}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
Assume for the moment that this holds. From the lower bound

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\hphantom{\geq\ }\iint_{B_{r}}\sup_{s\in[t,T]}\left(1+\log\Bigl(1+|V(s,x,v)|^{2}/2\Bigr)\right)^{\alpha}dxdv\\
\displaystyle\geq\mathscr{L}^{6}(B_{r}\setminus\widetilde{G}_{\lambda})(1+\log(1+\lambda^{2}/2))^{\alpha},\end{array}

(6.7)

with
\widetilde{G}_{\lambda}
the sublevel of

, we get that

\mathscr{L}^{6}(B_{r}\setminus\widetilde{G}_{\lambda})\leq\frac{A}{(1+\log(1+\lambda^{2}/2))^{\alpha}}.

(6.8)

Next, we remark that by the first equation in (
6.4
), whenever
(x,v)\in\widetilde{G}_{\lambda}
one has

|X(s,x,v)|\leq|x|+|s-t|\lambda
, and
|Z(s,x,v)|\leq|x|+(1+T)\lambda
.
Thus for
\lambda>r
, one has
B_{r}\setminus G_{\lambda}\subset B_{r}\setminus\widetilde{G}_{(\lambda-r)/(1+T)}
,
which enables to conclude the proposition (for
\lambda\leq r
we can just bound

\mathscr{L}^{6}(B_{r}\setminus G_{\lambda})
by
\mathscr{L}^{6}(B_{r})
).

Step 2.) By Step 1, it is enough to prove that we have a decomposition

\left(1+\log\left(1+\frac{|V(s,\cdot,\cdot)|^{2}}{2}\right)\right)^{\alpha}\leq f_{1}+f_{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v}),

(6.9)

for
(x,v)\in B_{r}
, where
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
are independent of
s\in[t,T]
.
Let

\beta(y)=\left(1+\log\left(1+y\right)\right)^{\alpha},\quad\mbox{for }y\geq 0.

(6.10)

Then

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}(y)=\frac{\alpha(1+\log(1+y))^{\alpha-1}}{1+y},\\
\displaystyle 0<-\beta^{\prime\prime}(y)\leq\frac{(1+\log(1+y))^{\alpha-1}}{(1+y)^{2}}.\end{array}

(6.11)

Using (
6.2
), we compute

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\left[\beta\left(\frac{|V(s,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)\right]

(6.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(-\partial_{s}[U(s,X(s,x,v))]+\partial_{t}U(s,X(s,x,v))\Bigr)\beta^{\prime}\left(\frac{|V(s,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{s}\left[U(s,X(s,x,v))\beta^{\prime}\left(\frac{|V(s,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+U(s,X(s,x,v))\beta^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{|V(s,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)V(s,x,v)\cdot E(s,X(s,x,v))

\displaystyle+\partial_{t}U(s,X(s,x,v))\beta^{\prime}\left(\frac{|V(s,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right).

Thus, integrating between
t
and
s
,

\displaystyle\left(1+\log\left(1+\frac{|V(s,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)\right)^{\alpha}

(6.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha U(s,X(s,x,v))}{\left(1+\frac{|V(s,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)\left(1+\log\left(1+\frac{|V(s,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)\right)^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha U(t,x)}{\left(1+\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}\right)\left(1+\log\left(1+\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}\right)\right)^{1-\alpha}}+\left(1+\log\left(1+\frac{{|v|^{2}}}{2}\right)\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\int^{s}_{t}\left\{U(\tau,X(\tau,x,v))V(\tau,x,v)\cdot E(\tau,X(\tau,x,v))\beta^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{|V(\tau,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\partial_{t}U(\tau,X(\tau,x,v))\beta^{\prime}\left(\frac{|V(\tau,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)\right\}d\tau.

Step 3.)
Since
E(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we have by the Sobolev embedding that

U(t,\cdot)\in L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Thus clearly

\frac{U(t,x)}{1+\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}}\in L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})\subset L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v}).

(6.14)

Next, since
\omega=+1
and
\rho\geq 0
, one has

, with
U_{\rho}\geq 0
.
Thus
U\geq-\|U_{\rho_{b}}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
Thus the first three terms in the expansion (

) are upper bounded in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})
.
It remains to estimate the integral. We can bound it by
\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}

\Phi_{1}:=\int^{T}_{t}\left|U(\tau,X(\tau,x,v))V(\tau,x,v)\cdot E(\tau,X(\tau,x,v))\beta^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{|V(\tau,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)\right|d\tau,

(6.15)

\Phi_{2}:=\int^{T}_{t}\left|\partial_{t}U(\tau,X(\tau,x,v))\beta^{\prime}\left(\frac{|V(\tau,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)\right|d\tau.

(6.16)

Note that
\Phi_{1}
,
\Phi_{2}
are independent of
s
.
We estimate
\Phi_{1}
in
L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})\subset L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})
.
Passing the

norm under the integral and

(X(\tau,x,v),V(\tau,x,v))

(x,v)
, this gives (up to a factor

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\frac{U(\tau,x)v\cdot E(\tau,x)}{\left(1+\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\log\left(1+\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}\right)\right)^{1-\alpha}}\right|^{3/2}dxdv

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|U(\tau,x)E(\tau,x)|^{3/2}dx\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{2^{3/4}\,dv}{\left(1+\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}\right)^{9/4}\left(1+\log\left(1+\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}\right)\right)^{3(1-\alpha)/2}}

Thus
\Phi_{1}\in L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})
.

Step 4.) For
\Phi_{2}
, we notice that

satisfies (
2.6
) and
E=-\nabla_{x}U

\partial_{t}U=-\omega(-\Delta_{x})^{-1}{\rm{div}}_{x}{J}.

(6.17)

|||\partial_{t}U(\tau,\cdot)|||_{M^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq c||{J}(\tau,\cdot)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(6.18)

Therefore, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\left|\left|\frac{\partial_{t}U(\tau,X(\tau,x,v))}{\left(1+\frac{|V(\tau,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)\left(1+\log\left(1+\frac{|V(\tau,x,v)|^{2}}{2}\right)\right)^{1-\alpha}}\right|\right|\right|_{M^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})}

\displaystyle\leq L^{2/3}\left|\left|\left|\frac{\partial_{t}U(\tau,x)}{\left(1+\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}\right)\left(1+\log\left(1+\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}\right)\right)^{1-\alpha}}\right|\right|\right|_{L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3};M^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}))}

where the last integral is convergent since

.
From the inclusion

,
and integrating (

) over

, we get (
6.9
) as desired.
∎

7.
Renormalized solutions and Lagrangian solutions

We recall the different notions of weak solutions for the Vlasov-Poisson system.
We shall always assume that
1\leq N\leq 3
, and
we consider an initial datum
f^{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v})
,
f^{0}\geq 0
.
We introduce first renormalized solutions, following
[
12
,
14
]
.

Definition 7.1
.

We say that
f\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v}))
,
f\geq 0
,
is a solution to the Vlasov equation (
1.1
) in the renormalized sense if for all test functions
\beta\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
with
\beta
bounded, we have that

\partial_{t}\beta(f)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\beta(f)+{\rm{div}}_{v}\Bigl(E(t,x)\beta(f)\Bigr)=0,

(7.1)

in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v})
.

We next introduce the notion of Lagrangian solutions.

Definition 7.2
.

Let be given a vector field
b(t,x,v)=(v,E(t,x))
as in (
3.6
) for some
\rho\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
,
\rho\geq 0
,
and
\rho_{b}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We assume that
E\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, and that (
2.6
) holds
with
{J}\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.
We assume furthermore that either
N=1
or
2
, or
N=3
and
\omega=+1
,
\rho_{b}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for some
p>3/2
.
We consider regular Lagrangian flows
Z
as in Definition
4.1
, except that now
s\in[0,T]

instead of
s\in[t,T]
(forward-backward flow), and with compression constant
L
independent of
t\in[0,T]
.
Then according to Subsections
3.3
,
3.4
, Proposition
6.2
,
Proposition
6.3
, the vector field
b
satisfies assumptions
(R1a), (R2a), (R3)
.
Therefore, Theorem
5.2
yields the uniqueness of the forward-backward regular Lagrangian flow
Z=(X,V)
.
The whole theory of
[
10
]
then applies indeed, with very little modifications in the proofs.
In particular there is existence and uniqueness of the forward-backward regular Lagrangian flow, with compression constant
1
,
and stability. We can thus define in accordance with
[
10
]
a Lagrangian solution

f
to the Vlasov equation (
1.1
) by

f(t,x,v)=f^{0}\Bigl(X(s=0,t,x,v),V(s=0,t,x,v)\Bigr),\qquad\mbox{for all }t\in[0,T],

(7.2)

for arbitrary
f^{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v})
.
It verifies in particular
f\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v}))
, and it is indeed also a renormalized solution.

Definition 7.3
.

We define a Lagrangian solution to the Vlasov-Poisson system as a couple
(f,E)
such that

(1)

f\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v}))
,
f\geq 0
,
|v|^{2}f\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v}))
,

(2)

E(t,x)
is given by the convolution (
1.6
) with
\rho(t,x)=\int f(t,x,v)dv
,

\rho_{b}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\rho_{b}\geq 0
(and if
N=3
,
\omega=+1
,
\rho_{b}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for some
p>3/2
),

(3)

E\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}))
,

(4)

The relation (
2.6
) holds with
{J}(t,x)=\int vf(t,x,v)dv
,

(5)

f
is a Lagrangian solution to the Vlasov equation, in the sense of (
7.2
).

8.
Existence of Lagrangian solutions

8.1.
Compactness

In this subsection we prove two compactness results, Theorems
8.2
and
8.3
,
for families of Lagrangian solutions to the Vlasov-Poisson system,
with strongly or weakly convergent initial data.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
g(x)=\frac{x}{|x|^{N}}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and denote by
\tau_{h}g(x)=g(x+h)
.
Then for any
1<p<\frac{N}{N-1}
,

\|\tau_{h}g(x)-g(x)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c|h|^{\alpha},

(8.1)

with
\alpha=1-N+N/p>0
, and where

depends on
N,p
.

Proof.

Fix
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
h\not=0
. For
|x|>2|h|
, we have for all
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,
|x+\theta h|\geq|x|-\theta|h|>|x|/2
,
thus we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle|\tau_{h}g(x)-g(x)|\leq|h|\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq 1}|\nabla g(x+\theta h)|\\
\displaystyle\hphantom{|\tau_{h}g(x)-g(x)|}\leq|h|\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq 1}\frac{c_{N}}{|x+\theta h|^{N}}\leq c_{N}\frac{|h|}{|x|^{N}}.\end{array}

(8.2)

Then we estimate

\displaystyle\int\limits_{|x|>2|h|}\frac{|h|^{p}}{|x|^{Np}}dx=c_{N}|h|^{p}\int\limits^{\infty}_{2|h|}r^{N-1-Np}dr=c_{N}|h|^{p}\frac{(2|h|)^{N-Np}}{Np-N}=c_{N,p}|h|^{p-Np+N}.

Next, for
|x|\leq 2|h|
, we write

|\tau_{h}g(x)-g(x)|\leq\frac{1}{|x+h|^{N-1}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{N-1}},

(8.3)

and clearly

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int\limits_{|x|\leq 2|h|}\left(\frac{1}{|x+h|^{(N-1)p}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{(N-1)p}}\right)dx\\
\displaystyle\leq 2\int\limits_{|y|\leq 3|h|}\frac{dy}{|y|^{(N-1)p}}=c_{N}\int\limits^{3|h|}_{0}r^{N-Np+p-1}dr=c_{N,p}|h|^{N-Np+p},\end{array}

(8.4)

since the last integral is convergent for
p<\frac{N}{N-1}
.
∎

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
(f_{n},E_{n})
be a sequence of Lagrangian solutions to the Vlasov-Poisson system satisfying

f_{n}^{0}\to f^{0}\text{ in }L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v}),

(8.5)

and

\iint|v|^{2}f_{n}(t,x,v)dxdv+\int|E_{n}(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq C,\qquad\mbox{for all }t\in[0,T].

(8.6)

Then, up to a subsequence
f_{n}
converges strongly in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v}))
to
f
,

E_{n}
converges in
C([0,T];L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
to
E
, and
(f,E)

is a Lagrangian solution to the Vlasov-Poisson system with initial datum
f^{0}
.
Moreover, the regular forward-backward Lagrangian flow
Z_{n}(s,t,x,v)
converges to
Z(s,t,x,v)

locally in measure in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, uniformly in
s,t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Step 1.) (Equi-integrability)

Because of (
7.2
) and (
8.5
) we have

\|f_{n}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|f_{n}^{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq M.

(8.7)

Then because of the bounds (
8.7
), (
8.6
),
and applying Propositions
6.2
,
6.3
, one has for any
r>0

\mathscr{L}^{2N}\{(x,v)\in B_{r}:\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}|Z_{n}(s,t,x,v)|>\gamma\}\rightarrow 0,\mbox{ as }\gamma\rightarrow\infty,\mbox{uniformly in }t,n.

(8.8)

Since the sequence
f_{n}^{0}
is uniformly equi-integrable, and since
Z_{n}
is measure-preserving,
we have by (
7.2
) and (
8.8
) that
f_{n}(t,\cdot)
is equi-integrable,
uniformly in
t,n
. Consequently,
\rho_{n}(t,\cdot)
is also equi-integrable, uniformly in
t,n
.
Using the bound (
8.6
),
vf_{n}(t,\cdot)
is also equi-integrable,
and therefore
{J}_{n}(t,\cdot)
is also equi-integrable, uniformly in
t,n
.

Step 2.) (Compactness of the field)

In order to prove that
E_{n}(t,x)\to E(t,x)\textrm{ in }L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x})
,
we first look at the compactness in
x
.
Denote by
\tau_{h}E_{n}(t,x)=E_{n}(t,x+h)
. Then using (
1.6
),

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\hphantom{\leq}||\tau_{h}E_{n}(t,\cdot)-E_{n}(t,\cdot)||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\tau_{h}\frac{x}{|x|^{N}}-\frac{x}{|x|^{N}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}||\rho_{n}(t,\cdot)-\rho_{b}(\cdot)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.\end{array}

(8.9)

Thus according to Lemma
8.1
, we get for any
1<p<\frac{N}{N-1}
that

||E_{n}(t,x+h)-E_{n}(t,x)||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0,\qquad\mbox{as }h\to 0,\mbox{ uniformly in }t,n.

(8.10)

Then, because of (
2.6
), we have that
\partial_{t}E_{n}
is bounded in

L^{\infty}((0,T);\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
. Applying Aubin’s lemma, we conclude that

E_{n}
is compact in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x})
.
Thus after extraction of a subsequence,
E_{n}(t,x)\to E(t,x)
strongly in
L^{1}((0,T);L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}))
.

Step 3.) (Convergence of the flow)

Because of the bound (
8.6
), one has
E\in L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.
Also, using the uniform bounds on
\rho_{n}
,
{J}_{n}
in
L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
and
the uniform equi-integrability obtained in Step 1, one has up to a subsequence
\rho_{n}\rightarrow\rho
,
{J}_{n}\rightarrow{J}

in the sense of distributions, with
\rho,{J}\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.
We can pass to the limit in (
1.6
) and (
2.6
).
Therefore,
b=(v,E)
satisfies the assumptions
(R1a), (R2a), (R3)
and Definition
7.2
applies.
According to
[
10
, Lemma 6.3]
, since (
8.8
) holds,
and
\rho_{n}
are equi-integrable, we deduce the convergence of
Z_{n}
to
Z

locally in measure in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v}
, uniformly with respect to
s,t\in[0,T]
,
where
Z
is the regular forward-backward Lagrangian flow associated to
b
.

Step 4.) (Convergence of
f
)

Using the convergence (
8.5
), we can apply
[
10
, Proposition 7.3]
,
and we conclude that
f_{n}\rightarrow f
in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v}))
,
where
f
is the Lagrangian solution to the Vlasov equation with coefficient
b
and initial datum
f^{0}
.
It follows that
\rho_{n}\to\rho=\int fdv
in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}))
.
By lower semi-continuity, we get from (
8.6
) that

\iint|v|^{2}f(t,x,v)dxdv+\int|E(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq C,\qquad\mbox{for all }t\in[0,T].

(8.11)

The bound (
8.6
) gives also that
{J}_{n}\rightarrow J=\int vfdv

in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}))
.
Therefore,
(f,E)
is a Lagrangian solution to the Vlasov-Poisson system.
Using (
1.6
), we get that
E_{n}\rightarrow E
in
C([0,T];L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
,
which concludes the proof.
∎

.

8.2.
Existence

We conclude this section by the existence of Lagrangian solutions to the Vlasov-Poisson system
for initial datum in
L^{1}
with finite energy, in the repulsive case.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
N=1,2
or
3
, and let
f^{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v})
,
f^{0}\geq 0
.
Define
\rho^{0}
and
E^{0}
by

\rho^{0}(x)=\int f^{0}(x,v)dv,\qquad E^{0}(x)=\frac{\omega}{|S^{N-1}|}\frac{x}{|x|^{N}}*(\rho^{0}(x)-\rho_{b}(x)),

(8.13)

with
\omega=+1
(repulsive case),

\rho_{b}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\rho_{b}\geq 0
, and in the case
N=3

\rho_{b}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for some
p>3/2
.
Assume that the initial energy is finite,

\iint|v|^{2}f^{0}(x,v)dxdv+\int|E^{0}(x)|^{2}dx<\infty.

(8.14)

Then there exists a Lagrangian solution
(f,E)
to the Vlasov-Poisson system
defined for all time, having
f^{0}
as initial datum, and satisfying for all
t\geq 0

\iint|v|^{2}f(t,x,v)dxdv+\int|E(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq\iint|v|^{2}f^{0}(x,v)dxdv+\int|E^{0}(x)|^{2}dx.

(8.15)

Proof.

We use the classical way of getting global weak solutions to the Vlasov-Poisson system,
i.e. we approximate the initial datum
f^{0}
by a sequence of smooth data
f_{n}^{0}\geq 0
with compact support.
We approximate also
\rho_{b}
by smooth
\rho_{b}^{n}\geq 0
with compact support (with
\int(\rho^{0}_{n}-\rho_{b}^{n})dx=0

if
N=1,2
). It is possible to do that with the upper bounds

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{limsup}}_{n\to\infty}\iint|v|^{2}f^{0}_{n}(x,v)dxdv\leq\iint|v|^{2}f^{0}(x,v)dxdv,\\
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{limsup}}_{n\to\infty}\int|E^{0}_{n}(x)|^{2}dx\leq\int|E^{0}(x)|^{2}dx.\end{array}

(8.16)

Then, for each
n
, there exists a smooth classical solution
(f_{n},E_{n})
with initial datum
f^{0}_{n}
,
to the Vlasov-Poisson system, defined for all time
t\geq 0
. Note that we can alternatively consider
a regularized Vlasov-Poisson system.
Since
\omega=+1
, the conservation of energy (
2.8
) gives for all
t\geq 0
,

\iint|v|^{2}f_{n}(t,x,v)dxdv+\int|E_{n}(t,x)|^{2}dx=\iint|v|^{2}f_{n}^{0}(x,v)dxdv+\int|E_{n}^{0}(x)|^{2}dx.

(8.17)

The couple
(f_{n},E_{n})
is in particular a Lagrangian solution to the Vlasov-Poisson system, for
all intervals
[0,T]
.
We can therefore apply Theorem
8.2
. Extracting a diagonal subsequence, we get the convergence
of
(f_{n},E_{n})
to
(f,E)
as stated in Theorem
8.2
, where
(f,E)
is a Lagrangian
solution to the Vlasov-Poisson system defined for all time, with
f^{0}
as initial datum.
The bound (
8.11
), together with (
8.16
), gives (
8.15
).
∎

Let us end with a remark on measure densities.
When considering a sequence of solutions to the Vlasov-Poisson system, the vector fields

b_{n}=(v,E_{n})
have a gradient in
(x,v)
of the form

Db_{n}=\left(\begin{matrix}D_{1}b_{n}^{1}&D_{2}b_{n}^{1}\\
D_{1}b_{n}^{2}&D_{2}b_{n}^{2}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\mathrm{Id}\\
S(\rho_{n}-\rho_{b})&0\end{matrix}\right),

(8.18)

where the index
1
stands for
x
,
2
for
v
, and where

S
is a singular integral operator.
If we require only that
D_{1}b_{n}^{2}
converges in the sense of distributions to
D_{1}b^{2}=S(\rho-\rho_{b})
,
for some measure
\rho\in\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then we are in the setting of
[
8
]
.
If
\rho_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{1}((0,T);\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
,
and
b_{n}\to b
strongly in
L^{1}((0,T);L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{v}))
with
b
satisfying (
8.18
),
we conclude that
Z_{n}\to Z
strongly, where
Z
is the regular Lagrangian flow associated to
b
.
However, we are not able to define the push forward (
7.2
) of a measure
f^{0}
.
This prevents from applying the context of
[
8
]
to the Vlasov-Poisson system with
measure data.

Holomorphic forms and non-tautological cycles on moduli spaces of curves
Veronica Arena, Samir Canning, Emily Clader, Richard Haburcak, Amy Q. Li, Siao Chi Mok, Carolina Tamborini

Abstract
. We prove, for infinitely many values of g g and n n , the existence of non-tautological algebraic cohomology classes on the moduli space ℳ g , n \mathcal{M}_{g,n} of smooth, genus- g g , n n -pointed curves. In particular, when n = 0 n=0 , our results show that there exist non-tautological algebraic cohomology classes on ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g} for g = 12 g=12 and all g ≥ 16 g\geq 16 . These results generalize the work of Graber–Pandharipande and van Zelm, who proved that the classes of particular loci of bielliptic curves are non-tautological and thereby exhibited the only previously-known non-tautological class on any ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g} : the bielliptic cycle on ℳ 12 \mathcal{M}_{12} . We extend their work by using the existence of holomorphic forms on certain moduli spaces ℳ ¯ g , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n} to produce non-tautological classes with nontrivial restriction to the interior, via which we conclude that the classes of many new double-cover loci are non-tautological.



Introduction

While the full cohomology ring of the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
of stable curves is generally intractable to study, there is a distinguished subring called the tautological ring that admits an explicit generating set and yet is rich enough to contain most cohomology classes coming from natural algebraic cycles. Specifically, the tautological rings
RH^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\subseteq H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
are defined simultaneously for all
g
and
n
as the smallest system of
\mathbb{Q}
-subalgebras that contains the fundamental classes
[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}]
and is closed under pushforward by the forgetful and gluing maps between the moduli spaces. There is an analogous subring
RH^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n})\subseteq H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n})
, defined as the image of
RH^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
under restriction. A natural question, for both
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, is to understand when the equality
RH^{*}=H^{*}
occurs.

On
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, all cohomology classes are tautological by work of
[
Kee92
]
, and on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}
, all even-degree cohomology classes are tautological by work of Petersen
[
Pet14
]
. It follows that all algebraic cohomology classes on
\mathcal{M}_{0,n}
and
\mathcal{M}_{1,n}
are tautological. There are several more cases in the literature where, for small values of
g
and
n
, all algebraic cohomology on
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is shown to be tautological
[
PT14
,
Fab90a
,
Fab90b
,
Iza95
,
PV15
,
CL23
,
CL22b
,
CLP23
]
; see Figure
1
for a summary.

The first example of a non-tautological algebraic class was produced by Graber and Pandharipande
[
GP03
]
: the class on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2,20}
consisting of curves that admit a double cover of a genus-one curve where the marked points are pairwise switched by the covering involution. Van Zelm
[
vZ18
]
significantly extended these results, showing that a similarly-defined bielliptic cycle is non-tautological in an infinite family of cases; in particular, his results show that when
g\geq 2
, there are only finitely many pairs
(g,n)
for which it is possible that
RH^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})=H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
. Lian
[
Lia22
]
generalized these results further, showing that loci of higher-degree covers of higher-genus curves also give rise to non-tautological classes.

Producing non-tautological algebraic classes on the interior
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, on the other hand, has proved significantly harder. Graber–Pandharipande’s original cycle restricts to a non-tautological class on
\mathcal{M}_{2,20}
, but the bielliptic cycle considered by van Zelm was only shown to restrict to a non-tautological class on
\mathcal{M}_{g,2m}
when
g\geq 2
and
g+m=12
. In particular, prior to the present work, there were only eleven pairs
(g,n)
for which the existence of a non-tautological algebraic cycle on
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
was known.

Our main result vastly extends the previous work on non-tautological classes on
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, exhibiting such classes in an infinite family of cases; see Figure

]

, there is a particular gluing map

under which, for certain values of

, and

, one can prove that

(1)

i^{*}[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow h,0,2m}]=\alpha[\Delta]+B

for

In the special case when

.

Let

and set

[\mathcal{B}_{g\rightarrow h,0,2m}]\in H^{6h-6+2k}(\mathcal{M}_{g,2m})

is non-tautological.

From here, by applying Theorem
B

Remark
.

Since tautological classes on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2m}

1.
Preliminaries on double cover cycles

In this section, we introduce the double cover cycles of interest and prove the cases in which equation (
1
) holds.

1.1.
Double cover cycles

The loci of double covers we consider are the images of moduli stacks of admissible double covers under forgetful morphisms to the moduli space of curves. We use the definition of the moduli space of admissible covers from
[
SvZ20
]
, which is a finite cover of the the space of admissible covers defined in
[
ACV03
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Admbar(g,h)_{2m}
be the stack of admissible double covers, which parameterizes tuples

(f:C\rightarrow D;x_{1},\ldots,x_{r};y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{2m-1},y_{2m})

such that

•

f:C\to D
is a double cover of connected nodal curves of arithmetic genus
g
and
h
, respectively;

•

x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in C
are precisely the smooth ramification points of
f
;

•

y_{1},\dots,y_{2m}\in C
are such that the involution induced by
f
exchanges
y_{2i-1}
and
y_{2i}
;

•

the image under
f
of each node of
C
is a node of
D
;

•

the pointed curves
\big(C;(x_{i})_{i=1}^{r},(y_{i})_{i=1}^{2m}\big)
and
\big(D;(f(x_{i}))_{i=1}^{r},f(y_{2i-1})_{i=1}^{m}\big)
are stable.

Remark 1.2
.

A Riemann–Hurwitz calculation shows that
r=2g+2-4h
.

For any
n\leq r
, there is a natural map

(2)

\phi_{n}:\Admbar(g,h)_{2m}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+2m}

sending an admissible cover to the stabilization of
(C;x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{2m})
, and we define

\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow h,n,2m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\phi_{n}\left(\Admbar(g,h)_{2m}\right).

The map
\Admbar(g,h)_{2m}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,r+m}
sending an admissible cover to the marked target curve
D
is finite, so one has

\dim\left(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\to h,n,2m}\right)=\dim\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,r+m}\right)=2g+m-h-1.

Calculating the codimension thus gives

\left[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\to h,n,2m}\right]\in H^{2(g+h+n+m-2)}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2m+n}\right).

\tau(Q_{i})=Q_{j}

f(\hat{Q}_{i})=f(\hat{Q}_{j})=N
for some node
N
of
T
, which is necessarily a non-separating node. However, the fact that
\tau(C_{1})=C_{2}
means that the covering involution on
S
swaps the corresponding components
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, and
f
maps
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
birationally to the component
T_{1}
of
T
containing
N
. Since
N
is a non-separating node,
T_{1}
has geometric genus at most
h-1
, contradicting the fact that it is birational to the curves
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of geometric genus
h
.
∎

In light of Lemmas
1.4
and
1.5
, to finish the proof of Proposition
1.3
, it suffices to show that
\tau
cannot map each component to itself. Here, the argument diverges from
[
vZ18
]
, and the numerical conditions in Proposition
1.3
are used.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
g\geq 2h
. If
m\geq 1
or
g\geq 4h+2
, then
\tau
cannot fix
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
.

Proof.

First, note that, due to the ordering of marked points in the definition of
j
in (
4
), the map
\tau
must send marked points on
C_{1}
to marked points on
C_{2}
. Thus,
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
cannot be fixed by
\tau
if
m\geq 1
.

Now, assume that
m=0
, so
g\geq 4h+2
by assumption. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\tau
fixes both
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. As in Lemma
1.5
, we first note that the preimage in
S
of each node
Q_{i}\in C
must be a node
\hat{Q}_{i}\in S
and not a contracted rational component. To see this, note that a contracted rational component must have two marked ramification points that are not nodes (by the definition of admissible covers), so the nodes are not ramification points and are therefore swapped by the covering involution; this implies that the involution
\tau
swaps the branches of
Q_{i}
, contradicting that
\tau
fixes
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
.

In light of the above, the set of nodes of
S
is
\{\hat{Q}_{1},\ldots,\hat{Q}_{k}\}
, and each of these is sent by
f
to a node by the definition of admissible covers. Since
\tau
induces an involution on the nodes of
S
, this implies that there are at least
\frac{k}{2}
nodes in
T
. Furthermore, each node of
T
is non-separating; to see this, note that the complement of any node in
T
is the image under
f
of the complement of either one or two nodes in
S
. Since the
k
nodes of
S
are all non-separating, and
k>2
by the condition
g\geq 4h+2
, it follows that these complements are both connected. We have therefore shown that
T
has at least
\frac{k}{2}
non-separating nodes. Furthermore,
T
has two irreducible components; this follows from the fact that
S
has two irreducible components (since
C
has two irreducible components and we have already seen that no component of
S
is contracted in
C
) and both of these components are fixed by the covering involution. Therefore, the arithmetic genus of
T
is at least

\frac{k}{2}-1=\frac{g}{2}-h-\frac{1}{2}>h,

contradicting the fact that the arithmetic genus of
T
is
h
.
∎

We can now give a quick proof of Proposition
1.3
.

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

By Lemma
1.6
, we must have
\tau(C_{1})=C_{2}
, and therefore the map
\zeta:\mathcal{M}_{h,k}\rightarrow F
is surjective on closed points by Lemma
1.5
. The result now follows from Lemma
1.4
.
∎

2.
Holomorphic forms and pure weight cohomology

In this section, we review the relevant background on holomorphic forms, and we prove, in certain genus-one and genus-two cases, existence results for holomorphic forms on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. The key reason that such forms are of interest for us is the following.

Fact 2.1
.

Any nonzero element of
H^{k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
or
H^{0,k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
has nonzero image under the restriction map
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\rightarrow H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n})
.

To see this, one uses the long exact sequence in cohomology, together with the Thom isomorphism, to yield an exact sequence

(6)

H^{k-2}(\widetilde{\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n}})\rightarrow H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\rightarrow H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n}),

where
\widetilde{\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n}}
is the normalization of the boundary, which is a disjoint union of smooth and proper Deligne–Mumford stacks. The first map in this exact sequence is a direct sum of morphisms of pure Hodge structures of type
(1,1)
, so after complexification, its image intersects
H^{k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
and
H^{0,k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
trivially. Therefore, exactness implies that any nonzero element of
H^{k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
or
H^{0,k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})

maps to a nonzero element of
H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n})\otimes\mathbb{C}
, which proves Fact
2.1
.

Recalling the statement of Theorem
B
from the introduction, we will be particularly interested in the existence of holomorphic
(3h-3+k)
-forms on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k}
. When
h=1
, these are already known to exist when
k
is odd and sufficiently large.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
k\geq 11
be odd and
k\neq 13
. Then
H^{k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,k})\neq 0
.

Proof.

This is well-known; see
[
Get98
]
or
[
CLP23
, Proposition 2.2]
.
∎

We now show that a similar result holds when
h=2
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
k\geq 14
be even. Then
H^{k+3,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2,k})\neq 0
.

In order to prove Proposition
2.3
, we will make use of certain facts regarding the weight filtration on cohomology, which we now review. For any variety or Deligne–Mumford stack
X
, Deligne’s mixed Hodge theory
[
Del71
,
Del74
]
implies that
H^{k}(X)=H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})
admits an increasing filtration

0=W_{-1}H^{k}(X)\subseteq W_{0}H^{k}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq W_{2k}H^{k}(X)=H^{k}(X),

called the weight filtration. When
X
is smooth, the weights are bounded in the interval
[k,2k]

[
PS08
, Proposition 4.20]
; that is, we have

0=W_{k-1}H^{k}(X)\subseteq W_{k}H^{k}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq W_{2k}H^{k}(X)=H^{k}(X).

The lowest-weight component
W_{k}H^{k}(X)\subseteq H^{k}(X)
is called the pure weight cohomology of
X
; it is a pure Hodge structure containing the classes of all algebraic cycles in
H^{k}(X)
. When
X
is both smooth and proper, we have
W_{k}H^{k}(X)=H^{k}(X)
.

More generally, if
\mathbb{V}
is a polarizable variation of Hodge structures of weight
j
on a smooth variety
X
, then
H^{k}(X,\mathbb{V})
admits a weight filtration with weights bounded in the interval
[k+j,2k+j]
. Furthermore, compactly-supported cohomology
H_{c}^{k}(X,\mathbb{V})
also admits a weight filtration, whose weights are bounded in the interval
[j,k+j]
.

Now, consider the case
X=\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
. This is a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack and therefore
H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n})
has weights in the interval
[k,2k]
. In this case, the pure weight cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is related to the cohomology of the compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
as follows.

Fact 2.4
.

The image of the restriction
H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\rightarrow H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n})
is
W_{k}H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n})
.

Indeed, for any Zariski open subset
U
of a smooth proper Deligne–Mumford stack
X
the restriction map
H^{k}(X)\rightarrow W_{k}H^{k}(U)
is surjective
[
PS08
, Proposition 4.20]
. Because
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is smooth and proper, we have
W_{k}H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})=H^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
, yielding Fact
2.4
.

Equipped with this background, we are prepared to prove Proposition
2.3
.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{2,k}^{\text{rt}}
be the moduli space of genus-two curves with rational tails. Explicitly,
\mathcal{M}_{2,k}^{\text{rt}}
is the fiber product of the inclusion
\mathcal{M}_{2}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
with the forgetful map
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2,k}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
, and thus it comes equipped with a smooth and proper forgetful map
f:\mathcal{M}_{2,k}^{\rt}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{2}
. In particular, the Leray spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(\mathcal{M}_{2},R^{q}f_{*}\mathbb{Q})\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(\mathcal{M}_{2,k}^{\rt},\mathbb{Q})

degenerates at
E_{2}
.

Because
\mathcal{M}_{2}
is affine,
H^{p}(\mathcal{M}_{2},R^{q}f_{*}\mathbb{Q})=0
for
p>3=\dim(\mathcal{M}_{2})
. Moreover, when
q
is odd,
H^{p}(\mathcal{M}_{2},R^{q}f_{*}\mathbb{Q})=0
because of the action of the hyperelliptic involution on the fibers of
R^{q}f_{*}\mathbb{Q}
. From the spectral sequence, we obtain an injection of mixed Hodge structures

(7)

0\rightarrow H^{3}(\mathcal{M}_{2},R^{k}f_{*}\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{k+3}(\mathcal{M}_{2,k}^{\rt}).

In light of (
7
), to prove the proposition, it suffices to show that the
(k+3,0)
part of
W_{k+3}H^{3}(\mathcal{M}_{2},R^{k}f_{*}\mathbb{Q})
is nonzero. Indeed, by the analogue of Fact
2.4
for the rational-tails moduli space, the image of
H^{k+3}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2,k})\rightarrow H^{k+3}(\mathcal{M}^{\text{rt}}_{2,k})
is precisely
W_{k+3}H^{k+3}(\mathcal{M}^{\text{rt}}_{2,k})
. This restriction map is a morphism of pure Hodge structures, so the fact that the
(k+3,0)
part of the Hodge structure
W_{k+3}H^{k+3}(\mathcal{M}^{\text{rt}}_{2,k})
is nonzero implies that
H^{k+3,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2,k})\neq 0
.

By
[
PT14
, Propositon 4.5]
, the local system
R^{k}f_{*}\mathbb{Q}
contains a summand
\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3}
. Explicitly,
\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3}
is the restriction to
\mathcal{M}_{2}
of the symplectic local system on
\mathcal{A}_{2}
(the moduli space of principally polarized abelian surfaces) corresponding to the representation of
\mathrm{Sp}_{4}
of highest weight
(\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3)
. The Thom–Gysin exact sequence yields a right exact sequence of Hodge structures

(8)

\displaystyle W_{k+1}H^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3})

\displaystyle\rightarrow W_{k+3}H^{3}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3})

\displaystyle\rightarrow W_{k+3}H^{3}(\mathcal{M}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3})\rightarrow 0,

where the first map is of Hodge type
(1,1)
.
Let
H^{3}_{!}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3})
denote the image of compactly-supported cohomology under the natural map

H^{3}_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3})\rightarrow H^{3}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3}).

Because the mixed Hodge structure
H_{c}^{3}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3})
has weights at most
k+3
and
H^{3}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3})
has weights at least
k+3
, we have that
H^{3}_{!}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3})
is a pure Hodge structure of weight
k+3
. By
[
FC90
]
, the
(k+3,0)
part of the pure Hodge structure
H^{3}_{!}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3})
is naturally identified with
S_{6,\frac{k}{2}}
,
the space of cusp forms for
\mathrm{Sp}_{4}
of type
\mathrm{Sym}^{6}\otimes\det^{\otimes\frac{k}{2}}
; see also
[
FP13
, Section 3]
. This space is nonzero for even
k\geq 16
,
k\neq 18

[
Tsu82
,
BCFvdG17
]
. Moreover, for degree reasons, the
(k+3,0)
part of

H^{3}_{!}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3})
is not in the image of the first map in (
8
). Hence, we obtain a sub-Hodge structure of
W_{k+3}H^{3}(\mathcal{M}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\frac{k}{2}+3,\frac{k}{2}-3})
with
(k+3,0)
part nonzero. This proves the claim for all
k\neq 14,18
.

A similar argument shows that the
(k+3,0)
part of the Hodge structure
W_{k+3}H^{k+3}(\mathcal{M}_{2,k}^{\rt})
is nonzero for
k=14,18
, but this time using the local systems
\mathbb{V}_{7,7}
and
\mathbb{V}_{9,9}
. By
[
Pet15
, Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.3]
,
H^{3}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{7,7})
contains a copy of the space
S_{18}
and
H^{3}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{9,9})
contains a copy of the space
S_{22}
, where
S_{\ell}
is the pure Hodge structure corresponding to cusp forms for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
of weight
\ell
. The
(\ell-1,0)
parts of these pure Hodge structures are nonzero when
\ell=18,22
, so the claim is proved in these remaining cases.
∎

.

An alternative way to handle the

is to construct a holomorphic

-form on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2,k}

3.
Non-tautological double cover cycles

In this section, we apply Proposition
1.3
to prove Theorem
B
: namely, that
[\mathcal{B}_{g\rightarrow h,0,2m}]
is non-tautological under a certain assumption on the existence of holomorphic forms on a moduli space of stable curves. By combining this result with the genus-one and genus-two cases in which such forms were shown to exist (Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
), we deduce Theorem
A
.

3.1.
Non-tautological double cover cycles on the interior

First, let us recall the statement of Theorem
B
.

Theorem B
.

Let
g\geq 2h
and set
k:=g-2h+m+1
. Suppose that

H^{3h-3+k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\neq 0.

If
m\geq 1
or
g\geq 4h+2
, then

[\mathcal{B}_{g\rightarrow h,0,2m}]\in H^{6h-6+2k}(\mathcal{M}_{g,2m})

is non-tautological.

Proof.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
[\mathcal{B}_{g\rightarrow h,0,2m}]
is tautological. Given that tautological classes on
\mathcal{M}_{g,2m}
are, by definition, the restrictions of tautological classes on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2m}
, it follows from excision that

(9)

[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow h,0,2m}]=T+B

where
T
is a tautological class and
B
is an algebraic cycle pushed forward from the boundary
\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2m}
. Pulling back both sides of (
9
) under
i:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2m}
and applying the Hodge–Künneth decomposition, we obtain an element of

H^{3h-3+k,3h-3+k}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k}\right)\cong\bigoplus_{a+c=b+d=3h-3+k}H^{a,b}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\otimes H^{c,d}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k}).

We claim that

(a)

i^{*}[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow h,0,2m}]
has a contribution in
H^{3h-3+k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\otimes H^{0,3h-3+k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})
;

(b)

i^{*}T
has no contribution in
H^{3h-3+k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\otimes H^{0,3h-3+k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})
;

(c)

i^{*}B
has no contribution in
H^{3h-3+k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\otimes H^{0,3h-3+k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})
.

These three statements together contradict the equation

i^{*}[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow h,0,2m}]=i^{*}T+i^{*}B,

so proving them will complete the proof.

To prove (a), note that by Proposition
1.3
, we have
i^{*}[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow h,0,2m}]=\alpha[\Delta]+B^{\prime}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
and some algebraic cycle
B^{\prime}
supported on
\partial(\mathcal{M}_{h,k}\times\mathcal{M}_{h,k})
. The assumption that
H^{3h-3+k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\neq 0
(hence
H^{0,3h-3+k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\neq 0
, as well) implies that there is a nonzero contribution to the Hodge–Künneth decomposition of
[\Delta]
in the component
H^{3h-3+k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\otimes H^{0,3h-3+k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})
. The class
B^{\prime}
restricts trivially to the interior, and so it has no contribution in this component because any nonzero class in
H^{3h-3+k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\otimes H^{0,3h-3+k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})
restricts nontrivially to the interior by Fact
2.1
. Therefore,

\alpha[\Delta]+B^{\prime}
has a nonzero contribution in
H^{3h-3+k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\otimes H^{0,3h-3+k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})
.

To prove (b), note that because
T
is tautological, every component of the Künneth decomposition of
i^{*}T
is tautological and therefore algebraic
by
[
GP03
, Proposition 1]
. Thus, the only contributions to
i^{*}T
under the Hodge–Künneth decomposition can be in components of the form
H^{p,p}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\otimes H^{q,q}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})
.

What remains, then, is to prove (c). To do so, we first note that
B
can be assumed algebraic: indeed, both
T
and
[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow h,0,2m}]
are algebraic, and the excision exact sequence can be applied in the Chow ring to obtain a representative of
B
in Chow. Since
B
is pushed forward from
\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2m}
, it is a sum of classes of irreducible subvarieties, each supported on a boundary divisor of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2m}
. By addressing each of these classes separately, it suffices to prove (c) in the case that
B
is supported on a single boundary divisor
D
. This boundary divisor is the image of a map

i_{2}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2m},

where
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
is either
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g-1,2m+1}
or a product
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},n_{1}+1}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},n_{2}+1}
. If
B
is supported on
D
, then
B=i_{2*}(B^{\prime\prime})
for a class
B^{\prime\prime}\in H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime})
. From here, we consider two cases, depending on whether
D
contains the image of
i
or not.

First, suppose that
D
contains the image of
i
. (This happens, for instance if
D=D_{\text{irr}}
is the closure of the locus of curves with a non-separating node and
g>2h
so that
i
imposes at least two nodes.) Then, similarly to the proof of
[
vZ18
, Theorem 2]
, we can factor
i
as

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k}\xrightarrow{i_{1}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{i_{2}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2m}.

Thus,

i^{*}(B)=i_{1}^{*}i_{2}^{*}(B)=i_{1}^{*}i_{2}^{*}i_{2*}(B^{\prime\prime})=i_{1}^{*}(c_{1}(N))\cdot i_{1}^{*}B^{\prime\prime}

by the projection formula, where
N
is the normal bundle to the gluing map
i_{2}
. Since
i_{1}^{*}(c_{1}(N))\in H^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})
is algebraic, its contribution to the Hodge–Künneth decomposition can only be in components of the form
H^{p,p}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\otimes H^{q,q}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})
in which
2p+2q=2
and hence at least one of
p
or
q
is nonzero. Because the decomposition respects products, it is therefore impossible for
i_{1}^{*}(c_{1}(N))\cdot i_{1}^{*}B^{\prime\prime}
to have a contribution in the component
H^{3h-3+k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\otimes H^{0,3h-3+k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})
.

Finally, suppose that
D
does not contain the image of
i
. (This happens, for instance, if
g>2h
and
D\neq D_{\text{irr}}
.) Then we claim that
i^{*}B
is pushed forward from
\partial(\mathcal{M}_{h,k}\times\mathcal{M}_{h,k})
. To prove this, suppose instead that
i^{*}B
has nonempty intersection with
\mathcal{M}_{h,k}\times\mathcal{M}_{h,k}
. The intersection describes pairs of smooth curves
(C_{1},C_{2})
whose images under
i
land in
B
, which is contained in
D
by assumption. For each pair of smooth curves
(C_{1},C_{2})
, the resulting curve
C\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}i(C_{1},C_{2})
only has nodes arising from the pairwise gluing of the
k-m
points, so the boundary divisors containing
C
are fully determined by the value of
k-m
. Since
i(\mathcal{M}_{h,k}\times\mathcal{M}_{h,k})
intersects
D
nontrivially, it follows that
i(\mathcal{M}_{h,k}\times\mathcal{M}_{h,k})
must lie in
D
, and by continuity, the entire image of
i
must also lie in
D
. This is a contradiction, and so
i^{*}B
is pushed forward from
\partial(\mathcal{M}_{h,k}\times\mathcal{M}_{h,k})
.

Thus,
i^{*}B
maps to zero under the restriction to
H^{6h-6+2k}(\mathcal{M}_{h,k}\times\mathcal{M}_{h,k})
. Any nonzero class in
H^{3h-3+k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\otimes H^{0,3h-3+k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})
restricts nontrivially to the interior by Fact
2.1
, so
i^{*}B
can have no nonzero contribution in this Hodge–Künneth component. This proves (c) and therefore finishes the proof.
∎

We apply Theorem
B
in the case where
h=1
or
h=2
, in which case the values of
k
for which the condition
H^{3h-3+k,0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,k})\neq 0
of Theorem
B
is satisfied are described by Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
.

Corollary 3.1
.

If
g\geq 2
and
m\geq 0
are such that
g+m
is even and either
g+m=12
or
g+m\geq 16
, then the class

[\mathcal{B}_{g\rightarrow 1,0,2m}]\in H^{2g+2m-2}(\mathcal{M}_{g,2m})

is non-tautological. If
g\geq 4
and
m\geq 0
are such that
g+m
is odd and
g+m\geq 17
, then the class

[\mathcal{B}_{g\rightarrow 2,0,2m}]\in H^{2g+2m}(\mathcal{M}_{g,2m})

is non-tautological.

In particular, Corollary
3.1
exhibits non-tautological classes on
\mathcal{M}_{g,2m}
whenever
g\geq 4
and
m\geq 0
satisfy either
g+m=12
or
g+m\geq 16
, and similarly for
g=2
and
g=3
as long as
g+m
is even. Thus, the proof of Theorem
A
is immediate.

This line of reasoning also yields new examples of non-tautological classes on the compact moduli space: the locus of double covers of genus-two curves, extending results of Lian
[
Lia22
]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
g\geq 4
, and let
m\geq 0
be such that
g+m
is odd and
g+m\geq 17
.
Then the class

[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow 2,n,2m}]\in H^{2(g+m)}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+2m})

is non-tautological for all
0\leq n\leq 2g-6
.

Proof.

The
n=0
case follows from Corollary
3.1
. From here, one can use exactly the same argument as in
[
vZ18
, Lemma 8]
to show that if
[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow 2,n,2m}]
is non-tautological for some
n
, then
[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow 2,n+1,2m}]
is also non-tautological; the key point is that the pushforward of
[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow h,n+1,2m}]
under the forgetful morphism
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1+2m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+2m}
is a scalar multiple of
[\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow h,n,2m}]
by dimension considerations. Thus, the corollary follows for
n\leq 2g-6
, which, by Remark
1.2
, is the maximal number of ramification points.
∎

3.2.
Non-tautological classes on curves with rational tails

We conclude the paper by remarking that, although Theorem
A
only furnishes non-tautological classes on
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
when
n
is even, it is possible to use it to produce non-tautological classes with odd numbers of marked points by expanding to the larger moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\text{rt}}_{g,n}
of curves with rational tails. To do so, we denote by
\mathcal{B}^{\text{rt}}_{g\rightarrow h,n,2m}
the restriction of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g\rightarrow h,n,2m}
to
\mathcal{M}^{\text{rt}}_{g,n+2m}
, and we recall that the tautological ring of
\mathcal{M}^{\text{rt}}_{g,n+2m}
is defined as the image of
RH^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+2m})
under restriction. Then we have the following result.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
1\leq n\leq 2g-4h+2
.
If
[\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{rt}}_{g\rightarrow h,n-1,2m}]
is non-tautological, then
[\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{rt}}_{g\rightarrow h,n,2m}]
is non-tautological.

Proof.

The forgetful map
\pi:\mathcal{M}_{g,n+2m}^{\rt}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g,n-1+2m}^{\rt}
is proper and

\pi_{*}[\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{rt}}_{g\rightarrow h,n,2m}]=[\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{rt}}_{g\rightarrow h,n-1,2m}].

The result follows because tautological classes push forward to tautological classes under forgetful maps.
∎

Note that if
\mathcal{B}_{g\rightarrow h,n,2m}
is non-tautological, then so is
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{rt}}_{g\rightarrow h,n,2m}
. Thus, combining Proposition
3.3
with Corollary
3.1
, we find that there exist non-tautological algebraic cycles in
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{\text{rt}}_{g,n})
whenever
g\geq 2
and either
2g+n\in\{24,25\}
or
2g+n\geq 32
. Because all algebraic classes on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}
are already known to be tautological, this settles the question of the existence of non-tautological algebraic classes in
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{\text{rt}}_{g,n})
for all but finitely many values of
g
and
n
.

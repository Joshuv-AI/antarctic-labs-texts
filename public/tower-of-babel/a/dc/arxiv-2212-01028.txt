1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the everywhere regualiry of the minima of the
following class of vectorial integral functional of the calculus of variation

(1.1)

\mathcal{F}\left(u,\Omega\right)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{m}\left|\nabla u^{\alpha}\right|^{p}+G\left(x,u,\left|\nabla u^{1}\right|,...,\left|\nabla u^{m}\right|\right)\,dx

We make the following assumptions about the function
G
.

H.1:

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2\
and let
G:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}_{0,+}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a Caratheodory function, where
\mathbb{R}_{0,+}=\left[0,+\infty\right)
and
\mathbb{R}_{0,+}^{m}=\mathbb{R}_{0,+}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}_{0,+}
with
m\geq 1
; we make the following growth conditions on
G
: there exists a constant
L>1
such that

\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{m}\left|\xi^{\alpha}\right|^{q}-\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{m}\left|s^{\alpha}\right|^{q}-a\left(x\right)\leq G\left(x,s^{1},...,s^{m},\left|\xi^{1}\right|,...,\left|\xi^{m}\right|\right)\leq L\left[\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{m}\left|\xi^{\alpha}\right|^{q}+\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{m}\left|s^{\alpha}\right|^{q}+a\left(x\right)\right]

for
\mathcal{L}^{n}
a. e.
x\in\Omega
, for every
s^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
and for every
\xi^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha=1,...,m
and
m\geq 1
and with
a\left(x\right)\in L^{\sigma}\left(\Omega\right)
,
a(x)\geq 0
for
\mathcal{L}^{n}
a. e.

x\in\Omega
,
\sigma>\frac{n}{p}
,
1\leq q<\frac{p^{2}}{n}
and
1<p<n
.

Assuming that the previous growth hypothesis H.1 holds, we prove
the following regularity result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
; if
u\in W^{1,p}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
, with
m\geq 1
, is a local minimum of the functional
1.1

and
H.1
holds then
u^{\alpha}\in C_{loc}^{o,\beta_{0}}\left(\Omega\right)
for every
\alpha=1,...,m
, with
\beta_{0}\in\left(0,1\right)
.

We find the previous result nontrivial and quite surprising for three
reasons; the first reason lies in the existence of numerous examples of
non-regular vector problems, refer to [13, 19, 23]; the second reason is due
to the existence, olso, of many regularity results for vectorial problems
but all these results have strong hypotheses on the density function, which
is either very regular or in some sense convex, refer to [1-11, 14-18, 20,
21, 25, 30, 35-39, 42-46], differently from the works cited we only assume
that the density
G
is a Caratheodory function; finally the third reason
consists in the fact that the proof is extremely elementary, in the sense
that it uses known techniques introduced for the scalar case, referring to
[12, 22, 24, 28, 29, 31, 32, 33, 40, 41].

Recently, in [7-11, 25, 33] new classes of vectorial problems, with regular
weak solutions, have been introduced. In particular in [7], Cupini, Focardi,
Leonetti and Mascolo introduced the following class of vectorial functionals

(1.2)

\int\limits_{\Omega}f\left(x,\nabla u\right)\,dx

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m},n>1,m\geq 1
and

f\left(x,\nabla u\right)=\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{m}F_{\alpha}\left(x,\nabla u^{\alpha}\right)+G\left(x,\nabla u\right)

where

is a Carathéodory
function satisfying the following standard growth condition

k_{1}\left|\xi^{\alpha}\right|^{p}-a\left(x\right)\leq F_{\alpha}\left(x,\xi^{\alpha}\right)\leq k_{2}\left|\xi^{\alpha}\right|^{p}+a\left(x\right)

for every

and for almost every
x\in\Omega

real positive constants,

and

Mascolo analyze two different types of hypotheses on the

started by assuming that

with

(1.4)

\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{m}\Phi_{i,\alpha}\left(\left|\xi_{i}^{\alpha}\right|\right)\leq F_{\alpha}\left(x,\xi^{\alpha}\right)\leq L\left[\bar{B}_{\alpha}^{\beta_{\alpha}}\left(\left|\xi^{\alpha}\right|\right)+a\left(x\right)\right]

In particular, using the techniques presented in [25, 26, 28, 29], the

For completeness we remember that if
\Omega
is a open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u
is a Lebesgue measurable function then
L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
is the set of the class of the Lebesgue measurable function such
that
\int\limits_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p}\,dx<+\infty
and
W^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)
is the set of the function
u\in L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
such that its waek derivate
\partial_{i}u\in L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
. The spaces
L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
and
W^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)
are Banach spaces with the respective norms

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)}=\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

\left\|u\right\|_{W^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)}=\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\partial_{i}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)}

We say that the function
u:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
belong in
W^{1,p}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
if
u^{\alpha}\in W^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)
for every
\alpha=1,...,m
, where
u^{\alpha}
is the
\alpha
component of the
vector-valued function
u
; we end by remembering that
W^{1,p}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
is a Banach space with the norm

\left\|u\right\|_{W^{1,p}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}=\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{m}\left\|u^{\alpha}\right\|_{W^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)}

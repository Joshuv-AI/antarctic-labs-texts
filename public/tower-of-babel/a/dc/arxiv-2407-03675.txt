On characterization of weakly porous sets via dyadic coverings Thanks: This research was supported by the Russian Science Foundation (grant No. 23-11-00171).
Andrei V. Vasin

Abstract
. We consider the class of weakly porous sets in Euclidean spaces. As our main goal, we give a precise characterization in terms of dyadic covering of these sets. Also, we obtain the Carleson embedding inequality for porous sets.

Primary 28A75; Secondary 28A80

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Background

A set
E
of the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
is called porous if there exists a constant
C
such that for any arc
I\subset\mathbb{T}
there
is a subarc
M(I)\subset I
containing no points of
E
and satisfying

|M(I)|>C|I|.

Dyn’kin proved the following uniform entropy characterization of the porous set
[
5
]
:

(1)

\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,E)}dx\ <\log\frac{1}{|I|}+C,

where
C
is not depending on
I
. There is a discrete form of (
1
)

(2)

\sum_{J\subset I,\;J\cap E=\emptyset}|J|\log\frac{1}{|J|}\leq|I|\left(\log\frac{1}{|I|}+C\right)

with
C
independent of
I
.

The porosity condition and estimate (
1
) appear naturally
in the free interpolation problems for different classes of analytic functions
smooth up to the boundary of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
(see
[
5
]
).

Studying the weak embedding property of singular inner functions, Borichev, Nicolau and Thomas
[
3
, Lemma 7]
found the Carleson type characterization of porosity: a set
E\subset\mathbb{T}
is porous if and only if there exists
C
such that for any arc
I\subset\mathbb{T}
one has

(3)

\sum_{J\cap E\neq\emptyset,\;J\in\mathcal{D}(I)}|J|\leq C|I|,

where
\mathcal{D}(I)
is a dyadic decomposition of
I
.

Thus, in (
2
) the description of a porous set involves arcs free from points of the set, while in (
3
) arcs intersecting the set are used.

Our main goal in this paper is to extend property (
3
)
to the higher dimensional case, and to find explicit relations between (
2
) and (
3
) as well. Note that extension of the Dyn’kin inequality to the higher dimensional case is known to be related to the Aikawa
condition (see
[
4
]
and references therein).
Also, a further extension of Borichev-Nicolau-Thomas characterization together with the dyadic technique
(see
Meyer-Coifman
[
7
, p. 59, Lemma 5]
) leads to the dyadic Carleson embedding inequality for the porous sets.

We deal with certain generalization, and consider weakly porous sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
These sets on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
appeared in the study of the properties of the Fuchsian groups
[
9
]
, where
it was proved that the function

\log dist^{-1}(x,E)

belongs to the space
BLO(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
E
is weakly porous.
Anderson, Lehrbäck, Mudarra and Vähäkangas
[
2
]
extended the definition of
weakly porous sets to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Also, they find when the distance function

dist^{-\alpha}(x,E)

belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{1}
.
A precise quantitative characterization in terms of the so-called
Muckenhoupt exponent of
E
is also given in
[
2
]
.

1.2.
Dyadic decomposition

Throughout this paper, we consider
\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the Euclidean distance and the

d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. The Lebesgue measure of
E
is denoted by
|E|
, and if
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
dist(x;E)

denotes the distance from
x
to the set
E
.

We only consider cubes which are half-open and have sides parallel to the
coordinate axes. That is, a cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a set of the form

Q=[a_{1};b_{1})\times\dots\times[a_{d};b_{d})

with side-length
\ell(Q)=b_{1}-a_{1}=\dots=b_{d}-a_{d}
.
The dyadic decomposition of a cube
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is

\mathcal{D}(R)=\bigcup_{j\geq 0}\mathcal{D}_{j}(R)

where each
\mathcal{D}_{j}(R)
consists of the
2^{jd}
pairwise disjoint (half-open) cubes
Q
, with side length

\ell(Q)=2^{-j}\ell(R)
, such that

R=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}(R)}Q

for every
j=0,1,\dots
. The cubes in
\mathcal{D}(R)
are called dyadic cubes (with respect to
R
).
Let
j>0
and
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}(R)
. Then there exists a unique dyadic cube
\pi Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j-1}(R)

satisfying
Q\subset\pi Q
, and
|\pi Q|=2^{d}|Q|
.

Definition 1
.

Let
E
be a nonempty set.
For each cube
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
let

\mathcal{D}(R,E)=\{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R):\>Q\cap E\neq\emptyset\}

be a family of all dyadic cubes intersecting
E
, and
let

\mathcal{F}(R,E)=\{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(R):Q^{\prime}\neq R,\;\>Q^{\prime}\cap E=\emptyset,\;\pi Q^{\prime}\cap E\neq\emptyset\}

be a family of all maximal pairwise disjoint dyadic cubes not intersecting
E
.

Denote by
M(R)
one of the largest dyadic cubes
Q\in\mathcal{D}(R)
not containing points of
E
.

We start with a key result about changing the order of summation that may be of independent interest.

Proposition 1
.

Let
E\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set such that closure of
E
has zero Lebesgue measure. Then

(4)

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}|Q|=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q^{\prime}|\log\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}.

Remark 1
.

The integral version of Proposition
1
is better known. Given a cube
R
,
\ell(R)<1
, and a closed set
E
with
|E|=0
, one has the following formula of integration:

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mu(\lambda)}{\lambda}d\lambda=\int_{R}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,E)}dx,

where
\mu(\lambda)=|\{x\in R:dist(x,E)<\lambda\}|
is the distribution function.

Remark 2
.

In this paper, we refer to the binary logarithm
\log x=\log_{2}x
.

The sums in (
4
) are bounded for a set
E
of finite entropy over
R
:

\int_{R}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,E)}dx<\infty.

It follows from the next result for sets of general type.

Proposition 2
.

Let
E\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set such that the closure of
E
has zero Lebesgue measure. Then
for each cube
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(5)

\left|\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q^{\prime}|\log\frac{1}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}-\int_{R}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,E)}dx\right|<C|R|,

and

(6)

\left|\log\frac{1}{\ell(M(R))}-\inf_{R}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,E)}\right|<C

with the constant
C=C(d)
.

The original Meyer-Coifman lemma concerns to the Carleson embedding inequality for the dyadic covering of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Lemma 3

(
[
7
, p. 59, Lemma 5]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a family of dyadic cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
p_{Q}
,
Q\in\mathcal{D^{\prime}}
, be the sequence of positive numbers such that

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D^{\prime}},\>Q\subset R}p_{Q}\leq C|R|

for each
R\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
C
independent of
R
. Then for every sequence
a_{Q}\geq 0
,
Q\in\mathcal{D^{\prime}}
, we have

(7)

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D^{\prime}}}a_{Q}p_{Q}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\>\sup_{Q\ni x,\,Q\in\mathcal{D^{\prime}}}a_{Q}\>dx.

Here we prove.

Proposition 4
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set such that closure of
E
has zero Lebesgue measure.
For each cube
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
let
\{a_{Q}\}_{\mathcal{D}(R,E)}
be a sequence of non-negative scalars. Then there is a constant
C
which depends only on
d
and
E
such that

\int_{R}\>\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}a_{Q}\chi_{Q}\>dx\leq\int_{R}\>\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}a_{Q}\chi_{Q}\>\left(\log\frac{\ell(R)}{dist(x,E)}+C\right)dx.

Here
\chi_{Q}
is the characteristic function of a cube
Q
.

Observe that the estimate above is sharp in a sense.

1.3.
Weakly porous sets

Definition 2
.

Let
E
be a nonempty set.
A set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called weakly porous, if there are constants
0<c,\,\delta<1
such that for each cube
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

there exists a finite family
\mathcal{F}(R,E,\delta)\subset\mathcal{D}(R)
of pairwise disjoint dyadic cubes
Q\in\mathcal{D}(R)
such that
Q\cap E=\emptyset
and
|Q|\geq\delta|M(R)|
, and

(8)

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}(R,E,\delta)}|Q|\geq c|R|.

Instead of dyadic cubes, also general subcubes of
R
could be used in the definition of
weak porosity. However, the dyadic formulation
[
2
]
is convenient from the point of view of our
proofs.

The following properties are easy to verify using the definition of weak porosity:

W1.

If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is porous, then
E
is weakly porous.

W2.

E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is weakly porous if and only if the closure
E
is weakly porous.

W3.

If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is weakly porous, then
|E|=0
. This is a consequence of the Lebesgue
differentiation theorem.

W4.

Weak porous set is nowhere dense.

By the John-Nirenberg theorem (see
[
8
, p.145]
), there is a condition of the weak porosity equivalent to
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Proposition 5

(
[
2
,
9
]
)
.

A set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is weakly porous if and only if the function

\log dist^{-1}(x,E)

belongs to the space
BLO(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This means that
there exists
C
such that for each cube
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
it holds

\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,E)}dx\leq\essinf_{R}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,E)}+C.

1.4.
Main results for weakly porous sets.

As application of Proposition
1
, we give the following characterization of weakly porous sets by means of dyadic coverings.

On the one side we prove the discrete version of the BLO condition from Proposition
5
, which involves the dyadic covering of complement of a weakly porous set.

On the other side we obtain the description by means of the dyadic covering of the weakly porous sets themselves.

Theorem 6
.

A set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is weakly porous if and only if there is a constant
C
such that for each cube

R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
any of the following properties hold.

(i)

For the family
\mathcal{F}(R,E)
of dyadic cubes it holds

\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q^{\prime}|\log\frac{1}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}\leq\left(\log\frac{1}{\ell(M(R))}+C\right)|R|.

(ii)

For the family
\mathcal{D}(R,E)
of dyadic cubes it holds

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}|Q|\leq\left(\log\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(M(R))}+C\right)|R|.

(iii)

Let

\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E)=\{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E):\;|Q|\leq|M(R)|\},

then

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E)}|Q|\leq C|R|.

Remark 3
.

Note that in all properties (i)–(iii) we have the same constant
C
.

1.5.
Main results for porous sets.

For the porous sets, when
|R|\leq C|M(R)|
with
C
independent of
R
, we have an extension to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the results from
[
3
,
5
]
and
application of
[
7
, p. 59]
.

Theorem 7
.

A set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is porous if and only if there is a constant
C
such that for each cube

R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
any of the following properties hold.

(i)

The Dyn’kin inequality: for each cube
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one has

\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q^{\prime}|\log\frac{1}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}\leq|R|\left(\log\frac{1}{\ell(R)}+C\right).

(ii)

The Borichev-Nicolau-Thomas characterization: for each cube
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
one has

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}|Q|\leq C|R|,

which is the Carleson measure property

(iii)

The Carleson embedding inequality: let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\{a_{Q}\}_{\mathcal{D}(R,E)}
be a sequence of non-negative scalars. Then there is the constant
C
which depends only on
d
,
p
and
E
such that

\left\|\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}a^{p}_{Q}\chi_{Q}\right)^{1/p}\right\|_{p}\leq C\left\|\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}a_{Q}\chi_{Q}\right\|_{p}.

Here
\chi_{Q}
is the characteristic function of a cube
Q
, and

\|f\|_{p}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}.

Observe that (iii) with maximal-function techniques implies the following inequality, which is related to
[
6
, Theorem 2.10]
.

Corollary 8
.

Let a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be porous,
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and let
\{a_{Q}\}_{\mathcal{D}(R,E)}
be a sequence of non-negative scalars. Then there is the constant
C
which depends only on
d,\,p
and
E
such that

\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}a_{Q}\chi_{Q}\right\|_{p}\leq C\left\|\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}a^{q}_{Q}\chi_{Q}\right)^{1/q}\right\|_{p}.

2.
Proofs

2.1.
Proof of Proposition
1

The underlying ideas are in principle similar to
those in
[
3
]
, but the higher dimensional case requires several nontrivial modifications.

Cubes of the family
\mathcal{F}(R,E)
are pairwise disjoint and

\bigcup_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}Q^{\prime}\supseteq R\backslash\overline{E}

for any cube
R
. Since

|\overline{E}|=0
, it holds that

\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q^{\prime}|=|R|.

So, for the left hand side of (
4
) we have, by changing the summation order,

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}|Q|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}\;\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q\cap Q^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}\;\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}|Q\cap Q^{\prime}|.

If
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(R)
and
Q^{\prime}\cap Q\neq\emptyset
, then
Q\subset Q^{\prime}
or
Q^{\prime}\subset Q
.
Since
Q\cap E\neq\emptyset
and
Q^{\prime}\cap E=\emptyset
, it holds
Q^{\prime}\subset Q
and so

|Q^{\prime}\cap Q|=|Q^{\prime}|
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}|Q|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}\quad\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E),\>Q\supset Q^{\prime}}|Q^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q^{\prime}|\quad\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E),\>Q\supset Q^{\prime}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q^{\prime}|\;\mathcal{N}(Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E),\>Q\supset Q^{\prime}),

where

\mathcal{N}(Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E),\>Q\supset Q^{\prime})
is a number of cubes from
\mathcal{D}(E,R)
containing
Q^{\prime}
. This equals to a number of embedded dyadic cubes in the sequence

Q\subset\pi Q\subset\dots\pi^{n}Q,

where the largest cube is
\pi^{n}Q=R
and the smallest cube is
Q=\pi Q^{\prime}
. Indeed, for each cube
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)
, it holds
\pi Q^{\prime}\cap E\neq\emptyset
, and hence
Q=\pi Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)
. Therefore,

\mathcal{N}(Q\in\mathcal{D}(E,R),Q\supset Q^{\prime})=\log\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q^{\prime})},

as required.

2.2.
Proof of Proposition
2

We need an elementary computational lemma.

Lemma 9
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a cube with side-length
\ell(Q)
and Lebesgue measure
|Q|
, and the boundary
\partial Q
. Then

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,\partial Q)}dx=\log\frac{1}{\ell(Q)}+C

with the constant
C=C(d)
.

Proof.

By scaling and translation invariance one can reduce the lemma to the case of the fixed cube
Q_{0}=[0;1)\times\dots\times[0;1)
. Then one can apply Proposition
5
with
R=Q_{0}
and
E=\partial Q_{0}
to obtain the result.

Alternatively, by elementary calculations, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,\partial Q)}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q_{0}}\log\frac{1}{\ell\>dist(x,\partial Q_{0})}\ell^{d}dx

\displaystyle=\log\frac{1}{\ell}+\int_{Q_{0}}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,\partial Q_{0})}dx

\displaystyle=\log\frac{1}{\ell}+C.

∎

Now, we prove (
5
).
Observe that
for each cube
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)
and
x\in Q^{\prime}
one has

dist(x,E)\leq 2\sqrt{d}\ell(Q^{\prime}),

hence

(9)

\log\frac{1}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}\leq\log\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{dist(x,E)}.

Therefore, since
|\overline{E}|=0
, it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q^{\prime}|\log\frac{1}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}\int_{Q^{\prime}}\log\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{dist(x,E)}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{R}\log\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{dist(x,E)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{R}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,E)}dx+C|R|

with
C=\log 2\sqrt{d}
.

Conversely, we have

\displaystyle\int_{R}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,E)}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}\int_{Q^{\prime}}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,E)}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}\int_{Q^{\prime}}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,\partial Q^{\prime})}dx,

where
\partial Q^{\prime}
is the boundary of a cube
Q^{\prime}
. Lemma
9
completes the proof of (
5
).

To prove (
6
), we argue as in the proof of (
5
). So, by (
9
), for each cube
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)
and
x\in Q^{\prime}
, one has

\displaystyle\log\frac{1}{\ell(M(R))}

\displaystyle\leq\log\frac{1}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\log\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{dist(x,E)},

which follows

\log\frac{1}{\ell(M(R))}\leq\inf_{R}\log\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{dist(x,E)}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sup_{R}dist(x,E)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{M(R)}dist(x,E)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\ell(M(R))}{2},

therefore,

\log\frac{1}{2\sqrt{d}\ell(M(R))}\leq\inf_{R}\log\frac{1}{dist(x,E)}\leq\log\frac{2}{\ell(M(R))},

which easily gets (
6
).

2.3.
Proof of Proposition
4

First, we observe the sharpness of Proposition
4
. Put
a_{Q}=1
if
Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)
, then by Proposition
2

and by Proposition
1
,
we obtain for these
a_{Q}
the inverse inequality

\displaystyle\int_{R}\>\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}\chi_{Q}\>\log\frac{\ell(R)}{dist(x,E)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{R}\>\log\frac{\ell(R)}{dist(x,E)}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q^{\prime}|\>(\log\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}+C)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}|Q|+C|R|

\displaystyle=\int_{R}\;\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}\chi_{Q}\>dx+C|R|

with
C=C(E)
.
Thus, we have

\int_{R}\>\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}\chi_{Q}\>\left(\log\frac{\ell(R)}{dist(x,E)}-C\right)dx\leq\int_{R}\;\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}\chi_{Q}\>dx.

In the forward direction Proposition
1
together with
dyadic arguments
[
7
, p. 59, Lemma 5]
imply Proposition
4
.
Indeed,
let
\chi_{(0<t<a_{Q})}
be the characteristic function, then

\displaystyle\int_{R}\>\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}a_{Q}\chi_{Q}\>dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}a_{Q}|Q|

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}|Q|\chi_{(0<t<a_{Q})}dt.

The set

\Omega_{t}=\{x:\>\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}a_{Q}\chi_{Q}>t\}

can be expressed as the union of all the cubes
Q
such that
a_{Q}>t
. Let
Q_{k}
denote the maximal dyadic cube contained in
\Omega_{t}
,
then

\Omega_{t}
is the union of all the maximal cubes
Q_{k}
, and for
t>0
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}|Q|\chi_{(0<t<a_{Q})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q\in\Omega_{t}}|Q|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\sum_{Q\in Q_{k}}|Q|.

To this end, we followed
[
7
, p. 59, Lemma 5]
. Now, using Proposition
1
, the property
|Q_{k}|\leq|R|
, and Proposition
2
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in Q_{k}}|Q|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(Q_{k},E)}|Q^{\prime}|\log\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(Q_{k},E)}|Q^{\prime}|\log\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q_{k}}\>\left(\log\frac{\ell(R)}{dist(x,E)}+C\right)dx.

Therefore, we obtain the estimate on the double sum

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sum_{Q\in Q_{k}}|Q|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\int_{Q_{k}}\>\left(\log\frac{\ell(R)}{dist(x,E)}+C\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\>\left(\log\frac{\ell(R)}{dist(x,E)}+C\right)dx.

We finish by observing

\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{\Omega_{t}}\>\left(\log\frac{\ell(R)}{dist(x,E)}+C\right)dx=\int_{R}\>\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}(R,E)}a_{Q}\chi_{Q}\>\left(\log\frac{\ell(R)}{dist(x,E)}+C\right)dx.

2.4.
Proof of Theorem
6

By Proposition
2
and Proposition
5
, the weak porosity of
E
is equivalent to (i).

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) easily follows from Proposition
1
.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) Arguing as in Proposition
1
, replace the family
\mathcal{D}(R,E)
by the family

\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E)
. It holds

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E)}|Q|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E)}\quad\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q\cap Q^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}\quad\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E)}|Q\cap Q^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}\quad\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E),\;Q\supset Q^{\prime}}|Q^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q^{\prime}|\,\mathcal{N}(Q\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E),\;Q\supset Q^{\prime}),

where
\mathcal{N}(Q\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E),\;Q\supset Q^{\prime})
is the number of cubes from
\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E)
in the sequence

Q\subset\pi Q\subset\dots\pi^{n}Q
. Here
\ell(\pi^{n}Q)=\ell(M(R))
and
Q=\pi Q^{\prime}
, that is,

\mathcal{N}(Q\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E),\;Q\supset Q^{\prime})=\log\frac{\ell(M(R))}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}.

Therefore,

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(R,E)}|Q|=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(R,E)}|Q^{\prime}|\log\frac{\ell(M(R))}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}.

Since both sums above are bounded by
C|R|
at the same time, one gets (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii), which
completes the proof of Theorem
6
.

On W 2 , p W^{2,p} -estimates for solutions of obstacle problems for fully nonlinear elliptic equations with oblique boundary conditions
Sun-Sig Byun, Jeongmin Han, Jehan Oh

Abstract
. This paper concerns fully nonlinear elliptic obstacle problems with oblique boundary conditions. We investigate the existence, uniqueness and W 2 , p W^{2,p} -regularity results by finding approximate non-obstacle problems with the same oblique boundary condition and then making a suitable limiting process.

1.
Introduction and main results

This paper concerns the existence, uniqueness and regularity for viscosity solutions to the following obstacle problem with oblique boundary data

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F(D^{2}u,Du,u,x)\leq f&\textrm{in $\Omega,$}\\
(F(D^{2}u,Du,u,x)-f)(u-\psi)=0&\textrm{in $\Omega$, }\\
u\geq\psi&\textrm{in $\Omega$,}\\
\beta\cdot Du=0&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{array}\right.

for a given obstacle
\psi\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
satisfying

\beta\cdot D\psi\geq 0
a.e. on
\partial\Omega
.
Here
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with its boundary
\partial\Omega\in C^{3}
,

F
is uniformly elliptic with constants
\lambda
and
\Lambda
, i.e.,

\lambda||N||\leq F(M+N,q,r,x)-F(M,q,r,x)\leq\Lambda||N||

for any
n\times n
symmetric matrices
M,N
with
N\geq 0
,
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\Omega
, and

\beta
is a vector-valued function with
||\beta||_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}=1
and
\beta\cdot\mathbf{n}\geq\delta_{0}
for some positive constant
\delta_{0}
,
where
\mathbf{n}
is the inner unit normal vector field of
\partial\Omega
.

The main purpose of this paper is to find an optimal
W^{2,p}
-regularity theory for (
1
).
More precisely, we want to identify the minimal condition of
F
with respect to
x
-variable
under which the Hessian of a solution is as integrable as both the nonhomogeneous term
f
and the Hessian of the obstacle
\psi
in the setting of
L^{p}
spaces for
n<p<\infty
.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
F=F(X,q,r,x)
is convex in
X

and satisfies

\displaystyle d(r_{2}-r_{1})\leq F(X,q,r_{1},x)-F(X,q,r_{2},x)

(1.5)

for any
X\in S(n)
,
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with
r_{1}\leq r_{2}
,
x\in\Omega
, and
some
d>0
.
We further assume that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{M}^{-}(\lambda,\Lambda,X_{1}-X_{2})-b|q_{1}-q_{2}|-c|r_{1}-r_{2}|\\
&\qquad\leq F(X_{1},q_{1},r_{1},x)-F(X_{2},q_{2},r_{2},x)\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}(\lambda,\Lambda,X_{1}-X_{2})+b|q_{1}-q_{2}|+c|r_{1}-r_{2}|\end{split}

(1.6)

for
X_{1},X_{2}\in S(n)
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\Omega
.
These assumptions are essential in order to derive our desired results for solutions of (
1
),
such as the existence, uniqueness and
W^{2,p}
-regularity.

With an oscillation function of
F
defined as

\displaystyle\Theta_{F}(x_{1},x_{2}):=\sup_{X\in S(n)\backslash\{0\}}\frac{|F(X,0,0,x_{1})-F(X,0,0,x_{2})|}{||X||}

alongside a small perturbation of
\Theta_{F}
from its integral average in the
L^{n}
-sense,
we shall prove the
W^{2,p}
-regularity for (
1
), as we now state the main result of the paper.
We remark that this approach to derive
W^{2,p}
-regularity was employed in
[
Caf89
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n<p<\infty
.
Assume that
F=F(X,q,r,x)
is convex in
X
, satisfies (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) and
F(0,0,0,x)\equiv 0
,

\partial\Omega\in C^{3},f\in L^{p}(\Omega),\beta\in C^{2}(\partial\Omega)
with
\beta\cdot\mathbf{n}\geq\delta_{0}
for some
\delta_{0}>0

and
\psi\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
.
Then there exists a small
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,\lambda,\Lambda,p,\delta_{0},||\beta||_{C^{2}(\partial\Omega)})>0

such that if

\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega},0<\rho<\rho_{0}}\bigg(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}\Theta_{F}(x,x_{0})^{n}\ dx\bigg)^{1/n}\leq\epsilon

(1.7)

for some
\rho_{0}>0
,
then there is a unique viscosity solution
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
of (
1
) with the following estimate

||u||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq c(||f||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+||\psi||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)})

for some constant
c=c(n,\lambda,\Lambda,p,\delta_{0},b,c,||\beta||_{C^{2}(\partial\Omega)},\partial\Omega,\operatorname{diam}(\Omega),\rho_{0})
.

One of the important issues regarding the obstacle problem is to study solutions near the boundary of the contact surface with the obstacle.
To this end, suitable approximation methods have been used.
In this regard, we revisit the argument made in
[
BLOP18
]
where the Dirichlet boundary problem was studied instead.
Our main difficulty in using such an argument comes from the situation that we are treating here the oblique boundary condition.
Thus, we need to modify the tools used in
[
BLOP18
]
properly to derive the desired boundary estimates in the present paper.
To do this, we verify several uniform properties of a solution for the corresponding non-obstacle problem such as
W^{2,p}
-regularity and comparison principle.

As a generalization of Neumann boundary problems,
researches on oblique derivative problems have been extensively made as
in
[
Lie84
,
LT86
,
Lie87
,
Saf95
,
DFP96
,
PRS00
]
.
In particular, several notable results for fully nonlinear elliptic equations were obtained in the notion of viscosity solutions.
The existence and uniqueness of fully nonlinear
oblique derivative problems were proved in
[
DI90
,
DI91
,
Ish91
]
.
For the regularity of the associated limiting problem,
there have been established
C^{2,\alpha}
-estimates, see
[
MS06
]
for the Neumann boundary
condition and
[
LZ18
]
for the oblique boundary condition, respectively.
In
[
BH20
]
a global
W^{2,p}
-regularity for the elliptic oblique derivative problem was proved.

On the other hand, the obstacle problem has been studied along with the free boundary problem.
We refer the reader to
[
Fri82
,
PSU12
]
for a general theory of the obstacle problem.
Regularity results for the elliptic obstacle problem can be found in
[
FS14
,
ALS15
,
IM16
,
BLOP19
]
.
We would like to point out that
C^{1}
-regularity for the obstacle problem
with the oblique boundary condition was shown in
[
Lie01
]
,
while
W^{2,p}
-regularity for the Dirichlet obstacle problem was given in
[
BLOP18
]
.
The main purpose of this paper is to derive a
W^{2,p}
-estimate for the oblique derivative problem
with a
W^{2,p}
-obstacle.

The remaining part of the paper is organized in the following way.
In the next section we introduce basic notation and give a brief exposition of viscosity solutions.
Section 3 deals with the associated oblique derivative problem without obstacles.
In particular we discuss the existence, uniqueness and
W^{2,p}
-regularity for the non-obstacle problem.
In the last section we finally give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.2.
Basic concepts

In this subsection, we briefly present some background knowledge for our discussion.
As usual, we are treating a viscosity solution.
To do this, we consider the following problem with oblique boundary data

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F(D^{2}u,Du,u,x)=f&\textrm{in $\Omega,$}\\
\beta\cdot Du=0&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$,}\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain.
There are several ways to define a viscosity solution depending on the choice of a test function.
In this paper, we take a test function
\varphi
in
W^{2,p}(\Omega)
.
The solution defined in this way is called an
L^{p}
-viscosity solution.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
F
be continuous in
X
and measurable in
x
.
Suppose
q>n
and
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)
.
A continuous function
u
is called an
L^{q}
-viscosity solution for (
2.2
) if the following conditions hold:

(a)

(subsolution) For each
\varphi\in W^{2,q}(\Omega)
, whenever
\epsilon>0
,
\mathcal{O}
is relatively open in
\overline{\Omega}
and

F(D^{2}\varphi(x),D\varphi(x),\varphi(x),x)\leq f(x)-\epsilon\quad\textrm{a.e. in}\ \mathcal{O}

and

\beta\cdot D\varphi(x)\leq-\epsilon\quad\textrm{a.e. on}\ \mathcal{O}\cap\partial\Omega,

u-\varphi
cannot attain a local maximum in
\mathcal{O}
.

(b)

(supersolution) For each
\varphi\in W^{2,q}(\Omega)
, whenever
\epsilon>0
,
\mathcal{O}
is relatively open in
\overline{\Omega}
and

F(D^{2}\varphi(x),D\varphi(x),\varphi(x),x)\geq f(x)+\epsilon\quad\textrm{a.e. in}\ \mathcal{O}

and

\beta\cdot D\varphi(x)\geq\epsilon\quad\textrm{a.e. on}\ \mathcal{O}\cap\partial\Omega,

u-\varphi
cannot attain a local minimum in
\mathcal{O}
.

We remark that it is also possible to take a
C^{2}
-function as a test function if
F
is continuous in each variable.
In this case, the solution is called a
C
-viscosity solution.
For a further discussion of a
C
-viscosity solution, we refer the reader to
[
CC95
]
.

Next we give some tools to treat viscosity solutions.
To do this, we introduce Pucci extremal operators.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
. For any
M\in S(n)
, the Pucci extremal operator
\mathcal{M}^{+}
and
\mathcal{M}^{-}
are defined as follows:

\mathcal{M}^{+}(\lambda,\Lambda,X)=\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}+\lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}

and

\mathcal{M}^{-}(\lambda,\Lambda,X)=\lambda\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}+\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i},

where
e_{i}
are eigenvalues of
X
.
Moreover, for
b>0
, we write

L_{b}^{\pm}(\lambda,\Lambda,u)=\mathcal{M}^{\pm}(\lambda,\Lambda,D^{2}u)\pm b|Du|,

respectively.

This definition allows us to introduce the class
S
.
These classes can be considered as classes of viscosity solutions.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
.
We define the class
\underline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,b,f)

(
\overline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,b,f)
, respectively
)
consisting of all functions
u
such that

L_{b}^{+}(\lambda,\Lambda,u)\geq f\qquad\big(L_{b}^{-}(\lambda,\Lambda,u)\leq f,\textrm{ respectively}\big)

in the viscosity sense in
\Omega
.
We also define

S(\lambda,\Lambda,b,f)=\overline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,b,f)\cap\underline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,b,f)

and

S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,b,f)=\overline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,b,|f|)\cap\underline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,b,-|f|).

Remark 2.4
.

Let
u
be a viscosity subsolution (supersolution, respectively) of

F(D^{2}u,Du,u,x)=f\qquad\textrm{in}\ \Omega,

where
F=F(X,q,r,x)
is uniformly elliptic with constants
\lambda,\Lambda
satisfying the structure condition (
1.6
).
Then we can observe that
u
satisfies

L_{b}^{+}(\lambda,\Lambda,u)+F(0,0,u,x)\geq f\qquad\textrm{in}\ \Omega

\big(L_{b}^{-}(\lambda,\Lambda,u)+F(0,0,u,x)\leq f\qquad\textrm{in}\ \Omega\textrm{, respectively}\big)

in the viscosity sense.

3.
Oblique derivative problems

Before establishing
W^{2,p}
-regularity (

) for the obstacle problem (
1

we first discuss some issues concerning the existence, uniqueness and regularity for the oblique derivative problem (
2.2

We recall Definition
2.3
to start with an Alexandroff-Bakelman-Pucci (ABP) maximum principle for the oblique boundary problem.
See
[
LZ18

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u
satisfy

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u\in\overline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,b,f)&\textrm{in $\Omega,$}\\
\beta\cdot Du\leq g&\textrm{on $\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega$ }\\
\end{array}\right.

in the viscosity sense for

and
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
.
Suppose that there exist
\xi\in\partial B_{1}
and
\delta_{1}>0
such that
\beta(x)\cdot\xi\geq\delta_{1}
for any
x\in\Gamma
.
Then

\sup_{\Omega}u^{-}\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega\backslash\Gamma}u^{-}+C(||f^{+}||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}+\max_{\Gamma}g^{+}),

where
C
only depends on
n,\lambda,\Lambda,b,\delta_{1}
and
\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)
.

We also give a weak Harnack’s inequality for supersolutions.
The proof can be found in
[
Win09
, Proposition 1.8]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

and

.
Suppose that

Then there exist

||u||_{L^{p_{0}}(B_{1/2})}\leq C(\inf_{B_{1/2}}u+||f||_{L^{p}(B_{1})}).

The following stability lemma, which can be found in
[

, Proposition 1.5]
,
will be used later

[

Proposition 3.3
.

For

, let
\Omega_{k}\subset\Omega_{k+1}
be an increasing sequence of bounded domains and

Let

Assume that for

-viscosity subsolutions (supersolutions, respectively) of

in

Suppose that

locally uniformly in
\Omega
, and for

\varphi\in W^{2,p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))

\displaystyle||(s-s_{k})^{+}||_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\to 0\qquad\big(||(s-s_{k})^{-}||_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\to 0\big),

where

Now we return to (

\displaystyle F(X_{1},q,r,x_{1})-F(X_{2},q,r,x_{2})\leq\omega(|x_{1}-x_{2}|(|q|+1)+\alpha|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2})

Lemma 3.5
.

Under the assumptions and conclusion in Lemma

\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega},0<\rho<\rho_{0}}\bigg(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}\Theta_{F}(x,x_{0})^{n}\ dx\bigg)^{1/n}\leq\epsilon

Proof.

According to
[

, Theorem 4.6]
,

belongs to

\displaystyle||u||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C(||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+||f||_{L^{p}(\Omega)})

Therefore, it suffices to obtain the estimate (

To prove this, we argue by contradiction.
Suppose not.
Then there exist sequences
\{u_{k}\}
and
\{f_{k}\}

is the viscosity solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F(D^{2}u_{k},Du_{k},u_{k},x)=f_{k}&\textrm{in $\Omega,$}\\
\beta\cdot Du_{k}=0&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$}\\
\end{array}\right.

with

\displaystyle||u_{k}||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}>k||f_{k}||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\textrm{for each }k\geq 1.

(3.14)

Consider
\tilde{u}_{k}=t_{k}^{-1}u_{k}
,
\tilde{f}_{k}=t_{k}^{-1}f_{k}

\tilde{F}_{k}(X,q,r,x)=t_{k}^{-1}F(t_{k}X,t_{k}q,t_{k}r,x),

where

.
Then

is a viscosity solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{F}_{k}(D^{2}\tilde{u}_{k},D\tilde{u}_{k},\tilde{u}_{k},x)=\tilde{f}_{k}&\textrm{in $\Omega,$}\\
\beta\cdot D\tilde{u}_{k}=0&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

We also see that

satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
3.4
and
||\tilde{u}_{k}||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}=1

By (

||\tilde{f}_{k}||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}<1/k
and this tends to zero as

Moreover, by weak compactness theorem, we can extract a proper subsequence
\{\tilde{u}_{k_{j}}\}\subset\{\tilde{u}_{k}\}

such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{u}_{k_{j}}\rightharpoonup\tilde{v}&\textrm{in $W^{2,p}(\Omega),$}\\
\tilde{u}_{k_{j}}\to\tilde{v}&\textrm{in $W^{1,p}(\Omega)$}\\
\end{array}\right.

for some

Since

and this yields

in

Lemma 3.7

First we set

G(X,q,r,x)=F(X+D^{2}u_{2},q+Du_{2},r+u_{2},x)-F(D^{2}u_{2},Du_{2},u_{2},x).

)-(

),

\displaystyle G(D^{2}w,Dw,w,x)=F(D^{2}u_{1},Du_{1},u_{1},x)-F(D^{2}u_{2},Du_{2},u_{2},x)\leq 0

in the viscosity sense,
and that

w\geq 0&\textrm{on $\partial V\backslash\Gamma$,}\\

on
(\partial V\cap\partial\Omega_{0})\backslash\Gamma
.
From ABP maximum principle (see

, Proposition 3.3]

on

.

Now define

for

.
Then we see that

in

According to

.

, there exists a small number

That is,

On the other hand, if

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w\in\overline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,b,0)&\textrm{in $V,$}\\

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section we establish our main result, Theorem
1.1
.
Our strategy is to construct a sequence of approximating oblique derivative problems to (
1
).
This construction makes it possible to utilize those results obtained in the previous section for (
2.2
).

In the process of the proof, we are going to use the following Schauder’s fixed point theorem (see
[
Con85
, Theorem V.9.5]
).

Lemma 4.1

(Schauder’s fixed point theorem)
.

Assume that
X
is a Banach space,
K\subset X
is closed, bounded and convex, and suppose that
S:K\to K
is compact.
Then
S
has a fixed point in
K
.

We now prove the main result of this paper.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and choose a non-decreasing function
\Phi_{\epsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\epsilon}(s)\equiv 0\quad\textrm{if}\quad s\leq 0;\qquad\Phi_{\epsilon}(s)\equiv 1\quad\textrm{if}\quad s\geq\epsilon,

(4.1)

and

\displaystyle 0\leq\Phi_{\epsilon}(s)\leq 1\quad\textrm{for any}\ \,s\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.2)

Set

g(x):=f(x)-F(D^{2}\psi,D\psi,\psi,x).

Then we have
g\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}||g||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}&\leq||f||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+||F(D^{2}\psi,D\psi,\psi,x)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq C(||f||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+||\psi||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)})\end{split}

(4.3)

for some
C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,b,c)>0
, since
f,D^{2}\psi\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
F
satisfies
(
1.6
).

We now consider the following oblique derivative problem without obstacles

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F(D^{2}u_{\epsilon},Du_{\epsilon},u_{\epsilon},x)=g^{+}\Phi_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}-\psi)+f-g^{+}&\textrm{in $\Omega,$}\\
\beta\cdot Du_{\epsilon}=0&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

We want to show that (
4
) has a unique viscosity solution.
For this, fix a function
v_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
.
Then according to Lemma
3.6
,
we know that there exists a unique viscosity solution
v_{\epsilon}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F(D^{2}v_{\epsilon},Dv_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon},x)=g^{+}\Phi_{\epsilon}(v_{0}-\psi)+f-g^{+}&\textrm{in $\Omega,$}\\
\beta\cdot Dv_{\epsilon}=0&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{array}\right.

with the estimate

\displaystyle||v_{\epsilon}||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C||g^{+}\Phi_{\epsilon}(v_{0}-\psi)+f-g^{+}||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(||f||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+||g||_{L^{p}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq C(||f||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+||\psi||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)})

for some
C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,p,\delta_{0},b,c,||\beta||_{C^{2}(\partial\Omega)},\rho_{0},\operatorname{diam}(\Omega))>0
, where we have used (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).
Thus,

||v_{\epsilon}||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0}

for some

C_{0}=C_{0}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,p,\delta_{0},b,c,||\beta||_{C^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\operatorname{diam}(\Omega),||f||_{L^{p}(\Omega)},||\psi||_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\rho_{0}).

Note that
C_{0}
is independent of
v_{0}
.
Now we can define a nonlinear operator
S_{\epsilon}:L^{p}(\Omega)\to W^{2,p}(\Omega)\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
such that
S_{\epsilon}v_{0}=v_{\epsilon}
with (
4
).
Write

K:=\{h\in L^{p}(\Omega):||h||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0}\}.

Note that
K
is a closed convex subset of
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
On the other hand, by Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem, we observe that
W^{2,p}(\Omega)
is compactly imbedded in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and so is in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
Hence, the closure of
S_{\epsilon}(A)
is compact for every
A\subset K
.
Meanwhile, by Proposition
3.3
, we can also conclude that
S_{\epsilon}
is a continuous operator.

Therefore, by Lemma
4.1
, there exists a function
u_{\epsilon}\in K
satisfying
S_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}=u_{\epsilon}
,
and this implies that
u_{\epsilon}
is a viscosity solution of (
4
).
Furthermore, from (
4.3
) and Lemma
3.6
, we also observe that

\displaystyle||u_{\epsilon}||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C(||f||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+||\psi||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)})

(4.6)

for some
C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,p,\delta_{0},b,c,||\beta||_{C^{2}(\partial\Omega)},\operatorname{diam}(\Omega),\rho_{0})
.
This shows that
\{u_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{2,p}(\Omega)
.

Recall that
p>n
. Then we observe that
W^{2,p}(\Omega)\subset\subset C^{1,\alpha_{0}}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
0<\alpha_{0}<1-n/p
by Morrey imbedding.
Therefore, we can find a subsequence
\{u_{\epsilon_{j}}\}
with
\epsilon_{j}\searrow 0
and a function

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{\epsilon_{j}}\rightharpoonup u\quad\textrm{in}\ W^{2,p}(\Omega),\\
u_{\epsilon_{j}}\to u\quad\textrm{in}\ W^{1,p}(\Omega)\subset C^{0,\alpha_{0}}(\overline{\Omega})\\
\end{array}\right.

as

.

Now we claim that

is indeed the unique viscosity solution of (
1
).
We first see that

is uniformly bounded and equicontiuous on
\partial\Omega
from (

) and Morrey imbedding.
Thus, by using Arzelá-Ascoli criterion, we have

\beta\cdot Du=0\quad\textrm{on}\ \partial\Omega

in the viscosity sense.
On the other hand, from (

F(D^{2}u_{\epsilon_{j}},Du_{\epsilon_{j}},u_{\epsilon_{j}},x)=g^{+}\Phi_{\epsilon_{j}}(u_{\epsilon_{j}}-\psi)+f-g^{+}\leq f\qquad\textrm{in}\ \Omega

for each

Passing to the limit

\Omega

Next, we show that

We first see that
\Phi_{\epsilon_{j}}(u_{\epsilon_{j}}-\psi)\equiv 0

If

Now suppose that

Therefore, we can conclude that

Moreover, from (

\displaystyle||u||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}||u_{\epsilon_{j}}||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C(||f||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+||\psi||_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)})

for some constant

For the uniqueness, let

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F(D^{2}u_{1},Du_{1},u_{1},x)\leq F(D^{2}u_{2},Du_{2},u_{2},x)=f&\textrm{in $G,$}\\

.
∎

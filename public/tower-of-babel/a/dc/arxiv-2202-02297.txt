Uniform tail estimates and L p ​ ( ℝ N ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) -convergence for finite-difference approximations of nonlinear diffusion equations
Félix del Teso, Jørgen Endal, Espen R. Jakobsen

Abstract
. We obtain new equitightness and C ⁡ ( [ 0 , T ] , L p ​ ( ℝ N ) ) C([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})) -convergence results for finite-difference approximations of generalized porous medium equations of the form ∂ t u − 𝔏 ⁡ [ φ ⁡ ( u ) ] = g in ℝ N × ( 0 , T ) , \partial_{t}u-\mathfrak{L}[\varphi(u)]=g\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)$}, where φ : ℝ → ℝ \varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R} is continuous and nondecreasing, and 𝔏 \mathfrak{L} is a local or nonlocal diffusion operator. Our results include slow diffusions, strongly degenerate Stefan problems, and fast diffusions above a critical exponent. These results improve the previous C ⁡ ( [ 0 , T ] , L loc p ​ ( ℝ N ) ) C([0,T];L_{\textup{loc}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})) -convergence obtained in a series of papers on the topic by the authors. To have equitightness and global L p ​ ( ℝ N ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) -convergence, some additional restrictions on 𝔏 \mathfrak{L} and φ \varphi are needed. Most commonly used symmetric operators 𝔏 \mathfrak{L} are still included: the Laplacian, fractional Laplacians, and other generators of symmetric Lévy processes with some fractional moment. We also discuss extensions to nonlinear possibly strongly degenerate convection-diffusion equations.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study finite-difference approximations of
degenerate nonlinear diffusion and convection-diffusion equations. We
work in a standard low regularity setting with bounded and integrable data and
(generalized) solutions in
C([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
. Even though the
natural mode of convergence here is in the
space where solutions live, existing results for such schemes give
convergence in
C([0,T];L_{\textup{loc}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
or

L_{\textup{loc}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
.
The purpose of this paper is to show in a systematic and general way
that with some additional effort
we will have the stronger convergence in

C([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
under mild or sometimes even no additional assumptions.

To be more precise we study generalized porous medium equations (and
extensions to convection-diffusion equations) in the context of bounded and integrable solutions. In detail, we study

(GPME)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\mathfrak{L}[\varphi(u)]=g\qquad\qquad&\text{in $Q_{T}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)$},\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}\qquad\qquad&\text{on $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},\end{cases}

where
u=u(x,t)
is the solution,
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is nondecreasing and
\gamma
-Hölder continuous with
\gamma\in(0,1]
,
g=g(x,t)
some bounded and integrable right-hand side,
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
T>0
. The general operator
\mathfrak{L}
is given as

(1.1)

\mathfrak{L}=\mathfrak{L}^{\sigma,\mu}:=L^{\sigma}+\mathcal{L}^{\mu}

where
L^{\sigma}:C_{\textup{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a possibly degenerate local diffusion operator

(1.2)

L^{\sigma}[\psi](x):=\textup{tr}\big(\sigma\sigma^{T}D^{2}\psi(x)\big)

where
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{P})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times P}
,
P\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\sigma_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and the anomalous or nonlocal diffusion operator

\mathcal{L}^{\mu}
is defined for any
\psi\in C_{\textup{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as

(1.3)

\mathcal{L}^{\mu}[\psi](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}}\big(\psi(x+z)-\psi(x)-z\cdot D\psi(x)\mathbf{1}_{|z|\leq 1}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z),

where
D
is the gradient,
\mathbf{1}_{|z|\leq 1}
a characteristic function, and

\mu
a nonnegative and symmetric Radon measure satisfying at least
the usual Lévy measure condition (see Section

3
). In this paper, to simplify, we will always choose either

\sigma\equiv 0
or
\sigma\sigma^{T}=I_{N}
. That is,
the local operator is given as
L^{\sigma}=c\Delta:=c\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{x_{i}x_{i}}^{2}
where

c\in\{0,1\}
.

Remark 1.1
.

(a)

Since
\mu
will be symmetric,
\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{r<|z|\leq 1}z\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)=0
,
and we have an equivalent definition of
\mathcal{L}^{\mu}
in (
1.3
)
in terms of a principal value integral:

\mathcal{L}^{\mu}[\psi](x)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{|z|>r}\big(\psi(x+z)-\psi(x)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)=\textup{P.V.}\int_{|z|>0}\big(\psi(x+z)-\psi(x)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z).

(b)

(
GPME
) is a pure diffusion equation. Our results also
holds for convection-diffusion equations and viscous conservation
laws, see Section
5.2
.

Equations of the form (
GPME
) (and also variants with convection) appear in numerous applications. We selectively mention reservoir simulation, sedimentation processes, and traffic flow in the local case
[
25
,
8
,
41
]
; cardiac electrophysiology and semiconductor growth in the nonlocal case
[
7
,
42
]
; and flows of fluids through porous media, mathematical finance, information theory, and phase transitions in both cases
[
40
,
12
,
11
,
38
,
6
]
. Important examples are strongly degenerate Stefan problems (cf.
[
17
,
18
]
) with
\varphi(u)=\max\{0,u-L\}
,
L>0
, and the full range of porous medium and fast diffusion equations (cf.
[
12
]
) with
\varphi(u)=u|u|^{m-1}
,
m>0
. The class of operators
\mathfrak{L}^{\sigma,\mu}
coincides with the generators of symmetric Lévy processes
[
4
,
37
,
1
]
. This includes e.g. the Laplacian
\Delta
, fractional Laplacians
-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
,
\alpha\in(0,2)
, relativistic Schrödinger type operators
m^{\alpha}I-(m^{2}I-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
,
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
m>0
, tempered stable processes
[
11
]
, and even discretizations of all of these. Since
\sigma
and
\mu
may be degenerate or even identically zero, equation (
GPME
) can be purely nonlocal, purely local, or a combination.

Our convergence results follow from compactness, stability, and
(very weak) uniqueness arguments. The compactness results depend on the type of equation under study, and
the properties available for such an equation. They are essential in
the context of existence, continuous dependence, and asymptotic
behaviour. For the latter example, this is particularly the case when
considering the rescaled solution in the “four-step method”
introduced by Kamin and Vázquez in
[
33
]
. In all of these
cases, an approximate solution
u_{n}
of the equation under study is
considered. Then one shows compactness of the family formed by
u_{n}

in order to be able to find a limit function. The limit must of course
be a solution of the original equation (or variants of it). In this
paper, the approximate solution will always come from a
finite-difference scheme for (
GPME
).

To prove compactness in
C([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
with
p\in[1,\infty)
, we
employ the well-known Arzelà-Ascoli and Kolmogorov-Riesz
compactness theorems (cf. Appendix
A
). A systematic
approach to these theorems are presented in Section

2
. Compared to some previous results in
this direction (see e.g. Lemma 2.2 in
[
34
]
and Theorem A.8
in
[
31
]
), we also deduce equitightness (uniform
tail control) for approximate solutions of (
GPME
). This allows us to (i) go from
L^{p}_{\textup{loc}}

to
L^{p}
compactness and (ii) to get a minimal and efficient
compactness argument in the sense that we avoid a lot of previously used tricks to fulfil the requirements of Arzelà-Ascoli and Kolmogorov-Riesz. In our generality uniform boundedness (
L^{\infty}
-stability) of
the solutions are still required to ensure
some estimates hold. In special cases however, this assumption probably
can be dropped.

As far as we know, equitightness results for (
GPME
) have not been presented under our general assumptions before (especially in the nonlocal case), and these results are really the core of this paper. Such estimates are deduced by taking, roughly speaking,
x\mapsto\mathbf{1}_{|x|>R}
as a test function in the very weak formulation of (
GPME
). This gives uniform tail control of the approximative solution (cf. e.g.
[
12
, Proof of Proposition 10.2]
). In examples when (
GPME
) conserves mass, we can summarize our equitightness results by saying that such estimates always holds in the local case, and also in the nonlocal setting when we assume that the nonlocal operator is comparable to a fractional order operator at infinity.

Finite-difference methods were developed in the full generality of (
GPME
) in
[
15
,
16
]
, where also relevant numerical simulations can be found. Some early works are
[
20
,
23
]
. We also refer to
[
21
]
(see also Part II of
[
22
]
) in the purely local case. In the case of (
GPME
) with an additional convection term, we mention e.g.
[
34
,
31
,
10
]
.

The numerical schemes which will be presented below include most of
the mentioned works on local and nonlocal cases. However, none of the
above showed convergence in
C([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
(but some could still
deduce that the limit itself belonged to that space, see also Section

5.1
). Our equitightness estimates
therefore improves convergence results already present in the
literature. Conservative and monotone finite-difference schemes for
scalar conservation laws are discussed e.g. in
[
31
, Theorem
3.8]
, and they immediately fall into our

C([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
-framework. By
adding a possibly nonlinear local diffusion term to such equations, we
obtain convection-diffusion equations with local diffusion. Such
equations have been studied in the context of finite-difference
approximations in
[
34
, Theorem 4.2]
. See also Theorem 3.9 and
Corollary 3.10 in
[
27
]
. Again, we can improve the convergence
from the respective
L_{\textup{loc}}^{1}(Q_{T})
and

C([0,T];L_{\textup{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
to
C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
since

L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-equitightness holds. In the nonlocal
diffusion setting, finite-difference schemes has just recently been
analyzed in rigorous detail and generality in
[
15
,
16
]
, see also
[
20
,
23
]
. The former two references
obtain compactness/convergence results in

C([0,T];L_{\textup{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, and the latter in

L_{\textup{loc}}^{1}(Q_{T})
. Our framework thus improves the
compactness/convergence results of all four papers.

Finally, we mention that our techniques do not give convergence rates of the scheme since we obtain
convergence via compactness plus uniqueness arguments. It is an
interesting and hard problem to show convergence rates in the general class
of equations studied here.
No general results are known up to now, both for (
GPME
) and the
convection-diffusion extension (
CDE
) discussed in Section

5.2
. The only known results we are aware of are
some hyberbolic type of convergence estimates obtained for
entropy solutions
via doubling of variables and regularisation
arguments, see
[
10
]
for nonlocal and
[
35
,
36
]

for local equations.

2.
On compactness and convergence in
C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))

Let us give an overview of the properties needed to deduce compactness and convergence in
C_{t}(L_{x}^{r})
with
r\in[1,\infty)
.

2.1.
Necessary and sufficient conditions for compactness

Consider a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{t}(L_{x}^{r})
, and assume that
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies:

(I)

(Equitight in space pointwise in time)
For all
t\in[0,T]
,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{n}\int_{|x|>R}|u_{n}(x,t)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(II)

(Equicontinuous in space pointwise in time)
For all
t\in[0,T]
and all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exists a modulus of continuity
\lambda
such that

\sup_{n}\|u_{n}(\cdot+\xi,t)-u_{n}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\lambda(|\xi|).

(III)

(Equicontinuous in time)
For all
t,s\in[0,T]
, there exists a modulus of continuity
\lambda
such that

\sup_{n}\|u_{n}(\cdot,t)-u_{n}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\lambda(|t-s|).

Properties (
I
)–(
III
) are exactly the necessary and sufficient conditions needed to obtain compactness in
C_{t}(L_{x}^{r})
(cf. the Arzelà-Ascoli and Kolmogorov-Riesz compactness theorems
A.1
and
A.3
).

Theorem 2.1

(Compactness)
.

Assume
r\in[1,\infty)
, and let

\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})).

The following are equivalent:

(a)

The sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies properties (
I
)–(
III
).

(b)

There exists a subsequence
\{u_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a
u\in C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
such that

u_{n_{k}}\to u\qquad\text{in $C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))$ as $k\to\infty$}.

Remark 2.2
.

The classical Kolmogorov-Riesz compactness theorem requires the family of functions to be equibounded, that is, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\sup_{n}\|u_{n}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\infty.

However, in
[
30
]
, it has been pointed out that such a property follows from properties (
I
)–(
II
).

Proof.

The fact that the sequence
x\mapsto\{u_{n}(x,t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies (
I
)–(
II
) is, by the Kolmogorov-Riesz compactness theorem (cf. Theorem
A.3
), equivalent with
\{x\mapsto\{u_{n}(x,t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\}
being relatively compact in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Finally, since the sequence
t\mapsto\{u_{n}(x,t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies (
III
), the proof is completed by an application of the Arzelà-Ascoli compactness theorem (cf. Theorem
A.1
).
∎

2.2.
Consequences

In our context,
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
will be a sequence of,
e.g., numerical approximations of some function
u
which could, e.g.,
be a distributional/very weak, entropy, energy, strong, mild, or
classical solution of (
GPME
). The next properties therefore
relate the above compactness with results for the underlying
equation. When we say
solve
and
solution
in this section, we mean in one of the
senses mentioned above. The solution concepts in
(
IV
) and (
V
) need to be the same.

(IV)

(Consistent
approximation)
Assume
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a consistent
approximation of
problem (P), i.e.,

u_{n}\to u\quad\text{in $C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))$ as $n\to\infty$}\qquad\implies\qquad\text{$u$ solves (P).}

Corollary 2.3

(Existence by consistent approximation)
.

Assume
r\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
be a sequence satisfying properties (
I
)–(
IV
). Then there exist a solution
u\in C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
of
(P)
.

Proof.

By (
I
)–(
III
), any subsequence
\{u_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
defined in Theorem
2.1
has a further subsequence converging to some function
u\in C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
. The consistency given by (
IV
) tells us that the limit
u
is a solution of (P).
∎

We end this discussion, by noting that full convergence of the sequence relies on uniqueness of the problem (P):

(V)

(Uniqueness)
There is at most one solution
u\in C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
of (P).

Proposition 2.4

(Convergence by uniqueness)
.

Assume
r\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
be a sequence satisfying properties (
I
)–(
V
). Then

u_{n}\to u\quad\text{in \hskip 10.22217pt$C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))$\hskip 10.22217ptas \hskip 10.22217pt$n\to\infty$},

where
u
is the unique solution of
(P)
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not converge to
u
in

C_{t}(L_{x}^{r})
. Then there is a subsequence and an
\varepsilon>0
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u-u_{n_{k}}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}>\varepsilon
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By compactness (Theorem

2.1
) there is a further subsequence

\{u_{n_{k_{j}}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converging in
C_{t}(L_{x}^{r})
to a function

\tilde{u}
which is a solution of (P) (Corollary

2.3
). However, uniqueness tells us that
u=\tilde{u}
, and we have a
contradiction. The whole sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
thus
converges to
u
in
C_{t}(L_{x}^{r})
.
∎

2.3.
Some variants

We will now discuss some variants of the above conditions which we will use in the paper, and also a comparison with other compactness results.

Equitight in space uniformly in time.

Now, we replace (
I
) and (
III
) by:

(i)

(Equitight in space uniformly in time)
For all
t\in[0,T]
,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{n}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{|x|>R}|u_{n}(x,t)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(iii)

(Equicontinuous in time)
For all
t,s\in[0,T]
and all compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exists a modulus of continuity
\lambda_{K}
such that

\sup_{n}\int_{K}|u_{n}(\cdot,t)-u_{n}(\cdot,s)|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\lambda_{K}(|t-s|).

We immediately see that (
i
) implies (
I
). Moreover:

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume
r\in[1,\infty)
. If the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
satisfies (
i
) and (
iii
), then for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\eta>0
such that for all
n
and all
0\leq t\leq s\leq T

(2.1)

|t-s|<\eta\implies\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}(x,t)-u_{n}(x,s)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x<\varepsilon.

Remark 2.6
.

The condition (
2.1
) is a weaker version of the original (
III
). However, the second item of the Arzelà-Ascoli compactness theorem
A.1
is still fulfilled, and hence, Theorem
2.1
still holds under the assumptions (
i
), (
II
), and (
iii
).

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and the compact set
K
such that
B_{R}(0)\subset K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then

\begin{split}&\sup_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}(x,t)-u_{n}(x,s)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\sup_{n}\int_{K}|u_{n}(x,t)-u_{n}(x,s)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x+2\sup_{n}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{|x|>R}|u_{n}(x,t)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
\end{split}

By (
i
), we choose
R>0
such that

2\sup_{n}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{|x|>R}|u_{n}(x,t)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

and then by (
iii
), we take
\eta>0
such that

\sup_{n}\int_{K}|u_{n}(x,t)-u_{n}(x,s)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Main results

3.1.
Assumptions and concept of solution

Consider the following typical assumptions on (
GPME
):

(
\textup{A}_{\varphi}
)

\displaystyle\varphi\in C_{\textup{loc}}^{0,\vartheta}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}),\text{$\vartheta\in(0,1]$, is nondecreasing}.

(
\textup{A}_{g}
)

\displaystyle g\text{ is measurable and }\textstyle\int_{0}^{T}\big(\|g(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|g(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

(
\textup{A}_{u_{0}}
)

\displaystyle u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

In this paper, we restrict ourselves to
\mathfrak{L}^{\sigma,\mu}=L^{\sigma}+\mathcal{L}^{\mu}
, where

(
\textup{A}_{\sigma}
)

\displaystyle L^{\sigma}=c\Delta:=c\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{x_{i}x_{i}}^{2}\qquad\text{where $c\in\{0,1\}$},

and
\mathcal{L}^{\mu}
is a general symmetric Lévy operator under the
usual assumption:

\displaystyle\mu\text{ is a nonnegative symmetric Radon measure on
}\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\text{ satisfying}

\displaystyle\int_{0<|z|\leq 1}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)+\int_{|z|>1}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)<\infty.

Remark 3.1
.

(a)

Assumption (

A
g

) is the same as saying that

g\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))

where the space is understood as iterated

-spaces in the sense of

. Of course, we have that

.

(b)

Without loss of generality, we can assume
\varphi(0)=0
(by adding constants to
\varphi
in (
GPME
)).

(c)

As solutions of (
GPME
) will be bounded, we can always assume that
\varphi\in C^{0,\vartheta}(\mathbb{R})
in (

A
φ

).

We will work with very weak solutions of (
GPME
):

Definition 3.1

(Very weak solutions)
.

Let
u_{0}\in L_{\textup{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
g\in L_{\textup{loc}}^{1}(Q_{T})
. Then
u\in L_{\textup{loc}}^{1}(Q_{T})
is a
very weak
solution of (

) if, for all

,
\varphi(u)\mathfrak{L}^{\sigma,\mu}[\psi]\in L^{1}(Q_{T})
and

\iint_{Q_{T}}\big(u\partial_{t}\psi+\varphi(u)\mathfrak{L}^{\sigma,\mu}[\psi]+g\psi\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(x)\psi(x,0)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Note that under the assumptions (

A
φ

)–(
3.1
), very weak
solutions of (

) are unique in
L^{1}(Q_{T})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
by

[

, Theorem 3.1]
. By the same reference,

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\qquad\text{and}\qquad u\in C([0,T];L_{\textup{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})),

and hence, they are moreover in
C([0,T];L_{\textup{loc}}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
for all

.

3.2.
Approximation through numerical method

To define our numerical scheme, we need to introduce a discrete grid in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)
. Consider a sequence of numbers defining a nonuniform gird in time
\mathcal{T}_{\Delta t}^{T}:=\{t_{j}\}_{j=0}^{J}
such that
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{J}=T
and let
\mathbb{J}:=\{1,\ldots,J\}
. The time steps are then denoted by

\Delta t_{j}=t_{j}-t_{j-1}>0\textup{ for every $j\in\mathbb{J}$}\qquad\textup{and}\qquad\Delta t=\max_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\{\Delta t_{j}\}.

Moreover, let the space step
h>0
, and consider the discrete subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
given by

\mathcal{G}_{h}:=h\mathbb{Z}^{N}=\{x_{\beta}:=h\beta:\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\}.

We are now ready to define our numerical scheme. Since
u_{0}
and
g
do not necessarily have pointwise values, we set, for
R_{h}:=h(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
,

(3.1)

U_{\beta}^{0}:=\frac{1}{h^{N}}\int_{x_{\beta}+R_{h}}u_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}\quad G_{\beta}^{j}:=\frac{1}{h^{N}\Delta t_{j}}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\int_{x_{\beta}+R_{h}}g(x,\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Then we seek a function
U:\mathcal{G}_{h}\times\mathcal{T}_{\Delta t}^{T}\to\mathbb{R}
which solves

(3.2)

\begin{split}U_{\beta}^{j}=U_{\beta}^{j-1}+\Delta t_{j}\mathcal{L}^{h}[\varphi(U_{\cdot}^{j})]_{\beta}+\Delta t_{j}G_{\beta}^{j}\quad\forall(\beta,j)\in(\mathbb{Z}^{N},\mathbb{J}).\end{split}

Remark 3.2
.

Note that the above scheme is implicit in the diffusion term. This is done to ensure a simple theoretical analysis for merely Hölder continuous
\varphi
. If we choose an explicit scheme, we need to rely on a CFL-type stability condition which involves
|\varphi|_{C^{0,1}}^{-1}
, and this condition will blow up if
\varphi
is not Lipschitz continuous. In the pure Hölder case, we would then need a further approximation of the nonlinearity. We refer the reader to
[
15
,
16
]
for details.

The discrete variants of
\partial_{t}
and
\mathfrak{L}^{\sigma,\mu}
will now be discussed. For the partial derivative in time, we use the simple backward difference:

\frac{\psi(t_{j})-\psi(t_{j-1})}{\Delta t_{j}}\qquad\text{for all $\psi:\mathcal{T}_{\Delta t}^{T}\to\mathbb{R}$}.

The finite-difference discretization of
\Delta
is well-known and given by

\Delta_{h}\psi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\psi(x+he_{i})+\psi(x-he_{i})-2\psi(x)}{h^{2}}.

Recently, such approximation was shown to be in the class of Lévy
operators for a certain finite measure
[
14
,
13
,
15
,
16
]
. We thus present a unified approach to
numerical discretizations for local and nonlocal operators. In fact,
by choosing the correct weights, we recover either local, nonlocal, or
combinations of both. Hence, consider the family of bounded,
symmetric, and monotone operators
\mathcal{L}^{h}:\ell^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{h})\to\mathbb{R}
given
by

\mathcal{L}^{h}[\psi](x_{\beta})=\mathcal{L}^{h}[\psi]_{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma\not=0}(\psi(x_{\beta}+z_{\gamma})-\psi(x_{\beta}))\omega_{\gamma,h}\quad\text{for all $\psi:\mathcal{G}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}$},

where
z_{-\gamma}=-z_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{G}_{h}
and

\sum_{\gamma\neq 0}\omega_{\gamma,h}<\infty
. The sum is
over
0\neq\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and the weights
\omega_{\gamma,h}
such
that
\mathcal{L}^{h}
converges to
\mathfrak{L}^{\sigma,\mu}
in a suitable
sense, see (
3.6
) below.

Remark 3.3
.

We can also write

\mathcal{L}^{h}[\psi]_{\beta}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\psi(x_{\beta}+z)-\psi(x_{\beta}))\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)\quad\textup{with}\quad\nu_{h}(z)=\sum_{\gamma\not=0}\delta_{z_{\gamma}}(z)w_{\gamma,h},

where
\delta_{z_{\gamma}}
is the dirac delta measure centered at
z_{\gamma}
(cf.
[
16
]
).

We then need the following discrete versions of (
3.1
):

\displaystyle\text{For all $h>0$ and $\gamma\neq 0$, }\omega_{\gamma,h}=\omega_{-\gamma,h}\geq 0\text{ and}

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{0<h<1}\bigg\{\sum_{h\leq|z_{\gamma}|\leq 1}|z_{\gamma}|^{2}\omega_{\gamma,h}+\sum_{|z_{\gamma}|>1}\omega_{\gamma,h}\bigg\}<\infty.

Remark 3.4
.

The parallel of the above assumptions with (
3.1
) might be easier to see in terms of
\nu_{h}
:

\sup_{0<h<1}\bigg\{\int_{0<|z|\leq 1}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)+\int_{|z|>1}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)\bigg\}<\infty.

In Appendix
C.3
, we also provide conditions for checking this assumption through operator consistency (see (
3.6
) below) and assumptions on the limit operator
\mathfrak{L}^{\sigma,\mu}
.

Equitightness and convergence in
C([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
with
r\in[1,\infty)
of numerical schemes will be presented for a suitable interpolant which extends the discrete solutions to the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)
. For that reason, let us define the piecewise
constant
space interpolant
\overline{U}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
of
U:\mathcal{G}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\overline{U}(x)=\sum_{x_{\beta}\in\mathcal{G}_{h}}\mathbf{1}_{x_{\beta}+R_{h}}(x)U_{\beta},\qquad\text{where $U_{\beta}=U(x_{\beta})$,}

and the piecewise
linear
in time and piecewise
constant
in space space-time interpolant
\widetilde{\overline{U}}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
of
U:\mathcal{G}_{h}\times\mathcal{T}_{\Delta t}^{T}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(3.3)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\overline{U}}(x,t)&=\overline{U^{0}}(x)\mathbf{1}_{\{t_{0}\}}(t)\\
&\quad+\sum_{t_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{\Delta t}^{T}\setminus{t_{0}}}\mathbf{1}_{(t_{j-1},t_{j}]}(t)\Big(\overline{U^{j-1}}(x)+\frac{t-t_{j-1}}{t_{j}-t_{j-1}}\big(\overline{U^{j}}(x)-\overline{U^{j-1}}(x)\big)\Big).\end{split}

\begin{split}&\|\widetilde{U}(\cdot,t)-\widetilde{U}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\leq\|\widetilde{U}(\cdot,t)-\widetilde{U}_{\delta}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\widetilde{U}_{\delta}(\cdot,t)-\widetilde{U}_{\delta}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\widetilde{U}_{\delta}(\cdot,s)-\widetilde{U}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\leq 2^{1+\frac{r-1}{r}}M_{u_{0},g}^{\frac{r-1}{r}}\big(\lambda_{u_{0}}(\delta)+\lambda_{g}(\delta)\big)+\frac{t-t_{j-1}}{\Delta t_{j}}\|U^{j}_{\delta}-U^{j-1}_{\delta}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\quad+\sum_{l=j-k+1}^{j-1}\|U^{l}_{\delta}-U^{l-1}_{\delta}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\frac{t_{j-k}-s}{\Delta t_{j-k}}\|U^{j-k}_{\delta}-U^{j-k-1}_{\delta}\big\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\leq 2^{1+\frac{r-1}{r}}M_{u_{0},g}^{\frac{r-1}{r}}\big(\lambda_{u_{0}}(\delta)+\lambda_{g}(\delta)\big)+C_{u_{0},g,\varphi,\omega,\rho}|t-s|\Big(\delta^{-2}+1\Big)\\
&\quad+\int_{s}^{t}\|g(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.\\
\end{split}

The proof is complete.

Appendix A
Known compactness theorems

We will use the following form of Arzelà-Ascoli’s compactness theorem (see e.g. Chapter X.2.5 in
[
5
]
or Section 2 in
[
39
]
):

Theorem A.1

(Arzelà-Ascoli)
.

Let
(K,d_{K})
be a compact metric space and
(X,d_{X})
a complete metric space. Define

C(K;X):=\{\text{continuous functions $\psi:K\to X$}\}

endowed with the uniform distance metric
\sup_{t\in K}d_{X}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Then
M\subset C(K;X)
is relatively compact if and only if, for all
t\in K
, the following two conditions are fulfilled:

(i)

(Pointwise compactness)

\{\psi(t)\in X:\psi\in M\}\subset X
is relatively compact.

(ii)

(Equicontinuity)
For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a neighbourhood
V
about
t
such that, for all
\psi\in M
and all
s\in V
,

d_{X}(\psi(t),\psi(s))<\varepsilon.

Remark A.2
.

(a)

A related version of the above theorem also exists. Define

\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset F(K;X):=\{\text{functions $\psi:K\to X$}\}

endowed with the uniform distance metric
\sup_{t\in K}d_{X}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Assume that
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies condition (i) and condition (ii) replaced by: for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\eta>0
such that

d_{K}(t,s)<\eta\quad\textup{$\forall 0\leq t\leq s\leq T$}\quad\implies\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{X}(\psi_{n}(t),\psi_{n}(s))<\varepsilon\quad\textup{$\forall\psi_{n}\in M$}.

Then there exists
\{\psi_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in F(K;X)
such that
\psi_{n_{k}}\to\psi
in
F(K;X)
as
k\to\infty
, and moreover,
\psi\in C(K;X)
. We refer the reader to
[
22
, Theorem C.11]
for a proof.

(b)

Note that by part (a) it is possible to conclude that the limit is indeed in
C(K;X)
even though the convergence is not in that space.

The standard Kolmogorov-Riesz’s compactness theorem (see e.g. Theorem 5 in
[
29
]
) is given as follows:

Theorem A.3

(Kolmogorov-Riesz)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then
M\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is relatively compact if and only if the following two conditions are fulfilled:

(i)

(Equicontinuity)
For all
\varepsilon>0
there is some
\eta>0
such that, for all
\psi\in M
and all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|\xi|<\eta
,

\|\psi(\cdot+\xi)-\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\varepsilon.

(ii)

(Equitightness)
For all
\varepsilon>0
there is some
R>0
such that, for all

\int_{|x|>R}|\psi(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x<\varepsilon^{p}.

Remark A.4

Recall that in
[

Appendix C

C.1.
Uniform estimates for discrete operators

In the local case
\mathfrak{L}^{\sigma,\mu}=\Delta
, we can always control the discretization by the operator itself.

Lemma C.1
.

Assume
p\in[1,\infty]
. Let
\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\Delta\psi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and recall that

\Delta_{h}\psi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\psi(x+he_{i})+\psi(x-he_{i})-2\psi(x)}{h^{2}}.

Then

\begin{split}\|\Delta_{h}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 2\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.\end{split}

Remark C.2
.

Here we immediately see that control of
\Delta\psi
gives control of
\Delta_{h}\psi
.

Proof.

An application of the Taylor theorem yields (
|he_{i}|=h<1
)

\begin{split}\Delta_{h}\psi(x)&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)D^{2}\psi(x+the_{i})e_{i}\cdot e_{i}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{-1}^{0}(1+t)D^{2}\psi(x+the_{i})e_{i}\cdot e_{i}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)\\
&=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-|t|)\Delta\psi(x+the_{i})\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Finally, by Jensen’s inequality and Tonelli’s lemma,

\begin{split}\|\Delta_{h}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}&=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta_{h}\psi(x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-|t|)^{p}|\Delta\psi(x+the_{i})|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq 2\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta\psi(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}=2\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.\end{split}

The proof is complete.
∎

In the nonlocal case
\mathfrak{L}^{\sigma,\mu}=\mathcal{L}^{\mu}
, we cannot argue as above, but we can give estimates on its discretizations in an analogous way.

Lemma C.3
.

Assume
p\in[1,\infty]
. Let
\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\psi,D\psi,D^{2}\psi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then:

(a)

Under
\sum_{\gamma\neq 0}\omega_{\gamma,h}<\infty
and for all
R>1
,

\begin{split}&\|\mathcal{L}^{h}[\psi]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\leq\|D^{2}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\bigg\{\sum_{h\leq|z_{\gamma}|\leq R}|z_{\gamma}|^{2}\omega_{\gamma,h}\bigg\}+2\|\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\bigg\{\sum_{|z_{\gamma}|>R}\omega_{\gamma,h}\bigg\}.\end{split}

(b)

Under
\sum_{\gamma\neq 0}\min\{|z_{\gamma}|^{2},|z_{\gamma}|\}\omega_{\gamma,h}<\infty
,

\begin{split}&\|\mathcal{L}^{h}[\psi]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\leq\|D^{2}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\bigg\{\sum_{h\leq|z_{\gamma}|\leq 1}|z_{\gamma}|^{2}\omega_{\gamma,h}\bigg\}+\|D\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\bigg\{\sum_{|z_{\gamma}|>1}|z_{\gamma}|\omega_{\gamma,h}\bigg\}.\end{split}

Remark C.4
.

We see that the estimation of
\|\mathcal{L}^{h}[\psi]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
only relies on information about
\|\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
,
\|D\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, and
\|D^{2}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
. However, there is no immediate control of
\mathcal{L}^{h}[\psi]
through
\mathcal{L}^{\mu}[\psi]
. Such control requires a deeper analysis, which we provide in Appendix
C.3
below.

Proof.

(a) Recall that

\mathcal{L}^{h}[\psi](x)=\sum_{\gamma\not=0}(\psi(x+z_{\gamma})-\psi(x))\omega_{\gamma,h}.

As usual, since
\omega_{\gamma,h}=\omega_{-\gamma,h}
, we use

\sum_{h\leq|z_{\gamma}|\leq R}z_{\gamma}\cdot D\psi(x)\mathbf{1}_{|z_{\gamma}|\leq R}\omega_{\gamma,h}=0

to take advantage of the Taylor theorem:

\sum_{h\leq|z_{\gamma}|\leq R}(\psi(x+z_{\gamma})-\psi(x))\omega_{\gamma,h}=\sum_{h\leq|z_{\gamma}|\leq R}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)D^{2}\psi(x+tz_{\gamma})z_{\gamma}\cdot z_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)\omega_{\gamma,h}.

We now apply a Minkowski type of inequality for integrals (cf. e.g. Theorem
B.1
) to get

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{L}^{h}[\psi]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigg|\sum_{h\leq|z_{\gamma}|\leq R}\Big(\psi(x+z_{\gamma})-\psi(x)-z_{\gamma}\cdot D\psi(x)\mathbf{1}_{|z_{\gamma}|\leq R}\Big)\omega_{\gamma,h}\bigg|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigg|\sum_{|z_{\gamma}|>R}\big(\psi(x+z_{\gamma})-\psi(x)\big)\omega_{\gamma,h}\bigg|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\|D^{2}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\bigg\{\sum_{h\leq|z_{\gamma}|\leq R}|z_{\gamma}|^{2}\omega_{\gamma,h}\bigg\}+2\|\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\bigg\{\sum_{|z_{\gamma}|>R}\omega_{\gamma,h}\bigg\}.\end{split}

(b) Here, we instead use the fundamental theorem of calculus when
|z_{\gamma}|>1
:

\psi(x+z_{\gamma})-\psi(x)=\int_{0}^{1}D\psi(x+tz_{\gamma})\cdot tz_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Otherwise, the proof can then be completed in a similar manner as part (a).
∎

\psi(x):=\begin{cases}|x|^{2}-1\qquad\qquad&\text{if $|x|\leq 1$}\\
2(|x|-1)\qquad\qquad&\text{if $|x|>1$,}\end{cases}

we have (
C.2
).

Proof.

We follow the proof of Proposition 3.2 in
[
15
]
. In our particular case,

D\psi(x)=\begin{cases}2x\qquad\qquad&\text{if $|x|\leq 1$}\\
2\frac{x}{|x|}\qquad\qquad&\text{if $|x|>1$,}\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad D^{2}\psi(x)=\begin{cases}2\qquad\qquad&\text{if $|x|\leq 1$}\\
0\qquad\qquad&\text{if $|x|>1$.}\end{cases}

We get

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{\nu_{h}}[\psi](0)&=\int_{|z|\leq 1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)z^{T}D^{2}\psi(tz)z\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)+\int_{|z|>1}\int_{0}^{1}D\psi(tz)\cdot tz\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)\\
&=\int_{|z|\leq 1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)z^{T}D^{2}\psi(tz)z\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)+\int_{1<|z|\leq 1/t}\int_{0}^{1}D\psi(tz)\cdot tz\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)\\
&\quad+\int_{|z|>1/t}\int_{0}^{1}D\psi(tz)\cdot tz\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)\\
&=\int_{|z|\leq 1}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)+\int_{1<|z|\leq 1/t}\int_{0}^{1}2t^{2}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)+\int_{|z|>1/t}\int_{0}^{1}2t|z|\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)\\
&\geq\int_{|z|\leq 1}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)+\int_{1<|z|\leq 1/t}\int_{0}^{1}2t^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t|z|\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)+\int_{|z|>1/t}\int_{0}^{1}2t\,\mathrm{d}t|z|\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)\\
&\geq\int_{|z|>0}\min\{|z|^{2},|z|\}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z).\end{split}

Hence,

\begin{split}\sup_{0<h<1}\int_{|z|>0}\min\{|z|^{2},|z|\}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)&\leq\sup_{0<h<1}\mathcal{L}^{\nu_{h}}[\psi](0)\\
&\leq\|\mathcal{L}^{\mu}[\psi]\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\sup_{0<h<1}|(\mathcal{L}^{\nu_{h}}-\mathcal{L}^{\mu})[\psi](0)|,\end{split}

which is finite due to the pointwise convergence and the assumption (
b
).
∎

In the case (
3.3
), we get:

Lemma C.8
.

Assumption (
3.3
) holds if
\mu
satisfies (
b
) and for

\psi(x):=\begin{cases}R^{\alpha-2}|x|^{2}-R^{\alpha}\qquad\qquad&\text{if $|x|\leq R$}\\
\phi(x)\qquad\qquad&\text{if $|x|>R$,}\end{cases}

where
\phi
is some nonnegative function such that
\psi\in C_{\textup{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=|\psi(0)|=R^{\alpha}
, we have (
C.2
) uniformly in
R
.

Proof.

Again, we follow the proof of Proposition 3.2 in
[
15
]
. We obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{\nu_{h}}[\psi](0)&\geq R^{\alpha-2}\int_{|z|\leq 1}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)+R^{\alpha-2}\int_{1<|z|\leq R}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)-\psi(0)\int_{|z|>R}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)\\
&\geq R^{\alpha-2}\int_{1<|z|\leq R}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)+R^{\alpha}\int_{|z|>R}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z).\end{split}

Hence, since
R>1
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
, we have
R^{\alpha-2}\leq 1
and

\begin{split}&\sup_{0<h<1}\bigg\{R^{\alpha-2}\int_{1<|z|\leq R}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)+R^{\alpha}\int_{|z|>R}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{h}(z)\bigg\}\\
&\leq\sup_{0<h<1}\mathcal{L}^{\nu_{h}}[\psi](0)\leq|\mathcal{L}^{\mu}[\psi](0)|+\sup_{0<h<1}|(\mathcal{L}^{\nu_{h}}-\mathcal{L}^{\mu})[\psi](0)|\\
&\leq\bigg(R^{\alpha-2}\int_{|z|\leq 1}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)+R^{\alpha-2}\int_{1<|z|\leq R}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)+\int_{|z|>R}|\psi(z)-\psi(0)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)\bigg)\\
&\quad+\sup_{0<h<1}|(\mathcal{L}^{\nu_{h}}-\mathcal{L}^{\mu})[\psi](0)|\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{|z|\leq 1}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)+R^{\alpha-2}\int_{1<|z|\leq R}|z|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)+2R^{\alpha}\int_{|z|>R}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)\bigg)\\
&\quad+\sup_{0<h<1}|(\mathcal{L}^{\nu_{h}}-\mathcal{L}^{\mu})[\psi](0)|,\end{split}

which is finite due to the pointwise convergence and the assumption (
b
).
∎

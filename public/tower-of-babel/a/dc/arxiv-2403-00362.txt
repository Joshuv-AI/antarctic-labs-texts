Equivariant cohomology for cyclic groups
Samik Basu, Pinka Dey

Abstract
. In this paper, we compute the R ​ O ​ ( C n ) RO(C_{n}) -graded coefficient ring of equivariant cohomology for cyclic groups C n C_{n} , in the case of Burnside ring coefficients, and in the case of constant coefficients. We use the invertible Mackey functors under the box product to reduce the gradings in the computation from R ​ O ​ ( C n ) RO(C_{n}) to those expressable as combinations of λ d \lambda^{d} for divisors d d of n n , where λ \lambda is the inclusion of C n C_{n} in S 1 S^{1} as the roots of unity. We make explicit computations for the geometric fixed points for Burnside ring coefficients, and in the positive cone for constant coefficients. The positive cone is also computed for the Burnside ring in the case of prime power order, and in the case of square free order. Finally, we also make computations at non-negative gradings for the constant coefficients.

Introduction

In the classical setting, equivariant homotopy theory was extremely useful in interpreting computations for classifying spaces of groups. The Atiyah-Segal completion theorem

[

[

[

In terms of computations of homotopy classes
[X,Y]

is an Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum, and this gives rise to “ordinary cohomology theories”. It takes a little work to define these in the equivariant context, as the homotopy groups carry a Mackey functor
[
28
]
structure coming from the restriction, transfer, and conjugation maps. It is fair then to consider Eilenberg MacLane spectra

for Mackey functors

[

RO(G)
.

For a finite group
G
, and a real representation
V
of
G
, one may endow

with a
G
-invariant inner product. This allows us to define

D(V)=\{v\in V\mid\langle v,v\rangle\leq 1\},~\mbox{ }~S(V)=\{v\in V\mid\langle v,v\rangle=1\},~\mbox{ }~S^{V}=D(V)/S(V),

-fold suspension functors

are invertible. We refer to
[

,
25

-spectra denoted by
\mbox{Sp}^{G}
. As the

-fold suspension functors are invertible, one has a spectrum

for

, which may be used to equip the homotopy classes of maps with a

-grading.

Given a

\underline{M}
at
\alpha\in RO(G)
is denoted by
H^{\alpha}_{G}(X;\underline{M}):=\pi_{-\alpha}^{G}F(X,H\underline{M})
for the underlying group, and
\underline{H}^{\alpha}_{G}(X;\underline{M}):=\underline{\pi}_{-\alpha}^{G}F(X,H\underline{M})
for the underlying Mackey functor. The notation

refers to the function spectrum. The first step towards computing such a cohomology theory is to compute it in the case

the sphere spectrum. This turns out to be a difficult problem to describe in general, and has been carried out in very restricted cases when the groups possess a simple structure for the poset of subgroups

,
5

6
,
30
,

(the constant Mackey functor) or

[

warn us that computations may not necessarily yield easy formulas. Here, we follow an opportunistic approach, and identify sectors of

The Mackey functors

1.1
.

Computational techniques.

Before we describe our results, we point out the computational techniques available to us. An advantage for cyclic groups is that the irreducible representations are described using powers of
\lambda
, the one dimensional complex representation associated to the inclusion
C_{n}\subset S^{1}
as the
n^{th}
-roots of unity. A
G
-representation
V
defines an Euler class
a_{V}\in\pi_{-V}(S^{0})
given by the inclusion
S^{0}\to S^{V}
. One has the equation
a_{V\oplus W}=a_{V}a_{W}
, which implies that for the cyclic group
C_{n}
, these are monomials in
a_{\lambda^{s}}
for
0\leq s<n
. The Euler class is part of a Gysin cofibre sequence

S(\lambda^{s})_{+}\to S^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a_{\lambda^{s}}}}{{\to}}S^{\lambda^{s}}.

Smashing with a spectrum
X
and computing
RO(G)
-graded homotopy groups gives a long exact sequence in which two terms are
\pi_{\bigstar}X
and a shift of
\pi_{\bigstar}X
, while the third
\pi_{\bigstar}(S(\lambda^{s})_{+}\wedge X)
can be computed from a proper subgroup of
C_{n}
. We call this the
cofibre sequence method
.

The second technique we use is the
Tate diagram method
. For
1\leq s<n
, write

E_{s}=S(\infty\lambda^{s})=\varinjlim_{n}S(n\lambda^{s}),~~~E^{s}=S^{\infty\lambda^{s}}=\varinjlim_{n}S^{n\lambda^{s}}.

We have the following commutative diagram
[
13
]
in which the rows are cofibre sequences.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{E_{s}}_{+}\wedge X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{s}\wedge X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{E_{s}}_{+}\wedge F({E_{s}}_{+},X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F({E_{s}}_{+},X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{s}\wedge F({E_{s}}_{+},X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

The idea is that the bottom row is computable using subgroups of the cyclic group, and in this way, we know the top left corner. The map
X\to E^{s}\wedge X
is basically obtained by inverting the Euler class
a_{\lambda^{s}}
in case
X
is a ring spectrum. Applying both of these together often leads to explicit calculations.

The third technique is called the
Bredon cohomology method
. This is given by the formulas for a representation
V

\pi_{n-V}(H\underline{M})\cong\pi_{n}(S^{V}\wedge H\underline{M})\cong H_{n}^{G}(S^{V};\underline{M}),

\pi_{V-n}(H\underline{M})\cong\pi_{-n}(S^{-V}\wedge H\underline{M})\cong H^{n}_{G}(S^{V};\underline{M}).

The groups
H_{n}^{G}(S^{V};\underline{M})
and
H^{n}_{G}(S^{V};\underline{M})
can be computed by writing down an equivariant CW complex structure on the space
S^{V}
, and then via cellular homology and cohomology. We use these three techniques judiciously to uncover computations of
\pi_{\bigstar}^{C_{n}}H\underline{A}
and
\pi_{\bigstar}^{C_{n}}H\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
for
\bigstar
lying in various sectors of
RO(C_{n})
.

1.5

Constructing cohomology classes.

The Euler classes

1.6
.

Geometric fixed points.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be the family of proper subgroups of
G
. The geometric fixed points of a
G
-spectrum

is defined as

\Phi^{G}(X)=(\widetilde{E{\mathcal{P}}}\wedge X)^{G}.

The
G

is characterized by the property

E{\mathcal{P}}^{H}=\begin{cases}\ast&\mbox{if }H\in{\mathcal{P}}\\
\varnothing&\mbox{if }H\not\in{\mathcal{P}},\end{cases}

is defined using the cofibre sequence

For the cyclic group

\widetilde{E{\mathcal{P}}}\simeq E^{s_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge E^{s_{k}}.

This points us to two consequences. For a commutative ring spectrum

is obtained from

by inverting the Euler classes and restricting to integer gradings. Further, iterating the Tate diagram method is useful in computing the homotopy groups of the geometric fixed point spectrum. These have been computed for

]

]
. If

),

)

.

Let

. For

be the subgroup of

. Then we have

1.7
.

Computations over the positive cone.

We write
the positive cone
for the sector of
RO(G)
consisting of elements of the form
n-V
where
V
is an actual representation of
G
. This is the sector where computations can be carried out using the Bredon cohomology method. The positive cone is also additively closed, so the part of
\pi_{\bigstar}^{G}(H\underline{A})
or
\pi_{\bigstar}^{G}(H\underline{\mathbb{Z}})
restricted to the positive cone is a graded commutative ring.
Observe that both the Euler classes and the orientation classes lie inside the positive cone. Our computations in this sector are inspired from the following theme for a cyclic group.

The positive cone is generated by the Euler classes and the orientation classes.

We write
\bigstar_{\textup{div}}
for
\bigstar_{\pm\textup{div}}\cap\big(\mbox{\it positive cone}\big)
, which is a linear combination
\ell-\epsilon\sigma-\sum_{d_{i}\mid n}~~b_{i}\mspace{2mu}\lambda^{d_{i}}
with
b_{i}\geq 0
and
\epsilon\geq 0
. It suffices to verify the theme above by restricting the attention to
\bigstar_{\textup{div}}
. For the constant coefficients
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
, the a-relations, and au-relations are given by

\frac{n}{d}a_{\lambda^{d}}=0\quad\textup{~and~}\quad\frac{d}{\gcd(d,s)}a_{\lambda^{s}}u_{\lambda^{d}}=\frac{s}{\gcd(d,s)}a_{\lambda^{d}}u_{\lambda^{s}}.

There are no u-relations. If
n
is even,
\sigma
is a non-orientable representation which has the Euler class
a_{\sigma}
satisfying
2a_{\sigma}=0
, but no orientation class. For
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
-coefficients we prove (see Theorem
7.7
and Theorem
7.9
)

Theorem B
.

For a cyclic group
C_{n}
,
\pi_{\bigstar_{\textup{div}}}^{C_{n}}(H\underline{\mathbb{Z}})
is the polynomial ring on the Euler classes
a_{\lambda^{d}}
(and
a_{\sigma}
if
n
is even), the orientation classes
u_{\lambda^{d}}
modulo the ideal generated by the a-relations and the au-relations.

Theorem B is used in
[
4
]
in the computation of equivariant cohomology of complex projective spaces for cyclic groups. This result confirms for us that there are no further relations among Euler classes and orientation classes in this case. We also point out that this is special for cyclic groups, as even for elementary Abelian groups with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
coefficients we have additional relations (Remark
4.19
). We also compute the positive cone for
\underline{A}
-coefficients but have to assume that either

a prime power or

is square free (see Theorem

, Theorem

and Theorem

)

Theorem C
.

Suppose that

is either a prime power or square free. The ring
\pi_{\bigstar_{\textup{div}}}^{C_{n}}H\underline{A}
is generated by Euler classes and orientation classes, modulo the ideal generated by the a-relations, u-relations, and the au-relations.

The proof of Theorem C goes differently for the prime power case, and the square free case. In the latter case, we use the trick employed in
[
6
]
which allows us to move from
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
-coefficients to
\underline{A}
-coefficients by a string of short exact sequences of Mackey functors.

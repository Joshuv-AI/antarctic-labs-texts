Abstract
In the paper we consider the point measure that corresponds to Arratia flow. The central limit theorem of the multiple integrals with respect to this measure was obtained.

Introduction.

Let
\{x(u,t),\ u\in\mathbb{R},\ t\geq 0\}
be the Arratia flow
[
1
]
. It is known
[
8
]
that for every
t>0
the set
x(\mathbb{R},t)
is countable and locally finite. Denote
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}:=x(\mathbb{R},t)
and define the point measure

N_{t}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{u_{i}}.

(1)

In this paper we investigate limits in the central limit theorem for integral functionals with respect to the factorial powers of the point measure
N_{t}.

Definition 1
.

(
[
11
]
)
n-th factorial power
N^{(n)}
of the point measure
N=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{u_{i}}
is defined by

N^{(n)}(C)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\neq\ldots\neq i_{n}\end{subarray}}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}{\{(u_{i_{1}},\ldots,u_{i_{n}})\in C\}},

where
C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

By
N^{\otimes n}
we denote the usual power of measure
N,
i.e. for
\Delta_{i}\in\mathbb{R},

N^{\otimes n}(\Delta_{1}\times...\times\Delta_{n})=N(\Delta_{1})\ldots N(\Delta_{n}).

Definition 2
.

A locally integrable function
\rho^{(n)}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

is called an
n-
point density function of a point measure
N
if, for any subset
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

the following formula holds

\mathbf{E}N^{(n)}(A)=\int_{A}\rho^{(n)}(\vec{x})d\vec{x}

For example, the Poisson process on the line with intensity measure
\mu(dx)=p(x)dx
has
n-
point density function that is equal to

\rho^{(n)}(\vec{x})=p^{\otimes n}(\vec{x})=p(x_{1})p(x_{2})\ldots p(x_{n}).

In the papers
[
12
]
,
[
15
]
the authors proved existence of the
n-
point density functions
\rho^{(n)}
for the point measure
N_{t}.

For instance,

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{(1)}(v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}

(2)

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{(2)}(v_{1},v_{2})=\dfrac{1}{\pi t}\left(1+\dfrac{\left|v_{2}-v_{1}\right|}{2\sqrt{t}}\cdot e^{-(v_{2}-v_{1})^{2}/4t}\cdot\int\limits_{\left|v_{2}-v_{1}\right|/\sqrt{t}}^{+\infty}e^{-v^{2}/4}\,dv-e^{-(v_{2}-v_{1})^{2}/2t}\right)

(3)

Theorem 4
.

The operator
A_{t}

which is defined for

Proof.

For non-negative function

\mathbf{E}A_{t}\phi=\int_{0}^{1}\phi(v)\rho^{(1)}(v)dv

and by the definition of

\mathbf{E}\left(A_{t}\phi\right)^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(v_{1})\phi(v_{2})\rho^{(2)}(v_{1},v_{2})dv_{1}\,dv_{2}+\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{2}(v_{1})\rho^{(1)}(v_{1})dv_{1},

For the point measure
N_{t}
its

Using obtained inequality, operator
A_{t}
can be extended from operator defined on
C([0;1])
to a continuous operator from
L_{2}([0;1])
to
L_{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P}).

∎

Theorem 8
.

Let
L_{2,{symm}}([0;1]^{k})
be the space of square integrable symmetric functions.
The linear operator
A^{k}_{t}
from
L_{2,symm}([0;1]^{k})
to
L_{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P})
which is defined as

A^{k}_{t}\phi=\int_{[0;1]^{k}}\phi(\vec{v})N^{(k)}_{t}(d\,v_{1},\ldots,d\,v_{k}),

is a continuous operator.

Proof.

By definition of
k-
point functions
\rho^{(k)}
of the measure
N_{t}^{(k)}

\mathbf{E}\int_{[0;1]^{k}}\phi(\vec{v})N^{(k)}_{t}(d\,v_{1},\ldots,d\,v_{k})=\int_{[0;1]^{k}}\phi(\vec{v})\rho_{t}^{(k)}(\vec{v})d\,\vec{v}.

Rewriting the square of integral with respect to factorial product as sum of series indexed with non-equal indexes we get

\mathbf{E}\left(\int_{[0;1]^{k}}\phi(\vec{v})N^{(k)}_{t}(d\,v_{1},\ldots,d\,v_{k})\right)^{2}=\\
=\mathbf{E}\sum_{i_{1},j_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{2}\neq i_{1}\\
j_{2}\neq j_{1}\end{subarray}}\ldots\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{k}\neq i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\\
j_{k}\neq j_{1},\ldots,j_{k-1}\end{subarray}}\phi(u_{i_{1}},\ldots,u_{i_{k}})\phi(u_{j_{1}},\ldots,u_{j_{k}})=\\
=\sum_{l=0}^{k}c_{l}\int_{[0;1]^{k}}\int_{[0;1]^{k-l}}\phi(\vec{u})\phi(u_{1},\ldots,u_{l},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k-l})\rho^{(2k-l)}(\vec{v},\vec{u})d\,\vec{v}d\,\vec{u}

with some constants
c_{l}\in\mathbb{N}.

Applying uniform estimation for the
n-
point densities
\rho_{t}^{(n)}\leq\frac{1}{(\pi t)^{n/2}}

[
12
]
we get

\mathbf{E}\left(\int_{[0;1]^{n}}\phi(\vec{v})N^{(n)}_{t}(d\,v_{1},\ldots,d\,v_{k})\right)^{2}\leq C\int_{[0;1]^{n}}\phi^{2}(\vec{v})d\vec{v}.

This proves the theorem.
∎

2
Finite-dimensional central limit theorem for
\mathbf{(A_{t})_{t>0}}

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}:\ f|_{[0;1]}\in L_{2}([0;1]),\ f(x)=f(x+1),\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
be a class of functions
f
such that:

1)
f
is 1-periodic;

2) restriction of
f
on the interval
[0;1]
is square integrable.

Similarly to definition of the operator
A_{t}
let us define the integral operator

A_{k,t}f=\int_{k}^{k+1}f(u)N_{t}(du).

Such operators are well-defined for functions
f\in\mathcal{P}.

In this section we will prove that the sequence
\{A_{k,t}f\}_{k\geq 1}
satisfies central limit theorem.

The main reason why we can expect the validity of the central limit theorem is the weak dependence of the sequence
\{A_{k,t}f\}_{k\geq 1}
. It can be checked that this sequence satisfies a mixing condition.
Recall that for a stationary sequence
\{\xi_{k}\}_{k\in K}
(here
K=\mathbb{R}\text{ or }K=\mathbb{N}
) its
\alpha-
mixing coefficient is defined as

\alpha_{\xi}(h)=\sup\{|\mathbf{P}(AB)-\mathbf{P}(A)\mathbf{P}(B)|,\ A\in\mathcal{F}(\xi)_{-\infty}^{0},\ B\in\mathcal{F}(\xi)_{h}^{\infty}\}

where
\mathcal{F}(\xi)_{a}^{b}=\sigma\{\xi_{k},\ a<k<b\}.

Lemma 9
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{P}
. For fixed
t>0
consider sequence

\xi_{k}=A_{k,t}f,

k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\alpha_{\xi}(h)\leq 4\int_{h/3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx\leq\frac{12}{h\sqrt{2\pi t}}\exp(-h^{2}/18).

Proof.

For arbitrary
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by
D_{n,m}
the set of vectors
\vec{u},\vec{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\vec{v},\vec{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, such that

u_{1}<u_{2}<\ldots u_{n}<v_{1}<v_{2}<\ldots<v_{m}
. Consider

\Delta:=\sup_{D_{n,m}}|\mathbf{E}e^{i(\vec{a},x(\vec{u},t))}e^{i(\vec{a},x(\vec{v},t))}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{x(u_{n},t)<0\}}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{x(v_{1},t)>h\}}-\\
-\mathbf{E}e^{i(\vec{a},x(\vec{u},t))}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{x(u_{n},t)<0\}}\mathbf{E}e^{i(\vec{a},x(\vec{v},t))}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{x(v_{1},t)>h\}}|

where we denoted by
x(\vec{u},t)=(x(u_{1},t),\ldots,x(u_{n},t)).
For
L<h/2
let
B
be the random event

B=\{x(L,t)>0,x(h-L,t)<h\}
. Denoting by
\tilde{x}
an independent copy of the Arratia flow we get

\Delta\leq\sup_{D_{n,m}}|\mathbf{E}e^{i(\vec{a},x(\vec{u},t))}e^{i(\vec{a},x(\vec{v},t))}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{x(u_{n},t)<0\}}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{x(v_{1},t)>h\}}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{B}-\\
-\mathbf{E}e^{i(\vec{a},\tilde{x}(\vec{u},t))}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{\tilde{x}(u_{n},t)<0\}}e^{i(\vec{a},x(\vec{v},t))}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{x(v_{1},t)>h\}}|+\mathbf{P}(\bar{B})\leq\\
\leq\sup_{D_{n,m}}|\mathbf{E}e^{i(\vec{a},\tilde{x}(\vec{u},t))}e^{i(\vec{a},x(\vec{v},t))}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{\tilde{x}(u_{n},t)<0\}}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{x(v_{1},t)>h\}}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{B}-\\
-\mathbf{E}e^{i(\vec{a},\tilde{x}(\vec{u},t))}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{\tilde{x}(v_{n},t)<0\}}e^{i(\vec{a},\tilde{x}(\vec{v},t))}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{\tilde{x}(v_{1},t)>h\}}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{B}|+2\mathbf{P}(\bar{B}),

where we use that the processes
x(u_{i},\cdot)
and
x(v_{j},\cdot)
are independent up to the meeting moment, i.e.
d\langle x(u_{i},\cdot),x(v_{j},\cdot)\rangle(t)=1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{\{x(u_{i},t)=x(v_{j},t)\}}dt.

Now, one can get that for
L=h/3
the upper bound
P(\bar{B})\leq 2\int_{h/3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx
. Since the family of functions
\{e^{i(\vec{a},x(\vec{u},t))}1\!\!\,\mathrm{I}_{x(u_{n},t)<0},\ u_{1}<u_{2}<\ldots<u_{n},\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
generates the
\sigma-
field
\sigma\{\xi_{k},k<0\}
the statement of the lemma follows from obtained inequality.
∎

It is known that if the
\alpha-
mixing coefficient for a stationary sequence decrease fast enough then under suitable moments conditions this sequence satisfies the central limit theorem
[

]
.

Theorem 10
.

1.

Let
f\in\mathcal{P}
such that
f|_{[0;1]}\in L_{3}([0;1])
. For any

we have

X_{t}^{n}(f)=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(A_{k,t}f-\mathbf{E}A_{k,t}f\right)}{\sqrt{n}}\Rightarrow\zeta_{f}(t),\ n\to\infty,

where
\zeta_{f}(t)
is a Gaussian random variable with zero mean and variance

\sigma^{2}_{t}(f)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}f(v_{1})f(v_{2})G_{t}(v_{1},v_{2})dv_{1}\,dv_{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}(v_{1})dv_{1},

with

G_{t}(v_{1},v_{2})=g_{t}(v_{1}-v_{2})+2\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}g_{t}(v_{1}-v_{2}+l),

and

g_{t}(v_{1}-v_{2})=\rho_{t}^{(2)}(v_{1},v_{2})-\frac{1}{{\pi t}}.

2.

For any
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{m}<T
and
f\in\mathcal{P}

\Big(X_{t_{1}}^{n}(f),\ldots,X_{t_{m}}^{n}(f)\Big)\Rightarrow\left(\zeta_{f}(t_{1}),\ldots\zeta_{f}(t_{m})\right)

where
\left(\zeta_{f}(t_{1}),\ldots\zeta_{f}(t_{m})\right)
is a Gaussian vector with zero mean and covariance matrix
\Sigma_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}}(f)=(c_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{m}
with

c_{ij}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{cov}\,\left(X_{t_{i}}^{n}(f),X_{t_{j}}^{n}(f)\right),\ c_{ii}=\sigma^{2}_{t_{i}}(f),

3.

For any functions
f^{(1)},\ldots,f^{(m)}\in\mathcal{P}
and
t>0
we have

\Big(X_{t}^{n}\left((f^{(1)}\right),\ldots,X_{t}^{n}\left(f^{(m)}\right)\Big)\Rightarrow\left(\zeta_{f^{(1)}}(t),\ldots,\zeta_{f^{(m)}}(t)\right),\ n\to\infty,

where
\left(\zeta_{t}(f^{(1)}),\ldots,\zeta_{t}(f^{(m)})\right)
is a centered Gaussian vector with covariance

\mathrm{cov}\,\left(\zeta_{t}(f^{(i)}),{\zeta_{t}(f^{(j)})}\right)=\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left[f^{(i)}(u)f^{(j)}(v)+f^{(i)}(v)f^{(j)}(u)\right]G_{t}(u,v)dudv+\\
+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{1}f^{(i)}(u)f^{(j)}(u)du.

Remark 11
.

The expression for the covariances
c_{i,l}
in the second statement of the theorem will be discussed in the next section.

Proof.

Proof of the first statement of the theorem.

For each
t>0
we denote

X_{t}^{n}(f)=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(A_{k,t}f-\mathbf{E}A_{k,t}f\right)}{\sqrt{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}Y_{k}

Due to periodicity of the function
f
and stationarity with respect to spatial variable of the Arratia flow, the sequence of random variables
\{Y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is strictly stationary.
By lemma
9
its mixing coefficient

\alpha_{Y}(h)\leq c_{T}\int_{h}^{\infty}e^{-y^{2}/2}dy.

By lemma
6

\mathbf{E}|Y_{k}|^{3}<\infty.
Now we can apply central limit theorem for weakly dependent random variables (
[
9
]
)

X_{t}^{n}(f)\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{t}(f)),\ \text{as }{n\to\infty,}

where

\sigma^{2}_{t}(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Var}\,{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}Y_{k}}=\\
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}\int_{0}^{n}f(v_{1})f(v_{2})\Big(\rho_{t}^{(2)}(v_{1},v_{2})-\frac{1}{{\pi t}}\Big)dv_{1}\,dv_{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{n}f^{2}(v_{1})dv_{1}

Note that the function
\Big(\rho_{t}^{(2)}(v_{1},v_{2})-\frac{1}{{\pi t}}\Big)
depends on

only via

(see formula (
3
)). Then we can define

g_{t}(v_{1}-v_{2}):=\rho_{t}^{(2)}(v_{1},v_{2})-\frac{1}{{\pi t}}.

Using periodicity of

\sigma^{2}_{t}(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Big[\int_{0}^{n}\int_{0}^{n}f(v_{1})f(v_{2})g_{t}(v_{1}-v_{2})dv_{1}\,dv_{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{n}f^{2}(v_{1})dv_{1}\Big]=\\
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Big[\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=0}^{n-1}\int_{k_{1}}^{k_{1}+1}\int_{k_{2}}^{k_{2}+1}\!\!\!f(v_{1})f(v_{2})g_{t}(v_{1}-v_{2})dv_{1}\,dv_{2}+n\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}(v_{1})dv_{1}\Big]=\\
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Big[\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}f(v_{1})f(v_{2})g_{t}(v_{1}+k_{1}-v_{2}-k_{2})dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\Big]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}(v_{1})dv_{1}=\\
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}f(v_{1})f(v_{2})\Big[2\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}(n-l)g_{t}(v_{1}-v_{2}+l)+ng_{t}(v_{1}-v_{2})\Big]dv_{1}\,dv_{2}+\\
+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}(v_{1})dv_{1}

For the function
g_{t}(v_{1}-v_{2}):=\rho_{t}^{(2)}(v_{1},v_{2})-\frac{1}{{\pi t}}
one can write the precise formula using (

\displaystyle g_{t}(v_{1}-v_{2})=\dfrac{1}{\pi t}\left(\dfrac{\left|v_{2}-v_{1}\right|}{2\sqrt{t}}\cdot e^{-(v_{2}-v_{1})^{2}/4t}\cdot\int\limits_{\left|v_{2}-v_{1}\right|/\sqrt{t}}^{+\infty}e^{-v^{2}/4t}\,dv-e^{-(v_{2}-v_{1})^{2}/2t}\right).

(5)

converges uniformly on

, so we can pass to the limit with respect to

Proof of the second and third statements of the theorem are similar to the first one.

3
Central limit theorem for conditional expectations.

Attempt to find covariances in the statement (2) of theorem
10
naturally leads to the question of calculation conditional expectations
\mathbf{E}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f(u)N_{t_{2}}(du)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{1}}^{x}\right)
.
In this section we study limit behaviour of the conditional expectations for
t_{1}<t_{2}

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbf{E}\left(\int_{0}^{n}f(u)N_{t_{2}}(du)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{1}}^{x}\right),\text{ as }n\to\infty

where, as before,
N_{t}
is the point measure associated to the Arratia flow
x
and
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{x}=\sigma\{x(u,s),\ u\in\mathbb{R},\ s\leq t\}.

To this aim we need representation of the Arratia flow in term of Brownian web. Recall that Brownian web is defined as a family of random processes
\{\varphi_{s,\cdot}(u)\in C([s,\infty))\}
such that, given
(u_{1},t_{1}),...,(u_{m},t_{m})
the processes
\varphi_{t_{1},\cdot}(u_{1}),...,\varphi_{t_{m},\cdot}(u_{m})
are coalescing Brownian motions (see, for instance,
[
6
]
). In this section we consider Arratia flow
x(u,t)=\varphi_{0,t}(u).
We construct the point measure
N_{t_{2}}
using measure
N_{t_{1}}
and the map
\varphi_{t_{1},t_{2}}
from Brownian web.

To describe general properties of such construction let us introduce the family of random measures on
\mathbb{R}
related to the point measure
\mu
and non-decreasing function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\varphi(x)=+\infty,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty}\varphi(x)=-\infty.

For every
k\geq 2
and vector
\vec{v}=(v_{1},...,v_{k})
denote

\displaystyle v_{*}=\min\{v_{1},...,v_{k}\},

\displaystyle v^{*}=\max\{v_{1},...,v_{k}\}.

Define the set

M_{k}^{\varphi}=\{\vec{v}:\varphi(v_{*})=\varphi(v^{*})\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}

Note, that
M_{k}^{\varphi}
is a Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}.
Denote by
\mu_{\varphi}^{(k)}
the restriction of the measure
\mu^{(k)}
on the set
M_{k}^{\varphi}.
Finally, define the measure
\mu_{k,\varphi}
as an image of measure
\mu_{\varphi}^{(k)}
under the mapping

\mathbb{R}^{k}\ni\vec{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})\mapsto\varphi(v_{*}).

Such measures can be used for the expression of the counting measure obtained from the measure
\mu
under the action of the function
\phi.
Define the counting measure
\nu=(\mu\circ\varphi^{-1})_{*}
by

\nu(\Delta)=|\{u\in\Delta:\ \mu(\varphi^{-1}\{u\})>0\}|.

The integrals with respect to measure
\nu
can be expressed in terms of integrals with respect to
\mu_{k,\varphi},\ k\geq 1.

Lemma 12
.

For a continuous function
f
on
\mathbb{R}

Theorem 22

\int_{0}^{1}{\begin{subarray}{c}k\\
\ldots\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{1}e_{1}(x_{1})\ldots e_{k}(x_{k})N^{\otimes k}(dx_{1}\ldots dx_{k})=\\
=\sum_{i_{1}}\sum_{i_{2}\neq i_{1}}\ldots\sum_{i_{k}\neq i_{k-1},\ldots,i_{1}}e_{1}(u_{i_{1}})\ldots e_{k}(u_{i_{k}})=\\
=\sum_{i_{1}}\sum_{i_{2}\neq i_{1}}\ldots\sum_{i_{k-1}\neq i_{k-2},\ldots,i_{1}}\left(\sum_{i_{k}}e_{1}(u_{i_{1}})\ldots e_{k}(u_{i_{k}})-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}e_{1}(u_{i_{1}})\ldots e_{k}(u_{i_{j}})\right)=\\
=\int_{0}^{1}{\begin{subarray}{c}k-1\\
\ldots\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{1}e_{1}(x_{1})\ldots e_{k-1}(x_{k-1})N^{\otimes k-1}(dx_{1}\ldots dx_{k-1})\int_{0}^{1}e_{k}(x)N(dx)-\\
-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\int_{0}^{1}{\begin{subarray}{c}k-1\\
\ldots\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{1}e_{1}(x_{1})\ldots e_{k-1}(x_{k-1})e_{k}(x_{j})N^{\otimes k-1}(dx_{1}\ldots dx_{k-1}).

Using the limit behaviour for
(k-1)-
multiple integral we get the statement of the theorem.

Homotopy types of diagrams of Chain Complexes
David Blanc, Surojit Ghosh, Aziz Kharoof

Abstract
. We study the homotopy theory of diagrams 𝔛 : ℐ → Ch \mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}\to\mbox{\sf Ch} of chain complexes over a field indexed by a finite poset ℐ \mathscr{I} , and show that it can be completely described in terms of appropriate diagrams of graded vector spaces.

Introduction

Higher homotopy theory studies the higher homotopy invariant structure of
\infty
-categories, model categories,
and other similar frameworks. Such invariants include differentials in spectral sequences, higher order homotopy and cohomology operations,
A_{\infty}
-,
L_{\infty}
-, and
E_{n}
-structures on objects, and homotopy types of diagrams, all subsumed in principle under the
k
-invariants of an
(\infty,1)
-category (see
[
DKS
,
HNP
,
BM
]
).
However, in general it is impossible to reduce this higher structure to a
1
-categorical description.

One approach to exhibiting a collection of such higher invariants explicitly
is to use the homotopy categories of the functor categories
\mathcal{C}^{\mathscr{I}}
for all

\mathscr{I}\in\mbox{\sf Cat}
to encode the invariants of a model category
\mathcal{C}
, say. This idea was first
realized in Heller’s notion of a
hyperfunctor
:
a sort of functor from the
2
-category
Cat
of all small categories to (large)
categories (see
[
He
]
). Grothendieck later introduced the term
derivators

for the version encoding also (co)limits. Recently, Renaudin showed (in
[
R
]
)
how reasonable model categories can be embedded in the category of derivators, and this was
extended to quasi-categories in
[
A
]
and
[
FKR
]
(with respect to
the simplicial enrichment of
[
MR
]
).

In this paper, we provide an explicit approach to analyzing the homotopy types of
diagrams, in what appears to be the simplest non-trivial example:
the category
Ch
of non-negatively graded chain complexes over
a field. We consider only the “linear
\infty
-category” version
(enriched in unbounded chain complexes), and we restrict ourselves to indexing
categories
\mathscr{I}
which are finite partially ordered sets. We are able to produce
an explicit (if complicated) description of the various homotopy categories of

\mbox{\sf Ch}^{\mathscr{I}}
only by making these very special assumptions. Nevertheless, we
believe that these may point the way to studying more general situations.

0.3
.

Overall strategy.

We determine the homotopy type of the given diagram
\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
by means of two sequences of diagrams: the
derived diagrams

\operatorname{Der}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}
(
k\geq 0
) in
graded vector spaces, which we think of as being purely formal, and the
hybridizations

\operatorname{Hyb}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}
(
k\geq 0
) in the category
\mbox{\sf Ch}_{+}
(see §
0.4
below): these are diagrams having maps of graded vector spaces (possibly of positive degree) up to dimension
k
, and maps of chain complexes in higher dimensions. These two types of diagrams are constructed inductively, beginning with

\operatorname{Der}^{(0)}\mathfrak{X}=H_{0}\mathfrak{X}
and
\operatorname{Hyb}^{(0)}\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}
. The hybridization
\operatorname{Hyb}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}
is obtained from
\operatorname{Hyb}^{(k-1)}\mathfrak{X}
through several intermediate stages:

(a)

Assume by induction that we have obtained from
\mathfrak{X}
a
(k-1)
-hybrid diagram

\mathfrak{Y}=\operatorname{Hyb}^{(k-1)}\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}^{(k-1)}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}_{+}
, with the property that
\mathfrak{X}
itself can be recovered up to weak equivalence from
\mathfrak{Y}
.

(b)

From
\mathfrak{Y}
we construct its derived diagram
\operatorname{Der}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}_{\operatorname{Der}}^{(k)}\to\operatorname{gr}\mbox{\sf Vect}
,
taking values in graded vector spaces (allowing maps of positive
degree). The indexing categories
\mathscr{I}_{\operatorname{Der}}^{(k)}
are themselves partially ordered sets describable in terms of the original indexing category
\mathscr{I}
.

(c)

From
\operatorname{Der}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}
we obtained the
expanded
derived diagram

\widehat{\mathfrak{Y}}:\mathscr{J}_{\operatorname{Ind}}^{[{2}]}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}_{+}
from the
\operatorname{Ind}
-completion of
\mathscr{J}:=\mathscr{I}^{(k-1)}
(which is no longer formal, and whose
(k+1)
-truncation is weakly equivalent to that of the original
\mathfrak{Y}
).

(d)

Finally, from
\widehat{\mathfrak{Y}}
and
\mathfrak{Y}
we produce the next stage in the induction, the
reconstructed

k
-hybrid diagram
\operatorname{Hyb}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}^{(k)}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}_{+}
.

In terms of these constructions – the details of which are given in Section
3
– our main result states:

Theorem A
.

Given a diagram
\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
in non-negatively graded chain complexes indexed by a finite partially ordered set, for each
k\geq 0
the associated derived diagram
\operatorname{Der}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}_{\operatorname{Der}}^{(k)}\to\operatorname{gr}\mbox{\sf Vect}
, in graded vector spaces, determines the homotopy type of the
k
-truncation of
\mathfrak{X}
.

See Theorem
3.25
below

.

Thus the sequence
(\operatorname{Der}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X})_{k=0}^{\infty}
encodes the weak homotopy type
of the original diagram
\mathfrak{X}
.

0.4 Notation
.

We denote by
Vect
the category of vector spaces over a fixed ground field
\mathbb{F}
, by

\operatorname{gr}\mbox{\sf Vect}
that of non-negatively graded objects over

(with linear maps
of degree
\geq 0
), by

that of non-negatively graded chain complexes
over
Vect
, and by
\mbox{\sf Ch}_{+}
the extension of

allowing also maps of
positive degree (possibly non-homogeneous). Note that
\operatorname{gr}\mbox{\sf Vect}
embeds in
\mbox{\sf Ch}_{+}

as a full subcategory, and we occasionally further embed
Vect
in
\operatorname{gr}\mbox{\sf Vect}
in dimension
0
.

The

on a chain complex
X
and the
suspension

\Sigma X
are defined as usual (see

[

, §1.5]
), and thus by induction we define
C^{k}X:=C(C^{k-1}X)
and

\Sigma^{k}X:=\Sigma(\Sigma^{k-1}X)
. The
reduced suspension

is given by shifting

upwards by one degree.

A finite partially ordered set

is called a

if it has sets
\mathcal{F}_{(0)}:=\{\alpha_{\operatorname{init}}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{n_{\operatorname{init}}}
of weak

\{\omega_{\operatorname{term}}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{n_{\operatorname{term}}}
of
a weak

(maxima). Such a lattice has a finite filtration

\mathcal{F}_{(0)}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{(1)}\dotsc\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{(N)}=\mathscr{I}

given by distance from
\mathcal{F}_{(0)}
.

For any
\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Obj}(\mathscr{I})
in such a lattice, we denote by
\mathscr{I}/{\beta}
the sub-poset of all (weak) predecessors of
\beta
(including
\beta
itself), by
\mathscr{I}/\!/{\beta}
the sub-poset of strict predecessors, and by
{\alpha}/\mathscr{I}
the sub-poset of all (weak) successors of
\alpha
(including
\alpha
itself).
If
\alpha<\beta
, the categories
{\alpha}/{\mathscr{I}}/{\beta}
and
{\alpha}/{\mathscr{I}}/\!/{\beta}
are defined analogously.
Similarly for
{\alpha}/{\mathscr{I}}/{\Gamma}
and
{\alpha}/{\mathscr{I}}/\!/{\Gamma}
when

\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\dotsc,\gamma_{m}\}
is a collection of objects such that

for each

.

.

Organization.

Section
1
provides background and establishes terminology on the category of diagrams of chain complexes. Section
2
analyzes the secondary structure of such diagrams. Section
3
defines the derived diagram and studies its basic properties, and proves Theorem A.
Section
4
provides a template for the full description of two infinite sequences of

1.
Diagrams of chain complexes

Our main object of study in this paper are diagrams of chain complexes
\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
indexed by
a lattice
\mathscr{I}
(see §
0.4
). Although our results can ultimately be placed in the context of “linear
\infty
-categories” – that is, categories enriched in (unbounded) chain complexes – the work itself is most easily carried out in the framework of the projective model structure on
\mbox{\sf Ch}^{\mathscr{I}}
(see
[
Hi
, §11.6]
), induced from the usual model structure on
Ch
(see
[
DS
, §7]
). First, we recall the few necessary ingredients we need on the model structures on diagrams in chain complexes:

1.1 Definition
.

A diagram
\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
as above is called
minimally cofibrant
if

(a)

For each
\alpha\in\operatorname{Obj}(\mathscr{I})
,
\mathfrak{X}(\alpha)
can be split (non-canonically) as a direct sum of
complexes of the form
K(V,m)=\widetilde{\Sigma}^{m}V
(with
V
in dimension
m
and
0

elsewhere) or
CK(V,m)
(which has copies of
V
in dimensions
m
and
m+1
,
and
0
elsewhere, with
\partial_{m+1}=\operatorname{Id}_{V}
), for some

V\in\mbox{\sf Vect}
and
m\geq 0
.

(b)

For every summand of
\mathfrak{X}(\beta)
of the form
CK(V,m)
, there is a
(non-identity) map
\phi:\alpha\to\beta
in
\mathscr{I}
such that
\mathfrak{X}(\phi)
induces
the inclusion
K(V,m)\hookrightarrow CK(V,m)
for some summand
K(V,m)
in

\mathfrak{X}(\alpha)
(so in particular this can only happen if
\beta
is not initial).

(c)

For each non-initial
\beta\in\operatorname{Obj}(\mathscr{I})
, the structure map

\operatornamewithlimits{colim}_{\mathscr{I}/\!/{\beta}}\,\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{X}(\beta)
is monic.

1.2
.

The
\operatorname{Ind}
-completion.

For any cofibrant diagram
\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
, we expect the extra information
(beyond its
homotopy diagram

H_{\ast}\mathfrak{X}
) to be encoded by its

\operatorname{Ind}
-
completion
– that is, the diagram obtained by repeatedly adding
formal colimits
\check{\beta}
for all sub-indexing categories

{\alpha}/{\mathscr{I}}/\!/{\beta}
in
\mathscr{I}
to obtain
\mathscr{I}_{\operatorname{Ind}}
, and then extending
\mathfrak{X}
to a colimit-preserving functor
\mathfrak{X}^{[{\infty}]}:\mathscr{I}_{\operatorname{Ind}}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
.
The weak homotopy types of
\mathfrak{X}
and
\mathfrak{X}^{[{\infty}]}
determine each other uniquely.
Note, however, that it is quite difficult to extract this extra information from
\mathfrak{X}^{[{\infty}]}
directly; our main goal in this paper is to provide a more explicit inductive description
of this data.

The usual
\operatorname{Ind}
-completion is defined iteratively, by adding formal colimits, then colimits of the new diagrams formed, and so on. However, for our purposes we shall be mostly interested only in the first stage, where we only add colimits of finite directed sub-diagrams of
\mathscr{I}
(multiple pushouts), and stop there, yielding

\mathfrak{X}^{[{2}]}:\mathscr{I}^{[{2}]}_{\operatorname{Ind}}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
.

1.8
.

Primary structure of a diagram.

The underlying

of a diagram

as above is given by

).

1.9 Remark
.

As noted in §
0.3
, our determination of the homotopy type of the given diagram
\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
is by means of two sequences of diagrams: the derived diagrams
\operatorname{Der}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}
(
k\geq 0
) in
graded vector spaces, and the hybridizations
\operatorname{Hyb}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}
(
k\geq 0
) in chain complexes.

Ideally, we would have liked our approximation at level
n
to contain all the
n
-th
order information about
\mathfrak{X}
, starting with
H_{\ast}\mathfrak{X}
globally encoding all the primary data. However, this requires taking more seriously the view of
Ch
as
a “linear
(\infty,1)
-category” – that is, a category enriched in (unbounded) chain complexes, including a version of
k
-truncation of the enrichment which captures
the idea of the
k
-stem
of an unbounded complex (see
[
BB
]
and compare

[
BBC
]
). In particular, we need to keep track of the various ways higher
truncations may map to lower ones, as well as the fact that

\operatorname{Hom}(\Sigma A,B)\cong\Omega\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)
. Thus we prefer the more pedestrian but simpler approach of using an induction on the truncations of the chain complexes themselves (which eventually captures all of the same information, but at a slower pace).

2.
Secondary structure of diagrams

Our analysis of the higher order structure in diagrams of chain complexes begins with the secondary
structure. As we shall see, not only can this be described explicitly, it is in fact all that is needed to capture all the higher structure, by means of our inductive process.

The basic example of secondary structure arises from the fact that the homotopy pushout of two maps from
K(V,k)
to zero objects is
K(V,k+1)
, which yields the example described in §
0.1
. For the general case, we need to understand how such pairs of zero maps can be related to each other.

2.1
.

Pairs of objects.

The general form of sub-diagram needed to describe the full secondary structure will consist of a collection of “zero squares” as
(
0.2
)(a), all with the same source (say,
K(V,k)
).

To analyze this situation, assume given a partially ordered set
\mathscr{I}
and a diagram

\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
as above. We fix a specific dimension
k\geq 0
to work in, and for any
\alpha<\beta
in
\mathscr{I}
, let

K^{\alpha}_{\beta}=K^{\alpha}_{\beta}(\mathfrak{X}):=\operatorname{Ker}(H_{k}\mathfrak{X}(\alpha)\to H_{k}\mathfrak{X}(\beta))~.

We denote by
\operatorname{Inc}(\mathscr{I})
the set of
incomparable
objects in
\mathscr{I}
– that is, pairs
\{\gamma,\delta\}
of objects for which
\gamma\not\leq\delta
and
\delta\not\leq\gamma
. For any
\{\gamma,\delta\}
in

\operatorname{Inc}({\alpha}/\mathscr{I})

Let

be the subcategory of

\gamma

\delta

to

is a (homotopy) pushout.
If we further restrict

Thus if

Multiple pairs.

More generally, if we have

3.
Derived diagrams

We now describe our main tool in analyzing the higher homotopy information of a diagram
\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
, where
\mathscr{I}
is a partially ordered set.
As explained in §
0.3
, our approach is inductive, producing two sequences of
approximations. Thus for each
k\geq 0
we have:

(a)

The
k
-
hybridization

\operatorname{Hyb}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}^{(k)}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}_{+}
which retains all
information about the weak homotopy type of
\mathfrak{X}
, in a form which is partly formal
(that is, a diagram of graded vector spaces), at the expense of making the indexing category
\mathscr{I}^{(k)}
more complicated.

(b)

The
k
-th
derived diagram

\operatorname{Der}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}_{\operatorname{Der}}^{(k)}\to\operatorname{gr}\mbox{\sf Vect}
, which is
purely formal, and encodes all homotopy information about
\mathfrak{X}
through degree
k
.

Here the sequence
(\operatorname{Der}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X})_{k=0}^{\infty}
is our ultimate product, expressing the weak homotopy type of
\mathfrak{X}
in terms of graded vector spaces. The sequence
(\operatorname{Hyb}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X})_{k=0}^{\infty}
is an auxiliary
construction, which allows us to pass from
\operatorname{Der}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}
to
\operatorname{Der}^{(k+1)}\mathfrak{X}
.

This section is devoted to a single step in this inductive process, starting from

\operatorname{Hyb}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}
and passing to
\operatorname{Hyb}^{(k+1)}\mathfrak{X}
. Thus we assume given a
k
-formal diagram
\mathfrak{Y}:\mathscr{J}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}_{+}
(of the form
\mathfrak{Y}=\operatorname{Hyb}^{(k)}\mathfrak{X}
, though this is not
used anywhere). Because the model category of (unbounded) chain complexes is stable,
the value of
k
does not matter, so for simplicity we may take
k=0
. The indexing category
\mathscr{J}
, which we think of as
\mathscr{I}^{(k)}
, is also partially ordered (although it is more complicated than the original
\mathscr{I}
).

.

Assume given

We now apply our methods to describe the complete set of higher order homotopy invariants (in graded vector spaces) for the weak homotopy type of two simple but important infinite sequences of diagrams in chain complexes.

4.1
.

Cubes.

Let
\mathscr{I}^{n}
denote the lattice of subsets of
[n]=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
, which we shall think of an
n
-dimensional cube with vertices labelled by

J=(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\cdots,\epsilon_{n})
with each
\epsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\}

(so
J
is the characteristic function of a subset of
[n]
).
We denote by
J_{A}
the vertex associated to
A\subseteq[n]
, and we have a unique map
J_{B}\to J_{A}
in
\mathscr{I}^{n}
exactly when
A\subseteq B
(the reason for reversing the direction will become apparent in §
5.8
below).

For a fixed
\mathbb{F}
-vector space
V
define the diagrams
\mathfrak{D}^{n}_{V}:\mathscr{I}^{n}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
by

\mathfrak{D}^{n}_{V}(J_{A})=\begin{cases}K(V,0)&\text{if}\ A=[n]\\
K(V,n-1)&\text{if}\ A=\emptyset\\
C^{n-|A|}K(V,0)&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
C^{k}
is the appropriate cone (see §
0.4
) and
|A|
denotes the cardinality of
A
.
The maps are determined by requiring that the diagram

(\mathfrak{D}^{n}_{V})\lvert_{\mathscr{I}^{n}\setminus\vec{0}}
(omitting the last vertex
\vec{0}:=(0,\cdots,0)
) be minimally cofibrant.
Note that the homotopy colimit for this sub-diagram is weakly equivalent to
K(V,n-1)
, and the weak homotopy type of
\mathfrak{D}^{n}_{V}
is determined by requiring that the structure map

\psi\colon\operatornamewithlimits{hocolim}_{\mathscr{I}^{n}\setminus\vec{0}}\ \mathfrak{D}^{n}_{V}~\to~\mathfrak{D}^{n}_{V}(\vec{0})

be a weak equivalence.
For
A\subseteq[n]
, we denote the kernel of the fold map by

Z(A):=\ker(\bigoplus_{i\in A}V_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\to}}V)
, where
V_{i}
is a copy of
V
.

4.8

The third order differential.

Note that even though the values of the three differentials in (
4.7

not
the same as those shown in (
4.6
), which landed in three different targets. Because we have a common target
\beta=\vec{0}

these three values are coordinated, because the homotopy colimit of the left half of (
4.7

two
copies of
\Sigma V
(see §
2.6
above), say

\Sigma V_{I}\amalg\Sigma V_{II}
.
Thus in order obtain the derived diagram
\mathfrak{Y}^{\prime}=\operatorname{Der}^{(2)}\mathfrak{D}^{3}_{V}
from (
4.7
) we need to
determine the two values
\hat{\psi}_{I}
and
\hat{\psi}_{II}
from
\Sigma V
to
H_{1}(\mathfrak{Y}(\beta))=0
, which are of course zero.

Thus we are in the graded situation of §
0.1
(but with maps of positive degree). Therefore,
(the non-primary part of)
\mathfrak{Y}^{\prime}
consists of a single
differential
d^{2}_{(1,2,3)}:V\to H_{2}(\mathfrak{D}^{3}_{V}(\beta))=V
(of degree two). In our case, this is just the isomorphism in the lower right corner of (
4.6
), by construction.

Note that this isomorphism represents the value of a third order operation – in fact, of a
d^{3}
-differential, in the case of a spectral sequence (see §
5.8
below). It is one of the advantages of our approach that all such higher order information is reduced to secondary structure (in a more complicated diagram), and from there to a description in graded vector spaces alone.

5.
Spectral sequences

As our terminology suggests, the approach described here may be helpful in understanding spectral sequences of chain complexes.
For simplicity, we concentrate mainly on the simplest case: that of a double complex (see

5.1

Double complexes and cubical diagrams.

A double complex

6.
Further directions

So far we have discussed a very specific type of diagrams - those indexed by finite partially ordered sets. However, this case is the most basic example, and should offer a useful guidepost for more general cases.

6.1
.

Parallel arrows.

If we assume that the indexing category
\mathscr{I}
is still directed (and finite), but allows parallel arrows, the diagrams become more complicated in terms of the linear algebra (or representation theory) needed to understand the primary information (that is, the diagram
H_{\ast}\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}\to\operatorname{gr}\mbox{\sf Vect}
).

However, the changes needed to deal with the
higher order
information, which are our main concern here, are minor. This is because if
\mathfrak{X}
is cofibrant, then given two (or more) arrows
f,g:\alpha\to\beta
in
\mathscr{I}
, on
V:=\operatorname{Ker}(f_{\ast})\cap\operatorname{Ker}(g_{\ast})\subseteq H_{k}\mathfrak{X}(\alpha)
, say, we will have disjoint copies of the cone
CV
in
\mathfrak{X}(\beta)
.
Thus the (homotopy) colimit of the diagram restricted to
V
will be the pushout
\Sigma V
,
as in §
2.1
. In effect, we may replace the indexing category
\mathscr{I}
by an expanded version

\mathscr{I}^{\prime}
in which, in addition to
\beta
(serving as a target of the non-zero part of the maps

f_{\ast}
and
g_{\ast}
), we have two new copies
\beta_{f}
and
\beta_{g}
,
at which we place the respective cones
CV
to obtain
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}:\mathscr{I}^{\prime}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
(as in the construction of the expanded derived diagram in §
3.10
). Note that the collapsing functor
c:\mathscr{I}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{I}
allows us to map
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
to
c^{\ast}\mathfrak{X}
, and thus compare the two.

6.2
.

Infinite diagrams.

Infinite directed diagrams may in principle be treated as the colimits of their finite segments. However, the case when
\mathscr{I}
is not directed – even in the simple case of a single non-trivial self map – can be much more complicated. One may try to approximate a diagram
\mathfrak{X}:\mathscr{I}\to\mbox{\sf Ch}
by finite segments – e.g., consider only a finite number of iterations of a self map, thought of as a diagram indexed by a finite linearly ordered set. However, as the case of homotopy actions of the cyclic group
C_{2}
shows, these finite approximations may not provide the full picture.

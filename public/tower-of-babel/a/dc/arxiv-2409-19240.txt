Exact solution to a class of problems for the Burgers’ equation on bounded intervals
Kwassi Anani, Mensah Folly-Gbetoula

Abstract
Burgers’ equation with fixed Dirichlet boundary conditions is considered on generic bounded intervals. By using the Hopf-Cole transformation and the exact operational solution recently established for linear reaction-diffusion equations with constant coefficients, an exact solution in the time domain is implicitly derived by means of inverse Laplace transforms. Analytic inverses, whenever they exist, can be obtained in closed form using Mellin transforms. However, highly efficient algorithms are available, and numerical inverses in the time domain are always possible, regardless of the complexity of the Laplace domain expressions. Two illustration tests show that the results coincide well with those of classical exact solutions. Compared to the solutions obtained with series expressions or by numerical methods, closed-form expressions, even in the Laplace domain, represent an innovative alternative and new perspectives can be envisaged. The exact solution via the inverse Laplace transform is shown to be more computationally efficient and thus provides a reference point for numerical and semi-analytical methods.

1
Introduction

The numerical simulation of linear and nonlinear convective and diffusive partial differential equations is of significant importance, particularly in the context of the Burgers’ equation (see e.g. Başhan 2022,
[
1
]
). This equation is one of the most straightforward nonlinear models derived from the equations of fluid dynamics, since it can be considered as a simplified form of the Navier-Stokes equations. The study of these equations is of significant importance in a number of fields, including applied mathematics, physics, and engineering. This is due to the fact that they provide a highly precise modeling tool for a number of transport mechanisms and wave phenomena. For a review of the applications of Burgers’ equation in mathematical physics, see Bonkile et al.
[
2
]
.

First historical solutions in terms of exact ratio of infinite series were obtained by the Hopf-Cole transformation
[
3
,
4
]
. A survey of exact solutions of the one-dimensional Burgers’ equation with different boundary and initial conditions has been done by Benton and Platzman in
[
5
]
. During the last decades, numerical methods are the most used for studying Burgers’ equations. One may cite a number of different numerical methods, including finite difference, finite element, spectral element, spline approximations, modified cubic B-splines collocation, hybrid numerical scheme, and so forth. For further examples, see Dhawan et al.
[
6
]
.
Additionally, semi-analytical methods have been employed in the context of Adomian decomposition, as evidenced by Naghipour and Manafian
[
7
]
, and in the approach of Zeidan et al.
[
8
]
. Furthermore, homotopy perturbation techniques have been utilized by Lal and Yadav
[
9
]
, while variational iterations have been applied by Biazar and Aminikhah
[
10
]
.

On the one hand, numerical methods are constantly improving to better adapt to variations in the problem parameters. On the other hand, semi-analytical methods do not always result in a closed form of the solution, and can thus consume a considerable amount of resources and CPU time. In the present paper, an exact solution is calculated in the time domain through the inverse Laplace transform operator. In the case where exact analytic inverses into the time domain are not available, computationally efficient algorithms exist and inverse Laplace transforms can be accomplished numerically. The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the problem and presents its solution by using the Hopf-Cole transformation, and a former operational solution obtained in the closed form for the reaction-diffusion equation with constant coefficients on finite intervals. Comparisons of the present solutions with classical exact and numerical results are performed in Section
3
. Concluding remarks and perspectives are given in the conclusion.

2
Exact implicit solution

The viscid Burgers’ equation is considered on a generic bounded interval of the real line

w_{t}(x,t)-a^{2}w_{xx}(x,t)+ww_{x}=0\quad l_{1}<x<l_{2},\quad 0<t\leq T,

(1)

with a non-homogeneous initial condition

w(x,0)=w_{0}(x),\quad l_{1}\leq x\leq l_{2},

(2)

and the following Dirichlet boundary conditions for
0\leq t\leq T
,

w(l_{1},t)=\alpha_{1},

(3)

w(l_{2},t)=\alpha_{2},

(4)

where
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are two arbitrary real numbers.
By applying the classical Hopf-Cole transformation

w(x,t)=-2a^{2}\frac{u_{x}(x,t)}{u(x,t)},

(5)

the system (
1
)-(
4
) is transformed unto a boundary value problem for the one-dimensional heat diffusion equation:

u_{t}(x,t)-a^{2}u_{xx}(x,t)=0,\quad l_{1}<x<l_{2},\quad 0<t\leq T,

(6)

subject to the initial condition

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u(l_{1},0)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2a^{2}}\int_{l_{1}}^{x}w_{0}(y)dy\right)=\varphi(x),\quad l_{1}\leq x\leq l_{2},

(7)

and to the homogeneous Robin boundary conditions for
0\leq t\leq T

\alpha_{1}u(l_{1},t)+2a^{2}u_{x}(l_{1},t)=0,

(8)

\alpha_{2}u(l_{2},t)+2a^{2}u_{x}(l_{2},t)=0.

(9)

Equation (
6
) is subject to the initial condition (
7
) (where
u(l_{1},0)
can be assumed not null without lost of generality), and to homogeneous Robin boundary conditions (
8
) and (
9
). Now, the system (
6
)-(
9
) is well defined with a boundary value problem for a linear reaction-diffusion equation with constant coefficients. The exact solution has been provided in the Laplace domain for such problems in Anani
[
11
]
.

We briefly recall that, for a nonhomogeneous linear reaction-diffusion equation with constant coefficients:

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-a^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+bu=f(x,t),\quad l_{1}<x<l_{2},\quad 0<t\leq T,

(10)

subject to the initial condition

u(x,0)=\varphi(x),\quad l_{1}\leq x\leq l_{2},

(11)

and to the boundary conditions for
0\leq t\leq T
:

\alpha_{1}u(l_{1},t)+\beta_{1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}(l_{1},t)=g_{1}(t),\quad\alpha_{1}^{2}+\beta_{1}^{2}\neq 0,

(12)

\alpha_{2}u(l_{2},t)+\beta_{2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}(l_{2},t)=g_{2}(t),\quad\alpha_{2}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}\neq 0,

(13)

its analog in the Laplace domain can be written in the form of a two-point boundary value problem as follows:

-a^{2}\frac{d^{2}U}{dx^{2}}(x,p)+(b+p)U(x,p)=F(x,p)+\varphi(x),

(14)

with the boundary conditions:

\alpha_{1}U(l_{1},p)+\beta_{1}\frac{dU}{dx}(l_{1},p)=G_{1}(p),\quad\alpha_{1}^{2}+\beta_{1}^{2}\neq 0,

(15)

\alpha_{2}U(l_{2},p)+\beta_{2}\frac{dU}{dx}(l_{2},p)=G_{2}(p),\quad\alpha_{2}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}\neq 0.

(16)

In equations (
14
)-(
16
),
p
is the Laplace domain variable for the time
t
,
U(x,p)
denotes the Laplace transform of
u(x,t)
, and
U_{x}
stands for the derivative
\frac{dU}{dx}
of
U(x,p)
. In equations (
10
)-(
13
), the source term
f(x,t)
and the function
\varphi(x)
verify Dirichlet conditions and are integrable with respect to
x
. Additionally, all time-dependent functions are presumed to be of exponential order in relation to
t
, as well as their temporal derivatives where they are involved. For a detailed examination of the requisite conditions for a function to have a Laplace transform, please refer to the following sources: Herron and Foster
[
12
]
or Debnath and Bhatta
[
13
]
. Thus,
F(x,p)
,
G_{1}(p)
and
G_{2}(p)
are respectively the Laplace transform of the source term
f(x,t)
, and the boundary-related functions
g_{1}(t)
and
g_{2}(t)
. Under these hypotheses, the uniqueness of the solution
u
has been proven. And, the exact operational solution of the system (
10
)-(
13
), or equivalently, the exact solution of the analog problem (
14
)-(
16
) is:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle U(x,p)=\frac{1}{2\,\sqrt{b+p}}\,{\exp\left({-{\frac{\left(l_{{2}}-x\right)\sqrt{b+p}}{a}}}\right)}\left[{U(l_{2},p)}\sqrt{b+p}+a{U_{x}(l_{2},p)}\right]\\
\displaystyle+{\frac{1}{2\,\sqrt{b+p}}{\exp\left({-{\frac{\left(x-l_{{1}}\right)\sqrt{b+p}}{a}}}\right)}\left[{U(l_{1},p)}\sqrt{b+p}-a{U_{x}(l_{1},p)}\right]}+R\left(x,p\right).\end{array}

(17)

In equation (
17
), the four
p
-domain coefficients
U(l_{1},p)
,
U_{x}(l_{1},p)
,
U(l_{2},p)
and
U_{x}(l_{2},p)
are determined by the following linear system of four equations:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\displaystyle U(l_{1},p)\\
\displaystyle U_{x}(l_{1},p)\\
\displaystyle U(l_{2},p)\\
\displaystyle U_{x}(l_{2},p)\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\displaystyle\alpha_{1}&\displaystyle\beta_{1}&\displaystyle 0&\displaystyle 0\\
\displaystyle 0&0&\displaystyle\alpha_{2}&\displaystyle\beta_{2}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{a}{2\sqrt{b+p}}&\displaystyle\frac{-\chi(l_{2}-l_{1},p)}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{-a\chi(l_{2}-l_{1},p)}{2\sqrt{b+p}}\\
\displaystyle\frac{-\chi(l_{2}-l_{1},p)}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{a\chi(l_{2}-l_{1},p)}{2\sqrt{b+p}}&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{-a}{2\sqrt{b+p}}\end{array}\right]\\
\times\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\displaystyle G_{1}(p)\\
\displaystyle G_{2}(p)\\
\displaystyle R(l_{1},p)\\
\displaystyle R(l_{2},p)\end{array}\right];\end{array}

where
\chi(x,p)=\exp\left(\frac{-x\sqrt{b+p}}{a}\right)
, and
R(x,p)
is the Laplace transform of the function

\begin{array}[]{ll}r(x,t)=\displaystyle\frac{\exp(-bt)}{2a\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{l_{1}}^{l_{2}}\varphi(\xi)\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(\xi-x)^{2}}{4a^{2}t}\right)\right]d\xi\\
+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2a\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{t}d\theta\int_{l_{1}}^{l_{2}}\frac{\exp(-b(t-\theta))}{\sqrt{(t-\theta)}}\exp\left(-\frac{(\xi-x)^{2}}{4a^{2}(t-\theta)}\right)f(\xi,\theta)d\xi.\end{array}

(18)

Returning now to the problem (
6
)-(
9
), the unique and exact operational solution of the system (
6
)-(
9
) for the null reaction term (
b=0
), can be reported as:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle U(x,p)=\frac{1}{2\,\sqrt{p}}\,{\exp\left({{\frac{\left(-l_{{2}}+x\right)\sqrt{p}}{a}}}\right)}\left[{U(l_{2},p)}\sqrt{p}+a{U_{x}(l_{2},p)}\right]\\
\displaystyle+{\frac{1}{2\,\sqrt{p}}{\exp\left({-{\frac{\left(x-l_{{1}}\right)\sqrt{p}}{a}}}\right)}\left[{U(l_{1},p)}\sqrt{p}-a{U_{x}(l_{1},p)}\right]}+R\left(x,p\right),\end{array}

(19)

As the source term is also null in equation (
6
),
R(x,p)
reduces to the Laplace transform of

r(x,t)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2a\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{l_{1}}^{l_{2}}\varphi(\xi)\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(\xi-x)^{2}}{4a^{2}t}\right)\right]d\xi,

(20)

namely,

R(x,p)=\mathcal{L}\{r(x,t)\}=\int_{l_{{1}}}^{l_{{2}}}\!{\frac{\varphi\left(\xi\right)}{2\,a\sqrt{p}}}\,\exp\left({-{\frac{\left|\xi-x\right|\sqrt{p}}{a}}}\right)\,{\rm d}\xi,

(21)

with
\mathcal{L}
being the Laplace transform operator. In the present case, it can be written:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\displaystyle U(l_{1},p)\\
\displaystyle U_{x}(l_{1},p)\\
\displaystyle U(l_{2},p)\\
\displaystyle U_{x}(l_{2},p)\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\displaystyle\alpha_{1}&\displaystyle 2a^{2}&\displaystyle 0&\displaystyle 0\\
\displaystyle 0&0&\displaystyle\alpha_{2}&\displaystyle 2a^{2}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{a}{2\sqrt{p}}&\displaystyle\frac{-\chi(l_{2}-l_{1},p)}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{-a\chi(l_{2}-l_{1},p)}{2\sqrt{p}}\\
\displaystyle\frac{-\chi(l_{2}-l_{1},p)}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{a\chi(l_{2}-l_{1},p)}{2\sqrt{p}}&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{-a}{2\sqrt{p}}\end{array}\right]\\
\times\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\displaystyle 0\\
\displaystyle 0\\
\displaystyle R(l_{1},p)\\
\displaystyle R(l_{2},p)\end{array}\right].\end{array}

(22)

The solutions of system (
22
) can be explicitly calculated, and it can be verified that:

\displaystyle\frac{U_{x}(l_{1},p)}{U(l_{1},p)}=-{\frac{{\alpha}_{{1}}}{2{a}^{2}}},

(23)

and

\displaystyle\frac{U_{x}(l_{2},p)}{U(l_{1},p)}=-{\frac{{\alpha}_{{2}}}{2{a}^{2}}},

(24)

showing that the boundary conditions of the Laplace analog for the time domain problem (

)-(

) are satisfied, since

and equations (
15
)-(
16
) reduce to:

\alpha_{1}U(l_{1},p)+2{a}^{2}U_{x}(l_{1},p)=0,

(25)

\alpha_{2}U(l_{2},p)+2{a}^{2}U_{x}(l_{2},p)=0.

(26)

Then, by using the inverse Laplace operator
\mathcal{L}^{-1}
, an exact solution of the problem (
1
)-(
4
) can be derived implicitly from equation (
5
) as:

\displaystyle w(x,t)=-2{a}^{2}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{U_{x}(x,p)\}}{\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{U(x,p)\}},

(27)

where

is given by the formula (
19
) together with the Laplace domain expression (
21
) of

, and the four solutions of the system (

). Also, the derivative:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle U_{x}(x,p)=\frac{1}{2\,a}\,{\exp\left({{\frac{\left(-l_{{2}}+x\right)\sqrt{p}}{a}}}\right)}\left[{U(l_{2},p)}\sqrt{p}+a{U_{x}(l_{2},p)}\right]\\

depends on the derivative

of

. In accordance with the general hypotheses that lead to the unified solution (

), the formulae (

. Therefore, the relation (

). For exact solutions containing infinite series expressions, solution (

Numerical algorithms for inverse Laplace transforms have been developed that are powerful tools to extend the applicability of the Laplace transform technique. An algorithm that has been demonstrated to be particularly efficient is described by de Hoog et al.
[

]
and has been implemented in Matlab as the function invlap.m by Hollenbeck in 1998. This allows to graphically illustrate the exact implicit solution (

Conclusion

This study has enabled the precise calculation of an exact solution from the Laplace domain to the time domain for the viscous Burgers’ equation on bounded intervals. Even in instances where an explicit analytical inverse is unavailable, the implicit closed-form solution has been demonstrated to be more efficient than classical numerical and semi-analytical methods, due to the availability of highly efficient algorithms for numerical inverse Laplace transforms. The Burgers’ equation appears in various fields of applied mathematics and its exact solution in time or Laplace domains, may serve as reference for numerical and semi-analytical methods. Further investigation is required into more general initial-boundary-value problems for the Burgers’ equation in one or higher dimensions.

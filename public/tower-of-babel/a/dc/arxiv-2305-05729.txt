A discrete three-dimensional divdiv complex on polyhedral meshes with application to a mixed formulation of the biharmonic problem
Daniele A. Di Pietro, Marien-Lorenzo Hanot

Abstract
In this work, following the Discrete de Rham (DDR) paradigm, we develop an arbitrary-order discrete divdiv complex on general polyhedral meshes. The construction rests 1) on discrete spaces that are spanned by vectors of polynomials whose components are attached to mesh entities and 2) on discrete operators obtained mimicking integration by parts formulas. We provide an in-depth study of the algebraic properties of the local complex, showing that it is exact on mesh elements with trivial topology. The new DDR complex is used to design a numerical scheme for the approximation of biharmonic problems, for which we provide detailed stability and convergence analyses. Numerical experiments complete the theoretical results. Key words. divdiv complex, discrete de Rham method, polyhedral meshes, biharmonic problems, mixed formulations MSC2020. 65N30, 65N99, 31B30

Polytopal methods additionally benefit from a higher-level point of view, which enables unknowns-reduction strategies such as serendipity

The key idea of DDR methods consists in replacing both the spaces and the operators in the complex with discrete counterparts.
Discrete spaces are spanned by vectors of polynomials with components attached to the mesh entities, while discrete operators are obtained mimicking integration by parts formula.
Applying this paradigm to the discretization of the divdiv complex involves a number of subtleties, from the decomposition of traces of tensor-valued fields to the identification of the appropriate integration by parts formulas.
We provide a complete study of the algebraic properties of the local complex showing how the design of the spaces and operators fits to ensure exactness on mesh elements with trivial topology.
Local exactness is one of the key ingredients to prove algebraic properties of the global complex following, e.g., the paradigm of
[

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\boldsymbol{H}(\DIV\VDIV,\Omega;\mathbb{S}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\DIV\VDIV\boldsymbol{\sigma}~v

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fv

\displaystyle\forall v\in L^{2}(\Omega).

2.7
Integration by parts formulas

A key element for the DDR-inspired construction are the integration by parts formulas collected in this section, which are used both to identify the components of the discrete spaces and to reconstruct the discrete differentials and the corresponding potentials.

2.7.1
Integration by parts formulas for
\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3})

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and let
\boldsymbol{v}:T:\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a vector-valued function, which we assume as smooth as needed in what follows.
For all
\boldsymbol{\sigma}:T\to\mathbb{T}
smooth enough, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{T}\DEV\GRAD{\boldsymbol{v}}:\boldsymbol{\sigma}

\displaystyle=-\int_{T}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\VDIV\boldsymbol{\sigma}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\int_{F}\boldsymbol{v}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}

(2.13)

\displaystyle=-\int_{T}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\VDIV\boldsymbol{\sigma}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\int_{F}\left({\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{\boldsymbol{n},F}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}+{\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{\boldsymbol{t},F}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\right),

where we have used the decomposition
\boldsymbol{v}={\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{\boldsymbol{n},F}+{\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{\boldsymbol{t},F}
of the trace of
\boldsymbol{v}
on
F
into its normal and tangential components to pass to the second line.

Let now
F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}
.
For all
\boldsymbol{w}:F\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
smooth enough and valued in the tangent space of
F
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{F}{(\DEV\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{t},F}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}

\displaystyle=\int_{F}{(\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{t},F}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}=\int_{F}\GRAD_{F}(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F})\cdot\boldsymbol{w}

(2.14)

\displaystyle=-\int_{F}(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F})\DIV_{F}\boldsymbol{w}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F})(\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}),

where, we have used the fact that the components extracted by
{(\DEV\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{t},F}
are not affected by the
\DEV
operator in the first equality
and a standard integration by parts on

to conclude.
For all
\boldsymbol{\sigma}:F\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
smooth enough and matrix-valued in the tangent space of
F
, on the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\int_{F}{(\DEV\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F}\tdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\int_{F}\GRAD_{F}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\boldsymbol{t},F}\tdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{F}\tr(\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})\boldsymbol{I}_{2}\tdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}

(2.15)

\displaystyle=-\int_{F}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\boldsymbol{t},F}\cdot\VDIV_{F}\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{F}\DIV\boldsymbol{v}~\tr\boldsymbol{\sigma}

\displaystyle+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}\left[(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\boldsymbol{t}_{E}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}+(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}\right],

where, in the second equality, we have used an integration by parts for the first term and decomposed the tangent trace of
\boldsymbol{v}
on
F

as
{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\boldsymbol{t},F}=(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\boldsymbol{t}_{E}+(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}

after noticing that
(\boldsymbol{t}_{E},\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})
forms an orthonormal basis of the plane orthogonal to
E
at each point of
E
.

2.7.2
Integration by parts formulas for
\boldsymbol{H}(\SYM\CURL,T;\mathbb{T})

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
For all
\boldsymbol{\tau}:T:\to\mathbb{T}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}:T\to\mathbb{S}
smooth enough, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{T}\SYM\CURL{\boldsymbol{\tau}}:\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\int_{T}\CURL{\boldsymbol{\tau}}:\boldsymbol{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\int_{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}:\CURL\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\int_{F}(\boldsymbol{\tau}\times\boldsymbol{n}_{F}):\boldsymbol{\sigma}

(2.16)

\displaystyle=\int_{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}:\CURL\boldsymbol{\sigma}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\int_{F}\boldsymbol{\tau}:(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\times\boldsymbol{n}_{F}).

Let now
F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}
.
We have, for
r:F\to\mathbb{R}
smooth enough,

\int_{F}{(\SYM\CURL\boldsymbol{\tau})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}\,r=\int_{F}\ROTF{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{t},F}\,r=\int_{F}{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{t},F}\cdot\CURL_{F}r-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\,r,

(2.17)

where we have used the fact that the component extracted by
{(\SYM\CURL\boldsymbol{\tau})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}
is on the diagonal, hence it is not affected by the
\SYM
operator
(so that, in particular,
{(\SYM\CURL\boldsymbol{\tau})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}={(\CURL\boldsymbol{\tau})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}=\ROTF{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{t},F}
).

For the tangential-tangential component of
\boldsymbol{\tau}
, standard integration by parts formulas on faces (corresponding, respectively, to
[
25
, Eqs. (3.12) and (3.15)]
) give:

\displaystyle\int_{F}\DIV_{F}\VROTF{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F}r

\displaystyle=-\int_{F}\VROTF{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F}\cdot\GRAD_{F}r+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}(\VROTF{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})r

(2.18)

\displaystyle=-\int_{F}{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F}\tdot\CURL_{F}\GRAD_{F}r+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}({\tau}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F}\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\cdot\GRAD_{F}r

\displaystyle+\int_{E}(\VROTF{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})\,r.

For all
E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}
, it holds:

\displaystyle\int_{E}({\tau}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F}\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\cdot\GRAD_{F}r

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\left[(\boldsymbol{t}_{E}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{t}_{E}}r+(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}}r\right]

(2.19)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\left[-\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}+\boldsymbol{n}_{F}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\right)\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{t}_{E}}r+(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}}r\right],

where we have
written
\GRAD_{F}r=\partial_{\boldsymbol{t}_{E}}r\,\boldsymbol{t}_{E}+\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}}r\,\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}
in the first equality and
used the fact that
\boldsymbol{\tau}
is traceless on the last line (so that, the trace being an invariant and
(\boldsymbol{t}_{E},\boldsymbol{n}_{FE},\boldsymbol{n}_{F})
an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\boldsymbol{t}_{E}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{t}_{E}+\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}+\boldsymbol{n}_{F}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}=0
).
Moreover, we have

\int_{E}(\VROTF{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})\,r=\int_{E}\ROTF(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F})\,r=\int_{E}\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{t}_{E}}(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})\,r-\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}}(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\,r\right]\\
=-\int_{E}(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{t}_{E}}r+\llbracket(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})\,r\rrbracket_{E}-\int_{E}\left[\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\GRAD(\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}\right]r,

(2.20)

where we have used an integration by parts on the first term to conclude.
Plugging (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) into (
2.18
) finally gives

\displaystyle\int_{F}\DIV_{F}\VROTF{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F}r=-\int_{F}{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{t}\boldsymbol{t},F}\tdot\CURL_{F}\GRAD_{F}r+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}}r

(2.21)

\displaystyle-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}\left(2\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}+\boldsymbol{n}_{F}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\right)\partial_{\boldsymbol{t}_{E}}r-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}\left[\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\GRAD(\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}\right]r

\displaystyle+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\llbracket(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})\,r\rrbracket_{E}.

Lemma 5

(Local commutation property)
.

The following commutation property holds

\underline{\boldsymbol{DG}}_{T}^{k}(\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DEV\GRAD,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{v})=\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\SYM\CURL,T}^{k}(\DEV\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})\hskip 22.99988pt\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{3}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

(3.7)

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{3}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Let us check the relation on the normal-normal component on edges.
By (
2.9
) with
\ell=k
,
we have
\mathfrak{G}_{E}^{k}(\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_{V_{1}})\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{E},\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_{V_{2}})\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{E},\pi_{\mathcal{P},E}^{k-1}(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{E}))=\pi_{\mathcal{P},E}^{k}(\partial_{\boldsymbol{t}_{E}}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{E})
.
Accounting for the previous relation
we have, for all
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(E;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
,

\int_{E}\left[\cancel{\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},E}^{k}}{(\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},E}-\frac{1}{3}\left(\tr\cancel{\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},E}^{k}}{(\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},E}+\cancel{\pi_{\mathcal{P},E}^{k}}(\partial_{\boldsymbol{t}_{E}}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\right)\boldsymbol{I}_{2}\right]\tdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\\
=\int_{E}{(\DEV\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},E}\tdot\boldsymbol{\sigma},

(3.8)

where the cancellation of the projectors is made possible by their definition.
Using again (
2.9
), this time with
\ell=k+1
, we infer that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{E}^{k+1}({\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_{V_{1}})}_{\boldsymbol{n},E},{\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_{V_{2}})}_{\boldsymbol{n},E},\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},E}^{k}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{\boldsymbol{n},E}))={}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},E}^{k+1}{(\DEV\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{t},E},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{E}^{k+1}({(\GRAD\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_{V_{1}}))}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},E},{(\GRAD\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_{V_{2}}))}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},E},\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},E}^{k}{(\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},E})={}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},E}^{k+1}{(\GRAD((\DEV\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})\boldsymbol{t}_{E}))}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},E}.

(3.10)

Combining (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we obtain

\underline{\boldsymbol{DG}}_{E}^{k}(\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DEV\GRAD,E}^{k}\boldsymbol{v})=\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\SYM\CURL,E}^{k}(\DEV\GRAD\boldsymbol{v})
.
The commutation for the components of
\underline{\boldsymbol{DG}}_{T}^{k}
on faces and on the element are proved in a similar fashion:
first by removing the projections in the definition (
3.3
) (respectively, (
3.4
) and (
3.5
)),
and then concluding with the integration by parts formula (
2.14
) (respectively, (
2.15
) and (
2.13
)).
∎

3.2.2
Discrete symcurl operator

The discrete symmetric curl operator on edges
\boldsymbol{SC}_{E}^{k+1}:\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\SYM\CURL,E}^{k}\to\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k+1}(E;\SYM\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
is defined,
for all
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{E}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\SYM\CURL,E}^{k}
, by:

\boldsymbol{SC}_{E}^{k+1}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{E}\coloneq\mathbb{C}\left(\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{\tau},E}-\mathfrak{G}_{E}^{k+1}({(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{V_{1}})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},E},{(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{V_{2}})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},E},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{E})\right),

(3.11)

where
\mathbb{C}
is the fourth-order tensor such that

\mathbb{C}\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{11}&\eta_{12}\\
\eta_{21}&\eta_{22}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{12}&\frac{-\eta_{11}+\eta_{22}}{2}\\
\frac{-\eta_{11}+\eta_{22}}{2}&-\eta_{21}\end{pmatrix}\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}.

(3.12)

There are two components for the symmetric curl operator on faces.

and all

(3.13)

while the second one,
{SC}_{D,F}^{k+1}:\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\SYM\CURL,F}^{k}\to\mathcal{P}^{k+1}(F)

For all
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{F}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\SYM\CURL,F}^{k}
and all

\displaystyle\int_{F}{SC}_{D,F}^{k+1}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{F}\,r

\displaystyle=-\int_{F}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}},F}\tdot\CURL_{F}\GRAD_{F}r+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\boldsymbol{t},E}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}}r

(3.14)

\displaystyle-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}\left(2\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{E}\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}+\boldsymbol{n}_{F}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{E}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\right)\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{t}_{E}}r-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{\tau},E}\,\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})\,r

\displaystyle+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\llbracket(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\,\boldsymbol{\tau}_{V}\,\boldsymbol{n}_{FE})\,r\rrbracket_{E}.

Recalling (
2.16
), the discrete symmetric curl operator on an element

(3.15)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\int_{F}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}},F}\cdot{(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\times\boldsymbol{n}_{F})}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{t},F}.

Finally, the discrete symmetric curl operator

It can be checked that the following commutation property with the interpolators holds:

(3.17)

4
A mixed method for biharmonic problems

In this section we consider the application of the spaces at the tail of the above complex to the mixed discretization of the biharmonic problem (
1.2
).
Throughout this section,
k\geq 0
is an integer corresponding to the polynomial degree of the scheme.

4.1
Local component product

We furnish
\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}
with the component inner product such that, for all
(\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T})\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\times\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}
,

[\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}]_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\coloneq\int_{T}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}},T}:\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}},T}+h_{T}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\int_{F}\left(\upsilon_{F}~\tau_{F}+h_{T}^{2}D_{\boldsymbol{\upsilon},F}~D_{\boldsymbol{\tau},F}\right)+h_{T}^{2}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\int_{E}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{E}:\boldsymbol{\tau}_{E}.

(4.1)

and we introduce the corresponding component norm such that

|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\coloneq[\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\forall\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}.

(4.2)

Using the boundedness of
L^{2}
-orthogonal projectors along with continuous trace inequalities on the faces and edges of
T
, it can be proved, similarly to
[
21
, Lemma 6]
, that

|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\tau}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\tau}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}+h_{T}|\boldsymbol{\tau}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}+h_{T}^{2}|\boldsymbol{\tau}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{2}(T;\mathbb{S}).

(4.3)

Moreover, for all
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}
, taking
v=DD_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}
in (
3.18
) and using Cauchy–Schwarz, discrete inverse and trace inequalities along with the definition (
4.2
) of
|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|{\cdot}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}
, we get the following boundedness property:

\|DD_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim h_{T}^{-2}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\qquad\forall\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}.

(4.4)

4.2
Discrete symmetric matrix potential

In order to reconstruct a symmetric matrix potential, we first need to reconstruct face traces of degree
k
.
To this purpose, for each
F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}
, we can apply the principles of the HHO potential reconstruction (see, e.g.,
[
23
, Section 5.1.3]
) to devise, from the vector of polynomials
(\upsilon_{F},(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{E}\boldsymbol{n}_{F})_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}})\in\mathcal{P}^{k-1}(F)\times\left(\bigtimes_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\mathcal{P}^{k}(E)\right)
, a function in
\mathcal{P}^{k}(F)
that can be interpreted as the normal-normal trace of a symmetric matrix-valued field on
T
(in passing, with these values one could actually compute a normal-normal trace in
\mathcal{P}^{k+1}(F)
, but this will not be needed in what follows).
The corresponding reconstruction operator
\gamma_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}^{k}:\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,F}^{k}\to\mathcal{P}^{k}(F)
is, by construction, polynomially consistent:
For all
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(T;\mathbb{S})
,
\gamma_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,F}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{|F}=\boldsymbol{n}_{F}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{|F}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}
.
Moreover, the following boundedness property holds:

\|\gamma_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{F}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim h_{F}^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\qquad\forall\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}.

(4.5)

The symmetric matrix potential
\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}:\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\to\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(T;\mathbb{S})
is then defined, mimicking (
2.22
), such that, for all
\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}
:
For all
(v,\boldsymbol{\tau})\in\mathcal{P}^{k+2}(T)\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{{\rm c},k}(T)
,

\int_{T}\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}:(\HESS v+\boldsymbol{\tau})=\int_{T}DD_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}~v+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{E}\boldsymbol{n}_{F})~v\\
+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\int_{F}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{F}~\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}_{F}}v-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\int_{F}D_{\boldsymbol{\upsilon},F}~v+\int_{T}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}},T}:\boldsymbol{\tau}.

(4.6)

By construction, the following polynomial consistency property holds:

\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}=\boldsymbol{\upsilon}\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\upsilon}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(T;\mathbb{S}).

(4.7)

Additionally, taking in (
4.6
)
(v,\boldsymbol{\tau})
such that
\HESS v+\boldsymbol{\tau}=\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}

(this is possible by virtue of the direct decomposition (
2.3
)),
using Cauchy–Schwarz, discrete trace, and inverse inequalities,
and invoking the boundedness (
4.4
) of
DD_{T}^{k}
and (
4.5
) of
\gamma_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}^{k}
, it is inferred:

\|\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\lesssim|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\qquad\forall\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}.

(4.8)

Remark 8

(Polynomially consistent symmetric curl)
.

For all
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k+1}(T;\mathbb{T})
, noticing that
{\rm sym}\CURL\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(T;\mathbb{S})
, it holds

\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}(\underline{\boldsymbol{SC}}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\SYM\CURL,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\tau})\overset{\eqref{eq:SCT.commutativity}}{=}\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}({\rm sym}\CURL\boldsymbol{\tau})\overset{\eqref{eq:TP:polynomial.consistency}}{=}{\rm sym}\CURL\boldsymbol{\tau},

showing that
\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\circ\underline{\boldsymbol{SC}}_{T}^{k}
provides a polynomially consistent approximation of the symmetric curl inside
T
.
A similar construction can be repeated to obtain a consistent approximation of dev grad.
Since this construction is not needed in the present discussion, we leave the details for a future work.

4.3
Global Hessian space, reconstructions, and discrete
L^{2}
-product

A global space
\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}
on the mesh
\mathcal{M}_{h}
is obtained patching together the local spaces by enforcing the single-valuedness of the unknowns attached to edges and faces shared by multiple elements.
The global divdiv operator
DD_{h}^{k}:\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}\to\mathcal{P}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

and symmetric matrix potential operator
\boldsymbol{P}_{h}^{k}:\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}\to\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h};\mathbb{S})
(with
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h};\mathbb{S})
symmetric matrix-valued version of the broken polynomial space (
2.1
)) are such that, for all
\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{h}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}
,

(DD_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{h})_{|T}\coloneq DD_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}
and
(\boldsymbol{P}_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{h})_{|T}\coloneq\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

We define the following
L^{2}
-like product:
For all
(\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{h},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}
,

(\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{h},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})_{\DIV\VDIV,h}\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T})_{\DIV\VDIV,T},

where

(\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T})_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\coloneq\int_{T}\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}:\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}+s_{T}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}).

(4.9)

Above,
s_{T}
is a symmetric positive semi-definite stabilisation bilinear form that ensures the positivity of
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\DIV\VDIV,T}
while preserving polynomial consistency.
A possible expression for
s_{T}
is the following:

s_{T}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T})=[\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T}-\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{T},\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}-\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}]_{\DIV\VDIV,T}.

(4.10)

The following polynomial consistency property easily follows from (
4.7
) and (
4.10
):

s_{T}(\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T})=0\qquad\forall(\boldsymbol{\upsilon},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T})\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(T;\mathbb{S})\times\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}.

(4.11)

Remark 9

(Difference between the component and discrete
L^{2}
-products)
.

The main difference between the local component
L^{2}
-product defined by (
4.1
) and the local discrete
L^{2}
-product defined by (
4.9
) is that the latter is consistent whenever its arguments are interpolate of polynomial functions, i.e.,

(\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon},\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\tau})_{\DIV\VDIV,T}=\int_{T}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}:\boldsymbol{\tau}\qquad\forall(\boldsymbol{\upsilon},\boldsymbol{\tau})\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(T;\mathbb{S}).

We close this section defining the norm induced by the
L^{2}
-product:
For
\bullet\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\cup\{h\}
,

\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\bullet}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,\bullet}\coloneq(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\bullet},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\bullet})_{\DIV\VDIV,\bullet}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\qquad\forall\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\bullet}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,\bullet}^{k}.

(4.12)

The norm dual to
\|{\cdot}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,h}
is denoted by
\|{\cdot}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,h,*}
.

4.4
Discrete problem and main results

Set, for the sake of brevity,
\mathcal{Z}_{h}^{k}\coloneq\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}\times\mathcal{P}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.
The discrete problem reads:
Find
(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},u_{h})\in\mathcal{Z}_{h}^{k}
such that

\displaystyle(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})_{\DIV\VDIV,h}+\int_{\Omega}DD_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}~u_{h}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k},

(4.13)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}DD_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h}~v_{h}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fv_{h}

\displaystyle\forall v_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),

or, equivalently:
Find
(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},u_{h})\in\mathcal{Z}_{h}^{k}
such that

\mathcal{A}_{h}((\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},u_{h}),(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},v_{h}))=\int_{\Omega}fv_{h}\qquad\forall(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},v_{h})\in\mathcal{Z}_{h}^{k},

(4.14)

with bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}:\mathcal{Z}_{h}^{k}\times\mathcal{Z}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathcal{A}_{h}((\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{h},w_{h}),(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},v_{h}))\coloneq(\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{h},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})_{\DIV\VDIV,h}+\int_{\Omega}DD_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}~w_{h}-\int_{\Omega}DD_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{h}~v_{h}.

We state hereafter the main analysis results for the numerical scheme defined above.
To this purpose, we equip
\mathcal{Z}_{h}^{k}
with the following norm:

\|(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},v_{h})\|_{\mathcal{Z},h}\coloneq\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,h}+\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},v_{h})\in\mathcal{Z}_{h}^{k}.

(4.15)

Theorem 10

(Well-posedness)
.

It holds

1\lesssim\inf_{(\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{h},w_{h})\in\mathcal{Z}_{h}^{k}\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},v_{h})\in\mathcal{Z}_{h}^{k}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{h}((\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{h},w_{h}),(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},v_{h}))}{\|(\underline{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}}_{h},w_{h})\|_{\mathcal{Z},h}\|(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},v_{h})\|_{\mathcal{Z},h}}.

(4.16)

Moreover, problem (
4.13
) (or, equivalently, (
4.14
)) admits a unique solution which satisfies

\|(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},u_{h})\|_{\mathcal{Z},h}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

See Section
4.6
.

Theorem 11

(Error estimate)
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{\sigma},u)\in\boldsymbol{H}(\DIV\VDIV,\Omega;\mathbb{S})\times L^{2}(\Omega)
denote the unique solution to the continuous problem (

), and assume the additional regularity

(4.17)

Well-posedness

This section contains the proof Theorem
10
preceeded by two preliminary results: a uniform equivalence of discrete
L^{2}

Lemma 12

(Uniform norm equivalence)
.

Recalling the definitions (
4.12
) and (
4.2
) of the discrete
L^{2}
- and component norms, it holds

\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\bullet}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,\bullet}\lesssim|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\bullet}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,\bullet}\lesssim\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\bullet}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,\bullet}\qquad\forall\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\bullet}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,\bullet}^{k}.

(4.18)

Proof.

It suffices to prove (
4.18
) for
\bullet=T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, as the result for
\bullet=h
follows squaring, summing over
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and passing to square roots.
We start by proving that

\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\lesssim|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\qquad\forall\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}.

(4.19)

To this end, we take a generic
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}
and use (
4.10
) to write

s_{T}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}}=|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}-\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\leq|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}+|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T},

(4.20)

where the conclusion follows from a triangle inequality.
We then use the boundedness (
4.3
) of the interpolator, discrete inverse inequalities, and the boundedness (
4.8
) of
\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}
to write

\displaystyle|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}+h_{T}|\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}+h_{T}^{2}|\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}

(4.21)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\lesssim|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}.

Plugging the above estimate into (
4.20
), we get

s_{T}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}

which, combined with the boundedness (
4.8
) of
\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}
, yields (
4.19
).

Let us now prove the converse inequality

|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\lesssim\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\qquad\forall\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}.

(4.22)

To this purpose, we start using a triangle inequality to write

|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\leq|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}-\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}+|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\eqcolon\mathfrak{T}_{1}+\mathfrak{T}_{2}.

(4.23)

For the first term, we recall (
4.10
) to write
\mathfrak{T}_{1}=s_{T}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}
, where the conclusion follows from the definitions (
4.12
) of
\|{\cdot}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}
and (
4.9
) of the local discrete
L^{2}
-product.
For the second term, we use the second line of (
4.21
) and again the definitions recalled above to write
\mathfrak{T}_{2}\leq\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}
.
Plugging the above estimates into (
4.23
) concludes the proof of (
4.22
).
∎

Lemma 13

(Inf-sup condition on
DD_{h}^{k}
)
.

The following inf-sup condition holds uniformly in
h
:

1\lesssim\inf_{v_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}\setminus\{\underline{\boldsymbol{0}}\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}DD_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}~v_{h}}{\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,h}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

(4.24)

Proof.

From the boundedness property (
4.3
) of the interpolator along with the uniform norm equivalence (
4.18
) and
h_{T}\leq h\lesssim 1
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and (
3.19
), it can be inferred that

\|\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}\boldsymbol{\tau}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,h}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\tau}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}

and

DD_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}\boldsymbol{\tau}=\pi_{\mathcal{P},h}^{k}(\DIV\VDIV\boldsymbol{\tau})

for all
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S})
,

(4.25)

where
\pi_{\mathcal{P},h}^{k}
denotes the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector on
\mathcal{P}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.
Since
\DIV\VDIV:\boldsymbol{H}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S})\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is surjective (see
[
27
, Point (iv) in Theorem 3.25]
), this shows that
DD_{h}^{k}
is a
B
-compatible operator in the sense of
[
11
, Section 8.4.1]
.
From the abstract theory therein, it can be inferred that (
4.24
) holds.
∎

We are now ready to prove the well-posedness of the discrete problem.

Proof of Theorem
10
.

By (
4.15
),
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\DIV\VDIV,h}
is coercive with respect to the norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,h}
with coercivity constant equal to 1.
In conjunction with the inf-sup condition (
4.24
) on
DD_{h}^{k}
, this classicaly yields (
4.16
).
The well-posedness of problem (
4.13
) (or, equivalently, (
4.14
)) then follows from classical arguments (see, e.g.,
[
20
, Proposition 7]
).
∎

.
To this purpose, we preliminarily need estimates of the discrete

Lemma 14

(Estimate of the consistency error for the discrete
L^{2}
-product)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S})
and define the discrete
L^{2}
-product consistency error

\mathcal{E}_{{\rm prod},h}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon};\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\coloneq\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}:\boldsymbol{P}_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}-(\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})_{\DIV\VDIV,h}.

(4.26)

Then, additionally assuming
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h};\mathbb{S})
, it holds

\|\mathcal{E}_{{\rm prod},h}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon};\cdot)\|_{\DIV\VDIV,h,*}\lesssim h^{k+1}|\boldsymbol{\upsilon}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h};\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}.

(4.27)

Proof.

We start by decomposing (
4.26
) as follows:

\mathcal{E}_{{\rm prod},h}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon};\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left[\mathfrak{T}_{1}(T)+\mathfrak{T}_{2}(T)+\mathfrak{T}_{3}(T)\right]

where, recalling that
\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon})=\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}
by (
4.7
),

\begin{gathered}\mathfrak{T}_{1}(T)\coloneq\int_{T}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}-\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}):\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T},\qquad\mathfrak{T}_{2}(T)\coloneq\int_{T}\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}-\boldsymbol{\upsilon}):\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T},\\
\mathfrak{T}_{3}(T)\coloneq-s_{T}(\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}).\end{gathered}

We next proceed to estimate these terms one by one.

The first term is readily treated using a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality along with the approximation properties of the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector (see
[
18
, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
23
, Theorem 1.45]
) for the first factor and the definitions (
4.12
) of
\|{\cdot}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}
and (
4.9
) of the local discrete
L^{2}
-product for the second:

|\mathfrak{T}_{1}(T)|\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\upsilon}-\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\|\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\lesssim h^{k+1}|\boldsymbol{\upsilon}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}.

For the second term, we preliminarily notice that

\|\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}-\boldsymbol{\upsilon})\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\overset{\eqref{eq:TP:boundedness}}{\lesssim}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}-\boldsymbol{\upsilon})|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\lesssim h^{k+1}|\boldsymbol{\upsilon}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})},

(4.28)

where the conclusion follows combining the boundedness (
4.3
) of
\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}
written for
\boldsymbol{\tau}=\boldsymbol{\upsilon}-\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}

with the approximation properties of the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector and
h_{T}\leq h
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
We can then use a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality along with (
4.12
) and (
4.9
) as for
\mathfrak{T}_{1}(T)
to write

|\mathfrak{T}_{2}(T)|\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}-\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon})\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\|\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\lesssim h^{k+1}|\boldsymbol{\upsilon}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}.

For the third term, recalling the polynomial consistency (
4.11
) of the stabilization bilinear form, we can write, for all
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}
,

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{T}_{3}(T)|

\displaystyle=|s_{T}(\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}-\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}),\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T})|

\displaystyle\leq\|\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}-\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon})\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:norm.equivalence}}{}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\DIV\VDIV,T}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}-\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}},T}^{k}\boldsymbol{\upsilon})|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}\overset{\eqref{eq:Eprod:T2(T):basic}}{\lesssim}h^{k+1}|\boldsymbol{\upsilon}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(T;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}.\qed

Lemma 15

(Estimate of the adjoint consistency error for the divdiv operator)
.

Let
v\in H^{2}(\Omega)
be such that
v=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}v=0
on
\partial\Omega
and define the divdiv adjoint consistency error linear form
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}(v;\cdot):\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
such that, for all
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}
,

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}(v;\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\coloneq\int_{\Omega}\HESS v:\boldsymbol{P}_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}-\int_{\Omega}v~DD_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}.

(4.29)

Then, additionally assuming
v\in H^{k+3}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, it holds,

\|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}(v;\cdot)\|_{\DIV\VDIV,h,*}\lesssim h^{k+1}|v|_{H^{k+3}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}.

(4.30)

Proof.

Let, for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
v_{T}\coloneq\pi_{\mathcal{P},T}^{k+2}v
.
Combining (
4.6
) for
(\boldsymbol{\tau},v)=(\boldsymbol{0},v_{T})
with the definition (
4.29
) of the adjoint consistency error, we get, for any
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}^{k}
, such that
\|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\DIV\VDIV,h}=1
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}(v;\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}(v_{T}-v)~DD_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\HESS(v-v_{T}):\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{T}

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}(\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{E}\boldsymbol{n}_{F})(v_{T}-v)

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\int_{F}\left[\gamma_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{F}~\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}_{F}}(v_{T}-v)+D_{\boldsymbol{\tau},F}~(v_{T}-v)\right],

where the insertion of the face or edge traces of
v
and of
\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}v
into the boundary terms is justified by their single-valuedness along with the assumed boundary conditions.
Using Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities along with the approximation properties of
v_{T}
, the definition (
4.2
) of
|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|{\cdot}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,T}
,
and the boundedness (
4.4
) of
DD_{T}^{k}
, (
4.8
) of
\boldsymbol{P}_{T}^{k}
, and (
4.5
) of
\gamma_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}^{k}
, we infer

\left|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\DIV\VDIV,h}(v;\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\right|\lesssim h^{k+1}|v|_{H^{k+3}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{\DIV\VDIV,h}\overset{\eqref{eq:norm.equivalence}}{\lesssim}h^{k+1}|v|_{H^{k+3}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}.\qed

Proof of Theorem
11
.

Accounting for the inf-sup condition (

), by

\boldsymbol{DG}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{t},F}^{k}

with test function
\boldsymbol{w}=\CURL_{F}r\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}^{k}(F)\subset\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}^{{-},k+1}(F)
(cf. (

)),
and noticing that
\CURL_{F}r\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{FE}=-\partial_{\boldsymbol{t}_{E}}r
on every edge

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{F}{SC}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n},F}^{k+1}\underline{\boldsymbol{DG}}_{F}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{F}\,r

\displaystyle=\int_{F}\boldsymbol{DG}_{\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{t},F}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{F}\cdot\CURL_{F}r-\!\!\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}\mathfrak{G}_{E}^{k+1}({(\boldsymbol{v}_{V_{1}})}_{\boldsymbol{n},E},{(\boldsymbol{v}_{V_{2}})}_{\boldsymbol{n},E},\boldsymbol{v}_{\boldsymbol{n},E})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\,r

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:defDGntF}}{}

\displaystyle-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}\omega_{FE}\int_{E}\mathfrak{G}_{E}^{k+1}({(\boldsymbol{v}_{V_{1}})}_{\boldsymbol{n},E},{(\boldsymbol{v}_{V_{2}})}_{\boldsymbol{n},E},\boldsymbol{v}_{\boldsymbol{n},E})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\,r

Appendix A
Results on local polynomial spaces

Lemma 17

(Isomorphism of the face divergence between polynomial spaces)
.

The operator
\VDIV_{F}:\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{{\rm c},\ell}(F)\to\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{\ell-1}(F;\mathbb{R}^{2})
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Take
\boldsymbol{x}_{F}=\boldsymbol{0}
, and write
\boldsymbol{x}=(x,y)^{\top}
.
By (
2.2
), it holds

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{{\rm c},\ell}(F)=\left\{\boldsymbol{A}(P_{1},P_{2})\coloneq\begin{pmatrix}x\,P_{1}&y\,P_{1}\\
x\,P_{2}&y\,P_{2}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}y\,P_{2}&-y\,P_{1}\\
-x\,P_{2}&x\,P_{1}\end{pmatrix}\,:\,P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathcal{P}^{\ell-1}(F)\right\}.

The divergence of a generic
\boldsymbol{A}(P_{1},P_{2})
is thus given by

\VDIV_{F}\boldsymbol{A}(P_{1},P_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}(x\partial_{x}+2y\partial_{y}+3)P_{1}-y\partial_{x}P_{2}\\
(2x\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}+3)P_{2}-x\partial_{y}P_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

This expression behaves well on monomials:
Given two couples
(i_{1},j_{1})
and
(i_{2},j_{2})
of non-negative integers, the above expression for
P_{1}(x,y)=x^{i_{1}}y^{j_{1}}
and
P_{2}(x,y)=\lambda x^{i_{2}}y^{j_{2}}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, becomes

\VDIV_{F}\boldsymbol{A}(x^{i_{1}}y^{j_{1}},\lambda x^{i_{2}}y^{j_{2}})=\begin{pmatrix}(i_{1}+2j_{1}+3)x^{i_{1}}y^{j_{1}}-\lambda i_{2}x^{i_{2}-1}y^{j_{2}+1}\\
\lambda(2i_{2}+j_{2}+3)x^{i_{2}}y^{j_{2}}-j_{1}x^{i_{1}+1}y^{j_{1}-1}\end{pmatrix}.

(A.1)

To prove the injectivity of
\VDIV_{F}
, let us show that
\VDIV_{F}\boldsymbol{A}(P_{1},P_{2})\equiv 0
implies that both
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are identically zero. We
see from (
A.1
) that each monomial
x^{i}y^{j}
of
P_{1}
must be cancelled by a monomial
x^{i+1}y^{j-1}
in
P_{2}
and vice-versa.
If
j=0
,
no contribution from
P_{2}
appears on the first component in (
A.1
),
and we must have
(i+3)x^{i}=0
(which is impossible since
i\geq 0
),
else there must be
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\VDIV_{F}\boldsymbol{A}(x^{i}y^{j},\lambda x^{i+1}y^{j-1})=\begin{pmatrix}\left[i+2j+3-(i+1)\lambda\right]x^{i}y^{j}\\
\left[\lambda(2i+j+4)-j\right]x^{i+1}y^{j-1}\end{pmatrix}\equiv 0.

This condition requires
\lambda=\frac{i+2j+3}{i+1}=\frac{j}{2i+j+4}
, i.e.,

(i+2j+3)(2i+j+4)=(i+1)j\implies 2(i+j)^{2}+10(i+j)+10=0,

which is impossible to satisfy,
showing that the only possibility for
\VDIV_{F}\boldsymbol{A}(P_{1},P_{2})\equiv 0
to hold is that
P_{1}=P_{2}\equiv 0
,
i.e.,
\VDIV_{F}
is injective on
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{{\rm c},\ell}(F)
.

Let us now prove its surjectivity by showing that every vector-valued field can be obtained as a divergence of an element of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{\ell}(F)
.
To this end, it suffices to consider the case where one component is a monomial and the other is zero.
Letting
(i,j)
denote a couple of non-negative integers such that
i+j\leq\ell
, the
above computation gives for
j>0
and
\lambda=\frac{j}{2i+j+4}
,

\VDIV_{F}\boldsymbol{A}\left(x^{i}y^{j},\frac{j}{2i+j+4}x^{i+1}y^{j-1}\right)=\frac{2(i+j)^{2}+10(i+j)+12}{2i+j+4}\begin{pmatrix}x^{i}y^{j}\\
0\end{pmatrix}

and, by symmetry,

\VDIV_{F}\boldsymbol{A}\left(\frac{i}{i+2j+4}x^{i-1}y^{j+1},x^{i}y^{j}\right)=\frac{2(i+j)^{2}+10(i+j)+12}{i+2j+4}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
x^{i}y^{j}\end{pmatrix},

which concludes the proof since
\left\{x^{i}y^{j}\,:\,\text{$i\geq 0$, $j\geq 0$, and $i+j$}\leq\ell-1\right\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{P}^{\ell-1}(F)
and its tensorization a basis of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{\ell-1}(F;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
1
.

Lemma
17
gives

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{\ell}(F)\cap\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{{\rm c},\ell}(F)=\{0\}
.
We only have to check that

\DIM\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{\ell}(F;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})=2\ell^{2}+6\ell+4=\DIM\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{\ell}(F)+\DIM\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{{\rm c},\ell}(F)
.
We can compute the dimension of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{\ell}(F)
from the isomorphism
\CURL_{F}:\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\ell+1}(F)\to\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}^{\ell}(F)
as follows:

\DIM\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{\ell}(F)=2\big(\DIM\mathcal{P}^{\ell+1}(F)-1\big)=\ell^{2}+5\ell+4.

On the other hand, the dimension of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{{\rm c},\ell}(F)
is given by Lemma
17
:

\DIM\boldsymbol{\mathcal{CG}}^{{\rm c},\ell}(F)=\DIM\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{\ell-1}(F;\mathbb{R}^{2})=\ell^{2}+\ell.

Summing the above expressions, the result follows.
∎

Dark energy in conformal Killing gravity
Junpei Harada

Abstract
The Friedmann equation, augmented with an additional term that effectively takes on the role of dark energy, is demonstrated to be an exact solution to the recently proposed gravitational theory named “conformal Killing gravity.” This theory does not explicitly incorporate dark energy. This finding suggests that there is no necessity to postulate the existence of dark energy as an independent physical entity. The dark energy derived from this theory is characterized by a specific equation of state parameter, denoted as ω \displaystyle\omega , which is uniquely determined to be − 5 / 3 \displaystyle-5/3 . If this effective dark energy is present, typically around 5% of the total energy density at the present time, and under the assumption of density parameters for matter and the cosmological constant, Ω m ∼ 0.25 \displaystyle\Omega_{\rm m}\sim 0.25 and Ω Λ ∼ 0.7 \displaystyle\Omega_{\Lambda}\sim 0.7 , respectively, the expansion of the universe at low redshifts ( z < 1.5 \displaystyle z<1.5 ) can exceed expectations, while the expansion at z > 1.5 \displaystyle z>1.5 remains unchanged. This offers a potential solution to the Hubble tension problem. Alternatively, effective dark energy could be a dominant component in the present-day universe. In this scenario, there is also the potential to address the Hubble tension, and furthermore, it resolves the coincidence problem associated with the cosmological constant.

Another derivation for
\displaystyle\omega=-5/3

As pointed out in Ref.
Mantica and Molinari 2023

Harada 2023
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nabla_{\rho}K_{\mu\nu}+\nabla_{\mu}K_{\nu\rho}+\nabla_{\nu}K_{\rho\mu}

18

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0=-\nabla_{\mu}K^{\mu}{}_{0}=\dot{\rho}_{\rm eff}+3\frac{\dot{a}}{a}(\rho_{\rm eff}+p_{\rm eff}).

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm eff}=p_{\rm eff}=0

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}

(23)

where we have used
\displaystyle p_{\rm m}=0

\displaystyle p_{\rm r}=\rho_{\rm r}/3

What we have shown above is that Eq. (

\displaystyle\omega=-5/3

The first description corresponds to the case in which we use only Eq. (
2

25

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\rho=\rho_{\rm m}+\rho_{\rm r}

The second description is the case in which we use the Friedmann equations (
22

25

These two descriptions are physically equivalent. While the second description (where dark energy appears) is practically convenient, at least in principle, we can choose only the first description (where dark energy is absent). Therefore, dark energy derived here is an effective concept in the sense that it depends on the chosen descriptions. In contrast, for example, matter and radiation represent real physical entities, and they necessarily appear in both the first and second description.

\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm eff}

V
Summary and conclusions

We have demonstrated that the recently derived evolution equation for the scale factor, Eq. (
2
), with no dark energy, is equivalent to the standard Friedmann equation which includes a specific type of dark energy. Consequently, there is no need to assume the existence of dark energy as a separate physical entity. The effective dark energy derived in this work is characterized by an equation of state parameter,
\displaystyle\omega=-5/3
, which is solely determined by the gravitational field equation (
2
).

As depicted in Fig.
1
, our findings demonstrate that when the effective dark energy (
\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm eff}\equiv 1-\Omega_{\rm m}-\Omega_{\Lambda}
) is present in a moderate amount, typically around 5% of the total energy density, it holds the potential to resolve the Hubble tension issue. Furthermore, even if the effective dark energy is dominant, typically around
\displaystyle 70\%
of the total energy density with
\displaystyle\Lambda=0
, it still holds the potential to address the Hubble tension. The latter case resolves the coincidence problem related to the cosmological constant, because only
\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm m}
is a unique independent component. This is theoretically an advantage than the models with nonzero
\displaystyle\Lambda
. As shown in Fig.
2
, the effective dark energy influences the deceleration parameter
\displaystyle q(z)
and the transition redshift
\displaystyle z_{q}
. Precisely determining these parameters could help in distinguishing whether the energy density of effective dark energy is zero or nonzero.

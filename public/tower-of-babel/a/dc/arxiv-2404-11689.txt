Introduction

The main goal of this paper is to use variational methods to show the existence of heteroclinic solutions for prescribed mean curvature equation of the type

[formula]

-div\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\right)+A(\epsilon x,y)V^{\prime}(u)=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ in }\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{2}

taking into account different geometric conditions on function
A:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\epsilon>0
. Hereafter, we mean by heteroclinic solution a function
u
that satisfies
(E)
and has the following asymptotic property at infinity

u(x,y)\rightarrow\alpha\text{ as }x\rightarrow-\infty\text{ and }u(x,y)\rightarrow\beta\text{ as }x\rightarrow+\infty,\text{ uniformly in }y\in\mathbb{R},

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
are global minima of the potential
{V}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Hereafter,
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{\alpha,\beta}\}
denotes a set of potentials that satisfy the following properties:

(
V_{1}
)

V_{\alpha,\beta}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
.

(
V_{2}
)

\alpha<\beta
and
V_{\alpha,\beta}(\alpha)=V_{\alpha,\beta}(\beta)=0
.

(
V_{3}
)

V_{\alpha,\beta}(t)\geq 0
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
V_{\alpha,\beta}(t)>0
for all
t\in(\alpha,\beta)
.

Moreover, we assume that the family
\mathcal{V}
also satisfies the following local uniform estimate involving the numbers
\alpha
and
\beta

(
V_{4}
)

Given

M=M(\lambda)>0
independent of

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(\alpha,\beta)}|V_{\alpha,\beta}^{\prime}(t)|\leq M,\quad\forall V_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{V}\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha,\beta\in(-\lambda,\lambda).

The reader is invited to see that we could consider the family

where the potentials

are the form

Another important family

The prescribed mean curvature operator

[
15

A specific class of such processes is modeled by the following equation

where the reaction function

is the classical double well Ginzburg-Landau potential and

As indicated in the previous paragraph,

2.1.

Lemma 2.1
.

If

, then for all

we have that
\tau_{k}w\in\Gamma_{\Phi}(\alpha,\beta)
and

.

Proof.

Initially, it is easy to see that
\tau_{k}w\in\Gamma_{\Phi}(\alpha,\beta)
for any

and
w\in\Gamma_{\Phi}(\alpha,\beta)
. On the other hand, for each

, a simple change variable combined with the periodicity of

in the variable

leads to

\begin{split}a_{j}(\tau_{k}w)&=\iint_{\Omega_{j}}\left(\Phi(|\nabla\tau_{k}w|)+A(x,y)V(\tau_{k}w)\right)dxdy\\
&=\iint_{\Omega_{j}}\left(\Phi(|\nabla w(x+k,y)|)+A(x+k,y)V(w(x+k,y))\right)dxdy\\
&=\iint_{\Omega_{j+k}}\left(\Phi(|\nabla w|)+A(x,y)V(w)\right)dxdy=a_{j+k}(w),\end{split}

from which it follows that

I(\tau_{k}w)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{j}(\tau_{k}w)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{j+k}(w)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{j}(w)=I(w),

and the proof is completed.
∎

that is,

Invoking estimates (

), (

) and (

which is absurd. Therefore, (

) occurs and
u\in\Gamma_{\Phi}(\alpha,\beta)
. To conclude the proof, it remains to show that

. For this purpose, given

there exists

such that

Letting

and after

Since

is arbitrary, we derive that

∎

In order to find a periodic solution

Now, we will show our last result in this subsection, which ends the study of the equation

Lemma 2.5

2.2.

Proposition 2.6

v\in K_{\Phi}^{o}(\beta)
, and the proof is over.
∎

We now finish this subsection by proving Theorem
1.6
as follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Our proof follows the method developed in
[
4
]
and we will do it in detail for the reader’s convenience. To begin with, thanks to Lemma
2.10
we can take
u\in K_{\Phi}^{o}(\beta)
. Here we will first show that

(2.81)

\iint_{\Omega_{0}}\left(\phi\left(|\nabla u|\right)\nabla u\nabla\psi+A(x,y)V^{\prime}(u)\psi\right)dxdy\geq 0,\penalty\ \text{ for all }\penalty\ \psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

which will guarantee that

\iint_{\Omega_{0}}\left(\phi\left(|\nabla u|\right)\nabla u\nabla\psi+A(x,y)V^{\prime}(u)\psi\right)dxdy=0,\penalty\ \text{ for all }\penalty\ \psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

implying that
u
is a weak solution of
(PDE)
.

In what follows, for each
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we will use the fact that
\psi(x,y)=\psi_{o}(x,y)+\psi_{e}(x,y)
, where

\psi_{e}(x,y)=\frac{\psi(x,y)+\psi(-x,y)}{2}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \psi_{o}(x,y)=\frac{\psi(x,y)-\psi(-x,y)}{2}.

In addition to these functions, let us consider for
t>0
the function

\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(x,y)=\max\left\{-\beta,\min\{\beta,\varphi_{t}(x,y)\}\right\},\quad(x,y)\in\Omega,

where

\varphi_{t}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u(x,y)+t\psi_{o}(x,y),&\mbox{if}\quad x\geq 0\text{ and }u(x,y)+t\psi_{o}(x,y)\geq 0,\\
-u(x,y)-t\psi_{o}(x,y),&\mbox{if}\quad x\geq 0\text{ and }u(x,y)+t\psi_{o}(x,y)\leq 0,\\
-\varphi_{t}(-x,y),&\mbox{if}\quad x<0.\end{array}\right.

Now, a direct computation shows that
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}\in\Gamma_{\Phi}^{o}(\beta)
. Then
(V_{6})
together with
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}
yields that

(2.82)

I(u+t\psi_{o})=I(\varphi_{t})\geq I(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})\geq c_{\Phi}^{o}(\beta)=I(u).

On the other hand, by Lemma
A.5
-(b),

(2.83)

\begin{split}I(u+t\psi)-I(u+t\psi_{o})\geq t\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}&\phi(|\nabla(u+t\psi_{o})|)\nabla u\nabla\psi_{e}dxdy\\
&+t^{2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}\phi(|\nabla(u+t\psi_{o})|)\nabla\psi_{o}\nabla\psi_{e}dxdy\\
&+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}A(x,y)\left(V(u+t\psi)-V(u+t\psi_{o})\right)dxdy.\end{split}

Since the functions
\phi(|\nabla(u+t\psi_{o})|)\nabla u\nabla\psi_{e}
and
\phi(|\nabla(u+t\psi_{o})|)\nabla\psi_{o}\nabla\psi_{e}
are odd in the variable
x
, then it is easily seen that

(2.84)

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}\phi(|\nabla(u+t\psi_{o})|)\nabla u\nabla\psi_{e}dxdy=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}\phi(|\nabla(u+t\psi_{o})|)\nabla\psi_{o}\nabla\psi_{e}dxdy=0,

and so, from (
2.82
)-(
2.84
),

I(u+t\psi)-I(u)\geq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}A(x,y)\left(V(u+t\psi)-V(u+t\psi_{o})\right)dxdy.

Consequently, as
A(x,y)V^{\prime}(u)\psi_{e}
is odd in the variable
x
, one gets

(2.85)

\begin{split}\iint_{\Omega}(\phi(|\nabla u|)&\nabla u\nabla\psi+A(x,y)V^{\prime}(u)\psi)dxdy=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{I(u+t\psi)-I(u)}{t}\\
&\geq\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}A(x,y)\frac{V(u+t\psi)-V(u+t\psi_{o})}{t}dxdy\\
&\geq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}A(x,y)V^{\prime}(u)(\psi-\psi_{o})dxdy=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}A(x,y)V^{\prime}(u)\psi_{e}dxdy=0,\end{split}

showing that the inequality (
2.81
) occurs for every
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Finally, slightly varying the same ideas discussed in the Subsection
2.1
, we can conclude the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
\Box

Appendix A
Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev Spaces

In the years 1932 and 1936 Orlicz considered and investigated in
[
20
,
21
]
the following class of functions

L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathcal{O}):\penalty\ \int_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)dx<+\infty\penalty\ \text{for some}\penalty\ \lambda>0\right\},

where
\mathcal{O}
is an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 1
and the function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
has the following properties:

(a)

\Phi
is continuous, convex and even,

(b)

\Phi(t)=0
if and only if
t=0
,

(c)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{\Phi(t)}{t}=0
and
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{\Phi(t)}{t}=+\infty
.

In these configurations, we say that
\Phi
is an
N
-function
. In Orlicz’s paper
[
20
]
, he also introduced an additional condition on the function
\Phi
the so called
\Delta_{2}
-condition
(
\Phi\in\Delta_{2}
for short) which says that there exist constants
C>0
and
t_{0}\geq 0
such that

[formula]

\Phi(2t)\leq C\Phi(t),\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall t\geq t_{0}.

Below are some examples of
N
-functions that satisfy
(\Delta_{2})
with
t_{0}=0
:

1.

\Phi_{1}(t)=\dfrac{|t|^{p}}{p}
with
p\in(1,+\infty)
,

2.

\Phi_{2}(t)=\dfrac{|t|^{p}}{p}+\dfrac{|t|^{q}}{q}
for
p<q
and
p,q\in(1,+\infty)
,

3.

\Phi_{3}(t)=|t|^{p}\ln(1+|t|)
, where
p\in(1,+\infty)
,

4.

\Phi_{4}(t)=(1+t^{2})^{\gamma}-1
with
\gamma>1
,

5.

\Phi_{5}(t)=(\sqrt{1+t^{2}}-1)^{\gamma}
for
\gamma>1
,

6.

\Phi_{6}(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}s^{1-\gamma}(\sinh^{-1}s)^{\beta}ds
with
0\leq\gamma<1
and
\beta>0
.

The following Minkowski functional

\|u\|_{L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)dx\leq 1\right\},\penalty\ \penalty\ u\in L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O}),

was introduced by Luxemburg in his thesis
[
18
]
and the reader can verify that
\|u\|_{L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})}
defines a norm on
L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})
, which is called the
Luxemburg norm
over
\mathcal{O}
. A direct computation ensures that
L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})
endowed with the Luxemburg norm is a Banach space. To illustrate this phenomenon in particular cases, consider
1<p<+\infty
and
\Phi_{p}(t)=\frac{|t|^{p}}{p}
to see that

L^{\Phi_{p}}(\mathcal{O})=L^{p}(\mathcal{O})\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ \|u\|_{L^{\Phi_{p}}(\mathcal{O})}=p^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})},

where
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}
is the usual norm of space
L^{p}(\mathcal{O})
. This fact makes it clear that the spaces
L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})
are more general than Lebesgue’s
L^{p}
spaces. However, these spaces may possess peculiar properties that do not occur in ordinary Lebesgue’s spaces. Now let us note as a point of interest that if
|\mathcal{O}|<+\infty
, then a direct computation shows that the embedding

L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\mathcal{O})

is continuous. In other words, this embedding is equivalent to the simple containment
L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})\subset L^{1}(\mathcal{O})
in which some topological properties are preserved. The
L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})
space became known in the literature as
Orlicz space
over
\mathcal{O}
. In
[
20
]
, Orlicz restricted himself to the case
\Phi\in\Delta_{2}
and in this case we may write the Orlicz spaces as follows

L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathcal{O}):\penalty\ \int_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi(|u|)dx<+\infty\right\}.

Furthermore, still under
\Delta_{2}
-condition we have the following fact

(A.1)

u_{n}\rightarrow u\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \penalty\ L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})\Leftrightarrow\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi(|u_{n}-u|)dx\rightarrow 0.

It is important to mention here that in general these facts do not occur when
\Phi
does not satisfy
(\Delta_{2})
. Orlicz was the first to investigate this case, that is, without the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, in his paper
[
21
]
in the year 1936.

A natural generalization of Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(\mathcal{O})
is the following Banach space associated with an
N
-function
\Phi

W^{1,\Phi}(\mathcal{O})=\left\{u\in L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O}):\penalty\ \frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}=u_{x_{i}}\in L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O}),\penalty\ i=1,...,N\right\},

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,\Phi}(\mathcal{O})}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})}+\|u\|_{L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})},

where
\nabla u=(u_{x_{1}},...,u_{x_{N}})
. Space
W^{1,\Phi}(\mathcal{O})
is usually called
Orlicz-Sobolev space
associated with
\Phi
over
\mathcal{O}
. It is impossible for the authors to give an exhaustive account here of the vast literature devoted to Orlicz-type spaces. For a quite comprehensive account of this topic, the interested reader might start by referring to
[
14
,
25
]
and the bibliography therein.

We are going to now consider some results that will be widely used in the development of this work. Initially we observe that given an
N
-function
\Phi
, the
complementary function

\tilde{\Phi}
associated with
\Phi
is defined by Legendre’s transformation

\tilde{\Phi}(s)=\max_{t\geq 0}\left\{st-\Phi(t)\right\}\text{ for }s\geq 0.

The function
\tilde{\Phi}
is also an
N
-function, and moreover, the functions
\Phi
and
\tilde{\Phi}
are complementary each other. An interesting example of such complementary functions are

\Phi(t)=\frac{|t|^{p}}{p}\penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \tilde{\Phi}(t)=\frac{|t|^{q}}{q}\penalty\ \text{ with }\penalty\ 1<p<+\infty\penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

An important result of the theory of Orlicz spaces is the following.

Lemma A.1
.

The space
L^{\Phi}(\mathcal{O})
is reflexive if, and only if,
\Phi
and
\tilde{\Phi}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

Proof.

See for instance
[
25
, Ch. IV, Theorem 10]
.
∎

\Phi
of the form (
1.7
) satisfying
(\phi_{1})
and
(\phi_{2})
is an
N
-function. Moreover, the reader can verify that examples 1 to 6 listed above are models of

(\phi_{1})
-

N
-functions of the form (

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\Phi
be an
N
-function of the form (
1.7
) satisfying
(\phi_{1})
-
(\phi_{2})
. Set

\xi_{0}(t)=\min\{t^{l},t^{m}\}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \xi_{1}(t)=\max\{t^{l},t^{m}\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall t\geq 0.

Then
\Phi
satisfies

\xi_{0}(t)\Phi(s)\leq\Phi(st)\leq\xi_{1}(t)\Phi(s),\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall s,t\geq 0.

In particular,
\Phi\in\Delta_{2}
.

Proof.

Let’s first show that condition
(\phi_{2})
leads to

(A.2)

l\leq\dfrac{\phi(t)t^{2}}{\Phi(t)}\leq m,\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall t>0.

Indeed, by
(\phi_{2})
we can write

l\phi(t)\leq\left(\phi(t)t\right)^{\prime}+\phi(t)\leq m\phi(t),\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall t>0,

from which it follows that

l\phi(t)t\leq\left(\phi(t)t^{2}\right)^{\prime}\leq m\phi(t)t,\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall t>0.

Therefore, integrating the last inequality we get (
A.2
), as we wanted. Finally, due to estimation (
A.2
) the proof becomes similar to that given in
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
. The details are left to the reader.
∎

Complete and tractable machine-independent characterizations of second-order polytime
Emmanuel Hainry ORCID:0000-0002-9750-0460, Bruce M. Kapron ORCID:0000-0002-3295-543X, Jean-Yves Marion ORCID:0009-0002-8262-3887, Romain Péchoux ORCID:0000-0003-0601-5425

Abstract
. The class of Basic Feasible Functionals 𝙱𝙵𝙵 {\tt BFF} is the second-order counterpart of the class of first-order functions computable in polynomial time. We present several implicit characterizations of 𝙱𝙵𝙵 {\tt BFF} based on a typed programming language of terms. These terms may perform calls to non-recursive imperative procedures. The type discipline has two layers: the terms follow a standard simply-typed discipline and the procedures follow a standard tier-based type discipline. 𝙱𝙵𝙵 {\tt BFF} consists exactly of the second-order functionals that are computed by typable and terminating programs. The completeness of this characterization surprisingly still holds in the absence of lambda-abstraction and is obtained through the use of closures. Moreover, the termination requirement can be specified as a completeness-preserving instance, which can be decided in time quadratic in the size of the program. As typing is decidable in polynomial time, we obtain the first tractable ( i.e. , decidable in polynomial time), sound, complete, and implicit characterization of 𝙱𝙵𝙵 {\tt BFF} , thus solving a problem open for more than 20 years.

Introduction

1.1.
Motivations

The class of second-order functions computable in polynomial time was introduced and studied by Mehlhorn
[
Meh76
]
, building on an earlier proposal by Constable
[
Con73
]
. Kapron and Cook characterized this class using oracle Turing machines, giving it the name Basic Feasible Functionals (
{\tt BFF}
):

{defi}
[
[
KC91
]
]
Let the size of an oracle
f
be the first-order function defined by
|f|(n)=\max_{|y|\leq n}|f(y)|
.
A functional F is in
{\tt BFF}
, if there are an oracle Turing machine
M
and a second-order polynomial
P
such that
M
computes
F
in time bounded by
P(|f|,|x|)
, for any oracle
f
and any input
x
.

The above characterization, that we take as a definition of
{\tt BFF}
, shows that functions in
{\tt BFF}
correspond to functionals computed by oracle Turing machines running in polynomial time. The polynomial time bound has a second-order nature since it takes a first-order function, the size of the oracle, as input.
Since then,
{\tt BFF}
was by consensus considered as the natural extension to second-order of the well-known class of (first-order) polynomial time computable functions,
{\tt FP}
. Notions of second-order polynomial time, while of intrinsic interest, have also been applied in a range of areas, including structural complexity theory
[
Meh76
]
, resource-bounded topology
[
Tow90
]
, complexity of total search problems
[
BCE+98
]
, feasible real analysis
[
KC12
]
, and verification
[
GAC12
]
.

Starting with Cobham’s seminal work
[
Cob65
]
, there have been several attempts to provide
machine-independent
characterizations of complexity classes such as (
{\tt P}
and)
{\tt FP}
, that is, characterizations based on programming languages rather than on machines. Beyond the purely theoretical aspects, the practical interest of such characterizations is to be able to automatically guarantee that a program can be executed efficiently and in a secure environment. For these characterizations to hold, some restrictions are placed on a given programming language. They ensure that a program can be simulated by a Turing machine in polynomial time and, therefore, corresponds to a function in
{\tt FP}
. This property is called
soundness
. Conversely, we would like any function in
{\tt FP}
to be computable by a program satisfying the restrictions. This property is called extensional
completeness
. For automation to be possible, it is necessary that the characterizations studied be
tractable
; that is, decidable in polynomial time. Moreover, they should preferably not require a prior knowledge of the program complexity. One speaks then of
implicit
characterization insofar as the programmer does not have to know an explicit bound on the complexity of the analyzed programs.

In the first-order setting, different restrictions and techniques have been developed to characterize the complexity class
{\tt FP}
. One can think, among others, of the safe recursion and ramified recursion techniques for function algebras
[
BC92
,
LM93
]
, of interpretation methods for term rewrite systems
[
BMM11
]
, or of light and soft linear logics typing-discipline for lambda-calculi
[
Gir98
,
BT04
,
BM10
]
.

In the second-order setting, a machine-independent characterization of
{\tt BFF}
was provided in
[
HKMP22b
]
. This characterization uses the tier-based (
i.e.
, safe/ramified recursion-based) type discipline introduced in
[
Mar11
]
on imperative programs for characterizing
{\tt FP}
and can be restated as follows:

{\tt BFF}=\lambda(\mbox{$\llbracket\mathrm{ST}\rrbracket$})_{2}.

\llbracket\mathrm{ST}\rrbracket
denotes the set of functions computed by typable and terminating programs;
\lambda
denotes the lambda closure: for a given set of functionals
\mathcal{S}
,
\lambda(\mathcal{S})
is the set of functionals denoted by simply-typed lambda-terms using constants in
\mathcal{S}
;
\mathcal{S}_{2}
is the restriction of
\mathcal{S}
to second-order functionals. Type inference for
\llbracket\mathrm{ST}\rrbracket
is fully automatic and can be performed in time cubic in the size of the analyzed program. However the above characterization has two main weaknesses:

•

It is not complete: As
\mbox{$\llbracket\mathrm{ST}\rrbracket$}\subsetneq{\tt BFF}
, the typed language alone is not complete for
{\tt BFF}
and a lambda closure (
i.e.
,
\lambda(\mathcal{S})
) of functionals computed by typable and terminating programs is required to ensure completeness.

•

It is not tractable: the set
\llbracket\mathrm{ST}\rrbracket
relies on a termination assumption and it is unclear whether the characterization still holds for a decidable or, for that matter, tractable termination technique.

Thus, providing a tractable, implicit, sound, and complete programming language for characterizing second-order polynomial time is still an open problem.

1.2.
Contributions

Our paper provides the first solution to this problem, open for more than 20 years (
[
IRK01
, Chapter 9]
). To this end, we introduce a higher-order programming language and design a suitable typing discipline that address the two weaknesses described above. The lambda closure requirement for completeness is removed by designing a suitable programming language that consists of a layer of simply-typed terms that can perform calls to a layer of imperative and non-recursive procedures following a tier-based type discipline. This language allows for some restricted forms of procedure composition that are handled by the simply-typed terms and also allows for some restricted forms of oracle composition that are managed through the use of
closures
, syntactic elements playing the role of first-order abstractions with free variables. The termination criterion is specified as a completeness-preserving instance, called
\mathrm{SCP}_{\mathrm{S}}
, of a variant of Size Change Termination
[
LJB01
]
introduced in
[
BL07
]
that can be checked in time quadratic in the size of the analyzed program. The main contributions of this paper are:

•

A programming language in which typable (
\mathrm{SAFE}
) and terminating (
\mathrm{SN}
) programs capture exactly
{\tt BFF}
(Theorem
3
).

•

A restriction to lambda-free programs, called rank-
0
programs, such that typable (
\mathrm{SAFE}_{0}
) and terminating (
\mathrm{SN}
) programs still capture exactly
{\tt BFF}
(Theorem
6
); hence showing that lambda-abstraction only provides a syntactic relaxation, and corresponds to a conservative extension in terms of computable functions.

•

A proof that type inference for
\mathrm{SAFE}
is
{\tt P}
-complete, and a type inference procedure running in time cubic in the program size for
\mathrm{SAFE}_{0}
(Theorem
8
).

•

A simple termination criterion, called
\mathrm{SCP}_{\mathrm{S}}
, preserving soundness and completeness of the characterizations both for
\mathrm{SAFE}
and for
\mathrm{SAFE}_{0}
(Theorem
11
) that can be checked in quadratic time.

•

A complete characterization of
{\tt BFF}
in terms of typable (
\mathrm{SAFE}
) and terminating (
\mathrm{SCP}_{\mathrm{S}}
) programs (Theorem
13
) that captures strictly more programs (Figure
1
) than
[
HKMP22b
]
, and is decidable in
{\tt P}
-time.

The programming language under study comes with some usual restrictions on its expressive power,
i.e.
, the typable programs it captures. Indeed, the studied characterizations of
{\tt BFF}
are tractable/decidable whereas knowing whether a program computes a function in
{\tt BFF}
is at least
\Sigma_{0}^{2}
-hard
[
Háj79
]
, hence not decidable. As a consequence,
false negatives
(
i.e.
, programs that are rejected by the characterizations but compute a function in
{\tt BFF}
) are unavoidable.

1.3.
Related work

Several tools providing machine-independent characterizations of distinct complexity classes have been developed in the field of Implicit Computational Complexity (ICC). Most of these tools are restricted to the study of first-order complexity classes. Whereas light logic approaches can deal with programs at higher types, their applications are restricted to first-order complexity classes such as
{\tt FP}

[
Gir98
,
BT04
,
BM10
]
. Interpretation methods were extended to higher-order polynomials in
[
BDL16
]
to study
{\tt FP}
and adapted in
[
FHHP15
]
and
[
HP20
]
to characterize
{\tt BFF}
. However, these characterizations are not decidable as they require checking of second-order polynomial inequalities.

[
CK89
]
and
[
IRK01
]
study characterizations of
{\tt BFF}
in terms of a simple imperative programming language that enforces an explicit external bound on the size of oracle outputs within loops. The corresponding restriction is not implicit by nature and is impractical from a programming perspective as the size of oracle outputs cannot be predicted. In this paper, the bound is programmer friendly because it is implicit and it only constrains the size of the oracle input.

The contributions of the paper are extensions of existing works:

•

The soundness of the characterization relies strongly on the notion of
continuation
, that fixes a given oracle (closure) for once in the imperative layer. If the oracle were allowed to be updated inside a while loop, depending on some local value, then the language would yield a class beyond
{\tt BFF}
, by computing exponential functions. Consequently, it would not be correct to think of
\mathrm{SAFE}_{0}
as a simple extension of the language IMP of
[
Win93
]
to higher-order because oracles need to be fixed once and for all in a call to a given imperative procedure.

•

The characterization of
{\tt BFF}
still holds in the absence of lambda-abstraction as a basic construct of the proposed programming language, in particular that completeness does not rely on lambda-abstractions apart from closures.
For that purpose, we consider an alternative characterization of
{\tt BFF}
that was introduced in
[
KS19
]
using the second-order restriction
PV^{\omega}_{2}
of the class
PV^{\omega}
from
[
CU93
]
. Note that this characterization is not implicit as it contains explicit bounds.
This is an important improvement over
[
HKMP22b
]
and
[
KS18
]
, both of which required external lambda-closure.

•

The type system is designed so that each procedure is typed exactly once. Types are not unique, but this does not prevent type inference from being polytime, as exhibiting one type is sufficient. The tractability of type inference is obtained by combining the tractability of type inference in the tier-based layer and in the simply-typed layer
[
Mai04
]
.

•

The particular choice of the termination criterion
\mathrm{SCP}_{\mathrm{S}}
was made to show that termination can be specified as a tractable/feasible criterion while preserving completeness. This is also a new result.
\mathrm{SCP}_{\mathrm{S}}
may include nested loops (as described in
[
BL07
]
) and can be replaced by any termination criterion capturing the programs used in the proof of completeness (Examples
4.2
,
4.2
, and
4.2
).
\mathrm{SCP}_{\mathrm{S}}
was chosen for its tractability, but not only: the
\mathrm{SCP}
criterion of
[
BL07
]
ensures termination by using an error state which breaks the control flow.
But this control-flow escape would damage the non-interference property needed for tier-based typing to guarantee time complexity bounds.

This paper is an extended and revised version of the paper
[
HKMP22a
]
presented at Foundations of Software Science and Computation Structures - 25th International Conference (FoSSaCS 2022), including complete proofs.

2.
A second-order language with imperative procedures

The syntax and semantics of the programming language designed to capture the complexity class
{\tt BFF}
are introduced in this section. Programs of this language consist in second-order terms in which imperative procedures are declared and called. These procedures have no global variables, are not recursive, and their parameters can be of order 1 (oracles) or 0. Local variables inside a procedure can only be of order 0.
Oracles are in read-only mode: they cannot be declared and, hence, modified inside a procedure. Oracles can only be composed at the term level through the use of closures, first-order abstractions that can be passed as parameters in a procedure call.

3.1.
Tiers and typing environments

Let
{\tt W}
be the type of words in
\mathcal{W}
.
Simple types
over
{\tt W}
are defined inductively by

{\tt T},{\tt T}^{\prime},\ldots::={\tt W}\ |\ {\tt T}\to{\tt T}.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\tt W}
be the set of simple types over
{\tt W}
. The order of a simple type in
\mathcal{T}_{\tt W}
is defined inductively by:

{\tt ord}({\tt T})=0
, if
{\tt T}={\tt W}
, and
{\tt ord}({\tt T})=\max(1+{\tt ord}({\tt T}_{1}),{\tt ord}({\tt T}_{2}))
, if
{\tt T}={\tt T}_{1}\to{\tt T}_{2}
.

Tiers
are elements of the totally ordered set
(\mathbf{N},\leq)
, where
\mathbf{N}=\{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},\ldots\}
is the set of natural numbers with order
\leq
. The strict order on
\mathbf{N}
will be denoted by
<
and the binary
\min
and
\max
denote the usual operations. We use bold symbols
\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k^{\prime}},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2},\ldots
to denote tier variables. For
n
tiers
\{\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n}\}
, we also define the
n
-ary max and min:
\max_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{k}_{i}
and
\min_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{k}_{i}
.
A
first-order tier
is of the shape
\mathbf{k}_{1}\to\ldots\to\mathbf{k}_{n}\to\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
, with
\mathbf{k}_{i},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{N}
.

A
simple typing environment

\Gamma_{\tt W}
is a finite partial map from
\mathbb{V}
to
\mathcal{T}_{\tt W}
, which assigns simple types to variables.

A
variable typing environment

\Gamma
is a finite partial map from
\mathbb{V}_{0}
to
\mathbf{N}
, which assigns single tiers to type-0 variables.

An
operator typing environment

\Delta
is a mapping that associates to some operator
\mathtt{op}
and some tier
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbf{N}
a set of first-order tiers
\Delta(\mathtt{op})(\mathbf{k})
of the shape
\mathbf{k}_{1}\to\ldots\to\mathbf{k}_{n}\to\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
where
n
is the arity of
\mathtt{op}
.

A
procedure typing environment

\Omega
is a mapping that associates to each procedure
\mathtt{p}
a pair
\langle\Gamma,\overline{\mathbf{k}}\rangle
consisting of a variable typing environment
\Gamma
and a triplet of tiers
\overline{\mathbf{k}}
. Let
\Omega_{i}\triangleq\pi_{i}(\Omega)
,
i\in\{1,2\}
.

Let
\textit{dom}(\Gamma)
,
\textit{dom}(\Gamma_{\tt W})
,
\textit{dom}(\Delta)
, and
\textit{dom}(\Omega)
denote the sets of variables typed by
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{\tt W}
, the set of operators typed by
\Delta
, and the set of procedures typed by
\Omega
, respectively.

For a procedure typing environment
\Omega
, it will be assumed that for every
\mathtt{p}\in\textit{dom}(\Omega)
,
\mathtt{param}(\mathtt{p})\cup{\tt local}(\mathtt{p})\subseteq\textit{dom}(\Omega_{1}(\mathtt{p}))
.

While operator and procedure typing environments are global,
i.e.
, defined for the whole program, variable typing environments are local,
i.e.
, relative to the procedure under analysis. In a program typing judgment, the simple typing environment can be viewed as the typing environment for the main program.

3.2.
Typing judgments and type system

The typing discipline includes two distinct kinds of typing judgments:

•

Procedure typing judgments

\Gamma,\Delta\vdash e:(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{in},\mathbf{k}_{out})
, with with
\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{in},\mathbf{k}_{out}\in\mathbf{N}
, and
e
an expression or a statement;

•

Term typing judgments

\Gamma_{\tt W},\Omega,\Delta\vdash\mathtt{prg}:{\tt T}
, with
{\tt T}\in\mathcal{T}_{\tt W}
.

The meaning of the procedure typing judgment is that the
expression tier
(or
statement tier
) is
\mathbf{k}
, the
innermost tier
is
\mathbf{k}_{in}
, and the
outermost tier
is
\mathbf{k}_{out}
.
The innermost (resp. outermost) tier is the tier of the innermost (resp. outermost) while loop guard where the expression or statement is located.
The meaning of term typing judgments is that the program
\mathtt{prg}
is of simple type
{\tt T}
under the operator typing environment
\Delta
, the procedure typing environment
\Omega
and the simple typing environment
\Gamma_{\tt W}
.

A program
\mathtt{prg}
(or term
\mathtt{t}
) is of
type-
i
, if
\Gamma_{\tt W},\Omega,\Delta\vdash\mathtt{prg}:{\tt T}
(
\Gamma_{\tt W},\Omega,\Delta\vdash\mathtt{t}:{\tt T}
) can be derived for some typing environments and type
{\tt T}
s.t.
{\tt ord}({\tt T})=i
.

3.3.
Safe programs

In this section, we restrict the set of admissible operators to prevent programs admitting exponential growth from being typable. A program satisfying such a restriction will be called
safe
.
We first define a safe operator typing environment by restraining how neutral and positive operators can be used, in the same way as in
[
HKMP22b
]
.

{defi}

[Safe operator typing environment]
An operator typing environment
\Delta
is
safe
if for each
\mathtt{op}\in\textit{dom}(\Delta)
with non null arity
n
,

\mathtt{op}
is neutral or positive,

\llbracket\mathtt{op}\rrbracket
is a polynomial time computable function,
and for each
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbf{N}
,
for each
\mathbf{k}_{1}\to\ldots\mathbf{k}_{n}\to\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\in\Delta(\mathtt{op})(\mathbf{k})
, the two conditions below hold:

(1)

\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\leq\min_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{k}_{i}\leq\max_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{k}_{i}\leq\mathbf{k}
,

(2)

if
\mathtt{op}
is a positive operator then
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}<\mathbf{k}
.

{exa}

Consider the operators
{\tt!\!=}
,
\mathtt{pred}
, and
\mathtt{suc}_{i}
discussed in Figure
1
and an operator typing environment
\Delta
that is safe and such that
{\tt!\!=}
,
\mathtt{pred}
,
\mathtt{suc}_{i}

\in\textit{dom}(\Delta)
.
We can set
\Delta({\tt!\!=})(\mathbf{1})=\{\mathbf{1}\to\mathbf{1}\to\mathbf{1}\}\cup\{\mathbf{k}\to\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{0}\ |\ \mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\leq\mathbf{1}\}

{\tt!\!=}
is neutral. However
\mathbf{1}\to\mathbf{0}\to\mathbf{1}\notin\Delta({\tt!\!=})(\mathbf{1})
as it breaks Condition (

,
\mathbf{1}\leq\min(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0})

3.4.
Intuitions

We now give some brief intuition to the reader on the type discipline in the particular case where exactly two tiers,
\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{1}
, are involved. The type system splits program variables, expressions, and statements between the two disjoint
tiers
:

•

\mathbf{0}
corresponds to a program component whose execution may result in an increase of the memory size and that cannot control the program flow (similar to safe inputs in
[
BC92
]
).

•

\mathbf{1}
corresponds to a program component whose execution cannot result in a memory increase and that may control the program flow (similar to normal inputs in
[
BC92
]
).

The type system of Figure
5
is composed of two sub-systems. The typing rules provided in Figure
5B
enforce that terms follow a standard simply-typed discipline. The typing rules of Figure
5A
will implement a standard non-interference type discipline à la Volpano et al.
[
VIS96
]
on the expression (and statement) tier, preventing data flows from tier
\mathbf{0}
to tier
\mathbf{1}
. The transition between the two sub-type-systems is performed in the rule (P-DEC) of Figure
5B
that checks that the procedure body follows the tier-based type discipline once and for all in a procedure declaration.

In Figure
5A
, as tier
\mathbf{1}
data cannot grow and are the only data driving the program flow, the number of distinct memory configurations on such data for a terminating procedure is polynomial in the size of the program input (
i.e.
, number of symbols). Hence a typable and terminating procedure has a
polynomial step count
in the sense of
[
Coo92
]
,
i.e.
, on any input, the execution time of a procedure is bounded by a first-order polynomial in the size of their input and the maximal size of any answer returned by an oracle call.

The innermost tier is used to implement a declassification mechanism on operators improving the type-system’s expressive power: an operator may be typed differently depending on its calling context (the statement where it is applied). This is the reason why more than 2 tiers can be used in general.

The outermost tier is used to ensure that oracles are only called on inputs of bounded size. This latter restriction on oracle calls enforces a semantic restriction, called
finite lookahead revision
, introduced in
[
KS17
,
KS18
]
and requiring that, during each computation, the number of calls performed by the oracle on an input of increasing size is bounded by a constant.

Let
\mathrm{MPT}
be the class of second-order functionals computable by an oracle Turing machine with a polynomial step count and a finite lookahead revision.

[
KS18
]
shows that
{\tt BFF}=\lambda(\mathrm{MPT})_{2}
. The type system of Figure
5
ensures that each terminating procedure of a well-typed program computes a function in
\mathrm{MPT}
.

5.1.

We first show that studying the termination of a safe program can be reduced to the study of the procedure termination.
For that purpose, we need to define what is meant for a procedure to terminate by looking at the program semantics of Figure

A procedure

Lemma 9

For a given

Proof 5.1
.

If all procedures of a given program are terminating then any procedure call appearing in a term is terminating.
As the terms are simply-typed, they are known to be terminating, as a direct consequence of the strong normalization of the simply-typed lambda-calculus
[

]

Hence, ensuring the termination of each procedure of a given safe program is a sufficient condition for the program to terminate.
The converse trivially does not hold as, for example, a procedure with an infinite loop may be declared and not be called within a given safe program.

5.2.

SCT relies on the fact that if all infinite executions imply an infinite descent in a well-founded order, then no infinite execution exists.
To apply this fact for proving termination,
[

]
defines Size-Change Graphs (SCGs) that exhibit decreases in the parameters of function calls and then studies the infinite paths in all possible infinite sequences of calls.
If all those infinite sequences have at least one strictly decreasing path, then the program must terminate for all inputs.
While SCT is
{\tt PSpace}
-complete, Ben-Amram and Lee
[

develop a more effective technique, called SCP, that is in
{\tt P}

The SCP technique is strong enough for our use case. In the literature, SCT and SCP are applied to pure functional languages.
As we shall enforce termination of procedures, we will follow the approach of
[

For each assignment of a procedure
\mathtt{p}
, we design a bipartite graph, called a SCG, whose nodes are type-
0

The bipartite graph is generated for any flattened assignment
\mathtt{x}\mathtt{\ :=\ }\mathtt{e}
by:

•

\mathtt{y}
,
\mathtt{y}\neq\mathtt{x}

\mathtt{y}
to right
\mathtt{y}
.

•

If
\mathtt{e}=\mathtt{y}
, we draw an arrow from left
\mathtt{y}
to right
\mathtt{x}
.

•

If
\mathtt{e}=\mathtt{op}(\overline{\mathtt{x}})

\mathtt{op}
a:

–

decreasing operator in

\mathtt{x}_{i}
to
\mathtt{x}
.

–

strictly decreasing operator in
i

\mathtt{x}_{i}
to
\mathtt{x}
.

In all other cases (neutral and non-decreasing operators, positive operators, oracle calls), no arrow is drawn. We will name this SCG graph G(

6.
Conclusion and future work

We have presented a typing discipline and a termination criterion for a programming language that is sound and complete for the class of second-order polytime computable functionals,
{\tt BFF}
. This characterization has three main advantages:

(1)

it is based on a natural higher-order programming language with imperative procedures;

(2)

it is pure as it does not rely on an extra semantic requirements (such as taking the lambda closure);

(3)

belonging to the set
\mathrm{SCP}_{\mathrm{S}}\cap\mathrm{SAFE}
can be decided in polynomial time.

The benefits of tractability is that our method can be automated. However the expressive power of the captured programs is restricted. This drawback is the price to pay for tractability and we claim that the full SCT method, known to be
{\tt PSpace}
-complete, could be adapted in a more general way to our programming language in order to capture more programs at the price of a worse complexity. Moreover, any termination criterion based on the absence of infinite data flows with respect to some well-founded order could work and preserve completeness of our characterizations. Another issue of interest is to study whether the presented approach could be extended to characterize
{\tt BFF}
in a purely functional language.

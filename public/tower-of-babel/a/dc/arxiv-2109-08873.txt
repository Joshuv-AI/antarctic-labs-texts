Formulation and verification of gyrokinetic simulation of kinetic-MHD processes in toroidal plasmas
Xishuo Wei, Pengfei Liu, Gyungjin Choi, Guillaume Brochard, Jian Bao, Yuehao Ma, Haotian Chen, Handi Huang, Zhihong Lin 1

Abstract
A set of electromagnetic formulations with gyrokinetic ions and drift kinetic electrons for global gyrokinetic simulations has been formulated and verified in the framework of Gyrokinetic Toroidal Code (GTC), which is particularly suitable for the low-frequency electromagnetic modes including drift waves, Alvén Eigenmodes and MHD modes. This gyrokinetic simulation model can reduce to the ideal MHD model in the long wavelength limit, and recover the well-known MHD dispersion relation. Especially, the magnetic compression perturbation and the equilibrium current are carefully treated in this model. Furthermore, the detailed implementation of the equations in Boozer coordinate system is provided. The internal kink mode simulations in DIII-D and ITER tokamaks using this model have been successfully bench-marked with other kinetic and MHD codes. The physical model has also been used to generate a database to train the machine learning surrogate model of GTC.



1
Introduction

The physical processes in magnetic confined plasmas cover a wide range of spatial-temporal scales, which are characterized by the single particle orbits. In the strong magnetized plasmas, the time scale of the fluctuations of interest are much longer than the gyro period of charged particles. Given this property, the gyrokinetic theory was raised
[
1
,
2
]
, which neglects the details of fast gyromotion of particles, and only focuses on the low frequency phenomena. The gyrokinetic framework studies the particle motion in the 5-D gyrokinetic phase space, thus greatly reducing the computational cost compared to the fully-kinetic calculations. Over the past several decades, the Gyrokinetic Toroidal Code (GTC), among many gyrokinetic simulation codes, has become the powerful tools to study the low frequency kinetic-MHD physics, thanks to the advances in physical models, numerical algorithms and computing power. GTC has been used to study the neoclassical transport
[
3
]
, the turbulent transport and zonal flows
[
4
]
, the messo-scale Alvén eigenmodes
[
5
]
, and the macro-scale MHD modes
[
6
]
. GTC has also been used for the simulations in various devices, including tokamaks, stellarators
[
7
]
and field-reversed configurations
[
8
]
. During the development of GTC, the simulation model has been extended to incorporate more complex physics or to improve numerical efficiencies. Such improvements include the collision operator
[
3
]
, the kinetic electron model
[
9
]
, the electromagnetic model in toroidal geometry
[
10
]
, the inclusion of equilibrium current
[
11
]
, the general toroidal geometry
[
12
]
and the compressional magnetic perturbation
[
13
]
. However, the verifications of these works only focus on the certain part of the simulation model, and these extensions are not entirely orthogonal. It is worthwhile to summarize all the previous works to a comprehensive physical model and provide the implementation details. This comprehensive model is the ’once-for-all’ gyrokinetic model in GTC to treat the low-frequency problem.

On the other hand, the kinetic-MHD problems has been studied using the ’hybrid MHD’ codes such as M3D-C1
[
14
]
, NOVA
[
15
]
, XTOR-K
[
16
]
. Recently, we have used the gyrokinetic model in GTC and GAM-solver
[
17
]
to carry out a benchmark of internal kink mode with the MHD codes. The simulation shows that in the long wave length limit, all the codes agree well. In this paper, we will show analytically how the gyrokinetic model reduces to the MHD model. Besides, we will emphasize on some factors that were considered not important in conventional gyrokinetic simulations, but crucial to get the correct results for MHD modes. The first one is to include an accurate equilibrium current. The electromagnetic model that considers equilibrium current is shown in
[
11
]
, but now we find that the radial current term
\hat{\delta}
in Boozer coordinate is critical to get the poloidal variation of equilibrium current and the current driven MHD instability. We also propose a method to accurately calculate the parallel equilibrium current in axisymmetric equilibiurm. The second factor is the compressible magnetic perturbation
\delta B_{\parallel}
.
\delta B_{\parallel}
was thought to be unimportant, especially in the low plasma
\beta
regime. But in GTC simulations of Kinetic Ballooning Mode(KBM)
[
13
]
, the
\delta B_{\parallel}
effect on the rapid nonlinear growth of mode is found. In the kink mode simulation, we show that
\delta B_{\parallel}
can be comparable to
\delta B_{\perp}
, and significant effect on linear growth rate from
\delta B_{\parallel}
is found. Apart from the kink mode benchmark, we have used the improved model to simulate more than 5000 DIII-D cases which can exhibit kink instability to build a database. We have used this database to train a surrogate model to predict the linear kink drive using experimental parameters, the results of which are presented in a separate paper
[
18
]
. The success application of the comprehensive model on kink mode benchmark and the large amount of kink mode simulations demonstrates the capability of this gyrokinetic model on the kinetic-MHD simulations.

This paper is organized as follows. In section 2, the model equations are given, in which the gyrokinetic ion model, drift kinetic electron models, and the gyrokinetic Maxwell equations are used. In the long wavelength limit, this model reduces to the ideal MHD model and the ideal MHD dispersion relation (the vorticity equation) can be found. In section 3 we show the simulation results from the kink mode benchmark and the results from over 5000 simulations of kink instability. The conclusion is given in Section 4.

2.2
Numerical schemes

Solving the equations in Section
2.1
accurately is numerically challenging. Different numerical improvements have been raised over the past few decades. The usage of
\delta f
method
[
25
,
26
]
can reduce the numerical noise of discrete particles. The ’split-weight’ method still solves the full electron drift kinetic equation, but the leading order ’adiabatic’ distribution function is solved analytically, thus removing the numerical noise associate with the adiabatic response. However, due to the large ion-to-electron mass ratio, the electromagnetic simulations including the kinetic effects of both ions and electrons is still challenging. In the simulation of waves with the Alfvénic polarization, the electrostatic parallel electric field and the inductive parallel electric field nearly canceled out and lead to a small
E_{\parallel}
. The calculation time derivative in
\partial_{t}A_{\parallel}
term often introduce numerical instability. If the the electron density and current are calculated directly from the electron distribution function, the numerical errors from electron particle noise will make the vector potential and electrostatic potential inconsistent, leading to an nonphysically large
E_{\parallel}
. In the low-
\beta
regime, the Alfvén branch will become a high frequency
\omega_{H}
mode.
[
27
]
. And the simulation time step has to be small enough to resolve this physically uninteresting mode. These problems are overcome with the new simulation schemes in GTC
[
9
,
28
,
10
,
29
]
.In this section, we will show the two schemes of solving the electromagnetic system and the scheme for electrostatic system.

2.2.1
Hybrid scheme

This scheme was developed by a series of work in
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
29
,
13
]
. The idea is to expand the electron drift kinetic equation based on the small parameter
\omega/(k_{\parallel}v_{\parallel})
since the modes we are interested in are low frequency modes, the leading order electron distribution is just the adiabatic response, then the drift kinetic equation and the parallel forbearance equation are solved order by order. In this way, we explicitly remove the
\omega_{H}
mode from the system. On the other hand, only the high order moments (pressure) of electrons are calculated from electron distribution function, and the lower order moments (density and parallel flow) of electrons are calculated by the conservative moments equation. Finally, the leading order
\delta A_{\parallel}
is solved analytically, thus reducing the numerical noise on
\delta A_{\parallel}
.

Heuristically, we can separate the perturbed parallel vector potential to two parts,
\delta A_{\parallel}=\delta A_{\parallel}^{L}+\delta A_{\parallel}^{NL}

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial\phi_{ind}}{\partial\alpha_{0}},

\delta f_{e}
, i.e. adiabatic distribution function, as,

\begin{split}\delta f_{e}^{ad}\equiv\frac{e\phi_{eff}}{T_{e}}f_{0e}-\frac{\mu}{T_{e}}\delta B_{\parallel}f_{0e}+\frac{\partial f_{0e}}{\partial\psi_{0}}\delta\psi+\frac{\partial f_{0e}}{\partial\alpha_{0}}\delta\alpha-\frac{e}{T_{e}}\frac{\partial\phi_{eq}}{\partial\psi_{0}}f_{0e}\delta\psi.\end{split}

(22)

Then the remaining un-adiabatic(or ’kinetic’) part of electron distribution function
\delta h_{e}\equiv\delta f_{e}-\delta f_{e}^{ad}
can be determined by substituting (
22
) to Vlasov equation, which yields

\begin{split}L\delta h_{e}=&-\delta Lf_{0e}-L\delta f_{e}^{ad}.\end{split}

(23)

Defining the particle weight as
w_{e}=\delta h_{e}/f_{e}
, we can get the electron weight equation

\begin{split}\frac{dw_{e}}{dt}=&L\frac{\delta h_{e}}{f_{e}}=-\left(1-\frac{\delta f_{e}^{ad}}{f_{0e}}-\delta h_{e}\right)\left(\frac{1+\delta f_{e}^{ad}/f_{0e}}{f_{0e}}\delta Lf_{0e}+L\frac{\delta f_{e}^{ad}}{f_{0e}}\right)\\
=&-\left(1-\frac{\delta f_{e}^{ad}}{f_{0e}}-\delta h_{e}\right)\frac{1+\delta f_{e}^{ad}/f_{0e}}{f_{0e}}\times\left\{\left(v_{\parallel}\frac{\delta\mathbf{B}_{\perp}}{B_{\parallel}^{*}}+\delta\mathbf{v}_{E}+\mathbf{v}_{b\parallel}\right)\cdot\nabla|_{\mu}f_{0e}\right.\\
&\left.-\frac{1}{m_{e}}\left[\frac{\mu\delta\mathbf{B}_{\perp}\cdot\nabla\left(B_{0}-\frac{e}{\mu}\phi_{eq}\right)}{B_{\parallel}^{*}}-e\frac{\mathbf{B}^{*}}{B_{\parallel}^{*}}\cdot\nabla\left(\delta\phi-\frac{\mu}{e}\delta B_{\parallel}\right)-e\frac{\partial\delta A_{\parallel}}{\partial t}\right]\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\parallel}}f_{0e}\right\}\\
&-\left(1-\frac{\delta f_{e}^{ad}}{f_{0e}}-\delta h_{e}\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{\delta f_{e}^{ad}}{f_{0e}}+\dot{\mathbf{R}}\cdot\nabla|_{\mu}\frac{\delta f_{e}^{ad}}{f_{0e}}+\dot{v}_{\parallel}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\parallel}}\frac{\delta f_{e}^{ad}}{f_{0e}}\right),\end{split}

(24)

where we have used the conclusion
L_{0}f_{0e}=0
.
By integrating the Vlasov equation in velocity space, we obtain the continuity equation which governs the evolution of
\delta n_{e}
[
13
]
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\partial\delta n_{e}}{\partial t}+\mathbf{B}_{0}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{n_{0e}\delta u_{\parallel e}}{B_{0}}\right)+B_{0}\delta\mathbf{v}_{E}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{n_{0e}}{B_{0}}\right)-n_{0}\left(\delta\mathbf{v}_{*}+\delta\mathbf{v}_{E}\right)\cdot\frac{\nabla B_{0}}{B_{0}}+\delta\mathbf{B}_{\perp}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{n_{0e}u_{\parallel 0e}}{B_{0}}\right)\\
&-\frac{\nabla\times\mathbf{B_{0}}}{eB_{0}^{2}}\cdot\left(\nabla\delta P_{\parallel e}+\frac{\left(\delta P_{\perp e}-\delta P_{\parallel e}\right)\nabla B_{0}}{B_{0}}-n_{0e}e\nabla\delta\phi\right)+\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\delta P_{\parallel e}\mathbf{b}_{0}\nabla\times\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{b}_{0}}{eB_{0}}\right)\\
&+\delta\mathbf{B}_{\perp}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{n_{0e}\delta u_{\parallel e}}{B_{0}}\right)+B_{0}\mathbf{v}_{E}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{\delta n_{e}}{B_{0}}\right)+\frac{\delta n_{e}}{B_{0}^{2}}\mathbf{b}_{0}\times\nabla B_{0}\cdot\nabla\phi+\frac{\delta n_{e}}{B_{0}^{2}}\nabla\times B_{0}\cdot\nabla\phi\\
&-\frac{\mathbf{b}_{0}\times\nabla\delta B_{\parallel}}{e}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{\delta P_{\perp e}+P_{\perp 0e}}{B_{0}^{2}}\right)-\frac{\nabla\times\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\nabla\delta B_{\parallel}}{eB_{0}^{2}}\left(\delta P_{\perp e}+P_{\perp 0e}\right)=0,\end{split}

(25)

with
\delta\mathbf{v}_{*}=\mathbf{b}_{0}\times\nabla\left(\delta P_{\parallel e}+\delta P_{\perp e}\right)/\left(n_{0e}m_{0e}\Omega_{e}\right)
, and
n_{0e}u_{\parallel 0e}=-\nabla\times\mathbf{B}_{0}/(e\mu_{0})+\sum_{s\neq e}Z_{s}n_{0s}u_{\parallel 0s}/e
denotes the electron equilibrium parallel flow. The detailed expressions of
P_{\perp 0e},P_{\parallel 0e},\delta P_{\perp,e},\delta P_{\parallel,e}
can be found in Appendix
A
.
The continuity equation can also be written in conservative form,

\frac{\partial\delta n_{e}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\Gamma=0,

(26)

where
\Gamma
is the particle flux,
\Gamma=2\pi\int dv_{\parallel}\int d\mu\dot{\mathbf{R}}f_{e}B_{0}/m_{e}
.
It should be pointed out that the continuity equation directly integrated
from the Vlasov equation is in gyrocenter space. Even if the electron polarization density approaches 0 when we take the limit
k_{\perp}\rho_{e}\rightarrow 0
, there is a finite magnetization density when we calculate the electron density in particle space,

\begin{split}\delta n_{e,particle}=\delta n_{e}+\frac{\delta B_{\parallel}}{B_{0}}n_{0e}.\end{split}

That is why the
\delta B_{\parallel}
term is included in the left hand side of gyrokinetic Poisson equation (
13

\delta n_{e}

\begin{split}\frac{e\phi_{eff}}{T_{e}}=\frac{\delta n_{e}-\int d\mathbf{v}\delta h_{e}}{n_{0e}}+\frac{\delta B_{\parallel}}{B_{0}}-\frac{\partial\ln n_{0e}}{\partial\psi_{0}}\delta\psi-\frac{\partial\ln n_{0e}}{\partial\alpha_{0}}\delta\alpha+\frac{e}{T_{e}}\frac{\partial\phi_{eq}}{\partial\psi_{0}}\delta\psi.\end{split}

(27)

One may notice that we have not formally define
\delta\phi_{ind}
and the system is not closed. In
[

\delta A_{\parallel}
are separated to zonal and non-zonal components,
\delta A_{\parallel}=\delta A_{\parallel 00}+\delta A_{\parallel,nz}

\delta A_{\parallel 00}

\delta A_{\parallel}^{NL}

\delta A_{\parallel 00}

-\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\delta A_{\parallel,00}+\frac{1}{\delta_{e}^{2}}\delta A_{\parallel,00}=\mu_{0}\left[\sum_{s\neq e}Z_{s}n_{s}\delta\bar{u}_{00\parallel,s}-en_{e}\delta u_{00\parallel e}\right].

Here
\delta_{e}=c/\omega_{pe}
is the electron skin depth. Later, inspired by

\delta A_{\parallel}
and close the system, and
\delta A_{\parallel}^{NL}
becomes a passive quantity that can be calculated alternatively. The conservative form of parallem momentum equation reads

\begin{split}\frac{\partial(\delta u_{\parallel e}+u_{\parallel 0e})}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\int d\mathbf{v}\dot{\mathbf{R}}v_{\parallel}f_{e}-\int d\mathbf{v}\dot{v}_{\parallel}f_{e}=0.\end{split}

We assume the equilbrium part is automatically satisfied, and only solve the perturbed part,

\begin{split}m_{e}n_{0e}\frac{\partial u_{\parallel e}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\delta\mathbb{P}+\delta\Xi-e\left(n_{0e}+\delta n_{e}\right)\frac{\partial A_{\parallel}}{\partial t}=0,\end{split}

(29)

\delta\mathbb{P}
and

(30)

Here

is the speed of light, and

is the plasma oscillation frequency. We can use (
30

\delta\phi_{ind}
, since the RHS can be expressed as a function of
\delta\phi_{ind}

\begin{split}\frac{\delta B_{\parallel}}{B_{0}}=&-\frac{\beta_{e}}{2+\beta_{e}+2\sum_{s\neq e}\beta_{s}}\left[\frac{3}{2}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\delta\phi\sum_{s\neq e}\frac{\beta_{s}}{\beta_{e}}\frac{Z_{s}}{T_{s}}\rho_{s}^{2}+\frac{5}{4}\nabla_{\perp}^{4}\delta\phi\sum_{s\neq e}\frac{\beta_{s}}{\beta_{e}}\frac{Z_{s}}{T_{s}}\rho_{s}^{4}\right.\\

Then we assume

2.2.3
Electrostatic scheme

This scheme was developed in
[
28
]
. In the electrostatic simulation,
\delta A_{\parallel}=0
and
\delta B_{\parallel}=0
is always satisfied, and the calculation procedure can be simplified.
In this case, the electron adiabatic response is simply defined as

\delta f_{e}^{ad}=\frac{e\delta\phi_{nz}}{T_{e}}f_{0e}.

(38)

The governing equation of electron weight equation becomes

\begin{split}\frac{dw_{e}}{dt}=&-\left(1-\frac{e\delta\phi_{nz}}{T_{e}}-\delta h_{e}\right)\frac{1+e\delta\phi_{nz}/T_{e}}{f_{0e}}\times\left(\delta\mathbf{v}_{E}\cdot\nabla|_{\mu}f_{0e}\right.\\
&\left.+\frac{e}{m_{e}}\frac{\mathbf{B}^{*}}{B_{\parallel}^{*}}\cdot\nabla\delta\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\parallel}}f_{0e}\right)\\
&-\left(1-\frac{e\delta\phi_{nz}}{T_{e}}-\delta h_{e}\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{e\delta\phi_{nz}}{T_{e}}+\dot{\mathbf{R}}\cdot\nabla|_{\mu}\frac{e\delta\phi_{nz}}{T_{e}}\right).\end{split}

(39)

In the leading order, we assume
\delta h_{e}^{(0)}=0
, so
\delta n_{e,nz}^{(0)}=n_{0e}e\delta\phi_{nz}^{(0)}/T_{e}
, and the Poisson equation can be solved. Note that this leading order assumption is only for the non-zonal component of electrons, so only the non-zonal potential is solved.

\sum_{s\neq e}\frac{Z_{s}n_{0s}}{T_{s}}\left(\delta\phi^{(0)}_{nz}-\delta\tilde{\phi}_{s}^{(0)}\right)+\frac{e^{2}\delta\phi^{(0)}_{nz}}{T_{e}}n_{0e}=\sum_{s\neq e}Z_{s}\delta\bar{n}_{nz,s}.

(40)

Then this
\phi^{(0)}_{nz}
is substituted in (
38
) and (
39
) to solve
\delta f_{e}^{ad,(1)}
and
\delta h_{e}^{(1)}
. The non-adiabatic density can be added to (
40
) to solve the next order potential

\sum_{s\neq e}\frac{Z_{s}n_{0s}}{T_{s}}\left(\delta\phi^{(1)}_{nz}-\delta\tilde{\phi}_{s}^{(1)}\right)+\frac{e^{2}\delta\phi^{(1)}_{nz}}{T_{e}}n_{0e}=\sum_{s\neq e}Z_{s}\delta\bar{n}_{nz,s}-e\left(\int d\mathbf{v}\delta h_{e}^{(1)}\right)_{nz}.

(41)

This iteration process can be repeated until desirable accuracy is reached. Then the zonal component potential can be solved using the flux averaged Poisson equation.

\sum_{s\neq e}\frac{Z_{s}n_{0s}}{T_{e}}\left(\delta\phi_{00}-\delta\tilde{\phi}_{00,s}\right)=\sum_{s\neq e}Z_{s}\delta\bar{n}_{00,s}-e\left(\int d\mathbf{v}\delta h_{e}\right)_{00}

(42)

2.3
Fluid simulation models

In the limit of long wavelength or when the thermal ion kinetic effect is not important, e.g. MHD modes, we can use the ion fluid equation to replace the ion Vlasov equation, and drop the electron kinetic corrections. The ion continuity equation and ion parallel momentum equation in long wavelength and low ion temperature limit can be integrated from the ion Vlasov equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\partial\delta n_{i}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\left[\left(n_{0i}+\delta n_{i}\right)\mathbf{v}_{E}+n_{0i}\delta u_{\parallel i}\frac{\mathbf{B_{0}}+\delta\mathbf{B_{\perp}}}{B_{0}}\right]=0,\\
&n_{0i}\frac{\partial\delta u_{\parallel i}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\left(n_{0i}\delta u_{\parallel i}\mathbf{v}_{E}\right)+\frac{Z_{i}}{m_{i}}n_{0i}{\mathbf{b}_{0}}\cdot\nabla\phi_{eff}=0.\end{split}

Conclusion

In this paper we formulate the nonlinear electromagnetic gyrokinetic model incorporating accurate perturbed magnetic compression and equilibrium flow. In the long wavelength limit, the ideal MHD dispersion relation is recovered. The model is used to study the current and pressure-driven internal kink mode. The kink instability is sensitive to the correct implementation of
\delta B_{\parallel}
and
J_{\parallel 0}
. A large number of kink cases chosen from DIII-D experiments is successfully simulated to build a database for machine learning. The implementation of this model is important for later electromagnetic simulations and coupling of different modes with different scales.

The form of
L_{0}
remains unchanged. Then the equilibrium distribution in the presence of island can be found using

(L_{0}+\delta L_{eq})\delta f_{s,eq}=-\delta L_{eq}F_{s0}.

(C.3)

Here
F_{s0}
is the equilbirium distribution function without magnetic island (i.e. shift Maxwellian). Here the self-consistent perturbation is forced to be 0, and only the island effect is considered. The flattening effect of magnetic islands will be found by solving this equation. After the steady state being reached, the selfconsistent turbulence simulation can be carried out by solving

(L_{0}+\delta L_{eq}+\delta L)\delta f=-(\delta L+\delta L_{eq})(F_{s0}),

(C.4)

with the initial value of
\delta f
being
\delta f_{s,eq}
. Also, we need to directly solve the original drift kinetic Vlasov equation for electrons, since the separation of adiabatic and non-adiabatic response is not valid with magnetic islands in the electrostatic case.

The electromagnetic equation can be derived similarly. In some cases, we would like to give a seed island and study the self-consistent evolution of turbulence and islands. Then the island magnetic field can be simply treated as part of the
\delta\mathbf{B}_{\perp}
in the simulation model. After solving the flattening effect in (
C.3
), we just need to solve the system described in
2.2.1
or
2.2.2
, and no special treatments are needed. The evolution of magnetic islands is reflected in (
30
).

Appendix D
Calculation of equilibrium parallel current

In the GTC ideal MHD simulations in tokamaks, kink mode can be driven by parallel
current and pressure gradient, the parallel current can be calculated
numerically in several ways.

D.1
Calculating current directly in Boozer coordinate system

We directly calculate
J_{\parallel 0}
from (
49
). The
\hat{\delta}
-current is given by

\hat{\delta}=-\frac{I\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}+g\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}}{\mathsf{g}^{\psi\psi}},

(D.1)

where
\mathsf{g}^{\alpha\beta}=\nabla\alpha\cdot\nabla\beta

is the element of the contra-variant metric tensor. One can note that
\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}/\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}\sim O\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)
.
But in the calculation of
\hat{\delta}
,
\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}
cannot be simply neglected
because
I/g\sim O\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)
, and the two terms are
comparable in (
D.1
). Axisymmetry exists for
2D equilibria, and the toroidal direction
\nabla\phi
is perpendicular
to
\nabla\psi
and
\nabla\theta
. However a transformation has
been applied to
\phi
to construct the Boozer toroidal angle
\zeta
.
For 2d equilibrium, the difference between
\zeta
and
\phi
is
given by
[
37
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nu&=\phi-\zeta\\
&=\int\left(\frac{g\mathcal{J}}{R^{2}}-q\right)\text{d}\theta.\end{split}

(D.2)

There is one degree of freedom to fully determine
\nu
function,
and we can simply choose
\nu\left(\theta=0\right)=0
. Now from
\phi\left(\psi,\theta\right)=\zeta+\nu

we can obtain
\partial_{\psi}\phi=\partial_{\psi}\nu
,
\partial_{\theta}\phi=\partial_{\theta}\nu
,
and
\partial_{\zeta}\phi=1
. Together with
R\left(\psi,\theta\right)

and
Z\left(\psi,\theta\right)
, the covariant metric tensor is constructed,

\mathsf{g}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\partial R}{\partial\alpha}\frac{\partial R}{\partial\beta}+R^{2}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\alpha}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\beta}+\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\alpha}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\beta},

(D.3)

and accordingly the contra-variant metric tensor is constructed from the covariant one,

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\psi}&\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}&\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}\\
\mathsf{g}^{\theta\psi}&\mathsf{g}^{\theta\theta}&\mathsf{g}^{\theta\zeta}\\
\mathsf{g}^{\zeta\psi}&\mathsf{g}^{\zeta\theta}&\mathsf{g}^{\zeta\zeta}\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{\Delta}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathsf{g}_{\theta\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\zeta\zeta}-\mathsf{g}_{\theta\zeta}\mathsf{g}_{\theta\zeta}&\mathsf{g}_{\psi\zeta}\mathsf{g}_{\theta\zeta}-\mathsf{g}_{\psi\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\zeta\zeta}&\mathsf{g}_{\psi\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\theta\zeta}-\mathsf{g}_{\theta\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\psi\zeta}\\
\mathsf{g}_{\psi\zeta}\mathsf{g}_{\theta\zeta}-\mathsf{g}_{\psi\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\zeta\zeta}&\mathsf{g}_{\psi\psi}\mathsf{g}_{\zeta\zeta}-\mathsf{g}_{\psi\zeta}\mathsf{g}_{\psi\zeta}&\mathsf{g}_{\psi\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\psi\zeta}-\mathsf{g}_{\psi\psi}\mathsf{g}_{\zeta\zeta}\\
\mathsf{g}_{\psi\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\theta\zeta}-\mathsf{g}_{\theta\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\psi\zeta}&\mathsf{g}_{\psi\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\psi\zeta}-\mathsf{g}_{\psi\psi}\mathsf{g}_{\zeta\zeta}&\mathsf{g}_{\psi\psi}\mathsf{g}_{\theta\theta}-\mathsf{g}_{\psi\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\psi\theta}\end{array}\right),

(D.4)

where
\Delta
is the determinant of the covariant metric tensor,

\Delta=\mathsf{g}_{\psi\psi}\mathsf{g}_{\theta\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\zeta\zeta}-\mathsf{g}_{\psi\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\psi\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\zeta\zeta}-\mathsf{g}_{\theta\zeta}\mathsf{g}_{\theta\zeta}-\mathsf{g}_{\psi\zeta}\mathsf{g}_{\psi\zeta}+2\mathsf{g}_{\psi\theta}\mathsf{g}_{\theta\zeta}\mathsf{g}_{\psi\zeta}
.
Note both the covariant and the contra-variant metric tensor are symmetric,

\mathsf{g}_{\alpha\beta}=\mathsf{g}_{\beta\alpha}
, and
\mathsf{g}^{\alpha\beta}=\mathsf{g}^{\beta\alpha}
.
The disadvantage of this method is that,
\nu
itself is a higher order term compared to
\phi
or
\zeta
, and often has large numerical errors near the separatrix. The numerical errors in
\hat{\delta}
term and therefore the error in the parallel current is amplified through (
D.3
) (
D.4
), and (
D.1
).

D.2
Calculating current using force balance

The total equilibrium current can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mu_{0}\mathbf{J}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}j^{\zeta}\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\zeta}+\mu_{0}j^{\theta}\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\theta},

(D.5)

where
\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\alpha}=\partial\mathbf{r}/\partial\alpha

is the covariant basis vector. Comparing it with the expression of
\nabla\times\mathbf{B}_{0}
in Boozer coordinate, we have
\mu_{0}j^{\theta}=-\partial_{\psi}g/\mathcal{J}
, then along with the equilibrium force balance equation
\mathbf{J}_{0}\times\mathbf{B}_{0}=\nabla p

we have

j^{\theta}q-j^{\zeta}=\frac{\partial p}{\partial\psi},

(D.6)

that is,

j^{\zeta}=-\frac{\partial p}{\partial\psi}-\frac{q}{\mu_{0}\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\psi},

(D.7)

and the parallel current can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\mu_{0}J_{\parallel 0}}{B_{0}}=\frac{\mu_{0}}{B_{0}^{2}}\left(gj^{\zeta}+Ij^{\theta}\right)=-\frac{dg}{d\psi}-\frac{\mu_{0}g}{B_{0}^{2}}\frac{dp}{d\psi}.\end{split}

(D.8)

This method is much more straightforward and numerically friendlier than
the first one. It only requires the two 1-dimensional derivatives
and does not need calculation of high order terms like
\hat{\delta}
or

\nu
. But it requires a fully consistent pressure profile with other
field quantities like
g
and
B
fields.

=&\frac{3P_{\perp e}^{tot}}{\mathcal{J}B_{0}^{4}e}\left[\frac{\partial\delta B_{\parallel}}{\partial\psi}\left(g\frac{\partial B_{0}}{\partial\theta}-I\frac{\partial B_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\right)+\frac{\partial\delta B_{\parallel}}{\partial\theta}\left(\hat{\delta}\frac{\partial B_{0}}{\partial\zeta}-g\frac{\partial B_{0}}{\partial\psi}\right)\right.\\
&\left.+\frac{\partial\delta B_{\parallel}}{\partial\zeta}\left(I\frac{\partial B_{0}}{\partial\psi}-\hat{\delta}\frac{\partial B_{0}}{\partial\theta}\right)\right]\\
&-\frac{1}{e\mathcal{J}B_{0}^{3}}\left[\hat{\delta}\left(\partial_{\theta}\delta B_{\parallel}\partial_{\zeta}\delta P_{\perp e}-\partial_{\zeta}\delta B_{\parallel}\partial_{\theta}\delta P_{\perp e}\right)\right.\\
&\left.I\left(\partial_{\zeta}\delta B_{\parallel}\partial_{\psi}P_{\perp e}^{tot}-\partial_{\psi}\delta B_{\parallel}\partial_{\zeta}\delta P_{\perp e}\right)+g\left(\partial_{\psi}\delta B_{\parallel}\partial_{\theta}\delta P_{\perp e}-\partial_{\theta}\delta B_{\parallel}\partial_{\psi}P_{\perp e}^{tot}\right)\right]\\

(E.18)

Here
P_{\perp e}^{tot}=P_{\perp 0e}+\delta P_{\perp e}
is the total
perpendicular electron pressure,
P_{\perp e}^{tot}=n_{0e}T_{e}+\delta P_{\perp e}^{na}+\delta n_{e}^{(0)}T_{e}+n_{0e}\partial_{\psi}T_{e}\delta\psi-n_{0e}T_{e}\delta B_{\parallel}/B_{0}
.

w_{\perp}^{nl}
represents the nonlinear magnetic flutter flow,

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{\perp nl}&=\delta\mathbf{B}_{\perp}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{n_{0e}\delta u_{\parallel e}}{B_{0}}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\left[\hat{\delta}\left(\partial_{\zeta}\lambda\partial_{\theta}\delta S-\partial_{\theta}\lambda\partial_{\zeta}\delta S\right)\right.\\
&\left.+I\left(\partial_{\psi}\lambda\partial_{\zeta}\delta S-\partial_{\zeta}\lambda\partial_{\psi}\delta S\right)+g\left(\partial_{\theta}\lambda\partial_{\psi}\delta S-\partial_{\psi}\lambda\partial_{\theta}\delta S\right)\right]\end{split}

(E.19)

where
\delta S
is the perturbed electron parallel flow,
\delta S=n_{0e}\delta u_{\parallel e}/B_{0}
.
In the continuity equation, we have dropped the terms at the order higher than
O(k_{\perp}L_{B})
, but we keep the second order derivative of equilibrium magnetic field

Appendix F
The implementation of Laplacian operator

In general curvilinear coordinate system, the Laplacian operator is expressed as in

\nabla^{2}f=\sum_{\alpha=1,2,3}\sum_{\beta=1,2,3}\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{\alpha}}\left(\mathcal{J}\nabla\xi^{\alpha}\cdot\nabla\xi^{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{\beta}}f\right),

(F.1)

where
\left(\xi^{1},\xi^{2},\xi^{3}\right)=\left(\psi,\theta,\zeta\right)
, and
\mathcal{J}
is the Jacobian,
f
is a perturbed field. Then for the Boozer coordinate system, we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f=

\displaystyle\mathsf{g}^{\psi\psi}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\psi^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\psi}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\psi}}{\partial\psi}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}}{\partial\theta}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}}{\partial\zeta}\right)

(F.2)

\displaystyle+\mathsf{g}^{\theta\theta}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\theta^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}}{\partial\psi}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\theta\theta}}{\partial\theta}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\theta\zeta}}{\partial\zeta}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathsf{g}^{\zeta\zeta}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\zeta^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\zeta}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}}{\partial\psi}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\theta\zeta}}{\partial\theta}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\zeta\zeta}}{\partial\zeta}\right)

\displaystyle+2\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\psi\partial\theta}+2\mathsf{g}^{\theta\zeta}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\theta\partial\zeta}+2\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\psi\partial\zeta}

Now we define the flux aligned coordinate
\left(\psi,\theta_{0},\zeta_{0}\right)
, with
\theta_{0}=\theta-\zeta/q
,
\zeta_{0}=\zeta
. The expression of the Laplacian operator in the flux aligned coordinates is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla^{2}f=&\mathsf{g}^{\psi\psi}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\psi^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\psi}\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\psi}}{\partial\psi}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}}{\partial\theta_{0}}+\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{0}}-\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\right)\left(\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}\right)\right]\\
&+\mathsf{g}^{\theta\theta}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\theta_{0}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{0}}\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}}{\partial\psi}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\theta\theta}}{\partial\theta_{0}}+\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{0}}-\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\right)\left(\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\theta\zeta}\right)\right]\\
&+\mathsf{g}^{\zeta\zeta}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{0}}-\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\right)^{2}f+\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{0}}-\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\right)f\times\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}}{\partial\psi}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\theta\zeta}}{\partial\theta_{0}}+\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{0}}-\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\right)\left(\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\zeta\zeta}\right)\right]\\
&+2\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\psi\partial\theta_{0}}+2\mathsf{g}^{\theta\zeta}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{0}}-\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\right)\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{0}}+2\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{0}}-\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\right)\frac{\partial f}{\partial\psi}.\end{split}

(F.3)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}f=&(\nabla-\mathbf{b}_{0}\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\nabla)\cdot(\nabla-\mathbf{b}_{0}\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\nabla)f\\
=&\nabla^{2}f-\mathbf{b}_{0}\mathbf{b}_{0}:\nabla\nabla f+\frac{\mathbf{b}_{0}}{B_{0}}\cdot[\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{B}_{0}](\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\nabla)f.\end{split}

(F.4)

It turns out
\nabla_{\perp}^{2}=\nabla^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
, where
\epsilon\sim r/R\sim k_{\parallel}/k_{\perp}\sim\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{\psi}/k_{\perp}^{2}\sim\mathsf{g}^{\theta\zeta}\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{\theta}/k_{\perp}^{2}
. Here we only solve the gyrokinetic Poisson equation to the first order, and the perpendicular Laplacian operator can be approximately obtained from full Laplacian operator by dropping all the second order terms,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}f=&\mathsf{g}^{\psi\psi}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\psi^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\psi}\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\psi}}{\partial\psi}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}}{\partial\theta_{0}}-\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\left(\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}\right)\right]\\
&+\left(\mathsf{g}^{\theta\theta}+\mathsf{g}^{\zeta\zeta}\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\theta_{0}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{0}}\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}}{\partial\psi}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\theta\theta}}{\partial\theta_{0}}-\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\left(\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\theta\zeta}\right)\right]\\
&-\frac{1}{J}\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{0}}\times\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}}{\partial\psi}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\theta\zeta}}{\partial\theta_{0}}-\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\left(\mathcal{J}\mathsf{g}^{\zeta\zeta}\right)\right]\\
&+2\mathsf{g}^{\psi\theta}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\psi\partial\theta_{0}}-2\mathsf{g}^{\theta\zeta}\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{0}}-2\mathsf{g}^{\psi\zeta}\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{0}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\psi}\end{split}

(F.5)

Appendix G
The construction of the Boozer coordinate system in the axisymmetric system

In the benchmark of the internal kink modes, it is proved that the self-consistent equilibrium, especially the consistency between the magnetic field and the parallel current, is important for the simulation accuracy. Here we describe a numerical algorithm to use the GEQDSK file to construct the Boozer coordinate system in the axisymmetric system. This algorithm is implemented using Python language and is used to generate the equilibrium data file for GTC simulations for the benchmark.
The information from GEQDSK file used to construct the Boozer coordinate system contains
\psi(R,Z),g(\psi),q(\psi)
, the last closed flux surface as a function of
(R,Z)
and the position of the magnetic axis
(R_{0},Z_{0})
. An open-source GEQDSK file parser using Python language can be found at
[
38
]
.

First of all, we can use the
\psi(R,Z)
data to get the function
R(\psi,\theta_{0})
and
Z(\psi,\theta_{0})
on
(\psi,\theta_{0})
grids. Here
\theta_{0}
is the geometrical poloidal angle,
\cos\theta_{0}=(R-R_{0})/\sqrt{\left(R-R_{0}\right)^{2}+\left(Z-Z_{0}\right)^{2}}
, where
(R_{0},Z_{0})
denote the position of magnetic axis. This can be done using the RBF(radial basis functions) inverse interpolation capability in the Scipy module
[
39
]
. Then poloidal magnetic field can be calculated from

\displaystyle B_{R}(\psi,\theta_{0})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{R}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial Z},

(G.1)

\displaystyle B_{Z}(\psi,\theta_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial R}.

The toroidal magnetic field can simply be written as
B_{t}(\psi,\theta_{0})=g/R
.

Then we can use the vector
(B_{R},B_{Z},B_{t})
to trace the magnetic field lines. For each flux surface, we can integrate numerically to get the relation between
\theta_{0}
and the toroidal angle
\phi
.

\displaystyle\theta_{0}=\int_{0}{d\phi\frac{RB_{p}}{rB_{t}}\frac{B_{Z}\cos{\vartheta}-B_{R}\sin{\vartheta}}{B_{p}}},

(G.2)

where
B_{p}=\sqrt{B_{R}^{2}+B_{Z}^{2}}
,
r=\sqrt{\left(R-R_{0}\right)^{2}+\left(Z-Z_{0}\right)^{2}}
,
\vartheta
is the local
\theta_{0}
value during the integration. Now we have the function of
\theta_{0}(\phi)
for arbitrary
\psi
.

Following the definition of Boozer coordinate, the field lines should be straight in the ’poloidal’ and ’toroidal’ plane, so we need to construct the angle
\theta^{\prime}
which satisfies
\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla\phi/\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla\theta^{\prime}=C(\psi)
. Using this relation, it’s easy to get the function of
\theta_{0}(\theta^{\prime})
for each flux surface and accordingly the functions
R(\psi,\theta^{\prime}),Z(\psi,\theta^{\prime}),B_{R}(\psi,\theta^{\prime}),B_{Z}(\psi,\theta^{\prime})
, and
B_{t}(\psi,\theta^{\prime})
.

Now the magnetic field satisfies the contra-variant form of the Boozer representation,
\mathbf{B}=q(\psi)\nabla\psi\times\nabla\theta^{\prime}-\nabla\psi\times\nabla\zeta_{0}
, where
\zeta_{0}=-\phi
. But we if we express the magnetic field following co-variant form
\mathbf{B}=\delta^{\prime}\nabla\psi+I^{\prime}\nabla\theta^{\prime}+g\nabla\zeta_{0}
, we will find that
I^{\prime}
depends and
\theta^{\prime}
. To eliminate the poloidal variation, we need another transformation on
\theta^{\prime}
and
\zeta_{0}
. From the co-variant form, we have

I^{\prime}=\mathbf{B}\cdot\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial\theta^{\prime}}=B_{R}\frac{\partial R}{\partial\theta^{\prime}}+B_{Z}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\theta^{\prime}},

(G.3)

and by flux averaging, we have
I=\oint{I^{\prime}d\theta}
. We define
\nu\equiv\int(I^{\prime}-I)d\theta^{\prime}
,
\theta_{b}=\theta^{\prime}+\nu
, and
\zeta=\zeta_{0}+q\nu
. The three coordinates
(\psi,\theta_{b},\zeta)
are the Boozer cooridnates. And we can get the equilibrium field quantities as functions of
(\psi,\theta_{b})
since we know the relation
\theta^{\prime}(\theta_{b})
. We can demonstrate that
\mathbf{B}=q\nabla\psi\times\nabla\theta_{b}-\nabla\psi\times\nabla\zeta
, and
\mathbf{B}=\hat{\delta}\nabla\psi+I\nabla\theta_{b}+g\nabla\zeta
, where
\hat{\delta}
can be simply calculated from

\hat{\delta}=B_{R}\frac{\partial R}{\partial\psi}+B_{Z}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\psi}.

An example of a constructed Boozer coordinate system is shown in Fig.
7
. The blue error bars in subplots (b) (c) (d) Fig.
7
shows the variation of quantities on one flux surface. These three subplots show the consistency between the covariant and contra-variant form of
B
field and also the invariance of
g,q,I
on flux surfaces. So the requirements of Boozer coordinate are satisfied.

The generated Boozer coordinate of DIII-D shot#178631. (a) Boozer coordinates on the poloidal plane. (b) Check the consistency between
q(\psi)
and
\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla\zeta/\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla\theta
. (c) Check the consistency between
I(\psi)
and
\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\theta}
. (d) Check the consistency between
\mathbf{e}_{\psi}\times\mathbf{e}_{\theta}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\zeta}
and
(gq+I)/B^{2}
.

Appendix H
Energy conservation and energy transferring rate

Following
[
40
]
, we can write out the nonlinear gyrokinetic electromagnetic energy invariant in the parallel-symplectic representation,

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{GY}=&\int d\mathbf{x}\int d\mathbf{v}f_{s}H_{GY}+\int d\mathbf{x}\left[-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu_{0}}\left|\mathbf{B}_{0}+\delta\mathbf{A}\right|^{2}\right],\end{split}

(H.1)

where
H_{GY}
is the gyrokinetic Hamiltonian expanded to the 2nd order of
e\delta\phi/T_{e}
,

\begin{split}H_{GY}=&\mu B_{0}+\frac{1}{2}mv_{\parallel}^{2}+Z_{s}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle-Z_{s}\left\langle\delta\mathbf{A}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{\perp}\right\rangle+\frac{Z_{s}^{2}}{2m_{s}}\left(\left\langle\left|\delta\mathbf{A}_{\perp}\right|^{2}\right\rangle+\left\langle\delta\tilde{A}_{\parallel}^{2}\right\rangle\right)\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left\{S_{1},Z_{s}\tilde{\psi}\right\}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(H.2)

\{,\}
is the unperturbed Poisson bracket in the guiding-center space, and

\begin{split}S_{1}&\approx\frac{q_{s}}{\Omega_{s}}\int\tilde{\psi}d\alpha\equiv\frac{q_{s}}{\Omega_{s}}\tilde{\Psi},\\
\psi&\equiv\phi-v_{\parallel}\delta A_{\parallel}-\delta\mathbf{A}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{\perp},\\
\tilde{\psi}&\equiv\psi-\langle\psi\rangle,\\
\delta\tilde{A}_{\parallel}&\equiv\delta A_{\parallel}-\left\langle\delta A_{\parallel}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(H.3)

The approximate definition of
S_{1}
stays valid as long as
\omega/\Omega_{c}\ll 1
. The Poisson bracket
\left\langle\left\{S_{1},Z_{s}\tilde{\psi}\right\}\right\rangle
can be written as

\begin{split}\left\langle\left\{S_{1},Z_{s}\tilde{\psi}\right\}\right\rangle&=\frac{Z_{s}^{2}}{\Omega_{s}}\left\langle\left\{\tilde{\Psi},\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{\Psi}\right\}\right\rangle\\
&=\frac{Z_{s}^{3}}{m_{s}\Omega_{s}}\left\langle\frac{\partial\tilde{\Psi}}{\partial\alpha}\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\Psi}}{\partial\alpha\partial\mu}-\frac{\partial\tilde{\Psi}}{\partial\mu}\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\Psi}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}\right\rangle\\
&=\frac{Z_{s}^{2}}{B_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left\langle\tilde{\psi}^{2}\right\rangle\end{split}

(H.4)

In GTC simulation we define kinetic energy and field energy as

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{k,s}=&\int d\mathbf{x}\int d\mathbf{v}f_{s}\left(\mu B_{0}+\frac{1}{2}m_{s}v_{\parallel}^{2}\right),\\
\mathcal{E}_{f}=&\int d\mathbf{x}\left[-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu_{0}}\left|\mathbf{B}_{0}+\delta\mathbf{A}\right|^{2}\right]\\
&+\sum_{s}\int d\mathbf{x}\int d\mathbf{v}f_{s}\left[Z_{s}\left(\left\langle\phi\right\rangle-\left\langle\delta\mathbf{A}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{\perp}\right\rangle\right)+\frac{Z_{s}}{2m_{s}^{2}}\left(\left\langle\left|\delta\mathbf{A}_{\perp}\right|^{2}\right\rangle+\left\langle\delta\tilde{A}_{\parallel}^{2}\right\rangle\right)\right.\\
&\left.-\frac{1}{2}\frac{Z_{s}^{2}}{B_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left\langle\tilde{\psi}^{2}\right\rangle\right].\end{split}

(H.5)

The last term in
\mathcal{E}_{f}
can be approximately calculated using the feature of equilibrium distribution function

\begin{split}-\frac{1}{2}\int d\mathbf{x}\int d\mathbf{v}f_{s}\frac{Z_{s}^{2}}{B_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left\langle\tilde{\psi}^{2}\right\rangle\approx\frac{1}{2}\int d\mathbf{x}\int d\mathbf{v}\frac{Z_{s}^{2}}{B_{0}}\frac{\partial f_{0s}}{\partial\mu}\left\langle\tilde{\psi}^{2}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(H.6)

For Maxwellian distribution

\frac{\partial f_{0s}}{\partial\mu}=-\frac{B_{0}}{T_{s}}f_{0s},

and for anisotropic slowing down distribution,

\frac{\partial f_{0s}}{\partial\mu}=-\left(\frac{3v}{v^{3}+v_{c}^{3}}\frac{B_{0}}{m_{s}}+4\frac{B_{a}}{m_{s}v^{2}}\frac{\left(\Lambda-\Lambda_{0}\right)\left(1-B_{0}\Lambda/B_{a}\right)}{\Delta\Lambda^{2}}\right)f_{0s}.

The energy conservation of the system can be verified by checking that
\sum_{s}\mathcal{E}_{k,s}+\mathcal{E}_{f}=const
.
When the particle moves in the phase space from
(\mathbf{R},\mu,v_{\parallel})
to
(\mathbf{R}^{\prime},\mu,v_{\parallel}^{\prime})
in a short time period
\Delta t
, we can write the change of the kinetic energy as

\begin{split}\Delta\mathcal{E}_{k,s}=&\int d\mathbf{x}\int d\mathbf{v}f_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime},\mu,v_{\parallel}^{\prime},t+\Delta t)\times\left(\mu B_{0}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}m_{s}v_{\parallel}^{\prime 2}\right)\\
&-\int d\mathbf{x}\int d\mathbf{v}f_{s}(\mathbf{R},\mu,v_{\parallel},t)\times\left(\mu B_{0}(\mathbf{R})+\frac{1}{2}m_{s}v_{\parallel}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(H.7)

Since the evolution of particle distribution function conserves phase shape density and volume, we have
d\mathbf{x}d\mathbf{v}f_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime},\mu,v_{\parallel}^{\prime},t+\Delta t)=d\mathbf{x}d\mathbf{v}f_{s}(\mathbf{R},\mu,v_{\parallel},t)
, and

\Delta\mathcal{E}_{k,s}=\int d\mathbf{x}\int d\mathbf{v}f_{s}(\mathbf{R},\mu,v_{\parallel},t)\times\left[\mu B_{0}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}m_{s}v_{\parallel}^{\prime 2}-\mu B_{0}(\mathbf{R})+\frac{1}{2}m_{s}v_{\parallel}^{2}\right].

(H.8)

The time derivation of
\mathcal{E}_{k}
is

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{E}_{k,s}}{dt}=

\displaystyle\int d\mathbf{x}\int d\mathbf{v}f_{s}\times\left[\mu\nabla B_{0}\cdot\frac{d\mathbf{R}}{dt}+m_{s}v_{\parallel}\frac{dv_{\parallel}}{dt}\right]

(H.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\mathbf{x}\int d\mathbf{v}f_{s}\times\left[\left.\frac{d\mathbf{R}}{dt}\right|_{B\perp}\cdot Z_{s}\left(-\nabla\left\langle\mathbf{\phi}\right\rangle+\nabla\left\langle\delta\mathbf{A}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{\perp}\right\rangle\right)-v_{\parallel}Z_{s}\frac{\partial\left\langle\delta A_{\parallel}\right\rangle}{\partial t}\right],

We have used the Hamiltonian to the first order in (
H.9
). And
\left.\frac{d\mathbf{R}}{dt}\right|_{B\perp}
is defined as the summation of the linear gyrocenter velocity and the magnetic fluttering velocity,

\left.\frac{d\mathbf{R}}{dt}\right|_{B\perp}=v_{\parallel}\frac{\mathbf{B}^{*}}{B_{\parallel}^{*}}+\frac{\mu}{Z_{s}}\frac{\mathbf{b}_{0}\times\nabla B_{0}}{B_{\parallel}^{*}}.

This time derivative of kinetic energy describes the energy transferring
rate from wave to particles, and we can verify the energy conservation by checking

\sum_{s}\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}_{f}}\frac{d\mathcal{E}_{k,s}}{dt}=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}_{f}}\frac{d\mathcal{E}_{f}}{dt}.

(H.10)

Wick and anti-Wick characterizations of linear operators on spaces of power series expansions
Joachim Toft

Abstract
. We study links between Wick, anti-Wick and analytic kernel operators on the Bargmann transform side. We consider classes of kernels, whose corresponding operators agree with the sets of linear and continuous operators on spaces of power series expansions, which are Bargmann images of Pilipović spaces. We show that in several situations, the sets of Wick and kernel operators with symbols and kernels in such classes agree. We also find suitable subclasses to these kernel classes, whose corresponding sets of Wick and anti-Wick operators agree. We also show ring, module and composition properties for such classes.

Introduction

A convenient way to represent linear operators acting on functions
defined on
the configuration space
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, is to apply the Bargmann
transform. In this process, these operators are transformed
into (analytic) integral operators
T_{K}
with
kernels
K
, or as analytic pseudo-differential operators

\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(a)
with symbols
a
, given by

\displaystyle T_{K}F(z)

\displaystyle=\pi^{-d}\int_{\mathbf{C}^{d}}K(z,w)F(w)e^{-|w|^{2}}\,d\lambda(w)

(0.1)

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(a)F(z)

\displaystyle=\pi^{-d}\int_{\mathbf{C}^{d}}a(z,w)F(w)e^{(z-w,w)}\,d\lambda(w),

(0.2)

respectively, when
F
is a suitable analytic function on
\mathbf{C}^{d}
,
where
d\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure
and
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
is the scalar product on
\mathbf{C}^{d}
.
Here
K(z,w)
and
a(z,w)
are analytic
functions or, more generally, power series expansions with respect to

(z,\overline{w})\in\mathbf{C}^{d}\times\mathbf{C}^{d}\simeq\mathbf{C}^{2d}
. (See
[
19
]

or Section
1
for notations.) The operator

\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(a)
is often called the Wick or Berezin operator with
(Wick) symbol
a
.

An important subclass of Wick operators are the anti-Wick operators,

\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}^{\operatorname{aw}}(a)
with suitable symbols
a(z,w)
being analytic with
respect to
(z,\overline{w})\in\mathbf{C}^{2d}
, and given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}^{\operatorname{aw}}(a)F(z)

\displaystyle=\pi^{-d}\int_{\mathbf{C}^{d}}a(w,w)F(w)e^{(z-w,w)}\,d\lambda(w),

(0.3)

where
F
is a suitable analytic function on
\mathbf{C}^{d}
. An important
feature for anti-Wick operators is that
\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}^{\operatorname{aw}}(a)
is
positive semi-definite when
a(z,z)\geq 0
for every
z\in\mathbf{C}^{d}
, which
is essential when performing certain types of energy estimates in quantum
physics. Any similar transitions on positivity from symbols to Wick operators are
in general not true. On the other hand for suitable
a
we have the expansion
formula

\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(a)=\sum_{|\alpha|<N}\frac{(-1)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}^{\operatorname{aw}}(b_{\alpha})+\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(c_{N})\quad\text{where}\quad b_{\alpha}=\partial_{z}^{\alpha}\overline{\partial}_{w}^{\alpha}a,

(0.4)

of Wick operators into a superposition of anti-Wick operators with error term

\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(c_{N})
, deduced in
[
32
]
. Here the first term

\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}^{\operatorname{aw}}(b_{0})=\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}^{\operatorname{aw}}(a)

on the right-hand side, possess the convenient positivity transition from
symbols to anti-Wick operators, while
c_{N}
is a linear combination of
expressions of the form

\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{|\alpha|-1}\partial_{z}^{\alpha}\overline{\partial}_{w}^{\alpha}a(w+t(z-w),w)\,dt,\qquad|\alpha|=N.

In several situations, this term is dominating, while
the reminder term
\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(c_{N})
is small compared to the other operators
in the expansion. (See e. g.
[
28
,
32
]
.)

1.1.
Gelfand-Shilov spaces

Let
0<s\in\mathbf{R}
be fixed. Then the (Fourier invariant)
Gelfand-Shilov space
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
(
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
) of
Roumieu type (Beurling type) consists of all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{S}_{s,h}}\equiv\sup\left(\frac{|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}f(x)|}{h^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\,\beta!)^{s}}\right)

(1.1)

is finite for some
h>0
(for every
h>0
). Here the supremum should be taken
over all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{N}^{d}
and
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
. The semi-norms

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathcal{S}_{s,h}^{\sigma}}
induce an inductive limit topology for the
space
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and projective limit topology for
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and
the latter space becomes a Fréchet space under this topology.

The space
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\neq\{0\}
(
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\neq\{0\}
), if and only if

s\geq\frac{1}{2}
(
s>\frac{1}{2}
).

The
Gelfand-Shilov distribution spaces

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
are the (strong) duals of
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, respectively.

Let
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{L^{2}}

be the scalar product in
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Then the duality between

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
can be obtained
by straight-forward extensions of the restriction of
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{L^{2}}

to
\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. In this setting we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{t}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(1.2)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{t}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\quad\frac{1}{2}<s<t.

Here and
in what follows we use the notation
A\hookrightarrow B
when the topological
spaces
A
and
B
satisfy
A\subseteq B
with continuous embeddings.

A convenient family of functions concerns the Hermite functions

h_{\alpha}(x)=\pi^{-\frac{d}{4}}(-1)^{|\alpha|}(2^{|\alpha|}\alpha!)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{1}{2}{|x|^{2}}}(\partial^{\alpha}e^{-|x|^{2}}),\quad\alpha\in{\mathbf{N}}^{d}.

The set of Hermite functions on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
is an orthonormal basis for

L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. It is also a basis for the Schwartz space and its distribution space,
and for any
\Sigma_{s}
when
s>\frac{1}{2}
,

\mathcal{S}_{s}
when
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
and their distribution
spaces. They are also eigenfunctions to the Harmonic
oscillator
H=H_{d}\equiv|x|^{2}-\Delta
and to the Fourier transform

\mathscr{F}
, given by

(\mathscr{F}f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\equiv(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\,dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

when
f\in L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Here
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
denotes the usual
scalar product on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. In fact, we have

H_{d}h_{\alpha}=(2|\alpha|+d)h_{\alpha}.

The Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}
extends
uniquely to homeomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and on
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Furthermore,

\mathscr{F}
restricts to
homeomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and on
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
and to a unitary operator on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Similar facts hold true
when the Fourier transform is replaced by a partial
Fourier transform.

Gelfand-Shilov spaces and their distribution spaces can also
be characterized by estimates of short-time Fourier
transforms, (see e. g.
[
17
,
29
,
35

1.3.
The Bargmann transform and spaces of analytic
functions

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
. Then the Bargmann transform
\mathfrak{V}_{d}f
of

f\in L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is the entire function given by

(\mathfrak{V}_{d}f)(z)=\pi^{-\frac{d}{4}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}(\langle z,z\rangle+|y|^{2})+2^{1/2}\langle z,y\rangle\Big)f(y)\,dy,\quad z\in\mathbf{C}^{d}.

We have

(\mathfrak{V}_{d}f)(z)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\mathfrak{A}_{d}(z,y)f(y)\,dy,\quad z\in\mathbf{C}^{d},

or

(\mathfrak{V}_{d}f)(z)=\langle f,\mathfrak{A}_{d}(z,\,\cdot\,)\rangle,\quad z\in\mathbf{C}^{d},

(1.40)

where the Bargmann kernel
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
is given by

\mathfrak{A}_{d}(z,y)=\pi^{-\frac{d}{4}}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}(\langle z,z\rangle+|y|^{2})+2^{1/2}\langle z,y\rangle\Big),\quad z\in\mathbf{C}^{d},y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(Cf.
[
4
,
5
]
.)
Here

\langle z,w\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}z_{j}w_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad(z,w)=\langle z,\overline{w}\rangle

when

z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{d})\in\mathbf{C}^{d}\quad\text{and}\quad w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{d})\in\mathbf{C}^{d},

and otherwise
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
denotes the duality between test function
spaces and their corresponding duals which is clear from the context.
We note that the right-hand side of (
1.40
) makes sense
when
f\in\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and defines an element in
A(\mathbf{C}^{d})
,
since

can be interpreted as an element
in
\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with values in
A(\mathbf{C}^{d})
.

It was proved by Bargmann in
[
4
]
that

is a bijective
and isometric map from
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to the Hilbert space

,
the set of entire functions
F
on

which fullfils

\|F\|_{A^{2}}\equiv\Big(\int_{\mathbf{C}^{d}}|F(z)|^{2}d\mu(z)\Big)^{1/2}<\infty.

(1.41)

Here

, where
d\lambda(z)
is
the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbf{C}^{d}
. The scalar product on

is given by

(F,G)_{A^{2}}\equiv\int_{\mathbf{C}^{d}}F(z)\overline{G(z)}\,d\mu(z),\quad F,G\in A^{2}(\mathbf{C}^{d}).

(1.42)

For future references we note that the latter scalar product induces the bilinear form

(F,G)\mapsto\langle F,G\rangle_{A^{2}}=\langle F,G\rangle_{A^{2}(\mathbf{C}^{d})}\equiv\int_{\mathbf{C}^{d}}F(z)G(z)\,d\mu(z)

(1.43)

on

1.4.
Wick and anti-Wick operators

Next we recall the definition of analytic
pseudo-differential operators, so-called Wick operators. Suppose that

a(z,w)\in\wideparen{A}(\mathbf{C}^{2d})

and
F\in A(\mathbf{C}^{d})
satisfy

w\mapsto a(z,w)F(w)e^{r|w|-|w|^{2}}\in L^{1}(\mathbf{C}^{d})

(1.54)

for every
r>0
and
z\in\mathbf{C}^{d}
. Then the
analytic
pseudo-differential operator
, or
Wick operator

\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(a)
with symbol
a
, is the linear and
continuous operator from
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbf{C}^{d})
to
A(\mathbf{C}^{d})
,
defined by the formula

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(a)F(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{C}^{d}}a(z,w)F(w)e^{(z,w)}\,d\mu(w),\quad z\in\mathbf{C}^{d},

(1.55)

when
F\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbf{C}^{d})
.
(Cf. e. g.
[
6
,
13
,
31
,
33
,
35
]
.) Here we remark
that
\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(a)F
is extendable in several ways, allowing

a
and
F
to belong to different spaces
where (
1.54
) is violated. For example, in

[
31
]
it is proved that the definition of
\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(a)

is uniquely extendable to any
a\in\wideparen{\mathcal{A}}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbf{C}^{2d})
,
and then
\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(a)
is continuous from
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbf{C}^{d})

to
\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbf{C}^{d})
.

The definition of the Wick operator in (
1.55
) resembles
on the definition of the classical Kohn-Nirenberg pseudo-differential operators
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. Let
A
be a real
d\times d
matrix.
Then the
pseudo-differential operator

\operatorname{Op}_{A}(\mathfrak{a})
with
symbol

\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
is the linear and continuous operator on

\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, given by

(\operatorname{Op}_{A}(\mathfrak{a})f)(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\iint\mathfrak{a}(x-A(x-y),\xi)f(y)e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}\,dyd\xi,\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

The definition of
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(\mathfrak{a})
extends to any
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,
and then
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(\mathfrak{a})
is continuous from
\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to
\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
(See e. g.
[
19
,
34
]
and the references therein.)

The normal (Kohn-Nirenberg) representation and the Weyl quantization
are obtained by choosing
A=0
and

respectively,
where

is the

In the literature it is also

(1.56)

for every

. Then the
anti-Wick operator

\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}^{\operatorname{aw}}(a)
with symbol
a
is the linear and continuous
operator from
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbf{C}^{d})
to
A(\mathbf{C}^{d})
, given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}^{\operatorname{aw}}(a)F(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{C}^{d}}a(w,w)F(w)e^{(z,w)}\,d\mu(w),\quad z\in\mathbf{C}^{d},

(1.57)

when
F\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbf{C}^{d})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Op}_{\mathfrak{V}}(a^{\operatorname{aw}})

(1.58)

when

is given by

\displaystyle a^{\operatorname{aw}}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\pi^{-d}\int_{\mathbf{C}^{d}}a(w_{1},w_{1})e^{-(z-w_{1},w-w_{1})}\,d\lambda(w_{1}).

(1.59)

Theoretical and experimental constraints on early-universe models in F ⁡ ( R ) F(R) gravity
Hua Chen, Taishi Katsuragawa, Shin’ichi Nojiri, Taotao Qiu

Abstract
This work investigates Early Dark Energy (EDE) scenarios as a potential precombination solution to the Hubble tension problem in the F ⁡ ( R ) F(R) gravity theory. We first develop a dimensionless quantity to visualize the density ratio between the EDE field and matter. Following existing scenarios, we then discuss conditions under which the Hubble tension could be alleviated by introducing a temporary injection of 10 % 10\% fractional energy around the time of matter-radiation equality between z = 10 3 − 10 4 z=10^{3}-10^{4} . We further confront these models with local gravity tests and find inconsistencies with observations. Taking a broader view, we convert constraints from local gravity tests into constraints for general EDE in F ⁡ ( R ) F(R) gravity. We eventually arrive at a no-go theorem for F ⁡ ( R ) F(R) gravity, which restricts nontrivial modifications to General Relativity in the early universe.



I
Introduction

The Hubble tension problem, which has been persistent in recent years, potentially implies new physics in the period of precombination
Di Valentino et al. 2021
;
Schöneberg et al. 2022
.
Among various proposals, the Early Dark Energy (EDE) scenario seems a promising precombination resolution to the Hubble tension
Karwal and Kamionkowski 2016
;
Poulin et al. 2018
;
Kamionkowski and Riess 2023
.
This scenario introduces a scalar field active only around matter-radiation equality (MRE).
Specifically, A
10\%
injection of fractional energy from the scalar field at MRE could effectively slow down the decay of the Hubble rate, resulting in a decrease of the sound horizon
r_{\mathrm{s}}
.
Because the angle subtended by the sound horizon
\theta_{\mathrm{s}}=r_{\mathrm{s}}/D_{A}
is precisely determined by the CMB measurements, the angular diameter distance decreases accordingly, leading to an increase in the Hubble constant.
When the scalar field overcame the Hubble friction at MRE, it started to roll down its potential and decayed faster than radiation.
Such a simple mechanism can significantly relieve the Hubble tension, although it requires fine-tuning in model parameters.
As a complement, the dark matter-coupled EDE model provides a natural trigger mechanism: when dark matter started to dominate the universe at MRE, it activated the dynamics of the scalar field
Karwal et al. 2022
.

As well as the EDE scenarios,
F(R)
gravity also introduces an additional scalar degree of freedom,
dubbed
scalaron
, compared with General Relativity (GR)
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2010
;
Nojiri et al. 2017
.
The scalaron has been extensively studied to explain the cosmic expansion’s early- and late-time acceleration associated with inflation and dark energy (DE).
With such successful applications, this work aims to explore the cosmological effect of the scalaron in the intermediate stage, which is far before and after the two acceleration periods.
In particular, we study the new physics of precombination in
F(R)
gravity, for which the scalaron acts as EDE in the same manner as the existing EDE scenarios.

Several works have utilized the
F(R)
gravity to tackle the Hubble tension
Schiavone et al. 2023
.
Nojiri
et al.

Nojiri et al. 2020
proposed a scenario unifying DE, EDE, and inflation.
Odintsov
et al.

Odintsov et al. 2021
;
Odintsov et al. 2023
utilized the EDE model proposed in Ref.
Nojiri et al. 2020
to study the Hubble tension problem and obtained a Hubble constant similar to the prediction in
\Lambda
CDM model.
Nojiri
et al.

Nojiri et al. 2022
proposed a saddle point condition as a remedy for the Hubble tension.
Schiavone
et al.

Schiavone et al. 2023
assumed a special solution of the scalaron field to produce a decreasing trend of the effective Hubble constant based on the idea of redshift-dependent Hubble constant inferred from a binned data analysis of the SNe Ia Pantheon sample within
2\sigma

Dainotti et al. 2021
;
Dainotti et al. 2022
.
They assumed a special solution, the
F(R)
function at the low-redshift limit and thereby reconstructed the
F(R)
function to be
F(R)\approx m^{2}B_{0}+B_{1}R+B_{2}R^{2}/m^{2}
, where
m
is a constant with the dimension of mass and
B_{0}
,
B_{1}
, and
B_{2}
are dimensionless constant.
Although phenomenologically successful, this model should also be compatible with other constraints, e.g., local gravity tests
Capozziello and Tsujikawa 2008
.
Especially, as we will show in this paper, a power-law form can easily cause problems since a small mass scale cannot protect the Ricci scalar going to infinity, i.e.,
\partial F/\partial R\propto R/m^{2}\to\infty
for
R\gg m^{2}
.

Our methodology, in this work, is to mimic the existing quintessential EDE scenarios by introducing new terms in the Lagrangian of
F(R)
gravity and investigate the parameter spaces allowing for injection of
10\%
fractional energy density around matter-radiation equality in between
z=10^{3}-10^{4}
.
Under this setup, we clarify all necessary conditions for a viable EDE model in
F(R)
gravity.
In particular, we develop a novel theoretical approach by constructing a dimensionless quantity, which enables us to visualize the density ratio of the scalaron and matter in a model-independent way.
We then utilize this quantity to study several benchmark models (including existing ones and new-proposed ones).
We find that these models can indeed produce the proper injection of fractional energy for appropriate parameter spaces.
However, we also find that local gravity tests exclude the parameter spaces.
On the other hand, the allowed parameter spaces diminish the precombination effect of EDE models in
F(R)
gravity.
The dilemma implies a potential no-go theorem of
F(R)
gravity as a precombination solution to the Hubble tension problem.
Furthermore, the difficulty of constructing viable models that pass local gravity tests leads us to investigate, conversely, the cosmological impact of local gravity tests on general
F(R)
models,
assuming the scalaron always followed its effective potential minimum.
We eventually propose a no-go theorem of
F(R)
gravity, which states that nontrivial modifications to GR are restricted in the early universe.

This paper is organized as follows:
In Sec.
II
, we introduce notations by briefly reviewing the basis of
F(R)
gravity.
In Sec.
III
, we clarify necessary conditions and construct dimensionless quantities; we then discuss a class of models in each subsection.
In Sec
IV
, we propose and prove a no-go theorem of
F(R)
gravity for EDE and general models.
Finally, we summarize our results and conclude this paper in section
V
.

II
Background evolution in
F(R)
gravity

The action of
F(R)
gravity is given by

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}F(R)\ +S_{\mathrm{Matter}}\,,

(1)

where
\kappa^{2}\equiv 8\pi G\equiv 1/M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}
, with
M_{\mathrm{Pl}}=2.44\times 10^{18}
GeV being the reduced Planck mass.

g
is the determinant of Jordan-frame metric
g_{\mu\nu}
,
and
S_{\mathrm{Matter}}
is the matter action describing Standard Model (SM) matter fields and dark matter.
For convenience, We define
F(R)\equiv R+f(R)
so that
f(R)
encodes all modifications to the GR.

Variation of the action with respect to the metric yields the modified field equation,

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}=\kappa^{2}T_{\mu\nu}+\kappa^{2}T_{\mu\nu}^{R}\,,

(2)

where
G_{\mu\nu}
is Einstein tensor,
G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}
,

T_{\mu\nu}
is the energy-momentum tensor for matter fields defined by

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{-2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{Matter}}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\,,

(3)

and
T_{\mu\nu}^{R}
is the effective energy-momentum tensor for the scalar degree of freedom (DOF), dubbed
scalaron
and given by

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{R}\equiv\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(F-R\right)g_{\mu\nu}+\left(1-F_{R}\right)R_{\mu\nu}+\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}F_{R}-g_{\mu\nu}\Box F_{R}\right]\,.

(4)

The equation of motion for the scalaron can be found directly from the trace of the field equation (
2
),

\displaystyle 3\square F_{R}(R)+F_{R}(R)R-2F(R)=\kappa^{2}T_{~\mu}^{\mu}\,.

(5)

Obviously, the higher derivative term
\square F_{R}(R)
is absent in GR, where
F(R)=R
and the equation reduces to
R=-\kappa^{2}T_{~\mu}^{\mu}
.

The action (
1
) can be rewritten in the form of scalar-tensor theories,

\displaystyle S=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\Phi R-U(\Phi)\right)+S_{\text{Matter}}\,,

where the scalar field
\Phi
,

\displaystyle\Phi=F_{R}(R)\,,

(6)

is directly coupled to the Ricci scalar, and the potential is given by

\displaystyle U(\Phi)=\frac{RF_{R}(R)-F(R)}{2\kappa^{2}}\,.

(7)

Sometimes, it is more intuitive to manifest the scalar DOF in the canonical form,

\displaystyle\tilde{S}=\int d^{4}x\ \sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\left[\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\tilde{R}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}(\tilde{\partial}_{\mu}\varphi)(\tilde{\partial}_{\nu}\varphi)-V(\varphi)+e^{-4\sqrt{1/6}\kappa\varphi}\mathcal{L}_{\text{Matter}}\right]\,,

(8)

through the conformal transformation,

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=F_{R}(R)g_{\mu\nu}\equiv e^{2\sqrt{1/6}\kappa\varphi}g_{\mu\nu}\,.

(9)

Here,
\varphi\equiv\sqrt{3/2}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}\ln F_{R}
is a new scalar field defined in the Einstein frame, and the potential is

\displaystyle V(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\frac{RF_{R}(R)-F(R)}{F_{R}^{2}(R)}=\frac{U(\Phi)}{F_{R}^{2}(R)}\,.

(10)

The energy-momentum tensor in both frames is related by

\tilde{T}_{\,\nu}^{\mu}=F_{R}^{-2}T_{\ \nu}^{\mu}
,
and so do the energy density
\tilde{\rho}=F_{R}^{-2}\rho

and pressure
\tilde{P}=F_{R}^{-2}P
.
One notes that the equation of state parameter
w=P/\rho
is frame-independent.
Assuming
w
is constant, the energy density conserved in the Einstein frame is given by
\tilde{\rho}_{\text{c}}\equiv\rho F_{R}^{-3(1+w)/2}
rather than
\tilde{\rho}
.
Note that the two frames are equivalent, but we should be careful about the physical interpretations.
However, because we are only concerned about the cosmic history after Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN), our analysis is almost frame-independent, as will be discussed in Sec.
III.1
.

The field equation in the Einstein frame is

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{R}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\kappa^{2}\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi)}+\kappa^{2}\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(11)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi)}\equiv-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{g}^{\rho\sigma}(\tilde{\partial}_{\rho}\varphi)(\tilde{\partial}_{\sigma}\varphi)+(\tilde{\partial}_{\mu}\varphi)(\tilde{\partial}_{\nu}\varphi)-\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}V(\varphi)\,.

(12)

In the flat FLRW spacetime, the energy density and pressure of the scalaron are then

\displaystyle\rho_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\varphi}{d\tilde{t}}\right)^{2}+V(\varphi)\,,

(13)

\displaystyle P_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\varphi}{d\tilde{t}}\right)^{2}-V(\varphi)\,,

(14)

where
\tilde{t}
is the cosmic time in the Einstein frame.
The Friedmann equation reads

\displaystyle 3M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}\tilde{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\varphi}+\tilde{\rho}_{\text{m}}+\tilde{\rho}_{\text{r}}\,,

(15)

where
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{m}}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{r}}
are the energy densities for matter (baryons and dark matter) and radiation, respectively.
It can be conveniently rewritten as,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varphi}+\Omega_{\text{m}}+\Omega_{\text{r}}=1\,,

(16)

where we have defined the density parameter,

\displaystyle\Omega_{i}\equiv\frac{\tilde{\rho}_{i}}{\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{cr}}}\equiv\frac{\tilde{\rho}_{i}}{3M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}\tilde{H}^{2}}\,.

(17)

In the Einstein frame, the equations of motion for matter fields and the scalaron are a coupled system,

\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{\rho}}{d\tilde{t}}+3\tilde{H}(\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{P})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{6}}(\tilde{\rho}-3\tilde{P})\frac{d\varphi}{d\tilde{t}}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\varphi}{d\tilde{t}^{2}}+3\tilde{H}\frac{d\varphi}{d\tilde{t}}+V_{,\varphi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{6}}(\tilde{\rho}-3\tilde{P})\,,

(19)

where the slope of the potential is

\displaystyle V_{,\varphi}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}\kappa}\frac{2F(R)-RF_{R}(R)}{F_{R}^{2}(R)}\,.

(20)

In other words, the total energy density is conserved,

\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{\rho}_{\text{tot}}}{d\tilde{t}}+3\tilde{H}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\text{tot}}+\tilde{P}_{\text{tot}}\right)=0\,.

(21)

Lastly, the effective mass squared of the scalaron is defined by

\displaystyle m_{\varphi}^{2}\equiv V_{\text{eff},\varphi\varphi}(\varphi_{\min})=\frac{1}{3F_{RR}(R_{\min})}\left(1-\frac{R_{\min}F_{RR}(R_{\min})}{F_{R}(R_{\min})}\right)\,,

(22)

where
R_{\mathrm{min}}
is the Ricci scalar responsible for the effective potential minimum and is evolving as
T_{~\mu}^{\mu}
evolves.

III
Hubble tension and Early dark energy

Before delving into constructing EDE, denoted by
f_{\text{ede}}
, in
F(R)
gravity, we first consider the scalaron dynamics in the early universe; by early, we mean the period much earlier than DE-dominant epoch with
R\gg R_{\mathrm{today}}
.
In the equation of motion for the scalaron (
5
) appears the trace of the energy-momentum tensor,
T_{~\mu}^{\mu}=-\rho+3P
, which behaves as an effective external force.
As shown in Ref.
Chen et al. 2022
, this term do not vanish even though
-T_{~\mu}^{\mu}\ll\rho_{\mathrm{r}}
deep into the radiation-dominated epoch.
So, at that time, the scalaron would have already been stabilized at its effective potential minimum, which is slowly evolving.
At this point, we assume that the scalaron evolution at the background level can be tracked through the time evolution of the effective potential minimum.
In other words, we only study the spatially averaged field minimum
\varphi_{\mathrm{min}}=\varphi(R_{\mathrm{min}})
by turning off the scalaron dynamics and evaluating
R_{\mathrm{min}}
through the stability condition,

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{min}}F_{R}\left(R_{\mathrm{min}}\right)-2F\left(R_{\mathrm{min}}\right)=\kappa^{2}T_{~\mu}^{\mu}\,.

(23)

Note that
F(R)>0
for
R>R_{1}
, where
R_{1}
is the Ricci scalar at a de Sitter point.
Likewise, it requires
F_{R}>0
and
F_{RR}>0
to avoid ghosts and tachyons for
R>R_{1}
.
We shall constrain model parameters theoretically using these three
stability conditions
.

Regarding the Hubble tension problem, we expect a non-negligible contribution of the scalaron to the cosmic energy budget between
z=10^{3}
and
z=10^{4}
, which peaks at MRE.
Specifically, we impose each component at MRE to satisfy

\displaystyle\rho_{\varphi}:\tilde{\rho}_{\text{m}}:\tilde{\rho}_{\text{r}}\approx 0.1:0.45:0.45\,.

(24)

With the scalaron playing the role of EDE means that its potential dominates over its kinetic energy, and therefore, we have
\rho_{\varphi}\approx V(\varphi)
and the density ratio of the scalaron and matter reads

\displaystyle\frac{V(\varphi_{\mathrm{min}})}{\tilde{\rho}_{\text{m}}}\sim\frac{2}{9}\,.

(25)

On the other hand, combining Eqs. (
10
) and (
23
) yields the density ratio,

\displaystyle\frac{V(\varphi_{\mathrm{min}})}{-\tilde{T}_{~\mu}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{U(\Phi_{\mathrm{min}})}{-T_{~\mu}^{\mu}}\approx\frac{U(\Phi_{\mathrm{min}})}{\rho_{\mathrm{m}}}=\frac{V(\varphi_{\mathrm{min}})}{\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{m}}}

(26)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2-R_{\mathrm{min}}F_{R}(R_{\mathrm{min}})/F(R_{\mathrm{min}})}-1\right)\,.

The first equality reflects that dimensionless quantities are frame-independent, as the equation-of-state parameter
w
, so our discussion applies to both frames.

-T_{~\mu}^{\mu}\approx\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
is valid after
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation, occurring much earlier than MRE. Hence, it applies to our current consideration.
The second line implies that the dimensionless density ratio can be translated into another dimensionless quantity defined by

\displaystyle r(R_{\mathrm{min}})\equiv\frac{R_{\mathrm{min}}F_{R}(R_{\mathrm{min}})}{F(R_{\mathrm{min}})}\approx 1+\frac{2V(R_{\mathrm{min}})/\tilde{\rho}_{\text{m}}}{2V(R_{\mathrm{min}})/\tilde{\rho}_{\text{m}}+1}\,.

(27)

It is more convenient to use
r
rather than the density ratio because
r
is a bound quantity, i.e.,
1\leq r\leq 2
; specifically,
r\approx 1
at matter-dominated epoch,
r\approx 31/17
today if we consider DE in
F(R)
gravity, and
r=2
at the de Sitter point.
In order for
V/\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{m}}\geq 2/9
at MRE, one requires
r\geq 17/13
.

III.1
Power-law EDE

We have constructed a dimensionless quantity
r(R_{\mathrm{min}})
to quantify the density ratio, assuming the scalaron follows the potential minimum in the period of interest.
In the following subsections, we shall apply this quantity to study several classes of EDE models.
We first consider the model with a power-law form given by

\displaystyle F(R)=R+\frac{1}{3n\left(n-1\right)m_{0}^{2(n-1)}}R^{n}\,,

(28)

where
m_{0}
is a mass scale to be determined.
The stability condition,
F_{R}>0
, constrains the parameter as
n>1
.
Substituting the model into Eq. (
27
) gives

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\frac{3n\left(n-1\right)+n\left(R_{\mathrm{min}}/m_{0}^{2}\right)^{n-1}}{3n\left(n-1\right)+\left(R_{\mathrm{min}}/m_{0}^{2}\right)^{n-1}}\,.

(29)

The asymptotic behavior of
r
reads

\displaystyle r(R_{\mathrm{min}})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1+\frac{1}{3n}\left(R_{\mathrm{min}}/m_{0}^{2}\right)^{n-1}\,,&R_{\mathrm{min}}\ll m_{0}^{2}\,.\\
n+3n\left(n-1\right)\left(1-n^{n-1}\right)\left(m_{0}^{2}/R_{\mathrm{min}}\right)^{n-1}\,,&R_{\mathrm{min}}\gg m_{0}^{2}\,.\end{cases}

(30)

It shows that the scalaron becomes negligible (
r\to 1
) for
R_{\mathrm{min}}\ll m_{0}^{2}
,
while
R_{\mathrm{min}}\gg m_{0}^{2}
,
r\to n
, implying a fixed density ratio at large curvature,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{V}{\tilde{\rho}_{\text{m}}}\right|_{R_{\mathrm{min}}\gg m_{0}^{2}}\approx\frac{n-1}{2\left(2-n\right)}\,.

(31)

To satisfy the condition
r(R_{\mathrm{min}}=R_{0})\geq 17/13
, the mass scale is constrained to be

\displaystyle\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{R_{0}}\leq\left[\frac{13n-17}{12n\left(n-1\right)}\right]^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\,,

(32)

where
R_{0}
is the critical Ricci scalar corresponding to

.
As shown in Fig.
1

However, the condition (
32

As shown in Fig.
2

n
goes to infinity.
It means
m_{0}^{2}\leq R_{0}
for all
n\geq 17/13
.
If we fix

by translating the experimental upper bound on the interaction range of the fifth force into the lower bound of the scalaron mass,

eV
Lee et al. 2020
,

32

III.2
Saddle EDE

In the previous section, it was demonstrated that the power-law term could not apply to the MRE epoch since its mass scale must be large (
m_{0}\gg\sqrt{R_{\mathrm{eq}}}
) in order to suppress the growth of
F_{R}
.
As suggested in Ref.
Nojiri et al. 2022
, to tackle the Hubble tension requires a saddle in the potential
V(R)
.
Hence, this section aims to develop possible saddle EDE models, or in other words, broken power-law models.

A general EDE model with a saddle potential
V(R(\varphi))
can be written as

\displaystyle f_{\text{ede}}=\frac{R^{n}\left(c_{1}R-c_{2}R_{0}\right)}{c_{3}R^{n}+R_{0}^{n}}\,.

(39)

where
c_{1},c_{2}>0
are dimensionless coefficients, and we fix the parameter as
R_{0}=4\times 10^{10}\Lambda
to roughly correspond to the Ricci scalar at MRE (

), with
\Lambda

to match

.
This model is similar to the Hu-Sawicki DE model; the difference is that

to

for model stability, where

Therefore, higher powers of the Ricci scalar in EDE are highly restricted since it may lead to

at

.
In this sense, a proper choice is
n=1
, and one can find two base models classified from their signs:

\displaystyle\text{Positive EDE:}\quad f_{+}(R)=\frac{c_{1}R^{2}}{c_{3}R+R_{0}}\,,

(40)

and

\displaystyle\text{Negative EDE:}\quad f_{-}(R)=-R_{0}\frac{c_{2}R}{c_{3}R+R_{0}}\,.

(41)

Generally speaking, there are vastly possible saddle models except for model (
39

We now investigate them separately.

Case 1:

IV
No-go theorem for EDE

In the previous section, we investigated possible scenarios of EDE in
F(R)
gravity. We found that all failed to pass the cosmological constraints from BBN and/or the experimental bounds from the violation of
the equivalence principle.
The problem lies in the field amplitude,
F_{R}(R)
, which is stringently constrained by experiments.
To quantify the density ratio in a model-independent way, we have assumed that the scalaron always followed the effective potential minimum during the period of interest; however, our results do not rely on this assumption.
For the power-was model,
F_{R}
is not prevented from tending to infinity if we fix the mass scale to explain the period of MRE. For the base saddle models, There is a residue constant in
F_{R}
, which diminishes the early-universe effect of the scalaron in order to pass the local gravity tests.

Although it is not a scrutiny for all possible scenarios, the difficulty of developing EDE in
F(R)
gravity leads us to reconsider the relationship of EDE,
F_{R}
(or
\varphi
), and experimental constraints.
We first summarize what conditions (but not all) a viable phenomenological
F(R)
model should satisfy:

1.

F(R),F_{R}(R),F_{RR}(R)>0
for
R>R_{\mathrm{1}}
, where
R_{\mathrm{1}}
is the maximal exact de Sitter solution.
Consequently,
F(R)
is a convex and monotonically increasing function, and
F_{R}(R)
is a monotonically increasing function.
The Jordan-frame scalaron field
\Phi\equiv F_{R}(R)
or the Einstein-frame scalaron field
\varphi\equiv\frac{\sqrt{6}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}}{2}\ln F_{R}(R)
is also a monotonically increasing function.

2.

RF_{R}(R)/F(R)\simeq 1
for the existence of a matter-dominated epoch.

3.

F_{R}\simeq 1
since today back to reheating when SM particles were created, for not too large variations of the mass of SM particles. A conservative theoretical bound applied to the BBN epoch is
(10/11)^{2}\lesssim F_{R}(R_{\mathrm{BBN}})\lesssim(10/9)^{2}
.

4.

\left|\varphi_{\mathrm{atm}}\right|<\left|\varphi_{\mathrm{galaxy}}\right|<10^{-15}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}
to pass the experimental bounds from the violation of the equivalence principle. It corresponds to
\left|F_{R}(R)-1\right|\ll 1
for matter density larger than the critical density of the galaxy.

We see that the power-law models fail to satisfy the third condition, and the saddle models fail to satisfy either the third or the fourth condition.

Assuming the scalaron always followed the effective potential minimum, the above conditions then point to a no-go theorem of
F(R)
gravity for general EDE scenario:
If there exists an EDE in cosmic history with matter density ranging from
\rho_{\mathrm{galaxy}}<\rho_{\mathrm{m}}<\rho_{\mathrm{atm}}
, then its energy density is negligible compared with matter density and can be neglected in the cosmic evolution.

The proof of this theorem is straightforward:
Firstly, the assumption that the scalaron followed the effective potential minimum enables us to estimate its background evolution by
\varphi_{\mathrm{min}}
, which is determined by matter density (or precisely speaking the trace of the energy-momentum tensor) through the stability condition (
23
).
Secondly, by EDE, we mean that the potential energy of the scalaron is larger than its kinetic energy; therefore,
\rho_{\varphi}\sim V(\varphi)
.
Thirdly, the experiments on the violation of the equivalence principle have placed stringent constraints on the field amplitude
\varphi_{\mathrm{min}}
responsible for matter density ranging from the atmosphere to galaxy (see the last condition).
The experimental bounds, i.e.,
\left|\varphi_{\mathrm{atm}}\right|<\left|\varphi_{\mathrm{galaxy}}\right|<10^{-15}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}
can be directly translated to matter density of cosmic background ranging from
\rho_{\mathrm{galaxy}}<\rho_{\mathrm{m}}<\rho_{\mathrm{atm}}
(see Fig.
11
for a comparison of matter density between local environment and cosmic background).
We always have
Therefore, we obtain that
\left|\varphi_{\mathrm{min}}\right|<\left|\varphi_{\mathrm{galaxy}}\right|<10^{-15}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}
,
resulting in a negligible deviation of the field amplitude from unity,
\left|F_{R}(R)-1\right|\ll 1
.
Fourthly,
F(R)
is a convex and monotonically increasing function, indicating no temporary sink in the
F(R)
function to decrease the denominator.
Finally, we find that
r(R(\varphi_{\mathrm{min}}))=RF_{R}/F\simeq 1
, or in other words,
\rho_{\varphi}\ll\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
during the period with matter density ranging from
\rho_{\mathrm{galaxy}}<\rho_{\mathrm{m}}<\rho_{\mathrm{atm}}
.

One observes that
\rho_{\mathrm{galaxy}}\ll\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(T_{\mathrm{MRE}})\ll\rho_{\mathrm{atm}}\sim\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(T_{\mathrm{BBN}})\ll-T_{\mu}^{\mu}(T_{\mathrm{BBN}})
.

The proof above is better understood by comparing EDE with the late-time DE scenarios in
F(R)
gravity.
For simplicity, we consider
F(R)=R-2\Lambda
, where the cosmological constant sources the late-time acceleration.
This model always has
F_{R}=1
and
r(R_{\mathrm{min}})=\frac{R}{R-2\Lambda}
.
one finds that
r(R_{\mathrm{today}})\sim 31/17
today, which contradicts our statement that
F_{R}(R)
points to
r\approx 1
.
The reason is that the Ricci scalar is decreased to be the same order as
2\Lambda
today, which significantly decreases
F(R)
in the denominator of
r
.
In the case of EDE, we have
F=R+f_{\mathrm{ede}}(R)
, where
f_{\mathrm{ede}}(R)
is characterized by a critical curvature
R_{0}
.
It is observed that the universe transitioned from high curvature
R\gg R_{0}
to low curvature
R\ll R_{0}
, and
F(R)
is a convex and monotonically increasing function. This implies that
f(R)\ll R
, even though the functional form of
f(R)
may be complex.
As a result,
r\approx 1
for the EDE scenario in
F(R)
gravity.

It should be noted that
\varphi\ll 1
in the period of our interest indicates that our analysis is almost frame-independent.
Moreover, our assumption is not unrealistic.
This is supported by Ref.
Chen et al. 2022
, which demonstrates that even though the scalaron was initially frozen in a position far away from the effective potential minimum due to the Hubble friction, the decoupling effect of SM particles from the thermal bath deep into the radiation-dominated epoch would effectively generate an external force, that drove it to the effective potential minimum.

As a ramification of the no-go theorem, the scalaron cannot be an EDE and contribute
10\%
fractional energy in the periods of MRE.
So, it cannot be a precombination remedy for the Hubble tension problem in the same manner as the ordinary EDE scenarios
Karwal and Kamionkowski 2016
;
Poulin et al. 2018
.

V
Conclusions

This paper has investigated several possible EDE models in
F(R)
gravity.
In particular, we have utilized these models as a recombination solution to the Hubble tension problem in the same manner as the existing EDE scenarios by imposing a

We considered two base models and found that saddle functions generally produce an unwanted residue constant in the
F_{R}

On the one hand, to pass local gravity tests and satisfy the BBN bound, the constant should be much less than unity.
On the other hand, to make the scalaron contribute nontrivial fractional energy density in the intermediate epoch, the constant should be in order of unity.
This dilemma leads to a breakdown of the
F(R)

We found that the problem mainly lies in
F_{R}(R)
, which led us to reconsider the correlation between local gravity tests and cosmic background evolution.
By summarizing the necessary conditions for a viable
F(R)
model and assuming that the scalaron always followed the effective potential minimum, we eventually proved a no-go theorem of
F(R)
gravity for general EDE scenario:
If there exists an EDE in cosmic history with matter density ranging from
\rho_{\mathrm{galaxy}}<\rho_{\mathrm{m}}<\rho_{\mathrm{atm}}
, then its energy density is negligible compared with matter density and can be neglected in the cosmic evolution.
As a ramification of the no-go theorem, we conclude that EDE in
F(R)
gravity cannot be a precombination remedy for the Hubble tension problem.

In this paper, we have only considered experimental bounds from tests of violation of the equivalence principle and theoretical bounds from the BBN epoch.
However, constraints from dense regions, such as the Earth and the Neutron star, must be considered.
It is thus attractive to constrain the functional form of general
F(R)
models describing the early universe by applying the existing experimental constraints to cosmology.
We will leave this exploration to future work.

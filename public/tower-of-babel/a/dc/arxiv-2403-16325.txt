Accumulated spectrograms for hyperuniform determinantal point processes
Makoto Katori, Pierre Lazag, Tomoyuki Shirai

Abstract
. We define the accumulated spectrogram associated to a locally trace class orthogonal projection operator and to a bounded set using the polar decomposition of its restriction on that set. We prove a convergence theorem for accumulated spectrograms along an exhaustion. We show that a radial determinantal point process on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} is always hyperuniform along the exhaustion formed by the dilations of a bounded open set, and as a consequence, we obtain that dilations of the corresponding accumulated spectrogram converge to the indicator function of the considered set, establishing thus a universal phenomenon. Our result is a generalisation of a theorem by Abreu-Gröchenig-Romero in [ 1 ] concerning time-frequency localization operators.

Introduction

In

We construct the equivalent of the accumulated spectrogram associated to an orthogonal projection and a bounded set

We formulate our main results in Section

The assumption of hyperuniformity we need can be expressed analytically, without linking it with the concept of determinantal point processes, see Remark

1.1.
Polar decomposition and accumulated spectrogram associated to a locally trace class orthogonal projection

The material we present here on polar decomposition is very standard, and can be found for instance in
[
18
]
, ch. 6. Our presentation is however quite detailed, to the extent that we will subsequently need elements of the construction. For the notion and properties of trace class operators, see e.g.
[
23
]
.

Let
S
be a locally compact, complete and separable metric space, and let
\lambda
be a Radon measure on
S
. We consider the separable complex Hilbert space
L^{2}(S,\lambda)
with inner product
\langle,\rangle
being linear in the first variable

\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{S}f(x)\overline{g(x)}d\lambda(x),\quad f,g\in L^{2}(S,\lambda).

Let
\Lambda\subset S
be a bounded Borel set and denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
the restriction operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}:L^{2}(S,\lambda)

\displaystyle\to L^{2}(S,\lambda)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}f,

where
\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}
is the indicator function of
\Lambda
. We let
\mathcal{I}_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(S,\lambda)
be the ideal of locally trace class operators on
L^{2}(S,\lambda)
, i.e. the ideal of operators
\mathcal{K}:L^{2}(S,\lambda)\rightarrow L^{2}(S,\lambda)
such that for any bounded set
\Lambda\subset S
, the operator
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
is of trace class. A locally trace class operator
\mathcal{K}
admits a kernel, i.e. a function
K:S\times S\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that for all
f\in L^{2}(S,\lambda)
and
\lambda
-almost every
x\in S

\mathcal{K}f(x)=\int_{S}K(x,y)f(y)d\lambda(y).

Let
\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{I}_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(S,\lambda)
be an orthogonal projection onto a closed subspace
L
of
L^{2}(S,\lambda)
. Since
L^{2}(S,\lambda)
is separable, we can consider a Borel set
S_{0}\subset S
verifying
\lambda(S\setminus S_{0})=0
and such that every function
f\in L
is defined everywhere on
S_{0}
. Such an operator defines a determinantal point process
\mathbb{P}
, see Section
2
below. For a bounded set
\Lambda\subset S
, the operator

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}

is of trace class and admits the spectral decomposition

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\otimes\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)},

where
\{\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset[0,1]
is the set of eigenvalues of
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}
ordered in the non-increasing order

\displaystyle 1\geq\mu_{1}^{(\Lambda)}\geq\mu_{2}^{(\Lambda)}\geq...\geq 0,

and
\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\in L^{2}(S,\lambda)
is the normalized eigenfunction of
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}
. The functions
\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}
form an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(S,\lambda)
. The representation (
2
) means that, for any
f\in L^{2}(S,\lambda)
, one has the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}f=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\langle f,\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}.

Remark 1.1
.

The representation (
2
) is essentially unique, in the sense that lack of uniqueness only arises for eigenvalues of multiplicity greater than one.

Consider now the operator

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}.

Its adjoint operator is the operator
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{K}
so that
\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}^{*}\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}
, and we have the polar decomposition

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}},

where
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
is the operator defined as follows: for
g=\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}}f
, we set
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}g=\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}f
, and we set
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}g=0
for
g\in\ker(\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda})=\ker(\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda})
. Since
(\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda})_{|\mathrm{ran}(\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda})}
is an isometry and since we have the orthogonal decomposition
L^{2}(S,\lambda)=\overline{\mathrm{ran}(\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}^{*})}\oplus\mathrm{ker}(\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda})
, the operator
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
is well defined. For
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

(5)

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}=\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)},

so that we can write the polar decomposition (
4
) as

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\sqrt{\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}}\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\otimes\Psi_{j}^{(\Lambda)},

meaning that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}f=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\sqrt{\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}}\langle f,\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle\Psi_{j}^{(\Lambda)},\quad f\in L^{2}(S,\lambda).

Observe that by construction, we have for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
that

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}=\sqrt{\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}}\Psi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}.

We associate to the operator
\mathcal{K}
and the set
\Lambda\subset S
a function in
L^{1}(S,\lambda)
called the
accumulated spectrogram
in the following way.

Definition 1.1
.

The
accumulated spectrogram
associated to
(\mathcal{K},\Lambda)
is the function
\rho_{\Lambda}\in L^{1}(S,\lambda)
defined by

\displaystyle\rho_{\Lambda}(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\Lambda}}|\Psi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}(x)|^{2},\quad x\in S_{0},

where
N_{\Lambda}
is the upper integer part of the trace of
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}
, i.e. the smallest integer that is larger than
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda})
.

1.2.
Main result

For
f\in L^{1}(S,\lambda)
, we denote by
||f||_{1}
its
L^{1}
norm. We are interested in accumulated spectrograms along an exhaustion of
S
.

Definition 1.2
.

An
exhaustion

\{\Lambda^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a family of subsets of
S
such that

\bullet

each
\Lambda^{(n)}
is a Borel relatively compact set;

\bullet

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, one has the inclusion
\Lambda^{(n)}\subset\Lambda^{(n+1)}
;

\bullet

the sets
\Lambda^{(n)}
cover
S
:
\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda^{(n)}=S
.

Our main result is the following. We refer to Section
2
below for the notion of hyperuniform determinantal point processes.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix an exhaustion
\{\Lambda^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
S
and assume that the determinantal point process associated to
\mathcal{K}
is hyperuniform along this exhaustion. Then one has the convergence in
L^{1}(S,\lambda)

(8)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{N_{\Lambda^{(n)}}}||\rho_{\Lambda^{(n)}}-K(\cdot,\cdot)\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda^{(n)}}||_{1}=0.

In the case where the state space
S
is the euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the Lebesgue measure
dx
and when the kernel
K
of
\mathcal{K}
is constant on its diagonal, Theorem
1.2
implies that dilations of the accumulated spectrograms form an approximation of the indicator function of a relatively compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
be a positive integer and consider the case
(S,\lambda)=(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
. Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded Borel set, let
K
be the kernel of a locally trace class orthogonal projection
\mathcal{K}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
such that the corresponding determinantal point process is hyperuniform along the exhaustion
\{R\Lambda\}_{R>0}
. Assume that
K
is constant on the diagonal and normalized, i.e. that we have
K(x,x)=1
for alomst all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We then have the convergence in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
:

\displaystyle\rho_{R\Lambda}(R\cdot)\overset{L^{1}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}

as
R\to+\infty
.

1.3.
The case of radial determinantal point processes on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

A particularly interesting case is the one of radial determinantal point processes on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
coming from orthogonal projection kernels. We define a radial determinantal point process as a determinantal point process having a radially symmetric correlation kernel.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a locally trace class orthogonal projection defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
with kernel
K
and let
\mathbb{P}
be the corresponding determinantal point process. We say that the point process
\mathbb{P}
is
radial
if there exists a function

\varphi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)

such that for alomst all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

|K(x,y)|^{2}=\varphi(||x-y||),

where
||x-y||
is the Euclidean distance from
x
to
y
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In particular, the correlation kernel of a radial determinantal point process is constant on its diagonal, and we assume that it is normalized such that
K(x,x)\equiv 1
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a radial determinantal point process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
coming from a locally trace class orthogonal projection. Then, for any relatively compact open set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have the convergence in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
:

\displaystyle\rho_{R\Lambda}(R\cdot)\overset{L^{1}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}

as
R\to+\infty
.

Corollary
1.4
is a direct consequence of Corollary
1.3
and the following Proposition asserting that any radial determinantal point process is hyperuniform along any exhaustion of the form
\{R\Lambda\}_{R>0}
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a radial determinantal point process on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
governed by an orthogonal projection operator. Then
\mathbb{P}
is hyperuniform along any exhaustion
\{R\Lambda\}_{R>0}
, where
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded open set with positive Lebesgue measure.

2.
Determinantal point processes

2.1.
Hyperuniform point processes

A configuration
\Xi
on
S
is a non-negative integers valued Radon measure on
S
. The space of configuration, denoted by
\text{Conf}(S)
, is again a complete separable metric space, and we equip it with its Borel sigma-algebra. A configuration
\Xi\in\text{Conf}(S)
is said to be
simple
if one has
\Xi(\{x\})\in\{0,1\}
for any
x\in S
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
point process
on
S
is a probability measure on
\text{Conf}(S)
. A point process is simple if it is supported on simple configurations.

See
[
5
]
for a general treatment of point processes. Informally, a point process is said to be hyperuniform if it has a small number variance. Here and in the sequel,
\mathbb{E}
denotes the expectation with respect to the point process
\mathbb{P}
and if
X\in L^{2}(\mathrm{Conf}(S),\mathbb{P})
,
\mathrm{var}(X)
denotes the variance of the random variable
X
, i.e.

\mathrm{var}(X)=\mathbb{E}\left[(X-\mathbb{E}[X])^{2}\right].

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a point process on
S
such that for any relatively compact Borel set
\Lambda\subset S
, we have
\Xi(\Lambda)\in L^{2}(\mathrm{Conf}(S),\mathbb{P})
. The point process
\mathbb{P}
on
S
is
hyperuniform
along an exhaustion

\{\Lambda^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
of
S
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\Xi(\Lambda^{(n)})]=\infty,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\Lambda^{(n)}))}{\mathbb{E}[\Xi(\Lambda^{(n)})]}=0.

2.2.
Determinantal point processes

Let
\lambda
be a Radon measure on
S
. For a simple point process
\mathbb{P}
on
S
and for a positive integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
n
-th correlation function with respect to
\lambda^{\otimes n}
, denoted by
\rho_{n}:S^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, if it exists, is defined by

(9)

\int_{\text{Conf}(S)}\overset{*}{\sum_{x_{1},...,x_{n}\in\mathrm{supp}(\Xi)}}f(x_{1},...,x_{n})d\mathbb{P}(\Xi)=\int_{S^{n}}f(x_{1},...,x_{n})\rho_{n}(x_{1},...,x_{n})d\lambda(x_{1})...d\lambda(x_{n}),

for any measurable compactly supported function
f:S^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Here, the sum
\overset{*}{\sum}
is taken over all pairwise distinct points
x_{1},...,x_{n}\in\mathrm{supp}(\Xi)
.

Definition 2.3
.

A point process
\mathbb{P}
on
S
is a
determinantal point process
on
(S,\lambda)
if there exists a kernel

K:S\times S\rightarrow\mathbb{C}.

such that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
n
-th correlation function exists and is given by

\rho_{n}(x_{1},...,x_{n})=\det\left(K(x_{i},x_{j})\right)_{i,j=1}^{n}.

See
[
4
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
for further background on determinantal point processes. A kernel
K
defines an integral operator
\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}:\hskip 2.84544ptL^{2}(S,\lambda)

\displaystyle\rightarrow L^{2}(S,\lambda)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(x\mapsto\int_{S}f(y)K(x,y)d\lambda(y)\right).

The following Theorem provides sufficient conditions for the kernel of a locally trace class operator to be the correlation kernel of a determinantal point process.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
17
]

[
24
]
,
[
21
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{I}_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(S,\lambda)
be a hermitian locally trace class operator. Then, its kernel
K
serves as the correlation kernel of a determinantal point process if and only if
0\leq\mathcal{K}\leq I
. In particular, any locally trace class orthogonal projection onto a closed subspace
L\subset L^{2}(S,\lambda)
gives rise to a determinantal point process.

For a determinantal point process
\mathbb{P}
with kernel
K
being the kernel of the integral operator
\mathcal{K}
and for a bounded Borel set
\Lambda\subset S
, we have from Definition
2.3
and formula (
9
) that

(10)

\mathbb{E}[\Xi(\Lambda)]=\int_{\Lambda}K(x,x)d\lambda(x)=\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda})=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}=\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}),

where
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}
were respectively defined by (
1
) and (
3
), and where the numbers
\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\in[0,1]
,
j=1,2,\dots
are the non-increasingly ordered eigenvalues of
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}
. The last equality in (
10
) follows the fact that the operators
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}
have the same non-zero eigenvalues. We also have

(11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\Lambda))=\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}-\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}^{2}).

Remark 2.2
.

For a determinantal point process given by a locally trace class operator
\mathcal{K}
and if
\{\Lambda^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
is an exhaustion, we then have that
\mathbb{P}
is hyperuniform along this exhaustion if and only if

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda(n)})=\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{K})=+\infty

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}((\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda^{(n)}})^{2})}{\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda^{(n)}})}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\sum_{j}(\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda^{(n)})})^{2}}{\sum_{j}\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda^{(n)})}}=1.

In the case when
\mathcal{K}
is an orthogonal projection, the reproducing property of its kernel

(12)

\displaystyle\int_{S}K(x,y)K(y,z)d\lambda(y)=K(x,z)

implies

(13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\Lambda))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\int_{S}|\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(x)-\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(y)||K(x,y)|^{2}d\lambda(x)d\lambda(y),

which also leads to another useful expression

(14)

\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\Lambda))=\int_{\Lambda}\int_{S\setminus\Lambda}|K(x,y)|^{2}d\lambda(x)d\lambda(y).

Observe that formula (
13
) implies a negative correlation property in the following sense: if
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}\subset S
are disjoint bounded Borel sets, then we have from the triangular inequality that

(15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\Lambda\sqcup\Lambda^{\prime}))\leq\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\Lambda))+\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\Lambda^{\prime})).

Also observe that from formula (
13
) or (
14
) and using the reproducing property (
12
), we have the upper bound

(16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\Lambda))\leq\mathbb{E}[\Xi(\Lambda)].

Inequalities (
15
) and (
16
) will be used in the proof of Proposition
1.5
.

3.1.1.
Preliminary results

Fix a relatively compact set
\Lambda\subset S
. For
x\in S_{0}
, we define the function

\displaystyle K_{x}:y\mapsto K(y,x),

and observe that we have

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}f(x)=\langle f,K_{x}\rangle

for all
f\in L^{2}(S,\lambda)
,
x\in S_{0}
.

We also set

\displaystyle G(x)=K(x,x)\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(x)-\langle\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}K_{x},K_{x}\rangle,\quad x\in S_{0}.

Observing that

\rho_{\Lambda}(x)-K(x,x)\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(x)=\rho_{\Lambda}(x)-\langle\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}K_{x},K_{x}\rangle-G(x),

we first estimate the left hand side of (
8
) with

(18)

\displaystyle||\rho_{\Lambda}-K(\cdot,\cdot)\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}||_{1}\leq||\rho_{\Lambda}-\langle\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}K_{\cdot},K_{\cdot}\rangle||_{1}+||G||_{1}.

Theorem
1.2
will now follow from Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
below, each of them estimating a term on the right hand side of inequality (

).

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\delta\in(0,1)
, set

(19)

\displaystyle C_{\delta}=\max\left\{\frac{1}{\delta},\frac{1}{1-\delta}\right\}.

Then, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, we have

(20)

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\Lambda}-\langle\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}K_{\cdot},K_{\cdot}\rangle\|_{1}\leq 1+2\delta\mathbb{E}[\Xi(\Lambda)]+2(1-\delta)C_{\delta}\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\Lambda)).

The second Lemma is a simple estimate for the
L^{1}
norm of
G
.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have

\|G\|_{1}\leq 2\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\Lambda)).

3.1.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We now prove Theorem
1.2
, assuming Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Fix a number
\delta\in(0,1)
and an exhaustion
\{\Lambda^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
S
such that the determinantal point process
\mathbb{P}
is hyperuniform along this exhaustion. By the inequality (
18
), Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we have that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle||\rho_{\Lambda^{(n)}}-K(\cdot,\cdot)\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda^{(n)}}||_{1}\leq 1+2\delta\mathbb{E}[\Xi(\Lambda^{(n)})]+2\{(1-\delta)C_{\delta}+1\}\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\Lambda^{(n)})).

Dividing this inequality by
N_{\Lambda^{(n)}}
and using the fact that the point process
\mathbb{P}
is hyperuniform along the exhaustion
\{\Lambda^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{N_{\Lambda^{(n)}}}||\rho_{\Lambda^{(n)}}-K(\cdot,\cdot)\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda^{(n)}}||_{1}\leq 2\delta.

Since
\delta>0
can be chosen arbitrarly small, the Theorem is proved.

3.1.3.
Proof of Corollary
1.3

For
R>0
, we have

\displaystyle||\rho_{R\Lambda}(R\cdot)-\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}||_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\rho_{R\Lambda}(Rx)-\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(x)|dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\rho_{R\Lambda}(Rx)-\mathbf{1}_{R\Lambda}(Rx)|dx.

Performing the change of variable
x\mapsto Rx
leads to

(21)

\displaystyle||\rho_{R\Lambda}(R\cdot)-\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}||_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{R^{d}}|\rho_{R\Lambda}(x)-\mathbf{1}_{R\Lambda}(x)|dx

(22)

4.
Proof of Proposition
1.5

4.1.
Hyperuniformity along balls and proof of Proposition
1.5

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer and let
\mathbb{P}
be a radial determinantal point on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, in the sense of Definition
1.3
. It will be seen in the proof of Proposition
1.5
below that it is sufficient to establish that the point process
\mathbb{P}
is hyperuniform along balls with linearly increasing radii. We state this fact in the following Proposition we prove further in Section
4.2
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, we denote by
B(x,r)
the open ball centered at
x
and of radius
r
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
\kappa>0
, the radial determinantal point process
\mathbb{P}
is hyperuniform along the exhaustion
\{B(0,R\kappa)\}_{R>0}
.

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and bounded. By the Vitali covering Theorem, see e.g.
[
8
]
, there exists a countable family of pairwise disjoint open balls
\{B(a_{k},r_{k})\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\Lambda
such that

\displaystyle\text{Leb}(\Lambda\setminus\sqcup_{k\geq 1}B(a_{k},r_{k}))=0.

We thus have for all
R>0
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(R\Lambda))=\mathrm{var}(\Xi(\sqcup_{k\geq 1}B(Ra_{k},Rr_{k})).

By the negative correlation property (
15
), we deduce that

(36)

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(R\Lambda))\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathrm{var}\left(\Xi\left(B(Ra_{k},Rr_{k})\right)\right)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathrm{var}\left(\Xi\left(B(0,Rr_{k})\right)\right),

where we used the translation invariance of the point process
\mathbb{P}
for the last equality. Dividing both sides of inequality (
36
) by
\mathbb{E}[\Xi(\Lambda)]
, we obtain

(37)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{var}(\Xi(R\Lambda))}{\mathbb{E}[\Xi(R\Lambda)]}\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\mathrm{var}\left(\Xi\left(B(0,Rr_{k})\right)\right)}{\mathbb{E}[\Xi(R\Lambda)]}.

We prove that each term in the sum on the right hand side of (
37
) goes to 0 as
R
goes to infinity. For any
k\geq 1
and any
R>0
, we have
\text{Leb}(B(0,Rr_{k}))\leq\text{Leb}(R\Lambda)
and thus

(38)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{var}\left(\Xi\left(B(0,Rr_{k})\right)\right)}{\mathbb{E}[\Xi(R\Lambda)]}\leq\frac{\mathrm{var}\left(\Xi\left(B(0,Rr_{k})\right)\right)}{\mathbb{E}[\Xi(B(0,Rr_{k}))]}.

By Proposition
4.1
, the right hand side of (
38
) goes to 0 as
R
goes to infinity. In order to apply the dominated convergence Theorem, observe that we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{var}\left(\Xi\left(B(0,Rr_{k})\right)\right)}{\mathbb{E}[\Xi(R\Lambda)]}\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Xi(B(0,Rr_{k}))]}{\mathbb{E}[\Xi(R\Lambda)]}=\frac{\text{Leb}(B(0,Rr_{k}))}{\text{Leb}(R\Lambda)}=\frac{\text{Leb}(B(0,r_{k}))}{\text{Leb}(\Lambda)},

and that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\text{Leb}(B(0,r_{k}))}{\text{Leb}(\Lambda)}=1.

The proof is complete.
∎

4.2.
Proof of Proposition
4.1

Proposition
4.1
will be a consequence of a geometric interpretation of the number variance given in terms of volumes of intersections of balls. This method is adapted from
[
7
, Prop. 1]
, concerning a family of determinant point processes in the Poincaré disk invariant under the action of Möbius transformations. We denote by
B(0,R)^{c}
the complementary set of the ball
B(0,R)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a radial point process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with correlation kernel
K
. Let
\varphi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be the function such that

\displaystyle|K(x,y)|^{2}=\varphi(||x-y||)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then we have for any
R>0
the exact formula

(39)

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(B(0,R))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(||x||)\mathrm{Leb}(B(0,R)^{c}\cap B(x,R))dx.

Proof.

From Formula (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(B(0,R))=\int_{y\in B(0,R)^{c}}\int_{x\in B(0,R)}\varphi(||x-y||)dxdy.

Performing the change of variable
x\mapsto x+y
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(B(0,R))=\int_{y\in B(0,R)^{c}}\int_{x\in B(y,R)}\varphi(||x||)dxdy.

By Fubini’s Theorem and since we have

\displaystyle y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,R),\hskip 2.84544ptx\in B(y,R)\hskip 2.84544pt\Leftrightarrow\hskip 2.84544ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\},\hskip 2.84544pty\in B(0.R)^{c}\cap B(x,R),

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(B(0,R))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(||x||)\mathrm{Leb}(B(0,R)^{c}\cap B(x,R))dx,

and the proof is complete.
∎

The intersection volume can be explicitly computed. For a positive integer
d
, we denote by
c_{d}
the volume of the unit ball
B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

c_{d}=\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2+1)}.

Proposition 4.3
.

For all
R>0
and all
x\in B(0,2R)
, we have

(40)

\mathrm{Leb}(B(0,R)^{c}\cap B(x,R))=2c_{d-1}R^{d}.\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)_{k}}{(2k+1)k!}\left(\frac{||x||}{2R}\right)^{2k+1},

where we use the Pochhammer symbol

\displaystyle(\alpha)_{k}=\alpha(\alpha+1)...(\alpha+k-1).

Proof.

By symmetry, we can assume that
x=r(1,0,...,0)^{t}
, where
0\leq r<2R
. We obviously have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Leb}(B(0,R)^{c}\cap B(x,R))=\text{Leb}(B(0,R))-\text{Leb}(B(x,R)\cap B(0,R)).

Besides, we have

\displaystyle y\in B(0,R)\cap B(x,R)\Leftrightarrow x/2-y\in B(0,R)\cap B(x,R),

and also

\displaystyle y=(y_{1},...,y_{d})^{t}\in B(0,R),\hskip 2.84544pty_{1}\geq r/2\Rightarrow y\in B(x,R).

This implies

(41)

\mathrm{Leb}(B(0,R)\cap B(x,R))=2\text{Leb}(y=(y_{1},...,y_{d})^{t}\in B(0,R),\hskip 2.84544pty_{1}\geq r/2).

For
s\in[r/2,R]
, we denote by
H_{s}
the affine hyperplane
\{y=(y_{1},...,y_{d})^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\hskip 2.84544pty_{1}=s\}
. The intersection
H_{s}\cap B(0,R)\cap B(x,R)=H_{s}\cap B(0,R)
is a
(d-1)
-ball of radius
\sqrt{R^{2}-s^{2}}
and with
(d-1)
-Lebesgue measure

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sqrt{R^{2}-s^{2}}^{d-1}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d-1}{2}+1\right)}=c_{d-1}\sqrt{R^{2}-s^{2}}^{d-1}.

We obtain from equation (
41
) that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Leb}(B(0,R)\cap B(x,R))

\displaystyle=2c_{d-1}\int_{r/2}^{R}\sqrt{R^{2}-s^{2}}^{d-1}ds=2c_{d-1}R^{d-1}\int_{r/2}^{R}\sqrt{1-(s/R)^{2}}^{d-1}ds

Performing the change of variable
u=s/R
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Leb}(B(0,R)\cap B(x,R))

\displaystyle=2c_{d-1}R^{d}\int_{r/2R}^{1}\left(1-u^{2}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}du

\displaystyle=2c_{d-1}R^{d}\int_{r/2R}^{1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)_{k}}{k!}u^{2k}\right)du

\displaystyle=2c_{d-1}R^{d}\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)_{k}}{(2k+1)k!}\left(1-\left(\frac{r}{2R}\right)^{2k+1}\right)\right\}.

The intervertion sum integral performed on the last line is justified since the series

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)_{k}}{k!}

is convergent, either as a finite sum when
d
is odd, while for even
d
, one has by Stirling formula

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)_{k}}{k!}=\frac{\Gamma\left(k-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)\Gamma(k+1)}\underset{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}\frac{k^{-(d-1)/2-1}}{\Gamma\left(-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)}.

With
r=0
, we obtain in particular the expression

\displaystyle\text{Leb}(B(0,R))=2c_{d-1}R^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)_{k}}{(2k+1)k!},

from which we deduce

\displaystyle\text{Leb}(B(0,R)^{c}\cap B(x,R))

\displaystyle=2c_{d-1}R^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)_{k}}{(2k+1)k!}\left(\frac{r}{2R}\right)^{2k+1},

and the proof is complete.
∎

We can now conclude the proof of Proposition
4.1
. For simplicity, we take
\kappa=1
and prove that the radial point process
\mathbb{P}
is hyperuniform along the exhaustion
\{B(0,R)\}_{R>0}
, the proof for the exhaustion
\{B(0,R\kappa)\}_{R>0}
being similar. We first use Proposition
4.2
and write, for

\frac{\mathrm{var}(\Xi(B(0,R))}{\mathbb{E}[\Xi(B(0,R)]}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Leb}(B(0,R))}\int_{B(0,2R)}\varphi(||x||)\mathrm{Leb}(B(0,R)^{c}\cap B(x,R))dx\\
+\int_{B(0,2R)^{c}}\varphi(||x||)dx.

By the reproducing property (
12
), the integral

(43)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(||x||)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K(x,0)|^{2}dx=K(0,0)=1

is finite, and thus

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,2R)^{c}}\varphi(||x||)dx\to 0

as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{var}(\Xi(B(0,R)))}{\mathbb{E}[\Xi(B(0,R)]}=2dc_{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)_{k}}{(2k+1)k!}\frac{1}{(2R)^{2k+1}}\int_{0}^{2R}r^{2k+1}r^{d-1}\varphi(r)dr+o(1).

The constant factor

(d-1)

given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{d-1}=dc_{d}.

We now use the following Lemma in order to treat the integrals

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
g\in L^{1}([0,+\infty),dr)
and let
\eta>0
. Then we have

(45)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{(2R)^{\eta}}\int_{0}^{2R}r^{\eta}g(r)dr=0.

Proof.

We use the fact that bounded compactly supported functions are dense in
L^{1}([0,+\infty),dr)
. Let

and let

be bounded and compactly supported such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}|g(r)-g_{0}(r)|dr\leq\varepsilon.

We now write

(47)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2R)^{\eta}}\left|\int_{0}^{2R}r^{\eta}g(r)dr\right|\leq\frac{1}{(2R)^{\eta}}\int_{0}^{2R}r^{\eta}|g(r)-g_{0}(r)|dr+\frac{1}{(2R)^{\eta}}\int_{0}^{2R}r^{\eta}|g_{0}(r)|dr.

Since the function
g_{0}
is bounded and compactly supported, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2R)^{\eta}}\int_{0}^{2R}r^{\eta}|g_{0}(r)|dr\to 0

as
R\to+\infty
. Since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2R)^{\eta}}\int_{0}^{2R}r^{\eta}|g(r)-g_{0}(r)|dr\leq\int_{0}^{2R}|g(r)-g_{0}(r)|dr

and recalling inequalities (
46
) and (
47
), we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{(2R)^{\eta}}\left|\int_{0}^{2R}r^{\eta}g(r)dr\right|\leq\varepsilon,

and the proof is complete.
∎

We apply Lemma
4.4
to the function

, which is integrable by formula (

Remark 4.5
.

One could ask, given a function
\varphi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
, whether there exists a kernel
K
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
of an orthogonal projection operator such that
|K(x,y)|^{2}=\varphi(||x-y||)
. A necessary condition is obtained from the reproducing property (
12
) for the kernel
K
that implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(||x-y||)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(||x||)dx=dc_{d}\int_{0}^{+\infty}r^{d-1}\varphi(r)dr=\varphi(0)^{1/2}.

\lambda^{\prime}
.

Definition 5.1
.

A
partial isometry
between
L^{2}(S,\lambda)
and
L^{2}(S^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
is a linear map

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}:L^{2}(S,\lambda)\to L^{2}(S^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})

such that

\displaystyle||\mathcal{W}f||_{L^{2}(S^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})}=||f||_{L^{2}(S,\lambda)}

for all
f
in the orthogonal complement of
\ker\mathcal{W}
.

If the linear map
\mathcal{W}:L^{2}(S,\lambda)\to L^{2}(S^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
is a locally Hilbert-Schmidt partial isometry, then the operator
\mathcal{K}:=\mathcal{W}^{*}\mathcal{W}
on
L^{2}(S,\lambda)
is the locally trace class orthogonal projection onto the closure of the range of
\mathcal{W}^{*}
, which is the orthogonal complement of the kernel of
\mathcal{W}
.

For a relatively compact subset
\Lambda\subset S
, we define the self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{W}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}^{*}
and consider its spectral decomposition

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}:=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}h_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\otimes h_{j}^{(\Lambda)}.

Recall that the functions
\Psi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}
together with the accumulated spectrogram
\rho_{\Lambda}
associated to
(\mathcal{K},\Lambda)
have been defined through the polar decomposition of
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
. In such a context, wa have another interpretation of the accumulated spectrogram.

Proposition 5.1
.

We have

(48)

\displaystyle\rho_{\Lambda}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\Lambda}}|\mathcal{W}^{*}h_{j}^{(\Lambda)}(x)|^{2}.

Proof.

We first notice that the spectral decomposition of the trace class hermitian operator
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{K}
can be written as

(49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{K}=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\Psi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\otimes\Psi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}.

Indeed, the operators
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
have the same non-zero eigenvalues, and for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu_{j}^{\Lambda}\neq 0
, we have from (
7
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{K}\Psi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}}}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda})\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}}}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda})\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\Phi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}=\mu_{j}^{(\Lambda)}\Psi_{j}^{(\Lambda)}.

The claim now follows from the fact that we have the conjugation

(50)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{W}^{*}(\mathcal{W}\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}^{*})\mathcal{W}.

∎

5.1.2.

Its adjoint operator is given by

\displaystyle V_{g}^{*}F(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F(x,\xi)g(t-x)e^{2i\pi\xi\cdot t}dxd\xi,\quad F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),\hskip 2.84544ptt\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For a bounded set

5.1.3.
The Heisenberg family of determinantal point processes

The state space is now
\mathbb{C}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, equipped with the euclidean norm
|z|^{2}=|z_{1}|^{2}+\dots+|z_{d}|^{2}
for
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{d})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
and the Lebesgue measure
dm(z)
. Taking
g
to be the normalized gaussian function

\displaystyle g(x)=2^{d/4}\exp\left(-\pi||x||^{2}\right),

and writing
z=x+i\xi
, we obtain that the kernel
K
of the operator
\mathcal{K}=V_{g}V_{g}^{*}
defined in the preceding section is given by

\displaystyle K(z,w)=e^{\pi(z\cdot\overline{w}-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2})},

so that

\displaystyle|K(z,w)|^{2}=e^{-\pi|z-w|^{2}}.

Up to the change of the measure
dm(z)\mapsto e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}dm(z)
, the kernel
K
is the reproducing kernel of the Bargmann-Fock space. We let
\mathbb{P}
be the radial determinantal point process with kernel
K
. In the case
d=1
, the point process
\mathbb{P}
is nothing but the infinite Ginibre enemble (
[
9
]
). Several generalizations of these point processes have been considered, such as determinantal point processes corresponding to poly-analytic Fock spaces or higher Landau levels (
[
20
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
16
]
), as well as finite versions (
[
2
]
,
[
12
]
), establishing hyperuniformity along balls. The name
Heisenberg
is justified from relations with the representation theory of the Heisenberg group. Note also that
[
13
]
and
[
16
]
give a full asymptotic expansion for the number variance of particles inside a ball of large radius.

5.2.
The Euclidean family of determinantal point processes associated to Paley-Wiener spaces

We derscribe here what we call the
Euclidean family of determinantal point processes
, according to the terminology of
[
14
]
, see also
[
27
]
. The term
Euclidean
refers to the invariance by the group of Euclidean motions of this family of determinantal point processes, i.e. they are both rotationally invariant and translation invariant. These radial determinantal point processes were also studied in
[
19
]
,
[
26
]
under the name
Fermi-sphere ensembles
. They also appear in the context of semi-classical analysis in
[
6
]
,
[
15
]
, see also
[
27
]
. Below, we use the method of the proof of Proposition
4.1
to that example to derive the first order asymptotics for the number variance of particles inside a ball of large radius.

We now give a proper defintion. We let
\mathcal{F}
be the Fourier transform on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-i\xi\cdot x}dx.

The Paley-Wiener space is defined as the subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
of functions having their Fourier transform supported in the unit ball
B(0,1)
. The projection operator onto the Paley-Wiener space is the operator
\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{B(0,1)}\mathcal{F}
and its kernel reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{d}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{B(0,1)}e^{i\xi\cdot(x-y)}d\xi=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\frac{J_{d/2}(||x-y||)}{||x-y||^{d/2}},

where
J_{d/2}
is the Bessel function of order
d/2
of the first kind

\displaystyle J_{d/2}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!\Gamma(1+d/2+k)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2k+d/2}.

Remark 5.2
.

If, instead of considering the unit ball
B(0,1)
, one considers a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is bounded and rotationally symmetric, i.e. if
OA=A
for any orthogonal transformation
O\in O(d)
, then the operator
\mathcal{K}_{A}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{A}\mathcal{F}
is a locally trace class orthogonal projection on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
with kernel

\displaystyle K_{A}(x,y)\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{A}e^{i\xi\cdot(x-y)}d\xi,

that is also radially symmetric. Thus, the operator
\mathcal{K}_{A}
gives rise to another example of a radial determinantal point process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Definition 5.2
.

The
Euclidean family of determinantal point processes

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=1,2,\dots
, is formed by the radial determinantal point processes on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with correlation kernel
\mathcal{S}_{d}
.

For
d=1
, one recovers the usual Paley-Wiener space and the Dyson’s sine process with kernel

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}(x,y)=\frac{\sin(x-y)}{\pi(x-y)}.

The hyperuniformity of the Euclidean family of determinantal point processes
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}
follows from the genral Propositions
1.5
and
4.1
. Using known asymptotics of the Bessel functions allows to obtain a more precise statement than Proposition
4.1
given by the following Proposition.

Proposition 5.3
.

As
R\to+\infty
, we have the asymptotics

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(B(0,R))=\frac{1}{2^{d-1}\pi^{3/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}\log(R)R^{d-1}+O(R^{d-1}).

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle g(r)=\frac{J_{d/2}(r)^{2}}{r},\quad r\geq 0,

so that Equation (
42
) reads in that particular case

(53)

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(B(0,R))

\displaystyle=\frac{2c_{d-1}\sigma_{d-1}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)_{k}}{k!(2k+1)}\frac{R^{d}}{(2R)^{2k+1}}\int_{0}^{2R}r^{2k+1}g(r)dr

\displaystyle+c_{d}\sigma_{d-1}R^{d}\int_{2R}^{+\infty}g(r)dr.

As
r\to+\infty
, we have the following asymptotics for the Bessel function

\displaystyle J_{d/2}(r)^{2}\sim\frac{1+\cos\left(2r-\frac{(d-1)\pi}{2}\right)}{\pi r},

from which we deduce that

(54)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2R}rg(r)dr=\frac{1}{\pi}\log(R)+O(1),

as well as

(55)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2R}r^{2k+1}g(r)dr=O(R^{2k})

uniformly for
k\geq 1
, and finally

(56)

\displaystyle\int_{2R}^{+\infty}g(r)dr=O(R^{-1}).

Inserting the estimates (
54
), (
55
) and (
56
) into Equation (
53
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\Xi(B(0,R))=\frac{c_{d-1}\sigma_{d-1}}{2^{d-1}\pi^{d+1}}R^{d-1}\log(R)+O(R^{d-1}).

The constant factor is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{c_{d-1}\sigma_{d-1}}{2^{d-1}\pi^{d+1}}=\frac{1}{2^{d-1}\pi^{3/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)},

which concludes the proof.
∎

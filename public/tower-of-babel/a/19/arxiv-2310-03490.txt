Prohibited Minors for Graphic Matroids that gives a Binary Gammoid after Splitting


Abstract
. Splitting operation in Matroid Theory does not preserve graphicness, connectedness, cographicness, etc. Also, the splitting of binary gammoid does not necessarily be binary gammoid after splitting. We have characterized a class of graphic matroids that gives binary gammoids after splitting. We have obtained prohibited minors for graphic and cographic matroid which gives binary gammoid after splitting using two and three elements.

In this section, we obtain prohibited minors for graphic matroids whose element splitting and es-splitting is a binary gammoid.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
B
be a graphic matroid. Then
B_{X}^{\prime}
is a binary gammoid if and only if

is not a minor of

, where

is as shown in Figure
7

X\subseteq E(B)
with

.

Proof.

Suppose, a graphic matroid
B
has a
M(G_{7})
minor, then proving
B_{X}^{\prime}
is not a binary gammoid is easy, for
X=\{x,y\}
. Conversely, suppose
B
does not contain
M(G_{7})
, then we will prove that
B_{X}^{\prime}
is a gammoid. On the contrary suppose not, then
B_{X}^{\prime}
will contain
M(K_{4})

. Thus,

. There are three cases as given below.

then

, thus

since

. Thus

Case (ii). If

5.
Cographic Matroids Whose splitting is a Binary Gammoid

We now characterize cographic matroid whose splitting is a binary gammoid. In section 3 we have characterized graphic matroids whose splitting is binary gammoid. The prohibited minors for cographic matroids whose splitting is a binary gammoid will depend on the quotients of
M(K_{4})
. Note that

can have graphic and non-graphic quotients. In Section 2 we have obtained all graphic quotients of

Lemma 5.1
.

All quotients of
M(K_{4})
are graphic.

Proof.

Suppose
Z
be a binary matroid with

, for
q\in E(Z)
. Let the quotient of
M(K_{4})
be denoted by
Q=Z/q
.
Case (i). If
q
is a loop or a coloop then
Z\backslash q=M(K_{4})
then
Z\backslash q=Z/q=M(K_{4})
. Here
M(K_{4})
is graphic.
Case (ii). If
q
is not a loop or a coloop. As
r(Z\backslash q)=3
and
E(Z\backslash q)=6
then
r(Z)=3
and
E(Z)=7
. Thus,
r(Q)=2
and
E(Q)=6
. Suppose
Q
is not graphic. Then, by Theorem
2.2
,
Q
contains minor

. A contradiction, as
r(Q)=2

. Hence

Thus from both cases, we say that all the quotients of

are graphic.
∎

In section 3 we have obtained prohibited minor from the graphic quotient of

, it is clear that all quotients of

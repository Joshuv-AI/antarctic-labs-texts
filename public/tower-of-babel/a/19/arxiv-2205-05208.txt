A poset version of Ramanujan results on Eulerian numbers and zeta values
Eric R Dolores-Cuenca 1, Jose L. Mendoza-Cortes 2

Abstract
. We explore the operad of finite posets and its algebras. We use order polytopes to investigate the combinatorial properties of zeta values. By generalizing a family of zeta value identities, we demonstrate the applicability of this approach. In addition, we offer new proofs of some of Ramanujan’s results on the properties of Eulerian numbers, interpreting his work as dealing with series inheriting the algebraic structure of disjoint unions of points. Finally, we establish a connection between our findings and the linear independence of zeta values.

1.0.1.
What we do

We introduce the action of an operad on zeta values. An operad of sets comprises a set of
n
-ary operations
O(n)
, with rules for composition and actions of the symmetric group. These rules of composition represent an abstraction of the composition of functions, while the action of the symmetric groups reflects the permutations of inputs. Consequently, an algebra over an operad

is a set

[

34

Consider an

, which we interpret as an operation, and let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
be posets. We define the action of

P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
to be the lexicographic sum of
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
over
P
. This means that we replace each vertex

with the entire poset

, and then take the resulting poset with the induced ordering. In other words, the operad of finite posets acts on the set of finite posets.

The language of operads enables us to study algebraic properties that are inherent to objects that have a defined composition, even if these objects do not satisfy all the criteria of traditional algebraic structures like groups or rings.

Our main result is the proof of the following identity. Let
\zeta(k)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{k}}
denote the Riemann zeta function. Given

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}{n\choose k}(\zeta(n+1)-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k+1}(\zeta(k+1)-1-1/2^{k+1}).

This identity relates the alternating sum of a particular combination of binomial coefficients and zeta values to a closed-form expression involving the Riemann zeta function evaluated at integer arguments. Notably, when we attempted to compute the left-hand side of Equation (
1

In the field of combinatorics, the order polynomial introduced by Stanley is a polynomial that is associated with a finite poset. It is well known that binomial polynomials serve as a basis for order polynomials. Our work shows that order polynomials inherit the action of the operad of finite posets, and that Equation (
1

Remark 2.2

In order to establish a relationship between

2.1.
Order series

There are two families of polynomials indexed by posets, strict order polynomials
\Omega^{\circ}(P,x)=\sum d_{P,i}{x\choose i},
and weak order polynomials

\Omega(P,x)=\sum(-1)^{|P|-i}d_{P,i}\left(\kern-3.00003pt\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{x}{i}\right)\kern-3.00003pt\right)
.

The generating series associated with each of these order polynomials can be defined. The strict order series is given by
\mathfrak{Z}\left(P\right)=\sum_{n=1}\Omega^{\circ}(P,n)x^{n}
, and the weak order series is defined as
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\left(P\right)=\sum_{n=1}\Omega(P,n)x^{n}
. To make the notation more concise, we will use
\mathfrak{Z}\left(n\right)
and
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\left(n\right)
to denote
\mathfrak{Z}\left(\langle n\rangle\right)
and
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\left(\langle n\rangle\right)
, respectively.

In practice, the use of order series simplifies the computations and clarifies identities. In fact, the study of order series has led to the discovery of new binomial identities and generalizations of the Chu-Vandermonde identity, as shown in
[
3
]
. Therefore, in this section, we focus on exploring the properties of order series.

Action of the operad of posets on zeta values

Christian Goldbach (1690-1764) showed that

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(\zeta(n)-1)=1.

In

Consider

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\choose k}(\zeta(n+1)-1)=\zeta(k+1),

discovered by
[

For sums
\sum_{i=0}a_{i}{x\choose i}
with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
a_{i}=0
except for a finite number of terms, we define

\displaystyle\tilde{n}(\sum_{i=0}a_{i}{x\choose i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\sum_{i=0}a_{i}{n\choose i})(\zeta(n+1)-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}a_{i}\zeta(i+1).

From Theorem
4.6
we deduce the following identity:

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}{n\choose k}(\zeta(n+1)-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k+1}(\zeta(k+1)-1-1/2^{k+1}),

together with

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}(\zeta(n+1)-1)=1/2,

they define a linear function
\tilde{n}_{2}
that sends a finite sum with rational coefficients
\sum_{i=0}a_{i}{x\choose i}
with

except for a finite number of terms, into

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+1}(\sum_{i=0}a_{i}{k\choose i})(\zeta(k+1)-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{0}}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(-1)^{i+1}a_{i}(\zeta(i+1)-1-\frac{1}{2^{i+1}}).

We will use the functions
\tilde{n},\tilde{n}_{2}
to define the action of the operad of posets on zeta values.

From Theorem
2.4
we know there is an action of the operad of posets on the vector space
\mathbb{Z}[\{{x\choose i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}]
, we show how to transfer the action to
\mathbb{Z}[\{\tilde{n}({x\choose i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}]
and to
\mathbb{Z}[\{\tilde{n}_{2}({x\choose i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}]

\tilde{n}
will become trivially an operadic morphism.

For a poset
P
, with order series
\Omega^{\circ}(P,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{|P|}d_{P,i}{x\choose i}
, we define
\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathbb{N}}\left(P\right):=\tilde{n}(\Omega^{\circ}(P,x))
and
\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathbb{N}}^{+}\left(P\right):=(-1)^{|P|+1}\tilde{n}_{2}(\Omega^{\circ}(P,x))
.

Corollary 5.1
.

The operad
\mathcal{FP}
acts non-trivially on the numbers
\{\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathbb{N}}^{+}\left(P\right)\}_{P\hbox{ finite poset}}
and on
\{\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathbb{N}}\left(P\right)\}_{P\hbox{ finite poset}}
.

Proof.

The action of a poset
P
on
\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathbb{N}}^{+}\left(P_{1}\right),\cdots,\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathbb{N}}^{+}\left(P_{|P|}\right)
, is given by

(-1)^{|P(P_{1},\cdots,P_{|P|})|+1}\tilde{n}_{2}(\Omega^{\circ}(P(P_{1},\cdots,P_{|P|}),x)).

We see that the action is not trivial since
\Omega^{\circ}(\langle k\rangle,x)
is assigned to
(\zeta(k+1)-1-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}})
, and different chains are sent to different zeta values.
We see that the action of the operad of posets is different from the action of the operad of series parallel posets because from Lemma
2.11
, the action of
\{x<y>z<w\}
is not equal to the action of any quaternary operation generated by
\{x,y\}
and
\{x<y\}
.

The same arguments apply to
\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathbb{N}}\left(P\right)
.
∎

We describe the action of a poset in
\mathbb{Z}\{{x\choose i}\}
by computing the integer coordinates, we call these coordinates structural constants. We use this point of view to find the action on zeta values of the operad of finite posets. First compute the constants on polytopes or order series, and then the map

\cup_{m+1}\Delta[m+1]\setminus(\cup_{m}\Delta[m])
. The action of
\{x,y\}

[

Poly(P),Poly(Q)

\tilde{n}_{2}
to conclude that

\{x,y\}(\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathbb{N}}^{+}\left(2\right),\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathbb{N}}^{+}\left(m\right))
is equal to

=-m(\zeta(m+1)-1-\frac{1}{2^{m+1}})+(m+1)(\zeta(m+2)-1-\frac{1}{2^{m+2}}).

Using Equation (

Theorem 5.2
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The numbers
\{1,\zeta[2],\zeta[3],\cdots\}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(2)

The functions
\tilde{n}

Proof.

Assuming

\tilde{n}(\tilde{n}_{2})
return the same value, each coordinate
\zeta(i+1)-1
must have the same coefficient.
Now, for different polinomials, there is a coordinate in which the values of
\tilde{n}(\tilde{n}_{2})
differ, otherwise the order polynomials agree on an infinite number of integers, but this implies that the polynomials are the same. Then
2

Assuming
2

\sum a_{i}{\zeta[i]}=0
. We can multiply by an integer to obtain integer coefficients:
\sum b_{i}{\zeta[i]}=0
. The preimage of this element
\sum b_{i}{x\choose i}
belongs to the kernel of the map, which is zero. But from
\sum b_{i}{x\choose i}=0
we deduce that
b_{i}=0
, due to linear independence of
\{{x\choose i}\}
.
∎

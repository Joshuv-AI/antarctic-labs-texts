The Generalized Superfactorial Function - GSF

Definition 0.4
.

Define the
n^{th}
-degree superfactorial as follows:

(0.5)

sf^{(n)}(x)=\prod^{x}_{k=1}sf^{(n-1)}(k),\hskip 5.69054ptn\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},\hskip 5.69054ptsf^{(0)}(x)\equiv x!

We see that the first degree superfactorial is equivalent to the superfactorial definition given by Sloane and Plouffe (1995):

sf^{(1)}(x)=sf(x)=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k!,\hskip 5.69054ptx\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

We now give an explicit formula for the GSF.

Theorem 0.5
.

The generalized superfactorial function is related to the r-simplex numbers (i.e.figurate numbers,
P_{n}(k)
) as follows:

sf^{(n)}(x)=\prod^{x}_{k=1}[(x-k)+1]^{P_{n}(k)}=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{P_{n}((x-k)+1)};\hskip 5.69054ptx\in\mathbb{Z}^{+};n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq}

Proof.

Theorem 3.2. is proven by using the Principle of Mathematical Induction.

Base Case:
n=0

\begin{split}sf^{(0)}(x)&=x!=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{1}\hskip 14.22636pt[\text{As noted in section 2.1}]\\
&=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{P_{0}((x-k)+1)}\hskip 14.22636pt[\text{From equation (2.3), }P_{0}(n)=1]\\
&=1^{1}2^{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot(x-1)^{1}x^{1}=x^{1}(x-1)^{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot 2^{1}1^{1}\\
&=x^{P_{0}(1)}(x-1)^{P_{0}(2)}(x-2)^{P_{0}(3)}\cdot\cdot\cdot 2^{P_{0}(x-1)}1^{P_{0}(x)}\\
&=\prod^{x}_{k=1}[(x-k)+1]^{P_{0}(k)}\end{split}

Inductive Hypothesis:

sf^{(m)}(x)=\prod^{x}_{k=1}[(x-k)+1]^{P_{m}(k)}

To prove:

sf^{(m+1)}(x)=\prod^{x}_{k=1}[(x-k)+1]^{P_{m+1}(k)}=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{P_{m+1}((x-k)+1)}

Now,

\begin{split}sf^{(m+1)}(x)&=\prod^{x}_{k=1}sf^{(m)}(k)\hskip 14.22636pt[\text{From equation (3.1)}]\\
&=\prod^{x}_{k=1}\left[\prod^{k}_{i=1}[(k-i)+1]^{P_{m}(i)}\right]\hskip 14.22636pt[\text{From Inductive Hypothesis}]\\
&=\prod^{1}_{i=1}[(1-i)+1]^{P_{m}(i)}\prod^{2}_{i=1}[(2-i)+1]^{P_{m}(i)}\cdot\cdot\cdot\prod^{x}_{i=1}[(x-i)+1]^{P_{m}(i)}\\
&=\left[1^{P_{m}(1)}\right]\left[2^{P_{m}(1)}1^{P_{m}(2)}\right]\left[3^{P_{m}(1)}2^{P_{m}(2)}1^{P_{m}(3)}\right]\cdot\cdot\cdot\left[x^{P_{m}(1)}(x-1)^{P_{m}(2)}(x-2)^{P_{m}(3)}\cdot\cdot\cdot 1^{P_{m}(x)}\right]\\
&=1^{\left[{{\sum^{x}_{k=1}P_{m}(k)}}\right]}2^{\left[{{\sum^{x-1}_{k=1}P_{m}(k)}}\right]}\cdot\cdot\cdot x^{\left[{{\sum^{1}_{k=1}P_{m}(k)}}\right]}\hskip 14.22636pt[\text{On rearranging terms}]\\
&=\left[1^{P_{m+1}(x)}\right]\left[2^{P_{m+1}(x-1)}\right]\cdot\cdot\cdot x^{P_{m+1}(1)}\hskip 14.22636pt[\text{From equation \ref{eq:main}}]\\
&=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{P_{m+1}((x-k)+1)}=\prod^{x}_{k=1}[(x-k)+1]^{P_{m+1}(k)}\end{split}

∎

The Generalized Hyperfactorial Function - GHF

Definition 0.6
.

Define the
n^{th}
-degree hyperfactorial as follows:

(0.6)

H^{(n)}(x)=\prod^{x}_{k=1}H^{(n-1)}(k)\hskip 5.69054pt;\hskip 5.69054ptH^{(1)}(x)\equiv H(x)=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{k}

We now give an explicit formula for the GHF.

Theorem 0.7
.

The generalized hyperfactorial function is related to the r-simplex numbers as follows:

H^{(n)}(x)=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{k[P_{n-1}((x-k)+1)]};\hskip 5.69054ptn\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

Proof.

Theorem 4.2. is proven by using the Principle of Mathematical Induction.

Base Case:
n=1

\begin{split}H^{(1)}(x)&=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{k}=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{k[P_{0}((x-k)+1)]}\hskip 14.22636pt[\text{Since}\hskip 5.69054ptP_{0}(\xi)=1,\forall\hskip 2.84526pt\xi\in N]\\
\end{split}

Inductive Hypothesis:

H^{(m)}(x)=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{k[P_{m-1}((x-k)+1)]}

To prove:

H^{(m+1)}(x)=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{k[P_{m}((x-k)+1)]}

\begin{split}H^{(m+1)}(x)&=\prod^{x}_{k=1}H^{(m)}(k)\hskip 14.22636pt[\text{From equation (4.1)}]\\
&=\prod^{x}_{k=1}\left[\prod^{k}_{i=1}i^{i[P_{m-1}((k-i)+1)]}\right]\hskip 14.22636pt[\text{From Inductive Hypothesis}]\\
&=\prod^{1}_{i=1}i^{i[P_{m-1}((1-i)+1)]}\prod^{2}_{i=1}i^{i[P_{m-1}((2-i)+1)]}\cdot\cdot\cdot\prod^{x}_{i=1}i^{i[P_{m-1}((x-i)+1)]}\\
&=\left[1^{1\cdot P_{m-1}(1)}1^{1\cdot P_{m-1}(2)}\cdot\cdot\cdot 1^{1\cdot P_{m-1}(x)}\right]\left[2^{2\cdot P_{m-1}(1)}2^{2\cdot P_{m-1}(2)}\cdot\cdot\cdot 2^{2\cdot P_{m-1}(x-1)}\right]\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\cdot\cdot\cdot\left[x^{x\cdot P_{m-1}(1)}\right]\hskip 14.22636pt[\text{On rearranging terms}]\\
&=1^{1\cdot\left[{{\sum^{x}_{k=1}P_{m-1}(k)}}\right]}2^{2\cdot\left[{{\sum^{x-1}_{k=1}P_{m-1}(k)}}\right]}\cdot\cdot\cdot x^{x\cdot\left[{{\sum^{1}_{k=1}P_{m-1}(k)}}\right]}\\
&=1^{1\cdot P_{m}(x)}2^{2\cdot P_{m}(x-1)}\cdot\cdot\cdot x^{x\cdot P_{m}(1)}\hskip 14.22636pt[\text{From equation \ref{eq:main}}]\\
&=\prod^{x}_{k=1}k^{k[P_{m}((x-k)+1)]}\end{split}

∎

(\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}m_{i_{j}})
is a convergent series (i.e. results in a finite sum), since only a finite number of terms are positive integers (rest are 0). The RHS of Lemma 6.8 is, therefore, simply:

(0.13)

\begin{split}\sum^{n}_{i=1}\Omega(x_{i})&=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\left(\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}m_{i_{j}}\right)=\left(\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}m_{1_{j}}\right)+\left(\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}m_{2_{j}}\right)+\cdot\cdot\cdot\left(\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}m_{n_{j}}\right)\\
&=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\left(m_{1_{j}}+m_{2_{j}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot m_{n_{j}}\right)\\
&=\boxed{\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\left(\sum^{n}_{i=1}m_{i_{j}}\right)}\end{split}

Now, notice that LHS of Lemma 6.8 is simply:

\begin{split}\Omega\left(\prod^{n}_{i=1}x_{i}\right)&=\Omega\left(\prod^{n}_{i=1}\left(\prod^{\infty}_{j=1}P_{j}^{m_{i_{j}}}\right)\right)\\
&=\Omega\left(\prod^{\infty}_{j=1}P_{j}^{m_{1_{j}}}\times\prod^{\infty}_{j=1}P_{j}^{m_{2_{j}}}\times\cdot\cdot\cdot\prod^{\infty}_{j=1}P_{j}^{m_{n_{j}}}\right)\\
&=\Omega\left(\prod^{\infty}_{j=1}P_{j}^{m_{1_{j}}+m_{2_{j}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot m_{n_{j}}}\right)\\
&=\Omega\left(\prod^{\infty}_{j=1}P_{j}^{\sum^{n}_{i=1}m_{i_{j}}}\right)\\
&=\boxed{\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\left(\sum^{n}_{i=1}m_{i_{j}}\right)}\end{split}

∎

Legendre’s formula
(otherwise referred to as

), gives us the number of times a prime number

Theorem 0.21
.

{}^{sf}\nu_{p}^{(n)}(x)=\sum^{x}_{i=1}\left[P_{n-1}(x+1-i)\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\left\lfloor\frac{i}{p^{k}}\right\rfloor\right)\right];\hskip 5.69054ptn\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

Base Case:
n=1

{}^{sf}\nu_{p}^{(1)}(x)=\nu_{p}\left(sf(x)\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{x}\nu_{p}(i!)=\sum_{i=1}^{x}P_{0}(x+1-i)\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\left\lfloor\frac{i}{p^{k}}\right\rfloor\right)

0.25
, and the fact that
\nu_{p}(a\cdot b)=\nu_{p}(a)+\nu_{p}(b)
(where
a
&

are integers).

Inductive Hypothesis:

{}^{sf}\nu_{p}^{(m)}(x)=\sum^{x}_{i=1}\left[P_{m-1}(x+1-i)\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\left\lfloor\frac{i}{p^{k}}\right\rfloor\right)\right];\hskip 5.69054ptm\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

To Prove:

{}^{sf}\nu_{p}^{(m+1)}(x)=\sum^{x}_{i=1}\left[P_{m}(x+1-i)\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\left\lfloor\frac{i}{p^{k}}\right\rfloor\right)\right];\hskip 5.69054pt(m+1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

Now,

\begin{split}{}^{sf}\nu_{p}^{(m+1)}(x)&=\sum^{x}_{i=1}\nu_{p}\left(sf^{(m)}(i)\right)=\sum^{x}_{i=1}{}^{sf}\nu_{p}^{(m)}(i)\\
&=\sum^{x}_{i=1}\sum^{i}_{j=1}\left[P_{m-1}(i+1-j)\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\left\lfloor\frac{j}{p^{k}}\right\rfloor\right)\right]\\
&=\sum^{x}_{j=1}\left[\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\left\lfloor\frac{j}{p^{k}}\right\rfloor\right)\sum^{x}_{i=j}P_{m-1}(i+1-j)\right]\\
\end{split}

However, note that:

\begin{split}\sum^{x}_{i=j}P_{m-1}(i+1-j)&=P_{m-1}(j+1-j)+P_{m-1}((j+1)+1-j)\cdot\cdot\cdot\cdot\\
&\hskip 11.38109pt+P_{m-1}((x-1)+1-j)+P_{m-1}(x+1-j)\\
&=P_{m-1}(1)+P_{m-1}(2)\cdot\cdot\cdot+P_{m-1}(x+1-j)=\sum^{x+1-j}_{i=1}P_{m-1}(i)\\
&=P_{m}(x+1-j)\hskip 5.69054pt[\text{From equation \ref{eq:main}}]\end{split}

Therefore, as required, we have:

{}^{sf}\nu_{p}^{(m+1)}(x)=\sum^{x}_{j=1}\left[\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\left\lfloor\frac{j}{p^{k}}\right\rfloor\right)P_{m}(x+1-j)\right]

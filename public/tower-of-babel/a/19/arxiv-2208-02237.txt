The three “layers” of graphene monolayer and their analog generalized uncertainty principles
A. Iorio, B. Ivetić, S. Mignemi, P. Pais

Abstract
We show that graphene, in its simplest form and settings, is a practical table-top realization of the analog of exotic quantum gravity scenarios, which are speculated to lead to certain generalized Heisenberg algebras. In particular, we identify three different energy regimes (the “layers”) where the physics is still of a pseudorelativistic (Dirac) type but more and more sensitive to the effects of the lattice. This plays here a role analog to that of a discrete space, where the Dirac quasiparticles live. This work improves and pushes further earlier results, where the physical meaning of the high energy momenta was clear, but the conjugate coordinates only had a purely abstract description. Here we find the physical meaning of the latter by identifying the mapping between the high-energy coordinates and low-energy ones, i.e., those measured in the lab. We then obtain two generalized Heisenberg algebras that were not noticed earlier. In these two cases, we have the striking result that the high-energy coordinates just coincide with the standard ones, measured in the lab. A third generalized Heisenberg algebra is obtained, and it is an improvement of the results obtained earlier in two respects: we now have an expression of the generalized coordinates in terms of the standard phase-space variables, and we obtain higher order terms. All mentioned results clearly open the doors to table-top experimental verifications of many generalized uncertainty principle-corrected predictions of the quantum gravity phenomenology.

In our case, we actually have more than one “high energy layer”. In fact, in principle, we might have

As we shall see, starting from the phase-space variables of the low energy set-up, that are somehow fixed, we have various natural choices for the two other “layers”. The analysis that we carry on from there, on the one hand, leads to

On the other hand, we solve the important problem of how to relate the high energy coordinates with the low energy phase-space variables. This latter point is of paramount importance to set-up experiments and identify the measurable effects of such deformations on the Dirac Hamiltonian descriptions. We shall not do that here, but should give a general recipe in the concluding remarks.

One important result here is that, in two important cases with GUPs, such high energy coordinates simply coincide with the low-energy coordinates. In other cases, such coordinates have complicated expressions in terms of the standard, measurable phase space variables.

As for the momenta, their high-energy expressions is given once and for all in terms of the low-energy, measurable momenta, by taking into account the effect of the dispersion relations at higher and higher order. We may say that it is the physics that dictates these expressions.

Once these expressions are under control, when we measure this or that observable, even though we only have access to low-energy momenta, we simply need to recognize the generalized momenta (and, when necessary, the generalized coordinates) in the given expression. This allows us to experimentally see the effects of the GUPs on the physics of the Dirac quasi-particles, which appears to us quite remarkable.

To conclude, let us say that the results of this paper pave the way to the use of graphene, and other Dirac materials, as table-top laboratories to reproduce GUP-corrected scenarios. First, in two crucial cases with GUPs, the high energy coordinates just coincide with the low-energy, measurable coordinates. ), which is not an exact expression but a series expansion in
A
. Indeed,

P_{0}^{i}=P^{i}\,(1-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)^{-1}=P^{i}\,(1+A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|+A^{2}\,|\vec{P_{0}}|^{2})+\mbox{O}(A^{3})

As
|\vec{P}|\,\equiv\,(P^{i}P_{i})^{1/2}=(P_{0}^{i}P_{0\,i})^{1/2}\,(1-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)^{2/2}=|\vec{P_{0}}|\,(1-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)
, therefore,

|\vec{P_{0}}|=|\vec{P}|\,(1-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)^{-1}=|\vec{P}|\,(1+A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|+A^{2}\,|\vec{P_{0}}|^{2})+\mbox{O}(A^{3})

(68)

Proceeding in an iterative way, up to order
O(A^{2})
,

|\vec{P_{0}}|=|\vec{P}|\,(1+A\,|\vec{P}|\,(1+A\,|\vec{P}|)+A^{2}\,|\vec{P}|^{2})=P^{i}\,(1+A\,|\vec{P}|+2A^{2}\,|\vec{P}|^{2})\;,

which gives the expressions (
V.1.1
) of supermomenta in terms of hypermomenta, up to order
O(A^{2})
,

P_{0}^{i}=P^{i}\,(1+A\,|\vec{P}|+2A^{2}\,|\vec{P}|^{2})\;,

and

1-A\,|\vec{P}_{0}|=1-A\,|\vec{P}|-A^{2}|\vec{P}|^{2}\;.

We can now write (
48
) just as hypervariables,

[X^{i},P^{j}]=\mathrm{i}\left((1-A\,|\vec{P}|-A^{2}\,|\vec{P}|^{2})\delta^{ij}-A\,\frac{P^{i}P^{j}}{|\vec{P}|}\,(1+A\,|\vec{P}|)\right)\;.

(69)

Let us infer how the commutator
[X^{i},X^{j}]
looks by demanding the Jacobi identity

[X^{i},[X^{j},P^{k}]]+[X^{j},[P^{k},X^{i}]]+[P^{k},[X^{i},X^{j}]]=0\,.

Rearranging the last expression, we get

[P^{k},[X^{i},X^{j}]]=-[X^{i},[X^{j},P^{k}]]+[X^{j},[X^{i},P^{k}]]=-Y^{ijk}+Y^{jik}\;,

where we defined
Y^{ijk}\equiv[X^{i},[X^{j},P^{k}]]
in the last equality.

Let us compute first
Y^{ijk}
up to order
O(A^{2})
.

\displaystyle Y^{ijk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[X^{i},[X^{j},P^{k}]]=[X^{i},\,\mathrm{i}\,(1-A\,|\vec{P}|-A^{2}\,|\vec{P}|^{2})\delta^{jk}-\mathrm{i}A\,\frac{P^{j}P^{k}}{|\vec{P}|}\,(1+A\,|\vec{P}|)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}A\,[X^{i},|\vec{P}|]\,\delta^{jk}-2\mathrm{i}A^{2}\,|\vec{P}|\,[X^{i},|\vec{P}|]\,\delta^{jk}-\mathrm{i}A\,[X^{i},P^{j}]\,P^{k}\frac{(1+A\,|\vec{P}|)}{|\vec{P}|}-\mathrm{i}A\,P^{j}\,[X^{i},P^{k}]\frac{(1+A\,|\vec{P}|)}{|\vec{P}|}

\displaystyle+\mathrm{i}A\,\frac{P^{j}P^{k}}{|\vec{P}|^{2}}\,[X^{i},|\vec{P}|]\,(1+A\,|\vec{P}|)-\mathrm{i}A^{2}\,\frac{P^{j}P^{k}}{|\vec{P}|}\,[X^{i},|\vec{P}|]\;.

We need the relation

[X^{i},|\vec{P}|]=[X^{i},(P^{j}\,P_{j})^{1/2}]=\frac{P_{j}[X^{i},P^{j}]}{(P^{j}\,P_{j})^{1/2}}=\frac{P_{j}}{|\vec{P}|}\,[X^{i},P^{j}]\;.

(70)

By using (
70
) and (
69
) in the last expression of
Y^{ijk}
, we get, up to order
O(A^{2})
,

\displaystyle Y^{ijk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}A\,\frac{P_{l}}{|\vec{P}|}\,[X^{i},P^{l}]\,\delta^{jk}-2\mathrm{i}A^{2}\,P_{l}\,[X^{i},P^{l}]\,\delta^{jk}-\mathrm{i}A\,[X^{i},P^{j}]\,P^{k}\frac{(1+A\,|\vec{P}|)}{|\vec{P}|}

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}A\,P^{j}\,[X^{i},P^{k}]\frac{(1+A\,|\vec{P}|)}{|\vec{P}|}+\mathrm{i}A\,\frac{P^{j}P^{k}P_{l}}{|\vec{P}|^{3}}\,[X^{i},P^{l}]\,(1+A\,|\vec{P}|)-\mathrm{i}A^{2}\,\frac{P^{j}P^{k}P_{l}}{|\vec{P}|^{2}}\,[X^{i},P^{l}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\,\frac{P_{l}}{|\vec{P}|}\,\left((1-A\,|\vec{P}|)\delta^{il}-A\,\frac{P^{i}P^{l}}{|\vec{P}|}\,\right)\,\delta^{jk}+2A^{2}\,P_{l}\,\delta^{il}\,\delta^{jk}+A\,\left((1-A\,|\vec{P}|)\delta^{ij}-A\,\frac{P^{i}P^{j}}{|\vec{P}|}\,\right)\,P^{k}\,\frac{(1+A\,|\vec{P}|)}{|\vec{P}|}

\displaystyle+A\,P^{j}\,\left((1-A\,|\vec{P}|)\delta^{ik}-A\,\frac{P^{i}P^{k}}{|\vec{P}|}\,\right)\,\frac{(1+A\,|\vec{P}|)}{|\vec{P}|}-A\,\frac{P^{j}P^{k}P_{l}}{|\vec{P}|^{3}}\left((1-A\,|\vec{P}|)\delta^{il}-A\,\frac{P^{i}P^{l}}{|\vec{P}|}\,\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\,\frac{P^{i}}{|\vec{P}|}\,\delta^{jk}-\,A^{2}\,P^{i}\,\delta^{jk}-A^{2}\,P^{i}\,\delta^{jk}+2A^{2}\,P^{i}\,\delta^{jk}+A\,\frac{P^{k}}{|\vec{P}|}\,\delta^{ij}-A^{2}\,\frac{P^{i}P^{j}P^{k}}{|\vec{P}|^{2}}

\displaystyle+A\,\frac{P^{j}}{|\vec{P}|}\,\delta^{ik}-A^{2}\,\frac{P^{j}P^{i}P^{k}}{|\vec{P}|^{2}}-A\,\frac{P^{j}P^{k}P^{i}}{|\vec{P}|^{3}}+A^{2}\frac{P^{j}P^{k}P^{i}}{|\vec{P}|^{2}}+A^{2}\,\frac{P^{j}P^{k}P^{i}}{|\vec{P}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\,\frac{P^{i}}{|\vec{P}|}\,\delta^{jk}+A\,\frac{P^{k}}{|\vec{P}|}\,\delta^{ij}+A\,\frac{P^{j}}{|\vec{P}|}\,\delta^{ik}-A\,\frac{P^{j}P^{k}P^{i}}{|\vec{P}|^{3}}\;.

We observe that
Y^{ijk}
is totally symmetric in the three indices at order
\mbox{O}(A^{2})
. Therefore,

[P^{k},[X^{i},X^{j}]]=-Y^{ijk}+Y^{jik}=0\;,

up to order
\mbox{O}(A^{2})
. The most natural choice for our algebra is then (
54
)

[X^{i},P^{j}]=\mathrm{i}\left[\delta^{ij}-A\,|\vec{P}|\,\left(\delta^{ij}+\frac{P^{i}P^{j}}{|\vec{P}|^{2}}\right)-A^{2}\,|\vec{P}|^{2}\,\left(\delta^{ij}+\frac{P^{i}P^{j}}{|\vec{P}|^{2}}\right)\right],\ \ \ [X^{i},X^{j}]=0,\ \ \ [P^{i},P^{j}]=0\,.

Appendix B
Canonical hypervariables from canonical supervariables: More details

Let us write first some useful identities. As
P^{i}=P^{i}_{0}(1-AP_{0})
,

|\vec{P}|\equiv(P^{i}P_{i})^{1/2}=(P_{0}^{i}P_{0i})^{1/2}(1-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)=|\vec{P_{0}}|(1-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)\;.

(71)

Also,

[X_{0}^{i},|\vec{P_{0}}|]=[X_{0}^{i},(P_{0}^{j}P_{0j})^{1/2}]=\frac{[X_{0}^{i},P_{0}^{k}]P_{0\,k}}{(P_{0}^{j}P_{0j})^{1/2}}=\mathrm{i}\frac{P^{i}_{0}}{|\vec{P_{0}}|}\;,

(72)

where in the last equation we used that
(\vec{X}_{0},\vec{P}_{0})
are canonical supervariables.

Now, we want to express the hypercoordinates
X^{i}
in terms of the supervariables,
X^{i}=X^{i}(\vec{X_{0}},\vec{P}_{0})
. Taking the ansatz

X^{i}=X^{i}_{0}+F\,X^{i}_{0}+G\,A^{2}\,(X^{j}_{0}P_{0\,j})\,P^{i}_{0}\;,

(73)

where
F=F(A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)
and
G=G(A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)
are two arbitrary dimensionless functions of
A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|
(which is also dimensionless). Then,

\begin{split}[X^{i},P^{j}]&=[X^{i}_{0}+F\,X^{i}_{0}+G\,A^{2}\,(X^{k}_{0}P_{0\,k})\,P^{i}_{0},P^{j}_{0}-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|\,P^{j}_{0}]\\
&=[X^{i}_{0},P^{j}_{0}]-A\,[X^{i}_{0},|\vec{P_{0}}|]P^{j}_{0}-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|[X^{i}_{0},P^{j}_{0}]+F[X^{i}_{0},P^{j}_{0}]-F\,A\,[X^{i}_{0},|\vec{P_{0}}|]P^{j}_{0}-F\,A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|[X^{i}_{0},P^{j}_{0}]\\
&+G\,A^{2}\,P^{k}_{0}P^{i}_{0}[X^{k}_{0},P^{j}_{0}]-G\,A^{3}\,P^{k}_{0}P^{i}_{0}P^{j}_{0}[X^{k}_{0},|\vec{P_{0}}|]-G\,A^{3}\,P^{k}_{0}\,P^{i}_{0}\,|\vec{P_{0}}|[X^{k}_{0},P^{j}_{0}]\;,\end{split}

where the sum is implicit when repeated indices appear. By using the canonical brackets of supervariables,
[X^{i}_{0},P^{j}_{0}]=\mathrm{i}\delta^{ij}
, and (
72
), we have

\begin{split}[X^{i},P^{j}]&=\mathrm{i}\delta^{ij}-\mathrm{i}A\,\frac{P^{i}_{0}}{P_{0}}P^{j}_{0}-\mathrm{i}A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|\,\delta^{ij}+\mathrm{i}F\,\delta^{ij}-\mathrm{i}F\,A\frac{P^{i}_{0}}{|\vec{P_{0}}|}P^{j}_{0}-\mathrm{i}F\,A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|\,\delta^{ij}\\
&+\mathrm{i}G\,A^{2}P^{k}_{0}P^{i}_{0}\delta^{kj}-\mathrm{i}G\,A^{3}P^{k}_{0}P^{i}_{0}P^{j}_{0}\frac{P^{k}_{0}}{|\vec{P_{0}}|}-\mathrm{i}G\,A^{3}P^{k}_{0}P^{i}_{0}\,|\vec{P_{0}}|\delta^{kj}\\
&=\mathrm{i}(1-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)(1+F)\delta^{ij}-\mathrm{i}\frac{A\,P^{i}_{0}P^{j}_{0}}{|\vec{P_{0}}|}(1+F-G\,A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|(1-2A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|))\;.\end{split}

In this case we demand the hypervariables to satisfy the algebra (
56
)

[X^{i},P^{j}]=\mathrm{i}\delta_{ij}\;,\;[X^{i},X^{j}]=0\;,\;[P^{i},P^{j}]=0\;.

Therefore we have the following algebraic system

\displaystyle(1-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)(1+F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle 1+F-G\,A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|(1-2AP_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\;,

whose unique solution is, unless
A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|=1
or
A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|=1/2
,

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|}{1-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|}

(74)

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A\,P_{0}(1-A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)(1-2A\,|\vec{P_{0}}|)}\;.

(75)

Inserting this solution into (
73
) we end up with (
57
).

1
Unramified
p
-adic representations

The aim of this section is to give a characterization of the unramified representations of
G_{K}
.

Lemma 1.1
.

For
r,n\geq 1
one has

\#\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p})=p^{(n-1)r^{2}}\cdot p^{s}\cdot t

with

s=s(r):=(r-1)r/2,\quad t=t(r):=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(p^{i}-1\right).

Proof.

For
n=1

\#\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p\mathbb{Z}_{p})=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\left(p^{r}-p^{j}\right)=p^{\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}j}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(p^{i}-1\right)=p^{s}\cdot t.

Each element of
\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p\mathbb{Z}_{p})
has
p^{(n-1)r^{2}}
different lifts to
\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.
∎

Lemma 1.2
.

Each matrix
U\in\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
can be uniquely written in the form

U=NV
with matrices
N,V\in\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
such that

N^{t}=1,\quad V^{p^{s}}\equiv 1\pmod{p}\text{ and }NV=VN.

Moreover, we can further decompose
N
into a product of matrices
N_{q}
of
q
-power order, one for each
q
dividing
t
, which commute pairwise and with
V
.

Proof.

For all
n\geq 1
there exist integers
a_{n},b_{n}
such that
a_{n}p^{s+(n-1)r^{2}}+b_{n}t=1
. Note that
a_{n}p^{s+(n-1)r^{2}}\equiv 1\equiv a_{n+1}p^{s+nr^{2}}\pmod{t}
and hence also
a_{n}\equiv a_{n+1}p^{r^{2}}\pmod{t}
. Since
U^{p^{s+(n-1)r^{2}}}
has order dividing
t
modulo
p^{n}
we deduce that

U^{a_{n}p^{s+(n-1)r^{2}}}=(U^{p^{s+(n-1)r^{2}}})^{a_{n}}\equiv(U^{p^{s+(n-1)r^{2}}})^{a_{n+1}p^{r^{2}}}=U^{a_{n+1}p^{s+nr^{2}}}\pmod{p^{n}}.

Similarly
b_{n}t\equiv b_{n+1}t\pmod{p^{s+(n-1)r^{2}}}
, i.e.
b_{n}\equiv b_{n+1}\pmod{p^{s+(n-1)r^{2}}}
, and we obtain

U^{b_{n}t}\equiv U^{b_{n+1}t}\pmod{p^{n}}.

Therefore we can define

N:=\lim_{n\to\infty}U^{a_{n}p^{s+(n-1)r^{2}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad V:=\lim_{n\to\infty}U^{b_{n}t}.

It is now clear that
V^{p^{s}}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
and that all the other requirements hold modulo
p^{n}
for any
n\geq 1
, hence they must also hold in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

The last assertion can be proved similarly, but actually the proof is easier since the order of
N
is finite. The pairwise commutativity follows from the fact that all the
N_{q}
are some powers of
N
.

It remains to prove uniqueness. Let
N_{1},V_{1}
and
N_{2},V_{2}
be as above and let
a_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
ta_{n}\equiv 1\pmod{p^{s+(n-1)r^{2}}}
. Then

V_{1}\equiv V_{1}^{ta_{n}}=(N_{1}V_{1})^{ta_{n}}=(N_{2}V_{2})^{ta_{n}}=V_{2}^{ta_{n}}\equiv V_{2}\pmod{p^{n}}.

Since this is true for all
n
, we deduce that
V_{1}=V_{2}
and hence also that
N_{1}=N_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 1.3
.

For
q\neq p
there is a one to one correspondence of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{q},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
with the set of all elements in
\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
of
q
-power order. This correspondence associates
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{q},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
with
\phi(1)\in\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. In particular if
q\nmid p\cdot t(r)
, then
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{q},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))=0
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{q},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
. By continuity, for any
n\geq 1
, the preimage of
\{M\in\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\mid M\equiv I\pmod{p^{n}}\}
must be a neighborhood of
0
in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
. In other words there must exist
m\geq 1
such that
\phi(q^{m})=\phi(1)^{q^{m}}\equiv I\pmod{p^{n}}
and we can clearly assume that
q^{m}
is always the maximal power of
q
dividing
t
. Hence there exists a fixed
m
such that for every
n\geq 1
we have
\phi(1)^{q^{m}}\equiv I\pmod{p^{n}}
and it follows that
\phi(1)^{q^{m}}=I
.

Conversely, let
U\in\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
be of order
q^{m}
, then we can set
\phi(x)=U^{x}
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
. The two maps are clearly inverse to one another.
∎

Lemma 1.4
.

There is a one to one correspondence of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
with the set of all elements in
\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
which project to elements of
p
-power order in
\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. This correspondence associates
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
with
\phi(1)\in\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
. As in the proof of the previous lemma, we deduce that
\phi(1)
is of
p
-power order modulo
p
.

Conversely let
U\in\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
be such that its order modulo
p
is some power of
p
. By Lemma
1.1
the order of
U
modulo
p
must divide
p^{s}
and, moreover, its order modulo
p^{n}
must divide
p^{s+(n-1)r^{2}}
. Therefore for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
U^{x}
is well-defined modulo
p^{n}
for all

and hence it is also well-defined as an element of
\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. This implies that we can set
\phi(x)=U^{x}
.
∎

Let

Lemma 1.5
.

There is a one to one correspondence of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
with the set of all elements in
\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, which associates
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
with
\phi(1)\in\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.2
, each matrix
U\in\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
can be decomposed as a product of the form
U=\left(\prod_{q|t}N_{q}\right)\cdot V
with matrices

, where

has a
q
-power-order and the order of

modulo

is a power of

. Moreover all these matrices commute with one another. By Lemma

and Lemma

we can define
\phi_{q}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{q},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
by
\phi_{q}(e_{q})=N_{q}
and
\phi_{p}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
by
\phi_{p}(e_{p})=V
. Note that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))=\prod_{q|t}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{q},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))\times\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathrm{Gl}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})).

So we can set

and note that
\phi(1)=\prod_{q|t}\phi_{q}(e_{q})\cdot\phi_{p}(e_{p})=U

∎

Lemma 1.8

Let

be the unramified extension of degree

Note also that

∎

We have shown that the

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

f_{\mathcal{F},L}\colon\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p}_{L})\times\mathcal{Z}^{G_{L}}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{L}^{G_{L}}.

Explicitly
f_{\mathcal{F},L}
is given by a power series of the form

on

By

(1)

The following lemma will be used to prove Lemma
2.4
, which will be one of the key ingredients in the proof of our main theorem.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
, let
q
be a power of
p
, let
A
,
B\in K^{n,n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
f:K\to K^{n}
be a function whose components are polynomials involving only terms of degree larger than
q
and constants. Assuming that
B
is invertible, the system of equations

f(x_{1})+Ax^{q}+Bx=0

(3)

has a solution
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in K^{n}
, where the
q
-th power
x^{q}
is taken componentwise.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, (
3
) consists of a single non-constant polynomial equation, since there must be a non-trivial term of degree
1
(
B
is assumed to be invertible). Since
K
is algebraically closed, it follows that (
3
) has a solution.

So let us assume the result holds for
n-1\geq 1
and let us prove it for
n
. Up to some elementary row operations we can assume that the first row of
A
has at most one nonzero entry
a_{1,1}
(note that we allow also
a_{1,1}=0
). Then the first equation of our system takes the form

f_{1}(x_{1})+a_{1,1}x_{1}^{q}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{1,j}x_{j}=0,

where some of the
b_{1,j}
are non-zero. Here we need to distinguish two cases.

Case 1:
b_{1,j}=0
for all
j>1
and
b_{1,1}\neq 0
. This means that
B
takes the form

B=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1,1}&0\\
\tilde{b}&\tilde{B}\end{pmatrix},

where
\tilde{B}\in K^{n-1,n-1}
is invertible and
\tilde{b}\in K^{n-1,1}
. In particular, the first equation is a non-trivial polynomial in the only variable
x_{1}
, which has a solution since
K
is algebraically closed. Substituting this value for
x_{1}
in all the other equations we obtain a new system of the shape

c+\tilde{A}\tilde{x}^{q}+\tilde{B}\tilde{x}=0,

where
\tilde{x}=(x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
and
c
is a constant vector in
K^{n-1}
, which we can interpret as a constant function
K\to K^{n-1}
. Note also that
\tilde{B}
is invertible as observed above. Therefore we can apply the inductive hypothesis to find a solution
\tilde{x}
; together with the already computed first component
x_{1}
, we obtain a solution of (
3
).

Case 2:
b_{1,j}\neq 0
for some
j>1
. Up to a permutation of the variables and normalizing the last coefficient, we can assume
b_{1,n}=1
. Up to some further elementary row operations (which do not modify the first row) we may assume that
b_{i,n}=0
for
i>1
. So the matrix
B
has the shape

B=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}&1\\
\tilde{B}&0\end{pmatrix},

where
\tilde{B}\in K^{n-1,n-1}
is clearly invertible and
\tilde{b}\in K^{1,n-1}
. Hence we can rewrite the first equation as

x_{n}=-f_{1}(x_{1})-a_{1,1}x_{1}^{q}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}b_{1,j}x_{j}.

(4)

Substituting this into the other equations of (
3
) we obtain a new system of the type

\tilde{f}(x_{1})+\tilde{A}\tilde{x}^{q}+\tilde{B}\tilde{x}=0,

(5)

where
\tilde{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
and
\tilde{B}
is as above. Note that (
5
) has the same shape as (
3
), since, as already mentioned,
\tilde{B}
is invertible. Note also that here we used that
q
is a multiple of the characteristic of the field, so that, when computing
x_{n}^{q}=(-f_{1}(x_{1})-a_{1,1}x_{1}^{q}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}b_{1,j}x_{j})^{q}
, all the mixed terms vanish. Therefore we can use the inductive hypothesis to find
x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}
and then take
x_{n}
as in (
4
). This will be a solution of (
3
).
∎

Now we are in a position to prove the following result which generalizes a well-known lemma, see e.g.

Lemma 2.4
.

Given an element
c\in\Upsilon_{L}
, there exists
x\in\Upsilon_{L}
such that
(F_{L}-1)\cdot x=c
. If
\tilde{x}
is such that
(F_{L}-1)\cdot\tilde{x}\equiv c\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{L_{0}}^{i}}
for some
i
, then we can assume
x\equiv\tilde{x}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{L_{0}}^{i}}
. In particular, if
c\in\prod_{r}U_{L_{0}}^{(1)}(\rho^{\mathrm{nr}})
, then we can assume
x\in\prod_{r}U_{L_{0}}^{(1)}(\rho^{\mathrm{nr}})
.

Proof.

Let
I
be the
r\times r
identity matrix and let
q\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

x^{F_{L}}\equiv x^{q}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{L_{0}}}
for all
x\in\mathcal{O}_{L_{0}}
. The determinant of the matrix
q\rho^{\mathrm{nr}}(F_{L})-I
is obviously a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Therefore there exists an inverse matrix
\mathcal{M}
of
q\rho^{\mathrm{nr}}(F_{L})-I
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then we take
x_{1}=\mathcal{M}\cdot c
and we have
(F_{L}-1)\cdot x_{1}\equiv c\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{L_{0}}}
.

Let us assume we have
x_{i}
such that
(F_{L}-1)\cdot x_{i}\equiv c\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{L_{0}}^{i}}
, i.e. such that there exists
a_{i}\in\prod_{r}U_{L_{0}}^{(i)}(\rho^{\mathrm{nr}})
with
((F_{L}-1)\cdot x_{i})\cdot a_{i}=c
, where the product of
((F_{L}-1)\cdot x_{i})
and
a_{i}
is done componentwise. We take
b_{i}\in\prod_{r}\mathcal{O}_{L_{0}}
so that
a_{i}=1+\pi_{L}^{i}b_{i}
, where
\pi_{L}
is an uniformizing element of
L
(and hence also of
L_{0}
) and
1
is the vector consisting only of ones. Since the residue field of
L_{0}
is algebraically closed, we can apply Lemma
2.3
and obtain a

such that

\rho^{\mathrm{nr}}(F_{L})y_{i}^{q}-y_{i}-b_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{L_{0}}}.

We set
x_{i+1}=x_{i}(1+y_{i}\pi_{L}^{i})
and we calculate

\begin{split}(F_{L}-1)\cdot x_{i+1}&=((F_{L}-1)\cdot x_{i})\cdot((F_{L}-1)\cdot(1+y_{i}\pi_{L}^{i}))\\
&\equiv ca_{i}^{-1}(1+\rho^{\mathrm{nr}}(F_{L})y_{i}^{q}\pi_{L}^{i})(1-y_{i}\pi_{L}^{i})\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{L_{0}}^{i+1}}\\
&\equiv ca_{i}^{-1}(1+\rho^{\mathrm{nr}}(F_{L})y_{i}^{q}\pi_{L}^{i}-y_{i}\pi_{L}^{i})\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{L_{0}}^{i+1}}\\
&\equiv ca_{i}^{-1}(1+b_{i}\pi_{L}^{i})\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{L_{0}}^{i+1}}\\
&\equiv c\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{L_{0}}^{i+1}}.\end{split}

Therefore we have an inductive construction for the

, which can be started with

in the case that

. Then the conclusions of the lemma hold for

.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
R
be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizing element
\pi
, let
K
be its field of quotients and let
f\in\mathrm{End}_{R}(R^{r})
. Then there exists
\omega\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq\omega

K\cdot\mathrm{im}(f)\cap\pi^{n}R^{r}\subseteq\pi^{n-\omega}\mathrm{im}(f).

Proof.

We set
\mathcal{U}=\mathrm{im}(f)
and
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}=R^{r}\cap K\cdot\mathcal{U}
. Since
R^{r}
is a noetherian ring, the ascending chain of ideals
R^{r}\cap\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\mathcal{U}
for
n>0
stabilizes. Therefore there exists
s\geq 0
such that
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}=R^{r}\cap\frac{1}{\pi^{s}}\mathcal{U}
and hence we have

\pi^{s}\tilde{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}.

By the Theorem of Artin-Rees there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq k
,

\tilde{\mathcal{U}}\cap\pi^{n}R^{r}\subseteq\pi^{n-k}(\tilde{\mathcal{U}}\cap(\pi^{k}R^{r})).

Therefore setting
\omega=s+k
we deduce that for all
n\geq\omega
,

K\cdot\mathcal{U}\cap\pi^{n}R^{r}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{U}}\cap\pi^{n}R^{r}\subseteq\pi^{n-k}(\tilde{\mathcal{U}}\cap(\pi^{k}R^{r}))\subseteq\pi^{n-\omega}\pi^{s}\tilde{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq\pi^{n-\omega}\mathcal{U}.

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Keeping all the notations of the previous lemma, for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m>n+\omega
we have

((\mathrm{im}(f)\cap\pi^{n}R^{r})+\pi^{m}R^{r})/(\pi^{n}\mathrm{im}(f)+\pi^{m}R^{r})\cong(\mathrm{im}(f)\cap\pi^{n}R^{r})/\pi^{n}\mathrm{im}(f).

Proof.

The natural map

\mathrm{im}(f)\cap\pi^{n}R^{r}\to((\mathrm{im}(f)\cap\pi^{n}R^{r})+\pi^{m}R^{r})/(\pi^{n}\mathrm{im}(f)+\pi^{m}R^{r})

(6)

is obviously surjective. Its kernel is

\mathrm{im}(f)\cap\pi^{n}R^{r}\cap(\pi^{n}\mathrm{im}(f)+\pi^{m}R^{r}).

We want to show that this is equal to
\pi^{n}\mathrm{im}(f)
. Note that

\pi^{n}\mathrm{im}(f)+\pi^{m}R^{r}\subseteq\pi^{n}R^{r}

and, recalling Lemma
2.5
, we get

\begin{split}\mathrm{im}(f)\cap\pi^{n}R^{r}\cap(\pi^{n}\mathrm{im}(f)+\pi^{m}R^{r})&=\mathrm{im}(f)\cap(\pi^{n}\mathrm{im}(f)+\pi^{m}R^{r})\\
&=\pi^{n}\mathrm{im}(f)+(\mathrm{im}(f)\cap\pi^{m}R^{r})\\
&=\pi^{n}\left(\mathrm{im}(f)+\left(\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\mathrm{im}(f)\cap\pi^{m-n}R^{r}\right)\right)\\

∎

(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{r}/\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{K}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{r}(\rho^{\mathrm{nr}}))^{G_{N}}\cong(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{r}/\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{K}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{r})/(\mathcal{M}_{M}\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{r}/\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{K}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{r})(\rho^{\mathrm{nr}})\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{r}/\mathcal{M}_{N}\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{r}(\rho^{\mathrm{nr}}).

∎

is centered in degree
0
and

with

-projective module. Since by Theorem

has finitely generated cohomology groups, the proof of

actually shows that we can assume that
A
and
B
are both finitely generated. By our Lemma

the module
\mathcal{I}_{N/K}(\rho^{\mathrm{nr}})
is cohomologically trivial. We hence apply
[

where

\textstyle{P^{-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial_{P}^{-1}}
\textstyle{P^{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial_{P}^{0}}

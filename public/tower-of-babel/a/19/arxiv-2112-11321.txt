Tight constraints on probabilistic convertibility of quantum states
Bartosz Regula

Abstract
We develop two general approaches to characterising the manipulation of quantum states by means of probabilistic protocols constrained by the limitations of some quantum resource theory. First, we give a general necessary condition for the existence of a physical transformation between quantum states, obtained using a recently introduced resource monotone based on the Hilbert projective metric. In all affine quantum resource theories (e.g. coherence, asymmetry, imaginarity) as well as in entanglement distillation, we show that the monotone provides a necessary and sufficient condition for one-shot resource convertibility under resource–non-generating operations, and hence no better restrictions on all probabilistic protocols are possible. We use the monotone to establish improved bounds on the performance of both one-shot and many-copy probabilistic resource distillation protocols. Complementing this approach, we introduce a general method for bounding achievable probabilities in resource transformations under resource–non-generating maps through a family of convex optimisation problems. We show it to tightly characterise single-shot probabilistic distillation in broad types of resource theories, allowing an exact analysis of the trade-offs between the probabilities and errors in distilling maximally resourceful states. We demonstrate the usefulness of both of our approaches in the study of quantum entanglement distillation.

In particular, probabilistic protocols — representing processes that depend on the outcome of some measurements, and thus have a certain probability of failing to perform the desired transformation — are ubiquitous in the exploitation of quantum resources in practice. They underlie, for instance, the majority of the commonly used schemes for the distillation of entanglement

;

: although some desired performance thresholds might not be achievable deterministically, one can often take a risk by sacrificing some success probability in the hopes of obtaining a better performance stochastically.

;

Therefore, constraining only deterministic protocols does not provide complete information about our capability to manipulate a given resource in practice — if no-go theorems that restrict deterministic conversion are easy to circumvent by simply allowing a small chance of failure, can they really be considered to place a meaningful limitation on practical transformation schemes?
In order to understand the ultimate limits of physically realisable transformations of a given resource, we argue that it is necessary to know the extent of the advantage that probabilistic resource manipulation can provide. The issue then is that most of the previous approaches were not suited to such a problem, and no systematic way of constraining the performance of probabilistic transformation protocols was known.
Here we aim to bridge this gap and establish methods which allow for a comprehensive characterisation of probabilistic convertibility between quantum states in general quantum resource theories.

We approach the problem of understanding probabilistic convertibility in two different ways. First, we aim to establish limitations on
all
possible probabilistic protocols — no matter how small their probability of success is. Despite no general bounds of this type being previously known, we show that the problem can be cast in a straightforward mathematical framework that allows us to not only obtain very broadly applicable restrictions, but also prove that they are the best ones possible under some assumption on the given resource theory.
And although such a constraint on all transformations might seem like overkill, we show that it leads to non-trivial bounds that improve on and extend previously known restrictions in several cases.
Nevertheless, should more fine-grained constraints be desired, we consider also a complementary approach that allows for a deeper understanding of the trade-offs between the errors incurred in transformation protocols and the probabilities with which they can be achieved.

Specifically, we first study the
projective robustness

\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
, a resource monotone based on the Hilbert projective metric
Bushell 1973
;
Reeb et al. 2011
that was recently introduced in
Regula 2022
. We provide rigorous proofs of the properties and applications of this quantity in constraining one-shot resource transformations, expanding the results originally announced in
Regula 2022
. We show in particular that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
is monotonic under all resource transformations, deterministic or probabilistic, which means that it provides a necessary condition for the existence of the most general physical conversion protocol between any two quantum states. Under a suitably general type of transformations known as resource–non-generating operations, we establish also a converse of this result, showing that the projective robustness together with a closely related monotone called the
free projective robustness

\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
can give sufficient conditions for resource convertibility. The latter result applies in particular to all affine resource theories — a broad class that includes the theories of quantum coherence, asymmetry, athermality, or imaginarity — as well as in the study of bi- and multi-partite entanglement distillation. Focusing on the task of probabilistic distillation (purification) of quantum resources, we show how
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
can be used to establish strong limitations on the error achievable in such manipulation, which become tight in relevant cases and yield significant improvements over previous results of this type. The properties of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
allow us to extend such bounds to many-copy distillation protocols, constraining the overheads required in probabilistic resource distillation. We also use
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
to show that in any convex resource theory there exist ‘isotropic-like’ quantum states whose fidelity with a pure state cannot be increased by any one-shot transformation protocol.

Our results also shed light on recent no-go theorems for probabilistic resource manipulation in Ref.
Fang and Liu 2020
: we demonstrate that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
leads to a significant improvement over the quantitative bounds on distillation errors shown in that work, and we prove that the restrictions derived there do not actually establish any trade-offs between probability of success and transformation accuracy.

The second approach of this work is concerned with the question: when a probabilistic transformation between given states
is
possible, what is the best achievable probability of success?
To address this, we introduce a family of convex optimisation problems that can precisely delineate the trade-offs between transformation errors and the probability of successful conversion in general quantum resource theories. We obtain upper and lower bounds on the maximal achievable probability of resource transformations, as well as bounds on the least error achievable with given probability. We further show that, for several types of resources, our bounds become tight in the task of resource distillation, where one aims to purify a given state to a maximally resourceful one. This allows us to obtain an exact quantitative characterisation of both exact and approximate one-shot probabilistic resource distillation.

Throughout this work, we exemplify our results by applying them to distillation of quantum entanglement, showing how our methods can both extend previously known insights as well as provide new ones.

Preliminaries

Our discussion will pertain to self-adjoint operators acting on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. We will be working in the real Hilbert space obtained by endowing the vector space of such operators with the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product
\left\langle A,B\right\rangle=\Tr AB

\mathbb{R}_{+}
(
\mathbb{R}_{++}
) to denote the non-negative (positive) reals,
\mathbb{H}
to denote all self-adjoint operators, and
\mathbb{H}_{+}
to denote positive semidefinite operators. We use
\mathbb{D}
to denote the set of density operators (quantum states), i.e. positive semidefinite operators of unit trace.
Following conventions common in optimisation theory, we take

and we will allow basic algebraic operations involving infinity, such as

and

II.1
Resource theories and resource–non-generating operations

In a broad sense, the question that we concern ourselves with in this work is the (im)possibility of achieving the transformation from a state
\rho
to another state
\rho^{\prime}
, with some physical constraints imposed on the physical processes available to us. Such constraints can be naturally characterised in the formalism of quantum resource theories
Horodecki and Oppenheim 2013b
;
Chitambar and Gour 2019
.
In this work, we always understand quantum resource theories to mean theories of
state
manipulation, although more general formulations of this formalism can also be made
Devetak et al. 2008
;
Coecke et al. 2016
;
del Rio et al. 2015
;
Takagi and Regula 2019
;
Liu and Yuan 2020
;
Gour and Winter 2019
.

We thus assume that a certain set of quantum states
\mathbb{F}
is designated as the
free states
, dividing the state space into states which carry no resources (
\rho\in\mathbb{F}
) and states which are deemed resourceful (
\rho\notin\mathbb{F}
).
Throughout this manuscript, we make the basic assumption that the set
\mathbb{F}
is topologically closed; some of our results will also require that
\mathbb{F}
be a convex set, which is also a natural assumption in most settings, as it simply means that probabilistically mixing two free states cannot produce a resourceful state.
When studying transformations of states between different spaces, we will implicitly understand that each space has its own associated set of free states
\mathbb{F}
.

In addition to the free states, a basic ingredient of any resource theory are the
free operations

\mathbb{O}
— these are the transformations available to us in the given restricted setting, the convertibility of states under which we wish to understand.
The choice of the free operations can be motivated by physical considerations, representing the operations which are particularly ‘easy’ or ‘cheap’ to implement without expending any resources. There can, however, be other motivations to study the conversion of resources under various types of free operations. For instance, it may be the case that enlarging the allowed operations even slightly, e.g. by supplying some additional resources for free, can allow one to overcome the limitations that more restricted operations suffer from
Eggeling et al. 2001
;
Audenaert et al. 2003
;
Ishizaka 2004
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010
;
Brandão and Gour 2015
;
Berta et al. 2022
;
Faist et al. 2015
.
In other contexts, it might be the case that natural choices of easily implementable operations are actually extremely weak and trivialise the given theory, making almost all transformations impossible
Gour et al. 2017
;
Sauerwein et al. 2018
;
Chitambar and Gour 2016
;
Lami et al. 2019
;
Lami 2020
. Depending on the resource theory, it might not even be clear what a suitable choice of physical free operations should be
Chitambar and Gour 2016
. In such cases, it is justified to look at transformations under various types of operations, and in particular to seek to understand general properties and restrictions shared by all feasible choices of ‘free’ channels.

The approach we take in this work is to consider all
resource–non-generating operations
, and hereafter we use
\mathbb{O}
to denote such maps. They constitute the largest set of free operations which is consistent with
\mathbb{F}
being the set of free states, in the sense that resourceful states cannot be created for free. Focusing for now on deterministic transformations (i.e. quantum channels), we define a channel
\mathcal{E}
to be resource non-generating if
\mathcal{E}(\sigma)\in\mathbb{F}
for all free
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
.

These transformations have already found success in the description of resources such as quantum entanglement
Brandão and Plenio 2010
;
Contreras-Tejada et al. 2019
;
Lami and Regula 2021
, quantum coherence
Chitambar and Gour 2016
, or quantum thermodynamics
Faist et al. 2015
;
Faist and Renner 2018
, as well as in general resource-theoretic frameworks
Chitambar and Gour 2019
;
Liu et al. 2019
;
Regula et al. 2020
. There are several reasons to study the transformations of resources under such operations. First, their crucial property is that any no-go result shown for the resource–non-generating operations
\mathbb{O}
will necessarily apply to all other physical types of free operations, by virtue of
\mathbb{O}
being the most permissive free transformations. Importantly, such restrictions can be considered to be robust: if a restriction is shown only for a subset
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}\subsetneq\mathbb{O}
, then it might be the case that some other, larger type of free operations can transcend it; however, a restriction valid for all transformations
\mathbb{O}
cannot be overcome by
any
free operations. In this sense, resource–non-generating operations are perfectly suited to characterise the ultimate limitations of a given theory, applicable regardless of the specific choice of free operations.

An important reason for our choice of resource–non-generating operations is simply that they can be defined in
any
resource theory: we do not need to assume almost anything about the structure of the considered resources, yet we will be able to make meaningful physical statements about their convertibility. From a technical standpoint, resource–non-generating operations are straightforward to describe mathematically, allowing for a simplified description of the operational aspects of theories where the more ‘natural’ choice of free operations may be very difficult to describe. A trade-off for this mathematical simplicity is that such transformations might not have a form which is easily implementable in practice, meaning that they might require additional resources to be physically realised
Faist et al. 2015
;
Chitambar and Gour 2019
. Nevertheless, they can always be understood as an ‘upper bound’ on what is achievable in practice within the given resource-theoretic setting.

II.2
Probabilistic resource manipulation

A general probabilistic protocol can be represented by a quantum instrument
Davies and Lewis 1970
;
Ozawa 1984

\{\mathcal{E}_{i}\}
, that is, a collection of maps which are completely positive and such that the overall transformation
\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}
is trace preserving. In this setting,
\Tr\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho)
can be understood as the probability corresponding to the
i
th outcome of the instrument, and thus the probability that the state
\rho
is transformed into
\frac{\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho)}
.

In the context of resource transformations, deterministic conversion between
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
is equivalent to the existence of a quantum channel
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
such that
\mathcal{E}(\rho)=\rho^{\prime}
. The problem is more involved in the case of probabilistic conversion. Since we are concerned with the transformation between two fixed states,
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
, we can simply coarse-grain the outcomes of the instrument into two: either the conversion succeeded, or it failed. For such an instrument
\{\mathcal{E}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{1}
to be considered resource non-generating, we then impose that each constituent map
\mathcal{E}_{i}
must be resource non-generating, but only in a probabilistic sense: we require that for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
, it holds that
\mathcal{E}_{i}(\sigma)=[\Tr\mathcal{E}_{i}(\sigma)]\,\sigma^{\prime}
for some
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}
.

A slightly different way of defining free probabilistic protocols that is sometimes encountered in the study of specific quantum resources is to treat the instrument
\{\mathcal{E}_{i}\}
as a single trace-preserving map
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\cdot)=\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\cdot)\otimes\ket{i}\!\bra{i}
and impose that the overall channel
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
belongs to
\mathbb{O}
, where the resource theory has been extended to include the ancillary classical system. In all of the settings considered explicitly here and in
Regula 2022
— in particular, in the resource theories of entanglement, coherence, and magic — the two definitions are fully equivalent.
However, the definition based on the ‘flagged’ channel
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
can be different from ours e.g. in quantum thermodynamics, where pure classical states do not come for free. The definition employed in this work, which only uses the instrument
\{\mathcal{E}_{i}\}
, has the advantage that it can be applied to general resource theories, without having to explicitly consider how the ancillary classical states are handled.

In fact, we will not need to consider instruments explicitly, and it will suffice to study the action of sub-normalised (probabilistic) free operations
\mathcal{E}
. These are completely positive and trace–non-increasing maps which are resource non–generating in the above sense. The equivalence between transformations under such maps and more general instruments is thanks to the fact that any probabilistic map can be understood as being part of a larger free instrument: given any sub-normalised free operation
\mathcal{E}
, we can define
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\cdot)\coloneqq[\Tr(\cdot)-\Tr\mathcal{E}(\cdot)]\sigma^{\prime}
for some fixed
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}
, and the instrument
\{\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\}
constitutes a valid free probabilistic instrument.

We thus use
\mathbb{O}
to denote both deterministic and probabilistic resource–non-generating maps:

\displaystyle\mathbb{O}=\big\{\mathcal{E}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\;\big|

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\text{ completely positive and trace non-increasing},

(1)

\displaystyle\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F},\,\exists\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F},\;p\in[0,1]\text{ s.t.\ }\mathcal{E}(\sigma)=p\sigma^{\prime}\big\}.

We then say that the transformation from
\rho
to
\rho^{\prime}
is possible with probability
p
if there exists an operation
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
such that
\mathcal{E}(\rho)=p\rho^{\prime}
. Here, the output space of the map can differ from its input space, and we assume that a corresponding set
\mathbb{F}
is defined in both spaces.

However, this is still not the most general way in which a probabilistic conversion can be realised. There exist cases where the transformation
\mathcal{E}(\rho)=p\rho^{\prime}
is impossible with any non-zero probability
p
, but the state
\rho^{\prime}
can nevertheless be approached arbitrarily closely

. In particular, it is possible that there exists a sequence of operations
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n}\in\mathbb{O}
such that
\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}0
but

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}\rho^{\prime}.

(2)

That is, the transformation from
\rho
to
\rho^{\prime}
might only be possible in the asymptotic limit where the probability of success vanishes as the fidelity approaches 1. We hereafter use the notation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}
to denote the existence of a free probabilistic protocol transforming
\rho
to
\rho^{\prime}
, whether it takes the form of a single operation
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
or a sequence of free operations satisfying (
2
). More precisely,

\displaystyle\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}\;\iff\;\rho^{\prime}\,\in\,\operatorname{cl}\left\{\left.\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O},\;\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)>0\right\},

(3)

where cl denotes closure. This form represents the most general physical transformation of a quantum resource allowed in the framework of this work.

II.3
Resource monotones

A resource monotone (or resource measure)
M_{\mathbb{F}}:\mathbb{H}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
is any function which is monotonic under deterministic free operations, that is,
M_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq M_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{E}(\rho))
for any channel
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}

Vedral et al. 1997
;
Chitambar and Gour 2019
. The crucial consequence of this definition is that any monotone can be used to certify the impossibility of deterministic resource conversion: if
M_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)<M_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})
, then there cannot exist a free channel which transforms
\rho
to
\rho^{\prime}
deterministically. When it comes to probabilistic transformations, a desirable feature of a resource monotone is its strong monotonicity
Vedral and Plenio 1998
;
Vidal 2000
, which states that the monotone should, on average, decrease under the action of a free probabilistic instrument
\{\mathcal{E}_{i}\}
:

\displaystyle M_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\sum_{i}\Tr\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho)\,M_{\mathbb{F}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho)}\right).

(4)

Such strong monotones can then be used to bound transformation probabilities of quantum resources. Specifically, assume that there exists a probabilistic operation
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
such that
\mathcal{E}(\rho)=p\rho^{\prime}
with some probability
p>0
. Eq. (
4
) then gives

\displaystyle p\leq\frac{M_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}{M_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})},

(5)

and so any strong monotone provides a restriction on probabilistic transformations. However, most known monotones can never be used to completely rule out the possibility of probabilistic resource conversion: this would require showing that the transformation cannot be realised with
any
probability
p
(or equivalently that
p
must be
0
), but this cannot be concluded from Eq. (
5
) for finite and non-zero values of
M_{\mathbb{F}}
. Even in the cases when tight restrictions on resource conversion can be obtained in such a setting, they typically require the optimisation over infinitely many monotones
Vidal 2000
, potentially limiting the practical applicability of such approaches.

Let us now introduce two resource measures that serve as inspiration for the concepts used in this work. The
(generalised) robustness

Vidal and Tarrach 1999
;
Datta 2009
is defined as

\displaystyle R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\left.\lambda\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq\lambda\sigma,\;\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\},

(6)

where the inequality is with respect to the positive semidefinite cone, in the sense that
A\leq B\iff B-A\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
. This monotone found a number of operational uses — it quantifies the advantages of resources in channel discrimination problems
Takagi et al. 2019
, and can be applied to benchmark the performance of resource distillation tasks
Regula et al. 2020
;
Regula and Takagi 2021
. A quantity which is, in a sense, dual to the robustness is the
resource weight

Lewenstein and Sanpera 1998

\displaystyle W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\left.\nu\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\geq\nu\sigma,\;\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\}.

(7)

Note that this quantity is technically an antitone rather than a monotone — it satisfies
W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\leq W_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{E}(\rho))
for free channels
\mathcal{E}
.
The resource weight can also be used to study resource advantages in a different type of channel discrimination problems
Uola et al. 2020
;
Ducuara and Skrzypczyk 2020
, and it has recently been applied to establish strong bounds on deterministic resource distillation errors and overheads
Regula and Takagi 2021
;
Fang and Liu 2022
. The similarity between the two measures is made precise by noticing that they can be both expressed in terms of the max-relative entropy
D_{\max}

Datta 2009

\displaystyle R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\coloneqq 2^{D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)}=\inf\left\{\left.\lambda\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq\lambda\sigma\right\}.

(8)

We then have

\displaystyle R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma),\qquad W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\left[\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}R_{\max}(\sigma\|\rho)\right]^{-1}\!.

(9)

We also introduce a variant of the max-relative entropy
R_{\max}
as

\displaystyle R_{\max}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho\|\sigma)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\left.\lambda\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq_{\mathbb{F}}\lambda\sigma\right\},

(10)

where now
\leq_{\mathbb{F}}
denotes inequality with respect to the cone generated by the free set
\mathbb{F}
; precisely,
A\leq_{\mathbb{F}}B\iff B-A\in\cone(\mathbb{F})
, where

\displaystyle\cone(\mathbb{F})\coloneqq\left\{\left.\lambda\sigma\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\;\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\}.

(11)

(or

)

Vidal and Tarrach 1999
, given by

, referred to as full-dimensional theories.

We note also that robustness and weight quantifiers can be naturally defined not only for quantum states, but also for unnormalised positive semidefinite operators; in such cases, we get that
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\lambda\rho)=\lambda R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
, and analogously for
W_{\mathbb{F}}
and
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}
.

III
Projective robustness

Our main object of study in this section will be the
projective robustness

\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}

Regula 2022
, defined as

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\,R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\,R_{\max}(\sigma\|\rho)

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\left.\frac{\lambda}{\nu}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\nu\sigma\leq\rho\leq\lambda\sigma,\;\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\}.

The motivation for the introduction of this quantity is twofold. On the one hand, the robustness and weight monotones are very useful in constraining deterministic resource transformations
Brandão and Gour 2015
;
Regula et al. 2020
;
Regula and Takagi 2021
;
Fang and Liu 2022
, but their applicability in probabilistic cases is significantly limited
Regula and Takagi 2021
;
Fang and Liu 2022
; the idea is then to exploit the complementary character of
R_{\mathbb{F}}
and
W_{\mathbb{F}}
to introduce a monotone which
combines
the properties of the two measures in order to establish much broader and tighter limitations. On the other hand, the projective robustness can be thought of as a minimisation of the Hilbert projective metric
\ln R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\,R_{\max}(\sigma\|\rho)

Bushell 1973
;
Reeb et al. 2011
, a quantity which has already been used to study transformations of pairs of quantum states
Reeb et al. 2011
;
Buscemi and Gour 2017
, but whose applications in more general resource-theoretic settings have not been explored.

Analogously, the
free projective robustness

Regula 2022
is given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\,R_{\max}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho\|\sigma)\,R_{\max}(\sigma\|\rho)

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\left.\frac{\lambda}{\nu}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\nu\sigma\leq\rho\leq_{\mathbb{F}}\lambda\sigma,\;\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\}.

Notice that this quantity combines
R_{\max}
, which is an optimisation with respect to the positive semidefinite cone
\mathbb{H}_{+}
, with
R_{\max}^{\mathbb{F}}
, which is defined using
\cone(\mathbb{F})
. It is not necessary to use
R_{\max}^{\mathbb{F}}
for both terms, and indeed this choice would trivialise the optimisation for all convex resource theories. The inclusion
\cone(\mathbb{F})\subseteq\mathbb{H}_{+}
entails that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
in general.

A geometric interpretation of the definition of the two measures is provided in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
Geometric definition of the projective robustness measures.
Using the known geometric interpretation of the original Hilbert’s projective metric
Bushell 1973
;
Kohlberg and Pratt 1982
, the quantity
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
can be understood as the minimal cross-ratio of the points
\tau
,
\rho
,
\sigma
, and
\omega
within the set of all density operators
\mathbb{D}
, with
\sigma
being a free state. Specifically,
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
is the minimal value of
\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert\rho-\omega\right\rVert_{\,}}{\left\lVert\vphantom{\rho}\sigma-\omega\right\rVert_{\,}}\frac{\left\lVert\sigma-\tau\vphantom{\rho}\right\rVert_{\,}}{\left\lVert\rho-\tau\right\rVert_{\,}}
such that
\tau,\omega\in\mathbb{D}
,
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
, and the four states are all colinear as shown in the Figure. Here, the choice of the norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\,}
is arbitrary. This can be seen by noticing that for any feasible
\sigma
such that
\nu\sigma\leq\rho\leq\lambda\sigma
we can equivalently write
\rho+(\lambda-1)\omega=\lambda\sigma
and
\rho-(1-\nu)\tau=\nu\sigma
for some states
\tau,\omega
; this then gives
\rho-\omega=\lambda(\sigma-\omega)
and
\rho-\tau=\nu(\sigma-\tau)
, from which the cross-ratio form follows.

The free projective robutness
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
is defined analogously as the least value of
\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert\rho-\omega^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\,}}{\left\lVert\vphantom{\rho}\sigma^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\,}}\frac{\left\lVert\sigma^{\prime}-\tau^{\prime}\vphantom{\rho}\right\rVert_{\,}}{\left\lVert\rho-\tau^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\,}}
where now both
\sigma^{\prime}
and
\omega^{\prime}
are constrained to be free states. It is not difficult to see that any optimal choice of such a decomposition will have all points
\tau,\sigma,\omega
or
\tau^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}
lying on the boundary of the corresponding set (
\mathbb{D}
or
\mathbb{F}
), since otherwise a better decomposition could be chosen along the same line.

III.1
Properties

We begin with a study of the basic properties of the projective robustness
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
. For clarity, we for now only assume that
\mathbb{F}
is a topologically closed subset of density operators.

Theorem 1
.

The projective robustness
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
of any state
\rho
satisfies the following properties.

(i)

\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
takes a finite value if and only if there exists a state
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\supp\sigma=\supp\rho
.

(ii)

It is invariant under scaling, that is,
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\lambda\rho)=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
.

(iii)

\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
can be computed as the optimal value of the optimisation problem

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left.\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq\widetilde{\sigma}\leq\gamma\rho,\;\widetilde{\sigma}\in\cone(\mathbb{F})\right\},

(15)

where the infimum is achieved as long as it is finite. When
\mathbb{F}
is a convex set, this is a conic linear optimisation problem, and it admits an equivalent dual formulation as

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left.\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle=1,\;B-A\in\cone(\mathbb{F})\textup{*},\;A,B\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\right\}

(16)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left.\frac{\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle}{\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\frac{\left\langle A,\sigma\right\rangle}{\left\langle B,\sigma\right\rangle}\leq 1\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F},\;A,B\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\right\}.

(17)

Here,
\cone(\mathbb{F})=\left\{\left.\lambda\sigma\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\;\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\}
is the cone induced by the set
\mathbb{F}
, and
\cone(\mathbb{F})\textup{*}=\left\{\left.X\in\mathbb{H}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\langle X,\sigma\right\rangle\geq 0\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\}
is its dual cone.

(iv)

\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
is faithful, that is, for any state
\rho
it holds that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=1\iff\rho\in\mathbb{F}
.

(v)

If the set of free states is closed under tensor product, in the sense that
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathbb{F}\Rightarrow\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2}\in\mathbb{F}
, then
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
is sub-multiplicative: for any states
\rho
and
\omega
, it holds that

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho\otimes\omega)\leq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega).

(18)

Alternatively, the weaker variant of sub-multiplicativity
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\otimes n})\leq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{n}
holds whenever
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\Rightarrow\sigma^{\otimes n}\in\mathbb{F}\;\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(vi)

When
\mathbb{F}
is a convex set,
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
is quasiconvex: for any
t\in[0,1]
, it holds that

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(t\rho+(1-t)\omega)\leq\max\{\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho),\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega)\}.

(19)

(vii)

\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
is lower semicontinuous, that is,
\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho_{n})
for any sequence
(\rho_{n})_{n}
of states converging to
\rho
.

(viii)

It can be bounded as

\displaystyle R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}\leq\;

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\leq R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,R_{\max}(\sigma^{\star}_{R}\|\rho)

(20)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\Omega_\mathbb{F}(\rho)}\leq R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1},

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\leq W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}\,R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma^{\star}_{W}),

(21)

where
\lambda_{\min}(\rho)
denotes the smallest eigenvalue of
\rho
,
\sigma^{\star}_{R}
is an optimal state such that
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma^{\star}_{R})
, and analogously
\sigma^{\star}_{W}
is an optimal state such that
W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=R_{\max}(\sigma^{\star}_{W}\|\rho)^{-1}
, whenever such states exist.

Let us comment on the unusual property (
i
) of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
: it always diverges for states which do not share support with a free state. There are two immediate consequences of this fact: (1) as long as
\mathbb{F}
contains a state of full rank (which is the case in virtually all theories of interest), then
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)<\infty
for all full-rank states; (2) for any pure state
\psi\notin\mathbb{F}
, we have that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\psi)=\infty
. Although the latter point might make the applicability of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
seem limited, we will see in Section
IV
that it can be easily circumvented by allowing a small error in the conversion, making
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
useful also in the study of transformations involving pure states.

Proof.

(i)

A necessary and sufficient condition for
R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)<\infty
can be seen to be
\supp\rho\subseteq\supp\sigma

Douglas 1966
. Applying the same reasoning to
R_{\max}(\sigma\|\rho)
then means that the supports must be equal.

(ii)

Follows from the fact that
R_{\max}(\lambda\rho\|\mu\sigma)=\lambda^{-1}\mu R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)
, which can be easily verified from the definition of
R_{\max}
.

(iii)

From the positivity of
R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)
for any states
\rho
and
\sigma
, we can see that

\displaystyle\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\,R_{\max}(\sigma\|\rho)

\displaystyle=\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\left[\inf\left\{\left.\lambda\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq\lambda\sigma\right\}\inf\left\{\left.\mu\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\sigma\leq\mu\rho\right\}\right]

(22)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left.\lambda\mu\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq\lambda\sigma,\;\sigma\leq\mu\rho,\;\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\}.

(23)

Observe that any feasible solution to the problem

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\left.\gamma\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq\widetilde{\sigma}\leq\gamma\rho,\;\widetilde{\sigma}\in\cone(\mathbb{F})\right\}

(24)

gives a feasible solution to Eq. (
23
) as

\displaystyle\sigma=\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}}{\Tr\widetilde{\sigma}},\quad\lambda=\Tr\widetilde{\sigma},\quad\mu=\frac{\gamma}{\Tr\widetilde{\sigma}}

(25)

with objective function value
\lambda\mu=\gamma
. Conversely, any feasible solution
\{\sigma,\lambda,\mu\}
to Eq. (
23
) gives a feasible solution to Eq. (
25
) as
\widetilde{\sigma}=\lambda\sigma
,
\gamma=\lambda\mu
. The two problems are therefore equivalent.

To show that the infimum is achieved when
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)<\infty
, take any optimal sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\gamma_{n}\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
and
\rho\leq\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}\leq\gamma_{n}\rho
. Since
\left\lVert\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}\right\rVert_{\,\infty}\leq\left\lVert\gamma_{n}\rho\right\rVert_{\,\infty}\leq\gamma_{n}
, the sequence
(\widetilde{\sigma}_{n})_{n}
is bounded, and so by the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem it has a convergent subsequence
(\widetilde{\sigma}_{n_{k}})_{k}\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$k\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}\widetilde{\sigma}
. The closedness of
\cone(\mathbb{F})
(itself a consequence of the assumed closedness of
\mathbb{F}
) ensures that
\widetilde{\sigma}\in\cone(\mathbb{F})
. But then
(\widetilde{\sigma}_{n_{k}}-\rho)_{k}
and
(\gamma_{n_{k}}\rho-\widetilde{\sigma}_{n_{k}})_{k}
also converge as sums of convergent sequences, and by the closedness of
\mathbb{H}_{+}
they must converge to a positive semidefinite operator. Hence
\rho\leq\widetilde{\sigma}\leq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\rho
as desired.

The dual form (Eq. (
16
)) is obtained through standard Lagrange duality arguments
Ponstein 2004
. The fact that strong duality (equality between the primal and the dual) holds can be ensured by Slater’s theorem
(
Rockafellar 1970
, Thm. 28.2)
, applicable since the choice of
B=\mathbbm{1}
,
A=\varepsilon\mathbbm{1}
for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
is strongly feasible for the dual.

Eq. (
17
) follows since any feasible solution to this program can be rescaled as
A\mapsto A/\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle
,
B\mapsto B/\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle
to give a feasible solution to the dual, and vice versa.
Here, we implicitly constrain ourselves to
B
such that
\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle\neq 0
and
\left\langle B,\sigma\right\rangle\neq 0\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
to make the expressions well defined; this can always be ensured by perturbing
B
by a small multiple of the identity.

(iv)

If
\rho\in\mathbb{F}
, then
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\leq R_{\max}(\rho\|\rho)^{2}=1
, and this is the least value that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
can take for any state. Conversely, if
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=1
, then by (
iii
) we have that there exists a state
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\sigma\leq\rho\leq\sigma
, which is only possible if
\rho=\sigma
.

(v)

If either of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
or
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega)
is infinite, then the relation is trivial, so assume otherwise. Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
be an optimal state such that
\rho\leq\lambda\sigma
and
\sigma\leq\mu\rho
with
\lambda\mu=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
, and analogously let
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}
be such that
\omega\leq\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime}\leq\mu^{\prime}\omega
with
\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega)
, which all exist by (
iii
). Then

\displaystyle 0\leq\rho\otimes(\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}-\omega)+(\lambda\sigma-\rho)\otimes\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}=(\lambda\sigma)\otimes(\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime})-\rho\otimes\omega

(26)

and analogously
(\lambda\sigma)\otimes(\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime})\leq(\lambda\mu\rho)\otimes(\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\omega)
, which shows that
\sigma\otimes\sigma^{\prime}
is a feasible solution for the projective robustness of
\rho\otimes\omega
, yielding
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho\otimes\omega)\leq\lambda\mu\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega)
. Weak sub-multiplicativity can be shown analogously, first choosing
\omega=\rho
and then by induction on
n
.

(vi)

As in (
v
), we take
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\rho\leq\lambda\sigma
and
\sigma\leq\mu\rho
, and
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\omega\leq\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime}\leq\mu^{\prime}\omega
. We then have that

\displaystyle t\rho+(1-t)\omega

\displaystyle\leq t\lambda\sigma+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}=\left[t\lambda+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}\right]\frac{t\lambda\sigma+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}{t\lambda+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}}

(27)

where
\displaystyle\frac{t\lambda\sigma+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}{t\lambda+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{F}
by convexity of
\mathbb{F}
. Now,

\displaystyle\frac{t\lambda\sigma+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}{t\lambda+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t\lambda\mu\rho+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\omega}{t\lambda+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}}\leq\frac{\max\{\lambda\mu,\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\}\left[t\rho+(1-t)\omega\right]}{t\lambda+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}}.

(28)

Using this state as a feasible solution for
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
gives

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(t\rho+(1-t)\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\left[t\lambda+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}\right]\frac{\max\{\lambda\mu,\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\}}{t\lambda+(1-t)\lambda^{\prime}}=\max\{\lambda\mu,\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\}

(29)

as was to be shown.

(vii)

Establishing lower semicontinuity is equivalent to showing that the sublevel sets
\left\{\left.\omega\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega)\leq\gamma\right\}
are closed for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}

(
Rockafellar 1970
, Thm. 7.1)
. Consider then a sequence
\omega_{n}\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}\omega
such that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega_{n})\leq\gamma\;\forall n
for some
\gamma
. By (
iii
), this entails that there exists
\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}\in\cone(\mathbb{F})
such that
\omega_{n}\leq\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}\leq\gamma\omega_{n}
for each
n
. Since
(\widetilde{\sigma}_{n})_{n}
then forms a bounded sequence, we can assume that it converges as
\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}\widetilde{\sigma}
, up to passing to a subsequence.
The closedness of
\cone(\mathbb{F})
ensures that
\widetilde{\sigma}\in\cone(\mathbb{F})
. The convergent sequences
(\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}-\omega_{n})_{n}
and
(\gamma\omega_{n}-\widetilde{\sigma}_{n})_{n}
then must converge to positive semidefinite operators by the closedness of the positive semidefinite cone. This gives
\omega\leq\widetilde{\sigma}\leq\gamma\omega
, showing that the sublevel set of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
is closed as desired. Since
\gamma
was arbitrary, the desired statement follows.

(viii)

The lower bound is obtained by noting that

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\,R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\,R_{\max}(\sigma\|\rho)

(30)

\displaystyle\geq\left[\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\right]\left[\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}R_{\max}(\sigma\|\rho)\right]

\displaystyle=R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}.

The upper bounds follow by using
\sigma^{\star}_{R}
and
\sigma^{\star}_{W}
as feasible solutions in the definition of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
. The relation with the smallest eigenvalue follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,R_{\max}(\sigma^{\star}_{R}\|\rho)

(31)

\displaystyle\leq R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,\max_{\omega\in\mathbb{D}}R_{\max}(\omega\|\rho)

\displaystyle=R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1},

where we relaxed the optimisation to all density matrices
\mathbb{D}
and used Lemma
2
below.

∎

We employed the following auxiliary lemma, first shown in
Haapasalo et al. 2014
.

Lemma 2
.

For any state
\rho
, it holds that
\displaystyle\max_{\omega\in\mathbb{D}}R_{\max}(\omega\|\rho)=\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1}
.

Proof.

If
\rho
is not full rank, we can choose
\omega
supported on
\ker(\rho)
so that
R_{\max}(\omega\|\rho)=\infty=\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1}
. Assuming then that
\rho
is full rank, for any
\omega\in\mathbb{D}
it holds that

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\max}(\omega)\,\mathbbm{1}

(32)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\max}(\omega)\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1}\,\rho

where we used that
\lambda_{\max}(\omega)=\min\left\{\left.\lambda\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\omega\leq\lambda\mathbbm{1}\right\}
and
\lambda_{\min}(\rho)=\max\left\{\left.\lambda\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\geq\lambda\mathbbm{1}\right\}
. This constitutes a feasible solution to
R_{\max}(\omega\|\rho)
, yielding

\displaystyle R_{\max}(\omega\|\rho)\leq\lambda_{\max}(\omega)\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1}\leq\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1}.

(33)

The proof is concluded by noticing that the bound is saturated by the choice of
\omega=\ket{\psi}\!\bra{\psi}
with
\ket{\psi}
being an eigenvector of
\rho
corresponding to
\lambda_{\min}(\rho)
.
∎

The quantity
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
, defined in Eq. (
14
), obeys a similar set of properties. The proof is obtained in complete analogy with the above.

Corollary 3
.

The free projective robustness
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
satisfies the following properties.

(i)

It is invariant under scaling, that is,
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\lambda\rho)=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
for any
\lambda>0
.

(ii)

When
\mathbb{F}
is a convex set,
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
can be computed as the optimal value of a conic linear optimisation problem:

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left.\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq_{\mathbb{F}}\widetilde{\sigma}\leq\gamma\rho,\;\widetilde{\sigma}\in\cone(\mathbb{F})\right\}

(34)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left.\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle=1,\;B-A\in\cone(\mathbb{F})\textup{*},\;A\in\cone(\mathbb{F})\textup{*},\;B\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\right\}

(35)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left.\frac{\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle}{\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\frac{\left\langle A,\sigma\right\rangle}{\left\langle B,\sigma\right\rangle}\leq 1\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F},\;A\in\cone(\mathbb{F})\textup{*},\;B\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\right\}.

(36)

Furthermore, the infimum in (
34
) is achieved as long as it is finite.

(iii)

\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is faithful, that is,
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=1\iff\rho\in\mathbb{F}
.

(iv)

When
\mathbb{F}
is a convex set,
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is quasiconvex:
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(t\rho+(1-t)\omega)\leq\max\{\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho),\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\omega)\}
for any
t\in[0,1]
.

(v)

\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is lower semicontinuous, that is,
\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho_{n})
for any sequence
(\rho_{n})_{n}
which converges to
\rho
.

(vi)

It can be bounded as

\displaystyle R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}\leq\;

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\leq R_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,R_{\max}(\sigma^{\star}_{R}\|\rho),

(37)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\Omega_\mathbb{F}^\mathbb{F}(\rho)}\leq R_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1}

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\leq W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}\,R_{\max}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho\|\sigma^{\star}_{W}),

(38)

where
\sigma^{\star}_{R}
is an optimal state such that
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=R_{\max}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho\|\sigma^{\star}_{R})
, and analogously
\sigma^{\star}_{W}
is an optimal state such that
W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=R_{\max}(\sigma^{\star}_{W}\|\rho)^{-1}
, whenever such states exist.

One could also establish the condition under which
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is finite, although it does not take a straightforward form as in the case of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
. Specifically, we have that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
is finite if and only if there exists a state
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\supp\sigma\subseteq\supp\rho
and
\rho+\widetilde{\tau}\propto\sigma
for some
\widetilde{\tau}\in\cone(\mathbb{F})
.
For our purposes, it will suffice to make note of the fact that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
, meaning that diverging projective robustness implies that the free projective robustness also must diverge.

Computability

We have seen in Theorem
1
and Corollary
3
that, in all convex resource theories,
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
are both given as the optimal values of convex optimisation problems.
As long as the constraint
\sigma\in\cone(\mathbb{F})
(or, equivalently,
X\in\cone(\mathbb{F})\textup{*}
) can be expressed using linear matrix inequalities, the two optimisation problems are computable as semidefinite programs (SDP).

An example is the resource theory of coherence, where
\mathbb{F}
denotes the set of all diagonal (incoherent) states in a given basis and

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\inf\left\{\left.\gamma\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq\widetilde{\sigma}\leq\gamma\rho,\;\widetilde{\sigma}\geq 0,\;\widetilde{\sigma}=\Delta(\widetilde{\sigma})\right\}

(39)

with
\Delta(\cdot)\coloneqq\sum_{i}\ket{i}\!\bra{i}\cdot\ket{i}\!\bra{i}
denoting the completely dephasing channel (diagonal map) in the incoherent orthonormal basis
\{\ket{i}\}
. A very similar expression can be obtained also, for instance, in the theory of imaginarity, where the free states
\mathbb{F}
are those that only have real coefficients in a given basis — here, we simply replace
\Delta
with the map
X\mapsto\Re X
. An SDP expression is also immediate when the set
\mathbb{F}
consists only of a single state — e.g. in the theories of athermality (thermodynamics) and purity — or when
\mathbb{F}
is a polytope, that is, a convex combination of a finite number of states, which is the case e.g. in the resource theory of magic.

Another important case is the resource theory of non-positive partial transpose, in which case
\mathbb{F}
consists of bipartite states whose partial transpose is positive, and we have the SDP expressions

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left.\gamma\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq\widetilde{\sigma}\leq\gamma\rho,\;\widetilde{\sigma}\geq 0,\;\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Gamma}\geq 0\right\}

(40)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left.\gamma\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq\widetilde{\sigma}\leq\gamma\rho,\;\rho^{\Gamma}\leq\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Gamma},\;\widetilde{\sigma}\geq 0,\;\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Gamma}\geq 0\right\}

with
(\cdot)^{\Gamma}
denoting partial transposition in any chosen basis.

III.2
Necessary condition for probabilistic transformations

The usefulness of the projective robustness measures stems from their exceptionally strong monotonicity, which can be used to establish much stronger limitations than commonly encountered monotones. Recall that, in probabilistic transformations of resources, monotonicity is typically required to hold on average.
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
, however, cannot be increased even if we postselect on a particular outcome of a probabilistic transformation.

Theorem 4

(
Regula 2022
)
.

If there exists a probabilistic free transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})

(41)

and

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime}).

(42)

Proof.

If there is no
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\supp\rho=\supp\sigma
, then
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\infty
and the result is trivial, so we shall assume otherwise. Then let
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
be a state such that
\rho\leq\lambda\sigma
and
\sigma\leq\mu\rho
with
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\lambda\mu
. Assume that there exists a sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n}\in\mathbb{O}
realising the transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}
. Notice that it necessarily holds that
\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)>0\;\forall n
, since
\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)
. We then have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)}

(43)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq\mu\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}

(44)

using the positivity of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
.
Since each
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is a free operation,
\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)}\in\mathbb{F}
.
This gives that

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}\right)\leq\left[\lambda\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}\right]\!\left[\mu\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)}\right]\!=\lambda\mu=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho).

(45)

As
\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)}\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}\rho^{\prime}
by hypothesis, lower semicontinuity of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
(Theorem
1
(
vii
)) implies that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\leq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
as desired.

The proof for
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is completely analogous, using the fact that any
\mathcal{E}_{n}\in\mathbb{O}
preserves the free cone
\cone(\mathbb{F})
.
∎

This yields a general no-go result: it is impossible to transform any state
\rho
into a state
\rho^{\prime}
such that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)<\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})
or
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)<\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})
, even probabilistically.

As an immediate consequence, we see that no state
\rho
with
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)<\infty
can be converted into a state
\rho^{\prime}
with
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})=\infty
, where the latter type includes in particular all resourceful pure states.
Furthermore, the submultiplicativity of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
ensures that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\otimes n})
is finite for all
n
, which means that the transformation
\rho^{\otimes n}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}
is impossible for any number of copies of
\rho
. In resource theories where
\mathbb{F}
contains a state of full rank, this precludes the transformation of any full-rank state into a non-free pure state, recovering a result of Ref.
Fang and Liu 2020
. However, the result here is strictly stronger, as it extends to any transformation where the target state has a diverging projective robustness, which goes beyond pure states.
For instance, in the theory of quantum entanglement, rank-deficient states
\rho^{\prime}
will have
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})=\infty
unless their support can be spanned by product vectors. Our findings thus extend previous no-go results that dealt with the impossibility of entanglement purification
Kent 1998
;
Jane 2002
;
Horodecki et al. 1999a
;
Horodecki and Demianowicz 2006
;
Regula et al. 2019
.
Similarly, for quantum coherence, it holds that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})=\infty
whenever the support of
\rho^{\prime}
cannot be spanned by incoherent vectors, which also allows us to generalise known restrictions on coherence distillation
Fang et al. 2018
;
Wu et al. 2020
.

We stress that Theorem
4
does not require the convexity of the set
\mathbb{F}
and thus holds also for more general types of quantum resource theories, with closedness of
\mathbb{F}
being the sole requirement. A weaker version of the result can be shown even without assuming closedness: in this case, we would no longer be able to rely on the lower semicontinuity of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
, but
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
would remain a monotone for all probabilistic protocols that succeed with a non-zero probability
p
(i.e., non-asymptotic ones).

We also note that we did not actually use the
complete
positivity of operations in
\mathbb{O}
; the statement of Theorem
4
applies also when
\mathbb{O}
consists of maps that are merely positive.

III.3
Sufficient condition for probabilistic transformations

In order to show that the non-increase of the projective robustness can serve as a
sufficient
condition for resource convertibility, we will need to consider different types of resource theories separately.

Hereafter in the manuscript, we make the assumption that
\mathbb{F}
is convex; as seen in Theorem
1
, this is required to make
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
convex optimisation problems, the strong duality of which will be a crucial ingredient of our proofs. We define the following classes of quantum resource theories
Gour 2017
;
Regula et al. 2020
:

(i)

Affine resource theories
are such that the set
\mathbb{F}
is the intersection of some affine subspace with the set of density matrices. In other words,
\mathbb{F}=\aff(\mathbb{F})\cap\mathbb{D}
, where
\aff
denotes the affine hull

\displaystyle\aff(\mathbb{F})=\left\{\left.\sum_{i}c_{i}\sigma_{i}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\sigma_{i}\in\mathbb{F},\;c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\;\sum_{i}c_{i}=1\right\}.

(46)

Examples include the theories of quantum coherence
Baumgratz et al. 2014
, asymmetry
Gour and Spekkens 2008
, thermodynamics
Horodecki and Oppenheim 2013a
, or imaginarity
Hickey and Gour 2018
;
Wu et al. 2021
.

(ii)

Full-dimensional resource theories
are those in which
\sspan(\mathbb{F})=\mathbb{H}
; in other words,
\mathbb{F}
has a non-zero volume as a subset of
\mathbb{D}
. Examples are the theories of quantum entanglement
Horodecki et al. 2009
, non-positive partial transpose
Horodecki et al. 2009
, or magic (non-stabiliserness)
Veitch et al. 2014
;
Howard and Campbell 2017
.

The two classes together encompass most known quantum resource theories of relevance.

Affine resource theories

We begin with a characterisation of affine resource theories.

Theorem 5
.

In any affine resource theory, there exists a resource–non-generating probabilistic transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}
if any of the following conditions is satisfied:

(i)

\infty>\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})
,

(ii)

\infty=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
and
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})<\infty
,

(iii)

R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\infty
.

Let us clarify this result first. As long as
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})<\infty
holds in the given affine resource theory, then conditions (i) and (ii) together with Theorem
4
imply that

\displaystyle\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}\;\iff\;\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime}),

(47)

as announced in
Regula 2022
. As we remarked before, the condition
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})<\infty
is actually satisfied for
all
states in virtually every theory of interest, and we only consider it here explicitly to account for pathological cases.

An important aspect of the above result is that the transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}
might only be possible with asymptotically vanishing probability. Should this be undesirable, one can also study the problem of when there exists a transformation which succeeds with a non-zero probability. We will give a concise sufficient condition in Lemma
8
shortly: the existence of a transformation with a non-zero probability of success can be guaranteed whenever
\rho
is full rank or a pure state.

We begin with a useful lemma based on the methods of
Regula et al. 2020
.

Lemma 6
.

In any affine resource theory, the projective robustness as well as its reciprocal can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left.\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle=1,\;\left\langle B-A,\sigma\right\rangle=0\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F},\;A,B\geq 0\right\},

(48)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left.\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle=1,\;\left\langle B-A,\sigma\right\rangle=0\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F},\;A,B\geq 0\right\}.

Proof.

Let us define
\Omega_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}
as the projective robustness defined with respect to the set
\aff(\mathbb{F})
, that is,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho)=\inf_{Z\in\aff(\mathbb{F})}R_{\max}(\rho\|Z)\,R_{\max}(Z\|\rho).

(49)

Importantly, for density operators, this makes no quantitative difference: any feasible
X\in\aff(\mathbb{F})
which satisfies
\rho\leq\lambda X
is necessarily positive, which means that all feasible
X
actually belong to
\mathbb{F}
, and so
\Omega_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho)=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
. However, by taking the dual of the program (
49
) as in the proof of Theorem
1
, we get

\displaystyle\Omega_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left.\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle=1,\;B-A\in\aff(\mathbb{F})\textup{*},\;A,B\geq 0\right\}.

(50)

The dual cone
\aff(\mathbb{F})\textup{*}\coloneqq\left\{\left.X\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\langle X,Z\right\rangle\geq 0\;\forall Z\in\aff(\mathbb{F})\right\}
can be shown to consist of operators
X
such that
\left\langle X,Z\right\rangle
is constant on the set
\aff(\mathbb{F})
, that is,
\left\langle X,Z\right\rangle=k
for all
Z\in\aff(\mathbb{F})
for some
k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. This can be seen as follows. Assume that
X\in\aff(\mathbb{F})\textup{*}
but
X
is not constant on
\aff(\mathbb{F})
, that is, there exists
Z,Z^{\prime}\in\aff(\mathbb{F})
such that
\left\langle X,Z\right\rangle=k
but
\left\langle X,Z^{\prime}\right\rangle=k^{\prime}
with
k>k^{\prime}
. Let us then define
\widetilde{Z}=tZ^{\prime}+(1-t)Z
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Clearly, any such
\widetilde{Z}
belongs to
\aff(\mathbb{F})
by definition. But, choosing
t>\frac{k}{k-k^{\prime}}
, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{Z},X\right\rangle

\displaystyle=tk^{\prime}+(1-t)k

(51)

\displaystyle=t(k^{\prime}-k)+k

\displaystyle<\frac{k}{k-k^{\prime}}(k^{\prime}-k)+k

\displaystyle=0,

which contradicts the assumption that
X\in\aff(\mathbb{F})\textup{*}
. Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\Omega_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left.\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle=1,\;\exists k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\text{ s.t.\ }\left\langle B-A,Z\right\rangle=k\;\forall Z\in\aff(\mathbb{F}),\;A,B\geq 0\right\}.

(52)

Notice first that it suffices to impose that
\left\langle B-A,\sigma\right\rangle=k\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
, since this is equivalent to
\left\langle B-A,Z\right\rangle=k\;\forall Z\in\aff(\mathbb{F})
.
Now, let
A,B
be feasible solutions such that
\left\langle B-A,\sigma\right\rangle=k\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
for some
k>0
. Then
A^{\prime}=A+k\mathbbm{1}
and
B
are also feasible, but with a strictly larger objective function value. Therefore, any optimal choice of
A,B
(or an optimal sequence of such operators) must satisfy
k=0
.
Putting everything together, we have shown that, for any state
\rho
,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\Omega_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left.\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle=1,\;\left\langle B-A,\sigma\right\rangle=0\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F},\;A,B\geq 0\right\}

(53)

which is precisely the desired form.

For the reciprocal, we notice that
R_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)^{-1}=\sup\left\{\left.\lambda\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\lambda\rho\leq\sigma\right\}
. This allows us to express

\displaystyle\Omega_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left.\lambda\mu\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\lambda\rho\leq X,\;\mu X\leq\rho,\;X\in\aff(\mathbb{F})\right\}

(54)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left.\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle=1,\;A-B\in\aff(\mathbb{F})\textup{*},\;A,B\geq 0\right\}.

An analogous series of arguments leads us to the expression in the statement of the Lemma.
∎

The main proof can be now shown by generalising an approach of Ref.
Reeb et al. 2011
(cf.
Buscemi and Gour 2017
).

Proof of Theorem
5
.

(i) Assume that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)>1
, since otherwise both states are free and the result is trivial. By hypothesis, we have that there exists a state
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\rho\leq\lambda\sigma
and
\sigma\leq\mu\rho
with
\lambda\mu=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
, and analogously there exists
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\rho^{\prime}\leq\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime}\leq\mu^{\prime}\rho^{\prime}
with
\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})
.
Since
\lambda\mu\geq\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}
by assumption, we can always choose a positive factor
k
such that
k\rho^{\prime}\leq\lambda\sigma^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime}\leq\mu k\rho^{\prime}
. To see this, notice that this is trivial if
\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}
and
\mu\geq\mu^{\prime}
; if
\mu<\mu^{\prime}
, then we take
k=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{\prime}}
; and in the case that
\lambda<\lambda^{\prime}
, it suffices to take
k=\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu}
.
Denoting
\rho^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq k\rho^{\prime}
, define the sequence of maps

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle B_{n},X\right\rangle\left(\lambda\sigma^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime\prime}\right)+\left\langle A_{n},X\right\rangle\left(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{\mu}\sigma^{\prime}\right),

(55)

where
(A_{n})_{n}
and
(B_{n})_{n}
are optimal sequences of feasible operators for
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
in Lemma
6
, that is, ones such that
\left\langle A_{n},\rho\right\rangle\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}\lambda\mu
.
Because the Choi operator of such a map is given by

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}=B_{n}\otimes\left(\lambda\sigma^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime\prime}\right)+A_{n}\otimes\left(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{\mu}\sigma^{\prime}\right),

(56)

the positive semidefiniteness of all involved operators ensures that
J_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}\geq 0
and thus that the map
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is completely positive
Choi 1975
.
For any
\pi\in\mathbb{F}
, the map furthermore satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(\pi)=\left(\lambda-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\left\langle B_{n},\pi\right\rangle\sigma^{\prime}\propto\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F},

(57)

which means that it is a free operation.
Crucially, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)=\underbrace{\frac{1}{\mu}}_{\in(0,1]}\underbrace{\vphantom{\frac{1}{\mu}}\left(\lambda\mu-\left\langle A_{n},\rho\right\rangle\right)}_{\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\,0}\sigma^{\prime}+\underbrace{\vphantom{\frac{1}{\mu}}\left(\left\langle A_{n},\rho\right\rangle-1\right)}_{\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\,\lambda\mu-1}\rho^{\prime\prime}.

(58)

That is, in the limit
n\to\infty
, the sequence of the output operators
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)
converges (up to normalisation) to the target state
\rho^{\prime}
, thus realising the desired free transformation. To complete the proof, we need to ensure that the maps
\mathcal{E}_{n}
are valid probabilistic operations by rescaling them to be trace–non-increasing maps as
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{E}_{n}/\left[\max_{\omega\in\mathbb{D}}\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\omega)\right]
. The sequence of probabilistic free operations
(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{n})_{n}
is precisely our desired protocol which achieves the transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}
.

Remark
.

Noticing that
\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}k(\lambda\mu-1)
, which is non-zero for any
\rho\notin\mathbb{F}
, one can wonder whether there actually exists a possibility that the protocol only succeeds asymptotically, in the sense of vanishing probability of success as in Eq. (
2
). That is indeed the case, and the culprit is the renormalisation coefficient

\displaystyle\max_{\omega\in\mathbb{D}}\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\omega)=\left\lVert(\lambda-k)B_{n}+\left(k-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)A_{n}\right\rVert_{\,\infty}

(59)

which is used to rescale the maps
\mathcal{E}_{n}
to be trace non-increasing.
In general, we have no guarantee that this quantity is bounded, and indeed it might be the case that both
\left\lVert A_{n}\right\rVert_{\,\infty}
and
\left\lVert B_{n}\right\rVert_{\,\infty}
diverge to infinity as
n\to\infty
; such divergent cases are precisely when we get
\Tr\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{n}(\rho)\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}0
after renormalising. On the other hand, when the supremum in the dual program for
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
is achieved by some operators
A
and
B
, then we can choose

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle B,X\right\rangle\left(\lambda\sigma^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime\prime}\right)+\left\langle A,X\right\rangle\left(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{\mu}\sigma^{\prime}\right)

(60)

as an optimal operation which satisfies
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
and
\mathcal{E}(\rho)\propto\rho^{\prime}
. This is the case, for instance, when there exists optimal sequences
(A_{n})_{n}
and
(B_{n})_{n}
which are both bounded: choosing
(n_{k})_{k}
as a sequence of indices such that both of the subsequences
(A_{n_{k}})_{k}
and
(B_{n_{k}})_{k}
converge, it suffices to take
A
and
B
defined as the limits of such subsequences. We will return to this problem in Lemma
8
.

(ii) For the case
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\infty
and
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\eqqcolon\lambda^{\prime}<\infty
, we will find it easier to work with the reciprocal function
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}
, as we have
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}=0
and the optimal value of the dual optimisation problem is finite. Using the dual form of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}
in Lemma
6
, there exists sequences
(A_{n})_{n},(B_{n})_{n}
such that
\left\langle A_{n},\rho\right\rangle\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}0
,
\left\langle B_{n},\rho\right\rangle=1\;\forall n
, and
\left\langle A_{n},\sigma\right\rangle=\left\langle B_{n},\sigma\right\rangle\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F},n\in\mathbb{N}
. This allows us to construct the maps

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle A_{n},X\right\rangle(\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime})+\left\langle B_{n},X\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}

(61)

where
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}
is a state such that
\rho^{\prime}\leq\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}
, which exists by assumption. Clearly,
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}\rho^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)=\left\langle B_{n},\sigma\right\rangle\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}
for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
. Renormalising the operations to be trace non-increasing, we get the desired transformation.

As before, when there exists a choice of
A
,
B
optimal for
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}
, then it suffices to take

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle A,X\right\rangle(\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime})+\left\langle B,X\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}.

(62)

(iii) The case of
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\infty
implies that
\rho
must have a non-trivial overlap with the subspace orthogonal to all states in
\mathbb{F}
, i.e., that there exists a vector
\ket{\psi}
such that
\left\langle\psi,\rho\right\rangle>0
but
\left\langle\psi,\sigma\right\rangle=0
for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
. Were this not the case, by convexity of
\mathbb{F}
we could take a free state whose support is the union of the supports of all free states, and the support of
\rho
would have to be contained in this subspace, contradicting the assumption that
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\infty
. We then construct the map

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle\psi,X\right\rangle\rho^{\prime},

(63)

which achieves the desired transformation with probability
\left\langle\psi,\rho\right\rangle
. The fact that
0\leq\psi\leq\mathbbm{1}
implies that
\left\langle\psi,\rho\right\rangle\in[0,1]
for any state
\rho
, and together with the fact that
\mathcal{E}(\sigma)=0
for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
, we indeed have that
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
.
∎

General resource theories

Let us now consider the case of non-affine resource theories. Here, the issue we encounter is that the simplified form of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
in Lemma
6
is no longer applicable, preventing us from following the same approach. To circumvent this, we will employ the free projective robustness
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}
.

Theorem 7
.

In any convex resource theory, there exists a resource–non-generating probabilistic transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}
if any of the following conditions is satisfied:

(i)

\infty>\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})
,

(ii)

\infty=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
and
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})<\infty
,

(iii)

R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\infty
.

Combining this with the monotonicity result of Theorem
4
, as long as
R_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})<\infty
(which is true for all states in any full-dimensional resource theory), we have the following:

\displaystyle\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\;\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime}),\;\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime}),

(64)

\displaystyle\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}

\displaystyle\Leftarrow\;\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime}).

We see here that this reduces to an ‘if and only if’ statement only when
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})
. Although this might not always be true, we will shortly see that states obeying this requirement can be defined in relevant cases (such as the problem of resource distillation), allowing us to use this result as a necessary and sufficient condition for resource conversion also in non-affine theories.

Proof.

(i) The basic idea is very similar to Theorem
5
. We construct the maps

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle B_{n},X\right\rangle\left(\lambda\sigma^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime\prime}\right)+\left\langle A_{n},X\right\rangle\left(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{\mu}\sigma^{\prime}\right),

(65)

where
(A_{n})_{n},(B_{n})_{n}
are optimal sequences of operators for
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
as before, but now
\sigma^{\prime}
is a state such that
\rho^{\prime\prime}\leq_{\mathbb{F}}\lambda\sigma^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime}\leq\mu\rho^{\prime\prime}
, with
\rho^{\prime\prime}
being the state
\rho^{\prime}
which has been rescaled if needed. The crucial difference now is that
\lambda\sigma^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime\prime}\in\cone(\mathbb{F})
. What this means is that, for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\left\langle B_{n},\sigma\right\rangle\left(\lambda\sigma^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime\prime}\right)+\left\langle A_{n},\sigma\right\rangle\left(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{\mu}\sigma^{\prime}\right)

(66)

\displaystyle\geq_{\mathbb{F}}\left\langle A_{n},\sigma\right\rangle\left(\lambda\sigma^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime\prime}\right)+\left\langle A_{n},\sigma\right\rangle\left(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{\mu}\sigma^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\left\langle A_{n},\sigma\right\rangle\sigma^{\prime}\,\in\cone(\mathbb{F})

which, by the convexity of
\cone(\mathbb{F})
, means that
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)\in\cone(\mathbb{F})
and hence that
\mathcal{E}_{n}\in\mathbb{O}
.
Here, we used that
\left\langle B_{n},\sigma\right\rangle\geq\left\langle A_{n},\sigma\right\rangle
for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
, which is a property obeyed by any feasible dual solution to
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
(cf. Theorem

). Clearly, we also have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)=\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\lambda\mu-\left\langle A_{n},\rho\right\rangle\right)\sigma^{\prime}+\left(\left\langle A_{n},\rho\right\rangle-1\right)\rho^{\prime\prime}\,\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}(\lambda\mu-1)\rho^{\prime\prime}\propto\rho^{\prime}

(67)

as before. The operations
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n}
therefore realise the desired transformation, and with a suitable rescaling they form a valid probabilistic transformation.

(ii) Taking the dual form of the reciprocal function
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}
, we can write

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}=\inf\left\{\left.\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle=1,\;\left\langle A-B,\sigma\right\rangle\geq 0\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F},\;A,B\geq 0\right\}.

(68)

Since
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}=0
by assumption, we can take the optimal choice of such operators satisfying
\left\langle A_{n},\rho\right\rangle\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}0
and construct the maps

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle A_{n},X\right\rangle(\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime})+\left\langle B_{n},X\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}

(69)

where
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}
is a state such that
\rho^{\prime}\leq_{\mathbb{F}}\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}
with
\lambda^{\prime}\coloneqq R_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})
. We can see that
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho)\xrightarrow{\vphantom{.}\smash{{\raisebox{-1.8pt}{\scriptsize{$n\!\to\!\infty$}}}}}\rho^{\prime}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq_{\mathbb{F}}\left\langle B_{n},X\right\rangle(\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime})+\left\langle B_{n},X\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}

(70)

\displaystyle=\left\langle B_{n},X\right\rangle\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\,\in\cone(\mathbb{F})

for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
, as was to be shown.

(iii) Exactly the same as in Theorem
5
.
∎

We now give sufficient conditions which strengthen the result to show that not only can the transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}
be realised probabilistically, it is actually achieved with a non-zero probability of success (that is, no asymptotic protocols are necessary).

Lemma 8
.

Let
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
be two states such that
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}
is possible probabilistically (as per Theorem
5
or
7
). If additionally either of the following is true:

(a)

\rho
is full rank and
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)<\infty
(i.e. there exists a full-rank free state), or

(b)

there is no free state
\sigma
such that
\supp\sigma\subseteq\supp\rho
(i.e.
W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=0
),

then there exists
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
such that
\mathcal{E}(\rho)=p\rho^{\prime}
for some
p>0
.

Note in particular that condition (b) is satisfied for any resourceful pure state.

Proof.

As discussed in the proof of Theorem
5
, the existence of such a map
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
can be ensured by showing that the dual optimisation problems for
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
or
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}
are achieved, in the sense that there exist feasible
A,B
such that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
(or
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}
) equals
\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle
.

(a) When
\rho
is a full-rank state with
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)<\infty
, then any
A\geq 0
feasible for
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
satisfies
\left\langle A,\omega\right\rangle\leq R_{\max}(\omega\|\rho)\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle\leq\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1}\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
for any state
\omega
by Lemma
2
. This means that
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\,\infty}=\max_{\omega\in\mathbb{D}}\left\langle A,\omega\right\rangle
is upper bounded by a constant independent of
A
. Similarly, the condition
\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle=1
ensures that
\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{\,\infty}\leq\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1}
. Any optimal sequences
(A_{n})_{n}
,
(B_{n})_{n}
are therefore bounded, and taking suitable convergent subsequences ensures that the supremum in the dual form of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
is achieved.

(b) Note first that
W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=0
implies
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\infty
, so we consider the quantity
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}
. In this case, we have that
\left\langle\Pi_{\rho},\rho\right\rangle=1
but
\left\langle\Pi_{\rho},\sigma\right\rangle<1\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
, where
\Pi_{\rho}
denotes the projection onto
\supp\rho
. Let
\zeta=\max_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\left\langle\Pi_{\rho},\sigma\right\rangle
and define

\displaystyle A\coloneqq\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}(\mathbbm{1}-\Pi_{\rho}),\quad B\coloneqq\Pi_{\rho}.

(71)

We see that
\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle=0
,
\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle=1
, and
\left\langle A-B,\sigma\right\rangle\geq 0\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
. This means that
A
and
B
are feasible solutions for
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}
which achieve the optimal value
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)^{-1}=0
.
∎

A general consequence of the results in Theorems
5
and
7
is that states with infinite projective robustness can always be interconverted probabilistically; that is,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})=\infty\;\Rightarrow\;\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime},\;\,\rho^{\prime}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho.

(72)

This includes, in particular, all pure resourceful states, and thus implies that no such state can be isolated: any pure state can be obtained from another resourceful pure state with some non-zero probability under resource–non-generating transformations.
This contrasts with some more restricted theories, such as entanglement manipulated by LOCC: in the bipartite setting, pure states can be interconverted only if they share the same Schmidt rank
Bennett et al. 1996b
;
Lo and Popescu 2001
, and in the multipartite setting, there exist inequivalent types of entanglement that cannot be interconverted
Dür et al. 2000
. We thus see that using the operations
\mathbb{O}
allows one to overcome such limitations in the regime of pure-state transformations.
In the context of entanglement theory, similar results about the convertibility of pure states have been shown previously through other approaches
Ishizaka 2004
;
Ishizaka and Plenio 2005
;
Contreras-Tejada et al. 2019
; our approach extend this to general resource theories and to states beyond pure.
Here we remark the rather interesting fact that, when
deterministic
transformations are concerned, the operational capabilities provided by the resource–non-generation operations
\mathbb{O}
are not always significantly larger than those of smaller, physically motivated operations — for example, in the concentration of entanglement from pure states, the maximal operations
\mathbb{O}
do not even outperform one-way LOCC
Regula et al. 2019
.

III.4
Projective robustness in channel discrimination

Another application of the projective robustness
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
can be obtained by connecting it with the advantage that the given resource provides in a class of channel discrimination tasks. This is essentially an extension of similar properties that were recently shown to hold for the robustness
Takagi et al. 2019
and weight
Uola et al. 2020
;
Ducuara and Skrzypczyk 2020
measures. Since this is not related to our main discussion concerned with the uses of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
in constraining resource manipulation, we defer the details to Appendix
A
.

IV
Probabilistic resource distillation

We will now show how our results can be employed to establish strong bounds on the performance of resource distillation tasks. In this setting, we aim to transform a given noisy state
\rho
into some pure resource state
\phi
, often taken to be a maximally resourceful state or a particularly useful state that is necessary in the practical utilisation of a given resource. The representative example of such a task is entanglement distillation, whose probabilistic characterisation attracted significant attention
Lo and Popescu 2001
;
Linden et al. 1998
;
Kent 1998
;
Horodecki et al. 1999a
;
Vidal 1999
;
Horodecki and Demianowicz 2006
.

However, we already know from Theorem
4
that transformations
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\phi
are never possible when
\rho
is a generic (full-rank) noisy state. This can be circumvented by allowing a small error in the conversion, which means that we will instead aim to achieve a transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\tau
, where
\tau
is a state such that
F(\tau,\phi)\geq 1-\varepsilon
with
F(\rho,\sigma)\coloneqq\|\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\vphantom{\rho}\sigma}\|_{1}^{2}
being the fidelity. We then endeavour to understand and bound the error
\varepsilon
necessarily incurred in such protocols.

Let us begin by recalling some bounds that have been previously established in this context for general resource theories. The robustness
R_{\mathbb{F}}
can provide both one-shot
Regula et al. 2020
and many-copy bounds
Regula and Takagi 2021
for distillation, with the latter particularly applicable to asymptotic transformation rates. When it comes to bounds on resource overheads — that is, the number of copies of a state required to realise a desired distillation protocol — the resource weight
W_{\mathbb{F}}
was found to provide strong constraints
Regula and Takagi 2021
;
Fang and Liu 2022
. However, such results were mostly concerned with deterministic transformations, and probabilistic conversion protocols significantly complicate the characterisation of distillation errors
Regula and Takagi 2021
;
Fang and Liu 2022
. We will see that the projective robustness
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
, which can be thought of as combining the properties of
R_{\mathbb{F}}
and
W_{\mathbb{F}}
, will allow us to establish broadly applicable bounds that overcome the limitations of previous approaches.

Another approach to constraining the distillation of general resources, including in a probabilistic setting, was introduced in Ref.
Fang and Liu 2020
in the form of the
eigenvalue bound
. This restriction, applicable to the distillation of pure states from full-rank input states, was claimed to establish a trade-off relation between the errors and the probability of success of transformations — although there are issues with this latter claim (as will be shown in Section
IV.2
), the result can nevertheless be used to lower bound transformation errors in general probabilistic protocols as considered here. However, we will see in several examples that it typically does not yield good restrictions: the bound obtained from the projective robustness
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
can never be worse than the eigenvalue bound, and in most cases it performs significantly better.

IV.1
Improved bounds on probabilistic distillation errors and overheads

We show that the projective robustness can be used to establish a tight bound on distillation error in general probabilistic transformations, giving a non-trivial restriction for all full-rank states as well as rank-deficient states that satisfy
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)<\infty
. The resourcefulness of the target state
\phi
will be quantified using the overlap
F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)\coloneqq\max_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\braket{\phi|\sigma|\phi}
, which effectively determines how difficult a given state is to distill. Note that
F_{\mathbb{F}}
is a reverse monotone, in the sense that it gets smaller the more resourceful a state is.

Theorem 9

(
Regula 2022
)
.

If there exists a free transformation
\rho\!\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\!\tau

\tau
is a state satisfying

for some resourceful pure state
\phi
, then

\displaystyle\varepsilon\geq\left(\frac{F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)}{1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,+1\right)^{-1}

(73)

where
F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)=\max_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\left\langle\phi,\sigma\right\rangle
. The statement is valid for all values of
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and all
F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)\in[0,1)
as long as one understands
0\infty\coloneqq\infty

The result can be viewed either as a restriction on achievable distillation fidelity
1-\varepsilon

\phi
, or as a constraint on how much of the given resource (quantified by
F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)
) can be distilled from
\rho

\varepsilon

We note that such thresholds were studied in the theory of quantum entanglement
Kent 1998
;
Horodecki et al. 1999a
, but even in that case no general quantitative bound was previously known.

Proof.

Assume first that
\varepsilon>0
and
F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)>0

\displaystyle\left\langle\tau,\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left\langle\sigma,\phi\right\rangle\leq\lambda F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi),

(74)

\displaystyle 1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\sigma,\mathbbm{1}-\phi\right\rangle\leq\mu\left\langle\tau,\mathbbm{1}-\phi\right\rangle.

Using the assumption

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\tau)=\lambda\mu\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon)\left(1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)\right)}{\varepsilon F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)},

(75)

and the transformation is not possible. If, however,

)) as well as with the achievable performance of distillation protocols. The achievable error is plotted in both the deterministic (
p=1
) case and the probabilistic case for a choice of
p\in\{0.75,0.5\}
(from top to bottom, respectively).

We see in
(a)
that, for a single copy of an isotropic state, probabilistic protocols offer no improvement in distillation error — the projective robustness bound is actually achievable deterministically, and indeed it is achievable by simply leaving the state
\rho
as it is and not performing any operations whatsoever. This provides a strengthening of the result of
Linden et al. 1998
, where it was shown that probabilistic LOCC cannot increase the distillation fidelity of a single isotropic state; we extend this to all PPT-preserving (or non-entangling) operations. (We will later see an analytical proof of this fact in Corollary
15
.) Note that the eigenvalue bound is too weak to reach the same conclusion.

In
(b)
, where two copies of an isotropic state are used, we see that probabilistic protocols offer an enhanced performance over deterministic ones. We also see that the protocol for
p=0.5
achieves the projective robustness bound, which means that no further improvement in fidelity can be obtained by choosing
p<0.5
.

The plot in
(c)
, on the other hand, suggests that an improved distillation fidelity can be obtained by lowering the probability below
p=0.5
. Note the extremely poor performance of the eigenvalue bound in comparison with the projective robustness and the achievable protocols.

We remark here that yet another approach to bounding achievable error in probabilistic transformations was introduced in Ref.
Regula and Takagi 2021
based on the strong monotonicity of the robustness and weight measures. Although accurate for
p\approx 1
, these bounds quickly become trivial as
p
decreases, which is why we do not include them in our comparison.

An important consequence of the universal bound of Theorem
9
is that we can use the subadditivity of the projective robustness to provide limitations on the overhead required in many-copy transformations. Specifically, consider a protocol where one aims to minimise the transformation error by using more input copies of the given state. Given access to
\rho^{\otimes n}
, we are then concerned with the question of how many copies are required to achieve a desired accuracy.

Corollary 10
.

Assume that the given convex resource theory is closed under tensor product, in the sense that
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\Rightarrow\sigma^{\otimes n}\in\mathbb{F}
.
If there exists a free transformation
\rho^{\otimes n}\!\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\!\tau
such that
\tau
is a state satisfying
F(\tau,\phi)\geq 1-\varepsilon
for some resourceful pure state
\phi
, then

\displaystyle n\geq\log_{\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}\frac{(1-\varepsilon)\left(1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)\right)}{\varepsilon F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)}.

(77)

Proof.

Follows from Eq. (
75
) and the submultiplicativity of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
(Theorem
1
(
v
)).
∎

The practical performance of this bound has already been demonstrated in Ref.
Regula 2022
, where it was shown in particular that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
significantly improves on the eigenvalue bound.
We note that, although we considered the target state to be a single system
\phi
, one can equivalently study the overhead in transformations of the type
\rho^{\otimes n}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\phi^{\otimes m}
. Importantly, the bound only depends on the overlap
F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)
of the target state, which in many resource theories satisfies
F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi^{\otimes m})=F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)^{m}
and is therefore simple to compute for many-copy target states (this includes the theories of entanglement
Shimony 1995
, coherence, athermality, and magic when
\phi
is a state of up to 3 qubits
Bravyi et al. 2019
).

Our bound here shows that the total overhead
n
of any distillation protocol must scale as
\Omega\left(\log\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)=\Omega\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)
, even if the transformation probability is allowed to vanish asymptotically. This strengthens the previous observation of Ref.
Fang and Liu 2020
where this was shown to hold for constant, non-vanishing probability of success.

Let us comment briefly on the applicability and usefulness of the restrictions introduced here.
The key property of the bounds of this section is to constrain all possible probabilistic transformations, regardless of their probability of success.
In some cases, rather than a constraint on
all
probabilistic protocols, one may wish to obtain bounds on transformations achievable for a certain fixed probability — such restrictions are the subject of the forthcoming Section
V
.
If constraints only on deterministic protocols are desired, there is no need to consider the projective robustness: in such a case, either the robustness-
Regula et al. 2020
;
Regula and Takagi 2021
or the weight-based
Regula and Takagi 2021
;
Fang and Liu 2022
bound will perform better than
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
in the context of resource distillation.

IV.2
Comparison with the eigenvalue bound

The eigenvalue bound from Ref.
Fang and Liu 2020
states that, for any full-rank input state
\rho
and any
\varepsilon
-error probabilistic protocol that succeeds with probability
p
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\geq\frac{\lambda_{\min}(\rho)\left(1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)\right)}{R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)},

(78)

where
\lambda_{\min}
denotes the smallest eigenvalue.
We have already seen in Figure
2
(cf. also
Regula 2022
) that the restrictions obtained using the projective robustness can significantly improve on this bound; we now explicitly prove that the
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
-based bounds are never worse than the eigenvalue bound.
In fact, we will also show that the dependence of the eigenvalue bound on the probability of success
p
is superficial — we can replace
p
with the constant
1
and the bound still holds for any probabilistic transformation, including protocols that succeed with asymptotically vanishing probability.

Lemma 11
.

If there exists a probabilistic transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\tau
such that
\tau
is a state satisfying
F(\tau,\phi)\geq 1-\varepsilon
for some resourceful pure state
\phi
, then

\displaystyle\varepsilon\geq\frac{1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)}{\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}\geq\frac{\lambda_{\min}(\rho)\left(1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)\right)}{R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}.

(79)

Proof.

The first inequality is essentially a weaker version of Theorem
9
; here, we write
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\tau)=\lambda\mu
, recall from Eq. (
74
) that

\displaystyle 1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\mu\left\langle\tau,\mathbbm{1}-\phi\right\rangle\leq\mu\varepsilon,

(80)

and use the trivial lower bound
\lambda\geq 1
to obtain

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\tau)\geq\mu\geq\frac{1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)}{\varepsilon}

(81)

using the monotonicity of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
. Finally, the upper bound
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\leq R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1}
(see Theorem
1
(
viii
)) gives the statement of the Lemma.
∎

Let us discuss a consequence of this finding. One of the stated aims of the eigenvalue bound was to understand trade-offs between the error
\varepsilon
and probability
p

Fang and Liu 2020
; in this sense, it can be viewed as a bound on the probability of success as

\displaystyle p\leq\frac{\varepsilon R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}{\lambda_{\min}(\rho)\!\left(1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)\right)}\eqqcolon v(\rho,\varepsilon),

(82)

However, our result implies that this bound is
unable
to give any non-trivial restrictions on the probability, since it holds that
\displaystyle v(\rho,\varepsilon)\geq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,/\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\tau)
.
What this means is that, when
v(\rho,\varepsilon)<1
, this implies that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\tau)>\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
and hence the transformation is actually impossible with any non-zero probability (as per Theorem
4
), while if
v(\rho,\varepsilon)\geq 1
, then the bound trivialises. Indeed, the stronger statement of the eigenvalue bound that we derived in Lemma
11
, and the ensuing
superficiality of the dependence of Eq. (
82
) on the probability
p
, means that the eigenvalue bound applies in the same way regardless of the probability of success of the distillation protocol.

Remark
.

Ref.
Fang and Liu 2020
concluded from the eigenvalue bound that probabilistic transformations from a full-rank state
\rho
to a resourceful pure state
\phi
is impossible in
any
resource theory. This is not strictly correct, as can be seen from the example where
\mathbb{F}=\{\ket{0}\!\bra{0}\}
and
\rho
is any full-rank one-qubit state. The state
\ket{1}
can then be probabilistically obtained from
\rho
by simply projecting onto it — such a projection cannot generate any resource when acting on
\ket{0}\!\bra{0}
, so it is a free probabilistic operation. The missing assumption in Ref.
Fang and Liu 2020
is that the robustness
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
is finite. (Note also that this no-go result is significantly strengthened by our Theorem
4
, as we discussed previously).

IV.3
Achievable fidelity

We now investigate the tightness of the general bound of Theorem
9
by constructing achievable protocols for the distillation of resources.

Theorem 12
.

Let
\phi
be a resourceful pure state in any convex resource theory. For any
\varepsilon
satisfying

\displaystyle\varepsilon\geq\left(\frac{\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}{R_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)-1}\,+1\right)^{-1}\!,

(83)

there exists a resource non-generating protocol
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\tau_{\varepsilon}
where
\tau_{\varepsilon}
is a state such that
F(\tau_{\varepsilon},\phi)\geq 1-\varepsilon
.

If the resource theory is affine, it suffices to take

\displaystyle\varepsilon\geq\left(\frac{\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}{R_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)-1}\,+1\right)^{-1}\!.

(84)

Proof.

If
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\infty
, then Theorems
5
–
7
already tell us that this transformation is possible with
\varepsilon=0
, so we assume that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)<\infty
. Let us then consider the two non-trivial cases: (i)
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)<\infty
, and (ii) the resource theory is affine and
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)<\infty
.
Notice that the chosen value of
\varepsilon
in (i) Eq. (
83
) or (ii) Eq. (
84
) entails that

\displaystyle\mathrm{(i)}\quad\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\left(R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)-1\right),

(85)

\displaystyle\mathrm{(ii)}\quad\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\left(R_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)-1\right).

Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
be a state such that (i)
\phi\leq_{\mathbb{F}}\lambda\sigma
with
\lambda=R_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)
or (ii)
\phi\leq\lambda\sigma
with
\lambda=R_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)
, and define the state

\displaystyle\tau_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\phi+\varepsilon\frac{\lambda\sigma-\phi}{\lambda-1}

(86)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[(1-\varepsilon)-\varepsilon\frac{1}{\lambda-1}\right]\phi+\varepsilon\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\sigma.

Let us first assume that
(1-\varepsilon)\geq\varepsilon\frac{1}{\lambda-1}
, i.e.
\varepsilon\leq\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}
.
We then have

\displaystyle\mathrm{(i)}\quad\tau_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq_{\mathbb{F}}\left[(1-\varepsilon)-\varepsilon\frac{1}{\lambda-1}\right]\lambda\sigma+\varepsilon\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\sigma

(87)

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)\lambda\sigma,

\displaystyle\mathrm{(ii)}\quad\tau_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\left[(1-\varepsilon)-\varepsilon\frac{1}{\lambda-1}\right]\lambda\sigma+\varepsilon\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\sigma

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)\lambda\sigma,

\displaystyle\mathrm{(i),(ii)}\quad\tau_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\sigma.

This gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{(i)}\quad\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\tau_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}(\lambda-1)

(88)

\displaystyle\mathrm{(ii)}\quad\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\tau_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}(\lambda-1),

which in particular implies that (i)
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\tau_{\varepsilon})\leq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
or (ii)
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\tau_{\varepsilon})\leq\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
, as the right-hand sides of (
85
) and (
88
) coincide. Invoking (i) Theorem
7
or (ii) Theorem
5
shows the existence of a probabilistic transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\tau_{\varepsilon}
. Since
F(\tau_{\varepsilon},\phi)\geq 1-\varepsilon
by construction, we have thus shown the desired achievability.

We need to consider separately the case of large error,
(1-\varepsilon)<\varepsilon\frac{1}{\lambda-1}
. Here, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathrm{(i)}

\displaystyle\tau_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon)\phi+(1-\varepsilon)(\lambda\sigma-\phi)+\left[\varepsilon\frac{1}{\lambda-1}-(1-\varepsilon)\right](\lambda\sigma-\phi)

(89)

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)\lambda\sigma+\left[\varepsilon\frac{1}{\lambda-1}-(1-\varepsilon)\right](\lambda\sigma-\phi)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\tau_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{F}

since
\phi\leq_{\mathbb{F}}\lambda\sigma
by assumption. Hence, the transformation into
\tau_{\varepsilon}
trivialises. In the case (ii), we cannot make the exact same argument, but we can simply change the target state into

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq(1-\varepsilon)\phi+(1-\varepsilon)(\lambda\sigma-\phi)+\left[\varepsilon\frac{1}{\lambda-1}-(1-\varepsilon)\right]\sigma\in\mathbb{F}

(90)

so that
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\tau^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
is again trivially possible.
∎

Combining Theorem
12
and Theorem
9
gives the bounds

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{R_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)-1}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)+1\right)^{-1}\geq\inf_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}}\left[1-F\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)},\phi\right)\right]\geq\left(\frac{F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)}{1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,+1\right)^{-1},

(91)

which are valid in any convex resource theory, and

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{R_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)-1}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)+1\right)^{-1}\geq\inf_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}}\left[1-F\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)},\phi\right)\right]\geq\left(\frac{F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)}{1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,+1\right)^{-1},

(92)

which hold for affine resources. We stress once again that the converse (lower) bounds are valid in any resource theory with any set of free operations contained in
\mathbb{O}
, while the achievable (upper) bounds are valid specifically for resource–non-generating operations
\mathbb{O}
.

A natural question then is: when can these bounds coincide, meaning that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
exactly characterises the least error achievable in distillation of
\phi
?
Comparing the bounds in Eqs. (
91
)–(
92
), we see that a necessary and sufficient condition for the upper and lower bounds to be equal is that
R_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)=F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)^{-1}
, or
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)=F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)^{-1}
in affine resource theories. Remarkably, states satisfying the latter equality exist in
every
convex quantum resource theory — these are the maximally resourceful (so-called ‘golden’) states
Regula et al. 2020
. Throughout this paper, we understand ‘maximally resourceful’ to mean specifically any state which maximises the robustness
R_{\mathbb{F}}
, the motivation for this choice being precisely that such states satisfy
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)=F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)^{-1}
. We formalise the consequences of such a choice as follows.

Corollary 13

(
Regula 2022
)
.

Let
{\phi_{\star}}
be a pure state which maximises the robustness
R_{\mathbb{F}}
among all states of the same dimension. Then, as long as either:

(i)

the given resource theory is affine, or

(ii)

it holds that
R_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})=R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})
,

then there exists a resource–non-generating protocol that achieves the bound of Theorem
9
. Specifically, for any input state
\rho
it holds that

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}}\left[1-F\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)},{\phi_{\star}}\right)\right]=\left(\frac{F_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})}{1-F_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,+1\right)^{-1}\!\!.

(93)

The choice of a maximally resourceful state as the distillation target is a natural one in most quantum resources. In the fundamental example of quantum entanglement,
{\phi_{\star}}
can be understood as any maximally entangled state
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\ket{ii}
of some dimension; indeed, these states satisfy
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})=F_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})^{-1}

Shimony 1995
;
Vidal and Tarrach 1999
, and so we obtain an exact quantitative characterisation of probabilistic distillation of entanglement. The same property holds true in multi-level bipartite entanglement of higher Schmidt rank
Johnston et al. 2018
, genuine multipartite entanglement
Contreras-Tejada et al. 2019
, as well as in the resource theory of multi-level quantum coherence
Johnston et al. 2018
. For other theories such as magic-state quantum computation, it is not always the case that
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})=F_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})^{-1}

Regula 2018
, but one can nevertheless find examples of states for which the bounds in (
91
) coincide — for example, the three-qubit Hoggar state, or the single-qutrit Strange and Norell states
Takagi et al. 2022
. For any such target state, (
93
) is satisfied.
We also stress that Corollary
13
gives an exact expression for the least achievable distillation error under resource–non-generating operations in
any
affine resource theory.

Proof.

The key property that we use is that
R_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})=F_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})^{-1}

(
Regula et al. 2020
, Theorem 4)
, which holds for any state
{\phi_{\star}}
that maximises the robustness in some dimension (noting that the input
\rho
and the target
{\phi_{\star}}
can act on spaces of different dimension). If the resource theory is affine, Theorem
12
immediately gives us our desired achievability. For non-affine theories, before we invoke Theorem
12
, we additionally need to ensure that
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})=F_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})^{-1}
in order to match the bound of Theorem
9
, which is why we require that
R_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})=R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})
.
∎

We can also give an alternative interpretation of the above bounds. For a fixed
\varepsilon>0
, they can be understood as establishing a limitation on the resourcefulness of any state
\ket{\phi}
that can be distilled from
\rho
up to the given error.

Corollary 14
.

If there exists a transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\tau
such that
\tau
is a state satisfying
F(\tau,\phi)\geq 1-\varepsilon
for some resourceful pure state
\phi
, then

\displaystyle F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)^{-1}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,+1.

(94)

Conversely, let
{\phi_{\star}}
be as in Corollary
13
. Then, for any
\varepsilon
such that

\displaystyle F_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})^{-1}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,+1,

(95)

there exists a resource–non-generating protocol
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\tau
where
\tau
is a state satisfying
F(\tau,{\phi_{\star}})\geq 1-\varepsilon
.

To understand this interpretation of the bound, it is helpful to consider the explicit example of the resource theory of entanglement. As mentioned previously, the maximally resourceful states
\ket{\Phi_{m}}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\ket{ii}
satisfy
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{m})=F_{\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{m})^{-1}=m
, which means that the two directions of the above bound match, and we obtain that

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\left.m\in\mathbb{N}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\tau,\;F(\tau,\Phi_{m})\geq 1-\varepsilon\right\}=\left\lfloor\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\,+1\right\rfloor,

(96)

with the
\left\lfloor\cdot\right\rfloor
needed because of the discrete character of the states
\{\Phi_{m}\}_{m}
. The above result, stated first in
Regula 2022
, can be understood as the value of entanglement that can be distilled probabilistically up to error
\varepsilon
— as we see, the projective robustness
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
of the input state determines this value exactly.

The fidelity of an isotropic state cannot be improved

The construction in the proof of Theorem
12
allows us to show the following no-go result.

Corollary 15
.

Let
\phi
be a pure state such that
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)=F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)^{-1}\eqqcolon\lambda
, for example any maximally resourceful state. Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and define
\rho_{\varepsilon}
as the state

\displaystyle\rho_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq(1-\varepsilon)\phi+\varepsilon\frac{\lambda\sigma-\phi}{\lambda-1},

(97)

where
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
is a state such that
\phi\leq\lambda\sigma
.

Then, there does not exist any free transformation protocol — probabilistic or deterministic — such that
\rho_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\tau
, where
\tau
is any state satisfying
F(\tau,\phi)\geq 1-\varepsilon^{\prime}>1-\varepsilon
. In other words, the fidelity of
\rho_{\varepsilon}
with the state
\phi
cannot be increased by any free operation.

Proof.

Following the proof of Theorem
12
, we see that

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}(\lambda-1).

(98)

Now, if there existed a transformation
\rho_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\tau
, then by Theorem
9
we would have

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})(1-F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi))}{\varepsilon^{\prime}\,F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)}

(99)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\lambda-1)

\displaystyle>\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}(\lambda-1),

which is a contradiction.
∎

The class of states
\rho_{\varepsilon}
constructed in (
97
) includes in particular the isotropic states
Horodecki et al. 1999a
encountered in the theories of entanglement and coherence, and indeed also more general ‘isotropic-like’ constructions that can be found in other resource theories
Takagi et al. 2022
. Corollary
15
gives an explicit proof of a fact that we have already seen in Figure
2
— namely, that the fidelity of entanglement distillation of an isotropic state cannot be improved — and provides an extension of the results of Refs.
Linden et al. 1998
;
Kent 1998
that showed an equivalent property for entanglement manipulation under LOCC.

States
\rho_{\varepsilon}
satisfying the conditions of the Corollary exist in any convex resource theory, since we can always take a maximally resourceful golden state
{\phi_{\star}}
of any dimension.
But note that golden states are not the only ones for which it may hold that
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)=F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)^{-1}
. For instance, in the resource theory of magic, the so-called Clifford magic states
Bravyi et al. 2019
also satisfy this property; this includes many relevant magic states of interest such as the
T
state.

We remark that the assumptions on
\phi
imply that
\phi
and
\frac{\lambda\sigma-\phi}{\lambda-1}
are necessarily orthogonal to each other.

V
Bounding probability and error trade-offs

We have seen that the projective robustness can be used to establish general limitations on probabilistic resource transformations. However, since
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
is monotonic under all probabilistic protocols, the restrictions obtained in this way can be considered too strong in some applications: one might instead be interested in understanding the least error achievable with a high probability of success, or in an exact quantification of the best achievable probability in a given transformation.
To address this, we consider a complementary approach for the characterisation of probabilistic transformations, with the explicit aim of tightly bounding the achievable performance in transforming two quantum states probabilistically under some constraints on the achievable probability or error. Although seemingly different, we will see that the approach is in fact closely related to the methods we used to study the projective robustness.

V.1
General bounds on achievable probability

We first aim to bound the maximal probability of success in the transformation between two states,

\displaystyle P(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime})\coloneqq\max\left\{\left.p\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\mathcal{E}(\rho)=p\rho^{\prime},\;\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}\right\}.

(100)

As before, any protocol
\mathcal{E}
here can be thought of as being a part of a larger instrument
\{\mathcal{E}_{i}\}
, with each
\mathcal{E}_{i}
a free operation.

Our main idea will be to construct measure-and-prepare maps that can perform a given transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime}
, taking suitable care to exactly quantify the probability with which the transformation is realised. Specifically, we will employ a class of transformations that we already encountered in the proof of Theorems
5
and
7
, namely, maps of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)=\left\langle W,X\right\rangle\phi+\left\langle Q,X\right\rangle\frac{\lambda\,\sigma-\phi}{\lambda-1}

(101)

for some operators
W
and
Q
.
Using such operations, we will be able to provide tight bounds on the achievable probability and fidelity of probabilistic transformations. Corresponding upper bounds will then be used to verify the tightness of the achievable bounds for relevant cases.

The methods studied here will be particularly suited to target states
\rho^{\prime}
that satisfy
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})=\infty
, such as resource distillation or conversion into states of low rank. Whether the approach can be extended also to other types of transformations, such as conversion between general states of full rank, remains an open question.

To begin, we need to define another resource measure, the overlap

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\coloneqq\max_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\,\left\langle\Pi_{\rho},\sigma\right\rangle

(102)

with
\Pi_{\rho}
denoting the projection onto
\supp\rho
. This quantity is a non-logarithmic variant of the min-relative entropy
Datta 2009
, and for pure states it reduces to the quantity
F_{\mathbb{F}}
that we encountered before, that is
V_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)=F_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)
. The second measure that we will use is the standard robustness
R_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}R_{\max}^{\mathbb{F}}(\rho\|\sigma)
that we encountered before.

As a motivating example, define the map

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle\mu\Pi_{\rho},X\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}+\left\langle\mathbbm{1}-\Pi_{\rho},X\right\rangle\frac{R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\,\sigma-\rho^{\prime}}{R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})-1},

(103)

where
\sigma
is a free state such that
\rho^{\prime}\leq_{\mathbb{F}}R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\,\sigma
, and
\mu
is some suitable parameter. Notice that this is a valid probabilistic quantum operation for any
\mu\in[0,1]
. In particular, choosing
\mu=\frac{1}{R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})-1}\frac{1-V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}{V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}
(or
\mu=1
, whichever is smaller) gives for any
\pi\in\mathbb{F}
with
\left\langle\Pi_{\rho},\pi\right\rangle\neq 0
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\pi)

\displaystyle\propto\,\rho^{\prime}+\frac{1}{\mu}\,\frac{1-\left\langle\Pi_{\rho},\pi\right\rangle}{\left\langle\Pi_{\rho},\pi\right\rangle}\,\frac{R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\sigma-\rho^{\prime}}{R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})-1}

(104)

\displaystyle\geq_{\mathbb{F}}\rho^{\prime}+\frac{1}{\mu}\,\frac{1-V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}{V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}\,\frac{R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\sigma-\rho^{\prime}}{R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})-1}

\displaystyle\geq_{\mathbb{F}}\rho^{\prime}+R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\sigma-\rho^{\prime}

\displaystyle\propto\,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}

where we used that
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\sigma-\rho^{\prime}\geq_{\mathbb{F}}0
by definition of
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}
, and we note that the third line is actually an equality when
\mu\neq 1
. In the case that
\left\langle\Pi_{\rho},\pi\right\rangle=0
, we also get that
\mathcal{E}(\pi)\geq_{\mathbb{F}}0
from the aforementioned fact that
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\sigma-\rho^{\prime}\geq_{\mathbb{F}}0
. This means that
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
, implying that

\displaystyle P(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime})\geq\min\left\{1,\,\frac{1}{R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})-1}\frac{1-V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}{V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}\right\}.

(105)

Note that the quantifier
V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
satisfies
V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=1
if and only if there exists a free state
\sigma
such that
\supp\sigma\subseteq\supp\rho
; this is equivalent to
W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)>0
.
This provides an alternative way to prove a result that we have already seen in Lemma
8
: for any state
\rho
s.t.
W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=0
and any target
\rho^{\prime}\notin\mathbb{F}
such that
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})<\infty
, it holds that
P(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime})>0
, and so any such state
\rho^{\prime}
is reachable with some probability. The bound of Eq. (
105
) was recently shown in other works for the case of pure-state inputs
\rho=\ket{\psi}\!\bra{\psi}
(which always have vanishing
W_{\mathbb{F}}
), first in the resource theory of entanglement
Contreras-Tejada et al. 2019
and later extended to multilevel quantum coherence
Zhang et al. 2021
.

To approach this problem more generally, let us consider the optimisation program

\displaystyle\Xi(\rho,\;t)\coloneqq\max\Big\{\left\langle\rho,W\right\rangle

\displaystyle\;\Big|\;0\leq W\leq Z\leq\mathbbm{1},

\displaystyle\left\langle W,\rho\right\rangle=\left\langle Z,\rho\right\rangle,

(106)

\displaystyle\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{t}\left\langle Z,\sigma\right\rangle\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\Big\},

where
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\}
is some chosen parameter. The problem is clearly feasible for any
t
(take
W=Z=0
) and the feasible sets are both compact, justifying the use of
\max
instead of
\sup
. Once again, in many theories of interest this quantity can be computed as an SDP. A quantity of this form previously appeared in
Rozpędek et al. 2018
for the resource theory of entanglement and in
Fang et al. 2018
for the case of quantum coherence, and a related optimisation problem was used in
Buscemi and Gour 2017
in the characterisation of transformations of pairs of quantum states. We then have the following.

Theorem 16
.

In any convex resource theory, the probability of transforming
\rho
into another state
\rho^{\prime}
with resource–non-generating operations is bounded as

\displaystyle\Xi\!\left(\rho,\;V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})^{-1}\right)\geq P(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime})\geq\Xi\!\left(\rho,\;R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\right).

(107)

The optimisation problem
\Xi(\rho,t)
can therefore be used to provide both lower and upper bounds on achievable probabilities. We will give a proof of this result shortly.

Note that the lower bound in Theorem
16
above relies on the standard robustness
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}
. An issue which arises here is that this quantity is not guaranteed to be finite in all resource theories, and in particular it is
never
finite for resourceful states in affine theories, trivialising Theorem
16
. This suggests that another approach is needed for such resources. Indeed, we consider a modified optimisation problem,

\displaystyle\Xi^{\aff}(\rho,\;t)\coloneqq\max\Big\{\left\langle\rho,W\right\rangle

\displaystyle\;\Big|\;0\leq W\leq Z\leq\mathbbm{1},

\displaystyle\left\langle W,\rho\right\rangle=\left\langle Z,\rho\right\rangle,

(108)

\displaystyle\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle=\frac{1}{t}\left\langle Z,\sigma\right\rangle\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\Big\}.

Note that the only difference between
\Xi
and
\Xi^{\aff}
is the equality in the last line of (
V.1
); similarly to the case of
\Omega_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}
encountered in the proof of Theorem
5
,
\Xi^{\aff}(\rho,t)
can be understood as
\Xi(\rho,t)
where the optimisation over the set
\mathbb{F}
is replaced with the affine hull
\aff(\mathbb{F})
. Using this modification, we will be able to obtain a bound that uses the robustness
R_{\mathbb{F}}
instead of the standard robustness
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}
, making it applicable to resources where
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}
diverges.

Theorem 17
.

In any convex resource theory, the probability of transforming
\rho
into another state
\rho^{\prime}
with resource–non-generating operations is bounded as

\displaystyle\Xi^{\aff}\!\left(\rho,\;V_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho^{\prime})^{-1}\right)\geq P(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\rho^{\prime})\geq\Xi^{\aff}\!\left(\rho,\;R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\right)\!.

(109)

Here, we defined

\displaystyle V_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho^{\prime})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sup_{X\in\aff(\mathbb{F})}\left\langle\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}},X\right\rangle

(110)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}c&\text{ if }\left\langle\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}},\sigma\right\rangle=c\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

where the second line can be seen as in the proof of Lemma
6
.
The bounds of this Theorem are useful particularly for affine resource theories, as they will typically trivialise when the theory is not affine.

We will find it convenient to split the proofs of Theorems
16
and
17
into two Propositions that establish upper and lower bounds.

Proposition 18
.

For any states
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
, it holds that

\displaystyle P(\rho\to\rho^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\Xi\left(\rho,\;V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})^{-1}\right),

(111)

\displaystyle P(\rho\to\rho^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\Xi^{\aff}\left(\rho,\;V_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho^{\prime})^{-1}\right).

Proof.

Assume that there exists a probabilistic free operation such that
\mathcal{E}(\rho)=p\rho^{\prime}
for some
p\in(0,1]
. Then

\displaystyle p=\left\langle\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}},\mathcal{E}(\rho)\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}}),\rho\right\rangle,

(112)

where
\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}
is the adjoint map of
\mathcal{E}
. Using that
\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\mathbbm{1})\leq\mathbbm{1}
since
\mathcal{E}
is positive and trace–non-increasing, we have
\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}})\leq\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\mathbbm{1})\leq\mathbbm{1}
. Additionally, we can notice that
\left\langle\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\mathbbm{1}-\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}}),\rho\right\rangle=0
. Now, for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}}),\sigma\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}},\mathcal{E}(\sigma)\right\rangle

(113)

\displaystyle=\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)\left\langle\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}},\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime}).

Together with
\left\langle\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\mathbbm{1}),\sigma\right\rangle=\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)
, this gives

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}}),\sigma\right\rangle\leq V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\left\langle\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\mathbbm{1}),\sigma\right\rangle,

(114)

which means that the operators
\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}})
and
\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\mathbbm{1})
are feasible solutions to the optimisation problem
\displaystyle\Xi\left(\rho,\;V_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})^{-1}\right)
with optimal value
p
, which is the stated result.

An analogous argument holds for
\Xi^{\aff}
. If
V_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho^{\prime})<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\Pi_{\rho^{\prime}}),\sigma\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)V_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho^{\prime}),

(115)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}(\mathbbm{1}),\sigma\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)

for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
due to the definition of
V_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}
, so we can use these operators as feasible solutions for
\Xi^{\aff}
. In the case that
V_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho^{\prime})
diverges, we can understand this result as the trivial bound
P(\rho\to\rho^{\prime})\leq 1
.
∎

Proposition 19
.

For any states
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
, it holds that

\displaystyle P(\rho\to\rho^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\Xi\left(\rho,\;R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\right),

(116)

\displaystyle P(\rho\to\rho^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\Xi^{\aff}\left(\rho,\;R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\right).

Proof.

Consider the case of
\Xi
first, and let
W,Z
be optimal operators in the definition of
\Xi\left(\rho,\;R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\right)
. If
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})=\infty
, then
\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle=0\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
and the map
\mathcal{E}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle W,X\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}
satisfies
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
trivially, so we are done. Otherwise, define the completely positive and trace–non-increasing map

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle W,X\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}+\left\langle Z-W,X\right\rangle\frac{\lambda\sigma-\rho^{\prime}}{\lambda-1}

(117)

where
\sigma
is an optimal state such that
\rho^{\prime}\leq_{\mathbb{F}}\lambda\sigma
with
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})=\lambda
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\left\langle W,\rho\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}

(118)

\displaystyle=\Xi\!\left(\rho,\;R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\right)\rho^{\prime}

since
\left\langle Z-W,\rho\right\rangle=0
, and for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}+\left\langle Z-W,\sigma\right\rangle\frac{\lambda\sigma-\rho^{\prime}}{\lambda-1}

(119)

\displaystyle\geq_{\mathbb{F}}\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}+(\lambda-1)\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle\frac{\lambda\sigma-\rho^{\prime}}{\lambda-1}

\displaystyle=\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle\lambda\sigma\in\cone(\mathbb{F}),

where we used that
\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\langle Z,\sigma\right\rangle\geq\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle
by definition of
\Xi
. This shows that
\mathcal{E}
is a free probabilistic operation achieving the desired transformation.

An analogous argument holds for
\Xi^{\aff}
, where we now construct

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle W,X\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}+\left\langle Z-W,X\right\rangle\frac{\lambda\sigma-\rho^{\prime}}{\lambda-1}

(120)

with
\rho^{\prime}\leq\lambda\sigma
, or
\mathcal{E}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle W,X\right\rangle\rho^{\prime}
if
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})=\infty
.
∎

V.2
Trade-offs in probabilistic resource distillation

The fact that both our upper and lower bounds for transformation probabilities are obtained in terms of the same optimisation problem,
\Xi(\rho,t)
or
\Xi^{\aff}(\rho,t)
, raises the question: when can the bounds be tight, characterising the achievable performance exactly? Similarly to how we established the tightness result in Section
IV
, we will show that such an equality can be obtained in the problem of resource distillation.

We can first consider the purifying transformation
\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\phi
to be achieved exactly, and ask about the best possible probability of success.
Recall that any convex resource theory admits a meaningful notion of a maximally resourceful state: a golden state
{\phi_{\star}}
, which maximises the robustness
R_{\mathbb{F}}
among all states, also minimises the overlap
V_{\mathbb{F}}
and satisfies
R_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})=V_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})^{-1}

Regula et al. 2020
.
We immediately see that, for such states, the upper and lower bounds of Theorems
16
and
17
can become equal, establishing an exact expression for the probability
P(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}{\phi_{\star}})
. Specifically, this is the case when either
R_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})=R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})
or
V_{\mathbb{F}}({\phi_{\star}})=V_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}({\phi_{\star}})
, which are conditions obeyed in theories such as quantum entanglement, coherence, or athermality.

We can extend this result to approximate distillation, which we encountered before in Section
IV
. Here, the output of the protocol is a state
\tau
that is not necessarily pure itself, but is close to the pure distillation target
\phi
. We thus define

\displaystyle P(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\phi,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max\big\{p\;\big|\;\mathcal{E}(\rho)=p\tau,\;\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O},\;F(\tau,\phi)\geq 1-\varepsilon\big\}.

(121)

Alternatively, turning the problem around, we can ask: for a given probability
p
, what is the best achievable distillation error
\varepsilon
? Let us denote this quantity as

\displaystyle E(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\phi,\,p)\coloneqq\min\big\{\varepsilon\;\big|

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho)=p\tau,\;\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O},\;F(\tau,\phi)\geq 1-\varepsilon\big\}.

(122)

In order to characterise this setting, we will need to consider a modification of the quantities
\Xi
and
\Xi^{\aff}
. First, for a fixed
\varepsilon
, we define

\displaystyle\Xi_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\;t)\coloneqq\max\Big\{\left\langle Z,\rho\right\rangle\;\Big|

\displaystyle 0\leq W\leq Z\leq\mathbbm{1},

(123)

\displaystyle\left\langle W,\rho\right\rangle=(1-\varepsilon)\left\langle Z,\rho\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{t}\left\langle Z,\sigma\right\rangle\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\big\},

\displaystyle\Xi_{\varepsilon}^{\aff}(\rho,\;t)\coloneqq\max\Big\{\left\langle Z,\rho\right\rangle\;\Big|

\displaystyle 0\leq W\leq Z\leq\mathbbm{1},

\displaystyle\left\langle W,\rho\right\rangle=(1-\varepsilon)\left\langle Z,\rho\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle=\frac{1}{t}\left\langle Z,\sigma\right\rangle\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\Big\}.

As expected, for
\varepsilon=0
we recover
\Xi(\rho,k)
and
\Xi^{\aff}(\rho,k)
. Optimisation problems of this form have been previously used to characterise probabilistic distillation of entanglement
Rozpędek et al. 2018
and quantum coherence
Fang et al. 2018
. The other family of programs that we will consider is defined, for a fixed
p
, as

\displaystyle\Theta_{p}(\rho,t)\coloneqq\max\Bigg\{\frac{\left\langle W,\rho\right\rangle}{p}\;\Bigg|

\displaystyle 0\leq W\leq Z\leq\mathbbm{1},

(124)

\displaystyle\left\langle Z,\rho\right\rangle=p,

\displaystyle\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{t}\left\langle Z,\sigma\right\rangle\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\Bigg\},

\displaystyle\Theta_{p}^{\aff}(\rho,t)\coloneqq\max\Bigg\{\frac{\left\langle W,\rho\right\rangle}{p}\;\Bigg|

\displaystyle 0\leq W\leq Z\leq\mathbbm{1},

\displaystyle\left\langle Z,\rho\right\rangle=p,

\displaystyle\left\langle W,\sigma\right\rangle=\frac{1}{t}\left\langle Z,\sigma\right\rangle\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\Bigg\}.

Both
\Xi_{\varepsilon}
and
\Theta_{p}
are, once again, convex optimisation problems, and they reduce to semidefinite programs in resource theories of coherence, non-positive partial transpose, athermality, and similar.

Of note is the fact that, were we to constrain the considered operations to be deterministic (trace-preserving), this would lead to fixing
Z=\mathbbm{1}
in our constructions, in which case (
124
) would reduce to the quantity
G(\rho,t)
previously defined in
Brandão and Plenio 2010
;
Regula et al. 2020
and closely related to the hypothesis testing relative entropy
D^{\varepsilon}_{H}

Buscemi and Datta 2010
;
Wang and Renner 2012
.

Our final result then uses these quantities to provide tight bounds on the probability and error of approximate probabilistic distillation in general resource theories.

Theorem 20
.

For any input state
\rho
, any resourceful pure state
\phi
, and any error
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
in any convex resource theory, the maximal probability achievable with resource–non-generating operations is bounded as

\displaystyle\Xi_{\varepsilon}\!\left(\rho,\;V_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq P(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\phi,\varepsilon)\geq\Xi_{\varepsilon}\!\left(\rho,\,R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)\right),

(125)

\displaystyle\Xi_{\varepsilon}^{\aff}\!\left(\rho,\;V_{\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\phi)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq P(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-4.78334pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\phi,\varepsilon)\geq\Xi_{\varepsilon}^{\aff}\!\left(\rho,\;R_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)\right)\!.

(126)

On the other hand, the smallest achievable error for a given probability
p\in(0,1]
is bounded as

\displaystyle\Theta_{p}\!\left(\rho,\;V_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)^{-1}\right)

An interesting fact about the result of Theorem

can be optimally distilled using measure-and-prepare operations as in (

We note, however, a difference between Theorem

also appear — in Theorem

Let us conclude by clarifying the connection between the optimisation problems considered in this section and the projective robustness
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
.
Taking the map
\mathcal{E}(X)\coloneqq\left\langle W,X\right\rangle\phi+\left\langle Q,X\right\rangle\frac{\lambda\sigma-\phi}{\lambda-1}
as before, recall that the main idea of the proof of Theorem

for a fixed

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle W,\rho\right\rangle}{\left\langle Q,\rho\right\rangle}=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}

(137)

under the same constraints. We can then get rid of the constraint

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\left.\frac{\left\langle W^{\prime},\rho\right\rangle}{\left\langle Q,\rho\right\rangle}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;W^{\prime},Q\geq 0,\;\left\langle W^{\prime},\sigma\right\rangle\leq\left\langle Q,\sigma\right\rangle\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\},

which is precisely

can then be understood to be analogous to the upper and lower bounds in Theorems

Note, however, that the simpler form of the projective robustness

and its properties make it useful as a bound even in settings where the approach of this section is difficult to apply: for instance, bounding the probability of many-copy transformations

VI
Conclusions

We introduced two general approaches to the characterisation of probabilistic resource transformations.

We first studied the projective robustness
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}

Regula 2022
— a monotone under all probabilistic transformations of a given quantum resource. We proved that it yields broadly applicable no-go theorems for the manipulation of quantum states in general resource theories. In many relevant contexts, we established that the constraints obtained through
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
are in fact necessary and sufficient, endowing the projective robustness with a fundamental operational meaning as a single quantity that fully determines the probabilistic convertibility between states with resource–non-generating operations. In resource distillation tasks, we showed that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
gives tight thresholds on the performance of all physical distillation schemes, expressed as universal bounds on the achievable accuracy and overhead in purifying quantum resources under the most general probabilistic manipulation protocols.

In our second approach, we developed precise quantitative restrictions on the achievable performance of one-shot probabilistic transformations. The bounds are all expressed in terms of convex conic optimisation problems, making them efficiently computable in many relevant cases. We showed that our restrictions become tight in broad types of resources when applied to the problem of distilling a maximally resourceful state, enabling us to provide an exact expression for the efficacy of distillation schemes in theories such as entanglement and coherence.

The importance of probabilistic manipulation protocols, and in particular the purification of noisy quantum resources, means that our results are bound to find use in many fundamental problems that underlie the practical exploitation of quantum mechanical phenomena. Here and in
Regula 2022
, we presented explicit applications of our methods in the distillation of quantum coherence, entanglement, and magic states; we stress, however, that the bounds are fully general, and can be applied in settings beyond the ones considered here. An understanding of the properties of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}
in any specific theory of interest will then provide additional insight into the manipulation of that resource.

A point of note is that many of our methods straightforwardly extend to settings beyond quantum theory.
An examination of the proofs of the necessary (Theorem
4
) and sufficient (Theorems
5
and
7
) conditions for resource convertibility reveals that we did not actually use any properties specific to quantum mechanics. Indeed, the results can be adapted exactly as stated to the broad setting of general probabilistic theories (GPTs)
Ludwig 1985
;
Hartkämper and Neumann 1974
;
Davies and Lewis 1970
;
Lami 2017
. The study of resource theories can indeed be formulated in such contexts
del Rio et al. 2015
;
Coecke et al. 2016
;
Takagi and Regula 2019
, and we invite the interested reader to
Takagi and Regula 2019
;
Lami et al. 2021
for a discussion of how to define quantities such as
R_{\mathbb{F}}
and
R^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathbb{F}}
in GPTs. The principal idea here is that the inequalities with respect to the cone of positive semidefinite operators are replaced with inequalities with respect to a cone
\mathcal{K}
that defines the state space of the given GPT; for instance, the projective robustness can be defined as

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathcal{K}}}^{\mathcal{K}}(\omega)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\left.\frac{\lambda}{\nu}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\omega-\nu\sigma\in\mathcal{K},\;\lambda\sigma-\omega\in\mathcal{K},\;\sigma\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathcal{K}}\right\},

(139)

where
\mathbb{F}_{\mathcal{K}}
is now a subset of states in the given GPT.
For simplicity, our results have focused on applications to
quantum
resources, and we did not consider GPTs in more detail here. We note also that the results concerning distillation, namely Sections
IV
and parts of Section
V
, appear to be much more difficult to generalise to GPTs, as they make heavy use of the properties of pure states in quantum theory. We expect the possibility of a complete extension of all our bounds beyond quantum theory to be highly dependent on the specifics of the given GPT.

Returning to quantum resource theories, an intriguing open question is the possibility to establish tighter bounds by exploiting known structures of the allowed set of free operations
\mathbb{O}
, since we know that the behaviour of probabilistic resource transformations can vary significantly between different sets of operations (as evidenced, for instance, by LOCC in entanglement theory). In particular, although our bounds are very suitable for the characterisation of high-rank noisy states, one could hope that another approach could yield better results when pure input states are considered — the price we pay for the generality of our results is that constraints such as those based on the Schmidt rank
Lo and Popescu 2001
;
Terhal and Horodecki 2000
are not recovered in our work.
Another follow-up question is how the phenomenon of catalysis
Jonathan and Plenio 1999
can improve achievable probabilities — it is known that catalysts often enhance the operational capabilities in resource manipulation, and it would be interesting to make this advantage precise in general probabilistic settings. Furthermore, following methods considered in
Regula and Takagi 2021
, one could extend our approach to establish analogous limitations on the probabilistic manipulation of quantum channels, leading to a generalisation of the framework considered here.

Projective robustness in channel discrimination

Let us consider a setting in which an ensemble of quantum channels is given as
\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\}
, and one of the channels from the ensemble is randomly applied to a chosen input state
\rho
with corresponding probability
p_{i}
. By measuring the output of this process with a chosen POVM
\{A_{i}\}
, we can attempt to distinguish which of the channels was applied to
\rho

\displaystyle p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\rho,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{A_{i}\})=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Tr\!\left[\mathcal{N}_{i}(\rho)A_{i}\right].

(140)

We then distinguish two scenarios. One is standard channel discrimination, in which our aim is to choose a measurement
\{A_{i}\}
which maximises the probability of successfully distinguishing which channel was applied. The other scenario is channel exclusion (anti-distinguishability)
Bandyopadhyay et al. 2014
, where our aim is instead to exclude a channel, i.e. determine to the best of our ability which of the channels was
not
applied; we then understand Eq. (
140
) as the average probability of guessing incorrectly, and we aim to minimise this quantity. In channel discrimination, the robustness
R_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
is known to quantify the advantage that a given input state
\rho
can provide over all free states
\sigma

Takagi et al. 2019
, while in channel exclusion, it is the weight
W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
which quantifies the advantage
Uola et al. 2020
;
Ducuara and Skrzypczyk 2020
.
Combining the two, we can show that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
quantifies the advantage when channel discrimination and channel exclusion tasks are considered
at the same time
.

.

Specifically,

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\}\\
\{A_{i}\},\{B_{i}\}\end{subarray}}\,\cfrac{\cfrac{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\rho,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{A_{i}\})}{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\rho,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{B_{i}\})}}{\max_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\cfrac{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\sigma,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{A_{i}\})}{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\sigma,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{B_{i}\})}}=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho),

(142)

where the maximisation is over all finite channel ensembles
\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and all POVMs
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
for which the expression in Eq. (
142
) is well defined.

Proof.

We begin by showing that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
always upper bounds the LHS of Eq. (
142
), so let us assume that it is finite and take
\rho\leq\lambda\sigma
,
\sigma\leq\mu\rho
such that
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\lambda\mu
. Let
\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\}
be any channel ensemble, and
\{A_{i}\},\{B_{i}\}
be any feasible POVMs. Then

\displaystyle\cfrac{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\rho,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{A_{i}\})}{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\rho,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{B_{i}\})}

\displaystyle\leq\cfrac{\lambda\,p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\sigma,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{A_{i}\})}{\frac{1}{\mu}\,p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\sigma,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{B_{i}\})}

(143)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\mu\,\max_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}}\cfrac{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\sigma^{\prime},\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{A_{i}\})}{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\sigma^{\prime},\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{B_{i}\})},

where the first inequality follows by the positivity of
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
and linearity of
p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}
in
\rho
. Since this holds for any feasible channel discrimination task, we get the desired inequality.

To show the other inequality, we will use a binary channel discrimination task based on a construction of Ref.
Takagi and Regula 2019
.
Let us then consider the channel ensemble
\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2}
where
\mathcal{N}_{1}
is the identity channel and
\mathcal{N}_{2}
is arbitrary, and the probabilities are chosen as
p_{1}=1,p_{2}=0
. Now take any operators
A,B
feasible for the dual formulation of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
in Eq. (
17
), and define the POVMs
\{A_{i}\},\{B_{i}\}
as
A_{1}=A/\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\,\infty}
,
A_{2}=\mathbbm{1}-A_{1}
and analogously for
B
. This gives

\displaystyle\cfrac{\cfrac{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\rho,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{A_{i}\})}{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\rho,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{B_{i}\})}}{\max_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\cfrac{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\sigma,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{A_{i}\})}{p_{{\mathrm{succ}}}(\sigma,\{p_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i}\},\{B_{i}\})}}

\displaystyle=\cfrac{\cfrac{\;\cfrac{\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle}{\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\,\infty}}\;}{\cfrac{\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle}{\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{\,\infty}}}}{\max_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\cfrac{\;\cfrac{\left\langle A,\sigma\right\rangle}{\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\,\infty}}\;}{\cfrac{\left\langle B,\sigma\right\rangle}{\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{\,\infty}}}}=\frac{\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle}{\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle}\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\frac{\left\langle B,\sigma\right\rangle}{\left\langle A,\sigma\right\rangle}\geq\frac{\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle}{\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle},

(144)

where the last inequality holds since any feasible
A,B
satisfy
\left\langle A,\sigma\right\rangle\leq\left\langle B,\sigma\right\rangle\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
. Here we have constrained ourselves to operators
A,B
such that
\left\langle A,\rho\right\rangle\neq 0\neq\left\langle B,\rho\right\rangle
and
\left\langle A,\sigma\right\rangle\neq 0\neq\left\langle B,\sigma\right\rangle\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
to ensure that Eq. (
142
) is well defined.
Since the supremum over all feasible
A,B
is precisely
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
, this establishes the projective robustness as a lower bound on the LHS of Eq. (
142
), concluding the proof.
∎

An algebraic model for rational 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2} -equivariant elliptic cohomology
Matteo Barucco

Abstract
. We construct a rational 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2} -equivariant elliptic cohomology theory for the 2 2 -torus 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2} , starting from an elliptic curve 𝒞 \mathcal{C} over ℂ \mathbb{C} and a coordinate data around the identity. The theory is defined by constructing an object E ​ 𝒞 𝕋 2 E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}} in the algebraic model category d ​ 𝒜 ​ ( 𝕋 2 ) d\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2}) , which by Greenlees and Shipley [ GS18 ] is Quillen-equivalent to rational 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2} -spectra. This result is a generalisation to the 2 2 -torus of the construction [ Gre05 ] for the circle 𝕋 \mathbb{T} . The object E ​ 𝒞 𝕋 2 E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}} is directly built using geometric inputs coming from the Cousin complex of the structure sheaf of 𝒞 × 𝒞 \mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C} .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

One of the first applications of the algebraic models for rational
\mathbb{T}
-spectra (
\mathbb{T}
is the circle group) introduced by Greenlees
[
Gre99
]
was the construction of an algebraic model for rational
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology
[
Gre05
]
. This result was part of a long quest started by Grojnowski
[
Gro07
]
for a satisfactory and comprehensive construction of equivariant elliptic cohomology. Equivariant elliptic cohomology is already a deep subject that shares an interesting bond with physics (see for example the Stolz-Teichner program
[
ST11
]
, or work of Berwick-Evans
[
Ber21
]
). The original motivation of Grojnowski was to construct certain elliptic algebras, but the subject found numerous applications to representation theory (for
G
connected compact Lie group), and for organizing moonshine phenomena (especially for
G
finite). We refer to the introduction of
[
BT18
]
and
[
GM20
]
for a more detailed account of the story of this subject. This quest peaked in recent years with the work of Lurie
[
Lur18
]
,
[
Lur18a
]
,
[
Lur19
]
, and Gepner and Meier
[
GM20
]
who defined an integral theory of equivariant elliptic cohomology for any compact Lie group, entirely in the setting of spectral algebraic geometry. A comparison theorem between the construction we present in this paper and the construction of Lurie, Gepner and Meier will be interesting to pursue and could potentially lead to explicit computations of the Lurie-Gepner-Meier theory in the rational case. This is beyond the scope of this paper, and even for the circle case unpublished work of Gepner and Greenlees shows it is a substantial task.

Essential invariants of
G
-spaces are
G
-equivariant cohomology theories: equivariant
K
-theory, equivariant cobordism, Bredon and Borel cohomology just to name a few examples. For
G
a compact Lie group, via stabilisation one can construct a category of
G
-spectra where every such cohomology theory
E_{G}^{*}(\_)
is represented by a
G
-spectrum
E
, in the sense that for any based
G
-space
X
we have
E_{G}^{*}(X)=[\Sigma^{\infty}X,E]^{G}_{*}
. The gain with the category of
G
-spectra is in the structure, in particular one can do homotopy theory in it. If we restrict our attention to rational
G
-equivariant cohomology theories to simplify the situation, they are represented by rational
G
-spectra: the localization of
G
-spectra at
H\mathbb{Q}
.

Algebraic models are a useful tool to study rational equivariant cohomology theories. The main idea is to define an abelian category
\mathcal{A}(G)
for
G
a compact Lie group and an homology functor

(1.1)

\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}:G\Spectra_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathcal{A}(G)

equipped with an Adams spectral sequence to compute maps in rational
G
-spectra, and therefore the values of the theory:

(1.2)

\Ext^{*,*}_{\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(X),\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(Y))\Longrightarrow[X,Y]^{G}_{*}.

For certain groups the homology functor
\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}
can be lifted to a Quillen-equivalence

(1.3)

G\Spectra_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq_{Q}d\mathcal{A}(G)

between rational
G
-spectra and the category
d\mathcal{A}(G)
of differential graded objects in
\mathcal{A}(G)
. When this happens algebraic models can also be used to build rational
G
-equivariant cohomology theories simply constructing objects in
d\mathcal{A}(G)
. This is exactly the method used in
[
Gre05
]
: building an object
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}
in
d\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T})
and using the Quillen-equivalence (
1.3
) for the circle
G=\mathbb{T}
to define a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology theory.

Greenlees and Shipley have extended the Quillen-equivalence (
1.3
) to tori of any rank
\mathbb{T}^{r}

[
GS18
, Theorem 1.1]
. It is therefore natural to try to generalize the construction of
[
Gre05
]
to higher dimensional tori. The first step of this project is to build an object
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\in d\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
representing
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology, which is precisely the intended purpose of this paper. The
\mathbb{T}^{2}
case is somehow separated from the general
\mathbb{T}^{r}
case. Namely the Adams spectral sequence (
1.2
) collapses at the second page for
\mathbb{T}^{2}
resulting in a neat and explicit description of the values of the theory on spheres of complex representations (
1.5
). Even if a similar description is expected to be true for higher dimensional tori, the Adams spectral sequence is not expected to collapse at the second page, and a substantial study of it may be necessary. Moreover the construction for
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is complicated enough to shed some light over compatibility constraints among connected subgroups of the same codimension, not visible in the circle case (like for example the use of completed coordinates needed for Lemma
5.33
). At the same time the situation is still simple enough to allow explicit visualization of the objects and to avoid use of combinatorics and inductive arguments necessary for higher tori, that would complicate the comprehension of the main ideas of the construction.

The point of this method is that the construction of an object in
d\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
so closely corresponds to the algebra of functions over the algebraic variety
\chi:=\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathcal{C}
is our fixed elliptic curve. The contact point is the Cousin complex (as introduced by Grothendieck
[
Har66
, Proposition 2.3]
) of the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\chi}
. From the algebraic geometry side this Cousin complex computes the cohomology of coherent sheaves over
\chi
, since it is a flabby resolution of
\mathcal{O}_{\chi}
. While in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
this Cousin complex clearly matches the terms of an injective resolution of our object
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
, and therefore computes the values
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\_)
via the Adams spectral sequence. As a consequence calculations of the theory
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\_)
are directly reduced to the cohomology of sheaves of the algebraic variety
\chi
. Fundamental examples are spheres of complex representations: given a complex
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-representation
V
with no fixed points we compute the values
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(S^{V})
over the one point compactification
S^{V}
, and check it corresponds to the cohomology of the coherent sheaf
\mathcal{O}(-D_{V})
, for a certain divisor
D_{V}
associated with
V
.

Two main subjects can benefit from our construction of
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
. From the algebraic models perspective, this is the first non-trivial explicit construction of an object in
d\mathcal{A}(G)
for higher dimensional tori, that is directly built in the algebraic model and does not come from a spectrum through the homology functor
\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}
. This is interesting since it is a first example of use of these algebraic models for higher tori as a building tool for new theories. Many steps of the construction can be replicated with different geometric inputs to potentially define new and interesting rational equivariant cohomology theories. From the elliptic cohomology perspective, on top of giving a definition in the rational
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-case closer to the actual geometry of
\mathcal{C}
, it can also enhance computations in the rational
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant case. For example proving a split condition between the construction of Greenlees
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}
and our construction of
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
will help towards this direction. The idea is to view a
\mathbb{T}
-space
X
as the quotient of a
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-space
Y
by the action of the subgroup
\mathbb{T}\times\{1\}
. If we can compute
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{*}(Y)
using the computation on spheres here presented, than using the splitting condition we will also know
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(X)
.

1.2.
The main result

The construction for the circle
[
Gre05
, Theorem 1.1]
works as follows. Greenlees starts from an elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
over a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra with a choice of coordinate
t_{e}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C},e}
vanishing to the first order at
e
, and with poles only at points of finite order of
\mathcal{C}
. From this he constructs uniformizers
t_{n}
for the points of exact order
n
:
\mathcal{C}\langle n\rangle
, and also changes the topology so that
\mathcal{C}\langle n\rangle
are the irreducible closed subsets. The ring of meromorphic functions in this new topology, together with the local rings at the various
\mathcal{C}\langle n\rangle
are then used as geometric input to build the object
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}\in d\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T})
, where the
t_{n}
are used to give the actions of the appropriate rings. After building
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}
, using the Adams spectral sequence he computes the values of the associated cohomology theory on spheres of complex representations
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(S^{V})
, showing it has all the right properties to be called rational
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology.

For higher dimensional tori we also have an algebraic model
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{r})
described in
[
Gre08
]
and
[
Gre12
]
, and the Quillen-equivalence (
1.3
)
[
GS18
, Theorem 1.1]
. Therefore we can use the same technique to construct a rational
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology theory: build an object
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\in d\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and with the Adams spectral sequence compute its values on spheres of complex representations.

To be more precise exactly as in
[
Gre05
]
we start from the data of an elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
over the complex numbers and a coordinate
t_{e}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C},e}
in the local ring at the identity of
\mathcal{C}
, vanishing to first order at
e
. From this we build our object
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
: this is the main theorem of this article. The construction of the object can be found in Section
5
while the computation on spheres is Theorem
6.1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For every elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{C}
and coordinate
t_{e}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C},e}
, there exists a formal differential graded object
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\in d\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
whose associated rational
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-equivariant cohomology theory
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{*}(\_)
is 2-periodic. The value on the one point compactification
S^{V}
for a complex
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-representation
V
with no fixed points is given in terms of the sheaf cohomology of a line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-D_{V})
over
\chi=\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
:

(1.5)

E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{n}(S^{V})\cong\begin{cases}H^{0}(\chi,\mathcal{O}(-D_{V}))\oplus H^{2}(\chi,\mathcal{O}(-D_{V}))&$n$ even\\
H^{1}(\chi,\mathcal{O}(-D_{V}))&$n$ odd.\end{cases}

The associated divisor
D_{V}
is defined as follows. First our elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
defines a functor
\mathfrak{X}
from compact abelian Lie groups to complex manifolds. If
H
is a compact abelian Lie group and
\mathcal{C}
our fixed elliptic curve, define

\mathfrak{X}(H):=\Hom_{\text{Ab}}(H^{*},\mathcal{C}).

Where we are considering group homomorphisms, and
H^{*}:=\Hom_{\text{Lie}}(H,\mathbb{T})
is the character group of
H
. Note this is an exact functor inducing an embedding
\mathfrak{X}(K)\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(H)
for every embedding
K\hookrightarrow H
.
The fundamental algebraic surface we are working on is
\chi:=\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\cong\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
. Note
\mathfrak{X}(H)
is a subvariety of
\chi
of the same dimension of
H
. For every non-trivial character
z
of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
consider the codimension
1
subgroup
\Ker(z)
, then
\mathfrak{X}(\Ker(z))
is a codimension
1
subvariety of
\chi
that is the associated divisor of
z
. If
V=\sum_{z}\alpha_{z}z
define

D_{V}:=\sum_{z}\alpha_{z}\mathfrak{X}(\Ker(z)),

where in both sums
z
runs over all the non-trivial characters of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, and
\alpha_{z}
is a non-negative integer.

To be more precise our construction of
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\in d\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
proceeds as follows. We will build an exact sequence of three injective objects in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
:

(1.6)

\mathbb{I}_{0}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{0}}\mathbb{I}_{1}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{1}}\mathbb{I}_{2}\to 0.

In
d\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
the map
\varphi_{0}
factors through the fibre of
\varphi_{1}
, and defines a map
\tilde{\varphi_{0}}
whose fibre we can define to be our object
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
. We will prove from this construction that the object
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
is formal: namely is quasi-isomorphic to its homology
H_{*}(E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
. Moreover the exact sequence we started with (
1.6
) is an injective resolution in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
of the homology
H_{*}(E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
. In this way in using the Adams Spectral sequence to compute the values of the theory, we will only need the sequence (
1.6
), so we can build it with the right geometric inputs to force the values on spheres of complex representations stated in (
1.5
).

The sequence (
1.6
) is built directly from geometric inputs coming from the elliptic curve. The bridge between the category
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and the variety
\chi
is the functor
\mathfrak{X}
on subgroups of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. On
\chi
we consider a different topology than the Zariski one that we call the torsion point topology
\chi^{\text{TP}}
: the generating closed subsets of this new topology are the irreducible components of the subvarieties
\mathfrak{X}(H)
where
H
runs over the closed subgroups of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. With this new topology we associate to every closed subgroup
H
a closed subset
\bar{\mathfrak{X}}(H)
. The irreducible parts of
\bar{\mathfrak{X}}(H)
have the same dimension of
H
, if
H\neq K
have the same dimension then
\bar{\mathfrak{X}}(H)
and
\bar{\mathfrak{X}}(K)
are disjoint, and every irreducible closed subset in
\chi^{\text{TP}}
is in one of the
\bar{\mathfrak{X}}(H)
. The TP-topology basically copies the poset structure of the closed subgroups of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
with a poset of closed subsets on the variety
\chi
, and forgets the rest. An essential aspect of this change of topology is that pushforward of Zariski sheaves have the same sheaf cohomology in the TP-topology.

Taking advantage of these two aspects of the TP-topology, we can consider the sheaf Cousin complex
CC^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\chi}^{\text{TP}})

[
Har66
, Proposition 2.3]
of the pushforward of the Zariski structure sheaf:
\mathcal{O}_{\chi}^{\text{TP}}
. This is a flabby resolution of
\mathcal{O}_{\chi}^{\text{TP}}
, whose
n
-th term decomposes as a direct sum over the irreducible closed subsets
C
of codimension
n
of constant sheaves with value a local cohomology module
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{C}(\mathcal{O}_{\chi}^{\text{TP}})
. In complete analogy the
n
-th term of the sequence (
1.6
) we want to build will encode information of the cohomology theory at the subgroups of codimension
n
. The correspondence
H\to\bar{\mathfrak{X}}(H)
will come in handy at this point.
Recall from
[
Gre08
]
that objects in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
are sheaves of modules over the space of closed subgroups of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. The object
\mathbb{I}_{n}
will be a direct sum over the closed subgroups
H
of codimension
n
of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
of constant sheaves with value the local cohomology
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\bar{\mathfrak{X}}(H)}(\mathcal{O}_{\chi}^{\text{TP}})
coming from the Cousin complex. The differentials
\varphi_{0}
, and
\varphi_{1}
will also come from the differentials in the Cousin complex.

To compute the value of the theory on a sphere of complex representation
S^{V}
, when
V
does not have fixed points, we use the Adams Spectral sequence (
1.2
)
[
Gre08
, Theorem 1.1]
. Using the injective resolution (
1.6
) computations are directly reduced to the Cousin complex
CC^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\chi}^{\text{TP}})
twisted by the coherent sheaf
\mathcal{O}(-D_{V})
: this turns out to be a flabby resolution of
\mathcal{O}(-D_{V})
giving its cohomology as a result.
From this construction it is clear that there is a deep connection between injective resolutions of objects in the algebraic category
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{r})
and the theory of Cousin complexes. The filtration by codimension of subgroups corresponds to the filtration by codimension of closed subsets in the Cousin complex, and also the decomposition in constant sheaves is common to both and corresponds term by term. This requires further investigation, and suggests that the same method can be used to build other interesting cohomology theories, possibly changing the geometric inputs.

2.
The correspondence subgroups-subvarieties

The goal of this section is to specify a correspondence between subgroups
H
of
G=\mathbb{T}^{2}
and certain varieties
\bar{\mathfrak{X}}(H)
. We have fixed an elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
over the complex numbers which defines a functor
\mathfrak{X}
from compact abelian Lie groups to complex manifolds.

Definition 2.1
.

If
H
is a compact abelian Lie group and
\mathcal{C}
our fixed elliptic curve, define

(2.2)

\mathfrak{X}(H):=\Hom_{\text{Ab}}(H^{*},\mathcal{C}).

Where we are considering group homomorphisms, and
H^{*}:=\Hom_{\text{Lie}}(H,\mathbb{T})
is the character group of
H
.

Let
\chi:=\mathfrak{X}(G)
be the complex abelian surface defined by the 2-torus. The functor
\mathfrak{X}
is exact and induces an embedding
\mathfrak{X}(H)\hookrightarrow\chi
for every subgroup
H
of
G
. Moreover
\mathfrak{X}(H)
has the same dimension as
H
and is a subgroup of
\chi
. We will only be interested in the functor (
2.2
) on subgroups of
G
, and therefore all the varieties
\mathfrak{X}(H)
will be subvarieties of
\chi
.

Definition 2.3
.

For every subgroup
H
of
G

Remark 3.2
.

A delicate remark is imperative here. The topological space

is not

: there are different points that are topologically undistinguishable (i.e. they are exactly in the same open subsets for the TP-topology). In view of the previous section and in particular Lemma

, we have a straightforward way to check when two points in

are topologically undistinguishable: they are topologically undistinguishable if and only if they belong exactly to the same subsets in the collection
\{{D_{ij}}\}_{ij}
. For example the generic point of
\chi^{\text{Zar}}
is topologically undistinguishable from any point that does not belong to any of the
{D_{ij}}
. This will not be an issue until we will need the theory of Cousin complexes as developed by Grothendieck. To apply all that machinery we will need a sober space (i.e. every closed irreducible subset has a unique generic point), and
\chi^{\text{TP}}
is not a sober space. There is an easy fix to this which is to consider the Kolmogorov quotient of
\chi^{\text{TP}}
:
\KQ(\chi^{\text{TP}})
, namely the quotient of
\chi^{\text{TP}}
by the equivalence relation of topologically undistinguishable points.
For the sake of clarity we will not change the underlying set of
\chi^{\text{Zar}}
and
\chi^{\text{TP}}
and we ask the reader to keep in mind that all the results we will prove for
\chi^{\text{TP}}
in this section apply as well if you substitute
\chi^{\text{TP}}
with its Kolmogorov quotient, since topologically undistinguishable points have exactly the same stalks and nothing changes from the point of view of sheaves. The same is true with the results in the rest of this paper with the only exceptions of the theory of Cousin complexes where we will stress the need of a sober space.

Notice that every subset in the generating collection
\{{D_{ij}}\}_{ij}
is a closed subset also in the Zariski topology. Therefore every TP-open is also a Zariski-open, and we have a well defined continuous map

3.1.

Since

Appendix A
Algebraic models

In this appendix we present a self-contained account of algebraic models for tori of any rank, therefore only in this appendix
G=\mathbb{T}^{r}
is an r-dimensional torus, with

. We also specify that all the modules over graded rings are graded and all the maps between graded modules are graded maps.

Algebraic models are a useful tool to study rational equivariant cohomology theories. The main idea is to define an abelian category
\mathcal{A}(G)
and an homology functor:

(A.1)

\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}:G\Spectra_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathcal{A}(G)

(A.2)

\Ext^{*,*}_{\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(X),\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(Y))\Longrightarrow[X,Y]^{G}_{*}.

In the case of tori the homology functor

can be lifted to a Quillen-equivalence
[
GS18

):

Theorem A.3

(Greenlees-Shipley)
.

The category
d\mathcal{A}(G)
of differential graded objects in
\mathcal{A}(G)
is Quillen-equivalent to rational
G
-spectra.

When this happens algebraic models can also be used to build rational
G
-equivariant cohomology theories simply constructing objects in

.

For any connected subgroup

\varphi^{H}f:\varphi^{H}X\to\varphi^{H}Y
for every connected subgroup
H
, compatible with the structure maps of
X
and
Y
(it makes the evident commutative diagrams between different levels commute
[
Gre08
, Definition 3.6]
).

Remark A.36
.

A morphism
f:X\to Y
in
\mathcal{A}(G)
is almost determined by what it does at the trivial subgroup level
f(1):X(1)\to Y(1)
(that we will call bottom level). This is because for any connected subgroup
H
the map
f
at the
H
th level
f(H):X(H)\to Y(H)
is then
\mathcal{E}_{H}^{-1}f(1)
. Therefore properties like injectivity, surjectivity or exactness for a sequence of morphisms can be checked at the bottom level.

A.5.
Injectives in
\mathcal{A}(G)

The injective objects in
\mathcal{A}(G)
that we will use are constant below a certain connected subgroup
H
, and zero elsewhere
[
Gre08
, Section 4.A.]
.

Definition A.37
.

An object
X\in\mathcal{A}(G)
is concentrated below a connected subgroup
H
if
X(K)=0
for every connected subgroup
K\nsubseteq H
. We denote
\mathcal{A}(G)_{H}
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{A}(G)
of objects concentrated below
H
.

Definition A.38
.

If
H
is a connected subgroup of
G
, and
T
is a graded torsion
\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}
-module, define
f_{H}(T)\in\mathcal{A}(G)
to be the constant sheaf below
H
with the following values:

(A.39)

f_{H}(T)(K):=\begin{cases}\mathcal{E}_{H}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\displaylimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}}}T&if $K\subseteq H$\\
0&if $K\nsubseteq H$\end{cases}

and structure maps either identities or zero.

Remark A.40
.

We require
T
to be torsion so that when we invert
\mathcal{E}_{K}^{-1}
for
K\nsubseteq H
we obtain zero. Therefore this requirement can be dropped when
H=G
.

Lemma A.41

(Lemma 4.1 of
[
Gre08
]
)
.

For any connected subgroup
H
of
G
there is an adjunction:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}(G)_{H}}

Tors-
\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}
-Mod
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{H}}
\dashv

where the left adjoint is the evaluation
\varphi^{H}
. Moreover for any torsion
\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}
-module
T
, and object
X\in\mathcal{A}(G)
we have:

(A.42)

\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}}(\varphi^{H}X,T)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{A}(G)}(X,f_{H}(T)).

This in order allows us to transfer torsion injectives
\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}
-modules into injective objects in
\mathcal{A}(G)
. First notice that if we are given for every
\tilde{H}\in\mathchoice{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}
an
H^{*}(BG/{\tilde{H}})
-torsion module
T(\tilde{H})
, then
\bigoplus_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}T(\tilde{H})
is naturally a torsion
\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}
-module, with the action given component by component.

A.6.
Spheres of complex representations

We can now define the fundamental homology functor
\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}

[
Gre08
, Definition 1.4]
. Given a rational
G
-spectrum
X
we can define the sheaf
\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(X)\in\mathcal{A}(G)
that on a connected subgroup
H
takes the value

\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(X)(H):=\mathcal{E}_{H}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\displaylimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}}}\pi_{*}^{G/H}(DE\mathcal{F}/{H}_{+}\wedge\Phi^{H}X).

Where
\Phi^{H}
is the geometric fixed point functor,
E\mathcal{F}_{+}
is the universal space for the family
\mathcal{F}
of finite subgroups with a disjoint basepoint added, and
DE\mathcal{F}_{+}=F(E\mathcal{F}_{+},S^{0})
is its functional dual (The function spectrum of maps from
E\mathcal{F}_{+}
to
S^{0}
).

Example A.45
.

For the sphere spectrum we obtain the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}

[
Gre08
, Theorem 1.5]
:

\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(S^{0})(H)=\mathcal{E}_{H}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\displaylimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}

Given a complex representation
V
of
G
with
V^{G}=0
we want to make explicit the object
\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(S^{V})

[
Gre12
, Section 2.B.]
. The value at each subgroup is computed with fixed points of the representation:

(A.46)

\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(S^{V})(H)=\mathcal{E}_{H}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\displaylimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}}}\Sigma^{V^{H}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}.

To describe the structure maps it is convenient to use the suspensions of the units:

\iota_{V^{H}}:=\Sigma^{V^{H}}(1)\in\Sigma^{V^{H}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}

so that for every inclusion of connected subgroups
K\subseteq H
the structure map
\beta_{K}^{H}
is determined by the suspended unit:

(A.47)

\beta_{K}^{H}(\iota_{V^{K}})=e(V^{K}-V^{H})^{-1}\otimes\iota_{V^{H}}

where the difference of the two representations simply means a splitting

V^{K}=V^{H}\oplus(V^{K}-V^{H}).

Remark A.48
.

The content of this section applies also in the case of a virtual complex representation
V=V_{0}-V_{1}
. The only thing to specify is the Euler class
e(V)=e(V_{0})/e(V_{1})
. As a result in (
A.46
) we have the desuspension
\Sigma^{-V_{1}^{H}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}
instead of the suspension.

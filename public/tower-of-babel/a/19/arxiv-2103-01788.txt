Generalized Rough Polyharmonic Splines for Multiscale PDEs with Rough Coefficients
Xinliang Liu, Lei Zhang \comma \corrauth and Shengxin Zhu \comma ∗

Abstract
In this paper, we demonstrate the construction of generalized Rough Polyhamronic Splines (GRPS) within the Bayesian framework, in particular, for multiscale PDEs with rough coefficients. The optimal coarse basis can be derived automatically by the randomization of the original PDEs with a proper prior distribution and the conditional expectation given partial information on edge or derivative measurements. We prove the (quasi)-optimal localization and approximation properties of the obtained bases, and justify the theoretical results with numerical experiments.

The derivative based GRPS can be seen as a higher order method.

Formulation

2.1
Bayesian homogenization framework

In the Bayesian homogenization framework
[
32
]
, the coarse space can be identified by a Bayesian inference problem (in particular, Gaussian process regression), through the randomization of the original deterministic problem (
1
),

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}v(x)=\zeta(x),\quad&\text{on }\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}v=0,\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2)

where
\zeta(x)
is a centered Gaussian process on
\Omega
with covariance
\Lambda(x,y)
. The solution
v(x)
is also a centered Gaussian process on
\Omega
with covariance

\Gamma(x,y):=\mathbb{E}[v(x)v(y)]=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}G(x,z)\Lambda(z,z^{\prime})G(y,z^{\prime})dzdz^{\prime},

(3)

where
G(x,y)
is the Green’s function such that
\mathcal{L}G(x,y)=\delta(x-y)
on
\Omega
,
\mathcal{B}G=0
on
\partial\Omega
.

Given an index set
\mathcal{I}
and a set of linearly independent
measurement functions

\Phi:=\{\phi_{i}(x)\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
, such that
\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\phi_{i}(x)\Gamma(x,y)\phi_{j}(x)\,{\rm d}x
are well-defined, we define the measurements
M:=(m_{1},\dots,m_{N})
, where
m_{i}:=\int_{\Omega}v(x)\phi_{i}(x)\,{\rm d}x
for
i\in\mathcal{I}
and
N=\#\mathcal{I}
. For example, when
\phi_{i}(x)=\delta(x-x_{i})
,
m_{i}
is the point value at
x_{i}
for any continuous
v
. Note that
M
is a centered Gaussian vector with covariance matrix
\Theta
, where

\Theta_{i,j}:=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\phi_{i}(x)\Gamma(x,y)\phi_{j}(y)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y.

The optimal approximation space can be identified through the conditional expectation of
v(x)
with respect to measurements
M
,

\mathbb{E}[v|M]=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}m_{i}\psi_{i}(x),

(4)

where
\psi_{i}
has the following explicit representation formula from the conditional expectation of Gaussian process,

\psi_{i}(x):=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\Theta_{i,j}^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(x,y)\phi_{j}(y)\,{\rm d}y.

(5)

\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
can be regarded as a set of posterior basis with respect to the noise
\zeta
and the measurement functions
\Phi
.
\Psi:=\mathrm{span}\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
can be used as a coarse space to approximate the solution of deterministic problem (
1
).
We note that the above formulation can be seen as a prototypical example for the emerging field of probabilistic numerics, and we refer interested readers to
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
12
]
for more details.

It is difficult to use (
5
) for numerical computation since it involves convolutions over the whole domain
\Omega
. We will give a variational formulation for
\psi_{i}
in the next Section
2.2
.
Before proceeding, we first discuss the choice of the noise
\zeta
and the measurement functions
\Phi
.

Choice of the noise
\zeta

For the centered Gaussian field
\zeta
, it reduces to the choice of the covariance function
\Lambda(x,y)
, which in turn determines the regularity of the solution space. There are two natural possibilities:

1.

(white noise) Taking
\zeta(x)
as the white noise, i.e.
\Lambda(x,y)=\delta(x,y)
. This is the choice for the RPS formulation in
[
40
,
32
]
.

2.

(
\mathcal{L}
noise) Taking the covariance operator of
\zeta
as
\mathcal{L}
, namely, for any
g
such that
\int g\mathcal{L}g\,{\rm d}x<\infty
,
\int g(x)\zeta(x)\,{\rm d}x
is a Gaussian random variable with mean 0 and variance
\int g\mathcal{L}.g\,{\rm d}x
. This is the choice for the Gamblet formulation in
[
33
]
.

Choice of
\Phi

The RPS formulation uses point value measurement functions
\phi_{i}(x)=\delta(x-x_{i})
, while the Gamblet formulation takes scaled volume characteristic functions as measurement functions, and volume averages as measurements. In this paper, we take edge averages or volume averaged first order derivatives as measurements to construct the approximation space
\Psi
. We postpone the specification of the measurements
\Phi
after we set up the discretization in Section
2.3
.

In the following, we refer to the basis with white noise as RPS basis, and the basis with
\mathcal{L}
noise as GRPS basis. For instance, we name the RPS basis with point measurement as RPS-P basis (or in short, RPS basis), GRPS basis with volume measurements as GRPS-V basis, and so on.

2.2
Variational formulation

We introduce the solution space to the original problem (
1
) as,

V:=\{v|\mathcal{L}v\in L^{2}(\Omega),\mathcal{B}v=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}.

(6)

We define the bilinear form
a:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
as

a(u,v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{L}u)(\mathcal{L}v)\,{\rm d}x,&\text{$\zeta$ is white noise },\\
\int_{\Omega}u\mathcal{L}v\,{\rm d}x,&\text{$\zeta$ is $\mathcal{L}$ noise},\end{array}\right.

(7)

and the norm
\|\cdot\|:=\big(a(\cdot,\cdot)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
[u,v]:=\int_{\Omega}uv\,{\rm d}x
is the scalar product.

Instead of using the Bayesian representation formula (
5
), we propose the following variational formulation to compute the basis. For any
i\in\mathcal{I}
,

\begin{cases}\psi_{i}=\argmin\limits_{v\in V}a(v,v)\\
s.t.\ [v,\phi_{j}]=\delta_{i,j},\ \forall j\in\mathcal{I}.\end{cases}

(8)

Then
\Psi:=\operatorname{span}\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
is the GRPS space.
The well-posedness of (
8
) and the variational property of the derived basis are shown in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
32
, Prop 4.2]

The constrained minimization problem (
8
) is strictly convex and admits a unique minimizer
\psi_{i}\in V
, which also satisfies the Bayesian formula (
5
). Furthermore,
\psi_{i}
fulfils the variational property in the sense that, for any
v
such that
[v,\phi_{j}]=0,\ \forall j\in\mathcal{I}
, we have
a(\psi_{i},v)=0
.

Remark 2.2
.

The constrained minimization problems (
8
) is equivalent to the following saddle point problem
[
32
]
, namely, finding
\psi_{i}\in V
and
\lambda\in\Phi
such that

\begin{cases}a(\psi_{i},v)+[\lambda,v]=0,\,\forall v\in V,\\
[\mu,\psi_{i}]=f_{i}(\mu),\,\forall\mu\in\Phi,\end{cases}

(9)

where
f_{i}
is a linear functional on
\Phi
such that
f_{i}(\phi_{j})=\delta_{i,j},\,\forall j\in\mathcal{I}
.

Numerical Method

In this section, we present the discretization of (
1
) using finite element methods. We focus on the second order elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}=-\operatorname{div}(\kappa(x)\nabla\cdot)
with a rough coefficient
\kappa(x)

a(u,v)=[g,v],\ \forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

where

J

Numerical homogenization

By

a(u_{H},v_{H})=[g,v_{H}],\ \forall v_{H}\in\Psi.

In practice, the equation (

u_{H}^{\ell}\in\Psi^{\ell}
the solution to

a(u_{H}^{\ell},v_{H})=[g,v_{H}],\ \forall v_{H}\in\Psi^{\ell}.

For the analysis, we assume that the coarse bases in

Let

First, we introduce a partition of unity. We use

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we generalize the RPS and Gamblet bases for numerical homogenization within the Bayesian framework. We propose to use the edge and first order derivative measurements to construct new generalized rough polyharmonic splines (GRPS) basis. Such a generalization requires some new techniques to prove the localization and convergence properties. Theoretical results on these new GRPS bases are developed and numerical justifications are provided. It seems that those bases are efficient for certain multiscale PDEs.

In this paper, we only consider the case of the second order elliptic operator, it is important to note that the framework works for general integro-differential operators
[
34
]
. For example, we can apply the method to heterogeneous elastic-plastic dynamics
[
45
]
, and furthermore, to nonlinear multiscale equations
[
28
]
.

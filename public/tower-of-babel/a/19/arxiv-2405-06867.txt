Rigidity of CMC hypersurfaces in 5-and 6-manifolds
Han Hong, Zetian Yan

Abstract
. We prove that nonnegative 3 3 -intermediate Ricci curvature combined with uniformly positive k k -triRic curvature implies rigidity of complete noncompact two-sided stable minimal hypersurfaces in a Riemannian manifold ( X 5 , g ) (X^{5},g) with bounded geometry. The stonger assumption of nonnegative 3 3 -intermediate Ricci curvature can be replaced by the nonnegativity of Ricci and biRic curvature. In particular, there is no complete noncompact stable minimal hypersurface in a closed 5 5 -dimensional manifold with positive sectional curvature. This extends result of Chodosh-Li-Stryker [J. Eur. Math. Soc (2025)] to 5 5 -dimension. We also establish rigidity results on CMC hypersurfaces with nonzero mean curvature in 5 5 - and 6 6 -manifolds.

1.
Introduction

An immersed complete two-sided stable minimal hypersurface
M^{n}
in orientable manifold
(X^{n+1},g)
has zero mean curvature and satisfies the inequality

(1.1)

\int_{M}\left(|A_{M}|^{2}+\Ric^{X}(\eta,\eta)\right)\psi^{2}\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\psi|^{2}

for any
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
, where
A_{M}
is the second fundamental form of the immersion,
\eta
is a unit normal vector on
M
and
\Ric^{X}
is the ambient Ricci curvature tensor. In a similar manner of stable geodesic in
2
-dimensions, stable minimal hypersurface is a powerful tool to study the ambient geometry of
(X^{n+1},g)
in higher dimensions. Assuming
M
is compact and orientable, standard arguments show that

(i)

When
\Ric^{X}>0
, there are no closed two-sided stable minimal hypersurfaces. If
\Ric^{X}\geq 0
, then
M
is totally geodesic and
\Ric^{X}(\eta,\eta)\equiv 0
along
M
; see
[
29
]
.

(ii)

If the ambient scalar curvature is strictly positive, then
M
also admits a metric of positive scalar curvature
[
25
,
24
]
. In three dimension, each component of
M
must be a
2
-sphere.

Now, if one assumes that
M^{n}
is non-compact with respect to the induced metric, it becomes very complicated and subtle. The theory has been well-developed in
3
-dimensions: if
M^{2}\hookrightarrow(X^{3},g)
is a complete noncompact two-sided stable minimal immersion, it holds that

(i)

When
R_{X}\geq 0
,
(M,g)
is conformal to either a plane or a cylinder
[
12
]
. In the latter case,
M
is totally geodesic, intrinsically flat and
R_{X}\equiv 0
along
M
.

(ii)

When
\Ric^{X}\geq 0
,
(M,g)
is totally geodesic, intrinsically flat and has
\Ric^{X}(\eta,\eta)\equiv 0
along
M

[
27
]
. Moreover, either
X^{3}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or its universal cover is isometric to the Riemann product
M^{2}\times\mathbb{R}

[
20
]
.

(iii)

When
R_{X}\geq 1
,
M
must be compact
[
26
]
.

Recently, Chodosh, Li and Stryker developed such theory in
4
-dimensions by combining nonnegativity of
2
-intermediate Ricci curvature with strict positivity of scalar curvature.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

If
(X^{4},g)
is a complete
4
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with weakly bounded geometry, satisfying

\operatorname{Ric}^{X}_{2}\geq 0\ \text{and}\ R_{X}\geq\delta>0,

where
\Ric_{2}^{X}
is the
2
-intermediate Ricci curvature (Definition
1.7
). Then any complete two-sided stable minimal immersion
M^{3}\rightarrow(X^{4},g)
is totally geodesic and has
\operatorname{Ric}^{X}(\eta,\eta)\equiv 0
along
M
.

It is natural to ask what happens in higher dimensions. A fundamental question is to determine which ambient curvature conditions ensure compactness or rule out the existence of complete stable minimal hypersurfaces. As discussed above in
3
-manifolds,
R_{X}\geq 1
implies compactness and
\Ric^{X}>0
implies non-existence. However, two examples constructed in
[
9
]
[Example 1.1,1.2] can be easily adapted to show that neither of these results can hold in higher dimensions.

Note that for most of aforementioned results, it is essential that no assumption is made on properness or volume growth of
M
. The result in
[
9
]
cannot extend to
5
-dimensions directly because
\Ric^{X}_{2}\geq 0
and
R_{X}>\delta
are tailor to obtain one-endness and volume growth of hypersurfaces in
4
-dimensions. Hence, exploring alternative curvature conditions to maintain one-endness and volume growth in higher dimensions is the main topic in this paper.

1.1.
Main results

The purpose of this article is to extend results in
[
9
]
to
5
and
6
dimensions under different curvature assumptions and stability of CMC hypersurfaces.

Before discussing our main results, we recall some definitions and introduce some notions.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
(X^{n+1},g)
,
n\geq 2
, be a Riemannian manifold. An immersed CMC hypersurface (two-sided if it is minimal)
M^{n}
in
X
is stable if

\int_{M}\left(|A_{M}|^{2}+\Ric^{X}(\eta,\eta)\right)\psi^{2}\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\psi|^{2}

for
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
(X^{n+1},g)
,
n\geq 2
, be a Riemannian manifold. Given a local orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
of
T_{p}X
and
k,\alpha>0
, we define
(k,\alpha)
-triRicci curvature
in the direction
(e_{i};e_{j},e_{l})
as

(1.2)

(k,\alpha)\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}(e_{i};e_{j},e_{l}):=k\Ric^{X}(e_{i},e_{i})+\alpha\text{-}\biRic_{e_{i}^{\perp}}(e_{j},e_{l}),

where
\alpha\text{-}\biRic_{e_{i}^{\perp}}(e_{j},e_{l})
is given by

\alpha\text{-}\biRic_{e_{i}^{\perp}}(e_{j},e_{l}):=\sum_{m\neq i}\R^{X}_{mjmj}+\alpha\sum_{m\neq i}\R^{X}_{mlml}-\alpha\R^{X}_{jljl},\quad j\neq l

i.e., we take the partial contraction on the subspace
e_{i}^{\perp}:=\vspan\{e_{l}\}_{l\neq i}
normal to
e_{i}
. Furthermore, we define

(k,\alpha)\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}(p)=\inf\{(k,\alpha)\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}(e_{i};e_{j},e_{l})\},

where the infimum is taken over all local orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
at
p
, and over all admissible index triples
(e_{i};e_{j},e_{l})
,
i\neq j\neq l
.
If
\alpha=1
, we simply denote it by
k\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}
.

Remark 1.4
.

When
n=2
or
3
,
1\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}
is just the half of the scalar curvature of
X
. In higher dimensions,
1\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}
is the
3
-intermediate curvature
\mathcal{C}_{3}
defined by Brendle-Hirsch-Johne
[
3
]
.

Remark 1.5
.

Direct calculation yields

\begin{split}&\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\sum_{j\neq i,l\neq i}k\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}(e_{i};e_{j},e_{l})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(kn(n-1)\Ric^{X}(e_{i},e_{i})+(2n-3)\sum_{j\neq i,l\neq i}\R^{X}_{jljl}\right)\\
&=kn(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\Ric^{X}(e_{i},e_{i})+(2n-3)(n-1)\sum_{j,l=1}^{n+1}\R^{X}_{jljl}\\
&=((k+2)n-3)(n-1)R^{X}.\end{split}

In particular, positive
k
-triRicci curvature implies that
X
has positive scalar curvature. Conversely,
\Sigma^{2}\times S^{3}(5/12)
has positive scalar curvature but does not have positive
1
- triRicci curvature where
\Sigma^{2}
is a closed hyperbolic surface and
S^{3}(5/12)
is a sphere with constant curvature
5/12
.

Remark 1.6
.

When
(X^{n+1},g)
is the hyperbolic space,
k\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}=3-(k+2)n.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
(X^{n+1},g)
,
n\geq 2
, be a Riemannian manifold. The
(n-1)
-intermediate Ricci curvature of
X
at
p\in X
in the direction
e_{l}
, is defined as

\Ric^{X}_{n-1}(e_{i};e_{l})=\sum_{j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}\setminus i}\R_{X}(e_{j},e_{l},e_{j},e_{l})

where
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
is any orthonormal basis of
T_{p}X
. Similarly, we define

\Ric^{X}_{n-1}(p)=\inf\{\Ric^{X}_{n-1}(e_{i};e_{l}):\ i\neq l\}.

We now state our results on minimal hypersurfaces in 5-manifolds; more general results can be found in Section
5
.

Theorem 1.8
.

If
(X^{5},g)
is a complete
5
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with weakly bounded geometry, satisfying

\operatorname{Ric}^{X}_{3}\geq 0\ \text{and}\ k\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}\geq\delta>0\ \text{for}\ k\in[1,2].

Then any complete two-sided stable minimal immersion
M^{4}\hookrightarrow X^{5}
is parabolic with at most two ends. In particular, it is totally geodesic and has
\operatorname{Ric}^{X}(\eta,\eta)\equiv 0
along
M.

It is possible to weaken non-negativity of
\Ric_{3}^{X}
to a mixed curvature condition. Note that
\Ric_{3}^{X}\geq 0
implies
\Ric^{X}\geq 0
and
\biRic_{X}\geq 0
, where
\biRic_{X}
is the bi-Ricci curvature (see Definition
2.2
in detail). In the Appendix, we provide a (one parameter family) closed
5
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(X^{5},g)
, satisfying

\operatorname{Ric}^{X}>0,\ \,\biRic_{X}>0,\ \,\text{and}\ \ 1\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}\geq\delta>0,

while
\Ric^{X}_{3}<0
somewhere. As a matter of fact, the result in Theorem
1.8
holds with
\Ric_{3}^{X}\geq 0
replaced by
\Ric^{X}\geq 0
plus
\biRic_{X}\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.9
.

If
(X^{5},g)
is a complete
5
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry, satisfying

\operatorname{Ric}^{X}\geq 0,\ \,\biRic_{X}\geq 0,\ \,\text{and}\ \ k\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}\geq\delta>0\ \text{for}\ k\in[1,2].

Then any complete two-sided stable minimal immersion
M^{4}\rightarrow X^{5}
is parabolic with at most two ends. In particular, it is totally geodesic and has
\operatorname{Ric}^{X}(\eta,\eta)\equiv 0
along
M.

Let us make some comments here. The first one is that if
(X^{5},g)
is a manifold with bounded geometry satisfying
\Ric^{X}>0
,
\operatorname{biRic}_{X}\geq 0
and
k\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}\geq\delta>0\ \text{for}\ k\in[1,2]
, then there is no complete two-sided stable minimal immersion. This follows directly from Theorem
1.9
. As a consequence, there is no two-sided stable minimal immersion in
(\mathbb{S}^{5},g_{can})
(previously proved in
[
6
]
). The second one is that there is no complete two-sided stable minimal immersion in manifold
X
with
\Ric^{X}>0
and
\biRic_{X}\geq\delta>0
. Indeed, uniformly positive
\biRic_{X}
implies that a complete two-sided stable minimal immersion must be compact
[
28
]
. Then the standard argument leads to contradiction. Finally, we would like to mention that the conclusion of at most two ends in above two theorems can be compared to the result proved by Fischer-Colbrie and Schoen
[
12
]
that a complete two-sided stable minimal surface in a manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature has at most two ends (plane or cylinder). Furthermore, we prove some related results in dimension
n+1=6
(see Section
5.2
).

1.2.
Main ideas

The strategy in this article follows from that in
[
9
]
. A common feature in the proofs of Theorem
1.8
and Theorem
1.9
is the estimate on volume growth of ends by using
\mu
-bubbles, a technique introduced by Gromov
[
14
]
. The method in
[
9
]
cannot extend to
5
-dimensions directly because one cannot control curvature terms from the second fundamental form, the ambient Ricci curvature and the positive Jacobi function in the second variation of the warped
\mu
-bubble constructed there. The novelty of our method is to introduce a parameter (the exponent of the warped function) which brings more freedom to exploit curvature conditions. The same strategy works well in the resolution of stable Bernstein problem in
\mathbb{R}^{6}

[
21
]
.

Another interesting feature of Theorem
1.8
and Theorem
1.9
is the combination of different curvature conditions which enter the proof at different places. For completeness, we present the overall strategy as follows.

Let
M^{n}\hookrightarrow(X^{n+1},g)

(4\leq n\leq 5)
be a complete two-sided stable minimal hypersurface.

(i)

To show the vanishing of
A_{M}
and
\operatorname{Ric}^{X}(\eta,\eta)
, we mainly prove that the hypersurface is parabolic. Then the standard equation
\Delta u+(|A_{M}|^{2}+\Ric^{X}(\eta,\eta))u=0
will imply the result.

(ii)

In dimension
6
, the fact that
M
has at most one nonparabolic end follows from the Schoen–Yau inequality which requires only
\biRic_{X}\geq 0
together with the infinite volume of
M
, ensured by the bounded geometry assumption. When
\Ric^{X}_{3}\geq 0
, bounded geometry condition can be weaken to weakly bounded geometry; see
[
9
]
[Theorem 4.1] for details. In contrast, in dimension
5
, the splitting result in Theorem
3.1
implies that
M^{4}
has at most two ends. Moreover, if
M^{4}
has two ends, it must be a product manifold, see
[
1
,
5
,
15
]
. Therefore, we only need to consider the case where
M^{4}
has one end.

(iii)

The construction of a good test function along the nonparabolic end is the main issue in the remaining proof. This is where the positivity of
k\operatorname{-triRic}
comes in. By conformal change, it is well known
[
24
]
that a stable minimal hypersurface admits a metric with positive scalar curvature if the ambient metric does. It is interesting to understand if the positivity of
k\operatorname{-triRic}
can be inherited by hypersurfaces in some sense.

As mentioned in
[
9
]
, it is difficult to prove the linear volume growth of the nonparabolic end. Similarly, we consider an exhaustion
\{\Omega_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
of
E
and use the theory of
\mu
-bubbles to show
\partial\Omega_{l}\cap E
has controlled volume. This is one difference comparing to
[
9
]
. To control curvature terms in the second variation of the
\mu
-bubble, we introduce a parameter
k
as the exponent of the warped function. Suitable choice of
k
yields the desired volume estimate; see Lemma
4.7
in detail. This is the step that needs the assumption of strictly positive
k\operatorname{-triRic}
.

(iv)

We need to upgrade volume control of the bubble to volume control of the slice which needs a lower bound on the intrinsic Ricci curvature of
M
. It is achieved by combining several curvature conditions and curvature estiamtes in Lemma
2.5
.

(v)

Finally, to prove parabolicity, we need to
construct test functions
\varphi_{i}
such that
\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}
tends to zero. This is achievable due to the facts that we have at most one nonparabolic end and the nonparabolic end satisfies almost linear volume growth.

1.3.
Organization

In Section 2, we recall some preliminaries on curvature estimates and ends of non-compact manifolds. In Section 3, we establish the splitting theorem and the one-end theorem under various curvature conditions. Section 4 is devoted to the balanced warped
\mu
-bubble exhaustion and volume growth estimates. The main results are proved in Section 5. The Appendix presents an example related to Theorem
1.9
.

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Curvature conditions

If
(X^{n+1},g)
is a Riemannian manifold, we denote the Riemann curvature tensor, the sectional curvature, the Ricci curvature tensor and the scalar curvature by
\R^{X}_{ijij}
,
\sec_{X}
,
\Ric^{X}_{ij}
and
\R^{X}
respectively. Let
M\hookrightarrow X^{n+1}
be an immersion. We write
\Ric^{M}
and
R^{M}
to denote the Ricci curvature and scalar curvature of the pullback metric on
M
. We set the mean curvature to be the trace of the second fundamental form of the immersion.

Definition 2.1
.

A manifold
X^{n+1}
has
bounded geometry
if there exist positive real numbers
\delta
and
\lambda
such that on
X^{n+1}

|\sec_{X}|\leqslant\delta^{2},\quad\inj_{X}\geqslant\lambda

where
\inj_{X}
is the injectivity radius.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X^{n+1},g)
be a Riemannian manifold and
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
be an orthonormal basis for
T_{p}X
,
p\in X
. The
bi-Ricci curvature
along directions
(e_{1},e_{2})
is defined by

\biRic_{X}(e_{1},e_{2}):=\sum_{i=2}^{n+1}\R^{X}(e_{1},e_{i},e_{i},e_{1})+\sum_{j=3}^{n+1}\R^{X}(e_{2},e_{j},e_{j},e_{2}).

We denote
\biRic_{X}:=\inf\{\biRic_{X}(e_{1},e_{2}):\ p\in X\ \text{and}\ e_{1},e_{2}\in T_{p}X\}.

This concept was used by Shen-Ye
[
28
]
to study diameter of stable minimal submanifolds.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(X^{n+1},g)
be a
n+1
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry. If
M^{n}\hookrightarrow(X^{n+1},g)
is an immersed CMC hypersurface, then

\Ric^{M}\geqslant-Cg,

where the positive constant
C
depends on
n
,
\sec_{X}
,
H^{M}
and
\sup|A^{M}|
.

Proof.

Choose an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
of
X^{n+1}
at a point
p\in M
with
e_{n+1}
normal to
M
. The traced Gauss equation gives

\Ric^{M}(e_{1},e_{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\R^{X}_{i1i1}+A^{M}(e_{1},e_{1})H^{M}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}A^{M}(e_{1},e_{j})^{2},

which yields the desired result.
∎

Without bounded geometry, there is an alternative way to ensure the lower bound on
\Ric_{M}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(X^{n+1},g)
be a
(n+1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with
\Ric^{X}_{n-1}\geq C_{1}
for a constant
C_{1}
. If
M^{n}\hookrightarrow(X^{n+1},g)
is an immersed CMC hypersurface, then

\Ric^{M}\geqslant-Cg,

where the positive constant
C
depends on
n
,
C_{1}
,
H^{M}
and
\sup|A^{M}|
. In particular,
C
can be chosen to be zero if
M
is totally geodesic and
C_{1}=0
.

Proof.

The result holds because

\displaystyle\Ric^{M}(e_{1},e_{1})=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\R^{X}_{i1i1}+A^{M}(e_{1},e_{1})H^{M}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}A^{M}(e_{1},e_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{5-n}{4}H_{M}^{2}-|A^{M}|^{2}

where the inequality follows from

|A^{M}|^{2}+H^{M}A^{M}(e_{1},e_{1})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}A^{M}(e_{1},e_{j})^{2}\geq\frac{5-n}{4}H_{M}^{2}.

∎

2.2.
Curvature estimates

In this subsection, we recall the standard curvature estimates for stable CMC hypersurfaces in non-compact manifolds.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(X^{n+1},g)

(4\leq n\leq 5)
be a
(n+1)
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with weakly bounded geometry. Then there is a constant
C
depending on
X
and
H^{M}
such that every complete noncompact stable CMC hypersurface (two-sided if it is minimal)
M^{n}\hookrightarrow(X^{n+1},g)
with the mean curvature
H^{M}
and compact boundary (possibly empty) satisfies

\sup_{q\in\Omega}|A^{M}(q)|\min\{1,d_{M}(q,\partial\Omega)\}\leqslant C

for any compact subset
\Omega\subset M
.

Proof.

Observe that when scaling the metric by the norm of the second fundamental form that tends to infinity, the limit of sequence of stable CMC hypersurface is a stable minimal hypersurface. Therefore, with the stable Bernstein theorem in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
and
\mathbb{R}^{6}
, the statement follows from
[
9
]
[Lemma 2.4] and
[
16
]
[Lemma 3.6] with minor modification; see also
[
23
]
.
∎

2.3.
Ends of manifold

We now recall some preliminary results on parabolicity and nonparabolicity of manifolds; see
[
19
]
for more details.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
M
be a complete noncompact manifold, and let
K\subset M
be a compact subset and let
E
be an unbounded connected component of
M\setminus K
with smooth boundary. Then
E
is said to be nonparabolic with respect to
K
if there exists a positive harmonic function
f
satisfying

f|_{\partial E}=1,\ \text{and}\ \inf_{E}f=0.

Otherwise,
E
is parabolic.

Definition 2.7
.

Fix a point
x\in M,
and fix
L>0.
A collection of open sets
\{E_{k}\}
is an end of
M
adapted to
x
and length scale
L
if each set
E_{k}
is an unbounded connected component of
M\setminus\bar{B}_{kL}(x)
and satisfies
E_{k+1}\subset E_{k}.

Without lost of generality, we usually ignore the fixed point
x
if it is clear in the context.

Definition 2.8
.

A boundary end
\{E_{k}\}_{k=1}
with length scale
L>0
is nonparabolic if for every
k
,
E_{k}
is an unbounded component of
M\backslash B_{kL}
and
E_{k}
is nonparabolic.

3.
Ends of hypersurfaces

3.1.
Splitting theorem and finite ends

In this subsection, we show that in a manifold
(X^{n+1},g)
, the number of ends of a stable CMC hypersurface
M^{n}
is at most two under certain conditions.

The stability of
M
implies that for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(M)
,

\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\geq\int_{M}(|A|^{2}+\Ric^{X}(\nu,\nu))\varphi^{2}.

Let
\Ric^{M}(x)=\inf_{x\in M}\{\Ric^{M}(\tau,\tau):|\tau|=1\ \text{and}\ \tau\in T_{x}M\}
. Suppose
e_{1}
is an unit tangent vector of
M
at
x
such that
\Ric^{M}(x)=\Ric^{M}(e_{1},e_{1})
. Then the curvature rearrangement gives

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\alpha\Ric^{M}(x)\varphi^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M}(|A|^{2}+\Ric^{X}(\nu,\nu)+\alpha\Ric^{M}(e_{1},e_{1}))\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\Ric^{X}(\nu,\nu)+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n}\R^{X}_{1i1i})\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{M}(|A|^{2}+\alpha A_{11}H-\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n}A^{2}_{1i})\varphi^{2}.

Terms involving the second fundamental from on the right-hand side is bounded from below by

\frac{4-(n-1)\alpha^{2}}{4((1-\alpha)n+\alpha)}H^{2},\quad\alpha<\frac{n}{n-1};

see Deng
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
. Moreover, the equality holds iff

A_{11}=-\frac{(n-1)\alpha-2}{2((1-\alpha)n+\alpha)}H,\quad A_{ii}=\frac{2-\alpha}{2((1-\alpha)n+\alpha)}H,\quad i\neq 1.

Assuming that
\alpha\text{-}\biRic_{X}(e_{1},\nu):=\Ric^{X}(\nu,\nu)+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n}\R^{X}_{1i1i}
satisfies

\alpha\text{-}\biRic+\frac{4-(n-1)\alpha^{2}}{4((1-\alpha)n+\alpha)}H^{2}\geq 0,

we have

\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\alpha\Ric^{M}(x)\varphi^{2}\geq 0.

Then it follows from works of Antonelli–Pozzetta–Xu
[
1
]
, Catino–Mari–Mastrolia–Roncoroni
[
5
]
and Hong–Wang
[
15
]
(using different ideas) that if
M
has at least two ends and
\alpha>\frac{n-1}{4}
, then
\Ric^{M}\geq 0
on
M.
By the classical Cheeger–Gromoll splitting theorem,
M
is a product manifold
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
with linear volume growth, and thus parabolic manifold.

Thus, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X^{n+1},g)
(
n\leq 5
) be a complete manifold and

\alpha\text{-}\biRic_{X}+\frac{4-(n-1)\alpha^{2}}{4((1-\alpha)n+\alpha)}H^{2}\geqslant 0

for
\alpha\in(\frac{n-1}{4},\frac{n}{n-1})
. Let
M^{n}\hookrightarrow(X^{n+1},g)
be a complete stable (two-sided if it is minimal) CMC immersion with the mean curvature
H
. Let
K\subset M

4.1.
Balanced
\mu
-bubbles

We recall some preliminary results on existence and stability for balanced warped
\mu
-bubbles. Given a Riemannian
n
-manifold
(M^{n},g)
with boundary,
n\leqslant 7
, we assume that a choice of labeling the components of
\partial M
is
\partial M=\partial_{-}M\cup\partial_{+}M
where neither of them are empty. For a fixed smooth function
u>0
on
M
and a smooth function on
\mathring{M}
with
h\to\pm\infty
on
\partial_{\pm}M
, consider the following balanced warped area functional:

\mathcal{A}_{k}(\Omega):=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}u^{k}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{M}\left(\chi_{\Omega}-\chi_{\Omega_{0}}\right)hu^{k}d\mathcal{H}^{n},\quad k>0,

for all Caccioppoli sets
\Omega
with
\Omega\Delta\Omega_{0}\Subset\mathring{M}
, where the reference set
\Omega_{0}
with smooth boundary satisfies

\partial\Omega_{0}\subset\mathring{M},\quad\partial_{+}M\subset\Omega_{0}.

We will call
\Omega
minimizing
\mathcal{A}_{k}
in this class a balanced warped
\mu
-bubble, or just
\mu
-bubble for short.

Remark 4.1
.

\mathcal{A}_{k}(\Omega)
is equivalent to the functional

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Omega)=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\widetilde{g}}-\int_{M}\left(\chi_{\Omega}-\chi_{\Omega_{0}}\right)hu^{\frac{-k}{n-1}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\widetilde{g}},

under the conformal change
\widetilde{g}=u^{\frac{2k}{n-1}}g
. The critical point of
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
is a hypersurface with the mean curvature
\tilde{H}=hu^{-\frac{k}{n-1}}
. This is equivalent to the statement in Lemma
4.3
. We find that it is more convenient to work with
\mathcal{A}_{k}(\Omega)
directly instead of
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Omega)
under the conformal change.

\mu

4.2.
Volume bound for bubbles

In this subsection, we are going to estimate the volume of
\mu
-bubbles constructed in stable two-sided CMC hypersurfaces. We first prove the following two technical propositions.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})
be an
n\times n
symmetric matrix with
n\geq 2
, and let
H=tr(A)
. Then, the following holds:

k|A|^{2}-a_{11}a_{nn}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{1i}^{2}-\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{in}^{2}+H(a_{11}+a_{nn})\geq\frac{2k^{2}+k-n+2}{2nk-3n+6}H^{2}

for any

(4.1)

k\geq\max\left\{1,\frac{3(n-2)}{2n}\right\}.

Equality holds if and only if

\begin{split}a_{11}&=a_{nn}=-\frac{2(n-2k-2)}{2nk-3n+6}H,\\
a_{ii}&=\frac{2k-1}{2nk-3n+6}H,\quad i\neq 1,n,\\
a_{ij}&=0,\quad~~\mbox{otherwise}~~.\end{split}

Proof.

Combining Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the fact

|A|^{2}\geq(a_{11}^{2}+a_{nn}^{2})+\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}a_{ii}^{2}+2(\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{1i}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}a_{in}^{2}),

we have

\displaystyle k|A|^{2}

\displaystyle-a_{11}a_{nn}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{1i}^{2}-\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{in}^{2}+H(a_{11}+a_{nn})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(k-1)(a_{11}^{2}+a_{nn}^{2})-a_{11}a_{nn}+\frac{k}{n-2}(H-a_{11}-a_{nn})^{2}

\displaystyle+(2k-1)(\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{1i}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}a_{in}^{2})+H(a_{11}+a_{nn})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k}{2}-\frac{3}{4}\right)(a_{11}+a_{nn})^{2}+\frac{k}{n-2}H^{2}-\frac{2kH}{n-2}(a_{11}+a_{nn})

\displaystyle+\frac{k}{n-2}(a_{11}+a_{nn})^{2}+H(a_{11}+a_{nn})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k}{2}-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{k}{n-2}\right)(a_{11}+a_{nn})^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2k}{n-2}\right)H(a_{11}+a_{nn})+\frac{k}{n-2}H^{2}.

The conclusion now follows from elementary calculations. When equality holds, we have
a_{11}=a_{nn}
and the diagonal entries
a_{ii}
are all equal for
i=2,\cdots,n-1
.
∎

Notice that
\frac{3(n-2)}{2n}<1
when
n\leq 5
. Thus, when applying Proposition
4.5
, we can merely assume
k\geq 1
when
n\leq 5.
We have a more general result as follows.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})
be an
n\times n
symmetric matrix and denote
H=\tr(A)
. For any
k\geq 1/2
and
\alpha\leq 2k
satisfying

(4.2)

\begin{split}&k-\frac{(n-2)\alpha^{2}}{4k}>0,\\
\quad k^{2}-&\frac{1}{4}-\frac{n-2}{4}\left(1+\alpha^{2}-\frac{\alpha}{k}\right)>0,\end{split}

there exists a positive constant
\delta=\delta(n,k,\alpha)
such that

I:=k|A|^{2}-\alpha a_{11}a_{nn}-\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{1i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{in}^{2}+H(\alpha a_{11}+a_{nn})\geq\frac{\delta}{n}H^{2}.

Proof.

We consider the matrix

(4.3)

P=\begin{pmatrix}k&\frac{1}{2}&\frac{1}{2}&\cdots&\frac{1}{2}\\
\frac{1}{2}&k&\frac{\alpha}{2}&\cdots&\frac{\alpha}{2}\\
\frac{1}{2}&\frac{\alpha}{2}&k&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{1}{2}&\frac{\alpha}{2}&0&\cdots&k\end{pmatrix}_{n\times n}.

By elementary row and column operations, it is easy to see that the matrix
P
is congruent to

\begin{pmatrix}k-\frac{n-2)}{4k}&\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{(n-2)\alpha}{2k}\right)&0&\cdots&0\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{(n-2)\alpha}{2k}\right)&k-\frac{(n-2)\alpha^{2}}{4k}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&k&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&k\end{pmatrix}_{n\times n}.

Therefore, the positive definiteness of
P
is equivalent to the positive definiteness of
Q
:

Q=\begin{pmatrix}k-\frac{n-2}{4k}&\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{(n-2)\alpha}{2k}\right)\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{(n-2)\alpha}{2k}\right)&k-\frac{(n-2)\alpha^{2}}{4k}\end{pmatrix}

which requires

(4.4)

\begin{split}k&-\frac{(n-2)\alpha^{2}}{4k}>0\\
\det Q=k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}-&\frac{n-2}{4}\left(1+\alpha^{2}-\frac{\alpha}{k}\right)>0.\end{split}

Then we set

x=\begin{pmatrix}a_{nn}\\
a_{11}\\
a_{22}\\
\vdots\\
a_{n-1,n-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

By assumptions (
4.2
) and Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

I\geq x^{T}Px\geq\delta|x|^{2}\geq\frac{\delta}{n}H^{2}

where
\delta
is the smallest eigenvalue of

.

∎

\begin{split}\Ric^{\Sigma}(e_{1},e_{1})&=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\R^{\Sigma}_{i1i1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\R^{M}_{i1i1}+A^{\Sigma}_{ii}A^{\Sigma}_{11}-\left(A^{\Sigma}_{i1}\right)^{2}\right)\\

&=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\R^{X}_{i1i1}+(H^{M}-A^{M}_{nn})A^{M}_{11}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(A^{M}_{i1})^{2}+H^{\Sigma}A^{\Sigma}_{11}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(A^{\Sigma}_{i1}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\Ric^{M}(\nu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(R^{X}_{inin}+A^{M}_{nn}A_{ii}^{M}-A_{ni}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}R^{X}_{inin}+H^{M}A^{M}_{nn}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(A_{in}^{M})^{2}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}-ku^{-1}&\Delta_{M}u+|A^{\Sigma}|^{2}+\Ric^{M}(\nu,\nu)+\alpha\Ric^{\Sigma}(e_{1},e_{1})\\
=&k|A^{M}|^{2}+k\Ric^{X}(\eta,\eta)+\Ric^{M}(\nu,\nu)+|A^{\Sigma}|^{2}+\alpha\Ric^{\Sigma}(e_{1},e_{1})\\
=&k\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\R^{X}_{i(n+1)i(n+1)}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\R^{X}_{inin}+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\R^{X}_{i1i1}\\
&+k|A^{M}|^{2}+\alpha(H^{M}-A^{M}_{nn})A^{M}_{11}-\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(A^{M}_{i1}\right)^{2}+H_{M}A^{M}_{nn}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(A^{M}_{in}\right)^{2}\\
&+|A^{\Sigma}|^{2}+\alpha H^{\Sigma}A^{\Sigma}_{11}-\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(A^{\Sigma}_{i1})^{2}\\
\geq&k\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\R^{X}_{i(n+1)i(n+1)}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\R^{X}_{inin}+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\R^{X}_{i1i1}\\
+&C(k,n)H_{M}^{2}+|A^{\Sigma}|^{2}+\alpha H^{\Sigma}A^{\Sigma}_{11}-\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(A^{\Sigma}_{i1})^{2}\ \end{split}

where the last inequality is due to
Proposition
4.5
or Proposition
4.6
and
C(k,n)
is defined in (

).

Now the inequality (
4.8
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{4}{4-k}

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\geq\int_{\Sigma}(-\alpha\Ric^{\Sigma}(e_{1},e_{1}))\phi^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\left(k\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\R^{X}_{i(n+1)i(n+1)}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\R^{X}_{inin}+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\R^{X}_{i1i1}+C(k,n)H_{M}^{2}\right)\phi^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\left(|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+\alpha H^{\Sigma}A^{\Sigma}_{11}-\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(A^{\Sigma}_{i1})^{2}\right)\phi^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}kH^{\Sigma}(\nabla_{\nu}\log u)\phi^{2}+\nabla_{\nu}h\phi^{2}+k(\nabla_{\nu}\log u)^{2}\phi^{2}.

We now estimate the following quantity

I:=|A^{\Sigma}|^{2}+\alpha H^{\Sigma}A^{\Sigma}_{11}-\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(A^{\Sigma}_{i1})^{2}+kH^{\Sigma}(\nabla_{\nu}\log u)+\nabla_{\nu}h+k(\nabla_{\nu}\log u)^{2}

and expect that

for some

.
In the same spirit in
[

, it is equivalent to show that the matrix

is positive semi-definite for some

According to the definition of

and choosing

realizing the minimum Ricci curvature

, we get

\begin{split}\frac{4}{4-k}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi|^{2}\geqslant\int_{\Sigma}\left(-\alpha\lambda_{\Ric}(\Sigma)+\varepsilon_{0}\right)\phi^{2}.\end{split}

Therefore, there is a positive smooth function

4.3.
Almost linear volume growth

In this part, we will upgrade the volume estimate of
\mu
-bubbles to the volume growth of ends in stable CMC hypersurfaces under several curvature conditions.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(X^{n+1},g)

(4\leq n\leq 5)
be a complete
(n+1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, and let
M^{n}\hookrightarrow(X^{n+1},g)
be a simply-connected stable two-sided CMC hypersurface. Let
\{E_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
be an end of
M^{n}
adapted to
p\in M
with length scale
L
, where
L
is the constant from Lemma
4.7
. Denote
M_{l}:=E_{l}\cap B_{(l+1)L}(p)
. Assume that either
X
has bounded geometry or
X
has weakly bounded geometry and
\Ric^{X}_{n-1}\geq C_{2}
for a constant
C_{2}
. Under assumptions in Lemma
4.7
, there is a universal constant
C>0
independent of
l
such that

\Vol(M_{l})<C

for all
l
sufficiently large.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
4.7
to the end
E_{l}
, which supplies an open set
\Omega_{l}
in
B_{L/2}(\partial E_{l})\cap E_{l}
with
\partial E_{l}\subset\partial\Omega_{l}
. Note that
\Omega_{l}\subset M_{l}
and
\partial E_{l+1}\cap\overline{\Omega_{l}}=\emptyset
by construction.

As claimed in
[
9
, Lemma 5.4]
,
\overline{M_{l}}
is connected and there exists a component
\Sigma^{*}_{l}
of the
\mu
-bubble
\Sigma_{l}:=\partial\Omega_{l}\backslash\partial E_{l}
separating
\partial E_{l}
and
\partial E_{l+1}
. In fact, if not, we can find a non-contractible closed curve, contradicting the simply-connectedness of
M^{n}
. By Lemma
4.7
, we have
\Vol(\Sigma_{l}^{*})\leqslant c
where
c
is independent of
l.

We now claim that there is a constant
D>0
independent of
l
such that
\Vol(M_{l})<D
. It can be deduced by using the area comparison theorem of hypersurfaces. This idea was firstly applied by Wei-Xu-Zhang in
[
30
]
. Due to the fact that
\Sigma_{l-1}^{*}
separates

and
E_{l}
, every hypersurface

for

lies behind

It follows from Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.3

\Vol(\partial B_{s}(p)\cap M_{l})\leq C\frac{e^{(n-1)s}}{e^{(n-1)(l-1)L}}\Vol(\Sigma_{l-1}^{*})\leq Ce^{2(n-1)L}

\Vol(M_{l})\leq CLe^{2(n-1)L}.

∎

Appendix A
Example

We provide a (in fact, a family of) closed
5
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(X^{5},g)
with bounded geometry, satisfying

\operatorname{Ric}_{X}\geq 0,\ \,\biRic_{X}\geq 0,\ \,\text{and}\ \ 1\operatorname{-triRic}_{X}\geq\delta>0,

while
\Ric_{3}<0
somewhere.

Consider the Berger sphere:
\mathbb{S}^{5}=\overline{G}/\overline{H}
, where
\overline{G}=SU(3)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
\overline{H}=SU(2)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
. We follow the notation in
[
33
]
. For the bi-invariant metric
\langle X,Y\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\tr(XY)
,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
is an
\Ad(H)
invariant splitting and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
has an orthonormal basis: for
r\in\{1,2\}
,

A=\frac{i}{\sqrt{3}}\left(E_{1,1}+E_{2,2}-2E_{3,3}\right),\quad e_{r}=E_{r,3}-E_{3,r},\quad f_{r}=i\left(E_{r,3}+E_{3,r}\right).

Moreover,
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{2}
, where

\mathfrak{p}_{1}:=\vspan\{e_{r},f_{r}\},\quad\mathfrak{p}_{2}=\mathbb{R}\cdot A.

Let
D
be a basis of the Lie algebra of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. By
[
33
]
[Theorem 3], the metric
g_{s}
defined by

g_{s}:=g|_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\times\mathfrak{p}_{1}}+s^{2}\cdot g|_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}\times\mathfrak{p}_{2}}

is naturally reductive with respect to the decomposition
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{p}}_{s}
, where

\overline{\mathfrak{p}}_{s}:=\vspan\{s^{2}A+(s^{2}-1)D,e_{r},f_{r}\}.

We will abbreviate

d_{s}=\frac{s^{2}A+(s^{2}-1)D}{s}.

Thus
\{d_{s},e_{r},f_{r}\}
,
r\in\{1,2\}
, is a
g_{s}
-orthonormal basis of
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}_{s}
. Since
\Ad(\overline{H})
maps any unit vector into a vector

v_{\alpha}:=\cos\alpha d_{s}+\sin\alpha e_{1},

without loss of generality, we can restrict ourselves to
v=v_{\alpha}
. For convenience, we set
\overline{e}_{1}=\sin\alpha d_{s}-\cos\alpha e_{1}
.

Following the calculation in
[
33
]
, we list sectional curvatures of
(\mathbb{S}^{5},g_{s})
:

\begin{split}\sec_{g_{s}}(v,\overline{e}_{1})=\frac{3}{4}s^{2},\quad&\sec_{g_{s}}(v,e_{2})=\sin^{2}\alpha+\frac{3s^{2}}{4}\cos^{2}\alpha,\\
\sec_{g_{s}}(v,f_{1})=\frac{3}{4}s^{2}+(4-3s^{2})\sin^{2}\alpha,\quad&\sec_{g_{s}}(v,f_{2})=\sin^{2}\alpha+\frac{3s^{2}}{4}\cos^{2}\alpha,\\
\sec_{g_{s}}(\overline{e}_{1},e_{2})=\cos^{2}\alpha+\frac{3s^{2}}{4}\sin^{2}\alpha,\quad&\sec_{g_{s}}(\overline{e}_{1},f_{1})=\frac{3}{4}s^{2}+(4-3s^{2})\cos^{2}\alpha,\\

Elementary calculation yields

\begin{split}\Ric_{g_{s}}(v,v)&=3s^{2}+\left(6-\frac{9}{2}s^{2}\right)\sin^{2}\alpha,\\

\Ric_{g_{s}}&\equiv 0,~~\mbox{otherwise.}~~\end{split}

When
s^{2}\in(16/9,4]
, we have

Next, we list all the bi-Ricci curvatures:

\begin{split}\biRic_{g_{s}}(v,\overline{e}_{1})&=6+\frac{3}{4}s^{2},\quad\biRic_{g_{s}}(v,f_{1})=6+\frac{3}{4}s^{2}+\left(2-\frac{3}{2}s^{2}\right)\sin^{2}\alpha,\\

and note that

is symmetric in

Direct calculation yields when

\displaystyle=1\operatorname{-triRic}(v;\overline{e}_{1},f_{2})=11-\frac{3}{2}s^{2}>0,

,
(\mathbb{S}^{5},g_{s})
has positive
\Ric
,

The Milstein scheme for singular SDEs with Hölder continuous drift
Máté Gerencsér , Gerald Lampl and Chengcheng Ling

Abstract
. We study the L p L^{p} rate of convergence of the Milstein scheme for SDEs when the drift coefficients possess only Hölder regularity. If the diffusion is elliptic and sufficiently regular, we obtain rates consistent with the additive case. The proof relies on regularisation by noise techniques, particularly stochastic sewing, which in turn requires (at least asymptotically) sharp estimates on the law of the Milstein scheme, which may be of independent interest. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): Primary 60H35, 60H10; Secondary 60H50, 60L90, 35B65. Keywords: Singular SDEs; Malliavin calculus; strong approximation; Milstein scheme; regularisation by noise; stochastic sewing; Zvonkin’s transformation.

Main idea of the proof

Let us briefly outline the strategy of the proof. The aim is to estimate the difference
X_{t}-X_{t}^{n}
which has the representation

\displaystyle X_{t}-X_{t}^{n}

\displaystyle=x_{0}-x_{0}^{n}+\int_{0}^{t}[b(X_{r})-b(X_{k_{n}(r)}^{n})]\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}[\sigma(X_{r})-\sigma(X_{k_{n}(r)}^{n})-(\nabla\sigma\sigma)(X_{k_{n}(r)}^{n})(W_{r}-W_{k_{n}(r)})]\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}=:I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}.

What is perhaps not immediate on the first sight is that the novel difficulties with the Milstein scheme do not lie with
I_{2}
. Indeed, writing

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}[\sigma(X_{r})-\sigma(X_{r}^{n})]\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}[\sigma(X_{r}^{n})-\sigma(X_{k_{n}(r)}^{n})-(\nabla\sigma\sigma)(X_{k_{n}(r)}^{n})(W_{r}-W_{k_{n}(r)})]\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}=:I_{21}+I_{22},

the Milstein scheme is designed precisely so that
I_{22}
is of order
n^{-1}
. The term
I_{21}
can be treated easily by an appropriate version of Gronwall’s lemma.
As for
I_{1}
, we write

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}[b(X_{r})-b(X_{r}^{n})]\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}[b(X_{r}^{n})-b(X_{k_{n}(r)}^{n})]\,\mathrm{d}r=:I_{11}+I_{22}.

The treatment of
I_{11}
relies on a version
Zvonkin’s transformation

[
40
,
37
]
, more precisely in the form of an Itô-Tanaka trick.
This transformation gives rise to further quantities similar to
I_{12}
, which (and
I_{12}
itself) are handled via
stochastic sewing

[
23
]
.
An important ingredient for stochastic sewing is the behavior of the law of the process, and the required estimates on the law of the Milstein approximation
X^{n}
are significantly more challenging than in the case of the Euler-Maruyama scheme (in fact, some “usual” bounds like two-sided heat kernel estimates do not even hold).
These estimates (see Section
Section

2
), which can be of independent interest, are derived via a Malliavin calculus toolbox.

Proof.

Note that for any

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\xi^{*}\mathcal{M}_{t}\xi=\int_{0}^{t}\|D_{s}X_{t}\xi\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

We notice that because of

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}((\det\mathcal{M}_{t})^{-\gamma})\leqslant{\mathbb{E}}(\lambda_{t}^{-d\gamma}).

We therefore estimate this last quantity.
Note that (

\displaystyle D^{k}_{s}X^{m}_{t}=u^{k,m}_{s}+\sum_{l=1}^{d_{1}}\int_{s}^{t}D^{k}_{s}(u_{r}^{m,l})\,\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}^{l}.

\displaystyle\|D_{s}X_{t}\xi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\|u_{s}\xi\|^{2}-\Big\|\Big(\int_{s}^{t}D_{s}u_{r}\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}\Big)\xi\Big\|^{2}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{*}-\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}D_{s}u_{r}\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}\Big\|^{2}.

After plugging (

Going back to the quantity we want to estimate, we write, for any

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(\lambda_{t}^{-\gamma d}>y)\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma d-1}{\mathbb{P}}(\lambda_{t}^{-1}>y)\,\,\mathrm{d}y

From (
4.10
), (
4.11
), (
4.12
), (
4.13
) and (
4.16
) we have the estimate

(4.18)

\displaystyle\|V_{t}\|_{L_{\omega}^{1}}\lesssim\big(n^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}+\epsilon}\big)^{p}.

Notice that

defined in (

) and

defined in (

from (

) is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-martingale. Therefore from an appropriate version of Gronwall’s inequality (for an “appropriate version” see e.g
[
24
, Lemma 3.8]
and (
4.18
) and
the claimed bound (

Appendix A
Proof for regularity estimates of PDEs

Proof of
Theorem

4.2
.

As for the existence and uniqueness, it was already shown in
[
16
, Theorem 4.3.1]
.
Therefore it is enough to show (
4.3
). We start with the case that
a
is a constant matrix and first order term of (
4.2
) vanishes.
We stress that all proportionality constants in the relations
\lesssim
below depend on
d,\alpha,\|b\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}},\lambda,
and not on
\theta
.

Case I.

a
is a constant positive definite matrix and
b\equiv 0
.

As explained in
[
17
, Proof of Ch.1, Section 6, 2. Lemma]
, for a general non-degenerate constant matrix
a
, by changing of coordinate it is enough to consider the following resolvent equation

(A.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta u-\theta u=f.

The solution to the above resolvent equation can be represented as

\displaystyle u=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta t}P_{t}f\,\mathrm{d}t.

Firstly, for
\gamma,\alpha>0
, based on the the fact that for
t\geqslant 1

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geqslant 1}\|P_{t}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\lesssim\|P_{1}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}},

and for
t\in(0,1]

\displaystyle\|P_{t}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{\gamma-\alpha}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}},

together with
e^{-\theta t}t^{\iota}\lesssim_{\iota}1
for any
\iota>0
uniformly in
\theta\geqslant 1
,
t>0
, we get for
t\geqslant 0

(A.2)

\displaystyle e^{-\theta t}\|P_{t}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{\gamma-\alpha}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

So we have for
\gamma\in[\alpha,\alpha+2)
and
\theta\geqslant 1

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\leqslant

\displaystyle\big(\int_{0}^{1}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\big)e^{-\theta t}\|P_{t}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\theta t}t^{-\frac{\gamma-\alpha}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\sup_{t\geqslant 1}\|P_{t}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-\theta t}\,\mathrm{d}t

(A.3)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\theta^{\frac{\gamma-(2+\alpha)}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\theta^{-1}e^{-\theta}\lesssim\theta^{\frac{\gamma-(2+\alpha)}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

For
\gamma=\alpha+2
, following the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 5.19]
, for any
\xi>0
, we write
u=:\kappa_{1}^{\xi}+\kappa_{2}^{\xi}=:\int_{0}^{\xi}e^{-\theta t}P_{t}f\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\xi}^{\infty}e^{-\theta t}P_{t}f\,\mathrm{d}t
.
From (
A.2
) we have for any
\nu\in(0,2)

\displaystyle\|\kappa_{1}^{\xi}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\nu+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\xi}t^{-\frac{\nu}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\lesssim\xi^{1-\frac{\nu}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}},

\displaystyle\|\kappa_{2}^{\xi}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2+\nu+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{\infty}_{\xi}t^{-1-\frac{\nu}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\lesssim\xi^{-\frac{\nu}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

By the K-method of interpolation (see e.g.
[
25
, Example 5.15, Proof of Theorem 5.19 (p.149)]
) we can conclude that

(A.4)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2+\alpha}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

Hence by (
A
) and (
A.4
) we can conclude for
\gamma\in[\alpha,\alpha+2]
and
\theta\geqslant 1

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\lesssim\theta^{\frac{\gamma-(2+\alpha)}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

Case II.

a
satisfies
4.1
and
b\equiv 0
.

Here we apply the frozen coefficient method. Let
\xi
be a nonnegative smooth function such that
\xi\equiv 1
near the origin with support in
B_{\delta}=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:|x|\leqslant\delta\right\}
, where
\delta>0
is to be chosen later. Define for
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(A.5)

\displaystyle\xi_{z}(x):=\xi(x-z),\quad a_{z}:=a(z),\quad u_{z}(x):=\xi_{z}(x)u(x),\quad f_{z}(x):=\xi_{z}(x)f(x).

Then we can observe that

\displaystyle a^{ij}_{z}\partial_{ij}u_{z}-\theta u_{z}=g_{z}

where

\displaystyle g_{z}:=

\displaystyle f_{z}-(a^{ij}\partial_{ij}u)\xi_{z}+a^{ij}_{z}\partial_{ij}u_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{z}-(a^{ij}-a^{ij}_{z})\partial_{ij}u\cdot\xi_{z}+a^{ij}_{z}(\partial_{i}u\partial_{j}\xi_{z}+\partial_{j}u\partial_{i}\xi_{z}+u\partial_{ij}\xi_{z}).

We use the fact that
a
satisfies
4.1
then get

\displaystyle\|g_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\|f_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\delta^{\alpha}\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2+\alpha}}+\lambda^{-1}(\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}}\|\partial_{i}\xi_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\|\partial_{ij}\xi_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}})\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\|f_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\delta^{\alpha}\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2+\alpha}}+\lambda^{-1}(\delta^{-(1+\alpha)}\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}}+\delta^{-(2+\alpha)}\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}})\big).

By interpolation, we have that for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
C_{\epsilon}>0
so that

\displaystyle\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}}\leqslant C_{\epsilon}\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\epsilon\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2+\alpha}},

it implies that

(A.6)

\displaystyle\|g_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\lesssim\big(\|f_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+(\delta^{\alpha}+\delta^{-(1+\alpha)}\epsilon)\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2+\alpha}}+(C_{\epsilon}\delta^{-(1+\alpha)}+\delta^{-(2+\alpha)})\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\big).

First by taking
\gamma=2+\alpha
and applying (
A.4
)
then

\displaystyle\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}\|g_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{2}\big(\|f_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+(\delta^{\alpha}+\delta^{-(1+\alpha)}\epsilon)\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2+\alpha}}+(C_{\epsilon}\delta^{-(1+\alpha)}+\delta^{-(2+\alpha)})\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\big)

with some
C_{1},C_{2}\lesssim 1
.
We first fix
\delta
to be small enough and then
\epsilon
to be small enough such that
C_{2}\big(\delta^{\alpha}+\delta^{-(1+\alpha)}\epsilon\big)<\frac{1}{2}
. Then we get

(A.7)

\displaystyle\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2+\alpha}}\lesssim\theta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\big(\|f_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\big).

Plug the above into (
A.6
) then get

(A.8)

\displaystyle\|g_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\lesssim\|f_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

We again use (
A
) by taking
\gamma=\alpha
, together with (
A.8
)

\displaystyle\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\leqslant C_{3}\theta^{-1}\|g_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\leqslant C_{4}\theta^{-1}\big(\|f_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\big),

again here the positive constants
C_{3},C_{4}\lesssim 1
. Take
\theta
to be large so that
C_{4}\theta^{-1}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
then we have

(A.9)

\displaystyle\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\lesssim\|f_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

Combining (
A
), (
A.8
) and (
A.9
) yields

\displaystyle\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\theta^{\frac{\gamma-(2+\alpha)}{2}}\|g_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\lesssim\theta^{\frac{\gamma-(2+\alpha)}{2}}\|f_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

We recall from
[
16
, Lemma 4.1.1]
the following holds (note that
\delta
at this point is already fixed):

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\leqslant C(\gamma,d)\sup_{z}\|u_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}.

On the other hand, by the elementary inequality
|u(x)v(x)-u(y)v(y)|\leqslant|u(x)||v(x)-v(y)|+|v(y)||u(x)-u(y)|
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{z}\|f_{z}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

Therefore, taking supermum over
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
yields

(A.10)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\lesssim\theta^{\frac{\gamma-(2+\alpha)}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

Case III.

a
satisfies
4.1
and
b\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
.

By (
A.10
), for
\theta>\theta_{0}
the following holds

(A.11)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\lesssim\theta^{\frac{\gamma-(2+\alpha)}{2}}\big(\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\|b\cdot\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\big).

By taking
\gamma=1+\alpha
, it is evident that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}}\leqslant

\displaystyle C\theta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\|b\cdot\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\big)\leqslant C\theta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+\|b\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}}\big)

with some
C\lesssim 1
. Now we take
\theta
to be larger enough so that

\displaystyle C\theta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|b\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2},

we denote this

as
\theta^{*}
(depending on

). Therefore for
\theta\geqslant\theta^{*}
, we get from (

) that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\lesssim\theta^{\frac{\gamma-(2+\alpha)}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

We get the desired estimate (
4.3
).

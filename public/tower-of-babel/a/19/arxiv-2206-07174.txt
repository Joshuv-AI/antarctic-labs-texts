Relations between e e and π \pi : Nilakantha’s series and Stirling’s formula
V. Yu. Irkhin

Abstract
Approximate relations between e e and π \pi are reviewed, some new connections being established. Nilakantha’s series expansion for π \pi is transformed to accelerate its convergence. Its comparison with the standard inverse-factorial expansion for e e is performed to demonstrate similarity in several first terms. This comparison clarifies the origin of the approximate coincidence e + 2 ​ π ≈ 9 e+2\pi\approx 9 . Using Stirling’s series enables us to illustrate the relations π 4 + π 5 ≈ e 6 \pi^{4}+\pi^{5}\approx e^{6} and π 9 / e 8 ≈ 10 \pi^{9}/e^{8}\approx 10 . The role of Archimede’s approximation π = 22 / 7 \pi=22/7 is discussed.

1
Nilakantha’s series

First we focus on the simplest relation

e+2\pi=9.001...,

(8)

or
2(\pi-3)\approx 3-e
.
We demonstrate a reason of this coincidence by using the series expansions.
For
e
we use the standard inverse-factorial series which may be represented as

e=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}=3-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{24}+\frac{1}{120}+\frac{1}{720}+...

(9)

and is rapidly convergent.

As for
\pi
, the situation is more complicated. The simple Gregory-Leibniz expansion

\frac{\pi}{4}=1-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{5}-\frac{1}{7}+...,

(10)

converges very slowly.
We apply Nilakantha’s series which can be obtained from (10) after acceleration of the convergence:

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{n+1}+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{4}{2n+1}\right)

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\frac{1}{n(2n+1)(n+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\frac{4}{(2n+1)^{3}-(2n+1)}.

The series (11) was discovered in India already in the 15th century and converges rather rapidly
[
1
]
.

After grouping of pairs of opposite-sign terms, (11) can be transformed as

\displaystyle 2\pi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6+\frac{1}{3}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{3}{n(n+1)(4n+1)(4n+3)}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6+\frac{1}{3}-\frac{3}{70}-\frac{1}{198}-\frac{1}{780}-...

so that the convergence becomes still faster.

One can see that the expansions (9) and (12) demonstrate a close similarity of several first terms, which justifies and clarifies the coincidence (8).

The structure of Nilakantha’s series also reproduces roughly the relation (1). We have from (12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(\pi^{2}+1)\approx\frac{1}{4}\left(3+\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{48}-\frac{1}{200}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\approx 3-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{32}-0.01\approx 3-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{24}

(13)

which corresponds to the expansion of

(9).

On substituting (8) into (2) we have

2
Stirling’s formula

Another way to relate
e
and
\pi
is using Stirling’s series
[
5
]
which gives a good accuracy even for
n=1
:

\displaystyle n!

\displaystyle\simeq{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\left({\frac{n}{e}}\right)^{n}\left(1+{\frac{1}{12n}}+{\frac{1}{288n^{2}}}+\cdots\right)

so that for any
n

e^{n}\simeq{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\frac{n^{n}}{n!}\left(1+{\frac{1}{12n}}+{\frac{1}{288n^{2}}}+\cdots\right)

(19)

Eq.(19) enables one to generate various approximations for
e
and
\pi
. For example, we obtain

e=\frac{e^{n+1}}{e^{n}}=\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n+1/2}\frac{1+\frac{1}{12(n+1)}+\cdots}{1+\frac{1}{12n}+\cdots}.

(20)

For half-integer
n
in (19), using the value of Euler’s Gamma function
\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}
we derive

e^{n+1/2}=\sqrt{2}\frac{(2n+1)^{n+1}}{(2n+1)!!}\left(1+{\frac{1}{6(2n+1)}}+\cdots\right)

(21)

which yields a good approximation even for
n=0
,
e=49/18\approx 2.72
.

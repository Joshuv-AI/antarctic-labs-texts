Moments of primes in progresssions to a large modulus
Sun-Kai Leung

Abstract
. Assuming a uniform variant of the Hardy–Littlewood prime k k -tuple conjecture, we compute moments of the number of primes not exceeding N N in arithmetic progressions to a common large modulus q q as a ⁡ ( mod ​ q ) a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q) varies. As a consequence, depending on the size of φ ⁡ ( q ) \varphi(q) with respect to N N , the prime count exhibits a Gaussian or Poissonian law. An interesting byproduct is that the least prime in arithmetic progressions follows an exponential distribution, where some unexpected discrepancies are observed for smooth q q .



1.
Introduction

The study of primes in arithmetic progressions began with Dirichlet’s celebrated theorem on infinitude of primes in 1837. It states that if
(a,q)=1,
i.e. admissible, then there are infinity many primes
p\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q).
A century later, as an application of Siegel’s theorem on Dirichlet L-functions, Walfisz (1936) successfully gave an estimate of the number of primes up to
N
in admissible arithmetic progressions modulo
q
provided that
q\leqslant(\log N)^{A},
which is now known as the Siegel–Walfisz theorem, i.e. if
(a,q)=1,
then

\displaystyle\psi(N;q,a):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
n\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)=\frac{N}{\varphi(q)}+O\left(N\exp\left(-c_{A}\sqrt{\log N}\right)\right)

as
N\to\infty,
where
\Lambda
is the von Mangoldt function.

Due to our limited understanding of potential Siegel zeros (if they exist), however, primes in arithmetic progressions to large moduli remain a mystery, especially when
\log q\asymp\log N.
In this direction, Linnik (1944) unconditionally proved the existence of absolute constants
c,L>0
such that the least prime in an (admissible) arithmetic progression modulo
q
is
\leqslant cq^{L}.
Nevertheless, an ambitious yet natural question arises: fixing a large modulus
q
, what is the distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions as
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
varies? In particular, one has to compute the variance

\displaystyle V(N;q):=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\left(\psi(N;q,a)-\frac{N}{\varphi(q)}\right)^{2}.

The mean of
V(N;q)
over all moduli
q\leqslant Q
has a long history, and related results are usually referred to as the “Barban–Davenport–Halberstam” type theorems, notably by Hooley, Montgomery and Vaughan in a series of papers. For individual
V(N;q),
however, much less is known. Hooley
[
Hoo75
]
conjectured that in some ranges of
q\leqslant N,
we have
V(N;q)\sim N\log q
as
N\to\infty.
Assuming the Riemann hypothesis and a uniform Hardy–Littlewood conjecture on prime pairs, Friedlander and Goldston
[
FG96
]
showed that the asymptotic holds for
q\in[N^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon},N].
Recently, Fiorilli
[
Fio15
]
and La Bretèche–Fiorilli
[
BF23
]
have respectively studied variance and moments of primes in arithmetic progressions from a Fourier perspective (under the generalized Riemann hypothesis and the linear independence conjecture).

In this paper, we revisit the “physical side” . Adapting the method of Montgomery–Soundararajan
[
MS04
]
, we compute the moments of primes in arithmetic progressions conditioning on a uniform variant of the prime
k
-tuple conjecture.

Using a modified Cramér model (see
[
Gra95
, pp.23-24]
), we propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(A uniform variant of the Hardy–Littlewood prime
k
-tuple conjecture)
.

Let
k,q,N,D\geqslant 1,
and
\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,D]
be a set consisting of
k
distinct multiples of
q
. Then there exists an absolute constant
0<\delta<1/2
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\Lambda(n+d)=\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{D};q)\left|\{1\leqslant n\leqslant N\,:\,(n,q)=1\}\right|+O_{k,\delta}((N+D)^{1-\delta}),

or equivalently (by partial summation) that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{d\in\mathcal{D}}1_{\mathcal{P}}(n+d)=\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{D};q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\log(n+d)\right)^{-1}+O_{k,\delta}((N+D)^{1-\delta}),

where
1_{\mathcal{P}}
is the prime indicator function, and
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{D};q)
is the singular series
defined as the Euler product

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\prod_{p\nmid q}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{D})}{p}\right)

with
v_{p}(\mathcal{H}):=|\{d\in\mathcal{D}\mod{p}\}|.

Note that
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{D};q)=\left(\varphi(q)/q\right)^{-1}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{D}),
where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{D}):=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{D})}{p}\right)

is the classical singular series.

Assuming the uniform variant of the prime
k
-tuple conjecture, we compute the moments of weighted prime counts in arithmetic progressions to a large modulus
q,
provided that
\varphi(q)\in[N^{1-\epsilon},\epsilon\frac{N}{\log N}],

which can be regarded as the progression counterpart of
[
MS04
, Theorem 3]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume Conjecture
1.1
. Let
N,q\geqslant 2,K\geqslant 0
be integers. Suppose the number of distinct prime factors
\omega(q)\leqslant(\log\log q)^{2}
and
N^{1-2\delta/(K+3)}\leqslant\varphi(q)\leqslant\frac{N}{\log N}
as
N\to\infty.
Then

\displaystyle M_{K}(N;q):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N/\varphi(q)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
n\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}\left(\Lambda(n)-\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\right)\right)^{K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{K}\times\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\left(\frac{1}{N/q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
n\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}\left(\log\frac{qn}{N}+B_{q}\right)\right)^{K/2}

\displaystyle+O_{K,\delta}\left(\left(\log N\right)^{\frac{K}{2}}\left(\frac{N}{\varphi(q)\log N}\right)^{-\frac{1}{8K}}+\left(\frac{N}{\varphi(q)}\right)^{\frac{K}{2}+1}(\log N)^{K}N^{-\delta}\right),

where
B_{q}:=1-\gamma-\log 2\pi-\sum_{p\mid q}\frac{\log p}{p-1}.

As the Erdős–Kac theorem (see
[
Kou19
, Theorem 15.1]
) says the number of distinct prime factors
w(q)
is approximately normal with mean and variance
\log\log q,
most moduli
q
’s have at most
(\log\log q)^{2}
prime factors. For instance, the Chernoff bound gives

\displaystyle|\{q\leqslant x\,:\,w(q)>(\log\log q)^{2}\}|\leqslant x(\log\log x)^{-(1-o(1))(\log\log x)^{2}}.

as
x\to\infty.
Therefore, Theorem
1.1
holds for almost all moduli
q\geqslant 2
provided that
N^{1-2\delta/(K+3)}\leqslant\varphi(q)\leqslant\frac{N}{\log N}.

Applying the method of moments,
the Gaussian law follows readily from Theorem
1.1
and an estimate in Section
5
.

Corollary 1.1
.

Assume Conjecture
1.1
. Suppose
\omega(q)\leqslant(\log\log q)^{2}
and

N^{1-\epsilon}\leqslant\varphi(q)\leqslant\epsilon\frac{N}{\log N}
for any
\epsilon>0.
If a reduced residue
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
is chosen uniformly at random, then as
N\to\infty
, we have the convergence in distribution to a standard Gaussian

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{q}\sqrt{N/\varphi(q)}}\left(\psi(N;q,a)-\frac{N}{\varphi(q)}\right)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,1),

where
\sigma_{q}^{2}:=\log\frac{q}{2\pi}-\gamma-\sum_{p\mid q}\frac{\log p}{p-1},
i.e. for any fixed
\alpha<\beta,
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\left|\left\{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\,:\,\frac{1}{\sigma_{q}\sqrt{N/\varphi(q)}}\left(\psi(N;q,a)-\frac{N}{\varphi(q)}\right)\in(\alpha,\beta]\right\}\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}dx.

Remark 1.1
.

Our corollary is complementary to a recent conjecture by
La Bretèche and Fiorilli

[
BF23
, Conjecture 1.9]

that in the range of
q\in[(\log\log N)^{1+\epsilon},N^{1-\epsilon}],
if
V\in\mathbb{R}
is fixed, then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\left|\left\{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\,:\,\left(\frac{N\log q}{\varphi(q)}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\psi(N;q,a)-\frac{\psi(N,\chi_{0,q})}{\varphi(q)}\right)>V\right\}\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{V}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}dx,

where
\psi(N,\chi_{0,q}):=\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant N}\Lambda(n)\chi_{0,q}(n)
with
\chi_{0,q}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
being the principal character.

Similarly, we also compute moments of the number of primes in arithmetic progressions to a large modulus
q,
provided that
\varphi(q)\asymp\frac{N}{\log N}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume Conjecture
1.1
. Let
k\geqslant 1
be an integer. Suppose
\varphi(q)\asymp\frac{N}{\log N}.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\pi(N;q,a)^{k}=\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log\log N}{\log N}\right)\right)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}\left(\frac{N}{\varphi(q)\log N}\right)^{j}

as
N\to\infty,
where
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}
is the Stirling number of the second kind, i.e. the number of ways to partition a set of
k
objects into
j
non-empty subsets.
The implied constant depends on
k
and the ratio
\varphi(q)/\frac{N}{\log N}
only.

Applying the method of moments, the Poissonian law follows readily from Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume Conjecture
1.1
. Let
k\geqslant 0
be a fixed integer. Suppose
\varphi(q)=(1+o(1))\frac{N}{\lambda\log N}
for some fixed
\lambda>0
as
N\to\infty.

Then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\left|\left\{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\,:\,\pi(N;q,a)=k\right\}\right|=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}.

How big is the first prime in a given arithmetic progression?
Let
p(q,a)
denote the least prime in the arithmetic progression
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q).
Then Linnik’s theorem states that there exists an absolute constant
L>0
such that
p(q,a)\ll q^{L}.
In fact, Xylouris
[
Xyl11
]
proved relatively recently that one can take
L=5.
However, we believe this is far from being the truth and Heath-Brown
[
Hea78
]
conjectured that
p(q,a)\ll\varphi(q)\log^{2}q.

Nonetheless, assuming the generalized Riemann hypothesis, Turán
[
Tur39
]
proved that
p(q,a)\ll_{\epsilon}\varphi(q)\log^{2+\epsilon}q
except possibly for
o(\varphi(q))
arithmetic progressions. Here, we show that as soon as
f(q)\to\infty
with
q\to\infty,
the least prime
p(q,a)
is
\leqslant f(q)\varphi(q)\log q
for almost all
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
under the uniform prime
k
-tuple conjecture. In fact, as an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
, the least prime in arithmetic progressions to a large modulus has an exponential limiting distribution.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assume Conjecture
1.1
. Let
t>0
be a fixed real number. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\left|\{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\,:\,p(q,a)\leqslant t\varphi(q)\log q\}\right|=1-e^{-t}.

In particular,
as
q\to\infty
, the mean of
p(q,a)
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}p(q,a)=(1+o(1))\varphi(q)\log q.

Remark 1.2
.

Erdős
[
Erd49
, Theorem 2]
proved unconditionally that for each fixed
t>0,
we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{q\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\left|\{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\,:\,p(q,a)\leqslant t\varphi(q)\log q\}\right|>0.

These results can be regarded as the progression counterparts of the work by Gallagher
[
Gal76
]
. See also
[
Gra89
]
in which
a
is fixed but
q
varies instead.

Finally, in Section
7
, we examine some statistics on the least primes in arithmetic progressions. The unexpected discrepancies observed for smooth moduli is explained in Section
8
.

2.
Reduced residues in a progression to a large modulus

Before embarking on the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we first compute the moments of the number of reduced residues in an arithmetic progression to a large modulus. To lighten the notation, from now on, all implied constants will depend on
K,k,q,\epsilon,\delta.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given integers
k\geqslant 0,Q\geqslant 1.
Let
q\geqslant 2
be an integer such that
(q,Q)=1,
and
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
be a reduced residue, i.e.
(a,q)=1.
Suppose
N\geqslant 2q
. Then

\displaystyle V_{k}(Q;q,a):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{m\ (\mathrm{mod}\ Q)}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
n\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(Q)}{Q}\right)^{-1}1_{(n+m,Q)=1}-1\right)\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1<r_{i}\mid Q}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}/r_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right),

where

\displaystyle E_{q,a}\left(\alpha\right):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
n\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}e\left(n\alpha\right).

Proof.

Let
c_{Q}(A)
denote the Ramanujan sum
\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{B\ (\mathrm{mod}\ Q)}e\left(\frac{AB}{Q}\right).
Then

\displaystyle 1_{(n,Q)=1}=\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{A\ (\mathrm{mod}\ Q)}c_{Q}(A)e\left(\frac{An}{Q}\right)

so that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
n\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(Q)}{Q}\right)^{-1}1_{(n+m,Q)=1}-1\right)=\frac{1}{\varphi(Q)}\sum_{A\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ Q)}c_{Q}(A)e\left(\frac{Am}{Q}\right)E_{q,a}\left(\frac{A}{Q}\right).

Recall the well-known identity (see
[
IK04
, p.44]
)

\displaystyle c_{Q}(A)=\frac{\mu(Q/(A,Q))}{\varphi(Q/(A,Q))}\varphi(Q).

Let
r=Q/(A,Q).
Then (
2.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{1<r|Q}\frac{\mu(r)}{\varphi(r)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)}e\left(\frac{bn}{r}\right)E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b}{r}\right).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle V_{k}(Q;q,a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{m\ (\mathrm{mod}\ Q)}\left(\sum_{1<r|Q}\frac{\mu(r)}{\varphi(r)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)}e\left(\frac{bm}{r}\right)E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b}{r}\right)\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1<r_{i}|Q}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})}\prod_{i=1}^{k}E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{m\ (\mathrm{mod}\ Q)}e\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)m\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1<r_{i}\mid Q}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}/r_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)

as desired.
∎

Using the lemma, we compute the moments of reduced residues in an arithmetic progression to a large modulus. This is essentially
[
Kup23
, Theorem 1.1]
with
c_{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv c_{k}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)
(in Kuperberg’s notation), except our error term depends on the modulus
q
explicitly, which is crucial to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Given integers
k\geqslant 0,Q\geqslant 1.
Let
q\geqslant 2
be an integer such that
(q,Q)=1,
and
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
be a reduced residue. Suppose
N\geqslant 2q
. Then

\displaystyle V_{k}(Q;q,a)=\mu_{k}V_{2}\left(Q;q,a\right)^{k/2}+O\left(\left(\frac{N}{q}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{7k}}\left(\frac{\varphi(Q)}{Q}\right)^{-2^{k}-\frac{k}{2}}\right).

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
MS04
, Theorem 1]
.
Without loss of generality, assume
1\leqslant a<q.
Let
1<r\mid Q
and
b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)
be a reduced residue. Then by definition, we have

\displaystyle E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
n\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{bn}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leqslant h\leqslant\frac{N-a}{q}}e\left(\frac{b}{r}(qh+a)\right)

\displaystyle=e\left(\frac{ba}{r}\right)\sum_{0\leqslant h\leqslant\frac{N-a}{q}}e\left(\frac{bqh}{r}\right).

Since by assumption
(q,r)=(b,r)=1,
we also have
(bq,r)=1.
Let
c\equiv bq\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)
be a reduced residue so that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left|E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b}{r}\right)\right|\leqslant F_{q}\left(\frac{c}{r}\right):=\min\left\{\frac{N}{q}+1,\left\|\frac{c}{r}\right\|^{-1}\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{k}
denote the set of
\boldsymbol{r}=(r_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant k}
for which the
r_{i}
’s are equal in pairs, and with no further equalities among them.
Then, one can argue as in the proof of
[
MV86
, Lemma 8]
that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r_{i}\mid Q\\
\boldsymbol{r}\notin\mathcal{D}_{k}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu^{2}(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}/r_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left|E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)\right|\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r_{i}\mid Q\\
\boldsymbol{r}\notin\mathcal{D}_{k}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu^{2}(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}/r_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}F_{q}\left(\frac{c_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\left(\frac{N}{q}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{7k}}\left(\frac{\varphi(Q)}{Q}\right)^{-2^{k}-\frac{k}{2}}.

Appealing to Lemma
2.1
, it remains to estimate

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r_{i}\mid Q\\
\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathcal{D}_{k}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}/r_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right).

We can assume
k
is even, for otherwise
\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
is empty. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{k}:=\{\sigma=\{\{i,j\}\}\,:1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant k,|\sigma|=k/2\}

denote the set of perfect matching of
\{1,\ldots,k\}.
Then, by definition (
2.3
) is

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r_{\tau_{\sigma}}\mid Q\\
\tau_{\sigma}=\{i,j\}\in\sigma\\
r_{\tau_{\sigma}}\text{ distinct}\end{subarray}}\prod_{\tau_{\sigma}\in\sigma}\frac{\mu^{2}(r_{\tau_{\sigma}})}{\varphi^{2}(r_{\tau_{\sigma}})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{\tau_{\sigma}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{\tau_{\sigma}})\\
\sum_{\tau_{\sigma}}c_{\tau_{\sigma}}/r_{\tau_{\sigma}}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}\prod_{\tau_{\sigma}\in\sigma}J(c_{\tau_{\sigma}},r_{\tau_{\sigma}}),

where

\displaystyle J(c,r):=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)\\
(c-b,q)=1\end{subarray}}E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b}{r}\right)E_{q,a}\left(\frac{c-b}{q}\right).

As in the proof of
[
MS04
, Theorem 1]
, the distinctness condition among the
r_{\tau_{\sigma}}
’s can be dropped, since the error induced is absorbed. Besides, for each
\sigma\in\mathcal{B},
the main contribution comes from
c_{\tau_{\sigma}}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q_{\tau_{\sigma}})
for all
\tau_{\sigma},
and the rest is also negligible. In this case, note that

\displaystyle J(0,r)=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)}\left|E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b}{r}\right)\right|^{2}.

Therefore, we are left with

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r_{\tau_{\sigma}}\mid Q\\
\tau_{\sigma}=\{i,j\}\in\sigma\end{subarray}}\prod_{\tau_{\sigma}\in\sigma}\frac{\mu^{2}(q_{\tau_{\sigma}})}{\varphi^{2}(q_{\tau_{\sigma}})}\prod_{\tau_{\sigma}\in\sigma}J_{i_{1},i_{2}}(0,q_{\tau_{\sigma}}),

which is by Lemma
2.1

\displaystyle\mu_{k}V_{2}\left(Q;q,a\right)^{k/2},

and hence the proposition follows.

∎

Therefore, it remains to compute
V_{2}(Q;q,a).
See
[
Kup23
, Lemma 3.2]
for an estimate without uniformity in the modulus
q
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q\geqslant 2
be a modulus satisfying the conditions in Theorem
1.1
.
Suppose
Q
is divisible by any prime
p\leqslant\left(N/q\right)^{2}
which is coprime to
q.
Then
for any
\epsilon>0,
we have

\displaystyle V_{2}(Q;q,a)=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-1}\sum_{r\mid Q}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)}-\log\left(\frac{N}{q}\right)+B_{q}\right)H+O\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\times\left(\frac{N}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\right),

where
H=d_{N;q,a}:=|\{1\leqslant n\leqslant N\,:\,n\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\}|
and
B_{q}:=1-\gamma-\log 2\pi-\sum_{p\mid q}\frac{\log p}{p-1}.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\left|E_{q,a}(\alpha)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
n\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}e(n\alpha)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n_{1},n_{2}\leqslant N\\
n_{1}\equiv n_{2}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}e((n_{1}-n_{2})\alpha).

Let
m=(n_{1}-n_{2})/q.
Then this becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leqslant|m|\leqslant H}\left(H-|m|\right)e\left(qm\alpha\right).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)}\left|E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b}{r}\right)\right|^{2}=\sum_{0\leqslant|m|\leqslant H}\left(H-|m|\right)c_{r}(qm),

where
c_{r}(qm)
is Ramanujan’s sum. Since
c_{r}(0)=1
and
c_{r}(-qm)=c_{r}(qm),
this is

\displaystyle\varphi(r)H+2\sum_{1\leqslant m\leqslant H}\left(H-m\right)c_{r}(qm)

so that by Lemma
2.1

(2.4)

\displaystyle V_{2}(Q;q,a)=H\sum_{1<r\mid Q}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)}+2\sum_{1\leqslant m\leqslant H}\left(H-m\right)\sum_{1<r\mid Q}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi^{2}(r)}c_{r}(qm).

Using the multiplicity of Ramanujan’s sum in
r
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r\mid Q}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi^{2}(r)}c_{r}(qm)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid Q\\
p\mid qm\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid Q\\
p\nmid qm\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid qQ\\
p\mid qm\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid qQ\\
p\nmid qm\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}\right).

Since by assumption
qQ
is divisible by any prime
p\leqslant(N/q)^{2},
this is

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid qm\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\nmid qm\\
\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-1}H^{-2}\right).

Here the main term is

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid qm\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\nmid qm\\
\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-2}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\times\mathfrak{S}(qm),

where
\mathfrak{S}(qm):=\mathfrak{S}(\{0,qm\})
for convenience.
Appealing to
[
FG96
, Proposition 3]
, we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\times 2\sum_{1\leqslant m\leqslant H}(H-m)\mathfrak{S}(qm)=H^{2}+\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\left(-H\log H+B_{q}H+I_{\delta}(H,q)\right)

for any
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2},
where
I_{\delta}(H,q)
is an integral

\displaystyle\ll\mathfrak{S}(2q)H^{1-\delta}\delta^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\delta\right)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\prod_{p\mid q}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{1-\delta}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{2(1-\delta)}}\right),

which is

\displaystyle\ll\log\log q\times H^{1-\delta}\delta^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\delta\right)^{-\frac{5}{2}}\prod_{p\mid q}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{1-\delta}}\right)

(see
[
FG96
, p. 322]
). Let
\delta=\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon
for some fixed small
\epsilon>0.
Then, since by assumption
q
has
\leqslant(\log\log q)^{2}
prime factors, i.e.
w(q)\leqslant(\log\log q)^{2},
the last product is

\displaystyle\ll\exp\left(\sum_{p\leqslant 10w(q)\log(2w(q))}\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}\right)\ll

\displaystyle\exp\left(100w(q)^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\exp\left(100(\log\log q)^{1-\epsilon}\right).

Also, since by assumption
H\gg\frac{N}{q}\geqslant\log q,
the integral
I_{\delta}(H,q)
is
\ll H^{\frac{1}{2}+3\epsilon}
so that (
2.7
) becomes

\displaystyle H^{2}+\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\left(-H\log H+B_{q}H+H^{\frac{1}{2}+3\epsilon}\right).

Therefore, combining with (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), the expression (
2.4
) is

\displaystyle H\left(\sum_{r\mid Q}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)}-1\right)+2\sum_{1\leqslant m\leqslant H}\left(H-m\right)\left(\sum_{r\mid Q}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi^{2}(r)}c_{r}(qm)-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\left(\sum_{r\mid Q}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)}\right)-H+H^{2}+\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\left(-H\log H+B_{q}H+H^{\frac{1}{2}+3\epsilon}\right)-H(H-1)+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-1}\sum_{r\mid Q}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)}-\log H+B_{q}\right)H+O\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\ \times H^{\frac{1}{2}+3\epsilon}\right)+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-1}\right).

Since
H\geqslant\log q
and
\varphi(q)\gg q/\log\log q,
the second error term is absorbed and the lemma follows.

∎

Remark 2.1
.

In fact, for any modulus
q\geqslant 2,
we always have
I_{\delta}(H,q)\ll H(\log\log q)^{3}
if
\delta=\frac{1}{\log\log q}
(see
[
FG96
, p. 322]
), so that

\displaystyle V_{2}(Q;q,a)=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-1}\sum_{r\mid Q}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)}-\log\frac{N}{q}+B_{q}\right)H+O\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\log\log q\times\frac{N}{q}\right).

Moreover, since

B_{q}\ll\sum_{p\mid q}\frac{\log p}{p-1}\ll\log\log q,

we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle V_{2}(Q;q,a)=H\sum_{r\mid Q}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)}+O\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\times\frac{N}{q}\log\frac{N}{q}\right).

3.
Properties of the singular series along progressions

Given
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\}
and
q\geqslant 1,
let
\mathfrak{S}_{0}\left(\mathcal{D};q\right)
denote the normalized singular series

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{i}>1\\
(r_{i},q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}/r_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{b_{i}d_{i}}{r_{i}}\right).

We are interested in such singular series due to the followng lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume Conjecture
1.1
. Let
\Lambda_{0}(n;q):=\Lambda(n)-\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}
for
n\geqslant 1,
and
\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,D]
be a set consisting of
k
distinct multiples of
q
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{0}(n+d;q)=\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D};q)\left|\{1\leqslant n\leqslant N\,:\,(n,q)=1\}\right|+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}(N+D)^{1-\delta}\right).

Proof.

Appealing to
[
MS04
, Lemma 3]
gives

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{D};q)=\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{i}\geqslant 1\\
(r_{i},q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}/r_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{b_{i}d_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)

so that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D};q)=\sum_{\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{D}}\left(-\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\right)^{k-|\mathcal{J}|}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J};q).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{0}(n+d;q)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\left(\Lambda(n+d;q)-\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{D}}\left(-\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\right)^{k-|\mathcal{J}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{d\in\mathcal{J}}\Lambda(n+d;q).

Assuming Conjecture
1.1
, this is

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{D}}\left(-\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\right)^{k-|\mathcal{J}|}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J};q)\times\left|\{1\leqslant n\leqslant N\,:\,(n,q)=1\}\right|+O\left(\left(\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\right)^{k}(N+D)^{1-\delta}\right).

Then, the lemma follows from (
3.1
).

∎

We also need a lemma on the mean of the singular series along an arithmetic progression. This is essentially
[
Kup23
, Theorem 1.2]
with
c_{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv c_{k}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)
(in Kuperberg’s notation), except our error term depends on the modulus explicitly, which is crucial to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given an integer
k\geqslant 0
and a modulus
q\geqslant 2
satisfying the conditions in Theorem
1.1
.
Let
R_{k}(N;q,a)
denote the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n_{i}\leqslant N\\
n_{i}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
n_{i}\text{ distinct}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}\left(\mathcal{N};q\right),

where
\mathcal{N}:=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\}.

Then as
N\to\infty,
we have

\displaystyle R_{k}(N;q,a)=\mu_{k}\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-1}H\left(-\log\frac{N}{q}+A_{q}\right)\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}+O\left(\left(\frac{N}{q}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{7k}+\epsilon}\right),

where

H=d_{N;q,a}:=|\{1\leqslant n\leqslant N\,:\,n\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\}|
and

\displaystyle A_{q}:=B_{q}+\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}=1-\gamma-\log 2\pi-\sum_{p\mid q}\frac{\log p}{p-1}+\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
MS04
, Theorem 2]
. Let
y\geqslant N/q.
Then by definition

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}\left(\mathcal{N};q\right)=\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant y\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{N})}{p}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>y\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{N})}{p}\right).

Since
n_{i}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
for any
i
and are distinct, we must have
v_{p}(\mathcal{N})=k
if
p>y
and
p\nmid q,
so that the last product is

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>y\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{N})}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>y\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=1+O\left(\frac{1}{y\log y}\right).

Also, since the first product is
\ll(\log y)^{k-1},
we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{N};q)\ll(\varphi(q)/q)^{-k}(\log y)^{k-1},

or more precisely

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant y\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{N})}{p}\right)+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\times\frac{(\log y)^{k-2}}{y}\right),

which is by
[
MS04
, Lemma 3]

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{i}\geqslant 1\\
(r_{i},q)=1\\
p\mid r\implies p\leqslant y\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}/r_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{b_{i}n_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\times\frac{(\log y)^{k-2}}{y}\right).

Let
y=(N/q)^{k+1}
and
Q=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant y,p\nmid q\end{subarray}}p.
Then

\displaystyle R_{k}(N;q,a)=\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r_{i}\mid Q\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}S(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k};q,a)+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\right),

where

\displaystyle S(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k};q,a):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n_{i}\leqslant N\\
n_{i}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
n_{i}\text{ distinct}\end{subarray}}\,\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}/r_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{b_{i}n_{i}}{r_{i}}\right).

To apply Proposition
2.1
, let us
remove the distinctness condition on the
n_{i}
’s. Let us denote

\displaystyle\delta_{i,j}:=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $i\neq j,$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant k}\left(1-\delta_{i,j}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{{if $d_{i}$'s are distinct,} }\\
0&\mbox{{otherwise. }}\end{cases}

When the left-hand side above is expanded, we obtain a linear combination of products
of the
\delta
symbols. Let
\Delta
denote such a product, and
|\Delta|
the number of factors in the
product. As in the proof of
[
MS04
, Theorem 2]
, we define an equivalence relation on these
\delta
-products by setting
\Delta_{1}\sim\Delta_{2}
if

\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
have the same value for all choices of
d_{i}
’s. Given a partition
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{M}\}
of the set

let

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}:=\prod_{m=1}^{M}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
i,j\in\mathcal{S}_{m}\end{subarray}}\delta_{i,j}.

Note that every equivalence class of
\delta
-products contains a unique
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
. Thus, we
have a bijective correspondence between equivalence classes of
\delta
-products and partitions
of
\{1,\ldots,k\}.
For a partition
\mathcal{P}
, denote

\displaystyle w(\mathcal{P}):=\sum_{\Delta\sim\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}}(-1)^{|\Delta|}

so that

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant k}(1-\delta_{i,j})=\sum_{\mathcal{P}}w(\mathcal{P})\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle S(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k};q,a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{M}\}\end{subarray}}w(\mathcal{P})\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}/r_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\end{subarray}}^{M}E_{q,a}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right).

As in
[
MS04
]
, using (
2.2
) and the trivial bound

\displaystyle\left|E_{q,a}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)\right|\leqslant\frac{N}{q}+1,

the contribution to
R_{k}(N;q,a)
from terms where
|S_{m}|>2
for some
m
is

\ll(\varphi(q)/q)^{-k}\left(N/q\right)^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\epsilon}
so that

\displaystyle R_{k}(N;q,a)=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{M}\}\\
|S_{m}|\leqslant 2\end{subarray}}w(\mathcal{P})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r_{i}\mid Q\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}/r_{i}\equiv\,0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=1}^{M}E_{q,a}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\left(\frac{N}{q}\right)^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\epsilon}\right).

Suppose that the partition
\mathcal{P}
consists of
l
doubleton sets
\mathcal{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{l}
and
k-2l
singleton sets
\mathcal{S}_{l+1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{k-l}
.
Since no other
\delta
-product is equivalent to
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
, and
|\mathcal{P}|=l
, so
w(\mathcal{P})=(-1)^{l}.
Therefore, the term in
R_{k}(N;q,a)
corresponding to
such partition
\mathcal{P}
is

(3.4)

\displaystyle(-1)^{l}\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r_{i}\mid Q\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(r_{i})}{\varphi(r_{i})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i})\\
\sum_{(i,j_{i})}b_{i}/r_{i}\equiv\,0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=1}^{l}E_{q,a}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)\prod_{m=l+1}^{k-l}E_{q,a}\left(\frac{b_{\mathcal{S}_{m}}}{q_{\mathcal{S}_{m}}}\right),

where we are slightly abusing notation in the final product by identifying the singletons

[
Kup23
]
.

For
1\leqslant m\leqslant l,
let
c_{m}
and

be defined by the relations

\displaystyle\frac{c_{m}}{t_{m}}\equiv\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}},\qquad 1\leqslant c_{m}\leqslant t_{m},\qquad(c_{m},t_{m})=1,

and denote

\displaystyle G\left(\frac{c}{t}\right):=E_{q,a}\left(\frac{c}{t}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r_{1},r_{2}\mid Q\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(r_{1})\mu(r_{2})}{\varphi(r_{1})\varphi(r_{2})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r_{i}),i=1,2\\
\frac{d_{1}}{r_{1}}+\frac{d_{2}}{r_{2}}\equiv\,\frac{c}{t}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\end{subarray}}1.

Then, (
3.4
) becomes

\displaystyle(-1)^{l}\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{m}\mid Q\\
(t_{m},q)=1\\
1\leqslant m\leqslant l\end{subarray}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{m}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ t_{m})\\
1\leqslant m\leqslant l\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\end{subarray}}^{l}G\left(\frac{c_{m}}{t_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r_{m}\mid Q\\

As in

]

and the contribution of the remaining terms is again

Also, since

\displaystyle G(1)=H\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r\mid Q\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)},

where

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\left(-H\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r\mid Q\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)}\right)^{l}V_{k-2l}(Q;q,a)+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\left(\frac{N}{q}\right)^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\left(\frac{N}{q}\right)^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant l\leqslant k/2\end{subarray}}{k\choose 2l}\mu_{l}\times\left(-H\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r\mid Q\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)}\right)^{l}V_{k-2l}(Q;q,a)+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\left(\frac{N}{q}\right)^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\epsilon}\right).

Appealing to Proposition

, this is

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant l\leqslant k/2\end{subarray}}\mu_{2l}\mu_{k-2l}{k\choose 2l}\times\left(-H\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<r\mid Q\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)}\right)^{l}V_{2}(Q;q,a)^{\frac{k}{2}-l}

\displaystyle+O\left(\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\left(\frac{N}{q}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{7k}}\left(\frac{\varphi(Q)}{Q}\right)^{-2^{k}-\frac{k}{2}}\right).

Since by assumption
N/q\geqslant\log q
and
Q=\prod_{p\leqslant y,p\nmid q}p,

the error term is
\ll\left(N/q\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{7k}+\epsilon}.

Also, since

8.
Unexpected discrepancies

Based on the last two figures, one may ask what accounts for the substantial discrepancies when
q
is smooth?

Let
x=t\varphi(q)\log q.
Then for any
x\leqslant q\left(\text{or }t\leqslant\frac{q}{\varphi(q)\log q}\right)
, every prime
\leqslant x
must be the least prime in some progressions, so that the proportion of reduced residues
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
for which
p(q,a)\leqslant x=t\varphi(q)\log q
is

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)}\sim\left(1+\frac{1}{\log q}\right)t.

However, for any
q<x\leqslant 2q\left(\text{or }\frac{q}{\varphi(q)\log q}<t\leqslant\frac{2q}{\varphi(q)\log q}\right)
, this is no longer true and under a uniform Hardy–Littlewood conjecture on prime pairs, the proportion of reduced residues becomes

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}(\pi(x)-\pi_{2}(x-q;q))

\displaystyle\sim\left(1+\frac{1-\mathfrak{S}(q)}{\log q}\right)\left(t-\frac{q}{\varphi(q)\log q}\right)+\frac{q}{\varphi(q)\log q}\left(1+\frac{1}{\log q}\right),

where
\pi_{2}(x-q;q):=|\{p\leqslant x-q\,:\,p,p+q\text{ are primes}\}|.
Similarly, transitions occur at every multiples of
\frac{q}{\varphi(q)\log q}
, which are noticeable if
q
is smooth (and not sufficiently large). When
q=30030.
this quantity is
\approx 0.50568
, which is much greater than the bin width
0.1
.

Nonetheless, since
\frac{q}{\varphi(q)\log q}\ll\frac{\log\log q}{\log q}\to 0
as
q\to\infty,
albeit very slowly, it will be negligible compared with any fixed bin width eventually.

A globally convergent fast iterative shrinkage-thresholding algorithm with a new momentum factor for single and multi-objective convex optimization
Hiroki Tanabe tanabehiroki@amp.i.kyoto-u.ac.jp

Abstract
Convex-composite optimization, which minimizes an objective function represented by the sum of a differentiable function and a convex one, is widely used in machine learning and signal/image processing. Fast Iterative Shrinkage Thresholding Algorithm (FISTA) is a typical method for solving this problem and has a global convergence rate of O ⁡ ( 1 / k 2 ) O(1/k^{2}) . Recently, this has been extended to multi-objective optimization, together with the proof of the O ⁡ ( 1 / k 2 ) O(1/k^{2}) global convergence rate. However, its momentum factor is classical, and the convergence of its iterates has not been proven. In this work, introducing some additional hyperparameters ( a , b ) (a,b) , we propose another accelerated proximal gradient method with a general momentum factor, which is new even for the single-objective cases. We show that our proposed method also has a global convergence rate of O ⁡ ( 1 / k 2 ) O(1/k^{2}) for any ( a , b ) (a,b) , and further that the generated sequence of iterates converges to a weak Pareto solution when a a is positive, an essential property for the finite-time manifold identification. Moreover, we report numerical results with various ( a , b ) (a,b) , showing that some of these choices give better results than the classical momentum factors.

Introduction

We consider the following convex-composite single (
m=1
) or multi-objective (
m\geq 2
) optimization problem:

\min_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\quad F(x),

(2)

where
F\colon\mathbf{R}^{n}\to(\mathbf{R}\cup\left\{\infty\right\})^{m}
is a vector-valued function with
F\coloneqq(F_{1},\dots,F_{m})^{\top}\hskip-1.0pt
.
We assume that each component
F_{i}\colon\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
is given by

F_{i}(x)\coloneqq f_{i}(x)+g_{i}(x)\quad\text{for all~$i=1,\dots,m$}

(3)

with convex and continuously differentiable functions
f_{i}\colon\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R},i=1,\dots,m
and closed, proper and convex functions
g_{i}\colon\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}\cup\left\{\infty\right\},i=1,\dots,m
, and each
\nabla f_{i}
is Lipschitz continuous.
As suggested in
Tanabe et al. 2019
, this problem involves many important classes.
For example, it can express a convex-constrained problem if each
g_{i}
is the indicator function of a convex set
S
, i.e.,

\indicator_{S}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }x\in S,\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4)

Multi-objective optimization has many applications in engineering
(
Eschenauer et al. 1990
)
, statistics
(
Carrizosa and Frenk 1998
)
, and machine learning (particularly multi-task learning
(
Sener 2018
;
Lin et al. 2019
)
and neural architecture search
(
Kim et al. 2017
;
Dong et al. 2018
;
Elsken et al. 2019
)
).
In the multi-objective case, no single point minimizes all objective functions simultaneously in general.
Therefore, we use the concept of
Pareto optimality
.
We call a point weakly Pareto optimal if there is no other point where the objective function values are strictly smaller.
This generalizes the usual optimality for single-objective problems.
In other words, single-objective problems are considered to be included in multi-objective ones.
Hence, in the following, unless otherwise noted, we refer to
Eq.

2
as multi-objective, including the case where
m=1
.

One of the main strategies for multi-objective problems is the
scalarization approach

(
Gass and Saaty 1955
;
Geoffrion 1968
;
Zadeh 1963
)
, which reduces the original multi-objective problem into a parameterized (or weighted) scalar-valued problem.
However, it requires an
a priori
parameters (or weights) selection, which might be challenging.
The meta-heuristics
(
Gandibleux et al. 2004
)
is also popular, but it has no theoretical convergence properties under reasonable assumptions.

Many descent methods have been developed in recent years
(
Fukuda and Graña Drummond 2014
)
, overcoming those drawbacks.
They decrease all objective values simultaneously at each iteration, and their global convergence property can be analyzed under reasonable assumptions.
For example, the steepest descent method
(
Fliege and Svaiter 2000
;
Fliege et al. 2019
;
Désidéri 2012
)
converges globally to Pareto solutions for differentiable multi-objective problems.
From a practical point of view, its applicability has also been reported in multi-task learning
(
Sener 2018
;
Lin et al. 2019
)
.
Afterwards, the projected gradient
(
Fukuda and Graña Drummond 2013
)
, Newton’s
(
Fliege et al. 2009
;
Gonçalves et al. 2021
)
, trust-region
(
Carrizo et al. 2016
)
, and conjugate gradient methods
(
Lucambio Pérez and Prudente 2018
)
were also considered.
Moreover, the proximal point
(
Bonnel et al. 2005
)
and the inertial forward-backward methods
(
Boţ and Grad 2018
)
can solve infinite-dimensional vector optimization problems.

For
Eq.

2
, the proximal gradient method
(
Tanabe et al. 2019
;
Tanabe et al. 2022a
)
is effective.
Using it, the merit function
(
Tanabe et al. 2022c
)
, which returns zero at the Pareto solutions and strictly positive values otherwise, converges to zero with rate
O(1/k)
under reasonable assumptions.
It is also shown that the generated sequence of iterates converges to a weak Pareto solution
(
Bello-Cruz et al. 2022
)
.
On the other hand, the accelerated proximal gradient method
(
Tanabe et al. 2022b
)
, which generalizes the Fast Iterative Shrinkage Thresholding Algorithm (FISTA)
(
Beck and Teboulle 2009
)
for convex-composite single-objective problems, has also been considered, along with a proof of the merit function’s
O(1/k^{2})
convergence rate.
However, the momentum factor used there is classical (
t_{1}=1,t_{k+1}=\sqrt{t_{k}^{2}+1/4}+1/2
), and the iterates’ convergence is not proven.

This paper generalizes the associated factor by
t_{1}=1,t_{k+1}=\sqrt{t_{k}^{2}-at_{k}+b}+1/2
with hyperparameters
a\in[0,1),b\in[a^{2}/4,1/4]
.
This is new even in the single-objective context, and it generalizes well-known factors.
For example, when
a=0
and
b=1/4
, it reduces to
t_{1}=1,t_{k+1}=\sqrt{t_{k}^{2}+1/4}+1/2
, proposed in
Nesterov 1983
;
Beck and Teboulle 2009
, and when
b=a^{2}/4
, it gives
t_{k}=(1-a)k/2+(1+a)/2
, suggested in
Chambolle and Dossal 2015
;
Attouch and Peypouquet 2016
;
Attouch et al. 2018
;
Su et al. 2016
.
We show that the merit function converges to zero with rate
O(1/k^{2})
for any
(a,b)
.
In addition, we prove the iterates’ convergence to a weak Pareto solution when
a>0
.
As discussed in
Section

4
, this suggests that the proposed method might achieve finite-iteration manifold (active set) identification
(
Sun et al. 2019
)
without the assumption of strong convexity.
Furthermore, we carry out numerical experiments with various
(a,b)
and observe that some
(a,b)
yield better results than the classical factors.

The outline of this paper is as follows.
We present some notations and definitions used in this paper in
Section

2.1
.

Section

2.2
recalls the accelerated proximal gradient method for
Eq.

2
and its associated results.
We generalize the momentum factor and prove that it preserves an
O(1/k^{2})
convergence rate in
Section

3
, and we demonstrate the convergence of the iterates in
Section

4
.
Finally,
Section

5
provides numerical experiments and compares the numerical performances depending on the hyperparameters.

Preliminaries

2.1
Definitions and notations

For every natural number
d
, write the
d
-dimensional real space by
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, and define

\mathbf{R}_{\mathord{+}}^{d}\coloneqq\left\{v\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}v_{i}\geq 0,i=1,\dots,d\right\}.

(5)

This induces the partial orders: for any
v^{1},v^{2}\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
,
v^{1}\leq v^{2}
(alternatively,
v^{2}\geq v^{1}
) if
v^{2}-v^{1}\in\mathbf{R}_{\mathord{+}}^{d}
and
v^{1}<v^{2}
(alternatively,
v^{2}>v^{1}
) if
v^{2}-v^{1}\in\interior\mathbf{R}_{\mathord{+}}^{d}
.
In other words,
v^{1}\leq v^{2}
and
v^{1}<v^{2}
mean that
v^{1}_{i}\leq v^{2}_{i}
and
v^{1}_{i}<v^{2}_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,d
, respectively.
Furthermore, let
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
be the Euclidean inner product in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, i.e.,
\left\langle v^{1},v^{2}\right\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{d}{v^{1}_{i}v^{2}_{i}}
, and let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
be the Euclidean norm, i.e.,
\left\lVert v\right\rVert\coloneqq\sqrt{\left\langle v,v\right\rangle}
.
Moreover, we define the
\ell_{1}
-norm and
\ell_{\infty}
-norm by
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{1}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lvert v_{i}\right\rvert
and
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\infty}\coloneqq\max_{i=1,\dots,d}\left\lvert v_{i}\right\rvert
, respectively.

We introduce some concepts used in the problem
Eq.

2
.
Recall that

X^{\ast}\coloneqq\left\{x^{\ast}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\text{There does not exist~$x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}$ such that~$F(x)<F(x^{\ast})$}\right\}

(6)

is the set of
weakly Pareto optimal
solutions for
Eq.

2
.
When
m=1
,
X^{\ast}
reduces to the optimal solution set.
Moreover, define the effective domain of
F
by

\dom F\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}F(x)<\infty\right\},

(7)

and write the level set of
F
on
c\in\mathbf{R}^{m}
as

\mathcal{L}_{F}(c)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}F(x)\leq c\right\}.

(8)

Furthermore, we express the image of
A\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
and the inverse image of
B\subseteq(\mathbf{R}\cup\left\{\infty\right\})^{m}
under
F
as

F(A)\coloneqq\left\{F(x)\in\mathbf{R}^{m}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}x\in A\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad F^{-1}(B)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}F(x)\in B\right\},

(9)

respectively.

Finally, let us recall the merit function
u_{0}\colon\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
for
Eq.

2
proposed in
Tanabe et al. 2022c
:

u_{0}(x)\coloneqq\sup_{z\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\min_{i=1,\dots,m}[F_{i}(x)-F_{i}(z)],

(10)

which returns zero at optimal solutions and strictly positive values otherwise.
The following theorem shows that
u_{0}
is a merit function in the Pareto sense.

Theorem 1
.

(
Tanabe et al. 2022c
, Theorem 3.1)

Let
u_{0}
be defined by
Eq.

10
.
Then,
u_{0}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
Moreover,
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
is weakly Pareto optimal for
Eq.

2
if and only if
u_{0}(x)=0
.

Note that when
m=1
, we have

u_{0}(x)=F_{1}(x)-F_{1}^{\ast},

(11)

where
F_{1}^{\ast}
is the optimal objective value.
Clearly, this is a merit function for scalar-valued optimization.

2.2
The accelerated proximal gradient method for multi-objective optimization

This subsection recalls the accelerated proximal gradient method for
Eq.

2
proposed in
Tanabe et al. 2022b
and its main results.
Recall that each
F_{i}
is the sum of a continuously differentiable function
f_{i}
and a closed, proper, and convex function
g_{i}
, and that
\nabla f_{i}
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
L_{i}>0
.
Define

L\coloneqq\max_{i=1,\dots,m}L_{i}.

(12)

The method solves the following subproblem at each iteration for given
x\in\dom F
,
y\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
, and
\ell\geq L
:

\min_{z\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\quad\varphi^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\ell}(z;x,y),

(13)

where

\varphi^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\ell}(z;x,y)\coloneqq\max_{i=1,\dots,m}\left[\left\langle\nabla f_{i}(y),z-y\right\rangle+g_{i}(z)+f_{i}(y)-F_{i}(x)\right]+\frac{\ell}{2}\left\lVert z-y\right\rVert^{2}.

(14)

From the strong convexity,
Eq.

13
has a unique optimal solution
p^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\ell}(x,y)
, i.e.,

p^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\ell}(x,y)\coloneqq\argmin_{z\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\varphi^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\ell}(z;x,y).

(15)

The following proposition characterizes weak Pareto optimality in terms of the mapping
p^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\ell}
.

Proposition 2
.

(
Tanabe et al. 2022b
, Proposition 4.1 (i))

Let
p^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\ell}(x,y)
be defined by
Eq.

15
.
Then,
y\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
is weakly Pareto optimal for
Eq.

2
if and only if
p^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\ell}(x,y)=y
for some
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

This implies that using
\left\lVert p^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\ell}(x,y)-y\right\rVert_{\infty}<\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
is reasonable as the stopping criteria.
We state below the accelerated proximal gradient method for
Eq.

2
.

Algorithm 1

Accelerated proximal gradient method for
Eq.

2

1:

Set
x^{0}=y^{1}\in\dom F,\ell\geq L,\varepsilon>0
.

2:

x^{\ast}
: A weakly Pareto optimal point

3:

k\leftarrow 1

4:

t_{1}\leftarrow 1

5:

while

\left\lVert p^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\ell}(x^{k-1},y^{k})-y^{k}\right\rVert_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon

do

6:

x^{k}\leftarrow p^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\ell}(x^{k-1},y^{k})

7:

t_{k+1}\leftarrow\sqrt{t_{k}^{2}+1/4}+1/2

8:

\gamma_{k}\leftarrow(t_{k}-1)/t_{k+1}

9:

y^{k+1}\leftarrow x^{k}+\gamma_{k}(x^{k}-x^{k-1})

10:

k\leftarrow k+1

11:

end

while

Algorithm

1
generates
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
such that
\left\{u_{0}(x^{k})\right\}
converges to zero with rate
O(1/k^{2})
under the following assumption.
This assumption is also used to analyze the proximal gradient method without acceleration
(
Tanabe et al. 2022a
)
and is not particularly strong as suggested in
(
Tanabe et al. 2022a
, Remark 5.2)
.

Assumption 2.1
.

(
Tanabe et al. 2022a
, Assumption 5.1)

Let
X^{\ast}
and
\mathcal{L}_{F}
be defined by
Eqs.

6
and
8
, respectively.
Then, for all
x\in\mathcal{L}_{F}(F(x^{0}))
, there exists
x^{\ast}\in X^{\ast}
such that
F(x^{\ast})\leq F(x)
and

R\coloneqq\sup_{F^{\ast}\in F(X^{\ast}\cap\mathcal{L}_{F}(F(x^{0}))}\inf_{z\in F^{-1}(\left\{F^{\ast}\right\})}\left\lVert z-x^{0}\right\rVert^{2}<\infty.

(16)

Theorem 3
.

(
Tanabe et al. 2022b
, Theorem 5.2)

Under
2.1
,
Algorithm

1
generates
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
such that

u_{0}(x^{k})\leq\frac{2\ell R}{(k+1)^{2}}\quad\text{for all~$k\geq 1$},

(17)

where
R\geq 0
is given by
Eq.

16
, and
u_{0}
is a merit function defined by
Eq.

10
.

The following corollary shows the global convergence of
Algorithm

1
.

Corollary 4
.

(
Tanabe et al. 2022b
, Corollary 5.2)

Suppose that
2.1
holds.
Then, every accumulation point of
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
generated by
Algorithm

1
is weakly Pareto optimal for
Eq.

2
.

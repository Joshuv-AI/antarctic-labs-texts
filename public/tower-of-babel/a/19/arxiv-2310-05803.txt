Triple-collinear splittings with massive particles Preprint: IFIC/23-46 arXiv: 2310.05803
Prasanna K. Dhani, Germán Rodrigo, and German F. R. Sborlini

Abstract
We analyze in detail the most singular behaviour of processes involving triple-collinear splittings with massive particles in the quasi-collinear limit, and present compact expressions for the splitting amplitudes and the corresponding splitting kernels at the squared-amplitude level. Our expressions fully agree with well-known triple-collinear splittings in the massless limit, which are used as a guide to achieve the final expressions. These results are important to quantify dominant mass effects in many observables, and constitute an essential ingredient of current high-precision computational frameworks for collider phenomenology.

Introduction

Owing to the non-Abelian nature of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) due to multi-gluon interactions, a very relevant topic of study is the singular structure of scattering amplitudes in various infrared (IR), soft and collinear, limits. The soft limit is achieved when one or more gluons are emitted in a hard-scattering process with vanishingly small energies and the collinear limit is achieved when two or more partons, quarks and gluons, are emitted parallel to each other i.e. with vanishingly small angles of separation. In these IR limits, the scattering amplitude for the process becomes singular and it exhibits a factorized structure
Collins:1989gx
;
Catani:2011st
;
Forshaw:2012bi
. The singular behaviour is given by the QCD factorization formulae
Collins:1989gx
and it is captured by factors which have minimal dependence on the hard-scattering process under consideration.

In quest for ever increasing precise theoretical predictions for observables at high-energy colliders such as the CERN’s Large Hadron Collider (LHC), the framework of perturbation theory plays a pivotal role. To this end, a higher order theory prediction requires to combine contributions from both virtual loop and real radiation Feynman diagrams for a meaningful comparison with the experimental data. Virtual loop diagrams in the IR region lead to singular configurations in four space-time dimensions. Similarly, real radiation of soft and/or collinear partons produce kinematical singularities after the phase space integration over the emitted partons. In the context of dimensional regularization (DR)
Bollini:1972ui
;
tHooft:1972tcz
;
Cicuta:1972jf
where one regulates these singularities by performing the computations in arbitrary space-time dimensions, the understanding of these IR singular configurations and its isolation for the construction of efficient methods to evaluate cross sections rely on the general factorization properties of hard-scattering QCD amplitudes in both soft and collinear limits. Based on this understanding, general algorithms for the computation of IR finite jet cross sections at next-to-leading order (NLO) were proposed through celebrated works in refs.
Catani:1996vz
;
Frixione:1995ms
. Going forward to the next perturbative orders i.e. next-to-NLO (NNLO) and next-to-NNLO (N3LO), in the last twenty years or so, a gigantic effort has been put forth and a variety of different techniques

However, close to the boundary of phase space, real and virtual loop contributions are strongly imbalanced due to differences in available phase space. As a result, the mechanism of IR pole cancellation leaves residual effects in the form of large logarithmic contributions for a wide class of hard-scattering observables. While there has been a lot of progress in understanding the collinear factorization of hard-scattering matrix elements with massless partons, the case with massive partons needs more dedicated efforts in view of modern day precision colliders. The production of heavy flavors such as bottom and charm quarks at high-energy colliders has been a topic of interest due to their relevance in Standard Model (SM) and beyond the SM physics. For better theory predictions, it is crucial to consider higher-order contributions beyond the LO in perturbation theory. To this end, it is also important to have control over explicit IR poles and possibly, large logarithmic contributions as a result of scale hierarchies. QCD factorization with massive particles plays a big role in achieving these goals.

It is well known that radiation from massive partons in the collinear limit is suppressed, which constitutes the so-called dead-cone effect
Dokshitzer:1991fd
, and the appearance of explicit collinear poles in perturbative computations is screened due to the presence of mass. However, they can lead to IR finite contributions proportional to ln
{}^{n}\left(Q^{2}/m^{2}\right)
, where
Q
is the typical scale of the hard-scattering process and
m
is the mass of the heavy parton. In the kinematical region where
Q^{2}\gg m^{2}
, these large logarithmic contributions can spoil both the numerical convergence and reliability of theoretical prediction of a generic observable. The good news is that these contributions can be computed in a process independent manner and resummed to all orders in perturbation theory as they are related to singular behaviour of matrix elements in the
m\rightarrow 0
limit. The singular behaviour is controlled by the QCD factorization formulae in the
quasi-collinear
limit
Catani:2000ef
;
Keller:1998tf
, in the same way that the IR divergences are controlled by the soft and collinear factorization formulae.

At
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{\rm S})
, double-parton quasi-collinear limit for various tree-level splitting processes are computed long back in refs.
Catani:2000ef
;
Keller:1998tf

Catani:1996vz
;
Frixione:1995ms
for the computation of jet observables. In this paper, we take the first step in extending the QCD factorization in quasi-collinear limit to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{\rm S}^{2})
and we study simultaneous collinear limit of three partons in sight of the importance for accurately predicting dominant mass effects in phenomenological analysis. We compute the complete set of splitting matrices at the amplitude level as well as splitting kernels at the squared amplitude level in arbitrary space-time dimensions,
d=4-2\epsilon
, and keep its dependence exact. At the squared amplitude level, we also fully take into account azimuthal correlations, specific to the gluon channel, which are necessary to construct some general algorithms to perform exact NNLO computations of jet cross sections involving massive particles.

The outline of the paper is the following. We start in section

2
Multiple collinear and quasi-collinear limits of scattering amplitudes

Let us briefly describe the multiple quasi-collinear limit of scattering amplitudes at tree-level, and introduce various concepts and definitions. We consider a generic hard-scattering process in QCD with massive quarks and anti-quarks, that involves
n
final-state partons along with any number of non-coloured particles such as Higgs bosons, electroweak bosons, photons etc. We denote as
{\cal M}^{(0)}\left(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m},\ldots,p_{n}\right)
the corresponding tree-level matrix element, with
p_{i}^{\mu}
being the four-momentum associated with
i^{\rm th}
-particle, while the dependence on additional non-QCD particles through their momenta and quantum numbers is always understood. We denote the flavor of parton
i
with
a_{i}
and we will avoid indicating the flavor indices explicitly unless they are strictly required.

The external partons
a_{i}
are on-shell i.e.
p_{i}^{2}=m_{i}^{2}
. Also, note that we always define the momenta
p_{i}
’s as outgoing, but the time component
p_{i}^{0}
of the four-momentum vector
p_{i}^{\mu}
is not constrained to be positive-definite. Different types of physical processes are represented by applying crossing symmetry to the same matrix element
{\cal M}^{(0)}
(for brevity, we also avoid indicating the dependence on momenta unless it is necessary). For instance, if
p_{i}
has positive energy,
{\cal M}^{(0)}
describes a physical process that produces the parton
i
in the final state; if
p_{i}
has negative energy, the matrix element
{\cal M}^{(0)}
describes a physical process produced by the collision of anti-parton
\bar{i}
in the initial state.

We are interested in analysing the behaviour of the hard-scattering matrix element
{\cal M}^{(0)}
in the kinematical configuration where
m
out of
n
external parton momenta become collinear to each other. Without any loss of generality, we assume them to be
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
and we define their sum to be
p_{1\ldots m}=p_{1}+\ldots+p_{m}
. The mass of the parent decaying particle with flavor
a
, which undergoes the splitting, is denoted by
m_{1\ldots m}
. Since, the parent decaying particle is generally off-shell (
p_{1\dots m}^{2}\neq m_{1\ldots m}^{2}
), we define an on-shell vector

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}^{\mu}=p_{1\dots m}^{\mu}-\frac{p_{1\ldots m}^{2}-m_{1\ldots m}^{2}}{2\,n\cdot p_{1\ldots m}}n^{\mu}\,,

(1)

such that it fulfils
\widetilde{P}^{2}=m_{1\ldots m}^{2}
.
Then, we introduce the Sudakov parametrization for the collinear momenta
Catani:2002hc
;
Catani:2011st
as follows

\displaystyle p_{i}^{\mu}=x_{i}\widetilde{P}^{\mu}+k_{\perp i}^{\mu}-\frac{k_{\perp i}^{2}+x_{i}^{2}m_{1\ldots m}^{2}-m_{i}^{2}}{x_{i}}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2\,n\cdot\widetilde{P}}\ ,\qquad i\in C\equiv\{1,\ldots,m\},

(2)

where the vector
\widetilde{P}
denotes the collinear direction and the introduction of an auxiliary light-like vector
n^{\mu}
(
n^{2}=0
) is necessary to specify how the collinear direction is approached or, equivalently, to specify the transverse components
k_{\perp i}
(
k_{\perp i}\cdot\widetilde{P}=k_{\perp i}\cdot n=0
, with
k_{\perp i}^{2}<0
).

Since, the collinear limit is invariant under longitudinal boosts along the direction of total momentum
p_{1\ldots m}
, we define following boost invariant kinematic variables

\displaystyle z_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}},

(3)

\displaystyle\tilde{k}_{i}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=k_{\perp i}^{\mu}-z_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{\perp j}^{\mu}~.

(4)

Note that these boost invariant variables automatically satisfy the constraints
\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_{i}=1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{k}_{\perp i}=0
and hence only
2(m-1)
of them are independent. In terms of the variables introduced in eqs. (
3
) and (
4
), the sub-energies and the dot products are expressed in the following way

\displaystyle s_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv(p_{i}+p_{j})^{2}=-z_{i}z_{j}\left(\frac{\tilde{k}_{j}}{z_{j}}-\frac{\tilde{k}_{i}}{z_{i}}\right)^{2}+(z_{i}+z_{j})\left(\frac{m_{i}^{2}}{z_{i}}+\frac{m_{j}^{2}}{z_{j}}\right)~,

(5)

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv 2p_{i}\cdot p_{j}=z_{i}z_{j}\left[-\left(\frac{\tilde{k}_{j}}{z_{j}}-\frac{\tilde{k}_{i}}{z_{i}}\right)^{2}+\frac{m_{i}^{2}}{z_{i}^{2}}+\frac{m_{j}^{2}}{z_{j}^{2}}\right]~,

(6)

and they are equivalent in the massless limit.

Quasi-collinear limit
Catani:2000ef
;
Keller:1998tf
is reached when the transverse momenta
k_{\perp}
is of
\mathcal{O}(m)
or small. It turns out that, by virtue of eqs. (
5
) and (
6
), the invariant mass of the splitting system depends on
\{k_{\perp i}^{2},m_{i}^{2}\}
and
m_{1\ldots m}^{2}
. So, this allows us to formally define the
quasi-collinear
kinematical region by uniformly rescaling these variables, i.e.

m_{i}\rightarrow\rho\,m_{i}~,\qquad k_{\perp i}\rightarrow\rho\,k_{\perp i}~,\qquad m_{1\ldots m}\rightarrow\rho\,m_{1\ldots m}~,

(7)

and taking the limit
\rho\rightarrow 0
. Keeping the most singular terms in
\rho
and neglecting sub-leading terms, we obtain the following amplitude level factorization formula

|{{\cal M}^{(0)}\left(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m},\ldots,p_{n}\right)}\rangle\simeq{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}^{(0)}_{a_{1}\dots a_{m}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m};\widetilde{P})\,|{\overline{{\cal M}}^{(0)}(\widetilde{P},p_{m+1},\ldots,p_{n})}\rangle\,,

(8)

where the factor
{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}^{(0)}_{a_{1}\dots a_{m}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m};\widetilde{P})
is a matrix in color + spin space, called the splitting matrix that fully embodies the leading singular behaviour when the parent particle
a
with momentum
p_{1\ldots m}
undergoes collinear splitting into the particles
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in C}
, and it only depends on information carried by the quasi-collinear particles. In eq. (
8
),
\overline{{\cal M}}^{(0)}
is called the reduced matrix element and it is obtained from the original amplitude by replacing
m
collinear partons by the single parent parton of momentum
\widetilde{P}
. Taking the massless limit of eq. (
8
), we obtain the well-known collinear factorization formula
Catani:2011st
. It is useful to define the colour-projected splitting matrices, and we have

Sp_{a_{1}\ldots a_{m}}^{(0)(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m};c)}\left(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m};\widetilde{P}\right)\equiv\langle c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}|{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}_{a_{1}\ldots a_{m}}^{(0)}\left(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m};\widetilde{P}\right)|c\rangle\,,

(9)

where
c
is the colour of the parent parton and
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
denote the colour indices of the collinear partons.

An important observation related to eq. (
8
) is that, the parent parton in the exact collinear or quasi-collinear limits is on-shell and it carries only physical degrees of freedom. In this way, the original scattering amplitude factorizes into two factors, namely the splitting matrix and the reduced matrix element, both containing
physical states
as external particles. Notice that this is valid both for massive and massless particles. The factorization formula in eq. (
8
) is gauge independent, however, in order to unveil the splitting amplitude directly we need to work in a
physical gauge
. Explicitly, in physical gauges we have the following relations

\displaystyle\frac{\imath\,d_{\mu\nu}(p,n)}{p^{2}+\imath 0}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\imath}{p^{2}+\imath 0}\,\sum_{\lambda\ {\rm phys.}}\,\varepsilon_{\mu}(\widetilde{P},\lambda)\varepsilon^{*}_{\nu}(\widetilde{P},\lambda)

(10)

for gluons using the polarization tensor
d_{\mu\nu}(p,n)
, and

\displaystyle\frac{\imath\,(\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu}+m)}{p^{2}-m^{2}+\imath 0}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\imath}{p^{2}-m^{2}+\imath 0}\,\sum_{\lambda\ {\rm phys.}}\,u(\widetilde{P},\lambda)\bar{u}(\widetilde{P},\lambda)

(11)

for quarks, where
\lambda
denotes the polarization (or spin).

Besides the splitting matrices, we are also interested in the polarized and unpolarized splitting kernels, which govern the collinear and quasi-collinear limits of the squared matrix elements. In order to define the polarized kernels, let us start from eq. (
8
) and consider a gluon-initiated splitting process. Stripping the polarization vector from the splitting amplitude, we have

\displaystyle|{{\cal M}^{(0)}\left(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m},\ldots,p_{n}\right)}\rangle\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\,{\rm phys.}}\,{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}^{(0),\mu}_{a_{1}\dots a_{m}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m};\widetilde{P})\,\varepsilon_{\mu}(\widetilde{P},\lambda)\,

(12)

\displaystyle\times|{{\cal M}^{(0)}(\widetilde{P}^{-\lambda},p_{m+1},\ldots,p_{n})}\rangle\,,

and computing the square, we obtain

\displaystyle|{\cal M}^{(0)}\left(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m},\ldots,p_{n}\right)|^{2}\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\,{\rm phys.}}\,\langle{{\cal M}^{(0)}(\widetilde{P}^{-\lambda^{\prime}},p_{m+1},\ldots,p_{n})}|\,\varepsilon_{\mu}(\widetilde{P},\lambda)\varepsilon^{*}_{\nu}(\widetilde{P},\lambda^{\prime})\,

(13)

\displaystyle\times\left[{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}^{(0),\nu}_{a_{1}\dots a_{m}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m};\widetilde{P})\right]^{\dagger}{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}^{(0),\mu}_{a_{1}\dots a_{m}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m};\widetilde{P})\,\,

\displaystyle\times|{{\cal M}^{(0)}(\widetilde{P}^{-\lambda},p_{m+1},\ldots,p_{n})}\rangle\,.

The second line of eq. (
13
) contains the product of two amputated splitting amplitudes, which leads us to define

\hat{P}^{(0),\,\mu\nu}_{a_{1}\dots a_{m}}\equiv\left(\frac{s_{1\ldots m}-m_{1\ldots m}^{2}}{8\pi\mu_{0}^{2\epsilon}\alpha_{\rm S}}\right)^{m-1}\left[{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}^{(0),\nu}_{a_{1}\dots a_{m}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m};\widetilde{P})\right]^{\dagger}{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}^{(0),\mu}_{a_{1}\dots a_{m}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m};\widetilde{P})\,,

(14)

that corresponds to the polarized splitting kernel, with the proper normalization and
\mu_{0}
denotes the DR scale. Note that analogous to the splitting matrix
{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}^{(0)}
at the amplitude level, the splitting kernel
\hat{P}^{(0)}
is a matrix in color + spin space and it encodes the leading singular behaviour of the squared amplitude in the quasi-collinear limit. In eq. (
14
), we have introduced
s_{1\ldots m}\equiv p_{1\ldots m}^{2}
and summed over final-state colours and spins, averaged over only colours of the parent decaying parton. By averaging over spins/polarizations of the parent parton, we obtain the
m
-parton unpolarized splitting kernel

\langle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{a_{1}\ldots a_{m}}\rangle
, which is a generalization of the customary
Altarelli-Parisi LO splitting function.
Explicitly, by fixing the normalization, the tree-level unpolarized splitting kernels are given by

\langle\hat{P}_{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}}^{(0)}\rangle=\left(\frac{s_{1\ldots m}-m_{1\ldots m}^{2}}{8\pi\mu_{0}^{2\epsilon}\alpha_{\rm S}}\right)^{m-1}\,{\overline{|{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}_{a_{1}\dots a_{m}}^{(0)}|^{2}}}~.

(15)

In the quark-initiated case, we have the relation

\langle\hat{P}_{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}}^{(0)}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\,\delta^{ss^{\prime}}\,\hat{P}^{(0),\,ss^{\prime}}_{a_{1}\dots a_{m}}\,,

(16)

because of helicity conservation. The spin indices
s
and
s^{\prime}
are of the parent collinear quark (anti-quark) in the amplitude and conjugate amplitude, respectively. In the gluon-initiated splitting process, the unpolarized kernel is obtained by contracting the polarized one with the polarization tensor of the parent parton, i.e.
d_{\mu\nu}(\widetilde{P},n)
, and dividing by
d-2
(number of initial-state polarizations in
d=4-2\epsilon

\displaystyle\langle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{a_{1}\ldots a_{m}}\rangle=\frac{d_{\mu\nu}(\widetilde{P};n)}{d-2}\hat{P}^{(0),\mu\nu}_{a_{1}\ldots a_{m}}.

(17)

As we explained before, we shall choose a physical gauge to calculate the splitting amplitudes. Therefore, we use the light-cone gauge (LCG) where

d_{\mu\nu}(k,n)=-g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{k_{\mu}n_{\nu}+n_{\mu}k_{\nu}}{n\cdot k}\,,

(18)

represents the physical polarization tensor of a gauge vector boson (gluon or photon)
with momentum
k

n
in eq. (
18
) is
taken identical to the light-like vector
n
introduced in eq. (
1
).

Massive double-collinear splitting amplitudes and splitting kernels

We recall in this section the known results for the double-collinear splitting matrices and kernels with massive quarks. There are two non-vanishing splitting processes, i.e.

\displaystyle Q\rightarrow Q_{1}g_{2}~,

(19)

\displaystyle g\rightarrow Q_{1}\bar{Q}_{2}~.

(20)

The remaining processes are related by charge conjugation invariance of QCD. Their corresponding splitting matrices are

\displaystyle{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}_{Q_{1}g_{2}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=g_{\rm s}\mu_{0}^{\epsilon}\boldsymbol{t}^{c_{2}}_{c_{1}c}\frac{1}{\tilde{s}_{12}}\overline{u}(p_{1})\not{\varepsilon}(p_{2})u(\widetilde{P})~,

(21)

\displaystyle{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}_{Q_{1}\bar{Q}_{2}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=g_{\rm s}\mu_{0}^{\epsilon}\boldsymbol{t}^{c}_{c_{1}c_{2}}\frac{1}{s_{12}}\overline{u}(p_{1})\not{\varepsilon}^{*}(\widetilde{P})v(p_{2})~,

(22)

where
\boldsymbol{t}^{c}_{c_{1}c_{2}}
are the customary Gell-Mann colour matrices in the fundamental representation and the Dirac spinors are represented by
u(p)
and
v(p)
. At the squared amplitude level, the splitting kernels are given by
Catani:2000ef
;
Keller:1998tf

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0),ss^{\prime}}_{Q_{1}g_{2}}

\displaystyle=\delta^{ss^{\prime}}C_{F}\left[\frac{1+z^{2}}{1-z}-\epsilon(1-z)-2\frac{m_{Q}^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{12}}\right],

(23)

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0),\mu\nu}_{Q_{1}\bar{Q}_{2}}

\displaystyle=T_{R}\left[-g^{\mu\nu}-4\frac{k_{\perp}^{\mu}k_{\perp}^{\nu}}{s_{12}}\right],

(24)

where
C_{F}=(N_{c}^{2}-1)/(2N_{c})
is the Casimir in the fundamental representation of SU(
N_{c}
) QCD with
N_{c}
colors. The corresponding Casimir in the adjoint representation is given by
C_{A}=N_{c}
and
T_{R}=1/2
. The momentum fraction
z
in eq. (
23
) is
z=z_{1}=1-z_{2}
and the transverse momentum vector
k_{\perp}^{\mu}
in eq. (
24
) is
k_{\perp}^{\mu}=k_{\perp 1}^{\mu}=-k_{\perp 2}^{\mu}
. As we anticipated in section
2
, the polarized splitting kernels are diagonal in the spin of the parent quark (anti-quark) but are not diagonal in the polarization of the parent gluon. To this end, we also obtain the corresponding unpolarized splitting kernels by using eqs. (
16
-
17
) and they are as follows

\displaystyle\langle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{Q_{1}g_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=C_{F}\left[\frac{1+z^{2}}{1-z}-\epsilon(1-z)-2\frac{m_{Q}^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{12}}\right],

(25)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{Q_{1}\bar{Q}_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=T_{R}\left[1-\frac{2}{1-\epsilon}\left(z(1-z)-\frac{m_{Q}^{2}}{s_{12}}\right)\right].

(26)

It is worth noticing that the mass dependence in
\hat{P}^{(0),\mu\nu}_{Q_{1}\bar{Q}_{2}}
appears implicitly inside the sub-energy
s_{12}
; the functional structure is analogous to the massless case. In the case of
\hat{P}^{(0),ss^{\prime}}_{Q_{1}g_{2}}
, besides a contribution sharing the same functional dependence as in the massless case, there is an additional term proportional to
m^{2}_{Q}
. We will see similar features in the massive triple-collinear splitting kernels.

4
Massive triple-collinear splitting amplitudes

There are four non-vanishing splitting processes, which are given by

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle\rightarrow\bar{Q}_{1}Q_{2}Q_{3}~,

(27)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle\rightarrow\bar{Q}_{1}^{\prime}Q_{2}^{\prime}Q_{3}~,

(28)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle\rightarrow g_{1}g_{2}Q_{3}~,

(29)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\rightarrow g_{1}Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}~.

(30)

The quark-antiquark pair
\bar{Q}_{1}^{\prime}Q_{2}^{\prime}
is of different flavour than
Q
and has a mass
m_{Q^{\prime}}
. All the other quarks and antiquarks have mass
m_{Q}
. In the following sections, we present results for the splitting matrices and splitting kernels corresponding to the processes in eqs. (
27
-
30
).

4.1
Splitting amplitudes with four massive quarks

We consider first the splitting amplitude of four massive quarks of the same flavour, eq. (
27
). In this case there are two colour structures and they are related by the permutation
2\leftrightarrow 3
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{1}:T_{R}\left(\delta_{c_{1}c_{3}}\delta_{cc_{2}}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\delta_{cc_{3}}\delta_{c_{1}c_{2}}\right)~,

(31)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{2}:T_{R}\left(\delta_{c_{1}c_{2}}\delta_{cc_{3}}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\delta_{cc_{2}}\delta_{c_{1}c_{3}}\right)~.

(32)

The l.h.s. of these expressions refer to operators, whilst the r.h.s. correspond to their projection according eq. (
9
). There are six distinct spin structures out of which three are related to the other three by the permutation
2\leftrightarrow 3
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{1}:\bar{u}(p_{3})\gamma^{\mu}u(\widetilde{P})\bar{u}(p_{2})\gamma_{\mu}v(p_{1})~,\quad\,\,\mathbf{S}_{2}:\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{p}_{2}u(\widetilde{P})\bar{u}(p_{2})\not{n}v(p_{1})~,

(33)

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{3}:\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{p}_{1}u(\widetilde{P})\bar{u}(p_{2})\not{n}v(p_{1})~,\qquad\mathbf{S}_{4}:\bar{u}(p_{2})\gamma^{\mu}u(\widetilde{P})\bar{u}(p_{3})\gamma_{\mu}v(p_{1})~,

(34)

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{5}:\bar{u}(p_{2})\not{p}_{3}u(\widetilde{P})\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{n}v(p_{1})~,\qquad\mathbf{S}_{6}:\bar{u}(p_{2})\not{p}_{1}u(\widetilde{P})\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{n}v(p_{1})~.

(35)

The splitting amplitude for the process is given by

\displaystyle{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}_{\bar{Q}_{1}Q_{2}Q_{3}}^{(0)}=\frac{g_{\rm s}^{2}\mu_{0}^{2\epsilon}}{s_{123}-m_{Q}^{2}}\left[\frac{\mathbf{C}_{1}}{s_{12}}\left(-\mathbf{S}_{1}+\frac{\mathbf{S}_{2}+\mathbf{S}_{3}}{n\cdot p_{12}}\right)-\frac{\mathbf{C}_{2}}{s_{13}}\left(-\mathbf{S}_{4}+\frac{\mathbf{S}_{5}+\mathbf{S}_{6}}{n\cdot p_{13}}\right)\right]~,

(36)

where
s_{123}\equiv(p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3})^{2}
and
p_{ij}\equiv p_{i}+p_{j}
. If, instead, we consider the splitting process where the quark-antiquark pair
\bar{Q}_{1}^{\prime}Q_{2}^{\prime}
is of different flavour and mass than the parent quark
Q
, eq. (
28
), the splitting amplitude is simplified to

\displaystyle{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}_{\bar{Q}_{1}^{\prime}Q_{2}^{\prime}Q_{3}}^{(0)}=\frac{g_{\rm s}^{2}\mu_{0}^{2\epsilon}}{s_{123}-m_{Q}^{2}}\frac{\mathbf{C}_{1}}{s_{12}}\left(-\mathbf{S}_{1}+\frac{\mathbf{S}_{2}+\mathbf{S}_{3}}{n\cdot p_{12}}\right)~.

(37)

The explicit dependence on the mass
m_{Q^{\prime}}
is hiding in
s_{123}
and
s_{12}
, and the implicit dependence is through the momenta
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
.

4.2
Q\rightarrow g_{1}g_{2}Q_{3}

In this case there are two colour structures and they are related by the permutation
1\leftrightarrow 2
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{1}:\left(\boldsymbol{t}^{c_{2}}\boldsymbol{t}^{c_{1}}\right)_{c_{3}c}~,\quad\mathbf{C}_{2}:\left(\boldsymbol{t}^{c_{1}}\boldsymbol{t}^{c_{2}}\right)_{c_{3}c}~,

(38)

and eight distinct spin structures

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{1}:\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{p}_{1}\not{\varepsilon}(p_{1})\not{\varepsilon}(p_{2})u(\widetilde{P})~,\qquad\;\;\;\mathbf{S}_{2}:\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{p}_{2}\not{\varepsilon}(p_{2})\not{\varepsilon}(p_{1})u(\widetilde{P})~,

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{3}:\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{\varepsilon}(p_{2})u(\widetilde{P})\,p_{3}\cdot\varepsilon(p_{1})~,\qquad\mathbf{S}_{4}:\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{\varepsilon}(p_{2})u(\widetilde{P})\,p_{2}\cdot\varepsilon(p_{1})~,

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{5}:\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{\varepsilon}(p_{1})u(\widetilde{P})\,p_{3}\cdot\varepsilon(p_{2})~,\qquad\mathbf{S}_{6}:\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{\varepsilon}(p_{1})u(\widetilde{P})\,p_{1}\cdot\varepsilon(p_{2})~,

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{7}:\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{p}_{2}u(\widetilde{P})\,\varepsilon(p_{1})\cdot\varepsilon(p_{2})~,\qquad\mathbf{S}_{8}:\bar{u}(p_{3})\not{p}_{1}u(\widetilde{P})\,\varepsilon(p_{1})\cdot\varepsilon(p_{2})~.

(39)

Notice that these spin structures fulfil
\mathbf{S}_{1}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{S}_{2}
,
\mathbf{S}_{3}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{S}_{5}
,
\mathbf{S}_{4}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{S}_{6}
and
\mathbf{S}_{7}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{S}_{8}
under the transformation
1\leftrightarrow 2
. The splitting amplitude for the process is given by

\displaystyle{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}^{(0)}_{g_{1}g_{2}Q_{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{s}^{2}\mu_{0}^{2\epsilon}}{\tilde{s}_{123}}\Bigg[\mathbf{C}_{1}\left(\frac{\mathbf{S}_{2}-2\mathbf{S}_{5}}{\tilde{s}_{23}}\right)+\mathbf{C}_{2}\left(\frac{\mathbf{S}_{1}-2\mathbf{S}_{3}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}\right)

\displaystyle+(\mathbf{C}_{2}-\mathbf{C}_{1})\frac{2}{\tilde{s}_{12}}\left(\mathbf{S}_{4}+\frac{n\cdot p_{2}}{n\cdot p_{12}}\mathbf{S}_{8}-\mathbf{S}_{6}-\frac{n\cdot p_{1}}{n\cdot p_{12}}\mathbf{S}_{7}\right)\Bigg]~,

(40)

being the full expression symmetric under the exchange
1\leftrightarrow 2
, as expected. Here, we define
\tilde{s}_{123}=\tilde{s}_{12}+\tilde{s}_{13}+\tilde{s}_{23}
, following the notation introduced in eq. (
6
).

4.3
g\rightarrow g_{1}Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}

In this case there are two colour structures given by:

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{1}:\left(\boldsymbol{t}^{c}\boldsymbol{t}^{c_{1}}\right)_{c_{2}c_{3}}~,\qquad\mathbf{C}_{2}:\left(\boldsymbol{t}^{c_{1}}\boldsymbol{t}^{c}\right)_{c_{2}c_{3}}~,

(41)

and six distinct spin structures

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{1}:\bar{u}(p_{2})\not{p}_{1}\not{\varepsilon}(p_{1})\not{\varepsilon}^{*}(\widetilde{P})v(p_{3})~,\qquad\quad\mathbf{S}_{2}:\bar{u}(p_{2})\not{\varepsilon}^{*}(\widetilde{P})v(p_{3})\,p_{2}\cdot\varepsilon(p_{1})~,

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{3}:\bar{u}(p_{2})\not{\varepsilon}^{*}(\widetilde{P})v(p_{3})\,p_{3}\cdot\varepsilon(p_{1})~,\quad\quad\,\mathbf{S}_{4}:\bar{u}(p_{2})\not{\varepsilon}(p_{1})v(p_{3})\,p_{1}\cdot\varepsilon^{*}(\widetilde{P})~,

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{5}:\bar{u}(p_{2})\not{p}_{1}v(p_{3})\,\varepsilon^{*}(\widetilde{P})\cdot\varepsilon(p_{1})~,\quad\quad\,\mathbf{S}_{6}:\bar{u}(p_{2})\not{n}v(p_{3})\,\varepsilon^{*}(\widetilde{P})\cdot\varepsilon(p_{1})~.

(42)

However, these spin structures can be grouped according to

\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{1}=\mathbf{S}_{1}-2\mathbf{S}_{2}\,,\quad\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{2}=2\,(\mathbf{S}_{2}+\mathbf{S}_{3}+\mathbf{S}_{4}-\mathbf{S}_{5})\,,

(43)

in such a way that the splitting amplitude for the process is written as

\displaystyle{{\mbox{\boldmath$Sp$}}}^{(0)}_{g_{1}Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{s}^{2}\mu_{0}^{2\epsilon}}{s_{123}}\Bigg[\mathbf{C}_{1}\left(\frac{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{1}+\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{2}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}\right)+\mathbf{C}_{2}\,\frac{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{1}}{\tilde{s}_{12}}+(\mathbf{C}_{1}-\mathbf{C}_{2})\left(\frac{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{2}}{s_{23}}+\frac{s_{123}}{s_{23}}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{6}}{n\cdot p_{23}}\right)\Bigg].

(44)

It is worth appreciating that, in the massless limit, we recover the same expressions presented in ref.
Czakon:2022fqi
: in fact, we explicitly defined the spinor bases to ease the comparison and check our results. Besides, we appreciate that both the massive and massless splitting amplitudes share the same spinor and colour structures.

5
Massive triple-collinear splitting kernels

In order to obtain compact expressions, we exploit the results provided in ref.
Catani:1999ss
for the massless triple-collinear kernels, including the definition of the kinematical variable

t_{ij,k}=2\frac{z_{i}\tilde{s}_{jk}-z_{j}\tilde{s}_{ik}}{z_{i}+z_{j}}+\frac{z_{i}-z_{j}}{z_{i}+z_{j}}\tilde{s}_{ij}\,.

(45)

The unpolarized splitting kernel involving two quark-antiquark pairs of different flavours is given by

\displaystyle\langle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{\bar{Q}_{1}^{\prime}Q_{2}^{\prime}Q_{3}}\rangle

\displaystyle=C_{F}T_{R}\Bigg\{\frac{\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{123}}{2s_{12}^{2}}\left[-\frac{t_{12,3}^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{123}}+\frac{4z_{3}+(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}{1-z_{3}}+(1-2\epsilon)\left(z_{1}+z_{2}-\frac{\tilde{s}_{12}}{\tilde{s}_{123}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{2m_{Q^{\prime}}^{2}}{s_{12}^{2}}\bigg[\frac{z_{3}\tilde{s}_{123}}{(1-z_{3})^{2}}(1+2z_{3}-3z_{3}^{2}+4z_{1}z_{2})-\frac{\tilde{s}_{23}}{1-z_{3}}(2-3z_{1}-5z_{2}+z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{\tilde{s}_{13}}{1-z_{3}}(2-5z_{1}-3z_{2}+z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2})-\epsilon\Big(\tilde{s}_{123}(1-z_{3})-\tilde{s}_{12}(1+z_{3})\Big)\bigg]-2\frac{m_{Q}^{2}\tilde{s}_{12}}{s_{12}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{4m_{Q^{\prime}}^{4}}{s_{12}^{2}}z_{3}\left[\epsilon+\frac{2z_{1}z_{2}}{(1-z_{3})^{2}}+\frac{2z_{3}}{1-z_{3}}\right]-4\frac{m_{Q}^{2}m_{Q^{\prime}}^{2}}{s_{12}^{2}}\Bigg\}~.

(46)

The splitting kernel involving two quark-antiquark pairs of the same flavour is decomposed as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{\bar{Q}_{1}Q_{2}Q_{3}}\rangle=\left[\langle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{\bar{Q}_{1}^{\prime}Q_{2}^{\prime}Q_{3}}\rangle\Big|_{m_{Q^{\prime}}=m_{Q}}+(2\leftrightarrow 3)\right]+\langle\hat{P}_{\bar{Q}_{1}Q_{2}Q_{3}}^{\rm{(0,id)}}\rangle~,

(47)

where the interference contribution is given by

\displaystyle\langle\hat{P}_{\bar{Q}_{1}Q_{2}Q_{3}}^{\rm{(0,id)}}\rangle

\displaystyle=C_{F}\left(C_{F}-\frac{C_{A}}{2}\right)\Bigg\{\frac{\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{13}}{s_{12}s_{13}}\Bigg[(1-\epsilon)\left(\frac{2\tilde{s}_{23}}{\tilde{s}_{12}}-\epsilon\right)+\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}}{\tilde{s}_{12}}\bigg\{\frac{1+z_{1}^{2}}{1-z_{2}}-\frac{2z_{2}}{1-z_{3}}

\displaystyle-\epsilon\left(\frac{(1-z_{3})^{2}}{1-z_{2}}+1+z_{1}-\frac{2z_{2}}{1-z_{3}}\right)-\epsilon^{2}(1-z_{3})\bigg\}-\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{13}}\frac{z_{1}}{2}\bigg\{\frac{1+z_{1}^{2}}{(1-z_{2})(1-z_{3})}

\displaystyle-\epsilon\left(1+2\frac{1-z_{2}}{1-z_{3}}\right)-\epsilon^{2}\bigg\}\Bigg]+\frac{m_{Q}^{2}}{s_{12}s_{13}}\Bigg[2\tilde{s}_{23}\frac{3-7z_{2}+z_{2}^{2}+z_{2}^{3}+4z_{2}^{2}z_{3}}{(1-z_{2})(1-z_{3})}

\displaystyle+2\tilde{s}_{12}\frac{(1-2z_{2})^{2}+(1-2z_{3})^{2}-z_{3}(1-z_{3}^{2})-z_{2}(1+2z_{3})+z_{2}z_{3}(2z_{2}+3z_{3})}{(1-z_{2})(1-z_{3})}

\displaystyle-2\epsilon\tilde{s}_{12}\frac{2(1-z_{2})^{2}+(1-z_{3})^{2}-z_{3}(1-z_{3}^{2})-2z_{2}z_{3}(1+z_{1})}{(1-z_{2})(1-z_{3})}

\displaystyle-2\epsilon\tilde{s}_{23}\frac{1-z_{2}^{2}+z_{2}(1-3z_{3})}{1-z_{3}}+\epsilon^{2}\Big(2\tilde{s}_{12}(1-z_{3})-\tilde{s}_{23}(1-z_{1})\Big)\Bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{2m_{Q}^{4}}{s_{12}s_{13}}\Bigg[2\frac{(1-2z_{2})^{2}-z_{2}(1-z_{2}z_{3})}{(1-z_{2})(1-z_{3})}-\epsilon\frac{(1-2z_{2})(3-2z_{2}-z_{2}z_{3})}{(1-z_{2})(1-z_{3})}+\epsilon^{2}\Bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle+(2\leftrightarrow 3)~.

(48)

The splitting kernel involving two gluons, and a massive quark as parent parton, admits the following decomposition

\displaystyle\langle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g_{1}g_{2}Q_{3}}\rangle=C_{F}^{2}\langle\hat{P}_{g_{1}g_{2}Q_{3}}^{\rm{(0,ab)}}\rangle+C_{A}C_{F}\langle\hat{P}_{g_{1}g_{2}Q_{3}}^{\rm{(0,nab)}}\rangle~,

(49)

where

\displaystyle\langle\hat{P}_{g_{1}g_{2}Q_{3}}^{\rm{(0,ab)}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}^{2}z_{3}}{2\tilde{s}_{13}\tilde{s}_{23}}\left[\frac{1+z_{3}^{2}}{z_{1}z_{2}}-\epsilon\frac{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}}{z_{1}z_{2}}-\epsilon(1+\epsilon)\right]+\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}\bigg[\frac{z_{3}(1-z_{1})+(1-z_{2})^{3}}{z_{1}z_{2}}

\displaystyle-\epsilon(z_{1}^{2}+z_{1}z_{2}+z_{2}^{2})\frac{1-z_{2}}{z_{1}z_{2}}+\epsilon^{2}(1+z_{3})\bigg]+(1-\epsilon)\left[\epsilon-(1-\epsilon)\frac{\tilde{s}_{23}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{m_{Q}^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{23}}\bigg[\frac{2(z_{2}-2z_{3}+z_{2}^{2}+2z_{3}^{2}+3z_{2}z_{3})}{z_{1}z_{2}}-\frac{2(2z_{2}+4z_{3}-z_{1}z_{3})}{z_{1}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\tilde{s}_{123}}{\tilde{s}_{23}}\frac{1+z_{2}^{2}+z_{3}^{2}+2z_{2}z_{3}}{z_{1}}-\frac{\tilde{s}_{12}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}\frac{z_{2}(1-z_{3})+4z_{3}}{z_{2}}+2\epsilon z_{1}\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}}{\tilde{s}_{23}}

\displaystyle+\epsilon(1-z_{3})\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}\bigg]+\frac{4m_{Q}^{4}}{\tilde{s}_{23}^{2}}+\frac{4m_{Q}^{4}}{\tilde{s}_{13}\tilde{s}_{23}}\Bigg\}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)~,

(50)

and

\displaystyle\langle\hat{P}_{g_{1}g_{2}Q_{3}}^{\rm{(0,nab)}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{(1-\epsilon)\left(\frac{t_{12,3}^{2}}{4\tilde{s}_{12}^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)+\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}^{2}}{2\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{13}}\bigg[\frac{(1-z_{3})^{2}(1-\epsilon)+2z_{3}}{z_{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{z_{2}^{2}(1-\epsilon)+2(1-z_{2})}{1-z_{3}}\bigg]-\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}^{2}}{4\tilde{s}_{13}\tilde{s}_{23}}z_{3}\left[\frac{(1-z_{3})^{2}(1-\epsilon)+2z_{3}}{z_{1}z_{2}}+\epsilon(1-\epsilon)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}}{2\tilde{s}_{12}}\left[(1-\epsilon)\frac{z_{1}(2-2z_{1}+z_{1}^{2})-z_{2}(6-6z_{2}+z_{2}^{2})}{z_{2}(1-z_{3})}+2\epsilon\frac{z_{3}(z_{1}-2z_{2})-z_{2}}{z_{2}(1-z_{3})}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}}{2\tilde{s}_{13}}\bigg[(1-\epsilon)\frac{(1-z_{2})^{3}+z_{3}^{2}-z_{2}}{z_{2}(1-z_{3})}-\epsilon\left(\frac{2(1-z_{2})(z_{2}-z_{3})}{z_{2}(1-z_{3})}-z_{1}+z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{z_{3}(1-z_{1})+(1-z_{2})^{3}}{z_{1}z_{2}}+\epsilon(1-z_{2})\left(\frac{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}}{z_{1}z_{2}}-\epsilon\right)\bigg]-\frac{2m_{Q}^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{12}}\frac{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}}{z_{1}z_{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{m_{Q}^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{13}\tilde{s}_{23}}\left[\frac{\tilde{s}_{12}}{2}\frac{z_{2}+4z_{3}-z_{2}z_{3}}{z_{2}}+\tilde{s}_{13}\frac{2z_{2}+4z_{3}-z_{1}z_{3}}{z_{1}}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\tilde{s}_{123}(1-z_{3})\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{m_{Q}^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{23}}\bigg[\tilde{s}_{12}\frac{3z_{1}z_{2}^{2}+z_{1}^{2}z_{2}-2z_{1}^{2}z_{3}+2z_{2}^{2}z_{3}+4z_{2}^{3}}{z_{1}z_{2}(1-z_{3})}

\displaystyle+2(\tilde{s}_{13}z_{2}-\tilde{s}_{23}z_{1})\frac{z_{1}^{2}+z_{1}z_{2}+z_{2}^{2}}{z_{1}z_{2}(1-z_{3})}\bigg]-\frac{2m_{Q}^{4}}{\tilde{s}_{13}\tilde{s}_{23}}\Bigg\}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)~.

(51)

Finally, the splitting kernel with a gluon as a parent parton is decomposed as

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0),\mu\nu}_{g_{1}Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}}=C_{F}T_{R}\hat{P}^{\rm{(0,ab)},\mu\nu}_{g_{1}Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}}+C_{A}T_{R}\hat{P}^{\rm{(0,nab)},\mu\nu}_{g_{1}Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}}~,

(52)

where

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{\rm{(0,ab)},\mu\nu}_{g_{1}Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}}

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\nu}\bigg[2-\frac{2\tilde{s}_{123}\tilde{s}_{23}+(1-\epsilon)(\tilde{s}_{123}-\tilde{s}_{23})^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{13}}+2m_{Q}^{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}}{\tilde{s}_{12}^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}}{\tilde{s}_{13}^{2}}-\frac{2\tilde{s}_{23}}{\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{13}}\right)

\displaystyle+4m_{Q}^{4}\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{s}_{12}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{s}_{13}^{2}}\right)\bigg]+\frac{4\tilde{s}_{123}}{\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{13}}\left[\tilde{k}_{2}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{3}^{\nu}+\tilde{k}_{3}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{2}^{\nu}-(1-\epsilon)\tilde{k}_{1}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{1}^{\nu}\right]

\displaystyle+8m_{Q}^{2}\left[\frac{\tilde{k}_{2}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{2}^{\nu}}{\tilde{s}_{13}^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{k}_{3}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{3}^{\nu}}{\tilde{s}_{12}^{2}}\right]-\frac{8m_{Q}^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{13}}(1-\epsilon)\tilde{k}_{1}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{1}^{\nu}~,

(53)

and

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{\rm{(0,nab)},\mu\nu}_{g_{1}Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}}

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{-\frac{2g^{\mu\nu}}{s_{23}^{2}}\bigg[\frac{z_{3}\tilde{s}_{12}(z_{2}\tilde{s}_{13}-z_{3}\tilde{s}_{12})}{(1-z_{1})^{2}}+\frac{z_{2}(1-2z_{2})(2-2z_{2}-5z_{3})}{2z_{1}(1-z_{1})^{2}}\tilde{s}_{23}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{23}}{z_{1}(1-z_{1})^{2}}\Big(z_{2}+z_{3}-2z_{2}^{2}-3z_{3}^{2}-3z_{2}z_{3}+2z_{2}^{3}+3z_{3}^{3}+5z_{2}^{2}z_{3}+6z_{2}z_{3}^{2}\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{s_{23}^{2}}\bigg[\left(\frac{4z_{3}^{2}\tilde{s}_{123}}{z_{1}(1-z_{1})}-\frac{2(1-z_{2})\tilde{s}_{23}}{z_{1}}\right)\tilde{k}_{2}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{2}^{\nu}-\left(\frac{4z_{2}z_{3}\tilde{s}_{123}}{z_{1}(1-z_{1})}-\frac{\tilde{s}_{23}(1-z_{1})}{z_{1}}\right)\tilde{k}_{2}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{3}^{\nu}

\displaystyle+\epsilon\tilde{s}_{23}\tilde{k}_{1}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{1}^{\nu}\bigg]-4g^{\mu\nu}\frac{m_{Q}^{2}}{s_{23}^{2}}\bigg[\frac{2z_{2}-4z_{2}^{2}-3z_{2}z_{3}}{(1-z_{1})^{2}}\tilde{s}_{23}+\tilde{s}_{12}\bigg(\frac{z_{3}(z_{2}-z_{3})}{(1-z_{1})^{2}}+\frac{z_{1}}{1-z_{1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1-z_{1}}{z_{1}}\bigg)\bigg]+\frac{4m_{Q}^{2}}{s_{23}^{2}}\bigg[\!\left(\!2+\frac{z_{3}(z_{2}-z_{3})}{z_{1}(1-z_{1})}\!\right)2\tilde{k}_{2}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{2}^{\nu}+\left(\!2-\frac{(z_{2}-z_{3})^{2}}{z_{1}(1-z_{1})}\right)\tilde{k}_{2}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{3}^{\nu}-\epsilon\tilde{k}_{1}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{1}^{\nu}\bigg]

\displaystyle+4g^{\mu\nu}\frac{m_{Q}^{4}}{s_{23}^{2}}\left[1-\frac{z_{1}}{1-z_{1}}-\frac{z_{2}z_{3}}{(1-z_{1})^{2}}\right]+\frac{g^{\mu\nu}}{2s_{23}}\bigg[\frac{2-3z_{1}}{1-z_{1}}\frac{\tilde{s}_{23}^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}-\frac{z_{3}(1-2z_{1})}{z_{1}(1-z_{1})}\frac{\tilde{s}_{12}^{2}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}

\displaystyle+\frac{z_{2}\tilde{s}_{13}}{z_{1}(1-z_{1})}-\frac{1-2z_{2}}{z_{1}}\tilde{s}_{12}-\frac{1-3z_{2}-4z_{3}+2z_{2}^{2}+5z_{3}^{2}+7z_{2}z_{3}}{z_{1}(1-z_{1})}\frac{\tilde{s}_{12}\tilde{s}_{23}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1-2z_{2}-z_{3}+3z_{2}^{2}+4z_{3}^{2}+7z_{2}z_{3}}{z_{1}(1-z_{1})}\tilde{s}_{23}\bigg]+\frac{1}{s_{23}}\bigg[\left(\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}-\frac{1-z_{3}}{z_{1}}\right)2\tilde{k}_{3}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{3}^{\nu}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{z_{2}-z_{3}-z_{2}^{2}+z_{3}^{2}+2z_{2}z_{3}}{z_{1}(1-z_{1})}\frac{\tilde{s}_{123}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}-\frac{z_{3}}{z_{1}}\frac{\tilde{s}_{23}}{\tilde{s}_{13}}+\frac{1-z_{1}}{z_{1}}\right)\left(\tilde{k}_{2}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{3}^{\nu}+\tilde{k}_{3}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{2}^{\nu}\right)

6
Conclusions and outlook

In this article, we have presented the first calculation of triple-collinear splitting amplitudes and (un)polarized splitting kernels with massive partons at tree-level in QCD. We have carefully compared the kinematics of the collinear and quasi-collinear limits, and have provided very compact explicit expressions for all the possible processes involving QCD partons.

Although collinear singularities are strictly absent for splitting processes with massive particles, mass-dependent logarithmically enhanced terms emerge in scattering amplitudes from the quasi-collinear region. Since these contributions depend on ratios of masses to the typical energy scale of the process, they could lead to large corrections in the high-energy limit. So, in order to improve theoretical predictions it is necessary to use subtraction counter-terms involving massive splitting kernels. At NLO, the massive dipole formalism
Catani:2002hc
required double-collinear splittings to build the corresponding counter-terms. The massive triple-collinear splittings provided in this article allow to extend the collinear subtraction formalism to reach NNLO accuracy, taking into account parton mass effects. This might play a crucial role in collider phenomenology involving heavy quarks. For instance, in forward charm-quark production
Maciula:2022lzk
, there are no complete calculations that keeps track of the mass of the charm quark, which is expected to have an important phenomenological impact.

Also, having access to the full set of triple-collinear splitting kernels with massive partons paves the road for more precise parton shower generators. Up to now, their improvement relied mostly on the inclusion of higher-logarithmic corrections, since mass effects were always consider as sub-dominant. However, as we are reaching next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic accuracy or even higher-orders, mass effects start to compete and can no longer be neglected.

Finally, the knowledge of triple-collinear splitting processes with massive partons could play an important role in the consistent treatment of mass effects in parton densities evolution. For this purpose, higher-order corrections to the Altarelli-Parisi kernels of splitting functions, keeping the exact mass dependence, and generalising the DGLAP equations to the quasi-collinear limit are required. The results provided in this work constitute a very first step towards this direction, and might be relevant in sight of the current precision high-energy collider phenomenology program. An independent calculation of the quark-initiated massive triple-collinear splitting kernels has recently been presented in ref.
Craft:2023aew
, which is in agreement with our result.

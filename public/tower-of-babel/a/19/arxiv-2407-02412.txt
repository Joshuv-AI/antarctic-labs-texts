k k -Leaf Powers Cannot be Characterized by a Finite Set of Forbidden Induced Subgraphs for k ≥ 5 k\geq 5
Max Dupré la Tour, Manuel Lafond, Ndiamé Ndiaye, Adrian Vetta

Abstract
A graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) is a k k -leaf power if there is a tree T T whose leaves are the vertices of G G with the property that a pair of leaves u u and v v induce an edge in G G if and only if they are distance at most k k apart in T T . For k ≤ 4 k\leq 4 , it is known that there exists a finite set ℱ k \mathscr{F}_{k} of graphs such that the class ℒ ⁡ ( k ) \mathcal{L}(k) of k k -leaf power graphs is characterized as the set of strongly chordal graphs that do not contain any graph in ℱ k \mathscr{F}_{k} as an induced subgraph. We prove no such characterization holds for k ≥ 5 k\geq 5 . That is, for any k ≥ 5 k\geq 5 , there is no finite set ℱ k \mathscr{F}_{k} of graphs such that ℒ ⁡ ( k ) \mathcal{L}(k) is equivalent to the set of strongly chordal graphs that do not contain as an induced subgraph any graph in ℱ k \mathscr{F}_{k} .

3.3
The Interior Gadget

Lastly, we have the most complex construction, that of

Now we require the following lemma which, again, is a consequence of the 4-Point Condition.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
d(t,x_{1})\leq\min\left\{d(t,x_{2}),d(t,x_{3})\right\}
and
d(y,x_{1})>\max\left\{d(y,x_{2}),d(y,x_{3})\right\}
, then:

d(t,x_{1})+d(x_{2},x_{3})<d(t,x_{2})+d(x_{1},x_{3})=d(t,x_{3})+d(x_{1},x_{2})

Proof.

Assume that
d(t,x_{1})+d(x_{2},x_{3})\geq\max\left\{d(t,x_{2})+d(x_{1},x_{3}),d(t,x_{3})+d(x_{1},x_{2})\right\}
. Then, by using this assumption and our bound on
d(t,x_{1})
, we get:

\displaystyle d(x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{d(x_{1},x_{3})+(d(t,x_{2})-d(t,x_{1})),d(x_{1},x_{2})+(d(t,x_{3})-d(t,x_{1}))\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{d(x_{1},x_{3}),d(x_{1},x_{2})\right\}

Then, by combining this bound with our bound on
d(y,x_{1})
, we get:

d(y,x_{1})+d(x_{2},x_{3})>\max\left\{d(y,x_{2})+d(x_{1},x_{3}),d(y,x_{3})+d(x_{1},x_{2})\right\}

This contradicts Theorem
3.1
. This implies that the assumption is wrong, that is, we must have:

d(t,x_{1})+d(x_{2},x_{3})<\max\left\{d(t,x_{2})+d(x_{1},x_{3}),d(t,x_{3})+d(x_{1},x_{2})\right\}.

Now, assume without loss of generality that
d(t,x_{2})+d(x_{1},x_{3})\geq d(t,x_{3})+d(x_{1},x_{2})
. Then, by Theorem
3.1
, we must have
d(t,x_{2})+d(x_{1},x_{3})\leq\max\left\{d(t,x_{1})+d(x_{2},x_{3}),d(t,x_{3})+d(x_{1},x_{2})\right\}
. Since
d(t,x_{2})+d(x_{1},x_{3})>d(t,x_{1})+d(x_{2},x_{3})
, this implies that
d(t,x_{2})+d(x_{1},x_{3})\leq d(t,x_{3})+d(x_{1},x_{2})
. So we get that
d(t,x_{2})+d(x_{1},x_{3})
is bounded above and below by
d(t,x_{3})+d(x_{1},x_{2})
so they must be equal.

So we get:

d(t,x_{1})+d(x_{2},x_{3})<d(t,x_{2})+d(x_{1},x_{3})=d(t,x_{3})+d(x_{1},x_{2}).

This completes the proof.

We remark that this result does not immediately imply that there is no characterization for the entire class of linear leaf powers using chordal graphs and a finite number of forbidden induced subgraphs.

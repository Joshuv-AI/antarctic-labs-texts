Random sections of ℓ p \ell_{p} -ellipsoids, optimal recovery and Gelfand numbers of diagonal operators
Aicke Hinrichs, Joscha Prochno, Mathias Sonnleitner

Abstract
We study the circumradius of a random section of an ℓ p \ell_{p} -ellipsoid, 0 < p ≤ ∞ 0<p\leq\infty , and compare it with the minimal circumradius over all sections with subspaces of the same codimension. Our main result is an upper bound for random sections, which we prove using techniques from asymptotic geometric analysis if 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty and compressed sensing if 0 < p ≤ 1 0<p\leq 1 . This can be interpreted as a bound on the quality of random (Gaussian) information for the recovery of vectors from an ℓ p \ell_{p} -ellipsoid for which the radius of optimal information is given by the Gelfand numbers of a diagonal operator. In the case where the semiaxes decay polynomially and 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty , we conjecture that, as the amount of information increases, the radius of random information either decays like the radius of optimal information or is bounded from below by a constant, depending on whether the exponent of decay is larger than the critical value 1 − 1 p 1-\frac{1}{p} or not. If 1 ≤ p ≤ 2 1\leq p\leq 2 , we prove this conjecture by providing a matching lower bound. This extends the recent work of Hinrichs et al. [Random sections of ellipsoids and the power of random information, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc., 2021+] for the case p = 2 p=2 .



Keywords:
Diagonal operator, Gelfand numbers, Grassmannian manifold,
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoid, optimal recovery, random section.

1
Introduction, motivation and main results

The diameter of a section of a symmetric convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
with a (random) subspace has been an object of interest at least since the study of Gelfand numbers of operators between finite-dimensional Banach spaces
[
6
,
9
,
10
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
29
,
30
]
. These numbers measure the smallest circumradius of the intersection with a subspace of a fixed (co)dimension. Often we are not in a position to exhibit optimal subspaces and thus it seems reasonable to first try to understand intersections with typical subspaces. Along this way we are led to the study of the diameter of intersections with random subspaces which are uniformly distributed on the Grassmannian manifold with respect to the Haar probability measure.

Connected to the field of asymptotic geometric analysis, there is a large body of work on this topic
initiated by Giannopoulos and V. D. Milman in
[
7
,
8
]
with particular focus on subspace dimension proportional to the dimension of the body (see also
[
26
]
).
In subsequent work Litvak, Pajor and Tomczak-Jaegermann
[
22
]
have shown that on the scale of proportional subspaces typical intersections are not much larger than minimal intersections.
It is important to note that, as pointed out in
[
7
, Example 2.2]
, one cannot expect these bounds to be sharp in full generality, in particular not for ellipsoids with highly incomparable semi-axes.

Mendelson, Pajor and Tomczak-Jaegermann
[
23
]
studied the intimately related problem of approximate reconstruction of vectors from a symmetric convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
using random Gaussian measurements. Approximation using random information underlies the success of the field of compressed sensing, dealing with the reconstruction of sparse vectors (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
5
]
). Somewhat related is the approximation of functions using samples at random points, which is
studied in the context of learning theory (see, e.g., the book
[
24
]
) and information-based complexity
[
13
,
14
]
.
It is the latter, specifically the work
[
14
]
, that serves as further motivation for this paper.
There the effectiveness of Gaussian information for recovering vectors in an ellipsoid in the Euclidean norm has been studied, which is related to the approximation of functions with decaying generalized Fourier coefficients, e.g., Korobov spaces
[
20
]
. In a more geometric parlance, the main result of
[
14
]
is that the equivalent problem of determining whether the circumradius of a random section of the ellipsoid is close to minimal has a positive solution depending on the square-summability of its semiaxes. In a nutshell, we seek to extend these results and study random sections of generalized
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoids. Such ellipsoids have also been studied, for instance, in
[
16
]
with focus on the asymptotic volume distribution of sections of such ellipsoids as the dimension of the underlying space tends to infinity, and in
[
32
]
where it is shown that such ellipsoids are examples where Dudley’s integral bound for Gaussian processes is not sharp.

1.1
Radii of random sections and optimal recovery

We aim at understanding the circumradius, or equivalently the diameter, of random sections of generalized ellipsoids and, in particular, whether it is comparable to the minimal circumradius of all sections of the same dimension or not. Given
0<p\leq\infty
our object of interest is the
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoid

\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\|(x_{j}/\sigma_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\|_{p}\leq 1\}\quad\text{with semiaxes}\quad\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{m}>0,

where
\|x\|_{p}:=(|x_{1}|^{p}+\cdots+|x_{m}|^{p})^{1/p}
and
\|x\|_{\infty}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}|x_{j}|
for
x=(x_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
denote the standard
\ell_{p}
-(quasi-)norms with unit balls
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{m}
.
To be precise, if
E
is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we denote the circumradius of the section
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap E
by

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},E):=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap E}\|x\|_{2}.

Note that for
0<p<1
the set
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
is not convex but still the unit ball of a quasi-normed space. Before we present our results, we introduce the closely related problem of recovery using linear information.

Assume we want to learn an unknown
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
given the information that
x\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
, that is, we have some control over the decay of the coordinates of
x
. Further, suppose we are given the linear information
N_{n}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
N_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
and
n\,(<m)
can be considerably smaller than
m
. The best we can do using the given knowledge about
x
can be measured by the worst-case error (also known as the radius of the information
N_{n}
)

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},N_{n}):=\inf_{\varphi}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}}\|x-\varphi(N_{n}x)\|_{2},

where the recovery mapping
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
can be an arbitrary mapping allowed to depend on
N_{n}
. The abuse of notation will be justified in a moment.
It follows from elementary results (see, e.g.,
[
25
, Lemma 4.3]
) that, if
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
is convex, that is, if
p\geq 1
,

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},N_{n})=\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},\ker N_{n}),

(1)

where the kernel
\ker N_{n}
is an
(m-n)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and so of codimension
n
, i.e., it belongs to the Grassmannian manifold
\mathcal{G}_{m,m-n}
, if we assume the rows of
N_{n}
to be linearly independent. This is the reason we call
\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},N_{n})
the radius of the information
N_{n}
. If
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
is non-convex, that is, if
0<p<1
, then (
1
) holds up to a factor of 2, see, e.g., Lemma
3
. Random information will be given by a random matrix
G_{n,m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
with i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries. It follows from rotation invariance that the distribution of
\ker G_{n,m}
is equal to the Haar probability measure on the Grassmannian
\mathcal{G}_{m,m-n}
. Thus, we may define the circumradius of the intersection of
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
with a random subspace of codimension
n
via the random quantity

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m})=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap E_{n}}\|x\|_{2},

where
E_{n}:=\ker G_{n,m}
. Obviously,
\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m})\geq\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n)
for each realization with the minimal radius

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n):=\inf_{E_{n}}\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},E_{n}),\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

the infimum ranging over all subspaces
E_{n}
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
of codimension
n
.
It is natural to ask to what extent the converse inequality holds true or, in other words, what is the radius of a typical intersection of
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
by a random subspace?

The special case
p=2
has been dealt with in
[
14
]
, where it has been shown that

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{2,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m})\leq C\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big(\sum_{j\geq\lfloor n/4\rfloor}\sigma_{j}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(2)

holds with exponentially high probability (in
n
), where
C\in(0,\infty)
is an absolute constant. Further, if the semiaxes
\sigma=(\sigma_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy
\|\sigma\|_{2}=\infty
, then, with an absolute constant
C\in(0,\infty)
,

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{2,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m})\geq C\sigma_{1}

with exponentially high probability, provided that
m\,(>n)
is large enough compared to
n
. Since
\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{2,\sigma}^{m},n)=\sigma_{n+1}
,
there is a dichotomy for the usefulness of Gaussian information compared to optimal information or, in more geometric parlance, the circumradius of a random section compared to the minimal one. We seek to extend this result to the class of
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoids
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
with
p\neq 2
.

1.2
Radii of random sections – main results

We are not able to answer the above question in full for the general case, in part due to the fact that the minimal radius
\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n)
is not known exactly for
1\leq p<2
, see the
Appendix
for more on optimal sections. This is one of the reasons why, apart from the following two theorems, we also present results for the important case of polynomially decaying semiaxes.

Here and in what follows, for two non-negative reals
a_{\alpha}
and
b_{\alpha}
depending on some parameter
\alpha
from an index set
I
, we write
a_{\alpha}\lesssim b_{\alpha}
, or equivalently
b_{\alpha}\gtrsim a_{\alpha}
, if there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that
a_{\alpha}\leq C\,b_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in I
. If both
a_{\alpha}\lesssim b_{\alpha}
and
a_{\alpha}\gtrsim b_{\alpha}
hold, we write
a_{\alpha}\asymp b_{\alpha}
. If the constant may depend on some parameter
\beta
, we shall write
a_{\alpha}\lesssim_{\beta}b_{\alpha}
and
a_{\alpha}\gtrsim_{\beta}b_{\alpha}
instead or, if both estimates hold,
a_{\alpha}\asymp_{\beta}b_{\alpha}
. As usual, given
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we shall denote the Hölder conjugate of
p
by
p^{*}
so that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{*}}=1
.

The first result provides an upper bound on the radius of random information with high probability and is in the spirit of the results obtained in
[
14
]
.

Theorem A
.

For all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq n<m
, we have

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m})\lesssim\begin{cases}n^{-1/2}\sup\limits_{k\leq j\leq m}\sigma_{j}\sqrt{\log(j)+1}&:\,p=1,\\
\sqrt{p^{*}}n^{-1/2}\Big(\sum\limits_{j=k}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{p^{*}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}&:\,1<p\leq\infty,\end{cases}

with probability at least
1-c_{1}\exp(-c_{2}n)
, where
c_{1},c_{2}\in(0,\infty)
are absolute constants, and
k\asymp n
for
p=1
while
k\asymp\frac{n}{p^{*}}
for
p>1
.

The proof relies on a famous theorem of Gordon
[
11
]
on subspaces escaping through a mesh, for which we first need to control the mean width of ‘rounded’ versions of our ellipsoids arising from intersections with a Euclidean ball of a suitable radius. The idea of cutting away the peaky regions of a convex body in this way to obtain improved bounds on its mean width is well known, see for example
[
23
, Section 2]
. Then, in order to bound this quantity for the
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoids, we adapt an approach already used in
[
14
]
. There the main approach had been a random matrix one, but it seems this approach cannot be adapted to our situation without loosing something compared to Theorem
A
.

Remark 1
.

Theorem
A
extends the upper bound (
2
). It shows that if
\|\sigma\|_{p^{*}}<\infty
we can expect the radius of random sections to decay at least as fast as
n^{-1/2}
. This also gives a bound on the minimal radius, see Corollary
3
formulated in terms of Gelfand numbers.

Remark 2
.

In the context of suprema of Gaussian processes we want to mention that
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoids with slowly decaying semiaxes are examples where Dudley’s upper bound is loose. This has been observed, for instance, by van Handel in
[
32
]
. For more information, we refer to the discussion at the end of Section
2
, where we exhibit a bound also depending on
p
, which is not present in
[
32
]
.

Employing methods commonly used in the field of compressed sensing, e.g., in a work of Foucart, Pajor, Rauhut and Ullrich
[
4
]
on the Gelfand widths of
\ell_{p}
-balls in the quasi-Banach regime
0<p\leq 1
, we deduce the following upper bound for the radius of random information when
0<p\leq 1
and the semiaxes have polynomial decay.

Theorem B
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
and
\sigma_{j}=j^{-\lambda}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, for some
\lambda>0
. Then there exist constants
C,D\in(0,\infty)
such that, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and all
1\leq n<m
with

n\geq D\log({\rm e}m/n),

we have

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},\ker G_{n,m})\lesssim_{p}\Big(\frac{\log({\rm e}m/n)}{n}\Big)^{\lambda+1/p-1/2}

with probability at least
1-2\exp(-Cn)
.

In fact, we shall prove a slightly more general result, Theorem
C
below, which also yields new bounds on Gelfand numbers of diagonal operators in the quasi-Banach regime (see Corollary
4
in the
Appendix
).

1.3
The power of random information for polynomial decay – discussion

In the following we discuss the consequences of our results for ellipsoids with polynomially decaying semiaxes
\sigma_{j}=j^{-\lambda}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, for some
\lambda>0
. It turns out that, at least when
1\leq p\leq 2
, we can show a dichotomy for the radius of a random section in comparison to the minimal section. Roughly speaking, we have the following equivalence, which will be made precise by the conjecture at the end of this subsection,

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m})\asymp\begin{cases}\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n)&:\lambda>\frac{1}{p^{*}},\\
\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},0)&:\lambda<\frac{1}{p^{*}}.\end{cases}

To illustrate this, we first provide known results on the minimal radius
\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n)
, which we deduce from results on Gelfand numbers of diagonal operators (see the
Appendix
for the latter).

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. The behavior of the minimal radius is known exactly when
p\geq 2
but can only be deduced up to subpolynomial factors when
1\leq p<2
.
To make this precise, we define the rate of polynomial decay (in
n
) of an infinite array
a=(a_{n,m})_{m\in\mathbb{N},1\leq n<m}
of real numbers by

\mathrm{decay}(a):=\sup\{\varrho\geq 0:\exists C\in(0,\infty)\text{ with }a_{n,m}\leq Cn^{-\varrho}\text{ for all }m\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }1\leq n<m\}.

Now, let
\frac{1}{s}:=(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})_{+}:=\max\{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p},0\}
and
\sigma_{j}=j^{-\lambda}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, for some
\lambda>\frac{1}{s}
. The minimal radius does not decay if
\lambda\leq\frac{1}{s}
. We have, see Corollary
5
,

\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n))=\begin{cases}\lambda\cdot\frac{p^{*}}{2}&:\,1\leq p<2\text{ and }\lambda<\frac{1}{p^{*}},\\
\lambda+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}&:\,\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3)

We can now compare this with our bounds on the radii of random sections. Theorem
A
gives for the above choice of
\sigma
, for any
\lambda>\frac{1}{p^{*}}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq n<m
,

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m})\lesssim_{p,\lambda}\begin{cases}n^{-\lambda-1/2}\sqrt{\log n}:&p=1,\\
n^{-\lambda+1/2-1/p}:&1<p\leq\infty,\end{cases}

(4)

with probability
1-c_{1}\exp(-c_{2}n)
.
This means, if
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\lambda>\frac{1}{p^{*}}
, then the polynomial decay rate of random information is, by (
3
), equal to

\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m}))=\lambda+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}=\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n)).

(5)

Similar to the above,
\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m}))
is defined to be the supremum over all
\varrho\geq 0
such there exist
C,C_{1},C_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m})\leq Cn^{-\varrho}

holds with probability at least
1-C_{1}\exp(-C_{2}n)
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq n<m
.
Thus, the bound of Theorem
A
on the decay rate is optimal for
\lambda>\frac{1}{p^{*}}
. If
\lambda\leq\frac{1}{p^{*}}
, however, it does not yield a useful result. Instead, we have a lower bound on the radius of random information, Proposition
1
below, which shows that if
1<p\leq 2
the radius of random information does not decay if
m
is large enough.

Proposition 1
.

Let
1<p\leq 2
and
\sigma_{j}=j^{-\lambda},j\in\mathbb{N}
, for some
\lambda
with
0<\lambda<\frac{1}{p^{*}}
. Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1),

n\in\mathbb{N}
and
m>n
large enough, we have

\mathbb{P}\Big[\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m})\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big]\geq 1-\varepsilon.

In other words, if
1<p\leq 2
and the semiaxes decay too slowly compared to
\frac{1}{p^{*}}
, random information is asymptotically as good as no information at all.

Remark 3
.

The boundary case
\lambda=\frac{1}{p^{*}}
is not covered by Proposition
1
. However, its statement remains true if
\sigma_{j}=j^{-1/p^{*}}a_{j},j\in\mathbb{N},
with
a_{j}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
. This can be deduced from Proposition
4
in Section

, from which Proposition
1
follows.

We obtain the following corollary on the polynomial decay.

.

Let

and
\sigma_{j}=j^{-\lambda},j\in\mathbb{N}
, for some

. Then

\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m}))=0.

Above the line

, where

we just deduced that Theorem

yields that random information is optimal up to an additional logarithmic factor if

. The decay rate is equal to
\lambda+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}
, see (

In the square, that is for

Conjecture
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\sigma_{j}=j^{-\lambda},j\in\mathbb{N},
with
\lambda>\frac{1}{s}=(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})_{+}

\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m}))=\begin{cases}\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n))&:\lambda>\frac{1}{p^{*}},\\

1.

2.

As a matter of fact, it seems reasonable to conjecture that
\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m}))=\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n))
as long as

, while
\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m}))=0
whenever
\|(\sigma_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|_{p^{*}}=\infty
. We leave this as an open problem for future investigation.

2
An upper bound via an
M^{*}
-estimate – the case
1\leq p\leq\infty

In this section, we will prove Theorem
A
. Our approach is based on estimates on the mean width of the intersection of the
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoid
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
with a Euclidean ball, which we obtain using Gordon’s
M^{*}
-estimate.

2.1
An
M^{*}
-estimate for
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m}

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a convex body and
h_{K}:\mathbb{S}^{m-1}\to\mathbb{R}
,
u\mapsto\sup_{y\in K}\langle u,y\rangle
be its support function. The (half) mean width of
K
is given by

M^{*}(K):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}h_{K}(u)\,{\rm d}\sigma^{m-1}(u),

where
\mathbb{S}^{m-1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1\}
is the Euclidean unit sphere and
\sigma^{m-1}
the normalized surface measure on it. Let
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots
be independent standard Gaussian random variables.
Then it is known that the mean width can be expressed through the expected supremum of a suitable Gaussian process (see, e.g.,
[
1
, Lemma 9.1.3]
), namely,

M^{*}(K)=\frac{1}{c_{m}}\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in K}\sum_{j=1}^{m}t_{j}g_{j},

(6)

where
c_{m}\asymp\sqrt{m}
. We shall use Gordon’s theorem on subspaces escaping through a mesh
[
11
]
in the form stated in
[
1
, Theorem 9.3.8]
with
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
there.

Proposition 2
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a convex body containing the origin in its interior. For any
1\leq n<m
there exists a subset of the Grassmannian
\mathcal{G}_{m,m-n}
with Haar measure at least
1-\frac{7}{2}\exp(-\frac{1}{72}a_{n}^{2})
such that for any subspace
E_{n}
in this set and all
x\in K\cap E_{n}
we have

\left\|x\right\|_{2}\leq 2\frac{a_{m}}{a_{n}}M^{*}(K),

where, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

a_{k}:=\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}g_{i}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=\frac{\sqrt{2}\Gamma((k+1)/2)}{\Gamma(k/2)}\asymp\sqrt{k}.

(7)

We first bound
M^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m})
, where
\varrho>0
will be chosen suitably later, and then apply Proposition
2
to
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m}
and translate the result to our setting. First, we present an elementary estimate for
\ell_{q}
-norms of structured Gaussian random vectors.

Lemma 1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq q<\infty
. If
b=(b_{j})_{j=1}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
X=(b_{j}g_{j})_{j=1}^{k}
with independent standard Gaussian random variables
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
, then

\gamma_{1}\left\|b\right\|_{q}\leq\mathbb{E}\left\|X\right\|_{q}\leq\gamma_{q}\left\|b\right\|_{q},\quad\text{where }\quad\gamma_{q}:=\big(\mathbb{E}\left|g_{1}\right|^{q}\big)^{1/q}\asymp\sqrt{q}.

Further,

\mathbb{E}\left\|X\right\|_{\infty}\asymp\sup_{1\leq j\leq k}b_{j}^{*}\sqrt{\log(j)+1},

where
(b_{j}^{*})_{j=1}^{k}
is the non-increasing rearrangement of
(|b_{j}|)_{j=1}^{k}
.

Proof.

For
1\leq q<\infty
the upper bound follows from Jensen’s inequality and the lower bound follows from
\left\|\mathbb{E}X^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{q}^{k}}\leq\mathbb{E}\left\|X^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{q}^{k}}=\mathbb{E}\left\|X\right\|_{\ell_{q}^{k}}
, where
X^{\prime}=(|b_{j}g_{j}|)_{j=1}^{k}
. The asymptotics for
q=\infty
are taken from
[
31
, Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4]
.
∎

We will combine this with (
6
) to estimate the mean width of the intersection as stated in the following proposition. A similar approach was used in
[
12
]
for
\ell_{p}
-balls.

Proposition 3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
0\leq k<m
and
\varrho>0
,

M^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m})\lesssim\begin{cases}m^{-1/2}\Big(\varrho\sqrt{k}+\sup\limits_{k+1\leq j\leq m}\sigma_{j}\sqrt{\log(j)+1}\Big)&:\,p=1,\\
\sqrt{p^{*}}m^{-1/2}\Big(\varrho\sqrt{k}+\big(\sum\limits_{j=k+1}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{p^{*}}\big)^{1/p^{*}}\Big)&:\,1<p\leq\infty.\end{cases}

Proof.

We shall use the representation (
6
) and first bound the supremum. For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
y\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m}
, it follows from Hölder’s inequality that, for every
0\leq k<m
,

\langle x,y\rangle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left|x_{j}y_{j}\right|+\sum_{j=k+1}^{m}\left|x_{j}y_{j}\right|\leq\varrho\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\sum_{j=k+1}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{p^{*}}\left|x_{j}\right|^{p^{*}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}},

where the first sum is empty if
k=0
.
Combining this estimate with Lemma
1
, we obtain that if
p>1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{y\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}y_{j}g_{j}\leq\varrho\,a_{k}+\gamma_{p^{*}}\Big(\sum_{j=k+1}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{p^{*}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}.

By the previously stated asympotics for
a_{k}
in (
7
) and
\gamma_{p^{*}}
in Lemma
1
, we obtain the statement for
p>1
.

If
p=1
, then we deduce from Lemma
1
that, for some suitable
C\in(0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{E}\,\sup_{y\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}y_{j}g_{j}\leq\varrho\sqrt{k}+C\sup_{k+1\leq j\leq m}\sigma_{j}\sqrt{\log(j)+1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

2.2
The proof of Theorem
A

With the
M^{*}
-estimates on rounded versions of our ellipsoids from the previous subsection, we are now ready to prove the upper bound on the radius of random information.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

It follows from Gordon’s
M^{*}
-estimate (Proposition
2
) applied to the convex body
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\rho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m}
that, with probability as claimed, a random subspace
E_{n}
of codimension
n
chosen uniformly according to the Haar probability on
\mathcal{G}_{m,m-n}
satisfies

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m},E_{n})=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m}\cap E_{n}}\left\|x\right\|_{2}\leq 2\frac{a_{m}}{a_{n}}M^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m}).

We start with the case
p>1
. Inserting the bound obtained in Proposition
3
, we obtain a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that, for any
0\leq k<m
and
1\leq n<m
,

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m},E_{n})\leq C\frac{\sqrt{p^{*}}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big(\varrho\sqrt{k}+\Big(\sum_{j=k+1}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{p^{*}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}\Big).

First, let
\frac{n}{p^{*}}>4C^{2}
. Setting
\varrho:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\Big(\sum_{j=k+1}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{p^{*}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}
with
k:=c\frac{n}{p^{*}}
, where the constant
c\in(0,\infty)
is chosen (sufficiently small) such that
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq k<m
and

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m},E_{n})<\varrho

and so in particular that

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},E_{n})<\varrho

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq n<m
. The latter is so because a set which has circumradius smaller than
\varrho
when intersected with
\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m}
must necessarily have itself circumradius smaller than
\varrho
. Noting that the kernel of a Gaussian random matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
is uniformly distributed on the Grassmannian
\mathcal{G}_{m,m-n}
and that

\varrho\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{p^{*}}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big(\sum_{j=k+1}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{p^{*}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}},

proves the result for
p>1
in this case. If
\frac{n}{p^{*}}\leq 4C^{2}
, then let
k=0
and let
\varrho>0
be large enough such that
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\subset\varrho\mathbb{B}_{2}^{m}
and thus

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},E_{n})\leq C\frac{\sqrt{p^{*}}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{p^{*}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}.

In both cases,
k+1\asymp\frac{n}{p^{*}}
. The proof for
p=1
is carried out analogously.
∎

We conclude this section by stating a bound on the supremum of a Gaussian process indexed by vectors in an
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoid. The result can be read off from the proof of Proposition
3
. In view of the dependence on the parameter
p
, it improves upon a bound of van Handel in
[
32
]
.

Corollary 2
.

For all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\sup_{y\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}g_{j}y_{j}\lesssim\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{1\leq j\leq m}\sigma_{j}\sqrt{\log(j)+1}&:\,p=1,\\
\sqrt{p^{*}}\big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{p^{*}}\big)^{1/p^{*}}&:\,1<p\leq\infty.\end{cases}

In
[
32
]
van Handel deduced this result for
1\leq p<\infty
with an unspecified constant from the majorizing measure theorem and noted in
[
32
, Remark 3.4]
that his approach is not sufficiently accurate to recover the correct behavior in
p
. In Corollary
2
, we obtain an upper bound on the behavior in
p
and thus complement his result.

Employing estimates for entropy numbers of diagonal operators (see, e.g.,
[
32
]
), it can be deduced from Corollary
2
that the ellipsoid
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
with semiaxes satisfying

\sigma\in\ell_{p^{*}}\text{ but }\sigma\not\in\ell_{p^{*},1}\text{ for }1<p<\infty\quad\text{or}\quad\quad\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sigma_{j}\sqrt{\log(j)+1}<\infty\text{ but }\sigma\not\in\ell_{\infty,1}

is an example, where Dudley’s bound fails to be sharp if the dimension becomes large. Here,
\ell_{p,q}
is a Lorentz space as defined in the
Appendix
.

3
An upper bound via compressed sensing techniques – the case
0<p<1

In this section we prove Theorem
B
using techniques from compressed sensing in the spirit of Foucart, Pajor, Rauhut and Ullrich
[
4
]
who have given upper and lower bounds for the Gelfand widths of
\ell_{p}
-balls in
\ell_{q}
with
0<p\leq 1
and
p<q\leq 2
. They build upon work by Donoho
[
3
]
and others. Our proof is an extension to
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoids. Before we present it, we shall explain some of the relevant concepts used in compressed sensing for the recovery of sparse vectors. We refer the reader to the monograph
[
5
]
for more information.

3.1
Elements from compressed sensing and bounds on the best
s
-term approximation

Let
m,s\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq s\leq m
and let
0<p\leq 1
. A vector
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called
s
-sparse if at most
s
of its coordinates are non-zero. The error of best
s
-term approximation of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
in the
\ell_{p}
-(quasi-)norm is

\sigma_{s}(x)_{p}:=\inf\{\|x-z\|_{p}:z\text{ is }s\text{-sparse}\}.

Given the information
N_{n}x=y
, where
N_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, sparse vectors can be reconstructed via
\ell_{p}
-minimization, that is,

\Delta_{p}(y):=\text{arg min}\|z\|_{p}\quad\text{subject to }N_{n}z=y.

Note that
\Delta_{p}
is a mapping from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
which depends on
N_{n}
. If the matrix
N_{n}
satisfies the restricted isometry property with a small restricted isometry constant
\delta_{2s}(N_{n})
of order
2s
, which is the smallest
\delta>0
such that

(1-\delta)\|x\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|Ax\|_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\delta)\|x\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for all }2s\text{-sparse }x\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

then
s
-sparse vectors
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
can be recovered exactly, i.e.,
x=\Delta_{p}(N_{n}x)
. It is widely known that Gaussian matrices satisfy this with high probability. See, for example, Theorem 9.2 in
[
5
]
, which we adapt in the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

For every
\delta\in(0,1)
there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that
\delta_{s}(n^{-1/2}G_{n,m})\leq\delta
with probability at least
1-2\exp(-C_{2}n)
provided that

n\geq C_{1}s\log({\rm e}m/s)

for
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

We will prove a more general version of Theorem
B
, where
q
will be allowed to be smaller than
2
. To this end, we introduce a notation for the radius of a section of
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
measured in the
\ell_{q}
-(quasi-)norm,
0<q\leq\infty
. Given any subspace
E_{n}
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with codimension
n
, we define

\mathrm{rad}_{q}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},E_{n}):=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}\cap E_{n}}\|x\|_{q}.

The following extension of the equality (
1
) to the quasi-Banach space setting will be useful. It is the analogue of
[
4
, Proposition 1.2]
for individual matrices/subspaces. For convenience we provide a short proof.

Lemma 3
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be such that
K=-K
and
K+K\subset C_{K}K
for some
C_{K}\geq 2
. Further, let
\|\cdot\|_{X}
be a quasi-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that for some
C_{X}\in(0,\infty)
and all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
we have
\|x+y\|_{X}\leq C_{X}(\|x\|_{X}+\|y\|_{X})
. Then

C_{X}^{-1}\,\mathrm{rad}_{X}(K,\ker N_{n})\leq\inf_{\varphi}\sup_{x\in K}\|x-\varphi(N_{n}x)\|_{X}\leq C_{K}\,\mathrm{rad}_{X}(K,\ker N_{n})

for all
N_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, where
\mathrm{rad}_{X}(K,E):=\sup_{x\in K\cap E}\|x\|_{X}
for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the infimum runs over all mappings
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Proof.

For the lower bound take
\varphi
arbitrary. For any
x\in K\cap\ker N_{n}
also
-x\in K\cap\ker N_{n}
and

\|x-\varphi(0)\|_{X}\geq C_{X}^{-1}\|x\|_{X}\quad\text{ or }\quad\|-x-\varphi(0)\|_{X}\geq C_{X}^{-1}\|x\|_{X}

(8)

holds. Moreover, the symmetry of
K\cap\ker N_{n}
implies

\sup_{x\in K}\|x-\varphi(N_{n}x)\|_{X}\geq\sup_{x\in K\cap\ker N_{n}}\max\{\|x-\varphi(0)\|_{X},\|-x-\varphi(0)\|_{X}\}.

Together with (
8
) this proves the lower bound.

For the upper bound we specify a map
\varphi
by
\varphi(y)=z
for any
y\in N_{n}(K)
, where
z\in K
with
N_{n}z=y
is arbitrary. Then

\sup_{x\in K}\|x-\varphi(N_{n}x)\|_{X}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},x_{2}\in K\\
N_{n}x_{1}=N_{n}x_{2}\end{subarray}}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{X}\leq\sup_{x\in C_{K}K\cap\ker N_{n}}\|x\|_{X}

since
x_{1}-x_{2}\in C_{K}K
if
x_{1},x_{2}\in K
and
N_{n}(x_{1}-x_{2})=0
if
N_{n}x_{1}=N_{n}x_{2}
. The fact that
C_{K}\,\mathrm{rad}_{X}(K,\ker N_{n})=\mathrm{rad}_{X}(C_{K}\,K,\ker N_{n})
concludes the proof.
∎

We will use this together with the following lemma on best sparse approximation of vectors in an
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoid. For
\ell_{q}
-approximation of vectors in
\ell_{p}
-balls by sparse vectors it is known that, for
0<p\leq q\leq\infty
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}_{p}^{m}}\sigma_{s}(x)_{q}\asymp_{p}s^{1/q-1/p}

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq s\leq m
(see, e.g.,
[
33
]
). If
p=q
, the approximation error cannot be expected to decay, whereas for
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoids we have the following lemma for the special case of polynomially decaying
\sigma
. The proof is an adaption of the proof for
\ell_{p}
-balls.

Lemma 4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<p\leq\infty
and
\sigma_{j}=j^{-\lambda}
,
1\leq j\leq m
, for some
\lambda>0
. Then, for all
1\leq s\leq m/2
,

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}}\sigma_{s}(x)_{p}\asymp_{p,\lambda}s^{-\lambda}

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
. Then

\sigma_{s}(x)_{p}\leq\Big(\sum_{j=s+1}^{m}(x_{j}^{*})^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

where
(x_{j}^{*})_{j=1}^{m}
is the non-increasing rearrangement of the moduli of the coordinates of
x
. We have

1\geq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{|x_{j}|^{p}}{\sigma_{j}^{p}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{(x_{j}^{*})^{p}}{\sigma_{\pi(j)}^{p}}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(x_{k}^{*})^{p}}{\sigma_{\pi(j)}^{p}},

for any
1\leq k\leq m
, where
\pi:\{1,\ldots,m\}\to\{1,\ldots,m\}
is a suitable permutation. Thus,

(x_{k}^{*})^{p}\leq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sigma_{\pi(j)}^{p}}\Big)^{-1}\leq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}^{p}}\Big)^{-1}\asymp_{p,\lambda}k^{-\lambda p-1}.

Inserting this bound above yields

\sigma_{s}(x)_{p}\lesssim_{p,\lambda}\Big(\sum_{j=s+1}^{m}k^{-\lambda p-1}\Big)^{1/p}\asymp_{p,\lambda}s^{-\lambda}.

The lower bound is achieved by a vector on the boundary of
\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}
having its support on the first
2s
coordinates and equal entries on these.
∎

3.2
The proof of Theorem
B

With the results of the previous subsection at our disposal, we are now prepared to prove the following generalization of Theorem
B
.

Theorem C
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
and
p<q\leq 2
. Assume that
\sigma_{j}=j^{-\lambda}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, for some
\lambda>0
. Then there exist constants
C,D\in(0,\infty)
such that, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and all
1\leq n<m
with

n\geq D\log({\rm e}m/n),

we have

\mathrm{rad}_{q}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},\ker G_{n,m})\lesssim_{p,q}\Big(\frac{\log({\rm e}m/n)}{n}\Big)^{\lambda+1/p-1/q}

with probability at least
1-2\exp(-Cn)
.

Proof.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq n<m
and
N_{n}:=n^{-1/2}G_{n,m}
. By Lemma
3
, for all realizations,

\mathrm{rad}_{q}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},\ker G_{n,m})\leq 2^{1/q}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}}\|x-\Delta_{p}(N_{n}x)\|_{q},

(9)

where we specified
\varphi(y)=\Delta_{p}(n^{-1/2}y)=
arg min
\|z\|_{p}
subject to
N_{n}z=n^{-1/2}y
for
y=G_{n,m}x
.

We follow the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
in order to obtain an upper bound. To this end, let
D\in(0,\infty)
be large enough such that

D/2>{\rm e}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{D/2}{1+\log(D/2)}>C_{1},

where
C_{1}\in(0,\infty)
is the constant from Lemma
2
with
\delta=1/3
. Also choose
s:=\lfloor n/D\log({\rm e}m/n)\rfloor
. Then, if
n\geq D\log({\rm e}m/n)
it holds that
n>C_{1}(2s)\log({\rm e}m/2s)
. By Lemma
2
the matrix
N_{n}
satisfies
\delta_{2s}(N_{n})\leq 1/3
with probability at least
1-2\exp(-C_{2}n)
. It follows, see (3.5) and (3.6) in
[
4
]
, that there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that with the same probability,

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}}\|x-\Delta_{p}(N_{n}x)\|_{q}\leq C\Big(\frac{\log({\rm e}m/n)}{n}\Big)^{1/p-1/q}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}}\sigma_{s}(x)_{p}.

With Lemma
4
the proof is complete if we can show that
s\leq m/2
. Indeed, since the function
n\mapsto n/\log({\rm e}m/n)
is increasing for
1\leq n\leq m
, we have
s\leq m/D<m/2{\rm e}
.

∎

If
n
is too small for Theorem
C
to apply, that is,
n<D\log({\rm e}m/n)
, we only have the trivial pointwise bound

\mathrm{rad}_{q}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},\ker G_{n,m})\leq\mathrm{rad}_{q}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m})\leq\sigma_{1}

by means of
\|\cdot\|_{q}\leq\|\cdot\|_{p}
.

Remark 4
.

Let us note that the proof does not work in the case where
p>1
as can already be seen in
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
. Moreover, in the case
p=1
the bound derived from Theorem
C
is worse than the bound given by Theorem
A
already for
m\gtrsim n^{2}
, i.e., for small codimension. Nonetheless, if
m
is proportional to
n
, the bound from Theorem
C
improves upon (
4
) obtained from Theorem
A
.

Remark 5
.

Theorem
C
provides a bound on Gelfand numbers of diagonal operators in the quasi-Banach regime, see Corollary
4
in the
Appendix
.

4
A lower bound – the case
1<p\leq 2

We use the following lemma from
[
14
, Lemma 25]
to prove the lower bound of Proposition
1
for slowly decaying semiaxes in the case of
1<p\leq 2
.

Lemma 5
.

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
it holds that, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq n<m
,

\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup\big\{x_{1}^{2}:\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1,G_{n,m}x=0\big\}\geq 1-\frac{n}{\varepsilon m}\Big]\geq 1-\varepsilon.

From this we can now deduce the lower bound as presented in Proposition
1
. We prove a slightly more general bound holding not just for polynomially decaying semiaxes. Plugging in semiaxes of polynomial decay then proves Proposition
1
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
1<p\leq 2
. Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq n<m
with
n\leq\varepsilon\sigma_{m}^{2}m^{2/p^{*}}
, we have

\mathbb{P}\Big[\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},G_{n,m})\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}}{1+\sigma_{1}}\Big]\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Proof.

By Lemma
5
, with probability at least
1-\varepsilon
, we find
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with

x_{1}^{2}\geq 1-\frac{n}{\varepsilon m},\quad\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1,\quad\text{and}\quad G_{n,m}x=0.

We estimate

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|x_{j}\right|^{p}}{\sigma_{j}^{p}}\Big)^{1/p}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}+\Big(\sum_{j=2}^{m}\frac{\left|x_{j}\right|^{p}}{\sigma_{j}^{p}}\Big)^{1/p}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{m}}\Big(\sum_{j=2}^{m}\left|x_{j}\right|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

and by means of Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\frac{1}{\sigma_{m}}\Big(\sum_{j=2}^{m}\left|x_{j}\right|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq\frac{m^{1/p-1/2}}{\sigma_{m}}\Big(\sum_{j=2}^{m}x_{j}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=\frac{m^{1/p-1/2}}{\sigma_{m}}\big(1-x_{1}^{2}\big)^{1/2}.

Since
1-x_{1}^{2}\leq\frac{n}{\varepsilon m}
, we have

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|x_{j}\right|^{p}}{\sigma_{j}^{p}}\Big)^{1/p}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}+1

if
n\leq\varepsilon m^{2p^{*}}\sigma_{m}^{2}
. In this case, we can normalize such that
\tilde{x}:=x/(1+\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}})
satisfies

\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},\quad G_{n,m}\tilde{x}=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|\tilde{x}\right\|_{2}=\frac{\sigma_{1}}{1+\sigma_{1}},

which completes the proof.
∎

Appendix – Gelfand numbers of diagonal operators, optimal radius, and polynomial semiaxes

Let
0<p\leq\infty
. We write
\ell_{p}
for the space of
p
-summable sequences and denote its (quasi-)norm by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.
For
0<p,t\leq\infty
, we define a Lorentz (quasi-)norm by

\left\|x\right\|_{p,t}:=\left\|j^{1/p-1/t}x_{j}^{*}\right\|_{t},

where
(x_{j}^{*})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is the non-increasing rearrangement of
(\left|x_{j}\right|)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with the convention that
1/\infty:=0
. We write
\ell_{p,t}
for the space of sequences with finite Lorentz (quasi-)norm
\|\cdot\|_{p,t}
. Note that
\ell_{p,p}=\ell_{p}
and
\ell_{p,t}\subset\ell_{r,t}
for every
0<p<r\leq\infty
.

Let
0<q\leq\infty
and
\sigma=(\sigma_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a non-increasing non-negative sequence, i.e.,
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\cdots\geq 0
. To
\sigma
we can associate the diagonal operator

D_{\sigma}\colon\ell_{p}\to\ell_{q},\quad x=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mapsto(\sigma_{j}x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}},

which, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, can be considered as an operator from
\ell_{p}^{m}
to
\ell_{q}^{m}
. Then the image

D_{\sigma}(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{m})=\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m}

is an
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoid.

Let
1\leq n<m
and consider an information mapping
N_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
with kernel
E_{n}
. A change of variables shows that

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},N_{n})=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}_{p}^{m}\cap\ker N_{n}}\left\|D_{\sigma}x\right\|_{2}.

We have that

\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n)=c_{n+1}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}^{m}\to\ell_{2}^{m})\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

(10)

where

c_{n+1}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}^{m}\to\ell_{q}^{m}):=\inf_{E_{n}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}_{p}^{m}\cap E_{n}}\left\|D_{\sigma}x\right\|_{q}

is the
(n+1)
-st Gelfand number of
D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}^{m}\to\ell_{q}^{m}
. Here, the infimum ranges over all subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with codimension at most
n
. For general background on Gelfand numbers and other
s
-numbers, we refer the reader to
[
19
]
and
[
27
]
.

Although we will need only the case
q=2
, it is natural to state the following result in a more general form, which can be found in
[
27
, Section 11.11]
for
q\geq 1
but the proof is in fact also valid for all
q>0
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
0<q\leq p\leq\infty
and
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\cdots\geq 0
. Then, for any
1\leq n\leq m
, we have

c_{n}(D_{\sigma}\colon\ell_{p}^{m}\to\ell_{q}^{m})=\Big(\sum_{j=n}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{r}\Big)^{1/r},

where
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}
if
q<p
and
r=\infty
if
q=p
.

In addition to Proposition
5
, we have for all
0<p,q\leq\infty
that

c_{1}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}^{m}\to\ell_{q}^{m})=\left\|D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}^{m}\to\ell_{q}^{m}\right\|=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{r}\Big)^{1/r},

where
\frac{1}{r}=(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})_{+}
. This shows that
\left\|\sigma\right\|_{r}<\infty
is necessary to ensure that the operators
D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}^{m}\to\ell_{q}^{m},m\in\mathbb{N},
are uniformly bounded. All of the above extends to the infinite-dimensional case in a canonical way. We state a result taken from Buchmann
[
2
]
, where one implication goes back to Linde
[
21
, Theorem 5]
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
r>0
with
\frac{1}{r}>(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})_{+}
as well as
0<t\leq\infty
. Then

\sigma\in\ell_{r,t}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\big(c_{n}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}\to\ell_{q})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{u,t},

where

1.

if
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
, then
\frac{1}{u}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
,

2.

if
1\leq p<q\leq 2
, then
\frac{1}{u}=\begin{cases}\frac{p^{*}}{2r}&:\frac{1}{r}<\frac{1}{p^{*}}\frac{1/p-1/q}{1/p-1/2},\\
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}&:\frac{1}{r}>\frac{1}{p^{*}}\frac{1/p-1/q}{1/p-1/2},\end{cases}

3.

if
1\leq p<2<q\leq\infty
, then
\frac{1}{u}=\begin{cases}\frac{p^{*}}{2r}&:\frac{1}{r}<\frac{1}{p^{*}},\\
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}&:\frac{1}{r}>\frac{1}{p^{*}},\end{cases}

4.

if
2\leq p<q\leq\infty
, then
\frac{1}{u}=\frac{1}{r}
.

By means of (
10
), Propositions
5
and
6
apply to the radius of optimal information. Note that some cases are missing, for example if
q=2
, there is a gap for
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p^{*}}
. In this case, we can deduce from an infinite-dimensional version of Theorem
A
the following corollary.

Corollary 3
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

c_{n}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}\to\ell_{2})\lesssim\begin{cases}n^{-1/2}\sup\limits_{k\leq j\leq\infty}\sigma_{j}\sqrt{\log(j)+1}&:\,p=1\\
\sqrt{p^{*}}n^{-1/2}\Big(\sum\limits_{j=k}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}^{p^{*}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}&:\,1<p\leq\infty,\end{cases}

where
k\asymp\frac{n}{p^{*}}
for
p>1
, while
k\asymp n
for
p=1
. In particular,
c_{n}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}\to\ell_{2})\in\ell_{2,\infty}
if
1<p\leq\infty
and
\sigma\in\ell_{p^{*}}
.

Proof.

We use (
10
) to state Theorem
A
for Gelfand numbers. Then, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
D_{\sigma}^{m}
be the restriction of the operator
D_{\sigma}
to the first
m
coordinates. This gives

c_{n}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}^{m}\to\ell_{q}^{m})=c_{n}(D_{\sigma}^{m}:\ell_{p}\to\ell_{q}),

and further, by continuity and Hölder’s inequality,

|c_{n}(D_{\sigma}^{m}:\ell_{p}\to\ell_{q})-c_{n}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}\to\ell_{q})|\leq\Big(\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}^{s}\Big)^{1/s},

with
\frac{1}{s}=(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})_{+}
. Letting
m\to\infty
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
completes the proof.
∎

Let us note that bounding the Gelfand numbers of operators into
\ell_{2}
via
M^{*}
-estimates has been done before, e.g., in
[
26
]
.

To the best of our knowledge, for
0<p<1
or
0<q<1
the asymptotic behavior of Gelfand numbers of diagonal operators is unknown. At least for the case of polynomial sequences, we can deduce the following result from Theorem
C
and the analogue of (
10
) for
0<q<2
.

Corollary 4
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
and
p<q\leq 2
. Assume that
\sigma_{j}=j^{-\lambda}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, for some
\lambda>0
. Then there exist constants
C,D\in(0,\infty)
such that, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and all
1\leq n<m
with

n\geq D\log({\rm e}m/n),

we have

c_{n}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}^{m}\to\ell_{q}^{m})\lesssim_{p,q}\Big(\frac{\log({\rm e}m/n)}{n}\Big)^{\lambda+1/p-1/q}.

To study the decay of Gelfand numbers of diagonal operators arising from a polynomially decaying sequence, the concept of a diagonal limit order has been introduced by Pietsch (see, e.g.,
[
28
, 6.2.5.3]
). The definition of decay given in Section
1.3
is basically a finite-dimensional analogue of it. As a corollary to Proposition
6
, we have the following result.

Corollary 5
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. If
\sigma_{j}=j^{-\lambda}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, for some
\lambda>(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})_{+}
, then

\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n))=\begin{cases}\lambda\cdot\frac{p^{*}}{2}&:\,1\leq p<2\text{ and }\lambda<\frac{1}{p^{*}},\\
\lambda+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}&:\,\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For
p\geq 2
this also follows from Proposition
5
showing that
\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n)\lesssim_{p,\lambda}n^{-\lambda+1/2-1/p}
for all
m>n
with a matching lower bound for
m
, say, larger than
2n
.

Proof.

We only prove the first case since the other case is analogous. To show that
\mathrm{decay}(\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{E}_{p,\sigma}^{m},n))\geq\lambda p^{*}/2
, it is sufficient by (
10
) to find
C\in(0,\infty)
such that, for all
\varrho<\lambda p^{*}/2
large enough,

c_{n,m}:=c_{n}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}^{m}\to\ell_{q}^{m})\leq Cn^{-\varrho}\quad\text{for all }m\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }1\leq n\leq m.

(11)

This is satisfied if the sequence of Gelfand numbers
c_{n}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}\to\ell_{q})\geq c_{n,m}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
) belongs to
\ell_{u,\infty}
with
u=1/\varrho
. By Proposition
6
, this holds if
\sigma\in\ell_{r,\infty}
with a certain
r>1/\lambda
, which is true by assumption.

For the other inequality we assume that (
11
) holds for some
\varrho>\lambda p^{*}/2
. Choosing
m
large enough compared to
n
, see the proof of Corollary
3
, we deduce from (
11
) that for some
\varrho>\lambda p^{*}/2
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

c_{n}:=c_{n}(D_{\sigma}:\ell_{p}\to\ell_{q})\leq 2Cn^{-\varrho}.

(12)

For every
r<1/\lambda
and
0<t<\infty
, it follows from
\sigma\not\in\ell_{r,t}
and Proposition
6
that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}c_{n}^{t}n^{tp^{*}/2r}=\infty,

which implies
c_{n}n^{p^{*}/2r}\log^{2/t}n\to\infty
, contradicting (
12
).
∎

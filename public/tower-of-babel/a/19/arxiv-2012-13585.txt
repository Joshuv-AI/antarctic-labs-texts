An inequality regarding differential polynomial
Sudip Saha 1

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove an inequality regarding the differential polynomial. This improves some recent results.

1.
Introduction and Main Results

Throughout this paper, we assume that the reader is familiar with the value distribution theory (
[
7
]
). Further, it will be convenient to let that
E
denote any set of positive real numbers of finite Lebesgue measure, not necessarily same at each occurrence. For any non-constant meromorphic function
f
, we denote by
S(r,f)
any quantity satisfying

S(r,f)=o(T(r,f))~~\text{as}~~r\to\infty,~r\not\in E.

Let
f
be a non-constant meromorphic function. A meromorphic function
a(z)(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
is called a
“small function”
with respect to
f
if
T(r,a(z))=S(r,f)
. For example, polynomial functions are small functions with respect to any transcendental entire function.

Definition 1.1
.

(
[
11
]
)
Let
a\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
. For a positive integer
k
, we denote

i)

by
N_{k)}\left(r,a;f\right)
the counting function of
a
-points of
f
whose multiplicities are not greater than
k
,

ii)

by
N_{(k}\left(r,a;f\right)
the counting function of
a
-points of
f
whose multiplicities are not less than
k
.

Similarly, the reduced counting functions
\overline{N}_{k)}(r,a;f)
and
\overline{N}_{(k}(r,a;f)
are defined.

Definition 1.2
.

(
[
10
]
)
For a positive integer
k
, we denote
N_{k}(r,0;f)
the counting function of zeros of
f
, where a zero of
f
with multiplicity
q
is counted
q
times if
q\leq k
, and is counted
k
times if
q>k
.

In 2003, I. Lahiri and S. Dewan proved the following theorem:

Theorem A
.

(
[
10
]
)
Let
f
be a transcendental meromorphic function and
\alpha(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function of
f
. If
\psi=\alpha(f)^{n}(f^{(k)})^{p}
, where
n(\geq 0),p(\geq 1),k(\geq 1)
are integers, then for any small function
a(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
of
\psi
we have

(p+n)T(r,f)\leq\overline{N}(r,\infty;f)+\overline{N}(r,0;f)+pN_{k}(r,0;f)+\overline{N}(r,a;\psi)+S(r,f).

In this paper we extend and improve the Theorem A. To state our next result, we recall a well known definition.

Definition 1.3
.

(
[
1
]
)
Let
n_{0j},n_{1j},\cdots,n_{kj}
be non-negative integers. Then the expression
M_{j}[f]=(f)^{n_{0j}}(f^{\prime})^{n_{1j}}\cdots(f^{(k)})^{n_{kj}}
is called a differential monomial generated by
f
. The quantities
d(M_{j})=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}n_{ij}
and
\Gamma_{M_{j}}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}(i+1)n_{ij}
are known as the degree and weight of the monomial
M_{j}
respectively.

The sum
P[f]=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{t}b_{j}M_{j}[f]
is called a differential polynomial generated by
f
, where
T(r,b_{j})=S(r,f)~(j=1,2,\cdots,t).
The quantities
\overline{d}(P)=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq t}\{d(M_{j})\}
and
\Gamma_{P}=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq t}\{\Gamma_{M_{j}}\}
are called degree and weight of the polynomial
P[f]
respectively.

The numbers
\underline{d}(P)=\min\limits_{1\leq j\leq t}\{d(M_{j})\}
and
k
(the highest order of the derivative of
f
in
P[f]
) are known as the lower degree and order of the polynomial
P[f]
.

P[f]
is called homogeneous if
\underline{d}(P)=\overline{d}(P)
.
P[f]
is called a linear differential polynomial if
\overline{d}(P)=1.
Otherwise
P[f]
is called a non-linear differential polynomial.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f(z)
be a transcendental meromorphic function and
\alpha(z)(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function of
f(z)
. Let
P[f]=\alpha\sum\limits_{j=1}^{t}M_{j}[f]
be a differential polynomial generated by
f
, where
M_{j}[f]=c_{j}(f)^{n_{0j}}(f^{\prime})^{n_{1j}}\cdots(f^{(k)})^{n_{kj}}~(j=1,2,\cdots,t)
such that
k(\geq 1)
is the order of
P[f]
,
~t(\geq 1),~n_{ij}(i=0,1,\cdots,k;j=1,2,\cdots,t)
are non-negative integers and
c_{j}(j=1,2,\cdots,t)
are small functions of
f
such that they do not have poles at the zeros of
f
. Then, for a small function
a(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\underline{d}(P)T(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,0;f)+\overline{N}(r,a;P[f])+\overline{N}(r,\infty;f)+n_{1e}N_{1}(r,0;f)

\displaystyle+n_{2e}N_{2}(r,0;f)+\cdots+n_{ke}N_{k}(r,0;f)+S(r,f),

where
(1\cdot n_{1e}+2\cdot n_{2e}+\cdots+k\cdot n_{ke})=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq t}(1\cdot n_{1j}+2\cdot n_{2j}+\cdots+k\cdot n_{kj})
.

Remark 1.1
.

Clearly, Theorem
1.1
extends and improves Theorem A.

2.
Necessary Lemmas

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
6
]
)
Let
A>1
, then there exists a set
M(A)
of upper logarithmic density at most

\delta(A)=\min\{(2e^{(A-1)}-1)^{-1},1+e(A-1)\exp(e(1-A))\}
such that for
k=1,2,3,\cdots

\limsup\limits_{r\to\infty,r\notin M(A)}\frac{T(r,f)}{T(r,f^{(k)})}\leq 3eA.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f
be a transcendental meromorphic function and
\alpha~(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function of
f
. Let,
\psi=\alpha(f)^{q_{0}}(f^{\prime})^{q_{1}}\cdots(f^{(k)})^{q_{k}}
, where
q_{0},q_{1},\cdots,q_{k}(\geq 1),k(\geq 1)
are non-negative integers. Then
\psi
is not identically constant.

Proof.

Since,
\alpha
is a small function of
f
, then
T(r,\alpha)=S(r,f)
. Therefore the proof follows from Lemma(3.4) of (
[
3
]
).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f
be a transcendental meromorphic function and
\alpha~(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function of
f
. Let,
\psi=\alpha(f)^{q_{0}}(f^{\prime})^{q_{1}}\cdots(f^{(k)})^{q_{k}}
, where
q_{0},q_{1},\cdots,q_{k}(\geq 1),k(\geq 1)
are non-negative integers. Then

T(r,\psi)\leq\left\{q_{0}+2q_{1}+\cdots+(k+1)q_{k}\right\}T(r,f)+S(r,f).

Lemma 2.4

(
[
2
,
4
]
)
.

Let,
f
be a meromorphic function and
P[f]
be a differential polynomial. Then

m\left(r,\frac{P[f]}{f^{\overline{d}(P)}}\right)\leq(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))m\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)+S(r,f).

3.
Proof of the Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
P[f]
is a differential polynomial generated by
f
, then using Lemma
2.4

\displaystyle T(r,(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(r,0;(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})+m\left(r,\frac{1}{(f)^{\overline{d}(P)}}\right)+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(r,0;(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})+m\left(r,\frac{P[f]}{(f)^{\overline{d}(P)}}\frac{1}{P[f]}\right)+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N(r,0;(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})+(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))m\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{1}{P[f]}\right)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(r,0;(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})+(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))m\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)+~T(r,P[f])

\displaystyle-N(r,0;P[f])+S(r,f).

(1)

\displaystyle\therefore T(r,(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N(r,0;(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})+(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))m\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)+~T(r,P[f])

\displaystyle-N(r,0;P[f])+S(r,f).

Using Nevanlinna’s second fundamental theorem, from (
1
) we get

(2)

\displaystyle T(r,(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N(r,0;(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})+\overline{N}(r,0;P[f])+\overline{N}(r,\infty;P[f])

\displaystyle+\overline{N}(r,a;P[f])-N(r,0;P[f])+(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))m\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)

\displaystyle+S(r,P[f])+S(r,f).

From definition of
P[f]
and using Lemma (
2.3
) we have
T(r,P[f])\leq K_{1}T(r,f)+S(r,f),
for some constant
K_{1}
. This implies
S(r,P[f])=S(r,f).
We also note that
\overline{N}(r,\infty;P[f])=\overline{N}(r,\infty;f)+S(r,f).

Thus from (
2
),

(3)

\displaystyle T(r,(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N(r,0;(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})+\overline{N}(r,0;P[f])+\overline{N}(r,\infty;f)

\displaystyle+\overline{N}(r,a;P[f])-N(r,0;P[f])+(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))m\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)

\displaystyle+S(r,f).

Let,
z_{0}
be a zero of
f(z)
with multiplicity
q~(\geq 1)
. For any
k
,
M_{j}[f]~~(j=1,2,\cdots,t)
has a zero at
z_{0}
of order atleast

\displaystyle qn_{0j}+(q-1)n_{1j}+(q-2)n_{2j}+\cdots+2n_{q-2~j}+n_{q-1~j}+r_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(n_{0j}+n_{1j}+\cdots+n_{q-1~j})-(1\cdot n_{1j}+2\cdot n_{2j}+\cdots+(q-1)\cdot n_{q-1~j})+r_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(n_{0j}+n_{1j}+\cdots+n_{q-1~j}+\cdots+n_{kj})-q(n_{qj}+n_{q+1~j}+\cdots+n_{kj})

\displaystyle-(1\cdot n_{1j}+2\cdot n_{2j}+\cdots+(q-1)\cdot n_{q-1~j})+r_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(d(M_{j}))-(1\cdot n_{1j}+2\cdot n_{2j}+\cdots+(q-1)\cdot n_{q-1~j}+qn_{qj}+\cdots+qn_{kj})+r_{j}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle q(\underline{d}(P))-(1\cdot n_{1j}+2\cdot n_{2j}+\cdots+(q-1)\cdot n_{q-1~j}+qn_{qj}+\cdots+qn_{kj}),~~~~\text{if}~~~~q\leq k

and

\displaystyle qn_{0j}+(q-1)n_{1j}+(q-2)n_{2j}+\cdots+(q-k)n_{kj}+r_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(n_{0j}+n_{1j}+\cdots+n_{kj})-(1\cdot n_{1j}+2\cdot n_{2j}+\cdots+k\cdot n_{kj})+r_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(d(M_{j}))-(1\cdot n_{1j}+2\cdot n_{2j}+\cdots+k\cdot n_{kj})+r_{j}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle q(\underline{d}(P))-(1\cdot n_{1j}+2\cdot n_{2j}+\cdots+k\cdot n_{kj}),~~~~\text{if}~~~~q>k,

where
r_{j}~(j=1,2,\cdots,t)
is the multiplicity of zero of
c_{j}~(j=1,2,\cdots,t)
at
z_{0}
, which must be a non-negative quantity.

Therefore,
P[f]
has a zero at
z_{0}
of order

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle q(\underline{d}(P))+r-(1\cdot n_{1e}+\cdots+(q-1)\cdot n_{q-1~e}+qn_{qe}+\cdots+q\cdot n_{ke})~~~\text{if}~~~q\leq k~~\text{and}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle q(\underline{d}(P))+r-(1\cdot n_{1e}+2\cdot n_{2e}+\cdots+k\cdot n_{ke})~~~\text{if}~~~q>k,

where
r=0
if
\alpha(z)
does not have a zero or pole at
z_{0}
,
r=s
if
\alpha(z)
has a zero of order
s
at
z_{0}
,
r=-s
if
\alpha(z)
has a pole of order
s
at
z_{0}
,
s
being a natural number and
\{n_{1e},n_{2e},\cdots,n_{ke}\}
is the set of values such that
(1\cdot n_{1e}+2\cdot n_{2e}+\cdots+k\cdot n_{ke})=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq t}(1\cdot n_{1j}+2\cdot n_{2j}+\cdots+k\cdot n_{kj})
. [We see that whenever
q(\geq 1)
and
k(\geq 1)
are fixed, then
\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq t}(1\cdot n_{1j}+2\cdot n_{2j}+\cdots+q\cdot n_{kj})
and
\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq t}(1\cdot n_{1j}+2\cdot n_{2j}+\cdots+k\cdot n_{kj})
will appear for same set of values
\{n_{1j},n_{2j},\cdots,n_{kj}\}
for some
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,t\}
.]

Therefore,

(4)

\displaystyle 1+q(\overline{d}(P))-q(\underline{d}(P))-r

\displaystyle+(1\cdot n_{1e}+\cdots+(q-1)\cdot n_{q-1~e}+qn_{qe}+\cdots+q\cdot n_{ke})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))+1-r

\displaystyle+(1\cdot n_{1e}+\cdots+(q-1)\cdot n_{q-1~e}+qn_{qe}+\cdots+q\cdot n_{ke})~~~~\text{if}~~~~q\leq k

and

(5)

\displaystyle 1+q(\overline{d}(P))-q(\underline{d}(P))-r+(1\cdot n_{1e}+2\cdot n_{2e}+\cdots+k\cdot n_{ke})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))+1-r+(1\cdot n_{1e}+2\cdot n_{2e}+\cdots+k\cdot n_{ke})~~~~\text{if}~~~~q>k.

Therefore, from (
4
) and (
5
) we have

\displaystyle N(r,0;(f)^{\overline{d}(P)})+\overline{N}(r,0;P[f])-N(r,0;P[f])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))N(r,0;f)

\displaystyle+\overline{N}(r,0;f)+n_{1e}N_{1}(r,0;f)

\displaystyle+n_{2e}N_{2}(r,0;f)+\cdots

\displaystyle+n_{ke}N_{k}(r,0;f)+S(r,f).

Therefore (
3
) gives

\displaystyle\overline{d}(P)T(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,0;f)+\overline{N}(r,a;P[f])+\overline{N}(r,\infty;f)+(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))N(r,0;f)

\displaystyle+(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))m\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)+n_{1e}N_{1}(r,0;f)+n_{2e}N_{2}(r,0;f)+\cdots

\displaystyle+n_{ke}N_{k}(r,0;f)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,0;f)+\overline{N}(r,a;P[f])+\overline{N}(r,\infty;f)+(\overline{d}(P)-\underline{d}(P))T(r,f)

\displaystyle+n_{1e}N_{1}(r,0;f)+n_{2e}N_{2}(r,0;f)+\cdots+n_{ke}N_{k}(r,0;f)+S(r,f).

\displaystyle\therefore\underline{d}(P)T(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,0;f)+\overline{N}(r,a;P[f])+\overline{N}(r,\infty;f)+n_{1e}N_{1}(r,0;f)

\displaystyle+n_{2e}N_{2}(r,0;f)+\cdots+n_{ke}N_{k}(r,0;f)+S(r,f).

∎

Limit laws for cotangent and Diophantine sums


Abstract
Limit laws for ergodic averages with a power singularity over circle rotations were first proved by Sinai and Ulcigrai, as well as Dolgopyat and Fayad. In this paper, we prove limit laws with an estimate for the rate of convergence for the sum ∑ n = 1 N f ⁡ ( n ​ α ) / n p \sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n\alpha)/n^{p} in terms of a 1 1 -periodic function f f with a power singularity of order p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 at integers. Our results apply in particular to cotangent sums related to Dedekind sums, and to sums of reciprocals of fractional parts, which appear in multiplicative Diophantine approximation. The main tools are Schmidt’s method in metric Diophantine approximation, the Gauss–Kuzmin problem and the theory of ψ \psi -mixing random variables.

Introduction

Cotangent and cosecant sums such as

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\cot(\pi n\alpha)}{n},\qquad\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n\sin(\pi n\alpha)},\qquad\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n^{p}|\sin(\pi n\alpha)|^{q}}

at various irrational
\alpha
were first studied by Hardy and Littlewood
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
in connection with Diophantine approximation and lattice point counting. Further connections to Dedekind sums, real quadratic fields and modularity relations were explored in
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
12
,
13
,
34
,
39
]
. The sums are sensitive to the Diophantine properties of
\alpha
. For instance, the first two sums are
O(\log N)
for a badly approximable
\alpha
, and this is sharp in general. In this paper, we consider similar sums at a randomly chosen
\alpha
, and find their limit distributions.

Throughout,
\alpha
is a
[0,1]
-valued random variable whose distribution
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
, denoted by
\mu\ll\lambda
. Sometimes we also assume that
\mu
has a positive Lipschitz density, that is,

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}(x)-\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}(y)\right|\leq L|x-y|\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}(x)\geq A

(1)

for all
x,y\in[0,1]
with some constants
L\geq 0
and
A>0
. Throughout the paper,
L
and
A
always denote the constants in (
1
).

Let
\mathrm{Stab}(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
denote the standard stable law with stability index
\alpha_{0}\in(0,2]
and skewness parameter
\beta_{0}\in[-1,1]
, defined as the probability distribution on
\mathbb{R}
whose characteristic function is

\varphi(t)=\exp\left(-|t|^{\alpha_{0}}\left(1-i\beta_{0}\,\mathrm{sgn}(t)\omega\right)\right)\quad\textrm{with}\quad\omega=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tan\frac{\alpha_{0}\pi}{2}&\textrm{if }\alpha_{0}\neq 1,\\
-\frac{2}{\pi}\log|t|&\textrm{if }\alpha_{0}=1.\end{array}\right.

We write simply “Cauchy” for the standard Cauchy distribution
\mathrm{Stab}(1,0)
. We use the notation
\overset{d}{\to}
for convergence in distribution, and
X\sim\nu
if a random variable
X
has distribution
\nu
. If
X_{N}\overset{d}{\to}\nu
and
\nu
has a continuous cumulative distribution function
F
, then the rate of convergence in the Kolmogorov metric is defined as
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\Pr(X_{N}\leq x)-F(x)|
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\alpha\sim\mu
with some
\mu\ll\lambda
. Then

\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\cot(\pi n\alpha)}{n}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Cauchy}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\frac{1}{\log N}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{|\cot(\pi n\alpha)|}{n}-E_{N}\right)\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1,1),

where
E_{N}=\frac{1}{\pi}(\log N)^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\log N\log\log N-c\log N
with the constant

c=\frac{2}{\pi}\left(\gamma+\log\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{12\zeta^{\prime}(2)}{\pi^{2}}-1\right)=0.77338986\ldots.

Under the assumption (
1
), in both relations the rate of convergence in the Kolmogorov metric is
\ll(\log N)^{-1/4}(\log\log N)^{1/4}
with an implied constant depending only on
L
and
A
.

Here
\gamma
denotes the Euler–Mascheroni constant and
\zeta^{\prime}(2)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2}\log n
is the derivative of the Riemann zeta function at the point 2. We also prove similar limit laws for cotangent sums with higher powers. For instance, we will show that if
\alpha\sim\mu
with some
\mu\ll\lambda
, then

\frac{1}{\frac{4}{5\pi}(\log N)^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\cot^{2}(\pi n\alpha)}{n^{2}}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/2,1),

(2)

as well as

\frac{1}{\sigma(\log N)^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\cot^{3}(\pi n\alpha)}{n^{3}}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/3,0)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{1}{\sigma(\log N)^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{|\cot(\pi n\alpha)|^{3}}{n^{3}}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/3,1)

(3)

with
\sigma=64/(35\Gamma(1/3)^{3})
, where
\Gamma
is the gamma function. Under the assumption (
1
), the rate of convergence in the Kolmogorov metric is
\ll(\log N)^{-1/3}(\log\log N)^{1/3}
in (
2
) resp.
\ll(\log N)^{-1/3}
in both relations in (
3
).

Theorem
1
and relations (
2
) and (
3
) are illustrations of limit laws for the more general sum
\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n\alpha)/n^{p}
in terms of a
1
-periodic function
f
with a
1/x^{p}
or
1/|x|^{p}
-type singularity at integers, see the main results Theorems
2
and
4
and Corollaries
3
and
5
below.

Our results should be compared to a theorem of Sinai and Ulcigrai
[
44
]
, who proved that if
(\alpha,\beta)
is a random variable uniformly distributed on the unit square, then the normalized sum
N^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\cot(\pi(n\alpha+\beta))
converges in distribution. The limit distribution was not explicitly identified, however. Their proof is based on the renewal theory of continued fractions, and applies to more general sums
N^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n\alpha+\beta)
in terms of a
1
-periodic function with a
1/x
-type singularity at integers. Higher order power singularities were investigated by Dolgopyat and Fayad
[
15
]
using dynamical methods, in which case the limit distributions can be described in terms of random lattices. See also Kesten
[
29
,
30
]
for the case of bounded
f
. The proofs in
[
15
,
44
]
do not yield a rate of convergence.

In contrast, we obtain classical stable laws as limit distributions, and an estimate for the rate of convergence. Our proofs are based on a combination of three main tools. Firstly, we generalize a classical method of Schmidt in metric Diophantine approximation
[
26
,
43
]
. Secondly, we use quantitative mixing properties of continued fractions related to the Gauss–Kuzmin problem. Finally, we use the theory of
\psi
-mixing random variables from probability theory
[
11
]
.

2
Main results

Let
\langle\cdot\rangle
resp.
\|\cdot\|
denote the signed resp. unsigned distance from the nearest integer function. That is, let
\langle x\rangle=x
and
\|x\|=|x|
on the interval
x\in(-1/2,1/2]
, and extend them with period
1
.

Let
\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2}
denote the product of the measures
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
. If a sequence of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued random variables
X_{N}
converges in distribution to
\nu
and
\nu
has a continuous cumulative distribution function
F
, then the rate of convergence in the Kolmogorov metric is defined similarly as

\sup_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\Pr(X_{N}\in(-\infty,x]\times(-\infty,y])-F(x,y)|.

2.1
Singularities of order 1

Our main result is a joint limit law for the sum of positive and negative parts of
f(n\alpha)/n
, where
f
has a
1/x
-type singularity at integers. The indicator of a set or relation
S
is denoted by
\mathds{1}_{S}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle}\mathds{1}_{\{\langle x\rangle>0\}}+g_{1}(x)
and
f_{2}(x)=\frac{1}{|\langle x\rangle|}\mathds{1}_{\{\langle x\rangle<0\}}+g_{2}(x)
with some
1
-periodic functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
that are of bounded variation on
[0,1]
. Let
\alpha\sim\mu
with some
\mu\ll\lambda
. Then

\left(\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{1}(n\alpha)/n-E_{1,N}}{(\pi/2)\log N},\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{2}(n\alpha)/n-E_{2,N}}{(\pi/2)\log N}\right)\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)\otimes\mathrm{Stab}(1,1),

where
E_{j,N}=\frac{1}{2}(\log N)^{2}+\log N\log\log N-c_{j}\log N
,
j=1,2
, with the constants

c_{j}=\gamma+\log\frac{2\pi}{3}-\frac{12\zeta^{\prime}(2)}{\pi^{2}}-1-\int_{0}^{1}g_{j}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Assuming (
1
), the rate of convergence in the Kolmogorov metric is
\ll(\log N)^{-1/4}(\log\log N)^{1/4}
with an implied constant depending only on
L
,
A
,
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
.

If
(X,Y)\sim\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)\otimes\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)
, then
(X-Y)/2\sim\mathrm{Cauchy}
and
(X+Y)/2-(2\log 2)/\pi\sim\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)
, as seen from the characteristic functions. The limit law of the difference resp. sum of the coordinates of the random vector in Theorem
2
thus corresponds to the following.

Corollary 3
.

Let
g
be a
1
-periodic function that is of bounded variation on
[0,1]
. Let
\alpha\sim\mu
with some
\mu\ll\lambda
. Then

\frac{1}{\pi\log N}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\frac{1}{\langle n\alpha\rangle}+g(n\alpha)}{n}-\log N\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Cauchy}

and

\frac{1}{\pi\log N}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\frac{1}{\|n\alpha\|}+g(n\alpha)}{n}-E_{N}\right)\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1,1),

where
E_{N}=(\log N)^{2}+2\log N\log\log N-c\log N
with the constant

c=2\left(\gamma+\log\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{12\zeta^{\prime}(2)}{\pi^{2}}-1\right)-\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Under the assumption (
1
), in both relations the rate of convergence in the Kolmogorov metric is
\ll(\log N)^{-1/4}(\log\log N)^{1/4}
with an implied constant depending only on
L
,
A
and
g
.

Theorem
1
is a special case of Corollary
3
. Indeed,
\pi\cot(\pi x)=1/\langle x\rangle+g(x)
with a
1
-periodic function
g
of bounded variation on
[0,1]
such that
\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0
. Similarly,
\pi|\cot(\pi x)|=1/\|x\|+g(x)
with a
1
-periodic function
g
of bounded variation on
[0,1]
such that
\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=2\log(2/\pi)
.

Corollary
3
with
g=0
yields limit laws for the Diophantine sums

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n\langle n\alpha\rangle}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n\|n\alpha\|}.

As a further example, note that Theorem
2
applies to the functions
f_{1}(x)=1/\{x\}
and
f_{2}(x)=1/(1-\{x\})
, where
\{\cdot\}
denotes the fractional part function, with some
g_{1}
,
g_{2}
such that
\int_{0}^{1}g_{1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{1}g_{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\log 2
, yielding a joint limit law for the Diophantine sums

\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n\{n\alpha\}},\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n(1-\{n\alpha\})}\right).

The almost sure asymptotics of these sums was found in
[
10
,
17
]
, see also
[
32
]
. Higher-dimensional analogues were studied in
[
18
,
19
,
35
]
using Fourier analytic and dynamical methods, but no limit law is known in this case. Diophantine sums of this form play an important role in multiplicative Diophantine approximation
[
2
]
.

Diophantine counting problems with shrinking targets, such as

\left|\left\{1\leq n\leq N\,:\,\|n\alpha\|<\delta_{n}\right\}\right|

are closely related, and satisfy the central limit theorem as well as various other probabilistic limit theorems under suitable assumptions on the sequence
\delta_{n}\to 0
, see Philipp
[
38
]
, Samur
[
42
]
, Fuchs
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
and references therein. Central limit theorems for higher dimensional Diophantine counting problems were proved by Dolgopyat, Fayad and Vinogradov
[
16
]
and Björklund and Gorodnik
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
using dynamical methods.

2.2
Higher order singularities

We also prove a limit law for functions with a higher order singularity at integers.

Theorem 4
.

Let
f_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{p}}\mathds{1}_{\{\langle x\rangle>0\}}+g_{1}(x)
and
f_{2}(x)=\frac{1}{|\langle x\rangle|^{p}}\mathds{1}_{\{\langle x\rangle<0\}}+g_{2}(x)
with some real constant
p>1
and some
1
-periodic functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
that satisfy
|g_{1}(x)|,|g_{2}(x)|\ll 1/\|x\|^{p^{\prime}}
with some real constant
0\leq p^{\prime}<p
. Let
\alpha\sim\mu
with some
\mu\ll\lambda
. Then

\left(\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{1}(n\alpha)/n^{p}+c\log N}{\sigma(\log N)^{p}},\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{2}(n\alpha)/n^{p}+c\log N}{\sigma(\log N)^{p}}\right)\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,1)\otimes\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,1),

where
c=\mathds{1}_{\{1<p<2\}}c_{p}
with some explicit constant
c_{p}
, and
\sigma=\zeta(2p)\left(\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)^{p}
. Assuming (
1
), the rate of convergence in the Kolmogorov metric is

\ll\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\log N)^{-\frac{p}{2(p+1)}}(\log\log N)^{\frac{p}{2(p+1)}}&\textrm{if }1<p\leq 1+\sqrt{3},\\
(\log N)^{-1/p}&\textrm{if }p>1+\sqrt{3}\end{array}\right.

with an implied constant depending only on
L
,
A
,
g_{1}
,
g_{2}
and
p
.

The centering term
c\log N
is not necessary for the limit law itself, only for the rate. In fact, it is only visible in the range
1<p<(1+\sqrt{17})/4=1.28\ldots
(when
1-p>-\frac{p}{2(p+1)}
). The explicit formula for the constant
c_{p}
,
1<p<2
is

c_{p}=\frac{6\zeta(2p)}{\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}\log\frac{4}{3}+B_{p}\right),

(4)

where

\begin{split}B_{p}&=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p}-\lfloor x\rfloor^{p}+x^{p-1}}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p-2}(6x+4)}{(x+1)(x+2)}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\phantom{={}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\{x\}-\frac{1}{2}\right)x^{p-1}\left(p\log\left(1+\frac{1}{x(x+2)}\right)-\frac{2}{(x+1)(x+2)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

The limit law of the difference resp. sum of the random vector in Theorem
4
corresponds to the following.

Corollary 5
.

Let
p>1
be a real constant, and let
g
be a
1
-periodic function that satisfies
|g(x)|\ll 1/\|x\|^{p^{\prime}}
with some real constant
0\leq p^{\prime}<p
. Let
\alpha\sim\mu
with some
\mu\ll\lambda
. Then

\frac{1}{2^{p}\sigma(\log N)^{p}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{sgn}(\langle n\alpha\rangle)}{|\langle n\alpha\rangle|^{p}}+g(n\alpha)}{n^{p}}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,0)

and

\frac{1}{2^{p}\sigma(\log N)^{p}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\frac{1}{\|n\alpha\|^{p}}+g(n\alpha)}{n^{p}}+2c\log N\right)\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,1),

where
c
and
\sigma
are as in Theorem
4
. Assuming (
1
), in both relations the rate of convergence in the Kolmogorov metric is the same as in Theorem
4
with an implied constant depending only on
L
,
A
,
g
and
p
.

Limit laws for cotangent sums such as relations (
2
) and (
3
) immediately follow as a special case.

3
Mixing properties of continued fractions

We refer to Khinchin
[
31
]
for a general introduction to Diophantine approximation and continued fractions, to Iosifescu and Kraaikamp
[
28
]
for a comprehensive account of the stochastic properties of continued fractions, and to Bradley
[
11
]
for a survey of various mixing coefficients and their applications in probability theory.

We work on the probability space
([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
, where
\mathcal{B}
is the family of Borel sets and
\mu\ll\lambda
, and write
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}
,
\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}
and
\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}
for the expected value, the variance and the covariance. The continued fraction expansion of
\alpha\in[0,1]
is written as
\alpha=[0;a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
with convergents
p_{k}/q_{k}=[0;a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}]
. The partial quotients
a_{k}
are thus random variables, and we use the common shorthand notation, say,
\mu(a_{k}=3)=\mu(\{\alpha\in[0,1]\,:\,a_{k}=3\})
for the probability of the event
a_{k}=3
. The most important special case is the Gauss measure
\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(B)=\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_{B}\frac{1}{1+x}\,\mathrm{d}x
,
B\in\mathcal{B}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{k}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
a_{j}
,
\ell\leq j\leq k
, and similarly let
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\infty}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
a_{j}
,
j\geq\ell
. A classical form of the Gauss–Kuzmin theorem
[
28
, p. 97]
states that under the assumption (
1
),

\left|\mu(B)-\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(B)\right|\ll e^{-a\ell}\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(B)\quad\textrm{uniformly in }B\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\infty}

(5)

with a small constant
a>0
and an implied constant depending only on
L
. Under the assumption (
1
), the
\psi
-mixing coefficients of the sequence of partial quotients
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots
, defined as

\psi(\ell)=\sup_{k\geq 1}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\in\mathcal{A}_{1}^{k},\,\,\mu(B)>0\\
C\in\mathcal{A}_{k+\ell}^{\infty},\,\,\mu(C)>0\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\mu(B\cap C)}{\mu(B)\mu(C)}-1\right|,\qquad\ell\geq 1,

satisfy
\psi(\ell)\ll e^{-a\ell}
, where the constant
a>0
and the implied constant depend only on
L
and
A
. In the special case
\alpha\sim\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}
the sequence
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots
is strictly stationary with distribution

\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(a_{k}=n)=\frac{1}{\log 2}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{n(n+2)}\right),\qquad k,n\geq 1.

The mixing properties of the partial quotients lead to various probabilistic limit theorems for the sum
\sum_{k=1}^{K}f(a_{k})
depending on the growth rate of the function
f
. In particular, for any
\mu\ll\lambda
,

\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}-A_{K}}{\frac{\pi}{2\log 2}K}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1,1),

(6)

where
A_{K}=\frac{1}{\log 2}K\log K+\frac{1}{\log 2}\left(\log\frac{\pi}{2\log 2}-\gamma\right)K
. Heinrich
[
27
]
proved that in the special case
\alpha\sim\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}
the rate of convergence in the Kolmogorov metric is
\ll(\log K)^{2}/K
. See Lemma
20
in Section
5
for a similar limit law with rate for power sums
\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}^{p}
,
p>1
.

4
Proof of Theorem
2

4.1
Overview

The proof of Theorem
2
consists of three main steps. We are interested in the Diophantine sums

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
\langle n\alpha\rangle>0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n\langle n\alpha\rangle}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
\langle n\alpha\rangle<0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n|\langle n\alpha\rangle|}.

We treat the terms such that
\langle n\alpha\rangle\geq 1/(2n)
resp.
\langle n\alpha\rangle\leq-1/(2n)
, and the terms such that
0<\langle n\alpha\rangle<1/(2n)
resp.
-1/(2n)<\langle n\alpha\rangle<0
separately. The threshold is chosen as
1/(2n)
so that in the latter case
n
is an integral multiple of some convergent denominator
q_{k}
by the theorem of Legendre
[
31
, p. 30]
.

By following the approach of Schmidt
[
26
,
43
]
with certain modifications, in Section
4.3
we show that the sum of all terms that are away from the singularity concentrates around
\frac{1}{2}(\log N)^{2}
:

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
\langle n\alpha\rangle\geq 1/(2n)\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n\langle n\alpha\rangle}&=\frac{1}{2}(\log N)^{2}+o(\log N)\quad\textrm{in measure},\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
\langle n\alpha\rangle\leq-1/(2n)\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n|\langle n\alpha\rangle|}&=\frac{1}{2}(\log N)^{2}+o(\log N)\quad\textrm{in measure}.\end{split}

In Section
4.4
we estimate the sum of the terms that are close to the singularity. Very roughly, we will show that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
0<\langle n\alpha\rangle<1/(2n)\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n\langle n\alpha\rangle}\approx\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq\frac{12\log 2}{\pi^{2}}\log N\\
k\textrm{ odd}\end{subarray}}a_{k},\qquad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
-1/(2n)<\langle n\alpha\rangle<0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n|\langle n\alpha\rangle|}\approx\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq\frac{12\log 2}{\pi^{2}}\log N\\
k\textrm{ even}\end{subarray}}a_{k}.

The appearance of the sum of partial quotients explains the limit distribution
\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)
in Theorem
2
and Corollary
3
. As we will see, the sum of partial quotients with odd resp. even indices are asymptotically independent, which leads to a product measure as limit distribution in Theorem
2
.

Finally, in Section
4.5
we find the contribution of a
1
-periodic function of bounded variation
g
by showing that

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{g(n\alpha)}{n}=\log N\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+o(\log N)\quad\textrm{in measure}.

Throughout the proof, we give quantitative error terms under the assumption (
1
).

4.2
Reduction to positive Lipschitz densities

The following lemma shows that it will be enough to prove all our results under the assumption (
1
).

Lemma 6
.

Let
X_{N}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a sequence of Borel measurable functions, and let
\nu
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
X_{N}(\alpha)\overset{d}{\to}\nu
whenever
\alpha\sim\mu
with some
\mu\ll\lambda
that satisfies (
1
). Then
X_{N}(\alpha)\overset{d}{\to}\nu
whenever
\alpha\sim\mu
with some
\mu\ll\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
\mu\ll\lambda
be arbitrary with density function
h=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a smooth, positive function
h_{\varepsilon}
on
[0,1]
such that
\int_{0}^{1}h_{\varepsilon}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
and
\int_{0}^{1}|h(x)-h_{\varepsilon}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x<\varepsilon
. Let
\mu_{\varepsilon}
be the Borel probability measure on
[0,1]
with density
h_{\varepsilon}
. In particular,
|\mu(B)-\mu_{\varepsilon}(B)|<\varepsilon
for any
B\in\mathcal{B}
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any Borel set such that
\nu(\partial S)=0
. Since
\mu_{\varepsilon}
has a positive Lipschitz density, by assumption
\mu_{\varepsilon}(X_{N}\in S)\to\nu(S)
. As
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, it follows that
\mu(X_{N}\in S)\to\nu(S)
. Hence
X_{N}(\alpha)\overset{d}{\to}\nu
for
\alpha\sim\mu
, as claimed.
∎

For the rest of Section
4
, let
\alpha\sim\mu
with some
\mu\ll\lambda
that satisfies (
1
). A small constant
a>0
whose value changes from line to line, and all implied constants depend only on
L
and
A
.

4.3
Away from the singularity

Let
f_{n}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a sequence of functions that vanish on
(-\infty,0)
, are nonnegative, nonincreasing, and integrable on
[0,\infty)
. This is a more general setup than the one used by Schmidt
[
43
]
and Harman
[
26
]
, who considered indicators of intervals
f_{n}=\mathds{1}_{[0,\delta_{n}]}
under suitable assumptions on the sequence
\delta_{n}\geq 0
. Let

I_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad F(N)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}I_{n},\quad\gamma_{n}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f_{n}(nx-j),\quad\gamma_{n,k}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
\mathrm{gcd}(j,n)\leq k\end{subarray}}^{n-1}f_{n}(nx-j).

Lemma 7
.

Let
1\leq k\leq N
be integers, and assume that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
d\mid n\end{subarray}}^{N}I_{n}\leq\frac{C}{d}\sum_{n=1}^{N}I_{n}

(7)

for all positive integers
d
. Then

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}(x)-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\frac{F(N)}{k},

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}(x)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}A(k,n,m)\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{n}(nx)f_{m}(mx)\,\mathrm{d}x+2C\frac{F(N)^{2}}{k}

with some nonnegative integers
A(k,n,m)=A(k,m,n)
that satisfy
\sum_{n=1}^{\ell}A(k,n,m)\leq\ell d_{k}(m)
for any positive integers
\ell
and
m
, where
d_{k}(m)=|\{1\leq d\leq k\,:\,d\mid m\}|
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi(k,n)=|\{0\leq j\leq n-1\,:\,\mathrm{gcd}(j,n)\leq k\}|
. We have
\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{n}(nx-j)\,\mathrm{d}x=I_{n}/n
for all
0\leq j\leq n-1
, hence

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\gamma_{n}(x)-\gamma_{n,k}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=I_{n}\left(1-\frac{\varphi(k,n)}{n}\right).

Summing over
1\leq n\leq N
and using the estimate

1-\frac{\varphi(k,n)}{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d>k\end{subarray}}|\{0\leq j\leq n-1\,:\,\mathrm{gcd}(j,n)=d\}|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d>k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d},

assumption (
7
) leads to

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}(x)-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}I_{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d>k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d}=\sum_{d=k+1}^{N}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
d\mid n\end{subarray}}^{N}I_{n}\leq\sum_{d=k+1}^{N}C\frac{F(N)}{d^{2}}\leq C\frac{F(N)}{k},

as claimed.

To prove the second claim of the lemma, observe that

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}=\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}I_{n}I_{m}\frac{\varphi(k,n)}{n}\cdot\frac{\varphi(k,m)}{m},

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}(x)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n,k}(x)\gamma_{m,k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

The monotonicity assumptions made on
f_{n}
ensure that the function
y\mapsto\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{n}(nx)f_{m}(mx-y)\,\mathrm{d}x
is nondecreasing on
(-\infty,0]
and nonincreasing on
[0,\infty)
. Following the steps in the proof of
[
43
, Lemma 4]
verbatim thus yields

\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n,k}(x)\gamma_{m,k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq I_{n}I_{m}+A(k,n,m)\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{n}(nx)f_{m}(mx)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
A(k,n,m)=|\{(i,j)\in[0,m)\times[0,n)\,:\,ni-mj=0,\,\,\mathrm{gcd}(i,m)\leq k,\,\,\mathrm{gcd}(j,n)\leq k\}|
. Schmidt
[
43
, Lemma 5]
proved that
\sum_{n=1}^{m}A(k,n,m)\leq md_{k}(m)
, but the same argument shows that more generally,
\sum_{n=1}^{\ell}A(k,n,m)\leq\ell d_{k}(m)
for all positive integers
\ell
. By the previous three formulas,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}(x)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}&\leq\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}A(k,n,m)\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{n}(nx)f_{m}(mx)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\phantom{\leq{}}+\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}I_{n}I_{m}\left(1-\frac{\varphi(k,n)}{n}\cdot\frac{\varphi(k,m)}{m}\right).\end{split}

The proof of the first claim of the lemma shows that here

\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}I_{n}I_{m}\left(1-\frac{\varphi(k,n)}{n}\cdot\frac{\varphi(k,m)}{m}\right)\leq\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}I_{n}I_{m}\left(1-\frac{\varphi(k,n)}{n}+1-\frac{\varphi(k,m)}{m}\right)\leq 2C\frac{F(N)^{2}}{k},

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 8
.

We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
\langle n\alpha\rangle\geq 1/2n\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n\langle n\alpha\rangle}=\frac{1}{2}(\log N)^{2}+\xi_{1,N}(\alpha)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
\langle n\alpha\rangle\leq-1/2n\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n|\langle n\alpha\rangle|}=\frac{1}{2}(\log N)^{2}+\xi_{2,N}(\alpha)

with some error terms
\xi_{j,N}(\alpha)
,
j=1,2
that satisfy

\mu\left(\frac{|\xi_{j,N}(\alpha)|}{\log N}\geq\frac{(\log\log N)^{1/3}}{(\log N)^{1/3}}\right)\ll\frac{(\log\log N)^{1/3}}{(\log N)^{1/3}}.

Proof.

Assume first that
\alpha\sim\lambda
. Consider the sequences of functions

f_{n}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2&\text{if }0\leq x<\frac{1}{2n},\\
\frac{1}{nx}&\text{if }\frac{1}{2n}\leq x\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
0&\text{else},\end{array}\right.\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\tilde{f}_{n}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2&\text{if }0\leq x<\frac{1}{2n},\\
0&\text{else}.\end{array}\right.

Let
k\in[1,N]
be an integer, to be chosen. Let
I_{n}
,
\gamma_{n}(x)
and
\gamma_{n,k}(x)
be as in Lemma
7
, and let
\tilde{I}_{n}
,
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(x)
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{n,k}(x)
be the same quantities in terms of
\tilde{f}_{n}
. Let

S_{N}(\alpha)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\gamma_{n}(\alpha)-\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(\alpha)\right)\quad\textrm{and}\quad S_{N,k}(\alpha)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\gamma_{n,k}(\alpha)-\tilde{\gamma}_{n,k}(\alpha)\right).

Observe that
f_{n}(x)-\tilde{f}_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{nx}\mathds{1}_{\{1/(2n)\leq x\leq 1/2\}}
, thus

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
\langle n\alpha\rangle\geq 1/2n\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n\langle n\alpha\rangle}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{n(n\alpha-j)}\mathds{1}_{\{1/(2n)\leq n\alpha-j\leq 1/2\}}=S_{N}(\alpha).

The sequences
I_{n}=(1+\log n)/n
and
\tilde{I}_{n}=1/n
are decreasing in
n
, hence condition (
7
) is satisfied with
C=1
. Lemma
7
thus yields

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}|S_{N}(\alpha)-S_{N,k}(\alpha)|\leq\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}(\alpha)-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}(\alpha)\right)+\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(\alpha)-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tilde{\gamma}_{n,k}(\alpha)\right)\ll\frac{(\log N)^{2}}{k},

and

\begin{split}\mathrm{Var}_{\lambda}S_{N,k}(\alpha)&\leq\mathrm{Var}_{\lambda}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}(\alpha)\right)+\mathrm{Var}_{\lambda}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tilde{\gamma}_{n,k}(\alpha)\right)\\
&\ll\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}A(k,n,m)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{n}(nx)f_{m}(mx)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{n}(nx)\tilde{f}_{m}(mx)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)+\frac{(\log N)^{4}}{k}.\end{split}

For any
1\leq n\leq m
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{n}(nx)f_{m}(mx)\,\mathrm{d}x&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{4+4\log(m/n)-2m/n^{2}}{m^{2}}&\textrm{if }m\leq n^{2},\\
\frac{2+2\log m}{m^{2}}&\textrm{if }m>n^{2}\end{array}\right.\\
&\leq\frac{4+4\log(m/n)}{m^{2}}\end{split}

and
\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{n}(nx)\tilde{f}_{m}(mx)\,\mathrm{d}x=2/m^{2}
. Summation by parts and the estimate
\sum_{n=1}^{\ell}A(k,n,m)\leq\ell d_{k}(m)
lead to

\begin{split}\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}A(k,n,m)&\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{n}(nx)f_{m}(mx)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{n}(nx)\tilde{f}_{m}(mx)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
&\ll\sum_{m=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{m}A(k,n,m)\frac{1+\log(m/n)}{m^{2}}\\
&=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m^{2}}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\log\frac{\ell+1}{\ell}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell}A(k,n,m)+\sum_{n=1}^{m}A(k,n,m)\right)\\
&\ll\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{d_{k}(m)}{m}=\sum_{d=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
d\mid m\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{m}\ll\sum_{d=1}^{k}\frac{\log N}{d}\ll\log N\log k.\end{split}

Choosing
k=\lfloor(\log N)^{3}\rfloor
, the previous estimates show that
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}|S_{N}(\alpha)-S_{N,k}(\alpha)|\ll 1
, and
\mathrm{Var}_{\lambda}S_{N,k}(\alpha)\ll\log N\log\log N
. In particular,

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}S_{N,k}(\alpha)=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}S_{N}(\alpha)+O(1)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\log n}{n}+O(1)=\frac{1}{2}(\log N)^{2}+O(1).

An application of the Markov and the Chebyshev inequalities yield

\begin{split}\lambda&\left(\frac{1}{\log N}\left|S_{N}(\alpha)-\frac{1}{2}(\log N)^{2}\right|\geq\frac{(\log\log N)^{1/3}}{(\log N)^{1/3}}\right)\\
&\leq\lambda\left(\frac{|S_{N}(\alpha)-S_{N,k}(\alpha)|}{\log N}\gg\frac{(\log\log N)^{1/3}}{(\log N)^{1/3}}\right)+\lambda\left(\frac{|S_{N,k}(\alpha)-\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}S_{N,k}(\alpha)|}{\log N}\gg\frac{(\log\log N)^{1/3}}{(\log N)^{1/3}}\right)\\
&\ll\frac{(\log\log N)^{1/3}}{(\log N)^{1/3}}.\end{split}

Since
\alpha\mapsto 1-\alpha
is a measure preserving map, we also have

\lambda\Bigg(\frac{1}{\log N}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
\langle n\alpha\rangle\leq-1/2n\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n|\langle n\alpha\rangle|}-\frac{1}{2}(\log N)^{2}\bigg|\geq\frac{(\log\log N)^{1/3}}{(\log N)^{1/3}}\Bigg)\ll\frac{(\log\log N)^{1/3}}{(\log N)^{1/3}}.

This finishes the proof for
\alpha\sim\lambda
.

The density
\mathrm{d}\mu/\mathrm{d}\lambda
is bounded above by a constant depending only on
L
, hence the previous two tail estimates remain true for any
\mu
that satisfies (
1
).
∎

4.4
Close to the singularity

We start with a tail estimate for sums of partial quotients.

Lemma 9
.

For any integers
M\geq 0
and
K\geq 1
, and any real
t>2\log K
,

\mu\left(\sum_{k=M+1}^{M+K}a_{k}\geq tK\right)\ll\frac{1}{t}.

Proof.

Diamond and Vaaler
[
14
, Eq. (6)]
showed that for any
t>0
,

\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}\left(\left|\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}-\frac{K\log K}{\log 2}\right|\geq tK\right)\ll\frac{1}{t}.

Since the partial quotients are strictly stationary, and the assumption
t>2\log K
implies that
(K\log K)/\log 2\leq(3/4)tK
,

\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}\left(\sum_{k=M+1}^{M+K}a_{k}\geq tK\right)\leq\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}\left(\left|\sum_{k=M+1}^{M+K}a_{k}-\frac{K\log K}{\log 2}\right|\geq\frac{tK}{4}\right)\ll\frac{1}{t}.

The density
\mathrm{d}\mu/\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}
is bounded above by a constant depending only on
L
, hence the previous tail estimate remains true for any
\mu
that satisfies (
1
).
∎

For any
N\geq 1
, let
K_{N}^{*}=K_{N}^{*}(\alpha)
denote the random index for which
q_{K_{N}^{*}}\leq N<q_{K_{N}^{*}+1}
, and let
K_{N}
be a deterministic even integer such that
|K_{N}-\frac{12\log 2}{\pi^{2}}\log N|\leq 1
.

Lemma 10
.

There exists a universal constant
\tau>0
such that

\mu\left(\left|K_{N}^{*}-K_{N}\right|\geq\tau(\log N\log\log N)^{1/2}\right)\ll\frac{1}{(\log N)^{1/2}}.

Proof.

In the special case
\alpha\sim\lambda
, the convergent denominators satisfy the central limit theorem with rate

\lambda\left(\frac{\log q_{n}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12\log 2}n}{\tau_{1}n^{1/2}}\leq x\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\frac{e^{-y^{2}/2}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\,\mathrm{d}y+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/2}}\right)

with a universal constant
\tau_{1}>0
, see Morita
[
36
]
, Vallée
[
45
]
and
[
28
, p. 194–195]
. In particular,

\lambda\left(\left|\log q_{n}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12\log 2}n\right|\geq 10\tau_{1}(n\log n)^{1/2}\right)\ll\frac{1}{n^{1/2}},

and hence

\lambda\left(\left|\log q_{K_{N}}-\log N\right|\geq\tau_{2}(\log N\log\log N)^{1/2}\right)\ll\frac{1}{(\log N)^{1/2}}

with a suitably large universal constant
\tau_{2}>0
. The general fact
q_{k+2}/q_{k}\geq 2
for all
k\geq 0
implies that
|\log q_{K_{N}^{*}}-\log q_{K_{N}}|\gg|K_{N}^{*}-K_{N}|
, thus

\lambda\left(\left|K_{N}^{*}-K_{N}\right|\geq\tau_{3}(\log N\log\log N)^{1/2}\right)\leq\lambda\left(\left|\log q_{K_{N}}-\log N\right|\geq\tau_{2}(\log N\log\log N)^{1/2}\right)

with a large enough universal constant
\tau_{3}>0
, and the claim follows for
\alpha\sim\lambda
.

The density
\mathrm{d}\mu/\mathrm{d}\lambda
is bounded above by a constant depending only on
L
, hence the tail estimate in the claim remains true for any
\mu
that satisfies (
1
).
∎

Let

u_{k}=\frac{1}{q_{k-1}\|q_{k-1}\alpha\|},\,\,\,k\geq 1\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad R(x)=\sum_{1\leq j<(x/2)^{1/2}}\frac{x}{j^{2}},\,\,\,x>0.

By a classical identity of continued fractions,

u_{k}=[a_{k};a_{k+1},a_{k+2},\ldots]+[0;a_{k-1},a_{k-2},\ldots,a_{1}].

(8)

In particular,
a_{k}<u_{k}<a_{k}+2
for all
k\geq 1
. The graph of
R(x)
consists of straight line segments with a jump of size
2
at the points
2m^{2}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. More precisely, let
r_{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}1/j^{2}
with the convention
r_{0}=0
. For any integer
m\geq 0
, we have
R(x)=r_{m}x
on the interval
x\in(2m^{2},2(m+1)^{2}]
. In particular,
R(x)=(\pi^{2}/6)x+O(x^{1/2})
.

Lemma 11
.

We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
0<\langle n\alpha\rangle<1/(2n)\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n\langle n\alpha\rangle}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\textrm{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{K_{N}}R(u_{k})+\xi_{1,N}(\alpha)\quad\textrm{and}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
-1/(2n)<\langle n\alpha\rangle<0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n|\langle n\alpha\rangle|}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\textrm{ even}\end{subarray}}^{K_{N}}R(u_{k})+\xi_{2,N}(\alpha)

with some error terms
\xi_{j,N}(\alpha)
,
j=1,2
that satisfy

\mu\left(\frac{|\xi_{j,N}(\alpha)|}{\log N}\geq\frac{(\log\log N)^{1/4}}{(\log N)^{1/4}}\right)\ll\frac{(\log\log N)^{1/4}}{(\log N)^{1/4}}.

Proof.

Let
k\geq 0
and
q_{k}\leq n<q_{k+1}
such that
\|n\alpha\|<1/(2n)
. Legendre’s theorem on Diophantine approximation
[
31
, p. 30]
implies that
q_{k}\mid n
. A multiple
n=jq_{k}
satisfies
\|jq_{k}\alpha\|<1/(2jq_{k})
if and only if
1\leq j<(2q_{k}\|q_{k}\alpha\|)^{-1/2}
. Since
\mathrm{sgn}(q_{k}\alpha-p_{k})=(-1)^{k}
, we have
\langle jq_{k}\alpha\rangle>0
if
k
is even, and
\langle jq_{k}\alpha\rangle<0
if
k
is odd. Therefore

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{k}\leq n<q_{k+1}\\
0<\langle n\alpha\rangle<1/(2n)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n\langle n\alpha\rangle}&=\mathds{1}_{\{k\textrm{ even}\}}\sum_{1\leq j<(2q_{k}\|q_{k}\alpha\|)^{-1/2}}\frac{1}{jq_{k}\|jq_{k}\alpha\|}=\mathds{1}_{\{k\textrm{ even}\}}R(u_{k+1}),\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{k}\leq n<q_{k+1}\\
-1/(2n)<\langle n\alpha\rangle<0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n|\langle n\alpha\rangle|}&=\mathds{1}_{\{k\textrm{ odd}\}}\sum_{1\leq j<(2q_{k}\|q_{k}\alpha\|)^{-1/2}}\frac{1}{jq_{k}\|jq_{k}\alpha\|}=\mathds{1}_{\{k\textrm{ odd}\}}R(u_{k+1}).\end{split}

For each
k\geq 0
we have
R(u_{k+1})\ll a_{k+1}
, hence summing over
k
leads to the desired formulas with error terms

|\xi_{j,N}(\alpha)|\ll\sum_{|k-K_{N}|\leq|K_{N}^{*}-K_{N}|+2}a_{k},\qquad j=1,2.

Lemma
10
shows that outside a set of
\mu
-measure
\ll(\log N)^{-1/2}
, the previous sum has
\ll(\log N\log\log N)^{1/2}
terms. We conclude by an application of Lemma
9
with
K\approx(\log N\log\log N)^{1/2}
and
t=(\log N)^{1/4}(\log\log N)^{-1/4}
.
∎

Our next goal is to replace
u_{k}
by
a_{k}
in the sums
\sum_{k}R(u_{k})
. We follow the approach of Samur
[
41
]
, who considered the same problem under a suitable assumption on the modulus of continuity of the function. Certain modifications are thus needed to handle the discontinuous function
R
. Recall that
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{k}
denotes the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the partial quotients
a_{j}
,
\ell\leq j\leq k
. We use the convention
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{k}=\mathcal{A}_{1}^{k}
if
\ell\leq 0
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\mu^{\prime}\ll\lambda
be another Borel probability measure on
[0,1]
that satisfies (
1
). For any
k\geq 1
and
\ell\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k})-\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\prime}}\left(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k})\mid\mathcal{A}_{k-\ell}^{k+\ell}\right)\right)^{2}\ll 2^{-\ell}.

Proof.

First, assume that
k-\ell\geq 1
. Let
b_{k-\ell},b_{k-\ell+1},\ldots,b_{k+\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary, and consider the set
S=\{\alpha\in[0,1]\,:\,a_{k-\ell}=b_{k-\ell},\ldots,a_{k+\ell}=b_{k+\ell}\}
. Further, let
I
be the set of all continued fractions of the form
[0;b_{k+1},\ldots,b_{k+\ell},\ldots]
, and let
J
be the set of all continued fractions of the form
[0;b_{k-1},\ldots,b_{k-\ell},\ldots]
. Then
I,J\subseteq[0,1]
are intervals with rational endpoints of length
\leq 2^{-\ell}
each.

Now let
\alpha\in S
. Formula (
8
) shows that
u_{k}\in b_{k}+I+J=\{b_{k}+x+y\,:\,x\in I,\,y\in J\}
. If
1\not\in I+J
, then the interior of
b_{k}+I+J
does not contain any integer. In particular,
R
does not have a jump in the interior of
b_{k}+I+J
, and hence
\sup_{\alpha\in S}R(u_{k})-\inf_{\alpha\in S}R(u_{k})\leq(\pi^{2}/3)2^{-\ell}
. If
1\in I+J
, then
R
has at most one jump (of size
2
) in the interior of
b_{k}+I+J
, and hence
\sup_{\alpha\in S}R(u_{k})-\inf_{\alpha\in S}R(u_{k})\leq(\pi^{2}/3)2^{-\ell}+2
. Since
R(a_{k})=R(b_{k})
is constant on
S
, we obtain

\sup_{\alpha\in S}(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k}))-\inf_{\alpha\in S}(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k}))\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}2^{-\ell}+\mathds{1}_{\{1\in I+J\}}2.

Consequently,

\left|R(u_{k})-R(a_{k})-\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\prime}}\left(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k})\mid\mathcal{A}_{k-\ell}^{k+\ell}\right)\right|\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}2^{-\ell}+\mathds{1}_{\{1\in I+J\}}2,\quad\alpha\in S,

and so

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k})-\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\prime}}\left(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k})\mid\mathcal{A}_{k-\ell}^{k+\ell}\right)\right)^{2}\ll 2^{-\ell}+\sum_{b_{k-\ell},\ldots,b_{k+\ell}\in\mathbb{N}}\mathds{1}_{\{1\in I+J\}}\mu(S).

A simple consequence of the mixing properties of the partial quotients is that here
\mu(S)\ll\mu(H)\mu(I)
with
H=\{\alpha\in[0,1]\,:\,a_{1}=b_{k-\ell},\,a_{2}=b_{k-\ell+1},\ldots,a_{\ell+1}=b_{k}\}
. For fixed
b_{k-\ell},\ldots,b_{k}
, the total
\mu
-measure of all intervals
I
that intersect the given interval
1-J
of length
\leq 2^{-\ell}
is
\ll 2^{-\ell}
. Thus

\sum_{b_{k-\ell},\ldots,b_{k+\ell}\in\mathbb{N}}\mathds{1}_{\{1\in I+J\}}\mu(S)\ll\sum_{b_{k-\ell},\ldots,b_{k}\in\mathbb{N}}\mu(H)2^{-\ell}=2^{-\ell},

and the claim follows.

The proof in the case
k-\ell<1
is similar, the only difference is that we use
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k+\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
instead of
b_{k-\ell},\ldots,b_{k+\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 13
.

For any
k\geq 1
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k}))=\kappa+O(e^{-ak})
with the constant

\kappa=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\log 2}+\frac{1}{\log 2}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{m^{2}}\log\frac{2m^{2}+1}{2m^{2}}-2\log\frac{2m^{2}+2}{2m^{2}+1}\right).

(9)

Proof.

Let
\ell=\lfloor k/2\rfloor
, and consider
X_{k}=R(u_{k})-R(a_{k})
and
Y_{k}=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k})\mid\mathcal{A}_{k-\ell}^{k+\ell})
. Lemma
12
shows that

|\mathbb{E}_{\mu}X_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}Y_{k}|\leq\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(X_{k}-Y_{k})^{2}\right)^{1/2}\ll 2^{-k/4}.

Since
Y_{k}
is bounded and
\mathcal{A}_{k-\ell}^{\infty}
-measurable, an application of the Gauss–Kuzmin theorem (
5
) gives
|\mathbb{E}_{\mu}Y_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}Y_{k}|\ll e^{-ak}
. Here
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}Y_{k}=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}X_{k}
, hence

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k}))=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k}))+O(e^{-ak}).

In particular,
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k}))
has the same value for any
\mu\ll\lambda
that satisfies (
1
) up to an error
O(e^{-ak})
. It is thus enough to compute the expected value with respect to
\lambda
.

Fix
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k-1}\in\mathbb{N}
, and consider
I=\{\alpha\in[0,1]\,:\,a_{1}=b_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1}=b_{k-1}\}
. The endpoints of the interval
I
are
(p_{k-2}+p_{k-1})/(q_{k-2}+q_{k-1})
and
p_{k-1}/q_{k-1}
, and it has length
\lambda(I)=1/(q_{k-1}(q_{k-1}+q_{k-2}))
. Let us write
\alpha\in I
in the form
\alpha=[0;b_{1},\ldots,b_{k-1},x]=(p_{k-1}x+p_{k-2})/(q_{k-1}x+q_{k-2})
with
x=[a_{k};a_{k+1},\ldots]
, and let
y_{k}=q_{k-2}/q_{k-1}=[0;b_{k-1},b_{k-2},\ldots,b_{1}]
. Then
u_{k}=x+y_{k}
by (
8
) and
a_{k}=\lfloor x\rfloor
, hence

\int_{I}(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k}))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(\alpha)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{R(x+y_{k})-R(\lfloor x\rfloor)}{(q_{k-1}x+q_{k-2})^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\lambda(I)(1+y_{k})\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{R(x+y_{k})-R(\lfloor x\rfloor)}{(x+y_{k})^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k})\mid[0;a_{k-1},a_{k-2},\ldots,a_{1}]=y\right)=W(y)

(10)

with the function

W(y)=(1+y)\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{R(x+y)-R(\lfloor x\rfloor)}{(x+y)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad y\in[0,1].

We proceed by computing the function
W
. Recall that
R(x)=r_{m}x
on the interval
x\in(2m^{2},2(m+1)^{2}]
, where
r_{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}1/j^{2}
. We first integrate on the interval
x\in(1,2)
, where
R(\lfloor x\rfloor)=R(1)=0
and
R(x+y)=\mathds{1}_{(2-y,2)}(x)(x+y)
:

\int_{1}^{2}\frac{R(x+y)-R(\lfloor x\rfloor)}{(x+y)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{2-y}^{2}\frac{1}{x+y}\,\mathrm{d}x=\log\frac{2+y}{2}.

(11)

Now let
m\geq 1
and
2m^{2}\leq n<2(m+1)^{2}
, and let us integrate on the interval
x\in(n,n+1)
. If
2m^{2}+1\leq n\leq 2(m+1)^{2}-2
, then
R(\lfloor x\rfloor)=R(n)=r_{m}n
and
R(x+y)=r_{m}(x+y)
, thus

\int_{n}^{n+1}\frac{R(x+y)-R(\lfloor x\rfloor)}{(x+y)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=r_{m}\left(\log\frac{n+1+y}{n+y}-\frac{n}{(n+y)(n+1+y)}\right).

If
n=2m^{2}
, then
R(\lfloor x\rfloor)=R(n)=r_{m-1}n=r_{m}n-2
and
R(x+y)=r_{m}(x+y)
, thus

\int_{n}^{n+1}\frac{R(x+y)-R(\lfloor x\rfloor)}{(x+y)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=r_{m}\left(\log\frac{n+1+y}{n+y}-\frac{n}{(n+y)(n+1+y)}\right)+\frac{2}{(2m^{2}+y)(2m^{2}+1+y)}.

If
n=2(m+1)^{2}-1
, then
R(\lfloor x\rfloor)=R(n)=r_{m}n
and
R(x+y)=r_{m}(x+y)
on
x\in(n,n+1-y)
and
R(x+y)=r_{m+1}(x+y)=r_{m}(x+y)+(x+y)/(m+1)^{2}
on
x\in(n+1-y,n+1)
, thus

\begin{split}\int_{n}^{n+1}\frac{R(x+y)-R(\lfloor x\rfloor)}{(x+y)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x&=r_{m}\left(\log\frac{n+1+y}{n+y}-\frac{n}{(n+y)(n+1+y)}\right)\\
&\phantom{={}}+\frac{1}{(m+1)^{2}}\log\frac{2(m+1)^{2}+y}{2(m+1)^{2}}.\end{split}

Summing the previous three formulas over
2m^{2}\leq n<2(m+1)^{2}
yields

\begin{split}\int_{2m^{2}}^{2(m+1)^{2}}\frac{R(x+y)-R(\lfloor x\rfloor)}{(x+y)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x&=r_{m}\sum_{n=2m^{2}}^{2(m+1)^{2}-1}\left(\log\frac{n+1+y}{n+y}-\frac{n}{(n+y)(n+1+y)}\right)\\
&\phantom{={}}+\frac{2}{(2m^{2}+y)(2m^{2}+1+y)}+\frac{1}{(m+1)^{2}}\log\frac{2(m+1)^{2}+y}{2(m+1)^{2}}.\end{split}

Summation by parts then leads to

\begin{split}\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{R(x+y)-R(\lfloor x\rfloor)}{(x+y)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x&=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{-1}{(m+1)^{2}}\sum_{n=2}^{2(m+1)^{2}-1}\left(\log\frac{n+1+y}{n+y}-\frac{n}{(n+y)(n+1+y)}\right)\\
&\phantom{={}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\log\frac{n+1+y}{n+y}-\frac{n}{(n+y)(n+1+y)}\right)\\
&\phantom{={}}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(2m^{2}+y)(2m^{2}+1+y)}+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{2}}\log\frac{2m^{2}+y}{2m^{2}}.\end{split}

The sums over
n
are telescoping:

\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\left(\log\frac{n+1+y}{n+y}-\frac{n}{(n+y)(n+1+y)}\right)=\log\frac{N+y}{2+y}+\frac{N}{N+y}-\frac{1}{2+y}-\sum_{n=2}^{N}\frac{1}{n+y}.

Using the relation
\lim_{N\to\infty}(\log(N+y)-\sum_{n=2}^{N}1/(n+y))=\Gamma^{\prime}(1+y)/\Gamma(1+y)+1/(1+y)
, after some simplification we arrive at

\begin{split}&\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{-1}{(m+1)^{2}}\sum_{n=2}^{2(m+1)^{2}-1}\left(\log\frac{n+1+y}{n+y}-\frac{n}{(n+y)(n+1+y)}\right)\\
&+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\log\frac{n+1+y}{n+y}-\frac{n}{(n+y)(n+1+y)}\right)\\
&=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(-\frac{\log(2m^{2}+y)}{m^{2}}-\frac{2}{2m^{2}+y}+\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{n=2}^{2m^{2}}\frac{1}{n+y}\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\left(1+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(1+y)}{\Gamma(1+y)}+\frac{1}{1+y}\right).\end{split}

Together with (
11
), we thus obtain

\begin{split}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{R(x+y)-R(\lfloor x\rfloor)}{(x+y)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x&=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(-\frac{\log(2m^{2})}{m^{2}}-\frac{2}{2m^{2}+1+y}+\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{n=2}^{2m^{2}}\frac{1}{n+y}\right)\\
&\phantom{={}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\left(1+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(1+y)}{\Gamma(1+y)}+\frac{1}{1+y}\right),\end{split}

which yields an explicit formula for the function
W(y)
. One readily checks that
W
is
C^{1}
, and hence is in particular of bounded variation on
[0,1]
.

Formula (
10
) together with the byproduct of Lévy’s solution to the Gauss–Kuzmin problem
[
28
, p. 41]

\sup_{y\in[0,1]}\left|\lambda([0;a_{k-1},\ldots,a_{1}]\leq y)-\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}([0,y])\right|\ll e^{-ak}

thus yield the desired formula

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}(R(u_{k})-R(a_{k}))&=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}W([0;a_{k-1},\ldots,a_{1}])=\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{W(y)}{1+y}\,\mathrm{d}y+O(e^{-ak})\\
&=\frac{1}{\log 2}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{m^{2}}\log\frac{2m^{2}+1}{2m^{2}}-2\log\frac{2m^{2}+2}{2m^{2}+1}\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\log 2}+O(e^{-ak}).\end{split}

Lemma 15
.

For any
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}\left(e^{i(t_{1}R(a_{1})+t_{2}R(a_{2}))}-1\right)&=-\frac{\pi^{3}}{12\log 2}|t_{1}|-i\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\log 2}t_{1}\log|t_{1}|-i\kappa^{\prime}t_{1}\\

(12)

Proof.

We may assume that

, otherwise the error term in the claim is larger than the main term. Recall that

Let us write

\begin{split}\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}\left(e^{it_{1}R(a_{1})}-1\right)\left(e^{it_{2}R(a_{2})}-1\right)\right|&\ll\sum_{b_{1},b_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{|e^{it_{1}R(b_{1})}-1|\cdot|e^{it_{2}R(b_{2})}-1|}{b_{1}^{2}b_{2}^{2}}\\

\frac{X_{K}+Y_{K}}{2}-\frac{2\log 2}{\pi}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{X_{K}-Y_{K}}{2}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Cauchy}

(16)

with rate
\ll K^{-1/2}
in the Kolmogorov metric.

Proof.

Let
\phi(t_{1},t_{2})=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}e^{it_{1}X_{K}+it_{2}Y_{K}}
be the characteristic function of the random vector
(X_{K},Y_{K})
, and let
\varphi(t)=\exp(-|t|(1+\frac{2i}{\pi}\mathrm{sgn}(t)\log|t|))
be the characteristic function of
\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)
. We start by estimating
\phi(t_{1},t_{2})
in the range
|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|\leq K/(\log K)^{3}
.

The random variables

Z_{k}=Z_{k,K}=\frac{t_{1}}{\frac{\pi^{3}}{24\log 2}K}R(a_{2k-1})+\frac{t_{2}}{\frac{\pi^{3}}{24\log 2}K}R(a_{2k}),\quad 1\leq k\leq K/2

are
\psi
-mixing with exponential rate. Observe that with a suitable constant
C>0
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(1-\cos Z_{k})&\geq\mu\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\leq|Z_{k}|\leq\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)\\
&\gg\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}\left(\frac{CK}{|t_{1}|}\leq a_{1}\leq\frac{2CK}{|t_{1}|},\,\,a_{2}=1\right)+\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}\left(a_{1}=1,\,\,\frac{CK}{|t_{2}|}\leq a_{2}\leq\frac{2CK}{|t_{2}|}\right)\\
&\gg\frac{|t_{1}|+|t_{2}|}{K}.\end{split}

Using
R(x)\ll x
and
\mu(a_{k}=n)\ll\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(a_{1}=n)\ll 1/n^{2}
, we also have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}|e^{iZ_{k}}-1|&\ll\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\min\left\{\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}R(a_{2k-1}),1\right\}+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\min\left\{\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}R(a_{2k}),1\right\}\\
&\ll\sum_{1\leq n\leq K/|t_{1}|}\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}n\cdot\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\sum_{n>K/|t_{1}|}\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\sum_{1\leq n\leq K/|t_{2}|}\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}n\cdot\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\sum_{n>K/|t_{2}|}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\\
&\ll\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{1}|}+\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{2}|}.\end{split}

An application of
[
27
, Lemma 1]
with
m=P\approx(\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{1}|}+\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{2}|})^{-1/2}\gg(\log K)^{3/2}(\log\log K)^{-1/2}
thus gives

\begin{split}\Bigg|\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\exp&\left(i\sum_{k=1}^{K/2}Z_{k}\right)-\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K/2}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(e^{iZ_{k}}-1)\right)\Bigg|\\
&\ll K\left(\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{1}|}+\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{2}|}\right)^{2}e^{-a(|t_{1}|+|t_{2}|)}+e^{-am}K\left(\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{1}|}+\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{2}|}\right)\\
&\ll\frac{|t_{1}|^{3/2}+|t_{2}|^{3/2}}{K^{1/2}}e^{-a(|t_{1}|+|t_{2}|)}+\frac{|t_{1}|(1+|\log|t_{1}||)+|t_{2}|(1+|\log|t_{2}||)}{K^{100}}.\end{split}

(17)

In the second step we used the fact that the assumption
|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|\leq K/(\log K)^{3}
ensures that

\left(\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{1}|}+\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{2}|}\right)^{2}\ll\frac{|t_{1}|^{3/2}+|t_{2}|^{3/2}}{K^{3/2}}.

Since
Z_{k}
is
\mathcal{A}_{2k-1}^{\infty}
-measurable, by the Gauss–Kuzmin theorem (
5
),

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(e^{iZ_{k}}-1)-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}(e^{iZ_{k}}-1)\right|\ll e^{-ak}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}|e^{iZ_{k}}-1|\ll e^{-ak}\left(\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{1}|}+\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{2}|}\right).

The variables
Z_{k}
,
1\leq k\leq K/2
are identically distributed under
\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}
, and thus we can apply Lemma
15
to deduce

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}(e^{iZ_{k}}-1)&=-\frac{2|t_{1}|}{K}-\frac{4i}{\pi}\cdot\frac{t_{1}\log|t_{1}|}{K}+i\frac{A_{K}}{\frac{\pi^{3}}{48\log 2}K^{2}}t_{1}-\frac{2|t_{2}|}{K}-\frac{4i}{\pi}\cdot\frac{t_{2}\log|t_{2}|}{K}+i\frac{A_{K}}{\frac{\pi^{3}}{48\log 2}K^{2}}t_{2}\\
&\phantom{={}}+O\left(\frac{|t_{1}|^{3/2}+|t_{2}|^{3/2}}{K^{3/2}}\right).\end{split}

By the previous two formulas,

\begin{split}\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K/2}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(e^{iZ_{k}}-1)\right)&=\varphi(t_{1})\varphi(t_{2})\exp\left(i\frac{A_{K}}{\frac{\pi^{3}}{24\log 2}K}(t_{1}+t_{2})\right)\\
&\phantom{={}}\times\left(1+O\left(\frac{|t_{1}|^{3/2}+|t_{2}|^{3/2}}{K^{1/2}}+\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{1}|}+\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{2}|}\right)\right).\end{split}

The previous formula together with (
17
) and the fact that
|\varphi(t_{1})\varphi(t_{2})|=\exp(-(|t_{1}|+|t_{2}|))
leads to the estimate

\begin{split}\left|\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-\varphi(t_{1})\varphi(t_{2})\right|&\ll\left(\frac{|t_{1}|^{3/2}+|t_{2}|^{3/2}}{K^{1/2}}+\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{1}|}+\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}\log\frac{K}{|t_{2}|}\right)e^{-a(|t_{1}|+|t_{2}|)}\\
&\phantom{\ll{}}+\frac{|t_{1}|(1+|\log|t_{1}||)+|t_{2}|(1+|\log|t_{2}||)}{K^{100}}\end{split}

(18)

uniformly in
|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|\leq K/(\log K)^{3}
.

Assuming
|t_{1}|\leq 1/K
, we have

|e^{it_{1}X_{K}}-1|\leq\min\left\{|t_{1}X_{K}|,2\right\}\ll|t_{1}|\log K+\min\left\{\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k},1\right\}.

An application of Lemma

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}|e^{it_{1}X_{K}}-1|\ll|t_{1}|\log K+\int_{0}^{1}\mu\left(\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\geq x\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\ll|t_{1}|\log K+\int_{2|t_{1}|\log K}^{1}\frac{|t_{1}|}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\ll|t_{1}|\log\frac{1}{|t_{1}|},

hence

|\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi(0,t_{2})|\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}|e^{it_{1}X_{K}}-1|\ll|t_{1}|\log\frac{1}{|t_{1}|}\quad\textrm{uniformly in }|t_{1}|\leq 1/K.

(19)

The same argument shows that

|\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi(t_{1},0)|\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}|e^{it_{2}Y_{K}}-1|\ll|t_{2}|\log\frac{1}{|t_{2}|}\quad\textrm{uniformly in }|t_{2}|\leq 1/K.

(20)

Finally, assume that
|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|\leq 1/K
. Then

|e^{it_{1}X_{K}}-1|\cdot|e^{it_{2}Y_{K}}-1|\ll\left(|t_{1}|\log K+\min\left\{\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k},1\right\}\right)\left(|t_{2}|\log K+\min\left\{\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k},1\right\}\right).

By Lemma

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left(\min\left\{\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k},1\right\}\cdot\min\left\{\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k},1\right\}\right)&\leq\int_{0}^{1}\mu\left(\frac{|t_{1}|}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\cdot\frac{|t_{2}|}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\geq x\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\ll|t_{1}t_{2}|(\log K)^{2}+\int_{4|t_{1}t_{2}|(\log K)^{2}}^{1}\frac{|t_{1}t_{2}|^{1/2}}{x^{1/2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\ll|t_{1}t_{2}|^{1/2},\end{split}

hence

|\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi(t_{1},0)-\phi(0,t_{2})+1|\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left(|e^{it_{1}X_{K}}-1|\cdot|e^{it_{2}Y_{K}}-1|\right)\ll|t_{1}t_{2}|^{1/2}

uniformly in
|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|\leq 1/K
.

denote the cumulative distribution function of
(X_{K},Y_{K})
, and let
G
be the cumulative distribution function of
\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)
. In particular,
\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)\otimes\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)
has characteristic function
\varphi(t_{1})\varphi(t_{2})
and cumulative distribution function

.

We first show that

and

with rate
\ll K^{-1/2}
. Choosing

in the classical (one-dimensional) Esseen inequality
[

, p. 142]
leads to

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|F(x,\infty)-G(x)|\ll\int_{-K^{1/2}}^{K^{1/2}}\left|\frac{\phi(t,0)-\varphi(t)}{t}\right|\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{K^{1/2}}.

Therefore

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}|F(\infty,y)-G(y)|\ll K^{-1/2}

We now prove that
(X_{K},Y_{K})\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)\otimes\mathrm{Stab}(1,1)

together with the one-dimensional rates established above lead to

\sup_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}|F(x,y)-G(x)G(y)|\ll\int_{-K^{1/2}}^{K^{1/2}}\int_{-K^{1/2}}^{K^{1/2}}\left|\frac{\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi(t_{1},0)\phi(0,t_{2})}{t_{1}t_{2}}\right|\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{2}+\frac{1}{K^{1/2}}.

Formulas (
19
)–(
21
) show that in the range
|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|\leq 1/K

|\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi(t_{1},0)\phi(0,t_{2})|\leq|\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi(t_{1},0)-\phi(0,t_{2})+1|+|\phi(t_{1},0)-1|\cdot|1-\phi(0,t_{2})|\ll|t_{1}t_{2}|^{1/2},

hence

\int_{-1/K}^{1/K}\int_{-1/K}^{1/K}\left|\frac{\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi(t_{1},0)\phi(0,t_{2})}{t_{1}t_{2}}\right|\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{2}\ll\frac{1}{K}.

Formula (
19
) shows that in the range

\int_{1/K}^{K^{1/2}}\int_{-1/K}^{1/K}\left|\frac{\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi(t_{1},0)\phi(0,t_{2})}{t_{1}t_{2}}\right|\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{2}\ll\frac{(\log K)^{2}}{K}.

The integral on the rectangles

and

\begin{split}|\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-&\phi(t_{1},0)\phi(0,t_{2})|\\
&\leq|\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-\varphi(t_{1})\varphi(t_{2})|+|\phi(t_{1},0)|\cdot|\phi(0,t_{2})-\varphi(t_{2})|+|\varphi(t_{2})|\cdot|\phi(t_{1},0)-\varphi(t_{1})|\\

&\phantom{\ll{}}+\frac{|t_{1}|(1+|\log|t_{1}||)+|t_{2}|(1+|\log|t_{2}||)}{K^{100}},\end{split}

hence

\int_{1/K}^{K^{1/2}}\int_{1/K}^{K^{1/2}}\left|\frac{\phi(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi(t_{1},0)\phi(0,t_{2})}{t_{1}t_{2}}\right|\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{2}\ll\frac{\log K}{K^{1/2}}.

Observe that

Lemma 17
.

Let
g
be a
1
-periodic function that is of bounded variation on
[0,1]
. Then

\mu\left(\left|\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{g(n\alpha)}{n}-\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\geq\frac{1}{(\log N)^{1/2}}\right)\ll\frac{1}{(\log N)^{1/2}}

with an implied constant depending only on
L
,
A
and
g
.

Proof.

Let

D_{n}(\alpha)=\sup_{I\subseteq[0,1]}\left|\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{I}(\{\ell\alpha\})-n\lambda(I)\right|

denote the discrepancy of the point set
\{\ell\alpha\}
,
1\leq\ell\leq n
, where the supremum is over all intervals
I\subseteq[0,1]
. Koksma’s inequality
[
33
, p. 143]
states that
|\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}g(\ell\alpha)-n\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x|\leq V(g;[0,1])D_{n}(\alpha)
, where
V(g;[0,1])
denotes the total variation of
g
on
[0,1]
. Summation by parts yields

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{g(n\alpha)}{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}g(\ell\alpha)}{n(n+1)}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}g(\ell\alpha),

thus

\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{g(n\alpha)}{n}-\log N\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\ll\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{D_{n}(\alpha)}{n^{2}}+\frac{D_{N}(\alpha)}{N}.

A classical discrepancy estimate
[
33
, p. 126]
states that for any
q_{k}\leq n<q_{k+1}
, we have
D_{n}(\alpha)\leq 2(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k+1})
. Hence

\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{g(n\alpha)}{n}-\log N\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\ll\sum_{k=0}^{K_{N}^{*}}\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k+1}}{q_{k}},

where
K_{N}^{*}=K_{N}^{*}(\alpha)
is the random index for which
q_{K_{N}^{*}}\leq N<q_{K_{N}^{*}+1}
. Since
\frac{12\log 2}{\pi^{2}}<1
, Lemma
10
shows that
K_{N}^{*}+1\leq\log N
outside a set of
\mu
-measure
\ll(\log N)^{-1/2}
. Since
q_{k}\geq F_{k+1}
for all
k\geq 0
, with
F_{1}=F_{2}=1
,
F_{k}=F_{k-1}+F_{k-2}
denoting the sequence of Fibonacci numbers,

\mu\left(\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{g(n\alpha)}{n}-\log N\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\gg\sum_{1\leq k\leq\log N}\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}}{F_{k}}\right)\ll\frac{1}{(\log N)^{1/2}}.

As
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}F_{k}^{-1/2}<\infty
, the inequality
\sum_{1\leq k\leq\log N}(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k})/F_{k}\gg(\log N)^{1/2}
implies that
(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k})/F_{k}^{1/2}\gg(\log N)^{1/2}
for some
k
. An application of Lemma
9
thus gives

\begin{split}\mu\left(\sum_{1\leq k\leq\log N}\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}}{F_{k}}\gg(\log N)^{1/2}\right)&\leq\sum_{1\leq k\leq\log N}\mu\left(\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}}{F_{k}^{1/2}}\gg(\log N)^{1/2}\right)\\
&\ll\sum_{1\leq k\leq\log N}\frac{k}{F_{k}^{1/2}(\log N)^{1/2}}\\
&\ll\frac{1}{(\log N)^{1/2}},\end{split}

and the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 18

If

An application of the union bound shows that

(22)

with some constant

. Here

\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}X_{k}\leq\frac{1}{\log 2}\sum_{1\leq n\leq t^{1/p}K}n^{2p}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{n(n+2)}\right)\ll t^{2-1/p}K^{2p-1},

therefore

\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}\left(\sum_{k=M+1}^{M+K}X_{k}\right)\ll\sum_{k,\ell=M+1}^{M+K}e^{-a|k-\ell|}t^{2-1/p}K^{2p-1}\ll t^{2-1/p}K^{2p}.

Further,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}\sum_{k=M+1}^{M+K}X_{k}=\frac{K}{\log 2}\sum_{1\leq n\leq t^{1/p}K}n^{p}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{n(n+2)}\right)\ll t^{1-1/p}K^{p},

which is less than
tK^{p}/2
provided that
t
is large enough. The Chebyshev inequality thus yields

\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}\left(\sum_{k=M+1}^{M+K}X_{k}\geq tK^{p}\right)\leq\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}\left(\left|\sum_{k=M+1}^{M+K}(X_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}X_{k})\right|\geq\frac{tK^{p}}{2}\right)\ll t^{-1/p},

which together with (
22
) proves the claim for the Gauss measure.

Assume next that
1/2\leq p<1
. Then

\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}X_{k}\ll\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}t^{2-1/p}K^{2p-1}&\textrm{if }1/2<p<1,\\
\log(tK)&\textrm{if }p=1/2,\end{array}\right.

and

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}\sum_{k=M+1}^{M+K}X_{k}=m_{p}K+O(t^{1-1/p}K^{p}),

where the error term is smaller than
tK^{p}/2
provided that
t
is large enough. We conclude similarly by the Chebyshev inequality.

The density
\mathrm{d}\mu/\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}
is bounded above by a constant depending only on
L
, hence the tail estimates in the claim remain true for any
\mu
that satisfies (
1
).
∎

The following lemma improves
[
5
, Corollary 3.2]
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
p>1
. For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|t|\leq 1/2
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}(e^{ita_{1}^{p}}-1)=-\frac{1}{\log 2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)|t|^{1/p}\left(1-i\,\mathrm{sgn}(t)\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)-H(t)+O(\varepsilon(t)),

where

H(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}id_{p}t&\textrm{if }1<p<2,\\
\frac{i}{\log 2}t\log\frac{1}{|t|}&\textrm{if }p=2,\\
0&\textrm{if }p>2,\end{array}\right.

with the constant

d_{p}=\frac{1}{(p-1)\log 2}+\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p}-\lfloor x\rfloor^{p}+x^{p-1}}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad 1<p<2,

(23)

and

\varepsilon(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|t|^{2/p}&\textrm{if }1<p\leq 2,\\
|t|^{1/(p-1)}&\textrm{if }p>2.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the claim for
0<t\leq 1/2
, as the case of negative
t
follows from complex conjugation. The expected value in the claim can be expressed as

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}(e^{ita_{1}^{p}}-1)=\int_{0}^{1}(e^{it\lfloor 1/x\rfloor^{p}}-1)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(x)=\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{it\lfloor x\rfloor^{p}}-1}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\log 2}(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}),

where

I_{1}=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{itx^{p}}-1}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad I_{2}=\int_{1}^{\infty}(e^{itx^{p}}-1)\left(\frac{1}{x^{2}+x}-\frac{1}{x^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad I_{3}=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{it\lfloor x\rfloor^{p}}-e^{itx^{p}}}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

A substitution gives
I_{1}=t^{1/p}\int_{t^{1/p}}^{\infty}(e^{ix^{p}}-1)/x^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
, and here the integral extended to
[0,\infty)
evaluates to

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix^{p}}-1}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+i\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right).

In particular,

I_{1}=-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)t^{1/p}\left(1-i\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)+I_{1}^{\prime}

with
I_{1}^{\prime}=-t^{1/p}\int_{0}^{t^{1/p}}(e^{ix^{p}}-1)/x^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
, and it remains to show that

\frac{1}{\log 2}(I_{1}^{\prime}+I_{2}+I_{3})=-H(t)+O(\varepsilon(t)).

(24)

Assume first that
1<p<2
. Then

\begin{split}I_{1}^{\prime}&=-t^{1/p}\int_{0}^{t^{1/p}}\frac{ix^{p}}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x-t^{1/p}\int_{0}^{t^{1/p}}\frac{e^{ix^{p}}-1-ix^{p}}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{i}{p-1}t+O\left(t^{1/p}\int_{0}^{t^{1/p}}x^{2p-2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
&=-\frac{i}{p-1}t+O(t^{2}),\end{split}

as well as

\begin{split}I_{2}&=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{-itx^{p}}{x^{3}+x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1+itx^{p}-e^{itx^{p}}}{x^{3}+x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-it\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p-1}}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x+O\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\min\{t^{2}x^{2p},1+tx^{p}\}}{x^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
&=-it\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p-1}}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x+O(t^{2/p}),\end{split}

and

\begin{split}I_{3}&=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{it(\lfloor x\rfloor^{p}-x^{p})}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(e^{itx^{p}}-1)it(\lfloor x\rfloor^{p}-x^{p})}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\phantom{={}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{itx^{p}}\left(e^{it(\lfloor x\rfloor^{p}-x^{p})}-1-it(\lfloor x\rfloor^{p}-x^{p})\right)}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=it\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\lfloor x\rfloor^{p}-x^{p}}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x+O\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\min\{tx^{p},1\}tx^{p-1}}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\min\{t^{2}x^{2p-2},1+tx^{p-1}\}}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
&=it\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\lfloor x\rfloor^{p}-x^{p}}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x+O(t^{2/p}).\end{split}

The previous three formulas yield (
24
).

Assume next that
p=2
. Then

|I_{1}^{\prime}|\leq t^{1/2}\int_{0}^{t^{1/2}}\frac{|e^{ix^{2}}-1|}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq t,

as well as

\begin{split}I_{2}&=\int_{1}^{t^{-1/2}}\frac{-itx^{2}}{x^{3}+x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{1}^{t^{-1/2}}\frac{1+itx^{2}-e^{itx^{2}}}{x^{3}+x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{t^{-1/2}}^{\infty}\frac{1-e^{itx^{2}}}{x^{3}+x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-it\log\frac{t^{-1/2}+1}{2}+O\left(\int_{1}^{t^{-1/2}}\frac{t^{2}x^{4}}{x^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{t^{-1/2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
&=-\frac{it}{2}\log\frac{1}{t}+O(t),\end{split}

and

\begin{split}I_{3}&=\int_{1}^{1/t}\frac{e^{itx^{2}}it(\lfloor x\rfloor^{2}-x^{2})}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{1}^{1/t}\frac{e^{itx^{2}}(e^{it(\lfloor x\rfloor^{2}-x^{2})}-1-it(\lfloor x\rfloor^{2}-x^{2}))}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{1/t}^{\infty}\frac{e^{it\lfloor x\rfloor^{2}}-e^{itx^{2}}}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-it\int_{1}^{1/t}\frac{e^{itx^{2}}\{x\}(\lfloor x\rfloor+x)}{x^{2}+x}\,\mathrm{d}x+O\left(\int_{1}^{1/t}\frac{t^{2}x^{2}}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{1/t}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
&=-2it\int_{1}^{1/t}\frac{e^{itx^{2}}\{x\}}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x+O(t).\end{split}

In the last step we replaced
\lfloor x\rfloor+x
by
2x
in the numerator, and
x^{2}+x
by
x^{2}
in the denominator. Here the average value
1/2
of
\{x\}
would give

-2it\int_{1}^{1/t}\frac{e^{itx^{2}}(1/2)}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{it}{2}\left(\int_{t}^{1}\frac{1}{y}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{t}^{1}\frac{e^{iy}-1}{y}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{1}^{1/t}\frac{e^{iy}}{y}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)=-\frac{it}{2}\log\frac{1}{t}+O(t),

where the last integral was estimated using integration by parts. On the other hand,
1/x=1/n+O(1/n^{2})
and
e^{itx^{2}}=e^{itn^{2}}+O(tn)
on the interval
x\in[n,n+1]
, hence

\int_{n}^{n+1}\frac{e^{itx^{2}}(\{x\}-1/2)}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{e^{itn^{2}}}{n}\int_{n}^{n+1}\left(\{x\}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}+t\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}+t\right),

and by summing over
1\leq n\leq 1/t
, we obtain

-2it\int_{1}^{1/t}\frac{e^{itx^{2}}(\{x\}-1/2)}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=O(t).

Therefore
I_{3}=-\frac{it}{2}\log\frac{1}{t}+O(t)
, and (
24
) follows.

Finally, assume that
p>2
. Then

\begin{split}|I_{1}^{\prime}|&\leq t^{1/p}\int_{0}^{t^{1/p}}x^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}x\ll t,\\
|I_{2}|&\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\min\{tx^{p},2\}}{x^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x\ll t^{2/p},\\
|I_{3}|&\ll\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\min\{tx^{p-1},1\}}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\ll t^{1/(p-1)},\end{split}

and (
24
) follows.
∎

Lemma 20
.

Let
p>1
. For any even integer

, let

X_{K}=\frac{1}{\sigma K^{p}}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\

k\textrm{ even}\end{subarray}}^{K}a_{k}^{p}+A_{K}\Bigg),

where

A_{K}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{d_{p}}{2}K&\textrm{if }1<p<2,\\
\frac{1}{\log 2}K\log K&\textrm{if }p=2,\\
0&\textrm{if }p>2,\end{array}\right.

23

\sigma=\left(\frac{1}{2\log 2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)^{p}

(X_{K},Y_{K})\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,1)\otimes\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,1)
with rate
\ll\varepsilon_{K}\log K
in the Kolmogorov metric, and

\frac{X_{K}+Y_{K}}{2^{p}}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,1)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{X_{K}-Y_{K}}{2^{p}}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,0)

with rate
\ll\varepsilon_{K}
in the Kolmogorov metric, where

\varepsilon_{K}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}K^{-1}&\textrm{if }1<p\leq 2,\\

Proof.

Following the steps in the proof of Lemma
15
shows that for any
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|\leq 1/2
,

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}\left(e^{it_{1}a_{1}^{p}}-1\right)\left(e^{it_{2}a_{2}^{p}}-1\right)\right|\ll|t_{1}|^{2/p}+|t_{2}|^{2/p}.

Therefore
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}\left(e^{i(t_{1}a_{1}^{p}+t_{2}a_{2}^{p})}-1\right)=w_{p}(t_{1})+w_{p}(t_{2})+O(|t_{1}|^{2/p}+|t_{2}|^{2/p})
with the same function

. An application of Lemma
19

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}\left(e^{i(t_{1}a_{1}^{p}+t_{2}a_{2}^{p})}-1\right)&=-\frac{\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}{\log 2}|t_{1}|^{1/p}\left(1-i\,\mathrm{sgn}(t_{1})\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)-H(t_{1})\\
&\phantom{={}}-\frac{\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}{\log 2}|t_{2}|^{1/p}\left(1-i\,\mathrm{sgn}(t_{2})\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)-H(t_{2})\\

(25)

Now let
\phi(t_{1},t_{2})=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(e^{it_{1}X_{K}+it_{2}Y_{K}})
be the characteristic function of the random vector
(X_{K},Y_{K})
, and let

Similarly to the proof of Lemma

, we deduce

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{\mu}}(1-\cos Z_{k})\gg\frac{|t_{1}|^{1/p}+|t_{2}|^{1/p}}{K}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{\mu}|e^{iZ_{k}}-1|\ll\frac{|t_{1}|^{1/p}+|t_{2}|^{1/p}}{K}.

Finally, we estimate the contribution of a
1
-periodic function with a lower order singularity at integers, and then prove Theorem
4
.

Lemma 21
.

Let
g
be a
1
-periodic function such that
|g(x)|\ll 1/\|x\|^{p^{\prime}}
with some constant
0\leq p^{\prime}<p
. Then

\mu\left(\frac{1}{(\log N)^{p}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{g(n\alpha)}{n^{p}}\right|\geq\frac{1}{(\log N)^{1/2}}\right)\ll\frac{1}{(\log N)^{1/2}}

with an implied constant depending only on
L
,
A
,
g
and
p
.

Proof.

We may assume that
1<p^{\prime}<p
. For any
k\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{q_{k}\leq n<q_{k+1}}\frac{|g(n\alpha)|}{n^{p}}\ll\frac{1}{q_{k}^{p}}\sum_{1\leq n<q_{k+1}}\frac{1}{\|n\alpha\|^{p^{\prime}}}.

For any two distinct integers
1\leq n,n^{\prime}<q_{k+1}
, we have
1\leq|n-n^{\prime}|<q_{k+1}
, hence by the best approximation property of continued fractions,
\|n\alpha-n^{\prime}\alpha\|\geq\|q_{k}\alpha\|
. An application of the pigeonhole principle thus shows that

\sum_{1\leq n<q_{k+1}}\frac{1}{\|n\alpha\|^{p^{\prime}}}\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(j\|q_{k}\alpha\|)^{p^{\prime}}}\ll\frac{1}{\|q_{k}\alpha\|^{p^{\prime}}}\ll a_{k+1}^{p^{\prime}}q_{k}^{p^{\prime}},

and consequently

\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{g(n\alpha)}{n^{p}}\right|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K_{N}^{*}}\sum_{q_{k}\leq n<q_{k+1}}\frac{|g(n\alpha)|}{n^{p}}\ll\sum_{k=0}^{K_{N}^{*}}\frac{a_{k+1}^{p^{\prime}}}{q_{k}^{p-p^{\prime}}},

where
K_{N}^{*}=K_{N}^{*}(\alpha)
is the random index for which
q_{K_{N}^{*}}\leq N<q_{K_{N}^{*}+1}
. Lemma
10
shows that
K_{N}^{*}+1\leq\log N
outside a set of
\mu
-measure
\ll(\log N)^{-1/2}
. Since
q_{k}\geq F_{k+1}
, with
F_{k}
denoting the sequence of Fibonacci numbers,

\mu\left(\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{g(n\alpha)}{n^{p}}\right|\gg\sum_{1\leq k\leq\log N}\frac{a_{k}^{p^{\prime}}}{F_{k}^{p-p^{\prime}}}\right)\ll\frac{1}{(\log N)^{1/2}}.

As
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}1/F_{k}^{(p-p^{\prime})/2}<\infty
, the inequality
\sum_{1\leq k\leq\log N}a_{k}^{p^{\prime}}/F_{k}^{p-p^{\prime}}\geq(\log N)^{p-1/2}
implies that
a_{k}^{p^{\prime}}/F_{k}^{(p-p^{\prime})/2}\gg(\log N)^{p-1/2}
for some
1\leq k\leq\log N
. Therefore

\begin{split}\mu\left(\sum_{1\leq k\leq\log N}\frac{a_{k}^{p^{\prime}}}{F_{k}^{p-p^{\prime}}}\gg(\log N)^{p-1/2}\right)&\leq\sum_{1\leq k\leq\log N}\mu\left(\frac{a_{k}^{p^{\prime}}}{F_{k}^{(p-p^{\prime})/2}}\gg(\log N)^{p-1/2}\right)\\
&\ll\sum_{1\leq k\leq\log N}\frac{1}{F_{k}^{(p-p^{\prime})/(2p^{\prime})}(\log N)^{(p-1/2)/p^{\prime}}}\\
&\ll(\log N)^{-(p-1/2)/p^{\prime}}.\end{split}

Here
(p-1/2)/p^{\prime}>1/2
, and the claim follows.
∎

|\xi_{j,N}(\alpha)|\ll\sum_{|k-K_{N}|\leq|K_{N}^{*}-K_{N}|+2}a_{k}^{p}.

Lemma
10
shows that outside a set of
\mu
-measure
\ll(\log N)^{-1/2}
, the previous sum has
\ll(\log N\log\log N)^{1/2}
terms. An application of Lemma
18
with
K\approx(\log N\log\log N)^{1/2}
and
t=(\log N/\log\log N)^{p^{2}/(2(p+1))}
thus yields

\mu\left(\frac{|\xi_{j,N}(\alpha)|}{(\log N)^{p}}\geq\left(\frac{\log\log N}{\log N}\right)^{\frac{p}{2(p+1)}}\right)\ll\left(\frac{\log\log N}{\log N}\right)^{\frac{p}{2(p+1)}}.

Lemma
12
remains true with
R
replaced by
R_{p}
, using the fact that in the range
1<p<3/2
the random variables
a_{k}^{p-1}
have finite second moment. Repeating the computations in Lemma
13
with
R
replaced by
R_{p}
leads to
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(R_{p}(u_{k})-R_{p}(a_{k}))=\kappa_{p}+O(e^{-ak})
with the constant

\begin{split}\kappa_{p}&=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12\log 2}\cdot\frac{2^{p}}{p-1}+\frac{1}{\log 2}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{2p}}\sum_{n=1}^{2m^{2}}n^{p}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{n(n+2)}\right)\\
&\phantom{={}}+\frac{\zeta(2p)}{\log 2}\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\frac{N^{p-1}}{p-1}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{p}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{n(n+2)}\right)\right).\end{split}

To evaluate the limit in the last line, let us apply the Euler–Maclaurin summation formula

\sum_{n=1}^{N}h(n)=\int_{1}^{N}h(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{h(1)+h(N)}{2}+\int_{1}^{N}h^{\prime}(x)(\{x\}-1/2)\,\mathrm{d}x

to the function
h(x)=x^{p}\log(1+\frac{1}{x(x+2)})
, whose derivative is

h^{\prime}(x)=x^{p-1}\left(p\log\left(1+\frac{1}{x(x+1)}\right)-\frac{2}{(x+1)(x+2)}\right)\ll x^{p-3}.

We have

\int_{1}^{N}x^{p}\frac{1}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{N^{p-1}}{p-1}-\frac{1}{p-1},

and using integration by parts,

\begin{split}\int_{1}^{N}x^{p}&\left(\log\left(1+\frac{1}{x(x+2)}\right)-\frac{1}{x^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-\frac{1}{p+1}\left(\log\frac{4}{3}-1\right)+O(N^{p-2})-\int_{1}^{N}\frac{x^{p+1}}{p+1}\left(\frac{-2}{x(x+1)(x+2)}+\frac{2}{x^{3}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-\frac{1}{p+1}\left(\log\frac{4}{3}-1\right)-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p-2}(6x+4)}{(x+1)(x+2)}\,\mathrm{d}x+O(N^{p-2}).\end{split}

Adding the previous two formulas yields the value of
\int_{1}^{N}h(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
up to
o(1)
error, and finally letting
N\to\infty
leads to the value of the limit

\begin{split}\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigg(\frac{N^{p-1}}{p-1}-&\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{p}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{n(n+2)}\right)\bigg)=\\
&\frac{2}{(p-1)(p+1)}-\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}\log\frac{4}{3}+\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p-2}(6x+4)}{(x+1)(x+2)}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&-\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\{x\}-\frac{1}{2}\right)x^{p-1}\left(p\log\left(1+\frac{1}{x(x+2)}\right)-\frac{2}{(x+1)(x+2)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Lemma
14
now reads

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\textrm{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{K_{N}}R_{p}(u_{k})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\textrm{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{K_{N}}R_{p}(a_{k})+\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2}K_{N}+\xi_{1,N}(\alpha)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\textrm{ even}\end{subarray}}^{K_{N}}R_{p}(u_{k})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\textrm{ even}\end{subarray}}^{K_{N}}R_{p}(a_{k})+\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2}K_{N}+\xi_{2,N}(\alpha)

with some error terms
\xi_{j,N}(\alpha)
,
j=1,2
that satisfy

\mu\left(\frac{|\xi_{j,N}(\alpha)|}{(\log N)^{p}}\geq\frac{1}{(\log N)^{\frac{2p-1}{3}}}\right)\ll\frac{1}{(\log N)^{\frac{2p-1}{3}}}.

Note that
\frac{2p-1}{3}>\frac{p}{2(p+1)}
, thus this error is negligible. Following the steps in Lemma
15
, relying on Lemma
19
instead of (
13
) yields

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}(e^{i(t_{1}R_{p}(a_{1})+t_{2}R_{p}(a_{2}))}-1)=\\
&-\frac{\zeta(2p)^{1/p}}{\log 2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)|t_{1}|^{1/p}\left(1-i\,\mathrm{sgn}(t_{1})\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)-i\kappa_{p}^{\prime}t_{1}\\
&-\frac{\zeta(2p)^{1/p}}{\log 2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)|t_{2}|^{1/p}\left(1-i\,\mathrm{sgn}(t_{2})\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)-i\kappa_{p}^{\prime}t_{2}+O\left(|t_{1}|^{1+\frac{1}{2p}}+|t_{2}|^{1+\frac{1}{2p}}\right)\end{split}

with the constant

\kappa_{p}^{\prime}=\zeta(2p)d_{p}+\frac{1}{\log 2}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{2p}}\sum_{n=1}^{2m^{2}}n^{p}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{n(n+2)}\right),

where
d_{p}
is as in (
23
). The analogue of Lemma
16
is the following. For any even integer
K\geq 2
, let

X_{K}=\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime}K^{p}}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\textrm{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{K}R_{p}(a_{k})+\frac{\kappa_{p}^{\prime}}{2}K\Bigg)\quad\textrm{and}\quad Y_{K}=\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime}K^{p}}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\textrm{ even}\end{subarray}}^{K}R_{p}(a_{k})+\frac{\kappa_{p}^{\prime}}{2}K\Bigg)

with the constant
\sigma^{\prime}=\zeta(2p)\left(\frac{1}{2\log 2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)^{p}
. Then
(X_{K},Y_{K})\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,1)\otimes\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,1)
with rate

in the Kolmogorov metric, and

\frac{X_{K}+Y_{K}}{2^{p}}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,1)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{X_{K}-Y_{K}}{2^{p}}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,0)

with rate
\ll K^{-p+1/2}
in the Kolmogorov metric. Note that
p-\frac{1}{2}>\frac{p}{2(p+1)}
, thus these errors are negligible. Altogether we obtain

\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
0<\langle n\alpha\rangle<1/(2n)\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n^{p}\langle n\alpha\rangle^{p}}+E_{N},\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
-1/(2n)<\langle n\alpha\rangle<0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{n^{p}|\langle n\alpha\rangle|^{p}}+E_{N}\Bigg)\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,1)\otimes\mathrm{Stab}(1/p,1)

with rate
\ll(\log N)^{-\frac{p}{2(p+1)}}(\log\log N)^{\frac{p}{2(p+1)}}
in the Kolmogorov metric, where

\sigma_{N}=\sigma^{\prime}K_{N}^{p}=\zeta(2p)\left(\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)^{p}(\log N)^{p}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{\log N}\right)\right)

Formula (

.

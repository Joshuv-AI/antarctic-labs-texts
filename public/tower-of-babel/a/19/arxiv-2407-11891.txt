Tamagawa number conjecture for CM modular forms and Rankin–Selberg convolutions Thanks: This research was partially supported by the NSF grants DMS-2101458 and DMS-2401321.
Francesc Castella

Abstract
. Let E / F E/F be an elliptic curve defined over a number field F F with complex multiplication by the ring of integers of an imaginary quadratic field K K such that the torsion points of E E generate over F F an abelian extension of K K . In this paper we prove the p p -part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer formula for E / F E/F in analytic rank 1 1 for primes p > 3 p>3 split in K K . This was previously known for F = ℚ F=\mathbb{Q} by work of Rubin [ Rub91 ] as a consequence of his proof of Mazur’s Main Conjecture for rational CM elliptic curves, but the problem for [ F : ℚ ] > 1 [F:\mathbb{Q}]>1 remained wide open. The approach introduced in this paper also yields a proof of similar results for CM abelian varieties A / K A/K and for CM modular forms, as well as an analogue in this setting of Skinner’s p p -converse to the theorem of Gross–Zagier and Kolyvagin.

1.1.
Statement of the main results

In this paper we prove the following result towards the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for elliptic curves with complex multiplication.

Theorem A
.

Let

F

K

{\rm End}_{F}(E)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{K}

\psi_{E}:F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the Hecke character associated to

{\rm ord}_{s=1}L(\psi_{E},s)=1,

be a prime split in
K

is the class number of
K

\mathfrak{f}_{\psi}
of

is prime to
p
and satisfies
\mathfrak{d}_{K}\|N_{F/K}(\mathfrak{f}_{\psi})
, where
\mathfrak{d}_{K}:=(\sqrt{-D_{K}})

{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(F)={\rm ord}_{s=1}L(E/F,s).

Moreover, for all primes
\wp\mid p
in
K

\#\text{\wncyr{W}}(E/F)[\wp^{\infty}]<\infty

{\rm ord}_{\wp}\biggl(\frac{L^{*}(E/F,1)}{{\rm Reg}(E)\cdot\Omega(E)}\biggr)={\rm ord}_{\wp}(\#\text{\wncyr{W}}(E/F)[\wp^{\infty}]\cdot{\rm Tam}(E/F)),

In other words, the

1.2.
About the proofs

The starting point in the proof of our main results is an idea introduced by Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna
[
BDP12
]
in their proof of (a generalization of) Rubin’s formula
[
Rub92
]
expressing the
p
-adic logarithm of Heegner points in terms of special values of Katz
p
-adic
L
-functions. In Rubin’s original proof of the formula, the arithmetic of CM elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
is studied using Heegner points for an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field
K^{\prime}
satisfying the Heegner hypothesis relative to the conductor of
E
. In particular, one is forced to take
K^{\prime}\neq K
, as the
L
-function

L(E/K,s)=L(E,s)^{2}

has always sign
+1
. Letting
\lambda
be the Hecke character of
K
attached to
E/\mathbb{Q}
, the ingenious idea of
[
BDP12
]
is to write

\lambda=\psi\chi

as the product of a suitable Hecke character
\psi
of infinity type
(-1,0)
and a ray class character
\chi
, so that
L(E,s)=L(\lambda,s)
appears as a factor of the Rankin–Selberg convolution

L(g/K,\chi,s)=L(\lambda,s)\cdot L(\psi^{\tau}\chi,s)

for
g=\theta_{\psi}
, where
\psi^{\tau}
denotes the composition of
\psi
with the action of the non-trivial automorphism of
K/\mathbb{Q}
, and exploit Heegner cycles for the pair
(g,\chi)
, as featured in the general Gross–Zagier formula
[
YZZ13
]
, to study the arithmetic of
\lambda
.

Towards the application of this idea to our problem, the first part of the paper is devoted to the study of the anticyclotomic Iwasawa Main Conjecture for “self-dual pairs”
(g,\chi)
of the form

(g,\chi)=(\theta_{\psi},\chi)

with
\psi
a Hecke a character of
K
of infinity type
(1-2r,0)
for any
r\geq 1
and
\chi
a finite order Hecke character with central character
\varepsilon_{\chi}=\varepsilon_{g}^{-1}
, where
\varepsilon_{g}
is the nebentypus of

Expanding on the work of Agboola–Howard
[
AH06
]
and Arnold
[
Arn07
]
, incorporating key ideas and results from Kato
[
Kat04
]
and Johnson-Leung–Kings
[

]
, we prove the Main Conjecture for

in this setting, both

•

in terms of the
p
-adic
L
-function

of Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna (Theorem
4.1.1
),

•

in terms of the

of
[

After inverting

2.2.2.
Anticyclotomic projection

We shall be particularly interested in the anticyclotomic projection of Katz’s

-adic

-functions twisted by characters that are self-dual in the following sense.

Definition 2.2.2
.

We say that a Hecke character
\phi

\phi^{\tau}=\phi^{-1}\mathbf{N}^{-1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\varepsilon_{\phi}=\eta_{K},

where

.

In particular, if
\phi
is self-dual then its
p
-adic avatar is a conjugate self-dual character of

, i.e.

\phi^{\tau}=\varepsilon_{\rm cyc}\phi^{-1}.

Note that for self-dual
\phi
, the Hecke
L
-function
L(\phi,s)
is self-dual, with a functional equation relating its values at
s
and
-s
. Moreover, the infinity type of such
\phi
is of the form
(1-r,r)
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and its conductor is invariant under complex conjugation.

Let
F/K
be an abelian extension contained in
K(\mathfrak{c}p^{\infty})
. For a character
\phi
of
{\rm Gal}(F/K)
valued in
\mathscr{O}
and having

as the prime-to-
p
part of its conductor, we denote by
\mathscr{L}_{v}(\phi)
the image of
\mathscr{L}_{v,\mathfrak{c}}
under the composite map

\Lambda_{\mathscr{O}}^{\rm ur}(K(\mathfrak{c}p^{\infty}))\xrightarrow{{\rm Tw}_{\phi}}\Lambda_{\mathscr{O}}^{\rm ur}(K(\mathfrak{c}p^{\infty}))\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda_{\mathscr{O}}^{\rm ur},

where

is the
\mathscr{O}
-linear isomorphism given by
\gamma\mapsto\phi(\gamma)\gamma
for
\gamma\in G_{\mathfrak{c}}

and the second arrow is the natural projection (noting that

contains

, and hence also

).

Remark 2.2.3
.

Although not reflected in the notation for simplicity, when we write

(resp.
\mathscr{L}_{\overline{v}}(\phi)
) it will be tacitly understood that this is obtained from
\mathscr{L}_{v,\mathfrak{c}}
(resp.
\mathscr{L}_{\overline{v},\mathfrak{c}}
) with
\mathfrak{c}
the prime-to-
p
part of the conductor of

.

2.3.
Factorization

Recall that if
\psi
is a Hecke character of infinity type
(1-2r,0)
and conductor

is an eigenform in

with

and

Proposition 2.3.1
.

Let
\psi
be a Hecke character of
K
of infinity type
(1-2r,0)
for some
r\geq 1
and conductor a cyclic ideal
\mathfrak{f}_{\psi}
of norm prime to

. Let

be a self-dual pair for some positive integer
c
prime to
pN_{g}
defined over

.
Then for every

\mathscr{L}_{w}(g,\chi)^{2}=u\cdot\mathscr{L}_{w}(\psi\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r})\cdot\mathscr{L}_{w}(\psi^{\tau}\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}),

where

is a unit in
\Lambda_{\mathscr{O}}^{{\rm ur},\times}
.

Proof.

We only explain the proof in the case
w=v
; the argument for
w=\overline{v}
is of course the same. By our assumption on
\mathfrak{f}_{\psi}
, the imaginary quadratic field
K
satisfies (
Heeg
) relative to
N_{g}
with
\mathfrak{N}_{g}=\mathfrak{d}_{K}\cdot\mathfrak{f}_{\psi}
. Let
\xi
be a character as in the statement of Theorem
2.1.3
, corresponding to a Hecke character of
K
of infinity type
(n,-n)
with
n\geq r
, and assume that
\xi
factors through
\Gamma
. Then the central
L
-value
L(\theta_{\psi}/K,\chi\xi,r)
factors as

L(\theta_{\psi}/K,\chi\xi,r)=L(\psi\chi\xi,r)\cdot L(\psi^{\tau}\chi\xi,r)=L(\psi\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi,0)\cdot L(\psi^{\tau}\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi,0).

The Hecke characters
\psi\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi
and
\psi^{\tau}\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi
have infinity type
(1-r+n,r-n)
and
(n+r,1-n-r)
, and one easily checks (see
[
BDP12
, Lem. 3.16]
) that they are both self-dual, with prime-to-

conductor
(c)\mathfrak{d}_{K}
and
(cM)\mathfrak{d}_{K}
, respectively, where
\mathfrak{d}_{K}:=(\sqrt{-D_{K}})
and

; thus they are in the range of interpolation of
\mathscr{L}_{v,(c)\mathfrak{d}_{K}}
and
\mathscr{L}_{v,(cM)\mathfrak{d}_{K}}
, respectively. From the self-duality of
\psi\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi
and
\psi^{\tau}\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi

(1-\psi^{-1}\chi^{-1}\xi^{-1}(v)p^{r-1})=(1-\psi\chi\xi(\overline{v})p^{-r}),\quad(1-\psi^{-{\tau}}\chi^{-1}\xi^{-1}(\overline{v})p^{r-1})=(1-\psi^{\tau}\chi\xi(\overline{v})p^{-r}),

and so from the interpolation formula in Theorem
2.2.1
we obtain

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{v,(c)\mathfrak{d}_{K}}(\psi\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi)

\displaystyle\cdot\mathscr{L}_{v,(c)\mathfrak{d}_{K}M}(\psi^{\tau}\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi)=\frac{\Omega_{p}^{2n-2r+1}}{\Omega^{2n-2r+1}}\cdot\frac{\Omega_{p}^{2n+2r-1}}{\Omega^{2n+2r-1}}\cdot\Gamma(n-r+1)\cdot\Gamma(n+r)

\displaystyle\times\biggl(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{D_{K}}}\biggr)^{n-r}\cdot\biggl(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{D_{K}}}\biggr)^{n+r-1}\cdot\bigl(1-\psi\chi\xi(\overline{v})p^{-r}\bigr)^{2}\cdot\bigl(1-\psi^{\tau}\chi\xi(\overline{v})p^{-r}\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\times L(\psi\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi,0)\cdot L(\psi^{\tau}\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi,0),

using that the prime-to-
p
conductors of
\psi\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi
and
\psi^{\tau}\chi\mathbf{N}^{-r}\xi
are
(c)\mathfrak{d}_{K}
and
(cM)\mathfrak{d}_{K}
, respectively, to equivalently write the primitive Hecke
L
-values. Noting that

(1-\psi\chi\xi(\bar{v})p^{-r}\bigr)\cdot\bigl(1-\psi^{\tau}\chi\xi(\bar{v})p^{-r}\bigr)=(1-a_{p}(g)\chi\xi(\overline{v})p^{-r}+\varepsilon_{g}(p)\chi\xi(\overline{v})^{2}p^{-1}),

comparing (
2.1
) with the interpolation property of Theorem
2.1.3
, the result follows.

4.2.

For an ideal

prime to

As a piece of notation, for an infinite abelian extension

Here we give a refinement of Yager’s work

]
(restricted to the anticyclotomic line) building on Kato’s explicit reciprocity law

]

Theorem 4.2.1
.

Let

above

6.1.
Periods and heights of Heegner points

Fix a prime

with

.

Definition 6.1.1
.

The
canonical period
of

is

\Omega_{g}^{\rm can}:=\frac{8\pi^{2}\langle g,g\rangle_{\Gamma}}{\eta_{g}},

where
\langle g,g\rangle_{\Gamma}=\int_{\Gamma_{1}(N_{g})\backslash\mathfrak{H}}g(z)\overline{g(z)}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}
is the Petersson norm of
g
, and
\eta_{g}
is the congruence number of
g
relative to
S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N_{g}))
(see
[
Hid81
, §7]
).

We are interested in the case where
g=\theta_{\psi}
is the theta series of a Hecke character
\psi
of an imaginary quadratic field
K
satisfying (
spl
). Suppose
L
is a number field containing the Fourier coefficients of
g
, and let
\Phi
be the completion of
L
at a fixed prime above
p
, with ring of integers
\mathscr{O}
.

From now on, we also suppose
p>3
.

Proposition 6.1.2
.

Let
\psi
be a Hecke character of
K
of infinity type
(-1,0)
, and conductor prime to
p
, and put
g=\theta_{\psi}
. Suppose
\psi^{-}:=\psi^{\tau}/\psi
satisfies conditions (S5)–(S7) of Definition
5.1.1
and has conductor divisible only by primes that are split in
K
. Then

\Omega_{g}^{\rm can}=\Omega^{2}

up to a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{g}
be the Hida family passing through the ordinary
p
-stabilization

g_{\alpha}:=g(q)-\varepsilon_{g}(p)p\psi(\overline{v})^{-1}g(q^{p}),

and let
\eta_{\bm{g}}
be its associated congruence power series, normalized so that its specialization at
the trivial character gives the congruence number
\eta_{g_{\alpha}}
of
g_{\alpha}
.
Under our hypotheses on
\psi^{-}
, it follows from the proof of the anticyclotomic Iwasawa main conjecture for CM fields by Hida–Tilouine
[
HT93
,
HT94
]
and Hida
[
Hid06
]
that

(6.1)

\eta_{\bm{g}}=h_{K}\cdot\mathscr{L}_{v}(\psi^{-}\mathbf{N}^{-1})

up to a
p
-adic unit, where
h_{K}=\#{\rm Pic}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
is the class number of
K
. (Note that in (
6.1
) we have used the functional equation of Theorem
2.2.1
to write
\mathscr{L}_{v}(\psi^{-}\mathbf{N}^{-1})
in place of
\mathscr{L}_{v}(\psi^{-})

Having infinity type

\psi^{-}\mathbf{N}^{-1}
is in the range of interpolation of the Katz

-adic

, and from there we obtain

(6.2)

h_{K}\cdot\frac{\mathscr{L}_{v}(\psi^{-}\mathbf{N}^{-1})(\mathds{1})}{\Omega_{p}^{2}}=\mathcal{E}_{p}(\psi^{-})\cdot h_{K}\cdot\frac{L(\psi^{-},1)}{\Omega^{2}},

where

. By Hida’s formula (see
[
HT93
, Thm. 7.1]
) and Dirichlet’s class number formula, we have

\langle g,g\rangle_{\Gamma}=\frac{D_{K}^{2}}{2^{3}\pi^{2}}\cdot\frac{h_{K}}{w_{K}\sqrt{D_{K}}}\cdot L(\psi^{-},1),

where

, and so together with (
6.1
), equality (
6.2
) can be rewritten as the equality

(6.3)

\frac{\eta_{g_{\alpha}}}{\Omega_{p}^{2}}\cdot u=\mathcal{E}_{p}(\psi^{-})\cdot\frac{8\pi^{2}\langle g,g\rangle_{\Gamma}}{\Omega^{2}}

for some unit

. Since the right-hand side of (

) is in
L^{\times}
as a consequence of the proof of Deligne’s conjecture for Hecke characters (see
[
GS81
]
and
[
Bla86
]
), it follows that

, and hence (

) together with the well-known relation

(see e.g.
[
Wil95
, Rem. 2.18]
) yields the result.
∎

Using Proposition
6.1.2
, we can now rewrite the general Gross–Zagier formula for modular curves in a form that will be convenient for us.

Let

K

Theorem 6.1.3

Let
\psi

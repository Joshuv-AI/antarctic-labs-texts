Cohomological support loci and Pluricanonical systems on irregular varieties
Zhi Jiang

Abstract
. For an irregular variety X X of general type, we show that if a general fiber F F of the Albanese morphism of X X satisfies certain Hodge theoretic condition, the 0 0 -th cohomological support loci of K X K_{X} generates Pic 0 ⁡ ( X ) \Pic^{0}(X) . We then show that the condition that the 0 0 -th cohomological support loci of K X K_{X} generates Pic 0 ⁡ ( X ) \Pic^{0}(X) can often be applied to prove the birationality of certain pluricanonical maps of X X .

1.
Introduction

In this article, we study the pluricanonical systems of irregular varieties.
This topic was initiated in a series of article of Jungkai Chen and Hacon
[
CH1
,
CH2
,
CH3
]
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of general type with
q(X)=h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})>0
.
Fix the Albanese morphism
a_{X}:X\rightarrow A_{X}
from
X
to its Albanese variety
A_{X}
. We denote by
F
a connected component of a general fiber of
a_{X}
. We say that
X
is of Albanese fiber dimension
m
if
\dim F=m
. The properties of the pluricanonical maps of
X
are closely related to the properties of the pluricanonical systems of
F
. When
F
or
X
is of low dimensions, the general picture is well-understood.

Theorem 1.1
.

[J.A.Chen-Hacon
[
CH1
]
; Jiang-Lahoz-Tirabassi
[
JLT
]
]
Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of general type, of maximal Albanese dimension. Then the linear system
|mK_{X}+P|
induces a birational map of
X
for any integer
m\geq 3
and
P\in\Pic^{0}(X)
.

Note that this is an optimal result, which can be easily deduced from the properties of pluricanonical systems on curves of genus
2
. A similar result was proved by the author and Hao Sun when
F
is a curve.

Theorem 1.2
.

[Jiang-Sun
[
JS
]
]
Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of general type, of Albanese fiber dimension
1
. Then the linear system
|mK_{X}+P|
induces a birational map of
X
for any integer
m\geq 4
and
P\in\Pic^{0}(X)
.

It is worth noting that the structure of

V^{0}(K_{X}):=\{P\in\Pic^{0}(X)\mid H^{0}(X,K_{X}\otimes P)\neq 0\}

plays an important role in the proof of both above theorems. We know from generic vanishing theory (see for instance
[
GL
]
and
[
Sim
]
) that

V^{j}(K_{X}):=\{P\in\Pic^{0}(X)\mid H^{j}(X,K_{X}\otimes P)\neq 0\},

for
j\geq 0
, is a union of torsion translates of abelian subvarieties of
\Pic^{0}(X)
. We say that

V^{j}(K_{X})
generates
\Pic^{0}(X)
if the irreducible components of
V^{j}(K_{X})
generate
\Pic^{0}(X)
as a group. One of the main points in the proof of Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
is to show that
V^{0}(K_{X})
always generates
\Pic^{0}(X)
in both cases.

The first main result of this paper is a general criteria for the property that
V^{0}(K_{X})
generates
\Pic^{0}(X)
.

Recall that we say a smooth projective variety
V
satisfies the infinitesimal Torelli condition if the natural cup product

\displaystyle H^{1}(V,T_{V})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(H^{0}(V,K_{V}),H^{1}(V,\Omega_{V}^{\dim V-1}))

is injective. The infinitesimal Torelli condition is of course closely related with the variation of Hodge structures of
V
.

Given a smooth projective variety of general type
V
. We know that the birational transformation group
\mathrm{Bir}(V)
is a finite group and acts naturally on
H^{0}(V,K_{V})
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
X
is of general type and
F
satisfies the following conditions:

(C1)

the canonical model of
F
is a smooth projective variety
V
which satisfies the infinitesimal Torelli condition;

(C2)

\mathrm{Bir}(F)
acts faithfully on
H^{0}(F,K_{F})
,

then
V^{0}(K_{X})
generates
\Pic^{0}(X)
.

This is a further extension of the corresponding results in
[
CH3
]
and
[
JS
]
. Indeed, it is well known that a non-hyperelliptic curve of genus
\geq 3
satisfies both conditions in the assumption of Theorem
1.3
. One of the main ingredient of the proof of Theorem
1.3
is a decomposition theorem of Hodge modules on abelian varieties proved in
[
PPS
]
. Actually our proof works also for higher cohomology support loci
V^{0}(R^{j}f_{*}K_{X})
(see Theorem
2.1
).

We also explore the relation between the structure of
V^{0}(K_{X})
and the properties of the pluricanonical systems of
X
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
V^{0}(K_{X})
generates
\Pic^{0}(X)
and
|K_{F}|
induces a birational map of
X
. Then the linear system
|3K_{X}+P|
induces a birational map of
X
for
P\in\Pic^{0}(X)
general.

The conditions in Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
apply to varieties whose Albanese fibers
F
has sufficiently positive canonical bundles.
It is well known that surfaces of general type does not satisfy the infinitesimal Torelli condition in general. Besides surfaces with
p_{g}=0
, there are surfaces with
p_{g}>0
, which does not satisfy the infinitesimal Torelli condition (see for instance
[

]
). Hence Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
do not apply to varieties of Albanese fiber dimension
2
. It is then surprising to the author that by taking hyperplane sections, the structure of cohomological support loci of varieties of Albanese fiber dimension
1
has implications on the pluricanonical systems of varieties of Albanese fiber dimension
2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
X
is a smooth projective variety of general type and a connected component of a general fiber of the Albanese morphism of
X
is a surface
F
with

and

. Then
|5K_{X}+P|
induces a birational map of

for all

Remark 1.6
.

In
[
CCCJ
]
, the authors shows that

always induces a birational map for any irregular

and this kind of result is out of reach in higher dimensions.

2.
The structure of
V^{0}(K_{X})

2.1.
Hodge Modules

The proof of the main result relies heavily on the language of Hodge modules. We recall some results about Hodge Modules on abelian varieties. All results can be found in
[
PPS
]
, where Pareschi, Popa, and Schnell applied the mechinary of Hodge modules to prove the general Chen-Jiang decomposition for higher direct image of canonical bundles on abelian varieties generalizing the main result in
[
CJ
]
. Indeed, Pareschi, Popa, and Schnell proved the decomposition of certain Hodge modules which is crucial for our purpose.

Let
X
be a complex manifold. We denote
\mathbf{HM}_{\mathbb{R}}(X,w)
be the category of real Hodge module on
X
of weight
w
, which is a semi-simple
\mathbb{R}
-linear abelian category,endowed with a faithful functor to the category of real perverse sheaves.
An object
M
of
\mathbf{HM}_{\mathbb{R}}(X,w)
consists of a regular holonomic left
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, a good filtration
F_{\cdot}M
by coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules, a perverse sheaf
M_{\mathbb{R}}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{R}
and an isomorphism
M_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathrm{DR}(\mathcal{M})
. The support of
M
is defined to be the support of the perverse sheaf
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. One can also define polarizable Hodge modules. One can check
[
PPS
]
or
[
Sai
]
for more details about this category.

(A)

Every object
M\in\mathbf{HM}_{\mathbb{R}}(X,w)
admits a locally finite decomposition by strict support:

M\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}M_{i},

where each
M_{i}\in\mathbf{HM}_{\mathbb{R}}(X,w)
has strict support equal to an irreducible analytic subvariety
Z_{i}\subset X
.

(B)

The category of polarizable real Hodge modules of weight
w
with strict support
Z\subset X
is equivalent to the category of generically defined polarizable real variations of Hodge structure of weight
w-\dim Z
on
Z
.

(C)

Denote by
\mathbb{R}_{X}[\dim X]\in\mathbf{HM}_{\mathbb{R}}(X,\dim X)
the polarizable real Hodge module corresponding to the constant real variation of Hodge structure on
X
. For any morphism
f:X\rightarrow A
from
X
to an abelian variety and an integer
j\geq 0
,
\mathcal{H}^{f}_{*}\mathbb{R}_{X}[\dim X]
is a polarizable real Hodge module of weight
j+\dim X
on
A
. Let
M=(\mathcal{M},F_{\cdot}\mathcal{M},M_{\mathbb{R}})
be the direct summand of
\mathcal{H}^{j}f_{*}\mathbb{R}_{X}[\dim X]
with strict support
f(X)
. Then the first non-trivial grading piece of
\mathcal{M}
is
R^{j}f_{*}\omega_{X}
.

(D)

Let
M
be as in
(C)
above. Then we associate a complex polarizable Hodge module
(M\oplus M,J_{M})
as in
[
PPS
]
, whose underlying perverse sheaf is simply
M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
. Then by
[
PPS
, Theorem 7.1 and Corollary 7.3]
, we know that

(M\oplus M,J_{M})\simeq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}q_{j}^{-1}(N_{j},J_{j})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}_{\rho_{j}},

where
q_{j}:A\rightarrow A_{j}
is a surjective morphism with connected fibers between abelian varieties,
\rho_{j}\in\mathrm{Char}(A)
is a unitary character, and
(N_{j},J_{j})\in\mathbf{HM}_{\mathbb{C}}(T_{j},\dim X-\dim q_{j})
is a simple polarizable complex Hodge module with
\chi(T_{j},N_{j},J_{j})>0
.

(E)

Under the assumption of
(D)
, then by
[
PPS
, Theorem D]
, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathrm{gr}_{k}^{F}\mathcal{M}\simeq\oplus_{j=1}^{n}q_{j}^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\otimes L_{j},

where
\mathcal{F}_{j}
is an M-regular sheaf on
T_{j}
and
L_{j}
is a torsion line bundle on
T
.

The proof of Theorem

We work in a more general setting.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that

is a smooth projective variety of general type. Let

be a morphism from

(C1’)

\mathrm{Bir}(F)

We argue by contradiction. Assume that the translates through the origin of the irreducible components of

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Let

R^{j}f_{*}\omega_{X}|_{K}\simeq R^{j}f_{b*}\omega_{Y}

We then consider the natural restriction morphism
\pi:\Pic^{0}(A)\rightarrow\Pic^{0}(K)

It is clear that

\pi(V^{0}(R^{j}f_{*}\omega_{X}))\subset V^{0}(R^{j}f_{b*}\omega_{Y}),

since
H^{0}(A,R^{j}f_{*}\omega_{X}\otimes P)\neq 0

H^{0}(K,R^{j}f_{b*}\otimes P|_{K})\neq 0
for

. We claim that

\pi(V^{0}(f_{*}\omega_{X}))=V^{0}(f_{b*}\omega_{Y})

. We choose

such that

q_{*}(R^{j}f_{*}\omega_{X}\otimes P)

is non-trivial, since its fiber at

is exactly
H^{0}(K,R^{j}f_{b*}\omega_{Y}\otimes Q)\neq 0

[

By the construction of

Repeating the above argument, we can assume furthermore that

is a simple abelian variety.
We now work with

Let

is surjective.

Let
M

\mathcal{H}^{j}f_{b*}\mathbb{R}[m]
with strict support

\mathcal{M}
of

By Subsection
2.1
(D), we know that

\displaystyle(M\oplus M,J_{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\bigoplus\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\bigoplus_{j}q^{-1}V_{j}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\rho_{j}}\big)\bigoplus\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime},

where

is the constant morphism,

are Hodge structures of weights
\dim F+j
,
\rho_{j}\in\mathrm{Char}(K)
are unitary characters, and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
is a direct sum of simple complex polarizable Hodge modules with positive holomorphic Euler characteristic.
The fact that

implies that
R^{j}f_{b*}\omega_{Y}
is indeed the first non-trivia grading piece of the underlying D-module of

is positive, any Hodge grading piece of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
has positive holomorphic Euler characteristic (see
[

).

By Subsection

(B),
\mathcal{H}^{j}f_{b*}\mathbb{R}[m]
corresponds to the variation of Hodge structure of

, where

belongs to the smooth locus of

and

is the fiber of

over

. Then
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a polarizable variation of complex Hodge structure, whose fiber over a general point

is a sub-Hodge structure of

containing
H^{j}(Y_{t},K_{Y_{t}})
. Note that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is trivial up to an étale cover of

.

Since
F
is a connected component of
Y_{t}
, the composition of the Kodaira-Spencer map with the cup product of variation of Hodge structures:

T_{K,t}\rightarrow H^{1}(F,T_{F})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(H^{j}(F,K_{F}),H^{j+1}(F,\Omega_{F}^{\dim F-1}))

is zero.

We now consider the relative canonical model of

:

{\widetilde{f}}_{b}:{\widetilde{Y}}\rightarrow K.

By the assumption, we know that a connected component of the fiber
{\widetilde{f}}_{b}
over

is the the canonical model

of

. By assumption,
V
is a smooth projective variety which satisfies the infinitesimal Torelli condition (C1’). Note that
H^{j}(F,K_{F})=H^{j}(V,K_{V})
.
and

is a direct summand, we have the commutative diagram:

\textstyle{T_{K,t}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{1}(F,T_{F})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Thus the composition of maps

satisfies the infinitesimal Torelli condition, the Kodaira-Spencer map

In the following, we shall apply the condition (C2’) to show that for a birational isotrivial morphism
f_{b}:Y\rightarrow K
,
\dim V^{0}(R^{j}f_{b*}\omega_{Y})=0
is absurd.

We assume without loss of generalities that
f_{b}:Y\rightarrow K
is primitive, i.e.
f_{b}
does not factor through étale covers of

. We consider the Stein factorization

f_{b}:Y\xrightarrow{h_{1}}N\xrightarrow{h_{2}}K

of

, where after birational modifications we may assume that

is smooth,
h_{1}
is a fibration and
h_{2}
is generically finite and surjective.
Since
K
is a simple abelian variety and
f_{b}
is primitive, either

is birational or
\deg h_{2}\geq 2
and

is of general type.

Note that
h_{1}
is a birationally isotrivial fibration. Since
F
is of general type, its birational automorphism group is finite, we then take a Galois cover
\rho:M\rightarrow N
such that the flat base change
M\times_{N}Y
is birational to

. If
\rho
factors through an abelian étale cover of

induced by an isogeny
\widetilde{K}\rightarrow K
, we replace
f_{b}:Y\rightarrow K
by
\widetilde{f_{b}}:\widetilde{Y}:=Y\times_{K}\widetilde{K}\rightarrow\widetilde{K}
. Note that
V^{0}(\widetilde{f_{b}}_{*}\omega_{\widetilde{Y}})
still consists of finitely many points. Therefore, without loss of generalities, we assume that
\rho:M\rightarrow N
does not factor through abelian étale covers of
N
induced by isogenies of
K
.

Let
G
be the Galois group of the cover
\rho
. Taking an

-equivariant resolution of singularities, we may assume that
M
is smooth. We then consider the canonical decomposition

\rho_{*}\omega_{M}=\omega_{N}\bigoplus_{1_{G}\neq\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\mathcal{V}_{\chi}

with respect to the

\mathrm{Irr}(G)
denotes the set of irreducible representations of

We claim that
h_{2*}\mathcal{V}_{\chi}
is M-regular on
K
for any

. If not, by the Chen-Jiang decomposition theorem (see
[
PPS
]
),
h_{2*}\mathcal{V}_{\chi}
has a numerically trivial line bundle

as a direct summand. Since
h^{\dim M}(M,h_{2*}\rho_{*}\omega_{M})=h^{\dim M}(K,h_{2*}\omega_{N})=1
,

is a non-trivial torsion line bundle on

. Since
f_{b}
is primitive,
h_{2}^{*}P
is also a non-trivial torsion line bundle on

. But
h^{\dim M}(M,\omega_{M}\otimes\rho^{*}h_{2}^{*}P^{-1})\neq 0
, thus
\rho^{*}h_{2}^{*}P=\mathcal{O}_{M}
, which implies that
\rho
factors through an abelian étale cover of

induced by an isogeny of

, which is a contradiction.

We are now ready to deduce a contradiction. We observe that
Y
is birational to the diagonal quotient
(M\times F)/G
, where

acts on

via an injective homomorphism

.

Since

, we then consider the representation decomposition

H^{j}(F,K_{F})=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}V_{\chi}.

By the condition (C2),

acts faithfully on

.
Hence for some
\chi_{0}\neq 1_{G}
,

is non-zero.

From the commutative diagram:

\textstyle{M\times F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(M\times F)/G\sim Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{f_{b}}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

we see that

\displaystyle R^{j}f_{b*}\omega_{Y}=h_{2*}R^{j}h_{1*}\omega_{Y}=h_{2*}R^{j}h_{1*}\omega_{(M\times F)/G}

\displaystyle=h_{2*}(\rho_{*}\omega_{M}\otimes H^{j}(F,K_{F}))^{G}=\bigoplus_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}(\mathcal{V}_{\chi}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V_{\chi^{\prime}})^{G}.

It is known from the character theory of representation of finite groups that for

Thus

\dim V^{0}(R^{j}f_{b*}\omega_{Y})=0
.

Remark 2.2

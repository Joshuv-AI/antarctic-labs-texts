Steady state solutions for the Gierer-Meinhardt system in the whole space
Marius Ghergu

Abstract
We are concerned with the study of positive solutions to the Gierer-Meinhardt system { − Δ ​ u + λ ​ u = u p v q + ρ ⁡ ( x ) in ​ ℝ N , N ≥ 3 , − Δ ​ v + μ ​ v = u m v s in ​ ℝ N , \begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u+\lambda u=\frac{u^{p}}{v^{q}}+\rho(x)&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\,,N\geq 3,\\[7.22743pt] \displaystyle-\Delta v+\mu v=\frac{u^{m}}{v^{s}}&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[7.22743pt] \end{cases} which satisfy u ⁡ ( x ) , v ⁡ ( x ) → 0 u(x),v(x)\to 0 as | x | → ∞ |x|\to\infty . In the above system p , q , m , s > 0 p,q,m,s>0 , λ , μ ≥ 0 \lambda,\mu\geq 0 and ρ ∈ C ⁡ ( ℝ N ) \rho\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N}) , ρ ≥ 0 \rho\geq 0 . It is a known fact that posed in a smooth and bounded domain of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , the above system subject to homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions has positive solutions if p > 1 p>1 and σ = m ​ q ( p − 1 ) ​ ( s + 1 ) > 1 \sigma=\frac{mq}{(p-1)(s+1)}>1 . In the present work we emphasize a different phenomenon: we see that for λ , μ > 0 \lambda,\mu>0 large, positive solutions with exponential decay exist if 0 < σ ≤ 1 0<\sigma\leq 1 . Further, for λ = μ = 0 \lambda=\mu=0 we derive various existence and nonexistence results and underline the role of the critical exponents p = N N − 2 p=\frac{N}{N-2} and p = N + 2 N − 2 p=\frac{N+2}{N-2} .



Keywords: Gierer-Meinhardt system; steady state solutions; existence and nonexistence

1
Introduction and the main results

In 1972, Gierer and Meinhardt
[
15
]
derived a mathematical model to account for
morphogenesis
in hydra, a tissues regeneration process through controlling the spatial distribution of cells. The morphogenesis was first observed by Trembley
[
29
]
in 1744 and is directed by two types of factors - chemical molecules and mechanical forces. Based on the findings of Turing
[
30
]
in 1952, the Gierer and Meinhardt model assumes the existence of two chemical substances (morphogens): a slowly diffusing
activator
and a fast diffusing
inhibitor
.
Their original model in
[
15
]
reads as

(GM)\quad\begin{cases}\displaystyle u_{t}=d\Delta u-\lambda u+\frac{u^{p}}{v^{q}}+\rho(x)&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle v_{t}=D\Delta v-\mu v+\frac{u^{m}}{v^{s}}&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\[7.22743pt]
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),v(x,0)=v_{0}(x)&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\[7.22743pt]
\end{cases}

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a smooth and bounded domain,
\nu
denotes the outer unit normal vector at
\partial\Omega
,
\lambda,\mu\geq 0
and
\rho\in C(\overline{\Omega})
is a nonnegative function. The nonnegative solution
(u,v)
of the above system defines the concentrations of the activator and inhibitor respectively while
\rho
is related to the basic production rate of the activator.
Finally, the exponents
p,q,m,s
are assumed to satisfy

p>1\,,\quad q>0\,,\quad m>0\,\quad\mbox{ and }\quad s>-1.

The inhibitor diffuses much faster compared to the activator, which means that the diffusion coefficients
d
and
D
satisfy
D\gg d>0
.

As explained in
[
18
]
, the dynamics of the Gierer-Meinhardt system is characterised by two numbers:

•

the net self-activation index
(p-1)/m
which is responsible for the strength of self-activation of the activator;

•

the net cross-inhibition index
q/(s+1)
which measures how strongly the inhibitor suppresses the production of the activator and that of itself.

From biological interpretations of the above exponents, it is assumed that

0<\frac{p-1}{m}<\frac{q}{s+1},

that is,

\sigma:=\frac{mq}{(p-1)(s+1)}>1.

Under the assumption
\sigma>1
it was obtained in
[
16
]
the existence of global in time solutions to
(GM)
; see also
[
6
,
8
,
17
,
20
,
23
,
26
]
. As already emphasised in
[
26
]
, various existence and nonexistence results on the system
(GM)
can be derived according to the case where the basic production rate
\rho
of the activator is identically zero or not.

One particular perspective in understanding the structure of
(GM)
is given by the study of the
shadow system
, an approach introduced by Keener
[
19
]
. More precisely, letting
D\to\infty
in
(GM)
we deduce
v(x,t)=w(t)
,
t\in(0,T)
and so we are lead to consider:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle u_{t}=d\Delta u-\lambda u+\frac{u^{p}}{w^{q}}+\rho(x)&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle w_{t}=-\mu w+\frac{u^{m}}{w^{s}}&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\[7.22743pt]
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),w(0)=w_{0}&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega.\\[7.22743pt]
\end{cases}

Various results on the shadow system of
(GM)
are obtained in
[
10
,
18
,
24
,
25
,
32
]
.

In the present work we investigate the steady state solutions of the Gierer-Meinhardt system
(GM)
in the whole space. Precisely, we consider the system

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u+\lambda u=\frac{u^{p}}{v^{q}}+\rho(x)&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\,,N\geq 3,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle-\Delta v+\mu v=\frac{u^{m}}{v^{s}}&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[7.22743pt]
u(x),v(x)\to 0\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

where
\rho\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\rho\geq 0
,
p,q,m,s>0
and
\lambda,\mu\geq 0
. We are looking for positive classical solutions of (
1.1
), that is, functions
u,v\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
u,v>0
and satisfy (
1.1
) at every point of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
In a smooth and bounded domain, the above system was discussed in
[
11
,
13
,
17
,
21
,
28
,
31
]
under various boundary conditions. In
[
22
]
the following related system in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
was investigated:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\varepsilon\Delta u+u=\frac{u^{2}}{v}&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle-\Delta v+v=u^{2}&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\\[7.22743pt]
u(x),v(x)\to 0\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.\end{cases}

It is obtained in
[
22
]
that for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, the system (
1.2
) admits a positive radial solution
(u,v)
such that
u
and
v
decay exponentially at infinity. Similar results in dimension
N=2
were previously obtained in
[
9
]
and
[
27
]
.

Let us note that if
(u,v)
is a positive solution to (
1.2
), then
(U,V)=(\varepsilon^{-1}u,\varepsilon^{-2}v)
is a solution to our system (
1.1
) for
\lambda=\varepsilon^{-1}
,
\mu=\varepsilon^{-2}
,
\rho\equiv 0
and
p=2
,
q=1
,
m=2
,
s=0
. In this case
\sigma=1
.

Turning to our system (
1.1
), we discuss the existence and nonexistence of solutions when
\rho\geq 0
. We shall see that only the case
p>1
is relevant to our study as for
0<p\leq 1
no positive solutions to (
1.1
) exist (see Theorem
1.1
below). Furthermore, the existence of solutions which decay exponentially at infinity is closely related to the conditions
p>1
and
\sigma\leq 1
as for (
1.2
).

Throughout this paper, for two positive functions
f,g\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we use the symbol
f(x)\simeq g(x)
to denote that the quotient
f/g
is bounded between two positive constants in a neighbourhood of infinity. Also, we say that
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
decays exponentially at infinity if
f(x)\simeq e^{-a|x|}
for some
a>0
.

Our first result on (
1.1
) concerns the case
\lambda,\mu>0
and reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\lambda,\mu>0
.

(i)

If
0<p\leq 1
then (
1.1
) has no positive solutions.

(ii)

If
p>1
,
\sigma>1
and
\mu>\big(\frac{m}{s+1}\big)^{2}\lambda
, then the system (
1.1
) has no positive solutions
(u,v)
with
u
decaying exponentially at infinity.

(iii)

Assume
p>1\geq\sigma
,
\rho>0
and
\rho(x)\simeq e^{-a|x|}
for some
a>0
. Then, there exist two positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
depending on
p,q,m,s
and
\rho
such that the system (
1.1
) has positive solutions
(u,v)
decaying exponentially at infinity provided that

(1.3)

C_{1}\leq\mu\leq C_{2}\lambda^{\frac{p(s+1)}{q}-m}.

The constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are described explicitly in the proof of Theorem
1.1
which we present in Section
3
.
Observe that since
\sigma\leq 1
one has

\frac{p(s+1)}{q}-m=\frac{s+1}{q}+m\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma}-1\Big)>0,

so the exponent of
\lambda
in (
1.3
) is positive. In particular, (
1.3
) states that solutions of (
1.1
) with exponential decay exist if
\lambda,\mu
are large enough.

In the following we take
\lambda=\mu=0
in (
1.1
) and derive a different set of result. We shall thus investigate the system

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u=\frac{u^{p}}{v^{q}}+\rho(x)&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle-\Delta v=\frac{u^{m}}{v^{s}}&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[7.22743pt]
u(x),v(x)\to 0\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

We first state the following nonexistence result.

Theorem 1.2
.

The systems (
1.4
) has no positive solutions in any of the following cases:

(i)

0<p\leq\frac{N}{N-2}
or
0<m\leq 2/(N-2)
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho(x)|x|^{2-N}dx=\infty
.

(iii)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2-N}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\rho(y)}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy\Big)^{m}dx=\infty
.

(iv)

\frac{N}{N-2}<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
,
\rho\equiv 0
and there exists
C>0
such that

(1.5)

|\nabla v(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|}v(x)\quad\mbox{ for all $|x|>1$ large.}

Part (iv) in Theorem
1.2
above is a consequence of Theorem 4.1 in Gidas and Spruck
[
14
]
. The result below provides sufficient conditions for which (
1.5
) holds.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assume
\frac{N}{N-2}<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
and
\rho\equiv 0
. Then, the system (
1.4
) has no positive solutions
(u,v)
which satisfy one of the conditions below:

(i)

There exists
0<a<\frac{(N-2)s+N}{m}
such that
u(x)\simeq|x|^{-a}
.

(ii)

u
and
v
are radial.

We next turn to existence results for the system (
1.4
). Using a dynamical system approach, it is obtained in
[
3
]
the existence of positive radial solutions to (
1.4
) if
\rho\equiv 0
. In the following we shall discuss the case where
\rho>0
satisfies

(1.6)

\alpha(1+|x|^{2})^{-a/2}\leq\rho(x)\leq\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-a/2}\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

for some
\beta>\alpha>0
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume
\rho
satisfies (
1.6
).

(i)

If
0\leq a\leq 2\big(1+\frac{1}{m}\big)
then (
1.4
) has no solutions.

(ii)

If
2\big(1+\frac{1}{m}\big)<a<N
,
0<\sigma<1
and the following conditions hold

(1.7)

m(a-2)<(N-2)s+N,

(1.8)

\frac{2p}{p-1}\leq a+\sigma\Big\{2\Big(1+\frac{1}{m}\Big)-a\Big\},

then, there exist
\varepsilon,\delta>0
depending on
p,q,m,s
and
a
such that for

(1.9)

0<\alpha<\varepsilon\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\alpha<\beta<\delta\alpha^{\sigma},

the system (
1.4
) has positive solutions.

Since
a<N
, it is easy to check that condition (
1.7
) holds if either
m\leq\frac{N}{N-2}
or
m\leq s+1
. We also have:

Corollary 1.5
.

Assume
m\leq\min\{p-1,s+1\}
,
0<\sigma<1
and
\rho
satisfies (
1.6
) and (
1.9
) where
2\big(1+\frac{1}{m}\big)<a<N
. Then, the system (
1.4
) has positive solutions.

In relation to Theorem
1.4
(ii), we note that since
2\big(1+\frac{1}{m}\big)<a<N
, condition (
1.8
) yields

\frac{2p}{p-1}\leq a<N\Longrightarrow p>\frac{N}{N-2}.

In fact, the exponent
\frac{N}{N-2}
is optimal in the following sense:

Corollary 1.6
.

(i)

If
0<p\leq\frac{N}{N-2}
then the system (
1.4
) has no positive solutions for all
q,m,s>0
and any function
\rho\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\rho\geq 0
.

(ii)

Conversely, for all
p>\frac{N}{N-2}
there exist
q,m,s>0
and a continuous function
\rho\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\rho\geq 0
, such that (
1.4
) has a positive solution.

Similarly, the result below states the optimality of the exponent
\frac{N+2}{N-2}
when it comes to the study of radial solutions to (
1.4
).

Corollary 1.7
.

Assume
\rho\equiv 0
.

(i)

If
0<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
then the system (
1.4
) has no positive radial solutions for all
q,m,s>0
;

(ii)

Conversely, for all
p>\frac{N+2}{N-2}
there exist
q,m,s>0
such that (
1.4
) has a positive radial solution.

The main tool in deriving the nonexistence of positive solutions to (
1.1
) and (
1.4
) is the use of various integral representations of solutions to semilinear elliptic equations in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which were recently obtained in
[
7
]
; we recall the relevant findings of
[
7
]
in Section 2.1 below. The existence of positive solutions to (
1.1
) and (
1.4
) combines the Schauder fixed point theorem with the study of the single equation

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\mu v=\psi(x)v^{-s}\quad\mbox{ in }\;\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
v(x)\to 0\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

where
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
has a specific decay rate at infinity and
\mu\geq 0
.

The remaining part of the manuscript is organised as follows. In Section 2 we derive some preliminary results related to integral representations for semilinear elliptic equations in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Sections 3 is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Section 4 we prove Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
. Finally, the proof of Theorem
1.4
and Corollaries
1.5
,
1.6
and
1.7
are given in Section 5.

2
Preliminary results

2.1
Fundamental solutions and integral representations

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm Re\,}w\leq 0,\,{\rm Im\,}w=0\}
. The modified Bessel functions
I_{s}(z)
and
K_{s}(z)
of the first and second kind respectively are defined as

(2.1)

I_{s}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Big(\displaystyle\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{2k+s}}{k!\Gamma(k+s+1)}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad K_{s}(z)=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{I_{-s}(z)-I_{s}(z)}{\sin(\pi s)},

where
\Gamma(z)
is the standard Gamma function.
For an integer
n
, the above definition of
K_{n}(z)
is understood in the sense
K_{n}(z)=\lim_{s\to n}K_{s}(z)
.
Given
\lambda>0
, the function

(2.2)

\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\lambda^{N-2}}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{N}}}\cdot\frac{K_{\frac{N}{2}-1}(\lambda^{1/2}|x|)}{|x|^{\frac{N}{2}-1}}\,,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\,,

is the fundamental solution of the operator
-\Delta+\lambda I
(see
[
4
, Section 3]
) in the sense that

(2.3)

(-\Delta+\lambda I)\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}=\delta_{0}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

The asymptotic behaviour of
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
is summarised below.

Lemma 2.1
.

(see
[
2
, Chapter 4, pag. 416]
,
[
7
, Section 2]
)

Let
\lambda>0
. The Bessel potential
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
given by (
2.2
) has the following properties:

(i)

There exists
c_{1}>1
such that

\frac{1}{c_{1}}|x|^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}\leq\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x)\leq c_{1}|x|^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}\quad\mbox{ for all }|x|>1.

(ii)

There exists
c_{2}>1
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{2}}|x|^{2-N}\leq\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x)\leq c_{2}|x|^{2-N}\quad\mbox{ for all }0<|x|<1.

(iii)

\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x)
is a decreasing function of
|x|
and
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(iv)

There exists
C>0
such that

(2.4)

\Big|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x)\Big|\leq C|x|^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}\quad\mbox{ for all }|x|>1.

The following result, recently obtained in
[
7
]
, establishes the analogy between the distributional solutions of

(2.5)

-\Delta u+\lambda u=f(x)\quad\mbox{ in }\;\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad N\geq 3,

and their integral representation with kernel
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(see
[
7
, Theorem 1.7]
)

Let
\lambda>0
and
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
If
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a distributional solution of (
2.5
) which satisfies

(2.6)

\liminf_{R\to\infty}R^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}\int_{R\leq|x-y|\leq 2R}|u(y)|e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x-y|}dy=0\quad\mbox{ for a.a. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

then

(2.7)

u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x-y)f(y)dy\quad\mbox{ for a.a. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
is the fundamental solution of
-\Delta+\lambda I
as given in (
2.2
).

Conversely, if

(2.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x-y)f(y)dy<\infty\quad\mbox{ for a.a. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

then the function
u
given by (
2.7
) is a distributional solution of (
2.5
).

Observe that if

\lim_{R\to\infty}R^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}R}\int_{R\leq|x-y|\leq 2R}|u(y)|dy=0\quad\mbox{ for a.a. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

then
u
satisfies (
2.6
). This follows from the fact that
e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x-y|}\leq e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}R}
in the annular region
\{R\leq|x-y|\leq 2R\}
. In particular, any bounded solution
u
of (
2.5
) admits the representation (
2.7
).

As a consequence, we have:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\lambda,\mu>0
and
(u,v)
be a solution of (
1.1
). Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
one has

(2.9a)

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x-y)\Big[\frac{u^{p}(y)}{v^{q}(y)}+\rho(y)\Big]dy\,,

(2.9b)

\displaystyle v(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x-y)\frac{u^{m}(y)}{v^{s}(y)}dy.

To end this section, let us mention that similar results hold for the Laplace operator
-\Delta
. Precisely, let

(2.10)

\mathcal{G}_{0}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{N/2}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{N-2}{2}\Big)|x|^{2-N}.

Then,
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is the fundamental solution of
-\Delta
in the sense that

-\Delta\mathcal{G}_{0}=\delta_{0}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

The result below was obtained in
[
5
]
and extended in
[
7
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

(see
[
5
, Theorem 2.4]
,
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
)

Let
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
N\geq 3
,
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function and
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a distributional solution of

(2.11)

-\Delta u=f(x)\quad\mbox{ in }\;\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and for a.a.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
u
satisfies

(2.12)

\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R^{N}}\int\limits_{R\leq|y-x|\leq 2R}|u(y)-\ell|dy=0.

(ii)

u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a distributional solution of (
2.11
),
{\rm essinf}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,u=\ell
and

-\Delta u\geq 0\quad\mbox{ in }\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(iii)

We have

(2.13)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{G}_{0}(x-y)f(y)dy<\infty\quad\mbox{ for a.a. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

and the following representation holds

(2.14)

u(x)=\ell+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{G}_{0}(x-y)f(y)dy\quad\mbox{ for a.a. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

As a consequence of Proposition
2.4
and similar to Lemma
2.3
we have:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(u,v)
be a solution of (
1.4
). Then, there exists a constant
c(N)>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
one has

(2.15a)

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=c(N)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x-y|^{2-N}\Big[\frac{u^{p}(y)}{v^{q}(y)}+\rho(y)\Big]dy\,,

(2.15b)

\displaystyle v(x)

\displaystyle=c(N)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x-y|^{2-N}\frac{u^{m}(y)}{v^{s}(y)}dy.

2.2
Some results on semilinear elliptic equations

In this section we collect some useful results on semilinear elliptic equations.

Lemma 2.6
.

(see
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
)

Let
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be such that

\liminf_{t\to 0}f(t)t^{-\frac{N}{N-2}}>0.

Then, the inequality

Lemma 2.9

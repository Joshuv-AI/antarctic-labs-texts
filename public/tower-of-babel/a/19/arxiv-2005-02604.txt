Abstract
In this note we study the Bochner formula on smooth metric measure spaces. We introduce weighted curvature conditions that imply vanishing of all Betti numbers.

Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented Riemannian manifold, let
\operatorname{vol}_{g}
denote its volume form and let
f
be a smooth function on
M
. The triple
\big(M,g,e^{-f}\operatorname{vol}_{g}\big)
is called a smooth metric measure space. Based on considerations from diffusion processes, Bakry–Émery
[
1
]
introduced the tensor

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{f}=\operatorname{Ric}+\operatorname{Hess}f

as a weighted Ricci curvature for a geometric measure space. In fact, this tensor appeared earlier in work of Lichnerowicz
[
3
]
. Volume comparison theorems for smooth metric measure spaces with
\operatorname{Ric}_{f}
bounded from below have been established by Qian
[
7
]
, Lott
[
4
]
, Bakry–Qian
[
2
]
and Wei–Wylie
[
8
]
.

In this note we study the Bochner technique on smooth metric measure spaces. The distortion of the volume element introduces a diffusion term to the Bochner formula

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\omega=(dd^{*}_{f}+d^{*}_{f}d)\omega=\nabla^{*}_{f}\nabla\omega+\operatorname{Ric}(\omega)-(\operatorname{Hess}f)\omega,

where
\operatorname{Ric}
is the Bochner operator on
p
-forms. Lott
[
4
]
proved that if
\operatorname{Ric}_{f}\geq 0
, then all
\Delta_{f}
-harmonic
1
-forms are parallel and, for compact manifolds,
H^{1}(M;\mathbb{R})
is isomorphic to the space of all parallel
1
-forms
\omega
which satisfy
\big\langle\nabla e^{-f},\omega\big\rangle=0
. Moreover, if
\operatorname{Ric}_{f}>0
, then all
\Delta_{f}
-harmonic
1
-forms vanish.

We introduce new weighted curvature conditions that imply rigidity and vanishing results for
\Delta_{f}
-harmonic
p
-forms for
p\geq 1
. We can restrict to
p
-forms
\omega
for
1\leq p\leq\big\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\big\rfloor
since
\omega
is parallel if and only if
\ast\omega
is parallel, where
\ast
denotes the Hodge star.

By convention, we will refer to the eigenvalues of the curvature operator simply as the eigenvalues of the associated curvature tensor.

{theorem*}

Let
\big(M^{n},g,e^{-f}\operatorname{vol}_{g}\big)
be a smooth metric measure space. For
1\leq p<\frac{n}{2}
set

\displaystyle h=\frac{1}{n-2p}\operatorname{Hess}f-\frac{\Delta f}{2(n-p)(n-2p)}g.

Let
\omega
be a
\Delta_{f}
-harmonic
p
-form with
|\omega|\in L^{2}\big(M,e^{-f}\operatorname{vol}_{g}\big)
for
1\leq p<\frac{n}{2}
. Let
\lambda_{1}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{n}{2}}
denote the eigenvalues of the weighted curvature tensor
\operatorname{Rm}+h\owedge g
.

If
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-p}\geq 0
, then
\omega
is parallel. If in addition
M
is compact, then
H^{p}(M)=\big\{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)\,|\,\nabla\omega=0\ \text{and}\ i_{\nabla f}\omega=0\big\}
.

If
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-p}>0
, then
\omega
vanishes. If in addition
M
is compact, then the Betti numbers
b_{p}(M)
and
b_{n-p}(M)
vanish for
1\leq p<\frac{n}{2}
.

For
p=1
the Ricci curvature of the modified curvature tensor is the Bakry–Émery Ricci tensor, and the assumption in the Theorem implies that it is nonnegative. In this sense the Theorem is a generalization of Lott’s
[
4
]
results for
1
-forms.

A stronger curvature assumption also allows control in the middle dimension
p=\frac{n}{2}
. Recall that a curvature tensor is
l
-nonnegative (positive) if the sum of its lowest
l
eigenvalues is nonnegative (positive).

{proposition*}

Let
\big(M^{n},g,e^{-f}\operatorname{vol}_{g}\big)
be a smooth metric measure space. Let
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{n}
denote the eigenvalues of
\operatorname{Hess}f
and let
1\leq p\leq\big\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\big\rfloor
.

Let
\omega
be a
\Delta_{f}
-harmonic
p
-form with
|\omega|\in L^{2}\big(M,e^{-f}\operatorname{vol}_{g}\big)
. If the weighted curvature tensor

\displaystyle\operatorname{Rm}+\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\mu_{i}}{2p(n-p)}g\owedge g

is
(n-p)
-nonnegative, then
\omega
is parallel. If it is
(n-p)
-positive, then
\omega
vanishes.

In particular, if
M
is compact, then
H^{p}(M)=\big\{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)\,|\,\nabla\omega=0\ \text{and}\ i_{\nabla f}\omega=0\big\}
and in case the weighted curvature tensor is
(n-p)
-positive, the Betti numbers
b_{p}(M)
and
b_{n-p}(M)
vanish.

The notation in this paper builds up on the presentation in
[
5
, Chapter 9]
and
[
6
]
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Algebraic curvature tensors

For an
n
-dimensional Euclidean vector space
(V,g)
let
\mathcal{T}^{(0,k)}(V)
denote the vector space of
(0,k)
-tensors and
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(V)
the vector space of symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors on
V
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(V)
denote the vector space of
(0,4)
-tensors with
T(X,Y,Z,W)=-T(Y,X,Z,W)=T(Z,W,X,Y)
. If
T
also satisfies the algebraic Bianchi identity, then
T
is called algebraic curvature tensor,
T\in\mathcal{C}_{B}(V)
.

The Kulkarni–Nomizu product of
S_{1},S_{2}\in\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(V)
is given by

\displaystyle(S_{1}\owedge S_{2})(X,Y,Z,W)=S_{1}(X,Z)S_{2}(Y,W)-S_{1}(X,W)S_{2}(Y,Z)

\displaystyle\hphantom{(S_1 \owedge S_2)(X,Y,Z,W) =}{}+S_{1}(Y,W)S_{2}(X,Z)-S_{1}(Y,Z)S_{2}(X,W).

With this convention the algebraic curvature tensor
I=\frac{1}{2}g\owedge g
corresponds to the curvature tensor of the unit sphere.

Recall that the decomposition of
\mathcal{C}(V)
into
O(n)
-irreducible components is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(V)=\langle I\rangle\oplus\langle\mathring{\operatorname{Ric}}\rangle\oplus\langle W\rangle\oplus\Lambda^{4}V,

where
\langle\mathring{\operatorname{Ric}}\rangle=S_{0}^{2}(V)\owedge g
is the subspace of algebraic curvature tensors of trace-free Ricci type,
S_{0}^{2}(V)=\big\{h\in\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(V)\,|\,\operatorname{tr}(h)=0\big\}
, and
\langle W\rangle
denotes the subspace of Weyl tensors.

Explicitly, every algebraic curvature tensor decomposes as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Rm}=\frac{\operatorname{scal}}{2(n-1)n}g\owedge g+\frac{1}{n-2}\mathring{\operatorname{Ric}}\owedge g+W.

2.2
Lichnerowicz Laplacians on smooth metric measure spaces

Let
(M,g,f)
be a smooth metric measure space. The formal adjoints of the exterior and covariant derivative with respect to the measure
e^{-f}\operatorname{vol}_{g}
are given by

\displaystyle d^{*}_{f}=d^{*}+i_{\nabla f}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla^{*}_{f}=\nabla^{*}+i_{\nabla f}.

More generally, for a vector field
U
on
M
, we will consider

\displaystyle d^{*}_{U}=d^{*}+i_{U}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla^{*}_{U}=\nabla^{*}+i_{U}.

The associated generalized Lichnerowicz Laplacian on
(0,k)
-tensors is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{U}T=\nabla^{*}_{U}\nabla T+\operatorname{Ric}(T)-(\nabla U)T,

where the curvature term is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}(T)(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(R(X_{i},e_{j})T)(X_{1},\dots,e_{j},\dots,X_{k}).

A tensor
T
is called
U
-harmonic
if
\Delta_{U}T=0
.

To emphasize that the curvature term is calculated with respect to the curvature tensor
\operatorname{Rm}
, we will also write
\operatorname{Ric}_{\operatorname{Rm}}(T)
for
\operatorname{Ric}(T)
.

Recall that for an endomorphism
L
of
V
and a
(0,k)
-tensor
T
we have

\displaystyle(LT)(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=-\sum_{i=1}^{k}T(X_{1},\dots,L(X_{i}),\dots,X_{k}).

In particular, the Ricci identity implies that the definition of the curvature term in the Lichnerowicz Laplacian naturally carries over to algebraic curvature tensors.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold and
U
a vector field on
M
. For a
(0,k)
-tensor
T
on
M
set
\operatorname{Ric}_{U}(T)=\operatorname{Ric}(T)-(\nabla U)T
.

(a)

Every
p
-form satisfies

\displaystyle(dd^{*}_{U}+d^{*}_{U}d)\omega=\nabla^{*}_{U}\nabla\omega+\operatorname{Ric}_{U}(\omega).

(b)

Every symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor satisfies

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\nabla^{*}_{U}T)(X)+\big(\nabla^{*}_{U}d^{\nabla}T\big)(X,X)=(\nabla^{*}_{U}\nabla T)(X,X)+\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{Ric}_{U}T)(X,X),

where
d^{\nabla}T(Z,X,Y)=(\nabla_{X}T)(Y,Z)-(\nabla_{Y}T)(X,Z)
.

Proof.

(a) The case
U=0
recovers the well-known Bochner formula. The generalized Hodge Laplacian satisfies

\displaystyle dd^{*}_{U}+d^{*}_{U}d=dd^{*}+d^{*}d+di_{U}+i_{U}d=\Delta+L_{U}.

In addition to the classical Lichnerowicz Laplacian we have on the right hand side

\displaystyle\nabla_{U}-(\nabla U)=L_{U}

and thus all diffusion terms balance out.

(b) As in (a), it suffices to consider all terms that depend on
U
and show that

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}i_{U}h)(X)+\big(i_{U}d^{\nabla}h\big)(X,X)=(\nabla_{U}h)(X,X)-\frac{1}{2}((\nabla U)h)(X,X).

This is a straightforward calculation

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}i_{U}h)(X)+\big(i_{U}d^{\nabla}h\big)(X,X)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(\nabla_{X}h)(U,X)+h(\nabla_{X}U,X)+(\nabla_{U}h)(X,X)-(\nabla_{X}h)(U,X)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(\nabla_{U}h)(X,X)+h(\nabla_{X}U,X)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(\nabla_{U}h)(X,X)-\frac{1}{2}((\nabla U)h)(X,X).

∎

Remark 2.2
.

The curvature tensor
\operatorname{Rm}
of a Riemannian manifold satisfies

\displaystyle\nabla^{*}_{U}\nabla\operatorname{Rm}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Ric}_{U}(\operatorname{Rm})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{X}\nabla_{U}^{*}\operatorname{Rm})(Y,Z,W)-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{U}^{*}\operatorname{Rm})(X,Z,W)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\nabla^{*}_U \nabla\operatorname{Rm}+ \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{Ric}_U( \operatorname{Rm}) =}{}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{Z}\nabla_{U}^{*}\operatorname{Rm})(W,X,Y)-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{W}\nabla_{U}^{*}\operatorname{Rm})(Z,X,Y).

A straightforward computation based on the second Bianchi identity shows that all terms that involve
U
cancel.

The Bochner technique with diffusion relies on the following basic observations. Firstly, the maximum principle implies:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold,
U
a vector field on
M
. Let
T
be a tensor such that

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{U}^{*}\nabla T,T)\leq 0.

If
|T|
has a maximum, then
T
is parallel.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that a
p
-form
\omega
satisfies
(dd^{*}_{U}+d^{*}_{U}d)\omega=0
if and only if
d\omega=0
and
d^{*}_{U}\omega=0
.

As in
[
4
]
, if
M
is compact and oriented, standard elliptic theory implies that

\displaystyle H^{p}(M)=\big\{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)\,|\,d\omega=0\ \text{and}\ d^{*}_{U}\omega=0\big\}.

Suppose that
\operatorname{Ric}_{U}\geq 0
on
p
-forms. It follows that a
p
-form
\omega
is
U
-harmonic if and only if
\omega
is parallel and
i_{U}\omega=0
. Thus,

\displaystyle H^{p}(M)=\big\{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)\,|\,\nabla\omega=0\ \text{and}\ i_{U}\omega=0\big\}.

If
U=\nabla f
, then we can use integration to conclude:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(M,g,f)
be a smooth metric measure space with
\int_{M}e^{-f}\operatorname{vol}_{g}<\infty
. If
T
is a
(0,k)
-tensor with
|T|\in L^{2}\big(M,e^{-f}\operatorname{vol}_{g}\big)
and

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{f}^{*}\nabla T,T)\leq 0,

then
T
is parallel.

3
Weighted Lichnerowicz Laplacians

The idea of this section is to define a weighted curvature tensor
\widetilde{\operatorname{Rm}}
so that for a given symmetric tensor
S
the curvature term of the Lichnerowicz Laplacian satisfies

\displaystyle g(\operatorname{Ric}_{\operatorname{Rm}}(T)-(S)T,T)=g\big(\operatorname{Ric}_{\widetilde{\operatorname{Rm}}}(T),T\big).

This will be achieved by adding a weight to the Ricci tensor of
\operatorname{Rm}
, leaving the Weyl curvature unchanged. The specific weight will depend on the irreducible components of the tensors of type
T
, e.g., it is different for forms and symmetric tensors.

Let
T
be a
(0,k)
-tensor. For
\tau_{ij}\in S_{k}
let
T\circ\tau_{ij}
denote the transposition of the
i
-th and
j
-th entries of
T
and for
h\in\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(V)
let
c_{ij}(h\otimes T)
denote the contraction of
h
with the
i
-th and
j
-th entries of
T
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
h\in\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(V)
let
H\colon V\to V
denote the associated symmetric operator. If
T\in\mathcal{T}^{(0,k)}(V)
, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{h\owedge g}(T)(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=2\sum_{i\neq j}(T\circ\tau_{ij})(X_{1},\dots,H(X_{i}),\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\operatorname{Ric}_{h \owedge g}(T) (X_1, \ldots, X_k)=}{}-\sum_{i\neq j}g(X_{i},X_{j})c_{ij}(h\otimes T)(X_{1},\dots,\widehat{X}_{i},\dots,\widehat{X}_{j},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\operatorname{Ric}_{h \owedge g}(T) (X_1, \ldots, X_k)=}{}-\sum_{i\neq j}h(X_{i},X_{j})c_{ij}(g\otimes T)(X_{1},\dots,\widehat{X}_{i},\dots,\widehat{X}_{j},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\operatorname{Ric}_{h \owedge g}(T) (X_1, \ldots, X_k)=}{}-(n-2)(HT)(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})+k\cdot\operatorname{tr}(h)T(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}).

Proof.

The algebraic curvature tensor
R=h\owedge g
satisfies

\displaystyle R(X,Y,Z,W)=g(H(X),Z)g(Y,W)-g(Y,Z)g(H(X),W)

\displaystyle\hphantom{R(X,Y,Z,W) =}{}+g(X,Z)g(H(Y),W)-g(H(Y),Z)g(X,W)

and hence

\displaystyle R(X,Y)Z=(H(X)\wedge Y+X\wedge H(Y))Z

is the corresponding
(1,3)
-tensor. It follows that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{h\owedge g}(T)(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{n}(R(X_{i},e_{a})T)(X_{1},\dots,e_{a},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\operatorname{Ric}_{h \owedge g}(T)(X_1, \ldots, X_k)}{}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{n}((H(X_{i})\wedge e_{a})T)(X_{1},\dots,e_{a},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\operatorname{Ric}_{h \owedge g}(T)(X_1, \ldots, X_k)=}{}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{n}((X_{i}\wedge H(e_{a}))T)(X_{1},\dots,e_{a},\dots,X_{k}).

It is straightforward to calculate

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{n}((X_{i}\wedge H(e_{a}))T)(X_{1},\dots,e_{a},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{a=1}^{n}T(X_{1},\dots,(H(e_{a})\wedge X_{i})X_{j},\dots,e_{a},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{n}T(X_{1},\dots,(H(e_{a})\wedge X_{i})e_{a},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{a=1}^{n}T(X_{1},\dots,g(H(e_{a}),X_{j})X_{i}-g(X_{i},X_{j})H(e_{a}),\dots,e_{a},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{n}T(X_{1},\dots,g(H(e_{a}),e_{a})X_{i}-g(e_{a},X_{i})H(e_{a}),\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{a=1}^{n}T(X_{1},\dots,g(e_{a},H(X_{j}))X_{i},\dots,e_{a},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}-\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{a=1}^{n}g(X_{i},X_{j})T(X_{1},\dots,H(e_{a}),\dots,e_{a},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{n}h(e_{a},e_{a})T(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{n}T(X_{1},\dots,H(g(e_{a},X_{i})e_{a}),\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i\neq j}T(X_{1},\dots,X_{i},\dots,H(X_{j}),\dots,X_{k})\text{ [here }X_{i}\text{ is in the j-th position]}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}-\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{a,b=1}^{n}g(X_{i},X_{j})h(e_{a},e_{b})T(X_{1},\dots,e_{b},\dots,e_{a},\dots,X_{k})+k\cdot\operatorname{tr}(h)T(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}T(X_{1},\dots,H(X_{i}),\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i\neq j}(T\circ\tau_{ij})(X_{1},\dots,H(X_{j}),\dots,X_{i},\dots,X_{k})\text{ [here }H(X_{j})\text{ is in the j-th position]}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}-\sum_{i\neq j}g(X_{i},X_{j})c_{ij}(h\otimes T)(X_{1},\dots,\widehat{X_{i}},\dots,\widehat{X_{j}},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+k\cdot\operatorname{tr}(h)T(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})+(HT)(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}).

Similarly one computes

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{n}((H(X_{i})\wedge e_{a})T)(X_{1},\dots,e_{a},\dots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i\neq j}(T\circ\tau_{ij})(X_{1},\dots,X_{j},\dots,H(X_{i}),\dots,X_{k})\text{ [here }X_{j}\text{ is in the j-th position]}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}-\!\sum_{i\neq j}h(X_{i},X_{j})c_{ij}(g\otimes T)\big(X_{1},{\dots},\widehat{X_{i}},{\dots},\widehat{X_{j}},{\dots},X_{k}\big)-(n-1)(HT)(X_{1},{\dots},X_{k}).

Adding up both terms yields
\operatorname{Ric}_{h\owedge g}(T)
as claimed.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(V,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Euclidean vector space and
h\in\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(V)
. The following hold:

1.

Every
T\in\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(V)
satisfies

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{h\owedge g}(T)=-nHT-2\langle T,h\rangle g-2\operatorname{tr}(T)h+2\operatorname{tr}(h)T,

\displaystyle g(\operatorname{Ric}_{h\owedge g}(T),T)=-ng(HT,T)-4\operatorname{tr}(T)\langle T,h\rangle+2\operatorname{tr}(h)|T|^{2}.

2.

Every
p
-form
\omega
satisfies

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{h\owedge g}(\omega)=-(n-2p)H\omega+p\operatorname{tr}(h)\omega,

\displaystyle g(\operatorname{Ric}_{h\owedge g}(\omega),\omega)=-(n-2p)g(H\omega,\omega)+p\operatorname{tr}(h)|\omega|^{2}.

3.

Every algebraic
(0,4)
-curvature tensor
\operatorname{Rm}
satisfies

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{h\owedge g}(\operatorname{Rm})=-2(h\owedge\operatorname{Ric})-2g\owedge(c_{24}(h\otimes\operatorname{Rm}))-(n-2)H\operatorname{Rm}+4\operatorname{tr}(h)\operatorname{Rm}.

Proof.

(a) Due to the symmetry of
T
it follows that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{h\owedge g}(T)(X_{1},X_{2})=2\{T(H(X_{1}),X_{2})+T(X_{1},H(X_{2})\}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\operatorname{Ric}_{h \owedge g}(T)(X_1,X_2) =}{}-2\{g(X_{1},X_{2})\langle h,T\rangle+h(X_{1},X_{2})\operatorname{tr}(T)\}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\operatorname{Ric}_{h \owedge g}(T)(X_1,X_2) =}{}-(n-2)(HT)(X_{1},X_{2})+2\operatorname{tr}(h)T(X_{1},X_{2}).

(b) Since
\omega\circ\tau_{ij}=-\omega
for every transposition
\tau_{ij}
it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}(\omega\circ\tau_{ij})(X_{1},\dots,H(X_{i}),\dots,X_{p})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\neq j}\omega(X_{1},\dots,H(X_{i}),\dots,X_{p})

\displaystyle=-(p-1)\sum_{i=1}^{p}\omega(X_{1},\dots,H(X_{i}),\dots,X_{p})

\displaystyle=(p-1)(H\omega)(X_{1},\dots,X_{p})

and furthermore
c_{ij}(g\otimes\omega)=c_{ij}(h\otimes\omega)=0
for all
i\neq j
. This implies the claim.

(c) The symmetries of the curvature tensor imply that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}(\operatorname{Rm}\circ\tau_{ij})(X_{1},\dots,H(X_{i}),\dots,X_{4})

\displaystyle\!\!\qquad{}=(H\operatorname{Rm})(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})+(H\operatorname{Rm})(X_{2},X_{3},X_{1},X_{4})+(H\operatorname{Rm})(X_{3},X_{1},X_{2},X_{4})=0

due to the first Bianchi identity.

Computing with respect to an orthonormal eigenbasis of
H
it follows that

\displaystyle(g(\cdot,\cdot)c_{12}(h\otimes\operatorname{Rm}))(X,Y,Z,W)=0,

\displaystyle(g(\cdot,\cdot)c_{13}(h\otimes\operatorname{Rm}))(X,Y,Z,W)=\sum_{a,b=1}^{n}g(X,Z)\operatorname{Rm}(g(H(e_{a}),e_{b})e_{b},Y,e_{a},W)

\displaystyle\hphantom{( g( \cdot, \cdot) c_{13}(h \otimes\operatorname{Rm})) (X, Y, Z, W)}{}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}g(X,Z)\operatorname{Rm}(H(e_{a}),Y,e_{a},W)

\displaystyle\hphantom{( g( \cdot, \cdot) c_{13}(h \otimes\operatorname{Rm})) (X, Y, Z, W)}{}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}g(Z,X)\operatorname{Rm}(e_{a},Y,H(e_{a}),W)

\displaystyle\hphantom{( g( \cdot, \cdot) c_{13}(h \otimes\operatorname{Rm})) (X, Y, Z, W)}{}=(g(\cdot,\cdot)c_{31}(h\otimes\operatorname{Rm}))(X,Y,Z,W).

This implies

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}(g(\cdot,\cdot)c_{ij}(h\otimes\operatorname{Rm}))(X,Y,Z,W)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{g(X,Z)\operatorname{Rm}(H(e_{i}),Y,e_{i},W)+g(X,W)\operatorname{Rm}(H(e_{i}),Y,Z,e_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+g(Y,Z)\operatorname{Rm}(X,H(e_{i}),e_{i},W)+g(Y,W)\operatorname{Rm}(X,H(e_{i}),Z,e_{i})\}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{g(X,Z)\operatorname{Rm}(Y,H(e_{i}),W,e_{i})-g(X,W)\operatorname{Rm}(Y,H(e_{i}),Z,e_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}-g(Y,Z)\operatorname{Rm}(X,H(e_{i}),W,e_{i})+g(Y,W)\operatorname{Rm}(X,H(e_{i}),Z,e_{i})\}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=2\left(g\owedge\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Rm}(\cdot,H(e_{i}),\cdot,e_{i})\right]\right)(X,Y,Z,W)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=2\left(g\owedge c_{24}(h\otimes\operatorname{Rm})\right)(X,Y,Z,W).

Similarly it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}(h(\cdot,\cdot)c_{ij}(g\otimes\operatorname{Rm}))=2\left(h\owedge c_{24}(g\otimes\operatorname{Rm})\right)=2\left(h\owedge\operatorname{Ric}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold and let
U
be a vector field on
M
. Set
S=\nabla U
and for
1\leq p<\frac{n}{2}
set

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{n-2p}S-\frac{1}{2(n-p)(n-2p)}\operatorname{tr}(S)I,

where
I\colon TM\to TM
denotes the identity operator.

Suppose that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{n}{2}}
of the weighted curvature tensor
\operatorname{Rm}+h\owedge g
satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-p}\geq 0

and let
\omega
be a
U
-harmonic
p
-form for
1\leq p<\frac{n}{2}
.

If
|\omega|
achieves a maximum, then
\omega
is parallel. If in addition the inequality is strict, then
\omega
vanishes.

Proof.

Proposition
3.2
(b) and
-I\omega=p\omega
imply that

\displaystyle g(\operatorname{Ric}_{h\owedge g}\omega,\omega)

\displaystyle=-(n-2p)g(H\omega,\omega)+p\operatorname{tr}(h)|\omega|^{2}=-g(((n-2p)H+\operatorname{tr}(h)I)\omega,\omega)

\displaystyle=-g\left(\left(S-\frac{\operatorname{tr}(S)}{2(n-p)}I+\frac{\operatorname{tr}(S)}{2(n-p)}I\right)\omega,\omega\right)=-g(S\omega,\omega).

Thus the Bochner formula takes the form

\displaystyle\Delta_{U}\omega=\nabla^{*}_{U}\nabla\omega+\operatorname{Ric}(\omega)-(\nabla U)\omega=\nabla^{*}_{U}\nabla\omega+\operatorname{Ric}_{\operatorname{Rm}+h\owedge g}(\omega).

The argument in
[
6
, proof of Theorem A]
shows that
\operatorname{Ric}_{\operatorname{Rm}+h\owedge g}(\omega)\geq 0
. Lemma
2.3
implies the claim.

If the inequality is strict, then the same argument shows that
\operatorname{Ric}_{\operatorname{Rm}+h\owedge g}(\omega)>0
unless
\omega=0
.
∎

The above approach only works for
p=\frac{n}{2}
if
S
is a multiple of the identity. However, we have

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and let
U
be a vector field on
M
. Set
S=\nabla U
and fix
1\leq p\leq\big\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\big\rfloor
. Let
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{n}
denote the eigenvalues of
S
. Suppose that the weighted curvature tensor

\displaystyle\operatorname{Rm}+\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\mu_{i}}{2p(n-p)}g\owedge g

is
(n-p)
-nonnegative. If
\omega
is a
U
-harmonic
p
-form
\omega
such that
|\omega|
has a maximum, then
\omega
is parallel. If in addition the weighted curvature tensor is
(n-p)
-positive, then
\omega
vanishes.

Proof.

Calculating with respect to an orthonormal eigenbasis for
S
it follows that

\displaystyle-g((S\omega),\omega)=-\sum_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{p}}(S\omega)_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}\omega_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}=\sum_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{p}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{i_{j}}\right)(\omega_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}})^{2}\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\mu_{i}\right)|\omega|^{2}.

Let
\{\lambda_{\alpha}\}
denote the eigenvalues of
(the curvature operator associated to)

\operatorname{Rm}
and let

be an orthonormal eigenbasis. It follows from

that

\displaystyle g(\operatorname{Ric}_{\operatorname{Rm}}(\omega),\omega)-g(S\omega,\omega)\geq\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}|\Xi_{\alpha}\omega|^{2}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\mu_{i}\!\right)|\omega|^{2}=\sum_{\alpha}\!\left(\!\lambda_{\alpha}+\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\mu_{i}}{p(n-p)}\!\right)|\Xi_{\alpha}\omega|^{2}.

The proof can now be completed as in Proposition

.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
T\in\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(V)

\operatorname{tr}(T)=0

\operatorname{Rm}+h\owedge g

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\geq 0.

If
T
is

-harmonic and
|T|

T
is parallel. If in addition the inequality is strict, then

Proof.

Proposition
3.2

\displaystyle g(\operatorname{Ric}_{h\owedge g}(T),T)

\displaystyle=-ng\left(\left(H+\frac{\operatorname{tr}(h)}{n}I\right)T,T\right)

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{X}\nabla^{*}_{U}T\right)(X)+\left(\nabla^{*}_{U}d^{\nabla}T\right)(X,X)=\left(\nabla^{*}_{U}\nabla T\right)(X,X)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\operatorname{Ric}_{\operatorname{Rm}+h\owedge g}T\right)(X,X).

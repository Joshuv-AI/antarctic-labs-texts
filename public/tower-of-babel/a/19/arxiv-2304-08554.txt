Abstract
. Consider the variation seminorm of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup H t H_{t} in dimension one, taken with respect to t t . We show that this seminorm defines an operator of weak type ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) for the relevant Gaussian measure. The analogous L p L^{p} estimates for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty were already known.

1.
Introduction

In order to measure the fluctuations of a family of linear bounded operators
A_{t}:L^{p}(X)\to L^{p}(X)
, where
t>0
and
X
is a measure space,
it may be useful
to consider quantities involving many differences
T_{t}f(\cdot)-T_{s}f(\cdot)
, with
s,t>0
and
f\in L^{p}(X)
.
Among these quantities, variation and oscillation seminorms are probably the best known. The corresponding variational
inequalities, stating that the
L^{p}
-norm of the variation or the oscillation of
(A_{t}f)_{t>0}
is uniformly bounded by the
L^{p}
-norm of
f
,
have attracted increasing interest in the last fifty years.

In fact, in 1976
D. Lépingle
proved a first variational inequality for a family of bounded martingales
[
19
]
, also providing a weak type
(1,1)
variant
(see also
[
22
]
for extensions and
a different proof).
Then V. F. Gaposhkin in
[
10
,
11
]

considered oscillational inequalities for standard ergodic averages.
Some years later, in 1989,
J. Bourgain proved the pointwise convergence of ergodic averages along polynomial orbits
by replacing the classical estimates for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function by variational seminorm bounds
[
4
]
.
Further results may be found
in
[
15
,
13
]
.

After the seminal work by Bourgain, in light of the applications to pointwise convergence phenomena, the study of variational inequalities
spread in many different contexts (for an updated survey, especially from the point of view of oscillation estimates, we refer to
[
24
]
).
The recent paper
[
23
]

deals with jump inequalities, seen as endpoint results for variation inequalities.
Focusing on the field of harmonic analysis, we recall here the cases of the Hilbert transform
[
5
]
, Fejér and Poisson kernels
[
17
]
,
families of truncations of Gaussian Riesz transforms
[
12
]
and
the heat and Poisson semigroups of the Laplacian and Hermite operator
[
9
,
2
]
.
Analogous results have been obtained for
semigroups associated with Fourier-Bessel expansions
[
3
]
, for spherical means or averages along curves
(t,t^{a})
in the plane
[
16
]
and
for differential and singular integral operators in some weighted Lebesgue spaces
[
20
,
21
]
.
Some results are also known for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup; see below.

To define the variation seminorm
v(\rho)
, let
\phi
be a real- or complex-valued function defined in an interval
I
. Then for
1\leq\rho<\infty

\|\phi\|_{v(\rho),I}:=\sup\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\phi(t_{i})-\phi(t_{i-1})|^{\rho}\right)^{1/\rho},

where the supremum is taken over all finite, increasing sequences
\left(t_{i}\right)_{0}^{n}
of points in
I
.
This is a seminorm which vanishes only for constant functions. We will often omit indicating the interval
I
.
The space
V(\rho,I)
consists of those functions
\phi
in
I
for which
\|\phi\|_{v(\rho),I}<\infty
. In this paper we will only consider the variation of continuous functions
\phi(t)
.

We next introduce the one-dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup.
Let
R(x)=x^{2}/2
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and define the measure
d\gamma_{\infty}(u)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\,\exp(-R(u))\,du
in
\mathbb{R}
.
The semigroup is then given by

H_{t}f(x)=\int f(u)\,K_{t}(x,u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u),\qquad t>0,

where
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
and the kernel
K_{t}
is

(1.1)

K_{t}(x,u)=\frac{e^{R(x)}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}}\,\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{(e^{-t}u-x)^{2}}{1-e^{-2t}}\right),\qquad t>0,\quad(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

The measure
\gamma_{\infty}
is the unique probability measure which is invariant under the semigroup.

We will consider the variation of the semigroup, i.e., the seminorm
\|H_{t}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),\mathbb{R}_{+}}
taken with respect to
t
and considered as an operator defined for
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
.

When
\rho>2

it is known that this operator is bounded from
L^{p}(\gamma_{\infty})
to
L^{p}(\gamma_{\infty})
for

1<p<\infty
, even for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup in any finite dimension. This follows from
[
14
]
, where a general symmetric diffusion semigroup is considered.
Another proof
can be found in

[
18
, Corollary 4.5]
; it is verified in

[
1
, page 31]
that this corollary can be applied in our setting.

The inspiration for the present work came from a comment in
[
1
, page 31]
saying that no variational weak type
(1,1)
bound is known for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup. We will prove the following one-dimensional result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each
\rho>2
the operator that maps
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
to the function

(1.2)

\|H_{t}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),\mathbb{R}_{+}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

where the
v(\rho)
seminorm is taken in the variable
t
, is of weak type
(1,1)
with respect to the measure
\gamma_{\infty}
.

In other words, the inequality

(1.3)

\gamma_{\infty}\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\|H_{t}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),\mathbb{R}_{+}}>\alpha\}\leq\frac{C}{\alpha}\,\|f\|_{L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})},\qquad\alpha>0,

holds for some
C>0
and all functions
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
.

The structure of this paper is as follows. Section
2
contains some preliminaries, mainly concerning the variation seminorm and the
t
derivative of
K_{t}
. In the following sections, Theorem
1.1
will be obtained as a direct consequence of Propositions
3.1
,
4.1
and
5.1
. Of these, Proposition
3.1

deals with the variation only for
t\geq 1
. For these values of
t
, the estimate (
1.3
) is slightly strengthened. In Section
4
, we split the operator given by the variation for
0<t\leq 1
into a local and a global part. This is done by means of a partition of the line into intervals where the density of
\gamma_{\infty}
is essentially constant. Then Proposition
4.1
, dealing with the global part, is proved.

Proposition
5.1
is an estimate of the local part of the variation in
0<t\leq 1
, and is stated and proved in the long
Section
5
. The proof goes via
Proposition
5.2
, which deals with one of the intervals of the partition at a time, and where
\gamma_{\infty}
is replaced by Lebesgue measure.
Finally, Proposition
5.2
is seen to follow from estimates for the variation of integrals of an
L^{1}
function over certain intervals, obtained as a consequence of a known theorem about the variation of mean values.

Theorem
1.1
is proved in the one-dimensional case, and
the handling of integrals over intervals in Section
5
just mentioned seems hard to extend to higher dimensions, because of geometrical obstructions. Only the results of Sections
3
and
4
extend easily.

We point out that in Sections
3
and
4
, we use arguments similar to some from the authors’ papers
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
. Rather than invoking the results from there, we prefer to give the proofs explicitly.

2.
Preliminaries

By
C<\infty
and
c>0
we denote many different absolute constants, and
X\lesssim Y
, or equivalently
Y\gtrsim X
, means
X\leq CY
.
We write
X\simeq Y
if both
X\lesssim Y
and
Y\lesssim X
.

Seminorms of type
\|.\|_{v(\rho),I}
will always be taken in one of the variables
t
or
\tau
.

We will let
\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)=\partial K_{t}(x,u)/\partial t
.

It is not immediately obvious that the function
x\mapsto\|H_{t}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is measurable. But
H_{t}f(x)
is continuous in
t
for each
x
, as seen by dominated convergence. In the definition of the
{v(\rho)}
seminorm, it is therefore enough to consider sequences of points
t_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
, thus only a countable family of sequences. The measurability follows.

We give some simple properties of the variation, and first observe that the seminorm

\|.\|_{v(\rho)}
is decreasing in
\rho
for
1\leq\rho<\infty
.
This seminorm is also subadditive in
I
, in the following sense. Take an inner point
\tau
of
I
and set
I_{+}=I\cap[\tau,+\infty)
and
I_{-}=I\cap(-\infty,\tau]
. Then for
1\leq\rho<\infty
and any
\phi

\|\phi\|_{v(\rho),I}\leq\|\phi\|_{v(\rho),I_{+}}+\|\phi\|_{v(\rho),I_{-}}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq\rho<\infty
.

(a) If
\phi\in C^{1}(I)
and
\phi^{\prime}\in L^{1}(I)
, then
\phi\in V(\rho,I)
and

\|\phi\|_{v(\rho),I}\leq\int_{I}|\phi^{\prime}(t)|\,dt.

(b) If
\phi
is monotone and bounded in
I
, then
\phi\in V(\rho,I)
and

\|\phi\|_{v(\rho),I}\leq 2\sup_{I}|\phi|.

Both parts here are easy for
\rho=1
and then follow for all
\rho
.

The variation of products can be estimated as follows.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\phi
and
\psi
be bounded functions defined in the interval
I
. Then
for any
1\leq\rho<\infty

\|\phi\psi\|_{v(\rho)}\leq\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\psi\|_{v(\rho)}+\|\phi\|_{v(\rho)}\|\psi\|_{\infty}.

To prove this, it is enough to write for an increasing sequence
(t_{i})
in
I

\phi(t_{i})\psi(t_{i})-\phi(t_{i-1})\psi(t_{i-1})=\phi(t_{i})(\psi(t_{i})-\psi(t_{i-1}))+(\phi(t_{i})-\phi(t_{i-1}))\psi(t_{i-1}),

and then take the
\ell^{\rho}
norm.

We next make some preparations for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The following estimate of the variation seminorm of
H_{t}f(x)
will be useful. Let the interval
I
be either

(0,1]
or
[1,\infty)
.
From Lemma
2.1
(a), we conclude that

\displaystyle\|H_{t}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),I}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int K_{t}(x,u)f(u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)\right|\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\left|\int\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)f(u)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)\right|\,dt

(2.1)

\displaystyle\leq\int\int_{I}\big|\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)\big|\,dt\,|f(u)|\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u).

To justify moving the differentiation inside the integral in the second step here, we refer to

[
8
, Lemma 5.3]
.

We compute and estimate
\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

t>0
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)=

\displaystyle\,K_{t}(x,u)\,\left(-\frac{e^{-2t}}{1-e^{-2t}}+\frac{e^{-2t}(e^{-t}u-x)^{2}}{(1-e^{-2t})^{2}}+\frac{e^{-t}u(e^{-t}u-x)}{1-e^{-2t}}\right).

Moreover, for
t\geq 1
one has

(2.3)

\displaystyle\big|\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)\big|\lesssim e^{R(x)}\exp\big(-c\left(e^{-t}\,u-x\right)^{2}\big)\big(e^{-t}\,|u|+e^{-2t}\big).

Proof.

We omit the proof of (
2.2
). When
t\geq 1
, (
2.2
) implies that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\big|\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)\big|\lesssim K_{t}(x,u)\big(|e^{-t}\,u-x|\,e^{-t}\,|u|+e^{-2t}\,(e^{-t}\,u-x)^{2}+e^{-2t}\big).

For
t\geq 1
(
1.1
) shows that
K_{t}\lesssim e^{R(x)}\,\exp\big(-c\left(e^{-t}\,u-x\right)^{2}\big)
. Changing the value of
c
here,
we may neglect the factors
e^{-t}\,u-x
in (
2.4
) and obtain
(
2.3
).
∎

3.
The case of large
t

In this section we consider the variation of
H_{t}f(x)
only for
1\leq t<\infty
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For each
\rho>2
the operator that maps
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
to the function

\|H_{t}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),[1,+\infty)},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

is of weak type
(1,1)
with respect to the measure
\gamma_{\infty}
.
In fact, one has the following stronger result: If
\rho>2
and
\|f\|_{L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})}=1
, then

(3.1)

\gamma_{\infty}\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\|H_{t}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),[1,\infty)}>\alpha\right\}\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha\sqrt{\log\alpha}},\qquad\alpha>2.

Notice
that, when
t
is large, the estimate (
1.3
) is enhanced by a logarithmic factor. In
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, an analogous phenomenon was
already observed both for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck maximal operator and for the Gaussian Riesz transform.

Proof.

Let

f
be normalized in
L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
.
We integrate (
2.3
), getting

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\big|\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)\big|\,dt\lesssim e^{R(x)}\int_{1}^{\infty}\exp\big(-c\left(e^{-t}\,u-x\right)^{2}\big)\big(e^{-t}\,|u|+e^{-2t}\big)\,dt.

In the last parenthesis in the second integrand here, we consider first only the term
e^{-t}\,|u|
and make the
change of variable
e^{-t}\,u-x=y
, separately for
u>0
and
u<0
. As a result,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\exp\big({-c(e^{-t}\,u-x)^{2}}\big)\,e^{-t}\,|u|\,dt\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(-cy^{2})\,dy\simeq 1.

Taking also the term
e^{-2t}
in the integral above into account, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\big|\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)\big|\,dt\lesssim e^{R(x)}.

Now (
2
) leads to

\displaystyle\|H_{t}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),[1,\infty)}\lesssim e^{R(x)}.

It is easily seen that

\gamma_{\infty}\left\{x:e^{R(x)}>\beta\right\}\lesssim\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{\log\beta}},\qquad\beta>2.

From this (
3.1
) follows, and since (
1.3
) is trivial for
\alpha\leq 2
, Proposition
3.1
is proved.
∎

4.
The global case with small
t

We first split the operator
H_{t}
in a local and a global part, in a way adapted to
\gamma_{\infty}
.
Let
\eta\geq 0
be a smooth function in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
which is 1 in
(0,1/2]
and 0 in
[1,\infty)
.
The local part of the semigroup is defined by

H_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}f(x)=\int f(u)\,K_{t}(x,u)\,\eta((1+|x|)|x-u|)\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u).

The global part
H_{t}^{\mathrm{glob}}=H_{t}-H_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is given by a similar expression, with

\eta(.)
replaced by
1-\eta(.)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For each
\rho>2
the operator that maps
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
to the function

\|H_{t}^{\mathrm{glob}}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),(0,1]},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

is of weak type
(1,1)
with respect to the measure
\gamma_{\infty}
.

Proof.

We first give an estimate of the number of zeros of the function
t\mapsto\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)
for
0<t<1
.
From (
2.2
) we see that we can write

\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)=K_{t}(x,u)\,\frac{P_{x,u}(e^{-t})}{(1-e^{-2t})^{2}},

where
P_{x,u}
is a polynomial of degree at most 4, with coefficients depending on
x
and
u
. Thus
\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)
can have at most four zeros in
(0,1)
.
Denote these zeros by
t_{1},\dots,t_{N-1}
; the
t_{i}
and also
N
will depend on
(x,u)
, and
N\leq 5
. Set also

t_{0}=0
and
t_{N}=1
.
Then

\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)|\,dt=\sum_{1}^{N}\left|\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\dot{K}_{t}(x,u)\,dt\right|\leq 10\,\sup_{(0,1]}K_{t}(x,u).

Since the computation (
2
) remains valid with an extra factor
1-\eta((1+|x|)|x-u|)
, we conclude

(4.1)

\|H_{t}^{\mathrm{glob}}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),(0,1]}\lesssim\int|f(u)|\sup_{(0,1]}K_{t}(x,u)\,(1-\eta((1+|x|)|x-u|))\,d\gamma_{\infty}(u)

We claim that for
0<t\leq 1
and all
(x,u)

(4.2)

\sup_{(0,1]}K_{t}(x,u)\,(1-\eta((1+|x|)|x-u|))\lesssim e^{R(x)}\,(1+|x|).

If
1-\eta((1+|x|)|x-u|)\neq 0
, we have
|x-u|>1/(2(1+|x|))
and thus for
0<t\leq 1
also

\displaystyle(1+|x|)^{-1}

\displaystyle<2|x-u|\leq 2|x-e^{t}\,x|+2|e^{t}\,x-u|=2(e^{t}-1)|x|+2e^{t}|x-e^{-t}u|

\displaystyle\leq 2et(1+|x|)+2e|x-e^{-t}u|.

Now, if
t(1+|x|)^{2}<1/(4e)
we get a bootstrap implying

(1+|x|)^{-1}<4e|x-e^{-t}u|.

Then we see from (
1.1
) that

\displaystyle e^{-R(x)}\,K_{t}(x,u)

\displaystyle\simeq t^{-1/2}\,\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(e^{-t}u-x)^{2}}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq t^{-1/2}\,\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{1}{16e^{2}t(1+|x|)^{2}}\right)\lesssim 1+|x|,

and (
4.2
) follows. On the other hand, if
t(1+|x|)^{2}\geq 1/(4e)
, (
4.2
) also follows, since
then
t^{-1/2}\lesssim 1+|x|
.

Combining now (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we get

\|H_{t}^{\mathrm{glob}}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),(0,1]}\lesssim e^{R(x)}\,(1+|x|)\,\|f\|_{L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})}.

This ends the proof of Proposition
4.1
, because

\gamma_{\infty}\left\{x:\,e^{R(x)}\,(1+|x|)>\beta\right\}\lesssim\frac{1}{\beta},\qquad\beta>0.

∎

5.
The local case with small
t

This section consists of the proof of the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

For each
\rho>2
the operator that maps
f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
to the function

\|H_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}f(x)\|_{v(\rho),(0,1]},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

is of weak type
(1,1)
with respect to the measure
\gamma_{\infty}
.

Splitting of the line into local intervals

The localization means that the value
H_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}f(x)
depends only on the restriction of
f
to the interval
\{u:\>|u-x|\leq 1/(1+|x|)\}
, and we will split the line into intervals of similar type.
Choose an increasing sequence
(x_{j})_{0}^{\infty}
with
x_{0}=0
such that for
j=0,1,\dots

x_{j+1}-\frac{1}{1+x_{j+1}}=x_{j}+\frac{1}{1+x_{j}}.

This recursion formula determines the sequence uniquely. We have
x_{j+1}-x_{j}<2
for all
j\geq 0
, so that
x_{j}\leq 2j
. Thus
x_{j+1}-x_{j}\gtrsim 1/j
and
x_{j}\to+\infty
as
j\to\infty
. (In fact,
x_{j}
is close to
2\,\sqrt{j}-1
, as shown in the Appendix.) For
j<0
we let

x_{j}=-x_{|j|}
.

The intervals

I_{j}=\left[x_{j}-\frac{1}{1+|x_{j}|},\>x_{j}+\frac{1}{1+|x_{j}|}\right],\qquad j\in\mathbb{Z},

are pairwise disjoint except for endpoints, and they cover
\mathbb{R}
.
If
\mathrm{supp}\,f\subset I_{j}
, we claim that the support of
H_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}f
is contained in the interval

\widetilde{I}_{j}=\left[x_{j}-\frac{4}{1+|x_{j}|},\>x_{j}+\frac{4}{1+|x_{j}|}\right].

To verify this, let
x\in\mathrm{supp}\,H_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}f
. Then
x
has distance at most
1/(1+|x|)
from some point in
I_{j}
, so that

(5.1)

|x-x_{j}|\leq\frac{1}{1+|x|}+\frac{1}{1+|x_{j}|},

which implies

1+|x|\geq 1+|x_{j}|-\frac{1}{1+|x|}-\frac{1}{1+|x_{j}|}\geq|x_{j}|-1\geq\frac{1+|x_{j}|}{3},

the last inequality holding only if
|x_{j}|\geq 2
. But if
|x_{j}|<2
, one has the same estimate, since then

1+|x|\geq 1\geq({1+|x_{j}|})/3
. The claim now follows from (
5.1
).

We also observe that if
\mathrm{supp}\,f\subset I_{j}
and
j>0
, then

\mathrm{supp}\,H_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}f\subset\{x\geq 0\}
. Indeed,
I_{j}\subset[1,\infty)
when

j>0
, so if
x\in\mathrm{supp}\,H_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}f
we must have
x\geq 1-1/(1+|x|)\geq 0
.

The intervals
\widetilde{I}_{j}
have bounded overlap. Therefore, it is enough to prove Theorem
1.1
for functions
f
supported in
I_{j}
, with a bound that is uniform in
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In each
\widetilde{I}_{j}
, the density of
\gamma_{\infty}
is essentially constant,
since
e^{-R(x)}\simeq e^{-R(x_{j})}
for
x\in\widetilde{I}_{j}
,
and this is uniform in
j
. Therefore, we can pass to Lebesgue measure in
u
and in
x
. We replace

f\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\infty})
, supported in
I_{j}
, by
g(u)=f(u)\,e^{-R(u)}\in L^{1}(du)
, with the same support. Instead of

H_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}
, we can then consider the operator

\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}g(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}}\,\int g(u)\,\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{(e^{-t}u-x)^{2}}{1-e^{-2t}}\right)\,\eta((1+|x|)|x-u|)\,du,

where we deleted the essentially constant factor
e^{R(x)}
.

We conclude from the above that the following proposition implies Proposition
5.1
.

Proposition 5.2
.

For
\rho>2
and each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the operator that maps
g\in L^{1}(I_{j})
to

\|\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}g(x)\|_{v(\rho),(0,1]}

is bounded from
L^{1}(I_{j})
to
L^{1,\infty}(\widetilde{I}_{j})
,
where the intervals are endowed with the Lebesgue measure. This is uniform in
j
.

Proof of Proposition
5.2

For symmetry reasons, it is enough to consider only
j\geq 0
and only points
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
With
j\geq 0
fixed, we let
g\in L^{1}(I_{j})
. The expression for
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\mathrm{loc}}g(x)
will be rewritten in terms of integrals of only
f(u)
over many intervals which depend on
t
. Here we follow

[
5
, proof of Lemma 2.4]
, writing

APPENDIX. Asymptotics of
x_{j}

Claim:

x_{j}=2\,\sqrt{j}-1+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{j}}\right),\qquad j\to+\infty.

To prove this, let
z_{j}=1+x_{j}
for
j=1,2,\dots
. The recursion formula says that

z_{j+1}-z_{j}=1/z_{j}+1/z_{j+1}
. We have

(5.28)

z_{j+1}^{2}-z_{j}^{2}=(z_{j+1}+z_{j})\left(\frac{1}{z_{j}}+\frac{1}{z_{j+1}}\right)=2+\frac{z_{j+1}}{z_{j}}+\frac{z_{j}}{z_{j+1}}>2.

Writing
z_{j}^{2}
as a telescoping sum, we obtain

z_{j}^{2}=z_{0}^{2}+\sum_{0}^{j-1}\left(z_{\nu+1}^{2}-z_{\nu}^{2}\right)\geq 1+2j,

and so

z_{j+1}-z_{j}\leq 2/\sqrt{1+2j}
. We continue with the sum in (
5.28
), getting

z_{j+1}^{2}-z_{j}^{2}=2+\frac{z_{j+1}-z_{j}}{z_{j}}+1+\frac{z_{j}-z_{j+1}}{z_{j+1}}+1=4+(z_{j+1}-z_{j})\left(\frac{1}{z_{j}}-\frac{1}{z_{j+1}}\right)\\
=4+\frac{(z_{j+1}-z_{j})^{2}}{z_{j}z_{j+1}}=4+O\left(\frac{1}{(1+2j)^{2}}\right).

Summing as above, we find

z_{j}^{2}=4j+O(1),

and so
z_{j}=2\,\sqrt{j}+O(1/\sqrt{j})
. This proves the claim.

Abstract
The scattering formulation of characteristic mode decomposition is utilized to extend modal analysis to lossless scatterers breaking time-reversal symmetry. This enables characteristic modes analysis on devices containing gyrotropic or moving media. The resulting nonreciprocity introduces features not observed in reciprocal scenarios, such as asymmetric phase progression in characteristic far fields. These new phenomena are carefully discussed using examples of varying complexity. Indicators of nonreciprocity based on modal data are also introduced.

II
Scattering-based characteristic modes

This paper uses a formulation of characteristic modes based on an object’s scattering matrix
[
4
]
recently formalized in
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
. In contrast to impedance formulations
[
2
,
14
]
, the scattering approach avoids problems reported in
[
6
]
in cases where the radiation matrix is not equal to the real (Hermitian) part of the impedance matrix, see
[
2
, Eqs. (10)–(11)]
.

Using the scattering matrix
\mathbf{S}
of a linear material object
[
4
]
, characteristic modes are defined by the eigenvalue problem
[
15
,
6
]

\mathbf{S}\mathbf{a}_{n}=s_{n}\mathbf{a}_{n},

(1)

where the complex eigenvalue
s_{n}=1+2t_{n}
is commonly parametrized by a real characteristic number
\lambda_{n}
via relation
s_{n}=-(1-\mathrm{j}\lambda_{n})/(1+\mathrm{j}\lambda_{n})
and where eigenvectors
\mathbf{a}_{n}
collect the expansion coefficients of the fields in the selected basis, such as spherical vector waves commonly used in the case of finite scatterers in free space
[
16
]
. The modal significance of an eigenvector is denoted as
\left|t_{n}\right|=\left|1+\mathrm{j}\lambda_{n}\right|^{-1}=\left|s_{n}-1\right|/2
. Other characteristic quantities, such as the characteristic currents, can be computed by exciting the system with the vector
\mathbf{a}_{n}
and evaluating the desired quantity
[
6
]
.

For lossless scatterers, the scattering matrix is unitary,
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{1}
, and pairs of characteristic excitations can be made orthonormal,
i
.
e
.,

\mathbf{a}_{m}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{a}_{n}=\delta_{mn},

(2)

where
\delta_{mn}
is the Kronecker delta and
H
denotes Hermitian transpose.

III
Characterizing Reciprocity and Nonreciprocity

Characteristic modes of a lossless scatterer satisfy
[
6
, App. B]

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{a}_{n}^{*}=s_{n}\mathbf{a}_{n}^{*}

(3)

due to the unitarity of
\mathbf{S}
. The scattering matrix is symmetric
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}}=\mathbf{S}
for reciprocal scatterers, in which case both
\mathbf{a}_{n}
and
\mathbf{a}_{n}^{*}
are eigenvectors of
\mathbf{S}
with the same eigenvalue. Hence, the eigenvectors can always be made real-valued
1
1

1

Eigenvectors for non-degenerate cases are generally equiphase (with orthonormal
\mathbf{a}_{n}
satisfying
|\mathbf{a}_{n}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{a}_{n}|=1
). For a degenerate case, the modes can exhibit phase progression, but linear combinations of vectors
\mathbf{a}_{n}
and
\mathbf{a}_{n}^{*}
can be used to produce real-valued eigenvectors.
, akin to the treatment of characteristic currents
[
2
]
. Due to symmetries of vector spherical waves
[
16
, Sec. 7.8]
, the real-valued characteristic vectors
\mathbf{a}_{n}
imply conjugate inversion symmetry
[
17
, Eqs. (29)–(30)]
of the characteristic far fields

\boldsymbol{F}_{n}(-\hat{\boldsymbol{r}})=-\boldsymbol{F}_{n}^{\ast}(\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}),

(4)

where
\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}
is an observation direction
\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}=\boldsymbol{r}/r
and the far field
\boldsymbol{F}
is defined by the scattered field
\boldsymbol{E}_{\mathrm{s}}
as
\boldsymbol{F}(\hat{\boldsymbol{r}})=r\,\mathrm{exp}\left\{\mathrm{j}kr\right\}\boldsymbol{E}_{\mathrm{s}}(\boldsymbol{r})
for
r\to\infty
.
This property also follows from selecting real-valued currents in reciprocal EFIE formulations of characteristic modes
2
2

2

The integral relationship between a real-valued current distribution and its radiated far-field
[
18
, §3.5-3.6]
illustrates this property.
.

The above-mentioned properties of reciprocal systems (real-valued eigenvectors, far-field inversion symmetry) are lost in nonreciprocal systems where
\mathbf{S}\neq\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}}
, and their absence can be utilized as distinguishing features to characterize nonreciprocity. To detect nonreciprocity of characteristic modes, the scattering matrix
\mathbf{S}
is decomposed into its symmetric and antisymmetric parts as
\mathbf{S}=(\mathbf{S}+\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}})/2+(\mathbf{S}-\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}})/2
. This implies that the cycle-mean outgoing power
P_{\mathrm{o}}
can be written as

P_{\mathrm{o}}=\dfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{S}\mathbf{a}|^{2}=\dfrac{1}{8}\left|(\mathbf{S}+\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}})\mathbf{a}\right|^{2}\\
+\dfrac{1}{8}\left|(\mathbf{S}-\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}})\mathbf{a}\right|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}\left(\left|\mathbf{S}\mathbf{a}\right|^{2}-\left|\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{a}\right|^{2}\right).

(5)

We denote the first term on the right-hand side as the reciprocal outgoing power
P_{\mathrm{o}}^{\mathrm{r}}
, while the second row corresponds to the nonreciprocal outgoing power
P_{\mathrm{o}}^{\mathrm{nr}}
. We then propose the ratio

\nu=\dfrac{P_{\mathrm{o}}^{\mathrm{nr}}}{P_{\mathrm{o}}}\in\left[0,1\right]

(6)

to characterize the presence of nonreciprocity for a given excitation at a particular frequency. Furthermore, modal far fields
\boldsymbol{F}_{n}
violating (
4
), such as those with asymmetric phase progression, are an indicator of nonreciprocal behavior. Together, the study of the quantity
\nu
and modal phase progression serve to classify individual characteristic modes as either reciprocal or nonreciprocal. Another classification of nonreciprocity based on the extinction cross section has recently been proposed in
[
19
]
, but is not readily applicable to characteristic modes.

IV-A

Ideal circulator

As an initial example demonstrating characteristic modes for nonreciprocal systems, we consider the scattering matrix of an ideal

\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\\
1&0&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}.

(7)

The characteristic modes (in this case, eigenmodes of the matrix
\mathbf{S}

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{\pm p}

\displaystyle=N^{-1/2}\left[1,\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{j}2p\pi/N},\cdots,\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{j}2p\pi(N-1)/N}\right]^{\mathrm{T}},

(8)

\displaystyle s_{\pm p}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{j}2p\pi/N},\quad 1\leq p\leq(N-1)/2,

with

\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{e}}=N^{-1/2}\left[1,1,\cdots,1\right]^{\mathrm{T}},\quad s_{\mathrm{e}}=1,

(9)

with

N

(10)

|\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{o}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{o}}|=1

Application of the definition in (

\nu_{\pm p}=\sin^{2}(2p\pi/N),\quad\nu_{\mathrm{e}}=0,\quad\nu_{\mathrm{o}}=0.

(11)

Similarly, the modal significance of the modes reads

\left|t_{\pm p}\right|=\left|\sin(p\pi/N)\right|,\quad\left|t_{\mathrm{e}}\right|=0,\quad\left|t_{\mathrm{o}}\right|=1.

(12)

These results show that characteristic modes of nonreciprocal devices (such as this ideal circulator) can exhibit a range of values of the parameter
\nu

\nu=0

cf

11

Scattering or transition matrices analogous to (
7

V
Discussion and Conclusions

This letter describes how characteristic mode analysis can be applied to nonreciprocal devices, thereby extending the established theoretical and computational state-of-the-art. This advance enables the analysis of problems involving gyrotropic materials, moving media, and lumped non-reciprocal devices. The decomposition employs scattering matrices, which can be computed numerically in arbitrary full-wave solvers. Certain cases, such as a spherical shell filled with bianisotropic material, even retain the potential for analytical solutions
[
16
]
.

Examples discussed throughout the letter illustrate how specific modal properties are lost when a system exhibits nonreciprocal behavior. Modal nonreciprocity varies with frequency, hindering straightforward single-frequency classification of a mode as being (non)reciprocal. One remaining challenge lies in defining a measure that effectively quantifies modal nonreciprocity.

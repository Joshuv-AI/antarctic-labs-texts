Global existence and optimal time-decay rates of the compressible Navier-Stokes-Euler system
Hai-Liang Li, Ling-Yun Shou

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem of the multi-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes-Euler system for two-phase flow motion, which consists of the isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations and the isothermal compressible Euler equations coupled with each other through a relaxation drag force. We first establish the local existence and uniqueness of the strong solution for general initial data in a critical homogeneous Besov space, and then prove the global existence of the solution if the initial data is a small perturbation of the equilibrium state. Moreover, under the additional condition that the low-frequency part of the initial perturbation also belongs to another Besov space with lower regularity, we obtain the optimal time-decay rates of the global solution toward the equilibrium state. These results imply that the relaxation drag force and the viscosity dissipation affect regularity properties and long time behaviors of solutions for the compressible Navier-Stokes-Euler system.



Key words:
Two-phase flow, Navier-Stokes equations, Euler equations, critical regularity, global existence, optimal time-decay rates

Introduction

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{j}(t)+\min\{1,2^{2j}\}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(a,b)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2^{2j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u-w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim{\text{nonlinear terms}}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{j}(t)}.

\displaystyle 2^{2j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u-w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\gtrsim\begin{cases}2^{2j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad&\mbox{if $j\leq 0,$ }\\
\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad&\mbox{if $j\geq-1.$}\end{cases}

With the help of (

1.19

-bound of

for high frequencies treating

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{3,j}(t)+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(b,w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\Big(\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{2}}+{\text{nonlinear terms}}\Big)\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{3,j}(t)}.

for low frequencies. Combing the above estimates of

to the Cauchy problem (

and

2
Local existence and uniqueness

To show the local existence, we first construct the local Friedrichs approximation (cf.
[
16
,
23
]
), establish the uniform a-priori estimates of the approximate sequence, and then show their convergence to the expected strong solution of the original Cauchy problem (
1.3
).

Step 1: Construction of approximate sequence

Let
u_{L}
be the unique global solution for the linear problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u_{L}-\mathcal{A}u_{L}=0,\quad\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t>0,\\
&u_{L}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

Define

\displaystyle\tilde{a}:=\frac{1}{1+a}-1,\quad\tilde{u}:=u-u_{L},\quad\Pi(\tilde{a}):=\int_{0}^{\tilde{a}}P^{\prime}(\frac{1}{1+s})\frac{1}{1+s}ds,

(2.2)

so that the system (
1.3
) can be re-written as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\tilde{a}+(\tilde{u}+u_{L})\cdot\nabla\tilde{a}=(\tilde{a}+1)\hbox{\rm div}\,(\tilde{u}+u_{L}),\\
&\partial_{t}\tilde{u}+(\tilde{u}+u_{L})\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}+\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla u_{L}-(1+\tilde{a})\mathcal{A}\tilde{u}\\
&\quad~=\tilde{a}\mathcal{A}u_{L}-u_{L}\cdot\nabla u_{L}+\nabla\Pi(\tilde{a})+e^{b}(\tilde{a}+1)(w-\tilde{u}-u_{L}),\\
&\partial_{t}b+w\cdot\nabla b+\hbox{\rm div}\,w=0,\\
&\partial_{t}w+w\cdot\nabla w+\nabla b+w=(\tilde{u}+u_{L}),\\
&(\tilde{a},\tilde{u},b,w)(x,0)=(\frac{1}{1+a_{0}}-1,0,b_{0},w_{0})(x).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

Let
L^{2}_{n}
be the set of
L^{2}
functions spectrally supported in the annulus
\mathcal{C}_{n}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}~|~\frac{1}{n}\leq|\xi|\leq n\}
endowed with the standard
L^{2}
topology, and
\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}
be the Friedrichs projectors defined by

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}\mathcal{F}f),\quad\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}
is the characteristic function on the annulus
\mathcal{C}_{n}
. Then the approximate sequence
(\widetilde{a}^{n},\widetilde{u}^{n},b^{n},w^{n})
is defined by solving the following equations:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\tilde{a}^{n}+\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}\big((\tilde{u}^{n}+u_{L}^{n})\cdot\nabla\tilde{a}^{n}\big)=-\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}\big((1+\tilde{a}^{n})\hbox{\rm div}\,(\tilde{u}^{n}+u_{L}^{n})\big),\\
&\partial_{t}\tilde{u}^{n}+\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}\big((\tilde{u}^{n}+u_{L}^{n})\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}^{n}+\tilde{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla u_{L}^{n}-(1+\tilde{a}^{n})\mathcal{A}\tilde{u}^{n}\big)\\
&\quad=\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}\big(-\tilde{a}^{n}\mathcal{A}u_{L}^{n}-u_{L}^{n}\cdot\nabla u_{L}^{n}+\nabla\Pi(\tilde{a}^{n})+e^{b^{n}}(1+\tilde{a}^{n})(w^{n}-\tilde{u}^{n}-u_{L}^{n})\big),\\
&\partial_{t}b^{n}+\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}\big(w^{n}\cdot\nabla b^{n}\big)+\hbox{\rm div}\,w^{n}=0,\\
&\partial_{t}w^{n}+\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}\big(w^{n}\cdot\nabla w^{n}\big)+\nabla b^{n}+w^{n}=(\tilde{u}^{n}+u_{L}^{n}),\\
&(\tilde{a}^{n},\tilde{u}^{n},b^{n},w^{n})(x,0)=(\tilde{a}_{0}^{n},\tilde{u}_{0}^{n},b_{0}^{n},w_{0}^{n})(x):=(\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}(\frac{1}{1+a_{0}}-1),0,\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}b_{0},\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}w_{0})(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

with
u_{L}^{n}:=\dot{\mathbb{E}}_{n}u_{L}
. One can show under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
that
(\tilde{a}_{0}^{n},b^{n}_{0},w^{n}_{0})
converges to
(\frac{1}{1+a_{0}}-1,b_{0},w_{0})
strongly in
\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}\times(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})\times(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and thus one can find a sufficiently large constant
n_{0}>0
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\quad\frac{3}{4\rho_{0+}}\leq 1+\tilde{a}^{n}_{0}\leq\frac{4}{3\rho_{0-}},\\
&\sum_{j\geq m-1}2^{\frac{d}{2}j}\|\tilde{a}^{n}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{3}{2}\sum_{j\geq m}2^{\frac{d}{2}j}\|\frac{1}{1+a^{0}}-1\|_{L^{2}},\quad n\geq n_{0},\quad m\in\mathbb{Z},\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle+C\big(\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u,w\cdot\nabla w)\|_{L^{1}_{t}(L^{2})}^{2}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}G\|_{L^{1}_{t}(L^{2})}^{2}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u-w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau,\quad j\leq 0.

Multiplying (

) by

and thence summing it over

, we have by (

)-(

) and (

\displaystyle\|u-w\|_{\widetilde{L}^{2}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(u_{0},w_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}}^{\ell}+\|(u,w)\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})}^{\ell}+\|(a,u,b)\|_{\widetilde{L}^{2}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1})}^{\ell}

(3.28)

\displaystyle+\|(u\cdot\nabla u,w\cdot\nabla w)\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})}^{\ell}+\|G\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(a_{0},u_{0},b_{0},w_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}}^{\ell}+\mathcal{X}^{2}(t).

Note that the inequality (

) also implies for

that

\displaystyle\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u-w)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u-w)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u_{0},w_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(2^{j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(a,u,b)\|_{L^{2}}+2^{-j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u,w\cdot\nabla w)\|_{L^{2}}+2^{-j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}G\|_{L^{2}}\big)d\tau.

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\|u-w\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1})}^{\ell}

(3.29)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(u_{0},w_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}}^{\ell}+\|(a,u,b)\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}_{2,1})}^{\ell}+\|(u\cdot\nabla u,w\cdot\nabla w)\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})}^{\ell}+\|G\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(a_{0},u_{0},b_{0},w_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}}^{\ell}+\mathcal{X}^{2}(t).

) and (

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(a,u,b,w)
be any strong solution to the Cauchy problem
(\ref{m1n})
. Then, it holds for any

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{2,j}(t)+\mathcal{D}_{2,j}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(2^{j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(au)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u,w\cdot\nabla b,w\cdot\nabla w)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}G\|_{L^{2}}+\|\hbox{\rm div}\,u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\|_{L^{2}}

\mathcal{E}_{2,j}(t)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{E}_{2,j}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(a,u,b,w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\eta_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big(\dot{\Delta}_{j}u~|~\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\big)_{L^{2}}\Big)\\

&\quad\quad\quad~~+\eta_{2}\big(\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\hbox{\rm div}\,\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big(\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u-w)~|~\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\big)_{L^{2}}\Big)\\
&\quad\quad\quad~~+\eta_{2}2^{-2j}\Big(\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}b\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\hbox{\rm div}\,\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big(\dot{\Delta}_{j}(w-u)~|~\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}b\big)_{L^{2}}\Big),\end{aligned}\right.

(3.31)

with
\eta_{2}\in(0,1)

Proof.

One can show from

-
(\ref{delta12})_{2}
that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big(\dot{\Delta}_{j}u~|~\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\big)_{L^{2}}\Big)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{k}\hbox{\rm div}\,\dot{\Delta}_{j}(au)=-[u\cdot\nabla,\partial_{k}\dot{\Delta}_{j}]a+u\cdot\nabla\partial_{k}\dot{\Delta}_{j}a+\partial_{k}\dot{\Delta}_{j}(a\hbox{\rm div}\,u),\quad k=1,...,d,\\
&\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u)=-[u\cdot\nabla,\dot{\Delta}_{j}]u+u\cdot\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}u,\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\big|\big(\nabla\hbox{\rm div}\,\dot{\Delta}_{j}(au)~|~\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\big)_{L^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle=\big|-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\big([u\cdot\nabla,\partial_{k}\dot{\Delta}_{j}]a~|~\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\big)_{L^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\big(\hbox{\rm div}\,u\nabla{\Delta}_{j}a~|~\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}(a\hbox{\rm div}\,u)~|~\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\big)_{L^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle\big|-\big(\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u)~|~\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(\nabla\hbox{\rm div}\,\dot{\Delta}_{j}(au)~|~\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\big)_{L^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\big(u\cdot\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}u~|~\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(u\cdot\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla a~|~\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}(a\hbox{\rm div}\,u)~|~\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}G\|_{L^{2}}+\|\hbox{\rm div}\,u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}a\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}(a\hbox{\rm div}\,u)\|_{L^{2}}+\|[u\cdot\nabla,\dot{\Delta}_{j}]u\|_{L^{2}}

Finally, we need the optimal regularity estimates for the Lamé system with constant coefficients.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
T>0
,
\mu>0
,
2\mu+\lambda>0
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
and
1\leq\varrho_{2}\leq\varrho_{1}\leq\infty
. Assume that
u_{0}\in\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}
and
f\in\widetilde{L}^{\rho_{2}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{s-2+\frac{2}{\varrho_{2}}}_{p,r})
hold. If
u
is a solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u-\mu\Delta u-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\hbox{\rm div}\,u=f,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t\in(0,T),\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),~\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

then
u
satisfies

\displaystyle\min\{\mu,2\mu+\lambda\}^{\frac{1}{\varrho_{1}}}\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\varrho_{1}}_{T}(\dot{B}^{s+\frac{2}{\varrho_{1}}}_{p,r})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}+\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\rho_{2}}_{T}(\dot{B}^{s-2+\frac{2}{\varrho_{2}}}_{p,r})}.

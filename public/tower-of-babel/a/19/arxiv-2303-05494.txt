A Formal Equivalence of Deformation Quantization and Geometric Quantization (of Higher Groupoids) and Non-Perturbative Sigma Models
Joshua Lackman

Abstract
Based on work done by Bonechi, Cattaneo, Felder and Zabzine on Poisson sigma models, we formally show that Kontsevich’s star product can be obtained from the twisted convolution algebra of the geometric quantization of a Lie 2-groupoid, one which integrates the Poisson structure. We show that there is an analogue of the Poisson sigma model which is valued in Lie 1-groupoids and which can often be defined non-perturbatively; it can be obtained by symplectic reduction using the quantization of the Lie 2-groupoid. We call these groupoid-valued sigma models and we argue that, when they exist, they can be used to compute correlation functions of gauge invariant observables. This leads to a (possibly non-associative) product on the underlying space of functions on the Poisson manifold, and in several examples we show that we recover strict deformation quantizations, in the sense of Rieffel. Even in the cases when our construction leads to a non-associative product we still obtain a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra and a “quantization” map. In particular, we construct noncommutative C ∗ C^{*} -algebras equipped with an S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) -action, together with an equivariant “quantization” map from C ∞ ​ ( S 2 ) . C^{\infty}(S^{2})\,. No polarizations are used in the construction of these algebras.

0.1
Deformation Quantization and the Poisson Sigma Model

In his seminal paper
[
30
]
, Kontsevich showed that every Poisson manifold
(M,\Pi)
admits a (formal) deformation quantization, ie. there is an associative product (called a star product)
\star
on
C^{\infty}(M)[[\hbar]]

1

1

The “star” in star product refers to the fact that the associative product is induced by a sequence of bidifferential operators, see
[
30
]
.
which, formally, deforms the product on

in such a way that

In
[
11

, Cattaneo and Felder provide a Poisson sigma model approach to defining the star product. The Poisson sigma model is, in particular, a topological bosonic open string theory, and the authors show that the star product can be obtained by computing a 3-point correlation function of boundary fields.

More precisely, the target space of the Poisson sigma model is a Poisson manifold
(M,\Pi)
and there are two bosonic fields, given by a vector bundle morphism

(X,\eta):TD\to T^{*}M

covering
X:D\to M\,,
where

is a disk with three cyclically ordered marked points on the boundary, denoted

The first fields are given by

and the second fields are given by the
X^{*}(T^{*}M)
-valued one forms

subject to the boundary condition that the pullback of
\eta
to
\partial D
takes values in the zero section. The star product is then given by

(f\star g)(m)=\int_{X(\infty)=m}f(X(1))g(X(0))\,e^{\frac{i}{h}S[X,\eta]}\,dX\,d\eta\,.

(0.1.1)

Here
m\in M\,,f\,,g\in C^{\infty}(M)\,,
the integral is over all fields
X\,,\eta

X(\infty)=m\,,
and the action is given by

S[X,\eta]=\int_{D}\eta\wedge dX+\frac{1}{2}\Pi|_{X}(\eta\,,\eta)\,.

(0.1.2)

The aforementioned authors show that the semiclassial expansion of this product about the classical solution
(X,\eta)=(m,0_{m})
is equivalent to Kontsevich’s star product.

An Equivalent Path Integral Over Lie Algebroid Morphisms

Later, in
[
6

The Main Idea

In this paper we discuss a possible quantization scheme for Poisson manifolds, one which doesn’t use polarizations in an essential way. We do this by taking a path integral perspective. Here we will summarize the idea:

1.

Let
(M,\Pi)
be a Poisson manifold. Consider the simplicial space

which in degree

V^{n}:=\text{Hom}_{\text{VB}}(\,T|\Delta^{n}|,\,T^{*}M),

We have that

2.

Associated with

]

3.

The space of Lie algebroid paths sits inside a simplicial space, denoted

\Pi_{\infty}^{(n)}(T^{*}M)=\{f\in\text{Hom}(\,T|\Delta^{n}|,\,T^{*}M\,):f|_{\text{vertices}}=0\}\,,

See

4.

The reduced phase space of

is the space of arrows of the source simply connected symplectic groupoid integrating

denoted
\Pi_{1}(T^{*}M).
In other words, we can think of

See

5.

Now since

6.

After doing this we can form the twisted convolution algebra of

7.

There is a canonical “quantization” map

\displaystyle f\mapsto\hat{f}\,,\;\hat{f}(\gamma)=f(\pi(\gamma(1/2)))\,,

Now there is a Lie 2-groupoid present, it is given by a quotient of

Using

We think of

Gauge Equivalences

Next, the authors of
[
6

define gauge equivalences in such a way that reduced phase space of the Poisson sigma model is the symplectic groupoid (if it exists). Furthermore, two algebroid morphims
X,\,X^{\prime}:TD\to T^{*}M
are gauge equivalent if they agree on the boundary. Using this notion of gauge equivalence, in order to make sense of

\int_{S^{2}}X^{*}\Pi\in 2\pi\hbar\mathbb{Z}\,,

(0.4.1)

for any algebroid morphism

The authors go on to speculate that in this case a non-perturbative definition of

should exist, and if
\Pi_{1}(T^{*}M)\rightrightarrows M
is the symplectic groupoid, then the path integral should be defined over the space of maps

[0,1]\to\Pi_{1}^{(1)}(T^{*}M)\,,

where
\Pi_{1}^{(1)}(T^{*}M)
is the space of arrows of
\Pi_{1}(T^{*}M)\rightrightarrows M\,.

What we do in this paper is different than what is speculated in the previous paragraph — we are essentially going to be doing a symplectic reduction of the Poisson sigma model. Rather than two morphisms
X,\,X^{\prime}:TD\to T^{*}M
being gauge equivalent if they agree on the boundary, we define them to be gauge equivalent if they agree on the boundary, up to Lie algebroid homotopy. For this to work, we require a slightly stronger condition than

X:TS^{2}\to T^{*}M\,,

\int_{S^{2}}X^{*}\Pi=0

(0.4.2)

as composable pairs of arrows (ie. a 2-simplex) in a higher integration of
(M,\Pi)\,.
We use this to compute correlation functions in the Poisson sigma model with respect to gauge invariant observables, non-perturbatively.

\mathbf{C^{*}}

Suppose we have a reasonable, non-perturbative definition of

Geometric Quantization of Poisson Manifolds Using Lie Groupoids

Twisted Convolution Algebra

To explain a bit of the preceding paragraphs, let us first recall twisted convolution algebras on Lie groupoids (when we say Lie groupoids we will always mean Lie 1-groupoids).

Let
G\rightrightarrows M
be a Lie groupoid. We let
G^{(0)}=M
and we let
G^{(n)}
be the space of composable
n
-tuples of arrows; together these form a simplicial set. There is a differential
\delta^{*}:C^{\infty}(G^{(n)})\to C^{\infty}(G^{(n+1)})
given by

\delta^{*}f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}d_{i}^{*}f\,,

(1.0.1)

where
d_{i}:G^{(n+1)}\to G^{(n)},\,i=0,\ldots,n,
are the corresponding face maps.

Now let
S\in C^{\infty}(G^{(2)})
be a cocycle, ie.
\delta^{*}S=0\,,
and let
d\mu
be a Haar measure on
G\,.
For
f,g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G^{(1)})\,,
their twisted convolution is denoted
f\ast g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G^{(1)})\,,
and is defined by

(f\ast g)(\gamma)=\int_{t(\gamma^{\prime})=t(\gamma)}f(\gamma^{\prime})g(\gamma^{\prime-1}\gamma)e^{S[\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime-1}\gamma]}\,d\mu(\gamma^{\prime})\,.

(1.0.2)

Let’s observe that

\{\gamma^{\prime}\in G^{(1)}:t(\gamma^{\prime})=t(\gamma)\}\cong\{(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime})\in G^{(2)}:\gamma^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime\prime}=\gamma\}\,.

(1.0.3)

Therefore, using the face maps
d_{0}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime})=\gamma^{\prime\prime}\,,d_{1}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime})=\gamma^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime\prime}\,,d_{2}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime})=\gamma^{\prime}\,,
we can rewrite
1.0.2
in the following way:

\displaystyle(f\ast g)(\gamma)=\int_{\begin{subarray}{l}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime})\in G^{(2)}\\

The advantage of rewriting the twisted convolution this way is that the above formula makes sense on higher groupoids.

Let us
emphasize

and for a composable pair
(g_{2},g_{1})\,,
the multiplication is given by

(g_{2},\lambda)\cdot(g_{1},\beta)=(g_{2}g_{1},\,\lambda\beta\,e^{S[g_{2},g_{1}]})\,.

(1.0.5)

The trivial multiplicative line bundle corresponds to the zero cocycle, and its twisted convolution algebra just gives the usual convolution algebra. Note that, our construction only gives multiplicative line bundles whose underlying line bundle itself is trivial — it is the multiplication that is nontrivial. However, in general the underlying line bundles need not be trivial, but we won’t be needing them; see
11
for more.

are the respective modular functions. In this case,

G/H\,.
This is always the case if

Relationship Between Groupoid and Poisson Sigma Models

Up until now we haven’t much discussed the sense in which the groupoid-valued sigma model computes correlation functions of gauge invariant observables. In
[
11
]
the authors consider two morphisms
X_{1},X_{2}:T|\Delta^{2}|\to T^{*}M
to be gauge equivalent if
\partial X_{1}=\partial X_{2}:T\partial|\Delta^{2}|\to M\,,
ie. two morphisms are equivalent if they agree on the boundary. For a thorough discussion of some gauge groups relevant to the Poisson sigma model, see
[
5
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
11
]
. Here we will sketch the idea of our gauge equivalences. Basically, the relationship goes: under symplectic reduction,

\displaystyle\text{Geometric quantization of }\Pi_{2}(T^{*}M)\leadsto\text{Geometric quantization of }\Pi_{1}(T^{*}M)\,,

(8.0.1)

\displaystyle f\star g\text{ with respect to }\Pi_{2}(T^{*}M)\leadsto f\star g\text{ with respect to }\Pi_{1}(T^{*}M)\,,

(8.0.2)

Recall that the reduced phase space of
\Pi_{\infty}^{(1)}(T^{*}M)
is
\Pi_{1}^{(1)}(T^{*}M)
(see
[
12
]
), so we are essentially performing reduction on the quantization we constructed on
\Pi_{2}(T^{*}M).
See section
0.3
and remark
2
.

We want to work with a slightly coarser gauge equivalence. Rather than considering two morphisms
X_{1},X_{2}:T|\Delta^{2}|\to T^{*}M
to be equivalent only if they agree on the boundary, we consider them to be equivalent if the three boundary components are Lie algebroid homotopy equivalent, with fixed endpoints. That is
d_{i}X_{1}\sim d_{i}X_{2}
as maps
Td_{i}|\Delta^{2}|\to T^{*}M,\,i=0,1,2,
where the endpoints of
d_{i}|\Delta^{2}|\,,i=0,1,2
are fixed throughout the homotopy. Equivalently, we consider
X_{1},X_{2}:T|\Delta^{2}|\to T^{*}M
to be equivalent if their images in
\Pi_{1}^{(2)}(T^{*}M)
under the map
\Pi_{\infty}(T^{*}M)\to\Pi_{1}(T^{*}M)
are equal.

Now let’s consider the multiplicative line bundle on
\Pi_{\infty}(T^{*}M)
induced by the cocycle
e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}S[\tilde{X}]}\,.
As shown in
[
11
]
, under the assumption that

\int_{S^{2}}X^{*}\Pi=2\pi\hbar\mathbb{Z},

(8.0.3)

for all morphisms
X:TS^{2}\to T^{*}M\,,
this multiplicative line bundle desdends to
\Pi_{1}(T^{*}M)\,.
This can be seen by the following construction (see
[
11
]
for a similar construction): consider the space
(\Pi_{\infty}^{(1)}(T^{*}M)\times\mathbb{C})/\sim,
where

(\lambda,\gamma)\sim(\lambda e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}S[X]},\gamma^{\prime})

(8.0.4)

if
X:TD\to T^{*}M
is a morphism from the disk with two marks points, and with boundaries
\gamma,\,\gamma^{\prime},
as in the the following picture:

\gamma\;
\;\gamma^{\prime}\;
\mathbf{X}

Now by the assumption
8.0.3
, no two points in the vector space over the same
\gamma
get identified, and this is why it descends to a line bundle. We will denote points in the equivalence classes by
[(\lambda,\gamma)]\,.
The projection is given by

[(\lambda,\gamma)]\mapsto[\gamma]\,.

(8.0.5)

The multiplication is defined by

[(\lambda,\gamma_{2})]\cdot[(\beta,\gamma_{1})]=[(\lambda\beta e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}S[X]},\gamma_{3})],

9.2

For the Poisson structures associated to exponential Lie groups (ie. Lie groups for which
\text{exp}:\mathfrak{g}\to G
is a diffeomorphism) and semisimple Lie groups, there is canonical degree 2-cocycle on the symplectic groupoid
G\ltimes\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with the desired properties, which we will now describe. First note that, there is a canonical Lie algebroid one form on
T^{*}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}\,,
given by

\alpha(f,v)=f(v)\,.

(9.2.1)

The significance of this one form is that
d_{CE}\alpha
is the Poisson structure, where
d_{CE}
is the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential.

Example 11
.

Let
G
be an exponential Lie group. Then there is a canonical function on

given by

\tilde{\alpha}(g,f)=f(\log{g})\,.

(9.2.2)

Now we have that
VE(\tilde{\alpha})=\alpha\,,
and since

is a cochain map, it follows that

is the Poisson structure. It follows that the antisymmetrization of
\delta^{*}\tilde{\alpha}
has the desired properties and we can take this to be the cocycle.

A particularly nice class of examples of exponential groups are nilpotent Lie groups. These are nice because the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula has only finitely many terms and we can identify the exponential map with the identity map on the Lie algebra, in which case the Haar measure is just the Lebesgue measure on the underlying vector space.

Final Remarks

To recap, based on work done by Bonechi, Cattaneo, Felder and Zabzine on Poisson sigma models, we formally showed that Kontsevich’s star product can be obtained from the geometric quantization of a Lie 2-groupoid. We then interpreted the Poisson sigma model as a sigma model valued in higher groupoids and argued that, if one can perform symplectic reduction (including dealing with infinities) on the quantization, then one can use a (non-perturbative) groupoid-valued sigma model to compute correlation functions of gauge invariant observables.

Now let’s discuss some generalizations of the ideas in this paper. First of all, it isn’t necessary to use the source simply connected integration in the groupoid-valued sigma model, we only used it because if the 2-cocycle exists on any 1-groupoid then it will exist on the source simply connected one.

Abstract
. 1.
introduction

Among the representation theory of Lie algebras, Whittaker modules are
interesting non-weight modules which play an important role in the classification of
irreducible modules for several Lie algebras.
Whittaker modules were first introduced by Arnal and Pinzcon for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, see
[
AP
]
. The classification of the irreducible modules for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
in
[
B
]
illustrates the importance of Whittaker modules.
It was shown that irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-modules can be divided into three families: weight modules, Whittaker modules, and modules obtained from irreducible elements in a noncommutative domian. Kostant studied Whittaker modules for any complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
in
[
K
]
. Whittaker modules defined by Kostant are closely associated with the triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{n}_{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{+}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Every Whittaker module depends on a Lie
algebra homomorphism
\psi:\mathfrak{n}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.
The map
\psi
is called non-singular if
\psi(x_{\alpha})\neq 0
for any simple root vector
x_{\alpha}
. Kostant gave a classification of all simple non-singular Whittaker modules. Some results on complex semisimple Lie algebras have been generalized to other algebras with triangular decompositions. For example, for Whittaker modules over algebras related to the Virasoro algebra, one can see
[
OW1
,
OW2
,
GLZ
,
LPX
,
LWZ
]
. Whittaker modules over quantum groups
U_{h}(\mathfrak{g})
,
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
and
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
were studied in
[
S
,
OM
,
XGL
]
, respectively. Whittaker modules have also been studied for generalized Weyl
algebras by Benkart and Ondrus, see
[
BO
]
. Whittaker modules for non-twisted affine
Lie algebras and several similar algebras were studied in
[
ALZ
,
C
,
CF
,
CJ
,
GZ
]
. In
[
BM
]
, Batra and Mazorchuk have constructed a more
general framework to describe the Whittaker modules. They considered Whittaker pairs
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
of Lie algebras, where
\mathfrak{n}
is a quasi-nilpotent Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
such that the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{n}
on the quotient
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{n}
is locally nilpotent, and
studied the category
\mathcal{WH}
of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules such that
\mathfrak{n}
acts locally finitely. Under this general Whittaker set-up in
[
BM
]
, they also determined a block decomposition of the category
\mathcal{WH}
according to the action of
\mathfrak{n}
. The characterizations of each block for most Lie algebras are still open.

Differential operators are important tools for studying representations for Lie algebras. To construct explicit representations of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
by differential operators, it is actually to find algebra homomorphisms from
U(\mathfrak{g})
to the Weyl algebra
\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
be the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
spanned by root vectors
X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Then
(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},\mathfrak{m}_{n})
is a Whittaker pair. An
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
M
is called a Whittaker module if the action of each element of
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
on
M
is locally finite, see
[
BM
]
. Similar as Kostant’s definition, a Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{m}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is called non-singular if
\phi(X_{-2\epsilon_{i}})\neq 0
, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. We will characterize non-singular Whittaker modules in this paper using differential operators.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some basic definitions and important facts including the weighting functors and Nilsson’s modules for
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
. In Section 3, we characterize the Whittaker category
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}
when
\mathbf{a}\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
, where
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}
consists of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-modules
M
such that
X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}-a_{i}^{2}
acts locally nilpotently for any
i=1,\dots,n
, and
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)=\{v\in M\mid X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}v=a_{i}^{2}v,\ i=1,\dots,n\}
is finite dimensional. Let
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\mu}
be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}
consisting of all
U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
-modules
M
with the central character
\chi_{\mu}
given by the highest weight
\mu
. We show that when
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\mu}
is non-empty, there is an equivalence between
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\mu}
and the category
\mathcal{W}^{\mathbf{a}}
of finitely generated

\mathcal{D}_{n}^{ev}
-modules such that
\partial_{i}^{2}-a_{i}^{2}
acts locally nilpotently for any
i
, where
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{ev}
is the subalgebra of the Weyl algebra
\mathcal{D}_{n}
generated by differential operators of even degree. In Section 4, we give a differential operators realization of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
from any
f\in A_{n}
, see Lemma
12
. Thus we have constructed many simple modules
P_{n}^{f}
over
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
. Furthermore, we show that these operators realizations exhaust all algebra homomorphisms from
U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
to
\mathcal{D}_{n}
which map each root vector
X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}
to
t_{i}t_{j}
.

In this paper, we denote by
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}^{*}
the sets of integers, positive integers, nonnegative
integers, complex numbers, and nonzero complex numbers, respectively. All vector spaces and algebras are over
\mathbb{C}
. For a Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
we denote by
U(\mathfrak{g})
its universal enveloping algebra,
Z(\mathfrak{g})
the center of
U(\mathfrak{g})
. We write
\otimes
for
\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some preliminary definitions and related results that will be used throughout the paper.
In particular, we introduce the notion of Whittaker modules in the sense of
[
BM
]
that are of main interest in this paper.

2.1.
The symplectic algebra
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}

Throughout the whole text, we fix an integer
n
bigger than
1
.
Recall that
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
is the Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{2n}
consisting of all
2n\times 2n
-matrices
X
satisfying
SX=-X^{T}S
where

S=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\\
\end{array}\right).

So
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
consists of all
2n\times 2n
-matrices of the following form

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&-A^{T}\\
\end{array}\right)

such that
B=B^{T}
,
C=C^{T}
, where
A,B,C\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. Let
e_{ij}
denote the matrix unit whose
(i,j)
-entry is
1
and
0
elsewhere.
Then

\mathfrak{h}_{n}=\mathrm{span}\{h_{i}:=e_{i,i}-e_{n+i,n+i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}

is a Cartan subalgebra (a maximal
abelian subalgebra whose adjoint action on
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
is diagonalizable)
of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.
Let
\Lambda^{+}
be the set of dominant integral weight,
\{\epsilon_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}\subset\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}
be such that
\epsilon_{i}(h_{k})=\delta_{i,k}
.
The root system of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
is precisely

\Delta=\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}|1\leq i,j\leq n\}\setminus\{0\}.

The positive root system is

\Delta_{+}=\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j},\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{l}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n,1\leq k,l\leq n\}.

We list root vectors in
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{r c l | c}\lx@intercol\hfil\mathrm{{Root\ vector}}\hfil\lx@intercol&\mathrm{{Root}}\\
\hline\cr X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}&:=&e_{i,n+j}+e_{j,n+i}&\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}\\
X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}&:=&e_{n+i,j}+e_{n+j,i}&-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\\
X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}&:=&e_{i,j}-e_{n+j,n+i}&\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j},\\
\end{array}

where
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, with
i\neq j
when we encounter
\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}
.

Then we can obtain a basis of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
as follows:

B:=\{X_{\alpha}|\alpha\in\Delta\}\cup\{h_{i}|1\leq i\leq n\}.

Set

\mathfrak{n}_{\pm}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\pm}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

Then the decomposition

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}=\mathfrak{n}_{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{n}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{+}

is a triangular decomposition of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
, and the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}:=\mathfrak{h}_{n}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{+}
is a Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.

For the convenience of later calculations,
we list some nontrivial Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
as follows:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\ [X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{l}}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{l}}-\delta_{li}X_{\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}},

\displaystyle[X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}},X_{-\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{l}}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{l}}+\delta_{il}X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}}+\delta_{ik}X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{l}}+\delta_{jl}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{k}},

\displaystyle[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{l}}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{l}}+\delta_{jl}X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{k}},

\displaystyle[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{-\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{l}}]

\displaystyle=-\delta_{il}X_{-\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}}-\delta_{ki}X_{-\epsilon_{l}-\epsilon_{j}}.

In particular,
[X_{2\epsilon_{i}},X_{-2\epsilon_{k}}]=4[e_{i,n+i},e_{n+k,k}]=\delta_{ik}4h_{i}
, where
i,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

2.2.
Weight modules

An
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
V
is called a weight module if
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
acts diagonally on
V
, i.e.,

V=\oplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}}V_{\lambda},

where
V_{\lambda}=\{v\in V\mid hv=\lambda(h)v,\forall\ h\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}\}
.
For a weight module
V
, denote

\mathrm{supp}(V)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}|V_{\lambda}\neq 0\}.

For a weight module
M
, a nonzero vector
v\in M_{\lambda}
is called a highest weight vector if
\mathfrak{n}_{+}v=0
. A module is called a highest weight module if it is generated by a highest weight
vector.
A weight module
M
is called a uniformly bounded module, if there is a

k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\dim M_{\lambda}\leq k
for any weight
\lambda\in\mathrm{supp}(M)
.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be the category consisting of uniformly
bounded weight modules.

2.3.
Whittaker modules

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{n}=\oplus_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathbb{C}X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}
which is a commutative subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.
Since the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
on the quotient
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}/\mathfrak{m}_{n}
is nilpotent,
(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},\mathfrak{m}_{n})
is a Whittaker pair in the sense of
[
BM
]
. An
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
M
is called a Whittaker module if the action of
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
on
M
is locally finite. For an
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in(\mathbb{C})^{n}
, we can define a
Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}:\mathfrak{m}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}(X_{-2\epsilon_{i}})=a_{i}^{2}
for any
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. A Whittaker module
M
is of type
\mathbf{a}
if for any
v\in M
there is a
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(x-\phi_{\mathbf{a}}(x))^{k}v=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}
.
We also define the subspace

\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)=\{v\in M\mid xv=\phi_{\mathbf{a}}(x)v,\ \forall\ x\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}\}

of
M
.
An element in
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is called a Whittaker vector.

Such Whittaker modules are more complicated than the classical Whittaker modules
defined by Kostant. For example,
\dim\text{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is not necessarily
1
for a simple Whittaker module
M
. We consider Whittaker modules under some natural finite condition. Let
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}
be the category of Whittaker
U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
-modules
M
of type
\mathbf{a}
such that
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is finite dimensional.

Remark 1
.

The condition that
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is finite dimensional
amounts to the condition that weight spaces are finite dimensional for a weight module, see the proof of Lemma
4
.

2.4.
Central characters

Let
\mathfrak{X}=\text{Hom}(Z(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}),\mathbb{C})
be the set of central characters of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
. We have a map
\chi:\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}
which maps
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}
to the central character of the Verma module
M(\mu)
.
By the Harish-Chandra’s Theorem, the map
\chi
is surjective. For each

{\mu}\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}
, denote by
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\mu}
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}
of all
U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
-modules
M
such that for any
v\in M
there is a
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(z-\chi_{\mu}(z))^{k}v=0
for all
z\in Z(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
.
Similarly, we have the full subcategory
\mathcal{B}^{\mu}
of
\mathcal{B}
for any
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}
. Moreover we have the block decompositions:

\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}=\oplus_{\mu\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}}\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\mu},\ \ \mathcal{B}=\oplus_{\mu\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}}\mathcal{B}^{\mu}.

2.5.
Weighting functor

We recall the weighting functor introduced in
[
N2
]
.
For a point
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
I_{\gamma}
be the maximal ideal of
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})=\mathbb{C}[h_{1},\dots,h_{n}]
generated by

h_{1}-\gamma_{1},\dots,h_{n}-\gamma_{n}.

For an
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
M
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, set
M^{\gamma}:=M/I_{\gamma}M
.
Let

\mathfrak{W}(M):=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}M^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}},

where
\omega_{n}=(1,\dots,1)
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}
, we identify
\lambda

with the vector
(\lambda(h_{1}),\cdots,\lambda(h_{n}))
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Nilsson defined a weight
module structure on
\mathfrak{W}(M)
, see Proposition 8 in
[
N2
]
.

Proposition 2
.

The vector space
\mathfrak{W}(M)
becomes a weight
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module under the following action:

(2.2)

X_{\alpha}\cdot(v+I_{\gamma}M):=X_{\alpha}v+I_{\gamma+\alpha}M,v\in M,\alpha\in\Delta.

We see that
h_{i}\cdot(v+I_{\gamma}M)=\gamma_{i}(v+I_{\gamma}M)
for any
i
. So
\mathfrak{W}(M)
is a weight module. In many cases, the
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
\mathfrak{W}(M)
is
0
. For example, if
M
is a simple weight module with a weight not in
-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}+\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, one can easily see that
\mathfrak{W}(M)=0
. We also note that
\mathfrak{W}(M)=M
if

M
is a simple weight
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module with a weight in
-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}+\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
If
M
is a
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-torsion free module of finite rank when restricted to
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
, then
\mathfrak{W}(M)
is
a uniformly bounded weight module with
\text{supp}(\mathfrak{W}(M))=-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}+\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

2.6.
Nilsson’s modules

Since
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
is commutative,
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})=\mathbb{C}[h_{1},\cdots,h_{n}]
as an associative algebra. In
[
N2
]
, Nilsson constructed an
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module structure on
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
as follows:

\displaystyle h_{i}\cdot g

\displaystyle=h_{i}g,

\displaystyle X_{2\epsilon_{i}}\cdot g

\displaystyle=(h_{i}-\frac{1}{2})(h_{i}-\frac{3}{2})\sigma_{i}^{2}(g),

\displaystyle X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}\cdot g

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{i}^{-2}(g),

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\cdot g

\displaystyle=(h_{i}-\frac{1}{2})(h_{j}-\frac{1}{2})\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}(g),i\neq j,

\displaystyle X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\cdot g

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{i}^{-1}\sigma_{j}^{-1}(g),i\neq j,

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\cdot g

\displaystyle=(h_{i}-\frac{1}{2})\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}^{-1}(g),i\neq j,

where
g\in U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
and
\sigma_{i}\in\text{Aut}(U(\mathfrak{h}_{n}))
such that
\sigma_{i}(h_{k})=h_{k}-\delta_{ik}
.
We denote by
N_{\mathbf{i}}
this
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module. It is easy to see that
N_{\mathbf{i}}
is a Whittaker module with respect to the pair
(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},\mathfrak{m}_{n})
of type
\mathbf{i}=(\mathrm{i},\dots,\mathrm{i})
, where
\mathrm{i}
is the imaginary number unit.
In
[
N2
]
, Nilsson showed that any
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module structure on
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
is equivalent to
N_{\mathbf{i}}
up to some automorphism of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.
We will show that any simple module in the block
\mathcal{WH}_{-\mathbf{i}}^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}}
is equivalent to
N_{\mathbf{i}}
.

3.
Non-singular Whittaker modules

In this section, we will characterize the category
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\mu}
when
\mathbf{a}\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
using the weighting functor. In this case, a module in
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\mu}
is non-singular. For convenience, set
t^{\mathbf{m}}=t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{m_{n}}
,

h^{\mathbf{m}}=h_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots h_{n}^{m_{n}}
, for any
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},...,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

3.1.
The category
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\mu}

We define the total order on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
satisfying the condition:
\mathbf{r}<\mathbf{m}
if
|\mathbf{r}|<|\mathbf{m}|
or

|\mathbf{r}|=|\mathbf{m}|
and
there is an
l\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
such that
r_{i}=m_{i}
when
1\leq i<l
and
r_{l}<m_{l}
, where
|\mathbf{m}|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}
. For each
\mathbf{m}
, the set
\{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}\mid\mathbf{r}<\mathbf{m}\}
is finite. Hence as an ordered set
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
For a nonzero
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, denote by
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}
the predecessor
of
\mathbf{m}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}
is the maximal element in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
such that
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}<\mathbf{m}
.

The following lemma gives a rough characterization of modules in
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\mu}
which is important for the later discussions.

Lemma 3
.

Any module
M
in

\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}
is a free
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-module with the basis
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
.

Proof.

First, we show that
M=U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
.

Denote
Y^{\mathbf{m}}=(X_{-2\epsilon_{1}}-a_{1}^{2})^{m_{1}}\cdots(X_{-2\epsilon_{n}}-a_{n}^{2})^{m_{n}}
, for any
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
.
Then the set
\{Y^{\mathbf{s}}\mid\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}\}
forms a basis of
U(\mathfrak{m}_{n})
.
Using the hypothesis that
a_{i}\neq 0
for any
i
and induction on
\mathbf{m}
, from
[h_{i},X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}]=-2X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}
, we can show that for any
\mathbf{m},\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
and nonzero
v\in\text{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
, we have that
Y^{\mathbf{s}}h^{\mathbf{m}}v=0
whenever
\mathbf{s}>\mathbf{m}
,

Y^{\mathbf{m}}h^{\mathbf{m}}v=k_{\mathbf{m}}v
for some nonzero scalar
k_{\mathbf{m}}
.

For each
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, let
\mathbb{I}_{\mathbf{m}}
be the ideal of
U(\mathfrak{m}_{n})
spanned by
Y^{\mathbf{s}}
with
\mathbf{s}>\mathbf{m}
, and
M_{\mathbf{m}}=\{w\in M\mid\mathbb{I}_{\mathbf{m}}w=0\}
. Clearly
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)=M_{\mathbf{0}}
.
For any nonzero
w\in M
, by the definition of
M
, there is an

\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
such that
w\in M_{\mathbf{m}}\setminus M_{\mathbf{m}^{\prime}}
, i.e.,

Y^{\mathbf{m}}w\neq 0
and
Y^{\mathbf{s}}w=0
for any
\mathbf{s}>\mathbf{m}
. So
Y^{\mathbf{m}}w\in\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
. We call
\mathbf{m}
the degree of
w
. By the above discussion,
Y^{\mathbf{m}}h^{\mathbf{m}}Y^{\mathbf{m}}w=k_{\mathbf{m}}Y^{\mathbf{m}}w
.
We use induction on the degree
\mathbf{m}
of

to show that
w\in U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
. Let
w^{\prime}=w-\frac{1}{k_{\mathbf{m}}}h^{\mathbf{m}}Y^{\mathbf{m}}w
. Then

Y^{\mathbf{m}}w^{\prime}=Y^{\mathbf{m}}w-\frac{1}{k_{\mathbf{m}}}Y^{\mathbf{m}}h^{\mathbf{m}}Y^{\mathbf{m}}w=0.

This implies that the degree of
w^{\prime}
is smaller than
\mathbf{m}
. By the induction
hypothesis,
w^{\prime}\in U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
. Consequently

.

Next we show that
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is a basis of
M

as a free
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-module. Suppose that
\{v_{i}|i=1,\dots,k\}
is a basis of the vector
space
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
. We need to show that
\{h^{\mathbf{m}}v_{i}\mid\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},i=1,\dots,k\}
is linearly independent.
Suppose that
w:=\sum_{\mathbf{r}\leq\mathbf{m}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{\mathbf{r},i}h^{\mathbf{r}}v_{i}=0
. Then from

Y^{\mathbf{m}}w=0
, we see that
c_{\mathbf{m},i}=0
. Consequently by induction on
\mathbf{m}
,

c_{\mathbf{r},i}=0
for any
\mathbf{r}<\mathbf{m}
and
i
. Thus
\{h^{\mathbf{m}}v_{i}\mid\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},i=1,\dots,k\}
is linearly independent. The proof is complete.

In order to study the category
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}}

.
Let

be the polynomial algebra in

Then the subalgebra of

over

is the unital associative algebra
over
\mathbb{C}
generated by

\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}

\mathcal{D}_{n}^{ev}

.
In the following lemma, we recall a differential operator realization of

[

Lemma 5
.

The map

(3.1)

\displaystyle\theta_{0}:\,\,U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{n}^{ev},

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle\mapsto t_{i}t_{j},

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle\mapsto t_{i}\partial_{j},\ \ i\neq j,

\displaystyle h_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto t_{i}\partial_{i}+\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle\mapsto-\partial_{i}\partial_{j},1\leq i,j\leq n,

defines a surjective algebra homomorphism.

Let
P_{n}
be the unital subalgebra of
A_{n}
generated by
t_{i}t_{j}
,
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. By Lemma
5
,

can be made to be an
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module called the Weil representation, see
[
M
]

is equivalent to the simple highest weight module

of the highest weight
-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}
up to an involution of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}

is the

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.

By Theorem 5.2 in
[
GS1

Lemma 6
.

If

is a module in
\mathcal{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}}

.

Using Lemma

and the weighting functor, we show that any module in

Lemma 7

If
M
is a module in
\mathcal{WH}_{\mathbf{a}}^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}}

\mathcal{D}_{n}^{ev}

By lemma

,
M
is a free

-module.
The module
\mathfrak{W}(M)

-module, i.e.

Next we will show that
\theta_{f}
in Lemma

exhausts all algebra homomorphisms
\theta
from

to
\mathcal{D}_{n}
such that
\theta(X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}})=t_{i}t_{j}

Firstly we give some formulas in

Lemma 13
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, these formulas hold as follows:

(1)

[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k}]=\delta_{jl}2kX_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-1}X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}},1\leq i\neq j\leq n

(2)

[X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k}]=-2kX_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-1}\big(\delta_{jl}X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}}+\delta_{il}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\big),1\leq i\neq j\leq n;

(3)

[X_{-2\epsilon_{i}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k}]=-\delta_{il}4kX_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-1}\big(h_{i}+k-1\big),1\leq i\leq n.

Proof.

(1) According to
[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}]=2\delta_{jl}X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}
and
[X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{l}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}]=0
, we can compute that

\displaystyle[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k}]=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-t}[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}]X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{t-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}2\delta_{jl}X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-1}X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}=\delta_{jl}2kX_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-1}X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}.

(2) From
[X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}]=-2\big(\delta_{jl}X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}}+\delta_{il}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\big)
and

[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{t-1}]=\delta_{jl}2(t-1)X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{t-2}X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}},

we have that

\displaystyle[X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-t}[X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}]X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{t-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}-2X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-t}\big(\delta_{jl}X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}}X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{t-1}+\delta_{il}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{t-1}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}-2X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-t}\big(\delta_{il}X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{t-1}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}+\delta_{jl}X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{t-1}X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}}

\displaystyle+2(t-1)X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{t-2}\delta_{il}\delta_{jl}(X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{l}}+X_{\epsilon_{j}+\epsilon_{l}})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2kX_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-1}\big(\delta_{il}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}+\delta_{jl}X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}}\big)-2k(k-1)\delta_{il}\delta_{jl}X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-2}(X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{l}}+X_{\epsilon_{j}+\epsilon_{l}}).

Thus, by
i\neq j
, we can see that

[X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k}]=-2kX_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-1}\big(\delta_{jl}X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}}+\delta_{il}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\big),\quad i\neq j.

(3) By the similar computation in (2), we can obtain that

\displaystyle\ [X_{-2\epsilon_{i}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k}]

\displaystyle=-2kX_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-1}2\delta_{il}h_{i}-2\delta_{il}k(k-1)X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-2}2X_{2\epsilon_{l}}

\displaystyle=-\delta_{il}4kX_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-1}\big(h_{i}+k-1\big).

The proof is complete.

(2) It is similar as (1)

(3) For

, we can calculate that

\displaystyle[X_{-2\epsilon_{i}},X^{\mathbf{m}}]

\displaystyle=[X_{-2\epsilon_{i}},\prod_{l=1}^{n}X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{m_{l}}]

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}X_{2\epsilon_{j}}^{m_{j}}[X_{-2\epsilon_{i}},X_{2\epsilon_{i}}^{m_{i}}]\prod_{s=i+1}^{n}X_{2\epsilon_{s}}^{m_{s}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}X_{2\epsilon_{j}}^{m_{j}}\Big(-4m_{i}X_{2\epsilon_{i}}^{m_{i}-1}\big(h_{i}+m_{i}-1\big)\Big)\prod_{s=i+1}^{n}X_{2\epsilon_{s}}^{m_{s}}

\displaystyle=-4m_{i}X^{\mathbf{m}-e_{i}}(h_{i}+m_{i}-1).

Hence, set
q_{ii}=X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}\cdot 1
, we can determine the action of
X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}
as follows:

\displaystyle X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}\cdot t^{2\mathbf{m}}

\displaystyle=X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}\cdot X^{\mathbf{m}}\cdot 1

\displaystyle=[X_{-2\epsilon_{i}},X^{\mathbf{m}}]\cdot 1+X^{\mathbf{m}}\cdot X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}\cdot 1

\displaystyle=-4m_{i}X^{\mathbf{m}-e_{i}}(h_{i}+m_{i}-1)\cdot 1+X^{\mathbf{m}}\cdot X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}\cdot 1

\displaystyle=(1-2p_{ii})t_{i}^{-1}\partial_{i}t^{2\mathbf{m}}-\partial_{i}^{2}t^{2\mathbf{m}}+q_{ii}t^{2\mathbf{m}}

\displaystyle=\big(q_{ii}+(1-2p_{ii})t_{i}^{-1}\partial_{i}-\partial_{i}^{2}\big)t^{2\mathbf{m}}.

(4) When
i\neq j
, let’s suppose
i<j
, and the case for
i>j
is similar. According to
[X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k}]=-2kX_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{k-1}\big(\delta_{jl}X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}}+\delta_{il}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\big)
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle[X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X^{\mathbf{m}}]=[X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},\prod_{l=1}^{n}X_{2\epsilon_{l}}^{m_{l}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2m_{i}X^{\mathbf{m}-e_{i}}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}-4m_{i}m_{j}X^{\mathbf{m}-e_{i}-e_{j}}X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}-2m_{j}X^{\mathbf{m}-e_{j}}X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}}.

Denote
q_{ij}=X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\cdot 1
. Then, we have

\displaystyle X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\cdot t^{2\mathbf{m}}

\displaystyle=X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\cdot X^{\mathbf{m}}\cdot 1

\displaystyle=[X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X^{\mathbf{m}}]\cdot 1+X^{\mathbf{m}}\cdot X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\cdot 1

\displaystyle=\big(q_{ij}-p_{ij}t_{i}^{-1}\partial_{i}-p_{ji}t_{j}^{-1}\partial_{j}-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\big)(t^{2\mathbf{m}}).

Consequently the proof is completed.

∎

The following easy lemma will be used in the proof of Theorem
16

Lemma 15
.

For any
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
and
p\in A_{n}
, the differential equation

(t_{i}\partial_{i}+1)(q)=p

has at most one solution
q
in
A_{n}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the fact that the map
t_{i}\partial_{i}+1:A_{n}\rightarrow A_{n}
is injective.

∎

Combining the above preparatory arguments, we can give all possible algebra homomorphisms from
U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
to the Weyl algebra
\mathcal{D}_{n}
which map each root vector
X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}
to
t_{i}t_{j}
.

Theorem 16
.

If
\theta
is an algebra homomorphism from
U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
to
\mathcal{D}_{n}
such that

\theta(X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}})=t_{i}t_{j},

for any
i,j
, then there is an
f\in A_{n}
such that
\theta=\theta_{f}
, that is

(1)

\theta(X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}})=t_{i}t_{j}
,

(2)

\theta(X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}})=t_{i}\partial_{j}(f)+t_{i}\partial_{j},i\neq j
,

(3)

\theta(h_{i})=t_{i}\partial_{i}(f)+t_{i}\partial_{i}+\frac{1}{2}
,

(4)

\theta(X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}})=-(\partial_{i}(f)+\partial_{i})(\partial_{j}(f)+\partial_{j})
,

where
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to determine
p_{ij},q_{ij}
in Lemma
14
. For any
f\in A_{n}
, we set
f_{(i)}=\partial_{i}(f)
.

Specifically, according to
[h_{i},h_{j}]=0
, which means
[p_{ii}+t_{i}\partial_{i},p_{jj}+t_{j}\partial_{j}]=0
, we can calculate

t_{i}\partial_{i}(p_{jj})=t_{j}\partial_{j}(p_{ii}),1\leq i,j\leq n.

Then we can obtain that
p_{ii}=t_{i}f_{(i)}+c_{i}
for some
f\in A_{n}
and
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

By
[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}}]=h_{i}-h_{j}
, we deduce that

p_{ij}t_{j}\partial_{i}+t_{i}\partial_{j}p_{ji}-p_{ji}t_{i}\partial_{j}-t_{j}\partial_{i}p_{ij}=p_{ii}-p_{jj}.

Consequently we have
c_{i}=c_{j}
for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Set
b=c_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq n
, then
p_{ii}=t_{i}f_{(i)}+b
.

For
i\neq j
,
[h_{i},X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}]=X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}
implies that
(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)(p_{ij})=t_{i}\partial_{j}(p_{ii})
. Solving this differential equation, we can check that
p_{ij}=t_{i}f_{(j)}
is the unique solution of it.

For
i\neq j
, from
[h_{i},X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}]=-X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}
, we get

(4.2)

t_{i}\partial_{i}(q_{ij})+q_{ij}=-p_{ij}t_{i}^{-1}\partial_{i}(p_{ii})-p_{ji}t_{j}^{-1}\partial_{j}(p_{ii})-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(p_{ii}).

We can calculate it more explicitly,

\displaystyle t_{i}\partial_{i}(q_{ij})+q_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-p_{ij}t_{i}^{-1}\partial_{i}(p_{ii})-p_{ji}t_{j}^{-1}\partial_{j}(p_{ii})-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(p_{ii})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big(t_{i}f_{(j)}t_{i}^{-1}\partial_{i}\big(t_{i}f_{(i)}+b\big)+t_{j}f_{(i)}t_{j}^{-1}\partial_{j}\big(t_{i}f_{(i)}+b\big)+\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\big(t_{i}f_{(i)}+b\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big(f_{(i)}f_{(j)}+t_{i}f_{(j)}\partial_{i}(f_{(i)})+f_{(i)}t_{i}\partial_{j}(f_{(i)})+\partial_{j}(f_{(i)})+t_{i}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(f_{(i)})\Big).

We can check that
q_{ij}=-f_{(i)}f_{(j)}-\partial_{j}(f_{(i)})
is the unique solution of the equation (
4.2
).

Similarly, for
i=j
, we can get
q_{ii}=-f_{(i)}^{2}-\partial_{i}(f_{(i)})-(2b-1)t_{i}^{-1}f_{(i)}
.

Furthermore, for
i\neq j
, by
[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}]=-2X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}
, we deduce that

t_{i}\partial_{j}(q_{ii})-(1-2p_{ii})t_{i}^{-1}\partial_{i}(p_{ij})+\partial_{i}^{2}(p_{ij})=-2q_{ij}.

So we have
(1-2b)t_{i}^{-1}f_{(j)}=0
, that is,
b=\frac{1}{2}
and
q_{ij}=-f_{(i)}f_{(j)}-\partial_{j}(f_{(i)})
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Therefore from Lemma

14
, we can complete the proof.

∎

4.2.
New
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-modules

Recall that
P_{n}
is the unital subalgebra of
A_{n}
generated by
t_{i}t_{j}
, for
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. For any
f\in P_{n}
,
via the homomorphism
\theta_{f}
in (
3.1
),
P_{n}
becomes an
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module

P_{n}^{f}
. In Theorem
16
, we actually have classified all
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module structures on
P_{n}
satisfying
X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\cdot g(t)=t_{i}t_{j}g(t)
for any
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\},g(t)\in P_{n}
.

Corollary 17
.

If there is an
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module structure on
P_{n}
such that

X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\cdot g(t)=t_{i}t_{j}g(t)
for any
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\},g(t)\in P_{n}
, then this

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module structure
is isomorphic to
P_{n}^{f}
for some
f\in P_{n}
.

The following proposition gives a description of
P_{n}^{f}
.

Proposition 18
.

Let
f,f^{\prime}\in P_{n}
.

(1)

The
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
P_{n}^{f}
is simple.

(2)

As
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-modules,
P_{n}^{f}\simeq P_{n}^{f^{\prime}}
if and only if
f-f^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

(1) One can check directly that
P_{n}
is a
simple
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{ev}
-module. Note that the image of
\theta_{f}
is

\mathcal{D}_{n}^{ev}
. So
P_{n}^{f}
is a simple
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module.

(2) The sufficiency is obvious, it is enough to consider the necessity. Let
\varphi:P_{n}^{f}\rightarrow P_{n}^{f^{\prime}}
be an
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module isomorphism. Following
\varphi(X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\cdot t^{0})=X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\cdot\varphi(t^{0})
, we know that
\varphi(t^{0})\neq 0
.
From
\theta_{f}(X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}})=t_{i}t_{j}
and
\varphi(X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\cdot t^{0})=X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\cdot\varphi(t^{0})
, we see that
\varphi(g(t))=g(t)\varphi(t^{0})
for any
g(t)\in P_{n}
. Then for any
i\neq j
, we have that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\varphi(X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\cdot t^{0})-X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\cdot\varphi(t^{0})

\displaystyle=\varphi(t_{i}f_{(j)})-(t_{i}f^{\prime}_{(j)}+t_{i}\partial_{j})\cdot\varphi(t^{0})

\displaystyle=-t_{i}\big(\partial_{j}+f^{\prime}_{(j)}-f_{(j)}\big)\varphi(t^{0}).

Consequently we see that
\partial_{j}+f^{\prime}_{(j)}-f_{(j)}
is not injective on
P_{n}
. So we have
f_{(j)}=f^{\prime}_{(j)}
and
\varphi(t^{0})\in\mathbb{C}^{*}t^{0}
for any
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. That is
f-f^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
, which completes the proof.

∎

Therefore we have constructed several simple modules
P_{n}^{f}
over
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
generalizing the Weil representation.

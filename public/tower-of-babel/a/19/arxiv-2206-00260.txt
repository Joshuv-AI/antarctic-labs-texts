Multi-block Min-max Bilevel Optimization with Applications in Multi-task Deep AUC Maximization
Quanqi Hu, Yongjian Zhong, Tianbao Yang

Abstract
In this paper, we study multi-block min-max bilevel optimization problems, where the upper level is non-convex strongly-concave minimax objective and the lower level is a strongly convex objective, and there are multiple blocks of dual variables and lower level problems. Due to the intertwined multi-block min-max bilevel structure, the computational cost at each iteration could be prohibitively high, especially with a large number of blocks. To tackle this challenge, we present two single-loop randomized stochastic algorithms, which require updates for only a constant number of blocks at each iteration. Under some mild assumptions on the problem, we establish their sample complexity of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ 4 ) \mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{4}) for finding an ϵ \epsilon -stationary point. This matches the optimal complexity for solving stochastic nonconvex optimization under a general unbiased stochastic oracle model. Moreover, we provide two applications of the proposed method in multi-task deep AUC (area under ROC curve) maximization and multi-task deep partial AUC maximization. Experimental results validate our theory and demonstrate the effectiveness of our method on problems with hundreds of tasks.



1
Introduction

We consider multi-block min-max bilevel optimization problem of the following formulation

\displaystyle\min_{\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}}\max_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}F(\textbf{x},\boldsymbol{\alpha}):=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\{f_{i}(\textbf{x},\alpha_{i},\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x})):=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\in\mathcal{P}_{i}}[f_{i}(\textbf{x},\alpha_{i},\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x});\xi)]\right\}\qquad\text{(upper)}

(1)

\displaystyle\text{s.t. }\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x})=\argmin_{\textbf{y}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}}g_{i}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}_{i}):=\mathbb{E}_{\zeta\in\mathcal{Q}_{i}}[g_{i}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}_{i};\zeta)],\quad\text{for }i=1,2,\dots,m.\qquad~~~~~~~\text{(lower)}

where
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
are smooth functions and
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{\alpha}}
is a convex set. In particular, in this paper we assume that for each
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
f_{i}(\textbf{x},\alpha_{i},\textbf{y}_{i})
is strongly concave in the dual variable
\alpha_{i}
but can be nonconvex in the primal variable
x
, and
g_{i}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}_{i})
is strongly convex in
\textbf{y}_{i}
. The
upper problem

\min_{\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}}\max_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}F(\textbf{x},\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is a min-max optimization problem where the
lower problems

\{\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x})=\argmin_{\textbf{y}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}}g_{i}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
are involved as variables. For each
block

i
, the
upper-level objective

f_{i}(\textbf{x},\alpha_{i},\textbf{y}_{i})
and the
lower-level objective

g_{i}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}_{i})
depend only on its corresponding block of variables
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and
y
, i.e.
\alpha_{i}
and
\textbf{y}_{i}
. This problem has important applications in machine learning, e.g., multi-task deep AUC maximization as presented in section
3
.

Tackling problem (
1
) is challenging as it involves solving a min-max problem with coupled multiple minimization problems simultaneously. The naive way for solving it is to do multiple gradient ascents and descents for
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and
y
, respectively, to ensure a good estimation of the gradient for updating
x
. However, this approach has two major drawbacks. As the algorithm involves a double loop structure, it can be computationally expensive and give suboptimal theoretical complexity. On the other hand, the multi-block structure requires data sampling from distributions
\mathcal{P}_{i},\mathcal{Q}_{i}
for all blocks, which may lead to an impractical demand for memory.

Multi-block Bilevel Optimization.
There are some recent studies considering bilevel optimization with multi-block structure.
[
10
]
extends their single-block bilevel optimization algorithm to multi-block structure. Their algorithm requires two independently sampled block batches and for all sampled blocks, each variable needs update using variance reduction technique STORM

[

1.2
Our Contributions

In Section
2.1
, we present two simple single loop single timescale stochastic methods with randomized block-sampling for solving a general form of multi-block min-max bilevel optimization problem under the nonconvex strongly concave (upper) strongly convex (lower) setting. Both methods employ SGD for updating selected
\textbf{y}_{i}
for their corresponding lower-level problems, employs SGA for updating the selected
\alpha_{i}
, and employs a momentum update for the primal variable

based on the sampled

. Then we show in Section

, theoretically, that they converge to
\epsilon
-stationary point with complexity
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-4})
under a general unbiased stochastic oracle model. Our result for the single-block setting matches the lower bound for solving smooth, potentially nonconvex optimization through queries to an unbiased stochastic gradient oracle under a bounded variance condition
[
1
]
. Finally, in section
3
we present two applications of multi-block min-max bilevel optimization in deep AUC maximization: multi-task deep AUC maximization and multi-task deep partial AUC maximization. In section

A Stochastic Algorithm for Multi-block Min-max Bilevel Optimization (v1)

First, we propose a simple single loop stochastic algorithm
1
to solve the multi-block min-max bilevel optimization problem. At the beginning of each iteration, we first sample a set of blocks
I_{t}
and data batches
\mathcal{B}^{t}_{i},\tilde{\mathcal{B}}^{t}_{i}
for each selected block

. Then we update estimators of
\alpha_{i}(\textbf{x}_{t})
and
\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x}_{t})
for all selected blocks

using one step of SGA and SGD. Then, we compute an estimator of the hessian inverse
H^{t+1}
of the lower-level objective and compute a gradient estimator
\Delta^{t+1}
of the upper-level objective. Finally, we compute the moving average estimator
\textbf{z}_{t+1}
of
\nabla F(\textbf{x}_{t})
and update
\textbf{x}_{t+1}
. Note that the design of our algorithm on the min-max bilevel optimization part is inspired by
[
11
]
, and it is similar to their momentum-based algorithms PDSM (for min-max problem) and SMB (for bilevel problem) in their paper. In fact, if we set the number of blocks to be one and remove
y

1
is the same as PDSM. Similarly, if the number of blocks is one and the dual variables in the upper level problem are removed, then the proposed algorithm becomes similar as SMB except for the hessian inverse update with reasons explain shortly. In other words, our proposed method is a generalized form of momentum-based algorithm for min-max and bilevel optimization problems. Additionally, Algorithm
1
only updates
O(1)

\alpha_{i}
and the variables
\textbf{y}_{i}
of the lower-level problems. These make the analysis of Algorithm

To further understand Algorithm

\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x})=\argmin_{\textbf{y}_{i}}g_{i}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}_{i})

are directly available from the unbiased stochastic oracles. However, since functions

and

are implicit, estimating the gradient

is difficult. In fact, one may apply the corollary of Theorem 1 in

\displaystyle\nabla F(\textbf{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\nabla_{x}f_{i}(\textbf{x},\alpha_{i}(\textbf{x}),\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x}))+\nabla\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x})\nabla_{y}f_{i}(\textbf{x},\alpha_{i}(\textbf{x}),\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x}))\right).

A standard approach in bilevel optimization literature
[
8
]
for computing

is to derive
\nabla\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x})=-\nabla^{2}_{xy}g_{i}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x}))[\nabla^{2}_{yy}g_{i}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x}))]^{-1}

\displaystyle\nabla F(\textbf{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\nabla_{x}f_{i}(\textbf{x},\alpha_{i}(\textbf{x}),\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x}))-\nabla^{2}_{xy}g_{i}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x}))[\nabla^{2}_{yy}g_{i}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x}))]^{-1}\nabla_{y}f_{i}(\textbf{x},\alpha_{i}(\textbf{x}),\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x})).

All components in this gradient can be easily obtained from unbiased stochastic oracles except for the inverse of hessian
[\nabla^{2}_{yy}g_{i}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}_{i}(\textbf{x}))]^{-1}
for all blocks. This could be problematic in the sense of theory and practical implementation. For practical implementation, we do not want to update the hessian inverse estimators for all blocks, which is prohibitive when the number of blocks is large.
A common approach used in the literature of SBO is to use Neumann series
[

]

H_{i}^{t+1}=\frac{k_{t}}{C_{gyy}}\prod_{j=1}^{q}\left(I-\frac{1}{C_{gyy}}\nabla_{yy}^{2}g_{i}(\textbf{x}_{t},\textbf{y}_{i}^{t};\xi_{i}^{t})\right)

2.2
Convergence Analysis

In this section, we present brief convergence analysis of Algorithm
1
and Algorithm
2
. The detailed theorems and proofs are deferred to the appendix.

2.2.1
Convergence analysis of Algorithm
1

The key point of this analysis is the gap between the true gradient
\nabla F(\textbf{x}_{t})
and its estimator
\textbf{z}^{t+1}
. To this end, we define

\displaystyle\nabla F(\textbf{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{t},\textbf{y}^{t}):=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{\nabla_{x}f_{i}(\textbf{x}_{t},\alpha_{i}^{t},\textbf{y}_{i}^{t})-\nabla^{2}_{xy}g_{i}(\textbf{x}_{t},\textbf{y}_{i}^{t})\mathbb{E}_{t}[H_{i}^{t}]\nabla_{y}f_{i}(\textbf{x}_{t},\alpha_{i}^{t},\textbf{y}_{i}^{t})\right\}.

One may notice that the estimator
\Delta^{t+1}
is in fact approximating
\nabla_{x}F(\textbf{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{t},\textbf{y}^{t})
instead of
\nabla_{x}F(\textbf{x}_{t})
. We exploit the moving average formulation of
\textbf{z}^{t+1}
and decompose the gap into two parts,
\|\Delta^{t+1}-\nabla_{x}F(\textbf{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{t},\textbf{y}^{t})\|^{2}
and
\|\nabla_{x}F(\textbf{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{t},\textbf{y}^{t})-\nabla_{x}F(\textbf{x}_{t})\|^{2}
. These two gaps are determined by how well
\textbf{y}^{t}
,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{t}
and
H_{i}^{t}
approximate
\textbf{y}(\textbf{x}_{t})
,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\textbf{x}_{t})
and
[\nabla^{2}_{yy}g_{i}(\textbf{x}_{t},\textbf{y}_{i}^{t})]^{-1}
, respectively. In other words, we aim to bound the following three errors,
\|\textbf{y}(\textbf{x}_{t})-\textbf{y}^{t}\|^{2}=:\delta_{y,t}
,
\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\textbf{x}_{t})-\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{t}\|^{2}:=\delta_{\alpha,t}
and
\|[\nabla^{2}_{yy}g_{i}(\textbf{x}_{t},\textbf{y}_{i}^{t})]^{-1}-H_{i}^{t}\|^{2}
.

We first bound the variance
\mathbb{E}[\delta_{y,t}]
by proving the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Consider the updates for
\textbf{y}^{t}
in Algorithm
1

2.2
and
2.3

\eta_{2}\leq\min\{\frac{\mu_{g}}{L_{g}^{2}},\frac{2m}{|I_{t}|\mu_{g}}\}

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\delta_{y,t}]\leq\frac{2m}{|I_{t}|\eta_{2}\mu_{g}}\delta_{y,0}+\frac{4m\eta_{2}T\sigma^{2}}{\mu_{g}B}+\frac{8m^{3}C_{y}^{2}\eta_{0}^{2}}{|I_{t}|^{2}\eta_{2}^{2}\mu_{g}^{2}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\textbf{z}_{t+1}\|^{2}]

One may also bound the second variance
\mathbb{E}[\delta_{\alpha,t}]
based on the previous variance

following a similar strategy.

Consider the updates for
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{t}
in Algorithm

2.2
,
2.3

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{t}[\delta_{\alpha,t}]\leq\frac{4m}{\eta_{1}\mu_{f}|I_{t}|}\delta_{\alpha,0}+\frac{24L_{f}^{2}}{\mu_{f}^{2}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\delta_{y,t}]+\frac{8m\mu_{f}\eta_{1}\sigma^{2}T}{B}+\frac{32m^{3}C_{\alpha}^{2}\eta_{0}^{2}}{\eta_{1}^{2}\mu_{f}^{2}|I_{t}|^{2}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\textbf{z}_{t+1}\|^{2}].

Due to the lower bound assumption of

3
Applications in Multi-task Deep (Partial) AUC Maximization

In this section, we present two applications of multi-block min-max bilevel optimization: multi-task deep AUC maximization and deep partial AUC maximization. (partial) AUC is a performance measure of classifiers for imbalanced data. Recent studies have shown great success of deep AUC maximization in various domains (e.g., medical image classification and molecular property prediction)
[
26
,
33
,
40
]
. However, efficient algorithms for multi-task deep (partial) AUC maximization have not been well developed. For multi-task deep AUC maximization, we solve an existing formulation by our algorithm. For multi-task deep partial AUC maximization, we propose a new bilevel formulation and solve it by our algorithm.

3.1
Muti-task Deep AUC maximization

Following the previous work
[
26
,
40
]
, deep AUC maximization problem can be formulated as a non-convex strongly concave min-max optimization problem
\min_{\textbf{w},a,b}\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}L_{\text{AUC}}(\textbf{w},a,b,\alpha)
. However, training a deep neural network from scratch by optimizing AUC loss does not necessarily lead to a good performance
[
39
]
. To address this issue,
[
39
]
proposed a compositional training strategy for deep AUC maximization:

\min_{\textbf{w},a,b}\max_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}L_{\text{AUC}}(\textbf{w}-\tilde{\eta}\nabla L_{\text{CE}}(\textbf{w}),a,b,\alpha),

where
L_{\text{CE}}
denotes the cross-entropy loss. The outer objective remains to be the AUC loss, while the inner objective is a gradient descent step of minimizing the traditional cross-entropy loss. This method has shown superior performance on various datasets
[
39
]
. We extend this formulation to multi-task problems and reformulate it into a multi-block min-max bilevel optimization:

\displaystyle\min_{(\textbf{w}_{l},\textbf{w}_{h}),\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}\max_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}L_{\text{AUC}}(\textbf{u}^{i}(\textbf{w}_{l},\textbf{w}_{h}),a^{i},b^{i},\alpha^{i})

\displaystyle\text{s.t. }\textbf{u}^{i}(\textbf{w}_{l},\textbf{w}_{h})=\argmin_{\textbf{u}^{i}}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\textbf{u}^{i}-((\textbf{w}_{l},\textbf{w}_{h}^{i})-\tilde{\eta}\nabla L_{\text{CE}}(\textbf{w}_{l},\textbf{w}_{h}^{i}))\right\|^{2},

where
\textbf{w}_{l}
denotes the weight for the encoder network that is shared for all tasks, and
\textbf{w}_{h}=(\textbf{w}_{h}^{1},\dots,\textbf{w}_{h}^{m})
denote the task-owned classification heads. The upper objective is strongly concave in terms of dual variables
\alpha_{i}
and the lower level objective is strongly convex in terms of
\textbf{u}^{i}
. The hessian of the lower-level objective is the identity matrix. Hence, there is no need to track and estimate the hessian matrix.

3.2
Multi-task Deep Partial AUC Maximization

Some real-world applications (e.g., medical diagnosis
[
2
]
) cannot tolerate a model with a high False Positive Rate (FPR) even though it has significant performance in AUC. Hence, a measure of interest is one-way partial AUC (pAUC), which puts a restriction on the range of FPR (i.e., FPR
\in[\rho_{l},\rho]
, where
0\leq\rho_{l}\leq\rho\leq 1
). Below, we focus on the case
\rho_{l}=0
. However, our method can be easily extended for handling
\rho_{l}>0
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{+},\mathcal{D}_{-}
denote the set of positive and negative data for a binary classification task, respectively. Let
\mathcal{D}_{-}[K]
denote the top-K negative examples according to their prediction scores. Let
n_{+},n_{-}
denote the number of positive and negative samples respectively. Then we have partial AUC optimization with a pairwise square loss formulated as following
[
37
]
:

\displaystyle\min_{\textbf{w}}\frac{1}{n_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}}\frac{1}{n_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K]}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i})+c)^{2},

where
K=n_{-}\rho
,
c
is a constant and
h_{\textbf{w}}(\cdot)
denotes the prediction score on a data. A key challenge for solving the above problem is to deal with the non-differentiable top-K selector
\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K]
, which depends on the model parameters
w
. This challenge has been recently tackled in
[
38
,
42
]
. We focus on the comparison with the first work as it is optimization oriented similar to ours and also has the state-of-the-art performance. They formulate the problem into either a weakly convex minimization or approximate it by a smooth objective in a compositional form. A caveat of their algorithms (named SOPA, SOPA-s) is that they need to maintain and update
n_{+}
auxiliary variables with one for each positive data. If we apply their algorithms for multi-task problems, one needs to maintain and update
\sum_{i=1}^{m}n^{i}_{+}
auxiliary variables, which could dramatically slow down the convergence.

To address this problem, we first transform it into a min-max optimization problem. Let
a(\textbf{w})=\frac{1}{n_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}}h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i})
and
b(\textbf{w})=\frac{1}{n_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K]}h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})
.
Then we can write the problem as (cf. Appendix B for a derivation)

\displaystyle\min_{\textbf{w},a,b}\frac{1}{n_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i})-a)^{2}+\frac{1}{n_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}}\mathbb{I}(\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K])(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-b)^{2}+(b(\textbf{w})-a(\textbf{w})+c)^{2}.

To tackle non-continuous non-differentiable indicator function
\mathbb{I}(\cdot)
we can replace it by a sigmoid function. To tackle non-differentiability of the top-K selector
\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K]
, we follow
[
29
]
and formulate it as lower-level optimization problem, i.e.,
\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K]
is equivalent to
h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})>\lambda(\textbf{w})
, where
\lambda(\textbf{w})
represents the
K+1
-th largest scores among all negative examples, which can be approximated by a solution from a smooth strongly convex minimization problem as following:

\displaystyle\lambda(\textbf{w})=\argmin_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}L(\lambda,\textbf{w}):=\frac{K+\varepsilon}{n_{-}}\lambda+\frac{\tau_{2}}{2}\lambda^{2}+\frac{1}{n_{-}}\sum_{\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}}\tau_{1}\ln(1+\exp((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x})-\lambda)/\tau_{1})),

where
\varepsilon,\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
are small constants. Based on above, the multi-task deep partial AUC minimization problem can be formulated as a multi-block min-max bilevel optimization problem give by:

\displaystyle\min_{\textbf{w},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}\max_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{n^{k}_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{+}}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i};k)-a_{k})^{2}+\frac{1}{n^{k}_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{-}}\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-{\lambda}_{k}(\textbf{w}))(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-b_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle+2\alpha_{k}\Bigg(\frac{1}{n_{-}^{k}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}^{k}}\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-{\lambda}_{k}(\textbf{w}))h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\frac{1}{n^{k}_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{+}}h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i};k)+c\Bigg)-\alpha_{k}^{2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle{\lambda}_{k}(\textbf{w})=\argmin_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}L_{k}(\lambda,\textbf{w}):=\frac{K+\varepsilon}{n_{-}}\lambda+\frac{\tau_{2}}{2}\lambda^{2}+\frac{1}{n_{-}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{-}}\tau_{1}\ln(1+\exp((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda)/\tau_{1})),

where
\mathcal{D}^{k}_{+/-}
denote the positive/negative data set of the
k
-th task,
h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x};k)
denote the prediction score for the
k
-th classifier, and
\phi(s)=\frac{1}{1+\exp(-s)}
is the sigmoid function. The upper-level objective is strongly concave in terms of
\alpha_{k}
and the lower-level objective is strongly convex in terms of
\lambda_{k}
.

We develop a tailored algorithm based on Algorithm
1
for solving the above formulation of multi-task pAUC maximization as shown in Algorithm
3
in Appendix
C

G^{t+1}

C

C

G^{t+1}

Conclusion and Future Work

We have developed two simple single loop randomized stochastic algorithms for solving multi-block min-max bilevel optimization problems. These algorithms require updates for only constant number of blocks in each iteration. We showed that both of them achieve an oracle complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-4})
, which matches the optimal complexity order for solving stochastic nonconvex optimization under a general unbiased stochastic oracle model. At the same time, we hope our work inspires others to find more novel applications of our idea.

Algorithm 3

Min-Max Bilevel Optimization for pAUC Maximization (MMB-pAUC)

Let
\mathcal{D}_{-}[K]
denote the top-K negative examples according to their prediction scores. Let
n_{+},n_{-}
denote the number of positive and negative label samples respectively. Then we have partial AUC loss formulated as following

\displaystyle\min_{\textbf{w}}\frac{1}{n_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}}\frac{1}{n_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K]}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i})+c)^{2}

where
K=n_{-}\rho
. Let
a(\textbf{w})=\frac{1}{n_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}}h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i})
and
b(\textbf{w})=\frac{1}{n_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K]}h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})
.
Then we can transform the objective as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}}\frac{1}{n_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K]}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-b(\textbf{w})+a(\textbf{w})-h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i})+b(\textbf{w})-a(\textbf{w})+c)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i})-a(\textbf{w}))^{2}+\frac{1}{n_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K]}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-b(\textbf{w}))^{2}+(b(\textbf{w})-a(\textbf{w})+c)^{2}

Then we can write the problem as

\min_{\textbf{w},a,b}\frac{1}{n_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i})-a)^{2}+\frac{1}{n_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}}\mathbb{I}(\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K])(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-b)^{2}+(b(\textbf{w})-a(\textbf{w})+c)^{2}

(34)

Replacing the indicator function by
\mathbb{I}(\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K])=\psi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-\lambda(\textbf{w}))
, where
\lambda(\textbf{w})
represents the
K+1
-th largest scores among all negative examples, which can be represented as

\lambda(\textbf{w})=\arg\min_{\lambda}\frac{K+\varepsilon}{n_{-}}\lambda+\frac{1}{n_{-}}\sum_{\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x})-\lambda)_{+}.

We smooth the problem as

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}(\textbf{w})=\argmin_{\lambda}L(\lambda,\textbf{w}):=\frac{K+\varepsilon}{n_{-}}\lambda+\frac{\tau_{2}}{2}\lambda^{2}+\frac{1}{n_{-}}\sum_{\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}}\tau_{1}\ln(1+\exp((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x})-\lambda)/\tau_{1})).

Due to the fact that the last term
(b(\mathbf{w})-a(\mathbf{w})+c)^{2}
in Problem
34
cannot be directly obtained since
a(\mathbf{w})
and
b(\mathbf{w})
are expectations, one may use
p^{2}=\max_{\alpha}2p\alpha-\alpha^{2}
to get

(b(\mathbf{w})-a(\mathbf{w})+c)^{2}=\max_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}[K],\,\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}}\left[2\alpha(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i})+c)-\alpha^{2}\right]

(35)

Then by replacing the top-K selector with
\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-\hat{\lambda}(\textbf{w}))
, the partial AUC minimization problem can be formulated as a min-max bilevel optimization problem.

\displaystyle\min_{\textbf{w},a,b}\frac{1}{n_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i})-a)^{2}+\frac{1}{n_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}}\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-\hat{\lambda}(\textbf{w}))(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-b)^{2}

\displaystyle+\max_{\alpha}2\alpha\left(\frac{1}{n_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}}\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-\hat{\lambda}(\textbf{w}))h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-\frac{1}{n_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}}h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i})+c\right)-\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle s.t.,\hat{\lambda}(\textbf{w})=\argmin_{\lambda}L(\lambda,\textbf{w}):=\frac{K+\varepsilon}{n_{-}}\lambda+\frac{\tau_{2}}{2}\lambda^{2}+\frac{1}{n_{-}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}}\tau_{1}\ln(1+\exp((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j})-\lambda)/\tau_{1}))

We consider multi-task partial AUC maximization, which is then given by

\displaystyle\min_{\textbf{w},\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\max_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{n^{k}_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{+}}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i};k)-a_{k})^{2}+\frac{1}{n^{k}_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{-}}\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\hat{\lambda}_{k}(\textbf{w}))(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-b_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle+2\alpha_{k}\Bigg(\frac{1}{n_{-}^{k}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}^{k}}\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\hat{\lambda}_{k}(\textbf{w}))h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\frac{1}{n^{k}_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{+}}h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i};k)+c\Bigg)-\alpha_{k}^{2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle s.t.,\hat{\lambda}_{k}(\textbf{w})=\argmin_{\lambda}L_{k}(\lambda,\textbf{w}):=\frac{K+\varepsilon}{n_{-}}\lambda+\frac{\tau_{2}}{2}\lambda^{2}+\frac{1}{n_{-}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{-}}\tau_{1}\ln(1+\exp((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda)/\tau_{1}))

For function
\phi
, we use sigmoid function
\phi(s)=\frac{1}{1+\exp(-s)}=\sigma(s)
.
\nabla\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda_{k}(\textbf{w}))=\sigma(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda_{k}(\textbf{w}))(1-\sigma(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda_{k}(\textbf{w}))(\nabla h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\nabla\lambda_{k}(\textbf{w}))
, where
\nabla\lambda_{k}(\textbf{w})=-(\nabla_{\lambda\lambda}^{2}L_{k}(\lambda,\textbf{w}))^{-1}\nabla_{\textbf{w}\lambda}^{2}L_{k}(\lambda,\textbf{w})
. Let
H_{k}=(\nabla_{\lambda\lambda}^{2}L_{k}(\lambda,\textbf{w}))
, and

\displaystyle\nabla_{\textbf{w}\lambda}^{2}L_{k}(\lambda,\textbf{w})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\textbf{w}}\left[\nabla_{\lambda}L_{k}(\lambda,\textbf{w})\right]

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\textbf{w}}\left(\frac{K+\varepsilon}{n_{-}}+\tau_{2}\lambda-\frac{1}{n_{-}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{-}}\frac{\exp((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda)/\tau_{1})}{1+\exp((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda)/\tau_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n_{-}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{-}}\nabla_{\textbf{w}}\left(\frac{\exp((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda)/\tau_{1})}{1+\exp((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda)/\tau_{1})}\right)

As a result in order to compute in Pytorch
\nabla\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda(\textbf{w}))
we can define the following loss

\displaystyle L^{k}_{\phi}(\textbf{w})

\displaystyle=st(\sigma(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda_{k})(1-\sigma(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda_{k}))\cdot

(36)

\displaystyle\left(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-st([H_{k}]^{-1})\frac{1}{B_{-}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{B}^{k}_{-}}\frac{\exp((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda_{k})/\tau_{1})}{1+\exp((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda_{k})/\tau_{1})}\right)

(37)

so that
\nabla L^{k}_{\phi}(\textbf{w})=\nabla\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda(\textbf{w}))
.
Hence for updating
\textbf{w},\textbf{a},\textbf{b}
we can define the following loss and the Pytorch will compute the gradient automatically:

\displaystyle G^{t+1}:=\frac{1}{|I_{t}|}

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I_{t}}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{B^{k}_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}^{k}_{+}}(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i};k)-a_{k})^{2}+\frac{1}{B^{k}_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{B}^{k}_{-}}st(\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda_{k}))(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-b_{k})^{2}

(38)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{B^{k}_{-}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{B}^{k}_{-}}L^{k}_{\phi}(\textbf{w})st((h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-b_{k})^{2})+2\alpha_{k}\bigg(\frac{1}{B_{-}^{k}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{B}_{-}^{k}}st(\phi(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)-\lambda_{k}))h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{B_{-}^{k}\rho}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{j}\in\mathcal{B}_{-}^{k}}L^{k}_{\phi}(\textbf{w})st(h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{j};k))-\frac{1}{B^{k}_{+}}\sum_{\textbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}^{k}_{+}}h_{\textbf{w}}(\textbf{x}_{i};k)+c\bigg)\Bigg\}

The loss for

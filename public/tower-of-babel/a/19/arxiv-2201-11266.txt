Differential modules and dormant opers of higher level
Yasuhiro Wakabayashi

Abstract
. In the first half of the present paper, we study higher-level generalizations of differential modules in positive characteristic. These objects may be regarded as ring-theoretic counterparts of vector bundles on a curve equipped with an action of the ring of (logarithmic) differential operators of finite level introduced by P. Berthelot (and C. Montagnon). The existence assertion for a cyclic vector of a differential module is generalized to higher level under mild conditions. In the second half, we introduce (dormant) opers of level N > 0 N>0 on a pointed smooth curve whose structure group is either GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} or PGL n \mathrm{PGL}_{n} . Some of the results on higher-level differential modules are applied to prove a duality theorem between dormant PGL n \mathrm{PGL}_{n} -opers of level N N and dormant PGL p N − n \mathrm{PGL}_{p^{N}-n} -opers of level N N . Finally, in the case where the underlying curve is a 3 3 -pointed projective line, we establish a bijective correspondence between dormant PGL 2 \mathrm{PGL}_{2} -opers of level N N and certain tamely ramified coverings.

Introduction

0.1.

A
differential module
over a differential ring
(R,\partial:R\rightarrow R)
is an
R
-module
E
equipped with an additive map
\nabla:E\rightarrow E
satisfying the Leibniz rule:
\nabla(a\cdot v)=\partial(a)\cdot v+a\cdot\nabla(v)
(
a\in R
,
v\in E
).
If
R
is of characteristic
0
, a differential module may be regarded as a ring-theoretic counterpart of
a
\mathcal{D}
-module (or equivalently, a sheaf equipped with a flat connection) on an algebraic curve.
On the other hand, differential modules and
\mathcal{D}
-modules in characteristic
p>0
have many different features from those in characteristic
0
(see, e.g.,
[
And
]
,
[
Hon
]
,
[
Kat2
]
,
[
Kat3
]
).
For example, unlike the case of characteristic
0
, Picard-Vessiot theory fails for differential modules in characteristic
p
.
Despite this problem, such objects have attracted a lot of attention for various reasons, including in relation to the Grothendieck-Katz
p
-curvature conjecture.
We should note that there are variations of the sheaf “
\mathcal{D}
” defined on an algebraic variety
X
in characteristic
p
.
One is the ring of crystalline differential operators (following the wording in
[
BMR
]
), which we denote by
\mathcal{D}^{(0)}_{X}
.
Giving a
\mathcal{D}^{(0)}_{X}
-module is equivalent to giving
an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module together with a flat connection.
This means that the notion of a
\mathcal{D}^{(0)}_{X}
-module corresponds exactly to a differential module in the usual sense.
Another variant is the ring of differential operators
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}_{X}
in the sense of Grothendieck (cf.
[
Gro
, Section 16.8.1]
).
A
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}_{X}
-module is often called a
stratified sheaf

and interpreted as
an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module admitting infinite Frobenius descent.
The ring-theoretic counterpart
is known as an
iterative differential module
(cf.
[
Oku
, § 1.2]
,
[
vdPS
, Chap. 13, § 13.3]
).
The notion of a stratified sheaf was introduced in D. Gieseker’s paper
(cf.
[
Gie
, Definition 1.1]
) and discussed in, e.g.,
[
dSa
]
,
[
Esn
]
,
[
EsMe
]
,
[
Kin1
]
, and
[
Kin2
]
.
We also can find descriptions of iterative differential modules in, e.g.,
[
Ern
]
,
[
MavdP
]
, and
[
Rös
]
.

Next, let us recall
the ring of differential operators
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(m)}
of level
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, as introduced by P. Berthelot (cf.
[
PBer1
]
,
[
PBer2
]
); this kind of sheaf is an essential ingredient in defining arithmetic
\mathcal{D}
-modules, and
it may be positioned between
\mathcal{D}^{(0)}_{X}
and
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(\infty)}
.
In fact,
the ring of crystalline differential operators coincides with Berthelot’s
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(0)}
(i.e.,
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(m)}
for
m=0
), and
there exists an inductive system

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(0)}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(1)}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(2)}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(m)}\rightarrow\cdots

satisfying
\varinjlim_{m}\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(m)}=\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}_{X}
.
Moreover, C. Montagnon generalized
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(m)}
to the case where the underlying scheme is equipped with a log structure (cf.
[
Mon
, Chap. 2, § 2.3, Definition 2.3.1]
).
This generalization allows us to deal with
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}_{X}
-modules (in the logarithmic sense) for (possibly singular) pointed curves
X
.

In the first half of the present paper,
we consider ring-theoretic counterparts of (both non-logarithmic and logarithmic versions of)
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(m)}
-modules, in other words,
higher-level generalizations of differential modules.
The central character is
an
m
-differential ring
(resp., an
m
-log differential ring
), which is defined as a ring
R
in characteristic
p
equipped with a collection of
certain additive endomorphisms
\partial_{\langle\bullet\rangle}:=\{\partial_{\langle j\rangle}\}_{j=0}^{p^{m}}
(cf. Definition
1.2.1
, (ii)).
Each such data
\mathscr{R}:=(R,\partial_{\langle\bullet\rangle})
yields a possibly noncommutative ring
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
generated by the elements of
R
and the set of abstract symbols
\{\partial_{\langle j\rangle}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}

extending the endomorphisms
\{\partial_{\langle j\rangle}\}_{j=0}^{p^{m}}
.
(The definition of the related ring can be found in
[
Kin1
, Chap. 1, § 1.1, Definition 1.1.1]
.)
In particular, we obtain the notion of a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module, which corresponds to the notion of a
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(m)}
-module.

An important ingredient in the theory of differential modules
is the concept of a
cyclic vector
.
A cyclic vector of a differential module
(E,\nabla)
is an element
v\in E
such that the elements
\nabla^{0}(v)\left(=v\right),\nabla^{1}(v),\cdots,\nabla^{l}(v)
(for some
l\geq 0
) form a basis.
The choice of such an element enables

(E,\nabla)
to be interpreted as a higher-order linear differential operator on
R
;
accordingly,
each element in
E
that is horizontal with respect to
\nabla

can be described as a root function of that operator.
A fundamental result is the one proving the existence of a cyclic vector in a general situation (cf.
[
ChKo
]
,
[
Kat4
]
).
We refer the reader to, e.g.,
[
Adj
]
,
[
DBer
]
,
[
Del
]
, and
[
Kov
]
, for
various results concerning cyclic vectors.

Given an
m
-differential ring
\mathscr{R}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module
(E,\nabla)
,
we can describe the notion of an
m
-cyclic vector
of
(E,\nabla)
(cf. Definition
1.5.1
) as a higher-level generalization of a cyclic vector in the classical sense.
An
m
-cyclic vector of
(E,\nabla)
is, by definition, an element of
E
such that

\nabla_{\langle 0\rangle}(v)\left(=v\right),\nabla_{\langle 1\rangle}(v),\cdots,\nabla_{\langle l\rangle}(v)
(for some
l\geq 0
) forms a basis of
E
.
Also, by a

\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module
, we mean a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module together with an
m
-cyclic vector.
Our study of
m
-cyclic vectors is motivated by the observation that
a pinned
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(0)}
-module
is essentially the same as a locally defined
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-oper on a curve;
as such,
various properties of
m
-cyclic vectors can be used to examine higher-level generalizations of opers.
The first main result of the present paper generalizes the classical assertion of the existence of a cyclic vector to higher level.

1.
Differential modules and cyclic vectors of higher level

1.1.
Modified binomial coefficients

\displaystyle\begin{Bmatrix}j\\
j^{\prime}\end{Bmatrix}_{(m)}:=\frac{q^{(m)}_{j}!}{q^{(m)}_{j}!\cdot q^{(m)}_{j-j^{\prime}}!},\hskip 28.45274pt\left\langle\begin{matrix}j\\

1.2.
Differential rings of higher level

Let us fix an integer

and a ring

over

.
In the following discussion, the non-resp’d portion deals with the non-logarithmic case and the resp’d portion deals with the logarithmic case.

Definition 1.2.1
.

(i)

Let

R_{0}
.
An

-derivation
(resp.,
m
-log derivation

(b)

If

*

\displaystyle{\partial_{\langle j\rangle}(a\cdot b)=\sum_{j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}=j}\begin{Bmatrix}j\\
j^{\prime}\end{Bmatrix}\cdot\partial_{\langle j^{\prime}\rangle}(a)\cdot\partial_{\langle j^{\prime\prime}\rangle}}(b)
for any elements

*

\displaystyle{\partial_{\langle j^{\prime}\rangle}\circ\partial_{\langle j-j^{\prime}\rangle}=\left\langle\begin{matrix}j\\
j^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right\rangle\cdot\partial_{\langle j\rangle}}\left(\text{resp.,}\ \partial_{\langle j^{\prime}\rangle}\circ\partial_{\langle j-j^{\prime}\rangle}=\sum_{j^{\prime\prime}=\mathrm{max}\{j^{\prime},j-j^{\prime}\}}^{j}\left\langle\begin{matrix}j\\

(ii)

-log differential ring
) over

, we mean the pair

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}:=(R,\partial_{\langle\bullet\rangle})

-derivation (resp.,

-log derivation)

.
(In particular, a

Finally, by an

-differential field

-differential ring

Remark 1.2.2
.

(i)

If we are given a

on

,
then
\partial_{\langle 1\rangle}

(over

) in the usual sense.
Under the correspondence

(ii)

Let

be a ring over

.
The second equality in condition (b) above implies that

-derivation (resp., an
m
-log derivation)

Let us fix an

-differential ring (resp.,

subject to the following relations:

•

•

\displaystyle{\partial_{\langle j\rangle}\cdot a=\sum_{j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}=j}\begin{Bmatrix}j\\
j^{\prime}\end{Bmatrix}\cdot\partial_{\langle j^{\prime}\rangle}(a)\cdot\partial_{\langle j^{\prime\prime}\rangle}}

\displaystyle{\partial_{\langle j^{\prime}\rangle}\cdot\partial_{\langle j-j^{\prime}\rangle}=\left\langle\begin{matrix}j\\

the two-sided

[

1.3.
Differential modules of higher level

Let us take an
R
-module

By a
(left)
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module structure
on

a left
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-action (i.e., an

-algebra homomorphism)

An

-module structure is called a
(left)
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module

.
Moreover, we can define, in a natural manner, the notion of an isomorphism between
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-modules.
Given a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module structure
\nabla
on
E
and an integer

,
we shall write
\nabla_{\langle j\rangle}:=\nabla(\partial_{\langle j\rangle})
.
If
\mathscr{R}
is non-logarithmic (resp., logarithmic), then the assignment
\nabla\mapsto\{\nabla_{\langle j\rangle}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
determines a bijective correspondence between the set of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module structures on

and the set of collections of
R_{0}
-linear endomorphisms
\{\nabla_{\langle j\rangle}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
of

satisfying the following conditions:

•

\nabla_{\langle 0\rangle}=\mathrm{id}_{E}
;

•

\displaystyle{\nabla_{\langle j\rangle}(a\cdot v)=\sum_{j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}=j}\begin{Bmatrix}j\\
j^{\prime}\end{Bmatrix}\cdot\partial_{\langle j^{\prime}\rangle}(a)\cdot\nabla_{\langle j^{\prime\prime}\rangle}(v)}
for any integer

,

•

\displaystyle{\nabla_{\langle j^{\prime}\rangle}\circ\nabla_{\langle j-j^{\prime}\rangle}=\left\langle\begin{matrix}j\\

for any integers

with

.

Because of this correspondence, we will not distinguish these two additional structures on

.

Remark 1.3.1
.

Let us consider the case of

.
Suppose that
(E,\nabla)
is a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(0)}
-module.
Then, the
R
-module

together with the endomorphism
\nabla_{\langle 1\rangle}\left(:=\nabla(\partial_{\langle 1\rangle})\right)
specifies a differential module, in the classical sense, over the differential ring corresponding to
\mathscr{R}
(cf. Remark
1.2.2
, (i)).

Remark 1.3.2
.

Let
\{\partial_{\langle j\rangle}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
be an extended
m
-derivation (resp., extended
m
-log derivation) on
R
.
To make the integer

explicit,
we here write
\partial_{\langle j\rangle}^{(m)}:=\partial_{\langle j\rangle}
(

For an integer

Let us set

-algebra homomorphism

\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}

We shall denote by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{D}^{(m)}_{\mathscr{R}})

1.4.
Varying levels

Fix an integer
m\geq 0
and
an integer
l
with
0\leq l\leq m+1
.
Furthermore, we set

(17)

\displaystyle R^{l}:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{p^{l-1}}\mathrm{Ker}(\partial_{\langle j\rangle})\left(=\bigcap_{j=0}^{l-1}\mathrm{Ker}(\partial_{\langle p^{j}\rangle})\right),

which is an

-subalgebra of

-algebras

\displaystyle R^{m+1}\subseteq R^{m}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq R^{1}\subseteq R^{0}=R.

to be the

\displaystyle F^{(l)*}_{j}(E):=R^{j}\otimes_{R^{j+l}}E.

For simplicity,

.

Also, for each

,
the endomorphism
\partial_{\langle jp^{l}\rangle}
is restricted to an

-linear endomorphism of

for this restriction.
Then, the collection

(20)

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}^{l}:=(R^{l},\{\partial_{\langle jp^{l}\rangle}\}_{0\leq j\leq p^{m-l}})

forms an

-differential ring over

.
In particular, we obtain the

-algebra

\displaystyle\left(R^{m+1}=\right)\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}^{m+1}}^{(-1)}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}^{m}}^{(0)}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}^{m-1}}^{(1)}\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}^{1}}^{(m-1)}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}.

Next,
given a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module
(E,\nabla)
,
we shall write

(22)

\displaystyle E^{l}:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{p^{l-1}}\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla_{\langle j\rangle})\left(=\bigcap_{j=0}^{l-1}\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla_{\langle p^{j}\rangle})\right),

where

.
In particular, we obtain a sequence of inclusions between modules

(23)

\displaystyle E^{m+1}\subseteq E^{m}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq E^{1}\subseteq E^{0}=E.

Note that

forms an

-module via the natural inclusion

, and
\nabla_{\langle jp^{l}\rangle}
(for each

R^{l}
-linear endomorphism of

; we will abuse the notation by writing

for this restriction.
One may verify that the collection

(24)

-module structure on

.

1.5.

-cyclic vectors

Let

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of an

Definition 1.5.1
.

Let
(E,\nabla)
be a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module.
An element

of

is called an
m
-cyclic vector
of

if
there exists a positive integer

such that

(31)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\langle 0\rangle}(v)\left(=v\right),\nabla_{\langle 1\rangle}(v),\cdots,\nabla_{\langle n-1\rangle}(v)

forms a basis of the

-module

.
(In particular, a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module admitting an

Remark 1.5.2
.

Suppose that

and
\mathscr{R}
is non-logarithmic.
Then,
each
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(0)}
-module structure
\nabla

for every

It follows that a

Definition 1.5.3
.

(i)

A

-module
is a triple

(32)

\displaystyle(E,\nabla,v)

consisting of a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module
(E,\nabla)

and an
m
-cyclic vector

of it.
For a pinned

-module
(E,\nabla,v)
, the

of

is defined as the rank of the free

-module

.

(ii)

Let
(E,\nabla,v)
and
(E^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime},v^{\prime})
be pinned
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-modules.
An
isomorphism of pinned
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-modules
from
(E,\nabla,v)
to
(E^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime},v^{\prime})
is a morphism of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-modules

with

.

Let us describe several basic properties on pinned
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-modules:

Proposition 1.5.4
.

(i)

Any morphism of pinned
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-modules is surjective.

(ii)

Suppose that we are given a pinned
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module
(E,\nabla,v)
and a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}^{(m)}
-module
(E^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime})
.
Denote by
\mathrm{Hom}((E,\nabla),(E^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime}))
the set of morphisms of
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}_{\mathscr{R}}
-modules from
(E,\nabla)
to
(E^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime})
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{length}_{R}(\mathrm{Res}(\nabla))=\sum_{l=0}^{m}p^{l}\cdot\mathrm{length}_{R}(\mathrm{Res}(\overline{\nabla}^{l}).

3.4.

-cyclic vector

The following assertion helps us to understand the exponents of dormant pinned
\breve{\mathcal{D}}_{R}^{(m)}

Proposition 3.4.1

(E,\nabla)

\breve{\mathcal{D}}_{R}^{(m)}

Denote by
(\widehat{E},\widehat{\nabla})
the

-adic completion of

of

of
\widehat{E}

Proposition 6.2.1
.

Let

First, we shall suppose that the set
\phi(\{[0],[1],[\infty]\})

\phi

\mathrm{deg}(\phi)\geq\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{\infty}

(162)

\displaystyle-2\left(=2\cdot(\text{genus of $\mathbb{P}$})-2\right)=-2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\phi)+\sum_{x=0,1,\infty}(\lambda_{x}-1)\leq-(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{\infty})-3.

Next, suppose that
\phi(\{[0],[1],[\infty]\})

After possibly applying a linear transformation, we may assume that these two elements coincide with
\{[0],[\infty]\}

\displaystyle=(\iota_{2}\circ\alpha|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}/\mathcal{L}^{\natural}_{1}})\left(\frac{vg}{wu_{2}}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathcal{L}^{\natural}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=(\alpha|_{\mathrm{Coker}(\tau_{1})}\circ\iota_{1})\left(\frac{g}{u_{1}}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathcal{L}^{\natural}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha|_{\mathrm{Coker}(\tau_{1})}\left(\left(\frac{g}{u_{1}}\cdot u_{1},\frac{g}{u_{1}}\cdot 1\right)\text{mod}\ \mathrm{Im}(\tau_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=(\alpha|_{\mathrm{Coker}(\tau_{1})}\circ\pi_{1})((g,h)).

This shows the desired commutativity of (

).

and

In particular,
\alpha^{\prime}
comes from an isomorphism

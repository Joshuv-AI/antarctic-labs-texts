Gluon radiation from a classical point particle: Recoil effects
Isobel Kolbé, Mawande Lushozi

Abstract
The gluon radiation spectrum of a classical particle struck by a sheet of colored glass, is a key ingredient in understanding the distribution of energy and baryon density in the fragmentation region, particularly in the initial stages of heavy ion collisions. However, the currently known classical spectrum has a troublesome high-momentum tail Kajantie et al. 2019 ; Kajantie et al. 2020 . By comparing tree-level bremsstrahlung of a spin-less quark to the above-mentioned known result, we propose an interpolating formula that takes into account the recoil of the struck particle, and therefore produces the correct perturbative behaviour at high momentum of the radiated gluon.

Introduction

In the context of developing a description of the early stages of heavy-ion collisions, great success has been achieved by describing the initial condition of the collision in terms of the Color Glass Condensate (CGC), followed by a highly coherent phase of matter (called the Glasma), which produces rapid thermalization to the Quark Gluon Plasma
Lappi and McLerran 2006
;
Gelis et al. 2009
.
The CGC (and subsequent Glasma) picture relies on the realization that the density of gluons in a boosted nucleus becomes so large that their transverse separation becomes negligible.
Traditionally, this high density of charges is then used to motivate a separation of degrees of freedom that allows one to consider a classical evolution of the small-
x
modes in the presence of static large-
x
color charges.
Here, the density of color charge is very high, so for sufficiently low transverse resolution, one observes the charge as classical, i.e. belonging to a high-dimensional representation of the color group
SU(3)
.

The picture described above can be carried over to understand the fragmentation region, that part of phase-space in which the fragments from a collision have rapidity similar to that of the initial projectile or target
Anishetty et al. 1980
;
Kajantie and McLerran 1982
;
Kajantie and McLerran 1983
.
In a heavy-ion collider experiment, the fragmentation region corresponds to the very far forward (or backward) region and may offer access to higher density regions of the quantum chromo-dynamical (QCD) phase diagram
Kolbé et al. 2021
;
McLerran et al. 2019
.

A few years ago, McLerran set forth an interesting program to calculate properties of matter produced in the fragmentation region in a manner that generalizes the notion of the Glasma to include finite (net) baryon density
McLerran 2018
. Important first steps were taken by Mclerran
et al.
McLerran et al. 2019
in calculating the space-time evolution of baryon density, then by Kajantie
et al.
Kajantie et al. 2019
;
Kajantie et al. 2020
in calculating the resulting gluon radiation classically.
The latter result (hereinafter “the classical result”) is rather remarkable and elegant. However, the authors highlight that the result is only valid in the kinematic region where the recoil of the struck quark may be neglected. Indeed, it does not reproduce the perturbative
\sim 1/k_{T}^{4}
behavior at large gluon transverse momenta
k_{T}
(cf.
Dumitru and McLerran 2002
). In this paper, it is our intention to offer a remedy to the aforementioned shortcoming, in the form of an interpolating formula which is readily useful for phenomenology.

The literature on gluon radiation, both classically and in perturbative QCD, is extensive, but traditionally deals with computations in the lab frame (see for example
Kovchegov and
Mueller 1998
;
Kopeliovich et al. 1999
;
Kopeliovich et al. 2000
, at NLO
Li and Skokov 2021a
;
Li and Skokov 2021b
;
Li and Skokov 2022
, and references in
Kajantie et al. 2019
;
Kajantie et al. 2020
).
Extending such calculations to the target’s fragmentation region by boosting the kinematics is not straightforward. We do not attempt that here, but instead remain in the target’s rest frame as done by Kajantie
et al.
. When studying the space-time evolution of energy and the matter formed in the target fragmentation region, it can be very helpful to work in the rest frame of the target; it is in this frame that one can clearly understand baryon compression, as well as baryon stopping power in terms of the saturation scale
Q_{\text{sat}}

Anishetty et al. 1980
;
Kajantie and McLerran 1982
;
Kajantie and McLerran 1983
;

McLerran 2018

The way in which we arrive at our result builds on the work done in
Lushozi et al. 2020
, wherein it is observed that it is also possible to compute an “analogue” to the classical result in perturbative QCD (pQCD) (hereinafter “the pQCD result”) in order to account for the recoil of the struck quark. This was done in the hope that a comparison with the classical result would allow one to write down an ansatz for the spectrum of gluon bremsstrahlung that is able to both describe the non-perturbative physics of interactions encompassed in the framework of the Color Glass Condensate (CGC)
McLerran and
Venugopalan 1994
;
McLerran, Larry 2018
;
Kajantie and McLerran 1983
, and produce the correct perturbative behaviour in the high momentum (of the radiated gluon) limit.
Unfortunately, the inclusion of the physics of spin in the pQCD result
Lushozi et al. 2020
, in addition the the fact that the pQCD result is presented at the level of the amplitude squared, obscures any such attempt at comparison.

We solve the problem of reconciling the discordant known perturbative and non-perturbative (classical) results by making a pQCD-inspired modification of the classical result.
In order to see more clearly the relationship between the classical and pQCD results, we make two simplifications to the pQCD calculation of
Lushozi et al. 2020
, both of which bring the quark closer in nature to the classical point particle studied by Kajantie
et al.
Kajantie et al. 2019
;
Kajantie et al. 2020
;
Wong 1970
:
First, we remove the quark’s spin from the problem. Secondly, we treat the quark’s color charge as classical (
i.e.
, commuting). We are thus led to study, pertubatively, gluon bremsstrahlung in
scalar QCD in the limit of classical color charge
(we will make this statement more precise in
section

2.2
and
section

6
).
The result (hereinafter “the scalar result”) takes a simple form at the amplitude level, and is therefore easy to compare with the classical result, even at the level of the cross-section.
We argue that one may simply adjust the form of the classical result to match that of the scalar result.
What one is left with is then an ansatz for gluon bremsstrahlung in the fragmentation region (hereinafter “the ansatz”) that is able to fully take account of non-perturbative interactions, but also produces the correct high-frequency limit.
That is, the low-frequency limit of our ansatz is precisely the classical result, while the high-frequency limit falls off in the expected way as
\sim 1/k_{T}^{4}
.
This paper formalizes the proffered solution and carefully explores the relevant limits. We also offer a physics interpretation of the connection between the classical result and the ansatz.

What we achieve in the end is a generalisation of the gluon radiation spectrum calculated by Kajantie
et al.
,to include the effects of the particle’s recoil.

\displaystyle\kern-50.00008pt\times\left[\frac{k^{i}}{k_{T}^{2}+2(k^{-})^{2}}-\frac{k^{i}-\xi p^{\prime i}}{|\mathbf{k}-\xi\mathbf{p}^{\prime}|^{2}+\xi^{2}m^{2}}\right]^{2}\,\,.

(3.4)

An ansatz motivated by the scalar result

Comparing

to the perturbative result in

leads us to propose the following ansatz for gluon bremsstrahlung in the fragmentation region.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dN}{dyd^{2}k_{T}}\right|_{\text{An.}}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}}{16\,\pi^{3}}\frac{1}{1+\xi}\,4\int\frac{d^{2}h}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{S}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{h})\times

\displaystyle\kern-40.00006pt\times\left[\frac{h^{i}}{h_{T}^{2}+2(k^{-})^{2}}-(1+\xi)\frac{k^{i}-\xi p^{\prime i}}{|\mathbf{k}-\xi\mathbf{p}^{\prime}|^{2}+\xi^{2}m^{2}}\right]^{2},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\kern-50.00008pt=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}}{16\pi^{3}}\,4\int\frac{d^{2}h}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{S}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{h})\times

\displaystyle\kern-40.00006pt\times\left[\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., bulk}}+\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., brems}}\right]^{2}\,,

(3.6)

where, in

, we have performed a split in the same spirit as is done by Kajantie
et al.
so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., bulk}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\xi}}\left(\frac{h^{i}}{h_{T}^{2}+2(k^{-})^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle-\left.\frac{k^{i}}{k_{T}^{2}+2(k^{-})^{2}}\right)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., brems}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\xi}}\left(\frac{k^{i}}{k_{T}^{2}+2(k^{-})^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle-\left.(1+\xi)\frac{k^{i}-\xi p^{\prime i}}{|\mathbf{k}-\xi\mathbf{p}^{\prime}|^{2}+\xi^{2}m^{2}}\right)\,.

(3.8)

From the perspective of the diagrams in

, or that

, which is equivalent to
\xi\ll 1
. Notice then that, in the no-recoil limit in which

2.2
.

To see that
eq.

3.6
indeed cures the undesired high-
k_{T}
behavior of the classical result,
we will separate the bremsstrahlung, bulk, and interference contributions to the gluon radiation distribution, respectively given by

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dN}{dyd^{2}k_{T}}\right|_{\text{An., brems}}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}}{4\pi^{3}}\int\frac{d^{2}h}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{S}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{h})\left|\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., brems}}\right|^{2}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dN}{dyd^{2}k_{T}}\right|_{\text{An., bulk}}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}}{4\pi^{3}}\int\frac{d^{2}h}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{S}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{h})\left|\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., bulk}}\right|^{2}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dN}{dyd^{2}k_{T}}\right|_{\text{An., int}}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}}{2\pi^{3}}\int\frac{d^{2}h}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{S}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{h})\left(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., bulk}}\right)\left(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., brems}}\right).

It is helpful to express the above contributions in terms of the variables
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
and

, giving

\displaystyle\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., bulk}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-x}\left(\frac{h^{i}}{h_{T}^{2}+m^{2}x^{2}}-\frac{k^{i}}{k_{T}^{2}+m^{2}x^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., brems}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-x}\left(\frac{k^{i}}{k_{T}^{2}+m^{2}x^{2}}-\frac{k^{i}-xq^{i}}{|\mathbf{k}-x\mathbf{q}|^{2}+m^{2}x^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-x}\left(\frac{k^{i}}{k_{T}^{2}+m^{2}x^{2}}-\frac{k^{i}}{|\mathbf{k}-x\mathbf{q}|^{2}+m^{2}x^{2}}+\frac{xq^{i}}{|\mathbf{k}-x\mathbf{q}|^{2}+m^{2}x^{2}}\right)\,.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dN}{dyd^{2}k_{T}}\right|_{\text{An., int}}=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}}{2\pi^{3}}\int d^{2}h\,\tilde{S}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{h})\mathcal{M}^{i}_{\text{An., bulk}}\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., brems}}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}}{2\pi^{3}}\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{i}_{\text{An., brems}}\int d^{2}h\,\tilde{S}(-\mathbf{h})\left(\frac{h^{i}+k^{i}}{|\mathbf{h}+\mathbf{k}|^{2}+m^{2}x^{2}}-\frac{k^{i}}{k_{T}^{2}+m^{2}x^{2}}\right)

4
Conclusion

In this work we have presented a formula for the gluon bremsstrahlung spectrum from a single target quark struck by a sheet of colored glass. The extension to the fragmentation region of a nucleus-nucleus collision can be carried out in a straight forward manner as shown in
Kolbé et al. 2021
. We now have the final puzzle piece for the initial conditions of the fragmentation region of heavy-ion collisions, using the classical framework laid out by Kajantie
et al.
Kajantie et al. 2019
;
Kajantie et al. 2020
.

Our formula utilizes both the correct non-perturbative, low-
k_{T}
physics, inspired by the work of Kajantie
et al.
Kajantie et al. 2019
;
Kajantie et al. 2020
, as well as the correct perturbative high-
k_{T}
behaviour, inspired by the result for gluon bremsstrahlung by a classical scalar field, presented here in
section

2.2
.

In order to arrive at an ansatz for the spectrum of gluon radiation in the fragmentation region, we have used the form of the perturbative scalar result, at the amplitude level, to
inspire an appropriate modification of the associated classical result.
This approach, with a little more work, could possibly allow for the inclusion of spin effects. It would be interesting to see how the radiation formula is modified in that case.

We would like to formalize what we mean by “classical color charge”. First note that the usual identities (see for instance
Peskin and Schroeder 1995
) may be generalized to an arbitrary representation
R

C_{R}

1.

“Normalization Casimir”:

\displaystyle C(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}

(Fundamental rep.)

\displaystyle\overset{SU(3)}{\longrightarrow}C(N)=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle C(G)

\displaystyle=N

(Adjoint rep.)

\displaystyle\overset{SU(3)}{\longrightarrow}C(G)=3

“Two-Casimir”:

\displaystyle C_{2}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{2}-1}{2N}

\displaystyle\overset{SU(3)}{\longrightarrow}C_{2}(N)=C_{F}=\frac{4}{3}

\displaystyle C_{2}(G)

\displaystyle=N

(Adjoint rep.)

\displaystyle\overset{SU(3)}{\longrightarrow}C_{2}(G)=C_{A}=3

(6.2)

\displaystyle f^{abc}T^{b}T^{c}=

Derivation of the gluon multiplicity distribution

In this section we will derive
eq.

2.14
, the expression for the gluon multiplicity distribution.
We consider the process
p^{\mu}+P^{\mu}\rightarrow p^{\prime\mu}+P^{\prime\mu}+k^{\mu}
with
q^{\mu}
the exchange momentum.

We start by defining the gluon multiplicity distribution
\frac{dN}{d^{2}\mathbf{k}\,dy}
in terms of a suitable normalization of the cross section
\frac{d\sigma}{d^{2}\mathbf{q}\,dy}
for the process. We will take the Born cross section (the same scattering without radiation) to define the normalization, as in
Gunion and Bertsch 1982
:

\frac{d\sigma}{d^{2}\mathbf{k}\,dy}=\int d^{2}\mathbf{q}\frac{d\sigma_{\text{Born}}}{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}\frac{dN}{d^{2}\mathbf{k}\,dy}.

(8.1)

We need to compute the two cross sections. In the usual manner, the Born cross section is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{\text{Born}}}{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{flux}\frac{1}{4PM}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\text{Born}}\right|^{2},

(8.2)

where
P=P^{+}
and
M=\sqrt{2}\,m
for
m
the quark mass. The
flux
factor will cancel.

The bremsstrahlung cross section requires more work as the phase-space integral is non-trivial.
The expression that needs to be computed is

\displaystyle d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{5}}\frac{1}{flux}\left|\mathcal{M}\right|^{2}\frac{d^{3}P^{\prime}}{2E_{P^{\prime}}}\delta_{+}\left(p^{\prime 2}-m^{2}\right)\frac{d^{3}k}{2E_{k}}.

(8.3)

Now, notice that, since
P^{\prime}=P-q
, so that we may perform a change of variables

\displaystyle d^{3}P^{\prime}

\displaystyle=d^{4}P^{\prime}\delta^{(4)}\left(\vec{P^{\prime}}^{2}-m_{P^{\prime}}^{2}\right)\Theta(P^{\prime})

(8.4)

\displaystyle=d^{4}(P-q)\delta^{(4)}\left((P-q)^{2}-m_{P^{\prime}}^{2}\right)

(8.5)

\displaystyle=\int dq^{+}dq^{-}d^{2}\mathbf{q}\,\delta^{+}\left(\left(P-q\right)^{2}\right),

(8.6)

since
m_{P^{\prime}}\simeq 0
(because
P^{\prime}
is the largest scale), and
P^{\prime}
has only a “+”-component. Since the derivative of
x^{2}
is 0 at
x=0
, the delta function in
eq.

8.6
is poorly defined. However, the relevant part of
eq.

8.3
is, in fact,

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle\frac{d^{3}P^{\prime}}{2E_{P^{\prime}}}\delta^{+}(p^{\prime 2}-m^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2E_{P^{\prime}}}\int dq^{+}\,dq^{-}\,d^{2}\mathbf{q}\,\delta^{+}\left((P-q)^{2}\right)\,\delta^{+}(p^{\prime 2}-m^{2}).

(8.7)

We may do a change of variables for the second delta function, using the kinematics, given in
eq.

2.5
:

\displaystyle p^{\prime 2}-m^{2}=

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{m}{\sqrt{2}}+q^{+}-\frac{\mathbf{k}^{2}}{\sqrt{2}m}\frac{1}{x}\right)\frac{m}{\sqrt{2}}(1-x)

\displaystyle-(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{k})^{2}-m^{2}

(8.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}m(1-x)q^{+}+m^{2}(1-x)-\mathbf{k}^{2}\frac{1-x}{x}

\displaystyle-(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{k})^{2}-m^{2}.

(8.9)

Equation

8.9
is a function of
q^{+}
, and
p^{\prime 2}-m^{2}=0
has a root at
q^{+}_{0}
:

q^{+}_{0}=\frac{-m^{2}(1-x)+\mathbf{k}^{2}(\frac{1-x}{x})+(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{k})^{2}-m^{2}}{\sqrt{2}m(1-x)}.

(8.10)

Now, using the identity

\displaystyle\delta\left(g(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{\delta(x-x_{i})}{|g^{\prime}(x_{i})|},

(8.11)

for
x_{i}
the roots of
g(x)=0
, with
g(x)
the right hand side of
eq.

8.9
, we may write
eq.

8.7
as

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}P^{\prime}}{2E_{P^{\prime}}}\delta^{+}(p^{\prime 2}-m^{2})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{2E_{P^{\prime}}}\int d^{2}\mathbf{q}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,m(1-x)}

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}\mathbf{q}\,\frac{1}{4PM}\frac{1}{1-x}.

(8.12)

The last element of
eq.

8.3
is

\displaystyle\frac{d^{3}k}{2E_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{dx}{x}d^{2}\mathbf{k}=\frac{1}{2}\,dy\,d^{2}\mathbf{k}.

(8.13)

Substituting
eqs.

8.12
and
8.13
into
eq.

8.3
gives

\displaystyle d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{5}}\frac{1}{flux}\int d^{2}\mathbf{q}\,dy\,d^{2}\mathbf{k}\,\frac{1}{4PM}\frac{1}{1-x}\frac{1}{2}\left|\mathcal{M}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dy\,d^{2}\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{5}}\frac{1}{flux}\frac{1}{8PM}\frac{1}{1-x}\int d^{2}\mathbf{q}\left|\mathcal{M}\right|^{2}

(8.14)

We now have all the ingredients to compute the gluon multiplicity distribution
eq.

8.1
by substituting in
eq.

8.2
and
eq.

8.12
:

\displaystyle\frac{dN}{d^{2}\mathbf{k}\,dy}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{1-x}\frac{\left|\mathcal{M}\right|^{2}}{\left|\mathcal{M}_{\text{Born}}\right|^{2}}.

(8.15)

Finally, since
x=\frac{\xi}{1+\xi}
, we arrive at the expression for the gluon multiplicity distribution used in the current manuscript:

\displaystyle\frac{dN}{d^{2}\mathbf{k}\,dy}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi)\frac{\left|\mathcal{M}\right|^{2}}{\left|\mathcal{M}_{\text{Born}}\right|^{2}}.

(8.16)

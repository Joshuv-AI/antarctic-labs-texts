Asymptotic stability of rarefaction waves for compressible Navier-Stokes equations with relaxation
Yuxi Hu and Xuefang Wang

Abstract
. The asymptotic stability of rarefaction wave for 1-d relaxed compressible isentropic Navier-Stokes equations is established. For initial data with different far-field values, we show that there exists a unique global in time solution. Moreover, as time goes to infinity, the obtained solutions are shown to converge uniformly to rarefaction wave solution of p p -system with corresponding Riemann initial data. The proof is based on L 2 L^{2} energy methods. Keywords : Compressible Navier-Stokes equations; Rarefaction waves; Stability AMS classification code : 35B25, 76N10



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the system of one-dimensional isentropic compressible Navier Stokes equations in Lagrange coordinates as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v_{t}=u_{x},\\
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=\tilde{S}_{x},\end{cases}

(1.1)

with

\displaystyle\tau\tilde{S}_{t}+\tilde{S}=\mu\frac{u_{x}}{v},

(1.2)

where
v,u,p,\tilde{S}
denote the specific volume per unit mass, fluid velocity, pressure and stress tensor, respectively. The equations (
1.1
) are the
consequences of conservation of mass and balance of momentum, respectively. The viscosity coefficient
\mu
is assumed to be a positive constant as well as the relaxation parameter
\tau
. Moreover, we assume the pressure
p
satisfy
p(v)=av^{-\gamma}
with
\gamma>1
being the adiabatic index.

In the constitutive relation (
1.2
),
\tau
is the relaxtion time describing the time lag in the response of the stress tensor to the velocity gradient. In fact, even in simple fluid, water for example, the“time lag” exists, but it is very small ranging from
1 ps to 1 ns, see
[
14
,
19
]
. However, Pelton et al.
[
17
]
showed that such a“time lag” cannot be neglected, even for simple fluids, in
the experiments of high-frequency (20GHZ) vibration of nano-scale mechanical devices immersed in water-glycerol mixtures. It turned out
that, cp. also
[
2
]
, equation (
1.2
) provides a general formalism to characterize the fluid-structure interaction
of nano-scale mechanical devices vibrating in simple fluids. A similar relaxed constitutive relation was already proposed by Maxwell in
[
15
]
, in order to describe the relation of stress tensor and velocity gradient for a non-simple fluid.

The system (
1.1
) is coupled with the initial conditions

\displaystyle(v,u,\tilde{S})(0,x)=(v_{0},u_{0},\tilde{S}_{0})(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.3)

with

\lim\limits_{{x}\rightarrow{\pm\infty}}(v_{0},u_{0},\tilde{S}_{0})(x)=(v_{\pm},u_{\pm},0).

Here, we assume, for the far field conditions,
v_{+}\neq v_{-},u_{+}\neq u_{-}
in general. Furthermore,
(v_{-},u_{-})
and
(v_{+},u_{+})
are supposed to be the Riemann initial data which generates a centered rarefaction wave
for the following
p-
system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v_{t}=u_{x},\\
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=0.\end{cases}

(1.4)

That means, there exists a continuous weak solution with the form
(v^{r},u^{r})(x/t)
of
p-
system (
1.4
) with

\displaystyle(v^{r},u^{r})(0,x)=\begin{cases}(v_{-},u_{-}),\quad x<0,\\
(v_{+},u_{+}),\quad x>0\end{cases}

(1.5)

The main purpose of this paper is devoted to establish the asymptotic stability of the rarefaction wave
(v^{r},u^{r},0)
defined above. More precisely, we assume the initial data
(v_{0},u_{0},\tilde{S}_{0})
is close
to
(v_{0}^{r},u_{0}^{r},0)
in a suitable sense and the amplitude
\delta=|v_{+}-v_{-}|+|u_{+}-u_{-}|
of the rarefaction wave is sufficiently small. Then, we will show that there exists a unique global defined solution

(v,u,\tilde{S})
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) which approaches the rarefaction wave
(v^{r},u^{r},0)
uniformly in
x
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. That is, we have

\|(v-v^{r},u-u^{r},\tilde{S}\|_{L^{\infty}}\rightarrow 0,\quad\mathrm{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

See Theorem
2.1
for more details.

Note that, when
\tau=0
, the system (
1.1
), (
1.2
) reduce to the classical isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations, which read as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v_{t}=u_{x},\\
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=\left(\frac{\mu u_{x}}{v}\right)_{x}.\end{cases}

(1.6)

For such system, the well-posedness and asymptotic stability results have been widely studied with various initial data, see
[
7
,
8
,
12
,
13
]
. In particular, Matsumura and Nishihara
[
12
]
first established the asymptotic stability of rarefaction waves with small amplitude in 1986. Later, they
[
13
]
extended their results to large data cases. The half-space problem was conducted by Kawashima and Zhu
[
8
]
. A similar asymptotic stability result was also obtained for the full system of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations, see
[
9
]
.

The asymptotic stability of rarefaction waves were also shown for other related system, see
[
11
]
for the Broadwell model,
[
10
]
for a model system of radiating gas,
[
16
,
1
]
for a model of hyperbolic balance law.

One should note that it is not obvious that the results which hold for the classical systems also hold for the relaxed system. Indeed, and for example, Hu and Wang
[
5
]
and Hu, Racke and Wang
[
6
]
showed that, for the one-dimensional isentropic and/or non-isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes system, solutions exist globally with arbitrary large initial data for classical system, while solutions blow up in finite time with some large initial data for the corresponding relaxed system. A similar qualitative change was observed before for certain thermoelastic systems, where the non-relaxed system is exponentially stable, while the relaxed one is not, see Quintanilla and Racke resp. Fernández Sare and Muñoz Rivera
[
18
,
3
]
for plates, and Fernández Sare and Racke
[
4
]
for Timoshenko beams.

The paper is organized as follows. Some preliminaries and main theorem (Theorem
2.1
) are given in Section 2. In Section 3, following to
[

,

]
, we construct smooth approximations of rarefaction waves. The original problem is reformulated in Section 4 and there the statements of asymptotic stability results for the reformulated problems are given (Theorem
4.1
). The a priori estimates are also given in Section 4 (Proposition
4.2
). Last, the proof of the a priori estimates (Proposition
4.2
) are given in Section 5.

2.
Preliminaries and main results

Let’s first recall the definition of rarefaction waves of p-system (
1.4
) with Riemann initial data (
1.5
). For fixed
(v_{-},u_{-})(v_{-}>0,u_{-}\in\mathbb{R})
, we define a proper neighbourhood
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}_{v,u}^{2}:=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle R_{1}(v_{-},u_{-}):=\left\{(v,u)\in\omega|u=u_{-}-\int_{v_{-}}^{v}\lambda_{1}(s)\mathrm{d}s,u\geq u_{-}\right\},

\displaystyle R_{2}(v_{-},u_{-}):=\left\{(v,u)\in\omega|u=u_{-}-\int_{v_{-}}^{v}\lambda_{2}(s)\mathrm{d}s,u\geq u_{-}\right\}

and

\displaystyle RR(v_{-},u_{-}):=\left\{(v,u)\in\omega|u\geq u_{-}-\int_{v_{-}}^{v}\lambda_{1}(s)\mathrm{d}s,u\geq u_{-}-\int_{v_{-}}^{v}\lambda_{2}(s)\mathrm{d}s\right\},

where
\lambda_{1}(v)=-\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v)}
,
\lambda_{2}(v)=\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v)}
are eigenvalues of the matrix

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
p^{\prime}(v)&0\end{pmatrix}.

If
(v_{+},u_{+})\in RR(v_{-},u_{-})
, then Riemann problem (
1.4
), (
1.5
) admits a continuous weak solution of the form
(v^{r},u^{r})(x/t)
which is called centered rarefaction wave.

Let

I_{0}:=\|(v_{0}-v_{0}^{r},u_{0}-u_{0}^{r})\|_{L^{2}}+\|((v_{0})_{x},(u_{0})_{x})\|_{H^{1}}+\|\tilde{S}_{0}\|_{H^{2}}.

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(v_{+},u_{+})\in RR(v_{-},u_{-})
and
\delta=|v_{+}-v_{-}|+|u_{+}-u_{-}|
. Assume the initial data
(v_{0},u_{0},\tilde{S}_{0})
satisfy

\displaystyle(v_{0}-v_{0}^{r},u_{0}-u_{0}^{r},\tilde{S}_{0})\in L^{2},

\displaystyle((v_{0})_{x},(u_{0})_{x})\in H^{1},\tilde{S}_{0}\in H^{2}

and there exists a positive constant
\epsilon_{0}
such that if
I_{0}+\delta<\epsilon_{0}
, then the initial value problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) has a unique global solution in time satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(v-v_{0}^{r},u-u_{0}^{r},\tilde{S})\in C^{0}(0,+\infty,L^{2}),\\
(v_{x},u_{x})\in C^{0}(0,+\infty,H^{1})\cap L^{2}(0,+\infty,H^{1}),S\in C^{0}(0,+\infty,H^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,+\infty,H^{2}).\end{cases}

(2.1)

Moreover, this solution approaches the rarefaction wave
(v^{r},u^{r},0)
uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
:

\displaystyle\|(v(t,x)-v^{r}(x/t),u(t,x)-u^{r}(x/t),\tilde{S}(t,x))\|_{L^{\infty}}\rightarrow 0,\quad\mathrm{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

(2.2)

3.
Smooth approximation of rarefaction waves

We start from the inviscid Burgers equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w_{t}^{r}+w^{r}w_{x}^{r}=0,\\
w^{r}(0,x)=w_{0}^{r}(x)=\begin{cases}w_{-},\quad x<0,\\
w_{+},\quad x>0.\end{cases}\end{cases}

(3.1)

Let
w_{-}<w_{+}
, then (
3.1
) has a continuous weak solution
w^{r}(x/t)

\displaystyle w^{r}(x/t)=\begin{cases}w_{-},\quad x/t\leq w_{-},\\
x/t,\quad w_{-}\leq x/t\leq w_{+},\\
w_{+},\quad\xi\geq w_{+}.\end{cases}

(3.2)

This solution is called central rarefaction wave connecting the two constant state
w_{-}
and
w_{+}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w_{t}+ww_{x}=0,\\

(3.3)

where
\hat{w}=\frac{w_{+}+w_{-}}{2},\tilde{w}=\frac{w_{+}-w_{-}}{2}
,
\epsilon>0
is a constant.

is a constant satisfying

for
q>\frac{3}{2}
. Then we have the following lemma

Lemma 3.1
.

[

,
13
]

Let
w_{-}<w_{+}
, then there exists a smooth solution
w(t,x)
of system (
3.3
) satisfying

1)

,

2)
\forall 1\leq p\leq\infty
, there exists a constant
C_{p}
such that

\displaystyle\|w_{x}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{p}\min\left(\epsilon^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\tilde{w},\tilde{w}^{\frac{1}{p}}t^{-1+\frac{1}{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}w\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\min\left(\epsilon^{2-\frac{1}{p}}\tilde{w},\epsilon^{(1-\frac{1}{2q})(1-\frac{1}{p})}\tilde{w}^{-\frac{p-1}{2pq}}t^{-1-\frac{p-1}{2pq}}\right).

3) if

\displaystyle|w(t,x)-w_{-}|\leq C\tilde{w}(1+(\epsilon x)^{2})^{-\frac{q}{3}}(1+(\epsilon w_{-}t)^{2})^{-\frac{q}{3}},

\displaystyle|w_{x}|\leq C\epsilon\tilde{w}(1+(\epsilon x)^{2})^{-\frac{q}{2}}(1+(\epsilon w_{-}t)^{2})^{-\frac{q}{2}}.

4) if

\displaystyle|w(t,x)-w_{-}|\leq C\tilde{w}(1+(\epsilon x)^{2})^{-\frac{q}{3}}(1+(\epsilon w_{+}t)^{2})^{-\frac{q}{3}},

\lim\limits_{{t}\rightarrow{\infty}}\sup_{\mathbb{R}}|w(t,x)-w^{r}(x/t)|=0
.

4.
Reformulation of the problem

Let
(v^{r},u^{r})(x/t)
be centered rarefaction wave for system (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) which is given in (
3.4
), and let
(V,U)(t,x)
be the smooth approximations of
(v^{r},u^{r})(x/t)
. Note that
(V,U)
are the functions defined in (
3.5
) and satisfying (
3.6
). Now, let
\varphi=v-V,\psi=u-U,S=\tilde{S}-\mu\frac{U_{x}}{V}
be perturbations, then

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\varphi_{t}=\psi_{x},\\
\psi_{t}+[p(\varphi+V)-p(V)]_{x}-S_{x}=\mu\left(\frac{U_{x}}{V}\right)_{x}-g(V)_{x},\\
\tau S_{t}+S-\frac{\mu}{v}\psi_{x}=-\tau\mu\left(\frac{U_{x}}{V}\right)_{t}-\frac{U_{x}\cdot\varphi}{vV},\end{cases}

(4.1)

with

\displaystyle(\varphi,\psi,S)(x,0)=(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0},S_{0}),

(4.2)

where

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}=v_{0}-V_{0},\psi_{0}=u_{0}-U_{0},S_{0}=\tilde{S}_{0}-\mu\frac{(U_{0})_{x}}{V_{0}}.

(4.3)

For this reformulated problem (
4.1
)-(
4.2
), we have the following theorem of global existence and asymptotic stabiltiy.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(v_{+},u_{+})\in RR(v_{-},u_{-})
and
\delta=|v_{+}-v_{-}|+|u_{+}-u_{-}|
. Assume the initial data
(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0},S_{0})\in H^{2}
and let
E_{0}:=\|(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0},S_{0})\|_{H^{2}}
. Then, there exists a positive constant
\delta_{1}
such tht if
E_{0}+\delta<\delta_{1}
, the initial value problem (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) has a unique global solution in time satisfying

\displaystyle(\varphi,\psi,S)(t,x)\in C^{0}(0,+\infty,H^{2}),

(4.4)

\displaystyle(\varphi_{x},\psi_{x})(t,x)\in L^{2}(0,+\infty,H^{1}),\quad S(t,x)\in L^{2}(0,+\infty,H^{2}).

(4.5)

Moreover, this solution decay to
(0,0,0)
uniformly in
x
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
:

\displaystyle\|(\varphi,\psi,S)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\rightarrow 0,\quad\mathrm{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty.

(4.6)

To prove Theorem
4.1
, the key point is to show the a priori estimate of solutions to the problem (
4.1
), (
4.2
). To do this, we introduce the energy norm
E(t)
as follows

E(t)=\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|(\varphi,\psi,S)(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}(\|(\varphi_{x},\psi_{x},S_{x})\|_{H^{1}}+\|\sqrt{V_{t}}\varphi\|^{2}+\|S\|^{2})\mathrm{d}t.

The a priori estimate result is stated as follows.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
T>0
and
(\varphi,\psi,S)(t,x)
be a solution to the problem (
4.1
), (
4.2
) such that

(\varphi,\psi,S)(t,x)\in C^{0}([0,T],H^{2})\cap C^{1}([0,T],H^{1}).

Then, there exists a positive constant
\delta_{2}
which is independent of
T
such that if

\displaystyle E(T)+\delta<\delta_{2},

(4.7)

then the solution
(\varphi,\psi,S)
satisfies

\displaystyle E(t)\leq C(E_{0}+E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+\delta^{\theta})

(4.8)

for
t\in(0,T)
with
\theta=\min\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\}
.

We will give the proof of Proposition
4.2
in Section 5.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
. Firstly, we choose
\delta_{2}
such that
C\delta_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then, under the assumption (
4.7
) and using (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle E(t)\leq 2C(E_{0}+\delta^{\theta}).

(4.9)

Now, we choose
\epsilon_{0}
small enough such that

. Then, we get
E(t)+\delta<\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}
which closes the assumption

) by noting that
E_{0}+\delta<\epsilon_{0}<\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}
. Therefore, based on the local existence theorem and the a priori estimate (

), we can get a global solution in time with regularity (
4.4
) and (

) by the classical continuation methods.

Now, we show the convergence result (

). Firstly, by Sobolev interpolation theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|(\varphi,\psi,S)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|(\varphi,\psi,S)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(\varphi_{x},\psi_{x},S_{x})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\|(\varphi_{x},\psi_{x},S_{x})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|(\varphi_{x},\psi_{x},S_{x})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(\varphi_{xx},\psi_{xx},S_{xx})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

Thus, the convergence
\|(\varphi_{x},\psi_{x},S_{x})\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow 0
as

implies (
4.6
) immediately. Let
\Psi(t):=\|(\varphi_{x},\psi_{x},S_{x})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Then, by (

),
\Psi(t)\in L^{1}(0,+\infty)
. From the system (

), we can easily get
\Psi^{\prime}(t)\in L^{1}(0,+\infty)
. Therefore, we get
\Psi(t)\in W^{1,1}(0,+\infty)
which implies
\Psi(t)\rightarrow 0
as

. Thus the proof of Theorem

Finally, by using Theorem

Proof of Theorem 2.1.
Assume that
I_{0}+\delta
is suitable small where

I_{0}=\|(v_{0}-v_{0}^{r},u_{0}-u_{0}^{r})\|_{L^{2}}+\|((v_{0})_{x},(u_{0})_{x})\|_{H^{1}}+\|\tilde{S}_{0}\|_{H^{2}},

\displaystyle\|(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|(v_{0}-v_{0}^{r},u_{0}-u_{0}^{r})\|_{L^{2}}+\|(v_{0}^{r}-V_{0},u_{0}^{r}-U_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\leq I_{0}+C\delta,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0})\|_{H^{1}}+\|S_{0}\|_{H^{2}}\leq\|\partial_{x}(v_{0},u_{0})\|_{H^{1}}+\|\tilde{S}_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}(V_{0},U_{0})\|_{H^{1}}+\|\mu\frac{\partial_{x}U_{0}}{V_{0}}\|_{H^{2}}\leq I_{0}+C\delta.

Therefore, we derive that
E_{0}+\delta\leq I_{0}+C\delta
which can be sufficiently small. Therefore, by using Theorem
4.1
, we get a unique global solution
(\varphi,\psi,S)

\displaystyle\frac{v}{\mu}S^{2}-\frac{V_{t}\varphi S}{V}+(p(V+\varphi)-p(V)-p^{\prime}(V)\varphi)V_{t}\geq C(S^{2}+V_{t}\varphi^{2}).

,

\displaystyle M_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{\prime\prime}(\varphi+V)(\varphi_{x}+V_{x})\varphi_{x}\varphi_{xx}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi_{xx})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi_{x}^{4}+V_{x}^{2}\varphi_{x}^{2})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi_{xx})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(E^{2}(t)+\delta^{8}).

Similarly, we get for
k=0
,

\displaystyle M_{0}^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi_{x})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{2}V_{x}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi_{x})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\sup\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|V_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi_{x})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(E_{0}+\delta),

and
k=1
,

\displaystyle M_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi_{xx})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(V_{xx}^{2}+V_{x}^{4}+\varphi_{x}^{2}V_{x}^{2})\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi_{xx})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(\delta^{3-\frac{2}{q}}+\delta).

By using the dissipation of
S_{x}
, we have

\displaystyle M_{k}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\varphi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}S)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\varphi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+E_{0}+\delta^{\theta}).

M_{k}^{5}
and
M_{k}^{6}
can be estimated in the same way as in Lemme
5.2
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle M_{0}^{5}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\partial_{xx}U|+|U_{x}||V_{x}|)|\varphi_{x}|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi_{x}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{0}^{t}(\|\partial_{xx}U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|U_{x}|_{L^{4}}^{4}+|V_{x}|_{L^{4}}^{4})\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi_{x}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(\delta^{3-\frac{2}{q}}+\delta),

and

\displaystyle M_{1}^{5}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(\|U_{xxx}\|+\|U_{xx}\|_{L^{4}}\|V_{x}\|_{L^{4}}+\|V_{xx}\|_{L^{4}}\|U_{x}\|_{L^{4}}+\|V_{x}^{2}\|_{L^{4}}\|U_{x}\|_{L^{4}})\|\partial_{xx}\varphi\|\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{xx}\varphi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(\delta^{3-\frac{2}{q}}+\delta^{5-\frac{3}{q}}+\delta).

Note that
\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{x}g(V)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we get

M_{0}^{6}\leq\sup\|\varphi_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{x}g(V)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Besides, we have

\displaystyle M_{1}^{6}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|V_{xx}|+|V_{x}|^{2})|\varphi_{xx}|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi_{xx})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(\delta^{3-\frac{2}{q}}+\delta).

Combining the above estimates, summing up
k
from
0
to
1
, we finally get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\varphi_{x}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{x}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t+C(E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+E_{0}+\delta^{\theta}).

(5.10)

Now, we derive the dissipative estimates of
\psi_{x}
. Multiplying the equation by
\partial_{x}^{k+1}\psi
, we derive that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mu}{v}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\psi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=:\sum\limits_{{j}={1}}^{5}N_{k}^{j},

where

N_{k}^{1}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tau\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{k}S\partial_{x}^{k+1}\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,N_{k}^{2}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{k}S\partial_{x}^{k+1}\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

N_{k}^{3}=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\partial_{x}^{k}\left(\frac{\mu}{v}\partial_{x}\psi\right)-\frac{\mu}{v}\partial_{x}^{k+1}\psi\right)\partial_{x}^{k+1}\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

N_{k}^{4}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tau\mu\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{k}\left(\frac{U_{x}}{V}\right)\partial_{x}^{k+1}\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,N_{k}^{5}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{k}\left(\frac{U_{x}\varphi}{vV}\right)\partial_{x}^{k+1}\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

we shall show that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{{j}={1}}^{5}N_{k}^{j}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mu}{v}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\psi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{x}\varphi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t+C(E^{\frac{3}{2}}+E_{0}+\delta^{\theta}),

(5.11)

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{x}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\|\varphi_{x}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t+C(E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+E_{0}+\delta^{\theta}).

(5.12)

Combining (
5.10
) and (
5.12
) together, we get the desired result. Therefore, we only need to show (
5.11
) hold.

Firstly, using Lemma
5.2
and integrating by part with respect to
x
and
t
, we have

\displaystyle N_{k}^{1}\leq C(E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+\delta^{\theta}+E_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}S\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{k}\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

while, using equation
\eqref{5.6}_{2}
and Lemma
5.2
, and exploiting the same methods as above (estimates of
M_{k}^{j},j=2,\dots 6
, for example), we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tau\partial_{x}^{k+1}S\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{k}\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tau\partial_{x}^{k+1}S\left(-\partial_{x}^{k}(p^{\prime}(\varphi+V)\varphi_{x})-\partial_{x}^{k}((p^{\prime}(\varphi+V)-p^{\prime}(V))V_{x})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\tau\partial_{x}^{k+1}S+\mu\partial_{x}^{k+1}\left(\frac{U_{x}}{V}\right)-\partial_{x}^{k+1}g(V)\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\varphi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}S)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+\delta^{\theta}+E_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\varphi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+\delta^{\theta}+E_{0}),

which gives the estimates of
N_{k}^{1}
. Moreover, it’s easy to see that

\displaystyle N_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mu}{v}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\psi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{0}^{t}\|S\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mu}{v}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\psi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+\delta^{\theta}+E_{0}).

Now, we estimate the term
N_{k}^{j},j=3,4,5
. Note that, for
k=0
,
N_{0}^{3}
vanishes, and for
k=1
,

\displaystyle N_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mu v_{x}}{v^{2}}\psi_{x}\psi_{xx}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mu}{v}(\psi_{xx})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|V_{x}|^{2}+|\varphi_{x}|^{2})|\psi_{x}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mu}{v}(\psi_{xx})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(E^{2}(t)+\delta^{8}).

On the other hand, for
k=0
, using the equation (
3.6
) and Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle N_{0}^{4}+N_{0}^{5}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|g(V)_{xx}|+|p(V)_{xx}|+|U_{x}|^{2}+|U_{x}||\varphi|\right)|\psi_{x}|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mu}{v}|\psi_{x}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(E_{0}+\delta+\delta^{3-\frac{2}{q}}),

and for
k=1
, comparing with the estimates of
R_{1}^{6}
and
R_{1}^{7}
, we do have

\displaystyle N_{1}^{4}+N_{1}^{5}\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mu}{v}|\psi_{xx}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C(E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+E_{0}+\delta^{\theta}).

This finish the proof of the Lemma.
∎

Combining the results of Lemma
5.1
-
5.3
, the proof of the Proposition
4.2
is finished.

A note on the reducedness and Gröbner bases of Specht ideals
Satoshi Murai, Hidefumi Ohsugi, Kohji Yanagawa

Abstract
. The Specht ideal of shape λ \lambda , where λ \lambda is a partition, is the ideal generated by all Specht polynomials of shape λ \lambda . Haiman and Woo proved that these ideals are reduced and found their universal Gröbner bases. In this short note, we give a short proof for these results.



1.
Introduction

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over an infinite field
K
.
Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
and
{\rm Tab}({\lambda})
the set of tableaux of shape
\lambda
.
For a tableau
T\in{\rm Tab}({\lambda})
, its
Specht polynomial

f_{T}
is the product of all
x_{i}-x_{j}
such that
i
and
j
are in the same column of
T
.
The ideal
I_{\lambda}
of
S
generated by all Specht polynomials of shape
\lambda
is called the
Specht ideal
(or the
Garnir ideal
) of shape
\lambda
.
Such ideals are known to be related to various topics in mathematics including combinatorial commutative algebra
[
10
,
12
,
13
]
, algebra and combinatorics of subspace arrangements
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, graph theory
[
4
,
7
,
9
]
, equivariant cohomologies of Springer fibers
[
5
]
and symmetric system of equations
[
11
]
.
Haiman and Woo proved, in their unpublished manuscript
[
5
]
, two basic but important results on Specht ideals. They proved that these ideals are reduced and found their universal Gröbner bases.
In this short note, we give a short proof for these results.

We quickly explain the results of Haiman and Woo (undefined notation will be explained in the next section).
They actually consider ideals which are more general than Specht ideals.
Let
P_{n}
be the set of all partitions of
n
,
and let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a lower filter of
P_{n}
w.r.t. a dominance order
\lhd
.
We call the ideal

I_{\mathcal{F}}=\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathcal{F}}}I_{\lambda}

the
Specht ideal of a filter
{\mathcal{F}}
.
These ideals generalize usual Specht ideals, since
I_{\lambda}=I_{\{\mu\in P_{n}\mid\mu\unlhd\lambda\}}
.
Indeed, it is known that
I_{\lambda}
contains
I_{\mu}
when
\mu\lhd\lambda
(see
[
11
, Theorem 1]
).
Consider the natural permutation action of the
n
-th symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
K^{n}
.
For each point
\bm{a}\in K^{n}
,
the stabilizer subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
for
\bm{a}
by this action must be isomorphic to a Young subgroup
\mathfrak{S}_{\mu_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{S}_{\mu_{r}}

for some
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r})\in P_{n}
.
This partition
\mu
is called the
orbit type
of
\bm{a}
and will be denoted by
\Lambda(\bm{a})
.
For example,
\Lambda((4,0,2,4,2,4))=(3,2,1)
.
For
\mu\in P_{n}
we define
H_{\mu}=\{\bm{a}\in K^{n}\mid\Lambda(\bm{a})=\mu\}
. For any subset
\mathcal{G}\subset P_{n}

we define the reduced ideal

\textstyle J_{\mathcal{G}}=\{f\in S\mid f(\bm{a})=0\mbox{ for all }\bm{a}\in\bigcup_{\mu\in\mathcal{G}}H_{\mu}\}.

Now we state the result of Haiman and Woo
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Haiman–Woo)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset P_{n}
be a nonempty lower filter w.r.t. the dominance order and let
G_{\mathcal{F}}=\{f_{T}\mid T\in\bigcup_{\lambda\in{\mathcal{F}}}{\rm Tab}({\lambda})\}
.

(1)

I_{\mathcal{F}}=J_{P_{n}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}}
, in particular,
I_{\mathcal{F}}
is reduced.

(2)

G_{\mathcal{F}}
is a universal Gröbner basis for
I_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
(1) was proved by Li and Li
[
7
]
when
P_{n}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}
is a set of the form
\{\mu\in P_{n}\mid\mu\unrhd(m,1^{n-m})\}

and by Kleitman and Lovász
[
9
]
when
{\mathcal{F}}
is a set of the form
\{\lambda\in P_{n}\mid\lambda\unlhd(m,1^{n-m})\}
.
Also, in the latter case, the Gröbner basis result is due to de Loera
[
4
]
.

The proof of the theorem given in
[
5
]
is based on a careful analysis of the module
M_{v}=I_{\lambda}/I_{\{\mu\in P_{n}\mid\mu\lhd\lambda\}}
.
We give a short proof of the theorem based on induction on the numbers of the variables.

2.
Notation

A
partition
of a positive integer
n
is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})
with
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}=n
.
We write
P_{n}
for the set of all partitions of
n
.
For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})\in P_{n}
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{l})\in P_{n}
,
we write
\lambda\unrhd\mu
if
\lambda
is equal to or larger than
\mu
w.r.t. the dominance order,
that is,

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}\geq\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{k}\ \ \ \mbox{ for }k=1,2,\dots,\min\{m,l\}.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a subset of
P_{n}
.
We say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
lower filter
of
P_{n}
if it satisfies that, for any
\lambda\in{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mu\in P_{n}
with
\mu\unlhd\lambda
, one has
\mu\in\mathcal{F}
.
We define an
upper filter
of
P_{n}
similarly.

A partition
\lambda
is frequently represented by its Young diagram. For example
(4,2,1)
is represented as

. A
(Young) tableau
of shape
\lambda
is a bijective filling of the Young diagram of
\lambda

by the integers in
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
For example,

3

7

4

5

6

is a tableau of shape
(4,2,1)
. Let
{\rm Tab}({\lambda})
be the set of all tableaux of shape
\lambda
.
Recall that the Specht polynomial
f_{T}
of
T\in{\rm Tab}({\lambda})
is the product of all
x_{i}-x_{j}
such that
i
and
j
are in the same column of
T
(and
j
is in a lower position than
i
).
For example, if
T
is the above tableau,
then
f_{T}=(x_{3}-x_{4})(x_{3}-x_{6})(x_{4}-x_{6})(x_{2}-x_{5}).

If
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is a column stabilizer of
T
, we have
f_{\sigma T}={\rm sgn}(\sigma)f_{T}
.
In this sense, to consider
f_{T}
, we may assume that
T
is
column standard
, that is, all columns are increasing from top to bottom. If a column standard tableau
T
is also row standard (i.e., all rows are increasing from left to right), we say
T
is
standard
. Let
{\rm STab}({\lambda})
be the set of all standard tableaux of shape
\lambda
. It is a classical result that
\{f_{T}\mid T\in{\rm STab}({\lambda})\}
is a basis of the vector space spanned by
\{f_{T}\mid T\in{\rm Tab}({\lambda})\}
.
We also remark that the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module
isomorphic to this vector space is called the Specht module of
\lambda
, and fundamental in the representation theory of symmetric groups (especially in the characteristic 0 case).

It is clear from the definition of Specht polynomials that
f_{T}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=0
if there are distinct integers
i
and
j
such that
a_{i}=a_{j}
and both
i
and
j
are in the same column of
T
.
Using this fact, it is not hard to see the following property (see
[
11
, Corollary 1]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\lambda\in P_{n}
and
T\in{\rm Tab}({\lambda})
.
Then
f_{T}(\bm{a})=0
for any
\bm{a}\in H_{\mu}
with
\mu\not\!\unlhd\lambda
.
In other words,
f_{T}
belongs to
J_{\mathcal{F}}
where
{\mathcal{F}}=\{\mu\in P_{n}\mid\mu\not\!\unlhd\ \lambda\}
.

Let
<
be a monomial order on
S
.
We write
\mathrm{in}_{<}(f)
for the initial monomial of
f\in S
w.r.t.
<
.
Let
I
be an ideal of
S
.
A finite subset
G\subset I
is called a
Gröbner basis

of
I
w.r.t.
<
if the ideal
\langle\mathrm{in}_{<}(f)\mid 0\neq f\in I\rangle

is generated by
\{\mathrm{in}_{<}(g)\mid g\in G\}
.
A finite subset
G
of
I
of
S
is called a
universal Gröbner basis
of
I
if
G
is a Gröbner basis of
I

w.r.t. any monomial order on
S
.
See, e.g.,
[
6
, Chapter 1]
for the details on Gröbner bases.

3.
A short proof of Theorem
1.1

For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})\in P_{n}

and an integer
k>0
,
we write
\lambda+\langle{k}\rangle
for the partition of
n+1
obtained by rearranging the sequence
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}+1,\dots,\lambda_{m})
,
where we set
\lambda+\langle{k}\rangle=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m},1)
when
k>m
.
For example
(4,2,2,1)+\langle{3}\rangle=(4,3,2,1)
.
Since
\lambda+\langle{j}\rangle\unlhd\lambda+\langle{i}\rangle
holds for any
\lambda\in P_{n}
and
i\leq j
,
we have the following immediately.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset P_{n}
be an upper filter, and let
\lambda\in P_{n-1}
.
If
\lambda+\langle{j}\rangle\in\mathcal{F}
, then
\lambda+\langle{i}\rangle\in\mathcal{F}
for all
i\leq j
.

For any subset
{\mathcal{F}}\subset P_{n}
,
we define

{\mathcal{F}}_{k}=\{\mu\in P_{n-1}\mid\mu+\langle{k}\rangle\in{\mathcal{F}}\}.

Note that if
{\mathcal{F}}
is an upper (lower) filter, then so is
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{411,33,42,51,6\}
, where
411
means
(4,1,1)
.
Then
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}=\{311,32,41,5\},

{\mathcal{F}}_{2}=\{32,41,5\}
, and
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}=\{41,5\}
for
k\geq 3
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset P_{n}
be an upper filter, and let
f
be a polynomial in
J_{{\mathcal{F}}}

of the form

f=g_{d}x_{n}^{d}+\cdots+g_{1}x_{n}+g_{0},

where
g_{0},\dots,g_{d}\in K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}]
and
g_{d}\neq 0
.
Then
g_{0},\dots,g_{d}
belong to
J_{{\mathcal{F}}_{d+1}}

Proof.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})\in{\mathcal{F}}_{d+1}
and
{\bm{a}}\in H_{\lambda}
.
Suppose that
\alpha_{i}\in K
appears
\lambda_{i}
times in
{\bm{a}}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
,
where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
are distinct each other.
By Lemma
3.1
, we have
\lambda+\langle{i}\rangle\in{\mathcal{F}}
for all
1\leq i\leq d+1
.

If
m<d+1
, then
\lambda+\langle{d+1}\rangle=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m},1)
.
Thus, for any
\alpha\in K\setminus\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\}
,

({\bm{a}},\alpha)
belongs to
H_{\lambda+\langle{d+1}\rangle}
, and hence
f({\bm{a}},\alpha)=0
.
If
m\geq d+1
, then
({\bm{a}},\alpha_{i})
belongs to
H_{\lambda+\langle{i}\rangle}
for each
i=1,2,\dots,d+1
.
Thus we have
f({\bm{a}},\alpha_{i})=0
for any
i=1,2,\dots,d+1
.

In both cases, the polynomial

f({\bm{a}},x_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{d}g_{k}({\bm{a}})x_{n}^{k}\in K[x_{n}]

of degree
d
has at least
d+1
roots.
This tells that

f({\bm{a}},x_{n})
is the zero polynomial in
K[x_{n}]
.
Hence each
g_{i}({\bm{a}})=0
.
Thus we have
g_{i}\in J_{{\mathcal{F}}_{d+1}}
for all
i
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset P_{n}
be a nonempty lower filter.
Then

\textstyle G_{\mathcal{F}}=\left\{f_{T}\mid T\in\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathcal{F}}{\rm Tab}({\lambda})\right\}

is a Gröbner basis of
J_{P_{n}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}}
w.r.t. the lexicographic order
<_{\mathrm{lex}}

with
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}
.

Proof.

We write
\mathrm{in}(f)=\mathrm{in}_{<_{\mathrm{lex}}}(f)
for
f\in S
.
We note that, if
T
is column standard and if the number
i
is in the
d_{i}
-th row of
T
, then we have
{\rm in}({f_{T}})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{d_{i}-1}
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=P_{n}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}
.
The proof is by induction on
n
.
Since Lemma
2.1
tells
G_{\mathcal{F}}\subset J_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}
,
what we must prove is that, for any polynomial
f\in J_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}
,
{\rm in}(f)
is divisible by
{\rm in}(f_{T^{\prime}})
for some tableau
T^{\prime}
of shape
\lambda\in{\mathcal{F}}
.
The assertion is trivial when
n=1
.
Suppose
n>1
.
Let

f=g_{d}x_{n}^{d}+\cdots+g_{1}x_{n}+g_{0}\in J_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}},

where
g_{0},\dots,g_{d}\in K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}]
and
g_{d}\neq 0
.
Since
g_{d}\in J_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{d+1}}
by Lemma
3.3
and since
G_{P_{n-1}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{d+1}}
is a Gröbner basis of
J_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{d+1}}

by the induction hypothesis,
there is a tableau
T
of shape
\mu
with

\mu\in P_{n-1}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{d+1}

such that
{\rm in}(f_{T})
divides
{\rm in}(g_{d})
.
(We note that
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{d+1}\neq P_{n-1}
since
g_{d}\neq 0
and
J_{P_{n-1}}=\{0\}
.)

Let
\lambda=\mu+\langle{d+1}\rangle
.
Consider the tableau
T^{\prime}\in{\rm Tab}({\lambda})
such that the image of each
i=1,2,\dots,n-1
is same
for
T
and
T^{\prime}
.
Since
\mu\notin{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{d+1}
implies
\lambda\in{\mathcal{F}}
,
we have
f_{T^{\prime}}\in G_{{\mathcal{F}}}
.
We claim
{\rm in}(f_{T^{\prime}})
divides
{\rm in}(f)
.
The image of
n
for
T
must be in the
(p+1)
-th row with
p\leq d
,
which tells that

{\rm in}({f_{T^{\prime}}})=x_{n}^{p}{\rm in}({f_{T}})
divides
{\rm in}(f)=x_{n}^{d}{\rm in}(g_{d})
, as desired.
Thus
G_{\mathcal{F}}
is
a Gröbner basis of
J_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Clearly, the subset

\textstyle\left\{f_{T}\mid T\in\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathcal{F}}{\rm STab}({\lambda})\right\}\subset G_{\mathcal{F}}

is a Gröbner basis of
J_{P_{n}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}}
w.r.t. the lexicographic order
with
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}
. However, this is still very far from a minimal Gröbner basis.
For example, combining the above observation with
[
8
, Lemma 4.3.1]
, we see that

\textstyle\left\{f_{T}\mid T\in{\rm STab}({\mu}),\,\mu\unlhd\lambda,\,\mu_{1}=\lambda_{1}\right\}

is a Gröbner basis of
I_{\lambda}
, but this is far from minimal yet (unless
\lambda=(m,1^{n-m})
).

Now we are in the position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
G_{\mathcal{F}}
is a Gröbner basis of
J_{P_{n}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}}
, we have

I_{\mathcal{F}}=\langle G_{\mathcal{F}}\rangle=J_{P_{n}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}}
.
In particular,
I_{\mathcal{F}}
is reduced, and
G_{\mathcal{F}}
is a Gröbner basis of
I_{\mathcal{F}}

w.r.t. the lexicographic order with

x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}
.
We claim that
G_{{\mathcal{F}}}
is a Gröbner basis for any monomial order
<
.
By the symmetry on the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
,

G_{\mathcal{F}}
is a Gröbner basis of
I_{\mathcal{F}}
w.r.t. any lexicographic order.
Let
<
be any monomial order.
Assume that
x_{i_{1}}<\cdots<x_{i_{n}}
w.r.t.
<
.
Since each Specht polynomial
f_{T}
is a product of linear forms,
we have
{\rm in}_{<}(f_{T})={\rm in}_{<^{\prime}}(f_{T})
where

<^{\prime}
is a lexicographic order induced by the ordering
x_{i_{1}}<\cdots<x_{i_{n}}
.
This tells that
G_{\mathcal{F}}
is a Gröbner basis of
I_{\mathcal{F}}
w.r.t.
<
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The assertion in Theorem
1.1
(2) holds even for a finite field
K
.
In fact, if
K
is finite, then Theorem
1.1
holds if the coefficient field is an infinite field
K(t)
of rational functions in
a variable
t
.
Since the coefficients of each
f_{T}
are
\pm 1
,
the variable
t
will never appear
when we apply Buchberger criterion to
G_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Abstract
. Generalizing a result of Törnquist and Weiss, we study the connection between the existence of Σ 2 1 \varSigma_{2}^{1} Sierpiński’s coverings of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , and a cardinal invariant of the upper semi-lattice of constructibility degrees known as breadth.

Primary 03E15, Secondary 03E45

1.
Introduction

In 1919 Sierpiński proved that
\mathsf{CH}
, the Continuum Hypothesis, is equivalent to the existence of two sets
A_{0},A_{1}
covering
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and such that every line parallel to the
x
-axis intersects
A_{0}
in a countable set, and every line parallel to the
y
-axis intersects
A_{1}
in a countable set.
Three decades later, in 1951, Sierpiński obtained another geometric statement equivalent to
\mathsf{CH}
:
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the union of three sets
A_{0},A_{1},A_{2}
such that every line parallel to
\mathbf{e}_{i}
has finite intersection with
A_{i}
, where
(\mathbf{e}_{0},\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2})
is the canonical basis for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
These results were generalized to higher dimensions by Kuratowski and Sierpiński (see
[
28
]
for a detailed account of the history of these results): for all
n,k\in\omega

(1)

2^{\aleph_{0}}\leq\aleph_{n+k}\Leftrightarrow\exists A_{0},\dots,A_{n+1}\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{n+2}=\bigcup_{i\leq n+1}A_{i}\\
\text{ and }\forall{i\leq n+1}\,\forall{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n+2})}\,(\mathopen{|}A_{i}\cap\ell\mathclose{|}<\aleph_{k})\bigr).

Here and below
\mathcal{L}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the subset of all lines in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that are parallel to the vector
\mathbf{e}_{i}
, the
i
-th vector of the canonical basis for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Setting
n=0
and
k=1
, or
n=1
and
k=0
in (
1
) the two theorems of Sierpiński from 1919 and 1951 are obtained.

Several other geometric statements yield a bound on the size of the continuum.
For example
2^{\aleph_{0}}\leq\aleph_{n+k}
is equivalent to: “the number of
\aleph_{k}
-fogs it takes the cover the plane is at most
n+2
”, and similarly for
\aleph_{k}
-clouds and
\aleph_{k}
-sprays.
(See Section
5.1
for the definitions of fog, cloud, and spray.)

The
A_{i}
s in (
1
) are constructed using a transfinite induction of length
\mathopen{|}\mathbb{R}\mathclose{|}
, so their descriptive complexity is bounded by the complexity of the well-ordering of
\mathbb{R}
.
On the other hand, some of the
A_{i}
s are neither measurable nor have the property of Baire (Lemma
1
), and therefore they can’t be
\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{1}_{2}
(provably in
\mathsf{ZFC}
).
In Gödel’s constructible universe
\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
, there is a good
\varSigma^{1}_{2}
well-ordering of
\mathbb{R}
, and Törnquist and Weiss in
[
31
]
proved that
\mathbb{R}\subseteq\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
is equivalent to either one of the following statements:

\displaystyle\exists A_{0},A_{1}\in\varSigma^{1}_{2}\bigl(A_{0}\cup A_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\text{ and }\forall i<2\forall\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{2})(\mathopen{|}\ell\cap A_{i}\mathclose{|}<\aleph_{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle\exists A_{0},A_{1},A_{2}\in\varSigma^{1}_{2}\bigl(A_{0}\cup A_{1}\cup A_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\text{ and }\forall i<3\forall\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{3})(\mathopen{|}\ell\cap A_{i}\mathclose{|}<\aleph_{0})\bigr).

In other words, Törnquist and Weiss showed that by asking the
A_{i}
s to be definable in the best possible way (i.e.
\varSigma_{2}^{1}
) in both Sierpiński’s 1919 and 1951 results, we get the “strongest” version of
\mathsf{CH}
(i.e.
\mathbb{R}\subseteq\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
).

In this paper, we generalize their result.
In Section
2
, we introduce all notions involved, i.e. constructibility degrees, the breadth of an upper semi-lattice and Sierpiński’s coverings.
Then, in Section
3
, we study the relationship between the breadth of the upper semi-lattice of constructibility degrees and the size of the continuum (Theorem
12
).
In Section
4
we generalize Törnquist and Weiss’ result by showing that the existence of a
\varSigma_{2}^{1}
covering as in (
1
), with
n+k>1
and
k\leq 1
, is equivalent to the breadth of the upper semi-lattice of constructibility degrees having a certain finite upper bound.
Finally, in Section
5
, we conclude with some open questions.

Notation

Our notation is standard—see e.g.
[
18
]
.
When we treat a transitive set
M
as a model-theoretic structure, we use the language of set theory, identifying
M
with the structure
\langle M,\in\rangle
.
We write
M\prec_{1}N
to mean that
\langle M,\in\rangle
is a
\Sigma_{1}
-elementary substructure of
\langle N,\in\rangle
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Sierpiński coverings

The theorem of Sierpiński-Kuratowski (
1
) deals with sets
A_{0},\dots,A_{n-1}
such that
\bigcup_{i<n}A_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and such that
\ell\cap A_{i}
is small, for any line
\ell
with direction
\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
In the applications below, small means being at most countable, or thin (Lemma
1
).
Recall that a set of reals is
thin
if it does not contain a non-empty perfect set.

Sets
A_{i}
as in (
1
) form a
Sierpiński covering
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
As mentioned in the introduction, we let

\mathcal{L}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\coloneqq\mathopen{\{}{\ell\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}}\boldsymbol{\mid}{\ell\text{ is a line parallel to }\mathbf{e}_{i}}\mathclose{\}}.

A line
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is uniquely determined by a point
\mathbf{p}
in

H_{i}=H_{i}^{n}\coloneqq\mathopen{\{}{(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\boldsymbol{\mid}{x_{i}=0}\mathclose{\}}

the coordinate hyperplane orthogonal to
\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
We denote by
\ell_{\mathbf{p}}
the line determined by
\mathbf{p}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
n\in\omega
and suppose that
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\bigcup_{i<n}A_{i}
and

\forall i<n\ \bigl(\mathopen{\{}{\mathbf{p}\in H_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mid}{\ell_{\mathbf{p}}\cap A_{i}\text{ is thin}}\mathclose{\}}\text{ is comeager in }H_{i}\bigr).

Then, the
A_{i}
s cannot all have the property of Baire.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise, and fix
i<n
.
By Kuratowski-Ulam theorem
[
22
, Theorem 8.41]
the set of all the
\mathbf{p}\in H_{i}
such that
\ell_{\mathbf{p}}\cap A_{i}
has the property of Baire in
H_{i}
, is comeager in
H_{i}
.
Therefore, there is a comeager set (in
H_{i}
) of
\mathbf{p}
’s such that
\ell_{\mathbf{P}}\cap A_{i}
is thin and has the property of Baire (in
H_{i}
).
However, a set that is thin and has the property of Baire is necessarily meager.
Therefore, for comeager-many
\mathbf{p}
’s, the set
\ell_{\mathbf{p}}\cap A_{i}
is meager.
It follows again from Kuratowski-Ulam theorem that the set
A_{i}
is meager in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
However, this leads to a contradiction with Baire’s theorem
[
22
, Theorem 8.4]
, as it implies that
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, being the union of finitely many meager sets, is meager.
∎

A similar result holds with Lebesgue-measurability in place of the property of Baire.

2.2.
Constructibility degrees

The
constructibility preorder

\leq_{\mathrm{c}}
on
\mathscr{P}(\omega)
is defined by

a\leq_{\mathrm{c}}b\text{ iff }a\in\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[b]\text{, which is equivalent to }\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[a]\subseteq\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[b].

Let
=_{\mathrm{c}}
be the induced equivalence relation, that is

a=_{\mathrm{c}}b\text{ iff }a\leq_{\mathrm{c}}b\leq_{\mathrm{c}}a\text{, which is equivalent to }\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[a]=\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[b].

The quotient
\mathscr{P}(\omega)/{=_{\mathrm{c}}}
with the induced order is the set of
constructibility degrees
, and it is denoted by
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
.
If
\mathbf{a}\in\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
, then set
\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[\mathbf{a}]
to be
\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[a]
for some/any
a\in\mathbf{a}
.
Note that
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
is a partial order with minimum
\mathbf{0}={\mathscr{P}(\omega)}\cap{\mathord{\mathrm{L}}}
, every degree
\mathbf{a}
has size
(\aleph_{1})^{\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[\mathbf{a}]}\leq\aleph_{1}
, and
\mathopen{\{}{\mathbf{b}}\boldsymbol{\mid}{\mathbf{b}\leq_{\mathrm{c}}\mathbf{a}}\mathclose{\}}
, the set of all predecessors of
\mathbf{a}\in\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
has size
\leq(\aleph_{1})^{\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[\mathbf{a}]}\leq\aleph_{1}
.
Our official definition of
\leq_{\mathrm{c}}
takes place on
\mathscr{P}(\omega)
, but since the Cantor space and the Euclidean line
\mathbb{R}
are
\varDelta^{1}_{1}
isomorphic
[
24
, Theorem 3E.7]
, we could have used the true reals without any problem.
The order
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
is an upper semi-lattice as
\mathbf{a}\vee\mathbf{b}=\boldsymbol{[}a\oplus b\boldsymbol{]}_{\mathrm{c}}
for some/any
a\in\mathbf{a}
and
b\in\mathbf{b}
, where

a\oplus b=\mathopen{\{}{2n}\boldsymbol{\mid}{n\in a}\mathclose{\}}\cup\mathopen{\{}{2n+1}\boldsymbol{\mid}{n\in b}\mathclose{\}}.

More generally, if
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\omega\times\omega\to\omega
is a recursive bijection, and
a_{n}\subseteq\omega
, then letting

\textstyle\bigoplus_{n\in\omega}a_{n}\coloneqq\mathopen{\{}{\langle n,k\rangle}\boldsymbol{\mid}{n\in\omega\text{ and }k\in a_{n}}\mathclose{\}}

we have that
a_{i}\leq_{\mathrm{c}}\bigoplus_{n\in\omega}a_{n}
for all
i<\omega
.
Thus the order
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
is
\sigma
-bounded—any countable set of elements
\mathopen{\{}{\boldsymbol{[}a_{n}\boldsymbol{]}_{\mathrm{c}}}\boldsymbol{\mid}{n\in\omega}\mathclose{\}}
has an upper bound
\boldsymbol{[}\bigoplus_{n\in\omega}a_{n}\boldsymbol{]}_{\mathrm{c}}
.
On the other hand
\mathopen{\{}{\boldsymbol{[}a_{n}\boldsymbol{]}_{\mathrm{c}}}\boldsymbol{\mid}{n\in\omega}\mathclose{\}}
need not have a least upper bound, i.e.
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
need not be a
\sigma
-complete upper semi-lattice
[
32
]
.
Again in
[
32
]
, it is also shown that
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
needs not be a lower semi-lattice.

The structure of
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
is highly non-absolute, being very sensitive to the ambient model.
If
\mathord{\mathrm{V}}=\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
, then
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
is the singleton
\mathopen{\{}{\mathbf{0}}\mathclose{\}}
, while if
\mathord{\mathrm{V}}=\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[r]
where
r
is a Cohen real, then
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
has the size of the continuum and a rich structure
[
2
]
.

Adamowicz
[
30
]
has shown that for every constructible, constructibly countable and well-founded upper semi-lattice with a lowest element, there is a generic extension of the constructible universe in which
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
is isomorphic to the given upper semi-lattice—see
[
13
,
27
]
for stronger results and more discussion on this.

Even though there is not yet a general result
à la
Adamowicz for uncountable upper semi-lattices, there are some scattered, yet interesting, results in this regard: in the iterated perfect set model, also known as iterated Sacks model, the continuum is
\aleph_{2}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
is well-ordered of order-type
\omega_{2}

[
5
,
14
]
; Groszek
[
15
]
has shown that
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
can, consistently with
\mathsf{ZFC}
, be isomorphic to the reverse copy of
\omega_{1}+1
; see also
[
20
,
21
]
for similar results.

2.3.
Breadth of an upper semi-lattice

Let
(P,\preceq)
be a preorder and
F\subseteq P
, we denote by
{\downarrow}F
the set of the predecessors of elements of
F
, i.e. the set
\mathopen{\{}{q\in P}\boldsymbol{\mid}{q\preceq p\text{ for some }p\in F}\mathclose{\}}
.
Given a
p\in P
we write
{\downarrow}p
instead of
{\downarrow}\mathopen{\{}{p}\mathclose{\}}
.
Two distinct elements
p,q\in P
are incomparable if neither
x\preceq y
nor
y\preceq x
; an antichain of
P
is a subset of pairwise incomparable elements.
The next result is folklore.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose
(P,\preceq)
is a preorder such that
\mathopen{|}{\downarrow}p\mathclose{|}<\aleph_{\alpha}
for all
p\in P
.
If every antichain has size
\leq\aleph_{\beta}
, then
\mathopen{|}P\mathclose{|}\leq\max(\aleph_{\alpha},\aleph_{\beta})
.

Proof.

Construct by induction
I_{\nu}\subseteq P
for
\nu<\aleph_{\alpha}
so that
I_{\nu}
is a maximal antichain of
P\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\nu}{\downarrow}I_{\xi}
.
(Some of the
I_{\nu}
s maybe empty, and if
I_{\nu}=\emptyset
then
I_{\xi}=\emptyset
for all
\nu<\xi<\aleph_{\alpha}
.)
We argue that
P=\bigcup_{\nu<\aleph_{\alpha}}{\downarrow}I_{\nu}
, so that
\mathopen{|}P\mathclose{|}\leq\sum_{\nu<\aleph_{\alpha}}\mathopen{|}{\downarrow}I_{\nu}\mathclose{|}\leq\aleph_{\beta}\cdot\aleph_{\alpha}
.
Towards a contradiction, suppose there is
\bar{p}\in P\setminus\bigcup_{\nu<\aleph_{\alpha}}{\downarrow}I_{\nu}
.
Then
I_{\nu}\neq\emptyset
for each
\nu<\aleph_{\alpha}
, so by maximality there is
q_{\nu}\in I_{\nu}
comparable with
\bar{p}
; as
\bar{p}\notin{\downarrow}I_{\nu}
, then
q_{\nu}\preceq\bar{p}
.
As the
I_{\nu}
s are disjoint, then the
q_{\nu}
s are distinct, and they all belong to
{\downarrow}p
, against our assumption on
P
.
∎

If
(P,\preceq)
is an upper semi-lattice and
F
is a finite subset of
P
, we denote by
\bigvee F
the least upper bound of
F
.
All upper semi-lattices considered here have a least element
\mathbf{0}
, so
\bigvee\emptyset=\mathbf{0}
.
A subset
I\subseteq P
is called an
ideal
if it is a downward-closed (i.e.
{\downarrow}I=I
) sub-semi-lattice of
P
; it is proper if
I\neq P
.

Definition 3

(Ditor,
[
9
, §4]
)
.

Given an upper semi-lattice
(P,\preceq)
, a finite subset
F\subseteq P
is
redundant
if there is a
p\in F
such that
p\preceq\bigvee F\setminus\mathopen{\{}{p}\mathclose{\}}
, otherwise we call it
irredundant
.

The
breadth
of
P
is the supremum of the cardinalities of irredundant subsets of
P
.
If
p\in P
, we say that
P

has breadth at most
\kappa
above
p
if the sub-semi-lattice
\mathopen{\{}{q\in P}\boldsymbol{\mid}{p\preceq q}\mathclose{\}}
has breadth at most
\kappa
.

Observe that if
P
has breadth
n
, then
n=0
if and only if
P
is a singleton
\{\mathbf{0}\}
, and
n=1
if and only if
P
is a linearly ordered set with at least two elements, and hence
n\geq 2
if and only if
P
has antichains of size at least
2
.

The notion of breadth yields much sharper bounds than the size of the antichains.

Theorem 4

(
[
9
]
)
.

If
P
is an upper semi-lattice of breadth
n>0
and such that
\mathopen{|}{\downarrow}p\mathclose{|}<\aleph_{\alpha}
for all
p\in P
, then

(a)

\mathopen{|}P\mathclose{|}\leq\aleph_{\alpha+n-1}
, and

(b)

\mathopen{|}I\mathclose{|}<\aleph_{\alpha+n-1}
for every proper ideal
I
of
P
.

Ditor also showed that this cardinal bound is sharp when
n=2
and
\aleph_{\alpha}
is regular.

Theorem 5

(
[
9
]
)
.

For every regular
\aleph_{\alpha}
, there exists an upper semi-lattice
P
of breadth
2
such that
\mathopen{|}P\mathclose{|}=\aleph_{\alpha+1}
and
\mathopen{|}{\downarrow}p\mathclose{|}<\aleph_{\alpha}
for every
p\in P
.

Then, Wehrung improved the latter result by showing that the cardinal bound of Theorem
4
is sharp also for every
n>2
and every uncountable regular
\aleph_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 6

(
[
33
]
)
.

For every uncountable regular
\aleph_{\alpha}
, for every
n>0
, there exists an upper semi-lattice
P
of breadth
n
such that
\mathopen{|}P\mathclose{|}=\aleph_{\alpha+n-1}
and
\mathopen{|}{\downarrow}p\mathclose{|}<\aleph_{\alpha}
for every
p\in P
.

See Section
5.2
for more discussion on these resuls and related open questions.

3.
The breadth of constructibility degrees

In this section, we explore the relationship between the breadth of the upper semi-lattice of constructibility degrees and the size of the continuum.
Consider the following statement:

(
\star
)

\forall M\prec_{1}\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}\left(\mathopen{|}M\mathclose{|}=2^{\aleph_{0}}\Rightarrow M=\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}\right),

where
\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}\coloneqq\mathopen{\{}{x}\boldsymbol{\mid}{\mathopen{|}\TC(x)\mathclose{|}<\omega_{1}}\mathclose{\}}
is the set of all hereditarily countable sets.

The property (

⋆

‣
3
) may be interpreted as a kind of minimality principle: as soon as a
\Sigma_{1}
-elementary substructure of
\langle\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}},\in\rangle
has size of the continuum, it already coincides with
\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
.
This interpretation is supported by the following result.

Proposition 7
.

Assume (

⋆

‣
3
).
Then, there are no
\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
-generic Cohen reals.

We need the following theorem, known as
Lévy-Shoenfield Absoluteness Theorem
.

Theorem 8

(Lévy-Shoenfield
[
17
, Theorem 36]
)
.

For every
a\in\mathbb{R}
, if
\theta=\omega_{1}^{\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[a]}
, then

\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\theta}[a]\prec_{1}\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}.

Proof of Proposition
7
.

Suppose otherwise towards a contradiction.
Then, it is well-known that there exists a perfect closed set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
such that any finite
F\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is a set of mutually Cohen generic reals over
\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
.
It follows that
\omega_{1}^{\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[F]}=\omega_{1}^{\mathord{\mathrm{L}}}
, for any such
F
.
Now fix an
x\in\mathcal{C}
and consider the transitive set

M=\bigcup_{F\in[\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathopen{\{}{x}\mathclose{\}}]^{<\omega}}\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\omega_{1}^{\mathord{\mathrm{L}}}}[F].

By Lévy-Shoenfield absoluteness theorem,
\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\omega_{1}^{\mathord{\mathrm{L}}}}[F]=\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\omega_{1}^{\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[F]}}[F]\prec_{1}\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
for every
F\in[\mathcal{C}]^{<\omega}
.
Therefore,
M\prec_{1}\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
.
Clearly
\mathopen{|}M\mathclose{|}=\mathopen{|}\mathcal{C}\mathclose{|}=2^{\aleph_{0}}
, but
x\notin M
, which contradicts (

⋆

‣
3
).
∎

We next explore the relationship between (

⋆

‣
3
) and the breadth of the constructibility degrees.
We need the following well-known fact.

Lemma 9
.

For every
M\prec_{1}\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
the following hold:

(a)

M
is transitive.

(b)

\mathopen{|}M\mathclose{|}=\mathopen{|}M\cap\mathbb{R}\mathclose{|}
.

(c)

If
\mathbb{R}\subseteq M
, then
M=\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
.

Proof.

(a)
.
Since
M\prec_{1}\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
, we must have
\omega\in M
.
Moreover, since
\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}\vDash
“Every set is countable”, the same sentence holds in
M
.
Fix a nonempty
x\in M
, then there is a function
f\in M
such that

M\vDash f\colon\omega\to x\text{ is a surjection}.

Hence the function
f
is a surjection of
\omega
onto
x
also according to
\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
(and
\mathord{\mathrm{V}}
).
Therefore
x\subseteq M
.
Thus,
M
is transitive.

(b)
,
(c)
.
It is well known (e.g. see
[
29
, §VII.3]
, or
[
18
, Lemma 25.25]
, or
[
19
, Proposition 13.8]
) that the map
G
which, given any real that recursively encodes an extensional and well-founded relation on
\omega
, returns the Mostowski collapse of such relation, is
\Delta_{1}
-definable over
\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
.
Moreover,

\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}\vDash\forall{x}\,\exists{y}\,(y\subseteq\omega\text{ and }G(y)=x).

Since
M\prec_{1}\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
, the same sentence is satisfied by
M
, thus
(b)
and
(c)
follow.
∎

Proposition 10
.

The following are equivalent:

(a)

\mathbb{R}\subseteq\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
.

(b)

\mathsf{CH}+\eqref{eq:mincont}
.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b)
.
Since
\mathbb{R}\subseteq\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
, then
\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}=\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\omega_{1}}
and
\mathsf{CH}
holds.
Fix an
M\prec_{1}\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\omega_{1}}
.
By a direct corollary of Gödel’s Condensation Lemma
[
8
, Lemma 5.10]
,
M=\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\leq\omega_{1}
.
When
\alpha<\omega_{1},\ M
is countable, otherwise it coincides with
\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\omega_{1}}=\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
.
Therefore (

⋆

‣
3
) holds.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a)
.
Note that we must have
\omega_{1}^{\mathord{\mathrm{L}}}=\omega_{1}
, as otherwise there would exists an
\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
-generic Cohen real, against Proposition
7
.
By Lévy-Shoenfield,

4.
Definable Sierpiński coverings

The next theorem shows the connection between the existence of

Sierpiński coverings and the breadth of the upper semi-lattice of the constructibility degrees.
The case

has already been shown by Törnquist and Weiss in

Theorem 15
.

For every
n\in\omega
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}

n

\ell\cap A_{i}
is countable.

\varSigma_{2}^{1}

A_{0},\dots,A_{n+2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+3}
such that
\mathbb{R}^{n+3}=\bigcup_{i\leq n+2}A_{i}
and for all
i\leq n+2
, for all
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n+3})
,
\ell\cap A_{i}
is finite.

Before delving into its proof, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 16
.

Given a
\Sigma_{1}
formula
\upvarphi(x,y)
in the language of set theory, the set

\left\{{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\boldsymbol{\mid}{x\leq_{\mathrm{c}}y\text{ and }\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[y]\vDash\upvarphi(x,y)}\right\}

is
\varSigma_{2}^{1}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that our set is
\Sigma_{1}
over
\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}

[
18
, Lemma 25.25]
.
In other words, we need to show that there is a
\Sigma_{1}
formula
\uppsi(x,y)
in the language of set theory such that

\bigl(x\leq_{\mathrm{c}}y\text{ and }\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[y]\vDash\upvarphi(x,y)\bigr)\Leftrightarrow\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}\vDash\uppsi(x,y).

By Gödel’s Condensation Lemma, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}\cap\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[y]
,

\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[y]\vDash\upvarphi(x,y)\Leftrightarrow\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\delta}[y]\vDash\upvarphi(x,y)\text{, for some countable ordinal }\delta.

Then, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3)

\left(x\leq_{\mathrm{c}}y\text{ and }\mathord{\mathrm{L}}[y]\vDash\upvarphi(x,y)\right)\Leftrightarrow\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}\vDash\exists\delta\bigl(x\in\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\delta}[y]\text{ and }\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\delta}[y]\vDash\upvarphi(x,y)\bigr).

The sentence “

\Delta_{1}(x,y,\delta)
over
\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
.
Regarding the sentence “
\mathord{\mathrm{L}}_{\delta}[y]\vDash\upvarphi(x,y)

[
8
, Ch. 1, §9]
or as a relativization
[
18
, Definition 12.6]

\Delta_{1}(x,y,\delta)
over
\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
.
Hence our set is
\Sigma_{1}
over
\mathord{\mathrm{H}}_{\omega_{1}}
.

•

of

intersects
A

; if

, if

\sigma

•

\aleph_{k}
-spray

of

intersects

; if

Fogs were introduced by R.O. Davies in

Theorem 19

(Bagemihl, Davies)
.

For every
n\in\omega
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

2^{\aleph_{0}}\leq\aleph_{n}
.

(b)

\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be covered with
n+2
fogs with pairwise non-parallel directions.

(c)

\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be covered with
n+1

\sigma
-fogs with pairwise non-parallel directions.

Each

\aleph_{k}
-fog with direction
\mathbf{e}_{i}
, and the main theorem in
[
4

generalizes the result by Kuratowski and Sierpiński.
Next, we consider the problem of covering the plane with clouds.

Theorem 20

(Komjáth, Schmerl)
.

For every
n\in\omega
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

2^{\aleph_{0}}\leq\aleph_{n}
.

(b)

\mathbb{R}^{2}
is covered by
n+2
clouds with distinct, non-collinear centers.

(c)

\mathbb{R}^{2}
is covered by
n+1

\sigma
-clouds with distinct, non-collinear centers.

The notion of cloud was introduced by P. Komjáth who proved in
[
23
]
the implication
(a)
\Rightarrow
(b)
for all
n

n=1
, while the general case of
(b)
\Rightarrow
(a)
is from
[
25
]
.
Using
[
10

one can easily generalize these results to
\sigma
-clouds, and obtain the equivalence with

.

The plane cannot be covered with finitely many (
\sigma
-)clouds with collinear centers, so the non-collinearity assumption is essential.
But this is the only obstacle: for any line

From now on, let

\mathbb{R}^{2}

For every
n\in\omega

.

(c)

Next, we consider the problem of covering the plane with sprays.

Theorem 22

(de la Vega, Schmerl)
.

For every
n\in\omega
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

2^{\aleph_{0}}\leq\aleph_{n}
.

(b)

\mathbb{R}^{2}

\sigma

J. H. Schmerl introduced the notion of spray in

R. de la Vega in

26

The implication

\Rightarrow

As for Theorem

5.3.
Covering
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with constructible continuous functions

Let
X
be a set,
n\geq 1
and
f\colon X^{n}\to X
.
We say that a point
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n+1}
is
covered
by
f
if there is a permutation
\pi
on
n+1
such that

f\big(x_{\pi(0)},\dots,x_{\pi(n-1)}\big)=x_{\pi(n)}.

A family
\mathcal{F}
of functions from
X^{n}
to
X
covers
A\subseteq X^{n+1}
if every point of
A
is covered by some member of
\mathcal{F}
.

Abraham and Geschke
[
1
]
have shown that, for each
n\geq 2
, it is consistent with
\mathsf{ZFC}
that
2^{\aleph_{0}}=\aleph_{n}
and that
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is covered by an
\aleph_{1}
subset of
C(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
.
In the iterated perfect set model (in which
2^{\aleph_{0}}=\aleph_{2}
) the following stronger property holds
[
16
]
,
[
11
, Theorem 73]
:
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is covered by
C(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
, where
C(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
is the set of all continuous real functions coded in
\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
.

It is easy to see that, for every
n>0
, if
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is covered by
C(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
, then
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{c}}
has breadth at most
n
: indeed, for every
n+1
reals
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, there would be a constructibly coded continuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and a permutation
\pi
on
n+1
such that
x_{\pi(n)}=f(x_{\pi(0)},\dots,x_{\pi(n-1)})
, and thus
x_{\pi(n)}
would be constructible relative to
\bigoplus_{i\neq\pi(n)}x_{i}
.
Therefore, our next and last question is a more demanding version of Question
26
, and a positive answer would yield a strengthening of Abraham and Geschke’s result (at least for
n=3
).

Question 28
.

Is it consistent relative to
\mathsf{ZFC}
that
2^{\aleph_{0}}=\aleph_{3}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is covered by
C(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathord{\mathrm{L}}
?

Abstract
In this article, we prove the product of two CR sets is again a CR set. This gives a negative answer to [ 5 , Question 4.2.] . We will use combinatorial arguments to prove our result.



Keywords: CR-sets, product space,
IP_{r}^{*}
sets

1  Introduction

For any nonempty set
X
, let
\mathcal{P}_{f}(X)
be the set of all nonempty finite subsets of
X.

Arithmetic Ramsey theory deals with the monochromatic patterns found in any given finite coloring of the
integers or of the natural numbers
{\mathbb{N}}
. Here “coloring” means disjoint partition and a set is called “monochromatic” if included in one part of the partition. A cornerstone result in this field of
research is Van der Waerden’s Theorem
[
8
]
, which states that for any finite coloring of the natural numbers one always finds arbitrarily long monochromatic arithmetic progressions. One can show that if a set
A
contains arithmetic progressions of arbitrary length (A.P. rich in short), then if
A=B\cup C
is a 2-coloring of
A
, one of
B
and
C
is A.P. rich. This property is called partition regularity.

The notions of
piecewise syndetic
set have been extensively studied in Ramsey theory and its connection with the Algebra in the Stone-Čech Compactification of
{\mathbb{N}}.
For every
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}},
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
-n+A=\{m:m+n\in A\}.

A set
A
is said to be piecewise syndetic if there exists a finite set
F
such that for every finite subset
G
of
{\mathbb{N}}
, there exists
y\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
G+y\subset\cup_{t\in F}(-t+A)
. This can be shown that piecewise syndetic sets are partition regular. For general semigroup one can define this notion naturally. For details, readers can see
[
7
]
. In
[
2
]
, authors introduced a more general notion of large sets: combinatorially rich sets for commutative semigroups. Later in
[
5
]
, authors introduced the notions of combinatorially rich sets for general semigroups and proved that these sets are partition regular. It can be easily proved that these sets are A.P. rich (in commutative semigroups).

Definition 1.1
.

[
5
, Definition 2.2]

Let
(S,\cdot)
be an arbitrary semigroup. A set
A\subseteq S
is said to be a
combinatorially rich
set (a CR-set) if and only
if for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that whenever
F\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}(\,{}^{\hbox{${\mathbb{N}}$}}\!S\,)
with
|F|\leq k
, there exist
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

a\in S^{m+1}
, and
t(1)<t(2)<\ldots<t(m)\leq r
in
{\mathbb{N}}
such that
for each
f\in F
,

a(1)\cdot f\big(t(1)\big)\cdot a(2)\cdot f\big(t(2)\big)\cdot a(3)\cdots a(m)\cdot f\big(t(m)\big)\cdot a(m+1)\in A.

Call
r=r(A,k).

Using the algebra of the Stone-Čech compactification of discrete semigroups, in
[
6
]
, authors proved several large sets are preserved under taking cartesian products. Later combinatorial proofs of these results were recovered in
[
3
]
.
Inspired by these studies, in
[
5
, Question 4.2.]
, authors asked if there exists two infinite semigroups
S
and
T
, and two CR-sets
A,B
in
S
and
T
respectively such that
A\times B
is not a CR-set in
S\times T
. Here we show that such a possibility can’t occur. We prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

If
S
and
T
be two semigroups and
A\subseteq S
,
B\subseteq T
are two CR-sets, then
A\times B
is CR set in
S\times T
.

2  Proof of our result

A set
A
in
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({\mathbb{N}})
is said to be an
IP
set if there is a sequence
\langle H_{n}\rangle_{n}
in
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({\mathbb{N}})
such that
\max H_{n}<\min H_{n+1}
and
A=FU(\langle H_{n}\rangle_{n})=\left\{\bigcup_{n\in K}H_{n}:K\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({\mathbb{N}})\right\}.
A consequence of Hindman’s theorem
[
4
]
says that for every finite coloring of
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({\mathbb{N}})
, there exists a monochromatic
IP
set. For every
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
, set
B
is called an
IP_{r}
set if there exists sequence
\langle H_{n}\rangle_{n=1}^{r}
in
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({\mathbb{N}})
such that
\max H_{n}<\min H_{n+1}
and
B=FU(\langle H_{n}\rangle_{n=1}^{r})=\left\{\bigcup_{n\in K}H_{n}:K(\neq\emptyset)\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,r\}\right\}.
A set is called
IP^{*}
(resp.
IP_{r}^{*}
) if that set intersects with every
IP
sets (resp.
IP_{r}
sets). Let us recall the following lemma essential for our purpose.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
A
and
B
be
IP_{r}^{*}
set (resp.
IP_{s}^{*}
set) in
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({\mathbb{N}})
. Then there exists
l=l(r,s)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
A\cap B
is an
IP_{l}^{*}
set.

1
, Propostion 2.5]
.

We also need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(S,\cdot)
be an arbitrary semigroup, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
A
be a CR-set in
S
, and let
F\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({}^{\hbox{${\mathbb{N}}$}}\!S)
with
|F|\leq k
. Let

\Theta=\{L\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({\mathbb{N}})\!\!:L=\{t(1),t(2),\ldots,t(m)\}_{<}\text{ and }(\exists a\in S^{m+1})(\forall f\in F)(a(1)\cdot f\big(t(1))\cdot a(2)\cdots a(m)\cdot f\big(t(m)\big)\cdot a(m+1)\in A)\}.

Let
r=r(A,k)
, and
\langle H_{n}\rangle_{n=1}^{r}

be a sequence in
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({\mathbb{N}})
such that
\max H_{n}<\min H_{n+1}
for
each
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,r-1\}
. Then there exists
K\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
such that

\bigcup_{n\in K}H_{n}\in\Theta
. In other words, for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}},

\Theta
is an
IP_{r}^{*}
set, where
r=r(k)
.

Proof.

For each
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
, let
\alpha_{n}=|H_{n}|
and write

H_{n}=\{b(n,1),b(n,2),\ldots,b(n,\alpha_{n})\}_{<}\,.

Pick

d\in S
and for
f\in F
, define

g_{f}\in{}^{\hbox{${\mathbb{N}}$}}\!S
by,

g_{f}(n)=f\big(b(n,1)\big)\cdot d\cdot f\big(b(n,2)\big)\cdot d\cdots\cdot d\cdot f\big(b(n,\alpha_{n})\big)\,\text{ if }n\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\},

and
g_{f}(n)=d
otherwise.
Now
\{g_{f}\!\!:f\in F\}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({}^{\hbox{${\mathbb{N}}$}}\!S)
with
|\{g_{f}\!\!:f\in F\}|\leq k.
So pick
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
a\in S^{m+1}
, and

t(1)<t(2)<\ldots<t(m)\leq r
in
{\mathbb{N}}
such that

a(1)\cdot g_{f}\big(t(1)\big)\cdot a(2)\cdots a(m)\cdot g_{f}\big(t(m)\big)\cdot a(m+1)\in A
.
Let
K=\big\{t(1),t(2),\ldots,t(m)\big\}
. Then
K
is as required.
∎

Now we are in the position to prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:
.

Let
A\subseteq S
and
B\subseteq T
be two CR-set. Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
F\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}(^{\mathbb{N}}(S\times T))
with
|F|\leq k.
Let
G=\{\pi_{1}\circ f:f\in F\}
and
H=\{\pi_{2}\circ f:f\in F\}
. Then
G\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}(^{\mathbb{N}}S)
and
H\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}(^{\mathbb{N}}T)
with
|G|=|H|\leq k.
Now from Lemma
2.2
, there exists
r,s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\Theta_{1}=\{L\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({\mathbb{N}})\!\!:L=\{t(1),t(2),\ldots,t(m)\}_{<}\text{ and }(\exists a\in S^{m+1})(\forall f\in G)(a(1)\cdot f\big(t(1))\cdot a(2)\cdots a(m)\cdot f\big(t(m)\big)\cdot a(m+1)\in A)\}
and

\Theta_{2}=\{L\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}({\mathbb{N}})\!\!:L=\{t(1),t(2),\ldots,t(p)\}_{<}\text{ and }(\exists b\in T^{p+1})(\forall f\in H)(b(1)\cdot f\big(t(1))\cdot b(2)\cdots b(p)\cdot f\big(t(p)\big)\cdot b(p+1)\in B)\}
are
IP_{r}^{*}
and
IP_{s}^{*}
sets respectively. So
\Theta=\Theta_{1}\cap\Theta_{2}
is an
IP_{l}^{*}
set, where
l=l(r,s)
is coming from Lemma
2.1
.

Hence there exists
\{t(1),t(2),\ldots,t(k)\}_{<}
in
\{1,2,\ldots,l\}
,
a\in S^{k+1}
, and
b\in T^{k+1}
such that for all
f\in F
,

(a(1),b(1))\cdot f\big(t(1))\cdot(a(2),b(2))\cdots(a(k),b(k))\cdot f\big(t(k)\big)\cdot(a(k+1),b(k+1))\in A\times B.

This completes the proof.

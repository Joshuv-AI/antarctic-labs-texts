Constructing K − K- optimal designs for different Scheffé models
Haosheng Jiang, Jiali Chen, Chongqi Zhang

Abstract
To avoid multicollinearity in regression analysis, [ 12 ] proposed K − K- optimality criterion. By far the most popular models for modeling the response of a mixture experiment are the Scheffé models. However, there have been no reports about constructing K − K- optimal designs for mixture models. The paper constructs K − K- optimal designs for first-order and second-order Scheffé models. The analytical solutions for first-order and second-order Scheffé models are obtained. A series of numerical results and examples are given to illustrate the theory.

In regression analysis, multicollinearity refers to a situation in which two or more regressions are highly
linearly related. The effects of multicollinearity include
(i)

severe multicollinearity (see

3
K-
optimal designs for first-order and second-order Scheffé models

We consider the common linear regression model

y=\mathbf{f}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{(x)}\mathbf{\theta}+\varepsilon(\mathbf{x}),

(8)

where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{q})^{\mathrm{T}}
is a
q-
dimensional vector of predictors which in the design
space
\mathcal{X}\subset S^{q-1}
,
\mathbf{f(x)}=(\mathbf{f(x_{1})},\mathbf{f(x_{2})},\ldots,\mathbf{f(x_{p})})^{\mathrm{T}}

is
p-
dimensional vector known linearly independent regression functions,
\mathbf{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{p})^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
denotes the vector of unknown parameters
and
\varepsilon(\mathbf{x})
is assumed to be independent of
\mathbf{x}
and independent identically distributed
with a normal distributed with mean 0 and constant variance
\sigma^{2}
.
We assume that the experimenter can take
n
independent observations of the form
y_{i}=\mathbf{f(x_{i})^{\mathrm{T}}\theta}+\varepsilon_{i}(i=1,\ldots,n)
at experimental points
\mathbf{x_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{x_{n}}
.

An approximate design
\xi
is a probability distribution with finite support on the factor space
\mathcal{X}
and is represented by

\displaystyle\xi(\mathbf{w})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathbf{x_{1}}&\mathbf{x_{2}}&$\ldots$&\mathbf{x_{n}}\\
\omega_{1}&\omega_{2}&$\ldots$&\omega_{n}\\
\end{array}\right),

where
\mathbf{x_{i}}
denotes a support point at which a measurement is taken, and the weight
vector
\mathbf{w}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots,\omega_{n})
with the weights satisfying
\omega_{i}>0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}=1
.
The information matrix of a design
\xi
for model (
8
) as

\mathbf{M(w)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathbf{f(x_{i})}\mathbf{f^{\mathrm{T}}(x_{i})}.

Matrix
\mathbf{M(w)}
is
p\times p
, always symmetric and positive semi-definite.
One can then define the condition number of the information matrix as

\kappa(\mathbf{M(w)})=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{M(w)})}{\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{M(w)})},&\mbox{if}\ \mathbf{M(w)}\ \mbox{is}\ \mbox{nonsingular},\\
\infty,&\mbox{if}\ \mathbf{M(w)}\ \mbox{is}\ \mbox{singular}.\\
\end{cases}

(9)

where
\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{M(w)})
and
\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{M(w)})
are the largest and the smallest eigenvalues, respectively.

The following definition, due to
[
12
]
, provides the
K-
optimal criterion for a design belonging to
\Xi
, where
\Xi
denote the set of all designs with nonsingular information matrix on
\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A design
\xi^{*}\in\Xi
is called
K-
optimal design for the model (
8
) if it minimizes

\phi(\mathbf{w})=\frac{\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{M(w)})}{\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{M(w)})}

(10)

over
\Xi
.

By Definition
3.1
, the
K-
optimal design problem is given by

\begin{cases}\arg\underset{\mathbf{w}}{\min}\phi(\mathbf{w})\\
\mbox{subject}\ \mbox{to}:\mathbf{w}\geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}=1,\\
\end{cases}

(11)

where
\mathbf{w}\geq 0
means that each entry of
\mathbf{w}
is non-negative. Then
\xi(\mathbf{w}^{*})
is called a
K-
optimal design if
\mathbf{w}^{*}
is a solution to (
11
).
To construct
K-
optimal designs for the first-order Scheffé model, we firstly give the following Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 3.2
.

In the first-order Scheffé polynomial with regression function

\mathbf{f(x)}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{q})^{\mathrm{T}}

the vertices of
(q-1)-
dimensional regular simplex, whose vertices lie on the design
region
S^{q-1}
, constitute the support points of an optimum design on the regular simplex
S^{q-1}
.
The corresponding design
\xi(\mathbf{w})
has the weight vector with equal
components
\mathbf{w}=(\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{q},\ldots,\frac{1}{q})
as the optimal design.

Proof.

Let
\xi
be an arbitrary design with probability measure on the regular simplex
S^{q-1}
.
The proof for the case of
q=1
is clear.
We consider the case of
q\geq 2
.
For all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,q\}
, let
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{i},\ldots,X_{q})\thicksim\xi
.

\xi
is invariant with respect to permutations of the components.
It follows
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{i},\ldots,X_{q}),(X_{i},\ldots,X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})\thicksim\xi
.
So we have
\omega_{1}=\omega_{i},i=2,\ldots,q
,
i.e.
,
\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}=\cdots=\omega_{q}
.
By
\mathbf{w}=(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{q})\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{q}\omega_{i}=1
, we have
\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}=\cdots=\omega_{q}=\frac{1}{q}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

The
K-
optimal design
\xi
for the first-order Scheffé model (
5
) has
the weight vector
\mathbf{w}^{*}=(\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{q},\cdots,\frac{1}{q})
on
the vertices of the regular simplex
S^{q-1}

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
, the design
\xi

A mixture experiment in formulating color photograhic dispersion

In formulation experiments and blending experiments, each component in the mixture can be varied within
lower and upper constraints. When each component in the mixture is restricted by lower
bounds
0\leq L_{i}\leq x_{i},i=1,2,\ldots,q
or upper bounds
0\leq x_{i}\leq U_{i},i=1,2,\ldots,q
.
Transforming the restricted components to pseudo-components
can be used, that is, using

x_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{x_{i}-L_{i}}{1-\sum_{i=1}^{q}L_{i}},i=1,2,\ldots,q

or

x_{i}^{*}=\frac{U_{i}-x_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{q}U_{i}-1},i=1,2,\ldots,q.

The quantity
1-\sum_{i=1}^{q}L_{i}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{q}U_{i}-1
must be greater than zero so that the simplex in the
x_{i}^{\prime}
and
x_{i}^{*}
lies
entirely inside the simplex in the
x_{i}
.
The pseudo-component space by transforming the restricted components to pseudo-components makes it easier to see details of the response surface in the restricted region.
For example, the package
mixexp
of
\mathbf{R}
creates two contour plots of a quadratic model fit to the data of
[
19
]
’s Table 4.1.
The contour plot is made over the actual component space and pseudo-component space as shown in Figure
2
.

Comparison of contour plots on unrestricted and pseudo-component space.

As can be seen, there is much more detail in the restricted space on the right.

5
Concluding remarks

[
15
]
points that it can be hard to find
K-
optimal designs analytically
for second-order response models.
The paper investigated
K-
optimal designs for first-order and second-order Scheffé models.
The analytical solutions for first-order and second-order Scheffé models are obtained.
An illustrative example is shown for constructing
K-
optimal design for second-order Scheffé model
with each component is restricted by lower and upper constraints.

To compare the efficiency of designs, let
\xi_{D}^{*}
,
\xi_{K}^{*}
denote the
D-
optimal
and
K-
optimal designs for model (
2
), the
D-
efficiency of the design
\xi_{K}^{*}
with
information matrix
\mathbf{M}(\xi_{K}^{*})
relative to the design
\xi_{D}^{*}
with
information matrix
\mathbf{M}(\xi_{D}^{*})
is defined as

Eff_{D}(\xi_{K}^{*})=\Big(\frac{det\{\mathbf{M}(\xi_{K}^{*})\}}{det\{\mathbf{M}(\xi_{D}^{*})\}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
p
is the number of unknown parameters in the model (
2
). The
K-
efficiency of the
D-
optimal
design
\xi_{D}^{*}
with
\kappa(\xi_{D}^{*})
relative to the design
\xi_{K}^{*}
with
\kappa(\xi_{K}^{*})
is defined by

Eff_{K}(\xi_{D}^{*})=\Big(\frac{\kappa\{\mathbf{M}(\xi_{K}^{*})\}}{\kappa\{\mathbf{M}(\xi_{D}^{*})\}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\kappa(\mathbf{M})
denotes the condition number of matrix
\mathbf{M}
.
We find that
Eff_{D}(\xi_{K}^{*})=99.95\%
for
q=3
, and
Eff_{K}(\xi_{D}^{*})=99.98\%
for
q=3
.
Our designs are thus shown to be more efficient.

An interesting research problem is to investigate
K-
optimal designs for other
mixture models, such as Becker model, Draper-John’s model and so on. In addition, algorithm
to search
K-
optimal designs
for mixture models is also interesting and challenging research problem.
We look forward to exploring these two problems in future research.

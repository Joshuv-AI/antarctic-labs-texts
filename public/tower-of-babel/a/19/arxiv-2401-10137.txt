Universal adjointation of isometry operations using conversion of quantum supermaps
Satoshi Yoshida, Akihito Soeda, Mio Murao

Abstract
Identification of possible transformations of quantum objects including quantum states and quantum operations is indispensable in developing quantum algorithms. Universal transformations, defined as input-independent transformations, appear in various quantum applications. Such is the case for universal transformations of unitary operations. However, extending these transformations to non-unitary operations is nontrivial and largely unresolved. Addressing this, we introduce isometry adjointation protocols that transform an input isometry operation into its adjoint operation, which include both unitary operation and quantum state transformations. The paper details the construction of parallel and sequential isometry adjointation protocols, derived from unitary inversion protocols using quantum combs and the (dual) Clebsch-Gordan transforms, and achieving optimal approximation error. This error is shown to be independent of the output dimension of the isometry operation. In particular, we explicitly obtain an asymptotically optimal parallel protocol achieving an approximation error ϵ = Θ ⁡ ( d 2 / n ) \epsilon=\Theta(d^{2}/n) , where d d is the input dimension of the isometry operation and n n is the number of calls of the isometry operation. The research also extends to isometry inversion and universal error detection, employing semidefinite programming to assess optimal performances. The findings suggest that the optimal performance of general protocols in isometry adjointation and universal error detection is not dependent on the output dimension, and that indefinite causal order protocols offer advantages over sequential ones in isometry inversion and universal error detection.

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{C}_{I}[\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\otimes n}](\cdot)\otimes\outerproduct{0}{0}+\mathcal{C}_{O}[\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\otimes n}](\cdot)\otimes\outerproduct{1}{1},

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{adjoint}}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\coloneqq V_{\mathrm{in}}^{\dagger}\cdot V_{\mathrm{in}}\otimes\outerproduct{0}{0}+\Tr[(\mathds{1}_{D}-\Pi_{{\mathrm{Im}}V_{\mathrm{in}}})\cdot]{\mathds{1}_{d}\over d}\otimes\outerproduct{1}{1},

(23)

and define the figure of merit by the worst-case diamond-distance error:

\displaystyle\epsilon=\sup_{V_{\mathrm{in}}\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{iso}}(d,D)}{1\over 2}\Big\|\mathcal{C}[\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\otimes n}]-\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{adjoint}}\Big\|_{\diamond},

(24)

where

is the diamond norm
[

]
.
We can show that the minimum value of

is achieved by the protocol outputting the following quantum instrument (see Theorems
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{C}_{I}[\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\otimes n}](\cdot)&=(1-\eta)\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\dagger}(\cdot)+\eta{\mathds{1}_{d}\over d}\Tr[\Pi_{{\mathrm{Im}}V_{\mathrm{in}}}\cdot]+\alpha{\mathds{1}_{d}\over d}\Tr[(\mathds{1}_{D}-\Pi_{{\mathrm{Im}}V_{\mathrm{in}}})\cdot],\\
\mathcal{C}_{O}[\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\otimes n}](\cdot)&=(1-\alpha){\mathds{1}_{d}\over d}\Tr[(\mathds{1}_{D}-\Pi_{{\mathrm{Im}}V_{\mathrm{in}}})\cdot],\end{split}

(25)

using
\alpha
and
\eta

0\leq\alpha,\eta\leq 1
.
We call
\alpha
and
\eta
the detection error and the decode error, respectively.
Using the detection error and the decode error, the diamond-distance error is given by (see Appendix
)

\displaystyle\epsilon=\max\{\alpha,\eta\}.

(26)

We denote the minimal value of
\epsilon
in parallel, sequential, and general protocols by
\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}^{(x)}(d,D,n)
for
x=\mathrm{PAR}
(parallel),
x=\mathrm{SEQ}
(sequential), and
x=\mathrm{GEN}
(general), respectively.

3.1.2
Schur-Weyl duality

We introduce the Schur-Weyl duality as follows. We consider representations of the special unitary group
\mathbb{U}(d)
and the permutation group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on a
n
-fold Hilbert space
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}(d)\ni U

\displaystyle\mapsto U^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n},

(50)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ni\pi

\displaystyle\mapsto P_{\pi}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n},

(51)

where
P_{\pi}
is a permutation operator on
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
defined by

\displaystyle P_{\pi}(\ket{\psi_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\ket{\psi_n})\coloneqq\ket{\psi_{\pi^{-1}(1)}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\ket{\psi_{\pi^{-1}(n)}}\quad\forall\ket{\psi_1},\ldots,\ket{\psi_n}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

(52)

The Schur-Weyl duality asserts a simultaneous irreducible decomposition of the two representations
U^{\otimes n}
and
P_{\pi}
given by

\displaystyle(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\mathbb{Y}^{d}_{n}}\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(d)}\otimes\mathcal{S}_{\mu},

(53)

\displaystyle U^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\mathbb{Y}^{d}_{n}}(U_{\mu})_{\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(d)}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mu}},

(54)

\displaystyle P_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\mathbb{Y}^{d}_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(d)}}\otimes(\pi_{\mu})_{\mathcal{S}_{\mu}},

(55)

where
\mu
runs in the set of Young diagrams with
n
boxes whose depth is less than or equal to
d
, denoted by
\mathbb{Y}^{d}_{n}
, and
U_{\mu}
and
\pi_{\mu}
are irreducible representations of
\mathbb{U}(d)
and
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on the linear spaces
\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(d)}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
, respectively.
5
5

5

The superscript
d
is put on
\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(d)}
since the properties of the representation space
\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(d)}
, e.g., the dimension, depends on the local dimension
d
. On the other hand, we do not put the superscript
d
on
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
like
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}^{(d)}
since the representation space
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}^{(d)}
is automorphic to
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}^{(d^{\prime})}
for an arbitrary local dimension
d^{\prime}
.

We denote the dimensions of the irreducible representation spaces
\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(d)}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
by
m_{\mu}^{(d)}
and
d_{\mu}
, respectively.
6
6

6

The dimension of
\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(d)}
is denoted by

since
\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(d)}
is the multiplicity space corresponding to the irreducible representation space
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
[see Eq. (
53
)].
They are given by
[
ceccherini2010representation
]

\displaystyle d_{\mu}

\displaystyle={n!\over\mathrm{hook}(\mu)},

(56)

\displaystyle m_{\mu}^{(d)}

\displaystyle={\prod_{(i,j)\in\mu}(d+j-i)\over\text{hook}(\mu)},

(57)

where
\mathrm{hook}(\mu)
for
\mu\in\mathbb{Y}^{d}_{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{hook}(\mu)=\prod_{(i,j)\in\mu}(\mu_{i}+\mu^{\prime}_{j}-i-j+1),

(58)

and

is the coordinate of a box in the Young diagram
\mu
such that

represents the row number running from bottom to top and

represents the column number running from left to right. The numbers
\mu_{i}
and
\mu^{\prime}_{j}
are the numbers of boxes in the
i
-th row and the
j
-th column, respectively.
The
n
-fold isometry operator

\displaystyle V^{\otimes n}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\mathbb{Y}^{d}_{n}}(V_{\mu})_{\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(d)}\to\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(D)}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mu}},

(59)

where

is an isometry operator, as shown in Ref.
[
yoshida2023universal
]

Due to Schur’s lemma, any operator commuting with
U^{\otimes n}
for all

can be written as a linear combination of the operators

defined by

\displaystyle E^{\mu,d}_{ij}\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{(d)}}\otimes\outerproduct{\mu, i}{\mu, j}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mu}}

(60)

for

, where

is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\Tr E^{\mu,d}_{ij}=m_{\mu}^{(d)}\delta_{ij},\;\;\;E^{\mu,d}_{ij}E^{\nu,d}_{kl}=\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{jk}E^{\mu,d}_{il},

(61)

where

is Kronecker’s delta defined by

\delta_{ij}=0

In particular,
\{m_{\mu}^{(d)-1/2}E^{\mu,d}_{ij}\}

under the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{Y}^{d}_{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{\mu}}{\Tr(E^{\mu,d}_{ji} \rho)\over m_{\mu}^{(d)}}E^{\mu,d}_{ij}.

(62)

(63)

(64)

bacon2007quantum

\mu

For instance, for

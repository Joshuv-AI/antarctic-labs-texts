1.
Introduction

In 1926, Khintchine
[
Khi26
]
introduced the notion of singularity in the sense of Diophantine approximation.
Recall that a vector
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be
singular
if, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
T_{0}>1
such that for all
T>T_{0}

the system of inequalities

(1.1)

{\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|qx_{i}-p_{i}|^{d}<\frac{\epsilon}{T}\quad\text{and}\quad 0<q<T}

admits an integer solution
(\mathbf{p},q)=(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}
.

The name
singular
is derived from the fact that the set of singular vectors is a Lebesgue null set.
On the other hand, the computation of the Hausdorff dimension of the set of singular vectors, or more generally singular matrices, has been a challenge
until the breakthrough
[
DFSU24
]
using a variational principle in the parametric geometry of numbers.
Historically, the first breakthrough was made in
[
Che11
]
, where it is proved that the Hausdorff dimension of the set of
2
-dimensional singular vectors is
4/3
.
This result was extended to the
d
-dimensional singular vectors in
[
CC16
]
, where the authors proved that the set of
d
-dimensional singular vectors has Hausdorff dimension
d^{2}/(d+1)
.
For general
m\times n
singular matrices, it was proved in
[
KKLM17
]
that the Hausdorff dimension of
m\times n
singular matrices is at most

mn(1-\frac{1}{m+n})
, and finally, it was shown in
[
DFSU24
]
that the upper bound is sharp.

In this article, we consider the weighted version of the singularity as follows:
Let
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{d})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{d}
be a
d
-tuple of positive real numbers such that
\sum_{i}w_{i}=1
and
w_{1}\geq\cdots\geq w_{d}
.
We say that a vector
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
w
-singular
if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
T_{0}>1
such that for all
T>T_{0}

the system of inequalities

(1.2)

{\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|qx_{i}-p_{i}|^{\frac{1}{w_{i}}}<\frac{\epsilon}{T}\quad\text{and}\quad 0<q<T}

admits an integer solution
(\mathbf{p},q)=(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}
. Denote by
\operatorname{Sing}(w)
the set of
w
-singular vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Here and hereafter, we always assume that the weight vector
w
satisfies the above assumption.

Note that if all weights have the same value, that is,
w_{i}=1/d
for all
i=1,\dots,d
, then the weighted system (
1.2
) is nothing but the unweighted system
(
1.1
).

In the weighted setting, it was shown in
[
LSST20
]
that the set of
2
-dimensional
w
-singular vectors has Hausdorff dimension
2-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}}
.
The aim of the present article is to extend this
2
-dimensional result to higher dimensional cases regarding the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension. Our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
d\geq 2
,

\dim\operatorname{Sing}(w)\geq d-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}},

where
\dim
refers to the Hausdorff dimension.

One of the main ingredients of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is Dani’s correspondence, which means that
w
-singular vectors correspond
to certain divergent trajectories in the space
\mathcal{L}_{d+1}
of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. More precisely,
let
a_{t}:=\operatorname{diag}\left(e^{w_{1}t},\dots,e^{w_{d}t},e^{-1}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{R})
and let

h(x):=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{d}&x\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
I_{d}
is the
d\times d
identity matrix.
Then
x
is
w
-singular if and only if the diagonal orbit
\left(a_{t}h(x)\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is divergent.

Our method for the proof of Theorem
1.1
is basically an extension of the method in
[
LSST20
]
, hence we also have the following result
as in
[
LSST20
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{d+1}
and any nonempty open subset
U
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Hausdorff dimension of the set

\{x\in U:\left(a_{t}h(x)\Lambda\right)_{t\geq 0}\text{ is divergent}\}

is at least
d-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}}
.

Theorem
1.2
implies the following corollary as in
[
LSST20
, Corollary 1.6]
(see also
[
GS20
,
AGMS21
]
).

Corollary 1.3
.

The Hausdorff dimension of the set

\{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{d+1}:\left(a_{t}\Lambda\right)_{t\geq 0}\text{ is divergent}\}

is at least
\dim\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{R})-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}}=(d+1)^{2}-1-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}}
.

Recently, Solan
[
Sol
]
established a variational principle in the parametric geometry of numbers for general flows. Following his notations,
we consider the following two subgroups:

\begin{split}H&=\{g\in\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{R}):a_{-t}ga_{t}\to I_{d+1}\text{ as }t\to\infty\},\\
H^{\prime}&=\{h(x)\in\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{R}):x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}.\end{split}

Note that
H
is the unstable horospherical subgroup of
a_{1}
. In the unweighted setting (
w_{1}=\dots=w_{d}
), the two subgroups
H
and
H^{\prime}
are the same, but in general,
H
is bigger than
H^{\prime}
.
One of the applications of the variational principle for general flows in
[
Sol
]
is to give an upper bound of the Hausdorff dimension of the set

\operatorname{Sing}(H,\Lambda;a_{t})=\{h\in H:\left(a_{t}h\Lambda\right)_{t\geq 0}\text{ is divergent}\}.

More precisely,
[
Sol
, Corollary 2.34]
implies that the Hausdorff dimension of
\operatorname{Sing}(H,\Lambda;a_{t})

is at most
\mathrm{dim}~H-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}}
.
On the other hand, Theorem
1.2
implies that the Hausdorff dimension of
\operatorname{Sing}(H,\Lambda;a_{t})
is at least

\dim H-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}}
, hence we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

The Hausdorff dimension of
\operatorname{Sing}(H,\Lambda;a_{t})

is
\dim H-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}}
.

Structure of the paper.
In Section
2
, we first recall fractal structures and self-affine structures. Then we estimate the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension of the associated fractal set. In Section

, we generalize the lattice point counting results in
[
LSST20
, Section 3]
to higher dimensional cases. In Section
4
, we construct a fractal set contained in the set of weighted singular vectors and prove Theorem
1.1
by estimating the Hausdorff dimension of the fractal set.

2.
Fractal sutructure and Hausdorff dimension

2.1.
Fractal structure

A
tree

\mathcal{T}
is a connected graph without cycles.
If we take a vertex
\tau_{0}
and fix it (we call it a
root
), then
\mathcal{T}
is a
rooted tree
.
In this paper, we identify
\mathcal{T}
with the set of vertices of
\mathcal{T}
.
It can be checked directly from the definition of
\mathcal{T}
that any
\tau\in\mathcal{T}
can be joined to
\tau_{0}
by a unique geodesic edge path.
We define the
height
of
\tau
as the length of the geodesic edge path joining
\tau
and
\tau_{0}
. Denote the set of vertices of height
n
by
\mathcal{T}_{n}
.
For any
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
, there exists a unique
\tau_{n-1}\in\mathcal{T}_{n-1}
such that
\tau
and
\tau_{n-1}
are adjacent.
In this case, we say that
\tau
is a
son
of
\tau_{n-1}
. Denote the set of all sons of
\tau_{n-1}
by
\mathcal{T}(\tau_{n-1})
.
The
boundary
of
\mathcal{T}
, denoted by
\partial\mathcal{T}
, is the set of all sequences
\{\tau_{n}\}=\{\tau_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
where
\tau_{n}
is a son of
\tau_{n-1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

A
fractal structure
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a pair
(\mathcal{T},\beta)
where
\mathcal{T}
is a rooted tree and
\beta
is a map from
\mathcal{T}
to the set of nonempty compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The
fractal
associated to
(\mathcal{T},\beta)
is the set

{\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T},\beta)=\bigcup_{\{\tau_{n}\}\in\partial\mathcal{T}}\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta(\tau_{n}).}

A fractal structure
(\mathcal{T},\beta)
is said to be
regular
if it satisfies the followings:

•

each vertex of
\mathcal{T}
has at least one son;

•

if
\tau
is a son of
\tau^{\prime}
, then
\beta(\tau)\subset\beta(\tau^{\prime})
;

•

for any
\{\tau_{n}\}\in\partial\mathcal{T}
,
\diam{\beta(\tau_{n})}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

2.2.

A

Similarly, for

L_{n}^{(1)}&\quad\text{for }j=j_{i}+1,\dots,d.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\rho_{i}}{\sqrt{d}}L_{i-1}^{(1)}+L_{i}^{(j)}\right)-\frac{1}{2}l_{j}(R_{1}^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}l_{j}(R_{2}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{3\rho_{i}}{4\sqrt{d}}L_{i-1}^{(1)}>0.

\displaystyle\leq\left(L_{i}^{(j)}+\frac{\rho_{i}}{4\sqrt{d}}L_{i-1}^{(1)}\right)^{j_{i}}\left(L_{n}^{(1)}\right)^{d-j_{i}}\#\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\vol(R^{\prime})\#\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\leq(d-j_{i}+1)\vol(R_{0}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq d4^{d}\left(L_{i-1}^{(1)}\right)^{j_{i}}\left(L_{n}^{(1)}\right)^{d-j_{i}},

3.
Counting lattice points in convex sets

In this section, we will generalize the results in
[
LSST20
, §3.2]
for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

(d\geq 3)
.
We first recall the notations and lemmas in
[
LSST20
, §3.1]
.

3.1.
Preliminaries for lattice point counting

For a positive integer
D\geq 1
, we write the
D
-dimensional Euclidean space by
\mathcal{E}_{D}=\mathbb{R}^{D}
.
For a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
and a lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
, let
\lambda_{i}(K,\Lambda)

(i=1,\dots,D)
be the
i
-th successive minimum of
\Lambda
with respect to
K
, that is, the infimum of those numbers
\lambda
such that
\lambda K\cap\Lambda
contains
i
linearly
independent vectors. Let
\vol(\cdot)
be the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
and let
\cov(\Lambda)
be the covolume of a lattice
\Lambda
, which
is the Lebesgue measure of a fundamental domain of
\Lambda
. Denote

\theta(K,\Lambda):=\frac{\vol(K)}{\cov(\Lambda)}.

For an affine subspace
H
of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
, let
\vol_{H}(\cdot)
be the Lebesgue measure on
H
with respect to the subspace Riemannian structure.
We write
\vol_{H}(S)=\vol_{H}(S\cap H)
for a Borel measurable subset
S
of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
by abuse of notation.
We say that a subspace
H
of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
is
\Lambda
-rational if
H\cap\Lambda
is a lattice in
H
,
and denote by
\cov_{H}(\Lambda)
the covolume of the lattice
H\cap\Lambda
in
H
.
We also use the same notations for the dual vector space
\mathcal{E}_{D}^{*}
with respect to the standard Euclidean structure.
For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}_{D}^{*}
, denote
H_{\varphi}=\mathrm{ker}\varphi
.

We use
\|\cdot\|
for the Euclidean norms on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
and
\mathcal{E}_{D}^{*}
. For a normed vector space
V
,
denote by
B_{r}(V)
(or
B_{r}
if
V=\mathbb{R}^{D}
) the ball of radius
r
centered at
0\in V
. We use
K
-norms on

\mathbb{R}^{D}
and
\mathcal{E}_{D}^{*}
defined by

\begin{cases}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{K}=\inf\{r>0:\mathbf{v}\in rK\},\quad&\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{D},\\
\|\varphi\|_{K}=\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in K}|\varphi(\mathbf{v})|,\quad&\varphi\in\mathcal{E}_{D}^{*}.\end{cases}

Recall that
\mathcal{L}_{D}
is the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{D}
, which can be identified with the homogeneous space
\operatorname{SL}_{D}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{D}(\mathbb{Z})
. For
g\in\operatorname{SL}_{D}(\mathbb{R})
let
g^{*}
be the adjoint action on
\mathcal{E}_{D}^{*}
defined by

\varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ g
. Then
g^{*}
can be represented by the transpose of
g
with respect to the standard
basis
\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{D}
of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
and the dual basis
\mathbf{e}_{1}^{*},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{D}^{*}
of
\mathcal{E}_{D}^{*}
.

The
dual lattice
of
\Lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{D}
is the lattice in
\mathcal{E}_{D}^{*}
defined by

\Lambda^{*}=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{E}_{D}^{*}:\varphi(\mathbf{v})\in\mathbb{Z},\ \forall\mathbf{v}\in\Lambda\}.

Let us define the following two sets:

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}&=\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(D)=\{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{D}:\|\mathbf{v}\|\geq\epsilon,\ \forall\mathbf{v}\in\Lambda\smallsetminus\{0\}\}=\{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{D}:\lambda_{1}(B_{1},\Lambda)\geq\epsilon\};\\
\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}^{*}&=\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}^{*}(D)=\{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{D}:\|\varphi\|\geq\epsilon,\ \forall\varphi\in\Lambda^{*}\smallsetminus\{0\}\}.\end{split}

Since
\mathcal{E}_{D}^{*}
can be naturally identified with

with the standard Euclidean
structure, we have

.

We define the subset

of

by

\mathcal{L}_{D}^{\prime}=\{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{D}:\Lambda\cap\mathbb{R}\mathbf{e}_{D}=r\mathbb{Z}\mathbf{e}_{D}\text{ for some $r$ with }1/2<r\leq 1\},

which plays an important role to prove the main theorem.

A nonzero vector
\mathbf{v}\in\Lambda
is said to be

for all

. The set of primitive vectors in

We summarize the lemmas in

3.2.
Lattice point counting in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

For

and a

-tuple

{M_{\mathbf{r}}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{d+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:|x_{i}|\leq r_{i},\ \forall i=1,\dots,d+1\}.}

Let

{M_{\mathbf{r}}^{*}=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{E}_{d+1}^{*}:|x_{i}^{\varphi}|\leq r_{i},\ \forall i=1,\dots,d+1\},}

where the element

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

.
For any real number

, there exists a positive real number

such that for every lattice
\Lambda
in

and every
(d+1)
-tuple

{\frac{1}{c_{0}\zeta(d+1)}\theta(M_{\mathbf{r}},\Lambda)\leq\#(M_{\mathbf{r}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda})\leq\frac{c_{0}}{\zeta(d+1)}\theta(M_{\mathbf{r}},\Lambda).}

Proof.

The proof follows directly from Lemma
3.1

It follows from the definition that

{\|\varphi\|_{\mathbf{r}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{M_{\mathbf{r}}}\leq(d+1)\|\varphi\|_{\mathbf{r}}.}

For

contains
i
linearly independent vectors.
We will give an upper bound of the number of

{\mathcal{S}(\Lambda,\mathbf{r},s):=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in M_{\mathbf{r}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda}:\varphi(\mathbf{v})=0\text{ for some }\varphi\in N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}(\mathbf{r},s)\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}}\right\},}

where
\widehat{\Lambda^{*}}
is the set of primitive vectors in
\Lambda^{*}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

For
d\geq 2
, let
\Lambda
be a unimodular lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with
q_{1}(\Lambda,\mathbf{r},s)\geq s^{-2}
.
Then

(1)

if
r_{m}=r_{M}
and
q_{d+1}(\Lambda,\mathbf{r},s)\leq ds^{-1/2}r_{M}
, then

\#\mathcal{S}(\Lambda,\mathbf{r},s)\ll s^{1/2}\cdot\mathrm{vol}(M_{\mathbf{r}});

(2)

if
r_{m}<r_{M}
and
q_{d+1}(\Lambda,\mathbf{r},s)\log q_{d+1}(\Lambda,\mathbf{r},s)\leq sr_{M}
, then

\#\mathcal{S}(\Lambda,\mathbf{r},s)\ll s^{2}\cdot\vol(M_{\mathbf{r}}).

Proof.

For simplicity, we denote by
N_{q}=N_{q}(\mathbf{r},s)
,
q_{i}=q_{i}(\Lambda,\mathbf{r},s)
and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(\Lambda,\mathbf{r},s)
.
If
N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}}
is empty then there is nothing to prove.
We assume that
N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}}
is nonempty.
It follows from the definition that

(3.4)

{\#\mathcal{S}\leq\sum_{\varphi\in N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}}}\#(H_{\varphi}\cap M_{\mathbf{r}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda}).}

We first claim that for every
\varphi\in N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}}
,

(3.5)

{\#(H_{\varphi}\cap M_{\mathbf{r}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda})\ll\frac{\mathrm{vol}(M_{\mathbf{r}})}{\|\varphi\|_{M_{\mathbf{r}}}}\leq\frac{\vol(M_{\mathbf{r}})}{\|\varphi\|_{\mathbf{r}}},}

where the second inequality follows from (
3.3
).
In fact, if
\#(H_{\varphi}\cap M_{\mathbf{r}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda})<d+1
, then it follows from (
3.3
) that

{\frac{\mathrm{vol}(M_{\mathbf{r}})}{\|\varphi\|_{M_{\mathbf{r}}}}\geq\frac{2^{(d+1)}r_{1}\dots r_{d+1}}{(d+1)\|\varphi\|_{\mathbf{r}}}\geq\frac{2^{(d+1)}r_{1}\dots r_{d+1}}{(d+1)^{2}sr_{M}}\gg\#(H_{\varphi}\cap M_{\mathbf{r}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda}).}

Otherwise,
H_{\varphi}\cap M_{\mathbf{r}}\cap\Lambda
has
d
linearly independent vectors, hence it follows from Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.6
that

{\#(H_{\varphi}\cap M_{\mathbf{r}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda})\ll\frac{\vol_{H_{\varphi}}(M_{\mathbf{r}})}{\cov_{H_{\varphi}}(\Lambda)}\ll\frac{\|\varphi\|\vol(M_{\mathbf{r}})}{\cov_{H_{\varphi}}(\Lambda)\|\varphi\|_{M_{\mathbf{r}}}}\ll\frac{\vol(M_{\mathbf{r}})}{\|\varphi\|_{M_{\mathbf{r}}}},}

which concludes the claim.

By (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), to prove Lemma
3.9
, it suffices to estimate

(3.6)

{\begin{split}\eta:=&\sum_{\varphi\in N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathbf{r}}^{-1}\\
=&\frac{1}{(d+1)sr_{M}}\#(N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})+\sum_{\varphi\in N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}}}\int_{\|\varphi\|_{\mathbf{r}}}^{(d+1)sr_{M}}\frac{1}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q.\end{split}}

We denote the first and second terms in the last line by
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
, respectively.

Observe that

(3.7)

{\begin{split}\eta_{2}&=\sum_{\varphi\in N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}}}\int_{q_{1}}^{(d+1)sr_{M}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{q}(\|\varphi\|_{\mathbf{r}})}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q\\
&=\int_{q_{1}}^{(d+1)sr_{M}}\sum_{\varphi\in N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{q}(\|\varphi\|_{\mathbf{r}})}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q\\
&\leq\int_{q_{1}}^{(d+1)sr_{M}}\frac{\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q.\end{split}}

where
\mathbbm{1}_{q}
denotes the indicator function of the set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x\leq q\}
.

On the other hand, for
i=2,\dots,d
, if
q_{i-1}\leq q<q_{i}
then we have
\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})=i\leq d
.
Thus

(3.8)

{\int_{q_{1}}^{q_{d}}\frac{\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q\leq\int_{q_{1}}^{q_{d}}\frac{d}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q\leq\frac{d}{q_{1}}\ll s^{2}\leq s^{1/2},}

where the third inequality follows from the assumption
q_{1}\geq s^{-2}
.

Now we are ready to prove the two assertions in Lemma
3.9
separately.

Proof of the assertion (
1
).

We claim that
\eta\ll s^{1/2}
under the assumption of (
1
), which concludes the assertion (
1
). Assume that
r_{m}=r_{M}
and
q_{d+1}\leq ds^{-1/2}r_{M}
.
Observe that by definition

(3.9)

{N_{(d+1)s^{-1/2}r_{M}}=M_{(s,\dots,s,(d+1)s^{-1/2}r_{M})}^{*}.}

We have an upper bound of
\eta_{1}
as

(3.10)

{\eta_{1}\leq\frac{\#(N_{(d+1)s^{-1/2}r_{M}}\cap\Lambda^{*})}{(d+1)sr_{M}}\ll\frac{\vol(N_{(d+1)s^{-1/2}r_{M}})}{(d+1)sr_{M}}\ll s^{d-3/2}\leq s^{1/2}.}

The first inequality follows from
s<1/2
, the second inequality follows from Lemma
3.3
,
and the third inequality follows from (
3.9
).

For an upper bound of
\eta_{2}
, we first compute

(3.11)

{\begin{split}\int_{sr_{M}}^{(d+1)sr_{M}}\frac{\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q&\leq\int_{sr_{M}}^{(d+1)sr_{M}}\frac{\#(N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\Lambda^{*})}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q\\
&\leq\frac{\#(N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\Lambda^{*})}{sr_{M}}\\
&\ll s^{1/2},\end{split}}

where the last inequality can be shown in the same way as in (
3.10
).

If
sr_{M}\leq q_{d}
, then it follows from (
3.8
) and (
3.11
) that
\eta_{2}\ll s^{1/2}
.
Now we suppose that
sr_{M}>q_{d}
.
For all
q_{d}<q\leq sr_{M}=sr_{m}
, observe that

N_{q}=M_{(q/r_{1},\dots,q/r_{d+1})}^{*}=\frac{q}{sr_{M}}N_{sr_{M}}.

Since
\lambda_{d}(N_{q},\Lambda)=\lambda_{d}(\frac{q}{sr_{M}}N_{sr_{M}},\Lambda)\leq 1\leq sr_{M}/q
, it follows from
Lemma
3.5
that

{\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})\leq\#\left(\frac{q}{sr_{M}}N_{sr_{M}}\cap\Lambda^{*}\right)\ll\left(\frac{q}{sr_{M}}\right)^{d}\#(N_{sr_{M}}\cap\Lambda^{*}).}

By
sr_{M}\leq ds^{-1/2}r_{M}
and Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{q}{sr_{M}}\right)^{d}\#(N_{ds^{-1/2}r_{M}}\cap\Lambda^{*})

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{q}{sr_{M}}\right)^{d}\vol(N_{ds^{-1/2}r_{M}})

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{q}{sr_{M}}\right)^{2}s^{d-1/2}r_{M}\ll\frac{q^{2}s^{-1/2}}{r_{M}}.

The last line follows from
\frac{q}{sr_{M}}\leq 1
and
s\leq 1
.
Thus we have

{\int_{q_{d}}^{sr_{M}}\frac{\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q\ll\int_{q_{d}}^{sr_{M}}\frac{s^{-1/2}}{r_{M}}\mathrm{d}q\ll s^{1/2}.}

It follows that
\eta_{2}\ll s^{1/2}
under the assumption of (
1
),
which concludes the assertion (
1
).
∎

Proof of the assertion (
2
).

We will prove that
\eta\ll s^{2}
under the assumption of (
2
).
Recall that we fix
0<s<1/2
. By the assumption, we have
q_{d+1}\geq q_{1}\geq s^{-2}>4
so that
q_{d+1}<sr_{M}<(d+1)sr_{M}
since
q_{d+1}\log q_{d+1}\leq sr_{M}
.
Thus
N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\Lambda^{*}
contains
d+1
linearly independent vectors. By Lemma
3.3
, we have

(3.12)

{\eta_{1}\leq\frac{\#(N_{(d+1)sr_{M}}\cap\Lambda^{*})}{(d+1)sr_{M}}\ll\frac{\vol(N_{(d+1)sr_{M}})}{(d+1)sr_{M}}\ll s^{d}\leq s^{2}.}

By (
3.7
), it suffices to show that

{\int_{q_{1}}^{(d+1)sr_{M}}\frac{\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q\ll s^{2}.}

We split the domain of integration as
(q_{1},q_{d})\cup(q_{d},q_{d+1})\cup(q_{d+1},sr_{M})\cup(sr_{M},(d+1)sr_{M})
and estimate the upper bounds of the integrals over these intervals.

For each
q\in(sr_{M},(d+1)sr_{M})
, it follows from Lemma
3.3
that
\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})\ll\vol(N_{q})\ll s^{d}q
. Thus we have

(3.13)

{\int_{sr_{M}}^{(d+1)sr_{M}}\frac{\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q\ll\int_{sr_{M}}^{(d+1)sr_{M}}\frac{s^{d}}{q}\mathrm{d}q=s^{d}\log(d+1)\ll s^{2}.}

For each
q\in(q_{d+1},sr_{M})
, it follows from Lemma
3.3
that
\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})\ll\vol(N_{q})\ll s^{d-1}q^{2}/r_{M}
. Thus we have

(3.14)

{\int_{q_{d+1}}^{sr_{M}}\frac{\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q\ll\int_{q_{d+1}}^{sr_{M}}\frac{s^{d-1}}{r_{M}}\mathrm{d}q\leq s^{d}\leq s^{2}.}

By (
3.8
), the integral over
(q_{1},q_{d})
is bounded above by
s^{2}
.

Now it remains to show that the integral over
(q_{d},q_{d+1})
is bounded above by
s^{2}
.
Let
H=\Span_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{q_{d}}\cap\Lambda^{*})
.
We claim that for every
q\in(q_{d},q_{d+1})
,

(3.15)

{\vol_{H}(N_{q})\leq\frac{q}{q_{d+1}}\vol_{H}(N_{q_{d+1}}).}

We first consider the case of
\mathbf{e}_{d+1}^{*}\notin H
. Let
\operatorname{\mathbf{pr}}^{*}
be the orthogonal projection onto
\Span_{\mathbb{R}}\{\mathbf{e}_{1}^{*},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{d}^{*}\}
.
Then the volume of
\operatorname{\mathbf{pr}}^{*}(N_{q})
is at most
q/q_{d+1}
times the volume of
\operatorname{\mathbf{pr}}^{*}(N_{q_{d+1}})
since
q/r_{M}<q_{d+1}/r_{M}<s
. This implies (
3.15
).
We now assume
\mathbf{e}_{d+1}^{*}\in H
. Then it follows from
N_{q}\subset N_{q_{d+1}}
that

\begin{split}\vol_{H}(N_{q})&=2q\vol_{\operatorname{\mathbf{pr}}^{*}(H)}\left(\operatorname{\mathbf{pr}}^{*}(N_{q})\right)\\
&\leq\frac{q}{q_{d+1}}2q_{d+1}\vol_{\operatorname{\mathbf{pr}}^{*}(H)}\left(\operatorname{\mathbf{pr}}^{*}(N_{q_{d+1}})\right)=\frac{q}{q_{d+1}}\vol_{H}(N_{q_{d+1}}).\end{split}

Thus the claim follows.

For each
q\in(q_{d},q_{d+1})
, we have

\displaystyle\#(N_{q}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\vol_{H}(N_{q})}{\cov_{H}(\Lambda^{*})}

by Lemma
3.3

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q}{q_{d+1}}\frac{\vol_{H}(N_{q_{d+1}})}{\cov_{H}(\Lambda^{*})}

by (
3.15
)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{q}{q_{d+1}}\#(N_{q_{d+1}}\cap H\cap\Lambda^{*})

by Lemma
3.3

\displaystyle\ll\frac{q}{q_{d+1}}\#(N_{q_{d+1}}^{\circ}\cap H\cap\Lambda^{*})

by Lemma
3.4

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{q_{d+1}}\#(N_{q_{d+1}}^{\circ}\cap\Lambda^{*})\leq\frac{q}{q_{d+1}}\#(N_{q_{d+1}}\cap\Lambda^{*})

\displaystyle\ll\frac{q}{q_{d+1}}\vol(N_{q_{d+1}})

by Lemma
3.3

\displaystyle\ll s^{d-1}\frac{q_{d+1}q}{r_{M}}.

Therefore, we have

(3.16)

{\int_{q_{d}}^{q_{d+1}}\frac{\#(N_{1}\cap\widehat{\Lambda^{*}})}{q^{2}}\mathrm{d}q\ll\int_{q_{d}}^{q_{d+1}}s^{2}\frac{q_{d+1}}{sr_{M}}\frac{1}{q}\mathrm{d}q\leq s^{2}\frac{q_{d+1}\log q_{d+1}}{sr_{M}}\leq s^{2}.}

By combining (
3.8
), (
3.13
), (
3.14
), and (
3.16
), the proof of (
2
) is completed.
∎

Combining the proofs of the two assertions, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.9
.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

,
\overline{b}_{t}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\overline{b}_{t,1},\dots,\overline{b}_{t,d+1}\right)

(3.20)

r_{m}=r_{1}<r_{M}=r_{\ell+1}

Proof.

4.2.
The lower bound calculation

In this subsection we complete the proof of main results.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{d})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{d}
where
w_{1}=\cdots=w_{\ell}>w_{\ell+1}\geq\cdots\geq w_{d}>0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{d}w_{i}=1
and let
(\mathcal{T},\beta)
be the self-affine strunction on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in the previous section. Then

{\dim\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T},\beta)\geq d-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}}.}

We will prove Proposition
4.4
using Corollary
2.2
. Let
C_{n},L_{n}^{(1)},\dots,L_{n}^{(d)}
be the positive constants defined as follows:

{C_{n}=\epsilon_{n}^{d}e^{\xi dnt},\quad L_{n}^{(i)}=2\epsilon_{n}e^{-w_{i}t_{n+1}-t_{n}},\forall i=1,\dots,d.}

It can be easily checked that the regular self-affine structure
(\mathcal{T},\beta)
satisfies the assumptions (
1
), (
2
), and (
3
) of
Theorem
2.1
. For the assumption (
4
) of Theorem
2.1
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be large and
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{n-1}
. Then

{\mathrm{dist}(\beta(x),\beta(y))\geq L_{n-1}^{(1)}\frac{c^{\prime}r^{d-1}}{4\sqrt{d}\epsilon_{n-1}}e^{\frac{1}{\ell}(w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d}-\xi\ell)nt},}

where
x,y\in\mathcal{T}(\tau)
are distinct and
c^{\prime}
is the positive constant in Lemma
3.7
.

Proof.

By the construction of
\mathcal{T}
and the definition of
b_{n}
, there are
1/2\leq s_{x},s_{y}\leq 1
such that

{s_{x}\mathbf{e}_{d+1}\in b_{n}a_{t_{n}}h(x)\mathbb{Z}^{d+1},\quad s_{y}\mathbf{e}_{d+1}\in b_{n}a_{t_{n}}h(y)\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}.}

Let us denote

{\begin{split}\mathbf{v}&=b_{n}a_{t_{n}}h(y-x)(b_{n}a_{t_{n}})^{-1}s_{x}\mathbf{e}_{d+1}\in b_{n}a_{t_{n}}h(y)\mathbb{Z}^{d+1},\\
\mathbf{v}&\wedge s_{y}\mathbf{e}_{d+1}=s_{x}s_{y}\sum_{i=1}^{d}u_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}\wedge\mathbf{e}_{d+1}.\end{split}}

Observe that

u_{i}=\begin{cases}(y_{i}-x_{i})e^{(w_{i}+1)t_{n}-\frac{1}{\ell}(w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d})nt}\quad&\text{for }1\leq i\leq\ell,\\
(y_{i}-x_{i})e^{(w_{i}+1)t_{n}+w_{i}nt}\quad&\text{for }\ell+1\leq i\leq d.\end{cases}

Since
x
and
y
are distinct, the vectors
\mathbf{v}
and
e_{y}\mathbf{e}_{d+1}
are linearly independent,
hence it follows from Lemma
3.7
that

(4.13)

{\sqrt{d}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\infty}\geq s_{x}s_{y}\|\mathbf{u}\|=\|\mathbf{v}\wedge s_{y}\mathbf{e}_{d+1}\|\geq c^{\prime}r^{d-1},}

where
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
denotes the max norm.

Let
x^{\prime}\in\beta(x)
and
y^{\prime}\in\beta(y)
.
Suppose that
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\infty}=|u_{i}|
for some
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
Then it follows from (
4.13
) that

\displaystyle\|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle\geq|y_{i}^{\prime}-x_{i}^{\prime}|\geq|y_{i}-x_{i}|-|x_{i}-x_{i}^{\prime}|-|y_{i}-y_{i}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\geq e^{-(w_{i}+1)t_{n}+\frac{1}{\ell}(w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d})nt}\left(\frac{c^{\prime}r^{d-1}}{\sqrt{d}}-2\epsilon_{n}e^{-\xi w_{i}(n+1)t-\frac{1}{\ell}(w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d})nt}\right)

(4.14)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-(w_{i}+1)t_{n}+\frac{1}{\ell}(w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d})nt}\frac{c^{\prime}r^{d-1}}{2\sqrt{d}}

\displaystyle=L_{n-1}^{(i)}\frac{c^{\prime}r^{d-1}}{4\sqrt{d}\epsilon_{n-1}}e^{\frac{1}{\ell}(w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d}-\xi\ell)nt}

\displaystyle\geq L_{n-1}^{(1)}\frac{c^{\prime}r^{d-1}}{4\sqrt{d}\epsilon_{n-1}}e^{\frac{1}{\ell}(w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d}-\xi\ell)nt}.

We choose
t\geq 1
large enough so that the third line (
4.14
) holds for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

On the other hand, if
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\infty}=|u_{i}|
for some
\ell+1\leq i\leq d
, then
we have

\displaystyle\|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle\geq|y_{i}^{\prime}-x_{i}^{\prime}|\geq|y_{i}-x_{i}|-|x_{i}-x_{i}^{\prime}|-|y_{i}-y_{i}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\geq e^{-(w_{i}+1)t_{n}-w_{i}nt}\left(\frac{c^{\prime}r^{d-1}}{\sqrt{d}}-2\epsilon_{n}e^{w_{i}nt-\xi w_{i}(n+1)t}\right)

(4.15)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-(w_{i}+1)t_{n}-w_{i}nt}\frac{c^{\prime}r^{d-1}}{2\sqrt{d}}

\displaystyle=L_{n-1}^{(1)}\frac{c^{\prime}r^{d-1}}{4\sqrt{d}\epsilon_{n-1}}e^{(w_{1}-w_{i})t_{n}-(\xi+w_{i})nt}

\displaystyle\geq L_{n-1}^{(1)}\frac{c^{\prime}r^{d-1}}{4\sqrt{d}\epsilon_{n-1}}e^{(w_{1}-w_{\ell+1})t_{n}-(\xi+w_{i})nt}

(4.16)

\displaystyle\geq L_{n-1}^{(1)}\frac{c^{\prime}r^{d-1}}{4\sqrt{d}\epsilon_{n-1}}e^{\frac{1}{\ell}(w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d}-\xi\ell)nt}.

We choose
t\geq 1
large enough so that the third line (
4.15
) and last line (
4.16
) hold for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Since
w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d}<\xi\ell
, we can choose
t\geq 1
large enough so that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

(4.17)

{\rho_{n}:=\frac{c^{\prime}r^{d-1}}{4\sqrt{d}\epsilon_{n-1}}e^{\frac{1}{\ell}(w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d}-\xi\ell)nt}\leq 1.}

The assumption (
4
) of Theorem
2.1
follows from Lemma
4.5
.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

We prove the proposition applying Corollary
2.2
. We first claim that the assumptions of Corollary
2.2
hold for some large
k,n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
.
To show
(i)
, observe that
\frac{L_{kn}^{(d)}}{L_{kn-1}^{(d)}}\leq\frac{L_{n}^{(1)}}{L_{n-1}^{(1)}}
if and only if

\frac{kn-1}{kn}\cdot\frac{n}{n-1}\leq e^{w_{d}\xi(kn+1)t-w_{1}\xi(n+1)t+\xi knt-\xi nt}

If
k\in\mathbb{N}
is large enough, then the right hand side of the above equation has a positive exponent, hence
\frac{L_{kn}^{(d)}}{L_{kn-1}^{(d)}}\leq\frac{L_{n}^{(1)}}{L_{n-1}^{(1)}}
, for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Similarly,
L_{kn_{0}-1}^{(d)}<L_{n_{0}-1}^{(1)}
also holds for some large
k,n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
C_{n}
has a positive exponent and
\rho_{n}
has a negative exponent, we can choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that
(ii)
and
(iii)
hold for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
. To show
(iv)
, observe that for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\rho_{n}^{\ell}C_{n}\prod_{j=\ell+1}^{d}L_{n}^{(j)}/L_{n-1}^{(j)}=const\times\frac{\epsilon_{n}^{2d-\ell}}{\epsilon_{n-1}^{d}}\geq const\times\epsilon^{d-\ell}2^{-d}n^{-d+\ell},

where the constant is independent of
n
. Thus we can choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that
(iv)
holds for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This proves the claim.

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\log(C_{n}L_{n}^{(\ell+1)}\cdots L_{n}^{(d)}/L_{n-1}^{(\ell+1)}\cdots L_{n-1}^{(d)})}{-\log(L_{n}^{(1)}/L_{n-1}^{(1)})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi dnt-\xi(w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d})(n+1)t-\xi(d-\ell)nt+o(n)}{\xi w_{1}(n+1)t+\xi nt+o(n)}

\displaystyle\to\frac{\ell-(w_{\ell+1}+\cdots+w_{d})}{1+w_{1}}=\ell-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}}\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty

Hence Corollary
2.2
implies

{\dim\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T},\beta)\geq(d-\ell)+\ell-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}}=d-\frac{1}{1+w_{1}}.}

∎

Tangent cones at infinity
Luis Renato G. Dias, Nilva Rodrigues Ribeiro

Abstract
. Let X ⊂ ℂ m X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m} be an unbounded pure k k -dimensional algebraic set. We define the tangent cones C 4 , ∞ ​ ( X ) C_{4,\infty}(X) and C 5 , ∞ ​ ( X ) C_{5,\infty}(X) of X X at infinity. We establish some of their properties and relations. We prove that X X must be an affine linear subspace of ℂ m {\mathbb{C}}^{m} provided that C 5 , ∞ ​ ( X ) C_{5,\infty}(X) has pure dimension k k . Also, we study the relation between the tangent cones at infinity and representations of X X outside a compact set as a branched covering. Our results can be seen as versions at infinity of results of Whitney and Stutz.

1.
Introduction

Let
(V,0)\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be an
n
-dimensional analytic set in some neighborhood of the origin in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. In
[
9
,
10
]
, Whitney considered six tangent cones
C_{i}(V,0)
,
i=1,\ldots,6
, and, since then, their properties and relations have been studied. The precise definitions for the six cones can be seen in
[
1
, p. 92]
,
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
. The cone
C_{3}(V,0)
is also known as the Zariski tangent cone of
V
.

The Whitney tangent cones
C_{3}(V,0),C_{4}(V,0)
and
C_{5}(V,0)
play an important role in various results. For instance, it is well known that
C_{3}(V,0)
determines the set of all projections
\pi
of
(V,0)
to
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
that are a branched cover when restricted to a small neighborhood of
0\in V
. In
[
8
]
, Stutz showed that if
\dim C_{4}(V,0)=n
, then
\pi
can be chosen such that the critical locus of
\pi
is exactly the singular set of
V
. Also in
[
8
]
, Stutz proved that if
\dim C_{5}(V,0)=n+1
, then a suitable choice of a projection of
(V,0)
to
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
gives a representation of
V
in terms of an hypersurface in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
; see also
[
1
]
and
[
6
]
.

In this paper, we define the Whitney tangent cones at infinity
C_{4,\infty}(X)
and
C_{5,\infty}(X)
for an unbounded pure
k
-dimensional algebraic set
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
and we also consider the Whitney tangent cone at infinity
C_{3,\infty}(X)
defined in
[
4
]
as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be an unbounded pure
k
-dimensional algebraic set.

\bullet

The Whitney tangent cone
C_{3,\infty}(X)
of
X
at infinity is defined as the set of the points
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
for which there exist sequences
(p_{j})_{j}
in
X
and
(t_{j})_{j}
in
{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|p_{j}\|=\infty
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}t_{j}p_{j}=v
.

\bullet

The Whitney tangent cone
C_{4,\infty}(X)
of
X
at infinity is defined as the set of the points
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}

for which there exists a sequence
(p_{j})_{j}
in
X\setminus{\rm Sing}X
and, for each
j
, there is a vector
v_{j}\in T_{p_{j}}X
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|p_{j}\|=\infty
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}v_{j}=v
.

\bullet

The Whitney tangent cone
C_{5,\infty}(X)
of
X
at infinity is defined as the set of points
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
for which there exist sequences
(p_{j})_{j}
and
(q_{j})_{j}
in
X
and
(t_{j})_{j}
in
{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|p_{j}\|=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|q_{j}\|=\infty
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}t_{j}(p_{j}-q_{j})=v
.

By Definition
1.1
, for
i=3,4,5
, the sets
C_{i,\infty}(X)\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
are closed and they are complexly homogeneous in the sense that for any
v\in C_{i,\infty}(X)
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
the vectors
\lambda v
also belong to
C_{i,\infty}(X)
.

In section §
2
, we prove basic properties of
C_{4,\infty}(X)
and
C_{5,\infty}(X)
. We prove that
C_{4,\infty}(X)
and
C_{5,\infty}(X)
are algebraic sets and that
C_{3,\infty}(X)\subset C_{4,\infty}(X)\subset C_{5,\infty}(X)
. Moreover, we prove that
\dim C_{5,\infty}(X)\leq 2k+1
. Then, since
\dim C_{3,\infty}(X)=k
(see
[
2
, Prop. 2.10]
), it follows that
k\leq\dim C_{4,\infty}(X)\leq\dim C_{5,\infty}(X)\leq 2k+1
; see Propositions
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.3
.

In Section
3
, we prove that any pure
k
-dimensional algebraic set
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
for which
C_{5,\infty}(X)
has pure dimension
k
must be an affine linear subspace of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
; see Theorem
3.5
.

The aim of Section
4
is to give versions at infinity of the above results of Stutz for complex algebraic set in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
as follows. Let
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be an unbounded pure
k
-dimensional algebraic set. Let
W\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be a fixed
m-k
dimensional vector space such that
W\cap C_{3,\infty}(X)=\{0\}
(this always exists by Lemma
4.2
). We may use the coordinates
(x,y)\in V\times W={\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, where
V
and
W
are orthogonal. Let
\pi:X\to V
be the restriction to
X
of the canonical projection
(x,y)\in V\times W\to x\in V
. We denote by
B_{R}
the ball in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
of radius
R
centered at the origin. We have:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
C_{4,\infty}(X)\cap W=\{0\}
. Then, there exists a sufficiently large
R
such that
{\rm Sing}X\setminus\bar{B}_{R}={\rm Sing}\,\pi\setminus\bar{B}_{R}
.

By a similar argument from the proof of Lemma
4.2
, it follows that
\dim C_{4,\infty}(X)=k
forces
C_{4,\infty}(X)\cap W=\{0\}
.

We denote
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m-k})
and
W^{i}:=\{(0,y)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\mid y_{i}=0\},
for
i=1,\ldots,m-k
. We denote
\pi_{i}:{\mathbb{C}}^{m}=V\times W\to{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{C}}^{k+1}
the projection defined by
\pi_{i}(x,y)=(x,y_{i})
. It follows that
\ker\pi_{i}=W^{i}
. We have:

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
C_{5,\infty}(X)\cap W^{i}=\{0\}
. Then
\pi_{i}(X)
is an algebraic hypersurface in
{\mathbb{C}}^{k+1}
and, for sufficiently large
R
, the restriction
\pi_{i}:X\setminus\bar{B}_{R}\to\pi_{i}(X\setminus\bar{B}_{R})
is a homeomorphism.

By a similar argument from the proof of Lemma
4.2
, it follows that for the hypothesis of Theorem
1.3
to be satisfied, it is necessary that
\dim C_{5,\infty}(X)\leq k+1
.

2.
Properties of the tangent cones at infinity

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be an unbounded pure
k
-dimensional algebraic set. Then,
C_{3,\infty}(X)\subset C_{4,\infty}(X)\subset C_{5,\infty}(X)
.

Proof.

Firstly, we prove that
C_{3,\infty}(X)\subset C_{4,\infty}(X)
.

Let
v\in C_{3,\infty}(X)
. By Definition
1.1
,
0\in C_{3,\infty}(X)\cap C_{4,\infty}(X)
. So it is sufficient to consider
v\neq 0
.

By Definition
1.1
, there exist sequences
(p_{j})_{j}
in
X
and
(t_{j})_{j}
in
{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|p_{j}\|=\infty
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}t_{j}p_{j}=v
. It is obvious that
\lim_{j\to\infty}t_{j}=0.

Since
X\setminus{\rm Sing}X
is dense on
X
, there exists a sequence
(q_{j})_{j}
in
X\setminus{\rm Sing}X
such that
\|p_{j}-q_{j}\|<\frac{1}{j}
. Then, by triangle inequality, we have
\|p_{j}\|\leq\|p_{j}-q_{j}\|+\|q_{j}\|\leq\frac{1}{j}+\|q_{j}\|.
Since
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|p_{j}\|=\infty
, also
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|q_{j}\|=\infty
.

Moreover, by triangle inequality, we have:

\|t_{j}q_{j}-v\|\leq\|t_{j}q_{j}-t_{j}p_{j}\|+\|t_{j}p_{j}-v\|\leq|t_{j}|\,\|q_{j}-p_{j}\|+\|t_{j}p_{j}-v\|.

Since both members of the right side converge to
0
, we have
\lim_{j\to\infty}t_{j}q_{j}=v.

Since
q_{j}\in X\setminus{\rm Sing}X
, it follows by the Curve Selection Lemma that there exist analytic curves
\psi:(0,\epsilon)\to(X\setminus{\rm Sing}X)
and
t:(0,\epsilon)\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\lim_{s\to 0}\|\psi(s)\|=\infty
and
\lim_{s\to 0}t(s)\psi(s)=v
.

Now we can use the same arguments from the proof of Lemma 2.2 of
[
4
]
and we get an analytic curve
\varphi
which is a reparametrization of
\psi
that satisfies:

a)

\varphi(s)\in X\setminus{\rm Sing}X
, for any
s
.

b)

\lim_{s\to 0}\|\varphi(s)\|=\infty.

c)

\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{\varphi(s)}{\|\varphi(s)\|}=-\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(s)}{\|\varphi^{\prime}(s)\|}=\frac{v}{\|v\|}.

It follows by a), b) and c) that
-\frac{v}{\|v\|}\in C_{4,\infty}(X)
. Since
C_{4,\infty}(X)
is a cone, it follows that
v\in C_{4,\infty}(X)
.

Now, we prove that
C_{4,\infty}(X)\subset C_{5,\infty}(X)
. Let
v\in C_{4,\infty}(X)
. By the definition of
C_{4,\infty}(X)
, there exists a sequence
(p_{j})_{j}
in
X\setminus{\rm Sing}X
and, for each
j
, a vector
v_{j}\in T_{p_{j}}X
, such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|p_{j}\|=\infty
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}v_{j}=v.

It follows from the definition of
T_{p_{j}}X
that, for any
j
, there exists
q_{j}
in
X\setminus{\rm Sing}X
and
t_{j}
in
{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\|p_{j}-q_{j}\|\leq\frac{1}{j}
,
t_{j}\leq\frac{1}{j}
, and

(1)

\|\frac{p_{j}-q_{j}}{t_{j}}-v_{j}\|\leq\frac{1}{j}.

It follows by triangle inequality that
\|p_{j}\|\leq\|p_{j}-q_{j}\|+\|q_{j}\|\leq\frac{1}{j}+\|q_{j}\|.
Since
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|p_{j}\|=\infty
, we have
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|q_{j}\|=\infty
. It follows by (
1
) and
\lim_{j\to\infty}v_{j}=v
that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{j}}(p_{j}-q_{j})=v,

which shows that
v\in C_{5,\infty}(X)
.
∎

The next two propositions are motivated by the following facts: by Corollary 3.1 of
[
4
]
,
C_{3,\infty}(X)
is an algebraic set; by Proposition 2.10 of
[
2
]
,
\dim C_{3,\infty}(X)=\dim X
.

We denote the complex projective space of dimension
m
by
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
. We identify
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
with the open set
\{[x_{0}:x_{1}:\ldots:x_{m}]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\mid x_{0}\neq 0\}
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
through the map
\tau:{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
,
\tau(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=[1:x_{1}:\ldots:x_{m}]
. The hyperplane at infinity is defined by
H_{\infty}:=\{[x_{0}:x_{1}:\ldots:x_{m}]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\mid x_{0}=0\}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be an unbounded pure
k
-dimensional algebraic set. Then,
C_{5,\infty}(X)
is an algebraic set of dimension less than or equal to
2k+1
.

Proof.

Let
A=\{(x,y,v)\in X\times X\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\mid(x-y)\wedge v=0\}
. We have that
A
is an algebraic set of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. We consider
\sigma:A\to X\times X
, defined by
\sigma(x,y,v)=(x,y)
.

We denote by
\Delta\subset X\times X
the subset of
X\times X
such that
(x,y)\in\Delta
if and only if
x=y
. For any
(x,y)\notin\Delta
, the fiber
\sigma^{-1}(x,y)
has dimension equal to
1
(in fact,
\sigma^{-1}(x,y)
is the set of the vectors that are complex proportional to
x-y
). Therefore,
\dim A=2q+1
.

We consider
A
and
\sigma^{-1}(\Delta)
as subsets of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. Let
\mathcal{A}:=\overline{A\setminus\sigma^{-1}(\Delta)}
be the closure of
A\setminus\sigma^{-1}(\Delta)
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, which is an algebraic set. We have
\dim\mathcal{A}=2k+1
.

Let
Z:=\mathcal{A}\cap(H_{\infty}\times H_{\infty}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m})
and
\tau:Z\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
defined by
\tau(x,y,v)=v
. It follows that
Z
is an algebraic set and by definition of
C_{5,\infty}(X)
, we have
\tau(Z)=C_{5,\infty}(X)
. This shows that
C_{5,\infty}(X)
is a constructible set. The proof that
C_{5,\infty}(X)
is closed in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
is straightforward (diagonal argument). Therefore,
C_{5,\infty}(X)
is an algebraic set.

Since
\dim\mathcal{A}=2k+1
, it follows that
\dim Z\leq 2k+1
. Therefore, the dimension of
\tau(Z)=C_{5,\infty}(X)
is less than or equal to
2k+1
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be an unbounded pure
k
-dimensional algebraic set. Then,
C_{4,\infty}(X)
is an algebraic set and
k\leq\dim C_{4,\infty}(X)\leq 2k+1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}(X)
be the vanishing ideal of
X
and let
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\}
be a set of generators of
\mathcal{I}(X)
. We consider
F=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}):{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{l}
. For any
p\in X\setminus{\rm Sing}X
, we have
\dim\ker d_{p}F=k
and
\ker d_{p}F=T_{p}X
, where
d_{p}F
denotes the differential of
F
at
p
; see section 2 of
[
5
]
.

Let
A=\{(p,v)\in X\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\mid v\in\ker d_{p}F\}
. Then
A
is an algebraic set of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. Since
{\rm Sing}X
is an algebraic set, it follows that
{\rm Sing}X\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
is an algebraic set of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. We consider
A
and
{\rm Sing}X\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
as subsets of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. Let
\mathcal{B}:=\overline{A\setminus({\rm Sing}X\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}})
be the closure of
A\setminus({\rm Sing}X\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m})
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, which is an algebraic set.

Let
Z:=\mathcal{B}\cap(H_{\infty}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m})
and let
\tau:Z\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be the restriction to
Z
of the projection
(x,v)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\to v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. Then
Z
is an algebraic set and, by the definition of
C_{4,\infty}(X)
, we have
\tau(Z)=C_{4,\infty}(X)
. This shows that
C_{4,\infty}(X)
is a constructible set. The proof that
C_{4,\infty}(X)
is closed in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
is straightforward (diagonal argument). Therefore,
C_{4,\infty}(X)
is an algebraic set.

By Proposition 2.10 of
[
2
]
,
\dim C_{3,\infty}(X)=\dim X
. By Proposition
2.2
,
\dim C_{5,\infty}(X)\leq 2k+1
. It follows by Proposition
2.1
that
k\leq\dim C_{4,\infty}(X)\leq 2k+1
, which completes the proof.
∎

The above results can be seen as versions at infinity of results of Whitney on tangent cones at a point of an analytic variety; see
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
.

3.
On the dimension of
\dim C_{5,\infty}(X)

The main result of this section is Theorem
3.5
. We begin by recalling the definition of an algebraic region.

Definition 3.1

(See p. 672 of
[
7
]
)
.

Let
m\geq 2
. A set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
is called an algebraic region of type
(k,m)
if there exist vector subspaces
V_{1},V_{2}
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
and positive real numbers
A,B
such that the following conditions hold:
\dim V_{1}=k
,
\dim V_{2}=m-k
,
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
and
\Omega
consists of those
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
for which:

(2)

\|z^{\prime\prime}\|\leq A(1+\|z^{\prime}\|)^{B},

where
z=z^{\prime}+z^{\prime\prime}
, with
z^{\prime}\in V_{1}
and
z^{\prime\prime}\in V_{2}
.

We have the following geometric property for algebraic sets:

Theorem 3.2

(See Theorem 2 of
[
7
]
)
.

A closed analytic subset
V\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
of pure dimension
k
is algebraic if and only if
V
lies in some algebraic region
\Omega
of type
(k,m)
.

The next result is motivated by Theorem 2.9 of
[
2
]
and it is useful for the proof of Theorem
3.5
. We remember that by Proposition
2.2
the cone
C_{5,\infty}(X)
is an algebraic set.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be a pure
k
-dimensional algebraic set. Suppose
C_{5,\infty}(X)
has a pure dimension
k
. Then there exists an algebraic region
\Omega
of type
(k,m)
such that
X
,
C_{3,\infty}(X)
and
C_{5,\infty}(X)
lie in
\Omega
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of Theorem 2.9 of
[
2
]
.

It follows by Theorem
3.2
that there exist an algebraic region
\tilde{\Omega}
of type
(k,m)
and vector spaces
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, with
\dim V_{1}=k
,
\dim V_{2}=m-k
,
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
, and positive real numbers
\tilde{A},B
such that for any
z\in\tilde{\Omega}
(hence, in particular, for
z\in C_{5,\infty}(X)
) we have:

(3)

\|z^{\prime\prime}\|<\tilde{A}(1+\|z^{\prime}\|)^{B},

where
z=z^{\prime}+z^{\prime\prime}
, with
z^{\prime}\in V_{1}
and
z^{\prime\prime}\in V_{2}
.

We assume in (
3
) that
B\geq 1
since
(1+t)^{s}\leq(1+t)
, for any
t\geq 0
and
0\leq s\leq 1
.

We claim that there exists a positive real number
R
such that, for any
w\in X
, we have:

(4)

\|w^{\prime\prime}\|<R(1+\|w^{\prime}\|)^{B},

where
w=w^{\prime}+w^{\prime\prime}
, with
w^{\prime}\in V_{1}
and
w^{\prime\prime}\in V_{2}
.

If the claim is not true, then there exists a sequence
(w_{j})_{j}
in
X
such that
\|w_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|>j(1+\|w_{j}^{\prime}\|)^{B}
and, up to a subsequence, we may suppose that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{w_{j}^{\prime\prime}}{\|w_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|}=y_{0}\in V_{2}
. We have
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|w_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|=\infty
. Since
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
is the direct sum of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|w_{j}\|=\infty
. Then

\frac{1}{j}>\frac{(1+\|w_{j}^{\prime}\|)^{B}}{\|w_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|}\geq\frac{(1+\|w_{j}^{\prime}\|)}{\|w_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|}\geq\frac{\|w_{j}^{\prime}\|}{\|w_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|}.

Let
t_{j}:=(\tilde{A})^{-1}\|w_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|
. We have
\lim_{j\to\infty}t_{j}=\infty
and

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{j}}w_{j}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{j}}\left(w_{j}^{\prime}+w_{j}^{\prime\prime}\right)=\tilde{A}y_{0}.

It follows that
\tilde{A}y_{0}\in C_{3,\infty}(X)
by Definition
1.1
. Now, since
V_{2}
is a linear space and
y_{0}\in V_{2}
, we have
\tilde{A}y_{0}\in V_{2}\cap C_{3,\infty}(X)
. By Proposition
2.1
, it follows that
\tilde{A}y_{0}\in V_{2}\cap C_{5,\infty}(X)
. This is a contradiction, because
\tilde{A}y_{0}
does not verify (
3
).

Therefore, there exists a positive real number
R
such that, for any
w\in X
,

\|w^{\prime\prime}\|<R(1+\|w^{\prime}\|)^{B},

where
w=w^{\prime}+w^{\prime\prime}
, with
w^{\prime}\in V_{1}
and
w^{\prime\prime}\in V_{2}
. Let
A:={\rm max}\{R,\tilde{A}\}
, then
X
and
C_{5,\infty}(X)
lie in an algebraic region
\Omega
of type
(k,m)
, with vector spaces
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, with
\dim V_{1}=k
,
\dim V_{2}=m-k
and positive real numbers
A
and
B
. Since
C_{3,\infty}(X)\subset C_{5,\infty}(X)
, it follows that
C_{3,\infty}(X)
lies in
\Omega
. This finishes the proof.
∎

In the proof of Theorem
3.5
, we use the definition of the degree of an algebraic set. We follow section 11.3 of
[
1
]
. As before, the complex projective space is denoted by
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
and
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
is identified with the open set
\{[x_{0}:x_{1}:\ldots:x_{m}]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\mid x_{0}\neq 0\}
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
through the map
\tau:{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
,
\tau(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=[1:x_{1}:\ldots:x_{m}]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be an algebraic set. The degree of
X
, denoted by
\deg X
, is the degree of its closure in
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
.

The next one is the main result of this section:

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be a pure
k
-dimensional algebraic set. Suppose
C_{5,\infty}(X)
has a pure dimension
k
. Then
X
is an affine space.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
, there exists an algebraic region
\Omega
of type
(k,m)
such that
X
,
C_{3,\infty}(X)
and
C_{5,\infty}(X)
lie in
\Omega
. Then, there exist vector spaces
V_{1},V_{2}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
and constants
A,B
as in Definition
3.1
. We may choose linear coordinates
(x,y)
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
such that
x\in V_{1}
and
y\in V_{2}
. In this coordinate system, we denote by
\pi_{\Omega}
the canonical projection from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
to
V_{1}
, i.e.,
\pi_{\Omega}(x,y)=x
. Moreover, we may assume that
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are orthogonal to each other and that
\pi_{\Omega}
is an orthogonal projection; see Theorem 3 of page 78 of
[
1
]
.

We have the following direct fact concerning the projection
\pi_{\Omega}
:

Lemma 3.6
.

The restrictions
\pi_{\Omega}:X\to V_{1}
and
\pi_{\Omega}:C_{5,\infty}(X)\to V_{1}
are proper maps.

\square

Then, it follows that the closures of
X
and
V_{2}
(respectively, the closures of
C_{5,\infty}(X)
and
V_{2}
) in
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
do not have points at infinity in common (here, the phrase “at infinity” means the set
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\setminus{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
). Then, it follows by Corollary 1 of
[
1
, p. 126]
the following fact:

Lemma 3.7
.

The restriction
\pi_{\Omega}:X\to V_{1}

(
respectively,
\pi_{\Omega}:C_{5,\infty}(X)\to V_{1}
)
is a ramified covering with number of sheets equal to
\deg X

(
respectively,
\deg C_{5,\infty}(X))
.

\square

Since
C_{5,\infty}(X)
is a cone, it follows by Lemma
3.6
that:

(5)

\ker\pi_{\Omega}\cap C_{5,\infty}(X)=\{0\}.

We will show that
\deg X=1
. Let
\Sigma\subset V_{1}
be the critical values of
\pi_{\Omega}:X\to V_{1}
and let
v\notin C_{3,\infty}(\Sigma)
. It follows that
tv\notin\Sigma
, for
t>0
big enough.

If
\deg X>1
, then there are two different liftings
\lambda_{1}(t),\lambda_{2}(t)
of the path
tv
by
\pi_{\Omega}:X\to V_{1}
, with
t>0
big enough. So
\pi_{\Omega}(\lambda_{1}(t))=\pi_{\Omega}(\lambda_{2}(t))=tv
.

It follows that

(6)

\left(\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)\right)\in\ker\pi_{\Omega}.

We may assume that there is a sequence
(t_{j})_{j}
in
{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}t_{j}=\infty
and

(7)

w:=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{1}(t_{j})-\lambda_{2}(t_{j})}{\|\lambda_{1}(t_{j})-\lambda_{2}(t_{j})\|}.

It follows that
w\in\ker\pi_{\Omega}
. We have
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|\lambda_{1}(t_{j})\|=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|\lambda_{2}(t_{j})\|=\infty
since
\pi_{\Omega}
is an orthogonal projection. Hence

w\in\ker\pi_{\Omega}\cap C_{5,\infty}(X).

But this is a contradiction with (
5
) since
w\neq 0
.

Thus,
\deg X=1
. By Proposition 3.3 of
[
3
]
, we have that
X
is an affine space.
∎

4.
Tangent cones and projections

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be an unbounded pure
k
-dimensional algebraic set fixed. We begin with the following definition:

Definition 4.1
.

We say that an
m-k
dimensional vector space
W\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
is transverse to
X
provided that
W\cap C_{3,\infty}(X)=\{0\}
.

The existence of such transverse vector spaces is shown in the next result.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists an
m-k
dimensional vector space transverse to
X
.

Proof.

By Theorem 2.9 of
[
2
]
, there exists an algebraic region
\Omega
of type
(k,m)
such that
X
and
C_{3,\infty}(X)
lie in
\Omega
. Then, there exist vector spaces
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, with
\dim V_{1}=k
,
\dim V_{2}=m-k
,
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}=V_{1}\times V_{2}
, and positive real numbers
A,B
such that for any
z\in\Omega
(hence, in particular, for
z\in X\cup C_{3,\infty}(X)
) we have:

(8)

\|z^{\prime\prime}\|<A(1+\|z^{\prime}\|)^{B},

where
z=z^{\prime}+z^{\prime\prime}
, with
z^{\prime}\in V_{1}
and
z^{\prime\prime}\in V_{2}
.

Since
C_{3,\infty}(X)
is complexly homogeneous in the sense that for any
v\in C_{3,\infty}(X)
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
the vector
\lambda v
also belong to
C_{3,\infty}(X)
, it follows that
V_{2}\cap C_{3,\infty}(X)=\{0\}
. So,
V_{2}
is transverse to
X
.
∎

Let
W\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be a fixed
m-k
dimensional vector space transverse to
X
. This always exists by Lemma
4.2
. We may use the coordinates
(x,y)\in V\times W={\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, where
V
and
W
are orthogonal.

Let
\pi:V\times W\to V
be the canonical projection,
\pi(x,y)=x
. Then:

Lemma 4.3
.

The restriction
\pi:X\to V
is a proper map.

Proof.

We claim that there exist positive constants
C
and
R
such that

X\subset\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}=V\times W\mid\|y\|<C\|x\|\}\cup B_{R}.

Indeed, if the claim is not true, there exists a sequence
\{(x_{j},y_{j})\}_{j}
in
X
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|(x_{j},y_{j})\|=\infty
and
\|y_{j}\|\geq j\|x_{j}\|
, for any
j
. So
\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{x_{j}}{\|y_{j}\|}=0
. Up to a subsequence, we may assume that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{y_{j}}{\|y_{j}\|}=y_{0}
. Then,
y_{0}\in W
and

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{\|y_{j}\|}(x_{j},y_{j})=(0,y_{0}).

By Definition
1.1
, it follows that
(0,y_{0})\in C_{3,\infty}(X)
. Hence
(0,y_{0})\in C_{3,\infty}(X)\cap W
. This is a contradiction, because
W
is transverse to
X
. Therefore, the claim holds.

Now, it is a direct consequence of the claim that
\pi
restricted to
X
is a proper map.
∎

It follows by Lemmma
4.3
that the closures of
X
and
W
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
do not have points at infinity in common (here, the phrase “at infinity” means the set
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\setminus{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
). Then, it follows by Corollary 1 of
[
1
, p. 126]
the following fact:

Lemma 4.4
.

The restriction
\pi:X\to V
is a ramified covering with number of sheets equal to
\deg X
.
\square

Now we are ready to give the

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The inclusion
{\rm Sing}X\subset{\rm Sing}\,\pi
is always true.

Now, assume that there is no a positive constant
R
such that
{\rm Sing}\,\pi\setminus\bar{B}_{R}\subset{\rm Sing}X\setminus\bar{B}_{R}
. Then, there exists a sequence
(p_{j})_{j}
in
{\rm Sing}\,\pi\setminus{\rm Sing}X
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|p_{j}\|=\infty
.

Then, we can take
w_{j}\in T_{p_{j}}X
,
\|w_{j}\|=1
, such that
d_{p_{j}}\pi(w_{j})=0
. Since
\pi
is a linear map, we have
d_{p_{j}}\pi(w_{j})=\pi(w_{j})
. Therefore,
w_{j}\in W=\ker\pi
, for any
j
.

Up to a subsequence, we may assume that
\lim_{j\to\infty}w_{j}=w_{0}
. Then
w_{0}\in W\cap C_{4,\infty}(X)
which is a contradiction.
∎

We have fixed an
m-k
plane
W
transverse to
X
and coordinates
(x,y)\in V\times W={\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, where
V
and
W
are orthogonal. We denote
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m-k})
and we set
W^{i}:=\{(0,y)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\mid y_{i}=0\},
for
i=1,\ldots,m-k
. We denote
\pi_{i}:{\mathbb{C}}^{m}=V\times W\to{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{C}}^{k+1}
the projection defined by
\pi_{i}(x,y)=(x,y_{i})
. So we have
\ker\pi_{i}=W^{i}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Since
C_{5,\infty}(X)\cap W^{i}=\{0\}
, it follows by Proposition
2.1
that
C_{3,\infty}(X)\cap W^{i}=C_{4,\infty}(X)\cap W^{i}=\{0\}
.

The restriction of
\pi_{i}
to
X
is a proper map since
C_{3,\infty}(X)\cap W^{i}=\{0\}
. In particular,
\pi_{i}:X\to{\mathbb{C}}^{k+1}
is a closed map. It follows that
\pi_{i}(X)\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{k+1}
is a closed constructible set and, therefore,
\pi_{i}(X)
is an algebraic set of dimension
k
. It follows that
\pi_{i}(X)
is a hypersurface in
{\mathbb{C}}^{k+1}
.

Claim 1
: for sufficiently large
R
, the restriction
\pi_{i}:X\setminus\bar{B}_{R}\to\pi(X\setminus\bar{B}_{R})
is an injective map.

Indeed, if the claim does not hold, then there exist sequences
(p_{j})_{j}
and
(q_{j})_{j}
in
X
such that
p_{j}\neq q_{j}
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|p_{j}\|=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|q_{j}\|=\infty
and
\pi_{i}(p_{j})=\pi_{i}(q_{j})
. In particular,
p_{j}-q_{j}\in W^{i}
. Up to a subsequence, we may assume that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{p_{j}-q_{j}}{\|p_{j}-q_{j}\|}=w_{0}
.

It follows that
w_{0}\neq 0
. By Definition
1.1
,
w_{0}\in C_{5,\infty}(X)
. Moreover,
w_{0}\in W^{i}
since
p_{j}-q_{j}\in W^{i}
for any
j
.

Then,
w_{0}\in W^{i}\cap C_{5,\infty}(X)
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
Claim 1
holds.

Claim 2
: for sufficiently large
R
, the restriction
\pi_{i}:X\setminus\bar{B}_{R}\to\pi(X\setminus\bar{B}_{R})
is a proper map.

Suppose that for sufficiently large
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have that
\pi_{i}:X\setminus\bar{B}_{n}\to\pi(X\setminus\bar{B}_{n})
is not proper. Then, since
\pi_{i}:X\to{\mathbb{C}}^{k+1}
is proper, there exists a sequence
(p_{jn})_{j}
in
X\setminus\bar{B}_{n}
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\pi_{i}(p_{jn})=y_{n}\in\pi(X\setminus\bar{B}_{n})
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}p_{jn}=p_{n}
, where
p_{n}\in X
and
\|p_{n}\|=n
. Thus, there exists
x_{n}\in X\setminus\bar{B}_{n}
such that
\pi_{i}(x_{n})=y_{n}
. In particular,
x_{n}\neq p_{n}
.

Then, up to a subsequence, we may assume that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_{n}-x_{n}}{\|p_{n}-x_{n}\|}=w
. It follows that
w\neq 0
. Since
p_{n}-x_{n}\in W^{i}
for any
n
, we have
w\in W^{i}
.

By construction,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|p_{n}\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}\|=\infty
. Hence,
w\in C_{5,\infty}(X).

Then
w\in W^{i}\cap C_{5,\infty}(X)
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
Claim 2
holds.

It follows directly from
Claim 1
and
Claim 2
that
\pi_{i}:X\setminus\bar{B}_{R}\to\pi(X\setminus\bar{B}_{R})
is a homeomorphism for sufficiently large
R
.
∎

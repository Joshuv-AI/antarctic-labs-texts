Bhabha scattering
in the gauge-Higgs unification

Abstract
We examine effects of Z ′ Z^{\prime} bosons in gauge-Higgs unification (GHU) models at e + ​ e − → e + ​ e − \mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-} Bhabha scatterings. We evaluate differential cross sections in Bhabha scatterings including Z ′ Z^{\prime} bosons in two types of SO ⁡ ( 5 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) × SU ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathrm{SO}(5)\times\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(3) GHU models. We find that deviations of differential cross sections in the GHU models from those in the SM can be seen at s = 250 ​ GeV \sqrt{s}=250\,\mathrm{GeV} . With 80 % 80\,\% -longitudinally polarized electron and 30 % 30\,\% -longitudinally polarized positron beams, the left-right asymmetries in the GHU A- and B-models are resolved at more than 3 ​ σ 3\,\sigma at L int = 250 ​ fb − 1 L_{\rm int}=250\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1} . We also show that Bhabha scattering with scattering angle less than 100 mrad can be safely used as luminosity measurements at e + ​ e − \mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-} colliders since the effects of Z ′ Z^{\prime} bosons are well suppressed for small scattering angle. We propose a new observable which can be measured at future TeV-scale e + ​ e − \mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-} linear colliders.

Gauge-Higgs unification

In GHU A- and B-models the electroweak
\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)
symmetry
is embedded in
\mathrm{SO}(5)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{X}
symmetry in the Randall-Sundrum warped space
[
46

whose metric is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z^{2}}\left[\eta_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}+\frac{dz^{2}}{k^{2}}\right],\quad 1\leq z\leq z_{L}=e^{kL}

(3)

\eta_{\mu\nu}=\diag(-1,+1,+1,+1)

We refer two 4D hyperplanes at
z=1
and
z=z_{L}
as the UV and IR branes, respectively.
The
\mathrm{SO}(5)
symmetry is broken to
\mathrm{SO}(4)\simeq\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R}
by the boundary conditions at
z=1,\,z_{L}
and
the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{X}
symmetry is broken to
\mathrm{U}(1)_{Y}
by a scalar field
localized on the UV brane.

\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{Y}
symmetry is broken to the
electromagnetic
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\rm EM}
symmetry by
the VEV of the
z
-component gauge fields of
\mathrm{SO}(5)/\mathrm{SO}(4)
.
The VEV is related to the gauge-invariant AB phase
\theta_{H}
.

Bhabha scattering in
\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}
colliders

We consider the
\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}
scattering in the center-of-mass frame.
In this frame, the Mandelstam variables
(s,t,u)
are given by

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=4E^{2},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=-\frac{s}{2}(1-\cos\theta)=-s\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2},

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=-\frac{s}{2}(1+\cos\theta)=-s\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}

(6)

where
E
is the energy of initial electron and positron, and
\theta
is the scattering angle of the electron.
Since the
\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}
scattering process consists
both
s-
and
t-
channel processes,
the scattering amplitude is written in terms of the following six building blocks:

\displaystyle S_{LL}=S_{LL}(s)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i}\frac{\ell_{V_{i}}^{2}}{s-M_{V_{i}}^{2}+iM_{V_{i}}\Gamma_{V_{i}}},

\displaystyle S_{RR}=S_{RR}(s)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i}\frac{r_{V_{i}}^{2}}{s-M_{V_{i}}^{2}+iM_{V_{i}}\Gamma_{V_{i}}},

\displaystyle S_{LR}=S_{LR}(s)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i}\frac{\ell_{V_{i}}r_{V_{i}}}{s-M_{V_{i}}^{2}+iM_{V_{i}}\Gamma_{V_{i}}},

\displaystyle T_{LL}=T_{LL}(s,\theta)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i}\frac{\ell_{V_{i}}^{2}}{t-M_{V_{i}}^{2}+iM_{V_{i}}\Gamma_{V_{i}}},

\displaystyle T_{RR}=T_{RR}(s,\theta)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i}\frac{r_{V_{i}}^{2}}{t-M_{V_{i}}^{2}+iM_{V_{i}}\Gamma_{V_{i}}},

\displaystyle T_{LR}=T_{LR}(s,\theta)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i}\frac{\ell_{V_{i}}r_{V_{i}}}{t-M_{V_{i}}^{2}+iM_{V_{i}}\Gamma_{V_{i}}},

(7)

where
M_{V_{i}}
and
\Gamma_{V_{i}}
are the mass and width of the vector boson
V_{i}
.

\ell_{V_{i}}
and
r_{V_{i}}
are left- and right-handed couplings of electrons to the vector boson

V_{i}
(
V_{0}=\gamma
,
V_{1}=Z
), respectively.
In particular, we have

\ell_{\gamma}=r_{\gamma}=Q_{\mathrm{e}}e
,
Q_{\mathrm{e}}=-1
,

\ell_{Z}=\frac{e}{\sin\theta_{W}^{0}\cos\theta_{W}^{0}}[I_{\mathrm{e}}^{3}-Q_{e}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{0}]
,

r_{Z}=\frac{e}{\sin\theta_{W}^{0}\cos\theta_{W}^{0}}[-Q_{e}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{0}]
,

I^{3}_{\mathrm{e}}=-\frac{1}{2}
in the SM.
Here
e
,
I_{\mathrm{e}}^{3}
and
\theta_{W}^{0}
are the electromagnetic coupling, weak isospin of the electron
and the bare Weinberg angle defined in (
5
), respectively.

When initial state electrons and positrons are longitudinally polarized,
the differential cross section is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d\cos\theta}(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\biggl\{(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})\frac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}}{d\cos\theta}+(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})\frac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}}{d\cos\theta}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})\frac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}}{d\cos\theta}+(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})\frac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}}{d\cos\theta}\biggr\},

(8)

where
P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}
and
P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}}
are the polarization of the electron and positron beam, respectively.
P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}=+1
(
P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}}=+1
) denotes purely right-handed electrons (positrons)
[
54
,
55
]
.

\sigma_{\mathrm{e}_{X}^{-}\mathrm{e}_{Y}^{+}}
(
X,Y=L,R
) denotes the cross section
for left-handed or right-handed electron and positron.
When the electron mass is neglected, these cross sections
are given by

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}}{d\cos\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi s}\left(u^{2}|S_{LL}+T_{LL}|^{2}+t^{2}|S_{LR}|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}}{d\cos\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi s}\left(u^{2}|S_{RR}+T_{RR}|^{2}+t^{2}|S_{LR}|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}}{d\cos\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}}{d\cos\theta}=\frac{1}{8\pi s}\cdot\left(s^{2}|T_{LR}|^{2}\right).

(9)

When
s,t\ll M_{Z}^{2}
,
the cross section is approximated by the one at the QED level,
where we obtain
S_{LL}=S_{RR}=S_{LR}=e^{2}/s
and

T_{LL}=T_{RR}=T_{LR}=e^{2}/t
, and

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{\rm QED}}{d\cos\theta}(P_{e^{-}},P_{e^{+}})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{4}}{16\pi s}\left\{(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})\frac{t^{4}+u^{4}}{s^{2}t^{2}}+(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})\frac{s^{2}}{t^{2}}\right\}.

(10)

For unpolarized electron or positron beams, the above expression reduces to
a familiar formula

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{\rm unpolarized}_{\rm QED}}{d\cos\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{4}}{16\pi s}\frac{s^{4}+t^{4}+u^{4}}{s^{2}t^{2}}.

(11)

We also note that in terms of building blocks (
7
) we
can write down components of s-, t-channels, and interference terms as

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d\cos\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\sigma^{\text{s-channel}}}{d\cos\theta}+\frac{d\sigma^{\text{t-channel}}}{d\cos\theta}+\frac{d\sigma^{\text{interference}}}{d\cos\theta},

(12)

where each component is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{\text{s-channel}}}{d\cos\theta}(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{32\pi s}\biggl\{(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})\left[u^{2}|S_{RR}|^{2}+t^{2}|S_{LR}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})\left[u^{2}|S_{LL}|^{2}+t^{2}|S_{LR}|^{2}\right]\biggr\},

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{\text{t-channel}}}{d\cos\theta}(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{32\pi s}\biggl\{(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})s^{2}|T_{LR}|^{2}+(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})s^{2}|T_{LR}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})u^{2}|T_{RR}|^{2}+(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})u^{2}|T_{LL}|^{2}\biggr\},

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{\text{interference}}}{d\cos\theta}(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi s}u^{2}\biggl\{(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})\mathrm{Re}(S_{RR}T_{RR}^{*})

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}})(1+P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})\mathrm{Re}(S_{LL}T_{LL}^{*})\biggr\}.

(13)

When initial electrons and/or positrons are longitudinally polarized, one can measure left-right asymmetries.
The left-right asymmetry of polarized cross sections is given by

\displaystyle A_{\text{LR}}(P_{-},P_{+})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\sigma(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}=-P_{-},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}}=-P_{+})-\sigma(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}=+P_{-},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}}=+P_{+})}{\sigma(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}=-P_{-},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}}=-P_{+})+\sigma(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}=+P_{-},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}}=+P_{+})}

\displaystyle=(P_{-}-P_{+})\cdot\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}-\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}}{(1+P_{-}P_{+})(\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}})+(1-P_{-}P_{+})(\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}})},

\displaystyle 1\geq P_{-}\geq 0,\quad 1\geq P_{+}\geq-1,

(14)

where the cross section in a given bin
[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]
is given by

\sigma\equiv\int_{\cos\theta_{1}}^{\cos\theta_{2}}\frac{d\sigma}{d\cos\theta}d\cos\theta
.
We have used
\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}

because
\frac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}}{d\cos\theta}-\frac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}}{d\cos\theta}=0
.
We can also define the left-right asymmetry of the differential cross section as

\displaystyle A_{\text{LR}}(P_{-},P_{+},\cos\theta)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\dfrac{d\sigma}{d\cos\theta}(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}=-P_{-},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}}=-P_{+})-\dfrac{d\sigma}{d\cos\theta}(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}=+P_{-},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}}=+P_{+})}{\dfrac{d\sigma}{d\cos\theta}(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}=-P_{-},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}}=-P_{+})+\dfrac{d\sigma}{d\cos\theta}(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}}=+P_{-},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}}=+P_{+})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(P_{-}+P_{+})\left(\dfrac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}}{d\cos\theta}-\dfrac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}}{d\cos\theta}\right)+(P_{-}-P_{+})\left(\dfrac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}}{d\cos\theta}-\dfrac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}}{d\cos\theta}\right)}{(1+P_{-}P_{+})\left(\dfrac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}}{d\cos\theta}+\dfrac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}}{d\cos\theta}\right)+(1-P_{-}P_{+})\left(\dfrac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}}{d\cos\theta}+\dfrac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}}{d\cos\theta}\right)}

\displaystyle=(P_{-}-P_{+})\cdot\frac{\Sigma_{LR-RL}}{(1+P_{-}P_{+})\Sigma_{LL+RR}+(1-P_{-}P_{+})\Sigma_{LR+RL}},

(15)

where we have used
d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{L}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{L}}/d\cos\theta=d\sigma_{\mathrm{e}^{-}_{R}\mathrm{e}^{+}_{R}}/d\cos\theta
and defined

\displaystyle\Sigma_{LL+RR}

\displaystyle\equiv 2s^{2}|T_{LR}|^{2},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{LR+RL}

\displaystyle\equiv u^{2}(|S_{LL}+T_{LL}|^{2}+|S_{RR}+T_{RR}|^{2})+2t^{2}|S_{LR}|^{2},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{LR-RL}

\displaystyle\equiv u^{2}(|S_{LL}+T_{LL}|^{2}-|S_{RR}+T_{RR}|^{2}).

(16)

In linear colliders we can measure the cross sections
for
(P_{\mathrm{e}^{-}},P_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})=(P_{-},P_{+})
,
(P_{-},-P_{+})
,
(-P_{-},P_{+})

and
(-P_{-},-P_{+})
.
Combining these quantities, one can make a new observable quantity
which does not depend on the value of
P_{\pm}
.
In
\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}
scatterings we have

A_{LR}(P_{-},+P_{+},\cos\theta)
and
A_{LR}(P_{-},-P_{+},\cos\theta)
as independent observables
and one may define the following non-trivial quantity:

\displaystyle A_{X}(\cos\theta)\equiv\frac{\Sigma_{LL+RR}-\Sigma_{LR+RL}}{\Sigma_{LL+RR}+\Sigma_{LR+RL}}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{P_{-}P_{+}}\cdot\dfrac{(P_{-}-P_{+})A_{\text{LR}}(P_{-},-P_{+},\cos\theta)-(P_{-}+P_{+})A_{\text{LR}}(P_{-},+P_{+}\cos\theta)}{(P_{-}-P_{+})A_{\text{LR}}(P_{-},-P_{+},\cos\theta)+(P_{-}+P_{+})A_{\text{LR}}(P_{-},+P_{+},\cos\theta)}.

(17)

where the second equality holds only when
P_{\pm}\neq 0
and
|P_{+}|\neq|P_{-}|
.
As is evident in the first line of (
17
),
A_{X}(\cos\theta)
is independent of
the magnitudes of
polarization
P_{\pm}
.
This quantity may be used to explore NP beyond the SM as discussed below.

Since
\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}
scattering contains
t
-channel processes,
forward scatterings dominate. Therefore unlike the
\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}\to f\bar{f}
(
f\neq\mathrm{e}^{-}
) scattering,
the forward-backward asymmetry of
\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}
scattering is a less-meaningful quantity.

We note that all of the above formulas can be applied
to
\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
(
\ell=\mu,\tau
) scatterings.

5
Summary

In this paper we examined the effects of
Z^{\prime}
bosons
in the gauge-Higgs unification (GHU) models in the
\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}
(Bhabha) scatterings.
We first formulated differential cross sections in Bhabha scattering including
Z^{\prime}
bosons.
We then numerically evaluated the deviations of differential cross sections in the two

\mathrm{SO}(5)\times\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)
GHU models (the A- and B-models) at
\sqrt{s}=250\,\text{GeV}
.
We found that at
L_{\mathrm{int}}=2\,\mathrm{ab}^{-1}
with unpolarized
\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}
beams, the deviation due to
Z^{\prime}
bosons
in the GHU models from the SM can be clearly seen.
We also found that for
80\,\%
-longitudinally polarized electron and
30\,\%
-polarized positron beams,
deviations of the differential cross sections from the SM
become as large as a few percent for
\cos\theta\sim 0.2
, and that
the A-model and the B-model are well distinguished
at more than
3\,\sigma
significance
at
L_{\mathrm{int}}=250\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
.
We also checked the effects of
Z^{\prime}
bosons are negligible
for the scattering angle smaller than
100
mrad at
\sqrt{s}=250\,\text{GeV}
. Therefore Bhabha scatterings at very small
\theta

can be safely used as the measurements of the luminosity in the
\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}
collisions.
Finally we introduced the new observable
A_{X}
.
We numerically evaluated it at
\sqrt{s}=250\,\text{GeV}
and

3\,\text{TeV}
.
Effects of the GHU models on
A_{X}

can be seen at future TeV-scale
\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}
colliders.

In this paper the effects of
Z^{\prime}
bosons are calculated at the Born level. Higher-order QED effects should be taken
into account for more precise evaluation
[
65
,
66
]
.

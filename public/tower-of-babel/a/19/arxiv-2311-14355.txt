Unveiling the pairing Symmetry of the superconducting Sn/Si(111) via angle-resolved THz pump spectroscopy
Mattia Iannetti 1, Tommaso Cea 1, Cesare Tresca 2, Lara Benfatto 3, Gianni Profeta 1,2

Abstract
Doping tin surface epitaxially grown on silicon, Sn/Si(111), with boron atoms yields the appearance of a superconducting (SC) phase below T c ∼ 4 − 5 T_{c}\sim 4-5 K. Even though the pairing mechanism remains unknown, experimental evidence of chiral d − d- wave superconductivity has been recently reported, then ruling out a phonon-mediated pairing. Here we study theoretically the SC phase and symmetries of the doped Sn/Si(111) within a t − J t-J model. We analyze the photo-excitation of the system by intense THz pulses and show that the polarization dependence of the induced current can distinguish between different symmetries of the SC gap, thus providing a novel experimental mean to investigate the spectroscopic features of the Sn/Si(111) across the SC transition.

III
Tracking the evolution of the GS in a time-dependent electric field

We introduce a uniform vector potential,
\boldsymbol{A}(t)
, in the Hamiltonian (
3
)
via the Peierls substitution:
\xi^{0}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\rightarrow\xi^{0}_{\boldsymbol{k}-e\boldsymbol{A}(t)}
,
where
e
is the electron charge and
c=1
.
It is convenient to describe the dynamics of the meanfield GS in terms of the quantum average of the Anderson’s pseudo-spin:

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\left\langle\Psi^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\boldsymbol{\tau}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{k}}\right\rangle
, where:

\Psi_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\left(c_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow},c^{\dagger}_{-\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow}\right)^{T}
.
At the meanfield level,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
describes a precession motion given by the equations:

\displaystyle\frac{\,d\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}{\,dt}(t)=2\boldsymbol{b}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(t)\times\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(t),

(5)

with:
\boldsymbol{b}_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\left(\Delta^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{k}},-\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{\boldsymbol{k}},\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}\right)
.
Imposing:

\Delta^{\prime}_{\alpha}=-\frac{J}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})\sigma^{x}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
,

\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}=\frac{J}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})\sigma^{y}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
and

\delta t_{\alpha}=-\frac{J}{2N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})\sigma^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
,
one has to solve the Eqs. (
5
) self-consistently at any time,
which is equivalent to solving the Bloch’s equations.
In this way one can track the dynamics induced by the incident field on both the gap’s function,
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{k}}(t)
,
and the occupation number,
n_{\boldsymbol{k}}(t)
, including all the non-linear effects emerging for very intense fields.
While
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{k}}(t)
describes the evolution of the SC condensate,
n_{\boldsymbol{k}}(t)
defines the electromagnetic current flowing through the sample, according to:

\boldsymbol{J}(t)=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{A}}\xi^{0}_{\boldsymbol{k}-e\boldsymbol{A}(t)}n_{\boldsymbol{k}}(t).

(6)

Experimentally,
\boldsymbol{J}
can be measured either directly by the voltage difference at the edge of the sample, or indirectly by the electric field reflected by the sample. Transmission measurements are not allowed because of the Si substrate.

On this respect, the present calculations performed in the clean limit should remain valid for what concerns the dependence of

V
Conclusions

We study the superconductivity reported recently in the Sn/Si(111)
ad-layers doped with boron.
Assuming that the interactions responsible for the pairing are of AFM nature, we describe the system by the
t-J
model.
Our meanfield analysis well reproduces the value of the AFM coupling,
J
, predicted by first principle calculations and the
d
-wave symmetry of the SC OP claimed by recent experimental findings
Ming et al. 2023
.
By perturbing the system with an intense THz electromagnetic field, we focus on the leading non-linear effects, the THG, showing that its dependence on the polarization of the incident field can distinguish between the chiral
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}+id_{xy}
- and the pure
d
-wave symmetry of the SC gap.
Therefore, our work i) provides a minimal efficient theoretical model for describing the pairing in the
emerging superconductor Sn/Si(111) and
ii) paves the way for unveiling the pairing symmetry therein
by means of the THz pump protocol, a method that has succeeded so far in investigating both conventional and high-
T_{c}
cuprate superconductors.

\displaystyle H_{J}=\frac{J}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{p}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\cos(\vec{q}\cdot\vec{a}_\alpha)\left(c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{q}/2,\uparrow}c_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}/2,\downarrow}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{q}/2,\downarrow}c_{\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{q}/2,\uparrow}-c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{q}/2,\uparrow}c_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}/2,\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{q}/2,\downarrow}c_{\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{q}/2,\downarrow}\right).

(S6)

To perform the meanfield approximation of
H_{J}
,
we consider all the quantum averages preserving spin and momentum,
we neglect the quantum fluctuations and we require the time-reversal symmetry.
This yields:

\displaystyle H^{MF}_{J}=-\frac{J}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{p}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\left[2\left(\left\langle c_{-\boldsymbol{p}\downarrow}c_{\boldsymbol{p}\uparrow}\right\rangle c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{-\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow}+h.c.\right)+\frac{n_{\boldsymbol{p}}}{2}\sum_{\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}c_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}\right]+E_{0},

(S7)

where:

\displaystyle E_{0}=\frac{2J}{N}\sum_{\alpha}\left|\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})\left\langle c_{-\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow}c_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow}\right\rangle\right|^{2}+\frac{J}{4N}\sum_{\alpha}\left[\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})n_{\boldsymbol{k}}\right]^{2}

(S8)

is an energy constant and

n_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\sum_{\sigma}\left\langle c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}c_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}\right\rangle
.
Note that we have neglected the terms involving only a constant shift of the chemical potential in the Eq. (
S7
). The meanfield Hamiltonian is the sum:

\displaystyle H_{tJ}^{MF}=H_{tb}+H^{MF}_{J}.

(S9)

Now we define:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2J}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})\left\langle c_{-\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow}c_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow}\right\rangle\text{ , }\Delta_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\sum_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})\Delta_{\alpha}

(S10a)

\displaystyle\delta t_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{J}{2N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})n_{\boldsymbol{k}}\text{ , }\delta\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\sum_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})\delta t_{\alpha}\text{ , }\xi_{\boldsymbol{{k}}}=\xi^{0}_{\boldsymbol{{k}}}+\delta\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}},

(S10b)

which allows to write
H_{tJ}^{MF}
as:

\displaystyle H^{MF}_{tJ}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left[\sum_{\sigma}\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}c_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}+\left(\Delta_{\boldsymbol{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{-\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow}+h.c.\right)\right]+E_{0},

(S11)

and:

\displaystyle E_{0}=\frac{N}{J}\sum_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\left|\Delta_{\alpha}\right|^{2}}{2}+\delta t^{2}_{\alpha}\right).

(S12)

\Delta_{\alpha}
and
\delta t_{\alpha}
have to be obtained self-consistently by using the Hamiltonian (
S11
) to compute the quantum averages. This yields the self-consistent equations:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})\frac{\Delta_{\boldsymbol{k}}}{E_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\tanh\left(E_{\boldsymbol{k}}/2T\right),

(S13a)

\displaystyle\delta t_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J}{2N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})\frac{\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}{E_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\tanh\left(E_{\boldsymbol{k}}/2T\right),

(S13b)

that must be solved along with the equation for the electronic density:

\displaystyle n=1-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\frac{\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}{E_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\tanh\left(E_{\boldsymbol{k}}/2T\right),

(S14)

which sets the value of
\mu
. In this work we consider:
n=0.9
, corresponding to the hole doping:
\delta=10\%
.

\displaystyle\epsilon_{MF}=\frac{E_{0}}{N}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left[\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}-E_{\boldsymbol{k}}\tanh\left(E_{\boldsymbol{k}}/2T\right)\right]+\mu n,

II
Evaluation of the AFM coupling from first principles calculations

We evaluate the nearest-neighbor exchange constant (
J
) between different Sn atoms through the Hubbard model described by the following Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle H=J_{fp}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\boldsymbol{S}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{j}

(S16)

We adopted a super-cell approach considering a 2
\sqrt{3}\times 3
cell, with respect to the Si(111) surface periodicity, with different collinear
magnetic configurations: two Sn atoms per cell magnetized ferromagnetic or anti-ferromagnetic each other.

Accordingly with literature
Profeta and Tosatti 2007
;
Tresca et al. 2018
;
Tresca and Calandra 2021
;
Tresca et al. 2023
, we modeled the
\alpha
-Sn/Si(111) surface by considering a layer of Sn atoms on top of three Si-bilayers; the Si-dangling bonds at the opposite side are capped with hydrogen atoms fixed to the relaxed positions obtained by capping the pristine Si(111) surface at one side.

The atomic position of the first five atomic layers are optimized (Sn atoms and the first two Si-bi-layers) whereas the remaining bi-layer is fixed to the Si bulk positions. More than 15 Å of vacuum is included.
Density functional theory calculations are performed with the
Quantum-Espresso
Giannozzi et al. 2009
;
Giannozzi et al. 2017
code. We used ultrasoft pseudopotentials with an energy cutoff up to 45 Ry. Integration over the Brillouin zone was performed using uniform 12
\times 6\times 1
grid.

The magnetic calculations were done using the semilocal approximation for the exchange and correlation term in a GGA+U framework
Profeta and Tosatti 2007
.
As showh in Fig.
S3
, the obtained results appear to be ”slightly” affected by the choice of the
U
parameter: in the range
U=
2÷5 eV,
J_{fp}
is monotonically decreasing as a function of
U
, without change of sign or order of magnitude.

The most favourable magnetic configuration is the anti-ferromagnetic one, with an energy difference of the order of
\Delta E_{FM-AFM}\sim 35
÷15 meV with respect to the the ferromagnetic phase.
The calculated antiferromagnetic exchange coupling results
J_{fp}\sim 10
meV (for
U=4
eV,
\Delta E_{FM-AFM}=0.021
eV)
Profeta and Tosatti 2007
.

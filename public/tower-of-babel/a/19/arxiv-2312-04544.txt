Abstract
. We establish a theorem concerning the normal forms by examining the newly presented concept of μ \mu -dichotomy. This work establishes the nonresonance condition based on the associated spectrum of this general nonautonomous hyperbolicity.

1.
Introduction

Since the contributions of H. Poincaré
[
11
]
, there has been a significant focus on the dynamical systems of the normal forms. This technique allows us to face the problem of linearizing a nonlinear system in the neighborhood of an equilibrium point; to address this question in a context of analytic linearization for analytic functions, the author introduces a condition referred to as the
nonresonant condition
which essentially states that the eigenvalues of the linearization around the equilibrium point satisfy

\lambda_{1}\neq\sum_{j=1}^{d}m_{j}\lambda_{j}
, for all
m_{1},\dots,m_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
with
2\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}m_{j}.
Recall that in the case of
C^{k}
vector fields, the
C^{r}
linearization, with
1\leq r\leq k\leq+\infty,
was given by S. Sternberg in
[
17
,
18
]
who regarded the criterion
2\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}m_{j}\leq k
instead of the one utilized by Poincaré.

Until the end of the previous century, the focus of normal forms theory primarily revolved around autonomous differentiable systems. The core results of this theory may be found in a comprehensive manner on V.I. Arnold
[
1
, Chapter 5]
, L. Stolovitch
[
19
]
and S. Wiggins
[
20
, Chapter 19]
.

1.1.
Nonautonomous Formal Norms

In
[
16
]
, S. Siegmund made a notable breakthrough by expanding on Poincaré’s result using the spectrum associated with the exponential dichotomy, which may be interpreted as a kind of hyperbolicity in a nonautonomous context. Later, in
[
4
]
, the same author joint with L.V. Cuong and T.S. Doan extend the Sternberg Theorem to the context of nonautonomous differential systems. Notice that both previous work, the nonresonce condition is fashioned in terms of the Sacker-Sell spectrum (see
[
12
,
14
]
).

In
[
21
]
, X. Zhang examines the nonuniform exponential dichotomy and establishes a normal form theorem inside this nonuniform framework, assuming nonresonance; this result is presented in terms of the spectrum of this dichotomy. Furthermore, it is important to note that in the work done by J. Chu
et al.

[
3
]
, the spectrum of this nonautonomous hyperbolicity is also formulated.

1.2.
Structure and novelty of the article

In a recent study, C. Silva
[
13
]
analizes the concept of
\mu
-dichotomy, which extends beyond prior dichotomies. Additionally, the author constructs the spectrum linked to this novel dichotomy. The main objective of this work is to develop the theory of normal forms for a differentiable system that exhibits
\mu
-dichotomy; this will be done by establishing nonresonance condition based on the spectrum associated with this nonautonomous hyperbolicty.

In Section 2, we establish the fundamental components necessary for this study. We establish the notations and significant definitions for both the linear and nonlinear equations under investigation. In addition, we provide clear definition for the concept of equivalence and develop the premise that we will use to demonstrate this notion.

Our main results are stated and proven in Section 3. Firstly, we provide the result of elimination nonresonant Taylor terms; this is done under the assumption that the linear component of the system exhibits a concept of uniformly bounded growth, while the nonlinearities are considered to be nonuniformly admissible (detailed definitions and examples may be found in Section 2). Secondly, we demonstrate the enhancement of this result when the nonlinearities are uniformly admissible, enabling us to derive the normal forms theorem.

In Section 4, we provide a way to address this problem when the linear component exhibits only nonuniform bounded growth. We also discuss the challenges that arise in this scenario when attempting to produce a result of normal forms.

2.2.
The perturbation

Now we consider a

Definition 2.10
.

We say that a map
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{Dom}_{F}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

and open set

it verifies:

ii)

F(\cdot,x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}I\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is measurable for all

.

Moreover, for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we say

is

-Carathéodory class
if:

iii)

for almost

and all

iv)

It is well known that under these conditions and if

Definition 2.11
.

Consider a growth rate
\mu
. We say that a measurable function
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}

\mu

If, furthermore

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\int_{-\infty}^{t}\psi(s)\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\delta}ds+\int_{t}^{\infty}\psi(s)\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{\delta}ds\right\}<+\infty,

we say that

uniformly
\mu
-admissible
.

For
\delta>0
, let us denote

\zeta_{\psi,\mu,\delta}^{+},\,\zeta_{\psi,\mu,\delta}^{-}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\zeta_{\psi,\mu,\delta}^{+}(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}\psi(s)\mu(s)^{-\delta}ds\,\quad\text{
and }\quad\zeta_{\psi,\mu,\delta}^{-}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\psi(s)\mu(s)^{\delta}ds.

Note that
\psi

\zeta_{\delta}^{+}

are continuous well defined functions for every

Remark 2.12
.

A simple but useful observation is that both
\mu

Example 2.13

For the exponential growth rate,
i.e.

\mu(t)=e^{t}

Example 2.14
.

Hörmander class functions are very important in the study of pseudo-differential operators, since they represent suitable symbols for them (

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert(-h)^{i}(t,x)\right\rVert}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\frac{MK^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}}{\mathbf{k}!}\right]^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert(-h)^{i-2}(t,x)\right\rVert}^{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\frac{MK^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}}{\mathbf{k}!}\right]^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{(i-2)|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x\right\rVert}^{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}},

hence

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\vartheta(t,x)\right\rVert}\leq\left[\frac{MK^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}}{\mathbf{k}!}\right]^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}\frac{1}{1-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x\right\rVert}^{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}},

which implies (
3.17
).

•

Step 4: For
m=2,\dots,|\mathbf{k}|-1
we have
D_{2}^{m}H^{-1}(t,0)=0
,
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D^{|\mathbf{k}|}_{2}H^{-1}(t,0)\right\rVert}\leq n^{|\mathbf{k}|}M
and for
m=|\mathbf{k}|+1,\dots,\ell
there is a constant
\widetilde{M}>0
such that
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D^{m}_{2}H^{-1}(t,0)\right\rVert}\leq\widetilde{M}.

The first two statements follow trivially from (
3.18
), (
3.17
) and (
3.16
). Now, for
m=|\mathbf{k}|+1,\dots,\ell
it is clear that
D^{m}_{2}H^{-1}(t,0)=D^{m}_{2}\vartheta(t,0)
.

Chose now
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|\mathbf{k}|^{p}-1>\ell
. We have

\vartheta(t,x)=\sum_{i=2}^{p-1}(-h)^{i}(t,x)+\sum_{i=p}^{\infty}(-h)^{i}(t,x).

In the same fashion as we proved (
3.17
), we have

\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1}{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x\right\rVert}^{\ell}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\sum_{i=p}^{\infty}(-h)^{i}(t,x)\right\rVert}=0,

thus, denoting
\widetilde{\vartheta}(t,x)=\sum_{i=2}^{p-1}(-h)^{i}(t,x)
, we have
D^{m}_{2}\vartheta(t,0)=D^{m}_{2}\widetilde{\vartheta}(t,0)
for
m=|\mathbf{k}|+1,\dots,\ell
. As
\widetilde{\theta}
is a finite sum of finite compositions of
h
, which has all of its derivatives bounded in the origin, there is some
\widetilde{M}>0
such that

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D^{m}_{2}H^{-1}(t,0)\right\rVert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D^{m}_{2}\widetilde{\vartheta}(t,0)\right\rVert}\leq\widetilde{M},\quad\forall\,m=|\mathbf{k}|+1,\dots,\ell.

•

Step 5: There is a uniformly
\mu
-admissible map
\widehat{\psi}
such that

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D_{2}^{m}R(t,0)\right\rVert}\leq\widehat{\psi}(t),\quad\,\forall\,m=2,\dots,\ell\text{ and almost all }t\in\mathbb{R},

where
R
is the map defined on Step 10 of the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

By the previous step, the maps

x\mapsto D_{2}h_{j}\left(t,H^{-1}(t,x)\right)\quad\text{and}\quad x\mapsto[x]^{\mathbf{k}}-\left[H^{-1}(t,x)\right]^{\mathbf{k}}\,,

have all their derivatives of order between
2
and
\ell
uniformly bounded on the origin. Then, by the definition of
R
, all of its derivatives of order between
2
and
\ell
are bounded by a positive linear combination of the derivatives of
F
, which are dominated by a uniformly
\mu
-admissible function by hypothesis (H2’).

By the definition of
G
given on Step 11 of the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we complete this demonstration.

∎

We conclude this section by presenting an immediate result achieved through the repetition of the preceding lemma. The statement presented is the Theorem of Normal Forms for systems that exhibit
\mu
-dichotomies.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose conditions (H1), (H2’) and (H3) hold. Then, there is a uniform
C^{\ell}
-local equivalence between (
2.4
) and

\dot{x}=A(t)x+G(t,x),

where
G=(G_{1},\dots,G_{n})
is a
C^{\ell}
-Carathéodory function that eliminates all Taylor terms in the origin up to order
\ell
, i.e.,

D_{2}^{m}G(t,0)=0,\quad\,\forall\,m=2,\dots,\ell,\text{ and almost all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

4.
Remarks on the nonuniform case

We finish this work by giving guidelines on how a result of elimination of nonresonant terms could work for the case where the linear part admits only a nonuniform bounded growth, as well as some of the difficulties that arise. The first of these is that, unlike the uniform case discussed on Remark
2.18
, a conjugation by a nonuniform Lyapunov function does not preserve condition (H2) in general.

Assume nevertheless that we have a setting where the linear part is block diagonalized and (H2) is verified. Let
\epsilon>0
. For each spectral interval
\lambda_{i}=[a_{i},b_{i}]
we choose two numbers
\hat{a}_{i}=\hat{a}_{i}(\epsilon)
and
\hat{b}_{i}=\hat{b}_{i}(\epsilon)
with

a_{i}-\epsilon\leq\hat{a}_{i}<a_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad b_{i}<\hat{b}_{i}\leq b_{i}+\epsilon.

Similarly as before, as
\Sigma_{\mathrm{N}\mu\mathrm{D}}(A_{i})=\lambda_{i}=[a_{i},b_{i}]
, from the simple fact that
\hat{b}_{i}>b_{i}
and
\hat{a}_{i}<a_{i}
it follows that exists constants
K=K(\epsilon)\geq 1
and

(4.1)

\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}(\epsilon)<0,\quad\beta_{i}=\beta_{i}(\epsilon)>0,\quad\theta_{i}=\theta_{i}(\epsilon),\quad\nu_{i}=\nu_{i}(\epsilon)\geq 0,

with

(4.2)

\alpha_{i}+\theta_{i}<0\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta_{i}-\nu_{i}>0,

such that for all
i=1,\dots,n
the following estimations follow

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi_{i}(t,s)\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\hat{b}_{i}}\right\rVert}\leq K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{\alpha_{i}}\mu(s)^{\mathrm{sgn}(s)\theta_{i}}\,,

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for all }t\geq s,

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi_{i}(t,s)\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\hat{a}_{i}}\right\rVert}\leq K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{\beta_{i}}\mu(s)^{\mathrm{sgn}(s)\nu_{i}}\,,

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for all }t\leq s,

thus

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi_{i}(t,s)\right\rVert}\leq K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{\alpha_{i}+\hat{b}_{i}}\mu(s)^{\mathrm{sgn}(s)\theta_{i}}\,,

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for all }t\geq s,

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi_{i}(t,s)\right\rVert}\leq K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{\beta_{i}+\hat{a}_{i}}\mu(s)^{\mathrm{sgn}(s)\nu_{i}}\,,

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for all }t\leq s.

Allow us to introduce
\eta^{+}(\epsilon)=\eta^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\eta^{+}(t)=\sup_{s\geq t}\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{\beta_{j}-\left[k_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}\alpha_{n}\right]}\cdot\mu(s)^{\mathrm{sgn}(s)\nu_{j}}\cdot\mu(t)^{\mathrm{sgn}(t)(k_{1}\theta_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}\theta_{n})},

and
\eta^{-}(\epsilon)=\eta^{-}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\eta^{-}(t)=\sup_{s\leq t}\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{\alpha_{j}-\left[k_{1}\beta_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}\beta_{n}\right]}\cdot\mu(s)^{\mathrm{sgn}(s)\theta_{j}}\cdot\mu(t)^{\mathrm{sgn}(t)(k_{1}\nu_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}\nu_{n})}.

From (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) it follows that both
\eta^{+}
and
\eta^{-}
are well defined and continuous. However, unlike the uniform case discussed on the previous section, we cannot just estimate these functions by a constant. On the other hand, other than (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we do not know much about the chosen constants in general, hence we ignore if
\eta^{+}
or
\eta^{-}
are increasing, decreasing or have some other special properties.

Following the same argument as in the previous section, we can show for
t\leq s

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi_{j}(t,s)\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}!}D_{x}^{\mathbf{k}}F_{j}(s,0)\cdot[\Phi(s,t)x]^{\mathbf{k}}\right\rVert}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(s)K^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}}{\mathbf{k}!}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{1}\right\rVert}^{k_{1}}\cdots\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{n}\right\rVert}^{k_{n}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{\beta_{j}+\hat{a}_{j}-\left[k_{1}(\alpha_{1}+\hat{b}_{1})+\cdots+k_{n}(\alpha_{n}+\hat{b}_{n})\right]}

\displaystyle\cdot\mu(s)^{\mathrm{sgn}(s)\nu_{j}}\cdot\mu(t)^{\mathrm{sgn}(t)(k_{1}\theta_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}\theta_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(s)\eta^{+}(t)K^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}}{\mathbf{k}!}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{1}\right\rVert}^{k_{1}}\cdots\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{n}\right\rVert}^{k_{n}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{a_{j}-\left[k_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}b_{n}\right]-(|\mathbf{k}|+1)\epsilon},

and for
t\geq s
we have

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi_{j}(t,s)\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}!}D_{x}^{\mathbf{k}}F_{j}(s,0)\cdot[\Phi(s,t)x]^{\mathbf{k}}\right\rVert}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(s)K^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}}{\mathbf{k}!}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{1}\right\rVert}^{k_{1}}\cdots\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{n}\right\rVert}^{k_{n}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{\alpha_{j}+\hat{b}_{j}-\left[k_{1}(\beta_{1}+\hat{a}_{1})+\cdots+k_{n}(\beta_{n}+\hat{a}_{n})\right]}

\displaystyle\cdot\mu(s)^{\mathrm{sgn}(s)\theta_{j}}\cdot\mu(t)^{\mathrm{sgn}(t)(k_{1}\nu_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}\nu_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(s)\eta^{-}(t)K^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}}{\mathbf{k}!}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{1}\right\rVert}^{k_{1}}\cdots\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{n}\right\rVert}^{k_{n}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{b_{j}-\left[k_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}a_{n}\right]+(|\mathbf{k}|+1)\epsilon}.

Thus, if we choose
\epsilon
to be small enough, we can once again define the map
h
and obtain

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h(t,x)\right\rVert}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\dfrac{K^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}}{\mathbf{k}!}\eta^{+}(t)\mu(t)^{\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}(j,\mathbf{k})}\zeta_{\psi,\mu,\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}(j,\mathbf{k})}^{+}(t)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{1}\right\rVert}^{k_{1}}\cdots\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{n}\right\rVert}^{k_{n}}&\text{ if (\ref{519}\hbox{}) holds},\\
\\
\dfrac{K^{|\mathbf{k}|+1}}{\mathbf{k}!}\eta^{-}(t)\mu(t)^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}(j,\mathbf{k})}\zeta_{\psi,\mu,\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}(j,\mathbf{k})}^{-}(t)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{1}\right\rVert}^{k_{1}}\cdots\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{n}\right\rVert}^{k_{n}}&\text{ if (\ref{520}\hbox{}) holds}.\end{array}\right.

Now we can proceed as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, following Steps 2-11. Nevertheless, by the rather uncontrollable nature of the maps
\eta^{+}
and
\eta^{-}
, we do not know if the trumpet neighborhoods are significantly reduced. On the other hand, in this context the uniformly admissible case is not able to turn these trumpets into tubular neighborhoods, unlike the framework discussed in Lemma
3.3
.

A hypocoercivity-exploiting stabilised finite element method for Kolmogorov equation Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Zhaonan Dong, Emmanuil H. Georgoulis, Philip J. Herbert

Abstract
We propose a new stabilised finite element method for the classical Kolmogorov equation. The latter serves as a basic model problem for large classes of kinetic-type equations and, crucially, is characterised by degenerate diffusion. The stabilisation is constructed so that the resulting method admits a numerical hypocoercivity property, analogous to the corresponding property of the PDE problem. More specifically, the stabilisation is constructed so that spectral gap is possible in the resulting “stronger-than-energy” stabilisation norm, despite the degenerate nature of the diffusion in Kolmogorov, thereby the method has a provably robust behaviour as the “time” variable goes to infinity. We consider both a spatially discrete version of the stabilised finite element method and a fully discrete version, with the time discretisation realised by discontinuous Galerkin timestepping. Both stability and a priori error bounds are proven in all cases. Numerical experiments verify the theoretical findings.

Introduction

In the study of kinetic modelling, degenerate evolution problems are abundant.
These may arise from the integration of stochastic processes, which upon integration typically give rise to Kolmogorov/Fokker-Planck type partial differential equations (PDEs). In many examples, diffusion (stemming from Brownian motion/randomness) is present in some of the spatial directions in the respective Kolmogorov/Fokker-Planck PDE.
Quite interestingly, despite this, many such PDEs with degenerate diffusion give rise to initial/boundary value problems that are, nonetheless, characterised by convergence to long time equilibria. Villani in
[
18
]
coined the term
hypocoercivity
to signify the subtle property of certain degenerate evolution (integro)differenetial operators to yield dissipation in directions where
no
diffusion is explicitly present. Hypocoercivity has been formulated as a general framework in proving decay to equilibrium for kinetic equations in the seminal work
[
18
]
by Villani, who conceptualised and expanded key ideas proposed in the seminal works of Hérau & Nier in
[
10
]
, Eckmann & Hairer in
[
6
]
, Mouhot & Neumann
[
13
]
and others.

To highlight the role of degeneracy of diffusion with respect to long-time behaviour, we consider the classical Kolmogorov equation:

\mathcal{L}u\equiv u_{t}-u_{xx}+xu_{y}=f,\quad\text{in }(0,t_{f}]\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\Omega}};

(1)

for some final time
t_{f}>0
, with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and a suitably smooth forcing
f\colon(0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, along with appropriate initial and boundary data.
(The above notation used above is typical in PDE literature; in kinetic applications, the operator
\mathcal{L}
may appear as
\mathcal{L}f:=f_{t}-f_{vv}+vf_{x}
with
v
denoting the particle velocity variable,
x
the displacement/position and
f
the respective probability density function.) Let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L_{2}(\Omega)}
the standard
L_{2}(\Omega)
-inner product, for some ’spatial’ domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, along with the respective norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}
.
Assuming suitable boundary conditions, such that the skew-symmetric term
(xu_{y},u)
vanishes, the standard energy argument for (
1
) yields

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|{u(t)}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}+\|{u_{x}}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}=(f,u)_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{2{\epsilon}}\|{f}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}+\frac{{\epsilon}}{2}\|{u}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}^{2},

(2)

for almost all
t\in(0,t_{f}]
and all
\epsilon>0
, where
t_{f}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is some final time.
Observing that a Poincaré-Friedrichs/spectral gap estimate of the form
\|{u}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}\leq C\|{\nabla u}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}
is not applicable, and since an estimate of the type
\|{u}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}\leq C\|{u_{x}}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}
may
not
hold, standard arguments involving Grönwall’s Lemma give

\|{u(t_{f})}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}\leq e^{{\epsilon}t_{f}}\big({\epsilon}^{-1}\|{f}\|_{{L_{2}(0,t_{{f}};L_{2}(\Omega))}}^{2}+\|{u_{0}}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}\big),

(3)

for any
{\epsilon}>0
, where we have used standard notation for Bochner spaces.
The presence of the term
e^{{\epsilon}t_{f}}
mean that the stability estimate (
3
) is not robust with respect to the final time.

The hypocoercivity of (
1
), however, can be manifested through non-standard energy arguments.
More specifically, by considering different inner products of form
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\rm hc}=(\cdot,\cdot)_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+(A\nabla\cdot,\nabla\cdot)_{L_{2}(\Omega)}
, where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\rm symm}
is a suitable positive semidefinite matrix, and the associated norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{{\rm hc}}:=\sqrt{(\cdot,\cdot)}_{\rm hc}
, one arrives at an energy estimate of the form

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|{u(t)}\|_{{\rm hc}}^{2}+c\big(\|{\nabla u}\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}+\|{u_{x}}\|_{{\rm hc}}^{2}\big)\leq(f,u)_{\rm hc},

for some constant
c>0
.
Crucially, if
u
is such that it satisfies a Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality, (e.g., satisfying suitable boundary conditions,) we can deduce

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|{u(t)}\|_{{\rm hc}}^{2}+c_{\rm hc}\|{u}\|_{{\rm hc}}^{2}\leq(f,u)_{\rm hc},

for some constant
c_{\rm hc}>0
which depends on
A
, so that

\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|{u(t)}\|_{{\rm hc}}^{2}+c_{\rm hc}\|{u}\|_{{\rm hc}}^{2}\leq c_{\rm hc}^{-1}\|{f}\|_{{\rm hc}}^{2},

using standard arguments.
Thus, Grönwall’s Lemma gives

\|{u(t_{f})}\|_{{\rm hc}}^{2}\leq e^{-c_{\rm hc}t_{f}}\|{u_{0}}\|_{{\rm hc}}^{2}+c_{\rm hc}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t_{f}}e^{-c_{\rm hc}(t_{f}-s)}\|{f(s)}\|_{{\rm hc}}^{2}\,{\rm d}s.

(4)

This is reminiscent of the standard parabolic decay estimates.
In particular, for
f=0
, this provides decay as
t\to\infty
.

Retaining hypocoercive structures upon discretisation of respective PDE problems is desirable. This is because numerical methods admitting such structures would give rise to robust performance for long time computations. So far, only few works have been concerned with such
numerical hypocoercivity
properties. We mention
[
15
]
for a study of hypocoercivity for a central difference method discretising the Kolmogorov equation (
1
), using the abstract framework from
[
18
, Theorem 18]
, and
[
5
]
where the approach from
[
15
]
was taken further to discuss the availability of spectral gap for central difference discretisations of the respective Fokker-Planck equation. Also, Foster, Lohéac & Tran
[
8
]
discuss the development of a Lagrangian-type splitting method based on a carefully constructed similarity transformation and linear finite elements over quasiuniform meshes, although no proof of decay to equilibrium is given for the numerical method itself. Also, Bessemoulin-Chatard & Filbet
[
1
]
present design principles for the construction of equilibrium-preserving finite volume methods for nonlinear degenerate parabolic problems. Recently, Bessemoulin-Chatard, Herda & Rey
[
2
]
presented an asymptotic-preserving in the diffusive limit finite volume scheme for a class of one-dimensional kinetic equations, using the hypocoercivity framework of Dolbeault, Mouhot & Schmeiser
[
4
]
.

Developing Galerkin finite element methods which preserve/admit hypocoercivity structures is a largely unexplored area. This is despite the potential advantages of Galerkin methods, allowing for high order discretisations, over unstructured, possibly locally adapted meshes. We are only aware of
[
9
]
, whereby a hypocoercivity-preserving family of spatially discrete non-conforming finite element methods for (
1
) is constructed and analysed. The key idea in the design of the methods in
[
9
]
is the construction of certain consistent stabilisations based on carefully selected numerical fluxes on the skeleton of the mesh, along with imposition of additional boundary conditions in the PDE problem.
The modified inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\rm hc}
, containing derivatives of order two or higher, results into the family of methods introduced in
[
9
]
to scale
like a fourth order discrete operator in space. This may result to higher computational cost if used in conjunction with a fully discrete implicit time-stepping fashion and iterative linear solvers for each time-step.

This work is concerned with constructing a new, hypocoercivity-exploiting, stabilised finite element method, which address three key issues that arose in
[
9
]
: 1) the new method scales like a second order discrete operator, thereby reinstating the expected computational complexity of discretising a second order differential operator, 2) both spatially discrete and fully discrete methods are presented, with the time discretisation realised by an arbitrary order discontinuous Galerkin timestepping approach, and 3) no additional boundary conditions other than standard Cauchy/Dirichlet-Neumann ones are required for the analysis of the new method.
On the other hand, however, the three aforementioned developments are possible
at the cost of a discretization-parameter dependent,
negative Grönwall exponent
when
compared to the one in
[
9
]
. In particular, the available spectral gap degenerates when increasing the dimension of the space-time approximation spaces (i.e., reducing meshsize/timestep and/or increasing polynomial degree). Nevertheless, for fixed discretisation parameters, hypocoercivity is proven and used, in turn, for the proof of
a priori
error bounds which are robust with respect to the final time
t_{f}
.

The remainder of this work is structured as follows.
In Section
2
, we discuss the special weak formulation of (
1
) which utilizes the concept of hypocoercivity in order to generate a contractive semigroup.
Following this, we discuss two semi-discretisations.
The first semi-discretisation is discussed in Section
3
comes in the form of a streamline-upwinded Petrov-Galerkin method which does not itself immediately achieve an optimal rate of convergence.
This is followed in Section
4
by a discretisation which makes use of both the properties discussed in Section
2
and the streamline-upwinded Petrov-Galerkin method. Both of these semi-discretisations make use of continuous finite elements in space. Once the semi-discrete case has been treated, we move to Section
5
which considers a full discretisation of (
1
) by use of discontinuous Galerkin methods in time.

(20)

\displaystyle+128\int_{0}^{t_{f}}\Big(|\kern-1.42271pt\|{\rho}|\kern-1.42271pt\|_{{\rm SUPG}}+\|{\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho}\|+(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\|{\sqrt{\tau}\rho_{xx}}\|_{{T}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{2}\,{\rm d}t.

\displaystyle\|e(t_{f})\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t_{f}}|\kern-1.42271pt\|{e}|\kern-1.42271pt\|_{{\rm SUPG}}^{2}\,{\rm d}t\leq

\displaystyle\|{e(0)}\|^{2}+C\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\Big(\max_{t\in I}|h^{s}u|^{2}_{H^{s}(T)}\Big)

4
A hypocoercivity-exploiting SUPG method

Starting from (
7
), we consider the spatially discrete finite element method: find

,

, such that

(\!({U_{t}},{V})\!)_{A}+a_{h}(U,V)=(\!({f},{V})\!)_{A}+(f,\tau(V_{t}+xV_{y})),

(26)

for all
V\in V_{h}
, for

a_{h}(U,V):=a_{h,{\rm SUPG}}(U,V)+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}(\nabla LU,A\nabla V)_{T}.

(27)

We set
U(0):=\hat{\pi}u_{0}\in V_{h}
, with
\hat{\pi}\colon H^{1}_{-}(\Omega)\to V_{h}
defined as the
A
-orthogonal projection operator onto the finite element space

.

Remark 3
.

The weak form (
26
) is a Petrov-Galerkin formulation arising formally by testing the PDE against
v+\tau(v_{t}+xv_{y})-\nabla\cdot(A\nabla v)
, modulo a boundary term arising from integration by parts.

4.1
Numerical hypocoercivity

To study the numerical hypocoercivity, we first define

\displaystyle|\kern-1.42271pt\|{w}|\kern-1.42271pt\|:=\Big(

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|{w_{x}}\|^{2}+\|{\sqrt{\gamma\delta}w_{y}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{\tau}(w_{t}+xw_{y})}\|^{2}+\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}}w_{x}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}}w}\|_{{\partial_{+}\Omega}}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}A}\nabla w}\|_{{\partial_{+}T}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

with
A
as in (
6
)
and
\nabla_{\mathcal{T}}
is the element-wise gradient operator, defined in in the last section of the Introduction.

We have the following coercivity result.

Lemma 4
((Hypo)coercivity).

For all
w\in V_{h}
, we have

a_{h}(w,w)\geq\frac{1}{4}|\kern-1.42271pt\|{w}|\kern-1.42271pt\|^{2},

(28)

when
\alpha=(8\delta)^{-1}
,
\beta=(24\delta^{2})^{-1}
,
\gamma=(64\delta^{3})^{-1}
, whereby for each
T\in\mathcal{T}
,

\delta|_{T}:=\delta_{T}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\max\left\{\frac{C_{inv}^{2}p^{2}\|{xn_{2}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial_{-}T)}}}{h_{T}},\frac{2\|{n_{1}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}C_{inv}^{4}p^{4}}{3h_{T}^{2}}\right\}}}\text{and }\tau|_{T}:=\frac{h_{T}^{2}}{4C_{INV}^{2}p^{4}},

(29)

where

C_{inv}>0
the trace-inverse inequality constant depending only on the shape-regularity of the mesh, viz.,
\|{v}\|_{{\partial T}}\leq C_{inv}ph_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{v}\|_{{T}}
, valid for
v\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(T)
,
T\in\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

Now, working as above, we have

\displaystyle a_{h}(w,w)=

\displaystyle\|{w_{x}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}}w}\|_{{\partial_{+}\Omega}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{\tau}(w_{t}+xw_{y})}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\Big((\nabla Lw,A\nabla w)_{T}-(w_{xx},\tau(w_{t}+xw_{y}))_{T}\Big),

whilst the last term on the right-hand side gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}:=

\displaystyle\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\beta\|{w_{y}}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\alpha(w_{y},w_{x})_{T}+(\frac{1}{2}xn_{2}\nabla w-n_{1}\nabla w_{x},A\nabla w)_{\partial T}

\displaystyle-(w_{xx},\tau(w_{t}+xw_{y}))_{T}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\beta\|{w_{y}}\|_{{T}}^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\epsilon_{1}}\|{w_{y}}\|_{{T}}^{2}-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2}\|{w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{|xn_{2}|A}\nabla w}\|_{{\partial_{-}T}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}A}\nabla w}\|_{{\partial_{+}T}}^{2}-\|{n_{1}}\|_{{L^{\infty}(\partial T)}}\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla w_{x}}\|_{{\partial T}}\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla w}\|_{{\partial T}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\|{C_{INV}p^{2}h^{-1}\sqrt{\tau}w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{\tau}(w_{t}+xw_{y})}\|_{{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big(1-\epsilon_{2}\Big)\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\Big(\beta-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\epsilon_{1}}\Big)\|{w_{y}}\|_{{T}}^{2}-\Big(\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2}+\frac{1}{8}\Big)\|{w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\Big({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\frac{C_{inv}^{2}p^{2}\|{xn_{2}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial_{-}T)}}}{2h_{T}}+\frac{\|{n_{1}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}C_{inv}^{4}p^{4}}{4h_{T}^{2}\epsilon_{2}}}}\Big)\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla w}\|_{{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{\tau}(w_{t}+xw_{y})}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}A}\nabla w}\|_{{\partial_{+}T}}^{2},

upon selecting
\tau:=C_{INV}^{-2}p^{-4}h^{2}/4
.
We set
\epsilon_{2}=\frac{3}{4}
, and recall the definition of
\delta_{T}
, for which we note that
\delta_{T}\neq 0
since
n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}=1

Continuing, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\Big(\beta-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\epsilon_{1}}\Big)\|{w_{y}}\|_{{T}}^{2}-\Big(\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2}+\frac{1}{8}\Big)\|{w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}-\delta_{T}\alpha\|{w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\delta_{T}\gamma\|{w_{y}}\|_{{T}}^{2}-2\delta_{T}\beta(w_{x},w_{y})_{T}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}A}\nabla w}\|_{{\partial_{+}T}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{\tau}(w_{t}+xw_{y})}\|_{{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\Big(\beta-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\epsilon_{1}}-\delta_{T}\gamma-\delta_{T}\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha\epsilon_{3}}\Big)\|{w_{y}}\|_{{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2}+\frac{1}{8}+(1+\epsilon_{3})\delta_{T}\alpha\Big)\|{w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}A}\nabla w}\|_{{\partial_{+}T}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{\tau}(w_{t}+xw_{y})}\|_{{T}}^{2}.

Setting
\epsilon_{1}=1
,
\epsilon_{3}=1
,
\alpha=(8\delta_{T})^{-1}
,

\beta=(24\delta_{T}^{2})^{-1}
, and
\gamma=\frac{9}{8}\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha}=(64\delta_{T}^{3})^{-1}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\frac{5\delta_{T}\gamma}{18}\|{w_{y}}\|_{{T}}^{2}-\frac{7}{8}\|{w_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}A}\nabla w}\|_{{\partial_{+}T}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{\tau}(w_{t}+xw_{y})}\|_{{T}}^{2}.

By combining the above estimates, we deduce

\displaystyle a_{h}(w,w)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\|{w_{x}}\|^{2}+\frac{5}{18}\|{\sqrt{\gamma\delta}w_{y}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}}w_{x}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}}w}\|_{{\partial_{+}\Omega}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}A}\nabla w}\|_{{\partial_{+}T}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{\tau}(w_{t}+xw_{y})}\|^{2},

from which the result follows.

Remark 5
.

The choice for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
is not unique in the above proof. The particular numbers have been chosen as a typical example of possible values.

The following result provides a mesh-dependent spectral gap.

Lemma 6
.

Assume that
h_{\max}=\max_{x\in\Omega}h<1
, and set
h_{\min}:=\min_{x\in\Omega}h
. Then, there exists a constant
1>c_{\rm hc}>0
, independent of
h
and
p
, such that, for all
w\in H^{1}(I;H_{-}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}))
, we have

|\kern-1.42271pt\|{w}|\kern-1.42271pt\|^{2}\geq\kappa\|{w}\|_{{A}}^{2},\qquad\text{with}\qquad\kappa\equiv\kappa(h,p):=c_{\rm hc}h_{\min}^{4}p^{-8}.

Proof.

Let
B:={\rm diag}(1,(32\delta^{2})^{-1})
. Then we have, respectively,

2|\kern-1.42271pt\|{w}|\kern-1.42271pt\|^{2}\geq\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla w}\|^{2}=\|{\sqrt{B-\nu A}\nabla w}\|^{2}+\nu\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla w}\|^{2}.

Upon defining
\bar{\delta}:=\min_{T\in\mathcal{T}}C_{\delta}^{T}p^{4}h_{\min}^{-2}
, we immediately have
\delta\geq\bar{\delta}
.
Selecting now
\nu=\bar{\delta}/2
, the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
,
i=1,2
of
B-\nu A
satisfy
\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq 1
and
\frac{h_{\min}^{4}}{384p^{8}}\leq\lambda_{2}<\frac{1}{2}
, which implies

\|{\sqrt{B-\nu A}\nabla w}\|^{2}\geq\lambda_{2}\|{\nabla w}\|^{2}\geq C_{\rm PF}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\|{w}\|^{2},

where
C_{\rm PF}>0
is the Poincaré-Friedrichs/spectral gap inequality
\|{w}\|^{2}\leq C_{\rm PF}\|{\nabla w}\|^{2}
for any
w\in H^{1}_{-}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{T})
, independent of
w
and discretisation parameters. Thus the result follows for
c_{\rm hc}=2^{-1}\min\{(192C_{\rm PF})^{-1},\min_{T\in\mathcal{T}}C_{\delta}^{T}\}
.

On the other hand, for the hypocoercivity-exploiting SUPG method (
26
), for
f=0
and
V=U
we have, respectively,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|{U}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{4}\|{U}\|_{{A}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|{U}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\kern-1.42271pt\|{U}|\kern-1.42271pt\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|{U}\|_{{A}}^{2}+a_{h}(U,U)=0,

implying

\|U(t_{f})\|\leq e^{-\kappa t_{f}/4}\|U(0)\|,

(30)

which improves upon (
16
). Therefore, the “hypocoercivity-inducing” stabilisation results into favourable dependence of the error with respect to the final time
t_{f}
as
t_{f}\to\infty
. Even though the negative exponential decay may only be effective for
t_{f}>\kappa^{-1}\sim p^{8}h_{\min}^{-4}
, it is nonetheless a manifestation of the concept of hypocoercivity, i.e., convergence to equilibrium despite the degenerate nature of the diffusion in Kolmogorov’s equation. We shall refer to the preservation of the hypocoercivity structure by a numerical method as
numerical hypocoercivity
.

5
A fully discrete hypocoercivity-exploiting method

We shall now modify the spatially discrete method (
26
) above and further discretise the evolutionary derivative by a discontinuous Galerkin (dG) timestepping method.

We begin by subdividing
I=(0,t_{f}]
, into the family
\mathcal{I}:=\{I_{n}:n=1,\ldots,N\}
, where
I_{n}:=(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
for a strictly increasing sequence

, and let

, with
k|_{I_{n}}:=k_{n}=t_{n}-t_{n-1}

We define space-time finite element space

V_{h,k}\equiv V_{h,k}^{p,q}:=\{V\in L_{2}(I;V_{h}^{p}):V|_{I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{q}(I_{n}),n=1,\dots,N\}

. We denote by
\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{V}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n}
the time-jump across
t_{n}
, viz.,

\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{V}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n}:=V(t_{n}^{+})-V(t_{n}^{-}):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}V(t_{n}+\epsilon)-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}V(t_{n}-\epsilon)

for
n=1,\dots,N

Starting from (
26
), we consider the following dG methods in time variable, hypocoercivity-exploiting SUPG method: find
U\in V_{h,k}
such that

B(U,V)=(\!({U(t_{0}^{-})},{V(t_{0}^{+})})\!)_{A}+\int_{0}^{t_{f}}\Big((\!({f},{V})\!)_{A}+(f,\tau(V_{t}+xV_{y}))\Big)\,{\rm d}t

for all

, whereby

\displaystyle B(U,V):=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{I_{n}}\left((\!({U_{t}},{V})\!)_{A}+a_{h}(U,V)\right)\,{\rm d}t+\sum_{n=2}^{N}(\!({\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{U}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}},{V(t_{n-1}^{+})})\!)_{A}

(36)

\displaystyle+(\!({U(t_{0}^{+})},{V(t_{0}^{+})})\!)_{A},

with

, for
\hat{\pi}\colon H^{1}_{-}(\Omega)\to V_{h}
A-orthogonal projection operator.

Of course, in practice (

\displaystyle\int_{I_{n}}\left((\!({U_{t}},{V})\!)_{A}+a_{h}(U,V)\right)\,{\rm d}t+(\!({U(t_{n-1}^{+})},{V(t_{n-1}^{+})})\!)_{A}

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\!({U(t_{n-1}^{-})},{V(t_{n-1}^{+})})\!)_{A}+\int_{I_{n}}\Big((\!({f},{V})\!)_{A}+(f,\tau(V_{t}+xV_{y}))\Big)\,{\rm d}t,

for all

,

on

.

5.1
Numerical hypocoercivity

To study the numerical hypocoercivity structure of the space-time method (
36
), we set
V=U
and
f=0
into (
37
) and, following standard arguments, (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Chapter 12]
,) we have

\frac{1}{2}\|{U(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{U(t_{n-1}^{+})}\|_{{A}}^{2}-(\!({U(t_{n-1}^{-})},{U(t_{n-1}^{+})})\!)_{A}+\int_{I_{n}}a_{h}(U,U)\,{\rm d}t=0,

or

\frac{1}{2}\|{U(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{U}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\int_{I_{n}}a_{h}(U,U)\,{\rm d}t=\frac{1}{2}\|{U(t_{n-1}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}.

(38)

From Lemmata
4
and
6
, we deduce

\|{U(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\|{\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{U}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{I_{n}}\|{U}\|_{{A}}^{2}\,{\rm d}t\leq\|{U(t_{n-1}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}.

(39)

Next, we manipulate the third term on the left-hand side of the last inequality to ensure a non-trivial spectral gap.
Employing the
hp
-version trace inverse estimate
[
19
]
, we have

k_{n}\|{U(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}\leq(q+1)^{2}\int_{I_{n}}\|{U}\|_{{A}}^{2}\,{\rm d}t,

(40)

Using the above estimate, we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa k_{n}}{2(q+1)^{2}}\|{U(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{I_{n}}\|{U}\|_{{A}}^{2}\,{\rm d}t,

which upon substitution into (

\Big(1+\frac{\kappa k_{n}}{2(q+1)^{2}}\Big)\|{U(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}\leq\|{U(t_{n-1}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2},

we deduce

\|{U(t_{f})}\|_{{A}}^{2}\leq\prod_{n=1}^{N}\Big(1+\frac{\kappa k_{n}}{2(q+1)^{2}}\Big)^{-1}\|{U(t_{0}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2},

Remark 10

From (

\|{U(t_{f})}\|_{{A}}\leq\Big(1+\frac{\kappa k}{2(q+1)^{2}}\Big)^{-\frac{t_{f}}{2k}}\|{U(t_{0}^{-})}\|_{{A}}\to e^{-\frac{\kappa}{4(q+1)^{2}}t_{f}}\|{U(t_{0}^{-})}\|_{{A}},

5.2
Error estimate

We define the space-time norm

|\kern-1.42271pt\|{U}|\kern-1.42271pt\|_{\rm st}^{2}:=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\|{\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{U}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n}}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\|{U(t_{N}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\|{U(t_{0}^{+})}\|_{{A}}^{2}\Big)+\sum_{n=1}^{n}\int_{I_{n}}\frac{1}{4}|\kern-1.42271pt\|{U}|\kern-1.42271pt\|^{2}\,{\rm d}t.

(43)

Set

. The consistency of the method implies the error equation:

\sum_{n=1}^{n}\int_{I_{n}}\left((\!({e_{t}},{V})\!)_{A}+a_{h}(e,V)\right)\,{\rm d}t+\sum_{n=2}^{n}(\!({\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{e}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}},{V(t_{n-1}^{+})})\!)_{A}+(\!({e_{0}^{+}},{V(t_{0}^{+})})\!)_{A}=0,

Definition 11
.

We define the space-time projection
\pi_{\rm st}:H^{1}(I_{n};H^{1}(\Omega))\to V_{h,k}^{p,q}
constructed for each

\pi_{\rm st}:=\tilde{\pi}^{t}\circ\hat{\pi}=\hat{\pi}\circ\tilde{\pi}^{t}

\int_{I_{n}}{\tilde{\pi}^{t}v}{w}\,{\rm d}t=\int_{I_{n}}{v}{w}\,{\rm d}t,

We comment on the approximation properties and stability of

h

[
16

17
]

\|{\partial_{t}^{m}(v-{\tilde{\pi}^{t}v})}\|_{{I_{n}}}\leq C_{app}\|{k^{r-m}\partial_{t}^{r}v}\|_{{I_{n}}},\qquad m=0,1

\|{{\tilde{\pi}^{t}v}}\|_{{I_{n}}}\leq\|{{v}}\|_{{I_{n}}}+C_{app}\|{k\partial_{t}v}\|_{{I_{n}}},

\displaystyle\int_{I_{n}}\|{\nabla^{\lambda}(v-\pi_{\rm st}v)}\|_{{T}}^{2}\,{\rm d}t\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|a_{h}(\eta,\xi)|}{|\kern-1.42271pt\|{\xi}|\kern-1.42271pt\|}\leq

\displaystyle+C_{inv}^{2}p^{2}h_{T}^{-1}\big(\|{\alpha xn_{2}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}\|{k^{r}\partial^{r}_{t}u_{x}}\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}+\|{\beta xn_{2}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}\|{k^{r}\partial^{r}_{t}u_{y}}\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C_{inv}^{2}p^{2}h_{T}^{-1}\big(\|{\beta(\gamma\delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}xn_{2}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}\|{k^{r}\partial^{r}_{t}u_{x}}\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{\gamma/\delta}xn_{2}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}\|{k^{r}\partial^{r}_{t}u_{y}}\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|{\beta(\gamma\delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}n_{1}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}\|{k^{r}\partial^{r}_{t}u_{xx}}\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{\gamma/\delta}n_{1}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}\|{k^{r}\partial^{r}_{t}u_{xy}}\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}\big),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{T}:=\big(\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}h}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial_{T}\cap\partial_{+}\Omega)}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{A}h^{-1}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(T)}}^{2}+\tau_{T}^{-1}h^{2}_{T}+\tau_{T}\|{x}\|_{{L_{\infty}(T)}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\tau_{T}h^{-2}_{T}+\|{C_{INV}(C_{\delta}^{T})^{-1}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(T)}}^{2}+\!1+\alpha^{2}+C_{inv}^{2}p^{2}\big(\|{\alpha xn_{2}h^{-1}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|{\beta xn_{2}h^{-1}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}+\|{\alpha n_{1}h^{-2}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}+\|{\beta n_{1}h^{-2}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\|{\alpha{\gamma\delta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(T)}}^{2}+\|{\beta{\gamma\delta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(T)}}^{2}+C_{inv}^{2}p^{2}\big(\|{\beta(\gamma\delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}xn_{2}h_{T}^{-1}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|{\sqrt{\gamma/\delta}xn_{2}h_{T}^{-1}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}+\|{\beta(\gamma\delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}n_{1}h_{T}^{-2}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|{\sqrt{\gamma/\delta}n_{1}h_{T}^{-2}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial T)}}^{2}\big)\big)\big(|h^{s}u|^{2}_{H^{s+1}(T)}+|h^{s}k\partial_{t}u|^{2}_{H^{s+1}(T)}\big)+\tau_{T}|h^{s+1}\partial_{t}u|^{2}_{H^{s+1}(T)}.

Then, using the definition of

,
\tau
, (

) and
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{I_{n}}\frac{1}{4}|\kern-1.42271pt\|{\xi}|\kern-1.42271pt\|^{2}\,{\rm d}t\leq|\kern-1.42271pt\|{\xi}|\kern-1.42271pt\|_{\rm st}^{2}
, we deduce

|\kern-1.42271pt\|{\xi}|\kern-1.42271pt\|_{\rm st}^{2}\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{E}_{n},

(54)

with
\mathcal{E}_{n}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}:=

\displaystyle\int_{I_{n}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\Big(k_{T}^{2r}\big(\|{\partial^{r}_{t}u}\|_{{\partial T\cap\partial_{+}\Omega}}^{2}+(1+h_{T}^{-2}+(h_{T}/k_{n})^{2})\|{\partial^{r}_{t}u}\|_{{T}}^{2}

(55)

\displaystyle+\|{\partial^{r}_{t}\nabla u}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\|{h^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial^{r}_{t}\nabla u}\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}+\|{h\partial^{r}_{t}\nabla^{2}u}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\|{h^{\frac{3}{2}}\partial^{r}_{t}\nabla^{2}u}\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+h_{T}^{2s}\big(|u|^{2}_{H^{s+1}(T)}+|k\partial_{t}u|^{2}_{H^{s+1}(T)}\big)\Big)\,{\rm d}t,

where

, independent of

,

, and of

,

, the shape-regularity of the mesh,
\|{x}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}}

Remark 13
.

The fully discrete error bound (
54
) is optimal in terms of spatial mesh size
h
in the space-time norm. Moreover, it is optimal in terms of time step
k
under the condition that
h\approx\mathcal{O}(k^{\frac{1}{2}})
. For the sufficiently regular solution
u
, the optimal error bound with quasi-uniform spatial and temporal meshes are given below with
s=p
and
r=q+1
,

|\kern-1.42271pt\|{\xi}|\kern-1.42271pt\|_{\rm st}^{2}\leq C\big(h^{2p}+k^{2q+1}\big).

Moreover, from the triangle inequality and best approximation, we deduce the following (optimal)
a priori
error estimate

|\kern-1.42271pt\|{u-U}|\kern-1.42271pt\|_{\rm st}^{2}\leq C\big(h^{2p}+k^{2q+1}\big).

(56)

We are now ready to state an
a priori
error bound for the fully discrete scheme.

Theorem 14
.

Assuming that the exact solution
u|_{I_{n}}\in H^{1}(I_{n};H^{s+1}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}))\cap H^{r}(I_{n};H^{2.5+\epsilon}(\Omega))
, with
1\leq r\leq q+1
,
1.5<s\leq p
,
p\geq 2
,
1\leq n\leq N
and arbitrary small
\epsilon>0
, then the error of (
35
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|{e(t_{f})}\|_{{A}}^{2}\leq\prod_{n=1}^{N}\big(1+\mu_{n}\big)^{-1}\|{u_{0}-\hat{\pi}u_{0}}\|_{{A}}^{2}+C\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\prod_{m=n+1}^{N}\big(1+\mu_{m}\big)^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{n}+C\mathcal{E}_{N},

(57)

for
\mu_{n}:=\kappa k_{n}/(4(q+1)^{2})
,
\kappa\equiv\kappa(h,p)=c_{\rm hc}h_{\min}^{4}p^{-8}
(as above),
\mathcal{E}_{n}
defined in (
55
) and a constant
C>0
, independent of
t_{f}
, of
u
and of
U
.

Proof.

First, using the dG-method (
37
) on each time interval
I_{n}
, with
n=1,\dots,N
, and choosing
V=\xi
, we have the following error equation,

\int_{I_{n}}\left((\!({e_{t}},{\xi})\!)_{A}+a_{h}(e,\xi)\right)\,{\rm d}t+(\!({\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{e}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}},{\xi(t_{n-1}^{+})})\!)_{A}=0.

(58)

Then, using the standard manipulation and (
51
) of projection
\pi_{st}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|{\xi(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{\xi}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}}\|_{{A}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|{\xi(t_{n-1}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\int_{I_{n}}a_{h}(\xi,\xi)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{I_{n}}a_{h}(\eta,\xi)\,{\rm d}t.

Next, applying Lemma
28
and Lemma
6
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\|{\xi(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|{\xi(t_{n-1}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{I_{n}}|\kern-1.42271pt\|{\xi}|\kern-1.42271pt\|^{2}\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{I_{n}}\frac{a_{h}(\eta,\xi)}{|\kern-1.42271pt\|{\xi}|\kern-1.42271pt\|}|\kern-1.42271pt\|{\xi}|\kern-1.42271pt\|\,{\rm d}t.

Then, standard estimates and Lemma
6
give

\frac{1}{2}\|{\xi(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|{\xi(t_{n-1}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{8}\int_{I_{n}}\|{\xi}\|_{{A}}^{2}\,{\rm d}t\leq C\mathcal{E}_{n}.

Applying the trace inverse inequality (
40
) and setting
\mu_{n}:=\kappa k_{n}/(4(q+1)^{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\big(1+\mu_{n}\big)\|{\xi(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}-\|{\xi(t_{n-1}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}\leq C\mathcal{E}_{n}.

(59)

Next, using
(\!({\eta(t_{n}^{-})},{\xi(t_{n}^{-})})\!)_{A}=(\!({\eta(t_{n-1}^{-})},{\xi(t_{n-1}^{-})})\!)_{A}=0
along with (
59
), we have

\displaystyle\big(1+\mu_{n}\big)\|{e(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}-\|{e(t_{n-1}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1+\mu_{n}\big)\|{\eta(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}-\|{\eta(t_{n-1}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+\big(1+\mu_{n}\big)\|{\xi(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}-\|{\xi(t_{n-1}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(1+\mu_{n}\big)\|{\eta(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+C\mathcal{E}_{n}.

By the best approximation results (
49
), we have that
\|{\eta(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}
can be bounded by
\mathcal{E}_{n}
, thereby, concluding

\displaystyle\big(1+\mu_{n}\big)\|{e(t_{n}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}\leq\|{e(t_{n-1}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+C\big(1+\mu_{n}\big)\mathcal{E}_{n}.

The last recurrence relation for

, implies

\displaystyle\|{e(t_{N}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}\leq\prod_{n=1}^{N}\big(1+\mu_{n}\big)^{-1}\|{e(t_{0}^{-})}\|_{{A}}^{2}+C\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\prod_{m=n+1}^{N}\big(1+\mu_{m}\big)^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{n}+C\mathcal{E}_{N}.

(60)

Finally, using the identity
e(t_{0}^{-})=u_{0}-\hat{\pi}u_{0}
, (
57
) is proven.

This is followed by measuring the error for an instationary problem which was estimated in (

To complete, we demonstrate the exponential decay of the

To state our results, we give the following definition of normed spaces:

\displaystyle L^{p,s}(\mathbb{R}):=\{\mu(x)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})|\langle x\rangle^{s}\mu(x)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\}\;\text{with}\;||\mu||_{L^{p,s}}:=||\langle x\rangle^{s}\mu||_{L^{p}},

\displaystyle W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}):=\{\mu(x)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})|\partial^{j}\mu(x)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}),j=1,\cdots,k\}\;\text{with}\;||\mu||_{W^{k,p}}:=\sum_{j=0}^{k}||\partial^{j}\mu||_{L^{p}},

\displaystyle H^{k,s}(\mathbb{R}):=\{\mu(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})|\langle x\rangle^{s}\mu(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\langle y\rangle^{k}\hat{h}(y)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\},

s=u+iv
with
u>\xi_{2}
and
u>v
and
z=\alpha+i\eta
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle|S(f)|\lesssim||f||_{L^{\infty}}||M^{(2)}_{RHP}||_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\iint_{\Omega_{21}}\frac{|\bar{\partial}R_{22}|e^{-\re(2it\theta)}}{|s-z|}\,\mathrm{d}A(s)\lesssim I_{1}+I_{2},

(7.6)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=\iint_{\Omega_{21}}\frac{|r^{\prime}(u)|e^{-\re(2it\theta)}}{|s-z|}\,\mathrm{d}A(s),\quad I_{2}=\iint_{\Omega_{21}}\frac{|s-\xi_{2}|^{-1/2}e^{-\re(2it\theta)}}{|s-z|}\,\mathrm{d}A(s).

According to the asymptotic behavior (
6.14
), we find

e^{-\re(2it\theta)}=e^{2t\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{2})(u-\xi_{2})v},\quad t\to\infty.

(7.7)

Then, we know

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2t\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{2})v^{2}}|r^{\prime}(u)||(s-z)^{-1}|\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2t\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{2})v^{2}}||r^{\prime}||_{L^{2}(v+\xi_{2},\infty)}||(s-z)^{-1}||_{L^{2}(v+\xi_{2},\infty)}\,\mathrm{d}v,

where

\displaystyle||(s-z)^{-1}||_{L^{2}(v+\xi_{2},\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{v+\xi_{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|s-z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}u\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(u-\alpha)^{2}+(v-\eta)^{2}}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|v-\eta|}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y=\frac{\pi}{|v-\eta|}

and
y=\frac{u-\alpha}{v-\eta}
.
Thus,
I_{1}
can be estimated by

\displaystyle I_{1}\lesssim t^{-1/4}.

(7.8)

Next, we consider the estimation of
I_{2}

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2t\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{2})v^{2}}|s-\xi_{2}|^{-1/2}|s-z|^{-1}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2t\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{2})v^{2}}||(|s-\xi_{2}|)^{-1/2}||_{L^{p}(v+\xi_{2},\infty)}||(s-z)^{-1}||_{L^{q}(v+\xi_{2},\infty)}\mathrm{d}v

where
p>2
and
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. It is easy to derive

\displaystyle||(|s-\xi_{2}|)^{-1/2}||_{L^{p}(v+\xi_{2},\infty)}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{v+\xi_{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|u-\xi_{2}+iv|^{p/2}}\mathrm{d}u\right)^{1/p}=\left(\int_{v}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)^{p/4}}\mathrm{d}u\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=v^{1/p-1/2}\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(1+x^{2}\right)^{p/4}}\right)\lesssim v^{1/p-1/2}.

Similar to the above estimates, it can be shown that

\displaystyle||(s-z)^{-1}||_{L^{q}(v+\xi_{2},\infty)}\lesssim|v-\eta|^{1/q-1}.

(7.9)

Then we have
|I_{2}|\lesssim t^{-1/4}
, which together with (
7.8
) and (
7.6
) gives (
7.5
).

∎

In order to discuss the long-time asymptotic behavior of the Cauchy problem (

)-(

as

at infty

(7.10)

where

and has the following property:

Proposition 7.2
.

For
|\xi|>1(\xi=\mathcal{O}(1))
,
|M^{(3)}_{1}(x,t)|\lesssim t^{-3/4}
as

.

Proof.

We only give details for the case

, and other cases can be handled with the same method.
Since
M^{(2)}_{RHP}
is bounded in the entire complex plane except the poles,
M^{(2)}_{RHP}
is bounded in the region
\Omega_{21}
.
As we have done in the proof of the proposition (
7.1
), let
s=u+iv
with
u>\xi_{2}
and
u>v
. Then,
M^{(3)}_{1}
has the following estimation

\displaystyle|M^{(3)}_{1}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\pi}||M^{(3)}||_{L^{\infty}}||M^{(2)}_{RHP}||_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\iint_{\Omega_{21}}|\bar{\partial}R_{21}e^{-2it\theta}|\,\mathrm{d}A(s)\lesssim I_{3}+I_{4},

(7.11)

where

\displaystyle I_{3}=\iint_{\Omega_{21}}|r^{\prime}(u)|e^{2t\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{2})(u-\xi_{2})v}\,\mathrm{d}A(s),

\displaystyle I_{4}=\iint_{\Omega_{21}}|s-\xi_{2}|^{-1/2}e^{2t\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{2})(u-\xi_{2})v}\,\mathrm{d}A(s).

Using the inequality of Cauchy-Schwarz, we obtain

8
Large-time asymptotics for NLS equation

Finally, from the above results in Sections 2-7, we start to construct the large time asymptotics for the solution of the Cauchy problem of the NLS equation (
1.1
)-(
1.2
)
and the obtained main result is as below.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
q(x,t)
be the solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with the initial data
q_{0}(x)\in\tanh(x)+H^{4,4}(\mathbb{R})
.
Then, for
|\xi|>1,\ \xi=\mathcal{O}(1)
, there exists a constant
T_{0}=T_{0}(q_{0},\xi)
such that for all
t>T_{0}
,

q(x,t)=e^{-i\alpha(\infty)}\left(1+t^{-1/2}h(x,t)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-3/4}\right),

(8.1)

where

\displaystyle\alpha(\infty)=\exp\left(\int_{I(\xi)}\frac{v(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right),

(8.2)

\displaystyle h(x,t)=\frac{\left(v(\xi_{1})\right)^{1/2}}{2\sqrt{t\pi}\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}\right)i}\left[\frac{\xi_{1}^{2}e^{-i\Phi_{1}}+e^{i\Phi_{1}}}{\sqrt{|\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{1})|}}+\frac{e^{-i\Phi_{2}}+\xi_{1}^{2}e^{i\Phi_{2}}}{\sqrt{|\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{1}^{-1})|}}\right],

(8.3)

with

\displaystyle v(z)=-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-|r(z)|^{2}),\Phi_{1}=\frac{\pi}{4}+\arg\Gamma(iv(\xi_{1}))-\arg\left(r_{\xi_{1}}\right),\Phi_{2}=\Phi_{1}+\alpha-iv(\xi_{1}),

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{\pi}{2}+4t\theta(\xi_{1})+v(\xi_{1})\log\left(4t^{2}|\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{1})\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{1}^{-1})|\right)+2\arg\Gamma(iv(\xi_{1}))+2\arg\frac{T_{1}(\xi_{1})}{T_{2}(\xi_{1}^{-1})},

and
T_{k}(z)(k=1,2)
have been given in (
4.14
).

Proof.

Inverting the sequence of transformations (
4.22
), (
5.19
), (
6.3
) and (
7.1
), we have for
z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathcal{U}_{\xi}

M(z)=T(\infty)^{\sigma_{3}}M^{(3)}(z)E(z)M^{out}(z)\big(R^{(2)}\big)^{-1}(z)T(z)^{-\sigma_{3}}.

(8.4)

For convenience, we take
z\to\infty
out of
\bar{\Omega}
and obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}M&=T(\infty)^{\sigma_{3}}\left(I+\frac{M_{1}^{(3)}}{z}+\cdots\right)\left(I+\frac{E_{1}}{z}+\cdots\right)\\
&\left(I+\frac{M^{out}_{1}}{z}+\cdots\right)\left(I+\frac{T_{1}\sigma_{3}}{z}+\cdots\right)T(\infty)^{-\sigma_{3}}\\
&=I+z^{-1}T(\infty)^{\sigma_{3}}\left[M_{1}^{(3)}+E_{1}+M^{out}_{1}+T_{1}\sigma_{3}\right]T(\infty)^{-\sigma_{3}}+\mathcal{O}(z^{-2}),\end{split}

where the sign with the subscript
1
indicates the coefficient of
z^{-1}
of this term.
By the reconstruction formula (
3.18
), we find

\displaystyle q(x,t)=T(\infty)^{-2}\left(1+t^{-1/2}h(x,t)\right)+\mathcal{O}(t^{-3/4}).

(8.5)

Moreover,
T(\infty)^{-2}
can be rewritten into the following form:

T(\infty)^{-2}=\exp\left(-2i\int_{I(\xi)}\frac{v(s)}{2s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)=e^{-i\alpha(\infty)}.

(8.6)

Thus, we have completed the proof.
∎

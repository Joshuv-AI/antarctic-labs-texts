Semiclassical analysis of two-scale electronic Hamiltonians for twisted bilayer graphene
Eric Cancès, Long Meng

Abstract
This paper investigates the mathematical properties of independent-electron models for twisted bilayer graphene by examining the density-of-states of corresponding single-particle Hamiltonians using tools from semiclassical analysis. This study focuses on a specific atomic-scale Hamiltonian H d , θ H_{d,\theta} constructed from Density-Functional Theory, and a family of moiré-scale Hamiltonians H d , K , θ eff H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff} containing the Bistritzer-MacDonald model. The parameter d d represents the interlayer distance, and θ \theta the twist angle. It is shown that the density-of-states of H d , θ H_{d,\theta} and H d , K , θ eff H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff} admit asymptotic expansions in the twist angle parameter ϵ := sin ⁡ ( θ / 2 ) \epsilon:=\sin(\theta/2) . The proof relies on a twisted version of the Weyl calculus and a trace formula for an exotic class of pseudodifferential operators suitable for the study of twisted 2D materials. We also show that the density-of-states of H d , θ H_{d,\theta} admits an asymptotic expansion in η := tan ⁡ ( θ / 2 ) \eta:=\tan(\theta/2) and comment on the differences between the expansions in ϵ \epsilon and η \eta .

Twisted bilayer graphene (TBG)

While thousands of physics papers on TBG have been published in the past 10 years, the mathematical literature about TBG is sparse.

A first series of works focuses on the chiral limit of the Bistritzer-MacDonald (BM) model
[
86
]
. The BM model is an empirical non-interacting model describing low-energy excitations in TBG in the
K
(or
K^{\prime}
) valley, on which most theoretical works on TBG are based (see
[
123
,
111
]
for interacting versions of this model). The BM Hamiltonian
H_{K,\theta}^{\rm BM}
is an unbounded self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
, which has the periodicity of the moiré cell. Its mathematical expression will be recalled below in Section
2.3
. This Hamiltonian depends on
\epsilon
and on two empirical parameters
w_{\rm AA}
and
w_{\rm AB}
characterizing interlayer interactions at AA and AB (or BA) stackings
[
86
]
. In this framework, magic angles are characterized by the existence of almost flat Bloch bands at the Fermi level
\mu=0
. The chiral limit of the BM Hamiltonian is obtained by setting
w_{\rm AA}=0
. In this limit, the BM Hamiltonian has chiral symmetry and can be studied using advanced mathematical analysis
[
118
,
128
,
127
]
sometimes combined with rigorously founded computer-aided proofs
[
120
,
117
,
125
]
. The most recent result states that the chiral BM Hamiltonian has perfectly flat Bloch bands for an infinite discrete sequence of twist angles
\theta_{1}>\theta_{2}>\theta_{3}>...
accumulating at
\theta_{\infty}=0

[
120
]
.

Except for a countable set of twist angles (which has nothing to do with the countable set of magic angles mentioned above), TBG is
not
a periodic system at the atomic scale. It is an incommensurate system, which makes its mathematical description quite involved. At the tight-binding level of theory, incommensurate multilayer 2D materials can be efficiently described using a C*-algebra formalism
[
95
]
, in the spirit of the approach introduced by Belissard and co-workers to handle disordered cyrstals
[
81
,
76
,
97
]
. The C*-algebra formalism has been used to compute macroscopic physical quantities of interest such as conductivity matrices
[
106
,
126
,
108
]
, or to study atomic relaxation in multilayer 2D materials
[
124
,
109
,
122
,
104
]
. For TBG, it has been shown both theoretically and experimentally that when the twist angle
\theta
becomes very small (typically lower than
0.5^{\circ}
), atomic relaxation becomes crucial and leads to the formation of moiré-scale energy favorable AB or BA Bernal stacking triangular regions separated by relatively thin walls. Formally extending the BM model to this setting, it has been shown that in the presence of gating, topologically protected transport on the triangular lattice of domain walls should occur
[
119
]
.

A natural mathematical question is to investigate the connections between atomic-scale models of TBG and effective moiré-scale models such as the BM model. One approach is to try and connect the two scales by analyzing the dynamics of low-energy traveling wave-packets. This idea was already used in e.g.
[
78
]
to derive the effective mass model for conducting electrons and holes in semiconductors, and in
[
92
]
to derive the massless Dirac model for low-energy excitations in monolayer graphene. Using this approach, it was shown in
[
125
]
that the BM model can be obtained as the limit when the twist angle goes to zero of an atomic-scale tight-binding model provided the hoping terms satisfy some decay and scaling properties. Likewise, it was shown in
[
121
]
, that an effective moiré-scale model similar, though not identical, to the BM model could be formally obtained in the small twist angle limit by a simple variational approximation of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation built from an approximate two-scale Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian

H_{d,\theta}:=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{x,z}+V_{d}\left((1-\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}x,\epsilon x,z\right)\quad\mbox{on }L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{C}}),\quad\mbox{with}\quad\epsilon:=\sin\frac{\theta}{2},

(1.1)

where
V_{d}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a potential periodic with respect to each of the first two variables (with different periods) and vanishing at infinity in the third variable (see Section
2.2
for details).
Note that
H_{d,\theta}
is incommensurate for all but a countable number of values of
\theta
.

In this article, we follow a different approach and study the density-of-states (DoS) of the two-scale Hamiltonian
H_{d,\theta}
using tools from semiclassical analysis. We first prove that the DoS of
H_{d,\theta}
admits an asymptotic expansion in
\epsilon:=\sin(\theta/2)
. We then establish a similar result for a family of effective moiré-scale Hamiltonians
H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff}
containing the BM Hamiltonian as well as the leading term of the Hamiltonian derived in
[
121
]
. We also show that
H_{d,\theta}
admits an asymptotic expansion in
\eta:=\tan(\theta/2)
and comment on the differences between the expansions in
\epsilon
and
\eta
.

Semiclassical analysis has been widely used in solid states physics in the past decades, notably to derive effective semiclassical dynamics of Bloch electrons in periodic crystals subjected to external electromagnetic fields
[
68
,
82
,
71
,
69
,
72
,
74
]
, or to mathematically justify reduced quantum models
[
73
,
75
,
78
]
. In particular, two-scale single-particle Hamiltonians modeling perfect crystals in the presence of space-dependent electromagnetic potentials
(A,\phi)
slowly varying at the atomic scale, have attracted a lot of attention. Such Hamiltonians are of the form

\displaystyle H_{\epsilon}^{A,\phi}=(-i\nabla_{x}+A(\epsilon x))^{2}+V(x)+\phi(\epsilon x)\quad\mbox{on }L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{C}}),

(1.2)

where
V:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
\Gamma
-periodic potential, with
\Gamma
a 3D lattice of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, representing the mean-field potential in the perfect crystal,
A:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, and can be embedded in a more general family of pseudodifferential operators of the form

P_{\epsilon}=P(\epsilon x,x,-i\nabla_{x}+A(\epsilon x))\quad\mbox{on }{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

It was observed in
[
68
]
that if
\widetilde{u}_{\epsilon}:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a solution
\Gamma
-periodic with respect to the first variable of

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{\epsilon}\widetilde{u}_{\epsilon}=\lambda\widetilde{u}_{\epsilon}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\widetilde{P}_{\epsilon}=P(X,x,-i\nabla_{x}-\epsilon\nabla_{X}+A(X))\quad\mbox{on }{\mathbb{R}}^{2d},

then
{u}_{\epsilon}(x):=\widetilde{u}(x,\epsilon x)
satisfies

\displaystyle P_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}=\lambda u_{\epsilon}.

(1.3)

The author then uses this idea to construct asymptotic solutions of (
1.3
) by considering
\widetilde{P}_{\epsilon}
as a pseudo-differential operator in the mesoscopic variable
X
with operator-valued symbol. A similar approach, combined with the introduction of a suitable Grushin problem, was used in
[
73
]
to construct a reduced effective Hamiltonian having the same spectrum as
P_{\epsilon}
in the neighborhood of some given
\lambda_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
An asymptotic expansion in
\epsilon
of
{\rm Tr\,}(f(H^{A,\phi}_{\epsilon}))
for
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
supported in a spectral gap of
H^{0,0}_{0}
, was then given
in
[
75
]
. Semiclassical analysis with operator-valued symbols, combined with adiabatic methods, was also used in
[
82
]
to provide a mathematical justification of Peierls substitution method, and compute the first-order correction to the standard semiclassical dynamics of Bloch electrons, involving Berry curvature terms (see also
[
74
]
and references therein).

To show that the DoS of the two-scale Hamiltonian
H_{d,\theta}
defined in (
1.1
) can be expanded as an asymptotic series in powers of
\epsilon:=\sin(\theta/2)
, we start from the observation that
H_{d,\theta}
is unitary equivalent to a pseudodifferential operator with operator-valued symbol
h_{d,\epsilon}(k,X)
, see (
3.8
) below. The symbol
h_{d,\epsilon}(k,X)
is quasi-periodic with respect to the momentum variable
k\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and depends on the variable
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
representing physically the position in the moiré cell, or equivalently the local stacking parameter. However, this is not a semiclassical symbol in the sense that it cannot be directly expanded as an asymptotic series in
\epsilon
of symbols in a suitable class. To circumvent this technical issue, we introduce a twisted version of the standard Weyl calculus (see (
3.17
)) and show that
H_{d,\theta}
is unitary equivalent to the twisted Weyl quantization of the
\epsilon
-independent symbol
h_{d,0}
:

H_{d,\theta}={\mathcal{U}}^{-1}{\rm Op}_{\epsilon}^{c}(h_{d,0}){\mathcal{U}},

where
{\mathcal{U}}
is the 2D Bloch transform with respect to the in-plane coordinates and
{\rm Op}_{\epsilon}^{c}
the twisted Weyl quantization operator.
We then use this relation to derive an asymptotic expansion in
\epsilon:=\sin(\theta/2)
of the density of states of
H_{d,\theta}
. To derive the asymptotic expansion of the DoS of
H_{d,\theta}
in powers of
\eta:=\tan(\theta/2)
, we first rescale the
x
-variable and then show that the so-obtained operator is unitary equivalent to a pseudodifferential operator with operator-valued symbol in a usual class of symbols.

Mathematical models

2.1
Monolayer graphene

We first set up some notation and review the basic properties of the monolayer graphene (MG) Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian that are relevant to this work. Before going further, we shall point out that the MG Hamiltonian we are considering here is an operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C})

92

is an effective atomic-scale Hamiltonian acting on

is identified with the plane containing the monolayer.

We denote the 3D position variable as
(x,z)^{T}
where

We consider in this section a graphene monolayer in the horizontal plane

1(a)
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Z}{\rm a}_{1}+\mathbb{Z}{\rm a}_{2}\qquad\mathrm{with}\quad{\rm a}_{1}=a_{0}\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\
-\sqrt{3}/2\end{pmatrix}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad{\rm a}_{2}=a_{0}\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\
\sqrt{3}/2\end{pmatrix},

where
a_{0}
is the graphene lattice constant (
a_{0}=\sqrt{3}a

the carbon-carbon distance in the honeycomb lattice).
We also define the reciprocal lattice of

by

\mathbb{L}^{*}:=\{G\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;|\;\forall R\in\mathbb{L},\;e^{2\pi iG\cdot R}=1\},

(see Fig.
1(b)
). More explicitly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}^{*}={\rm a}_{1}^{*}\mathbb{Z}+{\rm a}_{2}^{*}\mathbb{Z},\quad\textrm{with}\quad{\rm a}_{1}^{*}=\sqrt{3}k_{D}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{3}/2\\
-1/2\end{pmatrix}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad{\rm a}_{2}^{*}=\sqrt{3}k_{D}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{3}/2\\
1/2\end{pmatrix},

where
k_{D}:=\frac{4\pi}{3a_{0}}
. Then the corresponding Wigner-Seitz cell
\Omega
and first Brillouin zone

can be identified with the two-dimensional tori

\Omega\equiv\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{L},\quad\Omega^{*}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{L}^{*},

are located at points

\frac{1}{3}a_{1}+\frac{2}{3}a_{2}

\frac{2}{3}a_{1}+\frac{1}{3}a_{2}

The MG Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian is the operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C})
with domain
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C})
defined by

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{MG}}:=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{x}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}^{2}+V_{\mathrm{MG}}(x,z).

(2.1)

We assume that the potential
V_{\mathrm{MG}}
satisfies the following properties (compare with
[
89

).

Assumption 2.1

(MG potentials)
.

The potential
V_{\rm MG}
satisfies

1.

(Smoothness)
V_{\mathrm{MG}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R};{\mathbb{R}})
;

2.

(Symmetry)
V_{\mathrm{MG}}
is invariant under the Dg80
={\rm D}_{\rm 6h}\ltimes{\mathbb{L}}
(p6/mmm) symmetry group of graphene, i.e. for all
(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{MG}}(x+R,z)=V_{\mathrm{MG}}(x,z)\quad\mbox{for all }R\in{\mathbb{L}},

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{MG}}(x_{1},-x_{2},z)=V_{\mathrm{MG}}(x_{1},x_{2},z)=V_{\mathrm{MG}}(-x_{1},x_{2},z)

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{MG}}(x,z)=V_{\mathrm{MG}}(x,-z),

\displaystyle[{\rm R}_{\frac{\pi}{3}}V_{\mathrm{MG}}](x,z)=V_{\mathrm{MG}}(\mathcal{R}_{\frac{\pi}{3}}^{-1}x,z)=V_{\mathrm{MG}}(x,z),

where
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}
denotes the 2D rotation matrix of angle
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\theta}:=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}

(2.2)

and
{\rm R}_{\frac{\pi}{3}}
its action on the functions of

;

3.

(Decay property in the transverse direction) for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, there is
\delta_{\alpha}>0
and
C_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\partial_{x,z}^{\alpha}V_{\mathrm{MG}}(x,z)|\leq C_{\alpha}(1+|z|^{2})^{-\delta_{\alpha}}\qquad\textrm{with}\quad\partial_{x,z}:=(\partial_{x},\partial_{z}).

(2.3)

In this setting, the Bloch transform (also called Zak transform) is the unitary operator

{\mathcal{U}}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};\mathbb{C})\to{\mathcal{H}}:=L^{2}_{\rm qp}(\Omega^{*};L^{2}_{\rm per})

(2.4)

such that

\displaystyle\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{C}}),\quad({\mathcal{U}}u)_{k}(x,z)=\sum_{R\in\mathbb{L}}u(x+R,z)e^{-ik\cdot(x+R)},

\displaystyle L^{2}_{\rm per}:=\{f\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}))\;|\;\forall R\in\mathbb{L},\;f(x-R,z)=f(x,z)\mbox{ for a.a. }(x,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}\},

\displaystyle L^{2}_{\rm qp}(\Omega^{*};L^{2}_{\rm per}):=\{u_{\bullet}\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};L^{2}_{\rm per})\;|\;\forall G\in\mathbb{L}^{*},\;u_{k-G}=\tau_{G}u_{k}\mbox{ for a.a. }k\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\},

where

is the unitary operator on

acting by multiplication by the
\mathbb{L}
-periodic function
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\ni(x,z)\mapsto e^{iG\cdot x}\in U(1)
. The space
L^{2}_{\rm per}
is
endowed with the inner product

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}:=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}}u^{*}(x,z)v(x,z)\,dx\,dz,

and the space
{\mathcal{H}}
with the inner product

\langle u_{\bullet},v_{\bullet}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:=\fint_{\Omega^{*}}\langle u_{k},v_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}\,dk.

We also set, for all
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

H^{s}_{\rm per}:=\{u\in L^{2}_{\rm per}\;|\;\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{z}\beta u\in L^{2}_{\rm per},\,\forall(\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{N}}\mbox{ s.t. }|\alpha|+\beta\leq s\}

(endowed with its natural inner product). Using Fourier series in the

. Note that the functions in

-periodic in the longitudinal (in-plane) variable

Since

is

-periodic, it is decomposed by the Bloch transform

H_{\mathrm{MG}}={\mathcal{U}}^{-1}\left(\fint_{\Omega^{*}}^{\oplus}h_{\mathrm{MG}}(k)\,dk\right){\mathcal{U}},

where

is the operator on

with domain

Remark 2.2
.

1
and
3
in Assumption

2.2
Approximate Kohn-Sham model for twisted bilayer graphene

We consider two parallel layers of graphene, separated by a distance
d>0
, and with a twist angle
\theta\in[0,\frac{\pi}{3})
between the two, and neglect atomic relaxation. More precisely, the upper layer is placed in the plane
z=d/2
and rotated by
-\theta/2
around the
z
-axis, and the lower layer is placed in the plane
z=-d/2
and rotated by
\theta/2
around the
z
-axis.

We label the so-obtained configuration using the parameters
d
and
\epsilon:=\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)
. For convenience, we also set
c(\epsilon):=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}}{\epsilon}=\frac{1-\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}
. Note that the function
c(\epsilon)
is continuous on
[-1,1]
and real-analytic in
(-1,1)
, and that the rotation matrix
\mathcal{R}_{\theta/2}
can be decomposed as

\mathcal{R}_{\theta/2}=(1-c(\epsilon)\epsilon)\mathbb{I}_{2}-\epsilon J,\quad\textrm{with}\quad J:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

We then introduce the unitary operator
U_{d,\epsilon}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C})
defined by

\displaystyle(U_{d,\epsilon}f)(x,z)=f\left(\mathcal{R}_{\theta/2}x,z-\frac{d}{2}\right)=f\left((1-\epsilon c(\epsilon))x-\epsilon Jx,z-\frac{d}{2}\right),

(2.6)

and the following approximation of the TBG Kohn-Sham potential:

\displaystyle V_{{\rm TBG},d,\epsilon}^{\rm approx}(x,z):

\displaystyle=(U_{d,\epsilon}V_{\mathrm{MG}})(x,z)+(U^{-1}_{d,\epsilon}V_{\mathrm{MG}})(x,z)+V_{{\mathrm{int}},d}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=\pm 1}V_{\mathrm{MG}}\left((1-\epsilon c(\epsilon))x-s\epsilon Jx,z-s\frac{d}{2}\right)+V_{{\mathrm{int}},d}(z),

(2.7)

where
V_{{\mathrm{int}},d}
is a smooth function of the transverse variable
z
only, which can be computed numerically
[
121
]
. This approximation was introduced and justified by numerical simulations in
[
94
]
. Interestingly,
V_{{\rm TBG},d,\epsilon}^{\rm approx}
has a simple multiscale structure:

\displaystyle V_{{\rm TBG},d,\epsilon}^{\rm approx}(x,z):=V_{d}\left(\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}x,\epsilon x,z\right)=V_{d}(x-\epsilon c(\epsilon)x,\epsilon x,z),

(2.8)

where the function

V_{d}(x,X,z):=\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}V_{\mathrm{MG}}\left(x-\sigma JX,z-\sigma\frac{d}{2}\right)+V_{{\mathrm{int}},d}(z)

is
\mathbb{L}
-periodic w.r.t. the atomic-scale variable
x
and
J\mathbb{L}
-periodic w.r.t. the mesoscale variable
X
. The approximate Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian is then defined as

\displaystyle H_{d,\theta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{x}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}^{2}+V_{d}\left(\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}x,\epsilon x,z\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{x}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}^{2}+V_{d}(x-c(\epsilon)\epsilon x,\epsilon x,z).

(2.9)

To proceed further, we have to make assumptions on the mathematical properties of the potential
V_{{\mathrm{int}},d}
.

Assumption 2.3

(Interlayer correction potential)
.

The interlayer potential
V_{{\mathrm{int}},d}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};{\mathbb{R}})
satisfies

1.

(Smoothness)
V_{{\mathrm{int}},d}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};{\mathbb{R}})
;

2.

(Decay property) Let
d>0
.
For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, there exist
\delta_{\alpha}>0
and
C_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\forall z\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad|\partial_{z}^{\alpha}V_{{\mathrm{int}},d}(z)|\leq C_{\alpha}(1+|z|^{2})^{-\delta_{\alpha}}.

(2.10)

These assumptions are compatible with the numerical experiments we have done with the DFTK software
[
115
]
using GTH pseudopotentials.

2.3
Effective moiré-scale models for twisted bilayer graphene

Most theoretical investigations on TBG rely on continuous models
[
87
,
86
,
85
,
88
]
such as the Bistritzer-MacDonald (BM) model
[
86
]
, a 2D effective model at the moiré scale describing low-energy excitations in the
K
-valley. Up to a unitary transform, the BM Hamiltonian also describes the behavior of low-energy excitations in the
K^{\prime}
-valley. After suitable gauge transform, the BM Hamiltonian is the self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{4})
with domain
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{4})
given by

\displaystyle H_{K,\theta}^{\rm BM}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{\rm F}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{-\theta/2}\cdot(-i\nabla_{x}-K)&\mathbf{V}(\epsilon x)\\
\mathbf{V}(\epsilon x)^{*}&v_{\rm F}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\theta/2}\cdot(-i\nabla_{x}-K)\end{pmatrix}\qquad\mbox{(with $\epsilon:=\sin\frac{\theta}{2}$)},

where
v_{\rm F}>0
is the Fermi velocity in monolayer graphene, and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\pm\theta/2}:=e^{\mp i\frac{\theta}{4}\sigma_{3}}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})e^{\pm i\frac{\theta}{4}\sigma_{3}}
, with

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{2}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{3}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\qquad\mbox{(Pauli matrices)}.

The function
\mathbf{V}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
is
J\mathbb{L}
-periodic and depends on two empirical parameters
w_{\rm AA}
and
w_{\rm AB}
describing interlayer coupling in AA and AB (or BA) stackings respectively.

In this paper, we consider a more general class of effective moiré-scale Hamiltonians of the form:

\displaystyle H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff}:=T_{\epsilon}(-i\nabla_{x}-K)+\mathcal{V}_{d,K}(\epsilon x)\qquad\mbox{(with $\epsilon:=\sin\frac{\theta}{2}$)},

(2.11)

where
\mathcal{V}_{d,K}(X)\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{C}}^{4\times 4}_{\rm herm})
is a
J\mathbb{L}
-periodic potential and

\displaystyle T_{\epsilon}(\kappa):=\begin{pmatrix}v_{\rm F}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{-\theta/2}\cdot\kappa&0\\
0&v_{\rm F}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\theta/2}\cdot\kappa\end{pmatrix}\qquad\mbox{(with $\epsilon:=\sin\frac{\theta}{2}$)}.

(2.12)

We use the subscript
d
to emphasize that
\mathcal{V}_{d,K}
and
H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff}
actually dependent on
d
. This class of Hamiltonian contains the BM Hamiltonian as well as the leading term of the effective Hamiltonian derived in
[
121
]
.

The Hamiltonians
H^{\rm BM}_{K,\theta}
and
H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff}
are both
J\mathbb{L}
-periodic Hamiltonians on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{4})
and are therefore decomposed by the 2D Bloch transform

\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{4})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}:=L^{2}_{\rm qp}(\Omega^{*};L^{2}_{\#})

(2.13)

with

\displaystyle\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{C}}^{4}),\quad(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}u)_{k}(x)=\sum_{R\in\mathbb{L}}u(x+R)e^{-ik\cdot(x+R)},

\displaystyle L^{2}_{\#}:=\{f\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{4}))\;|\;\forall R\in\mathbb{L},\;f(x-R)=f(x)\mbox{ for a.a. }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\},

\displaystyle L^{2}_{\rm qp}(\Omega^{*};L^{2}_{\#}):=\{u_{\bullet}\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};L^{2}_{\#})\;|\;\forall G\in\mathbb{L}^{*},\;u_{k-G}=\tau_{G}u_{k}\mbox{ for a.a. }k\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\},

where
\tau_{G}
is the unitary operator on
L^{2}_{\#}
acting by multiplication by the
\mathbb{L}
-periodic function
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\ni x\mapsto e^{iG\cdot x}\in U(1)
. The space
L^{2}_{\#}
is
endowed with its usual inner product

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{L^{2}_{\#}}:=\int_{\Omega}u^{*}(x)v(x)\,dx,

and the space
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
with the inner product

\langle u_{\bullet},v_{\bullet}\rangle_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}:=\fint_{\Omega^{*}}\langle u_{k},v_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\#}}\,dk.

We finally denote by
H^{s}_{\#}
the corresponding periodic Sobolev spaces
(endowed with their usual inner products).

3
Main results

We will analyze the atomic-scale Hamiltonian
H_{d,\theta}
and the moiré-scale Hamiltonian
H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff}
through their density-of-states (DoS).
Consider a family
(\chi_{N})_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}\in(C^{\infty}_{\rm c}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}))^{{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
of spatial cutoff functions such that

\displaystyle 0\leq\chi_{N}(x)\leq 1\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\chi_{N}(x)=1\textrm{ if }\;x\in NJ\Omega,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\chi_{N}(x)=0\textrm{ if }\;x\notin(N+1)J\Omega,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|\partial^{\alpha}\chi_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}}<+\infty\quad\mbox{ for all }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2}.

(3.3)

For any
f\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}({\mathbb{R}};{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
, the traces per unit area of the operators
f(H_{d,\theta})
and
f(H^{\rm eff}_{d,K,\theta})
are defined as

\displaystyle\underline{\textrm{\rm Tr}}[f(H_{d,\theta})]:

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{1}{|NJ\Omega|}\mathrm{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\left(\chi_{N}f(H_{d,\theta})\chi_{N}\right)

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle\underline{\textrm{\rm Tr}}[f(H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff})]:

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{1}{|NJ\Omega|}\mathrm{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left(\chi_{N}f(H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff})\chi_{N}\right)

(3.5)

respectively.

Remark 3.1
.

The limit in (
3.5
) exists (in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\bigcup\{+\infty\}
) because
f
is nonnegative and
H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff}
is an
\epsilon^{-1}J\mathbb{L}
-periodic operator. The limit in (
3.4
) is also well-defined because
f
is nonnegative and
H_{d,\theta}
is either periodic (for a countable set of twist angles) or ergodic.

The DoS of
H_{d,\theta}
in the negative energy window and the DoS of
H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff}
are then the positive Borel measures
\nu_{H_{d,\theta}}
and
\nu_{H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff}}
on
(-\infty,0)
and
{\mathbb{R}}
respectively such that

\displaystyle\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}({\mathbb{R}};{\mathbb{R}}_{+})\mbox{ with support in }(0,+\infty),\quad

\displaystyle\underline{\textrm{\rm Tr}}[f(H_{d,\theta})]=\int_{(-\infty,0)}f(E)\,d\nu_{H_{d,\theta}}(E),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}({\mathbb{R}};{\mathbb{R}}_{+}),\quad

\displaystyle\underline{\textrm{\rm Tr}}[f(H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff})]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(E)\,d\nu_{H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff}}(E).

(3.7)

To formulate our main results, we need to recall some concepts and tools of semiclassical and spectral analysis. The first concept is the one of operator-valued symbols introduced in
[
67
,
73
]
and used notably e.g.
[
82
,
83
]
to analyze the effective dynamics of Bloch electrons in the adiabatic approximation. The key formulae in our approach are

H_{d,\theta}={\mathcal{U}}^{-1}{\rm Op}_{\epsilon}(h_{d,\epsilon}){\mathcal{U}},\qquad H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff}=\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}{\rm Op}_{\epsilon}(h^{\rm eff}_{d,K,\epsilon})\qquad\mbox{(with $\epsilon:=\sin\frac{\theta}{2}$)},

(3.8)

(see
[
82
, Eqns. (12)-(13)]
) where

—

{\mathcal{U}}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to{\mathcal{H}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
are the Bloch transforms defined in (
2.4
) and (
2.13
);

—

h_{d,\epsilon}
is the operator-valued symbol on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that for all
(k,X)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
h_{d,\epsilon}(k,X)
is the self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}_{\rm per}
with domain
H^{2}_{\rm per}
defined by: for all
u\in H^{2}_{\rm per}

[h_{d,\epsilon}(k,X)u](x,z):=\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(-i\nabla_{x}+k\right)^{2}u-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{z}u\right](x,z)+V_{d}(x-c(\epsilon)X,X,z)u(x,z);

(3.9)

—

h^{\rm eff}_{d,K,\epsilon}
is the operator-valued symbol on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that for all
(k,X)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
h^{\rm eff}_{d,K,\epsilon}(k,X)
is the self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}_{\#}
with domain
H^{1}_{\#}
defined by: for all
u\in H^{1}_{\#}

[h^{\rm eff}_{d,K,\epsilon}(k,X)u](x):=T_{\epsilon}(-i\nabla_{x}+k-K)u(x)+\mathcal{V}_{d,K}(X)u(x);

(3.10)

—

{\rm Op}_{\epsilon}(a)
is the self-adjoint operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
or
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
obtained from the operator-valued symbol
a(k,X)
by the following Weyl quantization rule:

[{\rm Op}_{\epsilon}(a)\phi]_{k}(r)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\epsilon)^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left[a\left(\frac{k+k^{\prime}}{2},X\right)\phi_{k^{\prime}}\right](r)\;e^{-i\frac{(k-k^{\prime})\cdot X}{\epsilon}}\,dk^{\prime}\,dX.

(3.11)

Remark 3.2
.

The sign in the phase factor in the Weyl quantization formula (
3.11
) is not the usual one. Formally, the operator defined by (
3.11
) is

a(k,i\epsilon\nabla_{k})
, while the usual definition leads to
a(k,-i\epsilon\nabla_{k})
. This is due to the fact that in this setting, the role usually assigned to the momentum variable is played by the position variable
X
. Note that in the special case when
a(k,-X)=a(k,X)
for all
(k,X)
, these two definitions agree.

Definition 3.3

(Operator-valued symbols)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a Banach space of bounded operators, and let
\omega:\;\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,+\infty)
be an order function in the sense that there exist
C_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
N_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\forall(k,X),(k^{\prime},X^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\omega(k,X)\leq C_{0}(1+|k-k^{\prime}|^{2}+|X-X^{\prime}|^{2})^{N_{0}/2}\omega(k^{\prime},X^{\prime}).

1.

A symbol
a\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathcal{E}})
is in the class
S^{\omega}({\mathcal{E}})
if for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}}}_{\alpha,\beta}(a):=\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega(k,X)^{-1}\left\|\partial_{k}^{\alpha}\partial_{X}^{\beta}a(k,X)\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}<\infty.

(3.12)

Let
\epsilon_{0}>0
. The function
a_{\bullet}:(0,\epsilon_{0}]\ni\epsilon\mapsto a_{\epsilon}\in S^{\omega}({\mathcal{E}})
is a symbol in the class
S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}({\mathcal{E}})
if for any
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}},\epsilon_{0}}_{\alpha,\beta}(a_{\bullet}):=\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]}{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}}}_{\alpha,\beta}(a_{\epsilon})<\infty.

Endowed with the family of seminorms
{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}}}_{\alpha,\beta}
(resp.
{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}},\epsilon_{0}}_{\alpha,\beta}
), the vector space
S^{\omega}({\mathcal{E}})
(resp.
S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}({\mathcal{E}})
) is a Fréchet space.

2.

A function
a_{\bullet}\in S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}({\mathcal{E}})
is called an
{\mathcal{E}}
-valued semiclassical symbol with order function
\omega
if there exists a sequence of
{\mathcal{E}}
-valued symbols
(a_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in(S^{\omega}({\mathcal{E}}))^{\mathbb{N}}
such that for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

a_{\epsilon}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\epsilon^{j}a_{j}+O_{S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}({\mathcal{E}})}(\epsilon^{n+1}).

(3.13)

The class of semiclassical symbols of order
\omega
is denoted as
S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}(\epsilon,{\mathcal{E}})
.

Definition 3.4

(
\tau
-equivariant operator-valued symbols)
.

Let
\omega:\;\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,+\infty)
be an order function. Assume
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{B}}(H^{s_{1}}_{\rm per},H^{s_{2}}_{\rm per})
for some
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, or
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathfrak{S}}_{1}(L^{2}_{\rm per})
(resp.
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{B}}(H^{s_{1}}_{\#},H^{s_{2}}_{\#})
for some
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, or
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathfrak{S}}_{1}(L^{2}_{\#})
). A symbol
a\in S^{\omega}({\mathcal{E}})
is called
\tau
-equivariant in the
k
-variable if

\forall(k,X)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad\forall G\in\mathbb{L}^{*},\quad a(k-G,X)=\tau_{G}a(k,X)\tau_{G}^{-1},

(3.14)

where
\tau_{G}
is the multiplication operator on
H^{s}_{\rm per}
(resp.
H^{s}_{\#}
) by the
\mathbb{L}
-periodic function
(x,z)\mapsto e^{iG\cdot x}
(resp.
x\mapsto e^{iG\cdot x}
). We denote by

1.

S^{\omega}_{\tau}({\mathcal{E}})
(resp.
S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}_{\tau}({\mathcal{E}})
) the class of symbols in
S^{\omega}({\mathcal{E}})
(resp. in
S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}({\mathcal{E}})
) which are
\tau
-equivariant with respect to the variable
k
;

2.

S^{\omega}_{\tau,\rm per}({\mathcal{E}})
(resp.
S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}_{\tau,\rm per}({\mathcal{E}})
) the class of symbols in
S^{\omega}({\mathcal{E}})
(resp. in
S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}({\mathcal{E}})
) which are
\tau
-equivariant with respect to the variable
k
and
J\mathbb{L}
-periodic with respect to the variable
X
.

Note that
\tau_{G}
defines a bounded operator on
H^{s}_{\rm per}
(resp.
H^{s}_{\#}
) for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, which is in addition unitary for
s=0
.

We will see in Section
7
that
h^{\rm eff}_{d,K,\bullet}
is a
\tau
-equivariant semiclassical symbol in the class
S^{\omega,1}_{\tau,\rm per}({\mathcal{E}})
with
\omega(k,X):=(1+|{\mathbf{k}}|^{2})^{1/2}
and
{\mathcal{E}}:={\mathcal{B}}(H^{1}_{\#};L^{2}_{\#})
. This will allow us to study the asymptotic expansion in
\epsilon
of
\underline{\textrm{\rm Tr}}[f(H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff})]
using techniques introduced in
[
75
,
82
]
. The situation is a bit more involved for the atomic-scale model. For any
0<\epsilon\leq 1
, the symbol
h_{d,\epsilon}
belongs to the class
S^{\omega}(\mathcal{E})
with
\omega(k,X):=1+|k|^{2}
and
\mathcal{E}:={\mathcal{B}}(H^{2}_{\rm per};L^{2}_{\rm per})
, and can thus be Weyl-quantized
[
83
]
. We have in fact
h_{d,\bullet}\in S^{\omega,1}_{\tau}({\mathcal{E}})
. On the other hand,
h_{d,\bullet}
is not a semiclassical symbol, because the coefficients of its asymptotic expansion are not all in
S^{\omega}({\mathcal{E}})
:

\displaystyle h_{d,\epsilon}(k,X)=\underbrace{h_{d,0}(k,X)}_{\in S^{\omega}({\mathcal{E}})}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\underbrace{X\cdot\nabla_{x}V(x,X)}_{\notin S^{\omega}({\mathcal{E}})}+\underbrace{\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{8}[D^{2}_{xx}V(x,X)](X,X)}_{\notin S^{\omega}({\mathcal{E}})}+...

(3.15)

In order to expand
\underline{\textrm{\rm Tr}}[f(H_{d,\theta})]
in powers of
\epsilon
, we circumvent this technical issue by introducing a twisted version of the standard Weyl calculus. The idea is to rewrite (
3.8
) as

H_{d,\theta}={\mathcal{U}}^{-1}{\rm Op}^{c}_{\epsilon}(h_{d,0}){\mathcal{U}}\qquad\mbox{(with $\epsilon:=\sin\frac{\theta}{2}$)},

(3.16)

where

{\rm Op}^{c}_{\epsilon}(a_{\epsilon}):={\rm Op}_{\epsilon}\left(a_{\epsilon}^{c}\right)\quad\mbox{with}\quad a^{c}_{\epsilon}(k,X):={\mathcal{T}}_{c(\epsilon)X}a_{\epsilon}(k,X){\mathcal{T}}_{c(\epsilon)X}^{-1},

(3.17)

where for each
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
{\mathcal{T}}_{x_{0}}
is the translation operator defined on each
H^{s}_{\rm per}
space by

({\mathcal{T}}_{x_{0}}u)(x,z)=u(x-x_{0},z).

Let us now introduce classes of symbols containing
h_{d,0}
as well as the symbols of
{\mathcal{U}}f(H_{d,\theta}){\mathcal{U}}^{-1}
, and suitable for the twisted Weyl quantization rule
{\rm Op}_{\epsilon}^{c}
defined in (
3.17
). We set

{\rm ad}_{\partial_{x}}:=[\partial_{x},\bullet].

Definition 3.5

(Operator-valued symbols for twisted Weyl calculus)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{B}}(H^{s_{1}}_{\rm per},H^{s_{2}}_{\rm per})
for some
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, or
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathfrak{S}}_{1}(L^{2}_{\rm per})
, and let
\omega:\;\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,+\infty)
be an order function.

1.

A symbol
a\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathcal{E}})
is in the class
S^{\omega}_{\rm twi}({\mathcal{E}})
if the following three conditions are satisfied:

(i)

a
is
\tau
-equivariant in the variable
k
, i.e.

\forall(k,X)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad\forall G\in\mathbb{L}^{*},\quad a(k-G,X)=\tau_{G}a(k,X)\tau_{G}^{-1};

(ii)

a
is
J\mathbb{L}
-periodic in the variable
X
, i.e.

\forall(k,X)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad\forall R\in J\mathbb{L},\quad a(k,X-R)=a(k,X);

(iii)

for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}}}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(a):=\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega(k,X)^{-1}\left\|\partial_{k}^{\alpha}\partial_{X}^{\beta}\mathrm{ad}_{\partial_{x}}^{\gamma}a(k,X)\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}<\infty.

(3.18)

Let
\epsilon_{0}>0
. The function
a_{\bullet}:(0,\epsilon_{0}]\ni\epsilon\mapsto a_{\epsilon}\in S^{\omega}_{\rm twi}({\mathcal{E}})
is a symbol in the class
S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}_{\rm twi}({\mathcal{E}})
if for any
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}},\epsilon_{0}}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(a_{\bullet}):=\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]}{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}}}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(a_{\epsilon})<\infty.

Endowed with the family of seminorms
{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}}}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
(resp.
{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}},\epsilon_{0}}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
), the vector space
S^{\omega}_{\rm twi}({\mathcal{E}})
(resp.
S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}({\mathcal{E}})
) is a Fréchet space.

2.

A function
a_{\bullet}\in S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}_{\rm twi}({\mathcal{E}})
is called a semiclassical symbol if there exists a sequence
(a_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in(S^{\omega}_{\rm twi}({\mathcal{E}}))^{\mathbb{N}}
such that for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

a_{\epsilon}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\epsilon^{j}a_{j}+O_{S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}_{\rm twi}({\mathcal{E}})}(\epsilon^{n+1}).

(3.19)

The class of semiclassical symbols of order
\omega
is denoted as
S_{\rm twi}^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}(\epsilon,{\mathcal{E}})
.

Note that if
a_{\bullet}\in S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}_{\rm twi}({\mathcal{E}})
, then
a^{c}_{\bullet}\in S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}({\mathcal{E}})
. Indeed, since

\displaystyle\partial_{k}(a^{c}_{\epsilon})=(\partial_{k}a_{\bullet})^{c}_{\epsilon},\qquad\partial_{X}(a^{c}_{\epsilon})=\left(\partial_{X}a_{\bullet}\right)^{c}_{\epsilon}-c(\epsilon)\left(\mathrm{ad}_{\partial_{x}}a_{\bullet}\right)^{c}_{\epsilon},

(3.20)

and
{\mathcal{T}}_{x_{0}}
is unitary on
H^{s}_{\rm per}
for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there exists, for all
\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
, a constant
C_{\beta}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
and
a_{\bullet}\in S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}_{\rm twi}({\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}},\epsilon_{0}}_{\alpha,\beta}(a^{c}_{\bullet})\leq C_{\beta}\sum_{\beta^{\prime}+\gamma=\beta}{\mathcal{N}}^{\omega,{\mathcal{E}},\epsilon_{0}}_{\alpha,\beta^{\prime},\gamma}(a_{\bullet}).

(3.21)

The symbol
a_{\epsilon}
can therefore be quantized for all
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
using the twisted Weyl quantization rule
{\rm Op}_{\epsilon}^{c}
defined in (
3.17
).

Let us point out that

\displaystyle a_{\bullet}\in S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}_{\rm twi}({\mathcal{E}})\Rightarrow\partial_{k}^{\alpha}\partial_{X}^{\beta}{\rm ad}_{\partial_{x}}^{\gamma}a_{\bullet}\in S^{\omega,\epsilon_{0}}_{\rm twi}({\mathcal{E}}),\qquad\forall\;\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}.

Thanks to (

(i)

Another essential tool for working out a semiclassical expansions of
\underline{\textrm{\rm Tr}}[f(H_{d,\theta})]
and
\underline{\textrm{\rm Tr}}[f(H_{d,K,\theta}^{\rm eff})]
is functional calculus for pseudodifferential operators based on Helffer-Sjöstrand’s formula (see e.g.
[

]
): for any self-adjoint operator
A
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{X}}
and any function

f(A)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f}(\zeta)(\zeta-A)^{-1}dL(\zeta),

(3.23)

where

denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
and where
\widetilde{f}
is an almost analytic extension of
f
, that is a function
\widetilde{f}\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\mathbb{C};{\mathbb{C}})
, satisfying

1.

{\mathrm{Supp}\,}(\widetilde{f})
is a complex neighborhood of
{\mathrm{Supp}\,}(f)
;

2.

\widetilde{f}(\zeta)=f(\zeta)
for any
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
;

3.

|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f}(\zeta)|=\mathcal{O}(|\Im\zeta|^{\infty})
, i.e., for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f}(\zeta)|=\mathcal{O}(|\Im\zeta|^{n})
when
\Im\zeta\to 0
.

Remark 3.6
.

There are several ways to define such almost analytic extensions. One way uses the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}(f)
and two cutoff functions
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(-1,1)
,
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(U)
(
U
is a suitable real neighborhood of
{\mathrm{Supp}\,}(f)
) with
\chi=1
near zero and
\varphi=1
in
{\mathrm{Supp}\,}(f)
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}(x+iy):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\varphi(x)\chi(y)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\xi(x+iy)}\chi(y\xi)\,\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)\,d\xi.

(3.24)

Lastly, we recall the definition of the Poisson bracket
\{a,b\}
of two operator-valued symbols
a,b
:

\displaystyle\{a,b\}:=\nabla_{X}a\cdot\nabla_{k}b-\nabla_{k}a\cdot\nabla_{X}b.

(3.25)

We also define

\displaystyle\{a,b\}_{2}:

\displaystyle=D^{2}_{kk}a:D^{2}_{XX}b+D^{2}_{XX}a:D^{2}_{kk}b-2D^{2}_{kX}:aD^{2}_{kX}b

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}\left(\partial_{k_{i}}\partial_{k_{j}}a\partial_{X_{i}}\partial_{X_{j}}b+\partial_{X_{i}}\partial_{X_{j}}a\partial_{k_{i}}\partial_{k_{j}}b-2\partial_{k_{i}}\partial_{X_{j}}a\partial_{X_{i}}\partial_{k_{j}}b\right)

In the case when
a\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}_{\rm per}))
and
b\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathcal{B}}(H^{2}_{\rm per};L^{2}_{\rm per}))
, we have
\{a,b\},\;\{a,b\}_{2}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathcal{B}}(H^{2}_{\rm per};L^{2}_{\rm per}))
. This property will be used below.

Several remarks are in orders:

1.

the two expansions (

) and (

\displaystyle\underline{\textrm{\rm Tr}}[f(H_{d,\theta})]

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}a_{2j}\epsilon^{2j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{\textrm{\rm Tr}}[f(H_{d,\theta})]\sim\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}a^{\prime}_{2j}\eta^{2j}=\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}a^{\prime}_{2j}\frac{\epsilon^{2j}}{(1-\epsilon^{2})^{j}},

and

respectively. It is not clear

which of these two formalisms lends itself best to numerical approximations. This point will be investigated in a future work;

2.

it is not immediate to see from the expressions of

(3.30)

This equality results from an integration by part, as shown in Section

;

3.

from each of the two expansions (

) and (

), asymptotic expansions of

in powers of

can be easily obtained by expanding

4.

our proof of (

4.1
Spectral properties of the operator
h_{d,0}(k,X)

As a prelude, we first prove some basic spectral properties of the operator
h_{d,0}(k,X)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
,

1.

for all
k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
h_{d,0}(k,X)
is a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}_{\rm per}
with domain
H^{2}_{\rm per}
and it holds

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm ess}(h_{d,0}(k,X))=[0,+\infty);

(4.1)

2.

there exists
M\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\end{subarray}}\sigma(h_{d,0}(k,X))\geq-M;

(4.2)

3.

for any
E<0
, there is a constant
m_{E}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Rank}({\mathds{1}}_{(-\infty,E]}(h_{d,0}(k,X)))\leq m_{E}.

(4.3)

Proof.

Let
k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. From Kato-Rellich theorem, the operator
h_{d,0}(k,X)
is self-adjoint on
L^{2}_{\rm per}
with domain
H^{2}_{\rm per}
. Let
\xi\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. It follows from Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
and the Kato-Seiler-Simon inequality
[
66
]
that

\displaystyle(h_{d,0}-\xi)^{-1}-\left(\frac{1}{2}(-i\nabla_{x}+k)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}^{2}-\xi\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=-(h_{d,0}-\xi)^{-1}V_{d}\left(\frac{1}{2}(-i\nabla_{x}+k)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}^{2}-\xi\right)^{-1}

is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator on
L^{2}_{\rm per}
. We deduce from Weyl’s essential spectrum theorem (see e.g.
[
66
, Theorem XIII.14 and Corollary 1]
) that

\sigma_{\rm ess}(h_{d,0}(k,X))=\sigma_{\rm ess}\left(\frac{1}{2}(-i\nabla_{x}+k)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}^{2}\right)=[0,+\infty).

The second assertion is an immediate consequence of the fact that

\sup_{x,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2},z\in\mathbb{R}}|V_{d}(x,X,z)|=:M<\infty.

We turn now to the proof of the third assertion. Let
E<0
. As
h_{d,0}(k,X)
is
\tau
-equivariant w.r.t.
k
and
J\mathbb{L}
-periodic w.r.t.
X
, it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(k,X)\in\overline{\Omega^{*}}\times J\overline{\Omega}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Rank}({\mathds{1}}_{(-\infty,E]}(h_{d,0}(k,X)))=:m_{E}<\infty.

(4.4)

For any bounded-from-below self-adjoint operator
A
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
with domain
D(A)
, and any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, we denote by

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}(A):=\inf_{V_{j}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{j}}\sup_{v\in V_{j}\,|\,\|v\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}=1}\left\langle v,Av\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},

(4.5)

where
{\mathcal{V}}_{j}
is the set of vector subspaces of
D(A)
of dimension
j
. Recall that by Courant-Fischer min-max formula,
\sigma_{j}(A)
is equal to the
j^{\rm th}
lowest eigenvalue of
A
(counting multiplicities) if
A
has at least
j
eigenvalues below
\inf(\sigma_{\rm ess}(A))
, and to
\inf(\sigma_{\rm ess}(A))
otherwise. Since the functions

\mu_{j}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\ni(k,X)\mapsto\mu_{j}(k,X):=\sigma_{j}(h_{d,0}(k,X))\in{\mathbb{R}}

(4.6)

form a nondecreasing sequence of continuous functions, the sets

C_{j}:=\mu_{j}^{-1}((-\infty,E])\bigcap(\overline{\Omega^{*}}\times J\overline{\Omega})=\mu_{j}^{-1}([-M,E])\bigcap(\overline{\Omega^{*}}\times J\overline{\Omega})

are compact and such that

\displaystyle C_{1}\supset C_{2}\supset\cdots.

If each
C_{j}
was not empty, then so would be
\bigcap_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}C_{j}
by virtue of the Cantor intersection theorem. For
(k_{*},X_{*})\in\bigcap_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}C_{j}
, we would have
-M\leq\sigma_{j}(h_{d,0}(k_{*},X_{*}))\leq E
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, which contradicts the fact that
\sigma_{\rm ess}(h_{d,0}(k_{*},X_{*}))\bigcap[-M,E]=\emptyset
. The inequality (
4.3
) is therefore satisfied with
m_{E}:=\max\{j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}\,|\,C_{j}\neq\emptyset\}
.
∎

First, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\omega^{-1}(k,X)\|h_{d,0}(k,X)\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(H^{2}_{\rm per},L^{2}_{\rm per})}<\infty.

\displaystyle\partial_{k}h_{d,0}(k,X)=-i\nabla_{x}+k,\quad\partial_{X}h_{d,0}(k,X)=\partial_{X}V_{d}(\cdot,X,\cdot),

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\omega^{-1}(k,X)\|\partial_{k}^{\alpha}\partial_{X}^{\beta}\mathrm{ad}_{\partial_{x}}^{\gamma}h_{d,0}(k,X)\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(H^{2}_{\rm per},L^{2}_{\rm per})}<\infty.

To show that

\displaystyle h_{d,0}-h_{d,0,f}\in S^{\omega}_{{\mathrm{twi}}}(\mathcal{B}(H^{2}_{\rm per},L^{2}_{\rm per})).

As

From this and Eqn. (

\displaystyle h_{d,0}-h_{d,0,f}=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{\partial}\widetilde{\xi}_{f}(\zeta)(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}dL(\zeta),

(4.12)

where
\widetilde{\xi}_{f}

Decomposing each

as

and using the

-equivariance of

and the fact that

\tau_{G}^{-1}(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})\tau_{G}=1+|-i\nabla_{x}+G|^{2}-\partial^{2}_{z},

we obtain that for all
\zeta\in{\rm Supp}(\widetilde{f})
and

\displaystyle\|(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})^{a}(\zeta-h_{d,0}(k,X))^{-1}(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})^{b}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}

\displaystyle=\|(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})^{a}(\zeta-h_{d,0}([k]+G,X))^{-1}(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})^{b}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}

\displaystyle=\|(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})^{a}\tau_{G}(\zeta-h_{d,0}([k],X))^{-1}\tau_{G}^{-1}(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})^{b}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}

\displaystyle\leq 4(1+|G|^{2})^{a+b}\|(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})^{a}(\zeta-h_{d,0}([k],X))^{-1}(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})^{b}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|G|^{2})^{a+b}|\Im\zeta|^{-1}\leq C^{\prime}(1+|k|^{2})^{a+b}|\Im\zeta|^{-1}=C^{\prime}\omega(k,X)^{a+b}|\Im\zeta|^{-1}.

(4.14)

The first inequality holds because of (
4.13
) while the second holds due to the facts that

and that there exist two constants

such that for all
(k,X)\in\Omega^{*}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and

\displaystyle\|(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})u\|_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}\leq C_{1}\|h_{d,0}([k],X)u\|_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}+C_{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}.

(4.15)

Since

is self-adjoint, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega^{-1}\|(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}\leq\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}=|\Im\zeta|^{-1},

and applying (
4.2
) first with
(a,b)=(1,0)
then with

, gives

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega^{-1}\|(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per},H^{2}_{\rm per})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega^{-1}\|(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}\leq C|\Im\zeta|^{-1},

and

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega^{-1}\|(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(H^{-2}_{\rm per},L^{2}_{\rm per})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega^{-1}\|(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}(1-\Delta_{x}-\partial^{2}_{z})\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}\leq C|\Im\zeta|^{-1}.

Concerning the derivatives
\partial_{k},\partial_{X}
and
\mathrm{ad}_{\partial_{x}}
of

such that for all

and

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{X}h_{d,0})(\zeta-h^{c}_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}=\|\big(\partial_{X}V_{d}(\cdot,X,\cdot)\big)(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}\leq C|\Im\zeta|^{-1}

and

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{k}h_{d,0})(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega^{-1}\|\partial(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(H^{-2}_{\rm per},L^{2}_{\rm per})}

Higher-order derivatives can be bounded by the same arguments, leading to

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|\partial_{k}^{\alpha}\partial_{X}^{\beta}\mathrm{ad}_{\partial_{x}}^{\gamma}(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}

(4.17)

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega^{-1}\|\partial_{k}^{\alpha}\partial_{X}^{\beta}\mathrm{ad}_{\partial_{x}}^{\gamma}(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per},H^{2}_{\rm per})}

(4.18)

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega^{-1}\|\partial_{k}^{\alpha}\partial_{X}^{\beta}\mathrm{ad}_{\partial_{x}}^{\gamma}(\zeta-h_{d,0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(H^{-2}_{\rm per},L^{2}_{\rm per})}

(4.19)

Together with (

Lemma 4.3
.

The symbol
(h_{d,0}-h_{d,0,f})
belongs to the class
S_{{\mathrm{twi}}}^{1}(\mathfrak{S}_{1}(L^{2}_{\rm per}))
.

Proof.

Let

. By Lemma
4.2
,

. It is easy to see that for any

\displaystyle\|\partial_{X}^{\alpha}\partial_{k}^{\beta}\mathrm{ad}_{\partial_{x}}^{\gamma}a\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}(L^{2}_{\rm per})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\,\mathrm{Rank}(\partial_{X}^{\alpha}\partial_{k}^{\beta}\mathrm{ad}_{\partial_{x}}^{\gamma}a).

To end the proof, it suffices to show that
\sup_{k,X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{Rank}(\partial_{X}^{\alpha}\partial_{k}^{\beta}\mathrm{ad}_{\partial_{x}}^{\gamma}a)<\infty
for any
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}

For

4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{Rank}(a)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{k,X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{Rank}({\mathds{1}}_{(-\infty,\delta]}(h_{d,0}))\leq m_{-\delta}<\infty.

Now we consider the derivatives w.r.t.

Letting

Thus, for any

, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{Rank}\left(\partial_{k}^{\alpha}\partial_{X}^{\beta}a\right)\leq 2^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\sup_{k,X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{Rank}\left(a\right)\leq 2^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}m_{-\delta}.

(4.20)

Let

be the orthonormal projector onto the vector space spanned by the function

\displaystyle a(k,X)=\sum_{1}^{n(k,X)}\xi_{f}(\mu_{j}(k,X))\left|\psi_{j}(k,X)\right>\left<\psi_{j}(k,X)\right|,

where

and therefore

\displaystyle\sup_{k,X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{Rank}\left(\mathrm{ad}_{\partial_{x}}^{\gamma}a\right)\leq 2^{|\gamma|}m_{-\delta}.

From (

) and proceeding as above, we have for all

\displaystyle\forall G\in\mathbb{L}^{*},\quad\forall(k,X)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad b_{\epsilon}(k-G,X)=\tau_{G}b_{\epsilon}(k,X)\tau_{G}^{-1}.

Let
G\in\mathbb{L}^{*}
. For all
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(a_{\epsilon}(\bullet-G,\bullet)){\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(\tau_{G}b_{\epsilon}\tau_{G}^{-1})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(\tau_{G}a_{\epsilon}\tau_{G}^{-1}){\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(\tau_{G}b_{\epsilon}\tau_{G}^{-1})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}((\tau_{G}a_{\epsilon}\tau_{G}^{-1}))\#(\tau_{G}b_{\epsilon}\tau_{G}^{-1}))

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(\tau_{G}(a_{\epsilon}\#b_{\epsilon})\tau_{G}^{-1})={\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}({\mathds{1}}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}})={\mathds{1}}_{{\mathcal{H}}},

and likewise
{\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(\tau_{G}b_{\epsilon}\tau_{G}^{-1}){\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(a_{\epsilon}(\bullet-G,\bullet))={\mathds{1}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
. It follows that

\forall 0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}^{\prime},\quad{\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(\tau_{G}b_{\epsilon}\tau_{G}^{-1})={\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(a_{\epsilon}(\bullet-G,\bullet))^{-1}={\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(b_{\epsilon}(\bullet-G,\bullet)).

It follows from Beals’ Theorem (see e.g.
[

and Step 3. in its proof) that
b_{\epsilon}(\bullet-G,\bullet)=\tau_{G}b_{\epsilon}\tau_{G}^{-1}
. Thus,
b_{\bullet}\in S_{\tau}^{1,\epsilon_{0}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{\rm per}))
.
∎

Finally, we recall the following variant of Calderon-Vaillancourt theorem.

Theorem 5.4

Let
\epsilon_{0}>0
and

{\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(a_{\epsilon})\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and

\displaystyle\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]}\|{\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(a_{\epsilon})\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}<\infty.

5.2.1
Integration by parts in semiclassical integrals

Let us first introduce some notation:

—

for any function
f\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
or
g\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}})
, we set

\displaystyle\forall(x,R,z)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{L}\times{\mathbb{R}},\quad

\displaystyle I_{\#}(f)(x+R):=f(x),

\displaystyle I_{\#}(g)(x+R,z):=g(x,z);

—

for any function
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, we denote by

\displaystyle f_{R}:=f|_{(\Omega+R)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{z}}\quad\mbox{so that}\quad f(x,z)=\sum_{R\in\mathbb{L}}f_{R}(x,z).

Since
({\mathcal{U}}f_{R})_{k}(x,z)=e^{-ik\cdot(x+R)}f(x+R,z)
for all

, we get

\displaystyle({\mathcal{U}}f)_{k}=\sum_{R\in\mathbb{L}}({\mathcal{U}}f_{R})_{k}\quad\mbox{with}\quad({\mathcal{U}}f_{R})_{k}=I_{\#}(e^{-ik\cdot(\bullet+R)}f(\bullet+R)).

(5.9)

Formally, we thus have for all

,

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{U}}g,{\mathrm{Op}_{\epsilon}}(a){\mathcal{U}}f\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi\epsilon)^{2}}\sum_{R\in\mathbb{L}}\sum_{R^{\prime}\in\mathbb{L}}\fint_{\Omega^{*}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}[{\mathcal{I}}_{\epsilon,R,R^{\prime}}^{g,f}({\rm Id},a)](k,k^{\prime},X)\,dk^{\prime}\,dX\,dk

(5.10)

where for all symbol
b\in S^{1}_{\tau}({\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}_{\rm per}))
and field
{\mathcal{L}}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathcal{B}}(S^{1}_{\tau}({\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}_{\rm per}))))
,

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{I}}_{\epsilon,R,R^{\prime}}^{g,f}({\mathcal{L}},b)](k,k^{\prime},X):=e^{-i\frac{(k-k^{\prime})\cdot X}{\epsilon}}\left\langle({\mathcal{U}}g_{R})_{k},\left[{\mathcal{L}}(k-k^{\prime},X)b\left(\frac{k+k^{\prime}}{2},X\right)\right]({\mathcal{U}}f_{R^{\prime}})_{k^{\prime}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}

(5.11)

Integration by parts with respect to
k
,
k^{\prime}
and
X
is a key tool to reformulate semiclassical expressions such as (

) as convergent series and integrals. To get rid of boundary terms in integrations by parts with respect to
k
, we use the following observation: since

is
\tau
-equivariant and

is quasi-periodic for all

, it holds

\displaystyle\forall G\in\mathbb{L}^{*},\quad[{\mathcal{I}}_{R,R^{\prime}}^{g,f}({\mathcal{L}},b)](k,k^{\prime}+G,X)=[{\mathcal{I}}_{R,R^{\prime}}^{g,f}({\mathcal{L}},b)](k-G,k^{\prime},X),

from which we deduce that

\displaystyle\fint_{\Omega^{*}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}[{\mathcal{I}}_{\epsilon,R,R^{\prime}}^{g,f}({\mathcal{L}},b)](k,k^{\prime},X)\,dk^{\prime}\,dX\,dk

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\fint_{\Omega^{*}}[{\mathcal{I}}_{\epsilon,R,R^{\prime}}^{g,f}({\mathcal{L}},b)](k,k^{\prime},X)\,dk^{\prime}\,dX\,dk

(5.12)

whenever the integrals are well-defined.

The integrand in (

) is integrable w.r.t.

(since

takes its value in a compact set) and

(in view of assumption (

)), but not

to formally integrate by parts with respect to the variable

\displaystyle\fint\limits_{\Omega^{*}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}[{\mathcal{I}}_{\epsilon,R,R^{\prime}}^{g,f}({\rm Id},a)](k,k^{\prime},X)dk^{\prime}dXdk

\displaystyle=\fint\limits_{\Omega^{*}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}[{\mathcal{I}}_{\epsilon,R,R^{\prime}}^{g,f}({\mathcal{L}}_{1,\epsilon}^{M_{0}},a)](k,k^{\prime},X)dk^{\prime}dXdk,

(5.13)

where

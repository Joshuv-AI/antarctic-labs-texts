Abstract
. We consider the moduli space of genus 4 curves endowed with a g 3 1 g^{1}_{3} (which maps with degree 2 onto the moduli space of genus 4 curves). We prove that it defines a degree 1 2 ​ ( 3 10 − 1 ) \frac{1}{2}(3^{10}-1) cover of the 9-dimensional Deligne-Mostow ball quotient such that the natural divisors that live on that moduli space become totally geodesic (their normalizations are 8-dimensional ball quotients). This isomorphism differs from the one considered by S. Kondō and its construction is perhaps more elementary, as it does not involve K3 surfaces and their Torelli theorem: the Deligne-Mostow ball quotient parametrizes certain cyclic covers of degree 6 of a projective line and we show how a level structure on such a cover produces a degree 3 cover of that line with the same discriminant, yielding a genus 4 curve endowed with a g 3 1 g^{1}_{3} .



Introduction

Shigeyuki Kondō constructed in
[
5
]
an isomorphism from a Zariski open subset of the moduli space of
nonsingular complex genus four curves
{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
onto a Zariski open subset of a ball quotient, where the latter covers the
9-dimensional ball quotient that appears in the Deligne-Mostow list. He also noted that this cannot be extended to the full

{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
, but that this is possible if we blow up the hyperelliptic locus
{\mathcal{H}}_{4}
(which is of codimension 2 in
{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
). The exceptional divisor then maps to a hyperball quotient, a property that also holds for the generic point of the thetanull locus. His approach is based on the fact that the generic point of
{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
is represented by a curve
X
of bidegree
(3,3)
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. The
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{3}
-cover of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
that totally ramifies along
X
is a K3 surface with
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{3}
-action and the Torelli theorem for such surfaces implies that its
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{3}
-Hodge structure is a complete invariant of that
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{3}
-surface and hence also of
X
(as the fixed point set of the
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{3}
-action). Such
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{3}
-Hodge structures are parametrized by a ball quotient of the above type.

The goal of this note is twofold. First to observe that there is a modular interpretation
of the hyperelliptic blowup as the moduli space of pairs consisting of a nonsingular complex
genus four curve and a
g^{1}_{3}
on that curve, where we note that a generic genus 4 curve has
two

g^{1}_{3}
’s and that this moduli space comes with an involution that exchanges them.

And second, to give this moduli space a complex hyperbolic structure without resorting to
K3 surfaces and their Torelli theorem. For this, we start from the fact that the
the generic point of the Deligne-Mostow ball quotient parametrizes
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
-covers

C\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
totally ramified over a 12 element subset
D\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and nowhere else.
We then show how a certain level structure on
C
gives rise to
a genus
4
curve
X
lying with degree 3 over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with discriminant
D
(so
X
comes with a
g^{1}_{3}
) and a correspondence between
C
and
X
.

Our approach is in the same spirit as our paper with Heckman
[
4
]
on the moduli space of rational elliptic surfaces (which here appears in the guise of the thetanull locus).
Most of that paper was concerned with the construction and comparison of certain
compactifications of that moduli space (which accounts for its length) and it is likely that a
similar program could be pursued here, where, of course, a compactification
which parametrizes curves of arithmetic genus 4 endowed with a
g^{1}_{3}
would be best.

Let us finally point out that our period map is quite different from the one introduced by Kondō, as the two discriminants that we associate to the two
g^{1}_{3}
’s on a generic genus
4

curve will not lie in the same
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
-orbit and hence have different image in the Deligne-Mostow ball quotient. Presumably the two period maps do not even coincide on the generic point of the thetanull locus, where the genus 4 curve has only one
g^{1}_{3}
.

1.
The nine-dimensional Deligne-Mostow ball quotient

This section is mostly a review of known results. Its main purpose is to recall them in a way that suits our purpose and to establish notation.

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
-covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

Let
C
be a nonsingular complex-projective curve endowed with an
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
-action that has 12 fixed points, is free
elsewhere and is such that its orbit space

is a copy of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

12

Note that the pair

(in

9

Since we find it convenient to make a distinction between what depends on the complex
structure and what only depends on the underlying topology, we also fix a closed oriented
surface
\Sigma
for of genus 25 endowed with a

-action of the type above, so with

-orbit space (a smooth

-action and

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
-curve as above with discriminant

. We also note that a diffeomorphism of

-orbit.

Hodge structure of a
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
-cover of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

We first note that the standard embedding
\chi:\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
and its complex conjugate are the only two primitive characters of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
. The group
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
acts on

and decomposes the latter into its character spaces:
H^{1}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{C}})=\oplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/6}H^{1}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{C}})_{\chi^{i}}

defined by

(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\sqrt{-1}\int_{\Sigma}\alpha\wedge\overline{\beta}
.
It has signature
(25,25)
and for this form the above decomposition into character spaces is perpendicular. In the presence of a complex structure it is also compatible with the Hodge structure.
For example, the decomposition

(1.1)

H^{1}(C;{\mathbb{C}})_{\chi}=H^{1}(C,\Omega_{C})_{\chi}\oplus\overline{H^{1}(C,\Omega_{C})_{\overline{\chi}}}.

is perpendicular with respect to this hermitian form with the first summand being positive definite of dimension one and the second summand negative definite of dimension nine. Since the group of covering transformations
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
acts trivially on

and so this projective space only depends on the pair
(S,D)
; we therefore denote it by

Its group

acts transitively with compact stabilizers; indeed, this ball is the symmetric domain associated to

{\mathbb{B}}
for

As we observed above, a complex structure on

determines one on

-action. The Hodge decomposition (

) then defines an element, the

.
The main result of Deligne and Mostow
[
3
]

[

implies that this Hodge point is a complete invariant of the relative isotopy class of this complex structure.

Action of a mapping class group

To make this last assertion precise, let
\operatorname{Diff}^{+}(S,D)
denote the group of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of

which preserve
D
. Since this group
\operatorname{Diff}^{+}(S,D)
acts on

via
\operatorname{PU}(S,D)
(and so

{\mathbb{B}}
). This action factors through its connected component group that we shall denote
\operatorname{Mod}(S,D)
and which we readily recognize as the spherical braid group on 12 strands. That group has as a distinguished conjugacy class the set of simple braids. We recall that a

is given by an (unoriented) arc
\delta
in

which connects two points of

and has no points of
D
in its interior: the associated simple braid

T_{\delta}\in\operatorname{Mod}(S,D)
has its support in a regular neighborhood of

and lets

make a half-turn in a counterclockwise direction.
These simple braids generate
\operatorname{Mod}(S,D)

by

and let

connect

already generate
\operatorname{Mod}(S,D)
.
It follows from the work of Deligne-Mostow that the image of
\operatorname{Mod}(S,D)
in

is an arithmetic group, which therefore acts properly discretely on
{\mathbb{B}}
with finite covolume.
We denote this image group by

.

A lattice over the Eisenstein ring

In order to make the action of a simple braid on
{\mathbb{B}}
somewhat more explicit, we make some general observations first. Write
\tau
for the primitive
6
th root of unity with positive imaginary part,
e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}/3}
, and regard it as a generator of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
. As a complex number it satisfies
\tau^{2}=\tau-1
, but this identity is of course not valid in the group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}]
. Given a
{\mathbb{Z}}[\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}]
-module

, let us denote by

resp.

the maximal submodule resp. quotient module on which

satisfies the identity
\tau^{2}=\tau-1

For example,
V_{\circ}
is the quotient of

by the subgroup of

with nontrivial

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
-stabilizer. Then

and

become modules over the ring of

{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{1})
. Note that
{\mathbb{R}}\otimes V^{\circ}\to{\mathbb{R}}\otimes V_{\circ}
is an isomorphism and can be identified with the summand of
{\mathbb{R}}\otimes V
which decomposes into planes on which
\tau
acts as a rotation over

.
Since

is a principal ideal domain, any finitely generated torsion free

-module is in fact free. So if

is a finitely generated free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module, then both

and

are finitely generated free
{\mathcal{E}}
-modules. If we are in addition given a
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
-invariant antisymmetric pairing

, then this gives rise to
the
\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{E}}
-valued hermitian form on

defined by

h_{a}(x,y):=\tfrac{1}{2}\tau a(x,y)-\tfrac{1}{2}a(x,\tau y)

(the justification for the perhaps somewhat unexpected normalization factor

will be given below). Note that
h_{a}(x,x)=-\tfrac{1}{2}a(x,\tau x)
and

h_{a}(x,y)-\overline{h_{a}(x,y)}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\tau-\overline{\tau})a(x,y)=\tfrac{1}{2}\theta a(x,y)
, where

\theta:=\tau-\overline{\tau}=-1+2\tau=\sqrt{-3}

is the ‘first’ purely imaginary number in
{\mathcal{E}}
.
We apply this to the

-module
H_{1}(\Sigma)
. We find that

and

are free

-modules of rank 10. The intersection pairing on

and defines via the receipe above on this lattice the

-valued hermitian form

We will write

endowed with this form.

Let us see what this induces on the preimage
\pi^{-1}\delta
of an arc

as above.
We now assume

be a lift of

. Then

is an embedded oriented circle (hence a

.
Now

Since we have the freedom of multiplying each

Mirror hyperballs

The simple braid
T_{\delta}
acts in
L
as the unitary transformation
s_{\delta}

s_{\delta}(x)=x+\tfrac{1}{3}(1-\tau^{2})h(x,a_{\delta})a_{\delta}=x+\tau h^{\prime}(x,a_{\delta})a_{\delta}

It is what is called in

Ordinary duality identifies

resp.

resp.

. We use Poincaré duality to identify these with

This identifies

with

. In particular,

acts on

as a triflection. So its fixed point set

is a hyperball (to which we shall
also refer as a

). Since the

, the group

permutes the mirror hyperballs transitively.

The group of unitary transformations of the
{\mathcal{E}}
-module

acts properly discretely on

with kernel its center
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
. The group

is a priori contained in the quotient of this group by its center (which is an arithmetic subgroup of

and the Baily-Borel theory asserts that

has the structure of a quasi-projective orbifold that can be completed to a normal projective variety by adding a finite set of

(in this case, just one).
The image of the Hodge point in this ball quotient

is a complete invariant of the isomorphism type of the
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{6}
-curve

with the given complex structure.

The
\operatorname{Mod}(S,D)
-centralizer of

acts on the mirror

The group

is as a quotient of

obtained by imposing the relation

. The

The Deligne-Mostow isomorphism

Let
{\mathcal{M}}(12)
denote the
\operatorname{PGL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-orbit space of the configuration space of
12
-element subsets of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(this is also equal to the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{12}
-orbit space of
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,12}
). The above construction defines a homomorphic map

{\mathcal{M}}(12)\to{\mathbb{B}}_{\Gamma}.

If we think of a
12
-element subset of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
as a reduced degree

12
-divisor, then the above map extends to the locus
{\mathcal{M}}(12)^{\rm st}
of Hilbert-Mumford stable positive degree
12
-divisors,
that is, divisors for which the multiplicities are at most
5
. The Deligne-Mostow theory asserts that this extension is in fact an isomorphism

{\mathcal{M}}(12)^{\rm st}\xrightarrow{\cong}{\mathbb{B}}_{\Gamma}

that takes
{\mathcal{M}}(12)
onto
{\mathbb{B}}_{\Gamma}^{\circ}
. It even extends to an isomorphism of their natural compactifications: the GIT compactification on the left and the Baily-Borel compactification on the right. Both are one-point compactifications, with the added point on the left being represented by a
6
-divisible divisor on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
whose support consists of two points.

2.
Trigonal structures on a genus four curve

\mathbf{g^{1}_{3}}
’s on a genus 4 curve

Let
X
be a smooth connected complex-projective curve of genus
4
and let
P
be a pencil of degree
3
on
X
(in other words, a
g^{1}_{3}
).
If
P
has no base point, then the pencil defines a morphism
X\to P
of degree
3
and by Riemann-Hurwitz, the discriminant divisor
D(X,P)
of this morphism is of degree
12
. Note that its multiplicities will be
\leq 2
.
In case
P
has a base point, then the residual base point free pencil
P^{\prime}
will have degree
2
and this makes
X
hyperelliptic with

P^{\prime}
being the hyperelliptic pencil. We will see that it is then natural to define the discriminant divisor of
P
to be
the degree
12
-divisor
D(X,P)
that is (via the identification of
P
with
P^{\prime}
) the sum of the discriminant divisor of
X\to P^{\prime}
(the image of the Weierstraß points, so of degree 10) plus twice the image in
P^{\prime}
of the fixed point
P
. So its multiplicities are then
\leq 3
.

Recall that if
X
is nonhyperelliptic, then a canonical image of
X
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
is the transversal intersection of a cubic surface with a quadric
Q
surface, the latter being unique and either a smooth (so isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
) or a quadric cone. In the first case, the projections on the two factors give us two pencils of degree
3
. In the second case, projection away from the vertex of the cone also yields a pencil of degree
3
; the line bundle is question then an effective (even) theta characteristic. It is well-known that this exhausts all the
g^{1}_{3}
’s in genus 4. For future reference we state this as a lemma (and only sketch the proof).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth connected complex-projective curve of genus
4
. If
X
is nonhyperelliptic,
then any
g^{1}_{3}
on
X
is as above: it comes with a residual
g^{1}_{3}
(in the sense that its members supplement the members of the given
g^{1}_{3}
to a canonical divisor) and there are no others (so the two being equal
if and only if the
g^{1}_{3}
is a theta characteristic). If
X
is hyperelliptic, then any
g^{1}_{3}
on
X
is the sum of an
x\in X
and its hyperelliptic
g^{1}_{2}
; in that case the pencil is a theta characteristic if and only if
x
is a Weierstraß point.

Proof.

This is well-known, as it is in a sense a geometric formulation of Clifford’s theorem in genus
4
. The proof runs as follows. If
P
is a
g^{1}_{3}
on
X
, then Clifford’s theorem implies that
P
is a complete linear system.
By Riemann-Roch we have a supplementary
g^{1}_{3}
on
X
, denoted
P^{\prime}
, whose members supplement the
members of
P
to a canonical divisor. So each member of
P
resp.
P^{\prime}
defines a line in

\check{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}))\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
which meets the canonical image of
X
in at most three points and

P^{\prime}
resp.
P
is realized by the pencil of planes through that line. We have
P=P^{\prime}
if and only if
P
is a
theta characteristic. The rest of the argument is left to the reader.
∎

A moduli stack of genus 4 curves with a
\mathbf{g^{1}_{3}}

We interpret this in terms of moduli spaces. Pairs
(X,P)
as above define a Deligne-Mumford stack that
we shall denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{4}(g^{1}_{3})
. It comes with an involution which assigns to
(X,P)
the residual pair
(X,P^{\prime})
that is characterized by the property that
P+P^{\prime}
lies in the canonical system.
The forgetful morphism
{\mathcal{M}}_{4}(g^{1}_{3})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
evidently factors through the orbit space of this involution.
Recall that the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
contains the thetanull locus
{\mathcal{M}}_{4}^{0}
(the locus for which the curve admits an even effective

{\mathcal{M}}_{4}^{0}
in turn contains the hyperelliptic locus
{\mathcal{H}}_{4}
as a substack of codimension one.

Consider the Stein factorization

{\mathcal{M}}_{4}(g^{1}_{3})\to\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{4}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{4}

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that
\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{4}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
is finite of degree two and ramified along
{\mathcal{M}}_{4}^{0}
, with the covering transformation being induced by the residual involution. It also shows that if
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{4}
stands for the (isomorphic) preimage of
{\mathcal{H}}_{4}
in
\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
, then
{\mathcal{M}}_{4}(g^{1}_{3})\to\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
is an isomorphism over
\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{4}\smallsetminus\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{4}
and is over

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{4}\cong{\mathcal{H}}_{4}
the universal hyperelliptic curve
{\mathcal{H}}_{4}(g^{1}_{3})
of genus
4
(so that will be a divisor). The strict transform of
{\mathcal{M}}_{4}^{0}
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{4}(g^{1}_{3})

The approach via a classification of coverings

The hadronic contribution to the running of the electromagnetic coupling and the electroweak mixing angle
Marco Cè, Teseo San José, Antoine Gérardin, Harvey B. Meyer, Kohtaroh Miura, Konstantin Ottnad, Andreas Risch, Jonas Wilhelm, Hartmut Wittig

Abstract
The electromagnetic coupling α \alpha and the electroweak mixing angle θ W \theta_{\mathrm{W}} are parameters of the SM ( SM ) that enter precision SM tests and play a fundamental rôle in beyond SM physics searches. Their values are energy dependent, and non-perturbative hadronic contributions are the main source of uncertainty to the theoretical knowledge of the running with energy. We present a lattice study of the leading hadronic contribution to the running of α \alpha and sin 2 ​ θ W \sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}} . The former is related to the HVP ( HVP ) function of electromagnetic currents, and the latter to the HVP mixing of the electromagnetic current with the vector part of the weak neutral currents. We use the TMR ( TMR ) method to compute the HVP on the lattice, estimating both connected and disconnected contributions on N f = 2 + 1 N_{\mathrm{f}}=2+1 non-perturbatively 𝒪 ⁡ ( a ) \order{a} -improved Wilson fermions ensembles from the CLS ( CLS ) initiative. The use of different lattice spacings and quark masses allows us to reliably extrapolate the results to the physical point. MITP/19-064 DESY 19-179

The running of the electromagnetic coupling

The predictions of the
SM
of particle physics are today tested to a high degree of precision in experiments that span a vast range of energy scales, from atomic physics to high-energy colliders.
The connection between these energy scales is encoded in the
running
with energy of the strength of interactions.
For instance, the fine-structure constant
\alpha=1/$137.035\,999\,139(31)$

[
1
]
is known from low-energy experiments to better than a part per billion.
However, its effective value for physics around the
Z
pole is
\hat{\alpha}^{(5)}(M_{Z})=1/$127.955(10)$

[
1
]
, a
7\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
larger value.
In the
on-shell
scheme, the running of
\alpha
at a given time-like momentum transfer
q^{2}
is described by

\alpha(q^{2})=\frac{\alpha}{1-\Delta\alpha(q^{2})},

(1)

in terms of the
\Delta\alpha(q^{2})
function.
While the lepton contribution to
\Delta\alpha(q^{2})
can be computed in perturbation theory, the estimate of the quark contribution at low energies requires non-perturbative calculations of hadronic physics.
Conventionally, the hadronic contribution
\Delta\alpha_{\mathrm{had}}(q^{2})
is related through the optical theorem to the
R
-ratio,
i.e.
the total cross-section
e^{+}e^{-}\to\text{hadrons}
over
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
, which is estimated using a compilation of experimental data.
This results in
\Delta\alpha_{\mathrm{had}}^{(5)}(M_{Z}^{2})=$0.027\,64(7)$

[
1
]
constituting the main contribution to the uncertainty on
\alpha(M_{Z}^{2})
.
In an alternative approach
[
2
]
, the Adler function
D(Q^{2})
at space-like
Q^{2}=-q^{2}
is estimated from the same data and used to compute the running up to
Q^{2}\approx$2\text{\,}{\mathrm{GeV}}^{2}$
.
Around this
Q^{2}
and above,
D(Q^{2})
can be computed reliably in perturbative
QCD
(
QCD
).
Crucially,
\Delta\alpha_{\mathrm{had}}(-Q^{2})
at space-like momenta
Q^{2}>0
is accessible to non-perturbative lattice techniques defined in Euclidean space-time
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
.
It is given by

\Delta\alpha_{\mathrm{had}}(-Q^{2})=4\uppi\alpha\bar{\Pi}^{\gamma\gamma}(Q^{2}),\qquad\bar{\Pi}^{\gamma\gamma}(Q^{2})=[\Pi^{\gamma\gamma}(Q^{2})-\Pi^{\gamma\gamma}(0)],

(2)

where
\bar{\Pi}(Q^{2})
is the subtracted
HVP
function

0

0

with
Q^{2}>0

HVP

(Q_{\mu}Q_{\nu}-\delta_{\mu\nu}Q^{2})\Pi^{\gamma\gamma}(Q^{2})=\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma\gamma}(Q^{2})=\int\differential[4]{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}Q\cdot x}\expectationvalue{j_\mu^\gamma(x) j_\nu^\gamma(0)}

(3)

of the electromagnetic current

j_{\mu}^{\gamma}=\frac{2}{3}\bar{u}\gamma_{\mu}u-\frac{1}{3}\bar{d}\gamma_{\mu}d-\frac{1}{3}\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}s+\frac{2}{3}\bar{c}\gamma_{\mu}c.

(4)

Lattice

can thus provide an estimate that does not depend on experimental

This is of great interest in the context of global

\Delta\alpha_{\mathrm{had}}(M_{Z}^{2})

Indeed, the best-fit result for the Higgs mass excluding kinematic constraints is
M_{H}=$90^{+17}_{-16}\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
,
1.9\sigma
below the measured value, and a shift of
\pm${10}^{-4}$

This is connected to the tension between the

[
6

for a review), because a solution of the
(g-2)_{\mu}
puzzle that involves an increase of the

a_{\mu}^{\mathrm{HLO}}
has to avoid a correlated increase of
\Delta\alpha_{\mathrm{had}}

[

A second connection to

Isolating the hadronic contribution

(5)

The proposed experiment is limited to

The running of the electroweak mixing angle

As a second quantity, we consider the electroweak mixing angle or Weinberg angle
\theta_{\mathrm{W}}
, that is, the parameter of the Standard Model of particle physics that parametrizes the mixing between electromagnetic and weak interactions

\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}=\frac{g^{\prime 2}}{g^{2}+g^{\prime 2}},\qquad e=g\sin\theta_{\mathrm{W}}=g^{\prime}\cos\theta_{\mathrm{W}},

(6)

where
g
and
g^{\prime}
are the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}
and
\mathrm{U}(1)_{Y}
couplings, respectively.
Beyond tree level, as the couplings themselves, its precise value is scheme and energy dependent.
In a given scheme, the mixing angle is measured to sub-permille precision at energies around
M_{Z}
,
e.g.

on-shell

\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}=1-M_{W}^{2}/M_{Z}^{2}=$0.223\,43(7)$
or in the
\overline{\hbox{ms}}
-scheme
\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{W}}(M_{Z})=$0.231\,22(7)$

[
1
]
.
Conversely, the mixing angle in the Thomson limit,
\absolutevalue*{q^2}\ll m_{e}^{2}
, can be defined in a scheme-independent way
[
12
,
13
]
.
Its experimental value is less well known, but upcoming experiments target up to a
0.15\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
precision at a momentum transfer of
4.5\text{\times}{10}^{-3}\text{\,}{\mathrm{GeV}}^{2}

[
14
,
15
]
.
A more precise value,
\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{W}}(0)=$0.238\,68(5)(2)$

[
16
]
, is obtained computing the running in the
\overline{\hbox{ms}}
-scheme from
M_{Z}
to low energies.
The first error is the uncertainty on the
Z
-pole value, while the second error is the total theoretical uncertainty on the running and it is dominated by the non-perturbative hadronic contribution.

The energy dependence of
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}
in the on-shell scheme can be written as

\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}(q^{2})=\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}\left[1+\Delta\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}(q^{2})\right],

(7)

where
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}
is the value in the low-energy limit.
Similarly to Eq (
2

Q^{2}=-q^{2}
is given by
[
17
,
2
]

\Delta_{\mathrm{had}}\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}(-Q^{2})=-\frac{e^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}}\bar{\Pi}^{Z\gamma}(Q^{2}),

(8)

where
\bar{\Pi}^{Z\gamma}(Q^{2})
is
HVP
mixing of the electromagnetic current
j_{\mu}^{\gamma}
and the vector part of the neutral weak current
j_{\mu}^{Z}

j_{\mu}^{Z}\big|_{\mathrm{vector}}=j_{\mu}^{T_{3}}\big|_{\mathrm{vector}}-\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}j_{\mu}^{\gamma},\qquad j_{\mu}^{T_{3}}\big|_{\mathrm{vector}}=\frac{1}{4}\bar{u}\gamma_{\mu}u-\frac{1}{4}\bar{d}\gamma_{\mu}d-\frac{1}{4}\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}s+\frac{1}{4}\bar{c}\gamma_{\mu}c.

(9)

As in the electromagnetic case,
\bar{\Pi}^{Z\gamma}(Q^{2})
is directly accessible to lattice computations

4.2
Autocorrelation study

Due to the update procedure of Monte Carlo simulations different configurations within one chain are not independent.
In order to give a reliable estimate of the observable’s uncertainty we need to take these autocorrelations into account.
For this work we have used the
\Gamma
-method
[
29
,
30
]
to estimate the autocorrelation time,
\tau_{\mathrm{int}}
and, from it, an appropriate bin size to obtain statistically independent samples.
Since autocorrelations are observable-dependent we have computed
\tau_{\mathrm{int}}
of
\Delta\alpha_{\mathrm{had}}
for the light and strange flavours on each ensemble at different energies.
Extra caution needs to be taken regarding the tail of the correlator, which shows the
SN
problem (see Section
4.4
).
To obtain a good estimate of
\tau_{\mathrm{int}}
we only take into account the correlator until a certain maximum value of
x_{0}
.
Repeating the process for different time cuts, we know where the noise of the tail starts to dominate and the value of
\tau_{\mathrm{int}}
.
We choose a bin size
B=2\tau_{\mathrm{int}}
for each ensemble to bin the correlator data and obtain a distribution of independent bootstrap samples to carry out the main analysis.

4.3
Finite-size corrections

In this work, we estimate finite-volume corrections using the same strategy described in Ref.
[
26
]
.
Namely, we compute the difference between the infinite- and finite-volume
I=1
correlator and apply it as a correction to the lattice data.
We use two distinct models to compute the difference, depending on whether the correlator is considered at short or long times.

At long times, we use the
I=1
correlator obtained from the time-like pion form factor
F_{\pi}(\omega)
.
In infinite volume, that is

G^{33}(x_{0},\infty)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{\omega}\omega^{2}\rho(\omega^{2})\mathrm{e}^{-\omega x_{0}},\qquad\rho(\omega^{2})=\frac{1}{48\uppi^{2}}\left(1-\frac{4M_{\pi}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\absolutevalue{F_\pi(\omega)}^{2},

(18)

while the finite volume expression is computed from the Lüscher energies
\omega_{n}
and the Lellouch-Lüscher amplitudes,

G^{33}(x_{0},L)=\sum_{n}\absolutevalue{A_n}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega_{n}x_{0}},

(19)

extracted applying the Lellouch-Lüscher formalism
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
.
In this work, we use the
GS
(
GS
) parametrization
[
34
]
of
F_{\pi}(\omega)
, that depends on two parameters,
m_{\rho}
and
g_{\rho\pi\pi}
, which are obtained either by fitting the correlator at long times or by a spectroscopic analysis
[
26
]
.
We emphasise that the model is used only to correct for the relatively small finite-volume effect of the correlator, while no modelling of the contribution from the correlator tail is assumed.
In the future, we plan to further reduce the model-dependence employing, where available, a full lattice determination of
F_{\pi}(\omega)

[
35
,
36
]
instead of the
GS
parametrization.

The
F_{\pi}(\omega)
-based model provides a good spectral representation of the correlator up to the three-pion threshold, thus we use it for the correlator correction at times
x_{0}>x_{0i}
, with
x_{0i}=(M_{\pi}L/4)^{2}/M_{\pi}
.
At smaller times
x_{0}\leq x_{0i}
we compute the difference between the infinite- and finite-volume correlator in scalar QED (
i.e.
NLO χPT)
[
22
,
21
]

G^{33}(x_{0},\infty)-G^{33}(x_{0},L)=\frac{1}{3}\left(\int\frac{\differential[3]{\vec{k}}}{(2\uppi)^{3}}-\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{\vec{k}}\right)\frac{\vec{k}^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-2x_{0}\sqrt{\vec{k}^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2}}}.

(20)

This model omits the pion self-interaction and it is known to only account for a fraction of the finite-volume correction to
\bar{\Pi}(Q^{2})
at
Q^{2}
values of
\order{\si{\GeV\squared}}

[
37
]
.
However, comparing the correlator on two pairs of ensembles in Table
1
with the same parameters except for different physical volumes, we observe that the finite-volume correction at times
x_{0}\leq x_{0i}
is smaller than our uncertainties.
Therefore, we are confident that this rather simple short-time description is sufficient at the current level of precision, while recent developments
[
38
]
show a promising path towards improving on it.

We can estimate the relative size of the finite-volume corrections.
For example, on ensembles D200, N302 and J303, the ratio of the correction over the total
\Delta\alpha_{\mathrm{had}}
at
Q^{2}=$1\text{\,}{\mathrm{GeV}}^{2}$
amounts to more than
1\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
.
The contribution, although small in absolute terms, is already bigger than the
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
statistical uncertainty on these ensembles and therefore needs to be included.

4.4
Signal-to-noise ratio

Both the correlator and its variance can be expressed, using the spectral decomposition, as an infinite tower of exponentials, but with different decay rates and amplitudes.
In general, this leads to an exponential deterioration of the signal with respect to the noise with Euclidean time, known as the
SN
problem.
As shown in Section
3
, this problem has a larger impact on the statistical error of
\bar{\Pi}(Q^{2})
at smaller
Q^{2}
.
Although it affects all flavour components, it is more acute for the light correlator, whose contribution remains sizeable longer in time.

As described in Ref.
[
21
]
, one possibility is to model the tail of the correlator with a single exponential,
i.e.

G(x_{0})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{data},&x_{0}<x_{0}^{\mathrm{cut}},\\
A\mathrm{e}^{-m_{V}x_{0}},&x_{0}\geq x_{0}^{\mathrm{cut}},\end{cases}

(21)

where
A
and
m_{V}
are obtained fitting the correlator.
In our case, due to the high statistics available,
x_{0}^{\mathrm{cut}}
lies between
1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{fm}
and
2.5\text{\,}\mathrm{fm}
, depending on the ensemble.
However, there is no guarantee that a single state describes the tail of the correlator.
This is particularly true for light ensembles in a large volume, where both the
\rho
resonance and the tower of two-
\pi
states are long range contributions.
Therefore, this method has its limitations, and we choose not to use it.
The results presented in the following sections are obtained using the correlator data until the last time slice available.
As explained in Section
3
, the contribution from the tail to
\bar{\Pi}(Q^{2})
is smaller than in the case of
a_{\mu}^{\mathrm{HLO}}
.

In the future, we are considering improving the statistical error on
\bar{\Pi}(Q^{2})
at small
Q^{2}
, especially on light-pion ensembles, substituting the correlator at long times with a spectral reconstruction, and controlling the systematics
e.g.
with the bounding method
[
39
,
26
]
. In its simplest incarnation, this relies on the fact that
G(x_{0})
at
x_{0}\geq x_{0}^{\mathrm{cut}}
is bounded by

0\leq G(x_{0}^{\mathrm{cut}})\mathrm{e}^{-E_{\mathrm{eff}}(x_{0}^{\mathrm{cut}})(x_{0}-x_{0}^{\mathrm{cut}})}\leq G(x_{0})\leq G(x_{0}^{\mathrm{cut}})\mathrm{e}^{-E_{0}(x_{0}-x_{0}^{\mathrm{cut}})},

(22)

where
E_{\mathrm{eff}}(x_{0})=-\differential\log(G(x_0))/\differential{x_0}
is the effective mass and
E_{0}
the ground state energy, to obtain the
x_{0}^{\mathrm{cut}}
so both bounds yield the same result for the observable.

6
Conclusions and outlook

We computed on the lattice the leading hadronic contribution to the running of the electromagnetic coupling
\alpha
and of the electroweak mixing angle
\theta_{\mathrm{W}}
.
After extrapolating to the physical point, the statistical error on the connected contribution amounts to
\approx$4\text{\times}{10}^{-5}$
which, for both quantities, is
1\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of the contribution at
Q^{2}=$1\text{\,}{\mathrm{GeV}}^{2}$
.
We also presented results for the disconnected contribution, not yet extrapolated at the physical point, which show that the statistical error is also around
1\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of the total contribution.
We included finite-volume corrections, that are instrumental for obtaining a reliable physical-point extrapolation.
The precision of our results is comparable to the phenomenological estimate, which is around
0.7\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
and
1\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
for the two quantities respectively
[
40
]
.
In particular, flavour contributions are naturally singled out on the lattice.
Thus, our results can be combined with the phenomenological analysis of the electroweak mixing angle to reduce its flavour separation systematics.

We are currently working on increasing the statistics of disconnected loops at physical meson masses.
Subsequently, we will extrapolate the disconnected and the quenched charm contribution to the physical point.
Moreover, a full assessment of systematic errors is missing from the results in Table
3
.
We plan to assess the systematics introduced by the extrapolation by varying the choice of the fit function, and, in the future, adding a new ensemble at the fine lattice spacing
a\approx$0.050\text{\,}\mathrm{fm}$
with a lighter pion mass of
M_{\pi}\approx$175\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}$
.
We plan, as described in Section
4.4
, to improve the treatment of the long-time tail of the lighter ensembles with the bounding method.
The scale setting introduces an error that can be estimated as explained in Section B.2 of Ref.
[
21
]
.
Also, a small systematics from the mistuning of the charm hopping parameter needs to be added.
Finally, to compare with the physical world, isospin-breaking effects from non-degenerate
u
and
d
quark masses and QED are to be included
[
41
,
42
]
.

Multiplicities and Betti numbers in local algebra via lim Ulrich points
Srikanth B. Iyengar, Linquan Ma, Mark E. Walker

Abstract
. This work concerns finite free complexes with finite length homology over a commutative noetherian local ring R R . The focus is on complexes that have length dim ​ R \mathrm{dim}\,R , which is the smallest possible value, and in particular on free resolutions of modules of finite length and finite projective dimension. Lower bounds are obtained on the Euler characteristic of such short complexes when R R is a strict complete intersection, and also on the Dutta multiplicity, when R R is the localization at its maximal ideal of a standard graded algebra over a field of positive prime characteristic. The key idea in the proof is the construction of a suitable Ulrich module, or, in the latter case, a sequence of modules that have the Ulrich property asymptotically, and with good convergence properties in the rational Grothendieck group of R R . Such a sequence is obtained by constructing an appropriate sequence of sheaves on the associated projective variety.

1.
Introduction

This paper investigates various questions about multiplicities of modules over (commutative noetherian) local rings, the most basic of which is:

For a given local ring
R
, what is the smallest possible length of a nonzero
R
-module having finite projective dimension?

This question is only interesting for Cohen-Macaulay rings: If
R
is not Cohen-Macaulay, then every nonzero module of finite projective dimension has infinite length; this is by Roberts’ New Intersection Theorem
[
31
]
. On the other hand, when
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\boldsymbol{x}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
form a system of parameters for
R
, the
R
-module
R/(\boldsymbol{x})
has finite projective dimension and its length satisfies

\ell_{R}(R/(\boldsymbol{x}))\geq e(R)

where
e(R)
denotes the Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity of
R
. In fact, at least when the residue field of
R
is infinite, equality holds when
\boldsymbol{x}
is chosen to be sufficiently general. This leads us to conjecture:

Conjecture 1
.

For a local ring
R
, every nonzero
R
-module
M
of finite projective dimension satisfies
\ell_{R}M\geq e(R)
.

One measure of its difficulty is that it implies the still open Lech’s conjecture
[
23
]
, recalled in Section
8
, at least for Cohen-Macaulay rings; see
[
25
, Chapter V]
.

It is not hard to verify Conjecture
1
when
\dim(R)\leq 1
; see Example
3.3
. It also holds trivially when
R
is regular, for then
e(R)=1
. For an arbitrary local ring the conjecture holds when the module
M
is extended from a module over a regular ring: if
A\to R
is a finite flat map of local rings with
A
regular,
N
is a finitely generated
A
-module, and
M=R\otimes_{A}N
, then
\ell_{R}M\geq e(R)
.

Nearly all cases in which we can establish Conjecture
1
for a local ring
R
, it is because
R
admits an Ulrich module: a nonzero maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
U
whose Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity,
e(U)
, equals its minimal number of generators,
\nu_{R}(U)
; this is the smallest possible value for the multiplicity. For instance, in Theorem
4.6
we prove the following result. Here
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
denotes the Grothendieck group of finitely generated
R
-modules modulo numerical equivalence, extended to
{\mathbb{R}}
; see Section
4
for details. Moreover
\beta^{R}_{i}(M)
is the
i
th Betti number of
M
.

Theorem 1
.

If
R
admits an Ulrich module
U
whose class
[U]
in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
is a multiple of
[R]
, then for each
R
-module
M
of finite length and finite projective dimension

\binom{\dim R}{i}\ell_{R}M\geq\beta^{R}_{i}(M)e(R)

for all
i
. In particular, Conjecture
1
holds for
R
.

A local ring
R
is a
strict complete intersection
if its associated graded ring
\mathrm{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is a complete intersection. Every strict complete intersection is a complete intersection; the converse holds for hypersurfaces, but fails if the codimension is at least two. It follows from a result of Backelin, Herzog, and Ulrich
[
15
]
that the hypothesis of Theorem
1
holds whenever
R
is a strict complete intersection. Thus Conjecture
1
holds for all such rings and, in particular, for all hypersurfaces.

Previously, Avramov, Buchwietz, Iyengar and Miller
[
2
, Section 1]
established lower bounds on the Loewy length of modules of finite projective dimension. One of their results is that if
R
is a strict complete intersection of codimension
c
, then

\ell\ell_{R}M\geq e(R)-c+1\,.

In particular,
\ell_{R}M\geq e(R)-c+1
, which also verifies Conjecture
1
for hypersufaces.

It is an open question whether every Cohen-Macaulay ring admits a nonzero Ulrich module. This appears to be a rather difficult problem; in any case, known examples are rare. However, for many arguments involving Ulrich modules what is needed is not an actual Ulrich module, but rather just a class in the real vector space
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
with suitable properties. In detail, we say that a nonzero class
\alpha
in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
is a
lim Ulrich point
if it is a positive linear combination of terms of the form

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{[U_{n}]}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

where
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is a
lim Ulrich sequence
of
R
-modules; that the limit above exists is an additional constraint. We refer the reader to
5.10
for the precise definition of lim Ulrich sequences, but, heuristically, it means that the modules in the list have the Ulrich property asymptotically.

For instance, the conclusion of Theorem
1
remains valid if, instead of requiring that
[R]=\frac{1}{r}[U]
in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
for some integer
r
and Ulrich module
U
, we have merely that
[R]
is a lim Ulrich point in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
.

Lim Ulrich sequences appear to be more common than Ulrich modules. In particular, the second author has shown that every standard graded algebra over a perfect field of positive characteristic admits a lim Ulrich sequence of modules. We revisit the construction of such sequence in Sections
6
and
7
, approaching them from a more geometric, and hence simpler, point of view.

In particular, we introduce the notion of a “lim Ulrich sequence of sheaves” on a projective scheme over a field. We prove a lim Ulrich sequence of sheaves gives rise to a lim Ulrich sequence of graded modules over the associated homogeneous coordinate ring; see Theorem
6.7
. Moreover, in Theorem
7.15
we establish that every projective scheme over an infinite, perfect field of positive characteristic
p
admits a lim Ulrich sequence of sheaves. These two results recover the second author’s construction of lim Ulrich sequence of modules over such rings. We hope that our results on lim Ulrich sequence of sheaves prove to be of wider interest; for instance, in a forthcoming paper we intend to use them to study the cone of cohomology tables of coherent sheaves on projective schemes.

To summarize, if
R
is the localization of a standard graded
k
-algebra
A
at its homogeneous maximal ideal, where
k
is a perfect field of positive characteristic, then
R
admits a lim Ulrich sequence
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
. Moreover, we are able to analyze the associated sequence of points in the Grothendieck group
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
. Alas, this sequence fails to establish that
[R]
is a lim Ulrich point. It is therefore not possible to deduce Conjecture
1
using this sequence of modules.

Rather, we prove that
[R]_{d}\in{\mathbb{E}}(R)
is a lim Ulrich point, where
[R]_{d}
is the
d
-th component of
[R]
with respect to the weight decomposition of
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
induced by the action of Frobenius. This leads to the result below where
\chi_{\infty}(M)
denotes the Dutta multiplicity of
M
.

Theorem 2
.

If
R
is the localization at its homogeneous maximal ideal of a standard graded algebra over a field of positive characteristic, and
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module
M
of finite length and finite projective dimension, then

\binom{\dim R}{i}\chi_{\infty}(M)\geq\beta^{R}_{i}(M)e(R)

for all
i
. In particular,
\chi_{\infty}(M)\geq e(R)
.

One has
\chi_{\infty}(M)=\ell_{R}(M)
when
R
is a complete intersection, and more generally when
R
is numerically Roberts, and also when
M
is the localization of a finitely generated graded module. This connects the theorem above to Theorem
1
.

Theorem
2
is in turn subsumed in Theorem
3
below that applies also to non Cohen-Macaulay rings. As mentioned earlier, when
R
is not Cohen-Macaulay every nonzero module of finite projective dimension has infinite length, and so our investigations for such rings focus instead on “short complexes supported on the maximal ideal”. This term refers to a non-exact complex of finite free
R
-modules of the form

F\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}0\longrightarrow F_{\dim(R)}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow F_{1}\longrightarrow F_{0}\longrightarrow 0\,,

such that
H_{i}(F)
has finite length for all
i
. The adjective “short” comes from the fact that the length of any finite free complex with nonzero finite length homology is at least
\dim R
; this is the New Intersection Theorem. When
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, such a short complex is necessarily the resolution of the module
H_{0}(F)
, and thus we are back in our original context.

For any finite free complex
F
with finite length homology, let
\chi(F)
denote its Euler characteristic. When
R
has positive characteristic
p
and
\varphi\colon R\to R
is the Frobenius endomorphism, the
Dutta multiplicity
of
F
is

\chi_{\infty}(F)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\chi((\varphi^{*})^{n}(F))}{p^{n\dim(R)}}\,.

Theorem
2
extends to non Cohen-Macaulay rings as follows; see Corollary
7.2
.

Theorem 3
.

If
R
is the localization at its homogeneous maximal ideal of a standard graded algebra over a field of positive characteristic, and
F
is a short complex supported on the maximal ideal, then

\binom{\dim R}{i}\chi_{\infty}(F)\geq\beta_{i}(F)e(R)

for all
i
. In particular,
\chi_{\infty}(F)\geq e(R)
.

Emboldened by this theorem, we pose:

Conjecture 2
.

For a local ring
R
, every short complex
F
supported on the maximal ideal satisfies

\chi_{\infty}(F)\geq e(R)\,.

We close this introduction with a few remarks. First, Conjecture
2
implies Lech’s conjecture; see Proposition
8.3
. Second, Roberts
[
32
]
has constructed short complexes
F
supported on the maximal ideal of a certain local ring such that
\chi(F)<0
. Therefore Conjecture
2
cannot hold with the usual Euler characteristic
\chi
in place of the Dutta multiplicity
\chi_{\infty}
.

Finally, Yhee
[
36
]
has recently constructed two-dimensional, complete, local domains
R
that admit no lim Ulrich sequences. The existence of such rings points to a limitation of the central technique used in this paper for addressing Conjectures
1
and
2
.
Nevertheless, both conjectures hold for Yhee’s examples. These topics are also discussed in Section
8
.

2.
Mulitiplicities and finite projective dimension

In this section we recall basic definitions and results concerning multiplicities and finite free complexes. We take the book of Bruns and Herzog
[
9
]
as our standard reference for this material. Throughout
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue field
k
. Set
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim R
. Given a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, set

\nu_{R}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{k}(M/\mathfrak{m}M)\,;

this is the size of a minimal generating set for
M
. The Krull dimension of
M
is denoted
\dim_{R}M
.

2.1
.

The key invariant in this work is the (modified) multiplicity
e_{d}(-)
, defined on the category of finitely generated
R
-modules by the formula:

e_{d}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}d!\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{R}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)}{n^{d}}\,,

where
\ell_{R}(-)
denotes length. When there is no cause of confusion we write
e(R)
instead of
e_{d}(R)
. It is a consequence of a theorem of Hilbert and Serre that
e_{d}(M)
is a nonnegative integer. Moreover,
e_{d}(M)=0
if and only if
\dim_{R}M<\dim R
. An important property of the multiplicity is that it is additive on short exact sequence; see
[
9
, Corollary 4.7.7]
for details.

In a few places we need also the
multiplicty
of
M
, defined by

e(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\dim_{R}M)!\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{R}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)}{n^{\dim_{R}M}}\,.

One has
e(M)=e_{d}(M)
if and only if
\dim_{R}M=\dim R
.

2.2
.

Given a sequence
\boldsymbol{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}r_{1},\dots,r_{n}
we write
K(\boldsymbol{r};M)
for the Koszul complex on
\boldsymbol{r}
with coefficients in
M
, and

\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{r};M)
for its homology module in degree
i
. When
\boldsymbol{r}
is a
multiplicity system
for
M
, meaning that
\ell_{R}(M/\boldsymbol{r}M)
is finite, the lengths of all the Koszul homology modules are finite, and in this case one sets

\chi(\boldsymbol{r};M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{r};M)\,.

If
\boldsymbol{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}r_{1},\dots,r_{d}
generate a minimal reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
, in the sense of
[
9
, Remark 4.6.9]
, then

(2.3)

e_{d}(M)=\chi(\boldsymbol{r};M)\,.

This result follows from the work of Auslander and Buchsbaum, and Serre
[
9
, Theorem 4.7.6 and Corollary 4.6.10]
. Such minimal reductions exist when the residue field
k
is infinite, so in this case one can compute the multiplicity as an Euler characteristic; see Corollary
4.5
for a version that covers all local rings.

In the same vein when
k
is infinite, there exist elements
\boldsymbol{r}
in
\mathfrak{m}
that form a system of parameters for
M
and satisfy

e(M)=\chi(\boldsymbol{r};M)\,.

When in addition
M
is Cohen-Macaulay any system of parameters for
M
is a regular sequence and hence for a sequence
\boldsymbol{r}
as above one gets that

e(M)=\ell_{R}(M/\boldsymbol{r}M)\,.

2.4
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. With
I\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{ann}_{R}M
, the annihilator ideal of
M
, and
\operatorname{grade}_{R}M
denoting the longest regular sequence in
I
, one has

0ptR-\dim_{R}M\leq\operatorname{grade}_{R}M\leq 0ptI\leq\dim R-\dim_{R}M\leq\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}M\,.

For a proof, see
[
4
, (2.3) and (2.4)]
. The rightmost inequality is useful only when
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}M
is finite, and then it is the Intersection Theorem of Peskine and Szpiro
[
28
, §1]
, proved in full generality by Roberts as a consequence of his New Intersection Theorem; see
2.6
. When
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}M
is finite the equality of Auslander and Buchsbaum reads

\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}M=0ptR-0pt_{R}M\,.

A finitely generated
R
-module
M
is said to be
perfect
if

\operatorname{grade}_{R}M=\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}M\,.

It is immediate from the inequalities in
2.4
that when the ring
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, an
R
-module
M
is perfect if and only if it Cohen-Macaulay and of finite projective dimension. Conversely if
R
has a nonzero module of finite length and finite projective dimension, then
R
is Cohen-Macaulay. This is a consequence of the New Intersection Theorem, recalled below; see
2.6
.

2.5
.

We write
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
for the category of finite free
R
-complexes, that is to say, bounded complexes of finitely generated free
R
-modules, with finite length homology. The
Euler characteristic
of such an
F
is the integer

\chi_{R}(F)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(F)\,.

For any integer
i
, the
i
th
Betti number
of
F
is

\beta^{R}_{i}(F)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(k,F)\,.

When
F
is minimal, that is to say,
d(F)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}F
, one has
\beta^{R}_{i}(F)=\operatorname{rank}_{R}F_{i}
. Set

\beta^{R}(F)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i}\beta^{R}_{i}(F)\,,

this is the
total Betti number
of
F
.

2.6
.

Consider
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
of the form

0\longrightarrow F_{n}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow F_{0}\longrightarrow 0

and with
\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\neq 0
. The New Intersection Theorem proved by Roberts
[
31
,
32
]
states that
n\geq\dim R
. In what follows we say
F
is a
short
complex in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
to indicate that it is a finite free complex as above with
n=\dim R
. When
R
is Cohen-Macaulay this is tantamount to saying that
F
is the free resolution of a finite length
R
-module, namely,
\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)
.

For any complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
as above and
R
-module
U
one has

0pt_{R}U=\dim R-\sup\{i\mid\operatorname{H}_{i}(F\otimes_{R}U)\neq 0\}\,.

This is a particular instance of the depth sensitivity of complexes in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
; see, for example,
[
17
, Corollary 6.4]
. In particular
\operatorname{H}_{i}(F\otimes_{R}U)=0
for
i\geq 1
when
U
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay.

2.7
.

Let
\varphi\colon(R,\mathfrak{m})\to(S,\mathfrak{n})
be a flat local map with
\mathfrak{m}S=\mathfrak{n}
. It is easy to check from definitions that
e(S)=e(R)
. Equally, for any
F\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
it is easy to verify that the complex
S\otimes_{R}F
is in
{\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{n}}(S)}
, and there is an equality

\beta^{S}_{i}(S\otimes_{R}F)=\beta^{R}_{i}(F)\qquad\text{for each $i$.}

Moreover
\chi(S\otimes_{R}F)=\chi(F)
. Given any extension of fields
k\to l
, there exists a flat local extension
S
of
R
as above whose residue field is
l
; see, for example,
[
7
, Appendice 2]
. This, and the discussion in the previous paragraph, often allow us to reduce the problem on hand to the case where the residue field of
R
is infinite.

3.
Euler characteristics of short complexes

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring and set
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim R
. We shall be interested in those rings
R
with the property that the Euler characteristic of any short complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, in the sense of
2.6
, satisfies inequalities:

(3.1)

\binom{d}{i}\chi_{R}(F)\geq\beta^{R}_{i}(F)e(R)\quad\text{for each $0\leq i\leq d$}\,.

If
R
is Cohen-Macaulay,
F
has homology only in degree
0
so
\chi_{R}(F)=\ell_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{0}(F))
. Thus Conjecture
1
is the case
i=0
of the inequalities above.

The inequalities in (
3.1
) do not always hold: Roberts
[
32
, §4]
has constructed
F
as above with negative Euler characteristic. However when
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, the negativity of the Euler characteristic cannot arise as an obstruction, and we do not know if the inequalities above hold for this class of rings. They are easy to verify when
R
is regular. The main result of this section, Theorem
3.8
, is that they hold also when
R
is a strict complete intersection. A stronger result holds when
R
is a standard graded ring and
F
is a complex of graded
R
-modules. This will be dealt with in forthcoming work; see also Corollary
7.2
and
7.3
.

We begin with some general observations. For
i=0
the inequality (
3.1
) reads

\chi_{R}(F)\geq\beta^{R}_{0}(F)e(R)\,.

Taking the sum over
i
in (
3.1
) yields an inequality

\chi_{R}(F)\geq\frac{\beta^{R}(F)}{2^{d}}e(R)\,.

The third author
[
34
]
proved that when
R
is complete intersection with
\operatorname{char}k\neq 2
, or when
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and contains a field of positive characteristic
p\geq 3
, then any
F\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
with
\operatorname{H}(F)\neq 0
satisfies

(3.2)

\beta^{R}(F)\geq 2^{d}\frac{|\chi_{R}(F)|}{\sum_{i}\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(F)}\,.

For now we record a simple example where these inequalities hold.

Example 3.3
.

If
\dim R\leq 1
, then (
3.1
) holds for all short complexes in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
.

Indeed, we can assume any such
F
is minimal, and so has the form

0\longrightarrow R^{a}\xrightarrow{\ \phi\ }R^{a}\longrightarrow 0\,.

with
\phi(R^{a})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}R^{a}
. With
\det(\phi)
denoting the determinant of
\phi
, from
[
12
, Lemma A.2.6]
one gets the first equality below

\chi(F)=\chi(K(\det(\phi);R))=e(\det(\phi),R)\geq e(\mathfrak{m}^{a},R)=a\cdot e(R)\,.

The second equality is from (
2.3
). The inequality holds because
\det(\phi)
is in
\mathfrak{m}^{a}
.

It will be helpful to consider all finitely generated
R
-modules
U
with the property that for any short complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
and integer
0\leq i\leq d
one has

(3.4)

\binom{d}{i}\chi_{R}(F\otimes_{R}U)\geq\beta^{R}_{i}(F)e_{d}(U)\,.

Observe that (
3.1
) is the case
U=R
. Here is a simple observation.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
F
be a short complex in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
. If an
R
-module
W
admits a finite filtration with subquotient modules satisfying (
3.4
) for
F
, then so does
W
.

Proof.

Let
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}
be the subquotients of the filtration in the hypothesis. Evidently
\dim_{R}W\geq\dim_{R}U_{j}
for each
j
, so the additivity of multiplicity
[
9
, Corollary 4.7.7]
yields the last equality below:

\displaystyle\binom{d}{i}\chi(F,W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\binom{d}{i}\chi_{R}(F,U_{j})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j}\beta^{R}_{i}(F)e_{d}(U_{j})

\displaystyle=\beta^{R}_{i}(F)e_{d}(W)\,.

The first equality is by the additivity of Euler characteristics and the inequality holds by hypothesis.
∎

The proof suggests working in the Grothendieck group on
R
. We pick up on this theme in the ensuing sections. Next we introduce a class of modules for which the inequalities in (
3.4
) hold.

Ulrich modules

For any finitely generated maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
M
there is an inequality

e_{d}(M)\geq\nu_{R}(M)\,.

See, for example,
[
8
, Proposition 1.1]
. An
Ulrich module
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
U
with
e_{d}(U)=\nu_{R}(U)
. When the residue field of
R
is infinite, this condition is equivalent to: there exists a system of parameters
\boldsymbol{x}
for
R
with

K(\boldsymbol{x};U)\simeq U/\mathfrak{m}U\,;

in particular,
\boldsymbol{x}U=\mathfrak{m}U
. In fact, when
U
is Ulrich any generic choice of
\boldsymbol{x}
has these properties. This characterization becomes helpful once we make the following observation which is immediate from the discussion in
2.7
.

3.6
.

If
U
is an Ulrich
R
-module and
(R,\mathfrak{m})\to(S,\mathfrak{n})
is a flat local map such that
\mathfrak{m}S=\mathfrak{n}
, then the
S
-module
S\otimes_{R}U
is Ulrich.

Proposition 3.7
.

Ulrich modules satisfy (
3.4
) for all short complexes in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
.

Proof.

Let
U
be an Ulrich module and
F
a short complex in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
. Given
2.7
and
3.6
, we can inflate the residue field of
R
if necessary to ensure that it is infinite. There then exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}r_{1},\dots,r_{d}
, where
d=\dim R
, such that the sequence
\boldsymbol{r}
is regular on
U
and
U/\boldsymbol{r}U\cong k^{e(U)}
. One has quasi-isomorphisms

K(\boldsymbol{r};F\otimes_{R}U)\simeq F\otimes_{R}K(\boldsymbol{r};U)\simeq F\otimes_{R}k^{e(U)}\simeq(F\otimes_{R}k)^{e(U)}

Therefore one gets equalities

\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{r};F\otimes_{R}U)=\beta^{R}_{i}(F)e(U)\,.

On the other hand, since
U
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay
H_{i}(F\otimes_{R}U)=0
for
i\geq 1
, as discussed
2.6
, so that one has quasi-isomorphisms

F\otimes_{R}U\simeq\operatorname{H}_{0}(F\otimes_{R}U)\cong\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\otimes_{R}U\,.

This yields a quasi-isomorphism

K(\boldsymbol{r};F\otimes_{R}U)\simeq K(\boldsymbol{r};\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\otimes_{R}U)\,.

From these computations we get

\displaystyle\beta^{R}_{i}(F)e(U)

\displaystyle=\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{r};F\otimes_{R}U)

\displaystyle=\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{r};\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\otimes_{R}U)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{d}{i}\ell_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\otimes_{R}U)

\displaystyle=\binom{d}{i}\chi_{R}(F\otimes_{R}U)\,.

This is the desired inequality.
∎

A local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a
strict complete intersection
if its associated graded ring
\mathrm{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is complete intersection. For example, every hypersurface is a strict complete intersection. Another example is the localization of a standard graded complete intersection at its homogenous maximal ideal.

Theorem 3.8
.

When
R
is a strict complete intersection local ring, any nonzero finite length
R
-module
M
of finite projective dimension satisfies

\binom{d}{i}\ell_{R}(M)\geq\beta^{R}_{i}(M)e(R)\quad\text{for each $0\leq i\leq d$}\,.

In particular,
\ell_{R}(M)\geq(\beta^{R}(M)/2^{d})\cdot e(R)
.

Proof.

The hypothesis on
R
implies that there exists a positive integer
s
such that the free module
R^{s}
has a finite filtration whose subquotients are Ulrich modules; this is implicit in
[
15
, §2]
; see also
[
25
, Theorem V.28]
. Thus Proposition
3.7
and Lemma
3.5
imply that (
3.4
) holds for
U\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R^{s}
and the minimal free resolution
F
of
M
. Given the equalities

\chi_{R}(F\otimes_{R}R^{s})=s\cdot\ell_{R}(M)\qquad\text{and}\qquad e(R^{s})=s\cdot e(R)\,,

the desired result follow.
∎

Next we prove the following result about perfect modules. This is connected to Lech’s conjecture; we postpone that discussion to Section
8
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
R
be a local ring with infinite residue field. If each short complex in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
satisfies (
3.1
) for
i=0
, then any perfect
R
-module
M
satisfies

e(M)\geq\nu_{R}(M)\cdot e(R)\,.

If (
3.1
) holds for all
i
, then

e(M)\geq\frac{\beta^{R}(M)}{2^{d-\dim_{R}(M)}}\cdot e(R)\,.

Proof.

Set
c\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim_{R}(M)
. Since the residue field of
R
is infinite, there exists a system of parameters
\boldsymbol{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}r_{1},\dots,r_{c}
for
M
such
that
e(M)=\chi(K\otimes_{R}M)
, where
K
is the Koszul complex on
\boldsymbol{r}
; see (
2.3
). Let
G
be a minimal free resolution of
M
. Since
M
is perfect the length of
G
equals
d-c
. It follows that
K\otimes_{R}G
is a short complex in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
. Thus the hypothesis yields the inequality below:

e(M)=\chi(K\otimes_{R}G)\geq\nu_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{0}(K\otimes_{R}G))\cdot e(R)=\nu_{R}(M)e(R)\,.

The equality on the left holds because
K\otimes_{R}G\simeq K\otimes_{R}M
, whereas the equality on the right holds because
\operatorname{H}_{0}(K\otimes_{R}G)=M/\boldsymbol{r}M
. This justifies the first claim.

In the same vein, if (
3.1
) holds for each
i
then

\binom{d}{i}e(M)\geq\beta^{R}_{i}(K\otimes_{R}G)e(R)\,.

Summing these gives
2^{d}e(M)\geq\beta^{R}(K\otimes_{R}G)e(R)
. Since

\beta^{R}(K\otimes_{R}G)=\beta^{R}(K)\beta^{R}(G)=2^{c}\beta^{R}(M)

the desired inequality follows.
∎

3.10
.

We have already noted that the inequalities in (
3.1
) do not hold for all local rings. They can also fail if we allow complexes that are not short. For example, let
G
be any short complex in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
with
\operatorname{H}(G)\neq 0
, choose an element
r\in\mathfrak{m}
, and let
F
be the mapping cone of the map
G\xrightarrow{r}G
. It is easy to see that
F
is a finite free complex of length
d+1
satisfying
\operatorname{H}(F)\neq 0
and
\chi(F)=0
.

In a different direction, it is of interest to consider versions of (
3.1
) where the Euler characteristic of
F
is replaced by the length of its homology module,
\operatorname{H}(F)
. This becomes relevant only when
R
is not Cohen-Macaulay. We have not much to say about this at the moment, except that here too the corresponding inequalities can fail for complexes that are not short; see
[
18
]
for similar phenomena.

Indeed, if
R
is a complete intersection of codimension
c
and
K
the Koszul complex on a minimal list of generators of
\mathfrak{m}
, then
\operatorname{H}_{1}(K)\cong k^{c}
and there is an isomorphism of
k
-algebras

\operatorname{H}(K)\cong\Lambda_{k}\mathsf{\Sigma}\operatorname{H}_{1}(K)\,;

see
[
9
, Theorem 2.3.9]
. Therefore
\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}(K)=2^{c}
. On the other hand the multiplicity of
R
can be much larger; for example, when

R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/(x_{1}^{d_{1}},\dots,x_{c}^{d_{c}})

one has
e(R)=d_{1}\cdots d_{c}
, which can be arbitrarily larger than
2^{c}
.

4.
Grothendieck groups

In this section we introduce Grothendieck groups of modules and related constructions. These play a crucial role in the remainder of this work. As before
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
will be a local ring and
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim R
.

4.1
.

Let
\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)
be the Grothendieck group of finitely generated
R
-modules. Let
\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
be the Grothendieck group of the category of finite free complexes with finite length homology,
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, modulo the exact complexes; see
[
13
]
. Given a finitely generated
R
-module
U
and a complex
L
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, set

\langle L,U\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\chi(L\otimes_{R}U)=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(L\otimes_{R}U)\,.

It induces a pairing on Grothendieck groups

\langle-,-\rangle\colon\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\,.

We write
\overline{\operatorname{G}_{0}}(R)
for
\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)
modulo the subgroup of those classes
\alpha
in
\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)
such that
\langle-,\alpha\rangle=0
on
\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
. This is the Grothendieck group of
R

modulo numerical equivalence
. Likewise we write
\overline{\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)
for the quotient of
\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
by classes
\beta
for which
\langle\beta,-\rangle=0
. Abusing notation a bit we write

\langle-,-\rangle\colon\overline{\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\overline{\operatorname{G}_{0}}(R)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}

also for the induced pairing.

For an abelian group
A
we set
A_{\mathbb{R}}=A\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
. In the sequel it will be convenient to work with the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector spaces

{\mathbb{E}}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\overline{\operatorname{G}_{0}}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\mathbb{K}}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\overline{\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\,.

One has an induced pairing of
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector spaces:

\langle-,-\rangle\colon{\mathbb{K}}(R)\otimes_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{E}}(R)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\,.

Each of the induced maps

{\mathbb{E}}(R)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}}({\mathbb{K}}(R),{\mathbb{R}})\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\mathbb{K}}(R)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}}({\mathbb{E}}(R),{\mathbb{R}})

is injective by construction. In particular, the pairing is perfect when the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
is finite dimensional. Kurano
[
22
, Theorem 3.1]
has proved that
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
is finite dimensional whenever
R
contains
{\mathbb{Q}}
as a subring, or is essentially of finite type over a field,
{\mathbb{Z}}
, or a complete discrete valuation ring. This applies in particular to the rings we consider in Section
6
onwards.

In what follows, the following set of primes in
R
will play a crucial role:

\Lambda(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid\dim(R/\mathfrak{p})=\dim R\}\,.

The assignment
[M]\mapsto(\ell_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda(R)}
induces an
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear map

(4.2)

{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda(R)}

This map is onto since it sends the collection
\{[R/\mathfrak{p}]\}_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda(R)}
to a basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda(R)}
. Moreover the kernel is generated by classes of the form
[R/\mathfrak{p}]
with
\dim(R/\mathfrak{p})<d
.

The following result is implicit in the proof of
[
22
, Proposition 3.7]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear map
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda(R)}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, the composition of the map (
4.2
) with
\gamma
coincides with
\langle\alpha,-\rangle
for some class
\alpha
in
{\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{l}
be the element in
\Lambda(R)
. Pick elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{l}
such that
x_{i}\not\in\mathfrak{p}_{i}
and
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}_{j}
for all
j\neq i
. Evidently the element
y_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i}x_{i}
is not in any of the
\mathfrak{p}_{j}
and so we may extend it to a system of parameters
y_{1},\dots,y_{d}
for
R
. For any positive integers
\boldsymbol{s}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}s_{1},...,s_{l}
, the elements

{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\boldsymbol{s}},y_{2},\dots,y_{d}\qquad\text{where ${\boldsymbol{x}}_{\boldsymbol{s}}=\sum_{i}x_{i}^{s_{i}}$,}

also form a system of parameters for
R
, as can be verified by going modulo
\mathfrak{p}_{j}
for each
j
. Let
K_{\boldsymbol{s}}
be the Koszul complex on the sequence above and consider the map

\langle K_{\boldsymbol{s}},-\rangle\colon{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\,.

As with any Koszul complex on a system of parameters, this map kills
[R/\mathfrak{p}]
whenever
\dim(R/p)<d
and hence it factors through the map (
4.2
). Thus it determines a map
{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda(R)}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
given by the tuple of integers

v_{\boldsymbol{s}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\langle K_{\boldsymbol{s}},R/\mathfrak{p}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle K_{\boldsymbol{s}},R/\mathfrak{p}_{l}\rangle)\,.

We verify that the collection of all such tuples
v_{\boldsymbol{s}}
, as the
\boldsymbol{s}
vary over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 1}^{l}
, span
{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
. Note that
v_{1,\dots,1}
is a tuple of strictly positive integers since
\langle K_{1,\dots,1},-\rangle
computes multiplicity with respect to an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Suppose

v_{1,\dots,1}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{l})\qquad\text{with $a_{i}\geq 1$.}

For any
i
the sequence
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\boldsymbol{s}},y_{2},\dots,y_{d}
is
x_{i}^{s_{i}},y_{2},\dots y_{d}
modulo
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, so one has

\langle K_{\boldsymbol{s}},R/\mathfrak{p}_{i}\rangle=s_{i}\langle K_{1,\dots,1},R/\mathfrak{p}_{i}\rangle\,.

Therefore
v_{\boldsymbol{s}}=(s_{1}a_{1},\dots,s_{d}a_{d})
. The result now follows easily.
∎

Here is one application of the preceding result.

Corollary 4.4
.

The map (
4.2
) factors through
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
.

Proof.

If
\beta\in{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is numerically equivalent to
0
then Proposition
4.3
proves that
\beta
goes to zero under the composition of (
4.2
) with any map
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda(R)}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and hence it maps to
0
under (
4.2
) itself.
∎

The next application concerns the multiplicity
e_{d}(-)
from
2.1
.

Corollary 4.5
.

The map
e_{d}(-)\colon{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
coincides with
\langle\alpha,-\rangle
for some class
\alpha
in
{\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. In particular it induces a map of
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector spaces

e_{d}(-)\colon{\mathbb{E}}(R)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\,.

Proof.

When the residue field of

is infinite, the Koszul complex on a minimal generating set for a minimal reduction of

does the job; see (

). In general, the local expression of the multiplicity
[

means that

factors through the map (

implies that

factors through

.
∎

These observations and constructions allow one to extend the proof of Lemma
3.5
to deduce the result below.

Theorem 4.6
.

If
[R]
is a positive
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear combination in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
of classes of Ulrich modules, then (
3.1
) holds for all short complexes in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
. ∎

Here are some simple examples where the hypothesis of the preceding result hold. The existence of Ulrich modules in these situation was recorded already in
[
8
]
; our only contribution is to observe that they generate the class of the ring in the Gronthendieck group.

Example 4.7
.

The hypothesis of Theorem
4.6
holds when
\dim R\leq 1
, and also when
R
is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of minimal multiplicity.

Indeed if
\dim R=0
the residue field

is evidently an Ulrich module and
[R]=\ell(R)\cdot[k]
in
\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)
.

Suppose

and set

. This is a one-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring, with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathfrak{m}S

, and hence also for

\langle-,k\rangle=0

. It follows that
[\mathfrak{n}^{e(R)-1}]=[S]
in

. It remains to note that
[\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}R]=0

, as desired.

Let

be Cohen-Macaulay of minimal multiplicity; by the discussion above, we can assume

. The

Since
\langle-,k\rangle=0
on

in

since the rank of

is nonzero.

4.8
.

The hypothesis of Theorem
4.6
holds also when

is a strict complete intersection, for in that case

has a finite filtration whose subquotients are Ulrich modules; this fact was key to proving Theorem

. This raises the question whether for any complete intersection ring the class of Ulrich modules spans

in

. However, it is not even known that such an

has an Ulrich module.

If

is a complete intersection of even dimension with an isolated singularity. It has been conjectured by Dao and Kurano

that

is one dimensional as a real vector space. If this conjecture holds, then by Theorem

, the existence of a single (nonzero) Ulrich module

-complex in

satisfies (

).

4.9
.

We topologize the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
by giving it the weakest topology for which every
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear map
{\mathbb{E}}(R)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous, where
{\mathbb{R}}
has the Euclidean topology. This is the usual Euclidean topology when
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
is finite dimensional. We topologize
{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
in the same way; with these topologies the quotient map
{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to{\mathbb{E}}(R)
is continuous.

If
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is a convergent sequence in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
(or in
{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
) with limit
\alpha
, then

\langle F,\alpha\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle F,\alpha_{n}\rangle\qquad\text{for $F$ in $\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)$.}

Our interest is in sequences with
\alpha_{n}=[M_{n}]/\nu_{R}(M_{n})
, where the
M_{n}
are nonzero finitely generated
R
-modules.

4.10
.

Let
R
be a local ring of positive characteristic
p
, with perfect residue field, and such that the Frobenius endomorphism

\varphi\colon R\longrightarrow R

is finite. We write
\varphi_{*}
for restriction of scalars along
\varphi
; the notation is in line with the one from algebraic geometry. Viewed as a functor on the category of
R
-modules,
\varphi_{*}
has as left adjoint the base change functor

\varphi^{*}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R^{\varphi}\otimes_{R}M\,,

where
R^{\varphi}
denotes
R
viewed as an
R
-
R
bimodule with the canonical left action and right action via
\varphi
.

Since
\varphi
is finite
\varphi_{*}
induces a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linear map on
\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)
; we denote this also
\varphi_{*}
. On the other hand,
\varphi^{*}
induces a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linear map on
{\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
, also denoted
\varphi^{*}
. By the projection formula, for
\alpha\in\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
and
\beta\in\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)
one has

\langle\varphi^{*}(\alpha),\beta\rangle=\langle\alpha,\varphi_{*}(\beta)\rangle\,.

Given this adjunction it is clear that
\varphi^{*}
and
\varphi_{*}
induce maps

\varphi^{*}\colon{\mathbb{K}}(R)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{K}}(R)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi_{*}\colon{\mathbb{E}}(R)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{E}}(R)\,,

and that these are adjoint to each other in the sense above.

4.11
.

We remain in the framework of
4.10
. For each integer
i\geq 0
set

{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{(i)}=\{\alpha\in{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mid\varphi_{*}(\alpha)=p^{i}\alpha\}

the eigenspace of
\varphi_{*}
with eigenvalue
p^{i}
. The result below is implicit in
[
21
, §2]
; see also
[
29
, Lemma 4]
. The subset
\Lambda(R)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
is as in (
4.2
).

Lemma 4.12
.

For
R
as in
4.10
there is an internal direct sum decomposition

{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{(i)}\,.

Moreover the assignment
[M]\mapsto(\ell_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda(R)}
induces an isomorphism

{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{(d)}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }{\mathbb{R}}^{\#\Lambda(R)}\,.

We write
[M]_{i}
for the image of
[M]
in
{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{(i)}
. We will encounter modules
M
that satisfy the following condition: There exists a
nonzero
integer
m
such that

(4.13)

\ell_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=m\cdot\ell(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda(R)
.

In this case
\dim M=\dim R
and
e_{d}(M)=m\cdot e(R)
; see
[
9
, Corollary 4.7.8]
. Examples include modules
M
of nonzero finite rank. When (
4.13
) holds one has

(4.14)

[M]_{d}=\frac{e_{d}(M)}{e(R)}[R]_{d}\,.

The result below will be used in the proof of Theorem
7.1
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
R
be as in
4.10
and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. If
e_{d}(M)\neq 0
, then in
{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\varphi_{*}^{n}(M)}{e_{d}(\varphi_{*}^{n}(M))}=\frac{[M]_{d}}{e_{d}(M)}\,.

When in addition
M
satisfies (
4.13
) the limit above equals
[R]_{d}/e(R)
.

Proof.

One has
e_{d}(\varphi_{*}^{n}(M))=p^{nd}e_{d}(M)
, since
e_{d}(-)
factors through
{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{(d)}
; see the proof of Corollary
4.5
. This fact and Lemma
4.12
yield the first equality below:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\varphi_{*}^{n}(M)}{e_{d}(\varphi_{*}^{n}(M))}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{[M]_{0}+\cdots+p^{nd}[M]_{d}}{p^{nd}e_{d}(M)}=\frac{[M]_{d}}{e_{d}(M)}\,.

For the second one, note that for any
\alpha
in
{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
and sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of nonzero integers converging to
\infty
, the sequence
(\alpha/a_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
converges to
0
in
{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

The last part of the statement is now immediate from (
4.14
).
∎

4.16
.

As noted earlier, the endomorphism
\varphi_{*}
on
{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
descends to
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
. Thus the decomposition in Lemma
4.12
induces an analogous decomposition on
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
. It follows from Corollary
4.4
that the isomorphism in
op. cit.
induces isomorphisms

{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{(d)}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }{{\mathbb{E}}(R)}^{(d)}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }{\mathbb{R}}^{\#\Lambda(R)}\,.

Another remark: While we have introduced these decompositions only in the context of
4.10
, they exist in great generality, covering also rings not necessarily of positive characteristic, using the Riemann-Roch isomorphism; see
[
12
]
.

4.17
.

Let
R
be a local ring of positive characteristic
p
. The
Dutta multiplicity
of a complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is

\chi_{\infty}(F)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\chi((\varphi^{*})^{n}(F))}{p^{nd}}\,.

When
R\to S
is flat map with
\mathfrak{m}S
the maximal ideal of
S
, then it is easy to verify that
(\varphi^{*})^{n}(S\otimes_{R}F)\cong S\otimes_{R}(\varphi^{*})^{n}(F)
, and it follows that
\chi_{\infty}(F)=\chi_{\infty}(S\otimes_{R}F)
. Given this remark and
2.7
, we can assume that
R
is complete and
k
is algebraically closed when computing Dutta multiplicities. This puts us in the context of
4.10
, and in this case the following expression for the Dutta multiplicity is well-known
[
32
, §5]
, and can be deduced easily from Lemma
4.15
.

(4.18)

\chi_{\infty}(F)=\langle F,[R]_{d}\rangle\,.

Since
\chi(F)=\langle F,[R]\rangle
the Dutta multiplicity is the Euler characteristic of
F
whenever
[R]=[R]_{d}
in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
. This is the case, for example, when
R
is a complete intersection ring, and more generally when
R
is numerically Roberts; see
[
22
, §6]
.

5.
Lim Cohen-Macaulay and lim Ulrich sequences

This section concerns the notion of a lim Ulrich sequence of modules introduced in
[
24
]
. Building on this, we introduce a notion of lim Ulrich point in the Grothendieck group modulo numerical equivalence. Our interest in such points is explained by Theorem
5.16
that derives bounds on intersection multiplicities and Betti numbers from the existence of such points.

Throughout
R
will be a local ring and we set
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim R
.

5.1
.

We say that a sequence
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of finitely generated
R
-modules is a
lim Cohen-Macaulay sequence
if each
U_{n}
is nonzero, and for any short complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
one has

(5.2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{i}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n}))}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=0\qquad\text{for $i\geq 1$.}

The definition is from
[
6
,
16
,
24
]
, with the caveat that in these sources the complexes
F
are restricted to be Koszul complexes on systems of parameters. These are equivalent notions. In fact, in checking whether a given sequence of modules is lim Cohen-Macaulay it suffices to test that condition (
5.2
) holds when
F
is a Koszul complex on a
single
system of parameters. This result is due to Bhatt, Hochster, and Ma
[
6
,
16
]
, and appears as Lemma
5.7
below.

The observation below will be useful in the proof of Lemma
5.7
and in many of the later arguments.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(W(n))_{n\geqslant 0}
be a sequence of
R
-complexes,
(v_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
a sequence of positive integers, and
s
an integer such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(W(n))}{v_{n}}=0\qquad\text{for all $i\neq s$.}

Then for any finite free complex
P
and integer
i
the following equality holds:

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(P\otimes_{R}W(n))}{v_{n}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i-s}(P\otimes_{R}\operatorname{H}_{s}(W(n)))}{v_{n}}\,.

The corresponding equality involving
\liminf
also holds.

In what follows it will be expedient to use the shorthand

(5.4)

h_{i}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(X)

for any
R
-complex
X
and integer
i
.

Proof of Lemma
5.3
.

The argument is a reduction to the following special case: If the limit of the sequence
(h_{i}(W(n))/v_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is
0
for each
i
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(P\otimes_{R}W(n))}{v_{n}}=0\quad\text{for all $i$.}

This can be verified by a simple induction on the number of nonzero terms in
P
.

Set
W^{\prime}(n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\tau_{\geqslant s}W(n)
, the good truncation of
W(n)
below
s
; see
[
35
, 1.2.7]
. Consider the induced exact sequence of complexes

(5.5)

0\longrightarrow W^{\prime}(n)\longrightarrow W(n)\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }W^{\prime\prime}(n)\longrightarrow 0\,.

By construction
\operatorname{H}_{i}(\pi)
is an isomorphism in degrees
i\leq s-1
and
\operatorname{H}_{i}(W^{\prime\prime}(n))=0
for
i\geq s
. Thus hypotheses yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(W^{\prime\prime}(n))}{v_{n}}=0\quad\text{for each $i$.}

Then the already establish part of the result implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(P\otimes_{R}W^{\prime\prime}(n))}{v_{n}}=0\quad\text{for each $i$.}

Tensoring (
5.5
) with
P
and taking homology yields for each
i
an exact sequence

\operatorname{H}_{i+1}(P\otimes_{R}W^{\prime\prime}(n))\to\operatorname{H}_{i}(P\otimes_{R}W^{\prime}(n))\to\operatorname{H}_{i}(P\otimes_{R}W(n))\to\operatorname{H}_{i}(P\otimes_{R}W^{\prime\prime}(n))

It follows from these computations that

(5.6)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(P\otimes_{R}W^{\prime}(n))}{v_{n}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(P\otimes_{R}W(n))}{v_{n}}

for each integer
i
. Observe also that by construction of (
5.5
) one has

\operatorname{H}_{i}(W^{\prime}(n))\cong\begin{cases}0&\text{for $i<s$}\\
\operatorname{H}_{i}(W(n))&\text{for $i\geq s$.}\end{cases}

Given this information and the equality (
5.6
) we can replace
W(n)
by
W^{\prime}(n)
and assume the
W(n)_{i}=0
for
i<s
and all
n
. Then consider the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow V(n)\longrightarrow W(n)\longrightarrow\mathsf{\Sigma}^{s}\operatorname{H}_{s}(W(n))\longrightarrow 0

where the map on the right is the canonical surjection and
V(n)
is its kernel. Thus

\operatorname{H}_{i}(V(n))\cong\begin{cases}0&\text{for $i=s$}\\
\operatorname{H}_{i}(W(n))&\text{for $i\neq s$.}\end{cases}

The hypotheses implies that the limit of the sequence
(h_{i}(V(n))/v_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is
0
for each
i
. Then the already establish part of the result implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(P\otimes_{R}V(n))}{v_{n}}=0\quad\text{for each $i$.}

Given this one can argue as before to deduce the first equality below.

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(P\otimes_{R}W(n))}{v_{n}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(P\otimes_{R}\mathsf{\Sigma}^{s}\operatorname{H}_{s}(W(n)))}{v_{n}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i-s}(P\otimes_{R}\operatorname{H}_{s}(W(n)))}{v_{n}}

This is the desired equality involving
\limsup
; the one for
\liminf
can be verified in exactly the same way.
∎

Here is the promised result about lim Cohen-Macaulay sequences, from
[
6
]
. We give a proof for the benefit of the first and third authors.

Lemma 5.7
.

A sequence
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of finitely generated
R
-modules is lim Cohen-Macaulay if (
5.2
) holds for the Koszul complex on a single system of parameters for
R
.

Proof.

Suppose (
5.2
) holds for
K(\boldsymbol{r};R)
, the Koszul complex on a system of parameters
\boldsymbol{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}r_{1},\dots,r_{d}
for
R
. We first verify that it also holds
K(r_{1}^{a_{1}},\dots,r_{d}^{a_{d}};R)
for any integers
a_{i}\geq 1
. To this end it suffices to verify that if (
5.2
) holds for
K(r_{1}^{a},\boldsymbol{r}_{\geqslant 2};R)
for some
a\geq 1
, then it also holds for
K(r_{1}^{a+1},\boldsymbol{r}_{\geqslant 2};R)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
denote the derived category of
R
. The composition of maps
R\xrightarrow{r_{1}^{a}}R\xrightarrow{r_{1}}R
induces an exact triangle

K(r_{1}^{a};R)\longrightarrow K(r_{1}^{a+1};R)\longrightarrow K(r_{1};R)

in
\mathcal{D}
, and hence an exact triangle

K(r_{1}^{a},\boldsymbol{r}_{\geqslant 2};U_{n})\longrightarrow K(r_{1}^{a+1},\boldsymbol{r}_{\geqslant 2};U_{n})\longrightarrow K(\boldsymbol{r};U_{n})\,.

Thus for each integer
i
there is an inequality

\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(r_{1}^{a+1},\boldsymbol{r}_{\geqslant 2};U_{n})\leq\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(r_{1}^{a},\boldsymbol{r}_{\geqslant 2};U_{n})+\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{r};U_{n})\,.

The desired result follows.

Let
F
be an
R
-complex with
\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}(F)
finite. In particular the kernel of the natural map
R\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F,F)
is primary to the maximal ideal of
R
. By the discussion in the previous paragraph one can find a system of parameters
\boldsymbol{r}
in this kernel with the property that (
5.2
) holds for
K\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K(\boldsymbol{r})
.

Since
\boldsymbol{r}
annihilates
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F,F)
the natural map
K\otimes F\to\mathsf{\Sigma}^{d}F
of
R
-complexes has a section in
\mathcal{D}
. This fact does not require
\boldsymbol{r}
to be a system of parameters, only that it annihilates
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F,F)
, and can be verified by an induction on the length of the sequence
\boldsymbol{r}
. The case of length one is immediate from the exact triangle

F\xrightarrow{\ r_{1}\ }F\longrightarrow K(r_{1};F)\longrightarrow\mathsf{\Sigma}F

for since the map on the left is zero in
\mathcal{D}
, the triangle above splits, that is to say, the map on the right has a section. Replacing
F
by
K(r_{1};F)
and using induction yields the desired statement. In this step one has to use the fact that
\boldsymbol{r}
annihilates
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(K(r_{1};F),K(r_{1};F))
; see
[
1
, §3]
, or
[
5
, 1.5.3]
.

From the preceding discussion we deduce that
\operatorname{H}_{j}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n})
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{H}_{j+d}(F\otimes_{R}K\otimes_{R}U_{n})
for each integer
i
. It thus suffices to verify that for any short complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
and
i\geq d+1
one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(F\otimes_{R}K\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=0\,.

Here we are using the shorthand introduced in (
5.4
). As
K
satisfies (
5.2
), Lemma
5.3
applied with
W(n)=K\otimes_{R}U_{n}
and
P=F
yields

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(F\otimes_{R}K\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(F\otimes_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(K\otimes_{R}U_{n}))}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

for each
i
. It remains to note that when
i\geq d+1
the terms of the limit on the right hand side are all zero since
F
is a short complex.
∎

Here is a simple consequence of the preceding result. The proof is straightforward so it is omitted; see
[
24
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Corollary 5.8
.

If
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
be a lim Cohen-Macaulay sequence of
R
-modules and
R\to S
is an flat local map with artinian closed fiber, then the sequence of
S
-modules
(S\otimes_{R}U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Cohen-Macaulay. ∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
be a lim Cohen-Macaulay sequence. One has inequality

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{e_{d}(U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}\geq 1\,.

Proof.

Given Corollary
5.8
and the fact that both
e_{d}(-)
and
\nu_{R}(-)
are unchanged when we extend the residue field, we can assume that the residue field of
R
is infinite. Let
K
be the Koszul complex on a minimal generating set for a minimal reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
. For each integer
n
one has

\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(K\otimes_{R}U_{n})\geq\nu_{R}(U_{n})\,.

This justifies the inequality below; the first equality is by (
2.3
):

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{e_{d}(U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\chi(K\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(K\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}\geq 1\,.

The second equality is by the lim Cohen-Macaulay condition.
∎

The preceding result motivates the definition below from
[
24
]
.

5.10
.

A sequence
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of
R
-modules is a
lim Ulrich sequence
if it is lim Cohen-Macaulay and satisfies

(5.11)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{e_{d}(U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=1\,.

This bundles two conditions in one: the limit should exist and it should be
1
.

If a nonzero
R
-module
U
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay, respectively, Ulrich, then the sequence with
U_{n}=U
for all
n
is lim Cohen-Macaulay, respectively, lim Ulrich. In particular lim Ulrich sequences exist when
\dim R\leq 1
; see Example
4.7
. Moreover if
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is a lim Ulrich sequence of
R
-modules and
(R,\mathfrak{m})\to(S,\mathfrak{n})
is a flat local map with
\mathfrak{m}S=\mathfrak{n}
, then the sequence of
S
-modules
(S\otimes_{R}U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Ulrich. This is immediate from Corollary
5.8
and
2.7
.

5.12
.

An element of
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
is a
lim Ulrich point
if it is in the strictly positive real cone spanned by points of the form

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{[U_{n}]}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}\,,

where the sequence
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Ulrich and the limit exists. A
lim Cohen-Macaulay point
has the obvious meaning.

For later use we record the observation that if
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
has a lim Cohen-Macaulay point and
R\to S
is a flat map with artinian closed fiber, then
{\mathbb{E}}(S)
has a lim Cohen-Macaulay point; this is immediate from Corollary
5.8
. The same holds for lim Ulrich points if also
\mathfrak{m}S=\mathfrak{n}
, where
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}
are the maximal ideals of
R
and
S
, respectively.

If
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is a lim Cohen-Macaulay sequence and the sequence
([U_{n}]/\nu_{R}(U_{n}))
converges in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
, say to a point
\alpha
, then

(5.13)

\langle F,\alpha\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\chi(F,U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

for any short complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
. The first equality holds because of the convergence, whereas the second one is a consequence of (
5.2
). It will also be useful to note that if the sequence
(U_{n})
is lim Ulrich then

(5.14)

e_{d}(\alpha)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{e_{d}(U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=1\,.

The second equality is from (
5.11
).

By the discussion above if
\alpha
is a lim Cohen-Macaulay point in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
then
\langle F,\alpha\rangle>0
for any short complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
with
\operatorname{H}(F)
nonzero; in particular
0
cannot be a lim Cohen-Macaulay point.

Lemma 5.15
.

If
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is a lim Cohen-Macaulay sequence, then for any short complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
one has

\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\langle F,U_{n}\rangle}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\langle F,U_{n}\rangle}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}\geq\nu_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\,.

Thus if
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
is finite dimensional
(U_{n}/\nu_{R}(U_{n}))
has convergent subsequence.

Proof.

Since
F
satisfies (
5.2
) one gets an equality

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\langle F,U_{n}\rangle}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{0}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}\,,

and similarly for the lim inf. It remains to observe that there are inequalities

h_{0}(F)\nu_{R}(U_{n})\geq h_{0}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n})\geq\nu_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{0}(F))\nu_{R}(U_{n})\,.

These hold because
\operatorname{H}_{0}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n})=\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\otimes_{R}U_{n}
, and for any
R
-modules
H,U
there are surjections

H\otimes_{R}R^{\nu_{R}(U)}\twoheadrightarrow H\otimes_{R}U\twoheadrightarrow(H/\mathfrak{m}H)\otimes_{k}(U/\mathfrak{m}U)\,.

This justifies the stated inequalities.

When
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
is finite dimensional, its topology is the Euclidean one, and it is dual to
{\mathbb{K}}(R)
. Since the classes of the complexes
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
span
{\mathbb{K}}(R)
, the already established part of the result means that
(U_{n}/\nu_{R}(U_{n}))
is bounded in the Euclidean metric. Then the stated assertion is now clear.
∎

The proof of the next result follows that of Proposition
3.7
.

Theorem 5.16
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring. If
\alpha
is a lim Ulrich point in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
, then for any short complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
and integer
i
there is an inequality

\binom{d}{i}\langle F,\alpha\rangle\geq\beta^{R}_{i}(F)e_{d}(\alpha)\qquad\text{where $d=\dim R$.}

In particular
2^{d}\langle F,\alpha\rangle\geq\beta_{R}(F)e_{d}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

By definition,
\alpha
is a positive linear combination of elements of the form

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{[U_{n}]}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

with
(U_{n})
a lim Ulrich sequence. Since both
\langle F,-\rangle
and
e_{d}(-)
are additive on
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
we can suppose that
\alpha
is an element of the form above. Thus by (
5.11
) the desired result is that

\binom{d}{i}\langle F,\alpha\rangle\geq\beta^{R}_{i}(F)\,.

Moreover, by the discussion in
5.10
, expanding
k
if necessary, we can pick a system of parameters
\boldsymbol{r}
that generates a minimal reduction for
\mathfrak{m}
. Let
K
be the Koszul complex on
\boldsymbol{r}
so that
e_{d}(M)=\chi(K\otimes_{R}M)
for any finitely generated
R
-module
M
; see (
2.3
). We estimate the limiting behavior of the sequence

\frac{h_{i}(K\otimes_{R}F\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

in two ways. These estimates are obtained by repeated application of Lemma
5.3
with
v_{n}=\nu_{R}(U_{n})
and
s=0
, and different choices of
W(n)
and
P
.

For one thing, since
(U_{n})
is a lim Cohen-Macaulay sequence we can apply it with
W(n)=F\otimes_{R}U_{n}
to get the first equality below:

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(K\otimes_{R}F\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(K\otimes_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n}))}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\binom{d}{i}\frac{h_{0}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

\displaystyle=\binom{d}{i}\langle F,\alpha\rangle\,.

The inequality holds because
K
is a finite free complex, of rank
\binom{d}{i}
in degree
i
. The second equality is by (
5.13
).

On the other hand noting that
K\otimes_{R}F\otimes_{R}U_{n}\cong F\otimes_{R}K\otimes_{R}U_{n}
as
R
-complexes and reversing the roles of
F
and
K
in the first step of the argument above yields

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(K\otimes_{R}F\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(F\otimes_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(K\otimes_{R}U_{n}))}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n}/\boldsymbol{r}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

Consider the exact sequences

(5.17)

0\longrightarrow\frac{\mathfrak{m}U_{n}}{\boldsymbol{r}U_{n}}\longrightarrow\frac{U_{n}}{\boldsymbol{r}U_{n}}\longrightarrow\frac{U_{n}}{\mathfrak{m}U_{n}}\longrightarrow 0

From this sequence and (
5.11
) it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{R}(\mathfrak{m}U_{n}/\boldsymbol{r}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=0\,.

Therefore Lemma
5.3
, now applied with
W(n)=\mathfrak{m}U_{n}/\boldsymbol{r}U_{n}
and
P\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}F
yields

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(F\otimes_{R}(\mathfrak{m}U_{n}/\boldsymbol{r}U_{n}))}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=0

for all
i
. Feeding this information back into the exact sequence (
5.17
) gives for each integer
i
the first equality below

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n}/\boldsymbol{r}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n}/\mathfrak{m}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(\operatorname{H}(F\otimes_{R}k)\otimes_{k}{U_{n}/\mathfrak{m}U_{n}})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}

\displaystyle=\beta^{R}_{i}(F)

In summary one gets that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(K\otimes_{R}F\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=\beta^{R}_{i}(F)\,.

Combining this with the upper bound
\binom{d}{i}\langle F,\alpha\rangle
for the limit obtained earlier yields the desired inequality.
∎

The corollary below, which extends Theorem
4.6
, is our main motivation for considering lim Ulrich points.

Corollary 5.18
.

Let
F
be a short complex in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
. If
[R]
is a lim Ulrich point in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
, then the inequalities in (
3.1
) hold for
\chi(F)
.

When the ring
R
is as in
4.10
and the class
[R]_{d}
in
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
is a lim Ulrich point, then the analogues of (
3.1
) for
\chi_{\infty}(F)
hold.

Proof.

These assertions are immediate from Theorem
5.16
, once we observe that
\chi(F)=\langle F,[R]\rangle
; for the second we need that
\chi_{\infty}(F)=\langle F,[R]_{d}\rangle
from (
4.18
).
∎

Even if the class of
R
is not a lim Ulrich point, just the existence of lim Ulrich points yields interesting estimates on Euler characteristics of short complexes.

Corollary 5.19
.

When
{\mathbb{E}}(R)
has a lim Ulrich point, for any short complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
and integer
i
there is an inequality

\binom{d}{i}\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\geq\beta^{R}_{i}(F)\qquad\text{where $d=\dim R$.}

In particular
2^{d}\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\geq\beta^{R}(F)
.

Proof.

This is immediate from Lemma
5.15
and Theorem
5.16
, given (
5.14
).
∎

Combining the inequality in the result above with (
3.2
), when it applies, yields

2^{d}\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\geq\beta^{R}(F)\geq 2^{d}\frac{|\chi_{R}(F)|}{\ell_{R}\operatorname{H}(F)}\,.

When
F
is a free resolution of a nonzero finite length
R
-module
M
, this reads

2^{d}\ell_{R}(M)\geq\beta^{R}(F)\geq 2^{d}\,.

We record another application of Lemma
5.3
, which recovers
[
24
, Lemma 2.9]
, and leads to the material presented in the next section. While the hypothesis is rather stringent, in the graded context one can run the argument given below degreewise yielding a much stronger result. This is the essential idea in the proof of Theorem
6.7
, which is why we bring this up.

Lemma 5.20
.

If
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is a sequence of nonzero
R
-modules such that for each integer
i\leq d-1
one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{R}\mathrm{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(U_{n})}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=0

then the sequence
(U_{n})
is lim Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

For each
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
one has a quasi-isomorphism

F\otimes_{R}U_{n}\simeq F\otimes_{R}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(U_{n})\,.

Thus applying Lemma
5.3
with
W_{n}=\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(U_{n})
and
v_{n}=\nu_{R}(U_{n})
yields for any integer
i
an equality

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(F\otimes_{R}U_{n})}{v_{n}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i+d}(F\otimes_{R}\mathrm{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}(U_{n}))}{v_{n}}\,.

When
F
is a short complex the homology modules on the right are zero whenever
i\geq 1
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.21
.

Let
k
be a field and
A
a
standard graded

k
-algebra, that is to say,
A_{0}=k
and
A
is finitely generated as a
k
-algebra by its component
A_{1}
of degree one. Let
\mathfrak{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A_{\geqslant 1}
be the homogenous maximal ideal of
A
. Set
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A_{\mathfrak{m}}
; this is a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}R
, and residue field
k
. Set
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim R=\dim A
.

Given a finitely generated graded
A
-module
M
, we write
e_{d}(M)
for the multiplicity with respect to the
\mathfrak{m}
. It can be computed as so:

e_{d}(M)=(d-1)!\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}(M_{n})}{n^{d-1}}\,.

In this context
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(A)
will be assumed to consist of graded free modules, with differentials respective the grading, and homology of finite rank over
k
. A short complex is to have length
d
. A sequence
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of finitely generated graded
A
-module is lim Cohen-Macaulay if (
5.2
) holds for each short complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(A)
; the sequence is lim Ulrich when in addition (
5.11
) holds.

Proposition 5.22
.

Let
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
be a sequence of finitely generated graded
A
-modules.

(1)

If (
5.2
) holds for the Koszul complex on a single homogenous system of parameters for
A
, then the given sequence is lim Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

The given sequence is lim Cohen-Macaulay if and only if the sequence of
R
-modules
((U_{n})_{\mathfrak{m}})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Cohen-Macaulay.

(3)

The given sequence is lim Ulrich if and only if the sequence of
R
-modules
((U_{n})_{\mathfrak{m}})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Ulrich.

Proof.

For any finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
one has equalities

\nu_{A}(M)=\nu_{R}(M_{\mathfrak{m}})\qquad\text{and}\qquad e_{d}(M)=e_{d}(M_{\mathfrak{m}})\,.

These facts will be used in the argument without further comment.

(2) Let
F
be a short complex in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(A)
. The
A
-modules
\operatorname{H}_{i}(F\otimes_{A}U_{n})
are
\mathfrak{m}
-torsion, so the natural localization map is an isomorphism:

(5.23)

\operatorname{H}_{i}(F\otimes_{A}U_{n})\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\operatorname{H}_{i}(F\otimes_{A}U_{n})_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong\operatorname{H}_{i}(F_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{R}(U_{n})_{\mathfrak{m}})\,.

In particular the localization map
\operatorname{H}_{i}(F)\to\operatorname{H}_{i}(F_{\mathfrak{m}})
is an isomorphism, so that
F_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a short complex in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
. It follows that if the sequence of
R
-modules
((U_{n})_{\mathfrak{m}})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Cohen-Macaulay, then so is the sequence of
A
-modules
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
. This settles the “if” part in (2).

Let
\boldsymbol{a}
be a homogenous system of parameters for
A
; their images in
R
, which we also denote
\boldsymbol{a}
, are a system of parameters for
R
.
Given this fact and the isomorphisms (
5.23
), it follows that when (
5.2
) holds for
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
and the Koszul complex
K(\boldsymbol{a};A)
, then (
5.2
) holds for
((U_{n})_{\mathfrak{m}})_{n\geqslant 0}
and the Koszul complex
K(\boldsymbol{a};R)
. Then Lemma
5.7
implies that the sequence
((U_{n})_{\mathfrak{m}})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Cohen-Macaulay. This settles the “only if” part of (2) and, given the “if” part, also (1).

(3) is now clear, given the equalities above.
∎

6.
Lim Ulrich sequences of sheaves

Throughout this section
k
is an infinite field,
A
a standard graded
k
-algebra; see
5.21
. Set
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim A
; we assume
d\geq 2
to avoid unimportant special cases. Set

X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Proj}(A)\qquad\text{and}\qquad c\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim X=d-1\,.

In this section we introduce lim Ulrich sequences of sheaves on
X
and relate these to lim Ulrich sequences of
A
-modules. This is used to recover a construction, due to the second author, of lim Ulrich sequences on
A
, when
k
has positive characteristic; see Theorem
7.15
. We begin by recalling some basic facts about sheaves on
X
.

6.1
.

For any coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
we set

\varGamma_{*}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\oplus_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t))

viewed as a graded
A
-module in the usual way. We shall be interested in coherent sheaves
{\mathcal{F}}
that satisfy the following condition:

(6.2)

\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t))=0\qquad\text{for $t\ll 0$}.

This is equivalent to the condition that
\varGamma_{*}({\mathcal{F}})
is finitely generated as an
A
-module. The following alternative characterization that will be useful in the sequel: When
\dim X\geq 1
, a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
satisfies (
6.2
) if and only if

0pt_{{\mathcal{O}}_{x}}{\mathcal{F}}_{x}\geq 1\quad\text{for all closed points $x\in X$,}

see
[
14
, Corollaire 1.4]
. In the remainder of this section
{\mathcal{F}}
will be a sheaf satisfying condition (
6.2
). Its
Euler characteristic
is

\chi({\mathcal{F}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}h^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}})\qquad\text{where}\qquad h^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}})\,.

The
Hilbert polynomial
of
{\mathcal{F}}
is a polynomial
h_{{\mathcal{F}}}(z)
with rational coefficients with

h_{{\mathcal{F}}}(t)=\chi({\mathcal{F}}(t))\quad\text{for all $t\in{\mathbb{Z}}$.}

This is a polynomial of degree at most
c
and the coefficient in degree
c
is
e_{d}(\varGamma_{*}({\mathcal{F}}))/c!
. Thus the
c
th difference of
h_{{\mathcal{F}}}(t)
is
e_{d}(\varGamma_{*}({\mathcal{F}}))
so that

e_{d}(\varGamma_{*}({\mathcal{F}}))=\sum_{0\leqslant i,j\leqslant c}(-1)^{i+j}{\binom{c}{j}}h^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t-j))\,.

In particular since the righthand side is independent of
t
we get

(6.3)

e_{d}(\varGamma_{*}({\mathcal{F}}))=\sum_{0\leqslant i,j\leqslant c}(-1)^{i+j}{\binom{c}{j}}h^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(-j))\,.

6.4
.

An
Ulrich sheaf
on
X
is a nonzero coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
such that
h^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t))=0
except possibly when

(6.5)

i=0\text{ and }t\geq 0,\qquad\text{or}\qquad i=c\text{ and }t\leq-c-1\,.

Sometimes it is convenient to view these conditions in terms of the total cohomology

h^{*}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t))\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i}h^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t)),

and as function of
t
, namely:
{\mathcal{F}}
is Ulrich if and only if for each
t
one has

h^{*}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t))=\begin{cases}h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t))&\text{when $t\geq 0$}\\
h^{c}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t))&\text{when $t\leq-c-1$}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

When
X={\mathbb{P}}^{c}_{k}
a sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
is Ulrich if and only if it is isomorphic to a direct sum of copies of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
. For a general
X
, let

\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{c}_{k}

be a finite
linear
projection; the linearity condition is that
\pi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{c}_{k}}(1)={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1)
. Such a projection exists because
k
is infinite. Then a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
is Ulrich precisely when
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{F}}
is an Ulrich sheaf on
{\mathbb{P}}^{c}_{k}
, that is to say, a direct sum of copies of
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{c}_{k}}
. This is a direct consequence of the projection formula

\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t))\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}({\mathbb{P}}^{c}_{k},\pi_{*}{\mathcal{F}}(t))\,.

We propose the following definition.

6.6
.

A
lim Ulrich sequence
of sheaves on
X
is a sequence
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of coherent sheaves on
X
for which the following properties hold:

(1)

h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})\neq 0
for all
n\gg 0
;

(2)

There exists an integer
t_{0}
such that
h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(t))=0
for
t\leq t_{0}
and all
n
;

(3)

There exists an integer
t_{1}
such that
h^{\geqslant 1}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(t))=0
for
t\geq t_{1}
and all
n
;

(4)

Except possibly when
i=0
and
t\geq 0
, or
i=c
and
t\leq-c-1
, one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(t))}{h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=0\,.

The range of values of
i
and
t
arising in (4) is precisely the one from (
6.5
).

The next result explains our interest in this notion; Lemma
5.20
is a precursor.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
k
be an infinite field,
A
a standard graded
k
-algebra, and set
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Proj}(A)
. If
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is a lim Ulrich sequence of sheaves on
X
, then the sequence of graded
A
-modules
(\varGamma_{*}({\mathcal{F}}_{n}))_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Ulrich.

Proof.

Set
U_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\varGamma_{*}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})
, viewed as a graded
A
-module. We write
U_{n,t}
for its component in degree
t
. Condition
6.6
(2) implies that
U_{n,t}=0
for
t\leq t_{0}
. In particular the
A
-module
U_{n}
is finitely generated; see the discussion below (
6.2
).

The lim Ulrich condition on the sequence
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
involves
\nu_{A}(U_{n})
, whereas the lim Ulrich condition on
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is in terms of
h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})
, that is to say,
\operatorname{rank}_{k}(U_{n,0})
. We begin by comparing these numbers: One has

\displaystyle\nu_{A}(U_{n})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rank}_{k}\mathrm{Coker}(A_{1}\otimes_{k}U_{n}\to U_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{rank}_{k}\mathrm{Coker}(A_{1}\otimes_{k}U_{n,-1}\to U_{n,0})

\displaystyle\geq h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})-\operatorname{rank}_{k}(A_{1})h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(-1))\,.

Thus from
6.6
(4) one obtains

(6.8)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{A}(U_{n})}{h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})}\geq 1\,.

This observation will be of use later on. At the end we prove that this limit is
1
.

We verify that the sequence
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Cohen-Macaulay by mimicking the argument from Lemma
5.20
. The advantage will be that since we are in the graded case, we can focus on one (internal) degree at a time. To that end recall that
\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(U_{n})
, the local cohomology of
U_{n}
at the homogeneous maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A_{\geqslant 1}
of
A
, acquires a grading from
U_{n}
. We write
\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(U_{n})_{t}
for the component in degree
t
. This can be computed in terms of the sheaf cohomology of
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
as follows:

\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(U_{n})_{t}\cong\begin{cases}0&i=0,1\\
\operatorname{H}^{i-1}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(t))&i\geq 2\,.\end{cases}

See, for example,
[
19
, Corollary 6.2]
, noting that since
U_{n}=\varGamma_{*}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})
, the sheafification of
U_{n}
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
. Combining this observation with condition
6.6
(3) yields

(6.9)

\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(U_{n})_{t}=0\qquad\text{for $t\geq t_{1}$ and all $i$ and $n$,}\\

whereas
6.6
(4) yields

(6.10)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(U_{n})_{t}}{h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=0\qquad\text{for $i\neq d$ and all $t$.}

At this point we have hypotheses akin to those in Lemma
5.20
. In the rest of the proof it will be convenient to extend the use of notation
5.4
to the graded context: Given a complex
W
of graded
A
-modules, set

h_{i,t}(W)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(W)_{t}\,.

Fix a homogeneous system of parameters
\boldsymbol{a}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}a_{1},\dots,a_{d}
of
A
and let
K
be the Koszul complex on this sequence. For any complex
X
of graded
A
-modules we write
h_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{a};W)
for
h_{i,t}(K\otimes_{A}W)
.

We verify (
5.2
) for
F=K
, which entails
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Cohen-Macaulay; see Lemma
5.7
. As in Lemma
5.20
we use the quasi-isomorphism

K(\boldsymbol{a};U_{n})\simeq K(\boldsymbol{a};\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(U_{n}))\,,

to transfer information we have on local cohomology modules to Koszul homology. For a start, given
U_{n,t}=0
for
t\leq t_{0}
and (
6.9
), for each
n
and
i
one has

(6.11)

h_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{a};U_{n})=0\qquad\text{for $t\leq t_{0}$ and $t\geq t_{1}+|\boldsymbol{a}|$,}

where
|\boldsymbol{a}|\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}|a_{1}|+\dots+|a_{d}|
. This can be proved by a simple spectral sequence argument or through a claim akin to the one below, analogous to Lemma
5.3
. Caveat:
W(n)
is the
n
th term of a sequence and is not to be confused with a twist.

Claim
.

Let
(W(n))_{n\geqslant 0}
be a sequence of
A
-complexes of graded modules,
(v_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
a sequence of nonzero integers, and
s
an integer such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i,t}(W(n))}{v_{n}}=0\qquad\text{for all $i\neq s$ and all $t$.}

Then for all
i
and
t
one has

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{a};W(n))}{v_{n}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i-s,t}(\boldsymbol{a};\operatorname{H}_{s}(W(n)))}{v_{n}}

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
5.3
and is omitted. Given (
6.10
) we can apply the claim with
W(n)=\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(U_{n})
and
v_{n}=h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})
, to obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{a};U_{n})}{h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i+d,t}(\boldsymbol{a};\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}(U_{n}))}{h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=0\quad\text{for $i\geq 1$ and all $t$.}

By (
6.11
) the Koszul homology modules are zero outside the range
t_{0}<t<t_{1}+|\boldsymbol{a}|
, so the preceding computation yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(\boldsymbol{a};U_{n})}{h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=0\qquad\text{for $i\geq 1$.}

Combining this with (
6.8
) we deduce

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{i}(\boldsymbol{a};U_{n})}{\nu_{A}(U_{n})}=0\qquad\text{for $i\geq 1$.}

Thus the sequence
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Cohen-Macaulay as claimed.

To verify that the sequence is lim Ulrich, it remains to verify (
5.11
). Applying (
6.3
) to each
U_{n}
and applying property
6.6
(4) yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{e_{d}(U_{n})}{h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=\sum_{0\leqslant i,j\leqslant c}\lim_{n\to\infty}(-1)^{i+j}{\binom{c}{j}}\frac{h^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(-j))}{h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=1\,.

Recalling (
6.8
) we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{e_{d}(U_{n})}{\nu_{A}(U_{n})}\leq 1\,.

Combining this inequality with (
5.9
) we deduce that the limit above equals
1
, as desired.
This completes the proof.
∎

6.12
.

In the preceding proof we first verified that the sequence
(\varGamma_{*}({\mathcal{F}}_{n}))_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Cohen-Macaulay using only conditions (1)–(3) in
6.6
and the following part of condition (4): For
1\leq i\leq c-1
and each
t
, and for
i=0
and
t=-1
, one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(t))}{h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=0\,.

One might thus call such a sequence
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
a lim Cohen-Macaulay sequence.

7.
Graded rings in positive characteristic

In this section we use Theorem
6.7
to recover and extend results of the second author
[
24
, Theorem 3.5]
on the existence of lim Ulrich sequences over standard graded rings defined over a field of positive characteristic.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
k
be a field of positive characteristic, infinite and perfect, and
R

Cohomology on
({\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{1})^{c}

Let
k
be a field (with no further restrictions),

a positive integer, and consider the
c
-fold product of the projective line:

Z\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}({\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{1})^{c}\,.

We will need to compute the cohomology of certain sheaves on

. To that end we recollect some basic results in this topic; for further details see, for example,
[

.

Let

be the

-graded ring where

and

are of multidegree
(0,\dots,1,\dots 0)
, where

is in the

-module determines a coherent sheaf on

. In particular for any
c
-tuple

on

associated to the

-module

. The functor assigning finitely generated multigraded modules to coherent sheaves on

-module admits a resolution of length at most

by direct sums of twists of

. Thus for each coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{N}}

where each

for various choices of

-tuples

. The cohomology of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(\boldsymbol{a})
is determined by the isomorphism

(7.4)

\operatorname{H}^{*}(Z,{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(\boldsymbol{a}))\cong\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{1}}(a_{1}))\otimes_{k}\cdots\otimes_{k}\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(a_{c}))

of graded
k
-vector spaces and the computation

h^{i}({\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{1}}(a))=\begin{cases}a+1&\text{if $i=0$ and $a\geq 0$}\\
-a-1&\text{if $i=1$ and $a\leq-2$}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Therefore for any integer

one gets

(7.5)

h^{*}({\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(a))=|a+1|\,.

These computations lead in the usual way to the following analog of the Serre vanishing theorem for projective spaces. In what follows given
\boldsymbol{a}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{c})
and

in

to indicate that

for each

\boldsymbol{a}\gg 0
to indicate that each

for each

Lemma 7.6
.

For any coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{N}}
on
Z
one has

\operatorname{H}^{i}(Z,{\mathcal{N}}(\boldsymbol{a}))=0\quad\text{for $\boldsymbol{a}\gg 0$ and $i\geq 1$.}

Proof.

It follows from the computations above that the desired result holds for sheaves of the form
{\mathcal{O}}(\boldsymbol{a})
. The general case follows since any coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{N}}
has a finite resolution by direct sum of sheaves of this form; see Lemma

∎

7.7

The function

\operatorname{Hilb}_{{\mathcal{N}}}(\boldsymbol{a})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}h^{i}(Z,{\mathcal{N}}(\boldsymbol{a}))\qquad\text{for $\boldsymbol{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{c}$,}

which is immediate from (

\operatorname{Hilb}_{{\mathcal{N}}}(\boldsymbol{a})=h^{0}(Z,{\mathcal{N}}(\boldsymbol{a}))\qquad\text{for $\boldsymbol{a}\gg 0$.}

Given an integer

\boldsymbol{b}_{n}(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}((t+1)p^{n},\dots,(t+c)p^{n})\qquad\text{for $n\geq 0$}\,.

Lemma 7.10

Lech’s conjecture revisited

Corollary
7.2
leads us to propose the following:

Conjecture 8.1
.

Let
R
be a complete local ring. If
F
is a short complex in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, then
\chi_{\infty}(F)\geq e(R)
.

We are no longer assuming
R
contains a field of positive characteristic, so we have to recall what Dutta multiplicity is in this generality: The ring
R
, being complete, is a quotient of a regular local ring, so one has the Riemann-Roch isomorphism

\tau\colon{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{\mathbb{Q}}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }A_{*}(R)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}A_{i}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}

where the target is the rational Chow group of
R
. Here
d=\dim R
as usual. The map
\tau
is hard to describe in general, except for the component in degree
d
, for
A_{d}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}
has a basis consisting of classes
[V(R/\mathfrak{p})]
, for
\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda(R)
as in (
4.2
), and

\tau_{d}\colon{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{\#\Lambda(R)}\quad\text{is induced by $M\mapsto(\ell_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda(R)}$.}

Compare with Lemma
4.12
. Writing
[R]_{d}
for inverse image of
\tau_{d}(R)
, the Dutta multiplicity of
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is

\chi_{\infty}(F)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\langle F,[R]_{d}\rangle

where
\langle-,-\rangle
is the usual pairing; see
4.1
. Usually, the Dutta multiplicity is defined using the action of
\operatorname{K}_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
on the Chow group but the form above is better suited for our approach; see
[
20
,
32
,
29
]
for details.

We are going to prove that Conjecture
8.1
implies Lech’s conjecture
[
23
]
:

8.2
.

Lech’s conjecture: Given a flat local map
R\to S
one has
e(R)\leq e(S)
.

We refer to
[
26
,
24
]
for a discussion on the history and current state of this conjecture, recording only that in
[
24
]
it is verified when
R
is standard graded. A key step in the proof is the construction of lim Ulrich sequences. Later in this section we exhibit rings that have no lim Ulrich sequences, but for which Conjecture
8.1
holds. The result below suggests an alternative approach to Lech’s conjecture.

Proposition 8.3
.

If Conjecture
8.1
holds for all complete local rings, then Lech’s conjecture holds.

Proof.

Let
R\to S
be a flat local map. A standard reduction allows one to assume
R
is a complete local domain,
S
is complete with algebraically closed residue field, and
\dim(S)=\dim(R)
; see
[
26
, Lemma 2.2]
. Then one has a Cohen factorization:

R\longrightarrow T\twoheadrightarrow S=T/J\,.

where
R\to T
is flat with regular closed fiber; see
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
. In particular,
e(R)=e(T)
and the ideal
J
of
T
is perfect; see
[
26
, Lemma 3.5]
. Moreover all the fibers of
R\to T
are complete intersections; see Tabaâ
[
33
, Théorème 2]
. In particular, since each minimal prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
J
contracts to
0
in
R
, it follows that the local ring
T_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong T_{\mathfrak{p}}/(\mathfrak{p}\cap R)T_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a complete intersection for every minimal prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
J
.

The residue field of
T
is infinite so we can pick elements
\boldsymbol{x}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
in
T
that form part of a system of parameters on
T
and whose images in
S
generate a minimal reduction of its maximal ideal, say
\mathfrak{n}
. Let
P
be a minimal

-free resolution of
S
. Since
J
is perfect the length of
P
is equal to
h\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}0pt(J)
.

Set

and
F\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K(\boldsymbol{x};T)\otimes_{T}P
; observe that this is a short complex in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{n}}(T)
. Conjecture
8.1
yields
\chi_{\infty}(F)\geq e(T)=e(R)
. Moreover, since
\boldsymbol{x}
generates a minimal reduction of
\mathfrak{n}
in
S
, we know that
\chi(F)=e(S)
. Thus to complete the proof it suffices to verify:

Claim
.

With notation as above, we have
\chi(F)=\chi_{\infty}(F)
.

The assignment
M\mapsto\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[\operatorname{H}_{i}(P\otimes_{T}M)]
induces a map

P\cap-\colon\operatorname{G}_{0}(T)\longrightarrow\operatorname{G}_{0}(S)\,.

Consider the multiplicity

e_{d}(-)\colon\operatorname{G}_{0}(S)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\,;

see Corollary
4.5
. We claim that there are equalities

\chi(F)=e_{d}(P\cap[T])\qquad\text{and}\qquad\chi_{\infty}(F)=e_{d}(P\cap[T]_{n})\,.

Indeed, both equalities are special cases of the more generality equality

e_{d}(P\cap-)=\langle F,-\rangle\qquad\text{on $\operatorname{G}_{0}(T)$.}

This holds because for any finitely generated
T
-module
M
one has equalities

\displaystyle\langle F,M\rangle

\displaystyle=\chi(F\otimes_{R}M)

\displaystyle=\chi(K(\boldsymbol{x};T)\otimes_{T}P\otimes_{T}M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\chi(K(\boldsymbol{x};\operatorname{H}_{i}(P\otimes_{T}M)))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}e_{d}(\operatorname{H}_{i}(P\otimes_{T}M))

\displaystyle=e_{d}(P\cap M)\,.

Let
\Lambda
denote the minimal primes of
S
defining its components of maximal dimension. Since
e_{d}(-)
factors through the localization map

\operatorname{G}_{0}(S)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda}\operatorname{G}_{0}(S_{\mathfrak{p}})

it suffices to verify that for each minimal prime
\mathfrak{p}
of

one has

(P\cap[T])_{\mathfrak{p}}=(P\cap[T]_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}}\qquad\text{in $\operatorname{G}_{0}(S_{\mathfrak{p}})$.}

The Riemann-Roch map
\tau
commutes with localization, in that we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{G}_{0}(T)}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A_{*}(T)}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{G}_{0}(T_{\mathfrak{p}})}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A_{*}(T_{\mathfrak{p}})}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

where the map on the right has a degree shift by

[

. Therefore one gets equalities

([T]_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}}=\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(\tau_{n}([T])_{\mathfrak{p}})=\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(\tau_{h}([T_{\mathfrak{p}}]))=[T_{\mathfrak{p}}]_{h}\,.

This leads to following computation where the last one is clear from the definition:

(P\cap[T]_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}}=P_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap[T_{\mathfrak{p}}]_{h}=P_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap[T_{\mathfrak{p}}]=(P\cap[T])_{\mathfrak{p}}

The second equality holds because

, for

is a complete intersection ring; see
[

, and also
[

. This completes the proof of the claim and hence of the desired result.
∎

Non-existence of lim Ulrich sequences

Recently Yhee
[
36
]
has constructed local rings that do not possess lim Ulrich sequences. In the following paragraphs we state a version of her results that suits us, referring to
[
36
]
for the more general statements and proofs. Our main goal here is to verify that Conjecture
8.1
holds for these rings nevertheless.

8.4
.

Let
R
be a local ring. As in
[
24
]
, a sequence
(U_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of finitely generated

-modules is said to be a
weakly lim Cohen-Macaulay sequence
if each
U_{n}
is nonzero, and for some system of parameters
\boldsymbol{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}r_{1},\dots,r_{d}
of

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\chi_{1}(K(\boldsymbol{r};U_{n}))}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=0,

where
\chi_{1}(K(\boldsymbol{r};U_{n}))\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(-1)^{i-1}\ell_{R}H_{i}(\boldsymbol{r};U_{n})

. If in addition (

) holds, then

is said to be a

.

It is clear from the definitions

,

and

that any lim Cohen-Macaulay sequence is weakly lim Cohen-Macaulay, and that any lim Ulrich sequence is weakly lim Ulrich. In
[

]
, it is proved that the existence of weakly lim Ulrich sequences implies Lech’s conjecture, and it is asked whether every complete local domain of positive characteristic admits a lim Ulrich or weakly lim Ulrich sequence

, Question 3.9]
. Following
[

]
, we describe a ring for which no such sequence exists.

8.5

Let

is a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(x,y)

Then

is a local

, with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}J

The following result follows from
[

Theorem 8.6
.

With notation as in

does not have weakly lim Ulrich sequence. ∎

Although the ring

holds for that ring.

Proposition 8.7

Let
R
be as in
8.5
. Each short complex

in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
satisifes

\chi_{\infty}(F)\geq e(R)\,.

Proof.

One has that

, so the desired result is that
\chi_{\infty}(F)\geq e(J,S)
. Since
R
is a complete local domain of dimension two one has
\chi_{\infty}(F)=\chi(F)
; see
[

, Proposition 3.7]
. Moreover as an
R
-module
S/R\cong(x,y)S/J
and hence the classes of
S
and

coincide in
{\operatorname{G}_{0}(R)}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. It follows that

\chi_{\infty}(F)=\chi(F)=\chi(S\otimes_{R}F)\,.

Set
G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}S\otimes_{R}F
. Evidently this is a short
S
-complex, and since

is regular, in particular Cohen-Macaulay,

has homology only in degree
0
, that is to say, it is a minimal free resolution of
\operatorname{H}_{0}(G)
. So we have to verify the following: Given the minimal free resolution of an

of finite length:

G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}0\longrightarrow S^{c}\longrightarrow S^{b}\longrightarrow S^{a}\longrightarrow 0

where the differential on

, then
\ell_{S}M\geq e(J,S)
.

When

is cyclic, we are in a situation where the Hilbert-Burch theorem applies and

has the form

G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}0\longrightarrow S^{b-1}\xrightarrow{\ d\ }S^{b}\xrightarrow{(s_{1}\dots s_{b})}S\longrightarrow 0

\ell_{S}M=\ell_{S}(S/(s_{1},s_{2}))=e((s_{1},s_{2}),S)\geq e(J,S)\,,

The other (in)equalities are clear. This settles the case

When

\ell_{S}M\geq 2\ell_{S}{(S/J)}\geq e(J,S)

where the second inequality again by Lech’s inequality, now applied to

.

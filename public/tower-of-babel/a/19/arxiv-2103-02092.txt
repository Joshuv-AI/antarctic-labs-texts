Anticyclotomic μ \mu -invariants of residually reducible Galois Representations
Debanjana Kundu, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Let E E be an elliptic curve over an imaginary quadratic field K K , and p p be an odd prime such that the residual representation E ⁡ [ p ] E[p] is reducible. The μ \mu -invariant of the fine Selmer group of E E over the anticyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension of K K is studied. We do not impose the Heegner hypothesis on E E , thus allowing certain primes of bad reduction to decompose infinitely in the anticyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension. It is shown that the fine μ \mu -invariant vanishes if certain explicit conditions are satisfied. Further, a partial converse is proven.



1.
Introduction

Iwasawa theory is the study of objects of arithmetic interest over infinite towers of number fields.
K. Iwasawa conjectured that over the
cyclotomic

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of a number field
F
, the
p
-primary part of the Hilbert class group is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module (see
[
17
]
).
This is known as Iwasawa’s
\mu=0
conjecture for the number field
F
.
In
[
9
]
, B. Ferrero and L. Washington proved this conjecture for all finite abelian extensions
F
over of
\mathbb{Q}
.
The Iwasawa theory of abelian varieties (in particular, elliptic curves) was initiated by B. Mazur in
[
25
]
.
The main object of study is the
p
-primary Selmer group of an elliptic curve
E
defined over a number field
F
, with good
ordinary
reduction at
p
.
The Selmer group over a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
is a cofinitely generated module over the Iwasawa algebra,
\Lambda
.
In
[
11
]
, R. Greenberg analyzed the algebraic structure of these Selmer groups.
For elliptic curves
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
, it is conjectured that if the residual representation on
E[p]
is irreducible, then
\mu
-invariant of the
p
-primary Selmer group over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, denoted
\mu(E/\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cyc}})
, vanishes (see
[
12
, Conjecture 1.11]
).

The
fine
Selmer group is a subgroup of the classical Selmer group obtained by imposing vanishing conditions at primes above
p
.
It had first been studied by K. Rubin
[
32
]
and B. Perrin-Riou
[
28
,
29
]
, under various guises.
The analysis of the fine Selmer groups is an essential part of K. Kato’s seminal work on the Iwasawa Main Conjecture for elliptic curves and modular forms (see
[
19
]
).
In recent years, their study has gained considerable momentum (see for instance
[
5
,
35
,
34
,
18
,
1
]
).
J. Coates and R. Sujatha conjectured that the the fine Selmer group of
E
over
F^{\operatorname{cyc}}
is
\Lambda
-cotorsion with associated
\mu
-invariant,
\mu^{\operatorname{fine}}(E/F^{\operatorname{cyc}})
, equal to zero
[
5
, Conjecture A]
.
The formulation of this conjecture makes no hypothesis on the reduction type at primes above
p
or the residual representation on
E[p]
.
However, such a statement need not be true for the (classical) Selmer group even for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
p
.
In fact, Mazur provided examples of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which the residual representation
E[p]
is reducible and
\mu(E/\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cyc}})
is non-zero.
There is a systematic approach towards finding examples of elliptic curves with positive
\mu(E/F^{\operatorname{cyc}})
(see
[
6
,
7
]
).

On the other hand, the conjecture by Coates and Sujatha predicts a close relationship in the growth of ideal class groups and fine Selmer groups in cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions.
Some evidence towards this is provided in
[
5
, Theorem 3.4]
.
In particular, the result of Ferrero and Washington implies that for an elliptic curve over an abelian number field,
\mu^{\operatorname{fine}}(E/F^{\operatorname{cyc}})
is zero when
E[p]
is reducible (see
[
5
, Corollary 3.6]
).
Subsequently, the relation in the growth of ideal class groups and fine Selmer groups has been studied in more general settings (see
[
23
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
).
In this paper, we study the relationship in the growth of fine Selmer groups and class groups in
anticyclotomic

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of imaginary quadratic fields.

Several authors have studied classical Selmer groups of elliptic curves (more generally, abelian varieties or modular forms) in anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of imaginary quadratic fields (see for example
[
33
,
2
,
30
]
).
In line with the conjecture of Coates and Sujatha, for an elliptic curve defined over the imaginary quadratic field
K
, its fine Selmer group over the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension is expected to be
\Lambda
-cotorsion with
\mu^{\operatorname{fine}}(E/K^{\operatorname{ac}})=0
(see
[
24
, Conjecture B]
).
Most results in literature focus on the case when the residual representation is irreducible.
In contrast, this paper primarily studies the case when the residual representation is
reducible
.
The first main result (see Theorem
3.2
) shows that if certain conditions are satisfied then the
\mu
-invariant of the
p
-primary fine Selmer group may be detected by a certain analogous fine Selmer group associated to the residual representation,
E[p]
.
Further, if
E[p]
is reducible then there are characters

\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}({}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muK}/K)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

which fit into a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{1})\rightarrow E[p]\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{2})\rightarrow 0.

Note that
\varphi_{2}=\overline{\chi}\varphi_{1}^{-1}
, where
\overline{\chi}
denotes the mod-
p
cyclotomic character.
This short exact sequence makes it possible to analyze the structure of the fine Selmer group associated to
E[p]
.
Next, we prove a partial converse to the above theorem (see Theorem
3.4
).
A key difference between the cyclotomic and anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions is the following: in the cyclotomic extension, all primes are finitely decomposed, whereas in the anticyclotomic extension, there are infinitely many primes which split completely.
For the aforementioned result of Coates and Sujatha in the cyclotomic extension case, this fact is crucially used (in the proofs of
[
5
, Lemmas 3.2, 3.7, Theorem 3.4]
).
The elliptic curve
E
is said to satisfy the Heegner hypothesis if all primes
v\nmid p
of
K
at which it has bad reduction are split completely in
K
.
By Fact
2.3
, such a a prime number is finitely decomposed in the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension,
K^{\operatorname{ac}}
.
We work in a more general setting, where primes of bad reduction of
E
may be infinitely decomposed in
K^{\operatorname{ac}}
, and thereby rely on a different strategy (from that of Coates and Sujatha) to prove our results.

Two elliptic curves,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, are said to be
p
-congruent if the
p
-torsion subgroups
E_{1}[p]
and
E_{2}[p]
are isomorphic as Galois modules.
In
[
14
]
, R. Greenberg and V. Vatsal studied the relation between cyclotomic invariants of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
which are
p
-congruent.
In
[
13
, Proposition 4.1.6]
, Greenberg reformulated the conjecture of Coates and Sujatha in terms of the vanishing of a second cohomology group with coefficients in
E[p]
.
From this, it is immediate that if elliptic curves
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
are
p
-congruent then
\mu^{\operatorname{fine}}(E_{1}/\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cyc}})=0
if and only if
\mu^{\operatorname{fine}}(E_{2}/\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cyc}})=0
.
In the same spirit, we prove a result for fine
\mu
-invariants over
K^{\operatorname{ac}}
(see Theorem
5.2
), without imposing any hypothesis on the reducibility of the residual representations.

The authors expect that the methods in this paper should generalize to residually reducible Galois representations arising from abelian varieties.
The results shall however be more technical and the arguments more cumbersome in the higher dimensional setting.
The authors choose a less general framework in which the inherent simplicity of the underlying ideas come across easily.

The paper is organized into six sections.
Preliminary notions are discussed in §
2
.
In §
3
and §
4
, we state and prove the main results: we establish a criterion for the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group in the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.
We also prove a partial converse to the above theorem.
In §
5
, we compare the anticyclotomic
\mu
-invariant for two elliptic curves which are
p
-congruent.
Finally, in §
6
we list examples illustrating the results in this article.

\overline{\Phi}
is the following map

{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\Phi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\left(\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K^{\operatorname{ac}})[p]\right)^{\vee}\rightarrow\Omega^{r+s}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Omega/({}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muf}_{j}(T))\right),

where
{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muf}_{j}(T)
is the mod-
p
reduction of

.
Since

is a distinguished polynomial,

and

The result follows.
∎

3.
A Criterion for the Vanishing of the fine
\mu
-invariant

In this section, we establish a criterion for the vanishing of the fine
\mu
-invariant in the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.
Before stating the result, let us introduce some notation.
We shall not assume that
{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E}
is reducible in this section, unless when explicitly stated.
However, when it is reducible, recall that the characters
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are such that
E[p]
fits into a short exact sequence of Galois-modules,

0\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{1})\rightarrow E[p]\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{2})\rightarrow 0.

Let
K(\varphi_{i})
be an extension of
K
fixed by
\ker\varphi_{i}
.
For an algebraic extension
\mathcal{F}
of
K
, let
\mathcal{F}(\varphi_{i})
be the composite
\mathcal{F}\cdot K(\varphi_{i})
.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{(i)}
be the maximal abelian unramified
p
-extension of
K_{n}(\varphi_{i})
split at primes above
S(K_{n}(\varphi_{i}))
(called the
p
-Hilbert
S
-class field extension).
Class field theory prescribes a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Gal}(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{(i)}/K_{n}(\varphi_{i}))\simeq\operatorname{Cl}_{S}(K_{n}(\varphi_{i}))[p^{\infty}],

where
\operatorname{Cl}_{S}(K_{n}(\varphi_{i}))[p^{\infty}]
is the
p
-primary part of the
S
-class group of
K_{n}(\varphi_{i})
.

Let
\rm{M}
be any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module on which
\operatorname{Gal}(K(\varphi_{i})/K)
acts by
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-linear automorphisms.
Let
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(K(\varphi_{i})/K)\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
be a character, and consider its Teichmüller lift,
\tilde{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(K(\varphi_{i})/K)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.
Set

\textrm{M}_{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{x\in\textrm{M}|g\cdot x=\tilde{\psi}(g)x\}.

There is a unique field extension
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{(i)}
of
K_{n}(\varphi_{i})
which is Galois over
K
with the additional property that
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{(i)}/K_{n}(\varphi_{i}))
is identified with
\left(\operatorname{Cl}_{S}(K_{n}(\varphi_{i}))[p^{\infty}]\right)_{\varphi_{i}}
.
Set
\mathcal{L}^{(i)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{n}
and
\mathcal{E}^{(i)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{E}^{(i)}_{n}
.
Further, setting
\mathcal{X}^{(i)}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathcal{L}^{(i)}/K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi_{i}))
,
standard arguments show that
\mathcal{X}^{(i)}
is a finitely generated torsion
\Lambda
-module.
Note that
\mathcal{X}^{(i)}
decomposes into a direct sum of
\Lambda
-submodules

\mathcal{X}^{(i)}=\bigoplus_{\psi}\mathcal{X}^{(i)}_{\psi},

where
\psi
ranges over the characters
\operatorname{Gal}(K(\varphi_{i})/K)\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
.
Recall that the
\psi
-eigenspace
\mathcal{X}^{(i)}_{\psi}
is defined as follows

\mathcal{X}^{(i)}_{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}^{(i)}\mid g\cdot x=\tilde{\psi}(g)x\}.

Let
Y_{i}
be the
\varphi_{i}
-eigenspace
\mathcal{X}^{(i)}_{\varphi_{i}}
and
\widetilde{Y}_{i}
be the
\varphi_{i}
-component of the
p
-Hilbert class field extension
\mathcal{M}^{(i)}
of
K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi_{i})
.
Denote their
\mu
-invariants (with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi)/K(\varphi_{i})
) by
\mu(Y_{i})
and
\mu(\widetilde{Y_{i}})
, respectively.
Note that
Y_{i}
is the quotient of
\widetilde{Y}_{i}
where all the primes in
S(K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi_{i}))
are split.
We identify
Y_{i}
with the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathcal{E}^{(i)}/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
.
The next result follows from the work of H. Hida
[
16
, Theorem I]
(see also
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
) and Rubin
[
31
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
p=v\overline{v}
in
K
.
Let
\operatorname{G}_{v}=\operatorname{Gal}({}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muK_{v}}/K_{v})
and assume that
\varphi_{1\restriction\operatorname{G}_{v}}\neq 1,\ \overline{\chi}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{v}}
(or equivalently,
\varphi_{2\restriction\operatorname{G}_{v}}\neq 1,\ \overline{\chi}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{v}}
).
Then,

\mu(\widetilde{Y}_{1})=\mu(\widetilde{Y}_{2})=\mu(Y_{1})=\mu(Y_{2})=0.

Proof.

Since
\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}=\overline{\chi}
, the condition on
\varphi_{1}
is equivalent to that on
\varphi_{2}
.
Let
\varphi_{i}
be either of the characters,
\varphi_{1}
or
\varphi_{2}
.
Let
\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}
be the maximal abelian pro-
p
extension of
K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi_{i})
which is unramified away from
v(K^{\operatorname{ac}})
.
Rubin identifies
\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}
with an anticyclotomic
p
-adic L-function, and Hida (also T. Finis) proves that the
\mu
-invariant of such an anticyclotomic
p
-adic L-function vanishes.
Combining their results, we deduce that
\mu(\mathcal{Q}^{(i)})=0
for
i=1,2
.
Since
\widetilde{Y_{i}}
is a quotient of
\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}
, it follows that
\mu(\widetilde{Y}_{i})=0
for
i=1,2
.
Hence,
\mu(Y_{i})=0
for
i=1,2
as well.
∎

The most explicit examples of reducible Galois representations arise from elliptic curves with
p
-torsion points over the base field.
In this setting, the conditions of Theorem
3.1
are not satisfied since
\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\}=\{1,\overline{\chi}\}
.
We now state the main theorems of this article.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose the following conditions hold.

(1)

{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E}
is reducible.

(2)

Hypothesis
2.5
is satisfied.

(3)

If

2.7
is satisfied.

(4)

, the

-invariant

is
\Lambda
-cotorsion with

.

This result is illustrated by Example

.
For elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
where hypotheses of Theorem

are satisfied, a similar result is proven in

]
.

Remark 3.3
.

When all primes

are split in

, the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant for

is equivalent to the vanishing of the

).

.

as a quotient of

.
The condition

is therefore a more optimal condition than requiring

.

The next result is a (partial) converse to Theorem

.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
K
.
Assume that

(1)

{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E}

(2)

is a cotorsion

-module with

.
If
{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E}

.

The next corollary takes note of a special case of interest.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over

and

be the set of primes dividing

, where
\mathcal{N}
is the conductor of

.
Assume that

.

(2)

, let

Note that

when the residual representation is of the form

&\overline{\chi}\\

.

3.4
and Proposition

.
∎

Example

illustrates Corollary

. Before proving the above results, a brief sketch of the method is provided.
The Kummer sequence

(3.1)

induces a comparison map

\Psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}(E[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K^{\operatorname{ac}})[p].

The main theorem is deduced from the following statements.

(1)

The kernel of
\Psi
is finite.
If Hypothesis
2.5
is satisfied, then

{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E}

(2)

Suppose that
{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E}

Further, if the conditions of Theorem

are satisfied, then the mod-
p

We now analyze the map

and deduce a criterion for the vanishing of the fine

-invariant.
To show that

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

be a prime such that

be a prime of

above

.

Since

(2)

,
\mu_{p}\subset K_{v}

, and

The claim follows from the proof of

and

For

, let

denote the natural map

induced from
(

)
.
For each prime

of

(

)
.

.

If Hypothesis
2.5
is satisfied, then

Proof.

The kernel of

is the product of

ranges over

First, consider the case when

Proposition 3.8
.

The kernel of
\Psi
is finite.

is also finite.

Proof.

The Kummer sequence
(

)
induces the commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{R}(E[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})}

Let

4.
Finiteness of the residual fine Selmer group

Throughout this section
{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E}
is assumed to be reducible.
Therefore,
E[p]
fits into the short exact sequence,

0\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{1})\rightarrow E[p]\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{2})\rightarrow 0.

This allows the analysis of the algebraic structure of the mod-
p
fine Selmer group
\mathcal{R}(E[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
in terms of the mod-
p
fine Selmer groups
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i})/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
for
i=1,2
(definitions given below).
This completes the proof of Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
S
be the set of primes dividing
\mathcal{N}p
, where
\mathcal{N}
is the conductor of
E
.
The fine Selmer group
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i})/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
is defined as follows

\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i})/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(K_{S}/K^{\operatorname{ac}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i}))\rightarrow\prod_{\eta\in S(K^{\operatorname{ac}})}H^{1}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}_{\eta},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i}))\right\}.

The dependence on
S
is suppressed in the notation.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that
{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E}
is reducible and let
\varphi_{i}
be one of the characters on the diagonal of
{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E}
.
Then,
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i})/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
is finite if and only if
\mu(Y_{i})=0
.

Proof.

Set
\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(K(\varphi_{i})/K)
.
Since the order of
\Delta
is coprime to
p
,

\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi_{i})/K)\simeq\Delta\times\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}/K).

The group
\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi_{i})/K)
acts on
\mathcal{X}^{(i)}
.
Let
g\in\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi_{i})/K)
and
x\in\mathcal{X}^{(i)}
.
Choose a lift
\tilde{g}\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathcal{L}^{(i)}/K)
, and set
g\cdot x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tilde{g}x\tilde{g}^{-1}
.
Since
\mathcal{X}^{(i)}
is abelian,
g\cdot x
is independent of the choice of lift,
\tilde{g}
.
This induces the action of
\Delta
on
\mathcal{X}^{(i)}
.
There is a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}(Y_{i},\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{X}^{(i)},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i}))^{\Delta}.

Since the order of
\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi_{i})/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
is prime to
p
, it follows (from restriction-corestriction) that the cohomology group
H^{j}(\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi_{i})/K^{\operatorname{ac}}),\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i}))=0
for
j>0
. It follows from the inflation-restriction sequence that

H^{1}(K_{S}/K^{\operatorname{ac}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Gal}(K_{S}/K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi_{i})),\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i}))^{\Delta},

where
\Delta
is identified with
\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}(\varphi_{i})/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
.
This induces an isomorphism

\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i})/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}(Y_{i}/pY_{i},\mathbb{F}_{p}).

It is an easy consequence of the structure theory of
\Lambda
-modules (see the argument in the proof of Lemma
2.11
) that the
\mu
-invariant of
Y_{i}
is zero if and only if
Y_{i}/pY_{i}
is finite.
The result follows.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E}
is reducible and
\varphi_{i}

Proof.

\begin{split}&\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i})/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{M}^{(i)}/p,\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i}))^{\Delta}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\widetilde{Y}_{i}/p,\mathbb{F}_{p}),\\

5.
Congruent Galois representations

In this section, we consider two elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
defined over
K
which are
p
-congruent, i.e. their residual representations are isomorphic,

{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E_{1}}\simeq{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E_{2}}.

We make no assumption on the reducibility of the residual representations.
However, it is assumed that both
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
satisfy the Heegner hypothesis.

Hypothesis 5.1

(Heegner hypothesis)
.

Let
E_{/K}
be an elliptic curve with conductor
\mathcal{N}
, and
S
be the set of primes dividing
\mathcal{N}p
.
Let
v\in S
and
l
be the prime number such that
v|l
.
Then,
l
splits in
K
if
l\neq p
.

We prove the following main result in this section.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves over
K
.
Suppose that the following assumptions hold.

(1)

Both
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
satisfy the Heegner hypothesis.

(2)

The residual Galois representations,
{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E_{1}}
and
{}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0mu\rho}_{E_{2}}
, are isomorphic.

Then,

\begin{split}&\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(E_{1}/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\text{ is }\Lambda\text{-cotorsion with }\mu^{\operatorname{fine}}(E_{1}/K^{\operatorname{ac}})=0\\
\Leftrightarrow&\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(E_{2}/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\text{ is }\Lambda\text{-cotorsion with }\mu^{\operatorname{fine}}(E_{2}/K^{\operatorname{ac}})=0.\end{split}

The key result used in the proof of the above theorem will be Proposition
3.10
, which states that

\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(E_{i}/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\text{ is }\Lambda\text{-cotorsion with }\mu^{\operatorname{fine}}(E_{i}/K^{\operatorname{ac}})=0\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{R}(E_{i}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\text{ is finite.}

In view of Lemma
2.11
, it suffices to prove the following result.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves satisfying the conditions of Theorem
5.2
.
Then
\mathcal{R}(E_{1}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
is finite if and only if
\mathcal{R}(E_{2}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
is finite.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{i}
be the conductor of
E_{i}
and
S_{i}
be the set of primes dividing
\mathcal{N}_{i}p
.
Denote by
T
the union
S_{1}\cup S_{2}
.
Since
E_{1}[p]
and
E_{2}[p]
are isomorphic as Galois modules, the mod-
p
fine Selmer groups are isomorphic, i.e.

\mathcal{R}^{T}(E_{1}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\simeq\mathcal{R}^{T}(E_{2}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})

(recall
\mathcal{R}^{T}(E_{i}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
from Definition
2.10
).
Let
v\in T
and
\eta\in v(K^{\operatorname{ac}})
.
Denote by
\operatorname{G}_{\eta}
the absolute Galois group of
K^{\operatorname{ac}}_{\eta}
and by
\operatorname{I}_{\eta}\subset\operatorname{G}_{\eta}
its inertia subgroup.
Set
H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}_{\eta},E_{i}[p])
to denote the kernel of the restriction map,

\operatorname{res}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{1}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}_{\eta},E_{i}[p])\longrightarrow H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{\eta},E_{i}[p])^{\operatorname{G}_{\eta}/\operatorname{I}_{\eta}}.

It follows from inflation-restriction that
H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}_{\eta},E_{i}[p])
can be identified with
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\eta}/\operatorname{I}_{\eta},E_{i}[p]^{\operatorname{I}_{\eta}})
. Since
\operatorname{G}_{\eta}/\operatorname{I}_{\eta}\simeq\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
, it follows that (see
[
26
, Proposition 1.7.7]
)

H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\eta}/\operatorname{I}_{\eta},E_{i}[p]^{\operatorname{I}_{\eta}})\simeq H_{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\eta}/\operatorname{I}_{\eta},E_{i}[p]^{\operatorname{I}_{\eta}})

and hence is finite.
By the Heegner hypothesis and Fact
2.3
, the set
v(K^{\operatorname{ac}})
is finite for
v\in T
.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\operatorname{nr}}(K^{\operatorname{ac}},E[p])\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{v}(K^{\operatorname{ac}},E[p])
denote the sum,

\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\operatorname{nr}}(K^{\operatorname{ac}},E_{i}[p])\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{\eta\in v(K^{\operatorname{ac}})}H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(K^{\operatorname{ac}}_{\eta},E_{i}[p]).

It follows from the above discussion that
\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\operatorname{nr}}(K^{\operatorname{ac}},E_{i}[p])
is finite for
v\in T
.

There is a natural exact sequence,

0\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{T}(E_{i}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\rightarrow\mathcal{R}(E_{i}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in T\setminus S_{i}}\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\operatorname{nr}}(K^{\operatorname{ac}},E_{i}[p]).

Since the last term in the above sequence is finite, it follows that
\mathcal{R}(E_{i}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
is finite if and only if
\mathcal{R}^{T}(E_{i}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})
is.
The result follows from this.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Note that the Heegner hypothesis implies Hypothesis
2.5
. Therefore, Proposition
3.10
applies in this setting and asserts that

\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(E_{i}/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\text{ is }\Lambda\text{-cotorsion with }\mu^{\operatorname{fine}}(E_{i}/K^{\operatorname{ac}})=0\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{R}(E_{i}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\text{ is finite.}

On the other hand, Proposition
5.3
states that

\mathcal{R}(E_{1}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\text{ is finite }\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{R}(E_{2}[p]/K^{\operatorname{ac}})\text{ is finite.}

The result follows.
∎

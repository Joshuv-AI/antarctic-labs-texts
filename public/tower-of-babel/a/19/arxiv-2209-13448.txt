A pathwise Regularization by noise phenomenon for the evolutionary p p -Laplace equation
Florian Bechtold, Jörn Wichmann

Abstract
. We study an evolutionary p p -Laplace problem whose potential is subject to a translation in time. Provided the trajectory along which the potential is translated admits a sufficiently regular local time, we establish existence of solutions to the problem for singular potentials for which a priori bounds in classical approaches break down, thereby establishing a pathwise regularization by noise phenomena for this non-linear problem.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
T>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We are interested in the evolutionary
p
-Laplace system

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}\,S(\nabla u)&=b(u)\quad&&\text{ on }\Omega\times[0,T],\\
u|_{\Omega}&=0\quad&&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\\
u(0,\cdot)&=u_{0}\quad&&\text{ on }\Omega,\end{aligned}

(1.1)

where
S(\xi)=\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{p-2}\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
b:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Equation (
1.1
) naturally arises as a perturbed gradient flow of the energy

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p}\,\text{\rm d}x

on the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
of weakly differentiable vectorfields with vanishing trace. Questions related to the well-posedness of (
1.1
) are highly linked to regularity of
b\circ u
and therefore
b
as well as the notion of solutions. In particular, in the absence of certain growth conditions, the study of (
1.1
) might be obstructed. Let us elaborate on this point in the following subsection.

Monotone operator theory

The
p
-Laplace operator

\displaystyle Au:=-\mathrm{div}\,S(\nabla u)

is a prominent example of a maximal monotone operator. The famous theory of monotone operators traces back to the early works of Minty
[
Min62
]
and Browder
[
Bro63
]
. It inspired many mathematicians to study well-posedness of monotone evolution equations and perturbations of it, see e.g.
[
Kom67
,
Kat67
,
Lio69
,
Ô77
,
Ô82
,
CH16
,
AH20
]
. A nice overview on monotone operators can be found e.g. in the books of Brezis
[
Bre73
]
, Barbu
[
Bar76
]
and Zeidler
[
Zei90b
]
.

Let us briefly recall some main ideas and in particular function spaces that naturally appear in the study of (
1.1
). Towards this end, let us set
b\circ u=f
and forget briefly that
f
depends on
u
and proceed formally. We can define the evolutionary problem as a steady problem

\displaystyle Bu:=\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u)=f.

(1.2)

In this setting, we now need to find a suitable function space such that
B
is surjective. First, note that for sufficiently smooth
u
it holds

\displaystyle\langle Bu,u\rangle=\left\lVert u(T)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\lVert u(0)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla u(s)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}\,\text{\rm d}s.

Therefore, a natural ansatz space to consider is

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}:=C\big([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{p}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\big).

Indeed, monotone operator theory implies that we can solve (
1.2
) in the weak sense for
f\in\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
with a quantified energy estimate

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}.

Additionally,
u
enjoys some time regularity as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{t}u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(0;T;L^{p}(\Omega))}^{p-1}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}.

Overall, we find that the solution map

\displaystyle L^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{X}^{\prime}\ni(u_{0},f)\mapsto u\in\mathbb{Y},

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}=\left\{v\in\mathbb{X}\big|\,v(0)=u_{0}\,,\,\partial_{t}v\in\mathbb{X}^{\prime}\right\}

is bounded, i.e.,

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}+\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(1.3)

A similar approach can be employed to construct weak solutions to (
1.1
) if
f=b\circ u
, provided that we can close the energy estimate (
1.3
). That is we need to remove the unknown solution
u
from the right hand side. This can be done by imposing a growth condition on
b
, e.g., for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle\left\lvert b(u)\cdot u\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{r}+1,

(1.4)

where
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Conceptually the cases
r\;\geqslant\;1
and
r<1
are completely different. The former case bounds the growth of
b
for large values of
\left\lvert u\right\rvert
. The latter one treats potentials whose singularities are located in the origin. The classical approach can cover the range
r\in(0,p\vee 2]
.

If the growth at infinity is to strong in comparison to the dissipation of the
p
-Laplace operator solutions may blow up in finite time. This was already observed in the linear scalar case,
p=2
and
N=1
, by Fujita
[
Fuj70
]
. It was later extended by Tsutsumi in
[
Tsu72
]
to the non-linear scalar case
S(\nabla u)_{i}=\left\lvert\partial_{i}u\right\rvert^{p-2}\partial_{i}u
for the prototype perturbation
b(u)=\max(u^{r-1},0)
. This leads to an upper bound
r\;\leqslant\;p\vee 2
.

The lower bound on the growth rate can be seen by choosing
b(u)=-|u|^{\eta-1}u
for some
r=\eta+1\;\leqslant\;0
, cf. Example
1.2
. In other words only minor singularities in zero can be treated with the classical weak solution approach.

In the following, we intend to study the effect of translations of the potential
b
along so called "regularizing paths"
w

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u)&=b(u-w)\quad&&\text{ on }\Omega\times[0,T],\\
u|_{\Omega}&=0\quad&&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\\
u(0,\cdot)&=u_{0}\quad&&\text{ on }\Omega.\end{aligned}

(1.5)

From a physical point of view, translating the potential in time can be interpreted as uncertainty as to the location of the origin of the potential. By a "regularizing path", we will understand a continuous path
w
that admits a sufficiently regular local time

Pathwise regularization by noise in a nutshell

The starting point for investigations of this type usually consists in the study of averaging operators along a path
w
, given by

(T^{-w}_{t}b)(u):=\int_{0}^{t}b(u-w_{s})ds.

As was recognized already in
[
CG16
]
, paths enjoying so called
\rho
irregularity
[
CG16
, Definition 1.3]
, i.e. paths whose Fourier transform of the occupation measure decrease sufficiently rapidly lead to a regularization effect in that function
u\mapsto(T^{-w}_{t}b)(u)
will enjoy higher regularity than
b
. The reason for this gain of regularity can be made clear as follows: Assuming
w
to also admit a local time
L
, we can rewrite the averaging operator thanks to the occupation times formula as

\int_{0}^{t}b(u-w_{s})ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}b(u-z)L_{t}(z)dz=(b*L_{t})(u).

If the Fourier transform of the occupation measure is decreasing rapidly in a certain quantifiable sense, the local time
L
will enjoy some high quantifiable spatial regularity. By Young’s inequality (see for example (
2.1
) or
[
KS22
]
for a generalization to the Besov space setting), this implies that the regularity of
T^{-w}_{t}b
will essentially increase by the regularity of
L_{t}
with respect to the regularity of
b
. While the canonical paths for which this gain in regularity can be quantified are realizations of fractional Brownian motion
[
CG16
]
,
[
GG21
]
,
[
Ger22
]
,
\rho
irregularity is in fact a typical property among Hölder continuous paths in the sense of prevalence
[
GG20
]
.

Taking this observation of increased regularity of the averaging operator as a starting point, one can exploit this local gain of regularity to further study Riemann-sum type expressions of the form

\mathcal{I}^{n}_{T}=\sum_{[s,t]\in\mathcal{P}^{n}([0,T])}(T^{-w}_{s,t}b)(u_{s})

for partitions
\mathcal{P}^{n}([0,T])
of
[0,T]
and a continuous function
u
. The tool that then ensures the convergence of such Riemann-sums in the limit
|\mathcal{P}^{n}|\to 0
is Gubinelli’s Sewing Lemma
[
Gub04
]
,
[
FH14
]
, also cited in the Appendix as Lemma
6.3
. Let us stress at this point already that since the Sewing Lemma is formulated in a "Hölder-space setting", convergence of
(\mathcal{I}^{n})_{n}
will however always require at least some Hölder regularity of the function
u
. Provided this is available, i.e.
u
is sufficiently Hölder regular and the averaging operator
(T^{-w}b)
sufficiently regular in time and space, the Sewing Lemma ensures the convergence of
(\mathcal{I}^{n})_{n}
as
|\mathcal{P}^{n}|\to 0
. Note that as this convergence eventually only requires information on the regularity of the averaging operator and not the regularity of
b
, this construction (which can be shown to coincide with the classical Lebesgue integral for regular
b
) naturally extends the definition of the Lebesgue integral to irregular and potentially even distributional
b
, i.e. one can define

\mathcal{I}_{T}=\lim_{|\mathcal{P}^{n}|\to 0}\sum_{[s,t]\in\mathcal{P}^{n}([0,T])}(T^{-w}_{s,t}b)(u_{s})=:\int_{0}^{T}b(u_{s}-w_{s})ds

Moreover, even in the case of a regular nonlinearity
b
where the above can be defined as a classical Lebesgue integral, this alternative definition provides alternative a priori bounds through the Sewing Lemma of which we will crucially make use in our work.

Application to the p-Laplace equation with shifted potential

From the above considerations and on the background of classical monotone operator theory, a first approach in studying (
1.5
) might consist in investigating the weak formulation

\langle u_{t}-u_{s},\varphi\rangle+\int_{s}^{t}\langle S(\nabla u_{r}),\nabla\varphi\rangle dr=\int_{s}^{t}\langle b(u_{r}-w_{r}),\varphi\rangle dr,

where the right hand side should be interpreted as

\int_{s}^{t}\langle b(u_{r}-w_{r}),\varphi\rangle dr:=\lim_{|\mathcal{P}^{n}|\to 0}\sum_{[s^{\prime},t^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{P}^{n}([s,t])}\langle(T^{-w}_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}b)(u_{s^{\prime}}),\varphi\rangle.

Note however that the classical monotone operator approach to this problem, working on the Gelfand triple
\big(W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega),L^{2}(\Omega),(W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega))^{\prime}\big)
, only yields a priori bounds that permit to conclude
u\in C([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega))
. In particular, no additional Hölder regularity in time on this spatial regularity scale is obtained, meaning the Sewing argument can’t be closed making the right hand side ill defined. To circumvent this problem, we employ a strong formulation to the problem, i.e. we strive for solutions
u
that satisfy

u_{t}-u_{s}-\int_{s}^{t}\mbox{div}S(\nabla u_{r})dr=\int_{s}^{t}b(u_{r}-w_{r})dr

(1.6)

understood as an equality in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and where for singular
b
the right hand side is understood in the sense

\int_{s}^{t}b(u_{r}-w_{r}):=\lim_{|\mathcal{P}^{n}|\to 0}\sum_{[s^{\prime},t^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{P}^{n}([s,t])}(T^{-w}_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}b)(u_{s^{\prime}}),

(1.7)

where the convergence on the right hand side holds in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, uniformly in time on
[0,T]
. Note that while such strong solutions naturally require higher regularity of the initial condition, namely
u_{0}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, they allow for further a priori bounds in
C^{0,1/2}\big([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)\big)
provided
b
is sufficiently regular (refer to section
3
). Having for regular
b
such a priori bounds at our disposal, we can then harness the regularizing effect of the averaging operator
T^{-w}
as discussed above to obtain a priori bounds that are robust even when considering singular potentials
b
(refer to section
4
). The so obtained new a priori bounds for singular
b
can then be used to obtain solutions to (
1.6
) using classical monotonicity arguments (refer to section
5
). In summary, this allows us to prove our main theorem:

Theorem 1.1

(Existence of robustified solution)
.

Let
p>1
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.
For
r\in[1,\infty)
and
q\in[r,\infty)
let
b:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfy
b\in L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Suppose that
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is continuous and admits a local time
L
which satisfies
L\in C^{0,\gamma}\big([0,T];W^{1,r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)
for some
\gamma\in(1/2,1)
.

Then there exists a robustified solution

\displaystyle u\in\left\{v\in L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\big|\,\partial_{t}v,\mathrm{div}S(\nabla v)\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)\right\}

to (
1.5
) in the sense of Definition
2.2
. Moreover, the following a priori bound is valid

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\Omega))}^{p}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u)\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{2}\\
&\hskip 30.00005pt\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{4}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}([0,T];W^{1,r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}^{2}.\end{aligned}

(1.8)

Suppose moreover that
b
satisfies the monotonicity condition, for all
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\displaystyle(b(u)-b(v))\cdot(u-v)\;\leqslant\;0,

(1.9)

then robustified solutions to (
1.5
) in the sense of Definition
2.2
are unique.

Let us illustrate this established regularization effect by means of a concrete example. A detailed verification of the claims of the Examples can be found in Appendix
6
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
K>0
and define the potential

\displaystyle b(u):=-|u|^{\eta-1}u\textbf{1}_{\{|u|\;\leqslant\;K\}}.

(1.10)

Then the following statements are true:

(1)

If
\eta\;\leqslant\;-1
, then (
1.1
), i.e. the problem without regularizing path does not have a weak solution for
u_{0}=0
.

(2)

Let
\eta\in(-N/2,0)
and

\displaystyle H<\frac{1}{(2-4\eta)\vee N}

(1.11)

and
w^{H}
be a
N
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H
. Then for almost any realization
w^{H}(\omega):[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
1.5
) has a robustified solution for any
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Essentially (
1
) shows that in the unperturbed setting the origin is a singular state, which is due to the singularity of
b
in zero. In contrast to this, the presence of the highly oscillating path
w^{H}(\omega)
ensures that the solution
u
does not spend too much time in the singularity of
b
. Condition (
1.11
) ensures that this effect is quantitatively sufficiently strong for the singularity not to obstruct existence theory, as formulated in our main theorem above. Overall, we can thus observe a regularization phenomenon in dimension
N\;\geqslant\;3
. In particular, for
N=3
and
\eta=1
, there is no weak solution to (
1.1
) for
u_{0}=0
, while (
1.5
) with
u_{0}=0
admits a robustified solution for almost every realization
w^{H}(\omega)
of a fractional Brownian motion, provided
H<1/6
.

Remark 1.3
.

Let us stress that we are employing a pathwise regularization by noise argument in the spirit of
[
HP21
]
relying on the study of local times and their regularities and not in the spirit of
[
CG16
]
,
[
GG21
]
based on the study of averaging operators. While results on the regularizing effect of averaging operators are slightly less optimal for realizations of fractional Brownian motion for example, they allow for a completely pathwise i.e. analytical treatment. In contrast, arguing in the spirit of
[
CG16
]
,
[
GG21
]
would require to employ a tightness argument. Moreover, if the perturbing path is the realization of a stochastic process
w
, note that as our proof employs a compactness argument, we lose measurability of our solution with respect to the probability space on which
w
is defined. While this can be retained in an approach along the lines of
[
CG16
]
,
[
GG21
]
, one would only obtain a probabilistically weak solution in that case.

Solution concepts

In the following, let us discuss different notions of solutions to (

Definition 2.1

(Classical)
.

A function

is called weak solution to (

) if

(1)

(Regularity)

(2)

(Tested equation) for all

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\Omega}(u_{t}-u_{0})\cdot\xi\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}S(\nabla u):\nabla\xi\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle b(u-w),\xi\right\rangle\,\text{\rm d}s.\end{aligned}

(2.2)

A weak solution

is called strong solution to (

) if additionally

(1)

(Regularity)
\partial_{t}u,\,\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u),\,b(u_{\cdot}-w_{\cdot})\in L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}

(2)

(Point-wise equation) for almost all

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u)=b(u-w).\end{aligned}

(2.3)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
b

Proof.

Since
u
is a strong solution, it suffices to show that for any

(\mathcal{I}A^{u})_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}b(u_{r}-w_{r})dr

Remark first that
A^{u}
does admit a sewing with values in

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\delta A^{u})_{svt}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert(b*L_{v,t})(u_{s})-(b*L_{v,t})(u_{v})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert b*L_{v,t}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1}}\left\lVert u_{s}-u_{v}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2+\epsilon}_{t}L^{2}}|t-s|^{1/2+\epsilon}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}|t-s|^{1/2}

for some
\epsilon>0
, meaning we may apply the Sewing Lemma
6.3
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert A^{u}_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}b(u_{r}-w_{r})dr\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}=\left\lVert\int_{s}^{t}b(u_{s}-w_{r})-b(u_{r}-w_{r})dr\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{C^{0,1}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\int_{s}^{t}|r-s|^{1/2}dr\lesssim\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{C^{0,1}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}|t-s|^{3/2},

from which we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A^{u})_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}b(u_{r}-w_{r})dr\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle\left\lVert A^{u}_{s,t}-(\mathcal{I}A^{u})_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}+\left\lVert A^{u}_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}b(u_{r}-w_{r})dr\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}=O(|t-s|^{1+\epsilon}).

Hence the function

t\to(\mathcal{I}A^{u})_{0,t}-\int_{0}^{t}b(u_{r}-w_{r})dr

is constant. As it starts in zero, this concludes the claim.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
b
be smooth and bounded and let
w\in C^{0,\alpha}_{t}
for some
\alpha>0
be a path with local time
L\in C^{0,1/2+\epsilon}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
for some
\epsilon>0
. Assume
u
to be a robustified solution, then

(1)

the sewing is weakly differentiable, i.e.,

\displaystyle t\mapsto(\mathcal{I}A^{u})_{t}\in W^{1,2}(0,T;L^{2}_{x}),

and moreover, we find

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\big[(\mathcal{I}A^{u})\big]\big|_{t}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{h\to 0}h^{-1}\big((\mathcal{I}A^{u})_{t+h}-(\mathcal{I}A^{u})_{t}\big)=b(u_{t}-w_{t}).

(2)

u
is a strong solution and for almost all
t\in I

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u)=b(u-w)

as an equation in
L^{2}_{x}
.

Proof.

First we will address (
1
). We will not use that
u
is a robustified solution and only exploit
u\in C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
but trace the dependents of the sewing and the regularity of the germ. In principle the statement of (
1
) is of independent interest.

Recall that
(\mathcal{I}A^{u})
is constructed as the germ

\displaystyle A_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}b(u_{s}-w_{\tau})\,\text{\rm d}\tau=\big(b*L_{s,t}\big)(u_{s}).

Additionally, we have as in the previous Lemma

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A^{u})_{s,t}-A_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2+\epsilon}_{t}L^{2}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert^{1+\epsilon}.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{A_{t,t+h}}{h}-b(u_{t}-w_{t})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert h^{-1}\int_{t}^{t+h}b(u_{t}-w_{\tau})-b(u_{t}-w_{t})\,\text{\rm d}\tau\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert h^{-1}\int_{t}^{t+h}\left[b\right]_{C^{0,1}}\left\lvert w_{\tau}-w_{t}\right\rvert\,\text{\rm d}\tau\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\left[b\right]_{C^{0,1}}\left[w\right]_{C^{0,\alpha}_{t}}\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{\alpha}.

Overall, division by
h
and passing with
h\to 0
implies thus

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{(\mathcal{I}A^{u})_{t+h}-(\mathcal{I}A^{u})_{t}}{h}-b(u_{t}-w_{t})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\rightarrow 0,

establishing the first claim.
Next, we take a look at (
2
). By part (
1
) the right hand side of (
2.5
) is weakly differentiable in
L^{2}_{x}
and due to the regularity assumptions
\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
and
\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u)\in L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
also the left hand side is differentiable. Therefore we may rescale (
2.5
) by
\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert^{-1}
and pass to the limit
t\to s
to obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u)=b(u-w).

∎

Remark 2.5
.

Note that the key difference between strong and robustified solutions lies in the fact that the latter can exploit the regularization property of the local time
L
associated with
w
. In particular, provided this local time is sufficiently regular, the definition is meaningful even in instances in which
b
only enjoys distributional regularity as discussed in the introduction.

3.
Classical a priori bounds for strong solutions

Singular potentials
b
that do not satisfy the above growth conditions are in general inaccessible to the classical theory as seen above. However, if we restrict ourselves to regularized approximations of
b
, i.e., we assume that there exists
(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\in C^{0,1}
such that
b_{\varepsilon}\to b\in L^{2q}
, then for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
the classical theory is applicable and existence of strong solutions to (
1.5
) for smooth
b
is a classical result. It can be found e.g. in the books
[
Lio69
,
GGZ74
,
Zei90b
]
. The objective of the present section is to trace the precise form of the a priori estimates that will be robustified in the next section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}_{x}\cap W^{1,p}_{0,x}
and
b_{\varepsilon}\in C^{0,1}
. Then there exists a unique solution
u^{\varepsilon}
solving

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u^{\epsilon}-\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u^{\epsilon})&=b_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-w)&&\quad\text{ on }\Omega\times[0,T]\\
u^{\epsilon}|_{\Omega}&=0&&\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times[0,T]\\
u^{\epsilon}(0,\cdot)&=u_{0}^{\varepsilon}&&\quad\text{ on }\Omega.\end{aligned}

(3.1)

in the strong sense of Definition
2.1
.
Moreover, the following a priori bounds are valid

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sup_{t\;\leqslant\;T}\frac{1}{p}\left\lVert\nabla u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}&+\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\partial_{t}u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{t})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t\\
&\;\leqslant\;\frac{3}{p}\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+3\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert b_{\varepsilon}(u_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{t})\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}t,\end{aligned}

(3.2)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\;\leqslant\;T}\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}\,\text{\rm d}t\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+2T\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert b_{\varepsilon}(u_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{t})\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}t.

(3.3)

Proof.

The existence of a unique strong solution
u^{\varepsilon}
to (
3.1
) is standard, see e.g.
[
Lio69
,
GGZ74
,
Zei90b
]
, so let us just recall the main steps employed: In order to obtain existence, one first performs a Galerkin projection to the problem. The existence of solutions to the so obtained finite dimensional problem is done through a fixed point theorem. Next, using monotonicity of the
p
-Laplace operator, one establishes a priori bounds uniformly along solutions to the projected problems. By Banach-Alaoglu, one extracts a weak-* convergent subsequence, whose limit one has to identify as a solution to the problem. Identifying the limit in the non-linearity
b
is done thanks to the Aubin-Lions Lemma
6.6
. Identifying the limit in the
p
-Laplace operator is done with Minty’s Lemma
6.5
. Finally, uniqueness is obtained by monotonicity of the
p
-Laplace operator.

We will argue on the weak (
3.3
) and strong (
3.2
) energy estimates separately.

The weak energy estimate (
3.3
) naturally occurs when multiplying (
3.1
) by
u^{\varepsilon}
. Integration in space and integration by parts imply

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\right)+\left\lVert\nabla u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}b_{\varepsilon}(u_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{t})\cdot u_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)\,\text{\rm d}x.

Integration in time, together with Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities result in

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}&+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla u_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}\,\text{\rm d}s=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}b_{\varepsilon}(u_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{s})\cdot u_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x)\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}s\\
&\;\leqslant\;\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+t\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert b_{\varepsilon}(u_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{s})\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}s+\frac{1}{4t}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert u_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}s.\end{aligned}

(3.4)

Finally, take the supremum in
t
over
(0,T)
to conclude

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\;\leqslant\;\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+T\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert b_{\varepsilon}(u_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{s})\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}s.

This and (
3.4
) establish the inequality (
3.3
).

The strong energy estimate follows from squaring both sides of (
3.1
) and integration in space and time

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\partial_{t}u_{s}^{\varepsilon}-\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u_{s}^{\varepsilon})\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert b_{\varepsilon}(u_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{s})\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}s.

Note that, due to integration by parts,

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\cdot u_{s}^{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}s=2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\nabla u_{s}^{\varepsilon}:S(\nabla u_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\frac{2}{p}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\left\lVert\nabla u_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}\,\text{\rm d}s=\frac{2}{p}\left(\left\lVert\nabla u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}-\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}\right).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p}\left\lVert\nabla u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\partial_{t}u_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u_{s}^{\varepsilon})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\frac{2}{p}\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert b_{\varepsilon}(u_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{s})\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}s.

The claim (
3.2
) immediately follows after taking the supremum in
t
over
(0,T)
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The weak energy estimate (
3.3
) can be generalized to less restrictive assumptions on
b_{\varepsilon}
, e.g. it is sufficient to assume
b_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon}-w)\in\big(C_{t}^{0}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{p}_{t}W^{1,p}_{0,x}\big)^{\prime}
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
be satisfied. Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\;\leqslant\;4\left\lVert u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\frac{3}{p}\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+(3+8T)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert b_{\varepsilon}(u_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{t})\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}t.

(3.5)

Proof.

Due to (
3.3
) it holds

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\;\leqslant\;4\left\lVert u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}+8T\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert b_{\varepsilon}(u_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{t})\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}t.

(3.6)

The fundamental theorem of calculus and Hölder’s inequality reveal

\displaystyle\left\lVert u_{t}^{\varepsilon}-u_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}=\left\lVert\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{t}u_{r}^{\varepsilon}\,\text{\rm d}r\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\;\leqslant\;\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert\partial_{t}u_{r}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}r.

The estimate (
3.2
) bounds

\displaystyle\left[u^{\varepsilon}\right]_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\;\leqslant\;\frac{3}{p}\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+3\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert b_{\varepsilon}(u_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{t})\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}t.

(3.7)

Adding (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) verifies the claim.
∎

4.
Robustified a priori bounds for the mollified problem

Given a singular potential
b\in L^{2q}
, we have seen in the previous section that for a mollification
b_{\epsilon}=b*\rho_{\epsilon}
, we obtain a unique strong solution
u^{\varepsilon}
to

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u^{\epsilon}-\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u^{\epsilon})=b_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-w).\end{split}

(4.1)

In the following, we show that in harnessing the regularizing effect due to the perturbing path
w
, robust a priori bounds uniformly in
\epsilon>0
can be obtained. More precisely, we establish the a priori bound

\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}W^{1,p}_{x}}^{p}+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\;\leqslant\;C_{T,\Omega}(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}^{4}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}}^{2})

uniformly in
\epsilon>0
. Towards this end, we first show that the right-hand side of (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) can be robustified in the sense of the following identification.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
r\in[1,\infty)
and
\gamma>1/2
. Suppose
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is continuous and admits a local time
L
such that
L\in C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}
and
b\in L^{2q}
for
q\in[r,\infty)
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
fixed, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|b_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})|^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}r=(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{0,T}

(4.2)

where
\mathcal{I}
denotes the sewing of the germ

A^{\epsilon}_{s,t}=\int_{\Omega}(b_{\epsilon}^{2}*L_{s,t})(u^{\epsilon}_{s})\,\text{\rm d}x,

and where we used the shorthand notation
b_{\epsilon}^{2}(u):=|b_{\epsilon}(u)|^{2}
. Moreover, there holds the a priori bound

|(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{s,t}|\;\leqslant\;C_{\Omega}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}^{2}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}}(1+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}})|t-s|^{\gamma}.

Proof.

Recall that by Corollary
3.3
, we have
u^{\epsilon}\in C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
for
\epsilon>0
fixed. The first part of the claim is established similarly to Lemma
2.3
, the main difference being that we exploit the regularity gained from the local time
L
in order to obtain the a priori bound in the second part of the claim. Let us start by remarking that for
\epsilon>0
fixed,
A^{\epsilon}
does indeed admit a sewing as

\displaystyle|\delta A^{\epsilon}_{s,r,t}|

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\int_{\Omega}|(b_{\epsilon}^{2}*L_{r,t})(u^{\epsilon}_{s})-(b_{\epsilon}^{2}*L_{r,t})(u^{\epsilon}_{r})|\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert b_{\epsilon}^{2}*L_{r,t}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1}}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}_{r}-u^{\epsilon}_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert b_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{q}}\left\lVert L_{r,t}\right\rVert_{W^{1,q^{\prime}}}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}|r-s|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}^{2}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}|r-s|^{1/2}|t-r|^{\gamma},

where we exploited that due to continuity of
w
,
(L_{t})_{t}
is of compact support uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
and thus
\left\lVert L_{t}\right\rVert_{W^{1,q^{\prime}}}\lesssim\left\lVert L_{t}\right\rVert_{W^{1,r^{\prime}}}
. Moreover, note that for
\epsilon>0
fixed, we have

\displaystyle\left|A^{\epsilon}_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}b_{\epsilon}^{2}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}r\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}b^{2}_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{s}-w_{r})-b^{2}_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}r\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert b_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{C^{1}}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\int_{s}^{t}|r-s|^{1/2}\,\text{\rm d}r

\displaystyle=\left\lVert b_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{C^{1}}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}|t-s|^{3/2}.

Similar to Lemma
2.3
, we can thus conclude that indeed

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}b_{\epsilon}^{2}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}r=(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{0,T}

for any
\epsilon>0
fixed. Moreover, exploiting the a priori bounds that come with the Sewing Lemma
6.3
, we infer

|(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{s,t}|\;\leqslant\;|A^{\epsilon}_{s,t}|+|(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{s,t}-A^{\epsilon}_{s,t}|\lesssim\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}^{2}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}}(1+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}).

where we used that

|A^{\epsilon}_{s,t}|\lesssim\left\lVert b^{2}_{\epsilon}*L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}^{2}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}}|t-s|^{\gamma},

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The sewing enables the local time to regularize the interplay of
b_{\varepsilon}
and
u^{\varepsilon}
. In particular the square
\left\lvert b_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon}-w)\right\rvert^{2}
only acts on
b_{\varepsilon}
and not on
u^{\varepsilon}
. Therefore, it is possible to work in the
L^{1}_{x}
framework for
u^{\varepsilon}
. Indeed, a short inspection of the proof of Lemma
4.1
shows that Hölder regularity of
u^{\varepsilon}
as a
L^{1}_{x}
-valued function is sufficient for the identification. Moreover, upon replacing
u^{\epsilon}
by a generic function
v\in C^{\alpha}_{t}L^{1}_{x}
, the identification (
4.2
) holds provided
\alpha+\gamma>1
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
r\in[1,\infty)
and
\gamma>1/2
. Suppose
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is continuous and admits a local time
L
such that
L\in C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}
and
b\in L^{2q}
for
q\in[r,\infty)
. Let
u^{\epsilon}
be the unique solution to (
3.1
) of Theorem
3.1
associated to the mollification
b_{\epsilon}
of
b
. Then we have the a priori bound

\begin{split}&\left\lVert\nabla u^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+\left\lVert u^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2r}}^{4}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}}^{2}.\end{split}

(4.3)

Proof.

Plugging the a priori bound from the above Lemma
4.1
back into Corollary
3.3
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;4\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\frac{3}{p}\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+(3+8T)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert b_{\varepsilon}(u_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)-w_{t})\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+C_{T,\Omega}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2r}}^{2}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}}(1+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}).

An application of Lemma
6.7
with
a=\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}
,
K=\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+C_{T,\Omega}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}_{x}}^{2}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}_{x}}
and
C=C_{T,\Omega}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}_{x}}^{2}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}_{x}}
results in

\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+C_{T,\Omega}^{2}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}^{4}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}}^{2}

(4.4)

uniformly in
\epsilon>0
.

Note that similarly due to (
3.2
) in Theorem
3.1
this also implies

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla u^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}^{p}\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}^{4}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}}^{2}

uniformly in
\epsilon>0
.
∎

Remark 4.4

(Refined a priori bounds)
.

We want to stress that even stronger a piori bounds than (
4.3
) are available. Indeed, the a priori bounds derived in Theorem
3.1
carry over to the robustified formulation, i.e., uniformly in
\varepsilon>0
it holds

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left\lVert u^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathrm{div}S(\nabla u^{\varepsilon})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\\
&\hskip 30.00005pt\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}+\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}^{4}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}}^{2}.\end{aligned}

(4.5)

Therefore, we construct robustified solutions in the sense of Definition
2.2
. In particular, the right hand side of (
2.5
) is weakly differentiable in

. However, it is non trivial to identify the derivative. For smooth potentials an identification is possible as presented in Lemma
2.4
(

).

5.
Passage to the limit

In the following, we show that exploiting the a priori bounds obtained in the previous section allows a passage to the limit on the level of the robustified formulation. Towards this end, we will exploit monotonicity of the
p
-Laplace operator as well as suitable function space embeddings.

Since
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain we can use the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem to conclude
W^{1,p}_{0,x}\hookrightarrow L^{1}_{x}
compactly. Therefore, applying the Aubin-Lions Lemma
6.6
in the form of (

) with

and
B=Y=L^{1}_{x}
, yields

\displaystyle L^{\infty}_{t}W^{1,p}_{x}\cap C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{1}_{x}\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow C_{t}L^{1}_{x}.

(5.1)

Hence, the a priori bounds (
4.4
) and (
4.3
) also yield a subsequence still denoted
(u^{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
converging strongly in
C_{t}L^{1}_{x}
. We denote the corresponding limit by
u
. In the following, we show that the strong convergence
u^{\epsilon}\to u
in
C_{t}L^{1}_{x}
allows to identify
u
as a solution to (
1.5
) in the robustified sense of Definition
2.2
meaning
u
satisfies

u_{t}-u_{s}-\int_{s}^{t}\mbox{div}S(\nabla u_{r})\,\text{\rm d}r=(\mathcal{I}A^{u})_{s,t}

where

. In particular, we need to establish convergence of the corresponding sewing terms on the right hand side. This point will be addressed in the next Lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
r\in[1,\infty)
and
\gamma>1/2
. Suppose
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is continuous and admits a local time
L
such that
L\in C^{0,\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}_{x}
and
b\in L^{2q}
for
q\in[r,\infty)
. Let
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}W^{1,p}_{x}\cap C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{1}_{x}
be as above, i.e. in particular such that
\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}-u\right\rVert_{C_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\to 0
. Then, for

A^{u}_{s,t}=(b*L_{s,t})(u_{s}),\qquad A^{u^{\epsilon}}_{s,t}=(b_{\epsilon}*L_{s,t})(u^{\epsilon}_{s})

we have
\left\lVert\mathcal{I}A^{u}-\mathcal{I}A^{u^{\epsilon}}\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\to 0
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left\lVert A^{u}_{s,t}-A^{u^{\epsilon}}_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert(b-b_{\epsilon})*L_{s,t}(u^{\epsilon}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}}+\left\lVert(b*L_{s,t})(u^{\epsilon}_{s})-(b*L_{s,t})(u_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\lesssim\left\lVert b-b_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}\left\lVert L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{(2q)^{\prime}}}+\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2r}}\left\lVert L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{W^{1,(2r)^{\prime}}}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}-u\right\rVert_{C_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\lesssim\left\lVert b-b_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}\left\lVert L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{r^{\prime}}}+\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}\left\lVert L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{W^{1,r^{\prime}}}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}-u\right\rVert_{C_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\lesssim|t-s|^{\gamma}\left(\left\lVert b-b_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}}+\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}^{2}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}-u\right\rVert_{C_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\right)\end{aligned}

(5.2)

where we exploited again that due to the compact support of
L
, we have
\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{L^{(2r)^{\prime}}}\lesssim\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{L^{r^{\prime}}}
and similarly for the corresponding Sobolev scales. Moreover, we have accordingly

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\lVert(\delta A^{u^{\epsilon}})_{s,r,t}\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}}&=\left\lVert(b_{\epsilon}*L_{r,t})(u^{\epsilon}_{r})-(b_{\epsilon}*L_{r,t})(u^{\epsilon}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert b_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}\left\lVert L_{r,t}\right\rVert_{W^{1,r^{\prime}}}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}_{s}-u^{\epsilon}_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{L^{2q}}\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma}_{t}W^{1,r^{\prime}}}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0,1/2}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}|t-s|^{\gamma+1/2}.\end{aligned}

(5.3)

The claim now follows from Lemma
6.4
.
∎

Identifying the limit in the other nonlinear term in the robustified formulation of definition
2.2
is now a classical consequence from Minty’s Lemma

. Overall, we have thus established the existence part of Theorem

.

Lemma 5.2

Local time and occupation times formula

We recall for the reader the basic concepts of occupation measures, local times and the occupation times formula. A comprehensive review paper on these topics is
[
GH80
]
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a measurable path. Then the occupation measure at time
t\in[0,T]
, written
\mu^{w}_{t}
is the Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

\mu^{w}_{t}(A):=\lambda(\{s\in[0,t]:\ w_{s}\in A\}),\quad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\lambda
denotes the standard Lebesgue measure.

The occupation measure thus measures how much time the process
w
spends in certain Borel sets. Provided for any
t\in[0,T]
, the measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we call the corresponding Radon-Nikodym derivative local time of the process
w
:

Definition 6.2
.

Let
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a measurable path. Assume that there exists a measurable function
L^{w}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\mu^{w}_{t}(A)=\int_{A}L^{w}_{t}(z)dz,

for any
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
t\in[0,T]
. Then we call
L^{w}
local time of
w
.

Note that by the definition of the occupation measure, we have for any bounded measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
that

\int_{0}^{t}f(w_{s})ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(z)\mu^{w}_{t}(dz).

(6.10)

The above equation (
6.10
) is called occupation times formula. Remark that in particular, provided
w
admits a local time, we also have for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

\int_{0}^{t}f(u-w_{s})ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u-z)\mu^{w}_{t}(dz)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u-z)L^{w}_{t}(z)dz=(f*L^{w}_{t})(u).

(6.11)

Sewing Lemma

Let us recall the Sewing Lemma due to
[
Gub04
]
(see also
[
FH14
, Lemma 4.2]
). Let
E
be a Banach space,
[0,T]
a given interval. Let
\Delta_{n}
denote the
n
-th simplex of
[0,T]
, i.e.

Lemma 6.5

(Minty’s Lemma/ Monotonicity trick
[
Zei90a
, (25.4b)]
)
.

Let

\displaystyle u^{n}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup u\quad in\ X

\displaystyle Au^{n}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\xi\quad in\ X^{*}

\displaystyle\langle Au^{n},u^{n}\rangle_{X^{*}\times X}

\displaystyle\to\langle\xi,u\rangle_{X^{*}\times X}

we may conclude
Au=\xi
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let

such that

\displaystyle a^{2}\;\leqslant\;K+Ca.

\displaystyle\frac{C}{2}-\sqrt{K+\frac{C^{2}}{4}}\;\leqslant\;a\;\leqslant\;\frac{C}{2}+\sqrt{K+\frac{C^{2}}{4}}.

The assertion is verified.

A primal-dual perspective for distributed TD-learning
Han-Dong Lim, Donghwan Lee

Abstract
The goal of this paper is to investigate distributed temporal difference (TD) learning for a networked multi-agent Markov decision process. The proposed approach is based on distributed optimization algorithms, which can be interpreted as primal-dual ordinary differential equation (ODE) dynamics subject to null-space constraints. Based on the exponential convergence behavior of the primal-dual ODE dynamics subject to null-space constraints, we examine the behavior of the final iterate in various distributed TD-learning scenarios, considering both constant and diminishing step-sizes and incorporating both i.i.d. and Markovian observation models. Unlike existing methods, the proposed algorithm does not require the assumption that the underlying communication network structure is characterized by a doubly stochastic matrix.



1
Introduction

Temporal-difference (TD) learning
(
Sutton 1988
)
aims to solve the policy evaluation problem in Markov decision processes (MDPs), serving as the foundational pillar for many reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms
(
Mnih et al. 2015
)
. Following the empirical success of RL in various fields
(
Kober et al. 2013
;
Li et al. 2019
)
, theoretical exploration of TD-learning has become an active area of research. For instance,
Tsitsiklis and Van Roy 1996
studied the asymptotic convergence of TD-learning, while non-asymptotic analysis has been examined in
Bhandari et al. 2018
;
Srikant and Ying 2019
;
Lee and Kim 2022
.

In contrast to the single-agent case, the theoretical understanding for TD-learning for networked multi-agent Markov decision processes (MAMDPs) has not been fully explored so far. In the networked MAMDPs, each agent follows its own policy and receives different local rewards while sharing their local learning parameters through communication networks. Under this scenario, several distributed TD-learning algorithms
(
Wang et al. 2020
;
Doan et al. 2019
;
Doan et al. 2021
;
Sun et al. 2020
;
Zeng et al. 2022
)
have been developed based on distributed optimization frameworks
(
Nedic and Ozdaglar 2009
;
Pu and Nedić 2021
)
.

The main goal of this paper is to provide finite-time analysis of a distributed TD-learning algorithm for networked MAMDPs from the perspectives of the primal-dual algorithms
(
Wang and Elia 2011
;
Mokhtari and Ribeiro 2016
)
. The proposed algorithms are inspired by the control system model for distributed optimization problems
(
Wang and Elia 2011
;
Lee 2023
)
, and at the same time, it can also be interpreted as the primal-dual gradient dynamics in
Qu and Li 2018
. In this respect, we first study finite-time analysis of continuous-time primal-dual gradient dynamics in
Qu and Li 2018
with special nullity structures on the system matrix. Based on the analysis of primal-dual gradient dynamics, we further provide a finite-time analysis of the proposed distributed TD-learning under both i.i.d. observation and Markov observation models. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

1.

An improved or comparable to the state of art convergence rate for continuous-time primal-dual gradient dynamics
(
Qu and Li 2018
)
with null-space constraints under specific conditions: the results can be applied to general classes of distributed optimization problems that can be reformulated as saddle-point problems
(
Wang and Elia 2011
;
Mokhtari and Ribeiro 2016
)
;

2.

Development of new distributed TD-learning algorithm inspired by
Wang and Elia 2011
;
Lee 2023
, which does not require a double stochastic matrix. This offers a significant advantage in specific scenarios, such as wireless ad hoc networks or broadcast-based communication, where node degrees (number of neighbours) are often unknown due to factors like message loss during transmission
(
Hendrickx and Tsitsiklis 2015
)
. This uncertainty makes it challenging to construct a doubly stochastic matrix, as most existing methods rely on precise knowledge of node degrees. In contrast, our algorithm does not require such additional information and thus remains effective in these environments;

3.

New mean-squared error bounds of the distributed TD-learning under our consideration for both i.i.d. and Markovian observation models and under various conditions of the step-sizes: the distributed TD-learning is based on the control system model in
Wang and Elia 2011
;
Lee 2023
which does not require doubly stochastic matrix corresponding to its associated network graph. Note that the doubly stochastic assumption is required in other distributed TD-learning algorithms based on the classical distributed optimization algorithms
(
Nedic and Ozdaglar 2009
;
Pu and Nedić 2021
)
;

4.

Empirical demonstrations of both the convergence and the rate of convergence of the algorithm are provided.

Related Works.

Distributed optimization has been an active research field. In this context,
Nedic and Ozdaglar 2009
investigated a distributed optimization algorithm over a communication network whose structure graph is represented by a doubly stochastic matrix. In this approach, each agent exchanges information with its neighbors, with the exchange being weighted by the corresponding element in the doubly stochastic matrix. Meanwhile,
Wang and Elia 2011
;
Notarnicola et al. 2023
provided control system approach to study distributed optimization problem.

The asymptotic convergence of distributed TD-learning has been studied in
Mathkar and Borkar 2016
;
Stanković et al. 2023
.
Doan et al. 2019
provided finite-time analysis of distributed TD-learning based on the distributed optimization algorithm
(
Nedic and Ozdaglar 2009
)
with i.i.d. observation model. Their analysis was extended to the Markovian observation model
(
Doan et al. 2021
)
.
Sun et al. 2020
studied distributed TD-learning based on
Nedic and Ozdaglar 2009
with the Markovian observation model using multi-step Lyapunov function
(
Wang et al. 2019
)
.
Wang et al. 2020
studied distributed TD-learning motivated by the gradient tracking method
(
Pu and Nedić 2021
)
.
Zeng et al. 2022
studied finite-time behavior of distributed stochastic approximation algorithms
(
Robbins and Monro 1951
)
with general mapping including TD-learning and Q-learning, using Lyapunov-Razumikhin function
(
Zhou and Luo 2018
)
.

In the context of policy evaluation,
Macua et al. 2014
;
Lee et al. 2018
;
Wai et al. 2018
;
Cassano et al. 2020
studied distributed versions of gradient-TD
(
Sutton et al. 2009
)
. The Gradient-TD method can be reformulated as saddle-point problem
(
Macua et al. 2014
;
Lee et al. 2022
)
, and the aforementioned works can be understood as distributed optimization over a saddle-point problem
(
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
)
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Markov decision process

Markov decision process (MDP) consists of five tuples
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}},\gamma,{\mathcal{P}},r)
, where
{\mathcal{S}}:=\{1,2,\dots,|{\mathcal{S}}|\}
is the collection of states,
{\mathcal{A}}
is the collection of actions,
\gamma\in(0,1)
is the discount factor,
{\mathcal{P}}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to[0,1]
is the transition kernel, and
r:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}
is the reward function. If action
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
is chosen at state
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, the transition to state
s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}
occurs with probability
{\mathcal{P}}(s,a,s^{\prime})
, and incurs reward
r(s,a,s^{\prime})
. Given a stochastic policy
\pi:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to[0,1]
, the quantity
\pi(a\mid s)
denotes the probability of taking action
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
at state
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
. We will denote
{\mathcal{P}}^{\pi}(s,s^{\prime}):=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathcal{P}}(s,a,s^{\prime})\pi(a\mid s)
, and
{\mathcal{R}}^{\pi}(s):=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}{\mathcal{P}}(s,a,s^{\prime})\pi(a\mid s)r(s,a,s^{\prime})
, which is the transition probability from state
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
to
s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}
under policy
\pi
, and expected reward at state
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, respectively.
d:{\mathcal{S}}\to[0,1]
denotes the stationary distribution of the state
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
under policy
\pi
. The policy evaluation problem aims to estimate the expected sum of discounted rewards following policy
\pi
, the so-called the value function,
v^{\pi}(s)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}r(s_{k},a_{k},s_{k+1})\middle|s_{0}=s,\pi\right]
for
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Given a feature function
\bm{\phi}:{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
, our aim is to estimate the value function through learnable parameter
{\bm{\theta}}
, i.e.,
v^{\pi}(s)\approx\bm{\phi}(s)^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}
, for
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, which can be achieved through solving the optimization problem,
\min_{{\bm{\theta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\bm{R}}^{\pi}+\gamma{\bm{P}}^{\pi}{\bm{\Phi}}{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\Phi}}{\bm{\theta}}\right\|^{2}_{{\bm{D}}^{\pi}}
, where
{\bm{D}}^{\pi}
is a diagonal matrix whose elements are
d(1),d(2),\dots,d(|{\mathcal{S}}|)
,
{\bm{P}}^{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{S}}|\times|{\mathcal{S}}|}
whose elements are
[{\bm{P}}^{\pi}]_{ij}:={\mathcal{P}}^{\pi}(i,j)
for
i,j\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
{\bm{R}}^{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{S}}|}
,
[{\bm{R}}^{\pi}]_{i}:=\mathbb{E}\left[r(s,a,s^{\prime})\middle|s=i\right]
for
i\in{\mathcal{S}}
, and
{\bm{\Phi}}:=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\phi}(1)&\bm{\phi}(2)&\cdots&\bm{\phi}(|{\mathcal{S}}|)\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{S}}|\times q}
. The solution of the optimization problem satisfies the so-called projected Bellman equation
(
Sutton et al. 2009
)
:

\displaystyle{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}{\bm{D}}^{\pi}{\bm{\Phi}}{\bm{\theta}}={\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}{\bm{D}}^{\pi}{\bm{R}}^{\pi}+\gamma{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}{\bm{D}}^{\pi}{\bm{P}}^{\pi}{\bm{\Phi}}{\bm{\theta}}.

Throughout the paper, we adopt the common assumption on the feature matrix, which is widely used in the literature
(
Bhandari et al. 2018
;
Wang et al. 2020
)
.

Assumption 1
.

\left\|\bm{\phi}(s)\right\|_{2}\leq 1
for all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\bm{\Phi}}
is full-column rank matrix.

2.2
Multi-agent MDP

Multi-agent Markov decision process (MAMDP) considers a set of agents cooperatively computing the value function for a shared environment. Considering
N
agents, each agent can be denoted by
i\in{\mathcal{V}}:=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, and the agents communicate over networks that can be described by a connected and undirected simple graph
{\mathcal{G}}:=({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{E}})
, where
{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{V}}\times{\mathcal{V}}
is the set of edges.
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
denotes the neighbour of agent
i\in{\mathcal{V}}
, i.e.,
j\in{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
if and only if
(i,j)\in{\mathcal{E}}
for
i,j\in{\mathcal{V}}
. Each agent
i\in{\mathcal{V}}
has its local policy
\pi^{i}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\to[0,1]
, where
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
is the action space of agent
i
, and receives reward following its local reward function
r^{i}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}
where
{\mathcal{A}}:=\Pi^{N}_{i=1}{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
. MAMDP consists of five tuples
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}},\gamma,{\mathcal{P}},\{r^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})
, where
{\mathcal{P}}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to[0,1]
is the Markov transition kernel. The agents share the same state
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, and when action
{\bm{a}}:=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N})\in{\mathcal{A}}
is taken, the state transits to
s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}
with probability
{\mathcal{P}}(s,{\bm{a}},s^{\prime})
, and for
i\in{\mathcal{V}}
, agent
i
receives
r^{i}(s,{\bm{a}},s^{\prime})
. The aim of the policy evaluation under MAMDP is to estimate the expected sum of discounted rewards averaged over
N
agents, i.e.,
v^{\pi}(s)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\gamma^{k}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r^{i}(s_{k},{\bm{a}},s_{k+1})\right]
, for
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
. While learning, each agent
i\in{\cal V}
can share its learning parameter over the communication network with its neighboring agents
j\in{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
. Following the spirit of single-agent MDP, the aim of each agent is now to compute the solution of the following equation:

\displaystyle{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}{\bm{D}}^{\pi}{\bm{\Phi}}{\bm{\theta}}={\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}{\bm{D}}^{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bm{R}}^{\pi}_{i}+\gamma{\bm{P}}^{\pi}{\bm{\Phi}}{\bm{\theta}}\right),

(1)

where
{\bm{R}}^{\pi}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{S}}|}
for
i\in{\mathcal{V}}
, whose elements are
[{\bm{R}}^{\pi}_{i}]_{j}=\mathbb{E}\left[r^{i}(s,{\bm{a}},s^{\prime})\mid s=j\right]
for
j\in{\mathcal{S}}
. The equation (
1
) admits a unique solution
{\bm{\theta}}_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
, given by

\displaystyle{\bm{\theta}}_{c}=({\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}{\bm{D}}^{\pi}({\bm{\Phi}}-\gamma{\bm{P}}^{\pi}{\bm{\Phi}}))^{-1}{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}{\bm{D}}^{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bm{R}}^{\pi}_{i}\right).

(2)

Note that the solution corresponds to the value function associated with the global reward
\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\gamma^{k}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r^{i}(s_{k},{\bm{a}}_{k},s_{k+1})
. Moreover, we will denote, for
1\leq i\leq N
,

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}:=

\displaystyle\gamma{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}{\bm{D}}^{\pi}{\bm{\Phi}}-{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}{\bm{D}}^{\pi}{\bm{P}}^{\pi}{\bm{\Phi}},\quad{\bm{b}}_{i}:={\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}{\bm{D}}^{\pi}{\bm{R}}^{\pi}_{i},

(3)

and
w:=\lambda_{\min}({\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}{\bm{D}}^{\pi}{\bm{\Phi}})
. The bound on the reward will be denoted by a positive constant
R_{\max}\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,
|r^{i}(s,{\bm{a}},s^{\prime})|\leq R_{\max},\;1\leq i\leq N,\forall{s,{\bm{a}},s^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{S}}
.

3
Analysis of primal-dual gradient dynamics

The so-called primal-dual gradient dynamics
(
Arrow et al. 1958
)
will be the key tool for the analysis of the proposed distributed TD-learning. The analysis provided in this section will serve as the foundation for the subsequent analysis in Section
4
. This section establishes exponential convergent behavior of the primal-dual gradient dynamics in terms of the Lyapunov method. To this end, let us consider the following constrained optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{{\bm{\theta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\quad

\displaystyle f({\bm{\theta}})\quad\text{such that}\quad{\bm{M}}{\bm{\theta}}=\bm{0}_{n},

(4)

where
{\bm{\theta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
{\bm{M}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a differentiable, smooth, and strongly convex function
(
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
)
. One of the popular approaches for solving (
4
) is to formulate it into the saddle-point problem
(
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
)
,
L({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{w}})=\min_{{\bm{\theta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{{\bm{w}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f({\bm{\theta}})+{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{M}}{\bm{\theta}}),
whose solution,
{\bm{\theta}}^{*},{\bm{w}}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, exists and is unique when
{\bm{M}}
has full-column rank
(
Qu and Li 2018
)
. If
{\bm{M}}
is rank-deficient, i.e., it is not full-column rank, there exists multiple
{\bm{w}}^{*}
solving the saddle-point problem.
It is known that its solution
{\bm{\theta}}^{*},{\bm{w}}^{*}
can be obtained by investigating the solution
{\bm{\theta}}_{t},{\bm{w}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the so-called primal-dual gradient dynamics
(
Qu and Li 2018
)
, with initial points
{\bm{\theta}}_{0},{\bm{w}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\dot{{\bm{\theta}}}_{t}=

\displaystyle-\nabla f({\bm{\theta}}_{t})-{\bm{M}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}_{t},\quad\dot{{\bm{w}}}_{t}={\bm{M}}{\bm{\theta}}_{t}.

Qu and Li 2018
studied exponential stability of the primal-dual gradient dynamics when
{\bm{M}}
is full column-rank, using the classical Lyapunov approach
(
Sontag 2013
)
. However, the proof relies on the invertibility of
{\bm{M}}
, and cannot be extended to the case when
{\bm{M}}
is rank-deficient. As for such case,
Ozaslan and Jovanović 2023
;
Cisneros-Velarde et al. 2020
;
Gokhale et al. 2023
proved exponential convergence to a particular solution
{\bm{\theta}}^{*},{\bm{w}}^{*}
using the tools based on singular value decomposition
(
Horn and Johnson 2012
)
. In this paper, we will consider the following particular scenarios:

1.

\nabla f({\bm{\theta}}_{t})={\bm{U}}{\bm{\theta}}_{t}
, where
{\bm{U}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, which is not necessarily symmetric but positive definite matrix, i.e.,
{\bm{U}}+{\bm{U}}^{\top}\succ 0
;

2.

{\bm{M}}
is symmetric and rank-deficient. Distributed algorithms are typical examples satisfying such condition and will be elaborated in subsequent sections.

We note that previous works considered general matrix
{\bm{M}}
, not necessarily a symmetric matrix. Moreover, note that the primal-dual gradient dynamics under such scenarios will appear in Section
4
as an ODE model of the proposed distributed TD-learning. The corresponding system can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\theta}}_{t}\\
{\bm{w}}_{t}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-{\bm{U}}&-{\bm{M}}^{\top}\\
{\bm{M}}&\bm{0}_{n\times n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\theta}}_{t}\\
{\bm{w}}_{t}\end{bmatrix},\quad{\bm{\theta}}_{0},{\bm{w}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(5)

To study its exponential stability, let us introduce the Lyapunov function candidate
V({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{w}})=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\theta}}\\
{\bm{M}}{\bm{M}}^{\dagger}{\bm{w}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}{\bm{S}}\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\theta}}\\
{\bm{M}}{\bm{M}}^{\dagger}{\bm{w}}\end{bmatrix}
, where
{\bm{S}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n\times 2n}
is some symmetric positive definite matrix, and
{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{w}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The candidate Lyapunov function considers projection of the iterate
{\bm{w}}_{t}
to the range space of
{\bm{M}}
. As in previous works, the difficulty coming from singularity of
{\bm{M}}
can be avoided by considering the range space and null space conditions of
{\bm{M}}
. In particular,
Ozaslan and Jovanović 2023
employed a Lyapunov function that involves the gradient of the Lagrangian function, and considered the projected iterate
{\bm{M}}{\bm{M}}^{\dagger}{\bm{w}}_{t}
, where
{\bm{M}}{\bm{M}}^{\dagger}
is the projection matrix onto range space of
{\bm{M}}
.
Cisneros-Velarde et al. 2020
exploited a quadratic Lyapunov function in
Qu and Li 2018
for the iterate
{\bm{\theta}}_{t}
and
{\bm{V}}{\bm{w}}_{t}
, where
{\bm{M}}:={\bm{T}}\bm{\Sigma}{\bm{V}}^{\top}
, which is the singular value decomposition of
{\bm{M}}
.
Gokhale et al. 2023
considered a positive semi-definite matrix
{\bm{S}}
and used semi-contraction theory
(
De Pasquale et al. 2023
)
to prove exponential convergence of the primal-dual gradient dynamics.

In this paper, we will adopt the quadratic Lyapunov function in
Qu and Li 2018
with the projected iterate
{\bm{M}}{\bm{M}}^{\dagger}{\bm{w}}_{t}
, and leverage the symmetric property of
{\bm{M}}
to show improved or comparable to the state of art convergence rate under the particular conditions newly imposed in this paper. In particular, when
{\bm{M}}
is symmetric, the fact that the projection onto the column space of
{\bm{M}}
and row space of
{\bm{M}}
being identical simplifies the overall bounds. We first present the following Lyapunov inequality.

Lemma 2
.

Let
{\bm{S}}:=\begin{bmatrix}\beta{\bm{I}}_{n}&{\bm{M}}\\
{\bm{M}}&\beta{\bm{I}}_{n}\end{bmatrix}
where
\beta:=\max\left\{\frac{2\lambda_{\max}({\bm{M}})^{2}+2+\left\|{\bm{U}}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{\min}({\bm{U}}+{\bm{U}}^{\top})},4\lambda_{\max}({\bm{M}})\right\}
. Then,
\frac{\beta}{2}{\bm{I}}_{2n}\prec{\bm{S}}\prec 2\beta{\bm{I}}_{2n}
, and we have, for any
{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{w}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\theta}}\\
{\bm{M}}{\bm{M}}^{\dagger}{\bm{w}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}{\bm{S}}\begin{bmatrix}-{\bm{U}}&-{\bm{M}}\\
{\bm{M}}&\bm{0}_{n\times n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\theta}}\\
{\bm{M}}{\bm{M}}^{\dagger}{\bm{w}}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\min\{1,\lambda^{+}_{\min}({\bm{M}})^{2}\}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\theta}}\\
{\bm{M}}{\bm{M}}^{\dagger}{\bm{w}}\end{bmatrix}\right\|^{2}_{2}.

The proof is given in

1

The Appendix can be found in
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.00638
Appendix Section
C.1
. Using the above Lemma
2
, we can now prove the exponential stability of the ODE dynamics in (
5
).

Theorem 3
.

Let
V({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{w}})=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\theta}}\\
{\bm{M}}{\bm{M}}^{\dagger}{\bm{w}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}{\bm{S}}\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\theta}}\\
{\bm{M}}{\bm{M}}^{\dagger}{\bm{w}}\end{bmatrix}
. For
{\bm{\theta}}_{0},{\bm{w}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have

\displaystyle V({\bm{\theta}}_{t},{\bm{w}}_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{\min\{1,\lambda^{+}_{\min}({\bm{M}})^{2}\}}{\max\left\{\frac{2\lambda_{\max}({\bm{M}})^{2}+2+\left\|{\bm{U}}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{\min}({\bm{U}}+{\bm{U}}^{\top})},4\lambda_{\max}({\bm{M}})\right\}}t\right)\right).

. We show that the above bound enjoys sharper or comparable to the state of the art convergence rate under particular conditions. With slight modifications, the Lyapunov function becomes identical to that of
Gokhale et al. 2023
. However, we directly rely on classical Lyapunov theory
(
Khalil 2015
)
rather than the result from semi-contraction theory
(
De Pasquale et al. 2023
)
used in
Gokhale et al. 2023
. The classical Lyapunov approach simplifies the proof steps compared to that of semi-contraction theory. The detailed comparative analysis is in Appendix Section

. The fact that
{\bm{M}}
is symmetric and considering the projected iterate
{\bm{M}}{\bm{M}}^{\dagger}{\bm{w}}_{t}
, provides improved and comparable bound. Furthermore, as will be clear in Section

, this enables us to extend the analysis to stochastic algorithms (TD-learning) without introducing involved analysis including (semi)-contraction theory or intricate Lyapunov function.

4
Distributed TD-learning

In this section, we propose a new distributed TD-learning algorithm to solve (
1
) based on the result in
Wang and Elia 2011
.
In this scenario, each agent keeps its own parameter estimate
{\bm{\theta}}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{q},\;1\leq i\leq N
, and the goal of each agent is to estimate the value function
v^{\pi}(s)\approx\bm{\phi}(s)^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}_{c}
satisfying (
1
) (the value function associated with the global reward
\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\gamma^{k}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r^{i}
) under the assumption that each agent has access only to its local reward
r^{i}
. The parameter of each agent can be shared over the communication network whose structure is represented by the graph
{\mathcal{G}}
, i.e., agents can share their parameters only with their neighbors over the network to solve the global problem. The connections among the agents can be represented by graph Laplacian matrix
(
Anderson Jr and Morley 1985
)
,
{\bm{L}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{S}}|\times|{\mathcal{S}}|}
, which characterizes the graph
{\mathcal{G}}
, i.e.,
[{\bm{L}}]_{ij}=-1
if
(i,j)\in{\mathcal{E}}
and
[{\bm{L}}]_{ij}=0
if
(i,j)\notin{\mathcal{E}}
, and
[{\bm{L}}]_{ii}=|{\mathcal{N}}_{i}|
for
i\in{\mathcal{V}}
. Note that
{\bm{L}}
is symmetric positive semi-definite matrix and
{\bm{L}}\bm{1}_{|{\mathcal{S}}|}=0
. To proceed, let us first introduce a set of matrix notations:

\displaystyle\bar{{\bm{L}}}:={\bm{L}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{q},\quad\bar{{\bm{D}}}^{\pi}:={\bm{I}}_{N}\otimes{\bm{D}}^{\pi},\quad\bar{{\bm{P}}}^{\pi}:={\bm{I}}_{N}\otimes{\bm{P}}^{\pi},

\displaystyle\bar{{\bm{R}}}^{\pi}=\begin{bmatrix}({\bm{R}}^{\pi}_{1})^{\top}&({\bm{R}}^{\pi}_{2})^{\top}&\cdots&({\bm{R}}^{\pi}_{N})^{\top}\end{bmatrix}^{\top},\quad\bar{{\bm{\Phi}}}:={\bm{I}}_{N}\otimes{\bm{\Phi}},

\displaystyle\bar{{\bm{A}}}={\bm{I}}_{N}\otimes{\bm{A}},\quad\bar{{\bm{b}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{b}}_{1}\\
{\bm{b}}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
{\bm{b}}_{N}\end{bmatrix},\quad\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\theta}}^{1}\\
{\bm{\theta}}^{2}\\
\vdots\\
{\bm{\theta}}^{N}\end{bmatrix},\quad\bar{{\bm{w}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{w}}^{1}\\
{\bm{w}}^{2}\\
\vdots\\
{\bm{w}}^{N}\end{bmatrix},

where
\otimes
denotes Kronecker product, and
\bar{{\bm{w}}}
is another collection of learnable parameters
\{{\bm{w}}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, where
{\bm{w}}^{i}
assigned to each agent
i
and
{\bm{b}}_{i}
is defined in (
3
).

Meanwhile,
Wang and Elia 2011

We are now in position to provide the first main result, a finite-time analysis of Algorithm

under the i.i.d. observation model, which is a common assumption in the literature, and provides simple and clean theoretical insights.

Theorem 5

1.

Suppose we use constant step-size
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{k}

. Using constant step-size, we can guarantee exponential convergence rate with small bias term

when

and

\eta

. Furthermore, the algorithm’s performance is closely tied to the properties of the graph structure.
\lambda^{+}_{\min}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})
, the smallest non-zero eigenvalue of graph Laplacian, characterizes the connectivity of the graph
Chung 1997
, and a graph with lower connectivity will yield slower convergence rate and larger bias.
\lambda_{\max}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})
is the largest eigenvalue of the graph Laplacian, and it can be upper bounded by twice the maximum degree of the graph
(
Anderson Jr and Morley 1985
)
. That is, a graph with higher maximum degree could incur slower convergence rate and larger bias. However, compared to
\lambda_{\min}^{+}({\bm{M}})
, we experimentally verify in Section
5
that

does not appear to be an important factor under particular cases, and there could exist a tighter bound without
\lambda_{\max}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})

convergence rate from the second item in Theorem

, and similar observations hold as in the constant step-size, i.e., the convergence rate depends on the smallest non-zero and maximum eigenvalue of graph Laplacian. Lastly, as in
Wang et al. 2020
, our bound does not explicitly depend on the number of agents,
N

Doan et al. 2019
and
Sun et al. 2020
, where the bias term and convergence rate scale at the order of
N
.

Furthermore, the known constant error bound for (single-agent) TD-learning, which is Theorem 2 of
Bhandari et al. 2018
is
O\left(\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{4}w^{2}}\right)

O\left(\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{3}w^{3}}\right)

\left\|\theta_{c}\right\|_{2}\leq O\left(\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)w}\right)

4.2
Markovian observation case

In this section, we consider the Markovian observation model, where the sequence of observations
\{s_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
follows a Markov chain. Compared to the i.i.d. observation model, the correlation between the observation and the updated iterates imposes difficulty in the analysis. To overcome this issue, an assumption on the Markov chain that ensures a geometric mixing property is helpful. In particular, the so-called ergodic Markov chain can be characterized by the metric called total variation distance
(
Levin and Peres 2017
)
,
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{S}}}|P(x)-Q(x)|,

where
P
and
Q
is probability measure on
{\mathcal{S}}
. A Markov chain is said to be ergodic if it is irreducible and aperiodic
(
Levin and Peres 2017
)
. An ergodic Markov chain is known to converge to its unique stationary exponentially fast, i.e., for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
\sup_{1\leq i\leq|{\mathcal{S}}|}d_{\mathrm{TV}}({\bm{e}}_{i}^{\top}({\bm{P}}^{\pi})^{k},\mu_{\infty})\leq m\rho^{k},

where
{\bm{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{S}}|}
for
1\leq i\leq N
is the
|{\mathcal{S}}|
-dimensional vector whose
i
-th element is one and others are zero,
\mu_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{S}}|}
is the stationary distribution of the Markov chain induced by transition matrix
{\bm{P}}^{\pi}
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
is a positive constant, and
\rho\in(0,1)
. The assumption on the geometric mixing property of the Markov chain is common in the literature
(
Srikant and Ying 2019
;
Wang et al. 2020
)
. The mixing time of Markov chain is an important quantity of a Markov chain, defined as

\displaystyle\tau(\delta):=\min\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid m\rho^{k}\leq\delta\}.

(14)

For simplicity, we will use
\tau:=\tau(\alpha_{T})
, where
T\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
denotes the total number of iterations, and
\alpha_{k}
, is the step-size at
k
-th iteration. If we use the step-size
\alpha_{k}=\frac{1}{1+k}
, the mixing time
\tau
only contributes to the logarithmic factor,
\log T
in the finite-time bound
(
Bhandari et al. 2018
)
. As in the proof of i.i.d. case, using the Lypaunov argument in Lemma
4
, we can prove the finite-time bound on the mean-squared error, following the spirit of
Srikant and Ying 2019
. To simplify the proof, we will investigate the case
\eta=1
.

Theorem 6
.

1.

Suppose we use constant step-size
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{T}
such that
\alpha_{0}\leq\bar{\alpha}
for some positive constant
\bar{\alpha}\in(0,1)
.
Then, we have, for
\tau\leq k\leq T
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k+1}\\
\bar{{\bm{L}}}\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{\dagger}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}_{k+1}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{(1-\gamma)w\min\{1,\lambda_{\min}^{+}({\bm{L}})^{2}\}}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{L}})^{2}}\alpha_{0}(k-\tau)\right)\right)

\displaystyle+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\alpha_{0}\tau\frac{R^{2}_{\max}}{w^{3}(1-\gamma)^{3}}\frac{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{L}})^{2}}{\min\{1,\lambda_{\min}^{+}({\bm{L}})^{2}\}}\right).

2.

Considering diminishing step-size, with
\alpha_{k}=\frac{h_{1}}{k+h_{2}}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}

\bar{h}_{1}
and
\bar{h}_{2}

h_{1}=\Theta(\bar{h}_{1})
and
h_{2}=\Theta(\bar{h}_{2})
,
we have for
\tau\leq k\leq T

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k+1}\\

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\tau}{k}\frac{qR_{\max}^{2}}{w^{4}(1-\gamma)^{4}}\frac{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{L}})^{5}}{\min\{1,\lambda_{\min}^{+}({\bm{L}})^{2}\}^{2}}\right).

The proof and the exact values can be found in Appendix
F.1

{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\tau}{k}\right)
can be verified, incorporating a multiplication by the mixing time
\tau

6
Conclusion

In this study, we have studied primal-dual gradient dynamics subject to some null-space constraints and its application to a distributed TD-learning. We have derived finite-time error bounds for both the gradient dynamics and the distributed TD-learning. The results have been experimentally demonstrated. Potential future studies include extending the study to finite-time bounds of distributed TD-learning with nonlinear function approximation.

Appendix B
Doubly stochastic matrix

Definition 7

(Doubly stochastic matrix
[
Doan et al. 2019
]
)
.

A doubly stochastic matrix
{\bm{W}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is a stochastic matrix of which the row sum and column sum equal one, i.e.,
\sum_{i=1}^{N}[{\bm{W}}]_{ji}=1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{N}[{\bm{W}}]_{ij}=1
for
1\leq j\leq N
. A doubly stochastic corresponding to a graph
{\mathcal{G}}:=({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{E}})
requires additional assumption that
[{\bm{W}}]_{ii}>0
for
i\in{\mathcal{V}}
, and
[{\bm{W}}]_{uv}=0
for
(u,v)\notin{\mathcal{E}}
.

One of the key advantage of our algorithm over other distributed TD algorithms is that we do not require doubly stochastic matrix corresponding to the graph network. We have outlined several reasons highlighting the importance of removing the requirement on doubly stochastic matrix:

To start with, in many real world scenarios, constructing a doubly stochastic matrix is known to be difficult, or even impossible. A typical example is the directed graph scenario. There are graph structures, which do not allow a construction of doubly stochastic matrix
Gharesifard and Cortés 2010
. However, our algorithm can be extended to the directed graph setting without major modifications. For example, as shown in
Gokhale et al. 2023
;
Kia et al. 2015
, only with appropriate constant multiplication, we can guarantee convergence under strongly-connected and weight-balanced digraph scenario. Meanwhile, in the distributed optimization literature, push-sum
[
Nedić and Olshevsky 2014
]
or push-pull
[
Pu et al. 2020
]
algorithms have been developed to cope with the assumption of mixing matrix in directed graph scenario. Nonetheless, both methods require knowledge of out-degree, which are sometimes not possible including the broadcast communication setting
[
Hendrickx and Tsitsiklis 2015
]
.

Moreover, when dealing with a time-varying graph, whenever the graph changes, the doubly stochastic matrix needs to be constructed again. However, our analysis can be easily extended to the time-varying graph setting without any modifications.

Lastly, as from our experiment, the performance of distributed TD algorithms using doubly stochastic matrix is quite sensitive to the choice of doubly stochastic matrix, and the results can be found in Appendix
G
.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bar{\bm{\delta}}(o_{k};\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}\middle|{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\right]-2\mathbb{E}\left[\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\bm{\delta}}(o_{k};\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k})\\
\bm{0}_{Nq}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\middle|{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\right]\begin{bmatrix}\bar{{\bm{A}}}\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}+\bar{{\bm{b}}}\\
\bm{0}_{Nq}\end{bmatrix}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\bar{{\bm{A}}}\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}+\bar{{\bm{b}}}\\
\bm{0}_{Nq}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bar{\bm{\delta}}(o_{k};\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}\middle|{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\bar{{\bm{A}}}\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}+\bar{{\bm{b}}}\\
\bm{0}_{Nq}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bar{\bm{\delta}}(o_{k};\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}\middle|{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\right].

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bm{\bar{{\epsilon}}}(o_{k};\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bar{\bm{\delta}}(o_{k};\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k})\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\delta(o_{k};{\bm{\theta}}^{i}_{k})\bm{\phi}(s_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|(r^{i}_{k}+\gamma\bm{\phi}^{\top}(s_{k}^{\prime}){\bm{\theta}}^{i}_{k}-\bm{\phi}^{\top}(s_{k}){\bm{\theta}}_{k}^{i})\bm{\phi}(s_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[2\sum^{N}_{i=1}\left(R_{\max}^{2}+4\left\|{\bm{\theta}}_{k}^{i}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2NR_{\max}^{2}+8\left\|\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}.

The second last inequality follows from the fact that
\left\|{\bm{a}}+{\bm{b}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq 2\left\|{\bm{a}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+2\left\|{\bm{b}}\right\|^{2}_{2}
for
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{Nq}
. The last inequality follows from the assumption that
\left\|\bm{\phi}(s)\right\|_{2}\leq 1
for
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
in Assumption
1
. Using triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bm{\bar{{\epsilon}}}(o_{k};\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle 2NR^{2}_{\max}+8\left\|\bar{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}-\bm{1}_{N}\otimes{\bm{\theta}}_{c}+\bm{1}_{N}\otimes{\bm{\theta}}_{c}\right\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2NR^{2}_{\max}+16\left\|\bm{1}_{N}\otimes{\bm{\theta}}_{c}\right\|^{2}_{2}+16\left\|\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2NR^{2}_{\max}+16N\left\|{\bm{\theta}}_{c}\right\|^{2}_{2}+16\left\|\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2NR^{2}_{\max}+16N\left(\frac{R_{\max}}{w(1-\gamma)}\right)^{2}+16\left\|\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{32NR_{\max}^{2}}{w^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2}}+16\left\|\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}\right\|^{2}.

The second inequality follows from the fact that
\left\|{\bm{a}}+{\bm{b}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq 2\left\|{\bm{a}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+2\left\|{\bm{b}}\right\|^{2}_{2}
for
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{Nq}
. The last inequality follows from Lemma
12
.

\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}\beta(\bar{{\bm{A}}}+\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-2\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}})+2\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{2}&\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{A}}}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}&-2\eta{\bm{I}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\end{bmatrix},

(20)

where the second last equality follows from the axiom of Moore-Penrose axiom of symmetric matrices in Lemma
8
in the Appendix Section
C
, i.e.,
\bar{{\bm{L}}}\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{\dagger}\bar{{\bm{L}}}=\bar{{\bm{L}}}\bar{{\bm{L}}}\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{\dagger}=\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{\dagger}\bar{{\bm{L}}}\bar{{\bm{L}}}=\bar{{\bm{L}}}
.

Now, it is enough to choose
c
that satisfies following relation:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\beta(\bar{{\bm{A}}}+\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-2\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}})+2\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{2}&\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{A}}}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}&-2\eta{\bm{I}}\end{bmatrix}\preceq-\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{I}}_{Nq}&\bm{0}_{Nq\times Nq}\\
\bm{0}_{Nq\times Nq}&\eta{\bm{I}}_{Nq}\end{bmatrix}

(21)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\beta(\bar{{\bm{A}}}+\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-2\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}})+2\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{2}&\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{A}}}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}&-2\eta{\bm{I}}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{I}}_{Nq}&\bm{0}_{Nq\times Nq}\\
\bm{0}_{Nq\times Nq}&\eta{\bm{I}}_{Nq}\end{bmatrix}\preceq\bm{0}_{2Nq\times 2Nq}.

Using the result
\bar{{\bm{A}}}+\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}\preceq 2(\gamma-1)w
from Lemma
13
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\beta(\bar{{\bm{A}}}+\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-2\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}})+2\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{2}&\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{A}}}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}&-2\eta{\bm{I}}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{I}}_{Nq}&\bm{0}_{Nq\times Nq}\\
\bm{0}_{Nq\times Nq}&\eta{\bm{I}}_{Nq}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}(2\beta(\gamma-1)w+1+2\eta\lambda_{\max}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})^{2}){\bm{I}}_{Nq}&\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{A}}}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}&-\eta{\bm{I}}\end{bmatrix}.

The inequality follows from the fact that
\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{2}
is positive semi-definite matrix. To make the above matrix negative definite, according to the Schur complement argument in Lemma
9
, we need

\displaystyle(2\beta(\gamma-1)w+1+2\eta\lambda_{\max}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})^{2}){\bm{I}}_{Nq}+\frac{1}{\eta}(\bar{{\bm{A}}}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}})(\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}})\prec 0,

(22)

which can be satisfied if the following holds for
c
:

\displaystyle(2\beta(\gamma-1)w+1+2\eta\lambda_{\max}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})^{2})+\frac{1}{\eta}\left\|\bar{{\bm{A}}}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}\right\|_{2}^{2}<0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{\eta}\left\|\bar{{\bm{A}}}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+1+2\eta\lambda_{\max}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})^{2}}{2(1-\gamma)w}<\beta.

Since
\left\|\bar{{\bm{A}}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq 4
from Lemma
13
, and
a^{2}+b^{2}\geq 2ab
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, it suffices to satisfy

\displaystyle\beta>\frac{8+\eta+4\eta^{2}\lambda_{\max}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})^{2}}{2\eta(1-\gamma)w}.

Therefore, choosing

\displaystyle\beta=\frac{8+\eta+4\eta^{2}\lambda_{\max}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})^{2}}{\eta(1-\gamma)w}

suffices to satisfy (
22
). Note that
\beta\geq\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)w}+\frac{8}{\eta(1-\gamma)w}+\frac{4\eta\lambda_{\max}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})^{2}}{(1-\gamma)w}>4\lambda_{\max}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)w}\geq 4\lambda_{\max}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})
. Applying the relation (
21
) to (
20
) yields the following result:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}\beta(\bar{{\bm{A}}}+\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-2\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}})+2\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{2}&\bar{{\bm{A}}}^{\top}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{A}}}-\eta\bar{{\bm{L}}}&-2\eta{\bm{I}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\end{bmatrix}\leq

\displaystyle-\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{I}}_{Nq}&\bm{0}_{Nq\times Nq}\\
\bm{0}_{Nq\times Nq}&\eta{\bm{I}}_{Nq}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\|\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\eta\left\|\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left\|\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\eta\lambda^{+}_{\min}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})^{2}\left\|\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{\dagger}\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\min\left\{1,\eta\lambda^{+}_{\min}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})^{2}\right\}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{{\bm{\theta}}}\\
\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{\dagger}\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2}^{2},

where the last inequality follows from the following relation:

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{\dagger}\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\bar{{\bm{L}}}^{\dagger}\right\|_{2}\left\|\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\right\|_{2}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}^{+}(\bar{{\bm{L}}})}\left\|\bar{{\bm{L}}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}\right\|_{2}.

Appendix E
Stochastic recursive update : i.i.d. observation model

In this section, we will consider the i.i.d. observation model of the sequence
\{o_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
and
o_{k}\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\times\Pi_{i=1}^{N}I
where
I
is the closed interval
[-R_{\max},R_{\max}]
in
\mathbb{R}
. We consider the following general stochastic recursive update
[
Robbins and Monro 1951
]
, for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
{\bm{z}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2Nq}
:

\displaystyle{\bm{z}}_{k+1}={\bm{z}}_{k}+\alpha_{k}({\bm{E}}{\bm{z}}_{k}+\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k})),

(23)

where
{\bm{E}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2Nq\times 2Nq}
,
\bm{\xi}(\cdot;{\bm{z}}):{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\times\Pi^{N}_{i=1}I\to\mathbb{R}^{2Nq}
is a function parameterized by
{\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2Nq}
, and
\alpha_{k}\in(0,1)
.

Assumption 17
.

1.

For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k})
satisfies the following bound:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq C_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]+C_{2}.

2.

For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
\{o_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
is sampled from i.i.d. distribution, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k})\middle|{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\right]=0,

where
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}:=\sigma(o_{1},o_{2},\dots,o_{k})
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

3.

There exists a positive symmetric definite matrix
{\bm{Q}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2Nq\times 2Nq}
and positive real constant
\kappa
such that, for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\displaystyle{\bm{z}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{E}}{\bm{Q}}{\bm{z}}_{k}\leq-\kappa\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|_{2}.

We will introduce one lemma:.

Lemma 18
.

Under the Assumption
17
, for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})^{\top}{\bm{Q}}({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})\right]\leq 2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\left(\left(\left\|{\bm{E}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+C_{1}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]+C_{2}\right).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})^{\top}{\bm{Q}}({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\alpha_{k}{\bm{E}}{\bm{z}}_{k}+\alpha_{k}\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k})\middle|\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{E}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\left(\left(\left\|{\bm{E}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+C_{1}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]+C_{2}\right).

The first inequality follows from positive definiteness of
{\bm{Q}}
. The first equality follows from the update in (
23
). The second inequality follows from the relation
\left\|{\bm{a}}+{\bm{b}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq 2\left\|{\bm{a}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\left\|{\bm{b}}\right\|_{2}^{2}
for
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2Nq}
. The last inequality follows from the first item in Assumption
17
.
∎

Theorem 19
.

Suppose Assumption
17
holds, and let
V({\bm{z}}):={\bm{z}}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}{\bm{z}}
for
{\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2Nq}
.

1.

Suppose we use constant step-size, i.e.,
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{k}
, and
\alpha_{0}\leq\frac{\kappa\lambda_{\min}({\bm{Q}})}{2\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\left(E^{2}+C_{1}\right)}
, where
E
is a positive constant that satisfies
\left\|{\bm{E}}\right\|_{2}\leq E
. For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k+1})\right]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}k\alpha_{0}\right)V({\bm{x}}_{0})+2\alpha_{0}C_{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\frac{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}{\kappa}+2\alpha_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}.

2.

Suppose we have
\alpha_{t}=\frac{h_{1}}{t+h_{2}}
for
t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
h_{1}\geq\max\{2,\frac{2\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}{\kappa}\}
and
\max\left\{2,h_{1},h_{1}\frac{2\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\left(E^{2}+C_{1}\right)}{\kappa\lambda_{\min}({\bm{Q}})}\right\}\leq h_{2}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k+1})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{h_{2}}{k+h_{2}}\right)^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}V({\bm{x}}_{0})+\frac{2\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}h_{1}^{2}}{(k-1+h_{2})}\frac{2^{\frac{2h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}}{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}-1}+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}.

Proof.

From simple algebraic manipulation in
Srikant and Ying 2019
, we have the following decomposition:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k+1})-V({\bm{z}}_{k})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})^{\top}{\bm{Q}}({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[2{\bm{z}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}{\bm{z}}_{k+1}\right]-2\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})^{\top}{\bm{Q}}({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[2{\bm{z}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})^{\top}{\bm{Q}}({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})\right]}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[2{\bm{z}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k}-\alpha_{k}{\bm{E}}{\bm{z}}_{k})\right]}_{I_{2}}+\underbrace{2\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{z}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}{\bm{E}}{\bm{z}}_{k}\right]}_{I_{3}}.

(24)

To bound
I_{1}
, the result in Lemma
18
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})^{\top}{\bm{Q}}({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k})\right]\leq 2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\left(\left(\left\|{\bm{E}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+C_{1}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]+C_{2}\right).

The term
I_{2}
becomes zero due to the second item in Assumption
17
, which leads to
\mathbb{E}\left[2{\bm{z}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k}-\alpha_{k}{\bm{E}}{\bm{z}}_{k})\right]=\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}\left[2{\bm{z}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k})\middle|{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\right]\right]=0
. Finally we can apply the third item in Assumption
17
to bound
I_{3}
. Collecting the terms to bound (
24
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[V({\bm{z}}_{k+1})-V({\bm{z}}_{k})]\leq

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\left(\left(\left\|{\bm{E}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+C_{1}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]+C_{2}\right)-2\kappa\alpha_{k}\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\left(\frac{\left\|{\bm{E}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+C_{1}}{\lambda_{\min}({\bm{Q}})}\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k})\right]+C_{2}\right)-2\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\frac{\left\|{\bm{E}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+C_{1}}{\lambda_{\min}({\bm{Q}})}-2\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{k}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k})\right]+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}.

(25)

The second inequality follows from
\lambda_{\min}({\bm{Q}})\left\|{\bm{z}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left\|{\bm{z}}\right\|_{{\bm{Q}}}^{2}\leq\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})\left\|{\bm{z}}\right\|_{2}^{2}
. Moreover, the step-size conditions for both constant step-size and diminishing step-size leads to

\displaystyle 2\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\frac{\left\|{\bm{E}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+C_{1}}{\lambda_{\min}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{k}^{2}-2\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{k}\leq 2\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\frac{E^{2}+C_{1}}{\lambda_{\min}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{k}^{2}-2\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{k}\leq-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{k}.

Applying the above result to (
25
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[V({\bm{z}}_{k+1})]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{k}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k})\right]+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Pi^{k}_{i=0}\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{i}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{0})\right]+2\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha^{2}_{i}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}\Pi_{j=i+1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{j}\right)+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{0})\right]+2\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha^{2}_{i}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\sum_{j=i+1}^{k}\alpha_{j}\right)+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2},

(26)

where the last inequality follows from the relation
1-x\leq\exp(-x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

1.

First, we will consider the case for the constant step-size. Using the fact that the step-size is constant, we can rewrite in (
26
) into

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k+1})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}k\alpha_{0}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{0})\right]

\displaystyle+2\sum^{k-1}_{i=0}\alpha_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{0}(k-i)\right)+2\alpha_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}k\alpha_{0}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{0})\right]+2\alpha_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{0}\right)}{1-\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{0}\right)}+2\alpha_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}.

The second inequality follows from summation of geometric series. Since
\exp(x)-1\geq x
for
x>0
, we have
\frac{1}{\exp(x)-1}\leq\frac{1}{x}
, and this leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k+1})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}k\alpha_{0}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{0})\right]+2\alpha_{0}^{2}C_{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\frac{1}{\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\alpha_{0}}+2\alpha_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}k\alpha_{0}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{0})\right]+2\alpha_{0}C_{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\frac{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}{\kappa}+2\alpha_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}.

2.

The result for diminishing step-size becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k+1})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}\right)V({\bm{z}}_{0})

\displaystyle+2\sum^{k-1}_{i=0}\alpha_{i}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\sum^{k-1}_{j=i+1}\alpha_{j}\right)+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\log\left(\frac{k+h_{2}}{h_{2}}\right)\right)\mathbb{E}[V({\bm{z}}_{0})]

\displaystyle+2\sum^{k-1}_{i=0}\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{(i+h_{2})^{2}}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}\exp\left(-\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\log\left(\frac{k-1+h_{2}}{i+1+h_{2}}\right)\right)+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{h_{2}}{k+h_{2}}\right)^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}V({\bm{z}}_{0})+2\sum^{k-1}_{i=0}\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{(i+h_{2})^{2}}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}\left(\frac{i+1+h_{2}}{k-1+h_{2}}\right)^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2},

The second inequality follows from
\int^{k}_{t=0}\frac{h_{1}}{t+h_{2}}dt\leq\sum^{k}_{i=0}\alpha_{i}
. From the choice of step-size, we have
\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}\geq 2
, which leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V({\bm{z}}_{k+1})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{h_{2}}{k+h_{2}}\right)^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}V({\bm{z}}_{0})

\displaystyle+2\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}\sum^{k-1}_{i=0}\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{(i+h_{2})^{2}}\left(\frac{i+1+h_{2}}{k-1+h_{2}}\right)^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{h_{2}}{k+h_{2}}\right)^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}V({\bm{z}}_{0})

\displaystyle+\frac{2\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}h_{1}^{2}}{(k-1+h_{2})^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}}2^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}\sum^{k-1}_{i=0}(i+h_{2})^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}-2}+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{h_{2}}{k+h_{2}}\right)^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}V({\bm{z}}_{0})

\displaystyle+\frac{2\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}h_{1}^{2}}{(k-1+h_{2})^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}}2^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}\int_{0}^{k}(s+h_{1})^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}-2}ds+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{h_{2}}{k+h_{2}}\right)^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}V({\bm{z}}_{0})

\displaystyle+\frac{2\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}h_{1}^{2}}{(k-1+h_{2})^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}}\frac{2^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}}{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}-1}(k+h_{1})^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}-1}+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{h_{2}}{k+h_{2}}\right)^{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}V({\bm{z}}_{0})

\displaystyle+\frac{2\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}h_{1}^{2}}{(k-1+h_{2})}\frac{2^{2\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}}}{\frac{h_{1}\kappa}{\lambda_{\max}({\bm{Q}})}-1}+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}C_{2}.

The second inequality follows from the fact that
i+h_{2}+1\leq 2i+2h_{2}
for
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The last inequality follows from the fact that

.

∎

Appendix F
Markovian observation model

We will consider a general stochastic recursive model with Markovian observation samples, for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
:

\displaystyle{\bm{z}}_{k+1}={\bm{z}}_{k}+\alpha_{k}({\bm{E}}{\bm{z}}_{k}+\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k})),

(27)

where
{\bm{E}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2Nq\times 2Nq}
,
{\bm{z}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2Nq}
and
\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k}):={\bm{W}}(o_{k}){\bm{z}}_{k}+{\bm{w}}(o_{k})
for
{\bm{W}}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\times\Pi^{N}_{i=1}I\to\mathbb{R}^{2Nq\times 2Nq}
, where
I
is closed interval
[-R_{\max},R_{\max}]
in
\mathbb{R}
, and
{\bm{w}}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\times\Pi^{N}_{i=1}I\to\mathbb{R}^{2Nq}
. We assume that the the sequence
\{o_{k}\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\times\Pi_{i=1}^{N}I\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is generated by an ergodic Markov chain. The proof follows the spirit of
Srikant and Ying 2019
. We will denote
T\in{\mathbb{N}}
as the total number of iterations and the mixing time
\tau:=\tau(\alpha_{T})
will be defined as in (
14
). We first introduce a set of assumptions:

Assumption 20
.

1.

For any
o\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\times\Pi^{N}_{i=1}I
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{W}}(o)\right\|_{2}\leq C_{1},\quad\left\|{\bm{w}}(o)\right\|_{2}\leq C_{2}.

2.

For
k\geq\tau
, there exists a positive constant
\Xi
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau})\middle|{\mathcal{F}}_{k-\tau}\right]\right\|_{2}\leq\Xi\alpha_{T}(\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+1),

where
{\mathcal{F}}_{k-\tau}=\sigma(o_{1},o_{2},\dots,o_{k-\tau})
.

3.

For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, there exists a positive definite matrix
{\bm{Q}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2Nq\times 2Nq}
and a positive constant
\kappa
such that

\displaystyle 2{\bm{z}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{E}}{\bm{Q}}{\bm{z}}_{k}\leq-\kappa\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|_{2}.

For simplicity of the proof, we will denote
E_{1}:=C_{1}+E
where
E
is a positive constant such that
\left\|{\bm{E}}\right\|_{2}\leq E
. We first present several useful lemmas.

Lemma 21
.

1.

For
k\geq\tau
and
k-\tau+1\leq s\leq k-1
, using constant step-size, i.e.,
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{T}
such that
\tau\alpha_{0}E_{1}\leq\ln 2
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{z}}_{s+1}\right\|_{2}\leq 2\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+\frac{4C_{2}}{E_{1}}.

2.

For
k\geq\tau
and
k-\tau+1\leq s\leq k-1
, using diminishing step-size, i.e.,
\alpha_{t}=\frac{h_{1}}{t+h_{2}}
for
t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
such that
\frac{\tau-1+2^{1/E_{1}h_{1}}}{2^{1/E_{1}h_{1}}-1}\leq h_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{z}}_{s+1}\right\|_{2}\leq 2\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+4C_{2}\tau\alpha_{k-\tau}.

Proof.

Applying triangle inequality to the recursion in (
27
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{z}}_{s+1}\right\|_{2}\leq(1+\alpha_{s}E_{1})\left\|{\bm{z}}_{s}\right\|_{2}+\alpha_{s}C_{2}.

Recursive formula leads to

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{z}}_{s+1}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Pi_{j=k-\tau}^{s}(1+\alpha_{j}E_{1})\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+\sum_{i=k-\tau}^{s-1}C_{2}\alpha_{i}\Pi_{j=i+1}^{s}(1+\alpha_{j}E_{1})+\alpha_{s}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\sum^{s}_{i=k-\tau}\alpha_{i}E_{1}\right)\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+\sum^{s-1}_{i=k-\tau}C_{2}\alpha_{i}\exp\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{s}\alpha_{j}E_{1}\right)+\alpha_{s}C_{2},

(28)

where the last inequality follows from the relation
1+x\leq\exp(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

1.

We will first prove the case when the step-size is constant. Using the fact that
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{s}
, we can rewrite (
28
) as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{z}}_{s+1}\right\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(\tau\alpha_{0}E_{1}\right)\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+\sum^{s-1}_{i=k-\tau}C_{2}\alpha_{0}\exp(\alpha_{0}E_{1}(s-i))+\alpha_{0}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(\tau\alpha_{0}E_{1}\right)\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+C_{2}\alpha_{0}\frac{\exp((\tau-1)\alpha_{0}E_{1})}{1-\exp(-\alpha_{0}E_{1})}+\alpha_{s}C_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\tau\alpha_{0}E_{1}\right)\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+C_{2}\alpha_{0}\frac{\exp(\tau\alpha_{0}E_{1})}{\exp(\alpha_{0}E_{1})-1}+\alpha_{s}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+C_{2}\frac{2}{E_{1}}+\alpha_{0}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+\frac{4C_{2}}{E_{1}}.

The second last inequality follows from the condition on the step-size,
\tau\alpha_{0}E_{1}\leq\ln 2
, and the fact that
\exp(x)\geq x+1
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.

We will prove the case for diminishing step-size. Plugging in
\alpha_{t}=\frac{h_{1}}{t+h_{2}}
for
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
to (
28
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{z}}_{s+1}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(E_{1}h_{1}\int^{s}_{k-\tau-1}\frac{1}{t+h_{2}}dt\right)\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle+C_{2}\sum^{s-1}_{i=k-\tau}\alpha_{i}\exp\left(E_{1}h_{1}\int^{s}_{i}\frac{1}{t+h_{2}}dt\right)+\alpha_{s}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{s+h_{2}}{k-\tau-1+h_{2}}\right)^{E_{1}h_{1}}\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+C_{2}\sum^{s-1}_{i=k-\tau}\alpha_{i}\left(\frac{s+h_{2}}{i+h_{2}}\right)^{E_{1}h_{1}}+\alpha_{s}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+C_{2}\sum^{k-1}_{i=k-\tau}2\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{s}C_{2}

(29)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+2C_{2}\tau\alpha_{k-\tau}+\alpha_{s}C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}+4C_{2}\tau\alpha_{k-\tau}.

The first inequality follows from the fact that
\sum_{i=a}^{b}\frac{1}{t+h_{2}}\leq\int^{b}_{a-1}\frac{1}{t+h_{2}}dt
for
a,b\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. The inequality in (
29
) follows from the following relation that for
k\geq\tau
,
k-\tau+1\leq s\leq k-1
and
k-\tau\leq i\leq s-1
, the condition
\frac{\tau-1+2^{1/E_{1}h_{1}}}{2^{1/E_{1}h_{1}}-1}\leq h_{2}
leads to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{s+h_{2}}{i+h_{2}}\right)^{E_{1}h_{1}}\leq\left(\frac{s+h_{2}}{k-\tau-1+h_{2}}\right)^{E_{1}h_{1}}\leq\left(\frac{k-1+h_{2}}{k-\tau-1+h_{2}}\right)^{E_{1}h_{1}}\leq 2.

The last inequality follows since
\frac{k-1+h_{2}}{k-\tau-1+h_{2}}
is decreasing function in
k
and it suffices to satisfy the inequality when
k=\tau
. This completes the proof.

∎

{\bm{z}}_{k}
and
{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}

Lemma 22
.

Considering constant step-size, i.e.,

\displaystyle E_{1}\alpha_{0}\tau\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}+C_{2}\alpha_{0}\tau.

2.

Considering diminishing step-size, i.e.,
\alpha_{t}=\frac{h_{1}}{t+h_{2}}
for

such that

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}-{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}-{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}^{2}

Proof.

(32)

The first inequality follows from triangle inequality. The first equality follows from the update in (

). The last inequality follows from the first item in Assumption

.

Considering the constant step-size, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}-{\bm{z}}_{k-\tau}\right\|_{2}\leq

Lemma 23
.

1.

Considering constant step-size, i.e.,
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{T}
, with
\alpha_{0}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{100\tau\max\{E_{1},C_{2}\}},\frac{C_{1}}{2\Xi}\right\}
, for
k\geq\tau
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\bm{z}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}(\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k}))]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\left((4\Xi+13C_{1}E_{1}+20C_{1}C_{2}+4E_{1}C_{2})\alpha_{0}\tau\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(25C_{1}C_{2}+10C_{2}^{2}+2\Xi+4E_{1}C_{2}\right)\alpha_{0}\tau\right).

2.

Considering diminishing step-size, i.e.,
\alpha_{t}=\frac{h_{1}}{t+h_{2}}
for
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\max\left\{\frac{\tau-1+2^{1/E_{1}h_{1}}}{2^{1/E_{1}h_{1}}-1},32\tau E_{1}h_{1},32\tau C_{2}h_{1},\frac{\Xi h_{1}}{2C_{1}}\right\}\leq h_{2}
, for
k\geq\tau
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\bm{z}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}(\bm{\xi}(o_{k};{\bm{z}}_{k}))]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{Q}}\right\|_{2}\left((4\Xi+13E_{1}C_{1}+8C_{1}C_{2}+4C_{2}E_{1})\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{z}}_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(13C_{1}C_{2}+4C_{2}^{2}+2\Xi+4C_{2}E_{1})\right)\alpha_{k-\tau}\tau.

Proof.

Following the spirit of
Srikant and Ying 2019
we can decompose the cross term in to follows four terms:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\bm{z}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}({\bm{z}}_{k+1}-{\bm{z}}_{k}-\alpha_{k}{\bm{E}}{\bm{z}}_{k})]

\displaystyle=

Lemma 24

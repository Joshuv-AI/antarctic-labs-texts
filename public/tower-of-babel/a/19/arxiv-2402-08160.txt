Finite Multiple Mixed Values
Jianqiang Zhao

Abstract




Abstract.
In recent years, a variety of variants of multiple zeta values (MZVs) have been defined
and studied. One way to produce these variants is to restrict the indices in the definition of MZVs to some fixed
parity pattern, which include Hoffman’s multiple
t
-values, Kaneko and Tsumura’s multiple
T
-values, and
Xu and the author’s multiple
S
-values. We have also considered the so-called multiple mixed values by allowing
all possible parity patterns and studied a few important relations among these values. In this paper, we will
turn to their finite analogs and their symmetric forms, motivated by a deep conjecture of Kaneko and Zagier which
relates the finite MZVs and symmetric MZVs, and a generalized version of this conjecture by the author to the Euler
sum (i.e., level two) setting. We will present a few important relations among these values such as the
stuffle, reversal, and linear shuffle relations. We will also compute explicitly the (conjecturally smallest) generating set in weight one and two cases. In the appendix we tabulate some dimension computation for various sub-spaces of the finite multiple mixed values and propose a conjecture.

Keywords
: (finite) multiple zeta values; (finite) Euler sums; (finite) multiple mixed values; (finite) multiple
t
-values; (finite) multiple
T
-values; (finite) multiple
S
-values.

AMS Subject Classifications (2020):
11M32, 11B68.

1
Introduction

1.1
Multiple zeta values and their finite analogs

For any composition of positive integers
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we define the
multiple zeta value
(MZV) by

\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}

(1.1)

and the
multiple zeta star value
(MZSV) by

\zeta^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\geq\dots\geq n_{d}\geq 1}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}.

(1.2)

These converge if and only if
s_{1}\geq 2
in which case we say
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}
is
admissible
. As usual, we call

|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|:=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{d}
the weight and
d
the depth. These values were first systematically studied
by Zagier
[
25
]
and Hoffman
[
3
]
independently.

In recent years, a lot of research has been done concerning the structure of different variants of multiple zeta values
due to their important applications in both mathematics and theoretical physics. For example, when we allow alternating
signs to appear then we obtain the so-called Euler sums (also called alternating MZVs) which play important roles
in the study of knot theory and Witten multiple zeta function associated with Lie algebra (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
22
]
).
If we allow not only
\pm 1
but more generally
N
th roots of unity then we can consider colored/cyclotomic MZVs of level
N
,
which have also appeared unexpectedly in the study of Feynman diagrams
[
2
]
.

On the other hand, the modular arithmetic nature of the partial sums of MZVs was first considered by Hoffman
[
4
]
and the last author
[
20
]
independently. Contrary to the classical cases above, not many variants of these sums exist. To set up the correct
theoretical framework for these variants, we define the following adéle-like ring which was first considered by Kontsevich and then applied
to the
p
-adic setting by Kaneko and Zagier
[
9
]
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primes. Set

\mathcal{A}:=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\bigg/\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}).

(1.3)

Then we can define the
finite multiple zeta values
(FMZVs) by the following:

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\left(H_{p}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{p>n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}\pmod{p}\right)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}.

(1.4)

In 2014, Kaneko and Zagier proposed a deep conjecture (see Conjecture
1.1
below for a generalization)
relating these values on the
p
-adic side
to MZVs on the Archimedean side via a mysterious connection. This conjecture is far from being proved but since then
a plethora of parallel results have been shown to hold on both sides simultaneously (see, e.g.,
[
10
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
).
In particular, for each positive integer
w\geq 2
, the element

\beta_{w}:=\Big(\displaystyle\frac{B_{p-w}}{w}\Big)_{w<p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}

(1.5)

is the finite analog of
\zeta(w)
, where
B_{n}
’s are the Bernoulli numbers defined by

\displaystyle\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}\displaystyle\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

And the Fermat quotient

{\texttt{q}}_{2}:=\Big(\displaystyle\frac{2^{p-1}-1}{p}\Big)_{2<p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}

(1.6)

is the analog of
-\zeta(\bar{1})=\log 2
.

1.2
Euler sums and their finite analogs

For
s_{1},\dots,s_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}=\pm 1
, we define the
Euler sums

\zeta\binom{s_{1},\dots,s_{d}}{{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}}:=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\;\prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{{\varepsilon}_{j}^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}.

(1.7)

To save space, if
{\varepsilon}_{j}=-1
then
\overline{s}_{j}
will be used and if a substring
S
repeats
n

times in the list then
\{S\}^{n}
will be used. For example,
\zeta(\{3\}^{n})=8^{n}\zeta(\{\bar{2},1\}^{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

(see
[
21
]
).

To state the relations between Euler sums concisely we define a kind of double cover of the set
\mathbb{N}
of positive integers.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be the set of
signed numbers

\mathbb{N}\cup\overline{\mathbb{N}}
where

\overline{\mathbb{N}}:=\{\bar{s}:s\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Define the absolute value function
|\cdot|
on
\mathbb{D}
by
|s|=|\bar{s}|=s

for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
and the sign function by
\sgn(s)=1
and
\sgn(\bar{s})=-1

for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
. We make
\mathbb{D}
a semi-group by defining
a commutative and associative
binary operation
\oplus
(called
O-plus
)
as follows: for all
a,b\in\mathbb{D}_{0}

a\oplus b:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{|a|+|b|},&\quad\hbox{if $\sgn(a)\neq\sgn(b)$;}\\
|a|+|b|,&\quad\hbox{if $\sgn(a)=\sgn(b)$.}\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

Finally, we define the conjugate operation
\overline{s}
toggling between
\mathbb{N}
and
\overline{\mathbb{N}}
.

For any
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{D}^{d}
, we define the
n
-th partial sum of the
Euler sums
by

\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n>n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{\sgn(s_{j})^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}^{|s_{j}|}}.

(1.9)

Similarly to FMZVs, finite
Euler sums
(FESs) are defined by

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\Big(\zeta_{p}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\pmod{p}\Big)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}.

(1.10)

In
[
23
, Conjecture 8.6.9]
the author of this paper extended Kakeko–Zagier conjecture to the setting of
the Euler sums. For
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{D}^{d}
, define the symmetrized version of the alternating Euler sums by

\displaystyle\zeta_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i}(-1)^{|s_{j}|}\sgn(s_{j})\right)\zeta_{\ast}(s_{i},\dots,s_{1})\zeta_{\ast}(s_{i+1},\dots,s_{d}),

\displaystyle\zeta_{\shuffle}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i}(-1)^{|s_{j}|}\sgn(s_{j})\right)\zeta_{\shuffle}(s_{i},\dots,s_{1})\zeta_{\shuffle}(s_{i+1},\dots,s_{d}),

where
\zeta_{\sharp}
(
\sharp=\ast
or
\shuffle
) are regularized values (see
[
23
, Proposition 13.3.8]
).
They are called
\sharp
-
symmetric Euler sums
. If
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
then they are called

\sharp
-
symmetric multiple zeta values
(
\sharp
-SMZVs or simply SMZVs if
\sharp
does not matter).

Conjecture 1.1
.

For any
w\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathsf{FES}_{w}
(resp.
\mathsf{ES}_{w}
) be the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space generated by
all FESs (resp. Euler sums) of weight
w
. Then there is an isomorphism

\displaystyle f_{\mathsf{ES}}:\mathsf{FES}_{w}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{ES}_{w}}{\zeta(2)\mathsf{ES}_{w-2}},

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\zeta_{\shuffle}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}).

Remark 1.2
.

Note that we can replace
\zeta_{\shuffle}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})
by
\zeta_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})
(see
[
23
, Exercise 8.7]
).

To better understand this mysterious relation is the primary motivation of this paper. We will mainly study a few variants of
finite analogs of Euler sums by presenting some results that are analogous to
those on the Archimedean side.

2
Multiple mixed values and their finite analogs

Put
\mathbb{Z}_{*}=\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}
. For any
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the
n
th partial sum of
multiple mixed values
(MMVs) by

M_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n>n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{1+\sgn(s_{j})(-1)^{n_{j}}}{2n_{j}^{|s_{j}|}}=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n>n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0\\
n_{j}\equiv(1-\sgn(s_{j}))/2\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{j}^{|s_{j}|}}

(2.11)

and the
finite multiple mixed values
(FMMVs) by

M_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\Big(M_{p}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\pmod{p}\Big)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}.

(2.12)

We call
|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|:=|s_{1}|+\cdots+|s_{d}|
the weight and
d
the depth.

The motivation to define the MMVs is to find a common generalization of a few variants of level two MZVs
including the following. For all admissible
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we define the

multiple
t
-values
(MtVs, see
[
5
]
),

multiple
T
-values
(MTVs, see
[
8
]
),
and
multiple
S
-values
(MSVs, see
[
17
]
) by

\displaystyle t({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=

\displaystyle\,M(-s_{1},\dots,-s_{j},\dots,-s_{d})=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2n_{j}-1)^{s_{j}}},

(2.13)

\displaystyle T({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=

\displaystyle\,M\Big((-1)^{d}s_{1},\dots,(-1)^{d-j+1}s_{j},\dots,-s_{d}\Big)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0\\
n_{j}\equiv d-j+1\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}},

(2.14)

\displaystyle S({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=

\displaystyle\,M\Big((-1)^{d-1}s_{1},\dots,(-1)^{d-j}s_{j},\dots,s_{d}\Big)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0\\
n_{j}\equiv d-j\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}},

(2.15)

respectively. Their finite analogs
t_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})
,
T_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})
, and
S_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})

are defined similarly as in (
1.4
) and (
1.10
). It is clear that

t_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{d}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}=\pm 1}\bigg(\prod_{1\leq j\leq d}{\varepsilon}_{j}\bigg)\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}\binom{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}}{{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}},\quad F_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{d}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}=\pm 1}\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq d\\
2|d-j\ \text{if $F=T$}\\
2\nmid d-j\ \text{if $F=S$}\end{subarray}}{\varepsilon}_{j}\bigg)\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}\binom{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}}{{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}.

More recently, another type of level two FMZVs is defined by Kaneko et al.
[
7
]
as follows
(after multiplying by
2^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}
on the following):

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\bigg(\displaystyle\sum_{p>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0,\ 2|n_{j}\,\forall j}\ \prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}\bigg)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{d}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}=\pm 1}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}\binom{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}}{{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}.

(2.16)

It is also clear that

\displaystyle\zeta^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0,\ 2|n_{j}\,\forall j}\ \prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}}\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}).

(2.17)

Definition 2.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. We define the
\sharp
-regularized MTVs

(
\sharp=\ast
or

\displaystyle F_{\sharp}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{d}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\sigma}_{1},\dots,{\sigma}_{d}=\pm 1}\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq d\\
2|d-j\ \text{if $F=T$}\\

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\Big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}(-1)^{s_{\ell}}\Big)\widetilde{F}_{\sharp}(s_{i},\dots,s_{1})F_{\sharp}(s_{i+1},\dots,s_{d}),&\quad\hbox{if $d$ is odd,}\end{array}\right.

where
\widetilde{F}=S+T-F
and we set

as usual.

In

f_{\mathsf{ES}}T_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=T_{\sharp}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\quad\text{and}\quad f_{\mathsf{ES}}S_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=S_{\sharp}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\pmod{\zeta(2)}.

(2.18)

Proposition 2.1
.

We have
\mathsf{FES}_{w}=\mathsf{FMMV}_{w}
for all
w\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

This follows easily from the identities

(-1)^{n}=\displaystyle\frac{(1+(-1)^{n})-(1-(-1)^{n})}{2},\quad 1=\displaystyle\frac{(1+(-1)^{n})+(1-(-1)^{n})}{2}.

∎

For any fixed weight
w\geq 1
we clearly have the inclusion relations between weight
w
pieces of the above subspaces:

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}&&\mathsf{FMSV}_{w}&\subseteq&\mathsf{FES}_{w}&\supseteq&\mathsf{FMTV}_{w}&&\\
&&\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\begin{turn} $\supseteq$ \end{turn}}}&&\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\begin{turn} $=$ \end{turn}}}&&\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\begin{turn} $\supseteq$ \end{turn}}}&&\\
\mathsf{FMZV}^{(2)}_{w}&\subseteq&\mathsf{FMMVe}_{w}&\subseteq&\mathsf{FMMV}_{w}&\supseteq&\mathsf{FMMVo}_{w}&\supseteq&\mathsf{FMtV}_{w}\end{array}

Kaneko et al.
[
7
, Conjecture 5]
conjectured that
\mathsf{FMZV}^{(2)}_{w}=\mathsf{FES}_{w}
for all
w\in\mathbb{N}
.
We further conjecture the equal signs in the second row above always hold (also see Conjecture
6.2
).

Conjecture 2.2
.

For sufficiently large weight
w\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}\mathsf{FMZV}_{w}&\subsetneq&\mathsf{FMSV}_{w}&\subsetneq&\mathsf{FES}_{w}&\supsetneq&\mathsf{FMTV}_{w}&\supsetneq&\mathsf{FMZV}_{w}\\
\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\begin{turn} $\supsetneq$ \end{turn}}}&&\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\begin{turn} $\supsetneq$ \end{turn}}}&&\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\begin{turn} $=$ \end{turn}}}&&\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\begin{turn} $\supsetneq$ \end{turn}}}&&\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\begin{turn} $\supsetneq$ \end{turn}}}\\
\mathsf{FMZV}^{(2)}_{w}&=&\mathsf{FMMVe}_{w}&=&\mathsf{FMMV}_{w}&=&\mathsf{FMMVo}_{w}&=&\mathsf{FMtV}_{w}\end{array}

Except for the middle vertical equal sign all the other relations are supported by numerical evidence but no formal proofs yet.

the following conjectural relations between the classical subspaces of Euler sums (cf. the Venn diagram at the end of

Problem 2.3
.

How can we verify numerically the inclusion
\mathsf{FMZV}_{w}\subseteq\mathsf{FMSV}_{w}\displaystyle\cap\mathsf{FMZV}^{(2)}_{w}
and
\mathsf{FMZV}_{w}\subseteq\mathsf{FMTV}_{w}\displaystyle\cap\mathsf{FMtV}_{w}
?
We only need to find a basis in each of the four subspaces on the right and show that every FMZV can be expressed as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear
combination of the basis elements. Is it even possible that both (or one) of the inclusions are actually equalities?

.

For sufficiently large weight
w\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}\mathsf{MZV}_{w}&\subsetneq&\mathsf{MSV}_{w}&\subsetneq&\mathsf{ES}_{w}&\supsetneq&\mathsf{MTV}_{w}&\supsetneq&\mathsf{MZV}_{w}\\

2.1
Stuffle relations

Stuffle relations hold in all of the above subalgebras of
\mathsf{FES}
in Conjecture
2.2

except
\mathsf{FMTV}
and
\mathsf{FMSV}
. For example, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{D}

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(a)\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(b)=\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(a,b)+\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(b,a)+\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(a\oplus b),

and if
a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{A}}(a,b)M_{\mathcal{A}}(c)=

\displaystyle\,M_{\mathcal{A}}(a,b,c)+M_{\mathcal{A}}(a,c,b)+M_{\mathcal{A}}(c,a,b)

\displaystyle\,+\delta_{\sgn(a),\sgn(c)}M_{\mathcal{A}}(a+c,b)+\delta_{\sgn(b),\sgn(c)}M_{\mathcal{A}}(a,b+c)

where
\delta_{s,t}
is the Kronecker symbol satisfying
\delta_{s,t}=1
if
s=t
and
\delta_{s,t}=0
otherwise.
Hence the stuffing for FMMVs occurs only when the two merging components have the same sign.

Problem 2.5
.

Do
\mathsf{FMTV}
and
\mathsf{FMSV}
form subalgebras of
\mathsf{FES}
? If not, what is the first instance of a product that is not closed?
Note, if the answer is negative then it cannot be verified rigorously with current level of knowledge.
This is similar to the situation for classical
\mathsf{MTV}
and
\mathsf{MSV}
and it is for
same type of reason. In the classical setting, counterexamples can only be verified numerically approximately but they cannot be proved
rigorously due to difficulty in proving transcendence result. In the
p
-adic cases, the transcendence problem for
\mathcal{A}
-numbers may be even harder. For example, we don’t even know if
\beta_{w}\in\mathcal{A}
vanishes or not for any odd
w\geq 3
although conjecturally
the density of primes
p>w
such that
B_{p-w}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
should be 0.

Here is the process to find such a product of two FMTVs of total weight
w
numerically, under the assumption that
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathsf{FES}_{w}=F_{w}
which is the same as
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathsf{FMMV}_{w}=F_{w}
by Proposition
2.1
. First, find a generating set
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{B}_{w}
of
\mathsf{FMTV}_{w}
which is possible by using linear shuffle, reversal and stuffle relations. Second, expanding the set into a a generating set
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{B}_{w}
of
\mathsf{FMMV}_{w}
. If
|\mathcal{M}\mathcal{B}_{w}|=F_{w}
then it is a basis by the assumption
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathsf{FMMV}_{w}=F_{w}
. Finally,
for each product of FMTVs of weight
w
we can express it using the conjectural basis
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{B}_{w}
. If
elements outside of
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{B}_{w}
are needed for such a product then it does not lie in
\mathsf{FMTV}_{w}
,
meaning the product of
\mathsf{FMTV}
is not closed.

Reversal Relations

Proposition 2.6
.

For all
d\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{A}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}M_{\mathcal{A}}(-{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}).

In particular, for all
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}

\displaystyle t_{\mathcal{A}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}\zeta^{(2)}_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}),

(2.20)

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{A}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}T_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\mathcal{A}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}S_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\quad\text{if }2|d,

2.3
Linear shuffle relations

The most nontrivial relations among finite MZVs and finite Euler sums is provided by the linear shuffle relations
which is closely related to the shuffle relations among the classical MZVs and Euler sums. In this subsection,
we will extend this to FMMVs and their alternating versions.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,

{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}=({\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
, and
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}

the partial sums of MMVs by

M_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n>k_{1}>\cdots>k_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{(1+{\varepsilon}_{1}(-1)^{k_{1}})\sigma_{1}^{(2k_{1}+1-{\varepsilon}_{1})/4}\cdots(1+{\varepsilon}_{d}(-1)^{k_{d}})\sigma_{d}^{(2k_{d}+1-{\varepsilon}_{d})/4}}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}.

(2.27)

When
n\to\infty
and
(s_{d},\sigma_{d})\neq(1,1)
we recover the alternating MMVs studied by Xu, Yan and the last author
in
[
18
,
19
]
. By taking
n
to be primes, we can now define the
finite alternating multiple mixed values

M_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):=\Big(M_{p}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\pmod{p}\Big)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}.

(2.28)

It turns out that in depth two, all the (non-alternating) MMVs have been given special names already,
which we now recall. Let
d=2
. Then we set

\displaystyle\zeta^{(2)}_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):=

\displaystyle\,M_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};1,1;{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}),\qquad

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):=

\displaystyle\,M_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};1,-1;{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}),

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):=

\displaystyle\,M_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};-1,1;{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}),\qquad

\displaystyle t_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):=

Moreover, to save space, if an alternating sign

S_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{2},3)\displaystyle\sum_{m>n>0,2\nmid m,2|n}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{(m+1)/2}}{m^{2}n^{3}}.

We recall briefly the main setup for the integral expression of alternating MMVs. Let

{\texttt{a}}=\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t},{\texttt{b}}=w_{+1}^{-1}:=\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t^{2}},\quad{\texttt{c}}=w_{-1}^{-1}:=\displaystyle\frac{-dt}{1+t^{2}},\quad\beta=w_{+1}^{+1}:=\displaystyle\frac{t\,dt}{1-t^{2}},\quad\gamma=w_{-1}^{+1}:=\displaystyle\frac{-t\,dt}{1+t^{2}}.

Namely,
w_{{\sigma}}^{{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}}=w_{{\sigma}}^{{\varepsilon}_{1}{\varepsilon}_{2}}

when

M({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}w_{0}^{s_{1}-1}w_{{\sigma}_{1}}^{{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}}w_{0}^{s_{2}-1}w_{{\sigma}_{1}{\sigma}_{2}}^{{\varepsilon}_{2},{\varepsilon}_{3}}\cdots w_{0}^{s_{r}-1}w_{{\sigma}_{1}{\sigma}_{2}\cdots{\sigma}_{r}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r}}.

For

\sgn({\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}):=(-1)^{\sharp\{i<r\mid{\sigma}_{i}={\varepsilon}_{i}={\varepsilon}_{i+1}{\varepsilon}_{i+2}\cdots{\varepsilon}_{r}=-1\}}.

Then for all
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}w_{0}^{s_{1}-1}w_{{\sigma}_{1}}^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\cdots w_{0}^{s_{r}-1}w_{{\sigma}_{r}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r}}=\sgn({\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}})M({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}};\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}})

5
Sum formulas of symmetric and finite MTVs of even depth

For all
w\geq d\in\mathbb{N}
let

I_{w,d}:=\{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}:|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|=w,\dep({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=d\}.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
w\geq d\in\mathbb{N}
with
w
odd and
d
even. Then for
F=T
and
S
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}F_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=0,\quad\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}F_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=0

Proof.

By reversal relation we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}T_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}(-1)^{w}T_{\mathcal{A}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=-\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}T_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=0.

The same argument works for FMSVs. For the symmetric values, by (
2.23
) we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}T_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}T_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=(-1)^{w}\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}T_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=-\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}T_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=0.

The same argument works for SMSVs. This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Restricted sum relations

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
w,d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\leq w
. Let

\displaystyle I_{w,d,i}:=

\displaystyle\,\{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}:|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|=w,\dep({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=d,s_{i}\geq 2\}\forall 1\leq i\leq d,

\displaystyle I_{w,d,i,i}:=

\displaystyle\,\{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}:|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|=w,\dep({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=d,s_{i}\geq 3\}\forall 1\leq i\leq d,

\displaystyle I_{w,d,i,j}:=

\displaystyle\,\{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}:|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|=w,\dep({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=d,s_{i}\geq 2,s_{j}\geq 2\}\forall 1\leq j<i\leq d.

Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}M_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}M^{\star}_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d,i}}M_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{i-1}\left(\binom{w-1}{i-1}+(-1)^{d}\binom{w-1}{d-i}\right)\beta_{w},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d,i}}M^{\star}_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{i-1}\left((-1)^{d}\binom{w-1}{i-1}+\binom{w-1}{d-i}\right)\beta_{w},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d,i,j}}M_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{d}\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d,i,j}}M^{\star}_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}N_{w,d,i,j}\beta_{w},

where
\beta_{w}
is defined by (
1.5
) and

N_{w,d,i,j}
is an integer explicitly given by
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proof.

For any prime
p
and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}
, by
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{\sigma}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{d}}H_{p}({\sigma}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}))\equiv\displaystyle\sum_{{\sigma}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{d}}H^{\star}_{p}({\sigma}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}))\equiv 0\pmod{p}

where
{\mathfrak{S}}_{d}
is the group of symmetry of
d
letters. By partitioning
I_{w,d}
into
equivalent classes under permutation we see quickly that

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=0.

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}M_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}\displaystyle\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{d}<p}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\displaystyle\frac{(1+(-1)^{n_{j}})+(1-(-1)^{n_{j}})}{2n_{j}^{s_{j}}}=\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in I_{w,d}}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=0.

The second and third equations follow from the sum formulas of Saito and Wakabayashi
[
12

and the last two from
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
immediately.
∎

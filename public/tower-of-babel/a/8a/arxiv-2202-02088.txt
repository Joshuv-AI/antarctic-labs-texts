Entropy of group actions beyond uniform lattices
Till Hauser, Friedrich Martin Schneider

Abstract
. Entropy of measure preserving or continuous actions of amenable discrete groups allows for various equivalent approaches. Among them are the ones given by the techniques developed by Ollagnier and Pinchon on the one hand and the Ornstein-Weiss lemma on the other. We extend these two approaches to the context of actions of amenable topological groups. In contrast to the discrete setting, our results reveal a remarkable difference between the two concepts of entropy in the realm of non-discrete groups: while the first quantity collapses to 0 in the non-discrete case, the second yields a well-behaved invariant for amenable unimodular groups. Concerning the latter, we moreover study the corresponding notion of topological pressure, prove a Goodwyn-type theorem, and establish the equivalence with the uniform lattice approach (for locally compact groups admitting a uniform lattice). Our study elaborates on a version of the Ornstein-Weiss lemma due to Gromov.

1.
Introduction

The concept of entropy for dynamical systems was introduced by the works
of A. N. Kolmogorov
[
Kol58
]
and J. G. Sinai
[
Sin59
]
and has since facilitated numerous
applications. The wealth of insights supported by this notion has
motivated a variety of useful generalizations to large classes of group
actions. Most notably, a powerful entropy theory has been developed both
for continuous and for measure preserving actions of amenable discrete
groups (see,
e.g.,
[
STZ80
,
OP82
,
OW87
,
Oll85
,
WZ92
,
LW00
,
HYZ11
,
CSCK14
,
Yan15
,
YZ16
]
). More recent, very influential advances beyond the realm of amenable groups include
sofic
entropy
(see
[
Bow18
]
for a comprehensive account) and

naive entropy

[
DFR16
,
Bur17
]
.

In many concrete situations the acting group of a dynamical
system comes along equipped with a natural group topology that is
compatible with the action. Therefore, it seems natural to build on
amenability of topological groups (which is weaker than amenability of
the underlying discrete group) in order to develop a robust,
well-behaved entropy theory. For instance, in the study of aperiodic
order, it is natural to consider actions of non-discrete, metrizable,

\sigma
-compact, locally compact (abelian) groups, such as

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In such case, one often uses a uniform lattice
\Lambda

(for example
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) and computes the entropy
of the original action as the entropy of the restricted action of the
discrete (abelian) group
\Lambda
. However, the existence of a uniform
lattice in a locally compact group is a strong requirement, and it is
natural to ask for an intrinsic definition of entropy for actions of
general (amenable) topological groups. In

[
TZ91
]
such an intrinsic definition is presented for actions
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and for amenable unimodular locally compact groups further ideas are discussed in

[
OW87
]
.

The purpose of the present work is to put forward entropy theory for
actions of (non-discrete) amenable topological groups. To this end, we
pursue and compare two approaches based on different approximation
techniques for amenable topological groups. While the first one combines
a topological version of Følner’s amenability
criterion
[
ST18
]
with ideas of
Ollagnier and Pinchon
[
OP78
,
OP82
,
Oll85
]
, the second one builds on the
concept of
van Hove
nets and the quasi-tiling theory developed by
Ornstein and Weiss
[
OW87
]
.

One of the main difficulties in defining entropy of non-discrete topological groups is that one wishes to consider a finite partition
\alpha
and compute a common refinement

\alpha_{A}\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\bigvee\nolimits_{g\in A}\left.\!\left\{g^{-1}(A)\,\right|A\in\alpha\right\}

for certain ’averaging’ subsets
A\subseteq G
.
In the definition of entropy one requires that
\alpha_{A}
is also a finite partition for which it is crucial that
A
is finite.
In the context of discrete amenable groups that causes no problem since Følner nets can be used for averaging and consist of finite sets.
However, in the general context of amenable unimodular locally compact groups
G
one often uses certain nets of compact subsets for averaging. To be more precise, let
\theta
denote a Haar measure on a unimodular locally compact group
G
. For compact subsets
K,A\subseteq G
, the
K
-boundary of
A
is defined as
\partial_{K}A\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=K\overline{A}\cap K\overline{G\setminus A}
.
A net
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
of compact subsets of
G
is called
van Hove
if
\theta(\partial_{K}A_{i})/\theta(A_{i})
converges to
0
for every compact subset
K\subseteq G
. For discrete amenable groups, the concepts of Følner and van Hove nets agree
[
Tem84
]
.
Since van Hove sets in non-discrete amenable locally compact groups are not necessarily finite, the classical definition of entropy cannot be applied directly.

In
[
ST18
]
a generalization of Følner nets to the context of general amenable topological groups is introduced that allows to average over finite sets.
To introduce this concept, let
G
be a topological group. For finite subsets
E,F\subseteq G
and any neighbourhood
U
of the neutral element in
G
, we define the
U
-matching number

\match_{\Rsh}(E,F,U)
as
the maximum cardinality of a subset
M\subseteq E
for which there exists an injection
\iota\colon M\to F
with
x\in U\iota(x)
for all
x\in M
. A net
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
of finite, non-empty subsets of
G
is called a
thin Følner net
if, for every
g\in G
and every identity neighborhood
U
in
G
, the ratio
\match_{\Rsh}(F_{i},gF_{i},U)/|F_{i}|
converges to
1
. According to
[
ST18
, Theorem 4.5]
, the topological group
G
is amenable if and only if
G
admits a thin Følner net. Now, let
(X,\mu)
be any probability space. As usual, for a finite measurable partition
\alpha
of
X
, we consider the
Shannon entropy
defined as

H_{\mu}(\alpha)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,-\sum\nolimits_{A\in\alpha\mid\mu(A)>0}\mu(A)\log(\mu(A)).

Based on the preceding discussion, if
G
is amenable, then one may define, for any measure preserving action of
G
on
(X,\mu)
, the corresponding
Ollagnier entropy
as

\left.\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(Oll)}(\pi)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\sup\left\{\lim_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}})}{|F_{i}|}\,\right|\alpha\text{ finite measurable partition of }X\right\},

where
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
is a thin Følner net.
See Subsection
2.4

for the notion of a measure preserving action of a topological group.
In Subsection
6.2
we will show that all the limits in this formula exist and are independent of the choice of a thin Følner net.
This will be achieved by generalizing a technique of
[
Oll85
]
.
To present this generalization, we topologize the set
\mathcal{F}(G)
of all finite subsets of
G
in a natural way. For details on this topology see Subsection
3.1
.
We will show that, for every measure preserving action of
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
, the induced map

\mathcal{F}(G)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\hskip 10.00002ptF\,\longmapsto\,H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F})

is continuous, right-invariant, and satisfies Shearer’s inequality (see Subsection
2.9
for the relevant definitions). This observation will be combined with the following abstract convergence theorem, where
\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathcal{F}(G)\setminus\{\emptyset\}
.

Theorem

(Topological version of Ollagnier’s lemma; Theorem
3.4
)
.

Let
G
be an amenable topological group, and let
f\colon\mathcal{F}(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a continuous, right-invariant function satisfying Shearer’s inequality. Then, for any thin Følner net
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
in
G
, the following limit exists and satisfies

\lim_{i\in I}\frac{f(F_{i})}{|F_{i}|}\,=\,\inf_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)}\frac{f(F)}{|F|}.

Now recall that for a measure preserving action
\pi
of a (not necessarily amenable) topological group
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
we define the
naive entropy
entropy as

\operatorname{E}^{(nv)}_{\mu}(\pi)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup_{\alpha}\inf_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F})}{|F|},

where the supremum is taken over all finite measurable partitions
\alpha
of
X
.
Similarly to the well known context of actions of discrete amenable groups, we also obtain from Ollagnier’s lemma that for measure preserving actions of general amenable groups we have

\operatorname{E}^{(Oll)}_{\mu}(\pi)\,=\,\operatorname{E}^{(nv)}_{\mu}(\pi).

From the following theorem we thus observe that

\operatorname{E}^{(Oll)}_{\mu}(\pi)=0
for all measure preserving actions of non-discrete amenable groups that are metrizable or locally compact.

Theorem
.

Let
G
be a non-discrete topological group that is metrizable or locally compact.
If
\pi
is a measure preserving action of
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
, then

\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(nv)}(\pi)=0
.

We achieve this result by finding an appropriate topological model for
\pi
. We then prove a similar result for naive topological entropy and use this to show that naive topological entropy bounds naive entropy (see Corollary
6.8
below for further details). We note that the phase space is not assumed to be a Lebesgue space and that our notion of topological model differs from the standard notion.

Unfortunately, this shows that the approach via thin Følner nets does not yield a useful invariant in the context beyond discrete groups and one needs to come back to the averaging along van Hove nets. A natural idea in this context is to reconsider uniform lattices and to note that a uniform lattice is a Delone subgroup. Here we call a subset
\omega\subseteq G

Delone
if
\omega
is
uniformly discrete
(i.e., there exists a neighbourhood
V
of the identity in
G
such that
(gV)_{g\in\omega}
is a disjoint family) and
relatively dense
(i.e., there exists a compact subset
K\subseteq G
such that
K\omega=G
).
Since any locally compact topological group contains Delone sets, it is natural to ask whether one can avoid the group structure in
\omega
while defining entropy.
As a net of averaging subsets of
\omega
we consider
(A_{i}\cap\omega)_{i\in I}
, where
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
is a van Hove net in
G
.
For a measure preserving action
\pi
of an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
, we define the
Ornstein-Weiss entropy
as

\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}(\pi)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup_{\alpha}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{A_{i}\cap\omega})}{\theta(A_{i})},

where the supremum is taken over all finite measurable partitions
\alpha
of
X
.
Note that, if
\omega
is a uniform lattice in
G
and
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
is a van Hove net, then
(A_{i}\cap\omega)_{i\in I}
constitutes a Følner net in
\omega
, and
\operatorname{dens}(\omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\lim_{i\in I}|A_{i}\cap\omega|/\theta(A_{i})
exists and is independent of the choice of a van Hove net (see Lemma
6.29
).
We thus obtain directly from our definition
that the Ornstein-Weiss entropy coincides with the usual notion of entropy whenever
G
contains a uniform lattice.
It is natural to ask whether our definition depends on the choice of a Delone set and the choice of a van Hove net.

Theorem
.

The Ornstein-Weiss entropy of a measure preserving action of an amenable unimodular locally compact group on a probability space is independent of both the choice of a van Hove net and the choice of a Delone set.

We achieve this independence in Section
6
as follows. As in the context of naive entropy we first find a topological model for our action. With the additional topological structure we then present an alternative approach to Ornstein-Weiss entropy, which does not involve Delone sets. In order to show independence of the choice of a van Hove net we will apply the following topological version of the Ornstein-Weiss lemma to this alternative approach.
To state our version of the Ornstein-Weiss lemma, let us denote by
\mathcal{K}(G)
the set of all compact subsets of
G
(see Subsection
2.9
for the relevant definitions concerning real-valued functions on
\mathcal{K}(G)
).

Theorem

(Ornstein-Weiss lemma; Theorem
4.8
)
.

Let
\theta
be a Haar measure on an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
, and let
f\colon\mathcal{K}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
be monotone, right-invariant, and subadditive. Then, for every van Hove net
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
in
G
, the limit

\lim_{i\in I}\frac{f(A_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}

exists, is finite, and does not depend on the particular choice of a van Hove net
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
.

The Ornstein-Weiss lemma is based on the quasi-tiling techniques developed by D. Ornstein and B. Weiss in
[
OW87
]
. In
[
Gro99
]
M. Gromov gives a brief sketch of a proof, which is worked out in detail in
[
Kri07
,
Kri10
,
HYZ11
,
CSCK14
]
. A proof of the Ornstein-Weiss lemma is also contained in the appendix of
[
LW00
]
.
Here we do not intend to list all important references on this behalf and recommend
[
Hau20b
]
for further historical comments.
However, note that none of these proofs (aside from
[
OW87
]
and the brief sketch in
[
Gro99
]
) considers non-discrete topological groups. In
[
Hau20b
]
this result was then extended to certain non-discrete topological groups studied in the context of aperiodic order but it remained open, whether the result holds for all amenable unimodular locally compact groups.
For a proof of the full statement, we show and use a slight improvement (Proposition
4.6
) of the quasi-tiling results of
[
OW87
]
in Section
4
. We note that the claim in
[
Gro99
]
is even stronger than our version: we did not succeed in avoiding the assumption of monotonicity in our proof, and it remains open whether this is possible.

As another application of these ideas we will furthermore define and study the
Ornstein-Weiss topological pressure
of a continuous action
\pi
of an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
and
f\in C(X)
as follows.
For a finite open cover
\mathcal{U}
and a compact subset
A\subseteq G
we denote

P_{f}(\mathcal{U})\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\log\left(\sum\nolimits_{U\in\mathcal{U}}\sup\nolimits_{x\in U}e^{f(x)}\right)

and

f_{A}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{A}f(g.x)d\theta(g)
.
For a finite subset
F\subseteq G
we furthermore consider the common refinement
\mathcal{U}_{F}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigvee_{g\in F}\{g^{-1}(U)\mid U\in\mathcal{U}\}
.
We define the
Ornstein-Weiss topological pressure
of
\pi
with respect to
f
as

\operatorname{p}_{f}^{(OW)}(\pi)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\sup_{\mathcal{U}}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{P_{f_{A_{i}}}(\mathcal{U}_{A_{i}\cap\omega})}{\theta(A_{i})},

where the supremum is taken over all finite open covers
\mathcal{U}
of
X
.
Again we will present a different approach to this notion that does not require the usage of Delone sets and apply the Ornstein-Weiss lemma to it to obtain the following.

Theorem
.

The Ornstein-Weiss topological pressure of a continuous action of an amenable unimodular locally compact group on a compact Hausdorff space is independent of the choice of a van Hove net and the choice of a Delone set.

We obtain the following version of Goodwyn’s theorem. See Theorem
6.31
for details. It remains open whether the variational principle holds in this context.

Theorem
.

Let
\pi
be a continuous action of an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
. If
\mu
is a
\pi
-invariant regular Borel probability measure on
X
and
f\in C(X)
, then

\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}(\pi)+\mu(f)\,\leq\,\operatorname{p}_{f}^{(OW)}(\pi).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
General notation

Let
X
be a set. The symmetric difference of two subsets
A,B\subseteq X
will be denoted by
A\triangle B\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)
. The cardinality of a finite subset
F\subseteq X
is denoted by
|F|
. We denote by
Y^{X}
the set of all maps from
X
to another set
Y
.
We denote by
\mathcal{F}(X)
the set of all finite subsets of
X
. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{F}_{+}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathcal{F}(X)\setminus\{\emptyset\}
. For finite sets
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
of subsets of
X
, we say that
\mathcal{U}
is
finer than

\mathcal{V}
if for each
U\in\mathcal{U}
there exists
V\in\mathcal{V}
such that
U\subseteq V
.
We also write
\mathcal{V}\preceq\mathcal{U}
in this situation. For two (indexed) families
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
and
(B_{j})_{j\in J}
of sets with
I\cap J=\emptyset
, we define
(A_{i})_{i\in I}\sqcup(B_{j})_{j\in J}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(C_{k})_{k\in I\cup J}
by

C_{k}\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\begin{cases}\,A_{k}&\text{if }k\in I,\\
\,B_{k}&\text{if }k\in J\end{cases}\hskip 20.00003pt(k\in I\cup J).

Moreover, if
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of sets and
A
is a set, then we let

(A_{i})_{i\in I}\sqcup(A)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,(A_{i})_{i\in I}\sqcup(A)_{J},

where
J
is a singleton set disjoint from
I
. Now, let
X
be a topological space. Then we denote by
\mathcal{K}(X)
the set of all compact subsets of
X
.
The closure of a subset
A\subseteq X
is denoted by
\overline{A}
and we write
\partial A
for the topological boundary of
A
in
X
. Finally, let
G
be a group. For any two subsets
A,B\subseteq G
, we define
AB\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{ab\mid a\in A,\,b\in B\}
. For
A\subseteq G
and
g\in G
, we let
gA\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{g\}A
and
Ag\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A\{g\}
.

2.2.
Compact Hausdorff spaces

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space. Let
C(X)
denote the space of all
continuous functions on
X
, and let
\mathbb{U}_{X}
denote the

uniformity
of
X
, i.e., the set of all neighbourhoods (in

X\times X
) of the diagonal
\{(x,x)\mid x\in X\}
. The members of

\mathbb{U}_{X}
are called
entourages
of
X
. Let
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
. Then
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
is said to be

symmetric
if
\eta=\{(y,x)\mid(x,y)\in\eta\}
, and
\eta
is
called
open
if
\eta
is open with respect to the product
topology on
X\times X
. Furthermore, let

\eta\eta\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\{(x,z)\in X\times X\mid\exists y\in X\colon\,(x,y),(y,z)\in\eta\}.

For any
x\in X
, we define
\eta[x]\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{y\in X\mid(y,x)\in\eta\}
. A set
\mathcal{F}
of subsets of
X
is said to
be
at scale
\eta
if each
M\in\mathcal{F}
satisfies

M^{2}\subseteq\eta
. Given an open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
, a
symmetric entourage
\kappa\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
is said to be

Lebesgue
with respect to
\mathcal{U}
if for each
x\in X
there
exists
U\in\mathcal{U}
such that
\kappa[x]\subseteq U
. A standard
argument shows that any open cover of a compact Hausdorff space admits
an open Lebesgue entourage. For further details on uniformities and, in
particular, the notion of
uniform continuity
, the reader is
referred to
[
Kel55
]
.

2.3.
Topological groups

Let
G
be a topological group, i.e., a group
G
equipped with a Hausdorff topology such that the mapping
G\times G\to G,\,(g,h)\mapsto gh^{-1}
is continuous. The neighborhood filter of the neutral element in
G
will be denoted by
\mathcal{U}(G)
.
As usual, a subset
A\subseteq G
of a topological group
G
will be called
precompact
if for any
U\in\mathcal{U}(G)
there exists a finite subset
F\subseteq G
such that
A\,\subseteq\,UF
. Note that precompact subsets of a locally compact topological group are characterized by having a compact closure.

2.4.
Actions of topological groups

For a start, let
G
be a group and let
X
be a set. A map
\pi\colon G\to X\times X
is called an
action (of
G
on
X
)
if

—

for every
g\in G
, the map
\pi^{g}\colon X\to X,\,x\mapsto\pi(g,x)
is a bijection, and

—

the resulting map
G\to\Sym(X),\,g\mapsto\pi^{g}
to the full symmetric group
\Sym(X)
, i.e., the group of all bijections from
X
to
X
, is a group homomorphism.

As is customary, if the action
\pi
is clear from the context, then we also write
g.x\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\pi(g,x)
and
g.A\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\pi^{g}(A)
for
g\in G
,
x\in X
and
A\subseteq X
.

Now, let
G
be a topological group. An action
\pi
of
G
on a topological space
X
is called
continuous
if
\pi\colon G\times X\to X
is continuous with respect to the product topology on
G\times X
. Given a continuous action
\pi
of
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
, we call a regular Borel probability measure
\mu
on
X

\pi
-invariant
(or simply
invariant
) if
\mu(g.A)=\mu(A)
for every
g\in G
and every measurable subset
A\subseteq X
. Furthermore, an action
\pi
of
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
is called
measure preserving
if

(1)

\pi^{g}\colon X\to X
is measurable for every
g\in G
,

(2)

\mu(A)=\mu(g.A)
for every
g\in G
and every measurable
A\subseteq X
, and

(3)

for every measurable subset
A\subseteq X
, the map

G\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\hskip 10.00002ptg\,\longmapsto\,\mu(A\triangle g.A)

is continuous, which is equivalent, by
\pi
being measure preserving, to requiring that

\mu(A\triangle g.A)\,\longrightarrow\ 0\,\text{ as }\,g\to e_{G}.

Note that (3) is trivially satisfied whenever
G
is discrete.
More generally, if
\pi
is an action of a locally compact group
G
that satisfies (1) and (2), then (3) is implied by
\pi\colon G\times X\rightarrow X
being measurable with respect to the product
\sigma
-algebra on
G\times X
. For a proof of the latter see
[
OW87
, Chapter II.§1, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Any continuous action
\pi
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
equipped with an invariant and regular Borel probability measure
\mu
is measure preserving.

Proof.

Consider a Borel measurable subset
A\subseteq X
and let
\varepsilon>0
. By regularity of
\mu
and Urysohn’s lemma, there exists
f\in C(X)
such that

\|\chi_{A}-f\|_{1}\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}
.
Thanks to a standard argument (see, e.g.,
[
EFHN15
, Theorem 4.17]
), we find a neighborhood
U
of the neutral element in
G
such that

\left\|f-f\circ\pi^{g}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}
for every
g\in U
.
In particular, if
g\in U
, then

\displaystyle\mu(A\triangle(g^{-1}).A)

\displaystyle=\|\chi_{A}-\chi_{(g^{-1}).A}\|_{1}=\left\|\chi_{A}-\chi_{A}\circ\pi^{g}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\chi_{A}-f\right\|_{1}+\left\|f-f\circ\pi^{g}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|f\circ\pi^{g}-\chi_{A}\circ\pi^{g}\right\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon.\qed

2.5.
Amenability

We continue with a brief review of the concept of amenability for general topological groups. To this end, let
G
be a topological group. Then
G
is called
amenable
if every continuous action of
G
on a non-empty compact Hausdorff space admits an invariant regular Borel probability measure. Equivalently,
G
is amenable if and only if every continuous action of
G
by affine homeomorphisms on a non-void compact convex subset of a locally convex topological vector space has a fixed point. A well-known result by Rickert
[
Ric67
, Theorem 4.2]
asserts that
G
is amenable if and only if there exists a left-invariant mean on the space of right-uniformly continuous bounded real-valued functions on
G
. For more details on amenability of general topological groups, we refer to
[
Pes06
,
GdlH17
]
.

For the purposes of the present work, a characterization of amenability of topological groups in terms of almost invariant finite non-empty subsets (Theorem
2.3
below) will be relevant. This result is due to
[
ST18
]
and constitutes a topological version of Følner’s amenability criterion
[
Føl55
]
. To recall the precise statement, we will clarify some convenient notation concerning matchings in bipartite graphs, essentially following
[
ST18
]
. Let us consider a
bipartite graph
, that is, a triple
\mathcal{B}=(E,F,R)
consisting of two finite sets
E
and
F
and some subset
R\subseteq E\times F
. A
matching
in
\mathcal{B}
is an injective mapping
\varphi\colon D\to F
such that
D\subseteq E
and
(x,\varphi(x))\in R
for all
x\in D
. The
matching number
of
\mathcal{B}
is defined as

\match(\mathcal{B})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup\{|D|\mid\varphi\colon D\to F\textnormal{ matching in }\mathcal{B}\}.

With regard to amenability of topological groups, the following type of bipartite graphs and matching numbers has been revealed as relevant in
[
ST18
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a topological group. For
U\in\mathcal{U}(G)
, let us define

U_{\Rsh}\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\left\{(x,y)\in G\times G\left|\,xy^{-1}\in U\right\}\!.\right.

For
E,F\in\mathcal{F}(G)
and
U\in\mathcal{U}(G)
, we define

\match_{\Rsh}(E,F,U)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\match\!\left(E,F,U_{\Rsh}\cap(E\times F)\right).

A net
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
of non-empty finite subsets of
G
will be called a
thin Følner net
in
G
if, for every
U\in\mathcal{U}(G)
and for every
g\in G
,

\frac{\match_{\Rsh}(F_{i},gF_{i},U)}{|F_{i}|}\,\overset{i\in I}{\longrightarrow}\,1.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
ST18
]
, Theorem 4.5)
.

A topological group is amenable if and only if it admits a thin Følner net.

For later use, let us include the following reformulation of the thin Følner property.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a topological group. For
F\in\mathcal{F}(G)
,
U\in\mathcal{U}(G)
and
g\in G
,

\match_{\Rsh}(F,gF,U)\,=\,\sup\nolimits_{\gamma\in\mathrm{Sym}(F)}|\{x\in F\mid(\gamma(x),gx)\in U_{\Rsh}\}|.

Proof.

(
\leq
) Let
\varphi\colon F\to gF
be a bijection and consider the set
D\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{x\in F\mid(x,\varphi(x))\in U_{\Rsh}\}
. Of course,
\gamma\colon F\to F,\,x\mapsto\varphi^{-1}(gx)
is a bijection. Note that
\varphi(x)=g\gamma^{-1}(x)
for each
x\in F
. Let
C\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{x\in F\mid(\gamma(x),gx)\in U_{\Rsh}\}
. For every
x\in F
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}(x)\in C\

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ \left(\gamma\!\left(\gamma^{-1}(x)\right),g\gamma^{-1}(x)\right)\in U_{\Rsh}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \left(x,g\gamma^{-1}(x)\right)\in U_{\Rsh}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ (x,\varphi(x))\in U_{\Rsh}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x\in D,

which means that
\gamma^{-1}(D)=C
, which in turn implies that
|C|=|D|
.

(
\geq
) Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{Sym}(F)
and define
C\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{x\in F\mid(\gamma(x),gx)\in U_{\Rsh}\}
. We consider the bijection
\varphi\colon F\to gF,\,x\mapsto g\gamma^{-1}(x)
and let
D\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{x\in F\mid(x,\varphi(x))\in U_{\Rsh}\}
. For every
x\in F
,

\displaystyle\gamma(x)\in D\

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ (\gamma(x),\varphi(\gamma(x)))\in U_{\Rsh}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (\gamma(x),gx)\in U_{\Rsh}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x\in C,

which means that
\gamma(C)=D
. Hence,
|C|=|D|
. ∎

2.6.
Geometric notions

We continue with a few bits of geometric terminology. Again, let
G
be a topological group.
A subset
\omega\subseteq G
is called

uniformly discrete
if there exists an identity neighborhood
U\in\mathcal{U}(G)
such that
(gU)_{g\in\omega}
is a disjoint family, in which case the set
\omega
will also be called
U
-discrete
.
A subset
\omega\subseteq G
is called

—

relatively dense
if there exists a compact subset
K\subseteq G
such that
K\omega=G
,

—

Delone
if
\omega
is uniformly discrete and relatively dense,

—

locally finite
if
\omega\cap K
is finite for every compact
K\subseteq G
.

Note that any Delone set in a locally compact topological group is locally finite.

2.7.
van Hove nets

Let
G
be a locally compact topological group.
Recall that
G
admits a
(left) Haar measure
, that is, a non-zero, regular, locally finite Borel measure
\theta
on
G
which satisfies
\theta(A)=\theta(gA)
for all Borel measurable
A\subseteq G
and all
g\in G
.
Haar measure is unique up to scaling, i.e., for two Haar measures
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
on
G
there exists a constant
c
such that
\theta^{\prime}=c\theta
. As usual,
G
is called
unimodular
if some (every) Haar measure
\theta
on
G
furthermore satisfies
\theta(Ag)=\theta(A)
for all Borel measurable
A\subseteq G
and all
g\in G
.

Throughout the manuscript, whenever
G
is assumed to be a locally compact group, we let
\theta
denote a fixed Haar measure on
G
. Now, let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group. For any measurable subset
A\subseteq G
we have
\theta(A)=\theta(A^{-1})
.
We define

\partial_{K}A\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=K\overline{A}\cap K\overline{G\setminus A}

for subsets
K,A\subseteq G
.
Note that
\partial_{K}A
is compact and hence measurable, whenever
K
is compact and
A
is precompact.
For
\varepsilon>0
and a compact subset
K\subseteq G
we say that a precompact and measurable subset
A\subseteq G
of positive Haar measure is

(\varepsilon,K)
-invariant
if

\alpha(A,K)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{\theta(\partial_{K}A)}{\theta(A)}<\varepsilon.

Recall from the introduction that a net
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
of compact subsets of
G
is called
van Hove
if
\lim_{i\in I}\alpha(A_{i},K)=0
for every compact
K\subseteq G
(where we assume implicitly that
\theta(A_{i})>0
for sufficiently large
i\in I
). A unimodular locally compact group is amenable if and only if it admits a van Hove net
[
Tem84
,
Hau20b
]
.
With the arguments from
[
Hau20a
, Subsection 2.6]
it is straightforward to obtain the following. We include some comments on the proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group and let
\theta
be a Haar measure on
G
. Let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a van Hove net in
G
and let
K\subseteq G
be non-empty and compact.

(i)

(KA_{i})_{i\in I}
is a van Hove net which satisfies

\lim_{i\in I}\theta(KA_{i})/\theta(A_{i})=1
.

(ii)

There exists a van Hove net
(B_{i})_{i\in I}
(with the same index set) for which we have
\lim_{i\in I}\theta(B_{i})/\theta(A_{i})=1
and which satisfies
KB_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
and
B_{i}^{c}\subseteq K^{-1}A_{i}^{c}
for all
i\in I
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to show (i)
[
Hau20b
, Proposition 2.3]
. In order to see (ii) we set

B_{i}:=\overline{\{g\in G;\,Kg\subseteq A_{i}\}}
and follow well known arguments as in
[
Hau20a
, Proposition 2.2]
in order to show
\lim_{i\in I}\theta(B_{i})/\theta(A_{i})=1
and
KB_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
. Furthermore, for
g\in B_{i}^{c}
we have
Kg\cap A_{i}^{c}\neq\emptyset
and hence
g\in K^{-1}A_{i}^{c}
.
∎

2.8.
Uniform lattices and van Hove nets

Let
G
be a locally compact group. A discrete subgroup
\omega
of
G
is called a
uniform lattice
if the quotient
G/\omega
is compact, i.e.,
\omega
is a Delone subgroup of
G
. A subset
C\subseteq G
is said to be
regular
if
\theta(\partial C)=0
for some (any) left Haar measure
\theta
on
G
.
A fundamental domain of a uniform lattice
\omega
is a Borel measurable set
C\subseteq G
such that for any
g\in G
there exist unique
c\in C
and
v\in\omega
such that
g=cv
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Any uniform lattice
\omega
in a unimodular locally compact group
G
admits a regular and precompact fundamental domain with non-empty interior.

Proof.

We first show that
G
admits a compact, regular neighbourhood of
e_{G}
which satisfies
M^{-1}M\cap\omega=\{e_{G}\}
. Choose a compact neighbourhood
\hat{M}
of
e_{G}
which satisfies
\hat{M}^{-1}\hat{M}\cap\omega=\{e_{G}\}
.
From
[
Kel55
, Chapter 6]
we know that there exist finitely many continuous pseudometrics
d_{1},\dots,d_{J}
on
\hat{M}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\eta\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\bigcap\nolimits_{j=1}^{J}\!\left.\left\{(x,y)\in\hat{M}^{2}\,\right|d_{j}(x,y)<\varepsilon\right\}\,\in\,\mathbb{U}_{\hat{M}}

and such that
\eta[e_{G}]
is contained in the interior of
\hat{M}
.
By a standard argument involving Froda’s theorem, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,J\}
there exists
r_{j}\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that the closed
d_{j}
-ball
\overline{B}_{r_{j}}^{d_{j}}(e_{G})
is regular. Then
M:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{J}\overline{B}_{r_{j}}^{d_{j}}(e_{G})
is a compact and regular neighbourhood of
e_{G}
which satisfies
M^{-1}M\cap\omega=\{e_{G}\}
.

Let
K\subseteq G
be a compact subset such that
K\omega=G
. Then
\{gU\mid g\in K\}
is an open cover of
K
and thus there exists a finite sequence
(g_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
such that
\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}g_{n}U\supseteq K
.
From
[
Mor15
,
Hau20b
]
we know that

C\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}g_{n}M\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}g_{i}M\omega\right)
is a precompact fundamental domain. Setting
F\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\omega\cap(\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}g_{n}M)^{-1}(\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}g_{n}M)
we obtain a finite set with

C\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}g_{n}M\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}g_{i}MF\right)
and
the regularity of
M
implies
C
to be regular. Since
M
has non-empty interior, so does
C
.
∎

2.9.
Combinatorial properties of set functions

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a set.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, a
k
-cover
of
X
is a finite family
(C_{i})_{i\in I}
of subsets of
X
such that

\forall x\in X\colon\hskip 20.00003pt|\{i\in I\mid x\in C_{i}\}|\,\geq\,k.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
be closed under finite unions and binary intersections. A map
f\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

—

is called
monotone
if
f(E)\leq f(F)
for all
E,F\in\mathcal{F}
with
E\subseteq F
,

—

is called
subadditive
if
f(\emptyset)=0
and
f(E\cup F)\leq f(E)+f(F)
for all
E,F\in\mathcal{F}
,

—

satisfies Shearer’s inequality
if, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, every
F\in\mathcal{F}
and every
k
-cover
(C_{i})_{i\in I}
of
F
by members of
\mathcal{F}
, we have
f(F)\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum\nolimits_{i\in I}f(C_{i}),

—

is called
strongly subadditive
if
f(\emptyset)=0
and
f(E\cup F)\leq f(E)+f(F)-f(E\cap F)
for all
E,F\in\mathcal{F}
.

The concept of covering multiplicity originates in the work of
[
Kel59
]
.
The notion of satisfaction of Shearer’s inequality stated above is a priori weaker that the one defined in
[
DFR16
]
, but is easily seen to be equivalent for monotone set functions. Also, it is shown in
[
DFR16
, Proposition 2.4]
that, for monotone set functions, strong subadditivity implies Shearer’s inequality, which in turn implies subadditivity.

3.
The Ollagnier lemma

In this section we will be concerned with a topological version of Ollagnier’s ergodic theorem
[
Oll85
, Proposition 3.1.9]
and with topological and measure-theoretic entropy of continuous action of amenable topological groups. For a start, we briefly agree on some terminology concerning continuity of real-valued set functions.

3.1.
Continuity of set functions

We are going to address some continuity matters for set functions. More
precisely, we will endow that set of all finite subsets of a topological
group with a suitable topology. For this purpose, let
G
be a
topological group. For any finite subset
F\subseteq G
and an identity
neighborhood
U\in\mathcal{U}(G)
, we define

U[F]\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(G)\mid\exists\varphi\colon F^{\prime}\to F\text{ bijective }\forall x\in F^{\prime}\colon\,x\varphi(x)^{-1}\in U\}.

It is straightforward to verify that

\{T\subseteq\mathcal{F}(G)\mid\forall F\in T\,\exists U\in\mathcal{U}(G)\colon\,U[F]\subseteq T\}.

constitutes a topology on
\mathcal{F}(G)
. Moreover,
for each
F\in\mathcal{F}(G)
, the collection

\{U[F]\mid U\in\mathcal{U}(G)\}

is a neighborhood basis of
F
in the resulting
topological space
\mathcal{F}(G)
. Henceforth, whenever the set of all
finite subsets of a topological group is considered as a topological
space, any statement will be referring to this topology. In connection
with the concept of set functions discussed in
Subsection
2.9
, we note that an important
family of continuous set functions on topological groups is provided by
Lemma
6.13
.

For later use, we record some additional observations concerning the topology introduced above and the Følner-type matching conditions for amenability discussed in Subsection
2.5
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be any non-discrete topological group. If
U\in\mathcal{U}(G)
and
E,F\in\mathcal{F}(G)
, then there exists
F^{\prime}\in U[F]
such that

\forall g\in E\setminus\{e_{G}\}\colon\hskip 10.00002ptF^{\prime}\cap gF^{\prime}\,=\,\emptyset.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{Z}
the set of all pairs
(F^{\prime},\varphi)
consisting of a finite subset
F^{\prime}\subseteq G
and an injective map
\varphi\colon F^{\prime}\to F
such that
x\varphi(x)^{-1}\in U
for all
x\in F^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime}\cap gF^{\prime}=\emptyset
for every
g\in E\setminus\{e_{G}\}
. Let
(F^{\prime},\varphi)\in\mathcal{Z}
such that
|F^{\prime}|=\sup\{|F^{\prime\prime}|\mid(F^{\prime\prime},\psi)\in\mathcal{Z}\}
. We claim that
|F^{\prime}|=|F|
. On the contrary, assume that
|F^{\prime}|<|F|
. Then there exists
y\in F\setminus\varphi(F^{\prime})
. Since
G
is a perfect Hausdorff space, every open non-empty subset of
G
is infinite. Consequently, there exists some

x\,\in\,Uy\setminus\left(F^{\prime}\cup\bigcup\nolimits_{g\in E}gF^{\prime}\cup g^{-1}F^{\prime}\right).

We define
F^{\prime\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=F^{\prime}\cup\{x\}
and
\psi\colon F^{\prime\prime}\to F
such that
\psi|_{F^{\prime}}=\varphi
and
\psi(x)=y
. We observe that
(F^{\prime\prime},\psi)
is a member of
\mathcal{Z}
. Since
|F^{\prime}|<|F^{\prime\prime}|
, this clearly contradicts our hypothesis. Hence,
|F^{\prime}|=|F|
and therefore
\varphi
is a bijection. This finishes the proof. ∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a topological group. If
F\in\mathcal{F}(G)
,
U\in\mathcal{U}(G)
and
x\in G
, then

U[Fx]\,=\,\{F^{\prime}x\mid F^{\prime}\in U[F]\}.

Proof.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}(G)
,
U\in\mathcal{U}(G)
and
x\in G
. If
E\in U[Fx]
, then there exists a bijection
\varphi\colon E\to Fx
such that
y\varphi(y)^{-1}\in U_{\Rsh}
for all
y\in E
, and so
\psi\colon Ex^{-1}\to F,\,y\mapsto\varphi(yx)x^{-1}
is a well-defined bijection satisfying

y\psi(y)^{-1}\!\,=\,y\!\left(\varphi(yx)x^{-1}\right)^{-1}\!\,=\,yx\varphi(yx)^{-1}\!\,\in\,U

for all
y\in Ex^{-1}
, whence
Ex^{-1}\in U[F]
and thus

E\,=\,Ex^{-1}x\,\in\,\{F^{\prime}x\mid F^{\prime}\in U[F]\}.

Conversely, if
F^{\prime}\in U[F]
, then there exists a bijective map
\varphi\colon F^{\prime}\to F
with
y\varphi(y)^{-1}\in U
for all
y\in F^{\prime}
, so that
\psi\colon F^{\prime}x\to Fx,\,y\mapsto\varphi(yx^{-1})x
is a well-defined bijection satisfying

y\psi(y)^{-1}\!\,=\,y\!\left(\varphi\!\left(yx^{-1}\right)x\right)^{-1}\!\,=\,yx^{-1}\varphi\!\left(yx^{-1}\right)^{-1}\!\,\in\,U

for all
y\in F^{\prime}x
, which shows that
F^{\prime}x\in U[Fx]
. This completes the proof. ∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a topological group, let
E,F_{0}\in\mathcal{F}(G)
, and let
U,V\in\mathcal{U}(G)
such that
V^{-1}=V
and
VVV\subseteq U
. Let
W\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigcap_{g\in E\cup\{e_{G}\}}g^{-1}Vg
. Then for all
F\in W[F_{0}]
and
g\in E
,

\match_{\Rsh}(F,gF,U)\,\geq\,\match_{\Rsh}(F_{0},gF_{0},W).

Proof.

Let
F\in W[F_{0}]
and
g\in E
. Fix any bijection
\varphi\colon F\to F_{0}
such that
(x,\varphi(x))\in W_{\Rsh}
for all
x\in F
. By Lemma
2.4
, there is a permutation
\gamma_{0}
on
F_{0}
with
|D_{0}|=\match_{\Rsh}(F_{0},gF_{0},W)
for

D_{0}\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\{x\in F_{0}\mid(\gamma_{0}(x),gx)\in W_{\Rsh}\}.

Consider
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\varphi^{-1}\circ\gamma_{0}\circ\varphi\in\Sym(F)
and let
D\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{x\in F\mid(\gamma(x),gx)\in U_{\Rsh}\}
. We claim that
\varphi^{-1}(D_{0})\subseteq D
. To prove this, let
x\in\varphi^{-1}(D_{0})
. Then
(\gamma_{0}(\varphi(x)),g\varphi(x))\in W_{\Rsh}
. Moreover, by our choice of
\varphi
, both

(\gamma(x),\gamma_{0}(\varphi(x)))\,=\,(\gamma(x),\varphi(\gamma(x)))\,\in\,W_{\Rsh}

and
(x,\varphi(x))\in W_{\Rsh}
, thus
gx(g\varphi(x))^{-1}=gx\varphi(x)^{-1}g^{-1}\in gWg^{-1}\subseteq V
, i.e.,
(g\varphi(x),gx)\in V_{\Rsh}
. It follows that
(\gamma(x),gx)\in U_{\Rsh}
, that is,
x\in D
. This proves our claim. Hence,

\match_{\Rsh}(F,gF,U)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{lemma:folner.permutations}}}{{\geq}}\,|D|\,\geq\,\left\lvert\varphi^{-1}(D_{0})\right\rvert\,=\,|D_{0}|\,=\,\match_{\Rsh}(F_{0},gF_{0},W).\qed

3.2.
Proof of Ollagnier’s lemma for topological groups

We have prepared everything to prove the announced topological version of Ollagnier’s convergence theorem.

Theorem 3.4

(Ollagnier lemma)
.

Let
G
be an amenable topological group. Furthermore, let
f\colon\mathcal{F}(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a continuous and right-invariant function satisfying Shearer’s inequality. Then, for any thin Følner net
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
in
G
, the following limit exists and satisfies

\lim\nolimits_{i\in I}\frac{f(F_{i})}{|F_{i}|}\,=\,\inf\nolimits_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)}\frac{f(F)}{|F|}.

Remark 3.5
.

In
[
Oll85
]
a proof of the theorem in the context of discrete amenable groups is presented. We thus assume in our proof w.l.o.g. that
G
is a non-discrete amenable topological group. Furthermore, we abbreviate
\diam M:=\sup_{(x,y)\in M^{2}}|x-y|
for
M\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

To prove the desired convergence, it suffices to show that

(1)

\forall K\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)\colon\hskip 10.00002pt\limsup\nolimits_{i\in I}\frac{f(F_{i})}{|F_{i}|}\,\leq\,\frac{f(K)}{|K|}.

For this purpose, let
K\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)
and
\varepsilon>0
. By right invariance of
f
, we may and will assume that
K
contains the neutral element of
G
. Since
K
is finite and
f
is continuous, there exists
U\in\mathcal{U}(G)
such that
U^{-1}=U
,
KK^{-1}\cap UU=\{e_{G}\}
and

(2)

\forall K^{\prime}\subseteq K\colon\hskip 10.00002pt\diam f(U[K^{\prime}])\,\leq\,\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

For each
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
and every
x\in G
, right invariance of
f
readily implies that

(3)

\diam f(U[K^{\prime}x])\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lemma:translated.neighborhood}}}{{=}}\,\diam\{f(K^{\prime\prime}x)\mid K^{\prime\prime}\in U[K^{\prime}]\}\,=\,\diam f(U[K^{\prime}])\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{approximation0}}}{{\leq}}\,\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Consider
C\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max\{1,\sup\{f(K^{\prime})\mid K^{\prime}\subseteq K\}\}
and note that, by right invariance of
f
,

(4)

\sup\{f(K^{\prime}x)\mid K^{\prime}\subseteq K,\,x\in G\}\,\leq\,C.

Let
\tau\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=1-\frac{\varepsilon}{3C|K|}
. Furthermore, let
V\in\mathcal{U}(G)
such that
V^{-1}=V
and
V^{3}\subseteq U
. Define

W\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in K\cup K^{-1}}g^{-1}Vg.

Since
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
constitutes a thin Følner net in
G
, there exists
i_{0}\in I
such that

(5)

\forall i\in I,\,i_{0}\leq i\ \forall g\in K\colon\hskip 10.00002pt\match_{\Rsh}(F_{i},gF_{i},W)\,\geq\,\tau|F_{i}|.

Let
i\in I
with
i_{0}\leq i
. Since
f
is continuous, there exists some
W_{0}\in\mathcal{U}(G)
such that
W_{0}\subseteq W
and
\diam f(W_{0}[F_{i}])\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
. By Lemma
3.1

and since we assume
G
to be non-discrete, there exists
F\in W_{0}[F_{i}]
with
F\cap gF=\emptyset
for every
g\in K\setminus\{e_{G}\}
. In particular, we deduce from
\diam f(W_{0}[F_{i}])\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

(6)

|f(F_{i})-f(F)|\,\leq\,\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Furthermore, thanks to Lemma
3.3
, assertion (
5
), and our choice of
W_{0}\subseteq W
, it follows that
\match_{\Rsh}(F,gF,U)\geq\match_{\Rsh}(F_{i},gF_{i},W)
whenever
g\in K
. Hence, for each
g\in K\setminus\{e_{G}\}
, there exists an injection
\psi_{g}\colon D_{g}\to gF
such that
D_{g}\subseteq F
,
|D_{g}|\geq\tau|F|
, and

(7)

\forall x\in D_{g}\colon\hskip 10.00002pt(x,\psi_{g}(x))\,\in\,U_{\Rsh}.

For each
g\in K\setminus\{e_{G}\}
, consider the bijection
\varphi_{g}\colon G\to G
defined by

\varphi_{g}(x)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\begin{cases}\,\psi_{g}^{-1}(x)&\text{if }x\in\psi_{g}(D_{g}),\\
\,\psi_{g}(x)&\text{if }x\in D_{g},\\
\,x&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\hskip 20.00003pt(x\in G).

Put
D_{e_{G}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=F
,
\psi_{e_{G}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\id_{F}
, and
\varphi_{e_{G}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\id_{G}
. Let

\gamma\,\colon\,K\times G\,\longrightarrow\,G,\hskip 10.00002pt(g,x)\,\longmapsto\,\varphi_{g}(gx).

We now claim that

(8)

\forall g_{0},g_{1}\in K\,\forall x\in G\colon\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma(g_{0},x)=\gamma(g_{1},x)\ \Longrightarrow\ g_{0}=g_{1}.

Indeed, if
x\in G
and
g_{0},g_{1}\in K
with
\gamma(g_{0},x)=\gamma(g_{1},x)
, then from
U^{-1}=U
and

(g_{0}x,\gamma(g_{0},x))\,=\,(g_{0}x,\varphi_{g_{0}}(g_{0}x))\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{matching}}}{{\in}}\,U_{\Rsh},\hskip 20.00003pt(g_{1}x,\gamma(g_{1},x))\,=\,(g_{1}x,\varphi_{g_{1}}(g_{1}x))\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{matching}}}{{\in}}\,U_{\Rsh}

we infer that
(g_{0}x,g_{1}x)\in(UU)_{\Rsh}
and therefore
g_{0}g_{1}^{-1}=(g_{0}x)(g_{1}x)^{-1}\in UU
, whence
g_{0}=g_{1}
as
KK^{-1}\cap UU=\{e_{G}\}
. Next, we prove that

(9)

\forall y\in G\colon\hskip 10.00002pt|\{x\in G\mid\exists g\in K\colon\,\gamma(g,x)=y\}|\,=\,|K|.

To see this, let
y\in G
. For each
g\in K
, the map
G\to G,\,x\mapsto\gamma(g,x)
is a bijection, whence

|\{x\in G\mid\gamma(g,x)=y\}|\,=\,1.

Since

\{x\in G\mid\exists g\in K\colon\,\gamma(g,x)=y\}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{injective}}}{{=}}\,\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\boldsymbol{\cdot}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\boldsymbol{\cdot}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\boldsymbol{\cdot}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\boldsymbol{\cdot}\crcr}}}}\displaylimits\nolimits_{g\in K}\{x\in G\mid\gamma(g,x)=y\},

it follows that

|\{x\in G\mid\exists g\in K\colon\,\gamma(g,x)=y\}|\,=\,\sum\nolimits_{g\in K}|\{x\in G\mid\gamma(g,x)=y\}|\,=\,|K|.

This proves (
9
). Now, we consider the finite set

H\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\{x\in G\mid\gamma(K\times\{x\})\cap F\neq\emptyset\}\,=\,\bigcup\nolimits_{g\in K}g^{-1}\varphi_{g}^{-1}(F)

and note that

(10)

|H|\,\leq\,\sum\nolimits_{g\in K}\left\lvert g^{-1}\varphi_{g}^{-1}(F)\right\rvert\,=\,|K||F|.

We observe that, by (
9
),

\forall y\in F\colon\hskip 10.00002pt|\{x\in H\mid y\in\gamma(K\times\{x\})\cap F\}|\,=\,|K|,

which entails that

(\gamma(K\times\{x\})\cap F)_{x\in H}
is a
|K|
-covering of
F
.
Since
f
satisfies Shearer’s inequality we observe

(11)

f(F)\,\leq\,\frac{1}{|K|}\sum\nolimits_{x\in H}f(\gamma(K\times\{x\})\cap F).

Moreover, for each
x\in G
, we have
|\gamma(K\times\{x\})|=|K|
due to (
8
), and then

(12)

\gamma(K\times\{x\})\,\in\,U[Kx]

by (
7
) and
U=U^{-1}
, wherefore

(13)

|f(\gamma(K\times\{x\}))-f(Kx)|\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{approximation0.1}}}{{\leq}}\,\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Also, if
x\in G
, then (
12
) implies that
\gamma(K\times\{x\})\cap F\in U[K^{\prime}x]
for some
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
, so that

|f(\gamma(K\times\{x\})\cap F)-f(K^{\prime}x)|\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{approximation0.1}}}{{\leq}}\,\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

and thus

(14)

f(\gamma(K\times\{x\})\cap F)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{supremum}}}{{\leq}}\,C+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Furthermore, since
e_{G}\in K
and
\varphi_{e_{G}}=\id_{G}
,

H_{0}\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\{x\in G\mid\gamma(K\times\{x\})\subseteq F\}\,=\,\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in K}g^{-1}\varphi_{g}^{-1}(F)\,=\,\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in K}\{x\in F\mid\varphi_{g}(gx)\in F\}.

Now, if
g\in K
and
x\in g^{-1}\psi_{g}(D_{g})
, then
\varphi_{g}(gx)=\psi^{-1}_{g}(gx)\in F
. This entails that

\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in K}g^{-1}\psi_{g}(D_{g})\,\subseteq\,\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in K}\{x\in F\mid\varphi_{g}(gx)\in F\}\,=\,H_{0}

and, in turn,

\displaystyle|H_{0}|\,

\displaystyle\geq\,\left\lvert\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in K}g^{-1}\psi_{g}(D_{g})\right\rvert\,=\,|F|-\left\lvert\bigcup\nolimits_{g\in K}F\setminus g^{-1}\psi_{g}(D_{g})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\,|F|-\sum\nolimits_{g\in K}\left\lvert F\setminus g^{-1}\psi_{g}(D_{g})\right\rvert\,=\,|F|-\sum\nolimits_{g\in K}|F\setminus D_{g}|

\displaystyle\geq\,|F|-|K|(1-\tau)|F|\,=\,\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{3C}\right)|F|.

Hence, considering the set

H_{1}\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,H\setminus H_{0}\,=\,\bigcup\nolimits_{g\in K}\left(g^{-1}\varphi_{g}^{-1}(F)\right)\!\setminus\,\bigcap\nolimits_{{g}^{\prime}\in K}\left(g^{\prime-1}\varphi_{g^{\prime}}^{-1}(F)\right)\!,

we conclude that

(15)

|H_{1}|\,\leq\,\sum\nolimits_{g\in K}\left\lvert\left(g^{-1}\varphi_{g}^{-1}(F)\right)\!\setminus H_{0}\right\rvert\,\leq\,|K|\left(|F|-|H_{0}|\right)\,\leq\,\frac{\varepsilon|F||K|}{3C}.

Consequently, it follows that

\displaystyle f(F_{i})\,

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{approximation1}}}{{\leq}}\,f(F)+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{shearer}}}{{\leq}}\,\frac{1}{|K|}\sum\nolimits_{x\in H}f(\gamma(K\times\{x\})\cap F)+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{|K|}\sum\nolimits_{x\in H_{0}}f(\gamma(K\times\{x\})\cap F)+\frac{1}{|K|}\sum\nolimits_{x\in H_{1}}f(\gamma(K\times\{x\})\cap F)+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{|K|}\sum\nolimits_{x\in H_{0}}f(\gamma(K\times\{x\}))+\frac{1}{|K|}\sum\nolimits_{x\in H_{1}}f(\gamma(K\times\{x\})\cap F)+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{approximation2}+\eqref{approximation3}}}{{\leq}}\,\frac{1}{|K|}\sum\nolimits_{x\in H_{0}}f(Kx)+\frac{\varepsilon|H_{0}|}{3|K|}+\frac{\left(C+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)|H_{1}|}{|K|}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{|K|}\sum\nolimits_{x\in H_{0}}f(K)+\frac{\varepsilon|H|}{3|K|}+\frac{C|H_{1}|}{|K|}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{cardinality}+\eqref{estimate}}}{{\leq}}\,\frac{f(K)|H_{0}|}{|K|}+\frac{\varepsilon|F|}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon|F|}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{f(K)|H_{0}|}{|K|}+\varepsilon|F|\,\leq\,\frac{f(K)|F|}{|K|}+\varepsilon|F|,

and therefore

\frac{f(F_{i})}{|F_{i}|}\,=\,\frac{f(F_{i})}{|F|}\,\leq\,\frac{f(K)}{|K|}+\varepsilon.

This proves (
1
) and hence the theorem. ∎

4.
The Ornstein-Weiss lemma

As described in the introduction we will next present a proof of the Ornstein-Weiss lemma for non-discrete locally compact groups using quasi-tiling techniques, which is based on
[
OW87
,
Gro99
,
LW00
,
Kri07
,
CSCK14
]
. Throughout this section, let
G
be an amenable unimodular locally compact group and recall that
\theta
denotes a Haar measure on
G
.

4.1.
On
\varepsilon
-disjointness and invariance

Let
\varepsilon>0
. A finite family
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
of compact subsets of
G
is called
\varepsilon
-disjoint
if there exists a family of compact subsets
B_{i}\subseteq A_{i}

(i\in I)
such that
(B_{i})_{i\in I}
are pairwise disjoint and
\theta(B_{i})>(1-\varepsilon)\theta(A_{i})
for each
i\in I
. For every
\varepsilon
-disjoint finite family
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
of compact subsets of
G
, it is straightforward to show that

(1-\varepsilon)\sum\nolimits_{i\in I}\theta(A_{i})\,\leq\,\theta\!\left(\bigcup\nolimits_{i\in I}A_{i}\right).

The lemmas in this subsection are standard and can for example be found in
[
OW87
,
Kri07
]
. We include the short proofs for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(A_{i})_{i\in F}
be a finite and
\varepsilon
-disjoint family of compact subsets of
G
. Then, for every compact
A\subseteq G
with
\theta(A\cap(\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}))<\varepsilon\theta(A)
, the extended finite family
(A_{i})_{i\in I}\sqcup(A)
is
\varepsilon
-disjoint.

Proof.

As
(A_{i})_{i\in F}
is
\varepsilon
-disjoint there exist disjoint compact subsets
B_{i}\subseteq A_{i}

(i\in F)
such that
\theta(B_{i})>(1-\varepsilon)\theta(A_{i})
for each
i\in F
.
By our assumptions,

\theta\!\left(A\setminus\left(\bigcup\nolimits_{i\in F}A_{i}\right)\right)\!\,\geq\,\theta(A)-\theta\!\left(\bigcup\nolimits_{i\in F}A_{i}\right)\!\,>\,(1-\varepsilon)\theta(A).

Thus, there is
\rho>0
such that

(1-\rho)\theta(A\setminus\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i})>(1-\varepsilon)\theta(A)
.
As
\theta
is regular, there exists a compact subset
B\subseteq A\setminus\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}
such that
\theta(B)\geq(1-\rho)\theta(A\setminus\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i})\geq(1-\varepsilon)\theta(A)
. For each
i\in I
, the set
B
is disjoint from
A_{i}
, thus disjoint from
B_{i}
. Hence,
(A_{i})_{i\in I}\sqcup(A)
is
\varepsilon
-disjoint.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}(G)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. If
(A_{i})_{i\in F}
is an
\varepsilon
-disjoint finite family of non-empty, compact subsets of
G
, then

\alpha\!\left(\bigcup\nolimits_{i\in F}A_{i},K\right)\!\,\leq\,\frac{\max_{i\in F}\alpha(A_{i},K)}{1-\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let us abbreviate
M\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max_{i}\alpha(A_{i},K)=\max_{i}\theta(\partial_{K}A_{i})/\theta(A_{i})
.
A straightforward argument shows that
\partial_{K}(\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i})\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in F}\partial_{K}A_{i}
, and hence

\displaystyle\theta\!\left(\partial_{K}\!\left(\bigcup\nolimits_{i\in F}A_{i}\right)\right)\!\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\theta\!\left(\bigcup\nolimits_{i\in F}\partial_{K}A_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\sum\nolimits_{i\in F}\theta(\partial_{K}A_{i})\,=\,\sum\nolimits_{i\in F}\theta(A_{i})\frac{\theta(\partial_{K}A_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\,\leq\,M\sum\nolimits_{i\in F}\theta(A_{i}).

Since the considered family is
\varepsilon
-disjoint, we obtain that
(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{i\in F}\theta(A_{i})\leq\theta\left(\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}\right)
and thus conclude that

\alpha\!\left(\bigcup\nolimits_{i\in F}A_{i},K\right)\!\,=\,\frac{\theta\!\left(\partial_{K}\bigcup\nolimits_{i\in F}A_{i}\right)}{\theta\!\left(\bigcup\nolimits_{i\in F}A_{i}\right)}\,\leq\,\frac{M\sum_{i\in F}\theta(A_{i})}{(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{i\in F}\theta(A_{i})}\,=\,\frac{M}{1-\varepsilon}.\qed

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}(G)
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Any precompact and measurable subsets
R,B\subseteq G
with
0<\theta(B)\leq\theta(R)
and
\theta(R\setminus B)\geq\varepsilon\theta(R)
satisfy

\alpha(R\setminus B,K)\leq\frac{\alpha(R,K)+\alpha(B,K)}{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

A straightforward argument shows that
\partial_{K}\left(R\setminus B\right)\subseteq\partial_{K}R\cup\partial_{K}B
. Thus, if
\varepsilon>0
and
\theta(R\setminus B)\geq\varepsilon\theta(R)
, then we conclude that

\frac{\theta\left(\partial_{K}\left(R\setminus B\right)\right)}{\theta(R\setminus B)}\,\leq\,\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\theta(\partial_{K}R)+\theta(\partial_{K}B)}{\theta(R)}\,\leq\,\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\theta(\partial_{K}R)}{\theta(R)}+\frac{\theta(\partial_{K}B)}{\theta(B)}\right).\qed

4.2.
On
(\varepsilon,A)
-fillings

Let
A,R\subseteq G
be measurable subset with positive Haar measure and let
\varepsilon>0
. Assume
A
to be compact.
We call
C\subseteq G
an
(\varepsilon,A)
-filling of
R
if
AC\subseteq R
and the family
(Ag)_{g\in C}
is
\varepsilon
-disjoint.
If
G
is discrete, then every precompact subset
R
of
G
is finite, and the cardinality of every
(\varepsilon,A)
-filling
C
of
R
is bounded by
|R|
.
The next lemma shows that we can bound the cardinality of such
C
even without the discreteness assumption.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
A\subseteq G
be a compact subset of positive Haar measure and
R\subseteq G
be a precompact, measurable subset. Then every
(\varepsilon,A)
-filling
C
of
R
satisfies

|C|\leq\frac{\theta(R)}{(1-\varepsilon)\theta(A)}.

In particular there are
(\varepsilon,A)
-fillings of
R
of finite maximal cardinality.

Proof.

As
(Ag)_{g\in C}
is an
\varepsilon
-disjoint family, we obtain

\theta(R)\,\geq\,\theta(AC)\,=\,\theta\!\left(\bigcup\nolimits_{g\in C}Ag\right)\,\geq\,(1-\varepsilon)\sum\nolimits_{g\in C}\theta(Ag)\,=\,(1-\varepsilon)|C|\theta(A).\qed

The idea of the proof of the next lemma is sketched in
[
Gro99
]
and given in detail for discrete groups in
[
Kri07
,
Kri10
,
CSCK14
]
. We include a full prove for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
A\subseteq G
be a compact subset,
R\subseteq G
be a precompact, measurable subset and assume both sets to have positive Haar measure. Let furthermore
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then, for every
(\varepsilon,A)
-filling
C
of
R
of finite maximal cardinality, we have

\theta(AC)\,\geq\,\varepsilon\left(1-\alpha(R,A^{-1})\right)\theta(R).

Proof.

Since
\theta(A)>0
, there exists
a\in G
such that
Aa
contains the identity
e_{G}
. Then
\theta(\partial_{(Aa)^{-1}}R)=\theta(a^{-1}\partial_{A^{-1}}R)=\theta(\partial_{A^{-1}}R)
, and upon translating
C
we may and will assume without lost of generality that
A
contains
e_{G}
.
For
g\in R\setminus\partial_{A^{-1}}R
, we have
g\in R\subseteq A^{-1}\overline{R}
and thus
g\notin A^{-1}\overline{R^{c}}
.
In particular,
Ag\cap\overline{R^{c}}
is empty, and we deduce that
Ag\subseteq R
.
If
g\notin C
, then necessarily

\theta(Ag\cap AC)\geq\varepsilon\theta(Ag)
, as otherwise, by Lemma
4.1
,
(Ag^{\prime})_{g^{\prime}\in C\cup\{g\}}
would be an
\varepsilon
-disjoint family, a contradiction to the maximal cardinality of
C
.
For
g\in C
we furthermore obtain
Ag\subseteq AC
and thus
\theta(Ag\cap AC)=\theta(Ag)\geq\varepsilon\theta(A)
from
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
This shows that, for every
g\in R\setminus\partial_{A^{-1}}R
,

\theta(Ag\cap AC)\,\geq\,\varepsilon\theta(A).

Let now
\chi_{M}
denote the characteristic function of a subset
M\subseteq G
. For any
g^{\prime}\in G
, we compute that

\theta(A)\,=\,\theta(A^{-1})\,=\,\int_{G}\chi_{A}(g^{-1})d\theta(g)\,=\,\int_{G}\chi_{A}(g^{\prime}g^{-1})d\theta(g).

Thus, Tonelli’s theorem implies

\displaystyle\theta(A)\theta(AC)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\theta(A)\chi_{AC}(g^{\prime})d\theta(g^{\prime})=\int_{G}\int_{G}\chi_{A}(g^{\prime}g^{-1})d\theta(g)\chi_{AC}(g^{\prime})d\theta(g^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{G}\chi_{A}(g^{\prime}g^{-1})\chi_{AC}(g^{\prime})d\theta(g^{\prime})d\theta(g)=\int_{G}\int_{G}\chi_{Ag\cap AC}(g^{\prime})d\theta(g^{\prime})d\theta(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\theta(Ag\cap AC)d\theta(g)\geq\int_{R\setminus\partial_{A^{-1}}R}\varepsilon\theta(A)d\theta(g)=\varepsilon\theta(A)\theta(R\setminus\partial_{A^{-1}}R).

Consequently, we arrive at

\theta(AC)\,\geq\,\varepsilon\theta(R\setminus\partial_{A^{-1}}R)\,\geq\,\varepsilon(\theta(R)-\theta(\partial_{A^{-1}}R))\,=\,\varepsilon\left(1-\frac{\theta(\partial_{A^{-1}}R)}{\theta(R)}\right)\theta(R).\qed

4.3.
On
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling

Let
A\in\mathcal{K}(G)
and let
\varepsilon>0
.
A finite family
(A_{i})_{i\in F}
of compact subsets of
G
is an
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling
of
A
if there exists a family of finite sets
(C_{i})_{i\in F}
such that

(a)

for every
i\in F
, the family
(A_{i}g)_{g\in C_{i}}
is
\varepsilon
-disjoint;

(b)

(A_{i}C_{i})_{i\in F}
is a disjoint family; and

(c)

\theta(A\cap\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i})\geq(1-\varepsilon)\theta(A)
.

A family
(C_{i})_{i\in F}
satisfying these conditions is referred to as a family of
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling centres
of
(A_{i})_{i\in F}
(with respect to
A
). In
[
OW83
,
OW87
,
LW00
]
it is shown that, for any van Hove sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, one can find a finite subset
F\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that, for every sufficiently invariant
A
, the family
(A_{i})_{i\in F}

\varepsilon
-quasi-tiles
A
. We will follow the ideas that lead to this concept and show that, with some modifications of these methods, one can also construct
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling centres
C_{i}
such that
R\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A\setminus\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i}
is ’relatively invariant’, i.e. that allows to control
\theta(\partial_{K}R)/\theta(A)
for some given compact subset
K\subseteq G
.

Proposition 4.6
.

For any van Hove net
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
, any cofinal subset
J\subseteq I
, any
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
and any non-empty and compact subset
K\subseteq G
, there exist a finite subset
F\subseteq J
,
\delta>0
and a compact subset
D\subseteq G
with the following property.
For any
(\delta,D)
-invariant and compact subset
A\subseteq G
the finite family
(A_{i})_{i\in F}
is an
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling of
A
and the
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling centres
(C_{i})_{i\in F}
can be chosen such that
\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i}\subseteq A
and such that
R\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A\setminus\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i}
satisfies
\theta(R)+\theta(\partial_{K}R)\leq\varepsilon\theta(A).

Proof.

As
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\left(1-{\varepsilon}/2\right)^{N}\leq\varepsilon/2
.
As
(A_{i})_{i\in J}
is a van Hove net, we can choose inductively
i_{n}\in J
for
n={{N}},\ldots,1
such that
A_{i_{n}}
has positive Haar measure, such that the
i_{n}
are pairwise distinct and such that

\displaystyle\alpha\left(A_{i_{n}},K\cup\left(\bigcup\nolimits_{m=n+1}^{{{N}}}A_{i_{m}}^{-1}\right)\right)\leq\varepsilon^{2(N-n)+4}.

We abbreviate
K_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=K\cup\left(\bigcup_{m=n+1}^{{{N}}}A_{i_{m}}^{-1}\right)

and observe that
A_{i_{n}}
is
(\varepsilon^{2(N-n)+4},K_{n})
-invariant for
n=0,\ldots,{{N}}
. Furthermore we have
K_{0}\supseteq K_{1}\supseteq\ldots\supseteq K_{N}=K
and
A_{i_{n}}^{-1}\subseteq K_{n-1}
for
n=1,\ldots,N
.

We set
F\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}\}
,
\delta\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\varepsilon^{2{N}+1}
and
D\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=K_{0}
and consider a compact and
(\delta,D)
-invariant subset
A
of
G
.
Set
R_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A
.
Using Lemma
4.4
we now choose inductively for
n=1,\ldots,M
finite
(\varepsilon,A_{i_{n}})
-fillings
C_{n}
of
R_{n-1}
of maximal cardinality, where we abbreviate

R_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=R_{n-1}\setminus A_{i_{n}}C_{n}
and
M\leq N
is the smallest integer where our choices lead to

\theta(R_{M})=\theta(R_{M-1}\setminus A_{i_{M}}C_{M})\leq\varepsilon\theta(R_{M-1})
and
N
if we never encounter this situation.
Thus, in particular, for
n=1,\ldots,M-1
there holds
\theta(R_{n})>\varepsilon\theta(R_{n-1})
. For
n\in\{M+1,\ldots,N\}
we set
C_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\emptyset
.
Note that
R_{n}
is precompact and measurable for
n=0,\ldots,M
and that there holds

R\,=\,R_{M}\,\subseteq\,R_{M-1}\,\subseteq\,R_{0}\,=\,A.

We will now show that defining
C_{i_{n}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=C_{n}
we get that
(C_{i})_{i\in F}=(C_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
is a family of
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling centres that fulfils the required properties.

We first show inductively for
n=0,\ldots,M-1
that
R_{n}
is
(\varepsilon^{2(N-n)+1},K_{n})
-invariant, i.e.

(1)

\displaystyle\alpha(R_{n},K_{n})\,\leq\,\varepsilon^{2(N-n)+1}.

This is clearly satisfied for
n=0
, as
R_{0}=A
,
K_{0}=D
and
\varepsilon^{2(N-0)+1}=\delta
.
To proceed inductively we assume
R_{n}
to be
(\varepsilon^{2(N-n)+1},K_{n})
-invariant for some
n<M-1
and as
K_{n+1}\subseteq K_{n}
we obtain

\alpha(R_{n},K_{n+1})\leq\varepsilon^{2(N-n)+1}.

Now recall that
A_{i_{n+1}}
is
(\varepsilon^{2(N-n)+4},K_{n+1})
-invariant.
As
C_{n+1}
is an
(\varepsilon,A_{i_{n+1}})
-filling we obtain that
(A_{i_{n+1}}g)_{g\in C_{n+1}}
is
\varepsilon
-disjoint and apply Lemma
4.2
to see

\displaystyle\alpha(A_{i_{n+1}}C_{n+1},K_{n+1})\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\max_{g\in C_{n+1}}\alpha(A_{i_{n+1}}g,K_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\alpha(A_{i_{n+1}},K_{n+1})}{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{\varepsilon^{2(N-n)+4}}{1-\varepsilon}\,\leq\,\varepsilon^{2(N-n)+1}.

For this we have used that
\varepsilon<1/2
yields that
\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)<1
.
As we assume
n<M-1
we obtain
\theta(R_{n}\setminus A_{i_{n+1}}C_{n+1})=\theta(R_{n+1})>\varepsilon\theta(R_{n})
. Thus, Lemma
4.3
yields that

\displaystyle\alpha(R_{n+1},K_{n+1})\,

\displaystyle=\,\alpha\left(R_{n}\setminus A_{i_{n+1}}C_{n+1},K_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{\alpha(R_{n},K_{n+1})+\alpha(A_{i_{n+1}}C_{n+1},K_{n+1})}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\,2\varepsilon^{2(N-n)}\,\leq\,\varepsilon^{2(N-n)-1}\,=\,\varepsilon^{2(N-(n+1))+1},

and we have completed the induction to show (
1
).

We next show that

(2)

\displaystyle\theta(R)\,\leq\,\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\theta(A).

This statement is satisfied whenever
M<N
, as then we obtain that

\theta(R)=\theta(R_{M})\leq\varepsilon\theta(R_{M-1})\leq\varepsilon\theta(A).

In order to show (
2
) we thus assume without lost of generality that
M=N
and that
\theta(R)>0
. Then
\theta(R_{N})=\theta(R)>0
and, for
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, we obtain

\theta(R_{n})\,\geq\,\varepsilon\theta(R_{n-1})\,\geq\,\varepsilon^{n}\theta(R_{0})\,=\,\varepsilon^{n}\theta(A)\,>\,0.

For
n\leq M=N
we have chosen
C_{n}
to be an
(\varepsilon,A_{i_{n}})
-filling of
R_{n-1}
of maximal cardinality. Thus we obtain from Lemma
4.5
, (
1
) and
A_{i_{n}}^{-1}\subseteq K_{n-1}
that

\frac{\theta(A_{i_{n}}C_{n})}{\theta(R_{n-1})}\,\geq\,\varepsilon\left(1-\alpha(R_{n-1},A_{i_{n}}^{-1})\right)\,\geq\,\varepsilon\left(1-\alpha(R_{n-1},K_{n-1})\right)\,\geq\,\varepsilon\left(1-\varepsilon^{2(N-(n-1))+1}\right)\,\geq\,\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

Thus,

\theta(R_{n})\,=\,\theta(R_{n-1})-\theta(A_{i_{n}}C_{n})\,<\,\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\theta(R_{n-1})

and we obtain from our choice of
N
that

\theta(R)\,=\,\theta(R_{N})\,<\,\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{N}\theta(R_{0})\,\leq\,\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\theta(A).

This shows the claimed statement (
2
).

As
C_{n}
is an
(\varepsilon,A_{i_{n}})
-filling of
R_{n-1}
we obtain from the construction
R_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=R_{n-1}\setminus A_{i_{n}}C_{n}
that
(A_{i_{n}}g)_{g\in C_{n}}
is
\varepsilon
-disjoint for all
n\leq M
and that
(A_{i}C_{i})_{i\in F}=(A_{i_{n}}C_{n})_{n=1}^{M}\sqcup(\emptyset)_{n=M+1}^{N}
is a disjoint family. In particular, we observe that
(A_{i}g)_{g\in C_{i}}
is
\varepsilon
-disjoint for all
i\in F
. Furthermore, one obtains

\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{M}A_{i_{n}}C_{n}\subseteq\bigcup_{n=0}^{M}R_{n}=R_{0}=A.

Thus (
2
) allows to compute

\theta\left(A\cap\bigcup\nolimits_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i}\right)=\theta(A)-\theta(R)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\theta(A).

This shows that
(A_{i})_{i\in F}
is an
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling of
A
and that
\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i}\subseteq A
.

By (
2
) it remains to show that
\theta(\partial_{K}R)\leq(\varepsilon/2)\theta(A).

Recall that
A_{i_{M}}
is
(\varepsilon^{2(N-M)+4},K_{M})
-invariant. As
K\subseteq K_{M}
we obtain

\alpha(A_{i_{M}},K)\leq\varepsilon^{4}.

Since
C_{M}
is an
(\varepsilon,A_{i_{M}})
-filling we observe that
(A_{i_{M}}g)_{g\in C_{M}}
is
\varepsilon
-disjoint and apply Lemma
4.2
to see

\displaystyle\alpha(A_{i_{M}}C_{M},K)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\max_{g\in C_{M}}\alpha(A_{i_{M}}g,K)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha(A_{i_{M}},K)}{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{3}.

Furthermore a straightforward argument shows that
\partial_{K}R_{M}=\partial_{K}(R_{M-1}\setminus A_{i_{M}}C_{M})\subseteq\partial_{K}R_{M-1}\cup\partial_{K}(A_{i_{M}}C_{M})
. As
K\subseteq D
we obtain that
A
is
(K,\delta)
-invariant and as
\delta\in(0,1)
we have
\theta(K)\leq\theta(A)
.
Thus (
1
) and
K\subseteq K_{M}\subseteq K_{M-1}
allow to compute

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(\partial_{K}R)}{\theta(A)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\theta(\partial_{K}R_{M-1})}{\theta(A)}+\frac{\theta(\partial_{K}A_{i_{M}}C_{M})}{\theta(A)}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(R_{M-1},K)+\alpha(A_{i_{M}}C_{M},K)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(R_{M-1},K_{M-1})+\varepsilon^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{3}+\varepsilon^{3}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.\qed

4.4.
A proof of the Ornstein-Weiss lemma for non-discrete groups

If
G
is a discrete group, then subadditivity and right invariance can be used to show that
f(F)/|F|\leq f(\{e_{G}\})
for every finite, non-empty subset
F\subseteq G
. This is not any longer the case in the non-discrete setting as presented in
[
Hau20a
, Remark 3.1 and Remark 4.6]
. We next present a result that can serve as a replacement of the mentioned boundedness.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{K}(G)\to[0,\infty)
be a monotone, right invariant and subadditive mapping, let
K
be a compact neighbourhood of
e_{G}
. Then there exists a constant
c_{K}>0
such that, for every non-empty, precompact, measurable subsets
R\subseteq G
,

f\left(\overline{R}\right)\,\leq\,c_{K}\theta\left(K\overline{R}\right).

Proof.

Note first that the subadditivity and the monotonicity of
f
imply that
f
only takes values in
[0,\infty)
.
Let
V
be a compact and symmetric neighbourhood of
e_{G}
that satisfies
V\subseteq K
and set
c_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=f(VV)/\theta(V)
.
For a non-empty and precompact subset
R\subseteq G
we let
F\subseteq\overline{R}
be a
V
-separated
subset, i.e. such that
(Vg)_{g\in F}
is a disjoint family. Then
VF=\bigcup_{g\in F}Vg
is a disjoint union and thus

\theta\left(V\overline{R}\right)\,\geq\,\theta(VF)\,=\,\sum_{g\in F}\theta(Vg)\,=\,|F|\theta(V).

In particular we obtain the cardinality of
F
to be bounded by
\theta(V\overline{R})/\theta(V)<\infty
. We thus assume without lost of generality that
F
is a
V
-separated subset of
\overline{R}
of maximal cardinality. Then for any
g\in\overline{R}
there is
g^{\prime}\in F
such that
Vg
and
Vg^{\prime}
intersect. Thus,
g\in VVg^{\prime}\subseteq VVF
and we observe that
\overline{R}\subseteq VVF
.
We compute that

\displaystyle f\left(\overline{R}\right)\,\leq\,f(VVF)\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\sum\nolimits_{g\in F}f(VVg)\,=\,|F|f(VV)

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{\theta(V\overline{R})}{\theta(V)}f(VV)\,=\,c_{K}\theta(V\overline{R})\,\leq\,c_{K}\theta(K\overline{R}).\qed

With the slight improvement of the Ornstein-Weiss quasi-tiling machinery at hand, we are now ready to prove the Ornstein-Weiss lemma for arbitrary amenable unimodular locally compact groups.

Theorem 4.8

(Ornstein-Weiss lemma)
.

Let
G
be an amenable unimodular locally compact group and let

f\colon\mathcal{K}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
be a monotone, subadditive and right-invariant function. Then for any van Hove net
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
in
G
the limit

\lim_{i\in I}\frac{f(A_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}

exists, is finite and independent of the choice of a van Hove net.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
and choose an arbitrary compact neighbourhood
K
of
e_{G}
. Then, by Lemma
4.7
, there exists a constant
c
such that
f(\overline{A})\leq c\theta(K\overline{A})
for every non-empty, precompact, measurable subset
A\subseteq G
. Consider
\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\liminf_{i\in I}{f(A_{i})}/{\theta(A_{i})}
. As
(KA_{i})_{i\in I}
is a van Hove net in
G
with
\lim_{i\in I}\theta(KA_{i})/\theta(A_{i})=1
, it follows that

\lambda\,=\,\liminf_{i\in I}{f(A_{i})}/{\theta(KA_{i})}\,\leq\,c\,<\,\infty.

In particular, there is a cofinal subset
J\subseteq I
such that
(f(A_{i}))/\theta(A_{i}))_{i\in J}
converges to
\lambda
. Thus, there exists
j\in J
such that for all
i\in J
with
i\geq j
we have

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{f(A_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\,\leq\,\lambda+\varepsilon.

Since also
\{i\in J\mid i\geq j\}
is cofinal in
I
we assume without lost of generality that (
3
) holds for all
i\in J
.
From Proposition
4.6
we obtain the existence of a
finite subset
F\subseteq J
,
\delta>0
as well as a compact, non-empty subset
D\subseteq G
such that any
(\delta,D)
-invariant, compact subset
A\subseteq G
can be
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiled and the
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling centres can be chosen with the additional properties as in Proposition
4.6
.

We now consider a compact and
(\delta,D)
-invariant subset
A\subseteq G
and choose
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling centres
C_{i}
such that these additional properties are satisfied, i.e. such that
\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i}\subseteq A
and such that
R\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A\setminus\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i}
satisfies
\theta(R)+\theta(\partial_{K}R)\leq\varepsilon\theta(A)
.
From
F\subseteq J
and (
3
) we obtain
f(A_{i})/\theta(A_{i})\leq\lambda+\varepsilon
for every
i\in F
, and we compute

\displaystyle\frac{f\left(\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i}\right)}{\theta(A)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in F}\sum_{g\in C_{i}}\frac{f(A_{i}g)}{\theta(A)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in F}\sum_{g\in C_{i}}\frac{f(A_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\frac{\theta(A_{i})}{\theta(A)}

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda+\varepsilon)\sum_{i\in F}\sum_{g\in C_{i}}\frac{\theta(A_{i})}{\theta(A)}.

By the properties of
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling centres,
((A_{i}g)_{g\in C_{i}})_{i\in F}
is an
\varepsilon
-disjoint family. Therefore,
\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i}\subseteq A
implies that

\sum_{i\in F}\sum_{g\in C_{i}}\frac{\theta(A_{i})}{\theta(A)}\,=\,\sum_{i\in F}\sum_{g\in C_{i}}\frac{\theta(A_{i}g)}{\theta(A)}\,\leq\,\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\frac{\theta(\bigcup_{i\in J}A_{i}C_{i})}{\theta(A)}\,\leq\,\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}.

We have shown that

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{f\left(\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i}\right)}{\theta(A)}\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{\lambda+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}.

Note that
e_{G}\in K
implies

K\overline{R}\subseteq R\cup\partial_{K}R.

As we require the
\varepsilon
-quasi-tiling centres to satisfy
\theta(R)+\theta(\partial_{K}R)\leq\varepsilon\theta(A)
, we obtain from the choice of the constant
c
at the beginning of the proof that

f\left(\overline{R}\right)\,\leq\,c\theta(K\overline{R})\,\leq\,c(\theta(R)+\theta(\partial_{K}R))\,\leq\,\varepsilon c\theta(A).

Thus (
4
) yields that

\frac{f(A)}{\theta(A)}\,\leq\,\frac{f(\bigcup_{i\in F}A_{i}C_{i})}{\theta(A)}+\frac{f\left(\overline{R}\right)}{\theta(A)}\,\leq\,\frac{\lambda+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}+\varepsilon c

for every
(\delta,K)
-invariant, compact subset
A\subseteq G
. Hence, considering another van Hove net
\left(B_{\iota}\right)_{\iota\in\tilde{I}}
, we see that

\limsup_{\iota\in\tilde{I}}\frac{f(B_{\iota})}{\theta(B_{\iota})}\,\leq\,\frac{\lambda+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}+\varepsilon c.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this shows that, for any two van Hove nets
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
and
(B_{\iota})_{\iota\in\tilde{I}}
in
G
,

\limsup_{\iota\in\tilde{I}}\frac{f(B_{\iota})}{\theta(B_{\iota})}\,\leq\,\lambda=\liminf_{i\in I}\frac{f(A_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\,<\,c,

which clearly implies the statement of the theorem.
∎

5.
Algebra isomorphism and topological models

As explained in the introduction we will need to find appropriate topological models for our measure preserving actions.

5.1.
Algebra isomorphism between measure preserving actions

Let
(X,\mu)
be a probability space and note that the relation given by
\mu(A\triangle B)=0
is an equivalence relation on all subsets
A,B\subseteq X
. Let
[A]
denote the equivalence class of
A\subseteq G
. The set
\Sigma(X)
of all equivalence classes is called the
measure algebra
of
X
. Note that
[A\cap B]
and
[A\cup B]
are independent of the choice of representatives from
[A]
and
[B]
respectively. This allows to write
[A]\cap[B]
and
[A]\cup[B]
respectively for these sets.
Furthermore, note that
\mu
is constant on each equivalence class
[A]\in\Sigma(X)
, which allows to write
\mu[A]
.
Consider now probability spaces
(X,\mu)
and
(Y,\nu)
.
We call a bijective mapping
\iota\colon\Sigma(X)\to\Sigma(Y)
, that satisfies
\iota([A]\cap[B])=\iota([A])\cap\iota([B])
,
\iota([A]\cup[B])=\iota([A])\cup\iota([B])

and
\mu[A]=\nu(\iota[A])
for all
[A],[B]\in\Sigma(X)
an
algebra isomorphism
between
(X,\mu)
and
(Y,\nu)
.

If
\pi
is a measure preserving action on
(X,\mu)
, then we may define
g.[A]\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=[g.A]
for all
[A]\in\Sigma(X)
and
g\in G
.
Given another measure preserving action
\varphi
of
G
on a probability space
(Y,\nu)
, by an
algebra isomorphism
between
\pi
and
\varphi
we will mean an algebra isomorphism
\iota
between
(X,\mu)
and
(Y,\nu)
such that
g.(\iota[A])=\iota(g.[A])
for all
g\in G
and
[A]\in\Sigma(A)
. Measure preserving actions
\pi
and
\varphi
are called
algebra isomorphic
, if there exists an algebra isomorphism between
\pi
and
\varphi
.

5.2.
About topological models

For regular Borel probability measure
\nu
on a compact Hausdorff space
K
we define the
support

\operatorname{supp}(\nu)
as the set of all
x\in K
such that for any an open neighbourhood
U
of
x
we have
\nu(U)>0
.

\nu
is said to have
full support
if
K=\operatorname{supp}(\nu)
.
Let
\pi
be an measure preserving action of a topological group
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
. A
topological model
of
\pi
is a continuous action
\varphi
of
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
K
together with an invariant regular Borel probability measure
\nu
that has full support and such that
\varphi
considered as a measure preserving action on
(K,\nu)
is algebra isomorphic to
\pi
.

Remark 5.1
.

Note that we do not assume
(X,\mu)
to be a Lebesgue space and that
K
is not assumed to be separable or to be metrizable. Note furthermore that in contrast to the usual Jewett-Krieger theorem we do not require a topological model to be uniquely ergodic (see
[
Jew70
,
Kri72
]
for the original work of R. Jewett and W. Krieger,
and
[
Wei85
,
Ros88
,
EFHN15
]
for generalizations to actions of amenable discrete groups).
In the usual Jewett-Krieger theorem one also uses a stronger notion of isomorphism than algebra isomorphism. Nevertheless, for our purposes (entropy theory) algebra isomorphisms are sufficient and allow us to state our results beyond Lebesgue spaces.

With similar arguments presented in
[
EFHN15
, Section 12.3]
one can show that any measure preserving action of a discrete group has a topological model. Since we are not aware of a reference beyond the context of discrete groups we include a full proof of the following.

Proposition 5.2
.

Every measure preserving action
\pi
of a topological group
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
admits a topological model.

For the proof of Proposition
5.2
we introduce the following notation from
[
EFHN15
]
.
Let
(X,\mu)
and
(Y,\nu)
be probability spaces and
p\in\{1,\infty\}
.
A bijective positive operator
T\colon L^{p}(X,\mu)\to L^{p}(Y,\nu)
is called a
Markov isomorphism
if
T
has a positive inverse and satisfies
T\chi_{X}=\chi_{Y}
and
\int_{Y}Tfd\nu=\int_{X}fd\mu
for all
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
.
A Markov isomorphism
T\colon L^{p}(X,\mu)\to L^{p}(X,\mu)
is called a
Markov automorphism
. Let
\operatorname{MAut}^{p}(X,\mu)
denote the set of all Markov automorphisms on
L^{p}(X,\mu)
and equip
\operatorname{MAut}^{p}(X,\mu)
with the strong operator topology.
From
[
EFHN15
, Theorem 13.15]
we quote that
\operatorname{MAut}^{p}(X,\mu)
is a topological group under composition.
From
[
EFHN15
, Proposition 13.6]
we furthermore know that the restriction mapping induces an isomorphism of topological groups (a homeomorphism that is also a group homomorphism) between
\operatorname{MAut}^{\infty}(X,\mu)
and
\operatorname{MAut}^{1}(X,\mu)
.

For a measure preserving action
\pi
we denote
\pi_{*}\colon G\to\operatorname{MAut}^{\infty}(X,\mu)
for the
Koopman representation
defined by
\pi_{*}(g)(f)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=f\circ\pi^{g}
for all
f\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
. Similarly we define the Koopman representation

\pi_{*}\colon G\to\operatorname{MAut}^{1}(X,\mu)
.
For an action
\varphi
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
for which each
\varphi^{g}
is continuous we furthermore define analogously the Koopman representation
\varphi_{*}\colon G\to\mathcal{L}(C(X))
, where
\mathcal{L}(C(K))
denotes the set of all bounded linear operators
C(K)\to C(K)
equipped with the strong operator topology. From
[
EFHN15
, Theorem 4.17]
we quote that
\varphi
is a continuous action, if and only if the Koopman representation
\varphi_{*}\colon G\to\mathcal{L}(C(X))
is continuous.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

To construct the topological model
\varphi
and the algebra isomorphism
\iota
for
\pi
we essentially follow the arguments from
[
EFHN15
, Section 12.3]
. We will then add to these arguments and argue that
\varphi
is indeed a continuous action. (This is trivially satisfied if
G
is discrete.) For the convenience of the reader we present the full construction.

Note that
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
is a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra with unit
\chi_{X}
. Thus, by the Gelfand-Naimark theorem, there exists a compact Hausdorff space
K
and a unital
C^{*}
-algebra isomorphism
\Phi\colon C(K)\to L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
.
By the Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem there exists a unique regular Borel probability measure
\nu
on
K
such that

\int_{K}fd\nu=\int_{X}\Phi(f)d\mu
for all
f\in C(K)
.
It is straightforward to observe that the canonical operator
C(K)\to L^{\infty}(K,\nu)
is a homeomorphism and that
\Phi
preserves the respective
L^{1}
-norms. In particular,
\nu
has full support.
With standard arguments one then obtains that
\Phi
extends uniquely to a Markov isomorphism
L^{1}(K,\nu)\to L^{1}(X,\mu)
, which we also denote by
\Phi
. The restriction of
\Phi
to the set of all characteristic functions in the corresponding
L^{1}
-spaces then induces an algebra isomorphism
\iota\colon\Sigma(K)\to\Sigma(X)
.

For
g\in G
we consider the Markov automorphism
\Phi^{-1}\circ\pi^{g}_{*}\circ\Phi\colon C(K)\to C(K)
. From
[
EFHN15
, Theorem 7.23]
we know that there exists a unique homeomorphism
\varphi^{g}
such that

\Phi^{-1}\circ\pi^{g}_{*}\circ\Phi=\varphi^{g}_{*}
. It is straightforward to check that this defines an action
\varphi\colon G\times K\to K
, that
\nu
is invariant and that
\iota
establishes an algebra isomorphism between the measure preserving action
\varphi
of
G
(equipped with the discrete topology) on
(K,\nu)
and
\pi
.

It remains to show that
\varphi
is continuous with respect to the original topology of
G
.
Since
\pi
is measure preserving, any characteristic function
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
satisfies

\|\pi^{g}_{*}f-f\|_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,0\,\text{ as }\,g\to e_{G}.

By a straightforward density argument, this readily entails the continuity of the Koopman representation
\pi_{*}\colon G\to\operatorname{MAut}^{1}(X,\mu)
.
Since restriction induces a homeomorphism between

\operatorname{MAut}^{1}(X,\mu)
and
\operatorname{MAut}^{\infty}(X,\mu)
, the Koopman representation
\pi_{*}\colon G\to\operatorname{MAut}^{\infty}(X,\mu)
is continuous, too.
For every
f\in C(K)
, we know that
\Phi(f)\in L^{\infty}(X,\nu)
and hence

(\pi^{g}_{*}\circ\Phi)(f)\,=\,(\pi_{*}^{g})(\Phi(f))\,\longrightarrow\,\Phi(f)\,\text{ as }\,g\to e_{G}.

Thus,
\varphi_{*}^{g}(f)=(\Phi^{-1}\circ\pi^{g}_{*}\circ\Phi)(f)\rightarrow f
as
g\to e_{G}
. This proves the continuity of the Koopman representation
\varphi_{*}\colon G\to\mathcal{L}(C(K))
, which implies the continuity of
\varphi
.
∎

6.
Entropy and topological pressure

Let
\pi
be an action of a group
G
on a set
X
, and let
\mathcal{U}
be any finite set of subsets of
X
. For
g\in G
, we define
\mathcal{U}_{g}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{g^{-1}.U\mid U\in\mathcal{U}\}
. For a finite set
F\subseteq G
, we furthermore define

\left.\mathcal{U}_{F}\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\left\{\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in F}g^{-1}.U(g)\,\right|U\in\mathcal{U}^{F}\right\}.

6.1.
Naive entropy and topological pressure

We next study naive entropy and naive topological pressure as a tool in order to gain insights on the Ollagnier entropy.
Let
G
be a topological group.
For a continously measure preserving action
\pi
of
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
we use the notation defined in the introduction to define the
naive entropy

\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(nv)}(\pi)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\sup_{\alpha}\inf_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F})}{|F|},

where the supremum is taken over all finite measurable partitions
\alpha

A naive version of Goodwyn’s theorem

The following version of Goodwyn’s theorem generalizes

Theorem 6.1

(Goodwyn’s theorem for naive entropy and topological pressure)

Let

We will need the following notions for the proof of Theorem

For finite measurable partitions
\alpha
and

we define the

\alpha

H_{\mu}(\alpha|\beta):=-\sum_{A\in\alpha,B\in\beta}\mu(A\cap B)\log\left(\frac{\mu(A\cap B)}{\mu(B)}\right).

For further properties of this concept the reader is referred to

[

Now let

of

. For a regular Borel probability measure

on

\mathcal{U}
on

\operatorname{R}_{\mu}(\mathcal{U})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup_{\alpha,\beta}H_{\mu}(\alpha|\beta),

where the supremum is taken over all finite measurable partitions
\alpha
and
\beta
of

, which are adapted to
\mathcal{U}
. The overlap ratio satisfies the following simple invariance property.
If

For a more detailed account on these notions see
[

]
.
From the arguments presented in
[

we obtain furthermore the following.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mu

of

, there exists an open cover
\mathcal{U}
of

We will furthermore need the following combinatorial lemma from
[

Lemma 6.3
.

Let

and

be given real numbers such that

and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}(a_{i}-\log(p_{i}))\leq\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}e^{a_{i}}\right)
.

The following lemma will be useful for the proofs of the Theorems
6.1
and
6.31

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\pi
be a continuous action of a topological group
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
. Let
\mu
be a
\pi
-invariant, regular Borel probability measure on
X
.
For every finite measurable partition
\alpha
of
X
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a finite open cover
\mathcal{U}
such that, for any

and any finite set

H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F})+\mu(f)\,\leq\,\operatorname{P}_{f}(\mathcal{U}_{F})+|F|\varepsilon.

Proof.

From Lemma
6.2
we obtain the existence of an open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
such that
\alpha
is adapted to
\mathcal{U}
and such that
\operatorname{R}_{\mu}(\mathcal{U})\leq\varepsilon
. Consider now
f\in C(X)
and
F\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)
. Consider furthermore a finite measurable partition
\beta
of

that is adapted to
\mathcal{U}_{F}
. Using Lemma

we compute

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(\beta)+\mu(f)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{B\in\beta}\mu(B)\left(\sup_{x\in B}f(x)-\log(\mu(B))\right)\leq\log\left(\sum_{B\in\beta}e^{\sup_{x\in B}f(x)}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\sum_{B\in\beta}\sup_{x\in B}e^{f(x)}\leq\log\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{F}}\sup_{x\in U}e^{f(x)}=\operatorname{P}_{f}(\mathcal{U}_{F}).

Now consider

and recall that
\mathcal{U}_{g}\preceq\mathcal{U}_{F}\preceq\beta

\left.\gamma^{(g)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{\bigcup\{B\in\beta\mid U_{B}=U\}\,\right|U\in\mathcal{U}_{g}\right\}\setminus\{\emptyset\}

we obtain a finite measurable partition of

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F}|\beta)\leq\sum_{g\in F}H_{\mu}(\alpha_{g}|\beta)\leq\sum_{g\in F}H_{\mu}(\alpha_{g}|\gamma^{(g)})\leq\sum_{g\in F}\operatorname{R}_{\mu}(\mathcal{U}_{g})\leq|F|\operatorname{R}_{\mu}(\mathcal{U})\leq|F|\varepsilon.

Combining our observations we thus obtain

H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F})+\mu(f)\leq H_{\mu}(\beta)+\mu(f)+H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F}|\beta)\leq\operatorname{P}_{f}(\mathcal{U}_{F})+|F|\varepsilon.\qed

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Of course, it suffices to verify that
\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(nv)}(\pi)+\mu(f)\leq\operatorname{p}_{f}^{(nv)}(\pi)+\varepsilon

In particular,

\displaystyle\inf_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F})}{|F|}+\mu(f)\,

\displaystyle=\,\inf_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F})+\mu(\sum_{F}{f})}{|F|}

\displaystyle\leq\,\inf_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)}\frac{\operatorname{P}_{\sum_{F}{f}}(\mathcal{U}_{F})}{|F|}+\varepsilon

Taking the supremum over all finite measurable partitions of

6.1.2.
Naive topological entropy for actions of non-discrete groups

It is well known that naive (measure-theoretic) and topological entropy can only take the values

]

Theorem 6.7
.

For any continuous action
\pi
of a topological group that contains an infinite precompact subset we have

.

Since any measure preserving action has a topological model by Proposition

Corollary 6.8

For any measure preserving action

.

.

Let

be a non-discrete topological group. If

Let

We conclude this subsection with an example of a non-precompact topological group that does not contain an infinite precompact subset, which has been communicated to the authors by Maxime Gheysens.

Example 6.12
.

Let
H
by any non-trivial discrete group (such as, for example, the additive group of integers). Furthermore, consider any
uncountable set
I
. We turn the direct power
G\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=H^{I}
into a topological group by endowing it with the
countable-box
topology
, i.e. the one generated by the basic open subsets of the form

\{g\in G\mid\forall i\in C\colon\,g_{i}\in U_{i}\},

where
C
is any countable subset of
I
and
(U_{i})_{i\in C}
is any family of subsets of
H
. It is easy to verify that this topology indeed constitutes a group topology on
G
. Moreover, this topology is non-discrete (for cardinality reasons) and Hausdorff (as it contains the usual product topology). Now, the topological group
G
has the additional property that each of its countable subsets is uniformly discrete. Indeed,
if
A
is a countable subset of
G
, then for any pair
(g,h)\in A\times A
with
g\neq h
, we can find an index
i(g,h)\in I
such that
g_{i(g,h)}\neq h_{i(g,h)}
, so that
A
will be
U
-discrete for the open identity neighborhood

U\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\{g\in G\mid\forall i\in C\colon\,g_{i}\in\{e_{H}\}\}\,\in\,\mathcal{U}(G),

where
C\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{i(g,h)\mid g,h\in A,\,g\neq h\}
.
Consequently,
G
cannot posses any countably infinite precompact subset, which – by the axiom of choice – entails that
G
does not contain any infinite precompact subset at all. Choosing
H
to be abelian, the group
G
will be abelian, too, thus amenable even as a discrete group.

6.2.
Ollagnier entropy

Let
\pi
be a measure preserving action of an amenable topological group
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
.
Recall from the introduction that we define
Ollagnier entropy
as

\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(Oll)}(\pi)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\sup_{\alpha}\lim_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}})}{|F_{i}|},

where the supremum is taken over all finite measurable partitions of
X
and where
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
is a thin Følner net.
Here the limit exists and is independent from the choice of a thin Følner net by Ollagnier’s Lemma (Theorem
3.4
), which can be applied by the following lemma.

Lemma 6.13
.

Let
\pi
be a measure preserving action of a topological group
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
.
For any finite measurable partition
\alpha
of
X
, the map

\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\hskip 10.00002ptF\,\longmapsto\,H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F})

is right-invariant, continuous and satisfies Shearer’s inequality.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be a finite measurable partition of
X
. It is standard to show that the considered map is right-invariant and satisfies Shearer’s inequality (see, e.g.,
[
DFR16
]
).
To show the continuity, consider any
F\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)
.
Since
\pi
is measure preserving, there exists
an open neighbourhood
V
of
e_{G}
such that

\mu(A\triangle g.A)<\varepsilon/(|F||\alpha^{F}|)
for each
A\in\alpha
.
Now, let
E\in V[F]
and choose a bijection
b\colon F\to E
such that
b(g)\in Vg
for each
g\in F
.
For any map
A^{(\cdot)}\colon F\to\alpha,\,g\mapsto A^{(g)}
, we observe that

\displaystyle\mu\!\left(\!\left(\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in F}g^{-1}.A^{(g)}\right)\triangle\left(\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in F}(b(g))^{-1}.A^{(g)}\right)\!\right)\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\mu\!\left(\bigcup\nolimits_{g\in F}g^{-1}.A^{(g)}\triangle(b(g))^{-1}.A^{(g)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\sum\nolimits_{g\in F}\mu\!\left(g^{-1}.A^{(g)}\triangle(b(g))^{-1}.A^{(g)}\right)

\displaystyle=\,\sum\nolimits_{g\in F}\mu\!\left((b(g)g^{-1}).A^{(g)}\triangle A^{(g)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{|F|\varepsilon}{|F||\alpha^{F}|}\,=\,\frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha^{F}|}.

This shows that

\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\hskip 10.00002ptF\,\longmapsto\,\sum\nolimits_{A^{(\cdot)}\in\alpha^{F}}\mu\!\left(\!\left(\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in F}g^{-1}.A^{(g)}\right)\triangle\left(\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in F}(b(g))^{-1}.A^{(g)}\right)\!\right)

is continuous.
By a standard argument (as for example contained in
[
Wal82
, Lemma 4.15]
), we thus obtain the continuity of
\mathcal{F}_{+}(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\,F\mapsto H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F})
.
∎

Theorem 6.14
.

Any measure preserving action
\pi
of an amenable topological group on a probability space
(X,\mu)
satisfies

\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(Oll)}(\pi)=\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(nv)}(\pi).

From Corollary
6.8
we conclude the following.

Corollary 6.15
.

Let
\pi
be a measure preserving action of an amenable topological group
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
. If
G
contains an infinite precompact subset, then

\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(Oll)}(\pi)=0.

6.3.
Ornstein-Weiss entropy

Let
G
be an amenable unimodular locally compact group and consider a van Hove net
\mathcal{A}=(A_{i})_{i\in I}
and a Delone set
\omega
in
G
.
For a measure preserving action
\pi
of
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
we define the
Ornstein-Weiss entropy
as

\operatorname{E}^{(OW)}_{\mu,\mathcal{A},\omega}(\pi)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup_{\alpha}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{A_{i}\cap\omega})}{\theta(A_{i})},

where the supremum is taken over all finite measurable partitions of
X
.
For a continuous action
\pi
of
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
and
f\in C(X)
we define the
Ornstein-Weiss topological pressure
as

\operatorname{p}_{f,\mathcal{A},\omega}^{(OW)}(\pi)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup_{\mathcal{U}}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{P_{f}(\mathcal{U}_{A_{i}\cap\omega})}{\theta(A_{i})},

where the supremum is taken over all finite open covers
\mathcal{U}
of
X
.
We will next show that these notions are independent of the choice of a van Hove net
\mathcal{A}
and a Delone set
\omega
.

6.3.1.
Ornstein-Weiss entropy

Consider a measure preserving action
\pi
of an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
on a probability space
(X,\mu)
.
Note that Ornstein-Weiss entropy is an invariant under algebra isomorphism of measure preserving actions (for a fixed van Hove net and a fixed Delone set). Thus by considering a topological model of
\pi
(see Proposition
5.2
) we thus assume without lost of generality that
X
is a compact Hausdorff space, that
\mu
is a regular Borel probability measure on
X
and that
\pi
is a continuous action on
X
.
This topological setup will allow us to present an alternative approach to Ornstein-Weiss entropy that facilitates an application of the Ornstein-Weiss lemma.

For
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
we define
H_{\mu}[\eta]\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\inf_{\alpha}H_{\mu}(\alpha)
, where the infimum is taken over all finite measurable partitions
\alpha
of
X
at scale
\eta
.
Now recall from Subsection
6.1.2
that we define
\eta_{A}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\cap_{g\in A}\{(x,y)\in X^{2}\mid(g,x,g.y)\in\eta\}

and that
\eta_{A}\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
for any compact subset
A\subseteq G
. It is straightforward to show that
\mathcal{K}(G)\ni A\mapsto H_{\mu}[\eta_{A}]
is monotone, right-invariant and subadditive. The Ornstein-Weiss lemma thus allows to define

\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}[\pi,\eta]\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\lim_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}[\eta_{A_{i}}]}{\theta(A_{i})}

independently from the choice of the van Hove net.
We will next show the following.

Theorem 6.16
.

Let
\pi
be a continuous action of an amenable unimodular locally compact group on a compact Hausdorff space
X
and
\mu
be an invariant regular Borel probability measure on
X
.
For any van Hove net
\mathcal{A}
and any Delone set
\omega
in
G
, we have

\operatorname{E}_{\mu,\mathcal{A},\omega}^{(OW)}(\pi)\,=\,\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}[\pi,\eta].

Corollary 6.17
.

For any measure preserving action
\pi
of an amenable unimodular locally compact group on a probability space
(X,\mu)
, the Ornstein-Weiss entropy

\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}(\pi)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\operatorname{E}_{\mu,\mathcal{A},\omega}^{(OW)}(\pi)

is independent of both the choice of a van Hove net and the choice of a Delone set.

Remark 6.18
.

We begin the proof of Theorem
6.16
with the following lemma.

Lemma 6.19
.

Let
\mu
be a regular Borel probability measure on a compact Hausdorff space
X
. For
\varepsilon>0
and a finite measurable partition
\alpha
of
X
there exists
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
such that
\alpha
is at scale
\eta
and such that
H_{\mu}(\alpha|\beta)<\varepsilon
for every finite measurable partition
\beta
of
X
at scale
\eta
.

Proof.

We represent
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{r}\}
.
It is well known that there exists
\delta>0
such that whenever

\gamma=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{r}\}

is a partition that satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu(A_{i}\triangle C_{i})<\delta
, then
H_{\mu}(\alpha|\gamma)<\varepsilon

[
Wal82
, Lemma 4.15]
).
As
\mu
is regular there exist compact subsets
D_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
with

\mu(A_{i}\setminus D_{i})\leq\delta/(2r^{2})
for
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
. Let

D_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}D_{i}
,
U_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=D_{0}\cup D_{i}
,
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}(U_{i}\times U_{i})
and
\mathcal{U}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{U_{1},\dots,U_{r}\}
.
Clearly
\alpha
is at scale
\eta
.

Consider a finite measurable partition
\beta
at scale
\eta
.
Let
B\in\beta
. Whenever
B\subseteq D_{0}
, then
B\subseteq U_{i}
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
. Otherwise, there exists
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
and
d\in B
with
d\in D_{i}
.
In particular, we observe
d\notin U_{j}
for
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
with
j\neq i
.
For
b\in B
we have

(b,d)\in B^{2}\subseteq\eta=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}U_{i}
and hence
b\in U_{i}
. This shows

B\subseteq U_{i}
and we have shown that
\beta
is finer than
\mathcal{U}
. In particular, there exists a finite measurable partition
\gamma=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{r}\}
of
X
with
C_{i}\subseteq U_{i}
and
\mathcal{U}\preceq\gamma\preceq\beta
.
Since
\gamma
is a partition we obtain
D_{i}\subseteq C_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
and compute
C_{i}\triangle A_{i}\subseteq(U_{i}\setminus D_{i})\cup(A_{i}\setminus D_{i})=D_{0}\cup(A_{i}\setminus D_{i})
and hence

\mu(C_{i}\triangle A_{i})\,\leq\,\mu(D_{0})+\mu(A_{i}\setminus D_{i})\,\leq\,\delta/(2r)+\delta/(2r^{2})\,\leq\,\delta/r.

Our choice of
\delta
thus implies
H_{\mu}(\alpha|\beta)\leq H_{\mu}(\alpha|\gamma)\leq\varepsilon
. ∎

Remark 6.20
.

Consider
\mathcal{U}
as constructed in the previous proof.
It is standard to show that any finite measurable partition
\beta
of
X
that is finer than

\mathcal{U}
satisfies
H_{\mu}(\alpha|\beta)\leq\varepsilon
. For example, this argument is carried out in the proof of
[
HYZ11
, Theorem 3.5]
. Nevertheless, we decided to include the short proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 6.21
.

Let
G
be an amenable unimodular locally compact group.
Let
\omega\subseteq G
be closed and let
K\subseteq G
be compact such that
K\omega=G
.
Let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a van Hove net in
G
and define

F_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\omega\cap A_{i}
for each
i\in I
.
Then, the net
(KF_{i})_{i\in I}
is van Hove and satisfies
\lim_{i\in I}\theta(KF_{i})/\theta(A_{i})=1
and

\lim_{i\in I}{\theta(KF_{i}\triangle A_{i})}/{\theta(A_{i})}\,=\,\lim_{i\in I}{\theta(KF_{i}\triangle A_{i})}/{\theta(KF_{i})}\,=\,0.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.5
we obtain the existence of a van Hove net
(B_{i})_{i\in I}
that satisfies
K^{-1}B_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
and
B_{i}^{c}\subseteq KA_{i}^{c}
for all
i\in I
and furthermore
\lim_{i\in I}\theta(B_{i})/\theta(A_{i})=1
. It is straightforward to show that
B_{i}\subseteq KF_{i}
. We compute

1\leftarrow\frac{\theta(B_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\leq\frac{\theta(KF_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\leq\frac{\theta(KA_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\rightarrow 1

and observe
\lim_{i\in I}\theta(KF_{i})/\theta(A_{i})=1
.
We furthermore obtain
(KF_{i})^{c}\subseteq B_{i}^{c}\subseteq KA_{i}^{c}
. For a compact subset
C\subseteq G
this allows to compute

\partial_{C}(KF_{i})=CKF_{i}\cap C\overline{(KF_{i})^{c}}\subseteq CKA_{i}\cap CK\overline{A_{i}^{c}}=\partial_{CK}A_{i}.

We deduce that
(KF_{i})_{i\in I}
is a van Hove net. To show
\lim_{i\in I}\theta(KF_{i}\triangle A_{i})/\theta(A_{i})=0
let
k\in K
. As
\mathcal{P}(X)
is an abelian group under
\triangle
, with the identity as inverse map and neutral element
\emptyset
, we compute

\displaystyle KF_{i}\triangle A_{i}

\displaystyle=KF_{i}\triangle\emptyset\triangle A_{i}=(KF_{i}\triangle kA_{i})\triangle(kA_{i}\triangle A_{i})\subseteq(KF_{i}\triangle kA_{i})\cup(kA_{i}\triangle A_{i})

\displaystyle\subseteq(KA_{i}\setminus kA_{i})\cup(kA_{i}\setminus KF_{i})\cup(kA_{i}\triangle A_{i})

\displaystyle\subseteq\partial_{K}A_{i}\cup(kA_{i}\setminus KF_{i})\cup\partial_{\{e_{G},k\}}A_{i}\cup\partial_{\{e_{G},k^{-1}\}}A_{i}.

Now recall that
B_{i}\subseteq KF_{i}
and
K^{-1}B_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
. Hence
B_{i}\subseteq kA_{i}
and we obtain

0\leq\frac{\theta(kA_{i}\setminus KF_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\leq\frac{\theta(kA_{i}\setminus B_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}=\frac{\theta(kA_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}-\frac{\theta(B_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\rightarrow 1-1=0.

Since
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
is van Hove, we conclude

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{\theta(KF_{i}\triangle A_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\leq\frac{\theta(\partial_{K}A_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}+\frac{\theta(kA_{i}\setminus KF_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}+\frac{\theta(\partial_{\{e_{G},k\}}A_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}+\frac{\theta(\partial_{\{e_{G},k^{-1}\}}A_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\rightarrow 0,

as desired and furthermore,
\lim_{i\in I}{\theta(KF_{i}\triangle A_{i})}/{\theta(KF_{i})}=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.16
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a van Hove net and
\omega
be a Delone set in
G
.
Let
K\subseteq G
be a compact subset such that
K\omega=G
and
e_{G}\in K
. Let
V
be a compact neighbourhood of
e_{G}
such that
(Vv)_{v\in\omega}
is a disjoint family.
We abbreviate
F_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A_{i}\cap\omega
and observe

\eta_{F_{i}}\supseteq\eta_{KF_{i}}=(\eta_{K})_{F_{i}}
.
Hence
H_{\mu}[\eta_{F_{i}}]\leq H_{\mu}[\eta_{KF_{i}}]=H_{\mu}[(\eta_{K})_{F_{i}}]
.
Since
\eta_{K}\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
we observe

\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}[\eta_{F_{i}}]}{\theta(A_{i})}\,\leq\,\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}[\eta_{KF_{i}}]}{\theta(A_{i})}\,\leq\,\sup_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}[\varepsilon_{F_{i}}]}{\theta(A_{i})}.

Taking the supremum over all
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
we obtain from Lemma
6.21
that

\displaystyle\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}[\pi,\eta]\,

\displaystyle=\,\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}[\eta_{A_{i}}]}{\theta(A_{i})}

\displaystyle=\,\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}[\eta_{KF_{i}}]}{\theta(A_{i})}\,=\,\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}[\eta_{F_{i}}]}{\theta(A_{i})}.

Now consider
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
and a finite measurable partition
\alpha
of
X
at scale
\eta
.
Then
\alpha_{F_{i}}
is at scale
\eta_{F_{i}}
and hence
H_{\mu}[\eta_{F_{i}}]\leq H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}})
. This shows

\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}[\pi,\eta]\,=\,\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}[\eta_{F_{i}}]}{\theta(A_{i})}\,\leq\,\sup_{\alpha}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}})}{\theta(A_{i})}\,=\,\operatorname{E}_{\mu,\mathcal{A},\omega}^{(OW)}(\pi),

where the last supremum is taken over all finite measurable partitions
\alpha
of
X
.

To show the reverse inequality, let
\varepsilon>0
and
\alpha
be a finite measurable partition of
X
. By Lemma
6.19
, there exists an entourage
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
such that, for any finite measurable partition
\gamma
of
X
at scale
\eta
, we have
H_{\mu}(\alpha|\gamma)<\varepsilon
.
For
i\in I
we consider a finite measurable partition
\beta
of
X
at scale
\eta_{F_{i}}
.
Clearly
\beta_{g^{-1}}:=\{g(B);\,B\in\beta\}
is at scale
\eta
for any
g\in F_{i}
and hence

H_{\mu}(\alpha|\beta_{g^{-1}})<\varepsilon
. This observation allows to compute

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}})\,

\displaystyle\leq\,H_{\mu}(\beta)+H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}}|\beta)\,\leq\,H_{\mu}(\beta)+H_{\mu}(\alpha_{g}|\beta)

\displaystyle=\,H_{\mu}(\beta)+H_{\mu}(\alpha|\beta_{g^{-1}})\,\leq\,H_{\mu}(\beta)+|F_{i}|\varepsilon.

Taking the infimum over all finite measurable partitions
\beta
of
X
at scale
\eta_{F_{i}}
, it follows that

H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}})\,\leq\,H_{\mu}[\eta_{F_{i}}]+|F_{i}|\varepsilon\,\leq\,H_{\mu}[\eta_{A_{i}}]+|F_{i}|\varepsilon.

Here we used
F_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
for the second inequality.
By our choice of
V
we furthermore know that
(Vg)_{g\in F_{i}}
is a disjoint family.
Hence

\theta({V}A_{i})\geq\theta(VF_{i})=\sum_{g\in F_{i}}\theta(Vg)=|F_{i}|\theta(V)
, and Lemma
6.21
implies that

\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{|F_{i}|}{\theta(A_{i})}=\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{|F_{i}|}{\theta(VA_{i})}\leq\frac{1}{\theta(V)}.

Combining our observations we conclude that

\displaystyle\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}})}{\theta(A_{i})}\,\leq\,\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}[\eta_{A_{i}}]}{\theta(A_{i})}+\varepsilon\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{|F_{i}|}{\theta(A_{i})}\,\leq\,\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}[\pi,\eta]+\frac{\varepsilon}{\theta(V)}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this shows that

\limsup_{i\in I}{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}})}/{\theta(A_{i})}\,\leq\,\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}[\pi,\eta]\,\leq\,\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}[\pi,\eta].

Taking the supremum over all finite measurable partitions
\alpha
of
X
now yields the desired statement.
∎

Remark 6.22
.

Note that the previous proof also shows that, for any finite measurable partition
\alpha
of
X
, there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
(independent of the choice of
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
or
\omega
) such that

\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{A_{i}\cap\omega})}{\theta(A_{i})}\leq c.

Indeed, there exists
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
such that
\limsup_{i\in I}{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{A_{i}\cap\omega})}/{\theta(A_{i})}\leq\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}[\pi,\eta]
, where the latter is finite as a consequence of the Ornstein-Weiss lemma.

Furthermore, with similar arguments as above one shows that the limit superior in the formula of Ornstein-Weiss entropy can be replaced by a limit inferior.

Recall that we introduced Delone sets, as we wanted
A_{i}\cap\omega
to be finite.
It is natural to ask, whether one can replace Delone sets by locally finite and relatively dense sets in our definition of entropy. The next example shows that this is not the case.

Example 6.23
.

Let
\mathbb{T}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbb{R}\big/\mathbb{Z}
be the circle equipped with the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
. Then
\pi^{g}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=x+g\mod 1
defines a continuous action of
\mathbb{R}
on
\mathbb{T}
with
\operatorname{E}_{\lambda}^{(OW)}(\pi)=0
.
Consider the finite measurable partition
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{[0,1/2),[1/2,1)\}
of
\mathbb{T}
.
Then
\omega\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbb{Z}\cup\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}([n,n+1]\cap\{2^{-n}z\mid z\in\mathbb{Z}\})
is a locally finite and relatively dense set for which

\alpha_{[0,n]\cap\omega}
consists of
2^{n}
intervals of equals length. Thus,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_{\lambda}(\alpha_{[0,n]\cap\omega})}{\theta([0,n])}\,=\,\log(2).

6.4.
Ornstein-Weiss topological pressure

Let
\pi
be a continuous action of an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
.
Consider any
f\in C(X)
and
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
.
Define
P_{f}[\eta]\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\inf_{\mathcal{U}}P_{f}(\mathcal{U})
, where the infimum is taken over all finite open covers
\mathcal{U}
of
X
at scale
\eta
.
We next show that the Ornstein-Weiss lemma can also be applied in this context.

Proposition 6.24
.

Let
\pi
be a continuous action of an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
, and let
f\in C(X)
and
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
. For every van Hove net
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
in
G
, the limit

\operatorname{p}_{f}[\pi,\eta]\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\lim_{i\in I}\frac{P_{f_{A_{i}}}(\eta_{A_{i}})}{\theta(A_{i})}

exists, is finite, and does not depend on the particular choice of
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to show that, if
f\geq 0
, then
\mathcal{K}(G)\ni A\mapsto P_{f_{A}}(\eta_{A})
is monotone, right-invariant and subadditive, and then the statement follows from the Ornstein-Weiss lemma.
In general, since
X
is compact, there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
f+c\geq 0
. Another standard argument shows that

P_{(f+c)_{A}}(\eta_{A})=P_{f_{A}}(\eta_{A})+c\theta(A)
for every compact subset
A\subseteq G
, and hence we obtain the general statement. ∎

Similar as above we have the following.

Theorem 6.25
.

Let
\pi
be a continuous action of an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
and
f\in C(X)
. For any van Hove net
\mathcal{A}
and any Delone set
\omega
in
G
, we have

\operatorname{p}_{f,\mathcal{A},\omega}^{(OW)}(\pi)=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{p}_{f}[\pi,\eta].

In particular,
\operatorname{p}_{f}^{(OW)}(\pi)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\operatorname{p}_{f,\mathcal{A},\omega}^{(OW)}(\pi)
is independent of the choice of a van Hove net and the choice of a Delone set.

Proposition 6.26
.

Let
\pi
be a continuous action of an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
.
For any
f\in C(X)
, any van Hove net
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
and any locally finite and relatively dense set
\omega
in
G
we have

\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{p}_{f}[\pi,\eta]=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{P_{f_{A_{i}}}(\eta_{(A_{i}\cap\omega)})}{\theta(A_{i})}.

The formula remains valid if the limit superior is replaced by a limit inferior.

Proof.

Let
K\subseteq G
be a compact subset such that
K\omega=G
and
e_{G}\in K
.
Define
F_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A_{i}\cap\omega
.
From Lemma
6.21
we know that
(KF_{i})_{i\in I}
is a van Hove net in
G
that satisfies

\lim_{i\in I}\theta(KF_{i})/\theta(A_{i})\,=\,1

and

\lim_{i\in I}\theta((KF_{i})\triangle A_{i})/\theta(KF_{i})=0
.
From

\displaystyle\left|\frac{P_{f_{KF_{i}}}(\eta_{KF_{i}})}{\theta(KF_{i})}-\frac{P_{f_{A_{i}}}(\eta_{KF_{i}})}{\theta(KF_{i})}\right|\leq\frac{\|f_{KF_{i}}-f_{A_{i}}\|_{\infty}}{\theta(KF_{i})}\leq\frac{\theta(KF_{i}\triangle A_{i})}{\theta(KF_{i})}\|f\|_{\infty}

we thus obtain

\operatorname{p}_{f}[\pi,\eta]=\lim_{i\in I}\frac{P_{f_{KF_{i}}}(\eta_{KF_{i}})}{\theta(KF_{i})}=\lim_{i\in I}\frac{P_{f_{A_{i}}}(\eta_{KF_{i}})}{\theta(KF_{i})}.

From
(\eta_{K})_{F_{i}}=\eta_{KF_{i}}
and
\eta_{K}\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
we thus observe

\displaystyle\operatorname{p}_{f}[\pi,\eta]\leq\sup_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{P_{f_{A_{i}}}(\varepsilon_{F_{i}})}{\theta(KF_{i})}\leq\sup_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\lim_{i\in I}\frac{P_{f_{A_{i}}}(\varepsilon_{KF_{i}})}{\theta(KF_{i})}=\sup_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{p}_{f}[\pi,\varepsilon].

Taking the supremum over all
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
yields the statement.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.25
.

Again we denote
F_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A_{i}\cap\omega
.
Consider
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
and a finite open cover

\mathcal{U}
at scale
\eta
. For

we observe that
\mathcal{U}_{F_{i}}

\eta_{F_{i}}

P_{f_{A_{i}}}[\eta_{F_{i}}]\,\leq\,P_{f_{A_{i}}}(\mathcal{U}_{F_{i}}).

\mathcal{V}

Remark 6.28
.

Lemma

facilitates a third approach to Ornstein-Weiss topological pressure.
Indeed, recall from this lemma that for any finite open cover
\mathcal{U}
and any compact set
A
of

\mathcal{V}
that
A
-refines
\mathcal{U}
.

6.5.
Restricting to uniform lattices

With the following lemma we introduce the notion of a
density

\operatorname{dens}(\omega)
of a uniform lattice
\omega
in an amenable unimodular amenable group. This allows to relate averaging along van Hove nets in
G
to averaging along van Hove nets in the discrete subgroup
\omega
.

Lemma 6.29
.

Let
\omega
be a uniform lattice in an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
and
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a van Hove net in
G
. Then
(A_{i}\cap\omega)_{i\in I}
is a van Hove net in
\omega
for which

\operatorname{dens}(\omega)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\lim_{i\in I}\frac{|A_{i}\cap\omega|}{\theta(A_{i})}

exists. This limit is independent of the choice of the van Hove net
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
.

Proof.

Let
C
be a regular and precompact fundamental domain of
\omega
with non-empty interior.
Let
K
denote the closure of
C
and
F_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A_{i}\cap\omega
.
Then
\theta(KF_{i})=\theta(K)|F_{i}|
and we obtain

\lim_{i\in I}|F_{i}|/\theta(A_{i})=\theta(K)^{-1}\lim_{i\in I}\theta(KF_{i})/\theta(A_{i})=\theta(K)^{-1}
from Lemma
6.21
.
With a standard argument one obtains that the existence of this limit (for all van Hove nets) implies the independence of the choice of a van Hove net
[
Kri10
]
.

In order to show that
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
constitutes a van Hove net in
\omega
, let
F\subseteq G
be a compact subset and denote by
\partial_{F}^{\omega}F_{i}
the
F
-boundary of
F_{i}
in
\omega
. A straightforward computation shows that

C\partial_{F}^{\omega}F_{i}\subseteq\partial_{KF}^{G}A_{i}
and hence

\displaystyle 0\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{|\partial_{F}^{\omega}F_{i}|}{|F_{i}|}\,=\,\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{\theta(C\partial_{F}^{\omega}F_{i})}{\theta(CF_{i})}\,\leq\,\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{\theta(\partial_{KF}^{G}A_{i})}{\theta(B_{i})}\,=\,\lim_{i\in I}\frac{\theta(\partial_{KF}^{G}A_{i})}{\theta(A_{i})}\,=\,0.\qed

From the following we obtain that Ornstein-Weiss entropy and topological pressure restrict to the classical definitions in the context of actions of discrete amenable groups. In particular, we obtain that our notion agrees with the notion considered in
[
Fel80
]
in the context of actions of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Theorem 6.30
.

Let
\pi
be an action of an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
and
\varphi
be the restriction of
\pi
to a uniform lattice
\omega\subseteq G
. Let
K
be the closure of a regular and precompact fundamental domain of
\omega

(i)

If
\pi
is a measure preserving action on a probability space
(X,\mu)
, so is
\varphi
and we have

\operatorname{E}^{(OW)}_{\mu}(\pi)=\operatorname{dens}(\omega)\operatorname{E}^{(OW)}_{\mu}(\varphi).

(ii)

If
\pi
is a continuous action on a compact Hausdorff space
X
, so is
\varphi
and for any
f\in C(X)
we have

\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f}(\pi)=\operatorname{dens}(\omega)\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f_{K}}(\varphi).

Proof of Theorem
6.30
.

We obtain the first formula as a direct consequence of the definition of the Ornstein-Weiss entropy and Lemma
6.29
.
In order to show the second formula we consider the closure
K
of a regular and precompact fundamental domain
C
of
\omega
and a van Hove net
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
in
G
.
Let
K
denote the closure of
C
and
F_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A_{i}\cap\omega
.
Now recall from Lemma
6.21
that
(KF_{i})_{i\in I}
is a van Hove net in
G
. From the proof of Lemma
6.29
we know that
\operatorname{dens}(\omega)=\theta(K)^{-1}
and from the regularity of
C
we observe
f_{KF_{i}}=\sum_{F_{i}}f_{K}
.
For
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
we compute

\displaystyle\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f}[\pi,\eta]=\lim_{i\in I}\frac{P_{f_{KF_{i}}}(\eta_{KF_{i}})}{\theta(KF_{i})}=\lim_{i\in I}\frac{P_{\sum_{F_{i}}f_{K}}((\eta_{K})_{F_{i}})}{\theta(K)|F_{i}|}=\operatorname{dens}(\omega)\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f_{K}}[\varphi,\eta_{K}].

Consider
k\in K
.
Since
\eta_{k}\supseteq\eta_{K}\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
for
\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
we observe

\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f_{K}}(\varphi)=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f_{K}}[\varphi,\eta_{k}]\leq\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f_{K}}[\varphi,\eta_{K}]\leq\sup_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f_{K}}[\varphi,\varepsilon]=\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f_{K}}(\varphi)

The combination of these observations yields

\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f}(\pi)=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f}[\pi,\eta]=\operatorname{dens}(\omega)\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}}\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f_{K}}[\varphi,\eta_{K}]=\operatorname{dens}(\omega)\operatorname{p}^{(OW)}_{f_{K}}(\varphi).\qed

6.6.
Goodwyn’s theorem for Ornstein-Weiss entropy

Our next objective is to show that Ornstein-Weiss entropy satisfies Goodwyn’s theorem.

Theorem 6.31

(Goodwyn’s theorem for Ornstein-Weiss entropy)
.

Let
\pi
be a continuous action of an amenable unimodular locally compact group
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
.
For every invariant, regular Borel probability measure
\mu
on
X
and every
f\in C(X)
,

\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}(\pi)+\mu(f)\,\leq\,\operatorname{p}_{f}^{(OW)}(\pi).

Proof of Theorem
6.31
.

Let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a van Hove net and
\omega
be a Delone set in
G
. Let
V
be a compact neighbourhood of the identity element in
G
and consider a
V
-discrete Delone set
\omega
in
G
. Let
F_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\omega\cap A_{i}
for all
i\in I
.
We obtain

|F_{i}|\theta(V)=\theta(VF_{i})\leq\theta(VA_{i})
and thus

\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{|F_{i}|}{\theta(A_{i})}\,=\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{|F_{i}|}{\theta(VA_{i})}\,\leq\,\frac{1}{\theta(V)}.

By Lemma
6.4
, for every finite measurable partition
\alpha
of
X
, there exists a finite open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
such that, for every
i\in I
,

H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}})+\mu(f_{A_{i}})\,\leq\,\operatorname{P}_{f_{A_{i}}}(\mathcal{U}_{F_{i}})+|F_{i}|\theta(V)\varepsilon.

Since
\mu
is invariant we know
\mu(f_{A_{i}})=\mu(f)\theta(A_{i})
. We thus obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}})}{\theta(A_{i})}+\mu(f)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\in I}\frac{H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{i}})+\mu(f_{A_{i}})}{\theta(A_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{\operatorname{P}_{f_{A_{i}}}(\mathcal{U}_{F_{i}})}{\theta(A_{i})}+\limsup_{i\in I}\frac{|F_{i}|}{\theta(A_{i})}\theta(V)\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{p}_{f}^{(OW)}(\pi)+\varepsilon.

Taking the supremum over all finite measurable partitions
\alpha
of
X
, we conclude that

\operatorname{E}_{\mu}^{(OW)}(\pi)+\mu(f)\,\leq\,\operatorname{p}_{f}^{(OW)}(\pi)+\varepsilon.

The desired statement now follows, as
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary.
∎

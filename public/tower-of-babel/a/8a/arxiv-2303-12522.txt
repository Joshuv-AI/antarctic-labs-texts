Global-in-time regular unique solutions with positive temperature to the 1d thermoelasticity
Piotr Michał Bies, Tomasz Cieślak

Abstract
We construct a unique regular solution to the minimal nonlinear system of the 1d thermoelasticity. The obtained solution has a positive temperature. Our approach is based on an estimate, using the Fisher information, which seems completely new in this context. It is combined with a recent inequality in [ 2 ] and embeddings, which allows us to obtain a new energy estimate. The latter is used in a half-Galerkin procedure yielding global solutions. The uniqueness and further regularity of such solutions are obtained.



Keywords
: Fisher’s information, 1D thermoelasticity, regular unique solutions

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 35M13, 35Q74, 74A15

Introduction

The present paper is devoted to the study of the one-dimensional minimal hyperbolic-parabolic system of thermoelasticity.
In the next section, we present the physical derivation of the system and discuss assumptions leading to the following system of equations. Our main result is a global-in-time existence of a unique regular solution to the obtained system.

Let us take
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that
a<b
and
T>0
. We are interested in real-valued functions of time and space
\theta
and
u
satisfying

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-u_{xx}=\mu\theta_{x},&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times(a,b),\\
\theta_{t}-\theta_{xx}=\mu\theta u_{tx},&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times(a,b),\\
u(\cdot,a)=u(\cdot,b)=\theta_{x}(\cdot,a)=\theta_{x}(\cdot,b)=0,&\textrm{on }(0,T),\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},\ u_{t}(0,\cdot)=v_{0},\ \theta(0,\cdot)=\theta_{0}>0,\end{cases}

(1)

where
\mu
is a constant, initial data
\theta_{0},u_{0},v_{0}
are given. The regularity of initial data is discussed later.
Functions
\theta
and
u
are interpreted as temperature and displacement of the 1D material (a rod for instance) respectively.
We consider the evolution of a rod undergoing heat displacement.

Notice that for the regular functions
\theta
and
u
one has the conservation of energy. Indeed, the following simple proposition holds.

Proposition 1.1
.

Regular solutions
\theta,u
of (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}u_{t}^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}u_{x}^{2}dx+\int_{a}^{b}\theta dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}v_{0}^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}u_{0,x}^{2}dx+\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{0}dx.

(2)

Proof.

We multiply the first equation of (
1
) by
u_{t}
and integrate over
(a,b)
. It yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{d}}{dt}\left(\int_{a}^{b}u_{t}^{2}dx+\int_{a}^{b}u_{x}^{2}dx\right)=\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{x}u_{t}dx.

(3)

Then, we integrate the second equation. We get

\displaystyle\frac{{d}}{dt}\int_{a}^{b}\theta dx=-\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{x}u_{t}dx.

(4)

Now, we add (
3
) and (
4
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{{d}}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}u_{t}^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}u_{x}^{2}dx+\int_{a}^{b}\theta dx\right)=0.

And so the total energy balance (
2
) is obtained.
∎

In the present paper, we shall obtain the existence of regular global-in-time a unique solution to (
1
) such that the temperature
\theta
is positive. Below we formulate the definition of the solution.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that
(u,\theta)
is a solution to (
1
) if:

•

The initial data is of regularity

\displaystyle u_{0}\in H^{2}(a,b)\cap H^{1}_{0}(a,b),\quad v_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(a,b),\quad\theta_{0}\in H^{1}(a,b).

We also require that there exists
\tilde{\theta}>0
such that
\theta_{0}(x)\geq\tilde{\theta}
for all
x\in[a,b]
.

•

Solutions
\theta
and
u
satisfy

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(a,b))\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,T,H^{1}_{0}(a,b))\cap W^{2,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(a,b)),

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(a,b))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(a,b)).

(5)

•

The momentum equation

\displaystyle u_{tt}-u_{xx}=\mu\theta_{x}

is satisfied for almost all
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(a,b)
.

•

The entropy equation

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{t}\psi dx+\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{x}\psi_{x}dx=\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta u_{tx}\psi dx

holds for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}[a,b]
and for almost all
t\in(0,T)
.

•

Initial conditions are attained in the following sense:

\theta\in C([0,T];L^{2}(a,b)),

u\in C([0,T];H^{1}_{0}(a,b)),\ u_{t}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(a,b)).

The following result is the main achievement of the paper.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
T>0
and any constant
\mu
there exists a unique solution of (
1
) in the sense of Definition
1.2
with a positive temperature.

As far as we know this is the first global-in-time existence and uniqueness result independent of the size of initial data for the minimal
thermoelasticity model. The minimal model is introduced as a particular case of the general 1D thermoelasticity model considered for instance in
[

:

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\alpha(\theta,u_{x})u_{xx}=\beta(\theta,u_{x})\theta_{x},&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times(a,b),\\
\gamma(\theta,u_{x})\theta_{t}-\delta(\theta_{x})\theta_{xx}=\beta(\theta,u_{x})\theta u_{tx},&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times(a,b),\\
u(\cdot,a)=u(\cdot,b)=\theta_{x}(\cdot,a)=\theta_{x}(\cdot,b)=0,&\textrm{on }(0,T),\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},\ u_{t}(0,\cdot)=v_{0},\ \theta(0,\cdot)=\theta_{0}>0.\end{cases}

(6)

The physical meaning of functions

and

[

4

. In
[

In the case of the studies of more general systems of the form (

A slightly different approach was taken in

\int_{a}^{b}\left[\left(\psi^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{xx}\right]^{2}dx\leq\frac{13}{8}\int_{a}^{b}\psi\left[\left(\log\psi\right)_{xx}\right]^{2}dx

2
Physical derivation

In the present article, we discuss a brief derivation of the physical foundations of a system (
1
) and discuss if the assumptions leading to it are physically relevant. For a more detailed explanation, we refer to
[
4
]
or to
[
17
]
.

System (
1
) is a one-dimensional version of the mechanical description of the balance of momentum as well as the balance of internal energy, under some assumptions on the constitutive relations. The general system reads

\begin{cases}\rho u_{tt}=\nabla\cdot\sigma,&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
e_{t}+\nabla\cdot q=\sigma\cdot\operatorname{div}u_{t},&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\end{cases}

(7)

where
\rho
is the density of the elastic body,
u
the displacement vector,
\sigma=\sigma(\theta,\nabla u)
(
\theta
is temperature) the stress tensor,
e=e(\theta,\nabla u)
the internal energy and
q=q(\theta,\nabla\theta)
the internal energy flux. They all can be represented by the Helmholtz free energy function
\psi:=e-\theta s
, where
s
is the entropy. Moreover,
\sigma
and
s
are expressed as

\sigma(\theta,\nabla u)=\psi_{F}(\theta,\nabla u),\;\;s(\theta,\nabla u)=-\psi_{\theta}(\theta,\nabla u),

subscripts denote the partial derivatives (
\psi_{F}
means the derivative with respect to the second variable).
The Helmholtz function representation of the parameters and 1D considerations lead
[
17
]
to the system (
6
).

However, one can impose further structural assumptions on the Helmholtz function (for the details see
[
4
]
), in particular, the decoupling of energy

e(\theta,\nabla u)=e_{1}(\theta)+e_{2}(\nabla u)

and entropy

s(\theta,\nabla u)=s_{1}(\theta)+s_{2}(\nabla u),

together with the following prescription of
e_{2},s_{2}

e_{2}(\nabla u)=\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2},\;s_{2}(\nabla u)=\mu\nabla\cdot u,

which means that we consider only small deformations (here
\nabla\cdot u
represents linearization of Jacobian).
Then we are led to the following simplified model for the balance of momentum and internal energy

\begin{cases}\rho u_{tt}=\nabla\cdot(\nabla u-\mu\theta Id),&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
C_{V}(\theta)\theta_{t}+\nabla\cdot q=-\mu\theta\operatorname{div}u_{t},&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\end{cases}

(8)

where
C_{V}:=\partial_{\theta}e_{1}(\theta)
is the heat capacity. When assuming further
the density
\rho=1
and the internal energy flux to satisfy the Fick law
q:=-\kappa(\theta)\nabla\theta
,
we arrive at the higher-dimensional extension of (
1
). Assuming further
\kappa=1
and
C_{V}=1
(the latter assumptions can be considered as an approximation of the high-temperature Dulong-Petit model), we obtain exactly
(
1
) in higher dimensions.

3
A priori estimates

The present section is devoted to the derivation of a crucial new estimate, which appears when estimating the evolution of the Fisher information of temperature along the trajectories of (
1
). Throughout this section we assume solutions of (
1
) to be regular.
Rigorous treatment will be given in Section
4
.

The first novel step is the following identity which holds along the trajectories of (
1
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let us assume that
u\in C^{3}([0,T]\times[a,b])
and
\theta\in C^{1,2}([0,T]\times[a,b])
,
\theta>0
are solutions of (
1
). Then, the following identity holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{d}}{dt}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\theta_{x}^{2}}{\theta}dx+\int_{a}^{b}u_{tx}^{2}dx+\int_{a}^{b}u_{xx}^{2}dx\right)=-\int_{a}^{b}\theta\left[(\log\theta)_{xx}\right]^{2}dx+\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\theta^{2}_{x}}{\theta}u_{tx}dx.

Proof.

We divide the second equation in (
1
) by
\theta^{1/2}
and obtain

\displaystyle 2\frac{{d}}{dt}\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}-2(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})_{xx}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\theta_{x}^{2}}{\theta^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}}=\mu\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{tx}.

(9)

In addition, we have
\frac{1}{2}\frac{\theta_{x}^{2}}{\theta^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}}=2\frac{(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})^{2}_{x}}{\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\frac{{d}}{dt}

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\theta_{x}^{2}}{\theta}dx=4\frac{{d}}{dt}\int_{a}^{b}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)^{2}_{x}dx=8\int_{a}^{b}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{xt}dx

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{rowlem1})}}{{=}}4\int_{a}^{b}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}\left[2(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})_{xx}+2\frac{(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})^{2}_{x}}{\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}+\mu\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{tx}\right]_{x}dx=-8\int_{a}^{b}\left[\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{xx}\right]^{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad+16\int_{a}^{b}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}\frac{\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{xx}}{\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}dx-8\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\left[\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}\right]^{4}}{\theta}dx+4\mu\int_{a}^{b}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}\left[\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{tx}\right]_{x}dx.

(10)

Next, observe that

\displaystyle-8

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left[\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{xx}\right]^{2}dx+16\int_{a}^{b}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}\frac{\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{xx}}{\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}dx-8\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\left[\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}\right]^{4}}{\theta}dx

\displaystyle=-8\int_{a}^{b}\theta\left(\frac{\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{xx}}{\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}-\frac{\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}^{2}}{\theta}\right)^{2}dx=-8\int_{a}^{b}\theta\left[\left(\log\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{xx}\right]^{2}dx

\displaystyle=-2\int_{a}^{b}\theta\left[\left(\log\theta\right)_{xx}\right]^{2}dx.

(11)

Moreover, we calculate

\displaystyle 4\mu\int_{a}^{b}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{tx}\right)_{x}dx

\displaystyle=-4\mu\int_{a}^{b}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{xx}\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{tx}dx

\displaystyle=-2\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{xx}u_{tx}dx+\mu\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\theta_{x}^{2}}{\theta}u_{tx}dx,

(12)

where we used the identity
\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{xx}=\frac{1}{2}\theta^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\theta_{xx}-\frac{1}{4}\theta^{-\tfrac{3}{2}}\theta_{x}^{2}
.

Now, plugging (
3
) and (
3
) in (
3
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{{d}}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\theta^{2}_{x}}{\theta}dx=-\int_{a}^{b}\theta\left[\left(\log\theta\right)_{xx}\right]^{2}dx-\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{xx}u_{tx}dx+\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\theta_{x}^{2}}{\theta}u_{tx}dx.

(13)

Next, we multiply the first equation on (
1
) by
u_{txx}
and we integrate over
[a,b]
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}u_{tt}u_{txx}dx-\int_{a}^{b}u_{xx}u_{txx}dx=\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{x}u_{txx}dx.

This after integration by parts gives us

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{d}}{dt}\left(\int_{a}^{b}u_{tx}^{2}dx+\int_{a}^{b}u_{xx}^{2}dx\right)=\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{xx}u_{tx}dx.

(14)

Then, we add (
14
) and (
13

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{d}}{dt}\left(\int_{a}^{b}u_{tx}^{2}dx+\int_{a}^{b}u_{xx}^{2}dx+\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\theta^{2}_{x}}{\theta}dx\right)=-\int_{a}^{b}\theta\left[\left(\log\theta\right)_{xx}\right]^{2}dx+\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\theta_{x}^{2}}{\theta}u_{tx}dx.

∎

The next step is to estimate the last term on the right-hand side of the identity in Lemma

Proposition 3.2

Let us assume that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\theta_{x}^{2}}{\theta}u_{tx}dx&\leq\varepsilon\int_{a}^{b}\theta\left[\left(\log\theta\right)_{xx}\right]^{2}dx+c_{1}\|u_{tx}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}\\
&\quad+\varepsilon_{1}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}^{2}dx\right)^{2}+c_{2}\int_{a}^{b}\left(\theta^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)_{x}^{2}dx\end{split}

(15)

holds and
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depend on
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(a,b)}
,
\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{1}(a,b)}

\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}
,

\mu

\varepsilon

Proof.

Let us consider the left-hand side of (

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\theta_{x}^{2}}{\theta}u_{tx}dx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{d}}{dt}

4
Existence of solutions

The present section is devoted to the rigorous construction of a global-in-time regular solution to (
1
).
To this end the a priori estimate obtained in Theorem
3.4
is used. However, a delicate construction of approximate solutions is required. In principle, there are many options for constructing solutions. It seems to us that the half-Galerkin procedure chosen by us leads to the final goal at the lowest price.

4.1
Approximate problem

In the wave equation part of (
1
) we shall use the Galerkin approximation. The heat equation will then be solved in a straight manner. The whole procedure follows the scheme proposed in
[
4
]
. We introduce a smooth basis
\{\varphi_{i}\}
of

By

Proposition 4.2

For every

5
Uniqueness of solutions

In this section, the uniqueness claim of Theorem
1.3
is achieved.
We prove the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

The solution satisfying (
1
) in the sense of Definition
1.2
is unique.

Proof.

Let us assume that
(u_{1},\theta_{1})
and
(u_{2},\theta_{2})
are two solutions of (
1
) in the sense of Definition
1.2
. Let us also denote
u=u_{1}-u_{2}
and
\theta=\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}
. Now, let us subtract the momentum equation for
(u_{2},\theta_{2})
from the momentum equation for
(u_{1},\theta_{1})
. In addition, w multiply the obtained identity by
u_{t}
and integrate over
[a,b]
. It yields

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}u_{tt}u_{t}dx+\int_{a}^{b}u_{x}u_{tx}dx=\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{x}u_{t}dx.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{d}}{dt}\left(\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}\right)\leq\varepsilon\|\theta_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+c\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}.

(35)

Similarly, we proceed with the entropy equation and test it by
\psi=\theta
, then

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{t}\theta dx

\displaystyle+\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{x}^{2}dx=\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{1}u_{1,tx}\theta dx-\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{2}u_{2,tx}\theta dx

\displaystyle=\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta^{2}u_{1,tx}dx+\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta_{2}u_{tx}\theta dx.

We integrate by parts the right-hand side of the above equality to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{d}}{dt}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\|\theta_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\mu\int_{a}^{b}\theta\theta_{x}u_{1,t}dx-\mu\int_{a}^{b}u_{t}\theta_{2,x}\theta dx-\mu\int_{a}^{b}u_{t}\theta_{2}\theta_{x}dx

\displaystyle:=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

(36)

Next, we estimate all the terms
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
in a similar manner, using the one-dimensional embeddings.

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq 2\mu\|u_{1,t}\|_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}\|\theta_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}\leq c(\varepsilon)\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\theta_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2},

where in the last inequality we used the embedding
H^{1}\subset L^{\infty}
and (
25
).

With
I_{2}
we proceed as follows

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mu\|u_{t}\theta\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}\|\theta_{2,x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}\leq\mu\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}\|\theta_{2,x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\theta\|_{H^{1}(a,b)}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}\leq\varepsilon\|\theta_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(a,b)},

where in the third inequality we used the fact that
\theta_{2}
satisfies (
5
).
Next, we estimate
I_{3}

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq\mu\|\theta_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}\|\theta_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}\leq\varepsilon\|\theta_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(a,b)}+c(\varepsilon)\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2},

again we used the fact that
\theta_{2}
satisfies (
5
) and embedding
H^{1}(a,b)\subset L^{\infty}(a,b)
.

Next, we plug in the above estimates of
I_{1},I_{2}
and
I_{3}

5

\frac{1}{2}\frac{{d}}{dt}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\|\theta_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}\leq 3\varepsilon\|\theta_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+c(\varepsilon)\left(\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}\right).

(37)

Adding (
37
) and (
35
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{d}}{dt}\left(\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}\right)+\|\theta_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\varepsilon\|\theta_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+c(\varepsilon)\left(\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}\right).

Now, let us take

, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{d}}{dt}\left(\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}\right)\leq c\left(\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}+\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(a,b)}^{2}\right),

and by the Gronwall inequality
\theta=u_{x}=u_{t}=0
for almost all

. Hence, we immediately get

and
\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}
.
∎

6
Conclusions

In our article, we have proved the existence of a global-in-time regular unique solution to the minimal nonlinear
thermoelasticity problem (
1
). Our solution is based on a new estimate, described in Section
3
.
The interesting problem for future research is the possibility of extending our estimates to the more general,
thermodynamically relevant system (
6
). Since Fisher’s information has a deep thermodynamical meaning, our approach seems to have a chance to be successful also in more general thermodynamically relevant problems. In particular, we hope that an extension of our Fisher’s entropy estimates coupled with a proper generalization of an equality
[

We have the following formal estimate (we assume that solutions are regular).

Proposition 6.1
.

Solution
(u,\theta)
to (
38
) satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2}}{\theta}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\operatorname{div}(u_{t})|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\operatorname{div}u|^{2}dx\right)=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\theta\left|D^{2}\log\theta\right|^{2}dx+\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2}}{\theta}\operatorname{div}(u_{t})dx.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the proof of Lemma
3.1
, so we only sketch it. First, the higher-dimensional Fisher information argument gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2}}{\theta}dx=-\int_{\Omega}\theta|D^{2}\log\theta|^{2}dx-\mu\int_{\Omega}\Delta\theta\operatorname{div}(u_{t})dx+\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2}}{\theta}\operatorname{div}(u_{t})dx.

Next, multiplying the wave equation in (
38

\nabla\operatorname{div}(u_{t})

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\operatorname{div}(u_{t})|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\operatorname{div}u|^{2}dx\right)=\mu\int_{\Omega}\Delta\theta\operatorname{div}(u_{t})dx.

The claim follows.

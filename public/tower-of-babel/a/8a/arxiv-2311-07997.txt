Deep-water limit of the intermediate long wave equation in L 2 L^{2}
Andreia Chapouto, Guopeng Li, Tadahiro Oh, and Didier Pilod

Abstract
. We study the well-posedness issue of the intermediate long wave equation (ILW) on both the real line and the circle. By applying the gauge transform for the Benjamin-Ono equation (BO) and adapting the L 2 L^{2} well-posedness argument for BO by Molinet and the fourth author (2012), we prove global well-posedness of ILW in L 2 L^{2} on both the real line and the circle. In the periodic setting, this provides the first low regularity well-posedness of ILW. We then establish convergence of the ILW dynamics to the BO dynamics in the deep-water limit at the L 2 L^{2} -level.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study
the intermediate long wave equation (ILW):

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2})\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{cases}\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M},

where

\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
.
The equation (
1.1
), introduced in
[
27
,
36
]
,
models
the internal wave propagation of the interface
in a stratified fluid of finite depth
\delta>0
,
and the unknown

denotes the amplitude of the internal wave at the interface.
See also Remark 1.1 in
[
39
]

The operator
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
in (
1.1
)
is defined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\delta}=\mathcal{T}_{\delta}-\delta^{-1}\partial_{x}^{-1},

where
\mathcal{T}_{\delta}

is given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{T}_{\delta}f}(\xi)=-i\coth(\delta\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}.

Here,
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
denotes
the Pontryagin dual of
\mathcal{M}
, i.e.

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{R}
if
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}
,
and

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{Z}
if
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{T}
,
and

\coth
denotes the usual hyperbolic cotangent function:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\coth(x)=\frac{e^{x}+e^{-x}}{e^{x}-e^{-x}}=\frac{e^{2x}+1}{e^{2x}-1},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}

with the convention
\coth(x)-\frac{1}{x}=0
for
x=0

Note that

is the inverse of the
so-called Tilbert transform (with an extra

appearing in the study of water waves

[

20

2

In particular,

is related to
the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map in a two-dimensional finite depth

and in the deep-water limit (
\delta\to\infty
),
it converges to the Hilbert transform

(with the Fourier multiplier
-i\textup{sgn}(\xi)

corresponding to
the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map

which indicates
possible convergence of ILW (
1.1
)
to
the Benjamin-Ono equation (BO):

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2}).

Indeed,

1.1

the deep-water regime (= the Benjamin-Ono regime) and the shallow-water regime

As such, it has been studied extensively from both the applied and theoretical points of view.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\text{mean: }&\int_{\mathcal{M}}udx,\qquad\text{mass: }M(u)=\int_{\mathcal{M}}u^{2}dx,\\

Remark 1.4

Given

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}f\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\delta^{-1}(1+\delta^{-s})\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}f\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}},

Proof.

With a slight abuse of notation,
let

denotes the multiplier
for the operator

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{\delta}(n)=n\big(\coth(\delta n)-\textup{sgn}(n)\big).

From

\displaystyle\coth(\delta n)-\textup{sgn}(n)=\textup{sgn}(n)\cdot\frac{2}{e^{2|\delta n|}-1}

Then,

∎

.

Lemma 2.4

Let

,

Lemma 2.5

Remark 4.2
.

In this section, we proved local and global well-posedness

(and then of ILW (

In the general case,

-norm case as in

42
,

,

Given

in invariant under the action of

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v_{\lambda}-\mathcal{T}_{\lambda\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{\lambda}=\partial_{x}(v_{\lambda}^{2}).

In the periodic setting,

This is the primary reason for the failure of local uniform continuity
of the solution map for ILW on
\mathbb{R}
constructed in Theorem
1.1
.
Nonetheless, under
the extra assumption (
3.9
),
one can still prove (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) on
\mathbb{R}

(but the argument uses the equation; see
[
44
, Lemma 4.1]

in the case of the BO equation),
giving local Lipschitz continuity
of the solution map restricted to the class
(
3.9
) (say, for fixed
v_{1}(0)
)
consisting of elements with the identical low-frequency parts.
See Section 4 in
[
44
]

for a further discussion.

5.
Deep-water limit of ILW in
L^{2}

In this section,
by slightly modifying the argument in Section
4
,
we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
As in the previous sections, we only consider the periodic case.
Moreover, we only consider the case with sufficiently small
L^{2}
-norms.

\bullet

Part 1:

In this part, we consider the mean-zero case.
Namely,
given
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{2}
, fix a mean-zero function
u_{0}\in H^{s}_{0}(\mathbb{T})

and
let
\{u_{0,\delta}\}_{1\leq\delta<\infty}

be a net in
H^{s}_{0}(\mathbb{T})

(of mean-zero functions)
such that

u_{0,\delta}
converges to
u_{0}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
as
\delta\to\infty
.

We first establish convergence of solutions to the renormalized ILW (
3.2
)
to the associated solution to the BO equation (
1.5
).
Let
u
be the global solution to the BO equation (
1.5
)
with
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}

constructed in
[
44
]
,
and
let
v_{\delta}
,
1\leq\delta<\infty
, be the global solution to (
3.2
) with

v_{\delta}|_{t=0}=u_{0,\delta}

constructed in Theorem
1.1
.
Recalling that
\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}=0
,
the renormalized ILW (
3.2
) formally reduces to the BO equation (
1.5
)
when
\delta=\infty
.
In view of this, we set
v_{\infty}=u
and
u_{0,\infty}=u_{0}
.
For each
1\leq\delta\leq\infty
,
let
F_{\delta}
be the mean-zero primitive of
v_{\delta}

as defined in (
3.4
),
and
let

W_{\delta}
and
w_{\delta}
be the associated gauged functions
as in (
3.10
) and (
3.12
).

Let
N^{s}_{T}
be as in (
4.1
).
Then, the a priori bound
(
4.6
)
holds uniformly in
1\leq\delta\leq\infty
(including
\delta=\infty
):

(5.1)

\displaystyle N^{s}_{T}(v_{\delta})

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\|u_{0,\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\big)\|u_{0,\delta}\|_{H^{s}}+N^{0}_{T}(v_{\delta})\big(1+N^{0}_{T}(v_{\delta})\big)N^{s}_{T}(v_{\delta})

for any
0<T\leq T_{*}
,
where
T_{*}>0
is now independent of
1\leq\delta\leq\infty
.
By assuming that

\|u_{0,\infty}\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,
it follows from
the convergence of
u_{0,\delta}
to
u_{0,\infty}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})

that there exists

\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\varepsilon)\geq 1
such that

\|u_{0,\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon

for any
\delta\geq\delta_{0}
.
Then,
from (
5.1
), we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle N^{0}_{T_{*}}(v_{\delta})\lesssim\varepsilon\qquad\text{and}\qquad N^{s}_{T_{*}}(v_{\delta})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0,\delta}\|_{H^{s}}\leq M

for some
M>0
,
uniformly in
\delta_{0}\leq\delta\leq\infty
.

Let
\widetilde{v}_{\delta}=v_{\delta}-v_{\infty}
,

\widetilde{W}_{\delta}=W_{\delta}-W_{\infty}
, and
\widetilde{w}_{\delta}=w_{\delta}-w_{\infty}
.
The difference

\widetilde{w}_{\delta}
satisfies

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}&\widetilde{w}_{\delta}-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}\widetilde{w}_{\delta}\\
&=-2\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{+,\textup{hi}}(W_{\delta}\mathbf{P}_{-}\partial_{x}\widetilde{v}_{\delta})-2\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{+,\textup{hi}}(\widetilde{W}_{\delta}\mathbf{P}_{-}\partial_{x}v_{\infty})\\
&\quad-2\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{+,\textup{hi}}(\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{iF_{\delta}}\mathbf{P}_{-}\partial_{x}\widetilde{v}_{\delta})-2\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{+,\textup{hi}}\big(\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}(e^{iF_{\delta}}-e^{iF_{\infty}})\mathbf{P}_{-}\partial_{x}v_{\infty}\big)\\
&\quad+i\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{+,\textup{hi}}(e^{iF_{\delta}}\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}v_{\delta})+0\\
&\quad-i\mathbf{P}_{0}(v_{\delta}^{2})\widetilde{w}_{\delta}-i\mathbf{P}_{0}(v_{\delta}^{2}-v_{\infty}^{2})w_{\infty}.\end{split}

Compare this with (
4.13
),
where the difference appears only
in the fifth term and the sixth term (which is
0
in (
5.3
)).
In estimating the fifth term
on the right-hand side of (
5.3
), we instead use (
3.31
).
Hence, from the discussion in Subsection
4.2

leading to (
4.20
)
with (
5.2
), we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\widetilde{w}_{\delta}\|_{Y^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(T_{*})}&\leq C\|\widetilde{w}_{\delta}(0)\|_{H^{s}}\\
&\quad+C\big(\varepsilon+\|w_{\delta}\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(T_{*})}\big)\Big(\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}L^{2}_{x}}+\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{4}_{T_{*},x}}+\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{X^{-1,1}(T_{*})}\Big)\\
&\quad+C\varepsilon\|w_{\infty}\|_{Y^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(T_{*})}\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}L^{2}_{x}}+T_{*}^{\theta}M\delta^{-1},\end{split}

uniformly in
\delta_{0}\leq\delta\leq\infty
.
From (
1.5
) and (
3.3
), we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widetilde{v}_{\delta}-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}\widetilde{v}_{\delta}=\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}\partial_{x}v_{\delta}+\partial_{x}\big((v_{\delta}+v_{\infty})\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\big).

Then, by a slight modification of the proof of (
3.15
) in
Lemma
3.4
with (
5.2
),
we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{X^{-1,1}(T_{*})}&\lesssim\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}L^{2}_{x}}+\varepsilon\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{4}_{T_{*},x}}+\delta^{-1}\|v_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}L^{2}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}L^{2}_{x}}+\varepsilon\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{4}_{T_{*},x}}+\varepsilon\delta^{-1},\end{split}

uniformly in
\delta_{0}\leq\delta\leq\infty
.

Lastly,
note that
(
5.5
) is different from
(
4.21
) only on the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.5
)
(under the identification of
v_{1}
and
v_{2}

with
v_{\delta}
and
v_{\infty}
, respectively).
Namely,
the bound (
4.33
)
holds except for

c_{1}\|\widetilde{v}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}

in (
4.33
), coming from (
4.24
)
and (
4.25
).
In this case, we instead apply
(
3.19
) with (
5.2
)
to estimate the contribution from the first term on the right-hand side
of (
5.5
).
This yields

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|J^{s}\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{p}_{T_{*}}L^{q}_{x}}&\lesssim\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\Big(\|w_{\infty}\|_{Y^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(T_{*})}+\|v_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}H^{s}_{x}}+\varepsilon\|v_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}H^{s}_{x}}\Big)\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}L^{2}_{x}}\\
&\quad+\varepsilon\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}H^{s}_{x}}+\|\widetilde{w}_{\delta}\|_{Y^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(T_{*})}+\varepsilon\delta^{-1}\end{split}

for
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{2}

and

(p,q)=(\infty,2)
or
(4,4)
,
uniformly in
\delta_{0}\leq\delta\leq\infty
,
where the last term represents
the contribution from the first term on the right-hand side
of (
5.5
).

Therefore, putting (
5.4
), (
5.6
), and (
5.7
)
together
with (
4.12
) (for
\widetilde{w}_{\delta}(0)
and
\widetilde{v}_{\delta}(0)
)
and proceeding as in Subsection
4.2

(first for
s=0
as in (
4.34
) and then for
s
as in (
4.36
)),
we obtain

(5.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|J^{s}\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}L^{2}_{x}}+\|J^{s}\widetilde{v}\|_{L^{4}_{T_{*},x}}+\|\widetilde{w}_{\delta}\|_{Y^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(T_{*})}+\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}\|_{X^{-1,1}(T_{*})}\\
&\quad\leq C(M)\|\widetilde{v}_{\delta}(0)\|_{H^{s}}+C(M)\delta^{-1},\end{split}

uniformly in
\delta_{0}\leq\delta\leq\infty
,
provided that
T_{*}>0
sufficiently small.
(Recall that

T_{*}>0
is independent of
1\leq\delta\leq\infty
.)
Here, the last term on the right-hand side of (
5.8
)
comes from the last term in (
5.6
).
Since the right-hand side of (
5.8
) tends to 0 as
\delta\to\infty
,
we conclude that, as
\delta\to\infty
,
the solution
v_{\delta}
to (
3.2
)
converges to the solution
u=v_{\infty}

to (
1.5
)
in
C([0,T_{*}];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{4}([0,T_{*}];W^{s,4}(\mathbb{T}))
.
Given any
T_{0}\gg 1
,
we can iterate this local-in-time convergence argument
and conclude convergence on the entire interval
[0,T_{0}]
.

Next, we transfer this convergence back to the original ILW
equation (
1.1
).
Let
u_{\delta}
be the global solution to (
1.1
)
with
u_{\delta}|_{t=0}=u_{0,\delta}
.
Then, from (
3.1
), we have

u_{\delta}=\tau_{-\delta^{-1}}v_{\delta}=\tau(-\delta^{-1},v_{\delta}).

Letting
u
be the global solution to the BO equation (
1.5
)
with
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}
as above,
it follows from the triangle inequality that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u-u_{\delta}\|_{C_{T_{0}}H^{s}_{x}}&\leq\|u-\tau(-\delta^{-1},u)\|_{C_{T_{0}}H^{s}_{x}}+\|\tau(-\delta^{-1},u)-u_{\delta}\|_{C_{T_{0}}H^{s}_{x}}\\
&\leq\|u-\tau(-\delta^{-1},u)\|_{C_{T_{0}}H^{s}_{x}}+\|u-v_{\delta}\|_{C_{T_{0}}H^{s}_{x}}.\end{split}

By noting that
u=\tau(0,u)
,
it follows from Lemma
2.5

that the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.9
)
tends to
0
as
\delta\to\infty
,
while the second term tends to
0
as
\delta\to\infty

in view of the discussion above.
This proves
convergence of
u_{\delta}

to
u
in
C([0,T_{0}];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))

for any
T_{0}>0
.

\bullet

Part 2:

In this step, we consider the general case without the mean-zero assumption.
Given
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
,
let
\{u_{0,\delta}\}_{1\leq\delta<\infty}

be a net in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})

such that

u_{0,\delta}
converges to
u_{0}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
as
\delta\to\infty
.
Note that the spatial mean
\mu(u_{0,\delta})
of
u_{0,\delta}

converges to the spatial mean
\mu(u_{0})
of
u_{0}
as
\delta\to\infty
.

Let
u
be the global solution to the BO equation (
1.5
)
with
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}

constructed in
[
44
]
,
and
let
u_{\delta}
,
1\leq\delta<\infty
, be the global solution to (
1.1
) with

u_{\delta}|_{t=0}=u_{0,\delta}

constructed in Theorem
1.1
.
Then, by setting

\widetilde{u}=\Gamma_{u_{0}}(u)
and
\widetilde{u}_{\delta}=\Gamma_{u_{0,\delta}}(u_{\delta})
,
where
\Gamma
is the Galilean transform defined in (
1.7
),
we see that

\widetilde{u}
(and
\widetilde{u}_{\delta}
, respectively) is the global solution to (
1.5
)
with the mean-zero initial data
\widetilde{u}|_{t=0}=\widetilde{u}_{0}=u_{0}-\mu(u_{0})

(and to (
1.1
)
with the mean-zero initial data
\widetilde{u}_{\delta}|_{t=0}=\widetilde{u}_{0,\delta}=u_{0,\delta}-\mu(u_{0,\delta})
,
respectively).
Hence, from Part 1,
we see that, given any
T_{0}>0
,
\widetilde{u}_{\delta}
converges to
\widetilde{u}

in
C([0,T_{0}];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))

as
\delta\to\infty
.

With
\tau_{h}
as in
(
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle u=\tau(2\mu(u_{0}),\widetilde{u})+\mu(u_{0})\qquad\text{and}\qquad u_{\delta}=\tau(2\mu(u_{0,\delta}),\widetilde{u}_{\delta})+\mu(u_{0,\delta}).

Therefore, in view of the convergence of

\mu(u_{0,\delta})
and
\widetilde{u}_{\delta}
to
\mu(u_{0})
and
\widetilde{u}
, respectively,
we conclude from the joint continuity of
\tau

(Lemma
2.5
) that

u_{\delta}
converges to
u

in
C([0,T_{0}];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))

as
\delta\to\infty
.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

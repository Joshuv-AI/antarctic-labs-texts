Perturbative PDF of the total magnetization of the 4D Ising model
Andrea Allais

Abstract
We compute, at one loop in perturbation theory, the probability density function of the total magnetization M M of the Ising model on the 4-torus and the 4-sphere. We develop a single perturbative expansion that is valid in the symmetric phase as well as the broken symmetry phase, provided that the correlation length is large compared to the system size L L . We find that, at the critical point, for large system size in lattice units, the PDF approaches p ⁡ ( M ) ∼ exp ⁡ ( − f ⁡ ( L ) ​ M 4 ) p(M)\sim\exp(-f(L)M^{4}) . Consequently, the critical value of the Binder cumulant of the total magnetization is U = 1 − 4 ​ Γ ​ ( 5 / 4 ) 2 3 ​ Γ ​ ( 3 / 4 ) 2 U=1-\frac{4\,\Gamma(5/4)^{2}}{3\,\Gamma(3/4)^{2}} . We validate our results by comparison with Monte Carlo simulation.



1
Main result

When the system size
L
is large, the probability distribution of the total
magnetization
M
of the Ising model changes qualitatively between the two
phases of the model. In the symmetric phase, if the correlation
length
\xi
is large in lattice units, but small compared to the system size,
the distribution is well approximated by a zero-mean normal. In the broken
symmetry phase, still for
1\ll\xi\ll L
, the distribution is well
approximated by a mixture of two normals centered at non-zero values
\pm M_{0}
.

In the opposite regime,
1\ll L\ll\xi
, in two and three dimensions, the
probability distribution of
M
is a non-trivial function of
M
. It is not
immediately clear if the same is also true in four dimensions. Since the field
theory that describes the Ising critical point in four dimensions becomes
weakly coupled at low energy, one may expect the probability distribution of

M
to remain a zero-centered normal even in this regime. This is in fact the
case for the total magnetization of a subsystem of intermediate size: large in
lattice units, but small compared to
L

Binder1981
. Here we show that
the same is not true for the total magnetization of the entire system. We
compute the logarithm of the PDF of
M
at one-loop in
4
dimensions, and show
that, for
1\ll L\ll\xi
, the distribution is not normal, but rather of the
form
-\log p(M)\sim f(L)M^{4}
. We find that corrections to this form vanish
very slowly with increasing system size, like
1/\log L
.

When the correlation length is large in lattice unit, the Ising model in 4
dimensions is described by the
\phi^{4}
field theory:

S[\phi]\equiv\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\phi\left(\Delta+r_{0}\right)\phi+\frac{1}{24}u_{0}\phi^{4}\right)\,.

(1)

Here we take the manifold
\mathcal{M}
to be a 4-torus of radius
R
,

i.e.

x_{i}\sim x_{i}+2\pi R
. In this field theory context, we define
the PDF of the average magnetization
m=M/V
as:

p(m)\equiv\frac{1}{Z}\int\left[\mathcal{D}\phi\right]\delta\left(m-\frac{1}{V}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\phi\right)e^{-S[\phi]}\,,

(2)

and we evaluate it at one loop in perturbation theory, obtaining:

\begin{split}-\log p(m)=\mathrm{const.}+V\Bigg(&\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(rR^{2}+\frac{g}{6}\left(f_{1}\left(rR^{2}\right)-1\right)+O\left(g^{2}\right)\right)+\\
&\frac{2\pi^{2}}{9}gm^{4}\left(1-\frac{g}{2}f_{2}\left(rR^{2}\right)+O\left(g^{2}\right)\right)+\\
&\frac{8\pi^{4}}{81}g^{3}m^{6}R^{2}\left(f_{3}\left(rR^{2}\right)+O\left(g\right)\right)+O\left(g^{4}m^{8}\right)\Bigg)\,.\end{split}

(3)

Here the coefficients
r
and
g
are the renormalized counterparts to
r_{0}

and
u_{0}
; the functions
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
and
f_{3}
are plotted in
fig.
2
, and an explicit expression (
25
)
is given below.

The dimension-2 coupling
r
controls the cross-over between the symmetric and
broken-symmetry phases. The expression (
3
) is valid for
rR^{2}\gtrsim-1/2
. For lower values of
r
, the perturbative vacuum becomes
unstable, invalidating the perturbative expansion. For this reason, the
functions
f_{i}
diverge as their argument approaches -1. On the other hand,

f_{1}
,
f_{2}
,
f_{3}
all go to zero as their argument approaches positive
infinity, and hence for large
r
the probability distribution of
m
is a
zero-centered normal:

p(m)\sim Ne^{-\frac{1}{2}Vrm^{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad rR^{2}\gg 1\,.

(4)

The dimensionless coupling
g
is the parameter of the perturbative expansion.
The expansion is valid for
g\lesssim 1
, provided
rR^{2}
is sufficiently far
from the bound discussed above.

In order to describe how
p(m)
depends on
R
at fixed bare couplings
r_{0}
,

u_{0}
, it is necessary to account for renormalization effects. The specific
renormalization scheme we used is described in section
2
,
and (
23
) gives expression for
p(m)
evaluated at a
generic renormalization scale
\mu
. However, for simplicity, we chose to
evaluate (
3
) at the scale
\mu^{2}=r+R^{-2}
. This choice of

\mu
is optimal for the reliability of perturbation theory, because it avoids
the emergence of large logarithms over the widest possible range of parameters.

At one loop, the Callan-Symanzik equations for
r
and
g
are:

\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{\mathrm{d}\mu}=g^{2}\,,\quad\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}\mu}=\frac{1}{3}gr\,.

(5)

These can be integrated and combined with the condition
\mu^{2}=r+R^{-2}
to
obtain a system of equations

At the critical point, the renormalized coupling
g
follows the simpler
Callan-Symanzik equation:

\frac{1}{g(R_{2})}-\frac{1}{g(R_{1})}=\log\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}\,,

(7)

and hence, as system size grows, the renormalized coupling
g
goes to zero as

1/\log(R)
. In this regime, the quartic term in (
3

dominates all the others. This is perhaps most evident if the PDF is expressed
in terms of the rescaled quantity
\bar{m}=g^{\frac{1}{4}}m
, whose variance
remains finite as
g\to 0
. Thus we conclude that, at the critical point, for
sufficiently large system size:

-\log p(m)\sim\mathrm{const}+\frac{2\pi^{2}}{9}Vgm^{4}\,.

(8)

In Monte Carlo simulations, the qualitative behavior of the distribution of the
magnetization is often characterized by measuring the so-called Binder
cumulant
Binder1981
:

U=1-\frac{\left\langle m^{4}\right\rangle}{3\left\langle m^{2}\right\rangle^{2}}\,.

(9)

This quantity is constructed to be independent of the overall scale of
m
,
and to be zero if
m
is normally distributed. From
(
8
) we conclude that, on a 4-Torus, at the
critical point:

U=1-\frac{4\,\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{4}\right)^{2}}{3\,\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2}}=0.27052\ldots

(10)

In fig.
1
, we show a comparison of the Binder cumulant
computed from (
3

Monte Carlo simulation of the
4D
Ising model. The agreement is excellent
except for the smallest system size
L=8
. Notice how slowly the finite size
Binder cumulant approaches the asymptotic value
(
10
).

2
Derivation on the 4-torus

We now describe briefly how the result (
3
) is obtained. The
perturbative approach is similar to the computation of the effective
action, as in
e.g.

WeinbergEffectiveAction
, except that we are
interested in the whole probability distribution of
m
, instead of
just the expected value. The main difficulty lies in evaluating the loop
integrals at finite size.

The average magnetization
m
is proportional to the zero-momentum mode of the
field:

\phi(x)=\frac{1}{V}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}}\phi_{n}e^{i\frac{n\cdot x}{R}}\,;\quad m=\frac{1}{V}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\ \phi(x)=\frac{1}{V}\phi_{n=0}\,.

(11)

Because of the delta function in (
2
), the zero-mode
becomes an external field, whereas all other modes are still part of the
functional integral. Separating the zero-mode from the other modes in the
action yields:

\begin{split}S(m,\phi)=&V\left(\frac{1}{2}r_{0}m^{2}+\frac{u_{0}}{24}m^{4}\right)+\frac{1}{2V}\sum_{n}\left(\frac{n^{2}}{R^{2}}+r_{0}+\frac{1}{2}u_{0}m^{2}\right)\phi_{n}\phi_{-n}+\\
&\frac{u_{0}}{6V^{2}}m\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}\phi_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{2}}\phi_{-n_{1}-n_{2}}+\frac{u_{0}}{24V^{3}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}\phi_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{2}}\phi_{n_{3}}\phi_{-n_{1}-n_{2}-n_{3}}\,,\end{split}

(12)

where all summations now are over
\mathbb{Z}^{4}\setminus\{0\}
.

We introduce renormalized couplings

u_{0}=u(1+u\delta u)\,;\quad r_{0}=r(1+u\delta r)\,,

(13)

\begin{split}-\log p(m)=\mathrm{const}+V\Bigg(&\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\left(r+u\left(\delta r+\frac{1}{2}I_{1}\right)+O(u^{2})\right)+\\
&\frac{u}{24}m^{4}\left(1+u\left(\delta u-\frac{3}{2}I_{2}\right)+O(u^{2})\right)+\\
&\frac{u^{3}}{48}m^{6}\left(I_{3}+O(u)\right)+O(u^{4}m^{8})\Bigg)\,,\end{split}

(14)

where

I_{k}=\frac{1}{V}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{e^{-s(n^{2}R^{-2}+r)}}{\left(n^{2}R^{-2}+r\right)^{k}}=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}-s\right)^{k-1}I_{1}

(15)

The summation
I_{1}
can be simplified substantially using the following trick:

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\frac{1}{V}\int_{s}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}\setminus\{0\}}e^{-t(n^{2}R^{-2}+r)}\\
&=\frac{1}{V}\int_{s}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\ e^{-tr}\left(\left(\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-tm^{2}R^{-2}}\right)^{4}-1\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{V}\int_{s}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\ e^{-tr}\left(\theta\!\left(tR^{-2}\right)^{4}-1\right)\,,\end{split}

(16)

where:

\theta(z)=\theta_{3}(0;e^{-z})=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-m^{2}z}\,.

(17)

Here
\theta_{3}
is a Jacobi theta function, but we will not need any of
its special properties beyond its asymptotic behavior, which can be easily
obtained from the definition.

We now extract the divergent and finite parts of
I_{1}
as
s\to 0
by
subtracting under the integral a function that has the same asymptotic behavior
as the integrand for
t\to 0
, but whose integral can be computed in closed
form. In order to do that, we need the asymptotic behavior of
\theta(z)
for

z\to 0
, which can be obtained from its definition using the Euler-Maclaurin
formula:

\theta(z)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{z}}+o(z^{k})\quad\mathrm{for}\,z\to 0\,.

(18)

Thus we have:

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,e^{-t\mu^{2}}t^{-2}\left(1+t(\mu^{2}-r)\right)+\frac{1}{R^{2}}f(rR^{2},\mu R)+O(s)\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{s}+r\left(\log s\mu^{2}+\gamma_{E}\right)-\mu^{2}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}f(rR^{2},\mu R)+O(s)\right)\,,\end{split}

(19)

where
\mu
is a renormalization scale that can be chosen at will, and:

f(\bar{r},\bar{\mu})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\ e^{-z\bar{r}}\left(\frac{\theta(z)^{4}-1}{\pi^{2}}-\frac{1+z\left(\bar{\mu}^{2}-\bar{r}\right)}{z^{2}}e^{-z\left(\bar{\mu}^{2}-\bar{r}\right)}\right).

(20)

We set the counterterms to:

\displaystyle\delta r=-\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{s}+r\left(\log s\mu^{2}+\gamma_{E}-1\right)\right)\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\delta u=-\frac{3}{32\pi^{2}}\left(\log s\mu^{2}+\gamma_{E}+1\right)\,.

(22)

The Callan-Symanzik equations (
5
) follow from this
subtraction choice.

Finally, we obtain:

\begin{split}-\log p(m)=&\mathrm{const}+V\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\left(r+\frac{u}{32\pi^{2}R^{2}}\left(f(rR^{2},\mu R)+(r-\mu^{2})R^{2}\right)+O(u^{2})\right)+\\
&\frac{u}{24}m^{4}\left(1+\frac{3u}{32\pi^{2}}f^{(1,0)}(rR^{2},\mu R)+O(u^{2})\right)+\\
&\frac{u^{3}}{48}m^{6}\left(\frac{R^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}f^{(2,0)}(rR^{2},\mu R)+O(u)\right)+O(u^{4}m^{8})\Bigg)\,,\end{split}

(23)

from which (
3
) is obtained by setting
g=\frac{3u}{16\pi^{2}}
and
\mu^{2}=r+R^{-2}
. This last choice is motivated as follows. The
subtraction in (
20
) is similar to:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{e^{-az}-e^{-bz}}{z}=\log\frac{b}{a}\,.

(24)

When the large-
z
asymptotic behavior of the two terms is not well
matched, the integral becomes large in magnitude, making the perturbative
expansion less reliable. With the choice
\mu^{2}=r+R^{-2}
, both terms in
(
20
) have the same asymptotic behavior
\sim e^{-z(\bar{r}+1)}
, thus avoiding the large log problem over the widest possible range of
parameters.

f_{i}
that
parametrize (
3

\displaystyle f_{1}(\bar{r})=f(\bar{r},\sqrt{\bar{r}+1})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\ e^{-z\bar{r}}\left(\frac{\theta(z)^{4}-1}{\pi^{2}}-\frac{(1+z)e^{-z}}{z^{2}}\right)\,,

(25)

3
Results for the 4-sphere

It is possible to obtain
p(m)
on the 4 sphere as well, with
similar methods. Here we highlight the main differences from the 4 torus.

Curved manifolds allow for an additional renormalizable coupling:

\Delta S=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{1}{2}\xi_{0}\phi^{2}\mathcal{R}\,,

(28)

where
\mathcal{R}
is the scalar curvature and
\xi_{0}
is a dimensionless bare
coupling. The free theory is Weyl invariant if
\xi_{0}=\frac{1}{6}
. In the
presence of interactions, the coupling needs to be renormalized, and its
renormalized counterpart
\xi
becomes a running coupling. At one loop, the
Callan-Symanzik equation for
\xi
is:

\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}\mu}=\frac{1}{3}g\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)\,.

(29)

From this expression it seems that
\xi
can be set to the critical value

\frac{1}{6}
at all energy scales. However, this turns out to be an illusion:
at higher orders in perturbation theory the Callan-Symanzik equation
becomes inhomogeneous
Brown1980
. Therefore, on a curved manifold,
\xi

is simply another free parameter of the scalar field theory.

The probability distribution of the total magnetization on the 4-sphere is
still given by (
3
), with the substitution:

rR^{2}\to\frac{\xi}{12}+rR^{2}\,,

(30)

where
R
is now the radius of the sphere, and with the functions
f_{i}
defined
as:

\displaystyle f_{1}(\bar{r})=(2-\bar{r})\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\ e^{-z(\bar{r}+4)}H(z)+\frac{7}{3}\,,

(31)

\displaystyle f_{2}(\bar{r})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\ e^{-z(\bar{r}+4)}\left(1+z(2-\bar{r})\right)H(z)+\frac{6}{\bar{r}+4}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle f_{3}(\bar{r})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\ e^{-z(\bar{r}+4)}\left(2+z(2-\bar{r})\right)zH(z)+\frac{\bar{r}+10}{(\bar{r}+4)^{2}}\,,

(33)

where:

H(z)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(2\ell+3)e^{-z(\ell(\ell+3)-4)}-\frac{1}{z}\,.

(35)

The functions
f_{i}
for the sphere are also displayed in
fig.
2
. They diverge for
\bar{r}\to-4
, signaling the
instability of the perturbative vacuum, and they go to zero for
\bar{r}\to\infty
.

On the unramified Eisenstein spectrum
David Kazhdan and Andrei Okounkov

Abstract
For a split reductive group 𝖦 L {}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{G} over a global field, we determine the spectrum of the spherical Hecke algebra coming from the unramified Eisenstein series for the minimal parabolic 𝖡 L {}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{B} . This is done using a certain decomposition of the Springer stack T ∗ ​ ( 𝖡 \ 𝖦 / 𝖡 ) T^{*}(\mathsf{B}\backslash\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{B}) for the Langlands dual group 𝖦 \mathsf{G} in the additive group of cobordisms of cohomologically proper derived quotient stacks.

The automorphic form setup

1.1.2

We denote by
\mathbb{F}
,
\mathbb{A}
, and
\mathbb{I}
a global field, its adeles, and
ideles, respectively. We consider a split reductive group over
\mathbb{F}
,
which we denote
{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{G}
because the bulk of our computations will be
with the Langlands dual complex reductive group
\mathsf{G}
. On both sides,
we denote by the letters
\mathsf{A}
,
\mathsf{B}
, and
\mathsf{U}

(resp.
{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{A},{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{B},{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{U}
) a maximal torus, a
Borel subgroup containing it, and its unipotent radical. We have

\mathsf{A}=\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\,,\quad{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{A}=\Lambda^{\vee}\!\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{m}}\,,

1.1.3

Let
\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}
denote the image of
the norm map
\mathbb{I}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. We have

\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}=\begin{cases}q^{\mathbb{Z}}\,,&\mathbb{F}=\Bbbk(\textup{curve})\,,|\Bbbk|=q\,,\\
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}=0\,.\end{cases}

For uniformity in formulas, one should interpret
q>1
as the generator
of this group, including the infinitesimal generator
t\frac{d}{dt}\in\Lie\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, in the number field case.

We have the dual map

\mathbb{C}\cong\Hom(\mathbb{R}_{>0},\mathbb{C}^{\times})\to\Hom(\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}},\mathbb{C}^{\times})\overset{\textup{\tiny def}}{=}\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\vee}\,,

where the first isomorphism sends

to

,
s\in\mathbb{C}
. In other words,

\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\vee}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}\left/\frac{2\pi i}{\ln q}\,\mathbb{Z}\right.\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}>0\,,\\
\mathbb{C}\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}=0\,.\end{cases}

(1)

In both cases we have a well-defined map

\Re:\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\vee}\to\mathbb{R}\,.

1.1.4

The characters of
{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{A}(\mathbb{A})

\textstyle{{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{A}(\mathbb{A})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\|a\|^{\lambda}}
\scriptstyle{\|a\|}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
\textstyle{\Lambda^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Given such

1.1.5

More generally, given a
function

\phi^{\vee}:\Lambda^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}\to\mathbb{C}

with compact support, one may consider the corresponding

The series (

If

1.1.7

Our object of interest in this paper is

\mathscr{H}_{\textup{Eis}}=\overline{\Span\left\{E_{\phi^{\vee}}(g)\right\}}\subset L^{2}\!\left(\,{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{A})\,\big/\,{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{F})\right)\,.

(7)

This is a Hilbert space with an action of a large commutative
*
-algebra
generated by the
spherical Hecke operators at all nonarchimedian places (and also
invariant differential operators at Archimedean places). Since
Eisenstein series are joint eigenfunctions of all these operators, we
may realize a dense (in the sense of having the same commutant)
subalgebra
\mathbf{sH}
of this algebra concretely as

\displaystyle\mathbf{sH}

\displaystyle=\textup{$W$-invariant functions of $\lambda$}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\left[\underline{\mathsf{A}}\,/\,W\right]\,,

(8)

where

\underline{\mathsf{A}}=\Lambda\otimes\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\vee}\cong\begin{cases}\mathsf{A}\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}>0\,,\\
\Lie\mathsf{A}\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}=0\,.\end{cases}

(9)

The problem that we solve in this paper is to determine the spectrum
of
\mathbf{sH}
in
\mathscr{H}_{\textup{Eis}}
.

1.1.8

As envisioned by Langlands,
\mathscr{H}_{\textup{Eis}}
breaks up into a direct sum, the pieces in which are indexed by the conjugacy
classes of nilpotent elements
e\in\mathfrak{g}=\Lie\mathsf{G}
. Here
\mathsf{G}
is the
Langlands dual group.

Recall that there are only finitely many conjugacy classes of
nilpotent elements in
\mathfrak{g}
. Further,
by the Jacobson-Morozov theorem, every
e
has the form

e=\phi\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

(10)

for a homomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\to\mathfrak{g}
. The element

h=\phi\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}\right)\,,

(11)

known as the
characteristic
of
e
, satisfies

[h,e]=2e\,.

Note this includes the case

(e,h)=(0,0)

which corresponds to
\phi=0
.
The nilpotent element
e

and its characteristic
h
determine each other up to the action of
the respective centralizers.

We denote by

\mathsf{C}_{e}\supset\mathsf{C}_{\phi}

the centralizers of
e
and of the pair
\{e,h\}
. The group
\mathsf{C}_{\phi}
is
reductive and is a maximal reductive subgroup of
\mathsf{C}_{e}
. We denote by

\mathsf{C}_{\phi,\textup{cmp}}\subset\mathsf{C}_{\phi}
a maximal compact subgroup. It is
also a maximal compact subgroup of
\mathsf{C}_{e}
.

1.1.9

Given
\phi
, we define

\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{e}=\begin{cases}q^{h/2}\mathsf{C}_{\phi,\textup{cmp}}\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}>0\,,\\
\frac{1}{2}h+\Lie\mathsf{C}_{\phi,\textup{cmp}}\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}=0\,.\end{cases}

(12)

These subsets of
\mathsf{G}
, resp.
\Lie\mathsf{G}
, are determined by
e

up-to conjugation.
Since

\underline{\mathsf{A}}\,/\,W=\textup{conjugacy classes of semisimple elements in}\begin{cases}\mathsf{G}\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}>0\,,\\
\Lie\mathsf{G}\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}=0\,,\end{cases}

we have a well-defined real-algebraic subvariety

\mathbf{S}_{e}=\textup{Image}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{e}\to\underline{\mathsf{A}}\,/\,W\right)\,.

(13)

We equip
\mathbf{S}_{e}
with the push-forward of the Haar measure

d_{\textup{Haar}}
from
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{e}
, which we denote by the same symbol.

1.1.10

The following is our main result. The represents the next step in a very long development of the
subject, see in particular
[

]
for a discussion of

Theorem 2
.

We have the decomposition

\mathscr{H}_{\textup{Eis}}=\bigoplus_{e}L^{2}\left(\mathbf{S}_{e},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{e}\right)\,,

(14)

consistent with the inclusion
\mathsf{S}_{e}\subset\spec\mathbf{sH}
,
where
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{e}
is a measure with a density with

.

We also give an explicit formula for spectral projectors and for the
measure
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{e}
, see Theorem
5
. The proof of Theorem
2
is
completed in Section
4.4
.

1.2
Large-scale structure of the argument

1.2.1

In his fundamental work
[
L
]
, R. Langlands introduced a contour
shift and the analysis of residues as a way to describe the discrete
spectrum of
\mathscr{H}_{\textup{Eis}}
as an
\mathbf{sH}
-module. In
[
L
]
, he
computed the discrete spectrum for groups for groups of rank 2.
In the work
[
Moe1
,
Moe2
]
of C. Mœglin and J.-L. Waldspurger this approach
was used to describe the discrete spectrum of
\mathscr{H}_{\textup{Eis}}
for all
classical group. In recent work of Volker Heiermann, Marcelo de Martino and Eric Opdam
[
13
]

this approach was extended to general split groups, in the number field case (computer
aided in the exceptional cases). The most recent progress will be
reflected in a new version of
[
13
]
, currently in preparation.

In this paper, we do something rather different, and our starting point
is the following geometric interpretation of the pairing
(
6
). In an equivalent and slightly more convenient
language, we will start with a geometric interpretation of
the linear functional, or distribution,
\Psi_{\textup{Eis}}

defined by

(f_{1},f_{2})_{\textup{Eis}}=(f_{1}\boxtimes f_{2}^{*},\Psi_{\textup{Eis}})\,.

(15)

Here

f^{*}(\lambda)=\overline{f}(-\lambda)\,,\quad\lambda\in\underline{\mathsf{A}}\,,

where minus refers to the group structure of
\underline{\mathsf{A}}
. See Section

2.1.6
for a discussion of what we mean by distributions in
this paper.

1.2.2

Recall the group
\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\vee}
defined in (
1
)
and note this is a 1-dimensional connected affine
algebraic group over

\mathbb{C}
. To such an object one can associate an equivariant complex-oriented
cohomology theory. This is contravariant
a functor that we denote by

\begin{matrix}\textup{topological spaces}\\
\textup{with an action of}\\
\textup{a compact group}\end{matrix}\quad\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt{H}^{\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}_{\textup{group}}\left(\textup{space}\big|\,\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\vee}\right)\hskip 8.19447pt}\quad\begin{matrix}\textup{supercommutative}\\
\textup{algebras over $\mathbb{C}$}\end{matrix}\quad.

(16)

Concretely, this is ordinary equivariant cohomology if
\chr\mathbb{F}=0
and

topological K-theory if

In particular, we have

\underline{\mathsf{A}}=\Spec{H}^{\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}_{\mathsf{A}_{\textup{cmp}}}\left(\pt\big|\,\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\vee}\right)\,,

and

1.2.3

While there is a fundamental unity between equivariant K-theory and
equivariant cohomology, there are also differences in details.
To avoid considering cases all the time and to
streamline the exposition, we will focus on

Equivariant cohomology we treat as the

In practice, this amounts to the following

(2)

by

(3)

define

and

see Table

1.2.5

As we recall in Section
2
below,
genera are usually considered for either smooth compact stably complex
manifolds or smooth proper algebraic varieties and involve the choice of an
arbitrary function
\psi(x)\in\mathbb{C}\left[\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\vee}\right]

\psi(\textup{origin})=1\,.

(19)

If we think of argument

as the
equivariant Chern class of a line bundle, then the origin corresponds

For manifolds with an

\left\{\begin{matrix}\textup{$\mathsf{A}$-equivariant}\\

Here

1.2.7

The starting point of our analysis is the following reformulation of
(
6
).

Theorem 3
.

The stack
\mathscr{T}
is cohomologically proper and

\Psi_{\textup{Eis}}=\psi_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathscr{T})\,,\quad\mathscr{T}=T^{*}(\mathsf{B}\backslash\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{B})\,,

(22)

where the function
\psi_{\mathbb{F}}(x)
satisfies the equation

\xi_{\mathbb{F}}(s)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\psi_{\mathbb{F}}(q^{-s})\psi_{\mathbb{F}}(q^{-1+s})}{(1-q^{s})(1-q^{1-s})}\,,&\mathscr{Q}_{\mathbb{F}}=q^{\mathbb{Z}}\,,\\
\dfrac{\psi_{\mathbb{F}}(-s)\psi_{\mathbb{F}}(-1+s)}{s(1-s)}\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}=0\,.\end{cases}

(23)

Note that since the zeros and poles of
\xi_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
are symmetric with
respect to
s\mapsto 1-s
, a function
\psi_{\mathbb{F}}(x)
solving these
equations can always be found by the Weierstraß factorization
theorem.

For instance, in the function field case, we have

\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(x)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{g}(1-\alpha_{i}/x)(1-\alpha_{i}^{-1}q/x)}{(1-1/x)(1-q/x)}\,,

(24)

where we use the variable
x=q^{s}
instead of the more conventional

t=q^{-s}
, and where
\{\alpha_{i}\}
are half of the Frobenius
eigenvalues,
|\alpha_{i}|=q^{1/2}
. Thus

\xi_{\mathbb{F}}(x)=x^{g-1}\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{g-1}}{\prod\alpha_{i}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{g}(\alpha_{i}-x)}{(1-x)}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{g}(\alpha_{i}-q/x)}{(1-q/x)}

(25)

gives the desired factorization (
26
), in which we can
choose either square root of the prefactor.

1.2.8

In general, the function

Z_{\psi}(x)=\Psi(x^{-1})\Psi(x/q)\,,\quad\Psi(x)=\frac{\psi(x)}{1-x^{-1}}\,.

Theorem

1.2.9

The next key point is that the spectral decomposition for
Eisenstein series corresponds to a certain decomposition of
\mathscr{T}

in the additive group of equivariant cohomologically proper
bordism classes.

In equivariant cohomologically proper situation, there are certain
convenient scissor relations, which look as follows.

1.2.10

To fix ideas, let

f:\mathsf{Y}\to\mathsf{X}

be an equivariant
embedding of a complex subvariety into a complex manifold. We now
stress that, while
\mathsf{X}\setminus\mathsf{Y}
is not proper, it may very well be cohomologically
proper and so have a well-defined bordism class
[\mathsf{X}\setminus\mathsf{Y}]
. We then define

\Thom(\mathsf{Y}\xrightarrow{\,\,f\,\,}\mathsf{X})=[\mathsf{X}]-[\mathsf{X}\setminus\mathsf{Y}]\,,

which we will usually abbreviate to just
\Thom(\mathsf{Y})
, the rest of the
data being understood. Clearly, it depends only on the neighborhood of

\mathsf{Y}
in
\mathsf{X}
, and only on the normal bundle if
\mathsf{Y}
is smooth.

Concretely, by the long exact sequence for local cohomology, we have

\displaystyle\psi(\Thom(\mathsf{Y}))

\displaystyle=\sum(-1)^{i}H^{i}_{\mathsf{Y}}(\psi(TX))\,,

(27)

in equivariant K-theory,
were
H^{i}_{\mathsf{Y}}
denotes cohomology with support in
\mathsf{Y}
.

1.2.11

We denote by
\mathscr{B}=\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{B}
the flag variety of
\mathsf{G}
.
It is easy to see from the definitions that, set-theoretically
(in the sense of set-theoretic support of the structure sheaf)

\mathscr{T}=\bigsqcup_{e}\left(\mathscr{B}^{e}\times\mathscr{B}^{e}\right)\big/\mathsf{C}_{e}\,,

(28)

where the sum is over the conjugacy classes of nilpotent
elements
e\in\mathfrak{g}=\Lie\mathsf{G}
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}^{e}

\displaystyle=\textup{the Springer fiber of $e$}

\displaystyle=\textup{fixed locus of $e$ acting on $\mathscr{B}$}\,,

and
\mathsf{C}_{e}
is the
centralizer of
e
. For instance, the piece corresponding to
the regular nilpotent
e
is open in
\mathscr{T}
, while the piece
corresponding to
e=0
is zero section

\mathsf{B}\setminus\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{B}\subset\mathscr{T}\,.

Needless to say,
Springer fibers are objects of paramount importance in geometric
representation theory, and it is not surprising that they also play
a key role in our work.

1.3
Future directions

1.3.1

Since the focus of the current section is on the automorphic side, we
will make the
\mathsf{G}\leftrightarrow{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{G}
switch to shorten the
notation. The general Langlands spectral decomposition of

\mathscr{H}=L^{2}\!\left(\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{A})\,\big/\,\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{F})\right)

is organized according to the data of a parabolic subgroup

\mathsf{G}\supset\mathsf{P}=\underbrace{\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{P}}}_{\textup{Levi}}\ltimes\underbrace{\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}}}_{\textup{unipotent}}

and
a cuspidal automorphic form

\boldsymbol{\Phi}:\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{A})\,\big/\,\mathsf{P}(\mathbb{F})\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}}(\mathbb{A})\to\mathbb{C}\,,

see
[

L
]
. This means that
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(gm)

is a cusp form on

for any

and, in
particular, an eigenfunction of the center of
\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{P}}
. We denote
by
\boldsymbol{\pi}
the representation of
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{A})
in the span of the
translates of
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
.

1.3.2

To construct an Eisenstein series from
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
, we consider the torus

\mathsf{A}_{\mathsf{P}}=\mathsf{P}/[\mathsf{P},\mathsf{P}]

and the natural maps

\displaystyle a_{\mathsf{P}}:

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}

\displaystyle\to\mathsf{A}_{\mathsf{P}}

(39)

\displaystyle\|\,\cdot\,\|:

\displaystyle\quad\mathsf{A}_{\mathsf{P}}(\mathbb{A})

\displaystyle\to\Lambda^{\vee}_{\mathsf{P}}\otimes\mathscr{Q}^{\vee}\,.

(40)

Here
\Lambda^{\vee}_{\mathsf{P}}
is the cocharacter lattice of
\mathsf{A}_{\mathsf{P}}
.
Note that we didn’t make the

\Lambda\leftrightarrow\Lambda^{\vee}
notational switch.

The function
\|a_{\mathsf{P}}(\,\cdot\,)\|
is invariant under

\mathsf{K}\cap\mathsf{P}
, and hence extends to a unique
function on
\mathsf{G}
via
the
\mathsf{G}=\mathsf{K}\mathsf{P}
decomposition. We denote this function
of
\mathsf{G}
by the same symbol. The definition
(
3
) generalizes as follows:

E(\boldsymbol{\Phi},\lambda,g)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{F})/\mathsf{P}(\mathbb{F})}\|a_{\mathsf{P}}(g\gamma)\|^{\lambda+\rho_{\mathsf{P}}}\,\boldsymbol{\Phi}(g\gamma)\,,

(41)

where

and

from the scaling of the Haar measure on
\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}}
.

As in
(

produces series of the form

E(\boldsymbol{\Phi},\lambda,g)=E(\mathscr{R}_{w}(\lambda)\boldsymbol{\Phi},w\cdot\lambda,g)\,,\quad w\in W\,,

(43)

for the series (

). Recall that the ratios of the

\xi
-function in (

1.3.4

In full generality, our knowledge of the singularities of the
intertwining operators
\mathscr{R}_{w}(\lambda)
is incomplete. However,
we can hypothesize that, in all situations, there is the following

3
.

General Langlands’ philosophy suggest that there is a certain

(44)

where integral denotes pushforward in the corresponding cohomology

\displaystyle\mathsf{X}

\displaystyle=T^{*}({}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{B}_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}\backslash{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{G}_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}/{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{B}_{\boldsymbol{\pi}})\,,

\displaystyle{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{G}_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}

\displaystyle=\textup{centralizer of ${}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{H}_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}$ in
${}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{G}$}\,,

(45)

\displaystyle{}^{\raisebox{2.0pt}{{\tiny{L}}}}\mathsf{B}_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}

In

Integrals and residues from K-theory point of view

2.1
Equivariant pushforwards as distributions

2.1.1

Let an algebraic group
\mathsf{G}
, not necessarily
connected or reductive, act on an algebraic variety
\mathsf{X}
and let
\mathscr{F}
be a

\mathsf{G}
-equivariant coherent sheaf on
\mathsf{X}
. For our goals in this paper,
it is enough to consider this situation over the field
\mathbb{C}
of
complex numbers. We will further assume that
\mathsf{X}

is quasiprojective and the
\mathsf{G}
-action on
X
is linearized, that is,
we will assume an existence of an equivariant embedding

\iota_{\mathsf{X}}:\,\mathsf{X}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(V)\,,

(46)

where
V
is a linear representation of
\mathsf{G}
. Below, we will meet
generalizations in which
\mathsf{X}
can be a scheme, a
derived scheme, or a stack. These are technical, but convenient
if one wants to broaden the applicability of the geometric tools reviewed
in this section.

2.1.2

Our main interest will be in the situation when
\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F})
, while
possibly
infinite-dimensional, has finite multiplicities of irreducible

\mathsf{G}
-modules. This is conveniently formalized by the concept of
cohomologically proper actions, see
[
12
]
and
Appendix
A.2
.

In what follows we assume that the action of
\mathsf{G}
on
\mathsf{X}
is
cohomologically proper.

2.1.3

For
V\in K_{\mathsf{G}}(\pt)
we define

(V,\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F}))=\chi(\mathsf{G},V\otimes\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F}))\,,

(47)

see Appendix
A.1
for our notational conventions related to
group cohomology.

Since we are in characteristic zero, we have the analog
of the Levi-Malcev decomposition

\mathsf{G}=\mathsf{G}_{\textup{red}}\ltimes\mathsf{G}_{\textup{uni}}\,,

(48)

due to G. Mostow
[
Mostow
]
. The irreducibles for
\mathsf{G}
are the
same as irreducibles for
\mathsf{G}_{\textup{red}}
. We will view

\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{G}_{\textup{red}}]^{\mathsf{G}_{\textup{red}}}=K_{\mathsf{G}}(\pt)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}

as our supply of
test functions
and will realize
\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F})

as a conjugation-invariant distributions on

\mathsf{G}_{\textup{red}}
via the pairing (
47
).

The tools of equivariant K-theory impose many
geometric relations on the totality of pushforwards
\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F})
.
These can be thus translated to relations among distributions.

2.1.4

More generally, we may have an action of a larger group

{\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}=\mathsf{G}\times\mathsf{Aut}

(49)

on
\mathsf{X}
, in which case the Euler characteristic (
47
)
takes values in
K_{\mathsf{Aut}}(\pt)
, that is, in
finite-dimensional virtual representations of
\mathsf{Aut}
.

An example of such group
\mathsf{Aut}
relevant to us
is the scaling action of
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}

that appears everywhere in this paper. Since all constructions
in this paper will be equivariant with respect to this scaling action,
such extra equivariance will always be understood.

2.1.5

In the discussion of cohomological properness, it is important to pay
attention to both the space and the group. In the current discussion,

\mathsf{X}/\mathsf{G}
is assumed to be cohomologically proper and
distributions (
47
) take values in functions on
\Aut
, or in numbers
after evaluation at
q\in\Aut
. This is the
setting in Theorem
3
with
\Aut=\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{q}
.

A weaker hypothesis is the cohomological properness of
\mathsf{X}/{\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}
.
In this case,
\chi(\mathsf{G},V\otimes\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F}))

is typically an infinite-dimensional
\Aut
-module. Its trace may converge
for certain
q\in\Aut
, but, in general, it should be interpreted as a
distribution on
\Aut
. This is the setting in Theorem
4
, where
the assumption
|q|>1
is important for the convergence of the trace
in (
36
). Evaluated at
q
in the region of convergence,
(
47
) defines a
\mathbb{C}
-valued distribution on
\mathsf{G}_{\textup{red}}
.

2.1.6

More precisely, the word

distribution

means the following in this paper.
For basic reasons of finite generation, the function

\mu\mapsto\tr q\Big|_{\Hom(V^{\mu},\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F}))}\,,\quad q\in\mathsf{Aut}\,,

where
V^{\mu}
is an irreducible representation with highest weight

\mu
, grows at most exponentially, and the exponential growth
is the typical situation.

Therefore, all test functions in this paper will be functions on

\mathsf{G}_{\textup{red}}
analytic in a certain sufficiently large (depending on the
geometric problem and on the norm of

q\in\mathsf{Aut}
) neighborhood of a maximal compact subgroup.
Similarly, for a Lie algebra we will consider functions analytic in
a neighborhood of the Lie algebra of the compact form. Analyticity
of the test functions means that the usual rules of operating
with contour integrals apply to distributions given by such integrals
with an analytic density.

Support of distributions will be also interpreted in the sense of
analytic functions. Namely, the support of a distribution
\chi
is the
reduced subvariety defined by largest ideal annihilated by
\chi
.

2.1.7

We observe that

K_{\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}(\mathsf{X})\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\chi\hskip 8.19447pt}\Hom(K_{\mathsf{G}}(\pt),K_{\mathsf{Aut}}(\pt))

(50)

is a map of
K_{\mathsf{G}}(\pt)
-modules. Whence the following

Lemma 2.1
.

\supp\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F})\subset\{g\in\mathsf{G}_{\textup{red}},\left(\supp\mathscr{F}\right)^{g}\neq\varnothing\}\,.

(51)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathsf{X}
is the reduced
support of
\mathscr{F}
. By localization in equivariant K-theory, the module
K_{\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}(\mathsf{X})

is supported over the points
g\in\Spec K_{\mathsf{G}}(\pt)
that have a fixed
point on
\mathsf{X}
, which proves the lemma.
∎

2.1.8

In this paper, we compute Euler characteristics. Therefore, without
loss of generality, we can replace
K_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{X})
, defined algebraically
or topologically, with its
numerical version
, where one mods
out by
\mathscr{F}
such that

\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{V})=0

for all equivariant vector bundles
\mathscr{V}
on
\mathsf{X}
. Even in the algebraic
K-theory situation, this quotient is often finitely generated over

K_{\mathsf{G}}(\pt)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\mathsf{X}
is cohomologically proper
and its numerical K-theory is finitely generated over
K_{\mathsf{G}}(\pt)
then the distributions

\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F})
span a finitely generated module over functions on

\mathsf{G}_{\textup{red}}
.

For concrete
\mathsf{X}
of importance to us in this paper, the
algebraic, topological, and numerical 0th K-groups all coincide and
are finitely generated over
K_{\mathsf{G}}(\pt)
.

2.2
Characteristic classes

2.2.1

If
\mathscr{V}
is a locally free sheaf on
\mathsf{X}

\mathscr{F}
in (

More precisely, given a univariate Laurent polynomial

\psi(x)\in R[x,x^{-1}]

\psi(\mathscr{V})=\prod\psi(x_{i})\in K_{\mathsf{G}}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R\,,

\mathscr{V}

2.2.2

In particular, if
\mathsf{X}
is smooth, we can take
\mathscr{V}=T\mathsf{X}

\psi_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{X})=\chi(\mathsf{X},\psi(T\mathsf{X}))\in\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(K_{\mathsf{G}}(\pt),R)\,.

2.2.3

For
\mathsf{X}

\psi_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{X})=\int_{\mathsf{X}}\prod\frac{1-e^{-\xi_{i}}}{\xi_{i}}\,\psi(e^{\xi_{i}})\,,

\{\xi_{i}\}
are the cohomological Chern roots of

.
From this it is clear that the totality of
\psi_{\mathsf{G}}(\,\cdot\,)

\mathsf{X}
.

Example

2.3

2.3.1

Another natural way to weaken the smoothness requirement

Proposition 2.3
.

For any

\displaystyle=\chi(\mathsf{G},\chi(\mathsf{H},\pi_{\mathsf{H}}^{*}(V)\otimes\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F})))

(63)

\displaystyle=\chi(\widetilde{\mathsf{G}},\pi_{\mathsf{H}}^{*}(V)\otimes\chi(\mathsf{X},\mathscr{F}))\,,

(64)

where (
63

over invariants and (
64
) uses (
187

∎

2.4
Localization

2.4.1

We begin by recalling how equivariant localization is typically used
for computation of Euler characteristics.

Suppose
\mathsf{X}
is smooth and let
g\in\mathsf{G}
be a semisimple element. Recall that
the smoothness of
\mathsf{X}
implies the smoothness of
\mathsf{X}^{g}
. We decompose

T\mathsf{X}\big|_{\mathsf{X}^{g}}=T\mathsf{X}^{g}+N_{\mathsf{X}/\mathsf{X}^{g}}\in K(\mathsf{X}^{g})\,,

(74)

where

N_{\mathsf{X}/\mathsf{X}^{g}}
the normal bundle to the fixed locus. Localization in equivariant K-theory implies the following

Proposition 2.6
.

If
\mathsf{X}^{g}
is proper and
\Psi(x)
is analytic at the weights
of
g
in the normal bundle
N_{\mathsf{X}/\mathsf{X}^{g}}
then
\chi(\mathsf{X},\psi(T\mathsf{X}))
is analytic at
g
and

\chi(\mathsf{X},\psi(T\mathsf{X}))(g)=\chi(\mathsf{X}^{g},\psi(T\mathsf{X}^{g})\otimes\Psi(N_{\mathsf{X}/\mathsf{X}^{g}}))\,.

(75)

2.4.2

The decomposition (
74
) also
makes sense also for a virtual bundle
T\mathsf{X}
. If

N_{\mathsf{X}/\mathsf{X}^{g}}=V_{+}-V_{-}\,,

where
V_{i}
are vector bundles on
\mathsf{X}^{g}
, then we need to require
that
\Psi(x)^{\pm 1}
is regular at the weights of
V_{\pm}
.

2.4.3

A fundamental property of localization formulas like (
75
) is
that they are regular as functions of
g
if
\mathsf{X}
itself is proper.
Note that terms in corresponding to connected components of
\mathsf{X}^{g}
have
poles in
g
, due to the pole of
\Psi(x)
at
x=1
. However, these
poles cancel when we take all components of
\mathsf{X}^{g}
into account.

2.4.4

If
\psi(x)
is meromorphic and
\mathsf{X}^{g}
is proper for some
g
in the identity
component of
\mathsf{G}
then (
75
) has a meromorphic extension to

\mathsf{G}
and

\psi_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{X})\equiv\chi(\mathsf{X}^{g},\psi(T\mathsf{X}^{g})\otimes\Psi(N_{\mathsf{X}/\mathsf{X}^{g}}))\,\delta_{\gamma}\!\mod\textup{torsion}\,,

(76)

where

\bullet

torsion refers to the torsion submodule for the action of

K_{\mathsf{G}}(\pt)
,

\bullet

the integration cycle
\gamma
is a deformation of

\delta_{\mathsf{G}_{\textup{cmp}}}

that avoids the poles of the
integrand.

Here
\mathsf{G}_{\textup{cmp}}\subset\mathsf{G}
is a maximal compact
subgroup.
Note that all choices of
\gamma
are equivalent modulo
torsion.

Dropping the properness assumption on
\mathsf{X}^{g}
in (
76

specifying the torsion components of
\psi_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{X})
requires a
certain care, in particular, attention to the
sign of the weights in the normal bundle to

\mathsf{X}^{g}
.

This leads to the analysis of the attracting and
repelling directions for the action of
g
in
N_{\mathsf{X}/\mathsf{X}^{g}}

in Sections
2.5
and
2.6
below, respectively.

2.5

2.5.1

Consider the following model situation. Let

\sigma:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to\mathsf{G}

(77)

be a 1-parameter subgroup such that there exists an action-preserving
fibration

\pi:(\mathsf{G},\mathsf{X})\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt(\pi_{\mathsf{G}},\pi_{\mathsf{X}})\hskip 8.19447pt}(\mathsf{G}^{\sigma},\mathsf{X}^{\sigma})

(78)

with

\pi=\lim_{z\to 0}\,(\textup{conjugation by $\sigma(z)$},\sigma(z))\,.

Note this implies that
\ker\pi_{\mathsf{G}}
lies in the unipotent radical of

\mathsf{G}
and that
\pi_{\mathsf{X}}
is a fibration in affine spaces if
\mathsf{X}
is
smooth.

Also note that since the semisimple conjugacy classes are closed,
every semisimple conjugacy class of
\mathsf{G}
intersects

\mathsf{G}^{\sigma}
. Put it differently,
\mathsf{G}^{\sigma}
contains a maximal
torus of
\mathsf{G}
. Therefore, the distributions of interest to us on
\mathsf{G}
are
induced from

2.7.1

A.1
Group cohomology

A.1.1

The cohomology of a complex algebraic group
\mathsf{G}
with
coefficients in a representation
V
is defined by

H^{i}(\mathsf{G},V)=\Ext^{i}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathbb{C},V)\,,

where
\mathbb{C}
is trivial representation and Ext’s are computed in the
category of algebraic
\mathsf{G}
-modules. These are the derived functors of
\mathsf{G}
-invariants

H^{0}(\mathsf{G},V)=V^{\mathsf{G}}\,.

They may be computed using the usual
cochain complex, see e.g.
[
FF
]
, in which the terms are given by
morphisms
\mathsf{G}^{\times n}\to V
of algebraic varieties.

A.1.2

We define

\chi(\mathsf{G},V)=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}H^{i}(\mathsf{G},V)\,.

(186)

This is linear in the
K_{\mathsf{G}}(\pt)
-class of
V
by the long exact
sequence of cohomology.

A.1.3

For a group extension

1\to\mathsf{G}_{1}\to\mathsf{G}\to\mathsf{G}_{2}\to 1\,,

we have

\chi(\mathsf{G},V)=\chi(\mathsf{G}_{2},\chi(\mathsf{G}_{1},V))

(187)

by the spectral sequence for derived functors of

V^{\mathsf{G}}=\left(V^{\mathsf{G}_{1}}\right)^{\mathsf{G}_{2}}\,.

Formula (
187
) reduces the computation of
\chi(\mathsf{G},V)
to that for
the unipotent radical and the reductive quotient in (
48
).

A.1.4

We have (essentially by definition)

\textup{$\mathsf{G}$ is reductive}\,\Leftrightarrow H^{>0}(\mathsf{G},\,\cdot\,)=0\,,

and if
\mathsf{U}
is a unipotent group then

{H}^{\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}(\mathsf{U},V)={H}^{\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}(\mathfrak{u},V)\,,\quad\mathfrak{u}=\Lie\mathsf{U}\,,

(188)

as a consequence of the Hochschild-Mostow spectral sequence, see

[
FF
]
, Section 1.§3.

A.1.5

We recall that Lie algebra cohomology in (
188
) is succinctly
computed by the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex

{H}^{\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}(\mathfrak{u},V)={H}^{\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}\left(V\to V\otimes\mathfrak{u}^{\vee}\to V\otimes\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{u}^{\vee}\to\dots\right)\,.

(189)

A.2
Cohomologically proper actions

A.2.1

Consider an action-preserving map

(\mathsf{G}_{1},\mathsf{X}_{1})\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt(f_{\mathsf{G}},f_{\mathsf{X}})\hskip 8.19447pt}(\mathsf{G}_{2},\mathsf{X}_{2})

(190)

with
f_{\mathsf{G}}
surjective. It is cohomologically proper if

{\mathsf{R}}^{\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}(f_{\mathsf{G}},f_{\mathsf{X}})_{*}\mathscr{F}

is a bounded complex of coherent sheaves for any
\mathscr{F}\in\Coh_{\mathsf{G}_{1}}(\mathsf{X}_{1})
.
Here

R^{0}(f_{\mathsf{G}},f_{\mathsf{X}})_{*}\mathscr{F}=\left(f_{\mathsf{X},*}\mathscr{F}\right)^{\ker f_{\mathsf{G}}}\,.

Obviously, cohomological proper maps are closed under composition and
a proper map is cohomologically proper.

A.2.2

By definition, an action of an algebraic group
\mathsf{G}
on a scheme
\mathsf{X}

is cohomologically proper if the canonical map

(\mathsf{G},\mathsf{X})\to(\{1\},\pt)

is cohomologically proper. In this case, we also say that the
quotient stack
\mathsf{X}/\mathsf{G}
is cohomologically proper.

A.2.3

A
nontrivial example of a cohomologically proper map is given by the
following

Proposition A.1
.

If the map
f_{\mathsf{X}}
in (
190
) is affine,
\ker f_{\mathsf{G}}
is reductive, and

(f_{\mathsf{X},*}\mathscr{O}_{\mathsf{X}_{1}})^{\ker f_{\mathsf{G}}}=\mathscr{O}_{\mathsf{X}_{2}}

then (
190
) is cohomologically proper

Proof.

It suffices to prove that for a finitely generated algebra
\mathscr{A}
with
an action of a reductive group
\mathsf{G}

\dim\mathscr{A}^{\mathsf{G}}<\infty\Leftrightarrow\forall V\,,\dim(\mathscr{A}\otimes V)^{\mathsf{G}}<\infty

where
V
ranges over finite-dimensional
\mathsf{G}
-modules.

Highest weights vectors of irreducible
\mathsf{G}
-submodules in
\mathscr{A}

span the graded algebra

\mathscr{A}^{\mathsf{U}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\,\textup{dominant weights}}\mathscr{A}^{\mathsf{U}}_{\lambda}\,,

(191)

where
\mathsf{U}\subset\mathsf{G}
is a
maximal unipotent subgroup. This algebra is finitely generated by
Theorem 3.13 in
[
VinPop
]
. Therefore

\dim\mathscr{A}^{\mathsf{U}}_{0}<\infty\Leftrightarrow\forall\lambda\,,\dim\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}^{\mathsf{U}}<\infty\,,

as was to be shown.
∎

A.2.4

Proposition A.2
.

The conclusion of Proposition
A.1
also holds
for
\ker f_{\mathsf{G}}=\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{a}}
.

Proof.

Suffices to prove that

\dim H^{1}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{a}},\mathscr{A})\leq\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{a}},\mathscr{A})\,,

for any algebraic module
\mathscr{A}
, that is, any module of the form

\mathscr{A}=\bigcup\mathscr{A}_{n}\,,\quad\dim\mathscr{A}_{n}<\infty\,.

Here
\mathscr{A}_{n}
is a finite-dimensional vector space with a nilpotent
operator, for which
H^{0}
and
H^{1}
are the kernel and the cokernel,
respectively. Therefore,

\dim H^{1}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{a}},\mathscr{A})\leq\lim\dim H^{1}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{a}},\mathscr{A}_{n})=\lim\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{a}},\mathscr{A}_{n})=\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{a}},\mathscr{A})\,.

∎

A.3
Flag varieties and Springer fibers

A.3.1

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a complex semisimple Lie group with
\Lie(\mathsf{G})=\mathfrak{g}
. Choose a
Borel subgroup
\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{G}
. We have

\mathsf{B}=\mathsf{A}\ltimes\mathsf{U}

where
\mathsf{U}
is the unipotent radical and
\mathsf{A}
a maximal torus. We denote

\mathfrak{b}=\Lie(\mathsf{B})\,,\quad\mathfrak{n}=\Lie\mathsf{U}\,.

We denote by
r
the rank of
\mathfrak{g}
.

A.3.2

The flag variety

\mathscr{B}\overset{\textup{\tiny def}}{=}\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{B}=\{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{g}\}=\{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{g}\}

may be seen as parametrizing Borel subalgebras
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, or the corresponding nilpotent subalgebras
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}=[\mathfrak{b}^{\prime},\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}]
,
with the adjoint action of
\mathsf{G}
. Further,

T^{*}\mathscr{B}=\{(e,\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}),e\in\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\}\,.

(193)

Here
e\in\mathfrak{g}
is a nilpotent element. The map

\mu:T^{*}\mathscr{B}\owns(e,\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})\mapsto e\in\mathfrak{g}

(194)

becomes the moment map upon the identification

\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}
given by an invariant bilinear form. We
will always treat this map
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{q}
-equivariantly,
where
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{q}
acts by
e\mapsto q^{-1}e
.

A.3.3

For any semisimple
h\in\mathfrak{g}
we have

\mathscr{B}^{h}=\bigsqcup_{w\in W^{h}\backslash W}\mathsf{G}^{h}\,w\,\mathfrak{n}\,,

(195)

where
w
ranges over coset representatives of minimal length, that
is, such that

w^{-1}\mathsf{B}^{h}w\subset\mathsf{B}\,.

Each component in (
195
) parametrizes
h
-stable maximal
nilpotent subalgebras
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
with the same dimensions of the graded
components as
w\cdot\mathfrak{n}
.

A.3.4

If
e\in\mathfrak{g}
is a nilpotent element then

\mathscr{B}^{e}=\mu^{-1}(e)

(196)

is the Springer fiber of
e
. Being the zero locus of a vector field,
it has a natural derived scheme structure with the
virtual tangent bundle
(1-q^{-1})T\mathscr{B}
.

A.4
Normalization of measures

A.4.1

While the spectral decomposition is obviously insensitive to the
precise normalization of the measures, it is worth mentioning
briefly the different choices that contribute to the normalization in
Langlands’ formula (
5
).

A.4.2

The Haar measure on
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{A})
is the pushforward
of the measure

d_{\textup{Haar}}(k)\times|a|^{-2\rho}d_{\textup{Haar}}(a)\times d_{\textup{Haar}}(u)

(197)

under the Iwasawa factorization map

\mathsf{K}\times\mathsf{A}\times\mathsf{U}\to\mathsf{G}\,.

There is a canonical way to normalize
the first and the last factor in (
197
) by

\vol(\mathsf{K})=\vol(\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{A})/\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{F}))=1\,.

(198)

Instead of the Iwasawa factorization, one can fix a measure
on the Borel subgroup
\mathsf{B}=\mathsf{A}\mathsf{U}
and normalize the volume
of the flag manifold

\vol(\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{A})/\mathsf{B}(\mathbb{A}))=1
. This is a more natural and a more
uniform normalization when working with Eisenstein series and
constant terms.

A.4.3

Because the group
\mathsf{U}
is unipotent, the measure
d_{\textup{Haar}}(u)

normalized as in (
198
) equals the
the Tamagawa measure

d_{\textup{Haar}}(u)=d_{\textup{\raisebox{-2.84526pt}{\includegraphics[height]{Tama.png}}}}(u)\,.

Recall that e.g. for the additive
group of a number
field we have

\mathbb{A}/\mathbb{F}=\prod_{\textup{finite $p$}}\mathscr{O}_{p}\times\left(\left.\prod_{p|\infty}F_{p}\right/\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{F}}\right)

and the Tamagawa measure of
\mathbb{A}/\mathbb{F}
equals the square root of the ratio of
the absolute value of discriminant (from the infinite places) and norms of the
differents (from the finite places), hence
=1
. It is through the
relation

\prod_{\textup{finite $p$}}\vol(\mathscr{O}_{p})=\left|\textup{discr}\mathscr{O}_{F}\right|^{-1/2}\,,

that the discriminant factor in completed zeta functions appears as
product of local contribution in (
5
).

A.4.4

For
\mathsf{A}
factor in (
197
), we take the Tamagawa measure. Since
\mathsf{A}\cong GL(1)^{r}
,
it suffices to discuss the multiplicative group
GL(1)
. Since

H^{0}(GL(1,\overline{F}),\mathscr{O}^{\times})/\overline{F}^{\times}=\mathbb{Z}\,,\quad H^{1}(GL(1,\overline{F}),\mathscr{O}^{\times})=0\,,

where
\mathbb{Z}
has the trivial Galois group action, we have
[
Ono
]

d_{GL(1),\textup{\raisebox{-2.84526pt}{\includegraphics[height]{Tama.png}}}}(x)=\prod_{\textup{finite $p$}}\frac{1}{1-p^{-1}}\frac{dx}{\,\,\,|x|_{p}}\times\prod_{p|\infty}\frac{dx}{\,\,\,|x|_{p}}\,.

A.4.5

Since we consider only unramified characters of
\mathsf{A}
, we
effectively work with the pushforward under the norm
map

\displaystyle d_{\mathscr{Q},\textup{\raisebox{-2.84526pt}{\includegraphics[height]{Tama.png}}}}

\displaystyle=\left(GL(1,\mathbb{A})\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\|\,\cdot\,\|\hskip 8.19447pt}\mathscr{Q}\right)_{*}d_{GL(1),\textup{\raisebox{-2.84526pt}{\includegraphics[height]{Tama.png}}}}

\displaystyle=\big(\Res_{s=1}\xi(s)ds\big)\,\frac{dq}{q}\,.

(199)

Here the second line follows the presentation of
\xi(s)
as an
integral with respect to
GL(1,\mathbb{A})

[
Tate
]
. In the
case when
\mathscr{Q}

\frac{dq}{q}

A.4.6

By the formula (
23
) we have

\Res_{s=1}\xi(s)ds=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\ln q}\,\textup{\small\linespread z}\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}>0\\
\textup{\small\linespread z}\,,&\chr\mathbb{F}=0\,.\end{cases}

(200)

Thus the natural measure on the dual group

\mathscr{Q}^{\vee}=\mathbb{C}\left/\frac{2\pi i}{\ln q}\,\mathbb{Z}\right.\,,

where
q=1

1

A.4.7

In

]

\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{A})

In the number field case, for the nonarchimedian places,

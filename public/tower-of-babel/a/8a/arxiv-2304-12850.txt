The aim of this paper is to investigate whether a minimizer exists

To present our findings lucidly, the following is a description of
the basic setting for graphs. Consider a simple, undirected, and
locally finite graph
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
, where
\mathbb{V}
represents the set of
vertices and
\mathbb{E}
represents the set of edges. Vertices
x
and
y

are considered neighbours, written as
x\sim y
, if there is an edge connecting them, meaning that
\{x,y\}\in\mathbb{E}
. A graph is considered
locally finite if each vertex has a finite number of neighbours.
Denote the set of functions on
\mathbb{G}
by
C(\mathbb{V})
. The Laplacian on

\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
is defined as, for any function
u\in C(\mathbb{V})
and
x\in\mathbb{V}
,

\Delta u(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{V},~y\sim x}\left(u(x)-u(y)\right).

For any function
u,v\in C(\mathbb{V})
, we can define the associated gradient by

\Gamma(u,v)(x):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{V},~y\sim x}\frac{1}{2}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x)).

In particular, let
\Gamma(u)=\Gamma(u,u)
for
simplicity. We denote the length of its gradient by

|\nabla u|(x):=\sqrt{\Gamma(u)(x)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{y\in\mathbb{V},~y\sim x}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Let
\mu
be the counting measure on
\mathbb{V}
, i.e., for any subset

A\subset\mathbb{V}
,
\mu(A)=\#\{x:x\in A\cap\mathbb{V}\}
. For any function
f
on

\mathbb{V}
, we write

\int_{\mathbb{V}}fd\mu:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}f(x),

whenever it makes sense. The corresponding energy is defined as

\mathcal{E}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{V}}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{V},~y\sim x}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}.

By definition,
H^{1}(\mathbb{V})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product given by

\langle u,v\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{V}}\left(\Gamma(u,v)+uv\right)d\mu=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{V},~y\sim x}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x))+\sum_{y\in\mathbb{V}}u(y)v(y).

The lattice graph
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
is a very representative locally finite graph, which consists of a group of vertices that are defined as
follows: the set of vertices

\mathbb{V}=\left\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):x_{j}\in\mathbb{Z},~1\leq j\leq 3\right\}

(1.7)

and the set of edges

\mathbb{E}=\left\{\{x,y\}:x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{3},~\sum_{j=1}^{3}|x_{j}-y_{j}|=1\right\}.

(1.8)

For a subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, we can define its volume and boundary area based on the counting measure as follows:

|\Omega|:=\#\{x:x\in\Omega\}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad|\partial\Omega|:=\left|\left\{{y\in\Omega,~\exists x\notin\Omega\,\,\,\textrm{such that}\ \{x,y\}\in E}\right\}\right|.

Let
C(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
be the set of real-valued functions on

\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
,
p\in[1,\infty]
, be
the space of
\ell^{p}
summable functions on
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
w.r.t. the counting measure. We write
\|\cdot\|_{p}
as the
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
norm,
i.e.,

\|u\|_{p}=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}|u(x)|^{p},\ &1\leq p<\infty,\\
\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}|u(x)|,&p=\infty.\end{cases}

By definition, we know that

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})}^{2}=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(|\nabla u(y)|^{2}+|u(y)|^{2}).

From
[
24

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})}\simeq\|u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})}=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}|u(y)|^{2}.

(1.9)

\begin{split}D(\varphi^{2},\varphi^{2})-D(\varphi_{1}^{2},\varphi_{1}^{2})-D(\varphi_{2}^{2},\varphi_{2}^{2})=~&2\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\sum_{y\neq x,\atop y\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\hfill}\frac{\varphi_{1}^{2}(x)\varphi_{2}^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}=2\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\sum_{y\neq x,\atop y\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\hfill}\frac{\varphi_{1}^{2}(x)\varphi_{2}^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}\\
\geq~&2\sum_{x\in B_{r}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\sum_{y\neq x,\atop y\in(B_{R}\setminus B_{r+1})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}\hfill}\frac{\varphi_{1}^{2}(x)\varphi_{2}^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}\\
=~&2\sum_{x\in B_{r}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\sum_{y\neq x,\atop y\in(B_{R}\setminus B_{r+1})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}\hfill}\frac{\varphi^{2}(x)\varphi^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}\\
\geq~&\frac{2}{r+R}\sum_{x\in B_{r}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(x)\sum_{y\in(B_{R}\setminus B_{r+1})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(y).\end{split}

(4.15)

By (
4.12
) and (
4.13
)-(
4.15
), we complete
the proof.
∎

The following lemma provides an upper bound for the mass

m=\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(y)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any ball
B_{R_{0}}(x)
with
R_{0}\geq 4
and

\sum\limits_{y\in B_{R_{0}}(x)}\varphi^{2}(y)\geq C_{0}
, there exists a universal constant
C_{0}
such that:

m=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(y)\leq 2\sum_{y\in B_{2R_{0}}(x)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(y).

Proof.

Assuming
x=0
for simplicity of notation, we consider the balls

B_{r}
that are concentric with
B_{R_{0}}
. For
r\geq 1
, we define

S(r)=\sum_{y\in B_{r}(x)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(y).

From Lemma
4.2
, we can deduce that for all
R>r+1
:

S(r+1)-S(r-1)\geq\frac{S(r)(S(R)-S(r+1))}{3(R+r)}.

Therefore, for
R\geq 16
and
r\in[\frac{R}{4}+1,\frac{R}{2}-1]
(so
that
r<R-2)
, by monotonicity of
S(r)
, one has

\displaystyle S(r+1)-S(r-1)\geq\frac{S(r)(S(R)-S(r+1))}{6R}\geq\frac{S\left(\frac{R}{4}\right)\left(S(R)-S\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)\right)}{6R}.

Integrating
r
from
\frac{R}{4}+1
to
\frac{R}{2}-1
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{R}{4}+1}^{\frac{R}{2}-1}S(r+1)-\sum_{\frac{R}{4}+1}^{\frac{R}{2}-1}S(r-1)=~

\displaystyle S\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)+S\left(\frac{R}{2}-1\right)-S\left(\frac{R}{4}+1\right)-S\left(\frac{R}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle 2S\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)-2S\left(\frac{R}{4}\right).

Hence we obtain

S\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)-S\left(\frac{R}{4}\right)\geq\frac{S\left(\frac{R}{4}\right)}{C_{1}}\left(S(R)-S\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)\right)

for some universal constant
C_{1}
. The inequality holds for any

R\geq 16
. In particular, for any
k\geq 0
, we have

\begin{split}S\left(2^{k-1}R\right)-S\left(2^{k-2}R\right)&\geq\frac{S\left(2^{k-2}R\right)}{C_{1}}\left(S\left(2^{k}R\right)-S\left(2^{k-1}R\right)\right)\\
&\geq\frac{S\left(\frac{R}{4}\right)}{C_{1}}\left(S\left(2^{k}R\right)-S\left(2^{k-1}R\right)\right).\\
\end{split}

Let us choose
C_{0}=2C_{1}
. Then, by taking
R=4R_{0}\geq 16
, we can
state that for all
k\geq 0
:

\begin{split}S\left(2^{k}R\right)-S\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)&=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(S(2^{i}R)-S(2^{i-1}R)\right)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(\frac{C_{1}}{S(\frac{R}{4})}\right)^{i+1}\left(S\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)-S\left(\frac{R}{4}\right)\right)\\
&\leq S\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)-S\left(\frac{R}{4}\right).\end{split}

In the last inequality, we utilized the fact that

S\left(\frac{R}{4}\right)=S\left(R_{0}\right)\geq C_{0}=2C_{1}
.
Then we get that

2S\left(2R_{0}\right)=2S\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)\geq S\left(2^{k}R\right)=\sum_{y\in B_{2^{k}R}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(y)

for any
k
. The lemma is proved by sending
k\to\infty
.
∎

Now we are ready to give the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let

R_{0}:=\inf_{R}\left\{\,\exists x\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\,\textrm{such that}\sum_{y\in B_{R}(x)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(y)\geq C_{0}\right\},

(4.16)

where
C_{0}
is the constant in Lemma
4.3
. It is clear
that
R_{0}\in(2,\infty)
for
m>C_{0}
. By Lemma
4.3
, we
obtain

m\leq 2\sum_{y\in B_{2R_{0}}(x)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(y).

For the ball
B_{2R_{0}}
, we can find
n
points
x_{1},...,x_{l}\in B_{2R_{0}}
such that

B_{2R_{0}}(x)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}B_{\left([R_{0}]-1\right)}(x_{i}),

where
n

. It follows that

m\leq 2\sum_{y\in B_{2R_{0}}(x)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(y)=2\sum_{y\in B_{2R_{0}}(x)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(y)\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{l}\sum_{y\in B_{\left([R_{0}]-1\right)}(x_{i})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi^{2}(y).

One deduces from (

.
∎

5.
The nonexistence of a minimizer for (
1.11
)

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.4
by contradiction. Suppose that there exists a minimizer
\Omega
, we first demonstrate that the energy satisfies the estimate:

\inf\limits_{{|\Omega|=V}}E(\Omega)\lesssim V.

(5.1)

On the other hand, we shall establish an estimate for the nonlocal term as follows:

\sum\limits_{x,y\in\Omega\hfill\atop y\neq x\hfill}{\frac{1}{|x-y|}}\gtrsim V\log V.

(5.2)

By combining (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we arrive at a contradiction and it leads to the original conclusion. To this end we define

E(V):=\inf\{E(\Omega)\mid|\Omega|=V\}.

(5.3)

The following lemma presents an inequality of subadditivity for

E(V)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assuming
V_{0}>1
,
V_{1}>1
, and
V=V_{0}+V_{1}
, it follows that

E(V)\leq E(V_{0})+E(V_{1}).

(5.4)

Proof.

By the definition of infimum, for any given
\epsilon\geq 0
, there are
bounded sets
{\Omega}_{i},i=0,1
, such that

E({\Omega}_{i})\leq E(V_{i})+\epsilon.

Additionally, there exists a large ball
B_{R}
such
that
{\Omega}_{i}\subset B_{R}(0)
. Let
{\widetilde{\Omega}}
be defined
as
{\Omega}_{0}+(d+{\Omega}_{1})
, where
d
is a shift vector with

|d|>2R
. It should be noted that when
x\in{\Omega}_{0}
and
y\in d+{\Omega}_{1}
, we have
|x-y|>d-2R>0
. It is evident that
|\partial\widetilde{\Omega}|=|\partial{\Omega}_{0}|+|\partial{\Omega}_{1}|
and

|\widetilde{\Omega}|=|{\Omega}_{0}|+|{\Omega}_{1}|
due to the definition
of
\widetilde{\Omega}
. Thus, based on the definition of
E(V)
, we
have:

\begin{split}E(V)&\leq E(\widetilde{\Omega})=|\partial{\Omega}_{0}|+|\partial{\Omega}_{1}|+\sum_{x,y\in{\Omega}_{0}\hfill\atop~\ y\neq x\hfill}{\frac{1}{|x-y|}}+\sum_{x,y\in d+{\Omega}_{1}\hfill\atop~\ ~\ y\neq x\hfill}{\frac{1}{|x-y|}}+2\sum_{x\in{\Omega}_{0},~y\in d+{\Omega}_{1}\hfill\atop~\ \hskip 7.3612pty\neq x\hfill}{\frac{1}{|x-y|}}\\
&\leq E({\Omega}_{0})+E({\Omega}_{1})+\frac{2V_{0}V_{1}}{|d|-2R}\\
&\leq E(V_{0})+E(V_{1})+2\epsilon+\frac{2V_{0}V_{1}}{|d|-2R}.\end{split}

As
|d|
tends to infinity and
\epsilon
tends to zero, the
inequality is established.
∎

The subsequent lemma establishes an estimate for
E(V)
and elucidates the properties of the minimizer
\Omega
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
V\gg 1
, then

(i)

We have
E(V)\lesssim V
.

(ii)

If
\Omega
is a minimizer of (
1.11
), then
\Omega
must be a connected set.

Proof.

(i).
By Lemma
2.1
,

|\partial\Omega|\sim V^{\frac{2}{3}}~\mbox{for a ball}~\Omega~\mbox{of volume}~V.

Using the fact that
|x-y|\geq 1
we have

\sum\limits_{x,y\in{\Omega}\hfill\atop~y\neq x\hfill}{\frac{1}{|x-y|}}\lesssim V^{2}.

Therefore

E(\Omega)\lesssim V^{2}+V^{\frac{2}{3}},

which implies

E(V)\lesssim 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq|V|<2.

(5.5)

Through the process of induction and utilizing the subadditivity
inequality (
5.4
), we are able to derive that

E(V)\lesssim N\quad\textrm{for}\quad N\leq V<(N+1).

(ii).
When
\Omega
is not a connected domain, we can divide it
into two separate regions
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
in such a way
that no point
x\in\Omega_{1}
is connected to any point

y\in\Omega_{2}
(i.e.,
x\nsim y
). Let us define

\widehat{\Omega}={\Omega}_{1}+(d+{\Omega}_{2}),

where
d
is a shift vector with

|d|>1
. It is clear that

|\partial\widehat{\Omega}|=|\partial{\Omega}_{1}|+|\partial{\Omega}_{2}|,\quad|\widehat{\Omega}|=|{\Omega}_{1}|+|{\Omega}_{2}|=V,

and

\sum\limits_{x,y\in{\widehat{\Omega}}\hfill\atop~y\neq x\hfill}\frac{1}{|x-y|}<\sum\limits_{x,y\in{\Omega}\hfill\atop~y\neq x\hfill}\frac{1}{|x-y|}.

Therefore, we have
E(\widehat{\Omega})<E(\Omega)
, which contradicts the definition of
\Omega
.
∎

The following lemma provides lower bound for the nonlocal expression

\sum\limits_{x,y\in{\Omega}\hfill\atop~y\neq x\hfill}\frac{1}{|x-y|}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
V\gg 1
and
\Omega
minimizes (
1.11
), then the following
estimate holds for the nonlocal expression:

\sum\limits_{x,y\in{\Omega}\hfill\atop~y\neq x\hfill}\frac{1}{|x-y|}\gtrsim V\log V.

(5.6)

Proof.

Our first assertion is that

|\Omega\cap B_{R}(x)|\gtrsim R\,\,\,\,\textrm{for all vertics}\,\,x\in\Omega\,\,\textrm{and radii}\,1<R<\frac{1}{2}\textrm{diam}(\Omega).

(5.7)

Let

be a fixed point in
\Omega
and

definition of the diameter of
\Omega

\Omega
is not entirely
contained within
B_{R}(x)
. Using the fact that
\Omega

a path
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{R}\in\Omega\cap B_{R}(x)
such that

.
This implies that
|\Omega\cap B_{R}(x)|\geq R

5.7
). As depicted in Figure (
3

Now we claim

\sum\limits_{x,y\in{\Omega}\hfill\atop~y\neq x\hfill}\frac{1}{|x-y|}\gtrsim V\log\textrm{diam}(\Omega).

It is worth noting that, for all

,

\begin{split}A(t):=\big|\{(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega||x-y|<t\}\big|=\sum_{x\in\Omega}\big|\{y\in\Omega||y-x|<t\}\big|=\sum_{x\in\Omega}\big|\Omega\cap B_{t}(x)\big|\gtrsim t|\Omega|.\end{split}

Long Random Matrices and Tensor Unfolding
Gérard Ben Arous, Daniel Zhengyu Huang, Jiaoyang Huang

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the singular values and singular vectors of low rank perturbations of large rectangular random matrices, in the regime the matrix is “long”: we allow the number of rows (columns) to grow polynomially in the number of columns (rows). We prove there exists a critical signal-to-noise ratio (depending on the dimensions of the matrix), and the extreme singular values and singular vectors exhibit a BBP type phase transition. As a main application, we investigate the tensor unfolding algorithm for the asymmetric rank-one spiked tensor model, and obtain an exact threshold, which is independent of the procedure of tensor unfolding. If the signal-to-noise ratio is above the threshold, tensor unfolding detects the signals; otherwise, it fails to capture the signals.

Researchers have made tremendous efforts to innovate effective methods for the analysis of tensor data.
The

In the matrix setting the

In the work
[

of Richard and Montanari, the algorithmic aspects of the spiked tensor model have been studied. They showed that tensor power iteration and approximate message passing algorithm with random initialization recovers the signal provided
\beta\gtrsim n^{(k-1)/2}

In
[

\displaystyle\mathsf{Mat}(\bm{X})=\beta{\bm{v}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}+Z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{q}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{k-q}}.

(1.1)

By taking

, they proved that the tensor unfolding algorithm reovers the signal provided
\beta\gtrsim n^{(\lceil k/2\rceil-1)/2}
, and predicted that the necessary and sufficient condition for tensor unfolding is
\beta\gtrsim n^{(k-2)/4}
.

Several other sophisticated algorithms for the spiked tensor model have been investigated in literature which achieves the sharp threshold
\beta\gtrsim n^{(k-2)/4}
: Sum-of-Squares algorithms

The candscape complexity of spiked tensor model was studied in
[

by Mei, Montanari, Nica and the first author. A new framework based on the Kac-Rice method that

In this paper, we revisit the tensor unfolding algorithm introduced by Richard and Montanari. The unfolded matrix
\mathsf{Mat}(\bm{X})
from (
1.1
) is a spiked matrix model in the form of “signal
+
noise”. However, it is different from spiked matrix models in random matrix literature, which requires the dimensions of the matrix to be comparable, namely the ratio of the number of rows and the number of columns converges to a fixed constant. In this setting, the singular values and singular vectors of the spiked matrix model (
1.1
) has been studied in
[
11
]
by Benaych-Georges and Nadakuditi.

For the unfolded matrix
\mathsf{Mat}(\bm{X})
, its dimensions are not comparable. As the size
n
of the tensor goes to infinity, the ratio of the number of rows and columns goes to zero or infinity, unless
q=k/2
(in this case
\mathsf{Mat}(\bm{X})
is a square matrix). In this paper, we study the singular values and singular vectors of the spiked matrix model (
1.1
) in the case where the number of rows (columns) grows polynomially in the number of columns (rows), which we call
low rank perturbation of long random matrices
. In the case when
\beta=0
, the estimates of singular values and singular vectors for long random matrices follow from
[
1
]
by Alex, Erdős, Knowles, Yau, and Yin.

To study the low rank perturbations of long random matrices, we use the master equations from
[
11
]
, which characterize the outliers of the perturbed random matrices, and the associated singular values. To analyze the master equations, we use the estimates of singular values and Green’s functions of long random matrices from
[
1
]
. Comparing with the setting that the ratio of the number of rows and the number of columns converges to a fixed constant, the challenge is to obtain uniform estimates for the errors in the master equations, which depends only on the number of rows. In this way, we can allow the number of columns to grow much faster than the number of rows.
For the low rank perturbation of long random matrices, we prove there exists a critical signal-to-noise ratio
\beta_{\rm c}
(depending on the dimensions of the matrix), and it exhibit a BBP type phase transition.
We also obtain estimates of the singular values and singular vectors for this model.
Moreover, we also have precise estimates when the signal-to-noise ratio
\beta
is close to the threshold
\beta_{\rm c}
. In particular, our results also apply when
\beta
is close to
\beta_{\rm c}
in mesoscopic scales. In an independent work
[
24
]
by Feldman, this model has been studied under different assumptions. We refer to Section
2.2
for a more detailed discussion of the differences.

Our results for low rank perturbation of long random matrices can be used to study the unfolded matrix
\mathsf{Mat}(\bm{X})
. For the signal-to-noise ratio
\beta=\lambda n^{(k-2)/4}
and any
1\leqslant q\leqslant k-1
, if
\lambda>1
, the PCA on
\mathsf{Mat}(\bm{X})
detects the signal tensor; if
\lambda<1
, the PCA on
\mathsf{Mat}(\bm{X})
fails to capture the signal tensor. This matches the conjectured algorithmic threshold for the spiked tensor model from
[
51
]
. It is worth mentioning that the threshold we get is independent of the tensor unfolding procedure, namely, it is independent of the choice of
q
. For
q>1
, a further recursive unfolding is needed to recover individual signals
{\bm{v}}_{i}
, which increases the computational cost. We propose to simply take
q=1
in the tensor unfolding algorithm for each coordinate axis, and unfold the tensor to an
n\times n^{k-1}
matrix, which gives good approximation of individual signals
{\bm{v}}_{i}
, provided
\lambda>1
. In tensor literature, this algorithm is exactly the truncated higher order singular value decomposition (HOSVD) introduced in
[
20
]
by De Lathauwer, De Moor and Vandewalle. Later, they developed the higher order orthogonal iteration (HOOI) in
[
21
]
, which uses the truncated HOSVD as initialization combining with a power iteration, to find the best low-multilinear-rank approximation of a tensor.
The performance of HOOI was analyzed in
[
57
]
by Zhang and Xia, for the spiked tensor model. It was proven that for the signal-to-noise ratio
\beta=\lambda n^{(k-2)/4}
satisfies
\lambda\geqslant C_{gap}
for some large constant
C_{gap}>0
, HOOI converges within a logarithm factor of iterations.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we state the main results on the singular values and vectors of low rank perturbations of long random matrices.
In Section
3
we study the spiked tensor model, as an application of our results on low rank perturbations of long random matrices. The proofs of our main results are given in Section
4
.

Main Results

In this section, we state our main results on the singular values and singular vectors of low rank perturbations of long random matrices. Let
Z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}
be an
n\times m
random matrices, with i.i.d. random entries
Z_{ij}
satisfying

Assumption 2.1
.

The entries of
Z
are i.i.d., and have mean zero and variance
1/n
, and for any integer

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{ij}]=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{E}[Z_{ij}^{2}]=\frac{1}{n},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{E}[Z_{ij}^{2p}]\leqslant\frac{C_{p}}{n^{p}},\hskip 10.00002pt1\leqslant i\leqslant n,\hskip 10.00002pt1\leqslant j\leqslant m.

We introduce the parameter
\phi:=\sqrt{m/n}
.
It is well-known that for

, if their ratio converges to
m/n=\phi^{2}\rightarrow\phi_{*}^{2}\in[1,\infty)
which is independent of

, the empirical eigenvalue distribution of

converges to the Marchenko-Pastur law

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\left(x-\left(\phi_{*}-1\right)^{2}\right)\left(\left(\phi_{*}+1\right)^{2}-x\right)}}{2\pi x}{\rm d}x,

(2.1)

supported on
[(\phi_{*}-1)^{2},(\phi_{*}+1)^{2}]
.

In this paper, we consider the case where the ratio

depends on

, satisfying that

for some constant
C>0
. In Section
2.1
, we collect some results on singular values of the long random matrix
Z
from
[
1
]

Using the estimates of the singular values and singular vectors of long random matrices as input, we can study low rank perturbations of long random matrices. We consider rank one perturbation of long random matrices in the form,

\displaystyle\beta{\bm{v}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}+Z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m},

(2.2)

where
{\bm{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
{\bm{u}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

We state our main results on the singular values and singular vectors of low rank perturbations of long random matrices (

2.1
Singular values of Long Random Matrices

Let
Z
be an
n\times m
random matrix, with entries satisfying Assumption
2.1
. Let
\phi=\sqrt{m/n}
satisfy that
n^{1/C}\leqslant\phi\leqslant n^{C}

\displaystyle s_{1}\geqslant s_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant s_{n}\geqslant 0.

They are square roots of eigenvalues of the sample covariance matrices
ZZ^{\top}
. As an easy consequence of
[
1

, we have the following theorem on the estimates of the largest singular value of

Theorem 2.2
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, let
\phi=\sqrt{m/n}
with
n^{1/C}\leqslant\phi\leqslant n^{C}
. Fix an arbitrarily small
\delta>0
, with high probability the largest singular value
s_{1}
of
Z
satisfies

\displaystyle|s_{1}-(\phi+1)|\leqslant\frac{n^{\delta}}{n^{2/3}},

(2.3)

provided
n
is large enough.

The results in
[
1
]
give estimates of each eigenvalues of the sample covariance matrix
ZZ^{\top}
away from
0
, see Theorem
4.1
. It also gives estimates of locations of each singular value of
Z
. In particular, it implies that the empirical singular value distribution
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{s_{i}}/n

is close to the pushforward of the Marchenko-Pastur law (after proper normalization) by the map
x\mapsto\sqrt{x}
,

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\rho_{\phi}(x){\rm d}x=\frac{\sqrt{\left(x^{2}-\left(\phi-1\right)^{2}\right)\left(\left(\phi+1\right)^{2}-x^{2}\right)}}{\pi x}{\rm d}x.

We remark that
\rho_{\phi}
depends on
m,n
through
\phi
and is supported on
[\phi-1,\phi+1]
. As
\phi\rightarrow\infty
with
n
,
\rho_{\phi}(x)
after shifting by
\phi
, i.e.
\rho_{\phi}(x+\phi)
, converges to the semi-circle distribution on
[-1,+1]
:

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}(x+\phi){\rm d}x\rightarrow\frac{2\sqrt{(x+1)(1-x)}}{\pi}.

See Figure

for some plots of

. One can see that the extreme singular values stick to the boundary of the support of the limiting empirical measure.

Low Rank Perturbations of Long Random Matrices

Let
Z
be an
n\times m
random matrix, with entries satisfying Assumption
2.1
. Let
\phi=\sqrt{m/n}
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
\phi\geqslant 1
, otherwise, we can simply study the transpose of

In this section, we state our main results on the rank one perturbation of long random matrices from (

):

\displaystyle\beta{\bm{v}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}+Z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m},

(2.4)

where
{\bm{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
{\bm{u}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
are unit vectors.
As in Theorem
2.2
, in this setting, the singular values of
Z
are roughly supported on
the interval
[\phi-1,\phi+1]
.
The following theorem states that there is an exact
n
-dependent threshold
\sqrt{\phi}
, if
\beta
is above the threshold,
\beta{\bm{v}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}+Z
has an outlier singular value; if
\beta
is below this threshold, there are no outlier singular values, and all the singular values are stick to the bulk.

Theorem 2.3
.

We assume Assumption
2.1
and
1\leqslant\phi=\sqrt{m/n}\leqslant n^{C}
. Let
\beta=\lambda\sqrt{\phi}
with
\lambda\geqslant 0
, fix arbitrarily small
{\mathfrak{c}}>0
, and denote

the largest singular value of
\beta{\bm{v}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}+Z
. For any small

, if

, with high probability, the largest singular value

of

\displaystyle\hat{s}_{1}=\sqrt{\phi^{2}+(\lambda^{2}+1/\lambda^{2})\phi+1}+\OO\left(\frac{n^{\delta}(\lambda-1)^{1/2}}{n^{1/2}\phi}\right).

If

Theorem 2.4
.

We assume Assumption
2.1
and
1\leqslant\phi=\sqrt{m/n}\leqslant n^{C}
.
Let
\beta=\lambda\sqrt{\phi}
with
\lambda\geqslant 0
, fix arbitrarily small
{\mathfrak{c}}>0
, and denote
\hat{s}_{1}
the largest singular value of
\beta{\bm{v}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}+Z
. For any small
\delta>0
, if

\displaystyle|\langle\hat{\bm{v}}_{1},{\bm{v}}\rangle|=\left(1+\OO\left(\frac{n^{\delta}}{n^{1/2}(\lambda-1)^{3/2}}\right)\right)\sqrt{\frac{(\lambda^{4}-1)}{\lambda^{4}+\lambda^{2}/\phi}}.

(2.7)

.

We remark that when the ratio

Remark 2.6
.

In this paper, for simplicity of notations, we only consider rank one perturbations of long random matrices. Our method can as well be used to study any finite rank perturbations of long random matrices.

The singular values and vectors of low rank perturbations of large rectangular random matrices has previously been studied in
[
11
]
by Benaych-Georges and Nadakuditi. Our main results Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
are generalization of their results in two directions. Firstly, in
[
11
]
, the ratio
\phi=\sqrt{m/n}
is assumed to converge to a constant independent of
n
. In our setting, we allow
\phi
to have polynomial growth in
n
. As we will see in Section
3
, this will be crucial for us to study the tensor principle component analysis. Secondly, we allow the signal-to-noise ratio to be close to the threshold (depending on
n
), and our main results characterize the behaviors of singular values and singular vectors in this regime. In an independent work
[
24
]
by Feldman, the singular values and vectors of multi-rank perturbations of long random matrices have been studied under the assumption that either the signal vectors contains i.i.d. entries with mean zero and variance one, or the noise matrix has Gaussian entries. Both proofs use the master equations which characterize the singular values and singular vectors of low rank perturbations of rectangular random matrices developed in
[
11
]
, see Section
4.2
. To analyze the master equation, in
[
24
]
, Feldman needs that the signal vectors have i.i.d. entries with mean zero and variance one. Our argument uses results from
[
1
]
, which gives Green’s function estimates of long random matrices, and works for any (deterministic) signal vectors.

3
Tensor PCA

As an application of our main results Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
, in this section, we use them to study the asymmetric rank-one spiked tensor model as introduced in
[
51
]
:

\displaystyle\bm{X}=\beta{\bm{v}}_{1}\otimes{\bm{v}}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{v}}_{k}+\bm{W},

(3.1)

where

•

\bm{X}\in\otimes^{k}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the
k
-th order tensor observation.

•

\bm{W}\in\otimes^{k}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a noise tensor. The entries of
\bm{W}
are independent random variables with mean zero and variance
1/n
.

•

\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
is the signal-to-noise ratio.

•

{\bm{v}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are unknown unit vectors to be recovered.

The goal is
to perform reliable estimation and inference on the unseen signal tensor
{\bm{v}}_{1}\otimes{\bm{v}}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{v}}_{k}
. We remark that for any rank-one tensor
T\in\otimes^{k}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, it can be uniquely written as
T=\beta{\bm{v}}_{1}\otimes{\bm{v}}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{v}}_{k}
, where
{\bm{v}}_{i}
are unit vectors. The model (

) is slightly more general than the asymmetric spiked tensor model in

, which assumes that

. In this section, we make the following assumption on the noise tensor:

Assumption 3.1
.

The entries of
\bm{W}
are i.i.d., and they have mean zero and variance

, and any integer

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bm{W}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}]=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{E}[\bm{W}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}^{2}]=\frac{1}{n},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{E}[\bm{W}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}^{2p}]\leqslant\frac{C_{p}}{n^{p}}.

The tensor unfolding algorithm in

unfolds the tensor

to an

\beta\gg n^{(k-2)/4}
. We recall the tensor unfolding algorithm. Take any index set
\mathbb{I}\subseteq[\![{1,k}]\!]

by unfolding along the axes indexed by

\displaystyle\mathsf{Mat}_{\mathbb{I}}(\bm{X})_{a,b}=\bm{X}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}.

If

We can view

as the sum of the unfolding of the signal tensor

and the noise tensor

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}_{\mathbb{I}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\text{vec}[\otimes_{j=1}^{q}{\bm{v}}_{k_{j}}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{q}},\hskip 10.00002pt{\bm{u}}_{\mathbb{I}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\text{vec}[\otimes_{j=1}^{k-q}{\bm{v}}_{\ell_{j}}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{k-q}},\hskip 10.00002ptZ_{\mathbb{I}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathsf{Mat}_{\mathbb{I}}(\bm{W})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{q}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{k-q}}.

Then we can rewrite

\displaystyle\mathsf{Mat}_{\mathbb{I}}(\bm{X})=\beta{\bm{v}}_{\mathbb{I}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}_{\mathbb{I}}+Z_{\mathbb{I}}(\bm{W})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{q}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{k-q}}.

(3.2)

To make (

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathsf{Mat}_{\mathbb{I}}}(\bm{X})=\frac{\beta}{n^{(q-1)/2}}{\bm{v}}_{\mathbb{I}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}_{\mathbb{I}}+\widetilde{Z_{\mathbb{I}}},\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{Z_{\mathbb{I}}}=\frac{{Z_{\mathbb{I}}}}{n^{(q-1)/2}}.

in the form of (

the largest singular value of the normalized unfolded matrix

Theorem 3.2
.

We assume Assumption
3.1
, and fix any index set
\mathbb{I}\subseteq[\![{1,k}]\!]

Tensor Unfolding

In tensor literature, the above algorithm is exactly the truncated higher order singular value decomposition (HOSVD) introduced in
[
20

[

to find a best low-multilinear-rank approximation of a tensor. The performance of HOOI was analyzed in

Remark 3.4

Given the unfolded

zero eigenvalues, and its other eigenvalues are given by
\pm s_{1},\pm s_{2},\cdots,\pm s_{n}

\displaystyle s(z)=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{z-s_{i}}+\frac{1}{z+s_{i}},

and the Green’s function of

\displaystyle G(z)=\left(z-\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&Z\\
Z^{\top}&0\end{array}\right]\right)^{-1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(z-ZZ^{\top}/z)^{-1}&(z-ZZ^{\top}/z)^{-1}(Z/z)\\
(Z^{\top}/z)(z-ZZ^{\top}/z)^{-1}&(z-Z^{\top}Z/z)^{-1}\end{array}\right].

is close to the Stieltjes transform of the symmetrized version of

\displaystyle m_{\phi}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{\rho_{\phi}(x)+\rho_{\phi}(-x)}{z-x}{\rm d}x=\int\frac{z\rho_{\phi}(x)}{z^{2}-x^{2}}{\rm d}x=\int\frac{z\rho_{MP}^{\phi}(x)}{z^{2}-x\phi}{\rm d}x.

More explicitly, using the formula of

from (

is given by

\displaystyle m_{\phi}(z)=\frac{-(\phi-1/\phi)+z^{2}/\phi\pm\sqrt{(z^{2}/\phi-(\sqrt{\phi}+1/\sqrt{\phi})^{2})(z^{2}/\phi-(\sqrt{\phi}-1/\sqrt{\phi})^{2})}}{2z/\phi},

(4.10)

and it satisfies the algebraic equation

\displaystyle m_{\phi}^{2}+\left(\frac{\phi^{2}-1}{z}-z\right)m_{\phi}+1=0.

(4.11)

Let
z=E+\mathrm{i}\eta
with
\kappa\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\min\{|E-(\phi-1)|,|E-(\phi+1)|\}
.
We denote the spectral domains

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bm{S}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{z=E+\mathrm{i}\eta:\kappa\leqslant{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1},n^{-1+{\mathfrak{c}}}\leqslant\eta\leqslant{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\},\\
&\tilde{\bm{S}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{z=E+\mathrm{i}\eta:E\not\in[(\phi-1),(\phi+1)],n^{-2/3+{\mathfrak{c}}}\leqslant\kappa\leqslant{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1},0<\eta\leqslant{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\}.\end{split}

(4.12)

The spectral domain
\bm{S}
contains the spectral information inside the bulk, and the spectral domain
\tilde{\bm{S}}
contains the spectral information close to the spectral edge.
We recall the following isotropic local law from

, which will be used in Sections

Theorem 4.2

(Isotropic Local Law)
.

For unit vectors
{\bm{a}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
{\bm{b}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, with high probability, uniformly for
z\in\bm{S}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\langle{\bm{a}},(z-ZZ^{\top}/z)^{-1}{\bm{a}}\rangle-m_{\phi}(z)\langle{\bm{a}},{\bm{a}}\rangle\right|\prec\sqrt{\frac{|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[m_{\phi}(z)]|}{n\eta}}+\frac{1}{n\eta},\\
&\left|\langle{\bm{a}},(z-ZZ^{\top}/z)^{-1}(Z/z){\bm{b}}\rangle\right|\prec\frac{1}{\phi}\left(\sqrt{\frac{|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[m_{\phi}(z)]|}{n\eta}}+\frac{1}{n\eta}\right),\\
&\left|\langle{\bm{b}},(z-Z^{\top}Z/z)^{-1}{\bm{b}}\rangle-\frac{\langle{\bm{b}},{\bm{b}}\rangle}{z-m_{\phi}(z)}\right|\prec\frac{1}{\phi^{2}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[m_{\phi}(z)]|}{n\eta}}+\frac{1}{n\eta}\right).\end{split}

Uniformly for
z\in\tilde{\bm{S}}
we have the improved estimates:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\langle{\bm{a}},(z-ZZ^{\top}/z)^{-1}{\bm{a}}\rangle-m_{\phi}(z)\langle{\bm{a}},{\bm{a}}\rangle\right|\prec\sqrt{\frac{|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[m_{\phi}(z)]|}{n\eta}},\\
&\left|\langle{\bm{a}},(z-ZZ^{\top}/z)^{-1}(Z/z){\bm{b}}\rangle\right|\prec\frac{1}{\phi}\sqrt{\frac{|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[m_{\phi}(z)]|}{n\eta}},\\
&\left|\langle{\bm{b}},(z-Z^{\top}Z/z)^{-1}{\bm{b}}\rangle-\frac{\langle{\bm{b}},{\bm{b}}\rangle}{z-m_{\phi}(z)}\right|\prec\frac{1}{\phi^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[m_{\phi}(z)]|}{n\eta}}.\end{split}

Lemma 4.3
.

For two

\displaystyle\det(I-AB^{\top})=\det(I-B^{\top}A).

The matrix

\displaystyle(I-AB^{\top})^{-1}=I+A(I-B^{\top}A)^{-1}B^{\top}.

(4.22)

Proof.

The identity (
4.21
) is Sylvester’s determinant theorem. And the second identity (
4.22
) is known as the matrix inversion lemma or Woodbury’s matrix identity.
∎

There are two cases, either

Theorem (
2.4
) gives the behavior of the projection of the singular vector
\hat{\bm{v}}_{1}
associated with the largest singular value of
\beta{\bm{v}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}+Z
on the signal direction. At the critical value
\lambda=1
, it states

\displaystyle|\langle\hat{\bm{v}}_{1},{\bm{v}}\rangle|^{2}\lesssim n^{-1/3+\oo(1)}.

We believe that it is optimal up to the
\oo(1)
error in the exponent.
More precisely, we conjecture that
exactly at the critical signal strength,
\beta=\sqrt{\phi}
, the projection of the singular vector
\hat{\bm{v}}
associated with the largest singular value of

on the signal
{\bm{v}}
direction satisfies

\displaystyle n^{1/3}|\langle\hat{\bm{v}},{\bm{v}}\rangle|^{2}\rightarrow\Theta,

(4.153)

where
\Theta
is a random variable of size
\OO(1)
.
The above statement (
4.153
) for low rank perturbations of Gaussian unitary matrices have been proven in
[
6
]
, where they give explicit characterization of the limiting objection
\Theta

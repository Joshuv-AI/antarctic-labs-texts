On MSR Subspace Families of Lines
Ferdinand Ihringer

Abstract
A minimum storage regenerating (MSR) subspace family of 𝔽 q 2 ​ m {\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2m} is a set 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} of m m -spaces in 𝔽 q 2 ​ m {\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2m} such that for any m m -space S S in 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} there exists an element in PGL ⁡ ( 2 ​ m , q ) \mathrm{PGL}(2m,q) which maps S S to a complement and fixes 𝒮 ∖ { S } {\mathcal{S}}\setminus\{S\} element-wise. We show that an MSR subspace family of 2 2 -spaces in 𝔽 q 4 {\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{4} has at most size 6 6 with equality if and only if it is a particular subset of a Segre variety. This implies that an ( k + 2 , k , 4 ) (k{+}2,k,4) -MSR code has k ≤ 7 k\leq 7 .

Introduction

Distributed storage systems (DSS) require codes which are particularly
suited for dealing with the unavailability of some storage nodes.
This leads to the investigation of
minimum storage regenerating (MSR)
codes
. It has been shown that MSR codes are closely linked
to MSR subspace families, cf.
[
1
,
3
,
5
]
,
which we will define below. Here we obtain a precise upper bound
on the size of
(k+2,k,4)
-MSR codes, to our knowledge the first open case,
using techniques from finite geometry.

Let
\mathrm{GL}(N,q)
denote the general linear group over the field with
q
elements,
that is the set of intervertible matrices over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
We denote the multiplicative group of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
.
Let
\mathrm{PGL}(N,q)=\mathrm{GL}(N,q)/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}

denote the corresponding projective general linear group.
In the below we use exponential notation for group actions.

Consider
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{rm}
.
We want to find a family
{\mathcal{F}}
of

m
-spaces
S_{i}
such that there exist

g_{i,j}\in\mathrm{PGL}(rm,q)
, where
j\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
, such that for all distinct
i,i^{\prime}
,
we have that
S_{i}+S_{i}^{g_{i,1}}+\ldots+S_{i}^{g_{i,r-1}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{rm}
and
that
S_{i^{\prime}}^{g_{i,j}}=S_{i^{\prime}}
. We call a family
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}\}
such that
such
g_{i,j}
exist a
minimum storage regenerating (MSR) subspace family

or
(rm,r)
-MSR subspace family, cf.
[
1
]
.
Note that in
[
1
]
only
g_{i,j}\in\mathrm{GL}(rm,q)
is required,
but this makes no difference as we only consider actions on subspaces.
Whenever we limit ourselves to the case that
r=2
,
we write
g_{i}
instead of
g_{i,1}

and
S_{i}+S_{i}^{g_{i}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2m}
is
equivalent to
S_{i}\cap S_{i}^{g_{i}}
being trivial.
In this document matrices act by left multiplication.
Throughout the document, let
e_{i}
denote
the
i
-th vector of the canonical basis.
We use projective notation and call
1
-spaces
points
,

2
-spaces
lines
, and
3
-spaces
planes
.
We also say that two subspace are disjoint if their intersection
is
\{0\}
.

Theorem 3 of
[
1
]
states that an
(rm,r)
-MSR
subspace family has size at most
2\ln(rm)/\ln(\frac{r^{2}}{r^{2}-r+1})
.
A detailed
analysis of the proof of Theorem 3 of
[
1
]

gives an upper bound of
7
,
see §
3.4
.
Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

A
(4,2)
-MSR subspace family has size at most
6
. In case of
equality,
q\geq 3
and the family is isomorphic to

\displaystyle\langle e_{1},e_{3}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle e_{2},e_{4}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle e_{1}{+}e_{2},e_{3}{+}e_{4}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle e_{3},e_{4}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle e_{1}{+}e_{3},e_{2}{+}e_{4}\rangle.

Finally, let us sketch the connection to MSR codes.
We follow (the far more detailed) discussion
which is given in
[
1
]
.

Recall that a
maximum distance seperable (MDS) code

{\mathcal{C}}

over a field
{\mathbb{F}}
with parameters
(n,k,\ell)

corresponds to the vectors in a
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear subspace
of
({\mathbb{F}}^{\ell})^{n}
such that any
k
entries of
c\in{\mathcal{C}}

determine
c
. Note that
a coordinate of
c
corresponds to a vector in
{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell}
.
An
(n,k,\ell)
-MSR code

is an
(n,k,\ell)
-MDR code where
for every
m\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
there exist
linear functions
h_{i,m}:{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell/r}

such that the code
m
-th entry of
c\in C
can be computed
by an
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear operation on

\langle h_{i,m}(c_{i}):i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{m\}\rangle\in{\mathbb{F}}^{(n-1)\ell/r}
.

Now let
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq({\mathbb{F}}^{\ell})^{n}
be an
(n,k,\ell)
-MSR code
with redundancy
r:=n-k
. As we can reconstruct a
c\in{\mathcal{C}}

from its first
k
symbols, we find invertible matrices

C_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell\times\ell}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}

and
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that

c_{k+i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{i,j}c_{j}.

For
c_{m}
with
m\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
we find matrices

S_{1,m},\ldots,S_{r,m}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell/r\times\ell}
such that
we can recover
c_{m}
from
S_{i,m}C_{i,m}c_{m}
.
In particular one can see, cf.
[
1
]
, that
the
\ell\times\ell
matrix
(S_{i,m}C_{i,m})_{i}

has full rank.

We say that an MSR code possesses
constant repair matrices

if
S_{i^{\prime},m}=S_{i,m}
for all
i,i^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
.
Theorem 2 of
[
3
]
implies that if there
exists an
(n,k,\ell)
-MSR code, then there also exists
an
(n-1,k-1,\ell)
-MSR code with constant repair matrices.
Now consider an
(n,k,\ell)
-MSR code with
constant repair matrices in the same notation as above.
Then we obtain an
(n,r)
-MSR subspace family:
For
S_{i}
take the row span of
S_{i,m}
.
For the
g_{i,j}
take the map of the row
vector
x
of
S_{i,m}
to
xC_{j+1,m}C_{1,m}^{-1}
.
Thus, Proposition 2 of
[
1
]
follows:

Proposition 1.2
.

If there exists an
(n,k,\ell)
-MSR code over a field
{\mathbb{F}}
,
then there exists an
(\ell,r)
-MSR subspace family with
k-1
subspaces.

Therefore, our main result implies

Corollary 1.3
.

A
(k+2,k,4)
-MSR code has
k\leq 7
.

Constructions

A group
G
acts
regularly
on a set
S
if for any
x,y\in S

there exists a unique
g\in G
with
x^{g}=y
.
A
(projective) frame
or
projective basis

is a tuple of
N+1
points of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{N}
of which all

N
-element subsets span
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{N}
. We use repeatedly
the well-known fact that
\mathrm{PGL}(N,q)
acts regularly on frames, that is
for two frames
{\mathcal{B}}=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{N+1})
,

{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}=(B_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,B_{N+1}^{\prime})
,
there exists a unique
g\in\mathrm{PGL}(N,q)
such that

B_{i}^{g}=B_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N+1\}
, cf.
[
4
, p. 19]
.
Let us give a construction which is essentially identical
to the construction given in Appendix A in
[
1
]
for
r=2
.
We denote the identity matrix by
I
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
q\geq 3
and let
\alpha
be an element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}
.
The following are MSR subspace families.

1.

For
(2,1)
-MSR subspace family, take

\displaystyle S_{1}=\langle e_{1}\rangle,

\displaystyle S_{2}=\langle e_{2}\rangle,

\displaystyle S_{3}=\langle e_{1}+e_{2}\rangle,

\displaystyle g_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\alpha&1-\alpha\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle g_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\alpha^{-1}&\alpha^{-1}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle g_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha\end{pmatrix}.

In particular,
S_{i}^{g_{i}}=\langle e_{1}+\alpha e_{2}\rangle
.

2.

For a
(4,2)
-MSR subspace family, take

\displaystyle S_{1}=\langle e_{1},e_{3}\rangle,

\displaystyle S_{2}=\langle e_{2},e_{4}\rangle,

\displaystyle S_{3}=\langle e_{1}{+}e_{2},e_{3}{+}e_{4}\rangle,

\displaystyle S_{4}=\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle,

\displaystyle S_{5}=\langle e_{3},e_{4}\rangle,

\displaystyle S_{6}=\langle e_{1}{+}e_{3},e_{2}{+}e_{4}\rangle,

\displaystyle g_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\

,

.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{S_{i}\}
be an
(2m,m)
-MSR subspace family of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2m}
of size
k
.
Then we obtain an MSR subspace family
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=\{S_{i}^{\prime}\}
of size

3
Upper Bounds

The idea for our proof of Theorem
1.1

is to generalize the following proof for MSR subspace
families in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{4}
. Call an
MSR subspace family
maximal
if it is not
contained in a larger MSR subspace family.

For a subspace
S
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{N}
, let
\mathrm{Stab}(S)
denote
the stabilizer of
S
in
\mathrm{PGL}(N,q)
. For us, usually

S
will be an
N/2
-space. Furthermore,
for a family
{\mathcal{S}}=\{S_{1},\ldots,\allowbreak S_{k}\}
of subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
,
let
\mathrm{Stab}({\mathcal{S}})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Stab}(S_{i})
denote the intersection of their stabilizers in
\mathrm{PGL}(N,q)
. We denote the neutral element of a group by
id
.

Lemma 3.1
.

An MSR subspace family
{\mathcal{S}}
with
\mathrm{Stab}({\mathcal{S}})=\{id\}
is maximal.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists an
m
-space
S
such that

{\mathcal{S}}\cup\{S\}
is an MSR subspace family.
Then there exists
g\in\mathrm{Stab}({\mathcal{S}})

such that
S^{g}\cap S
is trivial. This is a contradiction.
∎

As
\mathrm{Stab}(\{P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\})=\{id\}
for three pairwise distinct points
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
, we find that an MSR subspace family of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
has at
most size
3
. Similarly, we find

Corollary 3.2
.

An
(m,1)
-MSR subspace family of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
has at most size
m+1
.

We will also need the following simple observations.

Lemma 3.3
.

For an
(m,r)
-MSR subspace family
{\mathcal{S}}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2m}
, put
U=\mathrm{Stab}({\mathcal{S}})
.
Let
\ell\geq m+1
.
Suppose that one of the following (equivalent) conditions is satisfied:

(a)

There exists an
\ell
-space
H
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2m}
which is fixed point-wise by
U
.

(b)

There exists a frame
{\mathcal{B}}
of size
\ell+1
which is fixed element-wise by
U
.

Then
{\mathcal{S}}
is maximal.

Proof.

Both conditions are equivalent as
\mathrm{Stab}(H)
acts regularly on frames of
H
.
Suppose that
{\mathcal{S}}
is not maximal and we find an
m
-space
S
such that

{\mathcal{S}}\cup\{S\}
is an MSR subspace family. Then there exists a
g\in\mathrm{Stab}({\mathcal{S}})

such that
S\cap S^{g}
is trivial. This contradicts that
(S\cap H)^{g}=S\cap H

contains a point.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For an
(m,r)
-MSR subspace family
{\mathcal{S}}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{rm}
,
we have that

\dim(S_{1}\cap\ldots\cap S_{k})\leq\dim(S_{1}\cap\ldots S_{k-1})/m

for pairwise distinct
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof.

Put
d:=\dim(S_{1}\cap\ldots\cap S_{k-1})
.
Otherwise,
T:=S_{1}\cap\ldots\cap S_{k}
has dimension
larger than
d/r
. Hence,
\dim(\langle T^{g_{i}}\rangle)\leq d

for any
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}\in\mathrm{Stab}(S_{1})
.
Hence,
{\mathcal{S}}
cannot be an MSR subspace family
as this requires that
\dim(\langle T_{g_{i}}\rangle)=rd
.
∎

The following is a poor man’s version
of Lemma 7 in
[
1
]
. We state it for the
sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.5
.

For an
(m,r)
-MSR subspace family
{\mathcal{S}}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{rm}
,
we have that
|\mathrm{Stab}({\mathcal{T}})|>|\mathrm{Stab}({\mathcal{T}}^{\prime})|

for
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
if
{\mathcal{T}}
is a proper subset of
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
|{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}|=|{\mathcal{T}}|+1

and let
S
be the element in
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}\setminus{\mathcal{T}}
.
If
\mathrm{Stab}({\mathcal{T}})=\mathrm{Stab}({\mathcal{T}}^{\prime})
, then
then
S^{g}=S
for all
g\in\mathrm{Stab}({\mathcal{T}})
,
so
{\mathcal{S}}
cannot be an MSR subspace family.
∎

3.1
The Segre Variety

We will use some basic facts about Segre varieties over finite fields.
We will also give an explicit coordinatized example for
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2m}
,
our main interest in this document.
If we have
three pairwise disjoint
m
-spaces
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2m}
,
then there exist precisely
[m]:=(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)
lines

{\mathcal{R}}^{opp}:={\mathcal{R}}^{opp}(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}):=\{L_{1},\ldots,L_{[m]}\}

which meet
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
each in a point.
Furthermore, there exist precisely
q+1
subspaces

{\mathcal{R}}:={\mathcal{R}}(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}):=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{q+1}\}

which meet each element of
{\mathcal{R}}^{opp}
in a point.
We call
{\mathcal{R}}
a
regulus
, and
{\mathcal{R}}\cup{\mathcal{R}}^{opp}

is the
Segre variety

{\mathcal{S}}_{m-1,1,q}
.
For
m=2
, we call
{\mathcal{R}}^{opp}

the
opposite regulus
of
{\mathcal{R}}
,
and
S_{m-1,1,q}
the line set of a
hyperbolic quadric
.
Let
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}})={\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}}^{opp})
denote the point set of the lines
in
{\mathcal{R}}
.

The setwise stabilizer of
{\mathcal{R}}
(in
\mathrm{PGL}(2m,q)
) is isomorphic
to
\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)\times\mathrm{PGL}(m,q)

and acts on
{\mathcal{R}}\cup{\mathcal{R}}^{opp}
as expected,
that is one
\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)
acts on
{\mathcal{R}}
as on points of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
,
and one
\mathrm{PGL}(m,q)
acts on
{\mathcal{R}}^{opp}
as on points of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
.
This leads to the following well-known result on
\mathrm{Stab}(\{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}\})
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
be three pairwise disjoint
m
-spaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2m}
.
Then
\mathrm{Stab}(\allowbreak\{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}\})
fixes each element of
{\mathcal{R}}={\mathcal{R}}(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})

and
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{PGL}(m,q)
. ∎

Example 3.7
.

Let
m=2
.
Without loss of generality (as
\mathrm{PGL}(4,q)
acts transitively on triples of pairwise
disjoint lines)
{\mathcal{R}}
consists of the lines

\displaystyle\{\langle e_{1}+\alpha e_{2},e_{3}+\alpha e_{4}:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\rangle\cup\{\langle e_{2},e_{4}\rangle\},

and
{\mathcal{R}}^{opp}
consists of the lines

\displaystyle\{\langle e_{1}+\alpha e_{3},e_{2}+\alpha e_{4}:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\rangle\cup\{\langle e_{3},e_{4}\rangle\}.

Note that this is the canonical tensor product
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}\otimes{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
,
that is points of the first
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
correspond to lines in
{\mathcal{R}}

and the points of the second
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
correspond to lines in
{\mathcal{R}}^{opp}
.
The setwise stabilizer of
{\mathcal{R}}
in
\mathrm{PGL}(4,q)
is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a\alpha&b\alpha&a\beta&b\beta\\
c\alpha&d\alpha&c\beta&d\beta\\
a\gamma&b\gamma&a\delta&b\delta\\
c\gamma&d\gamma&c\delta&d\delta\\
\end{pmatrix}:\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}(2,q)\right\}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*},,

that is
\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)\times\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Stab}(\{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}\})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0&\beta&0\\
0&\alpha&0&\beta\\
\gamma&0&\delta&0\\
0&\gamma&0&\delta\\
\end{pmatrix}:\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}(2,q)\right\}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\alpha I&\beta I\\
\gamma I&\delta I\end{pmatrix}:\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}(2,q)\right\}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*},

and, clearly,
\mathrm{Stab}(\{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}\})
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)
.

Note that
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}})
corresponds to the points
\langle x\rangle
with
Q(x)=0

on a nondegenerate quadratic form
Q
of rank
2
,
for instance the points
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}})
in Example
3.7
are precisely the
points which vanish on
Q(x)=x_{1}x_{4}+x_{2}x_{3}
.
We find the following types of lines with respect to
{\mathcal{R}}
:

(L1)

A line of
{\mathcal{R}}
.

(L2)

A line of
{\mathcal{R}}^{opp}
.

(L3)

A line, say
\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle
, which meet
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}})
in two points (secants), say
Q(x)=x_{1}x_{2}
.
In this example the points of
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}})
are
\langle x_{1}\rangle
and
\langle x_{2}\rangle
.

(L4)

A line, say
\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle
, which meet
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}})
in one point (tangents), say
Q(x)=x_{1}^{2}
.
In this example the point of
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}})
is
\langle x_{2}\rangle
.

(L5)

A line, say
\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle
, which meet
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}})
in no point (passants), say
Q(x)=x_{1}^{2}+\alpha x_{1}x_{2}+\beta x_{2}^{2}

such that

is irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

And the following types of planes:

(P1)

A plane, say
\langle e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\rangle
, intersects
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}})
in conic (conic plane), that is there exists

Q
, say

,
such that
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}})
is the set of points
\langle x\rangle

of the plane with

.

(P2)

A plane, say
\langle e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\rangle
, intersects
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{R}})
in two lines (degenerate plane),
that is there exists a degenerate quadratic form
Q
, say
Q(x)=x_{2}x_{3}

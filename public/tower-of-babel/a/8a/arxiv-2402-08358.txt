De la Vallée Poussin filtered polynomial approximation on the half line
D.Occorsio, W.Themistoclakis

Abstract
On the half line we introduce a new sequence of near–best uniform approximation polynomials, easily computable by the values of the approximated function at a truncated number of Laguerre zeros. Such approximation polynomials come from a discretization of filtered Fourier–Laguerre partial sums, which are filtered by using a de la Vallée Poussin (VP) filter. They have the peculiarity of depending on two parameters: a truncation parameter that determines how many of the n n Laguerre zeros are considered, and a localization parameter, which determines the range of action of the VP filter that we are going to apply. As n → ∞ n\to\infty , under simple assumptions on such parameters and on the Laguerre exponents of the involved weights, we prove that the new VP filtered approximation polynomials have uniformly bounded Lebesgue constants and uniformly convergence at a near–best approximation rate, for any locally continuous function on the semiaxis. The theoretical results have been validated by the numerical experiments. In particular, they show a better performance of the proposed VP filtered approximation versus the truncated Lagrange interpolation at the same nodes, especially for functions a.e. very smooth with isolated singularities. In such cases we see a more localized approximation as well as a good reduction of the Gibbs phenomenon.



1
Introduction

The paper deals with the approximation of locally continuous functions
f
defined on the positive half-line. The function
f
we consider can exponentially grow at infinity or/and have an algebraic singularity at the origin. Measuring the approximation error with a suitable weighted uniform norm, the Weierstrass approximation theorem may be applied and
f
can be approximated by polynomials at the desired precision.

The topic has been extensively studied by many authors, which were looking for a polynomial approximating sequence having optimal Lebesgue constants in spaces of locally continuous functions equipped with uniform weighted norms
[
9
,
15
,
3
,
6
,
8
,
10
]
.Based on Laguerre zeros, we mention the classical Lagrange interpolating polynomial for which in
[
9
]
it has been proved that
the associated weighted Lebesgue constants diverge as
n^{\frac{1}{6}}
. In the same paper a ”remedium” was proposed to this bad behavior, by introducing a suitable additional node. In this case, sufficient and necessary conditions have been stated to get optimal Lebesgue constants growing with minimal order
\log n
. A truncated version of the latter Lagrange polynomial has been introduced and studied in
[
3
,
6
]
by involving only a very reduced number of function evaluations. Also in this case we get optimal Lebesgue constants, under suitable sufficient conditions.

In the present paper we introduce new approximation polynomials based on the same data of the truncated Lagrange-Laguerre interpolation but able to provide uniformly bounded Lebesgue constants and a near-best convergence order for any locally continuous function on the semiaxis. Such polynomials are defined by discretizing some VP means, namely delayed arithmetic means of the Fourier-Laguerre partial sums.

We recall that in literature such continuous VP means have been introduced as an useful tool to prove several theoretical results
[
13
,
14
]
.

In the Jacobi case it is known that by discretizing VP means we get near-est approximation operators that preserve the polynomials up to certain degree, have uniformly bounded Lebesgue constants
[
18
]
and, in some cases, are interpolating too. Here we show that, in the Laguerre case, the interpolation property as well as the polynomial preservation are not ensured. But, all the same, we prove the uniform convergence at a near-best approximation rate with uniformly bounded Lebesgue constants, under suitable assumptions.

The outline of the paper is the following. In Section 2 the necessary notations and preliminary results are introduced. Section 3 concerns the definition and the properties of the new VP discrete approximation polynomials. Section 4 deals with the numerical experiments and, finally, Section 5 regards the conclusions.

2
Notations and preliminary results

Along all the paper the notation
{\mathcal{C}}
will be used several times to denote a positive constant having different values in different formulas. We will write
{\mathcal{C}}\neq\mathcal{C}(a,b,\ldots)
in order to say that
\mathcal{C}
is independent of the parameters
a,b,\ldots
, and
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(a,b,\ldots)
to say that

\mathcal{C}
depends on
a,b,\ldots
. Moreover, if
A,B>0
are quantities depending on some parameters, we will write
A\sim B,
if there exists an absolute constant
{\mathcal{C}}>0
, independent on such parameters, such that

{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}B\leq A\leq{\mathcal{C}}B
.

For all
\gamma\geq 0,
we will consider the weight
u(x)=x^{\gamma}e^{-x/2},
and the space
C_{u}
of all locally continuous functions on the half line (i.e., continuous in
]0,\infty[
) such that

\lim_{x\to\infty}(fu)(x)=0,\qquad\mbox{and, in the case $\gamma>0$, also}\qquad\lim_{x\to 0}(fu)(x)=0.

We will take the space
C_{u}
equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{C_{u}}=\|fu\|:=\sup_{x\geq 0}\left|(fu)(x)\right|,

and for any compact set
D\subset[0,+\infty)
, we will use
\|f\|_{D}=\sup_{x\in D}|f(x)|
.

Note that functions in
C_{u}
may grow exponentially as
x\to+\infty
, and may have an algebraic singularity at the origin.

Denoting by
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
the space of the algebraic
polynomials of degree at most
n\geq 0
, we will consider the error of best approximation of
f\in C_{u}
in
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}

E_{n}(f)_{u}:=\inf_{P_{n}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\|(f-P_{n}){u}\|,

(1)

It is known that
[
4
,
5
,
11
,
12
]

\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{n}(f)_{{u}}=0,\qquad\forall f\in C_{u}.

For smoother functions, we consider the Sobolev-type spaces of order
r\in{\mathbb{N}}

W_{r}(u)=\left\{f\in C_{u}\ :\ f^{(r-1)}\in AC((0,+\infty))\mbox{ and }\ \|f^{(r)}\varphi^{r}u\|<+\infty\right\},

where
AC((0,+\infty))
denotes the set of all functions which are absolutely continuous on every closed subset of
(0,+\infty)
and
\varphi(x)=\sqrt{x}.

We equip these spaces with the norm

\|f\|_{W_{r}(u)}:=\|fu\|+\|f^{(r)}\varphi^{r}u\|.

For any function
f\in W_{r}(u)
the following estimate holds
[
1
,
4
]

E_{n}(f)_{{u}}\leq{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\|f\|_{W_{r}(u)}}{\sqrt{n^{r}}},\quad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f).

(2)

2.1
Truncated Lagrange interpolation at the zeros of Laguerre polynomials

Let
w_{\alpha}(x)=e^{-x}x^{\alpha}
be the Laguerre weight of parameter
\alpha>-1

and let
\{p_{n}(w_{\alpha})\}_{n}
be the
corresponding sequence of orthonormal Laguerre polynomials with positive leading
coefficients.
Denoting by
x_{k}:=x_{n,k},\ k=1,\dots,n,
the zeros of
p_{n}(w_{\alpha})
in increasing order, we recall that (see
[
17
]
)

\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{n}<x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots<x_{n}<4n+2\alpha-{\mathcal{C}}n^{\frac{1}{3}},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n).

(3)

In the sequel, we will be interested in taking only a part of these zeros.
More precisely, throughout the paper, the integer
j:=j(n)
will denote the index of the last zero of
p_{n}(w_{\alpha})
we consider. It is defined by

j=\min_{k=1,2,..,n}\left\{k:x_{k}\geq 4n\rho\right\},

(4)

with
0<\rho<1
arbitrarily fixed,

We recall that, inside the segment
[0,x_{j}]
, the distance between two consecutive zeros of
p_{n}(w_{\alpha})
can be estimated as follows

\Delta x_{k}\sim\Delta x_{k-1}\sim\sqrt{\frac{x_{k}}{n}},\qquad\quad\Delta x_{k}=x_{k+1}-x_{k},\qquad k=1,2,\dots,j.

With
j
defined in (
4
), let
L_{n+1}^{*}(w_{\alpha},f)
be the truncated Lagrange polynomial
[
6
]
defined by

L_{n+1}^{*}(w_{\alpha},f,x):=\sum_{k=1}^{j}f(x_{k})\ell_{n,k}(x),

(5)

where

\ell_{n,k}(x)=\frac{p_{n}(w_{\alpha},x)}{p^{\prime}_{n}(x_{k})(x-x_{k})}\frac{4n-x}{4n-x_{k}},\qquad k=1,2,\dots,j,

(6)

are the fundamental Lagrange polynomials corresponding to the zeros of
p_{n}(w_{\alpha},x)(4n-x)
.

It is known that the norm of the operator
L_{n+1}^{*}(w_{\alpha}):f\in C_{u}\to L_{n+1}^{*}(w_{\alpha},f)\in C_{u}
, i.e. the weighted Lebesgue constant

\|L_{n+1}^{*}(w_{\alpha})\|_{C_{u}}=\sup_{\|fu\|=1}\|L_{n+1}^{*}(w_{\alpha},f)u\|=\sup_{x\geq 0}\sum_{k=1}^{j}|\ell_{n,k}(x)|\frac{u(x)}{u(x_{k})},

(7)

diverges at least as
\log n
when
n\to\infty
. To be more precise, the following result holds
[
6
]

Theorem 2.1

[
6
]

Let
w_{\alpha}(x)=e^{-x}x^{\alpha},\ \alpha>-1
and
u(x)=e^{-\frac{x}{2}}x^{\gamma},\ \gamma\geq 0.
Under the assumption

\max\left(0,\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)\leq\gamma\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{5}{4},

(8)

we have

\|L_{n+1}^{*}(w_{\alpha},f)u\|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\|fu\|\log n,\quad\forall f\in C_{u},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f).

(9)

Moreover, as regards the approximation of
f\in C_{u}
provided by the polynomial
L_{n+1}^{*}(w_{\alpha},f)
we recall the following

Theorem 2.2

[
6
, Th.2.2]

For any
f\in C_{u},
under the assumption (
8
), we have

\|(f-L_{n+1}^{*}(w_{\alpha},f))u\|\leq{\mathcal{C}}(\log n)\left[E_{N}(f)_{u}+e^{-An}\|fu\|\right],

where
N=\left\lfloor\left(\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\right)n\right\rfloor\sim n
, and

A
and
{\mathcal{C}}
are positive constants independent of
n
and
f
.

2.2
Ordinary and truncated Gauss-Laguerre rules

According to the previous notation, the Gauss-Laguerre rule on the nodes in (
3
) is given by

\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(x)w_{\alpha}(x)dx=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(x_{k})\lambda_{n,k}(w_{\alpha})+R_{n}(f),\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

(10)

where
\lambda_{n,k}(w_{\alpha})=\left[\sum_{j=0}^{n}p_{j}(x_{k})\right]^{-1}
are the Christoffel numbers w.r.t.
w_{\alpha},
and the remainder term satisfies

R_{n}(f)=0,\qquad\forall f\in{\mathbb{P}}_{2n-1}.

(11)

For any
f\in C_{u}
, under the assumption

\alpha-\gamma>-1
, the following estimate holds

|R_{n}(f)|\leq\ {\mathcal{C}}E_{2n-1}(f)_{u},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f).

In
[
7
]
the “truncated” Gauss-Laguerre rule was introduced by using only the first
j
zeros of
p_{n}(w_{\alpha})
, with
j
defined in (
4
), i.e.

\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(x)w_{\alpha}(x)dx=\sum_{k=1}^{j}f(x_{k})\lambda_{n,k}(w_{\alpha})+R_{n,j}(f),

(12)

where

R_{n,j}(f)=0,\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{P}_{n-1}^{*}:=\left\{p\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n-1}:p(x_{k})=0,\quad k>j\right\}.

(13)

In
[
6
]
the following estimate was proved under the assumption

\alpha-\gamma>-1

|R_{n,j}(f)|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\ \left(E_{N}(f)_{u}+e^{-\mathcal{B}n}\|fu\|\right),\qquad\forall f\in C_{u},

where
N=\left\lfloor\left(\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\right)n\right\rfloor\sim n
, and
\mathcal{B},{\mathcal{C}}>0
are constants independent of
n,f
.

2.3
Cesaro and de la Vallée Poussin means

For any given
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}=\{0,1,2,...\}
, let
S_{n}(w_{\alpha},f)\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
the
n-
th Fourier sum of
f
in the orthonormal system
\{p_{i}(w_{\alpha})\}_{i=0,1,..}
, namely

S_{n}(w_{\alpha},f,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}(f)p_{i}(w_{\alpha},x)

with Fourier coefficients defined as

c_{i}(f)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(t)p_{i}(w_{\alpha},t)w_{\alpha}(t)dt.

(14)

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,....\}
, we recall the Cesaro means are defined as arithmetic means of the first
n-1
Fourier sums, i.e.

\sigma_{n}(w_{\alpha},f,x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}S_{k}(w_{\alpha},f,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mu_{n,i}^{m}c_{i}(f)p_{i}(w_{\alpha},x),

(15)

In
[
16
]
simple necessary and sufficient conditions have been stated in order that the map
\sigma_{n}(w_{\alpha}):C_{u}\to C_{u}
is uniformly bounded w.r.t.
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 2.3

[
16
, Theorems 1 and 5]

For all
\alpha>-1
, let
w_{\alpha}(x)=e^{-x}x^{\alpha}
and
u(x)=x^{\beta}\sqrt{w_{\alpha}(x)}
. The map
\sigma_{n}(w_{\alpha}):C_{u}\to C_{u}
is uniformly bounded w.r.t.
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, i.e.
we have

\|\sigma_{n}(w_{\alpha},f)u\|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\|fu\|,\quad\forall f\in C_{u},\quad\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f),

(16)

if and only if the following bounds are satisfied

-\min\left\{\frac{\alpha}{2},\frac{1}{4}\right\}<\beta<1+\min\left\{\frac{\alpha}{2},\frac{1}{4}\right\},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\leq\beta\leq\frac{7}{6}

(17)

Now, besides
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let us take an additional parameter
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
0<m<n
, and, for a given function
f
, let us consider the VP means, namely the following delayed arithmetic means of Fourier sums

\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f,x)=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{k=n-m}^{n+m-1}S_{k}(w_{\alpha},f,x),

(18)

From a computational point of view, this is equivalent to

\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n+m-1}\mu_{n,i}^{m}c_{i}(f)p_{i}(w_{\alpha},x),

(19)

that is a filtered version of
S_{n+m-1}(w_{\alpha},f)
with filter coefficients

\mu_{n,i}^{m}
defined as

\mu_{n,i}^{m}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\mbox{if}\quad i=0,\ldots,n-m,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\frac{n+m-i}{2m}&\mbox{if}\quad n-m+1\leq i\leq n+m-1.\\
\end{array}\right.

(20)

The filtered VP polynomial
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n+m-1}
can be also rewritten in integral form as

\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f,x)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(t)v_{n}^{m}(x,t)w_{\alpha}(t)dt,

(21)

where

v_{n}^{m}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{r=n-m}^{n+m-1}K_{r}(x,t),\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad K_{r}(x,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}p_{i}(w_{\alpha},x)p_{i}(w_{\alpha},t)

are the VP and Darboux kernels, respectively.

Note that
v_{n}^{m}(x,t)=v_{n}^{m}(t,x)
is a polynomial of degree
n+m-1
in both the variables that can be represented as

v_{n}^{m}(x,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n+m-1}\mu_{n,i}^{m}p_{i}(w_{\alpha},x)p_{i}(w_{\alpha},t).

An advantage of taking VP means instead of Cesaro means is given by the following invariance property

\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},P)=P,\quad\forall P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n-m}.

(22)

Such preservation property allows us to investigate the approximation provided by
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)
in
C_{u}
, by estimating the norm of the operator map
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha}):C_{u}\to C_{u}
, that is

\|\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha})\|_{C_{u}}=\sup_{\|fu\|=1}\|\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)u\|=\sup_{x\geq 0}\left[u(x)\int_{0}^{+\infty}|v_{n}^{m}(x,t)|\frac{w_{\alpha}(t)}{u(t)}dt\right].

(23)

To this aim, we introduce the following notation concerning the degree parameters
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
defining the VP mean:

\displaystyle m\sim n

iff

\displaystyle m<n\leq{\mathcal{C}}m,\qquad\qquad\mbox{holds with ${\mathcal{C}}>1$ independent of $n,m$},

(24)

\displaystyle m\approx n

iff

\displaystyle m<c_{1}m\leq n\leq c_{2}m,\quad\mbox{holds with $c_{2}\geq c_{1}>1$ independent of $m,n$.}

(25)

Note that
m\sim n
agrees with the notation introduced in Section
2
while
m\approx n
is a stronger condition that ensures
(n-m)\sim n
holds too.

If we take the additional parameter
m
such that
m\sim n
then from Theorem
2.3
we easily deduce the uniform boundedness of VP operator
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha})
in
C_{u}
for suitable choices of the weights
w_{\alpha}
and
u
. More precisely, we have the following

Theorem 2.4

Let the weight function
w_{\alpha}(x)=e^{-x}x^{\alpha}
and
u(x)=e^{-\frac{x}{2}}x^{\gamma},
satisfy the following bounds

\max\left\{\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{1}{4},\ 0\right\}<\gamma<\min\left\{\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{7}{6},\ \alpha+1\right\}

(26)

then, for any couple of positive integers
m\sim n
, the map
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha}):C_{u}\to C_{u}
is uniformly bounded w.r.t.
n
and
m
, i.e.

\|\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)u\|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\|fu\|,\quad\forall f\in C_{u},\quad\forall m\sim n,\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,m,f).

(27)

Proof of Theorem
2.4

Taking into account that

\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)=\frac{n+m}{2m}\sigma_{n+m}(w_{\alpha},f)-\frac{n-m}{2m}\sigma_{n-m}(w_{\alpha},f),

the statement follows by using the assumption (
24
) that implies

\frac{n+m}{2m}\leq\frac{{\mathcal{C}}+1}{2},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{n-m}{2m}\leq\frac{{\mathcal{C}}-1}{2},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,m),

and applying Theorem
2.3
with
\beta=\gamma-\frac{\alpha}{2}
, that satisfies (
17
) by virtue of (
26
).
\diamondsuit

Hence, concerning the error estimate the following result can be deduced

Theorem 2.5

Let the weight function
w_{\alpha}(x)=e^{-x}x^{\alpha}
and
u(x)=e^{-\frac{x}{2}}x^{\gamma},
satisfy (
26
). Then, for any
f\in C_{u}
and for all
m\sim n
, we have

E_{n+m-1}(f)_{u}\leq\|[f-\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)]u\|\leq{\mathcal{C}}E_{n-m}(f)_{u},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,m,f),

(28)

Moreover, if
m\approx n
then we get

\|f-\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)\|_{C_{u}}\sim E_{n}(f)_{u},\qquad\forall f\in C_{u}

(29)

with the constants in

independent of
n,m,f

Proof of Theorem
2.5

The first inequality in (
28
) trivially follows from
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n+m-1}
.

The second inequality in (

) can be deduced by means of (

3
VP filtered discrete approximation

In the previous section, we have seen that for any
f\in C_{u}
and suitable choices of
w_{\alpha}
, the polynomials
\{\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)\}_{m\approx n}
provide a near–best approximation of
f
. However, their construction requires the knowledge of the Fourier coefficients of the function
f
. To overcome this problem, in this section
we introduce the polynomial
{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)
, that we define as a discrete version of
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)
.
To be more precise, with
j=j(n)
defined in (
4
), we apply the Gauss-Laguerre rule in (
12
) to the coefficients
c_{i}(f)
in (
14
), getting

c_{n,i}^{(j)}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{j}f(x_{k})p_{i}(w_{\alpha},x_{k})\lambda_{n,k}(w_{\alpha})

(30)

Hence, similarly to (
19
) we define the following discrete VP filtered approximation polynomials

{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n+m-1}\mu_{n,i}^{m}c_{n,i}^{(j)}(f)p_{i}(w_{\alpha},x),\qquad 0<m<n

(31)

that can be equivalently written as

{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{j}f(x_{k})\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x),\qquad x\geq 0

(32)

with
j=j(n)
defined in (
4
), and

\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)=\lambda_{n,k}(w_{\alpha})v_{n}^{m}(x,x_{k})=\lambda_{n,k}(w_{\alpha})\sum_{i=0}^{n+m-1}\mu_{n,i}^{m}p_{i}(w_{\alpha},x_{k})p_{i}(w_{\alpha},x).

(33)

1
we show, as an example, the graphics of the
fundamental polynomial
\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)
, for
n
and
k
fixed, and
m
varying. As we can see for increasing
m

the oscillations of
\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)
progressively dampen. The major oscillating behavior is presented by the fundamental Lagrange polynomial
\ell_{n,k}(x)
that are plotted in the same figure.

Plots of the fundamental polynomials

We remark that, similarly to
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)
, for any function
f
we have
{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n+m-1}
, but in the case
f=P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n-m}
the invariance property (

V_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)
.

It can be easily proved that the norm of the operator map
{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha}):C_{u}\to C_{u}
takes the following form

\|{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha})\|_{C_{u}}=\sup_{\|fu\|=1}\|{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)\|=\sup_{x\geq 0}\sum_{k=1}^{j}\left|\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)\right|\frac{u(x)}{u(x_{k})}.

(34)

As the number of nodes increases, the behavior of such norm is an important measure of the conditioning, due to the linearity of
V_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha})
.
Under the same assumptions of Theorem
2.4
, the next theorem states that also the discrete filtered VP operator
{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha}):C_{u}\to C_{u}
is uniformly bounded w.r.t any
n\sim m
.

Theorem 3.1

If the weights
w_{\alpha}(x)=e^{-x}x^{\alpha}
and
u(x)=e^{-\frac{x}{2}}x^{\gamma}
satisfy (
26
) then, for any
m\sim n
, the map
{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha}):C_{u}\to C_{u}
is uniformly bounded w.r.t.
n
and
m
, i.e.

\|{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)u\|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\|fu\|,\quad\forall f\in C_{u},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,m,f).

(35)

Proof of Theorem
3.1

By Remez-type polynomial inequality
[
2
]
, since
{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n+m-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\|{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)u\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\max_{x\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\left|{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f,x)u(x)\right|,

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{A}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[a_{n,m},b_{n,m}],\quad a_{n,m}\sim\frac{c}{n+m-1},\quad b_{n,m}\sim 4(n+m)

by which

\|{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)u\|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\|fu\|\left(\max_{x\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{j}\left|\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)\right|\frac{u(x)}{u(x_{k})}\right)

(36)

Hence, our aim is to prove that

\Sigma_{n,m}(x):=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\left|\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)\right|\frac{u(x)}{u(x_{k})}\leq{\mathcal{C}},\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{A}_{n},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,m,x)

(37)

Start from (
33
) and use

\lambda_{n,k}(w_{\alpha})\sim\Delta x_{k}w_{\alpha}(x_{k}),\quad\Delta x_{k}=x_{k+1}-x_{k},

(38)

to obtain

\Sigma_{n,m}(x)\leq u(x)\sum_{k=1}^{j}\Delta x_{k}\frac{w_{\alpha}(x_{k})}{u(x_{k})}\left|v_{n}^{m}(x,x_{k})\right|,\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{A}_{n},

(39)

with
j
defined in (
4
).

Now, let us recall that the following Marcinkiewicz-type inequality was proved in
[
15
, Lemma 2.5]
for an arbitrary polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{nl}
with
l\in{\mathbb{N}}

\sum_{k=1}^{j}\Delta x_{k}|(Pu)(x_{k})|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\int_{x_{1}}^{4m\rho_{1}}|P(y)|u(y)dy,\quad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(m,P).

(40)

where
0<\rho<\rho_{1}<1
, and
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n)
.

By applying (
40
) to the the polynomial
P(y)=v_{n}^{m}(x,y)\in{\mathbb{P}}_{2n}
and the weights in (
39
), we get

\Sigma_{n,m}(x)\leq{\mathcal{C}}\int_{x_{1}}^{4n\rho_{1}}\frac{w_{\alpha}(t)}{u(t)}\left|v_{n}^{m}(x,t)\right|dt\leq{\mathcal{C}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{w_{\alpha}(t)}{u(t)}\left|v_{n}^{m}(x,t)\right|dt.

(41)

Consequently, by (
23
) and Theorem
2.4
, we deduce

\Sigma_{n,m}(x)\leq{\mathcal{C}}u(x)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{w_{\alpha}(t)}{u(t)}\left|v_{n}^{m}(x,t)\right|dt\leq{\mathcal{C}}\|\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha})\|_{C_{u}}\leq{\mathcal{C}},

i.e. (
37
) holds and the theorem follows.
\diamondsuit

Finally, by the next theorem we state that for suitable weight functions, the VP polynomials
V_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)
, similarly to their continuous version
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)
uniformly converge to
f
in the space
C_{u}
, with a near–best convergence order. Hence, in conclusion, the VP filtered polynomials
V_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)
are simpler to compute than the polynomials
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)
and share with the latter a near–best convergence rate in the spaces
C_{u}
.

Theorem 3.2

Let the weight function
w_{\alpha}(x)=e^{-x}x^{\alpha}
and
u(x)=e^{-\frac{x}{2}}x^{\gamma},
satisfy (
26
). Then, for any
f\in C_{u}
and for all
m\sim n
, we have

\|(f-{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f))u\|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\left(E_{q}(f)_{u}+e^{-An}\|fu\|\right),\quad q=\min\left\{n-m,\ \left\lfloor n\frac{\rho}{(1+\rho)}\right\rfloor\right\},

(42)

with
0<\rho<1
given by (
4
) and
{\mathcal{C}},A>0
independent of
n,m,f.

Moreover, if
m\approx n
then we get

\|f-V_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f)\|_{C_{u}}\sim E_{n}(f)_{u},\qquad\forall f\in C_{u}

(43)

with the constants in
\sim
independent of
n,m,f
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2

Let
P^{*}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n-m}
be the polynomial of best approximation of
f\in C_{u}
, i.e., let

E_{n-m}(f)_{u}=\inf_{P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n-m}}\|f-P\|_{C_{u}}=\|f-P^{*}\|_{C_{u}}.

(44)

Hence, consider the following decomposition

\|(f-{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f))u\|\leq\|[f-P^{*}]u\|+\|{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f-P^{*})u\|+\|[P^{*}-{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},P^{*})]u\|.

By applying Theorem
3.1
and (
44
) we deduce

\|(f-{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},f))u\|\leq{\mathcal{C}}E_{n-m}(f)_{u}+\|[P^{*}-{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},P^{*})]u\|

(45)

In order to estimate the last addendum, we note that using (
22
) and (
10
)–(
11
) we can write

\displaystyle P^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},P^{*},x)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}P^{*}(t)v_{n}^{m}(x,t)w_{\alpha}(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{*}(x_{k})v_{n}^{m}(x,t)\lambda_{n,k}(w_{\alpha})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{*}(x_{k})\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)

On the other hand, by (
32
), we have

V_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},P^{*},x)=\sum_{k=1}^{j}P^{*}(x_{k})\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x),\qquad j=\min\{k\ :\ x_{k}\geq 4n\rho\}

with
0<\rho<1
arbitrarily fixed. Thus, for any
x\geq 0
, it is

\displaystyle|P^{*}(x)-V_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha},P^{*},x)|u(x)=\left|\sum_{k=j+1}^{n}P^{*}(x_{k})\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)\right|u(x)

(46)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=j+1}^{n}|P^{*}(x_{k})||\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)|u(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{k>j}|(P^{*}u)(x_{k})|\left(\sum_{k=j+1}^{n}\left|\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)\right|\frac{u(x)}{u(x_{k})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|P^{*}u\|_{(4n\rho,+\infty)}\left(\max_{x\geq 0}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)\right|\frac{u(x)}{u(x_{k})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|P^{*}u\|_{(4n\rho,+\infty)}\ \|{V}_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha})\|_{C_{u}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\|P^{*}u\|_{(4n\rho,+\infty)}

where in the last two lines we have applied (
34
) and Theorem
3.1
, respectively.

Finally, we recall that for sufficiently large
n
, we have for any fixed positive
0<\rho<1

[
6
, Proposition 2.1]

\|fu\|_{[4n\rho,+\infty)}\leq E_{N}(f)_{u}+{\mathcal{C}}e^{-An}\|fu\|,\qquad\forall f\in C_{u}

(47)

where
N=\left\lfloor\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}n\right\rfloor,
and
{\mathcal{C}},A
positive constants independent of
m,f.

Consequently, by (
44
) and (
47
), we get

\displaystyle\|P^{*}u\|_{(4n\rho,+\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(P^{*}-f)u\|_{(4n\rho,+\infty)}+\|fu\|_{(4n\rho,+\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}E_{n-m}(f)_{u}+E_{M}(f)_{u}+{\mathcal{C}}e^{-An}\|fu\|

and the statement follows combining last bound with (
45
) and (
46
).

5
Conclusions

We have introduced and studied a new truncated polynomial operator
V_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha}):C_{u}\to C_{u}
, involving the values of
f
at only the first
j<<n
zeros of the Laguerre polynomial
p_{n}(w_{\alpha})
. Differently from
L_{n+1}^{*}(w_{\alpha})
, we have proved that the map
V_{n}^{m}(w_{\alpha}):C_{u}\to C_{u}
is uniformly bounded under suitable conditions on the weights
w_{\alpha}
and
u
and in view of this theoretical improvement, the maxima errors attained by VP approximation are better on average than those achieved by Lagrange one. However, the differences between the numerical errors in norm are not so large, since the factor
\log n
does not have a strong impact in the error estimate. The situation is instead quite different about the pointwise errors, especially in approximating smooth functions presenting some peaks or isolated pathologies (see Examples
4
-
5
). Indeed, in such cases the additional parameter
m
can be suitably modulated to reduce the Gibbs phenomenon. We observe a drastic reduction of the oscillations in VP polynomials as
m
increases, and even more w.r.t the Lagrange polynomial, the latter swinging more than anyone. Such reduction is valuable also in Example
6
where
f_{6}
is a bounded variation function. In these cases it is well–known that Lagrange polynomials present overshoots and oscillations not only close to the singularities, but also in the smooth part of the function. The graphics in Fig.
7
show how the Gibbs phenomenon can be reduced by VP polynomials, for suitable values of the parameter
\theta\in(0,1).

We conclude observing that the range where
\alpha,\gamma
vary for the boundedness of VP polynomials, is partially overlapping with that the norm of the Lagrange operator logarithmically behaves (compare (
8
) and (
26
)), and in any case the VP range is larger than the Lagrange one. However, numerical results (see Fig.
11
) allow us to conjecture that the range determined by Thm.
3.1
about the parameters
\alpha,\gamma
could actually be stretched. More precisely, we conjecture that the hypothesis
\max\{0,\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\}<\gamma\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{5}{4}
could replace (
26
).

Muckenhoupt-type weights and the intrinsic structure in Bessel Setting
Ji Li, Chong-Wei Liang, Fred Yu-Hsiang Lin, Chun-Yen Shen

Abstract




Abstract:
Fix
\lambda>-1/2
and
\lambda\not=0
. Consider
the Bessel operator (introduced by Muckenhoupt–Stein)

\triangle_{\lambda}:=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda}{x}\frac{d}{dx}
on
\mathbb{R_{+}}:=(0,\infty)
with
dm_{\lambda}(x):=x^{2\lambda}dx
and
dx
the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. In this paper, we study the Muckenhoupt-type weights which reveal the intrinsic structure in this Bessel setting along the line of Muckenhoupt–Stein and Andersen–Kerman. Besides, exploiting more properties of the weights
A_{p,\lambda}
introduced by Andersen–Kerman, we introduce a new class
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
such that the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function is bounded on the weighted
L^{p}_{w}
space if and only if
w
is in
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
. Moreover, along the line of Coifman–Rochberg–Weiss, we investigate the commutator
[b,R_{\lambda}]
with
R_{\lambda}:=\frac{d}{dx}(\triangle_{\lambda})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
to be the Bessel Riesz transform. We show that for
w\in A_{p,\lambda}
, the commutator
[b,R_{\lambda}]
is bounded on weighted
L^{p}_{w}
if and only if
b
is in the BMO space associated with
\triangle_{\lambda}
.

Introduction and Statement of Main Results

1.1
Background

In 1965, Muckenhoupt and Stein in
[
MS65
]
introduced the harmonic function theory associated with the Bessel operator
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
, defined by,

{\triangle_{\lambda}}:=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda}{x}\frac{d}{dx},\quad\lambda>0.

They developed a theory in the setting of

{\triangle_{\lambda}}
which parallels the classical one associated with the usual Laplacian
\triangle
. Results on the
{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,dm_{\lambda})}
-boundedness of conjugate
functions and fractional integrals associated with
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
were
obtained, where
p\in[1,\infty)
,
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:=(0,\infty)
and
{dm_{\lambda}}(x):=x^{2\lambda}\,dx
. Moreover, they also studied the particular Calderón–Zygmund operator associated with
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
: the Bessel Riesz transform
R_{\lambda}:=\frac{d}{dx}(\triangle_{\lambda})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
Since then, the Bessel context has been extensively studied, including the theory of Hardy and BMO spaces, Bessel Riesz transforms and the commutator estimates;
see for example,
[
AK81
]
,
[
Bet+10a
]
,
[
Bet+07
]
,
[
BFS09
]
,
[
Bet+10
]
,
[
Ker78
]
,
[
Vil08
]
,
[
YY11
]
,
[
Duo+17
]
,
[
Duo+18
]
,
[
BCR15
]
,
[
Bet+20
]
,
[
Che+22
]
and the references therein.

It is worth pointing out that in many subsequent works, the triple
(\mathbb{R}_{+},|\cdot|,{dm_{\lambda}})
is treated as a space of homogeneous type in the sense of Coifman and Weiss
[
CW77
]
. Together with the particular properties associated with the Bessel operator
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
and new techniques, classical results on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
have their extensions in the Bessel setting.

However, in some particular cases, this point of view may not reflect the intrinsic structure of the Bessel operator
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
. For example, when we treat
(\mathbb{R}_{+},|\cdot|,{dm_{\lambda}})
as a space of homogeneous type, the Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
weight is defined naturally following the classical setting, and the weighted
L^{p}
norm is

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}:=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x){dm_{\lambda}}(x)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad 1<p<\infty,\quad w\in A_{p}.

(1.1)

This is general enough to cover many more settings but does not fully capture the specific intrinsic structure of the Bessel Riesz transform.

In
1981
, Andersen and Kerman
[
AK81
]
introduced a new Muckenhoupt-type weights
A_{p,\lambda},\ 1<p<\infty,
associated with the Bessel operator
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
, defined as follows:

w\in A_{p,\lambda}
if there exists a constant
C>0
, such that for all
0<a<b<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{a}^{b}t^{p}w(t)dt\bigg)\bigg(\int_{a}^{b}t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\bigg)^{p-1}<C(b^{2(\lambda+1)}-a^{2(\lambda+1)})^{p}.

(1.2)

For
w\in A_{p,\lambda}
, the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)
norm is defined as follows

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}:=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(1.3)

Then they revealed the intrinsic structure of the Bessel Riesz transform
R_{\lambda}
by proving that: for
\lambda>-{\frac{1}{2}},\neq 0
,
1<p<\infty
, and a nonnegative measurable function
w
,

R_{\lambda}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)

if and only if

w\in A_{p,\lambda}
.

1.2
Aims and Questions

The aim of this paper is to continue the line of Muckenhoupt–Stein
[
MS65
]
and Andersen–Kerman
[
AK81
]
to study the Muckenhoupt-type weights in the Bessel Setting. Analogous to the classical Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
theory, the characterization of Muckenhoupt-type weights via suitable maximal function is the first main concern. Thus, the first natural question that we focus on is as follows.

Question 1:
What is the suitable Hardy–Littlewood maximal function to characterize the Muckenhoupt-type weights in the Bessel Setting?

Next, we would like to mention that in a recent result by Duong, Wick, Yang ,and the first author
[
Duo+17
]
, the
{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,dm_{\lambda})}
-boundedness of the commutator

[b,R_{\lambda}]
was fully characterized via using the standard BMO space on the triple
(\mathbb{R}_{+},|\cdot|,{dm_{\lambda}})
. This generalized the renowned result of Coifman–Rochberg–Weiss
[
CRW76
]
and bypassed the use of classical Fourier transform as it is not applicable in the Bessel setting. Moreover, the Bloom type two weight commutator was also studied very recently in
[
Duo+21
]
, via treating
(\mathbb{R}_{+},|\cdot|,{dm_{\lambda}})
as a space of homogeneous type again and using the weighted
L^{p}
as in (
1.1
).
Thus, along the line of Coifman–Rochberg–Weiss
[
CRW76
]
and the recent progression, the second natural question that we focus is on the weighted
L^{p}
estimate (via (
1.3
)) of the commutator

[b,R_{\lambda}]
under this new Muckenhoupt-type weights in the Bessel Setting.

Question 2:

Can we fully characterise the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)
-boundedness (via (
1.3
)) of the commutator
[b,R_{\lambda}]
for
w\in A_{p,\lambda}
? That is, for
p\in(1,\infty)
and for a suitable
\rm{BMO}
space associated with
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
,

\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\sim\|b\|_{\rm BMO}\quad?

In this paper, we give full-fledged answers to these questions, respectively.

1.3
Statement of main results

Our first observation on the inner structure of the Anderson–Kerman weight class is as follows.
Regarding the definition of
A_{p,\lambda}
as in (
1.2
) (especially from the right-hand side of (
1.2
)), we see that
the underlying measure is in fact
d\nu_{\lambda}(x):=x^{2\lambda+1}dx
. Hence, a natural definition of maximal function is associated with
d\nu_{\lambda}(x)
but not the usual one associated with
dm_{\lambda}(x)=x^{2\lambda}dx
. That is,

Definition 1.1

(
\lambda
-Maximal function)
.

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}(x):=\sup_{B\ni x}\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|f(y)|d\nu_{\lambda}(y).

To address
Question 1
, we may ask whether
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)

if and only if

w\in A_{p,\lambda}
.

Surprisingly, it turns out that the right class for the maximal function
M_{\lambda}
to be bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)

is another
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
defined as below.

Definition 1.2
.

For a non-negative measurable function
w
,
w\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all intervals
B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
we have

\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}w(t)dt\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{(2\lambda+1)p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\bigg)^{p-1}<C.

We now give a positive answer to
Question 1
.

Theorem 1.3

(
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)
-Boundedness for
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
)
.

Let
w
be a non-negative measurable function. Then for all
1<p<\infty

w\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow\|\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)},

The key to obtaining the above result is the following observation (as well as the related approach and techniques):
The following inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}f)(t)^{p}w(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}f)(t)^{p}\frac{w(t)}{\nu_{\lambda}(t)}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f(t)|^{p}\frac{w(t)}{\nu_{\lambda}(t)}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f(t)|^{p}w(t)dt

holds
if and only if the weight
\frac{w(t)}{\nu_{\lambda}(t)}
satisfies the classical
A_{p}
condition with respect to the doubling measure
\nu_{\lambda}
(when
\lambda>-1/2
). That is,
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all intervals
B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}

\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}\frac{w(t)}{\nu_{\lambda(t)}}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}\Big(\frac{w(t)}{\nu_{\lambda}(t)}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\bigg)^{p-1}<C.

Notice that

\bigg(\frac{w(t)}{\nu_{\lambda}(t)}\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\cdot\nu_{\lambda}(t)=\nu_{\lambda}(t)^{p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}=t^{(2\lambda+1)p^{\prime}}\cdot w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Equivalently,
w\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
.

Now, as a natural and further question, one may ask: Do
A_{p,\lambda}
and
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
enjoy the same characterization for the boundedness of
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
? Or are they actually the same class but just have different expressions?

The answer is
No
, that is,
A_{p,\lambda}
and
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
are different classes of weights. We will also exploit the structures for these two classes, respectively. Roughly speaking,
A_{p,\lambda}
is the suitable class associated with Bessel Riesz transform
R_{\lambda}
as it captures the intrinsic behavior for the kernel of
R_{\lambda}
, while
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
is the natural one associated with the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
since it can be viewed as a special case for the classic
A_{p}
weight on homogeneous space.

Proposition 1.4

(Structure of
A_{p,\lambda}
)
.

Let
\lambda>-\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda\not=0
. Then for
w\in A_{p,\lambda}
, we have the following properties

(1)

[w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}:=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{p}w(t)dt\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}w(t)^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}dt\bigg)^{p-1}\geq 1.

(2)
Define the following measures

\displaystyle d\mu(t):=t^{p}w(t)dt\quad\text{and}\quad d\sigma(t):=t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}{w(t)}^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}.

Then both of
d\mu
and
d\sigma
are doubling measures on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

(3)

(Dual Factor)

w\in A_{p,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow t^{(2\lambda-1)p^{\prime}}{w(t)}^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\in A_{p^{\prime},\lambda}.

(4)

(Nesting Structure)

\forall\lambda>-\frac{1}{2},\lambda\not=0,1\leq p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty\implies A_{p_{1},\lambda}\subset A_{p_{2},\lambda},

and

\forall p\in(1,\infty),\ \lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}>-1/2,\ \lambda_{2},\ \lambda_{1}\neq 0\implies A_{p,\lambda_{1}}\subset A_{p,\lambda_{2}}.

Proposition 1.5

(Relationships among
A_{p}
,
A_{p,\lambda}
and

\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
)
.

Let
w(t):=t^{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then

(1)

w\in A_{p}\Longleftrightarrow-1<\alpha<p-1.

(2)

w\in A_{p,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow-1-p<\alpha<p-1+2\lambda.

(3)

w\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow-1<\alpha<p-1+(2\lambda+1)p.

We now turn to the estimate of the commutator. Given a measurable function
b(x)
, we can define the commutator
[b,R_{\lambda}]
of the Bessel Riesz transform by

[b,R_{\lambda}](f)(x):=b(x)R_{\lambda}(f)(x)-R_{\lambda}(bf)(x).

The boundedness of commutators has a long history and has many important applications, for instance, the composition of differential operators. It is a well-known fact that for general Calderón–Zyumund operators
T
, the boundedness of commutators
[b,T]
is equivalent to
b\in{\rm BMO}
. Thus we now define the
\rm{BMO}
functions in our setting which will characterize the boundedness of
[b,R_{\lambda}]
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)
for
w\in A_{p,\lambda}
.

Definition 1.6

(Sharp
\lambda
-maximal function)
.

Let
w
be a non-negative, measurable function on
\mathbb{R_{+}}
, and
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R_{+}},\nu_{\lambda})
. Define the sharp
\lambda
-maximal function by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{\lambda}f(x)=\sup_{B\ni x,B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|f(t)-f_{B,\lambda}|d\nu_{\lambda}(t),

where

\displaystyle f_{B,\lambda}:=\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}f(t)\,d\nu_{\lambda}(t),\quad\text{and}\quad B=(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(1.4)

We say that
f
has
\lambda
-bounded mean oscillation if
\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{\lambda}f\in L^{\infty}.

Remark 1.7
.

Throughout the paper, we refer
\nu_{\lambda}(t)
to be
t^{1+2\lambda}
where
\lambda>\frac{-1}{2}
and
\lambda\not=0
.

Definition 1.8

(The space
{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R_{+}})
)
.

The space of
\lambda
-bounded mean oscillation on
(\mathbb{R_{+}},d\nu_{\lambda})
is given by

{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R_{+}}):=\{f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R_{+}},d\nu_{\lambda}):\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{\lambda}f\in L^{\infty}\},

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}}:=\|\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(1.5)

We now give a positive answer to
Question 2
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Suppose
b\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R_{+}},d\nu_{\lambda})
,
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p,\lambda}
.

If
b\in{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R_{+}})
, then

\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R_{+}},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R_{+}},w)}\lesssim\|b\|_{BMO_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

Conversely, if
\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}<\infty
,
then
b\in{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with

\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\lesssim\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}.

It is natural to ask what is the relationship between our
\rm{BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and the well-known
{\rm BMO}_{\Delta_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, which was used before

[
Duo+17
]
for unweighted estimate of
[b,R_{\lambda}]
, defined by

{\rm BMO}_{\Delta_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R_{+}}):=\{f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R_{+}},dm_{\lambda}):\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\Delta_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R_{+}})}<\infty\},

equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\Delta_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R_{+}})}:=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|dm_{\lambda}(x),

where

f_{B}=\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}f(x)dm_{\lambda}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad B=(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Indeed, they are equivalent in the norm sense.

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
\lambda>-1/2
and
\lambda\neq 0
,

{\rm BMO}_{\Delta_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R_{+}})
coincides with
{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

and they have equivalent norms.

2.1
A_{p,\lambda}
Class

In
[
AK81
]
, a class of weight
A_{p,\lambda}
is discovered by Andersen and Kerman who showed the following Theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)
-Boundedness for Bessel Riesz Transform
R_{\lambda}
)
.

For all
1<p<\infty
,
\lambda>-\frac{1}{2},\neq 0
, the following statemennts are equivalent:

(1).

w\in A_{p,\lambda}.

(2).

\|R_{\lambda}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\underset{p,\lambda}{\lesssim}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}.

(3).

\|R_{\lambda}(f)\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\underset{p,\lambda}{\lesssim}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}.

For the endpoint case, that is
p=1
, they also proved that

w\in A_{1,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow\|R_{\lambda}(f)\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\underset{\lambda}{\lesssim}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}.

It is worth pointing out that in their paper, one of the intriguing parts is that in the classical
A_{p}
theorem, the
A_{1}
class can be obtained from
A_{p},\>p>1
by letting
p\rightarrow 1^{+}
. However the
A_{1,\lambda}
case is not the limiting of
A_{p,\lambda}
as
p\rightarrow 1^{+}
.

2.2
Space of Bounded Mean Oscillation

For the equivalency of some weighted
\rm{BMO}
norms, we refer
[
Ho11
]
to the readers. In the following, we first prove the John–Nirenberg inequality in our
\rm{BMO}
class. The proof is essentially the same as the classical proof. For the sake of completeness of the paper, we give the details here.
Define the average associated with the doubling measure
\nu
by

f_{B,\nu}:=\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}f(t)d\nu(t),\quad\text{for~each~interval}\quad B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+},

and

\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}:=\|\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{\nu}f\|_{L^{\infty}},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{\nu}f(x)=\sup_{B\ni x,B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}|f(t)-f_{B,\nu}|d\nu(t)

is the sharp maximal function with respect to the doubling measure
\nu.

Lemma 2.2

(John–Nirenberg Inequality)
.

Let
\nu
be a doubling measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with doubling constant
c>1
. Then we have for all interval
B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
and all
t>0

\nu\left(\left\{x\in B:|f(x)-f_{B,\nu}|>t\right\}\right)\leq e\cdot\nu(B)e^{\frac{-At}{\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}}}

where
A=(e\cdot c)^{-1}
.

Proof.

First, we normalize
\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}=1
. Fix a
B=B^{(0)}
, we perform Calderón–Zygmund decomposition at height
\beta
to
f-f_{B,\nu}
on
B
to get a collection of intervals
\{B_{j}^{(1)}\}_{j}
. Then the process is repeated again to
f-f_{B_{j}^{(1)},\nu}
for each
B_{j}^{(1)}
to get a second collection
\{B_{l}^{(1)}\}_{l}
. Continuing this process and let
\{B_{j}^{(k)}\}_{j}
be the collection of intervals in the
k
-th generation. Then we have the following properties

\displaystyle(1).\quad\beta<\frac{1}{\nu(B_{j}^{(k)})}\int_{B_{j}^{(k)}}|f(x)-f_{B_{j^{\prime}}^{k-1},\nu}|d\nu(x)\leq c\cdot\beta,

\displaystyle(2).\quad|f_{B_{j}^{k},\nu}-f_{B_{j^{\prime}}^{k-1},\nu}|\leq c\cdot\beta,

\displaystyle(3).\quad\sum_{j}\nu(B_{j}^{(k)})\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{j^{\prime}}\nu(B_{j^{\prime}}^{(k-1)}),

\displaystyle(4).\quad|f-f_{B_{j^{\prime}}^{(k-1)},\nu}|\leq\beta\quad a.e.\quad on\quad B_{j^{\prime}}^{(k-1)}\setminus\bigcup_{j}B_{j}^{(k)},

where
B_{j^{\prime}}^{k-1}
is the parent of
B_{j}^{(k)}
.

From
(3)
, we immediately have

\sum_{j}\nu(B_{j}^{(k)})\leq\beta^{-k}\nu(B).

On the other hand, by
(3)
and
(4)
, we have

|f-f_{B,\nu}|\leq c\cdot k\beta\quad\text{on}\quad B\setminus\bigcup_{j}B_{j}^{(k)}.

Hence, we have

\left\{x\in B:|f(x)-f_{B,,\nu}|>c\cdot k\beta\right\}\subset\bigcup_{j}B_{j}^{(k)}.

For
t
in the range
ck\beta<t\leq c(k+1)\beta
for some
k>0
, we have

\displaystyle\nu\left(\left\{x\in B:|f(x)-f_{B,\nu}|>t\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\nu\left(\left\{x\in B:|f(x)-f_{B,,\nu}|>ck\beta\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\nu(B_{j}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-k}\nu(B)=e^{-k\cdot\log\beta}\cdot\nu(B).

Notice that
-k<1-\frac{t}{c\beta}
and take
\beta=e
, then we have the desired result.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Suppose
f\in{\rm BMO}_{\nu}
. Then we have the following two results.

(1)
There is a constant
C_{s}<+\infty
such that for any interval
B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

|s|<(ce\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}})^{-1}\implies\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}e^{s|f(x)-f_{B,\nu}|}d\nu\leq C_{s}:=1+e\cdot\frac{A^{-1}\cdot|s|\cdot\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}}{1-A^{-1}\cdot|s|\cdot\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}}.

(2)
For all
1<p<\infty,

\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}e^{sf(x)}d\nu(x)\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}e^{\frac{-1}{p-1}\cdot sf(x)}d\nu(x)\bigg)^{p-1}\leq C_{s}^{p}.

.

Proof.

(1)
By layer-cake representation, we have the following identity

\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}e^{h}d\nu=1+\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t}\nu\left(\left\{x\in B:|h(x)|>t\right\}\right)dt.

Then we plug in
h=s|f-f_{B,\nu}|
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}e^{s|f-f_{B,\nu}|}d\nu

\displaystyle=1+\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t}\nu\left(\left\{x\in B:|s||f(x)-f_{B,\nu}|>t\right\}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{t}\cdot e\cdot\nu(B)e^{\frac{-At}{|s|\cdot\|f\|{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}}}dt

\displaystyle=1+e\cdot\int^{\infty}_{0}\exp\left\{t\left(1-\frac{1}{ce|s|\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}}\right)\right\}dt

\displaystyle=1+e\cdot\frac{ce\cdot|s|\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}}{1-ce\cdot|s|\cdot\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}}<\infty.

(2)
From above, we have for all interval
B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}

|s|<\frac{A}{\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}}\implies\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}e^{s(f(x)-f_{B,\nu})}d\nu\leq C_{s}

and

\left|\frac{s}{p-1}\right|<\frac{A}{\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}}\implies\left(\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}e^{\frac{-1}{p-1}s(f(x)-f_{B,\nu})}d\nu\right)^{p-1}\leq C^{p-1}_{s}.

Multiplying them together, then we have

|s|<\frac{A}{\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}}}\cdot\min\{1,p-1\}\implies\sup_{B}\left(\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}e^{sf(x)}d\nu(x)\right)\left(\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}e^{\frac{-1}{p-1}sf(x)}d\nu\right)^{p-1}\leq C^{p}_{s}.

∎

and Properties of

(1)

[w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}\geq 1.

Proof.

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{1+2\lambda}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t\,w(t)^{1\over p}\,t^{2\lambda}\,w(t)^{-{1\over p}}dt

\displaystyle\leq[w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}^{1\over p}.

Thus,
we have

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\geq 1.

∎

(2)
The following two measures are both doubling on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
:

d\mu(t)=t^{p}w(t)dt,

and

d\sigma(t)=t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-{p^{\prime}\over p}}dt.

Proof.

Recall that a new average of
f
over interval

\displaystyle f_{B,\lambda}:=\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}f(t)\,t^{1+2\lambda}\,dt,

which implies that

\displaystyle(f_{B,\lambda})^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|f(t)|\ t^{1+2\lambda}\,dt\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|f(t)|\ t\,w(t)^{1\over p}\cdot t^{2\lambda}\,w(t)^{-{1\over p}}dt\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)^{p}}\left(\int_{B}|f(t)|^{p}t^{p}w(t)dt\right)\left(\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-{p^{\prime}\over p}}dt\right)^{p\over p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{B}|f(t)|^{p}t^{p}w(t)dt\right)\cdot\left(\int_{B}t^{p}w(t)dt\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{p}w(t)dt\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-{p^{\prime}\over p}}dt\right)^{p\over p^{\prime}}

Thus, if we take
f=\chi_{B}
and put
\eta B

B

\displaystyle\int_{\eta B}t^{p}w(t)dt\leq C_{p}[w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}\eta^{(2\lambda+2)p}\int_{B}t^{p}w(t)dt.

This shows that

is a doubling measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. One can show that

is a doubling measure as well.
∎

(3)
(Dual Factor)

w\in A_{p,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow t^{(2\lambda-1)p^{\prime}}w^{-1\over p-1}\in A_{p^{\prime},\lambda}.

Proof.

Assume that
w\in A_{p,\lambda}
, then we have for some
C>0
such that for all interval
B:=(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{p}w(t)dt\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-{p^{\prime}\over p}}dt\right)^{p\over p^{\prime}}<C.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}t^{p^{\prime}}t^{(2\lambda-1)p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-{1\over p-1}}dt\right)\cdot\left(\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p}(t^{(2\lambda-1)p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-{1\over p-1}})^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}-1}}dt\right)^{p^{\prime}-1},

which is equal to

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\right)\cdot\left(\int_{B}t^{p}w(t)dt\right)^{1\over p-1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{p^{\prime}}t^{(2\lambda-1)p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-{1\over p-1}}dt\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p}(t^{(2\lambda-1)p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-{1\over p-1}})^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}-1}}dt\right)^{p^{\prime}-1}\leq C.

The other direction is similar.
∎

(4)
(Nesting structure)

\forall\lambda>-\frac{1}{2},\neq 0,1\leq p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty\implies A_{p_{1},\lambda}\subset A_{p_{2},\lambda},

\forall p\in(1,\infty),\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}>-\frac{1}{2},\neq 0\implies A_{p,\lambda_{1}}\subset A_{p,\lambda_{2}}.

Suppose

\displaystyle\leq b^{{p_{2}}-{p_{1}}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}t^{p_{1}}w(t)dt\right)\cdot\left(\int_{a}^{b}t^{2\lambda{p_{2}}^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{{p_{2}}-1}}dt\right)^{p_{2}-1}

\displaystyle=b^{{p_{2}}-{p_{1}}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}t^{p_{1}}w(t)dt\right)\cdot\left(\int_{a}^{b}\left(t^{2\lambda{p_{1}\over p_{2}-1}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{{p_{2}}-1}}\right)t^{2\lambda{{p_{2}-p_{1}}\over{p_{2}-1}}}dt\right)^{p_{2}-1},

Applying Holder’s inequality with exponent

and
{p_{2}-1}\over{p_{2}-p_{1}}
to the second term, and hence

\displaystyle\left(\int_{a}^{b}t^{p_{2}}w(t)dt\right)\cdot\left(\int_{a}^{b}t^{2\lambda{p_{2}}^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{{p_{2}}-1}}dt\right)^{p_{2}-1}

\displaystyle\leq Cb^{{p_{2}}-{p_{1}}}(b^{2(\lambda+1)}-a^{2(\lambda+1)})^{p_{1}-1}

\displaystyle\cdot(b^{2\lambda+1}-a^{2\lambda+1})^{p_{2}-p_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(b^{2(\lambda+1)}-a^{2(\lambda+1)})^{p_{2}},

For the second part, we let

\displaystyle\leq C(b^{2(\lambda_{2}+1)}-a^{2(\lambda_{2}+1)})^{p}.

Thus,

.

4
Proof of Proposition
1.5

The main point of this proposition is to show that these three classes of weights,
A_{p}
,
A_{p,\lambda}
and
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
in general are different and especially
A_{p,\lambda}
and
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
are not contained in each other. Among explicit examples, we consider the simple but fundamental power weights.
Let
w(x)=|x|^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha
.

(1)

w\in A_{p}\Longleftrightarrow-1<\alpha<p-1.

First, we need
\alpha>-1
to make
w
to be doubling. Now given a ball
B=B(x_{0},R)
, where
x_{0}
is the center and
R
is the radius, we consider the following two cases:

•

Case(1)
:
|x_{0}|
dominates.

In the first case, for those
|x-x_{0}|<R
, we have
|x|\sim|x_{0}|
, hence

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int|x|^{\alpha}dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int|x|^{{-\alpha}\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\right)^{p-1}\sim|x_{0}|^{\alpha}|x_{0}|^{-\alpha}\sim 1.

•

Case(2)
:
R
dominates.

In the second case and by the doubling property, we may replace
B(x_{0},R)
by
B(0,5R)
and compute the following

\left(\frac{1}{R}\int_{-R}^{R}x^{\alpha}dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{R}\int_{-R}^{R}x^{-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}dx\right)^{p-1}.

If we want the above term to be finite uniformly in
R
, we only need to check both terms are integrable near
0
. Hence this gives the following conditions which give the range for
\alpha
.

\alpha>-1,\quad-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}>-1.

(2)

w\in A_{p,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow-1-p<\alpha<p-1+2\lambda.

The computations are similar to the above. We simply compute the following

\left(\frac{1}{R}\int_{-R}^{R}x^{\alpha+p}dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{R}\int_{-R}^{R}x^{2\lambda p^{\prime}-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}dx\right)^{p-1}

that gives the range for
\alpha
which is

\alpha+p>-1,\quad 2\lambda p^{\prime}-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}>-1.

(3)

w\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow-1<\alpha<p-1+(2\lambda+1)p.

Again, we compute the following

\left(\frac{1}{R}\int_{-R}^{R}x^{\alpha}dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{R}\int_{-R}^{R}x^{(2\lambda+1)p^{\prime}-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}dx\right)^{p-1},

which gives

\alpha>-1,\quad(2\lambda+1)p^{\prime}-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}>-1.

Moreover, these three classes coincide locally. Precisely, let us define a local version as the following.

Definition 4.1

(
A^{\text{loc}}_{p,\lambda}
,
\widetilde{A}^{\text{loc}}_{p,\lambda}
and
A^{\text{loc}}_{p,+}
)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\lambda>-\frac{1}{2},\neq 0.

(1)

w\in A^{\text{loc}}_{p,\lambda}
if there exist
C,k=k(w)>0
such that for all
B:=(a,b)\subset(0,\infty)
with
b\leq ka
,

\bigg({1\over\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{p}w(t)dt\bigg)\bigg({1\over\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\bigg)^{p-1}\leq C.

(2)

w\in\widetilde{A}^{\text{loc}}_{p,\lambda}
if there exist
C,k=k(w)>0
such that for all
B:=(a,b)\subset(0,\infty)
with
b\leq ka
,

\bigg({1\over\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}w(t)dt\bigg)\bigg({1\over\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{(2\lambda+1)p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\bigg)^{p-1}\leq C.

(3)

w\in A^{\text{loc}}_{p,+}
if there exist
C,k=k(w)>0
such that for all
(a,b)\subset(0,\infty)
with
b\leq ka
,

\bigg({1\over{b-a}}\int_{a}^{b}w(t)dt\bigg)\bigg({1\over{b-a}}\int_{a}^{b}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\bigg)^{p-1}\leq C.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
we have

A^{\text{loc}}_{p,+}=\widetilde{A}^{\text{loc}}_{p,\lambda}=A^{\text{loc}}_{p.\lambda}.

Indeed, we observe that for all
0<a<b<\infty
,

a^{p}\leq{{\int_{a}^{b}t^{p}w(t)dt}\over{\int_{a}^{b}w(t)dt}}\leq b^{p},\quad({1\over b})^{p}\leq{{\int_{a}^{b}w(t)dt}\over{\int_{a}^{b}t^{p}w(t)dt}}\leq({1\over a})^{p}

and

{1\over b^{p}}\leq{\left(\int_{a}^{b}t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\right)^{p-1}\over{\left(\int_{a}^{b}t^{(2\lambda+1)p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\right)^{p-1}}}\leq{1\over a^{p}},\quad a^{p}\leq{\left(\int_{a}^{b}t^{(2\lambda+1)p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\right)^{p-1}\over{\left(\int_{a}^{b}t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\right)^{p-1}}}\leq b^{p}.

Therefore from the assumption we immediately have

\widetilde{A}^{\text{loc}}_{p,\lambda}=A^{\text{loc}}_{p,\lambda}.

Likewise, we also have

A^{\text{loc}}_{p,+}=\widetilde{A}^{\text{loc}}_{p,\lambda}.

It might be worth pointing out that these simple properties for a local version of weights can be helpful when we study the boundedness of operators since we have many well-established results for the classical
A_{p}
weights.

5
Proof of Weak-Type Estimate on
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}

Our ultimate goal for the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
is to prove the weighted strong-type boundedness. Before doing so, we show the weak-type boundedness first.

Proof.

To begin with, we need to show that

w\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow w(\{x\in\mathbb{R_{+}}:\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}f(x)>\alpha\})\leq\frac{C}{\alpha^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R_{+}}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx,\forall\alpha>0.

(\Longleftarrow)

Let
B:=(a,b)\subseteq\mathbb{R_{+}}
be an interval such that

\int_{B}|f(x)|dx>0.

For all
0<\alpha<|f|_{B,\lambda}
, we have

B\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R_{+}}:{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(f\,\mathbbm{1}_{B})(x)>\alpha\}\implies w(B)\leq\frac{C}{\alpha^{p}}\cdot\int_{B}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx,

and hence

\displaystyle w(B)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)|x^{2\lambda+1}dx\right)^{p}\leq C\int_{B}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx.

Take
f(x)=w(x)^{1-p^{{}^{\prime}}}x^{{2\lambda+1}\over{p-1}}
, then we have

\left({1\over\nu_{\lambda}((a,b))}\int_{a}^{b}w(t)dt\right)\left({1\over\nu_{\lambda}((a,b))}\int_{a}^{b}t^{(2\lambda+1)p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\right)^{p-1}\leq C.

(\Longrightarrow)

Conversely,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)|x^{2\lambda+1}dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1\over{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}x^{{p^{\prime}}(2\lambda+1)}w(x)^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}dx\right)^{1\over{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1\over{p}}\left(\frac{w(B)}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\right)^{-1\over{p}},

the second inequality holds because of
w\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda},

and hence

w(B)\cdot\left({1\over{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}}\int_{B}|f(x)|x^{2\lambda+1}dx\right)^{p}\leq C\int_{B}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx.

By the classical covering method (
d\nu_{\lambda}
is a doubling measure), we have

\displaystyle w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}f(x)>\alpha\})

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{B}w(B)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{B}(|f|_{B,\lambda})^{-p}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq{C\over\alpha^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx,

where the first inequality follows from that
w
is doubling.
∎

Next, to prove the strong-type boundedness, we need the reverse structure of
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}.

6
Dyadic Maximal
\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda}
, Reverse Hölder Inequality on
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
, and Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this and the next section, we investigate the reverse structure by following the classical method. We prove the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition with respect to the doubling measure
\nu_{\lambda}
, and use this decomposition to show the reverse type inequality.

Definition 6.1

(Dyadic Maximal Function)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{k}
be a family of dyadic intervals on
\mathbb{R_{+}}
with side length
2^{-k}
, and
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R_{+}},\nu_{\lambda})
, the dyadic maximal function of
f
is defined by

\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda}f(x):=\sup_{k}|\mathbb{E}_{k,\lambda}f(x)|,

where

\mathbb{E}_{k,\lambda}f(x):=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{D}_{k}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|f|d\nu_{\lambda}\bigg)\mathbbm{1}_{B}(x).

Lemma 6.2

(Stopping Time Lemma)
.

The dyadic maximal function is of weak
(1,1)
, that is

\|\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{1,\infty}(R_{+},\nu_{\lambda})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(R_{+},\nu_{\lambda})}.

Proof.

Given any
\alpha>0
, we consider the set

\Omega_{k}:=\left\{\mathbb{E}_{k,\lambda}f>\alpha,\mathbb{E}_{i,\lambda}f\leq\alpha,\forall i<k\right\},\quad\forall k.

Then the level set of dyadic maximal function can be decomposed by

\{\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda}f>\alpha\}=\bigsqcup_{k}\Omega_{k},

and hence

\displaystyle\nu_{\lambda}(\{\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda}f>\alpha\})=\sum_{k}\nu_{\lambda}(\Omega_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{k}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{k,\lambda}f(x)d\nu_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{k}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f|d\nu_{\lambda}.

Thus, the dyadic maximal function is of weak
(1,1)
.
∎

By the stopping time lemma above, we have the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition with the doubling constant
4^{\lambda+1}
in this setting:

Theorem 6.3

(Calderón–Zygmund Decomposition)
.

For all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R_{+}},\nu_{\lambda})
and let
\alpha>0
be given. Then there exists a collection of disjoint dyadic intervals
B_{k}
such that

|f(x)|\leq\alpha,\quad\forall x\notin\bigcup_{k}B_{k};\quad\nu_{\lambda}\bigg(\bigcup_{k}B_{k}\bigg)\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{1}(R_{+},\nu_{\lambda})}

and

\alpha<\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B_{k})}\int_{B_{k}}|f|d\nu_{\lambda}\leq 4^{\lambda+1}\alpha.

Theorem 6.4

(Reverse Type Inequality)
.

Let
w\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
,
1<p<\infty
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for any interval
B\subset\mathbb{R_{+}}
,

To prove this result, we first need to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let

For

, the inequality always holds:

\displaystyle w(B)\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|f|d\nu_{\lambda}\right)^{p}\leq C\cdot\int_{B}|f|^{p}dw,

which implies that

w(B)\left(\frac{\nu_{\lambda}(S)}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\right)^{p}\leq C\cdot w(S),

If we replace
S
by

\displaystyle w(B)\left(1-\frac{\nu_{\lambda}(S)}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\right)^{p}\leq C\cdot(w(B)-w(S)).

Since
\nu_{\lambda}(S)\leq\alpha\nu_{\lambda}(B)

\displaystyle w(B)(1-\alpha)^{p}\leq C\left(w(B)-w(S)\right)\Longleftrightarrow w(S)\leq\frac{C-(1-\alpha)^{p}}{C}w(B),

which gives us the desired result with
\beta=1-C^{-1}(1-\alpha)^{p}\in(0,1)

Proof.

(Reverse Type Inequality)
Fix an interval

and consider an increasing sequence

By Calderón–Zygmund decomposition, for all
k

\{B^{k}_{m}\}_{m}

\displaystyle w(x)\nu_{\lambda}(x)^{-1}\leq\alpha_{k},\ \forall x\notin\bigcup_{m}B^{k}_{m};\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{k}<\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B^{k}_{m})}\int_{B^{k}_{m}}w\nu^{-1}_{\lambda}d\nu_{\lambda}\leq 4^{\lambda+1}\alpha_{k}.

Corollary 6.6

(Ratio Testing)
.

Theorem 7.5

For any
\varepsilon>0

\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\Delta_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R_{+}})}\subset{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Next, we prove
{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\subset{\rm BMO}_{\Delta_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R_{+}})
.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle{1\over m_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|b(t)-b_{B}|dm_{\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle\leq{2\over m_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|b(t)-b_{B,\lambda}|dm_{\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle\leq{1\over m_{\lambda}(B)}\left(\int_{B}|b(t)-b_{B,\lambda}|^{2}W(t)dm_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{1\over 2}\left(\int_{B}W(t)^{-1}dm_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{1\over 2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left({1\over W(B)}\int_{B}|b(t)-b_{B,\lambda}|^{2}W(t)dm_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{1\over 2},

where the last inequality follows from the
A_{2}
condition. Finally using John–Nirenberg inequality, we have

\left({1\over W(B)}\int_{B}|b(t)-b_{B,\lambda}|^{2}W(t)dm_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{1\over 2}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R_{+}})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

With the development of the new average and the new weighted class,
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
and
A_{p,\lambda}
, we can characterize the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)
-boundedness of the commutator
[b,R_{\lambda}]
. Our method in showing the upper bound is ignited by the powerful Cauchy Integral trick and the inner structure of
A_{p,\lambda}
. On the other hand, we apply the method in
[
Duo+21
]
to prove the lower bound of
[b,R_{\lambda}].
First off, we show that

w\in A_{p,\lambda}\implies\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R_{+}},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R_{+}},w)}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}}<\infty.

Proof.

Pick
z\in\mathbb{C}
and define the generating operator by the following

T_{z}(f):=e^{zb}\cdot R_{\lambda}(e^{-zb}f).

Note that for appropriate function
f
, we have

\displaystyle T_{z}(f)

\displaystyle=\left(1+zb+\cdots\right)\cdot R_{\lambda}\left((1-zb+\cdots)f\right)

\displaystyle=R_{\lambda}(f)+z\cdot\left(bR_{\lambda}(f)-R_{\lambda}(bf)\right)+z^{2}\cdot G

\displaystyle=R_{\lambda}(f)+z\cdot[b,R_{\lambda}]f+z^{2}\cdot G,

where
G
is an operator, and hence

[b,R_{\lambda}]f=\frac{d}{dz}\mid_{z=0}T_{z}(f).

By Cauchy Integral Formula,

[b,R_{\lambda}]f=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\frac{T_{z}(f)}{z^{2}}dz,

in which
D_{\varepsilon}
is the boundary of a disk with radius
\varepsilon>0.

Then by Minkowski’s integral inequality,

\displaystyle\|[b,R_{\lambda}]f\|_{L^{p}(R_{+},w)}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\|T_{z}(f)\|_{L^{p}(R_{+},w)}dz.

(8.1)

Now, we calculate the term inside in integral and get

\|T_{z}(f)\|_{L^{p}(R_{+},w)}=\|R_{\lambda}(e^{-zb}f)\|_{L^{p}(R_{+},e^{pRe(z)b}w)}.

Since
\|R_{\lambda}(f)\|_{L^{p}(R_{+},w)}\underset{A_{p,\lambda}}{\lesssim}\|f\|_{L^{p}(R_{+},w)}
, we need to check that whether

\displaystyle[e^{pRe(z)b}w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}<\infty.

(8.2)

For any interval
B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, consider the quantity

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{p}w(t)e^{pRe(z)b}dt\bigg)\cdot\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p^{{}^{\prime}}}w(t)^{1-p^{{}^{\prime}}}e^{-p^{{}^{\prime}}Re(z)b}dt\bigg)^{p-1}.

(8.3)

For the first term,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{p}w(t)e^{pRe(z)b}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}\frac{t^{p}w(t)}{\nu_{\lambda}(t)}e^{pRe(z)b}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}\left(\frac{t^{p}w(t)}{\nu_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{1+\varepsilon_{1}}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{1}}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}e^{(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})pRe(z)b}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{1+\varepsilon_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{p}w(t)dt\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}e^{(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})pRe(z)b}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{1+\varepsilon_{1}}},

where the second inequality holds by the reverse type inequality.

For the second term, we use the dual factor to argue. Note that

w\in A_{p,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow t^{(2\lambda-1)p^{\prime}}w^{-1\over p-1}\in A_{p^{\prime},\lambda}.

So,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p^{{}^{\prime}}}w^{1-p^{{}^{\prime}}}(t)e^{-p^{{}^{\prime}}Re(z)b}dt=\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}\frac{t^{2\lambda p^{{}^{\prime}}}w^{1-p^{{}^{\prime}}}(t)}{\nu_{\lambda}(t)}e^{-p^{{}^{\prime}}Re(z)b}d\nu_{\lambda}(t),

applying Hölder’s inequality and the reverse-type inequality, we have

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}\left(\frac{t^{2\lambda p^{{}^{\prime}}}w^{1-p^{{}^{\prime}}}(t)}{\nu_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{1+\delta}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}e^{-(1+\frac{1}{\delta})p^{{}^{\prime}}Re(z)b}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p^{{}^{\prime}}}w^{1-p^{{}^{\prime}}}(t)dt\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}e^{-(1+\frac{1}{\delta})p^{{}^{\prime}}Re(z)b}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}}.

Note that the constant
\varepsilon_{1}
and
\delta
can be chosen to be equal.
In conclusion, the quantity (
8.3
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{p}w(t)dt\right)\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{2\lambda p^{{}^{\prime}}}w^{1-p^{{}^{\prime}}}(t)dt\right)^{p-1}

(8.4)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}e^{(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})pRe(z)b}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{1+\varepsilon_{1}}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}e^{-(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})p^{{}^{\prime}}Re(z)b}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{1+\varepsilon_{1}}(p-1)}

\displaystyle\leq[w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}e^{(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})pRe(z)b}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{1+\varepsilon_{1}}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}e^{-(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})p^{{}^{\prime}}Re(z)b}d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\right)^{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{1+\varepsilon_{1}}(p-1)}.

Hence, if we choose
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that

\bigg|(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})\cdot p\cdot Re(z)\bigg|\leq\frac{A}{\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}}}\cdot\min\{1,p-1\},

then together with (2) of Corollary
2.3
, the estimate (
8.4
) implies that

[e^{pRe(z)b}w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}\lesssim[w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}\cdot\big(C(A,\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1})\big)^{p},

where
C(A,\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1})>1.
Thus, we see that (
8.2
) holds.

Now we return to the estimate (
8.1
).
If we let

\varepsilon=\frac{A}{p(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}}}\cdot\min\{1,p-1\}\implies\left|(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})p\,Re(z)\right|\leq\left|(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}})p\varepsilon\right|=\frac{A}{\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}}}\cdot\min\{1,p-1\},

which implies that

\displaystyle\|[b,R_{\lambda}]f\|_{L^{p}(R_{+},w)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\|T_{z}(f)\|_{L^{p}(R_{+},w)}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}[w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(R_{+},w)}.

That is

\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(R_{+},w)\to L^{p}(R_{+},w)}\lesssim[w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

In the last part of our paper, we prove the weighted lower bound of the commutator. In Section 7.5 of
[
Duo+21
]
, the authors proved the following argument holds for the kernel
K(x,y)
of the operator
R_{\lambda}
:

There exist positive constants
3\leq A_{1}\leq A_{2}
such that for any interval
B:=B(x_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, there exists another interval
\widetilde{B}:=B(y_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}

such that
A_{1}r\leq|x_{0}-y_{0}|\leq A_{2}r
, and for all
(x,y)\in(B\times\widetilde{B})
,
K(x,y)
does not change sign and

|K(x,y)|\gtrsim\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(\tilde{B})},

(8.5)

where
dm_{\lambda}:=x^{2\lambda}dx
with
dx
the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Definition 8.2
.

By a median value of a real-valued measurable function
f
over an interval
B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we mean a possibly non-unique, real number
\alpha_{B}(f)
such that

m_{\lambda}(\{x\in B:f(x)>\alpha_{B}(f)\})\leq\frac{1}{2}m_{\lambda}(B)\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,m_{\lambda}(\{x\in B:f(x)<\alpha_{B}(f)\})\leq\frac{1}{2}m_{\lambda}(B).

It is known that for a given function
f
and interval
B
, the median value exists and may not be unique; see, for example,
[
Jou83
]
. The following decomposition follows from the result in Section 5 of
[
Duo+21
]
.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
b
be a real-valued measurable function.
For any ball
B
, let
\widetilde{B}
be as in (
8.5
). Then
there exist measurable sets
E_{1},E_{2}\subset B
, and
F_{1},F_{2}\subset\widetilde{B}
, such that

(i)

B=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
,
\widetilde{B}=F_{1}\cup F_{2}
and
m_{\lambda}(F_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}m_{\lambda}(\widetilde{B})
,
i=1,2
;

(ii)

b(x)-b(y)
does not change sign for all
(x,y)
in
E_{i}\times F_{i}
,
i=1,2
;

(iii)

|b(x)-\alpha_{\widetilde{B}}(b)|\leq|b(x)-b(y)|
for all
(x,y)
in
E_{i}\times F_{i}
,
i=1,2
.

We now prove the lower bound for
[b,R_{\lambda}]
, that is

w\in{A_{p,\lambda}}\implies\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}}\lesssim\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}

Proof.

For given
b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\nu_{\lambda})
and for any ball
B
,
we will show that for any ball
B
,

\displaystyle\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}}{1\over\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|b(t)-b_{B,\lambda}|d\nu_{\lambda}(t)\lesssim\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}.

(8.6)

Without loss of generality,
let
B
be an interval in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We apply the conditions (
8.5
) and Lemma
8.3

to get sets
E_{i},F_{i},\,i=1,2
.

On the one hand, by Lemma
8.3
and (
8.5
), we have that for
f_{i}:=\chi_{F_{i}}
,
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{B}|[b,R_{\lambda}]f_{i}(x)|\,d\nu_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{E_{i}}|[b,R_{\lambda}]f_{i}(x)|\,d\nu_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{E_{i}}\int_{F_{i}}|b(x)-b(y)||K(x,y)|\,dm_{\lambda}(y)\,d\nu_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{E_{i}}\int_{F_{i}}\frac{|b(x)-\alpha_{\widetilde{B}}(b)|}{m_{\lambda}(\tilde{B})}\,dm_{\lambda}(y)\,d\nu_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{E_{i}}\frac{|b(x)-\alpha_{\widetilde{B}}(b)|}{m_{\lambda}(\tilde{B})}\,d\nu_{\lambda}(x)\cdot m_{\lambda}(F_{i})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}\left|b(x)-\alpha_{\widetilde{B}}(b)\right|\,d\nu_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\gtrsim{1\over\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}|b(x)-b_{B,\lambda}|d\nu_{\lambda}(x).

On the other hand, from Hölder’s inequality and the boundedness of
[b,R_{\lambda}]
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{B}|[b,R_{\lambda}]f_{i}(x)|\,d\nu_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{B}|[b,R_{\lambda}]f_{i}(x)|w(x)^{1\over p}\,w(x)^{-{1\over p}}\ x^{2\lambda+1}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\sum^{2}_{i=1}\left(\int_{B}|[b,R_{\lambda}]f_{i}(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\left(\int_{B}x^{(2\lambda+1)p^{{}^{\prime}}}\cdot w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{{}^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\sum^{2}_{i=1}\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\cdot w(F_{i})^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\left(\int_{B}x^{(2\lambda+1)p^{{}^{\prime}}}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{{}^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\cdot w(\widetilde{B})^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\left(\int_{B}x^{(2\lambda+1)p^{{}^{\prime}}}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{{}^{\prime}}}}.

Denote
B=(a,b)
and
\widetilde{B}=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
and notice that from the construction of
\widetilde{B}
in
[
Duo+21
]
,
\widetilde{B}
can always be chosen on the right hand side of
B
. Hence, let
B^{*}=(a,b^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\cdot w(\widetilde{B})^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\left(\int_{B}x^{(2\lambda+1)p^{{}^{\prime}}}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{{}^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\cdot\left(\int_{\widetilde{B}}b^{p}w(t)dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\left(\int_{B}x^{2\lambda p^{{}^{\prime}}}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{{}^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\cdot\left(\int_{\widetilde{B}}\frac{b^{p}}{t^{p}}\cdot t^{p}w(t)dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\left(\int_{B}x^{2\lambda p^{{}^{\prime}}}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{{}^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\cdot\left(\int_{B^{*}}t^{p}w(t)dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\left(\int_{B^{*}}x^{2\lambda p^{{}^{\prime}}}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{{}^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu_{\lambda}(B^{*})}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\cdot[w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}.

Since
\nu_{\lambda}
is doubling and the sizes of
B
and
B^{*}
are comparable and their distance is also comparable concerning their radius, we then have

\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}}\lesssim[w]_{A_{p,\lambda}}\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}.

Thus, (
8.6
) holds and the proof of the Theorem is complete.
∎

Corollary 8.4
.

Suppose
b\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and let
1<p<\infty
. Then if
b\in{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

w\in{A_{p,\lambda}}\implies\|[b,R_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w)}\sim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}}.

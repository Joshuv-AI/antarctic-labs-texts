L p L_{p} -cohomology and the geometry of p p -harmonic forms Thanks: The author was supported in part by Simons Foundation grant 391000626.
Mark Stern

Abstract
. In this note we describe basic geometric properties of p p -harmonic forms and p p -coclosed forms and use them to reprove vanishing theorems of Pansu and new injectivity theorems for the L p L_{p} -cohomology of simply connected, pinched negatively curved manifolds. We also provide a partial resolution of a conjecture of Gromov on the vanishing of L p L_{p} -cohomology on symmetric spaces.

Primary 58A14; Secondary 57T15

1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian
n
-manifold.
Let

L_{p}^{k}(M):=\{\text{measurable }k\text{-forms }f:f\in L_{p}\},

C_{p}^{k}(M):=\{f:f\in L_{p}^{k}(M)\text{ and }df\in L_{p}^{k+1}(M)\},

Z_{p}^{k}(M):=\{z\in C_{p}^{k}(M):dz=0\},

and

B_{p}^{k}(M):=dC_{p}^{k-1}(M).

Define the
L_{p}
-cohomology to be the quotient

H_{p}^{k}(M):=Z_{p}^{k}(M)/B_{p}^{k}(M).

When
M
is compact, the de Rham cohomology and the
L_{p}
-cohomology are isomorphic, for all
1<p<\infty
. For noncompact manifolds, however, these cohomology groups need not coincide. Moreover, in the noncompact case, for some
p
and
k
,
B_{p}^{k}(M)
may not be a closed subspace of
Z_{p}^{k}(M)
. In this case, the
L_{p}
-cohomology naturally decomposes into two (possibly trivial) summands: the reduced cohomology

H_{p,red}^{k}(M):=Z_{p}^{k}(M)/\bar{B}_{p}^{k}(M),

and the (either zero or infinite dimensional non-Hausdorff) torsion cohomology

T_{p}^{k}(M):=\bar{B}_{p}^{k}(M)/B_{p}^{k}(M),

where
\bar{B}_{p}^{k}(M)
denotes the
L_{p}
closure of
B_{p}^{k}(M)
.
Given
1<p<\infty
, and
0\leq k\leq n
,
we define the space of
p
-harmonic
k
-forms to be

\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k}(M):=\{h\in Z_{p}^{k}(M):d^{*}(|h|^{p-2}h)=0\}.

The
p
-harmonics play the role in
L_{p}
-cohomology that the
L^{2}
-harmonics play in
L^{2}
-cohomology: coupling the geometry and topology and providing simple tools for proving vanishing theorems.

The purpose of this note is to develop the elementary properties of the
p
-harmonics and apply them to the study of
L_{p}
-cohomology.
In Section 2, we collect in one place some basic elements of
L_{p}
-Hodge theory. Most of these somewhat elementary results are well known to analysts but perhaps less familiar to topologists. We prove the existence of canonical
L_{p}
-primitives, the isomorphism between
H_{p,red}^{k}(M)
and
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k}(M)
, and the
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k}(M)\leftrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\frac{p}{p-1}}^{n-k}(M)
Poincaré duality defined by a nonlinear
L_{p}
-Hodge star operator. In Section 3, we derive a Bochner formula for
p
-harmonics. This formula is well known, but rarely labeled or exploited as a Bochner formula in the literature. The usual package of vanishing theorems one obtains from the Bochner formula in the
L^{2}
case extends immediately to all
p\in(1,\infty)
.

In Section 4, we examine the extension of the monotonicity results of
[
DS22
]
to
p
-harmonics.
Given a
p
-harmonic form,
h_{p}
, and
x\in M
, define for
R<\mathrm{inj}_{M}
(the injectivity radius of
M
),

I_{p}(h_{p},x,R):=\int_{B_{R}(x)}|h_{p}|^{p}dv,

where
B_{R}(x)
denotes the geodesic ball of radius
R
and center
x
.
In
[
DS22
]
, we showed that for
M
compact,
\delta<R\leq\mathrm{inj}_{M}
, and for some constant
c(\tilde{M})>0
, depending only on the universal cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
, we have

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{b^{k}(M)}{vol(M)}\leq\sup_{h_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}^{k}(M)\setminus\{0\}}c(\tilde{M})\sup_{x\in M}\frac{\delta^{-n}I_{2}(h_{2},x,\delta)}{I_{2}(h_{2},x,R)}.

Here
b^{k}(M)
denotes the kth betti number of
M
.
If the sectional curvature of
M
,
\sec_{M}
, satisfies
-1\leq\sec_{M}<-\frac{k^{2}}{(n-k-1)^{2}},
then we also showed in
[
DS22
]
that

\frac{I_{2}(h_{2},x,\delta)}{I_{2}(h_{2},x,R)}
is exponentially small in
R-\delta
. This yields 2 new proofs of the vanishing of
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{k}(\tilde{M})
, when
M
has residually finite fundamental group and
-1\leq\sec_{M}<-\frac{k^{2}}{(n-k-1)^{2}}
. One proof follows from applying (
1.1
) to a tower of covers and applying the Lück approximation theorem (
[
Lu94
]
). The other proof (which does not require residual finiteness) follows immediately from the vanishing of
\frac{I_{2}(h_{2},x,\delta)}{\|h_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{M})}^{2}}=\frac{I_{2}(h_{2},x,\delta)}{I_{2}(h_{2},x,\infty)}=0.

Recall that the Singer conjecture for compact negatively curved manifolds
M
posits that

\mathcal{H}_{2}^{k}(\tilde{M})=0,\text{ for }k\not=\frac{dim(M)}{2}.

Hence we are very interested in relaxing the pinching conditions required for applying
[
DS22
]
to prove
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{k}(\tilde{M})=0
.

In Section 5, we show that if
-1\leq\sec_{M}\leq-\frac{(p-1)k}{n-k-1}
, or
-1\leq\sec_{M}\leq-\frac{n-k}{(k-1)(p-1)}
, then

\frac{I_{p}(h_{p},x,\delta)}{I_{p}(h_{p},x,R)}
is again exponentially small in
R-\delta
. Observe that as
p
tends to
1
or
\infty
, the pinching condition limits to the assumption of strictly negative curvature. (See Theorem
5.6
). Unfortunately, this does not immediately imply the Singer Conjecture, because for
p\not=2
, the
p
-harmonic forms induce a Banach space but not a Hilbert space structure on the
L_{p}
-cohomology, and the Hilbert space structure is used to deduce (
1.1
). On the other hand, exponential decay of the ratio
\frac{I_{p}(h_{p},x,\delta)}{I_{p}(h_{p},x,R)}
implies
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k}(\tilde{M})=0
. This gives a ’harmonic’ proof of Pansu’s vanishing theorems for reduced
L_{p}
-cohomology of complete simply connected manifolds for
p<\delta\frac{n-k-1}{k}+1
and dually for
p>\frac{n-k}{\delta(k-1)}+1.
See
[
Pan08
]
. In Section 6, we extend these estimates to
p
-coclosed forms (as defined in Definition
2.8
) in order to reproduce Pansu’s vanishing theorems
[
Pan08
]
for the torsion summand of the
L_{p}
-cohomology, for
p<\delta\frac{n-k}{k-1}+1
.

In Section 7, we consider the (necessarily reduced)
L_{p}
-cohomology for
p<\delta\frac{n-k}{k-1}+1
, and prove injectivity results for the natural mapping from
H_{p}^{k}(M)\to H_{q}^{k}(M)
for
p\leq q
and
q-p
small.

In Section 8, we turn from manifolds of pinched negative curvature to symmetric spaces, and
consider the following conjecture of Gromov
[
Gro85
, Section 8C1]
:

Conjecture 1.2
.

If
G
is a semisimple Lie group, with maximal compact subgroup
K
, then

H^{k}_{p}(G/K)=0,
for
1<p<\infty,
and
k<\text{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(G).

As an elementary application of the techniques for analyzing
p
-harmonic forms and
p
-coclosed forms developed in the preceding sections, we prove the following theorem, which provides a partial resolution of ths conjecture.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
G
is a simple Lie group, with maximal compact subgroup
K
, then

H^{k}_{p}(G/K)=0,
for
1<p\leq 2,
and
k<\text{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(G).

2.
The nonlinear Hodge theorem and
p
-harmonic forms

One form of an
L_{p}
-Hodge theorem is a decomposition of
L_{p}
forms into

(2.1)

\displaystyle\overline{Im(d)}\oplus Ker(\Delta)\oplus\overline{Im(d^{*})},

with domains defined appropriately on
L_{p}
forms. See for example
[
Sco95
]
. Such results are fundamental, and if verified in our context would imply a significant extension of our theorems. They do not, however, exploit many of the geometric properties that make
L_{p}
-cohomology interesting and distinct from
L^{2}
-cohomology.

For our purposes,
p
-harmonic forms will replace
Ker(\Delta)
in the above decomposition and
p
-coclosed primitives (defined below in Definition
2.8
) will replace primitives chosen from
\overline{Im(d^{*})}
.
Much of the content of this section is already apparent in
[
SS70
]
. See also
[
KLV21
, 11.2]
for a clear and careful treatment of related theorems for functions
and
[
ISS99
]
for both linear and nonlinear approaches to Hodge theory.

As a first step toward the Hodge theorem, we show the existence of canonical primitives for elements of
B_{p}^{k}(M)
. In the following,
M
is a complete manifold, possibly with boundary.

Lemma 2.2
.

For each
\zeta\in B_{p}^{k}(M)
,

\exists!\beta_{\infty}\in C_{p}^{k-1}(M)
such that
d\beta_{\infty}=\zeta
, and

\displaystyle d^{*}\left(|\beta_{\infty}|^{p-2}\beta_{\infty}\right)=0.

Proof.

Write

d^{-1}(\zeta)
for the set
\{\beta\in C_{p}^{k-1}(M):d\beta=\zeta\}.

Set
\nu(\zeta):=\inf_{\beta\in d^{-1}(\zeta)}\|\beta\|_{L_{p}}^{p}.
Let

\{\beta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset d^{-1}(\zeta)
satisfy
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|\beta_{j}\|_{L_{p}}^{p}=\nu(\zeta).
Passing to a subsequence if necessary, we can assume
\{\beta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
converges weakly in
L_{p}
to some
\beta_{\infty}\in L_{p}
.
Since
d
commutes with weak limits, we have
d\beta_{\infty}=\zeta
. Let
\phi
be a smooth compactly supported
(k-2)
-form and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}(|\beta_{\infty}+td\phi|^{p}-|\beta_{\infty}|^{p})dv=\int_{M}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}|\beta_{\infty}+std\phi|^{p}dsdv

(2.3)

\displaystyle=p\int_{M}\int_{0}^{1}|\beta_{\infty}+std\phi|^{p-2}\langle\beta_{\infty}+std\phi,td\phi\rangle dsdv.

Choose
t=-\frac{1}{n}\int_{M}|\beta_{\infty}|^{p-2}\langle\beta_{\infty},d\phi\rangle dv=:\frac{c}{n},

n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq-p\int_{M}|\beta_{\infty}|^{p-2}\langle\beta_{\infty},d\phi\rangle dv\int_{M}\int_{0}^{1}|\beta_{\infty}+s\frac{c}{n}d\phi|^{p-2}\langle\beta_{\infty}+s\frac{c}{n}d\phi,d\phi\rangle dsdv.

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
yields

(2.5)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq-p(\int_{M}|\beta_{\infty}|^{p-2}\langle\beta_{\infty},d\phi\rangle dv)^{2}.

Hence for all
\phi
smooth and compactly supported,

(2.6)

\displaystyle 0=\int_{M}|\beta_{\infty}|^{p-2}\langle\beta_{\infty},d\phi\rangle dv,

and therefore

(2.7)

\displaystyle d^{*}\left(|\beta_{\infty}|^{p-2}\beta_{\infty}\right)=0.

Since the
L_{p}
norm is strictly convex, it has a unique critical point. Hence
\beta_{\infty}
is unique.
∎

Definition 2.8
.

We call the primitive specified in Lemma
2.2
the
p
-coclosed primitive
.

For
z\in Z_{p}^{\ast}(M)
, let
[z]
and
[z]_{red}
denote the images of
z
in
H_{p}^{\ast}(M)
and
H_{p,red}^{\ast}(M)
respectively.

Theorem 2.9
.

(Nonlinear Hodge Theorem)
For each
\varphi\in H_{p,red}^{k}(M),

\exists!h\in Z_{p}^{k}(M)
such that
[h]_{red}=\varphi
, and

(2.10)

\displaystyle d^{*}(|h|^{p-2}h)=0.

Moreover,
\|h\|_{L_{p}(M)}\leq\|z\|_{L_{p}(M)}
, for all
z\in Z_{p}^{k}(M)
such that
[z]_{red}=\varphi
, with equality only if
h=z
.

Proof.

Let
z\in Z_{p}^{k}(M)
. Define

E_{z}:\bar{B}_{p}^{k}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
by

E_{z}(\beta):=\int_{M}|z-\beta|^{p}dv.

Let
\rho(z):=\inf_{\beta\in\bar{B}_{p}^{k}(M)}E_{z}(\beta).

Then
\rho(z)=0
if and only if
[z]_{red}=0.
Suppose
\rho(z)\not=0
. Let
\{\beta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\bar{B}_{p}^{k}(M)

satisfy
\lim_{j\to\infty}E_{z}(\beta_{j})=\rho(z)
. Then
\{\beta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is an
L_{p}
bounded sequence. Passing to a subsequence, we can assume
\{\beta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is weakly convergent to some
\zeta\in\bar{B}_{p}^{k}(M)
. By the weak lower semicontinuity of convex functions,

E_{z}(\zeta)\leq\rho(z),
and therefore
E_{z}(\zeta)=\rho(z).
Set
h:=z-\zeta.
We then have

[h]_{red}=[z]_{red}.

The proof that
d^{*}(|h|^{p-2}h)=0
and that
h
is the unique norm minimizer in its class is now the same as the proofs of the corresponding statements in Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
M
be a complete riemannian manifold. Then

H_{p,red}^{k}(M){\cong}\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k}(M).

We can also dualize our treatment of
L_{p}
Hodge theory. Define

\Omega_{p}^{k}(M):=\{\text{measurable }k-\text{forms }f:f\in L_{p}\text{ and }d^{*}f\in L_{p}\}.

F_{p}^{k}(M):=\{f\in\Omega_{p}^{k}(M):d^{*}f=0\},\text{ and }D_{p}^{k}(M):=d^{*}\Omega_{p}^{k+1}(M).

Define the
L_{p}
-homology to be

Y_{p}^{k}(M):=F_{p}^{k}(M)/D_{p}^{k}(M).

Define the torsion summand

\mathcal{T}_{p}^{k}(M):=\bar{D}_{p}^{k}(M)/D_{p}^{k}(M),

and the reduced homology

Y_{p,\text{red}}^{k}(M):=F_{p}^{k}(M)/\bar{D}_{p}^{k}(M).

Define the space of
p
-coharmonic forms to be

\mathcal{E}_{p}^{k}(M):=\{f\in F_{p}^{k}(M):d(|f|^{p-2}f)=0\}.

For complete manifolds, we again have
Y_{p,\text{red}}^{k}(M)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{k}(M)
.

Analytic Poincaré duality proofs survive the passage from
2-
harmonic to
p
-harmonic. Assume
M
is oriented. Let
\ast
denote the Hodge star operator. Define

\ast_{p}h:=\ast|h|^{p-2}h.

Then
|\ast_{p}h|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}=|h|^{p},
and

(2.12)

\displaystyle\ast_{\frac{p}{p-1}}(\ast_{p}h)=\ast^{2}h=\pm h.

In particular,
\ast_{p}h\in\mathcal{H}_{\frac{p}{p-1}}^{n-k}(M).

Interpreting Poincare duality differently, we also note that the map
h\to|h|^{p-2}h
defines a norm preserving bijection between
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{k}(M)
and
\mathcal{H}_{p^{\prime}}^{k}(M)
,
where
p^{\prime}:=\frac{p}{p-1}
denotes the dual exponent. Moreover
z\to\ast z
defines an isometric bijection between
T^{k}_{p}(M)
and
\mathcal{T}^{n-k}_{p}
.
This yields the following
L_{p}
-Poincaré duality theorem.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
M^{n}
be a complete oriented riemannian manifold. Then
\ast_{p}
defines a bijective norm preserving map from
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k}(M)
to
\mathcal{H}_{\frac{p}{p-1}}^{n-k}(M)
.
Moreover,
h\to|h|^{p-2}h
(respectively
z\to\ast z
) defines a norm preserving bijection between
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{k}(M)
and
\mathcal{H}_{p^{\prime}}^{k}(M)
(respectively between
T^{k}_{p}(M)
and
\mathcal{T}^{n-k}_{p}
).

See
[
Pan08
, Corollary 14]
for a proof of Poincaré duality for both reduced and torsion
L_{p}
-cohomology, not involving
p
-harmonic forms. For the convenience of the reader, we also give an elementary proof of the duality for the torsion part of the cohomology.

Proposition 2.14
.

T^{k}_{p}(M)=0
if and only if
T^{n-k+1}_{p^{\prime}}(M)=0
.

Proof.

By the Closed Image Theorem
[
BS18
, Theorem 6.2.3]
, the image of
d(C^{k-1}_{p}(M))
is closed if and only if
d^{*}(\Omega^{k}_{p^{\prime}}(M))
is closed. Using
d^{*}=\pm\ast d\ast
, we see that
d^{*}(\Omega^{k}_{p^{\prime}}(M))
is closed if and only if
d(\Omega^{n-k}_{p^{\prime}}(M))
is closed.
∎

3.
The Bochner formula

By elliptic regularity, a
p
-harmonic form
h
is smooth in a neighborhood of any point where it is nonzero. It is not necessarily smooth, however, at any point
x_{0}
where
h(x_{0})=0
, but it is Hölder continuous. (See
[
Uhl77
, Main Theorem]
). We will not repeat that difficult proof here, but we note that a first step in proving the simpler result that
|h|
is pointwise bounded involves extending the Bochner formula to
p
-harmonic forms. The usual vanishing theorems obtained via Bochner formulas for
2-
harmonics then apply to the general
p
- harmonic case with no substantive change.

Given a differential form
w
, let
e(w)
denote left exterior multiplication by
w
and let
e^{*}(w)
denote the adjoint operation. Thus, for example, if
w=dF
for some function
F
, then
e^{*}(w)=i_{\nabla F}
, where
i_{X}
denotes the interior product with
X
.

Proposition 3.1
.

(Uhlenbeck
[
Uhl77
, Theorem 1.10]
)
Let
h
be a
p
-harmonic form. Then
h
satisfies the Bochner formula

\displaystyle\Delta\frac{1}{2}|h|^{p}=-|h|^{p-2}\left(|\nabla h|^{2}-(2-p)|d|h||^{2}-\langle h,e(\omega^{i})e^{*}(\omega^{j})R_{ij}h\rangle\right)

(3.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{2-p}{2p}div\left([\langle e^{*}(\omega^{k})\frac{h}{|h|},e^{*}(\omega^{j})\frac{h}{|h|}\rangle-\langle e(\omega^{j})\frac{h}{|h|},e(\omega^{k})\frac{h}{|h|}\rangle](e_{k}|h|^{p})e_{j}\right).

Proof.

Compute in an orthonormal frame:

\displaystyle-\Delta\langle h,|h|^{p-2}h\rangle=2\langle\nabla_{j}h,\nabla_{j}|h|^{p-2}h\rangle-\langle\nabla^{*}\nabla h,|h|^{p-2}h\rangle-\langle h,\nabla^{*}\nabla|h|^{p-2}h\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|h|^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}-2(2-p)|h|^{p-2}|d|h||^{2}-\langle(dd^{*}+e(\omega^{i})e^{*}(\omega^{j})R_{ij})h,|h|^{p-2}h\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle h,(d^{*}d+e(\omega^{i})e^{*}(\omega^{j})R_{ij})|h|^{p-2}h\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|h|^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}-2(2-p)|h|^{p-2}|d|h||^{2}+\frac{2-p}{p}div(\langle e^{*}(\omega^{k})\frac{h}{|h|},e^{*}(\omega^{j})\frac{h}{|h|}\rangle e_{k}(|h|^{p})e_{j})

(3.3)

\displaystyle-\frac{2-p}{p}div(\langle e(\omega^{j})\frac{h}{|h|},e(\omega^{k})\frac{h}{|h|}\rangle e_{k}(|h|^{p})e_{j})-2\langle h,e(\omega^{i})e^{*}(\omega^{j})R_{ij}|h|^{p-2}h\rangle.

Hence
|h|^{p}
satisfies the claimed second order equation.
∎

Integrating the
p
-harmonic Bochner formula over
M
and applying the divergence theorem to eliminate the Laplacian term and the nonlinear divergence term yields the same corollaries as the usual
2-
harmonic formula.
Define the curvature operator

\mathcal{R}:=e(\omega^{j})e^{*}(\omega^{k})R_{kj}.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
\mathcal{R}
is positive on
k
-forms, then
H_{p,red}^{k}(M)=0
, for all
p\in(1,\infty).
If
\mathcal{R}
is nonnegative on
k
-forms, then any
p
-harmonic
k
-form must be covariant constant, and hence zero if the volume is infinite.

Corollary 3.5
.

If
M
is complete and Ricci flat with infinite volume,
H_{p,red}^{1}(M)=0
, for all
p\in(1,\infty).

Corollary 3.6
.

If
M^{n}
has bounded sectional curvature and positive injectivity radius, there exists
C_{M}>0
such that
\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C_{M}\|h\|_{L^{p}(M)}
, for every
h\in\mathcal{H}^{k}_{p}(M)
,
1<p<\infty
,
0\leq k\leq n
.

Proof.

The Bochner formula (
3.1
) shows that
|h|^{p}
is a subsolution to an elliptic equation of divergence form with uniformly bounded coefficients. The bounded sectional curvature and positive injectivity radius then allows one to use Moser iteration to control the sup norm of
|h|^{p}
in terms of its average value on geodesic balls of radius less than or equal to
\mathrm{inj}_{M}
. (See, for example,
[
LS18
, Proposition 2.2]
for the analogous
p=2
estimate.) This in turn implies a bound for
\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}
in terms of
(\mathrm{inj}_{M})^{-\frac{n}{p}}\|h\|_{L_{p}}.

∎

4.
Monotonicity estimates for
p
-harmonic forms

The monotonicity estimates for
p
-harmonic forms can be derived exactly as the
L^{2}
case treated in
[
DS22
]
. We repeat the computation here for the convenience of the reader.

Let
h\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k}(M).

Let
B_{R}
be a geodesic ball in
M
with boundary
S_{R}
. Let
r
denote the radial function and
\partial_{r}
denote the radial vector field in
B_{R}
.
Let
\{\lambda_{a}\}_{a}
denote the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{Hess\,}(r)
. Choose an orthonormal eigenframe
\{e_{a}\}_{a}
with dual coframe
\{\omega^{a}\}_{a}
, so that

e(\omega^{i})e^{*}(\omega^{j})\mathrm{Hess\,}(r)_{ji}=\sum_{a}\lambda_{a}e(\omega^{a})e^{*}(\omega^{a}).

Define the functions
w_{p}(h,r)
and
\mu_{p}(h,r)
by

(4.1)

\displaystyle w_{p}(h,r):=\frac{\int_{S_{r}}|h|^{p}\sum_{a}\lambda_{a}(\frac{1}{p}-|e^{*}(\omega^{a})\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2})d\sigma}{\int_{S_{r}}|h|^{p}d\sigma},

and

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mu_{p}(h,r):=\frac{\int_{S_{r}}|h|^{p}|e^{*}(dr)\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2}d\sigma}{\int_{S_{r}}|h|^{p}d\sigma}.

Proposition 4.3
.

With
w_{p},\mu_{p}
, and
h
as defined above,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\sigma}}w_{p}(h,r)|h|^{p}dv=e^{-\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}\frac{pw_{p}(h,s)}{1-p\mu_{p}(h,s)}\frac{ds}{s}}\int_{B_{\tau}}w_{p}(h,r)|h|^{p}dv.

Proof.

We use the fact that on a Riemannian manifold, we can express the Lie derivative acting on forms in terms of the covariant derivative:

(4.5)

\displaystyle L_{\partial_{r}}=di_{\partial_{r}}+i_{\partial_{r}}d=\nabla_{\partial_{r}}+e(\omega^{i})e^{*}(\omega^{j})\mathrm{Hess\,}(r)_{ji}.

By Stoke’s theorem we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\langle L_{\partial_{r}}h,|h|^{p-2}h\rangle dv=\int_{S_{R}}|h|^{p-2}|e^{*}(dr)h|^{2}d\sigma,

and by (
4.5
),

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\langle L_{\partial_{r}}h,|h|^{p-2}h\rangle dv=\int_{B_{R}}\langle(\nabla_{\partial_{r}}+e(\omega^{i})e^{*}(\omega^{j})\mathrm{Hess\,}(r)_{ji})h,|h|^{p-2}h\rangle dv

Combining (

\displaystyle\int_{S_{R}}(\frac{1}{p}-\mu_{p}(h,R))|h|^{p}d\sigma=\int_{B_{R}}w_{p}(h,r)|h|^{p}dv.

Setting

we rewrite (

\displaystyle(\frac{1}{p}-\mu_{p}(h,R))Y^{\prime}=w_{p}(h,R)Y(R).

Integrate this expression to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\sigma}}w_{p}(h,r)|h|^{p}dv=e^{-\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}\frac{pw_{p}(h,s)}{1-p\mu_{p}(h,s)}\frac{ds}{s}}\int_{B_{\tau}}w_{p}(h,r)|h|^{p}dv,

as claimed.
∎

5.
Negative Sectional Curvature

Let
M^{n}
be a nonnegatively curved n-manifold with
-1\leq\sec_{M}\leq-\delta^{2},

\delta\geq 0
, where
\sec_{M}
denotes the sectional curvature of
M
. Let
r
again denote the radial function of some geodesic ball,
B_{R}
, in
M
. By the Rauch comparison theorem
[
Pet16
, Theorem 6.4.3]
, the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{a}\}_{a}
of
\mathrm{Hess\,}(r)
satisfy

(5.1)

\displaystyle\delta\coth(\delta r)\leq\lambda_{a}\leq\coth(r),

(except for the zero eigenvalue associated to the radial direction)
and
\mathrm{tr}\mathrm{Hess\,}(r)
satisfies

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\mathrm{Hess\,}(r)\geq(n-1)\delta\coth(r).

In a neighborhood of some point
x_{0}\in B_{R}\setminus\{0\}
, let
\{\omega^{a}\}_{a=1}^{n-1}\cup\{dr\}
be a local orthonormal coframe such that the
\omega^{a}
are metrically dual to Hessian eigendirections with eigenvalue
\lambda_{a}
. Let
h
be a
k
-form, with
|h|(x_{0})\not=0
.
Let
J\subset\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
denote the indices of Hessian eigendirections such that at
x_{0}
,
|e^{*}(\omega^{a})\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2}>\frac{1}{p}.
Since
h
is a
k
-form, at
x_{0}
we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle|e^{*}(dr)\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2}+\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}|e^{*}(\omega^{a})\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2}=k.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\lambda_{a}(\frac{1}{p}-|e^{*}(\omega^{a})\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\delta\coth(\delta r)(\frac{n-1}{p}-k+|e^{*}(dr)\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2})+\sum_{a}(\lambda_{a}-\delta\coth(\delta r))(\frac{1}{p}-|e^{*}(\omega^{a})\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2})

\displaystyle\geq\delta\coth(\delta r)(\frac{n-1}{p}-k+|e^{*}(dr)\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2})+\sum_{a\in J}(\coth(r)-\delta\coth(\delta r))(\frac{1}{p}-|e^{*}(\omega^{a})\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2})

\displaystyle\geq\delta\coth(\delta r)(\frac{n-1}{p}-k)+(\coth(r)-\delta\coth(\delta r))(\frac{|J|}{p}-\min\{|J|,k\})

\displaystyle\geq\coth(r)[\delta(\frac{n-1}{p}-k)+(1-\delta)(\frac{k}{p}-k)]

(5.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{p}\coth(r)[\delta\frac{n-k-1}{k}+1-p].

This is positive for

p<\delta\frac{n-k-1}{k}+1
(which is less than
\frac{n}{k}
). In the quarter pinched case,
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
, and for
k\leq\frac{n-1}{2}

p<\delta\frac{n-k-1}{k}+1
specializes to
p<\frac{3}{2}.

Observe that the conjugate exponent to
\delta\frac{n-k-1}{k}+1
is
1+\frac{k}{\delta(n-k-1)},
(where by conjugate exponent, we mean the
p^{\prime}(=\frac{p}{p-1})
satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
).

For large
p
, we let

L
denote the eigendirections such that
|e^{*}(\omega^{a})\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2}<\frac{1}{p}.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\lambda_{a}(|e^{*}(\omega^{a})\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2}-\frac{1}{p})

\displaystyle=\delta\coth(\delta r)(k-\frac{n-1}{p}-|e^{*}(dr)\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2})+\sum_{a}(\lambda_{a}-\delta\coth(\delta r))(|e^{*}(\omega^{a})\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2}-\frac{1}{p})

\displaystyle\geq\delta\coth(r)(k-1-\frac{n-1}{p})+\sum_{a\in L}(\lambda_{a}-\delta\coth(r))(|e^{*}(\omega^{a})\frac{h}{|h|}|^{2}-\frac{1}{p})

(5.5)

\displaystyle\geq\coth(r)(n-k)\frac{p-1}{p}[\frac{\delta(k-1)}{n-k}-\frac{1}{p-1}].

This is positive for (
\frac{n}{k}<
)

\frac{n-k}{\delta(k-1)}+1<p.
For
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
and
k\geq\frac{n+1}{2}
this gives

3<p.
Note that
3
is the conjugate exponent to
\frac{3}{2}
. More generally the limiting exponents for
k
and
n-k
are conjugate for fixed
\delta
.

We have included the parenthetical comparisons to
\frac{n}{k}
for easy application of Corollary
4.15
.

Combining these positivity results with the estimates of the preceding section yields the following theorem.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
-1\leq\sec_{M}\leq-\delta^{2}<0.

Let
h
be a
p
-harmonic
k
-form on
M
. Let
\sigma\leq\tau\leq\mathrm{inj}_{M}
. If
p<\delta\frac{n-k-1}{k}+1
,
then
w_{p}(h,r)
is bounded below by a positive constant independent of

and
r
, and

(5.7)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\sigma}}w_{p}(h,r)|h|^{p}dv\leq e^{-(\tau-\sigma)k[\delta\frac{n-k-1}{k}+1-p]}\int_{B_{\tau}}w_{p}(h,r)|h|^{p}dv.

If
p>\frac{n-k}{\delta(k-1)}+1
,
then

(5.8)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\sigma}}w_{p}(h,r)|h|^{p}dv\leq e^{-(\tau-\sigma)k(p-1)[\frac{\delta(k-1)}{p}-\frac{(n-k)}{p(p-1)}]}\int_{B_{\tau}}w_{p}(h,r)|h|^{p}dv.

These monotonicity estimates give us a new proof of Pansu’s reduced
L_{p}
-cohomology vanishing theorem for simply connected complete manifolds of pinched negative curvature. The fact that we obtain precisely the same pinching constraints is not surprising as both proofs at some point rely on estimates for geometric quantities related to the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{Hess\,}(r)
.

Corollary 5.9
.

(Pansu)
Let
M^{n}
be a simply connected complete Riemannian n-manifold with
-1\leq\sec_{M}\leq-\delta^{2}<0.

If

, and

, or

, and

,
then

(5.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{k}_{p}(M)=0=H^{k}_{p,red}(M).

Proof.

Let

h\in\mathcal{H}^{k}_{p}(M)
. Take the limit as

\tau\to\infty
in Theorem
5.6
to deduce

\int_{B_{\sigma}}w_{p}(h,r)|h|^{p}dv=0
, for all
\sigma
and all balls. Since

for nonzero

.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

In the

imply for

is

-harmonic forms to obtain vanishing theorems for the non-Hausdorff summand of the cohomology, even though that summand does not admit

6.
Closure of exact forms

In this section we consider the torsion
T^{k}_{p}(M)
. We give a new proof of a vanishing theorem of Pansu (see
[
Pan08
]
).

Theorem 6.1
.

(Pansu)
Let
M
be a simply connected complete Riemannian manifold with
-1\leq\sec_{M}\leq-\delta^{2}<0.

If
k-1<\frac{n}{p}
, and

p<\delta\frac{n-k}{k-1}+1
,
then

(6.2)

\displaystyle T^{k}_{p}(M)=0.

Proof.

Let
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset B_{p}^{k}(M)
be an
L_{p}
Cauchy sequence, with
z_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L_{p}}}{{\to}}z\in Z_{p}^{k}(M).
Let
b_{j}
denote the
p
-coclosed primitive of
z_{j}
. (See Lemma
2.2
.)
Repeating the first steps of the derivation of our monotonicity estimates given in Section
4
, we have, since
db_{j}=z_{j}
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\langle\{d,e^{*}(dr)\}b_{j},|b_{j}|^{p-2}b_{j}\rangle dv=\int_{S_{R}}|e^{*}(dr)b_{j}|^{2}|b_{j}|^{p-2}d\sigma+\int_{B_{R}}\langle e^{*}(dr)z_{j},|b_{j}|^{p-2}b_{j}\rangle dv,

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\langle\{d,e^{*}(dr)\}b_{j},|b_{j}|^{p-2}b_{j}\rangle dv=-\int_{B_{R}}w_{p}(b_{j},r)|b_{j}|^{p}dv+\int_{S_{R}}\frac{1}{p}|b_{j}|^{p}d\sigma.

Combined, these yield

(6.3)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}w_{p}(b_{j},r)|b_{j}|^{p}dv=\int_{S_{R}}(\frac{1}{p}-\mu_{p}(b_{j},R))|b_{j}|^{p}d\sigma-\int_{B_{R}}\langle e^{*}(dr)z_{j},|b_{j}|^{p-2}b_{j}\rangle dv.

Our pinching hypotheses imply
w_{p}(b_{j},r)\geq\kappa_{k-1}>0
, for some positive constant
\kappa_{k-1}
. Hence, taking the limit as
R\to\infty
, the
S_{R}
integral vanishes, and Hölder’s inequality yields

(6.4)

\displaystyle\kappa_{k-1}\|b_{j}\|_{L_{p}}\leq\|z_{j}\|_{L_{p}}.

Since
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is Cauchy,
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is bounded. Passing to a subsequence, we can assume
b_{j}
coverges weakly in
L_{p}
to some
b.

Since
d
commutes with weak limits,
db=z
. Hence
\bar{B}_{p}^{k}/B_{p}^{k}=\{0\}.

∎

7.
Injectivity and pinched negative sectional curvature

Developing the techniques of the previous section, we next prove injectivity results for natural maps from

for

, and

By Corollary

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
M
be a complete, simply connected, negatively curved n-manifold, with
-1\leq\sec_{M}\leq-\delta^{2}<0
.
If
p<1+\delta\frac{n-k}{k-1}
and
\frac{q-p}{q}<\delta(n-k)-(p-1)(k-1)
,

then

is injective.

Proof.

Let
h
be a
p
-harmonic representative of an element of the kernel of
i_{p,q}^{k}
. Fix a point
o\in M
, and let
B_{R}
denote the geodesic ball of radius
R
and center

. Let
b_{R}
denote the
p
-coclosed primitive of
h
restricted to
\bar{B}_{R}
. By (
6.3
) we have for
T\leq R
,

(7.2)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{T}}w_{p}(b_{R},r)|b_{R}|^{p}dv=\int_{S_{T}}(\frac{1}{p}-\mu_{p}(b_{R},T))|b_{R}|^{p}d\sigma-\int_{B_{T}}\langle e^{*}(dr)h,|b_{R}|^{p-2}b_{R}\rangle dv.

Suppose for
\epsilon:=\frac{1}{2p}(\delta(n-k)-(p-1)(k-1)-\frac{q-p}{q})
, there exists a sequence
\{(T_{j},R_{j})\}_{j}

with
T_{j}\to\infty
and
R_{j}\geq T_{j}
, so that

\int_{B_{T_{j}}}|b_{R_{j}}|^{p}dv\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{p}}\int_{B_{T_{j}}}|h|^{p}dv.
Then passing to a subsequence, we can find
b_{\infty}\in L_{p}
such that the

converge weakly to

on compact sets. Therefore

, which implies

. Hence, for

,
\exists T_{0}>0
so that

(7.3)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{T}}|h|^{p}dv\leq\epsilon\int_{B_{T}}|b_{R_{j}}|^{p}dv,\forall T>T_{0}.

Setting
Y_{R}(T):=\int_{B_{T}}(w_{p}(b_{R},r)-\epsilon)|b_{R}|^{p}dv
, we combine (
7.2
) and (

) to obtain
for

,

\displaystyle Y_{R}(T)\leq\frac{(\frac{1}{p}-\mu_{p}(b_{R},T))}{w_{p}(b_{R},T)-\epsilon}\frac{dY_{R}(T)}{dT}.

Integrate this differential inequality from
T_{0}
to

to get

(7.4)

\displaystyle Y_{R}(R)\geq e^{\int_{T_{0}}^{R}\frac{w_{p}(b_{R},T)-\epsilon}{(\frac{1}{p}-\mu_{p}(b_{R},T))}dT}Y_{R}(T_{0})\geq e^{(R-T_{0})[\delta(n-k)-(p-1)(k-1)-p\epsilon]}Y_{R}(T_{0}).

On the other hand, since

\exists\beta_{q}\in L^{q}
such that

. Since
b_{R}
is the
L_{p}(B_{R})
norm minimizing primitive for

,
we have

\displaystyle\|b_{R}\|_{L_{p}(B_{R})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\beta_{q}\|_{L_{p}(B_{R})}^{p}\leq\|\beta_{q}\|_{L^{q}(M)}^{\frac{p}{q}}vol(B_{R})^{\frac{q-p}{q}}

(7.5)

\displaystyle\leq\|\beta_{q}\|_{L^{q}(M)}^{\frac{p}{q}}vol(B_{R})^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\leq c(n)\|\beta_{q}\|_{L^{q}(M)}^{\frac{p}{q}}e^{\frac{q-p}{q}R}.

As

, (
7
) and (

) are mutually contradictory, unless

is

∎

8.
Symmetric spaces

The curvature pinching hypotheses in the preceding sections are somewhat coarse, as they depend on the Rauch comparison theorem. In more homogeneous geometries, we can often compute Lie derivatives of useful vector fields explicitly and can therefore achieve sharper results. As illustration, we resolve part of a conjecture of Gromov.

In this section,
G
is a semisimple Lie group of noncompact type,
K
is a maximal compact subgroup, and
X:=G/K
is the associated symmetric space. Let
A
be a maximal real split torus of
G
. (So
\text{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(G)=dim(A).
) Let
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{k}
, and
\mathfrak{a}
denote the Lie algebras of
G
,
K
and
A
respectively. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}\oplus\mathfrak{k}
denote the Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
determined by
K
.

Conjecture 8.1
.

(Gromov)

H^{k}_{p}(X)=0,
for
1<p<\infty,
and
k<\text{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(G).

A choice of Weyl chamber determines a set
\Phi_{G}^{+}
of positive roots of
G
relative to
A
. When
G
is not real split, extend
\mathfrak{a}
to a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\hat{\Phi}_{G}^{+}
denote the set of roots of
\mathfrak{h}
whose restriction to
\mathfrak{a}
lie in
\Phi_{G}^{+}
. Let
N
be the unipotent subgroup of
G
whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
is the direct sum of the root spaces with root in
\hat{\Phi}^{+}_{G}
. Let
\{\tilde{N}_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\hat{\Phi}_{G}^{+}}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{n}
with respect to the metric determined by the Killing form and the Cartan involution. Let
\{\tilde{\omega}_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\hat{\Phi}_{G}^{+}}
denote the dual basis. We identify elements of the Lie algebras of
A
and
N
with left invariant vector fields on
A
, and
N
respectively, in the usual fashion. With this notation, we have the Iwasawa decomposition
G=NAK
, and
X
is isometric to
N\times A
equipped with the metric

g_{X}:=da^{2}+\sum_{\beta\in\hat{\Phi}^{+}_{G}}a^{-2\beta}(\tilde{\omega}^{\beta})^{2},

where

is a Euclidean metric on
A
. Orthonormalize our

subframe with respect to this metric, setting
N_{\beta}:=a^{\beta}\tilde{N}_{\beta},
and
\omega^{\beta}:=a^{-\beta}\tilde{\omega}^{\beta}.

We now adapt our monotonicity machinery to the symmetric setting.
Let
T
be a unit vector field in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
of

. Then

(8.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{N_{\beta}}T=-\beta(T)N_{\beta}.

Hence we can write the Lie derivative with respect to

(8.3)

\displaystyle L_{T}=di_{T}+i_{T}d=\nabla_{T}-\sum_{\beta\in\hat{\Phi}^{+}_{G}}\beta(T)e(\omega^{\beta})e^{*}(\omega^{\beta}).

(8.4)

\displaystyle 0=\int_{X}\langle L_{T}h,|h|^{p-2}h\rangle dv=\int_{X}\sum_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{G}}\beta(T)|h|^{p-2}(\frac{1}{p}|h|^{2}-|e^{*}(\omega^{\beta})h|^{2})dv.

Proposition 8.5
.

If there exists a unit vector
T\in\mathfrak{a}
and some
\delta>0
, so that for all
k
-forms

,

(8.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\hat{\Phi}^{+}_{G}}\beta(T)(\frac{1}{p}|h|^{2}-|e^{*}(\omega^{\beta})h|^{2})\geq\delta|h|^{2},

Proof.

Suppose
T
satisfying the hypotheses exists. Let
h
be a
p
-harmonic
k
-form. By (
8.4
), we have

(8.7)

\displaystyle\delta\int_{X}|h|^{p}dv\leq\int_{X}\sum_{\beta\in\hat{\Phi}^{+}_{G}}\beta(T)|h|^{p-2}(\frac{1}{p}|h|^{2}-|e^{*}(\omega^{\beta})h|^{2})dv=0.

Hence
h=0
.
∎

The preceding proposition can be used to prove many vanishing theorems. We explore its application to Conjecture
8.1
.

Theorem 8.8
.

Assume
G
is simple. Then

\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k}(X)=0
, for
k<\text{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(G)
and

.

Proof.

The vanishing of
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{k}(X)
, for

is well known. See
[
Bor85
]
, and more recently, see for example
[
Olb02
]
. So, we are left to treat the case

.
First assume
G
is real split. Let
\mu
denote the maximal root in
\Phi^{+}_{G}
.
Let
T=T_{G}
be the vector in
\mathfrak{a}
which is metrically dual to
\frac{\mu}{|\mu|}
. Then for
\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{G}

\beta(T)\in\{0,\frac{|\mu|}{2},|\mu|\}

|\mu|

Let

Then

(8.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{G}}\frac{\beta(T)}{p}|h|^{2}=\frac{(n_{G}(1)+2)|\mu|}{2p}|h|^{2}.

On the other hand,

(8.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{G}}\beta(T)|e^{*}(\omega^{\beta})h|^{2}\leq(k+1)\frac{|\mu|}{2}|h|^{2}.

Hence condition (
8.6
) is satisfied if
\frac{(n_{G}(1)+2)}{p}>(k+1).
In other words, if

(8.11)

\displaystyle p<\frac{n_{G}(1)+2}{k+1}.

The vanishing of the torsion part of the cohomology can be proved in a similar fashion.

Theorem 8.25
.

Let
G
be a simple Lie group. Then

T_{p}^{k}(X)=0
, for
p<\frac{n_{G}(2)+2n_{G}(1)}{k-1+\min\{k-1,n_{G}(2)\}}
. In particular,
T_{p}^{k}(X)=0
, for
k\leq\text{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(G)
and
p\leq 2

Proof.

Let
T_{G}
be the vector field defined in the proof of Theorem
8.8
. Let
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset B_{p}^{k}(X)
be an

with
z_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L_{p}}}{{\to}}z\in Z_{p}^{k}(X).
Let

denote the

. Then arguing as in Section 6, with
T_{G}
replacing the radial vector field, we have

(8.26)

\displaystyle\int_{X}\sum_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{G}}\beta(T_{G})|b_{j}|^{p-2}(\frac{1}{p}|b_{j}|^{2}-|e^{*}(\omega^{\beta})b_{j}|^{2})dv=-\int_{X}\langle i_{T_{G}}z_{j},|b_{j}|^{p-2}b_{j}\rangle dv.

Hence
\|b_{j}\|_{L_{p}}\leq\frac{|T_{G}|}{\delta}\|z_{j}\|_{L_{p}},
when

(8.27)

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{G}}\beta(T_{G})|b_{j}|^{p-2}(\frac{1}{p}|b_{j}|^{2}-|e^{*}(\omega^{\beta})b_{j}|^{2})\geq\delta|b_{j}|^{2},\text{ for some }\delta>0.

Arguing again as in Section 6, we deduce that

and therefore admits a subsequence that converges to a primitive for

. Hence

is exact, and therefore

B_{p}^{k}(X)
is closed. By (
8.24

8.27
) holds when

. As we have recorded in the proof of Theorem
8.8
, when
k\leq rank_{\mathbb{R}}G
,
p<\frac{n_{G}(2)+2n_{G}(1)}{k-1+\min\{k-1,n_{G}(2)\}}
holds for
p\leq 2
, for all simple Lie groups, except when

for the real split Lie groups of type
A_{n}
and

A_{n}
case. The
C_{n}

Consider an
(n-1)

\phi

\Phi^{+}_{SL_{n+1}}
. Write

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{G}}\beta(T_{G})(\frac{1}{2}|\phi|^{2}-|e^{*}(\omega^{\beta})\phi|^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{G}(1)+2}{2}|\phi|^{2}-|e^{*}(\omega^{\mu})\phi|^{2}-(n-1)|\phi|^{2}+\sum_{\alpha:\alpha(T_{G})=0}|e^{*}(\omega^{\alpha})\phi|^{2}+\sum_{j}|e^{*}(dt^{j})\phi|^{2}

which vanishes only if

where

and all
e^{*}(\omega^{\alpha})
, with
\alpha(T_{G})=0
. Combining (

\displaystyle\int_{X}\sum_{k}|e^{*}(dt^{k})b_{j}|^{2}dv=-2\int_{X}\langle i_{T_{G}}z_{j},b_{j}\rangle dv.

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\|e^{*}(dt^{k})b_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|z_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|b_{j}\|_{L^{2}}.

Remark 8.37
.

In the papers
[
BR20
]
,
[
BR23a
]
, and
[
BR23b
]
, Bourdon and Remy prove numerous vanishing and nonvanishing theorems for
L_{p}
-cohomology of symmetric spaces.
Their vanishing results have significant intersection with those obtained above and also include vanishing results for ranges of

that we have not considered in this note. As in Pansu’s work, their vanishing theorems are to a significant degree founded on the construction of primitives obtained by integration along orbits of flows. Our estimates utilize infinitesimal data associated with these same flows. Hence one expects significant overlap in the results reached by the two methods.

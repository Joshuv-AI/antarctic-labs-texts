Abstract
What can be said about the structure of graphs that do not contain an induced copy of some graph H H ? Rödl showed in the 1980s that every H H -free graph has large parts that are very sparse or very dense. More precisely, let us say that a graph F F on n n vertices is ε \varepsilon -restricted if either F F or its complement has maximum degree at most ε ​ n \varepsilon n . Rödl proved that for every graph H H , and every ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , every H H -free graph G G has a linear-sized set of vertices inducing an ε \varepsilon -restricted graph. We strengthen Rödl’s result as follows: for every graph H H , and all ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , every H H -free graph can be partitioned into a bounded number of subsets inducing ε \varepsilon -restricted graphs.

Introduction

What can be said about the structure of graphs that do not contain an induced copy of some graph
H
?
In the 1980s, Rödl
[
7
]
showed that every
H
-free graph has large parts that are very sparse or very dense.

To say that more precisely we need some definitions.
Graphs in this paper are finite and without loops or parallel edges. If
G
is a graph and
X\subseteq V(G)
, we denote the subgraph
of
G
induced on
X
by
G[X]
, and
\overline{G}
denotes the complement graph of
G
. If
G,H
are graphs, we say that

G
is
H
-free
if no induced subgraph of
G
is isomorphic to
H
.
For a graph
G
, let us say
X\subseteq V(G)
is
weakly
\varepsilon
-restricted

if one of
G[X],\overline{G}[X]
has at most
\varepsilon|X|^{2}
edges; and that
X
is
\varepsilon
-restricted
if one of the graphs
G[X]
,
\overline{G}[X]

has maximum degree at most
\varepsilon|X|
. Rödl
[
7
]
proved the following:

1.1 Theorem
.

For every graph
H
, and all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
H
-free graph
G
,
there is a weakly
\varepsilon
-restricted set
X\subseteq V(G)

with
|X|\geq\delta|G|
.

Every
\varepsilon
-restricted set is weakly
\varepsilon/2
-restricted, and every weakly
\varepsilon/2
-restricted set has a
subset of at least half its size that is
\varepsilon
-restricted. Thus an equivalent version of Rödl’s theorem is the following:

1.2 Theorem
.

For every graph
H
, and all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
H
-free graph
G
,
there is an
\varepsilon
-restricted set
X\subseteq V(G)

with
|X|\geq\delta|G|
.

Rödl’s theorem is an easy consequence of Szemerédi’s regularity lemma, and has proved extremely useful. For example, it is now a standard tool in approaching the Erdős-Hajnal conjecture (see for instance the breakthrough paper
[
2
]
, where it was crucial, and much subsequent work).
A proof of
1.2
not using the regularity lemma (and consequently with much better constants) was given by Fox and Sudakov
[
3
]
.

In this paper, we are concerned with
partitions
of
H
-free graphs such that
every
vertex class is either sparse or dense. It is easy to prove that
H
-free graphs can be partitioned into a bounded number of weakly
\varepsilon
-restricted subsets:

1.3 Theorem
.

For every graph
H
, and all
\varepsilon>0
,
there is an integer
N
such that for every
H
-free graph
G
, there is a partition of
V(G)

into at most
N
weakly
\varepsilon
-restricted subsets.

This can be shown by applying
1.1
repeatedly to partition most of the vertices into weakly

\varepsilon/2
-restricted subsets, and then adding the remaining vertices into the largest set. (See Nikiforov
[
5
]
for a strengthening of
1.3
.)

But what about partitions into sets that satisfy the stronger property of being
\varepsilon
-restricted?
This is much harder, and the main result of this paper is the following:

1.4 Theorem
.

For every graph
H
, and all
\varepsilon>0
,
there is an integer
N
such that for every
H
-free graph
G
, there is a partition of
V(G)

into at most
N

\varepsilon
-restricted subsets.

This is significantly stronger than
1.3
.

Here is a third statement midway between the last two: that under the same hypotheses,
V(G)
is the union of at most a bounded number of
\varepsilon
-restricted subsets (not
necessarily pairwise disjoint). This variation does not seem to be easy, although it does not imply
1.4

Some remarks: sets of cardinality at most two are always
\varepsilon
-restricted, and for
1.4

it is sometimes necessary to use some
\varepsilon
-restricted subsets of cardinality at most two, even in graphs
G

with
|G|
large. For example, let
G
be a star

K_{1,n}
with

large, and let
\varepsilon<1/3
: then every
\varepsilon
-restricted subset containing the centre of the star has cardinality
at most two. (Note that this is not the case for

; for example, a large star is already weakly
\varepsilon
-restricted.)

Second, our proof of
1.4
(and the proof in
[
6
]
of
2.2
, which
we will need to apply) does not use the regularity lemma. Thus we anticipate that the number

in

is significantly smaller
(as a function of

) than numbers that are produced via the regularity lemma, but we have not made an estimate for it.

If

are disjoint, we say that

is

-sparse to

) if every vertex in

has at most
\varepsilon|A|
neighbours in

;
and

is
\varepsilon
-dense
to

if

is
\varepsilon
-sparse to

.
The method of proof of

is via the following statement:

1.5 Theorem
.

For every graph

, and all
\varepsilon,\eta,\theta>0
, there exists an integer

-free graph

Let us give an idea of how
1.5

.
We show in section

\lceil 4/\varepsilon\rceil+1

, say
W_{0},\ldots,W_{K}
, with three properties (that each of

-restricted, and that

The role of

is to deduce something similar for successively shorter sequences of sets, say
W_{0},\ldots,W_{k}
, with
similar hypotheses. (If we can prove it for sequences of length one, then the main result follows, using the one-term sequence

.)
We will need to adjust the parameters of the sequence as

\varepsilon^{\prime}

such that

are
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-restricted, and adjust

To apply
1.5
, we will use induction on
\lceil 4/\varepsilon\rceil-k
; let
W_{0},\ldots,W_{k}
be the sequence we want to handle now. We apply

1.5
to

. That partitions

into a bounded number of
\varepsilon
-sparse sets and the pairs
(A_{s},B_{s})\;(1\leq s\leq m)

as in
1.5
. We could apply induction to the sequence
W_{0},\ldots,W_{k-1},A_{s},B_{s}

for each

, and deduce that the union of
W_{0},\ldots,W_{k-1},A_{s},B_{s}
can be partitioned into a bounded number of
\varepsilon
-restricted
sets for each
s
; but that does not help, since altogether these sets will cover the vertices of
W_{0}\cup\cdots\cup W_{k-1}

m
times instead of once. Instead, we first
partition each of

into
m
large subsets, one for each of the pairs
(A_{1},B_{1}),\ldots,(A_{m},B_{m})
that we need to

W_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,W_{k-1}^{\prime},A_{s},B_{s}
for each

, where

is the “large” subset of

that corresponds to
(A_{s},B_{s})
. The reason we partition each

into “large” subsets, is that then the new sequences
will have parameters not much worse than the sequence

, and so we can apply the inductive hypothesis to them.

Proving the main lemma

In this section we prove
1.5
. Let
A,B\subseteq V(G)
be disjoint, and let
c,\varepsilon>0
.
We say that
(A,B)
is
(c,\varepsilon)
-full
if
for all
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
|A^{\prime}|\geq c|A|
and
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
with
|B^{\prime}|\geq c|B|
, the number of edges between

is at least

\varepsilon|A^{\prime}|\cdot|B^{\prime}|
. Similarly,
(A,B)
is
(c,\varepsilon)
-empty
if it is
(c,\varepsilon)
-full in the complement graph.
Thus if
(A,B)
is
(c,\varepsilon)
-full, and
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
and
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
with
|A^{\prime}|/|A|,|B^{\prime}|/|B|\geq c^{\prime}>c

(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})

-full.

2.1 Lemma
.

Let

, and let

(\varepsilon^{|H|},\varepsilon)

such that the map sending

to

for each

is an isomorphism
from

Proof.
We proceed by induction on

. Since

,

. For each

be the set of neighbours of

, and for each

. Thus each

.
Let

Thus for all distinct

,

-full, and if

To build a new set

By

-restricted, where

There are two main issues to worry about.

•

First, when we applied

to

, both

might shrink by a constant factor, and we need to take care of the “lost”

•

Second, we have to arrange

,

C_{i+1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k}
for all
i\in I
, or

C_{i}
is
2\varepsilon^{\prime}
-dense to
C_{i+1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k}
for all
i\in I
. Let
C=\bigcup_{i\in I\cup\{k\}}C_{i}
.

(3)
C
is
\varepsilon
-restricted.

To see this, suppose first that
C_{i}
is
2\varepsilon^{\prime}
-sparse to
C_{i+1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k}
for all
i\in I
.
Let
v\in C_{j}
where
j\in I\cup\{k\}
, and let
I_{1}=\{i\in I:i<j\}
, and
I_{2}=\{i\in I\cup\{k\}:i>j\}
.
Since
C_{j}
is
2\varepsilon^{\prime}
-sparse to
C_{j+1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k}
, it follows that
v
has at most
2\varepsilon^{\prime}p(k-j)\leq\varepsilon p(k-j)/4

neighbours in
C_{j+1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k}
(and hence at most the same number
in
\bigcup_{i\in I_{2}}C_{i}
). For each
i\in I_{1}
, since
C_{i+1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k}
(and hence
C_{j}
) is
12\varepsilon^{\prime}
-sparse to
C_{i}
, it follows that

v
has at most
12\varepsilon^{\prime}p=\varepsilon p/4

neighbours in
C_{i}
; and therefore
v

has at most
\varepsilon pj/4
neighbours in
\bigcup_{i\in I_{1}}C_{i}
. Since
v
has at most
p
neighbours in
C_{j}
,
it follows
that
v
has at most

\varepsilon p(k-j)/4+\varepsilon pj/4+p=\varepsilon pk/4+p\leq\varepsilon pk/2\leq\varepsilon|C|

neighbours in
C
(here we use that
k\geq 4/\varepsilon
and
|C|\geq pk/2
),
and so
C
is
\varepsilon
-restricted. If
C_{i}
is
2\varepsilon^{\prime}
-dense to
C_{i+1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k}
for all
i\in I
, we use the same
argument in the complement.
This proves (3).

For each
i\in I
, since

|C_{i}|=|W_{k}|\leq 3|W_{i}|/4

and
W_{i}
is
\varepsilon/4
-restricted, it follows that
W_{i}\setminus C_{i}
is
\varepsilon
-restricted.
But then
V(G)
admits a partition into the sets
W_{i}\;(i\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\setminus I)
, the sets

W_{i}\setminus C_{i}\;(i\in I)
, and
C
, and these sets are all
\varepsilon
-restricted. This is a total of

k+1\leq 4/\varepsilon+2
sets, and
4/\varepsilon+2\leq 5/\varepsilon\leq 5/(3\varepsilon^{2})
. This proves
3.3
.

Next we combine
1.5
and
3.3
to prove an analogue of
3.3
for shorter and shorter sequences, and
hence to prove
1.4
. We will show the following (the
-1
at the end is for inductive purposes):

3.4 Theorem
.

Let
H
be a graph, and let
h=|H|^{2}
. Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1/3
, and let
K=\lceil 4/\varepsilon\rceil
.
Let
N
be as in
1.5
,
with
\varepsilon,\eta,\theta
replaced by
\varepsilon/(4(2h)^{K}),1/(3h),\varepsilon/(48(2h)^{K})
respectively.
Let
0\leq k\leq K
, and let

G
be an
H
-free graph admitting a
(k,(2h)^{k-K}\varepsilon/4)
-path-partition
(W_{0},\ldots,W_{k})
.
Then
V(G)
can be partitioned into at most
h^{K-k}(2400/\varepsilon^{2}+N)-1

\varepsilon
-restricted subsets.

Proof.
We may assume that
|H|\geq 2
and so
h\geq 4
.
We proceed by induction on
K-k
. If
K-k=0
then the result follows from
3.3
, so we assume that
k<K
, and the result
holds for
k+1
.
By

1.5
, there is
a partition of
W_{k}
into
nonempty sets

A_{1},\ldots,A_{m},B_{1},\ldots,B_{m},C_{1},\ldots,C_{n},

where
m\leq h
and
n\leq N
, such that:

•

A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
and
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
are
\varepsilon/(4(2h)^{K})
-restricted sets;

•

for
1\leq s\leq m
,
|B_{s}|\leq|A_{s}|/(3h)
;

•

for
1\leq s\leq m
,
B_{s}
is either
\varepsilon/(48(2h)^{K})
-sparse or
\varepsilon/(48(2h)^{K})
-dense to
A_{s}
.

Let
X
be the union of the sets
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
.
Since each of these sets is
\varepsilon/(4(2h)^{K})
-restricted and hence
\varepsilon
-restricted, it follows that
X
can be partitioned into at most

N

\varepsilon
-restricted sets.
If
m=0
, the theorem holds, since
V(G)
is the union of the sets
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
and
W_{0},\ldots,W_{k-1}
, and
n\leq N
and
k\leq 4/\varepsilon
;
so we assume that
m>0
. Consequently
|W_{k}|\geq|A_{1}|\geq 3h|B_{1}|\geq 3h
, and for
0\leq i<k
,
|W_{i}|\geq 12|W_{k}|\geq 36h
;
and hence
|W_{i}|/(2h)\geq 1
.
It follows that
\lceil|W_{i}|/(2h)\rceil\leq|W_{i}|/h
, and therefore there are
h
pairwise disjoint subsets of
W_{i}
,
each of cardinality at least
|W_{i}|/(2h)
. Consequently we may choose subsets
W_{i}^{1},\ldots,W_{i}^{m}
of
W_{i}
, pairwise disjoint and with union

W_{i}
, and each of cardinality at least
|W_{i}|/(2h)
.

(1)
For
1\leq s\leq m
,
(W^{s}_{0},\ldots,W^{s}_{k-1},A_{s},B_{s})
is a
(k+1,(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/4)
-path-partition
of
G[V_{s}]
, where
V_{s}=W^{s}_{0}\cup\cdots\cup W^{s}_{k-1}\cup A_{s}\cup B_{s}
.

To see this, we must show that

•

A_{s}
is
(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/4
-restricted;

•

|A_{s}|\geq 12|B_{s}|
;

•

B_{s}
is either
(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/48
-sparse or
(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/48
-dense to
A_{s}
;

and also that
for
0\leq i\leq k-1
:

•

W^{s}_{i}
is
(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/4
-restricted;

•

|W^{s}_{i}|\geq 12|B_{s}|
;

•

W^{s}_{i+1}\cup\cdots\cup W^{s}_{k-1}\cup A_{s}\cup B_{s}
is either
(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/48
-sparse or
(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/48
-dense to
W^{s}_{i}
.

The first three statements are immediate from the definition of the pair
(A_{s},B_{s})
.
For the last three,
let
0\leq i\leq k-1
. It follows that
W_{i}
is
(2h)^{k-K}\varepsilon/4
-restricted, and since

|W^{s}_{i}|\geq|W_{i}|/(2h)
,
we deduce that
W^{s}_{i}
is
(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/4
-restricted.

To show that
|W^{s}_{i}|\geq 12|B_{s}|
, observe that
|W_{i}|\geq 12|W_{k}|\geq 12|A_{s}|\geq 36h|B_{s}|
,
and so

|W^{s}_{i}|\geq|W_{i}|/(2h)\geq 18|A_{s}|
.

Finally, to show that
W^{s}_{i+1}\cup\cdots\cup W^{s}_{k-1}\cup A_{s}\cup B_{s}
is either
(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/48
-sparse or

(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/48
-dense to
W^{s}_{i}
,
observe that, since
(W_{0},\ldots,W_{k})
is a
(k,(2h)^{k-K}\varepsilon/4)
-path-partition, it follows that

W_{i+1}\cup\cdots\cup W_{k}
is either
(2h)^{k-K}\varepsilon/48
-sparse or
(2h)^{k-K}\varepsilon/48
-dense to
W_{i}
,
and hence so is

W^{s}_{i+1}\cup\cdots\cup W^{s}_{k-1}\cup A_{s}\cup B_{s};

and therefore the latter is either

(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/48
-sparse or
(2h)^{k+1-K}\varepsilon/48
-dense to
W^{s}_{i}
, since
|W^{s}_{i}|\geq|W_{i}|/(2h)
. This proves (1).

From (1) and the inductive hypothesis,
V_{s}
can be partitioned into at most

h^{K-k-1}(2400/\varepsilon^{2}+N)-1

\varepsilon
-restricted subsets, for
1\leq s\leq m
. Since the sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m},C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
are pairwise disjoint and have union
V(G)
,
we deduce that
V(G)
can be partitioned into at most

h(h^{K-k-1}(2400/\varepsilon^{2}+N)-1)+N\leq h^{K-k}(2400/\varepsilon^{2}+N)-1

\varepsilon
-restricted subsets.
This proves
3.4
.

To deduce
1.4
, we may assume that
\varepsilon\leq 1/3
, by reducing
\varepsilon
if necessary; then
1.4

is immediate from
3.4
with
k=0
, applied to the
(0,(2h)^{-K}\varepsilon/4)
-path-partition with one term
V(G)
.

]
has recently proved a strengthening of
1.4
, the following:

3.5 Theorem
.

For every graph
H
, and all
\varepsilon>0
,
there exist
C>0
and an integer

k

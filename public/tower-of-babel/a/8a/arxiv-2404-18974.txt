Abstract
In this article 1 1 1 A first version of this article [ 20 ] was published at the Journal of the London Mathematical Society. Ramsey’s theorem for pairs admits a decomposition into two combinatorially simpler statements, namely, the Ascending Descending Sequence principle and the Erdős-Moser theorem.

2.4

(T)

Given an ordinal

-large
(T)

\bbomega^{b}
-largeness

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}

We now develop a framework for proving that

Given a function

Definition 3.1

(
\mathsf{RT}
-like formula)
.

Given
n,k\in\omega
, an
\mathsf{RT}
-like formula
is a
\Pi_{2}^{1}
-formula of the form

(\forall f:[\mathbb{N}]^{n}\to k)(\exists Y)(Y\mbox{is infinite}\wedge\Psi(f,Y))

\Psi(f,Y)

\Delta^{0}_{0}
-formula.

\mathsf{RT}
-like statements were introduced and studied in the standard realm by Patey
[
25
]
, formulated in terms of forbidden patterns. The idea behind the formula

is that theorems from Ramsey theory are about structure and not the actual value of the domain. Thus, every solution is rewritten as a copy of the integers.

Remark 3.2
.

The notion of
\mathsf{RT}
-like statement is a particular case of Ramsey-like-
\Pi_{2}^{1}
-formula, introduced by defined by Patey and Yokoyama
[
24
]
. It already encompasses Ramsey’s theorem for pairs, the Ascending Descending Sequence principle and the Erdős-Moser theorem. This section could have been formulated and proven in the more general setting of Ramsey-like-
\Pi_{2}^{1}
-formulas, with the cost of some heavier notations as in
[
24
]
.

Since an
\mathsf{RT}
-like statement does not distinguish the solutions from the set of the integers, it proves a slightly stronger formula, stating that the class of solutions is dense in the partial order
([\mathbb{N}]^{\mathbb{N}},\subseteq)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma\equiv(\forall f:[\mathbb{N}]^{n}\to k)(\exists Y)(Y\mbox{is infinite}\wedge\Psi(f,Y))
be an
\mathsf{RT}
-like formula.
Then
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\Gamma
proves

(\forall f:[\mathbb{N}]^{n}\to k)(\forall X\mbox{ infinite})(\exists Y\subseteq X)(Y\mbox{is infinite}\wedge\Psi(f,Y))

Proof.

Let

be a coloring and

be an infinite set.
Let

be defined by
\hat{f}(i_{0},\dots,i_{n-1})=f(x_{i_{0}},\dots,x_{i_{n-1}})
.
By
\Gamma
, there is an infinite set

such that
\Psi(\hat{f},\hat{Y})
.
Let

. We claim that

holds.

Say

with
\Psi_{0}
a
\Delta^{0}_{0}
-formula.
Let
G\subseteq_{\mathtt{fin}}Y
, and let
\hat{G}=\{s:x_{s}\in G\}
.
Say

3.1

(b)

(1)

Definition 3.10

4
\Pi^{1}_{1}
conservation of the grouping principle

The grouping principle was defined by Patey and Yokoyama
[
24
]

as a combinatorial principle needed for an inductive construction of
\bbomega^{n}
-large solutions to the Erdos-Moser theorem. They proved that the grouping principle is
\Pi^{0}_{3}
conservative over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
and that it implies
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
. In this section, we prove that this principle is actually
\Pi^{1}_{1}
conservative over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.

There exist two notions of grouping: a finitary one (
\mathsf{FGP}^{n}_{k}
) and an infinitary one (
\mathsf{GP}^{n}_{k}
), the former following from the latter. We first define the infinitary version.

Definition 4.1

(
\mathcal{L}
-grouping)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a largeness notion and
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{n}\to k
a coloring.
A (finite or infinite) sequence of
\mathcal{L}
-large sets
F_{0}<F_{1}<\dots
of length
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\mathbb{N}\}
is an
\mathcal{L}
-grouping for
f
if for every
H\in[k]^{n}
,
f
is monochromatic on
\prod_{i\in H}F_{i}
.

In particular, for
n=1
,
F_{0}<F_{1}<\dots
is an
\mathcal{L}
-grouping if for every
i
,
F_{i}
is
f
-homogeneous.

For
\vec{f}=f_{0},\dots,f_{k-1}
a finite sequence of colorings of the same arity, we say that
F_{0}<F_{1}<\dots
is an
\mathcal{L}
-grouping for
\vec{f}
if
F_{0}<F_{1}<\dots
is an
\mathcal{L}
-grouping for every
f_{i}
(or, equivalently, if it is an
\mathcal{L}
-grouping for the product coloring
f:x\mapsto(f_{0}(x),\dots,f_{k-1}(x)
).

Statement

(Grouping principle)
.

\mathsf{GP}^{n}_{k}
: For every largeness notion
\mathcal{L}
and every coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{n}\to k
, there is an infinite
\mathcal{L}
-grouping.

The remainder of this section is dedicated to the proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

\mathsf{WKL}_{0}+\mathsf{GP}_{2}^{2}
is
\Pi_{1}^{1}
-conservative over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.

Following the reverse mathematical practice,
\mathsf{GP}^{2}_{2}
can be decomposed into the cohesiveness principle (
\mathsf{COH}
) and a
\Delta^{0}_{2}

\mathsf{GP}^{1}_{2}
(
\Delta^{0}_{2}\mbox{-}\mathsf{GP}^{1}_{2}

Statement
.

\Delta^{0}_{2}\mbox{-}\mathsf{GP}^{1}_{2}
: For every largeness notion
\mathcal{L}
and every
\Delta^{0}_{2}
set

\mathcal{L}
-grouping for
A
.

An infinite set

is

for an infinite sequence of sets

or

(Cohesiveness)

6
A conservation theorem over
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}

Since
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
is a
\forall\Pi^{0}_{4}
statement which is strictly stronger than
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
, then
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
(and
a fortiori

\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
) is not a
\forall\Pi^{0}_{4}
conservative extension of
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
. However, in this section, we shall give two proofs that it is the case for a particular kind of
\forall\Pi^{0}_{4}
formulas.
The first proof uses the techniques developed in this article, while the second is based on the fact that every countable model of
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
admits a
\Sigma^{0}_{n+1}
-elementary cofinal extension which is a model of
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{n+1}
.

Definition 6.1
.

A formula is
weakly
\forall\Pi^{0}_{4}
if it is of the form

\forall P\forall p\exists x\forall y\exists x^{\prime}<x\forall y^{\prime}<y\exists z\theta(P{\upharpoonright}_{z},p,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z)

where
\theta
is a
\Sigma^{0}_{0}
formula.

Intuitively, weakly
\forall\Pi^{0}_{4}
formulas are
\forall\Pi^{0}_{4}
formulas for which the applications of
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
are “hardcoded” in the syntax of the formula.

Proposition 6.2
.

Over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, every
\forall\Pi^{0}_{4}
formula is equivalent to a weakly
\forall\Pi^{0}_{4}
formula.

Proof.

Let
S=\forall P\forall p\exists x\forall y\exists z\theta(P{\upharpoonright}_{z},p,x,y,z)
be a
\forall\Pi^{0}_{4}
formula, where
\theta
is
\Sigma^{0}_{0}
.
Let
S^{\prime}=\forall P\forall p\exists x\forall y\exists x^{\prime}<x\forall y^{\prime}<y\exists z\theta(P{\upharpoonright}_{z},p,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z)
be its corresponding weakly
\forall\Pi^{0}_{4}
formula.

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash S\rightarrow S^{\prime}
. We now prove that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}\vdash S^{\prime}\rightarrow S
.
Fix some
P
and some
p
. By
S^{\prime}
, there is some
x
such that
\forall y\exists x^{\prime}<x\forall y^{\prime}<y\exists z\theta(P{\upharpoonright}_{z},p,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z)
.
Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to x
be defined by
f(y)=x^{\prime}
such that
\forall y^{\prime}<y\exists z\theta(P{\upharpoonright}_{z},p,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z)
. By
\mathsf{RT}^{1}
, which is equivalent to
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, there is some infinite
f
-homogeneous set
H
for some color
x^{\prime}<x
. We claim that
\forall y^{\prime}\exists z\theta(P{\upharpoonright}_{z},p,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z)
.
Indeed, given
y^{\prime}
, there is some
y>y^{\prime}
such that
y\in H
. Since
f(y)=x^{\prime}
, then by definition of
f
,
\exists z\theta(P{\upharpoonright}_{z},p,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z)
. This completes the proof.
∎

For the remainder of this section, fix a
\Sigma^{0}_{0}
-formula
\theta(x,y,z)
(with parameters) and let
T\equiv\forall x\exists y\forall x^{\prime}<x\exists y^{\prime}<y\forall z\theta(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z)
.

Proposition 6.3
.

For every
n\in\omega
,

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+T
proves that for every
k\geq 1
,
\bbomega^{n}\cdot k
-largeness
(T)
is a largeness notion.

Proof.

Exactly the proof of
Proposition

2.7
, but the new form of
T
removes the use of
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

If
\mathsf{WKL}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}\vdash\neg T
, then
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}\vdash\neg T
.

Proof.

Assume
\mathsf{WKL}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}\vdash\neg T
.
Then
\mathsf{WKL}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+T\vdash\bot
.
By
Theorem

1.6
with
\psi\equiv\bot
,
there is some

such that
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
proves

Hence
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
proves that exp-sparse
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness
(T)
is not a largeness notion, therefore, by

,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+T\vdash\bot
. By

),

.

∎

Proposition 6.5
.

\mathsf{WKL}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
is conservative over
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
for weakly
\forall\Pi^{0}_{4}
formulas.

First proof of
Proposition

6.5
.

Fix a
\Sigma^{0}_{0}
-formula
\zeta(P{\upharpoonright}_{z},p,x,y,z)

Suppose

Then there is a model

for some

and

. Enrich the language with a constant symbol for

and

. In particular,

-elementary extension.

Definition 6.6
.

A model

is a

. We write

.

The following theorem first appeared in Paris
[
23
]
, and was independently discovered by Harvey Friedman.

Theorem 6.7

(Paris
[
23
]
; Friedman)
.

Let

and
\mathcal{M}\models\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
. Then there is
\mathcal{N}\succeq_{n+1,\operatorname{cf}}\mathcal{M}
that satisfies
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{n+1}
.

We are now ready to give an alternative proof of
Proposition

6.5
.

Second proof of
Proposition

6.5
.

Fix a
\Sigma^{0}_{0}
-formula
\zeta(P{\upharpoonright}_{z},p,x,y,z)
, and let

T(P,p)\equiv\forall x\exists y\forall x^{\prime}<x\exists y^{\prime}<y\forall z\neg\zeta(P{\upharpoonright}_{z},p,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z)

Suppose
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}\not\vdash\forall P\forall p\neg T(P,p)
.
Then there is a model
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)\models\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}\wedge T(P,p)
for some
P\in S
and
p\in M
.
Enrich the language with a constant symbol for
P
and
p
.
By
Theorem

6.7
, there exists a model
\mathcal{N}=(N,U)\succeq_{2,\operatorname{cf}}\mathcal{M}
of
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.

We claim that
\mathcal{N}\models T(P,p)
. Fix some

. By cofinality, let

. Since

\mathsf{WKL}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}\not\vdash\forall P\forall p\neg T(P,p)

7
Lower bounds for largeness
(T)

The proof structure of the
\Pi^{0}_{4}
conservation theorem for Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colors followed closely the one of Patey and Yokoyama
[
24
]
. In particular, the generalized Parsons theorem for
\mathsf{WKL}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+T
(
Theorem

1.6
) is used together with the
\Pi^{1}_{1}
conservation of the grouping principle, to obtain a finitary version in terms of largeness
(T)
without explicit bounds (
Proposition

4.13
). Kołodziejczyk and Yokoyama
[
18
, Theorem 2.4]
computed explicit bounds to the finite grouping principle in terms of largeness, by iterating a pigeonhole lemma
[
18
, Lemma 2.2]
.

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof that the new bound of
Lemma

2.9
for the pigeonhole principle is optimal.

Definition 7.1
.

An
\bbomega^{n}
-decomposition
of an
\bbomega^{n}
-large set
X
is a finite sequence of
\bbomega^{n-1}
-large subsets
X_{0}<\dots<X_{a}
of
X\setminus\min X
for some
a\geq\min X-1
.
A finite set
X
is
minimal
for
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness if it is
\bbomega^{n}
-large
and for every
x\in X
,
X\setminus\{x\}
is not
\bbomega^{n}
-large.

Note that by regularity of largeness, for every
x,n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is some
y>x
such that
[x,y]
is minimal for
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness.

Lemma 7.2
.

Fix
n>0
. Let
X
be minimal for
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness.
Then it admits a unique
\bbomega^{n}
-decomposition
X_{0}<\dots<X_{a}
.
Moreover,
a=\min X-1
,
\{\min X\}\cup X_{0}\cup\dots X_{a}=X
and for every
i<a
,
X_{i}
is minimal for
\bbomega^{n-1}
-largeness.

Proof.

Let
X
be minimal for
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness for some
n>0
. Let
X_{0}<\dots<X_{a}
be an
\bbomega^{n}
-decomposition of
X
.

First, we claim that
a=\min X-1
. Indeed, if
a>\min X-1
, then
X\setminus X_{a}
would be
\bbomega^{n}
-large, contradicting minimality of
X
for
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness.

Second, we claim that
X_{i}
is minimal for
\bbomega^{n-1}
-largeness. Suppose not. Let
x\in X_{i}
be such that
X_{i}\setminus\{x\}
is
\bbomega^{n-1}
-large. Then
X\setminus\{x\}
would be
\bbomega^{n}
-large, again contradicting minimality of
X
for
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness.

Third, let us prove that
\{\min X\}\cup X_{0}\cup\dots X_{a}=X
. Suppose there is some
x\in X\setminus(\{\min X\}\cup X_{0}\cup\dots X_{a})
. Then once again,
X\setminus\{x\}
would be
\bbomega^{n}
-large.

Last, assume there is another
\bbomega^{n}
-decomposition
Y_{0}<\dots<Y_{a}
of
X
.
Then, by induction over
i\leq a
, we prove that
X_{i}=Y_{i}
. Indeed, assuming that
X_{j}=Y_{j}
for every
j<i
, since
X=\{\min X\}\cup X_{0}\cup\dots\cup X_{a}=\{\min X\}\cup Y_{0}\cup\dots\cup Y_{a}
,
then either
X_{i}\subseteq Y_{i}
, or
Y_{i}\subseteq X_{i}
. By minimality of
X_{i}
and
Y_{i}
for
\bbomega^{n-1}
-largeness,
X_{i}=Y_{i}
. This completes the proof of
Lemma

7.2
.
∎

The previous lemma justifies the following definition:

.

Fix a set

which is minimal for
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness.
The canonical

-block of

is

For

, the canonical

c

, where
X_{0}<\dots<X_{\min X-1}
is the unique
\bbomega^{n}
-decomposition of

.

.

Let
X
minimal for
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness for some

.
The
0
-blockfree

-block of

.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
X
minimal for
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness for some

. Its

-blockfree subset is a minimal
\bbomega^{n-1}

.

Proof.

Proceed by induction on

:

Case

: Let
X
be
\bbomega^{1}
-large, then its

-blockfree subset is equal to
\{\min X\}
which is minimal
\bbomega^{0}
-large.

Case

: Assume the property to be true at rank

, let
X_{0}<\dots<X_{\min X-1}
be the canonical
\bbomega^{n}
-decomposition of

into
\bbomega^{n-1}
-large sets. Let

be the

for every

\bbomega^{n-2}

, therefore the

\bbomega^{n-1}

Definition 7.6
.

Let
X
be minimal for
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness. Consider
\phi_{X}(x,y,c)
a
\Sigma_{0}

x
and
y
are in the same canonical

-block of
X
for

.

Let
\theta_{X}(x,y,z)
be the following
\Sigma_{0}

(x\in X\wedge z\in X\wedge z\geq y)\rightarrow\exists c\leq n,(\phi_{X}(y,z,c)\wedge\neg\phi_{X}(x,y,c)\wedge y>x\wedge y\in X)

Let
T_{X}
be the formula
\forall x\exists y\forall z\theta_{X}(x,y,z)
.

All of these formulas are
\Sigma_{0}
, since there are only finitely many elements of
X
to consider. Note that
n
is uniquely determined by
X
: it is the largest integer less or equal to
\max X
such that
X
is
\bbomega^{n}
-large. Being
\bbomega^{n}
-large is a
\Sigma_{0}
predicate in
X
and

, so the formula
\phi_{X}
is
\Sigma_{0}
uniformly in
X
.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
X
be minimal for
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness for some
n\geq 1
.
For every subsets
A<B
of
X
,
A
and
B
are

-apart if and only if
\theta_{X}(\max A,\min B,\max B)
holds.

Proof.

If
A
and
B
are
T_{X}
-apart, then there exists some
y\leq\min B
such that
\theta_{X}(\max A,y,\max B)
. Since
\max A\in X
,
\max B\in X
and
\max B\geq y
, unfolding the definition of
\theta_{X}
yield
y\in X
,
y>\max A
and there exists some
c\leq n
such that
\phi_{X}(y,\max B,c)
and
\neg\phi_{X}(\max A,y,c)
holds. Since
\min B\in[y,\max B]
and
\min B\in X
,
\phi_{X}(\min B,\max B,c)
also holds, and since

,
\neg\phi_{X}(\max A,\min B,c)
holds. So
\theta_{X}(\max A,\min B,\max B)
holds.

Conversely, if
\theta_{X}(\max A,\min B,\max B)
holds: since
\max A\in X
,
\max B\in X
and
\max B\geq\min B
, there exists some
c\leq n
such that
\phi_{X}(\min B,\max B,c)
and
\neg\phi_{X}(\max A,\min B,c)
holds. To show that
A
and
B
are

-apart, it is sufficient to show that
\theta_{X}(x,\min B,z)
holds for every

and

. If
x\notin X
or

or

then
\theta_{X}(x,\min B,z)

\bbomega

(T_{X})

8
Open questions

There exists a close connection between explicit bounds computation for largeness, and proof speedup theorems. In particular Kołodziejczyk, Wong and Yokoyama
[
17
]
proved that Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colors has at most polynomial speedup over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
for
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
sentences, using the fact that
\bbomega^{300n}
-largeness is sufficient to obtain a homogeneous
\bbomega^{n}
-large set for every instance of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
. The exponential bounds of largeness
(T)
for Ramsey’s theorem for pairs yields the following natural questions:

Question 8.1
.

Does
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
admit exponential proof speedup over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
?

Question 8.2
.

Is there a polynomial
p
such that for every
n
,
every
\bbomega^{p(n)}
-large
(T)
set is
\bbomega^{n}
-large
(T,\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2})
?

The lower bound for the pigeonhole principle uses a formula
T_{X}
which depends on the considered set
X
.

Question 8.3
.

Is there a
\Pi^{0}_{3}
formula
T
such that for every
n\in\omega
, every
\bbomega^{n}
-large
(T,\mathsf{RT}^{1}_{2})
set is
\bbomega^{2n-1}
-large
(T)
?

We note that Question
8.1
is essential for the original question on

-conservation (Question
1.1
). By the discussion of
[
6

]

Thus, a positive answer to Question

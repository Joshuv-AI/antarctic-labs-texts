Core-Level Photoelectron Spectroscopy Study of UTe 2 \mathrm{UTe_{2}}
Shin-ichi Fujimori 1, Ikuto Kawasaki 1, Yukiharu Takeda 1, Hiroshi Yamagami 1,2, Ai Nakamura 3, Yoshiya Homma 3, and Dai Aoki 3

Abstract
The valence state of UTe 2 \mathrm{UTe}_{2} was studied by core-level photoelectron spectroscopy. The main peak position of the U ​ 4 ​ f \mathrm{U}~4f core-level spectrum of UTe 2 \mathrm{UTe}_{2} coincides with that of UB 2 \mathrm{UB}_{2} , which is an itinerant compound with a nearly 5 ​ f 3 5f^{3} configuration. However, the main peak of UTe 2 \mathrm{UTe}_{2} is broader than that of UB 2 \mathrm{UB}_{2} , and satellite structures are observed in the higher binding energy side of the main peak, which are characteristics of mixed-valence uranium compounds. These results suggest that the U ​ 5 ​ f \mathrm{U}~5f state in UTe 2 \mathrm{UTe}_{2} is in a mixed valence state with a dominant contribution from the itinerant 5 ​ f 3 5f^{3} configuration.



The unconventional superconductivity in
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
has attracted much attention in recent years
[
1
,
2
]
.
Its electronic structure is essential to understand the origin of its superconductivity, and angle resolved photoelectron spectroscopy (ARPES) has been applied using soft X-ray (SX,
h\nu=565-800~\mathrm{eV}
)
[
3
]
and vacuum ultraviolet (VUV,
h\nu=30-150~\mathrm{eV}
)
[
4
]
synchrotron radiation.
However, these two ARPES studies presented contradicting views of the electronic structure of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
: The SX ARPES study concluded that the band-structure calculation treating
\mathrm{U}~5f
states as valence electrons can explain the overall electronic structure of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}

[
3
]
while the VUV ARPES study argued that the near-
E_{\mathrm{F}}
electronic structure is very similar to that of the band-structure calculation of
\mathrm{ThTe_{2}}
although there exist heavy bands around the
\mathrm{Z}
point
[
4
]
.
In addition, the partial
\mathrm{U}~5f
density of states (DOS) obtained by resonant photoelectron spectroscopy (RPES) at the
4d-5f
absorption edge (
h\nu=736~\mathrm{eV}
) has a dominant sharp peak at the Fermi energy
[
3
]
, while the on-resonant RPES spectrum measured at the
5d-5f
absorption edge (
h\nu=98~\mathrm{eV}
) has a dominant peak at a higher binding energy of
E_{\mathrm{B}}\sim 0.7~\mathrm{eV}

[
4
]
.
To solve this discrepancy, additional electronic structure studies on
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
are required.
Recently, Thomas
et al.
reported X-ray absorption spectrum (XAS) of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
under ambient and high pressures
[
5
]
.
They argued that
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
exhibits intermediate valence at ambient pressure, suggesting that the
\mathrm{U}~5f
state in
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
is hybridized with the ligand states.
In the present study, we further studied the
\mathrm{U}~5f
valence state of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
using core-level spectroscopy, which has the ability to probe the valence state of the local uranium site
[
6
,
7
]
.
The
\mathrm{U}~4f
spectrum of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
was compared with that of a typical itinerant compound,
\mathrm{UB_{2}}
, and localized compound,
\mathrm{UPd_{3}}
, as well as ferromagnetic superconductors
\mathrm{UGe_{2}}
,
\mathrm{UCoGe}
,
\mathrm{URhGe}
, and
\mathrm{UPt_{3}}
.

Photoemission experiments were conducted on the SX beamline BL23SU at SPring-8
[
8
]
.
The overall energy resolution in the angle-integrated photoelectron spectroscopy experiments at
h\nu=800~\mathrm{eV}
was approximately
140~\mathrm{meV}
.
The kinetic energy of photoelectrons is about
400~\mathrm{eV}
, which is considered to have enough bulk sensitivity since the
\mathrm{U}~4f
spectra of
\mathrm{URu_{2}Si_{2}}
measured at
h\nu=800~\mathrm{eV}

[
6
,
7
]
and
h\nu=5945~\mathrm{eV}

[
9
]
are essentially identical.
The sample temperature was kept at
20~\mathrm{K}
for all measurements.
Other experimental conditions are described in Ref.
\citen
UTe2_ARPES.

\mathrm{UB_{2}}
and
\mathrm{UPd_{3}}
are typical itinerant and localized compounds, respectively.
The band structure and Fermi surface of
\mathrm{UB_{2}}
are well explained by the band-structure calculation treating all
\mathrm{U}~5f
electrons as itinerant
[
10
]
.
The occupation number of the
\mathrm{U}~5f
state within the Muffin-Tin sphere is 2.82 in the calculation; thus, the local
\mathrm{U}~5f
electronic configuration of
\mathrm{UB_{2}}
can be considered the dominant
5f^{3}
configuration.
In contrast,
\mathrm{UPd_{3}}
is a uranium compound with a localized
5f^{2}
configuration.

These spectra all generally consist of a dominant main peak located at
E_{\mathrm{B}}=377-379~\mathrm{eV}
and a broad satellite structure distributed at
E_{\mathrm{B}}=381-387~\mathrm{eV}
.
These complex spectral shape originate from the transition from the ground state to multiple final states with different local
\mathrm{U}~5f
electronic configurations
[
11
]
.
There are several theoretical models of the origin of the
\mathrm{U}~4f
spectral profiles
[
12
,
13
]
; however, the quantitative analysis has not yet been established.
Nevertheless, different final states have different binding energies, which can be used to identify the local electronic configuration in the ground state.
Here, we discuss the electronic structure of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
based on a comparison with typical uranium compounds.

The main peak positions of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
,
\mathrm{UGe_{2}}
,
\mathrm{UCoGe}
, and
\mathrm{URhGe}
are almost identical (
E_{\mathrm{B}}=377-377.3~\mathrm{eV}
), and have a similar asymmetric peak structure with a long tail toward higher binding energies.
Their main peak positions are very similar to that of the itinerant
\mathrm{U}~5f
compound
\mathrm{UB_{2}}
(designated as A in Fig.
1
(b)), and are very different from the spectrum of
\mathrm{UPd_{3}}
(designated as B in Fig.
1
(b)).
This indicates that the dominant final state configurations in
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
as well as
\mathrm{UGe_{2}}
,
\mathrm{UCoGe}
, and
\mathrm{URhGe}
are identical to that of
\mathrm{UB_{2}}
, and the dominant
\mathrm{U}~5f
configurations in the ground states of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
,
\mathrm{UGe_{2}}
,
\mathrm{UCoGe}
, and
\mathrm{URhGe}
are also similar to that of
\mathrm{UB_{2}}
.
In contrast, the main peaks of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
as well as ferromagnetic superconductors are broader than that of
\mathrm{UB_{2}}
.
As seen in the spectrum of
\mathrm{UPd_{3}}
, the main peak consists of two peaks (
E_{\mathrm{B}}=378.9
and
377.2~\mathrm{eV}
), and the broadening in the main peaks of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
,
\mathrm{UGe_{2}}
,
\mathrm{UCoGe}
, and
\mathrm{URhGe}
may originate from a small contribution from the
\mathrm{UPd_{3}}
-type peak on the higher binding energy side of the main peaks, although this has not been resolved experimentally.
Moreover, the core-level spectrum of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
is accompanied by a satellite, which has been observed in the
\mathrm{U}~4f
core-level spectra of strongly-correlated or localized
5f^{2}
uranium compounds.
Thus, these results indicate that the ground state of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
is a mixed valence state with a dominant contribution from the
5f^{3}
configuration and some contribution from the
5f^{2}
configuration.
These result are consistent with the result of SX-ARPES study
[
3
]
and the XAS study
[
5
]
.
In addition, the core-level spectral shape of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
is similar to that of
\mathrm{UGe_{2}}
,
\mathrm{UCoGe}
, and
\mathrm{URhGe}
, which have essentially itinerant but correlated
\mathrm{U}~5f
states
[
14
,
15
,
7
]
, suggesting that
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
should be similar to them.

Here, we consider the relationship between the present result and the results of other studies on the electronic structure of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
.
In density functional theory (DFT) plus Hubbard
U
(DFT+
U
) and generalized gradient approximation plus
U
(GGA+
U
) with
U\gtrsim 2~\mathrm{eV}
, quasi-two-dimensional Fermi surfaces have been predicted
[
16
,
17
]
.
In these calculations, most of the
\mathrm{U}~5f
weight was away from the Fermi level by the introduction of the
U
, and the topology of the Fermi surface becomes almost identical to that of the DFT calculation for
\mathrm{ThTe_{2}}
.
Experimentally, the VUV ARPES study reported very similar near-
E_{\mathrm{F}}
electronic structure, although the existence of a heavy band around the
\mathrm{Z}
point was claimed
[
4
]
.
Furthermore, the VUV-RPES spectrum was interpreted based on the ground state with the dominant
5f^{2}
Hund’s rule ground state, which is based on the slightly mixed valent but essentially localized
5f^{2}
state
[
4
]
.
In such situation, its core-level spectrum should be similar to those of the localized compound
\mathrm{UPd_{3}}
or weakly hybridized compound
\mathrm{UPt_{3}}
.
However, the present result indicates that the hybridized (itinerant)
5f^{3}
configuration is dominant in the ground state of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
, and the
\mathrm{U}~5f
states should thus make dominant contributions to the state at the Fermi level.
The very different nature of
\mathrm{U}~5f
states observed in the VUV ARPES study may originate from the enhanced surface sensitivity of VUV PES (
\lesssim 5~\mathrm{\AA}
) compared with SX-PES (
\gtrsim 15~\mathrm{\AA}
), as similar discrepancies have been observed in strongly correlated
f
-electron materials
[
18
,
7
,
19
]
.

In summary, we applied core-level spectroscopy to
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
.
A comparison between the core-level spectral shape of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
and that of typical compounds demonstrated that the local electronic configuration of the
\mathrm{U}~5f
state in
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
is in the mixed valence state with a dominant contribution from the
5f^{3}
configuration.
Furthermore, the spectrum of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
is very similar to that of
\mathrm{UGe_{2}}
,
\mathrm{UCoGe}
, and
\mathrm{URhGe}
, suggesting that
\mathrm{U}~5f
should essentially have itinerant character, although there exist electron correlation effects.
The result indicates that the topology of the Fermi surface of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
should be considerably different from the localized model, such as the DFT calculation for
\mathrm{ThTe_{2}}
.

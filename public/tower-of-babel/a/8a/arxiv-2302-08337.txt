Polyocollection ideals and primary decomposition of polyomino ideals
Carmelo Cisto, Francesco Navarra, Dharm Veer

Abstract
. In this article, we study the primary decomposition of some binomial ideals. In particular, we introduce the concept of polyocollection , a combinatorial object that generalizes the definitions of collection of cells and polyomino, that can be used to compute a primary decomposition of non-prime polyomino ideals. Furthermore, we give a description of the minimal primary decomposition of non-prime closed path polyominoes. In particular, for such a class of polyominoes, we characterize the set of all zig-zag walks and show that the minimal prime ideals have a very nice combinatorial description.

Introduction

A polyomino is a finite collection of unit squares with vertices at lattice points in the plane joined edge by edge.
Qureshi
[
21
]
associated a finite type
K
-algebra

(over a field

.
An interesting question here is to relate the algebraic properties of the

-algebra

with the combinatorial properties of the polyomino

.
For certain classes of polyominoes, some algebraic properties of the associated algebra have been studied. For example,
the graded minimal free resolution of

for convex polyominoes has been studied in
[
8
,
10
]

and the Hilbert series of

has been studied in
[

,
18
,
22
,
17
,

]
.

We say that a polyomino

is prime (resp. non-prime) if the defining ideal of

, denoted by

, is prime (resp. non-prime).
The ideal

is called the

.
It is known that simple polyominoes are prime
[
15
,
23
]
.
Hibi-Qureshi
[
16
]
and Shikama
[
25
]
proved that the non-simple polyominoes obtained by removing a convex polyomino from a rectangle are prime.
Characterizing prime polyominoes is a difficult task;
to do that Mascia et. al.
[
19
]
defined zig-zag walks (see Definition
4.2
) for polyominoes.
They proved
[

that the existence of zig-zag walks determines the non-primality of the polyomino
and conjectured that
[
19
, Conjecture 4.6]
this property characterizes non-prime polyominoes.
This conjecture has been verified for certain classes of polyominoes, for example, for grid polyominoes
[
19
]
, for closed path polyominoes
[
3
]
and for weakly closed path polyominoes
[
4
]
.
In
[
20
]
, Mascia et. al. have determined some prime polyominoes using Gröbner basis.

Concerning other algebraic properties, several mathematicians have studied the combinatorial description of the height of polyomino ideals. For some classes of polyominoes, it has been proved that the height of the polyomino ideal
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
is the number of cells of
{\mathcal{P}}
.
For instance, Qureshi
[
21
]
proved this for convex polyominoes.
Herzog-Madani
[
15
]
extended this to simple polyominoes.
Extending this further, Herzog et. al.
[
13
]
recently proved this for the case when the polyomino ideal is unmixed.
Dinu and the second author of this article
[
7
]
proved this for closed path polyominoes and conjectured that this holds for all polyomino ideals. In this regard, we show that if removing a cell makes the polyomino simple, then the above conjecture holds (Proposition
4.10
).

In this article, we provide some properties about primary decomposition of polyomino ideals and we study the minimal primary decomposition of non-prime closed path polyominoes. The primary decomposition have been studied for some classes of binomial ideals, for instance in
[
12
]
for binomial edge ideals and in
[
11
]
for ideals generated by adjacent 2-minors. The first results explained in this paper allows to consider something similar for polyomino ideals. In particular, supported by some examples obtained by using the software
Macaulay2
(
[
9
]
) and inspired by the concept of
admissible set
in
[
11
]
, we find that for studying the primary decomposition of polyomino ideals one can consider a larger class of binomial ideals. This class is related to a combinatorial object that generalizes the concept of collection of cells and polyominoes. We call such a class of combinatorial objects
polyocollections
. For non-prime closed path polyominoes we provide, in our main result, a detailed description of the minimal primary decomposition.
We show that the polyomino ideal is the intersection of two minimal prime ideals (Theorem
4.19
), and both minimal prime ideals have a very nice combinatorial description.
One of the minimal primes is the toric ideal appeared in
[
19
, Section 3]
(see Theorem
4.16
).
We characterize zig-zag walks of non-prime closed path polyominoes and use that to define the other minimal prime which is generated by monomials and binomials. Finally, we also show that the height of these two ideals is equal to the number of the cells of the polyomino, and as a consequence the polyomino ideal is unmixed.

The article is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some basic notions on combinatorics related to polyominoes. In Section
3
, we define polyocollections and we associate a binomial ideal to it, generalizing the ideal associated to a collection of cells in
[
21
]
. Moreover, we provide a characterization of the primality of that binomial ideal in terms of a lattice ideal attached to the polyocollection and we give a primary decomposition of the radical of the ideal associated to the polyocollection. Section
4
is devoted to study the minimal primary decomposition of closed path polyominoes having zig-zag walks, equivalently the polyomino is non-prime (see
[
3
]
). In the last section, we highlight some possible future directions and open questions about polyominoes and polyocollection ideals.

3.1.
The lattice ideal related to a polyocollections

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a polyocollection. For
a\in V(\mathcal{C})
denote with
\mathbf{v}_{a}
the vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{|V(\mathcal{C})|}
having
1
in the coordinate indexed by
a
and
0
in all other coordinates. If
[a,b]\in\mathcal{C}
is an interval having
a,b
as diagonal corners, and
c,d
as anti-diagonal corners, we denote
\mathbf{v}_{[a,b]}=\mathbf{v}_{a}+\mathbf{v}_{b}-\mathbf{v}_{c}-\mathbf{v}_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{|V(\mathcal{C})|}
.
We define
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
the sub-lattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{|V(\mathcal{C})|}
generated by the vectors
\mathbf{v}_{I}
for all
I\in\mathcal{C}
.

Let
n=|V(\mathcal{C})|
, we recall some known notations. If
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
we denote as usual with
x^{\mathbf{v}}
the monomial in
S_{\mathcal{C}}
having
\mathbf{v}
as exponent vector. If
\mathbf{e}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we denote with
\mathbf{e}^{+}
the vector obtained from
\mathbf{e}
by replacing all negative components by zero, and
\mathbf{e}^{-}=-(\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{e}^{+})
.

Let
L_{\mathcal{C}}
be the lattice ideal of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
, that is the following binomial ideal in
S_{\mathcal{C}}
:

L_{\mathcal{C}}=(\{x^{\mathbf{e}^{+}}-x^{\mathbf{e}^{-}}\mid\mathbf{e}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}\})

In
[
21
, Theorem 3.5]
it is shown that if
\mathcal{P}
is a collection of cells then
L_{\mathcal{P}}
is a prime ideal. Using the same argument, we can prove that the same holds considering also polyocollections. We provide the proof of this fact for completeness.

Denote by
F(\mathcal{C})
the set of elements in
V(\mathcal{C})
that are not lower left corner of an interval in
\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a polyocollection and
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{v}_{I}\mid I\in\mathcal{C}\}\cup\{\mathbf{v}_{a}\mid a\in F(\mathcal{C})\}
. Then
\mathcal{B}
is a basis of the group
\mathbb{Z}^{|V(\mathcal{C})|}
.

Proof.

Let
n=|V(\mathcal{C})|
and
M
be the
n\times n
matrix whose column vectors are
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{v}_{I}\mid I\in\mathcal{C}\}\cup\{\mathbf{v}_{a}\mid a\in F(\mathcal{C})\}
. We refer to
M
as the matrix associated to
\mathcal{C}
. Observe that the columns of
M
can be labelled with the set
\{I\in\mathcal{C}\}\cup\{a\in F(\mathcal{C})\}
and the rows can be labelled with the set
V(\mathcal{C})
. In order to prove our result we show that
\det(M)\neq 0
, by induction on
|\mathcal{C}|
. If
|\mathcal{C}|=1
it is easy to check that
\det(M)=\pm 1
. Suppose
|\mathcal{C}|>1
and let
a\in V(\mathcal{C})
be such that it is a minimal element under the natural partial order on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
. Observe that in this case there exists an interval
J=[a,b]
in
\mathcal{C}
such that
a\notin I
for all
I\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{J\}
. Denote by
c,d
the anti-diagonal corner of
J
. We distinguish three cases.

Case 1

Either
c\in F(\mathcal{C})
or
d\in F(\mathcal{C})
. Without loss of generality, we consider
c\in F(\mathcal{C})
and
d\notin F(\mathcal{C})
. Let
M^{\prime}
be the matrix obtained by removing the row labelled with
a
and the column labelled by
J
. Observe that the column removed contains all
0
except for
1
in the position of
a
, so
\det(M)=\pm\det(M^{\prime})
. Moreover, let
M^{\prime\prime}
be the matrix obtained from
M^{\prime}
by removing the row labelled with
c
and the column labelled by
c
. It is easy to verify that
\det(M^{\prime})=\pm\det(M^{\prime\prime})
, so
\det(M)=\pm\det(M^{\prime\prime})
. Furthermore, it is not difficult to verify that
M^{\prime\prime}
is the matrix associated to the polyocollection
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}\setminus\{J\}
, so by induction
\det(M^{\prime\prime})\neq 0
, that is
\det(M)\neq 0
.

Case 2

Both
c\in F(\mathcal{C})
and
d\in F(\mathcal{C})
. In this case we can use the same argument, considering
\det(M)=\pm\det(M^{\prime\prime\prime})
, where
M^{\prime\prime\prime}
is the matrix associated to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}\setminus\{J\}
and it is obtained from
M
by removing the rows labelled with
a,c,d
and the columns labelled by
J,c,d
.

Case 3

c
and
d
are both the lower left corner of an interval in
\mathcal{C}
. Also in this case we can use the same argument, considering
\det(M)=\pm\det(M^{\prime})
, where
M^{\prime}
is the matrix associated to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}\setminus\{J\}
and it is obtained from
M
by removing the row labelled with
a
and the column labelled by
J
.

∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a polyocollection. Then
L_{\mathcal{C}}
is a prime ideal.

Proof.

Let
n=|V(\mathcal{C})|
. By Lemma
3.5
, every
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
can be expressed in an unique way as
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of the vectors in
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{v}_{I}\mid I\in\mathcal{C}\}\cup\{\mathbf{v}_{a}\mid a\in F(\mathcal{C})\}
.
In particular, for all
a\in V(\mathcal{C})
, we have
\mathbf{v}_{a}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\lambda_{I}^{(a)}\mathbf{v}_{I}+\sum_{b\in F(\mathcal{C})}\mu_{b}^{(a)}\mathbf{v}_{b}
, with
\lambda_{I}^{(a)},\mu_{b}^{(a)}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We define the following map:

\psi:S_{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow K[\{y_{b}^{\pm 1}\mid b\in F(\mathcal{C})\}]\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ \ x_{a}\mapsto\prod_{b\in F(\mathcal{C})}y_{b}^{\mu_{b}^{(a)}}.

It is not difficult to check that if
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\lambda_{I}^{(v)}\mathbf{v}_{I}+\sum_{b\in F(\mathcal{C})}\mu_{b}^{(v)}\mathbf{v}_{b}
, with
\lambda_{I}^{(v)},\mu_{b}^{(v)}\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
\psi(x^{\mathbf{v}})=\prod_{b\in F(\mathcal{C})}y_{b}^{\mu_{b}^{(v)}}
. Observe that
\ker\psi
is a toric ideal, in particular it is prime. We show that
L_{\mathcal{C}}=\ker\psi
.

\subseteq)
Let
x^{\mathbf{v}}-x^{\mathbf{w}}\in L_{\mathcal{C}}
, then
\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
. So
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{w}+\sum_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\alpha_{I}\mathbf{v}_{I}
, with
\alpha_{I}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and by construction the coefficients
\alpha_{I}
of
\mathbf{v}_{I}
, for
I\in\mathcal{C}
, does not contribute to the computation of
\psi(x^{\mathbf{v}})
. Therefore
\psi(x^{\mathbf{v}})=\psi(x^{\mathbf{w}})
.

\supseteq)
Let
x^{\mathbf{v}}-x^{\mathbf{w}}
be a minimal generator of
\ker\psi
. In particular,
\mathrm{supp}(x^{\mathbf{v}})\cap\mathrm{supp}(x^{\mathbf{w}})=\emptyset
, that is
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
\mathbf{e}^{+}=\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{e}^{-}=\mathbf{w}
. Moreover
\psi(x^{\mathbf{v}})=\psi(x^{\mathbf{w}})=\prod_{b\in F(\mathcal{C})}y_{b}^{\mu_{b}}
with
\mu_{b}\in\mathbb{Z}
. This means that
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\lambda_{I}^{(v)}\mathbf{v}_{I}+\sum_{b\in F(\mathcal{C})}\mu_{b}^{(v)}\mathbf{v}_{b}
and
\mathbf{w}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\lambda_{I}^{(w)}\mathbf{v}_{I}+\sum_{b\in F(\mathcal{C})}\mu_{b}^{(w)}\mathbf{v}_{b}
with
\mu_{b}^{(v)}=\mu_{b}^{(w)}=\mu_{b}
for each
b\in F(\mathcal{C})
. So
\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{C}}(\lambda_{I}^{(v)}-\lambda_{I}^{(w)})\mathbf{v}_{I}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
, in particular
x^{\mathbf{v}}-x^{\mathbf{w}}\in L_{\mathcal{C}}
.
∎

By the previous results, observe also that
L_{\mathcal{C}}
does not contain monomials. In fact, since it is a prime ideal, if it contains monomials then it contains a variable
x_{a}
for
a\in V(\mathcal{C})
. But all elements in
L_{\mathcal{C}}
have degree greater than 2.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a polyocollection. Then there exists a monomial

such that
L_{\mathcal{C}}=(I_{\mathcal{C}}:u)
. In particular
I_{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq L_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

The proof is exactly the same as that provided by
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
for a collection of cells.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a polyocollection. Then
I_{\mathcal{C}}
is a prime ideal if and only if
I_{\mathcal{C}}=L_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

If
I_{\mathcal{C}}=L_{\mathcal{C}}
, then trivially
I_{\mathcal{C}}
is prime by Theorem
3.6
. Suppose that
I_{\mathcal{C}}
is prime. By Lemma
3.7
we have that
I_{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq L_{\mathcal{C}}
and
L_{\mathcal{C}}=(I_{\mathcal{C}}:u)
for some monomial
u\in S_{\mathcal{C}}
. So, if
f\in L_{\mathcal{C}}
then
fu\in I_{\mathcal{C}}
and observe that
u\notin I_{\mathcal{C}}
. Since
I_{\mathcal{C}}
is prime then
f\in I_{\mathcal{C}}
, so we proved
I_{\mathcal{C}}\supseteq L_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Remark 3.9
.

Let

be a polyocollection and

if and only if

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a polyocollection and suppose that
I_{\mathcal{C}}
is not a prime ideal. Then for every minimal prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
I_{\mathcal{C}}
, there exists an admissible set
X
such that
\mathfrak{p}=J_{X}

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a minimal prime ideal of
I_{\mathcal{C}}
and let
Y=\{a\in V(\mathcal{C})\mid x_{a}\in\mathfrak{p}\}
. Note that
(\{x_{a}\mid a\in Y\})+I_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathfrak{p}
. We show that
Y
is an admissible set for
\mathcal{C}
. In fact, if
Y
is not an admissible set, then there exists an inner interval
I
of
\mathcal{C}
such that
|Y\cap V(I)|=1
or
Y\cap V(I)=\{a,b\}
with
a,b
both diagonal (or anti-diagonal) corners of
I
. So, considering the binomial
f_{I}=x_{a}x_{b}-x_{c}x_{d}
, we obtain that either
x_{a}x_{b}\in\mathfrak{p}
with
a,b\notin Y
or
x_{c}x_{d}\in\mathfrak{p}
with
c,d\notin Y
, that is a contradiction for the primality of
\mathfrak{p}
.

Now, we can write
(\{x_{a}\mid a\in Y\})+I_{\mathcal{C}}=(\{x_{a}\mid a\in Y\})+I_{\mathcal{C}^{(Y)}}
and it is contained in
\mathfrak{p}
. If
f\in L_{\mathcal{C}^{(Y)}}
then by Lemma
3.7
, there exists a monomial

such that

, and in particular
\mathrm{supp}(u)\cap\{x_{a}\mid a\in Y\}=\emptyset
. As consequence
u\notin\mathfrak{p}
, otherwise from the primality of
\mathfrak{p}
there exists a
x_{a}\in\mathrm{supp}(u)
such that
x_{a}\in\mathfrak{p}
, obtaining
a\in Y
, that is a contradiction. Therefore

, so
(\{x_{a}\mid a\in Y\})+L_{\mathcal{C}^{(Y)}}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
and by the minimality of

we obtain

.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a polyocollection. Then
\sqrt{I_{\mathcal{C}}}=\bigcap_{X}J_{X}
where
X
moves overall the admissible sets of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

We have
I_{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\bigcap_{X}J_{X}
where
X
moves overall the admissible sets of
\mathcal{C}
, so
\sqrt{I_{\mathcal{C}}}\subseteq\bigcap_{X}J_{X}
. Moreover each minimal prime of
I_{\mathcal{C}}
is also a minimal prime of
\sqrt{I_{\mathcal{C}}}
. Finally, since a radical ideal is the intersection of its minimal prime
ideals, the assertion of the theorem will follow.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a polyocollection and

is radical, then Theorem

provides a primary decomposition of it. Such decomposition is not minimal since the converse of Proposition

4.
Primary decomposition of closed path polyominoes

In this section, we consider a particular class of polyominoes, called

We already observed that a collection of cells (or a polyomino in particular) is also a polyocollection, but with respect to the previous section we give a more detailed (and combinatorial) description of the primary decomposition, taking advantage of the structure of closed path.

4.1.
Closed paths and their zig-zag walks

.

Let

Proof.

Let

then

Some questions arise from the results obtained in this work:

(a)

If
\mathcal{P}
is a collection of cells it is well know that some properties of the ideal
I_{\mathcal{P}}
are related to the combinatorics of
\mathcal{P}
. For instance it is well known that if
\mathcal{P}
is simple then
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is a normal Cohen-Macaulay domain of dimension
V(\mathcal{P})-|\mathcal{P}|
(see
[
15
,
23
]
). Also the Gorenstein property is known for some classes of polyominoes (see
[

,
6
,
21
,
22
,
24
]
). Moreover, referring to Figure
16
, note that
I_{\mathcal{P}^{(\{a,b\})}}
is not prime. In fact, if
\mathcal{W}
is a zig-zag walk of the polyomino in Figure
16a
, it is not difficult to see that the binomial
f_{\mathcal{W}}
is a zero divisor of
I_{\mathcal{P}^{(\{a,b\})}}
(by the same arguments explained at the beginning of Section
4
). Furthermore,
I_{\mathcal{P}^{(\{a,b\})}}
is radical, since the set of generators forms the reduced Gröbner basis with respect to a suitable order obtained as done in
[
5
]
. In light of the previous considerations, we wonder if some results holding for polyominoes can be extended also for polyocollections.

(b)

For a collection of cells
\mathcal{P}
, it is an open question whether the ideal
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is always radical. If this is true, then Theorem
3.13
always provides a primary decomposition of it. Such a question can be extended naturally for the ideal
I_{\mathcal{C}}
related to a polyocollection. Another property that could be investigated is the unmixedness for the ideal related to a polyomino (and in general for a polyocollection), that we find for closed paths.

(c)

In order to have a better description of the primary decomposition provided in Theorem
3.13
, we should have a better understanding of the ideal
L_{\mathcal{C}}
for a polyocollection
\mathcal{C}
. For a closed path
\mathcal{P}
we have proved that
L_{\mathcal{P}}=I_{\mathcal{P}}+Z_{\mathcal{P}}
, where
Z_{\mathcal{P}}
is the ideal generated by the binomials
f_{\mathcal{W}}
related to zig-zag walks. Anyway we know that this is not true for all polyominoes, as shown in
[
19
, Example 3.8]
. In general, for a polyomino
\mathcal{P}
,
L_{\mathcal{P}}=I_{\mathcal{P}}+T_{\mathcal{P}}
where
T_{\mathcal{P}}
is a binomial ideal and if
\mathcal{W}
is a zig-zag walk then
f_{\mathcal{W}}\in T_{\mathcal{P}}
. It would be interesting to know the (combinatorial) structure of the generators of
T_{\mathcal{P}}
for other classes of polyominoes (or polyocollections)
\mathcal{P}
.

(d)

For a closed path
\mathcal{P}
, we find that the minimal prime ideal of
I_{\mathcal{P}}
, different to
L_{\mathcal{P}}
, is related to an admissible set containing the (unique) necklace of all zig-zag walks. Anyway, in general a polyomino with zig-zag walks has different necklaces related to different zig-zag walks. The concept of zig-zag walk can be formulated also for polyocollections. In general, if
\mathcal{C}
is a polyocollection, we ask if in the primary decomposition provided in Theorem
3.13
, the minimal primes of
I_{\mathcal{C}}
different to
L_{\mathcal{C}}
are ideals of kind
J_{X}
where
X
is related in some way to a necklace of a zig-zag walk. Examples with
Macaulay2
suggest such a behavior for some polyomino ideals.

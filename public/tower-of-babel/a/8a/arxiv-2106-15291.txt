2D Navier-Stokes system and no-slip boundary condition for vorticity in exterior domains
A. V. Gorshkov

Abstract
. In this article we derive Biot-Savar law for exterior 2D domain. No-slip condition in terms of vorticity is expressed in integral form. For non-stationary fluid dynamics equations no-slip condition generates affine invariant manifold and no-slip integral relations on vorticity can be transferred to Robin-type boundary condition.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 76D07; Secondary 33C10

Preliminary

Many papers as well as current one are devoted to the question - does there exist any boundary condition for vorticity which corresponds to no-sleep ones - zero velocity on the boundary of the solid. In a playful way we can say that dolphins know the answer on this question, but keep silence. Dolphin’s skin can read vorticity distribution in order to prevent turbulent flow.

But of course the answer on the above question is negative - there is no any analogous to no-slip condition only in terms of vorticity without additional functions involved in (e.g. stream function, velocity). Biot-Savar law restores solenoidal velocity field
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})
induced by vorticity
w(\mathbf{x})
. It expresses vorticity via velocity field by some integral relations. If the flow interacts with solid by no-slip condition, then it turns to zero integral relations in Biot-Savar law which do not admit explicit integration. It seems to negate all efforts to find boundary vorticity. Nevertheless, the situation isn’t so definite.

Why it is so important to derive vorticity boundary condition? Fluid dynamics equations written in terms of vorticity favorably differ from ones covering velocity evolution. So, for 2D and 3D Navier-Stokes system the vorticity dynamics involves fewer number of equations and fewer number of unknown functions. If the system doesn’t involve boundary condition (e.g. Cauchy problem), then
\operatorname{curl}
operator significantly simplifies corresponding
initial-value problem
. But in case of
initial-boundary-value problem
it isn’t true by the reason above - Dirichlet no-slip boundary turn out to sophisticated integral condition.

Consider initial-boundary-value problem for the nonstationary Navier-Stokes system defined in exterior domain
\Omega
modelling flow around solid with given constant horizontal flow at infinity
\mathbf{v}_{\infty}=(\mathbf{v}_{1,\infty},\mathbf{v}_{2,\infty})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}-\Delta\mathbf{v}+(\mathbf{v},\nabla)\mathbf{v}=0=\nabla p

(0.1)

\displaystyle{\rm div}\penalty\ \mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})=0

(0.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(0,\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})

(0.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})=0,\penalty\ |\mathbf{x}|=r_{0}

(0.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})\to\mathbf{v}_{\infty},\penalty\ |\mathbf{x}|\to\infty.

(0.5)

Here
\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})=(v_{1}(t,\mathbf{x}),v_{2}(t,\mathbf{x}))
is the velocity field and
p(t,\mathbf{x})
is the pressure.

Applying the curl operator

w(t,\mathbf{x})=

{\rm curl}\penalty\ \mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})

=\partial_{\mathbf{x}_{1}}v_{2}-\partial_{\mathbf{x}_{2}}v_{1}
we get the system of equations for vorticity

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w(t,\mathbf{x})}{\partial t}-\Delta w+(\mathbf{v},\nabla)w=0,

(0.6)

\displaystyle w(0,\mathbf{x})=w_{0}(\mathbf{x})

(0.7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{curl}^{-1}w(t,\mathbf{x})\Big|_{|\mathbf{x}|=r_{0}}=0,

(0.8)

\displaystyle w(t,\mathbf{x})\to 0,\penalty\ |\mathbf{x}|\to\infty

(0.9)

with initial datum
w_{0}(\mathbf{x})={\rm curl}\penalty\ \mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})
.

The inverse operator
\operatorname{curl}^{-1}
arises a lot of questions like existence and uniqueness. Exterior domain with one additional boundary point at infinity only complicates this situation. We will find sufficient condition for well-posedness of this operator.

For 2D Navier-Stokes system and its approximations - Stokes and Oseen equations - we will derive boundary condition for vorticity. It will be expressed in terms of Fourier coefficients, so it will implicitly involve angular integration, but nevertheless, it will be pure initial-boundary-value problem.

In the paper we will exploit
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as real as well as complex plane depending on the context.
For points from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
we will use different real and complex notations including polar coordinates
r,\varphi
such as

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})
,
z=x_{1}+ix_{2}
,

.

1.
Biot-Savar law in exterior domains

Now we study when the solenoidal velocity field
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})
can be uniquely restored from its vorticity
w(\mathbf{x})
. Consider the following elliptic problem corresponding to Biot-Savar law in exterior domain
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\rm{div}\penalty\ \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\rm{curl}\penalty\ \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})=w(\mathbf{x}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})=0,\penalty\ \mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\to\mathbf{v}_{\infty},\penalty\ |\mathbf{x}|\to\infty,.

(1.4)

Exterior domains are not simply connected and the problem above could haven’t uniquie solution. For example, equations (
1.1
), (
1.2
) supplied with slip condition on the boundary

\left(\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{n}\right)=0,\penalty\ \mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega,

(1.5)

and fixed flow at infinity (
1.4
) have unique solution only if we fix circularity at infinity (
\mathbf{n}
is an outer normal to boundary). No-slip condition (
1.3
) is stronger than (
1.5
), and so some additional restrictions on
w(\mathbf{x})
are required. These restrictions can be realized via moment relations for vorticity. In
[
3
]
the solvability of the system above was researched in details for slip and no-slip conditions in exterior of the disc. Here we extend these results on more general domains and obtain boundary analogue of no-slip condition.

So, we need to fix circularity at infinity. From physical point of view it is natural to suppose zero-circularity:

\lim_{R\to\infty}\oint_{|\mathbf{x}|=R}\mathbf{v}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=0.

(1.6)

The relationship between Cartesian and polar coordinate systems for
\mathbf{v}_{\infty}
is given by formulas:

\displaystyle v_{\infty,r}=v_{\infty,x}\cos\varphi+v_{\infty,y}\sin\varphi

\displaystyle v_{\infty,\phi}=v_{\infty,y}\cos\varphi-v_{\infty,x}\sin\varphi.

Then its Fourier coefficients are determined as

\displaystyle v_{\infty,r,k}=\frac{\delta_{|k|,1}}{2}(v_{\infty,x}-ikv_{\infty,y})

(1.7)

\displaystyle v_{\infty,\phi,k}=\frac{\delta_{|k|,1}}{2}(v_{\infty,y}+ikv_{\infty,x}),

(1.8)

and

\displaystyle v_{\infty,\phi,k}=\left\{{\begin{matrix}iv_{\infty,r,k},k\geq 0,\\
-iv_{\infty,r,k},k<0.\end{matrix}}\right.

(1.9)

All Fourier coefficients of external flow equal to zero except
k=\pm 1
. For horizontal flow
\mathbf{v}_{\infty}=(v_{\infty},0)

\displaystyle v_{\infty,r,k}=\frac{\delta_{|k|,1}}{2}v_{\infty}

\displaystyle v_{\infty,\phi,k}=ik\frac{\delta_{|k|,1}}{2}v_{\infty}.

We have standard equities

v_{\infty,r,k}=\overline{v_{\infty,r,-k}},v_{\infty,\phi,k}=\overline{v_{\infty,\phi,-k}}.

2.
Boundary condition in exterior of the disc

In this section the domain under investigation will be exterior of the disc
B_{r_{0}}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\penalty\ |\mathbf{x}|>r_{0}\},\penalty\ r_{0}>0

2.1.
Biot-Savar law

In polar coordinates equations (
1.1

1.2

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{r}}{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}\left(rv_{r,k}\right)+{\frac{ik}{r}}v_{\varphi,k}=0,

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{r}}{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}\left(rv_{\varphi,k}\right)-{\frac{ik}{r}}v_{r,k}=w_{k}.

The basis for solutions of homogeneous system when

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}v^{1}_{r,k}\\
v^{1}_{\varphi,k}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ir^{-k-1}\\
r^{-k-1}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}v^{2}_{r,k}\\
v^{2}_{\varphi,k}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ir^{k-1}\\
-r^{k-1}\end{pmatrix}.

The solution of this system with boundary relations (
1.3
), (
1.4

[

\displaystyle v_{r,k}=\operatorname{sign}(k)\frac{ir^{-|k|-1}}{2}\int_{r_{0}}^{r}s^{|k|+1}w_{k}(s)\operatorname{ds}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +\operatorname{sign}(k)\frac{ir^{|k|-1}}{2}\int_{r}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}w_{k}(s)\operatorname{ds}+v_{\infty,r,k}

\displaystyle v_{\varphi,k}=\frac{r^{-|k|-1}}{2}\int_{r_{0}}^{r}s^{|k|+1}w_{k}(s)\operatorname{ds}

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\frac{r^{|k|-1}}{2}\int_{r}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}w_{k}(s)\operatorname{ds}+v_{\infty,\phi,k}.

(2.2)

No-slip condition (
1.3
) and (
1.9
) lead to moment relations on vorticity (
k\in\mathbb{Z}
):

\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}w_{k}(s)\operatorname{ds}=2ikv_{\infty,r,k}=2v_{\infty,\phi,k}.

(2.3)

From (
1.7
), (
1.8
) these moments don’t equal to zero only if
|k|=1
.

We rewrite moment relationship in terms of vorticity
w(\mathbf{x})
for
k\geq 0

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}w_{k}(s)\operatorname{ds}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}s^{-k+1}w(s,\varphi)e^{-ik\varphi}\operatorname{ds}d\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{w(\mathbf{x})}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=2ikv_{\infty,r,k},\penalty\ k\geq 0.

For
k<0
we have absolutely the same moments equity:

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}w_{k}(s)\operatorname{ds}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}s^{k+1}w(s,\varphi)e^{-ik\varphi}\operatorname{ds}d\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\overline{z^{k}}w(\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=2ikv_{\infty,r,k},\penalty\ k<0.

The last formula for
k<0

k\geq 0

Then for no-slip condition we have affine subspace

which must be invariant under Stokes, Oseen and Navier-Stokes flows:

\displaystyle M=\left\{w(\mathbf{x})\in L_{1}(B_{r_{0}})\penalty\ \Big|\penalty\ \int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{w(\mathbf{x})}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=4\pi ikv_{\infty,r,k},\penalty\ k\geq 0\right\}.

(2.4)

Note, that from (
1.7
), (
1.8
) the integral relations in definition of

are non-zero ones only if

For Fourier coefficients the invariance of

means that for vorticity flow which evolutes by cooefficients

holds

\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}w_{k}(t,s)\operatorname{ds}=const,\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{Z}.

In

3
]
was proved

2.2.
Stokes system

Consider Stokes flow for vorticity

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w(t,\mathbf{x})}{\partial t}-\Delta w(t,\mathbf{x})=0.

(2.5)

Supply it with Robin-type boundary condition:

r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+|k|w_{k}(t,r_{0})=0,\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{Z}

(2.6)

and

w(t,\mathbf{x})\to 0,\penalty\ |\mathbf{x}|\to\infty.

(2.7)

Then
M
is invariant under the flow
w(t,\cdot)
. Indeed, fix
k>0
and divide equation (
2.5

z^{k}
and integrate over exterior of the disc

2.4

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{w(t,\mathbf{x})}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0

\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\Delta w}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0.

In other hand

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\Delta w}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\Delta w}{s^{k}e^{ik\phi}}sdsd\varphi=2\pi\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-k+1}\Delta_{k}w_{k}(s)ds

\displaystyle=-2\pi r_{0}^{-k}\left(r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+kw_{k}(t,r_{0})\right)=0.

(2.8)

In similar way using complex conjugation of moment relations (
2.4
) we obtain boundary condition for
k<0

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}{\Delta w}\overline{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\Delta w}{s^{-k}e^{ik\phi}}sdsd\varphi=2\pi\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{k+1}\Delta_{k}w_{k}(s)ds

In

2.3.
Oseen system

Here we transfer integral relations (

Then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=-\int_{B_{r_{0}}}(\mathbf{v},\nabla z^{-k})w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{v_{1}+iv_{2}}{z^{k+1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}}{z^{k+1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

From (
2.4
)

k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}}{z^{k+1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0

and

\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}}{z^{k+1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

Using (
2.2
) we get

\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\Delta w}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=-2\pi r_{0}^{-k}\left(r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+kw_{k}(t,r_{0})\right).

Then for
k\geq 0

\displaystyle\left(r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+kw_{k}(t,r_{0})\right)=\frac{kr_{0}^{k}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}}{z^{k+1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}.

Since
w_{-k}(t,r)=\overline{w_{k}(t,r)}
then (
2.16
) holds for
k<0
. Theorem is proved.

∎

Remark
.

If
\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{\infty}\|
is small in some integral norm, then right side in (
2.16
) transferes to boundary condition (
2.6
). So, (
2.6
) becomes rather accurate approximation for Navier-Stokes system. From this fact naturally occurs boundary control problem with unknown function
u_{k}(t)
:

r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+|k|w_{k}(t,r_{0})=u_{k}(t),\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{Z},

(2.17)

where feedback control
u_{k}(t)
is treated as small correction of zero boundary condition at each time step.

3.
Boundary condition in exterior of simply connected domains

In this section we derive Biot-Savar law for more general domains. We will establish crucial fact that Robin-type boundary (
2.6
) stays actual for these domains.

3.1.
Biot-Savar Law

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B
, where
B
is bounded simple-connected domain with piecewise smooth boundary and
\Phi
be a Riemann mapping from
\Omega
into exterior of the disc
B_{r_{0}}
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(z)=z+O\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(z)=1+O\left(\frac{1}{z^{2}}\right).

Then
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}(\Phi^{-1}(z))=\mathbf{v}(\operatorname{Re}\Phi^{-1}(x_{1}+ix_{2}),\operatorname{Im}\Phi^{-1}(x_{1}+ix_{2}))
defines vector field in
B_{r_{0}}
. With help of Cauchy–Riemann relationship the equations (
1.1
), (
1.2
) turn to relation

\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}+i\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}=\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(z)}{|\Phi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}w.

(3.1)

Then system (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) after Riemann mapping in
B_{r_{0}}
takes the form

\displaystyle\rm{div}\penalty\ \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})=\operatorname{Im}\overline{\Phi^{\prime-}1(z)}w(\mathbf{x})

(3.2)

\displaystyle\rm{curl}\penalty\ \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})=\operatorname{Re}\overline{\Phi^{\prime-}1(z)}w(\mathbf{x})

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})=0,\penalty\ |\mathbf{x}|=r_{0}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\to\mathbf{v}_{\infty},\penalty\ |\mathbf{x}|\to\infty.

(3.5)

Denote

\displaystyle r_{k}(r)=[\operatorname{Im}\overline{\Phi^{\prime-1}(z)}w(\mathbf{x})]_{k},

\displaystyle q_{k}(r)=[\operatorname{Re}\overline{\Phi^{\prime-1}(z)}w(\mathbf{x})]_{k}.

Rewrite (
3.2
),(
3.3
) in polar coordinates in terms of Fourier coefficients
v_{r,k}
,
v_{\varphi,k}
:

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{r}}{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}\left(rv_{r,k}\right)+{\frac{ik}{r}}v_{\varphi,k}=r_{k}(r),

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{r}}{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}\left(rv_{\varphi,k}\right)-{\frac{ik}{r}}v_{r,k}=q_{k}(r).

Under assumption of zero-circularity (
1.6
) from Stokes’ Theorem we have

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\oint_{|\mathbf{x}|=R}\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\cdot d\mathbf{l}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Omega}w(\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{w(\mathbf{x})}{|\Phi^{\prime}|^{2}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\left[\frac{w(\mathbf{x})}{|\Phi^{\prime}|^{2}}\right]_{k=0}s\operatorname{ds}=0.

It guaranties solution’s uniqueness of the above system. Existence will be provided by moment relations below. The solution of the above system for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
is derived by the same way as formulas (
2.1
), (
2.1
) in exterior of the disc (see
[
3
]
for more details):

\displaystyle v_{r,k}=\operatorname{sign}(k)\frac{ir^{-|k|-1}}{2}\int_{r_{0}}^{r}s^{|k|+1}(q_{k}-i\operatorname{sign}(k)r_{k})\operatorname{ds}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +\operatorname{sign}(k)\frac{ir^{|k|-1}}{2}\int_{r}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}(q_{k}+i\operatorname{sign}(k)r_{k})\operatorname{ds}+v_{\infty,r,k}

\displaystyle v_{\varphi,k}=\frac{r^{-|k|-1}}{2}\int_{r_{0}}^{r}s^{|k|+1}(q_{k}-i\operatorname{sign}(k)r_{k})\operatorname{ds}

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\frac{r^{|k|-1}}{2}\int_{r}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}(q_{k}+i\operatorname{sign}(k)r_{k})\operatorname{ds}+v_{\infty,\phi,k}.

(3.7)

Formulas (
3.6
), (
3.1
) combined with (
1.3
) lead to relations on vorticity (
k\in\mathbb{Z}
):

\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}\left(q_{k}(s)+i\operatorname{sign}(k)r_{k}(s)\right)\operatorname{ds}=2ikv_{\infty,r,k}=2v_{\infty,\phi,k}.

(3.8)

This equities can be written in terms of
w(\mathbf{x})
. For
k\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}\left(q_{k}(s)+ir_{k}(s)\right)\operatorname{ds}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}s^{-|k|+1}\overline{\Phi^{\prime-1}(z)}w(s,\varphi)e^{-ik\varphi}\operatorname{ds}d\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\overline{\Phi^{\prime-1}(z)}\frac{w(\mathbf{x})}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=2ikv_{\infty,r,k},

where
z=x_{1}+ix_{2}
.

Following by the same way as in Theorem
3.2
we define affine subspace
M
via vorticity moments which must be invariant under fluid dynamics flows:

\displaystyle M=\left\{w(\mathbf{x})\in L_{1}(B_{r_{0}})\penalty\ \Big|\penalty\ \int_{B_{r_{0}}}\overline{\Phi^{\prime-1}(z)}\frac{w(\mathbf{x})}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=4\pi ikv_{\infty,r,k},\penalty\ k\geq 0\right\}.

(3.9)

In view of (
1.7
), (
1.8
) all moments in definition of
M
must be equal to zero except
k=1
.

Theorem 3.1

(Biot-Savart Law in exterior domain)
.

If
w(\Phi^{-1}(z))\in M
then there exists the unique solution of (
1.1
) - (
1.4
), (
1.6
) given by (
3.6
), (
3.1
) with Fourier coefficients
v_{r,k}
,
v_{\varphi,k}\in L_{\infty}(r_{0},\infty)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Given initial datum
\mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})
satisfying no-slip condition (
1.3
), infinity condition (
1.4
), zero-circularity (
1.6
), such that
w_{0}=\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}_{0}(\Phi^{-1}(z))

\in L_{1}(\Omega)
, and
w(t,\cdot)=\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}(\Phi^{-1}(z))
be the vorticity flow. Then
w_{0}\in M
and in order to conserve no-slip condition the affine subspace
M
must be invariant under the flow, e.g. for any time
t>0

w(t,\cdot)\in M
.

3.2.
Stokes system

Here we prove that Robin-type boundary (
2.6
) is really a no-slip boundary condition in exterior domains. It keeps moment relations (
3.8
) under Stokes flow, and thus
M
is invariant affine subspace and no-slip law at the boundary is satisfied.

We impose additional requirement on
\Omega
that
\Phi^{-1}
can be represented by absolutely converged series

\Phi^{-1}(z)=z+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{z^{n}}.

(3.10)

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B
, where
B
is bounded simple-connected domain with piecewise smooth boundary and
\Phi
be a Riemann map from
\Omega
into exterior of the disc satisfying (
3.10
), given initial datum
\mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})
,
\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}_{0}\in L_{1}(B_{r_{0}})
satisfying no-slip condition (
1.3
), infinity condition (
1.4
), zero-circularity (
1.6
) and
\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})
be the solution of (
0.1
)-(
0.5
) in
\Omega
. Then
w(t,\mathbf{x})
=
\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}(t,\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))
satisfies (
2.6
).

Proof.

After Riemann mapping Stokes equations reduce to scalar equation on vorticity

|(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\partial_{t}w(t,x)-\Delta w=0.

Fix
k>0
and divide this equation by
z^{k}
and integrate over exterior of the disc
B_{r_{0}}
. From moment relations (
3.9
)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)}}{z^{k}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0

Using
|(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)|^{2}=(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)}
and

(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}}{z^{n}}

with some coefficients
c_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}|(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)|^{2}w(t,x)\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)}}{z^{n+k}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)}}{z^{n+k}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0,

and thus

\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\Delta w}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0.

In other hand

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\Delta w}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\Delta w}{s^{k}e^{ik\phi}}sdsd\varphi=2\pi\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-k+1}\Delta_{k}w_{k}(s)ds

\displaystyle=-2\pi r_{0}^{-k}\left(r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+kw_{k}(t,r_{0})\right)=0.

Using
w_{-k}(t,r)=\overline{w_{k}(t,r)}
we will have condition (
2.6
) for
k<0
.

∎

3.3.
Navier-Stokes system

Navier-Stokes equation (
0.6
) after Riemann mapping reduces to

|(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\partial_{t}w(t,x)-\Delta w+B(v,w)=0,

where

B(v,w)=\operatorname{Re}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)-\operatorname{Im}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(\mathbf{v}^{\perp},\nabla w).

Supply this equation with

\displaystyle\left(r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+|k|w_{k}(t,r_{0})\right)=

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \left\{{\begin{matrix}\frac{|k|r_{0}^{|k|}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}){z^{-|k|-1}}\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}},\penalty\ k\geq 0,\\
\frac{|k|r_{0}^{|k|}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\overline{(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}})z^{-|k|-1}}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}},\penalty\ k<0.\end{matrix}}\right.

(3.11)

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Omega
,
\Phi
as in Theorem
3.3
, given initial datum
\mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})
,
\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}_{0}\in L_{1}(\Omega)
satisfying no-slip condition (
1.3
), infinity condition (
1.4
), zero-circularity (
1.6
) and
\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})
be the solution of (
0.1
)-(
0.5
) in
\Omega
. Then
w(t,\cdot)=\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}(t,\Phi^{-1}(z))
satisfies (
3.11
).

Proof.

Apply the same steps as in previous subsections: for fixed
k>0
divide this equation by
z^{k}
and integrate over
B_{r_{0}}
. In previous subsection we obtained

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}|(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)|^{2}w(t,x)\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0,

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\Delta w}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=-2\pi r_{0}^{-k}\left(r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+kw_{k}(t,r_{0})\right).

From (
3.2
), (
3.3
)

\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}w\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=-\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}w\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}.

Then using Cauchy-Riemann equations

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{B(v,w)}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)-\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}(\mathbf{v}^{\perp},\nabla w)\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\left(-\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}w\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}-\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}w\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}\right)\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{r_{0}}}w\left(\left(v,\nabla\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}\right)-\left(v^{\perp},\nabla\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}\right)\right)\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}}{z^{k+1}}(v_{1}+iv_{2})w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}}{z^{k+1}}\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

Since from (
3.9
)

k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}}{z^{k+1}}\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0,

then

\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{B(v,w)}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}}{z^{k+1}}(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty})w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}},

from which follows (
3.11
) for
k\geq 0
.

For
k<0
(
3.11
) follows from the identity
w_{-k}(t,r)=\overline{w_{k}(t,r)}
. Theorem is proved.

∎

This theorem says, that in case of Oseen approximation we can use Robin-type boundary (
2.6
) in general domain
\Omega
. And for Navier-Stokes system if
\|\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}\|
is small, then (
2.6
) works well. Nevertheless this boundary condition requires some corrections such as boundary control (
2.17
) in order to stay on invariance affine subspace
M
.

Parametrization of geometric Beilinson–Bloch heights via adelic line bundles
Yinchong Song

Abstract
Let S S be a smooth quasi-projective variety over complex field ℂ \mathbb{C} . For a smooth projective morphism π : X → S \pi:X\to S , we will introduce a new height pairing C ​ H hom p ​ ( X / S ) × C ​ H hom q ​ ( X / S ) → Pic ~ ​ ( S ) \displaystyle CH^{p}_{\hom}(X/S)\times CH^{q}_{\hom}(X/S)\to\widetilde{\mathrm{Pic}}(S) with Pic ~ ​ ( S ) \widetilde{\mathrm{Pic}}(S) the group of geometric adelic line bundles in the sense of Yuan–Zhang. It essentially parametrizes the asymptotic height pairing introduced by Brosnan and Pearlstein. We will show that this asymptotic height pairing coincides with Beilinson–Bloch pairing under certain conditions.



1
Introduction

In
[
Bei
]
, Beilinson introduced a (conditional) height pairing for homologically trivial cycles on a smooth projective variety
X
over a number field or a function field of one variable. In the function field case, it is a well-defined
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-valued pairing, and is conjectured to be
\mathbb{Q}
-valued. In the number field case, we only know the existence of archimedean local height, and the non-archimedean part is still a conjecture. Bloch also introduced a height pairing in
[
Blo
]
. In
[
RS
]
, they generalize the
\ell
-adic definition for high-dimensional base, and in
[
Kah
]
, they give a refined height pairing using cycles. These two pairing only agree under certain conditions.

In our paper, we will introduce a new height pairing over the complex function field with target adelic line bundles. Let
X,S
be quasi-projective varieties over
\mathbb{C}
, and
\pi:X\to S
be projective smooth of relative dimension
n
. Let
CH^{p}_{\hom}(X/S)
be the subgroup of Chow group of
X
generated by subvarieties on
X
which are flat over
S
and have homologically trivial fibers. For
p+q=n+1
, we will define a pairing

\displaystyle CH^{p}_{\hom}(X/S)\times CH^{q}_{\hom}(X/S)\to\widetilde{\mathrm{Pic}}(S)

which essentially parametrizes the asymptotic height pairing in
[
BP
]
. It is functorial under pull-back by
S^{\prime}\to S
where
S^{\prime}
smooth quasi-projective. If
K
has transcendental degree
1
, we will show that this height pairing agrees with Beilinson–Bloch height under certain conditions.

The proof will be an analytic proof, both complex and Berkovich analytic. To begin with, we first study some point set topology property of the Berkovich analytic space, especially under the hybrid norm. Then we study Yuan–Zhang’s adelic line bundles from a analytic point of view, and show that their theory behaves perfectly well under Berkovich analytification once we use hybrid norm to replace the usual norm in the local case. As a result, we get an analytic criterion of adelic line bundles.

For any two cycles
Z,W\in CH^{p}_{\hom}(X/S)
, Hain constructed a hermitian line bundle
\bar{L}_{Z,W}
on
S^{\mathrm{an}}
in
[
Hai90
]
, which was later studied in
[
BP
]
. Using our criterion, we see that
\bar{L}_{Z,W}
will determine a geometric adelic line bundle
\tilde{L}_{Z,W}
which parametrizes the asymptotic height pairing, thus we get our results. Finally, we show that asymptotic height pairing agrees with the geometric Beilinson–Bloch pairing under certain conditions.

1.1
Berkovich spaces

Let
k
be any noetherian ring equipped with a Banach semi-norm
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}}
. Let
U
be a quasi-projective integral scheme, flat over
k
. Then we obtain a Berkovich analytic space
U^{\mathrm{an}}
as in
[
Ber09
]
containing all possible valuations of residue fields of points in

whose restriction to

is bounded by
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}}
. It is a Hausdorff, locally compact topological space.

In the global case, let
(k,|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}})
be either
\mathbb{Z}
with the usual norm or a field with the trivial norm. For a quasi-projective variety
U
over
k
, we introduce two topological spaces related to
U^{\mathrm{an}}
in §
2
. We define the
interior part

U^{\beth}\subset U^{\mathrm{an}}
to be the set of points where the reduction map

\mathrm{red}:U^{\mathrm{an}}\dashrightarrow U

is well-defined, and define the
boundary part

U^{b}:=U^{\mathrm{an}}\backslash U^{\beth}

to be the complement. Let
\widetilde{U}^{b}:=U^{b}/\!\sim_{\mathrm{eqv}}
be the quotient space of
U^{b}
modulo the norm-equivalence relation. The space
U^{\beth}
is closely related to the analytification of formal algebraic
k
-schemes in
[
Thu
]
.

In the local case, let
k
is a complete field with a non-trivial valuation
|\cdot|
, and let
|\cdot|_{0}
be the trivial valuation on
k
. Define the hybrid norm
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{hyb}}:=\max\{|\cdot|,|\cdot|_{0}\}
. We will show that
(\Spec K)_{\mathrm{hyb}}^{\mathrm{an}}
is homeomorphic to the closed interval
[0,1]
. For a quasi-projective variety
U
over
k
, we have the hybrid analytification
U^{\mathrm{an}}_{\mathrm{hyb}}
with a structure map
\pi:U^{\mathrm{an}}_{\mathrm{hyb}}\to(\Spec K)^{\mathrm{an}}_{\mathrm{hyb}}\cong[0,1]
.

In practice, the space
U^{\mathrm{an}}_{\mathrm{hyb}}
could be viewed as a trivial fiber bundle
U^{\mathrm{an}}_{t}
parametrized by

, and when
t\to 0
, the spaces

“converge” to
U^{\mathrm{an}}_{0}
, the Berkovich space over trivially valued field. We can define the spaces
U^{\beth}
and
\widetilde{U}^{b}
similarly as the global case.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
2.5
)
In both local and global cases above, the topological spaces
U^{\beth}
and
\widetilde{U}^{b}
are compact Hausdorff.

In the local case, points in
U^{\mathrm{an}}_{t}
for

do not have reduction in

. Modulo equivalence, we see that
\widetilde{U}^{b}
would contain an open subspaces homeomorphic to

, which is just the usual Berkovich analytic space over
k
. We call
\widetilde{U}^{b}
the hybrid compactification of

. We will compare it with some other compactifications like
[
AN
,
BJ
]
in §

.

1.2
Adelic line bundles over quasi-projective varieties

Recently, Yuan and Zhang introduced adelic divisors and line bundles on quasi-projective varieties in
[
YZ
]
. Let
k=\mathbb{Z}
or a field, and let
X
be a projective flat integral scheme over
k
. If
k=\mathbb{Z}
, let
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(X)
be the group of Cartier divisors on
X
with a conjugation-invariant Green function
g_{D}
on
X_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{an}}
which induce a hermitian metric
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathscr{O}(D)_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{an}}
on
X_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{an}}
. If
k
is a field, this is called the geometric case, and by abuse of notation,
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(X)
is just
\mathrm{Div}(X)
, the group of Cartier divisors.

Now let
U
be quasi-projective flat integral scheme over
k
. Let
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)
be the group of adelic divisors on
U
defined in
[
YZ
, §2]
. It is a reasonable limit of a sequence of divisors on projective models. Roughly speaking, to give an adelic divisor
\bar{D}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)
, we need a usual divisor
D\in\mathrm{Div}(U)
, and a sequence
\bar{D}_{i}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(X_{i})_{\mathbb{Q}}
on different projective models
X_{i}
of
U
, such that all restrictions
D_{i}|_{U}
are equal to
D
, and the sequence
\{\bar{D}_{i}\}_{i}
satisfies a Cauchy condition on the boundary. See §
3
for more precise definitions.

Let
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)_{b}
be the subgroup of
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)
consisting of adelic divisors whose underlying divisor on
U
is trivial.

In
[
YZ
, §3]
, they also, define an analytification map of adelic divisors using Berkovich analytic space.

Now let
k
be either
\mathbb{Z}
or a field. If
k=\mathbb{Z}
, let
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}}
be the usual absolute norm. If
k
is a field, let
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}}
be the trivial norm on
k
. For
U
quasi-projective variety over
k
, and
U^{\mathrm{an}}
be the analytification of
U
..

A
metrized divisor

\bar{D}=(D,g_{D})
on
U^{\mathrm{an}}
consists of a Cartier divisor
D\in\mathrm{Div}(U)
and a continuous Green function

g_{D}:U^{\mathrm{an}}\backslash|D|^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R},

such that if
D
is locally defined by a function
f\in K(U)^{*}
, then
g_{D}(x)+\log|f(x)|
locally extends continuously to
|D|^{\mathrm{an}}
. The metric is called
norm-equivariant
if for any
x,y\in U^{\mathrm{an}}\backslash|D|^{\mathrm{an}}
satisfying
|\cdot|_{x}=|\cdot|_{y}^{t}
for some
t\in(0,\infty)
, then
g_{D}(x)=tg_{D}(y)
.

Let
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{eqv}}
be the group of norm-equivariant metrized divisors on
U^{\mathrm{an}}
. Let
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U^{\mathrm{an}}){\mathrm{eqv},b}
be the subgroup of
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{eqv}}
consisting of metrized divisors
(D,g_{D})
whose underlying divisor on
U
is trivial.

Yuan and Zhang defined a Berkovich analytification map of adelic line bundles in
[
YZ
, §3]
, and obtain a canonical injection
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)\to\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{eqv}}
mapping
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)_{b}
into
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{eqv},b}
. These groups are equipped with the boundary topology.

In our paper, we will show that the above analytification maps are in fact bijective.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Theorem
3.7
)
Let
k
be either
\mathbb{Z}
or a field. Let
U
be a quasi-projective flat integral scheme over
k
. Then the above canonical maps

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)\to\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{eqv}},

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)_{b}\to\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{eqv},b}

are homeomorphisms. If the boundary norm on both sides are given by the same boundary divisor, they are isometries.

Further, under the canonical isomorphism
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{eqv},b}\cong C(U^{\mathrm{an}}_{\mathrm{eqv}})
which maps the metrized divisor
(0,g)
to the function
g
, the boundary topology is equivalent to the locally uniform convergence topology.

Note that for non-normal variety
U
, a metrized divisor
(D,g_{D})
is not uniquely determined by its Green function. It may cause some problems if one tried to study metrized divisors purely analytically.

It is not true for essentially quasi-projective variety, as explained in Remark
3.15
.

Since adelic line bundles are limits of line bundles on projective models, it is equivalent to say that any continuous norm-equivariant metric on quasi-projective varieties can be approximated by model ones, which is a generalization of
[
Gub
, Theorem 7.12]
, and the proofs are similar.

We can also get similar results for local fields
K
. This time it is different from Yuan–Zhang’s one.

Let
K
be either a complete non-archimedean field with a non-trivial valuation, or
K=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
with the standard absolute norm. The norm on
K
is denoted by
|\cdot|
. Let
|\cdot|_{0}
be the trivial norm on
K
. Define the hybrid norm as

|\cdot|_{\mathrm{hyb}}=\max\{|\cdot|,|\cdot|_{0}\}.

Let
U
be a quasi-projective variety over
K
. If
K
is non-archimedean, let
\mathscr{O}_{K}
be the valuation ring of
K
; if
K
is archimedean, let
\mathscr{O}_{K}=K
. Then Yuan–Zhang defined the group of local adelic divisors
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/\mathscr{O}_{K})
. Let
U^{\mathrm{an}}=(U/(K,|\cdot|_{\mathrm{hyb}}))^{\mathrm{an}}
be the Berkovich space of
U
over
(K,|\cdot|_{\mathrm{hyb}})
. The local version of Theorem
1.2
is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Theorem
3.9
)
The analytification map

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/\mathscr{O}_{K})\to\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U_{\mathrm{hyb}}^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{eqv}}

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/\mathscr{O}_{K})_{b}\to\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U_{\mathrm{hyb}}^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{eqv},b}

are homeomorphisms. If the boundary norm on both sides are given by the same boundary divisor, they are isometries.

Further, under the canonical isomorphism
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U_{\mathrm{hyb}}^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{eqv},b}\cong C(U_{\mathrm{hyb}}^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{eqv}}
mapping the metrized divisor
(0,g)
to the function
g
, the boundary topology is equivalent to the locally uniform convergence topology on
U^{\mathrm{an}}_{\mathrm{hyb}}
.

As a result, we get a analytic criterion of adelic line bundles.

After that, we discuss the results on the toric varieties, and obtain a comparison theorem of toric adelic divisor in §
4
, special adelic divisors in
[
Son
, §5]
, and toroidal
b
-divisors in
[
BB
]
. In §

for integrable adelic line bundles which generalizes the Monge-Ampère measure in the complex case, and the Chambert-Loir measure in the non-archimedean case. The last application is a height pairing.

1.3
Beilinson–Bloch height

Let
K
be any field, and
X
be smooth projective over
K
. Let
CH^{p}(X)
be the Chow group of cycles of codimension
p
in
X
. Take a prime
\ell\neq\mathrm{char}\ K
, and let
CH^{i}_{\hom,\ell}(X)
be the kernel of the
\ell
-adic class map

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl_{\ell}}:CH^{p}(X)\to H^{2p}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X_{\bar{K}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(p)).

If
\mathrm{char}\ K=0
, then we can use Betti cohomology instead, and it is independent of the prime
\ell
. So we may drop
\ell
from the notation.

Now let
K
be the function field of a smooth projective curve
B
over an algebraically closed field
k
. In
[
Bei
]
, Beilinson introduced an
\ell
-adic height pairing for Chow group of homologically trivial cycles of complementary codimension

\displaystyle CH^{p}_{\hom}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\times CH^{q}_{\hom}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}

for
p+q=n+1
. He conjectures that this map is actually
\mathbb{Q}
-valued, and gives a conditional construction in the number field case.

In our paper, we will give a new pairing. Let
S
be quasi-projective variety over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
, and
\pi:X\to S
be a smooth projective morphism of relative dimension
n
. Let
CH_{\hom}^{p}(X/S)
to be the subgroup of Chow group
CH^{p}(X)
generated by codimension
p
cycles on
X
, flat over
S
, whose restriction to each fiber
X_{s}
is homologically trivial for each
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
.

Theorem 1.4
.

(Theorem
6.5
)
There is a height pairing

\displaystyle CH^{p}_{\hom}(X/S)\times CH^{q}_{\hom}(X/S)\to\widetilde{\mathrm{Pic}}(S/\mathbb{C})

parametrizing the asymptotic height pairing in
[
BP
]
. It is the unique pairing satisfying the following property.

1.

It is functorial for pull-back by morphisms
S^{\prime}\to S
with
S^{\prime}
a smooth quasi-projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
.

2.

If
\dim S=1
, it is compatible with Hain’s biextension hermitian line bundle in
[
Hai90
, 3.4]
and Lear’s extension in his unpublished PhD thesis.

See Theorem
6.5
for a precise statement.

Passing to limit on open subsets
U\subset S
, we get the following version.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
K
be a finitely generated field over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
X_{\eta}
be a smooth projective variety over
K
of dimension
n
. Then there is a pairing

\displaystyle CH^{p}_{\hom}(X_{\eta})\times CH^{q}_{\hom}(X_{\eta})\to\widetilde{\mathrm{Pic}}(\Spec K/\mathbb{C})

which is the direct limit of the pairing in
1.4
.

To get a comparison with Beilinson–Bloch height, assume
\dim S=1
. Let
\bar{S}
be the smooth projective model of
S
, and
\bar{\pi}:\bar{X}\to\bar{S}
be a projective flat morphism with
\bar{X}
smooth. Define
CH^{p}(\bar{X})^{0}
to be the kernel of the composition

\displaystyle CH^{p}(\bar{X})\to H^{2p}(\bar{X},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{0}(\bar{S},R^{2p}\bar{\pi}_{*}\mathbb{Q}).

It also occurs in Beilinson’s construction
[
Bei
, 1.2]
and is denoted by
CH^{p}_{\text{B}}(X)
in
[
Kah
, 2C]
.

Theorem 1.6
.

(Theorem
6.7
)
Suppose that
Z,W
are flat cycles of
X/S
, with homologically trivial fibers and disjoint support, of codimension
p,q
with
p+q=n+1
, such that one of them, say,
Z
, admits an extension
\tilde{Z}\in CH^{*}(\bar{X})^{0}
. In this case their geometric Beilinson–Bloch pairing exists. Locally near a point
s_{0}\in\bar{S}\backslash S
, choose a local coordinate, the archimedean Beilinson–Bloch height has the following asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle\langle Z_{s},W_{s}\rangle_{s,\mathrm{ar}}=-\langle Z,W\rangle_{0,\mathrm{na}}\log|s|+O(1)

for
s
close to
0
.

Here
\langle Z_{s},W_{s}\rangle_{s,\mathrm{ar}}
means the archimedean Beilinson–Bloch height, and
\langle Z,W\rangle_{0,\mathrm{na}}
means the geometric Beilinson Bloch height over the function field
K=K(S)
and at the place
0
.

Combine the two theorems, we get the following.

Corollary 1.7
.

For each
Z,W\in CH^{*}_{\hom}(X/S)
of complementary codimension, the adelic line bundle
\tilde{L}_{Z,W}
in Theorem
1.4
has the following property.

For each curve
C\subset S
, if
Z|_{\pi^{-1}(C)}
satisfies the same condition as in Theorem
1.6
, then

\displaystyle\deg_{C}\tilde{L}_{Z,W}|_{C}=\langle Z|_{\pi^{-1}(C)},W|_{\pi^{-1}(C)}\rangle_{K(C)}.

Here
\langle Z|_{\pi^{-1}(C)},W|_{\pi^{-1}(C)}\rangle_{K(C)}
is the geometric Beilinson–Bloch height over the function field of one variable

.

We discuss the relationship between our pairings and the one in
[
Kah
]
. The main difference is that ours are only defined for flat cycles, which work very well under pull-back maps. In
[
Kah
]
, they only get a functorial under finite surjective pull-backs. One reason is that there pairing is essentially a codimension
1
theory, and it cannot give describe about higher codimension.

To prove this result, we use Hain’s result on Hodge theory to get a hermitian line bundle
\bar{L}
on
S^{\mathrm{an}}
. Then we use our criterion of adelic line bundles to show that
\bar{L}
will determine a geometric adelic line bundle
\tilde{L}
.

It is natural to ask whether
\bar{L}
itself is adelic or not. For Gross-Schoen cycles, it is studied in
[
Yua
, 3.3.4]
and
[
GZ
]
. They all conjectured that the Beilinson–Bloch height in the number field case is parametrized by an adelic line bundle. Our pairing gives its function field analogue, and we believe that it could be generated to number field in the future.

2
Berkovich spaces

2.1
Preliminaries on Berkovich spaces

Let
k
be a commutative ring with unity
1
which is complete with respect to a semi-norm
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}}
. Such a pair
(k,|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}})
is called a
Banach ring
.

Affine case

Let
(k,|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}})
be a Banach ring, and
A
be a
k
-algebra. The
Berkovich space

\mathcal{M}(A)
is the topological space of multiplicative semi-norms
|\cdot|:A\to\mathbb{R}
whose restriction to
k
is bounded by
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}}
. For a point
x\in\mathcal{M}(A)
, the semi-norm is denoted by
|\cdot|_{x}
, and for
f\in A
, the semi-norm
|f|_{x}
is also denoted by
|f(x)|
, and thus gives a real-valued function
|f|:\mathcal{M}(A)\to\mathbb{R}
. The topology on
\mathcal{M}(A)
is defined as the weakest topology such that for each
f\in A
, the function
|f|:\mathcal{M}(A)\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous.

Two points
|\cdot|_{x},|\cdot|_{y}\in\ M(A)
are called
norm-equivalent
if there is a positive real number
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
|\cdot|_{x}=|\cdot|_{y}^{t}
.

We discuss two maps between
\Spec A
and
\mathcal{M}(A)
.

For each
x\in\mathcal{M}(A)
, the kernel of the semi-norm
|\cdot|_{x}:A\to\mathbb{R}
is a prime ideal. This induces a continuous
contraction map

\rho:\mathcal{M}(A)\to\Spec A

sending a point
x
to
\rho(x)=\ker|\cdot|_{x}
. Then the norm
|\cdot|_{x}
can be viewed as a norm on the residue field at
\rho(x)
. Let
H_{x}
be the completion of the field
(\kappa(\rho(x)),|\cdot|_{x})
, called the
residue field
at
x\in\mathcal{M}(A)
. If
H_{x}
is non-archimedean, let
R_{x}
be the valuation ring of
H_{x}
.

The other one is the
inclusion map

i:\Spec A\to\mathcal{M}(A)
sending a point
x\in\Spec A
to the point
(\kappa(x),|\cdot|_{0})
, where
|\cdot|_{0}
is the trivial norm on
\kappa(x)
, i.e.,
|a|_{0}=1
for all non-zero
a\in\kappa(x)
. This map is not continuous in general.

If
f:A\to B
is a
k
-algebra homomorphism,
x\in\mathcal{M}(B)
corresponding to the multiplicative semi-norm
|\cdot|_{x}
, then
|\cdot|_{x}\circ f:A\to\mathbb{R}
is a multiplicative semi-norm on
A
, thus induces a continuous map
f^{*}:\mathcal{M}(B)\to\mathcal{M}(A)
.

In our paper, we only care about the topological structure of Berkovich spaces, so we do not discuss the notion of analytic functions.

Scheme case

In general, if
(k,|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}})
is a Banach ring, and
X
is a scheme over
k
, then
X
can be covered by open affine subschemes
\{\Spec A_{j}\}
, and we may glue
\mathcal{M}(A_{j})
together to get the analytification
X^{\mathrm{an}}
, which is more rigorously written as
(X/(k,|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}}))^{\mathrm{an}}
if we need to emphasize the norm
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}}
. If
f:X\to Y
is a morphism of schemes, then
f
induces a continuous morphism
f^{\mathrm{an}}:X^{\mathrm{an}}\to Y^{\mathrm{an}}
, thus the analytification map is a functor from the category of schemes over
k
to the category of topological spaces.

Let
(k,|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}})
be a noetherian Banach ring and
X
be a scheme of finite type. By
[
Ber09
, Lemma 1.1, Lemma 1.2]
, we have:

Proposition 2.1
.

(1) The space
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is locally compact.

(1) If
X
is separated over
k
, then
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is Hausdorff.

(2) If
X
is proper over
k
, then
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is compact.

We see that the contraction maps
\rho_{j}:\mathcal{M}(A_{j})\to\Spec A_{j}
glue to a continuous map

\rho:X^{\mathrm{an}}\to X
, and the inclusion maps
i_{j}:\Spec A_{j}\to\mathcal{M}(A_{j})
glue to
i:X\to X^{\mathrm{an}}
. The inclusion map
i:X\to X^{\mathrm{an}}
is not continuous in general.

Reduction map

For a separated scheme
X
over
k
, we have a reduction map, which is only partially defined on a subset of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
. Assume that
k
is a Banach integral domain, and
X
is a noetherian separated scheme over
k
. Let
X^{\mathrm{an}}=(X/k)^{\mathrm{an}}
. There is a
reduction map

\displaystyle r:X^{\mathrm{an}}\dashrightarrow X

which is only defined on a subset of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
as follows.

For each non-archimedean point
x\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
such that the restriction of
|\cdot|_{x}
to
k
is bounded by the trivial semi-norm on
k
, the canonical map
\Spec H_{x}\to X\to\Spec k
extends to
\Spec R_{x}\to\Spec k
. Since

Hybrid Berkovich spaces

Now let

Proof.

|\pi|_{\mathrm{hyb}}\geqslant|\pi|_{\lambda}>1

For any non-zero

and hence
|x|_{\lambda}\leqslant 1
. Therefore, for any rational number

with
|\pi|_{t}^{a}\leqslant|x|_{t}\leqslant|\pi|_{t}^{b}

|\pi|_{\lambda}^{a}\leqslant|x|_{\lambda}\leqslant|\pi|_{\lambda}^{b}
. But
|\pi|_{\lambda}=|\pi|_{t}
, hence

for all

.
∎

To simplify the notations, we write

Proposition 2.7

We have an homeomorphism
\pi^{-1}((0,1])\cong(U/K_{1})^{\mathrm{an}}\times(0,1]

The proof of archimedean case is given in

Lemma 2.8

Let

be a real number, and
A
be an algebra over
\mathbb{R}
. Let
\|\cdot\|
be a multiplicative semi-norm on

|\cdot|

We only need to check the triangle inequality. By direct computation, we get:

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left|{n\choose i}\right|^{t}\|x\|^{i}\|y\|^{n-i}

\displaystyle\leqslant(n+1)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left|{n\choose i}\right|\|x\|^{\frac{i}{t}}\|y\|^{\frac{n-i}{t}}\right)^{t}

\displaystyle=(n+1)\left(\|x\|^{\frac{1}{t}}+\|y\|^{\frac{1}{t}}\right)^{nt}

Take

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{\frac{1}{t}}\leqslant\|x\|^{\frac{1}{t}}+\|y\|^{\frac{1}{t}}

Compactifications

The topological spaces
(U/K_{\bullet})^{\mathrm{an}}
with any norm on
K
as above are all Hausdorff and locally compact, but it is not compact unless
U
is projective over
K
. For many purpose we need to obtain compact spaces, and hence we define various compactifications of these spaces, especially of
(U/K_{1})^{\mathrm{an}}
.

The most common one is just given by a compactification of varieties. Namely, given an open immersion of varieties
U\subset X
with
X
projective, we obtain an compactifications
(U/K_{\bullet})^{\mathrm{an}}\to(X/K_{\bullet})^{\mathrm{an}}
. Of course it depends on
X
, so it is not intrinsic. One solution is that we can take all projective compactifications
X
and consider the inverse limit
\displaystyle\lim_{X\supset U}(X/K_{\bullet})^{\mathrm{an}}
, which is a kind of Zariski-Riemann spaces.

For the analytic space
(U/K_{1})^{\mathrm{an}}
, there is another compactification called hybrid compactification, as in
[
BJ
, §2]
. Although they only consider the case of complex manifolds, the essential idea works also for general valuation fields.

In the case of complex manifold, they add some non-archimedean part as the boundary, usually the dual complex of boundary divisor. In
[
BJ
, Definition 4.4]
, they also consider the inverse limit of all such compactifications. This definition is essentially the same as ours defined later.

In
[
AN
]
, they define the hybrid space of higher ranks, namely, the boundary itself contains both archimedean part and the non-archimedean part.

Using hybrid norms, we see that
(U/K_{\mathrm{hyb}})^{\mathrm{an}}
contains a canonical subset homeomorphic to
(U/K_{1})^{\mathrm{an}}\times(0,1]
, and thus by Theorem
2.5
, we have the following result.

Corollary 2.9
.

The topological space

\widetilde{U}^{b}:=\left((U/K_{\mathrm{hyb}})^{\mathrm{an}}\backslash U^{\beth}\right)/\!\sim_{\mathrm{eqv}}

is a compactification of
(U/K_{1})^{\mathrm{an}}
. Its boundary is the topological space
\widetilde{U}_{0}^{b}
derived from the Berkovich space
(U/K_{0})^{\mathrm{an}}
over the trivially-normed field.

This compactification works for all complete field
K
with a non-trivial norm
|\cdot|_{1}
, and all quasi-projective varieties
U
(not necessarily smooth).

We can also get other non-archimedean compactifications by retracting the boundary. Let
U
be a smooth quasi-projective variety with a smooth projective model
X
, such that
D=X\backslash U
is a simple normal crossing divisor. Let
\Delta(D)
is the dual complex of
D
. Thuillier has showed the following results in
[
Thu
, Proposition 4.7]

Theorem 2.10
.

There is a canonical subset
\mathcal{S}(U)^{*}\subset U_{0}^{b}
, homeomorphic to
\Delta(D)\times(0,\infty)
, such that the topological space
U_{0}^{b}
retracts to
\mathcal{S}(U)^{*}
.

It can be shown in the proof that this retraction map is equivariant. Hence we get a continuous surjective map
f:\widetilde{U}_{0}^{b}\to\Delta(D)
.

If we take the pushout of the map
f
and the canonical injection
\widetilde{U}_{0}^{b}\to\widetilde{U}^{b}
, then we get a compactification of the complex manifold
(U/K_{1})^{\mathrm{an}}
with boundary
\Delta(D)
. This reintroduces the work in
[
BJ
, §2]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.5

First, we show that

is compact Hausdorff in all cases, and then so is

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
k
be as in Assumption
2.4
. Then the interior part

is compact Hausdorff.

Proof.

Let
X
be a projective model of
U
, and
X^{\mathrm{an}}=(X/(k,|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}}))^{\mathrm{an}}
. Since
X
is proper over
k
,

is just the set of points in

such that

for all

. Equivalently, we have

\displaystyle X^{\beth}\cong(X/(k,|\cdot|_{0}))^{\mathrm{an}},

and hence

is compact Hausdorff.

By
[
Ber90
]
, the reduction map

is anti-continuous, i.e., the inverse image of an open subset is closed. Thus

is a closed subspace of

∎

Now we show that
\widetilde{U}^{b}
is compact Hausdorff case by case.

Trivial norm case

In this subsection we prove Theorem
2.5
in the case when
k
is any noetherian integral domain with the trivial norm
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Ban}}=|\cdot|_{0}
.

Let
U
be a quasi-projective integral flat scheme over
k
. Fix a projective model
X/k
of
U
, and let
D=X\backslash U
, with the reduced subscheme structure. Then the reduction map
r:X^{\mathrm{an}}\to X
is defined on the whole space
X^{\mathrm{an}}
.

Proof.

First we show the Hausdorffness of
\widetilde{U}^{b}=U^{b}/\!\sim_{\mathrm{eqv}}
.

Let
x,y\in U^{b}
be two points which are not norm-equivalent. Then
r(x),r(y)
are two points in
D
(possibly equal). Since
X
is projective, there exists an open affine subscheme
\Spec A\subset X
containing
r(x)
and
r(y)
. Then
\Spec A
also contains
\rho(x)
and
\rho(y)
, and
x,y
give two norm
|\cdot|_{x},|\cdot|_{y}
on
A
.

Since
r(x),r(y)\in\Spec A
, we have
|a|_{x}\leqslant 1
,
|a|_{y}\leqslant 1
for all
a\in A
.

Note that
\rho(y)\notin\overline{\{r(x)\}}
, as
\rho(y)\notin D
and
r(x)\in D
. Thus we have

\{a\in A:|a|_{x}<1\}\not\subset\{a\in A:|a|_{y}=0\}.

By prime avoidance, two prime ideals
\{a\in A:|a|_{x}=0\}
and
\{a\in A:|a|_{y}=0\}
cannot cover the ideal
\{a\in A:|a|_{x}<1\}
. Thus there exists
f_{x}\in A
such that
0<|f_{x}|_{x}<1
and
|f_{x}|_{y}\neq 0
.

Similarly, take
f_{y}\in A
such that
0<|f_{y}|_{y}<1
and
|f_{y}|_{x}\neq 0
. Let
f=f_{x}f_{y}
. Then we have
0<|f|_{x}<1
and
0<|f|_{y}<1
.

Since
x,y
are not norm-equivalent, switch
x,y
if necessary, we may find
g\in A
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{\log|g(x)|}{\log|f(x)|}<\frac{\log|g(y)|}{\log|f(y)|}.

Take any
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{\log|g(x)|}{\log|f(x)|}<a<\frac{\log|g(y)|}{\log|f(y)|},

Define two open subset of
(\Spec A)^{\mathrm{an}}
as

\displaystyle V_{1}=\{v\in(\Spec A)^{\mathrm{an}}:f(v)\neq 0,1;\ \frac{\log|g(v)|}{\log|f(v)|}<a\}

\displaystyle V_{2}=\{v\in(\Spec A)^{\mathrm{an}}:f(v)\neq 0,1;\ \frac{\log|g(v)|}{\log|f(v)|}>a\}

Here we assume that
\log 0=-\infty<a
for all real number

. Then
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
are two disjoint open subsets of

which are stable under norm-equivalence relation and separate

. Thus
\widetilde{U}^{b}
is Hausdorff.

For the compactness of
\widetilde{U}^{b}
, since
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is compact Hausdorff, it is a
T_{4}
-topological space. As
U^{\beth}
,

are both closed in
X^{\mathrm{an}}
, there exist disjoint open subsets
W_{1}\supset U^{\beth}
and
W_{2}\supset D^{\mathrm{an}}
. Let
W_{3}=X^{\mathrm{an}}\backslash(W_{1}\cup W_{2})
, then
W_{3}
is closed in
X^{\mathrm{an}}
, and hence compact.

We show that
W_{3}\to U^{b}\to\widetilde{U}^{b}
is surjective. Given any
x\in U^{b}
, choose an affine open subset
\Spec A
containing
r(x)
, then
x
corresponding to a norm
|\cdot|_{x}
on
A
. Define the line
L:[0,\infty]\to X^{\mathrm{an}}
by

\displaystyle L(t)=\begin{cases}i(\rho(x)),&\text{if }t=0;\\
|\cdot|_{x}^{t},&\text{if }t\in(0,\infty);\\
i(r(x)),&\text{if }t=\infty,\end{cases}

where

is the inclusion map
X\to X^{\mathrm{an}}
. Then we can check that
L
is continuous. Since
L(0)\in W_{1}
,and
L(\infty)\in W_{2}
, and
[0,\infty]
is connected, two disjoint open subsets

and

cannot cover

. Thus there exist

, and

. Then

Now
\widetilde{U}^{b}
is a continuous image of a compact space, and hence it is compact.
∎

Adelic divisors on Berkovich spaces

The adelic divisors and line bundles on quasi-projective varieties were introduced by Yuan–Zhang in
[
YZ
, §2]
. We just briefly review their definitions.

3.1
Adelic divisors

Let
k=\mathbb{Z}
or a field, and let
X
be a projective flat variety over
k
.

If
k=\mathbb{Z}
, an
arithmetic divisor
on
X
is a pair
(D,g_{D})
, where
D
is a Cartier divisor on
X
, and
g_{D}
is a continuous real-valued conjugation-invariant function on
(X\backslash D)(\mathbb{C})
(with respect to the analytic topology), such that if
D
is locally defined by
f
, then
g_{D}(x)+\log|f(x)|
extends continuously to
D(\mathbb{C})
. Equivalently,
g_{D}
induces a conjugation-invariant metric on the line bundle
\mathscr{O}(D)
over
X(\mathbb{C})
, such that the canonical section
1\in\mathscr{O}(D)
has the norm
\|1\|(x)=e^{-g_{D}(x)}
. The arithmetic divisor
(D,g_{D})
is called
effective
if
D
is effective and
g_{D}
is non-negative.

If
k
is a field, by abuse of notation, an arithmetic divisor on
X
is just a usual divisor on
X
. The set of arithmetic divisors on
X
is denoted by
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(X)
.

Now let
U
be a quasi-projective flat variety over
k
, and let
X
be a projective model of
U
. Define the
group of
(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z})
-divisors
as

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(X,U):=\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\times_{\mathrm{Div}(U)_{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathrm{Div}(U).

Thus a
(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z})
-divisor
(\bar{D},D^{\prime})
consists of an arithmetic
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\bar{D}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
and a usual divisor
D^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Div}(U)
such that their image in
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U)_{\mathbb{Q}}
are the same. By abuse of notations, we just write
\bar{D}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(X,U)
, and call
D^{\prime}
the integral part of
\bar{D}
.

The
group of model divisors
on
U
is defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U)_{\mathrm{mod}}:=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
X\end{subarray}}\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(X,U),

where
X
ranges over all projective model of
U
, and the morphisms are pull-back of divisors.

There is a boundary norm on
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U)_{\mathrm{mod}}
, which more rigorously is an extended norm, i.e. it allows that
|D|=\infty
for some
D
. Choose a fixed projective model
X
such that
Z=X\backslash U
is a divisor. If
k=\mathbb{Z}
, we further choose a positive Green function
g_{Z}
, and let
\bar{Z}=(Z,g_{Z})
. Let
\bar{D}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U)_{\mathrm{mod}}
. The
boundary norm

\|\cdot\|_{\bar{Z}}
is defined as

\displaystyle\|\bar{D}\|_{\bar{Z}}=\inf\{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}:-\varepsilon\bar{Z}\leqslant\bar{D}\leqslant\varepsilon\bar{Z}\},

where the partial order is given by effectivity of divisors. If the integral part of
\bar{D}
is non-zero, such an
\varepsilon
does not exist, and we assume that
\inf\emptyset=\infty
, thus
\|\bar{D}\|_{\bar{\mathbb{Z}}}=\infty
in this case.

Different boundary divisors give different boundary norms on
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U)_{\mathrm{mod}}
, but they all induce the same boundary topology. The
group of adelic divisor

\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)
is defined as the completion of
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U)_{\mathrm{mod}}
with respect to any boundary norm.

Let
\bar{D}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)
. Then by definition,
\bar{D}
is represented by a sequence
\bar{D}_{i}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(X_{i})
, where
X_{i}
are projective models of
U
. We may assume that if
j>i
, then there is a morphism
\pi_{ji}:X_{j}\to X_{i}
compatible with open immersions
U\subset X_{i}
, and
(X_{0},\bar{Z}=\bar{D}_{0})
is a boundary divisor defining the boundary norm. The sequence
\bar{D}_{i}
satisfies the Cauchy condition that there is a sequence
\epsilon_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
convergent to
0
such that

\displaystyle\epsilon_{i}\pi_{j0}^{*}\bar{Z}\leqslant\pi_{ji}^{*}\bar{D}_{i}-\bar{D}_{j}\leqslant\varepsilon_{i}\pi_{j0}^{*}\bar{Z}

for all
j>i
. In particular,
D_{i}|_{U}
are the same for all
i
, and is denoted by
D|_{U}
, called the integral part of
\bar{D}
.

Let
\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)_{b}\subset\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(U/k)

M\times N\to\mathbb{Z}
given by
(a,b)\mapsto\deg a\circ b:\mathbb{G}m\to\mathbb{G}m
. Then it induces the perfect pairing
M_{\mathbb{R}}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
. We will always identify
M\cong N^{\vee}
and
M_{\mathbb{R}}\cong N_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
.

A toric variety
X
is a variety containing an open subset
X_{0}\cong T
such that the translation action
T\times X_{0}\to X_{0}
extends to an action
T\times X\to X
. There are deep connections between toric varieties and polytopes, polyhedra, and combinatorics. For example, we will frequently use the following facts.

Theorem 4.1
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between complete normal toric varieties and a finite rational polyhedral decomposition of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

See
[
KKMS
, Theorem 6,7,8]
for a proof.

Let
X
be a complete normal toric variety. Each Cartier divisor supported on the boundary is also a Weil divisor, which is sum of codimension 1 subvarieties contained in
X\backslash X_{0}
. Since
T
acts on
X
, it must be sum of codimension 1 orbits of
T
. In particular, any boundary Weil divisor and Cartier divisors are
T
-invariant.

Now let
D
be a Cartier divisor
D
supported on the boundary. It will induce a supporting function on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
as follows. Recall that a point
a\in N
corresponds to a 1-parameter subgroup
\lambda_{a}:\mathbb{G}m\to X
. Since
X
is complete,
\lambda_{a}
extends to a function
\bar{\lambda}_{a}:\mathbb{A}^{1}\to X
, and
\bar{\lambda}_{a}^{*}D
is a Cartier divisor on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
supported at the closed point
0
. Its order is denoted by
\mathrm{ord}_{0}\ \bar{\lambda}_{a}^{*}D
.

Definition 4.2
.

The supporting function
\mathrm{SF}_{D}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
is the unique continuous conical(meaning that
f(\lambda x)=\lambda f(x)
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
) function such that
\mathrm{SF}_{D}(a)=\mathrm{ord}_{0}\ \bar{\lambda}_{a}^{*}D
for all
a\in N
.

To see that
SF_{D}
is well-defined, we refer to
[
CLS
, §4.2]
and
[
KKMS
, Theorem 9.]
. Be careful that the supporting function in
[
CLS
, §4.2]
differs with ours by a sign.

4.2
Analytification and tropicalization

In this subsection we study some analytic property of toric varieties. Equipped
k
with the trivial norm, we get an analytic space
T^{\mathrm{an}}
. Further, there is a
tropicalization map

\displaystyle\mathrm{trop}:T^{\mathrm{an}}\to N_{\mathbb{R}}

mapping a point
|\cdot|_{x}
to the linear functional
(\chi^{m}\mapsto-\log|\chi^{m}|_{x})_{m\in M}
. It is
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-equivariant.

If we fix a coordinate
T=\Spec k[T_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,T_{n}^{\pm 1}]
, then
M
,
N
have canonical basis
\{T_{i}\}
and dual basis
\{T_{i}^{\vee}\}
. Under this basis, the tropicalization map
\mathrm{trop}
has an explicit form

\displaystyle\mathrm{trop}:T^{\mathrm{an}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle|\cdot|_{x}

\displaystyle\mapsto(-\log|T_{1}|_{x},\ldots,-\log|T_{n}|_{x}).

The tropicalization map
\mathrm{trop}:T^{\mathrm{an}}\to N_{\mathbb{R}}
has a continuous section

\displaystyle\mathrm{emb}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\to T^{\mathrm{an}}

sending the linear functional
a:M\to\mathbb{R}
to the norm

\left|\sum_{m\in M}a_{m}\chi_{m}\right|=\max_{m\in M}|a_{m}|\mathrm{e}^{-\langle m,a\rangle}.

Let
q=\mathrm{emb}\circ\mathrm{trop}
. Then Thuillier showed in
[
Thu
, §2]
that
q
is a continuous retraction of
T^{\mathrm{an}}
to the its image
q(T^{\mathrm{an}})
. In particular,
q^{2}=q
and
\mathrm{trop}(x)=\mathrm{trop}(q(x))
.

We are interested in adelic divisors on
T
that are limit of T-invariant divisors on different toric varieties
X
. First, we give a new description of toric divisors.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
X
be a complete toric variety, and
D\in\mathrm{Div}(X)
with
D|_{T}=0
. Let
g_{D}:(X\backslash|D|)^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R}
be Green function of
D
, which is necessarily equivariant. Then its restriction to
T^{\mathrm{an}}
is the pull-back of the supporting function
\mathrm{SF}_{D}
under the tropicalization map
\mathrm{trop}:T^{\mathrm{an}}\to N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

We first prove that for any
x,y\in T^{\mathrm{an}}
with
\mathrm{trop}(x)=\mathrm{trop}(y)
,
g_{D}(x)=g_{D}(y)
. Since
q(x)=q(y)
, we may assume that
y=q(x)
.

Let
m:T\times X\to X
be the action of
T
on
X
, and
p_{2}:T\times X\to X
be the projection to the second factor. Note that
D
is
T
-invariant. Therefore, for the two morphisms
m,p_{2}:T\times X\to X
, we have
m^{*}D=p_{2}^{*}D
.

Let
g_{D}:(X\backslash|D|)^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R}
be the Green function of
D
. Then the Green function of
m^{*}D
is just the pull back of
g_{D}
via the multiplication morphism

\displaystyle m:(T\times(X\backslash D))^{\mathrm{an}}\to(X\backslash D)^{\mathrm{an}}

and similarly for
p_{2}^{*}D
. Since
m^{*}D=p_{2}^{*}D
(as usual divisors, not adelic divisors), for each point
z
in
(T\times X)^{\beth}\backslash(T\times D)^{\beth}
, we must have
g_{D}(m(z))=g_{D}(p_{2}(z))
. Now it follows from Lemma
4.4
below, and
g_{D}
is the pull-back of some continuous conical function
f
on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

To check that
f=\mathrm{SF}_{D}
, for each
a\in N
, the map
\lambda_{a}:\mathbb{G}m\to T
induces a map
\lambda_{a}^{\mathrm{trop}}:N_{\mathbb{G}m,\mathbb{R}}\to N_{T,\mathbb{R}}
, which is just the linear map sending the canonical base in
N_{\mathbb{G}m}
to
a\in N
, and the diagram

\textstyle{\mathbb{G}_{m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lambda_{a}}
\textstyle{N_{\mathbb{G}m,\mathbb{R}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i_{a}}
\textstyle{T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N_{T,\mathbb{R}}}

commutes. Then it reduces to the case that
T=\mathbb{G}m
, and then by direct computation.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Take any point
x\in X_{0}^{\mathrm{an}}\cong T^{\mathrm{an}}
, then there is a point
z\in(T\times X_{0})^{\mathrm{an}}
, such that
p_{1}(z)=q(x)\in T^{\beth}
,
p_{2}(z)=x\in X_{0}^{\mathrm{an}}
and
m(z)=q(x)
.

We will give an explicit proof, but the idea is the same as in
[
Thu
]
.

Proof.

Recall that
q(x)
is the norm given by the formula

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m\in M}c_{m}\chi^{m}\right|_{q(x)}=\max_{m\in M}|c_{m}||\chi^{m}|_{x}

We check directly that the norm

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m,n\in M}c_{mn}\chi^{m}\otimes\chi^{n}\right|_{z}=\max_{m\in M}\left(\left|\sum_{n\in M}c_{mn}\chi^{n}\right|_{x}\right)

satisfies our conditions.
∎

4.3
Toric adelic divisors

Now we introduce the toric adelic divisors on
T
and then compare it to toroidal
b
-divisors in
[
BB
]
and special adelic divisors in
[
Son
]
.

Let
f:N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
be any continuous conical function on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
\mathrm{trop}
is equivariant,
f\circ\mathrm{trop}
is an equivariant function on
T^{\mathrm{an}}
, and thus induces an adelic divisor. We will give a detailed description of such adelic divisors.

Definition 4.5
.

A toric adelic divisor
D
on a torus
T
is an adelic divisor such that
D|_{T}=0
and its Green function
g_{D}
is the pull-back of a continuous conic function
f
on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
under the tropicalization map.

Similarly as the model case, if an adelic divisor
D
is toric, then the function
f
has an explicit description.

Proposition 4.6
.

If
D
is a toric adelic divisor, then for each
a\in N
corresponding to the 1-dimensional parameter subgroup
\lambda_{a}:\mathbb{G}m\to T
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}^{*}D=f(a)[0]+f(-a)[\infty],

where
[0]
means the adelic divisor
[0]\in\mathrm{Div}(\mathbf{P}^{1})\subset\widehat{\mathrm{Div}}(\mathbb{G}_{m})
, and similarly for
[\infty]
.

The proof of it is th same as the model case. Hence the function
f
is also called the supporting function, and is denoted by
\mathrm{SF}_{D}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Now we are ready to prove the main theorem.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
D
be an adelic divisor with
D|_{T}=0
. The following two statements are equivalent.

(1) The adelic divisor
D
is toric: the Green function
g_{D}
is the pull back of th continuous conic function
SF_{D}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
under the tropicalization map
\mathrm{trop}:T^{\mathrm{an}}\to N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

(2) There is a Cauchy sequence
(X_{i},D_{i})
representing
D
such that each
X_{i}
is a complete toric variety and
D_{i}\in\mathrm{Div}(X_{i})
is supported in the boundary
X_{i}\backslash T
.

Proof.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1): By Proposition
4.3
and the fact that the limit of toric adelic divisors are still toric.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): We fix a projective model
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
of
T
, with boundary divisor
D_{0}=X\backslash T
, and consider the set of all functions
f:N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
which are conical, continuous, piecewise linear, convex on each face, and has integral value on
N
. By
[
KKMS
, Theorem 9]
, every such function gives a coherent sheaf of
T
-invariant fractional ideals contained in
i_{*}\mathscr{O}_{T}
, and then give a complete toric variety with a Cartier divisor on it whose supporting function is exactly
f
.

Take any norm on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, and let
\Sigma
be the unit sphere in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. The restriction gives an isomorphism
C(N_{\mathbb{R}})_{\mathrm{eqv}}
and
C(\Sigma)
. We will identify these two linear spaces.

Since the set of all such functions are closed under addition, taking minimum, and separating points in
\Sigma
, by the Boolean ring version of Stone-Weierstrass theorem, any continuous conical function can be approximated by these functions uniformly on all compact subset. Hence any toric adelic divisor can be approximated by model toric divisors.
∎

In fact, from the proof of Proposition
4.3
, we see that
D
is toric if and only if
D
is
T
-invariant, meaning that for the multiplication and projection maps
m,p_{2}:T\times T\to T
, two adelic divisors
m^{*}D
and
p_{2}^{*}D
are equal with respect to the partial compactification
T\times T\to T\times X
. Since we don’t introduce adelic divisors for partial compactification, we refer to
[
Son
, §3.1]
.

We have canonical injections

Such that the spaces of corresponding supporting function ae given by

Be careful that their definition of
\varphi_{D}
in

Proof.

The inclusion map

Suppose that the adelic divisor

Comparison with special adelic divisors

We can also compare toric adelic divisors with special adelic divisors in
[
Son
, §5.]
. We need to fix an smooth toric embedding
T\subset X
with
X
smooth complete. Then each Cartier divisor on
X
is also a Weil divisor. Let
D=X\backslash T
, and let
D=\sum D_{j}
be the decomposition of
D
into irreducible components. Since each
D_{j}
is also a Weil divisor, there is a unique ray
L_{j}
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
corresponding to
D_{i}
. Let
v_{j}\in N
be a primitive generator of
D_{j}
.

We view
\Spec k((t))
be the generic points in
\Spec k[[t]]
, then we can talk about order of adelic divisors on
\Spec k((t))
.

Definition 4.9
.

An adelic divisor on
T
is called special (with respect to
T\subset X
) if there is a continuous conic function
f:N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all morphism
i:\Spec k((t))\to T
with extension
\Spec k[[t]]\to X
(still denoted by
i
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{0}\ i^{*}E=f\left(\sum_{j}\mathrm{ord}_{0}i^{*}D_{j}\cdot v_{j}\right).

Note that we always have
\mathrm{ord}_{0}i^{*}D_{j}\geqslant 0
, and
\{j:\mathrm{ord}_{0}i^{*}D_{j}\neq 0\}=\{j:i([0])\in D_{j}\}
has cardinality at most
n+1
, hence the sum is in fact on a simplicial cone.

Such a function
f
is called the skeleton function in
[
Son
]
.

Theorem 4.10
.

An adelic divisor is toric if and only it is special with respect to a smooth toric compactification.

Proof.

Any smooth toric compactifications are locally of the form
\mathbb{G}m^{n}\subset\mathbb{A}^{k}\times\mathbb{G}m^{n-k}
, as it corresponding to a simplicial cone in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
generated by a subset of a basis

. See also
[
Ful93
, p. 29]
. Therefore, it suffices to consider the case that
T\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}
and that
i:k[[t]]\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
.

Let
E
ba a toric adelic divisor, and

be a map which extends to
\Spec k[[t]]\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
. Take the valuation

on

such that

. We also denote

to be the unique point in

, and

If

is toric, then the Green function

of

is determined by its tropicalization, ans we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{0}i^{*}E

\displaystyle=g_{E}(i^{\mathrm{an}}(s))=f(\mathrm{trop}(i^{\mathrm{an}}(s)))

\displaystyle=f\left(\sum_{j}\mathrm{ord}_{0}i^{*}D_{j}\cdot v_{j}\right)

Let

lies in the kernel of the composition map

\displaystyle CH^{p}(\bar{X})^{0}:=\ker\left(CH^{p}(\bar{X})\to H^{2p}(\bar{X},\mathbb{C})\to H^{0}(\bar{S},R^{2p}\bar{\pi}_{*}\mathbb{C})\right).

Then the Beilinson–Bloch height of

[

Assumption 6.6
.

Any flat cycle
Z\in CH^{p}(X/S)
admits an extension
\tilde{Z}\in CH^{p}(\bar{X})^{0}
.

Theorem 6.7
.

If the Assumption
6.6
above holds, the asymptotic height pairing agrees with the Beilinson–Bloch height pairing. In other words, choose a local coordinate near a point
s\in\bar{S}\backslash S
, assume that
0
is a singular fiber, we have

\displaystyle\langle Z_{s},W_{s}\rangle_{s,ar}=-\langle Z,W\rangle_{0,na}\log|s|+O(1)

for
s
close to
0
. Here
\langle Z_{s},W_{s}\rangle_{s,ar}
means the archimedean Beilinson–Bloch height at the smooth fiber
X_{s}
, and
\langle Z,W\rangle_{0,na}
means the geometric Beilinson–Bloch height for the field
K=K(S)
and the place
0
with bad reduction.

The error term
O(1)
is a harmonic function near
0
, and in particular, bounded near
0
.

In the following, we always assume that the Assumption
6.6
holds. Let
\bar{Z}
,
\bar{W}
be the Zariski closure of

on
\bar{X}
.

Lemma 6.8

There exists another model
\bar{X}^{\prime}
with an extension
\tilde{Z}^{\prime}\in CH^{p}(\bar{X}^{\prime})^{0}
, such that
\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}
intersects
\bar{W}
properly.

Proof.

Let
\bar{Z}_{i}
,
\bar{W}_{j}
are irreducible components of
Z
and
W
. By successive blowup of
\bar{Z}_{i}\cap\bar{W}_{j}
and taking proper transform, we may assume that
\bar{Z}\cap\bar{W}
is empty. Use resolution of singularity again, we assume that
X
is smooth with
X_{0}
normal crossing divisor. Then the pull-back of
\tilde{Z}
(which is a cycle class) could be represented by a cycle satisfying the Assumption
6.6
. So we may assume that
\bar{Z}
and
\bar{W}
has no intersection. Write
\tilde{Z}=\bar{Z}+Z_{0}
, then
Z_{0}
is a linear combination of
i_{j,*}Y
for some cycle
Y\in CH^{p-1}(X_{0,j})
, where
X_{0,j}
is a irreducible component of the singular fiber
X_{0}
of dimension
n
and
i:X_{0,j}\to X
be the injection.

Since each
X_{0,j}
is smooth of codimension
1
in
\bar{X}
, it is a divisor on
\bar{X}
, hence its scheme intersection with
W
coincide with cycle intersection
i_{j}^{*}\bar{W}
. Thus for cycle
Y
in
X_{0,i}
, we have projection formula

\displaystyle\bar{W}\cdot i_{j,*}Y=i_{j,*}(i_{j}^{*}\bar{W}\cdot Y)

Here
\cdot
means the intersection in Chow group. By moving lemma in
[
Ful98
, §11.4]
, there is a another cycle
Y^{\prime}
in
X_{0,j}
which intersects
i_{j}^{*}\bar{W}
properly, and we take
i_{j,*}Y^{\prime}
to replace

.
∎

Now we will compare the archimedean and non-archimedean height. We will use arithmetic intersection in
[

]
, and try to follow their the notations of Green currents.

Lemma 6.9
.

For each point
s_{0}\in\bar{S}\backslash S
, there is a local analytic coordinate
U\cong\Delta
centered at
s_{0}
, and a
(p-1,p-1)
-current on
g_{\tilde{Z}}
of
\tilde{Z}
on
\bar{\pi}^{-1}(U)
, such that it is a smooth form away from
|Z|
, and satisfied the current equation

\displaystyle dd^{c}g_{\tilde{Z}}=-\delta_{\tilde{Z}}.

Proof.

First we choose any Green form
g^{\prime}_{\tilde{Z}}
of
\tilde{Z}
as in
[
SABK
, §2]
. Then there is a smooth
(p,p)
-form
\omega^{p,p}
on
X
, such that

\displaystyle dd^{c}g^{\prime}_{\tilde{Z}}=-\delta_{\tilde{Z}}+\omega^{p,p}.

By our assumption on
\tilde{Z}
, there exist an analytic neighborhood
U
of
s_{0}
such that both
dd^{c}g_{\tilde{Z}}
and
\delta_{\tilde{Z}}
vanish in the current cohomology
H^{2p}_{cur}(\pi^{-1}(U),\mathbb{C})
, which agrees with the Betti or de Rham cohomology
H^{2p}(\pi^{-1}(U),\mathbb{C})
. Therefore, there is a smooth
(p-1,p-1)
-form
\omega^{p-1,p-1}
such that
dd^{c}\omega^{p-1,p-1}=\omega^{p,p}
. Then just take
g_{\tilde{Z}}=g^{\prime}_{\tilde{Z}}-\omega^{p-1,p-1}
.
∎

Now let
\tilde{Z}\in CH^{p}(\bar{X})^{0}
, and
W\in CH^{q}_{\hom}(X/S)
. Let
\bar{W}
be its Zariski closer. We may assume that
\tilde{Z}
and
\bar{W}
intersects properly.

Since now the theorem is pure analytically local, we may assume
\bar{S}=\Delta
, the unit disk, and
S=\Delta^{*}
. Let
g_{\tilde{Z}}
be the current in Lemma
6.9
. Take any other Green form
g_{\bar{W}}
of
\bar{W}
, and take arithmetic intersection
(\tilde{Z},g_{\tilde{Z}})*(\bar{W},g_{\bar{W}})
of
[
SABK
, II.3]
, which is of the form
(\tilde{Z}\cdot W,g_{\tilde{Z}}*g_{\bar{W}})
satisfying the equation

\displaystyle dd^{c}(g_{\tilde{Z}}*g_{\bar{W}})=-\delta_{\tilde{Z}\cdot\bar{W}}

by
[
SABK
, II.3, Theorem 4]
, where
\tilde{Z}\cdot\bar{W}
is the intersection product with Serre’s mulplicity formula.

Taking push forward, we get an equation of current

\displaystyle dd^{c}(\pi_{*}g_{\tilde{Z}}*g_{\bar{W}})=-\delta_{\pi_{*}(\tilde{Z}\cdot\bar{W})}=-\langle Z,W\rangle_{s_{0},\mathrm{na}}[s_{0}].

Lemma 6.10
.

The
(0,0)
-current
\pi_{*}(g_{\tilde{Z}}*g_{\bar{W}})
is represented by the function
h(s)=2\langle Z_{s},W_{s}\rangle_{a,\mathrm{ar}}
.

Proof.

By direct computation, for any compact support smooth
(1,1)
-form
f(s)\mathrm{d}s\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{s}
, we have

\displaystyle\qquad\ \langle\pi_{*}g_{\tilde{Z}}*g_{\bar{W}},f(s)\mathrm{d}s\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{s}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\bar{W}}g_{\tilde{Z}}\wedge\pi^{*}(f(s)\mathrm{d}s\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{s})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta^{*}}\left(\int_{W_{s}}g_{\tilde{Z}}\right)f(s)\mathrm{d}s\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta}2\langle Z_{s},W_{s}\rangle_{s,\mathrm{ar}}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{s}.

∎

Now we get the current equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\ 2\langle Z_{s},W_{s}\rangle_{s,\mathrm{ar}}=-\langle Z,W\rangle_{s_{0},\mathrm{na}}[s_{0}].

Solve this equation, we get

\displaystyle\langle Z_{s},W_{s}\rangle_{s,\mathrm{ar}}=-\langle Z,W\rangle_{s_{0},\mathrm{na}}\log|s|+O(1),

here
O(1)
is a harmonic near
s_{0}
, and in particular, bounded. Thus we get Theorem
6.7
.

Remark 6.11
.

In
[
SABK
]
, they only define push forward for projective smooth morphism. Here we actually use push forward for flat morphism.

Let
f:X\to S
is projective flat and generically smooth, and
(Z,g_{Z})
be an arithmetic Chow cycle, i.e.,
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}g_{Z}=-\delta_{Z}+\omega
for some smooth form
\omega
. If we apply push forward, we will get an equation

\displaystyle dd^{c}f_{*}g_{Z}=-f_{*}\delta_{Z}+f_{*}\omega.

However, for non-smooth
f
, the push forward
f_{*}\omega
will not be a smooth form on
S
anymore. Instead, it is a smooth form on the smooth locus of
f
, and along the boundary, it may have singularity.

Pilot Distributions for Phase Noise Estimation in Electro-Optic Frequency Comb Systems
Mohammad Farsi (1), Magnus Karlsson (2), and Erik Agrell (1)

Abstract




1
Introduction

Resource sharing among multiple wavelength channels is necessary for co-integrating multiple optical transceivers without exceeding power and heat constraints. The electro-optic frequency comb, serving as a multi-wavelength source, provides uniformly spaced carriers for spectral superchannels, enabling co-integration. Moreover, they have applications in metrology, sensing, and telecommunications.

The phase noise among different comb lines is fully correlated as a result of sharing the same light source. This enables potential resource savings by utilizing joint phase estimation. This becomes more important with high-order modulation formats where phase noise is more detrimental.

The inherent phase noise correlation between optical comb lines can reduce the computational complexity in digital signal processing through optical comb regeneration techniques
[
1
]
or improve the performance of pilot-aided tracking schemes such as reference-assisted (also known as master–slave) processing
[
2
,
3
]
or joint-channel processing
[
4
]
. Previous studies have shown that distributing pilots in time and channel in a grid fashion works best for multichannel systems
[
5
]
. In
[
6
,
7
]
it was shown that the
optimal pilot placement for space-division multiplexing systems depends on the amount
of phase noise correlation across channels. However, there has been no investigation of pilot placement in the context of electro-optic frequency comb systems where the channels are fully correlated.

In this paper, we explore the use of phase correlation in phase tracking in electro-optic frequency combs and analyze pilot placement methods. Optimal pilot positioning is studied and unlike a regular multichannel system, the outer comb lines are found to be optimal for certain estimators. The pilot distribution can substantially impact the bit error rate, with a potential for significant improvement.

Also, assume negligible (or already compensated) impairments with the exception of phase noise and amplified spontaneous emission noise. Reference-Assisted Phase Tracking Scheme

The reference-assisted strategy designates

channels as

\mathcal{D}=\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{D}\}

\mathcal{D}

[-M,M]

5
Pilot Distributions

The estimation technique can be done using either an reference-assisted or a joint-channel processing scheme, depending on the pilot distribution. Let
\alpha_{\mathrm{p}}\in[0,1]
represent the average rate across all channels.

Two pilot distributions for joint-channel processing were examined: reference-assisted distribution, in which pilots are positioned at the same time index across a group of reference channels identified by
\mathcal{D}
, and wrapped diagonal distribution, which places pilots along the diagonal in a wrapped pattern. In reference-assisted distribution, pilots are only located in the channels specified by
\mathcal{D}
, while in wrapped diagonal distribution, pilots are distributed across all channels using the wrapped diagonal approach. The pilot rate
\alpha_{\mathrm{p}}
determines the time spacing between the pilots for both distribution types. Fig.
1
depicts examples of the distributions. Moreover, the pilot rate of reference-assisted distribution is restricted to
\alpha_{\mathrm{p}}\leq D/L
and for wrapped diagonal distribution it is limited to
\alpha_{\mathrm{p}}\leq 1/L
.

Simulation Setup

Monte Carlo simulations of uncoded
64
-ary quadrature-amplitude modulation (QAM) at a symbol rate of
R_{\mathrm{s}}=20
Gbaud, using system model (
1
), were performed with
N=10^{5}
symbols per channel. The number of channels
L
was varied between
11
to
101
, with a fixed
\alpha_{\mathrm{p}}=1\%
pilot overhead and a signal-to-noise-ratio yielding a bit error rate of
10^{-3}
for phase noise-free transmission. The continuous wave laser linewidth was fixed at
\Delta\nu^{\mathrm{c}}=100\,\text{kHz}
while the radio frequency oscillator linewidth was varied within the practical range of
\Delta\nu^{\mathrm{r}}\in[1,10^{4}]\,\text{Hz}
. The extended Kalman smoother algorithm from
[
10
]
was used for phase tracking due to its adaptability to various pilot distributions. For any set of reference channels
\mathcal{D}
, we define the mean estimation error
\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{D}}=\mathbb{E}[\lVert\mathrm{T}\mathrm{Q}^{\dagger}\underline{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathcal{D}}_{k}\rVert^{2}]
.

7
Conclusion

Several types of pilot distributions were compared
through Monte Carlo simulations in terms of the
resulting bit error rate performance for tracking correlated
phase noise in electro-optic frequency comb systems. It was shown theoretically and confirmed by simulations that for certain phase estimators, the optimal reference channels are the outer channels (first and last), contrary to regular wavelength-division multiplexing channels where pilots are placed in all channels.

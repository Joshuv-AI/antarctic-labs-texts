Scale pressure for amenable group actions
Dandan Cheng, Qian Hao, Zhiming Li

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce the notion of scale pressure and measure theoretic scale pressure for amenable group actions. A variational principle for amenable group actions is presented. We also describe these quantities by pseudo-orbits. Moreover, we prove that if G G is a finitely generated countable discrete amenable group, then the scale pressure of G G coincides with the scale pressure of G G with respect to pseudo-orbits.

1.
Introduction

The concept of entropy, introduced into the realm of dynamical systems more than fifty years ago, has become an important ingredient in the characterization of the complexity of dynamical systems. In the late nineties, M. Gromov
[
3
]
proposed a new dynamical
concept of dimension that was meant to extend the usual topological
dimension to broader contexts. This notion, called mean dimension, is defined for continuous maps on compact
metric spaces in terms of the growth rate of refinements of
coverings of the phase space and was shown to be hardly computable
in general. This invariant of topological dynamical systems was used
by E. Lindenstrauss and B. Weiss introduced in
[
8
]
(it is a
very interesting work to study which other properties of the entropy
are maintained by the mean dimension and the metric mean dimension)
to answer a long-standing question in topological dynamics: does
every minimal topological dynamical system embed into
([0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}},\sigma)
? The answer is negative, since any system
embeddable in
([0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}},\sigma)
has topological mean
dimension at most one and
[
8
]
constructed a minimal system
with a mean dimension strictly greater than one. This concept,
inspired by the topological entropy, turns out to be a metric
version of Gromov’s notion. Moreover, it is an upper bound for the
mean dimension and, in general, easier to estimate. However, it
depends on the metric used, while the topological entropy is metric
independent. The upper and
lower metric mean dimensions, unlike Gromov’s concept, depend on
the metric adopted on the space and are nonzero only if the
topological entropy of the dynamics is infinite. Since continuous systems on manifold with infinite topological entropy are generic
[
17
]
, the metric mean dimension exhibits several intrinsic
features which makes it a rather compelling notion to be studied. There are many elegant approaches to mean dimensions
[
5
,
7
,
12
]
and mean dimensions with potential
[
11
]
.

This work was intended as an attempt to give a unified way to analyze systems with infinite entropy by introducing a notion of pressure-like complexity functions, which is called
scale pressure
, with respect to a scale function
s
and a potential function
f
. A variational principle of scale pressure is presented. We also describe these quantities by pseudo-orbits.

2.
Scale Pressures

More precisely, suppose that
X
is a compact metric space with metric
d
,
\mathcal{B}
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra,
G
is a countable discrete amenable group and
C(X,\mathbb{R})
is the Banach algebra of real-valued continuous functions on
X
equipped with the supreme norm:
\|\varphi\|:=\max\limits_{x\in X}|\varphi(x)|
,
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
. Let
(X,G)
be a
G
-action topological dynamical system,
\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
be collection of the
G
-invariant Borel probability measures of
X
and
\mathcal{E}_{G}(X)
be collection of the
G
-ergodic Borel probability measures of
X
. Recall that a group
G
is said to be
amenable
if there exists a sequence of finite subsets
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
G
which are asymptotically invariant, i.e.,

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{|F_{n}\vartriangle gF_{n}|}{|F_{n}|}=0,\text{ for all }g\in G.

Such sequences
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are called Følner sequences. We denote the collection of nonempty finite subsets of
G
by
F(G)
. For each
F\in F(G)
, set metrics
d_{F}(x,y)=\max\limits_{g\in F}d(gx,gy)
.

Recall that a Følner sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
G
is
tempered
if there exists a constant
C
which is independent of
n
such that

|\bigcup_{k<n}F_{k}^{-1}F_{n}|\leqslant C|F_{n}|,\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
E,J,K\subseteq X
and
F\in F(G)
. If for any
x\in K
there exists
y\in J
such that
d_{F}(x,y)<\varepsilon
, then we call
J
is an

(F,\varepsilon)
-spanning set of
K
. If for any
x,y\in E
we have
d_{F}(x,y)>\varepsilon
, then we call
E
is an

(F,\varepsilon)
-separated set
.

Let
sep_{\varepsilon,F}(X,G)
denote the maximal cardinality of an
(F,\varepsilon)
-separated subset of
X
and
spa_{\varepsilon,F}(X,G)
denote the smallest cardinality of an
(F,\varepsilon)
-spanning subset of
X
.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
s:(0,1)\to(0,\infty)
is called a
scale function
, if for any
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
,
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{s(\lambda x)}{s(x)}=1.
We denote by
\mathcal{S}
(resp.
\mathcal{S}^{*}
) the set of all (resp. non-increasing) scale functions.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{**}=\{s\in\mathcal{S}^{*}:\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\varepsilon\log\varepsilon}{s(\varepsilon)}=0\}.

For any Følner sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, let
S_{F_{n},\varphi}(x)=\sum\limits_{g\in F_{n}}\varphi(gx).

For any
\varepsilon>0
,
s\in\mathcal{S}
, we put

\displaystyle Q_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s):=

\displaystyle\inf\{\sum_{x\in J}\exp(s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(x)):\ J\ is\ an\ (F_{n},\varepsilon)\ spanning\ set\ of\ X\};

\displaystyle P_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s):=

\displaystyle\sup\{\sum_{x\in E}\exp(s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(x)):\ E\ is\ an\ (F_{n},\varepsilon)\ separated\ set\ of\ X\}.

Similarly, let

\displaystyle q_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s):=

\displaystyle\inf\{\sum_{A\in\alpha}\inf_{x\in A}\exp(s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(x)):\ \alpha\ \mbox{is\ a\ finite\ cover\ with\ mesh}(\alpha,d_{F_{n}})<\varepsilon\};

\displaystyle p_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s):=

\displaystyle\inf\{\sum_{A\in\alpha}\sup_{x\in A}\exp(s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(x)):\ \alpha\ \mbox{is\ a\ finite\ cover\ with\ mesh}(\alpha,d_{F_{n}})<\varepsilon\},

where mesh
(\alpha,d_{F_{n}}):=\max\limits_{A\in\alpha}\mbox{diam}_{d_{F_{n}}}(A)
.

Remark 2.1
.

(1)
0\leqslant Q_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s)\leqslant\|\exp(s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n},\varphi})\|spa_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(X,G)
,

0\leqslant P_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s)\leqslant\|\exp(s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n},\varphi})\|sep_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(X,G)
.

(2)
P_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(0,s)=sep_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(X,G)
,
Q_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(0,s)=spa_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(X,G)
.

(3)
Q_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s)\leqslant P_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s)\leqslant p_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s)
.

(4) If
d(x,y)<\varepsilon
implies
|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leqslant\delta
, then

p_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s)\leqslant e^{|F_{n}|\delta s(\varepsilon)}q_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s).

For
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
,
s\in\mathcal{S}
and
\varepsilon>0
, put

Q_{\varepsilon}(\varphi,s):=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log Q_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s);

P_{\varepsilon}(\varphi,s):=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log P_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s);

p_{\varepsilon}(\varphi,s):=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log p_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s);

q_{\varepsilon}(\varphi,s):=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log q_{\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s).

Let

Q(\varphi,s):=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{Q_{\varepsilon}(\varphi,s)}{s(\varepsilon)};

P(\varphi,s):=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{P_{\varepsilon}(\varphi,s)}{s(\varepsilon)};

p(\varphi,s):=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{p_{\varepsilon}(\varphi,s)}{s(\varepsilon)};

q(\varphi,s):=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{q_{\varepsilon}(\varphi,s)}{s(\varepsilon)}.

The following Proposition is a direct consequence of Remark
2.1
(3) and (4).

Proposition 2.1
.

For
s\in\mathcal{S}
, and
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
,

Q(\varphi,s)\leqslant P(\varphi,s)\leqslant p(\varphi,s)\leqslant q(\varphi,s).

Denote by
C(X,\mathbb{R}^{+})
the set of nonnegative continuous functions.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R}^{+})
,
s\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
,

Q(\varphi,s)=P(\varphi,s)=p(\varphi,s)=q(\varphi,s).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
and Remark 1.1 (4), we are left to show that
Q(\varphi,s)\geqslant q(\varphi,s)
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
and
(F_{n},\frac{\varepsilon}{2})
spanning set
J
, then the
d_{F_{n}}
balls centered at
J
of radius
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
cover
X
and these balls form an open cover
\alpha_{J}
with mesh
(\alpha,d_{F_{n}})<\varepsilon
.

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log\sum\limits_{y\in J}\exp(s(\frac{\varepsilon}{2})S_{F_{n},\varphi}(y))}{s(\frac{\varepsilon}{2})}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log\sum\limits_{A\in\alpha}\inf\limits_{x\in A}\exp(s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(x))}{s(\frac{\varepsilon}{2})}.

In the above inequality, we use the fact that
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
s\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
.
Therefore,
Q(\varphi,s)\geqslant q(\varphi,s)
.

∎

Definition 2.3
.

The map

SP(\cdot,\cdot):C(X,\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\},\varphi\mapsto SP(\varphi,s)=Q(\varphi,s)

is called
scale pressure of

G
with respect to
s
and
\varphi
.

For any invariant measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
,
\varepsilon>0
,
0<\delta<1
,
s\in\mathcal{S}
we put

\displaystyle P_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s):=

\displaystyle\inf\{\sum_{x\in E}\exp(s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(x)):\ E\mbox{ is\ an}\ (F_{n},\varepsilon)\mbox{\ spanning\ set\ of}\ D\subset X\ \mbox{with}\ \mu(D)\geqslant 1-\delta\};

Let

P_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(\varphi,s):=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log P_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon,F_{n}}(\varphi,s),

and

P_{\mu,\delta}(\varphi,s):=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{P_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(\varphi,s)}{s(\varepsilon)}.

For any finite measurable partition
\xi
of
(X,\mathscr{B})
and any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
, write

h_{\mu}(G,\xi):=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum\limits_{A\in\xi_{F_{n}}}\mu(A)\log\mu(A)
,
where
\xi_{F_{n}}=\bigvee\limits_{g\in F_{n}}g^{-1}\xi
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(X,G)
be a
G
-action topological dynamical system,
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
, we call

h_{\mu}(G):=\sup\{h_{\mu}(G,\xi):\ \xi\ \mbox{is\ a\ finite\ measurable\ partition\ of\ }(X,\mathscr{B})\}

the
measure-theoretic entropy
of
G
with respect to
\mu
.

For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
define
N_{\mu}(F_{n},\varepsilon,\delta)
as the minimal number of dynamical balls
B_{F_{n}}(x,\varepsilon)
needed to cover a set of measure strictly bigger than
1-\delta
. Then define
h_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(X,G)=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log N_{\mu}(F_{n},\varepsilon,\delta)
. It was proven by Y. Zhao in
[
14
]
that
h_{\mu}(G)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}h_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(X,G)
for any
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(G)
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and tempered Følner sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
G
with
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|F_{n}|}{\log n}=\infty
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a tempered Følner sequence in
G
with
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|F_{n}|}{\log n}=\infty
,
s\in\mathcal{S}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. For
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
,
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{G}(X)
,

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}P_{\mu,\delta}(\varphi,s)=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{h_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(X,G)}{s(\varepsilon)}+\int\varphi d\mu.

Proof.

The proof is further divided into two steps.

Step 1
First, we show that

(2.1)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}P_{\mu,\delta}(\varphi,s)\leqslant\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{h_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(X,G)}{s(\varepsilon)}+\int\varphi d\mu.

For any
\tau>0
, there is
0<\varepsilon<\tau
such that if
x,y\in X
,
d(x,y)<\varepsilon
, then
|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|<\tau.

Since
\mu
is ergodic, by Birkhoff ergodic theorem and Egorov theorem, there is a measurable set
B\in\mathscr{B}
with
\mu(B)>1-\frac{\delta}{2}
satisfies the following:
For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
N_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
N_{i+1}>N_{i}
and for any
n\geqslant N_{i}
,
x\in B
,
|\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(x)-\int s(\varepsilon)\varphi d\mu|<\frac{1}{i}.

We choose a sequence of positive integers
\{n_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying:

(1)
n_{i}\geqslant N_{i}
, for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
;

(2)
P_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(\varphi,s)=\lim\limits_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n_{i}}|}\log P_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon,F_{n_{i}}}(\varphi,s)
.

Let
C_{n_{i}}
be a set with
\mu(C_{n_{i}})>1-\frac{\delta}{2}
and

D_{n_{i}}
be an
(F_{n_{i}},\varepsilon)
spanning set of
C_{n_{i}}
with card
(D_{n_{i}})=N_{\mu}(F_{n_{i}},\varepsilon,\frac{\delta}{2})
.

Put
E_{n_{i}}=B\cap C_{n_{i}}
, then
\mu(E_{n_{i}})>1-\delta.
Let
J_{n_{i}}\subset D_{n_{i}}
be an
(F_{n_{i}},\varepsilon)
spanning set of
E_{n_{i}}
with smallest cardinality.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in J_{n_{i}}}\exp(s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n_{i}},\varphi}(x))

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in J_{n_{i}}}\exp[s(\varepsilon)|F_{n_{i}}|(\int\varphi d\mu+\frac{1}{i})]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle card(D_{n_{i}})\exp[s(\varepsilon)|F_{n_{i}}|(\int\varphi d\mu+\frac{1}{i})].

Therefore

P_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon,F_{n_{i}}}(\varphi,s)\leqslant card(D_{n_{i}})\exp[|F_{n_{i}}|(s(\varepsilon)\int\varphi d\mu+s(\varepsilon)\frac{1}{i})].

Hence,

P_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(\varphi,s)\leqslant h_{\mu,\frac{\delta}{2},\varepsilon}(X,G)+s(\varepsilon)\int\varphi d\mu.

Dividing by
s(\varepsilon)
and letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we have (
2.1
).

Step 2
Next, we show that

(2.2)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}P_{\mu,\delta}(\varphi,s)\geqslant\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{h_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(X,G)}{s(\varepsilon)}+\int\varphi d\mu.

By Birkhoff ergodic theorem and Egorov theorem, similarly to
Step 1
we can find a measurable set
B\in\mathscr{B}
with
\mu(B)>1-\delta
and a sequence of positive integers
\{n_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the following:

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
N_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in B
,
|\frac{1}{|F_{n_{i}}|}s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n_{i}},\varphi}(x)-\int s(\varepsilon)\varphi d\mu|<\frac{1}{i},
and

h_{\mu,2\delta,\varepsilon}(X,G)=\lim\limits_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n_{i}}|}\log N_{\mu,2\delta,\varepsilon,F_{n_{i}}}(X,G).

Let
C_{n_{i}}
be a set with
\mu(C_{n_{i}})>1-\delta
and

D_{n_{i}}
be an
(F_{n_{i}},\varepsilon)
spanning set of
C_{n_{i}}
. Put
E_{n_{i}}=B\cap C_{n_{i}}
, then
\mu(E_{n_{i}})>1-2\delta.
Let
J_{n_{i}}\subset D_{n_{i}}
be an
(F_{n_{i}},\varepsilon)
spanning set of
E_{n_{i}}
with smallest cardinality.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in D_{n_{i}}}\exp(s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n_{i}},\varphi}(x))

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in J_{n_{i}}}\exp(s(\varepsilon)S_{F_{n_{i}},\varphi}(x))

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in J_{n_{i}}}\exp[s(\varepsilon)|F_{n_{i}}|(\int\varphi d\mu-\frac{1}{i})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle card(J_{n_{i}})\exp[s(\varepsilon)|F_{n_{i}}|(\int\varphi d\mu-\frac{1}{i})]

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle N_{\mu}(F_{n_{i}},\varepsilon,2\delta)\exp[s(\varepsilon)|F_{n_{i}}|(\int\varphi d\mu-\frac{1}{i})].

Hence,

\displaystyle P_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(\varphi,s)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle h_{\mu,2\delta,\varepsilon}(X,G)+s(\varepsilon)\int\varphi d\mu.

Dividing by
s(\varepsilon)
and letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we have (
2.2
).

Definition 2.5
.

For any
0<\delta<1
,
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
,
s\in\mathcal{S}
, the map

SP_{\mu}(\cdot,\cdot):C(X,\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\},\varphi\mapsto SP_{\mu,\delta}(\varphi,s):=P_{\mu,\delta}(\varphi,s)

is called
measure-theoretic scale pressure of

G
with respect to
s
and
\varphi
.

One of our main results is the following variational principle, we need a condition.
For any finite measurable partition
\xi
and
r>0
, let
U_{r}(A):=\{x\in A:\exists y\in A^{c},\ \mbox{with}\ d(x,y)<r\}
and
U_{r}(\xi):=\bigcup\limits_{A\in\xi}U_{r}(A)
. Since
\bigcap\limits_{r>0}U_{r}(\xi)=\partial\xi
,
\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\mu(U_{r}(\xi))=\mu(\partial\xi)
, for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
, where
\partial\xi:=\bigcup\limits_{A\in\xi}\partial A
and
\partial A
is the boundary of
A
.

If a finite measurable partition
\xi
satisfies
\mu(\partial\xi)=0
for some
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{G}(X)
, then for any
\gamma>0
, we can find
0<r<\gamma
such that
\mu(U_{r}(\xi))<\gamma.
Let
r_{\mu,\gamma}:=\sup\{r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}:\exists\,\mbox{finite\,measurable\,partition}\,\xi\,\mbox{with}\,\mu(\partial\xi)=0,diam(\xi)<\gamma\,\mbox{and}\,\mu(U_{r}(\xi))<\gamma\}

and
r_{\gamma}:=\inf\limits_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{G}(X)}r_{\mu,\gamma}
.

Condition A
.

For any
\gamma>0
,
r_{\gamma}>0
and

\limsup\limits_{\gamma\rightarrow 0}\frac{s(r_{\gamma})}{s(\gamma)}=1.

Remark 2.2
.

If we choose
s\equiv 1
, then Condition A holds for the system such that for any
\gamma>0
,
r_{\gamma}>0
. If we take
s(x)=-\log x
, for example, a trivial example satisfying Condition A is one dimensional uniquely ergodic systems whose ergodic measure is Lebesgue measure.

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
s\in\mathcal{S}^{**}
,
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
and
0<\delta<1
, let

sp(\varphi,s,\delta):=\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\sup\limits_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)}P_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(\varphi,s)}{s(\varepsilon)}.

Theorem A
.

Let
(X,G)
be a
G
-action topological dynamical system satisfying Condition A.
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a tempered Følner sequence in
G
with
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|F_{n}|}{\log n}=\infty
. For any
S\in\mathcal{S}^{**}
,
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
and
0<\delta<1
,

SP(\varphi,s)=sp(\varphi,s,\delta).

Remark 2.3
.

The condition
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|F_{n}|}{\log n}=\infty
on the tempered Følner sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is only to guarantee the application of Shannon-McMillan-Breiman theorem (see for example
[
6
]
). Therefore, neither the quantity
P_{\mu,\delta,\varepsilon}(\varphi)
nor the result depends on the choice of Følner sequence.

To prove theorem A, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\gamma>0,
there exists
\mu_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
such that for all finite measurable partition
\xi
with diam
(\xi)<\gamma
and
\mu_{\gamma}(\partial\xi)=0
, we have
P_{\gamma}(\varphi,s)\leqslant h_{\mu_{\gamma}}(G,\xi)+S(\gamma)\int\varphi d\mu_{\gamma}
. Moreover,
\mu_{\gamma}
can be chosen to be ergodic.

Proof.

The proof follows the same line of
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
, with slight modification. For any
\gamma>0
, any tempered Følner sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{|F_{n}|}{\log n}=\infty
, let
E_{n}=\{x_{1},...,x_{sep_{\gamma,F_{n}}(X,G)}\}
be a maximal collection of
(F_{n},\gamma)
-separated points in
X
with

\log\sum\limits_{x\in E_{n}}\exp(s(\gamma)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(x))\geqslant\log P_{\gamma,F_{n}}(\varphi,s)-1.

Define

\sigma_{n}=\dfrac{\sum\limits_{y\in E_{n}}\exp(s(\gamma)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(y))\delta_{y}}{\sum\limits_{x\in E_{n}}\exp(s(\gamma)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(x))},

where
\delta_{x}
is the probability measure supported at
x
. Then define

\mu_{n}=\dfrac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{g\in{F_{n}}}\sigma_{n}\circ g^{-1}.

Consider a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

P_{\gamma}(\varphi,s)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{|F_{n_{k}}|}\log P_{\gamma,n_{k}}(\varphi,s)

and
\{\mu_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to some
\mu_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
.
Let
\xi
be a finite measurable partition with diam
(\xi)<\gamma
and
\mu(\partial\xi)=0
. Recall that
\xi_{F_{n}}=\bigvee\limits_{g\in{F_{n}}}g^{-1}\xi
.
Since each element of
\xi_{F_{n}}
contains at most one element of
E_{n}
, by the definition of
\sigma_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\sigma_{n}}(\xi_{F_{n}})+s(\gamma)\int S_{F_{n},\varphi}(x)d\sigma_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in E_{n}}\sigma_{n}(\{y\})[s(\gamma)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(y)-\log\sigma_{n}(\{y\})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\sum_{y\in E_{n}}\exp(s(\gamma)S_{F_{n},\varphi}(y)),

where

Generalized-Hukuhara-Gradient Efficient-Direction Method to Solve Optimization Problems with Interval-valued Functions and its Application in Least Squares Problems Journal: Information Sciences
Debdas Ghosh, Amit Kumar Debnath, Ram Surat Chauhan, Oscar Castillo

Abstract
This article proposes a general g ​ H gH -gradient efficient-direction method and a 𝒲 \mathcal{W} - g ​ H gH -gradient efficient method for the optimization problems with interval-valued functions . The convergence analysis and the step-wise algorithms of both the methods are presented. It is observed that the 𝒲 \mathcal{W} - g ​ H gH -gradient efficient method converges linearly for a strongly convex interval-valued objective function. To develop the proposed methods and to study their convergence, the idea of strong convexity and sequential criteria for g ​ H gH -continuity of interval-valued function are illustrated. In the sequel, a new definition of g ​ H gH -differentiability for interval-valued functions is also proposed. The new definition of g ​ H gH -differentiability is described with the help of a newly defined concept of linear interval-valued function. It is noticed that the proposed g ​ H gH -differentiability is superior to the existing ones. For a g ​ H gH -differentiable interval-valued function, the relation of convexity with the g ​ H gH -gradient of an interval-valued function and an optimality condition of an interval optimization problem are derived. For the derived optimality condition, a notion of efficient direction for interval-valued functions is introduced. The idea of efficient direction is used to develop the proposed gradient methods. As an application of the proposed methods, the least square problem for interval-valued data by 𝒲 \mathcal{W} - g ​ H gH -gradient efficient method is solved. The proposed method for least square problems is illustrated by a polynomial fitting and a logistic curve fitting.



1
Introduction

Each area of science, engineering, management, economics, and other practices, uses optimization techniques extensively
. Optimization techniques assist us to find the best under specified circumstances. The optimization problems with interval-valued functions (IVFs), known as interval optimization problems (IOPs), has become a significant research topic over the last two decades due to inherent imprecise and uncertain events in different real-world events.
In this paper, we attempt to derive a technique for IOPs to capture its solution set. The proposed method reduces to the steepest descent method for the optimization problems with real-valued functions.

1.1
Literature Survey

The ordering and subtraction of intervals has always been a issue in pursuit of an optimal solution for IOPs

[
25
]
. In order to deal with interval-valued data, Moore introduced interval arithmetic
[
45
,
46
]
. However, with the interval arithmetic in
[
45
,
46
]
, one cannot find the additive inverse of a nondegenerate interval (whose lower and upper limits are different), i.e., for a nondegenerate interval
A
, there does not exist an interval
B
such that
\textbf{A}\oplus\textbf{B}=\textbf{0}
. Due to this reason, Wu
[
58
,
59
,
60
]
used a new concept of difference of intervals, known as Hukuhara difference
[
31
]
for the difference of two nonempty, closed, bounded and convex subsets of a real linear space. In spite of the fact that the Hukuhara difference for intervals satisfies
\textbf{A}\ominus_{H}\textbf{A}=\textbf{0}
,
\textbf{A}\ominus_{H}\textbf{B}
can be calculated only when the width of
A
is greater than equal to that of
B
.
In order to overcome this inefficiency of Hukuhara difference of intervals, the ‘nonstandard subtraction’, introduced by Markov
[
43
]
, has been used and named as generalized Hukuhara difference (
gH
-difference) by Stefanini
[
50
,
52
]
. The generalized Hukuhara difference can be calculated for any pair of intervals and has the property that
\textbf{A}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{A}=\textbf{0}

[
50
]
.

In the ordering of intervals, as intervals are not linearly ordered in contrast to the real numbers, Ishibuchi and Tanaka
[
32
]
showed various partial ordering structures and solution concepts for IOPs. They suggested a method to solve a linear IOP by converting it to a bi-objective optimization problem, which is generalized by Chanas and Kuchuta
[
13
]
. For nonlinear IOPs, Ghosh studied a Newton method
[
22
]
and a quasi-Newton method
[
23
]
. Interestingly, many researchers proposed different
types
of algorithms to solve various types of practical IOPs, for instance, see
[
14
,
15
,
17
,
20
,
38
,
56
,
57
]
. Recently, Ghosh et al.
[
28
]
introduced variable ordering relations of intervals and proposed an algorithm to obtain the solutions to IOPs. However, research into the applicability of conventional optimization techniques for IOPs is still not concentrated. More surprisingly, although the interplay between geometry and calculus yield optimization techniques, the calculus for IVFs is not rigorously developed until now.

In the year of 2007, with the help of a Hausdorff metric between any two intervals, Wu
[
58
]
illustrated the concept of continuity of an IVF. In the same article
[
58
]
, based on the Hukuhara difference, the concept of Hukuhara-differentiability (
H
-differentiability) of an IVF has been proposed. Accordingly, the KKT optimality conditions for IOPs have been given in
[
58
]
. Further, applying the concept of
H
-differentiability, Wu
[
58
,
59
,
60
]
studied various duality theories of IOPs. Thereafter, showing the restrictiveness of

-differentiability, Chalco-Cano et al.
[
11
]
developed the calculus of IVFs based on the modified concept of the
gH
-difference, known as generalized-Hukuhara differentiability (
gH
-differentiability). Chalco-Cano et al.
[
10
]
and Ghosh et al.
[
26
]
also derived the KKT conditions and duality theories in the view of

-differentiability.

In the development of interval calculus, calculus for fuzzy-valued functions plays an important role because intervals are particular fuzzy numbers with a special membership function
. In connection with fuzzy calculus, Bede and Gal
[
5
]
introduced generalized (Hukuhara-based) differentiability; the paper motivated the search for a
gH
-difference for intervals and fuzzy numbers (see
[
50
,
51
,
52
]
) and applications to fuzzy generalized Hukuhara differentiability (see
[
6
]
). A recent contribution in this direction is the article by Stefanini and Arana-Jiménez
[
53
]
which contains definitions of total, directional and partial
gH
-derivatives for multi-variable interval- and fuzzy-valued functions.

In the existing literature on interval calculus, unlike the definition of differentiability of real-valued functions, none of the existing approaches used the concept of a
linear IVF
to define the differentiability of an IVF. Although similar to the definition of differentiability of real-valued functions, the authors of
[
22
]
and
[
53
]
introduced the new definitions of
gH
-differentiability for IVFs and studied the properties
gH
-differentiable IVFs. However, none of them also mentioned about the linear IVF and used the concept of linear IVF to define
gH
-differentiability for IVFs.

Since the last two decades, with the development of the calculus of IVFs and theories related to IOPs, many techniques, and their algorithmic implementations to obtain the efficient solutions of various types of practical IOPs have been appeared, for instance, see almost all the papers in the references.
However, the majority of the methods are provided from the perspective of conventional bi-objective optimization. Thus, to apply those techniques one has to explicitly express an IVF
F
in terms of its real-valued lower
\underline{f}
and upper
\overline{f}
boundary functions, which is quite restrictive. For example, in a general least square problem for interval-valued data (see Section

25

have studied a parametric form of IOPs and developed the theories and techniques to find efficient solutions to the IOPs with the objective functions that can be parametrically presented. However, for the parametric representation of an IVF one needs its explicit form which is often practically not possible, for instance, consider the function

1.2
Motivation and Contribution of the Paper

The literature on IOPs shows that there is still no emphasis on the study of conventional optimization strategies for IOPs
. Surprisingly, the basic descent method is not yet developed for IOPs. Further, to derive a technique for IOPs which is similar to the standard descent method, we need to rigorously establish the notion of
gH
-differentiability concept for IVFs. More importantly, it must be kept in mind that the derived technique must be applicable to general IVFs regardless of whether or not

(i)

the objective function can be expressed parametrically, or

(ii)

the explicit form of the lower and upper function of the objective function can be found.

After illustrating the concept of a linear IVF, this paper proposes a new definition of
gH
- differentiability
. It is shown that if an IVF is
gH
-differentiable at a point, its
gH
-gradient exists at that point. It is shown that the proposed definition of
gH
-differentiability is superior to the existing ones (see Remark
8
for details). With the help of
gH
-gradient, a few characterization results for a
gH
-differentiable convex IVF are derived. Also, several results related to the
gH
-gradient of a strong convex
gH
-differentiable IVF are studied.

Further, with the help of the proposed
gH
-differentiability for IVFs, this article develops a gradient descent method for interval optimization, namely a general
gH
-gradient efficient-direction method for IOPs
. Similar to the steepest descent method, a method is also proposed, named
\mathcal{W}
-gradient efficient method, to obtain efficient solutions of IOPs. The main advantages of the proposed methods are that one needs neither the explicit forms of upper and lower functions of the objective function nor parametric forms of the corresponding IVFs of an IOP. It is shown that the
\mathcal{W}
-gradient efficient method for IOP converges linearly in the case of strong convexity of the interval-valued objective function. In order to develop these methods, the notion of efficient-direction for an IVF and its several characteristics are studied.

Preliminaries and Terminologies

This section provides some basic terminologies and notions on intervals followed by the convexity and a few topics of differential calculus of IVFs.

2.1
Arithmetic of Intervals and their Dominance Relation

At first, this section describes the generalized concept of the difference of two intervals and the ordering concepts of intervals.
Along with these definitions, we use Moore’s interval addition (
\oplus
) multiplication (
\odot
) and division (
\oslash
)
[
45
,
46
]
throughout the paper.

Let the set of real numbers be denoted by
\mathbb{R}
and the set of all closed and bounded intervals be denoted by
I(\mathbb{R})
. Throughout the article, the elements of
I(\mathbb{R})
are represented by bold capital letters
{\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}},{\textbf{C}},\ldots
. To represent an element
\textbf{A}\in I(\mathbb{R})
in the interval form, the corresponding small letter is used in the following way:
\textbf{A}=[\underline{a},\overline{a}.
If
\underline{a}=\overline{a}]
, then
A
is called a
degenerate interval
.

It is to be mentioned that any singleton
\{p\}
of
\mathbb{R}
can be represented by an interval
\textbf{P}=[\underline{p},\;\overline{p}]
, where
\underline{p}=p=\overline{p}
. In particular,

\textbf{0}=\{0\}=[0,0]~~\text{and}~~\textbf{1}=\{1\}=[1,1].

Remark 1
.

It is easy to check that the addition and multiplication of intervals are commutative, the addition of intervals is associative, and

\textbf{A}\ominus\textbf{B}=\textbf{A}\oplus(-1)\odot\textbf{B}.

Since the property of subtraction of intervals cannot provide an additive inverse of a nondegenerate interval, in this article, we use the
gH
-difference of intervals, which is defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

(
gH
-difference of intervals

[
52
]
). Let

A
and
B
be two elements of
I(\mathbb{R})
. The
gH
-difference
between
A
and
B
, denoted
\textbf{A}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}
, is defined by an interval
C
such that

\textbf{A}=\textbf{B}\oplus\textbf{C}~\text{ or }~\textbf{B}=\textbf{A}\ominus\textbf{C}.

It is to be noted that for
\textbf{A}=\left[\underline{a},\overline{a}\right]
and

\textbf{B}=\left[\underline{b},\overline{b}\right]
,

\textbf{A}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}=\left[\min\{\underline{a}-\underline{b},\overline{a}-\overline{b}\},\max\{\underline{a}-\underline{b},\overline{a}-\overline{b}\}\right].

Thus,

\textbf{A}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{A}=\textbf{0}~~\text{and}~~\textbf{0}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{A}=(-1)\odot\textbf{A}.

Definition 2.2
.

(
Algebraic operations on
I(\mathbb{R})^{n}
). Let

\bar{\textbf{A}}=\left(\textbf{A}_{1},\textbf{A}_{2},\ldots,\textbf{A}_{n}\right)^{T}
and
\bar{\textbf{B}}=(\textbf{B}_{1},

\textbf{B}_{2},\ldots,\textbf{B}_{n})^{T}
be two elements of
I(\mathbb{R})^{n}
. An algebraic operation ‘
\star
’ between
\bar{\textbf{A}}
and
\bar{\textbf{B}}
, denoted
\bar{\textbf{A}}\star\bar{\textbf{B}}
, is defined by

\bar{\textbf{A}}\star\bar{\textbf{B}}=\left(\textbf{A}_{1}\star\textbf{B}_{1},\textbf{A}_{2}\star\textbf{B}_{2},\ldots,\textbf{A}_{n}\star\textbf{B}_{n}\right)^{T},

where
\star\in\{\oplus,\ \ominus,\ \ominus_{gH}\}
.

Definition 2.3
.

(
Dominance relation of interval

[
58
]
). For any two intervals
A
and
B
in
I(\mathbb{R})
,

(i)

if
\underline{a}~\leq~\underline{b}
and
\overline{a}~\leq~\overline{b}
, then
B
is said to be dominated by
A
and denoted by
\textbf{A}~\preceq~\textbf{B}
;

(ii)

if either
\underline{a}~\leq~\underline{b}
and
\overline{a}~<~\overline{b}
or
\underline{a}~<~\underline{b}
and
\overline{a}~\leq~\overline{b}
hold, then
B
is said to be strictly dominated by
A
and denoted by
\textbf{A}~\prec~\textbf{B}
;

(iii)

if
B
is not dominated by
A
, then
\textbf{A}~\npreceq~\textbf{B}
and if
B
is not strictly dominated by
A
, then
\textbf{A}~\nprec~\textbf{B}
;

(iv)

if
\textbf{A}~\npreceq~\textbf{B}
and
\textbf{B}~\npreceq~\textbf{A}
, then it will be said that none of
A
and
B
dominates the other, or
A
and
B
are not comparable.

One can note that Wu
[
58
,
60
]
used the term ‘superior than’ to describe the dominance relation between two intervals. However, in this article, we use the term ‘dominated by’ instead of ‘superior than’.

2.2
Convexity and Basic Differential Calculus of Interval-valued Functions

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. An IVF
\textbf{F}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow I(\mathbb{R})
, for each argument point
x\in\mathcal{X}
, is presented by the ontic (see
[
19
]
) way:

\textbf{F}(x)=\left[\underline{f}(x),\ \overline{f}(x)\right],

where
\underline{f}
and
\overline{f}
are real-valued functions on
\mathcal{X}
. The functions
\underline{f}
and
\overline{f}
are called the lower and the upper functions of
F
, respectively.

Definition 2.6
.

(
Convex IVF

[
58
]
). Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex set. An IVF

\textbf{F}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow I(\mathbb{R})

is said to be a convex function if for any two vectors
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
in

\mathcal{X}
,

\textbf{F}(\lambda_{1}x_{1}+\lambda_{2}x_{2})~\preceq~\lambda_{1}\odot\textbf{F}(x_{1})\oplus\lambda_{2}\odot\textbf{F}(x_{2})

for all
\lambda_{1},~\lambda_{2}\in[0,\ 1]
with
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1
.

Remark 2
.

(See
[
58
]
).

F
is convex if and only if
\underline{f}

and
\overline{f}
are convex.

Definition 2.7
.

(
Strongly convex IVF
). Let
\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. An IVF
\textbf{F}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow I(\mathbb{R})
is said to be strongly convex on
\mathcal{X}
if there exists a convex IVF
\textbf{G}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow I(\mathbb{R})
and a
\sigma>0
such that

\textbf{F}(x)=\textbf{G}(x)\oplus\frac{1}{2}\rVert x\rVert^{2}\odot[\sigma,\sigma]\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{X}.

Remark 3
.

It is to be observed that

\textbf{F}(x)=\textbf{G}(x)\oplus\frac{1}{2}\rVert x\rVert^{2}\odot[\sigma,\sigma]

implies

\displaystyle\left[\underline{g}(x),\overline{g}(x)\right]\oplus\frac{1}{2}\rVert x\rVert^{2}\odot[\sigma,\sigma]=\left[\underline{f}(x),\overline{f}(x)\right]

\displaystyle\text{or},

\displaystyle\left[\underline{g}(x)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\rVert x\rVert^{2},\overline{g}(x)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\rVert x\rVert^{2}\right]=\left[\underline{f}(x),\overline{f}(x)\right]

\displaystyle\text{or},

\displaystyle~\underline{g}(x)=\underline{f}(x)-\frac{\sigma}{2}\rVert x\rVert^{2}~\text{and}~\overline{g}(x)=\overline{f}(x)-\frac{\sigma}{2}\rVert x\rVert^{2}.

Therefore,

F

is strongly convex

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\textbf{G}~\text{is convex}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\underline{g}~\text{and}~\overline{g}~\text{are convex, by Remark \ref{rc1}}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\underline{f}~\text{and}~\overline{f}~\text{are strongly convex}.

Definition 2.8
.

(
gH
-continuity

[
22
]
).
Let
F
be an IVF on a nonempty subset
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\bar{x}
be an interior point of
\mathcal{X}

and
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be such that
\bar{x}+d\in\mathcal{X}
. The function

F
is said to be continuous at
\bar{x}
if

\lim_{\lVert d\rVert\rightarrow 0}\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+d)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right)=\textbf{0}.

Lemma 2.3
.

An IVF
F
on a nonempty subset
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
gH
-continuous if and only if

\underline{f}
and
\overline{f}
are continuous.

Proof.

See
B
.
∎

.

(

gH
-continuity

F

\mathcal{X}
of

is

-continuous at a point

See

Definition 2.9
.

(
gH
-Lipschitz continuous IVF

[
27
]

\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

(

Definition 2.11
.

(

It is to mention that here that Wu

Since an IOP is a special case of a fuzzy optimization problem

Theorem 4.5
.

(Optimality condition).

Let
F
be a
gH
-differentiable IVF on a nonempty subset
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
is an efficient solution of the IOP (
2
), then

0\in d^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\text{ for all}~d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
be an efficient solution of the IOP (
2
). Therefore, for all
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
so that
\bar{x}+\lambda d\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)~\nprec~\textbf{F}(\bar{x})

or,

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\nprec~\textbf{0},\text{ by Lemma \ref{ldr1}}

or,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\odot\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right)~\nprec~\textbf{0}

or,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\odot\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right)~\nprec~\textbf{0},\text{ since $\textbf{F}$ is $gH$-differentiable on $\mathcal{X}$}.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

d^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\nprec~\textbf{0}~\text{for all}~d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(3)

Replacing
d
by
-d
in (
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle(-d)^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\nprec~\textbf{0}

or,

\displaystyle(-1)\odot(d^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}))~\nprec~\textbf{0}.

Thus, by Lemma
2.2
,

\textbf{0}~\nprec~d^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}).

(4)

By (
3
) and (
4
), we get

0\in d^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\text{for all}~d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

∎

5

-gradient Efficient Methods for Interval Optimization Problem

This section develops
gH
-gradient efficient techniques to obtain the efficient solutions of the IOP
(
2

Definition 5.1
.

(
Efficient-direction
). Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A direction

is said to be an efficient-direction of an IVF
\textbf{F}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow I(\mathbb{R})
at
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
if there exists a
\delta~>~0
such that

(i)

\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\npreceq~\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)~\text{for all}~\lambda\in(0,\delta),

(ii)

there also exists a point
x^{\prime}=\bar{x}+\alpha d
with
\alpha\in(0,\delta)
and a positive real number

Remark 13
.

One may think that only the condition (
i
) of Definition
5.1
is sufficient to define an efficient-direction. However, it is not true in general. Because, for
A
,
B
and
\textbf{C}\in I(\mathbb{R})
,

\textbf{A}~\npreceq~\textbf{B}~~\text{and}~~\textbf{B}~\npreceq~\textbf{C}~\not\Longrightarrow~\textbf{A}~\npreceq~\textbf{C}~~\text{in general}.

For instance, consider

\textbf{A}=[4,6],~~\textbf{B}=[2,10]~~\text{and}~~\textbf{C}=[5,7].

We, then, see that

\textbf{A}~\npreceq~\textbf{B}~~\text{and}~~\textbf{B}~\npreceq~\textbf{C}~~\text{but}~~\textbf{A}~\prec~\textbf{C}.

That is why the condition (

) of Definition
5.1
is necessary to define an efficient direction.

Remark 14
.

For the degenerate case of the IVF
F
, i.e., for
\underline{f}(x)=\overline{f}(x)=f(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
, Definition
5.1
reduces to the following. A direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be an efficient-direction of
f
if there exists a
\delta~>~0
such that

f(\bar{x})~>~f(\bar{x}+\lambda d)~~\text{for all}~~\lambda\in(0,\delta).

Thus, an efficient direction for a degenerate IVF is a descent direction.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
F
be a
gH
-differentiable IVF on a nonempty subset
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, every direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that satisfies

\textbf{0}~\npreceq~d^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}),

(6)

is an efficient-direction of
F
at
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, where the corresponding efficient point
x^{\prime}
is provided by

x^{\prime}=\bar{x}+\alpha^{\prime}d~~\text{with}~~\alpha^{\prime}=\argeff_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}~\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\alpha d),

where by ‘
\argeff
’ of
\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\alpha d)
, we mean a point
\alpha^{\prime}
such that

\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\alpha d)~\nprec~\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\alpha^{\prime}d)~~\text{for all}~~\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Let
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a direction that satisfies the relation (
6
). Since
F
is
gH
-differentiable at
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, by Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
3.1
, we have

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\lambda}\odot\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right)=d^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}),

which implies

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\odot\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right)=d^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}).

Due to the relation (
6
), the last equation yields

\textbf{0}~\npreceq~\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\odot\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right).

(7)

Therefore,

\displaystyle[0,0]~\npreceq~\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\odot\Big[\min\left\{\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x}),\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right\},

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\max\left\{\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x}),\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right\}\Big]

or,

\displaystyle[0,0]~\npreceq~\bigg[\min\left\{\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x})\right),\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right)\right\},

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~\max\left\{\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x})\right),\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right)\right\}\bigg],

which implies

\min\left\{\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x})\right),\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right)\right\}~<~0.

(8)

Thus, we have following two cases.

\bullet

Case
1.

Let

\displaystyle\min\left\{\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x})\right),\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right)\right\}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x})\right).

Then, by the equation (
8
), we obtain

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x})\right)~<~0.

Therefore, there exists a
\delta_{1}~>~0
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,\delta_{1})
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x})\right)~<~0

or,

\displaystyle\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x})~<~0

or,

\displaystyle\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)~<~\underline{f}(\bar{x})

or,

\displaystyle\left[\underline{f}(\bar{x}),\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right]~\npreceq~\left[\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d),\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)\right].

\bullet

Case
2.

Let

\displaystyle\min\left\{\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x})\right),\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right)\right\}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right).

Then, by the equation (
8
), we get

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right)~<~0.

Thus, there exists a
\delta_{2}~>~0
such that for
\lambda\in(0,\delta_{2})
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right)~<~0

or,

\displaystyle\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x})~<~0

or,

\displaystyle\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)~<~\overline{f}(\bar{x})

or,

\displaystyle\left[\underline{f}(\bar{x}),\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right]~\npreceq~\left[\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d),\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)\right]

Choosing
\delta=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
, from Case
1
and Case
2
, we see that for all
\lambda\in(0,\delta)
,

\displaystyle\left[\underline{f}(\bar{x}),\overline{f}(\bar{x})\right]~\npreceq~\left[\underline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d),\overline{f}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)\right]

or,

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\npreceq~\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda d).

Hence,
d
satisfies the condition (
i
) of Definition
5.1
at
\bar{x}
.

Further, let us choose an
\alpha^{\prime}
such that

\alpha^{\prime}=\argeff_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}~\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\alpha d).

Therefore, there exists a
\bar{\delta}~>~0
such that for all
\alpha\in(\alpha^{\prime}-\bar{\delta},\alpha^{\prime}+\bar{\delta})
,

\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\alpha d)~\nprec~\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\alpha^{\prime}d).

Considering
\lambda=\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\alpha^{\prime}d+\lambda d)~\nprec~\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\alpha^{\prime}d)

or,

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(x^{\prime}+\lambda d)~\nprec~\textbf{F}(x^{\prime}),

where
x^{\prime}=\bar{x}+\alpha^{\prime}d
. Choosing
\delta^{\prime}=\min\{\alpha^{\prime},\bar{\delta}\}
, we have

\textbf{F}(x^{\prime}+\lambda d)~\nprec~\textbf{F}(x^{\prime})~~\text{for all}~~\lambda\in(-\delta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}).

Therefore,
d
satisfies the condition (
ii
) of Definition
5.1
at
\bar{x}
. Hence,
d
is an efficient-direction at
\bar{x}
.
∎

Remark 15
.

A question may arise here: in the definition of efficient-direction (Definition
5.1
), whether or not the condition (
i
) can be replaced by

\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda d)~\prec~\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\text{for all}~\lambda\in(0,\delta)\text{?}

(9)

To answer, we note that if we choose the relation (
9
) in place of the condition (

) of Definition

, then in the same way of proving Theorem
5.6
it can be proved that any

that satisfies

(10)

also holds the relation (
9
) and vice versa for a

-differentiable IVF

. However, the relation (
6
) is more general than the relation (

). Because there are some directions, along which there exists an efficient solution of an IOP, satisfy the relation (

) but do not satisfy the relation (

).

For instance, consider the IOP:

\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\textbf{F}(x)=[-1,2]\odot x_{1}^{2}\oplus[3,4]\odot x_{2}^{2},

(11)

where
\mathcal{X}=[-10,10]\times[-10,10]\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

In what follows, we show that
(\bar{x}_{1},0)^{T}\in\mathcal{X}
is an efficient solution of the IOP (
11
) for any
\bar{x}_{1}\in[-10,10]
. On contrary, let there exist two nonzero numbers
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
with
\left(\bar{x}_{1}+h_{1},h_{2}\right)^{T}\in\mathcal{X}
such that

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(\bar{x}_{1}+h_{1},h_{2})~\prec~\textbf{F}(\bar{x}_{1},0)

or,

\displaystyle[-1,2]\odot(\bar{x}_{1}+h_{1})^{2}\oplus[3,4]h_{2}^{2}~\prec~[-1,2]\odot\bar{x}_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle[-\bar{x}_{1}^{2}-2\bar{x}_{1}h_{1}-h_{1}^{2}+3h_{2}^{2},2x_{1}^{2}+4\bar{x}_{1}h+2h_{1}^{2}+4h_{2}^{2}]~\prec~[-\bar{x}_{1}^{2},2\bar{x}_{1}^{2}].

This implies

-2x_{1}h_{1}-h_{1}^{2}+3h_{2}^{2}~\leq~0~~\text{and}~~4x_{1}h_{1}+2h_{1}^{2}+4h_{2}^{2}~\leq~0.

Hence,
h_{2}^{2}~\leq~0
, which is not possible as
h_{2}~\neq~0
. So, there does not exist any
x\in\mathcal{X}
, which strictly dominates any
(\bar{x}_{1},0)^{T}\in\mathcal{X}
. Thus,
(\bar{x}_{1},0)^{T}

is an efficient solution of the IOP (

).

Now we choose a point
\hat{x}=(3,2)^{T}
and two directions
d^{\prime}=(1,-2)^{T}
and
d^{\prime\prime}=(5,-2)^{T}
. We also choose an
\alpha=1
. As

\textbf{F}(3,0)=[-1,2]~\prec~[5,10]=\textbf{F}(3,2)=\textbf{F}(\hat{x}),

\hat{x}
is not an efficient solution of the IOP (
11
). But the points
x^{\prime}=\hat{x}+\alpha d^{\prime}=(4,0)^{T}
and
x^{\prime\prime}=\hat{x}+\alpha d^{\prime\prime}=(8,0)^{T}
both are efficient solutions of the IOP (
11
). Therefore, both
d^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime\prime}
are efficient-directions of
F
at
\hat{x}
. Further, as the

-gradient of

is

\nabla\textbf{F}(x)=(D_{1}\textbf{F}(x),D_{2}\textbf{F}(x))^{T}=\left([-2,4]\odot x_{1},~[6,8]\odot x_{2}\right)^{T},

we have
\nabla\textbf{F}(\hat{x})=([-6,12],[12,16])^{T}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle d^{\prime}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\hat{x})=[-6,12]\oplus[-32,-24]=[-38,-12]

\displaystyle~\Longrightarrow~

\displaystyle d^{\prime}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\hat{x})~\prec~\textbf{0}~~\text{and also,}~~\textbf{0}~\npreceq~d^{\prime}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\hat{x}).

Again,

\displaystyle d^{\prime\prime}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\hat{x})=[-30,60]\oplus[-32,-24]=[-38,36]

5.1
General
gH
-gradient Efficient-Direction Method for Interval Optimization Problems

To produce the efficient solutions of the IOP (
2
) we provide Algorithm
1
. As Algorithm
1

(i)

uses
gH
-gradient at every iterative step and

(ii)

endeavors to find an efficient solution by sequentially moving along efficient-directions,

we name the method as general
gH
-gradient efficient-direction method. The term ‘general’ is due to the reason that we do not choose a special or a particular efficient-direction
d_{k}
; any general
d_{k}
that satisfies
\textbf{0}~\npreceq~d_{k}^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(x_{k})
will lead to reaching at an efficient point.

Algorithm 1

General
gH
-gradient efficient-direction method for IOP

1:

Given the initial point
x_{0}
and the IVF
\textbf{F}:\mathcal{X}(\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow I(\mathbb{R})
.

2:

Set
k=0
.

3:

If
0\in d^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(x_{k})
for all
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then Return
x_{k}
as an efficient-solution and Stop. Otherwise go to Step 3.

4:

Find a
d_{k}
such that
\textbf{0}~\npreceq~d_{k}^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(x_{k})
and an
\alpha_{k}
such that

\alpha_{k}=\argeff_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}~\textbf{F}(x_{k}+\alpha d_{k})

5:

Calculate

x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k}.

6:

Set
k\leftarrow k+1
and go to Step 2.

In the next, we give the convergence analysis of the Algorithm
1
. Towards the convergent analysis, we need the following notions of the algorithmic map and the closed map regarding IVFs.

Definition 5.2
.

(
Algorithmic map

[
4
]
). Let
\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. An algorithmic map
\mathcal{A}
of an algorithm is a point-to-set map on its domain
\mathcal{X}
which describes the iterating process of the algorithm such that if the sequence
\{x_{k}\}
is generated by the algorithm then
x_{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}(x_{k})
.

Remark 16
.

The map
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
that generates the sequence
\{x_{k}\}
in Algorithm
1
with
\textbf{F}(x_{k})~\npreceq~\textbf{F}(x_{k+1})
is an algorithmic map.

Definition 5.3
.

(
Closed map

[
4
]
). A point-to-set map
\mathcal{A}
from a nonempty subset
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to a subset
\mathcal{Y}
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is said to be closed at
x\in\mathcal{X}
if for any sequences
\{x_{k}\}
and
\{y_{k}\}
such that

x_{k}\to x~~\text{and}~~y_{k}\to y,~~\text{where}~~x_{k}\in\mathcal{X}~~\text{and}~~y_{k}\in\mathcal{A}(x_{k}),

we have
y\in\mathcal{A}(x)
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\textbf{F}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow I(\mathbb{R})
be an IVF on a nonempty open subset
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
D
be an element of
I(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Define a point-to-set map
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
\mathcal{L}(x,d)=\{y:y=x+\bar{\lambda}d\}
for some
\bar{\lambda}\in\textbf{D}
. Let
\textbf{F}(x+\lambda d)~\nprec~\textbf{F}(y)
for each
\lambda\in\textbf{D}
and
d~\neq~0
. If
F
is
gH
-continuous at
x
and
d~\neq~0
, then
\mathcal{L}
is closed at
(x,d)
.

Proof.

Let
\{(x_{k},d_{k})\}
be a sequence such that
(x_{k},d_{k})\to(x,d)
. Let
\{y_{k}\}
,
y_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(x_{k},d_{k})
, be a sequence such that
y_{k}\to y
. To prove the theorem, we have to show that
y\in\mathcal{L}(x,d)
.

It is to note that there exists
\lambda_{k}\in\textbf{D}
such that
y_{k}=x_{k}+\lambda_{k}d_{k}
for
k=1,\ 2,\ \ldots,\ n
. Since
d~\neq~0
, for large enough
k
we have
d_{k}~\neq~0
. Then,
\lambda_{k}=\frac{\lVert y_{k}-x_{k}\rVert}{\lVert d_{k}\rVert}
.

Taking the limit as
k\rightarrow\infty
we have
\lambda_{k}\to\frac{\lVert y-x\rVert}{\lVert d\rVert}=\bar{\lambda}
, say. Hence,
y=x+\bar{\lambda}d
. Furthermore, since
\lambda_{k}\in\textbf{D}
for each
k
and
D
is closed,
\bar{\lambda}\in\textbf{D}
.

Therefore, as
\lambda\in\textbf{D}
,
\textbf{F}(x_{k}+\lambda d_{k})~\nprec~\textbf{F}(y_{k})~~\text{for all }~k
, and
F
is
gH
-continuous, by Lemma
2.4
, evidently, we have
\textbf{F}(x+\lambda d)~\nprec~\textbf{F}(y)
. Hence,
y\in\mathcal{L}(x,d)
and so,
\mathcal{L}
is closed.

∎

Remark 17
.

As according to Theorem
5.7
, the map
\mathcal{L}
is closed, the composite map
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{D}
will be closed if the direction generating map
\mathcal{D}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, defined by
\mathcal{D}(x)=(x,d)
, is also closed.

Theorem 5.8
.

(Convergence of general
gH
-gradient efficient method for IOP).

Let
F
be a
gH
-differentiable IVF on a nonempty open subset
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be the set of all efficient points of
F
and
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
be an algorithmic map of Algorithm
1
. Suppose that the algorithm map
\mathcal{A}
produces the sequence
\{x_{k}\}
, which converges at
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
. Also, assume that there exists
M~>~0
such that
\lVert d_{k}\rVert~<~M
for all
k
. If
\bar{d}
is an accumulation point of
{d_{k}}
, then we have

0\in{\bar{d}}^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}).

(12)

Proof.

Since
\{d_{k}\}
is bounded, there exists an index set
K_{1}
such that
\lim\limits_{k\in K_{1}}d_{k}=\bar{d}
and
\lim\limits_{k\in K_{1}}x_{k}=\bar{x}
. We have the following two cases.

\bullet

Case
1.

If
\bar{d}=0
. Then, (
12
) is trivial.

\bullet

Case
2.

If
\bar{d}~\neq~0
. If possible, let us assume that the conclusion (
12
) is not true. Hence,

0\notin{\bar{d}}^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\Longrightarrow\bar{x}\notin\Omega.

Since, for
k\in K_{1},x_{k}\to\bar{x}
, also
x_{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}(x_{k})
and
x_{k+1}\to\bar{x}
, therefore,
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{A}(\bar{x})
. As
\mathcal{A}
is closed at
\bar{x}
due to Remark
17
. Thus,
\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\npreceq~\textbf{F}(\bar{x})
, which is a contradiction. Hence,

∎

5.2
\mathcal{W}
-gradient Method for Interval Optimization Problems

In this section, we develop a particular type of efficient-direction. Towards this, for two given numbers
w
,
w^{\prime}\in[0,1]
with
w+w^{\prime}=1
, we define a mapping
\mathcal{W}:I(\mathbb{R})^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\mathcal{W}(\textbf{A}_{1},\textbf{A}_{2},\ldots,\textbf{A}_{n})=(w\underline{a}_{1}+w^{\prime}\overline{a}_{1},w\underline{a}_{2}+w^{\prime}\overline{a}_{2},\ldots,w\underline{a}_{n}+w^{\prime}\overline{a}_{n})^{T}.

Remark 18
.

It is to observe that for any two elements
\bar{\textbf{A}}
,
\bar{\textbf{B}}
in
I(\mathbb{R})^{n}
,

\mathcal{W}(\bar{\textbf{A}}\oplus\bar{\textbf{B}})=\mathcal{W}(\bar{\textbf{A}})+\mathcal{W}(\bar{\textbf{B}}).

Lemma 5.1
.

For an interval
\textbf{A}=[\underline{a},\overline{a}]
,
(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})\odot[\underline{a},\overline{a}]~\nprec~\textbf{0}
, where
w
,
w^{\prime}\in[0,1]
with
w+w^{\prime}=1
.

Proof.

For an interval
\textbf{A}=[\underline{a},\overline{a}]
, we have the following three cases.

\bullet

Case
1.

If
\underline{a}~\geq~0
and
\overline{a}~\geq~0
. Then,
\underline{a}(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})~\geq~0
and
\overline{a}(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})~\geq~0
. Hence,

\textbf{0}\preceq(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})\odot[\underline{a},\overline{a}]~\Longrightarrow~(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})\odot[\underline{a},\overline{a}]~\nprec~\textbf{0}.

\bullet

Case
2.

If
\underline{a}~<~0
and
\overline{a}~\leq~0
. Then,
\underline{a}(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})~\geq~0
and
\overline{a}(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})~\geq~0
. Hence,

\textbf{0}\preceq(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})\odot[\underline{a},\overline{a}]~\Longrightarrow~(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})\odot[\underline{a},\overline{a}]~\nprec~\textbf{0}.

\bullet

Case
3.

If
\underline{a}~\leq~0
and
\overline{a}~>~0
. Then, either
(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})=0
or
(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})~\neq~0
.
If
(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})=0
, we have

(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})\odot[\underline{a},\overline{a}]=\textbf{0}~\Longrightarrow~(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})\odot[\underline{a},\overline{a}]~\nprec~\textbf{0}.

Further, if
(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})~\neq~0
, the terms
\underline{a}(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})
and
\overline{a}(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})
are alternative in sign. So,

(w\underline{a}+w^{\prime}\overline{a})\odot[\underline{a},\overline{a}]~\nprec~\textbf{0}.

∎

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty set and
\textbf{F}(x):\mathcal{X}\rightarrow I(\mathbb{R})
be
gH
-differentiable at a point
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
. Then, the direction
-\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}))
is an efficient-direction of
F
at
\bar{x}
, provided
0\not\in D_{i}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})
for at least one
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

Let
D_{i}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})=\textbf{A}_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and
0\not\in\textbf{A}_{i}
for at least one
i
. Thus,

(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})))^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}(w\underline{a}_{i}+w^{\prime}\overline{a}_{i})\odot[\underline{a}_{i},\overline{a}_{i}].

Let
0\not\in\textbf{A}_{i}
for
i=j
. Therefore,
\underline{a}_{j}
and
\overline{a}_{j}
both are either positive or negative. Thus,
(w\underline{a}_{j}+w^{\prime}\overline{a}_{j})~\neq~0
, and hence
(w\underline{a}_{j}+w^{\prime}\overline{a}_{j})\odot[\underline{a}_{j},\overline{a}_{j}]~\neq~\textbf{0}
. Therefore, according to the property of interval addition we get

(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})))^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\neq~\textbf{0}.

(13)

If possible, let
(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})))^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\prec~\textbf{0}
. Hence,
(w\underline{a}_{i}+w^{\prime}\overline{a}_{i})\odot[\underline{a}_{i},\overline{a}_{i}]~\prec~\textbf{0}
for at least one
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, which is not possible due to Lemma
5.1
. Therefore,

(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})))^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})~\nprec~\textbf{0}~\Longrightarrow~\textbf{0}\nprec-(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})))^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}).

(14)

By relations (
13
) and (
14
), we obtain

\textbf{0}\npreceq-(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x})))^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}).

Hence, by Theorem
5.6
,
-\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}))
is an efficient-direction of
F
at
\bar{x}
, where the corresponding efficient point
x^{\prime}
, given by

x^{\prime}=\bar{x}-\alpha^{\prime}\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}))~~\text{with}~~\alpha^{\prime}=\argeff_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}~\textbf{F}(\bar{x}-\alpha\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(\bar{x}))),

satisfies the condition (
i
) of Definition
5.1
at
\bar{x}
.
∎

2
. As Algorithm

\mathcal{W}
-

-gradient efficient-direction method. The letter ‘

Algorithm 2

\mathcal{W}
-
gH
-Gradient Method for IOP

1:

Give
w\in[0,1]
, the initial point
x_{0}
and the IVF
\textbf{F}:\mathcal{X}(\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow I(\mathbb{R})
.

2:

k=0
.

3:

If

4:

\alpha_{k}

Set

Lemma 5.2
.

For two elements
\bar{\textbf{A}}
and
\bar{\textbf{B}}
in
I(\mathbb{R})^{n}
and a vector
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\textbf{0}~\preceq~h^{T}\odot\left(\bar{\textbf{A}}\ominus_{gH}\bar{\textbf{B}}\right)~\Longrightarrow~\left(\mathcal{W}\left(\bar{\textbf{A}}\right)-\mathcal{W}\left(\bar{\textbf{B}}\right)\right)^{T}h~\geq~0.

Proof.

\displaystyle h^{T}\odot\left(\bar{\textbf{A}}\ominus_{gH}\bar{\textbf{B}}\right)

\displaystyle=h^{T}\odot\left(\left(\textbf{A}_{1},\textbf{A}_{2},\ldots,\textbf{A}_{n}\right)^{T}\ominus_{gH}\left(\textbf{B}_{1},\textbf{B}_{2},\ldots,\textbf{B}_{n}\right)^{T}\right)

\displaystyle=h^{T}\odot\left(\left(\textbf{A}_{1}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{1}\right),\left(\textbf{A}_{2}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{2}\right),\ldots,\left(\textbf{A}_{n}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{n}\right)\right)^{T}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}\odot\left(\textbf{A}_{i}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{i}\right).

According to Remark
1
, without loss of generality, let us assume

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}\odot\left(\textbf{A}_{i}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{i}\right)=~

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{k=1}^{p}h_{k}\odot\left(\textbf{A}_{k}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{k}\right)\oplus\bigoplus_{l=p+1}^{n}h_{l}\odot\left(\textbf{A}_{l}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{l}\right)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{k=1}^{p}\textbf{C}_{k}\oplus\bigoplus_{l=p+1}^{n}\textbf{C}_{l},

where
[\underline{c}_{k},\overline{c}_{k}]=h_{k}\odot\left(\textbf{A}_{k}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{k}\right)=\left[\left(\underline{a}_{k}-\underline{b}_{k}\right)h_{k},\left(\overline{a}_{k}-\overline{b}_{k}\right)h_{k}\right]
for all
k
’s and
[\underline{c}_{l},\overline{c}_{l}]=h_{l}\odot\left(\textbf{A}_{l}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{l}\right)=\left[\left(\overline{a}_{l}-\overline{b}_{l}\right)h_{l},\left(\underline{a}_{l}-\underline{b}_{l}\right)h_{l}\right]
for all
l
’s. Thus,

\displaystyle\textbf{0}~\preceq~h^{T}\odot\left(\bar{\textbf{A}}\ominus_{gH}\bar{\textbf{B}}\right)~\Longrightarrow~

\displaystyle\textbf{0}~\preceq~\bigoplus_{k=1}^{p}[\underline{c}_{k},\overline{c}_{k}]\oplus\bigoplus_{l=p+1}^{n}[\underline{c}_{l},\overline{c}_{l}]

\displaystyle~\Longrightarrow~

\displaystyle\textbf{0}~\preceq~\left[\sum_{k=1}^{p}\underline{c}_{k}+\sum_{l=p+1}^{n}\underline{c}_{l},\sum_{k=1}^{p}\overline{c}_{k}+\sum_{l=p+1}^{n}\overline{c}_{l}\right]

\displaystyle~\Longrightarrow~

\displaystyle 0~\leq~\sum_{k=1}^{p}\underline{c}_{k}+\sum_{l=p+1}^{n}\underline{c}_{l}~\text{and}~0~\leq~\sum_{k=1}^{p}\overline{c}_{k}+\sum_{l=p+1}^{n}\overline{c}_{l}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}\underline{c}_{k}+\sum_{l=p+1}^{n}\overline{c}_{l}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{p}\underline{c}_{k}+\sum_{l=p+1}^{n}\underline{c}_{l}\geq 0~\text{and}~\sum_{k=1}^{p}\overline{c}_{k}+\sum_{l=p+1}^{n}\underline{c}_{l}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{p}\underline{c}_{k}+\sum_{l=p+1}^{n}\underline{c}_{l}\geq 0

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}\left(\underline{a}_{k}-\underline{b}_{k}\right)h_{k}+\sum_{l=p+1}^{n}\left(\underline{a}_{l}-\underline{b}_{l}\right)h_{l}~\geq~0~\text{and}~\sum_{k=1}^{p}\left(\overline{a}_{k}-\overline{b}_{k}\right)h_{k}+\sum_{l=p+1}^{n}\left(\overline{a}_{l}-\overline{b}_{l}\right)h_{l}~\geq~0

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\underline{a}_{i}-\underline{b}_{i}\right)h_{i}~\geq~0~\text{and}~\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\overline{a}_{i}-\overline{b}_{i}\right)h_{i}~\geq~0

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle w\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\underline{a}_{i}-\underline{b}_{i}\right)h_{i}~\geq~0~\text{and}~w^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\overline{a}_{i}-\overline{b}_{i}\right)h_{i}~\geq~0

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}w\left(\underline{a}_{i}-\underline{b}_{i}\right)h_{i}+w^{\prime}\left(\overline{a}_{i}-\overline{b}_{i}\right)h_{i}~\geq~0

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{W}\left(\bar{\textbf{A}}\right)-\mathcal{W}\left(\bar{\textbf{B}}\right)\right)^{T}h~\geq~0.

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

If
F
is
gH
-differentiable IVF, then

{}\rVert\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))-\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(y))\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}~\leq~\rVert\nabla\textbf{F}(x)\ominus_{gH}\nabla\textbf{F}(y)\rVert_{I(\mathbb{R})^{n}}~~\text{for all}~~x,~y\in\mathcal{X}.

(16)

Proof.

Let

\nabla\textbf{F}(x)=\bar{\textbf{A}}=\left(\textbf{A}_{1},\textbf{A}_{2},\ldots,\textbf{A}_{n}\right)^{T}=\left([\underline{a}_{1},\overline{a}_{1}],[\underline{a}_{2},\overline{a}_{2}],\ldots,[\underline{a}_{n},\overline{a}_{n}]\right)^{T}

and

\nabla\textbf{F}(y)=\bar{\textbf{B}}=\left(\textbf{B}_{1},\textbf{B}_{2},\ldots,\textbf{B}_{n}\right)^{T}=\left([\underline{b}_{1},\overline{b}_{1}],[\underline{b}_{2},\overline{b}_{2}],\ldots,[\underline{b}_{n},\overline{b}_{n}]\right)^{T}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\rVert\nabla\textbf{F}(x)\ominus_{gH}\nabla\textbf{F}(y)\rVert_{I(\mathbb{R})^{n}}

\displaystyle~=~\rVert(\textbf{A}_{1}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{1},\textbf{A}_{2}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{2},\ldots,\textbf{A}_{n}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{n})\rVert_{I(\mathbb{R})^{n}}

\displaystyle~=~\rVert\textbf{A}_{1}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{1}\rVert_{I(\mathbb{R})}+\rVert\textbf{A}_{2}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{2}\rVert_{I(\mathbb{R})}+\cdots+\rVert\textbf{A}_{n}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{n}\rVert_{I(\mathbb{R})}.

We note that

\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))=(w\underline{a}_{1}+w^{\prime}\overline{a}_{1},w\underline{a}_{2}+w^{\prime}\overline{a}_{2},\ldots,w\underline{a}_{n}+w^{\prime}\overline{a}_{n})^{T}

and

\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(y))=(w\underline{b}_{1}+w^{\prime}\overline{b}_{1},w\underline{b}_{2}+w^{\prime}\overline{b}_{2},\ldots,w\underline{b}_{n}+w^{\prime}\overline{b}_{n})^{T}.

So,

\displaystyle\rVert\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))-\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(y))\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle~=~

\displaystyle\rVert(w(\underline{a}_{1}-\underline{b}_{1})+w^{\prime}(\overline{a}_{1}-\overline{b}_{1}),w(\underline{a}_{2}-\underline{b}_{2})+w^{\prime}(\overline{a}_{2}-\overline{b}_{2}),

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~\cdots,w(\underline{a}_{n}-\underline{b}_{n})+w^{\prime}(\overline{a}_{n}-\overline{b}_{n}))\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle~\leq~

\displaystyle\lvert w(\underline{a}_{1}-\underline{b}_{1})+w^{\prime}(\overline{a}_{1}-\overline{b}_{1})\rvert+\lvert w(\underline{a}_{2}-\underline{b}_{2})+w^{\prime}(\overline{a}_{2}-\overline{b}_{2})\rvert

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+\cdots+\lvert w(\underline{a}_{n}-\underline{b}_{n})+w^{\prime}(\overline{a}_{n}-\overline{b}_{n}\rvert

\displaystyle~\leq~

\displaystyle\max\{\lvert\underline{a}_{1}-\underline{b}_{1}\rvert,\lvert\overline{a}_{1}-\overline{b}_{1}\rvert\}+\max\{\lvert\underline{a}_{2}-\underline{b}_{2}\rvert,\lvert\overline{a}_{2}-\overline{b}_{2}\rvert\}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+\cdots+\max\{\lvert\underline{a}_{n}-\underline{b}_{n}\rvert,\lvert\overline{a}_{n}-\overline{b}_{n}\rvert\}

\displaystyle~=~

\displaystyle\rVert\textbf{A}_{1}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{1}\rVert_{I(\mathbb{R})}+\rVert\textbf{A}_{2}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{2}\rVert_{I(\mathbb{R})}+\cdots+\rVert\textbf{A}_{n}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}_{n}\rVert_{I(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle~=~

\displaystyle\rVert\nabla\textbf{F}(x)\ominus_{gH}\nabla\textbf{F}(y)\rVert_{I(\mathbb{R})^{\text{n}}}.

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
F
be
gH
-differentiable and a strongly convex IVF on a nonempty convex subset
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, there exists a
\sigma>0
such that

\left(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))-\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(y))\right)^{T}(x-y)~\geq~\sigma\rVert x-y\rVert^{2}~~\text{for all}~~x,~y\in\mathcal{X}.

Proof.

Since
F
is strong convex on
\mathcal{X}
, then there exists a convex IVF
G
on
\mathcal{X}
such that

\textbf{G}(x)\oplus\frac{1}{2}\rVert x\rVert^{2}\odot[\sigma,\sigma]=\textbf{F}(x)~~\text{for all}~~x\in\mathcal{X}.

where
\sigma~>~0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\nabla\textbf{G}(x)\oplus([\sigma x_{1},\sigma x_{1}],[\sigma x_{2},\sigma x_{2}],\ldots,[\sigma x_{n},\sigma x_{n}])^{T}=\nabla\textbf{F}(x)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow~

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{G}(x))+\sigma x=\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x)),~~\text{by Remark \ref{rw1}}.

This implies

{}\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{G}(x))=\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))-\sigma x.

(17)

Further, since
F
is
gH
-differentiable,
G
is
gH
-differentiable; as
G
is also convex IVF, by Lemma
5.2
and Theorem
3.4
, we have

\left(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{G}(x))-\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{G}(y))\right)^{T}(x-y)~\geq~0~~\text{for all}~~x,~y\in\mathcal{X}.

Then, from (
17
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))-\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(y))-\sigma(x-y)\right)^{T}(x-y)~\geq~0

\displaystyle~\Longrightarrow~

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))-\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(y))\right)^{T}(x-y)-\sigma\rVert x-y\rVert^{2}~\geq~0

\displaystyle~\Longrightarrow~

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))-\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(y))\right)^{T}(x-y)~\geq~\sigma\rVert x-y\rVert^{2}.

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

If a
gH
-differentiable IVF

on a nonempty subset

of

, we have

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Theorem 5.10
.

(Linear convergence under strong convexity)
.
Let
\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
F
is a strong convex and
gH
-differentiable IVF with
gH
-Lipschitz gradient on
\mathcal{X}
. Then, there exists a
\sigma>0
and an
L>0
such that if
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
be an efficient solution of the IOP (
2
), the mapping
H_{\alpha}(x)=x-\alpha\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))
with constant step size
\alpha\in\left[0,\frac{2\sigma}{L^{2}}\right]
satisfies

\rVert H_{\alpha}(x)-H_{\alpha}(\bar{x})\rVert~\leq~{\lVert x-\bar{x}\rVert}~\text{for all}~x\in\mathcal{X}.

Proof.

Let
F
be strong convex and
gH
-differentiable IVF with
gH
-Lipschitz gradient on
\mathcal{X}
. Therefore, for all
x,~y\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert H_{\alpha}(x)-H_{\alpha}(y)\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle~=~

\displaystyle\left\lVert\big(x-\alpha\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))\big)-\big(y-\alpha\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(y))\big)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle~=~

\displaystyle\lVert x-y\rVert^{2}-2\alpha(\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))-\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(y))^{T}(x-y)+\alpha^{2}\lVert\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(x))-\mathcal{W}(\nabla\textbf{F}(y))\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle~\leq~

\displaystyle{\lVert x-y\rVert}^{2}-2\alpha\sigma{\lVert x-y\rVert}^{2}+\alpha^{2}L^{2}{\lVert x-y\rVert}^{2}\text{ by Lemma \ref{lw4} and Lemma \ref{lw6}}

\displaystyle~\leq~

\displaystyle{\lVert x-y\rVert}^{2}\text{ since }\alpha\in\left[0,\frac{2\sigma}{L^{2}}\right].

Taking
y=\bar{x}
, we have

\rVert H_{\alpha}(x)-H_{\alpha}(\bar{x})\rVert~\leq~{\rVert x-\bar{x}\rVert}~~\text{for all}~~x\in\mathcal{X}.

Hence, the
\mathcal{W}
-
gH
-gradient efficient method converges linearly.
∎

Application

This section applies the
\mathcal{W}
-
gH
-gradient efficient method in solving the
least square problems
for interval-valued data.

Suppose a set of
n
pairs of data
(\textbf{X}_{k},\textbf{Y}_{k})
is given, where
\textbf{Y}_{k}\in I(\mathbb{R})
is the corresponding interval-valued output of
\textbf{X}_{k}\in I(\mathbb{R})^{p}
for all
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. We attempt to fit a function
\bm{\mathcal{H}}\left(\cdot;\beta\right):I(\mathbb{R})^{p}\to I(\mathbb{R})
, where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
is a parameter vector such that
\bm{\mathcal{H}}\left(\textbf{X}_{k};\hat{\beta}\right)
will be one of the best approximations of
\textbf{Y}_{k}
for all
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. By ‘one of the best approximations’ we mean that
\bm{\mathcal{H}}\left(\textbf{X};\beta\right)
gives a sum square error that is nondominated. Evidently, if
\hat{\beta}
is an efficient solution of the following IOP:

\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{l}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bm{\mathcal{E}}(\beta)}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}\left(\bm{\mathcal{H}}\left(\textbf{X}_{k};\beta\right)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{Y}_{k}\right)\odot\left(\bm{\mathcal{H}}\left(\textbf{X}_{k};\beta\right)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{Y}_{k}\right),

(20)

then
\bm{\mathcal{H}}\left(\textbf{X};\hat{\beta}\right)
can be considered as an efficient choice of the approximating function
\bm{\mathcal{H}}\left(\textbf{X};\beta\right)
.

It is noteworthy that the error function
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bm{\mathcal{E}}}
and the function
\bm{\mathcal{H}}\left(\textbf{X}_{k};\cdot\right)
are IVFs from
\mathbb{R}^{l}
to
I(\mathbb{R})
for all
\textbf{X}_{k}
. The partial
gH
-derivative of
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bm{\mathcal{E}}}
with respect to
\beta_{i}
is

D_{i}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bm{\mathcal{E}}(\beta)}=2\odot\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}\left(\bm{\mathcal{H}}\left(\textbf{X}_{k};\beta\right)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{Y}_{k}\right)\odot D_{i}\bm{\mathcal{H}}\left(\textbf{X}_{k};\beta\right)~\text{ for all }~i\in\{1,2,\ldots,l\}.

Hence, by applying the
\mathcal{W}
-
gH
-gradient efficient method on the IOP (
20
) one can easily obtain an efficient parameter vector
\hat{\beta}
for the function
\bm{\mathcal{H}}\left(\cdot;\beta\right)
. For examples, we consider the following two types of fitting with interval-valued data.

Conclusion and Future Directions

In this article, a general
gH
-gradient efficient-direction method and a
\mathcal{W}
-gradient efficient method for IOPs have been developed. The convergence analysis and the algorithmic implementations of both the methods have been presented. It has been shown that the
\mathcal{W}
-gradient efficient method converges linearly for a strongly convex interval-valued objective function. In the sequel, a few topics of calculus and convexity of IVFs have been proposed which were needed for the development of the methods. For a
gH
-differentiable IVFs, the relation between convexity and the gradient of a
gH
-differentiable IVF and an optimality condition of an IOP have been derived. Further, a notion of efficient-direction for IVFs has been introduced which is used to develop the general
gH
-gradient efficient and
\mathcal{W}
-gradient efficient methods. The proposed
\mathcal{W}
-gradient efficient method has been used to solve the least square problems with interval-valued data. The application has been exemplified by a polynomial fitting and a logistic curve fitting.

In connection with the proposed research, future research can evolve in several directions as follows.

1.

One may attempt to develop an appropriate approach to find the step lengths
\alpha_{k}
’s in Algorithms
1
and
2
. Towards this, one can also concentrate on developing exact or inexact line search techniques for IOPs.

2.

It is notable that in the definition of strongly convex IVFs (Definition
2.7
), we have taken a degenerate interval
[\sigma,\sigma]
for some
\sigma>0
. One can attempt to make a generalization of the used
[\sigma,\sigma]
to
[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]
.

3.

Analysis of the proposed method for more flexible IVFs, especially for nonconvex IVFs can be performed in the future.

4.

One may attempt to apply the proposed logistic regression on the classification problems with interval-valued data.

5.

In the future, a rigorous error analysis of the red and yellow portions of Figure
6
and Figure
7
can be performed.

6.

Future research can be made on applying the proposed methods in constrained least square problems with interval-valued data.

7.

Applications of least square technique in practical problems can be performed.

8.

Also in the future, one can try to develop the proposed methods of this article based on constrained interval analysis
[
40
]
.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
2.1

Proof.

(i)

\displaystyle\textbf{A}~\preceq~\textbf{B}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow[\underline{a},\overline{a}]~\preceq~[\underline{b},\overline{b}]

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\underline{a}~\leq~\underline{b}~\text{and}~\overline{a}~\leq~\overline{b}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\underline{a}-\underline{b}~\leq~0~\text{and}~\overline{a}-\overline{b}~\leq~0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left[\min\left\{\underline{a}-\underline{b},\overline{a}-\overline{b}\right\},~\max\left\{\underline{a}-\underline{b},\overline{a}-\overline{b}\right\}\right]~\preceq~\textbf{0}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\textbf{A}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}~\preceq~\textbf{0}.

(ii)

According to the Definition
3.2
,
\textbf{A}=\textbf{B}\Longleftrightarrow\textbf{A}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}=\textbf{0}
.

If
\textbf{A}~\neq~\textbf{B}
, then

\displaystyle\textbf{A}~\nprec~\textbf{B}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow[\underline{a},\overline{a}]~\nprec~[\underline{b},\overline{b}]

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\text{at least one of the inequalities}~~\underline{a}~>~\underline{b}~~\text{and}~~\overline{a}~>~\overline{b}~~\text{is true}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\max\left\{\underline{a}-\underline{b},\overline{a}-\overline{b}\right\}~>~0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\textbf{A}\ominus_{gH}\textbf{B}~\nprec~\textbf{0}.

∎

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
2.3

Proof.

Let
F
be
gH
-continuous at a point
\bar{x}
of the set
\mathcal{X}
. Thus, for any
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\bar{x}+d\in\mathcal{X}
,

\lim_{\lVert d\rVert\to 0}\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+d)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right)=\textbf{0},

which implies

\lim_{\lVert d\rVert\to 0}\left([\underline{f}(\bar{x}+d),\ \overline{f}(\bar{x}+d)]\ominus_{gH}[\underline{f}(\bar{x}),\ \overline{f}(\bar{x})]\right)=[0,\ 0].

Hence, by the definition of
gH
-difference we have

\lim_{\lVert d\rVert\to 0}(\underline{f}(\bar{x}+d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x}))\to 0\ \mbox{and}\ \lim_{\lVert d\rVert\to 0}(\overline{f}(\bar{x}+d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x}))\to 0,

i.e.,
\underline{f}
and
\overline{f}
are continuous at
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
.

Conversely, let the functions
\underline{f}
and
\overline{f}
be continuous at
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
. If possible, let
F
be not
gH
-continuous at
\bar{x}
. Then, as
\lVert d\rVert\to 0,\ (\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+d)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x}))\not\to\textbf{0}
. Therefore, as
\lVert d\rVert\to 0
at least one of the functions
(\underline{f}(\bar{x}+d)-\underline{f}(\bar{x}))
and
(\overline{f}(\bar{x}+d)-\overline{f}(\bar{x}))
does not tend to
0
. So it is clear that at least one of the functions
\underline{f}
and
\overline{f}
is not continuous at
\bar{x}
. This contradicts the assumption that the functions
\underline{f}
and
\overline{f}
both are continuous at
\bar{x}
. Hence,
F
is
gH
-continuous at
\bar{x}
.

∎

Appendix D
Proof for the problem in Remark
6

Proof.

Due to Definition
3.1
,

\textbf{F}(y)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{i})~\text{for all}~y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})^{T}\in\mathcal{X}.

Replacing
y
by
\lambda x
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}\in\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(\lambda x)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\lambda x_{i}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{i})=\lambda\odot\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{i})=\lambda\odot\textbf{F}(x)

Let us consider a pair of elements
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})^{T}
of
\mathcal{X}
. If the corresponding
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
are of same sign for all

, then

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(x)\oplus\textbf{F}(y)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{i})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{i})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+y_{i})\odot\textbf{F}(e_{i}),~~\text{by Remark \ref{ria1}}

\displaystyle=\textbf{F}(x+y).

Let a few of the
x_{i}
’s and the corresponding
y_{i}
’s are of different signs. Without loss of generality, let the first
p
number of
x_{i}
’s are of same signs with their corresponding
y_{i}
’s, thereafter consequtive
q-p
numbers of
x_{i}
’s are nonnegative but corresponding
y_{i}
’s are nonpositive, and the last
n-q
numbers of
x_{i}
’s are nonpositive but corresponding
y_{i}
’s are nonnegative, where
p~\leq~q~\leq~n
. Also, let

\textbf{F}(e_{i})=\textbf{A}_{i}=[\underline{a}_{i},\overline{a}_{i}],~\text{for all}~e_{i},~i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

Then, we have

\displaystyle~\textbf{F}(x)\oplus\textbf{F}(y)~=~\left(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{p}x_{k}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{k})\oplus\bigoplus_{l=p+1}^{q}x_{l}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{l})\oplus\bigoplus_{m=q+1}^{n}x_{m}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{m})\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{p}y_{k}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{k})\oplus\bigoplus_{l=p+1}^{q}y_{l}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{l})\oplus\bigoplus_{m=q+1}^{n}y_{m}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{m})\right)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{k=1}^{p}(x_{k}+y_{k})\odot\textbf{F}(e_{k})\oplus\bigoplus_{l=p+1}^{q}\left(x_{l}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{l})\oplus y_{l}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{l})\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\oplus\bigoplus_{m=q+1}^{n}\left(x_{m}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{m})\oplus y_{m}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{m})\right)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\textbf{B}\oplus\bigoplus_{l=p+1}^{q}\left(x_{l}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{l})\ominus v_{l}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{l})\right)\oplus\bigoplus_{m=q+1}^{n}\left(y_{m}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{m})\ominus u_{m}\odot\textbf{F}(e_{m})\right),

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{where}~\textbf{B}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{p}(x_{k}+y_{k})\odot\textbf{F}(e_{k}),~v_{l}=-y_{l}~\text{and}~u_{m}=-x_{m}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{for all}~l=p+1,~p+2,~\ldots,~q~\text{and}~m=q+1,~q+2,~\ldots,~r

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\textbf{B}\oplus\Bigg[\sum_{l=p+1}^{q}(\underline{a}_{l}x_{l}-\overline{a}_{l}v_{l})+\sum_{m=q+1}^{n}(\underline{a}_{m}y_{m}-\overline{a}_{m}u_{m}),

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\sum_{l=p+1}^{q}(\overline{a}_{l}x_{l}-\underline{a}_{l}v_{l})+\sum_{m=q+1}^{n}(\overline{a}_{m}y_{m}-\underline{a}_{m}u_{m})\Bigg]

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\textbf{B}\oplus\textbf{C},~~~\text{where}~\underline{c}=\sum_{l=p+1}^{q}(\underline{a}_{l}x_{l}-\overline{a}_{l}v_{l})+\sum_{m=q+1}^{n}(\underline{a}_{m}y_{m}-\overline{a}_{m}u_{m})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{and}~\overline{c}=\sum_{l=p+1}^{q}(\overline{a}_{l}x_{l}-\underline{a}_{l}v_{l})+\sum_{m=q+1}^{n}(\overline{a}_{m}y_{m}-\underline{a}_{m}u_{m}).

Further, we note that

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(x+y)=~

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{k=1}^{p}(x_{k}+y_{k})\odot\textbf{F}(e_{k})\oplus\bigoplus_{l=p+1}^{q}\left(x_{l}+y_{l}\right)\odot\textbf{F}(e_{l})\oplus\bigoplus_{m=q+1}^{n}\left(x_{m}+y_{m}\right)\odot\textbf{F}(e_{m})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\textbf{B}\oplus\bigoplus_{l=p+1}^{q}\left(x_{l}-v_{l}\right)\odot\textbf{F}(e_{l})\oplus\bigoplus_{m=q+1}^{n}\left(y_{m}-u_{m}\right)\odot\textbf{F}(e_{m})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\textbf{B}\oplus\bigoplus_{l=p+1}^{q}\left(x_{l}-v_{l}\right)\odot\left[\underline{a}_{l},\overline{a}_{l}\right]\oplus\bigoplus_{m=q+1}^{n}\left(y_{m}-u_{m}\right)\odot\left[\underline{a}_{m},\overline{a}_{m}\right]

Again, without loss of generality, we let that among
q-p
numbers of
x_{l}-v_{l}
the first
r-p
elements are nonnegative and rest are nonpositive, where
p~\leq~r~\leq~q
. Similarly, also we let that among
n-q
numbers of
y_{m}-u_{m}
the first
s-q
elements are nonnegative and rest are nonpositive, where
q~\leq~s~\leq~n
. Then, we have

\textbf{F}(x+y)=\textbf{B}\oplus\textbf{D},

where

\underline{d}=\sum_{l=p+1}^{r}\underline{a}_{l}\left(x_{l}-v_{l}\right)+\sum_{l=r+1}^{q}\overline{a}_{l}\left(x_{l}-v_{l}\right)+\sum_{m=q+1}^{s}\underline{a}_{l}\left(y_{m}-u_{m}\right)+\sum_{m=s+1}^{n}\overline{a}_{l}\left(y_{m}-u_{m}\right)

and

\overline{d}=\sum_{l=p+1}^{r}\overline{a}_{l}\left(x_{l}-v_{l}\right)+\sum_{l=r+1}^{q}\underline{a}_{l}\left(x_{l}-v_{l}\right)+\sum_{m=q+1}^{s}\underline{a}_{l}\left(y_{m}-u_{m}\right)+\sum_{m=s+1}^{n}\overline{a}_{l}\left(y_{m}-u_{m}\right).

Since all
x_{l}
,
v_{l}
,
y_{m}
,
u_{m}
are positive for all
p+1~\leq~l~\leq~q
and
q+1~\leq~m~\leq~n
, we obtain

\displaystyle\underline{a}_{l}x_{l}-\overline{a}_{l}x_{l}~\leq~\underline{a}_{l}(x_{l}-v_{l}),~~\underline{a}_{l}x_{l}-\overline{a}_{l}x_{l}~\leq~\overline{a}_{l}(x_{l}-v_{l}),

\displaystyle\underline{a}_{m}y_{m}-\overline{a}_{m}u_{m}~\leq~\underline{a}_{m}(y_{m}-u_{m}),~~\underline{a}_{m}y_{m}-\overline{a}_{m}u_{m}~\leq~\overline{a}_{m}(y_{m}-u_{m}),

\displaystyle\overline{a}_{l}x_{l}-\underline{a}_{l}x_{l}~\geq~\overline{a}_{l}(x_{l}-v_{l}),~~\overline{a}_{l}x_{l}-\underline{a}_{l}x_{l}~\geq~\underline{a}_{l}(x_{l}-v_{l}),

\displaystyle\overline{a}_{m}y_{m}-\underline{a}_{m}u_{m}~\geq~\overline{a}_{m}(y_{m}-u_{m}),~~\text{and}~~\overline{a}_{m}y_{m}-\underline{a}_{m}u_{m}~\geq~\underline{a}_{m}(y_{m}-u_{m}).

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\underline{c}~\leq~\underline{d}~~\text{and}~~\overline{c}~\geq~\overline{d},

\displaystyle\underline{b}+\underline{c}~\leq~\underline{b}+\underline{d}~~\text{and}~~\overline{b}+\overline{c}~\geq~\overline{b}+\overline{d}.

Therefore, either
\textbf{F}(x)\oplus\textbf{F}(y)=\textbf{B}\oplus\textbf{C}
and
\textbf{F}(x+y)=\textbf{B}\oplus\textbf{D}
are equal or none of them dominates the other for all
x
and
y
in
\mathcal{X}
.
∎

Appendix E
Proof of Lemma
3.1

Proof.

Let
F
be
gH
-differentiable at
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
. By Definition
3.2
, there exists a
\delta~>~0
such that

\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+d)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{L}_{\bar{x}}(d)=\lVert d\rVert\odot\textbf{E}(\textbf{F}(\bar{x});d)~~\text{for all}~~\lVert d\rVert~<~\delta,

(21)

where
\textbf{E}(\textbf{F}(\bar{x});d)\rightarrow\textbf{0}
as
\lVert d\rVert\rightarrow 0
.

Considering
d=\lambda h
for
\lambda~\neq~0
and
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\lambda|\lVert h\rVert~<~\delta
, from the equation (
21
), we obtain

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\odot\left[\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda h)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{L}_{\bar{x}}(\lambda h)\right]=\tfrac{|\lambda|\lVert d\rVert}{\lambda}\odot\textbf{E}(\textbf{F}(\bar{x});h)

or,

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\odot\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda h)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right)\ominus_{gH}\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\odot\textbf{L}_{\bar{x}}(\lambda h)=\tfrac{|\lambda|\lVert d\rVert}{\lambda}\odot\textbf{E}(\textbf{F}(\bar{x});h)

or,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\odot\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda h)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{L}_{\bar{x}}(h)=\textbf{0},~\text{since}~\textbf{L}_{\bar{x}}(\lambda h)=\lambda\odot\textbf{L}_{\bar{x}}(h).

Hence,

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\odot\left(\textbf{F}(\bar{x}+\lambda h)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(\bar{x})\right)=\textbf{L}_{\bar{x}}(h).

(22)

∎

. Let for any

,

\displaystyle\textbf{0}.

, the linear IVF

.

Appendix H
Proof of Theorem
3.3

Proof.

Let the function
F
be convex on
\mathcal{X}
. Then, for any
x,~y\in\mathcal{X}
and
\lambda\in(0,1]
, we get

\textbf{F}(x+\lambda(y-x))=\textbf{F}(\lambda y+\lambda^{\prime}x)~\preceq~\lambda\odot\textbf{F}(y)\oplus\lambda^{\prime}\odot\textbf{F}(x),~~\text{where}~~\lambda^{\prime}=1-\lambda.

Hence,

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(x+\lambda(y-x))\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(x)\preceq

\displaystyle(\lambda\odot\textbf{F}(y)\oplus\lambda^{\prime}\odot\textbf{F}(x))\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[\lambda\underline{f}(y)+\lambda^{\prime}\underline{f}(x),\lambda\overline{f}(y)+\lambda^{\prime}\overline{f}(x)]\ominus_{gH}[\underline{f}(x),\overline{f}(x)\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[\min\{\lambda\underline{f}(y)+\lambda^{\prime}\underline{f}(x)-\underline{f}(x),\lambda\overline{f}(y)+\lambda^{\prime}\overline{f}(x)-\overline{f}(x)\},

\displaystyle~~\max\{\lambda\underline{f}(y)+\lambda^{\prime}\underline{f}(x)-\underline{f}(x),\lambda\overline{f}(y)+\lambda^{\prime}\overline{f}(x)-\overline{f}(x)\}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[\min\{\lambda\underline{f}(y)-\lambda\underline{f}(x),\lambda\overline{f}(y)-\lambda\overline{f}(x)\},

\displaystyle~~\max\{\lambda\underline{f}(y)-\lambda\underline{f}(x),\lambda\overline{f}(y)-\lambda\overline{f}(x)\}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\odot\big[\min\{\underline{f}(y)-\underline{f}(x),\overline{f}(y)-\overline{f}(x)\},

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~\max\{\underline{f}(y)-\underline{f}(x),\overline{f}(y)-\overline{f}(x)\}\big],~~\text{since}~~\lambda~>~0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\odot(\textbf{F}(y)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(x)),

which implies

\frac{1}{\lambda}\odot(\textbf{F}(x+\lambda(y-x))\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(x))~\preceq~\textbf{F}(y)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(x).

Since
F
is
gH
-differentiable at
x\in\mathcal{X}
, taking
\lambda\to 0+
, by Theorem
3.1
, we have

(y-x)^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(x)~\preceq~\textbf{F}(y)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{F}(x)~~\text{for all}~x,~y\in\mathcal{X}.

∎

Appendix I
Proof of Theorem
3.4

Proof.

F
be convex on

. As

-differentiable on

, by Theorem

, for all

,

(25)

,

, let us suppose

) and (

\displaystyle\textbf{0}\preceq[\min\{\underline{b}-\underline{c},\overline{b}-\overline{c}\},\max\{\underline{b}-\underline{c},\overline{b}-\overline{c}\}]

\textbf{0}\preceq(x-y)^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(x)\ominus_{gH}(x-y)^{T}\odot\nabla\textbf{F}(y)~\text{ for all }x,~y\in\mathcal{X}.

Coexistence of Measures with Simple Lyapunov Spectrum for Fiber Bunched Cocycles
Jonathan DeWitt and Daniel Mitsutani

Abstract
. We prove that if a Hölder continuous fiber-bunched cocycle A ^ \hat{A} over an invertible hyperbolic transitive shift Σ ^ \hat{\Sigma} satisfies an appropriate strong irreducibility condition on Grassmannians, then Σ ^ \hat{\Sigma} admits an ergodic measure μ ^ \hat{\mu} with full support and product structure with simple Lyapunov spectrum if and only if any other ergodic measure with full support and product structure also has simple Lyapunov spectrum.



1.
Introduction

Lyapunov exponents describe the exponential growth or decay of norms of products of matrices which in many settings are used to determine different ergodic and dynamical properties of the system. The question of existence of a non-zero exponent has been widely studied with such applications in mind. A further question is whether all Lyapunov exponents have multiplicity 1, referred to as simple Lyapunov spectrum, which provides more detailed information on ergodic averages, and implies in many settings the existence of one dimensional invariant foliations for the system.

The study of criteria for non-zero exponents and simple spectrum for iid random products of matrices traces back to works of Furstenberg
[
Fur63
]
, Guivarch and Raugi
[
GR86
]
, and Goldsheid and Margulis
[
GM89
]
, among many others. In all of these works, a common feature is an irreducibility hypothesis on the set of matrices generating the random products which ensures that cancellations of the expanding and contracting directions of the matrices do not occur almost surely. Under such irreducibility hypotheses, their theorems give existence of a non-zero Lyapunov exponent
[
Fur63
]
or simple spectrum
[
GR86
]
, provided that the matrices generating the products satisfy necessary contraction and expansion properties.

Motivated by applications for partially hyperbolic systems and holomorphic foliations, these criteria were later extended (
[
BGMV03
]
,
[
BV04
]
) and refined (
[
AV07a
]
,
[
AV07b
]
) to apply to the setting of fiber-bunched matrix cocycles, where the product of matrices is formed as a skew product over a driving hyperbolic dynamical system, giving non-trivial and simple Lyapunov spectrum with respect to any measure satisfying a natural assumption known as local product structure. Fiber-bunching is a partial hyperbolicity-type condition which ensures the existence of holonomies for the cocycle, which in turn allows for many of the random features from the iid setting to apply in this setting.

In these works, the criteria found for non-trivial (i.e. not all zero) or simple (i.e. all distinct) Lyapunov exponents is a concrete setup, expressed as a transversality condition, which provides
sufficient
criterion for the existence of non-zero exponents
[
BGMV03
]
and simple spectrum
[
BV04
]
, respectively. These criteria in turn have been used to prove simplicity of spectrum generically for: fiber-bunched cocycles over hyperbolic systems
[
AV07a
]
, certain cocycles over partially hyperbolic systems
[
PV19
]
, and for fiber-bunched Anosov flows and geodesic flows in pinched negative curvature
[
Mit22
]
. It has also been applied to specific situations such as in the solution of the Kontsevich-Zorich conjecture in Teichmuller dynamics
[
AV07b
]
.

All criteria found so far for simple spectrum with respect to all reasonable measures in the fiber-bunched setting so far have only been sufficient criteria, in the form of existence a particular setup over a periodic point. The goal of this paper is to provide a criterion for fiber-bunched cocycles over hyperbolic systems to satisfy simple Lyapunov spectrum with respect to a natural class of measures under an irreducibility hypothesis in the spirit of Guivarch and Raugi
[
GR86
]
. The advantage of this approach is that an irreducibility hypothesis is evidently a
necessary
condition to guarantee that all measures have simple Lyapunov spectrum for a non-trivial reason.

Our main result shows that an irreducible fiber-bunched cocycle with one reasonable measure with simple Lyapunov spectrum in fact must have simple Lyapunov spectrum with respect to all reasonable measures.

Theorem A
.

Let
\hat{\Sigma}
be a transitive invertible subshift of finite type and
\hat{A}\colon\hat{\Sigma}\to\operatorname{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
a fully irreducible Hölder continuous fiber bunched cocycle.
Suppose there exists an ergodic, fully supported, invariant measure
\hat{\mu}
with continuous local product structure on
\hat{\Sigma}
with simple Lyapunov spectrum. Then any other ergodic, fully supported, invariant measure
\hat{\eta}
with continuous local product structure on
\hat{\Sigma}
also has simple Lyapunov spectrum.

The complete definitions in the theorem above are provided in Section
2
. The condition
full irreducibility
is defined in Section
3
. For fiber-bunched cocycles, it is the analogue on all Grassmannians
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
,
1\leq k\leq d
, of strong irreducibility for iid random products of matrices. We remark that full irreducibility is a strictly weaker hypothesis than the transversality condition required in
[
BGMV03
]
and other works, which is often referred to as twisting.

We will actually prove a finer result than Theorem
A
. Theorem
A
is proved by individually showing that each Lyapunov exponent must have multiplicity 1. In fact, its proof is immediate from:

Theorem B
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d-1
,
\hat{\Sigma}
a transitive subshift of finite type and
\hat{A}\colon\hat{\Sigma}\to\operatorname{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
a Hölder continuous fiber bunched cocycle strongly irreducible on
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
and on
\operatorname{Gr}(d-k,d)
.

Suppose there exists an ergodic, fully supported, invariant measure
\hat{\mu}
on
\hat{\Sigma}
with continuous local product structure and
\lambda_{k}(\hat{\mu})>\lambda_{k+1}(\hat{\mu})
. Then any other ergodic, fully supported, invariant measure
\hat{\eta}
with continuous local product structure on
\hat{\Sigma}
also has
\lambda_{k}(\hat{\eta})>\lambda_{k+1}(\hat{\eta})
.

The precise definition of
strong irreducibility on

\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
is given in Section
3
. Full irreducibility is defined as strong irreducibility on
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
for each
1\leq k\leq d-1
so it is clear that Theorem
A
follows from Theorem
B
.

Remark 1.1
.

Although requiring a condition on
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
and
\operatorname{Gr}(d-k,d)
might seem unexpected, one may compare with
[
BV04
, Thm. 1]
, which under a certain twisting condition establishes simultaneously that both the top and bottom Lyapunov exponent are one dimensional.

In
[
BGMV03
, Thm. 1]
, Bonatti, Gomez-Mont, and Viana prove an alternative for fiber-bunched cocycles: either every equilibrium state has at least two distinct Lyapunov exponents or there exists a holonomy invariant, cocycle invariant measure on its projective extension. This, in turn, should imply either a failure of irreducibility or conformality of the cocycle (which implies that all exponents are
0
for all invariant measures). For the sake of completeness, we include this statement in the following theorem:

Theorem C
.

Suppose that
\hat{\Sigma}
is a transitive hyperbolic system and that
\hat{A}\colon\Sigma\to\operatorname{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
is a fiber-bunched Hölder continuous cocycle that is strongly irreducible on
\operatorname{Gr}(1,d)
. Then exactly one of the following holds:

(1)

For every measure
\hat{\mu}
with full support and continuous product structure,
\hat{\mu}
has two distinct Lyapunov exponents, or;

(2)

\hat{A}
preserves a Hölder continuous conformal structure, and thus every invariant measure has all Lyapunov exponents equal to
0
.

In view of this theorem, Theorem
B
above is a version of this result which extends to the study of every Lyapunov exponent, and thus to the study of simplicity of Lyapunov spectrum.

1.1.
Prior Results.

To better frame the results of this paper, we now discuss in some detail some of the earlier results mentioned above.
We begin with a review of the case of iid matrix products. Throughout, we let
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{m})\subseteq\operatorname{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
,
d\geq 2
and let
G
denote the semigroup generated by the matrices
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
.

The foundational result on the existence of non-zero Lyapunov exponents is the theorem of Furstenberg
[
Fur63
]
: if
G
is not compact and
G
acts strongly irreducibly on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, that is,
G
does not preserve a non-trivial finite union of subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then almost surely the iid uniformly distributed random products of the matrices in
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
have two distinct Lyapunov exponents. Guivarch and Raugi
[
GR86
]
improve on this theorem to obtain a criterion for simplicity of the top Lyapunov exponent. They prove that if
G
acts strongly irreducibly on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and there exists a sequence of elements
g_{n}\in G
such that
\|g_{n}\|^{-1}g_{n}
converges to a rank
1
matrix, then
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}
. This implies a related result for
\lambda_{k}>\lambda_{k+1}
by considering the associated action on
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Finally, Goldsheid and Margulis
[
GM89
]
improve this theorem to only have a hypothesis on the algebraic closure
X
of
G
in
\operatorname{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
. All of the theorems above can be seen as presenting an alternative under an irreducibility hypothesis.

In
[
AV07b
]
, the Lyapunov exponents of a locally constant cocycle over a shift equipped with a measure having a type of product structure is considered. They show that this cocycle has simple Lyapunov spectrum if it is pinching and twisting. Here, pinching and twisting reduce to an irreducibility condition on
k
-planes as well as a condition that says one can separate the singular value of matrices in the sense that for all
K>0
, one can find an element
A
the cocycle acts by such that
\sigma_{k}(A)>K\sigma_{k+1}(A)
. In
[
CM16
]
, it is shown that almost every locally constant symplectic cocycle has simple spectrum.

Outside of the locally constant setting, it is more difficult to obtain results about Lyapunov exponents of cocycles. We describe here work studying fiber bunched cocycles over hyperbolic bases. In
[
BGMV03
]
, those authors show that there is an open and dense subset of the space of fiber bunched cocycles such that for every equilibrium state there are two distinct Lyapunov exponents by identifying a particular obstruction to hyperbolicity: a family of
f
-invariant, holonomy invariant probability measures on every fiber of the bundle
\Sigma\times\mathbb{RP}^{d-1}
that the cocycle acts on. Note that the result of
[
BGMV03
]
does not offer any information about which two Lyapunov exponents might be distinct nor their multiplicity. See also
[
ASV13
, Theorem B]
, where a similar result is obtained for cocycles over partially hyperbolic systems with
0
center exponents. In
[
Via08
]
, Viana shows, without a fiber bunching assumption, that for a fixed ergodic measure with product structure almost all Hölder continuous cocycles have two distinct Lyapunov exponents.

In the fiber bunched setting, a condition known as
pinching and twisting
is sufficient for a fiber bunched cocycle to have simple spectrum. In
[
BV04
]
, it is shown that a generic fiber bunched cocycle over a hyperbolic system has simple Lyapunov spectrum. This paper is one of the first where a type of pinching and twisting for fiber bunched cocycles appears. In
[
AV07a
]
, a simpler formulation of the pinching and twisting condition is given. Pinching and twisting has seen great application in the study of simplicity of Lyapunov spectrum. In
[
BPVL20
]
, it is shown that one can adapt the pinching and twisting criterion of Avila and Viana in order to show that, in a particular sense, generic cocycles over non-uniformly hyperbolic systems have simple Lyapunov spectrum. In
[
BBC+18
]
, a version of
[
Via08
]
for cocycles taking value in a semi-simple Lie group is proved, which says that a generic cocycle has two distinct Lyapunov exponents even when the cocycle lies, for example, in
\operatorname{Sp}(n,\mathbb{R})
. In
[
PV19
]
, the twisting and pinching criterion is extended to apply to cocycles over certain partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms.

1.2.
Discussion

When studying an abstract hyperbolic system, there is not always an obvious choice of measure that is the correct one to equip the system with. However, from the point of view of the thermodynamic formalism, equilibrium states provide the most natural choice of a measure. Consequently, it is conceptually satisfying to find criteria that apply to all these measures simultaneously.

On a technical level, this paper develops an approach to studying the Lyapunov exponents of fiber bunched cocycles that uses a different sort of “twisting” condition. In
[
AV07b
]
, the twisting condition requires that the cocycle holonomy around a particular homoclinic loop satisfies a specific condition that it can move subspaces off of themselves. This condition is localized to a very specific periodic point
\hat{p}
. In our setup, we have a measure
\hat{\mu}
that has simple spectrum and another measure
\hat{\eta}
that we’re interested in showing has simple spectrum. By using the Osceledec subspaces of
\hat{\mu}
we are able to obtain a sort of non-uniform twisting condition that is not localized near any particular point of
\hat{\Sigma}
. The Osceledec subspaces associated to
\hat{\mu}
are, in a certain sense, randomly distributed along local stable and unstable sets (See Subsection
3.1
). Our main technical contribution is figuring out how to use this feature in order to show convergence to a Dirac measure in Section
4
.

A secondary technical point is made in this paper: we have formulated and proved our results by using irreducibility conditions stated for Grassmannians
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
rather than conditions on exterior powers
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Although the difference is minor, we find that working with Grassmannians is conceptually cleaner and more in keeping with the approach pursued by Viana in his many works on Lyapunov exponents.

It remains interesting to determine if one can weaken the hypothesis that “pinching” occurs for a fully supported measure with continuous product structure to the existence of a single pinching orbit.

1.3.
Structure of Paper

Section
2
contains all necessary definitions and some standard constructions. In Section
3
, we state our irreducibility hypothesis for fiber-bunched cocycles and show that it implies the random distribution of unstable subspaces as well as a corresponding statement about
u
-states. Finally, Sections
4
and
5
contain the proof of Theorem
B
and Section
6
contains the proof of Theorem
C
.

2.1.

For

6

\hat{y}\in W^{u}_{loc}(\hat{x})
,
E^{u}_{k}(\hat{y})
exists and is given by:

E^{u}_{k}(\hat{y})=H^{u}_{\hat{x}\hat{y}}E^{u}_{k}(\hat{x}).

In particular
E^{u}_{k}
is uniformly continuous along local unstable manifolds where it is defined. The analogous statement holds for
E^{s}_{k}
with respect to
H^{s}
holonomies.

Proof.

We write the proof in the case of the stable bundle to avoid negative signs. The point is that for
\hat{x}\in W^{s}_{loc}(\hat{y})
, we have that

(7)

H^{s}_{\hat{\sigma}^{n}(\hat{x})\hat{\sigma}^{n}(\hat{y})}A^{n}(\hat{x})=A^{n}(\hat{y})H_{\hat{x}\hat{y}}^{s}.

and
H^{s}_{\sigma^{n}(\hat{x})\sigma^{n}(\hat{y})}\to\text{Id}
while the gaps between the singular values of
A^{n}(\hat{x})
are growing arbitrarily large. Writing
S_{k}(A)
for the span of the smallest
k
singular vectors of a matrix
A
. From (
7
) we have:

S_{k}(H^{s}_{\hat{\sigma}^{n}(\hat{x})\hat{\sigma}^{n}(\hat{y})}A^{n}(\hat{x}))=S_{k}(A^{n}(\hat{y})H^{s}_{\hat{x}\hat{y}}).

By directly estimating the norm restricted to subspaces by using the angles between singular subspaces of
A(\hat{x})
, one can deduce that
S_{k}(H^{s}_{\sigma^{n}(\hat{x})\sigma^{n}(\hat{y})}A^{n}(\hat{x}))
converges to
E^{s}_{k}(\hat{x})
and
S_{k}(A^{n}(\hat{y})H^{s}_{\hat{x}\hat{y}})
converges to
H^{s}_{\hat{y}\hat{x}}(E^{s}_{k}(\hat{y}))
.
Thus by passing to the limit we find that

E^{s}_{k}(\hat{x})=H^{s}_{\hat{y}\hat{x}}(E^{s}_{k}(\hat{y})),

as desired.

∎

2.2.
Product Structure of Measures

We now describe continuous local product structure. For each
1\leq i\leq m
, there exists the projection
[0;i]\to\pi_{s}([0;i])\times\pi_{u}([0;i])
. For convenience, we will denote by
\hat{\mu}^{s}_{i},\hat{\mu}^{u}_{i}
these pushforwards of
\mu|_{[0;i]}
by
\pi_{s}
and
\pi_{u}
on the one sided shifts. We say that
\hat{\mu}
has
continuous local product structure
if for each symbol
i
there is a continuous function
\psi\colon\hat{\Sigma}\to(0,\infty)
such that

(8)

\hat{\mu}|_{[0;i]}=\psi\cdot(\hat{\mu}^{s}_{i}\times\hat{\mu}^{u}_{i}).

Equation (
8
) is a description of the conditional measures on local stable and unstable leaves:

(9)

\int_{[0;i]}\phi\,d\hat{\mu}=\int_{[0;i]^{u}}\left(\int_{[0;i]^{s}}\phi\psi\,d\hat{\mu}^{s}_{i}\right)d\hat{\mu}^{u}_{i}.

Note that local unstable holonomy between local stable sets has a bounded Jacobian for these conditional measures, where the bound is given by
0<\max\psi/\min\psi<\infty
. In particular, from Equation (
9
) we have the following description of the conditional measures along local stable sets. On the local stable set containing the point
\hat{x}\in\hat{\Sigma}
, we may take

\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{x}}=\psi(\hat{y})\hat{\mu}^{s}_{i}\text{ for }\hat{y}\in W^{s}_{loc}(\hat{x}).

We now consider how the stable measures transform under application of the dynamics. Our goal is to show that we may arrange that the conditional measures along unstable leaves to not become distorted at small scale as we iterate the dynamics forward. The precise statement is in Lemma
2.6
.

The essence of the following proposition is that the conditional measures of
\hat{\mu}
along stable and unstable leaves have a continuous Jacobian with respect to pushforward.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
But18
, Prop. 3.1]
There are continuous functions
J_{u}\colon\Sigma^{u}\to(0,\infty)
and
J_{s}\colon\Sigma^{s}\to(0,\infty)
such that for each
1\leq i,j\leq l
and each Borel subset
K_{u}\subseteq[0;i]^{u}
,

\hat{\mu}^{u}_{i}(K_{u})=\int_{\hat{\sigma}_{u}^{-1}(K_{u})\cap[0;j,i]^{u}}J_{u}\,d\hat{\mu}^{u}_{j},

and likewise for each Borel subset
K_{s}\subseteq[0;i]^{s}
.

In the following lemma, we consider the preimage of a local stable set
W^{s}_{loc}(\hat{x})
. This preimage may intersect several of the cylinders
[0;i]
. For convenience, we write
\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{x},j}
for the conditional measure on
\hat{\sigma}^{-1}(W^{s}_{loc}(\hat{x})\cap[0;j])
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
\hat{\Sigma}
is a subshift of finite type as described above defined by a matrix
M
.
We have the following formula for the invariance of the measure under the shift. There exists a positive continuous function
J^{u}\colon\hat{\Sigma}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
that is constant on local stable leaves such that we have the following product formula for the measure. For each
i
,

\hat{\mu}|_{[0;i]}=\int_{\hat{x}\in W^{u}_{loc}(\hat{y})}\sum_{j\to i}\hat{\sigma}_{*}(\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{x},j})J^{u}(\hat{x})\,d\hat{\mu}^{u}_{i},

where the subscript in the sum indicates we are summing over all symbols
j
for which
ji
is subword permitted by
M
.

Proof.

To begin, we have from Proposition
2.4
that for each
\hat{y}\in[0;j,i]
exists
J^{u}_{ji}
such that

(10)

\hat{\sigma}_{*}(\hat{\mu}^{u}_{\hat{y}|_{[0;j,i]}})=J^{u}_{ji}d\hat{\mu}^{u}_{\hat{\sigma}(\hat{y})}.

We now obtain a formula for the pushforward of
\hat{\mu}|_{[0;j,i]}
. This measure may be written as

\int_{\hat{x}\in W^{u}_{loc}(\hat{y})\cap\hat{\sigma}^{-1}[0;i]}\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{x}}\,d\hat{\mu}^{u}_{\hat{y}}|_{W^{u}_{loc}(\hat{y})\cap[0;j,i]}.

By pushing this forward by
\hat{\sigma}
, we obtain an expression for the measure on
[-1;j,i]\subseteq[0;i]
:

\hat{\mu}|_{[-1;j,i]}=\int_{\hat{x}\in W^{u}_{loc}(\hat{\sigma}(\hat{y}))}\hat{\sigma}_{*}(\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{\sigma}^{-1}(\hat{x})})\,d\hat{\sigma}_{*}(\hat{\mu}^{u}_{\hat{y}}|_{W^{u}_{loc}(\hat{y})\cap[0;j,i]}).

But from equation (
10
), this is the same as

\int_{\hat{x}\in W^{u}_{loc}(\hat{y}))}\hat{\sigma}_{*}(\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{\sigma}^{-1}(\hat{x})})J^{u}_{ji}(\hat{x})\,d\hat{\mu}^{u}_{\hat{\sigma}(\hat{y})}.

Thus summing over all symbols
j
that may precede
i
, we obtain the expression promised by the lemma.

∎

The following fact is then straightforward to derive.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
\hat{\Sigma}
is a shift and that
\mu
is a
\hat{\sigma}
-invariant probability measure on
\hat{\Sigma}
with continuous product structure. Then the conditional measures satisfy the following property:
If
\hat{x}\in\hat{\Sigma}
and
K
a subset of
W^{s}_{loc}(\hat{x})
then for all
n\geq 0
we have that

\frac{\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{\sigma}^{n}(\hat{x})}(\hat{\sigma}^{n}(K))}{\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{\sigma}^{n}(\hat{x})}(\hat{\sigma}^{n}(W^{s}_{loc}(\hat{x})))}=\frac{\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{x}}(K)}{\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{x}}(W^{s}_{loc}(\hat{x}))}.

Proof.

Consider
\hat{\sigma}^{-1}(W^{s}_{loc}(\hat{x}))
. Then there are points
\hat{y}_{1},\ldots,\hat{y}_{k}
such that

\hat{\sigma}^{-1}(W^{s}_{loc}(\hat{x}))=\bigsqcup_{1\leq i\leq k}W^{s}_{loc}(\hat{y}_{i}).

From Lemma
2.5
, because
J^{u}
is constant along local stable leaves, it follows that there exist positive numbers
\xi_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
such that

\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{x}}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\xi_{i}\hat{\sigma}_{*}(\hat{\mu}^{s}_{\hat{y}_{i}}).

By iterating on this formula we obtain the lemma.

∎

2.3.
Grassmannians, Arrangements, and Quasi-Projective Maps

Here we introduce the definitions related to Grassmannian cocycles necessary to study Lyapunov exponents with multiplicity 1. Throughout
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the space of alternating
p
-forms over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\operatorname{Gr}(p,d)
the Grassmannian of
p
-dimensional subspaces, which includes into
\mathbb{P}\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as the decomposable
p
-forms, i.e., the
p
-forms
\eta
which may be written as
\eta=v_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{p}
. In what follows we endow
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
with the metric:

d_{\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)}(V,W)=\sup_{v\in V,||v||=1}\inf\{\|v-w\|:w\in W\}.

Any
\omega\in\Lambda^{d-p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
defines a
hyperplane

H_{\omega}\subseteq\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which is defined to be the kernel of the map
\eta\mapsto\eta\wedge\omega
. When
\omega
is defined by a decomposable
(d-p)
-form, we think of
\omega
as being “geometric” because it defines a
p
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence we may also define
geometric
hyperplanes in
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For a
d-p
-form
\omega
, the intersection of
H_{\omega}
with
\operatorname{Gr}(p,d)
is called a
hyperplane section
of
\operatorname{Gr}(p,d)
. Hence when a hyperplane section of
\operatorname{Gr}(p,d)
is defined by a decomposable
(d-p)
-form
\omega
, we call it a
geometric
hyperplane section. We call a finite intersection of hyperplane sections a
linear section
of
\operatorname{Gr}(p,d)
. In the case that a linear section of
\operatorname{Gr}(p,d)
is defined by geometric hyperplane sections, we call it a
geometric
linear section. Finally, a finite union of linear sections is called a
linear arrangement
, and when we take a finite union of geometric linear sections, we call this a
geometric
linear arrangement. Similarly, one may define a
geometric subspace
of
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as a finite intersection of geometric hyperplanes in
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Observe that in
\operatorname{Gr}(1,d)
linear sections are just subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Linear arrangements play an analogous role in defining irreducibility conditions as the classic strong irreducibility condition does in Furstenberg’s theorem on positivity of the top Lyapunov exponent.

The following lemma shows that linear arrangements indeed carry some properties of finite unions of linear subspaces to higher Grassmannians:

Lemma 2.7
.

[
Via14
, Lemma 8.4]
If
\{\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
is an arbitrary family of linear arrangements in
\operatorname{Gr}(p,d)
then
\cap_{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
coincides with the intersection of the
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
over a finite family. Hence the family of linear arrangements is closed under finite union and arbitrary intersections and moreover for
B\in\operatorname{GL}(d)
the inclusion
B(\mathcal{L})\subseteq(\mathcal{L})
implies that
B(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{L}
.

The space of linear sections
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{P}S\cap\operatorname{Gr}(p,d)
, where
S
denotes a subspace of
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is topologized via the topology for linear subspaces of
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Namely, we say that
\mathcal{S}_{n}\to\mathcal{S}
if and only if
S_{n}\to S
.

In
[
Via14
, Chapter 8]
, it is claimed that the space of geometric linear sections is compact in order to prove simplicity of spectrum under a version of twisting and pinching. However, as we see in Example
2.8
below, there seems to be an oversight in this claim. Because of this we state irreducibility criteria in terms of linear sections that are not necessarily geometric.

The dimension of a linear section
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{P}S\cap\operatorname{Gr}(p,d)
is defined simply as the dimension of
S
as a subspace of
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Once we remove the geometric restriction, we regain compactness of the space of linear sections of a fixed dimension:

Example 2.8
.

In this example, we show that if
D_{n}
is a sequence of geometric linear sections of a Grassmannian, then
\lim_{n}D_{n}
is not necessarily a geometric linear section: it is still a linear section, but it may no longer be geometric. We construct our example in
\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)
.

Let
\eta=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\in\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
and
\omega_{n}=(e_{1}+n^{-1}e_{3})\wedge(e_{2}+n^{-1}e_{4})\in\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
. We will write
e_{ij}:=e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
. Then
H_{\eta}
and
H_{\omega_{n}}
, the hyperplane sections defined by
\eta
and
\omega_{n}
respectively, are:

\displaystyle H_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\text{span}\langle e_{12},\,e_{13},\,e_{14},\,e_{23},\,e_{24}\rangle,

\displaystyle H_{\omega_{n}}

\displaystyle=\text{span}\langle e_{13},\,e_{24},\,e_{12}-n^{-2}e_{34},\,e_{14}+e_{23},\,e_{14}+n^{-1}e_{34}\rangle.

Thus

S_{n}:=H_{\eta}\cap H_{\omega_{n}}=\text{span}\langle e_{13},\,e_{24},\,e_{14}+e_{23},\,e_{12}+n^{-1}e_{14}\rangle,

is by definition a sequence of geometric linear sections when intersected with
\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)
. Call
D_{n}=S_{n}\cap\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)
, which may be identified with the decomposable tensors
S_{n}\subseteq\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
. We claim that
D=\lim_{n}D_{n}
is not a geometric linear section of
\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)
.

To see this we will first find a description of what
D
is, then we will show that
D
cannot be geometric because any decomposable
1
-form vanishing when wedged with all elements of
D
is a multiple of
e_{12}
.

To proceed we use that
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
is decomposable if and only if
\omega^{2}=0
. By using this we may calculate which tensors in
S_{n}
are decomposable. Namely we see that

(a(e_{12}+n^{-1}e_{14})+be_{13}+ce_{24}+d(e_{14}+e_{23}))^{2}=0

if and only if
d^{2}+adn^{-1}-bc=0
.

If we let
S=\lim_{n}S_{n}
then

S=\text{span}\langle e_{12},\,e_{13},\,e_{24},\,e_{14}+e_{23}\rangle,

In particular, if we calculate as before, we see that
S\cap\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)
is equal to the tensors of the form

(ae_{12}+be_{13}+ce_{24}+d(e_{14}+e_{23}))^{2}

with
d^{2}-bc=0
. Thus by comparing pointwise limits for different values of
a,b,c,d
, we find that
D=S\cap\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)
. In particular, note that some of the forms in
D
are
e_{12},e_{13},e_{24}
and
(e_{1}+e_{2})\wedge(e_{3}+e_{4})
. Thus any
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
that vanishes when wedged with things in
D
must be of the form

ae_{12}+be_{14}+ce_{23}

which is decomposable if and only if
bc=0
. In the cases that
a\neq 0
and
b=0
, the form we get is a multiple of
e_{2}\wedge(e_{1}+ce_{3})
, which does not vanish when wedged with
(e_{1}+e_{2})\wedge(e_{3}+e_{4})
unless
c=0
. So, the form must be
e_{12}
. Similarly, if
c=0
, the form is a multiple of
e_{1}\wedge(e_{2}+be_{4})
and we must have
b=0
as before. Thus if
a\neq 0
, the only forms we get are multiples of
e_{12}
. If
a=0
, then we get
e_{14}
and
e_{23}
neither of which vanish when wedged with
(e_{1}+e_{2})\wedge(e_{3}+e_{4})
. Thus the only decomposable forms vanishing when wedged with everything in
D
are multiples of
e_{12}
.

But this means that
D
cannot be a geometric linear section of
\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)
because it is not defined by
H_{e_{12}}
. Therefore the space of geometric linear sections is not compact.

When passing to the limit of the actions of linear maps on Grassmannians, it is useful to work with quasi-projective maps. We recall some definitions below.

If
P\in\operatorname{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
is a linear map then it induces a map
P_{\#}\colon\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)\to\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
for each
1\leq k\leq d
. We call such maps projective and often we denote the action of
P
on Grassmannians simply by
P
when there is no ambiguity.

The space of projective maps on
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
admits a very useful natural compactification which we now describe. Recall that we may embed
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
in
\mathbb{P}\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, so the space of projective maps of
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
embeds into the space of linear maps of
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with norm 1 with respect to the natural norm of
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since the latter is clearly compact, the closure of the set of projective maps regarded as above must also be, and its elements are referred to as
quasi-projective maps
. For more details of this construction, we refer the reader to
[
AV07a
, Section 2.1]
.

A quasi-projective map
Q
has a well-defined kernel when regarded as a linear map on
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We define
\ker Q\subseteq\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
to be the intersection of its kernel as a linear map on
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
, again using the embedding of
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
in
\mathbb{P}\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since linear maps of the form
\Lambda^{k}P
map rank 1 tensors to rank 1 tensors in
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the space of rank 1 tensors is compact in
\mathbb{P}\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for
\xi\in\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)\setminus\ker Q
, we may define
Q(\xi)\in\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
using the corresponding action of
Q
on
\mathbb{P}\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence quasi-projective maps can be regarded as functions
Q:\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)\setminus\ker Q\to\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
.

Lemma 2.9
.

[
AV07a
, Lemma 2.3]
The kernel of any quasi-projective map
Q
on
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
is contained in some hyperplane section of
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
.

For a measure
\nu
on
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
, the pushforward measure
Q_{*}\nu
is well-defined as long as
\nu(\ker Q)=0
. The following lemma will be very useful in our proof:

Lemma 2.10
.

[
AV07a
, Lem. 2.4]
If
(P_{n})
is a sequence of projective maps converging to some

of
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
and
(\nu_{n})
is a sequence of probability

\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
converging weakly to some probability

then

converges weakly to

.

3.
Strong Irreducibility and Distribution of Osceledec subspaces

The first key step in the proof of Furstenberg’s theorem and in the main theorem in
[

]
is using an irreducibility hypothesis to prove that the measure of

-states disintegrated over the

-sided shift cannot be concentrated on subspaces. We prove the corresponding result in our setting in Proposition
3.3
. For the rest of the paper we fix a fiber-bunched cocycle
\hat{A}:\hat{\Sigma}\to\operatorname{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})

Definition 3.1
.

A function
\mathcal{L}
from a topological space
X
to the set of linear arrangments in
\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
is said to be a continuous family of
k
-linear arrangements on
X
if there is an
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for each

\mathcal{L}(x)=\mathcal{S}_{1}(x)\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{S}_{n}(x),

where each
\mathcal{S}_{i}(x)
is of the form
\mathbb{P}S_{i}(x)\cap\operatorname{Gr}(k,d)
and the
S_{i}
are continuous functions from
X
to the space of linear subspaces of
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of a fixed dimension

6.
Furstenberg’s Theorem

In this section we show an analog of Furstenberg’s theorem in the fiber bunched setting. Compare the following theorem with
[
BGMV03
, Thm. 1]
, which offers a similar alternative between every measure being hyperbolic and there existing a holonomy invariant measure on the projective extension of the cocycle. The measure obtained in that theorem represents the flags obtained in the proof below.

Theorem
C
.

Suppose that
\hat{\Sigma}
is a transitive hyperbolic system and that
\hat{A}\colon\Sigma\to\operatorname{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
is a fiber-bunched Hölder continuous cocycle that is strongly irreducible on
\operatorname{Gr}(1,d)
. Then exactly one of the following holds:

(1)

For every measure
\hat{\mu}
with full support and continuous product structure
\hat{\mu}
has two distinct Lyapunov exponents, or;

(2)

\hat{A}
preserves a Hölder continuous conformal structure, and thus every invariant measure has all Lyapunov exponents equal to
0
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists some other such measure that has only one Lyapunov exponent. Then by
[
KS13
, Thm. 3.4]
, there exists a finite cover
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
of the cocycle on which we have that the cocycle preserves a continuous flag

\{0\}=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{0}\subset\cdots\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{k}=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}},

and continuous invariant conformal structures on each quotient
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{i+1}/\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{i}
. This flag projects down to a collection of invariant subspaces contained in the original bundle
\mathcal{E}
. Hence strong irreducibility implies that
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{1}=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{k}
, so the original cocycle preserves a conformal structure. Hence all invariant measures on
\hat{\Sigma}
have a single Lyapunov exponent and the result follows.

∎

Note that the above theorem gives no information on whether there are more than two exponents.

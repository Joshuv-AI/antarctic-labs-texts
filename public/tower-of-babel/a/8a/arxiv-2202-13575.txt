On an anisotropic double phase problem with singular and sign changing nonlinearity
Prashanta Garain and Tuhina Mukherjee

Abstract
. This article consists of study of anisotropic double phase problems with singular term and sign changing subcritical as well as critical nonlinearity. Seeking the help of well known Nehari manifold technique, we establish existence of at least two opposite sign energy solutions in the subcritical case and one negative energy solution in the critical case. The results in the critical case is even new in the classical p p -Laplacian case.

35A15, 35J62, 35J92, 35J75

1.
Introduction

In this article, we study existence of weak solutions for the following singular anisotropic double phase problem

(1.1)

\begin{split}-H_{p,q}u&=a(x)u^{-\delta}+\lambda b(x)u^{\alpha}\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&>0\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{split}

where
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
. The operator

(1.2)

H_{p,q}u=\text{div}(H(\nabla u)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u)+\mu(x)H(\nabla u)^{q-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u))

is referred to as the double phase anisotropic
(p,q)
-Laplace operator, where
\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u)
denotes the gradient of the Finsler-Minkowski norm
H
(defined in Section 2) with respect to
\nabla u
.

Unless otherwise stated, throughout the paper, we assume that

(H1)

\lambda>0
,
0<\delta<1
,
2\leq p<q<N
,
q<\alpha+1\leq p^{*}=\frac{Np}{N-p}
,
\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{1}{N}
and
0\leq\mu(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})
.

In the subcritical case, that is when
\alpha+1<p^{*}
, we assume that

(H2)

a>0
a.e. in
\Omega
such that
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
b\in L^{\frac{p^{*}}{p^{*}-\alpha-1}}(\Omega)
may change sign in
\Omega
.

Moreover, in the critical case
\alpha+1=p^{*}
, we assume that

(H3)

a>0
a.e. in
\Omega
and
a\in L^{\frac{p^{*}}{p^{*}-1+\delta}}(\Omega)
,

(H4)

b
is possibly sign-changing a.e. in
\Omega
with
b^{+}\not\equiv 0
and
b\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Equation (
1.1
) is closely associated with the double phase functional

v\longmapsto\int_{\Omega}(H(\nabla v)^{p}+\mu(x)H(\nabla v)^{q})\,dx,

where the integrand
F(x,\zeta)=H(\zeta)^{p}+\mu(x)(x)H(\zeta)^{q}
satisfies the non-standard growth condition

k_{1}|\zeta|^{p}\leq k_{2}H(\zeta)^{p}\leq F(x,\zeta)\leq k_{3}(1+H(\zeta)^{q})\leq k_{4}(1+|\zeta|^{q}),

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
k_{i}>0
for all
i=1,\cdots,4
. In the classical case
H(\zeta)=|\zeta|
, such functional was first introduced by Zhikov
[
22
,
21
]
which has a wide range of applications in material science, see Zhikov-Kozlov-Oleinik
[
13
]
and the references therein. The double phase behavior of such functional is captured by the weight function
\mu(\cdot)
. More precisely, the
p
-growth term governs the functional over the set
\{\mu(x)=0\}
and the
q
-growth term governs the functional over
\{\mu(x)\neq 0\}
which motivates (
1.1
) to be referred as a double phase equation. Further, a colossal amount of literature can be found, for example, in Colombo-Mingeone
[
7
,
6
]
, Baroni-Kuusi-Mingione
[
2
]
, Marcellini
[
16
,
17
]
, Ok
[
18
,
19
]
, Ragusa-Tachikawa
[
20
]
and the references therein.

We call (
1.1
) as a singular problem due to appearance of the term
u^{-\delta}
for
\delta\in(0,1)
, which clearly blows up near the boundary(origin). Such type of phenomenon in the context of double phase equation has captured the attention of researchers recently, refer Liu-Dai-Papageorgiou-Winkert
[
15
]
. More precisely, when
F(\zeta)=|\zeta|
, existence of multiple weak solutions has been proved in
[
15
]
for the singular double phase equation in the subcrtitical case but the nonlinearities were not sign-changing.

Although it is worth mentioning that anisotropic singular problem is very less understood. When
a(x)=0
, singular anisotropic problems is studied in Biset-Mebrate-Mohammed
[
3
]
, Farkas-Winkert
[
11
]
and Farkas-Fiscella-Winkert
[
10
]
, Bal-Garain-Mukherjee
[
1
]
. To the best of our knowledge, in the double phase context, anisotropic singular problems has been first discussed in Farkas-Winkert
[
12
]
, where the authors proved existence of one weak solution in the critical case.

In this article, our main motive is to study the equation (
1.1
) in both the subcritical and critical case. Another striking feature in the equation (
1.1
) lies in the sign changing behavior of the nonlinearity. To be more precise, in the subcritical case
\alpha+1<p^{*}
, we prove existence of multiple weak solutions (see Theorem
1.2
), whereas in the critical case
\alpha+1=p^{*}
, we prove existence of one weak solution (see Theorem
1.3
) of the problem (
1.1
). To prove our main results, we follow the Nehari manifold approach where we minimized the energy functional over suitable subsets of
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\Omega)
and considerably established them as weak solutions of our problem (
1.1
). Our aim behind this article is to develop the Nehari manifold theory for double phase anisotropic singular problems having sign changing weight function which is completely new as per current literature available. We also remark that the study of (
1.1
) in critical case for the classical
p
-Laplacian case for sign-changing weight functions is not studied till now. So we provide an interesting result in this regard.

Due to the double phase structure of our equation (
1.1
), we investigate the solutions in the Musielak-Orlicz Sobolev space
W_{0}^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
(see Section 2 for more details). The notion of weak solution for the equation (
1.1
) is defined as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
u\in W_{0}^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of the problem (
1.1
), if
u>0
a.e. in
\Omega
and for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

(1.3)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}(H(\nabla u)^{p-1}+\mu(x)H(\nabla u)^{q-1})\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u)\nabla\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(a(x)u^{-\delta}+\lambda b(x)u^{\alpha})\phi\,dx.\end{split}

For
\lambda>0
, we define the energy functional
I_{\lambda}:W_{0}^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
associated with the problem (
1.1
) by

(1.4)

\begin{split}I_{\lambda}(u)&=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{H(\nabla u)^{p}}{p}+\mu(x)\frac{H(\nabla u)^{q}}{q}\Big)\,dx-\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha+1}\int_{\Omega}b(x)|u|^{\alpha+1}\,dx.\end{split}

We say that
u\in W_{0}^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
has a positive (resp. negative) energy if
I_{\lambda}(u)>0
(resp.
<0
).

1.1.
Statement of the Main results:

Our main results in this article reads as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
(H1)
and
(H2)
are satisfied for
\alpha+1<p^{*}
. Then there exists a
\Lambda>0
such that for every
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
, the problem (
1.1
) admits at least two distinct weak solutions in
W_{0}^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
, one with positive and other with negative energy.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
(H1),\,(H3)
and
(H4)
are satisfied for
\alpha=p^{*}-1
. Then there exists a
\Lambda_{**}>0
such that for every
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda_{**})
, the problem (
1.1
) admits at least one negative energy weak solution.

1.2.
Functional setting and auxiliary results

Here,
H
is the Finsler-Minkowski norm, that is
H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty)
satisfies the following hypothesis:

(A1)

H(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

(A2)

H(x)=0
if and only if
x=0
.

(A3)

H\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
.

(A4)

H(tx)=|t|H(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(A5)

The Hessian matrix
\nabla^{2}(\frac{H^{2}}{2})(x)
is positive definite at every
x\neq 0
.

In this section, we will recall the main properties and embedding results for Musielak-Orlicz Sobolev spaces. To this end, we suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. For any
r\in[1,\infty)
, we denote by
L^{r}(\Omega)=L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and
L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
the usual Lebesgue spaces with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{r}
. Moreover, the Sobolev space
W^{1,r}_{0}(\Omega)
is equipped with the equivalent norm
\|\nabla\cdot\|_{r}
for
1<r<\infty
. Let hypothesis (H1) be satisfied and consider the nonlinear function
\mathcal{H}\colon\Omega\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x,t)=t^{p}+\mu(x)t^{q}.

Denoting by
M(\Omega)
the space of all measurable functions
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, we can introduce the Musielak-Orlicz Lebesgue space
L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
which is given by

\displaystyle L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in M(\Omega)\,:\,\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)<\infty\right\}

equipped with the Luxemburg norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\inf\left\{\tau>0\,:\,\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\frac{u}{\tau}\right)\leq 1\right\},

where the modular function
\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}:L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\displaystyle\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{H}(x,|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\big(|u|^{p}+\mu(x)|u|^{q}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x.

The norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathcal{H}}
and the modular function
\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}
have the following relations, see Liu-Dai
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
or Crespo-Blanco-Gasiński-Harjulehto-Winkert
[
8
, Proposition 2.13]
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
(H1)
be satisfied,
u\in L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
and
c>0
. Then the following hold:

(i)

If
u\neq 0
, then
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}=c
if and only if
\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\frac{u}{c})=1
.

(ii)

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}<1
(resp.
>1
,
=1
) if and only if
\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)<1
(resp.
>1
,
=1
).

(iii)

If
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}<1
, then
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{q}\leq\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\leq\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}
.

(iv)

If
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}>1
, then
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}\leq\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\leq\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{q}
.

(v)

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0
if and only if
\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\to 0
.

(vi)

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to\infty
if and only if
\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\to\infty
.

Furthermore, we define the seminormed space

\displaystyle L^{q}_{\mu}(x)(\Omega)=\left\{u\in M(\Omega)\,:\,\int_{\Omega}\mu(x)|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\right\}

endowed with the seminorm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{q,\mu}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\mu(x)|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

While, the corresponding Musielak-Orlicz Sobolev space
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
is defined by

\displaystyle W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\,:\,H(\nabla u)\in L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\Big\}

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}=\|H(\nabla u)\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Moreover, we denote by
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\Omega)
the completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
in
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
. From hypothesis (H1), we know that we can equip the space
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\Omega)
with the equivalent norm given by

\displaystyle\|u\|=\|H(\nabla u)\|_{\mathcal{H}},

see Proposition 2.18 of Colasuonno-Squassina
[
5
]
. It is known that
L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
,
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\Omega)
are uniformly convex and so reflexive Banach spaces, see Colasuonno-Squassina
[
5
, Proposition 2.14]
.
We end this section by recalling the following embeddings for the spaces
L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\Omega)
, see Colasuonno-Squassina
[
5
, Proposition 2.15]
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
(H1)
be satisfied. Then the following embeddings hold:

(i)

L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{1,r}_{0}(\Omega)
are continuous for all
r\in[1,p]
.

(ii)

W^{1,\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega)
is continuous for all
r\in[1,p^{*}]
and compact for all
r\in[1,p^{*})
.

(iii)

L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is continuous.

(iv)

L^{q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
is continuous.

Remark 1.6
.

Since
q<p^{*}
by hypothesis
(H1)
, we know from Proposition
1.5

(ii)
that
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
is compact.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Nehari set

We prove existence of weak solutions of (
1.1
) by variational methods. For
\lambda>0
, we define the energy functional
I_{\lambda}:W_{0}^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
associated with the problem (
1.1
) by

(2.1)

\begin{split}I_{\lambda}(u)&=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{H(\nabla u)^{p}}{p}+\mu(x)\frac{H(\nabla u)^{q}}{q}\Big)\,dx-\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx\\
&\quad-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha+1}\int_{\Omega}b(x)|u|^{\alpha+1}\,dx.\end{split}

Our approach is based on the Nehari manifold technique. To this end, we define the following subsets of
W_{0}^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
as follows:

(2.2)

M_{\lambda}=\{u\in W_{0}^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}:\langle I_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\}.

For
t>0
and
u\in W_{0}^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
, we define the fibering map
\phi_{u}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by
\phi_{u}(t)=I_{\lambda}(tu)
. It is easy to deduce the following:

\displaystyle\phi_{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{t^{p}H(\nabla u)^{p}}{p}+\mu(x)\frac{t^{q}H(\nabla u)^{q}}{q}\Big)\,dx-\frac{t^{1-\delta}}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\lambda t^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}\int_{\Omega}b(x)|u|^{\alpha+1}\,dx.

\displaystyle\phi_{u}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big({t^{p-1}H(\nabla u)^{p}}+\mu(x){t^{q-1}H(\nabla u)^{q}}\Big)\,dx-{t^{-\delta}}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-{\lambda t^{\alpha}}\int_{\Omega}b(x)|u|^{\alpha+1}\,dx.

\displaystyle\phi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big({(p-1)t^{p-2}H(\nabla u)^{p}}+\mu(x){(q-1)t^{q-2}H(\nabla u)^{q}}\Big)\,dx+\delta{t^{-\delta-1}}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-{\lambda\alpha t^{\alpha-1}}\int_{\Omega}b(x)|u|^{\alpha+1}\,dx.

We split the set
M_{\lambda}
as
M_{\lambda}=M_{\lambda}^{0}\cup M_{\lambda}^{+}\cup M_{\lambda}^{-}
, where

(2.3)

M_{\lambda}^{0}=\{u\in M_{\lambda}:\phi_{u}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(1)=0\},

(2.4)

M_{\lambda}^{+}=\{u\in M_{\lambda}:\phi_{u}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(1)>0\}

and

(2.5)

M_{\lambda}^{-}=\{u\in M_{\lambda}:\phi_{u}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(1)<0\}.

Our first lemma asserts the coercivity and existence of infimum of
I_{\lambda}
defined by (
2.1
) over the Nehari set
M_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The functional
I_{\lambda}
is coercive and bounded below over
M_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Since
u\in M_{\lambda}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\Big)\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx+\Big(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\Big)\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad-\Big(\frac{1}{1-\delta}-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\Big)\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx.

By Proposition
1.5
-(ii), we have
\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx\leq c\|u\|^{1-\delta}
for some constant
c>0
. Therefore, since
p<q
, we have

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\Big)\int_{\Omega}(H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx+H(\nabla u)^{q})\,dx-c\Big(\frac{1}{1-\delta}-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\Big)\|u\|^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\Big)\rho_{\mathcal{H}(\nabla u)}-c\Big(\frac{1}{1-\delta}-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\Big)\|u\|^{1-\delta}.

If
\|u\|>1
, by Proposition
1.4
, we have
\rho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\geq\|u\|^{p}
. Using this property in the above inequality, the result follows.
∎

Next, we prove that for small values of
\lambda
, the set
M_{\lambda}^{0}
is empty.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that
M_{\lambda}^{0}=\emptyset
, for every
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
.

Proof.

Suppose for every
\lambda^{*}>0
, there exists
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
such that
M_{\lambda}^{0}\neq\emptyset
. Hence for any given
\lambda>0
, let
u\in M_{\lambda}^{0}
. Then we have
\phi_{u}^{{}^{\prime}}(1)=0
and
\phi_{u}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(1)=0
, which gives

(2.6)

\int_{\Omega}(H(\nabla u)^{p}+\mu(x)H(\nabla u)^{q})\,dx=\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx+\lambda\int_{\Omega}b(x)|u|^{\alpha+1}\,dx

and

(2.7)

\int_{\Omega}((p-1)H(\nabla u)^{p}+(q-1)\mu(x)H(\nabla u)^{q})\,dx+\delta\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx=\lambda\alpha\int_{\Omega}b(x)|u|^{\alpha+1}\,dx

respectively. Putting the value of
\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx
from (
2.6
) in (
2.7
), we obtain

(2.8)

(p+\delta-1)\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx+(q+\delta-1)\int_{\Omega}\mu(x)H(\nabla u)^{q}\,dx=\lambda(\alpha+\delta)\int_{\Omega}b(x)|u|^{\alpha+1}\,dx.

Multiplying (
2.6
) by
(\alpha+\delta)
, we get

(2.9)

(\alpha+\delta)\int_{\Omega}(H(\nabla u)^{p}+\mu(x)H(\nabla u)^{q})\,dx=(\alpha+\delta)\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx+\lambda(\alpha+\delta)\int_{\Omega}b(x)|u|^{\alpha+1}\,dx.

Subtracting (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we get

(2.10)

(\alpha+1-p)\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx+(\alpha+1-q)\int_{\Omega}\mu(x)H(\nabla u)^{q}\,dx=(\alpha+\delta)\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx.

As in the proof of
[
15
, Proposition
3.3
]
from (
2.10
) we have

(2.11)

\|u\|\leq c_{1},

for some constant
c_{1}>0
. Moreover, as in the proof of
[
15
, Proposition
3.3
]
, from (
2.8
) we get

(2.12)

\|u\|\leq\left(\frac{1}{\lambda c_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1-p}}\quad\text{ or }\quad\|u\|\leq\left(\frac{1}{\lambda c_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1-q}}.

Letting
\lambda\to 0
in (
2.12
), we get
\|u\|\to\infty
, which contradicts (
2.11
). Hence the proof.
∎

For notational convenience, we set

B:=\int_{\Omega}b(x)|u|^{\alpha+1}\,dx\;\text{ and }\;A:=\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{1-\delta}\,dx.

Defining

\sigma_{u}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\Big({t^{p-1-\alpha}H(\nabla u)^{p}}+\mu(x){t^{q-1-\alpha}H(\nabla u)^{q}}\Big)\,dx-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}t^{-\alpha-\delta}}A,

we see that
\phi_{u}^{\prime}(t)=t^{\alpha}(\sigma_{u}(t)-\lambda B)
. Thus

tu\in M_{\lambda}\;\text{if and only if }\;\sigma_{u}(t)=\lambda B.

Because
q-1-\alpha>p-1-\alpha>-\delta-\alpha
, we assert that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\sigma_{u}(t)=-\infty,\;\lim_{t\to+\infty}\sigma_{u}(t)=0,\;\sigma_{u}(t)>0\;\text{for suitably large}\;t>0.

Consider

\displaystyle 0=\sigma_{u}^{\prime}(t)=(p-1-\alpha)t^{p-2-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\penalty\ dx+(q-1-\alpha)t^{q-2-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}\mu(x)H(\nabla u)^{q}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(\alpha+\delta)t^{-\delta-\alpha-1}A

\displaystyle\implies E_{u}(t):=(-p+1+\alpha)t^{p-1+\delta}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\penalty\ dx+(-q+1+\alpha)t^{q-1+\delta}\int_{\Omega}\mu(x)H(\nabla u)^{q}\penalty\ dx=(\alpha+\delta)A

Keeping into account
0<\delta<1<p<q<\alpha+1
, we observe the following about
E_{u}
:

(i)

E_{u}^{\prime}(t)>0,
for all
t>0
.

(ii)

\lim\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}E_{u}(t)=0
.

(iii)

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}E_{u}(t)=+\infty
.

From
(ii)
and
(iii)
, we know that there exists a
t_{\max}>0
such that
E_{u}(t_{\max})=(\alpha+\delta)A
. From
(i)
, we agree that this
t_{\max}>0
must be unique since
E_{u}
is an injective map.
Hence this gives that there exists a unique
t_{\max}>0
such that

\sigma_{u}^{\prime}(t_{\max})=0\;\text{and}\;\sigma_{u}\;\text{increases in}\;(0,t_{\max}).

Since for all
t>0
, define another map

\tilde{\sigma}_{u}(t)=t^{p-1-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\penalty\ dx-t^{-\alpha-\delta}A

then

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{u}^{\prime}(t)=(p-1-\alpha)t^{p-2-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}+(\alpha+\delta)t^{-\delta-\alpha-1}A

which implies
\max\limits_{t>0}\tilde{\sigma}_{u}(t)=\tilde{\sigma}_{u}(t_{0})
, where

t_{0}:=\left(\frac{(\alpha+\delta)A}{(\alpha-p+1)\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\penalty\ dx}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1+\delta}}.

Clearly
\sigma_{u}(t)\geq\tilde{\sigma}_{u}(t)
, therefore we get

\displaystyle\sigma_{u}(t_{\max})

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{u}(t_{0})\geq\tilde{\sigma}_{u}(t_{0})=t_{0}^{p-1-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}-t_{0}^{-\delta-\alpha}A

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha-p+1}{\alpha+\delta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+\delta}{p-1+\delta}}\left(\frac{\delta+p-1}{\alpha-p+1}\right)\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\penalty\ dx\right)^{\frac{\alpha+\delta}{p-1+\delta}}}{A^{\frac{\alpha+1-p}{p-1+\delta}}}.

Also we have the following-

\displaystyle A

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla w_{k})^{p}\penalty\ dx=l_{1}^{p},\;\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mu(x)H(\nabla w_{k})^{q}\penalty\ dx=l_{2}^{q}\;\text{and}\;\int_{\Omega}b(x)|w_{k}|^{p^{*}}\penalty\ dx=d^{p^{*}}>0.

Using (

)-(

, we have

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}(u_{0})+\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{H(\nabla w_{k})^{p}}{p}+\frac{\mu(x)H(\nabla w_{k})^{q}}{q}\right)\penalty\ dx-\frac{\lambda}{p^{*}}\int_{\Omega}b(x)|w_{k}|^{p^{*}}\penalty\ dx=m_{\lambda}^{+}+o_{k}(1)

that is

(2.65)

0>m_{\lambda}^{+}=I_{\lambda}(u_{0})+\frac{l_{1}^{p}}{p}+\frac{l_{2}^{q}}{q}-\frac{\lambda d^{p^{*}}}{p^{*}}.

Since
u_{k}\in M_{\lambda}^{+}
, again using (
2.56
)-(
2.59
) we get

(2.66)

o_{k}(1)+\int_{\Omega}\left(H(\nabla w_{k})^{p}+\mu(x)H(\nabla w_{k})^{q}\right)\penalty\ dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}b(x)|w_{k}|^{p^{*}}\penalty\ dx+\phi_{u_{0}}^{\prime}(1)=0

that is

(2.67)

l_{1}^{p}+l_{2}^{q}-\lambda d^{p^{*}}+\phi_{u_{0}}^{\prime}(1)=0.

Since
0<\lambda<\lambda_{*}
and
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
, there exists
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
such that

\phi_{u_{0}}^{\prime}(t_{1})=\phi_{u_{0}}^{\prime}(t_{2})=0
and
t_{1}u_{0}\in M_{\lambda}^{+}
,
t_{2}u_{0}\in M_{\lambda}^{-}
. We study following three cases:

(i)

t_{2}<1
;

(ii)

t_{2}\geq 1
and

(iii)

Case(i):
Define

. Then

and (

(l_{1}^{p}+l_{2}^{q}-d^{p^{*}})>0

2.67
), we get

t_{2}<1

So

. This helps us to obtain from (

\displaystyle m_{\lambda}^{+}=g(1)\geq g(t_{2})\geq\phi_{u_{0}}(t_{2})+\frac{t_{2}^{q}}{q}(l_{1}^{p}+l_{2}^{q}-\lambda d^{p^{*}})>\phi_{u_{0}}(t_{2})>\phi_{u_{0}}(t_{1})\geq m_{\lambda}^{+}

Case(ii):

\displaystyle S_{2}^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}\frac{l_{1}^{p}p^{*}}{p}<\lambda S_{2}^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}d^{p^{*}}\leq\lambda S_{2}^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}|b|_{\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|w_{k}|^{p^{*}}\penalty\ dx

∎

Theorem 2.19

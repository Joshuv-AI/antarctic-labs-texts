Estimating the Ratio of Means in a Zero-inflated Poisson Mixture Model Key words: Zero-inflated Poisson mixture, ratio of means, maximum likelihood estimator, EM algorithm, information matrix, standard error, Bayes estimator, conjugate prior, empirical Bayes estimator, zero-truncated Poisson distribution.
Michael D. Perlman

Abstract
The problem of estimating the ratio of the means of a two-component Poisson mixture model is considered, when each component is subject to zero-inflation, i.e., excess zero counts. The resulting zero-inflated Poisson mixture (ZIPM) model can be treated as a three-component Poisson mixture model with one degenerate component. The EM algorithm is applied to obtain frequentist estimators and their standard errors, the latter determined via an explicit expression for the observed information matrix. Bayes and empirical Bayes estimators also are obtained by means of conjugate priors and their data-based variants. Lastly, the ZIPM distribution and the ZTP (zero-truncated Poisson) distribution are compared.



1. Introduction.

Consider an ecological study aimed at determining the relative reproductive rate of a newly discovered invasive subspecies A of ant compared to that of the native subspecies B. The available data is indirect, consisting only of counts of nests in several standardized sites, rather than direct observations of individuals. Furthermore, the nests of the two subspecies are indistinguishable, (possibly) differing only in their relative numbers per site. If the expected numbers of nests per site for A and B are denoted by

Alternatively, it follows from (

4. Main problem:
{\bf Y}
,
{\bf Z}
,
{\bf M}
unobserved,
{\bf N}
observed.

Here
N_{ij}
is a zero-inflated Poisson mixture (ZIPM) rv:
N_{ij}
is an
\epsilon
-mixture of
M_{ij}
and
O_{ij}
, where
O_{ij}
is degenerate at 0, so
O_{ij}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda=0)
; while
M_{ij}
is a
\pi
-mixture of

\mathrm{Poisson}(t_{i}\mu)\equiv\mathrm{Poisson}(t_{i}\theta\lambda)
and
\mathrm{Poisson}(t_{i}\nu)\equiv\mathrm{Poisson}(t_{i}\lambda)
rvs. Thus this problem can be viewed as a three-component Poisson mixture model with one degenerate component and non-i.i.d observations. The three weights are

\pi\epsilon
,
(1-\pi)\epsilon
, and
1-\epsilon
, with the identifiability constraint
0<\pi\leq 1/2

For notational simplicity, set
\omega=(\pi,\epsilon,\mu,\nu)
. Under this three-component mixture model, the unconditional pmf of the observed data
\mathbf{N}\equiv\{N_{ij}\}
is

\displaystyle f_{\omega}(\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i,j}\left[\pi\epsilon e^{-t_{i}\mu}(t_{i}\mu)^{n_{ij}}+(1-\pi)\epsilon e^{-t_{i}\nu}(t_{i}\nu)^{n_{ij}}+(1-\epsilon)\,0^{n_{ij}}\right]/{\bf n}!,

where
0^{0}=1
and
{\bf n}!=\prod_{i,j}n_{ij}!
. Again the
K\equiv IJ
rvs

are independent but non-identically distributed (inid) if

are non-identical.

The sample space of
({\bf Y},({\bf Z},{\bf N}))
is
\Upsilon\times\Omega
, where
\Upsilon=\{0,1\}^{\cal J}
and

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=\big[\{0,1\}^{\cal K}\times(\mathbb{Z}_{+})^{\cal K}\big]\cap\{({\bf z},{\bf n})\,|\,\forall i,j,\,z_{ij}=0\Rightarrow n_{ij}=0\}

\displaystyle=\big[\{0,1\}^{\cal K}\times(\mathbb{Z}_{+})^{\cal K}\big]\cap\big\{({\bf z},{\bf n})\,\big|\,\forall i,j,\,n_{ij}(1-z_{ij})=0\big\}

\displaystyle=\big[\{0,1\}^{\cal K}\times(\mathbb{Z}_{+})^{\cal K}\big]\cap\big\{({\bf z},{\bf n})\,\big|\,\prod_{i,j}0^{n_{ij}(1-z_{ij})}=1\big\},

with
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
the set of nonnegative integers. The joint pmf of the unobserved and observed data
(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{N})
on
\Upsilon\times\Omega
is

\displaystyle f_{\omega}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z},\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle=f_{\pi}({\bf y})f_{\epsilon}({\bf z})f_{\mu,\nu}(\mathbf{n}\,|\,{\bf y},\mathbf{z})

(65)

\displaystyle=\prod\nolimits_{j}\pi^{y_{j}}(1-\pi)^{1-y_{j}}\prod\nolimits_{i,j}\epsilon^{z_{ij}}(1-\epsilon)^{1-z_{ij}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\prod\nolimits_{i,j}\left[e^{-t_{i}\mu}(t_{i}\mu)^{n_{ij}}\right]^{y_{j}z_{ij}}\left[e^{-t_{i}\nu}(t_{i}\nu)^{n_{ij}}\right]^{(1-y_{j})z_{ij}}0^{n_{ij}(1-z_{ij})}\big/{\bf n}!

\displaystyle=\left[\pi^{\bar{y}}(1-\pi)^{1-\bar{y}}\right]^{J}\left[\epsilon^{\bar{z}}(1-\epsilon)^{1-\bar{z}}\right]^{K}\left[e^{-\overline{tyz}\mu}\mu^{\overline{nyz}}e^{-\overline{t(1-y)z}\,\nu}\nu^{\overline{n(1-y)z}}\,\right]^{K}\frac{\prod_{i,j}t_{i}^{n_{ij}z_{ij}}0^{n_{ij}(1-z_{ij})}}{{\bf n}!}

(66)

\displaystyle=\left[\pi^{\bar{y}}(1-\pi)^{1-\bar{y}}\right]^{J}\left[\epsilon^{\bar{z}}(1-\epsilon)^{1-\bar{z}}\right]^{K}\left[e^{-\overline{tyz}\mu}\mu^{\overline{ny}}e^{-\overline{t(1-y)z}\,\nu}\nu^{\overline{n(1-y)}}\,\right]^{K}\cdot\Xi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{n}),

where
\mathbf{y}=\{y_{j}\}
,
\mathbf{z}=\{z_{ij}\}
,
\mathbf{n}=\{n_{ij}\}
,

\displaystyle\bar{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\sum\nolimits_{i,j}z_{ij},

\displaystyle\overline{tz}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\sum\nolimits_{i,j}t_{i}z_{ij}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i,j}t_{i}z_{ij},

\displaystyle\overline{tyz}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\sum\nolimits_{i,j}t_{i}y_{j}z_{ij}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i,j}t_{i}y_{j}z_{ij},

and similarly with
y
replaced by
1-y
. To obtain (
66

,

\displaystyle\overline{nyz}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\sum\nolimits_{i,j}n_{ij}y_{j}z_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\sum\nolimits_{i,j}n_{ij}y_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\sum\nolimits_{j}n_{j}y_{j}

\displaystyle=\overline{ny},

\displaystyle\Xi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{n}):

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{i,j}t_{i}^{n_{ij}z_{ij}}0^{n_{ij}(1-z_{ij})}}{{\bf n}!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{i,j}t_{i}^{n_{ij}}0^{n_{ij}(1-z_{ij})}}{{\bf n}!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{i}t_{i}^{n_{i}}\cdot\prod_{i,j}0^{n_{ij}(1-z_{ij})}}{{\bf n}!};

and similarly with
y
replaced by
1-y
.
Thus

determines an exponential family with support

and sufficient statistic

\displaystyle(\bar{Y},\bar{Z},\,\overline{tYZ},\,\overline{t(1-Y)Z},\,\overline{nY},\,\overline{n(1-Y)}).

\displaystyle=(N_{i,j}\,|\,i=1,\dots,I),

5. Conditional ZIPM = ZTP?

Consider two subsets of the index set
{\cal K}
and two subarrays of the data array
{\bf N}\equiv(N_{ij})
:

\displaystyle\Omega_{Z}^{\neq}

\displaystyle=\{(i,j)\,|\,Z_{ij}=1\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{N}^{\neq}

\displaystyle=\{(i,j)\,|\,N_{ij}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle{\bf N}_{Z}^{\neq}

\displaystyle=(N_{ij}\,|\,Z_{ij}=1)=(M_{ij}\,|\,Z_{ij}=1),

\displaystyle{\bf N}^{\neq}

\displaystyle=(N_{ij}\,|\,N_{ij}\neq 0)=(M_{ij}\,|\,M_{ij}\neq 0).

Both
\Omega_{Z}^{\neq}
and
\Omega_{N}^{\neq}
are random subsets,
\Omega_{Z}^{\neq}
is unobserved,
\Omega_{N}^{\neq}
is observed, and
\Omega_{N}^{\neq}\subseteq\Omega_{Z}^{\neq}
, so
{\bf N}^{\neq}\subseteq{\bf N}_{Z}^{\neq}
. Because
{\bf M}
is independent of
{\bf Z}
,
{\bf N}_{Z}^{\neq}
is a random subarray of the i.n.i.d. array
(M_{ij})
, where membership in this subarray depends only on
{\bf Z}
. Thus
{\bf N}^{\neq}
is also is a (smaller) random subarray of the i.n.i.d. array
(M_{ij})
, where membership depends on both
{\bf Z}
and the events
\{M_{ij}\neq 0\}
.

The latter fact suggest a question: Is the conditional distribution of the two-component ZIPM rv

N_{ij}
given
N_{ij}\neq 0
the same as the distribution of the mixture of the conditional distributions of the two Poisson components given that each is non-zero? The latter conditional distribution is the well-known
zero-truncated Poisson (ZTP) distribution
, also called positive Poisson, which has been thoroughly studied (cf. Johnson, Kemp, and Kotz (2005)). The ZTP distribution model also is an exponential family, with pmf given by

(107)

\displaystyle g_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\lambda^{x}}{(e^{\lambda}-1)x!},\qquad x=1,2,\dots.

If the answer to the above question is yes, then estimation of
\pi,\mu,\nu
and thus
\theta
could be based on only the set of non-zero
N_{ij}
. That is, discard all 0’s and view the remaining
N_{ij}
as
\pi
-mixtures of two ZTP components with parameters
t_{i}\mu
and
t_{i}\nu
. Because this involves only two mixture components rather than three as above, both being exponential families, and neither is degenerate, estimation methods such as the EM algorithm would be easier to carry out.

Unfortunately the answer to the question is no. If we abbreviate
N_{ij}
by
N
,
M_{ij}
by
M
, and
Z_{ij}
by
Z
, then the question can be exressed as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Is}

\displaystyle\ \ {\rm P}[N=x\,|\,N\neq 0]=\frac{\pi\mu^{x}}{(e^{\mu}-1)x!}+\frac{(1-\pi)\nu^{x}}{(e^{\nu}-1)x!},\qquad x=1,2,\dots?

However, for
x\geq 1
,

\displaystyle P[N=x\,|\,N\neq 0]

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm P}[ZM=x,ZM\neq 0]}{P[ZM\neq 0]}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm P}[M=x,Z=1,M\neq 0]}{P[Z=1,M\neq 0]}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm P}[M=x,M\neq 0]}{P[M\neq 0]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{\pi\mu^{x}}{e^{\mu}x!}+\frac{(1-\pi)\nu^{x}}{e^{\nu}x!}}{1-\frac{\pi}{e^{\mu}}-\frac{(1-\pi)}{e^{\nu}}},

since
M
and
Z
are independent, so the question becomes:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Is}

\displaystyle\ \ \frac{\frac{\pi\mu^{x}}{e^{\mu}x!}+\frac{(1-\pi)\nu^{x}}{e^{\nu}x!}}{1-\frac{\pi}{e^{\mu}}-\frac{(1-\pi)}{e^{\nu}}}=\frac{\pi\mu^{x}}{(e^{\mu}-1)x!}+\frac{(1-\pi)\nu^{x}}{(e^{\nu}-1)x!},\qquad x=1,2,\dots?

After some algebra, this equation simplifies to

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\mu}{\nu}\Big)^{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{e^{\mu}-1}{e^{\nu}-1}\right),

which cannot hold for all
x\geq 1
unless
\mu=\nu
.

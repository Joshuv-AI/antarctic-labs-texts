Some comments on ‘Unique Bernoulli g g -measures’ Thanks: Email: phulse@ed.ac.uk
Paul Hulse

Abstract
. Proofs of results due to Johansson, Öberg and Pollicott [ 3 ] are given which correct a bound used in the original. This leads to modifications to the most general results; however, the main corollaries are unaffected.

Introduction

The method of proof can be summarised roughly as follows.

2.
More on
g
-measures

Let
g\in{\mathcal{G}}
be continuous.
The operator

{\mathcal{L}}_{g}\colon C({X_{+}})\rightarrow C({X_{+}})
is defined by

{\mathcal{L}}_{g}f(x)=\sum_{y\in T_{+}^{-1}x}g(y)f(y)\qquad(f\in C({X_{+}})).

A measure
\mu\in M({X_{+}})
is a
g
-measure if and only if
\mu
is
{\mathcal{L}}_{g}
-invariant,
that is,
\mu({\mathcal{L}}_{g}f)=\mu(f)

(f\in C({X_{+}}))
.
Any weak*-limit of measures of the form

\mu_{k}(f)=n_{k}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}{\mathcal{L}}_{g}^{i}f(x^{(k)})

is a
g
-measure, while any weak*-limit of the form

\nu_{k}(f)={\mathcal{L}}_{g}^{n_{k}}f(x^{(k)})\qquad(f\in C({X_{+}}))

is a
g
-chain
(where
x^{(k)}\in{X_{+}}
and
n_{k}\nearrow\infty
).
Thus, if
g\in{\mathcal{G}}
is continuous, there is always at least one
g
-measure,
and uniqueness is equivalent to the convergence (pointwise or uniform) of

n^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{\mathcal{L}}_{g}^{i}f

to a constant for every
f\in C({X_{+}})
,
whereas there is a unique
g
-chain if and only if

{\mathcal{L}}_{g}^{n}f
converges to a constant.

The following result is proved in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
g\in{\mathcal{G}}
, and let

d_{n}=\sup\left\{\frac{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{$1$}}{2}\sum_{s\in S^{\{0\}}}|g(sx_{\mathbb{N}})-g(sy_{\mathbb{N}})|\,:\,x_{i}=y_{i},\,0\leq i\leq n-1\right\}\qquad(n\geq 1).

If
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-d_{i})=\infty
,
there is a unique
g
-measure
\mu\in M({X_{+}})
.
The natural extension of
\mu
is Bernoulli,
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{g}^{n}f\to\mu(f)
uniformly as
n\to\infty
, for all
f\in C({X_{+}})
.

The validity of (
2
) as a hypothesis follows from the above theorem.
To see this, note that

\inf_{s\in S^{\{0\}}}\frac{g(sx_{\mathbb{N}})}{g(sy_{\mathbb{N}})}=\left(\sup_{s\in S^{\{0\}}}\frac{g(sy_{\mathbb{N}})}{g(sx_{\mathbb{N}})}\right)^{-1},

and so if
x_{i}=y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
,

\left|1-\frac{g(sx_{\mathbb{N}})}{g(sy_{\mathbb{N}})}\right|\leq\begin{cases}1-\rho_{n}^{-1},&\text{if }g(sx_{\mathbb{N}})\leq g(sy_{\mathbb{N}}),\\
\rho_{n}-1,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\rho_{n}=\rho_{[0,n]}(g)
.
Since
1-\rho_{n}^{-1}\leq\rho_{n}-1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{s\in S^{\{0\}}}|g(sx_{\mathbb{N}})-g(sy_{\mathbb{N}})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in S^{\{0\}}}g(sy_{\mathbb{N}})\left|1-\frac{g(sx_{\mathbb{N}})}{g(sy_{\mathbb{N}})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{n}-1.

Moreover, if
g
is positive,
d_{i}
is uniformly bounded below 1, and so,

\displaystyle\prod_{i=k}^{n}(1-d_{i})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log(1-d_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(\sum_{i=k}^{n}(-d_{i}-Cd_{i}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{i=k-1}^{n-1}\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}(e^{\log\rho_{i}}-1)-C(e^{\log\rho_{i}}-1)^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-\frac{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{$1$}}{2}\sum_{i=k-1}^{n-1}(\log\rho_{i}+K(\log\rho_{i})^{2})\right),

for some constants
C
,
K
.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, we can choose
k
so that

K\log\rho_{i}<\varepsilon
for all
i\geq k-1
,
in which case,

\prod_{i=k}^{n}(1-d_{i})\geq\exp\left(-\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+\varepsilon\right)\sum_{i=k-1}^{n-1}\log\rho_{i}\right)=\prod_{i=k-1}^{n-1}\rho_{i}^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)}.

Thus,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{[0,i]}(g)^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)}=\infty\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-d_{i})=\infty.

g
-measures and couplings

In this section, we consider the block-couplings described in
[
3
]
.
We adopt the convention that
[m,n]=\emptyset
if
m>n
,

[\zeta]_{\emptyset}
and
empty intersections are the full space,
empty products are 1, and empty sums are 0.
If
s\in S
, then
s^{\Lambda}\in S^{\Lambda}
is such that
s^{\Lambda}_{i}=s

for all
i\in\Lambda

(
s^{\emptyset}
is the empty sequence).
We identify
\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}}

with the sequence space
(\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i})^{\mathbb{Z}}
in the obvious way,
and extend previous definitions and notation accordingly;
in particular,
[\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}]_{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}[\zeta_{i}]_{K}

(\zeta_{i}\in S_{i}^{\Lambda},\,K\subseteq\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Additionally,
\Delta_{\Lambda}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X\colon x_{\Lambda}=y_{\Lambda}\}
.

Each
g\in{\mathcal{G}}
determines,
for all
m\leq n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
a set of probability measures

\{\pi_{[m,n]}^{g}(\,\cdot\,|\,x)\,:\,x\in X\}
on
(X,{\mathcal{B}}_{[m,n]})
defined by

\pi_{[m,n]}^{g}\bigl([x^{\prime}]_{[m,n]}\,\big|\,x\bigr)=\prod_{i=m}^{n}\widetilde{g}_{i}(T^{i}x^{\prime})\qquad(x^{\prime}\in[x]_{(n,\infty)}).

(3.1)

The
\pi_{[m,n]}^{g}(\,\cdot\,|\,x)
are consistent in the sense that

\pi_{[m,n]}^{g}\bigl([x]_{[m,n]}\,\big|\,x\bigr)=\pi_{[m,i]}^{g}\bigl([x]_{[m,i]}\,\big|\,x\bigr)\pi_{(i,n]}^{g}\bigl([x]_{(i,n]}\,\big|\,x\bigr)\qquad(m\leq i<n),

and measurable in the sense that
\pi_{[m,n]}^{g}(f\,|\,x)
is measurable in
x

for all
{\mathcal{B}}_{[m,n]}
-measurable
f
.
Thus, they can be extended to a measurable set

\{\pi_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}}^{g}(\,\cdot\,|\,x)\,:\,x\in X\}

of probability measures on
(X,{\mathcal{B}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}})
.
Note that

\mu\bigl([x]_{[m,n]}\,\big|\,{\mathcal{B}}_{(n,\infty)}\bigr)(x)=\pi_{[m,n]}^{g}\bigl([x]_{[m,n]}\,\big|\,x\bigr)\qquad\text{a.e.}(\mu)

for any
g
-chain
\mu
.
Note also that (
3.1
) sets up a 1-1 correspondence between
such measurable sets of probability measures and elements of
{\mathcal{G}}
.

If
(M_{i},{\mathcal{A}}_{i},\mu_{i})

1\leq i\leq n
, are probability spaces,
a coupling of the measures
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}

is a probability measure
P

\prod_{i=1}^{n}(M_{i},{\mathcal{A}}_{i})

such that

P=\mu_{i}\circ\psi_{i}\qquad(1\leq i\leq n),

where
\psi_{i}\colon\prod_{j=1}^{n}M_{j}\to M_{i}
is the natural projection.
(Note we assume the product is ordered in the same way the measures are listed.)
For
\mu\in M(X)
and
\zeta\in S^{\Lambda}

(\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{Z}})

such that
\mu([\zeta])>0
,
let
\mu_{\zeta}
denote the conditional probability measure

\mu(\,\cdot\,|[\zeta])
.
If
\mu\in M(X)
is
T
-invariant, the dynamical system
(T,\mu)
is Bernoulli
if (and only if) the process determined by the partitions

\{[s^{\{i\}}]\colon s\in S\}
,
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, is very weak Bernoulli,
that is, given
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
subsets
G_{m}\subseteq S^{[1,m]}

(m\in{\mathbb{N}})
,
and couplings
P_{\zeta}
of
\bigl\{\mu,\,\mu_{\zeta}\bigr\}

on
{\mathcal{B}}_{[-n,0]}\times{\mathcal{B}}_{[-n,0]}

(\zeta\in G_{m})
such that

\sum_{\zeta\in G_{m}}\mu([\zeta])>1-\varepsilon

and

n^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}P_{\zeta}\bigl(\Delta_{\{-i\}}^{c}\bigr)<\varepsilon\qquad(\zeta\in G_{m},\,m\in{\mathbb{N}})

(see
[
4
]
).

Let
g_{1},g_{2}\in{\mathcal{G}}
. By a coupling of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
,
we mean a set of probability measures
\{P_{x,y}\,:\,x,y\in X\}

\{\pi^{g_{1}}_{[-n,0]}(\,\cdot\,|\,x),\,\pi^{g_{2}}_{[-n,0]}(\,\cdot\,|\,y)\}
,

.

\displaystyle=\bigl|\pi_{[-n,0]}^{g_{1}}\bigl(T^{n}[\zeta]\,\big|\,x\bigr)-\pi_{[-n,0]}^{g_{2}}\bigl(T^{n}[\zeta]\,\big|\,y\bigr)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq P_{x,y}\bigl(\Delta^{c}_{[-n,m-n]}\bigr)

for all
\zeta\in S^{[0,m]},\ 0\leq m\leq n,\ x,y\in X

Let

Given probability measures
\mu
and

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

\mu,\nu\in M(\Omega)

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\sqrt{\mu(\omega)\nu(\omega)}\geq 1-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\sqrt{\rho}-1\bigr)^{2},

\rho=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\left\{\frac{\mu(\omega)}{\nu(\omega)},\frac{\nu(\omega)}{\mu(\omega)}\right\}.

Proof.

For any
\omega\in\Omega

2\sqrt{\mu(\omega)\nu(\omega)}=\mu(\omega)+\nu(\omega)-\nu(\omega)\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\mu(\omega)}{\nu(\omega)}}\right)^{2}.

Note that

\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}\frac{\mu(\omega)}{\nu(\omega)}\geq\rho^{-1},

and so,

\left|\,1-\sqrt{\frac{\mu(\omega)}{\nu(\omega)}}\,\right|\leq\begin{cases}1-\sqrt{\rho^{-1}},&\nu(\omega)\geq\mu(\omega),\\
\sqrt{\rho}-1,&\nu(\omega)\leq\mu(\omega).\end{cases}

Since
1-\sqrt{\rho^{-1}}\leq\sqrt{\rho}-1
,
it follows that

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\sqrt{\mu(\omega)\nu(\omega)}\geq 1-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\sqrt{\rho}-1\bigr)^{2},

as required.

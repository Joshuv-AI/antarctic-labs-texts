Mappings preserving quantum Renyi’s entropies in von Neumann algebras
Andrzej Łuczak, Hanna Podsędkowska, Rafał Wieczorek

Abstract
. We investigate the situation when a normal positive linear unital map on a semifinite von Neumann algebra leaving the trace invariant does not change fixed quantum Renyi’s entropy of the density of a normal state. It is also shown that such a map does not change the entropy of any density if and only if it is a Jordan *-isomorphism on the algebra.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 46L53; Secondary: 81P45

Introduction

In the paper, the question of invariance of quantum Renyi’s entropies of a density under the action of a normal positive linear unital map
\Phi
is addressed in the case of a semifinite von Neumann algebra. This can be seen as a follow-up to the investigation in
[
2
]
where such a problem was analysed for the Segal entropy. It is interesting that in both the cases: Segal’s entropy and any single Renyi’s entropy the answer is the same, namely,
\Phi
restricted to the algebra generated by the density is a *-isomorphism. The question of invariance of Renyi’s entropy for every density is also considered, and it turns out that this holds if and only if
\Phi
is a Jordan *-isomorphism on the algebra.

In our analysis, we aim at full generality considering densities which may be unbounded. For such densities a number of specific Jensen’s inequalities are derived. These inequalities are well-known, in a more general form, for bounded operators.

It is interesting to observe that passing from the case of bounded densities to unbounded ones requires some arguments referring to the algebra of measurable operators, in particular, convergence in measure for such operators is widely exploited.

1.
Preliminaries and notation

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra of operators acting on a Hilbert space

and

. The translation-invariant measure topology is defined by a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of

\{N(\varepsilon,\delta):\varepsilon,\delta>0\}
, given by

\displaystyle N(\varepsilon,\delta)=\{x\in\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}:

there exists a projection

\displaystyle xp\in\mathscr{M},\quad\|xp\|\leqslant\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\tau(\mathbb{1}-p)\leqslant\delta\}.

The following ‘technical’ form of convergence in measure proved in

|x_{n}-x|=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,e_{n}(dt)

The domain of a linear operator

For each

, there is a measurable operator

\rho(x)=\tau(xh)=\tau(hx),\quad x\in\mathscr{M}.

The space of all such operators is denoted by

, and the correspondence above is one-to-one and isometric, where the norm on
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)

\|h\|_{1}=\tau(|h|),\quad h\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau).

(In the theory of noncommutative

Chebyschev’s inequality

\tau(e([\varepsilon,+\infty)))\leqslant\frac{\tau(|x|)}{\varepsilon}=\frac{\|x\|_{1}}{\varepsilon},

where

|x|=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,e(dt)

is the spectral representation, together with the relation (

-norm implies convergence in measure.

For
\rho\in\mathscr{M}_{*}^{+}
, the corresponding element in

is called the

Let

be a normal positive linear unital mapping such that
\tau\circ\Phi=\tau
(such maps are called

\Phi
can be extended to a bounded map on
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
(cf.
[
2

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)
. The
\alpha

S_{\alpha}(\rho)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\frac{\tau(h_{\rho}^{\alpha})}{\tau(h_{\rho})},

under the assumption

, i.e. for the spectral representation of
h_{\rho}

(2)

h_{\rho}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,e(dt),

we have

S_{\alpha}(\rho)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha}\,\tau(e(dt))}{\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,\tau(e(dt))}.

Accordingly, we define

such that

S_{\alpha}(h)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\frac{\tau(h^{\alpha})}{\tau(h)}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha}\,\tau(e(dt))}{\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,\tau(e(dt))},

where
h
has the spectral representation as in (
2
). For a map

as before, we are interested in the problem when this map does not change Renyi’s entropy of a density

S_{\alpha}(h)=S_{\alpha}(\Phi(h))

holds.

2.
Main theorem

In this section, we aim at proving the following main result of the paper.

Let
\Phi
be a normal linear unital mapping on
\mathscr{M}
such that
\tau\circ\Phi=\tau

\alpha>2

such that

h^{\gamma}\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)

(this assumption is automatically satisfied for a finite algebra).

Let us first explain the general idea of the proof. The invariance of entropy is obviously equivalent to the equality

(3)

\tau(\Phi(h^{\alpha}))=\tau(h^{\alpha})=\tau(\Phi(h)^{\alpha}).

From this equality, the equality

(4)

\Phi(h^{\alpha})=\Phi(h)^{\alpha}

is attempted to be derived, leading, in turn, to the final conclusion. It should be noted that this equality for
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2]

bounded

h
our final conclusion, which follows from
[
4
]
. Observe that in some cases the derivation of the relation (
4
) from (
3
) is simple. Namely, for
0<\alpha<1
, the function
[0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto t^{\alpha}
is operator concave, and Jensen’s inequality for
bounded
operators yields the inequality

\Phi(h^{\alpha})\leqslant\Phi(h)^{\alpha}.

For
1<\alpha\leqslant 2
the function
[0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto t^{\alpha}
is operator convex, and again Jensen’s inequality for
bounded
operators yields the inequality

\Phi(h^{\alpha})\geqslant\Phi(h)^{\alpha}.

In both the cases above, the faithfulness of
\tau
together with the relation (
3
) yield the equality (
4
). In the general case of not necessarily bounded densities
h
, we could attempt to derive appropriate Jensen’s inequalities (which we actually do for
1<\alpha\leqslant 2
) however, a simpler way is possible. Namely, it turns out that in the case
0<\alpha<1
,
the equality
(
3
)
alone is sufficient for proving the thesis
. For
\alpha>1
, we follow the route described above and obtain the equality (
4
). From this we get

\Phi(h^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}=\Phi(h)=\Phi\big((h^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\big)

which is the equality (
4
) with
h^{\alpha}
instead of
h
and
\frac{1}{\alpha}<1
instead of
\alpha
, and thus we get the equality (
3
) in the form

\tau\big(\Phi\big((h^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\big)\big)=\tau\big(\Phi(h^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\big).

As pointed out above, this gives the claim upon observing that

W^{*}(h^{\alpha})=W^{*}(h)
. Consequently, the proof of the theorem is divided into three parts: 1. Case
0<\alpha<1
, in which the thesis is proved;

2. Case
1<\alpha<2
and 3. Case
\alpha\geqslant 2
, in which cases the equality
\Phi(h^{\gamma})=\Phi(h)^{\gamma}
for some
\gamma>1
is obtained.

\displaystyle\tau(\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(u))=\frac{\sin\alpha\pi}{\pi}\int_{m}^{M}\frac{1}{s^{1-\alpha}}\tau(u(s\mathbb{1}+u)^{-1})\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\alpha\pi}{\pi}\int_{m}^{M}\frac{1}{s^{1-\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{s+t}\,\tau(p(dt))\,ds

\displaystyle\nearrow\frac{\sin\alpha\pi}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{1-\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{s+t}\,\tau(p(dt))\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\alpha\pi}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{1-\alpha}}\frac{t}{s+t}\,ds\,\tau(p(dt))=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha}\tau(p(dt))=\tau(u^{\alpha}),

which yields

\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(u)\to u^{\alpha}\quad\textit{in $\|\cdot\|_{1}$-norm}.

For
0\leqslant u_{1}\leqslant u_{2}
we have
s\mathbb{1}+u_{1}\leqslant s\mathbb{1}+u_{2}
thus

(s\mathbb{1}+u_{1})^{-1}\geqslant(s\mathbb{1}+u_{2})^{-1},

so

which yields

Passing to the limit with

,

, we get

which shows the operator monotonicity of the function

for elements from

(a result well-known for bounded operators).

Let
x_{n}
and

be as in the assumption. Then

\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(x_{n})\leqslant\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(x).

Further we have

\displaystyle x_{n}(s\mathbb{1}+x_{n})^{-1}-x(s\mathbb{1}+x)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(s\mathbb{1}+x_{n})^{-1}(x_{n}(s\mathbb{1}+x)-(s\mathbb{1}+x_{n})x)(s\mathbb{1}+x)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(s\mathbb{1}+x_{n})^{-1}(x_{n}-x)(s\mathbb{1}+x_{n})^{-1},

thus

\displaystyle\|x_{n}(s\mathbb{1}+x_{n})^{-1}-x(s\mathbb{1}+x)^{-1}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle s\|(s\mathbb{1}+x_{n})^{-1}\|_{\infty}\|x_{n}-x\|_{1}\|(s\mathbb{1}+x_{n})^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\frac{\|x_{n}-x\|_{1}}{s},

which shows that

From the Lebesgue monotone convergence theorem we obtain as

\displaystyle\tau(\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(x_{n}))=\frac{\sin\alpha\pi}{\pi}\int_{m}^{M}\frac{1}{s^{1-\alpha}}\tau(x_{n}(s\mathbb{1}+x_{n})^{-1})\,ds

\displaystyle\nearrow\frac{\sin\alpha\pi}{\pi}\int_{m}^{M}\frac{1}{s^{1-\alpha}}\tau(x(s\mathbb{1}+x)^{-1})\,ds=\tau(\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(x)),

i.e.

\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(x_{n})\nearrow\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(x)\quad\textit{in $\|\cdot\|_{1}$-norm}.

From the relation (

\tau(\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(x_{n}))\leqslant\tau(x_{n}^{\alpha})\leqslant\tau(x^{\alpha}),

which gives

\displaystyle\tau(\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(x))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau(\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(x_{n}))\leqslant\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\tau(x_{n}^{\alpha})\leqslant\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\tau(x_{n}^{\alpha})\leqslant\tau(x^{\alpha}),

and passing to the limit with

,
M\nearrow\infty
, in the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle\tau(x^{\alpha})=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\to 0\\
M\to\infty\end{subarray}}\tau(\widetilde{z}_{m}^{M}(x))\leqslant\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\tau(x_{n}^{\alpha})\leqslant\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\tau(x_{n}^{\alpha})\leqslant\tau(x^{\alpha}),

showing that

which by virtue of the inequality

∎

\Phi(h_{n}^{\alpha})\nearrow\Phi(h^{\alpha})\quad\textit{in $\|\cdot\|_{1}$-norm}.

In particular,

\Phi(h_{n})\to\Phi(h)\quad\textit{in measure}

\tau(\Phi(h_{n}^{\alpha}))\to\tau(\Phi(h^{\alpha})).

3.
Additional results

In this section, we consider the situation when the map
\Phi
does not change an arbitrary fixed entropy of every density.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Phi
be a normal linear unital mapping on
\mathscr{M}
such that
\tau\circ\Phi=\tau
, and let for an arbitrary fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)

S_{\alpha}(h)=S_{\alpha}(\Phi(h))

for every density
h
such that
h^{\alpha}\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
. Then
\Phi
is a Jordan

*-isomorphism on
\mathscr{M}
.

Proof.

Let
h\in\mathscr{M}
be a density such that
h^{\alpha}\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
and, in the case
\alpha>2
,
h^{\gamma}\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
for some
1<\gamma\leqslant 2
. From the Main Theorem it follows that
\Phi|W(h)
is a *-isomorphism, in particular, we have

\Phi(h^{2})=\Phi(h)^{2}.

On account of
[
2
, Proposition 9]
, for all
x\in\mathscr{M}
the following equality holds

(17)

\Phi(x\circ h)=\Phi(x)\circ\Phi(h),

where for
u,v\in\mathscr{M}

u\circ v=\frac{uv+vu}{2},

is the Jordan product.

Since such
h
’s as above are
\sigma
-weakly dense in
\mathscr{M}^{+}
, and
\Phi
is normal, it follows that the equation (
17
) holds for all
h
in
\mathscr{M}^{+}
, and consequently we have

\Phi(x\circ y)=\Phi(x)\circ\Phi(y).

for all
x,y\in\mathscr{M}
, thus
\Phi
is a Jordan *-homomorphism.

If
\Phi(x)=0
, then

0=\Phi(x)^{*}\Phi(x)+\Phi(x)\Phi(x)^{*}=\Phi(x^{*}x+xx^{*})=\Phi(x^{*}x)+\Phi(xx^{*})

thus

\Phi(x^{*}x)=\Phi(xx^{*})=0,

and it follows that
\tau(x^{*}x)=\tau(\Phi(x^{*}x))=0
, i.e.

\Phi
is an isomorphism.
∎

Remark
.

If
\Phi
is a Jordan *-isomorphism, then it is obviously

a *-isomorphism on the abelian algebra

, thus the equality

At the end let us comment on a general situation. In many cases the quantities of the form

(18)

H(h)=\tau(f(h))

where
h
is a density, and
f
is a continuous function, are considered (typical examples are

leading to Segal’s entropy or
f(t)=t^{\alpha}
leading to Renyi’s entropy). Then a natural question is the invariance of
H
with respect to some map
\Phi
, i.e. the relation

(19)

H(h)=H(\Phi(h)).

Putting aside technical problems (
h
may be unbounded), we have noticed that, in principle, if
\Phi
is a normal Jordan *-isomorphism, then
\Phi(f(h))=f(\Phi(h))
, thus the additional assumption
\tau\circ\Phi=\tau
yields the relation (
19
). Consequently, the condition: ‘
\Phi

is a normal Jordan *-isomorphism such that

\tau\circ\Phi=\tau
’ is
sufficient
for the invariance (
19
). A similar situation occurs when we deal with two densities. A typical example, which for simplicity we consider in finite dimension, is the relative entropy of two densities
h
and
k
defined as

D(h||k)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{tr}h(\log h-\log k),&\text{if $\operatorname{supp}h\leqslant\operatorname{supp}k$}\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Now if
\Phi
is a normal Jordan *-isomorphism leaving the trace ‘
\operatorname{tr}
’ invariant, we have
\operatorname{supp}\Phi(h)=\Phi(\operatorname{supp}h)
and
\operatorname{supp}\Phi(k)=\Phi(\operatorname{supp}k)
, thus
\operatorname{supp}h\leqslant\operatorname{supp}k
if and only if
\operatorname{supp}\Phi(h)\leqslant\operatorname{supp}\Phi(k)
in which case we get the equality

\displaystyle D(\Phi(h)||\Phi(k))=\operatorname{tr}\Phi(h)(\log\Phi(h)-\log\Phi(k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(\Phi(h)\circ(\log\Phi(h)-\log\Phi(k)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(\Phi(h)\circ(\Phi(\log h)-\Phi(\log k)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(\Phi(h)\circ\Phi(\log h-\log k))=\operatorname{tr}\Phi(h\circ(\log h-\log k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(h\circ(\log h-\log k))=\operatorname{tr}h(\log h-\log k)=D(h||k),

showing that the same condition: ‘
\Phi

is a normal Jordan *-isomorphism such that

\tau\circ\Phi=\tau
’ is again
sufficient
for the invariance of the relative entropy. The above reasoning shows that some results which state e.g. that a map leaving the relative entropy, or the
H
as in (
18

\Phi(x)=uxu^{*}
where

is a unitary or antiunitary or an isometry are incorrect (a good example of such a Jordan *-isomorphism which does not change the entropy but is not of the form as above, is transposition in
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
with respect to a given orthonormal basis).

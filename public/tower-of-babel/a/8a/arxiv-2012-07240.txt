1.
Introduction

In this paper, we would like to develop the differential transform theory of some operators related with the operators which is generated by the parabolic operator
L=\partial_{t}-\Delta
. In this theory, the central role is the heat diffusion semigroup generated by
L
that we denote by

e^{-\tau L}=e^{-\tau(\partial_{t}-\Delta)}=e^{-\tau\partial_{t}}\circ e^{\tau\Delta},\quad\text{with }\tau>0.

Clearly, this semigroup can be given by an integral with a concrete kernel. That is, for any function
\varphi
in Schwartz class
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle e^{-\tau(\partial_{t}-\Delta)}\varphi(x,t)=e^{\tau\Delta}\varphi(x,t-\tau)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}W(y,\tau)\varphi(x-y,t-\tau)dy,~(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},

where
W
is the Gauss-Weierstrass kernel

W(y,\tau)=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4\tau}}.

Let us describe a kind of fractional Poisson formula, which will play a central role in our study. For
0<\alpha<1
, the
fractional Poisson formula
of
f
is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}^{\alpha}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau^{2\alpha}}{4^{\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\tau^{2}/(4s)}e^{-sL}f(x)\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}e^{-\frac{\tau^{2}}{4r}L}f(x)\,\frac{dr}{r^{1-\alpha}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ \tau>0.

This means that the fractional Poisson formula can be obtained via the heat semigroup
\displaystyle\{e^{-sL}\}_{s>0}
. The formula above can be deduced from the theory of the spectral analysis. When
L=-\Delta
, Carffarelli and Silvestre
[
2
]
studied the fractional Poisson formula to solve an extension problems. Stinga and Torrea
[
13
]
defined this kind of Poisson formula for Hermite operator
L=-\Delta+|x|^{2}
. In the case
\alpha=1/2
,
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{1/2}
is the Bochner subordinated Poisson semigroup, see
[
12
]
.

We will mainly study some properties of the differential transforms associated to the operators generated by the parabolic operator. Let
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be an increasing sequence of positive real numbers, and
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be a bounded sequence of real numbers. With this two sequences, we consider the differential transform

Tf=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}v_{j}(T_{a_{j+1}}f-T_{a_{j}}f),

for operators
\{T_{s}\}_{s>0}
.
In this paper, we will study the properties of the differential transforms related to the fractional Poisson type operators
\{{\mathcal{P}}^{\alpha}_{\tau}\}_{\tau>0}
defined as (
1.2
). Moreover, combining (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), we have the pointwise formula as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}^{\alpha}f(x,t)=\frac{\tau^{2\alpha}}{4^{\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{e^{-(\tau^{2}+|y|^{2})/(4s)}}{(4\pi s)^{n/2}}f(x-y,t-s)dy\frac{ds}{s^{1+\alpha}},

The Fourier transform

\nu

Let
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be an increasing sequence of positive real numbers, and
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}v_{j}({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}^{\alpha}f(x,t)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}^{\alpha}f(x,t)).

For each

Then, we have the following formula

\displaystyle T_{N}^{\alpha}f(x,t)=\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}^{\alpha}f(x,t)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}^{\alpha}f(x,t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4^{\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{j+1}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j+1}^{2}/(4s)}-a_{j}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j}^{2}/(4s)}}{s^{1+\alpha}}\frac{e^{-|y|^{2}/(4s)}}{(4\pi s)^{n/2}}f(x-y,t-s)~dsdy

2.
Uniform boundedness of
T_{N}^{\alpha}

In this section, we shall deduce the uniform
L^{p}
-boundedness of the differential transform
T_{N}^{\alpha}
. Firstly, we should prove the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the differential transform
T_{N}^{\alpha}
by Fourier transform. This result will be presented in Proposition
2.2
. And the following lemma gives a useful estimate which will be used in the later proofs.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
. Then for any complex number
z_{0}
with
Rez_{0}>0
and
\displaystyle|\arg z_{0}|\leq{\pi}/{4}
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z_{0}u}e^{-\frac{z_{0}}{u}}\,\frac{du}{u^{\alpha}}=z_{0}^{1-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-r}e^{-z_{0}^{2}/r}}{r^{2-\alpha}}dr.

Now we present the uniform
L^{2}
-boundedness of the operator
T^{\alpha}_{N}
in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
n
,
\alpha
and
\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
, such that

\sup_{N}\|T_{N}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
. Using the Plancherel theorem, we have

\displaystyle\left\|T_{N}^{\alpha}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}^{\alpha}f-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}^{\alpha}f)\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}\leq C\|v_{j}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\left\|\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\left|\partial_{\tau}\widehat{{\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}^{\alpha}f}\right|d\tau\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}.

By using the second identity in (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\widehat{{\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}^{\alpha}f}(\xi,\varrho)

\displaystyle=C\partial_{\tau}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}\widehat{e^{-\frac{\tau^{2}}{4r}L}f}(\xi,\varrho)\,\frac{dr}{r^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle=C\partial_{\tau}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}e^{-\frac{\tau^{2}}{4r}(i\varrho+|\xi|^{2})}\widehat{f}(\xi,\varrho)\,\frac{dr}{r^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}\tau(i\varrho+|\xi|^{2})e^{-\frac{\tau^{2}}{4r}(i\varrho+|\xi|^{2})}\widehat{f}(\xi,\varrho)\,\frac{dr}{r^{2-\alpha}}.

Note that the Fourier transform above is well defined, see Remark
2.3
. Then we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|T_{N}^{\alpha}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\widehat{f}(\xi,\varrho)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}\tau(i\varrho+|\xi|^{2})e^{-\frac{\tau^{2}}{4r}(i\varrho+|\xi|^{2})}\,\frac{dr}{r^{2-\alpha}}\right|d\tau\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}.

Thus again by the Plancherel theorem, the remainder is devoted to prove the uniform boundedness of the multiplier

\big|\widehat{K_{N}^{\alpha}}(\xi,\varrho)\big|\leq C\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}\tau(i\varrho+|\xi|^{2})e^{-\frac{\tau^{2}}{4r}(i\varrho+|\xi|^{2})}\,\frac{dr}{r^{2-\alpha}}\right|d\tau\right|\leq C,\quad(\xi,\varrho)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}.

Taking
z_{0}=\tau\sqrt{i\varrho+|\xi|^{2}}
, we rewrite the above inequality as

\left|\widehat{K_{N}^{\alpha}}(\xi,\varrho)\right|\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}z_{0}e^{-\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{4r}}\,\frac{dr}{r^{2-\alpha}}\sqrt{i\varrho+|\xi|^{2}}\right|d\tau,\quad(\xi,\varrho)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}.

By Lemma
2.1
, for any
(\xi,\varrho)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}z_{0}e^{-\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{4r}}\,\frac{dr}{r^{2-\alpha}}\sqrt{i\varrho+|\xi|^{2}}\right|d\tau=2^{1-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|z_{0}^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{z_{0}}{2u}}e^{-\frac{z_{0}}{2}u}\frac{du}{u^{\alpha}}\sqrt{i\varrho+|\xi|^{2}}\right|d\tau.

Since
\left|\arg z_{0}\right|\leq\frac{\pi}{4}
, we have
|e^{-z_{0}/(2u)}|\leq e^{-c|z_{0}|/u}
and
|e^{-z_{0}u/2}|\leq e^{-c|z_{0}|u}
, where
c={\sqrt{2}/{4}}
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}z_{0}^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z_{0}/u}e^{-z_{0}u}\frac{du}{u^{\alpha}}\sqrt{i\varrho+|\xi|^{2}}d\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\sqrt{i\varrho+|\xi|^{2}}\big|\,|z_{0}|^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-c|z_{0}|/u}e^{-c|z_{0}|u}\frac{du}{u^{\alpha}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\sqrt{i\varrho+|\xi|^{2}}\big|\,|z_{0}|^{2\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-c|z_{0}|^{2}/v}e^{-cv}\frac{dv}{v^{\alpha}}d\tau.

Recall that
z_{0}=\tau\sqrt{i\varrho+|\xi|^{2}}
. Therefore, if we use
m
to denote the module of
\sqrt{i\varrho+|\xi|^{2}}
:
m=|\sqrt{i\varrho+|\xi|^{2}}|
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\sqrt{i\varrho+|\xi|^{2}}\right|\,|z_{0}|^{2\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-c|z_{0}|^{2}/v}e^{-cv}\frac{dv}{v^{\alpha}}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{2\alpha}\,\tau^{2\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-c(m\tau)^{2}/v}e^{-cv}\frac{dv}{v^{\alpha}}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}(m\tau)^{2\alpha-1}e^{-c(m\tau)^{2}/v}d(m\tau)e^{-cv}\frac{dv}{v^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{2\alpha-1}e^{-c\tau^{2}/v}d\tau e^{-cv}\frac{dv}{v^{\alpha}}\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-cv}dv\leq C,

where the constants
C
appeared above all are independent of
N.

Then the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Notice that the following integral

\frac{y^{2s}}{4^{s}\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y^{2}/(4\tau)}e^{-\tau(i\varrho+\lambda)}\,\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1+s}},\quad\varrho\in{\mathbb{R}},~\lambda\geq 0,~0<s<1.

is convergent, as the Cauchy integral theorem and analytic continuation of the formula with
\varrho=0
showing.
Notice that these integrals are related to Macdonald’s functions, see
[
7
]
.
Then (
1.2
) are well defined by using Fourier transform and Hermite expansions, respectively.

In this paper, we shall use the vector-valued Calderón-Zygmund theory in spaces of homogeneous type. One of the obvious changes in the parabolic setting is the metric of the underlying spaces. In our case, the parabolic distance is given by

d((x,t),(y,s))=\max\{|x-y|,|t-s|^{1/2}\},\quad\mbox{ for }(x,t),(y,s)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{1},

where the
\left|\cdot\right|
denotes the Euclidean distance.
Then
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
with the topology generated by the distance
d
and the compatible Borel measure
dxdt
forms a space of homogeneous type. On the space of homogeneous type
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},d,dxdt)
, we can define the Calderón-Zygmund operators. For full details, see
[
11
]
, also
[
8
]
for parabolic case. In the following, we shall show that the kernel
K_{N}^{\alpha}
of the operator
T_{N}^{\alpha}
is a Calderón-Zygmund kernel.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exists a constant
C
depending on
n,\alpha
and
\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
(not on
N
) such that, for any
(y,s)\neq(0,0),

i)

\displaystyle\left|K_{N}^{\alpha}(y,s)\right|\leq\frac{C}{(s^{1/2}+|y|)^{n+2}}
,

ii)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{y}K_{N}^{\alpha}(y,s)\right|\leq\frac{C}{(s^{1/2}+|y|)^{n+3}}
,

iii)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{s}K_{N}^{\alpha}(y,s)\right|\leq\frac{C}{(s^{1/2}+|y|)^{n+4}}
.

The proof of Proposition
2.4
involves a lemma in the following.

Lemma 2.5
.

Define a function
\displaystyle S(x,t)=\frac{e^{-{|x|^{2}\over ct}}}{t^{l+m}}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then, for any integer
l,m\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C>0
, such that

\left|S(x,t)\right|\leq\frac{C}{(t^{1/2}+|x|)^{2(l+m)}}.

Proof.

In fact, it is well known that, for any positive integer
m
, we have

e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{c}}\leq C_{m}(1+|x|)^{-m}.

Then

\displaystyle S(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{ct}}}{t^{l+m}}=\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{c}\big(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\big)^{2}}}{t^{l+m}}\leq\frac{C}{t^{l+m}}\big(1+\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\big)^{-2(l+m)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{t^{l+m}}\frac{t^{l+m}}{(t^{1/2}+|x|)^{2(l+m)}}\leq\frac{C}{(t^{1/2}+|x|)^{2(l+m)}}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

i)
This is just the growth condition for the kernel in the parabolic case. We have

\displaystyle|K_{N}^{\alpha}(y,s)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\frac{a_{j+1}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j+1}^{2}/(4s)}-a_{j}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j}^{2}/(4s)}}{s^{1+\alpha}}\frac{e^{-|y|^{2}/(4s)}}{s^{n/2}}\right|

\displaystyle=C\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|a_{j+1}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j+1}^{2}/(4s)}-a_{j}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j}^{2}/(4s)}\right|\frac{e^{-|y|^{2}/(4s)}}{s^{1+\alpha+n/2}}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|a_{j+1}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j+1}^{2}/(4s)}-a_{j}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j}^{2}/(4s)}\right|=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\partial_{u}\left(u^{2\alpha}e^{-u^{2}/(4s)}\right)du\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|(2\alpha u^{2\alpha-1}-\frac{u^{2\alpha+1}}{2s})e^{-u^{2}/(4s)}\right|du\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|(u^{2\alpha-1}+\frac{u^{2\alpha+1}}{2s})e^{-u^{2}/(4s)}\right|du

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{s}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\sqrt{s})^{2\alpha-1}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{s}}\right)^{2\alpha-1}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left(u/\sqrt{s}\right)^{2}}d\frac{u}{\sqrt{s}}

\displaystyle\quad\,+s^{\alpha-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{s}}\right)^{2\alpha+1}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left(u/\sqrt{s}\right)^{2}}d\frac{u}{\sqrt{s}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{\alpha}.

Then by Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle|K_{N}^{\alpha}(y,s)|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{e^{-|y|^{2}/(4s)}}{s^{n/2+1}}\leq\frac{C}{(s^{1/2}+|y|)^{n+2}}.

The proof of
ii)
and
iii)
are similar. We omit them here.
∎

Proof of Theorem

In this section, we will give the proof of the boundedness of the maximal operator
T^{*}.

The next proposition, parallel to Proposition 3.2 in
[
1
]
(see it also in
[
3
,
4
]
), shows that, without lost of generality, we may assume that

(3.1)

1<\rho\leq{a_{j+1}\over a_{j}}\leq\rho^{2},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

We omit its proof here.

Proposition 3.1
.

Given a
\rho
-lacunary sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and a multiplying sequence
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
, we can define a
\rho
-lacunary sequence
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
verifying the following properties:

(i)

1<\rho\leq\eta_{j+1}/\eta_{j}\leq\rho^{2},\quad\left\|\{\theta_{j}\}\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}=\left\|\{v_{j}\}\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
.

(ii)

For all
N=(N_{1},N_{2}),
there exists
N^{\prime}=(N_{1}^{\prime},N_{2}^{\prime})
with
T_{N}^{\alpha}=\tilde{T}_{N^{\prime}}^{\alpha},

where
\tilde{T}_{N^{\prime}}^{\alpha}
is the operator defined in (
1.3
) for the new sequences
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}.

It follows from this proposition that it is enough to prove all the results of this article
in the case of a
\rho
-lacunary sequence satisfying (
3.1
). For this reason, in the rest of the article
we assume that
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfies (
3.1
) without saying it explicitly.

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, we need a Cotlar’s type inequality to control the operator
T^{*}
by some Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators.

For any
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},
let

T_{M}^{*}f(x,t)=\sup_{-M\leq N_{1}<N_{2}\leq M}\left|T_{N}^{\alpha}f(x,t)\right|,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

And then we have

T^{*}f(x,t)=\sup_{M\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}T_{M}^{*}f(x,t).

Then we can start proving a pointwise estimate for the operators
T_{M}^{*}f
by the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator defined as

{\mathcal{M}}_{q}f(x,t)=\sup_{B\ni x}\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\left|f(y,t)\right|^{q}dy\right)^{1/q},\quad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},

and the maximal operator

{\mathcal{M}}^{-}_{q}f(x,t)=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{0}\left|f(x,t+s)\right|^{q}ds\right)^{1\over q},

for
1\leq q<\infty
. And we denote
{\mathcal{M}}={\mathcal{M}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{-}={\mathcal{M}}^{-}_{1}
, for simple.

Theorem 3.2
.

For each
q\in(1,\infty),
there exists a constant
C
depending only on
\alpha,\rho,

\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
and
n
, such that for every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and every
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,

T_{M}^{*}f(x,t)\leq C\left\{\left({\mathcal{M}}^{-}\circ{\mathcal{M}}\right)(T_{(-M,M)}^{\alpha}f)(x,t)+\left({\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{-}\circ{\mathcal{M}}_{q}\right)f(x,t)\right\}.

For the proof of this theorem we shall need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
\rho
-lacunary sequence and
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then

(i)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=m}^{M}v_{j}\left(\frac{a_{j+1}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j+1}^{2}/(4s)}-a_{j}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j}^{2}/(4s)}}{s^{1+\alpha}}\right)\right|\leq{{C_{\rho,v,\alpha}}\over a_{m}^{2}},

(ii)

if
k\geq m
and
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
|t-s|\geq ca_{k}^{2}(c>0)
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}v_{j}\frac{a_{j+1}^{2\alpha}e^{-{a_{j+1}^{2}\over 4(t-s)}}-a_{j}^{2\alpha}e^{-{a_{j}^{2}\over 4(t-s)}}}{(t-s)^{1+\alpha}}\right|\,\leq C_{\rho,v,\alpha}\frac{1}{a_{k}^{2}}\rho^{-2\alpha(k-m+1)}.

Proof.

For
(i)
, since
\rho\leq\frac{a_{j+1}}{a_{j}}\leq\rho^{2},
we have

\left|\sum_{j=m}^{M}v_{j}\left(\frac{a_{j+1}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j+1}^{2}/(4s)}-a_{j}^{2\alpha}e^{-a_{j}^{2}/(4s)}}{s^{1+\alpha}}\right)\right|\leq\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\sum_{j=m}^{M}\left({1\over a_{j+1}^{2}}+{1\over a_{j}^{2}}\right)\\
\leq C_{v,\alpha}{1\over a_{m}^{2}}\sum_{j=m}^{M}{\frac{\rho^{2}+1}{\rho^{2}}}\cdot{a_{m}^{2}\over a_{j}^{2}}=C_{\rho,v,\alpha}{1\over a_{m}^{2}}\sum_{j=m}^{M}\left|a_{m}\over a_{j}\right|^{2}\leq C_{\rho,v,\alpha}{1\over a_{m}^{2}}{\rho^{2}\over\rho^{2}-1}\leq C_{\rho,v,\alpha}{1\over a_{m}^{2}}.

Now we shall prove
(ii)
. By the mean value theorem, there exist
\xi_{j}
with
a_{j}\leq\xi_{j}\leq a_{j+1}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}v_{j}\frac{a_{j+1}^{2\alpha}e^{-{a_{j+1}^{2}\over 4(t-s)}}-a_{j}^{2\alpha}e^{-{a_{j}^{2}\over 4(t-s)}}}{(t-s)^{1+\alpha}}\right|=\left|\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}v_{j}\frac{(a_{j+1}-a_{j})2\xi_{j}^{2\alpha-1}e^{-{\xi_{j}^{2}\over 4(t-s)}}\left(\alpha-\frac{\xi_{j}^{2}}{4(t-s)}\right)}{(t-s)^{1+\alpha}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\left(\left|\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}\frac{(a_{j+1}-a_{j})2\alpha\xi_{j}^{2\alpha-1}e^{-{\xi_{j}^{2}\over 4(t-s)}}}{(t-s)^{1+\alpha}}\right|+\left|\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}\frac{(a_{j+1}-a_{j})2\xi_{j}^{2\alpha+1}e^{-{\xi_{j}^{2}\over 4(t-s)}}}{(t-s)^{2+\alpha}}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rho,v,\alpha}\left(\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}{\frac{a_{j}^{2\alpha}}{\left|t-s\right|^{1+\alpha}}}+\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}{\frac{a_{j}^{2\alpha+2}}{\left|t-s\right|^{2+\alpha}}}\right)\leq C_{\rho,v,\alpha}{1\over a_{k}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}\left|\frac{a_{j}}{a_{k}}\right|^{2\alpha}+\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}\left|\frac{a_{j}}{a_{k}}\right|^{2\alpha+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rho,v,\alpha}{1\over a_{k}^{2}}\rho^{-2\alpha(k-m+1)},

where we have used that
k\geq m.

∎

With Lemma

, we can give the proof of Theorem

.

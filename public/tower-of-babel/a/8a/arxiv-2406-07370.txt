Operad of posets 101: The Wixarika posets
José Antonio Arciniega-Nevárez 1, Marko Berghoff 2, Eric Rubiel Dolores-Cuenca 3

Abstract
. We study objects whose definition is linked to partial orders. An algebra over the operad of posets is a set whose endomorphisms contain the endomorphisms of posets. Notable examples of such algebras include the zeta values, order polytopes, and shuffle series. The language of operads provides us with tools to better understand the combinatorics of these objects. In these notes we describe a non-trivial example of a suboperad “The Wixarika posets”, alongside its associated algebras. This example is rich enough to showcase the particularities of the field, without delving into technicalities. An old version of these notes were used to give a minicourse in Govt. College University Lahore in Pakistan 2022, and at the BIRS-CMO workshop “6th meeting of the Mexican Mathematicians in the World” 2024.



1.
Introduction

Given a partially ordered set
X
, R. Stanley
[
Sta70
]
defined the strict and non-strict
n
-order polynomials of
X
,
\Omega^{\circ}(n)
and
\Omega(n)
, as the number of strict and non-strict order preserving maps from
X
to the chain
\langle n\rangle=\{1<\ldots<n
}. Work by D. G. Wagner
[
Wag92
]
described how order polynomials behave under the operations of concatenation and disjoint union of (series-parallel) posets.

Given a poset
X
, instead of its order polynomials, we consider the generating functions of the sequences
(\Omega^{\circ}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\Omega(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,

\mathfrak{Z}\left(X\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Omega^{\circ}(n)x^{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\left(X\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Omega(n)x^{n}.

We call them the (strict and non-strict) order series of
X
.
In the case of chains
\langle n\rangle
of length
n
one finds

\mathfrak{Z}\left(n\right):=\mathfrak{Z}\left(\langle n\rangle\right)=\frac{x^{n}}{(1-x)^{n+1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\left(n\right)=\frac{x}{(1-x)^{n+1}}.

Let
P(X)
denote the order polytope of
X
.
The non-strict order series
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\left(X\right)
of
X
and the Ehrhart series
\frac{h^{*}(x)}{(1-x)^{|X|+1}}
of
P(X)
(see e.g.
[
BS18
]
) are related by

\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\left(X\right)=x\frac{h^{*}(x)}{(1-x)^{|X|+1}}.

This means that one could study order series (and therefore also order polynomials) through
P(X)
.

We take here a different point of view. We argue that working directly with order series is advantageous as it highlights and clarifies symmetries in an underlying operadic algebra structure (outlined below).

The following two results are well-known (see
[
FS86
]
,

Wag92
]
,

Bra06
]
,

Lemma 1
.

If we denote the concatenation of posets
X,Y
by
X*Y
, and the disjoint union of chains
\langle k\rangle,\langle m\rangle
by
\langle k\rangle\sqcup\langle m\rangle
, then

\mathfrak{Z}\left(X*Y\right)=\mathfrak{Z}\left(X\right)(1-x)\mathfrak{Z}\left(Y\right),

\mathfrak{Z}\left({\langle k\rangle}\sqcup{\langle m\rangle}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}{m+n\choose k}{k\choose n}\mathfrak{Z}\left(m+n\right).

In the paper
[
ANBDC23
]
we prove the previous statement using exact sequences of sets.
Note that posets with order preserving maps are examples of Alexandrov spaces with continuous functions. This characterization allows to adapt techniques of homological algebra, such as exact sequences, to study posets and order polynomials.

Consider the operation

D(X)=\langle 1\rangle*\big(\langle 1\rangle\sqcup X\big)*\langle 1\rangle,

geometrically, we are adding a new minimum
x_{0}
and a new maximum
x_{1}
to
X
and we attach a handle on these points, that is, we add a third element
y
with
x_{0}<y<x_{1}
. If the input of
D
is an
n
-chain, we compute:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Z}\left(D({\langle n\rangle})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Z}\left(1\right)*\mathfrak{Z}\left(\langle 1\rangle\sqcup\langle n\rangle\right)*\mathfrak{Z}\left(1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{(1-x)^{2}}*(n\frac{x^{n}}{(1-x)^{n+1}}+(n+1)\frac{x^{n+1}}{(1-x)^{n+2}})*\frac{x}{(1-x)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\frac{x^{n+2}}{(1-x)^{n+3}}+(n+1)\frac{x^{n+3}}{(1-x)^{n+4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\mathfrak{Z}\left(n+2\right)+(n+1)\mathfrak{Z}\left(n+3\right).

We consider the free operad of sets
\mathcal{P}
, generated by the unary operation
D
and the associative binary operation
*
. For an introduction to operads see
[
Nao20
,
Cha07
,
LV12
,
HM22
,
Sta04
]
.

A
Wixarika poset
is an element of the algebra over the operad
\mathcal{P}
that is generated by the poset
\langle 1\rangle
. In other words, a Wixarika poset is a finite poset obtained from the chain
\langle 1\rangle
by iterated application of the operations
D
and
*
.

The Hasse diagrams of these posets resemble Wixárika collars, or Huichol collars, which are intricate colorful necklaces crafted by the Wixárika community in North America. An example is given in Figure
1
(in this paper we depict Hasse diagrams ordered from left to right; if there is an edge between two vertices
x,y
and
x
is on the left of
y
then
x<y
).

Let
X
be a Wixarika poset. In this paper we

•

relate the coefficients of
\mathfrak{Z}\left(X\right)
to topological invariants of the Hasse diagram of
X
and to combinatorial information of the order polytope of
X
(Section
3
),

•

describe an algorithm that decides whether a given
f
is the order series of a Wixarika poset (Section
3
),

•

construct an example of an algebra over the operad
\mathcal{P}
from number theory (Section
4
).

2.
An algorithm to compute the order series

To compute the order series, we follow
[
FS86
]
but we work at the level of power series instead of order polynomials. The elements of the operad
\mathcal{P}
can be described by binary rooted trees, where vertices with two incoming edges and one outgoing edge are labeled by
*
, and vertices with one incoming edge and one outgoing edge are labeled by
D
.
The action of the operad is given by replacing all the leaves of the tree by Wixarika posets.

Now consider the
\mathcal{P}
-homomorphism between the algebra over the operad
\mathcal{P}
generated by
\langle 1\rangle

and the algebra over
\mathcal{P}
generated by
\mathfrak{Z}\left(1\right)
. The morphism substitutes any input leaf by the power series associated to its poset. Here the action of
*,D
on power series is defined by

\mathfrak{Z}\left(P\right)*\mathfrak{Z}\left(Q\right):=\mathfrak{Z}\left(P*Q\right),D(\mathfrak{Z}\left(P\right)):=\mathfrak{Z}\left(D(P)\right).

Unraveling the definitions we can show linearity of
D
:

D(a\mathfrak{Z}\left(P\right)+b\mathfrak{Z}\left(Q\right))=aD(\mathfrak{Z}\left(P\right))+bD(\mathfrak{Z}\left(Q\right)).

Assume that the order series of Wixarika posets are drops of water on the leaves associated to an operation in
\mathcal{P}
. Going from top to bottom, the drops fall along the tree. Whenever a drop reaches a point labeled with an operation, we evaluate the corresponding operation and the output becomes the output drop. Continue until we reach the root. This gives the evaluation of the tree on the input posets.

For example, consider Figure
1
. On the left side we have at the top three posets
\langle 1\rangle
. Below is a sequence of transformations using
D
and
*
. At the bottom we have the final result, a Wixarika poset.
On the right side we have the corresponding tree of operations in which the leaves should be thought of as decorated by the poset
\langle 1\rangle
. The vertices with two incoming edges and one outgoing edge are labeled with
*
and the vertices with one incoming edge and one outgoing edge (represented as a circle) are labeled with
D
.

Figure 1.
To the left bottom we have a Wixarika poset, and above it a decomposition in terms of
*,D
. To the right we have the tree of operations.

The first step of our algorithm, replacement of the leaves
\langle 1\rangle
by their order series
\mathfrak{Z}\left(1\right)=\frac{x}{(1-x)^{2}}
, followed by evaluation of the operations, returns the power series

D(D(D(D(1)*\mathfrak{Z}\left(1\right)*D(1)))*\mathfrak{Z}\left(3\right))

=D(D(D((\mathfrak{Z}\left(3\right)+2\mathfrak{Z}\left(4\right))*\mathfrak{Z}\left(1\right)*(\mathfrak{Z}\left(3\right)+2\mathfrak{Z}\left(4\right))))*\mathfrak{Z}\left(3\right))

=D(D(D(\mathfrak{Z}\left(7\right)+4\mathfrak{Z}\left(8\right)+4\mathfrak{Z}\left(9\right)))*\mathfrak{Z}\left(3\right))

=D(D(D(\mathfrak{Z}\left(7\right)))*\mathfrak{Z}\left(3\right))+4D(D(D(\mathfrak{Z}\left(8\right)))*\mathfrak{Z}\left(3\right))

+4D(D(D(\mathfrak{Z}\left(9\right)))*\mathfrak{Z}\left(3\right))

Following the same procedure we obtain

=882\frac{x^{16}}{(1-x)^{17}}+7995\frac{x^{17}}{(1-x)^{18}}+27232\frac{x^{18}}{(1-x)^{19}}+

+43792\frac{x^{19}}{(1-x)^{20}}+33552\frac{x^{20}}{(1-x)^{21}}+9880\frac{x^{21}}{(1-x)^{22}}.

As a second step, to compute the order polynomial
\Omega^{\circ}(m)
, we use the expression
\frac{x^{n}}{(1-x)^{n+1}}=\sum_{m\geq n}{m\choose n}x^{m}
to extract from the order series the term of degree
x^{m}
. Because our work focuses on studying operations that preserve the number of labeling maps of posets, we know that the coefficient of
x^{m}
is the number of labelings of the poset using numbers from
1
to
m
.

In the previous example, for any
m
the number of labeling maps of the poset is given by

882{m\choose 16}+7995{m\choose 17}+27232{m\choose 18}+43792{m\choose 19}+33552{m\choose 20}+9880{m\choose 21}.

3.
Representability

We say a function
f(x)
is
represented
if there is a Wixarika poset
X
such that
f(x)=\mathfrak{Z}\left(X\right)
.

In order to decide whether a function can be represented, we determine topological invariants of a poset
X
from its associated power series
\mathfrak{Z}\left(X\right)
, such as the Betti number of the Hasse diagram of
X
(i.e., the number of independent cycles in the underlying undirected graph). With that information we describe an algorithm that
determines if
f(x)
can be represented. If this is the case, then the algorithm explicitly constructs all possible Wixarika posets
X
so that
f(x)=\mathfrak{Z}\left(X\right)
.

The following theorem states that the coefficients of the order series associated to a Wixarika poset are topological invariants of its Hasse diagram.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be a Wixarika poset. Consider its factorization, that is, the associated word
w
in the characters
*
and
D
. Let
f(x)=w(\mathfrak{Z}\left(1\right))
be the power series obtained by replacing every leaf of
w
by
\mathfrak{Z}\left(1\right)
.

(1)

The series
f(x)
admits a unique description

f(x)=d_{i}\mathfrak{Z}\left(i\right)+d_{i+1}\mathfrak{Z}\left(i+1\right)+\cdots+d_{k}\mathfrak{Z}\left(k\right),

where the coefficients
d_{j}
are non-negative integers.

(2)

The number
i
is the number of points in a maximal chain in
X
.

(3)

The number
k
is the number of points in
X
.

(4)

The difference
d=k-i
is the first Betti number of the Hasse diagram of
X
, equal to the number of times
D
occurs in
w
.

(5)

The difference
m=i-2d-1
is the number of times
*
occurs in
w
.

(6)

The term
m+1
is the number of leaves in the tree
w
.

(7)

\sum_{u=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-u}d_{u}=1

Sketch of the proof.

One shows that every Wixarika poset has a unique minimum and a unique maximum.
Then all statements follow from Lemma
1
by describing the possible values obtained under the operations
D,*
. The explicit description of
D
and
*
leads to the positivity of the coefficients.

Consider, for example, point 4: We know that by applying
D
we increase the first Betti number of the Hasse diagram of a poset by 1.
The first Betti number of a concatenation is the sum of the first Betti numbers of each element, because Wixarika posets have a unique maximum and a unique minimum. Since Wixarika posets are generated by
\langle 1\rangle
, and the Betti number of
\langle 1\rangle
is zero, it follows that the only way to increase the Betti number of a poset is by the operation
D
or concatenating the poset with another poset with non-zero Betti number.

The last point is obtained by considering the order polytope of the poset. We compare the order series of the poset with the Ehrhart series of the order polytope using

\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\left(X\right)=x\frac{h^{*}(x)}{(1-x)^{|X|+1}},

then the sum
\sum_{u=1}(-1)^{k-u}a_{u}
coincides with
h^{*}_{0}=1
.

For details see
[
ANBDC23
, Proposition 3.1]
.
∎

The paper
[
BHK18
]
calls the elements
\{\mathfrak{Z}\left(i\right)\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
the
chain basis
.
The coefficients of order series in the chain basis satify the following positivity property:

Theorem 2
.

Consider
\mathfrak{Z}\left(X\right)=\sum_{i=j_{0}}^{|X|}d_{i}\mathfrak{Z}\left(i\right),d_{i}\geq 0
. The order polytope of
{X}
is the union of
d_{|X|}
copies of the
|X|
- simplex.
The remaining coefficients
d_{i}
of
\mathfrak{Z}\left(X\right)=\sum d_{i}\mathfrak{Z}\left(i\right)
count the number of
i
-simplices that occur, in the canonical triangulation of the order polytope, as intersection of the
d_{|X|}
copies of those
|X|
-simplices.

Proof.

See
[
ANBDC23
, Proposition 3.6]
.
∎

The coefficients of power series associated to more general posets, for instance series-parallel posets (which are generated by concatenation and disjoint union), are not topological invariants. For example, the poset
\langle 1\rangle*(\langle 1\rangle\bigsqcup\langle 1\rangle)*\langle 1\rangle
has first Betti number
1
while the poset

\langle 2\rangle*(\langle 1\rangle\bigsqcup\langle 1\rangle)
has first Betti number
0
. Their order series, however, are the same.

In the other direction we have the following statement.

Theorem 3
.

Given a finite sum
f(x)=\sum d_{j}\mathfrak{Z}\left(j\right)
where
d_{j}
are non-negative integers, there is an algorithm to determine if
f(x)=\mathfrak{Z}\left(X\right)
for some Wixarika poset
X
. In the positive case the algorithm returns all possible posets representing
f(x)
.

Sketch of the proof.

We first verify that the function
f(x)
satisfy the requirements of
[
Hig
]
as well as Theorem
2
.
Then we restrict the search space using Theorem
1
.
Using colored operads, it is possible to reduce the search to a set
C
of representatives of equivalence classes of trees up to associativity. Now for every representative
P\in C
we first evaluate the first and last term of
\mathfrak{Z}\left(P\right)=b_{i}\zeta[i]+\cdots+b_{k}\zeta[k]
. If
b_{i}=d_{i},b_{k}=d_{k}
then we compute all terms
{\{b_{j}\}}_{i<j<k}
. The evaluation can be done with the tree of operations
[
FS86
]
but working with power series instead of polynomials. If we find a poset that produces
f(x)
, we then use
[
HPPW18
]
to find the remaining
Doppelgängers
, that is, posets that have the same power series
f(x)
. After this step we stop the algorithm. For details see
[
ANBDC23
, Proposition 3.2]
.
∎

4.
Algebras over the operad of Wixárika posets

The lexicographic sum of posets
[
Sch16
,
Tro92
]
associates to a poset
P
with
n
points a function
Poset^{\times n}\rightarrow Poset
. Informally, given
P=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n},\leq_{P})
, and
n
posets
\{O_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
, the lexicographic sum
P(O_{1},\cdots,O_{n})
of
\{O_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
along the poset
P
is the result of the following construction. One replaces the
i
-th point
p_{i}
by the
i
-th poset
O_{i}
. If two points belong to the same
O_{i}
we use the order of
O_{i}
. A point
x\in O_{i}
and a point
y\in O_{j}
are related by
x\leq y
if
p_{i}<_{P}p_{j}
.

Given two finite posets
X,Y
, the lexicographic sum of
X,Y
along
\{x,y\}
is
X\sqcup Y
, the lexicographic sum of
X,Y
along
\{x<y\}
is the concatenation
X\ast Y
, and the lexicographic sum of
(\langle 1\rangle,X,\langle 1\rangle,\langle 1\rangle,)
along
\{x,y,z|x<y<z,x<w<z\}
is
D(X)
.

There is a standard operad associated to any set
A
, the operad of endomorphisms
End_{A}
, with
End_{A}(n)=\{f:A^{n}\rightarrow A\}
. Then
Posets\subset End_{Posets}
via the lexicographic sum, since if
\mathcal{P}\subset Posets
, then we obtain
\mathcal{P}\subset End_{Posets}
.

The language of operads allows us to study sets in which Wixárika posets act via endomorphisms. An algebra over the operad of Wixárika posets is a set
A
together with maps
\mathcal{P}\rightarrow End_{A}
.

Order series (and order polynomials) are the most famous example of such algebras. If we consider
\mathfrak{Z}\left(\right):\text{Posets}\mapsto\text{Order series}
, then the formulas

\mathfrak{Z}\left(X\right)*\mathfrak{Z}\left(Y\right)=\mathfrak{Z}\left(X*Y\right)
and
\mathfrak{Z}\left(D(X)\right)=D\mathfrak{Z}\left(X\right)
show that
\mathfrak{Z}\left(\right)
is a
\mathcal{P}
-homomorphisms from posets to order series as algebras over the operad of Wixárika posets.

Another example of an algebra over the operad
\mathcal{P}
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}
that contains certain linear combinations of zeta values. We denote by
\zeta(i)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{i}}
the Riemann zeta function.

Theorem 4
.

For any finite poset
P
consider its vectors
(d_{P,1},\cdots,d_{P,|P|})
defined by
\mathfrak{Z}\left(P\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{|P|}d_{P,i}\mathfrak{Z}\left(i\right)
.

Then the set

\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{|P|}(-1)^{|P|-i}d_{P,i}(\zeta(i+1)-1-\frac{1}{2^{i+1}})\Big\}_{P\in\text{Posets}}

admits the structure of an algebra over the operad
\mathcal{P}
.

For the proof see
[
DcMC24
, Corollary 5.1]
. We list the works of several mathematicians that culminated in a link between posets and zeta values:

•

[
Eul55
]
: Introduction of Eulerian polynomial (the order polynomial associated to
\sqcup_{n}\langle 1\rangle
).

•

[
Ber85
]
Ramanujan(1920): An identity of rational zeta series (to the order series
\mathfrak{Z}\left(\sqcup_{n}\{x\}\right)
Ramanujan associates a series with zeta values).

•

[
Ehr62
]
: The work of Ehrhart relating integer polytopes with power series.

•

[
Đok64
]
: A general correspondance between some power series and rational zeta series.

•

[
Sta70
]
: The introduction of the order polynomial.

•

[
WZ03
]
: A chain of maps from posets to order polytopes, from order polytopes to order polinomials, and from order polynomials to rational numbers.

•

[
DcMC24
]
: A composition of
\mathcal{P}
-homomorphisms among algebras over the operad
\mathcal{P}
:
From posets to order polytopes, from order polytopes to order series, and from order series to zeta values.

As a corollary of Theorem
4
, there is a Hadamard product which is well-defined on a family of rational zeta series (the action of
\{x,y\}
on the left side of
[
DcMC24
, Eq. (18)]
).

We conclude this section by asking when an arbitrary number of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(\zeta(i+1)-1-\frac{1}{x^{i+1}})
is an element of this algebra? Since the
\mathcal{P}
-homomorphisms are surjective, we can apply Theorem
3
to test if an element belongs to the algebra over
\mathcal{P}
.

5.
Remarks

Category theory was invented to describe natural transformations, to compare common structures on different fields. The prologue of
[
BLL97
]
invites the combinatorics community to work with the abstract language of categories.

To motivate this community, which is already familiar with order series and Ehrhart theory, the preprint
[
ADCS23
]
solves a combinatorial problem using the structure of an algebra over the operad of posets: The problem is to enumerate shuffles between a deck and a tree. Here by shuffle we mean a generalization of the (riffle) shuffle of two decks of cards. The study of shuffles between a deck of cards and a tree was originated in the field of dendroidal homotopy theory
[
HM22
]
.

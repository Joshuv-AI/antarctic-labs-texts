Uniform convergence of optimal order under a balanced norm of a local discontinuous Galerkin method on a Shishkin mesh
Jin Zhang, Wenchao Zheng

Abstract
For singularly perturbed reaction-diffusion problems in 1D and 2D, we study a local discontinuous Galerkin (LDG) method on a Shishkin mesh. In these cases, the standard energy norm is too weak to capture adequately the behavior of the boundary layers that appear in the solutions. To deal with this deficiency, we introduce a balanced norm stronger than the energy norm. In order to achieve optimal convergence under the balanced norm in one-dimensional case, we design novel numerical fluxes and propose a special interpolation that consists of a Gauss-Radau projection and a local L 2 L^{2} projection. Moreover, we generalize the numerical fluxes and interpolation, and extend convergence analysis of optimal order from 1D to 2D. Finally, numerical experiments are presented to confirm the theoretical results.

Introduction

Singularly perturbed problems appear widely in many fields, such as fluid mechanics, electronic science, and energy development
[
1
,
10
]
. The typical characteristic of their solutions is the presence of layers.
To fully resolve layers and obtain uniform convergence with respect to singular perturbation parameters, layer-adapted meshes have been introduced since 1960s
[
13
]
and have been an active research field
[
6
,
16
]
.
Among them, the Shishkin meshes have been widely used and analyzed
[
9
,
14
,
17
]
, because it has a very simple structure.

On Shishkin meshes, standard numerical methods such as finite element method and finite difference method have been well analyzed
[
15
]
. These methods cannot compute the gradient of the solution accurately. However, in many physical applications, the gradient
is also very important because it is related to some important physical quantities, such as surface friction and temperature and so on. To meet this requirement, the local discontinuous Galerkin (LDG) method
[
4
]
is a good candidate.
The LDG method, as a kind of discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method, inherits many advantages of the DG method
[
3
]
, such as good stability, high-order accuracy and flexibility on
hp
adaptivity. These advantages make the LDG method more attractive to deal with those solutions with layers.
In recent years, the LDG method has been used to singularly perturbed problems; see
[
11
,
12
,
18
,
19
]
.

In this manuscript, we focus on singularly perturbed reaction-diffusion equations. For these problems, Mei et al.
[
8
]
and Cheng
[
2
]
have analyzed uniform convergence under an energy norm and a balanced norm, respectively. It should be mentioned that the balanced norm is more appropriate for layers than the energy norm.
In
[
2
]
, the authors proved a uniform convergence of optimal order if the smooth part of the solution belongs to the finite element space. However,
this condition is too strong to hold in general.

We aim to design a LDG method, which does not have the above drawback.
For this aim, we have carefully designed a new numerical flux, which is the key to our LDG method. Besides, we propose a special interpolation that consists of a Gauss-Radau projection and a local
L^{2}
projection. On the basis of the numerical fluxes and the interpolation, we prove that the LDG solution is convergent under the balanced norm with optimal order. Furthermore, we extend the LDG method and its convergence analysis from 1D to 2D.

The content of this paper is divided into the following parts. In Section 2, we prove the uniform convergence of the LDG method for a one-dimensional singularly perturbed problem. Firstly, we design a specific numerical flux and introduce a LDG method. Then the Shishkin mesh is defined and interpolations are presented. Finally, we get the optimal convergence order under the balanced norm. In Section 3, we prove the uniform convergence of the LDG method for a two-dimensional singularly perturbed problem, and the content distribution is similar to that in Section 2. In Section 4, we present numerical experiments to support the theoretical results. Throughout this article,

\varepsilon
and
N

\varepsilon
is a perturbation parameter and
N

LDG method for one-dimensional case

We consider the following one-dimensional singularly perturbed problem:

-\varepsilon u^{\prime\prime}+b(x)u=f(x),\quad\text{$x\in\Omega=(0,1)$},\quad u(0)=u(1)=0,

(1)

where
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
is a perturbation parameter,
b(x)\geqslant\beta^{2}\geq 0

f(x)
are sufficiently smooth functions on
\bar{\Omega}
. And the solution of problem
(
1

O(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ln(1/\varepsilon))
at
x=0

2.1
LDG method

We divide the domain
\Omega=(0,1)
into
N
cells:

0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\ldots<x_{N-1}<x_{N}=1.

Denote an arbitrary subinterval
[x_{j-1},x_{j}]
by
I_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,N
, and denote the step size of the subinterval as
h_{j}=x_{j}-x_{j-1}
. The discontinuous finite element space
V_{N}
is defined as

V_{N}=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):\text{$v|_{I_{j}}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{j}),1\leq j\leq N$ }\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{j})
is the space of polynomials of degree at most
k\geq 1
on
I_{j}
.
For
v\in V_{N}
, we define
v^{\pm}_{j}=\lim_{x\to x^{\pm}_{j}}v(x)
. Define a jump as:
[[v]]_{j}=v^{-}_{j}-v^{+}_{j}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,N-1
,
[[v]]_{0}=-v^{+}_{0}
and
[[v]]_{N}=v^{-}_{N}
.

Next, we will introduce the LDG method for problem (
1
). First, one has

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}q=u^{\prime},\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

\displaystyle-q^{\prime}+b(x)u=f(x),\quad\text{in $\Omega$}.

Define
\chi=(r,v)\in V_{N}\times V_{N}
to be any test function. Let
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{I_{j}}
be the inner product in
L^{2}(I_{j})
.
Find the numerical solution
W=(Q,U)\in V_{N}\times V_{N}
such that the following variational forms hold in each element
I_{j}
,

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\left<Q,r\right>_{I_{j}}+\left<U,r^{\prime}\right>_{I_{j}}-\hat{U}_{j}r^{-}_{j}+\hat{U}_{j-1}r^{+}_{j-1}=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\left<Q,v^{\prime}\right>_{I_{j}}+\left<bU,v\right>_{I_{j}}-\hat{Q}_{j}v^{-}_{j}+\hat{Q}_{j-1}v^{+}_{j-1}=\left<f,v\right>_{I_{j}},

where the numerical fluxes
\hat{Q}
and
\hat{U}
are defined by

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{j}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &Q^{+}_{0}+\lambda_{0}U^{+}_{0},\quad&&\text{$j=0$},\\
&Q^{+}_{j},\quad&&\text{$j=1,2,\ldots,N-1$},\\
&Q^{-}_{N}-\lambda_{N}U^{-}_{N},\quad&&\text{$j=N$},\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{j}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,\quad&&\text{$j=0,N$},\\
&U_{\frac{3}{4}N}^{-}+\lambda_{q}[[Q]]_{\frac{3}{4}N},\quad&&\text{$j=\frac{3}{4}N$},\\
&U^{-}_{j},\quad&&\text{$j=1,2,\ldots,\frac{3}{4}N-1,\frac{3}{4}N+1,\ldots,N-1$},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

with the stabilization parameters
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{N}=\sqrt{\varepsilon}
and
\lambda_{q}=1/\sqrt{\varepsilon}
.

The definition of the numerical flux

We
denote

(3)

\displaystyle-(Qv)_{N}^{-}+\sum_{j\in\{0,N\}}\lambda_{j}[[u]]_{j}[[v]]_{j}+\lambda_{q}[[Q]]_{3N/4}[[r]]_{3N/4}.

Regularity of the solution

Lemma 1
.

(Referene
[

) For

2.3
Shishkin mesh

In this section, we will introduce a Shishkin mesh.
Let
N\geq 4
be a multiple of 4
and the transition parameter be

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{\sigma\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ln N}{\beta}\leq\frac{1}{4},

where
\sigma\geq k+1
is a user-chosen parameter.
Divide
[\tau,1-\tau]
into
N/2
equidistant subintervals. Furthermore, there are N/4 subintervals evenly distributed in both the intervals
[0,\tau]
and
[1-\tau,1]
.
Then, the mesh points are given by

x_{j}=\left\{\begin{split}&4\frac{\sigma\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{\beta}t_{j}\ln N,\quad&&\text{for $j=0,1,\ldots,\frac{N}{4}$},\\
&\tau+2(1-2\tau)(t_{j}-\frac{1}{4}),&&\text{for $j=\frac{N}{4}+1,\ldots,\frac{3N}{4}$},\\
&1-4\frac{\sigma\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{\beta}(1-t_{j})\ln N,\quad&&\text{for $j=\frac{3N}{4}+1,\ldots,N$},\end{split}\right.

(6)

where
t_{j}\equiv\frac{j}{N}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,N
.
On the Shishkin mesh (
6
), for
1\leq j\leq N
, we have

C\sqrt{\varepsilon}N^{-1}\leq h_{j}\leq CN^{-1}.

2.4
Projections

In this section, we combine the local

1.

Local
L^{2}
projection
\pi
. For any
z\in L^{2}({\Omega})
,
\pi z\in V_{N}
is defined as:

\displaystyle\left<\pi z,v\right>_{I_{j}}=\left<z,v\right>_{I_{j}},\quad\forall v\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{j}),

for each element
I_{j}=(x_{j-1},x_{j})
,
j=1,2,\ldots,N
.

2.

Gauss-Radau projection
\pi^{\pm}
. For any
z\in H^{1}(I_{j})
,
\pi^{\pm}z\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{j})
is defined as:

\displaystyle\left<\pi^{\pm}z,v\right>_{I_{j}}=\left<z,v\right>_{I_{j}},\quad\forall v\in\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(I_{j}),

\displaystyle(\pi^{+}z)^{+}_{j-1}=z^{+}_{j-1},\quad(\pi^{-}z)^{-}_{j}=z^{-}_{j},

for each element
I_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,N
.

The proof of the existence and uniqueness of Gauss-Radau projection is referred to reference
[
20
]
.

For the analysis of the article, we define the following interpolations:

\displaystyle P^{-}_{N}u|_{I_{j}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\pi^{-}u,\quad&&\text{$j=1,2,\ldots,N/4,3N/4+1,\ldots,N-1$},\\
&\pi u,\quad&&\text{$j=N/4+1,\ldots,3N/4,N$},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle P^{+}_{N}q|_{I_{j}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\pi^{+}q,\quad&&\text{$j=2,3,\ldots,N$},\\
&\pi q,\quad&&\text{$j=1$}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Let

and
||v||_{I_{j}}^{2}=\left<v,v\right>_{I_{j}}
.

2.5
Convergence analysis

Recall
{w}=(q,u)
to be the exact solution of the problem (
1
).
From (
3
), we define an energy norm by

|||w|||^{2}_{E}=B(w;w)=\varepsilon^{-1}||q||^{2}+||b(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}u||^{2}+\sum_{j\in\{0,N\}}\lambda_{j}[[u]]_{j}^{2}+\lambda_{q}[[q]]^{2}_{\frac{3N}{4}}.

(11)

We introduce a more powerful balanced norm based on this energy norm, which is defined by

|||w|||^{2}_{B}=\varepsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}||q||^{2}+||b(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}u||^{2}+\sum_{j\in\{0,N\}}[[u]]_{j}^{2}+[[q]]^{2}_{\frac{3N}{4}}.

(12)

Theorem 1
.

Assume
\varepsilon\leq CN^{-1}
. On the Shishkin mesh (
6
), we take
\sigma\geq k+1
. Let
{W}=(Q,U)\in V_{N}\times V_{N}
be the numerical solution of the LDG scheme (
2
). Let
{w}=(q,u)
be the exact solution of the problem (
1
) satisfying Lemma
1
. Define
{e=w-W}
. There is a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|||e|||_{E}\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-k}+N^{-(k+1)}\right),

\displaystyle|||e|||_{B}\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-k}+(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}\right).

Proof.

We use interpolation
P^{+}_{N}q
and
P^{-}_{N}u
to divide the error
e
into two parts:
{e}=(q-Q,u-U)=\mathbb{\eta-\xi}
with

\displaystyle\mathbb{\eta}=(\eta_{q},\eta_{u})=(q-P^{+}_{N}q,u-P^{-}_{N}u),

\displaystyle\mathbb{\xi}=(\xi_{q},\xi_{u})=(Q-P^{+}_{N}q,U-P^{-}_{N}u)\in V_{N}\times V_{N}.

Owing to the consistency of numerical fluxes and the regularity assumptions of
u
and
q
, one has the error equation

B(\xi;\chi)=B(\eta;\chi),\quad\forall\chi=(r,v)\in V_{N}\times V_{N}.

(13)

Set
\chi=\xi
in (
13
). According to the integration by parts, we derive

B(\xi;\xi)=|||\xi|||_{E}^{2}.

(14)

Furthermore, by (
3
), we can get

B(\eta;\xi)\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{8}S_{i}.

Here,

\displaystyle S_{1}=\varepsilon^{-1}\left<\eta_{q},\xi_{q}\right>,\quad

\displaystyle S_{2}=\left<\eta_{u},\xi^{\prime}_{q}\right>,

\displaystyle S_{3}=\left<\eta_{q},\xi^{\prime}_{u}\right>,\quad

\displaystyle S_{4}=\left<b\eta_{u},\xi_{u}\right>,

\displaystyle S_{5}=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(\eta_{q})^{+}_{j}[[\xi_{u}]]_{j}-(\eta_{q})^{-}_{N}(\xi_{u})^{-}_{N},\quad

\displaystyle S_{6}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(\eta_{u})^{-}_{j}[[\xi_{q}]]_{j},

\displaystyle S_{7}=\lambda_{q}[[\eta_{q}]]_{3N/4}[[\xi_{q}]]_{3N/4},\quad

\displaystyle S_{8}=\sum_{j\in\{0,N\}}\lambda_{j}[[\eta_{u}]]_{j}[[\xi_{u}]]_{j}.

Then, we will analyze them separately.

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
8
), one has

\displaystyle S_{1}\leq C(\varepsilon^{-1/2}||\eta_{q}||)(\varepsilon^{-1/2}||\xi_{q}||)\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}|||\xi|||_{E}.

Owing to the orthogonality of the projection, we have
S_{2}=S_{3}=0
.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
9
), we have

\displaystyle S_{4}

\displaystyle=\left<b\eta_{u},\xi_{u}\right>_{(0,x_{N/4})\cup(x_{3N/4+1},x_{N-1})}

\displaystyle\leq C||\eta_{u}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ln N)^{1/2}|||\xi|||_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}(\ln N)^{1/2}N^{-(k+1)}|||\xi|||_{E}.

From the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (
10
) and
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{N}=\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, we can get

\displaystyle S_{5}

\displaystyle=(\eta_{q})^{+}_{0}(\xi_{u})^{+}_{0}-(\eta_{q})^{-}_{N}(\xi_{u})^{-}_{N}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\sum_{j\in\{0,N\}}\lambda_{j}^{-1}[[\eta_{q}]]^{2}_{j}\right]^{1/2}\left[\sum_{j\in\{0,N\}}\lambda_{j}[[\xi_{u}]]^{2}_{j}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/4}||\eta_{q}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|||\xi|||_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-(k+1)}|||\xi|||_{E}.

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality, (
9
),
\varepsilon\leq CN^{-1}
and
\lambda_{q}=\varepsilon^{-1/2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{6}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=N/4+1}^{3N/4-1}(\eta_{u})^{-}_{j}[[\xi_{q}]]_{j}+(\eta_{u})^{-}_{3N/4}[[\xi_{q}]]_{3N/4}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=N/4+1}^{3N/4-1}||\eta_{u}||_{L^{\infty}(I_{j})}h_{j}^{-1/2}||\xi_{q}||_{I_{j}\cup I_{j+1}}+C\lambda_{q}^{-1/2}||\eta_{u}||_{L^{\infty}(3N/4)}\left[\lambda_{q}[[\xi_{q}]]^{2}_{3N/4}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-k}|||\xi|||_{E}+C\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-(k+1)}|||\xi|||_{E}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (
10
) and
\lambda_{q}=\varepsilon^{-1/2}
, one has

\displaystyle S_{7}\leq C\left[\lambda_{q}[[\eta_{q}]]^{2}_{3N/4}\right]^{1/2}\left[\lambda_{q}[[\xi_{q}]]^{2}_{3N/4}\right]^{1/2}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-(k+1)}|||\xi|||_{E}.

From the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (
9
) and
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{N}=\varepsilon^{1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle S_{8}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\{0,N\}}\lambda_{j}[[\eta_{u}]]_{j}[[\xi_{u}]]_{j}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\sum_{j\in\{0,N\}}\lambda_{j}[[\eta_{u}]]^{2}_{j}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{j\in\{0,N\}}\lambda_{j}[[\xi_{u}]]^{2}_{j}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}||\eta_{u}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|||\xi|||_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-(k+1)}|||\xi|||_{E}.

We gather all the above estimates and get

\displaystyle B(\eta;\xi)\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}|||\xi|||_{E}.

Considering (
13
) and (

), we have

|||\xi|||^{2}_{E}=B(\xi;\xi)=B(\eta;\xi)\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-k}\right)|||\xi|||_{E}.

(15)

Therefore, it is straightforward to derive

\displaystyle|||\xi|||_{E}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+C\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-k},

(16)

\displaystyle|||\xi|||_{B}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/4}|||\xi|||_{E}\leq C(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+C\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-k}.

(17)

From Lemma
2
and the definition of norms in

and

, we can get

\displaystyle|||\eta|||_{E}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+CN^{-(k+1)},

(18)

\displaystyle|||\eta|||_{B}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+C(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}.

(19)

Combining the results of (

3
LDG method for two-dimensional case

We consider a two-dimensional singularly perturbed problem:

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\Delta u+b(x,y)u=f(x,y),\quad\text{in $\Omega=(0,1)^{2}$},

(20)

\displaystyle u(0)=0,\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}.

where
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
is a perturbation parameter,
b(x,y)\geqslant 2\beta^{2}>0
and
f(x,y)
are sufficient smooth functions on
\bar{\Omega}
. And it is well-known that the problem has a unique solution, which is characterized by the presence of boundary layers of width
O(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ln(1/\varepsilon))
along the entire boundary
\partial\Omega
.

3.1
LDG method

The two-dimensional layer-adapted meshes
\{(x_{i},y_{j}),i,j=1,\ldots,N\}
is constrcuted by the tensor-product of the one-dimensional layer-adapted meshes in the horizontal and vertical directions, where
x_{i}
is defined in (
6
) and
y_{j}
is defined similarly.

Define
I_{i}=(x_{i-1},x_{i})
and
J_{j}=(y_{j-1},y_{j})
for
i,j=1,\ldots,N
. We set
h_{i}=x_{i}-x_{i-1}
and
h_{j}=y_{j}-y_{j-1}
. Besides, let

\Omega_{N}=\{\text{$K_{ij}$: $K_{ij}=I_{i}\times J_{j}$ for $i,j=1,\ldots,N$}\}

be a rectangle partition of
\Omega
. Then the finite element space is defined as:

R_{N}=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):\text{$v|_{K_{ij}}\in\mathbb{Q}_{k}(K_{ij})$ },K_{ij}\in\Omega_{N}\},

where
\mathbb{Q}_{k}(K_{ij})
represents the space of polynomials on
K_{ij}
with a maximum degree of
k
in each variable. For
v\in R_{N}
and
y\in J_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,N
, we use
v^{\pm}_{i,y}=\lim_{x\to x^{\pm}_{i}}v(x,y)
and
v^{\pm}_{x,j}=\lim_{y\to y^{\pm}_{j}}v(x,y)
to express the traces evaluated from the four directions.
Define the jumps on the vertical and horizontal edges:

\displaystyle[[v]]_{i,y}=v^{-}_{i,y}-v^{+}_{i,y}\text{ for $i=1,2,\ldots,N-1$},\;

\displaystyle[[v]]_{0,y}=-v^{+}_{0,y},\;

\displaystyle[[v]]_{N,y}=v^{-}_{N,y}.

\displaystyle[[v]]_{x,j}=v^{-}_{x,j}-v^{+}_{x,j}\text{ for $j=1,2,\ldots,N-1$},\;

\displaystyle[[v]]_{x,0}=-v^{+}_{x,0},\;

\displaystyle[[v]]_{x,N}=v^{-}_{x,N}.

Next, we will introduce the LDG method for problem (
20
). First, one has

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}p=u_{x},\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}q=u_{y},\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

\displaystyle-p_{x}-q_{y}+bu=f,\quad\text{in $\Omega$}.

Define
Z=(v,s,r)\in R_{N}\times R_{N}\times R_{N}
to be any test function. Let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{D}
be the
L^{2}
inner product in the domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{I}

Find the numerical solution

(21)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}(Q,r)_{K_{ij}}+(U,r_{y})_{K_{ij}}-\left<\hat{U}_{x,j},r^{-}_{x,j}\right>_{I_{i}}+\left<\hat{U}_{x,j-1},r^{+}_{x,j-1}\right>_{I_{i}}=0,

\displaystyle(P,v_{x})_{K_{ij}}-\left<\hat{P}_{i,y},v^{-}_{i,y}\right>_{J_{j}}+\left<\hat{P}_{i-1,y},v^{+}_{i-1,y}\right>_{J_{j}}+(Q,v_{y})_{K_{ij}}-\left<\hat{Q}_{x,j},v^{-}_{x,j}\right>_{I_{i}}

For

\displaystyle(bU,v)+\varepsilon^{-1}(P,s)+\varepsilon^{-1}(Q,r)

,

3.4
Convergence analysis

Recall
t=(u,p,q)
to be the exact solution of the problem (
20
).
From (
22
), we define an energy norm:

\displaystyle|||t|||^{2}_{E}=B(t;t)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-1}||p||^{2}+\varepsilon^{-1}||q||^{2}+||b(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}u||^{2}

(28)

\displaystyle+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\sum_{i\in\{0,N\}}(\lambda_{i,y},[[u]]_{i,y}^{2})_{J_{j}}+\sum^{N}_{i=1}\sum_{j\in\{0,N\}}(\lambda_{x,j},[[u]]_{x,j}^{2})_{I_{i}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\lambda_{P},[[p]]^{2}_{{\frac{3N}{4}},y})_{J_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\lambda_{Q},[[q]]^{2}_{x,{\frac{3N}{4}}})_{I_{i}}.

And the balanced norm is defined as:

\displaystyle|||t|||^{2}_{B}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}||p||^{2}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}||q||^{2}+||b(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}u||^{2}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\sum_{i\in\{0,N\}}(1,[[u]]_{i,y}^{2})_{J_{j}}

(29)

\displaystyle+\sum^{N}_{i=1}\sum_{j\in\{0,N\}}(1,[[u]]_{x,j}^{2})_{I_{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\lambda_{P},[[p]]^{2}_{{\frac{3N}{4}},y})_{J_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\lambda_{Q},[[q]]^{2}_{x,{\frac{3N}{4}}})_{I_{i}}.

Theorem 2
.

Assume
\varepsilon\leq CN^{-1}
.
On the two-dimensional layer-adapted meshes
\{(x_{i},y_{j}),i,j=1,\ldots,N\}
, we take
\sigma\geq k+1
. Let
T=(U,P,Q)\in R_{N}\times R_{N}\times R_{N}
be the numerical solution of the LDG scheme (
21
). Let
t=(u,p,q)
be the exact solution of the problem (
20
) satisfying Lemma
3
. Define
{e=t-T}
. Besides, there is a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|||e|||_{E}\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-k}+N^{-(k+1)}\right),

\displaystyle|||e|||_{B}\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-k}+(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}\right).

Proof.

We use interpolation
P^{-}u
,
P^{+}_{x}p
and
P^{+}_{y}q
to divide the error into two parts:
{e=t-T}={\eta-\xi}
with

\displaystyle\mathbb{\eta}=(\eta_{u},\eta_{p},\eta_{q})=(u-P^{-}u,p-P^{+}_{x}p,q-P^{+}_{y}q),

\displaystyle\mathbb{\xi}=(\xi_{u},\xi_{p},\xi_{q})=(U-P^{-}u,P-P^{+}_{x}p,Q-P^{+}_{y}q)\in R_{N}\times R_{N}\times R_{N}.

Owing to the consistency of numerical fluxes and the smooth assumptions of
u
,
p
and
q
, we can obtain the error equation

B(\xi;Z)=B(\eta;Z),\quad\forall Z=(u,p,q)\in R_{N}\times R_{N}\times R_{N}.

(30)

Set
Z=\xi
in (
30
). According to the integration by parts, we derive

B(\xi;\xi)=|||\xi|||_{E}^{2}.

(31)

Furthermore, by (
22
), we can get

B(\eta;{Z})\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{15}S_{i},

where

\displaystyle S_{1}=(b\eta_{u},\xi_{u}),\quad S_{2}=\varepsilon^{-1}(\eta_{p},\xi_{p}),

\displaystyle S_{3}=(\eta_{u},\xi_{p,x}),\quad S_{4}=(\eta_{p},\xi_{u,x}),

\displaystyle S_{5}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left<(\eta_{u})^{-}_{i,y},[[\xi_{p}]]_{i,y}\right>_{J_{j}},

\displaystyle S_{6}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left<(\eta_{p})^{+}_{i,y},[[\xi_{u}]]_{i,y}\right>_{J_{j}}-\left<(\eta_{p})^{-}_{N,y},[[\xi_{u}]]_{N,y}\right>_{J_{j}}\right\},

\displaystyle S_{7}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{i\in\{0,N\}}\left<\lambda_{i,y}[[\eta_{u}]]_{i,y},[[\xi_{u}]]_{i,y}\right>_{J_{j}},

\displaystyle S_{8}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left<\lambda_{P}[[\eta_{p}]]_{3N/4,y},[[\xi_{p}]]_{3N/4,y}\right>_{J_{j}}.

Here, we only prove the convergence analysis for the part of
p
, because the remaining proof for
q
is similar to
p
. Furthermore, we will analyze the above parts separately.

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (
24
) and (
25
), one has

\displaystyle S_{1}\leq C||b^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta_{u}||||b^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{u}||\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}(\ln N)^{1/2}N^{-(k+1)}|||\xi|||_{E}.

\displaystyle S_{2}\leq(\varepsilon^{-1/2}||\eta_{p}||)(\varepsilon^{-1/2}||\xi_{p}||)\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}|||\xi|||_{E}.

Owing to the orthogonality of the projection, we have
S_{3}=S_{4}=0
.

Let
\Omega_{c}=\Omega-\left\{\left((0,x_{N/4})\times(y_{N/4},y_{3N/4})\right)\cup\left((x_{3N/4},x_{N-1})\times(y_{N/4},y_{3N/4})\right)\right\}
. From the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality, (
26
),
\varepsilon\leq CN^{-1}
and
\lambda_{P}=\varepsilon^{-1/2}
, we can get

\displaystyle S_{5}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{K_{ij}\in\Omega_{c},i\neq 3N/4}||(\eta_{u})^{-}_{i,y}||_{k_{ij}}||[[\xi_{p}]]_{i,y}||_{k_{ij}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left<(\eta_{u})^{-}_{3N/4,y},[[\xi_{p}]]_{3N/4,y}\right>_{J_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{j}^{1/2}h_{j}^{1/2}(h_{i}h_{j})^{-1/2}||\eta_{u}||_{L^{\infty}}N||\xi_{p}||+C(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{p}^{-1}h_{j}||(\eta_{u})||_{L^{\infty}(k_{ij})}^{2})^{1/2}|||\xi|||_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-k}|||\xi|||_{E}+C\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-(k+1)}|||\xi|||_{E}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (
27
), (
26
) and
\lambda_{0,y}=\lambda_{N,y}=\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, one has

\displaystyle S_{6}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-(k+1)}|||\xi|||_{E}.

\displaystyle S_{7}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-(k+1)}|||\xi|||_{E}.

From the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
\lambda_{P}=\varepsilon^{-1/2}
and (
27
), we have

\displaystyle S_{8}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-(k+1)}|||\xi|||_{E}.

We gather all the above results and get

\displaystyle B(\eta;\xi)\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}|||\xi|||_{E}.

Considering (
30
) and (
31
), we have

|||\xi|||^{2}_{E}=B(\xi;\xi)=B(\eta;\xi)\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}|||\xi|||_{E}.

Therefore, it is straightforward to derive

\displaystyle|||\xi|||_{E}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+C\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-k},

(32)

\displaystyle|||\xi|||_{B}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/4}|||\xi|||_{E}\leq C(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+C\varepsilon^{1/4}N^{-k}.

(33)

From Lemma
4
and the definition of norms in
28
and
29
, we can get

\displaystyle|||\eta|||_{E}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+CN^{-(k+1)},

(34)

\displaystyle|||\eta|||_{B}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}+C(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}.

(35)

Combining the results of (
32
), (
33
), (
34
) and (
35
), we prove Theorem
2
.
∎

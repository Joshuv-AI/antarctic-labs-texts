A positive density of elliptic curves is diophantine stable in certain Galois Extensions
Anwesh Ray, Pratiksha Shingavekar

Abstract
. Let p ∈ { 3 , 5 } p\in\{3,5\} and consider a cyclic p p -extension L / ℚ L/\mathbb{Q} . We show that there exists an effective positive density of elliptic curves E E defined over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , ordered by height, that is diophantine stable in L L .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivation

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and
L
be a number field. Following Mazur and Rubin
[
17
]
,
E
is said to be
diophantine stable
if the natural inclusion induces an equality
E(L)=E(\mathbb{Q})
. Mazur and Rubin show that if all
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
-automorphisms of
E
are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then there exist infinitely many prime-power cyclic extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
in which
E
is diophantine stable. We refer to
[
17
, Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.3]
for further details.

Assume that
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois with cyclic Galois group
G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
. Then the Hasse-Weil
L
-function of
E
over
L
decomposes as follows
\mathscr{L}_{E}(s,L)=\prod_{\chi}\mathscr{L}_{E}(s,\chi)
,
where
\chi
ranges over all Dirichlet characters
\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widehat{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. The Birch and Swinnerton–Dyer conjecture predicts that

\operatorname{rank}E(L)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}\mathscr{L}_{E}(s,L)=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}\mathscr{L}_{E}(s,\chi).

There are heuristics for the growth and stability of ranks of elliptic curves
E
in cyclic extensions, which are based on the relationship between modular symbols and the values of
\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}\mathscr{L}_{E}(s,\chi)
above. For further details, we refer to
[
18
]
.

There has been an increasing interest in the study of diophantine stability and rank growth of a given elliptic curve in families of number field extensions, see for instance
[
16
,
15
,
13
,
22
,
1
,
2
]
. Moreover, the study of such rank stability questions has close connections to Hilbert’s tenth problem, see
[
14
]
. These connections have recently been studied with rejuvenated interest by Garcia–Fritz and Pasten
[
10
]
and Kundu–Lei–Sprung
[
12
]
.

1.2.
Main result

In this article we formulate a new direction of enquiry. Instead of fixing an elliptic curve and varying the number field extension in a family, we shall fix an extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
and count the number of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
, ordered by height, such that
E(L)=E(\mathbb{Q})
. The result is proven only for cyclic extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
with
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, where
p\in\{3,5\}
.

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.8
)
.

Let
p
be a prime in
\{3,5\}
and
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Take
Z
to denote the primes that ramify in
L
. Assume that

(1)

p\notin Z
and
2
splits completely in
L
,

(2)

for all
\ell\in Z
, there exists an elliptic curve
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
, such that
\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]=0
and
\mathbb{E}^{-1}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]=0
. Here,
\mathbb{E}^{-1}
is the quadratic twist of
\mathbb{E}
by
-1
.

Then there is a positive density of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
such that

E(L)=E(\mathbb{Q})=0.

Evidently, these elliptic curves are
diophantine stable
in
L
.

Remark 1.1
.

At this point, several remarks are in order.

•

The results rely on the techniques of Bhargava and Shankar
[
4
,
5
,
3
]
, who study the average size of the
p
-Selmer group of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
. This is only done for primes
p=2,3,5
, however, the case when
p=2
is omitted since our methods do not apply to this case.

•

The condition (2) can be dealt with via explicit calculation (cf. the code in Remark
4.7
). Indeed, when
p=5
and

is large enough, the condition (2) is shown to be be satisfied using results of Howe
[
11
]
. We refer to Lemma

for further details.

•

An explicit lower density is obtained for the curves

that are diophantine stable in

. This expression is quite fascinating and involves locally defined invariants associated to the extension

, see the statement of Theorem

for details. For instance, when
p=3
and
L\subset\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{31})
is the cubic subfield, the lower density of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
that are diophantine stable in

is

\geq\frac{1}{4}\times\frac{1}{2^{21}}\times\left(1-\frac{1}{3}\right)\times\frac{585}{961}\times\prod_{\ell\neq 2,3,31}\left(1-\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{3}}\right).

1.3.
Organization

Including the introduction, the article consists of four sections. In section
2
, we study the stability of Selmer groups in prime cyclic extensions. In greater detail, let
p
be an odd prime number and
L/K
be an extension with
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Suppose that the
p
-Selmer group of
E
over
K
is
0
. Then if certain additional local condition are satisfied for
E_{/K}
, then, it follows that the
p
-Selmer group of
E
over
L
is also
0
. The Galois cohomological arguments presented in this section are essentially due to Česnavičius
[
8
]
and Brau
[
6
]
. Section
3
is preparatory in nature, and delineates the strategy that we take to prove our main result. We introduce both local and global density conditions on our families of elliptic curves that will play a role in our sieve theoretic arguments. Section
4
is devoted to the proof of our main result. In this section, we describe a suitable large family of elliptic curves defined by local conditions and show that a positive proportion of elliptic curves in this family are indeed diophantine stable in
L
. The analysis leads to an explicit lower bound for the density of elliptic curves that are diophantine stable in our fixed extension
L
. This is precisely the step in which the aforementioned results of Bhargava and Shankar are applied.

Data availability

No data was analyzed in proving the results in the article.

2.
Selmer groups and diophantine stability

In this section, we review the necessary background and notation required for our examination of elliptic curves over number fields. Some of the results in this section extend arguments due to Česnavičius
[
8
]
and the unpublished work of Brau
[
6
]
. Throughout, we denote by
K
a number field and use
\Omega(K)
(resp.
\Omega_{\infty}(K)
) to represent the set of finite primes (resp. archimedean places) of
K
. Given a prime number
\ell
,
\Omega_{\ell}(K)
denotes the primes in
\Omega(K)
that lie above
\ell
. Let
\overline{K}
be an algebraic closure of
K
, and
\operatorname{G}_{K}
denote the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
. For each prime
v\in\Omega(K)
, we select an embedding
\iota_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{K}\hookrightarrow\overline{K}_{v}
. Setting
\operatorname{G}_{K_{v}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}_{v}/K_{v})
, the inclusion
\iota_{v}
induces a Galois group inclusion
\iota_{v}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{G}_{K_{v}}
.

2.1.
Selmer groups associated to elliptic curves

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
K
. The Mordell-Weil group of
E
over
K
, denoted
E(K)
, comprises the
K
-rational points on
E
. Choose an algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
, and let
E[p^{n}]
be the
p^{n}
-torsion subgroup of
E(\overline{K})
. Let
\operatorname{G}_{K}
denote the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
and consider the natural action of
\operatorname{G}_{K}
on
E[p^{n}]
. The Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{n}}(E/K)
can be interpreted as a certain subgroup of the cohomology group
H^{1}(K,E[p^{n}])
, which measures the obstructions to representing
K
-rational points of
E
using elements of
E[p^{n}]
. It is defined as the kernel to the natural restriction map in Galois cohomology

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{n}}(E/K)=\ker\left(H^{1}(K,E[p^{n}])\to\bigoplus_{v}H^{1}(K_{v},E)[p^{n}]\right),

where
v
runs over all primes of
K
.

The
p^{n}
-Selmer group is closely related to the Mordell–Weil group and Tate–Shafarevich group. Indeed, there is a natural short exact sequence that describes this relationship

0\rightarrow E(K)/p^{n}E(K)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{n}}(E/K)\rightarrow\Sh(E/K)[p^{n}]\rightarrow 0.

Thus in particular,
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{n}}(E/K)
is trivial if and only if

•

E(K)
has rank
0
,

•

E(K)[p]=0
,

•

\Sh(E/K)[p^{\infty}]=0
.

2.2.
Growth and stability of Selmer groups

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve and
K
be a number field. Let
p
be an odd prime number. Fix a finite Galois extension
L/K
for which
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
is a
p
-group and assume that
G\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Set
k_{v}
to denote the residue field of
K
at
v
. If
v
is a prime of good reduction, we set
\widetilde{E}(k_{v})
to be the group of
k_{v}
-rational points of the mod-
v
reduction of
E
. Let
c_{v}(E/K)
be the Tamagawa number of
E
at
v
.

Assumption 2.1
.

Throughout, we assume that the following conditions hold.

(1)

The Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/K)=0
. In particular, this implies that the rank of
E(K)
is
0
and both
E(K)
and
\Sh(E/K)
have no nontrivial
p
-torsion.

(2)

At all primes
v
of
K
that lie above
p
, assume that
E
has good reduction.

(3)

If
v\in\Omega_{p}(K)
is ramified in
L
, we additionally require that
E
has ordinary reduction at
v
and
\widetilde{E}(k_{v})[p]=0
.

(4)

For all primes
v\nmid p
that are ramified in
L
, we require that
E
has good reduction at
v
. Moreover, for such primes
v
, we have that
\widetilde{E}(k_{v})[p]=0
.

(5)

At all primes
v\nmid p
at which
E
has split multiplicative reduction, we require that
p\nmid c_{v}(E/K)
.

(6)

The primes
v
of
K
of additive reduction split completely in
L
.

Define
S
as the set of primes
v\in\Omega(K)
satisfying at least one of the following conditions:

•

v\in\Omega_{p}(K)
,

•

E
has bad reduction at
v
,

•

v
is ramified in
L
.

Let
S(L)
denote the set of primes
w\in\Omega(L)
that lie above some prime
v\in S
. The natural restriction map

\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/K)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)^{G}

fits within the following fundamental diagram

(2.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0=\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(K_{S}/K,E[p])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{v\in S}H^{1}(K_{v},E)[p]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)^{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(L_{S}/L,E[p])^{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\bigoplus_{w\in S(L)}H^{1}(L_{w},E)[p]\right)^{G}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{K}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}

Here,
\beta
, and
\gamma
are restriction maps on global and local cohomology respectively. Since it is assumed that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/K)=0
, it follows from the Cassels-Poitou-Tate long exact sequence that
\Phi_{K}
is surjective. For
v\in S
, the restriction map is denoted by

\gamma_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{1}(K_{v},E)[p]\rightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{w|v}H^{1}(L_{w},E)[p]\right)^{G},

where
w
runs through all primes of
L
above

. We note that
\gamma
is the direct sum
\bigoplus_{v\in S}\gamma_{v}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Consider a finite dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
V
and the finite
p
-group
G
acting on

. If
V^{G}=0
, then it follows that

.

Proof.

The result follows via an easy application of Nakayama’s lemma. For further details, cf.
[
19
, Lemma 4.2]
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

With respect to notation above, assume that
\gamma_{v}
is injective for all
v\in S
. Then it follows that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)=0
, and consequently, the rank of
E(L)
is also 0.

Proof.

From (
2.1
), we arrive at the following long exact sequence

(2.2)

0=\operatorname{ker}(\alpha)\rightarrow\operatorname{ker}(\beta)\rightarrow\operatorname{ker}(\gamma)\rightarrow\operatorname{coker}(\alpha)\rightarrow\operatorname{coker}(\beta)\rightarrow\operatorname{coker}(\gamma).

Since we assume that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/K)=0
, it follows that
E(K)[p]=0
. From the inflation-restriction sequence

0\rightarrow H^{1}(L/K,E(L)[p])\rightarrow H^{1}(K_{S}/K,E[p])\xrightarrow{\beta}H^{1}(K_{S}/L,E[p])^{G}\rightarrow H^{2}(L/K,E(L)[p])

it follows that
\beta
is an isomorphism. This implies that
\operatorname{ker}\gamma
is isomorphic to the
\operatorname{coker}\alpha
. Since the maps
\gamma_{v}
are injective, so is
\gamma
. Hence,
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)^{G}=0
. The result then follows from Lemma
2.2
, which asserts that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)=0
.
∎

We are now left with studying the maps
\gamma_{v}
for
v\in S
, and the conditions under which they are injective. We first deal with the case in which

. It follows from the Assumption

that either

•

is totally ramified in

and that

has good reduction at
v
,

•

v
is unramified in
L
and

has nonsplit multiplicative,

•

v
splits completely in

and

has additive reduction at

,

•

v
is unramified in

,

.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
v\nmid p
be a prime in

at which

has good reduction. Then

is injective.

Proof.

It follows from the definition of

that

must be totally ramified in

. Recall that Assumption

requires that

. The result then follows from

.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

be a prime in

has bad reduction. Then,

is injective.

First we consider the case in which

has nonsplit multiplicative reduction at

. On the other hand, consider the case in which

has split multiplicative reduction. Note that in light of Assumption

. Then, the result follows from

. Finally, consider the case in which

has additive reduction at

. In this case, it is assumed that

for any prime

is injective.

Next, we show that the maps
\gamma_{v}
are injective for all primes

.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

be a prime (in

), then the map

is injective.

It follows from Assumption

that

is unramified in

is injective by

.

•

Next consider the case when

is ramified in

.

Let

3.
Asymptotics for elliptic curves

In this section, we shall fix a prime
p\in\{3,5\}
and a
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
-extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
. Given an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
, it is well known that up to isomorphism
E
admits a short Weierstrass equation of the form

E_{A,B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B,

where
(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
are such that for all primes
\ell
, either
\ell^{4}\nmid A
or
\ell^{6}\nmid B
. Such an equation is
globally minimal
and the pair
(A,B)
is uniquely determined. The discriminant of
E_{A,B}
is given by

\Delta_{A,B}=\Delta(E_{A,B})=-16(4A^{3}+27B^{2}),

and the
j
-invariant
is

j_{A,B}=j(E_{A,B})=\frac{2^{8}3^{3}A^{3}}{4A^{3}+27B^{2}}.

We shall denote by
\mathcal{C}
the set of all pairs of integers
(A,B)
such that
\ell^{4}\nmid A
or
\ell^{6}\nmid B
for all primes
\ell
. The association taking
(A,B)
to the isomorphism class of
E_{A,B}
, gives a natural parametrization of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
.

This parametrization of elliptic curves allows us to formulate counting questions. Given an elliptic curve
E_{A,B}
the
naive height
of
E_{A,B}
is defined as follows

h_{A,B}=h\left(E_{A,B}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{max}\{|A|^{3},|B|^{2}\}.

The elliptic curves we shall consider shall be ordered according to their height. Let us explain this further. Let
X>0
be a real number and
\mathcal{C}(X)
to be the family of elliptic curves
E_{A,B}
with height at most
X
, i.e.,

\mathcal{C}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(A,B)\in\mathcal{C}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h\left(E_{A,B}\right)\leq X\}.

It is easy to see that the set
\mathcal{C}(X)
is finite, in fact Brumer proves a precise asymptotic.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(X)
be given as above. One has the following asymptotic estimate for the growth of
\#\mathcal{C}(X)
, as
X
goes to
\infty
:

\#\mathcal{C}(X)=\frac{4X^{5/6}}{\zeta(10)}+O\left(\sqrt{X}\right).

Proof.

For a proof of this result, see
[
7
, Lemma 4.3]
.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Any set of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
can be identified with a subset
S
of
\mathcal{C}
. Given
X>0
, set
S(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=S\cap\mathcal{C}(X)
. The density of
S
(if it exists) is defined as follows

(3.1)

\mathfrak{d}(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#S(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}.

When the limit in the above expression is replaced by
\limsup
(resp
\liminf
), we write
\overline{\mathfrak{d}}(S)
(resp.
\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(S)
) to denote the upper (resp. lower) density. The set
S
is said to have
positive density
if
\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(S)>0
.

Remark 3.3
.

It is worth noting that the density
\mathfrak{d}(S)
is a quantity that need not be defined when the limit (
3.1
) does not exist. However, the upper and lower densities are always well defined.

The rank of an elliptic curve is a fundamental and subtle invariant. It is natural to view the rank as a random function of the set of all elliptic curves and study its distribution. Katz and Sarnak modeled the distribution of ranks of elliptic curves and conjectured the following.

Conjecture 3.4

(Rank distribution conjecture)
.

The density of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with rank
0
(resp.
1
) is
1/2
(resp.
1/2
).

The conjecture thus also predicts that the density of elliptic curves with rank
\geq 2
is
0
. Significant progress was made in seminal work of Bhargava and Shankar
[
4
,
5
,
3
]
. The idea is to relate Selmer groups of elliptic curves to integral forms. The integral forms are lattice points in certain fundamental regions in Euclidean space and techniques from the geometry of numbers are applied to study their distribution. Given a prime
p
, it is expected that the average size of the
p
-Selmer group of
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
is
(p+1)
. This has been proven to be the case for primes
p\leq 5
. We refer to work of Poonen and Rains
[
21
]
for predictions on the average size of Selmer groups and Tate–Shafarevich groups. More recently, Smith has proven various distribution results for the
2
-primary Selmer groups of twist families of abelian varieties. For further details, we refer to
[
24
,
26
,
25
]
.

In this section, we fix a Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, where
p\in\{3,5\}
. Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve. Following Mazur and Rubin
[
17
]
,
E
is said to be
diophantine stable
in
L
if the natural inclusion yields an equality
E(\mathbb{Q})=E(L)
. We assume throughout that
2
and
p
are unramified in
L
, and show that there is an effective positive density of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
that are diophantine stable in
L
. This is done by showing that

•

the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which
\operatorname{E}(L)_{\operatorname{tors}}=0
has density
1
.

•

The set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which
\operatorname{rank}E(L)=0
has positive density.

It then follows that the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which both
E(\mathbb{Q})=E(L)=0
has positive density. We shall obtain an effective lower bound for the lower density of this set of elliptic curves.

3.1.
Controlling the torsion

Consider the set
S_{1}\subset\mathcal{C}
, of all isomorphism classes of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
such that
E(L)_{\operatorname{tors}}=0
. In this section, we show that
S_{1}
has density
1
, i.e.,

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#S_{1}(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}=1.

An elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
gives rise to a family of Galois representations. Choose an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
and set
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
. Given a natural number
n
, let
E[n]
be the
n
-torsion subgroup of
E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Note that
E[n]\simeq\left(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
and that there is a natural action of
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
on
E[n]
. The associated Galois representation

\rho_{E,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(E[n])\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})

encodes many arithmetic properties of
E
. Suppose that
E
does not have complex multiplication. Then, Serre’s Open image theorem implies that for all but finitely many primes
\ell
, the mod-
\ell
representation
\rho_{E,\ell}
is surjective. On the other hand, a prime
\ell
for which
\rho_{E,\ell}
fails to be surjective is called an
exceptional prime
. In this context, we set
S_{1}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{C}
to be the set of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves with no exceptional primes.

Theorem 3.5

(Duke
[
9
]
)
.

The set
S_{1}^{\prime}
has density
1
, i.e., almost all isomorphism classes of elliptic curves (over
\mathbb{Q}
) have no exceptional primes.

Thus, in order to show that
S_{1}
has density
1
, it suffices to show that
S_{1}^{\prime}
is contained in
S_{1}
. In other words, if an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
has the property that it has no exceptional primes, then, it follows that
E(L)_{\operatorname{tors}}=0
. This assertion follows from the following result.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
-extension (where
p=3
or
5
) and let
\ell
be any prime number. Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve for which the representation

\rho_{E,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})

is surjective. Then, we have that
E(L)[\ell]=0
.

Proof.

By way of contradiction, assume that
E(L)[\ell]\neq 0
. Thus,
\rho_{E,\ell}(\operatorname{G}_{L})
is contained in the mirabolic subgroup
M\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\ast\\
0&\ast\\
\end{array}}\right)\right\}
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
. In particular, we find that

[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\rho_{E,\ell}(\operatorname{G}_{L})]\geq[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M]=\frac{(\ell^{2}-\ell)(\ell^{2}-1)}{(\ell-1)\ell}=\ell^{2}-1.

On the other hand,

[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\rho_{E,\ell}(\operatorname{G}_{L})]=[\rho_{E,\ell}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\rho_{E,\ell}(\operatorname{G}_{L})]\leq[\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{L}]\leq 5.

Thus, we find that
\ell^{2}-1\leq 5
, i.e.,
\ell=2
.

Assume therefore that
\ell=2
. Note that
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\simeq S_{3}
, and
M\simeq C_{2}
. Since
\operatorname{G}_{L}
is a normal subgroup of
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, it follows that
C_{2}
is normal in
S_{3}
, which is a contradiction. Thus, we have shown that
E(L)[\ell]=0
.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

The set
S_{1}
has density
1
.

Proof.

Recall that Theorem
3.5
asserts that the density of the set
S_{1}^{\prime}
is
1
. Hence, to prove the result, it suffices to show that
S_{1}^{\prime}\subset S_{1}
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve in
S_{1}^{\prime}
. This means by definition, that for all primes
\ell
, the Galois representation
\rho_{E,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
is surjective. Then by Lemma
3.6
, we get that
E(L)[\ell]=0
for all primes
\ell
. This in turn implies that
E(L)_{\operatorname{tors}}=0
and hence
E\in S_{1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Controlling the rank

In this section, we take
S_{2}\subset\mathcal{C}
to consist of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\operatorname{rank}E(L)=0
. Given a pair
(\overline{A},\overline{B})\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{2}
with
4\overline{A}^{3}+27\overline{B}^{2}\neq 0
, let

E_{\overline{A},\overline{B}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+\overline{A}x+\overline{B}
. Let
\ell
be a prime that ramifies in
L
, then set
\mathfrak{A}_{\ell}
to denote the number of pairs
(\overline{A},\overline{B})\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{2}
such that

•

4\overline{A}^{3}+27\overline{B}^{2}\neq 0
,

•

E_{\overline{A},\overline{B}}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]=0
and
E_{\overline{A},-\overline{B}}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]=0
.

We show in this section that
S_{2}
has positive density, and note that the set
S_{1}\cap S_{2}
consists of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
such that
E(L)=0
. Note that in particular,
E(\mathbb{Q})=0
and
E(L)=0
, hence
E
is diophantine stable in the fixed extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
for all
E\in S_{1}\cap S_{2}
. Theorem
3.7
asserts that
S_{1}
has density
1
. Thus, if
S_{2}
has positive density, then so does
S_{1}\cap S_{2}
. This then yields the following result.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
p
be a prime in
\{3,5\}
and
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Take
Z
to denote the primes that ramify in
L
. Assume that

(1)

p\notin Z
and
2
splits completely in
L
,

(2)

for all
\ell\in Z
, there exists an elliptic curve
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
, such that
\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]=0
and
\mathbb{E}^{-1}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]=0
. Here,
\mathbb{E}^{-1}
is the quadratic twist of
\mathbb{E}
by
-1
.

Then there is a positive density of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
such that

E(L)=E(\mathbb{Q})=0.

Evidently, these elliptic curves are
diophantine stable
in
L
.
Moreover, the lower density of this set of elliptic curves is at least
\eta_{p}\prod_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}
, where

\eta_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}&\text{ if }p=3;\\
\frac{3}{8}&\text{ if }p=5;\end{cases}

and

\delta_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{3}&\text{ if }\ell\notin Z\cup\{2,p\}\text{ and }\ell=3;\\
1-\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{3}}&\text{ if }\ell\notin Z\cup\{2,p\}\text{ and }\ell\neq 3;\\
1-\frac{1}{\ell}&\text{ if }\ell=p;\\
\frac{1}{2^{21}}&\text{ if }\ell=2;\\
\frac{\#\mathfrak{A}_{\ell}}{\ell^{2}}&\text{ if }\ell\in Z.\end{cases}

We note that (2) is a condition on the prime
\ell
, and it is shown that when
p=5
and
\ell\geq 5779
(resp.
p=3
and
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{4}
), this condition is satisfied, see Lemma
4.6
. We shall also give explicit lower bounds for the density of the set of elliptic curves that are diophantine stable in
L
(in terms of
E
and the set of primes that ramify in
L
). We shall show that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)=0
for a positive density of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
. This is achieved by applying Proposition
2.7
by showing that Assumption
2.1
(for
K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}
and
p\in\{3,5\}
) is satisfied for a positive density of elliptic curves. Recall that
p
is unramified in
L
, and hence, Assumption
2.1
(3) is satisfied.

Definition 3.9
.

Given any prime
\ell
, let
\Sigma_{\ell}
be a choice of a closed subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{2}
defined by a congruence condition. In other words, there is a sufficiently large integer
n_{\ell}\geq 1
and
\overline{\Sigma}_{\ell}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n_{\ell}}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
such that
(A,B)\in\Sigma_{\ell}
if and only if

\left(A\pmod{\ell^{n_{\ell}}},B\pmod{\ell^{n_{\ell}}}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n_{\ell}}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}.

Let
E_{A,B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
be an elliptic curve such that either
\ell^{4}\nmid A
or
\ell^{6}\nmid B
. Assume that choice of
\Sigma_{\ell}
is made for every prime number. Then, we set
\Sigma
to denote the tuple of conditions
(\Sigma_{\ell})_{\ell}
, where
\ell
ranges over all prime numbers. We associate the family
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
of elliptic curves to the tuple
(\Sigma_{\ell})_{\ell}
, where
E_{A,B}\in\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
if
(A,B)\in\Sigma_{\ell}
for all
\ell
. The family
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
is then said to be defined by congruence conditions.

Consider the following example. Given a prime
\ell
, we let
\overline{\Pi}_{\ell}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{6}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
consist of all tuples
(\overline{A},\overline{B})
such that
\ell^{4}\nmid\overline{A}
and
\overline{B}\neq 0
. Let
\Pi_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{2}
be the set of all tuples
(A,B)
such that

\left(A\pmod{\ell^{6}},B\pmod{\ell^{6}}\right)\in\overline{\Pi}_{\ell}
.
Thus, the set

\Pi_{\ell}=\{(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{2}\mid\text{ either }\ell^{4}\nmid A\text{ or }\ell^{6}\nmid B\}

corresponds to the minimal Weierstrass models of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a family of elliptic curves defined by congruence conditions, then we denote by
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{F})
the subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{2}
defined by

\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{F})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(A,B)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{A,B}\in\mathcal{F}\}

and let
\big(\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{F})\big)_{\ell}
be its
\ell
-adic closure in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{2}
.

Definition 3.10
.

A family
\mathcal{F}
of elliptic curves defined by congruence conditions is said to be large if the set
\big(\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{F})\big)_{\ell}
contains all pairs
(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{2}
such that
\ell^{2}\nmid\Delta(E_{A,B})
, for all but finitely many primes
\ell
.

Recall that the
root number

\omega(E)
of an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
is the sign of the functional equation of the
L
-function
L(E,s)
of
E
. It is widely believed that when the elliptic curves are ordered by height, the root numbers
+1
and
-1
occur equally often. The following result of Bhargava and Shankar
[
5
, Theorem 41]
then gives the density of elliptic curves with rank
0
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
p
be a prime in
\{3,5\}
. Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a large family of elliptic curves such that exactly
50\%
of the curves in
\mathcal{F}
, when ordered by height, have root number
+1
. Then if
p=3
(resp.
p=5
) at least
25\%
(resp.
37.5\%
) of the curves
E_{A,B}
in
\mathcal{F}
, when ordered by height, have
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E_{A,B}/\mathbb{Q})=0
.

Proof.

The statement of the result above is a little stronger than that of
[
5
, Theorem 41]
(resp.
[
3
, Proposition 40]
) since it is asserted that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E_{A,B}/\mathbb{Q})=0
. The proof of the mentioned Theorems rely on showing that this Selmer group is indeed
0
for at least
25\%
(resp.
37.5\%
) of curves in
\mathcal{F}
. When
p=3
, we refer to p. 617, ll. 11-12 of
[
5
]
. On the other hand, for
p=5
, see
[
3
, p.29, l. -7]
.
∎

For an elliptic curve
E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E_{A,B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
with
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}
, denote by
E^{-1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E^{-1}_{A,B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax-B
the twist by
-1
of
E_{A,B}
. Note that
\Delta(E^{-1})=\Delta(E)
and
j(E^{-1})=j(E)
.

The global root number is a product of local root numbers

\omega(E)=-\prod_{\ell}\omega_{\ell}(E).

The local root number at a prime
\ell
of multiplicative reduction is easy to describe. One has that

(3.2)

\omega_{\ell}(E)=\begin{cases}+1&\text{ if }E\text{ has good reduction or non-split multiplicative reduction at }\ell;\\
-1&\text{ if }E\text{ has split multiplicative reduction at }\ell.\end{cases}

There is a simple criterion for this at a prime
\ell\geq 3
of multiplicative reduction. The elliptic curve
E=E_{A,B}
has split multiplicative reduction precisely when
\left(\frac{6B}{\ell}\right)=1
(see
[
27
, Lemma 10]
). One has that
\left(\frac{-1}{\ell}\right)=1
if and only if
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Therefore, we deduce that for any prime
\ell\geq 3
at which
E
has multiplicative reduction,

(3.3)

\omega_{\ell}(E^{-1})=\omega_{\ell}(E)\Leftrightarrow\ell\equiv 1\pmod{4}.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
Z
be the set of primes
\ell
of
\mathbb{Q}
that ramify in

. Note that

by assumption. Also, let

be the family of elliptic curves defined by the following conditions

(2)

(3)

Let

Both

.

.

It follows from the conditions above that if

Let

Lemma 3.15
.

For
E\in\mathcal{E}
, we have that
\omega(E^{-1})=-\omega(E)
.

Proof.

Recall that
\Delta(E^{-1})=\Delta(E)
and
j(E^{-1})=j(E)
. Recall also that the root number
\omega(E)=-\prod_{\ell}\omega_{\ell}(E)
. We study the relationship between
\omega_{\ell}(E^{-1})
and
\omega_{\ell}(E)
for all primes
\ell
.

•

We begin with primes
\ell
not dividing
\Delta(E)
. Since
\Delta(E^{-1})=\Delta(E)
, both
E
and
E^{-1}
have good reduction at
\ell
. Thus,
\omega_{\ell}(E^{-1})=\omega_{\ell}(E)=1
.

•

Next, let
\ell\neq 2
be a prime dividing
\Delta(E)
. Recall that condition (3) in Definition
3.12
requires that
\Delta^{\prime}(E)
is squarefree. In particular,
\ell^{2}\nmid\Delta(E)
, and thus,
E
has multiplicative reduction at
\ell
. According to (
3.3
) we have that

\omega_{\ell}(E^{-1})=\omega_{\ell}(E)\text{ if and only if }\ell\equiv 1\pmod{4}.

Since it is assumed that
\Delta^{\prime}(E)\equiv 1\pmod{4}
(by part (3) of Definition
3.12
), we must have that the number of primes
\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4}
dividing
\Delta(E)
is even. Hence,
\omega_{\ell}(E^{-1})=-\omega_{\ell}(E)
for an even number of primes
\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4}
. Thus, we find that

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\neq 2,\\
\ell|\Delta(E)\end{subarray}}\omega_{\ell}(E^{-1})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\neq 2,\\
\ell|\Delta(E)\end{subarray}}\omega_{\ell}(E).

•

Finally, let us consider
\ell=2
. By part (4) of Definition
3.12
,
E
has additive reduction at
2
and
2\nmid j(E)
. It follows from
[
27
, Lemma 12]
that
\omega_{2}(E^{-1})=-\omega_{2}(E)
.

It follows from the analysis above that for
E\in\mathcal{E}
,

\begin{split}\omega(E^{-1})=&-\prod_{\ell}\omega_{\ell}(E^{-1})\\
=&-\prod_{\ell\nmid\Delta(E)}\omega_{\ell}(E^{-1})\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\neq 2,\\
\ell|\Delta(E)\end{subarray}}\omega_{\ell}(E^{-1})\times\omega_{2}(E^{-1})\\
=&-\prod_{\ell\nmid\Delta(E)}\omega_{\ell}(E)\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\neq 2,\\
\ell|\Delta(E)\end{subarray}}\omega_{\ell}(E^{-1})\times\left(-\omega_{2}(E^{-1})\right)\\
=&-\omega(E).\end{split}

∎

Recall that the

E
, denoted

, is defined to be
\operatorname{max}\{|A|^{3},B^{2}\}
. Thus,

remains unaltered under the twist by

. Hence, Lemma

implies that exactly

of the curves

\mathcal{E}
have root number

, when ordered by height. We note that there is a natural short exact sequence

and thus
\mathcal{E}^{0}
precisely consists of elliptic curves

for which

(1)

\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})=0
,

(2)

,

.

4.1.
A suitable large family of elliptic curves

We wish to show that the family

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{C}
consist of all elliptic curves

is a minimal pair such that

Lemma 4.2
.

With respect to notation above,
\mathcal{F}
is a subset of
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

It is clear from the definition of
\mathcal{F}
that the conditions (1) and (2) in Definition

\Delta^{\prime}(E)
is clearly squarefree for

•

E
and

•

•

and

and is congruent to

.

Thus, we find that
\mathcal{F}=\cap_{\ell}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, where
\ell
ranges over all prime numbers. It is clear from our definition that for
\ell\neq 2
, the elliptic curves
E\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
are minimal at
\ell
. On the other hand, for
\ell=2
, minimality at
2
follows from Tate’s algorithm. Thus, we find that
\mathcal{F}
consists of elliptic curves that are globally minimal (i.e., minimal at all primes
\ell
). In other words,
\mathcal{F}
is a subset of
\mathcal{C}
. We wish to compute the density of
\mathcal{F}
. This is done in 2 steps.

(1)

Given
X>0
, we set
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{(A,B)\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\mid\operatorname{max}\{|A|^{3},B^{2}\}\leq X\right\}
. We note that not all pairs
(A,B)\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
are minimal, but the pairs
(A,B)\in\mathcal{F}
are. We derive an asymptotic formula for
\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(X)
as
X\rightarrow\infty
.

(2)

Next, we show that the asymptotic formulae for
\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(X)
can be used to give an asymptotic formula for
\#\mathcal{F}(X)
. Using this formula, we derive an expression for the density of
\mathcal{F}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is contained in
\mathcal{E}
, this gives us lower bound for the lower density of
\mathcal{E}
.

In order to complete step (1) in an effective manner, we introduce the residue classes
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n_{\ell}})^{2}
associated to the local condition
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
for all primes. We then set
\delta_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}}{\ell^{2n_{\ell}}}
. The asymptotic for
\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(X)
is then given by

\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(X)\sim\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}}{\ell^{2n_{\ell}}}\times\#\{(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid\operatorname{max}\{|A|^{3},B^{2}\}\leq X\}\sim 4\delta_{\ell}X^{5/6}.

Definition 4.3
.

The set
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}
is defined as follows.

•

For
\ell\notin T
, we set
n_{\ell}=2
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
to consist of pairs
(\overline{A},\overline{B})
for which
4\overline{A}^{3}+27\overline{B}^{2}\neq 0
.

•

For
\ell\in Z
, take
n_{\ell}=1
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}
consist of
(\overline{A},\overline{B})
such that
4\overline{A}^{3}+27\overline{B}^{2}\neq 0
and
E_{\overline{A},\pm\overline{B}}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]=0
.

•

For
\ell=p
, take
n_{\ell}=1
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}
consist of
(\overline{A},\overline{B})
such that
4\overline{A}^{3}+27\overline{B}^{2}\neq 0
.

•

For
\ell=2
, we set
n_{\ell}=12
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/2^{12}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
to consist of pairs
(\overline{A},\overline{B})
where
(\overline{A},\overline{B})=(756,\pm 16)
.

For primes
\ell\notin Z
, we can calculate
\delta_{\ell}
explicitly.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\ell
be a prime, we find that
\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(X)\sim 4\delta_{\ell}X^{5/6}
, where

\delta_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{3}&\text{ if }\ell\notin T\text{ and }\ell=3;\\
1-\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{3}}&\text{ if }\ell\notin T\text{ and }\ell\neq 3;\\
1-\frac{1}{\ell}&\text{ if }\ell=p;\\
\frac{1}{2^{21}}&\text{ if }\ell=2.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Each of these conditions is described by congruence conditions on
(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. In other words, there is an integer
n_{\ell}>0
and a set of congruence classes

\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n_{\ell}})^{2}

such that
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
consists of all pairs
(A,B)
such that

(A\pmod{\ell^{n_{\ell}}},B\pmod{\ell^{n_{\ell}}})\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}.

It is thus clear that

\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(X)\sim\left(\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}}{\ell^{2n_{\ell}}}\right)\times 4X^{5/6}.

We count
\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}
in all cases.

•

First consider
\ell\notin T
. Recall that for
\ell\notin T
,
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
consists of all pairs
(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
\ell^{2}\nmid\Delta_{A,B}
. Thus,
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}
consists of all pairs
(\overline{A},\overline{B})\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2})^{2}
such that
-4\overline{A}^{3}\neq 27\overline{B}^{2}
. Note that
\ell\neq 2
since
2\in T
. First assume that
\ell=3
(this case potentially arises when
p=5
). In this case, the condition becomes
3\nmid\overline{A}
, and thus,
\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}=2\times 3^{3}
and thus,

\left(\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}}{\ell^{2n_{\ell}}}\right)=\left(\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}}{\ell^{4}}\right)=\frac{2}{3}.

Next, we move on the case when
\ell\neq 3
. Set
(C,D)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-\overline{A}/3,\overline{B}/2)
and count the number of pairs for which
C^{3}=D^{2}
. We consider two further cases. In the first case,
\ell\nmid C
, we set
(U,V)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(C,D/U)
and the equation becomes
U=V^{2}
. Thus, in this case, there are
(\ell^{2}-\ell)
values of
V
and
U
is determined by
V
. On the other hand, if
\ell|C
then
\ell|D
as well and there are
\ell^{2}
choices in this case. Thus, we get

\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}=\ell^{4}-(\ell^{2}-\ell)-\ell^{2}=\ell^{4}-2\ell^{2}+\ell.

From the above, we find that

\left(\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}}{\ell^{2n_{\ell}}}\right)=\left(\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}}{\ell^{4}}\right)=1-\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{3}}.

•

Next, consider the case when
\ell=p
. In this case, the condition is that
\ell\nmid\Delta_{A,B}
. Arguing as we did in the previous case, it is easy to see that

\left(\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}}{\ell^{2n_{\ell}}}\right)=\left(\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}}{\ell^{2}}\right)=\frac{(\ell^{2}-\ell)}{\ell^{2}}=1-\frac{1}{\ell}.

•

Recall that when
\ell=2
, the condition required that
A=4A^{\prime}
and
B=16B^{\prime}
, where
A^{\prime}\equiv 189\pmod{256}
and
B^{\prime}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{256}
. Thus, we find that

\frac{\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}{\ell^{2n_{\ell}}}=\frac{1}{2^{21}}.

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
z\geq 5
be a real number
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\prime}
be the complement of
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. There is an absolute constant
C_{1}>0
(independent of
\ell
) such that for all large enough values of
X
,

\#\left(\bigcup_{\ell>z}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\prime}(X)\right)\leq\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{C_{1}}{\ell^{2}}X^{5/6}+o(X^{5/6}).

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that
\ell\geq 5
. We refer to the first part of the proof of Lemma
4.4
. Thus,
\mathcal{G}_{z}=\bigcup_{\ell>z}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\prime}
is the set of
(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
\ell^{2}|(4A^{3}+27B^{2})
for some prime
\ell>z
. We may write
\mathcal{G}_{z}=\mathcal{B}_{1}\cup\mathcal{B}_{2}\cup\mathcal{B}_{3}
, where

•

\mathcal{B}_{1}
consists of pairs
(A,B)
for which
\ell|A
and
\ell|B
for some prime
\ell>z
,

•

\mathcal{B}_{2}
consists of pairs for which
\ell\nmid A,B
and
-4A^{3}\equiv 27B^{2}\pmod{\ell^{2}}
, and
4A^{3}+27B^{2}\neq 0
for some prime
\ell>z
.

•

Finally, let
\mathcal{B}_{3}
consist of the pairs for which
4A^{3}+27B^{2}=0
.

It is clear that

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{B}_{1}(X)=&\{(A,B)\in\mathcal{B}_{1}\mid\operatorname{max}\{|A|^{3},B^{2}\}\leq X\}\\
\leq&\sum_{\ell>z}4\left\lfloor\frac{X^{1/3}}{\ell}\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\frac{X^{1/2}}{\ell}\right\rfloor\leq\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{4X^{5/6}}{\ell^{2}}.\end{split}

Now suppose that
(A,B)\in\mathcal{B}_{2}(X)
. Note that
|4A^{3}+27B^{2}|\leq 31X
is nonzero and is divisible by
\ell^{2}
for some
\ell>z
. Therefore, we find that
\ell\leq\sqrt{31X}
. For each
A
there are at most
2
residue classes of
B
modulo
\ell
which satisfy
-4A^{3}\equiv 27B^{2}\pmod{\ell}
. Each residue class
B\pmod{\ell}
lifts to a unique residue class modulo
\ell^{2}
that satisfies
-4A^{3}\equiv 27B^{2}\pmod{\ell^{2}}
, by Hensel’s lemma. This requires that
B\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
. The number of such
B
s in the interval
[-X^{1/2},X^{1/2}]
in a given residue class modulo
\ell^{2}
is
\frac{2X^{1/2}}{\ell^{2}}+O(1)
. Thus, in all,

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{B}_{2}(X)\leq&\sum_{z<\ell\leq\sqrt{31X}}2X^{1/3}\left(\frac{2X^{1/2}}{\ell^{2}}+O(1)\right),\\
\leq&\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{4X^{5/6}}{\ell^{2}}+O\left(X^{1/3}\pi(\sqrt{31X})\right),\\
=&\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{4X^{5/6}}{\ell^{2}}+O\left(\frac{X^{5/6}}{\log X}\right).\end{split}

Hence,
\#\mathcal{B}_{2}(X)\leq\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{4X^{5/6}}{\ell^{2}}+o\left(X^{5/6}\right)
. Finally, it is easy to see that
\#\mathcal{B}_{3}(X)=o(X^{5/6})
since the value of
A
determines
B
for
(A,B)\in\mathcal{B}_{3}(X)
. Thus, taking any constant
C_{1}\geq 8
will work.
∎

Next, we treat primes
\ell\in Z
, i.e., primes that ramify in
L
. By assumption,
\ell\notin\{2,p\}
. Recall that
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}
is the subset of
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{2}
consisting of pairs
(\overline{A},\overline{B})
such that

•

4\overline{A}^{3}+27\overline{B}^{2}\neq 0
,

•

E_{\overline{A},\overline{B}}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]=0
and
E_{\overline{A},-\overline{B}}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]=0
.

Note that

\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(X)\sim 4\delta_{\ell}X^{5/6},

where
\delta_{\ell}=\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}}{\ell^{2}}
. We estimate the size of
\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}
for
\ell\in Z
.

Lemma 4.6
.

With respect to notation above, the following assertions hold.

(1)

For
\ell\in Z
, suppose that
p=5
. Then for
\ell\geq 5779
, we have that

\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}\geq\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\ell}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{\ell}-76}{\sqrt{\ell}}\right).

(2)

Suppose that
p=3
and that the only primes
\ell
that ramify in
L
are of the form
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Then for all primes
\ell\geq 233
, we have that

\#\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}\geq\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\ell}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{\ell}-15.18}{\sqrt{\ell}}\right).

Proof.

The complement of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}
in
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{2}
can be expressed as a union
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}\cup\mathcal{A}_{3}
. Here,
\mathcal{A}_{1}
consists of pairs
(\overline{A},\overline{B})\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{2}
such that
\Delta_{\overline{A},\overline{B}}=0
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
(resp.
\mathcal{A}_{3}
) consists of pairs for which
E_{\overline{A},\overline{B}}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]\neq 0
(resp.
E_{\overline{A},-\overline{B}}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]\neq 0
). Thus, we find that

\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\geq\ell^{2}-\#\mathcal{A}_{1}-\#\mathcal{A}_{2}-\#\mathcal{A}_{3}.

It is clear that

\#\mathcal{A}_{2}=\#\mathcal{A}_{3}.

As was discussed in the proof of Lemma
4.4
, we have that
\#\mathcal{A}_{1}=\ell
. Then it follows from
[
11
]
that

\#\mathcal{A}_{2}\leq\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\ell}{p-1}\right)\left(1+2.53\frac{p(p+1)}{\sqrt{\ell}}\right).

Therefore, we have that

\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\geq\ell^{2}-\ell-2\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\ell}{p-1}\right)\left(1+2.53\frac{p(p+1)}{\sqrt{\ell}}\right).

Consider the case when
p=5
. In this case, we get

\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\geq\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\ell}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{\ell}-76}{\sqrt{\ell}}\right).

This bound is nontrivial when
\sqrt{\ell}>76
, i.e.,
\ell\geq 5779
.

On the other hand, consider the case when
p=3
and assume that all primes that ramify in
L
are
\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. In this case,
E_{\overline{A},\overline{B}}\simeq E_{\overline{A},-\overline{B}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
. Hence, we find that
\mathcal{A}_{2}=\mathcal{A}_{3}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}\cup\mathcal{A}_{3}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}
and we have that

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\geq&\ell^{2}-\#\mathcal{A}_{1}-\#\mathcal{A}_{2},\\
\geq&\ell^{2}-\ell-\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\ell}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{15.18}{\sqrt{\ell}}\right)\\
=&\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\ell}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{\ell}-15.18}{\sqrt{\ell}}\right).\end{split}

This bound is nontrivial when
\sqrt{\ell}>15.18
, i.e.,
\ell\geq 233
.
∎

For
z>0

\mathcal{F}^{z}(X)
be the intersection
\cap_{\ell\leq z}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(X)
where
\ell
ranges over prime numbers that are

Proposition 4.8
.

With respect to notation above, the set
\mathcal{F}
has positive density given by
\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{F})\sim\prod_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}
.

Proof.

We prove our result by showing that

(4.2)

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{F}(X)}{4X^{5/6}}\leq\prod_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}.

and

(4.3)

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{F}(X)}{4X^{5/6}}\geq\prod_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}.

We have that
\mathcal{F}^{z}(X)\sim 4\prod_{\ell\leq z}\delta_{\ell}X^{5/6}
. Since
\mathcal{F}(X)
is contained in
\mathcal{F}^{z}(X)
, we find that

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{F}(X)}{4X^{5/6}}\leq\prod_{\ell\leq z}\delta_{\ell},

and letting
z\rightarrow\infty
, one has that

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{F}(X)}{4X^{5/6}}\leq\prod_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}.

Since

\mathcal{F}^{z}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{F}(X)\cup\left(\bigcup_{\ell>z}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\prime}(X)\right),

where we recall that
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\prime}
is the complement of
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
. Lemma
4.5
implies that there is an absolute constant
C_{1}>0
for which

\begin{split}\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{F}(X)}{4X^{5/6}}\geq&\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{F}^{z}(X)}{4X^{5/6}}-C_{1}\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}},\\
=&\prod_{\ell\leq Z}\delta_{\ell}-C_{1}\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}.\end{split}

Letting
z\rightarrow\infty
, we find that

(4.4)

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{F}(X)}{4X^{5/6}}\geq\prod_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}.

This completes the proof of the result.
∎

4.2.
Proof of the main result

In this section, we provide the proof of our main result, Theorem
3.8
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Setting

\eta_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}&\text{ if }p=3;\\
\frac{3}{8}&\text{ if }p=5;\end{cases}

we find that

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{E}^{0}(X)}{\#\mathcal{E}(X)}\geq\eta_{p}.

Proof.

The set
\mathcal{E}
is a large family and by Lemma
3.15
, exactly half the curves in
\mathcal{E}
have root number
1
. The result follows from Theorem
3.11
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.8
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve in the set
\mathcal{E}^{0}
. By Proposition
3.14
, the Assumption
2.1
is satisfied for
E
(and
K=\mathbb{Q}
). It follows from Proposition
2.7
that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)=0
. In particular, the rank of
E(L)
is
0
. Let
S_{3}
be the intersection
\mathcal{E}^{0}\cap S_{1}
. Given
E\in S_{3}
, we find that
\operatorname{rank}E(L)=0
and
E(L)_{\operatorname{tors}}=0
, hence,
E(L)=0
.

Theorem
3.7
asserts that
S_{1}
has density
1
. We note that for
\ell\in Z
, the cardinality of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}
is
\mathfrak{A}_{\ell}
by definition. Thus, we find that

\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(S_{3})=\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(\mathcal{E}^{0}).

Then it follows from Proposition
4.9
that

\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(\mathcal{E}^{0})\geq\eta_{p}\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(\mathcal{E}).

By Lemma
4.2
,
\mathcal{F}
is contained in
\mathcal{E}
, and thus,

\eta_{p}\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(\mathcal{E})\geq\eta_{p}\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(\mathcal{F}).

Finally, note that according to Proposition
4.8
,

\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{F})=\prod_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}.

Condition (2) implies that
\delta_{\ell}>0
for all primes
\ell\in Z
. On the other hand, that
\delta_{\ell}>0
for all primes
\ell\notin Z
follows from Lemma
4.4
.
In conclusion,
\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(S_{3})\geq\eta_{p}\prod_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}
, thus, the density of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
that are diophantine stable in
L
have positive lower density
\geq\eta_{p}\prod_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}
.
∎

On ξ \xi -process for DVCS-amplitude
I. V. Anikin

Abstract
In this note, we demonstrate in detail the ξ \xi -process implementation applied to the deeply virtual Compton scattering amplitude to ensure both the QCD and QED gauge invariance. The presented details are also important for understanding of the contour gauge used in different processes.

I
Introduction

At present, the role of the two-photon processes, such as
the deeply virtual Compton scattering (DVCS) and the hadron production in two-photon collisions,
in extracting of the information about the generalized parton
distributions (GPDs) and the generalized distribution amplitudes (GDAs) is very well understood in the physical community,
see the review
Belitsky:2005qn
and the references therein.
Both GPDs and GDAs belong to a new kind of parton distributions which give a possibility
to study many subtleties of the composite hadron structure.

We stress that the most of interesting physical effects are related to
the cases where the transversities of different nature present in the considered processes.
For example, the sizable transverse transfer momentum,
\Delta^{\perp}\not=0
, in the corresponding two-photon
processes leads to the significant single-spin asymmetries where the interference of
leading twist-
2
and higher twist-
3
contributions is dominant.
However, the necessity of twist-
3
contributions reveal the nontrivial problem with the
gauge invariance of the corresponding amplitudes compared to the leading twist-
2
amplitudes.
Generally speaking, it was clear that in the case of substantial transfer momentum
all sources of the kinematical and dynamical transversities have to be included in the consideration.
However, from the technical point of view this problem remains unsolved till the beginning of

00
’s when the theoretical solution of the gauge invariance problem appeared in
the DVCS amplitude has been found for the first time in
Anikin:2000em
.
Then, a series of works has been issued, see

Penttinen:2000dg
;
Belitsky:2000vx
;
Vanderhaeghen:2000bt
;
Radyushkin:2000jy
;
Belitsky:2000gz
;
Kivel:2000rb
;
Brodsky:2000xy
,
using the different approaches and extending the results of
Anikin:2000em
.

In
Anikin:2000em
, for the case of deeply virtual Compton scattering off (pseudo)scalar particles
the Ellis-Furmanski-Petronzio-Efremov-Teryaev factorization procedure has been adopted and
the complete expression for the DVCS amplitude up to twist-
3
contributions has been calculated.
However, the so-called
\xi
-process described in
Bogolyubov:1980nc
which ensures both the QCD and QED
gauge invariance of the DVCS-like amplitude has not been discussed in detail.
Meanwhile, the
\xi
-process plays the unique role not only for the gauge invariance but also for the
deep understanding of the sources of transversity.

In the present paper, we take remedial action and illuminate the important details of the
\xi
-process applied for the
DVCS-like amplitudes which are also useful for the application of the contour gauge.

II
\xi
-process

We begin this section with the definition of
\xi
-process applied for an arbitrary amplitude in the Abelian

U(1)
gauge theory, QED.
The extension to the non-Abelian
SU(3)
gauge theory, QCD, is rather trivial and it does demand additional explanations.

Let us consider the amplitude with one photon external line,
{\cal A}_{\mu}(q,p_{i}|A)
, where
q
corresponds to
the photon momentum while
p_{i}
denote the momenta that remain after singling out the photon momentum.
The
U(1)
gauge transformation (gradient transformation) is given by

\displaystyle A^{\Lambda}_{\mu}(x)=A_{\mu}(x)+\partial_{\mu}\Lambda(x).

(1)

Hence, after performing the gauge transformation of Eqn. (
1
), the amplitude
{\cal A}_{\mu}(q,p_{i}|A)

is
U(1)
gauge invariant if the term
\partial_{\mu}\Lambda(x)
of Eqn. (
1
) does not transform the given amplitude,
i.e.

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\mu}(q,p_{i}|A)={\cal A}_{\mu}(q,p_{i}|A^{\Lambda})\quad\text{if}\quad{\cal A}_{\mu}(q,p_{i}|\partial\Lambda)=0.

(2)

Using the
S
-matrix formalism,

\displaystyle S(g)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{T}\text{exp}\Big\{i\int dxg(x){\cal L}_{I}(x)\Big\}=

(3)

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n}\frac{i^{n}}{n!}\int dx_{1}...dx_{n}\,S^{(n)}(x_{1},...,x_{n})\,g(x_{1})...g(x_{n}),

the statement of Eqn. (
2
) takes the following form
Bogolyubov:1980nc

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{\mu}}\frac{\partial S^{(n)}(x_{1},...,x_{n})}{\partial A_{\mu}(\xi)}=0.

(4)

In our case, the set of diagrams with one external photon line can be obtained from the set of diagrams
without the external photon line by means of the insertion of the photon vertex, depending on
\xi
-position, into any external or internal lines.
This insertion is called the
\xi
-process. In other words, the
\xi
-process describes diagrammatically the following correspondence

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S^{(n)}(x_{1},...,x_{n})}{\partial A_{\mu}(\xi)}\Big|_{\xi=x_{i}}\Longleftrightarrow S^{(n-1)}(x_{1},...,x_{n-1}).

(5)

Hence, in order to fulfil the gauge invariance condition, see Eqn. (
4
),
we first insert the
\xi
-vertex into the external or internal lines and, then, we calculate the divergence over
\xi
-position
to get zero finally. Notice that for the amplitude written in
p
-representation the
differentiation over
\xi
-position has been replaced by the contraction with the inserted photon momentum
q
.
If we implement the
\xi
-process only for the internal lines we get the relations reflecting the Ward identity.

We are now in a position to consider the
\xi
-process applied for the DVCS parton subprocess, see Fig.
1
.
Having applied the QCD
\xi
-process to the deeply virtual Compton scattering off quarks, see Fig.
1

we obtain the following (here,
\hat{k}=k\cdot\gamma
and the causality prescriptions are irrelevant for the moment)

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha}(\text{dia.-}1)=

(6)

\displaystyle\bar{u}(k_{2})\gamma^{\nu}\frac{\hat{k}_{1}-\hat{\ell}+\hat{q}}{(k_{1}-\ell+q)^{2}}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\hat{k}_{1}-\hat{\ell}}{(k_{1}-\ell)^{2}}\gamma^{\alpha}u(k_{1})

and

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha}(\text{dia.-}2)=

(7)

\displaystyle\bar{u}(k_{2})\gamma^{\nu}\frac{\hat{k}_{1}-\hat{\ell}+\hat{q}}{(k_{1}-\ell+q)^{2}}\gamma^{\alpha}\frac{\hat{k}_{1}+\hat{q}}{(k_{1}+q)^{2}}\gamma^{\mu}u(k_{1})

and

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha}(\text{dia.-}3)=

(8)

\displaystyle\bar{u}(k_{2})\gamma^{\alpha}\frac{\hat{k}_{2}+\hat{\ell}}{(k_{2}+\ell)^{2}}\gamma^{\nu}\frac{\hat{k}_{1}+\hat{q}}{(k_{1}+q)^{2}}\gamma^{\mu}u(k_{1}).

Then, contracting the sum of diagrams with the gluon momentum
\ell
, we derive that

\displaystyle\ell_{\alpha}{\cal A}^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha}(\text{dia.-}1+\text{dia.-}2+\text{dia.-}3)=

(9)

\displaystyle-\bar{u}(k_{2})\gamma^{\nu}\frac{\hat{k}_{1}-\hat{\ell}+\hat{q}}{(k_{1}-\ell+q)^{2}}\gamma^{\mu}u(k_{1})+

\displaystyle\bar{u}(k_{2})\gamma^{\nu}\frac{\hat{k}_{1}-\hat{\ell}+\hat{q}}{(k_{1}-\ell+q)^{2}}\Big[1-(\hat{k}_{2}+\hat{q}^{\prime})\frac{\hat{k}_{1}+\hat{q}}{(k_{1}+q)^{2}}\Big]\gamma^{\mu}u(k_{1})+

\displaystyle\bar{u}(k_{2})\gamma^{\nu}\frac{\hat{k}_{1}+\hat{q}}{(k_{1}+q)^{2}}\gamma^{\mu}u(k_{1})\equiv 0.

Thus, Eqn. (
9
) demonstrates the QCD gauge invariance of the quark DVCS amplitude.

III
Factorization procedure

In this section, we remind briefly the main steps of factorization procedure for the DVCS process where we deal with
the substantial hadron momentum transfer, see
Anikin:2000em
for details. The DVCS process is defined as

\displaystyle\gamma^{*}(q)+\text{hadron}(p_{1})\to\gamma(q^{\prime})+\text{hadron}(p_{2})

(10)

with

\displaystyle\Delta=p_{2}-p_{1},\quad P=(p_{2}+p_{1})/2,\quad\bar{Q}=(q+q^{\prime})/2.

(11)

Here, the virtuality of the initial off-shell photon defines the so-called
large scale, i.e.
q^{2}=-Q^{2}\to\infty
, while the final photon is
on-shell and
q^{\prime\,2}=0
.
Due to this kinematics, the considered process is a hard exclusive
reaction and its amplitude can be studied within the factorization procedure.
In other words, the hard processes define the asymptotical
regime for the corresponding amplitude which can be related to the the light-cone formalism.
We introduce a light-cone basis which is constructed by the
“plus” and “minus” vectors as

\displaystyle p=\big(\Lambda,\,0,\,0,\,\Lambda\big)=(p^{+},0^{-},{\bf 0}_{\perp}),

\displaystyle n=\big(1/(2\Lambda),\,0,\,0,\,-1/(2\Lambda)\big)=(0^{+},n^{-},{\bf 0}_{\perp}),

\displaystyle p\cdot n=1,

(12)

where
\Lambda
is an arbitrary and dimensionful constant which is
expressed through the Lorentz invariants. The exact form of
\Lambda

as a function of invariants depends on the frame.
In our case, the vectors
p
and
n
are nothing but the average momentum
P
and the normalized real photon momentum
q^{\prime}/(P\cdot q^{\prime})
,
respectively. In this frame, the relevant vectors can be decomposed in terms of
the Sudakov (light-cone) basis as

\displaystyle 2P=2p+\bar{M}^{2}n\approx\,2p,

\displaystyle\Delta=p_{2}-p_{1}=-2\xi P+2\xi\bar{M}^{2}n+\Delta^{\perp}\approx-2\xi P+\Delta^{\perp},

\displaystyle P\cdot\Delta=0,\quad\Delta^{2}=t\approx 0,

(13)

where the approximation is valid up to the twist three accuracy, discarding the contributions associated with the twist four and
higher.

Symbolically, the factorization theorem allows the amplitude to be factorized in the form of convolution
as

\displaystyle\text{Amplitude}=\{\text{Hard (pQCD)}\}\otimes\{\text{Soft (npQCD)}\}\,.

(14)

In the most ideal case, both the hard and soft parts in Eqn. (
14
) are independent of each other,
UV- and IR-renormalizable. Moreover, various parton distributions which parametrize the soft part
have to manifest the universality property.

More exactly,
the factorization theorem states that
the short (hard) and long (soft) distance
dynamics can be separated out provided
Q^{2}
is large, and the DVCS-like amplitude takes the form of

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\mu\nu...}=\int d^{4}k_{1}d^{4}k_{2}...\,\text{tr}\big[E_{\mu\nu...}(k_{1},k_{2},...)\Phi(k_{1},k_{2},...)\big]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Q^{2}\to\infty}}{{\Rightarrow}}\int dx_{1}dx_{2}...\,\text{tr}\big[E_{\mu\nu...}(x_{1},x_{2},...)\,\Phi(x_{1},x_{2},...)\big]

\displaystyle+{\cal O}(1/Q^{2})

(15)

where
E_{\mu\nu...}
implies a product of corresponding propagators
which finally forms the hard part of amplitude, and the soft part is related to
the hadron matrix elements of quark-gluon nonlocal operators as

\displaystyle\Phi(k_{1},k_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal F}}}{{=}}\langle{\cal O}^{(\bar{\psi},\psi,A)}(z_{1},z_{2},0)\rangle,

\displaystyle\Phi(x_{i})=\int dk^{+}_{i}\,\delta(x_{i}-k_{i}\cdot n)\,\int dk^{-}_{i}\,d^{2}{\bf k}^{T}_{i}\,\Phi(k_{i}),

(16)

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal F}}}{{=}}
denotes the corresponding Fourier transforms defined below, see Eqn. (
III
).

The
1/Q^{2}
-corrections in the hard process amplitudes, see Eqn. (
III
), can be classified with the help of
the collinear and(or) geometrical twist defined for the corresponding operators.
The geometrical twist has been determined for local quark-gluon operators as

\displaystyle\tau(\text{twist})=d(\text{dimension})-j(\text{spin}),

(17)

while the collinear twist has been defined for non-local quark-gluon operators as

\displaystyle t(\text{coll. twist})=d(\text{dimension})-j_{a}(\text{spin projection}).

(18)

For instance, in the case of DIS , we have the simplest correspondence

\displaystyle\text{Loc.}{\cal O}^{\,\text{twist}=\tau}(\bar{\psi},\psi,A)\Longrightarrow\left(1/Q^{2}\right)^{\tau/2-1}.

(19)

It is also worth to remind the matching between the contributions of collinear and geometrical twists,
we have

\displaystyle\text{leading twist-}t\Leftrightarrow\text{leading twist-}\tau

(20)

\displaystyle\text{next-to-leading twist-}t\Leftrightarrow\tau\leq\text{next-to-leading twist-}t.

In other words, each of hadron correlators can be presented in the form of

\displaystyle(\text{{L-twist}-}t\,\,\text{operator})\oplus(\text{NL-twist-}t\,\,\text{operator})\oplus....

(21)

where

\displaystyle(\text{NL-twist-}t\,\,\text{operator})\ni(\text{{L-twist}-}\tau\,\,\text{operator}).

(22)

Basically, the factorization procedure (or theorem) gives a recipe for
an asymptotical estimation of the amplitude instead of a explicit calculation.
The latter takes place only if we have defined the hadron-parton interaction (effective) Lagrangian.

In contrast to the Drell-Yan-like (or the Sudakov-like) processes, the DVCS process deals with the only
dominant light-cone direction needed for the factorization procedure. We assume the direction
p^{+}
, see Eqn. (
III
),
to be a dominant one,
i.e.

p^{+}\sim[{\cal P}]\to\infty
.
Therefore, the convolution representation, see Eqn. (
III
), is based on the power-counting in according to

\displaystyle k\sim\big([{\cal P}],\mu^{2}/[{\cal P}],\mu\big)\equiv\big(k^{+},k^{-},{\bf k}_{\perp}\big)

(23)

applied for all relevant vectors. To get the amplitude factorized, we decompose the
function
E_{\mu\nu...}
around the dominant direction. This is given by the Taylor expansion as

\displaystyle E_{\mu\nu...}(k_{i})=E_{\mu\nu...}(x_{i}P)+

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{\mu\nu...}(k_{i})}{\partial k^{\alpha}_{i}}\Big|_{k_{i}=x_{i}P}\,(k_{i}-x_{i}P)^{\alpha}+\ldots,

(24)

together with

\displaystyle k_{i}^{\mu}=x_{i}P^{\mu}+(k_{i}\cdot P)n^{\mu}+k^{\mu}_{i\,\perp}\approx x_{i}P^{\mu}+k^{\mu}_{i\,\perp},\quad x_{i}=k_{i}\cdot n.

(25)

Notice that the Taylor expansion around the dominant direction, see Eqn. (
III
),
together with the power-counting, see Eqn. (
23
), lead to the certain constraints for the loop integrations
in the soft part.
Indeed, considering the positive domain of integration for the DVCS amplitude, we have

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\mu\nu}=\int_{0}^{\Lambda^{\infty}}dk^{+}E_{\mu\nu}(k^{+},0^{-},{\bf 0}^{\perp})

\displaystyle\times\int^{\mu}_{0}d^{2}{\bf k}^{\perp}\,\int^{\mu^{2}/\Lambda^{\infty}}_{0}dk^{-}\,\Phi(k^{+},k^{-},{\bf k}^{\perp})\Big|_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(26)

where
\Lambda^{\infty}=a[{\cal P}]
.
For the sake of simplicity, we here focus on the one-loop integration and the leading order of expansion.
We can see that in the soft part we have the cuts for the integrations over
dk^{-}
and
d^{2}{\bf k}^{\perp}

depending on
\mu
.
Alternatively, instead of the cut-off method we can use the dimension regularization method with the full regions of integration.
We can go over to the full integration with
the integration measure
dk^{-}\,d^{(D-2)}{\bf k}^{\perp}

and, then, we use the RG-equation to study the evolution regarding
\mu
,
i.e.

\displaystyle\int^{\mu}_{0}d^{2}{\bf k}^{\perp}\,\int^{\mu^{2}/\Lambda^{\infty}}_{0}dk^{-}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{RG}}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}\int^{\infty}_{0}d^{(D-2)}{\bf k}^{\perp}\,\int^{\infty}_{0}dk^{-}\Big|_{D=4-2\epsilon}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{\Longrightarrow}}\int d^{2}{\bf k}^{\perp}\,dk^{-}.

(27)

As a result, we obtain the factorized amplitude in form of mathematical convolution which reads

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\mu\nu}=\int_{-1}^{1}dx\,E_{\mu\nu}(xP^{+},0^{-},{\bf 0}^{\perp})

\displaystyle\times\int dk^{+}\delta(x-k^{+}/P^{+})\int d^{2}{\bf k}^{\perp}\,dk^{-}\,\Phi(k^{+},k^{-},{\bf k}^{\perp})

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{-1}^{1}dx\,E_{\mu\nu}(x)\,\Phi(x).

(28)

It is instructive to present the soft part of amplitude
\Phi(x)
through the
Fourier transform of the hadron matrix element of operator. It reads

\displaystyle\Phi^{[\Gamma]}(x)=\int(d^{4}k)\delta(x-kn)

\displaystyle\times\int(d^{4}z)e^{i(k-\Delta/2)z}\langle p_{2}|\bar{\psi}(0)\Gamma\psi(z)|p_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int(d\lambda)e^{i(x+\xi)\lambda}\langle p_{2}|\bar{\psi}(0)\Gamma\psi(\lambda n)|p_{1}\rangle

(29)

with the certain Fierz projection defined by
\Gamma
-matrix.
Up to now, our consideration has mainly been focused on the leading term in the Taylor expansion of
E_{\mu\nu}

and the quark operator related to the soft part of amplitude
\Phi(x)
up to now.

IV
The soft part of DVCS amplitude with the gluon radiations

We are in a position to discuss the gluon radiations from the quark lines which lead to
the quark-gluon operator (together with the Wilson line) in the function
\Phi
. Since the quark-gluon operators
induce the genuine twist three, we need to take into account the kinematical sources of transversity
related to the presence of the nontrivial
\Delta^{\perp}
in the parametrizations.

Sence in the present paper we study the
\xi
-process applied for the amplitude,
the kinematical twist three can be omitted. The comprehensive analysis of all sources of twist three can be found, for example,
in
Anikin:2011aa
;
Anikin:2009bf
.

The gluon radiation from the internal quark line, see Fig.
1

of genuine twist three which has been discussed in detail, see
Anikin:2011aa
;
Anikin:2009bf
. Moreover,
in our previous studies of DVCS-like processes, the role of diagrams with
the gluon radiation from the external quark line of the subprocess has been mentioned rather on the intuitive level.
We rectify the mentioned incompleteness in this paper.

\displaystyle\big[\overrightarrow{\hat{D}(z)}\psi(z)\big]=0\quad\text{or}\quad\big[\overrightarrow{\hat{D}(z)}\psi(z)\bar{\psi}(0)\big]=0

\displaystyle\langle p_{2}|\overrightarrow{\hat{D}(z)}\psi(z)\bar{\psi}(0)|p_{1}\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle p_{2}|\psi(z)\bar{\psi}(0)\overleftarrow{\hat{D}(0)}|p_{1}\rangle=0.

IV.1
Contour gauge and Elimination of longitudinal Wilson lines

In conclusion of this Section, we give a short discussion on the use of contour gauge which eliminates
the longitudinal component of gluon field. The necessary details can be found in
Anikin:2016bor

The axial kind of gauges, like
A^{+}=0

determined by a Wilson line with a fixed path. For example, the straightforward line
in the Wilson line connecting
\pm\infty

It is important that two different contour gauges are able to correspond to the same
local axial gauge.

The contour gauge is a subject of intense studies
(see, for example,
ContourG1

ContourG2

The preponderance of contour gauge
is that the quantum gauge theory starts to be free from the Gribov ambiguities.
Moreover, the contour gauge gives the simplest way to fix the residual gauge freedom.

we first fix an arbitrary point
(x_{0},\textbf{g}(x_{0}))

VI
Conclusions

One of the most interesting physical effects is related to
the cases with the sizeable transversities of the hard processes.
In DVCS-like processes, the substantial transfer momentum with
\Delta^{\perp}\not=0

can be associated with the several single-spin asymmetries where the interference of
leading twist-
2
and higher twist-
3
contributions is dominant.
On the other hand, the necessity of twist-
3
contributions reveal the nontrivial problem with the
gauge invariance of the corresponding amplitudes compared to the leading twist-
2
amplitudes.
It was clear that for such kind of processes it is mandatory to study all sources of the kinematical and dynamical transversities.
From the technical point of view, the gauge invariance problem
has been resolved for the DVCS amplitude for the first time
Anikin:2000em
. However, the full analysis
of the
\xi
-process in order to ensure the gauge invariance has not been presented.

In
Anikin:2000em
, the role of the gluon radiation from the external quark line of the subprocess has been hidden.
This sort of diagrams can be referred to the non-standard diagrams in contrast to the
standard diagrams where the gluon has radiated from the internal quark line of the subprocess.
Meanwhile, the non-standard diagrams form the corresponding Wilson lines which are important for the contour gauge
conception, see
Anikin:2010wz
.

In the present paper, we have rectified the mentioned incompleteness.
Having used the integral relations which has been induced by the QCD equations of motion,
we have demonstrated that the non-standard diagram contributions, see Fig.
1
, with the function
H_{3}
are identical to
the standard diagram contribution with the same function
H_{3}
which involves the quark-photon subprocess.
Hence, for the QED gauge invariance of the DVCS amplitude it is enough to include the genuine twist-
3
contribution of
the standard diagram involving the quark-photon subprocess together with the standard diagram with the quark-photon-gluon subprocess. Moreover, we have shown that the non-standard diagram contributions play the important role to use the contour gauge for the gluon field.

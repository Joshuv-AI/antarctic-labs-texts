Dynamics of Apparent Horizon and a null comparison principle
Xinliang An, Taoran He

Abstract
. This paper investigates the global dynamics of the apparent horizon. We present an approach to establish its existence and its long-term behaviors. Our apparent horizon is constructed by solving the marginally outer trapped surface (MOTS) along each incoming null hypersurface. Based on the nonlinear hyperbolic estimates established in [ 24 ] by Klainerman-Szeftel under polarized axial symmetry, we prove that the corresponding apparent horizon is smooth, asymptotically null and converging to the event horizon eventually. To further address the local achronality of the apparent horizon, a new concept, called the null comparison principle, is introduced in this paper. For three typical scenarios of gravitational collapse, our null comparison principle is tested and verified, which guarantees that the apparent horizon must be piecewise spacelike or piecewise null. In addition, we also validate and provide new proofs for several physical laws along the apparent horizon.



1.
Introduction

The explorations of trapped surfaces and the trapped region trace back to 1960s, when Penrose established his renowned incompleteness theorem. The boundary of the trapped region is a quasi-local object, known as the apparent horizon. Unlike the event horizon, i.e., the boundary of the causal past of the future null infinity, which requires global information about spacetime, the apparent horizon describes the boundary of the trapped region and demands only local information. This feature holds importance in practical applications, especially for numerical simulations of general relativity.

Over the decades, numerous efforts have been dedicated to unraveling the properties of the apparent horizon. However, the detailed understanding regarding the long-time behavior and the final state of apparent horizon is still lacking. Under spherical symmetry, Williams studied the asymptotic behavior of the apparent horizon in
[
31
]
. In this current paper, we initiate the study of the global dynamics of apparent horizon beyond spherical symmetry. Relying on the hyperbolic estimates established in
[
24
]
by Klainerman-Szeftel, we prove the existence and uniqueness of MOTS along each incoming null hypersurface. These MOTSs collectively form a three-dimensional hypersurface and we further prove it to be smooth, asymptotically null and eventually approaching the event horizon.

In general, a hypersurface being asymptotically null could be timelike at certain points. In this paper, we introduce a new concept, called the
null comparison principle
and we use it to guarantee that the apparent horizon is piecewise spacelike or piecewise null. Furthermore, we present three typical scenarios of gravitational collapse, in which the null comparison principle holds. These scenarios are in the perturbative Schwarzschild spacetimes
[
24
]
, isotropic and anisotropic gravitational-collapse spacetimes
[
4
]
and spacetimes containing naked singularities
[
5
]
.

Additionally, by virtue of achronal property proved for the apparent horizon, we also investigate the black hole thermodynamics along the apparent horizon. We verify and offer new proofs for several physical laws first formulated by Ashtekar and Krishnan in
[
13
,
14
]
.

1.1.
Main Results

1.2.1.
Choice of Coordinate System

To solve for MOTS, we perform a coordinate transformation in the interior region
{}^{(int)}\mathcal{M}
constructed by Klainerman-Szeftel
[
24
]
. There they adopted the incoming geodesic foliation with coordinates
(\underline{u},r,\theta,\varphi)
. Let
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4})
be the associate null frame. In their coordinates, the coordinates derivative
\partial_{r}
can be expressed as

\partial_{r}=[e_{3}(r)]^{-1}(e_{3}-\sqrt{\gamma}\underline{b}e_{\theta})\quad\text{with}\quad\underline{b}=e_{3}(\theta)\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma=\frac{1}{(e_{\theta}(\theta))^{2}}.

Due to the presence of

is not necessarily null. To construct the MOTS along a null hypersurface, we prefer a coordinate derivative to be null. This condition is achieved by choosing coordinates

\widetilde{\underline{u}}=\underline{u},\quad\widetilde{r}=r,\quad\widetilde{\theta}=\theta+f_{1}(\underline{u},r,\theta,\varphi),\quad\widetilde{\varphi}=\varphi.

Here we require

(1.1)

e_{3}(f_{1}+\theta)=0\quad\text{with}\quad 1+\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial\theta}\neq 0.

Following from chain rules, we get

\partial_{\widetilde{r}}=[e_{3}(r)]^{-1}\Big(e_{3}+(1+\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial\theta})^{-1}e_{3}(f_{1}+\theta)\partial_{\theta}\Big).

By the requirement (
1.1
), in our new coordinate system
(\widetilde{\underline{u}},\widetilde{r},\widetilde{\theta},\widetilde{\varphi})
we have

\partial_{\widetilde{r}}=[e_{3}(r)]^{-1}e_{3}
being null. It is important to note that in our analysis, we utilize the same null frame
(e_{3},e_{4},e_{1},e_{2})
as presented in Klainerman-Szeftel
[
24
]
. Hence, the hyperbolic estimates established in
[
24
]
remain unchanged and applicable in our framework.

1.2.3.

In
[

Remark 2
.

In
[
8
]
, Andersson-Mars-Simon explored the local achronality of apparent horizon through studying the stability operator of MOTS. They proved that, the apparent horizon is piecewise spacelike or null within a neighbourhood of the strictly stable outermost MOTS. They require that the linearized operator associated with the equation of MOTS possesses a strictly positive principle eigenvalue. Our null comparison principle is based on null foliations and we do not require the strictly positive condition. For example, we allow the principle eigenvalue of a corresponding elliptic operator to be zero and the null comparison principle is still applicable.

\mathscr{G}:=\{\mathbf{g}(\partial_{\underline{u}},e_{4}),\mathbf{g}(\partial_{\underline{u}},e_{3}),\mathbf{g}(\partial_{\underline{u}},e_{a})\}\quad\text{with}\quad a=1,2.

We also denote

M:=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\max\limits_{I\times[R_{1},R_{2}]\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(e_{4},e_{3},\nabla)^{k}(\Gamma,\mathscr{R},\log|f|,\mathscr{G})|(\underline{u},r,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}).

Here
\Gamma
represents the collection of all the Ricci coefficients, and

denotes the set comprising all the curvature components.

We proceed to calculate the Fréchet derivative of

with respect to

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
(\underline{u},R)\in I\times C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, with
R_{1}\leq R\leq R_{2}
, we have

\begin{split}&\partial_{R}L(R,\underline{u})[W]=\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R}}W+B_{a}\nabla^{a}W+\bigg(C+\frac{1}{2}f\nabla_{3}(f^{-1}{\rm tr\chi})\bigg)W\end{split}

with

|B_{a}|+|C|\leq C(M,R_{1},R_{2})\Big[|\nabla^{2}R|+|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2}\Big].

Proof.

According to the definition of Fréchet derivative, for any
W\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, we obtain

\begin{split}\partial_{R}L(R,\underline{u})[W]=&\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(F(R+\varepsilon W,\underline{u})-F(R,\underline{u})\Big)\\
=&\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R+\varepsilon W}}(R+\varepsilon W)-\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R}}R\Big)\\
&+\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big[\bigg((e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla(R+\varepsilon W)|^{2}\bigg)|_{\underline{u},R+\varepsilon W}\\
&-\bigg((e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla R|^{2}\bigg)|_{\underline{u},R}\Big]\\
&+\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big[\bigg(\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla(R+\varepsilon W)+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi\bigg)|_{\underline{u},R+\varepsilon W}\\
&-\bigg(\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla R+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi\bigg)|_{\underline{u},R}\Big]\\
=:&I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{split}

To evaluate
I_{1},I_{2}
,
I_{3}
, we appeal to the following commutation formulas in
[
3
]
. For
any scalar function

, we have

(2.16)

\begin{split}[\nabla_{3},\nabla]f=\frac{1}{2}(\eta+\underline{\eta})\nabla_{3}f-\underline{\chi}\cdot\nabla f\end{split}

and

(2.17)

\begin{split}[\nabla_{3},\Delta]f=&-\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}\Delta f-2\hat{\underline{\chi}}\cdot\nabla^{2}f+\underline{\beta}\cdot\nabla f+\frac{1}{2}(\eta+\underline{\eta})\cdot\nabla_{3}\nabla f-\eta\cdot\hat{\underline{\chi}}\cdot\nabla f\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}\eta\cdot\nabla f+\mbox{div }\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(\eta+\underline{\eta})\nabla_{3}f\bigg)-\mbox{div }\underline{\chi}\cdot\nabla f.\end{split}

We start to compute
I_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
I_{1}
, the following equality holds

\begin{split}I_{1}=&\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R+\varepsilon W}}R-\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R}}R\Big)=f\nabla_{3}(\Delta_{S_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}}}\tilde{R})W-f\nabla_{3}\Big(2f\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{ab}\nabla^{a}R\nabla^{b}R\Big)W.\end{split}

Proof.

Set
g
and
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
to be the induced metric and independent angular variables on
S_{\underline{u},r}
. For a scalar function

, we have

(2.18)

\begin{split}\Delta_{S_{\underline{u},r}}f=&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}(\sqrt{\det g}\,g^{\theta_{i}\theta_{l}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{l}})\\
=&g^{\theta_{1}\theta_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\theta_{1}\partial\theta_{1}}+g^{\theta_{2}\theta_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\theta_{2}\partial\theta_{2}}+2g^{\theta_{1}\theta_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\theta_{1}\partial\theta_{2}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{1}}(g^{\theta_{1}\theta_{1}})\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{1}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{1}}(g^{\theta_{1}\theta_{2}})\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{2}}\\
&+\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{2}}(g^{\theta_{2}\theta_{1}})\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{1}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{2}}(g^{\theta_{2}\theta_{2}})\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{2}}+\frac{1}{2}g^{\theta_{k}\theta_{j}}\frac{\partial g_{\theta_{j}\theta_{k}}}{\partial\theta_{i}}g^{\theta_{i}\theta_{l}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{l}},\end{split}

where
i,j,k,l=1,2
and

depends on
(\underline{u},r,\theta,\varphi)
. Here we also use the formula

\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}\det g=\det g\cdot g^{\theta_{k}\theta_{j}}\cdot\frac{\partial g_{\theta_{j}\theta_{k}}}{\partial\theta_{i}}.

Applying
Lemma

2.3
, together with
\partial_{r}R=0

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R+\varepsilon W}}R-\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R}}R\Big)=&\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\Delta_{S_{\underline{u},R+\varepsilon W}}R-\Delta_{S_{\underline{u},R}}R\Big)-\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big[\bigg(2f\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{bc}\nabla^{b}\tilde{R}\nabla^{c}\tilde{R}\bigg)|_{S_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}+\varepsilon W}}\\
&-\bigg(2f\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{bc}\nabla^{b}\tilde{R}\nabla^{c}\tilde{R}\bigg)|_{S_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}}}\Big]\\
=&\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(\Delta_{S_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}}}\tilde{R})W-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Big(2f\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{bc}\nabla^{b}\tilde{R}\nabla^{c}\tilde{R}\Big)W\\
=&f\nabla_{3}(\Delta_{S_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}}}\tilde{R})W-f\nabla_{3}\Big(2f\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{ab}\nabla^{a}R\nabla^{b}R\Big)W,\end{split}

where in the last line we use the fact that
\partial_{r}=fe_{3}
.
∎

Applying
Lemma

2.6
, along with (

) and (

and

as

\begin{split}I_{1}=&\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R+\varepsilon W}}(R+\varepsilon W)-\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R}}R\Big)\\
=&f\nabla_{3}(\Delta_{S_{\underline{u},R}}R)W-f\nabla_{3}\Big(2f\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{ab}\nabla^{a}R\nabla^{b}R\Big)W+\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R}}W\\
=&f\Big([\nabla_{3},\Delta]R+\Delta(\nabla_{3}R)\Big)W-f\nabla_{3}\Big(2f\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{ab}\nabla^{a}R\nabla^{b}R\Big)W+\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R}}W\\
=&f\Big(-\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}\Delta R-2\hat{\underline{\chi}}\cdot\nabla^{2}R+\underline{\beta}\cdot\nabla R+\frac{1}{2}(\eta+\underline{\eta})\cdot\nabla_{3}\nabla R-\eta\cdot\hat{\underline{\chi}}\cdot\nabla R+\frac{1}{2}\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}\eta\cdot\nabla R\\
&+\mbox{div }\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(\eta+\underline{\eta})\nabla_{3}R\bigg)-\mbox{div }\underline{\chi}\cdot\nabla R-\nabla_{3}\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{ab}\nabla^{a}R\nabla^{b}R+2\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{ab}\underline{\chi}^{a}_{c}\nabla^{c}R\nabla^{b}R\Big)W+\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},R}}W,\end{split}

\begin{split}I_{2}=&\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big[\bigg((e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla(R+\varepsilon W)|^{2}\bigg)|_{\underline{u},R+\varepsilon W}-\bigg((e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla R|^{2}\bigg)|_{\underline{u},R}\Big]\\
=&\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bigg((e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla R|^{2}\bigg)W+2(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)\nabla R\cdot\nabla W\\
=&\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla R|^{2}W+(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}|\nabla R|^{2}W\\
&+2(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)\nabla R\cdot\nabla W\\
=&fe_{3}\Big(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f\Big)|\nabla R|^{2}W+f(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)(-\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}|\nabla R|^{2}-2\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{bc}\nabla^{b}R\nabla^{c}R)W\\
&+2(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)\nabla R\cdot\nabla W.\end{split}

Note that in the derivation of

we use the equality

\nabla_{3}|\nabla R|^{2}=2\nabla_{3}\nabla R\cdot\nabla R=-\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}|\nabla R|^{2}-2\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{bc}\nabla^{b}R\nabla^{c}R.

In the same manner, we also obtain

\displaystyle I_{3}=

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\bigg(\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla R+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi\bigg)W+\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla W

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\bigg(\nabla_{3}\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)-\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\underline{\chi}\bigg)\cdot\nabla R

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{3}(f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi)\bigg)W+\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla W.

Summarizing all the above, we then conclude

\begin{split}&\partial_{R}L(R,\underline{u})[W]=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}=\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}}}W+B_{a}\nabla^{a}W+\bigg(C+\frac{1}{2}f\nabla_{3}(f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi)\bigg)W,\end{split}

where

|B_{a}|+|C|\leq C(M,R_{1},R_{2})\Big[|\nabla^{2}R|+|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2}\Big].

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For any
(\underline{u},R)\in I\times C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
with

|\partial_{\underline{u}}L(R,\underline{u})-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\underline{u}}(f^{-1}{\rm tr\chi})|\leq C(M,R_{1},R_{2})\Big[|\nabla^{2}R|+|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2}\Big].

Proof.

Via a direct computation, together with
Lemma

2.3
and
\partial_{\underline{u}}R=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{\underline{u}}L(R,\underline{u})=

\displaystyle[\partial_{\underline{u}},\Delta]R-2\partial_{\underline{u}}(f\hat{\underline{\chi}})_{ab}\nabla^{a}R\nabla^{b}R-4f\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{ab}[\partial_{\underline{u}},\nabla^{a}]R\nabla^{b}R+\partial_{\underline{u}}\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla R

\displaystyle+\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot[\partial_{\underline{u}},\nabla]R+\partial_{\underline{u}}(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla R|^{2}

\displaystyle+2(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)[\partial_{\underline{u}},\nabla]R\cdot\nabla R+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\underline{u}}(f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi).

To estimate

\begin{split}[e_{4},\nabla]R=&\frac{1}{2}(\eta+\underline{\eta})e_{4}R-\chi\cdot\nabla R,\\
[e_{4},\Delta]R=&-\mbox{tr}\chi\Delta R-2\hat{\chi}\cdot\nabla^{2}R+\beta\cdot\nabla R+\frac{1}{2}(\eta+\underline{\eta})\cdot\nabla_{4}\nabla R-\underline{\eta}\cdot\hat{\chi}\cdot\nabla R\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\mbox{tr}\chi\underline{\eta}\cdot\nabla R+\mbox{div }\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(\eta+\underline{\eta})e_{4}R\bigg)-\mbox{div }\chi\cdot\nabla R.\end{split}

Also notice that

\partial_{\underline{u}}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{g}(\partial_{\underline{u}},e_{3})e_{4}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{g}(\partial_{\underline{u}},e_{4})e_{3}+\mathbf{g}(\partial_{\underline{u}},e_{a})e^{a}.

Plugging all equalities above into the expression of

, combining with the definition of

|\partial_{\underline{u}}L(R,\underline{u})-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\underline{u}}(f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi)|\leq C(M,R_{1},R_{2})\Big[|\nabla^{2}R|+|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2}\Big].

∎

3.
Existence and Asymptotics of the Apparent Horizon

in the Setting of Klainerman-Szeftel

3.1.
Coordinates transformation

In
[
24
]
, when Klainerman-Szeftel studied the interior region
{}^{(int)}\mathcal{M}
, they adopted the incoming geodesic foliation with coordinate
(\underline{u},r,\theta,\varphi)
.

In
{}^{(int)}\mathcal{M}
, their spacetime metric takes the form

g=-\frac{4\underline{\varsigma}}{r\overline{\kappa}}d\underline{u}dr+\frac{\underline{\varsigma}^{2}(\overline{\kappa}+A)}{\overline{\underline{\kappa}}}d\underline{u}^{2}+\gamma(d\theta-\frac{1}{2}\underline{\varsigma}bd\underline{u}-\frac{\underline{b}}{2}\underline{\Theta})^{2}+e^{2\Phi}d\varphi^{2},

where

b=e_{4}(\theta),\quad\underline{b}=e_{3}(\theta),\quad\gamma=\frac{1}{(e_{\theta}(\theta))^{2}},\quad\underline{\Theta}:=\frac{4\underline{\varsigma}}{r\overline{\kappa}}dr-\underline{\varsigma}^{2}\frac{(\overline{\kappa}+A)}{\overline{\underline{\kappa}}}d\underline{u}.

The coordinate derivatives in
{}^{(int)}\mathcal{M}
can also be expressed as

(3.1)

\begin{split}&\partial_{r}=\frac{2}{r\overline{\underline{\kappa}}}e_{3}-\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{r\overline{\underline{\kappa}}}\underline{b}e_{\theta},\quad\partial_{\theta}=\sqrt{\gamma}e_{\theta},\\
&\partial_{\underline{u}}=\frac{1}{2}\underline{\varsigma}\Big[e_{4}-\frac{\overline{\kappa}+A}{\overline{\underline{\kappa}}}e_{3}-\sqrt{\gamma}(b-\frac{\overline{\kappa}+A}{\overline{\underline{\kappa}}}\underline{b})e_{\theta}\Big].\end{split}

Moreover, they show that for some integer
k_{small}\geq 2
and sufficiently small constants
\varepsilon_{0},\delta_{dec}>0
, the following estimates hold

(3.2)

\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq k_{small}}\sup\limits_{{}^{(int)}\mathcal{M}}\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}\Big(|\mathfrak{d}^{k}(\underline{\varsigma}-1)|+|\mathfrak{d}^{k}(\frac{\gamma}{r^{2}}-1)+|\mathfrak{d}^{k}b|+|\mathfrak{d}^{k}\underline{b}|+|\mathfrak{d}^{k}(\frac{e^{\Phi}}{r\sin\theta}-1)|+|\mathfrak{d}^{k}A|\Big)\lesssim\varepsilon_{0},

where
\mathfrak{d}:=\{e_{3},re_{4},r\nabla\}
.

Note that here
\partial_{r}
is not necessarily a null direction, since
g(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})=\underline{b}^{2}\gamma
may be non-zero.

To solve for the apparent horizon, in
{}^{(int)}\mathcal{M}
we consider a new coordinates system
(\widetilde{\underline{u}},\widetilde{r},\widetilde{\theta},\widetilde{\varphi})
. We fix

\widetilde{\underline{u}}=\underline{u},\quad\widetilde{r}=r,\quad\widetilde{\varphi}=\varphi,

slightly modify
\theta
and set

\widetilde{\theta}:=\theta+f_{1}(\underline{u},r,\theta,\varphi).

By the chain rule we get

\partial_{r}=\partial_{\widetilde{r}}+\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial r}\partial_{\widetilde{\theta}},\quad\partial_{\theta}=(1+\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial\theta})\partial_{\widetilde{\theta}}.

This implies that

\partial_{\widetilde{r}}=\frac{2}{r\overline{\underline{\kappa}}}\Big(e_{3}+(1+\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial\theta})^{-1}e_{3}(f_{1}+\theta)\partial_{\theta}\Big).

To ensure the vector
\partial_{\widetilde{r}}
to be null, we set

e_{3}(f_{1}+\theta)=0\quad\text{with}\ 1+\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial\theta}\neq 0.

In this new setting, we have the below estimate for
f_{1}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f_{1}
be the solution to
e_{3}(f_{1}+\theta)=0
with
f_{1}=0
along
r=r_{\mathcal{T}}
. Then in
{}^{(int)}\mathcal{M}
,
f_{1}
verifies the estimate

|f_{1}|+|\mathfrak{d}f_{1}|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.

Proof.

Via integrating
e_{3}(f_{1}+\theta)=0
along
e_{3}
direction from
r=r_{\mathcal{T}}
, together with the fact that
m_{0}\leq r\leq 3m_{0}
in
{}^{(int)}\mathcal{M}
, we derive the estimates for
f_{1}
. When
\mathfrak{d}=e_{3}
, we have

|e_{3}(f_{1})|=|-e_{3}(\theta)|=|\underline{b}|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.

For
\mathfrak{d}\in\{e_{4},\nabla\}
, commuting
e_{3}(f_{1}+\theta)
with
\mathfrak{d}
and noting that
\underline{\omega}=0
in
{}^{(int)}\mathcal{M}
, we infer that

\displaystyle e_{3}\big(e_{4}(f_{1}+\theta)\big)=[e_{3},e_{4}](f_{1}+\theta)=2(\eta-\underline{\eta})\cdot\nabla(f_{1}+\theta),

\displaystyle e_{3}\big(\nabla(f_{1}+\theta)\big)=[\nabla_{3},\nabla](f_{1}+\theta)=-\underline{\chi}\cdot\nabla(f_{1}+\theta).

Therefore, together with Grönwall’s inequality, the desired bound of
\mathfrak{d}f_{1}
follows from integrating the corresponding equation along
e_{3}
direction.
∎

3.2.

For notational simplicity, when there is no danger of confusion, we still write

Here

, and

denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the 2-dimensional sphere

\underline{u}
hypersurface.

Recall that in
[

]
, the following estimates in

are proved

(1)

For the Hawking mass

Here

(2)

For
\check{\Gamma}=\{\check{\underline{\kappa}},\hat{\underline{\chi}},\zeta,\underline{\eta},\check{\kappa},\hat{\chi},\check{\omega},\xi\}
and
\check{R}=\{\alpha,\beta,\check{\rho},\sigma,\underline{\beta},\underline{\alpha}\}

|\mathfrak{d}^{k}(\check{\Gamma},\check{R})|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.

Note that
\underline{\omega}=0
in

For
\overline{\underline{\kappa}},\overline{\omega},\overline{\kappa},\overline{\rho}

Here we use the convention
\mathfrak{d}=\{e_{3},re_{4},r\nabla\}
.

Since
f=e_{3}(r)^{-1}=\frac{2}{r}\overline{\underline{\kappa}}^{-1}
and
m_{0}\leq r\leq 3m_{0}
in
{}^{(int)}\mathcal{M}
, we obtain

|f+1|=|\overline{\underline{\kappa}}^{-1}(\frac{2}{r}+\overline{\underline{\kappa}})|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}},

and

\begin{split}e_{3}(f)=&e_{3}(\frac{2}{r}\overline{\underline{\kappa}}^{-1})=e_{3}(\frac{2}{r})\overline{\underline{\kappa}}^{-1}-\frac{2}{r}\frac{e_{3}(\overline{\underline{\kappa}})}{\overline{\underline{\kappa}}^{2}}=e_{3}(\frac{2}{r})\overline{\underline{\kappa}}^{-1}-\frac{2}{r}\frac{1}{\overline{\underline{\kappa}}^{2}}\Big(e_{3}(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r})-e_{3}(\frac{2}{r})\Big)\\
=&-\frac{2}{r}\frac{1}{\overline{\underline{\kappa}}^{2}}e_{3}(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r})+\overline{\underline{\kappa}}^{-2}e_{3}(\frac{2}{r})(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r})=-\frac{2}{r}\frac{1}{\overline{\underline{\kappa}}^{2}}e_{3}(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r})-\frac{1}{r}\frac{1}{\overline{\underline{\kappa}}}(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r}).\end{split}

This implies

|e_{3}(f)|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.

Differentiating
e_{3}(f)
with respect to
e_{3}
, we further deduce

\displaystyle e_{3}\big(e_{3}(f)\big)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}e_{3}\bigg(rf^{2}e_{3}(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r})+f(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\bigg(fe_{3}(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r})+2rfe_{3}(f)e_{3}(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r})+rf^{2}e_{3}(e_{3}(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r}))+e_{3}(f)(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r})+fe_{3}(\overline{\underline{\kappa}}+\frac{2}{r})\bigg),

which provides the bound

|e_{3}\big(e_{3}(f)\big)|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.

In below context, we also use

as a shorthand notation for

.
Noting
\mbox{tr}\chi^{\prime}=2fL(R(\theta,\varphi))
and the fact that

within the region

, we have that
\mbox{tr}\chi^{\prime}=0
holds true if and only if

. This gives the elliptic equation

(3.3)

\Delta^{\prime}_{M}R+\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla R+(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla R|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi=0.

The aim of this section is to solve this quasilinear elliptic equation (
3.3
) on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

With
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, we first establish a priori estimates for solutions to

(3.4)

\begin{split}0=&\Delta^{\prime}_{M}R(\theta,\varphi)+(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla R(\theta,\varphi)|^{2}\\
&+\lambda\Big((f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)+f^{-1}c)\cdot\nabla R+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi\Big)\\
&+(1-\lambda)\bigg(-\frac{1}{R(\theta,\varphi)}+\frac{2m_{\infty}}{R(\theta,\varphi)^{2}}\bigg).\end{split}

In
{}^{(int)}\mathcal{M}
, using the fact that
m_{0}\leq R(\theta,\varphi)\leq 3m_{0}
, for any fixed
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, we can rewrite (
3.4
) in the form below

(3.5)

\begin{split}0=&\Delta^{\prime}_{M}R(\theta,\varphi)-\frac{1}{R(\theta,\varphi)}|\nabla R(\theta,\varphi)|^{2}-\frac{1}{R(\theta,\varphi)}+\frac{2m_{\infty}}{R(\theta,\varphi)^{2}}\\
&+\frac{1}{R(\theta,\varphi)^{2}}\bigg(d_{1a}\nabla^{a}R+d_{2bc}\nabla^{b}R\nabla^{c}R+d_{3}\bigg).\end{split}

Here
d_{1a},d_{2bc},d_{3}
are functions of
(\underline{u},r,\theta,\varphi)
obeying

\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq 1}|\mathfrak{d}^{k}(d_{1a},d_{2bc},d_{3})|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.

Remark 4
.

For the sake of clarity, throughout this paper, we will utilize the symbol
d,d_{a},d_{bc}
to represent functions obeying

|d|,|d_{a}|,|d_{bc}|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.

We now can start to derive a priori estimates for solutions
R(\theta,\varphi)
to the equation (
3.5
).

3.2.1.
C^{0}
Estimate

A direct application of the maximum (minimum) principle on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
yields

Lemma 3.2
.

For
\underline{u}\geq 1
, with
R(\theta,\varphi)
being the solution to (
3.5
), the following inequality holds

(3.6)

|R(\theta,\varphi)-2m_{\infty}|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.

Proof.

Denote

R(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1}):=\max\limits_{(\theta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}R(\theta,\varphi),\quad R(\theta_{2},\varphi_{2}):=\min\limits_{(\theta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}R(\theta,\varphi).

Then at
(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})
, we have
\nabla^{\prime}R(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})=\nabla R(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})=0
and
\Delta^{\prime}_{M}R(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})\leq 0
. This gives

\begin{split}0=&\Delta^{\prime}_{M}R(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})-\frac{1}{R(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})}+\frac{2m_{\infty}}{R(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})^{2}}+\frac{d_{3}}{R(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})^{2}}\leq\frac{-R(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})+2m_{\infty}+d_{3}}{R(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})^{2}}.\end{split}

Hence we deduce

R(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})\leq 2m_{\infty}+d_{3}\leq 2m_{\infty}\Big(1+O(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}})\Big).

In the same fashion, we can also prove

R(\theta_{2},\varphi_{2})\geq 2m_{\infty}\Big(1-O(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}})\Big).

Combining these two estimates, we thus obtain (
3.6
).
∎

3.2.2.
C^{1}
Estimate

To establish the gradient estimate, we will employ Bochner’s formula, which takes the form:

\Delta^{\prime}_{M}|\nabla^{\prime}R|^{2}=2|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|^{2}+2\textrm{Ric}_{M}(\nabla^{\prime}R,\nabla^{\prime}R)+2\nabla^{{}^{\prime}a}R\nabla^{\prime}_{a}(\Delta^{\prime}_{M}R).

Here
\nabla^{\prime}
represents the induced covariant derivative on the deformed 2-sphere
M
. It is important to note that
\nabla^{\prime}R=\nabla R
, with
\nabla
being the the induced covariant derivative on
S_{\underline{u},r}
. In the below, when there is no danger of confusion, we will use
\nabla R
to substitute
\nabla^{\prime}R
.

To derive the desired
C^{0}
estimate for
\nabla R
, we express
\Delta^{\prime}_{M}\Big(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\Big)
with

h(R)=1+\frac{1}{2m_{\infty}^{2}}(R-2m_{\infty})^{2}.

Applying the Bochner’s formula and plugging in (
3.5
), we obtain

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\Delta^{\prime}_{M}\Big(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\Big)\\
=&h^{\prime}(R)\Delta_{M}^{\prime}R|\nabla R|^{2}+h^{\prime\prime}(R)|\nabla R|^{4}+\frac{2h^{\prime}(R)\nabla^{{}^{\prime}a}R}{h(R)}\cdot\nabla^{\prime}_{a}\Big(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\Big)-\frac{2h^{\prime}(R)h^{\prime}(R)}{h(R)}|\nabla R|^{4}\\
&+h(R)\bigg((2|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|^{2}+2\textrm{Ric}_{M}(\nabla R,\nabla R)+2\nabla^{{}^{\prime}a}R\nabla^{\prime}_{a}(\Delta_{M}^{\prime}R)\Big)\\
=&h^{\prime}(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{R}|\nabla R|^{2}+\frac{1}{R}-\frac{2m_{\infty}}{R^{2}}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\big(d_{1a}\nabla^{a}R+d_{2bc}\nabla^{b}R\nabla^{c}R+d_{3}\big)\bigg)+h^{\prime\prime}(R)|\nabla R|^{4}\\
&+\frac{2h^{\prime}(R)\nabla^{{}^{\prime}a}R}{h(R)}\cdot\nabla^{\prime}_{a}\Big(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\Big)-\frac{2h^{\prime}(R)h^{\prime}(R)}{h(R)}|\nabla R|^{4}+h(R)\Big(2|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|^{2}+2\textrm{Ric}_{M}(\nabla R,\nabla R)\Big)\\
&+2h(R)\nabla^{{}^{\prime}a}R\nabla^{\prime}_{a}\bigg(\frac{1}{R}|\nabla R|^{2}+\frac{1}{R}-\frac{2m_{\infty}}{R^{2}}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\big(d_{1a}\nabla^{a}R+d_{2bc}\nabla^{b}R\nabla^{c}R+d_{3}\big)\bigg).\end{split}

In view of the fact

\displaystyle\nabla^{\prime}_{a}\bigg(d(R(\theta,\varphi),\theta,\varphi)\bigg)=

\displaystyle\bigg(\nabla_{a}(d)+f\nabla_{a}R\cdot e_{3}(d)\bigg)(R(\theta,\varphi),\theta,\varphi)=\frac{d_{1a}+d_{2}\nabla_{a}R}{R},

we deduce

\begin{split}&\nabla^{{}^{\prime}a}R\nabla^{\prime}_{a}\bigg(\frac{1}{R}|\nabla R|^{2}+\frac{1}{R}-\frac{2m_{\infty}}{R^{2}}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\big(d_{1a}\nabla^{a}R+d_{2bc}\nabla^{b}R\nabla^{c}R+d_{3}\big)\bigg)\\
=&-\frac{1}{R^{2}}|\nabla R|^{4}+(-\frac{1}{R^{2}}+\frac{4m_{\infty}}{R^{3}})|\nabla R|^{2}+\frac{1}{R}\nabla^{a}R\nabla^{\prime}_{a}(|\nabla R|^{2})+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\nabla^{a}R\bigg(d_{6b}+d_{7bc}\nabla^{c}R\bigg)\nabla^{\prime}_{a}\nabla^{{}^{\prime}b}R\\
&+\frac{1}{R^{3}}\bigg(d_{1a}|\nabla R|^{2}\nabla^{a}R+d_{2ab}\nabla^{a}R\nabla^{b}R|\nabla R|^{2}+d_{3ab}\nabla^{a}R\nabla^{b}R+d_{4}|\nabla R|^{2}+d_{5a}\nabla^{a}R\bigg).\end{split}

Together with

2h(R)\cdot\nabla^{a}R\nabla^{\prime}_{a}(|\nabla R|^{2})=2\nabla^{a}R\nabla^{\prime}_{a}(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2})-2h^{\prime}(R)|\nabla R|^{4},

we then arrive at the below estimate

(3.8)

\begin{split}\Delta^{\prime}_{M}\Big(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\Big)\geq&h(R)\Big(2|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|^{2}+2\textrm{Ric}_{M}(\nabla R,\nabla R)\Big)+\bigg(\frac{2h^{\prime}(R)}{h(R)}+\frac{2}{R}\bigg)\nabla^{\prime a}R\nabla^{\prime}_{a}\Big(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\Big)\\
&+\Big(\frac{h^{\prime}(R)}{R}+h^{\prime\prime}(R)-\frac{2h^{\prime}(R)h^{\prime}(R)}{h(R)}-\frac{2h(R)}{R^{2}}-\frac{2h^{\prime}(R)}{R}+\frac{d_{1}}{R^{2}}\Big)|\nabla R|^{4}\\
&+\Big(h^{\prime}(R)(\frac{1}{R}-\frac{2m}{R^{2}}+\frac{d_{2}}{R^{2}})+2h(R)(\frac{d_{3}}{R^{3}}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}+\frac{4m_{\infty}}{R^{3}})\Big)|\nabla R|^{2}\\
&+\frac{h(R)}{R^{2}}\nabla^{a}R\bigg(d_{5b}+d_{6bc}\nabla^{c}R\bigg)\nabla^{\prime}_{a}\nabla^{{}^{\prime}b}R\\
&+d_{4}\bigg(|\frac{h^{\prime}(R)}{R^{2}}|\cdot|\nabla R|^{3}+\frac{h(R)}{R^{3}}|\nabla R|^{3}+\frac{h(R)}{R^{3}}|\nabla R|\bigg).\end{split}

Using the construction

h(R)=1+\frac{1}{2m_{\infty}^{2}}(R-2m_{\infty})^{2},

and the established
C^{0}
estimate

|R-2m_{\infty}|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}\quad\text{with}\ \varepsilon_{0}\ \text{being sufficiently small},

we derive

\begin{split}&|h(R)-1|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}},\quad|h^{\prime}(R)|=\frac{1}{m_{\infty}^{2}}|R-2m_{\infty}|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}},\quad h^{\prime\prime}(R)=\frac{1}{m_{\infty}^{2}},\\
&\frac{h^{\prime}(R)}{R}+h^{\prime\prime}(R)-\frac{2h^{\prime}(R)h^{\prime}(R)}{h(R)}-\frac{2h(R)}{R^{2}}-\frac{2h^{\prime}(R)}{R}+\frac{d_{1}}{R^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{4m_{\infty}^{2}},\\
&h^{\prime}(R)(\frac{1}{R}-\frac{2m}{R^{2}}+\frac{d_{2}}{R^{2}})+2h(R)(\frac{d_{3}}{R^{3}}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}+\frac{4m_{\infty}}{R^{3}})\geq\frac{1}{R^{2}}.\end{split}

With these estimates, we go back to (
3.8
) and obtain

\begin{split}&\Delta^{\prime}_{M}\Big(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\Big)-\frac{2h^{\prime}(R)\nabla^{{}^{\prime}a}R}{h(R)}\cdot\nabla^{\prime}_{a}\Big(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\Big)-\frac{2}{R}\nabla^{\prime}_{a}(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2})\\
\geq&h(R)\Big(2|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|^{2}+2\textrm{Ric}_{M}(\nabla R,\nabla R)+\frac{d_{1}}{R^{2}}(|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2})|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|\Big)\\
&+\frac{1}{4m_{\infty}^{2}}|\nabla R|^{4}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}|\nabla R|^{2}+\frac{d_{2}}{R^{3}}(|\nabla R|^{3}+|\nabla R|).\end{split}

To treat
2\textrm{Ric}_{M}(\nabla R,\nabla R)
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
\underline{u}\geq 1
, with
R(\theta,\varphi)
being the solution to (
3.5
), the following inequality holds

(3.9)

2\textrm{Ric}_{M}(\nabla R,\nabla R)\geq-d(|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}R|)|\nabla R|^{2}\quad\text{with}\quad 0\leq d\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.

Proof.

For the 2-dimensional manifold
M
, we have

\text{Ric}_{M}=K^{\prime}g_{M}

with
K^{\prime}
being its Gaussian curvature. Hence we get

2\textrm{Ric}_{M}(\nabla R,\nabla R)=2K^{\prime}|\nabla R|^{2}.

Using the frame
\{e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime},e_{3}^{\prime},e_{4}^{\prime}\}
, by the Gauss equation, we can express
K^{\prime}
as

(3.10)

K^{\prime}=-\rho^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\chi}^{\prime}\cdot\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{4}\mbox{tr}\chi^{\prime}\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}^{\prime}.

Employing (
2.7
) and observing that
\mbox{tr}\chi^{\prime}=0
on
M
, we infer

\begin{split}\hat{\chi}^{\prime}=\chi^{\prime}_{ab}-\mbox{tr}\chi^{\prime}g^{\prime}_{ab}=&\chi_{ab}+\nabla_{a}f\nabla_{b}R+f\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}R+\nabla_{b}f\nabla_{a}R+f\nabla_{b}\nabla_{a}R\\
&+e_{3}(f)f(\nabla_{a}R\nabla_{b}R+\nabla_{b}R\nabla_{a}R)-f^{2}(\underline{\chi}_{ac}\nabla^{c}R\nabla_{b}R+\underline{\chi}_{bc}\nabla^{c}R\nabla_{a}R)\\
&+f(\eta_{b}+\zeta_{b})\nabla_{a}R+f(\eta_{a}+\zeta_{a})\nabla_{b}R+f^{2}|\nabla R|^{2}\chi_{ab}-f^{2}\chi_{ac}\nabla_{b}R\nabla^{c}R\\
&-f^{2}\chi_{bc}\nabla_{a}R\nabla^{c}R-4\underline{\omega}f^{2}\nabla_{a}R\nabla_{b}R.\end{split}

Recall that in our setting the deformed null frame
\{e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime},e_{3}^{\prime},e_{4}^{\prime}\}
and
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
have the following connection

e_{3}^{\prime}=e_{3},\quad e_{a}^{\prime}=e_{a}+fe_{a}(R)e_{3},\quad e_{4}^{\prime}=e_{4}+2fe^{a}(R)e_{a}+f^{2}|\nabla R|^{2}e_{3}.

Therefore, a direct computation yields

\begin{split}\underline{\chi}^{\prime}(e_{a}^{\prime},e_{b}^{\prime})=&g(D_{e^{\prime}_{a}}e_{3},e_{b}^{\prime})=g(D_{e_{a}}e_{3}+fe_{a}(R)e_{3},e_{b}+fe_{b}(R)e_{3})=g(D_{e_{a}}e_{3},e_{b})=\underline{\chi}(e_{a},e_{b}),\end{split}

which gives

\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}^{\prime}=\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi},\quad\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{\prime}=\hat{\underline{\chi}}.

As for
\rho^{\prime}
, by definition we have

\begin{split}\rho^{\prime}&=\frac{1}{4}R(e_{4}+2fe^{a}(R)e_{a}+f^{2}|\nabla R|^{2}e_{3},e_{3},e_{4}+2fe^{b}(R)e_{b}+f^{2}|\nabla R|^{2}e_{3},e_{3})\\
&=\rho-2f\underline{\beta}_{b}\nabla^{b}R+f^{2}\underline{\alpha}_{bc}\nabla^{c}R\nabla^{b}R.\end{split}

Plugging this into (
3.10
), together with the estimate for
f
as well as hyperbolic estimates in
[
24
]
, we can bound
K^{\prime}
from below as

\begin{split}K^{\prime}=&-\rho+2f\underline{\beta}_{b}\nabla^{b}R-f^{2}\underline{\alpha}_{bc}\nabla^{c}R\nabla^{b}R+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{ab}\chi_{ab}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{ab}(\nabla_{a}f\nabla_{b}R+f\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}R+\nabla_{b}f\nabla_{a}R+f\nabla_{b}\nabla_{a}R)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{ab}e_{3}(f)f(\nabla_{a}R\nabla_{b}R+\nabla_{b}R\nabla_{a}R)-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{ab}f^{2}(\underline{\chi}_{ac}\nabla^{c}R\nabla_{b}R+\underline{\chi}_{bc}\nabla^{c}R\nabla_{a}R)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{ab}f(\eta_{b}+\zeta_{b})\nabla_{a}R+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{ab}f(\eta_{a}+\zeta_{a})\nabla_{b}R+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{ab}f^{2}|\nabla R|^{2}\chi_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{ab}f^{2}\chi_{ac}\nabla_{b}R\nabla^{c}R\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{ab}f^{2}\chi_{bc}\nabla_{a}R\nabla^{c}R-2\hat{\underline{\chi}}^{ab}\underline{\omega}f^{2}\nabla_{a}R\nabla_{b}R\\
\geq&\frac{2m_{\infty}}{R^{3}}-d(|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}R|+1)\geq-d(|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}R|)\quad\text{with}\ 0\leq d\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.\end{split}

Thus, we can conclude (
3.9
).
∎

We also need to compare
|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|
with
|\nabla^{2}R|
. By the property of covariant derivative, we have

\begin{split}\nabla_{a}^{\prime}\nabla_{b}^{\prime}R=\nabla_{a}^{\prime}\nabla_{b}R=&(\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}+f\nabla_{a}R\nabla_{3}\nabla_{b})R\\
=&\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}R-f\hat{\underline{\chi}}_{bc}\nabla_{a}R\nabla^{c}R-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}\nabla_{a}R\nabla_{b}R.\end{split}

Using established estimates for
f,\hat{\underline{\chi}},\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}
in
[
24
]
, this implies

|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|\geq|\nabla^{2}R|-\frac{1}{2R}|\nabla R|^{2}.

Incorporating with (
3.9
), we hence deduce

\displaystyle 2|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|^{2}+2\textrm{Ric}_{M}(\nabla R,\nabla R)+\frac{d_{1}}{R^{2}}(|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2})|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|^{2}-|d|\bigg(|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2}+|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|+\frac{1}{2R}|\nabla R|^{2}\bigg)|\nabla R|^{2}-|d_{1}|(|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2})|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(2-|d|)|\nabla^{\prime 2}R|^{2}-|d_{2}|\bigg(|\nabla R|^{2}+|\nabla R|^{4}\bigg).

Combining all above estimates, with
\varepsilon_{0}
being sufficiently small, we then arrive at

(3.11)

\begin{split}&\Delta^{\prime}_{M}\Big(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\Big)-\frac{2h^{\prime}(R)\nabla^{{}^{\prime}a}R}{h(R)}\cdot\nabla^{\prime}_{a}\Big(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\Big)-\frac{2}{R^{2}}\nabla^{\prime}_{a}(h(R)|\nabla R|^{2})\\
\geq&-|d|(|\nabla R|+|\nabla R|^{2}+|\nabla R|^{3}+|\nabla R|^{4})+\frac{1}{4m_{\infty}^{2}}|\nabla R|^{4}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}|\nabla R|^{2}\\
\geq&\frac{1}{8m_{\infty}^{2}}|\nabla R|^{4}+\frac{1}{2R^{2}}|\nabla R|^{2}-|d|^{2},\end{split}

where
|d|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}
.

We denote

[h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}](\theta_{3},\varphi_{3}):=\max\limits_{\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}[h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}](\theta,\varphi).

Applying the maximum principle, we obtain

0\geq\frac{1}{8m_{\infty}^{2}}|\nabla R|^{4}(\theta_{3},\varphi_{3})+\frac{1}{2R^{2}}|\nabla R|^{2}(\theta_{3},\varphi_{3})-d\geq\frac{1}{2R^{2}}|\nabla R|^{2}(\theta_{3},\varphi_{3})-|d|^{2}.

By the construction of
h
, we further deduce

h(R)|\nabla R|^{2}\leq h\big(R(\theta_{3},\varphi_{3})\big)|\nabla R|^{2}(\theta_{3},\varphi_{3})\leq 4|d|^{2}R^{2}.

Noting that
1/2\leq h(R)\leq 2
, we thus derive

|\nabla R|^{2}\leq 8|d|^{2}R^{2}\lesssim(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}})^{2}.

This gives the desired
C^{1}
estimate

(3.12)

|\nabla R|(\theta,\varphi)\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}\quad\text{for all}\ (\theta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

3.2.3.
C^{1,q}
Estimate and
C^{2,q}
Estimate

Examining the proof of Theorem 13.6 in
[
21
]
, one can see that obtaining
C^{1,q}
estimate from the gradient estimate for
R
follows directly. We have

Proposition 3.4
.

Assuming that
R
is the solution to (
3.5
), then there exists
0<q<1
independent of
\varepsilon_{0}
,
\underline{u}
, such that

\|\nabla R\|_{C^{0,q}(M_{\underline{u}})}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.

With a priori
C^{1,q}
estimates for
R
, through the direct application of the standard interior Schauder estimate, we obtain the
C^{2,q}
estimates.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assuming that
R
is the solution to (
3.5
), with the same
q\in(0,1)
as in
, the following inequality holds

\|R-2m_{\infty}\|_{C^{2,q}(M_{\underline{u}})}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\underline{u}^{1+\delta_{dec}}}.

3.3.
Continuity Argument

With above derived a priori estimates, we conduct the method of continuity to solve for the MOTS. We will solve the below quasilinear elliptic equation

\Delta^{\prime}_{M}R+\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla R+(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla R|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi=0.

Based on this equation, we insert a parameter
\lambda\in[0,1]
and consider

\begin{split}F(R(\theta,\varphi),\lambda):&=\Delta^{\prime}_{M}R(\theta,\varphi)+(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla R(\theta,\varphi)|^{2}\\
&+\lambda\Big(\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla R+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi\Big)\\
&+(1-\lambda)\bigg(-\frac{1}{R(\theta,\varphi)}+\frac{2m_{\infty}}{R(\theta,\varphi)^{2}}\bigg).\end{split}

For
\lambda=0
, we verify that
R(\theta,\varphi)\equiv 2m_{\infty}
is a solution to

F(R(\theta,\varphi),0)=\Delta^{\prime}_{M}R(\theta,\varphi)+(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla R(\theta,\varphi)|^{2}-\frac{1}{R(\theta,\varphi)}+\frac{2m_{\infty}}{R(\theta,\varphi)^{2}}=0.

For
0\leq\tilde{\lambda}\leq 1
, we assume that
\tilde{R}(\theta,\varphi)
is a solution to

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}}}\tilde{R}(\theta,\varphi)+(e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla\tilde{R}(\theta,\varphi)|^{2}

\displaystyle+\tilde{\lambda}\Big(\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla\tilde{R}+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi\Big)

\displaystyle+(1-\tilde{\lambda})\bigg(-\frac{1}{\tilde{R}(\theta,\varphi)}+\frac{2m_{\infty}}{\tilde{R}(\theta,\varphi)^{2}}\bigg).

Given any
\lambda\in[0,1]
(which may be different from
\tilde{\lambda}
), we are interested in the Fréchet derivative of
F
with respect to
\tilde{R}
at
(\tilde{R}(\theta,\varphi),\tilde{\lambda})
. We have

(3.13)

\begin{split}\partial_{R}F(\tilde{R}(\theta,\varphi),\lambda)[W]=&\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(F(\tilde{R}+\varepsilon W,\lambda)-F(\tilde{R},\lambda)\Big)\\
=&\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}+\varepsilon W}}(\tilde{R}+\varepsilon W)-\Delta^{\prime}_{S_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}}}\tilde{R}\Big)\\
&+\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big[\bigg((e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla(\tilde{R}+\varepsilon W)|^{2}\bigg)|_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}+\varepsilon W}\\
&-\bigg((e_{3}(f)-\frac{1}{2}f\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}-2\underline{\omega}f)|\nabla\tilde{R}|^{2}\bigg)|_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}}\Big]\\
&+\lambda\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big[\bigg(\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla(\tilde{R}+\varepsilon W)+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi\bigg)|_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}+\varepsilon W}\\
&-\bigg(\big(f^{-1}\nabla f+(\eta+\zeta)\big)\cdot\nabla\tilde{R}+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\mbox{tr}\chi\bigg)|_{\underline{u},\tilde{R}}\Big]\\
&+(1-\lambda)\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(-\frac{1}{\tilde{R}+\varepsilon W}+\frac{1}{\tilde{R}}+\frac{2m_{\infty}}{(\tilde{R}+\varepsilon W)^{2}}-\frac{2m_{\infty}}{\tilde{R}^{2}}\Big)\\
=:&I_{1}+I_{2}+\lambda I_{3}+(1-\lambda)I_{4}.\end{split}

Utilizing
Lemma

2.5
and its proof, we now establish the invertibility of
\partial_{R}F(\tilde{R},\lambda)
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\tilde{\lambda}\in[0,1]
and assume that
\tilde{R}
is the solution to
F(\tilde{R},\tilde{\lambda})=0
. Then the operator
\partial_{R}F(\tilde{R},\lambda)[W]:C^{2,q}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\rightarrow C^{0,q}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
is invertible for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Here
q\in(0,1)
is the same Hölder exponent as in the a priori estimates for
\tilde{R}
.

Proof.

Applying
Lemma

2.5
, together with the established hyperbolic estimates in
[
24
]
,

Definition 4.1
.

For a given
\underline{u}\in J

M_{\underline{u}}=\{r=R(\underline{u},\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\}

and if
\mbox{tr}\tilde{\chi}\geq 0

\tilde{R}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\geq R(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
.

Leveraging the strong maximum principle for elliptic equations arising from the definition of outgoing null expansion, we can exclude situations that
\tilde{R}=R
only occurs at some but not all points
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that the null comparison principle holds for the MOTS

\widetilde{\Sigma}=\{r=\tilde{R}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\}

\tilde{R}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\equiv R(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
or
\tilde{R}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})<R(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
, and if
\mbox{tr}\tilde{\chi}|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\geq 0

\tilde{R}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\equiv R(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
or
\tilde{R}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})>R(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})

. Recalling

We then define the below operator

Let

Definition 4.4

Given a set

Lemma 4.6

Let

foliated by spacelike sections
\Sigma_{s}

Proof.

It suffices to show that any non-zero tangent vector

and a future directed timelike vector

(2)

Now we select

Along

0=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Delta}N_{r}(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}-\chi^{ab}\chi_{ab}-2\tilde{\eta}^{a}\tilde{\eta}_{a}),

where we use the fact that the integral of
\nabla^{a}\tilde{\eta}_{a}
is
0
according to the divergence theorem on
M_{\underline{u}}
. By further employing the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, for the first term on the right side of (
5.15
), we obtain

(5.16)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Delta}N_{r}(|\chi|^{2}+2|\tilde{\eta}|^{2})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Delta}N_{r}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\int_{M_{\underline{u}}}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}=4\pi(r_{2}-r_{1}).

To derive the remaining part on the right of (
5.15
), that concerns the angular momentum, we proceed to contract
H^{i}_{V}
with
X
and evaluate the integral

\int_{\Delta}\Omega X_{i}H^{i}_{V}=\int_{\Delta}\Omega X_{i}\nabla_{j}(K^{ij}-\mbox{tr}Kq^{ij}).

It follows from the integration by parts that

(5.17)

\displaystyle 0=-\int_{M_{\underline{u}_{2}}}\Omega j^{X}+\int_{M_{\underline{u}_{1}}}\Omega j^{X}-\int_{\Delta}D_{j}(\Omega X)_{i}P^{ij}.

At the same time, with
\Omega=\Omega(R_{M})
, we have
D_{j}\Omega=|\partial\Omega|\hat{r}_{j}
. This implies

D_{j}(\Omega X)_{i}=(D_{j}\Omega)X_{i}+\Omega D_{j}X_{i}=|\partial\Omega|X_{i}\hat{r}_{j}+\Omega D_{j}X_{i}.

Substituting the above expression into (
5.17
) yields

(5.18)

\int_{M_{\underline{u}_{2}}}\Omega j^{X}-\int_{M_{\underline{u}_{1}}}\Omega j^{X}-\int_{\Omega_{1}}^{\Omega_{2}}d\Omega\int_{M_{\underline{u}}}j^{X}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Delta}\Omega P^{ij}\mathcal{L}_{X}q_{ij},

where we use the fact
X^{i}\hat{r}_{i}=0
. The sum of (
5.16
) and (
5.18
) then gives the desired conservation law (
5.15
).
∎

We then move forward to prove the differential form of the first law. This was formulated in
[
13
]
by Ashtekar-Krishnan. The flux across
\Delta=\mathcal{AH}_{s}\cap\{\underline{u}_{1}\leq\underline{u}\leq\underline{u}_{2}\}
is defined to be

\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}:=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\Delta}\bigg(N_{r}(|\chi|^{2}+2|\tilde{\eta}|^{2})-\Omega P^{ab}\mathcal{L}_{X}q_{ab}\bigg).

Assume there is a well-defined horizon energy
\tilde{E}_{M}
such that, at each MOTS
M_{\underline{u}}
, with
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}
defined above, it holds

\tilde{E}_{M}(\underline{u}_{2})-\tilde{E}_{M}(\underline{u}_{1})=\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}.

We also define the generalized angular momentum with respect to
X
along
M_{\underline{u}}
to be

\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}:=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{M_{\underline{u}}}j^{X}.

With these notations, we can rewrite (
5.15
) as

(5.19)

\frac{1}{2}(r_{2}-r_{2})+\Omega_{2}\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}(\underline{u}_{2})-\Omega_{1}\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}(\underline{u}_{1})-\int_{\Omega_{1}}^{\Omega_{2}}\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}d\Omega=\tilde{E}_{M}(\underline{u}_{2})-\tilde{E}_{M}(\underline{u}_{1}).

Taking a differentiation on both sides of (
5.19
), by virtue of the fact

dr=\frac{dr}{dR_{M}}(\frac{dA_{M}}{dR_{M}})^{-1}dA_{M}=\frac{1}{8\pi R_{M}}\frac{dr}{dR_{M}}dA_{M}

and

d(\Omega\tilde{J}^{X}_{M})-\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}d\Omega=\Omega d\tilde{J}^{X}_{M},

we then derive the differential form of the first law along
\mathcal{AH}_{s}
:

d\tilde{E}_{M}:=\frac{\overline{\kappa}}{8\pi}dA_{M}+\Omega d\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}.

Here
\overline{\kappa}=\frac{1}{2R_{M}}\frac{dr}{dR_{M}}
is called the effective surface gravity in
[
14
]
and
\Omega
corresponds to the angular velocity of MOTS
M_{\underline{u}}
.

With a specific choice of functions
r=r(R_{M})
and
\Omega=\Omega(R_{M})
, the horizon energy
\tilde{E}_{M}
can be expressed explicitly as suggested in
[
13
,
14
]
. Noting that
\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}
can be regarded as a function of
R_{M}
along
\mathcal{AH}_{s}
, we set

\overline{\kappa}=\kappa_{0}(R_{M},\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}),\quad\Omega=\Omega_{0}(R_{M},\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}),

where

\kappa_{0}(R,J):=\frac{R^{4}-4J^{2}}{2R^{3}\sqrt{R^{4}+4J^{2}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega_{0}(R,J):=\frac{\sqrt{R^{4}+4J^{2}}}{2R}.

Thus
r=r(R_{M})
can be solved through the equation
\frac{1}{2R_{M}}\frac{dr}{dR_{M}}=\overline{\kappa}(R_{M})
. Recall that the canonical horizon mass is defined to be

M(R,J):=\frac{1}{2R}\sqrt{R^{4}+4J^{2}}.

Then a straightforward check yields

dM:=\frac{\kappa_{0}}{8\pi}dA+\Omega_{0}dJ\qquad\quad\text{with}\ A:=4\pi R^{2}.

According to the choice of
r=r(R_{M})
and
\Omega=\Omega(R_{M})
, we then conclude

\tilde{E}_{M}=M(R_{M},\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}).

We further demonstrate the consistency between the first law along
\mathcal{AH}_{s}
and the first law along the null piece
\mathcal{AH}_{n}
as presented in (
5.8
). Notice that the angular momentum
\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}
satisfies

\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{M_{\underline{u}}}j^{X}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{M_{\underline{u}}}K_{ij}X^{i}\hat{r}^{j}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{M_{\underline{u}}}\zeta^{a}X_{a}.

For the angular momentum of MOTS
M_{\underline{u}}
along
\mathcal{AH}_{n}
, it reads

J_{M}:=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{M_{\underline{u}}}\tilde{\omega}(X).

Since
X\in TM_{\underline{u}}
, we deduce

\tilde{\omega}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{D}_{X}l,n)=-\zeta^{a}X_{a}.

This leads to

J_{M}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{M_{\underline{u}}}\zeta^{a}X_{a}=\tilde{J}^{X}_{M}.

Hence the definition of the angular momentum of MOTS
M_{\underline{u}}
along
\mathcal{AH}_{s}
matches with the corresponding one defined on
\mathcal{AH}_{n}
. Merging these together, we then get the unified form

d{E}_{M}=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}dA_{M}+\Omega d{J}_{M}.

5.3.
The second law

In black hole thermodynamics, the second law asserts that the entropy is a non-decreasing function of towards the future. Recall that
A_{M}
is defined as the area of MOTS
M_{\underline{u}}=\{r=R(\underline{u},\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\}
along
\underline{H}_{\underline{u}}
and it depends only on
\underline{u}
. Defining
A_{M}(\underline{u})
as the entropy as in
[
3
,
4
,
14
]
, in below we prove that
A_{M}(\underline{u})
is non-decreasing with respect to
\underline{u}
.

Notice that at any point on the MOTS
M_{\underline{u}}
, we have a null frame
\{e^{\prime}_{1},e^{\prime}_{2},e_{3}^{\prime},e_{4}^{\prime}\}
with
TM_{\underline{u}}=\text{span}\{e^{\prime}_{1},e^{\prime}_{2}\}
and the outgoing (with respect to
e_{4}^{\prime}
) and incoming (with respect to
e_{3}^{\prime}
) null expansions of
M_{\underline{u}}
obey
\mbox{tr}\chi^{\prime}=0
and
\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}^{\prime}<0
, respectively.
Choose a non-zero vector
X
tangent to
\mathcal{AH}
and normal to the MOTS
M_{\underline{u}}
. When it points outwards, since
X\in\text{span}\{e^{\prime}_{3},e^{\prime}_{4}\}
, we can express it as

(5.20)

X=\alpha e_{4}^{\prime}+\beta e_{3}^{\prime}\qquad\text{with}\ \alpha\geq 0.

Note that
\alpha\neq 0
and this is because the incoming null direction
e_{3}^{\prime}
is transverse to
\mathcal{AH}
. Recalling (
2.5
), we now have

e_{3}^{\prime}=e_{3}\quad\text{and}\quad e_{4}^{\prime}=e_{4}+2fe^{a}(R)e_{a}+f^{2}|\nabla R|^{2}e_{3}.

A direct check gives

e_{3}^{\prime}(\underline{u})=e_{3}(\underline{u})=0,\quad e_{4}^{\prime}(\underline{u})=e_{4}(\underline{u})>0.

With
X
acting on
A_{M}
, we get

(5.21)

X(A_{M})=c\frac{dA_{M}}{d\underline{u}}\qquad\text{with}\quad c=X(\underline{u})=\alpha e_{4}^{\prime}(\underline{u})>0.

On the other hand, a straightforward computation implies

(5.22)

\begin{split}X(A_{M})=\int_{M_{\underline{u}}}\mbox{div }_{M_{\underline{u}}}X=\int_{M_{\underline{u}}}\alpha\mbox{tr}\chi^{\prime}+\beta\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}^{\prime}=\int_{M_{\underline{u}}}\beta\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}^{\prime}.\end{split}

Within the null piece
\mathcal{AH}_{n}
, note that
X
must be null, and according to (
5.20
) we thus have
\beta\equiv 0
. Applying (
5.22
), this yields
X(A_{M})=0
and via (
5.21
) this further implies
\frac{dA_{M}}{d\underline{u}}=0
in the null piece
\mathcal{AH}_{n}
. As for the spacelike piece
\mathcal{AH}_{s}
, observing that
\beta<0
(since
X
is spacelike) and
\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}^{\prime}<0
, combining (
5.21
) and (
5.22
) we hence deduce that
\frac{dA_{M}}{d\underline{u}}>0
. Summarizing the above analysis, we then confirm the second law of black hole thermodynamics along
\mathcal{AH}
:

Proposition 5.4
.

Defining
A_{M}(\underline{u})
(the area of MOTS
M_{\underline{u}}
) as the entropy, along
\mathcal{AH}
it is a non-decreasing function with respect to
\underline{u}
. More specifically, we have

\frac{dA_{M}}{d\underline{u}}=0\,\,\text{in the null piece}\,\,\mathcal{AH}_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{dA_{M}}{d\underline{u}}>0\,\,\text{in the spacelike piece}\,\,\mathcal{AH}_{s}.

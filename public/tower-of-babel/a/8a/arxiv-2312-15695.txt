Cameron–Martin Type Theorem for a Class of non-Gaussian Measures
Mohamed Erraoui, José Luís da Silva

Abstract
In this paper, we study the quasi-invariant property of a class of non-Gaussian measures. These measures are associated with the family of generalized grey Brownian motions. We identify the Cameron–Martin space and derive the explicit Radon-Nikodym density in terms of the Wiener integral with respect to the fractional Brownian motion. Moreover, we show an integration by parts formula for the derivative operator in the directions of the Cameron–Martin space. As a consequence, we derive the closability of both the derivative and the corresponding gradient operators.

Introduction

The main goal of this paper (see Theorem
3.5
below) is to prove the Cameron–Martin theorem for the class of non-Gaussian processes, called generalized grey Brownian motion (ggBm) denoted by
B_{\beta,\alpha}
,
0<\beta\leq 1
and
0<\alpha\leq 2
. Specifically, we are looking for suitable (random) shifts
\xi
such that the distributions of
B_{\beta,\alpha}
and
B_{\beta,\alpha}+\xi
are equivalent. In other words, we are studying the quasi-invariance property of the ggBm law.

W. Schneider
[
Sch90a
,
Sch90b
,
Sch92
]
was the first to introduce this type of process, which he called grey Brownian motion (gBm). This provided stochastic models for slow-anomalous diffusion. A. Mura then extended this to ggBm, which can be used as non-Markovian stochastic models for either slow or fast-anomalous diffusion; see
[
Mur08
]
.
The remarkable characteristic of ggBm is that it can be expressed in terms of
B^{\alpha/2}
, a fractional Brownian motion (fBm) with Hurst parameter
\alpha/2
. The first representation of ggBm is given in
[
MP08
]
as the product of fBm
B^{\alpha/2}
and a non-negative random variable
Y_{\beta}
with
M
-Wright density function (see Subsection
2.3
below for details), that is,

\big\{B_{\beta,\alpha}(t)\;t\geq 0\big\}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}\big\{\sqrt{Y_{\beta}}B^{\alpha/2}(t),\;t\geq 0\big\},

(1)

where
\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}
means equality in law.
The representation (
1
) is particularly interesting since it allows us to infer a variety of properties of ggBm
B_{\beta,\alpha}
from those of fBm
B^{\alpha/2}
. For example, the Hölder continuity of the trajectories.

An alternative representation of ggBm is given in terms of a subordination of fBm. The stochastic representation through subordinated processes is very natural, as it gives a direct physical interpretation, see Remark 9.3 in
[
Mur08
]
. However, the subordinated representations already given for ggBm in
[
MTM08
,
dSE15
,
dSE20
]
represent ggBm only in one dimensional time marginal law. Therefore, they cannot characterize the complete stochastic structure of the process, nor can they substitute ggBm when more than one marginal law is involved, as in the Cameron-Martin theorem.
So, another representation involving finite-dimensional distributions is needed for the problem that concerns us here. Our investigation yields the following representation (see Proposition
2.14
)

\big\{B_{\beta,\alpha}(t)\;t\geq 0\big\}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}\big\{B^{\alpha/2}(tY_{\beta}^{1/\alpha}),\;t\geq 0\big\},

(2)

which allows us to achieve the main goal of the paper, that is, the Cameron–Martin theorem for ggBm, the integration by parts formula, and the closability of the derivative and gradient operators, see Sections
3
and
4
below.
Let us now explain the approach we adopt.

First, the general setting is as follows:
Let
\mathbb{W}
be the classical Wiener space,
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})
its Borel
\sigma
-algebra, and
H=\alpha/2\in[1/2,1)
. Furthermore, let
\mathbb{P}_{H}
be the unique probability measure on
\mathbb{W}
such that the canonical process
W^{H}
is a fBm. The distribution of the random variable

as the identity map

\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+},\;\tau\mapsto\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}(\tau):=\tau.

The ggBm is realized on the probability space

After considering three essential elements - the representation (
2
) for ggBm, the Cameron–Martin type theorems for fBm, and subordinated Bm (
[

]
) - we conclude that the natural choice for

is

,

, where

is the Cameron–Martin space associated with fBm

; see Subsection

. Our proof is based on an appropriate use of the exponential martingale and the Cameron–Martin theorem for fBm.

It is a well-known fact that an important consequence of the quasi-invariant property is the integration by parts formula.
This is what we have obtained in Theorems
4.3
and
4.8
for the partial derivative of a bounded smooth cylinder functions

in the direction of

Finally, using the integration by parts formula, we prove in Theorem

,

, of the gradient

, the unique element in

verifying

(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)=\big(\nabla_{1}F(w,\tau),h\big)_{\mathcal{H}_{H}},\quad w\in\mathbb{W},\;\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

where

denotes the inner product given in Eq. (
8
); see also the diagram in Fig.

.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we discuss the types of processes we are working with, namely the fBm and ggBm. In addition, the Cameron–Martin space and the Wiener integral w.r.t. fBm are reviewed. Finally, two useful representations of ggBm and its canonical realization are shown.

2.1
Fractional Brownian Motion

In this subsection, we review the definition and important properties of the fBm motion that are necessary for what follows. For more
details, see, for example, the book
[
Mis08
]
, and references
therein.

Definition 2.1

(Fractional Brownian motion)
.

A (one-side, normalized) fBm with Hurst index
H\in\big[1/2,1)
is a Gaussian process
B^{H}=\big\{B^{H}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\big\}

on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, satisfying the properties

1.

B^{H}(0)=0
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., that is,
B^{H}
starts at zero
almost surely.

2.

\mathbb{E}[B^{H}(t)]=0,

t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the process
B^{H}

is centered.

3.

The covariance kernel
R_{H}
of
B^{H}
is given, for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, by

R_{H}(t,s):=\mathbb{E}\big[B^{H}(t)B^{H}(s)\big]=\frac{1}{2}\big(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\big).

(4)

Remark 2.2
.

1.

Note that
\mathbb{E}\big[\big(B^{H}(t)-B^{H}(s)\big)^{2}\big]=|t-s|^{2H}

and
B^{H}
is a Gaussian process, then
B^{H}
has a modification with continuous trajectories, according to the Kolmogorov theorem. We will always consider such a modification and keep the same notation.

2.

In general, for every
n\geq 1
, it holds

\mathbb{E}\big[|B^{H}(t)-B^{H}(s)|^{n}\big]=\sqrt{2^{n}\pi^{-1}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)|t-s|^{nH}.

3.

For
H=1/2
the process
B^{1/2}
becomes the standard
Brownian motion (Bm) with covariance kernel
R_{1/2}(t,s)=t\wedge s
.

4.

For any
a>0
, the process
\{a^{-H}B^{H}(at),t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\}
has the same
distribution as
B^{H}
. In other words,
B^{H}
is a
H
-self-similar
process.

5.

The characteristic function has the form

C_{\lambda}(t):=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}B^{H}(t_{k})\right)\right]=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda,\Sigma_{H}(t)\lambda^{\top}\big)\right),

where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\lambda^{\top}
is the transpose of
\lambda
,
t_{k}\geq 0
,
k=1,\dots,n
,

\Sigma_{H}(t)=(R_{H}(t_{k},t_{j}))_{k,j=1}^{n}
is the covariance
matrix and
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

6.

We denote by
\mathbb{P}_{H}:=\mathbb{P}_{B^{H}}
the unique probability measure in
(\mathbb{W},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W}))
such that the canonical process

W(t)(w):=w(t),\quad\forall t\geq 0,\;w\in\mathbb{W},

is a fractional Brownian motion
which we denote by
W^{H}
. For
H=1/2
,
\mathbb{P}_{1/2}
becomes the Wiener measure.

The integral representation of the covariance kernel
R_{H}
plays
an important role in the construction of the Cameron–Martin space introduced in the following. We state this representation of
R_{H}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3

(cf.
[
DU99
]
Lemma 3.1)
.

The covariance kernel
R_{H}
(also called reproducing kernel Hilbert
space (RKHS)) has the following integral representation

R_{H}(t,s)=\int_{0}^{t\wedge s}K_{H}(t,r)K_{H}(s,r)\,\mathrm{d}r,\quad t,s\geq 0.

(5)

The corresponding kernel
K_{H}
is given by

1.

For
H=1/2

K_{1/2}(t,r)=\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t]}(r).

(6)

2.

For
H\in(1/2,1)
(see Lemma 4.3 in
[
Dec22
]
)

K_{H}(t,r)=\frac{r^{1/2-H}}{\Gamma(H-1/2)}\int_{r}^{t}u^{H-1/2}(u-r)^{H-3/2}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}u\,\mathbbm{1}_{(0,t]}(r).

(7)

2.2
Wiener Integral with Respect to Fractional Brownian Motion

Now we introduce the Cameron–Martin space of the fBm with
H\geq 1/2
. It plays an important role in defining the Wiener integral w.r.t. fBm and the Cameron–Martin theorem below; more details can be found in
[
BP88
]
,
[
Dec22
]
and the references therein.

Definition 2.4

(Cameron-Martin space of the fBm)
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}_{H}
be the vector space spanned by the covariance kernel
R_{H}
, that is,

\mathfrak{R}_{H}:=\mathrm{span}\{R_{H}(t,\cdot)\mid t\geq 0\}

equipped with the scalar product

\big(R_{H}(t\,\cdot),R_{H}(s,\cdot)\big)_{\mathfrak{R}_{H}}:=R_{H}(t,s).

(8)

The Cameron–Martin space of the fBm with Hurst index
H
, denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{H}
, is the completion of
\mathfrak{R}_{H}
with respect to the norm associated with the scalar product (
8
).

Remark 2.5
.

The Cameron–Martin space, as defined above, is not very practical. It is possible to have a more suitable characterization of
\mathcal{H}_{H}
, see Theorem 3.3 in
[
DU99
]
(in the special case of the time interval
[0,1]
) or Theorem 3.1 in
[
BP88
]
for a general time interval.

Therefore, our goal is to obtain a more convenient description of the Cameron–Martin space
\mathcal{H}_{H}
.
To this end, we first introduce the linear operator
\mathcal{K}_{H}

associated with the kernel
K_{H}
. More precisely, for every
f\in L^{2}(\lambda)
, we define

(\mathcal{K}_{H}f)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Note that using (
5
), the covariance kernel
R_{H}
is the image of
K_{H}(t,\cdot)
under
\mathcal{K}_{H}
, that is,

\mathcal{K}_{H}(K_{H}(t,\cdot))(s)=R_{H}(t,s),\quad t,s\geq 0.

(9)

Hence
K_{H}(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\lambda)
for any
t\geq 0
, indeed

\|K_{H}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}=R_{H}(t,t)=t^{2H},

ensuring that the operator
\mathcal{K}_{H}
is well-defined on
L^{2}(\lambda)
for every
t\geq 0
.
This implies that

\mathcal{K}_{H}(\mathfrak{K}_{H})=\mathfrak{R}_{H},

(10)

where
\mathfrak{K}_{H}
is the vector space spanned by the kernel
K_{H}(t,\cdot)
, that is,

\mathfrak{K}_{H}:=\mathrm{span}\{K_{H}(t,\cdot)\mid t\geq 0\}.

Using the explicit forms of
K_{H}
in (
6
), (
7
) and applying the Fubini theorem (when
H>1/2
),
\mathcal{K}_{H}f
can be expressed as follows, with
t\geq 0

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}_{H}f)(t)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{2}
, 2cm.

(11a)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}_{H}f)(t)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{x^{H-1/2}}{\Gamma(H-1/2)}\int_{0}^{x}(x-r)^{H-3/2}r^{1/2-H}f(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle H>\frac{1}{2}
.

(11b)

See, for example,
[
DU99
]
for (
11b
).
It is not hard to see from the equalities (
11a
) and (
11b
) that

\forall t\geq 0,\;\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=0\;\Longrightarrow\;f=0,\;\mathrm{a.e.}

(12)

meaning that the operator
\mathcal{K}_{H}
is one-to-one and
\mathfrak{K}_{H}
is dense in
L^{2}(\lambda)
for
H\geq 1/2
. As a consequence,
\mathcal{K}_{H}:L^{2}(\lambda)\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{H}\big(L^{2}(\lambda)\big)
is a bijective isometry when
\mathcal{K}_{H}\big(L^{2}(\lambda)\big)
is provided with the scalar product

(\mathcal{K}_{H}h,\mathcal{K}_{H}g)_{\mathcal{K}_{H}(L^{2}(\lambda))}:=(h,g)_{2},\quad h,g\in L^{2}(\lambda).

On the other hand, (
10
) and (
12
) imply that

\mathcal{K}_{H}:\big(\mathfrak{K}_{H},(\cdot,\cdot)_{2}\big)\longrightarrow\big(\mathfrak{R}_{H},(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathfrak{R}_{H}}\big)

is a bijective isometry. By a density argument,
\mathcal{K}_{H}
is extended to an isomorphism between the Hilbert spaces
L^{2}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{H}_{H}
, that is,

\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{H}:\big(L^{2}(\lambda),(\cdot,\cdot)_{2}\big)\longrightarrow\big(\mathcal{H}_{H},(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}\big).

We are ready to state the following useful characterization of the Cameron–Martin space.

Theorem 2.6

(Characterization of
\mathcal{H}_{H}
)
.

The Cameron–Martin space
\mathcal{H}_{H}
can be identified with
\mathcal{K}_{H}(L^{2}(\lambda))
, the space of functions
h=\mathcal{K}_{H}\dot{h}
,
\dot{h}\in L^{2}(\lambda)
, given by

h(t)=(\mathcal{K}_{H}\dot{h})(t)=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0,

(13)

equipped with the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{K}_{H}(L^{2}(\lambda))}
.

Remark 2.7
.

1.

With the identification given in (
13
), for every
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
the function
\dot{h}\in L^{2}(\lambda)
is given by
\dot{h}:=\mathcal{K}_{H}^{-1}h
.

2.

In the special case
H=1/2
,
\mathcal{H}_{1/2}

is the well-known space
AC(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R})
of absolutely continuous functions, vanishing at
0
, whose derivative belongs to
L^{2}(\lambda)
. The representation (
13
) takes the form

h(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\,\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0,

(14)

where
\dot{h}
is the derivative in the sense of distributions of
h
.

We introduce the Wiener integral w.r.t.
B^{H}
,
H>1/2
, following the approach in
[
DU99
,
Dec22
,
BP88
]
for which we address the interested reader for more details.

Definition 2.8

(Wiener integral)
.

The Wiener integral w.r.t. fBm is defined
as the extension to
\mathcal{H}_{H}
of the isometry

\delta_{H}:\mathcal{K}_{H}(\mathfrak{K}_{H})\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{P})

defined by

R_{H}(t,\cdot)=\mathcal{K}_{H}(K_{H}(t,\cdot))\mapsto\delta_{H}\big(\mathcal{K}_{H}(K_{H}(t,\cdot))\big):=B^{H}(t),\quad\forall t\geq 0.

By linearity we have

\delta_{H}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathcal{K}_{H}(K_{H}(t_{i},\cdot))\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}B^{H}(t_{i}),\quad a_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\;i=1,\dots,n.

In general, for every (deterministic) function
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
, there exists a sequence
h_{n}:=\mathcal{K}_{H}(\dot{h}_{n})
,
\dot{h}_{n}\in\mathfrak{K}_{H}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
h=\mathcal{H}_{H}\mbox{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}
which leads to the Wiener integral w.r.t. fBm of
h

\int_{0}^{\infty}h(t)\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}(t):=\delta_{H}(h):=L^{2}(\mathbb{P})\mbox{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{H}(h_{n})=L^{2}(\mathbb{P})\mbox{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{H}(\dot{h}_{n})).

Therefore,
\delta_{H}(h)
is a centered Gaussian random variable with variance equal to
\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{2}
.

Remark 2.9
.

It is easy to conclude from the definition of Wiener integral that:

1.

The process

B:=\{\delta_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{H}(\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t]}))\mid t\geq 0\}

(15)

is a standard Brownian motion and

\delta_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{H}(u))=\int_{0}^{\infty}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}B(s),\quad u\in L^{2}(\lambda),

where the integral on the right side is taken in the Wiener–Itô
sense. In particular, we have

B^{H}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}B(s).

(16)

2.

Using the Remark
2.7
-1 we have, for every

h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}

\int_{0}^{\infty}h(t)\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{K}_{H}^{-1}h)(t)\,\mathrm{d}B(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}B(t).

(17)

For more details, see
[
Dec22
, Lemma 4.4]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

For every
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
,
H>1/2
, and
any bounded measurable functional
F:\mathbb{W}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(B^{H}+h)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(B^{H})\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}h(t)\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{2}\right)\right]

(18)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:Wiener-integral-fBm-Bm}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(B^{H})\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}B(s)-\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{h}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\right].

Remark 2.11
.

Since for every
h=\mathcal{K}_{H}(\dot{h})\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
with
\dot{h}\in L^{2}(\lambda)
, the exponential martingale
\left\{\mathcal{E}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\cdot}}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}B(s)\right)(t),t\geq 0\right\}
is uniformly integrable, therefore

\mathcal{E}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\cdot}}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}B(s)\right)(\infty)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}B(t)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\dot{h}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right),

is well-defined. Thus, formula (
18
) can be rewritten as follows

\mathbb{E}[F(B^{H}+h)]=\mathbb{E}\left[F(B^{H})\mathcal{E}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\cdot}}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}B(s)\right)(\infty)\right].

(19)

Remark 2.12
.

The above construction may also be realized if we consider a finite time interval
[0,T]
,
T>0
. That is,

1.

the Cameron–Martin space
\mathcal{H}_{H}(T)
is identified as the Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}_{H}\big(L^{2}([0,T],\lambda)\big)
with the scalar product:

2.

the Wiener integral of

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{T}}h(s)\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}(s):=\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{T}}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}B(s),

2.3
Generalized Grey Brownian Motion

2.3.1
Definition and Representations

Let
0<\beta<1
and
1\leq\alpha<2
be given.
A continuous stochastic
process defined on
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)

is called a ggBm, denoted by
B_{\beta,\alpha}=\{B_{\beta,\alpha}(t),\,t\geq 0\}
,
see
[
MM09
]
, if:

1.

B_{\beta,\alpha}(0)=0
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

2.

Any collection
\big\{B_{\beta,\alpha}(t_{1}),\ldots,B_{\beta,\alpha}(t_{n})\big\}

with
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}<\infty
has a characteristic function
given, for any
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
by

\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left(i\sum_{k=1}^{n}\theta_{k}B_{\beta,\alpha}(t_{k})\right)\right)=E_{\beta}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{\top}\Sigma_{\alpha,n}\theta\right),

(21)

where

\Sigma_{\alpha,n}=\big(t_{k}^{\alpha}+t_{j}^{\alpha}-|t_{k}-t_{j}|^{\alpha}\big)_{k,j=1}^{n}

and
E_{\beta}
is the Mittag-Leffler (entire) function

E_{\beta}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(\beta n+1)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

The generalized grey Brownian motion has the following properties:

1.

For each
t\geq 0
, the moments of any order are given by

\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}[B_{\beta,\alpha}^{2n+1}(t)]&=0,\\
\vskip 4.0pt\cr\mathbb{E}[B_{\beta,\alpha}^{2n}(t)]&=\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}\Gamma(\beta n+1)}t^{n\alpha}.\end{cases}

2.

The covariance function has the form

\mathbb{E}[B_{\beta,\alpha}(t)B_{\beta,\alpha}(s)]=\frac{1}{2\Gamma(\beta+1)}\big(t^{\alpha}+s^{\alpha}-|t-s|^{\alpha}\big),\quad t,s\geq 0.

(22)

3.

For each
t,s\geq 0
, the characteristic function of the increments
is

\mathbb{E}\big[e^{i\theta(B_{\beta,\alpha}(t)-B_{\beta,\alpha}(s))}\big]=E_{\beta}\left(-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}|t-s|^{\alpha}\right),\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

(23)

4.

The process
B_{\beta,\alpha}
is non Gaussian,
\alpha/2
-self-similar
with stationary increments.

5.

The ggBm is not a semimartingale. Furthermore,
B_{\alpha,\beta}

cannot be of finite variation in
[0,1]
and by scaling and stationarity
of the increment on any interval in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

The ggBm admits different representations in terms of well-known processes. The most common is given in
[
MP08
]
in the form

\big\{B_{\beta,\alpha}(t),\,t\geq 0\big\}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}\big\{\sqrt{Y_{\beta}}B^{\alpha/2}(t),\,t\geq 0\big\}.

(24)

Here,
\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}
means equality in law, the nonnegative random variable
Y_{\beta}
has density
M_{\beta}
, called the
M
-Wright probability density
function, with the Laplace transform

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s\tau}M_{\beta}(\tau)\,d\tau=E_{\beta}(-s),

(25)

and
B^{\alpha/2}
is a fBm independent of
Y_{\beta}
.
The generalized moments of the density
M_{\beta}
of order
\delta>-1
are finite and are given (cf.
[
MP08
]
) by

\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{\delta}M_{\beta}(\tau)\,d\tau=\frac{\Gamma(\delta+1)}{\Gamma(\beta\delta+1)}.

(26)

For our purposes, we give a more suitable representation of
B_{\beta,\alpha}
as a subordination of fBm, which is essential in what follows.

Proposition 2.14
.

The ggBm has the following representation

\big\{B_{\beta,\alpha}(t),\,t\geq 0\big\}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}\big\{B^{\alpha/2}(tY_{\beta}^{1/\alpha}),\,t\geq 0\big\}.

(27)

Proof.

We only need to show that the representations (
24
) and (
27
) have the same finite-dimensional distribution. For every
\theta=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\theta_{k}B^{\alpha/2}(t_{k}Y_{\beta}^{1/\alpha})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\theta_{k}B^{\alpha/2}(t_{k}y^{1/\alpha})\right)\right]M_{\beta}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\theta_{k}y^{1/2}B^{\alpha/2}(t_{k})\right)\right]M_{\beta}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\theta_{k}Y_{\beta}^{1/2}B^{\alpha/2}(t_{k})\right)\right].dn

∎

2.3.2
Canonical Realization

We will distinguish two classes from the family
B_{\beta,\alpha}
depending on the parameter
\alpha
.

Case

\alpha=1
.

The corresponding class was introduced by Schneider
[
Sch90a
,
Sch90b
]
. We denote it by
B_{\beta}:=B_{\beta,1}
,
0<\beta<1
, and call it the grey Brownian motion. It follows from (
27
) that
B_{\beta}
is realized as the subordination of the Brownian motion by the process
\{tY_{\beta},t\geq 0\}
.
In the space of continuous functions, this realization is given below.

First, recall from Remark
2.2
-6 that
W^{1/2}
is a standard Brownian motion on the classical Wiener space
\big(\mathbb{W},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W}),\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\big)
.

Second, let
(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})

be the probability space where
\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}
is the law
of the random variable
Y_{\beta}
. Then
Y_{\beta}
is realized
on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
as the identity map

\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+},\;\tau\mapsto\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}(\tau):=\tau.

(28)

Since
Y_{\beta}
and
B^{1/2}
are independent, the grey Brownian motion
B_{\beta}
can be realized as the canonical process
X_{\beta}
defined on the product space
\big(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}\big)

by

X_{\beta}(t)(w,\tau):=W^{1/2}(t\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}(\tau))(w)=w(t\tau),\quad t\geq 0,\quad w\in\mathbb{W},\;\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(29)

We denote its law by
\mu_{\beta}
.

Case

\alpha\in(1,2]
.

Let
H:=\frac{\alpha}{2}>1/2
and
W^{H}
be the fractional Brownian motion on the space
\big(\mathbb{W},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W}),\mathbb{P}_{H}\big)
, see Remark
2.2
-6. As
Y_{\beta}

and
B^{H}
are independent, the generalized grey Brownian motion
B_{\beta,2H}
can be realized as the canonical process
X_{\beta,2H}
defined on the product space
\big(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}\big)

by

X_{\beta,2H}(t)(w,\tau):=W^{H}\big(t\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}^{1/(2H)}(\tau)\big)(w)=w\big(t\tau^{1/(2H)}\big),\;t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\;w\in\mathbb{W}.

In this case, the law of
X_{\beta,2H}
is denoted by
\mu_{\beta,2H}
.

3
The Cameron–Martin Theorem

3.1
For Grey Brownian Motion

For every
h\in\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
we denote by
X_{\beta}^{h}

the process defined on
\big(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}\big)
by

X_{\beta}^{h}(t):=X_{\beta}(t)+h(t\mathcal{Y}_{\beta})=W^{1/2}(t\mathcal{Y}_{\beta})+h(t\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}),\quad t\geq 0,

and its law is represented by
\mu_{\beta}^{h}
.

For
T>0
we consider the processes
X_{\beta,T}:=\big\{X_{\beta}(t)\mid t\in[0,T]\big\}
and
X_{\beta,T}^{h}:=\big\{X_{\beta}^{h}(t)\mid t\in[0,T]\big\}
and their laws, denoted by
\mu_{\beta,T}
and
\mu_{\beta,T}^{h}
, respectively.

The Cameron-Martin theorem for the grey Brownian motion
X_{\beta}
is expressed as follows:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
h\in\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
be given. Then we have:

1.

For every
T>0
the measures
\mu_{\beta,T}^{h}
and
\mu_{\beta,T}

are equivalent and the Radon-Nikodym density is given by

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta,T}^{h}}{\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta,T}}=\mathcal{E}\left(\int_{0}^{.}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)\!(T\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}).

(30)

2.

The measures
\mu_{\beta}^{h}
and
\mu_{\beta}
are equivalent and the Radon-Nikodym density is

\frac{\mathrm{d}{\mu_{\beta}^{h}}}{\mathrm{d}{\mu_{\beta}}}=\mathcal{E}\left(\int_{0}^{.}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)\!(\infty).

(31)

Remark 3.2
.

Before proving the theorem, we will elucidate the two equations (
30
) and (
31
).

1.

The expectation of
\mathcal{E}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{.}}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)\!(T\mathcal{Y}_{\beta})
with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}
is equal to
1
, that is,

\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\mathcal{E}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{.}}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)(T\tau)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)\,\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\mathrm{d}\tau)=1.

Indeed it follows from Tonelli’s theorem and the fact that the expectation of a martingale is constant in time, that is, for any
t\geq 0
we have

\int_{\mathbb{W}}\mathcal{E}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{.}}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)(t)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)=1.

2.

It follows from Remark
2.11
that the right-hand side of (
31
) is well-defined. On the other hand, since
\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\{0\})=0
we have
\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\{\infty\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}=\infty\})=1
, thus the r.h.s. of (
31
) implicitly depends on
Y_{\beta}
in the following sense:

\mathcal{E}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\cdot}}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)(\infty)=\mathcal{E}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\cdot}}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)(\infty\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}),

\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}
almost surely.

Proof.

1. Let
T>0
be given. It is sufficient to prove

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau)+h(t_{1}\tau),\ldots,w(t_{n}\tau)+h(t_{n}\tau)\big)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)\,\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\mathrm{d}\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\ldots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big)\mathcal{E}\left(\int_{0}^{.}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)\!(T\tau)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)\,\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\mathrm{d}\tau),

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}\leq T
and
f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(the set of continuous bounded functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
), see
[
Bil95
, Thm. 3.3]
.

For every fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the classical Cameron–Martin formula applied to the bounded measurable functional on
\mathbb{W}_{T\tau}

w\mapsto f\left(w\left(t_{1}\tau\right),\ldots,w\left(t_{n}\tau\right)\right)

yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{W}}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau)+h(t_{1}\tau),\ldots,w(t_{n}\tau)+h(t_{n}\tau)\big)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{W}_{T\tau}}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau)+h(t_{1}\tau),\ldots,w(t_{n}\tau)+h(t_{n}\tau)\big)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{W}_{T\tau}}f\left(w(t_{1}\tau),\ldots,w(t_{n}\tau)\right)\mathcal{E}\left(\int_{0}^{.}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)\!(T\tau)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{W}}f\left(w(t_{1}\tau),\ldots,w(t_{n}\tau)\right)\mathcal{E}\left(\int_{0}^{.}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)\!(T\tau)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w).

Therefore, we may integrate both sides of the above equality w.r.t
\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}

to obtain the result.

2. It follows by an easy adaption of the above arguments with
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{n}<+\infty

and the classical Cameron–Martin formula for functionals defined on

\mathbb{W}
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{W}
be given. If
\mu_{\beta}

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{\beta,2H})=\Omega^{H}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{w\left(\cdot\tau^{1/(2H)}\right)\mid w\in\mathbb{W},\tau\geq 0\right\}.

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{\beta,T})=\Omega^{1/2}_{T}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\tilde{w}\!\!\upharpoonright_{\left[0,T\right]}\,\mid\tilde{w}\in\Omega^{1/2}\right\}.

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{\beta,2H,T})=\Omega_{T}^{H}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\tilde{w}\!\!\upharpoonright_{\left[0,T\right]}\,\mid\tilde{w}\in\Omega^{H}\right\}.

4
Integration by Parts Formula and Closability of the Derivative Operator

As an application of the Cameron–Martin formulas from Section
3
, we first introduce the directional derivative in the directions of the elements
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
for smooth functionals. We show an integration by parts formula leading to the closability of the directional derivative on
L^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
,
p\geq 1
.

We begin by analyzing the class of gBm
X_{\beta,T}
,
T>0
, (Subsection
4.1
) and then move on to the broader class
X_{\beta,2H,T}
,
T>0
, of ggBm from Subsection
4.2
.

4.1
Derivative Operator for Grey Brownian Motion

We start by introducing the directional derivative in special directions (from the Cameron–Marin space
\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
) of a real-valued measurable function
F
on
\big(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}\big)
.

Given
T>0
,
h\in\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
, and
F:\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
the partial derivative of
F
in the direction
h
is defined by

(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{F(w+\varepsilon h,\tau)-F(w,\tau)}{\varepsilon},\quad w\in\mathbb{W},\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

whenever the limit exists.

We consider the class
\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}
of functions
F
of the form

F\big(w,\tau\big):=f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\ldots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big),\quad w\in\mathbb{W},\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

(34)

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}\leq T
and
f\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(the set of
C^{\infty}
bounded real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
). Note that this class is closely related to the process
X_{\beta,T}
. In fact, any
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}
of the form (
34
) is simply

F(w,\tau)=f\big(X_{\beta,T}(t_{1})(w,\tau),\dots,X_{\beta,T}(t_{n})(w,\tau)\big),

(35)

generally referred to as smooth cylindrical function. It is simple to verify that the derivative
\partial_{1,h}F
exists for any
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}
and we have

(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big)h(t_{i}\tau),\quad w\in\mathbb{W},\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

(36)

where
\partial_{i}
denotes the partial derivative w.r.t.
x_{i}
.

In what follows, we show that
\partial_{1,h}F
is represented by an element
of the Cameron-Martin space
\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
, denoted by
\nabla_{1}F
, where the subscript
1
is used to indicate w.r.t. the first variable
w
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}
be given. Then, for any
w\in\mathbb{W}
,

\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

1.

the map

\mathcal{H}_{1/2}\ni h\mapsto(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}

is a bounded linear functional on
\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
,

2.

there exists a unique element
\nabla_{1}F(w,\tau)\in\mathcal{H}_{1/2}

given by

\big(\nabla_{1}F(w,\tau)\big)(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big)(t\wedge t_{i}\tau),\quad t\geq 0

(37)

and

\dot{\nabla}_{1}F(w,\tau)(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big)\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{i}\tau)}(t)

(38)

satisfying

(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)=\big(\nabla_{1}F(w,\tau),h\big)_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}.

(39)

Proof.

1. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}

be given as in (
34
). The linearity of
the map is obvious. For every
h\in\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
, using the
representation (
14
) we have

\displaystyle|(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big)h(t_{i}\tau)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{t_{i}\tau}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|.

An application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the fact that

\|\dot{h}\|_{2}=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}
, yields

|(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}\sqrt{t_{i}\tau}\|\dot{h}\|_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}\sqrt{t_{i}\tau}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}.

(40)

2. The equality (
39
) follows from 1. and
the Riesz representation theorem. From (
36
)
and (
14
) we obtain

\displaystyle(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big)h(t_{i}\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big)\int_{0}^{t_{i}\tau}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big)\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{i}\tau)}(s)\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

The equalities (
37
) and (
38
)
easily follow.
∎

Next, we show that the directional derivative
\partial_{1,h}F
with
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}

and
h\in\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
, is an element of
L^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})

for any
p\geq 1
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}

and
p\geq 1
be given. Then

1.

\partial_{1,h}F\in L^{p}(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
, for any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
,
where
\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}}:=\sigma\big(X_{\beta,T}(t),t\in\left[0,T\right]\big)
is the natural
\sigma
-algebra generated by the process
X_{\beta}
up to time
T
.

2.

\nabla_{1}F\in L_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}):=L^{p}(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}};\mathcal{H}_{1/2})
.

Proof.

1. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}

be given as in (
34
). It follows from (
35
) and (
36
) that
F
and
\partial_{1,h}F
are
\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}}
-measurable. From the estimate (
40
)
we derive

\displaystyle\|\partial_{1,h}F\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}|(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}(\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})(w,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}\sqrt{t_{i}\tau}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}(\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})(w,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{p-1}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{p}t_{i}^{p/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{p/2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\tau).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{p-1}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{p}t_{i}^{p/2}\frac{\Gamma(p/2+1)}{\Gamma(\beta p/2+1)}<\infty.

The second inequality is the convexity inequality and the last equality
follows from (
26
).

2. The
\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}}
-measurability of
\nabla_{1}F
and
\dot{\nabla}_{1}F
can be easily derived from (
37
) and (
38
). From (
38
) and again the convexity inequality
we infer

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{1}F(w,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\dot{\nabla}_{1}F(w,\tau)(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big)\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{i}\tau)}(t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\tau\left(\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big)\right)^{2}.

This implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{1}F(w,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(n\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\tau\left(\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau)\big)\right)^{2}\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{p/2\vee(p-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(t_{i}\tau)^{p/2}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{p}.

Finally, the norm of
\|\nabla_{1}F\|_{L_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})}

is computed as

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{1}F\|_{L_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\|(\nabla_{1}F)(w,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}(\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})(w,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{p/2\vee(p-1)}\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(t_{i}\tau)^{p/2}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}(\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})(w,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{p/2\vee(p-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{p}t_{i}^{p/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\tau^{p/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{p/2\vee(p-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{p}t_{i}^{p/2}\frac{\Gamma(p/2+1)}{\Gamma(\beta p/2+1)}<\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

To investigate the closability of the directional derivative operator
\partial_{1,h}
and
\nabla_{1}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
,
p\geq 1
, we need the integration by parts formula for the gBm
X_{\beta,T}
,
T>0
. Below the expectation is taken w.r.t. the probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
T>0
be given. For any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
and
F,G\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\big[G\partial_{1,h}F\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[F\partial_{1,h}^{*}G\big],

(41)

where

\partial_{1,h}^{*}G=-\partial_{1,h}G+G\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(t)\right)(T\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}).

Proof.

Let
T>0
and
F,G\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}
be given. The Cameron-Martin formula (see Eq. (
30
)) for the grey Brownian motion says that, for every
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}F(w+\varepsilon h,\tau)\,G(w+\varepsilon h,\tau)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)\,\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\mathrm{d}\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}F(w,\tau)\,G(w,\tau)\,\mathcal{E}\left(\int_{0}^{.}\varepsilon\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)\!(T\tau)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)\,\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\mathrm{d}\tau).

We differentiate this equality w.r.t.
\varepsilon
and set
\varepsilon=0
. The assumptions on
h
and
F
allow us
to interchange the operations of differentiation and integration by using
the dominated convergence theorem, and we obtain the following

\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\partial_{1,h}F(w,\tau)\,G(w,\tau)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)\,\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\mathrm{d}\tau)\\
+\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}F(w,\tau)\,\partial_{1,h}G(w,\tau)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)\,\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\mathrm{d}\tau)\\
=\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}F(w,\tau)\,G(w,\tau)\left(\int_{0}^{T\tau}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)\,\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\mathrm{d}\tau).

Note that by Lemma
5.1
, all the terms in the above equality are well-defined. This concludes the proof.
∎

From the identity (
41
) we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.4
.

For every
p,q\geq 1
and
h\in\mathcal{H}_{1/2}

the derivative operator
\partial_{1,h}:\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}\longrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})

is closable on
L^{q}(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.

Proof.

Let
p,q\geq 1
be given. We have to show that if
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}

is a sequence such that
F_{n}\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{q}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})

and
\partial_{1,h}F_{n}\longrightarrow Z
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
, then

Z=0
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}

be fixed and
r\geq 1
. According to Proposition
4.2
-1
we have
\partial_{1,h}G\in L^{r}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
. In addition, using the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy
inequality, there exists a constant dependent only in
r
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{W}}\left|\left(\int_{0}^{T\tau}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(t)\right)(w)\right|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(w)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{r}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{T\tau}|\dot{h}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{r/2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{r}\|\dot{h}\|_{2}^{r/2}=C_{r}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{r/2}.

It is well known that the Wiener integral
\int_{0}^{\cdot}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(t)
can be obtained as the limit in probability of the Riemann sums; see
[
RY99
]
. Consequently,
\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(t)\right)(T\mathcal{Y}_{\beta})
is
\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}}
-measurable.
Thus,

\partial_{1,h}^{*}G=-\partial_{1,h}G+G\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(t)\right)(T\mathcal{Y}_{\beta})\in L^{r}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}).

Therefore, the integration by parts (
41
) and the fact that
\partial_{1,h}^{*}G\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
,
q^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
q
, yields

\mathbb{E}[GZ]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[G\partial_{1,h}F_{n}]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F_{n}\partial_{1,h}^{*}G]=0.

We can deduce that
Z=0
from the facts that the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}}
is generated by the elements of
\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}
and the density of
\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.
∎

The closability of the operator
\nabla_{1}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
is based on that of
\partial_{1,h}
. This is the subject of the following theorem.

Theorem 4.5
.

For every
p,q\geq 1
, the operator
\nabla_{1}:\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}\longrightarrow L_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})

is closable in
L^{q}(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.

Proof.

Let
p,q\geq 1
and
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}

be a sequence such that
F_{n}\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{q}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})

and
\nabla_{1}F_{n}\longrightarrow Z
in
L_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.
We have to show that
Z=0
in
L_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.
First, notice that for any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
we have

\partial_{1,h}F_{n}=(\nabla_{1}F_{n},h)_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}\longrightarrow(Z,h)_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}},\;\mathrm{in}\;L^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})\;\mathrm{as}\;n\to\infty.

Since
\partial_{1,h}
is closable (cf. Theorem
4.4
),
then
(Z,h)_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}=0
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.
Hence,

\mathbb{E}[G(Z,h)_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}]=0,\quad\forall G\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}.

By linearity we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Z,\sum_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}h_{i}\right)_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}\right]=0,

for any element of the set

S(\mathcal{H}_{1/2}):=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}h_{i}\,\middle|\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,G_{i}\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}\right\},

where
\{h_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{1/2},\;i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an orthonormal
basis of
\mathcal{H}_{1/2}
. Since
S(\mathcal{H}_{1/2})
is dense in
L_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
,
we conclude that
Z=0
in
L_{\mathcal{H}_{1/2}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.
∎

4.2
Derivative Operator for Generalized Grey Brownian Motion

We intend to broaden the findings from Subsection
4.1
to the class of processes
X_{\beta,2H,T}
,
T>0
, introduced in Subsection
3.2
.

We fix
H\in(1/2,1)
,
T>0
, and consider the class
\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}
of measurable functions on
\big(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}\big)
of the form

F\big(w,\tau\big):=f\big(w(t_{1}\tau_{H}),\ldots,w(t_{n}\tau_{H})\big),\quad w\in\mathbb{W},\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

(42)

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}\leq T
,
\tau_{H}=\tau^{1/(2H)}
, and
f\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Note that each
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}
of the form (
42
) is represented as

F(w,\tau)=f\big(X_{\beta,2H,T}(t_{1})(w,\tau),\dots,X_{\beta,2H,T}(t_{n})(w,\tau)\big).

(43)

Moreover, we can easily see that for every
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}
and
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
the partial derivative
\partial_{1,h}F
in the direction
h
exists and we have

\partial_{1,h}F(w,\tau)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau_{H}),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau_{H})\big)h(t_{i}\tau_{H}),\quad w\in\mathbb{W},\tau\geq 0.

(44)

The next proposition shows the two basic properties of the partial derivative operator
\partial_{1,h}
,
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}

be given. Then, for any
w\in\mathbb{W}
,

\tau\geq 0
,

1.

the map

\mathcal{H}_{H}\ni h\mapsto(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}

is a bounded linear functional on
\mathcal{H}_{H}
,

2.

there exists a unique element
\nabla_{1}F(w,\tau)\in\mathcal{H}_{H}

given, for any
t\geq 0
, by

\big(\nabla_{1}F(w,\tau)\big)(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau_{H}),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau_{H})\big)R_{H}(t_{i}\tau_{H},t),

(45)

and

\big(\dot{\nabla}_{1}F(w,\tau)\big)(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau_{H}),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau_{H})\big)K_{H}(t_{i}\tau_{H},t)

(46)

satisfying

(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)=\big(\nabla_{1}F(w,\tau),h\big)_{\mathcal{H}_{H}},\quad w\in\mathbb{W},\;\tau\geq 0.

(47)

Proof.

1. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}
be given. The linearity of
the map is obvious. For every
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
, using the
representation (
13
) we have

\displaystyle\big|(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau_{H}),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau_{H})\big)h(t_{i}\tau_{H})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{t_{i}\tau_{H}}K_{H}(t_{i}\tau_{H},s)\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|.

An application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the fact that

\|\dot{h}\|_{2}=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}
, and the equality (
5
) implies

\big|(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)\big|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}t_{i}^{H}\tau^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}.

(48)

2. The equality (
47
) follows from 1. and
the Riesz representation theorem. From (
44
)
and (
13
) we obtain

\displaystyle(\partial_{1,h}F)(w,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau_{H}),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau_{H})\big)h(t_{i}\tau_{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau_{H}),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau_{H})\big)\int_{0}^{t_{i}\tau_{H}}K_{H}(t_{i}\tau_{H},s)\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau_{H}),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau_{H})\big)K_{H}(t_{i}\tau_{H},s)\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, the equality (
46
)
is immediate and (
45
) follows from (
9
).
∎

The estimate (
48
) is used to demonstrate that the directional derivative
\partial_{1,h}F
,
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}
,
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
, is an element of
L^{p}(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})

for any
p\geq 1
, where
\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}}:=\sigma\big(X_{\beta,2H,T}(t),t\in\left[0,T\right]\big)
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}

and
p\geq 1
be given. Then

1.

\partial_{1,h}F\in L^{p}(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
, for any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
,

2.

\nabla_{1}F\in L_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}):=L^{p}(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}};\mathcal{H}_{H})
.

Proof.

1. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}
be given. It follows from (
43
) and (
44
) that
F
and
\partial_{1,h}F
are
\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}}
-measurable. Using the estimate (
48
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{1,h}F\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}t_{i}^{H}\tau^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}(\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})(w,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{p-1}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{p}t_{i}^{pH}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{p/2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\tau).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{p-1}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{p}t_{i}^{pH}\frac{\Gamma(p/2+1)}{\Gamma(\beta p/2+1)}<\infty.

The second estimate is the convexity inequality, and the last equality
follows from (
26
).

2. The
\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}}
-measurability of
\nabla_{1}F
and
\dot{\nabla}_{1}F
can be easily derived from (
45
) and (
46
).
From (
46
) and again the convexity inequality we infer

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{1}F(w,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\big(\dot{\nabla}_{1}F(w,\tau)\big)(t)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f\big(w(t_{1}\tau_{H}),\dots,w(t_{n}\tau_{H})\big)K_{H}(t_{i}\tau_{H},t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{2H}\tau\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

This implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{1}F(w,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(n\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{2H}\tau\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{p/2\vee(p-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{pH}\tau^{p/2}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{p}.

Finally, the norm of
\|\nabla_{1}F\|_{L_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})}

is computed as

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{1}F\|_{L_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{W\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\|(\nabla_{1}F)(w,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}(\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})(w,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{p/2\vee(p-1)}\int_{W\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{pH}\tau^{p/2}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathrm{d}(\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})(w,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{p/2\vee(p-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{p}t_{i}^{pH}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\tau^{p/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{p/2\vee(p-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\infty}^{p}t_{i}^{pH}\frac{\Gamma(p/2+1)}{\Gamma(\beta p/2+1)}<\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

We now get the integration by parts formula for ggBm that will be used to prove the closability of the derivative operators
\partial_{1,h}
and
\nabla_{1}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
T>0
be given. For any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
and
F,G\in\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\big[G\partial_{1,h}F\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[F\partial_{1,h}^{*}G\big],

(49)

where

\partial_{1,h}^{*}G=-\partial_{1,h}G+G\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{W}(t)\right)(T\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}^{1/(2H)}).

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the one of Theorem
4.3
with slight modifications.
∎

From the identity (
49
) we obtain the following

Theorem 4.9
.

For every
p,q\geq 1
and
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}

the derivative operator
\partial_{1,h}:\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}\longrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})

is closable in
L^{q}(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.

Proof.

Let
p,q\geq 1
be given. We have to show that if
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}

is a sequence such that
F_{n}\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{q}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})

and
\partial_{1,h}F_{n}\longrightarrow Z
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
, then

Z=0
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}

be fixed and
r\geq 1
. According to Proposition
4.7
-1
we have
\partial_{1,h}G\in L^{r}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
. In addition, using the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy
inequality, there exists a constant
C_{r}
dependent only in
r
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{W}}\left|\left(\int_{0}^{T\tau_{H}}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{W}(t)\right)(w)\right|^{r}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{H}(w)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{r}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{T\tau_{H}}|\dot{h}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{r/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{r}\|\dot{h}\|_{2}^{r/2}=C_{r}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{r/2}.

We know that the Wiener integral
\int_{0}^{\cdot}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{W}(t)
is the limit in probability of Riemann sums; see
[
RY99
]
. Furthermore, the
\sigma
-algebras of
W^{H}
and
\widetilde{W}
are the same; see Corollary 4.1 in
[
Dec22
]
. Consequently,
\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{W}(t)\right)(T\mathcal{Y}_{\beta})
is
\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}}
-measurable.
Thus,

\partial_{1,h}^{*}G=-\partial_{1,h}G+G\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\dot{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{W}(t)\right)(T\mathcal{Y}_{\beta})\in L^{r}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}).

Therefore, the integration by parts (
49
) and the fact that
\partial_{1,h}^{*}G\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
,
q^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
q
, yields

\mathbb{E}[GZ]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[G\partial_{1,h}F_{n}]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F_{n}\partial_{1,h}^{*}G]=0.

We can deduce that
Z=0
from the facts that the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}}
is generated by the elements of
\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}
and the density of
\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.
∎

Finally, we are ready to prove the closability of the operator
\nabla_{1}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
, which is the content of the following theorem.

Theorem 4.10
.

For every
p,q\geq 1
, the operator
\nabla_{1}:\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}\longrightarrow L_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})

is closable on
L^{q}(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.

Proof.

Let
p,q\geq 1
and
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}

be a sequence such that
F_{n}\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{q}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})

and
\nabla_{1}F_{n}\longrightarrow Z
in
L_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.
We have to show that
Z=0
in
L_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.
First, notice that for any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
we have

\partial_{1,h}F_{n}=(\nabla_{1}F_{n},h)_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}\longrightarrow(Z,h)_{\mathcal{H}_{H}},\;\mathrm{in}\;L^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})\;\mathrm{as}\;n\to\infty.

Since
\partial_{1,h}F_{n}
is closable (cf. Theorem
4.9
),
then
(Z,h)_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}=0
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.
Hence,

\mathbb{E}[G(Z,h)_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}]=0,\quad\forall G\in\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}.

By linearity we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Z,\sum_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}h_{i}\right)_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}\right]=0,

for any element of the set

S(\mathcal{H}_{H}):=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}h_{i}\,\middle|\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,G_{i}\in\mathcal{F}C_{b,H}^{\infty}\right\},

where
\{h_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{H},\;i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an orthonormal
basis of
\mathcal{H}_{H}
. Since
S(\mathcal{H}_{H})
is dense in
L_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
,
we conclude that
Z=0
in
L_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}^{X_{\beta,2H,T}},\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})
.
∎

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
H\in[1/2,1)
and
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
be given. Then, for every
t\geq 0
, we have

1.

The random variable
(w,\tau)\mapsto\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t\tau}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)(w)
belongs to
L^{2}\big(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}\big)
,
H=1/2
.

2.

The random variable
(w,\tau)\mapsto\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t\tau}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{W}(s)\right)(w)
belongs to
L^{2}\big(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}\big)
,
H\in(1/2,1)
.

Proof.

It is a consequence of the Tonelli theorem and Itô’s isometry formula. Indeed, since
W^{1/2}
and
\widetilde{W}
are Brownian motions under
\mathbb{P}_{1/2}
and
\mathbb{P}_{H}
,
H\in(1/2,1)
, respectively, we obtain the bound

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t\cdot}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{W}(s)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{P}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t\cdot}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{P}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\left(\int_{0}^{t\tau}\dot{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W^{1/2}(s)\right)^{2}\!\!(w)\,\mathbb{P}_{1/2}(\mathrm{d}w)\,\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\mathrm{d}\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\left(\int_{0}^{t\tau}\dot{h}^{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\beta}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\dot{h}\|_{2}.

∎

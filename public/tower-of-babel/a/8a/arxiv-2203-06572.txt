A comparison of the absolute and relative real analytic torsion forms
Jialin Zhu

Abstract
. In this paper we establish a comparison formula of the absolute and relative real analytic torsion forms over fibrations with boundaries. The key tool is a gluing formula of analytic torsion forms proved by M. Puchol, Y. Zhang and the author. As a consequence of the comparison formula, we prove another version of the gluing formula of the analytic torsion forms conjectured originally by the author.



0.
Introduction

In this paper, we first prove a comparison formula of the absolute and relative Bismut-Lott real analytic torsion forms by using the gluing formula of the Bismut-Lott torsion forms established recently by M. Puchol, Y. Zhang and the author
[
PZZ20
]
. As a consequence of the comparison formula, we prove another version of the gluing formula of the Bismut-Lott analytic torsion forms conjectured originally by the author in
[
Zhu15
, Conjecture 1.1]

As the analytic analogue of the Reidemeister topological torsion
[
Mil66
]
, Ray
and Singer
[
RS71
]
introduced the Ray-Singer analytic torsion associated to de Rham complex twisted by a flat vector bundle
F
over a
compact oriented Riemannian
manifold
M
. They also conjectured that the topological torsion and analytic torsion coincide,
proved by Cheeger
[
Che79
]
and Müller
[
Mül78
]
independently in the unitary case.
Bismut and Zhang
[
BZ92
]
and Müller
[
Mül93
]
simultaneously considered its generalizations. Müller extended
his result to the case where the dimension of the manifold is odd and only the metric induced on
\det{F}
is required to be flat.
Bismut and Zhang generalized the original Cheeger-Müller theorem to arbitrary flat vector bundles with arbitrary Hermitian
metrics.

In
[
BL95
]
, Bismut and Lott generalized the Ray-Singer torsion to smooth fibrations. They first proved a family index theorem for flat vector bundles, which relates the odd characteristic classes of a flat complex vector bundle on the total
space of the fibration to those of its direct image on the base. Then they improved their theorem to the level of differential forms, where the Bismut-Lott analytic torsion forms appear as the transgression terms.
Inspired by
[
BL95
]
,
Igusa and Klein

[
Igu02
]
extended the Reidemeister torsion to the family case and obtained the Igusa-Klein
topological torsion.

Lück
[
Lüc93
]
established the gluing formula for the analytic torsion for unitary flat vector bundles when the Riemannian metric has product structure near the boundary by using the results in
[
LR91
]
.
There are also other works on the gluing problem of the
analytic torsion (cf.
[
Has98
]
,
[
Vis95
]
). Finally,
Brüning and Ma
[
BM06
]
established the anomaly formula of the analytic torsion on manifolds with boundary,
then they
[
BM13
]
proved the gluing formula of analytic torsion for any flat vector bundles and without any assumptions on the product structures near the boundary. In
[
BM13
]
, Brüning and Ma essentially used the equivariant Bismut-Zhang theorem
[
BZ94
]
. In
[
PZZ21a
]
, We give a pure
analytic proof of Brüning-Ma’s gluing formula while the product structures of metrics are assumed.

The gluing formula of Bismut-Lott torsions was first established in two important cases by the author in
[
Zhu15
]
,
[
Zhu16
]
. In
[
Zhu15
]
, the gluing formula was proved under the assumption that there exists fiberwise Morse function. The proof is a generalization of Brüning-Ma’s proof in
[
BM13
]
to family case, based on the work of Bismut-Goette
[
BG01
]
. In
[
Zhu16
]
, the gluing formula was established by using adiabatic limit under the assumption that the fiberwise Dirac operator of the boundary is invertible. Recently, we proved the gluing formula in the general case
[
PZZ20
]
by using adiabatic limit, scattering theory and a Witten-type deformation. Our gluing formula plays a key role in establishing the equivalence between Bismut-Lott torsion and Igusa-Klein torsion under Igusa’s axiomatization
[
Igu08
]
of higher torsion invariants
[
PZZ21b
]
.

Fibrations with cylinder ends

1.1.
Product structures

Let
\pi:M\rightarrow S
be a smooth fibration with boundary
X:=\partial M
and a compact manifold
Z
as its standard fiber. We assume that the boundary
X
of
M
is a smooth fibration denoted by
\pi_{\partial}:X\rightarrow S
with fiber
Y
such that
Y=\partial Z
.

Definition 1.1
.

For a compact manifold
X
and a subset
I
of
\mathbb{R}
, we set
X_{I}:=X\times I
, for example
X_{[-a,a]}=X\times[-a,a]
(
a>0
),
X_{\mathbb{R}}=X\times(-\infty,+\infty)
.

Let
TM
be the tangent bundle of
M
. Let
TZ
be the vertical subbundle of
TM
. Let
T^{H}M
be the horizontal subbundle of
TM
, such that
TM=TZ\oplus T^{H}M
. Let
TY
be the vertical tangent bundle of the fibration
X
, which is a subbundle of
TZ|_{X}
. Let
N
be the normal bundle of
X\subset M
, i.e.,
N:=TM/TX
, then by our assumption we have
TZ/TY\cong N
. In this case,
N\rightarrow X
is a trivial line bundle.

Let
g^{TZ}
be a metric on
TZ
and
g^{TY}
be the metric on
TY
induced by
g^{TZ}
. We identity
N
with the orthogonal complement of
TY
in
TZ
with respect to
g^{TZ}
, thus we have
TZ|_{X}=TY\oplus N
.

Assume that
X_{[-1,2]}
is a product neighborhood of
X\subset M
, and
\partial M
is identified with
X_{\{2\}}
. Let
\psi:X\times[-1,2]\rightarrow X
be the projection on the first factor. We assume that

T^{H}M
and
g^{TZ}
have product structures on
X_{[-1,2]}
, i.e.,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (T^{H}M)|_{X}\subset TX,\quad(T^{H}M)|_{X_{[-1,2]}}=\psi^{*}((T^{H}M)|_{X}),\end{aligned}

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} g^{TZ}|_{(x^{\prime},x_{m})}=g^{TY}(x^{\prime})+dx^{2}_{m},\quad(x^{\prime},x_{m})\in X\times[-1,2].\end{aligned}

Then
T^{H}M:=(T^{H}M)|_{X}
gives a horizontal bundle of fibration
X
, such that
TX=T^{H}X\oplus TY
.

Let
(F,\nabla^{F})
be a flat complex vector bundle on
M
with a flat connection
\nabla^{F}
, i.e.,
(\nabla^{F})^{2}=0
. We trivialize
F
along the
x_{m}-
direction, by using the parallel transport with respect to
\nabla^{F}
, then we have
(F,\nabla^{F})|_{X_{[-1,2]}}=\phi^{*}\left(F|_{X},\nabla^{F}|_{X}\right)
. Under this trivialization, we assume

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} h^{F}|_{X_{[-1,2]}}=\psi^{*}(h^{F}|_{X}).\end{aligned}

1.2.
Bismut’s superconnection

Let
\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)
be the infinite-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}-
graded vector bundle over
S
whose fiber is
\Omega^{\bullet}(Z_{b},F)
at
b\in S
. Then we have

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Omega^{\bullet}(M,F)=\Omega^{\bullet}(S,\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)).\end{aligned}

The fiberwise Riemannian volume form
dv_{Z}
associated with
g^{TZ}
is a section of
\Lambda^{m}(T^{*}Z)\otimes o(TZ)
over
M
, where
o(TZ)
is the orientation line bundle of
TZ
. The Hermitian metric on the infinite dimensional bundle
\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)\rightarrow S
induced by
g^{TZ}
and
h^{F}
is defined as: for
s,\,s^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\bullet}(Z_{b},F)
,
b\in S
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle_{h^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)}}(b):=\int_{Z_{b}}\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle_{g^{\Lambda(T^{*}Z)\otimes F}}(x)dv_{Z_{b}}.\end{aligned}

Let
P^{TZ}:TM\rightarrow TZ
be the natural projection. For
U\in TS
, let
U^{H}
be the horizontal lift of
U
in
T^{H}M
, so that
\pi_{*}U^{H}=U
.

Definition 1.2
.

For
U\in TS
, the Lie derivative of
U^{H}
defines a connection on
\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)
,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \nabla^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)}_{U}s:=L_{U^{H}}s,\quad\text{for}\quad s\in C^{\infty}\left(S,\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)\right).\end{aligned}

Let
d^{Z}
be the exterior differentiation twisted by
\nabla^{F}
on the fibers
Z
. For
U_{1},U_{2}\in TS
, set

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} T(U_{1},U_{2})=-P^{TZ}[U^{H}_{1},U^{H}_{2}]\in C^{\infty}\left(M,TZ\right),\end{aligned}

which defines a tenso
T\in C^{\infty}\left(M,\pi^{*}(\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}S))\otimes TZ\right)
. Let
i_{T}
be the interior multiplication by
T
in the vertical direction.
The exterior differentiation
d^{M}
twisted by
\nabla^{F}
acting on
\Omega^{\bullet}(M,F)
can be decomposed as:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d^{M}=d^{Z}+\nabla^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)}+i_{T}.\end{aligned}

Let
\nabla^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)*},\,d^{M*},\,i_{T}^{*},\,d^{Z*}
be the formal adjoints of
\nabla^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)},\,d^{M},\,i_{T},\,d^{Z}
with respect to
h^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)}
in the senses of
[
BL95
, Def. 1.6]
. Then by (

) we have
d^{M*}=d^{Z*}+\nabla^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)*}+i_{T}^{*}
.
Let
N_{Z}
be the number operator on
\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)
, i.e., it acts by multiplication by
k
on
\Omega^{k}(Z,F)
.
For
t>0
, we set (cf.
[
BL95
, (3.50)]
)

(1.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} C_{t}&:=\frac{1}{2}\left(t^{-N_{Z}/2}d^{M*}t^{N_{Z}/2}+t^{N_{Z}/2}d^{M}t^{-N_{Z}/2}\right),\\
D_{t}&:=\frac{1}{2}\left(t^{-N_{Z}/2}d^{M*}t^{N_{Z}/2}-t^{N_{Z}/2}d^{M}t^{-N_{Z}/2}\right),\end{aligned}

where
D_{t}
is an odd element of
\Omega(S,\End(\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)))
and
C_{t}
is essentially the same as the Bismut superconnection introduced by Bismut in
[
Bis86
]
.

1.3.
Absolute and relative boundary conditions

Let
e_{\mathfrak{n}}
be the inward-pointing unit normal vector field on
X\subset M
, and
e^{\mathfrak{n}}
be its dual vector field, then we extend
e_{\mathfrak{n}},e^{\mathfrak{n}}
on
X_{[-1,2]}
. By the product structure (
1.2
), we have

e_{\mathfrak{n}}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{m}},\,e^{\mathfrak{n}}=-dx_{m}
on
X_{[-1,2]}
. For
\sigma\in\Omega^{\bullet}(M,F)
, we say that
\sigma
satisfies the
absolute boundary conditions
, if

(1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (i_{e_{\mathfrak{n}}}\sigma)|_{X}=(i_{e_{\mathfrak{n}}}d^{Z}\sigma)|_{X}=0.\end{aligned}

We say that
\sigma
satisfies the
relative boundary conditions
, if

(1.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (e^{\mathfrak{n}}\wedge\sigma)|_{X}=(e^{\mathfrak{n}}\wedge d^{Z}\sigma)|_{X}=0.\end{aligned}

Let
\Omega_{a/r}(Z,F)
be the space of differential forms verifying the absolute/relative boundary conditions respectively. Let
H_{a/r}(Z,F)
be the flat vector bundle of fiberwise absolute/relative cohomology groups with canonical connection
\nabla^{H_{a/r}(Z,F)}
. Set

(1.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\chi_{a/r}(Z)=\sum_{p=0}^{m}(-1)^{p}\rk H^{p}_{a/r}(Z),\quad\chi^{\prime}_{a/r}(Z,F)=\sum_{p=0}^{m}(-1)^{p}p\rk H^{p}_{a/r}(Z,F).\end{aligned}

Let
\varphi:\Omega(S)\rightarrow\Omega(S)
be the linear map such that for all homogeneous
\omega\in\Omega(S)
,
\varphi\omega=(2i\pi)^{-(\deg\omega)/2}\omega
. Let
f(x)=xe^{x^{2}}
and
f^{\prime}(x)=(1+2x^{2})e^{x^{2}}
. For
t>0
, we set

(1.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f^{\wedge}(D_{t},h^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)})_{a/r}:=\varphi\str\left[\frac{N}{2}f^{\prime}(D_{t})_{a/r}\right].\end{aligned}

The following definition of Bismut-Lott analytic torsion forms is given by
[
BL95
, Def. 3.22]
without boundaries and by
[
Zhu15
, Def. 2.18]
with boundaries.

Definition 1.3
.

The Bismut-Lott torsion forms with absolute/relative boundary conditions are defined as

(1.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}_{a/r}(T^{H}M,g^{TZ},h^{F})&=-\int^{+\infty}_{0}\left[f^{\wedge}(D_{t},h^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Z,F)})_{a/r}-\frac{\chi^{\prime}_{a/r}(Z,F)}{2}f^{\prime}(0)\right.\\
&\quad\quad\left.-\Big(\frac{1}{4}m\rk(F)\chi_{a/r}(Z)-\frac{\chi^{\prime}_{a/r}(Z,F)}{2}\Big)f^{\prime}(\frac{i\sqrt{t}}{2})\right]\frac{dt}{t}.\end{aligned}

2.2.
Computations of
\mathscr{T}_{a,a/r}(T^{H}X_{[-1,1]},g^{TY_{[-1,1]}},h^{F})
over
X_{[-1,1]}

If
\varphi
is a closed differential form on
Y_{[-1,1]}\cong[-1,1]\times Y
, we have the following decompositon

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \varphi=\varphi_{1}(u,y)+du\wedge\varphi_{2}(u,y),\quad\text{where}\quad(u,y)\in[-1,1]\times Y.\end{aligned}

If
\varphi
is a closed differential form, then we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&=d\varphi=(du\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+d^{Y})(\varphi_{1}(u,y)+du\wedge\varphi_{2}(u,y))\\
&=du\wedge\left(\frac{\partial\varphi_{1}}{\partial u}(u)-d^{Y}\varphi_{2}(u)\right)+d^{Y}\varphi_{1}(u).\end{aligned}

By Equation (
2.8
), we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \varphi_{1}(1)-\varphi_{1}(-1)=d^{Y}\int^{1}_{-1}\varphi_{2}(u)du,\quad d^{Y}(\varphi_{1}(u))=0,\quad\text{for all}\quad u\in[-1,1].\end{aligned}

We see that
\{\varphi_{1}(u):\,u\in[-1,1]\}
gives a family of closed differential forms in the same de Rham cohomology class on
Y
.

We
assume
that
\varphi
satisfies the absolute boundary condition at
Y_{\{-1\}}
and the relative boundary condition at
Y_{\{1\}}
, i.e.,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \big(i_{\frac{\partial}{\partial u}}\varphi\big)|_{u=-1}=0,\quad\left(du\wedge\varphi\right)|_{u=1}=0.\end{aligned}

Proposition 2.1
.

If we impose the relative boundary conditions at
Y_{\{1\}}
, then the de Rham cohomology groups
H^{*}(Y_{[-1,1]},Y_{\{1\}};F)
vanish.

Proof.

By (
2.8
)-(
2.10
), we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \varphi_{1}(1)=0,\quad\varphi_{1}(1)-\varphi_{1}(u)=d^{Y}\int^{1}_{u}\varphi_{2}(v)dv.\end{aligned}

Hence we get

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \varphi_{1}(u)=d^{Y}\int^{u}_{1}\varphi_{2}(v)dv.\end{aligned}

Then by (
2.7
), we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \varphi&=d^{Y}\int^{u}_{1}\varphi_{2}(v)dv+du\wedge\varphi_{2}(u)\\
&=(d^{Y}+du\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial u})\int^{u}_{1}\varphi_{2}(v)dv=d\int^{u}_{1}\varphi_{2}(v)dv.\end{aligned}

Set
\phi(u,y):=\int^{u}_{1}\varphi_{2}(v,y)dv
. It is easy to verify that
\phi
satisfies the absolute boundary condition at
Y_{-1}
and the relative boundary condition at
Y_{1}
. By (
2.13
), we get
\varphi=d\phi
, which means that all the closed forms are exact.
∎

By Definition
1.3
and Proposition
2.1
, we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}_{a,r}(T^{H}M_{3},g^{TZ_{3}},h^{F}):=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}f^{\wedge}(D_{t},h^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Y_{[-1,1]},F)})_{a,r}\frac{dt}{t},\end{aligned}

and

(2.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}_{a,a}(T^{H}M_{3},g^{TZ_{3}},h^{F}):=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}&\left[f^{\wedge}(D_{t},h^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Y_{[-1,1]},F)})_{a,a}-\frac{\chi^{\prime}(Y,F)}{2}f^{\prime}(0)\right.\\
&\left.-\left(\frac{1}{4}m\rk(F)\chi(Y)-\frac{\chi^{\prime}(Y,F)}{2}\right)f^{\prime}(\frac{i\sqrt{t}}{2})\right]\frac{dt}{t}.\end{aligned}

For Equation (
2.15
), we have used the fact that
H^{k}(Y_{[-1,1]};F)\cong H^{k}(Y;F)
for
0\leq k\leq m-1
.

Now we try to calculate
\mathscr{T}_{a,a/r}(T^{H}X_{[-1,1]},g^{TY_{[-1,1]}},h^{F})
explicitly.

Let
e_{a,r}^{t\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x^{\prime}_{m})
be the heat kernel on
[-1,1]
with the absolute boundary condition at

and the relative boundary condition at

be the heat kernel

on

with the absolute boundary conditions at
-1,\,1

(2.16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &e_{a,a}^{t\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x^{\prime}_{m})=e_{D,D}^{t\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x^{\prime}_{m})dx_{m}\otimes(dx_{m})^{*}+e_{N,N}^{t\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x^{\prime}_{m})1\otimes 1^{*},\\
&e_{a,r}^{t\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x^{\prime}_{m})=e_{D,N}^{t\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x^{\prime}_{m})dx_{m}\otimes(dx_{m})^{*}+e_{N,D}^{t\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x^{\prime}_{m})1\otimes 1^{*}.\end{aligned}

Here the notations

etc. mean that we impose the Dirichlet boundary condition at
-1
and
the Neumann boundary condition at

.
Let
D^{2}_{t}
be the operator on

. Let
\widetilde{D}_{t}
be the operator on the boundary fibration

)-(

(2.17)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D^{2}_{t}=\widetilde{D}^{2}_{t}+\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}.\end{aligned}

For their heat kernels, we have the following relations:

(2.18)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} e^{D^{2}_{t}}_{a,a}(x,x^{\prime})=e^{\widetilde{D}^{2}_{t}}(y,y^{\prime})\otimes e_{a,a}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x^{\prime}_{m}),\quad e^{D^{2}_{t}}_{a,r}(x,x^{\prime})=e^{\widetilde{D}^{2}_{t}}(y,y^{\prime})\otimes e_{a,r}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x^{\prime}_{m}).\end{aligned}

By (
1.13
), we have

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &f^{\wedge}(D_{t},h^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Y_{[-1,1]},F)})_{a,a}=\varphi\str\left[\frac{N}{2}f^{\prime}(D_{t})_{a,a}\right]\\
&=\varphi\int_{Y_{[-1,1]}}\tr\left[(-1)^{N}\frac{N}{2}(1+2D^{2}_{t})e^{D^{2}_{t}}_{a,a}(x,x)\right]dv_{x}\\
&=\frac{\varphi}{2}\int_{Y_{[-1,1]}}\tr\Big[(-1)^{N}N\big(1+2\widetilde{D}^{2}_{t}+\frac{t}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}\big)e^{\widetilde{D}^{2}_{t}}(y,y)\otimes e_{a,a}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x_{m})\Big]dv_{x}\\
&=\frac{\varphi}{2}\int_{Y_{[-1,1]}}\tr\Big[(-1)^{N}N\big(1+2\widetilde{D}^{2}_{t}\big)e^{\widetilde{D}^{2}_{t}}(y,y)\otimes e_{a,a}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x_{m})\Big]dv_{x}\\
&\qquad+\frac{\varphi}{2}\int_{Y_{[-1,1]}}\tr\Big[(-1)^{N}Ne^{\widetilde{D}^{2}_{t}}(y,y)\otimes\frac{t}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}e_{a,a}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x_{m})\Big]dv_{x}\\
&=-\frac{\varphi}{2}\int_{Y_{[-1,1]}}\tr\Big[(-1)^{N_{Y}}(N_{Y}+1)f^{\prime}(\widetilde{D}_{t})(y,y)\otimes e_{D,D}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x_{m})\Big]dv_{x}\\
&+\frac{\varphi}{2}\int_{Y_{[-1,1]}}\tr\Big[(-1)^{N_{Y}}N_{Y}f^{\prime}(\widetilde{D}_{t})(y,y)\otimes e_{N,N}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x_{m})\Big]dv_{x}\\
&\qquad-\frac{\varphi}{2}\int_{Y_{[-1,1]}}\tr\Big[(-1)^{N_{Y}}(N_{Y}+1)e^{\widetilde{D}^{2}_{t}}(y,y)\otimes\frac{t}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}e_{D,D}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x_{m})\Big]dv_{x}\\
&\qquad+\frac{\varphi}{2}\int_{Y_{[-1,1]}}\tr\Big[(-1)^{N_{Y}}N_{Y}e^{\widetilde{D}^{2}_{t}}(y,y)\otimes\frac{t}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}e_{N,N}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x_{m})\Big]dv_{x}.\end{aligned}

By the Mckean-Singer formula (cf.
[

, Theorem 3.50]
), we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 1=\chi_{a,a}([-1,1])=\int_{-1}^{1}e_{N,N}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x_{m})dx_{m}-\int_{-1}^{1}e_{D,D}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x_{m})dx_{m}.\end{aligned}

By
[

, Theorem 3.15]
, (
2.19
) and (

), we have

(2.21)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f^{\wedge}(D_{t},h^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Y_{[-1,1]},F)})_{a,a}=&\varphi\int_{Y}\str\Big[\frac{N_{Y}}{2}f^{\prime}(\widetilde{D}_{t})(y,y)\Big]dv_{y}\cdot\chi_{a,a}([-1,1])\\
&\quad-\frac{\varphi}{2}\int_{Y}\str\Big[f^{\prime}(\widetilde{D}_{t})(y,y)\Big]dv_{y}\cdot\int_{-1}^{1}e_{D,D}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x_{m})dx_{m}\\
&\quad-\frac{\varphi}{2}\int_{Y}\str\left[e^{\widetilde{D}^{2}_{t}}(y,y)\right]dv_{y}\cdot\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{t}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}e_{D,D}^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}(x_{m},x_{m})dx_{m}\\
&=f^{\wedge}(\widetilde{D}_{t},h^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Y,F)})-\frac{\rk(F)\chi(Y)}{2}\cdot\tr\big[(1+2t\frac{\partial}{\partial t})e^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}_{D,D}\big].\end{aligned}

(2.22)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f^{\wedge}(D_{t},h^{\Omega^{\bullet}(Y_{[-1,1]},F)})_{a,r}&=-\frac{\rk(F)\chi(Y)}{2}\cdot\tr\big[(1+2t\frac{\partial}{\partial t})e^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}_{D,N}\big].\end{aligned}

Lemma 2.2
.

We have

(2.23)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{\infty}\tr\Big[(1+2t\frac{\partial}{\partial t})e^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}_{D,D}+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(\frac{i\sqrt{t}}{2})\Big]\frac{dt}{t}=-4\log 2.\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}\tr\Big[(1+2t\frac{\partial}{\partial t})e^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}_{D,N}\Big]\frac{dt}{t}=-2\log 2.\end{aligned}

Proof.

The zeta functions associated with Laplacians are defined as the Mellin transform of the trace of heat operators
[
BGV04
, §9.6]
, i.e.,

(2.24)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \zeta_{D,D/N}(s):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s}\tr[e^{t\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}_{D,D/N}]\frac{dt}{t}.\end{aligned}

Let
\zeta(s)
be the Riemann zeta function and
\widetilde{\zeta}(s):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(k-\frac{1}{2})^{-s}
. Then by
[
Wei99
, §9]
, one has

(2.25)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \zeta(0)=-\frac{1}{2},\quad\zeta^{\prime}(0)=-\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi),\quad\widetilde{\zeta}(0)=0,\quad\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}(0)=-\frac{1}{2}\log 2.\end{aligned}

The zeta functions in (
2.24
) can also expressed as

(2.26)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \zeta_{D,D}(s)&=2\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2s}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2s}}=2\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2s}\zeta(2s),\\
\zeta_{D,N}(s)&=2\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2s}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k-\frac{1}{2})^{2s}}=2\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2s}\widetilde{\zeta}(2s).\end{aligned}

Then we have

(2.27)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \zeta_{D,D}^{\prime}(0)&=-4\log 2,\quad\zeta_{D,N}^{\prime}(0)=-2\log 2.\end{aligned}

Note that
f^{\prime}(x)=(1+2x^{2})e^{x^{2}}
. Proceeding as in
[
BL95
, Theorem 3.29]
,
by integration by parts we get that the left hand side of (
2.23
) is equal to

(2.28)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{\infty}\tr\Big[(1+2t\frac{\partial}{\partial t})e^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}_{D,D}+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(\frac{i\sqrt{t}}{2})\Big]\frac{dt}{t}=\zeta^{\prime}_{D,D}(0).\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}\tr\Big[(1+2t\frac{\partial}{\partial t})e^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}_{D,N}\Big]\frac{dt}{t}=\zeta^{\prime}_{D,N}(0).\end{aligned}

Then Equation (
2.23
) follows from (
2.27
) and (
2.28
). The proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

We have

(2.29)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}_{a,a}(T^{H}X_{[-1,1]},g^{TY_{[-1,1]}},h^{F})&=\mathscr{T}(T^{H}X,g^{TY},h^{F})-2\log 2\rk(F)\chi(Y),\\
\mathscr{T}_{a,r}(T^{H}X_{[-1,1]},g^{TY_{[-1,1]}},h^{F})&=-\log 2\rk(F)\chi(Y).\end{aligned}

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, (
2.14
), (
2.15
), (
2.21
) and (
2.22

(2.30)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}_{a,r}(T^{H}M_{3},g^{TZ_{3}},h^{F})&=\frac{\rk(F)\chi(Y)}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\cdot\tr\big[f^{\prime}(\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}})_{D,N}\big]\frac{dt}{t}\\
&=\frac{\rk(F)\chi(Y)}{2}\cdot(-2\log 2)\\
&=-\log 2\rk(F)\chi(Y),\end{aligned}

and

(2.31)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}_{a,a}(T^{H}M_{3},g^{TZ_{3}},h^{F})&=\mathscr{T}(T^{H}X,g^{TY},h^{F})\\
&-\rk(F)\chi(Y)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\tr\big[(1+2t\frac{\partial}{\partial t})e^{\frac{t}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{m}}}_{D,D}\big]-\frac{1}{4}f^{\prime}(\frac{i\sqrt{t}}{2})\right]\frac{dt}{t}\\

The proof is completed.

H^{k}(Y_{[-1,1]};F)\overset{\alpha_{k}}{\longrightarrow}H^{k}(Y;F)
is given by

(2.35)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}\left[0\rightarrow H^{k}(Y_{[-1,1]};F)\overset{\alpha_{k}}{\longrightarrow}H^{k}(Y;F)\rightarrow 0\right]=-\frac{\log 2}{2}\rk(H^{k}(Y;F)).\end{aligned}

By
[
BL95
, Theorem A1.4]
, (
2.34
) and (
2.35
), we have

(2.36)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime}}-\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}}&\doteq\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}\mathscr{T}\left[0\rightarrow H^{k}(Y_{[-1,1]};F)\overset{\alpha_{k}}{\longrightarrow}H^{k}(Y;F)\rightarrow 0\right]\\
&\doteq-\frac{\log 2}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}\rk(H^{k}(Y;F))\\
&\doteq-\frac{\log 2}{2}\rk(F)\chi(Y).\end{aligned}

By (
2.33
) and (
2.36
), we get

(2.37)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathscr{T}_{r}(T^{H}M_{1},g^{TZ_{1}},h^{F})-\mathscr{T}_{\rm a}(T^{H}M_{1},g^{TZ_{1}},h^{F})+\mathscr{T}(T^{H}X,g^{TY},h^{F})+\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}}\\
&\doteq\log 2\rk(F)\chi(Y)+\frac{\log 2}{2}\rk(F)\chi(Y)=\frac{3}{2}\log 2\rk(F)\chi(Y).\end{aligned}

By (
2.37
), we finally get the comparison formula of the absolute and
relative Bismut-Lott analytic torsion forms.

Theorem 2.4
.

Under the assumptions of product structures (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), we have

(2.38)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathscr{T}_{r}(T^{H}M_{1},g^{TZ_{1}},h^{F})-\mathscr{T}_{\rm a}(T^{H}M_{1},g^{TZ_{1}},h^{F})\\
&\qquad\qquad\doteq-\mathscr{T}(T^{H}X,g^{TY},h^{F})-\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}}+\frac{3}{2}\log 2\rk(F)\chi(Y),\end{aligned}

where
\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}}
is the torsion form associated with the second line in the diagram (

).

M\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}S

Z

X\subset M

M
into two smooth fibrations
M^{\prime}_{1}
and

X\rightarrow S
is also a smooth fibration over

with standard fiber
Y
, such that
Z=Z^{\prime}_{1}\cup_{Y}Z^{\prime}_{2}
. Let
X_{[-1,1]}
be a product neighborhood of

. Set

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} M_{1}=M^{\prime}_{1}\cup_{Y_{\{0\}}}X_{[0,1]},\quad M_{2}=X_{[-1,0]}\cup_{Y_{\{0\}}}M^{\prime}_{2},\quad M_{3}=X_{[-1,1]}.\end{aligned}

Then both

have
X_{[-1,1]}
as the cylinder parts, where the product structures of metrics (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) are assumed. This setting is described in Figure
3
.

By the gluing formula of Bismut-Lott torsion forms in
[
PZZ20
]
, the following identity holds modulo some exact differential forms over
S
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}(T^{H}M,g^{TZ},h^{F})-\mathscr{T}_{\rm a}(T^{H}M_{1},g^{TZ_{1}},h^{F})-&\mathscr{T}_{\rm a}(T^{H}M_{2},g^{TZ_{2}},h^{F})\\
&+\mathscr{T}_{\rm a,a}(T^{H}M_{3},g^{TZ_{3}},h^{F})+\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime}}\doteq 0,\end{aligned}

where
\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime}}
is the torsion form associated to

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{H}^{\prime}:\quad\cdots\rightarrow H^{k}(Z;F)\rightarrow H^{k}(Z_{1};F)\oplus H^{k}(Z_{2};F)\rightarrow H^{k}(Y_{[-1,1]};F)\rightarrow\cdots.\end{aligned}

Applying the comparison formula in Theorem
2.4
and (
2.36
), we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathscr{T}_{\rm a}(T^{H}M_{1},g^{TZ_{1}},h^{F})-\mathscr{T}(T^{H}X,g^{TY},h^{F})\\
&\doteq\mathscr{T}_{\rm r}(T^{H}M_{1},g^{TZ_{1}},h^{F})+\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}}-\log 2\rk(F)\chi(Y),\end{aligned}

where
\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}}
is the torsion form associated to

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}:\quad\cdots\rightarrow H^{k}(Z_{1},Y;F)\rightarrow H^{k}(Z_{1};F)\rightarrow H^{k}(Y_{[-1,1]};F)\rightarrow\cdots.\end{aligned}

we have
\mathscr{T}_{\rm a,a}(T^{H}M_{3},g^{TZ_{3}},h^{F})=\mathscr{T}(T^{H}X,g^{TY},h^{F})-2\log 2\rk(F)\chi(Y)
, then by (

where
\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}}
is the torsion form associated to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{H}:\quad\cdots\rightarrow H^{k}(Z_{1},Y;F)\rightarrow H^{k}(Z;F)\rightarrow H^{k}(Z_{2};F)\rightarrow\cdots.\end{aligned}

Lemma 3.1
.

The following identity holds modulo some exact differential forms,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime}}\doteq\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}}-\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{H}}.\end{aligned}

Proof.

The main idea to prove (
3.8
) is to decompose the long exact sequences of flat vector bundles into short exact sequences. We essentially follow the techniques used in
[
Ma02
, §3.1]
.

We start from the right hand side of (
3.8
). We denote

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}:\quad\cdots\overset{\delta^{\prime\prime}_{k-1}}{\rightarrow}H^{k}(Z_{1},Y;F)\overset{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{k}}{\rightarrow}H^{k}(Z_{1};F)\overset{\beta^{\prime\prime}_{k}}{\rightarrow}H^{k}(Y_{[-1,1]};F)\overset{\delta^{\prime\prime}_{k}}{\rightarrow}\cdots.\end{aligned}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{H}:\quad\cdots\overset{\delta_{k-1}}{\rightarrow}H^{k}(Z_{1},Y;F)\overset{\alpha_{k}}{\rightarrow}H^{k}(Z;F)\overset{\beta_{k}}{\rightarrow}H^{k}(Z_{2};F)\overset{\delta_{k}}{\rightarrow}\cdots.\end{aligned}

We use
(-1)^{l}\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}
to denote the same sequence as
\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}
but the degree of each component increased by
l
.
Then we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}&:\quad\cdots\overset{\beta^{\prime\prime}_{k-1}}{\rightarrow}&H^{k-1}(Y_{[-1,1]};F)&\overset{\delta^{\prime\prime}_{k-1}}{\rightarrow}&H^{k}(Z_{1},Y;F)&\overset{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{k}}{\rightarrow}&H^{k}(Z_{1};F)&\overset{\beta^{\prime\prime}_{k}}{\rightarrow}\cdots,\\
\mathscr{H}&:\quad\cdots\overset{\delta_{k-1}}{\rightarrow}&H^{k}(Z_{1},Y;F)&\overset{\alpha_{k}}{\rightarrow}&H^{k}(Z;F)&\overset{\beta_{k}}{\rightarrow}&H^{k}(Z_{2};F)&\overset{\delta_{k}}{\rightarrow}\cdots.\end{aligned}

For a long exact sequence

\mathscr{E}:\quad 0\rightarrow E^{0}\overset{f_{0}}{\rightarrow}E^{1}\overset{f_{1}}{\rightarrow}\cdots E^{k}\overset{f_{k}}{\rightarrow}E^{k+1}\overset{f_{k+1}}{\rightarrow}\cdots\overset{f_{n-1}}{\rightarrow}E^{n}\rightarrow 0,

we set
\mathscr{E}(k)
to be its truncation at the degree
k
, i.e.,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{E}(k):\quad 0\rightarrow E^{0}\overset{f_{0}}{\rightarrow}E^{1}\overset{f_{1}}{\rightarrow}\cdots E^{k}\overset{f_{k}}{\rightarrow}{\rm Im}(f_{k})\rightarrow 0.\end{aligned}

By using
[
Zhu15
, Lemma 2.7]
and
[
Ma02
, Lemma 3.1(d)]
, we will finish the proof by induction on the degree of truncation.

For
k=1
, on one hand we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}(3k-1)&:\quad 0\rightarrow&0\qquad&\rightarrow&H^{0}(Z_{1},Y;F)&\overset{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{0}}{\rightarrow}&H^{0}(Z_{1};F)&\overset{\beta^{\prime\prime}_{0}}{\rightarrow}{\rm Im}(\beta^{\prime\prime}_{0})\rightarrow 0,\\
\mathscr{H}(3k-1)&:\quad 0\rightarrow&H^{0}(Z_{1},Y;F)&\overset{\alpha_{0}}{\rightarrow}&H^{0}(Z;F)&\overset{\beta_{0}}{\rightarrow}&H^{0}(Z_{2};F)&\overset{\delta_{0}}{\rightarrow}{\rm Im}(\delta_{0})\rightarrow 0.\end{aligned}

On the other hand, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\mathscr{H}^{\prime}(3k-1)&:\quad 0\rightarrow&0&\rightarrow&H^{0}(Z;F)&\overset{\alpha^{\prime}_{0}}{\rightarrow}&H^{0}(Z_{1},Y;F)\oplus H^{0}(Z_{2};F)&\overset{\delta^{\prime}_{0}}{\rightarrow}{\rm Im}(\delta^{\prime}_{0})\rightarrow 0.\end{aligned}

By
[
Ma02
, Lemma 3.1(d)]
, we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}(-\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}(2))+\mathscr{T}(\mathscr{H}(2))=\mathscr{T}(-\mathscr{H}^{\prime}(2)).\end{aligned}

In the same way, we can prove

(3.16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}(-\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime}(3k-1))+\mathscr{T}(\mathscr{H}(3k-1))=\mathscr{T}(-\mathscr{H}^{\prime}(3k-1)).\end{aligned}

When
k
equals
n
in (
3.16
), we get

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathscr{T}(-\mathscr{H}^{\prime\prime})+\mathscr{T}(\mathscr{H})=\mathscr{T}(-\mathscr{H}^{\prime}).\end{aligned}

Since we have
\mathscr{T}((-1)^{k}\mathscr{H})=(-1)^{k}\mathscr{T}(\mathscr{H})
, Equation (
3.8
) follows from (
3.17
).
∎

3.1
and (
3.6

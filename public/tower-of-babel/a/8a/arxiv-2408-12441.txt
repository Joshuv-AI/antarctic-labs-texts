Automorphism Groups of Finite Extensions of Fields and the Minimal Ramification Problem
Alexei Entin

Abstract
. We study the following question: given a global field F F and finite group G G , what is the minimal r r such that there exists a finite extension K / F K/F with Aut ⁡ ( K / F ) ≅ G {\mathrm{Aut}}(K/F)\cong G that is ramified over exactly r r places of F F ? We conjecture that the answer is ≤ 1 \leq 1 for any global field F F and finite group G G . In the case when F F is a number field we show that the answer is always ≤ 4 [ F : ℚ ] \leq 4[F:{\mathbb{Q}}] . We show that assuming Schinzel’s Hypothesis H the answer is always ≤ 1 \leq 1 if F F is a number field. We show unconditionally that the answer is always ≤ 1 \leq 1 if F F is a global function field. We also show that for a broader class of fields F F than previously known, every finite group G G can be realized as the automorphism group of a finite extension K / F K/F (without restriction on the ramification). An important new tool used in this work is a recent result of the author and C. Tsang, which says that for any finite group G G there exists a natural number n n and a subgroup H ⩽ S n H\leqslant S_{n} of the symmetric group such that N S n ​ ( H ) / H ≅ G N_{S_{n}}(H)/H\cong G .



1.
Introduction

The Inverse Galois Problem (IGP) asks whether any finite group can be realized as the Galois group of some finite Galois extension
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
. It remains largely open, though many interesting cases have been solved, see e.g.
[
MM18
,
Sha58
,
Zyw15
,
Zyw23
]
.
A refinement of this problem, called the Minimal Ramification Problem (MRP), asks for a given finite group
G
what is the minimal number of ramified places of
{\mathbb{Q}}
(by a place of
{\mathbb{Q}}
we mean a finite prime or the infinite place) in a Galois extension
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
with
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/{\mathbb{Q}})\cong G
. Let us denote this number by

r(G)
. If there is no Galois extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
with Galois group
G
we set
r(G)=\infty
. It was conjectured by Boston and Markin
[
BM09
, Conjecture 1.2]
that for
G\neq 1
we have
r(G)=\max(d(G^{\mathrm{ab}}),1)
, where
d(H)
denotes the minimal number of generators of a group
H
. For progress on the MRP see
[
BM09
,
Pla04
,
KNS10
,
BSS20
,
BSEF23
]
. The function field analogue of this problem was studied by the author, Bary-Soroker and Fehm
[
BSEF23
]
, by the author and Pirani
[
EP24
, §1.1]
and by Shusterman
[
Shu24
]
.

In another direction, the following weak form of the IGP has been considered: given a finite group
G
, does there exist a finite extension
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
, not necessarily Galois, such that
{\mathrm{Aut}}(K/{\mathbb{Q}})\cong G
? This question was answered affirmatively for every finite group
G
by E. Fried and Kollár
[
FK78
]
(their proof contained a small gap later corrected by M. Fried
[
Fri80
]
). A simpler alternative proof, using the Hilbert Irreducibility Theorem (HIT), was given by M. Fried
[
Fri80
]
. A more elementary argument avoiding the use of HIT was given by Geyer
[
Gey83
]
. The result has been generalized to all global base fields (in place of
{\mathbb{Q}}
) by Takahashi
[
Tak80
]
, to all Hilbertian fields by Legrand and Paran
[
LP18
]
and to an even broader class of base fields by Deschamps and Legrand
[
DL21
]
. In the present paper we give a new and shorter proof of these results, which applies to an even broader class of fields (see Theorem
4
and its proof below). For the corresponding problem for transcendental extensions, with possibly infinite automorphism groups, see
[
DG87
]
.

The main focus of the present paper is the problem of realizing a given finite group
G
as
{\mathrm{Aut}}(K/F)
for some finite extension
K/F
of a fixed global field
F
, such that the number of places of
F
ramified in
K/F
is as small as possible.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
F
be a number field and
G
a finite group. There exists a finite extension
K/F
such that
{\mathrm{Aut}}(K/F)\cong G
, ramified over at most a single place of
F
.

A conditional proof (assuming Schinzel’s Hypothesis H) and partial results towards Conjecture
1.1
will be given in §
3
. The question of whether one can find an everywhere unramified extension with
{\mathrm{Aut}}(K/F)=G
is more difficult and will be discussed briefly in §
1.1
.

We will show that our problem for a general finite group
G
is closely related to the MRP in the special case of the symmetric and alternating groups. For a finite extension of global fields
K/F
let us denote by
{\mathrm{Ram}}(K/F)
the set of places of
F
ramified in the extension and define

r_{F}(G)=\min_{K/F\,\mathrm{Galois}\atop{{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/F)\cong G}}|{\mathrm{Ram}}(K/F)|,

setting
r_{F}(G)=\infty
if there is no Galois extension
K/F
with
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/F)=G
. Our main result is the following

Theorem 1
.

Let
F
be a global field,
G
a finite group. There exists a finite extension
K/F
such that
{\mathrm{Aut}}(K)\cong G
, ramified over at most

r=\underline{\lim}_{n\to\infty}\min(r_{F}(S_{n}),r_{F}(A_{n}))
places of
F
.

We will use Theorem
1
to derive partial results and evidence towards Conjecture
1.1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
F
be a number field and
G
a finite group.

(i)

There exists a finite extension
K/F
such that
{\mathrm{Aut}}(K/F)\cong G
, ramified over at most
4[F:{\mathbb{Q}}]
places of
F
.

(ii)

Assume that
r_{\mathbb{Q}}(S_{n})=1
(this is a special case of the Boston-Markin conjecture mentioned above) for infinitely many

. Then Conjecture

holds for
F={\mathbb{Q}}
.

(iii)

Assume Schinzel’s Hypothesis H (see
[

, p. 4]

In addition to Theorem

, a key ingredient in the proof of Theorem

, Theorem 1.11]
to arbitrary number fields and is of independent interest.

Proposition 1.2
.

Assume Schinzel’s Hypothesis H (in its usual form over the integers). Then

for any number field

.

Theorem
1
will be proved in §

and Theorem

(as well as Proposition

Remark 1.3

In the statements of Theorem

with the required properties. This can be shown by augmenting the proofs with the argument used in the proof of Theorem

Remark 1.4

Our method can be used to handle other ramification conditions, as long as the corresponding result is available for infinitely many symmetric or alternating groups. For example, it was conjectured by Roberts and Venkatesh (

We also establish a strong function field analogue of Conjecture

Theorem 3
.

Let

.

Remark 1.5
.

The extension

is called geometric if

have the same fields of constants. This assumption appears in the function field analogue of the MRP studied in

.

1.1.
Everywhere unramified extensions

One may ask under what conditions Conjecture
1.1
(resp. Theorem
3
) can be strengthened to require
K/F
to be everywhere unramified. This clearly cannot happen if
F={\mathbb{Q}}
if
G\neq 1
, because
{\mathbb{Q}}
has no nontrivial unramified extensions. But it can happen for other number fields, with the answer depending on
G
for some of them. This is clearly related to the question of whether
F
has an infinite unramified extension, which is known to be difficult (see
[
Mai00
,
Bri10
,
Kim16
,
Won11
]
for a sample of special cases).

In the function field case one may expect a more uniform answer. If
F
has genus 0, it has no nontrivial geometric unramified extensions. If
F
has genus 1 then all of its geometric unramified extensions come from isogenies, are all abelian, and the possible Galois groups are precisely the subgroups of the reduced class group
\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(F)
(this follows from class theory or the theory of elliptic curves). In the case of genus
\geq 2
we may pose the following

Question 1.6
.

Let
F
be a global function field of genus
g\geq 2
. Is it necessarily true that
r_{F}(A_{n})=0
for infinitely many
n
?

If the answer is positive, by Theorem
1
there exists a finite unramified
K/F
with
{\mathrm{Aut}}(K/F)\cong G
, for any finite group
G
. If we consider a univariate function field
F
with an algebraically closed field of constants (instead of a finite field), a general result of Stevenson
[
Ste96
, Corollary 3.5]
guarantees a positive answer to the above question. The case of a finite field of constants appears to be open. We note that an unramified
S_{n}
-extension cannot exist if the reduced class group
\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(F)
has odd order (this follows from class field theory).

1.2.
Extensions without restriction on ramification

Legrand and Paran
[
LP18
, Theorem 1.1]
have shown that for any Hilbertian field
F
and finite group
G
, there exist infinitely many non-isomorphic finite extensions
K/F
such that
{\mathrm{Aut}}(K/F)\cong G
. For the definition and basic properties of Hilbertian fields see
[
FJ08
, §12]
. This result was extended to a broader class of fields by Deschamps and Legrand
[
DL21
, Théorème 4.1]
. We give a new and simpler proof of these results and extend them to a yet broader class of fields.

Theorem 4
.

Let
F
be a field that has a Galois
S_{n}
-extension or a Galois
A_{n}
-extension for infinitely many
n
. Then for any finite group
G
there exist infinitely many non-isomorphic finite extensions
K/F
with
{\mathrm{Aut}}(K/F)\cong G
.

Theorem
4
will be proved in §
2
.
The condition on
F
in Theorem
4
is weaker than the assumptions in
[
DL21
, Théorème 4.1]
, which we do not repeat here in detail. We only mention that
[
DL21
, Théorème 4.1]
requires that
F
has Galois
A_{n}
-extensions for infinitely many
n
, in addition to other restrictions. It is easy to construct fields satisfying the assumption of Theorem
4
but not this stronger condition. For example take
F
to be the compositum of all Galois extensions
L/{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/{\mathbb{Q}})
has some
A_{n}
as a quotient (then
F
has Galois
S_{n}
-extensions for all
n
, but no Galois
A_{n}
-extensions). Finally we note that every Hilbertian field clearly satisfies the assumption of Theorem
4
because it is an elementary fact that a Hilbertian field has a Galois
S_{n}
-extension for every
n
(just specialize the coefficients of the generic polynomial
x^{n}+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\ldots+a_{0}
which has Galois group
S_{n}
), which gives another proof of
[
LP18
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Let
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}:F\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the real embeddings of
F
and denote by
\mathcal{O}_{F}
the ring of integers of
F
. Denote also
d=[F:{\mathbb{Q}}]
, let
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
be a primitive element of
F/{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\alpha_{1}=\alpha,\,\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{d}
its conjugates over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
be independent variables. We will use the shorthand
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
. Consider the generic monic discriminant

D(\mathbf{A})=D(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})={\mathrm{disc}}_{X}(X^{n}+A_{1}X^{n-1}+\ldots+A_{n}).

Denote

(3.1)

\prod_{i=1}^{n}(X-i)=X^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}X^{n-i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]

and set

P=\prod_{p\leq dn(n-1)\atop{\mathrm{prime}}}p.

Set

(3.2)

f(X;\mathbf{A},T)=X^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}(T^{i}+A_{i})X^{n-i}+P\alpha A_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{F}[\mathbf{A},T][X].

Substituting
T=0
we see that
{\mathrm{Gal}}(f(X;\mathbf{A},0)/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{A}))\cong S_{n}
and therefore
{\mathrm{Gal}}(f(X;\mathbf{A},T)/{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{A},T))\cong S_{n}
(since the Galois group can only shrink under specialization and extension of the base field and both groups are contained in
S_{n}
).
We aim to show (assuming Hypothesis H) that for a suitable choice of
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\,t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
the specialized polynomial
f(X;\mathbf{a},t)\in\mathcal{O}_{F}[X]
has Galois group
S_{n}
over
F
and its splitting field over
F
is ramified over at most one place. This would establish
r_{F}(S_{n})\leq 1
.

Denote

(3.3)

D_{j}(\mathbf{A},T)=D\left(c_{1}(A_{1}+T),\ldots,c_{n-1}(A_{n-1}+T^{n-1}),c_{n}(A_{n}+T^{n})+P\alpha_{j}A_{n}\right)\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha_{j})[\mathbf{A},T],\quad 1\leq j\leq d.

It is well-known that the generic discriminant
D(\mathbf{A})
is absolutely irreducible (i.e. irreducible in
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}[\mathbf{A}]
) and consequently
D_{j}(\mathbf{A},T)\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}[\mathbf{A},T]
are irreducible (they are obtained from
D
by an invertible change of variables). It is easy to see that for a suitable choice of primitive element
\alpha
(which we henceforth assume to have been made) the polynomials
D_{1},\ldots,D_{d}
are distinct and therefore (e.g. using (
3.6
) below) non-associate. Since
{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
acts transitively on
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}
(and hence on
D_{1},\ldots,D_{d}
) it follows that

(3.4)

H(\mathbf{A},T)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}D_{j}(\mathbf{A},T)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\mathbf{A},T]

is irreducible in
{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathbf{A},T]
. We note that (by (
3.2
),(
3.3
),(
3.4
)) for any
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\,t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have

(3.5)

H(\mathbf{a},t)=N_{F/{\mathbb{Q}}}({\mathrm{disc}}(f(X;\mathbf{a},t))).

By
[
FJ08
, Theorem 13.3.5(c) and Corollary 12.2.3]
we can pick
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
such that

(i)

H(\mathbf{a},T)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[T]
is irreducible in
{\mathbb{Q}}[T]
.

(ii)

{\mathrm{Gal}}(f(X;\mathbf{a},T)/{\mathbb{Q}}(T))\cong S_{n}
.

(iii)

The reduction modulo
p
of
X^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}a_{i}X^{n-i}
is separable for any prime
p|P

(to satisfy (iii) we just need to pick each
a_{i}
in a suitable arithmetic progression modulo
P
).

Fix a choice of
\mathbf{a}
satisfying (i-iii). We claim that it automatically satisfies the following strengthening of (i):

(iv)

H(\mathbf{a},T)
is irreducible in
{\mathbb{Z}}[T]
.

Indeed it is easy to see from (
3.3
),(
3.1
) and the fact that
D(\mathbf{A})
is homogeneous of degree
n(n-1)
with respect to the weights
w(A_{i})=i

[
GKZ94
, (12.1.24)]
that

(3.6)

D_{j}(\mathbf{A},T)=\left(\prod_{1\leq i<k\leq n}(k-i)^{2}\right)T^{n(n-1)}+\mbox{lower degrees in }T

and therefore (using (
3.4
))

H(\mathbf{a},T)=\left(\prod_{1\leq i<k\leq n}(k-i)^{2d}\right)T^{dn(n-1)}+\mbox{lower degrees in }T.

It follows that for a prime
p>n
we have
p\nmid H(\mathbf{a},T)
. If
p\leq n
then
p|P
, and substituting
T=0
in (
3.2
) and using (iii) and that
H(\mathbf{a},0)=N_{F/{\mathbb{Q}}}({\mathrm{disc}}_{t}(f(X;\mathbf{a},0)))
by (
3.5
), we see that
p\nmid H(\mathbf{a},0)
and therefore
p\nmid H(\mathbf{a},T)
in this case as well. Thus
H(\mathbf{a},T)
is content-free (not divisible by any prime) and (i) implies (iv).

By Proposition
3.2
there exist
v,u\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(v)

(u,v)=1
.

(vi)

For any
t\in\mathbb{Z}
with
t\equiv u\pmod{v}
we have
{\mathrm{Gal}}(f(X;\mathbf{a},t)/{\mathbb{Q}})\cong S_{n}
.

(vii)

H(\mathbf{a},u)\not\equiv 0\pmod{v}
.

A suitable
u
can be found for a sufficiently large
v
, because for the
v
given by Proposition
3.2
at least
\delta v
residues
(u\bmod v)\in({\mathbb{Z}}/v{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
satisfy (vi) for some
\delta>0
independent of
v
, and the number of roots of
H(\mathbf{a},T)
modulo
v
is bounded by a constant independent of
v
, since the latter has a bounded number of prime factors (here we are using that
H(\mathbf{a},T)
is nonzero modulo any prime by (iv)).

Next we want to use Hypothesis H to pick a large
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
H(\mathbf{a},u+vs)
is prime. We need to show that there is no local obstruction, i.e. for any (rational) prime
p
we may pick
s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
p\nmid H(\mathbf{a},u+vs)
. If
p|P
then by (
3.2
),(
3.5
) and assumption (iii) above we have that

H(\mathbf{a},0)=N_{F/{\mathbb{Q}}}({\mathrm{disc}}_{X}(f(X;\mathbf{a},0)))\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

If
p\nmid P
, then once again using the fact that
D(\mathbf{A})
has degree

with respect to the weights
w(A_{i})=i
we obtain

p>dn(n-1)\geq\deg_{T}(D_{1}(\mathbf{a},T)\cdots D_{d}(\mathbf{a},T))=\deg_{T}H(\mathbf{a},T)
. In the case
p\nmid v
we see that

has at most
\deg_{T}H(\mathbf{a},T)<p
roots modulo
p
, so some
s
is not a root.
In the case
p|v
, by assumption (vii) above we may take
s=0
.
We have established that there is no local obstruction to
H(\mathbf{a},u+vs)
being prime and since
H(\mathbf{a},u+vX)
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Q}[X]
(because
H(\mathbf{a},X)
is by (i)), Hypothesis H implies that there are arbitrarily large
t=u+vs
with the following properties:

(viii)

H(\mathbf{a},t)
is prime.

(ix)

t\equiv u\pmod{v}
.

We claim that for sufficiently large
t
satisfying (viii-ix) the polynomial
f(X;\mathbf{a},t)
satisfies the required properties. First observe that by (vi) above we have
{\mathrm{Gal}}(f(X;\mathbf{a},t)/{\mathbb{Q}})\cong S_{n}
. Next since
H(\mathbf{a},t)=N_{F/{\mathbb{Q}}}({\mathrm{disc}}_{X}(f(X;\mathbf{a},t)))
is prime, we have that
\mathfrak{q}=({\mathrm{disc}}_{X}f(X;\mathbf{a},t))
is a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
and is therefore the only possible finite place over which the splitting field of
f(X;\mathbf{a},t)
over

ramifies. Finally if for each embedding
\sigma_{j}:F\to{\mathbb{R}}
and polynomial
g=\sum{g_{i}}X^{i}\in F[X]
we denote
g^{\sigma_{j}}=\sum\sigma_{j}(g_{i})X^{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}[X]
, we see from (
3.1
) and (

) that the roots of
f(tX;\mathbf{a},t)^{\sigma_{j}}
approach

as

and in particular have to be real for

sufficiently large (since they cannot occur in conjugate pairs). Consequently the splitting field of
f(X;\mathbf{a},t)
over

is unramified over the infinite places of

and is therefore ramified only over

. This concludes the proof that
r_{F}(S_{n})\leq 1
and establishes Proposition
1.2
.

4.
Function fields: proof of Theorem
3

Let
F
be a global function field with field of constants
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and characteristic
p
,
P
a place of
F
.
A simple consequence of the Riemann-Roch theorem is that there exists
T\in F\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
that has a pole only at
P
(see e.g.
[
Ros02
, Theorem 6.13]
for a more general statement).

If
p>2
, by
[
EP24
, Theorem 1]
for any
n\geq p
there exists a Galois extension
M_{n}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T)
with
{\mathrm{Gal}}(M_{n}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T))\\
=A_{n}
, ramified only over the place
T=\infty
. In the case
p=2
such an extension exists for any
9\leq n\equiv 1\pmod{8}
by
[
Abh93
, Theorem 2]
and
[
AOS94
, Equation (5.27)]
(one takes the splitting field of
f(X)=X^{n}+TX^{n-4}+1
). In what follows we only consider
n
such that
\max(p,9)\leq n\equiv 1\pmod{8}
.

The extension
M_{n}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T)
is necessarily geometric (since
A_{n}
has no cyclic quotients). The extensions
M_{n}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T)
are all pairwise linearly disjoint since the groups
A_{n}
are simple and non-isomorphic. Therefore we may find
n\geq n_{0}(G)
such that
M=M_{n}
is linearly disjoint from
F
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T)
. Here
n_{0}(G)
is chosen to satisfy the assertion of Proposition
2.2
.

Let
L
be the compositum of
F
and
M
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T)
(inside a common algebraic closure). Since
F,M
are linearly disjoint over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T)
we have
{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/F)\cong{\mathrm{Gal}}(M/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T))\cong A_{n}
. The extension
L/F
is geometric because
A_{n}
has no cyclic quotients. The extension
L/F
can only ramify over a place of
F
that lies over the place
T=\infty
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T)
(since
T=\infty
is the only ramified place of
M/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T)
). By assumption the only pole of
T
is
P
, so
P
is the only place lying over
T=\infty
and
L/F
is unramified outside of
P
.

Denote
\Gamma={\mathrm{Gal}}(L/F)\cong A_{n}
. By Proposition
2.2
and the assumption
n\geq n_{0}(G)
, there exists
H\leqslant\Gamma
such that
N_{\Gamma}(H)/H\cong G
. By Proposition
2.1
there exists a subextension
F\subset K\subset L
with
{\mathrm{Aut}}(K/F)\cong G
. It is geometric and unramified outside of
P
(since
L/F
is), so it satisfies the requirements of Theorem
3
. This completes the proof of Theorem
3
.

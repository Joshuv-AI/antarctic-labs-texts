Deciphering the Belle II data on 𝑩 → 𝑲 ​ 𝝂 ​ 𝝂 ¯ \boldsymbol{B\to K\nu\bar{\nu}} decay in the (dark) SMEFT with minimal flavour violation
Biao-Feng Hou, Xin-Qiang Li, Meng Shen, Ya-Dong Yang, and Xing-Bo Yuan

Abstract
Recently, the Belle II collaboration announced the first measurement of the branching ratio ℬ ⁡ ( B + → K + ​ ν ​ ν ¯ ) \mathcal{B}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}) , which is found to be about 2.7 ​ σ 2.7\sigma higher than the Standard Model (SM) prediction. We decipher the data with two new physics scenarios: the underlying quark-level b → s ​ ν ​ ν ¯ b\to s\nu\bar{\nu} transition is, besides the SM contribution, further affected by heavy new mediators that are much heavier than the electroweak scale, or amended by an additional decay channel with undetected light final states like dark matter or axion-like particles. These two scenarios can be most conveniently analyzed in the SM effective field theory (SMEFT) and the dark SMEFT (DSMEFT) framework, respectively. We consider the flavour structures of the resulting effective operators to be either generic or satisfy the minimal flavour violation (MFV) hypothesis, both for the quark and lepton sectors. In the first scenario, once the MFV assumption is made, only one SM-like low-energy effective operator induced by the SMEFT dimension-six operators can account for the Belle II excess, whose parameter space is, however, excluded by the Belle upper bound of the branching ratio ℬ ⁡ ( B 0 → K ∗ 0 ​ ν ​ ν ¯ ) \mathcal{B}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu}) . In the second scenario, it is found that the Belle II excess can be accommodated by 22 of the DSMEFT operators involving one or two scalar, fermionic, or vector dark matters as well as axion-like particles. These operators also receive dominant constraints from the B 0 → K ∗ 0 + inv B^{0}\to K^{*0}+\rm inv and B s → inv B_{s}\to\rm inv decays. Once the MFV hypothesis is assumed, the number of viable operators is reduced to 14, and the B + → π + + inv B^{+}\to\pi^{+}+\rm inv and K + → π + + inv K^{+}\to\pi^{+}+\rm inv decays start to put further constraints on them. Within the parameter space allowed by all the current experimental data, the q 2 q^{2} distributions of the B → K ( ∗ ) + inv B\to K^{(*)}+\rm inv decays are then studied for each viable operator. We find that the resulting prediction of the operator 𝒬 q ​ χ = ( q ¯ p ​ γ μ ​ q r ) ​ ( χ ¯ ​ γ μ ​ χ ) \mathcal{Q}_{q\chi}=\big(\bar{q}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}q_{r}\big)\big(\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu}\chi\big) with a fermionic dark matter mass m χ ≈ 700 ​ MeV m_{\chi}\approx 700\,\rm MeV can closely match the Belle II event distribution in the bins 2 ≤ q 2 ≤ 7 ​ GeV 2 2\leq q^{2}\leq 7\,{\rm GeV}^{2} . In addition, we, for the first time, calculate systematically the longitudinal polarization fraction F L F_{L} of K ∗ K^{\ast} in the B → K ∗ + inv B\to K^{*}+\text{inv} decays within the DLEFT. By combining the decay spectra and F L F_{L} , almost all the DSMEFT operators are found to be distinguishable from each other. Finally, the future prospects at Belle II, CEPC and FCC-ee are also discussed for some of these FCNC processes.

Minimal Flavour Violation

In this section, we discuss the idea of MFV hypothesis and its application in the SMEFT and DSMEFT.

2.1
Quark sector

In the interaction eigenbasis, the SM Yukawa interactions in the quark sector are described by the Lagrangian

\displaystyle-{\mathcal{L}}_{Y}=\bar{q}\,Y_{d}Hd+\bar{q}\,Y_{u}\tilde{H}u+\mathrm{h.c.},

(2)

where
\tilde{H}\equiv i\sigma_{2}H^{*}
with
H
being the SM Higgs doublet. The fields
q
and
u,d
denote the left-handed quark doublet and the right-handed quark singlets in the SM, respectively. The Yukawa coupling matrices
Y_{u,d}
are
3\times 3
complex matrices in flavour space. In the SM, these Yukawa interactions violate the global flavour symmetry
Gerard:1982mm
;
Chivukula:1987py

\displaystyle G_{\rm QF}=SU(3)_{q}\otimes SU(3)_{u}\otimes SU(3)_{d}.

(3)

We can formally recover this flavour symmetry by promoting the Yukawa matrices
Y_{u,d}
to spurion fields
DAmbrosio:2002vsn
. The Yukawa spurions should transform under
G_{\rm QF}
as

\displaystyle Y_{u}\sim\big(\boldsymbol{3},\boldsymbol{\bar{3}},\boldsymbol{1}\big),\qquad Y_{d}\sim\big(\boldsymbol{3},\boldsymbol{1},\boldsymbol{\bar{3}}\big).

(4)

Then, we can construct two useful basic building blocks
{\mathsf{A}}\equiv Y_{u}Y_{u}^{\dagger}
and
{\mathsf{B}}\equiv Y_{d}Y_{d}^{\dagger}
, which transform as
(\boldsymbol{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{8},\boldsymbol{1},\boldsymbol{1})
under the flavour group
G_{\rm QF}
. As will be seen later, these building blocks are useful to parameterize the quark flavour transitions in effective theories.

In the SMEFT and DSMEFT, there are four types of quark currents relevant for the down-type FCNC transitions, which read

\displaystyle\bar{q}\gamma^{\mu}{\mathcal{C}}q,\qquad\bar{d}\gamma^{\mu}{\mathcal{C}}d,\qquad\bar{q}{\mathcal{C}}d,\qquad\bar{q}\sigma^{\mu\nu}{\mathcal{C}}d,

(5)

where
{\mathcal{C}}
denotes the Wilson coefficient of the operator
{\mathcal{Q}}
and is a
3\times 3
matrix in quark flavour space. Here and in the following, we suppress the operator index
i
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{C}}
denoting
{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
. In the MFV hypothesis, it is assumed that at low energy the Yukawa couplings are the only source of flavour violation in the SM and beyond
DAmbrosio:2002vsn
. Technically, this hypothesis implies that the quark currents in eq. (
5
) should be invariant under the flavour group
G_{\rm QF}
. Therefore, the Wilson coefficient
{\mathcal{C}}
can be written in the following form:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm MFV}=\begin{cases}f({\mathsf{A}},{\mathsf{B}})&\text{for \;}\bar{q}\gamma^{\mu}{\mathcal{C}}q,\\

(6)

{\mathsf{A}}
and
{\mathsf{B}}

\displaystyle+

, and make them complex generally. However, it is found that the imaginary parts of the coefficients are tiny (e.g.,

In the MFV framework discussed above, the quark currents are all given in the interaction eigenbasis. In order to rotate to the fermion mass eigenbasis, the following transforms should be performed:

\displaystyle u_{L}\to U_{u}u_{L},\qquad u_{R}\to W_{u}u_{R},\qquad d_{L}\to U_{d}d_{L},\qquad d_{R}\to W_{d}d_{R},

Therefore, the CKM matrix is given by

The current

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{L}U_{d}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\mu}{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm MFV}U_{d}d_{L}\approx\bar{d}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}V^{\dagger}\big(\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon_{1}\hat{\lambda}_{u}^{2}+\epsilon_{2}\hat{\lambda}_{u}^{4}\big)Vd_{L},

Analogous to the quark sector, this flavour symmetry can be restored by promoting the Yukawa couplings

For the three SMEFT operators,

{\mathsf{B}}_{\ell}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm MFV}\approx\kappa_{0}+\kappa_{1}{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}+\kappa_{2}{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}^{2},

(22)

where the coefficients
\kappa_{0,1,2}
are free real parameters. In the numerical analysis, we keep only the leading lepton flavour violation term
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}
for simplicity, i.e.,
\kappa_{2}=0

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}(\kappa_{0}\mathds{1}+\kappa_{0}\Delta_{\ell})e_{L}+\bar{\nu}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}(\kappa_{0}\mathds{1}+\kappa_{0}\hat{\lambda}_{\nu}^{2})\nu_{L},

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ell}=U\hat{\lambda}_{\nu}^{2}U^{\dagger},

\boldsymbol{M_{1}\to M_{2}+\nu\bar{\nu}}

In this section, we first summarize the SMEFT framework with the most general flavour structure as well as in the MFV hypothesis. Then, the NP effects on various rare FCNC decays are discussed.

3.1
SMEFT

If new particles are much heavier than the electroweak scale and the electroweak symmetry breaking is realized linearly, their effects can be parameterized by a series of higher dimensional gauge-invariant operators in the SMEFT (see refs.
Brivio:2017vri
;
Isidori:2023pyp
for recent reviews). The SMEFT Lagrangian at dim-6 takes the form
Buchmuller:1985jz
;
Grzadkowski:2010es

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm SMEFT}=\sum_{i}\frac{{\mathcal{C}}_{i}}{\Lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{i},

(27)

where
\Lambda
denotes the NP scale. The dim-6 effective operators
{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}
are built out of the SM fields and respect the SM gauge symmetry
SU(3)_{C}\otimes SU(2)_{L}\otimes U(1)_{Y}
, with
{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
the corresponding dimensionless Wilson coefficients.

For the
b\to s\nu\bar{\nu}
transition, the relevant operators are given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{lq}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{l}_{p}\gamma^{\mu}l_{r}\big)\big(\bar{q}_{s}\gamma_{\mu}q_{t}\big)\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{Hq}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\big(H^{\dagger}i\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}H\big)\big(\bar{q}_{p}\gamma^{\mu}q_{r}\big)\,,

(28)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{lq}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{l}_{p}\gamma^{\mu}\tau^{I}l_{r}\big)\big(\bar{q}_{s}\tau^{I}\gamma_{\mu}q_{t}\big)\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{Hq}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\big(H^{\dagger}i\overleftrightarrow{D}^{I}_{\mu}H\big)\big(\bar{q}_{p}\tau^{I}\gamma^{\mu}q_{r}\big),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{ld}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{l}_{p}\gamma^{\mu}l_{r}\big)\big(\bar{d}_{s}\gamma_{\mu}d_{t}\big),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{Hd}

\displaystyle=\big(H^{\dagger}i\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}H\big)\big(\bar{d}_{p}\gamma^{\mu}d_{r}\big),

where
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+ig_{s}T^{a}G^{a}_{\mu}+ig_{2}t^{I}W_{\mu}^{I}+ig_{1}YB_{\mu}
denotes the covariant derivative under the SM gauge symmetry. Here
T^{a}=\lambda^{a}/2
and
t^{I}=\tau^{I}/2
are the
SU(3)
and
SU(2)
generators, where
\lambda^{a}
and
\tau^{I}
stand for the Gell-Mann and Pauli matrices, respectively.
l
and
q
denote the left-handed lepton and quark doublets respectively, while
d
is the right-handed down-type quark singlet, all being given in the mass eigenbasis. The generation indices of the SM fields are characterized by
p
,
r
,
s
,
t
.

The operators
{\mathcal{Q}}_{Hq}^{(1)}
,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{Hq}^{(3)}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{Hd}
can induce a tree-level
\bar{s}\gamma^{\mu}bZ_{\mu}
coupling, and thus contribute to the
b\to s\nu\bar{\nu}
transition. However, the same
\bar{s}\gamma^{\mu}bZ_{\mu}
interaction also affects the
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
processes
Altmannshofer:2009ma
;
Buras:2014fpa
. Due to the
SU(2)_{L}
gauge invariance, its contributions to these two processes are of equal magnitudes and lepton flavour universal. Thus, it is impossible to explain the Belle II data on the branching ratio
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})
while satisfying the stringent constraints from the precisely measured
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
observables, e.g., the branching ratio
{\mathcal{B}}(B_{s}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
. Explicitly, it is found that
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})
can only be enhanced by about
20\%
after considering other relevant experimental constraints
Allwicher:2023xba
. Therefore, we will not consider these operators and focus on the remaining three four-fermion operators in the following.

In the MFV hypothesis, by using eqs. (
13
), (
15
) and (
23
), the relevant four-fermion operators take the following forms:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{ld}{\mathcal{Q}}_{ld}

\displaystyle=\Big[\bar{e}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}\left(\kappa_{0}\mathds{1}+\kappa_{1}\Delta_{\ell}\right)e_{L}+\bar{\nu}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}\big(\kappa_{0}\mathds{1}+\kappa_{1}\hat{\lambda}_{\nu}^{2}\big)\nu_{L}\Big]\Big[\bar{d}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}(\epsilon_{0}\mathds{1})d_{R}\Big],

(29)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{lq}^{(1,3)}{\mathcal{Q}}_{lq}^{(1,3)}

\displaystyle\supset\Big[\bar{e}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}\left(\kappa_{0}\mathds{1}+\kappa_{1}\Delta_{\ell}\right)e_{L}\pm\bar{\nu}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}\big(\kappa_{0}\mathds{1}+\kappa_{1}\hat{\lambda}_{\nu}^{2}\big)\nu_{L}\Big]\Big[\bar{d}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}\left(\epsilon_{0}\mathds{1}+\epsilon_{1}\Delta_{q}\right)d_{L}\Big],

in the mass eigenbasis. Here we have suppressed the operator indices for the MFV parameters
\epsilon_{0,1}
and
\kappa_{0,1}
are suppressed. It is noted that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{ld}
does not involve any quark FCNC interactions, and thus has no contribution to the

transition.

3.2.1

decay

The

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{L}^{\nu_{i}\nu_{j}}=\big(\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}b\big)\big(\bar{\nu}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\nu_{j}\big),\qquad{\mathcal{O}}_{R}^{\nu_{i}\nu_{j}}=\big(\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}P_{R}b\big)\big(\bar{\nu}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\nu_{j}\big).

In the SM,

\displaystyle C_{L,{\rm NP}}^{\nu_{i}\nu_{j}}=c_{\nu}\big(\big[\mathcal{C}_{lq}^{(1)}\big]_{ij23}-\big[\mathcal{C}_{lq}^{(3)}\big]_{ij23}\big),\qquad C_{R,{\rm NP}}^{\nu_{i}\nu_{j}}=c_{\nu}\big[\mathcal{C}_{ld}\big]_{ij23},

with the normalization factor

\displaystyle C_{L,{\rm MFV}}^{\nu_{i}\nu_{j}}

\displaystyle=c_{\nu}\Delta_{q}^{23}\big[\epsilon_{1}^{(1)}\big(\kappa_{0}^{(1)}+\kappa_{1}^{(1)}\lambda_{\nu_{i}}^{2}\big)-\big((1)\to(3)\big)\big]\delta_{ij},

(33)

\displaystyle C_{R,{\rm MFV}}^{\nu_{i}\nu_{j}}

Starting with the effective weak Hamiltonian in eq. (

\displaystyle A^{ij}_{\|}(s_{B})

\displaystyle=-2\sqrt{2}N\,(1+\tilde{m}_{K^{*}})\,(C^{\nu_{i}\nu_{j}}_{L}-C^{\nu_{i}\nu_{j}}_{R})\,A_{1}(s_{B}),

\displaystyle A^{ij}_{0}(s_{B})

\displaystyle=-\frac{N(C^{\nu_{i}\nu_{j}}_{L}-C^{\nu_{i}\nu_{j}}_{R})}{\widetilde{m}_{K^{*}}\sqrt{s_{B}}}\biggl[(1-\tilde{m}_{K^{*}}^{2}-s_{B})(1+\tilde{m}_{K^{*}})A_{1}(s_{B})

\displaystyle\hskip 184.9429pt-\lambda(1,\tilde{m}_{K^{*}}^{2},s_{B})\frac{A_{2}(s_{B})}{1+\tilde{m}_{K^{*}}}\biggr],

(35)

with

where
\kappa_{L}=(2.231\pm 0.013)\times 10^{-10}(\lambda/0.225)^{8}
encodes, besides some other factors, the hadronic matrix element that can be again extracted from the
K_{\ell 3}
decay with the help of isospin symmetry
Marciano:1996wy
;
Mescia:2007kn
. The parameter
\delta_{\epsilon}
encodes, on the other hand, the highly suppressed indirect CP-violating contribution
Buchalla:1998ux
.

4
\boldsymbol{M_{1}\to M_{2}+\text{DM}}

In this section, we first recapitulate the theoretical framework of the DSMEFT and DLEFT, and then detail the calculation of
M_{1}\to M_{2}+\text{DM}
decays.

4.1
DSMEFT

It is known that EFT can provide a systematic framework for parameterizing various effects induced by the DM particles. The DSMEFT is a generalization of the SMEFT
Buchmuller:1985jz
;
Grzadkowski:2010es
to include the DM particles, which can be either lighter or heavier than the electroweak scale and are gauge singlets under the SM gauge group
Aebischer:2022wnl
;
Liang:2023yta
;
Song:2023jqm
. It is also assumed that electroweak symmetry breaking must be implemented via the usual Higgs mechanism. Following the convention of ref.
Aebischer:2022wnl
, the DSMEFT Lagrangian takes the form

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{DSMEFT}}

\displaystyle\supset\frac{1}{\Lambda}\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}^{(5)}+\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}\sum_{j}{\mathcal{C}}_{j}{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}^{(6)},

(43)

where
\Lambda
is the NP scale above which new degrees of freedom appear and interact with the SM and DM particles. The dim-
d
effective operators
{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}^{(d)}
involve at least one DM field and respect the SM
SU(3)_{C}\otimes SU(2)_{L}\otimes U(1)_{Y}
gauge symmetry.
{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
denote the corresponding dimensionless Wilson coefficients. We will define the DSMEFT at the electroweak scale
\mu_{\rm EW}
.

In the DSMEFT framework, three types of DM particles can be added: spin-0 scalar
\phi
, spin-1/2 fermion
\chi
and spin-1 vector
X
. Generally, each type of the DM particles can have multiple generations. For simplicity, the case of one generation DM will be assumed in this paper. For the operators vanishing in the case of one generation, however, two generations of DM particles will be considered. Furthermore, in order to affect the rare
B
-meson and kaon FCNC decays, only the operators that can induce tree-level FCNC interactions among the down-type quarks are included in our analysis. Finally, motivated by the large excess of
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}+\nu\bar{\nu})
reported by Belle II
Belle-II:2023esi
, we will focus on the dim-5 and dim-6 operators, which are less suppressed than the ones with dim
\geq 7
. Consequently, the DSMEFT operators we are considering must involve two down-type quarks and at least one DM field. Furthermore, in order to mimic the rare
B
-meson and kaon FCNC decays involving a dineutrino final state, the DM particles must be light enough to be produced in these decays. For each type of the DM particles, the relevant effective operators are given in the following.

For the scalar DM particles, there are four operators,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{d\phi}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{q}_{p}d_{r}H\big)\phi+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{d\phi^{2}}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{q}_{p}d_{r}H\big)\phi^{2}+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{\phi q}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{q}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}q_{r}\big)\big(i\phi_{1}\overleftrightarrow{\partial^{\mu}}\phi_{2}\big),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{\phi d}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}d_{r}\big)\big(i\phi_{1}\overleftrightarrow{\partial^{\mu}}\phi_{2}\big),

(44)

where
\phi_{1}\overleftrightarrow{\partial^{\mu}}\phi_{2}=\phi_{1}({\partial^{\mu}}\phi_{2})-({\partial^{\mu}}\phi_{1})\phi_{2}
, and
\phi_{(i)}
is the real scalar DM field with the generation index characterized by
i
. For the operators which are not hermitian, “
+\mathrm{h.c.}
” should be added to them to indicate that the hermitian conjugated operators should be included in the DSMEFT Lagrangian eq. (
43
). In addition, constructions of the operators
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\phi q}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\phi d}
requires at least two generations of scalar DM fields.

For the fermionic DM particles, there are only two operators,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{q\chi}=\big(\bar{q}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}q_{r}\big)(\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu}\chi\big),\qquad\qquad{\mathcal{Q}}_{d\chi}=\big(\bar{d}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}d_{r}\big)(\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu}\chi\big),

(45)

where
\chi
denotes the fermionic DM field. In this paper, all the observables considered are insensitive to the chirality of the fermionic DM. Without loss of generality,
\chi
will be therefore chosen as the right-handed Dirac field.

For the vector DM particles, by using the field strength tensor as the building block, there is only one operator,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{dHX}=\big(\bar{q}_{p}\sigma_{\mu\nu}d_{r}\big)HX^{\mu\nu}+\mathrm{h.c.},

(46)

where
X_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}X_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}X_{\mu}
, and
X_{\mu}
denotes the real vector DM field. Taking
X_{\mu}
an additional building block, the following operators also appear
Song:2023jqm
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{dX}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}d_{r}\big)X^{\mu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{HdX}

\displaystyle=\big(H^{\dagger}H\big)\big(\bar{d}_{p}\gamma^{\mu}d_{r}\big)X_{\mu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{qX}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{q}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}q_{r}\big)X^{\mu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{HqX}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\big(H^{\dagger}H\big)\big(\bar{q}_{p}\gamma^{\mu}q_{r}\big)X_{\mu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{dX^{2}}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{q}_{p}d_{r}H\big)X_{\mu}X^{\mu}+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{HqX}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\big(H^{\dagger}\tau^{I}H\big)\big(\bar{q}_{p}\tau^{I}\gamma^{\mu}q_{r}\big)X_{\mu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{qXX}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{q}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}q_{r}\big)X^{\mu\nu}X_{\nu}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{dXX}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}d_{r}\big)X^{\mu\nu}X_{\nu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{q\tilde{X}X}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{q}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}q_{r}\big)\tilde{X}^{\mu\nu}X_{\nu}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{d\tilde{X}X}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}d_{r}\big)\tilde{X}^{\mu\nu}X_{\nu}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{DqX^{2}}

\displaystyle=i\big(\bar{q}_{p}\gamma^{\mu}D^{\nu}q_{r}\big)X_{\mu}X_{\nu}+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{DdX^{2}}

\displaystyle=i\big(\bar{d}_{p}\gamma^{\mu}D^{\nu}d_{r}\big)X_{\mu}X_{\nu}+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{dHX^{2}}

\displaystyle=(\bar{q}_{p}\sigma_{\mu\nu}d_{r}H)X_{1}^{\mu}X_{2}^{\nu}+\mathrm{h.c.},

(47)

where
\tilde{X}^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}X_{\rho\sigma}/2
. It is noted that the rare
B
-meson and kaon decay rates induced by the operators involving the vector DM field
X_{\mu}
are divergent in the
m_{X}\to 0
limit. Such a divergence is caused by the longitudinal polarization of the vector field and related to the phenomenon that it is impossible to consistently define a massless limit for a vector field without an active gauge symmetry
Kamenik:2011vy
. As in refs.
Kamenik:2011vy
;
Williams:2011qb
;
He:2022ljo
, this singularity is treated by assuming some kind of Higgs mechanism in the dark sector. As a result, the Wilson coefficients of the operators in eq. (
4.1
) should be proportional to some powers of
(m_{X}/\Lambda)
and can be redefined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{i}=\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\cdot\begin{cases}(m_{X}/\Lambda)^{2}&\text{ for }{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}={\mathcal{Q}}_{dX^{2}},{\mathcal{Q}}_{DdX^{2}},{\mathcal{Q}}_{DqX^{2}},{\mathcal{Q}}_{dHX^{2}},\\
(m_{X}/\Lambda)&\text{ for }{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}=\text{others}.\end{cases}

(48)

These redefined Wilson coefficients
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
will be used in the following analysis.

We can also include the axion or axion-like particles (ALPs)
Peccei:1977hh
;
Peccei:1977ur
;
Weinberg:1977ma
;
Wilczek:1977pj
in the EFT approach
Georgi:1986df
;
Brivio:2017ije
;
Chala:2020wvs
;
Bauer:2020jbp
;
Galda:2021hbr
;
Song:2023lxf
. They are pseudo Nambu-Goldstone bosons with non-trivial properties, and can emerge from spontaneous breaking of some global symmetries. Up to dim-5, there are only two operators,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{qa}=\big(\bar{q}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}q_{r}\big)\partial^{\mu}a,\qquad\qquad{\mathcal{Q}}_{da}=\big(\bar{d}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}d_{r}\big)\partial^{\mu}a,

(49)

where
a
denotes the ALP field.

In the MFV hypothesis discussed in section
2
, the Wilson coefficients of the DSMEFT operators should take special flavour structures. Explicitly, by using the quark currents in eqs. (
13
)-(
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm MFV}_{i}=\begin{cases}\epsilon_{0}^{i}\hat{\lambda}_{d}+\epsilon_{1}^{i}\Delta_{q}\hat{\lambda}_{d}&\text{for }{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}={\mathcal{Q}}_{d\phi},{\mathcal{Q}}_{d\phi^{2}},{\mathcal{Q}}_{dHX},{\mathcal{Q}}_{dHX^{2}},{\mathcal{Q}}_{dX^{2}},\\
\epsilon_{0}^{i}\mathds{1}+\epsilon_{1}^{i}\Delta_{q}&\text{for }{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\phi q},{\mathcal{Q}}_{q\chi},{\mathcal{Q}}_{qXX},{\mathcal{Q}}_{q\tilde{X}X},{\mathcal{Q}}_{DqX^{2}},{\mathcal{Q}}_{qX},{\mathcal{Q}}_{HqX}^{(1,3)},{\mathcal{Q}}_{qa},\\
\epsilon_{0}^{i}\mathds{1}&\text{for }{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\phi d},{\mathcal{Q}}_{d\chi},{\mathcal{Q}}_{dXX},{\mathcal{Q}}_{d\tilde{X}X},{\mathcal{Q}}_{DdX^{2}},{\mathcal{Q}}_{dX},{\mathcal{Q}}_{HdX},{\mathcal{Q}}_{da},\end{cases}

(50)

for the DSMEFT operators discussed above. Here the Wilson coefficient
{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm MFV}_{i}
are given for the down-type quark currents expressed in the mass eigenbasis, and they are
3\times 3
matrices in flavour space. Generally, the real parameters
\epsilon_{0,1}^{i}
are different from each other for different operators
{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}
. We can see that the operators listed in the last line of the above equation do not generate tree-level FCNC interactions among the down-type quarks.

4.2
DLEFT

Below the electroweak scale, the NP effects induced by the DM particles can be described by the DLEFT
Aebischer:2022wnl
. This EFT is obtained by integrating out all the heavy particles with masses at or above the electroweak scale and can be considered as an extension of the LEFT
Buchalla:1995vs
;
Jenkins:2017jig
by including besides the light SM fields also the light DM particles. The DLEFT respects the SM
SU(3)_{C}\otimes U(1)_{\rm em}
gauge symmetry, and does not contain the Higgs boson. Thus, it can be considered without reference to the DSMEFT for the light DM and light SM particles interacting at energies below the electroweak scale. Following the same convention as in ref.
Aebischer:2022wnl
, we can write the DLEFT Lagrangian as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{DLEFT}}\supset\frac{1}{\Lambda}\sum_{i}L_{i}{\mathcal{O}}_{i}^{(5)}+\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}\sum_{j}L_{j}{\mathcal{O}}_{j}^{(6)},

(51)

up to dim-6, where
{\mathcal{O}}_{i}^{(d)}
denote the dim-
d
effective operators and
L_{i}
the corresponding Wilson coefficients.

Analogous to the DSMEFT case, we can write down all the relevant DLEFT operators involving the scalar, fermionic and vector DM fields. For the scalar DM, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{d\phi}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{Lp}d_{Rr})\phi+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{\phi d}^{L}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{Lp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Lr})\big(i\phi_{1}\overleftrightarrow{\partial^{\mu}}\phi_{2}\big),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{d\phi^{2}}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{Lp}d_{Rr})\phi^{2}+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{\phi d}^{R}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{Rp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Rr})\big(i\phi_{1}\overleftrightarrow{\partial^{\mu}}\phi_{2}\big),

(52)

where
d_{L}
and
d_{R}
denote the left-handed and right-handed down-type quarks, respectively. For the fermionic DM, the relevant operators are given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{d\chi}^{V,\,LR}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{Lp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Lr})(\bar{\chi}_{a}\gamma^{\mu}\chi_{b}),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{d\chi}^{V,RR}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{Rp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Rr})(\bar{\chi}_{a}\gamma^{\mu}\chi_{b}),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{d\chi}^{S,LR}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{Rp}d_{Lr})(\chi_{a}^{T}C\chi_{b})+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{d\chi}^{S,RR}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{Lp}d_{Rr})(\chi_{a}^{T}C\chi_{b})+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{d\chi}^{T,RR}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{Lp}\sigma^{\mu\nu}d_{Rr})(\chi_{a}^{T}C\sigma_{\mu\nu}\chi_{b})+\mathrm{h.c.},

(53)

with the charge conjugate operator defined by
C=i\gamma^{2}\gamma^{0}
. For the vector DM, there is only one operator,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{dX}^{T}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{Lp}\sigma_{\mu\nu}d_{Rr})X_{a}^{\mu\nu}+\mathrm{h.c.},

(54)

by using the field strength
X_{\mu\nu}
as the building block. However, including
X_{\mu}
as additional building block, the following operators are obtained:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{dX}^{L}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{Lp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Lr}\big)X^{\mu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{dX}^{R}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{Rp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Rr}\big)X^{\mu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{dXX}^{L}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{Lp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Lr}\big)X^{\mu\nu}X_{\nu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{dXX}^{R}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{Rp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Rr}\big)X^{\mu\nu}X_{\nu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{d\tilde{X}X}^{L}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{Lp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Lr}\big)\tilde{X}^{\mu\nu}X_{\nu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{d\tilde{X}X}^{R}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{Rp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Rr}\big)\tilde{X}^{\mu\nu}X_{\nu},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{DdX^{2}}^{L}

\displaystyle=i\big(\bar{d}_{Lp}\gamma^{\nu}D^{\mu}d_{Lr}\big)X_{\mu}X_{\nu}+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{DdX^{2}}^{R}

\displaystyle=i\big(\bar{d}_{Rp}\gamma^{\nu}D^{\mu}d_{Rr}\big)X_{\mu}X_{\nu}+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{dX^{2}}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{Lp}d_{Rr}\big)X_{\mu}X^{\mu}+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{dX^{2}}^{T}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{d}_{Lp}\sigma_{\mu\nu}d_{Rr}\big)X_{1}^{\mu}X_{2}^{\nu}+\mathrm{h.c.},

(55)

where
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+ig_{s}T^{a}G_{\mu}^{a}+ieQA_{\mu}
denotes the covariant derivative of the
SU(3)_{C}\otimes U(1)_{\rm em}
gauge symmetry. Here we have chosen the basis where the combinations of
X_{\mu\nu}
and
X_{\mu}
are similar as in the DSMEFT operators of eq. (
4.1
). One can prove that this basis is equivalent to the one adopted in ref.
He:2022ljo
. In addition, there are two operators involving the ALP field, which take the form

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{da}^{L}=\big(\bar{d}_{Lp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Lr}\big)\partial^{\mu}a,\qquad\qquad{\mathcal{O}}_{da}^{R}=\big(\bar{d}_{Rp}\gamma_{\mu}d_{Rr}\big)\partial^{\mu}a.

(56)

Although the DLEFT can be considered without reference to the DSMEFT, the former should arise as the low-energy limit of the latter, if we start from the DSMEFT. At the electroweak scale
\mu_{\rm EW}
, the tree-level matching conditions between these two EFTs are summarized below:

•

Scalar DM:

\displaystyle L_{\phi d}^{L}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{\phi q},

\displaystyle L_{\phi d}^{R}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{\phi d},

\displaystyle L_{d\phi^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda}{\mathcal{C}}_{d\phi^{2}},

\displaystyle L_{d\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda}{\mathcal{C}}_{d\phi}.

(57)

•

Fermionic DM:

\displaystyle L_{d\chi}^{V,\,LR}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{q\chi},

\displaystyle L_{d\chi}^{V,\,RR}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{d\chi},

\displaystyle L_{d\chi}^{S,\,LR}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle L_{d\chi}^{S,\,RR}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle L_{d\chi}^{T,\,RR}

\displaystyle=0.

(58)

•

Vector DM:

\displaystyle L_{dX}^{T}=\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda}{\mathcal{C}}_{dHX},L_{dX}^{L}={\mathcal{C}}_{qX}+\frac{v^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\big({\mathcal{C}}_{HqX}^{(1)}+{\mathcal{C}}_{HqX}^{(3)}\big),L_{dX}^{R}={\mathcal{C}}_{dX}+\frac{v^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{C}}_{HdX},

\displaystyle L_{dXX}^{L}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{qXX},

\displaystyle L_{d\tilde{X}X}^{L}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{q\tilde{X}X},

\displaystyle L_{DdX^{2}}^{L}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{DqX^{2}},

\displaystyle L_{dX^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda}{\mathcal{C}}_{dX^{2}},

\displaystyle L_{dXX}^{R}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{dXX},

\displaystyle L_{d\tilde{X}X}^{R}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{d\tilde{X}X},

\displaystyle L_{DdX^{2}}^{R}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{DdX^{2}},

\displaystyle L_{dX^{2}}^{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda}{\mathcal{C}}_{dHX^{2}}.

(59)

•

ALP:

\displaystyle L_{da}^{L}={\mathcal{C}}_{qa},\qquad\qquad L_{da}^{R}={\mathcal{C}}_{da}.

(60)

Here all the Wilson coefficients are given in the mass eigenbasis. In the MFV hypothesis, the Wilson coefficients in DSMEFT take the form of eq. (
50
).

Since the above matching conditions are given at the electroweak scale, i.e.,
L_{i}\equiv L_{i}(\mu_{\rm EW})
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\equiv{\mathcal{C}}_{i}(\mu_{\rm EW})
, the renormalization group (RG) evolution should be performed in the DLEFT to obtain the corresponding Wilson coefficients at the typical scales of
B
-meson and kaon decays. In the DLEFT, as the DM particles are color singlet, we need only consider the QCD RG evolution of the quark current in each DLEFT operator. Furthermore, the different DLEFT operators do not mix under the QCD RG running. Accordingly, the RG equations take the form

\displaystyle\frac{dL_{i}(\mu)}{d\ln\mu}=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\gamma_{i}\,L_{i}(\mu),

(61)

where
L_{i}(\mu)
denote the Wilson coefficients of the operators
{\mathcal{O}}_{i}
at the scale
\mu
.
\gamma_{i}
denote the anomalous dimension matrices (ADM) for the operators
{\mathcal{O}}_{i}
. They can be obtained from the ADM of the semi-leptonic operators in the LEFT
Buchalla:1995vs
;
Jenkins:2017dyc
. For the DLEFT operators with a non-zero matching condition, the values of these ADM are given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}=\begin{cases}-6C_{F}&\text{for }{\mathcal{O}}_{i}={\mathcal{O}}_{d\phi},{\mathcal{O}}_{d\phi^{2}},{\mathcal{O}}_{dX^{2}},\\
+2C_{F}&\text{for }{\mathcal{O}}_{i}={\mathcal{O}}_{dX}^{T},{\mathcal{O}}_{dX^{2}}^{T},\\
0&\text{for }{\mathcal{O}}_{i}={\mathcal{O}}_{\phi d}^{L,R},{\mathcal{O}}_{d\chi}^{V,LR},{\mathcal{O}}_{d\chi}^{V,RR},{\mathcal{O}}_{dX}^{L,R},{\mathcal{O}}_{dXX}^{L,R},\,{\mathcal{O}}_{d\tilde{X}X}^{L,R},{\mathcal{O}}_{DdX^{2}}^{L,R},\end{cases}

(62)

with
C_{F}=(N_{c}^{2}-1)/(2N_{c})
and
N_{c}=3
. Numerically, we find

\displaystyle L_{i}(4.8{\,\rm GeV})

\displaystyle=L_{i}(m_{Z})\cdot\begin{cases}1.37&\text{for }{\mathcal{O}}_{i}={\mathcal{O}}_{d\phi},{\mathcal{O}}_{d\phi^{2}},{\mathcal{O}}_{dX^{2}},\\
0.90&\text{for }{\mathcal{O}}_{i}={\mathcal{O}}_{dX}^{T},{\mathcal{O}}_{dX^{2}}^{T},\end{cases}

(63)

\displaystyle L_{i}(2.0{\,\rm GeV})

\displaystyle=L_{i}(m_{Z})\cdot\begin{cases}1.59&\text{for }{\mathcal{O}}_{i}={\mathcal{O}}_{d\phi},{\mathcal{O}}_{d\phi^{2}},{\mathcal{O}}_{dX^{2}},\\
0.86&\text{for }{\mathcal{O}}_{i}={\mathcal{O}}_{dX}^{T},{\mathcal{O}}_{dX^{2}}^{T},\end{cases}

(64)

which can be used in the
B
-meson and kaon decays, respectively.

5.1
\boldsymbol{M_{1}\to M_{2}+\nu\bar{\nu}}

The recent Belle II measurement of the
B^{+}\to K^{+}+\text{inv}
decay could be explained by NP contributions to the
b\to s\nu\bar{\nu}
transition. In this case, the theoretical prediction of the related
M_{1}\to M_{2}+\text{inv}
processes takes the form

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm th}(M_{1}\to M_{2}+\text{inv})=\sum_{i,j=e,\mu,\tau}{\mathcal{B}}(M_{1}\to M_{2}\nu_{i}\bar{\nu}_{j}).

(65)

The NP contributions to these processes can be generally described in the framework of SMEFT. We refer to refs.
Calibbi:2015kma
;
Allwicher:2023xba
;
Bause:2023mfe
;
Chen:2024jlj
for the recent relevant studies. In the following, we also focus on the SMEFT but with the MFV hypothesis.

In the SMEFT, as mentioned in section
3.2.1
, one interesting implication of the MFV hypothesis is that the
d_{i}\to d_{j}\nu\bar{\nu}
transitions are all governed by only one effective operator at low energy. As a consequence, the ratio of the branching fractions of any two
b\to s\nu\bar{\nu}
decays should be independent of the NP contribution. Considering as an example the
B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
and
B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu}
decays, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})}{{\mathcal{B}}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu})}=\frac{{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm SM}}{{\mathcal{B}}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm SM}}=0.46\pm 0.07,

(66)

where summation over the neutrino flavours has been taken into account. In this ratio, all the relevant SMEFT operators, i.e.
{\mathcal{Q}}_{lq}^{(1)}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{lq}^{(3)}
, have been included simultaneously.
4
4

4

The ratio still holds after including the operators
{\mathcal{Q}}_{Hq}^{(1,3)}
, although they are not considered in our analysis as mentioned in section
3.1
.
When deriving this ratio, it is noted that only the MFV hypothesis in the quark sector is required to eliminate the right-handed operator
{\mathcal{O}}_{R}
. Therefore, the ratio still holds even by assuming generic couplings in the lepton sector, i.e., without the leptonic MFV. In addition, as both the CKM and Wilson coefficients involved are cancelled in this ratio, the theoretical uncertainty is reduced compared to that of the individual branching ratios. If future experimental measurements find any deviation from the theoretical value, it could imply new sources of flavour violation beyond the Yukawa couplings. By using the above ratio, the Belle
90\%
CL bound
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu})<18\times 10^{-6}
would imply that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm MFV}<10.5\times 10^{-6},

(67)

which is obviously lower that the
90\%
CL lower bound of the Belle II measurement,
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})>12.5\times 10^{-6}
. Or inversely, the Belle II measurement
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})=(23_{-6}^{+7})\times 10^{-6}
would indicate that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm MFV}=\big(50_{-16}^{+17}\big)\times 10^{-6},

(68)

which is incompatible with the Belle
90\%
CL bound
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu})<18\times 10^{-6}
. As a conclusion, the recent Belle II measurement of the
B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
decay cannot be explained in the SMEFT with MFV hypothesis. However, the MFV prediction of
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu})
is well within the reach of Belle II at
5{\,\rm ab}^{-1}
(cf. table
2
), which can be used as a further test of the MFV hypothesis considered here.

Similarly, the ratio of the branching fractions of a
b\to s\nu\bar{\nu}
and a
b\to d\nu\bar{\nu}
decay is also independent of the NP contribution in the SMEFT with MFV hypothesis. As an illustration, let us consider the
B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
and
B^{+}\to\pi^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
decays, and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})}{{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to\pi^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})}=\frac{{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm SM}}{{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to\pi^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm SM}}=29.7\pm 5.6,

(69)

where summation over the neutrino flavours has been included. Taking as input the Belle II measurement in eq. (
1
), this ratio implies that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to\pi^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm MFV}=\big(7.8_{-2.6}^{+2.8}\big)\times 10^{-7},

(70)

which shows a
2.3\sigma
deviation from the SM prediction
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to\pi^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm SM}=(1.40\pm 0.18)\times 10^{-7}
, but is still compatible with the current
90\%
CL experimental bound
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to\pi^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})<140\times 10^{-7}
. Of course, similar ratios can also be constructed for other processes to test the framework used here.

In the MFV framework, all the quark FCNC or the LFV processes are related with each other. Explicitly, for the operators
{\mathcal{Q}}_{lq}^{(i)}
(
i=1,3
), all the quark FCNC transitions are governed by the two parameter products
\epsilon_{1}^{(i)}\kappa_{0}^{(i)}
and
\epsilon_{1}^{(i)}\kappa_{1}^{(i)}
. In order to constrain them, we consider several quark (and lepton) FCNC processes, including the
B\to K^{(*)}\nu\bar{\nu}
,
B\to K^{(*)}e\mu
,
B\to\pi e\mu
,
B_{s}\to e\mu
,
B_{s}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
as well as
K\to\pi\nu\bar{\nu}
decays. Besides the input parameters listed in table
1
, the lepton number breaking scale
\Lambda_{\rm LN}=10^{14}{\,\rm GeV}
and the lightest neutrino mass
m_{\nu_{1}}=0.2\,\text{eV}
are used in the leptonic MFV. Numerically, we find that the dominant constraints come from the
B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
,
B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu}
,
B^{0}\to K^{*0}e^{-}\mu^{+}
and
B_{s}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays. The allowed parameter regions of
\epsilon_{1}^{(i)}\kappa_{0}^{(i)}
and
\epsilon_{1}^{(i)}\kappa_{1}^{(i)}
are shown in figure
1
. We can see that there is a small gap between the allowed regions of the
B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
and
B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu}
decays. This reflects the discrepancy between the MFV prediction in eq. (
68
) and the current Belle bound on
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu})
.

5.2
\boldsymbol{M_{1}\to M_{2}+\text{DM}}

The large excess of
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})
reported by Belle II can also be explained by light NP particles that contribute to the signal as
B^{+}\to K^{+}+\text{missing energy}
. In this case, the theoretical predictions of the
M_{1}\to M_{2}+\text{inv}
decay can be written as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm th}(M_{1}\to M_{2}+\text{inv})={\mathcal{B}}_{\rm SM}(M_{1}\to M_{2}+\nu\bar{\nu})+{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm NP}(M_{1}\to M_{2}+\text{DM}).

(72)

As discussed in section
4
, the effects of light NP particles in the second term can be generally described in the EFT framework. In the following, we investigate this explanation in the DSMEFT framework, in which the Wilson coefficients are taken to be the most general form or satisfy the MFV hypothesis.

5.2.1
General flavour structure

In the DSMEFT with general flavour structure, only the
b\to s+\text{inv}
processes are directly related to the
B^{+}\to K^{+}+\text{inv}
decays, which include
B^{0}\to K^{0}+\text{inv}
,
B^{0,+}\to K^{*\,0,+}+\text{inv}
,
B_{s}\to\phi+\text{inv}
and
B_{s}\to\text{inv}
decays. In the following, we investigate constraints on the DSMEFT operators from the experimental data on these decays as summarized in table
2
, and discuss future prospects at Belle II, CEPC and FCC-ee. As discussed in section
4.1
, seven DSMEFT operators are not hermitian. In order to compare with the MFV analysis in the next subsection, contributions from the hermitian conjugation of these operators are neglected in the numerical analysis in this subsection, i.e.,
{\mathcal{C}}_{ji}=0
is taken for
d_{i}\to d_{j}
processes.
5
5

5

Let us consider
{\mathcal{Q}}_{d\phi^{2}}
as an example. For the
b\to s\phi\phi
process,
[{\mathcal{C}}_{d\phi^{2}}]_{23}\neq 0
and
[{\mathcal{C}}_{d\phi^{2}}]_{32}=0
are taken in the general DSMEFT analysis. In the MFV hypothesis, the coupling matrix eq. (
18
) implies that
[{\mathcal{C}}_{d\phi^{2}}]_{23}\gg[{\mathcal{C}}_{d\phi^{2}}]_{32}
, which results in the approximation
[{\mathcal{C}}_{d\phi^{2}}]_{32}\approx 0
.
For example, contributions to the
b\to s\phi\phi
processes from the operator
{\mathcal{Q}}_{d\phi^{2}}
are calculated by taking
[{\mathcal{C}}_{d\phi^{2}}]_{23}\neq 0
and
[{\mathcal{C}}_{d\phi^{2}}]_{32}=0
. Two exceptions are the operators
{\mathcal{Q}}_{DqX^{2}}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{DdX^{2}}
. Consider the former as an example, we take
[{\mathcal{C}}_{DqX^{2}}]_{ji}=[{\mathcal{C}}_{DqX^{2}}]_{ij}^{*}
for the
d_{i}\to d_{j}
processes to compare with the MFV analysis, where its MFV coupling matrix in eq. (
17

{\mathcal{C}}_{DqX^{2}}

A.2
.

6
Conclusion

The recent Belle II measurement of
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})
is about
2.7\sigma
higher than the SM prediction. In this work, we have deciphered the data with two different NP scenarios: the underlying quark-level
b\to s\nu\bar{\nu}
transition is, besides the SM contribution, further affected by some heavy new mediators that are much heavier than the electroweak scale, or amended by an additional decay channel with light new final states that are sufficiently long-lived to escape the detector and appear as missing-energy signals. To make our analyses model-independent as much as possible, we have studied these two scenarios in the SMEFT and the DSMEFT framework, respectively. Furthermore, the flavour structures of the resulting effective operators are taken to be either of the most generic form or satisfy the MFV hypothesis, both for the quark and lepton sectors.

In the first scenario, we have focused on the implications of the MFV hypothesis. It is found that, once the MFV hypothesis is assumed for the quark sector while without any assumption on the lepton sector, only one SM-like low-energy effective operator
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}^{\nu_{i}\nu_{j}}
can be induced by the SMEFT dim-6 operators. As a consequence, the ratio of the branching fractions of any two
b\to s\nu\bar{\nu}
decays remains a constant, which can be used to test the MFV hypothesis. In particular, the resulting ratio
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm MFV}/{\mathcal{B}}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm MFV}=0.46\pm 0.07
is inconsistent with the current
90\%
CL lower limit
0.70
derived by combining the recent Belle II measurement of
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})
and the Belle upper bound on
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}\nu\bar{\nu})
. Thus, the Belle II excess cannot be explained in the SMEFT with MFV hypothesis for the quark sector. Such a conclusion is true irrespective of the flavour structure of the lepton sector. As the uncertainty of the Belle II measurement of
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})
is still quite large, we have also used all the relevant processes but without the
B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
decay to constrain the parameter space of the MFV parameters, and then predicted that
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm MFV}<10.5\times 10^{-6}
and
3.5<{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm MFV}<5.0\times 10^{-6}
, which correspond to the cases without and with the leptonic MFV, respectively. These predictions can be further tested at the Belle II with more statistics.

In the second scenario, it is found that the Belle II excess can be accommodated by 22 DSMEFT operators with dim
\leq 6
, which include 4 operators involving scalar, 2 operators involving fermionic, 14 operators involving vector DM, as well as 2 operators involving ALP. For each operator, the current experimental constraints on the corresponding Wilson coefficient and DM (ALP) mass are derived. For most of these operators, large parts of the parameter spaces required to account for the
B^{+}\to K^{+}+\text{inv}
excess are excluded by the
B^{0}\to K^{*0}+\text{inv}
and
B_{s}\to\text{inv}
decays. Nevertheless, none of the operators is fully excluded by the current data. The future Belle II (
5{\,\rm ab}^{-1}
), CEPC and FCC-ee (Tera-
Z
phase) data on
B_{s}\to\phi+\text{inv}
and
B_{s}\to\text{inv}
decays are, however, expected to cover almost all the parameter spaces needed for explaining the Belle II excess. Furthermore, the
B^{0}\to K^{0}+\text{inv}
decay can provide an independent probe of the NP mechanism proposed for the Belle II excess. Once the MFV hypothesis is assumed, 8 out of the 22 viable DSMEFT operators do not induce the down-type FCNC interactions, and hence cannot account for the Belle II excess. For the remaining 14 operators, their MFV couplings make the
b\to d
and
s\to d
processes also relevant. For
{\mathcal{Q}}_{d\phi}
,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{qa}
,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{qX}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{HqX}^{(1,3)}
, which all involve one DM field, the
K^{+}\to\pi^{+}+\text{inv}
decay provides the strongest constraint in the low-mass region
m_{\rm DM}\lesssim m_{K}-m_{\pi}
. For
{\mathcal{Q}}_{q\chi}
,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{dHX}
,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{qX}
,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{HqX}^{(1,3)}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{dHX^{2}}
, especially for the ones (e.g.,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{dHX^{2}}
) that make no contribution to the
B_{s}\to\text{inv}
decay, the
B^{+}\to\pi^{+}+\text{inv}
decay puts the most stringent bound in the high-mass region. In addition, the
\Upsilon(1S)\to\text{inv}
decay provides the strongest constraint in the high-mass region for the operator
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\phi q}
with
m_{\phi_{2}}-m_{\phi_{1}}<200{\,\rm MeV}
. Within the parameter space allowed by all the current experimental data, the differential branching ratios (as well as the longitudinal polarization fractions) of the
B\to K^{(*)}+\text{inv}
decays are then studied for each viable operator. We find that the resulting prediction of the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{q\chi}=\big(\bar{q}_{p}\gamma_{\mu}q_{r}\big)\big(\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu}\chi\big)
with a fermionic dark matter mass
m_{\chi}\approx 700{\,\rm MeV}
can closely match the Belle II event distribution in the bins
2\leq q^{2}\leq 7{\,\rm GeV}^{2}
. Our numerical results also show that the longitudinal polarization fractions are complementary to the branching ratios. By combining them, all the DSMEFT operators are distinguishable from each other, except the operators
{\mathcal{Q}}_{qXX}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{q\tilde{X}X}
in the case of
m_{X}=700{\,\rm MeV}
.

At the Belle II with
5{\,\rm ab}^{-1}
dataset, the branching ratio
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{+}\to K^{+}+\text{inv})
is expected to be measured with an uncertainty of
19\%
. If the central value of the current Belle II measurement remains unchanged but the uncertainty is reduced accordingly in the future, the discrepancy from the SM prediction will increase to
4\sigma
level. At the same time, in the future, the
B^{0}\to K^{0}+\text{inv}
,
B^{0}\to K^{*0}+\text{inv}
,
B^{+}\to\pi^{+}+\text{inv}
,
B^{+}\to\rho^{+}+\text{inv}
and
B_{s}\to\text{inv}
decays at Belle II
Belle-II:2018jsg
;
Belle-II:2022cgf
, the
B_{s}\to\phi+\text{inv}
at CEPC
Li:2022tov
and FCC-ee
Amhis:2023mpj
as well as the
K\to\pi+\text{inv}
at NA62
NA62:2017rwk
and KOTO
Yamanaka:2012yma
, are all expected to provide complementary information about the (D)SMEFT operators as well as the validity of the MFV hypothesis studied here.

The new singlets introduced in the DSMEFT could be the DM candidates in the universe. In this case, the parameter space derived in our work could be further constrained by cosmological observations, such as the DM relic density and the Big Bang nucleosynthesis, as well as the DM direct detection
Nollett:2013pwa
;
Cheung:2012gi
;
Balazs:2014rsa
;
Liem:2016xpm
;
GAMBIT:2021rlp
. For the operators involving one new particle, their couplings should also suffer from additional constraints to ensure that the particle is sufficiently long-lived to escape the detector. In addition, the DM FCNC couplings can affect the neutral-meson mixings, such as
B_{s}-\bar{B}_{s}
mixing
MartinCamalich:2020dfe
;
Bauer:2021mvw
. At the LHC, the large couplings of the new singlets to the third generation in MFV can lead to the DM production associated with top and bottom quarks
Lin:2013sca
. However, a complete and dedicated study of all these constraints should go beyond the EFT framework and specific UV completions are needed. Therefore, we leave these explorations for our future work.

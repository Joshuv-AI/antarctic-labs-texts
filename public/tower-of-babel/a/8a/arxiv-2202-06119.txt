Remarks on the L p L^{p} convergence of Bessel–Fourier series on the disc
Ryan L. Acosta Babb

Abstract
The L p L^{p} convergence of eigenfunction expansions for the Laplacian on planar domains is largely unknown for p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 . After discussing the classical Fourier series on the 2-torus, we move onto the disc, whose eigenfunctions are explicitly computable as products of trigonometric and Bessel functions. We summarise a result of Balodis and Córdoba 1999 regarding the L p L^{p} convergence of the Bessel–Fourier series in the mixed norm space L rad p ​ ( L ang 2 ) L^{p}_{\mathrm{rad}}(L^{2}_{\mathrm{ang}}) on the disk for the range 4 3 < p < 4 \tfrac{4}{3}<p<4 . We then describe how to modify their result to obtain L p ​ ( 𝔻 , r ​ d ​ r ​ d ​ t ) L^{p}(\mathbb{D},r\,\mathrm{d}{r}\,\mathrm{d}{t}) norm convergence in the subspace L rad p ​ ( L ang q ) L^{p}_{\mathrm{rad}}(L^{q}_{\mathrm{ang}}) ( 1 p + 1 q = 1 \tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{q}=1 ) for the restricted range 2 ⩽ p < 4 {2\leqslant p<4} .



1
Introduction

For a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, we can truncate its Fourier series by “spherical modes”

S_{N}f:=\sum_{\left|k\right|\leqslant N}\widehat{f}(k)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}k\cdot x},

(1)

or by “cubic modes”

S_{[N]}f:=\sum_{\left|k_{j}\right|\leqslant N}\widehat{f}(k)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}k\cdot x},

(2)

where

k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|k\right|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|k_{j}\right|^{2}.

It is well known that
S_{N}f
from
Eq.

1
fails in general to converge to
f
when
p\neq 2
.
This follows, by standard transference arguments
(
Grafakos 2014a
, see)
,
from Fefferman’s (
Fefferman 1971
) result that the
indicator function of the ball is not an
L^{p}
-bounded Fourier multiplier for any
p\neq 2
.

(See
Grafakos 2014b
, for a detailed discussion and related results.)

On the other hand, the square truncations from
Eq.

2
are perfectly well-behaved
for all
1<p<\infty

(
Grafakos 2014a
, see again)
.

Recently,
Fefferman et al. 2021
have asked whether,
given a differential operator with an orthonormal family
w_{k}
of eigenfunctions,
there is a choice
\{w\in E_{N}:N\in\mathbb{N}\}
of eigenfunctions such that the “truncations”

S_{E_{N}}f=\sum_{w\in E_{N}}\left\langle{f,w}\right\rangle w

are “well-behaved” in
L^{p}
for all
1<p<\infty
.

That this is not possible in general can be shown by considering the disc
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
The eigenfunctions for the Laplacian on
\mathbb{D}
are of the form

\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta m}J_{m}(j_{m}^{n}r)\quad\text{for}\quad(r,\theta)\in[0,1]^{2},(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}

corresponding to the respective eigenvalues
4\pi^{2}m^{2}+(j_{n}^{m})^{2}
.
Here
J_{m}:=J_{\left|m\right|}
denotes a Bessel function of the first kind and
j_{n}^{m}:=j_{n}^{\left|m\right|}
its non-negative zeros

(
Watson 1995
, see)
.

Consider the function
f(r)=r^{-3/2}
, which lies in the space
{L^{p}([0,1],r\,\mathrm{d}{r})}
for
1\leqslant p<4/3
.

Wing 1950
proved that, for any choice of
J_{m}
, the 1-dimensional Bessel series of
f
fails to converge in
L^{p}([0,1],r\,\mathrm{d}{r})
.
By letting
g(r,t):=f(r)
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, it follows that the 2-dimensional Bessel–Fourier series of
g
is

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{m,n}J_{m}(j_{n}^{m}r)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}mt}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n}J_{m}(j_{n}^{m}r)

and so does not converge to
g
for any
m\geqslant 0
,
regardless of the truncation method
.

Thus, restrictions on the range of
p
are to be expected.
A natural range is
4/3<p<4
, since this is precisely the range that works for the 1-dimensional Bessel series

(
Wing 1950
)
.

(It is instructive to compare this to the ranges of
L^{p}
convergence for the Bochner-Riesz means on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
; see
Grafakos 2014a
, for details. We will return to this question later.)

The best result known at this time is due to
Balodis and Córdoba 1999
, who reduced the problem of convergence on the disc
to extant results on the convergence of Fourier and 1-dimensional Bessel series, albeit with a modified norm.
We will exploit their argument to obtain
L^{p}
convergence in a certain subspace of
L^{p}
.

2
Mixed-norm convergence

Define the space
L^{p}_{\mathrm{rad}}(L^{2}_{\mathrm{ang}})
by the inequality

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p,2}:=\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{m}\left|f_{m}(r)\right|^{2}\right)^{p/2}r\,\mathrm{d}{r}\right]^{1/p}\equiv\left\lVert\left\lVert(f_{m}(r))\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}({r\,\mathrm{d}{r}})}<\infty,

(3)

where
f_{m}(r)
are the Fourier coefficients of the angular function
t\mapsto f(r,t)
:

f_{m}(r):=\int_{0}^{1}f(r,\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}m\theta}\,\mathrm{d}{\theta}.

Denote by
S_{N,M}f
the partial sums of the Bessel–Fourier series of
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
:

S_{N,M}f(r,t):=\sum_{m=-M}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{m,n}J_{m}(j_{n}^{m}r)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}mt}.

(4)

(We drop the superscript “
(d)
” present in
Balodis and Córdoba 1999
, since
d=2
will remain fixed in our discussion.)

Theorem 1

(
Balodis and Córdoba 1999
)
.

The operators
S_{N,M}
are uniformly bounded on
L^{p}_{\mathrm{rad}}(L^{2}_{\mathrm{ang}})

if, and only if,
\frac{4}{3}<p<4
when
N\geqslant AM+1
for an absolute constant
A>0
.

The norm convergence of the series to
f
follows by the usual uniform boundedness argument.
(See
Balodis and Córdoba 1999
;
cf. the analogous Fourier series argument in
Grafakos 2014a
.)

To attack the proof of
Theorem

1
, they exploited the presence of the Fourier coefficients
in the norm (
3
).
Indeed,
S_{N,M}f
is a trigonometric polynomial whose
m
th
Fourier mode (
\left|m\right|\leqslant M
) is

S_{N,m}f_{m}(r)\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{m,n}J_{m}(j_{n}^{m}r),

(5)

which is precisely the 1-dimensional Bessel series summation operator for the radial function
r\mapsto f_{m}(r)

in terms of the
m
th
order Bessel function
J_{m}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{N,M}f\right\rVert_{p,2}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{m=-M}^{M}\left|S_{N,m}f_{m}(r)\right|^{2}\right)^{p/2}r\,\mathrm{d}{r}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert(S_{N,m}f_{m})_{m}\right\rVert_{L^{p}({r\,\mathrm{d}{r};\ell^{2}})},

so the boundedness of
S_{N,M}
in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{rad}}(L^{2}_{\mathrm{ang}})
is reduced
to a uniform bound for vector-valued inequalities.
Such bounds must be independent of the length,
2M+1
, of the vector

\left(S_{N,m}f_{-m},\ldots,S_{N,m}f_{m}\right).

Note that
S_{N,-m}=S_{N,m}
by our convention that
J_{m}=J_{\left|m\right|}
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The functions
f_{m}
and
f_{-m}
, however, are distinct in general,
as they correspond to distinct Fourier coefficients.

Let us now turn to
L^{p}
convergence on the disc, where the relevant norm is

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{D}})}=\left\lVert\left\lVert f(r,t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}({\,\mathrm{d}{t}})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}({r\,\mathrm{d}{r}})}.

For
p\neq 2
we cannot replace the inner “angular”
L^{p}
norm by a sum of Fourier coefficients

(
Katznelson 2004
, see Chap. IV of)
.
However, for
p\geqslant 2
we have the
Reverse Hausdorff-Young Inequality
:

\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\leqslant\left\lVert(\widehat{g}(k))_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\quad\text{whenever}\quad p\geqslant 2\text{ and }\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

We therefore have

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{D}})}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert f(r,t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T},\,\mathrm{d}{t}})}^{p}r\,\mathrm{d}{r}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{k}\left|f_{k}(r)\right|^{q}\right)^{p/q}r\,\mathrm{d}{r}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle=:\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p,q}.

Using this norm, we define the space

L^{p}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\ell_{\mathrm{ang}}^{q}):=\left\{f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D}):\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p,q}<\infty\right\}.

Careful inspection of the proofs in
Balodis and Córdoba 1999
shows that the space

\ell^{2}
can be replaced by
\ell^{q}
throughout when

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1\quad\text{and}\quad 2\leqslant p<4.

The kernel of the 1-dimensional summation operators
S_{N,m}
in
Eq.

5

are controlled by weighted, vector-valued norms on the operators

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(t)}{2-x-t}\,\mathrm{d}{t}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(t)}{x+t}\,\mathrm{d}{t},

(6)

the Hilbert Transform and the Hardy-Littlewood Maximal Functional.
The weight
r^{1-p/2}
satisfies the 1-dimensional Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
condition if, and only if,

-1<1-\frac{p}{2}<p-1\quad\text{that is}\quad\frac{4}{3}<p<4

(see
Grafakos 2014a
, Example 7.1.7)
and, when this is the case,
we have the inequalities

\left\lVert(\mathscr{M}f_{k})_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{p}({r^{1-p/2};\ell^{q}})}+\left\lVert(Hf_{k})_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{p}({r^{1-p/2};\ell^{q}})}\lesssim_{p,q}\left\lVert(f_{k})_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{p}({r^{1-p/2};\ell^{q}})}.

(See
Córdoba 1989
, (B) on p. 25.)

Furthermore, the kernels of the operators in
Eq.

6
are nice enough
that both are bounded on the space
L^{p}([0,1],r^{1-p/2}\,\mathrm{d}{r})
and, since they are positive,
they admit vector-valued extensions to
\ell^{q}
too
(
Grafakos 2014a
, Theorem 5.5.10)
.

There is one more operator to consider on p. 280 of
Balodis and Córdoba 1999
:

T_{N,m}f(x):=\sqrt{x}f_{\nu}(A_{N}^{\nu}x)\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}f_{\nu}(A_{N}^{\nu}t)f(t)\,\mathrm{d}{t}

with
f_{\nu}
as in Lemma 1 of
Balodis and Córdoba 1999
.
For the stated range of
p
,
p/q\geqslant 1
, so we can apply Jensen’s Inequality
as on p. 280 of
Balodis and Córdoba 1999
, and we obtain the

L^{p}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\ell_{\mathrm{ang}}^{q})
-boundeness of
T_{N,\nu}
too.

Theorem 2
.

The operators
S_{N,M}
are uniformly bounded on
L_{\mathrm{rad}}^{p}(\ell_{\mathrm{ang}}^{q})

if
2\leqslant p<4
, when
N\geqslant AM+1
for an absolute constant
A>0
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
2\leqslant p<4
. Then, if
N_{k},M_{k}
are sequences of natural numbers such that
N_{k}\geqslant AM_{k}+1

and
M_{k}\to\infty
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert S_{N_{k},M_{k}}f-f\right\rVert_{p,q}=0

for all
f\in L_{\mathrm{rad}}^{p}(\ell_{\mathrm{ang}}^{q})
.
In particular,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert S_{N_{k},M_{k}}f-f\right\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{D}})}=0

for all
f\in L_{\mathrm{rad}}^{p}(\ell_{\mathrm{ang}}^{q})
.

Note, however, that this method does not allow us to conclude
L^{p}
norm convergence
for all

f\in L^{p}
,
but only for the smaller space
L_{\mathrm{rad}}^{p}(\ell_{\mathrm{ang}}^{q})\subsetneq L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
.
The following function lies in
L^{p}

(
Zygmund and Fefferman 2003
, since it is continuous, see)

but its Fourier coefficients are not
\ell^{q}
summable for any
q>2
:

g(t):=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{ik\log{k}}}{\sqrt{k}(\log{k})^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t}.

In other words,
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D})\setminus L_{\mathrm{rad}}^{p}(\ell_{\mathrm{ang}}^{q})
.

The above example, which is a counterexample to Plancherel’s theorem in
L^{p}
, hints at the underlying issue:
we did not obtain
L^{p}

bounds
for the partial sum operators,
so we cannot apply the proof of
3
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
directly.

In light of the existing results
(
Balodis and Córdoba 1999
;
Wing 1950
;
Benedek and Panzone 1972/73
)
,
we might offer the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

For all
4/3<p<4
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert S_{N_{k},M_{k}}f-f\right\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{D}})}=0,

for some appropriate choice of
N_{k},M_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geqslant 1
.
For
p
outside of this range, convergence fails in general.

However, it is not completely clear that we can expect this.

Córdoba 1989
proved that the ball multiplier is bounded on the mixed norm space

L^{p}_{\mathrm{rad}}(L^{2}_{\mathrm{ang}})
in the range
2n/(n+1)<p<2n/(n-1)
that was originally conjectured for
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
but disproved in
Fefferman 1971
.
As we saw above, the
L^{p}_{\mathrm{rad}}(L^{2}_{\mathrm{ang}})
argument works by essentially “eliminating” the angular eigenfunctions
and reducing the problem to bounds on the one-dimensional Bessel–Fourier series.
This “trick” is not available in
L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, owing to the failure of Plancherel’s Theorem for
p\neq 2
,
so obtaining uniform bounds on the operators
S_{N,M}
is considerably more difficult.

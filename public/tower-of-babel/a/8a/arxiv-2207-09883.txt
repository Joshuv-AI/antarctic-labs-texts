Exact Results for the Orbital Angular Momentum of Magnons on Honeycomb Lattices
Randy S. Fishman, Lucas Lindsay, Satoshi Okamoto

Abstract
We obtain exact results for the orbital angular momentum (OAM) of magnons at the high symmetry points of ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AF) honeycomb lattices in the presence of Dzyallonshinskii-Moriya (DM) interactions. For the FM honeycomb lattice in the absence of DM interactions, the values of the OAM at the corners of the Brillouin zone (BZ) ( 𝐤 1 ∗ = ( 0 , 2 ​ 3 / 9 ) ​ 2 ​ π / a {{\bf k}^{*}_{1}}=(0,2\sqrt{3}/9)2\pi/a , 𝐤 2 ∗ = ( 1 / 3 , 3 / 9 ) ​ 2 ​ π / a , … {{\bf k}^{*}_{2}}=(1/3,\sqrt{3}/9)2\pi/a,\ldots ) are alternately ± 3 ℏ / 16 \pm 3\hbar/16 for both magnon bands. The presence of DM interactions dramatically changes those values by breaking the degeneracy of the two magnon bands. The OAM values are alternately 3 ​ ℏ / 8 3\hbar/8 and 0 for the lower magnon band and − 3 ℏ / 8 -3\hbar/8 and 0 for the upper magnon band. For the AF honeycomb lattice, the values of the OAM at the corners of the BZ are ∓ ( 3 ​ ℏ / 16 ) ​ κ \mp(3\hbar/16)\kappa on one of the degenerate magnon bands and ± ( 3 ​ ℏ / 8 ) ​ ( 1 + κ / 2 ) \pm(3\hbar/8)(1+\kappa/2) on the other, where κ \kappa measures the anisotropy and the result is independent of the DM interaction.



I
Introduction

The conversion of spin into orbital angular momentum and back has been of great interest since the
early measurements of Einstein and deHass
Einstein15
and Barnett
Barnett15
. One recently posed question

Mat11a
;
Mat11b
;
Neumann20
;
Streib21
is whether spin excitations,
commonly called magnons, can have both spin and orbital angular momentum (OAM). While the magnon produced by a ferromagnet (FM) with moments up
has spin
{\cal S}=-\hbar
, its OAM

{\cal L}
is not known. The observation of the OAM associated with magnons would have wide-ranging physical ramifications.
Since the total angular momentum
{\cal J}={\cal S}+{\cal L}
is conserved
Tsukernik66
;
Garmatyuk68
,
magnons with
{\cal S}
parallel to
{\cal L}
and those with
{\cal S}

antiparallel to
{\cal L}
will behave differently under collisions and in external fields. Consequently, memory storage devices may be able to utilize the OAM of
magnons in the developing field of magnonics
Karen14
. It is likely that the magnon Hall
Fuji09
;
Katsura10
;
Onose10
and Nernst
Cheng16
effects
will sensitively depend
on the OAM of the magnons. Another fascinating possibility is that magnons with OAM can
be created and controlled by the interaction with photons
Marrucci06
and phonons
Zhang14
;
Hamada18
that also carry OAM.

In a recent paper
fishun
, two of us demonstrated that the magnons of collinear magnets may exhibit OAM even for simple collinear magnets.
Surprisingly, the OAM of magnons on FM and antiferromagnetic (AF) zig-zag and honeycomb lattices in two dimensions depends on the network
of exchange interactions and not on spin-orbit (SO) coupling, as was proposed in Ref.
Neumann20
. Rather, we found that the confinement of
magnons can generate an effective OAM by disrupting the locally symmetric environment at each lattice site.
In that sense, our previous efforts followed earlier studies of the OAM of magnons in confined
spherical

Haigh16
;
Sharma17
;
Osada18
and cylindrical
Jiang20
;
Lee22
;
Kamenetskii21
geometries. At least formally, our results also reduced to the well-known
expressions of Matsumoto and Murakami
Mat11a
;
Mat11b
for the self-rotation of magnon packets in the case of FM systems at low
excitation energies.

Our previous work was based almost entirely on
numerical simulations. In this paper, we obtain
exact results for the OAM of magnons on FM and AF honeycomb lattices at the corners
{\bf k}^{*}
of the Brillouin zone (BZ) in reciprocal space
(see Fig. 2) in the presence of Dzyalloshinzki-Moriya (DM) interactions, which are induced by SO interactions.
In addition to providing checks on our numerics, this work also provides deeper insight into the profound and unexpected effects of the DM interaction on the
OAM of honeycomb lattices. For a FM honeycomb lattice, we find that DM interactions can dramatically change the amplitude of the OAM near the BZ corners.

Section II summarizes the OAM formalism for the magnons of FM and AF honeycomb lattices. Section III presents our results for the FM honeycomb lattice
in the absence of DM interactions.
We then add DM interactions in Section IV. Section V treats the AF honeycomb
lattice. Finally, Section VI contains a conclusion.

II
OAM Formalism

As sketched in Fig. 1, honeycombs are non-Bravais lattices
with two unique sites 1 and 2.
The total magnetization
{\bf M}_{i}
at site
i
can be written in terms of the dynamical magnetization
\bm{\mu}_{i}
as

{\bf{M}}_{i}=\bm{\mu}_{i}+{\bf n}_{i}\sqrt{M_{0}^{2}-\mu_{i}^{2}},

(1)

where the static magnetization
{\bf{M}}_{0i}=2\mu_{\rm B}S\,{\bf n}_{i}
lies along
{\bf n}_{i}=\pm{\bf z}
and
\bm{\mu}_{i}\cdot{\bf n}_{i}=0
.
The “pseudo-momentum”
{\bf p}_{i}
is derived
fishun

from the energy-momentum tensor
T_{\alpha\beta}

Landau60
for a model spin Lagrangian

L=\frac{1}{4\mu_{\rm B}M_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\dot{\bm{\mu}}_{i}\times{\bf n}_{i}\bigr)\cdot\bm{\mu}_{i}-H_{2},

(2)

where
H_{2}
is obtained by expanding the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{N}J_{\alpha\beta}\,M_{i\alpha}M_{i+1,\beta}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf M}_{i}\cdot{\bf h}_{i}

(3)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{K}{4\mu_{\rm B}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}M_{iz}^{2}-\frac{1}{8\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}^{2},

to second order in
\mu_{i}
.
Here, the exchange
J_{\alpha\beta}
includes possible antisymmetric terms like the DM interaction, the magnetic dipole
field
{\bf h}_{i}
satisfies
\nabla\times{\bf h}_{i}=0
and
\nabla\cdot{\bf h}_{i}=-4\pi\nabla\cdot{\bf M}_{i}
,
and

is the easy-axis anisotropy along
{\bf z}

;
Garmatyuk68
that

can also be obtained from the Euler-Lagrange equations for

Consequently,

\displaystyle p_{i\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}_{i}}{\partial x_{\alpha}}\cdot\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\bm{\mu}_{i}}}

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\mu_{\rm B}M_{0}}\bigl({\bf n}_{i}\times\bm{\mu}_{i}\bigr)\cdot\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}_{i}}{\partial x_{\alpha}}.

is the momentum at site
i

Within linear spin-wave theory, the
1/S
quantization conditions

{\mu}_{i}^{+}=\mu_{ix}n_{iz}+i\mu_{iy}=2\mu_{\rm B}\sqrt{2S\hbar}\,a_{i},

(5)

{\mu}_{i}^{-}=\mu_{ix}n_{iz}-i\mu_{iy}=2\mu_{\rm B}\sqrt{2S\hbar}\,a_{i}^{\dagger}

a_{i}
and
a_{i}^{\dagger}
that satisfy the real-space commutation relations

[a_{i},a_{j}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{ij}

[a_{i},a_{j}]=0

satisfying the

[a_{{\bf k}}^{(r)},a_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}^{(s)\dagger}]=\delta_{rs}\,\delta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}

[a_{{\bf k}}^{(r)},a_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}^{(s)}]=0

{\bf z}
as
fishun

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}({\bf R}_{i}\times{\bf p}_{i})\cdot{\bf z}

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\mu_{\rm B}M_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl\{{\mu}_{i}^{+}\,\hat{l}_{zi}\,{\mu}_{i}^{-}-{\mu}_{i}^{-}\,\hat{l}_{zi}\,{\mu}_{i}^{+}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{2}\sum_{{\bf k}}\Bigl\{a_{{\bf k}}^{(r)}\,{\hat{l}_{z{\bf k}}}\,a_{{\bf k}}^{(r)\dagger}-a_{{\bf k}}^{(r)\dagger}\,{\hat{l}_{z{\bf k}}}\,a_{{\bf k}}^{(r)}\Bigr\},

(8)

(9)

are the OAM operators in real and momentum space.

Converting
a_{{\bf k}}^{(r)}
and
a_{{\bf k}}^{(r)\dagger}
to the Boson operators

and
b_{{\bf k}}^{(n)\dagger}

\displaystyle a_{{\bf k}}^{(r)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{2}\Bigl\{X^{-1}({\bf k})_{rn}\,b_{{\bf k}}^{(n)}+X^{-1}({\bf k})_{r,n+2}\,b_{-{\bf k}}^{(n)\dagger}\Bigr\},

(10)

\displaystyle a_{-{\bf k}}^{(r)\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{2}\Bigl\{X^{-1}({\bf k})_{r+2,n}\,b_{{\bf k}}^{(n)}+X^{-1}({\bf k})_{r+2,n+2}\,b_{-{\bf k}}^{(n)\dagger}\Bigr\}.

The zero-temperature expectation value of
{\cal L}_{z}

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{zn}({\bf k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\bf k},n|{\cal L}_{z}|{\bf k},n\rangle

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{2}\Bigl\{X^{-1}({\bf k})_{rn}\,{\hat{l}_{z{\bf k}}}\,X^{-1}({\bf k})_{rn}^{*}

\displaystyle-X^{-1}({\bf k})_{r+2,n}\,{\hat{l}_{z{\bf k}}}\,X^{-1}({\bf k})^{*}_{r+2,n}\Bigr\}.

Due to the linear terms

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\frac{R_{l\alpha}}{|{\bf R}_{l}|}\,\delta_{l}f({\bf r})

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}\exp(i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r})

\displaystyle i\overline{k}_{\alpha}\exp(i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r})

For the honeycomb lattice in Fig. 1, summing over the three translation vectors

\displaystyle\overline{k}_{x}a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin(3k_{x}a/2)\cos(\sqrt{3}k_{y}a/2),

\displaystyle\overline{k}_{y}a

\displaystyle+\sin(\sqrt{3}k_{y}a)\Bigr\}.

and

(36)

for the lower and upper magnon bands, respectively.

VI
Conclusion

The theory developed in this paper can be easily extended to treat many real three-dimensional materials that contain
honeycomb lattices lying within their
ab
planes. Sivadas
et al.

Sivadas15
reviewed the
the magnetic phase diagrams of honeycomb systems with chemical formula ABX
3
.
Some examples of FM honeycomb lattices within that class are CrSiTe
3
and CrGeTe
3

Carteaux95a
;
Carteaux95b
;
Casto15
.
Two other Cr-based FM honeycomb systems are CrI
3

McGuire15
;
Chen18t
and
CrCl
3

Li22
. Two examples of
AF honeycomb lattices are MnPS
3
and MnPSe
3

Wildes98
.

The exact calculations in this paper reveal several pertinent points about the OAM of magnons on honeycomb lattices. First, finite results for the OAM at high-symmetry
points
{\bf k}^{*}
are only reached if periodic functions
\overline{k}_{x}
and
\overline{k}_{y}
are used in the OAM operator
{\hat{l}_{z{\bf k}}}
. If non-periodic terms
k_{x}
and
k_{y}
were retained in the OAM
operator, then
the OAM would diverge at the high-symmetry points
{\bf k}^{*}
. Second, the OAM of the FM and AF honeycomb lattices are closely connected. The average OAM of the major and minor AF honeycomb bands
equals the OAM of the FM bands. Third, the largest OAM for the pure honeycomb lattice with amplitude
3\hbar/8
is still substantially less than
\hbar
. So the spin angular momentum of
a single spin flip is bigger than the largest OAM. Fourth,
the DM interaction produced by SO coupling controls the behavior of the OAM near the corners of the BZ, no matter how small the size of
d
. We conclude that once
the network of exchange interactions generates an OAM by confining the magnons, SO coupling can still have important effects. Experimentally, the maximum OAM expected
for a FM honeycomb material with even a small to moderate value of the DM interaction should be about
0.375\hbar
.

Although performed at zero temperature, these calculations indicate the available magnon states that can be filled at nonzero temperatures. For the FM honeycomb lattice with
D=0
,
the symmetry relation
{\cal L}_{zn}({\bf k})=-{\cal L}_{zn}(-{\bf k})
for magnon bands
n=1
and 2 implies that the thermal average
\langle{\cal L}_{z}\rangle
vanishes due to the integral over wavevectors
{\bf k}
.
As discussed above, the DM interaction
D
breaks that symmetry relation leaving only
{\cal L}_{z1}({\bf k})=-{\cal L}_{z2}(-{\bf k})
. Consequently, the lower or upper magnon band will carry
positive or negative OAM, respectively. This implies that
\langle{\cal L}_{z}\rangle
peaks at a temperature
T
of order
D^{2}/J
. We will examine this behavior more carefully in future work.

Even for the simple case of a honeycomb lattice with a single exchange interaction, nearest-neighbor DM interactions, and easy-axis anisotropy,
many questions remain unanswered. What is the coupling between the spin and orbital
angular momentum: do they tend to lie parallel or antiparallel? What is the effect of next-neighbor exchange couplings for both FM and AF honeycomb lattices? Of magnetic fields?
Is there some easy way to understand the qualitative difference between the OAM of the major and minor bands of the AF honeycomb lattice? Since the major and minor
bands lie at the same energy and momentum, can OAM be easily exchanged between the magnons of the two bands?
We hope that future research on the OAM of magnons in other non-Bravais lattices will be motivated by the exact results presented in this work.

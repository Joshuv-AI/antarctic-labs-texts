Second and third harmonic generation of acoustic waves in a nonlinear elastic solid in one space dimension
Fernando Lund

Abstract
The generation of second and third harmonics by an acoustic wave propagating along one dimension in a weakly nonlinear elastic medium that is loaded harmonically in time with frequency ω 0 \omega_{0} at a single point in space, is analyzed by successive approximations starting with the linear case. It is noted that nonlinear waves have a speed of propagation that depends on their amplitude, a reflection of the fact that nonlinear oscillators have an amplitude-dependent period. It is also noted that both a free medium as well as a loaded medium generate higher harmonics, but that although the second harmonic of the free medium scales like the square of the linear wave, this is no longer the case when the medium is externally loaded. The shift in speed of propagation due to the nonlinearities is determined imposing that there be no resonant (“secular”) terms in a successive approximations solution scheme to the homogeneous (i.e., “free”) problem. The result is then used to solve the inhomogeneous (i.e., “loaded”) case also by successive approximations, up to the third order. At second order, the result is a second harmonic wave whose amplitude is modulated by a long wave, whose wavelength is inversely proportional to the shift in the speed of propagation of the linear wave due to nonlinearities. The amplitude of the long modulating wave scales like the amplitude of the linear wave to the four thirds. It depends both on the third- and fourth-order elastic constants, as well as on the frequency and amplitude of the loading. At short distances from the source, in a distance scale that depends both on nonlinearities as well as on the loading frequency and on the amplitude of the linear wave, a scaling proportional to the amplitude of the linear wave squared is recovered, as is a second harmonic amplitude that grows linearly with distance from the source, and depends on the third-order elastic constant only. The third order solution is the sum of four amplitude-modulated waves, two of them oscillate with frequency ω 0 \omega_{0} and the other two, third harmonics, with 3 ​ ω 0 3\omega_{0} . In each pair, one term scales like the amplitude of the linear wave to the five-thirds, and the other to the seven-thirds.

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma ka^{2}}{{(2\pi)^{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})[\hat{v}^{(0)}(k-k^{\prime})+\dots]\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime\prime}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})k^{\prime\prime}[\hat{v}^{(0)}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})+\dots][\hat{v}^{(0)}(k^{\prime\prime})+\dots]

\frac{a^{2}}{{c_{0}^{2}}}\frac{d^{2}\hat{v}^{(0)}(k)}{dt^{2}}+K_{k}^{2}\hat{v}^{(0)}(k)=0

has the solution

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{(0)}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{k}^{(0)}e^{i(\Omega^{(0)}_{k}+\Omega^{(1)}_{k}+\Omega^{(2)}_{k}+\dots)t}+{B_{k}^{(0)}e^{-i(\Omega^{(0)}_{k}+\Omega^{(1)}_{k}+\Omega^{(2)}_{k}+\dots)t}}

(39)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{(0)}_{+}(k)+{\hat{v}^{(0)}_{-}(k)}

(40)

with
\Omega^{(0)}_{k}\equiv c_{0}K_{k}/a
and
\Omega^{(i)}_{k}=c_{i}K_{k}/a;i=1,2\dots
. We have allowed for a frequency of oscillation that deviates from the linear case, with corrections that may be
k
dependent, and will depend on the amplitudes
A_{k}^{(0)}
and
B_{k}^{(0)}
.

The order one equation is

\displaystyle\hskip-18.49988pt\frac{a^{2}}{{c_{0}^{2}}}\frac{d^{2}\hat{v}^{(1)}(k)}{dt^{2}}+K_{k}^{2}\hat{v}^{(1)}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\beta ka^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})\hat{v}^{(0)}(k^{\prime})\hat{v}^{(0)}(k-k^{\prime})+\frac{2\Omega^{(0)}_{k}\Omega^{(1)}_{k}}{c_{0}^{2}/a^{2}}\hat{v}^{(0)}(k)

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\beta ka^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})\left(\hat{v}^{(0)}_{+}(k^{\prime})+{\hat{v}^{(0)}_{-}(k^{\prime})}\right)\left(\hat{v}^{(0)}_{+}(k-k^{\prime})+{\hat{v}^{(0)}_{-}(k-k^{\prime})}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 36.99976pt+\frac{2\Omega^{(0)}_{k}\Omega^{(1)}_{k}}{c_{0}^{2}/a^{2}}u^{(0)}(k)

(42)

which has the same structure as (
28
).
Take a closer look at (
41
): it is a harmonic oscillator driven by the terms on the right-hand-side. The first term on the right is a bilinear combination of the order zero oscillator: Infinitely many of them combine to drive the
k-th
order one oscillator. But not all of them will contribute to a resonant driving. Indeed, since
u^{(0)}(k)
is a linear combination of oscillators with frequencies
\pm\Omega_{k}
, the only way for the bilinear combination to give rise to a resonance is to have either
k^{\prime}=k
, or
k^{\prime}=0
. But in both cases the kernel of the integrand vanishes. The conclusion is that this bilinear combination does not lead to secular terms and, consequently,

\Omega^{(1)}_{k}=0

(43)

In order go to the next order of approximation we need the solution to

\frac{a^{2}}{{c_{0}^{2}}}\frac{d^{2}\hat{v}_{++}^{(1)}(k)}{dt^{2}}+K_{k}^{2}\hat{v}_{++}^{(1)}(k)=RHS_{++}

(44)

which is

\hat{v}_{++}^{(1)}(k)=\frac{i\beta ka^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})A_{k^{{}^{\prime}}}^{(0)}A_{k-k^{{}^{\prime}}}^{(0)}\frac{e^{i(\Omega_{k-k^{\prime}}+\Omega_{k^{\prime}})t}}{\left(K_{k-k^{\prime}}+K_{k^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-K_{k}^{2}}\,.

(45)

A similar reasoning leads to

\hat{v}_{--}^{(1)}(k)=\frac{i\beta ka^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})B_{k^{{}^{\prime}}}^{(0)}B_{k-k^{{}^{\prime}}}^{(0)}\frac{e^{-i(\Omega_{k-k^{\prime}}+\Omega_{k^{\prime}})t}}{\left(K_{k-k^{\prime}}+K_{k^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-K_{k}^{2}}

(46)

\hat{v}_{+-}^{(1)}(k)=\frac{i\beta ka^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})A_{k^{{}^{\prime}}}^{(0)}B_{k-k^{{}^{\prime}}}^{(0)}\frac{e^{i(\Omega_{k^{\prime}}-\Omega_{k-k^{{}^{\prime}}})t}}{\left(K_{k^{{}^{\prime}}}-K_{k-k^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)^{2}-K_{k}^{2}}

(47)

and the order-one solution to the homogeneous problem is (ignoring the possibility of solutions to the homogeneous equation)

\hat{v}^{(1)}(k)=\,\hat{v}^{(1)}_{++}(k)+\hat{v}^{(1)}_{--}(k)+2\hat{v}^{(1)}_{+-}(k)

(48)

III.2
Order two

This is

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\frac{d^{2}\hat{v}^{(2)}(k)}{dt^{2}}+K_{k}^{2}\hat{v}^{(2)}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\beta ka^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})[\hat{v}^{(1)}(k^{\prime})\hat{v}^{(0)}(k-k^{\prime})+\hat{v}^{(0)}(k^{\prime})\hat{v}^{(1)}(k-k^{\prime})]

(49)

\displaystyle\hskip-18.49988pt+\frac{\gamma ka^{2}}{{(2\pi)^{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})\hat{v}^{(0)}(k-k^{\prime})\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime\prime}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})k^{\prime\prime}\hat{v}^{(0)}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})\hat{v}^{(0)}(k^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+\frac{2\Omega^{(0)}_{k}\Omega^{(2)}_{k}}{c_{0}^{2}/a^{2}}\hat{v}^{(0)}(k)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle RHS^{\beta}+RHS^{\gamma}+\frac{2\Omega^{(0)}_{k}\Omega^{(2)}_{k}}{c_{0}^{2}/a^{2}}\hat{v}^{(0)}(k)

and we must identify the possible resonant terms in the RHS.

III.2.1
Beta

We shall now take the first order solution that was obtained as a “limit” of a discrete chain. Consider

\displaystyle RHS^{\beta}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{i\beta ka^{2}}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})\hat{v}^{(1)}(k^{\prime})\hat{v}^{(0)}(k-k^{\prime})

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}ka^{4}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}{k^{\prime}}^{2}(k-k^{\prime})\left[A_{k-k^{\prime}}^{(0)}e^{i\Omega_{k-k^{\prime}}t}+{B_{k-k^{\prime}}^{(0)}}e^{-i\Omega_{k-k^{\prime}}t}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})A_{k^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{(0)}A_{k^{\prime}-k^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{(0)}\frac{e^{i(\Omega_{k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}+\Omega_{k^{\prime\prime}})t}}{\left(K_{k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}+K_{k^{\prime\prime}}\right)^{2}-K_{k^{\prime}}^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})B_{k^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{(0)}B_{k^{\prime}-k^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{(0)}\frac{e^{-i(\Omega_{k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}+\Omega_{k^{\prime\prime}})t}}{\left(K_{k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}+K_{k^{\prime\prime}}\right)^{2}-K_{k^{\prime}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+2\left.\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})A_{k^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{(0)}B_{k^{\prime}-k^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{(0)}\frac{e^{i(\Omega_{k^{\prime\prime}}-\Omega_{k^{\prime}-k^{{}^{\prime\prime}}})t}}{\left(K_{k^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}-K_{k^{\prime}-k^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\right)^{2}-K_{k^{\prime}}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle RHS^{\beta}_{+++}+RHS^{\beta}_{+--}+RHS^{\beta}_{-++}+RHS^{\beta}_{---}+RHS^{\beta}_{++-}+RHS^{\beta}_{-+-}

As noted, this is the sum of six terms. We are looking for those terms whose time dependence is given by

To expand a little bit: Eqn. (
49

\hat{v}^{(2)}(k)

\displaystyle\left(K_{k^{\prime}-k}+K_{k}\right)^{2}-K_{k^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}{k^{\prime}}^{2}k(k^{\prime}-k)a^{4}

(53)

\displaystyle\left(K_{k}-K_{k^{\prime}-k}\right)^{2}-K_{k^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle 4k(k-k^{\prime})a^{2}

(54)

ka\to 0

We start by considering the first four terms in the right-hand-side of (

\displaystyle RHS^{\beta}_{+++}+RHS^{\beta}_{+--}

\displaystyle+RHS^{\beta}_{-++}+RHS^{\beta}_{---}

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\beta^{2}ka^{4}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}{k^{\prime}}^{2}(k-k^{\prime})\left[A_{k-k^{\prime}}^{(0)}e^{i\Omega_{k-k^{\prime}}t}+{B_{k-k^{\prime}}^{(0)}}e^{-i\Omega_{k-k^{\prime}}t}\right]

\displaystyle\,\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})A_{k^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{(0)}A_{k^{\prime}-k^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{(0)}\frac{e^{i(\Omega_{k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}+\Omega_{k^{\prime\prime}})t}}{\left(K_{k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}+K_{k^{\prime\prime}}\right)^{2}-K_{k^{\prime}}^{2}}\right.

(56)

\displaystyle+\left.RHS^{\beta}_{-++}+RHS^{\beta}_{---}\right|_{k^{\prime\prime}=k}

\displaystyle\,-\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2}a^{4}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}\frac{{k^{\prime}}^{2}(k-k^{\prime})^{2}}{\left(K_{k^{\prime}-k}+K_{k}\right)^{2}-K_{k^{\prime}}^{2}}

with

IV
Back to the inhomogeneous case

In order to deal with the inhomogeneous case we revisit (
11
), and note that now not all the oscillators labeled by
k
are equivalent: two of them, the ones with
k=\pm\tilde{\omega}_{0}
, are driven at resonance. Hence they will not respond by oscillating with frequency
\omega_{0}
but with an amplitude-modified frequency. The magnitude of this correction was determined in the previous section. Equivalently, we let the leading order approximation to be a wave that travels not with velocity
c_{0}
but
c_{0}+c_{2}
, with
c_{2}\ll c_{0}
:

u^{(1)}(x,t)=-\frac{S_{0}}{2\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}_{0}}\cos(\omega_{0}t-\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}_{0}x)

(87)

where
\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}_{0}
differs from
\tilde{\omega}_{0}=\omega_{0}/c_{0}
. The amplitude of this wave is fixed by the boundary condition imposed at the origin, Eq. (
4
). However, this function is not a solution of (
2
) but of

\frac{1}{(c_{0}+c_{2})^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}u^{(1)}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}u^{(1)}}{\partial x^{2}}=S_{0}\delta(x)\sin\omega_{0}t\\

(88)

with
\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}_{0}\equiv\omega_{0}/(c_{0}+c_{2})
. Or, in Fourier space,

\frac{1}{(c_{0}+c_{2})^{2}}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}u^{(1)}(k)+k^{2}u^{(1)}(k)=S_{0}\sin\omega_{0}t

(89)

whose solution is a modification of (
13
):

\hat{u}^{(1)}(k)=\hat{u}^{(1)}_{+}(k)+{\hat{u}^{(1)}_{-}(k)}

(90)

with

\hat{u}^{(1)}_{\pm}(k,t)=\pm\frac{(S_{0}/2i)e^{\pm i\omega_{0}t+\epsilon t}}{k^{2}-(\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}_{0}\mp i\epsilon)^{2}}

(91)

so that the privileged oscillators, the ones that are being driven at resonance, respond with an amplitude

\left.\hat{u}^{(1)}(k,t)\right|_{k=\pm\tilde{\omega}_{0}}=\frac{S_{0}}{4i}\frac{1}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}^{2}(c_{2}/c_{0})}\left[e^{i\omega_{0}t}-e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\right]

(92)

which is very large compared to the oscillators with
k\neq\pm\tilde{\omega}_{0}
, since
(c_{2}/c_{0})
is very small. We identify these two oscillators with the ones that were considered in the previous section when computing the amplitude-dependent frequency:

\hat{v}^{(0)}(k,t)={\cal A}\left[\delta(\tilde{k}-1)-\delta(\tilde{k}+1)\right]\left[e^{ic_{0}kt}-e^{-ic_{0}kt}\right]

(93)

with

{\cal A}\equiv\frac{S_{0}}{{4}i}\frac{1}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}^{2}(c_{2}/c_{0})}\hskip 30.00005pt\mbox{and \hskip 10.00002pt $\tilde{k}\equiv k/k_{0}=c_{0}k/\omega_{0}$}\,.

(94)

Comparing with (
39
) we see that

A_{k}^{(0)}={\cal A}\left[\delta(\tilde{k}-1)-\delta(\tilde{k}+1)\right]=-B_{k}^{(0)}\,,

(95)

so that, using (
86
) and (
94
) we have

\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{0}}\right)^{3}=-\frac{S_{0}^{2}}{16{\pi^{2}}}\left[\beta^{2}+\gamma\right]\,.

(96)

With this information at hand we reformulate the successive approximation scheme including from the beginning the fact that, to leading order, the
k
-th oscillator does not oscillate with a frequency
c_{0}k
but with a frequency
(c_{0}+c_{2})k
:

\displaystyle\hskip-36.99976pt\frac{1}{(c_{0}+c_{2})^{2}}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\left[\hat{u}^{(1)}(k)\right]+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\left[\hat{u}^{(2)}(k)+\hat{u}^{(3)}(k)+\dots\right]

\displaystyle+k^{2}\left[\hat{u}^{(1)}(k)+\hat{u}^{(2)}(k)+\hat{u}^{(3)}(k)+\dots\right]=S_{0}\sin\omega_{0}t

(97)

\displaystyle{\hskip-36.99976pt-\frac{i\beta k}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})\left[\hat{u}^{(1)}(k^{\prime})+\hat{u}^{(2)}(k^{\prime})+\dots\right]\left[\hat{u}^{(1)}(k-k^{\prime})+\hat{u}^{(2)}(k-k^{\prime})+\dots\right]}

\displaystyle\hskip-27.74982pt+\frac{\gamma k}{{(2\pi)^{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})[\hat{u}^{(1)}(k-k^{\prime})+\dots]\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime\prime}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})k^{\prime\prime}[\hat{u}^{(1)}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})+\dots][\hat{u}^{(1)}(k^{\prime\prime})+\dots]

whose leading order solution has already been given in (
90
).
To go back to position space we take the FT of (
90
) and note that this is just (
19
) with the
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
replaced by
\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}_{0}
:

\displaystyle\hat{u}^{(1)}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{S_{0}}{2\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}_{0}}\cos\left(\omega_{0}t-\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}_{0}|x|\right)

(98)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{S_{0}}{2\tilde{\omega}_{0}}\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{0}}\right)\cos\left(\omega_{0}t-\tilde{\omega}_{0}(1-c_{2}/c_{0})|x|\right)

(99)

This is a monochromatic plane wave with speed of propagation
c_{0}(1+c_{2}/c_{0})
. There is also a correction to the amplitude. Said amplitude, to leading order, is

{\mathsf{A}}_{1}\equiv\frac{S_{0}}{2\tilde{\omega}_{0}}{\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{0}}\right)}

(100)

IV.1
Second order

If
u^{(1)}(k)
is the solution to (
89
), the next order equation is

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\hat{u}^{(2)}(k)}{c_{0}^{2}dt^{2}}+k^{2}\hat{u}^{(2)}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\beta k}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})\hat{u}^{(1)}(k^{\prime})\hat{u}^{(1)}(k-k^{\prime})

(101)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\beta k}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})\left[\hat{u}^{(1)}_{+}(k^{\prime})+{\hat{u}^{(1)}_{-}(k^{\prime})}\right]\left[\hat{u}^{(1)}_{+}(k-k^{\prime})+{\hat{u}^{(1)}_{-}(k-k^{\prime})}\right]

and we need, using (
91
)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\hat{u}_{++}^{(2)}(k)}{c_{0}^{2}dt^{2}}+k^{2}\hat{u}_{++}^{(2)}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\beta k}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})\hat{u}^{(1)}_{+}(k^{\prime})\hat{u}^{(1)}_{+}(k-k^{\prime})

(102)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\beta S_{0}^{2}k}{8\pi}e^{2i\omega_{0}t+2\epsilon t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}\frac{k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})}{[k^{\prime 2}-({\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}}_{0}-i\epsilon)^{2}][(k-k^{\prime})^{2}-({\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}}_{0}-i\epsilon)^{2}]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{+}\frac{\beta S_{0}^{2}}{4}\frac{k\tilde{\omega}_{0}e^{2i\omega_{0}t+2\epsilon t}}{k^{2}-4(\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}_{0}-i\epsilon)^{2}}

(103)

where we have neglected a term of order
(c_{2}/c_{0})
in the numerator, that is, in the amplitude of the driver of the second harmonic. The solution to (
103
) is

\hat{u}_{++}^{(2)}(k)={+}\frac{\beta S_{0}^{2}}{4}\frac{k\tilde{\omega}_{0}e^{2i\omega_{0}t+2\epsilon t}}{[k^{2}-4({\tilde{\omega}}_{0}-i\epsilon)^{2}][k^{2}-4({\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}}_{0}-i\epsilon)^{2}]}\,.

(104)

Similarly

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\hat{u}_{--}^{(2)}(k)}{c^{2}dt^{2}}+k^{2}\hat{u}_{--}^{(2)}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\beta k}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime}){\hat{u}^{(1)}_{-}(k^{\prime})}\,{\hat{u}^{(1)}_{-}(k-k^{\prime})}

(105)

and

\hat{u}_{--}^{(2)}(k)={-}\frac{\beta S_{0}^{2}}{4}\frac{k\tilde{\omega}_{0}e^{-2i\omega_{0}t+2\epsilon t}}{[k^{2}-4({\tilde{\omega}}_{0}+i\epsilon)^{2}][k^{2}-4({\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}}_{0}+i\epsilon)^{2}]}

(106)

We also need

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\hat{u}_{+-}^{(2)}(k)}{c^{2}dt^{2}}+k^{2}\hat{u}_{+-}^{(2)}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\beta k}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})\hat{u}^{(1)}_{+}(k^{\prime}){\hat{u}^{(1)}_{-}(k-k^{\prime})}

(107)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\beta S_{0}^{2}k}{8\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}\frac{k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})}{[k^{\prime 2}-({\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}}_{0}-i\epsilon)^{2}][(k-k^{\prime})^{2}-({\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}}_{0}+i\epsilon)^{2}]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

(108)

and

\hat{u}_{+-}^{(2)}(k)=\hat{u}_{-+}^{(2)}(k)=0.

(109)

so that

\hat{u}^{(2)}(k)=\hat{u}_{++}^{(2)}(k)+\hat{u}_{--}^{(2)}(k)\,.

(110)

Let us consider now waves traveling to the right for
x>0
. This indicates that the integral on the complex
k
-plane needed to obtain
u(x,t)
has to be closed along the upper-half plane. This picks poles where the real part of
k
is negative when
\omega_{0}
is positive:

\displaystyle u_{++}^{(2)}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\hat{u}_{++}^{(2)}(k)e^{ikx}

(111)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta S_{0}^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{0}}{4}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\frac{ke^{ikx}e^{2i\omega_{0}t}}{[k^{2}-4({\tilde{\omega}}_{0}-i\epsilon)^{2}][k^{2}-4({\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}}_{0}-i\epsilon)^{2}]}

(112)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\beta S_{0}^{2}}{{64}\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}(c_{2}/c_{0})}\left[e^{-2i\tilde{\omega}_{0}x}-e^{-2i{\tilde{\tilde{\omega}}}_{0}x}\right]e^{2i\omega_{0}t}

(113)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta S_{0}^{2}}{{32}\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}(c_{2}/c_{0})}e^{2i\omega_{0}t-2i\tilde{\omega}_{0}(1-c_{2}/2c_{0})x}\sin\tilde{\omega}_{0}xc_{2}/c_{0}\,.

(114)

The same reasoning leads to

\displaystyle u_{--}^{(2)}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta S_{0}^{2}}{{32}\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}(c_{2}/c_{0})}e^{-2i\omega_{0}t+2i\tilde{\omega}_{0}(1-c_{2}/2c_{0})x}\sin\tilde{\omega}_{0}xc_{2}/c_{0}

(115)

Finally

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt{u^{(2)}(x,t)=}

\displaystyle\,u_{++}^{(2)}(x,t)+u_{--}^{(2)}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\beta S_{0}^{2}}{{16}\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}(c_{2}/c_{0})}\cos\left[2\omega_{0}t-2\tilde{\omega}_{0}(1-c_{2}/2c_{0})|x|\right]\sin\left[\tilde{\omega}_{0}xc_{2}/c_{0}\right]

(116)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\,\frac{\beta S_{0}^{2}x}{{16}}\cos\left[2\omega_{0}t-2\tilde{\omega}_{0}(1-c_{2}/2c_{0})|x|\right]\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{when $\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{0}xc_{2}}{c_{0}}\ll 1$}

(117)

Consequently the second order solution is given by (
116
). It represents a plane wave of frequency
2\omega_{0}
traveling at speed
c_{0}+c_{2}/2
, whose amplitude is modulated by another wave of wavenumber
\tilde{\omega}_{0}c_{2}/2c_{0}
. The amplitude of the latter is, using (

) and (

{\mathsf{A}}_{2}\equiv\frac{\beta S_{0}^{2}}{{16}\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}(c_{2}/c_{0})}=\frac{S_{0}^{4/3}{\pi^{2/3}}}{2^{8/3}\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}}\frac{\beta}{\left[\beta^{2}+\gamma\right]^{1/3}}=\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{0}^{1/3}{\pi^{2/3}}\beta}{{2}^{4/3}\left[\beta^{2}+\gamma\right]^{1/3}}\,{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{4/3}\,.

(118)

That is, the second order amplitude of the modulating wave is proportional to the leading order amplitude to the four third
s
. The coefficient of proportionality involves both the second order (
\beta
) and the third order (
\gamma
) elastic constants.

It is interesting to check what happens at short distances from the source,
x\tilde{\omega}_{0}c_{2}\ll c_{0}
. In this case, from (
117
) we have

{\mathsf{A}}_{2\,\,{\rm small}\,\,x}=\frac{\beta S_{0}^{2}x}{{16}}=\frac{1}{{4}}\beta\,x\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}^{2}\,{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{2}

(119)

in agreement with the value quoted in the literature (see for example Eq. (34) in Ref.
[
20
]
or Eq. (34) in Ref.
[

]
). Note however that the notion of “small

” depends on the amplitude of the leading order wave, the frequency of the driving load, as well as on the nonlinear elastic constants:

x\ll\frac{(2\pi)^{2/3}}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}^{5/3}}\frac{1}{\left({\mathsf{A}}_{1}\right)^{2/3}}\frac{1}{\left[\beta^{2}+\gamma\right]^{1/3}}\,.

(120)

To conclude this section, we note that the second harmonic of a sound wave carries information not only about the third order elastic constants, but also about the fourth order elastic constants. The latter will also appear in the generation of third order harmonics, to which we now turn our attention.

IV.2
Third order

One important motivation to study second harmonic generation is to measure second harmonic amplitudes and use the resulting measurement to infer the second order constant
\beta
for a given material. As we have seen in the last section, the amplitude of the second harmonic by itself is not enough since it only provides a combination of
\beta
and
\gamma
. To disentangle the two, it is necessary to measure the third harmonic as well.

The equation to be solved is

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\hat{u}^{(3)}(k)}{c^{2}dt^{2}}+k^{2}u^{(3)}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\beta k}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})\left[\hat{u}^{(1)}(k^{\prime})+\hat{u}^{(2)}(k^{\prime})\right]\left[\hat{u}^{(1)}(k-k^{\prime})+\hat{u}^{(2)}(k-k^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle\hskip-27.74982pt+\frac{\gamma k}{{(2\pi)^{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}(k-k^{\prime})[\hat{u}^{(1)}(k-k^{\prime})]\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk^{\prime\prime}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})k^{\prime\prime}[\hat{u}^{(1)}(k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime})][\hat{u}^{(1)}(k^{\prime\prime})]

with
\hat{u}^{(1)}(k)
given by (
98
) and
\hat{u}^{(2)}(k^{\prime})
by (
117
) with
c_{2}/c_{0}
given by (
96
). A rather tedious but straightforward calculation using repeatedly Cauchy’s theorem leads to

u^{(3)}(x,t)=u^{(3)}_{\beta 3}(x,t)+u^{(3)}_{\beta 1}(x,t)+u^{(3)}_{\gamma 3}(x,t)+u^{(3)}_{\gamma 1}(x,t)

(122)

where

\displaystyle u^{(3)}_{\beta 3}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}S_{0}^{3}}{{64}\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}(c_{2}/c_{0})^{2}}\left[\frac{2}{3}\cos 3(\omega_{0}t-\tilde{\omega}_{0}|x|)-\cos 3(\omega_{0}t-\tilde{\omega}_{0}[1-c_{2}/3c_{0}]|x|)\right.

(123)

\displaystyle\hskip 40.00006pt+\left.\frac{1}{3}\cos 3(\omega_{0}t-\tilde{\omega}_{0}[1-c_{2}/c_{0}]|x|)\right]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta^{2}S_{0}^{3}\tilde{\omega}_{0}x^{2}}{{64}}\cos 3(\omega_{0}t-\tilde{\omega}_{0}|x|)\hskip 60.00009pt\mbox{when $\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{0}xc_{2}}{c_{0}}\ll 1$}

(124)

\displaystyle u^{(3)}_{\beta 1}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}S_{0}^{3}}{{16}\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}(c_{2}/c_{0})^{2}}\cos(\omega_{0}t-\tilde{\omega}_{0}|x|)\sin^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{0}xc_{2}/2c_{0}

(125)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}S_{0}^{3}\tilde{\omega}_{0}x^{2}}{{64}}\cos(\omega_{0}t-\tilde{\omega}_{0}|x|)\hskip 70.0001pt\mbox{when $\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{0}xc_{2}}{c_{0}}\ll 1$}

(126)

\displaystyle u^{(3)}_{\gamma 3}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{-}\frac{\gamma S_{0}^{3}}{{96}\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}(c_{2}/c_{0})}\sin\left[3\omega_{0}t-3\tilde{\omega}_{0}(1-c_{2}/2c_{0})|x|\right]\sin 3\tilde{\omega}_{0}|x|c_{2}/2c_{0}

(127)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle{-}\frac{\gamma S_{0}^{3}x}{{64}}\sin\left[3\omega_{0}t-3\tilde{\omega}_{0}(1-c_{2}/2c_{0})|x|\right]\hskip 25.00003pt\mbox{when $\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{0}xc_{2}}{c_{0}}\ll 1$}

(128)

\displaystyle u^{(3)}_{\gamma 1}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma S_{0}^{3}}{{32}\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}(c_{2}/c_{0})}\sin\left[\omega_{0}t-\tilde{\omega}_{0}(1-c_{2}/2c_{0})|x|\right]\sin\tilde{\omega}_{0}|x|c_{2}/2c_{0}

(129)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma S_{0}^{3}|x|}{{64}}\sin\left[\omega_{0}t-\tilde{\omega}_{0}(1-c_{2}/2c_{0})|x|\right]\hskip 40.00006pt\mbox{when $\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{0}|x|c_{2}}{c_{0}}\ll 1$}

(130)

Thus, the third order solution to Eq. (
1
) is the sum of four terms. Two of them are third harmonics, evolving in time as
3\omega_{0}t
, and the other two evolve as
\omega_{0}t
. In both cases one of them is proportional to
\beta^{2}
and the other to
\gamma
. The “
\beta
” terms scale with the amplitude of the source as
S_{0}^{5/3}
and the “
\gamma
” terms scale as
S_{0}^{7/3}
.

V
Discussion and outlook

We have provided a solution to the problem of an acoustic wave propagating along one dimension in a weakly nonlinear elastic medium, loaded at the origin by a monochromatic periodic forcing of frequency
\omega_{0}
and amplitude
S_{0}
, Eq. (
1
), by successive approximations when the amplitude of the forcing is small. The leading order solution, Eq. (
98
), is a plane wave whose speed of propagation is modified from the linear
c_{0}
to
c_{0}+c_{2}
, with
c_{2}/c_{0}
given by (
96
), a modification that depends on the amplitude of the loading and on the nonlinear parameters of the medium. The second order solution is given by (
116
). It is a plane wave of frequency
2\omega_{0}
, a second harmonic, whose amplitude depends both on the second order
\beta
and third order
\gamma
coefficients, and is modulated by a wave of long wavelength, proportional to
c_{0}/c_{2}
. The amplitude of this second harmonic scales with the amplitude
{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
of the leading term as
{\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{4/3}
and not as its amplitude squared, as is the case for free nonlinear oscillations. At short distances from the origin, the expression often found in the literature is recovered. The third order solution is the sum of four terms, Eqs. (
123
), (
125
), (
127
) and (
129
). They are amplitude-modulated plane waves, with long modulation wavelengths, proportional to
c_{0}/c_{2}
. Two of them are of frequency
3\omega_{0}
, third harmonics, and the other two are of frequency
\omega_{0}
.

The amplitude of the second harmonic solution involves not only the distance from the source and the second order parameter
\beta
, but the third order parameter
\gamma
as well. This means that measuring the amplitude of the second harmonic as a function of distance from the source can be used to determine both
\beta
and
\gamma
.

A natural next step to take, using the reasoning reported herein, could be to study the behavior of the response of a weakly nonlinear elastic medium to a loading that is transverse rather than longitudinal. As indicated, for example by Wang and Achenbah
[
29
]
, the nonlinear coupling between both modes could lead to interesting insights of use for non-destructive testing. After that, nonlinear Rayleigh waves, also of importance in nondestructive testing, present themselves as attractive candidates for study. And after that, nonlinear waves in plates and rods.

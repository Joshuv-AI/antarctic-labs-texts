Monochromatic polynomial sumset structures on ℕ \mathbb{N}
Zhengxing Lian and Rongzhong Xiao

Abstract
. In the paper, we search for monochromatic infinite additive structures involving polynomials over ℕ \mathbb{N} . It is proved that for any r ∈ ℕ r\in\mathbb{N} , any two distinct natural numbers a , b a,b , and any 2 2 -coloring of ℕ \mathbb{N} , there exist two sets B , C ⊂ ℕ B,C\subset\mathbb{N} with | B | = r |B|=r and | C | = ∞ |C|=\infty such that there exists some color containing B + a ​ C B+aC and B + b ​ C B+bC .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Infinite sumsets structures

In 1974, Hindman
[
13
]
proved a conjecture of Graham and Rothschild
[
8
]
by showing the following statement: For any finite coloring of
\mathbb{N}
, there exists a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that

\{\sum_{n\in F}x_{n}:F\ \text{is a non-empty finite subset of}\ \mathbb{N}\}

is monochromatic.

Hindman’s theorem implies that for any finite coloring of
\mathbb{N}
, there exists a sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
such that

\bigcup_{k\geq 1}(B_{1}+\cdots+B_{k})

is monochromatic. Naturally, ones seek to search for its density analogues. Very recently, Hernández, Kousek, and Radić
[
11
]
confirmed this by showing that for any
A\subset\mathbb{N}
with positive upper Banach density, there exists a sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
such that

\bigcup_{k\geq 1}(B_{1}+\cdots+B_{k})\subset A.

For more on infinite sumsets structures, see the survey
[
21
]
and these references
[
22
,
14
,
18
,
20
,
17
,
16
,
19
,
10
]
.

1.2.
Polynomial progressions in “large” sets

In 1927, van der Waerden
[
24
]
proved that for any finite coloring of
\mathbb{N}
, there exists some color containing arithmetic progressions of any given length. In 1975, Szemerédi
[
23
]
proved a conjecture of Erdös and Turán by showing that every
A\subset\mathbb{N}
with positive upper density contains arithmetic progressions of any given length. In 1977, Furstenberg
[
3
]
provided an ergodic theoretic proof for Szemerédi theorem. Following Furstenberg’s approach, in 1996, Bergelson and Leibman built polynomial extensions of van der Waerden’s theorem and Szemerédi’s by showing the following multiple recurrence:

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
2
, Theorem A]
)
Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[n]
with
P_{1}(0)=\cdots=P_{k}(0)=0
. Then for any measure preserving system
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
and any
A\in\mathcal{X}
with
\mu(A)>0
,

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu(A\cap T^{-P_{1}(n)}A\cap\cdots\cap T^{-P_{k}(n)}A)>0.

By using Theorem
1.1
iteratively and Furstenberg’s correspondence principle
[
3
]
, we have the following:

Proposition 1.2
.

Given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{A}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{d}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}[n]
and for each
1\leq i\leq d
,
P_{i}
has positive leading coefficient and zero constant term. Then for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
and any
A\subset\mathbb{N}
with positive upper Banach density, there exist two sets
B,C\subset\mathbb{N}
with
|B|=\infty
and
|C|=r
such that the set

\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{A}}(B+P(C))\subset A.

Although the establishment process of the above proposition is simple, it combines aspects of polynomial version of Szemerédi’s theorem and infinite sumsets structures in some sense.

1.3.
Main results

In this paper, we seek to search for certain additive structures in the spirit of Proposition
1.2
. Specifically, we try to find additive structures of the form

(1.1)

\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{A}}(B+P(C))

in some large sets, where
|C|=\infty
.

The following example demonstrates that the configuration (
1.1
) may not exist in some set with positive upper Banach density: Let
A=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\{n^{2}+n,\ldots,n^{2}+2n\}
. Then for any
B,C\subset\mathbb{N}
with
|C|=\infty
,
B+C^{2}
is not a subset of
A
.

Due to the above example, we have to search for the configuration (
1.1
) in the finite coloring setting.

Question 1.3
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N},k>1
and
r\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{d}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}[n]
and for each
1\leq i\leq d
,
P_{i}
has positive leading coefficient and zero constant term. Is it true that for any
k
-coloring of
\mathbb{N}
, there exist two sets
B,C\subset\mathbb{N}
with
|B|=r
and
|C|=\infty
such that the set

\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{A}}(B+P(C))

is monochromatic ?

Remark 1.4
.

(1)

By Ramsey theorem (see
[
9
, Theorem 5 of Chapter 1]
), we have that when
d=1
, the answer to Question
1.3
is positive.

(2)

When
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
C
is a finite set, Question
1.3
is a special case of Proposition
1.2
.

Unfortunately, for the most part, the answer to Question
1.3
is negative. This can be shown be the following counter-examples.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
P,Q\in\mathbb{Z}[n]
with positive leading coefficient and zero constant term and
P\neq Q
. Then:

(1)

If
\max\{\deg P,\deg Q\}>1
, there exists a
2
-coloring
\phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\{1,2\}
such that for any
i\in\{1,2\}
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set

\{m\in\mathbb{N}:\phi(n+P(m))=\phi(n+Q(m))=i\}

is empty or finite.

(2)

If
\deg P=\deg Q=1
, there exists a
3
-coloring
\phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\{1,2,3\}
such that for any
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set

\{m\in\mathbb{N}:\phi(n+P(m))=\phi(n+Q(m))=i\}

is empty or finite.

(3)

If
\deg P=\deg Q=1
, there exists a
2
-coloring
\phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\{1,2\}
such that for any
i\in\{1,2\}
, there are not two infinite subsets
B,C\subset\mathbb{N}
such that

\phi(B+P(C))=\phi(B+Q(C))=i.

Proposition 1.6
.

For any three distinct natural numbers
a,b,c
, there exists a
2
-coloring
\phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\{1,2\}
such that for any
i\in\{1,2\}
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set

\{m\in\mathbb{N}:\phi(n+am)=\phi(n+bm)=\phi(n+cm)=i\}

is empty or finite.

Fortunately for some though, for the rest case, the answer to Question
1.3
is positive.

Theorem 1.7
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, any two distinct natural numbers
a,b
and any
2
-coloring of
\mathbb{N}
, there exist two sets
B,C\subset\mathbb{N}
with
|B|=r
and
|C|=\infty
such that there exists some color which contains
B+aC
and
B+bC
.

Clearly, for any
2
-coloring of
\mathbb{N}
, there must be a cell which is “large” on numerous occasions. Rough speaking, the property of

2.4.
Conditional expectation and disintegration of a measure

Let
\pi:X\to Y
be a measure preserving transformation between two Lebesgue probability spaces
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
and
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\nu)
. For any
f\in L^{1}(\mu)
, the
conditional expectation of
f
with respect to
Y
is the function
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(f|Y)
, defined in
L^{1}(\mu)
, such that for any
A\in\mathcal{Y}
,

\int_{\pi^{-1}A}fd\mu=\int_{\pi^{-1}A}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(f|Y)d\mu.

Clearly, there exists an
\tilde{f}\in L^{1}(\nu)
such that
\tilde{f}\circ\pi=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(f|Y)
almost everywhere. So, we view
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(f|Y)
as an element of
L^{1}(\nu)
sometimes.

Moreover, there exists a unique
\mathcal{Y}
-measurable map
Y\to\mathcal{M}(X,\mathcal{X}),y\mapsto\mu_{y}
, called the
disintegration of
\mu
with respect to
Y
, under neglecting
\nu
-null sets such that for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(f|Y)(x)=\int_{X}fd\mu_{\pi(x)}

for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
, where
\mathcal{M}(X,\mathcal{X})
is the collection of probability measures on
(X,\mathcal{X})
, endowed with standard Borel structure.

2.5.
Measure preserving systems, factors and joingings

A tuple
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
is a

if
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
is a Lebesgue probability space and

is an invertible measure preserving transformation. We say that
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
is
ergodic
or
\mu
is
ergodic
(under
T
) if only the
T
-invariant subsets in
\mathcal{X}
have measure
0
or
1
.

Two measure preserving systems
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
and
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\nu,S)
are

\Phi:X_{0}\to Y_{0}
with
\Phi\circ T=S\circ\Phi
, where
X_{0}
is a
T
-invariant full measurable subset of
X
and
Y_{0}
is an
S
-invariant full measurable subset of
Y
.

A
factor
of a measure preserving system
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
is a
T
-invariant sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{X}
. A
factor map
from
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
to
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\nu,S)
is a measurable map
\pi:X_{0}\rightarrow Y_{0}
with
\pi\circ T=S\circ\pi
and such that
\nu
is the image of
\mu
under
\pi
, where
X_{0}
is a
T
-invariant full measurable subset of
X
and
Y_{0}
is an
S
-invariant full measurable subset of
Y
. In this case,
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{D})
is a factor of
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
. And every factor of
(X,\mathcal{X}\mu,T)
can be obtained in this way.

In this paper, we focus on the
Kronecker factor
of
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
, which is the sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{X}
spanned by the eigenfunctions of
X
with respect to
T
. When
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)

[
15
, Proposition 4.13.(iv)]

(Z,\mathcal{B}(Z),m,R)

Z

Z
.

In this paper, we need to use some properties of Kronecker factors.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
15
, Theorem 4.16]

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
be a measure preserving system. Then the subspace of
L^{2}(\mu\times\mu)
consisting of
T\times T
-invariant functions is the subspace spanned by functions of the form
\overline{f}_{1}\otimes f_{2}
, where each
f_{i}\in L^{2}(X,\mu),i=1,2
is an eigenfunction of
X
with respect to
T
such that the corresponding eigenvalues are equal.

By the above lemma, one can immediately give the following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
be a measure preserving system. Let
\psi\in L^{2}(\mu)
and it is orthogonal to all eigenfunctions of
X
with respect to
T
. Then for the sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{X}
spanned by all
T\times T
-invariant functions, one has that

\left\|\mathbb{E}_{\mu\times\mu}(\psi\otimes\overline{\psi}|\mathcal{I})\right\|_{2}=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, it is left to show that

\int(\psi\otimes\overline{\psi})\cdot(\overline{f}_{1}\otimes f_{2})d\mu\times\mu=0,

where each
f_{i}\in L^{2}(X,\mu),i=1,2
is an eigenfunction of
X
with respect to
T
such that the corresponding eigenvalues are equal.
By the assumption on
\psi
, we have that
\int\psi\cdot fd\mu=\int\overline{\psi}\cdot fd\mu=0
, where
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
is an eigenfunction of
X
. This finishes the proof.
∎

First, we introduce some notation.
Let
X=\{1,2\}^{\mathbb{Z}}

(X,T)

x

\mathbb{N}\to\{1,2\},n\mapsto x(n)

Fix
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
with

\tau=T\times T,\sigma=T^{a}\times T^{b}
. Let
U=(E_{1}\times E_{1})\cup(E_{2}\times E_{2})

E_{1}=\{x\in X:x(0)=1\}\ \text{and}\ E_{2}=\{x\in X:x(0)=2\}

N_{\sigma}(U,(y,z))=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\sigma^{n}(y,z)\in U\}
.

By the pigeonhole principle, we have that Theorem
1.7
is equivalent to the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
and any
x\in X
, there exist two sets
B,C\subset\mathbb{N}
with
|B|=r,|C|=\infty
such that for any
h\in B,k\in C
,
\tau^{h}\sigma^{k}(x,x)\in U
.

Now, for the topological dynamical system
(X\times X,\sigma)
, we provide a lemma, which classifies all minimal points into two classes.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(y,z)
be a minimal point of the topological dynamical system
(X\times X,\sigma)
. Then
(y,z)
must satisfy one of the following:

•

There exists
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}

y(d)\neq z(d),y(d)=y(d+a(b-a)k),\ \text{and}\ z(d)=z(d+b(b-a)k).

•

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, either

N_{\sigma}(U,\tau^{m}(y,z))\ \text{or}\ N_{\sigma}(U,\tau^{m+ab}(y,z))

is syndetic.

Proof.

Note that for any

,
\tau^{l}(y,z)
is still a minimal point of the topological dynamical system
(X\times X,\sigma)
. So, by Lemma

, we know that for any
l\in\mathbb{N}

Assume that there exists an
m\in\mathbb{N}

N_{\sigma}(U,\tau^{m}(y,z))\ \text{and}\ N_{\sigma}(U,\tau^{m+ab}(y,z))

are empty. As

, we have that

(3.1)

y(m+an)\neq z(m+bn)\ \text{and}\ y(m+ab+an)\neq z(m+ab+bn).

By replacing
n
by
n+a
and
n+b
in (

), we know that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

y(m+ab+an)\neq z(m+bn+b^{2})\ \text{and}\ y(m+an+a^{2})\neq z(m+ab+bn).

Note that for any
x\in X,t\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

. So, for any

z(m+ab+bn)=z(m+bn+b^{2})=z(m+ab+b(n+b-a))\ \text{and}

Let

Therefore,

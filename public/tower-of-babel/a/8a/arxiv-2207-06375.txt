Affine fractional Sobolev and isoperimetric inequalities
Julián Haddad, Monika Ludwig

Abstract
. Sharp affine fractional Sobolev inequalities for functions on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} are established. For each 0 < s < 1 0<s<1 , the new inequalities are significantly stronger than (and directly imply) the sharp fractional Sobolev inequalities of Almgren and Lieb. In the limit as s → 1 − s\to 1^{-} , the new inequalities imply the sharp affine Sobolev inequality of Gaoyong Zhang. As a consequence, fractional Petty projection inequalities are obtained which are stronger than the fractional Euclidean isoperimetric inequalities, and a natural conjecture for radial mean bodies is proved.

Primary 46E35, Secondary 35R11, 52A40.

1.
Introduction

Almgren and Lieb
[
2
]
established Pólya–Szegő inequalities
for fractional Sobolev norms. The equality case was settled by Frank and
Seiringer
[
12
]
. A consequence is the fractional Sobolev
inequality (cf.
[
24
,
12
]
): for
0<s<1
, there
is
\alpha_{n,s}>0
(with explicit value given in (
16
)) such
that for
f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(1)

\|f\|_{{\frac{n}{n-s}}}\leq\alpha_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

and there is equality if and only if
f
is, up to sets of measure zero,
a constant multiple of the indicator function of a ball. Here

\|f\|_{p}
denotes the
L^{p}
norm of
f
and

|\cdot|
the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
while

W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the fractional Sobolev space of

L^{1}
functions with finite right-hand side in (
1
). By
a result of Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu
[
4
]
,
for
f\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support,

(2)

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\alpha_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}^{1/n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\omega_{n}
is the
n
-dimensional volume of the unit ball in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the Sobolev space
of
L^{1}
functions
f
with weak
L^{1}
gradient
\nabla f
. Dávila
[
8
]

proved a result corresponding to (
2
) for compactly supported

f\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the space of
L^{1}
functions with bounded variation,
and the assumption on the support can be dropped (see

[
1
, Theorem 1.4]
). Hence, the sharp

L^{1}
Sobolev inequalities on
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})
follow from the fractional Sobolev inequalities (
1
).

Gaoyong Zhang
[
43
]
established a sharp affine Sobolev inequality
that is significantly stronger than the classical
L^{1}
Sobolev inequality.
For
n\geq 2
and
C^{1}
functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with
compact support, Zhang
[
43
]
proved that

(3)

\begin{split}\|f\|_{{\frac{n}{n-1}}}&\leq\frac{\omega_{n}}{2\omega_{n-1}}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\|\langle\nabla f(\cdot),\xi\rangle\|_{1}^{-n}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)^{-1/n}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}^{1/n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product, and integration
on the
(n-1)
-dimensional unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
is with respect to the

(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. The first inequality in (
3
) is now called the affine Sobolev inequality or
the Sobolev–Zhang inequality. It is affine since both terms remain invariant
under volume-preserving affine transformations. Tuo Wang

[
39
]
extended the Sobolev–Zhang inequality to
L^{1}
functions
of bounded variation and showed that there is equality, up to sets of measure
zero, in this generalized Sobolev–Zhang inequality precisely for constant
multiples of indicator functions of ellipsoids.

The main aim of this paper is to establish affine fractional Sobolev inequalities
for
0<s<1
that are stronger than the fractional Sobolev inequalities
in (
1
) and imply, in the limit as
s\to 1^{-}
, the Sobolev–Zhang
inequality.

Theorem 1
.

For
0<s<1
and
f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(4)

\|f\|_{{\frac{n}{n-s}}}\leq\alpha_{n,s}n\omega_{n}^{\frac{n+s}{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\,\Big\|\frac{f(\cdot+t\xi)-f(\cdot)}{t}\Big\|_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{-\frac{n}{s}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)^{-\frac{s}{n}}\\
\leq\alpha_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

There is equality in the first inequality if and only if
f
is, up to
sets of measure zero, a constant multiple of the indicator function of
an ellipsoid. There is equality in the second inequality for radially symmetric
functions.

In order to prove Theorem
1
, we introduce the
s
-fractional
polar projection body
\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f
associated to
f
, defined as the star
body whose gauge function (that is, the reciprocal of the radial function)
for
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
is

\|{\xi}\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\frac{f(x+t\xi)-f(x)}{t}\Big|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(see Section
3
for details). The affine fractional Sobolev
inequality can now be written as

(5)

\|f\|_{{\frac{n}{n-s}}}\leq\alpha_{n,s}n\omega_{n}^{({n+s})/n}|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|^{-s/n},

where
|\cdot|
denotes
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Since both
sides of (
5
) are invariant under translations of

f
, and for volume-preserving linear transformations

\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!\,(f\circ\phi^{-1})=\phi\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f,

it follows that (
5
) is an affine inequality.

To show that Theorem
1
implies the Sobolev–Zhang inequality,
we establish a result corresponding to (
2
). For

f\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we will prove that

(6)

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|^{-s/n}=2\,|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f|^{-1/n},

where
\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f
is the polar projection body of
f
which was defined (with
different notation) in
[
43
]
(also see
[
28
]
) for

\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
by

(7)

\|\xi\|_{\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle\nabla f(x),\xi\rangle|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover, we establish the corresponding statement for

f\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and also recover Wang’s generalized Sobolev–Zhang
inequality (without characterization of the equality case) from Theorem
1
(see Section
4
).

We prove Theorem
1
by first establishing affine fractional
Pólya–Szegő inequalities as a consequence of the Riesz rearrangement
inequality. For the equality case, we use anisotropic fractional Sobolev
norms (introduced in
[
24
]
) and the equality case of the
Riesz rearrangement inequality due to Burchard
[
6
]
. For

0<s<1
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite
s
-perimeter (see Section
2
), we define the fractional polar projection body as

\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E=\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!\operatorname{1}_{E}
, where
\operatorname{1}_{E}
is the indicator function
of
E
, and obtain from the affine fractional Pólya–Szegő inequalities
that

(8)

\Big(\frac{|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E|}{|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!{B^{n}}|}\Big)^{-s/n}\geq\Big(\frac{|E|}{|{B^{n}}|}\Big)^{(n-s)/n}

for
0<s<1
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite
s
-perimeter and finite
measure, where
{B^{n}}
is the Euclidean unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. From
these fractional Petty projection inequalities, Theorem
1
follows by a co-area formula for the fractional Sobolev
norm. In the limit as
s\to 1^{-}
, we obtain from (
8
) the generalized Petty projection inequality by Zhang

[
43
]
and Wang
[
39
]
. In addition, we prove versions
of our functional and geometric inequalities for Steiner symmetrization.

In Section
9
, we connect fractional polar projection bodies
to radial mean bodies. For a convex body
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(that
is,
E
is a compact convex set with non-empty interior) and
p>-1
, Gardner
and Zhang
[
15
]
defined the radial
p
-th mean body of
E
, by its
radial function for
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
, as

\rho_{\operatorname{R}_{p}E}(\xi)=\Big(\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\rho_{E-x}(\xi)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the limit of the above expression is taken for
p=0
. They proved
that

(9)

\frac{|\operatorname{R}_{p}\Delta|}{|\Delta|}\leq\frac{|\operatorname{R}_{p}E|}{|E|}

for a convex body
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
-1<p<n
with equality
precisely if
E
is a simplex, and that the inequality is reversed for

p>n
. Here,
\Delta
is any
n
-dimensional simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We
show that

\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E=\Big(\frac{s}{2|E|}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\operatorname{R}_{-s}E

for every convex body
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<s<1
. Hence, (
9
) can be rewritten as the following reverse of inequality (
8
):

\Big(\frac{|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E|}{|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!\Delta|}\Big)^{-s/n}\leq\Big(\frac{|E|}{|\Delta|}\Big)^{{(n-s)}/n}

for every convex body
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<s<1
. Moreover, using (
8
) and the Riesz rearrangement inequality, we show in
Theorem
20
that

\frac{|\operatorname{R}_{p}E|}{|E|}\leq\displaystyle\frac{|\operatorname{R}_{p}{B^{n}}|}{|{B^{n}}|}

for a convex body
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
-1<p<n
with equality precisely
if
E
is an ellipsoid, and that the inequality is reversed for

p>n
. Thereby, we establish a reverse of the Gardner–Zhang inequality (
9
) and prove a natural conjecture in the field.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Function spaces

For
p\geq 1
and measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, let

\|f\|_{p}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/p}.

We set
\{f\geq t\}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)\geq t\}
for

t\in\mathbb{R}
and use corresponding notation for level sets, etc. We
say that
f
is non-zero if
\{f\neq 0\}
has positive measure, and we identify
functions that are equal up to a set of measure zero. For
p\geq 1
, let

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Big\{f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}:f\text{ is measurable},\,\|f\|_{p}<\infty\Big\}.

Here and below, when we use measurability and related notions, we refer
to the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the indicator function
\operatorname{1}_{E}
is defined
by
\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)=1
for
x\in E
and
\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)=0
otherwise. We say
that two measurable sets are equivalent if their indicator functions are
equal up to a set of measure zero.

Let
0<s<1
. We define the
s
-fractional Sobolev space as

W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Big\{f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y<\infty\Big\}.

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we say that
f
admits a weak gradient
if there exists a vector field

\nabla f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
with measurable components such
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\nu(x),\nabla f(x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\operatorname{div}\nu(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}x

for every
\nu\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the set of smooth vector
fields
\nu:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
with compact support and

\operatorname{div}\nu
denotes the divergence of
\nu
. We set

W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\big\{f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):|\nabla f|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big\}.

Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the class of Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For

f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we say that
f
is a function of bounded variation
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if there is a finite vector-valued Radon measure

\mathrm{D}f:\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\nu(x),\sigma_{f}(x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\operatorname{div}\nu(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}x

for every
\nu\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where

|\mathrm{D}f|:\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to[0,\infty)
denotes the variation measure
of
\mathrm{D}f
and
\sigma_{f}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
the Radon–Nikodym
derivative of
\mathrm{D}f
with respect to
|\mathrm{D}f|
. We write

BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the space of
L^{1}
functions of bounded variation.
For more information on functions of bounded variation, we refer to

[
9
, Chapter 5]
.

2.2.
Symmetrization

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel set of finite measure. The Schwarz
symmetral of
E
, denoted by
E^{\star}
, is the centered Euclidean ball
with the same volume as
E
.

For a non-negative integrable function
f
and
t>0
, the superlevel set

\{f\geq t\}
is a measurable set of finite measure. The layer cake formula
states that

f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and allows us to recover the function
from its superlevel sets. The Schwarz symmetral of
f
, denoted by

f^{\star}
, is defined by

f^{\star}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}^{\star}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence,
f^{\star}
is determined by the properties
of being radially symmetric and having superlevel sets that are balls of
the same measure as the superlevel sets of
f
. Note that

f^{\star}
is often called symmetric decreasing rearrangement of

f
.

Let
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. The Steiner symmetral of
E
in the direction

\xi
, denoted by
E^{\xi}
, is defined by the property that for every
line
L
parallel to
\xi
, the set
L\cap E^{\xi}
is an interval of
the same length as
L\cap E
and symmetric with respect to the subspace
orthogonal to
\xi
.

For a non-negative integrable function
f
, the Steiner symmetral

f^{\xi}
is defined by

f^{\xi}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}^{\xi}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.3.
Star bodies

For a set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is star-shaped (with respect
to the origin), the gauge function

\|\cdot\|_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
is defined as

\|x\|_{K}=\inf\{\lambda>0:x\in\lambda K\},

and the radial function

\rho_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to[0,\infty]
as

\rho_{K}(x)=\|x\|_{K}^{-1}=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0:\lambda x\in K\}.

We call
K
a star body if its radial function is strictly positive and
continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

We say that a star-shaped set is
p
-convex with
0<p\leq 1
if

\|x+y\|_{K}^{p}\leq\|x\|_{K}^{p}+\|y\|_{K}^{p}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A
p
-convex body is a star body that
is also
p
-convex. For more information on
p
-convex sets, see, for example,

[
19
]
.

For star bodies
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p\in\mathbb{R}
, define
the dual mixed volume as

\tilde{V}_{p}(K,L)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n-p}\rho_{L}(\xi)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Note that

\tilde{V}_{p}(K,K)=|K|.

The dual mixed volume inequality (see

[
36
, Section 9.3]
or
[
13
, B.29]
) states that
for
p<0
or
p>n
,

(11)

\tilde{V}_{p}(K,L)\geq|K|^{({n-p})/n}|L|^{p/n},

and the reverse inequality holds for
0<p<n
. Equality holds for

p\neq 0,n
if and only if
K
and
L
are dilates, where we say that
star bodies
K
and
L
are dilates if
\rho_{K}=c\,\rho_{L}
on

{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
for some
c>0
.

For star bodies
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define

(12)

\tilde{V}_{\log}(K,L)=\frac{1}{n|K|}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n}\log\Big(\frac{\rho_{L}(\xi)}{\rho_{K}(\xi)}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Note that

\tilde{V}_{\log}(K,L)=\lim_{p\to 0}\log\Big(\frac{\tilde{V}_{p}(K,L)}{|K|}\Big)^{1/p}.

By (
11
), this implies that

(13)

\tilde{V}_{\log}(K,L)\leq\frac{1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{|L|}{|K|}\Big)

for star bodies
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(cf.
[
14
]
).

2.4.
Convex bodies and the Petty projection inequality

A set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a (proper) convex body if it
is compact and convex and has non-empty interior. For a convex body

K
, the support function
h_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined
as

h_{K}(y)=\max\{\langle x,y\rangle:x\in K\}.

A convex body
K
that contains the origin in its interior is also a star
body. The polar body of such
K
is defined as

K^{*}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 1\text{ for all }x\in K\}

and is a convex body containing the origin in its interior, while

\|x\|_{K}=h_{K^{*}}(x)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a convex body, the projection body is defined
as the convex body whose support function for
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
is

h_{\operatorname{\Pi}K}(\xi)=|\operatorname{proj}_{\xi^{\perp}}(K)|_{n-1},

where
\operatorname{proj}_{\xi^{\perp}}
denotes the orthogonal projection to the
hyperplane,
\xi^{\perp}
, orthogonal to
\xi
and

|\cdot|_{n-1}
stands for
(n-1)
-dimensional volume in

\xi^{\perp}
. Using the surface area measure
S(K,\cdot)
of
K
, we
can also write

h_{\operatorname{\Pi}K}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|\langle\xi,\eta\rangle|\,\mathrm{d}S(K,\eta)

for
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. Projection bodies were introduced by Minkowski, and

h_{\operatorname{\Pi}K}(\xi)
is called the brightness of
K
in the direction

\xi
(see
[
13
,
36
]
for the definition of surface
area measure and more information on projection bodies).

The important Petty projection inequality
[
32
]
states that for
a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(14)

|K|^{(n-1)/n}\leq\frac{\omega_{n}}{\omega_{n-1}}|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!K|^{-1/n}

with equality precisely for ellipsoids, where
\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!K
is the polar body
of
\operatorname{\Pi}K
. A new proof using Theorem
1
(and hence the
Riesz rearrangement inequality) is given in Section
7
. A previous proof of (
14
) that also
uses the Riesz rearrangement inequality and the so-called convolution square
of a convex body was given by Schmuckenschläger

[
35
]
. For generalizations of projection bodies of
convex bodies, see, for example,

[
3
,
7
,
21
,
17
,
27
,
30
,
23
]
,
and for further results related to the Sobolev–Zhang inequality, see,
for example,
[
18
,
29
,
28
,
31
,
40
]
.

2.5.
Anisotropic fractional Sobolev norms and perimeters

Let
0<s<1
and let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a star body. For

f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the anisotropic
s
-fractional Sobolev
norm of
f
with respect to
K
is

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

It was defined in
[
24
]
for
K
a convex body (also, see

[
25
]
). For a measurable set

E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the anisotropic
s
-fractional perimeter
with respect to a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
was introduced in

[
24
]
as

P_{s}(E,K)=\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}\frac{1}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
E^{c}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash E
is the complement of
E
in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The same definition will be used for a star body

K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The anisotropic
s
-fractional perimeter of

E
with respect to
K
is half of the anisotropic
s
-fractional Sobolev
norm of
\operatorname{1}_{E}
with respect to
K
. For a star body
K
and measurable

E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, this implies that
P_{s}(E,K)<\infty
if and
only if
\operatorname{1}_{E}\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If
K
is the Euclidean
unit ball, we write
P_{s}(E)
and obtain the well-known Euclidean

s
-fractional perimeter of
E
. We will use that

(15)

P_{s}({B^{n}})=\frac{2^{1-s}\pi^{(n-1)/2}n\omega_{n}}{s(n-s)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-s}{2}\right)}=\frac{2^{1-s}n\omega_{n}\omega_{n-s}}{s(1-s)\omega_{1-s}}

where
\omega_{q}=\pi^{q/2}/\Gamma(q/2+1)
for
q>0
and

\Gamma
is the gamma function. This can be obtained from the results
in
[
10
]
as described in
[
16
]
.

A simple way to obtain (
15
) is to use the Blaschke–Petkantschin
formula
[
37
, Theorem 7.2.7]
:

\displaystyle P_{s}({B^{n}})

\displaystyle=\int_{{B^{n}}\cap L\neq\emptyset}\int_{{B^{n}}\cap L}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash{B^{n}})\cap L}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}H^{1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}H^{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+s}}\,\mathrm{d}L

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int_{\xi^{\perp}}\int_{{B^{n}}\cap(z+\mathbb{R}\xi)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash{B^{n}})\cap(z+\mathbb{R}\xi)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}H^{1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}H^{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+s}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{n\omega_{n}(n-1)\omega_{n-1}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{|x|\leq\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}\int_{|y|\leq\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}H^{1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}H^{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+s}}\,t^{n-2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{n\omega_{n}(n-1)\omega_{n-1}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{1-s}{2}}t^{n-2}\,\mathrm{d}t\int_{|x|\leq 1}\int_{|y|\leq 1}\!\!\frac{\,\mathrm{d}H^{1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}H^{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)n\omega_{n-1}\omega_{n}}{2^{s}s(1-s)}\,{\rm B}\biggl(\frac{1-s}{2}+1,\frac{n-1}{2}\biggr)

where the outer integration in the first integral is on the affine Grassmannian
of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and we use the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff
measure for the direction of lines and the
(n-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue
measure in the orthogonal complement of a line. Here
H^{1}
denotes the

1
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and
\mathbb{R}\xi
is the line with
direction
\xi
while
\rm B
is the beta function defined by

{\rm B}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}dt
. For the optimal constant
in (
1
), we get

(16)

\alpha_{n,s}=\frac{\omega_{n}^{(n-s)/n}}{2P_{s}({B^{n}})}

since there is equality in (
1
) for indicator functions
of balls.

The following fractional co-area formula is a consequence of Fubini’s theorem
(cf.
[
38
, Example (2.9)]
):

\displaystyle\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

(17)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)-\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)|}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{s}(\{f\geq t\},K)\,\mathrm{d}t

for non-negative
f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a star body

K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.
Fractional polar projection bodies

Let
0<s<1
and
f\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We discuss properties of the

s
-fractional polar projection body
\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f
, that is, the star-shaped
set defined through its gauge function for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as

(18)

\|{\xi}\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\frac{f(x+t\xi)-f(x)}{t}\Big|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Notice that
\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}
is a
1
-homogeneous function.

We start with a simple observation. Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a star
body. Using (
18
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(y+z)-f(y)|}{\|z\|_{K}^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}y

(19)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\|t\xi\|_{K}^{-n-s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{|f(y+t\xi)-f(y)|}\,t^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}{\|\xi\|_{K}^{-n-s}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\Big\|\frac{f(\cdot+t\xi)-f(\cdot)}{t}\Big\|_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n+s}\rho_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}(\xi)^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

We will use the following result to rewrite this in a more convenient way.

Proposition 2
.

For non-zero
f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the set
\,\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f
is
an origin-symmetric
s
-convex body with the origin in its interior. Moreover,
there is
c>0
depending only on
f
such that

\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f\subseteq c(1-s)^{1/s}\,{B^{n}}
for every
s\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

First, note that since for
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
and
t>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|{f(x-t\xi)-f(x)}|\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)-f(x+t\xi)|\,\mathrm{d}x,

the set
\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f
is origin-symmetric.

Next, we show that
\rho_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}
is bounded by

c(1-s)^{1/s}
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
for some
c>0
depending only on
f
. We take

r>1
large enough so that

\int_{r{B^{n}}}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{2}{3}\|f\|_{1}
. For given

t\in(0,1)
, let
k>0
be an integer such that

2r\leq kt\leq 2r+t
. For
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
, using changes of variables,
the triangle inequality, and the fact that
r{B^{n}}
is disjoint from

r{B^{n}}-kt\xi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x+t\xi)-f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x+it\xi)-f(x+(i-1)t\xi)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x+kt\xi)-f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k}\Big(\int_{r{B^{n}}}(|f(x)|-|f(x+kt\xi)|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 52.0pt+\int_{r{B^{n}}-kt\xi}(|f(x+kt\xi)|-|f(x)|)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t}{2r+t}\Big(2\int_{r{B^{n}}}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x-2\int_{(r{B^{n}})^{c}}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2t}{2r+1}\biggl(\frac{2}{3}\|f\|_{1}-\frac{1}{3}\|f\|_{1}\biggr).

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\left\|\frac{f(\cdot+t\xi)-f(\cdot)}{t}\right\|_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\|f\|_{1}}{3(2r+1)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\|f\|_{1}}{3(2r+1)}\frac{1}{1-s},

which implies that
\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f\subseteq c(1-s)^{1/s}\,{B^{n}}
for a constant

c>0
depending only on
f
.

Next, we show that
\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}
is sublinear on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, for
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the triangle
inequality and a change of variables show that

(20)

\begin{split}\|\xi+\eta\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}&=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}\|f(\cdot+t\xi+t\eta)-f(\cdot)\|_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}(\|f(\cdot+t\xi+t\eta)-f(\cdot+t\xi)\|_{1}+\|f(\cdot+t\xi)-f(\cdot)\|_{1})\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\|\xi\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}+\|\eta\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s},\end{split}

which shows that
\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f
is an
s
-convex set.

Now, we show that
\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f
has the origin in its interior. Using the
relation (
19
) with
K={B^{n}}
, we get

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\|\xi\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

which is finite since
f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We choose
r>0
large
enough so that the set

A=\{\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}:\|\xi\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}<r\}
has positive

(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure and a basis

\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\}\subseteq A
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Writing

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\xi_{i}
and using (
20
), we get

(21)

\begin{split}\|x\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}&\leq\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{s}\|\xi_{i}\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}\Big)^{1/s}\\
&\leq r^{1/s}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{s}\Big)^{1/s}\\
&\leq r^{1/s}n^{1/s-1/2}\,\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq r^{1/s}n^{1/s-1/2}\|\Xi^{-1}\|\,|x|,\end{split}

where
\|\Xi^{-1}\|
is the operator norm of the inverse of the matrix
with columns
\xi_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. This shows that

\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f
has the origin as interior point.

To see that the gauge function is continuous combine (
20
) and (
21
) to obtain

\|\xi+\eta\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}\leq\|\xi\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}+\|\eta\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}\leq\|\xi\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}+d\,|\eta|^{s},

where
d>0
is independent of
\xi
and
\eta
. Applying (
20
) to the vectors
\xi+\eta
and
-\eta
, we get

\|\xi\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}\leq\|\xi+\eta\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}+\|-\eta\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s},

which again by (
21
) implies

\|\xi+\eta\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}\geq\|\xi\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}-d\,|\eta|^{s}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Using that
\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f
is a star body,
we rewrite (
19
) as

(22)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=n\,\tilde{V}_{-s}(K,\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f).

We remark that (
22
) resembles Lutwak’s formula (9.1)
from
[
26
]
,

\frac{n+1}{2}|K|\,V_{1}(L,\Gamma K)=\tilde{V}_{-1}(K,\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!L)

for convex bodies
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
V_{1}
is the first
mixed volume and
\Gamma K
the centroid body of
K
.

4.
The limit case
s\to 1^{-}
of fractional polar projection bodies

We prove (
6
) for functions of bounded variation. For

f\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the polar projection body is defined for

\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
by

\|\xi\|_{\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle|\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x)

(see
[
39
]
). Note that for
f\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, this
definition coincides with (
7
).

Theorem 3
.

Let
f\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
,

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\|\xi\|_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}^{s}=2\,\|\xi\|_{\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f}.

Moreover,

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|^{-s/n}=2\,|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f|^{-1/n}

and

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\tilde{V}_{-s}(K,\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f)=2\,\tilde{V}_{-1}(K,\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f)

for every star body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We require the following two lemmas.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\varphi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a measurable function.
If
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\varphi(t)=\varphi(0)
and

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s_{0}}\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
for some

s_{0}\in(0,1)
, then

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\varphi(0).

Proof.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, we choose
\delta\in(0,1)
so that

0\leq t\leq\delta
implies

|\varphi(t)-\varphi(0)|\leq\varepsilon
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}

\displaystyle\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\delta}t^{-s}\varphi(0)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\delta}t^{-s}(\varphi(t)-\varphi(0))\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\delta}^{\infty}t^{-s}\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta^{1-s}}{1-s}\varphi(0)+A+B

where
(1-s)|A|\leq\varepsilon\delta^{1-s}\leq\varepsilon
and

|B|\leq\delta^{s_{0}-s}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}t^{-s_{0}}\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\delta^{s_{0}-s}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s_{0}}\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

for
s\in(s_{0},1)
. Since
(1-s)B\to 0
and

\delta^{1-s}\to 1
as
s\to 1^{-}
, we can take
s
sufficiently close
to
1
so that
(1-s)|B|\leq\varepsilon
and

|\delta^{1-s}\varphi(0)-\varphi(0)|\leq\varepsilon
, obtaining

\left|(1-s)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t-\varphi(0)\right|\leq 3\varepsilon
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

For
f\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
,

\lim_{t\to 0}\Big\|\frac{f(\cdot+t\xi)-f(\cdot)}{t}\Big\|_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle|\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x).

Proof.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function with compact support,
and write
\operatorname{div}_{x}
for the divergence taken with respect to the variable
x
.

Using (
10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x+t\xi)-f(x)}{t}\,g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\frac{g(x-t\xi)-g(x)}{t}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla g(x-rt\xi),\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\operatorname{div}_{x}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}g(x-rt\xi)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\xi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}g(x-rt\xi)dr\Big)\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}|Df|(x).

Therefore

(23)

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x+t\xi)-f(x)}{t}&\,g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}|Df|(x).\end{split}

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
|\mathrm{D}f|
is a finite Radon measure, the set
of smooth functions with compact support is dense in the space of

L^{1}
functions w.r.t.
|\mathrm{D}f|
(cf.
[
11
, Proposition 7.9]
).
Hence, there is a smooth function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with
compact support such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g(x)-\operatorname{sgn}(\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle)|\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x)<\varepsilon

and
\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1+\varepsilon
. By (
23
),

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)\liminf_{t\to 0}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\frac{f(x+t\xi)-f(x)}{t}\Big|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\frac{f(x+t\xi)-f(x)}{t}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle|\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\,+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(g(x)-\operatorname{sgn}(\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle))\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle|\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x)-\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we obtain

\liminf_{t\to 0}\Big\|\frac{f(\cdot+t\xi)-f(\cdot)}{t}\Big\|_{1}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle|\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x).

For the opposite inequality, notice that

(24)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}h(x-rt\xi)\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x)\right|\leq\|h\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle|\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x)

for every
h\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Define
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
by

\varphi(t)=\Big\|\frac{f(\cdot+t\xi)-f(\cdot)}{t}\Big\|_{1}

and note that
\varphi(t)\leq\frac{2\|f\|_{1}}{t}
for
t>0
. By Lemma
4
and Lemma
5
,

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\Big\|\frac{f(\cdot+t\xi)-f(\cdot)}{t}\Big\|_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle|\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x).

By Proposition
2
, we can use the dominated convergence
theorem to obtain,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}n\,

\displaystyle|(1-s)^{-1/s}\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\Big((1-s)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\Big\|\frac{f(\cdot+t\xi)-f(\cdot)}{t}\Big\|_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{-n/s}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle\sigma_{f}(x),\xi\rangle|\,\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}f|(x)\Big)^{-n}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=n\,|\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f|

and

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}n(1-s)\tilde{V}_{-s}(K,\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n+s}\rho_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f}(\xi)^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=2\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n+1}\rho_{\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f}(\xi)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=2n\,\tilde{V}_{-1}(K,\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f),

which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.
Anisotropic fractional Pólya–Szegő inequalities

Almgren and Lieb
[
2
]
established the following Pólya–Szegő
inequality for fractional Sobolev norms. The equality case was settled
by Frank and Seiringer
[
12
]
. Let
0<s<1
and

n\geq 1
. For non-negative
f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(25)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f^{\star}(x)-f^{\star}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

with equality precisely if
\{f\geq t\}
is equivalent to a ball for almost
every
t>0
.

The following improvement of (
25
) is key to proving the main
theorems in Section
8
. It is a variation of

[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
0<s<1
and
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a star body. For

f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
a non-negative integrable function,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{n+s}_{K}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f^{\star}(x)-f^{\star}(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{n+s}_{K^{\star}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

For non-zero
f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there is equality if and only
if
K
is a centered ellipsoid and for almost every
t>0
, the level set

\{f\geq t\}
is equivalent to an ellipsoid homothetic to
K
or has measure
zero.

The inequality in Theorem
6
(without the equality case)
follows rather directly from the Riesz rearrangement inequality, which
is stated in full generality, for example, in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 7

(Riesz’s rearrangement inequality)
.

For
f,g,k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
non-negative measurable functions,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)k(x-y)g(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{\star}(x)k^{\star}(x-y)g^{\star}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

and the right-hand side is infinite if the left-hand side is infinite.

To establish the equality case in Theorem
6
, we use the
characterization of equality cases in a version of the Riesz rearrangement
inequality due to Burchard
[
6
]
.

Theorem 8

(Burchard)
.

Let
A,B
and
C
be sets of finite positive measure in

\,\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denote by
\alpha,\beta
and
\gamma
the radii
of their Schwarz symmetrals
A^{\star},B^{\star}
and
C^{\star}
. For

\,|\alpha-\beta|<\gamma<\alpha+\beta
, there is equality
in

(26)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{A}(y)\operatorname{1}_{B}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{C}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{A^{\star}}(y)\operatorname{1}_{B^{\star}}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{C^{\star}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

if and only if, up to sets of measure zero,

A=a+\alpha D,B=b+\beta D,C=c+\gamma D,

where
D
is a centered ellipsoid, and
a,b
and
c=a+b
are vectors in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

By the co-area formula (
17
), Theorem
6
is
an immediate consequence of the following result for anisotropic fractional
perimeters. We say that
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is homothetic to

D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
if there are
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\alpha>0
such that
A=a+\alpha\,D
.

Theorem 9
.

Let
0<s<1
and
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a star body. For

E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
measurable,

(27)

\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}\frac{1}{\|x-y\|^{n+s}_{K}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\geq\int_{E^{\star}}\int_{(E^{\star})^{c}}\frac{1}{\|x-y\|^{n+s}_{K^{\star}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

For
E
with finite positive
s
-perimeter, equality holds if and only
if
K
is a centered ellipsoid and
E
is equivalent to an ellipsoid homothetic
to
K
.

Proof.

For
z\neq 0
,

\|z\|_{K}^{-n-s}=\int_{0}^{\infty}k_{t}(z)\,\mathrm{d}t\text{, where }k_{t}(z)=\operatorname{1}_{t^{-1/({n+s})}K}(z),

and using Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}\frac{1}{\|x-y\|^{n+s}_{K}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)\operatorname{1}_{E^{c}}(y)k_{t}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t.

For fixed
t\in(0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)

\displaystyle\operatorname{1}_{E^{c}}(y)k_{t}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

(28)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)-\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)\operatorname{1}_{E}(y)\big)k_{t}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{n}{n+s}}|K||E|-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)\,k_{t}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{E}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

Clearly, the first term is invariant under Schwarz symmetrization. For
the second term, we can apply Theorem
7
to show that the left-hand
side does not increase under Schwarz symmetrization. Thus

(29)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)k_{t}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{E^{c}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{E^{\star}}(x)\,k^{\star}_{t}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{(E^{\star})^{c}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

for
t>0
and integrating this inequality for
t\in(0,\infty)
, we obtain (
27
).

If there is equality in (
27
), it follows from (
28
) that for almost every
t>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)\operatorname{1}_{t^{-1/(n+s)}K}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{E}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

is invariant under Schwarz symmetrization. Now we may apply Theorem
8
with
A=C=E
and
B=t^{-1/({n+s})}K
for suitable

t>0
. Since
A=C
, we have
a=c
, and this implies
b=0
. Hence,

K
is a centered ellipsoid, and
E
is equivalent to an ellipsoid homothetic
to
K
.
∎

To establish corresponding inequalities for Steiner symmetrization, we
require the following result.

Theorem 10

(Rogers
[
33
]
)
.

Let
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. For
f,g,k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
non-negative
and measurable,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)k(x-y)g(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{\xi}(x)k^{\xi}(x-y)g^{\xi}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

We remark that the above theorem is a special case of the inequalities
of Rogers
[
34
]
and Brascamp–Lieb–Luttinger

[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
.

As in the case of Schwarz symmetrization, we obtain the following consequences.

Theorem 11
.

Let
0<s<1
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. If
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a star body,
then

\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}\frac{1}{\|x-y\|^{n+s}_{K}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\geq\int_{E^{\xi}}\int_{(E^{\xi})^{c}}\frac{1}{\|x-y\|^{n+s}_{K^{\xi}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

for

measurable.

Theorem 12
.

Let
0<s<1
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. If

is a star body,
then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{n+s}_{K}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f^{\xi}(x)-f^{\xi}(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{n+s}_{K^{\xi}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

for

6.
Affine fractional Pólya–Szegő inequalities

We establish the following affine version of the fractional Pólya–Szegő
inequality (
25
) by Almgren and Lieb.

Theorem 13
.

For
0<s<1
and non-negative
f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(30)

|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|^{-s/n}\geq|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f^{\star}|^{-s/n}.

There is equality if and only if for almost every
t>0
, the level set

\{f\geq t\}
has measure zero or is homothetic to an ellipsoid (independent
of
t
) up to a set of measure zero.

Proof.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a star body. By Theorem
6
, (
22
), and (
11
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{-s}(K,\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f)

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{V}_{-s}(K^{\star},\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f^{\star})

\displaystyle\geq|K^{\star}|^{({n+s})/{n}}|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f^{\star}|^{-s/n}

\displaystyle=|K|^{({n+s})/{n}}|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f^{\star}|^{-s/n}.

Setting
K=\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|=\tilde{V}_{-s}(\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f,\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f)\geq|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|^{({n+s})/{n}}|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f^{\star}|^{-\frac{s}{n}},

which completes the proof of the inequality. The equality case follows
from the equality case of Theorem
6
.
∎

Multiplying (
30
) by
(1-s)
and letting
s\to 1^{-}
, we obtain
from Theorem
13
and Theorem
3
the following affine
Pólya–Szegő inequality by Cianchi, Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang

[
7
]
(see also
[
31
]
):

|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f|^{-1/n}\geq|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f^{\star}|^{-1/n}

for
f\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Using the same proof as for Theorem
13
for Steiner symmetrization
and replacing Theorem
6
with Theorem
12
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 14
.

Let
0<s<1
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. Then

|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|^{-s/n}\geq|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f^{\xi}|^{-s/n}

for non-negative
f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

As before, we obtain from Theorem
14
and Theorem
3
the following affine inequality:

|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f|^{-1/n}\geq|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!f^{\xi}|^{-1/n}

for
f\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.

7.
Fractional Petty projection inequalities

A set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite measure is a set of finite perimeter
if its indicator function
\operatorname{1}_{E}
is in
BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This allows
us to translate results for functions in
BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to sets of
finite perimeter and finite measure. The following result is an immediate
consequence of the affine fractional Pólya–Szegő inequality from
Theorem
13
.

Theorem 15
.

For
\,0<s<1
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite
s
-perimeter and
finite measure,

|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E|^{-s/n}\geq|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E^{\star}|^{-s/n}

with equality precisely if
E
is equivalent to an ellipsoid.

Here, we write
\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E
for the fractional polar projection body of

\operatorname{1}_{E}
. Note that

\|\xi\|^{s}_{\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\frac{|E\triangle(E-t\xi)|}{t}\,\mathrm{d}t

for
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite
s
-perimeter and finite measure.
Also note that (
22
) implies that

(31)

2\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}\frac{1}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=n\,\tilde{V}_{-s}(K,\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E)

for
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a star body.

From Theorem
15
, we easily obtain the first inequality
of the following result, which we call the fractional Petty projection
inequality.

Theorem 16
.

For
\,0<s<1
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite
s
-perimeter and
finite measure,

(32)

\Big(\frac{|E|}{|{B^{n}}|}\Big)^{{(n-s)}/n}\leq\Big(\frac{|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E|}{|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!{B^{n}}|}\Big)^{-s/n}\leq\frac{P_{s}(E)}{P_{s}({B^{n}})}.

There is equality in the first inequality if and only if
E
is equivalent
to an ellipsoid. There is equality in the second inequality if and only
if
E
is equivalent to a set of constant
s
-fractional brightness.

Here, we say that
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is of constant
s
-fractional
brightness if
\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E
is a dilate of
{B^{n}}
. The second inequality
in (
32
) and its equality case follow directly from (
31
) and the dual mixed volume inequality (
11
).

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter and finite measure.
The perimeter of
E
is defined as
P(E)=|\mathrm{D}\operatorname{1}_{E}|
. By
Dávila’s version of (
2
),

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\frac{P_{s}(E)}{P_{s}({B^{n}})}=\frac{P(E)}{P({B^{n}})}.

Taking the limit
s\to 1^{-}
in (
32
), we obtain from this
and Theorem
3
that

(33)

\Big(\frac{|E|}{|{B^{n}}|}\Big)^{{(n-1)}/n}\leq\Big(\frac{|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!E|}{|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!{B^{n}}|}\Big)^{-1/n}\leq\frac{P(E)}{P({B^{n}})}

for
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite perimeter and finite measure. The
first inequality is the generalized Petty projection inequality, which
was proved by Gaoyong Zhang
[
43
]
for compact sets with piecewise

C^{1}
boundary and in full generality by Tuo Wang
[
39
]
. For

E
a convex body, it is the classical Petty projection inequality (
14
). There is equality in the second inequality of (
33
) if and only if
E
is of constant brightness.

The Steiner inequality for fractional polar projection bodies is contained
in the following result, which is an immediate consequence of Theorem
14
.

Theorem 17
.

Let
\,0<s<1
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. Then

|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E|^{-s/n}\geq|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E^{\xi}|^{-s/n}

for
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite
s
-perimeter and finite Lebesgue
measure.

Taking the limit as
s\to 1^{-}
and using Theorem
3
, we
obtain from Theorem
17
the following Steiner inequality
for polar projection bodies, which was recently established by Youjiang
Lin
[
22
]
,

|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!E|^{-1/n}\geq|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!E^{\xi}|^{-1/n}

for
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a set of finite perimeter and finite Lebesgue
measure and
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. For convex bodies, this inequality was established
by Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang
[
27
]
and for compact sets, by Wang
and Xiao
[
41
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1

Before proving Theorem
1
, we establish an inequality
involving the Lorentz quasi-norm, which might be of independent interest.
For
1\leq q<\infty
and
1\leq r<\infty
, the Lorentz quasi-norm
is defined by

\|f\|_{q,r}=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{r-1}\big|\{|f|\geq t\}\big|^{r/q}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/r}

for measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proposition 18
.

For
\,0<s<1
and
f\in W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n-s},1}\leq\alpha_{n,s}n\omega_{n}^{({n+s})/n}|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|^{-s/n}

There is equality if and only if
f
has constant sign and for almost every

t>0
, the level set
\{|f|\geq t\}
has measure zero or is homothetic
to an ellipsoid (independent of
t
) up to a set of measure zero.

Proof.

First, assume that
f
is non-negative. By the co-area formula (
17
) and (
22
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{s}(\{f\geq t\},K)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=n\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{V}_{-s}(K,\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!\,\{f\geq t\})\,\mathrm{d}t.

Hence, setting
K=\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f
, we obtain from (
22
) and the dual mixed volume inequality (
11
) that

\displaystyle|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{V}_{-s}(\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f,\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!\,\{f\geq t\})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|^{({n+s})/n}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!\,\{f\geq t\}|^{-s/n}\,\mathrm{d}t

and

|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|^{-s/n}\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!\,\{f\geq t\}|^{-s/n}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Since there is equality in (
1
) for indicator functions
of balls, the equality case of the dual mixed volume inequality, applied
to (
31
) for
K={B^{n}}
, shows that

{\alpha_{n,s}n\omega_{n}^{({n+s})/n}}=|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!{B^{n}}|^{s/n}{|{B^{n}}|^{({n-s})/n}}.

By the fractional Petty projection inequality from Theorem
16
, it now follows that

(34)

\begin{split}|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f|^{-s/n}&\geq\frac{|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!{B^{n}}|^{-s/n}}{|{B^{n}}|^{({n-s})/n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{f\geq t\}|^{(n-s)/n}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n,s}n\omega_{n}^{({n+s})/n}}\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n-s},1}.\end{split}

In particular, the last term is finite. The equality case follows from
the equality case of Theorem
16
.

For general
f
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use

|f(x)-f(y)|\geq||f(x)|-|f(y)||
, where equality holds if and only if
f(x)
and
f(y)
are
both non-negative or non-positive. Applying this inequality in the definition
of
\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!f
, we obtain

|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!{|f|}|^{-s/n}\leq|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!{f}|^{-s/n}

with equality if and only if
f
has constant sign for almost everywhere
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Using (
34
) for
|f|
, we obtain
the inequality of the theorem and its equality case.
∎

We include the proof of the following well-known lemma for completeness
(cf.
[
43
]
for
s=1
).

Lemma 19
.

Let
0<s<1
. If
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is measurable, then

(35)

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)^{n/({n-s})}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{({n-s})/n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{g\geq t\}|^{({n-s})/n}\,\mathrm{d}t.

If the right-hand side is finite, then there is equality precisely if

g=c\operatorname{1}_{E}
for some
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite measure and

c\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let the right-hand side of (
35
) be finite. By Fubini’s theorem,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)^{n/({n-s})}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{n-s}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s/({n-s})}|\{g\geq t\}|\,\mathrm{d}t.

Since
r\mapsto|\{g\geq r\}|
is monotone decreasing, we obtain for

t>0
,

\displaystyle t^{s/({n-s})}|\{g\geq t\}|

\displaystyle=\big(t\,|\{g\geq t\}|^{({n-s})/n}\big)^{s/({n-s})}|\{g\geq t\}|^{({n-s})/n}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}|\{g\geq r\}|^{({n-s})/n}\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg)^{s/({n-s})}|\{g\geq t\}|^{({n-s})/n}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-s}{n}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\,\,}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}|\{g\geq r\}|^{({n-s})/n}\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg)^{n/({n-s})}.

Hence

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)^{n/({n-s})}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{({n-s})/n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{g\geq t\}|^{({n-s})/n}\,\mathrm{d}t

and there is equality precisely if
g=c\operatorname{1}_{E}
for some

E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite measure and
c\geq 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

The first inequality follows from Proposition
18
and Lemma
19
applied to

|f|
. The equality case is a consequence of the equality cases of these
inequalities.

For the second inequality, we set
K={B^{n}}
in (

) and apply the dual mixed volume inequality (

) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

There is equality precisely if

9.
Fractional Zhang projection inequalities and radial mean bodies

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body. For
p>-1
, Gardner and
Zhang
[
15
]
defined the radial
p
-th mean body of
E
, by its radial
function for
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
, as

\rho_{\operatorname{R}_{p}E}(\xi)^{p}=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\rho_{E-x}(\xi)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

for
p\neq 0
and as

\log(\rho_{\operatorname{R}_{0}E}(\xi))=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\log(\rho_{E-x}(\xi))\,\mathrm{d}x.

They showed that
\operatorname{R}_{p}E
is a star body for
p>-1
and a convex body
for
p\geq 0
.

Let
0<s<1
. For a star body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a convex body

E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we obtain from Fubini’s theorem that

\displaystyle\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}\frac{1}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\int_{(E-x)^{c}}\frac{1}{\|z\|_{K}^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x

(36)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\int_{E}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n+s}\rho_{E-x}(\xi)^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{|E|}{s}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n+s}\rho_{\operatorname{R}_{-s}E}(\xi)^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Hence, by (
31
),

\tilde{V}_{-s}(K,\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E)=\frac{2|E|}{s}\,\tilde{V}_{-s}(K,\operatorname{R}_{-s}E)

for every star body
K
. By the equality case of the dual mixed volume
inequality (
11
), it follows that

\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E=\Big(\frac{s}{2|E|}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\operatorname{R}_{-s}E

for every convex body
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Hence, we can reformulate the Gardner–Zhang inequalities (
9
) obtained in
[
15
]
to get inequalities for fractional
polar projection bodies. In particular, Theorem 5.5 and Lemma 5.7 from

[
15
]
imply that

\Big(\frac{|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!E|}{|\operatorname{\Pi^{\ast,{\it s}}}\!\Delta|}\Big)^{-s/n}\leq\Big(\frac{|E|}{|\Delta|}\Big)^{{(n-s)}/n}

for
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a convex body and
0<s<1
with equality precisely
if
E
is a simplex, where
\Delta
is any
n
-dimensional simplex in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Letting
s\to 1^{-}
and using Theorem
3
,
we obtain the Zhang projection inequality
[
42
]
(without the equality
case), which was reproved in
[
15
]
:

\Big(\frac{|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!E|}{|\operatorname{\Pi}^{*}\!\Delta|}\Big)^{-1/n}\leq\Big(\frac{|E|}{|\Delta|}\Big)^{{(n-1)}/n}

for
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a convex body.

Conversely, we can reformulate Theorem
16
for radial mean
bodies and obtain sharp affine isoperimetric inequalities for radial

p
-th mean bodies for
-1<p<0
. Using a variation of our approach to Theorem
16
, we obtain the following result. We have included the
case
p=n
from
[
15
, Lemma 5.7]
in the statement of the result.

Theorem 20
.

If
\,E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body, then

(37)

\begin{array}[]{r@{\hspace*{3pt}}c@{\hspace*{3pt}}ll}\displaystyle\frac{|\operatorname{R}_{p}E|}{|E|}\hskip 3.0&\leq\hfil\hskip 3.0&\displaystyle\frac{|\operatorname{R}_{p}{B^{n}}|}{|{B^{n}}|}&\quad\text{ for }\,-1<p<n,\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{|\operatorname{R}_{p}E|}{|E|}\hskip 3.0&\geq\hfil\hskip 3.0&\displaystyle\frac{|\operatorname{R}_{p}{B^{n}}|}{|{B^{n}}|}&\quad\text{ for }\,p>n,\end{array}

with equality if and only if
E
is an ellipsoid. Here,

\frac{|\operatorname{R}_{p}{B^{n}}|}{|{B^{n}}|}=\displaystyle\Big(\frac{2^{p+1}\omega_{n+p}}{(p+1)\omega_{n}\omega_{p+1}}\Big)^{n/p}

for
p>-1
and
p\neq 0,n
, and

\frac{|\operatorname{R}_{0}{B^{n}}|}{|{B^{n}}|}=2^{n}e^{\frac{n}{2}(\psi(\frac{1}{2})-\psi(\frac{n}{2}+1))},

where
\psi
is the digamma function. Moreover,

\frac{|\operatorname{R}_{n}E|}{|E|}=1

for every convex body
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

The inequality, including the equality case, for
-1<p<0
is covered in
Theorem
16
. We consider the cases
p\geq 0
. Let

K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a star body. For
p>0
, it follows from Fubini’s

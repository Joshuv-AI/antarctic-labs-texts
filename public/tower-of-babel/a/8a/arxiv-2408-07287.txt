Abductive Reasoning in a Paraconsistent Framework
Meghyn Bienvenu, Katsumi Inoue, Daniil Kozhemiachenko

Abstract
We explore the problem of explaining observations starting from a classically inconsistent theory by adopting a paraconsistent framework. We consider two expansions of the well-known Belnap–Dunn paraconsistent four-valued logic 𝖡𝖣 \mathsf{BD} : 𝖡𝖣 ∘ \mathsf{BD}_{\circ} introduces formulas of the form ∘ ϕ \circ\phi (‘the information about ϕ \phi is reliable’), while 𝖡𝖣 △ \mathsf{BD}_{\triangle} augments the language with formulas △ ​ ϕ \triangle\phi (‘there is information that ϕ \phi is true’). We define and motivate the notions of abduction problems and explanations in 𝖡𝖣 ∘ \mathsf{BD}_{\circ} and 𝖡𝖣 △ \mathsf{BD}_{\triangle} and show that they are not reducible to one another. We analyse the complexity of standard abductive reasoning tasks (solution recognition, solution existence, and relevance / necessity of hypotheses) in both logics. Finally, we show how to reduce abduction in 𝖡𝖣 ∘ \mathsf{BD}_{\circ} and 𝖡𝖣 △ \mathsf{BD}_{\triangle} to abduction in classical propositional logic, thereby enabling the reuse of existing abductive reasoning procedures.

Introduction

Logic-based abduction is an important form of reasoning with multiple applications in artificial intelligence, including diagnosis and commonsense reasoning
[
\citeauthoryearEiter and Gottlob1995
]
. An
abduction problem
can be generally formulated as a pair
\langle\Gamma,\chi\rangle
consisting of a set of formulas
\Gamma
(
theory
) and a formula
\chi
(
observation
) s.t.
\Gamma\not\models\chi
, and the task is to find an
explanation
, i.e., a formula
\phi
s.t.
\Gamma,\phi\models\chi
. Of course, not
every
formula is intuitively acceptable as an explanation which is why there are usually some restrictions on
\phi
. In particular,
\Gamma\cup\{\phi\}
should be consistent,
\phi
should not entail
\chi
by itself nor contain atoms not occurring in
\Gamma\cup\{\phi\}
,
\phi
should be syntactically restricted so as to be easily understandable, and
\phi
should constitute a weakest possible (or minimal) explanation, cf. discussion in
[
\citeauthoryearMarquis2000
, §4.2]
or
[
\citeauthoryearAliseda2006
, §3.3]
. Most commonly, the third desiderata is enforced by requiring abductive solutions to take the form of
terms
(conjunctions of literals), in which case the logically weakest solutions are simply the subset-minimal ones.

Note, however, that in classical propositional logic (
\mathsf{CPL}
) any
contradictory
theory is inconsistent. Thus, there is no explanation of
\chi
from a contradictory
\Gamma
. This can be circumvented in two ways. First, by
repairing

\Gamma
, i.e., making it consistent and then proceeding as usual (cf., e.g.,
[
\citeauthoryearDu, Wang, and Shen2015
]
). Second, by moving to a
paraconsistent
logic. The characteristic feature of such logics is the failure of the explosion principle —
p,\neg p\not\models q
.

Abduction in Paraconsistent Logics

The question of how to employ paraconsistent logics to perform abductive reasoning on classically inconsistent theories has already generated interest in the philosophical logic community. For example,
[
\citeauthoryearCarnielli2006
]
considers abduction in a three-valued logic called
Logic of Formal Inconsistency
(
\mathbf{LFI1}
), obtaining by expanding the language of
\mathsf{CPL}
(classical propositional logic) with new connectives
\bullet\phi
and
\circ\phi
(read ‘
\phi
has a non-classical value’ or ‘the information about
\phi
is unreliable’ and ‘
\phi
has a classical value’ or ‘the information about
\phi
is reliable’, respectively). More recently,
[
\citeauthoryearBueno-Soler et al.2017
]
and
[
\citeauthoryearChlebowski, Gajda, and Urbański2022
]
considered abductive explanations in the
minimal Logic of Formal Inconsistency
(
\mathbf{mbC}
), and
[
\citeauthoryearRodrigues et al.2023
]
consider abduction in a four-valued
Logic of Evidence and Truth
(
\mathsf{LET_{K}}
).

These studies showcase the interest of paraconsistent abduction, but as they issue from a different research community, the formulation of abductive solutions and the questions that are explored depart from those typically considered in knowledge representation and reasoning (KR). In particular, these works allow
arbitrary
formulas as solutions (rather than terms). Moreover, to the best of our knowledge, there are no results on the complexity of paraconsistent reasoning tasks (e.g., solution existence). Also, in
\mathbf{mbC}
and
\mathsf{LET_{K}}
,
\circ
and
\bullet
do not have truth-functional semantics, which complicates the comparison with classical logic and the reuse of established techniques.

Abduction in Belnap–Dunn Logic

The preceding considerations motivate us to revisit paraconsistent abduction by taking a KR perspective and adopting the well-known paraconsistent propositional logic

Importantly,
\mathsf{BD}_{\triangle}

Example 1

Assume that a valuable item was stolen and we have contradictory information about the theft: it was stolen by either Paula or Quinn, but both of them claim to have an alibi. This can be represented with

\mathsf{BD}_{\circ}

\circ p

\mathsf{BD}_{\triangle}
as follows:

can be explained by assuming that
Paula’s alibi was not disputed
—
\neg\triangle p
.

Contributions

In this paper, we formalize abductive reasoning in
\mathsf{BD}
and explore its computational properties. We consider abductive solutions defined as terms in
\mathsf{BD}_{\triangle}
and
\mathsf{BD}_{\circ}
and compare the classes of abduction problems that can be solved in
\mathsf{BD}_{\triangle}
and
\mathsf{BD}_{\circ}
. We establish an (almost complete) picture of the complexity of standard abductive reasoning tasks (solution recognition, solution existence, and relevance and necessity of hypotheses) in
\mathsf{BD}_{\triangle}
and
\mathsf{BD}_{\circ}
, for two entailment-based notions of minimality. We show how
\mathsf{BD}
abduction problems can be reduced to abduction in classical propositional logic (and vice-versa), which makes it possible
to apply classical consequence-finding methods to generate abductive solutions
in
\mathsf{BD}_{\triangle}
and
\mathsf{BD}_{\circ}
.

6
Generating Solutions to
\mathsf{BD}
Abduction Problems by Reduction to
\mathsf{CPL}

In this section, we show how to apply classical consequence-finding procedures to generate solutions for
\mathsf{BD}
abduction problems, by reducing
\mathsf{BD}
abduction to
\mathsf{CPL}
abduction.

We first observe that Lemma
1
allows us to faithfully translate abduction problems with
\mathcal{L}_{\triangle}
-solutions into
\mathsf{CPL}
.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\langle\Gamma,\chi,{\mathsf{H}}\rangle
be a
\mathsf{BD}
abduction problem. Then
\phi
is a (
\models_{\mathsf{BD}}
-, theory-minimal, proper)
\mathcal{L}_{\triangle}
-solution of
\mathbb{P}
iff
\phi^{\mathsf{cl}}
is a (
\models_{\mathsf{CPL}}
-, theory-minimal, proper) solution of
\mathbb{P}^{\mathsf{cl}}_{\triangle}=\langle\Gamma^{\mathsf{cl}},\chi^{\mathsf{cl}},{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{cl}}_{\triangle}\rangle
with
{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{cl}}_{\triangle}=\{p^{+}\mid p\in{\mathsf{H}}\}\cup\{p^{-}\mid\neg p\in{\mathsf{H}}\}\cup\{{\sim}l\mid\neg\triangle l\in{\mathsf{H}}\}
.

The translation of
\mathsf{BD}_{\circ}
abduction into
\mathsf{CPL}
abduction is, however, more complicated.

Definition 10
.

Let
\phi\!=\!\bigwedge\limits^{m}_{i=1}p_{i}\!\wedge\!\bigwedge\limits^{m^{\prime}}_{i^{\prime}=1}\!\neg p_{i^{\prime}}\!\wedge\!\bigwedge\limits^{n}_{j=1}\!\circ p_{j}\!\wedge\!\bigwedge\limits^{n^{\prime}}_{j^{\prime}=1}\!\bullet p_{j^{\prime}}
be an atomic
\mathcal{L}_{\circ}

be a finite set of propositional variables. The

of

\displaystyle\phi^{\sim}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge\limits^{m}_{i=1}p^{+}_{i}\!\wedge\!\bigwedge\limits^{m^{\prime}}_{i^{\prime}=1}p^{-}_{i^{\prime}}\!\wedge\!\bigwedge\limits^{n}_{j=1}p^{\circ}_{j}\!\wedge\!\bigwedge\limits^{n^{\prime}}_{j^{\prime}=1}\!\!{\sim}p^{\circ}_{j^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\phi^{\leftrightarrow}_{X}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge\limits_{q\in X}\!\!({\sim}q^{\circ}\!\leftrightarrow\!(q^{+}\!\leftrightarrow\!q^{-}))

\displaystyle\phi^{\circ}_{X}

\displaystyle=\phi^{\sim}\wedge\phi^{\leftrightarrow}_{X}

(7)

where the

Lemma 3

Let

Theorem 17
.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\langle\Gamma,\chi,{\mathsf{H}}\rangle
be a
\mathsf{BD}

iff
\phi^{\sim}

{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{cl}}_{\circ}=\{p^{+}\mid p\in{\mathsf{H}}\}\cup\{p^{-}\mid\neg p\in{\mathsf{H}}\}\cup\{p^{\circ}\mid\circ p\in{\mathsf{H}}\}\cup\{{\sim}p^{\circ}\mid\bullet p\in{\mathsf{H}}\}

Theorems

and

show that we can use classical techniques of abductive reasoning (such as the ones based upon consequence finding, cf.

\citeauthoryearInoue1992

\citeauthoryeardel Val2000

\citeauthoryearMarquis2000

\mathsf{BD}

Example 4
.

Recall Example

. We consider two
\mathsf{BD}

\mathbb{P}_{\triangle}=\langle\Gamma,\chi,{\mathsf{H}}_{\triangle}\rangle

\mathbb{P}_{\circ}=\langle\Gamma,\chi,{\mathsf{H}}_{\circ}\rangle

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=\{p\!\vee\!q,\neg p,\neg q\}

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=q

\displaystyle{\mathsf{H}}_{\triangle}

\displaystyle=\{p,\!\neg p,\!\neg\triangle p,\!\neg\triangle\neg p\}

\displaystyle{\mathsf{H}}_{\circ}

\displaystyle=\{p,\neg p,\circ p,\bullet p\}

and

\displaystyle=\{p^{+}\vee q^{+},p^{-},q^{-}\}

\displaystyle\chi^{\mathsf{cl}}

7
Discussion and Future Work

We have studied abductive reasoning in the four-valued paraconsistent logic
\mathsf{BD}
, motivating and comparing
\mathcal{L}_{\triangle}
- and
\mathcal{L}_{\circ}
-solutions. Our complexity analysis (Table
2
) provides an almost complete picture of the complexity of the main decision problems related to abduction.
In particular, we established that the complexity of solution existence in
\mathsf{BD}_{\triangle}
and
\mathsf{BD}_{\circ}
is not higher than in the classical case
[
\citeauthoryearEiter and Gottlob1995
,
\citeauthoryearCreignou and Zanuttini2006
,
\citeauthoryearPichler and Woltran2010
,
\citeauthoryearPfandler, Pichler, and Woltran2015
]
.
Moreover, by exhibiting reductions of abduction in
\mathsf{BD}_{\triangle}
and
\mathsf{BD}_{\circ}
to abduction in
\mathsf{CPL}
, we have shown that existing procedures for generating abductive solutions in classical logic can be employed for paraconsistent abduction.

A few questions remain open. First, we do not know the exact complexity of theory-minimal solution recognition and relevance. One way to approach this would be to establish the complexity of closely related notion of theory prime implicants in
\mathsf{CPL}

[
\citeauthoryearMarquis1995
]
. There is also the question of how to embed
\mathsf{BD}
abduction problems with
\mathcal{L}_{\circ}
-solutions into classical problems while preserving
\models_{\mathsf{BD}}
-minimal solutions (recall that Theorem
17
only preserves theory-minimal solutions). Also, since some
\mathsf{BD}
abduction problems can be solved by arbitrary
\mathcal{L}_{\circ}
-terms but not atomic ones, it would be interesting to explore the computational properties of
\mathcal{L}_{\circ}
-solutions based upon non-atomic
\mathcal{L}_{\circ}
-terms.

A more general direction for future work is to consider abduction in expansions of
\mathsf{BD}
. Of particular interest are
functionally complete
expansions of
\mathsf{BD}
, e.g., the bi-lattice language expansion or an expansion with a ‘quarter-turn’ connective from
[
\citeauthoryearRuet1996
]
(cf.
[
\citeauthoryearOmori and Sano2015
]
for more details). An important technical question would be whether all
\mathcal{L}_{\triangle}
- and
\mathcal{L}_{\circ}
-solutions can be represented as terms in functionally complete languages. A further challenge is to come up with an intuitive natural-language interpretation of literals and terms in such languages.
Another option is to consider theories containing implicative formulas (cf.
[
\citeauthoryearOmori and Wansing2017
]
for details). This would allow us to define Horn-like fragments of the languages in question. As solution existence in
classical
Horn abduction problems is
\mathsf{NP}
-complete
[
\citeauthoryearCreignou and Zanuttini2006
]
, it makes sense to check whether abduction in Horn
\mathsf{BD}
is also simpler than in the general case.

Additionally, we plan to consider
modal
expansions of
\mathsf{BD}
. Abduction in classical modal logic is well-researched. In particular,
[
\citeauthoryearLevesque1989
]
and
[
\citeauthoryearSakama and Inoue2016
]
study abduction in classical epistemic and doxastic logics;
[
\citeauthoryearMayer and Pirri1995
]
apply tableaux procedures to solution generation in
\mathbf{K}
,
\mathbf{D}
,
\mathbf{T}
, and
\mathbf{S4}
.
[
\citeauthoryearBienvenu2009
]
compares different definitions of prime implicates closely related to abductive solutions and provides complexity results and algorithms for prime implicate recognition and generation in multimodal
\mathbf{K}_{n}
;
[
\citeauthoryearNepomuceno-Fernández, Soler-Toscano, and Velázquez-Quesada2017
]
consider abduction in dynamic epistemic logic. Modal expansions of
\mathsf{BD}
are also well known (cf.
[
\citeauthoryearPriest2008
]
and
[
\citeauthoryearDrobyshevich2020
]
). There are also public announcement
[
\citeauthoryearRivieccio2014
]
and dynamic
[
\citeauthoryearSedlár2016
]

\mathsf{BD}
logics. Thus, it is natural to consider abductive reasoning in
modal paraconsistent
framework and see whether classical decision procedures and complexity results can be transferred there.

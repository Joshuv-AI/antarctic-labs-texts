On a Serrin-type overdetermined problem
Celentano A, Nitsch C, Trombetti C

Abstract
In this paper, we prove a Serrin-type result for an elliptic system of equations, overdetermined with both Dirichlet and a generalized Neumann conditions. With this tool, we characterize the critical shapes under volume constraint of some domain functionals. MSC 2020: 35B06; 35J57; 35N25. Keywords: Overdetermined problem; radial symmetry; first eigenvalue; torsional rigidity; domain derivative.

1
Introduction

In the study of overdetermined elliptic problems, Serrin’s pioneering work
[
18
]
marked a turning point. He proved the radial symmetry property of the solution to torsion problem, overdetermined with a Neumann boundary condition. More precisely, main result of
[
18
]
states the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a connected, bounded and open set whose boundary is of class
C^{2}
. If there exists a solution
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=1&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=c&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\end{cases}

(1)

where
\nu
is the inward normal to
\partial\Omega
. Then
\Omega=B_{R}(x_{0})
is a ball of radius
R
, centered at
x_{0}
and
u(x)=\frac{R^{2}-|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{2n}
.

As remarked in
[
18
]
, Theorem
1.1
still holds if Poisson equation in (
1
) is replaced by a non linear elliptic equation, whose structure satisfies some suitable conditions, provided the solution
u
has constant sign in
\Omega
. For instance, Poisson semilinear equation
-\Delta u=f(u)
can be taken into account.

The proof relies on two main tools: the moving planes method and a refinement of the Hopf Boundary Lemma
[
18
, Lemma 1]
. The former was initially introduced by Alexandrov in
[
1
]
to prove that the only compact, embedded
(n-1)
-dimensional
C^{2}
hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with constant mean curvature are the spheres. Serrin recovered this technique and adapted it to the PDEs framework. Since then, many generalization of this method have been made. We mention just a few of these. In
[
12
]
, it was extended and applied to show symmetry and monotonicity properties of positive solutions to a class of elliptic problems with Dirichlet boundary condition. Afterwards, it was improved in order to treat more general domains with weaker regularity assumptions on the solution, see
[
3
,
10
]
. The moving planes method can be applied successfully
also in the case of
p
-Laplace operator
[
8
,
9
]
. For a deep analysis involving also a quantitative approach, we refer to
[
7
]
.

We point out that many different proofs of Theorem
1.1
and further extensions are currently available in the literature, see
[
17
]
for a survey on this topic. Weinberger provided an alternative short proof in
[
19
]
, based on the use of a suitable auxiliary function, called
P
-function, and Pohožaev identity. These techniques were extended to handle a general quasilinear operator in
[
11
]
. An other approach, which combines Pohožaev identity together with Newton inequalities, is shown in
[
4
]
. With these tools, authors are also able to deal with a class of fully non linear equations, namely Hessian equations. We mention also the work
[
6
]
, where a deep connection between shape optimization and overdetermined problems is emphasized. Authors construct a shape functional minimized by solutions to Serrin’s problem. They infer the uniqueness of minimizer and then, expressing the optimality condition by means of the domain derivative, they show that the mean curvature of the boundary of the minimizer must be constant. Hence, it must be a ball.

In this paper, we deal with an extension of the Serrin’s problem to system of elliptic equations with Dirichlet datus, overdetermined with an other boundary condition which involves the normal derivatives of solutions and generalizes the Neumann one. Constraints of this type arise from shape optimization context, by imposing the optimality condition computed via domain derivative, see for instance
[
5
]
. The main result of this work is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a bounded, open, connected set of class
C^{2}
. For
i=1,...,m
, let
u_{i}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
,
u_{i}>0
in
\Omega
, be solutions to

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{i}=f_{i}(u_{i})&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
u_{i}=0&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\\
F\left(\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\nu},...,\frac{\partial u_{m}}{\partial\nu}\right)=c&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\end{cases}

(2)

where
\nu
is the inner unit normal to
\partial\Omega
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Let us assume that
f_{i}
and
F
satisfy the following assumptions:

(i)

f_{i}=g_{i}+h_{i}
, with
g_{i}
locally Lipschitz in
\R
and
h_{i}
non decreasing,

(ii)

F:[0,+\infty)^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
F\in C^{1}\left((0,+\infty)^{m}\right)
,

(iii)

\forall\,i\in\{1,...,m\}
,
\forall\,(x_{1},...,x_{m})\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
,
\forall h>0
, it holds

F(x_{1},...,x_{i},...,x_{m})\leq F(x_{1},...,x_{i}+h,...,x_{m})

(iv)

\exists\,j\in\{1,..,m\}
such that
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}}>0
in
(0,+\infty)^{m}
.

Then
\Omega
is a ball of radius
R
and the solutions
u_{i}
are radially symmetric. Moreover,

\dfrac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial r}<0\quad\text{for }\{0<r<R\},\,\forall\,i\in\{1,...,m\}.

The proof is based on moving planes method and the arguments used are inspired by those of Serrin. In the end, we provide an application of the previous result to the study of critical shapes of some domain functionals involving the torsional rigidity
T(\Omega)
and the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
of Dirichlet-Laplacian.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open, bounded, connected set of class
C^{2,\gamma}
for
\gamma\in(0,1)
. If
\Omega
is a critical shape under volume constraint for the functional
J(\Omega)=T^{\alpha}(\Omega)\lambda_{1}^{\beta}(\Omega)
, with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
not both zero and such that
\alpha\beta\leq 0
, then it is a ball.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2.1
, we provide a detailed exposure of the moving planes method, recalling also some useful results proved in
[
12
]
. In Section
2.2
, we give introductory notions and tools on the torsional rigidity, the first eigenvalue of Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary condition and domain derivative of shape functionals. Finally, in Section
3
we prove Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
.

2
Notions and preliminaries

2.1
The moving planes method

We provide a geometric description of the procedure of moving up planes perpendicular to a fixed direction, until a critical position is reached.
Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. Assigned a unit vector
\gamma
in
\R^{n}
and a real value
\lambda
, we define the hyperplane
T_{\lambda}=\{x\in\R^{n}\,|\,\gamma\cdot x=\lambda\}
, that is the boundary of the open halfspace
H_{\lambda}=\{x\in\R^{n}\,|\,\gamma\cdot x>\lambda\}
. If
\lambda
is large enough, then
T_{\lambda}\cap\overline{\Omega}=\emptyset
. Decreasing
\lambda
, the hyperplane
T_{\lambda}
moves continuously toward
\Omega
along the direction
-\gamma
and it begins to intersect
\overline{\Omega}
for
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
. If
\lambda<\lambda_{0}
, the open cap
\Sigma(\lambda)=H_{\lambda}\cap\Omega
is nonempty. We denote respectively by
\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda)
and
x^{\lambda}
, the reflection of the open cap
\Sigma(\lambda)
and of the point
x
in the hyperplane
T_{\lambda}
. At the beginning,
\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda)
will be in
\Omega
and as
\lambda
decreases, the reflected cap
\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda)
will remain in
\Omega
, at least until one of the following two events occurs:

(i)

\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda)
becomes internally tangent to
\partial\Omega
at some point
\overline{x}
not on
T_{\lambda}
,

(ii)

T_{\lambda}
reaches a position such that it is orthogonal to
\partial\Omega
at some point
\overline{y}
.

We denote by
T_{\lambda_{c}}
the hyperplane
T_{\lambda}
when it first reaches one of these positions. Now, we recall in a special form a result proved by Gidas, Ni and Nirenberg in
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be an open, connected set of class
C^{2}
. Let
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
,
u>0
in
\Omega
, be a solution to

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f(u)&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\end{cases}

(3)

with
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}
is locally Lipschitz in
\R
and
f_{2}
is non decreasing. Then

\nabla u(x)\cdot\gamma<0,\quad u(x)<u(x^{\lambda})\quad\forall\,x\in\Sigma(\lambda),\quad\forall\,\lambda\in(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{c}).

(4)

Thus
\nabla u\cdot\gamma<0
in
\Sigma(\lambda_{c})
. Furthermore if
\nabla u\cdot\gamma=0
at some point on
\Omega\cap T_{\lambda_{c}}
then necessarily
u
and
\Omega
are symmetric in the hyperplane
T_{\lambda_{c}}
.

Remark 2.1
.

As specified in
[
12
]
, Theorem
2.1
is verified for a class of nonlinear elliptic second order equations. With
\Omega
as before, let
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
be a solution to the following equation

{F(x,u,u_{x_{1}},...,u_{x_{n}},u_{x_{1}x_{1}},...,u_{x_{n}x_{n}})=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,}

(5)

which is elliptic in
\Omega
if there exist positive constants
m,M
such that

{m|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}F_{u_{ij}}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq M|\xi|^{2}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega.}

The function
F(x,z,p_{k},r_{i,j})
satisfies the following conditions:

a)

F
is continuous and it has continuous first derivative with respect to
z,p_{k}
and
r_{i,j}
for all values of these arguments, for every
x\in\Omega
;

b)

the function
g(x)=F(x,0,...,0)
verifies

{g(x)\geq 0\quad\forall\,x\in\partial\Omega\cap\{x_{1}>\lambda_{c}\},\quad\text{or}\quad g(x)<0\quad\forall x\in\partial\Omega\cap\{x_{1}>\lambda_{c}\};}

c)

for every
\lambda
such that
\lambda_{c}\leq\lambda<\lambda_{0}
, for
x\in\Sigma(\lambda)
and all values of the arguments
z,p_{k},r_{i,j}
with
z>0
e
p_{1}<0
, it results

{F(x^{\lambda},z,-p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{n},r_{1,1},-r_{1,\alpha},...,r_{\beta,\gamma})\geq F(x,z,p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{n},r_{1,1},r_{i,j}),}

where
i,j=1,...n
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma=2,...n
.

As a consequence of Theorem
2.1
, the following symmetry result holds
[
12
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}
is a locally Lipschitz function and
f_{2}
is non decreasing. Then, any positive solution
u\in C^{2}(\overline{B})
to the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f(u)&\text{in }B,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial B,\end{cases}

where
B
is a ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of radius
R
, has to be radial and

\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial r}<0,\quad\text{for }\,0<r<R.

In
[
12
]
, Theorem
2.1
is also extended to some domains with corners, by proving a refinement of the Hopf Boundary Lemma
[

reported below, which is a generalization of a result due to Serrin in

where

is a uniformly elliptic operator and its coefficients are uniformly bounded. Assume

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\rho_{x_{i}}\sigma_{x_{j}}\geq 0\quad\text{at O}.

(6)

If this is

a_{ij}\in C^{2}
in

, and that

2.2
The domain derivative

Fixed a bounded and open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the torsional rigidity of
\Omega
is

\displaystyle T(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}u_{\Omega}\,dx,

where
u_{\Omega}
is the torsion function of
\Omega
, that is the unique solution of the boundary value problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=1&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).\\
\end{cases}

Let
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
be the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions. It is known that
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)>0
, we assume it is simple and we indicate by
v_{\Omega}
the normalized corresponding eigenfunction, which is the solution to

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=\lambda_{1}(\Omega)v&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
v\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega),\\
\end{cases}

such that
\|v_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
.

Next, we recall the first order shape derivative of the two functionals
T(\Omega),\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
.

Let
\theta\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a Lipschitz continuous function. For given
t_{0}>0
, we denote by
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{0\leq t<t_{0}}
a family of perturbations of the domain
\Omega
of the form

\Omega_{t}=(I+t\theta)(\Omega),

where
I
is the identity in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
t_{0}
is small enough, then
\Phi(t,x)=I(x)+t\theta(x)
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism for every
0\leq t<t_{0}
. Accordingly,
\Omega_{t}
is measurable and open if and only if
\Omega
is measurable and open.

Definition 2.1
.

The derivative of a given functional
\mathcal{J}
at
\Omega
in the direction
\theta
is defined as

d\mathcal{J}(\Omega,\theta):=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{J}(\Omega_{t})-\mathcal{J}(\Omega)}{t},

(7)

if the limit exists.

For the next two results, we refer to
[
15
, Corollary 5.3.8, Theorem 5.7.1]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded and of class
C^{1}
set. Then

dT(\Omega,\theta)=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}|^{2}\theta\cdot n\,d\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
n
denotes the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded and of class
C^{2}
set. If
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
is a simple eigenvalue, then

d\lambda_{1}(\Omega,\theta)=-\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla v_{\Omega}|^{2}\theta\cdot n\,d\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Now, we consider the volume functional
V(\Omega)=|\Omega|
, which gives the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
. For a perturbation
\Phi
of the type above, it results
[
2
, Section 2.3]

dV(\Omega,\theta)=\int_{\Omega}\text{div}\,\theta\,dx.

Definition 2.2
.

The perturbation
\Phi(t,x)
is called volume preserving if

\int_{\Omega}\text{div}\,\theta\,dx=0.

(8)

Definition 2.3
.

A set
\Omega
is said a critical shape under volume constraint for a functional
\mathcal{J}
if (
7
) vanishes for every choice of
\theta
satisfying (
8
).

3
Proof of the main theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let fix a direction
\gamma
and let apply the procedure described before, until the hyperplane
T_{\lambda}
reaches a critical position. The goal is to prove that
\Omega
is symmetric with respect to the hyperplane
T_{\lambda_{c}}
. Once this fact is proved then the statement follows, since for every direction
\gamma
,
\Omega
would be symmetric with respect to the hyperplane normal to
\gamma
. Moreover, according to the construction,
\Omega
would also be simply connected, then it has to be a ball. In the end, an application of Theorem
2.2
shows that
u_{i}
is radially symmetric and non increasing, for all
i=1,...,m
.

We set
u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}
the maps defined in
\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda_{c})
by

u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}(x)=u_{i}(x^{\lambda_{c}})\quad x\in\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda_{c}),\quad\forall\,i\in\{1,...,m\}.

The functions
u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}
satisfy

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}=f_{i}(u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}})&\mbox{in }\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda_{c}),\\
u_{i}=u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}&\mbox{on }\partial\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda_{c})\cap\partial H_{\lambda_{c}},\\
u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}=0&\mbox{on }\partial\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda_{c})\setminus\partial H_{\lambda_{c}},\\
F\left(\frac{\partial u_{1}^{\lambda_{c}}}{\partial\nu},...,\frac{\partial u_{m}^{\lambda_{c}}}{\partial\nu}\right)=c&\mbox{on }\partial\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda_{c})\setminus\partial H_{\lambda_{c}}.\end{cases}

Since
\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda_{c})
is contained in
\Omega
, it’s possible to consider
u_{i}-u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}
. As a consequence of Theorem
2.1
,
u_{i}-u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}\geq 0
and it solves a linear differential inequality

0\geq h_{i}(u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}})-h_{i}(u_{i})=\Delta(u_{i}-u_{\lambda_{c}})+g_{i}(u_{i})-g_{i}(u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}})=\Delta(u_{i}-u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}})+c_{i}(u_{i}-u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}),

where

{c_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{g_{i}(u_{i}(x))-g_{i}(u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}(x))}{u_{i}(x)-u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}(x)}&\mbox{if }u(x)\neq u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}(x),\\
0&\mbox{if }u(x)=u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}(x).\end{cases}}

An application of the strong maximum principle gives either

u_{i}-u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}>0\quad\text{or}\quad u_{i}-u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}\equiv 0\quad\text{in }\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda_{c}).

The latter case would imply that
\Omega
is symmetric about
T(\lambda_{c})
. Suppose that case
i)
occurs, that is
\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda_{c})
is internally tangent to
\partial\Omega
at a point
\overline{x}
not belonging to
T_{\lambda_{c}}
and assume by contradiction that
u_{i}-u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}>0
in
\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda_{c})
for all
i=1,...,m
. Then Hopf Lemma ensures that

\frac{\partial(u_{i}-u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}})}{\partial\nu(\overline{x})}>0.

Hence, we get

\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\nu(\overline{x})}>\frac{\partial u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}}{\partial\nu(\overline{x})}\geq 0.

Using the hypothesis on the function
F
, we get a contradiction

c=F\left(\frac{\partial u_{1}^{\lambda_{c}}}{\partial\nu(\overline{x})},...,\frac{\partial u_{m}^{\lambda_{c}}}{\partial\nu(\overline{x})}\right)<F\left(\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\nu(\overline{x})},...,\frac{\partial u_{m}}{\partial\nu(\overline{x})}\right)=c.

In the case
ii)
, the proof makes use of Lemma
2.3
. The goal is to prove that
u_{i}-u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}
have in
\overline{y}
a second order zero, for some
i\in\{1,...,m\}
. Now, we fix a coordinate system with the origin at
\overline{y}
, the
x_{n}
axis in the direction of the inward normal to
\partial\Omega
at
\overline{y}
and the
x_{1}
axis in the direction of
\gamma
, that is normal to
T_{\lambda_{c}}
. In this coordinates system, the boundary of
\Omega
is locally described by

x_{n}=\phi(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n-1}),\quad\phi\in C^{2}.

By construction, the function
\phi
satisfies the following properties

\nabla\phi(0)=0,\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x_{1}\partial x_{j}}(0)=0\quad\text{for }j=2,...,n-1.

(9)

Indeed,
\nu(0)=(-\nabla\phi(0),1)=(0,...,0,1)
which gives the former in (
9
). Moreover,
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{1}}
has an extremum point at
0
with respect to all but the first coordinates directions, since
\Sigma^{\prime}(\lambda_{c})\subseteq\Omega
. Let us set

u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})=u_{i}(-x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}).

In order to prove that all the first and second derivatives of
u_{i}
and
u_{i}^{\lambda_{c}}
coincide at
0
, it is enough to show

\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{1}}(0)=0,\quad\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial x_{1}\partial x_{j}}(0)=0\quad\text{for }j=2,...,n.

Since
u_{i}\in C^{2}
, the boundary condition
u_{i}=0
on
\partial\Omega
can be written as

u_{i}(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n-1},\phi)=0\quad\text{for }i=1,...,m.

(10)

Differentiating (
10
) with respect to
x_{j}
for
j=1,...,n-1
, we have

\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{n}}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{j}}=0.

(11)

Evaluating (
11
) at
0
and for
j=1,...,n-1
, it results
\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(0)=0
.
Differentiating (
11
) with respect to
x_{k}
for
k=1,...,n-1
, we get

\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{n}}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial x_{n}\partial x_{k}}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial x_{n}\partial x_{n}}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{n}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}=0.

(12)

Evaluating (
12
) at
0
, for
j=1
and
k=2,...,n-1
, it results
\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial x_{1}\partial x_{k}}(0)=0
.
If it exists
j
such that
\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial x_{n}}(0)=0
, then the normal derivative
\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial\nu}
has a minimum in
0
. Since

Abstract
In this two-part work, we propose an algorithmic framework for solving non-convex problems whose objective function is the sum of a number of smooth component functions plus a convex (possibly non-smooth) or/and smooth (possibly non-convex) regularization function. The proposed algorithm incorporates ideas from several existing approaches such as alternate direction method of multipliers (ADMM), successive convex approximation (SCA), distributed and asynchronous algorithms, and inexact gradient methods. Different from a number of existing approaches however, the proposed framework is flexible enough to incorporate a class of non-convex objective functions, allow distributed operation with and without a fusion center, and include variance reduced methods as special cases. Remarkably, the proposed algorithms are robust to uncertainties arising from random, deterministic, and adversarial sources. The part I of the paper develops two variants of the algorithm under very mild assumptions and establishes first order convergence rate guarantees. The proof developed here allows for generic errors and delays, paving the way for different variance-reduced, asynchronous, and stochastic implementations, outlined and evaluated in the part II.

We summarize the contributions as follows:

•

An ADMM algorithm for solving problems with non-convex objective functions is proposed. The proposed algorithm uses updates where the non-convexity is handled within SCA framework, thereby subsuming both first order ADMM and MM-based ADMM algorithms

The convex surrogate functions used in lieu of the loss function can be inexact and based on old iterates. The resulting error in the gradient of the surrogate function is allowed to be arbitrary but is shown to be critical in determining the convergence of the proposed algorithm.

Since the proposed algorithm entails ADMM-style updates, it inherits the flexibility of ADMM and its amenability to different distributed architectures, such as fusion-centric and fully distributed.

III-B

General update rule for DISC-ADMM

An overarching theme of the present work is the design and analysis of the SCA-ADMM algorithm capable of handling errors and delays in the message passing process. Errors are inexorable in distributed settings, arising from communications noise, computational limitations, or from the lack of sufficient data at each node. We consider inexact ADMM updates that utilize an approximate surrogate function
g_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k};{\mathbf{z}}_{k},{\bm{\xi}}_{k})
where
{\bm{\xi}}_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
is a random vector that represents the environmental or system state, and
\bar{{\mathbf{x}}}={\mathbf{z}}_{k}\in{\mathcal{X}}
is the point of majorijation. As a simple example, the approximate surrogate may be constructed as in Example 1 but using an incorrect gradient
\nabla g_{k}({\mathbf{z}}_{k})+{\mathbf{e}}_{k}
where
{\mathbf{e}}_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the random error. In order to write the inexact asynchronous DISC-ADMM updates compactly, some new functions are introduced:

\displaystyle{\tilde{\ell}}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{z}},{\mathbf{y}}_{k};{\bar{{\mathbf{z}}}}_{k}):=

\displaystyle{\tilde{g}}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k};{\bar{{\mathbf{z}}}}_{k})+\langle\mathbf{P}_{k}{\mathbf{y}}_{k},{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{z}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|\mathbf{P}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{z}})\right\|^{2}

(23)

\displaystyle{\tilde{\ell}}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{z}},{\mathbf{y}}_{k};{\bar{{\mathbf{z}}}}_{k},{\bm{\xi}}_{k}):=

\displaystyle{\tilde{g}}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k};{\bar{{\mathbf{z}}}}_{k},{\bm{\xi}}_{k})+\langle\mathbf{P}_{k}{\mathbf{y}}_{k},{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{z}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|\mathbf{P}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{z}})\right\|^{2}

(24)

\displaystyle{\tilde{u}}(\{{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\},{\mathbf{z}},\{{\mathbf{y}}_{k}\};{\bar{{\mathbf{z}}}}):=

\displaystyle{\tilde{h}}({\mathbf{z}};{\bar{{\mathbf{z}}}})+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\langle\mathbf{P}_{k}{\mathbf{y}}_{k},{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{z}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|\mathbf{P}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{z}})\right\|^{2}.

(25)

Here,
{\tilde{\ell}}_{k}
in (
23
) is the convex surrogate for the local augmented Lagrangian
\ell_{k}
in (
8
). The expression in (
24
) is an inexact approximation of (
23
) where the random variable
{\bm{\xi}}_{k}
is the source of the error.
Likewise,
{\tilde{u}}
in (
25
) is the convex surrogate of
u
in (
9
). With these definitions, the
{\mathbf{z}}
-update takes the form

\displaystyle{\mathbf{z}}^{t+1}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{{\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathcal{X}}}{\tilde{u}}(\{{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t}\},{\mathbf{z}},\{{\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{t}\};{\mathbf{z}}^{t}).

(26)

That is, the
{\mathbf{z}}
-update uses the convex surrogate
{\tilde{h}}
calculated at the previous iterate
{\mathbf{z}}^{t}
. More specifically, at the iteration
t+1
, the master node calculates the surrogate function
\tilde{h}({\mathbf{z}};\bar{{\mathbf{z}}})
at
\bar{{\mathbf{z}}}={\mathbf{z}}^{t}
. The function
\tilde{h}({\mathbf{z}};\bar{{\mathbf{z}}})
is evaluated only through
{\mathbf{z}}^{t}
available at the master node, and can be calculated exactly. In the case when
h({\mathbf{z}})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}h_{k}(z_{k})
, the update splits into
K
parallel updates to be carried out at each node individually.

For the
{\mathbf{x}}_{k}
-update several modifications are introduced so as to make the algorithm resilient to errors, delays, and losses. First, the update utilizes the inexact surrogate
{\tilde{\ell}}_{k}
in (
24
) in place of
\ell_{k}
in (
10b
). Second, in order to encourage
{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1}
to stay close to
{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t}
, the Bregman divergence term
d_{\phi}(\mathbf{P}_{k}{\mathbf{x}}_{k},\mathbf{P}_{k}{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t})
is also included in the objective function of (
10b
). Given any two points
{\mathbf{u}}
,
{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the Bregman divergence is given by
d_{\phi}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}):=\phi({\mathbf{u}})-\phi({\mathbf{v}})-\left\langle\nabla\phi({\mathbf{v}}),\;{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{v}}\right\rangle

[
44
,
45
]
where
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a strictly convex and continuously differentiable function. As a simple example, the choice
\phi({\mathbf{u}})=\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{u}}\right\|^{2}_{2}
translates to
d_{\phi}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})=\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{v}}\right\|_{2}^{2}
. Given
{\bar{{\mathbf{z}}}}\in{\mathcal{X}}
, the modified update sub-problem becomes

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1}=\arg\min_{{\mathbf{x}}_{k}}{\tilde{\ell}}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{z}}^{t+1},{\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{t};{\bar{{\mathbf{z}}}},{\bm{\xi}}_{k})+\frac{1}{\eta}{d_{\phi}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t})}

(27)

where the penalty parameter
\eta>0
. Since
d_{\phi}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t})
is always convex in
{\mathbf{x}}
, the update sub-problem (
27
) is convex.

Third, asynchrony is introduced by calculating the surrogate at an older iterate
{\mathbf{z}}^{[t+1]_{k}}
instead of the most recently calculated
{\mathbf{z}}^{t+1}
. Here, the index
[t+1]_{k}
satisfies
t+1-\tau_{1}\leq[t+1]_{k}\leq t+1
where maximum tolerable delay
\tau_{1}
is bounded. For notation ease, we collect all random components realized at time
t
into the vector
{\bm{\xi}}^{t}
so that the inexact asynchronous surrogate function used at iteration
t
is given by
{\tilde{\ell}}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{z}}^{t+1},{\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{t};{\mathbf{z}}^{[t+1]_{k}},{\bm{\xi}}^{[t+1]_{k}})
. Fourth, we let the
{\mathbf{x}}_{k}
update to be optional so that only a subset
{\mathcal{S}}^{x}_{t}
of nodes may perform the update at time
t
. It is remarked that the update must still be carried out often enough and the precise requirements will be detailed later. Summarizing, the
{\mathbf{x}}_{k}
update at node
k
may be written as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1}=\begin{cases}\arg\min_{{\mathbf{x}}_{k}}~{\tilde{\ell}}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{z}}^{t+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{t}_{k};{\mathbf{z}}^{[t+1]_{k}},{\bm{\xi}}^{[t+1]_{k}})\\
\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{\eta}d_{\phi}(\mathbf{P}_{k}{\mathbf{x}}_{k},\mathbf{P}_{k}{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t})&\hskip-42.67912ptk\in{\mathcal{S}}^{x}_{t}\\
{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t}&\hskip-42.67912ptk\notin{\mathcal{S}}^{x}_{t}\end{cases}

(28)

Finally, the dual updates are also optional and are given by

\displaystyle{\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{t+1}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}{\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{t}+\rho\mathbf{P}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{t+1})&k\in{\mathcal{S}}^{x}_{t}\\
{\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{t}&k\notin{\mathcal{S}}^{x}_{t}\end{cases}

(29)

Before concluding, it is remarked that the gradient error incurred due to the inexact update is given by

\displaystyle{\mathbf{e}}^{t+1}_{k}:=\nabla{\tilde{g}}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1};{\mathbf{z}}^{[t+1]_{k}},{\bm{\xi}}^{[t+1]_{k}})-\nabla{\tilde{g}}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1};{\mathbf{z}}^{[t+1]_{k}})

(30)

\displaystyle=\nabla{\tilde{\ell}}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{t+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{t}_{k};{\mathbf{z}}^{[t+1]_{k}},{\bm{\xi}}^{[t+1]_{k}})

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt-\nabla{\tilde{\ell}}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{t+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{t}_{k};{\mathbf{z}}^{[t+1]_{k}}).

The subsequent performance analysis will depend on the average error given by

\displaystyle E_{T}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\mathbb{E}}\left\|{\mathbf{e}}_{k}^{t}\right\|^{2}

(31)

Different from the stochastic gradient or stochastic ADMM settings, the gradient errors in (
30
) are not required to be independent or unbiased. Consequently, the proposed algorithms are applicable to inexact variants where the error is deterministic, such as the accelerated methods, variance-reduced methods, and algorithms with quantization errors or perturbations.

Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a unified framework for solving distributed non-convex optimization problems under required constraints of big data and networked-system applications. The proposed algorithms are provably convergent and amenable for distributed and decentralized implementation. As a byproduct of our investigation, we also enrich the theory of SCA methods and the inexact gradient methods. We proposed a more general approximation scheme that adds to flexibility in choosing surrogate functions. The presented analysis of integrating generalized inexact gradient framework within the non-convex ADMM holds an independent contribution for the modified gradient literature and has the potential for the extension to other optimization approaches.

\displaystyle\left(\{{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1}\},{\mathbf{z}}^{t+1},\{{\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{t+1}\}\right)

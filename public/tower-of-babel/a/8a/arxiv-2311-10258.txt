Uniform regularity for degenerate elliptic equations in perforated domains
Zhongwei Shen, Jinping Zhuge

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with a class of degenerate elliptic equations with rapidly oscillating coefficients in periodically perforated domains, which arises in the study of spectrum problems for uniformly elliptic equations in perforated domains. We establish a quantitative convergence rate and obtain the uniform weighted Lipschitz and W 1 , p W^{1,p} estimates.

35B27, 35J70

1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider a class of degenerate elliptic operators with rapidly oscillating coefficients,

(1.1)

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=-\text{\rm div}(\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x)A_{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla),

in a periodically perforated domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, where
\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\phi(x/\varepsilon)
is a periodic scalar function
that vanishes on the boundaries of holes and
A_{\varepsilon}(x)=A(x/\varepsilon)

a periodic matrix-valued function. The parameter
\varepsilon>0
is assumed
to be small. The operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
arises in the study of the asymptotic expansions of the spectrum
for the uniformly elliptic operator
-\text{\rm div}(A_{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla)
in perforated domains; see
[
15
]
and
[
11
, Chapter III]
.

To describe
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, let
Y=(-1/2,1/2)^{d}
be a unit cell in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\{\tau_{i}:i=1,2,\dots,m\}
a finite
number of mutually disjoint open sets (holes) in
Y
with smooth and connected boundaries.
We assume that dist
(\tau_{i},\partial Y)\geq c_{0}
and dist
(\tau_{i},\tau_{j})\geq c_{0}
for
i\neq j
, where
c_{0}>0
.
Let

T=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\overline{\tau_{i}}\quad\text{ and }\quad T_{\varepsilon}=\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varepsilon(z+T).

For a bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<\varepsilon<1
, we define

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\setminus T_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varepsilon(z+T).

Throughout the paper we shall assume that

(1.2)

\text{\rm dist}(\partial\Omega,\partial T_{\varepsilon})\geq c_{0}

for some
c_{0}>0
. As a result,
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\partial\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
,
where
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\cap\partial T_{\varepsilon}
and dist
(\partial\Omega,\Gamma_{\varepsilon})\geq c_{0}
. The restrictive geometric assumption (
1.2
) is essential for the uniform regularity near the boundary
\partial\Omega
. The typical examples in applications satisfying this assumption include rectangular domains with sides parallel to the coordinate planes.

Next, we describe the conditions on
\phi
and
A
.
Throughout the paper, we assume that
\phi=\phi(y)
and
A=A(y)
are
Y
-periodic, and that the matrix
A=A(y)=(a_{ij}(y))_{d\times d}
is symmetric, Hölder continuous, and uniformly elliptic, i.e.,

(1.3)

\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle\geq\mu|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{ for any }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

for some
\mu>0
.
We assume that
\phi(x)
degenerates as a distance function from the holes, i.e.,

(1.4)

\phi(y)\approx\text{\rm dist}(y,T)\quad\text{ for }y\in Y,

where the notation
a\approx b
indicates that
a
is comparable to
b
in the sense that
cb\leq a\leq Cb
for some universal constants
c,C>0
. It is worth noting that
\phi^{2}
fails to be a Muckenhoupt
A_{2}
weight. We refer the reader to
[
7
,
16
,
14
,
5
,
6
]
and references therein for
regularity estimates of degenerate or singular elliptic equations.
We further assume that
\phi\in C^{1,\alpha}(Y\setminus T)
for some
\alpha>0
and that

(1.5)

\|\text{\rm div}(A\nabla\phi)\|_{L^{p_{0}}(Y\setminus T)}\leq C

for some
p_{0}>d
(this is needed for the small-scale Lipschitz estimate). Clearly, this condition is satisfied if
A
and
\phi
are sufficiently smooth. Another example is the case that
\phi
is
the ground state for the Schrödinger operator
-\text{\rm div}(A\nabla)+V
in
Y\setminus T
with
periodic conditions on
\partial Y
and Dirichlet condition on
\partial T
,
where
V\in L^{p_{0}}(Y\setminus T)
for some
p_{0}>d
. In the application to the spectral problems (see
[
15
,
11
]
and (
1.11
) below),
\phi
is the principal Dirichlet eigenfunction of
-\text{div}(A\nabla)
,
which satisfies both (
1.4
) and (
1.5
).

The following two theorems are the main results of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
u_{\varepsilon}
is the weak solution of

(1.6)

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\phi_{\varepsilon}f\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

(i)

If
\Omega
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
domain and
p>d
, then

(1.7)

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

(ii)

If
\Omega
is
C^{1}
or convex and
1<p<d
,
\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{d}
, then

(1.8)

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

In both cases, the constant
C_{p}
is independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is a
C^{1}
or convex domain and
u_{\varepsilon}
the weak solution of

(1.9)

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\text{\rm div}(\phi_{\varepsilon}f)+F\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Then for
1<p<\infty
,

(1.10)

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\left\{\|f\|_{p}+\|F\|_{p}\right\},

where
C_{p}
is independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

Thanks to the Lax-Milgram Theorem, the boundary value problems
(
1.6
) and (
1.9
) are solvable in the energy space
V_{\varepsilon}
; see Section
3
. We
point out that no boundary condition is needed on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}=\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial\Omega
due to the strong
degeneracy of the coefficients near
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
. The regularity results in the main theorems can be applied to the Dirichlet eigenfunctions of the operator
-\text{div}(A_{\varepsilon}\nabla)
in the perforated domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
; see
[
11
]
.
Indeed, a computation shows that if
-\text{\rm div}(A_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=\lambda_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and

u_{\varepsilon}=\phi_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}
, where
\phi
is the principal eigenfunction of
-\text{\rm div}(A\nabla)
in
Y\setminus T
with
the principal eigenvalue
\overline{\lambda}
, then

(1.11)

-\text{\rm div}(\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon})=(\lambda_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda})\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

In
[
15
]
the observation above was used to show that
\lambda^{k}_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}+\mu^{k}_{0}+o(1)
as
\varepsilon\ \to 0
,
where
\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{k}
is the
k
th Dirichlet eigenvalue of
-\Delta
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and

\mu^{k}_{0}
the
k
th Dirichlet eigenvalue for a second-order elliptic operator with constant coefficients in
\Omega
.
Using some uniform estimates for eigenfunctions, it was proved in
[
11
]
that

|\lambda^{k}_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}-\mu^{k}_{0}|\leq C\varepsilon,

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
.

The proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
boil down into estimates at two different scales. At small scales (below
\varepsilon
), we need to establish the weighted Lipschitz and
W^{1,p}
estimates in a cell with
\varepsilon=1
. Due to the strong degeneracy of the coefficients (see (
1.4
)), the classical Schauder estimates as well as those for degenerate equations in the existing literature
cannot apply directly. Instead, we use a technique of Moser’s iteration to establish an unweighted
L^{\infty}
estimate
for the equation
-\text{\rm div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla u)=\phi F
.
A transformation, which reduces the degenerate equation to a nondegenerate equation,
is then used to prove the weighted Lipschitz estimates. This gives us the small-scale Lipschitz estimates and therefore the small-scale
W^{1,p}
estimates by a real-variable argument.
At large scales, we first establish a quantitative convergence rate and use it to obtain the uniform estimates above the
\varepsilon
-scale. A careful analysis involving the harmonic extension and weighted Sobolev inequalities
is carried out to handle the difficulties caused by the degeneracy of the coefficients and the holes.
Finally, the excess decay iteration and the real-variable argument, as standard tools in the homogenization theory, are
used to establish the weighted Lipschitz estimate and
W^{1,p}
estimate, respectively.
For large-scale regularity estimates for uniformly elliptic operators with oscillating coefficients,
we refer to the reader to
[
3
,
10
,
2
,
1
,
8
]
and references therein.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the weighted Sobolev spaces and establish some useful inequalities of independent interest. In Section
3
, we establish the Lipschitz estimate at small scales for the degenerate elliptic equations. In Section
4
, we use the techniques of homogenization to derive a quantitative convergence rate and a local first-order approximation. In Section
5
, we establish the large-scale Lipschitz estimate and prove Theorem
1.1
part (i). In Section
6
, we establish both the small-scale and large-scale
W^{1,p}
estimates and prove Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.1
part (ii). In Appendix, we list some properties of the smoothing operators and nontangential maximal functions that have been used in this paper.

2.
Inequalities in weighted Sobolev spaces

In this section, we establish several useful inequalities in
\phi
- or
\phi_{\varepsilon}
-weighted Sobolev spaces in the
L^{p}
settings.
Some of the inequalities in the case
p=2
may be found in
[
15
,
11
]
.

Recall that
Y_{*}=(-1/2,1/2)^{d}\setminus T
.
For
1\leq p<\infty
, let

L^{p}_{\phi}(Y_{*})=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(Y_{*}):v\phi\in L^{p}(Y_{*})\right\},

L^{p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}):v\phi_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\right\}.

Let
W^{1,p}_{\phi}(Y_{*})
be a
\phi
-weighted Sobolev space defined by

W^{1,p}_{\phi}(Y_{*})=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(Y_{*}):v\in L^{p}_{\phi}(Y_{*})\text{ and }\nabla v\in L^{p}_{\phi}(Y_{*})^{d}\right\}.

Similarly, define the
\phi_{\varepsilon}
-weighted Sobolev space in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
by

W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}):v\in L^{p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\text{ and }\nabla v\in L^{p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{d}\right\}.

Let
W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
denote the subspace of
W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
that contains the functions vanishing on
\partial\Omega
.

A key technique for studying the weighted Sobolev spaces is the harmonic extension. For each hole
\tau_{i}\subset T
, let
\tau^{\prime}_{i}
be the extended smooth hole such that
\tau_{i}\subset\tau_{i}^{\prime}
and

\text{dist}(\tau_{i}^{\prime},\tau_{j}^{\prime})\geq c_{0}/4,\quad\text{dist}(\tau_{i}^{\prime},\partial Y)\geq c_{0}/4,\quad\text{dist}(\partial\tau_{i}^{\prime},\tau_{i})\geq c_{0}/4.

Let
T^{\prime}=\cup_{i=1}^{m}\overline{\tau_{i}^{\prime}}\subset Y
be the union of the extended holes and
Y_{*}^{\prime}=(-1/2,1/2)^{d}\setminus T^{\prime}
.
The harmonic extension operator

\mathcal{E}:W^{1,p}(Y_{*}^{\prime})\to W^{1,p}(Y)

is a linear operator defined as follows: for every
f\in W^{1,p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})
,
\mathcal{E}(f)=f
in
Y^{\prime}_{*}
,

\Delta\mathcal{E}(f)=0
in
T^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}(f)=f
on
\partial T^{\prime}
in the sense of trace. In this case, we say
\mathcal{E}(f)
is the harmonic extension of
f
from
Y^{\prime}_{*}
to
Y
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
1<p<\infty
, the operator
\mathcal{E}:W^{1,p}(Y_{*}^{\prime})\to W^{1,p}(Y)
is bounded. Moreover,

\|\nabla\mathcal{E}(f)\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(Y_{*}^{\prime})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in W^{1,p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})
.
Since
T
is
C^{1}
, the classical extension theorem in Sobolev spaces implies that there exists
\tilde{f}\in W^{1,p}(Y)
such that
\tilde{f}=f
in
Y^{\prime}_{*}
. Moreover,
\|\tilde{f}\|_{W^{1,p}(Y)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})}
.
Let
u_{f}=\mathcal{E}(f)
be the solution of the
Dirichlet problem
-\Delta u_{f}=0
in
T^{\prime}
and
u_{f}=f
on
\partial T^{\prime}
in the sense of trace. Note that
v_{f}:=u_{f}-\tilde{f}
satisfies
-\Delta v_{f}=\nabla\cdot\nabla\tilde{f}
in
T^{\prime}
and
v_{f}=0
on
\partial T^{\prime}
. Now the
W^{1,p}
estimate leads to

\|\nabla v_{f}\|_{L^{p}(T^{\prime})}\leq C\|\nabla\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(T^{\prime})}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})}.

By the triangle inequality,

(2.1)

\|\nabla u_{f}\|_{L^{p}(T^{\prime})}\leq\|\nabla v_{f}\|_{L^{p}(T^{\prime})}+\|\nabla\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(T^{\prime})}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})}.

Note that
u_{f}
is the harmonic extension of
f
from
Y^{\prime}_{*}
to
Y
. Then for any constant
L
,
u_{f}-L
is the harmonic extension of
f-L
. Let
L=\fint_{Y^{\prime}_{*}}f
and apply (
2.1
) to
f-L
, we have

\|\nabla u_{f}\|_{L^{p}(T^{\prime})}\leq C\|f-L\|_{W^{1,p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})},

where we have used the fact that
Y_{*}^{\prime}
is connected.
Since
\mathcal{E}(f)=f
in
Y^{\prime}_{*}
and
\mathcal{E}(f)=u_{f}
in
T^{\prime}
, the last inequality yields the desired estimate.
∎

The next two lemmas give Sobolev embedding theorems in the weighted spaces
W^{1,p}_{\phi}(Y_{*})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C
such that

\|u\|_{L^{p}(Y_{*})}\leq C\|\phi\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(Y_{*})}+C\|u\|_{L^{p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})}

for any
u\in W^{1,p}_{\phi}(Y_{*})
.

Proof.

Since
\phi(x)\approx\text{dist}(x,T)
,
the inequality is essentially a version of Hardy’s inequality. We give an elementary proof. By flatting the boundary of each hole locally, it suffices to show a one-dimensional estimate:

(2.2)

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}|v(t)|^{p}dt\leq C\int_{0}^{1}|tv^{\prime}(t)|^{p}dt+C\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}|v(t)|^{p}dt.

Let
\eta
be a smooth cutoff function such that
\eta=1
in
[0,1/2]
,
\eta(1)=0
and
|\eta^{\prime}|\leq 3
. Then for
t\in(0,1/2)
,

|v(t)|^{p}=\bigg|\int_{t}^{1}(|v(s)|^{p}\eta(s))^{\prime}ds\bigg|\leq C\int_{t}^{1}|v(s)|^{p-1}|v^{\prime}(s)|ds+C\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}|v(s)|^{p}ds.

Thus, by the Fubini theorem and the Young inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}|v(t)|^{p}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}|v(s)|^{p-1}s|v^{\prime}(s)|ds+C\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}|v(s)|^{p}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}|v(s)|^{p}ds+C\int_{0}^{1}|sv^{\prime}(s)|^{p}ds+C\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}|v(s)|^{p}ds.

This gives the desired inequality (
2.2
).
∎

For convenience, for
p\in[1,d)
, let
p^{*}
be given by
\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{d}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Assume
u\in W^{1,p}_{\phi}(Y_{*})
.

(i)

For
1\leq p<d
, there exists a constant
C
such that

(2.3)

\|\phi u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(Y_{*})}\leq C\|\phi\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(Y_{*})}+C\|u\|_{L^{p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})}.

(ii)

For
p>d
, there exists a constant
C
such that

(2.4)

\|\phi u\|_{C^{\alpha}(Y_{*})}\leq C\|\phi\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(Y_{*})}+C\|u\|_{L^{p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})},

where
\alpha=1-\frac{d}{p}
.

Proof.

Let
1\leq p<d
.
By Lemma
2.2
,
u\in L^{p}(Y_{*})
, which, together with the fact
\phi(x)\approx\text{\rm dist}(x,T)
, implies that

\phi u=0
on
\partial T
.
We apply the Sobolev-Poincaré inequality in
Y_{*}
to obtain

\displaystyle\|\phi u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(Y_{*})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla(\phi u)\|_{L^{p}(Y_{*})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(Y_{*})}+C\|u\|_{L^{p}(Y_{*})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(Y_{*})}+C\|u\|_{L^{p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})},

where we have used Lemma
2.2
and the assumption
\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(Y_{*})}\leq C
.
This proves (i). The proof of (ii) is similar.
∎

Now we establish several useful weighted inequalities in the perforated domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. The main idea is a technique
of “multiscale inequalities”, which means that a general inequality over
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is broken down into inequalities in each
\varepsilon
-periodic cells, together with a global estimate in
\Omega
with no holes.
Let

T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}=\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varepsilon(T^{\prime}+z)\quad\text{ and }\quad\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\setminus T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}.

Without loss of generality, we assume that the extended holes
\varepsilon(z+T^{\prime})
do not intersect with the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
This allows us to define the harmonic extension for any function
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})
. Precisely, let
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}:W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})\to W^{1,p}(\Omega)
be the harmonic extension operator such that
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)=u
in
\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
and
\Delta\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)=0
in
T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega
.

Theorem 2.4

(Embedding from weighted to unweighted)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. There exists
C>0
such that for any
u\in W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
,

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Proof.

We apply a technique of “multiscale inequalities”. First, we see that
u\in W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
implies
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})
. Let
\tilde{u}=\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)
be the harmonic extension of
u
. Lemma
2.1
implies

\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y)\cap\Omega}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p}\leq C\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y^{\prime}_{*})\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\leq C\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y^{\prime}_{*})\cap\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}.

Summing over
z
(including the boundary cells), we obtain

(2.5)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}.

Note that
\partial\Omega
does not intersect with
\varepsilon(z+T^{\prime}_{*})
. Thus,
\tilde{u}=u=0
on
\partial\Omega
. The Poincaré inequality gives

(2.6)

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}|u|^{p}\leq\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{u}|^{p}\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}.

Next, we consider each cell
\varepsilon(z+Y_{*})
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. By Lemma
2.2
and rescaling,

\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y_{*})\cap\Omega}|u|^{p}\leq C\varepsilon^{p}\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y_{*})\cap\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+C\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y^{\prime}_{*})\cap\Omega}|u|^{p}.

Summing over
z
, we get

(2.7)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|u|^{p}\leq C\varepsilon^{p}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+C\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}|u|^{p}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p},

where we have used (
2.6
) in the last inequality. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The inequality (taken from (
2.7
))

(2.8)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|u|^{p}\leq C\varepsilon^{p}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+C\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}|u|^{p}

can be viewed as a version of the so-called multiscale Poincaré inequality. The
\varepsilon
appearing before the gradient term is not classical and it is related the multiscale feature of the inequality.

Theorem 2.6

(Embedding from weighted to unweighted II)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. There exists
C>0
such that for any
u\in W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
,

(2.9)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

and

(2.10)

\inf_{L\in\mathbb{R}}\|u-L\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Proof.

The inequality (
2.9
) follows from (
2.8
) and the fact that
\phi_{\varepsilon}\approx 1
in
\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
. We only need to prove (
2.10
). Note that if
\tilde{u}=\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)
is the harmonic extension of
u
, then
\tilde{u}-L
is the harmonic extension of
u-L
. We set

(2.11)

L=\fint_{\Omega}\tilde{u}.

We mimic the proof of Theorem
2.4
. In fact,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|u-L|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{p}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+C\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}|u-L|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{p}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+C\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{u}-L|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{p}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{p}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{p}|\nabla u|^{p},

where we have used the classical Poincaré inequality in the third inequality.
∎

Theorem 2.7

(Weighted Sobolev-Poincaré inequality I)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
u\in W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
.

(i)

If
1<p<d
and
\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{d}
, there exists
C>0
such that

(2.12)

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

(ii)

If
p>d
, there exists
C>0
such that

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Proof.

(i) We first prove

(2.13)

\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Let
\tilde{u}=\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)
be the harmonic extension of
u
from
\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
to the entire
\Omega
constructed as before. Then we have (
2.5
). Since
u=\tilde{u}
in
\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
,
by the classical Sobolev-Poincaré ienquality in
\Omega
and (
2.5
), we obtain

(2.14)

\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})}=\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Next, we show

(2.15)

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})}.

We will again use the technique of “multiscale inequalities”. Precisely,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}u|^{p^{*}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z}\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y_{*})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}u|^{p^{*}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z}\varepsilon^{d}\int_{(z+Y_{*})\cap\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Omega}|\phi(x)u(\varepsilon x)|^{p^{*}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z}C\varepsilon^{d}\bigg(\int_{(z+Y_{*})\cap\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Omega}|\phi(x)\varepsilon\nabla u(\varepsilon x)|^{p}dx\bigg)^{{p^{*}}/p}

\displaystyle+\sum_{z}C\varepsilon^{d}\bigg(\int_{(z+Y^{\prime}_{*})\cap\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Omega}|u(\varepsilon x)|^{p}dx\bigg)^{{p^{*}}/p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z}C\bigg(\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y_{*})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u|^{p}\bigg)^{{p^{*}}/p}+\sum_{z}C\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y^{\prime}_{*})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|u|^{p^{*}},

where we have used (
2.3
) in the third line. Now we recall the following elementary
inequality: for any nonnegative sequence
a_{i}\geq 0
and
\beta\geq 1
,

(2.17)

\sum_{i}a_{i}^{\beta}\leq\Big(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\Big)^{\beta}.

Applying this inequality to the first term of the last line of (
2.16
), we obtain

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}u|^{p^{*}}\leq C\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u|^{p}\bigg)^{{p^{*}}/p}+C\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}|u|^{p^{*}}.

This proves (
2.15
). Clearly, the estimates (
2.13
) and (
2.15
) together yield (
2.12
).

(ii) If
p>d
, (
2.14
) is replaced by

\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

where
\alpha=1-\frac{d}{p}
.

Now consider in each periodic cell
\varepsilon(z+Y_{*})
(the estimate for the boundary cells is the same), and apply (
2.4
) in
z+Y_{*}
after scaling,

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon(z+Y_{*})}|\phi_{\varepsilon}u|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in z+Y_{*}}|\phi(x)u(\varepsilon x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\nabla(u(\varepsilon x))\|_{L^{p}(z+Y_{*})}+C\|u(\varepsilon x)\|_{L^{p}(Y^{\prime}_{*})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1-d/p}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\varepsilon(z+Y_{*}))}+C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\varepsilon(z+Y^{\prime}_{*}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1-d/p}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

This ends the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.8

(Weighted Sobolev-Poincaré inequality II)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
u\in W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
.

(i)

If
1<p<d
, there exists
C>0
such that

(2.18)

\inf_{L\in\mathbb{R}}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(u-L)\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

and

(2.19)

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

(ii)

If
p>d
, there exists
C>0
such that

\inf_{L\in\mathbb{R}}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(u-L)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

and

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Proof.

For (
2.18
), the proof is almost identical to Theorem
2.7
with replacing
u
by
u-L
. The only difference is that when we use the classical Sobolev-Poincaré inequality in (
2.14
), we take
L
to be the average of
\tilde{u}
over
\Omega
(as in (
2.11
)) to
obtain

\|u-L\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})}\leq\|\tilde{u}-L\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

The rest of the proof is exactly the same.

For (
2.19
), we replace (
2.14
) by

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})}=\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

The rest of the proof is exactly the same. The proof for (ii) is similar.
∎

Remark 2.9

(Compact embedding)
.

Let
1<p<d
and
\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{d}
. Then for any
1\leq q<p^{*}
, the embedding
W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\to L^{q}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
is compact. This general fact
is not used in this paper.

3.
Regularity of degenerate equations at small scales

Let
V_{\varepsilon}=W^{1,2}_{\phi_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
.
For
f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{d}
and
F\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
, we call
u_{\varepsilon}\in V_{\varepsilon}
a weak solution to the boundary value
problem,

(3.1)

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\text{\rm div}(\phi_{\varepsilon}f)+F\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

if for any
v\in V_{\varepsilon}
,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v=-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}f\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}Fv.

The existence and uniqueness of the weak solution
u_{\varepsilon}
follow readily from the Lax-Milgram Theorem by using the inequality

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}
for
v\in V_{\varepsilon}
; see Theorem
2.7
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the energy estimate,

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\right\}.

In this section, we focus on the local regularity at small scales for the special equation
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\phi_{\varepsilon}F
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. By setting
u(x)=u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon x)
, this equation can be rescaled to

(3.2)

-\text{div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla u)=\varepsilon^{2}\phi(x)F(\varepsilon x)\quad\text{in }\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

In order to establish
the small-scale estimates of the equation (
3.2
) for all the points in
\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, we need to consider three cases separately.

•

Interior case
:
x_{0}\in\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and
B_{4r}(x_{0})\subset\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. In this case, for any
x\in B_{2r}(x_{0})
,
\phi(x)\approx\phi(x_{0})
. Thus, the equation (
3.3
) in
B_{2r}(x_{0})
can be reduced to a uniformly elliptic equation and the classical interior estimate applies.

•

Boundary case I
:
x_{0}\in\varepsilon^{-1}\partial\Omega
and
B_{4r}(x_{0})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega\subset\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. In this case
B_{4r}(x_{0})
does not intersect with the holes in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Hence, for
x\in B_{2r}(x_{0})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega
,
\phi(x)\approx\phi(x_{0})
. Then the classical boundary estimate of elliptic equations applies.

•

Boundary case II
:
x_{0}\in\varepsilon^{-1}\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\varepsilon^{-1}\partial\Omega
, i.e.,
x_{0}
is on the boundary of holes. For sufficiently small and
fixed
r_{0}<1
and
r<r_{0}
, we have
B_{2r}(x_{0})
only intersects with the boundary of one hole and does not get close to the other holes.
Note that in this case, the coefficient
\phi(x)
degenerates as a distance function from the hole and the classical elliptic theory does not apply.

As the interior case and the boundary case I are classical, we only need to focus on the regularity estimates of the boundary case II. The corresponding estimates in each cell
Y^{*}
(including the boundary cells) may be derived by a covering argument.

Let
|z|_{\infty}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|z_{j}|
for
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
Y_{*}^{+}=\cup_{|z|_{\infty}\leq 1}(z+Y_{*})
be the enlarged
cell of
Y_{*}
. In view of (
3.2
), we
consider the rescaled equation in an enlarged cell

(3.3)

-\text{\rm div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla u)=\phi F\quad\text{in }Y_{*}^{+}.

Let
u\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(Y_{*}^{+})
. We call
u
a weak solution of (
3.3
) if

\phi u\in L^{2}(Y_{*}^{+})
,
\phi|\nabla u|\in L^{2}(Y_{*}^{+})
, and for any
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Y^{+})
, we have

\int_{Y_{*}^{+}}\phi^{2}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=\int_{Y_{*}^{+}}\phi Fv,

where
Y^{+}=\cup_{|z|_{\infty}\leq 1}(z+Y)
.

We first prove the local boundedness of weak solutions by the Moser’s iteration.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u
be a weak solution of (
3.3
) and
F\in L^{p}(Y_{*})
for some
p>d
.
If
B
is centered on
\partial T
and
r=\text{\rm diam}(B)\leq c_{0}/4
, then

(3.4)

\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B\cap Y_{*}}|u|\leq Cr^{-1}\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+Cr\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Proof.

By rescaling, it suffices to consider the case

\text{diam}(B)=1
. Let
B_{r}=rB
and
\widetilde{B}_{r}=B_{r}\cap Y_{*}
. Let
1/2<r<R<1
and
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R})
be such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
in
B_{R}
,
\eta=1
in
B_{r}
and
|\nabla\eta|\leq C(R-r)^{-1}
. Let
m\geq 2
,
\ell\geq 1
, and

u_{\ell}^{+}=\min\{|u|,\ell\},\qquad v_{\ell}=(u_{\ell}^{+})^{m-2}u.

Note that

\nabla v_{\ell}=(m-1)(u_{\ell}^{+})^{m-2}\nabla u\chi_{\{|u|<\ell\}}+(u_{\ell}^{+})^{m-2}\nabla u\chi_{\{|u|\geq\ell\}}.

By a limiting argument, it is not hard to show that we may
test the equation (
3.3
) against
v_{\ell}\eta^{2}
.
It follows that

(3.5)

\displaystyle(m-1)\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}(A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u)\chi_{\{|u|<\ell\}}(u_{\ell}^{+})^{m-2}\eta^{2}+\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}(A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u)\chi_{\{|u|\geq\ell\}}(u_{\ell}^{+})^{m-2}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi Fv_{\ell}\eta^{2}-2\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}(A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta)v_{\ell}\eta.

Let

w_{\ell}=(u_{\ell}^{+})^{\frac{m}{2}-1}u\eta.

Then

(3.6)

\nabla w_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2}m(u_{\ell}^{+})^{\frac{m}{2}-1}(\nabla u)\eta\chi_{\{|u|<\ell\}}+(u_{\ell}^{+})^{\frac{m}{2}-1}(\nabla u)\eta\chi_{\{|u|\geq\ell\}}+(u_{\ell}^{+})^{\frac{m}{2}-1}u\nabla\eta.

This, together with (
3.5
), gives

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}|\nabla w_{\ell}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Cm\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi|F||v_{\ell}|\eta^{2}+Cm\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}|\nabla u||\nabla\eta||v_{\ell}|\eta

\displaystyle+C\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}(u_{\ell}^{+})^{m-2}u^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Using Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality, we deduce that

(3.8)

\displaystyle m\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi|F||v_{\ell}|\eta^{2}\leq\|\delta F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}\|(\delta m)^{-1}|u_{\ell}^{+}|^{\frac{m-2}{2}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}\|m^{2}\phi w_{\ell}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{m}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{m}+\big(\frac{1}{m\delta}\big)^{2m/(m-2)}\||u_{\ell}^{+}|^{\frac{m-2}{2}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2m/(m-2)}+m^{4}\|\phi w_{\ell}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2},

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma}=1
and
\delta=\delta(m)>0
is a suitable number to be determined. Since
p>d
, we have
2<\gamma<2^{*}
(an obvious modification is needed for
d=2
through the proof).
By Hölder’s inequality and the boundedness of
\eta
,

(3.9)

\||u_{\ell}^{+}|^{\frac{m-2}{2}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2m/(m-2)}\leq\||u_{\ell}^{+}|^{\frac{m}{2}}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2}\leq\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2}.

By an interpolation inequality,

(3.10)

\|\phi w_{\ell}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2}\leq\big(\frac{1}{m^{5}\delta}\big)\|\phi w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2}+C(m^{5}\delta)^{\alpha}\|\phi w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2},

where
\alpha>0
is a constant depending only on
p
and
d
.
Substituting (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) into (
3.8
), we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle m\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi|F||v_{\ell}|\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{m}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{m}+Cm^{4}(m^{5}\delta)^{\alpha}\|\phi w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\big(\frac{1}{m\delta}\big)^{2m/(m-2)}\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2}+\frac{1}{m\delta}\|\phi w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2}.

On the other hand, using
(
3.6
)
, we have

(u_{\ell}^{+})^{\frac{m}{2}-1}|\nabla u|\eta\leq|\nabla w_{\ell}-(u_{\ell}^{+})^{\frac{m}{2}-1}u\nabla\eta|.

This allows us to estimate the second integral on the right-hand side of (
3.7
) as follows,

(3.12)

m\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}|\nabla u||\nabla\eta||v_{\ell}|\eta\leq\frac{1}{m\delta}\|\phi\nabla w_{\ell}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}+C(m^{3}\delta+m)\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}(u_{\ell}^{+})^{m-2}u^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Now, inserting (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) into (
3.7
), we obtain

(3.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}|\nabla w_{\ell}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{m}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{m}+C(m^{3}\delta+m^{4}(m^{5}\delta)^{\alpha}+m)\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}(u_{\ell}^{+})^{m-2}u^{2}(|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\eta^{2})

\displaystyle+C\big(\frac{1}{m\delta}\big)^{2m/(m-2)}\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2}+\frac{C}{m\delta}\|\phi w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{2}+\frac{C}{m\delta}\|\phi\nabla w_{\ell}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}

Note
w_{\ell}=0
on
\partial B_{R}\cap Y_{*}
. Recall two inequalities given by Lemma
2.3
(i) and Lemma
2.2
,

(3.14)

\|\phi w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}\leq C\|\phi\nabla w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})},

and

(3.15)

\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}\leq C\|\phi\nabla w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}.

Choosing
\delta=C_{1}/m
with sufficiently large
C_{1}>0
, we obtain from (
3.13
) that

(3.16)

\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}|\nabla w_{\ell}|^{2}\leq\frac{C^{m}}{m^{m}}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{m}+Cm^{4+4\alpha}\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}(u_{\ell}^{+})^{m-2}u^{2}(|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\eta^{2}).

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

(3.17)

\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}|w_{\ell}|^{2\gamma_{0}}\bigg)^{1/2\gamma_{0}}\leq\|\phi w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{\theta}\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}^{1-\theta}\leq C\|\phi\nabla w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{R})},

where
\theta=d/(d+2)
and
\gamma_{0}=1+2/d>1
.
Hence, it follows from (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}_{r}}\phi^{2}(u_{\ell}^{+})^{(m-2)\gamma_{0}}|u|^{2\gamma_{0}}\bigg)^{1/\gamma_{0}m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{m}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}+\bigg\{\frac{Cm^{4+4\alpha}}{(R-r)^{2}}\bigg\}^{1/m}\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}(u_{\ell}^{+})^{m-2}u^{2}\bigg)^{1/m}.

The above estimate holds for any
\ell>1,m\geq 2
and
1/2<r<R<1
. Moreover, the constant
C
is independent of
\ell,m,R
and
r
. By an iteration starting from
m=2
and letting
\ell\to\infty
, we can see that
\int_{\widetilde{B}_{r}}\phi^{2}|u|^{m\gamma_{0}}
is finite for any
r<1
and
m\geq 2
. Hence, sending
\ell\to\infty
in (
3.18
), we get

(3.19)

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}_{r}}\phi^{2}|u|^{\gamma_{0}m}\bigg)^{1/\gamma_{0}m}\leq\frac{C}{m}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{R})}+\bigg\{\frac{Cm^{4+4\alpha}}{(R-r)^{2}}\bigg\}^{1/m}\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}_{R}}\phi^{2}|u|^{m}\bigg)^{1/m}.

Now, we iterate the inequality (
3.19
) by choosing an infinite sequence of shrinking radii
r_{k}
with
1/2<r_{0}\leq 1
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}r_{k}=1/2
. Let
\theta\in(0,1)
(particularly, we may pick
\theta=1/2
in the following calculation). Define

r_{k}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(1-\theta)\theta^{k}.

and

p_{0}=2,\quad p_{k+1}=\gamma_{0}p_{k}=2\gamma_{0}^{k+1}.

Applying (
3.19
) with
m=p_{k}
and
R=r_{k},r=r_{k+1}
, we obtain

\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}_{r_{k+1}}}\phi^{2}|u|^{p_{k+1}}\bigg)^{1/p_{k+1}}\leq\frac{C}{p_{k}}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{r_{k}})}+\bigg\{\frac{Cp_{k}^{2+2\alpha})}{(1-\theta)^{2}\theta^{k}}\bigg\}^{2/p_{k}}\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}_{r_{k}}}\phi^{2}|u|^{p_{k}}\bigg)^{1/p_{k}}.

By iteration, we get

\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}_{r_{k+1}}}\phi^{2}|u|^{p_{k+1}}\bigg)^{1/p_{k+1}}\leq\bigg\{\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{CD_{k}}{2\gamma_{0}^{k-j}}\bigg\}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{r_{0}})}+D_{k}\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}_{r_{0}}}\phi^{2}|u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where

D_{k}=\prod_{j=0}^{k}\bigg\{\frac{C\gamma_{0}^{(2+2\alpha)j}}{(1-\theta)^{2}\theta^{j}}\bigg\}^{1/\gamma_{0}^{j}}.

Due to the fact
\gamma_{0}>1
, we have
\sum_{j}(j/\gamma_{0}^{j})<\infty
.
It follows that
D_{k}\leq C_{\theta}
independent of
k
. Hence,

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{CD_{k}}{2\gamma_{0}^{k-j}}\leq C_{\theta}.

Consequently,

\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}_{r_{k+1}}}\phi^{2}|u|^{p_{k+1}}\bigg)^{1/p_{k+1}}\leq C_{\theta}\big(\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{r_{0}})}+\|\phi u\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{r_{0}})}\big).

Finally, taking
k\to\infty
and noting that
r_{k}>1/2
, we derive

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{B}_{1/2})}\leq C_{\theta}\big(\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{r_{0}})}+\|\phi u\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{B}_{r_{0}})}\big).

This proves the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u
be a weak solution of (
3.3
) and
F\in L^{p}(Y_{*})
for some
p>d
.
If
B
is centered on
\partial T
and
\text{\rm diam}(B)\leq c_{0}/4
, then

(3.20)

\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi\nabla u|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi\nabla u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+Cr\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

where
C
depends on
p,\phi,A
and
T
.

Proof.

Let
r=\text{diam}(B)
.
Let
{v}=\phi u
.
Using the assumption that
A
is symmetric, a computation shows that

\text{\rm div}(A\nabla v)=\text{\rm div}(A\nabla\phi)u+\phi^{-1}\text{div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla u).

As a result,
{v}
satisfies

(3.21)

-\text{\rm div}(A\nabla v)=Vu+F\quad\text{ in }Y_{*},

where

V=-\text{\rm div}(A\nabla\phi).

Note that
v=0
on
\partial T
in the sense of trace.
Under the assumptions that
A
is Hölder continuous and
V\in L^{q}(Y_{*})
for some
q>d
, it follows from (
3.21
) and
the standard elliptic regularity theory

(3.22)

\displaystyle\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B\cap Y_{*}}|\nabla{v}|

\displaystyle\leq Cr\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|Vu|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}+Cr\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq Cr\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|V|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B\cap Y_{*})}+Cr\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|V|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+Cr\bigg\{1+r\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|V|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg\}\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap Y_{*}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

where
p,q>d
and we have used Lemma
3.1
in the last inequality.

Note that
\nabla v=(\nabla\phi)u+\phi\nabla u
. Thus, by (
3.22
) and (
3.4
),

(3.23)

\displaystyle\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi\nabla u|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B\cap Y_{*}}|(\nabla\phi)u|+\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B\cap Y_{*}}|\nabla v|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg\{r^{-1}+\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|V|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg\}\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+Cr\bigg\{1+r\bigg(\fint_{B\cap Y_{*}}|V|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg\}\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap Y_{*}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Finally, observe that
u-L
satisfies the same equation
(
3.3
) for any constant
L
. Thus
\phi u
in (
3.23
) can be replaced by
\phi(u-L)
for any
L
. Moreover, it follows by a similar argument as in the proof of
the Poincaré inequality (
2.10
) that

(3.24)

\inf_{L\in\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi(u-L)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq Cr\bigg(\fint_{3B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi\nabla u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

It follows from (
3.23
) that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi\nabla u|

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{1+\|V\|_{L^{q}(Y_{*})}\big\}\bigg(\fint_{3B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi\nabla u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+Cr\big\{1+\|V\|_{L^{q}(Y_{*})}\big\}\bigg(\fint_{3B\cap Y_{*}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

By a simple covering argument, on may replace
3B\cap Y_{*}
in the right-hand side of (
3.25
) by
B\cap Y_{*}
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in V_{\varepsilon}
be a solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\phi_{\varepsilon}F\text{ in }\varepsilon(z+Y_{*}^{+})

for some
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Then for
p>d
,

(3.26)

\sup_{\varepsilon(z+Y_{*})}|u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(z+Y^{+}_{*})}|\phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(z+Y^{+}_{*})}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

and

(3.27)

\sup_{\varepsilon(z+Y_{*})}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(z+Y^{+}_{*})}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\varepsilon\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(z+Y^{+}_{*})}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Proof.

By rescaling we may assume
\varepsilon=1
.
We cover
z+Y_{*}
by a finite number of balls
\{B(x_{i},c)\}
, where either
x_{i}\in z+\partial T
or
B(x_{i},3c)\subset Y_{*}^{+}
.
In the case
x_{i}\in z+\partial T
, we use the estimates in Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
.
If
B(x_{i},3c)\subset Y_{*}^{+}
, then
\phi\approx 1
in
B(x_{i},2c)
.
This allows us to apply the classical regularity theory.
∎

Finally, we prove the local Lipschitz estimate near the boundary.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,\alpha}
domain. Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in V_{\varepsilon}
be a solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\phi_{\varepsilon}F
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.
Then for
p>d
,

(3.28)

\sup_{B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

and

(3.29)

\sup_{B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\varepsilon\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

where
B=B(x_{0},C\varepsilon)
for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

We cover
B(x_{0},C\varepsilon)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}
by a finite number of balls
\{B(x_{i},c\varepsilon)\}
such that the balls may be divided into three
cases: (1)
x_{i}\in\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial\Omega
, (2)
B(x_{i},3c\varepsilon)\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, and (3)
x_{i}\in\partial\Omega
.
The first two cases have already been discussed in the proof of the last theorem.
If dist
(x,\partial\Omega)\leq c\varepsilon
, then dist
(\partial\Omega,\partial T_{\varepsilon})\geq c_{0}\varepsilon
and thus
\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)\approx 1
.
As a result, the desired estimate for the third case
follows readily from the classical boundary Lipschitz estimates.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

If
\Omega
is only a Lipschitz domain, then we do not expect the boundary Lipschitz estimates in Theorem
3.4
. However, since the boundary
\partial\Omega
does not touch the holes, we can still get the Lipschitz estimate in a neighborhood of the holes
T
. Precisely, let
T^{\prime}
be the extended holes given by
T^{\prime}=\{x\in Y_{*}:\text{dist}(x,T)\leq c_{0}/8\}
. Then due to Lemma
3.2
, even if
\varepsilon(z+Y_{*})\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
, we still have

(3.30)

\sup_{\varepsilon(z+T^{\prime}\setminus T)}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(z+Y^{+}_{*})\cap\Omega}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\varepsilon\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(z+Y_{*}^{+})\cap\Omega}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

4.
Quantitative homogenization

In this section, we will show the quantitative convergence rate (i.e., the first-order approximation) for the degenerate equation in a perforated domain,

(4.1)

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\phi_{\varepsilon}f\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{\varepsilon}=g\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

where
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
with
p>d
and
g\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. An algebraic convergence rate will be essential in proving the large-scale uniform regularity.

We call
u_{\varepsilon}
a weak solution of (
4.1
) if
u_{\varepsilon}-g\in V_{\varepsilon}

and

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}f\psi

for any
\psi\in V_{\varepsilon}
. Since
H^{1}(\Omega)\subset W^{1,2}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
, it is not hard to see that (
4.1
) has a unique solution in
V_{\varepsilon}
satisfying

(4.2)

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\big).

4.1.
Correctors and the homogenized equation

Let

V=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(Y_{*}):\phi v\in L^{2}(Y_{*}),\ \phi\nabla v\in L^{2}(Y_{*})^{d},\int_{Y_{*}}v=0\text{ and $v$ is $Y$-periodic}\right\}

be a Hilbert space with norm
\|v\|_{V}=\|\phi\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(Y_{*})}
.
Let
\chi_{j}\in V
be the weak solution of

-\text{\rm div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla\chi_{j})=\text{\rm div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla y_{j})\quad\text{ in }Y_{*},

i.e. for any
v\in V
,

\int_{Y_{*}}\phi^{2}A\nabla\chi_{j}\cdot\nabla v=-\int_{Y_{*}}\phi^{2}A\nabla y_{j}\cdot\nabla v.

The vector-valued function
\chi=(\chi_{j})
is called the first-order correctors for the
operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=-\text{\rm div}(\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla)
.
Observe that

\text{\rm div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla(\chi_{j}+y_{j}))=0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(z+T).

It follows from Theorem
3.3
that
|\chi|\in L^{\infty}(Y_{*})
and

|\phi\nabla\chi|\in L^{\infty}(Y_{*})
.

The homogenized coefficient matrix
\widehat{A}=(\widehat{a}_{ij})
is defined by

\widehat{a}_{ij}=\int_{Y_{*}}\phi^{2}a_{ik}\partial_{k}\left(y_{j}+\chi_{j}\right)dy,

where
1\leq i,j\leq d
and the repeated index
k
is summed from
1
to
d
. Note that

\widehat{a}_{ij}=\int_{Y_{*}}\phi^{2}A\nabla(y_{j}+\chi_{j})\cdot\nabla(y_{i}+\chi_{i})\,dy.

Since
A
is positive definite and
\phi>0
in
Y_{*}
, it is not hard to see that the constant matrix
\widehat{A}
is positive
definite.
Under the assumption that
A
is symmetric,
\widehat{A}
is also symmetric.
The homogenized operator for
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
is given by

\mathcal{L}_{0}=-\text{\rm div}(\widehat{A}\nabla).

4.2.
First-order approximation

In this subsection, we derive the error estimate of the first-order approximation for the boundary value problem.
We will show that an effective approximate problem for (
4.1
) is given by

(4.3)

\mathcal{L}_{0}(u_{0})=F_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad\text{and}\quad u_{0}=g\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

where
F_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
is the extension of
\phi_{\varepsilon}f
by zero from
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
to
\Omega
.
Throughout this subsection, we only assume that
\Omega
is a Lispchitz domain satisfying the geometric assumption (
1.2
).

Let
c_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{4}c_{0}]
be a constant. Let
\eta_{\varepsilon}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a cutoff function such that
\eta_{\varepsilon}=1
if
\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>2c_{1}\varepsilon
,
\eta_{\varepsilon}=0
if
\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<c_{1}\varepsilon
, and
|\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}
. Define

\Omega(t\varepsilon)=\left\{x\in\Omega:\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<tc_{1}\varepsilon\right\}.

Observe that
\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon}
is supported in a thin layer
\Omega(2\varepsilon)\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon)=\{x\in\Omega:c_{1}\varepsilon\leq\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<2c_{1}\varepsilon\}
.
Let

(4.4)

w_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}-\varepsilon\chi_{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)(\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon}.

Since
F_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
u_{0}\in H_{\rm loc}^{2}(\Omega)
. Recall that
\chi_{\ell}
and
\phi\nabla\chi_{\ell}
are both bounded.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}.

Proof.

First of all,
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
implies
\psi\in V_{\varepsilon}
. Thus
\psi
can be used as a test function.
We calculate directly

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\nabla\psi

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\chi_{\ell})_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\ell})_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\ell})_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\ell}u_{0}\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi.

Using the equations (
4.1
) and (
4.3
), we have

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi=\int_{\Omega}\widehat{A}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\nabla\psi.

Inserting this equation into (
4.6
), we obtain

(4.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\widehat{A}-\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}-\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\chi)_{\varepsilon})(\nabla u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}(\widehat{A}-\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon})\nabla u_{0}(1-\eta_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla\psi

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\ell})_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\ell})_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\ell}u_{0}\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi.

Next, we define the flux correctors. Let
B=\widehat{A}-\phi^{2}A-\phi^{2}A\nabla\chi
, or in component form

b_{ij}=\widehat{a}_{ij}-\phi^{2}a_{ij}-\phi^{2}a_{ik}\partial_{k}\chi_{j}.

Observe that
b_{ij}\in L^{\infty}(Y)
, and

\int_{Y}b_{ij}=0,\qquad\partial_{i}b_{ij}=0.

Then it is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
) that we can find the flux correctors
\Phi=(\Phi_{kij})\in H_{\rm per}^{1}(Y)
such that

b_{ij}=\partial_{k}\Phi_{kij},\qquad\Phi_{kij}=-\Phi_{ikj}.

Moreover,
\Phi_{kij}\in C^{\alpha}(Y)
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Therefore, by using the skew-symmetry of
\Phi
and integration by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\widehat{A}-\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}-\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\chi)_{\varepsilon})(\nabla u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{k}(\varepsilon\Phi_{kij}(x/\varepsilon))(\partial_{j}u_{0})(\partial_{i}\psi)\eta_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{kij}(x/\varepsilon)(\partial_{k}\partial_{j}u_{0})(\partial_{i}\psi)\eta_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{kij}(x/\varepsilon)\partial_{j}u_{0}\partial_{i}\psi\partial_{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}.

The first integral on the right-hand side is bounded by
C\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
,
while the second integral is bounded by

(4.8)

C\varepsilon\int_{\Omega(2\varepsilon)}|\nabla u_{0}||\nabla\psi|\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}.

Finally, note that the third integral on the right-hand side of (
4.7
) is also bounded by
C\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, and the second and fourth integrals are bounded by the right-hand side of (
4.8
). Summing up all these estimates, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Now we pick a particular test function
\psi
. We point out that
w_{\varepsilon}
itself, even with a cut-off on the boundary is not a good choice for
\psi
since
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
may not lie in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
(we only know
\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
). We will use the harmonic extension to handle the possible singularity of
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
near the holes.
Let
\delta\in(0,c_{1}]
. Define

\Omega^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:\text{dist}(x,T_{\varepsilon})>\delta\varepsilon\right\}.

Note that
|\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\delta
. We shall
extend the function
u_{\varepsilon}
and
\chi(x/\varepsilon)
from
\Omega^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}
to
\Omega
with a suitable choice of
\delta
. We define
T^{\delta}=\{x\in Y:\text{dist}(x,T)\leq\delta\}
and
Y_{*}^{\delta}=Y\setminus T^{\delta}
.

Another technical tool we need is the smoothing operator. Let
0\leq\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{c_{1}}(0))
and
\int_{B_{c_{1}}(0)}\zeta=1
and define the standard smoothing operator by

\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varepsilon^{-d}\zeta(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon})f(y)dy.

Many properties about the above smoothing operators can be found in
[
13
, Chapter 3.1]
. We include some useful properties in Appendix for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
be given as above. Let
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
be the harmonic extension of
u_{\varepsilon}
from
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
to
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. It holds

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\delta^{-1}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}\big(\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle+C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon/2))}^{2}+C\delta\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}).

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{\chi}(x/\varepsilon)
denote
the harmonic extension of
\chi_{\varepsilon}=\chi(x/\varepsilon)
from
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
to
\Omega
. For simplicity, we sometimes also use the notations
(\chi_{\ell})_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)
and
(\nabla\chi)_{\varepsilon}=(\nabla\chi)(x/\varepsilon)
(similar notations apply for
\tilde{\chi}
and
\nabla\tilde{\chi}
).

Let
\eta_{\varepsilon}
be given as before. Define

w_{\varepsilon}^{*}=u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}-\varepsilon\chi_{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon},

and

w^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}-\varepsilon\tilde{\chi}_{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon}.

It is easy to verify that
w^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Write

(4.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla w_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla w_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla(w_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon}^{*})+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla(w_{\varepsilon}^{*}-w_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Estimate of
I_{1}
:
Since
w^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we apply Lemma
4.1
to obtain

(4.11)

|I_{1}|\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}\|\nabla w^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\|\nabla w^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}.

By the triangle inequality,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\|\nabla w^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|\nabla(\tilde{\chi}_{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|(\nabla\tilde{\chi})_{\varepsilon}\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon/2))}.

The boundedness of the harmonic extension (see Lemma
2.1
) implies

\|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})}\leq C\delta^{-1}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

where we used the fact
\phi_{\varepsilon}\geq c\delta
on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
. The boundedness of
\phi\nabla\chi
implies

\|\nabla\tilde{\chi}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C\|\nabla{\chi}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\delta}_{*})}\leq C\delta^{-1},

where
Y^{\delta}_{*}=\{x\in Y:\text{dist}(x,T)>\delta\}
.
Hence, we can use a property of the smoothing operator (see Lemma
A.1
) to obtain
the estimate of the third term in the right-hand side of (
4.12
),

\|(\nabla\tilde{\chi})_{\varepsilon}\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}\leq C\|\nabla\tilde{\chi}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\delta^{-1}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

To handle the second term in (
4.11
), we note that
\Omega(2\varepsilon)
is contained in
\Omega^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}
, provided that
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4}c_{1})
. Thus
w^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}=w^{*}_{\varepsilon}
and
\phi_{\varepsilon}\approx 1
in
\Omega(2\varepsilon)
. Consequently,

\displaystyle C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\|\nabla w^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla(w^{*}_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}^{2}.

Observe that
w_{\varepsilon}^{*}-w_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\chi_{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)(\partial_{\ell}u_{0}-\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0}))\eta_{\varepsilon}
and

\displaystyle\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla(w_{\varepsilon}^{*}-w_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\phi_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\chi_{\ell})_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0}-\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0}))\eta_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\phi_{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\ell})_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\partial_{\ell}u_{0}-\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\partial_{\ell}u_{0}))\eta_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\ell})_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0}-\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0})).

Now, by using the boundedness of
\chi
and
\phi\nabla\chi
, as well as the properties of the smoothing operator
\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}
(see Lemma
A.1
and Lemma
A.2
), we see that

(4.13)

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla(w^{*}_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon/2))}.

As a result of (
4.11
) - (
4.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\delta^{-1}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}\big(\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle+C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon/2))}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}.

Estimate of
I_{2}
:
It follows from (
4.13
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

|I_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon/2))}^{2}.

Estimate of
I_{3}
:
Again, it follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

(4.14)

|I_{3}|\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+4\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla(w^{*}_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}.

Recall that
w_{\varepsilon}^{*}=w_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
. Thus
\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla(w^{*}_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})
is supported in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
. Moreover,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla(w^{*}_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})

\displaystyle=\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle-\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla(\varepsilon\chi_{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon})+\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla(\varepsilon\tilde{\chi}_{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon}).

We estimate the last term of the above identity over
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
. Note that
\phi_{\varepsilon}\leq C\delta
and
\eta_{\varepsilon}=1
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
. It follows from the triangle inequality and the boundedness of
\tilde{\chi}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla(\varepsilon\tilde{\chi}_{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}}|\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla^{2}u_{0})|^{2}+C\delta^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}}|\nabla\tilde{\chi}(x/\varepsilon)|^{2}|\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon)}|\nabla^{2}u_{0}|^{2}+C\delta^{2}\|\nabla\tilde{\chi}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{*}\setminus Y^{\delta}_{*})}^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2},

where we have used Lemma
A.1
in the last inequality. By the
L^{2}
regularity estimate of the nontangential maximal function for the harmonic function
\tilde{\chi}
in the Lipschitz holes
T^{\delta}=\{x\in Y:\text{dist}(x,T)<\delta\}
, we have (see Lemma
A.5
in Appendix)

\|\nabla\tilde{\chi}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{*}\setminus Y^{\delta}_{*})}^{2}\leq C\delta\|\nabla_{\rm\tan}\chi\|_{L^{2}(\partial T^{\delta})}^{2}\leq C\delta^{-1}.

Hence,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla(\varepsilon\tilde{\chi}_{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\leq C\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon)}|\nabla^{2}u_{0}|^{2}+C\delta\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}.

Similarly (and easier), using the boundedness of
\chi
and
\phi\nabla\chi
, we have

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla(\varepsilon{\chi}_{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{0})\eta_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\leq C\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon)}|\nabla^{2}u_{0}|^{2}+C\delta\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}.

Combining these with (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2})

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}^{2}+C\delta\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Finally, the desired estimate follows from (
4.10
) and the estimates of
I_{1},I_{2}
and
I_{3}
.
∎

4.3.
Convergence rates

In general, if
\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain and
g\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
, we do not have
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, though we still have
u_{0}\in H^{2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
due to the interior
H^{2}
estimate. However, we have the following estimates proved in
[
12
]
(a sketch of the proof is also given in Appendix).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain and let
u_{0}
be the weak solution of (
4.3
) with
F=F_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
g\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
. Then

(4.16)

\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(c\varepsilon))}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\big),

and

(4.17)

\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\big).

The above lemma indicates that the right-hand side of (
4.9
) is small by choosing
\delta
appropriately small, except for the last integral. The smallness of the last integral follows from the small-scale higher integrability of
\phi\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
and
\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
. In particular, the small-scale Lipschitz estimate proved earlier ensures the smallness of the last integral and therefore a (suboptimal) convergence rate.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain. Let
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
for some
p>d
and
u_{\varepsilon}
a solution of (
4.1
). Then

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\delta\big(\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2-\frac{2d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\big).

Proof.

This lemma essentially relies only on the small-scale interior Lipschitz estimate, i.e., Theorem
3.3
and Remark
3.5
. Indeed, for
\delta<c_{0}/8
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon(z+T)\subset\Omega}\int_{\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}\setminus T)}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\varepsilon(z+T)\subset\Omega}C\varepsilon^{d}\delta\sup_{\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}\setminus T)}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\varepsilon(z+T)\subset\Omega}C\varepsilon^{d}\delta\bigg\{\fint_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap\varepsilon(z+Y_{*}^{+})}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap\varepsilon(z+Y_{*}^{+})}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{2/p}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\varepsilon(z+T)\subset\Omega}C\delta\bigg\{\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap\varepsilon(z+Y_{*}^{+})}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{d}\varepsilon^{2-\frac{2d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\big(\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2-\frac{2d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\big).

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the same as in Lemma
4.4
and

\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
the harmonic extension of
u_{\varepsilon}
from
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
to
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Then for
\delta<c_{0}/8
,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\delta\big(\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2-\frac{2d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\big).

Proof.

First, we write

(4.18)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}=\sum_{\varepsilon(z+T)\subset\Omega}\int_{\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}\setminus T)}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

We then consider a single cell
\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}\setminus T)\subset\Omega
. By Theorem
3.3
and Remark
3.5
,

(4.19)

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varepsilon(z+Y_{*}\setminus T^{\delta}))}\leq C\delta^{-1}\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(z+Y^{+}_{*})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\varepsilon\delta^{-1}\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(z+Y_{*})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Recall that
\Delta\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=0
in
\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta})
and
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}
on
\varepsilon(z+\partial T^{\delta})
. Also,
T^{\delta}=\cup_{i}\tau_{i}^{\delta}
is a union of pairwise disjoint Lipschitz holes with connected boundaries.
In each hole, we can apply the
L^{2}
regularity estimate of the nontangential estimate (see (
A.2
) in Appendix) to obtain

\displaystyle\|(\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\varepsilon(z+\partial T^{\delta}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{\rm tan}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\varepsilon(z+\partial T^{\delta}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\varepsilon(z+\partial T^{\delta}))}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varepsilon(z+Y_{*}\setminus T^{\delta}))}.

Consequently,

(4.20)

\int_{\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}\setminus T)}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\delta^{3}\varepsilon\int_{\varepsilon(z+\partial T^{\delta})}|(\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})^{*}|^{2}\leq C\delta^{3}\varepsilon^{d}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varepsilon(z+Y_{*}\setminus T^{\delta}))}^{2}.

Combining (
4.19
) and (
4.20
), we see that

\int_{\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}\setminus T)}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\delta\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y^{+}_{*})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+C\delta\varepsilon^{d}\varepsilon^{2-\frac{2d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}.

Summing over
z
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\delta\big(\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2-\frac{2d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\big),

as desired.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain,
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
for some
p>d
and
g\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
. Then

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\big).

Proof.

First, by the energy estimate (
4.2
) and Lemma
4.3
, we have

\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(c\varepsilon))}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(c\varepsilon))}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\big).

Hence, Lemma
4.2
implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-1}+\varepsilon+\delta)\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\big)+C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}\big(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-1}+\varepsilon+\delta)\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\big),

where we have used Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
in the second inequality. Since
\delta
is arbitrary, we pick
\delta=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}
to obtain the desired estimate.
∎

The following local convergence rate is particularly useful for us. Let
Q_{r}
be a cube with side length
r
and
Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}=Q_{r}\setminus T_{\varepsilon}
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon})
be the weak solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\phi_{\varepsilon}f
in
Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}
with
r=m\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon
. There exists a weak solution
u_{0}\in H^{1}(Q_{r})
of the homogenized equation
\mathcal{L}_{0}(u_{0})=\phi_{\varepsilon}f
in
Q_{\frac{4}{3}r}
such that
w_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

(4.21)

\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigg\{\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+r^{2}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{2/p}\bigg\},

where
w_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}-\varepsilon\chi(x/\varepsilon)\cdot\nabla u_{0}
in
Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}
. Moreover,

(4.22)

\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}|^{2}\leq C\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigg\{\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+r\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{2/p}\bigg\},

The estimates still hold if
Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}
is replaced by
Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}
with
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}(x_{0})
.

Proof.

We give the proof for (
4.21
).
The inequality (
4.22
) follows from (
4.21
) by using a weighted Poincaré’s inequality and Caccioppoli’s inequality.
By rescaling, it suffices to show (
4.21
) for the case
r=1
. Let
t\in(1,\frac{3}{2})
be a suitable side length such that
Q_{t}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies the geometric assumption (
1.2
) (in particular
\partial Q_{t}\subset Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}
) and

(4.23)

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\partial Q_{t})}\leq C\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(Q^{\varepsilon}_{3/2})}.

The choice of such

is possible due to the assumptions on

and the co-area formula.
Let
u_{0}
be the weak solution of

(4.24)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{0}(u_{0})&=\phi_{\varepsilon}f,\quad\text{in }Q_{t}^{\varepsilon},\\
u_{0}&=u_{\varepsilon},\quad\text{on }\partial Q_{t}.\end{aligned}\right.

Taking the estimates in Theorem
4.7
into Lemma
4.2
with

, we obtain

(4.25)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\partial Q_{t})}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(Q^{\varepsilon}_{2})}^{2}\big).

Here the cutoff function
\eta_{\varepsilon}
in the original definition of

(

Finally, notice that we can subtract any constant

from

in the above estimate. Hence,

∎

Remark 4.8
.

The exponent
\frac{1}{4}
in Theorem
4.6
and Theorem

is not optimal . By adjusting the cutoff function

, we can actually improve it to

. If we know a priori that

(for instance,
\Omega

and

), then it may be improved to

holds under the condition

or
\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla f\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
(even with

[
11
, Chapter III.3]
.

5.
Lipschitz estimates

In Section
3
, we have proved the small-scale Lipschitz estimate.
In this section, we establish the large-scale Lipschitz estimate which leads to the full-scale Lipschitz estimate. Our argument here follows from the scheme of
[
12
]
(also see
[
13
]
).

We define

(5.1)

H_{\varepsilon}(u;r)=\inf_{M\in\mathbb{R}^{d},q\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{r}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u-M\cdot x-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+r\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

and

(5.2)

I_{\varepsilon}(u;r)=\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{r}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+r\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Recall that the interior Caccioppoli inequality for the weak solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\phi_{\varepsilon}f
can be written as

(5.3)

\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq CI_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};2r).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\phi_{\varepsilon}f
in
Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}
with
f\in L^{p}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon})
for some
p>d
. Then there exist
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,

(5.4)

H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\theta)\leq\frac{1}{2}H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};1)+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}I(u_{\varepsilon};2).

Proof.

Let
u_{0}
be given as in the proof of Theorem
4.7
with
r=1
. Then

(5.5)

\bigg(\fint_{Q_{1}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};2).

Note that we have replaced
u_{\varepsilon}
by
u_{\varepsilon}-L
on the right-hand side of (
4.22
) and taken infimum over all
L\in\mathbb{R}
.
On the other hand, the energy estimate and (
4.23
) implie

(5.6)

\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{t})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{3/2}^{\varepsilon})}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q_{3/2}^{\varepsilon})}.

By the interior estimate of the equation
\mathcal{L}_{0}(u_{0})=\phi_{\varepsilon}f
in
Q_{1}
,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(Q_{3/4})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{3/2}^{\varepsilon})}+C\|f\|_{L^{p}(Q_{3/2}^{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq CI(u_{\varepsilon};2),

where
\alpha=1-\frac{d}{p}
and we have used the Caccioppoli estimate in the last line.

Now let
\theta\in(0,1/2)
. By the triangle inequality and (
5.5
)

(5.8)

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\theta)

\displaystyle\leq H_{\varepsilon}(u_{0};\theta)+\frac{1}{\theta}\bigg(\fint_{Q^{\varepsilon}_{\theta}}|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq H_{\varepsilon}(u_{0};\theta)+\frac{C}{\theta^{1+d/2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};2).

Next, we claim that for any
\theta\in(0,1/2)
(independent of
\varepsilon
),

(5.9)

H_{\varepsilon}(u_{0};\theta)\leq C\theta^{\alpha}H_{\varepsilon}(u_{0};3/4)+C\varepsilon I_{\varepsilon}(u_{0};2).

In fact, for
\theta\in(0,1/2)
, by observing that
\mathcal{L}_{0}(u_{0}-M\cdot x-q)=\mathcal{L}_{0}(u_{0})=\phi_{\varepsilon}f
in
Q_{1}
for any
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
q\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(5.10)

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(u_{0};\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{\alpha}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{C^{\alpha}(Q_{1/2})}+\theta^{1-d/p}\|F\|_{L^{p}(Q^{\varepsilon}_{\theta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{\alpha}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{Q_{3/4}}|u_{0}-M_{0}\cdot x-q_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(Q^{\varepsilon}_{3/4})}\bigg\},

where we choose
M_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
q_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(5.11)

\bigg(\fint_{Q_{3/4}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{0}-M_{0}\cdot x-q_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}=\inf_{M\in\mathbb{R}^{d},q\in\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{3/4}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{0}-M\cdot x-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Let
a_{0}=\int_{Y_{*}}\phi^{2}>0
. Then it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\sqrt{a_{0}}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{3/4}}|M_{0}\cdot x+q_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{3/4}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|M_{0}\cdot x+q_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{3/4}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

This further implies that

(5.12)

|M_{0}|+|q_{0}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{3/4}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{3/4})}.

Moreover,
there exists a bounded
Y
-periodic vector-valued function
\psi
such that
\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\psi_{\varepsilon})=\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}-a_{0}
.
It follows from the integration by parts, (
5.12
) and (
5.7
) that

\displaystyle\bigg|\bigg(\fint_{Q_{3/4}}(\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}-a_{0})|u_{0}-M_{0}\cdot x-q_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\bigg(\fint_{\partial Q_{3/4}}|\psi_{\varepsilon}\cdot n|^{2}|u_{0}-M_{0}\cdot x-q_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\bigg(\fint_{Q_{3/4}}|\psi_{\varepsilon}||u_{0}-M_{0}\cdot x-q_{0}||\nabla u_{0}-M_{0}|\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|u_{0}\|_{C^{0,1}(Q_{3/4})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};2).

This, combined with (
5.10
) and (
5.11
), gives

H(u_{0};\theta)\leq C\theta^{\alpha}H(u_{0};3/4)+C\varepsilon I(u_{\varepsilon};2).

Thus, the claim (
5.9
) is proved.

Finally, by (
5.8
), (
5.9
) and (
5.5
), we arrive at

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\theta)

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{\alpha}H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};3/4)+C\theta^{-1-\frac{d}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}I(u_{\varepsilon};2)

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{\alpha}H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};1)+C\theta^{-1-\frac{d}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}I(u_{\varepsilon};2).

Note that the constant
C
above is independent of
\theta
.
As a result, we can choose and fix
\theta\in(0,1/2)
such that
C\theta^{\alpha}\leq 1/2
. This gives (
5.4
) as desired.
∎

We recall an iteration lemma.

Lemma 5.2

(
[

)
.

Let

and

be two nonnegative, continuous functions on the interval

. Let

. Suppose that there exists a constant

such that

\max_{r\leq t\leq 2r}H(t)\leq C_{0}H(2r)\quad\text{and}\quad\max_{r\leq s,t\leq 2r}|h(s)-h(t)|\leq C_{0}H(2r)

for any

. Suppose further that

(5.14)

H(\theta r)\leq\frac{1}{2}H(r)+C_{0}\beta(\varepsilon/r)(H(2r)+h(2r)),

for any

, where

and

is a nonnegative, nondecreasing function on

such that

\max_{\varepsilon\leq r\leq 1}(H(r)+h(r))\leq C(H(1)+h(1)),

where

depends only on

and the function
\beta(t)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Under the same condition as Lemma
5.1
, for
\varepsilon\leq r\leq 2
, we have

(5.17)

\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Proof.

By rescaling, (
5.4
) holds for any
\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0}\leq r\leq 1
, i.e.,

(5.18)

H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\theta r)\leq\frac{1}{2}H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};r)+C_{0}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{8}}I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};2r).

To apply Lemma
5.2
, we set
H(t)=H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};t)
. In view of the definition of
H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};t)
, we can find
M_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

H(t)=H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};t)=\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{t}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u-M_{t}\cdot x-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+t\bigg(\fint_{Q_{t}^{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Define
h(t)=|M_{t}|
. Then it is obvious that

(5.19)

I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};t)\leq H(t)+Ch(t).

Hence, we can rewrite (
5.18
) as, for
\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0}\leq r\leq 1/2
,

H(\theta r)\leq\frac{1}{2}H(r)+C_{0}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{8}}(H(2r)+h(2r)),

which verifies the main condition (
5.14
) in Lemma
5.2
with
\beta(t)=t^{\frac{1}{8}}
. Clearly, this particular
\beta(t)
satisfies (
5.15
).

Finally, to see (
5.13
), we only need to use the properties of
H(t)=H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};t)
and
h(t)=|M_{t}|
. In fact, for any
r\leq t\leq 2r
, we have
H(t)\leq CH(2r)
by enlarging the region from
Q^{\varepsilon}_{t}
to
Q^{\varepsilon}_{2r}
with comparable volumes. Moreover, for any
r\leq s,t\leq 2r
,

\displaystyle|h(s)-h(t)|\leq|M_{s}-M_{t}|\leq\frac{C}{r}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|(M_{s}-M_{t})\cdot x|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{r}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{\varepsilon}-M_{s}\cdot x-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{r}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{\varepsilon}-M_{t}\cdot x-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{r}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{\varepsilon}-M_{s}\cdot x-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{r}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{\varepsilon}-M_{t}\cdot x-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq CH(s)+CH(t)\leq C_{0}H(2r).

This verifies the condition (
5.13
). It follows from Lemma
5.2
that

\max_{\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0}\leq r\leq 1}(H(r)+h(r))\leq C(H(1)+h(1)),

By the Caccioppli inequality (
5.3
) and (
5.19
), for
\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0}\leq r\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{Q^{\varepsilon}_{r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};2r)\leq C(H(2r)+h(2r))

\displaystyle\leq C(H(1)+h(1))

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

where in the last inequality we also used the weighted Poincaré inequality (
2.18
) and the size estimate of
h(1)
. Note that the estimate in the range
\varepsilon\leq r\leq\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0}
follows trivially from the case
r=\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0}
with a larger constant
C
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The above estimates continue to hold near the boundary if
\Omega
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
domain and
u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\partial\Omega
. In fact, if
Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}
is centered on
\partial\Omega
, then we only need to replace
Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}
by
Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega
in the statement of Theorem
5.3
. For the proof, we modify the function
H_{\varepsilon}
and
I_{\varepsilon}
as follows:

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(u;r)

\displaystyle=\inf_{M\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{r}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u-M\cdot x|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\|M\cdot x\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}\cap\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle+r\|\nabla_{\rm tan}(M\cdot x)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}\cap\partial\Omega)}+r^{2}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

and

I_{\varepsilon}(u;r)=\frac{1}{r}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+r\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

The details are omitted (see
[
12
]
for a similar proof).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(i).

Without loss of generality assume
\text{diam}(\Omega)\approx 1
.
First, by the small-scale Lipschitz estimate (
3.29
), we have

\sup_{Q_{\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{m_{0}\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\varepsilon\bigg(\fint_{Q_{m_{0}\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Then by the large-scale Lipschitz estimate (
5.17
) for
r=m_{0}\varepsilon
(including the boundary case in Remark
5.4
), we have

\bigg(\fint_{Q_{m_{0}\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Combining the last two estimates, we obtain

\sup_{Q_{\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Since the above estimate can be translated arbitrarily, it implies

(5.20)

\sup_{Q_{1}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

and

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

This implies (
1.7
) by the energy estimate (
4.2
).
∎

6.
W^{1,p}
estimates

In this section, we prove the global
W^{1,p}
estimates uniform in
\varepsilon
in both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. Like Lipschitz estimate, the small-scale and large-scale estimates are handled separately.

We begin with the small-scale
W^{1,p}
estimate. In view of the discussion in Section
3
, it is sufficient to focus on the boundary case II.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
p>2
and
f\in L^{p}(Y_{*})^{d},F\in L^{p}(Y_{*})
. Suppose that
u
is a weak solution of

-\text{\rm div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla u)=\text{\rm div}(\phi f)+F,\quad\text{\rm in }Y_{*}.

Then if
B_{0}
is a ball centered on
\partial T
and
\text{\rm diam}(B_{0})\leq c_{0}/4
, then

\bigg(\fint_{\frac{1}{2}B_{0}\cap Y_{*}}|\phi\nabla u|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{2B_{0}\cap Y_{*}}|\phi\nabla u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{2B_{0}\cap Y_{*}}(|f|^{p}+|F|^{p})\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\}.

The proof relies on the following interior real-variable argument.

Theorem 6.2

(
[
13
, Theorem 4.2.3]
)
.

Let
q>2
and
B_{0}
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
U\in L^{2}(4B_{0})
and
f\in L^{p}(4B_{0})
for some
2<p<q
. Suppose that for each ball or cube
B\subset 2B_{0}
with properties that
\text{\rm diam}(B)\leq r_{0}\text{\rm diam}(B_{0})
, there exist two measurable function
U_{B}
and
R_{B}
defined in
2B
, such that
|U|\leq|U_{B}|+|R_{B}|
in
2B
, and

\bigg(\fint_{{2B}}|R_{B}|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\leq N_{1}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{{4B}}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{{4B}}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\},

\bigg(\fint_{{2B}}|U_{B}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq N_{2}\bigg(\fint_{{4B}}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\eta\bigg(\fint_{{4B}}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
N_{1},N_{2}>1
and
0<r_{0}<1
. Then there exists
\eta_{0}>0
, depending only on
N_{1},N_{2},r_{0},p
and
q
, with the property that if
0\leq\eta<\eta_{0}
, then
U\in L^{p}(B_{0})
and

(6.1)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{0}}|U|^{p}dx\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{4B_{0}}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{4B_{0}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\}.

where
C
depends at most on
N_{1},N_{2},r_{0},p
and
q
.

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Without loss of generality, assume
\text{diam}(B_{0})=c_{0}/10
. Let
B\subset B_{0}
be a ball such that either
4B\subset Y_{*}
or
B
is centered on
\partial T
. For the first case,
\phi(x)\approx\phi(y)\approx\text{diam}(B)
for any
x,y\in 2B
. Let
v
be the weak solution of

-\text{div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla v)=\text{\rm div}(\phi f)+F\quad\text{in }2B,\quad\text{ and }\quad v=0\quad\text{on }\partial(2B).

By the energy estimate, we have

(6.2)

\|\phi\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(2B)}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(2B)}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(2B)}\big).

Let
w=u-v
. Then
w
satisfies

-\text{div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla w)=0\quad\text{in }2B,\quad\text{ and }\quad w=u\quad\text{on }\partial(2B).

Due to the fact
\phi(x)\approx\phi(y)
for
x,y\in 2B
, the classical Lipschitz estimate yields

(6.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{B}|\phi\nabla w|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{2B}|\phi\nabla w|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{2B}|\phi\nabla u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{2B}(|f|+|F|)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where we have used (
6.2
) in the last inequality.

For the second case that
B
is centered on
\partial T
, we again let
v
be the weak solution of

-\text{div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla v)=\text{\rm div}(\phi f)+F\quad\text{in }2B\cap Y_{*},\quad\text{ and }\quad v=0\quad\text{on }\partial(2B)\cap Y_{*}.

The energy estimate implies

(6.4)

\|\phi\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(2B\cap Y_{*})}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(2B\cap Y_{*})}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(2B\cap Y_{*})}\big).

Then
w=u-v
satisfies

-\text{div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla w)=0\quad\text{in }2B\cap Y_{*},\quad\text{ and }\quad w=u\quad\text{on }\partial(2B)\cap Y_{*}.

Now, we use (
3.2
) to get

(6.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi\nabla w|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi\nabla w|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap Y_{*}}|\phi\nabla u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{2B\cap Y_{*}}(|f|+|F|)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where we have used (
6.4
) in the last inequality.

Now, we explain how the interior real-variable argument is applied in
B_{0}
to
U=\phi\nabla u
. Let
U
be extended across
\partial T
to
2B_{0}
by zero. For each
B\subset B_{0}
,
B
can be covered by a finite number of balls
B^{\prime}_{j}
(with finite overlaps) satisfying either one of the following three cases: (i)
4B^{\prime}_{j}\subset Y_{*}
; (ii)
B^{\prime}_{j}
is centered on
\partial T
; (iii)
4B\cap Y_{*}=\emptyset
. Moreover,
\text{diam}(B^{\prime}_{j})\approx\text{diam}(B)
. In all three cases, we have
|U|\leq U_{j}+R_{j}
in
2B^{\prime}_{j}
with
U_{j}=|\phi\nabla v|
and
R_{j}=|\phi\nabla w|
supported in
2B^{\prime}_{j}
, where
v
and
w
have been constructed previously for cases (i) and (ii). The case (iii) is trivial since
U=0
in
2B_{0}\setminus Y_{*}
. Therefore, by (
6.2
), (
6.3
), (
6.4
) and (
6.5
), we have

\sup_{B^{\prime}_{j}}|R_{j}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{2B^{\prime}_{j}}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{2B^{\prime}_{j}}(|f|+|F|)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

and

\bigg(\fint_{2B^{\prime}_{j}}|U_{B}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{2B^{\prime}_{j}}(|f|+|F|)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
F
and
f
are also extended by zero. Now, using
B\subset\cup_{j}B^{\prime}_{j}
and setting
R_{B}=\sum_{j}R_{j}
and
U_{B}=\sum_{j}U_{j}
, we have
|U|\leq R_{B}+U_{B}
and

\sup_{B}|R_{B}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{2B}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{2B}(|f|+|F|)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

and

\bigg(\fint_{B}|U_{B}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{2B}(|f|+|F|)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Since
B\subset B_{0}
is arbitrary, we apply Theorem
6.2
with
\eta=0
to obtain

\bigg(\fint_{\frac{1}{2}B_{0}}|U|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{2B_{0}}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{2B_{0}}(|f|+|F|)^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Since
U=\phi\nabla u
and
f,F
are extended by zero in the holes, the last inequality implies the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{d},F\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{\phi_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
be the weak solution of (
1.9
) in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.
Suppose
\varepsilon(k+Y_{*})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset
. Then,

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+Y_{*})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+Y_{*}^{+})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+Y_{*}^{+})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}+C\varepsilon\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+Y_{*}^{+})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Proof.

We first rescale the problem. Let
v(x)=u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon x)
. Then
v
satisfies

-\text{div}(\phi^{2}A\nabla v)=\text{\rm div}(\varepsilon\phi f(\varepsilon\cdot))+\varepsilon^{2}F(\varepsilon\cdot)\quad\text{in }(k+Y_{*}^{+})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

Moreover,
v=0
on
(k+Y_{*}^{+})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\partial\Omega
. Then, by Lemma
6.1
and the classical
W^{1,p}
estimate away from
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{(k+Y_{*})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi\nabla v|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{(k+Y_{*}^{+})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi\nabla v|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\bigg(\fint_{(k+Y_{*}^{+})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|f(\varepsilon\cdot)|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+Y_{*}^{+})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|F(\varepsilon\cdot)|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Rescaling back to
u_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

estimate uniform in

Theorem 6.4

(A variant of
[
13
, Theorem 4.2.6]
)
.

Let
q>2
and
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Let
U\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and

for some

. Let
0\leq t<r_{0}\text{\rm diam}(\Omega)
be a fixed number. Suppose that for each ball or cube
B
with properties that

and either
4B\subset\Omega
or
B
is centered on
\partial\Omega
, there exist two measurable function
U_{B}
and
R_{B}
on
\Omega\cap 2B
, such that
|U|\leq|U_{B}|+|R_{B}|
on
\Omega\cap 2B
, and

(6.6)

\bigg(\fint_{\Omega\cap 2B}|R_{B}|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\leq N_{1}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{\Omega\cap 4B}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{\Omega\cap 4B}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\},

(6.7)

\bigg(\fint_{\Omega\cap 2B}|U_{B}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq N_{2}\bigg(\fint_{\Omega\cap 4B}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\eta\bigg(\fint_{\Omega\cap 4B}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where

and

. Then there exists
\eta_{0}>0
, depending only on

and the Lipschtiz character of

, with the property that if

, then
U\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and

(6.8)

\bigg(\fint_{\Omega}\bigg(\fint_{B_{t}(x)\cap\Omega}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{p/2}dx\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{\Omega}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{\Omega}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where

depends at most on

. In particular, if

Theorem 6.5
.

Assume that
\Omega
is
C^{1}
or convex. Let
p>2
and
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{d}
and
F\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the weak solution of (
1.9
). Then there exits
m_{0}>0
such that for any
m_{0}\varepsilon\leq r\leq\text{\rm diam}(\Omega)
, we have

(6.10)

\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{p/2}dx\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\big).

Proof.

We will apply Theorem
6.4
to
U=\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
with balls replaced by cubes. Let
Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}(x)=Q_{r}(x)\setminus T_{\varepsilon}
with
r=m\varepsilon
for some integer
m\geq m_{0}
. To make sure that
Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}(x)
satisfies the geometric assumption (
1.2
), we temporarily assume that
x
is the center of some cell
\varepsilon(k+Y)
. Moreover, we will consider
Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}(x)
such that either
Q_{4r}^{\varepsilon}(x)\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}
or
Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}(x)
is centered in a boundary cell (i.e., a cell intersecting with
\partial\Omega
).
Consider

(6.11)

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\text{div}(\phi_{\varepsilon}f)+F\quad\text{in }Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega,\quad\text{and}\quad u_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{on }Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega.

Let
v_{\varepsilon}
be the solution of

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})=0\quad\text{in }Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega,\quad\text{and}\quad v_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{on }\partial(Q_{2r}\cap\Omega).

Then
w_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(w_{\varepsilon})=\text{div}(\phi_{\varepsilon}f)+F\quad\text{in }Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega,\quad\text{and}\quad w_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{on }\partial(Q_{2r}\cap\Omega).

Recall that
Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega
satisfies the geometric assumption (
1.2
). Thus, the energy estimate implies

(6.12)

\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|F|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Next, we consider the regularity of
v_{\varepsilon}
. We distinguish between two cases. If
Q_{4r}^{\varepsilon}
is contained in
\Omega
, then we have already proved the local uniform Lipschitz estimate of
v_{\varepsilon}
in (

) for
r>m_{0}\varepsilon
, i.e.,

(6.13)

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

In this case, let
U=\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
,

and

. Then by (

\|R_{Q}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{\varepsilon}_{r})}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}}(|f|+|F|)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

for

and

\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}}|U_{Q}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}}(|f|+|F|)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Previously we have assumed
r=m\varepsilon
with some integer

and
Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}
is centered in a cell
\varepsilon(k+Y_{*})
. But these assumptions can be removed by slightly adjusting the size of the cubes. Also the holes can be removed simply by a natural zero-extension. Hence, the conditions (

) and (
6.7

.

If
Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}
is centered in a boundary cell, we apply Theorem
4.7
to approximate
v_{\varepsilon}
with good functions such that the real-variable argument can apply. Precisely, by Theorem

v_{0}
satisfying

\mathcal{L}_{0}(v_{0})=0\quad\text{in }Q_{\frac{4}{3}r}\cap\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad v_{0}=0\quad\text{on }Q_{\frac{4}{3}r}\cap\partial\Omega,

and

(6.14)

\fint_{Q^{\varepsilon}_{r}\cap\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}-\nabla v_{0}-\nabla\chi(x/\varepsilon)\cdot\nabla v_{0}|^{2}\leq C\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Since

is

or convex, for any

,
\nabla v_{0}\in L^{q}(Q_{r}\cap\Omega)
. Hence, by the boundedness of
\phi\nabla\chi
, we obtain for some
q>p
,

\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{q}|\nabla v_{0}+\nabla\chi(x/\varepsilon)\nabla v_{0}|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Thus, if we set
U=\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
,
R_{Q}=\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{0}+\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla\chi(x/\varepsilon)\cdot\nabla v_{0}
and
U_{Q}=U-R_{Q}
, then

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|R_{Q}|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}(|f|+|F|)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

and

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|U_{Q}|\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{8}}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}|U|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{2r}^{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega}(|f|+|F|)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

Let
m_{0}>0
be large enough such that

Cm_{0}^{-\frac{1}{8}}<\eta_{0},

where
\eta_{0}
is given in Theorem
6.4
depending on
C,p,q
and
\Omega
. Hence, the conditions (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) are also satisfied in this boundary case provided
r>m_{0}\varepsilon
. As a consequence of Theorem
6.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{r}^{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{p/2}dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg\{|\Omega|^{1/p-1/2}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\int_{\Omega}(|f|+|F|)^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

for all
r>m_{0}\varepsilon
. This proves the theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Note that
p=2
corresponds to exactly the energy estimate. Let
p>2
. Then Theorem
6.5
with
r=m_{0}\varepsilon
implies

(6.15)

\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{m_{0}\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{p/2}dx\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\big).

Now Lemma
6.3
implies for any
x\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{Q_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{m_{0}\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{m_{0}\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}+C\bigg(\fint_{Q_{m_{0}\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Substituting the last inequality into (
6.15
), we obtain

(6.16)

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\bigg[\fint_{Q_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\bigg]dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\big)

\displaystyle+C\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\bigg[\fint_{Q_{m_{0}\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg]dx\bigg)^{1/p}+C\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\bigg[\fint_{Q_{m_{0}\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|F|^{p}\bigg]dx\bigg)^{1/p}.

It is not hard to see that for any
g\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\bigg[\fint_{Q_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|g|^{p}\bigg]dx\approx\int_{\Omega}\bigg[\fint_{Q_{m_{0}\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|g|^{p}\bigg]dx\approx\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|g|^{p}.

This and (
6.16
) imply the desired estimate for
p>2
.

Finally, the case
1<p<2
follows from the case
p>2
by duality.
∎

(ii).

This is a dual statement of Theorem
1.2
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the weak solution of (
1.6
) with
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
and
1<p<d
. Let
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)^{d}
and

be the weak solution of

(6.17)

-\text{\rm div}(\phi^{2}_{\varepsilon}A_{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon})=\text{\rm div}(\phi_{\varepsilon}g)\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ and }\quad v_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Then, by the equation (
1.6
) and (

), we have

and Theorem

(i) imply

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot g\bigg|

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

where

and

, i.e.,

Appendix

Smoothing operators.

Let
\alpha>0
be fixed. Let
0\leq\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\alpha}(0))
and
\int_{B_{\alpha}(0)}\zeta=1
and define the standard smoothing operator by

\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}f(x)=\int_{B_{\alpha\varepsilon}(x)}\varepsilon^{-d}\zeta(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon})f(y)dy=\int_{B_{\alpha\varepsilon}(0)}\varepsilon^{-d}\zeta(\frac{y}{\varepsilon})f(x-y)dy.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Omega(\varepsilon)=\{x\in\Omega:\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<\alpha\varepsilon\}
. Note that if
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, then
\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}f
is well-defined in
\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon)
.
Some properties of the smoothing operator are listed below, whose proofs may be found in
[
13
, Chapter 3.1]
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Assume
g\in L^{p}_{\rm per}(Y)
and
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. Then

\|g(x/\varepsilon)\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

where
C
depends only on
p
and
\alpha
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Then

\|\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}f-f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

where
C
depends only on
p
and
\alpha
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
q=\frac{2d}{d+1}<2
. Assume
g\in L^{2}_{\rm per}(Y)
and
f\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
. Then

\int_{\Omega(2t)\setminus\Omega(t)}|g(x/\varepsilon)|^{2}|\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}f|^{2}\leq Ct\|g\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}\|f\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{2},

where
t\geq\varepsilon
and
C
depends only on
\Omega
and
\alpha
.

The following is a related lemma without smoothing.

Lemma A.4
.

Under the same assumption as Lemma
A.3
, we have

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}\leq Ct^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)},

for any
t>0
.

Nontangential maximal functions.

We consider the solvability and regularity of the Dirichlet problem in a Lipschitz domain:

(A.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=0&\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=f&\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Assume
f\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. Define the nontangential maximal function
u^{*}(Q)
with
Q\in\partial\Omega
by

u^{*}(Q)=\sup\{|u(x)|:x\in\Omega\text{ and }\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>\beta|x-Q|\},

where
\beta
is a fixed constant chosen according to the Lipschitz character of
\partial\Omega
. It has been proved (see
[
4
]
) that (
A.1
) is solvable with
f\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
and

\|u^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

Similarly, we may define

(\nabla u)^{*}(Q)=\sup\{|\nabla u(x)|:x\in\Omega\text{ and }\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>\beta|x-Q|\}.

Then if, in addition,
f\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
and
\partial\Omega
is connected, it was proved (see
[
9
]
) that

(A.2)

\|(\nabla u)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla_{\rm tan}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

As a simple corollary, we have

Lemma A.5
.

Let
f\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
and
u
be a solution of (
A.1
). Assume
\partial\Omega
is connected.
Then

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}\leq Ct^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla_{\rm tan}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

for any
t>0
.

Sketch of the proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Let
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\mathcal{L}_{0}(v)=F\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\|v\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. By the trace theorem,

\|v\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

and

(A.3)

\int_{\Omega(\varepsilon)}|\nabla v|^{2}\leq C\varepsilon\|v\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C\varepsilon\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Let
w=u_{0}-v
, then
w
satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{0}(w)=0\quad\text{in }\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad w=g-v\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

By Lemma
A.5
, we have

(A.4)

\|(\nabla w)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|g-v\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}+C\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

It follows that

\int_{\Omega(\varepsilon)}|\nabla w|^{2}\leq C\varepsilon\int_{\partial\Omega}|(\nabla w)^{*}|^{2}\leq C\varepsilon\big(\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big).

This proves (
4.17
) in view of (
A.3
).

Let
x\in\Omega
and
\delta(x)=\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
. Then by the interior estimate,

|\nabla^{2}w(x)|\leq\frac{C}{\delta(x)}\bigg(\fint_{B_{\delta(x)/2}}|\nabla w|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

It follows that

\int_{\{\delta(x)=t\}}|\nabla^{2}w|^{2}\leq Ct^{-3}\int_{t/2<\delta(y)<2t}|\nabla w|^{2}dy\leq Ct^{-2}\int_{\partial\Omega}|(\nabla w)^{*}|^{2}

Consequently, the co-area formula leads to

\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega(c\varepsilon)}|\nabla^{2}w|^{2}\leq\int_{c\varepsilon}^{\text{diam}(\Omega)}Ct^{-2}dt\int_{\partial\Omega}|(\nabla w)^{*}|^{2}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}|(\nabla w)^{*}|^{2}.

This estimate together with the
H^{2}
estimate of
v
and (
A.4
) gives (
4.16
).
∎

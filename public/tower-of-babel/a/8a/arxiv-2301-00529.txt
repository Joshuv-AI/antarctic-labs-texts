Introduction

Denote by

The space
SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})/A

[

[

]

In the theory of harmonic analysis on homogeneous spaces, one of the central problems is whether a symmetric invariant differential operator has a unique self-adjoint extension, as it would enable a simultaneous study of spectral decomposition of both the regular representation and the invariant differential operators (e.g. see
[
BS1
]
,
[
BS2
]
for the case of reductive symmetric spaces). There is considerable literature on essential self-adjointness for natural operators on a complete Riemannian or Hermitian manifold. See for instance
[
Ga1
]
,
[
Ga2
]
,
[
Ga3
]
,
[
Roe
]
,
[
Co
]
,
[
Ri
]
for the Hodge-Laplace-Beltrami operator,
[
AV
]
for the
\bar{\partial}
-Laplacian,
[
P
]
,
[
W
]
for the Dirac operator, and
[
Ch
]
for certain first order differential operators. For differential operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, much more is known (
[
RS
]
), and we mention here that certain ellipticity (
[
IK
]
), or semi-boundedness together with temperedness (
[

]
), guarantees the essential self-adjointness.

For general homogeneous spaces, a classical result shows that the symmetric elements in the image of the center of the universal enveloping algebra are essentially self-adjoint (
[
Seg
]
,
[
NS
]
, or
[
Th
]
). As it can be shown that the center of
\mathbb{D}\left(SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})/A\right)
equals the image of the center of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{R}))
, it follows

Proposition 1.2
.

Every symmetric differential operator in the center of
\mathbb{D}\left(SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})/A\right)
is essentially self-adjoint.

For non-central elements, the most general result is due to Van den Ban
[
Ba
]
, who established the essential self-adjointness of the symmetric invariant differential operators for semi-simple symmetric pairs, even if it is not a generalized Gelfand pair, semiboundedness is absent, or the underlying manifold is non-Riemannian.
Beyond that, to the best of our knowledge, there is no general theory ensuring the essential self-adjointness in the pseudo-Riemannian setting, even for the Laplacian operators (see
[
KK1
]
,
[
KK2
]
).

The major part of the paper is to devoted to

Theorem 1.3
.

The differential operators
D_{12},D_{13},D_{23}
on
SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})/A
are essentially self-adjoint.

We now briefly describe the basic ideas for the proofs. We exploit the universal enveloping algebra to extract the algebraic structure of
\mathbb{D}(SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})/A)
, and the normal form theory in
[
FH
]
to determine the center of
\mathbb{D}(SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})/A)
. To study the essential self-adjointness of symmetric operators, we modify the scheme of
[
Ba
]
. The elegant proof in
[
Ba
]
is to decompose the differential operator into a bounded sum of left derivatives so that the wild growth of the coefficients can be treated as bounded ones. Unfortunately, in this non-spherical case, the left derivatives are too degenerate to span the whole space of invariant differentials in a mild way. We make the observation that by choosing the cutoff functions and the mollifiers in a compatible way instead of separating the Gårding type space as the operator core, one may gain extra decays to balance the extraordinary growth of the coefficients. In fact, the chosen cutoff functions are annihilated by the wildest terms and contribute desired decays thereafter.

The organization of the paper is as follows. §
2
is devoted to the algebraic structure of
\mathbb{D}(SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})/A)

D_{12}
in §
3.4
. In §
3.5
, we establish the density of
C^{\infty}_{c}\left(SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})/A\right)
in
{\rm Dom}(D_{12})
, and, as a consequence, prove Theorem
1.3
. The explicit formulas for the generators of the left derivatives and of the left invariant differentials are given in Appendices
A.1
and
A.2
, respectively.

Structure of the Algebra of the Invariant Differentials

2.1.
Generators of the invariant differentials on
SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/A

Let
S(\mathfrak{g})
be the symmetric algebra over
\mathfrak{g}
. Then for a basis
\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
,

can be identified with the algebra of polynomials

(8)

\sum\limits_{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}a_{k_{1}\cdots k_{n}}X_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{k_{n}},\,\,a_{k_{1}\cdots k_{n}}\in\mathbb{C}.

Theorem 2.1

(Theorem 4.3 in Chapter 2 of
[

]
)
.

There is a unique linear bijection

from

to
\mathbb{D}(G)
such that
\lambda(X^{m})=R(X^{m})
for

and

(9)

(\lambda(P)f)(g):=\left.P\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}\right)f\left(g\exp(t_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+t_{n}X_{n})\right)\right|_{t_{1}=\cdots=t_{n}=0},

for

and

[
He
]

(10)

\lambda(Y_{1}\cdots Y_{k})=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}R\left(Y_{\sigma(1)}\cdots Y_{\sigma(k)}\right),

is the symmetric group of degree

.

Denote by

the

\begin{split}&X_{ij}:=E_{ij},\,\,1\leq i\neq j\leq n,\,\,\,\,\,X_{ll}:=E_{ll}-E_{nn},\,\,1\leq l\leq n-1,\end{split}

which constitute a basis of

.

Proposition 2.2
.

The algebra
\mathbb{D}(SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/A)
is generated by

(12)

\left\{(\mu\circ\lambda)\left(E_{i_{1}i_{2}}E_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots E_{i_{k-1}i_{k}}E_{i_{k}i_{1}}\right)\left|\,\begin{matrix}2\leq k\leq n,\,1\leq i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}\leq n\\
i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}\,\,{\rm are\,\,distinct}\end{matrix}\right.\right\}.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

By (
3
), the invariant differential operators on

, where

is the Lie algebra of

.

Take

. There is a polynomial
P_{D}
such that
\lambda(P_{D})=D
by Theorem

. Arrange

in the following lexicographic order,

(13)

\small P_{D}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\,\,1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\cdots\leq i_{t}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}\leq n;\\

{\rm if\,\,}1\leq u<v\leq k{\rm\,\,and\,\,}i_{u}=i_{v},{\rm\,\,then\,\,}j_{u}\leq j_{v}\end{subarray}}p^{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{k}}X_{i_{1}j_{1}}X_{i_{2}j_{2}}\cdots X_{i_{t}j_{t}}\cdots X_{i_{k}j_{k}}.

By definition,

(14)

(Df)(g)=\left.P_{D}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{11}},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n(n-1)}}\right)f\left(g\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq n\\
(i,j)\neq(n,n)\end{subarray}}t_{ij}\cdot X_{ij}\right)\right)\right|_{t=0}.

For each

(15)

\small\begin{split}\left(D\right.&(f\circ R_{a})\circ R_{a}^{-1}\left.\right)(g)=\left.P_{D}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{11}},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n(n-1)}}\right)f\left(ga^{-1}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq n\\

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\,\,1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\cdots\leq i_{t}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}\leq n;\\

\prod_{l=1}^{n}a_{ll}=1

,
1\leq i_{t},j_{t}\leq n
, and
(i_{t},j_{t})\neq(n,n)

(17)

p^{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{k}}=p^{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{k}}\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}a_{i_{t}i_{t}}^{-1}a_{j_{t}j_{t}}.

P_{D}=p^{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{k}}\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}a_{i_{t}i_{t}}^{-1}a_{j_{t}j_{t}}X_{i_{1}j_{1}}X_{i_{2}j_{2}}\cdots X_{i_{t}j_{t}}\cdots X_{i_{k}j_{k}}.

\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}a_{i_{t}i_{t}}^{-1}a_{j_{t}j_{t}}=\prod\limits_{l=1}^{n}a_{ll}^{\sigma_{l}(\mathcal{I}_{k})},

\sigma_{l}(\mathcal{I}_{k}):={\rm cardinality\,\,of\,\,}\{1\leq t\leq n\,|\,j_{t}=l\,\}\,\,-{\rm cardinality\,\,of\,\,}\{1\leq t\leq n\,|\,i_{t}=l\,\}.

\sigma_{1}(\mathcal{I}_{k})=\sigma_{2}(\mathcal{I}_{k})=\cdots=\sigma_{n}(\mathcal{I}_{k})=0.

\{(i_{t},j_{t})|1\leq t\leq k\}=\bigsqcup_{l_{s}\geq 2}\left\{(i^{s}_{1},i^{s}_{2}),(i^{s}_{2},i^{s}_{3}),\cdots,(i^{s}_{l_{s}-1},i^{s}_{l_{s}}),(i^{s}_{l_{s}},i^{s}_{1})\left|\begin{matrix}i^{s}_{u}\neq i^{s}_{v}\,\,{\rm for\,\,}\\

Noticing that each simple cycle in (

, we can conclude that
(\exp\circ j)(\mathfrak{p})
and

intersects transversally at

. Since

\mathcal{P}
is a local homeomorphism between

Let

G/H

2.1

(51)

\begin{split}&(D_{ij}f)(eA)=\left.\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{ij}\partial{x_{ji}}}f\left(\pi\left(\exp(x_{ij}X_{ij}+x_{ji}X_{ji})\right)\right)\right|_{x_{ij}=x_{ji}=0},\\

for distinct

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{12}}\right)^{k+l}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{23}}\right)^{j+l}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{31}}\right)^{l+i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{21}}\right)^{k+m}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{13}}\right)^{m+i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{32}}\right)^{j+m}.

3.2.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\Delta
is a formally self-adjoint differential operator on
X
.
If the graph of
\Delta
on the domain

is dense in the graph of
\Delta
on the domain

with respect to the

\Delta
is symmetric on
{\rm Dom}(\Delta)
, that is,

(92)

(\Delta f,g)=(f,\Delta g)\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,all\,\,}f,g\in{\rm Dom}(\Delta).

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Since
C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
is dense in
{\rm Dom}(\Delta)
with respect to the graph
L^{2}\times L^{2}
norm, for
f,g\in{\rm Dom}(\Delta)
there exist sequences
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
,
\{g_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
, such that

(93)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{2}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\Delta f_{n}-\Delta f\|_{L^{2}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|g_{n}-g\|_{L^{2}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\Delta g_{n}-\Delta g\|_{L^{2}}=0.

Then,

(94)

\begin{split}&|(\Delta f,g)-(f,\Delta g)|\leq|(\Delta f,g)-(\Delta f,g_{n})|+|(\Delta f,g_{n})-(\Delta f_{n},g_{n})|\\
&\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+|(\Delta f_{n},g_{n})-(f_{n},\Delta g_{n})|+|(f_{n},\Delta g_{n})-(f_{n},\Delta g)|+|(f_{n},\Delta g)-(f,\Delta g)|\\
&=|(\Delta f,g-g_{n})|+|(\Delta f-\Delta f_{n},g_{n})|+|(f_{n},\Delta g_{n}-\Delta g)|+|(f_{n}-f,\Delta g)|\\
&\leq\|\Delta f\|_{L^{2}}\cdot\|g-g_{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta f-\Delta f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\cdot\|g_{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\cdot\|\Delta g_{n}-\Delta g\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{2}}\cdot\|\Delta g\|_{L^{2}}.\\
\end{split}

Taking the limit of (
94

3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that the operator
\Delta
is symmetric on
{\rm Dom}(\Delta)

Remark 3.18
.

In the proof of Theorem
3.17
, one can see that our cutoff functions are specifically designed to treat the operator
D_{12}
. Firstly, the first order derivative of our cutoff functions with respect to the variable
z_{12}
contributes a decay of the order
(z_{12}\log z_{12})^{-1}
, which is better than the more standard
z_{12}^{-1}
decay. By stretching the cutoff functions
in an inhomogeneous way, one can balance a linear growth of the other variables
z_{13},z_{23}
exactly by the remaining factor
(\log z_{12})^{-1}
, so that certain terms in
D_{12}
can be dealt with. Secondly, some other terms in
D_{12}
force us to arrange the cutoff functions so that they are totally annihilated by those differentials such as
z_{23}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{13}}-z_{13}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{23}}
, in order to kill the wild terms such as
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{12}}(\psi_{m}*f)
. Recall that the singled out differential
z_{23}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{13}}-z_{13}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{23}}
has an intrinsic geometric explanation as the rotation caused by an element in
SO_{3}(\mathbb{R})
.
Because of these restrictions, the cutoff functions are left with little wiggle room, and in particular when we perturb the cutoff functions for instance by convolutions, they are no longer suitable for our task. Therefore, to deal with the cases
D_{123}
and
D_{213}
, one may want to decompose the operators in new geometric coordinates so that the separation of terms is more transparent and suitable cutoff functions can be constructed.

Differentially private projection-depth-based medians
Kelly Ramsay & Dylan Spicker

Abstract
We develop ( ϵ , δ ) (\epsilon,\delta) -differentially private projection-depth-based medians using the propose-test-release (PTR) and exponential mechanisms. Under general conditions on the input parameters and the population measure, (e.g. we do not assume any moment bounds), we quantify the probability the test in PTR fails, as well as the cost of privacy via finite sample deviation bounds. Next, we show that when some observations are contaminated, the private projection-depth-based median does not break down, provided its input location and scale estimators do not break down. We demonstrate our main results on the canonical projection-depth-based median, as well as on projection-depth-based medians derived from trimmed estimators. In the Gaussian setting, we show that the resulting deviation bound matches the known lower bound for private Gaussian mean estimation. In the Cauchy setting, we show that the “outlier error amplification” effect resulting from the heavy tails outweighs the cost of privacy. This result is then verified via numerical simulations. Additionally, we present results on general PTR mechanisms and a uniform concentration result on the projected spacings of order statistics, which may be of general interest.



1
Introduction

Privacy has become a central concern in data collection and analysis owing to considerations around personal autonomy, freedom of expression, and fairness and equality.
Differential privacy has emerged as a powerful framework for maintaining privacy in data collection and analysis.
It has recently been adopted by many high profile institutions, including the United States Census Bureau
[
1
]
.
This has spurred investigations of many classical statistical problems through a differentially private lens.
Among such problems is median estimation.

Early literature on differentially private median estimation focuses on the univariate setting
[
13
,
3
,
7
,
29
]
.
In the multivariate setting, several authors have studied differentially private medians through via differentially private mean estimation.
These works have mainly focused either on the Tukey median
[
6
,
19
,
4
]
, or variants of it
[
17
]
.
In addition, as part of a more general framework for private high-dimensional estimation,
Liu et al., 2021b
studied one instance of a projection-depth-based median based on the trimmed mean and the trimmed variance.
Given that their focus was mean estimation, all of these works enforce a bound on the number of moments possessed by the population measure.
However, a classical method for assessing the robustness of a median is to examine its behavior in the case where the population measure possesses no moments.
Recently,
Ramsay et al., 2022
considered this problem.
They studied differentially private multivariate medians arising from depth functions, including the Tukey median, without enforcing any moment bounds on the population measure.
However, their framework does not cover projection-depth-based medians
[
31
,
32
]
.

Unlike the Tukey median and those studied by
Ramsay et al., 2022
, when the location and scale measures are chosen appropriately, projection-depth-based medians simultaneously possess affine equivariance and achieve a high breakdown point
[
31
,
32
]
.
Affine equivariance is a critical property for location estimators, as it ensures that the units of measurement do not have an effect on the resulting estimate, see, for example,
Zuo and Serfling, 2000
.
In addition, the breakdown point is a common measure of the robustness of an estimator. It measures the fraction of points which must be corrupted to make an estimate arbitrarily large
[
16
]
.
For instance, the breakdown point of the Tukey median lies between
(1+d)^{-1}
and
1/3

[
21
]
, whereas the projection-depth-based medians generally retain a much higher finite sample breakdown point, (e.g., approximately
1/2-d/n

[
31
]
).
This finite sample breakdown point is carried over to the private setting, (see Theorem
3.4
).
One may also note that, when the location and scale measures are chosen appropriately, the projection-depth-based medians enjoy higher relative efficiency than that of the Tukey median
[
31
]
.
In addition,
[
9
]
recently considered the behavior of a new subclass of projection-depth-based medians inspired by the median of means estimator.
They demonstrate under general assumptions, e.g., the population measure need not have any moments, and under adversarial contamination this subclass of medians achieves sub-Gaussian rates with respect to Mahalanobis error.
Motivated by these properties, our work develops and studies differentially private projection-depth-based medians.
We consider a general location estimation setting and do not place any moment bounds on the population measure.

Our main tool for privatizing projection-depth-based medians is the propose-test-release (PTR) algorithm, which was introduced by
Dwork and Lei, 2009
in the context of differentially private estimation of various robust, univariate statistics.
Our work takes inspiration from that work, as well as from the works of
Brunel and Avella-Medina, 2020
and
Brown et al., 2021
.

Brunel and Avella-Medina, 2020
used PTR to develop sub-Gaussian median estimators in the univariate setting, and

Brown et al., 2021
developed a private Gaussian mean estimator, using a PTR mechanism based on safety and the exponential mechanism. We incorporate both ideas into our proposed mechanism.
Notably, the mechanism proposed by
Brown et al., 2021
was generalized by
Liu et al., 2021b
to be an all-purpose solution to various private, statistical problems.
Our work builds on that of
Liu et al., 2021b
, in the sense that their mean estimator fits into the class of projection-depth-based estimators.

Our contributions are as follows: We first propose a class of
(\epsilon,\delta)
-differentially private projection-depth-based medians.
Next, under general conditions on the population measure, we quantify both the probability that the PTR mechanism fails to release an estimate and the cost of privatizing the projection-depth-based medians (Theorems
3.1
, and
3.2
, respectively).
To measure the cost of privacy, we provide finite sample bounds on the distance between a private projection-depth-based median and the corresponding population value, which hold with high probability.
Next, we show that when some observations are contaminated, the private projection-depth-based median does not break down, provided its input location and scale estimators do not break down (Theorem
3.4
).
We then demonstrate our methods for the canonical projection-depth-based median, as well as on projection-depth-based medians derived from trimmed estimators.
In particular, we show that Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
apply provided the “projected densities” are bounded below in an interval containing a range of inner quantiles (Condition
4
).
As a first example, we show that in the Gaussian setting, our deviation bound matches the optimal lower bound for sub-Gaussian mean estimation given by
Kamath et al., 2018
and
Cai et al., 2021
(Example
3.1
).
We then show that in the Cauchy setting, the increase in error resulting from contamination or heavy tails, which we call the “outlier error amplification” is larger than the cost of privacy (Example
3.2
).
We confirm this phenomenon with numerical studies (Section
4
).
Lastly, we provide some results for analysis of general PTR mechanisms (Section
5
), as well as a uniform concentration result on the projected spacings of order statistics (Lemma
C.2
), which may be of general interest.

Preliminaries

2.1
Outlyingness and projection-depth-based medians

We first review projection-depth-based medians, starting with
projected outlyingness

[
27
,
10
,
11
,
33
,
31
]
.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the set of measures on
\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\mathscr{B}(S)
denotes the Borel sets of
S
.
For a measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u
from the unit
(d-1)
-sphere,
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, let
\nu_{u}
be the law of
X^{\top}u
if
X\sim\nu
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a translation and scale equivariant estimator
\mu
and a translation invariant and scale equivariant estimator
\sigma
, the projected outlyingness at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to
\nu
is

O(x,\nu)=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}O_{u}(x,\nu_{u})=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{\left|x^{\top}u-\mu(\nu_{u})\right|}{\sigma(\nu_{u})}.

(2.1)

For brevity, we will refer to the projected outlyingness as simply the outlyingness, and denote
\mu(\nu_{u})
as
\mu_{u}
,
\sigma(\nu_{u})
as
\sigma_{u}
,
\mu(\hat{\nu}_{u})
as
\hat{\mu}_{u}
, and
\sigma(\hat{\nu}_{u})
as
\hat{\sigma}_{u}
.
The sample version of the outlyingness function is given by
O(x,\hat{\nu})
.
The canonical choices of
\mu
and
\sigma
are the median and the median absolute deviation, respectively.
The outlyingness based on the median and the median absolute deviation was first developed by
Stahel, 1981
and
Donoho, 1982
.
It was later studied in general by
Zuo, 2003
.
Choosing
\mu
and
\sigma
to be the trimmed mean and trimmed variance recovers the objective function used in the PTR mechanism developed by
Liu et al., 2021b
for differentially private mean estimation.
Lastly, choosing
\mu
and
\sigma
to be the median of means and a median of means version of the mad recovers the outlyingness studied by
Depersin and Lecué, 2022
.
In our examples, we consider the trimmed mean, the median, trimmed mean absolute deviation, median absolute deviation, and the interquartile range.
The minimizer of the outlyingness function is a
projection-depth-based median

[
27
,
10
,
31
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a translation and scale equivariant estimator
\mu
and a translation invariant and scale equivariant estimator
\sigma
, the projection-depth-based median of
\nu
is

\theta\coloneqq\theta(\mu,\sigma,\nu)=\int_{\argmin O}xdP,

where
P
is uniform on
\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}O(x,\nu)
.

The projection-depth-based medians enjoy several nice properties, including local and global robustness, affine equivariance, strong
\sqrt{n}
-consistency, and a relatively simple limiting distribution
[
31
,
32
]
.
To the best of our knowledge, among the competing affine equivariant medians, when the location and scale measures are chosen appropriately, (e.g. the median and median absolute deviation), the projection-depth-based medians have the highest breakdown point
[
31
]
.
In Section
3
, we show that when some observations are contaminated, the private projection-depth-based median does not break down, provided its input location and scale estimators do not break down (Theorem
3.4
).
Projection-depth-based medians have been shown to have higher asymptotic efficiency, relative to competitors
[
31
]
.
In addition, recently,
Depersin and Lecué, 2022
showed that the median of means version of the projection-depth-based median exhibits sub-Gaussian rates, under adversarial contamination and fairly general assumptions.
For more information on projection-depth-based medians, we refer the reader to
[
27
,
10
,
31
,
32
,
9
]
.

2.2
Differential privacy

Next, we review necessary concepts from differential privacy.
For a textbook introduction to differential privacy, see
Dwork and Roth, 2014
.
A
dataset
of size
n\times d
is a collection of
n
points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{X}_{n}=\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, possibly with repetitions.
Let
\mathbf{D}_{n\times d}
be the set of all datasets of size
n\times d
.
For datasets
\mathbb{X}_{n}
and
\mathbb{Y}_{n}
, let
\mathbb{X}_{n}\triangle\mathbb{Y}_{n}
represent the dataset containing all rows in
\mathbb{X}_{n}
which are not contained in
\mathbb{Y}_{n}
.
Define
\mathbf{S}(\mathbb{X}_{n},m)=\left\{\mathbb{Y}_{n}\in\mathbf{D}_{n\times d}\colon|\mathbb{X}_{n}\triangle\mathbb{Y}_{n}|=m\right\}
, the set of datasets of size
n\times d
which differ from
\mathbb{X}_{n}
in exactly
m
rows.
Define the empirical measure of
\mathbb{X}_{n}
,
{\hat{\nu}}_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}
, as that which assigns
1/n
probability to each row in
\mathbb{X}_{n}
.
For a dataset with empirical measure
{\hat{\nu}}_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}
define

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1}({\hat{\nu}}_{\mathbb{X}_{n}},m)=\{\tilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon\tilde{\nu}={\hat{\nu}}_{\mathbb{Y}_{n}}\text{ for some }\mathbb{Y}_{n}\in\mathbf{S}(\mathbb{X}_{n},m)\}.

Suppose that we observe a dataset
{\mathbb{X}}_{n}
comprised of
n
i.i.d. samples from a measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and wish to estimate a parameter
\theta\in\Theta
.
One way to do this is via a
mechanism
.
Formally, a mechanism,
P_{\hat{\nu}}
, maps an empirical measure
\hat{\nu}
to
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Theta)
.
The estimator
\tilde{\theta}
is then taken to be a draw from
P_{\hat{\nu}}
.
When the corresponding measure is clear from context, we will suppress it in the notation, denoting
P_{\hat{\nu}}
as
P
.
To produce a differentially private statistic,
P_{\hat{\nu}}
should satisfy the following privacy guarantee.

Definition 2.3
.

A mechanism
P
is
(\epsilon,\delta)
-differentially private if for any dataset
\mathbb{X}_{n}
, any
\tilde{\nu}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(\nu_{\mathbb{X}_{n}},1)
, and any measurable set
B\in\mathscr{B}(\Theta)
, it holds that

P_{\hat{\nu}_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}}(B)\leq e^{\epsilon}P_{\tilde{\nu}}(B)+\delta.

(2.2)

The pair
(\epsilon,\delta)
represent the privacy budget, where typically
\epsilon
is a small constant and
\delta=O(n^{-k})
for some positive integer
k
. Smaller values of both
\epsilon
and
\delta
enforce stricter privacy guarantees.
The parameter
\epsilon
controls the level of privacy assured to each individual, and
\delta
is the probability of leakage, or, the probability that the privacy condition
P_{\hat{\nu}_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}}(B)\leq e^{\epsilon}P_{\tilde{\nu}}(B)
fails to hold.
If
\delta=0
, the mechanism is said to be
\epsilon
-differentially private.

3
Main results

3.1
Private projection-depth-based medians

In this section, we introduce private projection-depth-based medians.
Private projection-depth-based medians rely on an instantiation of the
exponential mechanism

[
22
]
.
For some
\tau>0
, let
A_{\tau,\hat{\nu}}=\{O(x,\hat{\nu})\leq\tau\}
.
Given
\tau,\eta,\epsilon>0
, the exponential mechanism applied to the outlyingness function
\EM_{\hat{\nu}}(\cdot;\tau,\eta,\epsilon)
is the measure whose density satisfies

d\EM_{\hat{\nu}}(\cdot;\tau,\eta,\epsilon)\propto\mathbbm{1}\left\{x\in A_{\tau,\hat{\nu}}\right\}\exp(-O(x,{\hat{\nu})\epsilon/4\eta})dx.

For brevity, we write
\EM_{\hat{\nu}}(\cdot;\tau,\eta,\epsilon)
as simply
\EM_{\hat{\nu}}
when
\tau,\eta,\epsilon
are clear from the context.
Now, this mechanism alone is not differentially private.
That is because there are some datasets which are not “safe”.

Safety
, introduced by
Brown et al., 2021
for Gaussian mean estimation, is a localized version of the
(\epsilon,\delta)
-differential privacy guarantee.
We present a definition specific to our context. (For a generalized definition, see Section
5
.)
Take
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(n,d)
to be the set of empirical measures constructed from datasets of size
n\times d
,
\{\nu_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}\colon\ \mathbb{X}_{n}\in\mathbf{D}_{n\times d}\}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given
\epsilon,\delta,\tau,\eta>0
,
\hat{\nu}
is
(\epsilon,\delta)
-safe if, for all
\tilde{\nu}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\hat{\nu},1)
and
B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds that

\EM_{\hat{\nu}}(B)\leq e^{\epsilon}\EM_{\tilde{\nu}}(B)+\delta\qquad\text{and}\qquad\EM_{\tilde{\nu}}(B)\leq e^{\epsilon}\EM_{\hat{\nu}}(B)+\delta.

The
(\epsilon,\delta)
-safe set is defined as

\mathbf{S}(\epsilon,\delta)=\{\hat{\nu}\in\widehat{M}_{1}(n,d)\colon\ \hat{\nu}\text{ is }(\epsilon,\delta)\text{-safe}\}.

Indeed, for all common choices of
\mu,\ \sigma
, such as the median and median absolute deviation, or the trimmed mean and variance, there exists
\hat{\nu}\notin\mathbf{S}(\epsilon,\delta)
, which implies that
\EM_{\hat{\nu}}
is not differentially private.
However, because the common choices of
\mu
and
\sigma
are robust statistics, in the sense that they are not corrupted under small levels of contamination, we expect most
\hat{\nu}
to be safe.
When we expect
\hat{\nu}
to be safe with high probability, it is natural to develop a
propose-test-release
(PTR) mechanism
[
13
]
.

PTR mechanisms are characterized by a three-step procedure.
First, you
propose
a mechanism
P_{\hat{\nu}}
.
Second, you privately
test
if
\hat{\nu}
is safe.
Finally, if the test is passed, you
release
a draw from
P_{\hat{\nu}}
. If the test is not passed, nothing is released.
In the event that nothing is released, the PTR mechanism still uses up the
(\epsilon,\delta)
privacy budget.
As such, it is critical that the test is passed with high probability.
For our estimators, we take
P_{\hat{\nu}}=\EM_{\hat{\nu}}
.
Since the test step of the algorithm is differentially private, it is a mechanism, and so we refer to it as the test mechanism.
Our proposed mechanism’s test mechanism relies on the
safety margin
.

Definition 3.2
.

Given
\epsilon,\delta>0
, the safety margin of
\hat{\nu}
is

\SM(\hat{\nu})\coloneqq\SM(\hat{\nu};\epsilon,\delta)=\inf\{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\colon\ \exists\tilde{\nu}\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}(\hat{\nu},m)\ s.t.\ \tilde{\nu}\notin\mathbf{S}(\epsilon,\delta)\}.

The safety margin is the minimum number of points that we need to modify in the original dataset in order for it to become unsafe.
Observe that if
\SM(\hat{\nu})>0
, then
\hat{\nu}
is safe.
Let
W
be a standard Laplace random variable.
The test is defined as follows:

Z=\mathbbm{1}\left\{\SM(\hat{\nu})+\frac{2}{\epsilon}W>\frac{2\log(1/2\delta)}{\epsilon}\right\},

(3.1)

where, if
Z=1
, then the test is passed.

We can now introduce the differentially private projection-depth-based median which, given
\mu
and
\sigma
, outputs a private version of
\theta
, the projection-depth-based median based on
\mu
and
\sigma
.

Definition 3.3
.

Given parameters
\epsilon,\delta,\eta,\tau>0
and estimators
\mu
and
\sigma
,
the private projection-depth-based median is defined as

\tilde{\theta}=\begin{cases}Y\sim\EM_{\hat{\nu}}&Z=1\\
\perp&Z=0\end{cases}.

Note that if
\tilde{\theta}=\perp
, it is meant that nothing is output by the algorithm.
That is,
\perp
can be interpreted as a null value.
The private projection-depth-based median is
(\epsilon,\delta)
-differentially private (Theorem
5.1
).
Note that the mechanisms of
Brown et al., 2021
and
Liu et al., 2021b
are PTR mechanisms based on
Z
and the exponential mechanism.

Brown et al., 2021
considers a different cost function and
Liu et al., 2021b
consider several cost functions, one of which is the outlyingness based on the trimmed mean and variance.
The performance of the proposed PTR algorithm is naturally summarized by both the probability that the test is passed (
\mathbb{E}Z
) and the accuracy of the approximation of
\theta
by
\tilde{\theta}
.
With these two quantities, we characterize both the likelihood that the mechanism releases a statistic, and how useful that statistic is likely to be.

Remark 3.3
.

It is popular to consider the error of an estimator with respect to the Mahalanobis metric, e.g.

9
]
, as it accounts for the differing scales across different directions.
However, we aim to develop a general theory which includes population measures for which the Mahalanobis metric may not be suitable.
As a result, we develop results based on the Euclidean metric.
However,
Liu et al., 2021b
and
Depersin and Lecué, 2022
have considered the behavior of different subclasses of projection-depth-based medians (both privately and non-privately, respectively).
Their results show good performance of these projection-depth-based medians, i.e., under their assumptions, they achieve sub-Gaussian rates.
Furthermore, if we restrict to the median of means version of the projection-depth-based median and the assumptions of
Depersin and Lecué, 2022
, one can straightforwardly substitute the techniques of
Depersin and Lecué, 2022
into the proof of Theorem
3.2
where appropriate, which would give the rate of convergence of the median of means version of the private projection-depth-based median under the Mahalanobis metric.

Before concluding this section, we present a result concerning the robustness of the private projection-depth-based median.
The finite sample breakdown point of an estimator is the maximum fraction of points which can be set to arbitrary values, under which the estimator lies in the interior of the parameter space
[
12
]
.
For a set
A
, let
\interior(A)
denote the interior of
A
.
Formally, for an estimator
T
of parameter
\theta\in\Theta
, the finite sample breakdown point of
T
with respect to
\hat{\nu}
is

\BP(T,\hat{\nu})=\sup\{\frac{m}{n}\colon\forall\tilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\hat{\nu},m),\ T(\tilde{\nu})\in\interior(\Theta)\}.

Given
\hat{\nu}\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(n,d)
, define

\BP(\EM,\hat{\nu})=\sup\{\frac{m}{n}\colon\forall\tilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\hat{\nu},m),\exists\ C_{\hat{\nu},\alpha,\tau}<\infty\\
\text{such that}\EM_{\tilde{\nu}}(\{\lVert X\rVert<C_{\hat{\nu},\alpha,\tau}\})=1\}.

\BP(\EM,\hat{\nu})
is the maximum fraction of points which can be set to arbitrary values, under which the estimate is bounded with probability 1. (Note that if
\lVert\tilde{\theta}\rVert<\infty
, then
\tilde{\theta}\in\interior(\Theta)
.)
We now present the following result, pertaining to the robustness of the private projection-depth-based median.

Theorem 3.4
.

For all
0<\eta,\delta,\epsilon,\tau<\infty
it holds that
\BP(\EM,\hat{\nu})\geq\BP(\mu,\hat{\nu})\wedge\BP(\sigma,\hat{\nu}).

Proof.

Note that by definition
\EM_{\hat{\nu}}(\{x\colon O(x,\hat{\nu})\leq\tau\})=1
.
Therefore, it suffices to show that the set
\{x\colon O(x,\tilde{\nu})\leq\tau\}
is bounded for all
\tilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\hat{\nu},k)
, where
k<n(\BP(\mu)\wedge\BP(\sigma))
.
Consider
\tilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\hat{\nu},k)
, where
k<n(\BP(\mu)\wedge\BP(\sigma))
.
We have that

\{x\colon O(x,\tilde{\nu})\leq\tau\}\subset\{x\colon\tau\inf_{u}\sigma(\tilde{\nu}_{u})-\sup_{u}\mu(\tilde{\nu}_{u})\leq x^{\top}u\leq\tau\sup_{u}\sigma(\tilde{\nu}_{u})+\sup_{u}\mu(\tilde{\nu}_{u})\}.

This set is bounded, provided that
\mu(\tilde{\nu}_{u})
and
\sigma(\tilde{\nu}_{u})
are bounded and
\sigma(\tilde{\nu}_{u})>0
. Furthermore,
\mu(\tilde{\nu}_{u})
and
\sigma(\tilde{\nu}_{u})
are bounded and
\sigma(\tilde{\nu}_{u})>0
follows from the fact that
\tau<\infty
, the condition on
k
and the definition of the breakdown point.
∎

Therefore, if the data is contaminated such that
\mu
and
\sigma
do not break down, then Theorem
3.4
implies that the differentially private projection-depth-based median will also not breakdown.

4
Numerical experiments

To complement our theoretical results, we provide a numerical demonstration of our proposed methods.
In order to compute the private projection-depth-based median, we employ discretized Langevin dynamics (as was done by
Ramsay et al., 2022
) and compute outlyingness by (randomly) discretizing the unit sphere, see Appendix
A
for the algorithm.
More efficient algorithms could be constructed using the methods and results of
Dyckerhoff et al., 2021

[
26
]
.
The purpose of the implementation is to numerically investigate the cost of privatizing the projection-depth-based median and compare to some other private measures of location, and the non-private projection-depth-based median.
Our code is available on
GitHub
.

We compute the private and non-private projection-depth-based median based on the median, with the median absolute deviation as the scale estimate.
We compare the performance of the projection-depth-based medians to the private smoothed integrated dual depth median
[
24
]
, the non-private sample mean, and the private coin-press mean of
Biswas et al., 2020
.
Following
Ramsay et al., 2022
, for the integrated dual depth median, we set the smoothing parameter to 10, the prior as
\mathcal{N}(0,25dI)
. For the coin-press mean, we set the bounding ball to have a radius of
10\sqrt{d}
and the algorithm was run for four iterations.

Empirical root mean squared error (ERMSE) of the location estimates under (left) Gaussian data and (right) contaminated Gaussian data. 5
General results on propose-test-release mechanisms

In this section, we elaborate on the notion of safety
[
6
,
19
]
in the general case of PTR mechanisms.
We show that safety is both sufficient and necessary for privacy of a PTR mechanism. We also present general results for analyzing a PTR mechanism based on the exponential mechanism.
We begin by introducing a general notion of a test mechanism.

Definition 5.1
.

A test mechanism is any mechanism,
Q
, such that for all
\hat{\nu}\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(n,d)
,
Q_{\hat{\nu}}
is a Bernoulli measure with probability of success
\lambda(\hat{\nu})
, for some
0\leq\lambda(\hat{\nu})\leq 1
.

Definition
5.1
simply says that
Q
maps an empirical measure
\hat{\nu}
to a Bernoulli measure with probability of success
\lambda(\hat{\nu})
, for some
0\leq\lambda(\hat{\nu})\leq 1
.
Note that every test mechanism is characterized by its associated
\lambda
function.

Definition 5.2
.

Given a mechanism
Q
whose range is
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Theta)
and a test mechanism characterized by
\lambda
, the PTR mechanism based on
(Q,\lambda)
is given by the map

P_{\hat{\nu}}\colon(B)\mapsto\lambda(\hat{\nu})Q_{\hat{\nu}}(B\backslash\{\perp\})+\left[1-\lambda(\hat{\nu})\right]\mathbbm{1}\left\{\perp\in B\right\},

where
B\in\mathscr{B}(\Theta\cup\{\perp\})
and
\hat{\nu}\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(n,d)
.

The probability of any event under the PTR mechanism
P
applied to
\hat{\nu}
will be the probability that the test is passed, multiplied by the probability of the event under the mechanism
Q
applied to
\hat{\nu}
, plus the probability of the test failing if
\perp
is an element of the event.
We now introduce generalized definitions of safety and the safety margin, which apply to arbitrary mechanisms. Subsequently, we will introduce a generalized definition of the exponential mechanism, which applies to an arbitrary cost function
\phi
.

Definition 5.3
.

Given
\epsilon,\delta>0
, the pair
(Q,\hat{\nu})
is
(\epsilon,\delta)
-safe if, for all
\tilde{\nu}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\hat{\nu},1)
and measurable sets
B
, it holds that

Q_{\hat{\nu}}(B)\leq e^{\epsilon}Q_{\tilde{\nu}}(B)+\delta\qquad\text{and}\qquad Q_{\tilde{\nu}}(B)\leq e^{\epsilon}Q_{\hat{\nu}}(B)+\delta.

The
(\epsilon,\delta)
-safe set of
Q
is

\mathbf{S}(Q,\epsilon,\delta)=\{\hat{\nu}\in\widehat{M}_{1}(n,d)\colon\ (Q,\hat{\nu})\text{ is }(\epsilon,\delta)\text{-safe}\}.

Observe that Definition
5.3
is a generalized version of Definition
3.1
.
Definition
3.1
is a special case of Definition
5.3
, with
Q=\EM
.
Note that
Q
is allowed to depend on
\epsilon
and
\delta
.
Safety is linked to the privacy of a given PTR mechanism.
Colloquially, if
\lambda(\hat{\nu})
is large, then the dataset must satisfy some level of safety.
In addition, sufficient conditions for differential privacy of a PTR mechanism are that the test mechanism is differentially private and that
\lambda(\hat{\nu})
is very small if
\hat{\nu}
is unsafe.

Definition 5.4
.

Given
\epsilon,\delta>0
and a mechanism
Q
, the safety margin of an empirical measure
\hat{\nu}
with respect to
Q
, is

\SM(\hat{\nu},Q;\epsilon,\delta)=\inf\{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\colon\ \exists\tilde{\nu}\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}(\hat{\nu},m)\ s.t.\ \tilde{\nu}\notin\mathbf{S}(Q,\epsilon,\delta)\}.

Observe that Definition
5.4
is a generalized version of Definition
3.2
.
That is, Definition
3.2
is a special case of Definition
5.4
with
Q
always set to
\EM
.
When
\epsilon
and
\delta
are clear from the context, we omit them, denoting the safety margin as
\SM(\hat{\nu},Q).

If
\lambda(\hat{\nu})
is small whenever
\hat{\nu}
is not in the safe set of
Q
and the test mechanism is differentially private, then a PTR mechanism will be differentially private.

Theorem 5.1
.

Given
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3}>0
, let
P
be the PTR mechanism based on
Q
and
\lambda
.
If
\lambda
is
(\epsilon_{1},\delta_{1})
-differentially private and is such that for any
\hat{\nu}\notin\mathbf{S}(Q,\epsilon_{2},\delta_{2})
,
\lambda(\hat{\nu},\epsilon_{1},\delta_{1})<\delta_{3}
, then
P

is
(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2},\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}\vee\delta_{3})
-differentially private.

Proof.

First, consider
\hat{\nu}\in\mathbf{S}(Q,\epsilon_{2},\delta_{2})
. By composition, the differential privacy bound with parameters
(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2},\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})
will be satisfied.
Now, consider
\hat{\nu}\notin\mathbf{S}(Q,\epsilon_{2},\delta_{3})
.
For sets such that
\perp\notin A
, then
P_{\hat{\nu}}(A)\leq\lambda(\hat{\nu})\leq\delta_{3}.

For sets such that
\perp\in A
, then

P_{\hat{\nu}}(A)\leq P_{\hat{\nu}}(\{\perp\})+\delta_{3}\leq P_{\tilde{\nu}}(\{\perp\})e^{\epsilon_{1}}+\delta_{2}+\delta_{3}.\qed

The previous theorem gives sufficient conditions on both the privacy guarantee of the test mechanism and the relationship between the test mechanism and the safe set of
Q

Here we outline the algorithm used to compute the private, projection-depth-based median in Section
4
.
We will approximate

by
\widehat{O}(x,\hat{\nu})=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{N}}O_{u}(x,\hat{\nu})
, where
\mathcal{U}_{N}
is a set of
N
unit vectors sampled uniformly from
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
The algorithm consists of two parts, executing the test and sampling from the exponential mechanism.
For the latter, observe that the outlyingness function is quasiconvex, i.e., its lower level sets are convex.
This allows us to apply an envelope theorem to compute the gradient of
\widehat{O}_{u}(x,\hat{\nu})
with respect to
x

x\in A_{\tau}
, let

be the unit vector that maximizes

.
Then, for any

, it holds that

\displaystyle\nabla\widehat{O}(x,\nu)

\displaystyle=u_{x}\frac{\mathrm{sign}(x^{\top}u_{x}-\mu_{u_{x}})}{\sigma_{u_{x}}}.

To compute the non-private projection-depth-based median, we use gradient descent, and, to sample from the exponential mechanism, we use Langevin Dynamics.
Given that Langevin Dynamics samples approximately from the exponential mechanism, we note that this may violate the differential privacy condition, and stress that this implementation is just to confirm the theoretical properties of the proposed private median.
Of course, more sophisticated methods to compute the outlyingness could be developed, such as those based on
[

, but we leave that for future work. Indeed, given the caveat on using Langevin Dynamics to sample from the exponential mechanism mentioned above, an algorithm to compute the private projection-depth-based median is an interesting open problem.

contains less than 1/2 of the projected observations.
This is equivalent to bounding:

\Pr\Big(\bigcup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\{\hat{\xi}_{1/2,u}<\xi_{q^{\prime},u}\}\Big)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Pr\Big(\bigcup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\{\hat{\xi}_{1/2,u}>2\xi_{1/2,u}-\xi_{q^{\prime},u}\}\Big).

(E.4)

Starting with the left-hand term, utilizing the mean value theorem, together with the upper bound on the projected density and an application of Lemma
C.1
, yields

\displaystyle\Pr\Big(\bigcup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\{\hat{\xi}_{1/2,u}<\xi_{q^{\prime},u}\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left(\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{\xi}_{1/2,u}-\xi_{1/2,u}|>\inf_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}[\xi_{1/2,u}-\xi_{q^{\prime},u}]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left(\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{\xi}_{1/2,u}-\xi_{1/2,u}|>(1/2-q^{\prime})/L\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{cn}{d}\right)^{d}e^{-2nM^{2}(1/2-q^{\prime})^{2}/L^{2}}.

Similarly, the right-hand term in (
E.4
) satisfies

\displaystyle\Pr\Big(\bigcup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\{\hat{\xi}_{1/2,u}>\xi_{1/2,u}+\xi_{q^{\prime},u}\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{cn}{d}\right)^{d}e^{-2nM^{2}(1/2-q^{\prime})^{2}/L^{2}}.

Thus,

\Pr\left(\inf_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\hat{q}_{u}<q^{\prime}\right)\lesssim\left(\frac{cn}{d}\right)^{d}e^{-2nM^{2}[(1/2-q^{\prime})/L\wedge M/3]^{2}}.

An analogous argument gives that

\Pr\Big(\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\tilde{q}_{u}>1-q^{\prime}\Big)\lesssim\left(\frac{cn}{d}\right)^{d}e^{-2nM^{2}[(1/2-q^{\prime})/L\wedge M/3]^{2}}.

Let
E_{11}=\{q^{\prime}<\hat{q}_{u}<\tilde{q}_{u}\leq 1-q^{\prime}\}
and note that we have just shown that

\Pr\left(E_{11}^{c}\right)\lesssim\left(\frac{cn}{d}\right)^{d}e^{-2nM^{2}[(1/2-q^{\prime})/L\wedge M/3]^{2}}.

(E.5)

The next step is to show that
\{\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\xi_{\hat{q},u}-\xi_{q,u}|\geq t|E_{11}\}\subset\{\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\\
q^{\prime}<p<1-q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}|\xi_{p,u}-\hat{\xi}_{p,u}|\gtrsim t|E_{11}\}
.
To this end, observe that

\displaystyle\{\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\xi_{\hat{q},u}-\xi_{q,u}|\geq t|E_{11}\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\{\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}<p<1/2\\
|\xi_{p,u}-\xi_{q,u}|>t\end{subarray}}|\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{u}(p)|-|\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{u}(q)|\leq 0\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\{|\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{u}(q)|-\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}<p<1/2\\
|\xi_{p,u}-\xi_{q,u}|>t\end{subarray}}|\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{u}(p)|\geq 0\}

\displaystyle\subset\{8\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\\
q^{\prime}<p<1-q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}|\xi_{p,u}-\hat{\xi}_{p,u}|\geq\inf_{p<1/2,\ |\xi_{p,u}-\xi_{q,u}|>t}|\mathcal{C}_{u}(p)|\}.

It remains to show that for any
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
\inf_{0<p<1/2,\ |\xi_{p,u}-\xi_{q,u}|>t}|\mathcal{C}_{u}(p)|\gtrsim t
.
This is equivalent to

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<p<1/2\\
|\xi_{p,u}-\xi_{q,u}|>t\end{subarray}}|\xi_{q,u}-\xi_{p,u}+\xi_{q+1/2,u}-\xi_{p+1/2,u}|\gtrsim t.

Now,

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<p<1/2\\
|\xi_{p,u}-\xi_{q,u}|>t\end{subarray}}|\xi_{q,u}-\xi_{p,u}+\xi_{q+1/2,u}-\xi_{p+1/2,u}|\\
=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<p<1/2\\
|\xi_{p,u}-\xi_{q,u}|>t\end{subarray}}[|\xi_{q,u}-\xi_{p,u}|+|\xi_{q+1/2,u}-\xi_{p+1/2,u}|]\geq t.

The first equality comes from the fact that
|\xi_{p,u}-\xi_{q,u}+\xi_{q+1/2,u}-\xi_{p+1/2,u}|
is of the form of
|a+b|
where
sign(a)=sign(b)
.
The second inequality comes from the fact that we are minimizing over
|\xi_{p,u}-\xi_{q,u}|>t
and the fact that the second term is non-negative.
Thus,

\{|\xi_{\hat{q},u}-\xi_{q,u}|\geq t|E_{11}\}\subset\{\sup_{q^{\prime}<p<1-q^{\prime}}|\xi_{p,u}-\hat{\xi}_{p,u}|\geq t/8\}.

(E.6)

By an analogous argument,

\{|\xi_{q,u}-\xi_{\tilde{q},u}|\geq t|E_{11}\}\subset\{\sup_{q^{\prime}<p<1-q^{\prime}}|\xi_{p,u}-\hat{\xi}_{p,u}|\geq t/8\}.

(E.7)

Now, combining equations (
E.1
), (
E.2
), (
E.5
), (
E.6
) and (
E.7
),

\displaystyle\Pr\left(S_{n}(\sigma)\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Pr\left(\sup_{q^{\prime}<p<1-q^{\prime}}|\xi_{p,u}-\hat{\xi}_{p,u}|\geq t/16\right)+\left(\frac{cn}{d}\right)^{d}e^{-2n[(1/2-q^{\prime})M/L\wedge 1/3]^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{cn}{d}\right)^{d}e^{-2nM^{2}t^{2}/16^{2}}+\left(\frac{cn}{d}\right)^{d}e^{-2n[(1/2-q^{\prime})M/L\wedge 1/3]^{2}}.\qed

Lemma E.3
.

Suppose that
\nu=\mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma)
. Then there exists a universal constant
c>0
such that for all
0\leq\alpha<1/2
Condition
2
holds for
(\nu,\tm_{\alpha},\tad_{\alpha})
with

\zeta_{n,\mu}(t)=\zeta_{n,\sigma}(t)=e^{-cn(1-2\alpha)t^{2}/\lVert\Sigma^{-1/2}\rVert^{2}+d}.

Proof.

WLOG, assume that
\mu=0
, and let
Z_{\alpha}\sim N(0,\Sigma/n(1-2\alpha))
.
Then, it follows from Gaussian concentration of measure

\Pr\left(S_{n}(\tm_{\alpha})\geq t\right)=\Pr\left(\sup_{u}|\bar{X}_{\alpha}^{\top}u|\geq t\right)=\Pr\left(\lVert\bar{X}_{\alpha}\rVert\geq t\right)\lesssim e^{-n(1-2\alpha)t^{2}/\lVert\Sigma^{-1/2}\rVert^{2}}.

(E.8)

Now, we focus on
\Pr\left(S_{n}(\tad_{\alpha})\geq t\right)
.
First, we have that

\Pr\left(S_{n}(\tad_{\alpha})\geq t\right)\leq\Pr\left(S_{n}(\tad_{\alpha})\geq t/\lVert\Sigma^{-1/2}\rVert\right).

From now on, assume that
\nu=\mathcal{N}(0,I)
and let
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\sim N(0,I)
.

\displaystyle S_{n}(\tad_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\tad_{\alpha}(\hat{\nu}_{u})-\tad_{\alpha}(\nu_{u})|

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\frac{1}{n(1-2\alpha)}\sum_{i=\alpha n}^{n-\alpha n}|X_{i}^{\top}u-\bar{X}_{\alpha}^{\top}u|-\int|Z^{\top}u|d\nu|

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{n(1-2\alpha)}\sum_{i=\alpha n}^{n-\alpha n}||X_{i}^{\top}u-\bar{X}_{\alpha}^{\top}u|-\sqrt{2\pi}|

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{n(1-2\alpha)}\sum_{i=\alpha n}^{n-\alpha n}||Z_{i}^{\top}u|-\sqrt{2\pi}|+\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\bar{X}_{\alpha}^{\top}u-\mu_{u}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n(1-2\alpha)}\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\lVert|\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\top}u|-\sqrt{2\pi}\mathbbm{1}\rVert_{1}+\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\bar{X}_{\alpha}^{\top}u-\mu_{u}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n(1-2\alpha)}}\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\lVert|\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\top}u|-\sqrt{2\pi}\mathbbm{1}\rVert_{2}+\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\bar{X}_{\alpha}^{\top}u-\mu_{u}|

\displaystyle\coloneqq I+II.

Applying this bound, along with a union bound yields that there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\Pr\left(S_{n}(\tad_{\alpha})\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left(I+II\geq Ct\right)\leq\Pr\left(I\geq Ct/2\right)+\Pr\left(II\geq Ct/2\right).

Now, for
II
, applying (
E.8
) yields that

\Pr\left(II\geq Ct/2\right)\leq e^{-n(1-2\alpha)t^{2}/\lVert\Sigma^{-1/2}\rVert^{2}}.

(E.9)

We now focus on
I
, and apply an
\epsilon
-net argument.
Recall that the set
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
is an
\epsilon
-net on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
if for all
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, there exists
v\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
such that
\lVert u-v\rVert\leq\epsilon
.
Letting
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
be an arbitrary
\epsilon
-net on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, it is straightforward (see, e.g.,
[
30
, Exercise 4.4.4]
) to show that for any
c>0
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<\epsilon<1/2
,

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}||y^{\top}u|-c|\leq\frac{C}{1-2\epsilon}\sup_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}||y^{\top}u|-c|.

Furthermore, there exists
\mathcal{N}_{1/4}
such that
|\mathcal{N}_{1/4}|\leq 9^{d}

[
30
, see Corollary 4.2.13]
.
Applying these facts, along with Gaussian concentration of measure yields that there exists a universal constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\Pr\left(I\geq Ct/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq 9^{d}\sup_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{1/4}}\Pr\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n(1-2\alpha)}}\lVert|\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\top}u|-\sqrt{2\pi}\mathbbm{1}\rVert_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=9^{d}\Pr\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n(1-2\alpha)}}\lVert|\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}|-\sqrt{2\pi}\mathbbm{1}\rVert_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-n(1-2\alpha)ct^{2}+d}.

This completes the proof.

∎

Appendix F
Cauchy lower bound

Here, we present a lower bound on the sample complexity of estimating the location parameter from a measure with Cauchy marginals.
For a set
S
, let
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,\epsilon,n}(S)
be the set of maps from
\hat{\nu}
to
\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
which satisfy (
2.2
).
Note that
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,\epsilon,n}(S)
is essentially the set of all differentially private estimators which lie in
S
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of measure with Cauchy marginals over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Theorem F.1
.

For all
n,d\geq 1
,
t\geq 0
it holds that

n=\Omega\left(\frac{d}{t^{2}}\vee\frac{d}{t\epsilon}\right),

samples are required for

\inf_{T_{\epsilon}\sim\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,\epsilon,n}(\mathbb{R})}\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{E}|T_{\epsilon}(\hat{\nu})-\theta(\nu)|\leq t.

(F.1)

Proof.

We apply Corollary 4 of
[
2
]
, of which a simpler version is restated below for clarity.
For
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, let
n^{*}=\inf\{n\colon\inf_{T_{\epsilon}\sim\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,\epsilon,n}(\mathbb{R})}\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}}\int|T_{\epsilon}(\hat{\nu})-T_{\epsilon}(\nu)|d\nu^{n}\leq\tau\}.

Corollary F.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

For all
\epsilon,\tau>0
and any
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m}\}\subset\mathcal{P}
.
If for all
i\neq j
, it holds that
|T_{\epsilon}(Q_{i})-T_{\epsilon}(Q_{j})|\geq 3\tau
,
\KL(Q_{i},Q_{j})\leq\beta
, and
\TV(Q_{i},Q_{j})\leq\gamma
, then
then
n^{*}=\Omega\left(\log m(\beta^{-1}\vee(\gamma\epsilon)^{-1})\right)
.

Let
P_{\theta}
be the Cauchy measure with location parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and let
P_{\theta}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}P_{\theta_{i}}
for
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Now, consider a 1/2-packing of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{M}\}
.
Recall that the packing number of the unit ball is at least
2^{d}
, and so
M\geq 2^{d}
.
Next, define
\Theta=\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{M}\}
, where
\theta_{i}=4\alpha v_{i}
for some
\alpha>0
.
It follows that for
i\neq j
,
\lVert\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}\rVert=4\alpha\lVert v_{i}-v_{j}\rVert\geq\alpha
and that
\lVert\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}\rVert\leq\lVert\theta_{i}\rVert+\lVert\theta_{j}\rVert\leq 8\alpha
.

Now, let
\KL(P,Q)
denote the Kullback–Leibler divergence between two measures
P
and
Q
.
We now bound the Kullback–Leibler divergence between
\KL(P_{\theta_{i}},P_{\theta_{j}})
for
j\neq i
.
To this end, we have that

\displaystyle\KL(P_{\theta_{i}},P_{\theta_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\log\left(1+|\theta_{ik}-\theta_{jk}|^{2}/4\right)\leq\lVert\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}\rVert^{2}/4\leq 16\alpha^{2}.

Applying Corollary
F.2
, we have that

n^{*}=\Omega(\frac{d}{\delta^{2}}\vee\frac{d}{\delta\epsilon})
.
∎

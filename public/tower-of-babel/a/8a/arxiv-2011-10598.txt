On tameness of zonoids
Antonio Lerario, Léo Mathis

Abstract
. We prove that in a globally subanalytic family of convex bodies the set of zonoids is log-analytic, and in particular it is definable in the o–minimal structure generated by globally subanalytic sets and the graph of the exponential function.



1.
Introduction

A
zonoid
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body that can be approximated, in the Hausdorff metric, by finite sums of line segments. Zonoids appear in several different contexts of modern mathematics: functional analysis, measure theory, stochastic geometry, geometric convexity, enumerative geometry
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
13
,
17
,
19
]
. Despite the simple definition, the problem of deciding whether a given convex body is a zonoid is considerably hard, and it is referred to as the zonoid problem.

In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
every centrally symmetric convex body is a zonoid, but for
n\geq 3
no simple geometric characterization exists for zonoids in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For example, an interesting non-trivial characterization says that a convex body is a zonoid if and only if its support function is conditionally positive definite
1
1

1

A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
conditionally positive definite
, see
[
14
, Notes for Section 3.5, pag. 204]
, if for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}=0
we have

\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}f(x_{i}-x_{j})\geq 0.

: this characterization involves nice inequalities on the values of the support function, but an
infinite
number of them.

In this paper we show that, as soon as we restrict to a certain “tame” family of convex bodies, the set of zonoids in this family can be described using only a finite number of “tame” conditions. Here we will express the tameness condition in terms of definability in some o–minimal structure. The structures that we will consider are the o–minimal structure generated by globally subanalytic sets, denoted by
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}
, and the o–minimal structure generated by
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}
and the graph of the exponential function, denoted by
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
.

Theorem 1
.

If a family of convex bodies is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}
, the set of zonoids in this family is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
.

Remark 2
.

Our study was motivated by the following question, suggested by B. Sturmfels: given a semialgebraic convex body, is there an algorithm to decide whether it is a zonoid? For example, let
p\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{d}
be a polynomial of degree
d
in
n
variables and let
K_{p}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ p(x)\geq 0\right\}
. We define
P:=\left\{p\in\mathbb{R}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]_{d}\ |\ \textrm{$K_{p}$ is a convex body}\right\}
. This defines a tame (semi-algebraic) family of convex bodies. Is there an algorithm that, given the polynomial
p
, decides whether
K_{p}
is a zonoid? To answer this question, consider the set:

(1.1)

\mathscr{Z}(P):=\left\{p\in P\ |\ K_{p}\text{ is a zonoid}\right\}.

If
\mathscr{Z}(P)
is semialgebraic, such an algorithm (in the sense of
[
2
]
) exists. In general, for
n\geq 3
, it is not known if
\mathscr{Z}(P)
is semi-algebraic. Our Theorem
1
above proves that it is at least definable in the o–minimal structure
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an,exp}}
, and therefore it cannot be “wild” (in particular it shares with semialgebraic sets many finiteness properties). Our proof of Theorem
1
is constructive but it is not clear to us whether this can be called an algorithm.

Remark 3
.

If the support function – and not just the body – is polynomial, then it is easy to see that the condition of being a zonoid is semialgebraic in the coefficients of the polynomial.

Remark 4
.

As we will see below, a crucial ingredient in our proof is the fact that a parametric integral of a function which is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}
is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
. A major open question in o–minimal geometry is the following: given a function
f:P\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
definable in some o–minimal structure
\mathcal{M}
, is there an o–minimal expansion
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
such that the function
p\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(p,x)\mathrm{d}x
is definable in
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
? This is true for
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}
, for which we can take
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
. If this question has an affirmative answer, then the proof of Theorem
1
can be easily extended to get the following statement: given a family of convex bodies definable in some o–minimal strucutre
\mathcal{M}
, the set of zonoids in this family is definable in some o–minimal expansion
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. For the moment this is just a conjecture.

Remark 5
.

The main idea of the proof is to reduce the problem to the study of a “tame” family of distributions on the unit interval. More precisely, our first step consists in associating to our family of convex bodies a new tame family of rotational invariant convex bodies. The support functions of these convex bodies depend on one variable only and zonoids correspond to members of the family such that an appropriate integral transform of their support function is a non–negative measure. Using the language of distributions, we show that the condition of being a non–negative measure can be expressed in a tame way using a second primitive of the integral transform.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Zonoids

Let us recall some definitions from convex geometry. For a more detailed treatment we refer to
[
14
]
. Our main object of study are

convex bodies
, i.e. non-empty, compact and convex subsets of Euclidean space. Recall that the
support function
of a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is the function on
h_{K}:S^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
given by:

(2.1)

h_{K}(u):=\sup\left\{\langle u,x\rangle\ |\ x\in K\right\}.

A convex body
K
is called a
zonoid
if there exists an even, non–negative measure
\mu
on
S^{n}
such that the support function of
K
can be written in the following form

(2.2)

h_{K}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S^{n}}|\langle u,x\rangle|\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

We will use the following notion introduced by Lonke in
[
12
]
.

Definition 6
.

Let
f:S^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function and
e\in S^{n}
. We denote by

(2.3)

O(e):=\mathrm{Stab}_{O(n+1)}(e)\simeq O(n)\subset O(n+1)

the stabilizer of
e
in
O(n+1)
, endowed with the normalized Haar measure
\mathrm{d}g
. We then define the measurable function
S_{e}f:S^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(2.4)

S_{e}f(u):=\int_{O(e)}f\left(g(u)\right)\mathrm{d}g.

If
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a centrally symmetric convex body whose support function is
h_{K}
we define
S_{e}K
to be the convex body whose support function is given by:

(2.5)

h_{S_{e}K}:=S_{e}h_{K}.

The fact that
S_{e}h_{K}
is the support function of a convex body follows from the characterization of support functions as positively homogeneous sublinear functions
[
14
, Section 1.7]
.

Using Definition
6
one can give the following alternative characterization of zonoids, see
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma 7
.

A convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a zonoid if and only if for all
e\in S^{n}
,
S_{e}K
is a zonoid.

2.2.
o–minimal structures and tameness

We denote by
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}
the o–minimal structure generated by the globally subanalytic sets and by
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
the o–minimal structure generated by the globally subanalytic sets together with the graph of the exponential function, see
[
16
]
.

If
P
is a globally subanalytic set, following
[
6
]
, we denote by
\mathscr{C}(P)
the
\mathbb{R}
-algebra of real valued functions generated by all globally subanalytic functions on
X
and all the functions of the form
x\mapsto\log f(x)
, where
f:X\to(0,\infty)
is globally subanalytic. A function in
\mathscr{C}(X)
is called a
constructible
function. Notice that functions definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}
are constructible and that constructible functions are definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}.

In the sequel we will simply say that a set or a function definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}
is
subanalytic
(omitting the word “global”).

We will use the following crucial result
[
6
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
P
be subanalytic and
F\in\mathscr{C}(P\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Suppose that for all
p\in P
the function
F(p,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is integrable. Then the the function
I(F):P\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
I(F)(p):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}F(p,x)\mathrm{d}x
is constructible, and in particular definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
.

Remark 9
.

In the case
F:P\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is semialgebraic, then the parametrized integral function
I(F)
is definable in a structure strictly smaller than
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
, see
[
10
]
.

Corollary 10
.

If
h:P\times S^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is constructible, the function
(e,p,u)\mapsto S_{e}h(p,u)
is also constructible.

Proof.

Consider a subanalytic function
F:S^{n}\times O(n)\to O(n+1)
such that for almost all
e\in S^{n}
the function
F(e,\cdot)
is a subanalytic isomorphism between
O(n)
and
O(e)
. (Since we are only requiring that
F
is definable, such function can also be defined piecewise.) Then we can write:

(2.6)

S_{e}h(p,u)=\int_{O(n)}h(p,\left(F(e,\tilde{g})(u)\right)|\det JF(e,\cdot)|d\tilde{g},

where
d\tilde{g}
is the normalized Haar measure on
O(n)
. Since the integrand is constructible, the result follows by applying Theorem
8
after noticing that there is a diffeomorphism, definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}
, between an open dense of subset of
O(n)
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
m=n(n-1)/2
(for instance one can take the restriction of the Riemannian exponential map at the identity on an appropriate subanalytic domain).
∎

Definition 11
.

Let
P
be a subanalytic set. A subanalytic family of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a subanalytic set
T\subset P\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that for every
p\in P
the set

(2.7)

K_{p}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\ |\ (p,x)\in T\right\}

is a convex body. For
p\in P
, we will denote by
h_{p}
the support function of
K_{p}
(instead of
h_{K_{p}}
).

If
T\subset P\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a subanalytic family of convex bodies, the function
H:P\times S^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, given by
H(p,u)=h_{K_{p}}(u)
, is subanalytic.
Moreover, if
P
and
T\subseteq P\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
are subanaytic, denoting by
T_{p}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\ |\ (p,x)\in T\right\},
it is immediate to see that the following sets are subanalytic:

(i)

\mathscr{K}(P):=\left\{p\in P\ |\ T_{p}\text{ is a convex body}\right\};

(ii)

\mathscr{K}_{0}(P):=\left\{p\in P\ |\ T_{p}\text{ is a centrally symmetric convex body, centered at the origin}\right\}.

The following lemma is a variation of the classical Cell Decomposition Theorem from
[
16
]
, adapted to our needs.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{\rho}\}
be a finite family of real valued, subanalytic functions on
S\times[-1,1]
, where
S
is a subanalytic set. For every
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a partition of
S
into finitely many subanalytic sets
S=\coprod_{j=1}^{s}S_{j}
and for every
j=1,\ldots,s
there are subanalytic functions

(2.8)

-1\equiv a_{j,1}<a_{j,2}<\cdots<a_{j,\nu_{j}-1}<a_{j,\nu_{j}}\equiv 1:S_{j}\to[-1,1]

such that for every
r=1,\ldots,\rho
, for every
i=1,\ldots,\nu_{j}-1
and for every
x\in S_{j}
the function

(2.9)

f_{r}(x,\cdot)|_{(a_{j,i}(x),a_{j,i+1}(x))}:(a_{j,i}(x),a_{j,i+1}(x))\to\mathbb{R}

is of class
C^{m+2}.

Proof.

Consider the subanalytic set
B\subset P\times(-1,1)
consisting of all the pairs
(x,z)
such that for all
r=1,\ldots,\rho
the function
f_{r}(x,\cdot):(-1,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{m+2}
at
z\in(-1,1)
. The set
B
is subanalytic because it is defined by a first order formula in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}
. Let
\Sigma
denote the complement of
B
in
S\times(-1,1)
and denote by
p_{1}:S\times(-1,1)\to S
the projection on the first factor.

By the Trivialization Theorem
[
16
, Theorem (1.7), Chapter 9]
there exists a partition
S=\coprod_{j=1}^{s}S_{j}
into subanalytic sets such that map
p_{1}:S\times(-1,1)\to S
can be trivialized over each
S_{j}
, respecting
\Sigma
. This means that there are subanalytic sets
T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}\subset(-1,1)
and subanalytic homeomorphisms

(2.10)

\psi_{j}:S_{j}\times(-1,1)\to S_{j}\times(-1,1)

such that
p_{1}(\psi_{j}(x,z))=x
for every
x\in S_{j}
and
\psi_{j}(S_{j}\times T_{j})=(S_{j}\times(-1,1))\cap\Sigma
.

Observe that for every
x\in S
the set
p_{1}^{-1}(x)\cap\Sigma
is a zero-dimensional subanalytic set, since the function
f(x,\cdot)
is subanalytic and therefore of class
C^{m+2}
outside of a finite number of points. Therefore each
T_{j}
is zero-dimensional and (if nonempty) consists of a finite set of points, which we assume to be ordered:

(2.11)

T_{j}=\{t_{j,2},\ldots,t_{j,\nu_{j}-1}\}.

Denote now by
p_{2}:S\times(-1,1)
the projection on the second factor and pick
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. If
T_{j}=\emptyset
, then we set
\nu_{j}=2
,
a_{j,1}(x)\equiv-1
and
a_{j,2}(x)\equiv 1.
If
T_{j}\neq\emptyset
we define, for
i=2,\ldots,\nu_{j}-1
, the desired functions by:

(2.12)

a_{j,i}(x)=p_{2}(\psi_{j}^{-1}(x,t_{j,i})).

∎

2.3.
Distributions

Let
X
be a smooth manifold. We denote by
C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
the space of compactly supported, smooth functions on
X
, endowed with the
C^{\infty}
topology (see
[
9
]
). The space of
distributions
on
X
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(X)
: it is the dual of
C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
, endowed with the weak–
*
topology.

Remark 13
.

If
X
is compact
C^{\infty}_{c}(X)=C^{\infty}(X)
.

Remark 14
.

Let
G
be a group. Any group action
G\curvearrowright X
gives rise to a group action on
C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
by
g\cdot\varphi(x):=\varphi\left(g^{-1}\cdot x\right)
for all
g\in G
,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
and
x\in X
. This induces a group action on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(X)
defined by
\langle g\cdot\rho,\varphi\rangle:=\langle\rho,g^{-1}\cdot\varphi\rangle
for all
g\in G
,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
and
\rho\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(X)
. We denote by
C^{\infty}_{c}(X)^{G}
(respectively
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(X)^{G}
) the functions (respectively distributions) which are invariant under these actions. Note that
\left(C^{\infty}_{c}(X)^{G}\right)^{*}=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(X)^{G}
.

In our case
X
will be
S^{n}
,
[-1,1]
or
(-1,1)
an
G
will be
O(e)
for some
e\in S^{n}
.

Definition 15
.

We denote by
C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})^{O(e)}
(respectively
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{even}(S^{n})^{O(e)}
) the functions (respectively distributions) on
S^{n}
invariant by the group generated by
O(e)
and the antipodal map
u\mapsto-u
. Similarly we denote by
C^{\infty}_{even}([-1,1])
(respectively
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{even}([-1,1])
) the even functions (respectively distributions) on
[-1,1]
.

Remark 16
.

Recall that there is a dense embedding
C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{even}(S^{n})
,
f\mapsto\rho_{f}
given, for all
g\in C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})
, by:

(2.13)

\langle\rho_{f},g\rangle:=\int_{S^{n}}f(x)g(x)\mathrm{d}x,

where
\mathrm{d}x
denotes the integration with respect to the standard volume form on
S^{n}
. There is a similar dense embedding for
[-1,1]
.

Lemma 17
.

Consider the map
\sigma:[-1,1]\to[0,1]
given by
\sigma(z):=\sqrt{1-z^{2}}
. Then the operator
\mathcal{P}
defined for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{even}([-1,1])
by
\mathcal{P}\varphi:=\varphi\circ\sigma
is a continuous automorphism of
C^{\infty}_{even}([-1,1])
.

Proof.

It is enough to know that for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{even}([-1,1])
there exists
\psi\in C^{\infty}([0,1])
such that
\varphi(z)=\psi(z^{2})
and that the map
\varphi\mapsto\psi
is continuous in the

topology. This is done in
[
18

.
∎

Proposition 18
.

For every
e\in S^{n}
there is a linear homeomorphism

(2.14)

\zeta_{n}:C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})^{O(e)}\to C^{\infty}_{even}([-1,1]).

Its inverse is given by
(\zeta_{n}^{-1}\varphi)(u):=\varphi\left(\langle u,e\rangle\right)
. By density this gives a linear homeomorphism
\zeta_{n}:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{even}(S^{n})^{O(e)}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{even}([-1,1]).

Proof.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})^{O(e)}
and let us consider an orthonormal basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n},e
. In this basis

(2.15)

(\zeta_{n}f)(z)=f(0,\ldots,0,\sqrt{1-z^{2}},z).

Denoting by
\gamma:[-1,1]\to S^{n}
the curve
\gamma(z):=(0,\ldots,0,\sqrt{1-z^{2}},z)
, we see that
(\zeta_{n}f)=f\circ\gamma
.

This function is cleary even and
C^{\infty}
away from
z=\pm 1
. We show now that it is also smooth near these points. Consider the local coordinate chart
\pi
around the north pole
(0,\ldots,0,1)
that is the projection on the first
n
coordinates. Then near the north pole,
\zeta_{n}f=\tilde{f}\circ\pi\circ\gamma
where
\tilde{f}:=f\circ\pi^{-1}
. The function
\tilde{f}
is smooth because it is a composition of smooth functions. Consider now first the case
n=1
, for which we have

(2.16)

(\zeta_{1}f)(z)=\tilde{f}(\sqrt{1-z^{2}})=(\mathcal{P}\tilde{f})(z).

In this case it is enough to apply the previous lemma. For the general case we note that
\zeta_{n}
is the composition of
\zeta_{1}
with the restriction to any
2
-plane containing
e
. This proves that
\zeta_{n}f\in C^{\infty}_{even}([-1,1]).

The map
\zeta_{n}
is linear and, by (
2.16
) and Lemma
17
, it is also continuous. Its inverse, given by
(\zeta_{n}^{-1}\varphi)(u):=\varphi\left(\langle u,e\rangle\right)
, is also clearly continuous in the
C^{\infty}
topology.
∎

The isomorphism
\zeta_{n}=\zeta_{n,e}
defined above depends on

Proposition 19
.

Let
F:P\times S^{n}\times S^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a constructible function such that for all

and
p\in P
the function
f_{p,e}:=F(p,e,\cdot)
is

-invariant. Then the function
(p,e,z)\mapsto(\zeta_{n,e}f_{p,e})(z)
is a constructible function on
P\times S^{n}\times[-1,1]
.

Proof.

Take a subanalytic function
v_{0}:S^{n}\to S^{n}
such that for all
e\in S^{n}
,
\langle e,v_{0}(e)\rangle=0
. Then we can write
(\zeta_{n,e}f_{p,e})(z)=F(p,e,ze+\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\ v_{0}(e))
. Since
F
is constructible, then:

(2.17)

F(p,x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\left(g_{i}(p,x,y)\prod_{j=1}^{b_{i}}\log f_{i,j}(p,x,y)\right),

for some subanalytic functions
g_{i}:P\times S^{n}\times S^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and
f_{i,j}:P\times S^{n}\times S^{n}\to(0,\infty).

In particular:

(2.18)

\displaystyle(\zeta_{n,e}f_{p,e})(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\left(g_{i}(p,e,ze+\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\ v_{0}(e))\prod_{j=1}^{b_{i}}\log f_{i,j}(p,e,ze+\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\ v_{0}(e))\right)

(2.19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\left(\tilde{g}_{i}(p,e,z)\prod_{j=1}^{b_{i}}\log\tilde{f}_{i,j}(p,e,z)\right)

with
\tilde{g}_{i,j}:P\times S^{n}\times[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
and
\tilde{f}_{i,j}:P\times S^{n}\times[-1,1]\to(0,\infty)
defined by
\tilde{g}_{i}(p,e,z)=g_{i}(p,e,ze+\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\ v_{0}(e))
and
\tilde{f}_{i,j}(p,e,z)=f_{i,j}(p,e,ze+\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\ v_{0}(e)).
Since
\tilde{g}_{i}
and
\tilde{f}_{i,j}

(p,e,z)\mapsto(\zeta_{n,e}f_{p,e})(z)
is constructible.
∎

\varphi

Proposition 21

(i)

;

(ii)

let
x\in[-1,1]
and let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left([-1,1]\right)
such that
\supp(\rho)=\{x\}
. Then there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
r_{0},\ldots,r_{N}
such that
\rho=\sum_{i=0}^{N}r_{i}D^{i}\delta_{x}
; if moreover
\rho\geq 0
, then
r_{0}\geq 0
and
r_{i}=0
for all
i>0;

(iii)

let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left((-1,1)\right).
Then
\rho\geq 0
iff there is a convex function
\gamma:(-1,1)\to\mathbb{R}
such that
D^{2}\gamma=\rho
.

Remark 22
.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(S^{n})^{O(e)}
. Then
\rho\geq 0
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(S^{n})^{O(e)}
if and only if
\zeta_{n}\rho\geq 0
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{even}([-1,1])
. This is because
f\geq 0
iff
\zeta_{n}f\geq 0
.

A straightforward computation gives also the following lemma.

Lemma 23
.

Let
\psi
be
k
times differentiable on
[a,b]
with
-1\leq a<b\leq 1
. Then the following properties are true:

(i)

If
\psi^{(k)}\in L^{1}([a,b])
, then

(2.21)

D^{k}\psi=\psi^{(k)}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\psi^{(i)}(a)D^{k-1-i}\delta_{a}-\psi^{(i)}(b)D^{k-1-i}\delta_{b};

(ii)

for every
x\in[a,b]
we have

(2.22)

\psi\cdot D^{k}\delta_{x}=(-1)^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\psi^{(k-i)}(x)D^{i}\delta_{x}.

Next lemma characterizes non–negative measures on
[-1,1]
.

Lemma 24
.

Let
\Lambda\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([-1,1])
and let
W^{2,1}\left([-1,1]\right)
be the Sobolev space of (equivalence classes of) functions on
[-1,1]
with integrable second derivative. Then
\Lambda\geq 0
if and only if the following two conditions are both satisfied:

(i)

There is
\gamma:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
in
W^{2,1}([-1,1])
convex such that
D^{2}\gamma|_{(-1,1)}=\Lambda|_{(-1,1)};

(ii)

There exist
\lambda_{-1},\lambda_{1}\geq 0
such that:

(2.23)

\Lambda-\gamma^{(2)}=\lambda_{-1}\delta_{-1}+\lambda_{1}\delta_{1}.

Proof.

Assume that (i) and (ii) are satisfied. Then
\gamma^{(2)}\geq 0
and therefore (
2.23
) defines a positive measure.

Now suppose
\Lambda\geq 0
. Then
\Lambda|_{(-1,1)}\geq 0
and by point (iii) of Proposition
21
there is
\gamma:(-1,1)\to\mathbb{R}
convex such that
D^{2}\gamma|_{(-1,1)}=\Lambda|_{(-1,1)}
. Since
\Lambda
is finite on the closed interval
[-1,1]
, then
\gamma^{(2)}
is integrable near
\{\pm 1\}
, i.e.
\gamma
is in
W^{2,1}\left([-1,1]\right)
(in particular
\gamma
is also defined in
\{\pm 1\}
). This shows that (i) is satisfied.

Now since
\gamma^{(2)}=D^{2}\gamma|_{(-1,1)}=\Lambda|_{(-1,1)}
, then
\Lambda-\gamma^{(2)}
has support on
\{\pm 1\}
and by point (ii) of Proposition
21
it is a sum of deltas and its derivatives. Since both
\Lambda
and
\gamma^{(2)}
are measures, then
\Lambda-\gamma^{(2)}
has no derivatives of deltas. Thus there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{-1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\Lambda=\gamma^{(2)}+\lambda_{-1}\delta_{-1}+\lambda_{1}\delta_{1}
. Since
\Lambda\geq 0
point (ii) follows.
∎

2.4.
Integral Transforms

We recall now some classical construction of integral transforms. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
for more details.

The
Cosine Transform

T_{n}
and the
Radon Transform

R_{n}
are the endomorphisms of
C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})
given for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})
by

(2.24)

T_{n}f(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S^{n}}|\langle u,x\rangle|f(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\textrm{and}\quad R_{n}f(u):=\int_{S\left(u^{\perp}\right)}f(x)\mathrm{d}x,

where
S\left(u^{\perp}\right)=S^{n}\cap u^{\perp}
is the unit sphere on
u^{\perp}
and
\mathrm{d}x
denotes the integration with respect to the standard volume forms of the corresponding spheres.

Remark 25
.

The two operators
T_{n}
and
R_{n}
are linear continuous bijections from the space
C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})
to itself. One can define the transpose of these operators on the dual space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{even}(S^{n})
in the usual way. More precisely, for every
\rho\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{even}(S^{n})
and for every
f\in C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})
, we set:

(2.25)

\langle(T_{n})^{t}\rho,f\rangle:=\langle\rho,T_{n}f\rangle,

and similarly we do for
(R_{n})^{t}
.

With this definition it turns out that

(2.26)

(T_{n})^{t}|_{C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})}=T_{n}\ \text{and }(R_{n})^{t}|_{C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})}=R_{n},

when the space of smooth functions is embedded into the space of distribution in the usual way (see Remark
16
). Thus
(T_{n})^{t}
and
(R_{n})^{t}
can be seen as extensions of the original operators to the space of distributions, and we will still denote these extension by
T_{n}
and
R_{n}
, see
[
7
]
.

Observe that a convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a zonoid if and only if there is a non negative measure
\mu
on
S^{n}
such that:

(2.27)

h_{K}=T_{n}\mu.

Following
[
7
]
, we define the following differential operator on
C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})
:

(2.28)

\square:=\Delta_{S^{n}}+n,

where
\Delta_{S^{n}}
is the Spherical Laplacian on
S^{n}
.
The various operators defined so far satisfy the following intertwining properties
[
7
, Proposition 2.1]
:

(2.29)

\square T_{n}=R_{n}\quad\textrm{and}\quad T_{n}^{-1}=\square R_{n}^{-1}.

We use now Proposition
18
to turn these operators into operators on the space of distributions on the interval, as follows.

Definition 26
.

We define the operators
r_{n},t_{n}
on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([-1,1])
by:

(2.30)

r_{n}:=\zeta_{n}R_{n}\zeta_{n}^{-1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad t_{n}:=\zeta_{n}T_{n}\zeta_{n}^{-1}.

Remark 27
.

Since
\square
commutes with the action of
O(n+1)
, it preserves the space
C^{\infty}_{even}(S^{n})^{O(e)}
. Using the isomorphism
\zeta_{n}
we consider the operator
\zeta_{n}\square\zeta_{n}^{-1}
that we still denote by
\square
. This is a differential operator on
C^{\infty}_{even}\left([-1,1]\right)
. For any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{even}([-1,1])
, it is given by

(2.31)

\square\varphi(z)=(1-z^{2})\ \varphi^{(2)}(z)-nz\varphi^{(1)}(z)+n\varphi(z)

In fact, let
\theta=\theta(u)
be the angle between
e
and
u
. By Proposition
18
,
f:=\zeta_{n}^{-1}\varphi
as a function of
\theta
is given by
f(\theta)=\varphi(\cos(\theta))
. Equivalently
\varphi(z)=f\left(\arccos(z)\right)
. It is then enough to consider the spherical Laplacian of a function that depends only on
\theta
, which is given by
\Delta_{S^{n}}f(\theta)=(\sin\theta)^{1-n}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left((\sin\theta)^{n-1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\right),
and apply the change of variables
z=\cos(\theta)
to obtain (
2.31
).

The following proposition gives an integral expression for
r_{n}
.

Proposition 28
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{even}([-1,1])
and let
z^{\prime}:=\sqrt{1-z^{2}}
. We have:

(2.32)

(r_{n}\varphi)(z)=c_{n}\frac{1}{(z^{\prime})^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{z^{\prime}}\varphi(w)\left((z^{\prime})^{2}-w^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\mathrm{d}w

where
c_{n}=2\Vol_{n-2}(S^{n-2})
.

Proof.

Let
f:=\zeta_{n}^{-1}\varphi
in such a way that
r_{n}\varphi=\zeta_{n}R_{n}f
. Consider an orthonormal basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n},e
and let
u(z):=z\ e+z^{\prime}\ v_{n}
. The unit sphere in the space
u(z)^{\perp}
can be parametrized by
z^{\prime}\cos\phi\ e+z\cos\phi\ v_{n}+\sin\phi\ v
where
\phi\in[0,\pi]
and
v
belongs to the unit sphere of
\Span\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}\right\}
. Then, using the
O(e)
invariance and the fact that
f
is even, we get

(2.33)

\zeta_{n}R_{n}f(z)=R_{n}f(u(z))=2\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}f\left(z^{\prime}\cos\phi\ e+z\cos\phi\ v_{n}\right)(\sin\phi)^{n-2}\Vol_{n-2}\left(S^{n-2}\right)\mathrm{d}\phi.

We then note that
f\left(z^{\prime}\cos\phi\ e+z\cos\phi\ v_{n}\right)=\varphi(z^{\prime}\cos\phi)
and apply the change of variable
w=z^{\prime}\cos\phi
to get (
2.32
).
∎

We notice the particular cases:

(2.34)

r_{2}\varphi(z)=c_{2}\int_{0}^{z^{\prime}}\frac{\varphi(w)}{\sqrt{(z^{\prime})^{2}-w^{2}}}\mathrm{d}w\quad\textrm{and}\quad r_{3}\varphi(z)=\frac{c_{3}}{z^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{z^{\prime}}\varphi(w)\mathrm{d}w.

The equation on the left in (
2.34
) is known as Abel Equation and the second one can be inverted as follows. Recall that the operator
\mathcal{P}
from Lemma
17
is defined by
\mathcal{P}\varphi(z)=\varphi(z^{\prime})
, with
z^{\prime}=\sqrt{1-z^{2}}
.

Corollary 29
.

The inverse of
r_{3}
is given by
r_{3}^{-1}=\frac{1}{c_{3}}\left(1+z\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\right)\circ\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

We differentiate equation (
2.34
) to obtain
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}}r_{3}\varphi(z)=-\frac{1}{z^{\prime}}r_{3}\varphi(z)+\frac{c_{3}}{z^{\prime}}\varphi(z^{\prime})
. Then write
\varphi=r_{3}^{-1}\psi
and change
z^{\prime}
for
z
.
∎

The following can be deduced from (
2.32
) with a straightforward computation.

Proposition 30
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{even}([-1,1])
. For any positive integer
k\leq n/2-1

(2.35)

\left(\frac{1}{z^{\prime}}\frac{d}{dz^{\prime}}\right)^{k}\left((z^{\prime})^{n-2}r_{n}\varphi(z)\right)=c_{n,k}\ (z^{\prime})^{n-2-2k}r_{n-2k}\varphi(z),

where
c_{n,k}=\frac{(n-3)!!}{(n-3-2k)!!}\frac{c_{n-2k}}{c_{n}}
.

Together with Corollary
29
this implies the following.

Corollary 31
.

For odd
n:=2m+1
,
m\geq 1
and for
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{even}([-1,1])
the inverse of
r_{n}
is given by

(2.36)

\left(r_{2m+1}\right)^{-1}\psi(z)=\frac{1}{c_{2m+1,m-1}c_{3}}\ z\ \left(\frac{1}{z}\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{m}\left(z^{2m-1}\mathcal{P}\psi(z)\right)

Proof.

Apply Proposition
30
with
k=m-1
to obtain

(2.37)

\varphi(z)=\frac{1}{c_{2m+1,m-1}}r_{3}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{z^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{z^{\prime}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}}\right)^{m-1}(z^{\prime})^{2m-1}r_{2m+1}\varphi(z)\right).

Then write
\psi:=r_{2m+1}\varphi
and apply Corollary
29
. It is then enough to note that, as operators,
\left(z\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}+1\right)\frac{1}{z}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}
.
∎

Remark 32
.

Note that in (
2.36
) the coefficients of the differential operator on the right hand side are
polynomial
functions of
z
. Indeed the term of lowest degree is
z\left(\frac{1}{z}\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{m}\left(z^{2m-1}\right)
which is of degree zero. In other words, there exist polynomials
Q_{k,m}
such that

(2.38)

\left(r_{2m+1}\right)^{-1}\psi(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}Q_{k,m}(z)\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\right)^{k}\mathcal{P}\psi(z).

Thus we can still define the operator
\sum_{k=0}^{m}Q_{k,m}(z)D^{k}\mathcal{P}
on distributions. By density this is also equal to the inverse of
r_{2m+1}
.

Remark
32
together with (
2.29
) and Remark
27
proves the following.

Lemma 33
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}\left([-1,1]\right)
. There are polynomials
P_{k,m}
such that

(2.39)

\left(t_{2m+1}\right)^{-1}\psi=\sum_{k=0}^{m+2}P_{k,m}(z)\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\right)^{k}\mathcal{P}\psi(z).

This allows us to define the operator
\left(t_{2m+1}\right)^{-1}:=\sum_{k=0}^{m+2}P_{k,m}D^{k}\mathcal{P}
on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left([-1,1]\right)
.

Next proposition guarantees that, when applied to a constructible function, the operator on the right hand side of (
2.39
) gives back a constructible function.

Proposition 34
.

Let
\eta:P\times S^{m+1}\times[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a constructible function, and write
\eta(p,e,z)=\eta_{p,e}(z)
. Then

(2.40)

\left((t_{2m+1})^{-1}\eta_{p,e}\right)(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{m+2}P_{k,m}(z)\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\right)^{k}\mathcal{P}\eta_{p,e}(z),

is a constructible function of
(p,e,z).

Proof.

Since
\eta
is constructible, there are subanalytic functions
g_{1},\ldots,g_{a}
and
f_{i,1},\ldots,f_{i,b_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,a
such that:

(2.41)

\eta_{p,e}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\left(g_{i}(p,e,z)\prod_{j=1}^{b_{i}}\log f_{i,j}(p,e,z)\right).

Recall that the operator
\mathcal{P}
defined in Lemma
17
acts as
\mathcal{P}\psi(z)=\psi(\sqrt{1-z^{2}})
. In particular:

(2.42)

\mathcal{P}\eta_{p,e}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\left(g_{i}(p,e,\sqrt{1-z^{2}})\prod_{j=1}^{b_{i}}\log f_{i,j}(p,e,\sqrt{1-z^{2}})\right),

which is still constructible. The derivative of a constructible function is constructible (since
d\log h(z)=\frac{h^{\prime}(z)}{h(z)}
) and the multiplication of a constructible function by a polynomial is constructible. Therefore the right hand side of (
2.40
) is constructible.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1
.

In this section we prove Theorem
1
, which states that in a globally subanalytic family of convex bodies the set of zonoids is log-analytic. We formulate this as follows (the fact that we only use log-analytic functions in the definability follows from the proof).

Theorem 35
.

Let
P
be a subanalytic set and let
\left\{K_{p}\ |\ p\in P\right\}
be a subanalytic family of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then the set
\mathscr{Z}(P):=\left\{p\in P\ |\ K_{p}\text{ is a zonoid}\right\}
is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
.

Proof.

Let us consider
n=2m+1
with
m\geq 1
. We will prove that the set

(3.1)

S\mathscr{Z}:=\left\{(p,e)\in P\times S^{n}\ |\ S_{e}K_{p}\text{ is a zonoid}\right\}

is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
. By Lemma
7
we have that
\mathscr{Z}(P)=\left\{p\in P\ |\ \forall e\in S^{n},\ (p,e)\in S\mathscr{Z}\right\}
. Thus if
S\mathscr{Z}
is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
then
\mathscr{Z}(P)
is also definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
.

For
p\in P
let
h_{p}
be the support function of
K_{p}
and let
e\in S^{n}
. Then by (
2.27
)
S_{e}K_{p}
is a zonoid if and only if
\left(T_{2m+1}\right)^{-1}S_{e}h_{p}\geq 0
.

Let
\eta=\eta_{p,e}:=\zeta_{n}S_{e}h_{p}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{even}([-1,1])
and let
\Lambda=\Lambda_{p,e}:=t_{2m+1}^{-1}\eta\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{even}([-1,1])
. Then, by Remark
22
,
S_{e}K_{p}
is a zonoid if and only if
\Lambda\geq 0
. By Lemma
24
this is equivalent to have the following two conditions satisfied:

(i)

there is
\gamma:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
in
W^{2,1}([-1,1])
convex such that
D^{2}\gamma|_{(-1,1)}=\Lambda|_{(-1,1)}
;

(ii)

there exist
\lambda_{-1},\lambda_{1}\geq 0
such that
\Lambda-\gamma^{(2)}=\lambda_{-1}\delta_{-1}+\lambda_{1}\delta_{1}
.

The function
S_{e}h_{p}
is constructible as a function of
(p,e,u)
(Corollary
10
) thus, by Proposition
19
,
\eta
is also constructible (as a function of
(p,e,z)
). In particular there are subanalytic functions
g_{1},\ldots,g_{a}
and
f_{i,1},\ldots,f_{i,b_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,a
such that:

(3.2)

\eta_{p,e}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\left(g_{i}(p,e,z)\prod_{j=1}^{b_{i}}\log f_{i,j}(p,e,z)\right).

We apply now Lemma
12
to the family of subanalytic functions
\mathcal{F}=\{g_{i},f_{i,j}\}_{i=1,\ldots,a,j=1,\ldots,b_{i}}
defined on the subanalytic set
S=P\times S^{n}
, obtaining the corresponding partition
S=\coprod_{k=1}^{s}S_{k}
into subanalytic sets and the subanalytic functions
a_{k,1},\ldots,a_{k,\nu_{k}}:S_{k}\to[-1,1]
. We will show that for every
k=1,\ldots,s
the set of points
(p,e)\in S_{k}
such that
S_{e}K_{p}
is a zonoid is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
; since we are dealing with finitely many
S_{k}
, and since each
S_{k}
is subanalytic, the result will follow.

In order to simplify the notation, we omit the dependence on the subscript
k\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
and call
S=S_{k}
and
\nu=\nu_{k}
. For every
i=1,\ldots,\nu-1

we define
I_{i}=I_{i}(p,e):=]a_{i},a_{i+1}[
and
\bar{a}_{i}:=(a_{i}+a_{i+1})/2\in I_{i}
(the endpoints of this interval and its mid-point depend on
(p,e)
in a subanalytic way) . Let also
\eta_{i}:=\eta_{p,e}(\cdot)|_{I_{i}}
; this is a constructible function of
(p,e,z)
, since:

(3.3)

\eta_{i}(z)=\underbrace{\eta_{p,e}(z)}_{\mathrm{constructible}}\cdot\underbrace{\chi_{(a_{i}(p,e),a_{i+1}(p,e))}(z)}_{\mathrm{subanalytic}}.

By Proposition
34

\left(t_{2m+1}\right)^{-1}\eta_{i}
is a constructible function of
(p,e,z)
; for every fixed
(p,e)
it is continuous when restricted to
I_{i}
and, as a distribution, by Lemma
33
, it equals
(\Lambda_{p,e})|_{I_{i}}
.
For
i=1,\ldots,\nu
, we define the following function on
I_{i}
:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}_{p,e,i}(z)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\bar{a}_{i}}^{z}\int_{\bar{a}_{i}}^{s}\left(\left(t_{2m+1}\right)^{-1}\eta_{i}\right)(w)\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}s

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{z}\int_{-1}^{s}\left(\left(t_{2m+1}\right)^{-1}\eta_{p,e}(\cdot)|_{I_{i}(p,e)})(w)\right)\chi_{(\bar{a}_{i}(p,e),s)}(w)\chi_{(\bar{a}_{i}(p,e),z)}(s)\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}s

The integrand is constructible and Theorem
8
implies therefore that
\tilde{\psi}_{p,e,i}
is also constructible (as a function of
p
,
e
and
z
).

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
we let
\psi_{p,e}^{\alpha,\beta}(z)
be the function on
[-1,1]\setminus\{a_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,\nu}
given by

(3.6)

\psi_{p,e}^{\alpha,\beta}(z):=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\left(\tilde{\psi}_{p,e,i}(z)+\alpha_{i}z+\beta_{i}\right)\chi_{(a_{i},a_{i+1})}(z).

Condition (i) above is satisfied if and only if there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
such that
\psi_{p,e}^{\alpha,\beta}
extends to a convex function on
[-1,1]
; note that if such
\alpha,\beta
exist, the convex function
\psi_{p,e}^{\alpha,\beta}
is in
W^{2,1}([-1,1])
because its second derivative equals
\Lambda=t_{2m+1}^{-1}\eta
(which is a distribution, thus locally, and therefore globally on
[-1,1]
, integrable).

Since
\tilde{\psi}_{p,e,i}
is constructible, and the characteristic functions of the intervals
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
are also constructible, we can express this condition as a first order formula involving only constructible objects, therefore (i) is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
.

Suppose (i) is satisfied, then we can write
\Lambda_{p,e}-(\psi_{p,e}^{\alpha,\beta})^{(2)}
as

(3.7)

\Lambda_{p,e}-(\psi_{p,e}^{\alpha,\beta})^{(2)}=\sum_{i=0}^{m+2}\left(\lambda^{(i)}_{-1}D^{i}\delta_{-1}+\lambda^{(i)}_{1}D^{i}\delta_{1}\right),

where the order of the distribution is bounded by
m+2
because of Lemma
33
. Moreover the numbers
\lambda_{\pm 1}^{(i)}
are functions of
(p,e)
which are definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
. Indeed by Lemma
23
, their expression only involves the value of
P_{k,m}
from (
2.39
),
\eta_{p,e}
and their derivatives at the points
\pm 1
. Thus condition (ii) is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
as well:

(3.8)

\mathrm{(ii)}\iff\lambda_{\pm 1}^{(0)}\geq 0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lambda_{\pm 1}^{(i)}=0\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,m+2.

This proves that the set
S\mathscr{Z}:=\left\{(p,e)\in P\times S^{2m+1}\ |\ \Lambda_{p,e}\geq 0\right\}
is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\mathrm{exp}}
, as claimed.

Now if
n=2m
is even,
m\geq 1
, we consider the natural embedding

(3.9)

\displaystyle\iota:\mathbb{R}^{2m+1}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2m+2}

(3.10)

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2m+1})

\displaystyle\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2m+1},0),

which induces an embedding
\iota_{*}
at the level of convex bodies. A convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2m+1}
is a zonoid if and only if
\iota_{*}K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2m+2}
is a zonoid. Moreover we have

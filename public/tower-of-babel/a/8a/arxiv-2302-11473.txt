1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the following eigenvalue problem for the Dirichlet fractional
p\&q-
Laplacian

(2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+\mu(-\Delta)_{q}^{s}\,u+|u|^{p-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u=\lambda\ V(x)|u|^{p-2}u\quad&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0\quad&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded domain,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
1<q<p<\frac{N}{s}
,
\mu>0
and
V\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is such that the Lebesgue measure of
\{x\in\Omega\colon\ V(x)>0\}
is positive. The operator
(-\Delta)_{\alpha}^{s}
, with
\alpha\in\{p,q\}
, is the so called fractional
\alpha
-Laplacian operator which, up to normalization factors, may be defined for every function
u\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
as

(-\Delta)_{\alpha}^{s}u(x)=2\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{r}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\alpha-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s\alpha}}dy\quad\left(x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Problems of this type appear in the case of two different materials that involve power hardening exponents
p
and
q
. Let us point out that the combination of the operators
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u
and
(-\Delta)_{q}^{s}
appearing in problem (
2
) can be seen as a class of fractional unbalanced double phase problems. Such patterns are strictly connected with mathematical physics (fractional white-noise limit, fractional quantum mechanics, fractional superdiffusion, etc), see
[
3
]
. This operator also can be regarded as the fractional analog of the
p\&q-
Laplacian
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta_{q}
, which comes from the study of general reaction diffusion equations with nonhomogeneous diffusion

u_{t}=\operatorname{div}[A(u)\nabla u]+c(x,u)\text{ and }A(u)=|\nabla u|^{p-2}+|\nabla u|^{q-2}.

These kinds of problems stem from a wide range of important applications, including models of elementary particles, plasma physics, biophysics, reaction-diffusion equations, elasticity theory, etc, see the survey paper
[
39
]
for a more detailed account. Several results for
p\&q-
Laplacian problems obtained in bounded domains and in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
can be found in
[
11
,
13
,
14
,
25
,
32
,
45
]
and the references therein. Due to the numerous applications and challenging problems due to the nature of the fractional
p\&q-

operators, the corresponding non-local problems have attracted the attention of many researchers in recent years. For instance, see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
10
]
for the existence and multiplicity results for problems involving the fractional
p\&q-
Laplacian. However, to the best of our knowledge, there is not much literature available regarding eigenvalues problems including the fractional
p\&q-
Laplacian. The latter is crucial to understand and to derive many critical properties of the operator.

The study of eigenvalue problems has been a challenging labor whose beginning dates back to the mid-20th century, more precisely: Given two functionals
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
defined on a suitable space
\mathcal{X}
and a prescribed number
c
, the task of analyzing the existence of numbers
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and elements
u\in\mathcal{X}
satisfying (in some appropriated sense) equations of the type

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u)=\lambda\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(u),\qquad\mathcal{A}(u)=c,

where
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
denote the Fréchet derivatives of the functionals.

The prototypical
p-
Laplace operator (
p>1
) then became a focus of study, and in particular, to understand its spectral structure: Given an open and bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, to determine the existence couples
(\lambda,u)
satisfying the equation

(3)

-div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}u)=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega

in a suitable sense. In the seminal work of García Azorero and Peral Alonso
[
34
]
, they proved the existence of a variational sequence of eigenvalues tending to
+\infty
. However, they couldn’t establish whether it exhausts the spectrum unless
p=2
or
N=1
. Several properties on eigenvalues (and their corresponding eigenfunctions) were addressed by Anane et al
[
5
,
6
]
and Lindqvist
[
37
]
, among others, and also for more general boundary conditions than Dirichlet.

The non-local counterpart of problem (
3
) governed by the well-known
fractional
p-
Laplace
operator takes the form

(4)

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

The main difficulty here comes from the fact that this operator takes into account long-range interactions. Problem (
4
) was introduced in
[
36
]
. Several properties on eigenvalues and eigenfunctions were obtained in
[
27
,
33
,
36
]
. Allowing a growth behavior more general than power in problem (
4
) existence of eigenvalues was studied in
[
8
]
. See also
[
9
,
24
,
38
]
for problems involving the mixed fractional Laplacians.

Letting
\mu\rightarrow 0^{+}
, equation (
2
) becomes

(5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+|u|^{p-2}u=\lambda\ V(x)|u|^{p-2}u\quad&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0\quad&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

We define

(6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(p,V)=\inf_{u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega),\atop u\neq 0}\left\{\frac{[u]_{s,p}^{p}+\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{\int_{\Omega}V(x)|u|^{p}dx}\colon\quad\int_{\Omega}V(x)|u|^{p}dx>0\right\},

where
[\cdot]_{s,p}
is the Gagliardo semi-norm (see Section 2 for the precise definition).

By Theorem 3.3 of
[
40
]
for the special linear case of the fractional Laplacian and
V\equiv 1
, the infimum in (
6
) is achieved and
\lambda_{1}(p,1)>0
.

We aim to give a complete study of the spectrum of the previous equation and we plan to prove the following statements:

(i)

There exists a nondecreasing sequence of nonnegative eigenvalues obtained by the Alexander-Spanier Cohmology with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}-
coefficients method
(\lambda_{k})
tending to
\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

(ii)

The first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(p,V)
is simple and only eigenfunctions associated with
\lambda_{1}(p,V)
do not
change sign.

(iii)

The first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(p,V)
is isolated.

(iv)

We characterize the second eigenvalue by means of variational formulations, where the second eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}(p,V)
is well-defined, i.e.,

\lambda_{2}(p,V)=\min\{\lambda\colon\ \lambda\ \text{is an eigenvalue of \eqref{quation-p} and}\ \lambda>\lambda_{1}(p,V)\}>\lambda_{1}(p,V).

Because of the non-homogeneity of the operator in problem (
2
), many of the properties that eigenvalues of problem (
5
) are not inherited in the non-homogeneous case: for instance, isolation, simplicity and a variational characterization of the first eigenvalue, or a variational formula for the second one. Moreover, the spectrum of (
2
) will be continuous, and in principle, it is not clear the meaning of a first or second eigenvalue. In other word if we denote by
\sigma^{s}_{p,q}(V)
the spectrum of (
2
), which main

\sigma^{s}_{p,q}(V)=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\colon\ \lambda\ \text{is an eigenvalue of}\ \eqref{mainequation}\right\},

we can completely describe the spectrum as follows:

\sigma^{s}_{p,q}(V)=\begin{cases}(\lambda_{1}(p,V),+\infty),&\mbox{if }V\geq 0\\
(-\infty,-\lambda_{1}(p,-V))\cup(\lambda_{1}(p,V),+\infty),&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The main result reads:

Theorem 1.1
.

(i)

If
-\lambda_{1}\left(p,-V\right)\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}\left(p,V\right)
holds, then for any
\mu>0
, (
2
) has no non-trivial solutions.

(ii)

If
\lambda_{1}\left(p,V\right)\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{2}\left(p,V\right)
holds, then for any
\mu>0
, (
2
) has no sign-changing solutions.

(iii)

Tf
\lambda>\lambda_{1}\left(p,V\right)
or
\lambda<-\lambda_{1}\left(p,-V\right)
holds, then for any
\mu>0
, (
2
) has at least one positive solution.

Our second aim, is to analyze the stability of eigenvalues for equation (
2
) when the fractional parameter goes to
1^{-}
, in which case the limiting problem of (
2
) is formally given by

(7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)_{p}\,u+\mu(-\Delta)_{q}\,u+|u|^{p-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u=\lambda\ V(x)|u|^{p-2}u\quad&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0\quad&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

More precisely, we aim to show that any accumulation point
\lambda
of the set
\left\{\lambda^{s}\right\}_{s\in(0,1)}
(different from
\lambda_{1}(p,V)
) belongs to
\sigma_{p,q}^{1}
, where
\lambda^{s}\in\sigma_{p,q}^{s}
is such that
\sup_{s\in(0,1)}\lambda^{s}<\infty
(see Theorem
5.2
). For the limit problem (
7
), the continuity of the spectrum has been first studied by
[
41
]
. This investigation pursued in the second part of the paper is based upon the results by Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu
[
17
,
18
]
. We have that if
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)[u]_{s,p,\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{p}=K(p,N)\|\nabla u\|_{p,\Omega}^{p},

where the constant
K(p,N)
is given by

K(p,N):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\langle\sigma,\mathbf{e}\rangle|^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\sigma),\quad\mathbf{e}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}.

This line of research was developed, for instance, in
[
15
,
16
,
22
,
23
,
30
,
31
]
, and was found to be applicable to many cases.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 includes preliminaries and useful results about fractional order Sobolev spaces, the fractional

p\&q-
Laplacian. In Section 3, we give a complete study of the spectrum when
\mu=0
. Then we study the case when

in Section 4 and the case when

in Section 5.

2.
Preliminaries and useful results

2.1.
Fractional order Sobolev spaces

Given a fractional parameter
0<s<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we define the fractional order Sobolev space
W^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
as

W^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right):=\left\{u\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right):[u]_{s,p}<\infty\right\}

where the Gagliardo semi-norm
[\cdot]_{s,p}
is defined as

[u]_{s,p}^{p}:=K(N,s,p)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdy.

The constant
K(N,s,p)
is a normalizing constant defined as

K(N,s,p):=(1-s)\mathcal{K}(N,p),\qquad\text{ where }\quad\mathcal{K}(N,p)^{-1}:=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\omega_{n}^{p}\,dS_{\omega}

and
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The main feature of this constant is that, for any
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it holds that

\lim_{s\uparrow 1}[u]_{s,p}^{p}=\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p},

where the limit above is understood as an equality if
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\liminf_{s\uparrow 1}[u]_{s,p}^{p}=\infty
otherwise. This is the celebrated result obtained by Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu in
[
17
]
.

The space
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a Banach space with norm given by
\|u\|_{s,p}=\left(\|u\|_{p}^{p}+[u]_{s,p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
. This is a separable space and reflexive for
p>1
.
Given an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we then consider

W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right):u=0\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega\right\}.

By Poincaré inequality, it follows that
[\cdot]_{s,p}
defines a norm in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{s,p}.

It is well-known that
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega)
with

\begin{array}[]{ll}r\in(1,p^{*}_{s})&\quad\text{ if }sp<N\\
r=p_{s}^{*}&\quad\text{ if }sp\neq N\\
r\in(1,\infty)&\quad\text{ if }sp=N\end{array}

We also consider the usual
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
endowed with the norm
[u]_{1,p}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx
.

2.2.
The fractional
p-
Laplacian

For any
0<s\leq 1\leq p<\infty
we consider the energy functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{s,p}\colon W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathcal{J}_{s,p}(u)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p}[u]_{s,p}^{p}&\text{ if }0<s<1\\
\frac{1}{p}\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}&\text{ if }s=1.\end{cases}

We define a nonlinear operator
A_{s}\colon W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\left\langle A_{s}(u),v\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{K(N,s,p)}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\left\langle A_{1}(u),v\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}u\cdot\nabla v\,dx.

The functional
\mathcal{J}_{s,p}
is Fréchet differentiable and
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{s,p}\colon W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is continuous and for every
u,v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{s,p}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle A_{s}(u),v\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{1,p}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle A_{1}(u),v\right\rangle.

Therefore, for any
0<s\leq 1<p
we define the
fractional
p-
Laplace operator
as
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}:=\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{s,p}
.

We recall a useful property of the fractional
p-
Laplacian. Since
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is uniformly convex, it follows from
[
42
, Proposition 1.3]
that the operator
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
is of type
(S)
, i.e., every sequence
(u_{j})\subset W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
u_{j}\rightarrow u
weakly and
(\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{s,p}(u_{j}),u_{j}-u)\rightarrow 0
has a subsequence that converges strongly to
u
.

The usual
p-
Laplace operator can be seen as the limit of the fractional
p-
Laplacian as
s\uparrow 1
in the following sense:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
30
, Lemma 2.8]

Let
s_{k}\uparrow 1
and
v_{k}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
be such that
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left[v_{k}\right]_{s_{k},p}^{p}<\infty
. Assume, without loss of generality, that
v_{k}\rightarrow v
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega).
Then, for every
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we have

\left\langle\left(-\Delta_{p}\right)^{s_{k}}u,v_{k}\right\rangle\rightarrow\left\langle-\Delta_{p}u,v\right\rangle.

2.3.
Setting of the problem

In the case of the usual Sobolev spaces, for any
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
, it is easy to see that
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\subset W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. In the fractional case this kind of embedding is NOT TRUE. In fact, in
[
21
, Lemma 2.6]
it is proved that

W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{s_{2},q}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\text{for any }0<s_{1}<s_{2}<1\leq q<p<\infty,

this also holds when
p=q
(see
[
35
, Theorem 2.2]
).
However, the embedding

W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{s,q}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\text{ for any }0<s<1\leq q<p<\infty.

is not true (see
[
43
, Theorem 1.1]
). So, in order to deal with our problem (
2
), we consider the space

\mathcal{W}^{s}:=W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{s,q}_{0}(\Omega)

endowed with the norm
[\cdot]_{s}:=[\cdot]_{s,p}+[\cdot]_{s,q}
.

We also consider the energy functional related to equation (
2
) given by

\mathcal{J}_{s}(u)=\frac{1}{p}[u]_{s,p}^{p}+\frac{\mu}{q}[u]_{s,q}^{q}.

It is well-defined on the space
\mathcal{W}^{s}
for
0<s\leq 1
. In fact, it is straightforward to see that
\mathcal{J}_{s}\in C^{1}\left(\mathcal{W}^{s},\mathbb{R}\right)
and its Fréchet derivative is given by

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{s}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{s,p}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle+\mu\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{s,q}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle

for any
u,v\in\mathcal{W}^{s}
.

The expression
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{s}
defines what we call
fractional
p\&q-
Laplacian
.

We say that a function
u\in\mathcal{W}^{s}
is an eigenfunction of (
2
) corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
if

\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{s}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle+\int_{\Omega}(|u|^{p-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u)v\,dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}V(x)|u|^{p-2}uv\,dx\quad\text{ for all }v\in\mathcal{W}^{s}.

2.4.
Some properties of the fractional
p\&q-
Laplacian

It is well known that the operator
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
is strictly monotone. This is a consequence of the well known Simon inequality (see
[
44
]
)

\left(|a|^{p-2}a-|b|^{p-2}b\right)\cdot(a-b)\geq\begin{cases}c|a-b|^{p}&\text{ if }p\geq 2\\
c\frac{|a-b|^{2}}{(|a|+|b|)^{2-p}}&\text{ if }1<p<2,\end{cases}

for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\in\mathbb{N})
, where the constant
c
depends on
p
and
N
. From the monotonicity of
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
, it is easy to see that

\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{s}(u)-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{s}(v),u-v\rangle\geq 0\qquad u,v\in\mathcal{W}^{s}.

Moreover, from Lemma
2.1
it is immediate to see that
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{s}(u)\rightarrow\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{1}(u)
as
s\uparrow 1
in the following sense:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
s_{k}\uparrow 1
and
v_{k}\in\mathcal{W}^{s_{k}}
be such that
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left[v_{k}\right]_{s_{k}}<\infty
. Assume, without loss of generality, that
v_{k}\rightarrow v
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega).
Then, for every
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we have

\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{s_{k}}(u),v_{k}\rangle\rightarrow\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{1}(u),v\rangle,\quad\text{for any}\quad\mu>0.

The previous lemma says that, for any
\mu>0
,

\left\langle\left(-\Delta_{p}\right)^{s_{k}}u+\mu\left(-\Delta_{q}\right)^{s_{k}}u,v_{k}\right\rangle\rightarrow\left\langle-\Delta_{p}u-\mu\Delta_{q}u,v\right\rangle.

3.
The case
\mu\rightarrow 0^{+}

3.1.
The first eigenvalue

In this section we assume that the potential
V
is a positive weight of class
L^{r}(\Omega)
, with
r>\frac{N}{sp}
. Let

L^{p}(\Omega,V)=\left\{u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable }:V|u|^{p}\in L^{1}(\Omega)\right\}.

This space is endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p,V}
which is defined by

\|u\|_{p,V}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}V(x)|u(x)|^{p}\,dx.

We remark that the embedding
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega,V)
is compact. Indeed, by our assumption, for any
p>1
,
r^{\prime}p<p^{*}
, and then, by
[

W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r^{\prime}p}(\Omega)
is compact, and
L^{r^{\prime}p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega,V)
is continuous, since
\|u\|_{p,V}^{p}\leq\|V\|_{r}\|u\|_{r^{\prime}p}^{p}
for all

Proposition 3.1
.

The infimum in (
6

u_{1}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
normalized such that
\int_{\Omega}V(x)|u|^{p}dx=1

Proof.

Observe that we can write

\lambda_{1}(p,V)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{\mathcal{P}(u)}{\mathcal{B}(u)}

with
\mathcal{P}(u)=[u]_{s,p}^{p}+\|u\|_{p}^{p}
,
\mathcal{B}(u)=\|u\|_{p,V}^{p}
and
\mathcal{S}=\{u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\},\ \|u\|_{p,V}=1\}

\mathfrak{B}
is continuous with respect to weak sequential convergence,
\mathcal{B}^{{}^{\prime}}(u)=0
only at

, and
\mathcal{P}
is coercive on

, we can apply, for instance, Theorem 6.3.2 of
[

]
to conclude the result.
∎

Proposition 3.2

(Minimum principle)
.

If
u
is a nonnegative eigenfunction of (

) associated to

then
u>0
in
\Omega

Proof.

In light of

it is enough to prove that if

is an eigenfunction associated to

then

in all connected components of
\Omega
. Suppose, by contradiction, that there is

a connected component of
\Omega
such that

in

Taking

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\psi(x)-\psi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\int_{K}\psi(y)\int_{\Omega\backslash K}(u(x))^{p-1}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\right)dxdy=0,\ \forall\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(K).

Then

∎

\Omega_{\pm}

Lemma 3.1

If

(\lambda C\|V\|_{r})^{\frac{1}{\frac{p}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}}\leq|\Omega_{\pm}|

where

Proof.

Due to Proposition

5.

First, we give a convergence principle of solutions of a more general type equation with this form

(23)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+\mu(-\Delta)_{q}^{s}\,u+|u|^{p-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u=f(x,u)\quad&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0\quad&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

Here, we need to impose some conditions on

in order to guarantee that weak solutions are well defined:

(

f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(

We will prove that any accumulation point of the sequence of solutions is in fact a solution to the local limit problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)_{p}\,u+\mu(-\Delta)_{q}\,u+|u|^{p-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u=f(x,u)\quad&\text{in }\Omega\\

Theorem 5.1
.

On the power of standard information for tractability for L 2 L_{2} -approximation in the randomized setting
Wanting Lu, Heping Wang

Abstract
. We study approximation of multivariate functions from a separable Hilbert space in the randomized setting with the error measured in the weighted L 2 L_{2} norm. We consider algorithms that use standard information Λ std \Lambda^{\rm std} consisting of function values or general linear information Λ all \Lambda^{\rm all} consisting of arbitrary linear functionals. We use the weighted least squares regression algorithm to obtain the upper estimates of the minimal randomized error using Λ std \Lambda^{\rm std} . We investigate the equivalences of various notions of algebraic and exponential tractability for Λ std \Lambda^{\rm std} and Λ all \Lambda^{\rm all} for the normalized or absolute error criterion. We show that in the randomized setting for the normalized or absolute error criterion, the power of Λ std \Lambda^{\rm std} is the same as that of Λ all \Lambda^{\rm all} for all notions of exponential and algebraic tractability without any condition. Specifically, we solve four Open Problems 98, 100-102 as posed by E.Novak and H.Woźniakowski in the book: Tractability of Multivariate Problems, Volume III: Standard Information for Operators, EMS Tracts in Mathematics, Zürich, 2012.

Introduction

We study multivariate approximation
{\rm APP}=\{{\rm APP}_{d}\}_{d\in{N}}
, where

{{\rm APP}}_{d}:F_{d}\to G_{d}\ \ {\rm with}\ \ {\rm APP}_{d}\,f=f

is the compact embedding operator,
F_{d}
is a separable
Hilbert function space on
D_{d}
,
G_{d}
is a weighted
L_{2}
space
on
D_{d}
,
D_{d}\subset{R}^{d}
, and the dimension
d
is large
or even huge. We consider algorithms that use finitely many
information evaluations. Here information evaluation means linear
functional on
F_{d}
(general linear information) or function value
at some point (standard information). We use
\Lambda^{\rm all}
and

\Lambda^{\rm std}
to denote the extended class of all linear
functionals (not necessarily continuous) and the extended class of
all function values (defined only almost everywhere),
respectively.

For a given error threshold

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, the information complexity
n(\varepsilon,d)
is defined
to be the minimal number of information evaluations for which the
approximation error of some algorithm is at most
\varepsilon
.
Tractability is aimed at studying how the information complexity

n(\varepsilon,d)
depends on
\varepsilon
and
d
. There are two kinds of
tractability based on polynomial convergence and exponential
convergence. The algebraic tractability (ALG-tractability)
describes how the information complexity
n(\varepsilon,d)
behaves as a
function of
d
and
\varepsilon^{-1}
, while the exponential
tractability (EXP-tractability)
does as one of
d
and
(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})
. The existing notions of
tractability mainly include strong polynomial tractability (SPT),
polynomial tractability (PT), quasi-polynomial tractability (QPT),
weak tractability (WT),
(s,t)
-weak tractability (
(s,t)
-WT),
and uniform weak tractability (UWT).
In
recent years the study of algebraic and
exponential tractability has attracted much interest, and a great number of
interesting results
have been obtained
(see

[
31
,
32
,
33
,
43
,
9
,

,

,
39
,
6
,
5
,
21
,
36
,
45
,
15
,
2
,
26
,
37
]
and the references therein).

This paper is devoted to investigating the equivalences of
various notions of algebraic and exponential tractability for

\Lambda^{\rm std}
and
\Lambda^{\rm all}

in the randomized setting (see
[
33
, Chapter
22]
). The class
\Lambda^{\rm std}
is much smaller and much more
practical, and is much more difficult to analyze than the class

\Lambda^{\rm all}
. Hence, it is very important to study the power of

\Lambda^{\rm std}
compared to
\Lambda^{\rm all}
. There are many paper
devoted to this field (see

2.
Preliminaries and Main Results

2.1.
Deterministic worst case setting

For
d\in{N}
, let
F_{d}
be a separable Hilbert space
of
d
-variate functions defined on
D_{d}\subset{R}^{d}
,

G_{d}=L_{2}(D_{d},\rho_{d}(x)dx)
be a weighted
L_{2}
space, where

D_{d}
is a Borel measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
positive Lebesgue measure,
\rho_{d}
is a probability density
function on
D_{d}
. We consider the multivariate approximation
problem
{\rm APP}=\{{\rm APP}_{d}\}_{d\in{N}}
in the
deterministic worst case setting which is defined via the compact
embedding operator

(2.1)

{{\rm APP}}_{d}:F_{d}\to G_{d}\ \ {\rm with}\ \ {\rm APP}_{d}\,f=f.

We approximate
{\rm APP}_{d}f
by algorithms

A_{n,d}f
of the form

(2.2)

A_{n,d}f=\phi_{n,d}(L_{1}(f),L_{2}(f),\dots,L_{n}(f)),

where

L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{n}
are general linear functionals on
F_{d}
,
and
\phi_{n,d}:\;{R}^{n}\to G_{d}
is an arbitrary measurable mapping. The worst case
approximation error for the algorithm
A_{n,d}
of the form
(
2.2
) is defined as

e^{\rm wor}(A_{n,d})=\sup_{f\in F_{d},\ \|f\|_{F_{d}}\leq 1}\|{\rm APP}_{d}\,f-A_{n,d}f\|_{G_{d}}.

The
n
th minimal worst case
error is defined by

e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})=\inf_{A_{n,d}\ {\rm with}\ L_{i}\in\Lambda^{\rm all}}e^{\rm wor}(A_{n,d}),

where the infimum is taken over all algorithms of the form
(
2.2
).

For
n=0
, we use
A_{0,d}=0
. We obtain the so-called initial
error
e^{\rm wor}(0,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})
, defined by

e^{\rm wor}(0,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})=\sup_{f\in F_{d},\ \|f\|_{F_{d}}\leq 1}\|{\rm APP}_{d}\,f\|_{G_{d}}.

From
[
31
,
14
]
we know that
e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})
depends on the eigenpairs

\big\{(\lambda_{k,d},e_{k,d})\big\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of the operator

W_{d}={\rm APP}_{d}^{*}\,{\rm APP}_{d}\colon F_{d}\to F_{d},

where
{\rm APP}_{d}
is given by (
2.1
),
{\rm APP}_{d}^{*}
is
the adjoint operator of
{\rm APP}_{d}
, and

\lambda_{1,d}\geq\lambda_{2,d}\geq\dots\lambda_{n,d}\dots\geq 0.

That is,
\{e_{j,d}\}_{j\in{N}}
is an orthonormal basis in

F_{d}
, and

W_{d}\,e_{j,d}=\lambda_{j,d}\,e_{j,d}.

From
[
31
, p. 118]

we get that the
n
th minimal worst case
error is

e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})=(\lambda_{n+1,d})^{1/2},

and it is achieved by the optimal algorithm

S_{n,d}^{*}f=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle f,e_{k,d}\rangle_{F_{d}}\,e_{k,d},

that is,

(2.3)

e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})=\sup_{f\in F_{d},\ \|f\|_{F_{d}}\leq 1}\|f-S_{n,d}^{*}f\|_{G_{d}}=(\lambda_{n+1,d})^{1/2}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that all the eigenvalues
are positive. We set

\eta_{k,d}=\lambda_{k,d}^{-1/2}e_{k,d},\ \ k\in{N}.

We remark that
\{e_{k,d}\}
is an orthonormal basis in

F_{d}
,
\{\eta_{k,d}\}
is an orthonormal system in
G_{d}
, and
for
f\in F_{d}
,

\langle f,e_{k,d}\rangle_{{F_{d}}}\,e_{k,d}=\langle f,\eta_{k,d}\rangle_{{G_{d}}}\,\eta_{k,d},

and

(2.4)

S_{n,d}^{*}f=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle f,\eta_{k,d}\rangle_{G_{d}}\,\eta_{k,d}

2.2.
Randomized setting

In the randomized setting, we consider randomized algorithms

A_{n,d}^{\omega}
of the form

(2.5)

A_{n,d}^{\omega}(f)=\phi_{n,d,\omega}(L_{1,\omega}(f),\cdots,L_{n,\omega}(f)),\ L_{j,\omega}\in\Lambda,\ 1\leq j\leq n,

where
\Lambda\in\{\Lambda^{\rm all},\Lambda^{\rm std}\}
,
\phi_{n,d,\omega}
and
L_{j,\omega}

could be randomly selected according to some probability space

(\Omega,\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
, for any fixed
\omega\in\Omega
,

A_{n}^{\omega}
is a deterministic method with cardinality
n=n(f,\omega)
,
the number
n=n(f,\omega)
may be randomized and adaptively depend on
the input, and the cardinality of
(A_{n,d}^{\omega})
is then defined
by

{\rm Card}(A_{n,d}^{\omega})=\sup_{f\in F_{d},\ \|f\|_{F_{d}}\leq 1}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}n(f,\omega).

The randomized approximation error for the algorithm

A_{n,d}^{\omega}
of the form (
2.5
) is defined as

e^{\rm ran}(A_{n,d}^{\omega})=\sup_{f\in F_{d},\ \|f\|_{F_{d}}\leq 1}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\big\|{\rm APP}_{d}\,f-A_{n,d}^{\omega}(f)\big\|^{2}_{G_{d}}\Big)^{1/2}.

The
n
th minimal randomized error for
\Lambda\in\{\Lambda^{\rm all},\Lambda^{\rm std}\}
is defined by

e^{\rm ran}(n,d;\Lambda)=\inf_{A_{n,d}^{\omega}\ {\rm with}\ L_{i,\omega}\in\Lambda}e^{\rm ran}(A_{n,d}^{\omega}),

where the infimum is taken over all randomized algorithms

A_{n,d}^{\omega}
of the form (
2.5
) with
{\rm Card}(A_{n,d}^{\omega})\leq n
.

For
n=0
, we use
A_{0,d}^{\omega}=0
. We have

e^{\rm ran}(0,d;\Lambda)=e^{\rm wor}(0,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})=(\lambda_{1,d})^{1/2}.

There are many papers devoted to studying randomized approximation
and relations of
e^{\rm ran}(n,d;\Lambda)
and
e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda)

for
\Lambda\in\{\Lambda^{\rm all},\Lambda^{\rm std}\}
(see
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
17
,
22
,
23
,
27
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
33
,
40
,
42
]
).

This paper is devoted to discussing the equivalence of
tractability for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
and
\Lambda^{\rm std}
in the
randomized settings. For
\Lambda^{\rm std}
the authors in

[
42
,
33
,
17
]
used simplified randomized algorithms of the
form

(2.6)

A_{n,\vec{t}}(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(t_{j})g_{j,\vec{t}},

where
\vec{t}=[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]

for some random points
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
from
D_{d}
, which are
independent, and each
t_{j}
is distributed according to some
probability. The functions
g_{j,\vec{t}}\in G_{d}
may depend on the
selected points
t_{j}
’s but are independent of
f
. For any
f
,
we view
A_{n,\cdot}(f)
as a random process, and
A_{n,\vec{t}}(f)
as its specific realization.

We stress that algorithms of the form (
2.6
) belong to a
restricted class of all randomized algorithms, which are called
randomized linear algorithms. Indeed, we assume that
n
is not
randomized, and for a fixed
\vec{t}
we consider only linear
algorithms in
f(t_{j})
. In this paper we also consider algorithms
of the form (
2.6
). However, in
[
42
,
33
,
17
]

t_{j}
’s
are assumed to be independent, while in this paper we only
assume that
\vec{t}
is distributed according to some probability,
and do not assume that
t_{j}
’s are independent.

The information complexity can be studied using either the
absolute error criterion (ABS) or the normalized error criterion
(NOR). For
\diamond\in\{{\rm wor,ran}\}
,
\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
, and
\Lambda\in\{\Lambda^{\rm all},\Lambda^{\rm std}\}
, we
define the information complexity
n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)
as

(2.7)

n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)=\inf\{n\ \big|\ e^{\diamond}(n,d;\Lambda)\leq\varepsilon{\rm CRI}_{d}\},

where

{\rm CRI}_{d}=\left\{\begin{split}&\ \ 1,\;\quad\qquad\text{for $\star$=ABS,}\\
&e^{\diamond}(0,d,\Lambda),\text{ for $\star$=NOR}\end{split}\right.\ \ =\ \ \left\{\begin{split}&\ 1,\;\qquad\ \ \ \qquad\text{ for $\star$=ABS,}\\
&\big(\lambda_{1,j}\big)^{1/2},\ \qquad\text{ for $\star$=NOR.}\end{split}\right.

We remark that

e^{\rm wor}(0,d,\Lambda^{\rm all})=e^{\rm ran}(0,d,\Lambda^{\rm all})=e^{\rm ran}(0,d,\Lambda^{\rm std}).

Since

\Lambda^{\rm std}\subset\Lambda^{\rm all},
we get

e^{\rm ran}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq e^{\rm ran}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm std}).

It follows that for
\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
,

(2.8)

n^{{\rm ran},\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq n^{{\rm ran},\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std}).

2.3.
Notions of tractability

In this subsection we briefly recall the various tractability
notions. Let
{\rm APP}=\{{\rm APP}_{d}\}_{d\in{N}}
,

\diamond\in\{{\rm wor,ran}\}
,
\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
, and

\Lambda\in\{\Lambda^{\rm all},\Lambda^{\rm std}\}
. In the
\diamond

setting for the class
\Lambda
, and for error criterion
\star
,
we say that
{\rm APP}
is

\bullet
Algebraic strongly polynomially tractable (ALG-SPT) if
there exist
C>0
and non-negative number
p
such that

(2.9)

n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq C\varepsilon^{-p},\ \text{for all}\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

The exponent
ALG-
p^{\diamond,\star}(\Lambda)
of ALG-SPT is defined as the
infimum of
p
for which (
2.9
) holds;

\bullet
Algebraic polynomially tractable (ALG-PT) if there
exist
C>0
and non-negative numbers
p,q
such that

n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq Cd^{q}\varepsilon^{-p},\ \text{for all}\ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1);

\bullet
Algebraic quasi-polynomially tractable (ALG-QPT) if
there exist
C>0
and non-negative number
t
such that

(2.10)

n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq C\exp(t(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln{\varepsilon^{-1}})),\ \text{for all}\ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

The exponent ALG-
t^{\diamond,\star}(\Lambda)
of ALG-QPT is defined as the infimum of
t
for
which (
2.10
) holds;

\bullet
Algebraic uniformly weakly tractable (ALG-UWT) if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{\varepsilon^{-\alpha}+d^{\beta}}=0,\ \text{for all}\ \alpha,\beta>0;

\bullet
Algebraic weakly tractable (ALG-WT) if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{\varepsilon^{-1}+d}=0;

\bullet
Algebraic
(s,t)
-weakly tractable (ALG-
(s,t)
-WT) for
fixed
s,t>0
if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{\varepsilon^{-s}+d^{t}}=0.

Clearly, ALG-
(1,1)
-WT is the same as ALG-WT. If
{\rm APP}
is
not ALG-WT, then
{\rm APP}
is called intractable.

If
the
n
th minimal error decays faster
than any polynomial and is exponentially convergent, then we
should study tractability with
\varepsilon^{-1}
being replaced by

(1+\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
, which is called exponential tractability.
Recently, there have been many papers studying exponential
tractability (see
[
6
,
5
,
46
,
37
,
21
,
15
,
2
,
26
]
).

In the definitions of ALG-SPT, ALG-PT, ALG-QPT, ALG-UWT, ALG-WT,
and ALG-
(s,t)
-WT, if we replace
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
by
(1+\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
, we get the definitions of
exponential strong
polynomial tractability
(EXP-SPT),
exponential polynomial
tractability
(EXP-PT),
exponential quasi-polynomial
tractability
(EXP-QPT),
exponential uniform weak
tractability

(EXP-UWT),
exponential weak tractability

(EXP-WT), and
exponential
(s,t)
-weak tractability

(EXP-
(s,t)
-WT), respectively. We now give the above notions of exponential tractability in
detail.

Let
{\rm APP}=\{{\rm APP}_{d}\}_{d\in{N}}
,
\diamond\in\{{\rm wor,ran}\}
,
\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
, and
\Lambda\in\{\Lambda^{\rm all},\Lambda^{\rm std}\}
. In the
\diamond
setting for the class

\Lambda
, and for error criterion
\star
, we say that
{\rm APP}
is

\bullet
Exponential strongly polynomially tractable (EXP-SPT) if
there exist
C>0
and non-negative number
p
such that

(2.11)

n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq C(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1)^{p},\ \text{for all}\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

The exponent
EXP-
p^{\diamond,\star}(\Lambda)
of EXP-SPT is defined as the
infimum of
p
for which (
2.11
) holds;

\bullet
Exponential polynomially tractable (EXP-PT) if there
exist
C>0
and non-negative numbers
p,q
such that

n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq Cd^{q}(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1)^{p},\ \text{for all}\ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1);

\bullet
Exponential quasi-polynomially tractable (EXP-QPT) if
there exist
C>0
and non-negative number
t
such that

(2.12)

n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq C\exp(t(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1))),\ \text{for all}\ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

The exponent EXP-
t^{\diamond,\star}(\Lambda)
of EXP-QPT is defined as the infimum of
t
for
which (
2.12
) holds;

\bullet
Exponential uniformly weakly tractable (EXP-UWT) if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{\alpha}+d^{\beta}}=0,\ \text{for
all}\ \alpha,\beta>0;

\bullet
Exponential weakly tractable (EXP-WT) if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+d}=0;

\bullet
Exponential
(s,t)
-weakly tractable (EXP-
(s,t)
-WT)
for fixed
s,t>0
if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\diamond,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}}=0.

2.4.
Equivalences of tractability for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in
the worst case and randomized settings

In this subsection, we introduce the equivalences of tractability
for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the worst case and randomized settings. It
follows from
[
30
]
,
[
31
, p. 284]

\frac{1}{2}e^{\rm wor}(4n-1,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq e^{\rm ran}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all}),

which means that for

\frac{1}{4}\left(n^{\rm wor,\star}(2\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})+1\right)\leq n^{\rm ran,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq n^{\rm wor,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm all}).

2.13

Main results

We shall give main results of this paper in this subsection. The
first important progress about the relation between

and
e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})
was
obtained by Wasilkowski and Woźniakowski in

constructing iterated Monte Carlo methods. They showed that the

and
e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})

of ALG-SPT and ALG-PT for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
and
\Lambda^{\rm std}
for
the normalized error criterion in the randomized setting. Novak
and Woźniakowski in
[

and Krieg in
[

the above randomized algorithms and showed that

is asymptotically of the same order as

e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})
given that
e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})
is regularly decreasing. However, the
obtained relations are heavily dependent of the initial error,
and are not sharp if
e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})
is
exponentially convergent.

If nodes

S_{m,d}^{*}(f)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\langle f,\eta_{k,d}\rangle_{G_{d}}\eta_{k,d}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|f-S_{\rm X}^{m}(f)\|_{G_{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|f-S_{m,d}^{*}(f)\|_{G_{d}}^{2}+\|S_{\rm X}^{m}(f-S_{m,d}^{*}(f))\|_{G_{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|g\|_{G_{d}}^{2}+\|S_{\rm X}^{m}(g)\|_{G_{d}}^{2},

where
g=f-S_{m,d}^{*}(f)
.

We recall that

S_{\rm X}^{m}(g)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathaccent 869{c}_{k}\eta_{k,d},\,\mathaccent 869{c}=(\mathaccent 869{c}_{1},\dots,\mathaccent 869{c}_{m})^{T}=((\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{*}\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{-1}(\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{*}\mathaccent 869{\rm g},

where

\mathaccent 869{\rm g}=(\mathaccent 869{g}(x^{1}),\cdots,\mathaccent 869{g}(x^{n}))^{T},\ \ \ \mathaccent 869{g}=\frac{g}{\sqrt{h_{m,d}}}.

Since
\{\eta_{k,d}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal
system in
G_{d}
, we get

\displaystyle\|S_{\rm X}^{m}(g)\|_{G_{d}}^{2}=\|\mathaccent 869{c}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|((\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{*}\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{-1}(\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{*}\mathaccent 869{\rm g}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|((\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{*}\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{-1}\|\cdot\|(\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{*}\mathaccent 869{\rm g}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{n^{2}}\|(\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{*}\mathaccent 869{\rm g}\|_{2}^{2},

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
is the Euclidean norm of a vector. We have

\displaystyle\|(\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{*}\mathaccent 869{\rm g}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\overline{\mathaccent 869{\eta}_{k,d}(x^{j})}\cdot\mathaccent 869{g}(x^{j})\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\mathaccent 869{\eta}_{k,d}(x^{j})}\mathaccent 869{g}(x^{j})\mathaccent 869{\eta}_{k,d}(x^{i})\overline{\mathaccent 869{g}(x^{i})}.

It follows that

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\int_{\|\mathaccent 869{H}_{m}-I_{m}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}}\|(\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{*}\mathaccent 869{\rm g}\|_{2}^{2}\,d\mu_{m,d}(x^{1})\ldots d\mu_{m,d}(x^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{D_{d}^{n}}\|(\mathaccent 869{L}_{m})^{*}\mathaccent 869{\rm g}\|_{2}^{2}\,d\mu_{m,d}(x^{1})\ldots d\mu_{m,d}(x^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{D_{d}^{n}}\overline{\mathaccent 869{\eta}_{k,d}(x^{j})}\mathaccent 869{g}(x^{j})\mathaccent 869{\eta}_{k,d}(x^{i})\overline{\mathaccent 869{g}(x^{i})}\,d\mu_{m,d}(x^{1})\ldots d\mu_{m,d}(x^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}J_{k,i,j},

where

J_{k,i,j}=\int_{D_{d}^{n}}\overline{\mathaccent 869{\eta}_{k,d}(x^{j})}\mathaccent 869{g}(x^{j})\mathaccent 869{\eta}_{k,d}(x^{i})\overline{\mathaccent 869{g}(x^{i})}\,d\mu_{m,d}(x^{1})\ldots d\mu_{m,d}(x^{n}).

If
i\neq j
and

1\leq k\leq m
, then

J_{k,i,j}=|\langle\mathaccent 869{g},\mathaccent 869{\eta}_{k,d}\rangle_{H_{d}}|^{2}=|\langle g,\eta_{k,d}\rangle_{G_{d}}|^{2}=0;

If
i=j
, then

J_{k,i,j}=\|\mathaccent 869{\eta}_{k,d}\cdot\mathaccent 869{g}\|_{H_{d}}^{2}.

Since

h_{m,d}(x)=\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}|\eta_{k,d}(x)|^{2}
,
we get

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{i=j=1}^{n}J_{k,i,j}=n\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\mathaccent 869{\eta}_{k,d}\cdot\mathaccent 869{g}\|_{H_{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{D_{d}^{n}}|\mathaccent 869{g}(x)\mathaccent 869{\eta}_{k,d}(x)|^{2}\rho_{d}(x)h_{m,d}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=n\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{D_{d}^{n}}\frac{|g(x)\eta_{k,d}(x)|^{2}}{h_{m,d}(x)}\rho_{d}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=n\int_{D_{d}^{n}}m|g(x)|^{2}\rho_{d}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=nm\cdot\|g\|_{G_{d}}^{2}.

Hence, by (
2.3
) we have

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\|\mathaccent 869{H}_{m}-I_{m}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}}\|f-S_{X}^{m}(f)\|_{G_{d}}^{2}\,d\mu_{m,d}(x^{1})\ldots d\mu_{m,d}(x^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{G_{d}}^{2}+\frac{4}{n^{2}}J\leq(1+\frac{4m}{n})\|g\|_{G_{d}}^{2}\leq(1+\frac{4m}{n})(e^{\rm wor}(m,d;\Lambda^{\rm all}))^{2}.

We conclude that

\mathbb{E}\left(\|f-S_{\rm X}^{m}(f)\|_{G_{d}}^{2}\ \Big|\ \|\mathaccent 869{H}_{m}-I_{m}\|\leq 1/2\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{\|\mathaccent 869{H}_{m}-I_{m}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}}\|f-S_{X}^{m}f\|^{2}_{G_{d}}d\mu_{m,d}(x^{1})\ldots d\mu_{m,d}(x^{n})}{\mathbb{P}(\|\mathaccent 869{H}_{m}-I_{m}\|\leq\frac{1}{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{4m}{n}\right)\frac{1}{1-\delta}\,(e^{\rm wor}(m,d;\Lambda^{\rm all}))^{2},

where in the last inequality we used (
3.4
). This completes
the proof of Theorem 2.2.
\hfill\Box

Proof of Theorem 2.3.

It follows that

\displaystyle n^{\rm ran,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})

\displaystyle=\min\big\{n\,\big|\,e^{\rm ran}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq\varepsilon{\rm CRI}_{d}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq\min\big\{n\,\big|\,4e^{\rm wor}(m,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq\varepsilon{\rm CRI}_{d}\big\}

We note that

m=\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{48\sqrt{2}\ln(4n)}\Big\rfloor\geq\frac{n}{48\sqrt{2}\ln(4n)}-1.

Taking logarithm on both sides of (

).

We note

\displaystyle+\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}\Big)\big(n^{\rm wor,\star}(\frac{\varepsilon}{A_{\delta}},d;\Lambda^{\rm all})+1\big),

[

and the equivalence of ALG-SPT under the condition (
4.1
)
with
s_{\lambda}=0
.

results of ALG-PT and ALG-SPT without any condition. Hence, the
condition (
4.1
) is unnecessary. This solves Open Problem
101 as posed by Novak and Woźniakowski in
[
33
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

We consider the problem
{\rm APP}=\{{\rm APP}_{d}\}_{d\in{N}}

in the randomized setting for the absolute error criterion. Then,

\bullet

{\rm ALG}
-
{\rm PT}
for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
is
equivalent to
{\rm ALG}
-
{\rm PT}

for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
.

\bullet

{\rm ALG}
-
{\rm SPT}
for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
is
equivalent to
{\rm ALG}
-
{\rm SPT}
for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
. In
this case, the exponents of
{\rm ALG}
-
{\rm SPT}
for

\Lambda^{\rm all}
and
\Lambda^{\rm std}
are the same.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.8
) that ALG-PT (ALG-SPT) for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
means ALG-PT (ALG-SPT) for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the randomized setting.
Since ALG-PT (ALG-SPT) for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the worst case
setting is equivalent to
{\rm ALG}
-
{\rm PT}
(ALG-SPT) for

\Lambda^{\rm all}

in the randomized setting, it suffices to show that ALG-PT (ALG-SPT) for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the worst case
setting means that ALG-PT (ALG-SPT) for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
in
the randomized setting.

Suppose that ALG-PT holds for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the worst
case setting. Then there exist
C\geq 1
and non-negative
p,q

such that

(4.2)

n^{\rm wor,ABS}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq Cd^{q}\varepsilon^{-p},\ \ \text{for all}\ \ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

It follows from
(
2.19
) and (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle n^{\rm ran,ABS}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega}\Bigl(Cd^{q}(\frac{\varepsilon}{4})^{-p}+1\Bigr)^{1+\omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega}(2C\,4^{p})^{1+\omega}d^{q(1+\omega)}\varepsilon^{-p(1+\omega)},

which means that ALG-PT holds for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
in
the randomized setting.

If ALG-SPT holds for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the worst case
setting, then (
4.2
) holds with
q=0
. Using the same
method we obtain

n^{\rm ran,ABS}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq C_{\omega}(2C\,4^{p})^{1+\omega}\varepsilon^{-p(1+\omega)},

which means
that ALG-SPT holds for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
in the randomized
setting. Furthermore, since
\omega
can be arbitrary small, by
Corollary 2.1 we have

\displaystyle{\rm ALG\!-\!}p^{\rm ran,ABS}(\Lambda^{\rm std})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm ALG\!-\!}p^{\rm wor,ABS}(\Lambda^{\rm all})

\displaystyle={\rm ALG\!-\!}p^{\rm ran,ABS}(\Lambda^{\rm all})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm ALG\!-\!}p^{\rm ran,ABS}(\Lambda^{\rm std}),

which means that the exponents of
{\rm ALG}
-
{\rm SPT}
for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
and
\Lambda^{\rm std}
are the
same. This completes the proof of Theorem 4.1.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

We consider the problem
\rm APP=\{APP_{d}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}

{\rm ALG}
-
{\rm QPT}

{\rm ALG}
-
{\rm QPT}

\Lambda^{\rm std}

{\rm ALG}
-
{\rm QPT}
for

\Lambda^{\rm all}

\Lambda^{\rm std}

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Theorem 4.1,

\Lambda^{\rm std}

Suppose that ALG-QPT holds for
\Lambda^{\rm all}

C\geq 1

{\rm UWT}

for

.

Proof.

{\rm APP}

{\rm APP}
is ALG-
(s,t)

Proof of Theorem 2.5.

5.
Equivalence results of exponential tractability

First we consider exponential convergence. Assume that there exist
two constants
A\geq 1
and
q\in(0,1)
such that

(5.1)

e^{\rm wor}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq Aq^{n+1}\sqrt{\lambda_{1,d}}\,.

Novak and Woźniakowski
proved in
[
33
, Theorem 22.18]
that there exist two constants

C_{1}\geq 1
and
q_{1}\in(q,1)
independent of
d
and
n
such that

(5.2)

e^{\rm ran}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq C_{1}A\,q_{1}^{\sqrt{n}}\,\sqrt{\lambda_{1,d}}\,.

If
A,q
in (
5.1
)
are independent of
d
, then

n^{\rm wor,NOR}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq C_{2}(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1),

and

n^{\rm ran,NOR}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq C_{3}(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1)^{2}.

Novak and Woźniakowski posed
Open Problem 100 which states

(1) Verify if the upper bound in (
5.2
) can be
improved.

(2) Find the smallest
p
for which there holds

n^{\rm ran,NOR}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq C_{4}(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1)^{p}.

We know that
p\leq 2
, and if
(
5.1
) is sharp then
p\geq 1
.

The following theorem gives a confirmative solution to Open
Problem 100 (1). We improve enormously the upper bound
q_{1}^{\sqrt{n}}
in (
5.2
) to
q_{2}^{\frac{n}{\ln(4n)}}
in (
5.5
),
where
q_{1},q_{2}\in(q,1)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
m,n\in{N}
and

(5.3)

m=\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{48\sqrt{2}\ln(4n)}\Big\rfloor.

Then we have

(5.4)

e^{\rm ran}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq 4e^{\rm wor}(m,d;\Lambda^{\rm all}).

Specifically, if (
5.1
) holds, then we have

(5.5)

e^{\rm ran}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq 4Aq_{2}^{\frac{n}{\ln(4n)}}\sqrt{\lambda_{1,d}}\,,

where
q_{2}=q^{\frac{1}{48\sqrt{2}}}\in(q,1)
.

Proof.

Inequality (
5.4
) is just (
3.7
), which
has been proved. If (
5.1
) holds, then by (
5.3
) and
(
5.4
) we get

e^{\rm ran}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq 4A\,q^{\big\lfloor\frac{n}{48\sqrt{2}\ln(4n)}\big\rfloor+1}\sqrt{\lambda_{1,d}}\,\leq 4Aq^{\frac{n}{48\sqrt{2}\ln(4n)}}\sqrt{\lambda_{1,d}}=4Aq_{2}^{\frac{n}{\ln(4n)}}\sqrt{\lambda_{1,d}}.

This completes the proof of Theorem 5.1.
∎

Now we consider the equivalences of various notions of
exponential tractability for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
and

\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the randomized setting. As far as we
know, there is hardly any result for these equivalences.

First we consider the equivalences of EXP-PT and EXP-SPT for

\Lambda^{\rm std}
and
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the randomized
setting.
We obtain the following equivalent
results of ALG-PT and ALG-SPT without any condition.

Theorem 5.2
.

We consider the problem
\rm APP=\{APP_{d}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
in
the randomized setting for the absolute or normalized error
criterion. Then,

\bullet

{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm PT}
for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
is
equivalent to
{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm PT}

for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
.

\bullet

{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm SPT}
for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
is
equivalent to
{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm SPT}
for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
. In
this case, the exponents of
{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm SPT}
for

\Lambda^{\rm all}
and
\Lambda^{\rm std}
are the same.

Proof.

Again, it is enough to prove that EXP-PT for
\Lambda^{\rm all}

in the worst case setting implies EXP-PT for
\Lambda^{\rm std}

in the randomized setting.

Suppose that EXP-PT holds for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the worst
case setting. Then there exist
C\geq 1
and non-negative
p,q
,
for
\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
such that

(5.6)

n^{\rm wor,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq Cd^{q}(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1)^{p},\ \text{for all}\ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

It follows from
(
2.19
) and (
5.6
) that

\displaystyle n^{\rm ran,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega}\Bigl(Cd^{q}(\ln(\frac{\varepsilon}{4})^{-1}+1)^{p}+1\Bigr)^{1+\omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega}(2C)^{1+\omega}(1+\ln 4)^{p(1+\omega)}d^{q(1+\omega)}(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1)^{p(1+\omega)},

which means that EXP-PT holds for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
in
the randomized setting.

If EXP-SPT holds for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the worst case
setting, then (
5.6
) holds with
q=0
. We obtain

n^{\rm ran,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq C_{\omega}(2C)^{1+\omega}(1+\ln 4)^{p(1+\omega)}(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1)^{p(1+\omega)},

which means that
EXP-SPT holds for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
in the randomized setting.
Furthermore, in this case we have

\displaystyle{\rm EXP\!-\!}p^{\rm ran,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm std})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm EXP\!-\!}p^{\rm wor,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm all})

\displaystyle={\rm EXP\!-\!}p^{\rm ran,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm all})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm EXP\!-\!}p^{\rm ran,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm std}),

which means that the exponents of
{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm SPT}
for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
and
\Lambda^{\rm std}
are the
same. This completes the proof of Theorem 5.2.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

We remark that if (
5.1
) holds with
A,q
independent of
d
, then the problem APP is EXP-SPT for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the randomized setting for the
normalized error criterion, and the exponent
{\rm EXP\!-\!}p^{\rm wor,NOR}(\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq 1
. If (
5.1
) is sharp, then

{\rm EXP\!-\!}p^{\rm wor,NOR}(\Lambda^{\rm all})=1
.

Open Problem 100 (2) is equivalent to finding the exponent
{\rm EXP\!-\!}p^{\rm ran,NOR}(\Lambda^{\rm std})
of
{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm SPT}
. By Theorem 5.2 we obtain that if (
5.1
) holds, then
{\rm EXP\!-\!}p^{\rm ran,NOR}(\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq 1,
and if
(
5.1
) is sharp, then
{\rm EXP\!-\!}p^{\rm ran,NOR}(\Lambda^{\rm std})=1
.

This solves Open Problem 100 (2) as posed by Novak and
Woźniakowski in
[
33
]
.

Next we consider the equivalence of EXP-QPT for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
and
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the randomized setting.
We obtain the following equivalent
result of EXP-QPT without any condition.

Theorem 5.4
.

We consider the problem
\rm APP=\{APP_{d}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
in
the randomized setting for the absolute or normalized error
criterion.
Then,
{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm QPT}
for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
is
equivalent to
{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm QPT}

for
\Lambda^{\rm std}
. In this case, the exponents
of
{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm QPT}
for

\Lambda^{\rm all}
and
\Lambda^{\rm std}
are the same.

Proof.

Again,
it is enough to prove that EXP-QPT for

\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the worst case setting implies EXP-QPT for

\Lambda^{\rm std}
in the randomized setting.

Suppose that EXP-QPT holds for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the worst
case setting. Then there exist
C\geq 1
and non-negative
t

such that

(5.7)

n^{\rm wor,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq C\exp(t(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1))),\ \text{for all}\ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

It follows from (
2.19
)
and (
5.7
) that for
\omega>0
,

\displaystyle\quad\ n^{\rm ran,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega}\Bigl(n^{\rm wor,\star}(\varepsilon/4,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})+1\Bigr)^{1+\omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega}\Bigl(C\exp\big(t(1+\ln{d})\big(1+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+\ln 4+1))\big)+1\Bigr)^{1+\omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega}(2C)^{1+\omega}\exp\big((1+\omega)t(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln(\ln 4+1)+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1))\big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega}(2C)^{1+\omega}\exp\big(t^{*}(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1))\big),

where
t^{*}=(1+\omega)(1+\ln(\ln 4+1))t
, in the third
inequality we used the fact

\ln(1+a+b)\leq\ln(1+a)+\ln(1+b),\ \ \ a,b\geq 0.

This implies that EXP-QPT holds for

\Lambda^{\rm std}
in the randomized setting.

Next we show that the exponents EXP-
t^{\rm ran,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm all})
and EXP-
t^{\rm ran,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm std})
are equal if
EXP-QPT holds for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
in the worst case setting.
We have

\displaystyle{\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\rm wor,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm all})={\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\rm ran,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq{\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\rm ran,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm std}).

It suffices
to show that

{\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\rm ran,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq{\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\rm wor,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm all}).

Note that using (
2.19
) we can
only obtain that

{\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\rm ran,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq(1+\ln 4)\cdot{\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\rm wor,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm all}).

Instead we use
(
2.20
). For sufficiently small
\delta>0
and
\omega>0
, it
follows from (
2.20
) and (
5.7
) that

\displaystyle\quad\ n^{\rm ran,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega,\delta}\big(n^{\rm wor,\star}(\frac{\varepsilon}{A_{\delta}},d;\Lambda^{\rm all})+1\big)^{1+\omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega,\delta}(2C)^{1+\omega}\exp\big((1+\omega)t(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln(\ln A_{\delta}+1)+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1))\big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega,\delta}(2C)^{1+\omega}\exp\big((1+\omega)t(1+\ln(\ln A_{\delta}+1))(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1))\big),

where

A_{\delta}=\big(1+\frac{1}{12\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta}}
.
Taking the infimum over
t
for which (
5.7
) holds, and
noting that
\lim\limits_{(\delta,\omega)\to(0,0)}(1+\omega)(1+\ln(\ln A_{\delta}+1))=1
,
we get that

\displaystyle{\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\rm ran,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq{\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\rm wor,\star}(\Lambda^{\rm all}).

This completes the proof of Theorem 5.4.
∎

PDE constrained shape optimisation with first-order and Newton-type methods in the W 1 , ∞ W^{1,\infty} topology
Klaus Deckelnick a, Philip J. Herbert b, and Michael Hinze c

Abstract
We present a general shape optimisation framework based on the method of mappings in the W 1 , ∞ W^{1,\infty} topology. We propose steepest descent and Newton-like minimisation algorithms for the numerical solution of the respective shape optimisation problems. Our work is built upon previous work of the authors in Deckelnick, Herbert, and Hinze, ESAIM: COCV 28 (2022) , where a W 1 , ∞ W^{1,\infty} framework for star-shaped domains is proposed. To illustrate our approach we present a selection of PDE constrained shape optimisation problems and compare our findings to results from so far classical Hilbert space methods and recent p p -approximations.

Introduction

This work considers the use of vector-valued Lipschitz continuous functions in the task of optimising shapes.
The use of Lipschitz functions for shape optimisations is not necessarily new, having appeared in
[
DHH22
]
in a limited setting of star-shaped domains.
While it may be possible to formulate many problems in a star-shaped setting, it lacks the natural formulation which is useful to practitioners, therefore making uptake in the community less likely.
Recent work which aimed to approximate this Lipschitz approach used a
p
-Laplacian.
Of course one is interested in the limit
p\to\infty
, however it can prove troublesome to utilise significantly large
p
in computation.
Our approach will not require the use of degenerate elliptic operators, but remain appropriate for the implementation by practitioners and will appear in upcoming work.
This article will not concern itself with an extensive analysis of the convergence of the proposed algorithms, but will focus on the ease of implementation and examples.
A particular novelty which we consider is the use of second order data in this Lipschitz setting for which we demonstrate an effective algorithm for its implementation in this shape context.
An analysis for a model problem with a first order method is presented within
[
DHH23
]
.
The second order method we present, or some variety of it would be very interesting to analyse.

We are interested in the numerical solutions of a number of shape optimisation problems

\min\mathcal{J}(\Omega),\,\Omega\in\mathcal{S},

(1)

where
\mathcal{S}
is a collection of admissible domains.
This collection and the functional
\mathcal{J}
will vary depending on the application.
To find, at least local, minima of this problem, we will consider a descent method.
By this, we mean that, given
\Omega\in\mathcal{S}
, we seek
V^{*}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V^{*}]<0
and set
\Omega_{\rm new}=({\rm id}+\alpha V^{*})(\Omega)
for some suitably chosen
\alpha>0
.
To ensure that the map
\id+\alpha V^{*}
is a homeomorphism, it is sufficient to restrict to
\alpha
to be small enough that
\alpha|DV^{*}|<1
a.e., where,
|\cdot|
is pointwise the spectral (operator) norm.
While it is sufficient to take any sub-multiplicative norm, the spectral norm is convenient as it relates the Lipschitz and
W^{1,\infty}
semi-norms.

In the literature, it is common to seek
V^{*}
in a Hilbert space
H
which represents the negative gradient i.e.

(V^{*},\eta)_{H}=(-\nabla_{H}\mathcal{J}(\Omega),\eta)_{H}:=-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[\eta]

(2)

for all
\eta\in H
, or equivalently, one might seek

V^{*}\in\argmin\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|V\|_{H}^{2}+\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]:V\in H\right\}.

(3)

A crucial issue in this context is the regularity of the solution
V^{*}
to problem (
3
), which strongly depends on the regularity of the current domain
\Omega
as well as the choice of
H
.
It for example is not clear whether
\id+\alpha V^{*}
defines a Lipschitz transformation for many frequent choices of
H
. In order to avoid these issues it was
suggested in
[
DHH22
]
to work directly in the space
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
and to consider the following problem

V^{*}\in\argmin\left\{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]:V\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}),|DV|\leq 1\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega\right\}.

(4)

In
[
DHH22
]
this idea was analysed and implemented for shape optimisation problems involving star-shaped domains.
It is the purpose of this
paper to extend this approach to more general domains including the use of Newton–type methods.

2
Shape derivatives and Lagrangian calculus

2.1
Preliminaries

In what follows we denote by
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
a convex hold-all domain. We
consider the shape optimisation problem

\min\mathcal{J}(\Omega),\,\Omega\in\mathcal{S},

(5)

where
\mathcal{S}
is a collection of admissible domains such that
\Omega\Subset D
for all

\Omega\in\mathcal{S}
.
Here, we use the symbol
\Subset
to denote compactly contained.
It is not difficult to see that
\id+V
is a bi-Lipschitz transformation from

D
to
D
provided that
V\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|DV\|_{L^{\infty}}<1
.
Assuming that
(\id+V)(\Omega)\in\mathcal{S}
for such
V
we say that
\mathcal{J}
is shape
differentiable at
\Omega
if (cf.
[
ADJ21
, Definition 4.1]
)

V\mapsto\mathcal{J}\bigl((\id+V)(\Omega)\bigr)
is Fréchet–differentiable at
V=0
as a mapping
from
W_{0}^{1,\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
\mathbb{R}
. An update step in a descent algorithm based on the Fréchet derivative of

\mathcal{J}
will then seek a direction
V\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]<0
. In order to determine the direction of steepest descent we are led to the problem of finding

V^{*}\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

V^{*}\in\argmin\left\{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]:V\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d}),|DV|\leq 1\mbox{ a.e. in }D\right\}.

(6)

Let us note that we are including the hold-all domain within this minimisation problem for the determination of a direction of steepest descent, along with a Dirichlet boundary condition on the boundary of the hold-all domain.
Note that the fact that
D
is convex ensures that
V
is a Lipschitz continuous function with Lipschitz constant
1
.
Using the direction (
6
) within a descent algorithm hence requires the solution of a highly nontrivial constrained
minimisation problem which can be approximated at the discrete level with the help of an alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM).

The above approach will lead to a first order method.
If
\mathcal{J}
is twice shape differentiable, it is worthwhile considering
a Newton–type approach as well. This can be achieved by replacing the minimisation problem (
6
) by

\min\left\{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\Omega)[V,V]+\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]:V\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,|DV|\leq 1\mbox{ a.e. in }D\right\},

(7)

where
t>0
may be interpreted as a damping factor.
Here, the evaluation of
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\Omega)
is by no means straightforward and we will use the Lagrangian calculus described in the next subsection
to carry out the calculations for the class of problems that we are interested in.
Our motivation for the formulation of (
7
) is the following approximation: for
t\in(-1,1)
and
V\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
|DV|\leq 1
a.e. in
D
it holds that

\mathcal{J}((\id+tV)(\Omega))=\mathcal{J}(\Omega)+t\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\Omega)[V,V]+\mbox{higher order terms}.

(8)

Throwing away these higher order terms and minimising over the admissible
V
, we recover (
7
).

Under appropriate conditions, one may show the existence of solutions of (
6
) and (
7
), the approach being similar to results presented in
[
PWF18
]
and
[
DHH22
]
.
In Theorem
1
we verify the existence of solutions with some reasonable assumptions.
In a discrete setting, the assumptions given for the continuous case are trivially satisfied.

2.2
Lagrangian framework for PDE–constrained optimisation

For the ease of exposition, let us consider a shape functional of the form

\mathcal{J}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}j(\cdot,y_{\Omega})\,{\rm d}x,

(9)

where
j\colon D\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is assumed to be sufficiently smooth and
y_{\Omega}
denotes
the solution of a PDE posed in
\Omega
.
Let us note that one may also consider the gradient of the solution in the functional
\mathcal{J}
which follows very similarly, but adds a layer of complexity to the already large formulae.
We shall adapt the Lagrangian framework developed in Sections 1.6.4 and 1.6.5 of

[
Hin+08
]
in order to compute
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})
and
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})
at a
fixed
domain

\hat{\Omega}\Subset D
. The main aspect of the Lagriangian method is to, in effect, decouple the state,
y_{\Omega}
, from, in the setting we consider, the shape,
\Omega
.
Denoting by
B
a small open neighbourhood of
0
in
W_{0}^{1,\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d})
we associate with
V\in B
the
perturbed domain
\Omega_{V}:=(\id+V)(\hat{\Omega})
. By transforming to
\hat{\Omega}
we find that, for the choice made in (
9
),
\mathcal{J}(\Omega_{V})=J(V,y_{\Omega_{V}}\circ(\id+V))
, where

J(V,y):=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}j(\id+V,y)\det(\iden+DV)\,{\rm d}\hat{x},

(10)

and we note that
|\det(\iden+DV)|=\det(\iden+DV)
if
|DV|
is sufficiently small.
The derivatives of this choice of
J
may be found in Appendix
A.1
.
In order to incorporate the PDE constraint we let
y=y_{\Omega_{V}}\circ(\id+V)
and suppose that
y_{\Omega_{V}}
solves the given
PDE problem on
\Omega_{V}
if and only if
e(V,y)=0
for some mapping

e\colon B\times X\rightarrow Z
. Here,
X
,
Z
are suitable function spaces on
\hat{\Omega}
and we assume in what follows
that
e_{y}(0,\hat{y})\colon X\rightarrow Z
is invertible, where
\hat{y}=y_{\hat{\Omega}}
. After choosing
B
smaller if necessary to apply an Implicit Function Theorem,
there exists for every
V\in B
a unique
y=y(V)\in X
such that
e(V,y(V))=0
, so that we may write

\mathcal{J}(\Omega_{V})=J(V,y(V))

where, in the context of optimal control, the map
V\mapsto\mathcal{J}(\Omega_{V})
takes the role of a reduced cost functional.
In order to calculate the derivatives of
\mathcal{J}
it is convenient to introduce the
Lagrange functional
L\colon X\times B\times Z^{*}\to\mathbb{R}

L(y,V,p)={J(V,y)}+\langle p,e(V,y)\rangle,

(11)

so that

\mathcal{J}(\Omega_{V})=L(y(V),V,p)\quad\mbox{ for any }p\in Z^{*}.

If we denote by
p(V)
the solution of
L_{y}(y(V),V,p(V))=0
, one immediately obtains that

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V]=L_{V}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})[V],

(12)

where
\hat{p}=p(0)
. In a similar way one finds for the second derivative

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V,W]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{yy}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})[y^{\prime}(0)[V],y^{\prime}(0)[W]]+L_{yV}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})[V,y^{\prime}(0)[W]]

\displaystyle+L_{Vy}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})[y^{\prime}(0)[V],W]+L_{VV}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})[V,W],

where
y^{\prime}(0)[V]
is the derivative of
W\mapsto y(W)
at
W=0
in direction
V\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
which satisfies

\langle p,e_{y}(0,\hat{y})[y^{\prime}(0)[V]]\rangle=-\langle p,e_{V}(0,\hat{y})[V]\rangle,\quad\mbox{ for all }p\in Z^{*}.

(13)

For the implementation of the Newton-like method in (
7
), it is necessary to evaluate
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V,W]
many times.
In order to carry out the corresponding calculations as efficiently as possible we would like to avoid the frequent evaluation of

y^{\prime}(0)[W]
. To do, let us write

\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V,W]=\langle h_{1},y^{\prime}(0)[W]\rangle+\langle h_{2},W\rangle,

where

\displaystyle\langle h_{1},y\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{yy}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})[y^{\prime}(0)[V],y]+L_{yV}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})[V,y],

\displaystyle\langle h_{2},W\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{Vy}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})[y^{\prime}(0)[V],W]+L_{VV}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})[V,W].

We then first define
g\in Z^{*}
as the solution of

\langle g,e_{y}(0,\hat{y})[y]\rangle=\langle h_{1},y\rangle\quad\forall y\in X

(14)

and then set

\langle h_{3},W\rangle=-\langle g,e_{V}(0,\hat{y})[W]\rangle,\quad W\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d}).

This gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V,W]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle h_{1},y^{\prime}(0)[W]\rangle+\langle h_{2},W\rangle=\langle g,e_{y}(0,\hat{y})[y^{\prime}(0)[W]]\rangle+\langle h_{2},W\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle g,e_{V}(0,\hat{y})[W]\rangle+\langle h_{2},W\rangle=\langle h_{2}+h_{3},W\rangle.

The evaluation of
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V,\cdot]
hence essentially requires the solutions of (
13
) and of the adjoint problem (
14
).

2.3
Existence of descent-like directions

Now it is demonstrated how to construct the first and second derivatives, we demonstrate the well-posedness of the problem given in (
7
).
The result is very similar to those which appear in
[
PWF18
]
and
[
DHH22
]

Theorem 1
.

Given
t\geq 0
, suppose that the maps

\displaystyle V\mapsto

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]\mbox{ and}

(15)

\displaystyle V\mapsto

\displaystyle t\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\Omega)[V,V]

(16)

are weak-
*
lower-semi-continuous, then there exists

V^{*}\in\argmin\left\{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V,V]+\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V]:V\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,|DV|\leq 1\right\}.

(17)

Proof.

Consider a sequence
\{V_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\{V\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d}):|DV|\leq 1\}
such that

\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V_{n},V_{n}]+\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V_{n}]\searrow\\
\searrow\inf\left\{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V,V]+\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V]:V\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,|DV|\leq 1\right\}\mbox{ as }n\nearrow\infty.

(18)

It holds that
V_{n}
is bounded, hence there is a weak-
*
convergent subsequence, say
\{V_{n_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, and limit
V^{*}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
|DV_{h}^{*}|\leq 1
a.e. in
D
and
V_{n_{k}}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}V^{*}
in
W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Furthermore, by the assumed weak-
*
lower-semi-continuity of
t\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V,V]
and
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V]
, we have that
V^{*}
is a minimiser.
∎

Let us briefly discuss the assumptions above.
The condition that
V\mapsto\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]
is weak-
*
lower-semi-continuous is typically verified in practice by observing that there is
g_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g_{1}\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
such that

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]=\int_{\Omega}\left(g_{0}\cdot V+g_{1}:DV\right){\rm d}x,

(19)

which provides weak-
*
continuity.
The second condition is more tricky.
As we have seen in Section
2.2
that the construction of the second derivative is non-trivial, as such the condition is less easy to explicitly check.
However it is the case that, if the mapping
V\mapsto\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\Omega)[V,V]
non-negative, then it holds that it is weak-
*
lower semi-continuous.
This certainly depends on the shape optimisation problem at hand.
However it is perhaps not too unreasonable to assume that, near a minimiser, the second derivative of the energy is non-negative.

3
Example shape optimisation problems

Here, we now discuss a few example shape optimisation problems.

3.1
Poisson problem

As a first PDE constraint we here consider the Poisson problem.
We set
X=H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})
and
Z=X^{*}
.
Since we are in a reflexive setting, we use the canonical injection and identify
Z^{*}
with
X
.
By
y_{\Omega}
we denote
the solution of

-\Delta y_{\Omega}=F\mbox{ in }{\Omega},\quad y_{\Omega}=0\mbox{ on }\partial{\Omega}

(20)

for a given
F\in L^{2}(D)
.
In particular, we find that
y_{\Omega_{V}}\circ(\id+V)
is a solution of
e(V,y)=0
where
e\colon B\times H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})\rightarrow H^{-1}(\hat{\Omega})
is given by

\langle e(V,y),p\rangle=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(A(V)\nabla y\cdot\nabla p-F\circ(\id+V)p\right)\det(\iden+DV)\,{\rm d}\hat{x},\quad p\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega}),

(21)

and
A(V):=(\iden+DV)^{-1}(\iden+DV)^{-T}
.
Derivatives of the map
e
may be found in Appendix
A.2.1
.
With the Lagrange functional

L(V,y,p)=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}j(\id+V,y)\det(\iden+DV)\,{\rm d}\hat{x}+\langle e(V,y),p\rangle

we deduce from (
12
) the well–known formula

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{V}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})[V]

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(j(\cdot,\hat{y})\Div V+j_{x}(\cdot,\hat{y})\cdot V+\mathcal{A}[V]\nabla\hat{y}\cdot\nabla\hat{p}-\hat{p}\Div(FV)\right)d\hat{x},

where

\mathcal{A}[V]:=\iden\Div V-DV-DV^{T},

(23)

\hat{y}\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})
satisfies
e(0,\hat{y})=0
, and the adjoint
\hat{p}\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})
satisfies
L_{y}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})=0
, i.e.

\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\nabla\hat{p}\cdot\nabla\eta\,{\rm d}\hat{x}=-\int_{\hat{\Omega}}j_{y}(\cdot,\hat{y})\eta\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\quad\mbox{ for all }\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega}).

(24)

3.2
Bi-Laplace-type equation

Let us next consider the minimsation of
\mathcal{J}
as in (
9
) subject to the linear PDE of fourth order

\Delta^{2}y_{\Omega}=F\mbox{ in }\Omega,\quad y_{\Omega}=\Delta y_{\Omega}=0\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.

(25)

If the boundary of
\Omega
is sufficiently regular the above problem can be split into two second order Poisson problems by introducing
-\Delta y_{\Omega}
as an additional variable.
Let us note that this splitting is analytically useful to ensure that the shape derivative exists in the sense of
[
ADJ21
, Definition 4.1]
, due to the fourth order nature of the problem.
Let us comment that this need not be necessary since the shape differentiability, particularly boundedness in Lipschitz functions, with a fourth order constraint was demonstrated in
[

]
in a surface context, while
[
Las17
]
shows this for a fourth order eigenvalue problem.

On the fixed domain, we set
X=\left(H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})\right)^{2}
and
Z=X^{*}
.
Again we will use the canonical injection to identify
Z^{*}
with
X
.
Posing the split formulation of (
25
) on
\Omega_{V}
and transforming it back onto
\hat{\Omega}
in the same way as above we write the map
e
which represents the PDE constraint as,

\displaystyle\langle e(V,y),p\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\bigl(A(V)\nabla y_{1}\cdot\nabla p_{2}-y_{2}p_{2}\bigr)\det(\iden+DV)\,{\rm d}\hat{x}

(26)

\displaystyle+\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\bigl(A(V)\nabla y_{2}\cdot\nabla p_{1}-F\circ(\id+V)p_{1}\bigr)\det(\iden+DV)\,{\rm d}\hat{x},

for all
y=(y_{1},y_{2}),p=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\left(H_{0}^{1}(\hat{\Omega})\right)^{2}
.
Derivatives of the map

may be found in Appendix
A.2.2
.
Similar to (
22
) we obtain for the shape derivative

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(j(\cdot,\hat{y}_{1})\Div V+j_{x}(\cdot,\hat{y}_{1})\cdot V+\mathcal{A}[V]\nabla\hat{y}_{1}\cdot\nabla\hat{p}_{2}-\hat{y}_{2}\hat{p}_{2}\Div V\right)\,{\rm d}\hat{x}

(27)

\displaystyle+\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(\mathcal{A}[V]\nabla\hat{y}_{2}\cdot\nabla\hat{p}_{1}-\Div(FV)\hat{p}_{1}\right)\,{\rm d}\hat{x},

where
\hat{y}=(\hat{y}_{1},\hat{y}_{2})\in\left(H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})\right)^{2}
satisfies
e(0,\hat{y})=0
and the adjoint
\hat{p}=(\hat{p}_{1},\hat{p}_{2})\in(H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega}))^{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\nabla\hat{p}_{2}\cdot\nabla\eta_{1}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\hat{\Omega}}j_{y}(\cdot,\hat{y}_{1})\eta_{1}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\quad\forall\eta_{1}\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega}),

(28)

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\nabla\hat{p}_{1}\cdot\nabla\eta_{2}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{p}_{2}\eta_{2}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\quad\forall\eta_{2}\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega}).

(29)

3.3
Optimisation of the first eigenvalue for the Laplacian

Our aim is to apply the above Lagrangian framework also for the optimisation of the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplacian, i.e.

\mathcal{J}(\Omega)=\lambda_{1}(\Omega),

(30)

where
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
is defined by

\lambda_{1}(\Omega):=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{2}\,{\rm d}x:z\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\,\int_{\Omega}z^{2}\,{\rm d}x=1\right\}.

(31)

With the notation of Section
2.2
we again fix a
\hat{\Omega}\Subset D
which we now assume to be connected and set

\Omega_{V}=(\id+V)(\hat{\Omega})
. We transform the eigenvalue relation

-\Delta z_{\Omega_{V}}=\lambda z_{\Omega_{V}}\mbox{ in }\Omega_{V},\quad z_{\Omega_{V}}=0\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{V}

together with the condition
\int_{\Omega_{V}}z_{\Omega_{V}}^{2}{\rm d}x=1
onto
\hat{\Omega}
and write it in the form
e(V,y)=0
,
where
e\colon B\times X\rightarrow Z
, with
X=H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})\times\mathbb{R},Z=X^{*}
, and

\displaystyle\langle e(V,y),p\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(A(V)\nabla z\cdot\nabla q-\lambda zq\right)\,\det(\iden+DV)\,{\rm d}\hat{x}

(32)

\displaystyle+\mu\left(1-\int_{\hat{\Omega}}z^{2}\,\det(\iden+DV)\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\right),

for
y=(z,\lambda),p=(q,\mu)\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})\times\mathbb{R}
. Derivatives of the map
e
may be found
in Appendix
A.2.3
.
Let
\hat{z}\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})
be an eigenfunction to the first Dirichlet
eigenvalue
\hat{\lambda}
with
\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{z}^{2}\,{\rm d}x=1
. Then we have for all
p=(q,\mu)\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})\times\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\langle e_{V}(0,\hat{y})[V],p\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(\mathcal{A}[V]\nabla\hat{z}\cdot\nabla q-\hat{\lambda}\Div V\hat{z}q-\mu\Div V\hat{z}^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}\hat{x},

(33)

\displaystyle\langle e_{y}(0,\hat{y})[(\eta,\tilde{\eta})],p\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla q-\hat{\lambda}\eta q-\tilde{\eta}\hat{z}q-2\mu\hat{z}\eta\right)\,{\rm d}\hat{x},

(34)

where
\hat{y}=(\hat{z},\hat{\lambda})
.
Since
\hat{\lambda}
is simple, cf.
\hat{\Omega}
is connected and
\hat{\lambda}
is the first Dirichlet eigenvalue
[
Gil+77
, Theorem 8.38]
, it can be shown that

e_{y}(0,\hat{y})\colon H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow H^{-1}(\hat{\Omega})\times\mathbb{R}
is invertible. Thus we
can write for
V\in B

\mathcal{J}(\Omega_{V})=J(V,y(V)),\quad\mbox{ where }J(V,(z,\lambda))=\lambda.

The Lagrange functional is given by
L(y,V,p)=\lambda+\langle e(V,y),p\rangle
so that we derive with the help of (
22
)

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V]=L_{V}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})[V]=\langle e_{V}(0,\hat{y})V,\hat{p}\rangle.

(35)

The adjoint
\hat{p}=(\hat{q},\hat{\mu})
is given by the relation
L_{y}(\hat{y},0,\hat{p})=0
, i.e.

\tilde{\eta}+\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla\hat{q}-\hat{\lambda}\eta\hat{q}-\tilde{\eta}\hat{z}\hat{q}-2\hat{\mu}\hat{z}\eta\right){\rm d}\hat{x}=0\quad\forall(\eta,\tilde{\eta})\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})\times\mathbb{R}.

We infer that
\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{z}\hat{q}\,d\hat{x}=1
as well as

\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla\hat{q}-\hat{\lambda}\eta\hat{q}\right){\rm d}\hat{x}=2\hat{\mu}\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{z}\eta\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\quad\forall\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega}).

Choosing
\eta=\hat{z}
we deduce that
\hat{\mu}=0
, so that
\hat{q}
is an eigenfunction for the eigenvalue
\hat{\lambda}
.
Since
\hat{\lambda}
is simple and
\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{z}\hat{q}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}=1
we infer that
\hat{q}=\hat{z}
and hence by
(
35
) that (cf.
[
Hen06
,
HP18
]
)

\lambda_{1}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})[V]=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(\mathcal{A}[V]\nabla\hat{z}\cdot\nabla\hat{z}-\hat{\lambda}\Div V\hat{z}^{2}\right){\rm d}\hat{x}.

(36)

It is known that the first eigenvalue scales with volume, as such we are interested in fixing the volume of
\Omega
.
While it is known that the minimiser of the first eigenvalue is a ball, the methodology is interesting and can
be applied to more complicated eigenvalue problems.

4
Discretisation

Our aim is to formulate a descent algorithm which produces in each step a polygonal domain and which replaces
a possible PDE constraint with a corresponding finite element approximation. To begin, let
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}
be
a triangulation of the hold–all domain
D
.
We look for discrete directions of descent in the finite element spaces

\mathcal{V}_{h}^{n}:=\left\{V_{h}\in C^{0}(\overline{D};\mathbb{R}^{d}):V_{h}|_{T}\in P^{1}(T;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{n},\,V_{h}=0\mbox{ on }\partial D\right\},

(37)

where
P^{1}(T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes polynomials of degree at most one on
T
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{n}
is to be determined for
n\geq 1
.
With a polygonal initial domain,
\Omega_{0}
which is a union of the triangles in the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}
, we will set
\Omega_{n+1}=(\id+t_{n}V_{n})(\Omega_{n})
for
n\geq 1
, where
t_{n}\in(0,1)
is a step size and we will shortly explain how to choose
V_{n}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{n}
.
As well as updating the domain, the triangulation will also be updated,
\mathcal{T}^{n+1}_{h}=\{(\id+t_{n}V_{n})(T):T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{n}\}
.
By the choice of
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{n}
and the fact that
V_{n}
will satisfy
|DV_{n}|\leq 1
, it holds that the updated mesh will be admissible since
t_{n}\in(0,1)
.

4.1
Choice of descent direction

Let
n\geq 0
be fixed and let us denote the polygonal domain
\hat{\Omega}=\Omega_{n}\Subset D
which is a union of triangles in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{n}
.
For simplicity we will henceforth neglect the dependence on
n
.
Given
t\geq 0
, we aim to find
V_{h}^{*}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle V_{h}^{*}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\argmin\left\{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V_{h},V_{h}]\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V_{h}]:V_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h},|DV_{h}|\leq 1\mbox{ a.e. in }D\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\argmin\left\{\int_{D}\phi(DV_{h}){\rm d}x+\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V_{h},V_{h}]+\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V_{h}]:V_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}\right\}.

In this setting,
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}
and
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}
are suitable approximations of
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})
and
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})
respectively.
The function
\phi
is given by

\phi(A):=\begin{cases}0,&|A|\leq 1,\\
\infty,&|A|>1.\end{cases}

(38)

The approximations of the shape derivatives of
\mathcal{J}(\Omega)
may be given in many forms, it may not be the case that one wishes to take the shape derivative of the discrete energy, one may prefer to do some post-processing.
This leads to discussions of order of discretisation and optimisation which we do not wish to include here.

Let us now state that there exists a solution.
The proof of which is almost identical to that of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

Given fixed
h>0
and
t\geq 0
, there exists

V_{h}^{*}\in\argmin\left\{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V_{h},V_{h}]+\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V_{h}]:V_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h},\,|DV_{h}|\leq 1\right\}.

(39)

Unlike before, we did not need to make conditions on
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}
or
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}
, this is due to the finite dimensional nature ensures that the existence of a strongly convergengent subsequence of an infimising sequence.

Let us comment that the question of in what way and with which estimates does
V_{h}^{*}
converge as
h\to 0
is open.
In
[
Bar21
]
, a problem similar to the case
t=0
is considered.
Using discontinuous elements, the author was able to provide estimates on the energy.
Let us note that this lack of estimate is not expected to be prohibitive to an initial analysis of our proposed algorithm.

In order to find the function
V_{h}^{*}
, we use an alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) approach in order to solve the above problem.
To do so, we set

\mathcal{Q}_{h}:=\left\{q_{h}\in L^{2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}):q_{h}|_{T}\in P^{0}(T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),\,\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}

(40)

and consider for a given
\tau>0
the functional
\mathcal{L}_{\tau}\colon\mathcal{V}_{h}\times\mathcal{Q}_{h}\times\mathcal{Q}_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\tau}(V_{h},q_{h};\lambda_{h}):=&\int_{D}\left(\phi(q_{h})+\lambda_{h}:(DV_{h}-q_{h})\right){\rm d}x+\frac{\tau}{2}\|DV_{h}-q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}^{2}\\
&+\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V_{h},V_{h}]+\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V_{h}].\end{split}

(41)

The idea of ADMM is to alternatively minimise
\mathcal{L}_{\tau}
over
q_{h}
and
V_{h}
,
then perform an update to
\lambda_{h}
and repeat this until a certain quantity is small.
More precisely, the algorithm has the following form:

Algorithm 3
.

0. Choose
V^{0}_{h}
and
\lambda^{0}_{h}
such that
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V^{0}_{h}]<\infty

1. Set
R=\infty
,
j=1

While
R>tol
:

2. Find
q^{j}_{h}\in\argmin\{\mathcal{L}_{\tau}(V^{j-1}_{h},q_{h};\lambda^{j-1}_{h}):q_{h}\in\mathcal{Q}_{h},|q_{h}|\leq 1\}

3. Find
V^{j}_{h}\in\argmin\{\mathcal{L}_{\tau}(V_{h},q^{j}_{h};\lambda^{j-1}_{h}):V_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}\}

4. Set
\lambda^{j}_{h}=\lambda^{j-1}_{h}+\tau(DV^{j}_{h}-q^{j}_{h})

5. Set
R=\left(\|\lambda^{j}_{h}-\lambda^{j-1}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}^{2}+\tau^{2}\|DV^{j}_{h}-DV^{j-1}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

6. Update j = j+1

Let us also mention
[
BM20
]
which considers more general ADMM methods with variable
\tau
.
Particularly, in our experiments we make use of such an algorithm with variable
\tau
, namely
[
BM20
, Algorithm 3.19]
.

In a finite dimensional setting, since we have the existence of minimisers, c.f. Theorem
2
, it is almost immediate that Algorithm
3
converges.
It is necessary to check the sign of
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\Omega)_{h}
; in the locality of a minimiser, one might expect that it is non-negative.

Theorem 4

(ADMM converges)
.

Suppose that
t\in[0,1)
and
t\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}
is a non-negative operator, then it holds that Algorithm
3
converges.

This follows from
[
HY15
]
.
The assumption that
t\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\Omega)_{h}
is non-negative is made in order to ensure the appropriate functional is convex, which is a sufficient condition for the convergence of ADMM.

4.2
Evaluation of
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}

4.2.1
PDE–constrained shape optimisation

Let us formulate suitable approximations of the shape derivatives derived in
2.2
. Given the polygonal domain

\hat{\Omega}
we denote by
\mathbb{S}_{h}(\hat{\Omega})
the space of linear finite elements on
\hat{\Omega}
(resolved by a subtriangulation of

\mathcal{T}_{h}
) which vanish on
\partial\hat{\Omega}
. If the constraint is given by (
20
) we set

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V_{h}]=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(j(\cdot,\hat{y}_{h})\Div V_{h}+j_{x}(\cdot,\hat{y}_{h})\cdot V_{h}+\mathcal{A}[V_{h}]\nabla\hat{y}_{h}\cdot\nabla\hat{p}_{h}-\hat{p}_{h}\Div(FV_{h})\right){\rm d}\hat{x},

(42)

for
V_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, where
\hat{y}_{h},\hat{p}_{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{h}(\hat{\Omega})
satisfy

\begin{split}\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\nabla\hat{y}_{h}\cdot\nabla\eta_{h}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}=&\int_{\hat{\Omega}}F\eta_{h}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\quad\forall\eta_{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{h}(\hat{\Omega}),\\

(43)

On the other hand, if the constraint is given by (

) then we let

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V_{h}]=&\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(j(\cdot,\hat{y}_{h,1})\Div V_{h}+j_{x}(\cdot,\hat{y}_{h,1})\cdot V_{h}+\mathcal{A}[V]\nabla\hat{y}_{h,1}\cdot\nabla\hat{p}_{h,2}-\hat{y}_{h,2}\hat{p}_{h,2}\Div V_{h}\right)\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\\
&+\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(\mathcal{A}[V_{h}]\nabla\hat{y}_{h,2}\cdot\nabla\hat{p}_{h,1}-\Div(V_{h}F)\hat{p}_{h,1}\right)\,{\rm d}\hat{x},\end{split}

(44)

for
V_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
.
Here,
\hat{y}_{h}=(\hat{y}_{h,1},\hat{y}_{h,2})\in(\mathbb{S}_{h}(\hat{\Omega}))^{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\nabla\hat{y}_{h,1}\cdot\nabla\eta_{h}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{y}_{h,2}\eta_{h}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\quad\forall\eta_{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{h}(\hat{\Omega}),

(45)

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\nabla\hat{y}_{h,2}\cdot\nabla\eta_{h}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}F\eta_{h}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\quad\forall\eta_{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{h}(\hat{\Omega})

(46)

while
\hat{p}_{h}=(\hat{p}_{h,1},\hat{p}_{h,2})\in(\mathbb{S}_{h}(\hat{\Omega}))^{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\nabla\hat{p}_{h,2}\cdot\nabla\eta_{h}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\hat{\Omega}}j_{y}(\cdot,y_{h,1})\eta_{h}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\quad\forall\eta_{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{h}(\hat{\Omega}),

(47)

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\nabla\hat{p}_{h,1}\cdot\nabla\eta_{h}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{p}_{h,2}\eta_{h}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\quad\forall\eta_{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{h}(\hat{\Omega}).

(48)

4.2.2
Optimisation of the first eigenvalue for the Laplacian

For a given polygonal domain we determine
\hat{z}_{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{h}(\hat{\Omega})
and
\hat{\lambda}_{h}>0
such that
\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{y}_{h}^{2}\,{\rm d}x=1
and

\hat{\lambda}_{h}=\inf\left\{\int_{\hat{\Omega}}|\nabla\hat{z}_{h}|^{2}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}:\hat{z}_{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{h}(\hat{\Omega}),\,\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{z}_{h}^{2}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}=1\right\}=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}|\nabla\hat{z}_{h}|^{2}\,{\rm d}\hat{x}.

Supposing that the eigenvalue
\lambda_{h}
is simple we let, recalling (
36
)

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})_{h}[V_{h}]=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(\mathcal{A}[V_{h}]\nabla\hat{z}_{h}\cdot\nabla\hat{z}_{h}-\hat{\lambda}_{h}\hat{z}_{h}^{2}\Div V_{h}\right)\,{\rm d}\hat{x}\mbox{ for }V_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}.

(49)

5
Numerical experiments

We now provide numerical experiments for the applications we have described.
In the integrals for the energy we use quadrature of order 2, while for the shape derivatives, we are using
the order which is automatically decided by the software.

As mentioned above we will solve the state and adjoint PDEs with a finite element approximation.
The finite element approximation is performed with DUNE
[
Bas+21
]
, making particular use of the DUNE Python bindings
[
DNK20
,
DN18
]
.
We consider a construction of update direction using four different approaches.
Our approaches will be:

•

The direction of steepest descent method using the
W^{1,\infty}
-topology, constructed with an adaptive ADMM method, as mentioned after Algorithm
3
.
This will be referred to as
p=\infty
.

•

A Newton-type direction, which will be a discrete minimiser of (
7
) for a given
t>0
, referred to as the Newton method.
Much like the
p=\infty
case above, this will be constructed with an adaptive ADMM method.

•

To compare against existing approaches, for
p=2,4
, we will consider the minimiser of

In the case that

, this is seen to coincide with the discrete case of (
3
) with
H=H^{1}_{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the Dirichlet inner product.
We will refer to these cases by their

value.

The discrete functions produced by the

and
p=4
methods will be normalised so that they have a

semi-norm of

.
This normalisation is performed so that we need not check whether the mesh has overlapped.
For each of the experiments, we will set the hold-all domain to be the box

.
With these directions, we will move the vertices of our mesh according to an Armijo step rule.
We will stop after 20 shape updates have been made.
In most cases the domain has become close to stationary at this point and the Armijio step-size has become rather small.

The energy along the iterations will be plotted.
In the case that the minimiser is known, the origin will be offset by the known value, when the minimiser is not known, the origin will be offset by the smallest value attained in the experiment.

Let us further elaborate on Figure
12
, particularly the mesh produced by the infinity method.
We note that the mesh in the centre for the infinity method appears less regular than the other methods.
This appears to occur due to a somewhat undesirable approximation of the shape derivative, which should be concentrated at the boundary.
The undesirable approximation is due to the FEM approximation giving interior contributions to the shape derivatives, so-called
spurious
contributions, in the limit of the mesh becoming infinitely fine this should disappear.
Investigation suggests that these are more apparent in the infinity method but disappear when using shape derivatives with only boundary contributions.
This sort of expert knowledge is often exploited in the literature and appeared in e.g.
[
SSW16
]
to ’delete’ contributions to the shape derivative from nodes on the interior.

Conclusion

In this work we have introduced two new frameworks in which to perform shape optimisation.
These methods are more applicable to physical scenarios than the previous works involving
W^{1,\infty}
as they are not restricted to star-shaped domains.
While our examples had star-shaped final domains, our framework allows for more general shapes and is more readily applicable to industrial problems.
From the experiments, it was seen that our introduced first order method did not necessarily perform well energetically, however the meshes the method produced are regular.
The second order method we introduced performs well both energetically and in terms of the regularity of the mesh, a downside however is the need to tune the damping parameter
t
.

Calculations for the second shape derivatives

Here we collect the derivatives of

and
e
for the examples in Section

These derivatives are particularly useful for the calculation of the second shape derivative.
All of the maps and functions we consider are sufficiently smooth that we may exchange the order of differentiation.
It will be convenient to define

\mathbb{D}[V,W]:=\left(\Div(V)\Div(W)-\Tr(DVDW)\right).

A.1
Derivatives for the energy functionals

Let us consider
J
as in (
10
), that is
J(V,y):=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}j(\id+V,y)\det(\iden+DV)

for some fixed
\hat{\Omega}\Subset D
.
When

is twice differentiable, it holds that

\displaystyle J_{V}(0,y)[V]=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\Div Vj(\cdot,y)+j_{x}(\cdot,y)\cdot V,\quad J_{y}(0,y)[\eta]=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}j_{y}(\cdot,y)\,\eta,

\displaystyle J_{yV}(0,y)[\eta,V]=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\Div Vj_{y}(\cdot,y)\,\eta+j_{yx}(\cdot,y)\cdot V\eta,\quad J_{yy}(0,y)[\eta,\xi]=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}j_{yy}(\cdot,y)\eta\xi,

\displaystyle J_{VV}(0,y)[V,W]=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\mathbb{D}[V,W]j(\cdot,y)+\Div Vj_{x}(\cdot,y)\cdot W+\Div Wj_{x}(\cdot,y)\cdot V+j_{xx}(\cdot,y)V\cdot W.

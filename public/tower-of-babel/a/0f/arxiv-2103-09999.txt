Lower bound for the T T count via unitary stabilizer nullity
Jiaqing Jiang, Xin Wang

Abstract
We introduce magic measures to quantify the nonstabilizerness of multiqubit quantum gates and establish lower bounds on the T T count for fault-tolerant quantum computation. First, we introduce the stabilizer nullity of multi-qubit unitary, which is based on the subgroup of the quotient Pauli group associated with the unitary. This unitary stabilizer nullity extends the state-stabilizer nullity by Beverland et al. to a dynamic version. In particular, we show this nonstabilizerness measure has desirable properties such as subadditivity under composition and additivity under tensor product. Second, we prove that a given unitary’s stabilizer nullity is a lower bound for the T T count, utilizing the above properties in gate synthesis. Third, we compare the state- and the unitary-stabilizer nullity, proving that the lower bounds for the T T count obtained by the unitary-stabilizer nullity are never less than the state-stabilizer nullity. Moreover, we show an explicit n n -qubit unitary family of unitary-stabilizer nullity 2 ​ n 2n , which implies that its T T count is at least 2 ​ n 2n . This gives an example where the bounds derived by the unitary-stabilizer nullity strictly outperform the state-stabilizer nullity by a factor of 2 2 . We finally showcase the advantages of unitary-stabilizer nullity in estimating the T T count of quantum gates with interests.

The relevant magic monotones can be summarized as follows. For a pure state

Moreover, since they involve optimization, it is hard to derive explicit expressions for those magic monotones for

It is unclear and nontrivial to generalize the definition from states to unitaries. Beyond all the above, there are also magic monotones defined for qudit systems of odd prime dimension, which cannot be directly used for qubit systems

In this work, we define the unitary-stabilizer nullity. Instead of counting the operators in the Pauli group, which stabilize

In other words, we calculate the size of the intersection between

Denoting the size as

Intuitively,

To use

We then compare the lower bounds of the

.
We also prove that for any diagonal

Moreover, we give an explicit

In general, the unitary-stabilizer nullity can be computed in time

Finally, we connect the unitary-stabilizer nullity and the state-stabilizer nullity with auxiliary systems. Specifically, we prove that

is the
2n
-qubit maximally entangled state. We also give numerical results that

the

that for several gates of practical interest, the unitary-stabilizer unitary can derive

better

, and for all
{{\textbf{u}}}^{\prime}\neq{{\textbf{u}}}
,
P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}^{\prime}|{{\textbf{v}}})=0
.

Proof.

We give only proof to the first statement, the second is similar. Recall that

\displaystyle P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}U\sigma_{{\textbf{v}}}U^{\dagger})/2^{n},

(6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}\sigma_{{\textbf{v}}})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &2^{n},&\text{ if ${{\textbf{u}}}={{\textbf{v}}}$}\\
&0,&\text{ else.}\end{aligned}\right.

(7)

Since
U
is Clifford,
U(\cdot)U^{\dagger}
is an isomorphism of
\hat{{\cal P}}_{n}
, i.e.
U\hat{{\cal P}}_{n}U^{\dagger}=\hat{{\cal P}}_{n}
. In other words,
U(\cdot)U^{\dagger}
is a permutation over the Pauli operators
\{\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}\}_{{{\textbf{u}}}\in 4^{[n]}}
, up to a
\pm 1
sign. Thus by Eq. (
6
) and Eq. (
7
), we know

P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})=\pm 1
if and only if
U\sigma_{{{\textbf{v}}}}U^{\dagger}=\pm\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}
. Thus the lemma holds.
\blacksquare

Similar results also hold for general unitary
U
beyond Clifford operators, which is proved in the following lemma.

Lemma 2

For any
n
-qubit unitary
U
,
if there exist
{{\textbf{u}}},{{\textbf{v}}}\in 4^{[n]}
such that
P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})=\pm 1
. Then

\displaystyle U\sigma_{{\textbf{v}}}U^{\dagger}=\pm\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}},

(8)

\displaystyle\forall{{\textbf{u}}}^{\prime}\neq{{\textbf{u}}},P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}^{\prime}|{{\textbf{v}}})=0.

(9)

Proof.

Recall that from equation (
4
)
we know the Pauli transfer matrix of
U
, whose
({{\textbf{u}}},{{\textbf{v}}})
-th element is
P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}},{{\textbf{v}}})
, is orthogonal.
Specially, choosing
{{\textbf{v}}}^{\prime}={{\textbf{v}}}
in Eq. (
4
) we know

\displaystyle\sum_{{{\textbf{u}}}}|P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})|^{2}=1.

(10)

Thus if
P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})=\pm 1
, we have
\forall{{\textbf{u}}}^{\prime}\neq{{\textbf{u}}},P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}^{\prime}|{{\textbf{v}}})=0.
Since
U\sigma_{{\textbf{v}}}U^{\dagger}=\sum_{{\textbf{u}}}P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}},
thus we complete the proof.

\blacksquare

In the following, we give a simple but useful connection between the unitary-stabilizer nullity and the subgroup associated with
U
. Recall that
s(U)
is the number of
\pm 1
s in the unitary Pauli function, as in Definition
5
.
In the following lemma, we prove that
s(U)
equals to the size of
{\cal P}_{U}
, that is the intersection of
U{\cal P}_{n}U^{\dagger}
and
{\cal P}_{n}
.
Note that
{\cal P}_{n}
is the quotient Pauli group rather than the Pauli group
\hat{{\cal P}_{n}}
.

Lemma 3

For any
n
-qubit unitary
U
,
{\cal P}_{U}
is a subgroup of
{\cal P}_{n}
. What is more,

\displaystyle|{\cal P}_{U}|=|{\cal P}_{U^{\dagger}}|=s(U).

(11)

Proof.

By Lemma
2
, we know every
\pm 1
in the Pauli transfer matrix will imply a distinct element in
{\cal P}_{U}
. That is if
P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}_{{{\textbf{v}}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})=\pm 1
, then
\sigma_{{{\textbf{u}}}_{{{\textbf{v}}}}}\in{\cal P}_{U}
. Besides, for different
v
, such
{{\textbf{u}}}_{{{\textbf{v}}}}
are different, this comes from the fact that the Pauli transfer matrix is orthogonal, thus any row cannot have two
\pm 1
s. Thus

|{\cal P}_{U}|\geq|s(U)|
. On the other hand, for every element in
{\cal P}_{U}
, that is a
\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}
such that
U\sigma_{{\textbf{v}}}U^{\dagger}=\pm\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}
for some
v
, we would have

|P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})|=1
, thus
|{\cal P}_{U}|\leq|s(U)|
.
Thus
|P_{U}|=s(U)
. Besides, we have
\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}U\sigma_{{\textbf{v}}}U^{\dagger})=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{{\textbf{v}}}U^{\dagger}\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}U)
,
thus the Pauli transfer matrix of
U^{\dagger}
is the transpose of the Pauli transfer matrix of
U
. Thus

|{\cal P}_{U^{\dagger}}|=|s(U)|=|{\cal P}_{U}|
.

\blacksquare

An interesting corollary is that the unitary-stabilizer nullity is always an integer, although we do not utilize this property in the following sections.

Corollary 4

For any
n
-qubit unitary
U
,
v(U)
must be an integer, or equivalently,
s(U)=2^{k}
for some non-negative integer
k
.

By Lemma
3
, we know
s(U)=|{\cal P}_{U}|
.
Since
{\cal P}_{U}
is a subgroup of
{\cal P}_{n}
, where
|{\cal P}_{n}|=4^{n}
, by group theory, we know that
s(U)|4^{n}
.
Thus
v(U)=2n-\log_{2}s(U)

is always an integer.

III.2
Basic properties of the unitary-stabilizer nullity

In this section, we prove the basic properties of the unitary-stabilizer nullity, that is the faithfulness, invariance under Clifford operations,
additivity under tensor product and subadditivity under composition.

Theorem 5
(Faithfulness)

Let
U
be an
n
-qubit unitary. Then
v(U)\geq 0
. Furthermore,
v(U)=0
if and only if
U
is a Clifford unitary. Or equivalently,
s(U)\leq 4^{n}
and
s(U)=4^{n}
if and only if
U
is a Clifford unitary.

Proof.

For any
n
-qubit unitary
U
, we firstly prove
v(U)\geq 0
, or equivalently,
s(U)\leq 4^{n}
. By Lemma
2
, if there exist
{{\textbf{u}}},{{\textbf{v}}}
such that
P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})=1
then for any
{{\textbf{u}}}^{\prime}\neq{{\textbf{u}}},{\cal P}_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}^{\prime}|{{\textbf{v}}})=0
. Thus every
\pm 1
in the Pauli function must come together with
4^{n}-1
number of
0s
. Thus
s(U)\leq 4^{n}
.

If
s(U)=4^{n}
, by Lemma
2
, for every Pauli operator
\sigma_{{\textbf{v}}}
,
U\sigma_{{\textbf{v}}}U^{\dagger}
is a Pauli operator, thus
U
is a Clifford unitary. On the other hand, if
U
is a Clifford operator, by Lemma
1
, it is easy to verify
s(U)=4^{n}.

\blacksquare

Theorem 6
(Invariant under Clifford operation)

For an
n
-qubit unitary
U
and an
n
-qubit Clifford unitary
V
,

\displaystyle v(VU)=v(U),\quad s(VU)=s(U).

(12)

Similarly, it also holds that
v(UV)=v(U),s(UV)=s(U)
.

Proof.

Recall that the unitary Pauli function

\displaystyle P_{VU}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}VU\sigma_{{\textbf{v}}}U^{\dagger}V^{\dagger}/2^{n}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}V^{\dagger}\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}VU\sigma_{{\textbf{v}}}U^{\dagger}/2^{n}.

(13)

Since
V
is Clifford, then
V^{\dagger}(\cdot)V
is an isomorphism over the Pauli group, i.e.
V^{\dagger}\hat{{\cal P}}_{n}V=\hat{{\cal P}}_{n}
. Further, since
V^{\dagger}\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}V
does not change the eigenvalue of
\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}
, where is
\pm 1
. Thus

\displaystyle V^{\dagger}\{\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{u}}}\in 4^{[n]}\}V=\{p_{{\textbf{u}}}\sigma_{{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{u}}}\in 4^{[n]},p_{{\textbf{u}}}=\pm 1\}.

(14)

In other words, the set of unitary Pauli functions satisfies

\displaystyle\{P_{VU}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})\}=\{p_{{\textbf{u}}}P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})\,|\,p_{{\textbf{u}}}=\pm 1\}.

(15)

Thus
s(VU)=s(U)
, thus
v(VU)=s(U)
. Similarly, it holds that
v(VU)=v(U),s(VU)=s(U)
.

\blacksquare

Theorem 7
(Additivity under tensor product)

Given
n
-qubit unitary
U
and
m
-qubit unitary
V
, the following holds

\displaystyle v(U\otimes V)=v(U)+v(V).

(16)

Proof.

For simplicity, given
{{\textbf{u}}}\in 4^{[n+m]}
, we write
{{\textbf{u}}}^{(1)}
as the first
n
bits of
u
, i.e.
{{\textbf{u}}}^{(1)}={{\textbf{u}}}_{1}{{\textbf{u}}}_{2}...{{\textbf{u}}}_{n}
. We write
{{\textbf{u}}}^{(2)}
as the last
m
bits of
u
, i.e.
{{\textbf{u}}}^{(2)}={{\textbf{u}}}_{n+1}{{\textbf{u}}}_{n+2}...{{\textbf{u}}}_{n+m}
.
It suffices to notice that

\displaystyle P_{U\otimes V}({{\textbf{u}}}|{{\textbf{v}}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_{{{\textbf{u}}}^{(1)}}\otimes\sigma_{{{\textbf{u}}}^{(2)}}\left(U\otimes V\right)\sigma_{{{\textbf{v}}}^{(1)}}\otimes\sigma_{{{\textbf{v}}}^{(2)}}\left(U^{\dagger}\otimes V^{\dagger}\right)/2^{(n+m)}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_{{{\textbf{u}}}^{(1)}}U\sigma_{{{\textbf{v}}}^{(1)}}U^{\dagger}/2^{n}\cdot\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_{{{\textbf{u}}}^{(2)}}V\sigma_{{{\textbf{v}}}^{(2)}}V^{\dagger}/2^{m}

\displaystyle=P_{U}({{\textbf{u}}}^{(1)}|{{\textbf{v}}}^{(1)})\cdot P_{V}({{\textbf{u}}}^{(2)}|{{\textbf{v}}}^{(2)}).

(17)

Thus we have

s(U\otimes V)=s(U)s(V)
,

2(n+m)-\log_{2}s(U\otimes V)=(2n-\log_{2}s(U))+(2m-\log_{2}s(V))
,
and then
v(U\otimes V)=v(U)+v(V)
.

\blacksquare

Lemma 8

For any
n
-qubit unitary
U
and
V
,

\displaystyle|{\cal P}_{U^{\dagger}}\cap{\cal P}_{V}|\leq s(UV).

(18)

Recall that

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{UV}

\displaystyle=UV{\cal P}_{n}V^{\dagger}U^{\dagger}\cap{\cal P}_{n}

\displaystyle=U\left(V{\cal P}_{n}V^{\dagger}\cap U^{\dagger}{\cal P}_{n}U\right)U^{\dagger}.

(19)

Noting that
U(\cdot)U^{\dagger}
is an injection, we have

\displaystyle|{\cal P}_{UV}|

\displaystyle=|V{\cal P}_{n}V^{\dagger}\cap U^{\dagger}{\cal P}_{n}U|

\displaystyle\geq|V{\cal P}_{n}V^{\dagger}\cap U^{\dagger}{\cal P}_{n}U\cap{\cal P}_{n}|

\displaystyle\geq|{\cal P}_{V}\cap{\cal P}_{U^{\dagger}}|.

(20)

By Lemma
3
we know
|{\cal P}_{UV}|=s(UV)
, we conclude

|{\cal P}_{U^{\dagger}}\cap{\cal P}_{V}|\leq s(UV)
.

The following lemma is a variation of Lagrange’s theorem in group theory. It connects the sizes between subgroups and their intersections. A detailed proof is provided in Appendix.
C.4
.

Lemma 9

Suppose
A,B
are subgroups of
H
, then

|A\times B|=\frac{|A|\cdot|B|}{|A\cap B|}
.
Since
A\times B\subseteq H
we have

|A|\cdot|B|\leq|H|\cdot|A\cap B|
.

Utilizing Lemma
9
, we finally arrive at the statement that the unitary-stabilizer nullity satisfies the subadditivity under composition.

Theorem 10
(Subadditivity under composition)

Given
n
-qubit unitary
U
and
V
, the following holds

\displaystyle v(UV)\leq v(U)+v(V).

(21)

Or equivalently

s(U)s(V)\leq 4^{n}s(UV)
.

Proof.

Let
H={\cal P}_{n},A={\cal P}_{U^{\dagger}},B={\cal P}_{V}
, we know
A,B
are subgroups of
H
. By Lemma
9
we have

\displaystyle|{\cal P}_{U^{\dagger}}|\cdot|{\cal P}_{V}|\leq|{\cal P}_{U^{\dagger}}\cap{\cal P}_{V}|\cdot 4^{n}.

(22)

Combine with Lemma
8
, we have

\displaystyle|{\cal P}_{U^{\dagger}}|\cdot|{\cal P}_{V}|\leq|s(UV)|\cdot 4^{n}.

(23)

Further by Lemma
3
, we know

\displaystyle|{\cal P}_{V}|=s(V),|{\cal P}_{U^{\dagger}}|=|{\cal P}_{U}|=s(U).

(24)

thus finally we get

\displaystyle s(U)s(V)\leq 4^{n}s(UV).

(25)

\blacksquare

By the additivity under tensor product, i.e. Theorem

\displaystyle v(T\otimes I_{2}\otimes I_{2}...\otimes I_{2})=v(T)+0=1.

From Theorem

0

Suppose

\displaystyle v(U)

\displaystyle\leq 0+t(U)\cdot v(T)

\displaystyle=t(U).

V
Conclusion

Resource theory for magic monotones has wide applications in quantum information. In this work, we introduce a magic monotone for unitaries, and use it to lower bound the
T
count in the circuit synthesis task. This lower bound quantifies the minimum magic resources required for the computation, and lower bounds the runtime of classical simulation algorithms based on the Clifford+
T
gate set. We also give numerical results which show that our magic monotone can give better
T
count lower bounds for some gates when compared with other magic monotones.

Specifically,
in this paper, for any
n
-qubit unitary
U
, we introduce its unitary-stabilizer nullity as
v(U)=2n-s(U)
, where
s(U)
is the number of
\pm 1s
in the Pauli transfer matrix. We prove that the unitary-stabilizer nullity satisfies several desirable properties, and in particular establish that it is a lower bound for the
T
count of
U
. We show that the lower bound obtained by the unitary-stabilizer nullity never performs worse than the state-stabilizer nullity. We also give an
n
-qubit unitary family of unitary-stabilizer nullity
2n
, providing examples where the unitary-stabilizer nullity strictly outperforms. For the diagonal unitaries, we prove the lower bounds obtained by the state- and the unitary-stabilizer nullity are the same. Besides, we connect the unitary-stabilizer nullity and the state-stabilizer nullity with auxiliary systems. Moreover, the results in this work may shed lights for the study of resource theories of quantum dynamics (e.g., Refs.
Díaz et al. 2018
;
Wang and Wilde 2019
;
Rosset et al. 2018
;
Li et al. 2020
;
Regula and Takagi 2021
;
Liu and Winter 2019
;
Wang and Wilde 2023
).

Meaningful magic monotones with desirable properties are very useful to exploit the power of nonstabilizer resources in
quantum computation. Our work has, in particular, shown the applications of unitary-stabilizer nullity in estimating the
T
count, which is central for resource estimation for fault-tolerant computation
Bravyi and Kitaev 2005
, performance analysis for classical-simulation algorithms
Seddon et al. 2021
;
Bravyi and Gosset 2016
;
Bravyi et al. 2016
,
and even for the complexity of the
parameterized Quantum Merlin-Arthur problem
(QMA problem)
Arunachalam et al. 2022
.
There are many other applications beyond. For one thing, we could apply the unitary-stabilizer nullity to benchmark the gate synthesis task under stabilizer operations
Welch et al. 2014
;
Heyfron and Campbell 2018
;
Ross and Selinger 2016
. Using the result in Theorem 13, the benchmark method can be generalized for any non-Clifford gates. For another, one might use the unitary-stabilizer nullity to quantify the nonstabilizerness of quantum circuits
Oliviero et al. 2022
;
Haug and Kim 2022
on near-term quantum devices, as we could extract the unitary Pauli function on the quantum hardware, via firstly measuring the states

Protocols for evaluating the nonstabilizerness experimentally

Beyond this work, there are still various interesting open questions

C.2

In this section, we show that for any

(70)

Thus the state-stabilizer nullity does not satisfy the subadditivity under composition.
In particular, suppose

(71)

Set

\displaystyle U=e^{iX}HC^{-1},V=CHC^{-1}.

(72)

Since

is an eigenstate of

\displaystyle v_{s}(U|\psi\rangle)=v_{s}(e^{iX}|+\rangle)=0.

n
-qubit case.

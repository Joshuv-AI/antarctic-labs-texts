A fully nonlinear locally constrained anisotropic curvature flow
Yong Wei, Changwei Xiong

Abstract
. Given a smooth positive function F ∈ C ∞ ​ ( 𝕊 n ) F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n}) such that the square of its positive 1 1 -homogeneous extension on ℝ n + 1 ∖ { 0 } \mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\} is uniformly convex, the Wulff shape W F W_{F} is a smooth uniformly convex body in the Euclidean space ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} with F F being the support function of the boundary ∂ W F \partial W_{F} . In this paper, we introduce the fully nonlinear locally constrained anisotropic curvature flow ∂ ∂ t X = ( 1 − E k 1 / k σ F ) ν F , k = 2 , ⋯ , n \frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=(1-E_{k}^{1/k}\sigma_{F})\nu_{F},\quad k=2,\cdots,n in the Euclidean space, where E k E_{k} denotes the normalized k k th anisotropic mean curvature with respect to the Wulff shape W F W_{F} , σ F \sigma_{F} the anisotropic support function and ν F \nu_{F} the outward anisotropic unit normal of the evolving hypersurface. We show that starting from a smooth, closed and strictly convex hypersurface in ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} ( n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 ), the smooth solution of the flow exists for all positive time and converges smoothly and exponentially to a scaled Wulff shape. A nice feature of this flow is that it improves a certain isoperimetric ratio. Therefore by the smooth convergence of the above flow, we provide a new proof of a class of the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequalities for anisotropic mixed volumes of smooth convex domains in the Euclidean space.

1.
Introduction

Given a smooth positive function
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
such that the square of its positive
1
-homogeneous extension on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}
is uniformly convex, the Wulff shape
W_{F}
is defined as the set

W_{F}=\bigcap_{\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:x\cdot\nu\leq F(\nu)\right\},

which uniquely solves the isoperimetric problem for the interface energy functional (or called the anisotropic perimeter)

\mathcal{E}(K)=\int_{\partial^{*}K}F(\nu)d\mathcal{H}^{n}

among sets of finite perimeter and of given volume. Here
\partial^{*}K
denotes the reduced boundary of
K
and
\nu
denotes the outward unit normal vector field. See
[
5
,
7
,
18
]
and
[
6
]
. The function
F
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is called the support function of the Wulff shape
W_{F}
. The assumption on
F
ensures that
W_{F}
is a smooth uniformly convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and its boundary
\Sigma_{F}=\partial W_{F}
is also called the Wulff shape determined by the support function
F
. When
F\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, the Wulff shape is just the unit round sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

The Wulff shape plays the similar role in the anisotropic geometry (also called the relative or Minkowski differential geometry) as the one the unit sphere plays in the Euclidean geometry; see
[
4
]
and references therein. In fact, the Wulff shape allows us to define the anisotropic normal of a smooth hypersurface
M^{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by a map
\nu_{F}:\ M\to\Sigma_{F}
, which takes each point
x\in M
to the point in
\Sigma_{F}
with the same oriented tangent plane. The anisotropic Weingarten map
{\mathcal{W}}_{F}
is the derivative of the anisotropic normal
\nu_{F}
, which is a linear map from
T_{x}M
to itself at each point. The eigenvalues
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
of
{\mathcal{W}}_{F}
are called the anisotropic principal curvatures. We define the
k
th anisotropic mean curvature of
M
as the normalized
k
th elementary symmetric function
E_{k}
of the anisotropic principal curvatures
\kappa
:

{E}_{k}=E_{k}(\kappa)={\binom{n}{k}}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\kappa_{i_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{k}},\quad k=1,\cdots,n.

In particular,
H_{F}=nE_{1}(\kappa)
denotes the anisotropic mean curvature of
M
. On the other hand, we can define the anisotropic support function
\sigma_{F}:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by the decomposition

X=\sigma_{F}\nu_{F}+V^{i}\partial_{i}X,\quad V^{i}\partial_{i}X\in T_{x}M

of the position vector
X
. If we denote by
\nu
the outward unit normal of
M
in the Euclidean geometry, the anisotropic support function
\sigma_{F}
is related to the isotropic support function
\sigma=\langle X,\nu\rangle
by the equation
\sigma_{F}=\sigma/{F}(\nu)
.

In
[
19
]
, we introduced a volume-preserving anisotropic mean curvature flow
X:M\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1},n\geq 2
of star-shaped hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
which satisfies

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}X=(n-H_{F}\sigma_{F})\nu_{F},\\
X(\cdot,0)=X_{0}(\cdot).\end{cases}

This flow was inspired by the isotropic one introduced earlier by Guan and Li
[
10
]
. Denote by
M_{t}=X(M,t)
the evolving hypersurface and by
\Omega_{t}
the domain enclosed by
M_{t}
. By the anisotropic Minkowski identity (
2.16
), the volume of
\Omega_{t}
along the flow (
1.1
) remains a constant

\frac{d}{dt}|\Omega_{t}|~=~\int_{M_{t}}(n-H_{F}\sigma_{F})d\mu_{F}=0,

where
d\mu_{F}=F(\nu)d\mu
is the anisotropic area form on
M
determined by the anisotropy
F
. We proved that the flow (
1.1
) starting from any star-shaped and closed hypersurface exists for all time
t\in[0,\infty)
and converges smoothly to a scaled Wulff shape. As an application, we provided a new proof of a class of the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequalities for smooth convex domains in the Euclidean space.

The flow (
1.1
) is quasi-linear as the speed involves only the anisotropic mean curvature and the anisotropic support function. In this paper, we introduce a fully nonlinear locally constrained anisotropic curvature flow:

(1.2)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=(1-E_{k}^{1/k}(\kappa)\sigma_{F})\nu_{F},\quad k=2,\cdots,n.

When
k=1
, the flow (
1.2
) reduces to the flow (
1.1
) up to a constant
n
which is volume-preserving. However, for
k\geq 2
the flow (
1.2
) does not preserve the enclosed volume or any mixed volume generally. Recall that the mixed volumes of a smooth convex domain
\Omega
relative to the Wulff shape
W_{F}
can be expressed as (see §
2.4
)

(1.3)

V_{n-k}(\Omega,W_{F})=\int_{M}E_{k}(\kappa)d\mu_{F},\quad k=0,\cdots,n-1,

and
V_{n+1}(\Omega,W_{F})=(n+1)|\Omega|
,
V_{0}(\Omega,W_{F})=(n+1)|W_{F}|
. Define the following higher order anisotropic isoperimetric ratio

(1.4)

\mathcal{I}_{k}(\Omega,W_{F})=\frac{V_{n+2-k}(\Omega,W_{F})}{V_{n+1}(\Omega,W_{F})^{\frac{n+2-k}{n+1}}},\quad k=2,\cdots,n.

We show that
\mathcal{I}_{k}(\Omega_{t},W_{F})
is monotone non-increasing along the flow (
1.2
). This is a motivation for us to introduce the flow (
1.2
).

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma_{F}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1},n\geq 2,
be a given smooth, closed and strictly convex hypersurface enclosing the origin with the support function
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. Let
M_{0}=\partial\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth, closed and strictly convex hypersurface with the origin contained in
\Omega_{0}
. Then the flow (
1.2
) starting from
M_{0}
has a unique smooth solution for all time
t\in[0,\infty)
and the evolving hypersurface
M_{t}
converges smoothly and exponentially to
\bar{r}\Sigma_{F}
for some
\bar{r}>0
as
t\to\infty
.

Since the initial hypersurface
M_{0}
is strictly convex and encloses the origin, we know that
M_{0}
is star-shaped with respect to the origin. This is equivalent to that the support function
\sigma=\langle X,\nu\rangle
is positive everywhere on
M_{0}
, where
X
and
\nu
denote the position vector and the outward unit normal of
M_{0}
respectively. It follows that the anisotropic support function
\sigma_{F}=\sigma/{F(\nu)}
is positive on
M_{0}
as well. The proof of Theorem
1.1
consists of the following steps. First, we apply the maximum principle to the evolution equation of
\sigma_{F}
to show that
\sigma_{F}
is bounded from below by a positive constant along the flow (
1.2
), which means that the evolving hypersurface
M_{t}
remains to be star-shaped with respect to the origin for positive time
t>0
. Then we can write
M_{t}
as a graph of the radial function
\rho
over the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and the flow (
1.2
) is equivalent to a fully nonlinear parabolic equation of the function
\gamma=\log\rho
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The short time existence of the flow (
1.2
) follows immediately. To derive the a priori estimates of the solution
M_{t}
, we first apply the comparison principle to the equation (
1.2
) to obtain the
C^{0}
estimate. Then the uniform positive lower bound of the anisotropic support function
\sigma_{F}
together with the
C^{0}
estimate implies the a priori
C^{1}
estimate for the solution.

Since the flow (
1.2
) is fully nonlinear, we still need the
C^{2}
estimate in order to get the higher order regularity estimate. For this purpose, we employ the anisotropic Gauss map parametrization which we will review in §
5
. As we already have the
C^{1}
estimate, the
C^{2}
estimate is equivalent to the bounds on the anisotropic principal curvatures. Equivalently, it suffices to estimate the upper bound on the anisotropic principal radii of curvature
\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{n}
, which are the eigenvalues of the following
(0,2)
-tensor on the Wulff shape
\Sigma_{F}
,

(1.5)

\tau_{ij}[s]=\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}s+\bar{g}_{ij}s-\frac{1}{2}Q_{ijk}\bar{\nabla}_{k}s.

Here
s
is the anisotropic support function considered as a function on the Wulff shape
\Sigma_{F}
which coincides with
\sigma_{F}
introduced above. See Section
5
for the detail. Section
6
is devoted to proving the upper bound on
\tau_{i}
. The method is to apply the maximum principle to the evolution equation of
\tau_{ij}[s]
together with an auxiliary function involving the anisotropic support function
s
and its gradient. This is the most technical part in the proof, where an observation on smooth symmetric functions due to Guan, Shi and Sui
[
11
]
will be used.

Once the
C^{2}
estimate is obtained, the higher order regularity estimate of the solution follows from the standard parabolic theory. This implies the long time existence of the smooth solution. The smooth convergence to a scaled Wulff shape follows from the improving of the isoperimetric ratio
\mathcal{I}_{k}(\Omega,W_{F})
. In
[
10
]
, the exponential convergence of the flow (
1.1
) is based on the exponential decay of gradient
|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}\gamma|
of the radial function, which is an immediate consequence of the evolution of
|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}\gamma|
and the long time existence of the flow (
1.1
). In the anisotropic case, the evolution of
|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}\gamma|
does not behave well. Instead, we will prove the exponential convergence by studying the linearization of the flow (
1.2
) around the Wulff shape. The monotonicity of the volume
V_{n+1}(\Omega,W_{F})
and mixed volume
V_{n+2-k}(\Omega,W_{F})
will be used crucially to derive a faster decay of the average integral of anisotropic support function. With the help of a special class of Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequalities, we obtain the exponential convergence of the flow with the decay rate determined by the first non-zero eigenvalue of the self-adjoint operator (
9.2
) on the Wulff shape.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we provide a new proof of the following Alexandrov–Fenchel inequalities.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma_{F}=\partial W_{F}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1},n\geq 2,
be a smooth, closed and strictly convex hypersurface enclosing the origin with the support function
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. For any smooth, closed and strictly convex hypersurface
M
which encloses a bounded domain
\Omega
, we have

(1.6)

\int_{M}E_{k}(\kappa)d\mu_{F}~\geq~(n+1)|\Omega|^{\frac{n-k}{n+1}}|W_{F}|^{\frac{k+1}{n+1}},\quad k=0,\dots,n-2,

with the equality if and only if
M
is a scaled Wulff shape. Here
|\Omega|
and
|W_{F}|
are the volume of
\Omega
and the Wulff shape
W_{F}
respectively,
E_{k}(\kappa)
denotes the
k
th normalized anisotropic mean curvature of
M
, and
d\mu_{F}
the anisotropic area element.

E_{k}(\kappa)

will be given in §
7
. Finally, we complete the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
in the §
8

9
. Throughout the paper the Einstein convention on the summation for indices is used unless otherwise stated.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review some preliminaries of the anisotropic geometry, the mixed volumes and the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequalities.

2.1.
The Wulff shape

Let
F
be a smooth positive function on the standard sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that the matrix

(2.1)

A_{F}(x)~=~\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}{F}(x)+F(x)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}},\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}

is positive definite on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}
denotes the covariant derivative on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then there exists a unique smooth strictly convex hypersurface
\Sigma_{F}
given by

\Sigma_{F}=\{\phi(x)|\phi(x):=F(x)x+\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}{F}(x),~x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}

whose support function is given by
F
. Denote the closure of the enclosed domain of
\Sigma_{F}
by
W_{F}
. We call
W_{F}
(and
\Sigma_{F}
) the Wulff shape determined by the function
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. When
F
is a constant, the Wulff shape is just a round sphere.

The smooth function
F
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
can be extended homogeneously to a
1
-homogeneous function on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by

F(x)=|x|F({x}/{|x|}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}

and setting
F(0)=0
. Then it is easy to show that
\phi(x)=DF(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
D
denotes the gradient on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The homogeneous extension
F
defines a Minkowski norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, that is,
F
is a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
D^{2}(F^{2})
is uniformly positive definite on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}
. We can define a dual Minkowski norm
F^{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by

(2.2)

F^{0}(z):=\sup_{x\neq 0}\frac{\langle x,z\rangle}{{F}(x)},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

Then the Wulff shape
W_{F}
can be written as

W_{F}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:{F}^{0}(z)\leq 1\},

and
\Sigma_{F}=\partial W_{F}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:F^{0}(z)=1\}
.

2.2.
Anisotropic curvatures

Let
M
be a smooth hypersurface in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with a unit normal vector field
\nu
, and
\Sigma_{F}
be the Wulff shape defined in §
2.1
. We define the anisotropic unit normal of
M
as the map
\nu_{F}:M\to\Sigma_{F}
given by

\nu_{F}(x)~=~\phi(\nu(x))={F}(\nu(x))\nu(x)+\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}{F}(\nu(x))~\in~\Sigma_{F}

for any point
x\in M
. It follows from the
1
-homogeneity of
F
that
\nu_{F}(x)=DF(\nu(x))
. The anisotropic Weingarten map is a linear map

\mathcal{W}_{F}=d\nu_{F}:T_{x}M\to T_{\nu_{F}(x)}\Sigma_{F}.

Note that
\mathcal{W}_{F}=A_{F}\circ\mathcal{W}
, where
\mathcal{W}=d\nu=(h_{i}^{j})
is the Weingarten map of the hypersurface
M
, and that the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{W}
are the (isotropic) principal curvatures
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
. The eigenvalues of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
are called the anisotropic principal curvatures of
M
, and we denote them by
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
. If we write
\mathcal{W}_{F}=(\hat{h}_{i}^{j})
in local coordinates, then

(2.3)

\hat{h}_{i}^{j}(x)=(A_{F}(\nu(x)))_{i}^{k}h_{k}^{j}(x).

In particular, it follows from (
2.3
) that
E_{n}(\kappa)=\det(A_{F}(\nu))E_{n}(\lambda)
.

There is another formulation of the anisotropic curvatures, which was introduced by Ben Andrews in
[
1
]
and reformulated by Chao Xia in
[
20
]
. The idea is to define a new metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
using the second derivatives of the dual function
{F}^{0}
of the homogeneous extension of
F
. This new metric on the
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is defined as

(2.4)

G(z)(U,V):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n+1}\frac{\partial^{2}({F}^{0})^{2}(z)}{\partial z^{i}\partial z^{j}}U^{i}V^{j}

for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}
and
U,V\in T_{z}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The third derivatives of
F^{0}
give a
(0,3)
-tensor

(2.5)

Q(z)(U,V,Y):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n+1}\frac{\partial^{3}({F}^{0})^{2}(z)}{\partial z^{i}\partial z^{j}\partial z^{k}}U^{i}V^{j}Y^{k}

for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}
and
U,V,Y\in T_{z}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The
1
-homogeneity of
F^{0}
implies that

\displaystyle G(z)(z,z)=

\displaystyle 1,\quad G(z)(z,V)=0,\quad\mathrm{for}\quad z\in\Sigma_{F},~\mathrm{and}~V\in T_{z}\Sigma_{F},

(2.6)

\displaystyle Q(z)(z,U,V)=

\displaystyle 0,\quad\mathrm{for}\quad z\in\Sigma_{F},~\mathrm{and}~U,V\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

For a smooth hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the anisotropic normal
\nu_{F}(x)
lies in
\Sigma_{F}
. Then

\displaystyle G(\nu_{F})(\nu_{F},\nu_{F})=

\displaystyle 1,\quad G(\nu_{F})(\nu_{F},U)=0,\quad\mathrm{for}~U\in T_{x}M,

\displaystyle Q(\nu_{F})(\nu_{F},U,V)=

\displaystyle 0,\quad\mathrm{for}~U,V\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

Thus
\nu_{F}(x)
is perpendicular to
T_{x}M
with respect to the metric
G(\nu_{F}(x))
. This induces a Riemannian metric
\hat{g}
on
M
from
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},G)
by

(2.7)

\hat{g}(x):=G(\nu_{F}(x))|_{T_{x}M},\quad x\in M.

Then we can state the anisotropic Gauss and Weingarten formulas for
(M,\hat{g})\subset(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},G)
:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\partial_{j}X=

\displaystyle-\hat{h}_{ij}\nu_{F}+\hat{\nabla}_{\partial_{i}}\partial_{j}+A_{ijk}\hat{g}^{k\ell}\partial_{\ell}X,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\nu_{F}=

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{i}^{k}\partial_{k}X,

where
X
is the position vector of
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
\hat{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\hat{g}
,
\hat{h}_{ij}
stands for the second fundamental form of
(M,\hat{g})\subset(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},G)
,
and the anisotropic Weingarten map relates to
\hat{h}_{ij}
by
\mathcal{W}_{F}=(\hat{h}_{i}^{j})=(\sum_{k}\hat{g}^{jk}\hat{h}_{ik})
. We also have the anisotropic analogue of the Gauss equation and the Codazzi equation:

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{ijk\ell}~=

\displaystyle~\hat{h}_{ik}\hat{h}_{j\ell}-\hat{h}_{i\ell}\hat{h}_{jk}+\hat{\nabla}_{\ell}A_{jki}-\hat{\nabla}_{k}A_{j\ell i}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{g}^{pm}A_{jkp}A_{m\ell i}-\hat{g}^{pm}A_{j\ell p}A_{mki},

(2.11)

\hat{\nabla}_{k}\hat{h}_{ij}+\hat{h}_{j}^{\ell}{A}_{\ell ki}~=~\hat{\nabla}_{j}\hat{h}_{ik}+\hat{h}_{k}^{\ell}{A}_{\ell ji},

where
\hat{R}
is the Riemannian curvature tensor of
\hat{g}
, and
A
is a
(0,3)
-tensor

(2.12)

{A}_{ijk}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{h}_{i}^{\ell}Q_{jk\ell}+\hat{h}_{j}^{\ell}Q_{i\ell k}-\hat{h}_{k}^{\ell}Q_{ij\ell}\right),

with
Q_{ijk}=Q(\nu_{F})(\partial_{i}X,\partial_{j}X,\partial_{k}X)
. Note that by the definition (
2.5
),
Q
is totally symmetric in all three indices. Hence the tensor
{A}_{ijk}
is symmetric for the first two indices.

When the hypersurface
M
is the Wulff shape
\Sigma_{F}
, the anisotropic normal
\nu_{F}
is just the position vector, and the anisotropic principal curvatures are all equal to
1
. Then
A_{ijk}=-Q_{ijk}/2
which is symmetric in all indices. We use the notations
\bar{g}
,
\bar{\nabla}
and
\bar{R}
to denote the induced metric on the Wulff shape
\Sigma_{F}
from
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},G)
, its Levi-Civita connection and curvature tensor respectively. Then
\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}Q_{ijk}
is totally symmetric in four indices (Prop. 2.2 in
[
22
]
). In this case, the Gauss equation (
2.2
) is simplified as

(2.13)

\bar{R}_{ijk\ell}=\bar{g}_{ik}\bar{g}_{j\ell}-\bar{g}_{i\ell}\bar{g}_{jk}+\frac{1}{4}\bar{g}^{pq}Q_{i\ell p}Q_{qjk}-\frac{1}{4}\bar{g}^{pq}Q_{ikp}Q_{j\ell q}.

2.3.
Anisotropic support function

Recall that the anisotropic support function
\sigma_{F}:{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by the following decomposition

X=\sigma_{F}\nu_{F}+V^{i}\partial_{i}X,

where
V^{i}\partial_{i}X
is the tangential part of the position vector
X
along the hypersurface, and so

(2.14)

\sigma_{F}=G(\nu_{F})(X,\nu_{F}).

The anisotropic support function
\sigma_{F}
is related to the Euclidean support function
\sigma=\langle X,\nu\rangle
by

\sigma_{F}={\sigma}/{{F}(\nu)}.

For any smooth hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we define the anisotropic area functional as

|M|_{F}:=\int_{M}F(\nu)d\mu,

where
d\mu
is the area form of the induced metric on
M
from the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. We set
d\mu_{F}=F(\nu)d\mu
and call it the anisotropic area form of
M
.

We recall the following generalized Minkowski identities.

Proposition 2.1

(see
[
12
]
)
.

Let
M=\partial\Omega
be a smooth closed hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. We have the following generalized Minkowski identities:

(2.15)

\int_{M}\sigma_{F}d\mu_{F}~=~(n+1)|\Omega|,

and

(2.16)

\int_{M}E_{k+1}(\kappa)\sigma_{F}d\mu_{F}~=~\int_{M}E_{k}(\kappa)d\mu_{F}

for
k=0,1,\cdots,n-1
, where
E_{k}(\kappa)
denotes the
k
th anisotropic mean curvature of
M
.

2.4.
Mixed volumes and Alexandrov–Fenchel inequalities

Let
\Omega_{1},\cdots,\Omega_{n+1}
be convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The Minkowski sum
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\epsilon_{i}\Omega_{i}
is the set of points in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\epsilon_{i}x_{i}
, where
x_{i}\in\Omega_{i}
and
\epsilon_{i}
are real numbers. The volume of this Minkowski sum is a polynomial of degree
n+1
in
\epsilon_{i}
, and its coefficients are called
mixed volumes
:

V[\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\cdots,\Omega_{n+1}]=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{\partial^{n+1}}{\partial\epsilon_{1}\cdots\partial\epsilon_{n+1}}\mathrm{Vol}(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\epsilon_{i}\Omega_{i}).

The mixed volumes satisfy a quadratic inequality called the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality:

(2.17)

V[\Omega_{1},\Omega_{1},\Omega_{3},\cdots,\Omega_{n+1}]V[\Omega_{2},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3},\cdots,\Omega_{n+1}]\leq V[\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3},\cdots,\Omega_{n+1}]^{2}

for any convex bodies
\Omega_{1},\cdots,\Omega_{n+1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
See Schneider’s book
[
16
, §7.3]
for a detailed introduction. The general inequality (
2.17
) has many important special cases, one of which is reviewed as follows. Given a fixed strictly convex body
W_{F}
, define

V_{k}(\Omega,W_{F})=V[\underbrace{W_{F},\cdots,W_{F}}_{n+1-k},\underbrace{\Omega,\cdots,\Omega}_{k}].

Then
V_{n+1}(\Omega,W_{F})=(n+1)\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega)
and
V_{0}(\Omega,W_{F})=(n+1)\mathrm{Vol}(W_{F})
. The inequality (
2.17
) implies the following inequalities:

(2.18)

V_{j}^{k-i}(\Omega,W_{F})~\geq~V_{i}^{k-j}(\Omega,W_{F})V_{k}^{j-i}(\Omega,W_{F})

for any convex body
\Omega
and any integers
i,j,k
with
0\leq i<j<k\leq n+1
, which generalize the classical isoperimetric inequality. If
\partial\Omega
is in class
C^{2}
, the mixed volumes
V_{k}(\Omega,W_{F})
are related to the following boundary anisotropic curvature integrals:

V_{n-k}(\Omega,W_{F})=\int_{\partial\Omega}E_{k}(\kappa)d\mu_{F},\quad k=0,\cdots,n,

where
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
are the anisotropic principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega
with respect to the Wulff shape
W_{F}
. In the case that
W_{F}
is the unit Euclidean ball
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
, we denote the isotropic mixed volumes by
V_{k}(\Omega)=V_{k}(\Omega,\mathbb{B}^{n+1})
for simplicity of the notation. When
\partial\Omega
is in class
C^{2}
,

V_{n-k}(\Omega)=\int_{\partial\Omega}E_{k}(\lambda)d\mu,\quad k=0,\cdots,n,

where
\lambda
are the (isotropic) principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega
.

It is interesting to provide new proofs of the inequality (
2.18
) (and the more general one (
2.17
)) by using geometric flows, and to prove them for a broader class of domains. Several progress has been made in this direction. For example, McCoy
[
14
]
used the mixed volume preserving flow to give a new proof of (
2.18
) for the case
W_{F}=\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
,
i=0
and
\partial\Omega
is smooth and convex. Schulze
[
17
]
applied the flow by powers of the mean curvature to reprove the isoperimetric inequality for bounded domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(
n\leq 7
). Guan and Li
[
9
]
used the inverse curvature flow to prove (
2.18
) for the case
W_{F}=\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
,
i=0
and
\partial\Omega
is smooth,
(n-j)
-convex and star-shaped. For the general Wulff shape
W_{F}
, Xia
[
21
]
proved the inequality (
2.18
) for
i=0
,
j=n-1
,
k=n
and a star-shaped,
F
-mean convex domain
\Omega
using the inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow. The authors of the present paper reproved in
[
19
]
the inequality (
2.18
) for
i=0,k=n+1
and any
j=2,\cdots,n
for smooth and convex domains by applying the volume-preserving flow (
1.1
).

4.
C^{0}
and

In this section, we derive the
C^{0}
and
C^{1}

to the flow (

4.1.
Radial graph parametrization

We view the Euclidean space as a warped product space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}=[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
equipped with the metric

g_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}=d\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}},\quad\rho\in(0,+\infty).

Then any star-shaped hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
can be written as a graph over
\mathbb{S}^{n}
:

X(\theta)=(\rho(\theta),\theta),\quad\>\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

This is also equivalent to that the support function
\sigma=\langle X,\nu\rangle
is positive everywhere on the hypersurface. Take a local coordinate system
\{\theta^{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
for
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, denote by
e_{ij}
the components of the round metric
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and set
\gamma=\log\rho
. Then the unit normal vector field of the hypersurface is given by

(4.1)

\nu=\frac{1}{\omega}(1,-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}\rho),

where
\omega=\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}\gamma|^{2}}
. Since the vector field
\rho\partial_{\rho}
is just the position vector
X
, we see that the support function
\sigma=\rho/\omega
. The first and second fundamental forms of
M
can be expressed as

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}(e_{ij}+\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}),\quad g^{ij}=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\left(e^{ij}-\frac{\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}}{\omega^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho}{\omega}(-\gamma_{ij}+\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}+e_{ij}),

\displaystyle h^{i}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho\omega}\left(e^{ik}-\frac{\gamma_{i}\gamma_{k}}{\omega^{2}}\right)(-\gamma_{kj}+\gamma_{k}\gamma_{j}+e_{kj}).

It follows from (
2.3
) that the anisotropic Weingarten matrix
\mathcal{W}_{F}=(\hat{h}_{i}^{j})
satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{i}^{j}=

\displaystyle(A_{F}(\nu))_{i}^{k}h_{k}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho\omega}(A_{F}(\nu))_{i}^{k}\left(e^{j\ell}-\frac{\gamma_{j}\gamma_{\ell}}{\omega^{2}}\right)(-\gamma_{\ell k}+\gamma_{\ell}\gamma_{k}+e_{k\ell})

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho\omega}(A_{F}(\nu))_{i}^{k}\left(\delta_{k}^{j}-\left(e^{j\ell}-\frac{\gamma_{j}\gamma_{\ell}}{\omega^{2}}\right)\gamma_{\ell k}\right),

where
\nu
is evaluated as in (
4.1
).

4.2.
Star-shapedness and short time existence

We assume that the initial hypersurface
M_{0}
is strictly convex with the origin contained inside the domain
\Omega_{0}
bounded by
M_{0}
. Then
M_{0}
is star-shaped with respect to the origin. It follows that the support function
\sigma
of

is positive, which implies that the anisotropic support function
\sigma_{F}=\sigma/F(\nu)
is positive on
M_{0}
as well.

Lemma 4.1
.

If the initial hypersurface
M_{0}
is star-shaped with respect to the origin, then the anisotropic support function
\sigma_{F}
of the solution

to the flow (

Proof.

Since
\Psi=E_{k}^{1/k}
is inverse-concave with respect to its arguments, we have (see, e.g.,
[
2

\dot{\Psi}^{ij}\hat{h}_{i}^{k}\hat{h}_{k}^{j}\geq\Psi^{2}.

Then the evolution equation (

implies that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma_{F}\geq

\displaystyle~\sigma_{F}\dot{\Psi}^{ij}\left(\hat{\nabla}^{j}\hat{\nabla}_{i}\sigma_{F}+A_{qip}\hat{g}^{jp}\hat{\nabla}^{q}\sigma_{F}\right)+\Psi\hat{\nabla}^{k}\sigma_{F}G(\nu_{F})(X,\partial_{k}X)+(\sigma_{F}\Psi-1)^{2}

\displaystyle~\sigma_{F}\dot{\Psi}^{ij}\left(\hat{\nabla}^{j}\hat{\nabla}_{i}\sigma_{F}+A_{qip}\hat{g}^{jp}\hat{\nabla}^{q}\sigma_{F}\right)+\Psi\hat{\nabla}^{k}\sigma_{F}G(\nu_{F})(X,\partial_{k}X).

\sigma_{F}(y,t)\geq\min_{y\in{M}}\sigma_{F}(y,0)>0.

∎

Since

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega}{\rho}{F(\nu)}-\frac{1}{\rho\omega}\Psi\left((A_{F}(\nu))_{i}^{k}\left(\delta^{j}_{k}-\left(e^{j\ell}-\frac{\gamma_{j}\gamma_{\ell}}{\omega^{2}}\right)\gamma_{k\ell}\right)\right)

is evaluated as in (

is strictly convex, the short time existence of (

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M_{0}
be a smooth, closed and strictly convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then there exists a unique smooth solution
M_{t}
of the flow (

4.3.
C^{0}
estimate

Since
M_{0}
is closed and strictly convex, and it encloses the origin, we see that it is star-shaped with respect to the origin. Then there exist two constants
0<r<R
such that

rW_{F}\subset\Omega_{0}\subset RW_{F}.

Due to the fact that the anisotropic principal curvatures of
\Sigma_{F}
are equal to

and the anisotropic support function of

is a constant function

, both the hypersurfaces

and

are fixed along the flow (

So we have the

estimate.

4.4.
C^{1}
estimate

The
C^{1}
estimate follows from the estimate (
4.4
) on the anisotropic support function and the

estimate. Recall that

(4.8)

\sigma_{F}=\frac{\sigma}{{F}(\nu)}=\frac{\rho}{\omega{F}(\nu)}=\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}\gamma|^{2}}{F}(\nu)}.

By the estimate (

\sigma_{F}(y,t)\geq\min_{y\in{M}}\sigma_{F}(y,0),

which combined with (

) and the

estimate implies that

where

depends on
{F}
,

,

and
|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}\gamma|(\cdot,0)
.

5.
Anisotropic Gauss map parametrization

To derive the curvature estimate of the solution
M_{t}
, we will employ the anisotropic Gauss map parametrization of
M_{t}
. In this section, we first review some basic results on this parametrization.

Given a smooth, closed and strictly convex hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the Gauss map is defined as
\nu:M\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
which maps the point
x\in M
to the outward unit normal
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
at this point. The Gauss map of a smooth closed strictly convex hypersurface is a nondegenerate diffeomorphism between
M
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Since the Wulff shape
W_{F}
is also strictly convex, we can define a map from
\mathbb{S}^{n}
to
\Sigma_{F}=\partial W_{F}
which maps
\nu
to
\nu_{F}=DF(\nu)
. Then the anisotropic Gauss map
\nu_{F}:M\to\Sigma_{F}
is the composition of the above two maps and satisfies

\nu_{F}(x)=DF(\nu(x))

for each point
x\in M
. It follows that the anisotropic Gauss map is a nondegenerate diffeomorphism as well. Thus we can use it to reparametrize
M
:

X:\Sigma_{F}\to M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\quad X(z)=X(\nu_{F}^{-1}(z)),\quad z\in\Sigma_{F}.

The anisotropic support function of
M
is then defined as a function on
\Sigma_{F}
by
s(z)=G(z)(z,X(z))
for
z\in\Sigma_{F}
. Comparing it with the definition (
2.14
) of
\sigma_{F}
, we see that
\sigma_{F}(x)=s(\nu_{F}(x))
.

Let
\bar{g}
and
\bar{\nabla}
denote the induced metric and its Levi-Civita connection on
\Sigma_{F}
from
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},G)
respectively. The anisotropic principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
of a strictly convex hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
are related to its anisotropic principal radii of curvature
\tau=(\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{n})
by

\kappa_{i}=1/{\tau_{i}},\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

It has been shown that the anisotropic principal radii of curvature
\tau=(\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{n})
of
M
are eigenvalues of the following matrix on the Wulff shape
\Sigma_{F}
(see
[
20
]
)

(5.1)

\tau_{ij}[s]=\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}s+\bar{g}_{ij}s-\frac{1}{2}Q_{ijk}\bar{\nabla}_{k}s,

where
s:\Sigma_{F}\to\mathbb{R}
is the anisotropic support function on the hypersurface
M
.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have the following Codazzi and Simons type equations for
\tau_{ij}

(5.2)

8.
Long time existence and smooth convergence

In this section we prove that the solution
M_{t}
of the flow (
1.2
) exists for all positive time and converges smoothly to a scaled Wulff shape centered at the origin.

8.1.
Long time existence

In §
6

on the anisotropic principal curvature

has uniform

8.2.
Smooth convergence

We need to show that the solution

converges to a scaled Wulff shape centered at the origin as the time

. Along the flow (

), we know from §

is monotone non-decreasing. By the

is bounded from above by the volume of

By the uniform regularity estimates of

, the integrand in the time integral of (

Since

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M_{t}}\left(E_{1}-E_{2}^{1/2}\right)\sigma_{F}d\mu_{F}\leq~\int_{M_{t}}\left(E_{1}-E_{k}^{1/k}\right)\sigma_{F}d\mu_{F}~\to~0,\qquad\mathrm{as}~t\to\infty.

\displaystyle 0~\leq

\displaystyle~E_{1}-E_{2}^{1/2}~\to~0,\quad\mathrm{uniformly~in}~C^{\infty}\quad\mathrm{as}~t\to\infty.

\displaystyle n^{2}(n-1)(E_{1}^{2}-E_{2})

\displaystyle n^{2}(n-1)(E_{1}+E_{2}^{1/2})(E_{1}-E_{2}^{1/2})~\to~0

The uniform regularity estimates of

of the flow (

Lemma 9.1

The linearization of (

Write

\displaystyle h(z,t),

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}^{ij}(\bar{r}\bar{g})\tau_{ij}[h(z,t)]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left(\bar{\Delta}h(z,t)+nh(z,t)-\frac{1}{2}\bar{g}^{ij}Q_{ijk}\bar{\nabla}_{k}h(z,t)\right).

Then the linearization of (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h(z,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n\bar{r}}\left(\bar{\Delta}h(z,t)-\frac{1}{2}\bar{g}^{ij}Q_{ijk}\bar{\nabla}_{k}h(z,t)\right).

The operator on the right-hand side is self-adjoint and non-positive; see

Let

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sigma(z,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n\bar{r}}\mathcal{L}\sigma(z,t)+O(|\sigma(z,t)|_{C^{2}}^{2}).

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Sigma_{F}|_{F}}\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\sigma d\mu_{F}.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n\bar{r}}\int_{\Sigma_{F}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}\varphi_{k}^{2}(z,t)d\mu_{F}+C\int_{\Sigma_{F}}|\sigma||\sigma(z,t)|_{C^{2}}^{2}d\mu_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n\bar{r}}\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\lambda_{1}\sum_{k\geq 0}\varphi_{k}^{2}(z,t)d\mu_{F}+\frac{1}{n\bar{r}}\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\lambda_{1}\varphi_{0}^{2}(z,t)d\mu_{F}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{n\bar{r}}\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\sum_{k\geq 2}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{1})\varphi_{k}^{2}(z,t)d\mu_{F}+C\int_{\Sigma_{F}}|\sigma||\sigma(z,t)|_{C^{2}}^{2}d\mu_{F}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{n\bar{r}}\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\sigma(z,t)^{2}d\mu_{F}+\underbrace{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{n\bar{r}|\Sigma_{F}|_{F}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\sigma(z,t)d\mu_{F}\right)^{2}}_{I}

(9.3)

\displaystyle\quad+C\underbrace{\int_{\Sigma_{F}}|\sigma||\sigma(z,t)|_{C^{2}}^{2}d\mu_{F}}_{II}.

We will show that the terms
I,II
decay faster than
\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\sigma(z,t)^{2}d\mu_{F}
. Then the above inequality (
9
) implies the exponential decay of
\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\sigma(z,t)^{2}d\mu_{F}
.

To estimate the term
I
, we explore the monotonicity of the volume
V_{n+1}(\Omega,W_{F})
and the mixed volume
V_{n+2-k}(\Omega,W_{F})
. Under the anisotropic Gauss map parametrization,

\displaystyle V_{n+1}(\Omega,W_{F})=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{F}}sE_{n}(\tau[s])d\mu_{F},

\displaystyle V_{n+2-k}(\Omega,W_{F})=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{F}}sE_{n-k+1}(\tau[s])d\mu_{F},

where
s=s(z)
is the anisotropic support function of
\partial\Omega
and
d\mu_{F}
is the anisotropic area form on the Wulff shape
\Sigma_{F}
. By the monotonicity, we have

(9.4)

\displaystyle V_{n+1}(\Omega_{t},W_{F})\leq

\displaystyle V_{n+1}(\bar{r}W_{F},W_{F})=\bar{r}^{n+1}|\Sigma_{F}|_{F},

(9.5)

\displaystyle V_{n+2-k}(\Omega_{t},W_{F})\geq

\displaystyle V_{n+2-k}(\bar{r}W_{F},W_{F})=\bar{r}^{n+2-k}|\Sigma_{F}|_{F}.

Expanding the left-hand side of (
9.4
) and (
9.5
) using the fact
\sigma(z,t)=s(z,t)-\bar{r}
is sufficiently small, we obtain

\displaystyle 0~\geq

\displaystyle~V_{n+1}(\Omega_{t},W_{F})-\bar{r}^{n+1}|\Sigma_{F}|_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{F}}sE_{n}(\tau[s])d\mu_{F}-\bar{r}^{n+1}|\Sigma_{F}|_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\left(\bar{r}+\sigma+O(\sigma^{2})\right)\left(\bar{r}^{n}+\bar{r}^{n-1}(\bar{L}\sigma+n\sigma)+\bar{r}^{n-2}O(|\sigma|_{C^{2}}^{2})\right)d\mu_{F}

\displaystyle\quad-\bar{r}^{n+1}|\Sigma_{F}|_{F}

(9.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+1)\bar{r}^{n}\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\sigma d\mu_{F}+\int_{\Sigma_{F}}O(|\sigma|_{C^{2}}^{2})d\mu_{F}

and

\displaystyle 0~\leq

\displaystyle~V_{n+2-k}(\Omega_{t},W_{F})-\bar{r}^{n+2-k}|\Sigma_{F}|_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{F}}sE_{n-k+1}(\tau[s])d\mu_{F}-\bar{r}^{n+2-k}|\Sigma_{F}|_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\left(\bar{r}+\sigma+O(\sigma^{2})\right)\left(\bar{r}^{n-k+1}+\frac{n-k+1}{n}\bar{r}^{n-k}(\bar{L}\sigma+n\sigma)+\bar{r}^{n-k-1}O(|\sigma|_{C^{2}}^{2})\right)d\mu_{F}

\displaystyle\quad-\bar{r}^{n+2-k}|\Sigma_{F}|_{F}

(9.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-k+2)\bar{r}^{n-k+1}\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\sigma d\mu_{F}+\int_{\Sigma_{F}}O(|\sigma|_{C^{2}}^{2})d\mu_{F}.

Combining (
9
) and (
9
) implies that

(9.8)

\left(\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\sigma d\mu_{F}\right)^{2}\leq C\left(\int_{\Sigma_{F}}|\sigma|_{C^{2}}^{2}d\mu_{F}\right)^{2}.

Then
I+II
on the right-hand side of (
9
) satisfies

(9.9)

I+II\leq~C\left(\int_{\Sigma_{F}}|\sigma|_{C^{2}}^{2}d\mu_{F}\right)^{2}+C\left(\int_{\Sigma_{F}}|\sigma|_{C^{2}}^{4}d\mu_{F}\right)^{1/2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}

for some constant
C
.

We recall the following special case of Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality; see
[
3
, Theorem 3.70]
.

Lemma 9.2
.

For integers
j,k
satisfying
{j}/{k}\leq\theta<1
, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
j,k,\theta,n
and the manifold
\Sigma
such that

(9.10)

\|\bar{\nabla}^{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}\leq C\|\bar{\nabla}^{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{\theta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{1-\theta}

for any function
f\in W^{k,2}(\Sigma)
with
\int_{\Sigma}fd\mu_{F}=0
, where

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{j}{n}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{k}{n}\theta

for which
p
is nonnegative.

Though
\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\sigma d\mu_{F}\neq 0
, by setting

f=\sigma-\frac{1}{|\Sigma_{F}|_{F}}\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\sigma d\mu_{F}

in (
9.10
), we see that (
9.10
) still holds for
\sigma
. Using the uniform bound
\|\sigma\|_{C^{k}(\Sigma_{F})}\leq C(k)
for all
k\geq 0
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma_{F}}|\sigma|_{C^{2}}^{2}d\mu_{F}\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\left(|\bar{\nabla}^{2}\sigma|^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}\sigma|^{2}+|\sigma|^{2}\right)d\mu_{F}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}^{2(1-\frac{2}{k})}+\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}^{2(1-\frac{1}{k})}+\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}^{4(1-\frac{2}{k})}

(9.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}^{3}

by choosing
k\geq 8
. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma_{F}}|\sigma|_{C^{2}}^{4}d\mu_{F}\right)^{1/2}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\Sigma_{F}}\left(|\bar{\nabla}^{2}\sigma|^{4}+|\bar{\nabla}\sigma|^{4}+|\sigma|^{4}\right)d\mu_{F}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}^{4(1-\frac{2+n/4}{k})}+\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}^{4(1-\frac{1+n/4}{k})}+\|\sigma\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma_{F})}^{2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(9.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\sigma\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma_{F})}^{1/2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}

by choosing
k\geq 8+n
. Substituting (
9.9
) and (
9
), (
9
) into (
9
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{n\bar{r}}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}+C\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}^{2}+C\|\sigma\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma_{F})}^{1/2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}

(9.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{n\bar{r}}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}\left(1-C\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}-C\|\sigma\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma_{F})}^{1/2}\right).

For sufficiently large time
t
, we have

1-C\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}-C\|\sigma\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma_{F})}^{1/2}\geq 1/2.

This implies that

(9.14)

\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{F})}\leq Ce^{-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2n\bar{r}}t}.

By Sobolev Embedding Theorem,

\|\sigma\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma_{F})}\leq C\|\sigma\|_{W^{j,2}(\Sigma_{F})}

for
j>n/2
. Interpolation inequality (
9.10
) together with the exponential decay (

(9.15)

\|\sigma\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma_{F})}\leq Ce^{-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2n\bar{r}}(1-\delta)t}

for any

. Substituting (

9
), we can improve the decay rate in (
9.14
) to any constant smaller than
\frac{\lambda_{1}}{n\bar{r}}
. Applying the interpolation inequality (
9.10
) and Sobolev Embedding Theorem we eventually obtain that

(9.16)

\|\sigma\|_{C^{k}(\Sigma_{F})}\leq C(k,\delta)e^{-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{n\bar{r}}(1-\delta)t}

for any given
0<\delta\ll 1
, where
\lambda_{1}>0
is the first non-zero eigenvalue of the operator
\mathcal{L}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Appendix A
Codazzi and Simons type equations

In this appendix, we provide a detailed calculation to derive the Codazzi and Simons type equations in Lemma
5.1
.

Lemma A.1
.

We have the following Codazzi and Simons type equations for
\tau_{ij}
:

(A.1)

\bar{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{k\ell}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{k\ell p}\tau_{jp}~=~\bar{\nabla}_{k}\tau_{j\ell}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{j\ell p}\tau_{kp}

and

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{k\ell}=

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}\tau_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{ijp}\bar{\nabla}_{p}\tau_{k\ell}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{k\ell p}\bar{\nabla}_{p}\tau_{ij}+\bar{g}_{ij}\tau_{k\ell}-\bar{g}_{kj}\tau_{i\ell}

\displaystyle+\bar{g}_{i\ell}\tau_{kj}-\bar{g}_{k\ell}\tau_{ij}+\frac{1}{4}\left(Q_{jkq}Q_{ipq}-Q_{kpq}Q_{qij}\right)\tau_{p\ell}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left(Q_{j\ell q}Q_{ipq}-Q_{\ell pq}Q_{qij}\right)\tau_{kp}+\frac{1}{4}\left(Q_{k\ell q}Q_{ipq}-Q_{kpq}Q_{qi\ell}\right)\tau_{pj}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left(Q_{k\ell q}Q_{jpq}-Q_{kpq}Q_{qj\ell}\right)\tau_{ip}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{p}Q_{ijk}\tau_{\ell p}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{p}Q_{ij\ell}\tau_{kp}

(A.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{p}Q_{jk\ell}\tau_{ip}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{p}Q_{ik\ell}\tau_{jp}.

Proof.

(i) Taking the covariant derivative of the equation (
5.1
), we have

(A.3)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{k\ell}=

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{j}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}s+\bar{g}_{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{j}s-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{j}Q_{k\ell p}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s-\frac{1}{2}Q_{k\ell p}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s,

(A.4)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{k}\tau_{j\ell}=

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}s+\bar{g}_{j\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{k}s-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{k}Q_{j\ell p}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s-\frac{1}{2}Q_{j\ell p}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s.

Using the Ricci identity and (
2.13
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{j}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}s-\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}s=\bar{R}_{jk\ell p}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s

(A.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{j\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{k}s-\bar{g}_{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{j}s+\frac{1}{4}Q_{k\ell q}Q_{qjp}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s-\frac{1}{4}Q_{j\ell q}Q_{qkp}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s.

Combining (
A.3
)–(
A.5
) and using the total symmetry of
Q_{ijk}
and
\bar{\nabla}_{i}Q_{jk\ell}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{k\ell}-\bar{\nabla}_{k}\tau_{j\ell}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}Q_{j\ell p}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s-\frac{1}{2}Q_{k\ell p}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s+\frac{1}{4}Q_{k\ell q}Q_{qjp}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s-\frac{1}{4}Q_{j\ell q}Q_{qkp}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}Q_{j\ell p}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s-\frac{1}{2}Q_{k\ell p}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\bar{\nabla}_{p}s+\frac{1}{2}Q_{k\ell q}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{q}\bar{\nabla}_{j}s+s\bar{g}_{qj}-\tau_{qj}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}Q_{j\ell q}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{q}\bar{\nabla}_{k}s+s\bar{g}_{qk}-\tau_{qk}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}Q_{j\ell p}\tau_{pk}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{k\ell p}\tau_{pj}.

(ii) For the Simons type equation, we take the covariant derivative of the equation (
A.1

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{k\ell}=

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\tau_{j\ell}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\left(Q_{j\ell q}\tau_{kq}-Q_{k\ell p}\tau_{jp}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\tau_{j\ell}+\bar{R}_{ikjp}\tau_{p\ell}+\bar{R}_{ik\ell p}\tau_{pj}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\left(Q_{j\ell q}\tau_{kq}-Q_{k\ell p}\tau_{jp}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}\tau_{ji}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\left(Q_{jiq}\tau_{\ell q}-Q_{j\ell p}\tau_{ip}\right)+\bar{g}_{ij}\tau_{k\ell}-\bar{g}_{kj}\tau_{i\ell}+\bar{g}_{i\ell}\tau_{kj}-\bar{g}_{k\ell}\tau_{ij}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left(Q_{kjq}Q_{qip}-Q_{kpq}Q_{qij}\right)\tau_{p\ell}+\frac{1}{4}\left(Q_{k\ell q}Q_{qip}-Q_{kpq}Q_{qi\ell}\right)\tau_{pj}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\left(Q_{j\ell q}\tau_{kq}-Q_{k\ell p}\tau_{jp}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}\tau_{ji}+\bar{g}_{ij}\tau_{k\ell}-\bar{g}_{kj}\tau_{i\ell}+\bar{g}_{i\ell}\tau_{kj}-\bar{g}_{k\ell}\tau_{ij}+\frac{1}{4}\left(Q_{kjq}Q_{qip}-Q_{kpq}Q_{qij}\right)\tau_{p\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}\tau_{ji}+\bar{g}_{ij}\tau_{k\ell}-\bar{g}_{kj}\tau_{i\ell}+\bar{g}_{i\ell}\tau_{kj}-\bar{g}_{k\ell}\tau_{ij}+\frac{1}{4}\left(Q_{kjq}Q_{qip}-Q_{kpq}Q_{qij}\right)\tau_{p\ell}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left(Q_{k\ell q}Q_{qip}-Q_{kpq}Q_{qi\ell}\right)\tau_{pj}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{k}Q_{jiq}\tau_{\ell q}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{k}Q_{j\ell p}\tau_{ip}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{i}Q_{j\ell q}\tau_{kq}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{i}Q_{k\ell p}\tau_{jp}+\frac{1}{4}Q_{j\ell p}\left(Q_{ipq}\tau_{kq}-Q_{kpq}\tau_{iq}\right)

Using the total symmetry of

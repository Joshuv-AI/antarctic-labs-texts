Diagonalization Based Parallel-in-Time Method for a Class of Fourth Order Time Dependent PDEs Thanks: Submitted.
Gobinda Garai, Bankim C. Mandal

Abstract
. In this paper, we design, analyze and implement efficient time parallel method for a class of fourth order time-dependent partial differential equations (PDEs), namely biharmonic heat equation, linearized Cahn-Hilliard (CH) equation and the nonlinear CH equation. We use diagonalization technique on all-at-once system to develop efficient iterative time parallel methods for investigating the solution behaviour of said equations. We present the convergence analysis of Parallel-in-Time (PinT) algorithms. We verify our findings by presenting numerical results.

1.
Introduction

In this work we are interested in designing time parallel algorithm for linear and non-linear fourth order time-dependent PDEs based on Waveform relaxation (WR) and the diagonalization technique. The simplest linear fourth order time-dependent PDE is given by

u_{t}=-\Delta^{2}u,\;(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],

(1.1)

and the linearized Cahn-Hilliard equation is given by

u_{t}=-\beta^{2}\Delta u-\epsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u,\;(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],

(1.2)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},d=1,2
. The equation (
1.1
) is related to modelling of clamping plate, thin beam etc. The existence of solution and numerical solution of (
1.1
) can be seen in
[
22
]
for different choices of boundary conditions. The equation (
1.2
) is the linearization of the Cahn-Hilliard (CH) equation about a solution in spinodal region, see
[
9
,
20
]
, where the parameter
\beta
takes value in
[-1/\sqrt{3},1/\sqrt{3}]
.
We also formulate time parallel algorithm for the nonlinear CH equation.
The CH equation has been suggested as a prototype to represent the evolution of a binary melted alloy below the critical temperature in
[
3
,
4
]
, it also arises from the Ginzburg-Landau free energy functional:

\mathcal{E}(u):=\int_{\Omega}F(u)+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2},

where
F(u)=0.25(u^{2}-1)^{2}
and
\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}
is the gradient energy and
\epsilon(0<\epsilon\ll 1)
is the thickness of the interface. The CH equation with the natural homogeneous Neumann boundary condition takes the form

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta v,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
v=f(u)-\epsilon^{2}\Delta u,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
\partial_{\nu}u=0=\partial_{\nu}v,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
f(u)=F^{\prime}(u)
and
\nu
is outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
. The solution
u(x,t)
of (
1.3
) is always bounded, thus we assume the following

\max_{u}|f^{\prime}(u)|\leq M,

(1.4)

where
M
is a non-negative constant. The solution of the CH equation involves two different dynamics, one is phase separation which is quick in time, and another is phase coarsening which is slow in time. The fine-scale phase regions are formed during the early stage and they are separated by the interface of width
\epsilon.
Whereas during phase coarsening, the solution tends to an equilibrium state which minimizes
the system energy.
By differentiating the energy functional
\mathcal{E}(u)
and total mass
\int_{\Omega}u
with respect to time
t
, we get

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(u)\leq 0,\mbox{\hskip 28.45274pt}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u=0.

(1.5)

The relations in (
1.5
) says that the CH equation describes energy minimization and the total mass conservation while the system evolves. Since the non-increasing property (
1.5
) of the total energy is an essential feature of the CH equation, it is a key issue for long time simulation that is expected to be preserved by numerical methods.
The CH equation is a nonlinear equation and is impossible to find its analytical solution but there exists solution of the CH equation as shown in
[
11
,
26
]
. Various research have been done on finding numerical scheme for the CH equation to approximate the solution with either Dirichlet
[
8
,
12
]
or Neumann boundary conditions
[
10
,
14
,
29
,
11
,
9
,
30
,
6
,
5
]
. A review on different numerical treatments to the CH equation can be found in
[
24
]
. The possible application of the CH equation as a model are: image inpainting
[
1
]
, tumour growth simulation
[
32
]
, population dynamics
[
7
]
, dendritic growth
[
23
]
, planet formation
[
31
]
, etc.

The above described works are all in time stepping fashion for advancement of evolution of the CH equation. To get a good accuracy of the numerical solution of CH equation one needs the spatial mesh size
h
is of
O(\epsilon)
or even finer. Then the discretized equation will result in a very large scale algebraic system that should be solved sequentially for capturing the long term behaviour of the CH equation.
Consequently, it is of great importance to accelerate the simulation using parallel computation, which can be fulfilled by time parallel technique. In this work, we lay our efforts on designing PinT method for above mentioned evolution equations.
In the last few decades there are lot of efforts on formulating various type of time parallel technique, for an overview see
[
15
]
. In this work we use the WR
[
25
]
and diagonalization technique
[
17
,
16
,
27
]
to get a PinT algorithm. A diagonalization based method was first proposed in
[
27
]
, which rely on reformulating space-time discretization as an
all-at-once
system using tensor product and use diagonalization of temporal discretization matrix. For temporal discretization matrix to be diagonalizable one needs to use variable time step size as given in
[
17
]
; for equidistant time step the temporal discretization matrix is a Jordan block. The described method in
[
17
]
for variable time step size suffers form roundoff error and the accuracy of temporal variable. The works in
[
28
,
19
]
use Strang type block
\alpha
-circulant matrix as a preconditioner for
all-at-once
system and the resulting preconditioned system can be solved using diagonalization technique. Recently, in
[
18
]
Gander & Wu proposed a time parallel iterative algorithm which uses uniform time step to solve initial value problem. A
periodic-like
initial condition is imposed to get a Strang type
\alpha
-circulant temporal matrix, which is diagonalizable. We use similar approach in this paper.

The rest of this paper is arrange as follows. We introduce in Section
2
the time parallel algorithm in one and two spatial dimension for the equation (
1.1
), and study the convergence behaviour. In Section
3
we formulate the time parallel algorithm for the equation (
1.2
), and present the convergence result. In Section
4
we design the PinT algorithm for the equation (
1.3
), and present the convergence result. To illustrate our analysis, the accuracy and robustness of the proposed formulation, we show numerical results in Section
5
.

2.
Linear Time-Dependent Biharmonic Equation

In this section we consider the linear time-dependent biharmonic equation as given below

\begin{cases}u_{t}=-\Delta^{2}u,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
\partial_{\nu}u=0=\partial_{\nu}(\Delta u),&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
\nu
is outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
. The PinT algorithm for (
2.1
), based on WR iteration and
periodic-like
initial condition, is the following

\begin{cases}u_{t}^{k}=-\Delta^{2}u^{k},&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
u^{k}(x,0)=\alpha u^{k}(x,T)-\alpha u^{k-1}(x,T)+u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}u^{k}=0=\partial_{\nu}(\Delta u^{k}),&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],\end{cases}

(2.2)

where
k\geq 1
is the WR iteration index and
\alpha
is the PinT free parameter. Evidently upon convergence of (
2.2
) the term
\alpha(u^{k}(x,T)-u^{k-1}(x,T))
is cancelled and therefore the converged solution is the solution of (
2.1
). We now introduce the diagonalization based implementation of (
2.2
).

2.1.
Discretization and PinT Formulation

Using the centred finite difference scheme for spatial derivative in an interval with equidistant grid points and homogeneous Neumann boundary condition as described in (
2.1
), we have the following semi-discrete system corresponding to (
2.2
)

\begin{cases}\dot{\textbf{u}}^{k}(t)+A\textbf{u}^{k}(t)=0,\\
\textbf{u}^{k}(0)=\alpha\textbf{u}^{k}(T)-\alpha\textbf{u}^{k-1}(T)+\textbf{u}_{0},\end{cases}

(2.3)

where
\textbf{u}=(u^{1},u^{2},\cdots,u^{N_{x}})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}}
, and
A=\Delta^{2}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}\times N_{x}}
with

\Delta_{h}=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\begin{bmatrix}-2&2\\
1&-2&1\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&1&-2&1\\
&&&2&-2\end{bmatrix}.

(2.4)

Employing the linear
\theta
-method to (
2.3
) yields

\begin{cases}\frac{\textbf{u}_{n}^{k}-\textbf{u}_{n-1}^{k}}{\Delta t}+A\left(\theta\textbf{u}_{n}^{k}+(1-\theta)\textbf{u}_{n-1}^{k}\right)=0,&n=1,2,\cdots,N_{t}\\
\textbf{u}_{0}^{k}=\alpha\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k}-\alpha\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k-1}+\textbf{u}_{0},\end{cases}

(2.5)

where
N_{t}={T}/{\Delta t}
and
\theta\in[0,1]
. We consider
\theta=1
and
\theta=1/2
, which corresponds to Backward-Euler and Trapezoidal scheme. After gathering all the space-time points in (
2.5
), we have the following
all-at-once
or
space-time
system

\left(C_{1}^{(\alpha)}\otimes I_{x}+C_{2}^{(\alpha)}\otimes A\right)\textbf{U}^{k}=\textbf{b}^{k-1},

(2.6)

where
\textbf{U}^{k}=(\textbf{u}_{1}^{k},\textbf{u}_{2}^{k},\cdots,\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}N_{t}},C_{1}^{\alpha},C_{2}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{t}\times N_{t}}
and
\textbf{b}^{k-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{t}N_{x}}
are given by

C_{1}^{(\alpha)}=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\begin{bmatrix}1&&&-\alpha\\
-1&1\\
&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&-1&1\end{bmatrix}_{N_{t}\times N_{t}},C_{2}^{(\alpha)}=\begin{bmatrix}\theta&&&(1-\theta)\alpha\\
1-\theta&\theta\\
&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&1-\theta&\theta\end{bmatrix}_{N_{t}\times N_{t}},

(2.7)

\textbf{b}^{k-1}=\left((\textbf{u}_{0}-\alpha\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k-1})\left(\frac{1}{\Delta t}I_{x}-(1-\theta)A\right),\textbf{0},\cdots,\textbf{0}\right)^{T}.

The matrices
C_{1,2}^{(\alpha)}
are Strang type
\alpha-
circulant matrices and can be diagonalizable as stated in the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(Diagonalization of
\alpha
-circulant matrix, see
[
2
]
)
.

The matrices
C_{1,2}^{(\alpha)}
defined in (
2.7
) can be diagonalized as

C_{j}^{(\alpha)}=VD_{j}V^{-1},D_{j}=\diag(\sqrt{N_{t}}\mathbb{F}\Gamma_{\alpha}C_{j}^{(\alpha)}(:,1)),\;j=1,2,

where
V=\Gamma_{\alpha}^{-1}\mathbb{F}^{*},\mathbb{F}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{t}}}\left[\omega_{0}^{(l_{1}-1)(l_{2}-1)}\right]_{l_{1},l_{2}=1}^{N_{t}}
(with
i=\sqrt{-1}
and
\omega_{0}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{N_{t}}}
),
\Gamma_{\alpha}=\diag(1,\alpha^{\frac{1}{N_{t}}},\cdots,\alpha^{\frac{N_{t}-1}{N_{t}}})
and
C_{j}^{(\alpha)}(:,1)
being the first column of
C_{j}^{(\alpha)},j=1,2
.

Using the property of tensor product, we can factor the
all-at-once
system
C_{1}^{(\alpha)}\otimes I_{x}+C_{2}^{(\alpha)}\otimes A
as
(V\otimes I_{x})(D_{1}\otimes I_{x}+D_{2}\otimes A)(V^{-1}\otimes I_{x})
, and we can solve (
2.6
) by performing the following three steps

\displaystyle\text{Step}-(1)

\displaystyle S_{1}=(\mathbb{F}\otimes I_{x})(\Gamma_{\alpha}\otimes I_{x})\textbf{b}^{k-1},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\text{Step}-(2)

\displaystyle S_{2,n}=(\lambda_{1,n}I_{x}+\lambda_{2,n}A)^{-1}S_{1,n},\;n=1,2,\cdots N_{t},

\displaystyle\text{Step}-(3)

\displaystyle\textbf{U}^{k}=(\Gamma_{\alpha}^{-1}\otimes I_{x})(\mathbb{F}^{*}\otimes I_{x})S_{2},

where
D_{j}=\diag(\lambda_{j,1},\cdots,\lambda_{j,N_{t}}),S_{j}=(S_{j,1}^{T},\cdots,S_{j,N_{t}}^{T})^{T}
for
j=1,2
. The eigenvalues
\lambda_{1,n}
and
\lambda_{2,n}
are defined by

\lambda_{1,n}:=1-\alpha^{\frac{1}{N_{t}}}e^{-i\frac{2n\pi}{N_{t}}},\;\lambda_{2,n}:=\theta+(1-\theta)\alpha^{\frac{1}{N_{t}}}e^{-i\frac{2n\pi}{N_{t}}}

In (
2.8
), Step-(1) and Step-(3) can be computed using FFT with
O(N_{x}N_{t}\log N_{t})
operations. Step-(2) is fully parallel. The convergence behaviour of the above described algorithm are given in the following section.

2.2.
Convergence Analysis

In this section we present the error estimate for (
2.2
) at different level.

2.2.1.
Error estimate at continuous level

Let
e^{k}(x,t)=u^{k}(x,t)-u(x,t)
be the error function at each PinT iteration. Then from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) we have the following error equation at continuous level

\begin{cases}e_{t}^{k}=-\Delta^{2}e^{k},&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
e^{k}(x,0)=\alpha(e^{k}(x,T)-e^{k-1}(x,T)),&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(2.9)

Taking Fourier transform in space for the equation (
2.9
), we obtain the system of differential equations for each Fourier mode
\omega
as follows

\begin{cases}\hat{e}_{t}^{k}=-\omega^{4}\hat{e}^{k},&t\in(0,T],\\
\hat{e}^{k}(0)=\alpha(\hat{e}^{k}(T)-\hat{e}^{k-1}(T)).\end{cases}

(2.10)

Solving (
2.10
) for each Fourier mode we have

\hat{e}^{k}(t)=e^{-\omega^{4}t}\hat{e}^{k}(0).

(2.11)

From (
2.11
) we have
\parallel\hat{e}^{k}(T)\parallel_{l^{2}}\leq e^{-\omega^{4}T}\parallel\hat{e}^{k}(0)\parallel_{l^{2}}
and from
\hat{e}^{k}(0)=\alpha(\hat{e}^{k}(T)-\hat{e}^{k-1}(T))
we have
\parallel\hat{e}^{k}(0)\parallel_{l^{2}}\leq|\alpha|\parallel\hat{e}^{k}(T)\parallel_{l^{2}}+|\alpha|\parallel\hat{e}^{k-1}(T)\parallel_{l^{2}}
. Thus by using the first inequality twice in the second inequality we have

\parallel\hat{e}^{k}(0)\parallel_{l^{2}}\leq|\alpha|e^{-\omega^{4}T}\parallel\hat{e}^{k}(0)\parallel_{l^{2}}+|\alpha|e^{-\omega^{4}T}\parallel\hat{e}^{k-1}(0)\parallel_{l^{2}},

(2.12)

and this imply

\parallel\hat{e}^{k}(0)\parallel_{l^{2}}\leq\frac{|\alpha|e^{-\omega^{4}T}}{1-|\alpha|e^{-\omega^{4}T}}\parallel\hat{e}^{k-1}(0)\parallel_{l^{2}},

(2.13)

where
\parallel\bullet\parallel_{l^{2}}
is the sequential
l^{2}
norm for the Fourier frequency variable.

Theorem 2.2

(Error estimate at continuous level)
.

Let
\{u^{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be the functions generated by PinT method (
2.2
), with
|\alpha|<1
. Then, the error function
e^{k}(x,t)
satisfies the following linear convergence estimate

\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\left(\frac{|\alpha|e^{-\omega_{\min}^{4}T}}{1-|\alpha|e^{-\omega_{\min}^{4}T}}\right)^{k}\parallel e^{0}\parallel_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))},

where
\omega_{\min}
is the lowest Fourier frequency.

Proof.

From (
2.11
) we have
\max\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\parallel\hat{e}^{k}(t)\parallel_{l^{2}}\leq\parallel\hat{e}^{k}(0)\parallel_{l^{2}}
, then using (
2.13
)
and Parseval-Plancherel identity we get

\max\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\parallel e^{k}(t)\parallel_{L^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{|\alpha|e^{-\omega^{4}T}}{1-|\alpha|e^{-\omega^{4}T}}\right)^{k}\parallel e^{0}(0)\parallel_{L^{2}}.

Now by observing that the function
g_{1}(y)=\frac{|\alpha|e^{-y}}{1-|\alpha|e^{-y}}
is monotonic decreasing, result follows immediately.
∎

2.2.2.
Error estimate at Semi-discrete level

We perform the error analysis at semi-discrete level, to see the dependency on spatial mesh size
h
. Let
\textbf{e}^{k}(t)=\textbf{u}^{k}(t)-\textbf{u}(t)
be the error function at each PinT iteration. Then from semi-discrete version of equation (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) we have the following error equation at semi-continuous level

\begin{cases}\dot{\textbf{e}}^{k}(t)+A\textbf{e}^{k}(t)=0,\\
\textbf{e}^{k}(0)=\alpha(\textbf{e}^{k}(T)-\textbf{e}^{k-1}(T)).\end{cases}

(2.14)

On solving (
2.14
) we have

\textbf{e}^{k}(t)=\textbf{e}^{-At}\textbf{e}^{k}(0).

(2.15)

By letting
t=T
in (
2.15
) and substitute in
\textbf{e}^{k}(0)=\alpha(\textbf{e}^{k}(T)-\textbf{e}^{k-1}(T))
, we obtain

\textbf{e}^{k}(0)=\frac{-\alpha\textbf{e}^{-AT}}{1-\alpha\textbf{e}^{-AT}}\textbf{e}^{k-1}(0).

(2.16)

Before analyzing further we first discuss spectral distribution of
A
. Eigenvalues of
\Delta_{h}
in (
2.4
) are

\lambda_{p}=\frac{2}{h^{2}}\left\{\cos\left(\frac{(p-1)\pi}{N_{x}-1}\right)-1\right\},p=1,\cdots,N_{x}.

These
\lambda_{p}
’s are distinct and satisfy
\lambda_{p}\leq 0,\forall p
. Hence the eigenvalues of
A
are
\lambda_{p}^{2}
, distinct and the spectrum of
A
,
\sigma(A)\subset[0,\infty)
. Thus the matrix
A
is diagonalizable as

A=PDP^{-1},D=\diag(\lambda_{1}^{2},\lambda_{2}^{2},\cdots,\lambda_{N_{x}}^{2}),

where
P
is made of eigenvectors corresponding to
\lambda_{i}^{2}
. Then for any vector norm
\parallel\bullet\parallel
we have from (
2.16
)

\parallel P\textbf{e}^{k}(0)\parallel\leq\max\limits_{z\in\sigma(AT)}W(z)\parallel P\textbf{e}^{k-1}(0)\parallel,\;W(z)=\frac{|\alpha e^{-z}|}{|1-\alpha e^{-z}|}.

Now from (
2.15
) we have
\max\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\parallel\textbf{e}^{k}(t)\parallel\leq\parallel\textbf{e}^{k}(0)\parallel
, hence it is sufficient to analyze the behaviour of the function
W(z)
to capture the error contraction. As
\sigma(A)\subset[0,\infty)
we have
z\in[0,\infty)
and the function
W(z)
is monotonic decreasing for
z\in[0,\infty)
. The convergence result given in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3

(Error estimate at Semi-discrete level)
.

Let
\textbf{u}^{k}
be the
k-
th iteration of the PinT algorithm (
2.3
) with
|\alpha|<1/2
. Then we have the following linear convergence estimate

\parallel P\textbf{e}^{k}\parallel_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\leq\left(\frac{|\alpha|}{1-|\alpha|}\right)^{k}\parallel P\textbf{e}^{0}\parallel_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}.

Proof.

Proof of the theorem can be realized from the above discussion.
∎

2.2.3.
Error estimate at fully discrete level

We now present the error analysis at fully discrete level, to see the dependency on temporal mesh size
\Delta t
and the time integrators. Let
\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}=\textbf{u}_{n}^{k}-\textbf{u}_{n}
be the error vector at each PinT iteration. Then for linear
\theta-
method we have

\begin{cases}(I_{x}+\theta\Delta tA)\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}=(I_{x}-(1-\theta)\Delta tA)\textbf{e}_{n-1}^{k},&n=1,2,\cdots,N_{t}\\
\textbf{e}_{0}^{k}=\alpha(\textbf{e}_{N_{t}}^{k}-\textbf{e}_{N_{t}}^{k-1}).\end{cases}

(2.17)

Using recurrence relation in (
2.17
) we have

\begin{cases}\textbf{e}_{N_{t}}^{k}=\left(R_{\theta}(\Delta tA)\right)^{N_{t}}\textbf{e}_{0}^{k},&R_{\theta}(\Delta tA)=(I_{x}+\theta\Delta tA)^{-1}(I_{x}-(1-\theta)\Delta tA)\\
\textbf{e}_{0}^{k}=\alpha(\textbf{e}_{N_{t}}^{k}-\textbf{e}_{N_{t}}^{k-1}).\end{cases}

(2.18)

Then similar to the analysis of semi-discrete case we have

\parallel P\textbf{e}^{k}(0)\parallel\leq\max\limits_{z\in\sigma(\Delta tA)}\widetilde{W}(z)\parallel P\textbf{e}^{k-1}(0)\parallel,\;\widetilde{W}(z)=\frac{|\alpha R_{\theta}^{N_{t}}(z)|}{|1-\alpha R_{\theta}^{N_{t}}(z)|},

where the matrix
P
as described earlier. So analyzing
\widetilde{W}(z)
is sufficient to see the error contraction. For
\theta=1
, we have
R_{1}(z)=\frac{1}{1+z}
for
z\in[0,\infty)
, which is a decreasing function in
z
. The function
g_{2}(y)=\frac{|\alpha|y}{1-|\alpha|y}
is monotonic increasing. Hence
\max\limits_{z\in\sigma(\Delta tA)}\widetilde{W}(z)\leq g_{2}(R_{1}(0))
for backward-Euler method. For
\theta=1/2
, we have
R_{1/2}(z)=\frac{1-\frac{z}{2}}{1+\frac{z}{2}}
for
z\in[0,\infty)
, which is a decreasing function in
z
. Similarly we have
\max\limits_{z\in\sigma(\Delta tA)}\widetilde{W}(z)\leq g_{2}(R_{1/2}(0))
for the Trapezoidal rule.

Theorem 2.4

(Error estimate at fully discrete level)
.

For
\theta=1
and
\theta=1/2
the discrete error
\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}
at
k-
th iterate of the PinT algorithm (
2.5
) with
|\alpha|<1/2
satisfies the following linear convergence estimate

\max\limits_{n=1,2,\cdots,N_{t}}\parallel P\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}\parallel\leq\left(\frac{|\alpha|}{1-|\alpha|}\right)^{k}\parallel P\textbf{e}_{0}^{0}\parallel.

Proof.

Proof of the theorem can be realized from the above discussion.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

We can extend the above analysis for 2D and 3D at each level.

(1)

In 2D, Theorem
2.2
will also hold with the convergence factor
\frac{|\alpha|e^{-(\omega_{\min}^{4}+\xi_{\min}^{4})T}}{1-|\alpha|e^{-(\omega_{\min}^{4}+\xi_{\min}^{4})T}}
, where
\omega_{\min}
and
\xi_{\min}
are the lowest Fourier frequency for spatial variables. Similarly one can write the convergence factor in 3D.

(2)

The spectrum of two dimensional discrete Laplacian
I\otimes\Delta_{h}+\Delta_{h}\otimes I
with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition and equidistant grid points in both spatial direction
is given by

\lambda_{p,q}=\frac{2}{h^{2}}\left\{\cos\left(\frac{(p-1)\pi}{N_{x}-1}\right)-1\right\}+\frac{2}{h^{2}}\left\{\cos\left(\frac{(q-1)\pi}{N_{x}-1}\right)-1\right\},p,q=1,\cdots,N_{x},

where
\Delta_{h}
is one dimensional discrete Laplacian given in (
2.4
). Hence one can observe that the spectral property remains same for
A
in 2D, so Theorem
2.3
holds true in 2D.

(3)

Theorem
2.4
also depends on spectral distribution of
A
, hence it also holds in 2D.

3.
Linearised CH Equation

In this section we consider the linearised CH equation in 1D as given below

\begin{cases}u_{t}=-\beta^{2}\Delta u-\epsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T]\\
\partial_{\nu}u=0=\partial_{\nu}(\Delta u),&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T]\\
u(x,0)=u_{0},&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(3.1)

The PinT algorithm for (
3.1
), is based on WR iteration and periodic-like initial condition, is the following

\begin{cases}u_{t}^{k}=-\beta^{2}\Delta u^{k}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u^{k},&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
u^{k}(x,0)=\alpha u^{k}(x,T)-\alpha u^{k-1}(x,T)+u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
\end{cases}

(3.2)

where
k\geq 1
is the WR iteration index and
\alpha\in(0,1)
is the PinT free parameter. The problem (
3.1
) is linear, so the discretization and PinT formulation at semi-discrete level and fully-discrete level will follow from (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) respectively by taking
A=\beta^{2}\Delta_{h}+\epsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}\times N_{x}}
, where
\Delta_{h}
as defined in (
2.4
). Thus the PinT algorithm for (
3.1
) is given by (
2.8
) for above prescribed
A
. Thus we will discuss only the convergence behaviour in the next section.

3.1.
Convergence analysis

We present the convergence result for (
3.1
) at different level in 1D, procedure of getting error estimate is same as process given in subsection
2.2
.

Theorem 3.1

(Error estimate at continuous level)
.

Let
\{u^{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be the functions generated by PinT method (
3.2
), with
|\alpha|<1/2
. Then, the error function
e^{k}(x,t)=u^{k}(x,t)-u(x,t)
satisfies the following linear convergence estimate

\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\rho^{k}\parallel e^{0}\parallel_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))},

where the convergence factor
\rho
is given by

\rho^{k}=\begin{cases}e^{\widetilde{g}(z^{*})}\left(\frac{|\alpha|e^{\widetilde{g}(z^{*})}}{1-|\alpha|e^{\widetilde{g}(z^{*})}}\right)^{k},&\text{for}\;z<\frac{\beta^{2}\sqrt{T}}{\epsilon^{2}},\\
\left(\frac{|\alpha|}{1-|\alpha|}\right)^{k},&\text{for}\;z\geq\frac{\beta^{2}\sqrt{T}}{\epsilon^{2}},\\
\end{cases}

with
\widetilde{g}(z)=-\epsilon^{2}z^{2}+\beta^{2}\sqrt{T}z,\;z^{*}=\frac{\beta^{2}\sqrt{T}}{2\epsilon^{2}}
.

Proof.

Following the continuous error analysis in Subsection
2.2
, we have for (
3.2
)

\hat{e}^{k}(t)=e^{\left(\beta^{2}\omega^{2}-\epsilon^{2}\omega^{4}\right)t}\hat{e}^{k}(0),

(3.3)

\parallel\hat{e}^{k}(0)\parallel_{l^{2}}\leq\frac{|\alpha|e^{\left(\beta^{2}\omega^{2}-\epsilon^{2}\omega^{4}\right)T}}{1-|\alpha|e^{\left(\beta^{2}\omega^{2}-\epsilon^{2}\omega^{4}\right)T}}\parallel\hat{e}^{k-1}(0)\parallel_{l^{2}},

(3.4)

where
\omega
is a Fourier variable. Observe that the term
\beta^{2}\omega^{2}-\epsilon^{2}\omega^{4}
is not always less than zero. By that we mean for small frequency mode
\omega
the term
\beta^{2}\omega^{2}-\epsilon^{2}\omega^{4}
is grater than zero, given the barrier on the parameter
\beta,\epsilon
. Considering above fact, we define
\widetilde{g}(z)=-\epsilon^{2}z^{2}+\beta^{2}\sqrt{T}z
, where
z=\omega^{2}\sqrt{T}>0
. The function
\widetilde{g}(z)>0
for
z<\frac{\beta^{2}\sqrt{T}}{\epsilon^{2}}
, and having maxima at
z=z^{*}=\frac{\beta^{2}\sqrt{T}}{2\epsilon^{2}}
. The function
\widetilde{g}(z)\leq 0
for
z\geq\frac{\beta^{2}\sqrt{T}}{\epsilon^{2}}
. Finally we get the error estimate by
using Parseval-Plancherel identity on the error solution (
3.3
) and recurrence relation (
3.4
), and
noticing that the function
g_{3}(y)=\frac{|\alpha|e^{y}}{1-|\alpha|e^{y}}
is monotonic increasing.
∎

Theorem 3.2

(Error estimate at semi-discrete level)
.

Let
\textbf{u}^{k}
be the
k-
th semi-discrete iteration of the PinT algorithm (
3.2
) with
|\alpha|<1/2
, and set
\textbf{e}^{k}(t)=\textbf{u}^{k}(t)-\textbf{u}(t)
be the error at
k-
th iterate. Then we have the following linear convergence estimate

\parallel P\textbf{e}^{k}\parallel_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\leq\rho^{k}\parallel P\textbf{e}^{0}\parallel_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))},

where the convergence factor
\rho
is given by

\rho^{k}=\begin{cases}e^{-\widecheck{g}(z^{*})}\left(\frac{|\alpha|e^{-\widecheck{g}(z^{*})}}{1-|\alpha|e^{-\widecheck{g}(z^{*})}}\right)^{k},&\text{for}\;z>-\frac{\beta^{2}T}{\epsilon^{2}},\\
\left(\frac{|\alpha|}{1-|\alpha|}\right)^{k},&\text{for}\;z\leq-\frac{\beta^{2}T}{\epsilon^{2}},\\
\end{cases}

with
\widecheck{g}(z)=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{T}z^{2}+\beta^{2}z,\;z^{*}=-\frac{\beta^{2}T}{2\epsilon^{2}}
.

Proof.

Following the semi-discrete error analysis in Subsection
2.2
, we have for the semi-discrete version of (
3.2
)

\textbf{e}^{k}(t)=\textbf{e}^{-At}\textbf{e}^{k}(0),

(3.5)

\textbf{e}^{k}(0)=\frac{-\alpha\textbf{e}^{-AT}}{1-\alpha\textbf{e}^{-AT}}\textbf{e}^{k-1}(0),

(3.6)

where
A=\beta^{2}\Delta_{h}+\epsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}_{h}
. The eigenvalues of
A
are
\mu_{p}=\beta^{2}\lambda_{p}+\epsilon^{2}\lambda_{p}^{2}
for
p=1,2,\cdots N_{x}
, where
\lambda_{p}
is as defined earlier. Clearly
\mu_{p}
’s are distinct, so
A
is diagonalisable, and can be written as
A=PDP^{-1}
. Thus as earlier it is sufficient to analyze
\max\limits_{r\in\sigma(AT)}W(r)
, where
W(r)=\frac{|\alpha e^{-r}|}{|1-\alpha e^{-r}|}
. Now observe that
\mu_{p}
is not always grater than or equal to zero for all
p
’s, given the condition on
\beta,\epsilon
. So we define
\widecheck{g}(z)=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{T}z^{2}+\beta^{2}z
, where
z=\lambda_{p}T\leq 0
. The function
\widecheck{g}(z)<0
for
z>-\frac{\beta^{2}T}{\epsilon^{2}}
, having minima at
z=z^{*}=-\frac{\beta^{2}T}{2\epsilon^{2}}
. The function
\widecheck{g}(z)\geq 0
for
z\leq-\frac{\beta^{2}T}{\epsilon^{2}}
. Now the result will follow using (
3.6
) on (
3.5
) and observing that the function
g_{4}(y)=\frac{|\alpha|e^{-y}}{1-|\alpha|e^{-y}}
is monotonic decreasing.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Error estimate at fully discrete level)
.

For
\theta=1
and
\theta=1/2
the fully discrete error
\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}=\textbf{u}_{n}^{k}-\textbf{u}_{n}
at
k
-th iteration of the PinT algorithm (
3.2
) with
|\alpha|<1/2
satisfies the following linear convergence estimate

\max\limits_{n=1,2,\cdots,N_{t}}\parallel P\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}\parallel\leq\rho^{k}\parallel P\textbf{e}_{0}^{0}\parallel,\;\rho=\frac{|\alpha|R_{\theta}^{N_{t}}(z^{*})}{1-|\alpha|R_{\theta}^{N_{t}}(z^{*})}.

Proof.

At this point it is clear that analyzing the behaviour of the quantity
\max\limits_{z\in\sigma(\Delta tA)}\widetilde{W}(z)
is enough, where
\widetilde{W}(z)=\frac{|\alpha R_{\theta}^{N_{t}}(z)|}{|1-\alpha R_{\theta}^{N_{t}}(z)|}
. Now the stability function corresponding to
\theta=1
is
R_{1}(z)=\frac{1}{1+\phi(z)}
, where
\phi(z)=-\beta^{2}z+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\Delta t}z^{2}
with
z=-\lambda_{p}\Delta t\geq 0
. The function
R_{1}(z)
is monotonic increasing for
z\leq\frac{\beta^{2}\Delta t}{2\epsilon^{2}}
and monotonic decreasing for
z>\frac{\beta^{2}\Delta t}{2\epsilon^{2}}
. Observing that the function
g_{5}(y)=\frac{|\alpha|y^{N_{t}}}{1-|\alpha|y^{N_{t}}}
is monotonic increasing. So we have
\max\limits_{z\in\sigma(\Delta tA)}\widetilde{W}(z)\leq\frac{|\alpha|R_{1}^{N_{t}}(z^{*})}{1-|\alpha|R_{1}^{N_{t}}(z^{*})}
, where
z^{*}=\frac{\beta^{2}\Delta t}{2\epsilon^{2}}
. For
\theta=1/2
the stability function is
R_{1/2}(z)=\frac{1-\frac{\phi(z)}{2}}{1+\frac{\phi(z)}{2}}
, and a similar analysis holds for
R_{1/2}(z)
and we get the estimate for
\theta=1/2
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

We can extend the above analysis to 2D and 3D at each level.

In 2D, Theorem

also holds for the convergence factor

, where

are Fourier variables in 2D. Similarly one can write the convergence factor

The spectrum of two dimensional discrete operator

(3)

.

(4)

For the general fourth order operator of the form

(3.7)

having the following PinT algorithm

\begin{cases}u_{t}^{k}=-\Delta^{2}u^{k}+\Delta u^{k},&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
u^{k}(x,0)=\alpha u^{k}(x,T)-\alpha u^{k-1}(x,T)+u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\

a similar analysis can be done to get the error estimate at continuous level where the convergence factor

Using the same reasoning as above one can get the error estimate for (

) at semi-discrete and fully discrete level.

4.
The CH Equation

In this section we formulate the iterative PinT algorithm for the CH equation

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta f(u)-\epsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T]\\
\partial_{\nu}u=0=\partial_{\nu}(\Delta u),&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T]\\
u(x,0)=u_{0},&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(4.1)

The PinT algorithm at continuous level for (
4.1
) is based on WR iteration and periodic-like initial condition, as the following:

\begin{cases}u_{t}^{k}=\Delta f(u^{k})-\epsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u^{k},&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
u^{k}(x,0)=\alpha u^{k}(x,T)-\alpha u^{k-1}(x,T)+u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
\end{cases}

(4.2)

where
k\geq 1
is the WR iteration index and
\alpha
is the PinT free parameter. Before formulating the PinT algorithm at fully discrete level, we can observe that non-increasing property of the energy also holds for the algorithm (
4.2
). This means that
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(u^{k})\leq 0
for all
k\geq 1
.

4.1.
Formulation of PinT algorithm

We construct two PinT algorithm for the CH equation (
4.1
) depending on the discretization of the equation (
4.1
). The first one is fully implicit and conditionally gradient stable, with the condition
\Delta t\leq O(\epsilon^{2})
, see
[
9
]
. And the other is an unconditional gradient stable nonlinear splitting scheme of Eyre in
[
12
,
13
]
. Firstly we formulate the PinT algorithm for the fully implicit case.
We discretize the spatial derivatives using central finite difference scheme in an interval with equidistant grid points and homogeneous Neumann boundary condition as shown in (
4.1
). We obtain the following semi-discrete CH equation in (
4.3
) and the corresponding semi-discrete PinT iterative algorithm in (
4.4
)

\begin{cases}\dot{\textbf{u}}(t)=H(t,\textbf{u}),\\
\textbf{u}(0)=\textbf{u}_{0},\end{cases}

(4.3)

\begin{cases}\dot{\textbf{u}}^{k}(t)=H(t,\textbf{u}^{k}),\\
\textbf{u}^{k}(0)=\alpha\textbf{u}^{k}(T)-\alpha\textbf{u}^{k-1}(T)+\textbf{u}_{0},\end{cases}

(4.4)

where
H(t,\textbf{u}):\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}}
is given by
H(t,\textbf{u})=\Delta_{h}f(\textbf{u})-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}\textbf{u}
. Applying backward-Euler in time in (
4.3
), we have the following fully discrete scheme in (
4.5
) and the corresponding fully discrete PinT iterative algorithm in (
4.6
)

\begin{cases}\frac{\textbf{u}_{n}-\textbf{u}_{n-1}}{\Delta t}=H(t_{n},\textbf{u}_{n}),\\
\textbf{u}(t_{0})=\textbf{u}_{0},\end{cases}

(4.5)

\begin{cases}\frac{\textbf{u}_{n}^{k}-\textbf{u}_{n-1}^{k}}{\Delta t}=H(t_{n},\textbf{u}_{n}^{k}),\\
\textbf{u}^{k}_{0}=\alpha\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k}-\alpha\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k-1}+\textbf{u}_{0}.\end{cases}

(4.6)

By gathering all the space-time points in (
4.6
), we have the following nonlinear all-at-once or nonlinear space-time system of
\textbf{U}^{k}
as

\left(C_{1}^{(\alpha)}\otimes I_{x}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}=\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\widetilde{f}(\textbf{U}^{k})-\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}-\epsilon^{2}\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}^{2}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}+\textbf{b}^{k-1},

(4.7)

where
I_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{t}\times N_{t}}
is the identity matrix,
\widetilde{f}(\textbf{U}^{k}):=\left((\textbf{u}_{1}^{k})^{3},(\textbf{u}_{2}^{k})^{3},\cdots,(\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k})^{3}\right)^{T}
and

\textbf{b}^{k-1}=\left(\frac{I_{x}}{\Delta t}\left(\textbf{u}_{0}-\alpha\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k-1}\right),0,\cdots,0\right)^{T}.

To solve the nonlinear system (
4.7
), we apply the quasi-Newton method on
G(\textbf{U}^{k})
, where

G(\textbf{U}^{k}):=\left(C_{1}^{(\alpha)}\otimes I_{x}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}-\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\widetilde{f}(\textbf{U}^{k})+\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}+\epsilon^{2}\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}^{2}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}-\textbf{b}^{k-1}.

(4.8)

We define the following matrix

J(\textbf{U}^{k}):=\begin{bmatrix}J_{s}(\textbf{u}_{1}^{k})&&&&\\
&J_{s}(\textbf{u}_{2}^{k})&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&&J_{s}(\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k})\end{bmatrix},

(4.9)

where
J_{s}(\textbf{u}_{n}^{k}):=\diag((3u_{n}^{1,k})^{2},\cdots,(3u_{n}^{N_{x},k})^{2})
, for
n=1,\cdots,N_{t}
. Now we write the Jacobian of
G
as the following

G^{\prime}(\textbf{U}^{k})=\left(C_{1}^{(\alpha)}\otimes I_{x}\right)-\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)J(\textbf{U}^{k})+\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)+\epsilon^{2}\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}^{2}\right).

(4.10)

Now the quasi-Newton method to solve (
4.8
) becomes computing the following iteration for
m=1,2,\cdots

\textbf{U}^{k}_{m}=\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1}-(\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1}))^{-1}G(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1}),

(4.11)

where
\widetilde{G}^{\prime}
is an approximation to the Jacobian
G^{\prime}
. The approximated Jacobian
\widetilde{G}^{\prime}
has the following form

\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(\textbf{U}^{k})=\left(C_{1}^{(\alpha)}\otimes I_{x}\right)-\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\widetilde{J}(\textbf{U}^{k})\right)+\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)+\epsilon^{2}\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}^{2}\right),

(4.12)

where
\widetilde{J}(\textbf{U}^{k}):=I_{t}\otimes\left(\frac{1}{N_{t}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{t}}J_{s}(\textbf{u}_{n}^{k})\right)
.
This process of approximating the Jacobian has been successfully applied in
[
16
,
18
,
21
]
. We rewrite (
4.11
) using (
4.8
) and (
4.12
) to get the following

\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})\textbf{U}^{k}_{m}=\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\widetilde{f}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})-\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\widetilde{J}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})\right)\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1}+\textbf{b}^{k-1}.

(4.13)

We use the diagonalizability of
C_{1}^{(\alpha)}
and the tensor product to factor the system
\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})
as
(V\otimes I_{x})(D_{1}\otimes I_{x}-I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\widetilde{J}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})+I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}+\epsilon^{2}I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}^{2})(V^{-1}\otimes I_{x})
, and then we solve (
4.13
) by performing the following three steps

\displaystyle\text{Step}-(1)

\displaystyle S_{1}=\left(\mathbb{F}\otimes I_{x}\right)\left(\Gamma_{\alpha}\otimes I_{x}\right)\textbf{r}^{k}_{m-1},

(4.14)

\displaystyle\text{Step}-(2)

\displaystyle S_{2,n}=\left(\lambda_{1,n}I_{x}-\Delta_{h}\widetilde{J}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})+\Delta_{h}+\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}\right)^{-1}S_{1,n},\;n=1,2,\cdots N_{t},

\displaystyle\text{Step}-(3)

\displaystyle\textbf{U}_{m}^{k}=\left(\Gamma_{\alpha}^{-1}\otimes I_{x}\right)\left(\mathbb{F}^{*}\otimes I_{x}\right)S_{2},

where
\textbf{r}^{k}_{m-1}:=\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\widetilde{f}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})-\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\widetilde{J}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})\right)\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1}+\textbf{b}^{k-1}
, and
D_{1},S_{1},S_{2},\lambda_{1,n}
are defined earlier. We call this algorithm PinT-I.

Next we formulate the PinT algorithm for the CH equation (
4.1
) using the nonlinear splitting scheme of Eyre
[
12
,
13
]
. The idea here is to split the homogeneous free
energy
F(u)
into the sum of a convex term and a concave term, and then treating the convex term implicitly and the concave term explicitly to obtain a nonlinear unconditionally stable first order in time and second order in space accurate approximation of the CH equation. The iterative PinT algorithm at fully discrete level corresponding to nonlinear splitting is the following

\begin{cases}\frac{\textbf{u}_{n}^{k}-\textbf{u}_{n-1}^{k}}{\Delta t}=\Delta_{h}(\textbf{u}_{n}^{k})^{3}-\Delta_{h}\textbf{u}_{n-1}^{k}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}\textbf{u}_{n}^{k},\\
\textbf{u}^{k}_{0}=\alpha\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k}-\alpha\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k-1}+\textbf{u}_{0}.\end{cases}

(4.15)

By gathering all the space-time points in (
4.15
), we have the following nonlinear all-at-once system of
\textbf{U}^{k}
as

\left(C_{1}^{(\alpha)}\otimes I_{x}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}=\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\widetilde{f}(\textbf{U}^{k})-\left(C_{3}^{(\alpha)}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}-\epsilon^{2}\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}^{2}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}+\textbf{b}_{0}^{k-1},

(4.16)

where
C_{3}^{(\alpha)}
is the matrix
C_{2}^{(\alpha)}
for
\theta=0
in (
2.7
), and
\textbf{b}_{0}^{k-1}=\left((\frac{I_{x}}{\Delta t}-\Delta_{h})\left(\textbf{u}_{0}-\alpha\textbf{u}_{N_{t}}^{k-1}\right),0,\cdots,0\right)^{T}.

To solve the nonlinear system (
4.16
), we apply the quasi-Newton method on
Q(\textbf{U}^{k})
, where

Q(\textbf{U}^{k}):=\left(C_{1}^{(\alpha)}\otimes I_{x}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}-\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\widetilde{f}(\textbf{U}^{k})+\left(C_{3}^{(\alpha)}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}+\epsilon^{2}\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}^{2}\right)\textbf{U}^{k}-\textbf{b}_{0}^{k-1}.

(4.17)

The Jacobian of
Q
is the following

Q^{\prime}(\textbf{U}^{k})=\left(C_{1}^{(\alpha)}\otimes I_{x}\right)-\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)J(\textbf{U}^{k})+\left(C_{3}^{(\alpha)}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)+\epsilon^{2}\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}^{2}\right).

(4.18)

The quasi-Newton method to solve (
4.17
) consists of the following iteration for
m=1,2,\cdots

\textbf{U}^{k}_{m}=\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1}-(\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1}))^{-1}Q(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1}),

(4.19)

where
\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}
is an approximation to the Jacobian
Q^{\prime}
in (
4.18
). The approximated Jacobian
\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}
has the following form

\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}(\textbf{U}^{k})=\left(C_{1}^{(\alpha)}\otimes I_{x}\right)-\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\widetilde{J}(\textbf{U}^{k})\right)+\left(C_{3}^{(\alpha)}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)+\epsilon^{2}\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}^{2}\right).

(4.20)

Rewriting (
4.19
) using (
4.17
) and (
4.20
) yields

\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})\textbf{U}^{k}_{m}=\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\widetilde{f}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})-\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\widetilde{J}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})\right)\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1}+\textbf{b}_{0}^{k-1}.

(4.21)

We use the diagonalizability of
C_{1}^{(\alpha)},C_{3}^{(\alpha)}
and the tensor product to factor the system
\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})
as
(V\otimes I_{x})(D_{1}\otimes I_{x}-I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\widetilde{J}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})+D_{3}\otimes\Delta_{h}+\epsilon^{2}I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}^{2})(V^{-1}\otimes I_{x})
, and then we solve (
4.21
) by performing usual three steps

\displaystyle\text{Step}-(1)

\displaystyle S_{1}=\left(\mathbb{F}\otimes I_{x}\right)\left(\Gamma_{\alpha}\otimes I_{x}\right)\textbf{r}^{k}_{m-1},

(4.22)

\displaystyle\text{Step}-(2)

\displaystyle S_{2,n}=\left(\lambda_{1,n}I_{x}-\Delta_{h}\widetilde{J}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})+\lambda_{3,n}\Delta_{h}+\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}\right)^{-1}S_{1,n},\;n=1,2,\cdots N_{t},

\displaystyle\text{Step}-(3)

\displaystyle\textbf{U}_{m}^{k}=\left(\Gamma_{\alpha}^{-1}\otimes I_{x}\right)\left(\mathbb{F}^{*}\otimes I_{x}\right)S_{2},

where
\textbf{r}^{k}_{m-1}:=\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\right)\widetilde{f}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})-\left(I_{t}\otimes\Delta_{h}\widetilde{J}(\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1})\right)\textbf{U}^{k}_{m-1}+\textbf{b}_{0}^{k-1}
,
D_{3}=\diag(\lambda_{3,1},\cdots\\
,\lambda_{3,N_{t}})
,
\lambda_{3,n}=\alpha^{\frac{1}{N_{t}}}e^{-i\frac{2n\pi}{N_{t}}}
and
D_{1},S_{1},S_{2},\lambda_{1,n}
are defined earlier. We call this algorithm PinT-II.

Remark 4.1
.

One can extend the algorithm (
4.14
) and (

) to 2D and 3D in a natural way.

4.2.
Convergence analysis for the nonlinear CH equation

In this section we present the convergence result for the PinT algorithm (
4.2
). First we discuss the convergence at continuous setting.

Theorem 4.2

(Error estimate at continuous level)
.

Let
{u}^{k}
be the
k
-th iterate of the PinT algorithm (
4.2
), and
u_{0},u_{0}^{0}\in L^{2}
. Then
{u}^{k}
converges to
u
of (
4.1
) in
L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{2})
.

Proof.

Let
e^{k}(x,t)=u^{k}(x,t)-u(x,t)
be the error at
k
-th iteration, then we have the following error equation

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial e^{k}}{\partial t}=\Delta\left(f(u^{k})-f(u)\right)-\epsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}e^{k},&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
e^{k}(x,0)=\alpha(e^{k}(x,T)-e^{k-1}(x,T)),&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(4.23)

Multiplying the 1st equation (
4.23
) with
e^{k}
and integrate over the domain
\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\left(f(u^{k})-f(u)\right)\nabla e^{k}-\epsilon^{2}\parallel\Delta e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}

(4.24)

\displaystyle\leq M\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{2}\parallel\nabla e^{k}\parallel_{2}-\epsilon^{2}\parallel\Delta e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{2}\left(\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}+\parallel\nabla e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}\right)-\epsilon^{2}\parallel\Delta e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{2}\left(\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}+\parallel\Delta e^{k}\parallel_{2}\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{2}\right)-\epsilon^{2}\parallel\Delta e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{*}\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\parallel\Delta e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}\leq C^{*}\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2},

where in the first inequality we use Lipschitz condition of
f
, in the second inequality we use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, in the third inequality we use the inequality
\parallel\nabla e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}\leq\parallel\Delta e^{k}\parallel_{2}\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{2}
, in the fourth inequality we use the fact that the term
\frac{M}{2}\parallel\Delta e^{k}\parallel_{2}\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{2}\leq\frac{M^{2}}{8\epsilon^{2}}\parallel e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\parallel\Delta e^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}
, and
C^{*}=\left(\frac{M}{2}+\frac{M^{2}}{8\epsilon^{2}}\right)
.
From (
4.24
) we get the following

\parallel e^{k}(.,t)\parallel_{2}\leq\parallel e^{k}_{0}\parallel_{2}e^{C^{*}t}.

(4.25)

Now taking norm on the 2nd equation of (
4.23
) and using the inequality (
4.25
) at
t=T
we obtain the following recurrence relation

\parallel e^{k}_{0}\parallel_{2}\leq\gamma\parallel e^{k-1}_{0}\parallel_{2},\text{where}\;\gamma=\frac{|\alpha|e^{C^{*}T}}{1-|\alpha|e^{C^{*}T}}.

(4.26)

We consider the sum
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\parallel e^{k}(.,T)\parallel_{2}
and show that sum is independent of
k
for
|\alpha|<\frac{1}{2e^{C^{*}T}}
. Using (
4.26
) in (
4.25
) at
t=T
we get the following

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\parallel e^{k}(.,T)\parallel_{2}\leq e^{C^{*}T}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\parallel e^{k}_{0}\parallel_{2}\leq e^{C^{*}T}\parallel e^{0}_{0}\parallel_{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma^{k}.

(4.27)

For
|\alpha|<\frac{1}{2e^{C^{*}T}}
we have
\gamma<1
, so the series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}
is convergent. Hence
from (
4.27
) we have
\parallel e^{k}(.,T)\parallel_{2}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Hence we have the result.
∎

Next we show the convergence behaviour of the PinT algorithm (
4.4
) for the CH equation in the semi-discrete setting. We assume
f
to be Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
M
in (
1.4
) and we know Lipschitz implies one-sided Lipschitz. So in the semi-discrete context, we get
f
to be one-sided Lipschitz satisfying

\langle f(\textbf{u}_{1})-f(\textbf{u}_{2}),\textbf{u}_{1}-\textbf{u}_{2}\rangle\leq M\parallel\textbf{u}_{1}-\textbf{u}_{2}\parallel_{2}^{2},\forall t\in[0,T],\textbf{u}_{1},\textbf{u}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}},

(4.28)

where
\langle\cdot\rangle
is the Euclidean inner product.
Now using (
4.28
) and the fact that
\frac{\langle\Delta_{h}^{2}y,y\rangle}{\parallel y\parallel_{2}^{2}}=\lambda_{p}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{x}}}\parallel\Delta_{h}^{2}\parallel_{\infty}
,
\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}}
, it is easy to see that
H
is also one-sided Lipschitz, satisfying the following one-sided Lipschitz condition

\langle H(t,\textbf{u}_{1})-H(t,\textbf{u}_{2}),\textbf{u}_{1}-\textbf{u}_{2}\rangle\leq L^{*}\parallel\textbf{u}_{1}-\textbf{u}_{2}\parallel_{2}^{2},\forall t\in[0,T],\textbf{u}_{1},\textbf{u}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}},

(4.29)

where
L^{*}=M\parallel\Delta_{h}\parallel_{\infty}-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\sqrt{N_{x}}}\parallel\Delta_{h}^{2}\parallel_{\infty}=\frac{4M}{h^{2}}-\frac{16\epsilon^{2}}{{\sqrt{N_{x}}}h^{4}}<0
. Observe that
L^{*}<0
imply
h^{3/2}<\frac{4\epsilon^{2}}{M}
by taking
N_{x}\sim O(\frac{1}{h})
. As
L^{*}<0
, we take
L^{*}=-L
, where
L=-M\parallel\Delta_{h}\parallel_{\infty}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\sqrt{N_{x}}}\parallel\Delta_{h}^{2}\parallel_{\infty}>0
. Then the revised one-sided Lipschitz condition for
H
is the following

\langle H(t,\textbf{u}_{1})-H(t,\textbf{u}_{2}),\textbf{u}_{1}-\textbf{u}_{2}\rangle\leq-L\parallel\textbf{u}_{1}-\textbf{u}_{2}\parallel_{2}^{2},\forall t\in[0,T],\textbf{u}_{1},\textbf{u}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}}.

(4.30)

Theorem 4.3

(Error estimate at semi-discrete level)
.

Let
\textbf{u}^{k}
be the
k
-th iteration of the PinT algorithm (
4.4
) with
|\alpha|<1
, and
H(t,\textbf{u})
satisfies one-sided Lipschitz condition (
4.30
) with the constant
L>0
defined above. Then the semi-discrete error
\textbf{e}^{k}(t)=\textbf{u}^{k}(t)-\textbf{u}(t)
at
k
-th iteration satisfies the following convergence estimate

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\parallel\textbf{e}^{k}(t)\parallel_{2}\leq\left(\frac{|\alpha|e^{-LT}}{1-|\alpha|e^{-LT}}\right)^{k}\parallel\textbf{e}^{0}(0)\parallel_{2}.

(4.31)

Proof.

The proof follows from the Theorem 5.1 in
[
18
]
.
∎

We now show the convergence behaviour of the PinT algorithm (
4.6
) for the CH equation at fully discrete level. Let
\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}=\textbf{u}_{n}^{k}-\textbf{u}_{n}
be the error vector at each PinT iteration.

Theorem 4.4

(Error estimate at fully discrete level)
.

The fully discrete error
\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}
at
k-
th iterate of the PinT algorithm (
4.6
) with
|\alpha|<1
satisfies the following convergence estimate

\max\limits_{n=1,2,\cdots,N_{t}}\parallel\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}\parallel_{2}\leq\rho^{k}\parallel\textbf{e}_{0}^{0}\parallel_{2},\rho=\frac{|\alpha|C_{1}}{1-|\alpha|C_{1}},

where the explicit expression of
C_{1}
is given in (
4.36
).

Proof.

From (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), the error
\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}
satisfies

\begin{cases}\frac{\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}-\textbf{e}_{n-1}^{k}}{\Delta t}=H(t_{n},\textbf{u}_{n}^{k})-H(t_{n},\textbf{u}_{n}),&n=1,2,\cdots,N_{t}\\
\textbf{e}_{0}^{k}=\alpha(\textbf{e}_{N_{t}}^{k}-\textbf{e}_{N_{t}}^{k-1}).\end{cases}

(4.32)

For the Euclidean inner product, we have

\left\langle\frac{\textbf{e}_{n}-\textbf{e}_{n-1}}{\Delta t},\textbf{e}_{n}\right\rangle\geq\frac{\parallel\textbf{e}_{n}\parallel_{2}^{2}-\parallel\textbf{e}_{n-1}\parallel_{2}^{2}}{2\Delta t},

(4.33)

for the error vector
\textbf{e}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}}
, for all
n=1,2,\cdots,N_{t}
.
Taking inner product in (
4.32
) with
\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}
, we get

\left\langle\frac{\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}-\textbf{e}_{n-1}^{k}}{\Delta t},\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}\right\rangle=\left\langle H(t_{n},\textbf{u}_{n}^{k})-H(t_{n},\textbf{u}_{n}),\textbf{u}_{n}^{k}-\textbf{u}_{n}\right\rangle.

(4.34)

Then using the inequality (
4.33
) and
one-sided
Lipschitz condition (
4.30
) for discrete time points in the equation (
4.34
) we obtain

\frac{\parallel\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}-\parallel\textbf{e}_{n-1}^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}}{2\Delta t}\leq-L\parallel\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}\parallel_{2}^{2}

(4.35)

Inequality (
4.35
) yields

\parallel\textbf{e}_{N_{t}}^{k}\parallel_{2}\leq\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{1+2L\Delta t}\right)^{N_{t}}}\parallel\textbf{e}_{0}^{k}\parallel_{2}=C_{1}\parallel\textbf{e}_{0}^{k}\parallel_{2}.

(4.36)

Now from the second equation of (
4.32
) we have
\parallel\textbf{e}_{0}^{k}\parallel_{2}=|\alpha|\parallel\textbf{e}_{N_{t}}^{k}\parallel_{2}+|\alpha|\parallel\textbf{e}_{N_{t}}^{k-1}\parallel_{2}\leq|\alpha|C_{1}\parallel\textbf{e}_{0}^{k}\parallel_{2}+|\alpha|C_{1}\parallel\textbf{e}_{0}^{k-1}\parallel_{2}
, where on the second inequality we use (
4.36
) twice. Hence we have

\parallel\textbf{e}_{0}^{k}\parallel_{2}\leq\frac{|\alpha|C_{1}}{1-|\alpha|C_{1}}\parallel\textbf{e}_{0}^{k-1}\parallel_{2}

(4.37)

From (
4.33
), we have
\max\limits_{n=1,2,\cdots,N_{t}}\parallel\textbf{e}_{n}^{k}\parallel_{2}\leq\parallel\textbf{e}_{0}^{0}\parallel_{2}
and together with (
4.37
) gives the required convergence estimate.
∎

6.
Conclusions

We formulated and studied time parallel algorithms for a class of fourth order PDEs. We present rigorous convergence analysis for all the proposed PinT methods. We observe robust convergence behaviour for linear as well as nonlinear models.

Inelastic dark matter, small scale problems, and the XENON1T excess
Seungwon Baek

Abstract
We study a generic model in which the dark sector is composed of a Majorana dark matter χ 1 \chi_{1} , its excited state χ 2 \chi_{2} , both at the electroweak scale, and a light dark photon Z ′ Z^{\prime} with m Z ′ ∼ 10 − 4 m_{Z^{\prime}}\sim 10^{-4} eV. The light Z ′ Z^{\prime} enhances the self-scattering elastic cross section χ 1 ​ χ 1 → χ 1 ​ χ 1 \chi_{1}\chi_{1}\to\chi_{1}\chi_{1} enough to solve the small scale problems in the N N -body simulations with the cold dark matter. The dark matter communicates with the SM via kinetic mixing parameterized by ϵ \epsilon . The inelastic scattering process χ 1 ​ χ 1 → χ 2 ​ χ 2 \chi_{1}\chi_{1}\to\chi_{2}\chi_{2} followed by the prompt decay χ 2 → χ 1 ​ Z ′ \chi_{2}\to\chi_{1}Z^{\prime} generates energetic Z ′ Z^{\prime} . By setting δ ≡ m χ 2 − m χ 1 ≃ 2.8 \delta\equiv m_{\chi_{2}}-m_{\chi_{1}}\simeq 2.8 keV and ϵ ∼ 10 − 10 \epsilon\sim 10^{-10} the excess in the electron-recoil data at the XENON1T experiment can be explained by the dark-photoelectric effect. The relic abundance of the dark matter can also be accommodated by the thermal freeze-out mechanism via the annihilation χ 1 ​ χ 1 ​ ( χ 2 ​ χ 2 ) → Z ′ ​ Z ′ \chi_{1}\chi_{1}(\chi_{2}\chi_{2})\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime} with the dark gauge coupling constant α X ∼ 10 − 3 \alpha_{X}\sim 10^{-3} .

Introduction

There are clear evidences that dark matter (DM) exists in the universe,
although its particle nature is almost unknown.
Since the standard model (SM) lacks candidate for the dark matter, new physics (NP) models
are required to incorporate DM.
The standard cold dark matter (CDM) model has been very successful in predicting the large scale structure of the universe.
However, the
N
-body simulations with CDM predict cuspy density profiles, while observations of rotation curves of dwarf galaxies
and low surface brightness galaxies point towards flat cores. Self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) with self-scattering cross sections,
\sigma_{\chi\chi}/m_{\chi}\sim 1\,\rm{cm^{2}/g}
, can be
a possible solution to this problem
Tulin:2017ara
. The constraint,
\sigma_{\chi\chi}/m_{\chi}\lesssim 0.5\,{\rm cm^{2}/g}
, coming from collision of galaxy clusters
Harvey:2015hha

can be evaded by the SIDM’s velocity-dependent cross sections.
Also the non-observation of DM in the DM-nucleon scattering experiments can be naturally explained in the inelastic DM models
Blennow:2016gde
.

In the year 2020, XENON1T collaboration has announced an excess of electron recoil near
2-3
keV energy
Aprile:2020tmw
.
Although the result can be explained by
\beta
decays of tritium contamination, it can also be attributed to
NP contributions such as the solar axion or anomalous neutrino magnetic moment with about 3
\sigma
significance.
However, the latter possibilities are in strong tension with the star cooling constraints
Aprile:2020tmw
.
The excess may also be the result of the dark matter scattering with electrons inside the xenon atom.
In this case the signal can give valuable information on the nature of the dark matter.

The XENON1T excess has been considered in various inelastic DM models

In

we studied exothermic DM scattering on electron to explain the anomaly.
We found that both scalar and fermionic DM models can accommodate the XENON1T excess.

In this paper we consider another possible realization of inelastic DM.

U(1)_{X}

A
U(1)_{X}

For the inelastic dark matter we study the Lagrangian of the dark sector (DS) including the kinetic energy

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm SM}-\frac{1}{4}\hat{X}_{\mu\nu}\hat{X}^{\mu\nu}+{1\over 2}m_{\hat{X}}^{2}\hat{X}_{\mu}\hat{X}^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\epsilon}\,\hat{X}_{\mu\nu}\hat{B}^{\mu\nu}+{1\over 2}m_{\hat{Z}}^{2}\hat{Z}_{\mu}\hat{Z}^{\mu}

\displaystyle+\overline{\chi}(i\not{D}-m_{\chi})\chi-{\delta\over 4}(\overline{\chi^{c}}\chi+h.c.),

\hat{X}_{\mu}

Baek:2020owl

U(1)_{Y}

\hat{\epsilon}\ll 1
.
We do not specify the origin of the dark gauge boson mass

\hat{\epsilon}\sim 10^{-10}

So it suffices to keep only the linear terms in
\hat{\epsilon}

In this case we get
Babu:1997st

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{W}A_{\mu}-s_{W}Z_{\mu}-\hat{\epsilon}c_{W}^{2}Z^{\prime}_{\mu},

\displaystyle\hat{W}^{3}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{W}A_{\mu}+c_{W}Z_{\mu}-\hat{\epsilon}c_{W}s_{W}Z^{\prime}_{\mu},

\displaystyle\hat{X}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}_{\mu}+\hat{\epsilon}s_{W}Z_{\mu},

(2)

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\chi_{2}+i\chi_{1}),

\displaystyle\chi^{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\chi_{2}-i\chi_{1}),

(4)

\displaystyle\chi_{1}^{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{1},\ \ \ \chi_{2}^{c}=\chi_{2},

(5)

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle-ig_{X}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\overline{\chi_{2}}\gamma^{\mu}\chi_{1}-\hat{\epsilon}ec_{W}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\overline{e}\gamma^{\mu}e.

\chi_{1}\leftrightarrow\chi_{2}

3
The relic abundance

In the early universe the dark sector (DS) can be in thermal equilibrium with the SM sector via process such as

Z^{\prime}f\rightleftharpoons\gamma(Z,g)f
, and
Z^{\prime}\gamma(Z,g)\rightleftharpoons f\overline{f}
with
f
a SM fermion.
In this case the Boltzmann equation for the DM number density reads
Griest:1990kh

\displaystyle\frac{dn}{dt}+3Hn=-\sum_{i,j=1,2}\langle\sigma_{ij}v\rangle(n_{i}n_{j}-n_{i}^{\rm eq}n_{j}^{\rm eq}),

(8)

where
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
with
n_{i}\equiv n_{\chi_{i}}
.
Following the procedure in
Griest:1990kh
, we obtain
the freeze-out temperature
T_{f}
of the DM by solving

\displaystyle x_{f}=\log\frac{0.0382\,g_{1}m_{1}M_{\rm pl}x_{f}^{1/2}\langle\sigma_{\rm eff}v\rangle}{g_{*}^{1/2}},

(9)

where
x_{f}\equiv m_{1}/T_{f}
with
m_{1}\equiv m_{\chi_{1}}
and
M_{\rm pl}\simeq 1.22\times 10^{19}
GeV is the Planck mass. The effective thermal-averaged cross section

\langle\sigma_{\rm eff}v\rangle
is obtained by

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{\rm eff}v\rangle=\sum_{i,j=1,2}\langle\sigma_{ij}v\rangle r_{i}r_{j},\quad r_{i}=\frac{g_{i}(1+\Delta_{i})^{3/2}e^{-x_{f}\Delta_{i}}}{\sum_{i=1,2}g_{i}(1+\Delta_{i})^{3/2}e^{-x_{f}\Delta_{i}}},

(10)

where
\Delta_{i}=(m_{i}-m_{1})/m_{1}
.
Since
\Delta_{2}(=\delta/m_{1})\ll 1
,
x_{f}\Delta_{2}\ll 1
, and
\sigma_{11}\simeq\sigma_{22}\gg\sigma_{12}
in our scenario, we can approximate

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{\rm eff}v\rangle\simeq{1\over 2}\langle\sigma_{11}v\rangle.

(11)

Explicitly we get the dominant
s
-wave contributions to the DM annihilations to be

\displaystyle\sigma_{ii}v

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sigma v(\chi_{i}\chi_{i}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})\simeq\frac{\pi\alpha_{X}^{2}}{m_{1}^{2}}+O(v^{2}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{ij}v

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sigma v(\chi_{i}\chi_{j}\to f\overline{f})\simeq\frac{2\pi\epsilon^{2}N_{c}^{f}\alpha_{\rm em}\alpha_{X}(2m_{1}^{2}+m_{f}^{2})(m_{1}^{2}-m_{f}^{2})^{1/2}}{9m_{1}^{5}}+O(v^{2}),

(12)

where
i\not=j
(
i,j=1,2
),
N_{c}^{f}
is the color factor of
f
,
\epsilon\equiv\hat{\epsilon}c_{W}
, and we used
m_{Z^{\prime}},\delta\ll m_{1}
.
We see that
\sigma_{ij}v
(
i\not=j
) is
\epsilon^{2}
-suppressed and negligible compared to
\sigma_{ii}v
.

Let us comment on a possible issue in the annihilation cross section. At high energy,
s\to\infty
, the cross section,
\sigma(\chi_{i}\chi_{i}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})
, behaves like

\displaystyle\sigma(\chi_{i}\chi_{i}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})=\frac{\alpha_{X}^{2}\pi\delta^{2}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{4}}+O\left(1\over s\right),

(13)

which violates the perturbative unitarity. So we need a UV completion of (
1
) to cure this problem.
For example, we can introduce Higgs field(s) coupled to
\chi_{i}
. In this UV completion the Higgs mediated contribution
to
\chi_{i}\chi_{i}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
is
p
-wave, and does not change the
s
-wave term in (
12
).
The resulting relic density obtained from
s
-wave contribution only from (
12
) has corrections of order
O(3/x_{f})\simeq O(0.1)
.
So we can take (
1
) as a leading effective
Lagrangian for
\chi_{i}
and
Z^{\prime}
for a large class of microscopic theories where other NP particles are integrated out.

By solving the Boltzmann equation (
8
) with the condition (
9
) the final relic density from
s
-wave only in (
12
) is obtained to be

\displaystyle\Omega h^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2\times 1.038\times 10^{19}\,x_{f}\;{\rm GeV}^{-1}}{g_{*S}(T_{f})/g_{*}^{1/2}(T_{f})M_{\rm pl}\langle\sigma_{11}v\rangle}

\displaystyle\approx 0.12\left(3.59\times 10^{-3}\over\alpha_{X}\right)^{2}\left(m_{\chi_{1}}\over 100\,{\rm GeV}\right)^{2},

(14)

where
g_{*S}
and
g_{*}
are defined in
Kolb:1988aj
.

4
The small scale problem

Given a DM with mass
m_{1}
, the value of the dark gauge coupling constant
\alpha_{X}
to yield the correct relic abundance can be predicted
from (
14
). When these two parameters
m_{1}
and
\alpha_{X}

and the DP mass
m_{Z^{\prime}}
are known,
we can calculate the elastic scattering,
\chi_{1}\chi_{1}\to\chi_{1}\chi_{1}
, and inelastic scattering,
\chi_{1}\chi_{1}\to\chi_{2}\chi_{2}
, cross sections for a given DM relative velocity
v
. Since for
m_{Z^{\prime}}\ll m_{1}
the non-perturbative Sommerfeld effect becomes
significant, the perturbative
calculation cannot be applied here. Following the Ref.
Blennow:2016gde
, we solve the corresponding Schrödinger equation
in the CM-frame to calculate the two cross sections,

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{m_{1}}+V(\vec{r})\right]\Psi(\vec{r})=\frac{k^{2}}{m_{1}}\Psi(\vec{r}),

(15)

where
\Psi
is the
2\times 1
matrix wavefunction for the DM states with the upper component for the
\chi_{1}\chi_{1}
state and the lower component for the

\chi_{2}\chi_{2}
state,
\vec{r}=\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2}
is the relative spatial coordinate of colliding DM particles, and
\vec{k}=m_{1}\vec{v}/2
is the relative momentum with
\vec{v}
the
relative velocity.
The potential is written in the
2\times 2
-matrix form,

\displaystyle V(r)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\frac{\alpha_{X}}{r}e^{-m_{Z^{\prime}}r}\\
-\frac{\alpha_{X}}{r}e^{-m_{Z^{\prime}}r}&2\delta\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(16)

As in
Blennow:2016gde

Ershov:2011zz

(
15

The system of coupled radial equations in (
15

The numerical stability is enhanced by using the modified variable phase method presented in
Ershov:2011zz
.
We introduce dimensionless parameters
Blennow:2016gde

\displaystyle a=\frac{k}{\alpha_{X}m_{1}},\quad b=\frac{\alpha_{X}m_{1}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}},\quad c=\sqrt{a^{2}-{2\delta\over\alpha_{X}^{2}m_{1}}},\quad x=m_{1}\alpha_{X}r,

(17)

15

\displaystyle\Bigg[{d^{2}\over dx^{2}}-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{x^{2}}+\begin{pmatrix}a^{2}&0\\
0&c^{2}\end{pmatrix}\Bigg]\chi(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-{1\over x}e^{-x/b}\\
-{1\over x}e^{-x/b}&0\end{pmatrix}\chi(x),

where
\chi(x)=xR(x)

\Psi(\vec{r})=R(r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)

2\times 2

\displaystyle\chi_{ij}(x)=\big(f(p_{i}x)\delta_{ik}-h^{(+)}(p_{i}x)M_{ik}(x)\big)\alpha_{kj}(x),

(19)

where
i,j,k=1,2

i,j

We use
f(x)=xj_{\ell}(x),h^{(+)}(x)=ixh^{(1)}_{\ell}(x)

Here
j_{\ell}(x)
(
h_{\ell}^{(1)}(x)

The boundary condition
\chi_{ij}(x=0)=0

M_{ij}(x=0)=0

We can write the scattering amplitude in terms of the matrix
M(x=\infty)

\displaystyle f(\theta)=-i\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)P_{\ell}(\cos\theta)\begin{pmatrix}{1\over 2k}&0\\

The differential cross sections for the scattering of identical particles are obtained by

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_{\xi}=\left|f(\theta)+\xi f(\pi-\theta)\right|^{2},

(21)

where
\xi=+1(-1)

Assuming the DM is unpolarized, we average over the spin states to get

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}={1\over 4}\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_{\xi=+1}+{3\over 4}\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_{\xi=-1},

(22)

where the first (the second) term is the contribution from the spin singlet (triplet) state with symmetric (antisymmetric) spatial wave funtion.
Then the inelastic scattering cross section is obtained by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm inel}(\chi_{1}\chi_{1}\to\chi_{2}\chi_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{k^{\prime}\over 2k}\int\left[{1\over 4}\left|f_{21}(\theta)+f_{21}(\pi-\theta)\right|^{2}+{3\over 4}\left|f_{21}(\theta)-f_{21}(\pi-\theta)\right|^{2}\right]d\Omega

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{kk^{\prime}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{\ell}(2\ell+1)\left|M^{\ell}_{21}(x=\infty)\right|^{2},

6
Conclusions

Although the existence of dark matter is well-established, its particle nature is almost unknown.
The small scale problem and the recent observation of the excess in electron-recoil at XENON1T experiment may reveal the nature of dark matter.
We studied a dark matter model which can address these issues while explaining its abundance in the universe.

In the model the Majorana dark matter candidate
\chi_{1}
has its excited partner
\chi_{2}
with mass difference
\delta\simeq 2.8
keV.
The dark mass is about
100
GeV and it can explain the current relic density by the thermal freeze-out mechanism whose main annihilation
process is
\chi_{1}\chi_{1}(\chi_{2}\chi_{2})\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
. We find that the necessary dark gauge coupling is
\alpha_{X}\sim 10^{-3}
.

The light
Z^{\prime}
can also mediate (in)elastic scattering
\chi_{1}\chi_{1}\to\chi_{1(2)}\chi_{1(2)}
. We solve the Schrödinger equation numerically to calculate
the cross sections. We get the elastic cross section large enough to explain the small scale problems
\sigma_{\rm el}/m_{\chi_{1}}\sim 1\;{\rm cm^{2}/g}
.

The dark sector can communicate with the SM sector through kinetic mixing parameterized by
\epsilon\sim 10^{-10}
.
In the current universe the rate for the up-scattering
\chi_{1}\chi_{1}\to\chi_{2}\chi_{2}
followed by
\chi_{2}\to\chi_{1}Z^{\prime}
can be enhanced by the small

Z^{\prime}
mass
m_{Z^{\prime}}\ll\delta
. The energetic
Z^{\prime}
is absorbed by the xenon atom at the XENON1T detector via the mechanism similar to the photoelectric effect.
The above mentioned values of
\epsilon
and
\delta
can explain the spectrum and the excess event rate observed by the XENON1T.

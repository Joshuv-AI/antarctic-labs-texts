Quantum duality principle for quantum continuous Kac–Moody algebras Thanks: Partially supported by the MIUR Excellence Department Project awarded to the Department of Mathematics of the University of Rome “Tor Vergata”, CUP E83C18000100006.
Fabio GAVARINI

Abstract
. For the quantized universal enveloping algebra U ℏ ​ ( 𝔤 X ) U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X}) associated to a continuous Kac-Moody algebra 𝔤 X \mathfrak{g}_{X} as in [ ApS20 ] , we prove that a suitable formulation of the Quantum Duality Principle holds true, both in a “formal” version — i.e., applying to the original definition of U ℏ ​ ( 𝔤 X ) U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X}) as a formal QUEA over 𝕜 ⁡ [ [ ℏ ] ] {\Bbbk[[\hbar]]} — and in a “polynomial” one — i.e., for a suitable polynomial form of U ℏ ​ ( 𝔤 X ) U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X}) over 𝕜 ⁡ [ q , q − 1 ] {\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}\, . In both cases, the QDP states that a suitable subalgebra of the given quantization of the Lie bialgebra 𝔤 X \mathfrak{g}_{X} is in fact a suitable quantization (in formal or in polynomial sense) of a connected Poisson group G X ∗ G_{X}^{*} dual to 𝔤 X \mathfrak{g}_{X}\, .

1.
Introduction

Quantum groups, in their standard formulation as suitable topological Hopf algebras on a ring of formal power series
{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}\,
, exist in two versions. Namely, our quantum group is called a quantized universal enveloping algebra (or QUEA, in short), when its specialization at
\,\hbar=0\,
is the universal enveloping algebra of some Lie algebra (actually a
Lie bialgebra
), or a quantum formal series Hopf algebra (in short, a QFSHA) when its specialization is (the algebra of functions on) a formal algebraic/Lie group — actually, a
Poisson group
. The categories of QUEA’s and of QFSHA’s are antiequivalent to each other via linear duality, just like it happens for their semiclassical counterparts. Surprisingly enough, they are also
equivalent
, through explicit equivalence functors, originally sketched in
[
Dri87
, §7]
, and later detailed in
[
Gav02
]
: in a sloppy formulation, this phenomenon is known as
Quantum Duality Principle
— hereafter shortened as QDP.

Roughly speaking, the QDP claims that every QUEA, resp. every QFSHA, can be “renormalized” as to give rise to a QFSHA, resp. to a QUEA: in either case, the new quantum algebra — sometimes called “the Drinfeld-Gavarini dual” of the original one — is a quantization of the object (Poisson group or Lie bialgebra, respectively) which is
Poisson dual
to the object that the original quantum algebra is a quantization of. In particular, if
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
is a QUEA quantizing
U(\mathfrak{g})
then the QDP provides an explicit, functorial construction of a suitable Hopf subalgebra
{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}
of
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
which is a quantization of
F[[G^{*}]]\,
, where
G^{*}
is the formal Poisson group dual to the Lie bialgebra
\mathfrak{g}\,
.
In fact, by construction
{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}
is in fact a
{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}
–integral form of
\,\Bbbk(\!(\hbar)\!)\otimes_{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\,
,  just like
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
itself is. In the other direction, if
F_{\hbar}[[G]]
is any QFSHA for the formal Poisson group
G
then the QDP provides a different
{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}
–integral form
{F_{\hbar}[[G]]}^{\vee}
of
\,\Bbbk(\!(\hbar)\!)\otimes_{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}F_{\hbar}[[G]]\,
that is indeed a QUEA for
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,
.

Note that the geometrical objects
\mathfrak{g}
and
G
(and their Poisson dual) considered by the QDP in its original formulation are finite dimensional, though some aspects of its functorial construction do apply to the infinite setup as well.

In a different approach, where quantum groups are defined as standard (i.e., non-topological) Hopf algebras over the field
\Bbbk(q)
— that is, à la Jimbo-Lusztig, say — so that one deals with “polynomial QUEA” and “polynomial QFA (=Quantum Function Algebras)”, a suitable polynomial version of the QDP has been developed (cf.
[
Gav02
]
). In short, in this context one considers a Hopf algebra
\mathbb{H}
over
\Bbbk(q)
and an
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
–integral form
H
of it: the latter then are called QUEA or QFA depending on whether
\;H\Big/(\,q-1)\,H\;
has the form
U(\mathfrak{g})
or
F[G]\,
,  whence one writes
\,H=\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\,
or
\,H={\mathcal{F}}_{q}[G]\,
,  respectively. Then the “polynomial” QDP provides functorial recipes (direct adaptation of Drinfeld’s original ones)
\,\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\mapsto{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\,
and
\,{\mathcal{F}}_{q}[G]\mapsto{{\mathcal{F}}_{q}[G]}^{\vee}\,
such that
{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}
is a QFA for
G^{*}
and
{{\mathcal{F}}_{q}[G]}^{\vee}
is a QUEA for
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
(actually, the complete result is much stronger, see
[
Gav02
, Theorem 2.2]
). In particular,
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and
{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}
are two
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
–integral forms of the same
\mathbb{H}
, and similarly for
{\mathcal{F}}_{q}[G]
and
{{\mathcal{F}}_{q}[G]}^{\vee}\,
. Indeed, in concrete examples, when the
\Bbbk(q)
–algebra
\mathbb{H}
is given by a presentation by generators and relations, the difference between the two integral forms
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and
{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}
amounts to a different choice of generators (roughly, a different “normalization” of them), and similarly for
{\mathcal{F}}_{q}[G]
and
{{\mathcal{F}}_{q}[G]}^{\vee}
again. For instance, for the usual Jimbo-Lusztig quantum group
\mathbb{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
over a finite-dimensional semisimple
\mathfrak{g}
one can realize that (up to details)
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is nothing but Lusztig’s
restricted
form, while
{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}
is De Concini-Procesi‘s
unrestricted
one. As both can be defined even over
{\mathbb{Z}}\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]\,
,  thus leading to (different) theories of
quantum groups at roots of 1
, we also see how the polynomial QDP is somehow deeply intertwined with the theory of quantum groups at roots of 1 — although a formal, sound and complete theory about that correlation has still to be unveiled.

To date, the impact of the QDP — either in formal or in polynomial version — on the development of quantum group theory has been paramount, in a pervasive manner (although not always explicitly recognized). Nevertheless, as in real life examples and constructions of QUEA’s are available way more than of QFSHA’s (or QFA’s), people mostly applied the QDP in the direction
\,\text{QUEA}\mapsto\text{QFSHA}\,
(or
\,\text{QUEA}\mapsto\text{QFA}\,
, in the polynomial case).

For instance, the formal QDP was used in the very construction of (formal) QUEA’s of Lie bialgebras — cf.
[
ATL18
,
ATL19
,
EtK96
,
Enr01
,
Enr05
,
Hal06
,
EnH07
]
— sometimes even extending its range of application to the infinite-dimensional framework. These results were also extended to broader contexts, such as that of quasi-Hopf QUEA (over quasi-Lie bialgebras) — cf.
[
EnH04
]
— that of super Lie bialgebras — cf.
[
Gee06
]
— that of (quantum) groupoids — cf.
[
ChG15
]
— that of (quantization of)
\Gamma
–Lie bialgebras and Poisson-Hopf stacks over groupoids — cf.
[
EnH08
,
HXT06
]
— and that of Yangians — cf.
[
KaWWY
,
FiT19
]
. In another direction, the formal QDP was also applied to study quantum
R
–matrices and associated structures (and variations on this theme), both from a geometrical point of view and a representation-theoretic one, as in
[
GaH01
,
GaH03
,
EGH03
,
EEM05
]
. Another geometrical application was to quantum homogeneous spaces, as in
[
CiG06
]
, where the QDP was suitably extended to formal quantizations (both infinitesimal and global) of Poisson homogeneous spaces.

On the other hand, the polynomial version of the QDP is applied to (or is definitely underlying) the construction and study of new QFA’s — in a finite dimensional setup (cf.
[
DPr95
]
for the uniparameter case, and
[
Gav98-2
,
GaGa
]
for the multiparameter case) or an infinite one (cf.
[
Bec94
,
Bec96
,
BeK96
,
Gav00
]
) — or new QUEA’s — in a finite (cf.
[
Gav98-1
,
GaR07
]
) or infinite (cf.
[
Gav00
]
) dimensional setup. In a more geometrical perspective, it was applied — again in the “direction”
\,\text{QUEA}\mapsto\text{QFA}\,
to the study of quantum
R
–matrices (and related subjects) in
[
Gav97
,
Gav01
]
— respectively for finite and affine type Kac–Moody Lie bialgebras, and in the study of Poisson homogeneous spaces in
[
CFG08
]
— where a suitable version of polynomial QDP is tailored ad hoc for the projective case — in
[
FiG11
]
— where quantum Grassmannians are treated — and in
[
CiG14
]
— where a more general construction is provided. Still on a geometrical side, in the wake of a very fruitful research line, the polynomial QDP was applied in
[
HaL16
]
to provide a new topological invariant of integral homology spheres.

Finally, despite being a phenomenon that is intrinsically “quantum” in nature, the QDP (in polynomial version) had also found a remarkable application back in “classical” Hopf algebra theory — cf.
[
Gav05-2
]
— with lot of immediate applications at hand — e.g., see
[
Gav05-1
]
for a pretty interesting example.

The purpose of the present work is to prove yet another instance of the QDP, both formal and polynomial, namely in the direction
\,\text{QUEA}\mapsto\text{QF(SH)A}\,
for the quantization of the
continuous Kac–Moody algebras
by Appel, Sala and Schiffmann (see
[
ASS18
,
ApS20
]
. Indeed, these (topological) Lie bialgebras, hereafter denoted by
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
, are uncountably infinite-dimensional, hence one cannot directly apply the QDP as stated and proved in
[
Gav02
]
. Instead, starting from the formal QUEA
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
we provide a direct definition of a suitable subalgebra
\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
of
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
and then we prove that it has exactly the properties predicted by the QDP, in particular
\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
is a QFSHA whose semiclassical limit is
F\big[\big[G^{*}\big]\big]\,
. Finally, we also prove that this
\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
actually admits also a description that coincides with the one prescribed by the usual Drinfeld’s functor
\,U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\mapsto{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\,
.

As a second step, we introduce a suitable
polynomial
QUEA
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
— easy to guess as a subalgebra of
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
— and we realize for it the (polynomial) QDP by introducing by hands its appropriate Drinfeld-Gavarini dual
{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\,
. Here again, we cannot apply the general recipe given in
[
Gav07
]
(as the latter applies to the finite dimensional case only), but we give instead a direct definition of a suitable integral form
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
,  inspired by what is done for
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
when
\mathfrak{g}
finite Kac–Moody (cf.
[
DPr95
]
and
[
Gav98-2
]
) or affine Kac–Moody (see
[
Bec96
,
BeK96
]
and
[
Gav00
]
). Later on, we also prove that this
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
does coincide with what comes out if one literally applies the recipe for Drinfeld’s functor
\,\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\mapsto{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\,
as given in
[
Gav07
]
.

An important feature of the construction sketched above is the following. In the “indirect” construction, mentioned above, of the Drinfeld-Gavarini dual
{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}
as a suitable
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
–integral form of
\,\Bbbk(q)\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\,
when
\mathfrak{g}
is Kac–Moody finite or affine (as in the works of De Concini-Procesi, Beck and the author), a critical step is the construction of suitable “quantum root vectors” for any root, that are
not
available from scratch. However, the Lie bialgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
have a Kac–Moody like presentation which includes, as generators, the (analogue of) “root vectors” for all possible “roots”; even more, the same is true for the QUEA’s
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
and
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
alike. Therefore, the “critical step” mentioned before is already fixed from scratch, so that performing the same construction of the
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
–integral form
\,\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})={\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\,
of
\,\Bbbk(q)\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
as mentioned above becomes an easy task. Up to technicalities, the very same strategy can be followed in order to define
\,{U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}=\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
,  again because all needed quantum root vectors are already given by definition.

As a last remark, we point out that both the QUEA
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
— for the (topological) Lie bialgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
— and the QFA
\,\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})={\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\,
— for the Poisson group
G_{X}^{*}
— are actually defined over
{\mathbb{Z}}\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]\,
:  hence, an “arithmetic theory” for specializations at roots of 1, much like Lusztig (for the QUEA side) and De Concini-Procesi and Beck (for the QFA side) did, in principle is at hand.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we briefly recollect from the literature the main material that we shall deal with.

2.1.
Quantization of Lie bialgebras and of (formal) Poisson groups

Hereafter we fix a base field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero. We recall the following from
[
ChP95
]
.

For any Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over
\Bbbk\,
, its universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
has a canonical structure of Hopf algebra, which is cocommutative and connected. If
\mathfrak{g}
is also a Lie bialgebra, with Lie cobracket
\delta\,
, then
\delta
uniquely extends to define a
Poisson cobracket

\,\delta:U(\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})\,
, just by imposing that it fulfill the co-Leibnitz identity
\;\delta(x\,y)\,=\,\delta(x)\,\Delta(y)+\Delta(x)\,\delta(y)\;
. Conversely, if the Hopf algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
is actually even a Hopf
co-Poisson
algebra, then its Poisson co-bracket
\delta
maps
\mathfrak{g}
into
\,\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\,
, thus yielding a Lie cobracket for
\mathfrak{g}
that makes the latter into a Lie bialgebra.

Dually, let
G
be any formal algebraic group
G
over
\Bbbk\,
: by this we loosely mean that
G
is the spectrum of its formal function algebra
F[[G]]\,
, the latter being a topological Hopf algebra which is commutative and
I
–adically complete, where
\,I:=\textsl{Ker}\,(\epsilon)\,
is the augmentation ideal of
F[[G]]\,
. Then
G
is a (formal) Poisson group if and only if its formal function algebra
F[[G]]
is actually a
Poisson
(formal) Hopf algebra, with respect to some Poisson bracket
\{\,\ ,\ \}\,
. In this case, the cotangent space
\,I\big/I^{2}\,
of
G\,
, has Lie bracket induced by
\{\,\ ,\ \}
via
\;[x,y]:=\big\{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\big\}\;\big(\text{mod\ }I^{2}\,\big)\;
for all
\,x,y\in I\big/I^{2}\,
with
\,x=x^{\prime}\;\big(\text{mod\ }I^{2}\,\big)\,
,
\,y=y^{\prime}\;\big(\text{mod\ }I^{2}\,\big)\,
: this makes
I\big/I^{2}
into a Lie algebra, but its dual
\,\mathfrak{g}=\textsl{Lie}\,(G):={\big(I\big/I^{2}\,\big)}^{*}\,
is also a Lie algebra (the tangent Lie algebra to
G\,
) and the two structures are compatible, so that
\,\mathfrak{g}^{\star}:=I\big/I^{2}\,
is a
Lie bialgebra
indeed.

We come now to
quantizations
of the previous co-Poisson/Poisson structures.

\underline{\text{QUEA}}
:
A
quantized universal enveloping algebra
(QUEA) is a (topological) Hopf algebra
U_{\hbar}
in
\operatorname{Vect}_{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}
such that

(1)

U_{\hbar}
is topologically complete with respect to the
\hbar
–adic topology — or, equivalently,
U_{\hbar}
is isomorphic, as a topological
\Bbbk
–module, to
{U_{0}}[[\hbar]]\,
, where
\;U_{0}\cong U_{\hbar}\big/\hbar\,U_{\hbar}\;
is seen as a discrete topological vector space;

(2)

U_{0}\>=U_{\hbar}\big/\hbar\,U_{\hbar}\,
is a connected, cocommutative Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk
— or, equivalently,
\,U_{0}\,
is isomorphic to an enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
for some Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\,
;  then the formula

\;\displaystyle{\delta(x)\,:=\,\frac{\;\Delta\big(x^{\prime}\big)-\Delta^{21}\big(x^{\prime}\big)\;}{\hbar}\;\mod\hbar\,U_{\hbar}^{\,\widehat{\otimes}\,2}}\;

— where
\,x^{\prime}\in U_{\hbar}\,
is any lift of
\,x\in\mathfrak{g}\,
— defines a co-Poisson structure on
\,U_{0}=U(\mathfrak{g})\,
,  hence a Lie bialgebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}\,
.

In this case, we say that
U_{\hbar}
is a
quantization
of the co-Poisson Hopf algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})\,
, or (with a slight abuse of language) of the Lie bialgebra
\mathfrak{g}\,
.

\underline{\text{QFSHA}}
:
A
quantized formal series Hopf algebra
(QFSHA) is a (topological) Hopf algebra
F_{\hbar}
in
\operatorname{Vect}_{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}
such that

(1)

F_{\hbar}
is topologically complete with respect to the
I_{\hbar}
–adic topology, where
\,I_{\hbar}:=\textsl{Ker}\big(\epsilon_{F_{\hbar}}\big)+\hbar\,F_{\hbar}\,
;

(2)

F_{0}:=F_{\hbar}\big/\hbar\,F_{\hbar}\,
is a commutative,
I
–adically complete topological Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk\,
, where
I
is the augmentation ideal — or, equivalently,
\,F_{0}\,
is isomorphic to the algebra of functions of formal algebraic group
F[[G]]\,
; then the formula

\;\displaystyle{\{x,y\}\,:=\,\frac{\;\big[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\big]-\big[y^{\prime},x^{\prime}\big]\;}{\hbar}\;\mod\hbar\,F_{\hbar}}\;

— where
\,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in F_{\hbar}\,
are lifts of
\,x,y\in F[[G]]\,
— defines a Poisson bracket in
F[[G]]\,
, thus making
G
into a (formal) Poisson group.

In this case, we say that
F_{\hbar}
is a
quantization
of the Poisson Hopf algebra
F[[G]]\,
, or (with a slight abuse of language) of the formal Poisson group
G\,
.

2.2.
Continuous Kac-Moody Lie bialgebras

Hereafter we shall shortly recall the notion of
continuous Kac-Moody Lie bialgebras
, following
[
ApS20
]
and references therein, where the reader may find the (many) details that we shall skip.

By a
vertex space

X
we mean, roughly, a Hausdorff topological space locally modeled over
{\mathbb{R}}\,
. Then one lifts the notion of
connected interval
from
{\mathbb{R}}
to
X\,
, in such a way that the set of all possible
intervals in
X
, denoted
\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
, is naturally endowed with two
partially defined
operations: a sum
\oplus
, given by concatenation of intervals, and a difference
\ominus
, given by set difference whenever the outcome is again in
\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
. Moreover, generalizing a standard tool for quivers, the set
\mathsf{Int}(X)
is equipped with
a non-symmetric bilinear form
\,\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle\colon\mathsf{Int}(X)\times\mathsf{Int}(X)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\,
along with its symmetrization
\,\left(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\right)\colon\mathsf{Int}(X)\times\mathsf{Int}(X)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\,
— the
Euler form
on
\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
.

We refer to the datum
\;{\mathcal{Q}}_{X}\,:=\,\big(\,\mathsf{Int}(X),\oplus,\ominus,\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle,\left(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\right)\big)\,
as the
continuous quiver
of
X
. Hereafter, we denote by
\mathfrak{f}_{X}
the
{\mathbb{Z}}
–span — inside the space of functions
\Bbbk^{X}
— of the characteristic functions
{\mathbf{1}}_{\alpha}\,
,
\,\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
.
Note
that in
\mathfrak{f}_{X}
the relations
\;{\mathbf{1}}_{\alpha\oplus\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\oplus\beta}({\mathbf{1}}_{\alpha}+{\mathbf{1}}_{\beta})\;
— for all
\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
— hold true, where
\,\delta_{\alpha\oplus\beta}:=1\,
if the sum
\,\alpha\oplus\beta\,
is actually defined and
\,\delta_{\alpha\oplus\beta}:=0\,
otherwise.

In
[
ASS18
]
(where
\,\Bbbk:={\mathbb{C}}\,
), for every continuous quiver
{\mathcal{Q}}_{X}
a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
is constructed, called the
continuous Kac-Moody algebra of
\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{X}
, whose definition mimics that of Kac-Moody algebras. Namely, we first consider the Lie
\Bbbk
–algebra
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{X}
generated by
\mathfrak{f}_{X}
and the elements
x^{\pm}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)
, with relations

\big[\xi_{\alpha},\xi_{\beta}\big]\,=\,0\;\;,\quad\big[\xi_{\alpha},x^{\pm}_{\beta}\big]\,=\,\pm\left(\alpha|\beta\right)x^{\pm}_{\beta}\;\;,\quad\big[x^{+}_{\alpha},x^{-}_{\beta}\big]\,=\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}\xi_{\alpha}+\mathsf{a}_{\alpha\beta}\big(x^{+}_{\alpha\ominus\beta}-x^{-}_{\beta\ominus\alpha}\big)

where
\,\xi_{\alpha}:={\mathbf{1}}_{\alpha}\,
and
\,\mathsf{a}_{\alpha\beta}:={(-1)}^{\langle{\alpha},{\beta}\rangle}\left(\alpha|\beta\right)\,
.
Note
that this implies that also all the relations

[formula]

\xi_{\alpha\oplus\beta}\;=\;\delta_{\alpha\oplus\beta}(\xi_{\alpha}+\xi_{\beta})

hold true, just because
\;{\mathbf{1}}_{\alpha\oplus\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\oplus\beta}(\,{\mathbf{1}}_{\alpha}+{\mathbf{1}}_{\beta})\;
in
\mathfrak{f}_{X}\,
. Then we endow
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{X}
with a suitable grading and we set
\,\mathfrak{g}_{X}:=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{X}\big/\mathfrak{r}_{X}\,
,  where
\,\mathfrak{r}_{X}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{X}\,
is the sum of all two–sided graded ideals having trivial intersection with
\mathfrak{f}_{X}\,
. An explicit description of
\mathfrak{r}_{X}
is given in
[
ASS18
]
, thus eventually one explicitly describes
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
as the Lie
\Bbbk
–algebra generated by the elements
\,\xi_{\alpha},x^{\pm}_{\alpha}\,
(
\,\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
),  subject to the following relations:

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill\big[\xi_{\alpha},\xi_{\beta}\big]\,=\,0\quad,\qquad\xi_{\alpha\oplus\beta}\;=\;\delta_{\alpha\oplus\beta}(\xi_{\alpha}+\xi_{\beta})\hfill\quad\qquad\forall\;\;\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{Int}{X}\quad

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill\big[\xi_{\alpha},x^{\pm}_{\beta}\big]=\pm\left(\alpha|\beta\right)x^{\pm}_{\beta}\;,\quad\big[x^{+}_{\alpha},x^{-}_{\beta}\big]=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\,\xi_{\alpha}+\mathsf{a}_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{+}_{\alpha\ominus\beta}-x^{-}_{\beta\ominus\alpha}\right)\hfill\quad\forall\,\;\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{Int}(X)

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill\big[x^{\pm}_{\alpha},x^{\pm}_{\beta}\big]\,=\,\pm\,\mathsf{a}_{\alpha,\,\alpha\oplus\beta}\cdot x^{\pm}_{\alpha\oplus\beta}\hfill\qquad\forall\;\;(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathsf{Serre}(X)\quad

where
\mathsf{Serre}(X)
is the set of all pairs
\,(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\times\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
that obey some suitable conditions. The general formulation of these conditions is quite technical, given in terms of the sum among elements of
\mathsf{Int}(X)
, of the Euler form
\,\left(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\right)\,
, and a notion of “partition” of an interval
\,x_{\alpha}\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
as (roughly speaking) an ordered decomposition of it into sum of “smaller” intervals. Albeit looking quite tricky, this notion in fact has a very natural motivation; nevertheless, the technicalities reach far beyond our present scope, so we skip them (we shall not really them, indeed) referring instead to the original source
[
ASS18
]
for the interested reader.

Note that the
{\mathbb{Z}}
–span of the
\xi_{\alpha}
’s is a copy of
\mathfrak{f}_{X}
inside
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
.

In addition, naturally defined
Borel subalgebras

\mathfrak{b}_{X}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{X}^{-}
exist in
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
,  namely
\mathfrak{b}_{X}^{\pm}
is the Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
generated by all the
\xi_{\alpha}
’s and the
x^{\pm}_{\beta}
’s with
\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
.

Finally, it is also shown that
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
bears a (canonical) structure of quasitriangular,
topological
Lie bialgebra, whoses Lie cobracket is given on generators by

\delta(\xi_{\alpha})\,:=\,0\quad,\qquad\delta(x^{\pm}_{\alpha})\;:=\;\xi_{\alpha}\wedge x^{\pm}_{\alpha}+{\textstyle\sum_{\beta\oplus\gamma=\alpha}}\,\mathsf{a}_{\beta,\,\alpha}\,x^{\pm}_{\beta}\wedge x^{\pm}_{\gamma}

— the sum being convergent, in a natural sense. Then both Borel subalgebras
\mathfrak{b}_{X}^{\pm}
are Lie sub-bialgebras, and the Euler form restricts to a non-degenerate pairing of Lie bialgebras
\;\left(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\right)\colon\mathfrak{b}_{X}^{+}\otimes{\big(\mathfrak{b}_{X}^{-}\big)}^{\scriptscriptstyle\operatorname{cop}}\!\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\Bbbk\;
. It follows that the canonical element
\,r_{X}\in\mathfrak{b}_{X}^{+}\widehat{\otimes}\mathfrak{b}_{X}^{-}\,
corresponding to
\left(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\right)
defines a
quasi-triangular structure
on
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
.

2.3.
Quantization of continuous Kac-Moody Lie bialgebras

We now recall the construction of QUEA’s that provide quantizations of the continuous Kac-Moody Lie bialgebras mentioned above: we follow again
[
ApS20
]
, still referring to that source for all the details that we shall skip.

Given a continuous quiver
{\mathcal{Q}}_{X}
and the associated continuous Kac-Moody Lie bialgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
,  one defines also a suitable QUEA
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
: its definition is modeled on that of
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
, but it depends on two additional partial operations on
\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
: the

strict union of two non-orthogonal intervals
\alpha
and
\beta
,
denoted
\,{\alpha}\triangledown{\beta}\,
,  and the

strict intersection of two non-orthogonal intervals
\alpha
and
\beta
,
denoted
\,{\alpha}\negthinspace\vartriangle\negthinspace{\beta}\,
(roughly, these are slight variations of set-theoretical union and intersection, respectively).

Now, for given
{\mathcal{Q}}_{X}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
as above, the
continuous quantum group of
X

is the unital,
\hbar
–adically complete, associative
{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}
–algebra
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
generated by
\mathfrak{f}_{X}
— whose spanning elements are now denoted by
\,\varXi_{\alpha}:={\mathbf{1}}_{\alpha}\,
(
\,\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
) — and the elements
X^{\pm}_{\alpha}
(
\,\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,)
,  obeying the following defining relations
(for all
\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
, with the additional constraint
\,(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathsf{Serre}(X)\,
for the last relation)

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill\varXi_{\alpha}\,\varXi_{\beta}-\varXi_{\beta}\,\varXi_{\alpha}=\,0\;\;,\;\;\;\;\varXi_{\alpha\oplus\beta}=\,\delta_{\alpha\oplus\beta}\big(\varXi_{\alpha}+\varXi_{\beta}\big)\;\;,\;\;\;\;\varXi_{\alpha}\,X^{\pm}_{\beta}-X^{\pm}_{\beta}\,\varXi_{\alpha}\,=\,\pm\left(\alpha|\beta\right)X^{\pm}_{\beta}\hfill

\hfil\displaystyle X^{+}_{\alpha}\,X^{-}_{\beta}-X^{-}_{\beta}\,X^{+}_{\alpha}\;\;=\;\;\delta_{\alpha\beta}\frac{K_{\alpha}-K_{\alpha}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}\;+\hfill

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill+\;\mathsf{a}_{\alpha\beta}\!\left(q^{\mathsf{c}^{+}_{\alpha\beta}}X^{+}_{\alpha\ominus\beta}K_{\beta}^{\mathsf{a}_{\alpha\beta}}\!-q^{\mathsf{c}^{-}_{\alpha\beta}}K_{\alpha}^{\mathsf{a}_{\alpha\beta}}X^{-}_{\beta\ominus\alpha}\right)\!+\mathsf{b}_{\beta\alpha}q^{\mathsf{b}_{\beta\alpha}}\big(q-q^{-1}\big)X^{+}_{({\alpha}\triangledown{\beta})\ominus\beta}K_{{\alpha\,}\negthinspace\vartriangle\negthinspace{\,\beta}}^{\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\beta}}\,X^{-}_{({\alpha}\triangledown{\beta})\ominus{\alpha}}

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill X^{\pm}_{\alpha}X^{\pm}_{\beta}\,-\,q^{\mathsf{r}_{\alpha\beta}}\,X^{\pm}_{\beta}X^{\pm}_{\alpha}\;=\;\pm\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\beta}\,q^{\mathsf{s}^{\pm}_{\alpha\beta}}\,X^{\pm}_{\alpha\oplus\beta}\,+\,\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\beta}\,\big(q-q^{-1}\big)\,X^{\pm}_{{\alpha}\triangledown{\beta}}X^{\pm}_{{\alpha\,}\negthinspace\vartriangle\negthinspace{\,\beta}}\hfill

where we set
\,q:=\exp\big(\hbar/2\big)\,
and
\,K_{\alpha}:=\exp\big(\hbar\,\varXi_{\alpha}/2\big)\,
,  and we assume
\,X^{\pm}_{\alpha\odot\beta}:=0\,
whenever
\,\alpha\odot\beta\,
is not defined, for
\,\odot=\oplus,\ominus,{}\triangledown{},{}\negthinspace\vartriangle\negthinspace{}\,
; moreover, the various coefficients are defined as follows:

\hfil\displaystyle\mathsf{a}_{\alpha\beta}\,:=\,{(-1)}^{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}\left(\alpha|\beta\right)\;\;,\;\quad\mathsf{c}^{+}_{\alpha\beta}\,:=\,2^{-1}\left(\,\mathsf{a}_{\beta,\,\alpha\ominus\beta}-1\right)\;\;,\;\quad\mathsf{c}^{-}_{\alpha\beta}\,:=\,2^{-1}\left(\,\mathsf{a}_{\beta\ominus\alpha,\,\alpha}+1\right)

\hfil\displaystyle\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\beta}\,:=\,\mathsf{a}_{\alpha,\,{\alpha}\triangledown{\beta}}\;\;,\;\quad\mathsf{r}_{\alpha\beta}\,:=\,(\,1-\delta_{\alpha\beta}){(-1)}^{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}\left(\alpha|\beta\right)^{2}\;\;,\;\quad\mathsf{s}^{\pm}_{\alpha\beta}\,:=\,2^{-1}\left(\,\mathsf{a}_{\beta,\,\alpha\oplus\beta}\pm 1\right)

In addition, one also considers the
{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}
–subalgebras
U_{\hbar}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{\pm}_{X}\big)
in
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
generated by
\mathfrak{f}_{X}
and the

\,\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
). Moreover, both have an obvious presentation by generators and relations similar to that of
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
(roughly speaking, one just has to pick only the relevant generators and the relations involving them).

The result concerning the Hopf algebra structure of
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{X})
is the following:

Theorem 2.3.1

(cf.
[
ApS20
, Theorem 5.11]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{X}
be a continuous quiver and
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{X})
the corresponding continuous quantum group.

(1)  The algebra
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{X})
is a topological Hopf algebra with respect to the antipode and coproduct defined on the generators by

\hfil\displaystyle\epsilon\big(\varXi_{\alpha}\big)\,:=\,0\,=:\,\epsilon\big(X_{\alpha}^{\pm}\big)\quad,\qquad\qquad\Delta(\xi_{\alpha})\ :=\varXi_{\alpha}\otimes 1+1\otimes\varXi_{\alpha}

\hfil\displaystyle\Delta\big(X^{+}_{\alpha}\big)\,:=\,X^{+}_{\alpha}\otimes 1+K_{\alpha}^{+1}\otimes X^{+}_{\alpha}\,+\,{\textstyle\sum_{\alpha=\beta\oplus\gamma}}\,\mathsf{a}_{\beta,\,\alpha}\,\mathsf{s}^{+}_{\gamma\beta}\cdot\big(\,q-q^{-1}\big)\,K_{\gamma}^{+1}X^{+}_{\beta}\otimes X^{+}_{\gamma}

\hfil\displaystyle\Delta\big(X^{-}_{\alpha}\big)\,:=\,1\otimes X^{-}_{\alpha}+X^{-}_{\alpha}\otimes K_{\alpha}^{-1}\,-\,{\textstyle\sum_{\alpha=\beta\oplus\gamma}}\,\mathsf{a}_{\beta,\,\alpha}\,\mathsf{s}^{-}_{\gamma\beta}\cdot\big(\,q-q^{-1}\big)\,X^{-}_{\beta}\otimes X^{-}_{\gamma}K_{\beta}^{-1}

— where
\Delta
takes values in the completion of the algebraic tensor product with respect to the “weak &
\hbar
–adic topology” — while the antipode is given, as usual, by the formula

\;S:=\sum_{n}m^{(n)}\circ{({\textsl{id}}-\iota\circ\epsilon)}^{\otimes n}\circ\Delta^{(n)}\;

where
m^{(n)}
and
\Delta^{(n)}
denote the
n

(2)
U_{\hbar}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{+}_{X}\big)\,
, resp.
U_{\hbar}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{-}_{X}\big)\,
,  is a Hopf
{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}
–subalgebra of
\,U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
.

(3)  There exists a unique non-degenerate Hopf pairing

\left(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\right)\colon U_{\hbar}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{+}_{X}\big)\otimes{\big(U_{\hbar}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{-}_{X}\big)\big)}^{\scriptscriptstyle\operatorname{cop}}\!\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\Bbbk((\hbar))

defined on the generators by the formulas

\left(1|1\right)\,:=\,1\quad,\qquad\left(\,\varXi_{\alpha}\,|\,\varXi_{\beta}\right)\,:=\frac{\;\left(\alpha|\beta\right)\;}{\;\hbar\;}\quad,\qquad\big(\,X^{+}_{\alpha}\,\big|\,X^{+}_{\beta}\,\big)\,:=\frac{\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{\;q-q^{-1}\;}

and zero otherwise. In particular, one has
\,\big(\,K_{\alpha}^{\;}\big|\,K_{\beta}^{\,}\big)=q^{\left(\alpha|\beta\right)}\,
.

(4)  Through the Hopf pairing
\left(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\right)\,
, the Hopf algebras
\,U_{\hbar}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{+}_{X}\big)\,
and
\,U_{\hbar}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{-}_{X}\big)\,
give rise to a match pair of Hopf algebras; via this,
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{X})
is realized as a quotient of the double cross product Hopf algebra
\;U_{\hbar}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{+}_{X}\big)\triangleright\negthinspace\negthinspace\triangleleft\,U_{\hbar}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{-}_{X}\big)\,
obtained by identifying the two copies of the commutative subalgebra
\mathfrak{f}_{X}\,
. In particular,
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{X})
is a topological quasi-triangular Hopf algebra.

(5)  The topological quasi-triangular Hopf algebra
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
is a quantization of the topological quasi-triangular Lie bialgebra
\,\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
,  through the (unique) isomorphism
\;U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\Big/\hbar\,U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,\cong\,U(\mathfrak{g}_{X})\;

given
\;\varXi_{\alpha}\,\mapsto\,\xi_{\alpha}\;
and
\;X_{\alpha}^{\pm}\,\mapsto\,x_{\alpha}^{\pm}\;
for all
\,\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
.

Similarly,
U_{\hbar}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{\pm}_{X}\big)
is a quantization of the Lie bialgebra
\mathfrak{b}_{X}^{\pm}\,
.

3.
QDP for formal quantum continuous KM-algebras

We are now ready to present the Quantum Duality Principle (=QDP) for the QUEA associated with
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
, namely
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
. Indeed, we cannot get it as a direct application of the general result in
[
Gav02
]
, because
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
is infinite-dimensional. However, from the general result in [
loc. cit.
] we can still cook up a suitable formulation of the QDP expressly tailored as to match the case of
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
.

We begin here with a formulation of the QDP in the “formal” version — i.e., for quantizations “ à la Drinfeld” such as the formal
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
introduced in §
2.3
above.
However, as our construction is somewhat indirect, and as such it might seem to come out of the blue, we first start by explaining the very motivation that guides our construction, shedding light on some key technical steps in the general setup.

3.1.
Drinfeld’s functor
\,U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\mapsto{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\,
and its description

We deal now with a QUEA
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
over a Lie
\Bbbk
–bialgebra
\mathfrak{g}
— cf. §
2.1
. The QDP as originally devised by Drinfeld applies to
\mathfrak{g}
of
finite dimension
: indeed, it is proved in
[
Gav02
]
that most steps leading to the main result (the proof of the QDP in its full extent) actually still hold true in greater generality as well, but the whole result as such does not. However, one can carefully modifies some assumptions — e.g., the nature of the (quantum) Hopf algebras we are dealing with, in particular their topology — so to finally achieve a suitably modified version of the QDP and some related byproducts. Hereafter, we revisit in detail some steps of the analysis carried on in
[
Gav02
]
, so that we will be later able to adapt them to the QUEA’s
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
on the infinite-dimensional (topological) Lie bialgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
.

Definition 3.1.1
.

(Drinfeld’s functors)
We define Drinfeld’s functors on QUEA’s and QFSHA’s (only on objects — we do not need them on morphisms) as follows:

(a)
Let
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
be any QUEA (cf. §
2.1
),
and assume for simplicity that
\mathfrak{g}
be finite-dimensional. Let
\,\iota:{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\,
and
\,\epsilon:U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}\,
be its unit and counit maps; moreover, for every
\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\,
set
\,\delta_{n}:=({\textsl{id}}-\iota\circ\epsilon)\circ\Delta^{(n)}\,
. Then we define

{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\;:=\;\Big\{\,\eta\in U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\,\Big|\;\delta_{n}(\eta)\in\hbar^{n}\,{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\otimes n}\;\;\forall\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}\,\Big\}

(b)
Let
F_{\hbar}[[G]]
be any QFSHA (cf. §
2.1
),
and assume for simplicity that
G
be finite-dimensional. Let
\,\epsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle F}:F_{\hbar}[[G]]\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}\,
be its counit map, and consider also
\,I_{F_{\hbar}}:=\hbar\,F_{\hbar}[[G]]+\textsl{Ker\/}(\epsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle F})\,
. Then we define

{F_{\hbar}[[G]]}^{\vee}\;:=\;\hbar
–adic completion of
\,\sum_{n\geq 0}\hbar^{-n}I_{F_{\hbar}}^{\;{}^{\scriptstyle n}}

\oslash

When
\mathfrak{g}
is finite-dimensional, one proves (cf.
[
Gav02
]
) that
{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}
is a QFSHA quantizing
G^{*}\,
,  the formal Poisson group dual to
\mathfrak{g}\,
(see also Remark
3.1.5
below for possible generalizations). Moreover, the following, alternative description is possible (it was mentioned in
[
Gav02
, §3.5]
, but in a somewhat confused manner):

Proposition 3.1.2
.

Given a
\Bbbk
–basis
{\{\overline{y}_{i}\}}_{i\in I}
of
\mathfrak{g}\,
,  there exist
\,y_{i}\in U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\,
such that:

(a)
\,\epsilon(y_{i})=0\,
,
\,\big(\,y_{i}\!\mod\hbar\,U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\big)=\overline{y}_{i}\,
and
\;y^{\prime}_{i}:=\hbar\,y_{i}\,\in\,{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\,
for all
\,i\in I\,
;

(b)
\,{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}
is the completion of the unital
{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}
–subalgebra of
\,U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by all the
x^{\prime}_{i}
’s with respect to its
I^{\prime}_{\hbar}
–adic topology, where
I^{\prime}_{\hbar}
is the ideal (in that subalgebra) generated by
\hbar
and all the
x^{\prime}_{i}
’s, so that

\;{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}=\Bbbk\big[\big[{\{x^{\prime}_{i}\}}_{i\in I}\cup\{\hbar\}\big]\big]\;

Proof.

By
[
Gav02
, Proposition 3.6]
,
\,{\mathcal{F}}_{\hbar}:={U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\,
is a QFSHA. Moreover, from
[
Gav02
, §3.1]
we have
\,{\mathcal{F}}_{\hbar}=\Bbbk\big[\big[{\{x_{i}\}}_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\cup\{\hbar\}\big]\big]\,
for some
\,x_{i}\in\textsl{Ker\/}\big(\,\epsilon_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\hbar}}\big)\,
— for each
\,i\in{\mathcal{I}}\,
, the latter being an index set — such that their cosets
\,\big(\,(\,x_{i}\!\mod\hbar\,{\mathcal{F}}_{\hbar}\,)\!\mod J_{\mathcal{F}}^{\,2}\,\big)\,

\Bbbk
–basis of
\;J_{\mathcal{F}}\big/J_{\mathcal{F}}^{\,2}\,\cong\,\mathfrak{g}\,
,  where

is the kernel of the counit of
\,{\mathcal{F}}_{\hbar}\Big/\hbar\,{\mathcal{F}}_{\hbar}\;

[
Gav02

is a QUEA, and we have an explicit description of it: namely, as a

\;{\mathcal{F}}_{\hbar}^{\,\vee}=\big(\,\Bbbk\big[{\{\check{x}_{i}\}}_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\big]\big)[[\hbar]]\,
,  i.e. it is the
\hbar
–adic completion of the
{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}
–subalgebra of
\,\Bbbk(\!(\hbar)\!)\otimes_{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}{\mathcal{F}}_{\hbar}\,

\,\check{x}_{i}:=\hbar^{-1}x_{i}\,

\;{\mathcal{F}}_{\hbar}^{\,\vee}\!\Big/\,\hbar\,{\mathcal{F}}_{\hbar}^{\,\vee}\,=\,U(\mathfrak{k}^{*})\;

\,\check{x}_{i}\,\big(\,\text{mod}\;\hbar\,{\mathcal{F}}_{\hbar}^{\,\vee}\,\big)\,

\,i\in{\mathcal{I}}\,

\Bbbk

\mathfrak{k}^{*}\,

Remark 3.1.3
.

It is worth stressing here a key point. Proposition
3.1.2
above ensures that
{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}
can be described as an algebra of “formal series” in the elements
\,y^{\prime}_{i}=\hbar\,y_{i}\,
, which are re-scaling of suitable lifts in
\textsl{Ker\/}\big(U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\big)
of elements
\overline{y}_{i}
in a
\Bbbk
–basis of
\mathfrak{g}\,\big(\subseteq U(\mathfrak{g})\big)\,
. However, we must point out that
this heavily depends on the choice of these lifts
, in particular it is definitely
false
that the same might hold true with
any
lifts of the
\overline{y}_{i}
’s — easy counterexamples exist, already for
\,\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\,
and its standard Drinfeld’s quantization.

On the other, choosing inside
\,\textsl{Ker\/}\big(U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\big)\cap{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\,
any lift
{\{\,y_{i}\,\}}_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
of a
\Bbbk
–basis of
\mathfrak{g}
as in claim
(a)
of Proposition
3.1.2
is indeed enough to guarantee that claim
(b)
holds true as well; this is the content of next result, which we will apply later on.

Lemma 3.1.4
.

Let
\,x^{\prime}_{i}\in{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\,
— for all
\,i\in I\,
— have the following properties:
\,x^{\prime}_{i}=\hbar\,x_{i}\,
for some
\,x_{i}\in U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\,
such that
\,\epsilon(x_{i})=0\,
(for all
\,i\in I\,
) and the set
\,{\big\{\,\overline{x}_{i}:=\big(\,x_{i}\!\!\mod\hbar\,U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\big)\big\}}_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\,
is a
\Bbbk
–basis of
\mathfrak{g}\,
. Then
{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}
is the
I^{\prime\prime}_{\hbar}
–adic completion of the unital
{\Bbbk[[\hbar]]}
–subalgebra
{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime\prime}
of
\,U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by all the
x^{\prime}_{i}
’s, where
\,I^{\prime\prime}_{\hbar}:=\hbar\,{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime\prime}+\textsl{Ker}\,\big(\,{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime\prime}\,\big)\,
. In a nutshell,

\;{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}=\Bbbk\big[\big[{\{x^{\prime}_{i}\}}_{i\in I}\cup\{\hbar\}\big]\big]\;
.

Proof.

Let us consider an element
\,x^{\prime}\in{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\setminus\{0\}\,
. Then there exist unique
\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\,
and
\,x\in U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\setminus h\,U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\,
such that
\,x^{\prime}=h^{n}x\,
;  applying
[
Gav02
, Lemma 3.3]
, we find that for
\,\bar{x}:=\big(\,x\!\mod\hbar\,U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\big)=U(\mathfrak{g})\,
we have
\,\partial(\bar{x})\leq n\,
with respect to the canonical filtration of
U(\mathfrak{g})\,
,  that is
\bar{x}
can be written as a linear combination of PBW monomials (w.r.t. any
\Bbbk
–basis of
\mathfrak{g}\,
) whose degree are less or equal than
n\,
. In particular, for the given
\Bbbk
–basis
{\{\overline{x}_{i}\}}_{i\in I}
of
\mathfrak{g}
we have
\,\bar{x}=P\big({\{\bar{x}_{i}\}}_{i\in I}\big)\,
for some polynomial
P
in the
\bar{x}_{i}
’s with coefficients in
\Bbbk
and degree
\,\partial(P)\leq n\,
. Then

x_{\langle 0\rangle}\,:=\,P\big({\{x_{i}\}}_{i\in I}\big)\,\equiv\,x\mod\,h\,U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})

hence
\,x=x_{\langle 0\rangle}+h\,x_{\langle 1\rangle}\,
for some
\,x_{\langle 1\rangle}\in U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\,
,  and also
\,x^{\prime}=\hbar^{n}x=\hbar^{n}x_{\langle 0\rangle}+\hbar^{n+1}\,x_{\langle 1\rangle}\,
. Now we can write

\hbar^{n}x_{0}\;=\;\hbar^{n}P\big({\{x_{i}\}}_{i\in I}\big)\;=\;P_{0}\big({\{\,\hbar\,x_{i}=:x^{\prime}_{i}\,\}}_{i\in I}\big)\;=\;P_{0}\big({\{\,x^{\prime}_{i}\,\}}_{i\in I}\big)\;=:\;x^{\prime}_{(0)}

where

is again a polynomial (in the
x^{\prime}_{i}
’s) of degree bounded by

;  but then
\;x^{\prime}_{(0)}\,:=\,P_{0}\big({\{\,x^{\prime}_{i}\,\}}_{i\in I}\big)\,\in\,{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\;
and the above yields

h^{n+1}x_{\langle 1\rangle}\,=\,x^{\prime}-x^{\prime}_{(0)}\,\in\,{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}

Now, if
\,x^{\prime}_{(1)}:=h^{n+1}x_{\langle 1\rangle}=0\,
we are done; if not, we repeat the same argument with
x_{(1)}
in the role of
\,x_{(0)}:=x^{\prime}\,
. Iterating this procedure, we end up with a sequence
\,x^{\prime}_{(k)}:=P_{k}\big({\{\,x^{\prime}_{i}\,\}}_{i\in I}\big)\in{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\,
,
\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,
,  where each

is a polynomial in the

’s with coefficients in
\Bbbk\,
,  such that
\,x^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\hbar^{k}x^{\prime}_{(k)}\,
in
{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\prime}\,
,  whence the claim.

Remark 3.1.5

A careful checking of all the analysis of Drinfeld’s functors carried on in

3.3.
Formal QDP for
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
: the intrinsic recipe

In the previous subsection, we introduced a subalgebra
\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
of
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
, then we proved that it is actually a Hopf algebra, and even a QFSHA that is a quantization of
G_{X}^{*}\,
, the formal Poisson group dual to
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
. In this sense, we have realized the QDP for the QUEA
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
.

The explicit construction of
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
is inspired by similar constructions for the standard, well-known
polynomial

U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
associated with a Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of finite or affine type following Drinfeld and Jimbo (cf.
[
Gav98-2
,
Gav00
]
, and references therein): in these cases, the explicit construction amounts to taking, within the given QUEA, the subalgebra generated by renormalized lifts of vectors in
\mathfrak{g}\;\big(\subseteq U(\mathfrak{g})\big)\,
. In the present framework one also has the additional advantage that
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
is already endowed, by definition, with suitable built-in “quantum root vectors”
X_{\alpha}^{\pm}
that are lifts of the “root vectors”
x_{\alpha}^{\pm}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
: this makes things easier than in the case of finite or affine Kac-Moody
\mathfrak{g}\,
.

It is explained in
[
Gav02
]
that even the general construction provided by the QDP in its full extent can still be realized in this way: however, there exists no way whatsoever for making this rough idea into a precise recipe. On the other hand, the core formulation of the QDP for QUEA does provide an explicit recipe for the like of
\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
, given in intrinsic terms. We shall now show that the same definition makes sense for the QUEA
U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
, and in fact provides a different realization of the same Hopf subalgebra
\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
that we considered above.

.

(cf. Definition

(a)
)

be as in §

4.1.
The polynomial QUEA
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,

Besides the original formulation by Drinfeld (cf.
[
Dri87
]
and
[
Gav02
]
) in the framework of “formal” quantization, another version of the QDP was developed in
[
Gav07
]
for the setup of “polynomial” quantization, that is for genuine Hopf algebras over some ground ring
R

In this spirit, our first step is to “extract” from “formal” QUEA

Next result instead explains why we call
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
a QUEA again, though polynomial; the proof, again, is straightforward, but first we have to set the framework.

Definition 4.1.3
.

We define
(polynomial) quantized universal enveloping algebra (=QUEA) over
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
any Hopf
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
–algebra
U_{q}
(in the standard sense) such that the quotient Hopf algebra over
\;{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}\big/(\,q-1)\,{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}\,\cong\,\Bbbk\;
given by
\,\;U_{1}\,:=\,U_{q}\big/(\,q-1)\,U_{q}\;\,
is connected and cocommutative — or, equivalently,
\,U_{1}\,
is isomorphic to an enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
for some Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\,
. For any such QUEA, the formula

\;\displaystyle{\delta(x)\,:=\,\frac{\;\Delta\big(x^{\prime}\big)-\Delta^{21}\big(x^{\prime}\big)\;}{q-1}\;\mod(\,q-1)\,U_{q}^{\,\otimes 2}}\;

— where
\,x^{\prime}\in U_{q}\,
is any lift of
\,x\in\mathfrak{g}\,
— yields a co-Poisson structure on
\,U_{1}=U(\mathfrak{g})\,
,  hence a Lie bialgebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}\,
.

In this case, we say that
U_{q}
is a
(polynomial) quantization
of the co-Poisson Hopf algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})\,
, or (with a slight abuse of language) of the Lie bialgebra
\mathfrak{g}\,
.

\oslash

The previous definition now gives sense to the following result:

Theorem 4.1.4
.

The Hopf algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
is a polynomial QUEA, and more precisely it is a polynomial quantization of the Lie bialgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
,  as there is an isomorphism
\,\;\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\Big/(\,q-1)\,\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,\cong\,U(\mathfrak{g}_{X})\;\,
of
co-Poisson Hopf algebras given by

\dot{K}_{\alpha}^{\pm 1}\mapsto\,1\;\;,\quad\dot{H}_{\alpha}\,\mapsto\,\xi_{\alpha}\;\;,\quad\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\,\mapsto\,2\,x_{\alpha}^{\pm}\qquad\qquad\forall\;\;\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)

In a similar sense,
\mathcal{U}_{q}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{\pm}_{X}\big)
is a quantization of the Lie bialgebra
\mathfrak{b}_{X}^{\pm}\,
.

Proof.

This is a straightforward consequence of the very presentation of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
given in Proposition
4.1.2
above.
∎

Observation 4.1.5
.

It is worth remaking that in the formulas occurring in Proposition
4.1.2

all coefficients that show up actually belong to the (sub)ring

{\mathbb{Z}}\big[\,q,q^{-1}\big]\,
. This implies that the Hopf algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
can be defined
over

{\mathbb{Z}}\big[\,q,q^{-1}\big]\,
,  hence one can go and consider
R
–integral forms of it over any ring
R\,
,  then possibly look for specializations at roots of unity, etc. — much like in the study of quantum groups by Lusztig and others, including (in an infinite dimensional setting, such as is that of
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
) the study in
[
Gav00
]
for the case of quantum
dual
affine Kac–Moody algebras.

4.2.
QDP for
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
: the polynomial version

We are now ready to present our realization of the QDP for the polynomial QUE
\,\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
introduced above.

Definition 4.2.1
.

Given
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
as above, we define
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
as follows: it is the unital
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
–subalgebra of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
generated by all the elements

[formula]

\overline{K}{}_{\alpha}^{\,\pm 1}:=\,\dot{K}_{\alpha}^{\pm 1}\;\;,\quad\overline{H}_{\alpha}:=\big(\,q-1\big)\,\dot{H}_{\alpha}\;\;,\quad\overline{X}{}_{\alpha}^{\,\pm}:=(\,q-1)\,\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\quad

Similarly, we define
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{+}_{X}\big)\,
, resp.
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}\big(\mathfrak{b}^{-}_{X}\big)\,
, in the same way, but discarding the
\overline{X}{}_{\alpha}^{\,-}

\overline{X}{}_{\alpha}^{\,-}
’s (
\,\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)
) from the set of generators; it follows then that
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}\big(\mathfrak{b}_{X}^{\pm}\big)

\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})

\,I\big/I^{2}\,

\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,

\,I\big/I^{2}\,

\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,

\,\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\big/\hbar\,\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,

3.2.3

Using notation
\,\widetilde{U}_{q}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,

(4.1)

\big[\,{\check{X}}_{\alpha}^{\,\pm}\,,{\check{X}}_{\beta}^{\,\pm}\,\big]\,\;\mapsto\;\,\big[\,2\,x_{\alpha}^{\,\pm}\,,\,2\,x_{\beta}^{\,\pm}\,\big]

\hfil\displaystyle=\;\Bigg(\,\frac{\,\big[\,\overline{X}{}_{\alpha}^{\,\pm},\overline{X}{}_{\beta}^{\,\pm}\,\big]\,}{q-1}\;\Big(\,\text{\rm mod\ }(\,q-1)\,\widetilde{U}_{q}\Big)\Bigg)\;\text{\rm mod\ }I^{2}\;=

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill=\;\Bigg(\,\frac{\,\overline{X}{}_{\alpha}^{\,\pm}\,\overline{X}{}_{\beta}^{\,\pm}-\overline{X}{}_{\beta}^{\,\pm}\,\overline{X}{}_{\alpha}^{\,\pm}\,}{q-1}\;\Big(\,\text{\rm mod\ }(\,q-1)\,\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}\Big)\Bigg)\;\text{\rm mod\ }I^{2}\;=

\hfil\displaystyle=\;\Bigg(\Bigg({(\,q-1)}^{-1}\bigg(\big(\,q^{\mathsf{r}_{\alpha\beta}}-1\big)\,\overline{X}{}^{\,\pm}_{\beta}\,\overline{X}{}^{\,\pm}_{\alpha}\,+\,\big(\,q-q^{-1}\big)\,\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\beta}\,\Big(\pm q^{\mathsf{s}^{\pm}_{\alpha\beta}}\overline{X}{}^{\,\pm}_{\alpha\oplus\beta}\,+\,\overline{X}{}^{\,\pm}_{{\alpha}\triangledown{\beta}}\,\overline{X}{}^{\,\pm}_{{\alpha\,}\negthinspace\vartriangle\negthinspace{\,\beta}}\,\Big)\bigg)\Bigg)\hfill

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill\text{\rm mod\ }(\,q-1)\,\widetilde{U}_{q}\,\Bigg)\;\;\text{\rm mod\ }I^{2}\;=

\hfil\displaystyle=\;\Bigg(\bigg({\big(\,{\mathsf{r}_{\alpha\beta}}\big)}_{q}\,\overline{X}{}^{\,\pm}_{\beta}\,\overline{X}{}^{\,\pm}_{\alpha}\,+\,\big(1+q^{-1}\big)\,\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\beta}\,\Big(\pm q^{\mathsf{s}^{\pm}_{\alpha\beta}}\overline{X}{}^{\,\pm}_{\alpha\oplus\beta}\,+\,\overline{X}{}^{\,\pm}_{{\alpha}\triangledown{\beta}}\,\overline{X}{}^{\,\pm}_{{\alpha\,}\negthinspace\vartriangle\negthinspace{\,\beta}}\,\Big)\bigg)\;\;\hfill

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill\text{\rm mod\ }(\,q-1)\,\widetilde{U}_{q}\,\Bigg)\;\;\text{\rm mod\ }I^{2}\;=

\hfil\displaystyle=\;\bigg(\mathsf{r}_{\alpha\beta}\,\check{X}^{\,\pm}_{\beta}\,\check{X}^{\,\pm}_{\alpha}\,+\,2\,\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\beta}\Big(\pm\check{X}^{\,\pm}_{\alpha\oplus\beta}\,+\,\check{X}^{\,\pm}_{{\alpha}\triangledown{\beta}}\,\check{X}^{\,\pm}_{{\alpha\,}\negthinspace\vartriangle\negthinspace{\,\beta}}\,\Big)\bigg)\;\;\text{\rm mod\ }I^{2}\;=\hfill

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill=\;\Big(\pm 2\,\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\beta}\,\check{X}^{\,\pm}_{\alpha\oplus\beta}\Big)\;\text{\rm mod\ }I^{2}\;\;\mapsto\;\;\pm\,4\,\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\beta}\,x^{\,\pm}_{\alpha\oplus\beta}\,=\,4\,\big[\,x_{\alpha}^{\pm}\,,\,x_{\beta}^{\pm}\,\big]\,=\,\big[\,2\,x_{\alpha}^{\pm}\,,\,2\,x_{\beta}^{\pm}\,\big]

— where we wrote
\,{(n)}_{q}:={{\,q^{n}-1\,}\over{\,q-1\,}}=\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}q^{s}\,
and
\,{(-n)}_{q}:=-q^{-1}{(n)}_{q^{-1}}\,
for all
\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\,
and we took into account that
\,\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\beta}=\mathsf{a}_{\alpha,\,\alpha\oplus\beta}\,
— so that (
4.1
) is proved.
∎

The previous analysis has also the following, additional outcome:

Corollary 4.2.5
.

The Hopf algebra
\;F\big[G^{*}_{\!X}\big]\,=\,\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{1}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,:=\,\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\Big/(\,q-1)\,\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\;
is isomorphic to the Hopf algebra

\;\Bbbk\Big[{\big\{X^{+}_{\alpha}\,,K^{\pm 1}_{\alpha},\,X^{-}_{\alpha}\big\}}_{\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)}\Big]\;

of polynomials / Laurent polynomials in the variables
X^{+}_{\alpha}\,
,
X^{-}_{\alpha}
(non-invertible) and
K^{\pm 1}_{\alpha}
(invertible), for all
\,\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
. Thus, the Poisson group
G^{*}_{\!X}\,
, as an affine variety, is the direct product of an affine space

\,\mathcal{X}^{+}\!:=\textsl{Spec}\Big(\Bbbk\Big[{\big\{X^{+}_{\alpha}\big\}}_{\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)}\Big]\Big)
,
an affine torus

\,\mathcal{K}:=\textsl{Spec}\Big(\Bbbk\Big[{\big\{K^{\pm 1}_{\alpha}\big\}}_{\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)}\Big]\Big)
,
and another affine space

\,\mathcal{X}^{-}\!:=\textsl{Spec}\Big(\Bbbk\Big[{\big\{X^{-}_{\alpha}\big\}}_{\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)}\Big]\Big)
,
all having infinite dimension equal to
\,\big|\mathsf{Int}(X)\big|\,
,  in short
\;G^{*}_{\!X}\,\cong\,\mathcal{X}^{+\!}\times\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{X}^{-}\;
.

Finally, the subvarieties
\,\mathcal{X}^{+\!}\times\mathcal{K}\,
,
\,\mathcal{K}\,
and
\,\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{X}^{-}\,
are Poisson subgroups of
\,G^{*}_{\!X}\,
.

Proof.

The claim follows from Theorem
4.2.4
and the presentation of
\,F\big[G^{*}_{\!X}\big]=\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{1}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
that one gets from the presentation of
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
in Proposition
4.2.2
.
∎

Observation 4.2.6
.

Much like we did for
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
in Observation
4.1.5
, it is worth remarking that in the formulas occurring in Proposition

all coefficients that show up actually belong to the (sub)ring

{\mathbb{Z}}\big[\,q,q^{-1}\big]\,
. Thus the Hopf algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
can actually be defined
over

{\mathbb{Z}}\big[\,q,q^{-1}\big]\,
,  hence one can consider
R
–integral forms of it over any ring
R\,
,  hence possibly look for specializations at roots of unity, etc., much like in the study of quantum groups by Kac, De Concini, Procesi and others, including — in an infinite dimensional setting, such as is that of
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
— the study in
[
Bec94
,
Bec96
]
for the case of quantum affine Kac–Moody algebras.

4.3.
Polynomial QDP for
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
: the intrinsic recipe

In §
4.2
above we introduced a
R_{q}
–subalgebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
, we proved that it is a Hopf
R_{q}
–algebra, and even a QFA that is a quantization of
G_{X}^{*}\,
, a well-defined affine Poisson group dual to
\mathfrak{g}_{X}\,
. In this sense, we have found a concrete realization of the QDP for the polynomial QUEA
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
.

Note that the very definition of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
strictly mimics the similar constructions for the standard polynomial QUEA
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
associated (following Drinfeld and Jimbo) with a Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of finite or affine type (cf.
[
Gav98-2
,
Gav00
]
, and references therein). In fact, the present framework is even more convenient as we already have in
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
,  by definition, some built-in “quantum root vectors”
\dot{X}{}_{\alpha}^{\pm}
that lift (up to a factor 2) the “root vectors”
x_{\alpha}^{\pm}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
— whereas in the case of finite or affine Kac-Moody
\mathfrak{g}
one has to construct them out of “simple root vectors”.

We shall now show that, like we did with
\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
for the “non-polynomial version of the QDP, we can provide for
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
an intrinsic description.

Definition 4.3.1
.

For every
\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\,
, let
\,\delta_{n}:=({\textsl{id}}-\iota\circ\epsilon)\circ\Delta^{(n)}\,
. Then for
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
as in Definition
4.1.1
, we set

{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\;:=\;\Big\{\,\eta\in\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,\Big|\;\delta_{n}(\eta)\in{(\,q-1)}^{n}\,{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\otimes n}\;\;\forall\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}\,\Big\}

\oslash

Indeed, the above just applies the general definition of the Drinfeld’s functor
\,H\mapsto H^{\prime}\,
for Hopf algebras
H
in much larger generality (than QUEA’s alone), which is detailed in
[
Gav07
, §2.1]
extending Drinfeld’s original idea.

The key point now is our next result:

Theorem 4.3.2
.

With notation as before, we have
\,\;\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,=\,{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\;\,
.

Proof.

The proof adapts the arguments used for Theorem
3.3.2
, up to technicalities.

By the properties of the maps
\delta_{n}\,(\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\,)\,
explained in
[
Gav07
, Lemma 3.2]
, one gets easily that
\,{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\,
is a unital
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
–subalgebra of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
. Moreover, a direct check shows that
\;\overline{H}_{\alpha}\,,\overline{X}{}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\in{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\;
for all
\,\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
;  but then it is also
\,\overline{K}{}_{\alpha}^{+1}=1+\overline{H}_{\alpha}\,\in\,{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\,
. On the other hand, we have

[formula]

\overline{K}{}_{\alpha}^{\,+1}\cdot{\textstyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}}{(-1)}^{n}\overline{H}{}_{\alpha}^{\,n}\;=\;1-{(-1)}^{N}\overline{H}{}_{\alpha}^{\,N}\;\in\;1+{(\,q-1)}^{N}\,\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})

and then multiplying by
\,K_{\alpha}^{\,+1}=\overline{K}{}_{\alpha}^{\,+1}\,
this yields

\hfil\displaystyle\quad{\textstyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}}{(-1)}^{n}\overline{H}{}_{\alpha}^{\,n}\;=\;K_{\alpha}^{\,-1}-{(-1)}^{N}\overline{H}{}_{\alpha}^{\,N}\,\overline{K}{}_{\alpha}^{\,-1}\quad\Longrightarrow\hfill

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill\Longrightarrow\quad K_{\alpha}^{\,-1}\,=\,{\textstyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}}{(-1)}^{n}\overline{H}{}_{\alpha}^{\,n}+{(-1)}^{N}\overline{H}{}_{\alpha}^{\,N}\,\overline{K}{}_{\alpha}^{\,-1}\,=\;\eta_{N}+\chi_{N}\quad

for all
\,N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}\,
,  where
\,\eta_{N}\in{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\,
and
\,\chi_{N}\in{(\,q-1)}^{N}\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
. But then

[formula]

\delta_{N}\big(K_{\alpha}^{\,-1}\big)\;=\;\delta_{N}(\eta_{N})+\delta_{N}(\chi_{N})\;\in\;{(\,q-1)}^{N}\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})^{\otimes N}

whence
\,K_{\alpha}^{\,-1}\in{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\,
as well (
\,\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
). Thus all the generators of
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
belong to
{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}
and the latter is a
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
–algebra, hence
\;\widetilde{U}_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\subseteq{U_{\hbar}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\;
.

As to the converse inclusion, we need first to lay the groundwork.

First of all, let us denote by
\,\mathcal{U}_{q}\big(\hskip 1.0pt\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{X}\big)\,
, resp.
\,\mathcal{U}_{q}\big(\,\mathfrak{f}_{X}\big)\,
, resp.
\,\mathcal{U}_{q}\big(\hskip 1.0pt\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{X}\big)\,
,  the unital
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
–subalgebra of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
generated by the
\dot{X}{}^{+}_{\alpha}
’s,  resp. the
\dot{K}{}^{\pm 1}_{\alpha}
’s and the
\dot{H}_{\alpha}
’s,  resp. the
\dot{X}{}^{-}_{\alpha}
’s. Then, by a standard argument, one easily deduces from the relations in the presentation of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})
— cf. Proposition
4.1.2
— that the latter admits the “triangular decomposition”

(4.2)

\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\;\;\cong\;\;\mathcal{U}_{q}\big(\hskip 1.0pt\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{X}\big)\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}\big(\,\mathfrak{f}_{X}\big)\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}\big(\hskip 1.0pt\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{X}\big)

As a second step, let us fix any total order on
\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
. Then, from the relations among all the
\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{\,\pm}
’s, one easily finds (again by standard arguments) that the set of ordered monomials

\;\Big\{\,{\overrightarrow{\prod}_{\alpha}}{\big(\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{\pm}}\,\Big|\;n_{\alpha}^{\pm}\in{\mathbb{N}}\,,\;\forall\;\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,,\textsl{\ a.\ a.\ }0\Big\}\;

— where “
\,\overrightarrow{\prod}\,
” denotes an ordered product, and the
\alpha
’s range within
\mathsf{Int}(X)\,
,  and again “
a. a. 0
’’ stands for ‘‘ almost all 0 ’’ --- is a PBW-type spanning set of
\,\mathcal{U}_{q}\big(\hskip 1.0pt\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}_{X}\big)
over
\,{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}\,
— in fact, it is even a
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
–basis. Similarly, from the relations among all the
\dot{H}_{\alpha}
’s and all the
\dot{K}{}^{\pm 1}_{\gamma}
’s one finds that the set of (ordered) products

\;\Big\{\,{\overrightarrow{\prod}_{\alpha}}{\big(\dot{H}_{\alpha}\big)}^{e^{+}_{\alpha}}{\big(\dot{K}_{\alpha}^{-1}\big)}^{n^{-}_{\alpha}}\,\Big|\;e_{\alpha}^{\pm}\in{\mathbb{N}}\,,\;0\in\big\{e_{\alpha}^{+},e_{\alpha}^{-}\big\}\,,\;\forall\;\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,,\textsl{\ a.\ a.\ }0\Big\}\;

is a PBW-type spanning set of
\,\mathcal{U}_{q}\big(\,\mathfrak{f}_{X}\big)
over
\,{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}\,
. In the end, this together with (
4.2
) tells us that the set of all ordered monomials of the form

(4.3)

{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{+}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{+}}\cdot\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{H}_{\alpha}\big)}^{e^{+}_{\alpha}}\!{\big(\dot{K}_{\alpha}^{-1}\big)}^{e^{-}_{\alpha}}\cdot\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{-}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{-}}

is a
{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}
–spanning set for
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
. On the other hand, we recall — cf. the proof of Theorem
3.3.2
— that, at the semiclassical level), the set of ordered monomials

(4.4)

\bigg\{\;{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(x_{\alpha}^{+}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{+}}\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}\,\xi_{\alpha}^{\,e_{\alpha}}\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(x_{\alpha}^{-}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{-}}\;\bigg|\;\,n_{\alpha}^{+}\,,e_{\alpha}\,,n_{\alpha}^{-}\in{\mathbb{N}}\,,\,\;\forall\;\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)\,,\textsl{\ a.\ a.\ }0\;\bigg\}

is a PBW-type
\Bbbk
–spanning set for
U(\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
. Note also that, through the isomorphism in Theorem
4.1.4
, every PBW monomial in (
4.3
) maps onto a corresponding monomial in (
4.4
) multiplied by a suitable power of 2 .

Now let
\,\eta\in{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}\,
. Using the spanning set in (
4.3
), we can expand
\eta
as

(4.5)

\eta\;=\,{\textstyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}}\,{(\,q-1)}^{\ell}\,\eta_{\ell}\;\quad\text{with}\quad\eta_{\ell}\in\big(\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\setminus(\,q-1)\,\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\big)\cup\{0\}\quad\forall\;\;\ell

in such a way that each non-zero
\eta_{\ell}
in (
4.5
) in turn expands as a linear combination of quantum PBW monomials from (
4.3
), say

(4.6)

\eta_{\ell}\;\;=\;\hskip-3.0pt\sum_{\underline{n}^{+}\!,\,\underline{e}^{+},\,\underline{e}^{-},\,\underline{n}^{-}}\hskip-9.0pt\kappa_{\ell\,;\,\underline{n}^{-}\!,\,\underline{e}^{-}}^{\,\underline{e}^{+}\!,\,\underline{e}^{+}}\cdot{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{+}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{+}}\cdot\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{H}_{\alpha}\big)}^{e^{+}_{\alpha}}\!{\big(\dot{K}_{\alpha}^{-1}\big)}^{e^{-}_{\alpha}}\cdot\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{-}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{-}}

with
\;\kappa_{\ell\,;\,\underline{n}^{-}\!,\,\underline{e}^{-}}^{\,\underline{e}^{+}\!,\,\underline{e}^{+}}\in{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}\setminus(\,q-1)\,{\Bbbk\big[\,q\,,q^{-1}\big]}\;
— where
\underline{n}^{\pm}
, resp.
\underline{e}^{\pm}\,
, is the string of the
n_{\alpha}^{\pm}
’s, resp. of the
e_{\alpha}^{\pm}
’s.

If
\;{(\,q-1)}^{\ell}\,\eta_{\ell}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\;
for all
\,\ell\in\{0\,,\dots,N\}\,
,  then
\,\eta\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
— by the first part of the proof — and we are done. Otherwise, let
\,\ell_{0}\in\{0\,,\dots,N\}\,
be the smallest index such that
\;{(\,q-1)}^{\,\ell_{0}}\,\eta_{\ell_{0}}\not\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\;
;  then

\eta_{+}\;:=\;\eta-{\textstyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\ell_{0}-1}}{(\,q-1)}^{\,\ell}\,\eta_{\ell}\;=\;{\textstyle\sum_{\ell=\ell_{0}}^{N}}{(\,q-1)}^{\,\ell}\,\eta_{\ell}\,\;\in\;\,{\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})}^{\prime}

By construction we have
\,\eta_{\ell_{0}}\in\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\setminus{(\,q-1)}\,\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
,  thus the coset

\overline{\eta}_{\ell_{0}}\;:=\;\big(\,\eta_{\ell_{0}}\!\!\mod{(\,q-1)}\,\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,\big)

in
\;\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\Big/{(\,q-1)}\,\mathcal{U}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,=\,U(\mathfrak{g}_{X})\;
is non-zero, hence it has a well-defined degree
\,\partial\big(\overline{\eta}_{\ell_{0}}\big)\,
with respect to the standard filtration in
U(\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
. But then
[
EtK96
, Lemma 4.12]
gives
\,\partial\big(\overline{\eta}_{\ell_{0}}\big)\leq\ell_{0}\,
:  therefore, the cosets of all quantum PBW monomials occurring (with non-zero coefficients) in (
4.6
) when
\,\ell=\ell_{0}\,
are (semiclassical) PBW monomials from (
4.4
) that have degree bounded by
\,\ell_{0}\,
. Since the coset of the quantum PBW monomial

\;{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{+}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{+}}\cdot\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{H}_{\alpha}\big)}^{e^{+}_{\alpha}}\!{\big(\dot{K}_{\alpha}^{-1}\big)}^{e^{-}_{\alpha}}\cdot\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{-}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{-}}\;

is the rescaled (semiclassical) PBW monomial

\;2^{|\,\underline{n}^{+}|+|\,\underline{e}^{+}|+|\,\underline{n}^{-}|}\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(x_{\alpha}^{+}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{+}}\cdot\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\xi_{\alpha}\big)}^{e^{+}_{\alpha}}\cdot\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(x_{\alpha}^{-}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{-}}\;

— where by
\,|\,\underline{s}|\,
we denote the sum of all elements in any string
\,\underline{s}={(s_{\alpha})}_{\alpha\in\mathsf{Int}(X)}\,
with almost all entries being zero — we conclude that this bound on the degree reads

|\,\underline{n}^{+}|+|\,\underline{e}^{+}|+|\,\underline{n}^{-}|\;\leq\;\ell_{0}\qquad\qquad\forall\quad\kappa_{\ell\,;\,\underline{n}^{-}\!,\,\underline{e}^{-}}^{\,\underline{e}^{+}\!,\,\underline{e}^{+}}\not=\,0

But this implies

\hfil\displaystyle{(\,q-1)}^{\ell_{0}}\,\eta_{\ell_{0}}\;=\hskip-7.0pt\sum_{\underline{n}^{+}\!,\,\underline{e}^{+},\,\underline{e}^{-},\,\underline{n}^{-}}\hskip-13.0pt\kappa_{\ell\,;\,\underline{n}^{-}\!,\,\underline{e}^{-}}^{\,\underline{e}^{+}\!,\,\underline{e}^{+}}\,{(\,q-1)}^{\ell_{0}}\cdot{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{+}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{+}}\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{H}_{\alpha}\big)}^{e^{+}_{\alpha}}\!{\big(\dot{K}_{\alpha}^{-1}\big)}^{e^{-}_{\alpha}}\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\dot{X}_{\alpha}^{-}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{-}}\;=

\hfil\displaystyle=\hskip-7.0pt\sum_{\underline{n}^{+}\!,\,\underline{e}^{+},\,\underline{e}^{-},\,\underline{n}^{-}}\hskip-13.0pt\kappa_{\ell_{0};\,\underline{n}^{-}\!,\,\underline{e}^{-}}^{\,\underline{e}^{+}\!,\,\underline{e}^{+}}\,{(\,q-1)}^{\ell_{0}-|\,\underline{n}^{+}|+|\,\underline{e}^{+}|+|\,\underline{n}^{-}|}\cdot{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\bar{X}_{\alpha}^{+}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{+}}\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\bar{H}_{\alpha}\big)}^{e^{+}_{\alpha}}\!{\big(\bar{K}_{\alpha}^{-1}\big)}^{e^{-}_{\alpha}}\,{\textstyle\overrightarrow{\prod\limits_{\alpha}}}{\big(\bar{X}_{\alpha}^{-}\big)}^{n_{\alpha}^{-}}

which means
\;{(\,q-1)}^{\ell_{0}}\,\eta_{\ell_{0}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\,
— by the first part of the proof — against the assumption
\;{(\,q-1)}^{\ell_{0}}\,\eta_{\ell_{0}}\not\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\,\mathfrak{g}_{X})\;
,  a contradiction.
∎

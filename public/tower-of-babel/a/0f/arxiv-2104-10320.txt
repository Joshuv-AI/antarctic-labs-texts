Feedback Stabilization and Output Tracking for Discrete-Time Lipschitz Nonlinear Systems via Iterative Convex Approximations
Sebastian A. Nugroh o, Suyash C. Vishno i, Ahmad F. Tah a, Christian G. Claude l

Abstract
The stabilization of unstable nonlinear systems and tracking control are challenging engineering problems due to the encompassed nonlinearities in dynamic systems and their scale. In the past decades, numerous observer-based control designs for dynamic systems in which the nonlinearity belongs to Lipschitz functions have been proposed. However, most of them only focus on output feedback and consequently, state feedback design remains less developed. To that end, this paper is dedicated to the problem of full-state feedback controller design for discrete-time Lipschitz nonlinear systems. In addition, we present a simple iterative method for improving the convergence of the closed-loop performance. It is later demonstrated that our approach can be conveniently extended and utilized for output tracking.

The majority of the research focuses on asymptotic stabilization of the system with the help of output feedback or observer-based controllers designed for a class of Lipschitz nonlinear systems. Full State Feedback Controller Synthesis

In this section, we propose a state feedback controller for the system given in (
1

We first consider a regulation problem with a state feedback controller. The state feedback control law is designed as follows

\displaystyle\bm{u}[k]=-\bm{K}\bm{x}[k],

(4)

where
\bm{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is the constant matrix gain chosen such that the nonlinear discrete-time system (

Assumption 1.

There exists an equilibrium point
\bm{x}_{eq}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for (
1
) with control law (
4
) such that

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{eq}

\displaystyle=\bm{A}\bm{x}_{eq}+\bm{G}\bm{f}(\bm{x}_{eq},\bm{u}_{eq})+\bm{B}\bm{u}_{eq},

(5)

where
\bm{u}_{eq}=-\bm{K}\bm{x}_{eq}
is the input vector at equilibrium.

Let us define
\bm{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
\bm{e}:=\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{eq}
. That is,
\bm{e}
represents the deviation of the state
\bm{x}
from its equilibrium
\bm{x}_{eq}
. From (
1
) and (
5

4

\displaystyle\bm{e}[k+1]

\displaystyle=(\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K})\bm{e}[k]+\bm{G}\Delta\bm{f}[k],

(6)

where
\Delta\bm{f}[k]:=\bm{f}(\bm{x}[k],\bm{u}[k])-\bm{f}(\bm{x}_{eq},\bm{u}_{eq}).

Now, our objective is to find a computationally amenable way to compute
\bm{K}
such that the system (
6

6

Theorem 1.

Consider nonlinear discrete-time system (
6
) with control law presented in (
4
). The system in (
6
) is exponentially stable if there exist
\bm{Q}\in\mathbb{S}^{n},\;\bm{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\;\epsilon,\kappa,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
which solve the following feasibility problem

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}(\alpha-1)\bm{Q}+\epsilon(\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{u}\kappa)^{2}\bm{I}&*&*\\
\bm{O}&-\epsilon\bm{I}&*\\
\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K}&\bm{G}&-\bm{Q}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\prec 0

(7a)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}-\kappa\bm{I}&\bm{K}\\
\bm{K}^{\top}&-\kappa\bm{I}\end{bmatrix}\preceq 0

(7b)

\displaystyle\bm{Q}\succ 0,\epsilon\geq 0,\kappa>0,0<\alpha<1.

(7c)

Proof.
Consider the nonlinear discrete-time system (
6
). Note that the term
\Delta\bm{f}(\cdot)
is equal to

\displaystyle\lvert\lvert\Delta\bm{f}\rvert\rvert_{2}=\lvert\lvert\bm{f}(\bm{x},\bm{u})-\bm{f}(\bm{x}_{eq},\bm{u}_{eq})\rvert\rvert_{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\lvert\lvert(\bm{f}(\bm{x},\bm{u})-\bm{f}(\bm{x}_{eq},\bm{u}))+(\bm{f}(\bm{x}_{eq},\bm{u})-\bm{f}(\bm{x}_{eq},\bm{u}_{eq}))\rvert\rvert_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\lvert\lvert\bm{f}(\bm{x},\bm{u})-f(\bm{x}_{eq},\bm{u})\rvert\rvert_{2}+\lvert\lvert\bm{f}(\bm{x}_{eq},\bm{u})-\bm{f}(\bm{x}_{eq},\bm{u}_{eq})\rvert\rvert_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\gamma_{x}\lvert\lvert\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{eq}\rvert\rvert_{2}+\gamma_{u}\lvert\lvert\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{eq}\rvert\rvert_{2}

\displaystyle\quad=(\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{u}\lvert\lvert\bm{K}\rvert\rvert_{2})\lvert\lvert\bm{e}\rvert\rvert_{2}

(8)

To design a stabilizing
\bm{K}
, then consider a Lyapunov function candidate

V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
constructed as

\displaystyle V(\bm{e}[k])=\bm{e}[k]^{\top}\bm{Q}\bm{e}[k],Q\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

(9)

for
Q\succ 0
. First, we will show that the condition
\Delta V(\bm{e}[k])+\alpha V(\bm{e}[k])\leq 0
for
0<\alpha<1
leads to exponential stability.
Note that
\Delta V(\bm{e}[k])+\alpha V(\bm{e}[k])\leq 0
for
0<\alpha<1
implies

\displaystyle V(\bm{e}[k+1])\leq(\alpha-1)V(\bm{e}[k]).\,

(10)

It can be shown by induction that the inequality (
10

\displaystyle V(\bm{e}[k])\leq(\alpha-1)^{k}V(\bm{e}[0]).\,

(11)

for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. From (
9

\displaystyle\lvert\lvert\bm{e}[k]\rvert\rvert_{2}^{2}\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\bm{Q})^{-1}V(\bm{e}[k]).

(12)

Now, from (
11

\displaystyle\lvert\lvert\bm{e}[k]\rvert\rvert_{2}^{2}\leq(\alpha-1)^{k}\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\bm{Q})^{-1}V(\bm{e}[0]).

Since
V(\bm{e}[0])\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(\bm{Q})\lvert\lvert\bm{e}[k]\rvert\rvert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lvert\lvert\bm{e}[k]\rvert\rvert_{2}\leq(\alpha-1)^{\frac{k}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(\bm{Q})}{\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\bm{Q})}}\lvert\lvert\bm{e}[0]\rvert\rvert_{2},

(14)

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}[k]^{\top}\bm{\Xi}\bm{\xi}[k]\leq 0,

(15)

for all

From (
2

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}[k]^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}-(\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{u}\kappa)^{2}\bm{I}&*\\

(16)

Applying the S-lemma

15

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\Psi}&*\\

(17)

\displaystyle\bm{\Psi}

\displaystyle:=(\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K})^{\top}\bm{Q}(\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K})+(\alpha-1)\bm{Q}

\blacksquare

In some practical situations however, it is desirable to steer some of the states to follow some reference points. To achieve this, define an integrator as follows

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{y}[k+1]=\bm{E}\bm{e}_{y}[k].

(18)

\bm{e}_{y}:=\bm{r}-\bm{C}\bm{x}

IV
Solution Approach via LMIs

The optimization problem
\mathbf{P1}
and the feasibility problem described in (
7
) require us to solve bilinear matrix inequalities (BMIs) which cannot be solved by means of a standard LMI solver like MOSEK
[
15
]
. On that regard, the following steps are proposed first to solve feasibility problem described in (
7
).

Step 1:
For a fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
and predefined
\varrho<0
, solve

\displaystyle\minimize_{\bm{X},\,\bm{Z},\,\nu}\quad\nu

(21a)

\displaystyle\subjectto\;\;

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}(\alpha-1)\bm{X}&\bm{A}\bm{X}-\bm{B}\bm{Z}\\
\bm{X}\bm{A}^{\top}-\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{B}^{\top}&-\bm{X}\end{bmatrix}-\nu\bm{I}\prec 0

(21b)

\displaystyle\bm{X}\succ 0,\varrho\leq\nu<0.

(21c)

Step 2:
For a fixed
\kappa>0
, set
\bm{Q}_{0}=\bm{X}^{-1}
and solve

\displaystyle\find\quad\epsilon\geq 0,\,\bm{K},\,0<\alpha<1

(22a)

\displaystyle\subjectto\;\;

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}(\alpha-1)\bm{Q}_{0}+\epsilon\gamma_{\kappa}^{2}\bm{I}&0&\bm{A}^{\top}-\bm{K}^{\top}\bm{B}^{\top}\\
0&-\epsilon\bm{I}&\bm{G}^{\top}\\
\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K}&\bm{G}&-\bm{Q}_{0}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\prec 0

(22b)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}-\kappa\bm{I}&\bm{K}\\
\bm{K}^{\top}&-\kappa\bm{I}\end{bmatrix}\preceq 0,

(22c)

where
\gamma_{\kappa}:=\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{u}\kappa
.

The rationale behind Step 1 is motivated by the fact that the matrix
\bm{\Psi}
should be negative definite. Indeed, the matrix inequality
\bm{\Psi}\prec 0
is equivalent to the following condition

(\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K})^{\top}\bm{Q}(\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K})+(\alpha-1)\bm{Q}\prec-\epsilon(\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{u}\kappa)^{2}\bm{I}.

Due to
\epsilon(\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{u}\kappa)^{2}
being nonnegative and its value generally increases as the Lipschitz constants are increasing, the largest eigenvalue of
(\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K})^{\top}\bm{Q}(\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K})+(\alpha-1)\bm{Q}
has to be made sufficiently negative. Since
(\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K})^{\top}\bm{Q}(\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K})+(\alpha-1)\bm{Q}\prec 0
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}(\alpha-1)\bm{X}&\bm{A}\bm{X}-\bm{B}\bm{Z}\\
\bm{X}\bm{A}^{\top}-\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{B}^{\top}&-\bm{X}\end{bmatrix}\prec 0,

(23)

where
\bm{X}=\bm{Q}^{-1}
and
\bm{Z}=\bm{K}\bm{Q}^{-1}
. Thanks to the Schur complement lemma, then the problem described by (
21
) is proposed, which bounds the left-hand side of (
23
).

Remark 1.

If the problem described by (
22
) is feasible for a fixed
\kappa>0
and a
\bm{Q}_{0}\succ 0
, then the system (
6
) is exponentially stable with control law (
4
). If (
22
) is otherwise infeasible, then other possible values for
\kappa
and
\bm{Q}_{0}
have to be sought and used until (
22
) has at least a feasible solution. Unfortunately, finding a systematic method to find
\kappa
and
\bm{Q}_{0}
such that (
22
) is feasible—even though the existence of a solution can be guaranteed—is difficult and beyond the scope of the paper. To that end, this research direction is left for future research.

Note that the solvability of the problem described by (
22
) does not necessarily give a rapid closed-loop stabilization’s convergence rate. Hence, to improve it, we propose the following approach to optimize the convergence rate, which in principle, solving
P1
by means of
successive convex approximation
(SCA), which is pioneered in
[
16
]
. The SCA basically approximates the BMIs with a series LMIs. In doing so, each of the BMI terms is firstly expressed as a difference between convex and concave functions. Later, the concave parts are over-approximated with their first order Taylor approximations.
This approach have been successfully implemented to tackle BMIs in several applications such as sensor and actuator selection problem
[
17
]
and robust control of power networks
[
18
]
.
To proceed with this approach, the nonconvex problem
P1
is transformed into an equivalent problem—provided below.

\displaystyle(\mathbf{P2})\;\;

\displaystyle\minimize_{\bm{Q},\bm{K},\kappa,\epsilon,\alpha,t,w}\quad t

(24a)

\displaystyle\subjectto\;\;

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}(\alpha-1)\bm{Q}+\epsilon w\bm{I}&*&*\\
\bm{O}&-\epsilon\bm{I}&*\\
\bm{A}-\bm{B}\bm{K}&\bm{G}&-\bm{Q}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\prec 0

(24b)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}-w&\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{u}\kappa\\
\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{u}\kappa&-1\end{bmatrix}\preceq 0,\;

(24c)

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:SDP_base_3},\,\eqref{eq:SDP_base_2},\,\bm{Q}\preceq t\bm{I},\,t>0,\,w\geq 0

(24d)

The additional constraint (
24c
) in
P2
above is a consequence of the over-bounding of the term
\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{u}\kappa
with a new scalar variable
w\geq 0
such that
(\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{u}\kappa)^{2}\leq w
.

The problem
P2
is still highly nonconvex due to the terms
\bm{Q}^{-1}
,
\alpha\bm{Q}
and
\epsilon w
. To use the SCA, each of these terms need to be convexified. First, we decompose the bilinear term
\alpha\bm{Q}
as follows

\displaystyle\alpha\bm{Q}=\dfrac{1}{4}\left(\left(\alpha\bm{I}+\bm{Q}\right)^{\top}\left(\alpha\bm{I}+\bm{Q}\right)-\left(\alpha\bm{I}-\bm{Q}\right)^{\top}\left(\alpha\bm{I}-\bm{Q}\right)\right).

(25)

Define
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\bm{Q}):=-\left(\alpha\bm{I}-\bm{Q}\right)^{\top}\left(\alpha\bm{I}-\bm{Q}\right)
. Now, let
\tilde{\alpha}
and
\tilde{\bm{Q}}
be the points of linearization. Since
\mathcal{H}(\cdot)
is concave, then its first order Taylor approximation is a global over-estimator. As such,
\mathcal{H}(\cdot)
can be over-approximated by a linear function

\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\bm{Q})\preceq\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha,\bm{Q},\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\bm{Q}})
where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha,\bm{Q},\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\bm{Q}})&:=(\tilde{\alpha}^{2}-2\tilde{\alpha}\alpha)\bm{I}+2\tilde{\alpha}\bm{Q}+2\alpha\tilde{\bm{Q}}\\
&\quad\tilde{\bm{Q}}^{2}-2\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\bm{Q}}-\tilde{\bm{Q}}{\bm{Q}}-{\bm{Q}}\tilde{\bm{Q}}.\end{split}

From (
25
) and
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\bm{Q})\preceq\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha,\bm{Q},\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\bm{Q}})
, we thus have

\displaystyle\alpha\bm{Q}\preceq\dfrac{1}{4}\left(\left(\alpha\bm{I}+\bm{Q}\right)^{\top}\left(\alpha\bm{I}+\bm{Q}\right)+\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha,\bm{Q},\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\bm{Q}})\right).

(26)

Second, we decompose the bilinear term
\epsilon w
as follows

\displaystyle\epsilon w=\dfrac{1}{4}\left((\epsilon+w)^{2}-(\epsilon-w)^{2}\right).

(27)

Let
\mathcal{F}(\epsilon,w):=-(\epsilon-w)^{2}
. Its first order Taylor approximation can be represented by the following linear function

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\begin{split}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\epsilon,w,\tilde{\epsilon},\tilde{w})&:=\tilde{\epsilon}^{2}+\tilde{w}^{2}-2\tilde{\epsilon}\tilde{w}-2\epsilon(\tilde{\epsilon}-\tilde{w})-2w(\tilde{w}-\tilde{\epsilon}),\end{split}

where
\tilde{\epsilon}
and
\tilde{w}
are the points of linearization. Since
\mathcal{F}(\epsilon,w)\leq\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\epsilon,w,\tilde{\epsilon},\tilde{w})
and from (
27
), we get

\displaystyle\epsilon w\leq\dfrac{1}{4}\left((\epsilon+w)^{2}+\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\epsilon,w,\tilde{\epsilon},\tilde{w})\right).

(28)

Based on (
26
) and (
28
), the left-hand side of (
24b
) can be over approximated by the following matrix inequality

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt\begin{bmatrix}-\bm{Q}+\tfrac{1}{4}\left(\alpha\bm{I}+\bm{Q}\right)^{\top}\left(\alpha\bm{I}+\bm{Q}\right)\\

VI
Concluding Remarks and Future Work

In this paper we present a new sufficient condition to achieve exponential stability for discrete-time Lipschitz nonlinear systems. The prominent feature of our approach is its ability to accommodate nonlinear systems where the states and inputs are nonlinearly coupled. The proposed methodology can also be directly tailored for output regulation purpose. In addition to this, we present a simple algorithm to improve the convergence rate of the closed-loop performance which only requires the utilization of a convex programming solver. Potential future research directions include extending the proposed approach for nonlinear discrete-time systems which nonlinearity belongs to function sets other than Lipschitz and developing a robust stabilization method to tackle disturbance and unknown inputs.

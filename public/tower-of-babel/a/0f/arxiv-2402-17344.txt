Impact of strong magnetic field, baryon chemical potential, and medium anisotropy on polarization and spin alignment of hadrons
Bhagyarathi Sahoo, Captain R. Singh, Raghunath Sahoo

Abstract
The recent observation of global spin polarization of Λ \Lambda ( Λ ¯ \bar{\Lambda} ) hyperons and the spin alignment of ϕ \phi and K ∗ 0 K^{*0} vector mesons create remarkable interest in investigating the particle polarization in the relativistic fluid produced in heavy-ion collisions at GeV/TeV energies. Among other sources of spin polarization phenomena, the Debye mass of a medium plays a crucial role in particle polarization. Any modification brought to the effective mass due to the temperature, strong magnetic field ( e ​ B eB ), baryonic chemical potential ( μ B \mu_{B} ), medium anisotropy ( ξ \xi ), and vorticity, etc., certainly affects the particle spin polarization. In this work, we explore the global hyperon spin polarization and the spin alignment of vector mesons corresponding to the strong magnetic field, baryonic chemical potential, and medium anisotropy. We find that the degree of spin polarization is flavor-dependent for hyperons. Meanwhile, vector meson spin alignment depends on the hadronization mechanisms of initially polarized quarks and anti-quarks. Medium anisotropy significantly changes the degree of spin polarization compared to the magnetic field and baryon chemical potential.

II.1
Debye Mass under Strong Magnetic Field

The magnetic field produced in the peripheral heavy-ion collisions is believed to decay quickly. So, it is expected to only affect the partonic phase during the early stage of the evolution process. Magnetic fields tend to align charged particles in a specific direction, resulting in the polarization of the quarks. Given that quarks possess a fractional electric charge, a magnetic field could potentially modify the orientation of quarks within the QGP phase. Consequently, in the presence of a strong magnetic field, the total Debye mass for partons in the static limit has been calculated through the temporal component of one loop vacuum self-energy diagram and has the following form

IV
Conclusion

In summary, we explored the global spin polarization of hyperons and spin alignment of vector mesons in the presence of a magnetic field, baryon chemical potential, and medium momentum-space anisotropy. The individual and combined effect of these parameters on hyperons and vector mesons spin polarization have been studied in the recombination and fragmentation process of hadronization. We found that the spin polarization of hyperons and spin alignment of vector mesons increases with the increase in magnetic field. In this study, the baryon chemical potential has negligible dependence on hyperon spin polarization. The medium anisotropy significantly affects the spin polarization and spin alignment of hadrons. It is interesting to note that the recombination process dominates at low
p_{T}
in comparison with the fragmentation process. It implies that hadronization mechanisms play a crucial role to estimate the spin polarization of hadrons. Therefore, for a comprehensive study, one may explore the global spin polarization of hadrons considering different hadronization mechanisms in realistic hydrodynamic or transport model simulations.

The hyperon spin polarization obtained through the global quark polarization method is comparably higher than the experimentally measured hyperon spin polarization in heavy-ion collisions at mid-rapidity at the RHIC energies. However, a thermodynamic three fluid dynamic (3FD) model considering total rapidity into account predicts a higher net hyperons spin polarization and it increases with the increasing center of mass energies
Ivanov:2019ern
;
Ivanov:2022ble
, which suggest to have comprehensive understanding of hyperon spin polarization phenomena, the total rapidity coverage is essential. In addition to the magnetic field, baryon chemical potential, and medium momentum-space anisotropy effects explored in this work, there may be various other sources that may affect the hyperon spin polarization. Such sources could be medium rotation, hadronic interactions, anisotropic flows in the medium, and fluctuation of other force fields such as vorticity, temperature gradient, shear tensor, strong force field, etc. However, the contribution of these sources on hyperon spin polarization and spin alignment of vector mesons has yet to be studied collectively. Additionally, this Debye mass-based polarization may have a substantial implication on particle production yield and must be explored in future studies in ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions. The qualitative prediction of global spin polarization is an open challenge and requires further investigation.

\displaystyle\delta^{4}(p_{3}+p_{4}-p_{1}-p_{2})\delta^{2}(q_{T}+p_{T})\frac{d^{3}p_{3}}{2E_{3}}\frac{d^{3}p_{4}}{2E_{4}}

(30)

In the presence of magnetic field
eB
(suppose
\textbf{B}=B\hat{z}
) the single charged particle energy eigen value (
E_{iz}
) is given by

E_{iz}=\sqrt{p_{z}^{2}+m_{i}^{2}+2l|Q_{i}eB|}

(31)

where
l
= 0, 1, 2 ….. is the order of the Landau energy levels,
Q_{i}e
is the fractional charge of quarks, and
p_{z}
is the component of momentum along the z direction.

Furthermore, in the presence of the magnetic field, the three-dimensional integral is converted into the one-dimensional integral;

\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}=\frac{|Q_{i}eB|}{2\pi}\int\frac{dp_{z}}{2\pi}

(32)

Since
q_{T}
and
k_{T}
are the momentum transfer of the mediated gluons, the integral over
q_{T}
and
k_{T}
present in Eq.
30
remains unaffected in the presence of magnetic field at the lowest order.

So, the fermionic contribution of Eq.
30
can be written as

\int\delta^{4}(p_{3}+p_{4}-p_{1}-p_{2})\delta^{2}(p_{3T}-p_{4T})\frac{d^{3}p_{3}}{2E_{3}}\frac{d^{3}p_{4}}{2E_{4}}

(33)

The four-dimensional delta function function can be written as;

\delta^{4}(p_{3}+p_{4}-p_{1}-p_{2})=\delta(E_{3}+E_{4}-E_{1}-E_{2})\delta^{3}(\vec{p}_{3}+\vec{p}_{4}-\vec{p}_{1}-\vec{p}_{2})

(34)

In the presence of magnetic field the above Eq.
33
reduces to

\displaystyle(|Q_{i}eB|)^{2}(2\pi)^{2}\sum_{l}\int\delta(E_{3z}+E_{4z}-E_{1z}-E_{2z})

\displaystyle\delta(p_{3z}+p_{4z}-p_{1z}-p_{2z})\delta(p_{3z}-p_{4z})\frac{dp_{3z}}{2E_{3z}}\frac{dp_{4z}}{2E_{4z}}

(35)

Now Let’s solve
\delta(E_{3z}+E_{4z}-E_{1z}-E_{2z})

\displaystyle\delta(E_{3z}+E_{4z}-E_{1z}-E_{2z})=\delta\bigg(\sqrt{p_{3z}^{2}+m_{3}^{2}+2l|Q_{3}eB|}+\sqrt{p_{4z}^{2}+m_{4}^{2}+2l|Q_{4}eB|}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{p_{1z}^{2}+m_{1}^{2}+2l|Q_{1}eB|}-\sqrt{p_{2z}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}+2l|Q_{2}eB|}\bigg)

(36)

Using the property of the delta function, we have

\delta(f(x))=\sum_{x_{0}}\frac{\delta(x-x_{0})}{\bigg|\frac{df(x)}{dx}\bigg|_{x=x_{0}}}

(37)

where
x_{0}
is the root of the function f(x).

Let’s denote the function inside the delta function as
f(p_{3z})

\displaystyle f(p_{3z})=\sqrt{p_{3z}^{2}+m_{3}^{2}+2l|Q_{3}eB|}+\sqrt{p_{4z}^{2}+m_{4}^{2}+2l|Q_{4}eB|}

\displaystyle-E_{1z}-E_{2z}

(38)

Putting
f(p_{3z})
= 0, we get two roots of
p_{3z}
. Let’s suppose
p^{*}_{3z}
is the positive solution of
f(p_{3z})
;

and

\frac{df(p_{3z})}{dp_{3z}}\vline_{p_{3z}=p^{*}_{3z}}=\bigg(\frac{E_{3z}+E_{4z}}{E_{3z}E_{4z}}\bigg)p^{*}_{3z}

(39)

Using the energy conservation
E_{3z}+E_{4z}=E_{1z}+E_{2z}
and replacing Eq.
42
in Eq.
35
, we obtain;

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi|Q_{i}eB|)^{2}}{4}\sum_{l}\int\delta(p_{3z}-p^{*}_{3z})\delta(p_{3z}+p_{4z}-p_{1z}-p_{2z})

\displaystyle\delta(p_{3z}-p_{4z})\frac{dp_{3z}dp_{4z}}{(E_{1z}+E_{2z})p^{*}_{3z}}

(40)

=\frac{(2\pi|Q_{i}eB|)^{2}}{4}\sum_{l}\frac{1}{(E_{1z}+E_{2z})p_{3z}}\\

(41)

Using the property of the delta function, we obtain

\int\delta(p_{3z}+p_{4z}-p_{1z}-p_{2z})\delta(p_{3z}-p_{4z})dp_{3z}dp_{4z}=1

(42)

So, Eq.(11) becomes,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{\lambda_{3}}}{d^{2}x_{T}}=\sum_{l}\sum_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{4}}\frac{(2\pi|Q_{i}eB|)^{2}c_{qq}}{16F(E_{1z}+E_{2z})p_{3z}}\int\frac{d^{2}q_{T}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{d^{2}k_{T}}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle e^{i\bf{(k_{T}-q_{T})\cdot x_{T}}}\mathcal{M}_{fi}(q)\mathcal{M}_{fi}^{*}(k)

(43)

where
\lambda_{3}
=
\uparrow
or
\downarrow
denotes that spin of quark
q_{1}
after the scattering is in the positive or negative direction of the normal (
\hat{n}
) to the reaction plane.

The Eq.
43
is the final expression for differential scattering cross section in the presence of the magnetic field.

The global quark polarization after the scattering is obtained using the relation given in Ref.
Liang:2007ma
;
Gao:2007bc
;

P_{q}=\frac{<\Delta\sigma>}{<\sigma>}

(44)

where,

<\Delta\sigma>=\int d^{2}x_{T}\;\;f_{qq}(x_{T},b,Y,\sqrt{s})\frac{d^{2}\Delta\sigma}{d^{2}x_{T}}

(45)

<\sigma>=\int d^{2}x_{T}\;\;f_{qq}(x_{T},b,Y,\sqrt{s})\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{d^{2}x_{T}}

(46)

where,
f_{qq}(x_{T},b,Y,\sqrt{s})
is the impact parameter (
x_{T}
) distribution. In this study, a simplistic uniform distribution was considered, such that;

f_{qq}(x_{T},b,Y,\sqrt{s})\propto\theta(x)=\begin{cases}1,\;\;\;x>0\\
0,\;\;\;x<0\end{cases}

(47)

and
\frac{d^{2}\Delta\sigma}{d^{2}x_{T}}
,
\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{d^{2}x_{T}}
denotes the polarized and unpolarized cross section at the fixed impact parameter. These are calculated using the mathematical expression;

\frac{d^{2}\Delta\sigma}{d^{2}x_{T}}=\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{\uparrow}}{d^{2}x_{T}}-\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{\downarrow}}{d^{2}x_{T}}

(48)

\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{d^{2}x_{T}}=\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{\uparrow}}{d^{2}x_{T}}+\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{\downarrow}}{d^{2}x_{T}}

(49)

Here, the quark scattering is obtained through a static potential model, in which the quark incident in the z-direction is scattered by an effective static potential induced by another constituent of the QGP. Hence, in this case, we have

\mathcal{M}_{fi}(q)=g\;\bar{u}_{\lambda}(p+q)\cancel{A}(q)u(p)

(50)

where
A(q)=(A_{0}(q),0)
and
A_{0}(q)=g/(q^{2}+m_{D}^{2})
is the screened static potential with Debye screen mass
m_{D}

One can easily obtain,

\mathcal{M}_{fi}(q)\mathcal{M}_{fi}^{*}(k)=g^{2}A_{0}(q)A_{0}(k)\bar{u}_{\lambda}(p+q)(\cancel{p}+m_{q})u_{\lambda}(p+k)

(51)

where
p\simeq(E,-\bf{p})
.
We choose
\hat{n}
as the quantization axis of spin and denote the eigenvalue by
\lambda=\pm 1
. For small angle scattering,
q_{T},k_{T}\simeq m_{D}<<E
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{fi}(q)\mathcal{M}_{fi}^{*}(k)=\sum_{l}4E_{z}^{2}A_{0}(q)A_{0}(k)

\displaystyle\bigg[1-i\lambda\frac{(\bf{q_{T}-k_{T}})\cdot(\hat{n}\times\bf{p})}{2E_{z}(E_{z}+m_{q})}\bigg]

(52)

Using Eq.
52
in Eq.
43
, the unpolarized and polarized scattering cross section in the presence of magnetic field can be obtained from the Eqs.
48
and
49
and are given by,

\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{d^{2}x_{T}}=(\pi|Q_{i}eB|)^{2}g^{4}c_{T}\int\frac{d^{2}q_{T}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{d^{2}k_{T}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\bf{(k_{T}-q_{T})\cdot x_{T}}}}{(q_{T}^{2}+m_{D}^{2})(k_{T}^{2}+m_{D}^{2})}

(53)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\Delta\sigma}{d^{2}x_{T}}=i(\pi|Q_{i}eB|)^{2}g^{4}c_{T}\sum_{l}\int\frac{d^{2}q_{T}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{d^{2}k_{T}}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{\bf{(k_{T}-q_{T})}\cdot(\hat{n}\times\bf{p})e^{i\bf{(k_{T}-q_{T})\cdot x_{T}}}}{2E_{z}(E_{z}+m_{q})(q_{T}^{2}+m_{D}^{2})(k_{T}^{2}+m_{D}^{2})}

(54)

Let’s evaluate the integral

\int\frac{d^{2}q_{T}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\bf{q_{T}\cdot x_{T}}}}{q_{T}^{2}+m_{D}^{2}}=\int\frac{q_{T}dq_{T}}{2\pi}\frac{J_{0}(q_{T}x_{T})}{q_{T}^{2}+m_{D}^{2}}=\frac{K_{0}(m_{D}x_{T})}{2\pi}

(55)

Similarly,

\int\frac{d^{2}k_{T}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\bf{k_{T}\cdot x_{T}}}}{k_{T}^{2}+m_{D}^{2}}=\frac{K_{0}(m_{D}x_{T})}{2\pi}

(56)

Using these integral expression in Eq.
53
and Eq.
54
, we have

\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{d^{2}x_{T}}=\frac{(|Q_{i}eB|)^{2}g^{4}c_{T}}{4}K_{0}^{2}(m_{D}x_{T})

(57)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\Delta\sigma}{d^{2}x_{T}}=\sum_{l}\frac{(|Q_{i}eB|)^{2}g^{4}c_{T}}{4}\frac{(\bf{p}\times\hat{n})\cdot x_{T}}{2E_{z}(E_{z}+m_{q})}

\displaystyle m_{D}K_{0}(m_{D}x_{T})K_{1}(m_{D}x_{T})

(58)

where
J_{0}
and
K_{0}
are the Bessel and modified Bessel functions, respectively.

Substituting Eq.
57
and Eq.
58
in Eq.
44
and averaging over the relative angle between the parton
x_{T}
and nuclear impact parameter b from -
\pi/2
to +
\pi/2
and over
x_{T}
, one can obtain the global quark polarization,

P_{q}=\sum_{l}\frac{-\pi m_{D}\bf{p}}{2E_{z}(E_{z}+m_{q})}

(59)

The Eq.
59
is the final expression of global quark polarization in the presence of magnetic field.

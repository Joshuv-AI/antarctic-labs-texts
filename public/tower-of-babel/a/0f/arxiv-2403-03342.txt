Motives, Periods, and Functoriality
Pierre Deligne & A. Raghuram

Abstract
. Given a pure motive M M over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} with a multilinear algebraic structure 𝗌 \mathsf{s} on M M , and given a representation V V of the group respecting 𝗌 \mathsf{s} , we describe a functorial transfer M V M^{V} . We formulate a criterion that guarantees when the two periods of M V M^{V} are equal. This has an implication for the critical values of the L L -function attached to M V . M^{V}. The criterion is explicated in a variety of examples such as: tensor product motives and Rankin–Selberg L L -functions; orthogonal motives and the standard L L -function for even orthogonal groups; twisted tensor motives and Asai L L -functions.

The main theorem on critical motives

The
L
-function of a motive over a number field

coincides with the
L
-function of the motive over
{\mathbb{Q}}
deduced from it by restriction of scalars. Because of

{\mathbb{Q}}.
They will be thought in terms of corresponding system of realizations in Betti,
de Rham, and
\ell

is given with

Let

be a motive with coefficients in
E
. We will review what it means for

to be critical, and we will discuss the definition of the periods

attached by the first author (
[
D79
]
) to critical motives. We will exhibit cases where

The inspiration for the proof comes from Yoshida
[
Y01
]
.

The periods

are interesting because of their conjectural relation with the critical values of the
L
-function attached to
M.
The
\ell
-adic realizations are needed to define this

-function, but the definition of

depends only on the Betti and de Rham realizations, and our criterion
for their equality can be stated and proved for systems of Betti and de Rham realizations which are defined in Sect.

below.

Motives admit a tensor product and duals, so that one can can consider a motive

together with a multilinear algebraic structure
\mathsf{s}
. From

, one can obtain new motives

by multilinear algebraic constructions. Here is a way to formalize this. Fix a realization functor

with

{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector spaces, for instance Betti or de Rham. Let

(

or

) be the algebraic group
of automorphisms of
(M_{R},\mathsf{s}_{R})
. The comparison isomorphism

will induce an isomorphism

Then to any representation

of

corresponds a motive

deduced from

and compatible with tensor product, and

The transfer

Our aim is to find structures

and representations

2.1.
Betti–de Rham systems of realizations

A weight
w
Betti–de Rham system of realizations
M
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
consists of the following data (i)-(iii), obeying (iv):

(i)

Betti realization:
for
C
an algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{R}}
, a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space
M_{B}\{C\}
, depending functorially on
C
. If

{\mathbb{C}}:={\mathbb{R}}[i]/(i^{2}+1)
is the standard algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{R}}
, this amounts to the data of
M_{B}:=M_{B}\{{\mathbb{C}}\},
and of the
involution
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
induced by the complex conjugation automorphism of
{\mathbb{C}}
. We call the involution
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
the Frobenius at
infinity. It gives a decomposition
M_{B}=M^{+}\oplus M^{-}
of
M_{B}
into its
\pm 1
eigenspaces.

(ii)

de Rham realization:
a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space
M_{\rm dR}
together with a finite decreasing filtration
\mathcal{F}
,
the Hodge filtration
.

(iii)

comparison isomorphism:
an isomorphism

M_{\rm B}\{C\}\otimes C\to M_{\rm dR}\otimes C
of
C
-vector spaces that is functorial in
C.

Let
\sigma_{\rm B}
(resp.,
\sigma_{\rm dR}
) be the antilinear involution of
M_{\rm B}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
(resp.,
M_{\rm dR}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
) which is the tensor product of the identity and of the complex conjugation on
{\mathbb{C}}
. The action by functoriality of
the complex conjugation on
M_{B}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
is
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}\cdot\sigma_{\rm B}
. The data in (iii) amounts to the data of an isomorphism

M_{\rm B}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}\to M_{\rm dR}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
which carries
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}\cdot\sigma_{\rm B}
into
\sigma_{\rm dR}.
In other words,

M^{+}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}\,\oplus\,M^{-}\otimes i{\mathbb{R}}
maps isomorphically onto
M_{\rm dR}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}.

(iv)

One requires that if the complexified Hodge filtration is transported by the comparison isomorphism to a filtration of
M_{B}\otimes{\mathbb{C}},
still
denoted
\mathcal{F}
, it and its complex conjugate
\bar{\mathcal{F}}=\sigma_{\rm B}(\mathcal{F})
are
w
-opposite, meaning that
M_{\rm B}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
is the direct sum
of the
M^{p,q}:=\mathcal{F}^{p}\cap\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{q}
for
p+q=w.
One calls the decomposition
M_{\rm B}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}={\oplus}_{p+q=w}M^{p,q}

the Hodge decomposition
.
One has
\mathcal{F}^{a}=\oplus_{p\geq a}M^{p,q},
and
M^{p,q}
and
M^{q,p}
are complex conjugates. As
\mathcal{F}^{p}
is dR-rational,
it is stable under
\sigma_{\rm B}\cdot\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
, identified with
\sigma_{\rm dR}.
It follows that
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
maps
\mathcal{F}
into
\bar{\mathcal{F}}
, hence

M^{p,q}
to
M^{q,p}.

One defines morphisms, tensor products, and duals in an obvious way. The Hodge decomposition is functorial. It follows that any morphism is strictly compatible with the Hodge filtrations and that the Betti–de Rham systems of realizations of weight
w
form an abelian category. The tensor product of systems of realizations of weight

w
and
w^{\prime}
is of weight
w+w^{\prime}
, and the dual of
M
of weight
w
is of weight
-w.

Examples

The motivating example is

, the cohomology in degree

of a smooth projective variety

over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with

, and

the algebraic de Rham cohomology of

The Tate object

, of weight

and Hodge type

is defined by
{\mathbb{Q}}(1)_{\rm B}\{C\}=2\pi i{\mathbb{Q}}\subset C
, and

purely of filtration
-1
. As

is of rank one, its

tensor power, denoted

is
the dual of

. It is also

and the

The

Tate twist of

Critical Betti–de Rham realizations

Definition 2.2.1
.

We say that

(1)

M^{+}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}\ \longrightarrow\ (M_{\rm dR}/\mathcal{F}^{0})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}.

The

]

One defines

for

for

Criticality of

If

The invariant

(resp., to

The map (

Suppose we have a family of systems

To understand, it is convenient to work in a graded tensor category larger than Betti–de Rham systems over

3.3.3.
A remark on Hodge numbers

As before, a system with coefficients in

is a system

-module structure

A crucial complication is that

-modules

are usually not free. A consequence is that when

a corresponding

is defined, the factors

depend on

But their product over

is independent of

Very briefly, a weight
w

over a number field

with coefficients in a number field

consists of:

•

(Betti) For each embedding
\sigma
of

, an

-vector space

; together with a Hodge decomposition

-modules;

•

(Real Frobenius) an involutive isomorphism

of

-vector spaces which upon complexification

to

•

(de Rham) an

-module

, endowed with a Hodge filtration

; and

•

(Comparison) a comparison isomorphism

of

-modules,
inducing

In the Hodge decomposition, the summand

being an

-module amounts to specifying a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space

M_{\sigma,\tau}^{p,q}
for every embedding
\tau:E\to{\mathbb{C}}.
Define the Hodge numbers of

as:

(9)

h_{\sigma,\tau}^{p,q}\ =\ \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(M_{\sigma,\tau}^{p,q}).

Proposition 3.3.4
.

With notations as above, the Hodge number

depends only on the restriction of
\sigma
to the largest CM or totally real subfield of the base field
L
, and
only on the restriction of
\tau
to the largest CM or totally real subfield of the coefficient field

.

Proof.

From
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
, one has

(10)

h_{\sigma,\tau}^{p,q}\ =\ h_{c\sigma,\tau}^{q,p}.

Since
L
and
E
are number fields,
\sigma
and
\tau
take values in the algebraic closure
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
in
{\mathbb{C}}
. There is an action

on

, and

From (

∎

3.6.
Motives via multiplication induction: Asai motives.

3.6.1.

There is a notion of

to

of

which is a Betti–de Rham realization over
F
of rank
n^{[K:F]}
and weight
[K:F]w
. To define
\otimes_{K/F}M
over

for the etale topology over

. For

[K:F]

F

of

in

, and the base-change to

.

an etale algebra over

, that is a finite product of finite extensions
K_{i}
of

. A Betti–de Rham realization

over

of realizations over

\bigotimes_{K/F}M\ =\ \bigotimes_{i}\bigotimes_{K_{i}/F}M_{i}

Statements true for tensor products can often be generalized to multiplicative induction.

Theorem 3.6.2
.

K/F
be an extension of totally real number fields of degree

Let

be a Betti–de Rham realization over

The method of proof is the same as in Sect.
3.5
, starting this time with
H^{0}({\rm Spec}(F))
and
H^{0}({\rm Spec}(K)),
their ring structures, and the

H^{0}({\rm Spec}(F))\ \to\ H^{0}({\rm Spec}(K)),

and its

Let

\textstyle{J:=\Sigma_{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

If we consider, in addition,

Action of
S_{\pi^{-1}(i)}

{\mathbb{C}}
-points, one has:

(\prod_{K/F}X)_{\sigma}({\mathbb{C}})\ =\ \prod_{j=1}^{d}X_{\eta_{j}}({\mathbb{C}}),

where,

(\prod_{K/F}X)_{\sigma}=(\prod_{K/F}X)\times_{F,\sigma}{\mathbb{C}}
and
X_{\eta_{j}}:=X\times_{K,\eta_{j}}{\mathbb{C}}.

By the Künneth theorem, one has

(20)

H^{p}((\prod_{K/F}X)_{\sigma}({\mathbb{C}}),{\mathbb{Q}})\ =\ \bigoplus_{\sum p_{j}=p}\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}H^{p_{j}}(X_{\eta_{j}}({\mathbb{C}}),{\mathbb{Q}}).

Now consider the Betti–de Rham realization for the motive
H^{q}(X)
over
K
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Q}}
. If
q
is even,
the Betti realization of the multiplicative induction

\bigotimes_{K/F}H^{q}(X)
is the piece in
H^{q[K:F]}((\prod_{K/F}X)({\mathbb{C}}),{\mathbb{Q}})
corresponding to the summand in (
20
) with
p_{1}=\cdots=p_{d}=q.

3.6.4.
Motivating example-II: Asai motives

To a Hilbert modular form
f
for a real quadratic extension
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
, Asai
[
As77
]
attached a degree-
4

L
-function which has all the usual analytic properties of an automorphic
L
-function
under suitable assumptions on
f
.
This
L
-function is now called the Asai
L
-function, or the twisted tensor
L
-function.
As is now well-known, the Asai
L
-function is the standard
L
-function of a
suitable automorphic form
{\rm As}(f)
on
{\rm GL}(4)/{\mathbb{Q}}
obtained by Langlands’s principle of functoriality which on the Galois side is given by multiplicative induction: a two-dimensional irreducible representation
V
of
{\rm Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/K)
corresponding to
f
gives after multiplicative induction a four-dimensional representation
{\rm As}(V)=\otimes_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}V
of
{\rm Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
that corresponds to
{\rm As}(f)
; see
[
Kr03
]
.
The Asai
L
-function is one of the
L
-functions in the Langlands-Shahidi family of
L
-functions. More generally, given a quadratic extension of totally real number fields
K/F
, and given a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}/K
, the principle of functoriality predicts an automorphic representation
{\rm As}(\pi)
of

{\rm GL}_{n^{2}}/F
, which is cuspidal under some regularity assumption on
\pi
. Furthermore, suppose that
\pi
is motivic and
M=M(\pi)
is the conjectural motive associated to
\pi
, then
conjecturally,
{\rm As}(\pi)
is also motivic, and one expects
\otimes_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(M(\pi))\ =\ M({\rm As}(\pi)).

The period relation in Thm.
3.6.2
then gives a motivic explanation to the rationality results for
the ratios of critical values of Asai
L
-functions in the forthcoming
[
KR
]
.

3.7.
Symplectic and Orthogonal Motives

3.7.1.
The case when the base field is
{\mathbb{Q}}

We consider a Betti–de Rham system of realizations
M
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with coefficients in
E
.
In the even weight case we assume the vanishing of the middle Hodge number. We assume
M
is given a linear algebra structure
\mathsf{s}
such that

G(\mathsf{s})
is contained in a group
{\rm GSp}
of symplectic similitudes, or in the connected component
{\rm GO}^{\circ}
of a group
{\rm GO}
of orthogonal similitudes.
The group
{\rm GO}^{\circ}
is also sometimes denoted
{\rm GSO}.

Theorem 3.7.2
.

If the multiplier of
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
is
1
, then
c^{+}_{E}(M)=c^{-}_{E}(M)
.

Let us elaborate our setting a bit more. One starts with
M
of rank
2n
over
E,
and with an
L
of rank
1
over
E
. In practice (motivic case),
L
will be
L(\chi)(m),

with
L(\chi)
the system of realizations defined by a Dirichlet character
\chi
with values in
E
and with
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}.
The assumption of
‘the multiplier of
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
is
1
’ translates to
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{m}.

In the symplectic case, the linear algebra structure
\mathsf{s}
is a non-degenerate alternating
E
-bilinear pairing

B:M\otimes_{E}M\to L.

In the orthogonal case, one has to give more than a non-degenerate symmetric
E
-bilinear pairing

B:M\otimes_{E}M\to L.

Such a pairing induces a similar pairing on
\det(M):=\wedge^{2n}M
:

\det(B):\det(M)^{\otimes^{2}}\to L^{\otimes^{2n}}.

This new pairing amounts to a triviliazation of

(\det(M)\otimes L^{\otimes^{-n}})^{\otimes^{2}}.

The required multilinear algebra structure
\mathsf{s}
consists of the
B
, together with a trivialization of
\det(M)\otimes L^{\otimes^{-n}}
(isomorphism with the unit object)
inducing the above trivialization of its tensor square.

Proof.

For
x\in M^{+}
and
y\in M^{-}
, on the one hand, one has

B(\mathsf{F}_{\infty}x,\mathsf{F}_{\infty}y)=B(x,-y)=-B(x,y),
but on the other hand one also has

B(\mathsf{F}_{\infty}x,\mathsf{F}_{\infty}y)=B(x,y),

hence,
B(x,y)=0;
whence,
M_{B}
is the symplectic or orthogonal direct sum of
M^{+}
and
M^{-}.

We have to show that
Z(\mathsf{F}_{\infty})
acts with the same determinant on
M^{+}
and
M^{-}
. The dimension
n
of

M^{+}
and
M^{-}
is even. In the symplectic case, because
M^{+}
and
M^{-}
are symplectic. In the
orthogonal case, the connected component of the group of similitudes is given by

\det(g)={\rm multiplier}(g)^{n},

forcing
n
to be even since the multiplier of
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
is
1
and determinant
(-1)^{n}.

An element
g
in
G(\mathsf{s})
centralizing
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
is the sum of an
g^{+}
acting on
M^{+}
and
g^{-}
acting on
M^{-}
. In the symplectic case,
g^{+}
and
g^{-}
are symplectic
similitudes with the same multipliers, hence the same determinant

\det(g^{\pm})={\rm multiplier}(g^{\pm})^{n/2}.

In the orthogonal case, they are orthogonal similitudes with the same multiplier. The ratios

\det(g^{\pm})/{\rm multiplier}(g^{\pm})^{n/2}\in\{\pm 1\},

but
\det(g)/{\rm multiplier}(g)^{n}=1
means that they are equal: for some sign
\epsilon
, one has

\det(g^{\pm})={\rm multiplier}(g^{\pm})^{n/2}\cdot\epsilon,

proving that
\det(g^{+})=\det(g^{-}).

∎

3.7.3.
The case when the base field is totally real

Thm.
3.7.2
will continue to hold for
M
and
L
Betti–de Rham systems of realizations over a totally real field
F
. The parity assumption is now that for
each complex embedding
\sigma
of
F
, the involution
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
of
L_{\sigma}
is trivial. In the motivic case, one will want
L
to be
L(\chi)(m)
for
\chi
an
abelian character of
{\rm Gal}(\overline{F}/F)
with values in
E^{*}
, and the parity assumption is that for each infinite Frobenius
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
one has

\chi(\mathsf{F}_{\infty})=(-1)^{m}.

The relevant group
G(\mathsf{s})
for
{\rm Res}_{F/{\mathbb{Q}}}(M)
is now an extension of the symmetric group
S_{T},
of the set
T
of complex embeddings of
F
, by
the product of the (connected components) of the groups of similitudes of the
M_{\sigma}
’s. The group
Z(\mathsf{F}_{\infty})
is the extension of
S_{T}
by the product
of the centralizers. The proof that the action of this product of centralizers on

{\rm Res}_{F/{\mathbb{Q}}}(M)^{+}=\prod M_{\sigma}^{+},\quad{\rm and}\quad{\rm Res}_{F/{\mathbb{Q}}}(M)^{-}=\prod M_{\sigma}^{-},

have the same determinant is as before. It remains to compute a ratio of determinants on the quotient
S_{T}
. For this, we may first extend the field of coefficients to
split the forms
B
on
M_{\sigma}
. One can then find basis
e^{\pm}_{i,\sigma}
of the
M_{\sigma}^{\pm}
and representatives for elements of
S_{T}

which for each
(\pm,i)
, permute the
e^{\pm}_{i,\sigma}
. As
n
is even, for such a choice both determinants are trivial.

3.7.4.
Example: The tensor product of two rank-two motives-II

We revisit the example discussed in
3.1.5
. Using the notations therein, if
\varphi\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\omega)
is a primitive cuspform and

M(\varphi)
the associated rank-two motive, then
M(\varphi)
is symplectic but does not satisfy the parity condition in the hypothesis of Thm.
3.7.2
. The symplectic
form
B
is an alternating form
B_{\varphi}:M(\varphi)\otimes M(\varphi)\to{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega)(1-k).

Since the Dirichlet character
\omega
is
the nebentypus character of a weight
k
modular form, one has
\omega(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. Hence
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
acts on the rank-one motive
{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega)(1-k)
via

\omega(-1)\cdot(-1)^{1-k}=-1.
In particular, Thm.
3.7.2
does not apply to
M(\varphi)
–but this is expected since the two periods for a general (non-CM) cuspform

\varphi\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\omega)
are expected to be algebraically independent.

Now suppose, for
j=1,2
we have
\varphi_{j}\in S_{k_{j}}(\Gamma_{0}(N_{j}),\omega_{j}).
Assume
k_{1}\neq k_{2}.
The orthogonal form

B_{\varphi_{1}}\otimes B_{\varphi_{2}}
on the tensor product motive
M(\varphi_{1})\otimes M(\varphi_{2})
takes values in the rank-one motive
{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{1}\omega_{2})(2-k_{1}-k_{2})
, on which
the action of
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
is via
(\omega_{1}\omega_{2})(-1)\cdot(-1)^{2-k_{1}-k_{2}}=1,
satisfying the hypothesis of the theorem. It remains to check that
G(\mathsf{s})
is contained in the connected
component
{\rm GO}^{\circ}
. This is a special case of the following general lemma.

Lemma 3.7.5
.

Let
(V_{1},B_{1})
and
(V_{2},B_{2})
be nondegenerate symplectic vector spaces over a field
F
. Then
V=V_{1}\otimes_{F}V_{2}
equipped with
B=B_{1}\otimes B_{2}
is a nondegenerate
orthogonal space over
F
. Under the canonical map
{\rm GL}(V_{1})\times{\rm GL}(V_{2})\to{\rm GL}(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})
, the image of

{\rm GSp}(V_{1},B_{1})\times{\rm GSp}(V_{2},B_{2})
is contained inside
{\rm GSO}(V,B).
(In particular,
{\rm GL}(2)\times{\rm GL}(2)
maps inside
{\rm GSO}(4).
)

Proof.

Suppose the dimension of
V_{1}
is
2n_{1}
and

{\rm GSp}(V_{1},B_{1})
has a similitude character
\mu_{1}
then for
g_{1}\in{\rm GSp}(V_{1},B_{1})
one has
\det(g_{1})^{2}=\mu_{1}(g_{1})^{2n_{1}}.
Similarly,
for
g_{2}\in{\rm GSp}(V_{2},B_{2}),
one has
\det(g_{2})^{2}=\mu_{2}(g_{2})^{2n_{2}}.
For the element
g_{1}\otimes g_{2}
acting on
(V,B)
,
the similitude character
\mu
and the determinant are given by

\mu(g_{1}\otimes g_{2})=\mu_{1}(g_{1})\mu_{2}(g_{2})
and
\det(g_{1}\otimes g_{2})=\det(g_{1})^{2n_{2}}\det(g_{2})^{2n_{1}},
respectively.
One checks that
\det(g_{1}\otimes g_{2})\cdot\mu(g_{1}\otimes g_{2})^{\dim(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})/2}=1
which implies that
g_{1}\otimes g_{2}\in{\rm GSO}(V,B).

∎

3.7.6.
Example: The conjectural motive attached to representations of
{\rm O}(2n)

In this paragraph we discuss the relation of Thm.
3.7.2
with the main result of
[
BR20
]
. Suppose
{\rm O}(2n)
is a split orthogonal group
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and
\sigma
is a cuspidal automorphic representation
of
{\rm O}(2n)({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
satisfying the conditions:

(i)

the Arthur parameter
\Psi
of
\sigma
is a cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}(2n)/{\mathbb{Q}}
;

(ii)

\sigma
is globally generic with respect to some Whittaker datum
\psi
;

(iii)

n
is even, and the
\sigma_{\infty}
is a discrete series representation of
{\rm O}(n,n)
which is locally generic with respect to
\psi_{\infty}.

We will see below that Thm.
3.7.2
applies to the conjectural motive
M
attached to
\sigma
giving us then a motivic explanation
of the main theorem of
[
BR20
]
recalled in (
).

The motive is attached not so much as to
\sigma
, but rather to its Arthur parameter
\Psi
, which by (i) is a cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}(2n)/{\mathbb{Q}}
.
However,
\Psi
is not a representation that is of motivic type, in as much as it is not algebraic, and as in
[
C90
]
, one remedies the situation by considering:

\Psi^{T}:=\Psi\otimes|\!|\ |\!|^{1/2}

a half-integral Tate-twist of
\Psi
(one may also consider
\Psi\otimes|\!|\ |\!|^{m/2}
for any odd integer
m
) which is of motivic type. Let
M=M(\Psi^{T})
be the conjectural rank
2n
motive associated to
\Psi^{T}.
For the expected details of this dictionary between motives and automorphic forms, the reader is referred to
[
HR20
, Chap. 7]
.

To get the Hodge types of
M
, recall from
[
BR20
, 2.2.8]
that the Langlands parameter of the discrete series representation
\sigma_{\infty}
, which by definition of
the transfer
\sigma\mapsto\Psi
is the same as the Langlands parameter
\varphi(\Psi_{\infty})
of
\Psi_{\infty},
is of the form:

\oplus_{j=1}^{n}I(\chi_{\ell_{j}})
, for a decreasing sequence of even positive integers
\ell_{1}>\ell_{2}>\cdots>\ell_{n}
,
where
\chi_{\ell}
is the character of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
that maps
z
to
(z/\bar{z})^{\ell/2}
, and
I(\chi_{\ell})
is its induction to a
2
-dimensional irreducible
representation of the Weil group
W_{\mathbb{R}}
of
{\mathbb{R}}
. We can read off the exponents of the characters of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
appearing in
\varphi(\Psi^{T}_{\infty})
. One aspect of the dictionary

\Psi^{T}\leftrightarrow M
is that if
(p,q)
is a Hodge type for
M
if and only if
z^{-p}\bar{z}^{-q}
appears in
\varphi(\Psi^{T}_{\infty}\otimes|\!|\ |\!|^{(1-2n)/2}
). One concludes that
the Hodge types of
M(\Psi^{T})
are of the form:

\left\{\left(\frac{\pm\ell_{j}+2n-2}{2},\frac{\mp\ell_{j}+2n-2}{2}\right)\right\}_{1\leq j\leq n}.

The purity weight of the motive is

w=w(M)=2n-2.

Next, we look at
L
-functions. One has the following relations:

L(s,\sigma)\ =\ L(s,\Psi),

i.e., the degree-
2n

L
-function of
\sigma
on
{\rm O}(2n)
corresponding to the inclusion
{\rm O}(2n,{\mathbb{C}})\hookrightarrow{\rm GL}(2n,{\mathbb{C}})
of dual groups,
is the standard
L
-function
L(s,\Psi)
attached to
\Psi.
Of course, by definition of Tate-twist, one also has

L(s,\Psi^{T})\ =\ L(s+1/2,\Psi).

Another aspect of the dictionary
\Psi^{T}\leftrightarrow M
is that

L(s,\Psi^{T}\otimes|\!|\ |\!|^{(1-2n)/2})\ =\ L(s,M).
Hence,
L(s,\Psi)=L(s+n-1,M).
The same relations hold for local and global
L
-functions. One deduces
for the symmetric square
L
-functions the relation
L(s,{\rm Sym}^{2},\Psi)=L(s+2n-2,{\rm Sym}^{2}(M)).

Since
\Psi
is a transfer from
{\rm O}(2n)
, it is part of the Arthur classification, that
L(s,{\rm Sym}^{2},\Psi)
has a pole at
s=1
, or that
L(s,{\rm Sym}^{2}(M))
has a pole at
s=2n-1.

This happens when the Tate motive
{\mathbb{Q}}(2-2n)
appears in
{\rm Sym}^{2}(M)
; in other words, we have an orthogonal structure
\mathsf{s}
:

M\otimes M\to{\mathbb{Q}}(2-2n).

If
g\in G(\mathsf{s})
the group preserving
\mathsf{s}
, then its multiplier is

\mu(g)=|\!|g|\!|^{2-2n}.

Also, the multiplier of
\mathsf{F}_{\infty}
on
{\mathbb{Q}}(2-2n)
is
(-1)^{2-2n}=1
satisfying the parity condition in Thm.
3.7.2
.
The determinant of
M
is the central character of
\Psi^{T}\otimes|\!|\ |\!|^{(1-2n)/2}
. The central character of
\Psi
is trivial since it is a transfer from
the split orthogonal group, hence the determinant character of
M
is

\det(M)(g)=|\!|g|\!|^{(2-2n)n}.

It follows that
\det(M)(g)\mu(g)^{-n}=1
, i.e.,
G(\mathsf{s})
is contained in
{\rm GSO}(M)
. Hence, Thm.
3.7.2
applies to the motive
M
as claimed.

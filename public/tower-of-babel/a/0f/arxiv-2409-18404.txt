On congruence properties of poly-Bernoulli numbers with negative upper-indices
Yasuo Ohno, Mika Sakata

Abstract
For any integer k k , M. Kaneko ( [ 9 ] ) defined k k -th poly-Bernoulli numbers as a kind of generalization of classical Bernoulli numbers using k k -th polylogarithm. In case when k k is positive, k k -th poly-Bernoulli numbers is a sequence of rational numbers as same as classical Bernoulli numbers. On the other hand, in case when k k is negative, it is a sequence of positive integers, and many combinatoric and number theoretic properties has been investigated. In the present paper, the negative case is treated, and their congruence and p p -adic properties are discussed. Beside of them, application of the results to obtain a congruence property for the number of lonesum matrices is also mentioned.



1
Introduction

Poly-Bernoulli numbers
\{\mathbb{B}^{(k)}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is a sequence defined, in
[
9
]
, for any integer
k
by the generating series

\displaystyle\frac{\Li_{k}(1-e^{-t})}{1-e^{-t}}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathbb{B}^{(k)}_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

where
\Li_{k}(t)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{t^{n}}{n^{k}}
.
In addition, Keneko defined another sequence
\{C^{(k)}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
by

\displaystyle\frac{\Li_{k}(1-e^{-t})}{e^{t}-1}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}C^{(k)}_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

in
[
3
]
, it will be called poly-Bernoulli numbers of “type-
C
” in the current paper.
It is well-known that
\mathbb{B}^{(1)}_{n}
(resp.
C^{(1)}_{n}
) is the classical Bernoulli numbers with

\mathbb{B}^{(1)}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
(resp.
C^{(1)}_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
).
Moreover, for any integer
k
, the numbers
\mathbb{B}^{(k)}_{n}
and
C^{(k)}_{n}
are related with each other as follows:

\mathbb{B}^{(k)}_{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}C^{(k)}_{m},\quad C^{(k)}_{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-m}\binom{n}{m}\mathbb{B}^{(k)}_{m}\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathbb{B}^{(k)}_{n}=C^{(k)}_{n}+C^{(k-1)}_{n-1}.

An explicit formula for each poly-Bernoulli numbers is given
in
[
9
, Theorem 1]
and
[
10
, Theorem 1]

as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(k)}_{n}=(-1)^{n}\sum^{n}_{m=0}\frac{(-1)^{m}m!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}}{(m+1)^{k}}

(1)

and

\displaystyle C^{(k)}_{n}=(-1)^{n}\sum^{n}_{m=0}\frac{(-1)^{m}m!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+1}{m+1}}{(m+1)^{k}},

(2)

where
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}
denotes the Stirling number of the second kind, defined by

\displaystyle\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{m}}{m!}=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

or equivalently by

\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\sum_{l=0}^{m}(-1)^{l}\binom{m}{l}l^{n},

(3)

for any non-negative integers
n
and
m
.

Studying the divisibility of the denominators of classical Bernoulli numbers by prime numbers is important and derives the Kummer congruence and
p
-adic theory of
L
functions.
Poly-Bernoulli numbers with positive upper indices are sequence of rational numbers, and their divisibility by prime numbers in the denominators has been studied (
[
2
,
4
,
9
]
et.al.).
Hoffman (
[
7
]
) showed an interesting congruence between poly-Bernoulli numbers with positive upper indices and finite multiple zeta values. It is also known in
[
3
]
that poly-Bernoulli numbers with positive upper indices appear at the negative integer points of the Arakawa–Kaneko multiple zeta functions, and its congruence property and a kind of periodicity are also studied by the authors in
[
15
]
.

In case when the upper-index is negative,
poly-Bernoulli numbers are sequence of positive integers.
These sequences and each term of them are meaningful objects not only in number theory but also in combinatorics,
and a number of interesting results have been obtained.
For example, a beautiful relation named “duality” are well known.
For any non-negative integers
k
and
n
, both poly-Bernoulli numbers have each duality formula(
[
9
, Theorem 2]
,
[
10
, Corollary of Theorem 1]
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n}=\mathbb{B}^{(-n)}_{k},

(4)

and

\displaystyle C^{(-k-1)}_{n}=C^{(-n-1)}_{k}.

(5)

Moreover a pretty formula

\sum_{l=0}^{n}(-1)^{l}\mathbb{B}^{(-l)}_{n-l}=0

is obtained in
[
4
]
for any positive integer
n
.
More combinatorially interesting properties are known by many papers including

[
1
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
11
,
13
,
16
]
.

In the present paper, poly-Bernoulli numbers with negative upper-indices are treated,
and their congruence and
p
-adic properties are investigated.
Beside of them,
application of the results to obtain a congruence property for the number of lonesum matrices, via Brewbaker’s work, is also mentioned.

Throughout this paper, the characters
p
and
p_{j}
express prime numbers,
and
M
and
N
express positive integers.
Moreover,
\varphi(M)
denotes the Euler totient function of
M
,
which gives the number of positive integers relatively prime to
M
and less than
M
.
For example,
\varphi(p^{N})=p^{N-1}(p-1)
.

2
Periodicity

In this section, we present our results on the properties of poly-Bernoulli numbers with negative upper indices modulo
M
.

First, we show that poly-Bernoulli numbers with negative upper indices have a period modulo
p^{N}
, as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p
be a prime number, and
k,N
be positive integers.
Suppose that integers
n,m\geq N
satisfies
n\equiv m\pmod{\varphi(p^{N})}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n}\equiv\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{m}\pmod{p^{N}}.

Proof.

Assuming that
n,m\geq N
, using the explicit formula (
1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-n)}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\sum^{k}_{l=0}(-1)^{l}l!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{l}(l+1)^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\sum^{k}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
p\not\hskip 2.0pt|\hskip 2.0ptl+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{l}l!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{l}(l+1)^{n}\pmod{p^{N}}.

Euler’s totient theorem, namely the natural generalization of Fermat’s little theorem,
confirms

(l+1)^{n}\equiv(l+1)^{m}
(mod
p^{N}
) for any non-negative integers
l
satisfying
p\not|\hskip 2.0ptl+1
,
and it leads us to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-n)}_{k}\equiv\mathbb{B}^{(-m)}_{k}\pmod{p^{N}}.

Thus we obtain the claim
\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n}\equiv\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{m}\pmod{p^{N}}
, by applying the duality formula (
4
) to the both sides.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Theorem
2.1
originally obtained by both Kitahara
[
12
]
and Ohno–Sakata
[
14
]
almost at the meantime independently,
however the methods are different with each other, i.e. the proof in
[
12
]
uses
p
-adic analytic distribution.

By Theorem
2.1
, we see that poly-Bernoulli numbers with negative upper-index have a period modulo a positive integer
M
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
k
and
M
be positive integers, and
M=p_{1}^{e_{1}}p_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots p_{l}^{e_{l}}

be the prime factorization of
M
, where
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{l}
are distinct prime divisors, of
M
.
For any integers
n,m\geq\max\{e_{j}\;|\;1\leq j\leq l\}

satisfying
n\equiv m\pmod{\varphi(M)}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n}\equiv\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{m}\pmod{M}.

Proof.

Under the given conditions,
M=p_{1}^{e_{1}}p_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots p_{l}^{e_{l}}

and
\varphi(M)|(n-m)
, Euler’s product formula leads us to

\varphi(p_{j}^{e_{j}})|(n-m)
for all
j
satisfying
1\leq j\leq l
,
and then by Theorem
2.1
, we have

p_{j}^{e_{j}}|\left(\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n}-\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{m}\right)
,
for all such
j
.
Thus we obtain
M|\left(\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n}-\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{m}\right)
.
∎

Now, we briefly touch on the combinatorial aspect.
A matrix whose entries are 0 or 1 and is uniquely determined by its row and column sum vectors
is called “lonesum”. For any integers
k
and
n
,
we denote the number of lonesum matrices of size
k\times n
by
L(k,n)
.
Corollary
2.3
together with Brewbaker’s relation:

\displaystyle L(k,m)=\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{m}

(6)

(
[
6
, Theorem 2]
) immediately derive the period of the number of lonesum matrices, as follows.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
k
and
M
be positive integers, and
M=p_{1}^{e_{1}}p_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots p_{l}^{e_{l}}

be the prime factorization of
M
, where
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{l}
are distinct prime divisors, of
M
.
For any integers
n,m\geq\max\{e_{j}\;|\;1\leq j\leq l\}

satisfying
n\equiv m\pmod{\varphi(M)}
, we have

L(k,n)\equiv L(k,m)\pmod{M}.

Similar to Theorem
2.1
, we get the period for poly-Bernoulli numbers of type-
C
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
p
be a prime number, and
k,N
be positive integers.
Suppose that integers
n,m\geq N
satisfies
n\equiv m\pmod{\varphi(p^{N})}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle C^{(-k)}_{n}\equiv C^{(-k)}_{m}\pmod{p^{N}}.

Proof.

It seems that a reasonable way to prove it would be to give a direct proof, rather than using Theorem
2.1
.

Assuming that
n,m\geq N
, using the explicit formula (
2
), we have

\displaystyle C^{(-n-1)}_{k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}\sum^{k-1}_{l=0}(-1)^{l}l!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{l+1}(l+1)^{n+1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}\sum^{k-1}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
p\not\hskip 2.0pt|\hskip 2.0ptl+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{l}l!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{l+1}(l+1)^{n+1}\pmod{p^{N}}.

Euler’s totient theorem leads us to obtain

\displaystyle C^{(-n-1)}_{k-1}\equiv C^{(-m-1)}_{k-1}\pmod{p^{N}}

By applying the duality formula (
4
) to the both sides,
we obtain the claim.
∎

3
Congruence properties

In this section, we discuss the congruence properties of poly-Bernoulli numbers with negative upper-indices.
First, we show the following relation.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any odd prime
p
and any non-negative integer
k
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{p-1}\equiv\begin{cases}1\pmod{p}&\text{if $k=0$ {\rm or} $k\not\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}$, }\\
2\pmod{p}&\text{if $k\neq 0$ {\rm and} $k\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}$. }\end{cases}

Proof.

In case when
k=0
, it is easy to see
\mathbb{B}^{(0)}_{p-1}=\mathbb{B}^{(1-p)}_{0}=1
.

In case when
k>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{p-1}\sum^{p-1}_{m=0}(-1)^{m}m!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p-1}{m}(m+1)^{k}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum^{p-2}_{m=1}(-1)^{m}m!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p-1}{m}(m+1)^{k}\pmod{p}

using the explicit formula (
1
).
Applying the defining formula (
3
) of Stirling numbers of the second kind,
it turns to

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{p-1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum^{p-2}_{m=1}(m+1)^{k}\sum^{m}_{l=1}(-1)^{l}\binom{m}{l}l^{p-1}\pmod{p}.

Fermat’s little theorem leads us to

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{p-1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum^{p-2}_{m=1}(m+1)^{k}\sum^{m}_{l=1}(-1)^{l}\binom{m}{l}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum^{p-2}_{m=1}(m+1)^{k}\left(\sum^{m}_{l=0}(-1)^{l}\binom{m}{l}-1\right)\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum^{p-2}_{m=1}(m+1)^{k}\left((1-1)^{m}-1\right)\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\sum^{p-2}_{m=1}(m+1)^{k}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1\pmod{p}&\text{if $k\not\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}$, }\\
2\pmod{p}&\text{if $k\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}$. }\end{cases}

∎

The corresponding property of the above theorem for poly-Bernoulli numbers of type-
C
is as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any odd prime
p
and any non-negative integer
k
, we have

\displaystyle C^{(-k-1)}_{p-2}\equiv\begin{cases}0\pmod{p}&\text{if $k\not\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}$, }\\
1\pmod{p}&\text{if $k\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}$. }\end{cases}

Proof.

By the explicit formula (
2
) for poly-Bernoulli numbers and
the explicit formula (
3
) for Stirling numbers of the second kind,

\displaystyle C^{(-k-1)}_{p-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{p-2}\sum^{p-2}_{m=0}(-1)^{m}m!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p-1}{m+1}(m+1)^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{p-2}_{m=0}(m+1)^{k}\sum^{m+1}_{l=0}(-1)^{l}\binom{m+1}{l}l^{p-1}

Fermat’s little theorem leads to

\displaystyle C^{(-k-1)}_{p-2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum^{p-2}_{m=0}(m+1)^{k}\sum^{m+1}_{l=1}(-1)^{l}\binom{m+1}{l}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum^{p-2}_{m=0}(m+1)^{k}\left\{(1-1)^{m+1}-1\right\}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\sum^{p-2}_{m=0}(m+1)^{k}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\begin{cases}0\pmod{p}&\text{if $k\not\equiv 0\bmod{p-1}$, }\\
1\pmod{p}&\text{if $k\equiv 0\bmod{p-1}$. }\end{cases}

∎

Furthermore Fermat’s little theorem leads us to the following property.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any odd prime
p
and any non-negative integer
k
, we have

\displaystyle C^{(-k-1)}_{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod{p}.

Proof.

By the explicit formula (
2
),

\displaystyle C^{(-k-1)}_{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{p-1}\sum^{p-1}_{m=0}(-1)^{m}m!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p}{m+1}(m+1)^{k+1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum^{p-2}_{m=0}(-1)^{m}m!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p}{m+1}(m+1)^{k+1}\pmod{p}.

Using the defining formula (
3
) of Stirling numbers of the second kind,

\displaystyle C^{(-k-1)}_{p-1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\sum^{p-2}_{m=0}(m+1)^{k}\sum^{m+1}_{l=1}(-1)^{l}\binom{m+1}{l}l^{p}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\sum^{p-2}_{m=0}(m+1)^{k+1}\sum^{m+1}_{l=1}(-1)^{l}\binom{m}{l-1}l^{p-1}\pmod{p}

Fermat’s little theorem leads to

\displaystyle C^{(-k-1)}_{p-1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\sum^{p-2}_{m=0}(m+1)^{k+1}\sum^{m+1}_{l=1}(-1)^{l}\binom{m}{l-1}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\sum^{p-2}_{m=0}(m+1)^{k+1}\sum^{m}_{l=0}(-1)^{l+1}\binom{m}{l}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum^{p-2}_{m=0}(m+1)^{k+1}(1-1)^{m}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 1\pmod{p}.

∎

The duality formula (
4
) for poly-Bernoulli numbers leads to the following.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
k
and
n
be positive integers.
If
k\equiv n\equiv 0,1\pmod{p-1}
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n}\equiv 2\pmod{p}.

Proof.

In case when
n\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}
, it holds by
Theorem
3.1
that
\mathbb{B}^{(-n)}_{p-1}\equiv 2\pmod{p}
, thus we obtain
\mathbb{B}^{(1-p)}_{p-1}\equiv 2\pmod{p}
.
In case when
n\equiv 1\pmod{p-1}
, by the explicit formula (
1
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-1)}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{1}\sum^{1}_{m=0}(-1)^{m}m!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1}{m}(m+1)^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)\cdot 2=2.

Using the duality (
4
) and the periodicity (Theorem
2.1
), we obtain the claim in both cases.
∎

Next we obtain the following property using the congruence relation between poly-Bernoulli numbers and classical Bernoulli numbers.

Theorem 3.5
.

If
p
is a prime number with
p\geq 7
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{(-p+3)}_{p-3}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

Proof.

We have
\mathbb{B}^{(-p+3)}_{p-3}=C^{(-p+3)}_{p-3}+C^{(-p+2)}_{p-4}
by the definition.
Using the duality formula (
5
), we obtain
\mathbb{B}^{(-p+3)}_{p-3}=2C^{(-p+2)}_{p-4}
.
Fermat’s little theorem leads to
C^{(-p+2)}_{n}\equiv C_{n}\pmod{p}
for
1\leq n\leq p-2
.
Then we have
\mathbb{B}^{(-p+3)}_{p-3}\equiv 2C_{p-4}\pmod{p}
.
By the given condition
p\geq 7
,
p-4\geq 3
is odd, so we have

C_{p-4}=0
from the well known property of classical Bernoulli numbers.
Thus we obtain
\mathbb{B}^{(-p+3)}_{p-3}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
∎

Adding up the poly-Bernoulli numbers of one period,
we obtain the following results.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
p
be a prime, and
k,N
be positive integers.
For any positive integer
n\geq N
, we have

\displaystyle\sum^{\varphi(p^{N})-1}_{i=0}\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n+i}\equiv\sum^{\varphi(p^{N})-1}_{i=0}\mathbb{B}^{(-n-i)}_{k}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{N}}.

Proof.

If
n=N
, using the explicit formula (
1
) and the duality (
4
), we compute as

\displaystyle\sum^{\varphi(p^{N})-1}_{i=0}\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{N+i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\varphi(p^{N})-1}_{i=0}\mathbb{B}^{(-N-i)}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\varphi(p^{N})-1}_{i=0}(-1)^{k}\sum^{k}_{m=0}(-1)^{m}m!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{m}(m+1)^{N+i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\sum^{k}_{m=1}(-1)^{m}m!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{m}\sum^{\varphi(p^{N})-1}_{i=0}(m+1)^{N+i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\sum^{k}_{m=1}(-1)^{m}m!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{m}\frac{(m+1)^{N}\{(m+1)^{\varphi(p^{N})}-1\}}{m}.

Euler’s totient theorem leads us to obtain

\displaystyle\sum^{\varphi(p^{N})-1}_{i=0}\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{N+i}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{N}}.

The case
n>N
reduces to the case
n=N
, by applying the periodicity given in Theorem
2.1
.
∎

By Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
3.6
,
we generalize the results for modulo natural numbers.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
k
and
M
be positive integers, and
M=p_{1}^{e_{1}}p_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots p_{l}^{e_{l}}

be the prime factorization of
M
, where
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{l}
are distinct prime divisors, of
M
.
For any integer
n\geq\max\{e_{j}\;|\;1\leq j\leq l\}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum^{\varphi(M)-1}_{i=0}\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n+i}\equiv\sum^{\varphi(M)-1}_{i=0}\mathbb{B}^{(-n-i)}_{k}\equiv 0\pmod{M}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
,

\displaystyle\sum^{\varphi(M)-1}_{i=0}\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n+i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\varphi(p_{1}^{e_{1}})\cdots\varphi(p_{l}^{e_{l}})-1}_{i=0}\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n+i}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\not=r\end{subarray}}^{l}\varphi(p_{j}^{e_{j}})\sum^{\varphi(p_{r}^{e_{r}})-1}_{i=0}\mathbb{B}^{(-k)}_{n+i}\pmod{p_{r}^{e_{r}}}

for
1\leq r\leq l
. By Theorem
3.6
, the right-hand side is congruent to 0
\bmod{\ p_{r}^{e_{r}}}
.
Using the duality (
4
), we obtain the claim.
∎

Tying up Brewbaker’s relation (
6
) and Corollary
3.7
, we immediately obtain the following interesting property on the number of lonesome matrices.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
k
and
M
be positive integers, and
M=p_{1}^{e_{1}}p_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots p_{l}^{e_{l}}

be the prime factorization of
M
, where
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{l}
are distinct prime divisors, of
M
.
For any integer
n\geq\max\{e_{j}\;|\;1\leq j\leq l\}
, we have

\sum^{\varphi(M)-1}_{i=0}L(k,n+i)\equiv\sum^{\varphi(M)-1}_{i=0}L(n+i,k)\equiv 0\pmod{M}.

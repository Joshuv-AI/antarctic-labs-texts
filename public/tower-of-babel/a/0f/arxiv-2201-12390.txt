Spin relaxation rate for heavy quarks in weakly coupled QCD plasma
Masaru Hongo, Xu-Guang Huang, Matthias Kaminski, Mikhail Stephanov, Ho-Ung Yee

Abstract
We compute the relaxation rate of the spin density of heavy quarks in a perturbative QCD plasma to leading-log order in the coupling constant g g . The spin relaxation rate Γ s \Gamma_{s} in spin hydrodynamics is shown to be Γ s ∼ g 4 ​ log ⁡ ( 1 / g ) ​ T ​ ( T / M ) 2 \Gamma_{s}\sim g^{4}\log(1/g)T(T/M)^{2} in the heavy-quark limit T / M ≪ 1 T/M\ll 1 , which is smaller than the relaxation rate of other non-hydrodynamic modes by additional powers of T / M T/M . We demonstrate three different methods to evaluate the spin relaxation rate: 1) the Green-Kubo formula in the spin hydrodynamic regime, 2) the spin density correlation function in the strict hydrodynamic limit, and 3) quantum kinetic theory of the spin distribution function in momentum space. We highlight the interesting differences between these methods, while they are ultimately connected to each other by the underlying Ward-Takahashi identity for the non-conserved spin density.

2
Correlation functions in spin hydrodynamics

Our purpose is to consider the dynamics of spin density attached to the non-relativistic fermion
\psi
.
The crucial point here is that the Lagrangian (
1
)
enjoys the approximate heavy quark
{\rm SU}(2)
symmetry that acts on
\psi
as

\psi\to\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\mathrm{a}}\sigma_{\mathrm{a}}}\psi
. Only the Pauli (third) term in eq. (
1
) breaks such heavy quark symmetry, and one can show the following Ward-Takahashi identity as the equation of motion for the spin density operator:

\partial_{0}J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{J}_{\mathrm{a}}=\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}\quad(\mathrm{a}=1,2,3),

(2)

where we introduced the spin current
J^{\mu}_{\mathrm{a}}
and the source term as

J^{\mu}_{\mathrm{a}}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mathrm{a}}\psi\\
-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4M}\big[\psi^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{D}\psi)-(\bm{D}\psi)^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mathrm{a}}\psi\big]\end{pmatrix},\quad\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}\equiv-\frac{g}{2M}\epsilon_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{c}}\psi^{\dagger}B^{\mathrm{b}}\sigma^{\mathrm{c}}\psi.

(3)

Equation (
2
) indicates that
spin for the heavy quark is not conserved due to the Pauli term between the spin and color magnetic field.
However, it is crucial to note that the source term
\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}
has the factor
1/M
, so that it is suppressed in the heavy-quark limit.
In other words, one can regard eq. (
2
) as an approximate conservation law if
M
is large enough.

We can coarse-grain operator equation (
2
)
to obtain the relationship between the coarse-grained variables for
which we shall use the same notations as for the operators.
The existence of hydrodynamic description implies that all coarse-grained
operators can be expressed as local functionals of the conserved
densities such as
J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}
as well as
\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}
using constitutive equations.
The form of these equations is strongly constrained by the second law of
thermodynamics, which requires that the entropy functional
S[J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}]=\int\mathrm{d}^{3}xs(J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}})
is not decreasing in time.
Using coarse-grained eq. (
2
), we can express
the local entropy production rate as

\partial_{0}s+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{s}=\bm{J}_{\mathrm{a}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\frac{\partial s}{\partial J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}}+\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}\frac{\partial s}{\partial J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}}\geq 0\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\bm{s}\equiv\frac{\partial s}{\partial J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}}\bm{J}_{\mathrm{a}},

(4)

for which we required the local second law of thermodynamics.
This means that both the source
\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}
and current
J^{i}_{a}
must be proportional to

T\frac{\partial s}{\partial J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}}\equiv-\mu_{\mathrm{a}}+b_{\mathrm{a}},

(5)

and its spatial gradients, where we defined internal spin chemical potential
\mu_{\mathrm{a}}
, which is a
function of spin density
J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}
, as well as external potential for
the spin
b_{\mathrm{a}}
, which could be the fluid thermal vorticity, or the external magnetic field times gyromagnetic
ratio, or even the torsion. We note that the external spin potential
b_{\mathrm{a}}
is different from the dynamical magnetic field
B_{\mathrm{a}}
. The second law of thermodynamics in eq. (
4
) thus
constrains the constitutive equations to leading order in gradients up
to semi-positive-definite kinetic coefficients:

\bm{J}_{\mathrm{a}}=-T\sigma_{s}\bm{\nabla}\left(\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{a}}-b_{\mathrm{a}}}{T}\right),

(6a)

\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}=-\lambda_{s}\left(\mu_{\mathrm{a}}-b_{\mathrm{a}}\right).

(6b)

Substituting constitutive equations (
6
) into the approximate conservation law (
2
),
we obtain hydrodynamic equation of motion for spin density
J^{0}_{a}
:

\partial_{0}J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left[T\sigma_{s}\bm{\nabla}\left(\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{a}}-b_{\mathrm{a}}}{T}\right)\right]=-\lambda_{s}\left(\mu_{\mathrm{a}}-b_{\mathrm{a}}\right).

(7)

Equilibrium is achieved at a value of
J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}
at which the internal
and external spin potentials are equal, i.e.,
\mu_{\mathrm{a}}=b_{\mathrm{a}}
.
At this point, the entropy is maximized, according to eq. (
5
) and
does not increase, according to eq. (
4
).
Linearizing equation of motion (
7
) in small deviations around that equilibrium, we find

\partial_{0}\delta J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}-D_{s}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\delta J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}+\sigma_{s}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\delta b_{\mathrm{a}}=-\Gamma_{s}\delta J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}+\lambda_{s}\delta b_{\mathrm{a}},

(8)

where we introduced a spin diffusion coefficient
D_{s}
and spin relaxation rate
\Gamma_{s}
as

D_{s}\equiv\frac{\sigma_{s}}{\chi_{s}}\quad\mbox{and }\quad\Gamma_{s}\equiv\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\chi_{s}}

(9)

using the spin susceptibility
\chi_{s}
defined as usual by

\frac{\partial\mu_{\mathrm{a}}}{\partial J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}=\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}\,\chi_{s}^{-1}.

(10)

Equation (
8
) describes linear response of the spin
density to perturbations of the external spin potential, and we can
use it to determine the retarded Green’s function for spin density
Kadanoff-Martin1963

G^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}_{R}(\omega,\bm{k})=\frac{\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{s}+\sigma_{s}\bm{k}^{2})}{\omega+\mathrm{i}(\Gamma_{s}+D_{s}\bm{k}^{2})}\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}.

(11)

We then find that the retarded spin density correlator has a pole at the imaginary value of

\omega(\bm{k})=-\mathrm{i}(\Gamma_{s}+D_{s}\bm{k}^{2})+O(\bm{k}^{4}),

(12)

which does not vanish in
\bm{k}\to\bm{0}
limit. Therefore, in
hydrodynamic limit
\bm{k}\to\bm{0}
the spin density shows relaxational
behavior with characteristic time
\tau_{s}=\Gamma_{s}^{-1}
.

If the spin relaxation time
\tau_{s}
is much longer than other microscopic time scales, then the hydrodynamic regime
\omega\ll\Gamma
can be split into two subregimes.
The strict hydrodynamic regime, i.e.,
\omega
is much
smaller than any relaxation scales, including
\omega\ll\Gamma_{s}
, and
the regime where
\Gamma_{s}\ll\omega\ll\Gamma
.
The latter is the so-called
Hydro
+
regime
Stephanov:2017ghc
, where a small subset of non-hydrodynamic modes
relaxes on a scale comparable to the hydrodynamic time scale.
Correspondingly, there are two ways to obtain the spin relaxation rate
\Gamma_{s}=\lambda_{s}/\chi_{s}
as we shall explain below.

By taking a strict hydrodynamic limit
\omega\to 0
at
\bm{k}=\bm{0}
of the spin density
correlator (
11
) and using eq. (
9
), we obtain one way to evaluate the spin relaxation rate as

\lim_{\omega\to 0}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\frac{1}{\omega}G_{R}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega,\bm{k}=\bm{0})=\frac{\chi_{s}}{\Gamma_{s}}\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}.

(13)

Alternatively, we can take the limit
\omega\ll\Gamma
, while still
maintaining
\omega\gg\Gamma_{s}
:

\lim_{\Gamma_{s}\ll\omega\ll\Gamma}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}{\omega}G_{R}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega,\bm{k}=\bm{0})={\lambda_{s}}\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}.

(14)

Since the imaginary part of the retarded Green’s function is related
to the fluctuation correlator
G_{rr}
through the fluctuation-dissipation relation
G_{rr}=(2T/\omega)\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}G_{R}
,
we can rewrite both eqs. (
13
) and (
14
) in
terms of
G_{rr}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}
.
Moreover, since, at
\bm{k}=\bm{0}
, by eq. (
2

\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}=\partial_{0}J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}
, the correlator of the spin density
J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}
can be related to the correlator of the source
\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}
by a Ward-Takahashi identity, and we obtain

G_{rr}^{\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}\Theta_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega,\bm{k}=\bm{0})=\omega^{2}G_{rr}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega,\bm{k}=\bm{0})\,.

(15)

Using this identity and the fluctuations-dissipation relation we can rewrite
eq. (
14
) in terms of the symmetric correlator of
\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}

\frac{1}{2T}\lim_{\Gamma_{s}\ll\omega\ll\Gamma}G_{rr}^{\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}\Theta_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega,\bm{k}=\bm{0})=\lambda_{s}\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}},

(16)

which gives another useful formula to evaluate heavy-quark rotational viscosity
\lambda_{s}

3
Description of the methods

Before we present our detailed computations of the spin relaxation rate in three different methods in section
4
, let us give a brief overview of these methods, which summarizes the main ideas, as well as the differences between them.

Based on the previous section, let us start with the retarded Green’s function of spin density in zero wavenumber limit
\bm{k}\to\bm{0}
:

G_{R}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega)=\frac{\mathrm{i}\chi_{s}\Gamma_{s}}{\omega+\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{s}}\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}},

(17)

where we use the shorthand notation for the zero wave number Green’s function as
G(\omega)=G(\omega,\bm{k}=\bm{0})
.
The same correlation function computed in finite temperature pQCD must agree with (
17
) in both spin and strict hydrodynamic regimes in
\omega\ll\Gamma\sim g^{4}\log(1/g)T
.
The fact that
\Gamma_{s}\sim\Gamma\left(\frac{T}{M}\right)^{2}\ll\Gamma

allows us to consider two different regimes of
\omega
, i.e.,
the spin hydrodynamic one
\omega\gg\Gamma_{s}
and
the strict hydrodynamic one
\omega\ll\Gamma_{s}
.

(1) The Green-Kubo formula in spin hydrodynamic regime:

In the spin hydrodynamic regime, we have an expansion in powers of
\Gamma_{s}/\omega
as

G_{R}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega)=\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\chi_{s}\Gamma_{s}}{\omega}+{\chi_{s}\Gamma_{s}^{2}\over\omega^{2}}+\cdots\right)\delta^{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}\quad\mathrm{at}\quad\Gamma_{s}\ll\omega\ll\Gamma.

(18)

The first term can also be regarded as the result of taking the controlled limit we discussed in the previous section, i.e.,
\lim_{\Gamma_{s}\ll\omega\ll\Gamma}G_{R}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega)
.
We see that the series is organized in terms of increasing powers of
\Gamma_{s}
, which is equivalent to increasing powers of the coupling constant
g
.
Since
\Gamma_{s}\to 0
in the perturbative limit
g\to 0
, the condition for the expansion to work, i.e.,
\omega\gg\Gamma_{s}
, is valid for any fixed non-zero value of
\omega
. This means that the diagrammatic perturbation series of the correlation function in field theory should be one-to-one correspondent to the expansion in eq. (
18
), as long as we keep
\omega
fixed and finite.
Especially, matching the first term in eq. (
18
) with the first leading diagrams in naive perturbation theory in
g
, we are able to compute
\Gamma_{s}
in simple perturbative computations.
We emphasize that this is possible only because the spin density is not a conserved quantity, and we have a finite non-vanishing relaxation rate
\Gamma_{s}
that is perturbative in
g
.
This is in sharp contrast to the correlation functions for conserved quantities, for which the density-density correlation function in
\bm{k}=\bm{0}
limit simply vanishes identically for all
\omega
due to the Ward-Takahashi identity.

In the diagrammatic computation, it turns out to be easier to compute the Wightman correlation function
G^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}_{12}(\omega)
, which is related to the retarded correlation function
G_{R}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega)
by the fluctuation-dissipation relation in
\omega\ll T
regime,

G_{12}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega)={2T\over\omega}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}G^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}_{R}(\omega),

(19)

where
T/\omega
comes from the limit of Bose-Einstein distribution
n_{B}(\omega)
in the small frequency limit
\omega\ll T
.
Therefore, our discussion above implies that the leading diagram for
G_{12}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}
in the naive perturbation theory should match to
2T\chi_{s}\Gamma_{s}\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}/\omega^{2}
in
\Gamma_{s}\ll\omega\ll T
regime.
As we discussed in the previous section, one can, in fact, use the Ward-Takahashi identity to relate the correlation functions of spin density
J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}
with those of source operator
\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}
, i.e.,
\partial_{0}J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}=\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}
in
\bm{k}=\bm{0}
limit.
In frequency space, this implies
\omega^{2}G^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}_{12}(\omega)=G^{\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}\Theta_{\mathrm{b}}}_{12}(\omega)
, which we have also checked diagrammatically for our leading order diagrams.
The former involves three diagrams, while the latter turns out to be only one diagram in leading order.

We, therefore, use the latter to compute the spin relaxation rate, by matching

G_{12}^{\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}\Theta_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega\to 0)=2T\chi_{s}\Gamma_{s}\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}},

(20)

with the leading order diagram in the naive perturbation theory in
g
.

Note that this is the Wightman expression of eq. (
16
).
As we show in the next section in detail, we are able to determine
\Gamma_{s}
in leading-log of coupling constant based on this formula as

\Gamma_{s}=C_{2}(R)\frac{g^{2}m_{D}^{2}T}{6\pi M^{2}}\log(1/g),

(21)

where
m_{D}^{2}={g^{2}T^{2}}(2N_{c}+N_{F})/6
is the Debye mass squared, and
C_{2}(R)
is the Casimir invariant of the color representation
R
of the heavy quark, which is
C_{2}(F)=(N_{c}^{2}-1)/(2N_{c})
for fundamental representation of
{\rm SU}(N_{c})
.

(2) The spin density correlation function in the strict hydrodynamic regime:

On the other hand, in the strict hydrodynamic regime of
\omega\ll\Gamma_{s}
,
which is the true hydrodynamic limit of
\omega\to 0
,
we have an expansion in
\omega/\Gamma_{s}
as

G_{R}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega)=\left(\chi_{s}+\mathrm{i}{\chi_{s}\over\Gamma_{s}}\omega+{\cal O}(\omega^{2})\right)\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}\quad\mathrm{at}\quad\omega\ll\Gamma_{s},

(22)

and the same
\Gamma_{s}
can be computed from the leading imaginary part in small frequency limit.
In this limit, the imaginary part of the retarded correlation function becomes sensitive to infrared singularities arising from the diverging mean-free path of heavy quarks in
g\to 0
limit, which is called the pinching singularities
Jeon:1994if
.

The pinching singularity should be regulated by including the imaginary part of self-energy in the heavy-quark propagators, i.e., the damping rate, which represents the scatterings with thermal background medium that gives rise to a finite mean-free path.
The proper evaluation of correlation functions in this limit further requires a resummation of infinite ladder diagrams
Jeon:1994if
;
ValleBasagoiti:2002ir
;
Jimenez-Alba:2015bia
, i.e., the vertex corrections, in addition to the damping rate in the propagators: only after this, the Ward-Takahashi identity (or the conservation law in the case of exact global symmetry) is fulfilled
Aarts:2002tn
;
Hidaka:2010gh
.
One can also understand the pinching singularity from the Boltzmann equation.
In the language of kinetic theory, the damping rate and the vertex correction correspond to the loss and the gain terms, respectively, in the collision terms of the Boltzmann equation.
The appearance of
\Gamma_{s}
in the denominator, which brings in a non-analyticity of the correlation function in coupling
g
, is a manifestation of pinching singularities of spin density correlation function that is regulated by a finite relaxation rate of spin density.

The fact that only
\Gamma_{s}
, not
\Gamma
in general, appears in the spin density correlation function after resuming an infinite number of ladder diagrams is also an interesting difference from the case of conserved charges, where the density-density correlation function at
\bm{k}=0
is simply zero.
The case of conserved charges can be understood by replacing
\Gamma_{s}
with an infinitesimal
\epsilon
that goes to zero.
In this limit, the imaginary part linear in
\omega
seems to have a divergent coefficient
{1/\epsilon}
, but the condition of expansion
\omega\ll\epsilon
is never justified. The proper thing to do is to go back to eq. (
17
) with
\Gamma_{s}
replaced by
\epsilon
, and the density-density correlation function vanishes in
\epsilon\to 0
limit.
The transport coefficients for conserved charges, e.g., shear viscosity and conductivity, are computed from the current-current correlation functions instead, which shows the similar non-analytic behavior of
1/\Gamma\sim 1/[g^{4}T\log(1/g)]
from resummation of infinite ladder diagrams. The Ward-Takahashi identity for
spin density should be responsible for how the spin density correlation function has a similar but different non-analytic behavior of
1/\Gamma_{s}
after resummation, which depends only on the spin-violating interactions that are suppressed by powers of
T/M
.

Another way to look at the difference between
\omega\gg\Gamma_{s}
and
\omega\ll\Gamma_{s}

in the diagrammatic evaluation of correlation functions is the following.
In the expansion in eq. (
18
), which is organized by the naive perturbation theory in
g
, each term in the series becomes of the same order when
\omega\sim\Gamma_{s}
, and one needs to include all terms to properly evaluate the correlation function in
\omega\ll\Gamma_{s}
limit. Diagrammatically, this necessitates a summation over an infinite number of diagrams. The relevant subset of diagrams in leading order is captured by the diagrams with pinching singularities in the reorganized perturbation theory with damping rate included in the propagators.

(3) The quantum kinetic theory for spin:

Our final method of computing the spin relaxation rate of heavy quarks is the quantum kinetic theory of spin density matrix, developed in ref.
Li:2019qkf
.
The time evolution of a spin distribution function in momentum space,
\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)
, is described by the quantum Boltzmann equation with collision terms,

{\partial\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)\over\partial t}=\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}[\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)]

(23)

where
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}
is the quantum collision operator acting on the spin distribution function (not to be confused with
\Gamma_{s}
). The leading-log expression for
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}
is available for a massive quark with its mass satisfying the condition
M\gg gT
, and we can take
M\gg T
limit for our purpose. The result is organized in powers of
T/M
,

\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}=\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(0)}+\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}+\cdots,

(24)

and the leading term reproduces the momentum diffusion equation with the heavy-quark drag force known in literature
Moore:2004tg
.

The leading term conserves the total spin density, i.e.,

\int_{\bm{p}}\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(0)}[\bm{S}(\bm{p})]=0
with
\int_{\bm{p}}\equiv\int\mathrm{d}^{3}p/(2\pi)^{3}
for any
\bm{S}(\bm{p})
, and the spin relaxation in leading order is given by the next term
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}
. The eigenmodes of
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(0)}
have eigenvalues of order
\sim g^{4}\log(1/g)T(T/M)
, which are non-hydrodynamic modes of the microscopic theory, except the zero mode,
\bm{S}^{(0)}(\bm{p})=\bm{S}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{p}}
with
E_{p}={\bm{p}}^{2}/(2M)
for any constant vector
\bm{S}_{0}
, that has zero eigenvalue of
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(0)}
. The zero mode represents the equilibrium distribution of spin polarization in momentum space in leading order, which takes a simple Boltzmann distribution. In other words, the zero mode coefficients
\bm{S}_{0}
corresponds to the spin density in the spin hydrodynamics. How the zero mode relaxes by the spin violating term,
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}
, gives us the spin relaxation rate in hydrodynamics,
\Gamma_{s}
.
Writing
\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)=\bm{S}_{0}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{p}}
, and inserting this leading order expression to the quantum kinetic equation (
24
), we obtain, to the leading order in
1/M
,

{\partial\bm{S}_{0}(t)\over\partial t}=-\Gamma_{s}\bm{S}_{0}(t)\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\Gamma_{s}=-{\int_{\bm{p}}\hat{\Gamma}_{S}^{(1)}[\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{p}}]\over\int_{\bm{p}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{p}}}.

(25)

We used the fact that
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}[\bm{S}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{p}}]=\bm{S}_{0}\hat{\Gamma}_{S}^{(1)}[\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{p}}]
with a scalar operator
\hat{\Gamma}_{S}^{(1)}
, due to rotational invariance of the collision term.
We emphasize that the sub-leading corrections in
1/M
to the expression,
\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)=\bm{S}_{0}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{p}}
, exists in general, but are removed upon integration over
\bm{p}
, due to the fact that
\int_{\bm{p}}\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(0)}[\bm{S}(\bm{p})]=0
for any
\bm{S}(\bm{p})
. This is the procedure that identifies
\Gamma_{s}
correctly to leading order in
1/M
in quantum kinetic theory.

4
Evaluation of the spin relaxation rate in perturbative QCD

In this section,
we present our detailed evaluations of the spin relaxation rate in three different ways, all of which lead to the same result given in (
21
).
We first compute
\Gamma_{s}
via the Green-Kubo formula
of the source correlation and the spin density correlation functions in two different regimes,
\omega\gg\Gamma_{s}
and
\omega\ll\Gamma_{s}
, in sections
4.1
and
4.2
, respectively.
In section
4.3
, we evaluate the spin relaxation rate in yet another way, based on the quantum kinetic theory of the spin distribution function in momentum space.

4.1
Method 1: Green-Kubo formula in
\omega\gg\Gamma_{s}
regime

As we described in the previous section, the spin relaxation rate can be computed by the source-source correlation function,
G_{12}^{\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}\Theta_{\mathrm{b}}}(\omega\to 0)
, evaluated in the naive perturbation theory, and matching that with
2T\chi_{s}\Gamma_{s}\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}
, where
\chi_{s}
is the spin susceptibility [recall eq. (
20
)].

Let us first summarize the basic ingredients for diagrammatic computation.
The real-time propagators for the non-relativistic heavy quarks and the relativistic gluons are

\begin{split}S_{12}(k)&=\parbox{71.13188pt}{\vskip 8.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-S12.pdf}}=-n_{F}(k^{0})2\pi\delta(k^{0}-E_{\bm{k}}),\\
S_{21}(k)&=\parbox{71.13188pt}{\vskip 8.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-S21.pdf}}=\big[1-n_{F}(k^{0})\big]2\pi\delta(k^{0}-E_{\bm{k}}),\\
D^{\mu\nu}_{12}(k)&=\parbox{71.13188pt}{\vskip 12.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-D12.pdf}}=n_{B}(k^{0})\rho^{\mu\nu}(k),\\
D^{\mu\nu}_{21}(k)&=\parbox{71.13188pt}{\vskip 12.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-D21.pdf}}=\big[1+n_{B}(k^{0})\big]\rho^{\mu\nu}(k),\end{split}

(26)

where
E_{\bm{k}}=\bm{k}^{2}/2M
is the non-relativistic kinetic energy of heavy quark,
\rho^{\mu\nu}(k)
is the gluon spectral density, and
the Fermi-Dirac and Bose-Einstein distribution functions are

n_{F}(k^{0})=1/(\mathrm{e}^{\beta k^{0}}\mathrm{e}^{\beta M}+1)
and

n_{B}(k^{0})=1/(\mathrm{e}^{\beta k^{0}}-1)
, respectively.
For simplicity, we use the notation
k^{\mu}=(k^{0},\bm{k})
(thus,
k^{0}
corresponds to
\omega
in previous sections), and omit the trivial color structures.
Note that the appearance of fugacity
\mathrm{e}^{-\beta M}
in
n_{F}
is due to the fact that the non-relativistic energy is measured from the rest mass energy
M
.
3
3

3

We assume that the (relativistic) heavy-quark chemical potential is zero, which leads to a dilute limit
n_{F}\sim\mathrm{e}^{-\beta M}\ll 1
when
T\ll M
.

First of all, we note that the source operator
\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}=-(g/2M)\epsilon_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{c}}\psi^{\dagger}B^{\mathrm{b}}\sigma^{\mathrm{c}}\psi
already contains the small parameter
g/M
, and it also carries the gluon field.
The leading diagram in the naive perturbation theory is the 2-loop sunset diagram [see figure in eq. (
31
) below].
Since the gluon field in
\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}
carries a non-zero frequency-momentum in general (of order
gT
as we will see), the two propagators of heavy quarks do not carry the same frequency-momentum, and there is no infrared singularity of pinching poles in the computation.
Therefore, we can safely use the free propagator for heavy quarks in our leading order evaluation.

Moreover, due to the on-shell conditions imposed on the heavy-quark momenta, as can be seen in eqs. (
26
), the gluon momentum is constrained to be spacelike. The free gluon spectral density has support only on light-like momenta.
The leading correction to the gluon spectral density
\rho^{\mu\nu}(k)
for spacelike momenta comes from the Hard Thermal Loop (HTL) self-energy, which is well-known in literature
Braaten:1989mz
;
LeBellac2000
;
Blaizot:2001nr
.
Our computation is based on these ingredients.

The simple scattering picture is also possible by cutting the sun-set diagram in half.
The resulting diagram simply represents the
t
-channel scattering of an on-shell heavy quark with hard thermal particles of momenta
T
in the background plasma. It turns out that the leading-log comes from the soft
t
-channel scatterings, with exchanged gluons carrying momenta in the range
m_{D}\ll|\bm{k}|\ll T
.
This is similar to other leading-log results for transport coefficients
Baym:1990uj
;
Arnold:2000dr
.

For convenience, we here summarize the HTL results
Braaten:1989mz
(see, e.g., refs.
LeBellac2000
;
Blaizot:2001nr
for reviews on the HTL).
The HTL spectral density for gluons
takes the following form,

\rho^{\mu\nu}=\big[\delta^{\mu 0}\delta^{\nu 0}\rho_{L}(k)+P^{\mu\nu}_{T}(\bm{k})\rho_{T}(k)\big]\quad\mathrm{with}\quad P^{\mu\nu}_{T}(\bm{k})\equiv\delta^{\mu 0}\delta^{\nu 0}+\eta^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\bm{k}^{\mu}\bm{k}^{\nu}}{\bm{k}^{2}}.

(27)

where the longitudinal and transverse spectral functions are

\rho_{L}(k)=-2\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\left[\frac{1}{\bm{k}^{2}-\Pi_{L}(k)}\right]\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\rho_{T}(k)=2\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\left[\frac{1}{\bm{k}^{2}-(k^{0})^{2}-\Pi_{T}(k)}\right],\quad

(28)

with the corresponding HTL self energies given by

\begin{split}\Pi_{L}(k)&=-m_{D}^{2}\left[1+\frac{k^{0}}{2|\bm{k}|}\log\frac{k^{0}-|\bm{k}|+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}{k^{0}+|\bm{k}|+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\right],\\
\Pi_{T}(k)&=-\frac{m_{D}^{2}}{2}\left[\frac{(k^{0})^{2}}{|\bm{k}|^{2}}+\left(\frac{(k^{0})^{2}}{|\bm{k}|^{2}}-1\right)\frac{k^{0}}{2|\bm{k}|}\log\frac{k^{0}-|\bm{k}|+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}{k^{0}+|\bm{k}|+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\right].\end{split}

(29)

Here,
m_{D}\sim gT
denotes the Debye mass (more precisely,
m_{D}^{2}=(1/3)g^{2}T^{2}(N_{c}+N_{F}/2)
with
N_{F}
light flavors).
In the region
gT\ll|\bm{k}|\ll T
, and also for the on-shell constraint
k^{0}=\bm{v}\cdot\bm{k}\ll|\bm{k}|
where
\bm{v}
is the heavy-quark velocity of order
\sqrt{T/M}\ll 1
,
the spectral functions can be further simplified as
LeBellac2000

\rho_{L}(k)\simeq\frac{\pi m_{D}^{2}k^{0}}{|\bm{k}|^{5}},\quad\rho_{T}(k)\simeq\frac{\pi m_{D}^{2}k^{0}}{2|\bm{k}|^{5}},

(30)

which we will use in the following calculation.

Based on these ingredients, we now evaluate the spin relaxation rate by our method.
Working out the Feynman rule for the source operator involving color magnetic field, we arrive at the following expression for the spin-traced source-source correlation function,

\displaystyle\chi_{s}\Gamma_{s}

\displaystyle={1\over 6T}\delta^{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}G_{12}^{\Theta_{\mathrm{a}}\Theta_{\mathrm{b}}}(k^{0}\to 0)=\frac{g^{2}}{12M^{2}T}\delta^{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}\lim_{k^{0}\to 0}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\left[\parbox{142.26378pt}{\vskip 0.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-source-source.pdf}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{(N_{c}^{2}-1)g^{2}}{6M^{2}T}\underbrace{\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}S_{12}(p)S_{21}(p+q)|\bm{q}|^{2}n_{B}(q^{0})\rho_{T}(q)}_{F(k^{0}\to 0)}.

(31)

Using the free propagator for heavy quarks and the HTL spectral density for the gluons, we can perform the integral as

\begin{split}F(k^{0}\to 0)&=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\big[1-n_{F}(p^{0}+q^{0})\big]2\pi\delta(p^{0}+q^{0}-E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}})\\
&\hskip 80.0pt\times n_{F}(p^{0})2\pi\delta(p^{0}-E_{\bm{p}})|\bm{q}|^{2}n_{B}(q^{0})\frac{\pi m_{D}^{2}q^{0}}{2|\bm{q}|^{5}}\\

&\hskip 80.0pt\times\delta\left(q^{0}-\frac{\bm{q}^{2}}{2M}-\frac{|\bm{p}||\bm{q}|}{M}z\right)n_{B}(q^{0})\frac{\pi m_{D}^{2}q^{0}}{2|\bm{q}|^{3}},\end{split}

(32)

where we restricted the
|\bm{q}|
-integral in the soft regime discussed above.
Note that the
q^{0}
-integral is non-vanishing only in the restricted window,

\omega_{-}(\bm{p},\bm{q})\leq q^{0}\leq\omega_{+}(\bm{p},\bm{q})\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\omega_{\pm}(p,q)\equiv\frac{\bm{q}^{2}}{2M}\pm\frac{|\bm{p}||\bm{q}|}{M}.

(33)

Recalling that the fermion mass
M
is much larger than any other scales in our problem, we find that the leading-order contribution comes from the
q^{0}\to 0
part of the integrand.
Then, using
n_{B}(q^{0})\simeq 1/(\beta q^{0})
and
the dilute heavy-quark limit
1-n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})\simeq 1
, and performing both

and
q^{0}
integrals to obtain

\displaystyle F(k^{0}\to 0)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})\int_{gT}^{T}\mathrm{d}|\bm{q}||\bm{q}|^{2}\frac{M}{|\bm{p}||\bm{q}|}\big[\omega_{+}(\bm{p},\bm{q})-\omega_{-}(\bm{p},\bm{q})\big]\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\pi m_{D}^{2}}{2|\bm{q}|^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Tm_{D}^{2}}{4\pi}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})\int_{gT}^{T}\mathrm{d}|\bm{q}|\frac{1}{|\bm{q}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{Tm_{D}^{2}}{4\pi}n(T)\log(1/g),

(34)

where we introduced the heavy-quark number density as

\displaystyle{n(T)\equiv\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})}
.
Substituting this result into eq. (
31
),
we obtain the leading-log result as

\chi_{s}\Gamma_{s}\simeq n(T)\frac{(N_{c}^{2}-1)g^{2}m_{D}^{2}}{24\pi M^{2}}\log(1/g).

(35)

The spin susceptibility
\chi_{s}
can be computed by introducing a spin chemical potential

\mu^{\mathrm{a}}
that couples to the spin in the Hamiltonian as

-\bm{\mu}\cdot\bm{S}(=-\mu^{\mathrm{a}}S_{\mathrm{a}})
with a spin of heavy quarks
\bm{S}
.
This induces the modification of distribution functions according to spin direction as
n_{F}(E_{\bm{q}}-\bm{\mu}\cdot\bm{S})\approx n_{F}(E_{\bm{q}})-n_{F}^{\prime}(E_{\bm{q}})(\bm{\mu}\cdot\bm{S})
.
Then, the induced net spin density along the direction of
\bm{\mu}
to linear order is

-N_{c}\int{\mathrm{d}^{3}p\over(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{S=\pm 1/2}{S}n_{F}^{\prime}(E_{\bm{p}}){S}\mu=-{N_{c}\over 2}\int{\mathrm{d}^{3}p\over(2\pi)^{3}}n_{F}^{\prime}(E_{\bm{p}})\mu\equiv\chi_{s}\mu,

(36)

where
N_{c}
is the dimension of the fundamental representation of the color gauge group
{\rm SU}(N_{c})
.
As a result, we obtain the spin susceptibility
\chi_{s}
as

\chi_{s}=-{N_{c}\over 2}\int{\mathrm{d}^{3}p\over(2\pi)^{3}}n_{F}^{\prime}(E_{\bm{p}})\approx{N_{c}\over 2}\beta\int{\mathrm{d}^{3}p\over(2\pi)^{3}}n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})={N_{c}\over 2T}n(T),

(37)

where we used
n_{F}^{\prime}(E)\approx-\beta n_{F}(E)
in dilute approximation.

With this, we finally find the leading-log result of the spin relaxation rate
\Gamma_{s}
as

\Gamma_{s}=C_{2}(F)\frac{g^{2}m_{D}^{2}T}{6\pi M^{2}}\log(1/g),

(38)

where we introduced the Casimir operator for the heavy-quark fundamental representation
F
as

C_{2}(F)=(N_{c}^{2}-1)/(2N_{c})
.
By working out color factors with a general representation, it can be shown that the result for a general representation
R
is given by replacing
C_{2}(F)
with
C_{2}(R)
, leading to the result presented in eq. (
21
).

4.2
Method 2: Spin density correlation function in
\omega\ll\Gamma_{s}
regime

As explained in section
3
, a diagrammatic evaluation of the spin density correlation function in the strict hydrodynamic regime
\omega\ll\Gamma_{s}
requires a summation of an infinite number of ladder diagrams that are enhanced by infrared pinching singularities.
The summation is achieved by solving a Bethe-Salpeter (or Schwinger-Dyson) equation for the vertex corrections given by rungs in the ladder diagrams.
In each diagram, the propagators should also include the leading imaginary part of the self-energy, which, via the optical theorem, is proportional to the total cross section of the heavy quark interacting with the background plasma, i.e., the relaxation rate.
Thus, the computation proceeds in the same way as the procedure for the perturbative evaluation of transport coefficients
Jeon:1994if
;
ValleBasagoiti:2002ir
;
Aarts:2002tn
;
Hidaka:2010gh
;
Jimenez-Alba:2015bia
, except for the additional heavy-quark expansion.
In the following, we divide our computation into three parts: we first derive the resummed formula for the spin density correlation, next find the Bethe-Salpeter equation for the effective vertex, and finally solve it in the heavy-quark limit.

1) Resummed formula for spin density correlation:

We choose to work in the real-time formalism in Schwinger-Keldysh contours
Schwinger:1960qe
;
Keldysh:1964ud
, especially in
r/a
basis defined by
O_{r}={1\over 2}(O_{1}+O_{2})
and
O_{a}=O_{1}-O_{2}
, in terms of the doubled fields
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
living on forward and backward contours in time, 1 and 2, respectively. The retarded two point correlation function is related to
ra
correlation function by
G_{R}=\mathrm{i}G_{ra}
.
4
4

4

Our definition of the retarded correlation function differs from the conventional definition in literature by a sign.

We would like to compute
ra
correlation function of spin density,

G^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}_{ra}\equiv\langle J^{0}_{~\mathrm{a},r}J^{0}_{~\mathrm{b},a}\rangle
, in the strict hydrodynamic regime of
\omega\to 0
, which is expected to behave as follows [recall eq. (

G^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}_{ra}(\omega)\approx\left(-\mathrm{i}\chi_{s}+{\chi_{s}\over\Gamma_{s}}\omega+\cdots\right)\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}.

(39)

The pinchingd singularities affect only the real part of this

ra

G_{R}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}
, which contains the spin relaxation rate
\Gamma_{s}
.
We also note that the imaginary part of
G_{R}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}

Due to the presence of the pinching singularities,
we now need to use the following resummed heavy-quark propagators in

\begin{split}S_{ra}(k)&=\parbox{71.13188pt}{\vskip 10.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-Sra.pdf}}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{k^{0}-E_{\bm{k}}+\mathrm{i}\zeta_{\bm{k}}/2},\\

where

, and

\displaystyle\simeq{d_{R}\over 4}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left[n_{F}(p^{0}+k^{0})-n_{F}(p^{0})\right]S_{ra}(p+k)S_{ar}(p)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big[\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(p;k)\sigma^{\mathrm{b}}\big]

\displaystyle\simeq{k^{0}}{d_{R}\over 4}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}n_{F}^{\prime}(p^{0})\frac{\mathrm{i}}{p^{0}-E_{\bm{p}}+\mathrm{i}\zeta_{\bm{p}}/2}\frac{\mathrm{i}}{p^{0}-E_{\bm{p}}-\mathrm{i}\zeta_{\bm{q}}/2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big[\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(p;k=0)\sigma^{\mathrm{b}}\big]

\displaystyle\simeq-{k^{0}}{d_{R}\over 4}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}n_{F}^{\prime}(E_{\bm{p}})\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\left[\frac{\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})}{\zeta_{\bm{p}}}\sigma^{\mathrm{b}}\right],

(42)

where we take the small
k^{0}\to 0
expansion together with
\bm{k}\to\bm{0}
, and
define
n_{F}^{\prime}(p^{0})\equiv\mathrm{d}n_{F}(p^{0})/\mathrm{d}p^{0}
.
To proceed the last line, we use the pinching pole approximation valid at leading order,

\frac{1}{p^{0}-E_{\bm{p}}+\mathrm{i}\zeta_{\bm{p}}/2}\frac{1}{p^{0}-E_{\bm{p}}-\mathrm{i}\zeta_{\bm{p}}/2}\longrightarrow{2\pi\over\zeta_{\bm{p}}}\delta(p^{0}-E_{\bm{p}}),

(43)

and also define the on-shell vertex function
\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})\equiv\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(p^{0}=E_{\bm{p}},\bm{p};\bm{k}=\bm{0})
.

The rotational invariance dictates that the on-shell vertex function divided by the heavy-quark relaxation rate should take the form

\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})\equiv{\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})\over\zeta_{\bm{p}}}=\sigma^{\mathrm{a}}f_{1}(|\bm{p}|)+p^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma})f_{2}(|\bm{p}|)+p^{\mathrm{a}}f_{3}(|\bm{p}|){\bm{1}}_{2\times 2}+\epsilon^{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{c}}p^{\mathrm{b}}\sigma^{\mathrm{c}}f_{4}(|\bm{p}|),

(44)

in terms of four possible scalar functions
f_{i}(|\bm{p}|)~(i=1,2,3,4)
that depend on
|\bm{p}|
only. Upon momentum integration in eq. (
42
), the last two terms do not contribute to
G^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}_{ra}
due to angular integration, but there is another reason to expect that
f_{3,4}
in fact vanish.
Under parity transformation, the spin vertex
\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}
remains the same, while the momentum
\bm{p}
flips its sign.
Therefore,
f_{3}
and
f_{4}
should be absent in a parity-even plasma without, e.g., axial chemical potential.

Inserting the above expression of
\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})/\zeta_{\bm{p}}
in eq. (
42
), and replacing
p^{\mathrm{a}}p^{\mathrm{b}}
with
{1\over 3}\bm{p}^{2}\delta^{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}
after angular integration, we arrive at the expression

G^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}_{ra}(\omega)=-{\omega}{d_{R}\over 2}\left[\int{\mathrm{d}^{3}p\over(2\pi)^{3}}n^{\prime}_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})F(|\bm{p}|)\right]\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}},

(45)

where
F(|\bm{p}|)\equiv f_{1}(|\bm{p}|)+{\bm{p}^{2}\over 3}f_{2}(|\bm{p}|)
. Our remaining task is to find the function
F(|\bm{p}|)
by solving the Bethe-Salpeter equation for the summation of the ladder diagrams.

2) Deriving the Bethe-Salpeter equation:

The Bethe-Salpeter equation for
\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})
is an integral equation,
\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})=\sigma^{\mathrm{a}}+\Delta\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})
, with the source
\sigma^{\mathrm{a}}
and the integral kernel
\Delta\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})
.
The equation is drawn diagrammatically as

\parbox{85.35826pt}{\vskip 0.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-BS1.pdf}}~=~\parbox{85.35826pt}{\vskip 0.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-BS2.pdf}}~+~\underbrace{\parbox{85.35826pt}{\vskip 0.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-BS3.pdf}}}_{\Delta\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})}

(46)

Again, focusing only on the contributions with pinching
singularities,
we identify the following three diagrams contributing to the integral kernel
\Delta\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})
:

\begin{split}\Delta\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})&=\parbox{85.35826pt}{\vskip 0.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-delta-Sigma1.pdf}}+\parbox{85.35826pt}{\vskip 0.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-delta-Sigma2.pdf}}~+~\parbox{85.35826pt}{\vskip 0.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-delta-Sigma3.pdf}}\;\;,\end{split}

(47)

where we denote the interaction vertex of heavy quark with gluon field
A^{\alpha}
as
H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})
.
Explicitly, these diagrams give us the expression for the integral kernel as

\begin{split}\Delta\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})&=(-\mathrm{i}g)^{2}C_{2}(R)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\Big[H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})S_{rr}(p+q)\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(p+q)S_{ra}(p+q)H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})D^{\alpha\beta}_{ra}(q)\\
&\hskip 110.0pt+H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})S_{ar}(p+q)\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(p+q)S_{rr}(p+q)H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})D^{\alpha\beta}_{ar}(q)\\
&\hskip 110.0pt+H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})S_{ar}(p+q)\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(p+q)S_{ra}(p+q)H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})D^{\alpha\beta}_{rr}(q)\Big],\end{split}

(48)

where
D^{\alpha\beta}(q)=\langle A^{\alpha}(q)A^{\beta}(-q)\rangle
is the gluon propagator in thermal equilibrium, and
C_{2}(R)
is the second-order Casimir of the heavy-quark representation.
In this expression, the interaction vertex
H_{\alpha}
and the gluon propagator
D^{\alpha\beta}
do not include color generators.

Note that the interaction vertex
H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})
consists of three parts,
two of which arise from the first two terms in the Lagrangian (
1
), respecting the
{\rm SU}(2)
spin symmetry. They are proportional to the identity matrix
\bm{1}_{2\times 2}
.
The first term in eq. (
1
) gives Coulomb interaction with
A^{0}
field, and the second term gives the magnetic interaction proportional to the current, which is further suppressed by the small velocity of heavy quarks,
|\bm{p}|/M=v\sim\sqrt{T/M}\ll 1
.
We thus only need the leading spin conserving interaction in our computation and neglect the second term in the following.
On the other hand, we need to keep the third term in eq. (
1
), which is responsible for spin relaxation in leading order.
The interaction vertex is therefore given by

H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})=\delta_{\alpha 0}\bm{1}_{2\times 2}-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2M}\epsilon_{\alpha\mathrm{b}\mathrm{c}}\sigma^{\mathrm{b}}q^{\mathrm{c}}.

(49)

where the temporal component of the Levi-Civita symbol is zero:
\epsilon_{0\mathrm{b}\mathrm{c}}=0
.

We again collect only the pinching pole contributions that arise from pairing
S_{ra}
and
S_{ar}
for
\Delta\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})
.
Relying on this pinching pole approximation
and recalling

D_{rr}^{\alpha\beta}(q)=\left(\frac{1}{2}+n_{B}(q^{0})\right)(D_{ra}^{\alpha\beta}(q)-D_{ar}^{\alpha\beta}(q))
,
we arrive at

\displaystyle\Delta\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle\simeq(-\mathrm{i}g)^{2}C_{2}(R)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\big[n_{F}(p^{0}+q^{0})+n_{B}(q^{0})\big]\left[H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(p+q)H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 104.0pt\times\big[D^{\alpha\beta}_{ra}(q)-D^{\alpha\beta}_{ar}(q)\big]S_{ar}(p+q)S_{ra}(p+q)

\displaystyle\simeq g^{2}C_{2}(R)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\big[n_{F}(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}})+n_{B}(q^{0})\big]\left[H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})\frac{\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p}+\bm{q})}{\zeta_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}}H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})\right]\rho^{\alpha\beta}(q)

\displaystyle\hskip 86.0pt\times 2\pi\delta(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}}-E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}),

(50)

where
D^{\alpha\beta}_{ra}(q)-D^{\alpha\beta}_{ar}(q)=\rho^{\alpha\beta}(q)
is the gluon spectral density, for which we use the HTL result summarized in eqs. (
27
)-(
30
) for our leading-log computation.
We also use the on-shell condition
p^{0}=E_{\bm{p}}
, that is imposed in the evaluation of
G^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}_{ra}
in eq. (
42
).

In terms of the function
\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})\equiv\Sigma^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})/\zeta_{\bm{p}}
, which can be shown to correspond to a linearized distribution function in kinetic theory, the integral equation depicted in eq. (
46
) then takes the following form

\displaystyle-\sigma^{\mathrm{a}}

\displaystyle=-\zeta_{\bm{p}}\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})+g^{2}C_{2}(R)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\big[n_{F}(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}})+n_{B}(q^{0})\big]\left[H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p}+\bm{q})H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})\right]\rho^{\alpha\beta}(q)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.0pt\times 2\pi\delta(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}}-E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}).

(51)

The important observation, that is common in the similar types of integral equations appearing in the computation of transport coefficients, is that the relaxation rate
\zeta_{\bm{p}}
from the imaginary part of one-loop self-energy diagram is computed by a very similar expression to the integral kernel,

\begin{split}\zeta_{\bm{p}}&={1\over 2}g^{2}C_{2}(R)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\big[n_{F}(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}})+n_{B}(q^{0})\big]\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\left[H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})\right]\rho^{\alpha\beta}(q)\\
&\hskip 90.0pt\times 2\pi\delta(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}}-E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}),\end{split}

(52)

where the factor
1/2
in front comes from the average over spin states since the relaxation rate is independent of the spin states due to rotational invariance.
This allows us to combine the two terms in the right-hand side of the integral equation (
51
), and we finally arrive at the Bethe-Salpeter equation for
\phi^{\mathrm{a}}
as

\displaystyle-\sigma^{\mathrm{a}}

\displaystyle=g^{2}C_{2}(R)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left(H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p}+\bm{q})H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})-{1\over 2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\left[H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})\right]\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})\right)\rho^{\alpha\beta}(q)

\displaystyle\hskip 90.0pt\times\big[n_{F}(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}})+n_{B}(q^{0})\big]2\pi\delta(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}}-E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}).

(53)

In the following, we will evaluate this integral equation in leading-log approximation by expanding in powers of small
\bm{q}\sim gT
to quadratic order to derive a second-order differential equation for
\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})
in
\bm{p}
space.
This can be considered as the linearized collision term in quantum kinetic theory, perturbed by an external source for the spin density given by the left-hand side of the integral equation.

Before moving to the evaluation of
\Gamma_{s}
, it is instructive to consider a limit where the
{\rm SU}(2)
spin symmetry is respected, by assuming that
H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})
contains only the leading term in
1/M
, i.e., the identity matrix in spin space. In this case, the integral equation becomes

\displaystyle-\sigma^{\mathrm{a}}

\displaystyle\simeq g^{2}C_{2}(R)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\big[\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p}+\bm{q})-\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})\big]\rho_{L}(q)

\displaystyle\hskip 84.0pt\times\big[n_{F}(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}})+n_{B}(q^{0})\big]2\pi\delta(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}}-E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}).

(54)

To see what happens in this limit, we integrate both sides in
\bm{p}
-space after multiplying a factor

W(\bm{p})=n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})[1-n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})]
.
By suitable shift of variables and using the properties of the spectral density

\rho(q^{0},\bm{q})=-\rho(-q^{0},\bm{q})=\rho(q^{0},-\bm{q})
,
one can show that the right-hand side vanishes after using the identity

W(\bm{p}+\bm{q})\big[n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})+n_{B}(E_{\bm{p}}-E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}})\big]+W(\bm{p})\big[n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}})+n_{B}(E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}-E_{\bm{p}})\big]=0.

(55)

This parallels the conservation property of total charges in the collision term in kinetic theory.
Since
W(\bm{p})>0
, the left-hand side is not zero after
\bm{p}
-integration, which means that the only solution for
\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})
in this limit is
\phi^{\mathrm{a}}\to\infty
.
This would imply
G_{ra}^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}\sim{\chi_{s}\over\Gamma_{s}}\omega\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}\to\infty
, which correctly gives us
\Gamma_{s}\to 0
in the limit.
From this consideration, it becomes clear that a finite value of
\Gamma_{s}
results only from the spin violating interaction in the vertex
H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})
due to the Pauli term.
This should be closely related to the (non-)conservative Ward-Takahashi identity for spin density.

3) Solving the Bethe-Salpeter equation in heavy-quark limit:

The most laborious part of our computation in this method is the leading-log evaluation of the integral equation (
53
).
We first note the presence of a scale hierarchy in the current problem.
The leading-log contributions come from the soft momentum exchange
gT\ll|\bm{q}|\ll T
, and the on-shell condition implies
q^{0}=E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}-E_{\bm{p}}\approx\bm{v}_{\bm{p}}\cdot\bm{q}\ll|\bm{q}|

with
\bm{v}_{\bm{p}}\equiv\bm{p}/M

, where we use the fact that the typical momentum of heavy quark in eq. (
42
) is
\bm{p}\sim\sqrt{MT}
from
E_{\bm{p}}\sim T
.
This gives us a hierarchy of scales
q^{0}\ll|\bm{q}|\ll|\bm{p}|
.
In the integral equation, we, therefore, expand each term in powers of
|{\bm{q}}|/|\bm{p}|\sim g
, and higher-order terms in
\bm{q}
and
q^{0}
bring about terms of higher powers of coupling constant
g
.
To our leading-log computation, it is thus sufficient to expand the terms in the integral equation to second order in
\bm{q}
and
q^{0}

ValleBasagoiti:2002ir
.

For example, recalling the spin structure of
\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})
consistent with rotational symmetry,
\phi^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})=\sigma^{\mathrm{a}}f_{1}(|\bm{p}|)+p^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma})f_{2}(|\bm{p}|)
, the expansion of
f_{1}(|\bm{p}+\bm{q}|)
would be

f_{1}(|\bm{p}+\bm{q}|)\approx f_{1}(|\bm{q}|)+\left(|\bm{q}|\cos\theta+{|\bm{q}|^{2}\over 2|\bm{p}|}\sin^{2}\theta\right)f^{\prime}_{1}(|\bm{p}|)+{1\over 2}|\bm{q}|^{2}\cos^{2}\theta f^{\prime\prime}_{1}(|\bm{p}|),

(56)

where
\theta
is the angle between
\bm{p}
and
\bm{q}
. Similarly, we need for our purpose

n_{F}(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}})+n_{B}(q^{0})\approx n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})+{1\over\beta q^{0}}-{1\over 2}+{\cal O}(q^{0}).

(57)

Furthermore, we can safely neglect
n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})
in the following since it is given by
n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})\approx\mathrm{e}^{-\beta M}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}\ll 1
in the dilute limit of heavy quarks.
It is also useful to perform the integration over the angle
\theta
first, by making the energy
\delta
-function into the one for the angle
\theta
,

\delta(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}}-E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}})={M\over|\bm{p}||\bm{q}|}\delta\left(\cos\theta-{M\over|\bm{p}||\bm{q}|}\left(q^{0}-{|\bm{q}|^{2}\over 2M}\right)\right),

(58)

together with restricting the integration range for
q^{0}
as

{|\bm{q}|^{2}\over 2M}-{|\bm{p}||\bm{q}|\over M}\leq q^{0}\leq{|\bm{q}|^{2}\over 2M}+{|\bm{p}||\bm{q}|\over M}.

(59)

Finally, we use the gluon spectral density in the Coulomb gauge

\rho^{\alpha\beta}(q)=\delta^{\alpha 0}\delta^{\beta 0}\rho_{L}(q)+P_{T}^{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q})\rho_{T}(q),

(60)

where
P_{T}^{ij}(\bm{q})=\delta^{ij}-q^{i}q^{j}/|\bm{q}|^{2}
is the projection operator for spatial components
i
and
j
.
The HTL resummed longitudinal and transverse spectral densities in the regime
q^{0}\ll|\bm{q}|\ll T
become the ones given in eq. (
30
), which is sufficient for our leading-log computation.

With all these ingredients used in the integral equation (
53
), performing the leading-log integral for
q
and comparing the spin structures in both sides of the equation, we arrive at the two coupled second-order differential equations for
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
as

\displaystyle{1\over 2}f^{\prime\prime}_{1}(|\bm{p}|)+{1\over|\bm{p}|}{f^{\prime}_{1}(|\bm{p}|)}+f_{2}(|\bm{p}|)-{|\bm{p}|\over 2TM}f^{\prime}_{1}(|\bm{p}|)-{1\over M^{2}}f_{1}(|\bm{p}|)

\displaystyle=-{1\over T^{2}\Gamma},

(61a)

\displaystyle{1\over 2}f_{2}^{\prime\prime}(|\bm{p}|)+{3\over|\bm{p}|}f^{\prime}_{2}(|\bm{p}|)-{|\bm{p}|\over 2TM}\left(f^{\prime}_{2}(|\bm{p}|)+{2\over|\bm{p}|}f_{2}(|\bm{p}|)\right)-{1\over M^{2}}f_{2}(|\bm{p}|)

\displaystyle=0,

(61b)

where we define

\Gamma\equiv{C_{2}(R)}{g^{2}m_{D}^{2}\log(1/g)\over 6\pi T}\sim g^{4}\log(1/g)T.

(62)

Interestingly, the two equations can be combined to give a single differential equation for
F(|\bm{p}|)=f_{1}(|\bm{p}|)+{1\over 3}|\bm{p}|^{2}f_{2}(|\bm{p}|)
, that is precisely what we need in order to evaluate the correlation function
G^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}_{ra}
in eq. (
45
),

{1\over 2}F^{\prime\prime}(|\bm{p}|)+{1\over|\bm{p}|}F^{\prime}(|\bm{p}|)-{|\bm{p}|\over 2TM}F^{\prime}(|\bm{p}|)-{1\over M^{2}}F(|\bm{p}|)=-{1\over T^{2}\Gamma},

(63)

which can be analytically solved in the heavy-quark limit.

Before solving this equation in
1/M
expansion, it is a useful check to see that the first three terms, that arise from the leading spin-conserving Coulomb interaction, reproduces the conservation property of the collision term in kinetic theory,

\int\mathrm{d}^{3}p\,\,W(\bm{p})\left({1\over 2}F^{\prime\prime}(|\bm{p}|)+{1\over|\bm{p}|}F^{\prime}(|\bm{p}|)-{|\bm{p}|\over 2TM}F^{\prime}(|\bm{p}|)\right)=0,

(64)

where the weight factor in our dilute limit becomes
W(\bm{p})=n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})[1-n_{F}(E_{\bm{p}})]\sim\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}=\mathrm{e}^{-\bm{p}^{2}/(2TM)}
, up to a constant factor.
Therefore, without the last term, which violates the conservation of spin, the solution would diverge
F\to\infty
.

This implies that the solution has the following expansion in
1/M
as

F(|\bm{p}|)={M^{2}}F_{(0)}(|\bm{p}|)+F_{(1)}(|\bm{p}|)+{\cal O}(M^{-2}),

(65)

and we are now only interested in the leading solution
F^{(0)}
.
Inserting this expansion into the differential equation (
63
),
we obtain the hierarchy of equations

\displaystyle{1\over 2}F_{(0)}^{\prime\prime}(|\bm{p}|)+{1\over|\bm{p}|}F_{(0)}^{\prime}(|\bm{p}|)-{|\bm{p}|\over 2TM}F_{(0)}^{\prime}(|\bm{p}|)

\displaystyle=0,

(66a)

\displaystyle{1\over 2}F_{(1)}^{\prime\prime}(|\bm{p}|)+{1\over|\bm{p}|}F_{(1)}^{\prime}(|\bm{p}|)-{|\bm{p}|\over 2TM}F_{(1)}^{\prime}(|\bm{p}|)-F_{(0)}(|\bm{p}|)

\displaystyle=-{1\over T^{2}\Gamma}.

(66b)

It is easy to find the solution
F_{(0)}(|\bm{p}|)=C
by inspection, where
C
is a constant. To determine
C
, we integrate both sides of the second equation with the weight factor
W(\bm{p})=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}
, that removes the term with
F^{(1)}
, and we arrive at
C=1/(T^{2}\Gamma)
.
With this leading solution

F(|\bm{p}|)={M^{2}\over T^{2}\Gamma}+{\cal O}(M^{0}),

(67)

together with eq. (
45
), we finally arrive at the correlation function

G^{J^{0}_{\mathrm{a}}J^{0}_{\mathrm{b}}}_{ra}(\omega)=-{\omega}{M^{2}\over T^{2}\Gamma}\left[{d_{R}\over 2}\int{\mathrm{d}^{3}p\over(2\pi)^{3}}n_{F}^{\prime}(E_{\bm{p}})\right]\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}},

(68)

which should be identified as
\omega{\chi_{s}\over\Gamma_{s}}\delta_{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}
, according to eq. (
39
).
Using
the spin susceptibility
\chi_{s}

\chi_{s}=-{d_{R}\over 2}\int{\mathrm{d}^{3}p\over(2\pi)^{3}}n_{F}^{\prime}(E_{\bm{p}}),

(69)

our result (
68
) finally gives us the spin relaxation rate
\Gamma_{s}

\Gamma_{s}=\Gamma{T^{2}\over M^{2}}={C_{2}(R)}{g^{2}m_{D}^{2}\log(1/g)T\over 6\pi M^{2}},

(70)

which precisely coincides with eq. (
38
) obtained in the previous subsection.

4.3
Method 3: Quantum kinetic theory for spin in heavy-quark limit

Kinetic theory is an intuitive and powerful framework for describing time-dependent dynamics of weakly interacting quasi-particles in phase space (position-momentum space)
Landau:Kinetic
.
It is a statistical description of the system in terms of particle number distribution in phase space.

When the two spin states are nearly degenerate in energy, and the quantum correlation time between two spin states is comparable to a macroscopic time scale of interests, one should instead consider the full
2\times 2
density matrix distribution in spin space,
\hat{\rho}(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)
, to properly describe time-dependent dynamics of spin polarization of quasi-particles, that is, the quantum kinetic theory.
The
2\times 2
density matrix in spin basis can be decomposed into the form

\hat{\rho}(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)={1\over 2}f(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)\bm{1}_{2\times 2}+\bm{S}(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)\cdot\bm{\sigma},

(71)

where
f(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[\hat{\rho}(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)]
is identified as the usual particle number distribution function, while
\bm{S}(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\left[\hat{\rho}(\bm{x},\bm{p},t){\bm{\sigma}\over 2}\right]
has the interpretation of the spin distribution function. The time evolution equation for each of them may be written in a similar form as the Boltzmann equation with proper collision terms. Alternatively, the equation written in terms of
\hat{\rho}
itself should take a form of the Lindblad equation, in general.

In ref.
Li:2019qkf
, the quantum Boltzmann equation of
f(\bm{p},t)
and
\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)
for massive quarks has been constructed to leading-log order of QCD coupling constant, when they are spatially homogeneous,

{\partial f(\bm{p},t)\over\partial t}=\hat{\Gamma}_{f}[f(\bm{p},t)]\,,\quad{\partial\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)\over\partial t}=\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}[\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)],

(72)

where the collision terms, or the relaxation operators,

\hat{\Gamma}_{f}
and
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}
, are universally of order
g^{4}\log(1/g)T
.
They take a form of second order differential operator in momentum space
\bm{p}
.
The
\hat{\Gamma}_{f}
is nothing but the usual collision term for quark number distribution function in QCD.
The collision term for the spin polarization, i.e.
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}
, is a novel object, whose explicit form in spatially homogeneous limit can be found in ref.
Li:2019qkf
.

We are interested in the heavy-quark limit, i.e.,
M\gg T
, of
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}
, that determines the relaxation dynamics of heavy-quark spin in momentum space, which ultimately gives us the spin relaxation rate
\Gamma_{s}
in the spin hydrodynamic regime.
Although it is possible to take
M\gg T
limit of the result in ref.
Li:2019qkf
, we present in this section the derivation of
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}
directly from our non-relativistic effective theory (see also the derivation based on the Kadanoff-Baym formalism in appendix
A
).
For clarity of the presentation, we will keep only the leading spin-conserving Coulomb interaction and the leading spin-violating Pauli-interaction, as in the previous section.
This is sufficient to obtain the correct spin relaxation rate at the leading order.
For more details of our method of derivation, we refer to ref.
Li:2019qkf
.

The full density operator
of heavy quark in spatially homogeneous limit is written in momentum basis as

\hat{\rho}(t)=\int{\mathrm{d}^{3}p\over(2\pi)^{3}}\hat{\rho}(\bm{p},t),

(73)

where
\hat{\rho}(\bm{p},t)
is the density matrix in spin space in the subspace of a given momentum
\bm{p}
.
More explicitly, we can express it as
\hat{\rho}(\bm{p},t)=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}|\bm{p},s\rangle\rho_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{p},t)\langle\bm{p},s^{\prime}|
, where
|\bm{p},s\rangle
is the eigenstate of momentum
\bm{p}
and spin basis state
s
. The
2\times 2
matrix
\rho_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{p},t)
is equivalent to the Wigner function of non-relativistic two-component quark field operators of spin 1/2, and our method in the following
gives the same result as in the Kadanoff-Baym approach at leading order as shown in appendix
A
.

The density matrix evolves in time by quantum mechanical unitary evolution in the thermal QCD plasma,

\hat{\rho}(t+\Delta t)=\bigg\langle\hat{U}_{1}(t+\Delta t,t)\hat{\rho}(t)\hat{U}_{2}^{\dagger}(t+\Delta t,t)\bigg\rangle_{A},

(74)

where
\langle\cdots\rangle_{A}
denotes the thermal average of background gluon fields interacting with the heavy quark, and the subscript
1
and
2
refers to as the forward and backward Schwinger-Keldysh contours, respectively.
This is because the forward contour 1 describes the evolution of ket state
|\bm{p},s\rangle
, while the backward contour 2 is for the conjugate bra state
\langle\bm{p},s^{\prime}|
.
Note also that the gluon fields in the evolution operator
\hat{U}_{1,2}
are the Schwinger-Keldysh fields
A^{1,2}_{\mu}
on the contours 1 and 2, respectively, whose correlation functions satisfy the fluctuation-dissipation relations in thermal equilibrium.

Expanding
\hat{U}(t+\Delta t,t)
up to the second-order of the quark-gluon interactions in the interaction picture and performing a thermal average of gluon two-point correlation functions, one obtains the time evolution equation for the density matrix as

{\partial\hat{\rho}(\bm{p},t)\over\partial t}=g^{2}C_{2}(R)(\Gamma_{\rm cross}+\Gamma_{\rm self\,energy}),

(75)

where
\Gamma_{\rm cross}
arises from the correlation of first order terms in quark-gluon interaction in contours 1 and 2, while
\Gamma_{\rm self\,energy}
is from the second order terms in each contour separately.
The diagrammatic representation is depicted in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
The “cross” contribution (a), and the two self-energy contributions (b) and (c). In these figures, time flows from the right to the left.
To emphasize the HTL contribution in the gluon propagator, we put the blobs in these figures.

Explicitly, we find

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm cross}

\displaystyle=\int{\mathrm{d}^{4}q\over(2\pi)^{4}}H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})\hat{\rho}(\bm{p}-\bm{q},t)H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})D_{12}^{\alpha\beta}(q)2\pi\delta(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}-\bm{q}}-E_{\bm{p}}),

(76)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm self\,energy}

\displaystyle=-{1\over 2}\int{\mathrm{d}^{4}q\over(2\pi)^{4}}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big[H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})\big]D^{\alpha\beta}_{21}(q)2\pi\delta(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}-\bm{q}}-E_{\bm{p}})\hat{\rho}(\bm{p},t),

(77)

where
D_{12}^{\alpha\beta}(q)
and
D_{21}^{\alpha\beta}(q)
are
the thermal gluon correlation functions on Schwinger-Keldysh contours defined in eq. (
26
), and

H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})
is the heavy quark-gluon interaction vertex given in eq. (
49
).
In deriving the self-energy term, we need to use the identity

D_{11}(t)\theta(t)+D_{22}(t)\theta(-t)=D_{21}(t)
,
and also the fact that the self-energy is proportional to identity matrix in spin space due to parity symmetry.

One can understand the structure of the above equation (
75
) from the viewpoint of the Lindblad equation
Lindblad:1975ef
:

{\partial\hat{\rho}\over\partial t}=\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}\hat{\rho}\gamma_{i}^{\dagger}-{1\over 2}\{\gamma_{i}^{\dagger}\gamma_{i},\hat{\rho}\}_{+}.

(78)

with the anti-commutator

\{A,B\}_{+}\equiv AB+BA
.
The self-energy term corresponds to a dissipative loss of probability, while the cross term is a gain term that ensures the total probability conservation. The index
i
becomes the
t
-channel gluon momentum
q
.
One can explicitly check that the total trace of the density matrix in momentum-spin space is indeed conserved in our evolution equation.
It is also possible to show at this stage that the Boltzmann equilibrium density matrix,

\hat{\rho}_{\rm eq}(\bm{p})\propto{\bf 1}_{2\times 2}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}
,
is a zero mode of the collision operator.

The computation of the above
\Gamma_{\rm cross}
and
\Gamma_{\rm self\,energy}
in leading-log order is very similar to that in the previous section. Since we are interested in the spin polarization of the density matrix, we focus only on the spin part,
\hat{\rho}(\bm{p})\sim\bm{S}(\bm{p})\cdot\bm{\sigma}
, in the following.
Using the gluon spectral density (
27
) in Coulomb gauge, a simple algebra leads to

{\partial\rho(\bm{p},t)\over\partial t}=g^{2}C_{2}(R)\int{\mathrm{d}^{4}q\over(2\pi)^{4}}\left[C_{L}\,\rho_{L}(q)-{1\over 2M^{2}}C_{T}\,\rho_{T}(q)\right]2\pi\delta(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}-\bm{q}}-E_{\bm{p}}),

(79)

where we introduced

\begin{split}C_{L}&=\big[\bm{S}(\bm{p}-\bm{q})-\bm{S}(\bm{p})\big]\cdot\bm{\sigma}\,\,n_{B}(q^{0})-\bm{S}(\bm{p})\cdot\bm{\sigma},\\
C_{T}&=\big[(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{\sigma})\big(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{S}(\bm{p}-\bm{q})\big)+\bm{q}^{2}\bm{S}(\bm{p})\cdot\bm{\sigma}\big]n_{B}(q^{0})+\bm{q}^{2}\bm{S}(\bm{p})\cdot\bm{\sigma}.\end{split}

(80)

Note that the
C_{L}
comes from the Coulomb interaction respecting
the spin symmetry, while
C_{T}
does from the Pauli interaction that breaks it.

As in the previous sections, we perform the angular integration of
\cos\theta
between
\bm{p}
and
\bm{q}
first, using the relation
\cos\theta={M\over|\bm{p}|}{q^{0}\over|\bm{q}|}+{|\bm{q}|\over 2|\bm{p}|}
.
This replaces the
q
integration as

\int{\mathrm{d}^{4}q\over(2\pi)^{4}}2\pi\delta(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}-\bm{q}}-E_{\bm{p}})\to{1\over(2\pi)^{2}}{M\over|\bm{p}|}\int_{gT}^{T}\mathrm{d}|\bm{q}||\bm{q}|\int\mathrm{d}q^{0},

(81)

with the
q^{0}
-integration range

-{|\bm{q}|^{2}\over 2M}-{|\bm{p}||\bm{q}|\over M}\leq q^{0}\leq-{|\bm{q}|^{2}\over 2M}+{|\bm{p}||\bm{q}|\over M}
.
In computing the
\bm{q}
-integration, we have to use
\bm{q}=\bm{q}_{L}+\bm{q}_{T}=|\bm{q}|\cos\theta\hat{\bm{p}}+\bm{q}_{T}
with
\cos\theta
given as above, while the
\bm{q}_{T}
-integration gives zero after polar angle integration.
Similarly,
q^{i}q^{j}
is replaced by

q^{i}q^{j}\to|\bm{q}|^{2}\left[\cos^{2}\theta\hat{p}^{i}\hat{p}^{j}+{1\over 2}\sin^{2}\theta\left(\delta^{ij}-\hat{p}^{i}\hat{p}^{j}\right)\right].

(82)

The leading-log arises from the expansion of
C_{L,T}
up to second order in
\bm{q}\sim gT\ll\bm{p}
, and performing leading-log integral in
|\bm{q}|
, with the range from
m_{D}\sim gT
to the upper bound of
T
, where our expression of HTL gluon spectral density breaks down.

The result for
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}
is organized as the
1/M
expansion,

\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}=\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(0)}+\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}+\cdots,

(83)

where
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(0)}
arises from the longitudinal Coulomb interaction,
C_{L}
, while the next order term
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}
of order
1/M^{2}
results from the Pauli-interaction,
C_{T}
.
The leading term
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(0)}
, which conserves the total spin density, turns out to be identical to the leading term in
\hat{\Gamma}_{f}
, which describes the momentum diffusion of heavy quark with the known heavy-quark drag force coefficient
\eta_{D}
as
Moore:2004tg

\hat{\Gamma}_{S}^{(0)\mathrm{a}}[\bm{S}(\bm{p})]=\eta_{D}\bm{\nabla}_{p}\cdot\left(\bm{p}\,S^{\mathrm{a}}+TM\bm{\nabla}_{p}S^{\mathrm{a}}\right)\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\eta_{D}=C_{2}(R){g^{2}m_{D}^{2}\log(1/g)\over 12\pi M}

(84)

This results in the Fokker-Planck equation for the density matrix equivalent to the Langevin equation of heavy-quark Brownian motion, with each component
S^{\mathrm{a}}(\bm{p})
playing a role of distribution in momentum space,

\dot{\bm{p}}=-\eta_{D}\bm{p}+\bm{\xi}(t)\,,\quad\langle\xi^{\mathrm{a}}(t)\xi^{\mathrm{b}}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\kappa\delta^{\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}}\delta(t-t^{\prime}),

(85)

with
\kappa=2MT\eta_{D}
from the fluctuation-dissipation relation.
Physically, what happens is that the spin attached to the heavy quark simply follows the motion of the heavy quark in momentum space. The same result has also been obtained to this order in ref.
Yang:2020hri
.
The next leading collision operator,
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}
, which is new and encodes the spin-violating effect, is given by a simple expression

\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}[\bm{S}(\bm{p})]=-\eta_{D}{2T\over M}\bm{S}(\bm{p})=-C_{2}(R){g^{2}m_{D}^{2}T\log(1/g)\over 6\pi M^{2}}\bm{S}(\bm{p}),

(86)

from which we can evaluate the spin relaxation rate in the leading-log.

The leading
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(0)}
term determines the relaxation rates of non-hydrodynamic modes of spin distribution in momentum space.
In other words, its non-zero eigenvalue corresponds to the relaxation rate of the non-hydrodynamic eigenmode.
The only exception is the mode with zero eigenvalue, which corresponds to the quasi-hydrodynamic
(or Hydro+) mode of spin density.
It is easy to guess what the zero mode should be: it is the equilibrium Boltzmann distribution,
\bm{S}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}
, with any constant vector
\bm{S}_{0}
.
Indeed, it is a simple algebra to see that
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(0)}[\bm{S}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}]=0
, while the next order term
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}
gives the relaxation dynamics of spin that we are interested in.

After integration over
\bm{p}
, the spin density in position space is given by

\bm{S}=\int{\mathrm{d}^{3}p\over(2\pi)^{3}}\bm{S}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}=\left(\frac{TM}{2\pi}\right)^{3/2}\bm{S}_{0},

(87)

and
\bm{S}_{0}
can be identified as the quasi-hydrodynamic mode of spin density. Therefore, we are led to write down the spin distribution function in
the
1/M
expansion as

\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)=\bm{S}_{0}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}+\Delta\bm{S}(\bm{p},t),

(88)

where
\Delta\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)
contains all non-hydrodynamic modes which relax much faster than
\bm{S}_{0}(t)
. It is defined by requiring
\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)
and
\bm{S}_{0}(t)
to satisfy the matching condition (
87
),
which means that
\int_{\bm{p}}\Delta\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)=0
.
It is expected that
\Delta\bm{S}(\bm{p},t)
is smaller than the leading term by the hydrodynamic expansion parameter
\omega/\Gamma
.
In the regime of spin hydrodynamics where
\omega\sim\Gamma_{s}\sim(T/M)^{2}\Gamma
,
the derivative expansion becomes equivalent to
T/M\ll 1
expansion.
The relaxation rate of the quasi-hydrodynamic mode,
\bm{S}_{0}(t)
,
is the spin relaxation rate
\Gamma_{s}
that we are interested in.

After using the expansion (
88
) in the quantum Boltzmann equation (
72
),
and integrating over
\bm{p}
, we arrive at

{\partial\bm{S}_{0}(t)\over\partial t}={\int_{\bm{p}}\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}[\bm{S}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}]\over\int_{\bm{p}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}},

(89)

where we used the matching condition (
87
), as well as the conservation property
\int_{\bm{p}}\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(0)}[\bm{S}(\bm{p})]=0
for any
\bm{S}(\bm{p})
.
We dropped the higher-order terms in the
1/M
and small frequency expansion, e.g.,
by neglecting the term such as

\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}[\Delta\bm{S}(\bm{p})]
.
With our result of
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}
given in eq. (
86
), we have

\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}_{S}^{(1)}[\bm{S}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}]=-{\eta_{D}}{2T\over M}\bm{S}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}},

(90)

which enables us rewrite eq. (
89
) as the following simple relaxation equation:

{\partial\bm{S}_{0}(t)\over\partial t}=-\Gamma_{s}\bm{S}_{0}(t).

(91)

Here, we eventually identify the spin relaxation rate
\Gamma_{s}
from the quantum kinetic theory as

\Gamma_{s}={\eta_{D}}{2T\over M}{\int_{\bm{p}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}\over\int_{\bm{p}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{\bm{p}}}}={\eta_{D}}{2T\over M}={C_{2}(R)}{g^{2}m_{D}^{2}\log(1/g)T\over 6\pi M^{2}},

(92)

which agrees with the result of the diagrammatic methods in the previous subsections [see eqs. (
38
) and (
70
)].

5
Summary and Outlook

In this paper, we have evaluated the spin relaxation rate
\Gamma_{s}
for the heavy quark, based on pQCD to leading-log order of coupling constant
g
.
We have formulated three different methods to evaluate
\Gamma_{s}
:
1) the Green-Kubo formula based on the source-source correlator in the spin hydrodynamic regime, 2) the spin density correlator in the strict hydrodynamic regime, and 3) the quantum kinetic equation for the heavy-quark spin distribution.
While each method demonstrates a different view on spin dynamics,
all of these lead to the same result
\Gamma_{s}\sim g^{4}\log(1/g)T(T/M)^{2}
[see eqs. (
38
), (
70
), and (
92
)].
Thanks to the additional
(T/M)^{2}
factor, the heavy quark spin shows a parametrically slow dynamics compared to other non-hydrodynamic modes.
This scale hierarchy
\Gamma_{s}\ll\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is the relaxation rate for other non-hydrodynamic modes, guarantees the existence of the spin hydrodynamic regime as a well-defined example of Hydro+
Stephanov:2017ghc
.

Several outlooks related to the present paper are in order.
While we rely on finite-temperature pQCD to evaluate the spin relaxation rate,
our formulation — in particular the heavy quark rotational viscosity,
\lambda_{s}
in terms of the source or spin correlation functions — is applicable even in the strong-coupling regime, where we have the AdS/CFT correspondence as another theoretical tool
Mcinnes:2018wzw
;
Garbiso:2020puw
;
Chen:2020ath
;
Cartwright:2021qpp
, and in particular the methods first developed for holographic spin liquids in ref.
Gallegos:2020otk
.
It would be interesting to ask how we can formulate heavy-quark spin relaxation in the strong-coupling regime of QCD-like theories, such as
\mathcal{N}=4
super Yang-Mills theory, in a manner similar to the heavy quark drag force
Herzog:2006gh
;
Gubser:2006bz
;
Casalderrey-Solana:2006fio
.
It will give an important benchmark in another extreme limit of the theory, which would be useful in the phenomenological analysis of spin dynamics within the QGP created in heavy-ion collisions.

Another interesting outlook is to apply our result in astrophysics or condensed matter systems.
In fact, while we focus on the heavy-quark spin relaxation in QCD plasma in the present paper, our formulation works as well in other systems where spin is approximately conserved.
For example, the relaxation rate of proton spin in QED plasma can be studied by our methods with a minor modification.
Also, it is interesting to apply the present formulation to various spin systems in condensed matter physics, which deviate from the Heisenberg model only by a small symmetry-breaking perturbation.
In this case, the effective field theory with symmetry breaking terms (see, e.g., refs.
Gongyo:2016dzp
;
Hongo:2020xaw
) may be a useful starting point to describe the relaxation rate of spin in condensed matter physics.
We leave all these as future work.

Appendix A
Derivation of quantum kinetic equation from Kadanoff-Baym formalism

In this appendix, we derive the quantum kinetic equation (
75
)-(
77
) for heavy quarks based on the real-time Kadanoff-Baym formalism
Kadanoff-Baym1961
.

Our starting point is the Schwinger-Dyson equation for heavy-quark Green’s function in the real-time formalism, diagrammatically given by

\begin{split}\parbox{71.13188pt}{\vskip 6.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-S12-full.pdf}}&=\parbox{71.13188pt}{\vskip 6.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-S12.pdf}}~+~\sum_{a,b=1}^{2}~\parbox{113.81102pt}{\vskip 0.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-S0-Sigma-S.pdf}}\\
&=\parbox{71.13188pt}{\vskip 6.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-S12.pdf}}~+~\sum_{a,b=1}^{2}~\parbox{113.81102pt}{\vskip 0.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-S-Sigma-S0.pdf}}\;\;,\end{split}

(93)

where
\Sigma_{ab}~(a,b=1,2)
denotes the heavy-quark self-energy in the
12
basis on the Schwinger-Keldysh contour.
Note that Green’s functions in the
ra
basis are related to those in the
12
basis as

\begin{split}S_{ra}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(S_{11}-S_{12}+S_{21}-S_{22}\right),\\
S_{ar}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(S_{11}+S_{12}-S_{21}-S_{22}\right).\end{split}

(94)

After a little cumbersome computation,
we can rewrite the Schwinger-Dyson equation depicted in eq. (
93
) as follows:

\begin{split}S_{12}&=S^{(0)}_{12}-\mathrm{i}S^{(0)}_{ra}\Sigma_{ra}S_{12}-\mathrm{i}S^{(0)}_{ra}\Sigma_{12}S_{ar}-\mathrm{i}S^{(0)}_{12}\Sigma_{ar}S_{ar}\\
&=S^{(0)}_{12}-\mathrm{i}S_{ra}\Sigma_{ra}S^{(0)}_{12}-\mathrm{i}S_{ra}\Sigma_{12}S^{(0)}_{ar}-\mathrm{i}S_{12}\Sigma_{ar}S^{(0)}_{ar}.\end{split}

(95)

Acting the free inverse propagator

(S^{(0)})^{-1}
satisfying

(S^{(0)})^{-1}S^{(0)}_{ra}=\mathrm{i}=S^{(0)}_{ar}(S^{(0)})^{-1}

and

(S^{(0)})^{-1}S^{(0)}_{12}=0=S^{(0)}_{12}(S^{(0)})^{-1}

to this equation, we obtain
the left and right Schwinger-Dyson equations

\displaystyle(S^{(0)})^{-1}S_{12}

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{ra}S_{12}+\Sigma_{12}S_{ar},

(96a)

\displaystyle S_{12}(S^{(0)})^{-1}

\displaystyle=S_{ra}\Sigma_{12}+S_{12}\Sigma_{ar}.

(96b)

We then take the difference of these two equations.
Assuming the two-point function
S_{12}
has a weak dependence on the center-of-mass coordinate, we can rely on the derivative expansion.
Moreover, recalling the explicit form of the free propagator

(S^{(0)})^{-1}=\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{2M}\bm{\nabla}^{2}
, we find that the difference of the Schwinger-Dyson equations becomes

\begin{split}\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{M}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{x}\right)S_{12}(x,p)=&-\mathrm{i}\big[\Sigma_{ra}(x,p)\star S_{12}(x,p)+\Sigma_{12}(x,p)\star S_{ar}(x,p)\\
&\hskip 14.0pt-S_{ra}(x,p)\star\Sigma_{12}(x,p)-S_{12}(x,p)\star\Sigma_{ar}(x,p)\big].\end{split}

(97)

where we introduced the Wigner transform
of two-point functions
and the Moyal product of
A(x,p)
and
B(x,p)
as

\displaystyle G(x,p)

\displaystyle\equiv\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}r}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}p^{0}r^{0}+\mathrm{i}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{r}}G\left(x+\frac{1}{2}r,x-\frac{1}{2}r\right),

(98)

\displaystyle A(x,p)\star B(x,p)

\displaystyle\equiv\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(\partial_{x}\cdot\partial_{p^{\prime}}-\partial_{x^{\prime}}\cdot\partial_{p})}A(x,p)B(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})\Big|_{x^{\prime}=x,p^{\prime}=p}.

(99)

Expressing some parts of

S_{ra},S_{ar},\Sigma_{ra}
, and
\Sigma_{ar}

in the
12
basis, and reorganizing them,
we obtain a quantum transport equation for the real-time Green’s function
S_{12}
as

\begin{split}\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{M}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{x}\right)S_{12}+[\mathrm{i}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\Sigma_{ra},S_{12}]_{\star}-[\Sigma_{12},\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}S_{ra}]_{\star}=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\Big[\{\Sigma_{21},S_{12}\}_{\star}-\{\Sigma_{12},S_{21}\}_{\star}\Big],\end{split}

(100)

where we introduced a commutator

[A,B]_{\star}\equiv A\star B-B\star A
and
anti-commutator
\{A,B\}_{\star}\equiv A\star B+B\star A
with the Moyal product, respectively.
For our purpose, we can neglect all
\bm{x}
-dependence and the terms with commutators in the left-hand side since the collision term is captured by the right-hand side of this equation.
Moreover, expanding the Moyal product simplifies the right-hand side as
\{A,B\}_{\star}\simeq AB+BA
.
Then, by evaluating the right-hand side (the collision term) with a usual product, we identify this equation as the quantum kinetic equation for heavy quarks including spin, which agrees with eq. (
75
) in the main text.

Let us evaluate the self-energy appearing in the right-hand side of eq. (
100
).
As usual, the leading diagram is given by

\begin{split}-\mathrm{i}\Sigma_{ab}(p)&=~\parbox{91.04872pt}{\vskip-22.0pt\includegraphics[width]{fig-leading-self-energy.pdf}}=(\mathrm{i}g)^{2}C_{2}(R)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}D^{\beta\alpha}_{ba}(q)H_{\alpha}(-\bm{q})S_{ab}(p+q)H_{\beta}(\bm{q}),\end{split}

(101)

where the interaction vertex
H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})
between the heavy quark and gluon is given by eq. (
49
).
Relying on the quasi-particle approximation for heavy quarks, we parametrize the lesser and greater Green’s functions as

S_{12}(t,\bm{p})=-\rho(t,\bm{p})2\pi\delta(p^{0}-E_{\bm{p}})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad S_{21}(t,\bm{p})=[\bm{1}_{2\times 2}-\rho(t,\bm{p})]2\pi\delta(p^{0}-E_{\bm{p}}),

(102)

with a
2\times 2
distribution function
\rho(t,\bm{p})
.
As a result, we obtain the following expression for the self-energy:

\begin{split}-\mathrm{i}\Sigma_{12}(p)&=g^{2}C_{2}(R)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}H_{\alpha}(-\bm{q})\rho(t,\bm{p}+\bm{q})H_{\beta}(\bm{q})D^{\alpha\beta}_{21}(q)2\pi\delta(p^{0}+q^{0}-E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}),\\
-\mathrm{i}\Sigma_{21}(p)&=g^{2}C_{2}(R)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}H_{\alpha}(-\bm{q})\big[\rho(t,\bm{p}+\bm{q})-\bm{1}_{2\times 2}\big]H_{\beta}(\bm{q})D^{\alpha\beta}_{12}(q)2\pi\delta(p^{0}+q^{0}-E_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}).\end{split}

(103)

Thanks to the quasi-particle approximation,
the heavy-quark propagator is accompanied by the delta function, which we can eliminate by performing the
p^{0}
-integration.
Furthermore, we consider the dilute system of heavy quarks, and neglect
\mathcal{O}(\rho^{2})
-terms.
As a consequence, we derive a simplified form for the quantum kinetic equation as

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\rho&\simeq g^{2}C_{2}(R)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\bigg[H_{\alpha}(\bm{q})\rho(t,\bm{p}-\bm{q})H_{\beta}(-\bm{q})D^{\alpha\beta}_{12}(q)2\pi\delta(q^{0}+E_{\bm{p}-\bm{q}}-E_{\bm{p}})\\

Balayage of Radon measures of infinite energy
on locally compact spaces

Abstract




Abstract.
For suitable kernels on a locally compact space
X
, we develop a theory of inner balayage of quite general Radon measures
\omega
(not necessarily of finite energy) to arbitrary
A\subset X
. In the case where
A
is Borel, this theory provides, as a by-product, a theory of outer balayage. We prove the existence and the uniqueness of inner (outer) swept measures, analyze their properties, and provide a number of alternative characterizations. In spite of being in agreement with Cartan’s theory of Newtonian balayage, the results obtained require essentially new methods and approaches, since in the case in question, useful specific features of Newtonian potentials may fail to hold. The theory thereby established generalizes substantially the existing ones, pertaining either to
\omega
of finite energy, or to some particular
A
(e.g. quasiclosed). This work covers many interesting kernels in classical and modern potential theory, which looks promising for possible applications.

Introduction and general conventions

The present paper deals with the theory of potentials on a locally compact (Hausdorff) space
X
with respect to a kernel
\kappa
,

\kappa:X\times X\to[0,\infty]
.

In more details, under suitable requirements on a kernel
\kappa
, we develop a theory of
inner
balayage
\omega^{A}
of quite a general Radon measure
\omega
(not necessarily of finite energy) to
arbitrary

A\subset X
(Theorems
2.5
,
2.7
,
4.8
,
5.2
). In the case where
A
is Borel, the theory thereby established provides, as a by-product, a theory of
outer
balayage
\omega^{*A}
(Theorems
2.11
,
4.8
,
5.2
). Regarding the theory of outer balayage, cf. also Theorem 4.12 by Fuglede
[
17
]
, pertaining to

sets, that is, to those

which can be approximated in outer capacity by closed sets
[
16
]
. (It is worth emphasizing here that a quasiclosed set is not necessarily Borel, and vice versa.)

As to the history of the question, the theory of inner balayage of any
\omega
of
finite
energy to any
A\subset X
was developed in the author’s recent papers
[
26
]
–
[
28
]
, and this was performed for any

Such a theory was further extended in

. To this end, we needed, however, to impose upon

\kappa
, and

, were required in
[

to form a strongly closed cone. (This would occur, for instance, if

[
30

In this work, we generalize the theory established in
[

]
to

A\subset X
. Moreover, some of the results thereby obtained are new even in the case treated in
[
31
]
. In particular, we show that if the space

1.1.
General conventions

To begin with, we shall first introduce some notions and notations, to be used throughout this paper.

Given a locally compact space
X
, we denote by
\mathfrak{M}
the linear space of all (real-valued Radon) measures
\mu
on
X
equipped with the
vague
topology of pointwise convergence on the class
C_{0}(X)
of all continuous functions
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
of compact support
{\rm Supp}(\varphi)
, and by
\mathfrak{M}^{+}
the cone of all positive
\mu\in\mathfrak{M}
, where
\mu
is
positive
if and only if
\mu(\varphi)\geqslant 0
for all positive
\varphi\in C_{0}(X)
. (For the theory of measures and integration on a locally compact space, we refer to Bourbaki
[
8
]
or Edwards
[
14
]
.)

Given
\mu,\nu\in\mathfrak{M}
, the
mutual energy
and the
potential
are introduced by

\displaystyle I(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle:=\int\kappa(x,y)\,d(\mu\otimes\nu)(x,y),

\displaystyle U^{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle:=\int\kappa(x,y)\,d\mu(y),\quad x\in X,

respectively, provided the value on the right is well defined as a finite number or
\pm\infty
. For
\mu=\nu
, the mutual energy
I(\mu,\nu)
defines the
energy

I(\mu,\mu)=:I(\mu)
of
\mu\in\mathfrak{M}
.

In what follows, a kernel
\kappa
is assumed to satisfy the
energy principle
, or equivalently to be
strictly positive definite
, which means that
I(\mu)\geqslant 0
for all (signed)
\mu\in\mathfrak{M}
, and moreover that
I(\mu)
equals
0
only for the zero measure. Then all
\mu\in\mathfrak{M}
of finite energy
0\leqslant I(\mu)<\infty
form a pre-Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
with the inner product
\langle\mu,\nu\rangle:=I(\mu,\nu)
and the energy norm
\|\mu\|:=\sqrt{I(\mu)}
, cf.
[
15
, Lemma 3.1.2]
. The topology on
\mathcal{E}
determined by means of this norm, is said to be
strong
.

In addition, we always assume that
\kappa
satisfies the
consistency
principle, which means that the cone

\mathcal{E}^{+}:=\mathcal{E}\cap\mathfrak{M}^{+}
is
complete
in the induced strong topology, and that the strong topology on
\mathcal{E}^{+}
is

than the induced vague topology on
\mathcal{E}^{+}
; such a kernel is said to be

(Fuglede
[
15
]

converges

to the same unique measure
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{E}^{+}
, the strong topology on
\mathcal{E}
as well as the vague topology on
\mathfrak{M}
being Hausdorff.

Yet another permanent requirement upon
\kappa
is that it satisfies the

and
Ugaheri maximum principles
, where the former means that for any
\mu\in\mathcal{E}^{+}
and any
\nu\in\mathfrak{M}^{+}
with
U^{\mu}\leqslant U^{\nu}

\mu

, depending on

-Ugaheri’s maximum principle is referred to as

[

Abstract




Abstract

Consider the Lane-Emden system

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=v^{p},\quad u>0,\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\\
-\Delta v=u^{q},\quad v>0,\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u=v=0,\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
and
q\geq p>0
. The asymptotic behavior of
least energy solutions
of this system was studied in
[
22
,
7
]
for
N\geq 3
. However, the case
N=2
is different and remains completely open. In this paper, we study the case
N=2
with

q=p+\theta_{p}
and
\sup_{p}\theta_{p}<+\infty
. Under the following natural condition that holds for least energy solutions

\limsup_{p\to+\infty}p\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla v_{p}\mathrm{d}x<+\infty,

we give a complete description of the asymptotic behavior of
positive solutions

(u_{p},v_{p})
(i.e., not only for least energy solutions) as
p\to+\infty
. This seems the first result for asymptotic behaviors of the Lane-Emden system in the two dimension case.

35J50, 35J15, 35J60.

1
Introduction

We consider the Lane-Emden system

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=v^{p},\quad u>0,\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\\
-\Delta v=u^{q},\quad v>0,\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u=v=0,\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
and
p,q>0
.
Without loss of generality, we always assume
q\geq p
.

The system (
1.1
) has received great interest and has been widely studied in the literature; see e.g.
[
3
,
7
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
22
,
23
,
26
]
, the review article
[
10
]
and the references therein. It is well known that the existence and nonexistence of solutions of (
1.1
) is closely related to the so-called critical hyperbola

\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}=\frac{N-2}{N}.

When

\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}>\frac{N-2}{N},

which is known as the subcritical case, it was proved in
[
9
]
by the degree method that (
1.1
) has a positive solution for
p,q>1
, and later it was proved in
[
3
]
by the variational method that (
1.1
) has a least energy solution as long as
pq\neq 1
. However, if

\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}\leq\frac{N-2}{N},

it was proved in
[
26
]
that (
1.1
) has no solutions provided
\Omega
is star-shaped.

In view of the above existence theory, a natural question that interests us arises:

Question
:
What is the limiting profile of positive solutions of (
1.1
) when
(p,q)
converges to the critical hyperbola
{\cal H}
? Here

{\cal H}:=\left\{(p,q)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:~p,q>0,~~\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}=\frac{N-2}{N}\right\}.

It turns out that this problem is challenging and was only studied in
[
22
,
7
]
. They considered the case
N\geq 3
so that the critical hyperbola
{\cal H}
is a curve in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. They studied the special case that

(p,q)=(p,q_{\varepsilon})
satisfies

\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q_{\varepsilon}+1}=\frac{N-2}{N}+\varepsilon,\quad p\in\Big(\frac{2}{N-2},\frac{N+2}{N-2}\Big],\;q_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon>0.

(1.2)

Then by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
so that
(p,q_{\varepsilon})\to(p,q)\in{\cal H}
, they proved that, among other things, the least energy solution
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})

of (
1.1
) must blow up, i.e.,
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty},\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\to+\infty
, and a suitable scaling of
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})

converges to a positive solution of the Lane-Emden system in the whole space

\begin{cases}-\Delta U=V^{p},\quad U>0,\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
-\Delta V=U^{q},\quad V>0,\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
U(x),V(x)\to 0,\quad\text{as}~|x|\to+\infty.\end{cases}

Furthermore, the blowup set of
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
consists of a single point.

As far as we know, the above problem for the case
N=2
has not been studied in the literature and is completely open. Remark that in contrast with the case
N\geq 3
,
N=2
implies that the critical hyperbola
{\cal H}
becomes the single point
(+\infty,+\infty)
, i.e., we have to consider
(p,q)\to(+\infty,+\infty)
, which makes the problem quite different comparing to the case
N\geq 3
.
The purpose of this paper is to give the first answer of this problem for the case
N=2
under an additional assumption
|q-p|\leq C
during
(p,q)\to(+\infty,+\infty)
. Thus, up to a subsequence, in this paper we always assume
that

N=2,\quad q=p+\theta_{p},\quad\theta_{p}\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{p}\to\theta\quad\text{as}~p\to+\infty.

(1.3)

Under the following natural condition

\limsup_{p\to+\infty}p\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla v_{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq\beta,

(1.4)

where
\beta
is a positive constant,
we will give a complete description of the limiting profile of positive solutions
(u_{p},v_{p})
as
p\to+\infty
.
The condition (
1.4
) comes naturally from the bound state solution of (
1.1
),
i.e., solutions with finite energy.
In particular, we will prove in Section
4.2
that the least energy solutions of (
1.1
) satisfy the condition (
1.4
) with
\beta=8\pi e
.

Our another motivation comes from the study of asymptotic behaviors of positive solutions to the single Lane-Emden equation in dimension two.
In precise, let
p=q
in (
1.1
), we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u_{p}-v_{p})|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v_{p}^{p}-u_{p}^{p})(u_{p}-v_{p})\leq 0,

which means
u_{p}=v_{p}
. So system (
1.1
) with
N=2
turns to be the single Lane-Emden equation

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=u^{p},\quad u>0,\quad\text{in}~\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\\
u=0,\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.5)

and the condition (
1.4
) turns to be

\limsup_{p\to\infty}p\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{p}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\beta.

(1.6)

The asymptotic behavior of least energy solutions of (
1.5
) as
p\to\infty
has been completely studied in
[
28
,
29
,
1
]
, where it was proved that
least energy solutions
u_{p}
of (
1.5
) satisfy the condition (
1.6
) with
\beta=8\pi e
.
Recently, for high-energy positive solutions of (
1.5
), i.e., under the condition (
1.6
) with
\beta>8\pi e
,
the asymptotic behavior has been obtained in a series of papers
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
13
]
.
Therefore, our study of the Lane-Emden system (
1.1
) in this paper can be seen as a generalization of these works to the system case.

To state our result, we need to introduce the following Liouville system which will appear as a limiting system of (
1.1
)

\begin{cases}-\Delta U=e^{V},\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\\
-\Delta V=e^{U},\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\\
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{U}\mathrm{d}x,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{V}\mathrm{d}x<+\infty.\end{cases}

(1.7)

It was proved in
[
6
]
that for any solution of (
1.7
), there must be

U(x)=V(x)=\log\frac{8\lambda^{2}}{(1+\lambda^{2}|x-y|^{2})^{2}}

for some
\lambda>0
and
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Denote
B_{r}(x)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,|\,|y-x|<r\}
.

Here is our main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(u_{p},v_{p})
be a family of positive solutions to (
1.1
) satisfying (
1.3
)-(
1.4
).
Then there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and a finite set of concentration points
{\cal S}=\left\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\right\}\subset\Omega
such that
up to a subsequence,
(u_{p},v_{p})
satisfies the following properties:
for small
r>0
and
i=1,\cdots,k
, set

u_{p}(x_{i,p})=\max_{B_{2r}(x_{i})}u_{p}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{p}(y_{i,p})=\max_{B_{2r}(x_{i})}v_{p},

then

(1)

u_{p}(x_{i,p})\to\sqrt{e}
,
v_{p}(y_{i,p})\to\sqrt{e}
as
p\to+\infty
. In particular,
\|u_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\to\sqrt{e}
and
\|v_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\to\sqrt{e}
.

(2)

x_{i,p}\to x_{i}
,
y_{i,p}\to x_{i}
and
\frac{x_{i,p}-y_{i,p}}{\mu_{i,p}}\to 0
as
p\to+\infty
, where

\mu_{i,p}^{-2}=pu_{p}^{p-1}(x_{i,p})\to+\infty.

(3)

as
p\to+\infty
,

pu_{p}(x),pv_{p}(x)\to 8\pi\sqrt{e}\sum_{i=1}^{k}G(x,x_{i})\quad\text{in}~{\cal C}_{loc}^{2}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus{\cal S}),

where
G(x,y)
is the Green function of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
under the Dirichlet boundary condition.

(4)

the concentration points
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
satisfy

\nabla R(x_{i})-2\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{k}\nabla G(x_{i},x_{j})=0,\quad\text{for every $i=1,\cdots,k$},

where
R(x)=H(x,x)
is the Robin function and

H(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x-y|-G(x,y)

is the regular part of
G(x,y)
.

(5)

let

\begin{cases}w_{i,p}(x)=\frac{p}{u_{p}(x_{i,p})}(u_{p}(x_{i,p}+\mu_{i,p}x)-u_{p}(x_{i,p})),\\
z_{i,p}(x)=\frac{p}{u_{p}(x_{i,p})}(v_{p}(x_{i,p}+\mu_{i,p}x)-u_{p}(x_{i,p})),\end{cases}

then
(w_{i,p},z_{i,p})\to(U_{\theta},V_{\theta})
in
{\cal C}_{loc}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
where
(U_{\theta}+\frac{\theta}{2},V_{\theta})
is a solution of the Liouville system (
1.7
) and

U_{\theta}(x)=\log\frac{1}{(1+\frac{1}{8}e^{\frac{\theta}{2}}|x|^{2})^{2}},\quad V_{\theta}(x)=\log\frac{e^{\frac{\theta}{2}}}{(1+\frac{1}{8}e^{\frac{\theta}{2}}|x|^{2})^{2}},

with
\theta
defined in (
1.3
).

(6)

p\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla v_{p}\to k8\pi e
,
p\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{p}|^{2}\to k8\pi e
and
p\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{p}|^{2}\to k8\pi e
as
p\to+\infty
.

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

Our result is different from
[
22
,
7
]
on two aspects. The first one is that comparing to the case
N\geq 3
, the limiting behavior for
N=2
is quite different, and it turns out that
\|u_{p}\|_{\infty},\|v_{p}\|_{\infty}\to\sqrt{e}
and a suitable scaling of
(u_{p},v_{p})
converges to a solution of the Liouville system (
1.7
). The second one is that in contrast with
[
22
,
7
]
where they only studied least energy solutions, here we also study high-energy positive solutions.

(2)

Noting that in Theorem
1.1
-(5), we scale
(u_{p},v_{p})
at the local maximum points
x_{i,p}
of
u_{p}
.
If we scale
(u_{p},v_{p})
at the local maximum points
y_{i,p}
of
v_{p}
, there is also a similar
convergence conclusion, see Remark
4.1
. From condition (
1.4
) and Theorem
1.1
-(6), we see that

k\leq\left[\frac{\beta}{8\pi e}\right].

Since we will prove in Section
4.2
that the least energy solution
(u_{p},v_{p})
of (
1.1
) satisfies

\limsup_{p\to\infty}p\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla v_{p}\leq 8\pi e,

we immediately obtain

Corollary 1.2
.

The least energy solution
(u_{p},v_{p})
of (
1.1
) must satisfy

p\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla v_{p}\to 8\pi e,\quad\text{as}~p\to\infty,

and must behave as one-point concentration, i.e.,
k=1
in Theorem
1.1
.
Moreover, the concentration point
x_{1}
is a critical point of the Robin function
R(x)
.

In contrast with the single Lane-Emden equation (
1.5
), since we deal with a system, we face additional difficulties.
The first one is how to construct the concentration set
{\cal S}
.
For the single equation, to construct the concentration set, the most used idea is induction.
In precise, one can construct the concentration set following three steps (Let us take the single Lane-Emden equation
-\Delta u_{p}=u_{p}^{p}
with
p\to\infty
for example):

Step 1.

One considers the global maximum point
x_{1,p}
of
u_{p}

and proves that a suitable scaling of
u_{p}
at
x_{1,p}

converges to a solution
U
of the limiting equation
-\Delta U=e^{U}
and
x_{1,p}\to x_{1}
, i.e.
x_{1}
is a concentration point;

Step 2.

Assume there exist
k
families of points
{x_{i,p}}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, satisfying
\mu_{i,p}^{-2}=pu_{p}(x_{i,p})^{p-1}\to\infty
and

({\cal P}_{k}^{1})

\lim_{p\to\infty}|x_{i,p}-x_{j,p}|/\mu_{j,p}=\infty
for any
i\neq j
;

({\cal P}_{k}^{2})

the scaling
p(u_{p}(x_{i,p}+\mu_{i,p}x)-u_{p}(x_{i,p}))/u_{p}(x_{i,p})
converges to a solution
U
of
-\Delta U=e^{U}
and
x_{i,p}\to x_{i}
, i.e.
{\cal S}_{k}=\left\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\right\}
is a set of concentration points;

then one can prove that either the following
({\cal P}_{k}^{3})
holds or there exists another family of points
x_{k+1,p}
satisfying
\mu_{k+1,p}^{-2}=pu_{p}(x_{k+1,p})^{p-1}\to\infty
,
x_{k+1,p}\to x_{k+1}
such that
({\cal P}_{k+1}^{1})
-
({\cal P}_{k+1}^{2})
hold
for
{\cal S}_{k+1}=\left\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1}\right\}
; where

({\cal P}_{k}^{3})

there exists
C>0
such that

\min_{1\leq i\leq k}|x-x_{i,p}|^{2}pu_{p}(x)^{p-1}\leq C,\quad\forall x\in\Omega.

Step 3.

By Steps 1-2, one can use the induction method. By the energy condition (
1.6
),
one shall prove that the induction must stop at finite steps, i.e., there exists a set
{\cal S}_{m}=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}\}
such that

({\cal P}_{m}^{1})
-
({\cal P}_{m}^{3})
hold, but there is no other family of points
x_{m+1,p}

such that
({\cal P}_{m+1}^{1})
-
({\cal P}_{m+1}^{2})
hold. Finally, one can prove that
{\cal S}_{m}
is the desired concentration set
by
({\cal P}_{m}^{1})
-
({\cal P}_{m}^{3})
.

In early years, this idea was used to construct the bubble form decomposition of Palais-Smale sequence by Lions
[
24
,
25
]

and by Struwe
[
30
]
for high dimensions, which is the basis to study multi-bubble blow up behaviors of Lane-Emden equations
or Brezis-Nirenberg problems, see e.g.
[
2
,
8
]
.
After that, this idea was used to study multi-bubble blow up behaviors for many equations, such as
for two dimensional critical equations
[
17
,
18
]
,
for two dimensional Lane-Emden equations
[
14
,
15
]
,
for Lin-Ni’s problem
[
19
]
, etc.
However, for the Lane-Emden system (
1.1
), it seems too difficult to find the proper induction conditions
({\cal P}_{k}^{1})
,
({\cal P}_{k}^{2})
and
({\cal P}_{k}^{3})
.
So we don’t follow this idea to construct the concentration set
{\cal S}
.
Instead, we will use PDE methods and the measure theory to prove that except for finite many points,
u_{p},v_{p}
converge to
0
as
p\to\infty
.
Then we can conclude that the concentration set must be finite.
This is inspired by Wei and Ren’s idea in
[
28
,
29
]
, where they study the asymptotic behavior of least energy solution to Lane-Emden equation. It seems even new to construct the concentration set for single Lane-Emden equations.

Another difficulty is to compare two local maximums
\max_{B_{2r}(x_{i})}u_{p}
and
\max_{B_{2r}(x_{i})}v_{p}
.
For
x_{i}\in{\cal S}
, set

u_{p}(x_{i,p})=\max_{B_{2r}(x_{i})}u_{p}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{p}(y_{i,p})=\max_{B_{2r}(x_{i})}v_{p}.

At first, we do not know whether to scale
(u_{p},v_{p})
at
x_{i,p}
or
y_{i,p}
, since we have no information about
the values of
\|u_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x_{i}))}
and
\|v_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x_{i}))}
.
Moreover, noting that
q\geq p
, the roles of
u_{p}
and
v_{p}
in concentration are unequal,
which means that we can not directly assume
\|u_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x_{i}))}\geq\|v_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x_{i}))}

or
\|u_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x_{i}))}\leq\|v_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x_{i}))}
.
Fortunately, using the classification result of the Liouville system (
1.7
), we can prove that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{p\to+\infty}p(\|v_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x_{i}))}-\|u_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x_{i}))})

(1.8)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{p\to+\infty}p(\|v_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x_{i}))}-\|u_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x_{i}))})<+\infty,

which promises the convergence in Theorem
1.1
-(5); see Lemma
4.1
.
We point out that our idea to handle the Lane-Emden system for
N=2
is different from
[
22
,
7
]
for
N\geq 3
.
In
[
22
,
7
]
, since
p
is fixed and
q_{\varepsilon}\to q
in their assumption (
1.2
), the Lane-Emden system is equivalent to a single equation

\begin{cases}-\Delta(-\Delta u_{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{p}}=u_{\varepsilon}^{q_{\varepsilon}},\quad u_{\varepsilon}>0,\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u_{\varepsilon}=\Delta u_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Thus they can consider the scaling of
u_{\varepsilon}
, and then
v_{\varepsilon}
is given by
v_{\varepsilon}=(-\Delta u_{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{p}}
.
Apparently, their idea is not suitable for our case
N=2
due to
p,q\to+\infty
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we give some preliminaries for the blow up analysis.
In Section 3, we prove the existence of a finite concentration set.
In Section 4, we use blow up techniques to prove Theorem
1.1
.
Throughout the paper, we denote by
C,C_{0},C_{1},\cdots
to be positive constants independent of
p
but may be different in different places.

2
Preliminaries

First, we recall the Green function
G(x,y)

of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
with the Dirichlet boundary condition:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{x}G(x,y)=\delta_{y}\quad&\text{in}~\Omega,\\
&G(x,y)=0\quad&\text{on}~\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

where
\delta_{y}
is the Dirac function. It has the following form

G(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x-y|-H(x,y),\quad(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega,

where the function
H(x,y)
is the regular part of
G(x,y)
.
It is well known that
H
is a smooth function in
\Omega\times\Omega
, both

G
and
H
are symmetric in
x
and
y
, and there is some constant
C>0
such that

|G(x,y)|\leq C|\log|x-y||,\qquad\forall\,x,y\in\Omega.

(2.2)

Let
R(x)=H(x,x)

be the Robin function of
\Omega
.

For later usage, we need the following Pohozaev identities.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any solution
(u,v)
of (
1.1
),
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
and
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p+1}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}v^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2}{q+1}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}u^{q+1}\mathrm{d}x

(2.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nu\right\rangle\left\langle\nabla v,x-y\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla v,\nu\right\rangle\left\langle\nabla u,x-y\right\rangle\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle\left\langle x-y,\nu\right\rangle\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x}+\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}\left\langle x-y,\nu\right\rangle\left(\frac{v^{p+1}}{p+1}+\frac{u^{q+1}}{q+1}\right)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x},

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nu\right\rangle\partial_{i}v+\left\langle\nabla v,\nu\right\rangle\partial_{i}u\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x}+\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle\nu_{i}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x}=\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}\left(\frac{v^{p+1}}{p+1}+\frac{u^{q+1}}{q+1}\right)\nu_{i}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x},

(2.4)

where
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
is the outer normal vector of
\partial\Omega^{\prime}
, and
i=1,2
.

Proof.

By direct computations, we have

\displaystyle-\Delta u(x-y)\cdot\nabla v-\Delta v(x-y)\cdot\nabla u

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{div}\left(\nabla u(x-y)\cdot\nabla v+\nabla v(x-y)\cdot\nabla u\right)+\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left((x-y)\cdot\nabla v\right)+\nabla v\cdot\nabla\left((x-y)\cdot\nabla u\right)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{div}\left(\nabla u(x-y)\cdot\nabla v+\nabla v(x-y)\cdot\nabla u-(x-y)\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\right).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle v^{p}(x-y)\cdot\nabla v+u^{q}(x-y)\cdot\nabla u=\operatorname{div}\left((x-y)\Big(\frac{v^{p+1}}{p+1}+\frac{u^{q+1}}{q+1}\Big)\right)-2\left(\frac{v^{p+1}}{p+1}+\frac{u^{q+1}}{q+1}\right).

Then using (
1.1
) and the divergence theorem, we obtain (
2.3
).

Similarly, we have

-\Delta u\partial_{i}v-\Delta v\partial_{i}u=-\operatorname{div}(\nabla u\partial_{i}v+\nabla v\partial_{i}u)+\partial_{i}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v),

and

v^{p}\partial_{i}v+u^{q}\partial_{i}u=\partial_{i}\left(\frac{v^{p+1}}{p+1}+\frac{u^{q+1}}{q+1}\right).

Then using (
1.1
) and the divergence theorem, we obtain (
2.4
).
∎

Since
(u_{p},v_{p})
satisfies (
1.4
), we have

p\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q+1}=p\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p+1}=p\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla v_{p}\leq\beta+o_{p}(1),

(2.5)

where
o_{p}(1)
denotes those quantities satisfying
\lim_{p\to\infty}o_{p}(1)=0
.
Then by Hölder inequality,

\limsup_{p\to\infty}p\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\leq\limsup_{p\to\infty}\left(p\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p+1}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}(p|\Omega|)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}\leq\beta,

(2.6)

\limsup_{p\to\infty}p\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q}\leq\limsup_{p\to\infty}\left(p\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q+1}\right)^{\frac{q}{q+1}}(p|\Omega|)^{\frac{1}{q+1}}\leq\beta.

(2.7)

Now we prove that
u_{p},v_{p}
are uniformly bounded as
p\to+\infty
.
For simplicity, we denote
\|u\|_{\infty}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

1\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\|u_{p}\|_{\infty}\leq\limsup_{p\to\infty}\|u_{p}\|_{\infty}\leq C,

(2.8)

1\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\|v_{p}\|_{\infty}\leq\limsup_{p\to\infty}\|v_{p}\|_{\infty}\leq C.

(2.9)

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{1}>0
,
\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\infty}=1
, be the eigenfunction with respect
to the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
with the Dirichlet boundary condition.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(v_{p}^{p-1}-\lambda_{1}(\Omega))v_{p}\varphi_{1}+\int_{\Omega}(u_{p}^{q-1}-\lambda_{1}(\Omega))u_{p}\varphi_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta u_{p}\varphi_{1}+\Delta\varphi_{1}v_{p})+(-\Delta v_{p}\varphi_{1}+\Delta\varphi_{1}u_{p})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{1}-\nabla v_{p}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{1})+(\nabla v_{p}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{1}-\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{1})

\displaystyle=0.

So one of
\int_{\Omega}(v_{p}^{p-1}-\lambda_{1}(\Omega))v_{p}\varphi_{1}
and
\int_{\Omega}(u_{p}^{q-1}-\lambda_{1}(\Omega))u_{p}\varphi_{1}

is nonnegative. Without loss of generality, we assume
\int_{\Omega}(v_{p}^{p-1}-\lambda_{1}(\Omega))v_{p}\varphi_{1}\geq 0
,
which implies
\|v_{p}\|_{\infty}\geq\lambda_{1}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
.
Meanwhile, by the Green representation formula,

\displaystyle v_{p}(x)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)u_{p}(y)^{q}dy\leq\|u_{p}\|_{\infty}^{q}\int_{\Omega}|G(x,y)|dy\leq C\|u_{p}\|_{\infty}^{q},

where
C
is independent of
x\in\Omega
. Thus

\|u_{p}\|_{\infty}\geq(C^{-1}\|v_{p}\|_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C^{\frac{-1}{q}}\lambda_{1}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{q(p-1)}}.

As a result, we obtain
\liminf_{p\to\infty}\|u_{p}\|_{\infty}\geq 1
and
\liminf_{p\to\infty}\|v_{p}\|_{\infty}\geq 1
.

To prove the second part, we recall
[
21
, Proposition 2.7]
that there is
C>0
independent of
x\in\Omega
such that

\|G(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{p+1}(\Omega)}^{p+1}\leq C(p+1)^{p+2},\quad\text{for $p$ large enough}.

Then
by the Green representation formula, for any
x\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle u_{p}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)v_{p}(y)^{p}\mathrm{d}y\leq\|G(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{p+1}(\Omega)}\|v_{p}\|_{L^{p+1}(\Omega)}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C(p+1)^{\frac{p+2}{p+1}}\left(\frac{C}{p}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}\leq C,\quad\text{for $p$ large enough},

so
\limsup_{p\to\infty}\|u_{p}\|_{\infty}\leq C
.
Similarly, we can prove
\limsup_{p\to\infty}\|v_{p}\|_{\infty}\leq C
.
∎

3
Existence of the concentration set

3.1
The first concentration point

We introduce some notations. Let
x_{p},y_{p}\in\Omega
be such that
u_{p}(x_{p})=\|u_{p}\|_{\infty}
and
v_{p}(y_{p})=\|v_{p}\|_{\infty}
.
Take a subsequence if necessary, we assume

x_{p}\to x_{0},\quad y_{p}\to y_{0},\quad\text{as}~p\to\infty,

(3.1)

and

u_{p}(x_{p})\to l,\quad v_{p}(y_{p})\to\tilde{l},\quad\text{as}~p\to\infty.

(3.2)

Then
x_{0},y_{0}\in\bar{\Omega}
and
l,\tilde{l}\geq 1
. Let

\mu_{p}^{-2}=pu_{p}^{p-1}(x_{p}),

(3.3)

by Lemma
2.2
, we know that
\mu_{p}\to 0
.

First, we prove that the maximum points
x_{p},y_{p}
are uniformly away from the boundary of
\Omega
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
\delta_{0}>0
independent of
p
such that for
p
large,

dist(x_{p},\partial\Omega),~dist(y_{p},\partial\Omega)\geq\delta_{0}.

Proof.

We set

\bar{u}_{p}=\frac{u_{p}}{\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}},\quad\bar{v}_{p}=\frac{v_{p}}{\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q}},

(3.4)

and

f_{p}=\frac{v_{p}^{p}}{\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}},\quad g_{p}=\frac{u_{p}^{q}}{\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q}}.

(3.5)

Then
\|f_{p}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=\|g_{p}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=1
and

\begin{cases}-\Delta\bar{u}_{p}=f_{p},\quad\bar{u}_{p}>0,\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\\
-\Delta\bar{v}_{p}=g_{p},\quad\bar{v}_{p}>0,\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\\
\bar{u}_{p}=\bar{v}_{p}=0,\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(3.6)

Since
\|f_{p}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=\|g_{p}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=1
, it follows from the elliptic
L^{p}
estimate with the duality argument
(cf.
[
5
]
) that
\bar{u}_{p},\bar{v}_{p}
are uniformly bounded in
W^{1,s}(\Omega)

for any
1\leq s<2
. In particular,

\|\bar{u}_{p}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)},\|\bar{v}_{p}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C_{s},\quad 1\leq s<2.

(3.7)

If
\Omega
is a convex domain, by the moving plane method,
it was proved in
[
27
, Section 31.1, (31.19)]
that there exists a constant
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

\left\langle\nabla\bar{u}_{p}(x),\nu(y)\right\rangle\leq 0,~~\left\langle\nabla\bar{v}_{p}(x),\nu(y)\right\rangle\leq 0,\quad\text{for}\quad y\in\partial\Omega,~x\in\Sigma(y,\lambda_{0}),

where
\Sigma(y,\lambda):=\left\{x\in\Omega:~\left\langle y-x,\nu(y)\right\rangle\leq\lambda\right\}
and
\nu(y)
denotes the outer normal vector of
\partial\Omega
at
y
.
Then by
[
27
, Lemma 13.2]
, there exist
\delta_{0},C>0
depending only on
\Omega
and
\lambda_{0}
such that

\|\bar{u}_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\leq C\int_{\Omega}\bar{u}_{p}\varphi_{1}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|\bar{u}_{p}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C,

\|\bar{v}_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\leq C\int_{\Omega}\bar{v}_{p}\varphi_{1}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|\bar{v}_{p}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C,

where
\varphi_{1}
is given in Lemma
2.2
and

\omega=\left\{x\in\Omega:~dist(x,\partial\Omega)\leq\delta_{0}\right\}.

While for
\Omega
being not convex, as pointed out in
[
27
, Remark 31.5(ii)]
, by using the Kelvin transform we can also obtain

\|\bar{u}_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)},~\|\bar{v}_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\leq C.

(3.8)

As a result, we see from (
2.6
)-(
2.7
) that

\|u_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\leq C\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{C}{p},\quad\|v_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\leq C\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q}\leq\frac{C}{p}.

(3.9)

This, together with
\liminf_{p\to\infty}u_{p}(x_{p}),\liminf_{p\to\infty}v_{p}(y_{p})\geq 1
, implies
x_{p},y_{p}\not\in\omega
for
p
large.
∎

Define the scaling of
u_{p},v_{p}
at
x_{p}
by

\begin{aligned} w_{p}(x)&=\frac{p}{u_{p}(x_{p})}\left(u_{p}(x_{p}+\mu_{p}x)-u_{p}(x_{p})\right),\\
z_{p}(x)&=\frac{p}{u_{p}(x_{p})}\left(v_{p}(x_{p}+\mu_{p}x)-u_{p}(x_{p})\right),\end{aligned}\quad\quad x\in\Omega_{p}:=\frac{\Omega-x_{p}}{\mu_{p}}.

(3.10)

Lemma
3.1
implies
x_{0},y_{0}\in\Omega
and so
\Omega_{p}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
as
p\to\infty
.
It is easy to check that

1+\frac{w_{p}(x)}{p}=\frac{u_{p}(x_{p}+\mu_{p}x)}{u_{p}(x_{p})},\quad 1+\frac{z_{p}(x)}{p}=\frac{v_{p}(x_{p}+\mu_{p}x)}{u_{p}(x_{p})},

and (note
\mu_{p}^{-2}=pu_{p}^{p-1}(x_{p})
and
q=p+\theta_{p}
)

\begin{cases}-\Delta w_{p}=(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p})^{p},\quad\text{in}~\Omega_{p},\\
-\Delta z_{p}=u_{p}(x_{p})^{\theta_{p}}(1+\frac{w_{p}}{p})^{q},\quad\text{in}~\Omega_{p},\\
w_{p}=z_{p}=-p,\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega_{p}.\end{cases}

(3.11)

Moreover,

w_{p}(0)=0=\max_{\Omega_{p}}w_{p}.

As pointed out in Section 1, one difficulty is the lack of the relationship between the values
u_{p}(x_{p})=\|u_{p}\|_{\infty}
and
v_{p}(y_{p})=\|v_{p}\|_{\infty}
at the moment.
To settle it, we give the following important lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
\limsup_{p\to\infty}p(v_{p}(y_{p})-u_{p}(x_{p}))\leq C
for some
C\geq 0
. Then up to a subsequence of
p\to\infty
,

w_{p}\to\bar{U}_{\theta},\quad z_{p}\to\bar{V}_{\theta},\quad\text{in}~{\cal C}_{loc}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),

where
l=\lim_{p\to\infty}u_{p}(x_{p})
,
(\bar{U}_{\theta}+\theta\log l,\bar{V}_{\theta})
is a solution of (
1.7
) and

\bar{U}_{\theta}(x)=\log\frac{1}{(1+\frac{1}{8}l^{\theta}|x|^{2})^{2}},\quad\bar{V}_{\theta}(x)=\log\frac{l^{\theta}}{(1+\frac{1}{8}l^{\theta}|x|^{2})^{2}}.

(3.12)

Proof.

For any
R>0
, we have
B_{R}(0)\subset\Omega_{p}
for
p
large.
Take
w_{p}=w_{p,1}+w_{p,2}
in
B_{R}(0)
with

\begin{cases}-\Delta w_{p,1}=(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p})^{p},\quad\text{in}~B_{R}(0),\\
w_{p,1}=0,\quad\text{on}~\partial B_{R}(0),\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}-\Delta w_{p,2}=0,\quad\text{in}~B_{R}(0),\\
w_{p,1}=w_{p},\quad\text{on}~\partial B_{R}(0).\end{cases}

Since
\limsup_{p\to\infty}p(v_{p}(y_{p})-u_{p}(x_{p}))\leq C
and
\liminf_{p\to\infty}u_{p}(x_{p})\geq 1
imply
z_{p}(x)\leq C
, we obtain

0\leq\Big(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\Big)^{p}\leq\Big(1+\frac{C}{p}\Big)^{p}\leq C,

which means
0\leq-\Delta w_{p,1}\leq C
.
Thus
\|w_{p,1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\leq C
and
w_{p,2}=w_{p}-w_{p,1}\leq C
.
By Harnack inequality, we see that if
\inf_{B_{R}(0)}w_{p,2}\to-\infty
, then
\sup_{B_{R}(0)}w_{p,2}\to-\infty
as
p\to\infty
,
which contradicts to
w_{p,2}(0)=w_{p}(0)-w_{p,1}(0)\geq-C
.
So we have
\|w_{p,2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\leq C
, and hence
\|w_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\leq C
.
Then by elliptic estimates (see
[
20
, Theorems 3.9 and 8.8]
), we have

\|\nabla w_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\leq C,\quad\text{and}\quad\|w_{p}\|_{W^{2,2}(B_{R}(0))}\leq C.

Up to a subsequence, we can assume
w_{p}\to w
in
{\cal C}(B_{R}(0))
and
w_{p}\rightharpoonup w
in
W^{2,2}(B_{R}(0))
.

Similarly, we take
z_{p}=z_{p,1}+z_{p,2}
with
z_{p,1}=0
on the boundary
\partial B_{R}(0)
and
z_{p,2}
is harmonic in
B_{R}(0)
.
Since
u_{p}(x_{p})\to l
and
\theta_{p}\to\theta
, we have
0\leq-\Delta z_{p,1}=u_{p}(x_{p})^{\theta_{p}}(1+\frac{w_{p}}{p})^{q}\leq C
, which yields
\|z_{p,1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\leq C
.
From here and
z_{p}\leq C
, we obtain
z_{p,2}=z_{p}-z_{p,1}\leq C
. We will prove by contradiction that
z_{p,2}\geq-C
.
Suppose
\inf_{B_{R}(0)}z_{p,2}\to-\infty
as
p\to\infty
, then
\sup_{B_{R}(0)}z_{p,2}\to-\infty
by Harnack inequality.
This implies
\sup_{B_{R}(0)}z_{p}\leq\sup_{B_{R}(0)}z_{p,1}+\sup_{B_{R}(0)}z_{p,2}\to-\infty
,
so
\sup_{B_{R}(0)}(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p})^{p}\to 0
, i.e.,
\|\Delta w_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\to 0
as
p\to\infty
.
Thus in the distribution sense,
-\Delta w=0
in
B_{R}(0)
.
By choosing
R=R_{n}\to\infty
, repeating the above process, we can obtain that up to a subsequence,

w_{p}\to w
in
{\cal C}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
w_{p}\rightharpoonup w
in
W^{2,2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, where

-\Delta w=0\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad w\leq w(0)=0.

By Liouville’s Theorem,
w\equiv 0
. However, by Fatou’s Lemma,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{w}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{p}}\Big(1+\frac{w_{p}}{p}\Big)^{p}\mathrm{d}x=\liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{p}{u_{p}(x_{p})}\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{p\to\infty}\Big(p\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q+1}\Big)^{\frac{p}{q+1}}(p|\Omega|)^{\frac{\theta_{p}+1}{q+1}}\leq C,

(3.13)

which contradicts to
w=0
.
So we prove that
\inf_{B_{R}(0)}z_{p,2}\geq-C
and then
\|z_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\leq C
.
Then by elliptic estimates, we have
\|\nabla z_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\leq C
and
\|z_{p}\|_{W^{2,2}(B_{R}(0))}\leq C
.

Since
R>0
is arbitrary, the above argument shows that
w_{p},z_{p}
are uniformly bounded in
W_{loc}^{2,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap{\cal C}_{loc}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Then by the Schauder estimates, we conclude that
w_{p},z_{p}
are uniformly bounded in
{\cal C}_{loc}^{2,\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, and so
up to a subsequence, we may assume

w_{p}\to w
and
z_{p}\to z
in
{\cal C}_{loc}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
with

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=e^{z},\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\\
-\Delta z=l^{\theta}e^{w},\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.\end{cases}

Similar to (
3.1
), we have
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{w}<\infty
and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{z}<\infty
.
Thus
(w+\theta\log l,z)
is a solution of (
1.7
), and hence

w(x)+\theta\log l=z(x)=\log\frac{8\lambda^{2}}{(1+\lambda^{2}|x-y|^{2})^{2}}

for some
\lambda>0
and
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Finally, by
w(0)=0=\max_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}w
, we obtain
y=0
and
\lambda^{2}=\frac{1}{8}l^{\theta}
, so
w=\bar{U}_{\theta}
and
z=\bar{V}_{\theta}
. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{\mu}_{p}^{-2}=pv_{p}^{p-1}(y_{p})\to\infty
and
define the scaling of
u_{p},v_{p}
at
y_{p}
by

\begin{aligned} \tilde{w}_{p}(x)&=\frac{p}{v_{p}(y_{p})}\left(u_{p}(y_{p}+\tilde{\mu}_{p}x)-v_{p}(y_{p})\right),\\
\tilde{z}_{p}(x)&=\frac{p}{v_{p}(y_{p})}\left(v_{p}(y_{p}+\tilde{\mu}_{p}x)-v_{p}(y_{p})\right),\end{aligned}\quad\quad x\in\tilde{\Omega}_{p}:=\frac{\Omega-y_{p}}{\tilde{\mu}_{p}}.

(3.14)

By Lemma
3.1
, we see
\tilde{\Omega}_{p}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
as
p\to\infty
.
It is easy to check that

1+\frac{\tilde{w}_{p}(x)}{p}=\frac{u_{p}(y_{p}+\tilde{\mu}_{p}x)}{v_{p}(y_{p})},\quad 1+\frac{\tilde{z}_{p}(x)}{p}=\frac{v_{p}(y_{p}+\tilde{\mu}_{p}x)}{v_{p}(y_{p})},

and

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{w}_{p}=(1+\frac{\tilde{z}_{p}}{p})^{p},\quad\text{in}~\tilde{\Omega}_{p},\\
-\Delta\tilde{z}_{p}=v_{p}(y_{p})^{\theta_{p}}(1+\frac{\tilde{w}_{p}}{p})^{q},\quad\text{in}~\tilde{\Omega}_{p},\\
\tilde{w}_{p}=\tilde{z}_{p}=-p,\quad\text{on}~\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{p}.\end{cases}

(3.15)

Moreover,
\tilde{z}_{p}(0)=0=\max_{\tilde{\Omega}_{p}}\tilde{z}_{p}
. Suppose
\limsup_{p\to\infty}p(u_{p}(x_{p})-v_{p}(y_{p}))\leq C
for some
C\geq 0
. Then
\tilde{w}_{p}\leq C
in
\tilde{\Omega}_{p}
. Consequently,
we can follow the proof of Lemma
3.2
and obtain that up to a subsequence,

\tilde{w}_{p}\to\tilde{U}_{\theta},\quad\tilde{z}_{p}\to\tilde{V}_{\theta},\quad\text{in}~{\cal C}_{loc}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),\quad\text{as}~p\to\infty,

where
\tilde{l}:=\lim_{p\to\infty}v_{p}(y_{p})
,
(\tilde{U}_{\theta}+\theta\log\tilde{l},\tilde{V}_{\theta})
is a solution of (
1.7
) and

\tilde{U}_{\theta}(x)=\log\frac{\tilde{l}^{-\theta}}{(1+\frac{1}{8}|x|^{2})^{2}},\quad\tilde{V}_{\theta}(x)=\log\frac{1}{(1+\frac{1}{8}|x|^{2})^{2}}.

(3.16)

Now we compare the maximum values of
u_{p},v_{p}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

0\leq\liminf_{p\to+\infty}p(v_{p}(y_{p})-u_{p}(x_{p}))\leq\limsup_{p\to+\infty}p(v_{p}(y_{p})-u_{p}(x_{p}))\leq C.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
p(v_{p}(y_{p})-u_{p}(x_{p}))\to d<0
up to a subsequence, where
d
might be
-\infty
.
Applying Lemma
3.2
, we see that
w_{p}\to\bar{U}_{\theta}
and
z_{p}\to\bar{V}_{\theta}
in
{\cal C}^{2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
as
p\to\infty
.
Then

\max z_{p}\geq z_{p}(0)\to\bar{V}_{\theta}(0)=\theta\log l\geq 0.

However,

\max z_{p}=\frac{p(v_{p}(y_{p})-u_{p}(x_{p}))}{u_{p}(x_{p})}\to\frac{d}{l}<0,

which is a contradiction. This proves
\liminf_{p\to+\infty}p(v_{p}(y_{p})-u_{p}(x_{p}))\geq 0
.

Similarly, to prove
\limsup_{p\to+\infty}p(v_{p}(y_{p})-u_{p}(x_{p}))\leq C
, we assume by contradiction that
p(v_{p}(y_{p})-u_{p}(x_{p}))\to+\infty
up to a subsequence. Then
we can apply
Remark
3.1
, which asserts that

\tilde{w}_{p}\to\tilde{U}_{\theta}
and
\tilde{z}_{p}\to\tilde{V}_{\theta}
in
{\cal C}^{2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
up to a subsequence.
Consequently,

-\infty\leftarrow\frac{p(u_{p}(x_{p})-v_{p}(y_{p}))}{v_{p}(y_{p})}=\max\tilde{w}_{p}\geq\tilde{w}_{p}(0)\to\tilde{U}_{\theta}(0)=-\theta\log\tilde{l},

again a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Lemma
3.3
shows that the assumptions in Lemma
3.2
and Remark
3.1
hold automatically, so the conclusions of both Lemma
3.2
and Remark
3.1
always hold, and
l=\tilde{l}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

It holds

\frac{C_{1}}{p}\leq\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q},~\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{p},

for
p
large enough, where
C_{1},C_{2}>0
.

Proof.

From (
2.6
)-(
2.7
), we have

\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q},~\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{p}
for
p
large.
On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
3.2
that

\liminf_{p\to\infty}p\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q}=\liminf_{p\to\infty}u_{p}(x_{p})^{\theta_{p}+1}\int_{\Omega_{p}}\Big(1+\frac{w_{p}}{p}\Big)^{q}\geq l^{\theta+1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{\bar{U}_{\theta}}=8\pi l,

\liminf_{p\to\infty}p\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}=\liminf_{p\to\infty}u_{p}(x_{p})\int_{\Omega_{p}}\Big(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\Big)^{p}\geq l\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{\bar{V}_{\theta}}=8\pi l,

which means
\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q},~\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\geq\frac{C_{1}}{p}
for
p
large.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Define

L_{p}:=\max\left\{\frac{p\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q}}{e(\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p})^{1/p}},\frac{q\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}}{e(\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q})^{1/q}}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad L:=\limsup_{p\to\infty}L_{p}.

Then
\frac{8\pi l}{e}\leq L\leq\frac{\beta}{e}
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

3.2
Finiteness of the concentration points

Let
\bar{u}_{p},\bar{v}_{p}
and
f_{p},g_{p}
be defined as in the proof Lemma

Lemma 3.7
.

For any

(1)

if and only if for any

(2)

Proof.

We only prove (1), since (2) can be proved similarly.
First, take

, we want to prove that there exists
R_{0}>0
such that

\|\bar{v}_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{0}}(x_{*}))}\leq C
as

.
To this goal, we recall (

) in the proof of Lemma

that
\|\bar{v}_{p}\|_{L^{3/2}(\Omega)}\leq C
.
We claim that there exist small
R_{0}>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\|g_{p}\|_{L^{1+\delta_{0}}(B_{2R_{0}}(x_{*}))}\leq C,\quad\text{as}~p\to\infty.

(3.17)

Once (
3.17
) is proved, we can apply the weak Harnack inequality (
[
20
, Theorem 8.17]
) to obtain

\|\bar{v}_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{0}}(x_{*}))}\leq C\left(\|\bar{v}_{p}\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{2R_{0}}(x_{*}))}+\|g_{p}\|_{L^{1+\delta_{0}}(B_{2R_{0}}(x_{*}))}\right)\leq C.

Now we need to check the claim (
3.17
).
Let

\alpha_{p}(x)=\frac{u_{p}(x)}{(\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q})^{1/q}}=\frac{\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}}{(\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q})^{1/q}}\bar{u}_{p}(x).

Since
\frac{\log x}{x}\leq\frac{1}{e}
for any

, we obtain

\log\alpha_{p}(x)\leq\frac{1}{e}\alpha_{p}(x),\quad\forall x.

Therefore, for any
x\in\Omega

\displaystyle g_{p}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{p}(x)^{q}}{\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q}}=\exp\left(q\log\alpha_{p}(x)\right)\leq\exp\left(\frac{q}{e}\alpha_{p}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{q\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}}{e(\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q})^{1/q}}\bar{u}_{p}(x)\right)\leq\exp\left(L_{p}\bar{u}_{p}(x)\right),

where
L_{p}
is defined in Corollary
3.5
. Since
L=\limsup_{p\to\infty}L_{p}
and
\delta>0
, we have

g_{p}(x)\leq\exp\left((L+\tfrac{\delta}{2})\bar{u}_{p}(x)\right)\quad\text{for $p$ large}.

Since
x_{*}\not\in\Sigma_{\mu}(\delta)
, it follows from the definition of
\delta
-regular points that there exists
R_{1}>0
such that
B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*})\subset\Omega
and

\int_{B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*})}f_{p}<\frac{4\pi}{L+\delta}\quad\text{for $p$ large}.

Take
\bar{u}_{p}=\bar{u}_{p,1}+\bar{u}_{p,2}
with
\bar{u}_{p,1}=0
on the boundary
\partial B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*})

and
\bar{u}_{p,2}
is harmonic in
B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*})
.
By the maximum principle,
\bar{u}_{p,1}>0
and
\bar{u}_{p,2}>0
in
B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*})
.
By Lemma
3.6
, we have

\int_{B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*})}\exp\left(\frac{\gamma\bar{u}_{p,1}(x)}{\|f_{p}\|_{L^{1}(B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*}))}}\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{\gamma},\quad\text{for any $\gamma\in(0,4\pi)$}.

Note that
0<\bar{u}_{p,2}<\bar{u}_{p}
in

. Then by the mean value theorem for harmonic functions and (

), we obtain

\|\bar{u}_{p,2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{1}}(x_{*}))}\leq C\|\bar{u}_{p,2}\|_{L^{1}(B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*}))}\leq C\|\bar{u}_{p}\|_{L^{1}(B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*}))}\leq C\|\bar{u}_{p}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C.

Take
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\gamma:=4\pi(1+\delta_{0})\frac{L+\frac{\delta}{2}}{L+\delta}<4\pi
. Then by the above estimates, we conclude that for
p

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{*})}g_{p}(x)^{1+\delta_{0}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{*})}\exp\left((1+\delta_{0})(L+\tfrac{\delta}{2})\bar{u}_{p}(x)\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{*})}\exp\left((1+\delta_{0})(L+\tfrac{\delta}{2})\bar{u}_{p,1}(x)\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*})}\exp\left((1+\delta_{0})(L+\tfrac{\delta}{2})\bar{u}_{p,1}(x)\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*})}\exp\left(4\pi(1+\delta_{0})\frac{L+\frac{\delta}{2}}{L+\delta}\frac{\bar{u}_{p,1}(x)}{\|f_{p}\|_{L^{1}(B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*}))}}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=C\int_{B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*})}\exp\left(\frac{\gamma\bar{u}_{p,1}(x)}{\|f_{p}\|_{L^{1}(B_{2R_{1}}(x_{*}))}}\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{\gamma}.

Thus by choosing
R_{0}=R_{1}/2
, we finish the proof of the claim (
3.17
).

Finally, given any
x_{*}\in\Sigma_{\mu}(\delta)
, we claim that
for any
R>0
,
\|\bar{v}_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{*}))}\to+\infty
as
p\to\infty
.
If not, there exists
R_{1}>0
such that up to a subsequence,
\|\bar{v}_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{1}}(x_{*}))}\leq C
as
p\to\infty
.
Then by Lemma
3.4

v_{p}(x)=\bar{v}_{p}(x)\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q}\leq C\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{q}\leq\frac{C}{p}\quad\text{for}~x\in B_{R_{1}}(x_{*}),

and hence

\int_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{*})}f_{p}=\frac{\int_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{*})}v_{p}^{p}}{\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}}\leq Cp\int_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{*})}\left(\frac{C}{p}\right)^{p}\to 0\quad\text{as $p\to\infty$.}

Thus by the definition,
x_{*}\not\in\Sigma_{\mu}(\delta)
, a contradiction. This finishes the proof.

.

\Omega\setminus{\cal S}
, it follows from the standard elliptic estimates that

pu_{p}(x)\to\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}G(x,x_{i}),\quad pv_{p}(x)\to\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}G(x,x_{i}),\quad\text{in}~{\cal C}_{loc}^{2}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus{\cal S}),\quad\text{as}~p\to\infty.

It remains to prove
\gamma_{i}=\tilde{\gamma}_{i}=8\pi l_{i}
. For every
i=1,\cdots,k
, since

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to\infty}p\int_{B_{d}(x_{i})}v_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{p\to\infty}p\int_{B_{d/2}(x_{i,p})}v_{p}^{p}=\lim_{p\to\infty}u_{p}(x_{i,p})\int_{B_{\frac{d}{2\mu_{i,p}}}(0)}\Big(1+\frac{z_{i,p}}{p}\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle\geq l_{i}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{V_{i,\theta}}=8\pi l_{i},

and

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to\infty}p\int_{B_{d}(x_{i})}u_{p}^{q}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{p\to\infty}p\int_{B_{d/2}(x_{i,p})}u_{p}^{q}=\lim_{p\to\infty}u_{p}(x_{i,p})^{\theta_{p}+1}\int_{B_{\frac{d}{2\mu_{i,p}}}(0)}\Big(1+\frac{w_{i,p}}{p}\Big)^{q}

\displaystyle\geq l_{i}^{\theta+1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{U_{i,\theta}}=8\pi l_{i},

we get
\gamma_{i},\tilde{\gamma}_{i}\geq 8\pi l_{i}
.

Noting that for
h=1,\cdots,k
and
x\in B_{r}(x_{h})\setminus\{x_{h}\}
, we have

p\nabla u_{p}(x)\to\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\nabla_{x}G(x,x_{i})=-\frac{\gamma_{h}}{2\pi}\frac{x-x_{h}}{|x-x_{h}|^{2}}+O(1),

(4.8)

p\nabla v_{p}(x)\to\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}\nabla_{x}G(x,x_{i})=-\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{h}}{2\pi}\frac{x-x_{h}}{|x-x_{h}|^{2}}+O(1).

(4.9)

Applying the Pohozaev identity (
2.3
) with
y=x_{h}
,
\Omega^{\prime}=B_{d}(x_{h})
,
(u,v)=(u_{p},v_{p})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{2p^{2}}{p+1}\int_{B_{d}(x_{h})}v_{p}^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2p^{2}}{q+1}\int_{B_{d}(x_{h})}u_{p}^{q+1}\mathrm{d}x

(4.10)

\displaystyle=-d\int_{\partial B_{d}(x_{h})}\left\langle p\nabla u_{p},p\nabla v_{p}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x}+2d\int_{\partial B_{d}(x_{h})}\left\langle p\nabla u_{p},\nu\right\rangle\left\langle p\nabla v_{p},\nu\right\rangle\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle+d\int_{\partial B_{d}(x_{h})}\frac{p^{2}v_{p}^{p+1}}{p+1}+\frac{p^{2}u_{p}^{q+1}}{q+1}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x}.

Using (
4.8
)-(
4.9
) and (
4.7
), we obtain

\text{Right hand side of \eqref{tem-5}}=\frac{\gamma_{h}\tilde{\gamma}_{h}}{2\pi}+O(d)+o_{p}(1).

Meanwhile, since Lemma
4.1
shows
\max_{B_{d}(x_{h})}v_{p}\to l_{h}
and
\max_{B_{d}(x_{h})}u_{p}\to l_{h}
as
p\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Left hand side of \eqref{tem-5}}

\displaystyle\leq 2l_{h}\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(p\int_{B_{d}(x_{h})}(u_{p}^{q}+v_{p}^{p})\right)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=2l_{h}(\gamma_{h}+\tilde{\gamma}_{h})+o_{d}(1)+o_{p}(1).

So letting
p\to\infty
and
d\to 0
in (
4.10
), we get
\frac{\gamma_{h}\tilde{\gamma}_{h}}{2\pi}\leq 2l_{h}(\gamma_{h}+\tilde{\gamma}_{h})
or equivalently,

\frac{1}{\gamma_{h}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{\gamma}_{h}}\geq\frac{1}{4\pi l_{h}}.

From here and
\gamma_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h}\geq 8\pi l_{h}
, we conclude that
\gamma_{h}=\tilde{\gamma}_{h}=8\pi l_{h}
.
∎

To proceed, we give a decay estimate of
w_{i,p},z_{i,p}
, which will be used to apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
\gamma\in(0,4)
, there exist small
r_{\gamma}>0
, large
R_{\gamma}>0
,
p_{\gamma}>1
and
C_{\gamma}>0
such that

w_{i,p}(x),~z_{i,p}(x)\leq\gamma\log\frac{1}{|x|}+C_{\gamma},

(4.11)

for any
2R_{\gamma}\leq|x|\leq\frac{r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{i,p}}
,
p\geq p_{\gamma}
and
i=1,\cdots,k
.

Proof.

First we prove it for
w_{i,p}
.
For simiplicity, we omit the parameter
i
and denote

w_{p}=w_{i,p},~z_{p}=z_{i,p},~~\mu_{p}=\mu_{i,p},~x_{p}=x_{i,p},~\Omega_{p}=\Omega_{i,p}.

By the Green Representation formula we have for any
x\in\Omega_{p}
,

\displaystyle u_{p}(x_{p}+\mu_{p}x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}G(x_{p}+\mu_{p}x,y)v_{p}^{p}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{p}(x_{p})}{p}\int_{\Omega_{p}}G(x_{p}+\mu_{p}x,x_{p}+\mu_{p}z)\left(1+\frac{z_{p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z.

Then

w_{p}(x)=-p+\int_{\Omega_{p}}G(x_{p}+\mu_{p}x,x_{p}+\mu_{p}z)\left(1+\frac{z_{p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z.

Since
w_{p}(0)=0
and
G(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x-y|-H(x,y)
, we have

\displaystyle w_{p}(x)

\displaystyle=w_{p}(x)-w_{p}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{p}}\left[G(x_{p}+\mu_{p}x,x_{p}+\mu_{p}z)-G(x_{p},x_{p}+\mu_{p}z)\right]\left(1+\frac{z_{p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Omega_{p}}\log\frac{|z|}{|z-x|}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{p}}\left[H(x_{p}+\mu_{p}x,x_{p}+\mu_{p}z)-H(x_{p},x_{p}+\mu_{p}z)\right]\left(1+\frac{z_{p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=:I+II.

Since
H
is smooth, we have
II=O(1)
if
|\mu_{p}x|\leq r_{\gamma}
with
r_{\gamma}
small to be chosen later.

Let
\varepsilon=\frac{2\pi}{3}(4-\gamma)>0
and take
R_{\gamma}>0
such that
\int_{B_{R_{\gamma}}(0)}e^{V_{i,\theta}}>\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{V_{i,\theta}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=8\pi-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, where
V_{i,\theta}
is given in Lemma
4.1
.
Then for
p
large enough,

\int_{B_{R_{\gamma}}(0)}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\geq\int_{B_{R_{\gamma}}(0)}e^{V_{i,\theta}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>8\pi-\varepsilon.

(4.12)

Next, we divide the integral
I
in the following way

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left\{|z|\leq R_{\gamma}\right\}}\log\frac{|z|}{|z-x|}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left\{|z|\geq\frac{2r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{p}}\right\}}\log\frac{|z|}{|z-x|}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left\{R_{\gamma}\leq|z|\leq\frac{2r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{p}},~|z|\leq 2|z-x|\right\}}\log\frac{|z|}{|z-x|}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left\{R_{\gamma}\leq|z|\leq\frac{2r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{p}},~|z|\geq 2|z-x|\right\}}\log|z|\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left\{R_{\gamma}\leq|z|\leq\frac{2r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{p}},~|z|\geq 2|z-x|\right\}}\log\frac{1}{|z-x|}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}.

Let
2R_{\gamma}\leq|x|\leq\frac{r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{p}}
.
We observe that if
|z|\leq R_{\gamma}
, then
|x|\geq 2|z|
,
|x-z|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|
and hence

\log\frac{|z|}{|z-x|}\leq\log\frac{2R_{\gamma}}{|x|}\leq 0.

From here and (
4.12
), we have

I_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{2R_{\gamma}}{|x|}\int_{\left\{|z|\leq R_{\gamma}\right\}}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\leq C+(4-\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi})\log\frac{1}{|x|}.

If
|z|\geq\frac{2r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{p}}
, then
|z|\geq 2|x|
and hence

\log\frac{2}{3}\leq\log\frac{|z|}{|z-x|}\leq\log 2,

which implies

I_{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{p}}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}=\frac{Cp}{u_{p}(x_{p})}\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\leq C.

Similarly,

I_{3}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{p}}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\leq C.

From the proof of Lemma
4.2
and
u_{p}(x_{p})\to l_{i}
, we see that

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{p\to\infty}\int_{\left\{|z|\leq\frac{2r}{\mu_{p}}\right\}}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}=\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{p}{u_{p}(x_{p})}\int_{B_{2r}(x_{p})}v_{p}^{p}=8\pi.

Thus we can choose
r_{\gamma}>0
small such that for
p
large,

\int_{\left\{|z|\leq\frac{2r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{p}}\right\}}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\leq 8\pi+\varepsilon

and then (
4.12
) yields

\int_{\left\{R_{\gamma}\leq|z|\leq\frac{2r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{p}}\right\}}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\leq 2\varepsilon.

We also observe that
|z|\geq 2|z-x|
implies
|z|\leq 2|x|
,
so

I_{4}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\log 2|x|\int_{\left\{R_{\gamma}\leq|z|\leq\frac{2r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{p}}\right\}}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\leq C-\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}\log\frac{1}{|x|}.

Finally, recall Lemma
4.1
that

\max z_{p}\leq\frac{p(v_{p}(y_{p})-u_{p}(x_{p}))}{u_{p}(x_{p})}\to\frac{C_{i}}{l_{i}},\quad\text{as $p\to\infty$},

which implies
\max z_{p}\leq C
for
p
large.
Since
|z-x|\geq 1
implies
\log\frac{1}{|z-x|}\leq 0
, we have

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left\{R_{\gamma}\leq|z|\leq\frac{2r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{p}},~|z|\geq 2|z-x|,|z-x|\leq 1\right\}}\log\frac{1}{|z-x|}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\frac{C}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\left\{|z-x|\leq 1\right\}}\log\frac{1}{|z-x|}\mathrm{d}z\leq C.

As a result, we get

I=\sum_{j=1}^{5}I_{j}\leq C_{\gamma}+(4-\frac{3\varepsilon}{2\pi})\log\frac{1}{|x|}=\gamma\log\frac{1}{|x|}+C_{\gamma}.

This proves
w_{p}(x)\leq\gamma\log\frac{1}{|x|}+C_{\gamma}
for
2R_{\gamma}\leq|x|\leq\frac{r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{p}}
and
p
large.

Using the following facts
z_{i,p}(0)\to V_{i,\theta}(0)
,

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\lim_{p\to\infty}u_{p}^{\theta_{p}}(x_{i,p})\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left(1+\frac{w_{i,p}}{p}\right)^{q}\geq l_{i}^{\theta}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{U_{i,\theta}}=8\pi,

and

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{p\to\infty}u_{p}^{\theta_{p}}(x_{i,p})\int_{\left\{|z|\leq\frac{2r}{\mu_{i,p}}\right\}}\left(1+\frac{w_{i,p}}{p}\right)^{q}=\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{p}{u_{p}(x_{i,p})}\int_{B_{2r}(x_{i,p})}u_{p}^{q}=8\pi,

we can prove a similar result for
z_{i,p}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

For any
0<\gamma<4
and for
p
large, if
2R_{\gamma}\leq|x|\leq\frac{r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{i,p}}
, then Lemma
4.3
implies

\left(1+\frac{w_{i,p}(x)}{p}\right)^{q}=e^{q\log(1+\frac{w_{i,p}(x)}{p})}\leq e^{\frac{q}{p}w_{i,p}(x)}\leq\frac{C_{\gamma}}{|x|^{\gamma}}.

Meanwhile, by the convergence of
w_{i,p}
in
{\cal C}_{loc}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, we have
\left(1+\frac{w_{i,p}(x)}{p}\right)^{q}\leq C
for
|x|\leq 2R_{\gamma}
and
p
large.
Therefore, for any
|x|\leq\frac{r_{\gamma}}{\mu_{i,p}}
and
p
large, we have

0\leq\left(1+\frac{w_{i,p}(x)}{p}\right)^{q}\leq\frac{C_{\gamma}}{1+|x|^{\gamma}},

and similarly,

0\leq\left(1+\frac{z_{i,p}(x)}{p}\right)^{p}\leq\frac{C_{\gamma}}{1+|x|^{\gamma}}.

As a direct application of the above decay estimates, we prove

Lemma 4.4
.

It holds

l_{i}=\sqrt{e},\quad\text{for any}~i=1,\cdots,k.

Proof.

By Remark
4.2
, there is
r>0
and
p_{0}>0
such that for any
|x|\leq\frac{r}{\mu_{i,p}}
and
p\geq p_{0}
, we have

0\leq\left(1+\frac{z_{i,p}(x)}{p}\right)^{p},\;\left(1+\frac{w_{i,p}(x)}{p}\right)^{q}\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{3}}.

(4.13)

Since
|G(x_{i,p},y)|\leq C|\log|x_{i,p}-y||\leq C
for any
y\in\Omega\setminus B_{r}(x_{i,p})
, we have

\left|\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}(x_{i,p})}G(x_{i,p},y)v_{p}^{p}(y)\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq C\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}(y)\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{p}.

Then by the Green representation formula, we have

\displaystyle u_{p}(x_{i,p})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}G(x_{i,p},y)v_{p}^{p}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}(x_{i,p})}G(x_{i,p},y)v_{p}^{p}(y)\mathrm{d}y+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{p}(x_{i,p})}{p}\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\mu_{i,p}}}(0)}G(x_{i,p},x_{i,p}+\mu_{i,p}z)\left(1+\frac{z_{i,p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z+o_{p}(1),

so

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p}\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\mu_{i,p}}}(0)}G(x_{i,p},x_{i,p}+\mu_{i,p}z)\left(1+\frac{z_{i,p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z=1.

On the other hand,
by (
4.13
), Lemma
4.1
and the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we get

\lim_{p\to\infty}\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\mu_{i,p}}}(0)}\left(1+\frac{z_{i,p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{V_{i,\theta}}=8\pi,

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to\infty}\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\mu_{i,p}}}(0)}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|z|+H(x_{i,p},x_{i,p}+\mu_{i,p}z)\right)\left(1+\frac{z_{i,p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|z|+H(x_{i},x_{i})\right)e^{V_{i,\theta}(z)}\mathrm{d}z=C<\infty.

From here,
G(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x-y|-H(x,y)
and
\mu_{i,p}^{-2}=pu_{p}(x_{i,p})^{p-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\mu_{i,p}}}(0)}G(x_{i,p},x_{i,p}+\mu_{i,p}z)\left(1+\frac{z_{i,p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\log\mu_{i,p}}{p}\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\mu_{i,p}}}(0)}\left(1+\frac{z_{i,p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p}\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\mu_{i,p}}}(0)}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|z|+H(x_{i,p},x_{i,p}+\mu_{i,p}z)\right)\left(1+\frac{z_{i,p}(z)}{p}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{\log p}{p}+\frac{p-1}{p}\log u_{p}(x_{i,p})\right)(8\pi+o_{p}(1))+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=2\log l_{i}+o_{p}(1).

Thus
2\log l_{i}=1
, i.e.,
l_{i}=\sqrt{e}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

It holds

p\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{p}|^{2}\to k8\pi e,\quad p\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{p}|^{2}\to k8\pi e,\quad p\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla v_{p}\to k8\pi e,

as
p\to\infty
.

Proof.

By (
4.7
), (
4.13
) and the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we have

\displaystyle p\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{p}|^{2}

\displaystyle=p\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}u_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p\int_{B_{r}(x_{i,p})}v_{p}^{p}u_{p}+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{p}^{2}(x_{i,p})\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\mu_{i,p}}}(0)}\left(1+\frac{z_{i,p}}{p}\right)^{p}\left(1+\frac{w_{i,p}}{p}\right)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle\to\sum_{i=1}^{k}l_{i}^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{V_{i,\theta}}=k8\pi e,\quad\text{as}~p\to\infty.

The other assertions can be proved similarly.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
i=1,\cdots,k
, we have

\frac{x_{i,p}-y_{i,p}}{\mu_{i,p}}\to 0,\quad\text{as}~p\to\infty.

Proof.

Suppose that up to a subsequence,
\frac{|x_{i,p}-y_{i,p}|}{\mu_{i,p}}\to+\infty
as
p\to\infty
.
Let
\tilde{\mu}_{i,p}
,
\tilde{z}_{i,p}
be defined as in Remark
4.1
.
Then it follows from Lemma
4.1
that

\frac{\mu_{i,p}^{2}}{\tilde{\mu}_{i,p}^{2}}=\left(\frac{v_{p}(y_{i,p})}{u_{p}(x_{i,p})}\right)^{p-1}=\Big(1+\frac{C_{i}/l_{i}+o_{p}(1)}{p}\Big)^{p-1}\to e^{C_{i}/l_{i}}\in[1,+\infty).

Thus for any
R>0
, we have
B_{\mu_{i,p}R}(x_{i,p})\cap B_{\tilde{\mu}_{i,p}R}(y_{i,p})=\emptyset
for
p
large, therefore,

\displaystyle k8\pi e+o_{p}(1)=p\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla v_{p}=p\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p+1}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{k}p\int_{B_{\mu_{j,p}R}(x_{j,p})}v_{p}^{p+1}+p\int_{B_{\mu_{i,p}R}(x_{i,p})}v_{p}^{p+1}+p\int_{B_{\tilde{\mu}_{i,p}R}(y_{i,p})}v_{p}^{p+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_{p}^{2}(x_{j,p})\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left(1+\frac{z_{j,p}}{p}\right)^{p+1}+v_{p}^{2}(y_{i,p})\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left(1+\frac{\tilde{z}_{i,p}}{p}\right)^{p+1}

\displaystyle\to e\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{B_{R}(0)}e^{V_{j,\theta}}+e\int_{B_{R}(0)}e^{\tilde{V}_{i,\theta}},\quad\text{as}~p\to\infty.

Since
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{V_{j,\theta}}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{\tilde{V}_{i,\theta}}=8\pi
, taking
R>0
large enough
we obtain
k8\pi e\geq(k+\frac{1}{2})8\pi e
, a contradiction.
Thus
\frac{y_{i,p}-x_{i,p}}{\mu_{i,p}}
are uniformly bounded and up to a subsequence,
\frac{y_{i,p}-x_{i,p}}{\mu_{i,p}}\to a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
as
p\to\infty
.
Since
z_{i,p}(\frac{y_{i,p}-x_{i,p}}{\mu_{i,p}})=\max z_{i,p}
, we have
V_{i,\theta}(a)=\max V_{i,\theta}=V_{i,\theta}(0)
and so
a=0
.
∎

Finally we compute the location of the blow up points
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

It holds

\nabla R(x_{i})-2\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{k}\nabla G(x_{i},x_{j})=0,

for every
i=1,\cdots,k
.

Proof.

We only prove the conclusion for
i=1
.
Applying the Pohozaev identity (
2.4
) with
\Omega^{\prime}=B_{r}(x_{1})
and
(u,v)=(u_{p},v_{p})
, we obtain from (
4.7
) that

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial B_{r}(x_{1})}\left\langle p\nabla u_{p},\nu\right\rangle p\partial_{i}v_{p}+\left\langle p\nabla v_{p},\nu\right\rangle p\partial_{i}u_{p}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x}+\int_{\partial B_{r}(x_{1})}\left\langle p\nabla u_{p},p\nabla v_{p}\right\rangle\nu_{i}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x}

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{r}(x_{1})}\left(\frac{p^{2}v_{p}^{p+1}}{p+1}+\frac{p^{2}u_{p}^{q+1}}{q+1}\right)\nu_{i}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x}=o_{p}(1),\quad i=1,2.

Set

h(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}G(x,x_{i}),

then it follows from Lemmas
4.2
and
4.4
that

p\nabla u_{p}(x)\to 8\pi\sqrt{e}\nabla h(x),\quad p\nabla v_{p}(x)\to 8\pi\sqrt{e}\nabla h(x),

uniformly for
x\in\partial B_{r}(x_{1})
as
p\to\infty
.
Thus we deduce from (
4.14
) that

\int_{\partial B_{r}(x_{1})}\left\langle\nabla h,\nu\right\rangle\partial_{i}h-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla h|^{2}\nu_{i}\mathrm{d}\sigma=0,\quad i=1,2.

(4.15)

Denote
\bar{h}(x)=H(x,x_{1})-\sum_{j=2}^{k}G(x,x_{j})
, we have
\bar{h}\in{\cal C}^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{1}))
and
h(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x-x_{1}|-\bar{h}(x)
.
For any
x\in\partial B_{r}(x_{1})
, by direct computations we get

\left\langle\nabla h(x),\nu\right\rangle\partial_{i}h(x)-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}\nu_{i}=\frac{(x-x_{1})_{i}}{8\pi^{2}r^{3}}+\frac{1}{2\pi r}\partial_{i}\bar{h}(x)+O(1).

Inserting this into (
4.15
), we see that
\partial_{i}\bar{h}(\xi_{r,i})=O(r)
for some
\xi_{r,i}\to x_{1}
as
r\to 0
.
So letting
r\to 0
, we have
\nabla\bar{h}(x_{1})=0
. This, together with
R(x)=H(x,x)
, implies

\nabla R(x_{1})-2\sum_{j=2}^{k}\nabla G(x_{1},x_{j})=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The proof is a combination of the above results.
∎

4.2
The least energy solution

To introduce the least energy solution in
[
3
]
, we need some notations.
Consider the energy functional

I_{p}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v|^{p+1}}{p+1}+\frac{|u|^{q+1}}{q+1},\quad\text{for}~u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

and

J_{p}(u)=\frac{p}{p+1}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}-\frac{1}{q+1}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q+1}

defined on
E=W_{0}^{1,1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)
, endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{E}^{1+\frac{1}{p}}=\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}.

Then
I_{p}\in{\cal C}^{1}(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
and
J_{p}\in{\cal C}^{1}(E)
.
Clearly
(u,v)
is a solution of (
1.1
) if and only if
(u,v)
is a critical point of
I_{p}
,
if and only if
u
is a critical point of
J_{p}
and
v=(-\Delta u)^{\frac{1}{p}}
.
A solution
(u_{p},v_{p})
of (
1.1
) is called
a least energy solution
if
u_{p}
is a critical point of
J_{p}

with the least energy among all nontrivial critical points of
J_{p}
.
It was proved in
[
3
]
that (
1.1
) has a least energy solution
(u_{p},v_{p})

such that
v_{p}=(-\Delta u_{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}
and
J_{p}(u_{p})=\inf_{u\in{\cal N}_{p}}J_{p}(u)
with

{\cal N}_{p}:=\left\{u\in E\setminus\{0\}:~J_{p}^{\prime}(u)[u]=\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}-\int_{\Omega}u^{q+1}=0\right\}.

Here we prove that this least energy solution satisfy (
1.4
) with
\beta=8\pi e
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
p,q
satisfy (
1.3
). Then for a sequence of least energy solutions
(u_{p},v_{p})
defined as above, it holds

\limsup_{p\to+\infty}p\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla v_{p}\leq 8\pi e.

(4.16)

Proof.

We construct a proper test function. Without loss of generality, we assume
0\in\Omega
.
Let

\varphi_{p}(x)=\sqrt{e}\left(1+\frac{z(y)}{p}\right),\quad\text{with}~y=\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{p}},

where
z(y)=-2\log(1+\frac{1}{8}e^{\frac{\theta}{2}}|y|^{2})
and
\varepsilon_{p}^{-2}=pe^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
.
Take
r>0
small such that
B_{2r}(0)\subset\Omega
.
Take a cut-off function
\eta\in{\cal C}^{\infty}_{0}(B_{2r}(0))
such that

0\leq\eta\leq 1,~~\eta(x)=1~\text{for}~|x|\leq r,

and

|\nabla\eta(x)|\leq\frac{C}{r},\quad|\Delta\eta(x)|\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}},\quad~\text{for}~r\leq|x|\leq 2r.

Let
\psi_{p}=\varphi_{p}\eta
. Then

\int_{\Omega}|\Delta\psi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{|x|\leq r}|\Delta\varphi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{r\leq|x|\leq 2r}|\Delta\varphi_{p}\eta+2\nabla\varphi_{p}\nabla\eta+\varphi_{p}\Delta\eta|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}\mathrm{d}x=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

By
-\Delta z(y)=e^{z(y)+\frac{\theta}{2}}
and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{z(y)+\frac{\theta}{2}}=8\pi
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq r}|\Delta\varphi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{e^{\frac{p+1}{2p}}}{p^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\varepsilon_{p}^{2/p}}\int_{|y|\leq\varepsilon_{p}^{-1}r}|\Delta z(y)|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e}{p}\int_{|y|\leq\varepsilon_{p}^{-1}r}e^{(z(y)+\frac{\theta}{2})(1+\frac{1}{p})}\mathrm{d}y=\frac{8\pi e}{p}(1+o_{p}(1)).

On the other hand, by

(a+b+c)^{1+\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2(a^{1+\frac{1}{p}}+b^{1+\frac{1}{p}}+c^{1+\frac{1}{p}}),\quad\forall a,b,c\geq 0,\;p\geq 2,

we have

I_{2}\leq C\left[\int_{r\leq|x|\leq 2r}|\Delta\varphi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{1}{r^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}\int_{r\leq|x|\leq 2r}|\nabla\varphi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{1}{r^{2+\frac{2}{p}}}\int_{r\leq|x|\leq 2r}|\varphi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}\right].

By direct computations, we have

\int_{r\leq|x|\leq 2r}|\Delta\varphi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{e}{p}\int_{\varepsilon_{p}^{-1}r\leq|y|\leq 2\varepsilon_{p}^{-1}r}e^{(z(y)+\frac{\theta}{2})(1+\frac{1}{p})}\mathrm{d}y=\frac{o_{p}(1)}{p},

and similarly,

\int_{r\leq|x|\leq 2r}|\nabla\varphi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{o_{p}(1)}{p},\quad\int_{r\leq|x|\leq 2r}|\varphi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{o_{p}(1)}{p}.

Therefore,

\int_{\Omega}|\Delta\psi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{8\pi e}{p}(1+o_{p}(1)).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi_{p}^{q+1}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{q+1}{2}}\varepsilon_{p}^{2}\int_{|y|\leq 2\varepsilon_{p}^{-1}r}\eta^{q+1}(\varepsilon_{p}y)\left(1+\frac{z(y)}{p}\right)^{q+1}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\frac{\theta_{p}}{2}+1}}{p}\left(\int_{|y|\leq\varepsilon_{p}^{-1}r}e^{z(y)}(1+o_{p}(1))\mathrm{d}y+o_{p}(1)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi e}{p}(1+o_{p}(1)),

so
t_{p}\psi_{p}\in{\cal N}_{p}
with

t_{p}=\left(\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\Delta\psi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}{\int_{\Omega}\psi_{p}^{q+1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{pq-1}}=1+o_{p}(1).

Since
u_{p}
is a minimzier of
J_{p}|_{{\cal N}_{p}}
and for any
u\in{\cal N}_{p}
,

J_{p}(u)=\left(\frac{p}{p+1}-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{1+\frac{1}{p}},

we have

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla v_{p}=\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}\leq t_{p}^{1+\frac{1}{p}}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta\psi_{p}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{8\pi e}{p}(1+o_{p}(1)),

so (
4.16
) holds.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Applying Theorem
1.1
with
\beta=8\pi e
, we can prove the conclusion.
∎

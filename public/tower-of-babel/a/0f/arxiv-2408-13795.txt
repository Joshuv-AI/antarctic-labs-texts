On second-order variational analysis of variational convexity of prox-regular functions
Helmut Gfrerer

Abstract




Abstract.
Variational convexity, together with ist strong counterpart, of extended-real-valued functions has been recently introduced by Rockafellar. In this paper we present second-order characterizations of these properties, i.e., conditions using first-order generalized derivatives of the subgradient mapping. Up to now, such characterizations are only known under the assumptions of prox-regularity and subdifferential continuity and in this paper we discard the latter. To this aim we slightly modify the definitions of the generalized derivatives to be compatible with the
f
-attentive convergence appearing in the definition of subgradients. We formulate our results in terms of both coderivatives and subspace containing derivatives. We also give formulas for the exact bound of variational convexity and study relations between variational strong convexity, tilt-stable local minimizers and strong metric regularity of some truncation of the subgradient mapping.

Key words.
Variational convexity, variational strong convexity, tilt-stable local minimizers, coderivative, subspace containing derivative

AMS Subject classification.
49J53, 49J52, 90C31

1
Introduction

It is well-known that the subgradient mapping
\partial f
of a lower semicontinuous (lsc) function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=(-\infty,\infty]
is maximally monotone if and only if the function is convex. If one replaces maximal monotonicity of
\partial f
by local monotonicity around some reference pair then one arrives at the notion of
variational convexity
of
f
, which, loosely speaking, states that some localization of the subgradient mapping
\partial f
is indistinguishable from that of a convex function. This is a more subtle property than local convexity of
f
. Variational strong convexity can be defined in this scheme with replacing convexity by strong convexity and monotonicity of the subgradient mapping by strong monotonicity. Variational strong convexity (without naming it) goes back to the characterization of
tilt-stable
local minimizers by Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
16
]
, but the definitions of variational (strong) convexity were only introduced more than twenty years later by Rockafellar
[
18
]
.

Variational (strong) convexity is an important tool in the analysis of nonconvex optimization problems. E.g., in the presence of variational convexity, the first-order optimality condition
0\in\partial f(\bar{x})
is also sufficient for
\bar{x}
being a local minimizer, whereas variational strong convexity also ensures that
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable local minimizer
[
18
]
. The reader can find other applications to the proximal point and Augmented Lagrangian methods in the recent papers
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
.

Another important concept in variational analysis is
prox-regularity
as introduced by Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
15
]
. Prox-regularity has numerous applications in the stability analysis of solutions of nonconvex optimization problems and, more generally, in second-order calculus. Its close relation with variational convexity was pointed out by Rockafellar
[
21
]
.

As already mentioned, variational (strong) convexity can be characterized by the local (strong) monotonicity of the subgradient mapping. There are other characterizations available in the literature, e.g., by the uniform quadratic growth condition
[
18
,
21
]
or by local (strong) convexity of the Moreau envelope
[
9
]
. The aim of this paper is to provide second-order characterizations of (strong) variational convexity of prox-regular functions, where second-order analysis is understood as first-order analysis of the subgradient mapping. Another type of second-order analysis by using second-order expansions of the function, is out of the scope of this paper.
To the best of our knowledge, such second-order characterizations in terms of regular/limiting coderivatives of
\partial f
are only known under the additional assumption that
f
is subdifferentially continuous
[
9
]
. We also refer the reader to the very recent book of Mordukhovich
[
12
]
, where results for the infinite-dimensional case can be found as well.

The reason why the classical second-order constructions do not work in the absence of subdifferential continuity, can be simply explained by the fact that for variational convexity we have to work with
f
-attentive localizations of the subgradient mapping whereas the usual limiting coderivative of
\partial f
does not take
f
-attentive convergence into account. Two possible remedies appear to be manifest: We can either work with generalized derivatives of
f
-attentive localizations of
\partial f
or we modify the definition of the generalized derivative so that
f
-attentive convergence is incorporated. Although we will show that both approaches are equivalent, the former has the disadvantage that usually we do not know in advance the
f
-attentive neighborhood which we have to work with, whereas the latter results in purely point-based objects. We refer the reader to the very recent paper
[
11
]
where a neighborhood-based characterization of variational convexity was given in terms of the regular/limiting coderivative of some
f
-attentive localization of
\partial f
.

Another goal of this paper is to point out that for characterizing variational (strong) convexity we are not restricted to the use of
f
-attentive limiting coderivatives but we can equivalently use so-called
subspace containing derivatives
(SC derivatives) as introduced in
[
4
]
. The SC derivative of a set-valued mapping is a generalized derivative whose elements are subspaces and can be considered as a generalization of the B-differential for single-valued mappings to set-valued ones. Indeed, the elements of the B-differential are linear mappings, i.e., mappings whose graph is a linear subspace, but the subspaces contained in an SC derivative are not necessarily graphs of linear mappings. The subspaces belonging to the so-called adjoint SC derivative are always contained in the graph of the limiting coderivative and therefore the adjoint SC derivative can be considered as a kind of skeleton for the limiting coderivative. Moreover, the computation of the SC derivative seems to be simpler than the one of the limiting coderivative. SC derivatives have been successfully applied in various fields, like the development of semismooth
∗
Newton methods
[
4
,
7
,
8
,
3
]
, the characterization of tilt-stable local minimizers of prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous functions
[
4
]
, the isolated calmness property on a neighborhood
[
4
,
5
]
and computing semismooth derivatives of implicitly defined mappings
[
6
]
.

The paper is organized in the following way. Section 2 contains all the known notions and results of variational analysis and generalized differentiation used in this paper. In Section 3 we introduce the new notion of
f
-attentive generalized derivatives of the subdifferential mapping
\partial f
, provide some elementary calculus and present their relation with generalized derivatives of
f
-attentive localizations of
\partial f
under prox-regularity. Section 4 provides various neighborhood-based second-order characterizations of variational convexity, whereas in Section 5 we present purely point-based characterizations of variational strong convexity and tilt-stability and we establish equivalence between these properties and strong meric regularity of a truncation of the subgradient mapping. Moreover, formulas for the exact bound of the level of variational convexity and tilt-stability are given. The sole assumption we use is prox-regularity, subdifferential continuity is not supposed.

2
Preliminaries from variational analysis

Given an extended real-valued function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and a point
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\mid\,f(x)<\infty\}
, the
regular subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}
is given by

\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x}):=\Big\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\mid\,\liminf_{x\to\bar{x}}\frac{f(x)-f(\bar{x})-\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\geq 0\Big\},

while the
(limiting) subdifferential
is defined by

\partial f(\bar{x}):=\{x^{*}\,\mid\,\exists x_{k}\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits_{f}\bar{x},x_{k}^{*}\to x^{*}\mbox{ with }x_{k}^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}f(x_{k})\ \forall k\},

where
x_{k}\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits_{f}\bar{x}
denotes
f
-attentive
convergence, i.e.

x_{k}\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits_{f}\bar{x}\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ x_{k}\to\bar{x}\mbox{ and }f(x_{k})\to f(\bar{x}).

Definition 2.1
.

An lsc function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is called prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}

if
f
is finite at
\bar{x}
with
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
and there
exist
\epsilon>0
and
r\geq 0
such that

f(x^{\prime})\geq f(x)+\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle-\frac{r}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|^{2}

whenever
x^{\prime}\in{\cal B}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})
and
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial_{\rho}f\cap({\cal B}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})\times{\cal B}_{\epsilon}({\bar{x}^{\ast}}))
with
\rho:=f(\bar{x})+\epsilon
, where the mapping
\partial_{\rho}f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

\mathrm{gph}\,\partial_{\rho}f:=\{(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f\,\mid\,f(x)<\rho\}=\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f\cap(L_{f}(\rho)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})

specifies an
f
-truncation of
\partial f
.

Obviously, if the requirements of Definition
2.1
are fulfilled for some
r\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
, they are also fulfilled for any
r^{\prime}\geq r
and every
0<\epsilon^{\prime}\leq\epsilon
. Further, it is easy to see that
f
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
with parameter
r\geq 0
if and only if there exists an open convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
along with some
\rho>f(\bar{x})
such that

f(x^{\prime})\geq f(x)+\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle-\frac{r}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|^{2}\quad\mbox{ whenever }x^{\prime}\in U,\ (x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial_{\rho}f\cap(U\times V).

The requirement
f(x)<\rho
ensures that the considered subgradients
x^{*}
correspond to normals to
\mathrm{epi}\,f
at points
(x,f(x))
close to
(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))
. This condition can be omitted if
f
is
subdifferentially continuous
at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
in the sense that for any sequence
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
we have
\lim_{k\to\infty}f(x_{k})=f(\bar{x})
. Subdifferential continuity is widely used in second-order variational analysis but one of the main goals of the paper is to avoid it. Recall that an lsc convex function
f
is prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous at any
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
for every subgradient
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
.

If
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
, it is well-known that for any twice continuously differentiable function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
the function
f+g
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for the subgradient
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x})\in\partial(f+g)(\bar{x})
, cf.
[
22
, Exercise 13.35]
. Subdifferential continuity, if present, is likewise preserved.

Allowing the prox-regularity parameter
r
also to be negative leads to the following definition.

Definition 2.2

(cf.
[
21
]
)
.

Let
s
be a real number. We say that the lsc function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
has the
s
-level uniform quadratic growth property at
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
for the subgradient
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
, if there is an open convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
together with some real
\rho>f(\bar{x})
such that

f(x^{\prime})\geq f(x)+\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle+\frac{s}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|^{2}\quad\mbox{ whenever }x^{\prime}\in U,\ (x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial_{\rho}f\cap(U\times V).

(2.1)

Recall that a mapping
S:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
monotone
if

\langle y_{1}-y_{2},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq 0\mbox{ for all }(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S

and it is called
\sigma
-strongly monotone
if
S-\sigma I
is monotone.
Further, a monotone mapping
S:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
maximally monotone
if for any monotone mapping
S^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathrm{gph}\,S\subset\mathrm{gph}\,S^{\prime}
there holds
\mathrm{gph}\,S=\mathrm{gph}\,S^{\prime}
.

There exist also local versions of these notions. A mapping
S:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called monotone relative to a set
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if

\langle y_{1}-y_{2},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq 0\mbox{ for all }(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S\cap W.

The mapping
S
is called
locally monotone
around a point
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S
if there exists a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
such that
S
is monotone with respect to
U\times V
. Finally,
S
is called
locally maximal monotone
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S
if there exists a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
such that
S
is monotone relative to
U\times V
and
\mathrm{gph}\,S\cap(U\times V)=\mathrm{gph}\,S^{\prime}\cap(U\times V)
for any monotone mapping
S^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
\mathrm{gph}\,S^{\prime}\supset\mathrm{gph}\,S\cap(U\times V)
.

The following proposition is an immediate consequence of a result proven very recently by Khanh, Khoa, Mordukhovich and Phat
[
10
, Theorem 3.3]
, which is also valid in the infinite dimensional case.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
S:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a mapping and consider
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

S
is locally maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

(ii)

There exists a maximally monotone mapping
\bar{S}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
such that
\mathrm{gph}\,S\cap(U\times V)=\mathrm{gph}\,\bar{S}\cap(U\times V)
.

Remark 2.4
.

Our definition of local maximal monotonicity corresponds to the one given in
[
16
]
while the property stated in Proposition
2.3
(ii) is related to another notion of local maximal monotonicity introduced in
[

]
.

Next we define
f
-attentive localizations of the subdifferential mapping
\partial f
.

Definition 2.5

(cf.
[
21
]
)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an lsc function and let
s\in\mathbb{R}
be given. We say that
\partial f
has at
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
an
f
-attentive localization that is
s
-monotone, if there exists an open convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
and a real
\rho>f(\bar{x})
such that

\langle y^{*}-x^{*},y-x\rangle\geq s\|y-x\|^{2}\quad\mbox{whenever }(x,x^{*}),(y,y^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial_{\rho}f\cap(U\times V).

For
s=0
this is plain monotonicity of
\partial f
relative to the set
(U\cap L_{f}(\rho))\times V
.

For local maximality properties of this
f
-attentive localizations of
\partial f
we refer to
[
21
]
.

Another important concept is variational (strong) convexity which was recently introduced by Rockafellar
[
18
]
and extended in
[
21
]
. We augment this definition by introducing the
exact bound of variational convexity
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
s
be a real number. The lsc function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
will be called variationally
s
-convex at
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
if there is some open convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
, an lsc function
\widehat{f}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and a real
\rho>f(\bar{x})
such that the quadratically shifted function
\widehat{f}-\frac{s}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex on
U
,
\widehat{f}\leq f
on
U
and

\mathrm{gph}\,\partial_{\rho}f\cap(U\times V)=\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\widehat{f}\cap(U\times V)\quad\mbox{and $f(x)=\widehat{f}(x)$ at the common elements $(x,x^{*})$}.

If this holds with
s=0
, we will call
f
variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
, whereas in case
s>0
we call
f
to be variationally strongly convex with modulus
s
.

The exact bound for variational convexity of
f
is defined as the supremum of all
s
such that
f
is variationally
s
-convex at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
and is denoted by
{\rm var\;co\,}(f;\bar{x}|{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
. We set
{\rm var\;co\,}(f;\bar{x}|{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=-\infty
if no such
s
exist.

Note that
{\rm var\;co\,}(f;\bar{x}|{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
can attain also the value
\infty
. Moreover, even if
\bar{s}:={\rm var\;co\,}(f;\bar{x}|{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\in\mathbb{R}
,

does not have to be variationally
\bar{s}
-convex. E.g., for the function
f_{1}(x)=-x^{4}
and
f_{2}(x)=0
on
\mathbb{R}
we have
{\rm var\;co\,}(f_{i};0|0)=0
,

, but
f_{1}
is not variationally convex at
0
for
0
whereas

has this property there.

The following fundamental equivalence between the properties from Definitions

,

Theorem 2.7
.

Given an lsc function

, a pair

and a real

, the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

\bar{x}
for

.

(ii)

for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}

(iii)

.

We now turn our attention to generalized derivatives of set-valued mappings. Given a set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\Omega-\bar{x}}{t}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\mid\,\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,\ x_{k}\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{\Omega}\bar{x}:\ u=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{t_{k}}\},

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\big(T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\big)^{\circ},

\displaystyle N_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\limsup_{x\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{\Omega}\bar{x}}\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(x).

Given a set-valued mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
and a point
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
, we now define the graphical derivative
DF(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the regular coderivative
\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the limiting coderivative
D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,DF(\bar{x},\bar{y}):=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{(y^{*},x^{*})\,\mid\,(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{(y^{*},x^{*})\,\mid\,(x^{*},-y^{*})\in N_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\}.

Let us now consider the SC (subspace containing) derivatives for
F
as defined very recently in
[
4
,
5
]
. We denote by
{\cal Z}_{nm}
the metric space of all
n
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
with metric

d_{\cal Z}(L_{1},L_{2}):=\|P_{L_{1}}-P_{L_{2}}\|,

where
P_{L_{i}}
,
i=1,2
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
L_{i}
. Since orthogonal projections are bounded, the metric space
{\cal Z}_{nm}
is compact.

Given a subspace
L\in{\cal Z}_{nm}
we define its adjoint subspace
L^{*}\in{\cal Z}_{mn}
by

L^{*}=\{(v^{*},u^{*})\,\mid\,(u^{*},-v^{*})\in L^{\perp}\}.

Note that
(L^{*})^{*}=L
and
d_{\cal Z}(L_{1},L_{2})=d_{\cal Z}(L_{1}^{*},L_{2}^{*})
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a mapping.

1.

We say that
F
is graphically smooth of dimension
n
at
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
if
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)\in{\cal Z}_{nm}
. By
\mathcal{O}_{F}

we denote the subset of
\mathrm{gph}\,F
, where
F
is graphically smooth of dimension
n
.

2.

The SC derivative of
F
is the mapping
{\mathcal{S}}F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows{\cal Z}_{nm}
given by

{\mathcal{S}}F(x,y)=\{L\in{\cal Z}_{nm}\,\mid\,\exists(x_{k},y_{k})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{\cal O}_{F}}(x,y):\ \lim_{k\to\infty}d_{\cal Z}(T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x_{k},y_{k}),L)=0\},

whereas the adjoint SC derivative
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows{\cal Z}_{mn}
is defined by

{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F(x,y)=\{L^{*}\,\mid\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}F(x,y)\}.

Note that the adjoint SC derivative was defined in
[
5
]
in a different but equivalent manner. The adjoint SC derivative has the remarkable property that

L^{*}\subset\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})\ \forall L^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

(2.2)

The adjoint SC derivative may be therefore considered as a kind of skeleton for the limiting coderivative. Let us now discuss the differences in computing the limiting coderivative

D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and the SC derivative
{\mathcal{S}}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. The limiting coderivative is based on the limiting normal cone
N_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
which is the upper limit of regular normal cones. By taking the definition of the regular normal cone via the polar cone of the tangent cone, this means that we have to compute tangent cones
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)
for all
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
close to
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. In contrary, in order to compute the SC derivative
{\mathcal{S}}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, we only need the tangent cone at those points
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
where
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)
is a subspace. This does not only mean that we need to compute less tangent cones, but also the computation of the required tangent cones should be simpler. Usually, when the computation of a tangent cone gets involved, it is not a subspace and can be therefore discarded.

For the subdifferential of a prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous function we have the following result, which can be derived from
[
3
, Proposition 3.6, Lemma 3.4]
and gives a certain basis representation of subspaces

.

Proposition 2.10
.

Assume that the lsc function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
. Then there exists an open convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
such that for every
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f\cap(U\times V)
there holds
\emptyset\not={\mathcal{S}}(\partial f)(x,x^{*})={\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\partial f)(x,x^{*})
. Further, for every
L\in{\mathcal{S}}(\partial f)(x,x^{*})
there holds
L=L^{*}
and there exist unique symmetric
n\times n
matrices
P,W
with the following properties:

(i)

L={\rm rge\;}(P,W):=\{(Pp,Wp)\,\mid\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
,

(ii)

P^{2}=P
, i.e.,
P
represents the orthogonal projection onto some subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(iii)

W(I-P)=I-P
.

Conditions (ii) and (iii) above ensure that the following relations hold:

\displaystyle(I-P)W=\big(W(I-P)\big)^{T}=(I-P)^{T}=I-P=W(I-P),

(2.3)

\displaystyle PW=WP=PWP,

(2.4)

\displaystyle W=WP+W(I-P)=PWP+(I-P).

(2.5)

In what follows, we denote by
{\cal M}_{P,W}\partial f(x,x^{*})
the collection of all pairs of symmetric
n\times n
matrices
(P,W)
satisfying properties (ii) and (iii) of Proposition
2.10
and
{\rm rge\;}(P,W)\in{\mathcal{S}}(\partial f)(x,x^{*})
. Hence

{\mathcal{S}}(\partial f)(x,x^{*})=\{{\rm rge\;}(P,W)\,\mid\,(P,W)\in{\cal M}_{P,W}\partial f(x,x^{*})\}.

Note that in case when

is twice continuously differentiable then

, cf.
[
3
]

3
f
-attentive derivatives of the subgradient mapping

It is easy to see why the classical generalized derivatives are not suited for first-order analysis of the subdifferential mapping in the absence of subdifferential continuity. Consider the tangent cone

T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=\left\{(u,u^{*})\,\mid\,\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):(u,u^{*})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})-(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})}{t_{k}}\right\}.

Here, ordinary convergence
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
appears, but in the definition of the subdifferential we use
f
-attentive convergence. Apparently we should not mix the two different types of convergence and therefore we should also require that
f(x_{k})\to f(\bar{x})
in the absence of subdifferential continuity.

In the sequel we denote
f
-attentive convergence in the graph of the subgradient mapping
\partial f
by

(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ (x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\mbox{ and }f(x_{k})\to f(\bar{x}).

The following definitions are obtained by simply replacing in the definitions of tangents, regular/limiting normals to
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
and generalized derivatives of
\partial f
the ordinary convergence
(x,x^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
by
(x,x^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a function and
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
.

1.

The
f
-attentive tangent cone to
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is given by

T^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\left\{(u,u^{*})\,\mid\,\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,\ (x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):(u,u^{*})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})-(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})}{t_{k}}\right\}.

(3.8)

2.

The
f
-attentive regular normal cone to
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is defined as
\widehat{N}^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\big(T^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\big)^{\circ}
and the
f
-attentive limiting normal cone to
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
amounts to

N^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{(x,x^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})}\widehat{N}^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(x,x^{*}).

3.

The
f
-attentive graphical derivative
D_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
, the
f
-attentive regular coderivative
\widehat{D}_{f}^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
and the
f
-attentive limiting coderivative
D_{f}^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
of
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
are the set-valued mappings from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,D_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=T^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}_{f}^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\{(u,u^{*})\,\mid\,(u^{*},-u)\in\widehat{N}^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(x,x^{*})\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,D_{f}^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\{(u,u^{*})\,\mid\,(u^{*},-u)\in N^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(x,x^{*})\}.

4.

Let
{\cal O}_{\partial f}^{f}
denote the collection of all points
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
such that
T^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(x,x^{*})
is an
n
-dimensional subspace. Then the
f
-attentive SC derivative of
\partial f
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is defined by

{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\{L\in{\cal Z}_{nn}\,\mid\,\exists(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\subset{\cal O}^{f}_{\partial f},(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):d_{{\cal Z}}\big(L,T^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\big)\to 0\}.

Finally the adjoint
f
-attentive SC derivative of
\partial f
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is defined by

{\mathcal{S}}_{f}^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\{L^{*}\,\mid\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\}.

If
f
is subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for the subgradient
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
it follows immediately from the definitions that

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),\ \widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),

\displaystyle D_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=D(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),\ \widehat{D}^{*}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=\widehat{D}^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}).

If there exists a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
such that for every
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f\cap(U\times V)
the function
f
is subdifferentially continuous at
x
for
x^{*}
then there also holds

\displaystyle N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),\ D^{*}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=\widehat{D}^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})={\mathcal{S}}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),\ {\mathcal{S}}^{*}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})={\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}).

For the
f
-attentive objects there hold the following elementary calculus rules.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a function, let
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
, let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be twice continuously differentiable and consider the function
h:=f+g
. Then

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial h}^{h}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))=\bar{A}T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial h}^{h}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))=\bar{A}^{-T}\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),\ N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial h}^{h}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))=\bar{A}^{-T}N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,D_{h}(\partial h)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))=\bar{A}\,\mathrm{gph}\,D_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}_{h}^{*}(\partial h)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))=\bar{A}\,\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}_{f}^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,D_{h}^{*}(\partial h)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))=\bar{A}\,\mathrm{gph}\,D_{f}^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{h}(\partial h)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))=\{\bar{A}L\,\mid\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{h}^{*}(\partial h)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))=\{\bar{A}L^{*}\,\mid\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),\}

where

\bar{A}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})&I\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

By elementary calculus rules, cf.
[
22
, Exercise 8.8]
, we have
\partial h(x)=\partial f(x)+\nabla g(x)
for all
x
where
f(x)
is finite. Now consider a point
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
. Then it is easy to see that for any sequence
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
there holds
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(x,x^{*})
if and only if
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*}+\nabla g(x_{k}))\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{h}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial h}(x,x^{*}+\nabla g(x))
. Further, for every sequence
t_{k}\downarrow 0
there holds
((x_{k},x_{k}^{*})-(x,x^{*}))/t_{k}\to(u,u^{*})
if and only if

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{(x_{k},x_{k}^{*}+\nabla g(x_{k}))-(x,x^{*}+\nabla g(x))}{t_{k}}=(u,u^{*}+\nabla^{2}g(x)u).

Hence
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial h}^{h}(x,x^{*}+\nabla g(x))=A(x)T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(x,x^{*})
with
A(x):=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&0\\
\nabla^{2}g(x)&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. The matrix
A(x)
is apparently nonsingular and hence

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial h}^{h}(x,x^{*}+\nabla g(x))

\displaystyle=\big(A(x)T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(x,x^{*})\big)^{\circ}=\big(A(x){\rm conv\,}T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(x,x^{*})\big)^{\circ}

\displaystyle=A(x)^{-T}\big({\rm conv\,}T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(x,x^{*})\big)^{\circ}=A(x)^{-T}\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(x,x^{*})

by
[
17
, Corollary 16.3.2]
. Since
\lim_{x\to\bar{x}}A(x)
equals the nonsingular matrix
\bar{A}
, we may also infer that

\displaystyle N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial h}^{h}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))

\displaystyle=\limsup_{(x,x^{*}+\nabla g(x))\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{h}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial h}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))}\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial h}^{h}(x,x^{*}+\nabla g(x))

\displaystyle=\limsup_{(x,x^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})}A(x)^{-T}\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(x,x^{*})=\bar{A}^{-T}N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}).

Taking into account the identities
\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}^{*}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=S\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
,
\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=SN_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
and
S\bar{A}^{-T}S^{-1}=\bar{A}
, where
S:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-I\\
I&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, we obtain that

\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}^{*}_{h}(\partial h)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))=S\bar{A}^{-T}\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}^{f}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=S\bar{A}^{-T}S^{-1}\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}^{*}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=\bar{A}\,\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}^{*}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})

and, analogously, that
\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}_{h}(\partial h)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))=\bar{A}\,\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
. Finally, there holds
{\cal O}_{\partial h}^{h}=\{(x,x^{*}+\nabla g(x))\,\mid\,(x,x^{*})\in{\cal O}_{\partial f}^{f}\}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{h}(\partial h)(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))=\{\bar{A}L\,\mid\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\}
follows from
[
4
, Lemma 3.1(iii)]
. The formula for the adjoint SC derivative is a consequence of

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{h}^{*}(\partial h)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}+\nabla g(\bar{x}))

\displaystyle=\{S(\bar{A}L)^{\perp}\,\mid\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\}=\{S\bar{A}^{-T}L^{\perp}\,\mid\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\}

\displaystyle=\{(S\bar{A}^{-T}S^{-1})(SL^{\perp})\,\mid\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\}=\{\bar{A}L^{*}\,\mid\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(\partial f)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\}.

∎

We will now show that for prox-regular functions the
f
-attentive objects defined in Definition
3.1
correspond to their ordinary counterparts applied to an
f
-attentive
\epsilon
-localization of the subdifferential.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a function and
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
. Given
\epsilon>0
, the
f
-attentive
\epsilon
-localization of the subgradient mapping
\partial f
around
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is the multifunction
\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}=\mathrm{gph}\,\partial_{f(\bar{x})+\epsilon}f\cap({\cal B}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})\times{\cal B}_{\epsilon}({\bar{x}^{\ast}})).

We start our analysis with the following lemma inspired by
[
15
, Proposition 2.3]
. Recall that a set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be locally closed at a point
\bar{x}
(not necessarily in
C
) if

C\cap\bar{\cal B}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})
is closed for some
\epsilon>0
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that the lsc function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
with parameter values
r\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
and let
\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
denote the
f
-attentive
\epsilon
-localization of
\partial f
around
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

Let
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
be a sequence converging to some
(\hat{x},\hat{x}^{*})\in{\cal B}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})\times{\cal B}_{\epsilon}({\bar{x}^{\ast}})
. Then
f(\hat{x})=\lim_{k}f(x_{k})
is finite and
(\hat{x},\hat{x}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
and therefore
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\hat{x},\hat{x}^{*})
.

(ii)

Assume that
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\hat{x},\hat{x}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
. Then
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
for all
k
sufficiently large.

(iii)

For every
(\hat{x},\hat{x}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
and for every
\eta>0
there exists some
\delta>0
such that
f(x)<f(\hat{x})+\eta
for all
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}\cap({\cal B}_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times{\cal B}_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{*}))
.

(iv)

The set
\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
is locally closed at each of its points.

Proof.

ad (i): From the definition of prox-regularity we deduce that
f(\hat{x})\geq f(x_{k})+\langle x_{k}^{*},\hat{x}-x_{k}\rangle-\frac{r}{2}\|\hat{x}-x_{k}\|^{2}
and therefore
f(\hat{x})\geq\limsup_{k}f(x_{k})
. By lower semicontinuity of
f
we also have
f(\hat{x})\leq\liminf_{k}f(x_{k})
and
f(x_{k})\to f(\hat{x})\leq f(\bar{x})+\epsilon
follows. The inclusion
\hat{x}^{*}\in\partial f(\hat{x})
now follows from the definition of the limiting subdifferential.

ad (ii): Since
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})
converges in
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
to
(\hat{x},\hat{x}^{*})
belonging to the open set
{\cal B}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})\times{\cal B}_{\epsilon}({\bar{x}^{\ast}})
, we have
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f\cap({\cal B}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})\times{\cal B}_{\epsilon}({\bar{x}^{\ast}}))
for all
k
sufficiently large. Further, since
f(x_{k})\to f(\hat{x})<f(\bar{x})+\epsilon
, we also have
f(x_{k})<f(\bar{x})+\epsilon
for all
k
sufficiently large and consequently
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
for these
k
.

ad (iii): By contraposition. Assume on the contrary that there is
(\hat{x},\hat{x}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
and
\eta>0
such that for every natural number
k
there is some
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}\cap({\cal B}_{1/k}(\hat{x})\times{\cal B}_{1/k}(\hat{x}^{*}))
with
f(x_{k})\geq f(\hat{x})+\eta
and therefore
\liminf_{k}f(x_{k})\geq f(\hat{x})+\eta
. However, from (i) we conclude
f(\hat{x})=\lim_{k}f(x_{k})
, a contradiction.

ad (iv): Consider
(\hat{x},\hat{x}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
and choose
\eta>0
with
f(\hat{x})+\eta<f(\bar{x})+\epsilon
and
\delta>0
small enough such that the compact neighborhood
C:=\bar{\cal B}_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times\bar{\cal B}_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{*})
of
(\hat{x},\hat{x}^{*})
is contained in
{\cal B}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})\times{\cal B}_{\epsilon}({\bar{x}^{\ast}})
and
f(x)<f(\bar{x})+\eta
for all
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}\cap C
. We now show that
\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}\cap C
is closed.
Consider a sequence
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}\cap C
converging to some
(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
. Clearly
(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})\in C
and by (i) we have
f(\tilde{x})=\lim_{k}f(x_{k})\leq f(\hat{x})+\eta<f(\bar{x})+\epsilon
and
\tilde{x}^{*}\in\partial f(\tilde{x})
implying
(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}\cap C
. Hence
\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}\cap C
is closed and the lemma is proved.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume that the lsc function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
with parameter values
r\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
and let
\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
denote the
f
-attentive
\epsilon
-localization of
\partial f
around
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
. Then for every
(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
the following relations hold:

(i)

T^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
,
\widehat{N}^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})=\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})

(ii)

,
\widehat{D}_{f}^{*}(\partial f)(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})=\widehat{D}^{*}\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})

(iii)

\emptyset\not={\mathcal{S}}_{f}(\partial f)(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})={\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})={\mathcal{S}}_{f}^{*}(\partial f)(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})={\mathcal{S}}^{*}\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})

and for every

there holds
L=L^{*}
and there exist unique symmetric
n\times n
matrices

satisfying the properties (i)–(iii) of Proposition
2.10
.

Proof.

ad (i): Consider
(u,u^{*})\in T^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
together with sequences
t_{k}\downarrow 0
und
(u_{k},u_{k}^{*})\to(u,u^{*})
with
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*}):=(\tilde{x}+t_{k}u_{k},\tilde{x}^{*}+t_{k}u_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
. By Lemma
3.4
we have
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}
for all
k
sufficiently large and
(u,u^{*})\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
follows. This proves
T^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})\subset T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
. In order to prove the reverse inclusion, consider
(u,u^{*})\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
together with sequences
t_{k}\downarrow 0
and
(u_{k},u_{k}^{*})\to(u,u^{*})
with
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*}):=(\tilde{x}+t_{k}u_{k},\tilde{x}_{k}^{*}+t_{k}u_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
. From Lemma
3.4
we conclude that
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})

(u,u^{*})\in T^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
and the equality

is established. From this equality the second equality in (i) immediately follows from the definition.

Now let us prove the third equality in (i). Consider
(u^{*},u)\in N^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
together with sequences
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{f}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})

k

for these

yielding
N^{f}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})\subset N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
. Conversely, consider
(u^{*},u)\in N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
together with sequences
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{f}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{*})
and
(u_{k}^{*},u_{k})\to(u^{*},u)

ad (ii): This is an immediate consequence of (i).

ad (iii): Consider

for
\tilde{x}^{*}

On neighborhood-based second-order characterizations of variational

We start our analysis with two preparatory lemmata.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that we are given two lsc functions

Then

is variationally
s
-convex at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
, provided
\psi
has this property.

Proof.

Assume that
\psi
is variationally
s
-convex at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
and consider an open convex neighborhood
\widehat{U}\times\widehat{V}
of
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
together with a function
\widehat{\psi}\leq\psi
on
\widehat{U}
and some
\widehat{\rho}>0
such that
\widehat{\psi}-\frac{s}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex on
\widehat{U}
and

\mathrm{gph}\,\partial_{\widehat{\rho}}\psi\cap(\widehat{U}\times\widehat{V})=\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\widehat{\psi}\cap(\widehat{U}\times\widehat{V})\quad\mbox{and $\psi(x)=\widehat{\psi}(x)$ at the common elements $(x,x^{*})$}.

Taking
\tilde{\rho}:=\min\{\widehat{\rho},\rho\}
, by Remark
2.8
(ii) we can find an open convex neighborhood
\tilde{U}\times\tilde{V}\subset\widehat{U}\times\widehat{V}

Theorem 5.2
.

Let

be an lsc function and let
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
and
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})

be given. Then the following statements are equivalent:

is variationally strongly convex at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}

\sigma
.

(ii)

f
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}

\bar{x}

f
-truncation

\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
with modulus
\sigma^{-1}
.

In order to unburden notation, we may again assume that

. The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) follows from
[

, Theorem 3]
together with the fact that variational convexity implies prox-regularity, cf. Remark

from the proof of the implication (ii)

.

For showing the implication (ii)
\Rightarrow

\Rightarrow
(iii) in Theorem

. From tilt-stability of

\bar{x}
and

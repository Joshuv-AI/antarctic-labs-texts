Abstract
A kinematically complete quasi-free ( p , p ​ n ) (p,pn) experiment in inverse kinematics was performed to study the structure of the Borromean nucleus 17 B, which had long been considered to have neutron halo. By analyzing the momentum distributions and exclusive cross sections, we obtained the spectroscopic factors for 1 ​ s 1 / 2 1s_{1/2} and 0 ​ d 5 / 2 0d_{5/2} orbitals, and a surprisingly small percentage of 9(2) % \% was determined for 1 ​ s 1 / 2 1s_{1/2} . Our finding of such a small 1 ​ s 1 / 2 1s_{1/2} component and the halo features reported in prior experiments can be explained by the deformed relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov theory in continuum, revealing a definite but not dominant neutron halo in 17 B. The present work gives the smallest s s - or p p -orbital component among known nuclei exhibiting halo features, and implies that the dominant occupation of s s or p p orbitals is not a prerequisite for the occurrence of neutron halo.

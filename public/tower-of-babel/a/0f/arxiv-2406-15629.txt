Embedded multi-boson exchange: A step beyond quantum cluster theories
Dominik Kiese, Nils Wentzell, Igor Krivenko, Olivier Parcollet, Karsten Held, Friedrich Krien

Abstract
We introduce a diagrammatic multi-scale approach to the Hubbard model based on the interaction-irreducible (multi-boson) vertex of a small cluster embedded in a self-consistent medium. The vertex captures short-ranged correlations up to the length scale of the cluster, while long-ranged correlations are recovered from a set of diagrammatic equations for the Hedin three-leg vertex. By virtue of the crossing symmetry, the Fierz decoupling ambiguity of the Hubbard interaction is resolved exactly. Our benchmarks for the half-filled Hubbard model on the square lattice are in very good agreement with numerically exact diagrammatic Monte Carlo simulations.

Introduction

Quantum embedding methods, such as dynamical mean-field theory

1

self-consistent, non-interacting bath. While it is very successful in
describing local quantum fluctuations, DMFT suffers from several limitations,
in particular the lack of momentum dependence of the self-energy or
feedback of the long range fluctuations onto the electronic degrees of freedom. In
order to overcome those limitations, two main directions have been explored in
recent years.

First, cluster approaches
[
4
]
enlarge the impurity problem to

They describe short range quantum fluctuations, reintroduce some coarse grained momentum dependency
of the self-energy and allow one to compute non-local order parameters, e.g. d-wave superconducting order.
They also provide some systematic control, as

corresponds to DMFT while
N_{c}=\infty
is the exact solution.
The calculation of large clusters is however quite limited (except at high temperatures)
due to the limitations of the quantum impurity solvers (in particular the fermionic sign problem in quantum Monte Carlo).
As a result, some weak coupling mechanisms involving the effect of
long-ranged correlations on electronic degrees of freedom are out of reach for these methods.
For example, in the square-lattice Hubbard model at weak coupling

II
SBE decomposition

The SBE decomposition introduced in Ref.
[
35
]
corresponds to a classification of the vertex diagrams into various
reducible or irreducible classes, similar to the parquet decomposition
[
48
]
.
In the latter case, diagrams are (ir)reducible with respect to cutting pairs of Green’s function lines (‘
GG
-(ir)reducible’).
A
GG
-reducible diagram can be either reducible in the horizontal (
ph
) or vertical (
\overline{\text{ph}}
) particle-hole channel,
or in the particle-particle (
pp

GG
-irreducible.

In the SBE decomposition, on the other hand, (ir)reducibility implies that a diagram can (not)
be split into two parts by removing a bare interaction vertex (‘
U
-(ir)reducible’), see also Fig.
1
, where
U
denotes the Hubbard interaction.
With the exception of the bare interaction itself, each
U
-reducible diagram belongs to one
and only one of the three
GG
-reducible parquet classes
[
35
,
49
]
.

As a result, the full vertex may be written as a sum of the
U
-reducible and -irreducible diagrams (cf. Fig.
1
):

\displaystyle\bm{F}=\bm{\Lambda}^{U}+\sum_{r}\bm{\Delta}^{r}-2\bm{U},\;\;\;r\in\{\text{ph},\overline{\text{ph}},\text{pp}\}.

(1)

Here,
\bm{\Lambda}^{U}
is the
U
-irreducible residual vertex of the SBE decomposition;
the
U
-reducible diagrams are denoted as
\bm{\Delta}^{\text{ph}},\bm{\Delta}^{\overline{\text{ph}}},
and
\bm{\Delta}^{\text{pp}}
.
The last term
2\bm{U}
is a double-counting correction, taking into account that the bare interaction arises to leading order from each
\bm{\Delta}
.

For simplicity we adopt a matrix notation using bold symbols:
each vertex is initially a tensorial quantity, such as
F^{\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{4}}
(
\ell_{i}
is a fermionic multi-index including frequency, momentum, spin and possibly orbital degrees of freedom),
which we denote as a matrix
(\bm{F})^{ab}
with
a=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
and
b=(\ell_{3},\ell_{4})
.
Further below we focus on the single-band case, but up to this point the notation generalizes to multiple orbitals.

The key observation of Ref.
[
35
]
is that the
U
-reducible diagrams may be written
in terms of the Hedin vertices
\bm{\gamma}
,
\overline{\bm{\gamma}}

2

2

The vertices
\bm{\gamma}
and

and the screened interaction
\bm{W}
:

\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}^{r}=\overline{\bm{\gamma}}^{r}\bm{W}^{r}\bm{\gamma}^{r}.

(2)

This corresponds to a

bosonic line
\bm{W}

enclosed between two three-leg vertices, hence the name SBE decomposition.
As discussed in the literature
[
9
,
35
]
,

\bm{\gamma}
and
\bm{W}
are connected to fermion-boson and boson-boson correlation functions, respectively.

In practice, when
\bm{F}
and the
\bm{\Delta}

1

Note that

and

and three frequencies and momenta, respectively, as discussed in the following.

III
Approximation of
\Lambda^{U}

The SBE decomposition (
1
) shows that the full vertex
\bm{F}
receives a feedback
from the screened interaction
\bm{W}

In a regime of long-range antiferromagnetic correlations

For

(3)

3
3

3

We require

\displaystyle\bm{\Lambda}^{U,\text{lat}}_{\bm{k}_{1}\bm{k}_{2}\bm{k}_{3}}\approx\sum_{\bm{R}_{1}\bm{R}_{2}\bm{R}_{3}}e^{i\bm{k}_{1}\bm{R}_{1}+i\bm{k}_{2}\bm{R}_{2}+i\bm{k}_{3}\bm{R}_{3}}\bm{\Lambda}^{U,c}_{\bm{R}_{1}\bm{R}_{2}\bm{R}_{3}}\,,

The eMBEX cycle is schematically summarized in Fig.

(1)

Calculate the interaction irreducible cluster vertex

(4)

Initialize the self-energy, screened interactions and Hedin vertices for the self-consistent SBE calculations on the lattice. One possible choice is

(5)

Calculate

(6)

Update the polarization

(7)

Use the equation of motion (

(9)

Repeat steps (5) - (8) until the solution is converged to sufficient numerical accuracy. We terminate the SBE loop once the relative deviation

(10)

\displaystyle W_{q,n+1}^{\text{s}}

VII
Conclusions

We presented and implemented a diagrammatic multi-scale method (eMBEX), which is based
on the interaction-irreducible vertex of a quantum cluster embedded in a noninteracting medium.
Through its diagrammatic construction, eMBEX satisfies the crossing symmetry on the two-particle level and we show in Appendix
C
that, as a result, the Fierz ambiguity of the Hubbard interaction is resolved exactly.

Based on four-site DCA calculations for the half-filled Hubbard model on the square lattice, we obtain results for the electronic self-energy that are in excellent agreement with the exact benchmark data. The calculations are performed in the weak-to-intermediate coupling regime,
where the antiferromagnetic correlation length exceeds the size of the cluster reference system, underlining the multi-scale feature of our approach.

We investigated two patching schemes for the four-site DCA, a simple square patching (
\text{DCA}_{4}
) and a star patching (
\text{DCA}_{4^{*}}
), cf. Fig.
2
,
where the former performs by far better in the quantitative sense. Nonetheless, we found value in the
\text{DCA}_{4^{*}}
patching scheme as a qualitatively different physical starting point. In their comparison the two patching schemes show that our method relies on a proper account of strong short-ranged correlations on the level of the cluster reference system.
Interestingly, the underestimation of short-ranged correlations in
\text{DCA}_{4^{*}}
leads to an overestimation of long-ranged correlations in the lattice approximation, pointing to a delicate balance between the two.

At the same time, we observed that the more strongly correlated starting point of
\text{DCA}_{4}
makes it more difficult
[
73
]
and costly to converge the self-consistent eMBEX cycle. Nevertheless, the very good agreement of four-site eMBEX with numerically exact benchmarks serves as a strong incentive to investigate more sophisticated approaches to find the fixed point. One could, for example, try to harness the combined advantages of linear preconditioning schemes
[
71
,
70
]
and compressed representations of two-particle vertex functions
[
74
,
75
]
.

Appendix B
Hedin equation with Fierz ambiguity

Let us derive here the Hedin equation (
9
) including the Fierz parameter
0\leq r\leq 1
, which allows to write the Hubbard interaction as

\displaystyle Un_{\uparrow}n_{\downarrow}=\frac{U}{2}\left[rnn+(r-1)mm\right]-\left(r-\frac{1}{2}\right)Un,

(21)

where
n=n_{\uparrow}+n_{\downarrow}
and
m=n_{\uparrow}-n_{\downarrow}
are the operators for the density and magnetic moment.
In Eq. (
21
) we consider only the easy-axis component,
s^{z}=\frac{m}{2}
, of the spin operator,
however, the following discussion holds analogously for a rotationally invariant splitting of the Hubbard interaction
[
9
]
.
The Hubbard Hamiltonian in the grand canonical ensemble can then be written in the form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=H_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\left[V^{\text{ch}}n_{i}n_{i}+V^{\text{sp}}m_{i}m_{i}\right]-(\mu+\mu_{r})N.

(22)

Here,
H_{0}
describes the hopping,
N=\sum_{i}n_{i}
is the total particle number operator.

V^{\text{ch}}=rU
and
V^{\text{sp}}=(r-1)U
parametrize the splitting of the Hubbard interaction.

\mu_{r}=\left(r-\frac{1}{2}\right)U
takes the shift of the chemical potential due to the reformulation of the Hubbard interaction into account.
For the model in Eq. (
22
) the equation of motion for the electronic Green’s function takes the form
[
76
]

\displaystyle\left[\imath\nu-\varepsilon_{k}+\mu+\mu_{r}\right]-G_{k}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V^{\text{ch}}\langle n\rangle-\sum_{q,\alpha=\text{ch},\text{sp}}G_{k+q}V^{\alpha}\gamma^{\text{red},\alpha}_{kq},

(23)

where
\gamma^{\text{red}}
is an interaction-reducible three-leg vertex, related to the four-point vertex
F
as,

\displaystyle\gamma^{\text{red},\alpha}_{kq}=1+\sum_{k^{\prime}}G_{k^{\prime}}G_{k^{\prime}+q}F^{\alpha}_{k^{\prime}kq}.

(24)

The shift
\mu_{r}
could be absorbed into the noninteracting Green’s function
G^{0}
, but here we move it to the self-energy
\Sigma
, which thus describes all changes induced by the middle (
V^{\text{ch}}+V^{\text{sp}}
) term on the right hand side of Eq. (
22
).
Then Eq. (
23
) reads,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}=-\mu_{r}+V^{\text{ch}}\langle n\rangle-\sum_{q,\alpha=\text{ch},\text{sp}}G_{k+q}V^{\alpha}\gamma^{\text{red},\alpha}_{kq},

(25)

where we used
[G^{0}_{k}]^{-1}-G_{k}^{-1}=\Sigma_{k}
.
The interaction-reducible three-leg vertex is related to the Hedin vertex via

\displaystyle\gamma^{\text{red},\text{ch}/\text{sp}}_{kq}=\frac{W^{\text{ch}/\text{sp}}_{q}}{\pm U}\gamma^{\text{ch}/\text{sp}}_{kq}.

(26)

Using this together with the definition of
V^{\text{ch}/\text{sp}}
we obtain the Hedin equation including the Fierz parameter,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}=

\displaystyle-\mu_{r}+rU\langle n\rangle

(27)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{q}G_{k+q}\left[rW^{\text{ch}}_{q}\gamma^{\text{ch}}_{kq}-(r-1)W^{\text{sp}}_{q}\gamma^{\text{sp}}_{kq}\right].

We now show that a crossing-symmetric approximation for
\Lambda^{U}

Of course, to this end, we use in the following only identities which are satisfied by the self-consistent eMBEX solution.

Firstly, we recognize that the eMBEX self-energy is given by Eq. (

,
and that the Hedin vertex
\gamma

via Eqs. (

Hence, in eMBEX Eq. (

) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}=

\displaystyle-\mu_{r}+\frac{U}{2}\langle n\rangle

(28)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle U\sum_{qk^{\prime}}G_{k+q}G_{k^{\prime}}G_{k^{\prime}+q}\left[rF^{\text{ch}}_{k^{\prime}kq}+(r-1)F^{\text{sp}}_{k^{\prime}kq}\right],

where
F^{\text{ch}/\text{sp}}
are given by the SBE decomposition in Eqs. (
20a
) and (
20b
).
Notice that the one-body term in this expression is modified compared to
Eq. (
27
) due to the leading term
1
in Eq. (
24
).

It was recently shown
[
77
]
that the SBE decomposition gives rise to a fluctuation decomposition of the self-energy,

\Sigma=-\mu_{r}+\frac{U}{2}\langle n\rangle+\Sigma^{\text{2nd}}+\Sigma^{\text{ch}}+\Sigma^{\text{sp}}+\Sigma^{\text{s}}+\Sigma^{\text{mb}}
,
where only the contribution of
\Lambda^{U}
to Eq. (
28
) depends explicitly on
r
:

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\text{mb}}_{k}=-U\sum_{qk^{\prime}}G_{k+q}G_{k^{\prime}}G_{k^{\prime}+q}\left[r\Lambda^{U,\text{ch}}_{k^{\prime}kq}+(r-1)\Lambda^{U,\text{sp}}_{k^{\prime}kq}\right].

(29)

The other terms,
\Sigma^{\text{2nd}},\Sigma^{\text{ch}},\Sigma^{\text{sp}},
and
\Sigma^{\text{s}}
,
do not depend on
r
and we ignore them in the following.
Notwithstanding the trivial term
\mu_{r}
,
the Fierz ambiguity is resolved if the
r
dependence can be eliminated from Eq. (
29
).
This can be done using the crossing symmetry:

\displaystyle\Lambda^{U,\alpha}_{kk^{\prime}q}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\Lambda^{U,\text{ch}}_{k,k+q,k^{\prime}-k}+[3-4\delta_{\alpha,\text{sp}}]\Lambda^{U,\text{sp}}_{k,k+q,k^{\prime}-k}\right).

(30)

We consider the
r
-dependent part of the right-hand-side of Eq. (
29
),

\displaystyle R_{k}=

\displaystyle-rU\sum_{qk^{\prime}}G_{k+q}G_{k^{\prime}}G_{k^{\prime}+q}\left[\Lambda^{U,\text{ch}}_{k^{\prime}kq}+\Lambda^{U,\text{sp}}_{k^{\prime}kq}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{rU}{2}\sum_{qk^{\prime}}G_{k+q}G_{k^{\prime}}G_{k^{\prime}+q}\left[\Lambda^{U,\text{ch}}_{k,k+q,k^{\prime}-k}+3\Lambda^{U,\text{sp}}_{k,k+q,k^{\prime}-k}\right]+\frac{rU}{2}\sum_{qk^{\prime}}G_{k+q}G_{k^{\prime}}G_{k^{\prime}+q}\left[\Lambda^{U,\text{ch}}_{k,k+q,k^{\prime}-k}-\Lambda^{U,\text{sp}}_{k,k+q,k^{\prime}-k}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rU\sum_{qk^{\prime}}G_{k+q}G_{k^{\prime}}G_{k^{\prime}+q}\left[\Lambda^{U,\text{ch}}_{k,k+q,k^{\prime}-k}+\Lambda^{U,\text{sp}}_{k,k+q,k^{\prime}-k}\right].

(31)

From the first to the second line we used the crossing symmetry (
30
).
After suitable shifts of the momentum-frequency summations we find
R_{k}=-R_{k}=0
and the Fierz ambiguity is resolved.

We set
r=\frac{1}{2}
in Eq. (
27
), without loss of generality, leading to Eq. (
9
) in the main text,
which has the only advantage over other choices of
r
that the slowly converging Matsubara sum
for the Hartree energy,
{U\langle n\rangle}/{2}
, is separated explicitly from the vertex correction
\gamma
.

More generally, the Fierz parameter
r
drops out in any crossing-symmetric approximation for the full vertex
F
,
which eliminates
r
already from Eq. (
28
).
As shown, in the eMBEX approach the crossing symmetry of
F
is satisfied if it holds on the level of the fully
U
-irreducible vertex
\Lambda^{U}
. One shows similarly that the Fierz ambiguity is resolved by the parquet approach
[
48
,
11
,
78
,
17
]
, where the crossing symmetry of
F
can be traced back to the corresponding fully
GG
-irreducible vertex function.

Appendix D
Symmetries

Figure 13:
Benchmark results for the half-filled Hubbard model at
U=4t
and
\beta t=5
. In this particular case, only eMBEX calculations using the irreducible vertex from
\text{DCA}_{4^{*}}
could be converged on the large
26\times 26
lattice of the main text. By using a linear preconditioning technique
[
71
,
70
]
we converged
\text{DCA}_{4}
results on smaller lattices (here:
6\times 6
).

Besides the spin-rotation invariance, which was discussed in the main text, the Hubbard model possesses several additional symmetries which can be taken advantage of. Here, we show the effect of complex conjugation, time-reversal (TRS) and crossing symmetry on the self-energy, screened interactions, Hedin and irreducible vertices. A more detailed overview about vertex symmetries in the Hubbard model, including their derivation, can be found in Refs.
[
79
,
7
]
.

Under complex conjugation the single-particle Green’s function behaves as

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{\bm{k}\nu}=G_{\bm{k},-\nu}\,,

(32)

which straightforwardly carries over to the self-energy,
{\overline{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}\nu}=\Sigma_{\bm{k},-\nu}}
. Time-reversal symmetry, on the other hand, does not yield extra constraints on the one-particle level. For the screened interactions
W^{\alpha}
the situation is similar, and we only exploit that

\displaystyle\overline{W}^{\alpha}_{\bm{q}\omega}=W^{\alpha}_{\bm{q},-\omega}\,.

(33)

The Hedin vertices, in contrast, transform non-trivially under time-reversal. In particular, TRS permutes the fermionic four-vectors for any mixed momentum/frequency notation
[
79
]
, which implies that only one of the two Hedin vertices per channel (see Eq. (
2
)) needs to be considered and we, henceforth, limit ourself to
\gamma^{\alpha}
. Complex conjugation and crossing symmetry on the other hand imply that

\displaystyle\gamma^{\text{ch}/\text{sp}}_{kq}

\displaystyle=\overline{\gamma}^{\text{ch}/\text{sp}}_{-k,-q}

\displaystyle\gamma^{\text{ch}/\text{sp}}_{kq}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\text{ch}/\text{sp}}_{q+k,-q}\,.

(34)

for the charge/spin channel, whereas the singlet channel transform as

\displaystyle\gamma^{\text{s}}_{kq}

\displaystyle=\overline{\gamma}^{\text{s}}_{-k,-q}

\displaystyle\gamma^{\text{s}}_{kq}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\text{s}}_{q-k,q}\,,

(35)

For the irreducible vertex, complex conjugation and time-reversal symmetry yield the identities

\displaystyle\overline{\Lambda^{U,\alpha}}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}\bm{q}\nu\nu^{\prime}\omega}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{U,\alpha}_{\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k},\bm{q},-\nu^{\prime},-\nu,-\omega}

(36)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{U,\alpha}_{kk^{\prime}q}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{U,\alpha}_{k^{\prime}kq}\,.

(37)

Since the crossing symmetry can be applied to the incoming or outgoing legs of
\Lambda^{U,\alpha}
we find that

\displaystyle\Lambda^{U,\alpha}_{kk^{\prime}q}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\Lambda^{U,\text{ch}}_{k,q+k,k^{\prime}-k}+\left[3-4\delta_{\alpha,\text{sp}}\right]\Lambda^{U,\text{sp}}_{k,q+k,k^{\prime}-k}\right)

(38)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\Lambda^{U,\text{ch}}_{q+k^{\prime},k^{\prime},k-k^{\prime}}+\left[3-4\delta_{\alpha,\text{sp}}\right]\Lambda^{U,\text{sp}}_{q+k^{\prime},k^{\prime},k-k^{\prime}}\right)\,,

(39)

for
\alpha\in\{\text{ch},\text{sp}\}
, whereas for the singlet channel

\displaystyle\Lambda^{U,\text{s}}_{kk^{\prime}q}=\Lambda^{U,\text{s}}_{q-k,k^{\prime},q}=\Lambda^{U,\text{s}}_{k,q-k^{\prime},q}\,.

(40)

Note that all these functions are also invariant when transforming all of their respective momentum arguments according to the point group symmetries of the lattice under consideration. For the square lattice with point group
C_{4v}
, we implement
\tfrac{\pi}{2}
-rotation,
{(\bm{k}_{x},\bm{k}_{y})\to(-\bm{k}_{y},\bm{k}_{x})}
, and mirror symmetry about the
\tfrac{\pi}{4}
-diagonal,
{(\bm{k}_{x},\bm{k}_{y})\to(\bm{k}_{y},\bm{k}_{x})}
, since these operations are sufficient to generate the irreducible wedge of the Brillouin zone.

Since the result of our QMC simulation is exact up to numerical noise, all symmetries are fulfilled within the sampling error. Therefore, symmetries can be utilized to optimize the Monte Carlo data for the cluster and, of course, to reduce the numerical effort for converging the SBE equations on the lattice. For most of the relations discussed above, the implementation is straightforward. To enforce the crossing symmetry, however, we have to take into account that it can be used on the incoming legs, outgoing legs
,
or both pairs of legs of the irreducible vertex. As is apparent from Eqs. (
38
), (
39
) and (
40
), each of these operations yields a separate relation between vertex components and as such, we have to factor in all of them in our symmetrization procedure. For the charge and spin channel, the symmetrized vertex reads:

\displaystyle\Lambda^{U,\alpha}_{kk^{\prime}q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\Lambda}^{U,\alpha}_{kk^{\prime}q}+\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\Lambda}^{U,\alpha}_{q+k^{\prime},q+k,-q}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}\left(\tilde{\Lambda}^{U,\text{ch}}_{k,q+k,k^{\prime}-k}+\left[3-4\delta_{\alpha,\text{sp}}\right]\tilde{\Lambda}^{U,\text{sp}}_{k,q+k,k^{\prime}-k}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}\left(\tilde{\Lambda}^{U,\text{ch}}_{q+k^{\prime},k^{\prime},k-k^{\prime}}+\left[3-4\delta_{\alpha,\text{sp}}\right]\tilde{\Lambda}^{U,\text{sp}}_{q+k^{\prime},k^{\prime},k-k^{\prime}}\right)\,,

(41)

where
\tilde{\Lambda}^{U,\alpha}
here corresponds to the
raw
vertex from QMC. For the singlet channel we likewise have

\displaystyle\Lambda^{U,\text{s}}_{kk^{\prime}q}=\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\Lambda}^{U,\text{s}}_{kk^{\prime}q}+\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\Lambda}^{U,\text{s}}_{q-k,q-k^{\prime},q}+\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\Lambda}^{U,\text{s}}_{q-k,k^{\prime},q}+\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\Lambda}^{U,\text{s}}_{k,q-k^{\prime},q}\,.

(42)

Note that once the cluster vertex is symmetrized this way, our lattice parametrization from Sec.
III
likewise obeys the crossing symmetry, which makes our eMBEX implementation manifestly agnostic to the Fierz ambiguity (see the discussion in App.
C
).

Figure

Appendix F
Magnetic correlations in single-site eMBEX

We discuss the diagrammatic content of eMBEX using a local
\Lambda^{U}
as input,

\Lambda^{U,\text{lat}}_{\bm{k}\bm{k^{\prime}}\bm{q}\nu\nu^{\prime}\omega}\approx{\Lambda}^{U,\text{loc}}_{\nu\nu^{\prime}\omega}
.
First, we note that this approximation does not yield the same nonlocal two-particle correlations as DMFT,
which are obtained through inversion of the Bethe-Salpeter equation
[
1
,
80
,
81
]
.
Instead, due to its crossing symmetry, eMBEX with
N_{c}=1

recovers some nonlocal two-particle correlations which
are not
included
in DMFT, while it also neglects others that
are
included in DMFT.

In particular, the
N_{c}=1
approximation does
not

recover the nearest-neighbor effective exchange energy scale
J=4t^{2}/U
in the strong-coupling limit, which we can see as follows.
In DMFT,
J
arises from the polarization diagram shown on the left of Fig.
14

[
82
,
81
]
.
Here, triangles denote the local spin-fermion coupling
\gamma^{\text{sp},\text{loc}}_{\nu\omega}
,
arrows the nonlocal DMFT Green’s function
\tilde{G}_{\bm{k}\nu}=G^{\text{DMFT}}_{\bm{k}\nu}-g^{\text{loc}}_{\nu}
,
where
g^{\text{loc}}
is the local Green’s funcion of the DMFT’s effective Anderson impurity model.

It is clear that diagrams included in
{\Lambda}^{U,\text{loc}}
contain only local propagators
g^{\text{loc}}
.
However, the diagram on the left of Fig.
14
contains four-point vertex corrections such as the one shown on the right,
where local single-boson exchange (see dashed boxes) is connected via nonlocal lines
\tilde{G}
;
wiggly lines denote the local screened interaction
w^{\text{sp}}_{\omega}
.
This is a nonlocal two-boson exchange diagram, hence, it is not included in
{\Lambda}^{U,\text{loc}}
.
For this reason, while in the benchmarks we show this approximation for reference and comparison,
a cluster size of
N_{c}>1
is mandatory in practical applications.

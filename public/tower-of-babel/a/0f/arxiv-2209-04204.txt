Hamiltonian Complete Number of some variants of Caterpillar graphs
Tayo Charles Adefokun 1, Kingsley Nosa Onaiwu 2

Abstract
. A graph G G is said to be Hamiltonian if it contains a spanning cycle. In other words, G G contains a cycle that passes through all the the vertices in the vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) of G G . A graph that does not contain a spanning cycle is a non-Hamiltonian graph. In this work, we investigate the the Hamiltonian completeness of certain trees. The Hamiltonian complete number λ H ​ ( G ) \lambda_{H}(G) of G G , a non-Hamiltonian graph is the optimal number of edges that if added to the edge set E ⁡ ( G ) E(G) of G G , then G G becomes Hamiltonian. Our focus is on certain classes of caterpillar graphs.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a graph on with vertex sets
V(G)
and edge set set
E(G)
. A Hamiltonian cycle on a
G
is a cycle that visits every vertex on
V(G)
exactly once. Therefore, we say that

is Hamiltonian if it contains a spanning cycle. Hamiltonian problem is the problem of determining if a graph contains a spanning cycle or not. This problem is
NP-
complete, and the complete characterization of Hamiltonian graph has not being obtained. Some even believe that such characterization in non-existent. Furthermore, the only available technique of determining if a graph contains a spanning cycle is by exhaustive searching.

Graphs, such as complete graphs, cycle graphs, tournament on odd number vertices
[
8
]
are all Hamiltonian graphs. All Hamiltonian graphs are bi-connected, which means that at least two edges will be removed from the graph in other to dissolve it into at least two components. However, not every by bi-connected graph is Hamiltonian. Attempts have been made on characterization of Hamiltonian graphs and the best known results are vertex degree characterization. Bondy-Chvatal in
[
3
]
observed that a graph is Hamiltonian if and only if its closure is Hamiltonian. Therefore, any graph whose closure is complete is Hamiltonian. (The closure of graph
G
on
n
vertices is obtained when every pair of non-adjacent vertices are connected by an edge not in
E(G)
. According to Dirac
[
4
]
, a simple graph
G
on
n-
vertices is Hamiltonian if
d(v)\geq\frac{n}{2}
for all
v\in V(G)
, and
n\geq 3
, while in
[
7
]
Ore showed that for any simple graph
G
of order
n\geq 3
,
G
is Hamiltonian if for all pair of non adjacent vertices, the sum of their degree is a least
n
. It was also observed on
[
6
]
that all
4-
connected planner triangulation contains a spanning cycle, while Tutte improved on this in
[
9
]
by showing that every
4-
connected Planner graph is in fact Hamiltonian.

Graphs that do not contain a spanning cycle is known as non-Hamiltonian graph. Such, trivially, is any
1-
connected graph or any graph that contains a leaf or a pendant. Paths, trees etc are non-Hamiltonian graphs. In
[
2
]
a result is obtained that showed some conditions for which a graph will not be Hamiltonian Also,
[
5
]
investigates a non-Hamiltonian
3-
connected cubic bipartite graph, where non-Hamiltonian cyclical 4-edge connected bi-cubic graph was constructed on
54
vertices.

In this work, we continue the study into a new thought on non-Hamiltonian graph which the first author introduced in
[
1
]
, which was motivated by the importance of Hamiltonian graphs in computer graphics, mapping, electronic circuitry, route planning and so on. It was asked in
[
1
]
that how many edges can be added to the
E(G)
of graph
G
in other to make
G
Hamiltonian? Trivially, for a Hamiltonian graph, the number is zero. The optimal number of such edges is called the Hamiltonian complete number
\lambda_{H}(G)
of graph
G
. The Hamiltonian complete number was investigated for some basic graphs in
[
1
]
.

In this work, we further look at other structurally non-Hamiltonian graphs. The
\lambda_{H}(G)-
numbers for various caterpillar graphs are obtained.

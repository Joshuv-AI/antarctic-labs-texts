Abstract
. In this paper, we establish estimates for the oscillation seminorm for the so-called Carleson–Dunkl operator on weighted L p ​ ( ℝ , w ⁡ ( x ) ​ | x | 2 ​ α + 1 ​ d ​ x ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w(x)|x|^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x) spaces with power weights w ⁡ ( x ) = | x | β w(x)=|x|^{\beta} . As a result, we obtain oscillation estimates for the standard Carleson operator on L rad p ​ ( ℝ n , | x | β ​ d ​ x ) L_{\rm rad}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x) . As a byproduct, we obtain a transference principle for radial multipliers on L rad p L_{\rm rad}^{p} spaces, in the spirit of the Rubio de Francia transference principle.

1.
Introduction and statement of results

In this paper, we establish a uniform oscillation inequality on weighted
L^{p}
spaces for the partial sums of the Dunkl transform. As a result, we are able to derive a uniform oscillation inequality for the standard Fourier transform on the real line and its higher-dimensional counterpart on radial functions. These results can be seen as a generalization of the oscillation inequality proven by Lacey and Terwilleger
[
17
]
. To establish our results, we utilize the recent observations made by Mirek, Szarek, and Wright
[
19
]
regarding oscillation estimates of projection operators. This approach allows us to bypass time-frequency analysis and use the existing estimates instead. To apply these estimates, we employ techniques developed by Stempak and Trebels
[
32
]
to transfer the estimates for the multipliers between Dunkl transforms of different orders. As a byproduct, we obtain a ’generalization’ of the Rubio de Francia transference principle for radial multipliers from
[
29
]
.

1.1.
Statement of results

Fix
\alpha\geq-1/2
and let
f
be a suitable function on
\mathbb{R}
.
The Dunkl transform
of order
\alpha
of function
f
is defined as follows:

(1.1)

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\frac{1}{2(yx)^{\alpha}}\big(J_{\alpha}(yx)-iJ_{\alpha+1}(yx)\big)|y|^{2\alpha+1}\,\mathrm{d}y,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

where
J_{\alpha}
is the Bessel function of the first kind of order
\alpha
. The Dunkl transform was introduced by Dunkl
[
5
]
as a generalization of the usual Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
related to some group of reflections. In our case, we will be exploring the one-dimensional case. Simple calculations based on the formulas for
J_{1/2}
and
J_{-1/2}
show that
\mathcal{D}_{-1/2}=\mathcal{F}
, where

(1.2)

\mathcal{F}f(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)e^{-ixy}\,\mathrm{d}y,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

is the Fourier transform. For more details about the Dunkl theory, we refer the reader to
[
24
,
25
]
and the references therein.

For
1\leq p<\infty
,
\alpha\geq-1/2
and non-negative weight
w
on
\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w|x|^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)
the space of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}
for which

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w|x|^{2\alpha+1}\mathrm{d}x)}:=\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)|x|^{2\alpha+1}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

The Dunkl transform
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
extends uniquely to an isometric isomorphism on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{2\alpha+1}\mathrm{d}x)
which satisfies the Plancherel identity

\|\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{2\alpha+1}\mathrm{d}x)}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)},\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{2\alpha+1}\mathrm{d}x).

Furthermore, the inverse Dunkl transform of order
\alpha
is defined as

\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}f(x):=\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}f(-x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then for
f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we have
(\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\alpha})f=(\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}\circ\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha})f=f
. Simple proofs of these facts may be found in
[
22
, Proposition 1.3]
.

In this paper, we will be interested in the partial sums of the Dunkl transform
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
. Namely, for any
f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we define the Dunkl transform partial sum operators

\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\alpha}f(x):=\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}\big(\mathds{1}_{[-t,t]}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}f\big)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
\mathbb{I}
be any subset of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
For a given increasing sequence
I=(I_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N})\subseteq\mathbb{I}
,
the
truncated oscillation seminorm
is defined for any family of measurable functions

(\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x):t>0)
with values in
\mathbb{C}
, by setting

(1.3)

\displaystyle O_{I,J}^{2}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x):t\in\mathbb{I})=\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{j}\leq t<I_{j+1}\\
t\in\mathbb{I}\end{subarray}}\left|\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x)-\mathfrak{a}_{I_{j}}(x)\right|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\quad\text{for all }J\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

For
J\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, we denote by
\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{I})
the family of all strictly increasing sequences of length
J+1
contained in
\mathbb{I}
. Furthermore, we denote by
\mathbb{D}:=\{2^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
the set of dyadic numbers.

Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\alpha\geq-1/2
and let
p\in[2,\infty)
. Assume that
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
is such that

-1<\beta+(\alpha+1/2)(2-p)<p/2-1.

When
p=2
, we additionally allow
\beta=0
. Then we have

\sup_{J\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{I\in\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\left\|O_{I,J}^{2}\big(\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\alpha}f:t>0\big)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\,|x|^{\beta+2\alpha+1}\,\mathrm{d}x)}\lesssim_{p,\alpha,\beta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\,|x|^{\beta+2\alpha+1}\,\mathrm{d}x)},

for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\,|x|^{\beta+2\alpha+1}\,\mathrm{d}x)
.
When
\beta=0
then range specified above can be translated into

2\leq p<\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha+1}.

In the special case
\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}
, the right-hand side is understood to be
+\infty
.

Furthermore, if
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies

(1.5)

-1<\beta+(\alpha+1/2)(2-p)<p-1,

then one has

\sup_{J\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{I\in\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{D})}\big\|O_{I,J}^{2}\big(\mathcal{S}_{2^{n}}^{\alpha}f:n\in\mathbb{Z}\big)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta+2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{p,\alpha,\beta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta+2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)},

for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta+2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)
. When
\beta=0
the condition (
1.5
) can be translated into

\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha+3}<p<\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha+1}.

In the special case
\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}
, the right-hand side is understood to be
+\infty
.

Since the one-dimensional Dunkl transform can be expressed in terms of the Hankel transform, and the latter is related to the Fourier transform of radial functions, we can use Theorem
1.4
to derive an oscillation inequality for the partial sums of the Fourier transform over balls for radial functions.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
n\geq 1
be a positive integer and let
p\in[2,\infty)
. Assume that
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies

-1<\beta+(n-1)(1-p/2)<p/2-1.

When
p=2
, we additionally allow
\beta=0
. Then we have

\sup_{J\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{I\in\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\big\|O_{I,J}^{2}\big(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathds{1}_{B(0,t)}\mathcal{F}f):t>0\big)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{p,n,\beta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)},

for any radial function
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)
. Furthermore, if
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

-1<\beta+(n-1)(1-p/2)<p-1,

then we have

\sup_{J\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{I\in\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{D})}\big\|O_{I,J}^{2}\big(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathds{1}_{B(0,2^{m})}\mathcal{F}f):m\in\mathbb{Z}\big)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{p,n,\beta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)},

for any radial function
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)
.

In the case when
n=1
, we can strengthen our results, but first, let us recall the definition of the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
. For
p\in(1,\infty)
, the weight
w\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
is in the
A_{p}
class if

\sup_{B}\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x){\rm d}x\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{-p^{\prime}/p}{\rm d}x\Big)^{p/p^{\prime}}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all intervals
B\subset\mathbb{R}
and
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
. These classes, introduced by Muckenhoupt in
[
20
]
, serve to characterize the boundedness of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal functions on weighted Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p/2}
. Then we have

\sup_{J\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{I\in\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\big\|O_{I,J}^{2}\big(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathds{1}_{[-t,t]}\mathcal{F}f):t>0\big)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{p,w}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)},

for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)
. In the case of the power weight
w(x)=|x|^{\beta}
we additionally allow
p=2
. Furthermore if,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}
, then we have

\sup_{J\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{I\in\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{D})}\big\|O_{I,J}^{2}\big(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathds{1}_{[-2^{n},2^{n}]}\mathcal{F}f):n\in\mathbb{Z}\big)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{p,w}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)},

for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)
.

The above result is a generalization of the result proven by Lacey and Terwilleger
[
17
]
to the setting of weighted spaces with weights from
A_{p}
. It is believed that the oscillation seminorm is some kind of endpoint of the
r
-variation seminorm corresponding to
r=2
, although currently, we do not know the exact nature of that endpoint. Recall that for any
r\geq 1
, the
r
-variation seminorm is defined for any family of measurable functions
(\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x):t>0)
by setting

\displaystyle V^{r}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x):t>0)=\sup_{J\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{0}<\cdots<t_{J}\\
t_{j}>0\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\big|\mathfrak{a}_{t_{j+1}}(x)-\mathfrak{a}_{t_{j}}(x)\big|^{r}\right)^{1/r}.

For more details, we refer to
[
18
]
. In this sense, our result can be seen as a closure of the variational estimates obtained by Di Plinio, Do, and Uraltsev
[
4
, Theorem 2]
, who established that for any
r\in(2,\infty)
,
p>r^{\prime}
, where
1/r+1/r^{\prime}=1
, and any
w\in A_{p/r^{\prime}}
, one has

\big\|V^{r}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathds{1}_{[-t,t]}\mathcal{F}f):t>0)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\lesssim_{p,w}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

Theorem 1.8

(Rubio de Francia transference theorem for radial multipliers)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
-1<\beta<p-1
be such that the sequence of even functions
(m_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a vector-valued bounded multiplier on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)
, that is

(1.9)

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m_{k}\mathcal{F}f)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}.

Then for any
n\geq 1
we have

(1.10)

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m_{k}(|\cdot|)\mathcal{F}f)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|^{\beta^{*}}{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{n}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|^{\beta^{*}}{\rm d}x)},

for any radial function
f\in L_{\rm rad}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)
, where
\beta^{*}:=\beta-(n-1)(1-p/2)
and
|x|
is the standard Euclidean norm of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The classical Rubio de Francia transference theorem
[
29
, Theorem 2.1]
states that for
p=2
and
-1<\beta<1
the inequality (
1.9
) implies that (
1.10
) holds for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)
. Our result is a generalization of this result to
p\neq 2
. In such a general setting, we can consider the subset of radial functions only, as shown by the famous disc multiplier theorem proven by Fefferman
[
7
]
. It is worth noting that our proof relies heavily on the nature of radial functions and does not cover the original Rubio de Francia result.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Dunkl and Hankel transforms

The Dunkl transform on the real line can be seen as a symmetrization of the Hankel transform. First, recall that for given
\alpha\geq-1/2

the Hankel transform
of order
\alpha
of a suitable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}f(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}{f(y)\frac{J_{\alpha}(xy)}{(xy)^{\alpha}}y^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}y},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Then the Dunkl transform
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
can be expressed as

(2.1)

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}f(x)=\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}f_{\rm e}(|x|)-ix\mathcal{H}_{\alpha+1}\big(f_{\rm o}/(\cdot)\big)(|x|),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

In analogy to the Dunkl transform, for any
f\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we define the partial sums of the Hankel transform as

\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{t}^{\alpha}f(x):={\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}\big(\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}f\big)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Having defined the partial sum operators, we may define the corresponding maximal functions. For
f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we define the
Carleson–Dunkl
operator as

(2.2)

\mathcal{C}_{\ast}^{\alpha}f(x):=\sup_{t>0}|\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\alpha}f|(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Similarly, we define the
Carleson–Hankel
operator as

(2.3)

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\ast}^{\alpha}f(x):=\sup_{t>0}|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{t}^{\alpha}f|(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

The
L^{p}
boundedness of the Carleson–Hankel operator was established by Kanjin
[
14
]
and independently by Prestini
[
23
]
at the end of the 80s.

Theorem 2.4

(Kanjin–Prestini)
.

Let
\alpha\geq-1/2
and let
p\in(1,\infty)
satisfy

(2.5)

\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha+3}<p<\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha+1}.

There exists a constant
C_{p,\alpha}>0
such that for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},x^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)
, we have

(2.6)

\big\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\ast}^{\alpha}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},x^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)}\leq C_{p,\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},x^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)}.

Of particular interest to us will be the approach discovered by Prestini
[
23
]
. Namely, in order to prove (
2.6
), she provided a pointwise estimate of the Hankel transform partial sums
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)
in terms of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function, the maximal Hilbert transform, the conjugate of the Hardy operator, and the classical Carleson–Hunt operator.

Theorem 2.7

(Prestini)
.

Let
\alpha\geq-1/2
and let
f\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. There exists a constant
C_{\alpha}>0
such that

\big|\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}\big(\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}f\big)(|x|)\big|\leq C_{\alpha}|x|^{-(\alpha+1/2)}(M_{\rm HL}+H+\mathcal{H}_{\ast}+\mathcal{C})((\cdot)^{\alpha+1/2}f)(|x|),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

where

(2.8)

M_{\rm HL}f(x):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{2r}\int_{-r}^{r}|f(x-y)|{\rm d}y,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

is the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function,

(2.9)

Hf(x):=\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\frac{|f(y)|}{y}{\rm d}y,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

is the conjugate Hardy operator,

(2.10)

\mathcal{H}_{\ast}f(x):=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\Bigg|\int_{|y|>\varepsilon}\frac{f(x-y)}{y}{\rm d}y\Bigg|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

is the maximal Hilbert transform, and

(2.11)

\mathcal{C}f(x):=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|\int_{|y|>\varepsilon}{e^{i\xi y}}\frac{f(x-y)}{y}{\rm d}y\Bigg|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}

is the Carleson–Hunt maximal operator. The function
f
on the right-hand side of the statement is understood as a function on
\mathbb{R}
, defined by setting
f(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
0
otherwise.

Several years following Prestini’s work, Romera and Soria
[
27
]
provided an alternative proof of the aforementioned theorem. Prestini’s estimate plays a pivotal role in our paper, enabling us to work with well-established operators (
2.8
)–(
2.11
) instead of
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}^{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\ast}^{\alpha}
.

In 2007, El Kamel and Yacoub
[
6
]
utilized the formula (
2.1
) together with Prestini’s estimate and proved the
L^{p}
boundedness of the Carleson–Dunkl operator
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}^{\alpha}
.

Theorem 2.12

(El Kamel–Yacoub)
.

Let
\alpha\geq-1/2
and let
p\in(1,\infty)
satisfy

(2.13)

\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha+3}<p<\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha+1}.

There exists a constant
C_{p,\alpha}>0
such that for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)
, we have

(2.14)

\big\|\mathcal{C}_{\ast}^{\alpha}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)}\leq C_{p,\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)}.

2.2.
Weighted estimates with weights from Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}

The next theorem is well-known, but we will provide its “proof” to give relevant references.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
w\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be a weight from the
A_{p}
class. Let
T
be one of the operators (
2.8
)–(
2.11
). Then there exists a constant
C_{w,p}:=C_{w,p}(T)>0
such that the weighted estimate

(2.16)

\big\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)}\leq C_{w,p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)},\quad f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x),

holds.

Proof.

The weighted inequality for the maximal function
M_{\rm HL}
is the primary reason for considering
A_{p}
classes, and it was proven by Muckenhoupt
[
20
]
. The inequality (
2.16
) for the conjugate Hardy operator
H
follows by duality and by the fact that
w\in A_{p}
if and only if
w^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A^{p^{\prime}}
where
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
. Namely, one has

\displaystyle\|Hf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{g:\,\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},w^{1-p^{\prime}}{\rm d}x)}\leq 1}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}Hf(x)g(x){\rm d}x\bigg|

\displaystyle=\sup_{g:\,\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},w^{1-p^{\prime}}{\rm d}x)}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(y)|\frac{1}{y}\int_{|x|<y}|g(x)|{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)}\|M_{\rm HL}g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},w^{1-p^{\prime}}{\rm d}x)}.

For the maximal Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}_{\ast}
, it follows from the general theory of weighted inequalities for singular integrals, see
[
9
, Chapter 9]
. Finally, the weighted estimates for the Carleson operator
\mathcal{C}
were proven by Hunt and Young
[
13
]
.
∎

Now, let
w\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be a weight such that

(2.17)

w(x)|x|^{2\alpha+1-p(\alpha+1/2)}\in A_{p}.

The set of such weights will be called
A_{p}^{\alpha}
. Obviously,
A_{p}^{-1/2}=A_{p}
. The following result is a straightforward generalization of Theorem
2.4
.

Proposition 2.18

(Weighted Kanjin–Prestini estimate)
.

Let
\alpha\geq-1/2
and let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
w\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be an even weight from
A_{p}^{\alpha}
. There exists a constant
C_{p,\alpha,w}>0
such that for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wx^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)
, we have

(2.19)

\big\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\ast}^{\alpha}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wx^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)}\leq C_{p,\alpha,w}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wx^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)}.

Proof.

This result follows from Theorems

Proposition 2.20

(Weighted Carleson–Dunkl estimate)

The proof is a simple repetition of the arguments given in
[

. Below, we provide a sketch of the proof. Let

be an even weight, and let

The above result seems to be new despite the fact that its proof is a trivial adaptation of well-known arguments.

2.3.
Modified Dunkl and Hankel transform

Recall that for given
\alpha\geq-1/2

the modified Hankel transform
of order
\alpha
of a suitable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by

(2.25)

H_{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{f(y)(xy)^{1/2}J_{\alpha}(xy){\rm d}x},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Motivated by the formula (
2.1
) Nowak and Stempak
[
22
]
have introduced
the modified Dunkl transform
as

(2.26)

D_{\alpha}f(x):=H_{\alpha}(f_{\rm e})(|x|)-i\operatorname{sgn}(x)H_{\alpha+1}(f_{\rm o})(|x|),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

The modified Dunkl transform exhibits similar properties to the standard one. Namely, the operator
D_{\alpha}
extends uniquely to an isometric isomorphism on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{d}x)
, which satisfies the Plancherel identity

\|D_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{d}x)}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{d}x)},\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{d}x).

Moreover, its inverse is given by

\widecheck{D}_{\alpha}f(x):=D_{\alpha}f(-x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then for
f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we have
(\widecheck{D}_{\alpha}\circ D_{\alpha})f=(D_{\alpha}\circ\widecheck{D}_{\alpha})f=f
. Proofs of these facts may be found in
[
22
, Proposition 1.3]
.

The standard Dunkl transform and its modified counterpart are related to each other by the following relation:

(2.27)

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}=M_{-(\alpha+1/2)}\circ D_{\alpha}\circ M_{\alpha+1/2},

where for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
M_{a}f(x):=|x|^{a}f(x)
is the multiplication operator.

The key feature of the modified Dunkl transform is the fact that for any
\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{2}
, the operator
D_{\alpha}
acts on the same space, in contrast to the standard Dunkl transform, which requires the measure
|x|^{2\alpha+1}\,\mathrm{d}x
.

|\mathcal{C}_{\ast}^{\alpha}f_{n}|(x)\lesssim_{\alpha}|x|^{-(\alpha+1/2)}\big(K((\cdot)^{\alpha+1/2})f_{n,{\rm e}})+K((\cdot)^{\alpha+1/2})f_{n,{\rm o}})\big)(|x|),

(3.1)

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|Kf_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w|x|^{2\alpha+1-p(\alpha+1/2)}{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{\alpha,w}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w|x|^{2\alpha+1-p(\alpha+1/2)}{\rm d}x)},

(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w|x|^{2\alpha+1-p(\alpha+1/2)}{\rm d}x;\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}))}
. Since the operator
K
is the sum of four operators, it is sufficient to show an appropriate estimate for each component. Let
w\in A_{p}
. The weighted vector-valued estimate for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|M_{\rm HL}f_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)}\leq C_{p,w}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)}

[

[

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|Hf_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)}\leq C_{p,w}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)}

) we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{t>0}|\mathcal{S}_{t}f_{m}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},w{\rm d}x)}

\displaystyle=c_{n,p}(2\pi)^{n}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{t}^{\alpha}f_{m,0}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wx^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)},

with
\alpha=(n-2)/2
. Since
w\in A_{p}^{\alpha}
, by Corollary
3.2
, the latter is bounded by

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{m,0}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w|x|^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{n}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{m}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},w{\rm d}x)},

where the last inequality follows by (
3.5
). This ends the proof of (
3.4
).
∎

3.2.
Rubio de Francia estimate (
2.36
) and transference of radial multipliers

To establish (
2.36
), we use the ideas presented by Stempak and Trebels
[
32
]
and show that we can derive estimates for
\alpha>-1/2
from the estimate for
\alpha=-1/2
. In order to do so, we use the so-called transplantation inequalities for the modified Dunkl transform. Nowak and Stempak
[
22
]
introduced the Dunkl transplantation operator

T_{\alpha\gamma}:=\widecheck{D}_{\alpha}\circ D_{\gamma},

for
\alpha,\gamma\geq-1/2
,
\alpha\neq\gamma
. Here we remind that
D_{\alpha}
is the modified Dunkl transform (
2.26
). Analogously, one may define the Hankel transplantation by setting

\widetilde{T}_{\alpha\gamma}:=H_{\alpha}\circ H_{\gamma},

for the same range of
\alpha
and
\gamma
as before. Here
H_{\alpha}
is the modified Hankel transform (
2.25
). The Hankel transplantation operators were introduced by Guy
[
11
]
, who proved the first transplantation inequality.

Let
w\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be an even non-negative weight. We are interested whenever the transplantation estimate

\big\|T_{\alpha\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{p,\alpha,\gamma,w}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w{\rm d}x)}

holds. Nowak and Stempak
[
22
, Proposition 2.1]
observed that the above inequality holds if and only if

\big\|\widetilde{T}_{\alpha\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{p,\alpha,\gamma,w}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w{\rm d}x)}

and if the analogous inequality with
\alpha
and
\gamma
replaced by
\alpha+1
and
\gamma+1
holds. In our case, we will be interested in a particular type of weights, namely power weights given by
w(x)=|x|^{\beta}
. Therefore, we want to know whenever

(3.6)

\big\|T_{\alpha\gamma}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}\leq C_{p,\alpha,\gamma,\beta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}

holds. From the above discussion, this problem can be reduced to establishing the transplantation inequalities for the Hankel transplantation operator with parameters
\alpha,\gamma
and
\alpha+1,\gamma+1
. Stempak
[
31
, Collorary 1.4]
showed that the inequality

\big\|\widetilde{T}_{\alpha\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{p,\alpha,\gamma,\beta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}

holds if one has

(3.7)

-p(\alpha+1/2)-1<\beta<p(\gamma+3/2)-1.

Consequently, the estimate (
3.6
) holds if condition (
3.7
) is satisfied. Since
T_{\alpha\gamma}
is a linear operator, by the
\ell^{2}
-extension theorem
[
8
, Theorem 5.5.1]
, we obtain the following vector-valued estimate

(3.8)

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|T_{\alpha\gamma}f_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}\leq C_{p,\alpha,\gamma,\beta}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}

holds if the condition (
3.7
) is satisfied.

The next result is a technical tool which guarantees that we may work with smooth compactly supported functions only. The proof is easy and is based on the similar result proven by Stempak and Trebels
[
32
]
in the context of the Hankel transform.

Fact 3.9
.

Assume
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\beta>-1-p(\alpha+1/2)
. Then the set
D_{\alpha}(C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}))
is dense in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Consider
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)
and split it into its even and odd components,
f=f_{\rm e}+f_{\rm o}
. These functions are uniquely defined by their values on the positive real numbers. By
[
32
, Corollary 4.8]
there are functions
g,h\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that

\displaystyle\big\|f_{\rm e}-H_{\alpha}(g)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},x^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}<\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\big\|f_{\rm o}-H_{\alpha+1}(h)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},x^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}<\varepsilon.

Now, let us define a new function on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
as

\widetilde{f}(x):=g(|x|)+i\operatorname{sgn}(x)h(|x|),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

Clearly,
\widetilde{f}\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
and it is easy to see that one has

\widetilde{f}_{\rm e}(x)=g(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{f}_{\rm o}(x)=ih(x),\quad\text{for}\quad x>0.

Moreover, by using the fact that the modified Dunkl transform can be expressed in terms of
H_{\alpha}
and
H_{\alpha+1}
, we get

D_{\alpha}\widetilde{f}(x)=H_{\alpha}(g)(|x|)+\operatorname{sgn}(x)H_{\alpha+1}(h)(|x|),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\big\|f-D_{\alpha}\widetilde{f}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|f_{\rm e}-H_{\alpha}(g)(\cdot)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}+\big\|f_{\rm o}-\operatorname{sgn}(\cdot)H_{\alpha}(h)(|\cdot|)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\big\|f_{\rm e}-H_{\alpha}(g)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},x^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}+2\big\|f_{\rm o}-H_{\alpha+1}(h)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},x^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}<4\varepsilon.

This ends the proof.
∎

Definition 3.10
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\alpha\geq-1/2
. Let
(m_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of bounded measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}
. We say that the sequence
(m_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a vector-valued multiplier for the modified Dunkl transform
D_{\alpha}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\widecheck{D}_{\alpha}(m_{n}D_{\alpha}f)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)},\quad f\in{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}.

The set of such multipliers will be denoted by
M_{p}^{\alpha,\beta}
. In a similar way, we define the vector-valued multipliers for the standard Dunkl transform
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}|x|^{2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)
. The set of such multipliers will be denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\alpha,\beta}
.

The following result states that a multiplier for the modified Dunkl transform
D_{\alpha}
is also a multiplier for
D_{\gamma}
, provided that the condition (
3.12
) is satisfied. A corresponding result for scalar multipliers for the modified Hankel transform was proven by Stempak and Trebels
[
32
, Corollary 2.1]
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\alpha,\gamma\geq-1/2
and let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy the following condition

(3.12)

-1-p\min{\big\{\alpha+1/2,\gamma+1/2\big\}}<\beta<-1+p\min{\big\{\alpha+3/2,\gamma+3/2\big\}}.

Then

M_{p}^{\gamma,\beta}=M_{p}^{\alpha,\beta}.

Proof.

Let
f\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
. Assume that
(m_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in M_{p}^{\gamma,\beta}
.
Let us note that
\widecheck{D}_{\alpha}(m_{n}D_{\alpha}f)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, hence we may write that

\widecheck{D}_{\alpha}(m_{n}D_{\alpha}f)=T_{\alpha\gamma}\widecheck{D}_{\gamma}(m_{n}D_{\alpha}f).

As a result we my apply (
3.8
) to get

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\widecheck{D}_{\alpha}(m_{n}D_{\alpha}f)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}\leq C_{p,\alpha,\gamma,\beta}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\widecheck{D}_{\gamma}(m_{n}D_{\alpha}f)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}

since the condition (
3.12
) is satisfied. Now, by using the fact that
D_{\gamma}(\widecheck{D}_{\gamma}g)=g
we may use the fact that
(m_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in M_{p}^{\gamma,\beta}
to estimate the above by

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\widecheck{D}_{\gamma}(m_{n}D_{\gamma}(\widecheck{D}_{\gamma}(D_{\alpha}f)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{p,m,\gamma,\beta}\big\|\widecheck{D}_{\gamma}(D_{\alpha}f)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}.

This shows
M_{p}^{\gamma,\beta}\subseteq M_{p}^{\alpha,\beta}
. To obtain the second part, we exchange the roles of
\alpha
and
\gamma
.
∎

The next fact allows us to connect the multipliers for the modified Dunkl transform
D_{\alpha}
with the multipliers for the standard Dunkl transform
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
.

Fact 3.13
.

Let
\alpha\geq-1/2
. Then
(m_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in M_{p}^{\alpha,\beta}
if and only if
(m_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\alpha,\beta^{*}}
where
\beta^{*}=\beta-(\alpha+1/2)(2-p)
.

Proof.

The proof uses the relation (
2.27
). We repeat the steps used during the proof of
[
22
, Proposition 3.4]
. We omit the details.
∎

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
\alpha,\gamma\geq-1/2
and let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy condition (
3.12
). Then

{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{\alpha,\beta}={\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{\gamma,\beta^{\ast}}.

In particular, when
\alpha=-1/2
, then
\beta^{\ast}=\beta-(1/2+\gamma)(2-p)
, and we have

\mathcal{M}_{p}^{-1/2,\beta}=\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\gamma,\beta^{\ast}}.

Proof.

The proof easily follows from Proposition
3.11
and Fact
3.13
.
∎

In other words, the above corollary implies that for
-1<\beta<p-1

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m_{n}\mathcal{F}f)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}\leq C_{p,m,\beta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta}{\rm d}x)}

implies

(3.15)

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}(m_{n}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}f)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta^{\ast}+2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)}\lesssim_{p,\alpha,\beta}C_{p,m,\beta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|x|^{\beta^{\ast}+2\alpha+1}{\rm d}x)},

where
\beta^{\ast}=\beta-(1/2+\alpha)(2-p)
. Now, we know that the Rubio de Francia estimate (
2.36
) holds when
\alpha=-1/2
and
w\in A_{p/2}
. Consequently, we get that for
w(x)=|x|^{\beta}
the inequality (
2.36
) holds if

-1<\beta^{\ast}+(1/2+\alpha)(2-p)<p/2-1.

In the case of dyadic intervals
I_{j}

, which in the case

-1<\beta^{\ast}+(1/2+\alpha)(2-p)<p-1.

Finally, for

2.36
) holds when
-1<\beta\leq 0
. Which ends the proof of (
2.36
) and consequently, the proof of Theorem

.

Geodesics on metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type
Yekun Yang †, Xiao Zhang ♭

Abstract
. Geodesic equations are solved when at least two of θ \theta , ϕ \phi and ψ \psi are constant, or r r is constant, on scalar flat metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type. They can also be solved also on Eguchi-Hanson metrics which are Ricci flat if only ϕ \phi is constant. However, the explicit solution of the geodesic equations is not available yet if only ψ \psi is constant.



1.
Introduction

Geodesics are the fundamental geometric object in differential geometry. They also reveal nature of gravitational phenomena in curved space-time. In general relativity, null geodesics are interpreted as trajectories of light, time-like geodesics are interpreted as trajectories of massive particles, and they are studied extensively in many exact spacetimes, cf.
[
2
,
3
]
and references therein. Besides enormous study of geodesics in Riemannian manifolds, Battista and Esposito recently investigated geodesic motion on Euclidean Schwarzschild metrics over
R^{2}\times S^{2}
and obtained its explicit form in terms of incomplete elliptic integrals of the first, the second and the third kinds
[
1
]
. As Euclidean Schwarzschild metrics belong to gravitational instantons, which play important roles in Euclidean approach of quantum gravity
[
7
]
, it is interesting to investigate geodesic motion on other gravitational instantons such as Eguchi-Hanson metrics
[
4
,
5
]
. This is the goal of our paper.

Eguchi-Hanson metrics are complete 4-dimensional Ricci flat, anti-self-dual ALE Riemannian metrics on
R_{\geq 0}\times P^{3}
. The more general metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type were constructed by LeBurn
[
10
]
using the method of algebraic geometry
[
10
]
and by the second author solving an ordinary differential equation
[
11
]
. They are scalar flat on
R_{\geq 0}\times S^{3}/Z_{d}

(d>2)
and provide counter-examples of Hawking and Pope’s generalized positive action conjecture
[
9
]
. It is important that gravitational instantons always have removable singularity. And it occurs at the largest positive root of potential functions for metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type. The geodesic completeness has not been proved in the literature for these types of metrics, as pointed out in
[
1
]
. This motivates us to solve geodesic equations passing through the removable singularity.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide a brief introduction to Eguchi-Hanson metrics and scalar flat metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type. We also give geodesic equations for metrics of Eguchi-Hanson with general radial potential functions. In Section
3
, we solve geodesic equations on scalar flat metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type in the following cases: (1) Subsection
3.1
, where
\theta
,
\phi
,
\psi
are constant, geodesics pass through the removable singularity; (2) Subsection
3.2
, where
\phi
,
\psi
are constant, geodesics pass through the removable singularity; (3) Subsection
3.3
, where
\theta
,
\phi
are constant, geodesics do not pass through the removable singularity. If geodesics pass through the removable singularity,
\psi
must be constant and the geodesic equations reduce to Subsection
3.1
; (4) Subsection
3.4
, where
\theta\in(0,\pi)
,
\psi
are constant. It yields that
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
if
\phi
is not constant; (5) Subsection
3.5
, where
r
is constant, geodesics pass through the removable singularity; (6) Subsection
3.6
, where
\phi
is constant. It yields that either
\psi
or
\theta
is constant, and the geodesic equations reduce to either Subsection
3.2
or Subsection
3.3
. In Section
4
, we solve geodesic equations on Eguchi-Hanson metrics, where only
\theta\in(0,\pi)
is constant and geodesics do not pass through the removable singularity. If geodesics pass through the removable singularity, then
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
,
\psi
is constant and the geodesic equations reduce to Subsection
3.4
. In Appendix
5
, we provide an brief introduction to incomplete elliptic integrals of the first, the second and the third kinds.

We point out that the explicit solution of geodesic equations is not available yet if only
\psi
is constant.

2.
Metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type and geodesic equations

In this section, we introduce the metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type and provide their geodesic equations. Let
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
, and
\sigma_{3}
be the Cartan-Maurer one-forms for
SU(2)\cong S^{3}
, defined by

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(\sin\psi d\theta-\sin\theta\cos\psi d\phi\big),

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(-\cos\psi d\theta-\sin\theta\sin\psi d\phi\big),

\displaystyle\sigma_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(d\psi+\cos\theta d\phi\big).

They satisfy

\displaystyle d\sigma_{1}=2\sigma_{2}\wedge\sigma_{3},\quad d\sigma_{2}=2\sigma_{3}\wedge\sigma_{1},\quad d\sigma_{3}=2\sigma_{1}\wedge\sigma_{2}.

Metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type are given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=f^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}\Big(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}+f\sigma_{3}^{2}\Big),

(2.1)

where
f\geq 0
is a smooth function, which is referred as the
potential
function.

If

\displaystyle f=1-\frac{B}{r^{4}}

(2.2)

for some
B>0
, and

\displaystyle r\geq\sqrt[4]{B},\quad 0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi,\quad 0\leqslant\phi\leqslant 2\pi,\quad 0\leq\psi\leq 2\pi,

(2.3)

the metrics (
2.1
) are Eguchi-Hanson metrics
[
4
,
5
]
, which are Ricci flat, geodesically complete and asymptotically local Euclidean, and
\sqrt[4]{B}
is the removable singularity. The underground manifold is, topologically,

\displaystyle R_{\geq 0}\times SU(2)/Z_{2}\cong R_{\geq 0}\times SO(3)\cong R_{\geq 0}\times P_{3}.

Let
n\geq 2
be a natural number. If

\displaystyle f=1-\frac{2A}{r^{2}}-\frac{B}{r^{4}},\quad A=-\frac{n-2}{2}\sqrt{\frac{B}{n-1}},

(2.4)

for some
B>0
, and

\displaystyle r\geq\sqrt[4]{\frac{B}{n-1}},\quad 0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi,\quad 0\leqslant\phi\leqslant 2\pi,\quad 0\leq\psi\leq\frac{4\pi}{n},

(2.5)

the metrics (
2.1
) are geodesically complete, asymptotically local Euclidean, and
\sqrt[4]{\frac{B}{n-1}}
is the removable singularity. The underground manifold is, topologically,

\displaystyle R_{\geq 0}\times SU(2)/Z_{n}\cong R_{\geq 0}\times S^{3}/Z_{n}.

With
A
given by (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle r^{4}-2Ar^{2}-B=\left(r^{2}-\sqrt{\frac{B}{n-1}}\right)\left(r^{2}+\sqrt{(n-1)B}\right).

Thus
\sqrt[4]{\frac{B}{n-1}}
is the largest positive simple root of
f
. This fact is crucial to solve radial geodesics passing through the removable singularity.

The nonvanishing metric components of (
2.1
) are

\displaystyle g_{rr}

\displaystyle=f^{-1},

\displaystyle g^{rr}

\displaystyle=f,

\displaystyle g_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{4},

\displaystyle g^{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle g_{\phi\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{4}\left(f\cos^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta\right),

\displaystyle g^{\phi\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta},

\displaystyle g_{\phi\psi}

\displaystyle=g_{\psi\phi}=\frac{r^{2}}{4}f\cos\theta,

\displaystyle g^{\phi\psi}

\displaystyle=g^{\psi\phi}=-\frac{4\cos\theta}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta},

\displaystyle g_{\psi\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{4}f,

\displaystyle g^{\psi\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{r^{2}}\left(\cot^{2}\theta+\frac{1}{f}\right).

The nonvanishing Christoffel symbols are

\displaystyle\Gamma^{r}_{rr}

\displaystyle=-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{r}_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{r}{4}f,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{r}_{\phi\phi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{f}{4}\left(\frac{\left(r^{2}f\right)^{\prime}}{2}\cos^{2}\theta+r\sin^{2}\theta\right),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{r}_{\phi\psi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\left(r^{2}f\right)^{\prime}}{8f^{-1}}\cos\theta,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{r}_{\psi\psi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{f\left(r^{2}f\right)^{\prime}}{8},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\theta}_{r\theta}

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{\phi}_{r\phi}=\frac{1}{r},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\theta}_{\phi\phi}

\displaystyle=\left(f-1\right)\sin\theta\cos\theta,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\theta}_{\phi\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{f}{2}\sin\theta,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\phi}_{\theta\phi}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{f}{2}\right)\cot\theta,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\phi}_{\theta\psi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{f}{2\sin\theta},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\psi}_{r\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}\cos\theta,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\psi}_{r\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\psi}_{\theta\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(f-1\right)\cos^{2}\theta-1}{2\sin\theta},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\psi}_{\theta\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{f\cot\theta}{2},

Thus geodesic equations of (
2.1
) for parameter
t
are

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}r}{dt^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}\Big(\frac{dr}{dt}\Big)^{2}-\frac{rf}{4}\Big(\frac{d\theta}{dt}\Big)^{2}-\frac{f}{4}\Big(r\sin^{2}\theta+\frac{(r^{2}f)^{\prime}}{2}\cos^{2}\theta\Big)\Big(\frac{d\phi}{dt}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{f(r^{2}f)^{\prime}}{4}\cos\theta\frac{d\phi}{dt}\frac{d\psi}{dt}-\frac{f(r^{2}f)^{\prime}}{8}\Big(\frac{d\psi}{dt}\Big)^{2}=0,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\theta}{dt^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{r}\frac{dr}{dt}\frac{d\theta}{dt}+(f-1)\sin\theta\cos\theta\Big(\frac{d\phi}{dt}\Big)^{2}+f\sin\theta\frac{d\phi}{dt}\frac{d\psi}{dt}=0,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\phi}{dt^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{r}\frac{dr}{dt}\frac{d\phi}{dt}-(f-2)\cot\theta\frac{d\phi}{dt}\frac{d\theta}{dt}-\frac{f}{\sin\theta}\frac{d\theta}{dt}\frac{d\psi}{dt}=0,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\psi}{dt^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\cos\theta\frac{dr}{dt}\frac{d\phi}{dt}+\frac{(r^{2}f)^{\prime}}{r^{2}f}\frac{dr}{dt}\frac{d\psi}{dt}+\frac{(f-1)\cos^{2}\theta-1}{\sin\theta}\frac{d\theta}{dt}\frac{d\phi}{dt}

\displaystyle+f\cot\theta\frac{d\theta}{dt}\frac{d\psi}{dt}=0.

(2.9)

Throughout the paper, we denote
F(\alpha,k^{2})
,
E(\alpha,k^{2})
, and
\Pi(h,\alpha,k^{2})
the incomplete elliptic integrals of the first, second, and third kind, where
0<k<1
,
h\in\mathbb{C}
(cf. Appendix), and

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\pm 1,\quad r_{0}=\sqrt[4]{\frac{B}{n-1}}

for
B>0
,
n\geq 2.

3.
Geodesics on scalar flat metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type

3.1.
Geodesics for constant
\theta
,
\phi
,
\psi

The geodesic equations reduce to

\frac{d^{2}r}{dt^{2}}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}\left(\frac{dr}{dt}\right)^{2}=0.

Theorem 3.1
.

The geodesics for scalar flat metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type with constant

\theta
,
\phi
and
\psi
and passing through
r_{0}
with conditions

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow r_{0}}t=t_{1},\quad\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow r_{0}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{f}}\frac{dr}{dt}=r_{1}

satisfy

\displaystyle t(r)=

\displaystyle t_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{r_{1}}\Big[\frac{\sqrt{r^{4}+(n-2)r_{0}^{2}r^{2}-B}}{r}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{n}r_{0}E\left(\alpha,k^{2}\right)+\frac{r_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}F\left(\alpha,k^{2}\right)\Big],

where

\displaystyle\alpha=\arcsin\sqrt{1-\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{r^{2}}},\quad k^{2}=1-\frac{1}{n}.

Proof:
The geodesic equation (
3.1
) implies that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{f}}\Big|\frac{dr}{dt}\Big|\Big)=0.

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{dr}=\frac{\varepsilon}{r_{1}\sqrt{f}}.

Changing variable
u=r^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{du}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2r_{1}}\frac{u}{\sqrt{u\big(u-r_{0}^{2}\big)\big(u+\sqrt{(n-1)B}\big)}}.

The theorem follows by integrating it from
r_{0}^{2}
to
r^{2}
. Q.E.D.

3.2.
Geodesics for constant
\phi
,
\psi

The geodesic equations reduce to

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}r}{dt^{2}}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}\Big(\frac{dr}{dt}\Big)^{2}-\frac{rf}{4}\Big(\frac{d\theta}{dt}\Big)^{2}=0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\theta}{dt^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{dr}{dt}\frac{d\theta}{dt}=0.

(3.3)

Theorem 3.2
.

Let constant

,

5.
Appendix: Elliptic integrals

In
[
8
]
, the incomplete elliptic integrals of the first, the second, and the third kinds are given by

\displaystyle F(\alpha,k^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\alpha}\frac{d\varrho}{\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}\varrho}},

\displaystyle E(\alpha,k^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\alpha}\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}\varrho}\,d\varrho,

\displaystyle\Pi(h,\alpha,k^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\alpha}\frac{d\varrho}{(1-h\sin^{2}\varrho)\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}\varrho}},

respectively, where
0<k<1
,
h\in\mathbb{C}
.

The following propositions can be derived straightforwardly.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
a,b,c,x\in\mathrm{R}
,
x>a>b>c
, and denote

f_{1}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(z-a)(z-b)(z-c)}},

then

\int_{a}^{x}f_{1}(z)dz=\frac{2}{\sqrt{a-c}}F\left(\alpha,k^{2}\right),

where

\displaystyle\alpha=\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{x-a}{x-b}},\quad k^{2}=\frac{b-c}{a-c}.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
a,b,c,x\in\mathrm{R}
,
x>a>b>c
, and denote

f_{2}(z)=\frac{z}{\sqrt{(z-a)(z-b)(z-c)}},

then

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{x}f_{2}(z)dz=

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{(x-a)(x-c)}}{\sqrt{x-b}}-2\sqrt{a-c}E\left(\alpha,k^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2a}{\sqrt{a-c}}F\left(\alpha,k^{2}\right),

where

\displaystyle\alpha=\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{x-a}{x-b}},\quad k^{2}=\frac{b-c}{a-c}.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
a,b,c,d,x\in\mathrm{R}
,
x>a>b>c
,
a>d
, and denote

f_{3}(z)=\frac{1}{(z^{2}-d^{2})\sqrt{(z-a)(z-b)(z-c)}},

then

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{x}f_{3}(z)dz=

\displaystyle\frac{2F(\alpha,k^{2})}{(b^{2}-d^{2})\sqrt{a-c}}-\frac{(a-b)\Pi(h_{1},\alpha,k^{2})}{d(b-d)(a-d)\sqrt{a-c}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(a-b)\Pi(h_{2},\alpha,k^{2})}{d(b+d)(a+d)\sqrt{a-c}},

where

\displaystyle\alpha=\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{x-a}{x-b}},\quad k^{2}=\frac{b-c}{a-c},\quad h_{1}=\frac{b-d}{a-d},\quad h_{2}=\frac{b+d}{a+d}.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let

\displaystyle\frac{2a_{2}^{2}}{(a_{2}-a_{4})(a_{2}-a_{5})\sqrt{a_{1}-a_{3}}}F(\alpha,k^{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{2a_{5}^{2}(a_{1}-a_{2})}{(a_{4}-a_{5})(a_{1}-a_{5})(a_{2}-a_{5})\sqrt{a_{1}-a_{3}}}\Pi(h_{2},\alpha,k^{2}),

Abstract
. Let f f be an elliptic modular form and p p an odd prime that is coprime to the level of f f . We study the link between divisors of the characteristic ideal of the p p -primary fine Selmer group of f f over the cyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} extension of ℚ \mathbb{Q} and the greatest common divisor of signed Selmer groups attached to f f defined using the theory of Wach modules. One of the key ingredients of our proof is a generalization of a result of Wingberg on the structure of fine Selmer groups of abelian varieties with supersingular reduction at p p to the context of modular forms.

Introduction

at a prime above

denote the

-representation of

defined by Deligne

-lattice

inside

and write

We write

for the conjugate modular form of

and we write

,

and

for the corresponding
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-modules attached to
{\overline{f}}

chosen to be

denotes the

Let

The Iwasawa algebra

is defined to be
\varprojlim\mathcal{O}[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}]
, where the connecting maps are projections.
After fixing a topological generator

of

, sending
\gamma
to
X+1

Given a
\Lambda
-module

. We write

for the
\Lambda
-module which is

\mathcal{O}
-module equipped with a
\Gamma
-action given by
\gamma\cdot_{\iota}m=\gamma^{-1}m
. Finally, if

, we write
F^{\iota}
for the power series
F(\frac{1}{1+X}-1)

For

or
{\overline{f}}
and

, let
\Sel_{0}(A_{g}(j)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})
denote the fine Selmer group of

over
\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc}
(whose definition will be reviewed in §

). The fine Selmer groups of abelian varieties were first systematically studied by Coates and Sujatha in
[
7
]
, and a little later by Wuthrich
[
34
]
. Various conjectures on the structure of these groups have been formulated and they are still wide open to this date. See also
[
15
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
for some recent results on the topic. In the context of modular forms, the fine Selmer groups have been studied in
[
1
,
11
,
12
,
10
]
.

It has been proved by Kato
[
13
]
that
\Sel_{0}(A_{g}(j)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}
is a finitely generated torsion
\Lambda
-module.
Let
\mathfrak{F}_{g,j}
denote a choice of characteristic element (i.e. a generator of the characteristic ideal
\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda}\Sel_{0}(A_{g}(j)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}
). The main goal of the present article is to study the divisors of
\mathfrak{F}_{f,i}
and
\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}
for a fixed integer
i
. These specific twists are considered due to the perfect pairing

T_{f}(i)\times A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)\rightarrow\mu_{p^{\infty}}

of

-modules.

We are able to relate the divisors of
\mathfrak{F}_{f,i}
and
\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}
to the greatest common divisors of the signed Selmer groups attached to
{\overline{f}}(k-i)
defined in
[
18

19

by the theory of Wach modules (see §

\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\sharp}
and
\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\flat}
for a choice of characteristic elements of the Pontryagin duals of these signed Selmer groups. Under a mild hypothesis on the local representations at bad primes and a hypothesis on the validity of Kato’s Iwasawa main conjecture for

(labelled

and

is an irreducible element of

that is outside a certain explicit set of linear factors,

and
F\nmid\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}
if and only if

In the case where

is the Tate module of an elliptic curve

with good supersingular reduction at

[

[
33

(see also
[
24
]
, where an alternative proof is given). In the present paper, we prove Theorem
4.4
by first establishing analogues of Wingberg’s result in the context of modular forms (see Theorems

and
3.4
). In
[
33

, Wingberg worked with Selmer groups defined using flat cohomology, whereas Matar worked with Selmer groups defined using Galois cohomology in

2.
Definitions of Selmer groups and related objects

2.1.
Fine Selmer groups

Let
L_{n}
be the unique subextension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc}
such that
[L_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]=p^{n}
. Given an algebraic extension
L
of
\mathbb{Q}
, we write
S(L)
for the primes of
L
lying above
pN
as well as the archimedean primes. We write
G_{S}(L)
for the Galois group of the maximal algebraic extension of
L
that is unramified outside
S(L)
.

For
g\in\{f,{\overline{f}}\}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the fine Selmer group of
A_{g}(j)
over
L
to be

\Sel_{0}(A_{g}(j)/L)=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(L),A_{g}(j))\rightarrow\prod_{v\in S(L)}H^{1}(L_{v},A_{g}(j))\right).

Recall that
\Sel_{0}(A_{g}(j)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}
is torsion over
\Lambda
and we write
\mathfrak{F}_{g,j}
for a choice of a generator of the characteristic ideal
\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda}\Sel_{0}(A_{g}(j)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}
.

The classical Selmer group of
A_{g}(j)
over
L
is defined to be

\Sel(A_{g}(j)/L)=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(L),A_{g}(j))\rightarrow\prod_{v\in S(L)}\frac{H^{1}(L_{v},A_{g}(j))}{H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(L_{v},A_{g}(j))}\right),

where
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(L_{v},A_{g}(j))
is defined as in
[
4
, §3]
.

2.2.
Wach modules and signed Coleman maps

We recall the definition of signed Coleman maps from
[
18
,
19
]
, which generalize those studied in
[
14
,
32
]
in the context of elliptic curves with supersingular reduction at
p
. Here, we do not require
p
to be a non-ordinary prime for
f
. The only requirement is that
p
is coprime to
N
so that the representation
V_{f}
is crystalline at
p
.

We shall write for
g\in\{f,{\overline{f}}\}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m\in\{1,2\}
,

\mathfrak{H}^{m}_{g,j}=\varprojlim_{n}H^{m}(L_{n},T_{g}(j)),\quad\mathbb{H}^{m}_{g,j}=\varprojlim H^{m}(L_{n,p},T_{g}(j)),

where the connecting maps are corestrictions and we have abused notation by writing
p
for the unique prime of
L_{n}
above
p
.
We write
\mathrm{loc}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{g,j}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}_{g,j}
for the localization map.

Let
T=T_{g}(k-1)
, where
g\in\{f,{\overline{f}}\}
. We write
\mathbb{N}(T)
for the Wach module of
T
. See
[
3
, §II.1]
for the precise definition of
\mathbb{N}(T)
. Roughly speaking, it is a filtered
\varphi
-module over the ring
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\pi\rrbracket
, where
\pi
is an element in the ring of of Witt vectors of
\displaystyle\varprojlim_{x\mapsto x^{p}}\mathbb{C}_{p}
, given by
[(1,\zeta_{p},\zeta_{p^{2}},...)]-1
and
\zeta_{p^{n}}
is a primitive
p^{n}
-th root of unity in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
such that
\zeta_{p^{n+1}}^{p}=\zeta_{p^{n}}
. One may regard
\pi
as a formal variable equipped with an action of
\varphi
and
\Gamma_{0}=\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
given by
\varphi(\pi)=(1+\pi)^{p}-1
and
\sigma(\pi)=(1+\pi)^{\chi_{\cyc}(\sigma)}-1
for
\sigma\in\Gamma_{0}
, where
\chi_{\cyc}
is the
p
-adic cyclotomic character. Let
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}),T)=\varprojlim_{n}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{n}}),T)
, where the connecting maps are corestrictions. Berger proved in
[
2
, Appendix A]
that there is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gamma_{0}\rrbracket
-modules

H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}),T)\cong\mathbb{N}(T)^{\psi=1},

where
\psi
is a canonical left inverse of
\varphi
and the superscript
\psi=1
denotes the kernel of
\psi-1
.

We recall from
[
18
,
19
]
that for
\bullet\in\{\sharp,\flat\}
, on choosing an appropriate basis of
\mathbb{N}(T_{g}(k-1))
, one can define signed Coleman maps

\mathrm{Col}_{g}^{\bullet}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}),T_{g}(k-1))\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gamma_{0}\rrbracket,

decomposing Perrin-Riou’s big logarithm map defined in
[
27
]
.

We define the twisted Coleman maps

\mathrm{Col}_{g,j}^{\bullet}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{H}^{1}_{g,j}\rightarrow\Lambda

as follows. We have the
\mathcal{O}
-isomorphism

H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}),T_{g}(j))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e^{k-1-j}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}),T_{g}(k-1)),

where
e
is a basis of the
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-representation
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)
, as given in
[
28
, §A.4]
. Let
\Tw
be the
\mathcal{O}
-linear automorphism on
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gamma_{0}\rrbracket
sending
\sigma\in\Gamma_{0}
to
\chi_{\cyc}(\sigma)\sigma
. We can define a
\Lambda
-morphism

\Tw^{i-k+1}\circ\ \mathrm{Col}_{g}^{\bullet}\circ e^{k-1-j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}),T_{g}(j))\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gamma_{0}\rrbracket

and obtain
\mathrm{Col}_{g,j}^{\bullet}
on taking the trivial isotypic component of
\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p})/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

2.3.
Signed Selmer groups and main conjectures

Let
H_{\bullet}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc,p},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))
denote the orthogonal complement of
\ker\mathrm{Col}_{f,i}^{\bullet}
under the local Tate pairing

H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}),T_{f}(i))\times H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc,p},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

We define the signed Selmer groups
\Sel^{\bullet}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})
as

\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc}),A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))\rightarrow\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc,p},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))}{H_{\bullet}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc,p},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))}\times\prod_{v\nmid p}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc,v},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))}{H_{\mathrm{f}}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc,v},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))}\right).

Note that when
A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)
is given by
E[p^{\infty}]
for some elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with good supersingular reduction at
p
or when
a_{p}({\overline{f}})=0
, one may choose an appropriate basis of the Wach module so that these Selmer groups coincide with the ones studied in
[
14
,
32
,
16
]
(see
[
18
, §§5.2-5.4]
).

Let
\mathbf{z}_{f,i}
be Kato’s zeta element inside
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},T_{f}(i))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
as defined in
[
13
]
. It is conjectured that
\mathbf{z}_{f,i}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},T_{f}(i))
(see Conjecture 12.10 of op. cit.). Assuming this (which we shall do for the rest of the article), recall from
[
18
, Eqn. (61)]
the Poitou–Tate exact sequence

(2.1)

0\rightarrow\frac{\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i}}{\Lambda\mathbf{z}_{f,i}}\rightarrow\frac{\mathrm{Image}\left(\mathrm{Col}_{f,i}^{\bullet}\right)}{(L_{p}^{\bullet})}\rightarrow\Sel^{\bullet}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}\rightarrow\Sel_{0}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}\rightarrow 0,

where
L_{p}^{\bullet}=\mathrm{Col}_{f,i}^{\bullet}\circ\mathrm{loc}_{p}(\mathbf{z}_{f,i})
is the signed
p
-adic
L
-function associated to
{\overline{f}}(k-i)
.
If
L_{p}^{\bullet}\neq 0
, then
\Sel^{\bullet}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}
is a finitely generated torsion
\Lambda
-module. We write
\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\bullet}
for a characteristic element of this module.

Kato
[
13
]
proved that the first and fourth terms of (
2.1
) are
\Lambda
-torsion. He further formulated the following Iwasawa main conjecture:

(2.2)

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i}/\Lambda\mathbf{z}_{f,i}=\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda}\Sel_{0}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}=(\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}).

It follows from (
2.1
) that it is equivalent to

(2.3)

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda}\Sel^{\bullet}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}=\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Image}\left(\mathrm{Col}_{f,i}^{\bullet}\right)/(L_{p}^{\bullet}),

provided that
L_{p}^{\bullet}\neq 0
.

For the rest of the article, we shall assume that the following hypothesis holds:

(H-IMC)
Kato’s Iwasawa main conjecture (
2.2
) holds and that both
L_{p}^{\sharp}
and
L_{p}^{\flat}
are non-zero.

Remark 2.1
.

We say a few words on the hypothesis
(H-IMC)
. In his seminal work
[
13
]
, Kato established that the inclusion “
\subseteq
” in (
2.2
) holds after tensoring by
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
under the assumption that the image of the representation
T_{f}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})}}
contains a copy of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, provided that certain local terms vanish (see in particular Theorem 12.5(4) of op. cit.). Note that in
[
13
, Conjecture 12.10]
, Kato’s main conjecture is formulated in terms of étale cohomology groups. Kobayashi and Kurihara showed that it can be recast in terms of Galois cohomology groups and fine Selmer groups (see for example
[
14
, Proposition 7.1]
). The reverse inclusion of (
2.2
) has been established in the monumental work of Skinner-Urban
[
31
]
for a
p
-ordinary modular form (under certain hypotheses, see Theorem 1 of op. cit. for details). In the non-ordinary form, there have been several recent breakthroughs in this reverse direction (for instance, see
[
5
,
6
,
9
]
).
Finally, the work
[
18
]
has supplied many sufficient conditions for the non-vanishing of
L_{p}^{\sharp}
and
L_{p}^{\flat}
(see in particular Corollary 3.29 and Proposition 3.39 in op. cit.). In view of these developments, it seems reasonable to assume that the hypothesis
(H-IMC)
, on which our main results are reliant, holds.

Lemma 2.2
.

There is a pseudo-isomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules

\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{e_{\eta}\mathrm{Image}(\mathrm{Col}_{f}^{\sharp}\oplus\mathrm{Col}_{f}^{\flat})}\sim\frac{\Lambda}{\prod_{j=0}^{k-2}(X-u^{j}+1)}.

It is enough to show that the
\mu

C=e_{\eta}\mathrm{Image}(\mathrm{Col}_{f}^{\sharp}\oplus\mathrm{Col}_{f}^{\flat}),\quad\text{and}\quad C_{\bullet}=e_{\eta}\mathrm{Image}\mathrm{Col}_{f}^{\bullet}

for
\bullet\in\{\sharp,\flat\}

Consider the tautological short exact sequence

0\rightarrow C\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}/C\rightarrow 0.

This gives the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\left(\Lambda^{2}/C\right)[\varpi]\rightarrow C/\varpi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Lambda^{2}/\varpi,

where
\varpi
is a fixed uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}

0\rightarrow\left(\Lambda/C_{\bullet}\right)[\varpi]\rightarrow C_{\bullet}/\varpi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{\bullet}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Lambda/\varpi,

\bullet\in\{\sharp,\flat\}

Recall from
[

that the

\ker\Phi_{\bullet}=(\Lambda/C_{\bullet})[\varpi]

and
\Phi=(\Phi_{\sharp}\oplus\Phi_{\flat})|_{C/\varpi}
by definitions. It implies that

\ker\Phi\subset\ker\Phi_{\sharp}\oplus\ker\Phi_{\flat}.

Hence,
\ker\Phi=\left(\Lambda^{2}/C\right)[\varpi]
is finite. In particular, the
\mu
-invariant of
\Lambda^{2}/C
is zero, which finishes the proof of the lemma.

3.
A generalization of a result of Wingberg

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian variety over
\mathbb{Q}
with supersingular reduction at
p
. Wingberg proved that there is a pseudo-isomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules

\left(\Sel(\mathcal{A}[p^{\infty}]/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}\right)_{{\Lambda-\mathrm{tor}}}\sim\left(\Sel_{0}(\mathcal{A}^{t}[p^{\infty}]/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}\right)^{\iota},

where
\mathcal{A}^{t}
is the dual abelian variety of
\mathcal{A}
(see
[
33
, Corollary 2.5]
). Here,
M_{\Lambda-\mathrm{tor}}
denotes the maximal torsion submodule of a
\Lambda
-module
M
,
\Sel(\mathcal{A}[p^{\infty}]/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})
is the
p
-primary Selmer group of
\mathcal{A}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc}
and
\Sel_{0}(\mathcal{A}^{t}[p^{\infty}]/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})
is the
p
-primary fine Selmer group of
\mathcal{A}^{t}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc}
defined in a similar manner as the fine Selmer groups for
A_{g}(j)
in §
2.1
above.
We prove the following analogue of Wingberg’s result in the context of modular forms.

Theorem 3.1
.

We have a pseudo-isomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules

\left(H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},A_{g}(j))^{\vee}\right)_{\Lambda-\mathrm{tor}}\sim\left(\mathfrak{H}^{2}_{g,j}\right)^{\iota},

where
g\in\{f,{\overline{f}}\}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

From the low degree terms of Nekovář’s spectral sequence (see
[
25
, Lemma 9.1.5]
)

\Ext_{\Lambda}^{r}(H^{3-s}_{\Iw}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},T_{g}(j)),\Lambda)\Rightarrow H^{3-r-s}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},A_{g}(j))^{\vee},

we obtain the following exact sequence of
\Lambda
-modules:

0\longrightarrow\Ext_{\Lambda}^{1}(H^{2}_{\Iw}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},T_{g}(j)),\Lambda)\longrightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},A_{g}(j))^{\vee}\longrightarrow\Hom_{\Lambda}(H^{1}_{\Iw}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},T_{g}(j)),\Lambda).

Since
\Hom_{\Lambda}(H^{1}_{\Iw}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},T_{g}(j)),\Lambda)
is a reflexive
\Lambda
-module by
[
26
, Corollary 5.1.3]
, it is
\Lambda
-torsion-free. Hence, it follows from the above exact sequence that there is an isomorphism

\left(H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},A_{g}(j))^{\vee}\right)_{\Lambda-\mathrm{tor}}\cong\Ext_{\Lambda}^{1}(H^{2}_{\Iw}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},T_{g}(j)),\Lambda)

of
\Lambda
-modules. By
[
26
, Proposition 5.5.13]
, the latter is pseudo-isomorphic to
\left(\mathfrak{H}^{2}_{g,j}\right)^{\iota}
, which concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

When
f
is non-ordinary at
p
, we shall establish a direct analog of Wingberg’s theorem on the level of Selmer groups under the following hypothesis on the local representation
A_{\overline{f}}
(see Theorem
3.4
below).

(H0)
For all
v\in S(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})
, the group
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc,v},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))
is finite.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that when
v|p
, the group
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc,v},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))
is always finite by
[
10
, Lemma 3.3]
. See also §5 of op. cit. where sufficient conditions and explicit examples of the finiteness of
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc,v},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))
for
v|N
are studied.

Lemma 3.3
.

Under
(H0)
, we have a pseudo-isomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules

\Sel_{0}(A_{f}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}\sim\mathfrak{H}^{2}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer.
By the Poitou–Tate exact sequence (see for example
[
28
, §A.3.1]
), we have the following exact sequence:

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{v\in S(L_{n})}H^{0}\left(L_{n,v},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)\right)\longrightarrow H^{2}\left(G_{S}(L_{n}),T_{f}(i)\right)^{\vee}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle H^{1}\left(G_{S}(L_{n}),A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{v\in S(L_{n})}H^{1}\left(L_{n,v},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)\right).

By definition,

\mathrm{Image}(\delta)=\ker(\tau)=\Sel_{0}\left(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/L_{n}\right).

This gives the exact sequence

\bigoplus_{v\in S(L_{n})}H^{0}\left(L_{n,v},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)\right)\longrightarrow H^{2}\left(G_{S}(L_{n}),T_{f}(i)\right)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\Sel_{0}\left(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/L_{n}\right)\longrightarrow 0.

By the local Tate duality, we have

H^{0}(L_{n,v},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))^{\vee}\cong H^{2}(L_{n,v},T_{f}(i))

for all
v\in S
.
Thus, after taking Pontryagin duals and inverse limits, we obtain

(3.1)

0\longrightarrow\Sel_{0}\left(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc}\right)^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{2}_{\Iw}\left(G_{S}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc}),T_{f}(i)\right)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})}H^{0}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc,v},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)\right)^{\vee}.

Therefore, the lemma follows from
(H0)
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that
f
is non-ordinary at
p
and
(H0)
is valid.
We have a pseudo-isomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules

\left(\Sel(A_{f}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}\right)_{\Lambda-\mathrm{tor}}\sim\left(\Sel_{0}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}\right)^{\iota}.

Proof.

Consider the defining sequence of the Selmer group

0\longrightarrow\Sel(A_{f}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})\longrightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},A_{f}(i))\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w\in S(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc},w},A_{f}(i))}{H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc},w},A_{f}(i))}.

By
[
4
, Proposition 3.8]
, there is an isomorphism

\varprojlim H^{1}(L_{n,w_{n}},T_{\overline{f}}(k-i))\cong\left(\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc},w},A_{f}(i))}{H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc},w},A_{f}(i))}\right)^{\vee},

where
w_{n}\in S(L_{n})
such that
w_{n}
lies below
w
and
w_{n+1}
. When
w\nmid p
, this is zero by
[
13
, §17.10]
.
When
w|p
, this is also zero by
[
29
, Theorem 0.6]
under the hypothesis that
f
is non-ordinary at
p
. Hence, we have an isomorphism

\Sel(A_{f}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})\cong H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},A_{f}(i))

of
\Lambda
-modules.
Combining this with Theorem
3.1
, we obtain a pseudo-isomorphism

\left(\Sel(A_{f}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}\right)_{\Lambda-\mathrm{tor}}\sim\left(\mathfrak{H}^{2}_{f,i}\right)^{\iota}

of
\Lambda
-modules. But the latter is pseudo-isomorphic to
\Sel_{0}(A_{f}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}
by Lemma
3.3
under
(H0)
. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.
Comparison of characteristic elements

4.1.
Preliminary lemmas

We prove several preliminary lemmas that will be used in the proofs of Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
below.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
(H0)
holds. If
F
is an irreducible element of
\Lambda
such that
F\nmid\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}
, then we have a psuedo-isomorphism

(\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i})[F^{\infty}]\sim\Sel_{0}(A_{f}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee,\iota}[F^{\infty}]

of
\Lambda
-modules. In particular,

(\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i})[F]
is finite if and only if
F^{\iota}\nmid\mathfrak{F}_{f,i}
.

Proof.

We have the Poitou–Tate exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))^{\vee}\rightarrow\Sel_{0}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}\rightarrow 0.

This gives the following short exact sequence

0\rightarrow(\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))^{\vee}\rightarrow\Sel_{0}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}\rightarrow 0,

which in turn induces the following exact sequence:

(4.1)

0\rightarrow(\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i})[F^{\infty}]\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))^{\vee}[F^{\infty}]\rightarrow\Sel_{0}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}[F^{\infty}].

By assumption,
\Sel_{0}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}[F^{\infty}]
is finite.
Therefore, we obtain a psuedo-isomorphism

(4.2)

(\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i})[F^{\infty}]\sim H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))^{\vee}[F^{\infty}].

By Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.3
, we have the pseudo-isomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc},A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))^{\vee}[F^{\infty}]\sim\Sel_{0}(A_{f}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee,\iota}[F^{\infty}].

Combining these two pseudo-isomorphisms finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
(H-IMC)
holds. Let
F
be an irreducible element of
\Lambda
such that
F\nmid\eta_{k,i}
. Recall that
\mathrm{loc}_{p}
denotes the localization map from
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i}
to
\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}
. Then one has a pseudo-isomorphism

\left(\Lambda^{2}/\Lambda(\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\sharp},\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\flat})\right)[F^{\infty}]\sim\left(\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/(\mathrm{loc}_{p}(\mathbf{z}_{f,i}))\right)[F^{\infty}]

of
\Lambda
-modules. Hence, it follows that
F\nmid\gcd(\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\sharp},\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\flat})
if and only if
\left(\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/(\mathrm{loc}_{p}(\mathbf{z}_{f,i}))\right)[F]
is finite.

Proof.

By (
2.4
) and (
2.3
), we have the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/(\mathrm{loc}_{p}(\mathbf{z}_{f,i}))\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}/\Lambda(\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\sharp},\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\flat})\rightarrow\Lambda/\eta_{k,i}\rightarrow 0.

This gives the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\left(\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/(\mathrm{loc}_{p}(\mathbf{z}_{f,i}))\right)[F^{\infty}]\rightarrow\left(\Lambda^{2}/\Lambda(\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\sharp},\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\flat})\right)[F^{\infty}]\rightarrow\Lambda/\eta_{k,i}[F^{\infty}].

The last term is finite since
F\nmid\eta_{k,i}
. Hence, the result follows.
∎

4.2.
Comparison between characteristic ideals of fine Selmer groups and signed Selmer groups

The goal of this section is to prove a generalization of
[
20
, Theorem 1.2]
(see Theorem
4.4
below). We shall do so via the following intermediate result:

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
(H-IMC)
and
(H0)
hold. Let
F
be an irreducible element of
\Lambda
such that
F\nmid\eta_{k,i}
and that
F\nmid\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}
. There is a pseudo-isomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules

\left(\Lambda^{2}/\Lambda(\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\sharp},\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\flat})\right)[F^{\infty}]\sim\Sel_{0}(A_{f}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee,\iota}[F^{\infty}].

In particular,
F^{\iota}\nmid\mathfrak{F}_{f,i}
if and only if
F\nmid\gcd\left(\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\sharp},\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\flat}\right)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, we have a pseudo-isomorphism

\left(\Lambda^{2}/\Lambda(\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\sharp},\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\flat})\right)[F^{\infty}]\sim\left(\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/(\mathrm{loc}_{p}(\mathbf{z}_{f,i}))\right)[F^{\infty}]

of
\Lambda
-modules. Consider the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i}/\Lambda\mathbf{z}_{f,i}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/(\mathrm{loc}_{p}(\mathbf{z}_{f,i}))\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i}\rightarrow 0.

Since
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i}/\Lambda\mathbf{z}_{f,i}
is a torsion
\Lambda
-module with
\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda}\left(\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i}/\Lambda\mathbf{z}_{f,i}\right)=\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}
not divisible by
F
, we have the following pseudo-isomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules

\left(\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/(\mathrm{loc}_{p}(\mathbf{z}_{f,i}))\right)[F^{\infty}]\sim\left(\mathbb{H}^{1}_{f,i}/\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{f,i}\right)[F^{\infty}].

By Lemma
4.1
, the latter is pseudo-isomorphic to
\Sel_{0}(A_{f}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee,\iota}[F^{\infty}]
. On combining these pseudo-isomorphisms, the theorem follows.
∎

We can now state and prove the main result of the article.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume that
(H-IMC)
and
(H0)
hold. Let
F
be an irreducible element of
\Lambda
such that
F\nmid\eta_{k,i}
. Then
F^{\iota}\nmid\mathfrak{F}_{f,i}
and
F\nmid\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}
if and only if
F\nmid\gcd\left(\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\sharp},\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\flat}\right)
.

Proof.

Theorem
4.3
tells us that if
F^{\iota}\nmid\mathfrak{F}_{f,i}
and
F\nmid\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}
, then
F\nmid\gcd\left(\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\sharp},\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\flat}\right)
. This proves the ”only if” implication.

Conversely, suppose that
F\nmid\gcd\left(\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\sharp},\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}^{\flat}\right)
. From the inclusion

\Sel_{0}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i))\subset\Sel^{\bullet}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)),

we have
F\nmid\mathfrak{F}_{{\overline{f}},k-i}
. The remaining assertion that
F^{\iota}\nmid\mathfrak{F}_{f,i}
now follows from Theorem
4.3
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with good supersingular reduction at
p
. In
[
20
]
, it is stated in the proof of Proposition 3.1 that by
[
33
, Corollary 2.5]
, we have the equality

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda}\left(\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}\right)_{\Lambda-\mathrm{tor}}=\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda}\Sel_{0}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}.

However, one of the two Selmer groups should be twisted by
\iota
in order for the equality to hold. As such, for the rest of the proof to go through, the additional hypothesis that the irreducible element
f
(not to be confused with the notation for a modular form in the present article) satisfies
(f)=(f^{\iota})
is required. Consequently, the statement of Theorem 1.2 in op. cit. should also be modified.

Specializing our Theorem
4.4
to the case
F=\varpi
, where
\varpi
is a uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}
, we may deduce the following:

Corollary 4.6
.

Assume that
(H-IMC)
and
(H0)
hold. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(a) The
\mu
-invariants of
\Sel_{0}(A_{f}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}
and
\Sel_{0}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}
are zero.

(b) At least one of
\Sel^{\sharp}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}
and
\Sel^{\flat}(A_{\overline{f}}(k-i)/\mathbb{Q}_{\cyc})^{\vee}
has trivial
\mu
-invariant.

Remark 4.7
.

Recall from
[
7
, Conjecture A]
,
[
12
, Conjecture A]
and
[
1
, Conjecture 1.2]
that the

for some elliptic curve

with good supersingular reduction at

.

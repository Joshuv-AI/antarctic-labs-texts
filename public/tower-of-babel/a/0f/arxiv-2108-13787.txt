On the sample-dependent minimal conductivity in weakly disordered graphene
Weiwei Chen, Yedi Shen, Bo Fu, Qinwei Shi, W. Zhu

Abstract
We present a unified understanding of the experimentally observed minimal dc conductivity in weakly disordered graphene. Firstly, based on linear response theory, we reveal that randomness or disorder inevitably induces momentum dependent corrections to the electron self-energy function, which naturally yields a sample-dependent minimal conductivity. Taking the long-ranged Gaussian and Coulomb potentials as examples, we derive the momentum dependent self-energy function within the Born approximation, and further validate it via numerical simulations using the large-scale Lanczos algorithm. The explicit momentum dependences of the self-energy on the intensity, concentration and range of potential are critically addressed. Therefore, our results provide a reasonable interpretation of the sample-dependent minimal conductivity observed in graphene samples.

Minimal conductivity with momentum dependent self-energy

The charge carriers of graphene near half filling can be modeled by a Dirac Hamiltonian

H=\hbar v_{f}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{k}+U(\bm{r}),

(1)

where
v_{f}\approx 10^{6}

m\ s^{-1}
is the Fermi velocity,
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y})
are the Pauli matrices of pseudospin (sublattice),
\bm{k}=(k_{x},k_{y})
is a two-component wave vector,
and
U(\bm{r})
describes the long-ranged random potential that is experienced by the Dirac electrons. In the pure case,
the eigenvalues and eigenstates of the Hamiltonian Eq. (
1
) are

E_{\bm{k}s}=s\hbar v_{f}k;\ \ \ \Psi_{\bm{k}s}(\bm{r})=\langle\bm{r}|\bm{k}s\rangle=\frac{e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}}{\sqrt{2\mathcal{V}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
se^{i\theta_{\bm{k}}}\end{array}\right)

(2)

where
s=\pm
denotes the band index (
s=+1
for the conduction band and
s=-1
for the valence band),
\mathcal{V}
is the sample area, and
\theta_{\bm{k}}=\arctan(k_{y}/k_{x})
is the angle of the wave vector
\bm{k}
down from the positive
x
-axis. The velocity
\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial H}{\partial k_{x}}
along the x direction in the eigenstates basis is

v_{x}(\bm{k})=v_{f}(\cos\theta_{\bm{k}}\sigma_{z}+\sin\theta_{\bm{k}}\sigma_{y}).

(3)

For simplicity, we model the random potential by
U(\bm{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{imp}}}\pm u_{0}e^{-|\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{i}|^{2}/\xi^{2}}
, where
\bm{R}_{i}
and
N_{\text{imp}}
are the location and total number of impurities,
\xi
is the characteristic length scale and the strength
\pm u_{0}
is randomly distributed with equal probability. We also checked that the results shown below are not sensitive to the form of the potentials. To do so, we checked the long-range screened Coulomb potential and obtained similar results (shown in Supplemental Material Sec. S2 C).

In general, the electron self-energy induced by the impurity scattering is both momentum- and energy-dependent. For the short-ranged disorder potential, the momentum dependence of the self-energy is negligible
Shon
;
Ludwig
;
Nersesyan
;
Sarma2011
. For the long-range disorder potential, the self-energy is usually treated in the on-shell approximation (
E=E_{\bm{k}s}
)
Adam
, i.e. the momentum dependence of the self-energy is replaced by the single-particle energy in the clean limit.
One of motivations for this paper is to elucidate that this on-shell approximation is not well justified around the Dirac point, leading to a momentum-dependent self-energy function (We present both analytical and numerical evidence to support this in the sections below). Here, we first reveal the influence of the momentum-dependent self-energy function on the minimal conductivity.
Accordingly, for the discussion, we can assume that the self-energy function contains a nonzero momentum-dependent contribution,

\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)\approx\Sigma_{1}(E)-\alpha s\hbar v_{f}k+i\Sigma_{2}(E)

(4)

where
s=\pm
is the band index, while
\Sigma_{1}(E)
and
\Sigma_{2}(E)
describe the real and imaginary parts of the energy-dependent terms.
Since the long-ranged random scalar disorder we considered here does not on average break the particle-hole symmetry
Altland1997
;
Ostrovsky
;
Fradkin
;
Aleiner
, the self-energy in the eigenstate basis satisfies the relations,
{\rm Re}\Sigma(\bm{k}+,E)=-{\rm Re}\Sigma(\bm{k}-,-E)
and
{\rm Im}\Sigma(\bm{k}+,E)={\rm Im}\Sigma(\bm{k}-,-E)
, which preserves the dispersion relation and broadens the width of the conduction and valence bands symmetrically about the Dirac point
Hu
.

Now we study the influence of this momentum dependent self-energy function on the minimal conductivity. Based on linear response theory, we can calculate the longitudinal conductivity at zero temperature using the Kubo formula
Bruus
(see Supplemental Material Sec. S1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}(E)=\sigma_{xx}^{RA}(E)-{\rm Re}\left[\sigma_{xx}^{RR}(E)\right]

(5)

with

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}^{RA}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\frac{e^{2}\hbar}{2\pi\mathcal{V}}{\rm Tr}\left[G^{R}(E)v_{x}G^{A}(E)v_{x}\right]_{c}

(6)

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}^{RR}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\frac{e^{2}\hbar}{2\pi\mathcal{V}}{\rm Tr}\left[G^{R}(E)v_{x}G^{R}(E)v_{x}\right]_{c}

(7)

where the factor 4 denotes the degeneracy of the real spin and valley,
{\rm Tr}[\cdots]
means the trace over both wave vector and pseudospin (sublattice) spaces, and the subscript
c
indicates a disorder configuration average. Here the RA contribution is analogous to the transport of classical particles within the relaxation time approximation and gives results close to conventional Boltzmann theory, while the RR contribution comes from calculations with a fully quantum mechanism and can be ignored in the limit
E\tau\gg 1
(but not when
E\tau\ll 1
).
G^{R/A}(E)
is the full retarded/advanced Green’s function. In the eigenstate basis, it is

G^{R/A}(\bm{k},E)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g^{R/A}_{+}(\bm{k},E)&0\\
0&g^{R/A}_{-}(\bm{k},E)\end{array}\right)

(8)

where

g^{R/A}_{s}(\bm{k},E)=\frac{1}{a-(1-\alpha)s\hbar v_{f}k\pm i\eta},\,\,\,\,\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}a=E-\Sigma_{1}(E)\\
\eta=-\Sigma_{2}(E)\\
\end{array}\right.

(9)

Plugging Eq. (
3
), Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
9
) into the Kubo formula Eq. (
5
), we obtain the conductivity

\sigma_{xx}(E)=\frac{2e^{2}}{\pi h}\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)^{2}}(1+\frac{a}{\eta}\arctan\frac{a}{\eta})+\frac{2e^{2}}{\pi h}\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)^{2}}\frac{\eta}{a}\arctan\frac{a}{\eta}

(10)

where the first and second terms denote the electron-hole incoherent and coherent contributions, respectively. At the Dirac point (
E=0
), these two parts contribute equally and the minimal conductivity is obtained as

\sigma_{\text{min}}=\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)^{2}}\frac{4e^{2}}{\pi h}.

(11)

Here we would like to give some remarks. First, Eq. (
11
) contains both electron-hole coherent and incoherent contributions, which is beyond traditional Boltzmann transport theory where the electron-hole incoherent contribution from the retarded-retarded (
RR
) channel is usually discarded
Trushin
.
Second, the influence of the momentum-dependent self-energy on the vertex correction is much smaller than the level of the bubble diagram (see Supplemental Materials Sec. S5). This is consistent with previous works showing that the vertex correction is negligible for calculations of the minimal conductivity
Durst
;
Shon
.
Third, from the result Eq. (
11
), it is clear that the minimal conductivity
\sigma_{\text{min}}
is independent of the energy-dependent part of the self-energy function, i.e.
\Sigma_{1}(E)
and
\Sigma_{2}(E)
, if they satisfy the condition,
\frac{a}{\eta}\to 0
when
E\to 0
, but
\sigma_{\text{min}}
is significantly influenced by the momentum dependent term. This behavior explains why the majority of previous theoretical studies predicted
\sigma_{\text{min}}=\frac{4e^{2}}{\pi h}
, independent of the strength and nature of the disorder
Ludwig
;
Nersesyan
;
Ziegler
.
Fourth, for long range disorder, previous calculations within the self-consistent Born approximation reported the momentum contribution to the self-energy function, however, the role of this term on the transport was unfortunately washed out in the widely-used on-shell approximation
Ostrovsky
.
Therefore, we claim that the application of the on-shell approximation around the Dirac point is questionable. Finally, with all of these non-trivial improvements, we point out that Eq. (
11
) shows that the minimal conductivity is enhanced by disorder, in contrast to the results from Boltzmann theory
Shon
;
Adam2009
;
Trushin
.
One interpretation is that the presence of potential fluctuations smooth on the scale of the graphene lattice spacing increases the conductivity through quantum interference effects
Adam
;
Nomura
;
Noro
;
Radchenko
;
Bardarson
;
Rycerz
.

Estimation of
\alpha
by the Born approximation

In order to theoretically incorporate insights gained from the momentum dependent self-energy, we take the long-ranged Gaussian potential as an example, with the correlation function as

\mathcal{K}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})=\langle U(\bm{r})U(\bm{r}^{\prime})\rangle=K_{0}\frac{(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{2\pi\xi^{2}}e^{-|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{2}/2\xi^{2}}

(12)

where
\xi
is the correlation length and
K_{0}
is a dimensionless parameter that parametrizes its magnitude. Its Fourier transformation form is

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\bm{q})=

\displaystyle\int d\bm{r}\mathcal{K}(\bm{r})e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}=K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}\bm{q}^{2}}{2}}.

(13)

Based on the Born approximation, the self-energy is

\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)=\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{K}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})G^{R}_{0}(\bm{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime},E)\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}

(14)

where
G^{R}_{0}(\bm{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime},E)=(E-s^{\prime}\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime}+i0^{+})^{-1}
is the Green’s function of the clean system in the eigenstate basis,
\theta=\theta_{\bm{k}}-\theta_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}
is the angle between the wave vectors
\bm{k}
and
\bm{k}^{\prime}
. In the following, we separate the calculation of the self-energy function into real and imaginary parts, by using the Sokhotsky’s formula,
\frac{1}{x+i0^{+}}=\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{x}-i\pi\delta(x)
, where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the Cauchy principal value.

The imaginary part of the self-energy can be solved as

{\rm Im}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)=-\frac{K_{0}}{4}|E|e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)}\left[I_{0}(\frac{\xi^{2}kE}{\hbar v_{f}})+sI_{1}(\frac{\xi^{2}kE}{\hbar v_{f}})\right]

(15)

where
I_{0}(x)
and
I_{1}(x)
are the zero and first order modified Bessel functions of the first kind
Gradshteyn
;
Abramowitz
.
Meanwhile, the real part of the self-energy is obtained as

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{4\pi}\mathcal{P}\int^{k_{c}}_{-k_{c}}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}(k^{2}+k^{\prime 2})}\frac{I_{0}(\xi^{2}kk^{\prime})+sI_{1}(\xi^{2}kk^{\prime})}{E-\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime}}.

(16)

where
k_{c}
is the ultraviolet momentum cutoff. Focusing on the Dirac physics (
\frac{|E|}{\hbar v_{f}},k\ll\frac{1}{\xi}
), we obtain the self-energy in powers of
k
and
E
,

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}}{4}|E|+\cdots

(17)

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{2\pi}E\ln|\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}k_{c}}|+\alpha E-\alpha s\hbar v_{f}k+\cdots

(18)

where
\cdots
stands for terms in the order of
O(kE^{2},k^{2}E,E^{3})
in imaginary part and
O(kE^{2}\ln E,k^{2}E\ln E,E^{3}\ln E)
in the real part.
From this result, a momentum dependent term appears in
Re\Sigma^{B}
, controlled by a dimensionless coefficient

\alpha=K_{0}\frac{\xi^{2}k^{2}_{c}}{8\pi}.

(19)

Note that, if the on-shell approximation (
E=E_{\bm{k}s}=s\hbar v_{f}k
) is allowed, the momentum dependent correction in Eq. (
48
) vanishes, recovering the previous results
Sarma2011
;
Shon
;
Adam
.

Here we stress that in the literature, the Born approximation is widely applied together with the on-shell approximation, so that the momentum dependence of the self-energy is ignored
Trushin
;
Noro
;
Adam
. Our findings show that in the low energy regime the on-shell approximation is not well justified, i.e. the momentum and energy dependence in the self-energy are decoupled as shown in Eqs. (
47
) and (
48
). Moreover, we find that the momentum dependent terms are less important in the imaginary part of self-energy than those in the real part, since in the latter they are at least two orders larger than the leading term in the imaginary part. Furthermore, our results can recover the predictions in the literature made using the on-shell approximation in the regime far away from the Dirac point
Adam
;
McCann
;
Nersesyan
, where the imaginary part of the self-energy is a constant independent of energy and momentum, and the real part of self-energy disappears (details are shown in Supplemental Material Sec. S2 A). Third, it should be noted that the Born approximation is essentially valid under weak disorder conditions (
K_{0}\lesssim 1
) since it corresponds to the first order of the self-energy perturbation expansion. In this respect, one may wonder how the higher order expansions influence this calculation. We address this by considering a self-consistent Born approximation, and we have also confirmed a momentum dependent self-energy function, when the on-shell approximation is abandoned (see Supplemental Material Sec. S2 B).
Finally, to further improve these perturbation calculations, we perform a renormalization group analysis on our model (Eq.
1
), and we confirm the momentum-dependent self-energy function cannot be neglected near the Dirac point (supplementary materials Sec. S3).
Taken as a whole, different methods including the perturbation theory based on the (self-consistent) Born’s approximation and renormalization group calculation, all reach the same conclusion, which strongly suggests our theoretical findings are robust and universal.

Numerical Simulations

Next we turn to numerical simulations using the Lanczos recursive method in momentum space

of the disordered system in the eigenbasis is directly calculated using the Lanczos recursive method, and the self-energy is obtained by the Dyson equation
\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)=g_{0}^{-1}(\bm{k}s,E)-g^{-1}(\bm{k}s,E)

where

Discussion and Summary

We have shown that in the presence of a long-ranged disorder potential, the self-energy function of the Dirac electrons has a peculiar dependence on the momentum, and this momentum dependence is of paramount importance for the transport properties of graphene, i.e. it produces a non-universal minimal conductivity. Furthermore, we elaborate the origin of this momentum dependent correction analytically and uncover its dependence on the disorder potential, which is also verified by unbiased numerical simulations.

In closing, we would like to make several remarks. First, our findings offer an intuitive way for understanding the existing numerical simulations
Nomura
;
Klos
;
Bardarson
where Coulomb impurities enhance the minimal conductivity.
We believe that the physics behind these numerical calculations is now clear, from clarifying the self-energy function.
Second, another main cautionary message is that the on-shell approximation is not well justified around the Dirac point.
Third, both analytical and numerical results show that the on-shell approximation tends to be valid in the limit
k,\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}\gg\frac{1}{\xi}
, which means that in the high energy region the self-energy function becomes momentum independent, in line with previous theories
Sarma2011
;
Shon
;
Adam
.
Finally, this work implies that a consistent theory for the quantum transport of graphene: Two main experimental observations, i.e. the linear dependence of the conductivity on carrier density
Nomura
and the sample-dependent minimal conductivity, can be well understood by the presence of long-ranged impurity potentials.

Appendix A
Kubo formula for dc conductivity in terms of Green’s function

The derivation of Eqs. (5-7) in the main text, Kubo formula for the dc conductivity has been discussed in detail in some books of many-body quantum theory
Mahan
;
Bruus
, so here we only briefly outline some significant steps. Based on the linear response theory, the dc conductivity given by

\sigma_{\alpha\beta}(E)=-\lim_{\omega\to 0}\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{{\rm Im}\mathcal{C}^{R}_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q},\omega)}{\omega}

(21)

where
\mathcal{C}^{R}_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q},\omega)
is a retarded current-current correlation function,

C^{R}_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q},\omega)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{1}{\mathcal{V}}\int dt\theta(t-t^{\prime})\langle[\hat{J}_{\alpha}(\bm{q},t),\hat{J}_{\beta}(-\bm{q},t^{\prime})]\rangle e^{i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}

(22)

where
\hat{J}
is the current operator,
\mathcal{V}
is the area of sample and the bracket
\langle\cdots\rangle
means the thermodynamic average over disorder. We use the Matsubara method to deal with the retarded correlation function, which indicates that the retarded correlation function is equivalent to the Matsubara function based on a analytical continuation:
\mathcal{C}(iq_{n})\xrightarrow{iq_{n}=\omega+i\eta}C^{R}(\omega)
.

We start the calculation from the Matsubara current-current correlation function,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q},iq_{n})=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar\mathcal{V}}\int_{0}^{\beta}d(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\langle T_{\tau}\hat{J}_{\alpha}(\bm{q},\tau)\hat{J}_{\beta}(-\bm{q},\tau^{\prime})\rangle e^{iq_{n}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\beta}d(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\langle T_{\tau}J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}c_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}(\tau)c_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\tau)J_{\beta,\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{q}}c_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})c_{\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{q}}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle e^{iq_{n}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\beta}d(\tau-\tau^{\prime})e^{iq_{n}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}{\rm Tr}[J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})J_{\beta,\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{q}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k}}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\delta_{\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\int_{0}^{\beta}d(\tau-\tau^{\prime})e^{iq_{n}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}{\rm Tr}[J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})J_{\beta,\bm{k}+\bm{q},\bm{k}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k}}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\int_{0}^{\beta}d(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{1}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{ik_{n}}{\rm Tr}[J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})e^{iq_{n}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}e^{ik_{n}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}J_{\beta,\bm{k}+\bm{q},\bm{k}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k}}(\tau_{1})e^{ik_{n}\tau_{1}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar\mathcal{V}\beta}\sum_{\bm{k},ik_{n}}{\rm Tr}[J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q},ik_{n}+iq_{n}}J_{\beta,\bm{k}+\bm{q},\bm{k}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k},ik_{n}}]

where
\beta=1/k_{B}T
, a closed fermion loop always gives a factor
-1
and the trace of a product of Dirac matrices.
\mathcal{G}
is Matsubara Green’s function. The current operators are been expressed creation and annihilate operators:
\hat{J}_{\alpha}(\bm{q},\tau)=\sum_{\bm{k}}J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}c_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}(\tau)c_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\tau)
. In addition, the identities
1=\int_{0}^{\beta}\delta(\tau_{1}-\tau^{\prime}+\tau)d\tau_{1}
and
\delta(\tau_{1}-\tau^{\prime}+\tau)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{ik_{n}}e^{ik_{n}(\tau_{1}-\tau^{\prime}+\tau)}
are applied. It is noticed that
ik_{n}=i(2n+1)\pi/\beta
denotes Fermi frequency and
iq_{n}=i2n\pi/\beta
denotes Boson frequency.

Next, we treat the summations of Matsubara Green’s functions with known branch cuts. According to the summation of
ik_{n}
, one can introduce a contour integral

I=\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{1}+\mathcal{C}_{2}+\mathcal{C}_{3}}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}f(z)\mathcal{G}(z+iq_{n})\mathcal{G}(z)

(24)

where
f(z)=\frac{1}{e^{\beta z}+1}
and the contours are shown in Fig.
4
, since for variable
z
the branch cut (also singularity line) of
\mathcal{G}(z)
is real axis, i.e.
z={\rm Re}z=\omega^{\prime}
, and the branch cut of
\mathcal{G}(z+iq_{n})
is
z=\omega-iq_{n}
. On the one hand, we calculate
I
by residue theorem

(25)

On the other hand, we calculate
I

(27)

\displaystyle+\left.J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q},\omega^{\prime}+iq_{n}}J_{\beta,\bm{k}+\bm{q},\bm{k}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k},\omega^{\prime}+i0^{+}}-J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q},\omega^{\prime}+iq_{n}}J_{\beta,\bm{k}+\bm{q},\bm{k}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k},\omega^{\prime}-i0^{+}}\right]

Then, we do the analytical continuity
iq_{n}\to\omega+i0^{+}
and get

\displaystyle C^{R}_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q},\omega)=

(28)

\displaystyle+\left.J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q},\omega^{\prime}+\omega+i0^{+}}J_{\beta,\bm{k}+\bm{q},\bm{k}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k},\omega^{\prime}+i0^{+}}-J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q},\omega^{\prime}+\omega+i0^{+}}J_{\beta,\bm{k}+\bm{q},\bm{k}}\mathcal{G}_{\bm{k},\omega^{\prime}-i0^{+}}\right]

\displaystyle+f(\omega^{\prime})\left[J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}G^{R}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\omega^{\prime}+\omega)J_{\beta,\bm{k}+\bm{q},\bm{k}}G^{R}_{\bm{k}}(\omega^{\prime})-J_{\alpha,\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}G^{R}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\omega^{\prime}+\omega)J_{\beta,\bm{k}+\bm{q},\bm{k}}G^{A}_{\bm{k}}(\omega^{\prime})\right]

Appendix B
Analytical Derivation of Self-Energy Function

B.1
Long-ranged Gaussian Potential (Born approximation)

In this section, we evaluate the self-energy function in the presence of long-ranged Gaussian potential by the Born approximation:

\displaystyle\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k},E)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\overline{|V(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})|^{2}}U^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}U_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}G^{R}_{0}(\bm{k}^{\prime},E)U^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}U_{\bm{k}}

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{K}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(g_{0}^{+}+g_{0}^{-})+\cos\theta(g_{0}^{+}-g_{0}^{-})&i\sin\theta(g_{0}^{+}-g_{0}^{-})\\
-i\sin\theta(g_{0}^{+}-g_{0}^{-})&(g_{0}^{+}+g_{0}^{-})-\cos\theta(g_{0}^{+}-g_{0}^{-})\end{array}\right)

where
\overline{\cdots}
represents the average of random configurations,
\theta=\theta_{\bm{k}}-\theta_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}
is the angle between momentum
\bm{k}
and
\bm{k}^{\prime}
, and
g_{0}^{\pm}=G^{R}_{0}(\bm{k}^{\prime}\pm,E)=1/(E+i\eta-E_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\pm})
is the retarded Green’s function for eigenstate with momentum
\bm{k}^{\prime}
and chiral
s=\pm 1
. Based on the parity analysis, we find the integral of off-diagonal term is vanishing so that the first-order self-energy function in eigenstates basis can be expressed as

\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)=\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{K}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})G^{R}_{0}(\bm{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime},E)\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}

(33)

which is the Eq.(14) in the main paper. After plugging the expression of
\mathcal{K}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})
into this equation, it is obtained

\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)=K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})^{2}}{2}}G^{R}_{0}(\bm{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime},E)\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}

(34)

For
E>0
, the imaginary part of the self-energy is

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)=

\displaystyle K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})^{2}}{2}}\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}[-\pi\delta(E-s^{\prime}\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})]

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi K_{0}\hbar v_{f}}{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{k^{\prime}dk^{\prime}d\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}(k^{2}+k^{\prime 2}-2kk^{\prime}\cos\theta)}{2}}(1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta)\delta(\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}-s^{\prime}k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}}{8\pi}Ee^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}d\theta e^{\frac{\xi^{2}kE}{\hbar v_{f}}\cos\theta}(1+s\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}}{4}Ee^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)}\left[I_{0}(\frac{\xi^{2}kE}{\hbar v_{f}})+sI_{1}(\frac{\xi^{2}kE}{\hbar v_{f}})\right]

where
\tilde{E}=\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}
. In the final expression above, we have introduced modified Bessel functions of the first kind, whose integral representation is

I_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{\pi}_{0}e^{z\cos\theta}\cos\alpha\theta d\theta-\frac{\sin\alpha\pi}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-z\cosh t-\alpha t}dt

(36)

and has series expansion,

I_{\alpha}(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{m=0}\frac{1}{m!\Gamma(m+\alpha+1)}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2m+\alpha},

(37)

asymptotic expansion for large
z
,

I_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi z}\sqrt[4]{1+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{z^{2}}}}\exp\left[-\alpha{\rm arsinh}\left(\frac{\alpha}{z}\right)+z\sqrt{1+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{z^{2}}}\right]\left[1+o\left(\frac{1}{z\sqrt{1+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{z^{2}}}}\right)\right].

(38)

Similarly, for
E<0
, the imaginary part of the self-energy is

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)=

\displaystyle K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})^{2}}{2}}\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}[-\pi\delta(E-s^{\prime}\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})]

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi K_{0}\hbar v_{f}}{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{k^{\prime}dk^{\prime}d\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}(k^{2}+k^{\prime 2}-2kk^{\prime}\cos\theta)}{2}}(1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta)\delta(\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}-s^{\prime}k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{8\pi}Ee^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}d\theta e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}kE\cos\theta}{\hbar v_{f}}}(1-s\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{4}Ee^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)}\left[I_{0}(-\frac{\xi^{2}kE}{\hbar v_{f}})-sI_{1}(-\frac{\xi^{2}kE}{\hbar v_{f}})\right]

Combining two conditions, we can get

{\rm Im}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)=-\frac{K_{0}}{4}|E|e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)}\left[I_{0}(\frac{\xi^{2}kE}{\hbar v_{f}})+sI_{1}(\frac{\xi^{2}kE}{\hbar v_{f}})\right]

(40)

For the limit
k,\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}\ll\frac{1}{\xi}
, the above result can be expanded using Eq.(
37
) as

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}}{4}|E|\left[1-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)+O(k^{4},E^{4},k^{2}E^{2})\right]\left[1+s\frac{\xi^{2}kE}{2\hbar v_{f}}+O(k^{2}E^{2})\right]

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}|E|}{4}\left[1-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)+\frac{s\xi^{2}kE}{2\hbar v_{f}}\right]+O(k^{4},E^{4},k^{2}E^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}|E|}{4}+O(kE^{2},k^{2}E,E^{3})

which is the Eq.(17) in the main paper.

For the limit
k,\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}\gg\frac{1}{\xi}
, it has following asymptotic expression by using Eq.(
38
)

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}}{4}|E|e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)}e^{\frac{\xi^{2}k|E|}{\hbar v_{f}}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar v_{f}}{2\pi\xi^{2}k|E|}}\left[1+s{\rm sgn}(E)\right]

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}}{4}|E|e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k-\frac{|E|}{\hbar v_{f}}\right)^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar v_{f}}{2\pi\xi^{2}k|E|}}\left[1+s{\rm sgn}(E)\right]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{4\xi^{2}}\delta(E-E_{\bm{k}s})

where
{\rm sgn}(E)
is sign function of
E
. The exponential decay term
e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k-\frac{|E|}{\hbar v_{f}}\right)^{2}}
and the term
\left[1+s{\rm sgn}(E)\right]
in the above at the second equation imply that the on-shell approximation (
E=E_{\bm{k}s}=s\hbar v_{f}k
) is valid in this condition based on the approximation
\lim\limits_{\sigma\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma}}\exp(-x^{2}/2\sigma)=\delta(x)
. The value of
{\rm Im}\Sigma^{B}
we obtained under the on-shell approximation is a momentum and energy independent constant.

The corresponding real part of self-energy function can be calculated by the Kramer-Kronig relation,

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\mathcal{P}\int^{E_{c}}_{-E_{c}}dE^{\prime}\frac{{\rm Im}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E^{\prime})}{E^{\prime}-E}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\mathcal{P}\int^{E_{c}}_{-E_{c}}dE^{\prime}\frac{-\frac{K_{0}}{4}|E^{\prime}|e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{\prime 2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)}\left[I_{0}(\frac{\xi^{2}kE^{\prime}}{\hbar v_{f}})+sI_{1}(\frac{\xi^{2}kE^{\prime}}{\hbar v_{f}})\right]}{E^{\prime}-E}

which is equivalent to the Eq.(16) in the main paper.

For the limit
k,\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}\ll\frac{1}{\xi}
, the corresponding real part of self-energy function is

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\mathcal{P}\int^{E_{c}}_{-E_{c}}dE^{\prime}\frac{-\frac{K_{0}}{4}|E^{\prime}|[1-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{\prime 2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)]\left[1+\frac{1}{4}(\frac{\xi^{2}kE^{\prime}}{\hbar v_{f}})^{2}+s\frac{\xi^{2}kE^{\prime}}{2\hbar v_{f}}\right]}{E^{\prime}-E}

(44)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\mathcal{P}\int^{E_{c}}_{-E_{c}}dE^{\prime}\frac{-\frac{K_{0}}{4}|E^{\prime}|\left[1+s\frac{\xi^{2}kE^{\prime}}{2\hbar v_{f}}-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}+\frac{E^{\prime 2}}{\hbar^{2}v^{2}_{f}}\right)\right]}{E^{\prime}-E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}\hbar v_{f}}{4\pi}\mathcal{P}\left[\int^{0}_{-k_{c}}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\frac{-\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\frac{s\xi^{2}k\tilde{E}^{\prime 2}}{2}+\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\tilde{E}^{\prime}(k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{\prime 2})}{\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E}}+\int_{0}^{k_{c}}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}+\frac{s\xi^{2}k\tilde{E}^{\prime 2}}{2}-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\tilde{E}^{\prime}(k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{\prime 2})}{\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}\hbar v_{f}}{4\pi}\mathcal{P}\int_{0}^{k_{c}}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\left[\frac{-\tilde{E}^{\prime}+\frac{s\xi^{2}k\tilde{E}^{\prime 2}}{2}+\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\tilde{E}^{\prime}(k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{\prime 2})}{\tilde{E}^{\prime}+\tilde{E}}+\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}+\frac{s\xi^{2}k\tilde{E}^{\prime 2}}{2}-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\tilde{E}^{\prime}(k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{\prime 2})}{\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}\hbar v_{f}}{4\pi}\mathcal{P}\int_{0}^{k_{c}}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{\tilde{E}^{\prime 2}-\tilde{E}^{2}}2\tilde{E}+\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime 3}}{\tilde{E}^{\prime 2}-\tilde{E}^{2}}s\xi^{2}k-\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}(k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{\prime 2})}{\tilde{E}^{\prime 2}-\tilde{E}^{2}}\xi^{2}\tilde{E}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}\hbar v_{f}}{2\pi}\left[\tilde{E}(\ln|\tilde{E}|-\ln|k_{c}|)-s\left(\frac{\xi^{2}k_{c}^{2}}{4}\right)k+\left(\frac{\xi^{2}k_{c}^{2}}{4}\right)\tilde{E}\right]+O(kE^{2}\ln E,k^{2}E\ln E,E^{3}\ln E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{2\pi}E\ln|\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}k_{c}}|+\alpha E-s\alpha\hbar v_{f}k+O(kE^{2}\ln E,k^{2}E\ln E,E^{3}\ln E)

with

\alpha=K_{0}\frac{\xi^{2}k_{c}^{2}}{8\pi}

(45)

so that we obtain the Eqs.(18) and (19) in the main paper. Here,
\tilde{E}=\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}
,
\tilde{E}^{\prime}=\frac{E^{\prime}}{\hbar v_{f}}
, and
k_{c}=\frac{E_{c}}{\hbar v_{f}}
. The modified Bessel functions are expanded as Eq. (
37
), and we keep the leading orders up to:
I_{0}(x)=1+\frac{x^{2}}{4}+o(x^{3})
and
I_{1}(x)=\frac{x}{2}+o(x^{3})
.

Then combining Eq.(
42
) and (
43
), we can obtain
{\rm Re}\Sigma^{B}
at the limit
k,\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}\gg\frac{1}{\xi}
as

{\rm Re}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)\approx\frac{K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{E-E_{\bm{k}s}}{\xi^{2}(E-E_{\bm{k}s})^{2}+(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}

(46)

To sum up, we obtain the self-energy function for
\frac{|E|}{\hbar v_{f}},k\ll\frac{1}{\xi}

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}}{4}|E|+\cdots

(47)

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{2\pi}E\ln|\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}k_{c}}|+\alpha E-\alpha s\hbar v_{f}k+\cdots

(48)

and for
k,\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}\gg\frac{1}{\xi}

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{4\xi^{2}}\delta(E-E_{\bm{k}s})

(49)

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\Sigma^{B}(\bm{k}s,E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{E-E_{\bm{k}s}}{\xi^{2}(E-E_{\bm{k}s})^{2}+(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}

(50)

B.2
Long-ranged Gaussian Potential (SCBA)

Going beyond the Born approximation, we try to calculate the self-energy function using the self-consistent Born approximation (SCBA). It is expected that SCBA contains partial higher-order corrections in the perturbative expansion. Inspired by the Born approximation, we assume the self-energy function at the limit
k,\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}\ll\frac{1}{\xi}
can be expressed in the form of

\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)=\Sigma_{1}(E)-s\alpha\hbar v_{f}k+i\Sigma_{2}(E)=\tilde{\Sigma}(E)-s\alpha\hbar v_{f}k

(51)

where
\tilde{\Sigma}(E)=\Sigma_{1}(E)+i\Sigma_{2}(E)
is the part that depends only on energy. Thus, the Feynman diagram of SCBA is given by

\displaystyle\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{K}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})G(\bm{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime},E)\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})^{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{E-E_{\bm{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime}}-\Sigma(\bm{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime},E)}\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})^{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{E-s^{\prime}(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime}-\tilde{\Sigma}}\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{2}\int\frac{k^{\prime}dk^{\prime}d\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})^{2}}{2}}\left[\frac{1+s\cos\theta}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})-(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime}}+\frac{1-s\cos\theta}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})+(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{k_{c}}k^{\prime}dk^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}(k^{2}+k^{\prime 2}-2kk^{\prime}\cos\theta)}{2}}\frac{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})+s\cos\theta(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-(1-\alpha)^{2}(\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{k_{c}}k^{\prime}dk^{\prime}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}(k^{2}+k^{\prime 2})}{2}}\left[\frac{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-(1-\alpha)^{2}(\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})^{2}}I_{0}(kk^{\prime}\xi^{2})+\frac{s(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-(1-\alpha)^{2}(\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})^{2}}I_{1}(kk^{\prime}\xi^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{k_{c}}k^{\prime}dk^{\prime}[1+o(k^{2}\xi^{2})]e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}k^{\prime 2}}{2}}\frac{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})[1+o(k^{2}\xi^{2})]+s(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime}[\frac{kk^{\prime}\xi^{2}}{2}+o(k^{2}\xi^{2})]}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-(1-\alpha)^{2}(\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})^{2}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{k_{c}}k^{\prime}dk^{\prime}\frac{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}k^{\prime 2}}{2}}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-(1-\alpha)^{2}(\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})^{2}}+s(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k\frac{\frac{\xi^{2}k^{\prime 2}}{2}e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}k^{\prime 2}}{2}}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-(1-\alpha)^{2}(\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\xi^{2}k_{c}^{2}}{2}}dx\frac{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})e^{-x}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}-x}+s(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k\frac{xe^{-x}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}-x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}\left[(E-\tilde{\Sigma})e^{-(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}}\left({\rm Ei}[(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}]-{\rm Ei}[(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}-\Lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}]\right)-s(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k(1-e^{-\Lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+s(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}e^{-(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}}\left({\rm Ei}[(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}]-{\rm Ei}[(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}-\Lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}]\right)\right]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}\left\{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})\left[\ln\frac{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-\Lambda^{2}}+\Lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}\right]-s(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k\left[\Lambda^{2}-(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\ln\frac{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-\Lambda^{2}}\right]\tilde{\xi}^{2}\right\}

where
\tilde{\xi}^{2}=\frac{\xi^{2}}{2(\hbar v_{f})^{2}(1-\alpha)^{2}}
,
\tilde{k}=s(1-\alpha)E_{c}
,
E_{c}=\hbar v_{f}k_{c}
, and
\Lambda=(1-\alpha)(\hbar v_{f}k_{c})
.
{\rm Ei}(x)
is exponential integral function and can be expanded as
{\rm Ei}(x)=\gamma+\ln x+x+o(x^{2})
. The first term in square brackets is consistent with the short-range disorder when setting
\alpha=0
and
\xi=0
, which can be solved in two limits
|E|\ll\Gamma_{0}
and
|E|\gg\Gamma_{0}
, where
\Gamma_{0}=E_{c}e^{-2\pi/K_{0}}
is an exponential small energy scale defined by the imaginary part of self-energy at Dirac point
Ostrovsky
. Similarly, we can simplify the first term in square brackets in the last line of Eq.(
52
) at two limits.

At the limit
|E|\ll\Gamma_{0}
, we estimate

\displaystyle(E-\tilde{\Sigma})\ln\frac{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-\Lambda^{2}}\approx

\displaystyle(E-\Sigma_{1}-i\Sigma_{2})\ln\frac{\Sigma_{2}^{2}+2i\Sigma_{2}(E-\Sigma_{1})}{\Lambda^{2}}=(E-\Sigma_{1}-i\Sigma_{2})\left[\ln\frac{\Sigma_{2}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+\ln(1+2i\frac{E-\Sigma_{1}}{\Sigma_{2}})\right]

(53)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle(E-\Sigma_{1}-i\Sigma_{2})\left[\ln\frac{\Sigma_{2}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+2i\frac{E-\Sigma_{1}}{\Sigma_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle(E-\Sigma_{1})\ln\frac{\Sigma_{2}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+2(E-\Sigma_{1})-i\Sigma_{2}\ln\frac{\Sigma_{2}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}

and

\displaystyle-(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\ln\frac{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-\Lambda^{2}}\approx

\displaystyle[2i(E-\Sigma_{1})\Sigma_{2}+\Sigma_{2}^{2}]\left[\ln\frac{\Sigma_{2}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+2i\frac{E-\Sigma_{1}}{\Sigma_{2}}\right]\approx\Sigma_{2}^{2}\ln\frac{\Sigma_{2}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}

(54)

In this condition, the Eq. (
52
) can be further reduced as

\displaystyle\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)\approx\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}\left\{(E-\Sigma_{1})\ln\frac{\Sigma_{2}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+2(E-\Sigma_{1})-i\Sigma_{2}\ln\frac{\Sigma_{2}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}-s(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k\Lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}\right\}

(55)

The above self-consistent equation can be separated into three parts corresponding to the
\Sigma_{1}
,
i\Sigma_{2}
, and
-s\alpha\hbar v_{f}k
parts in the self-energy function,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}\left[(E-\Sigma_{1})\ln\frac{\Sigma_{2}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+2(E-\Sigma_{1})\right]

(56)

\displaystyle i\Sigma_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}(-i\Sigma_{2})\ln\frac{\Sigma_{2}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle-s\alpha\hbar v_{f}k=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}[-s(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k]\Lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}

Solving the above self-consistent equations, we can approximately obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}{K_{0}}E

(57)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}=

\displaystyle-\Lambda e^{-\frac{2\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}{K_{0}}}

\displaystyle\alpha=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-K_{0}\frac{\xi^{2}k_{c}^{2}}{8\pi}}

At the limit
|E|\gg|{\rm Im}\Sigma|
, we estimate

(E-\tilde{\Sigma})\ln\frac{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-\Lambda^{2}}\approx E\ln\frac{E^{2}e^{-i\pi{\rm sgn}(E)}}{\Lambda^{2}}=E\left[\ln\frac{E^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}-i\pi{\rm sgn}(E)\right]=E\ln\frac{E^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}-i\pi|E|

(58)

\displaystyle-(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}\ln\frac{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}}{(E-\tilde{\Sigma})^{2}-\Lambda^{2}}\approx

\displaystyle-[E^{2}-2iE\Sigma_{2}]\left[\ln\frac{E^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}-i\pi{\rm sgn}(E)\right]\approx-E^{2}\ln\frac{E^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+i\pi E|E|

(59)

In this condition, the Eq. (
52
) can be further reduced as

\displaystyle\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)\approx\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}\left\{E\ln\frac{E^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}-i\pi|E|+(E-i\Sigma_{2})\Lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}-s(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k\Lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}\right\}

(60)

Similarly, this self-consistent equation can be solved by separating into three parts

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}(E\ln\frac{E^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+E\Lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2})

(61)

\displaystyle i\Sigma_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}(-i\pi|E|-i\Sigma_{2}\Lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2})

\displaystyle s\alpha\hbar v_{f}k=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}[-s(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k]\Lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{2}

Solving the above self-consistent equations, we can approximately obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}E\ln\frac{E^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+\frac{K_{0}\xi^{2}k_{c}^{2}}{8\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}E

(62)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}|E|

\displaystyle\alpha=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-K_{0}\frac{\xi^{2}k_{c}^{2}}{8\pi}}

Combining Eq. (
57
and Eq. (
62
, the resulting self-energy function obtained from SCBA is

\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\frac{2\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}{K_{0}}E-s\alpha\hbar v_{f}k-i\Lambda e^{-\frac{2\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}{K_{0}}};&|E|\ll\Gamma_{0}\\
&\frac{K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}E\ln\frac{E^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}E-s\alpha\hbar v_{f}k-i\frac{\pi K_{0}}{4\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}}|E|;&|E|\gg\Gamma_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

(63)

with

\alpha=\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-K_{0}\frac{\xi^{2}k_{c}^{2}}{8\pi}}.

(64)

From the SCBA’s results, we find that the dimensionless parameter
\alpha
, which controls the momentum linear dependent term in self-energy, has a upper limit
\frac{1}{2}
. Please note that this result naturally revert to the Born’s result at weak disorder limit,
\alpha\xrightarrow{K_{0}\frac{\xi^{2}k_{c}^{2}}{8\pi}\ll\frac{1}{4}}K_{0}\frac{\xi^{2}k_{c}^{2}}{8\pi}
. To sum up, we prove that the self-energy function is momentum-dependent via the SCBA.
More Feynman diagrams beyond the SCBA is hard to calculate, which will be further validated by the numerical simulations.

B.3
Long-range Screened Coulomb Potential

For simplicity, most of our discussions in this paper are based on the random Gaussian potential. Here, we present a calculation using the long-ranged screened Coulomb potential. We will elucidate that, the main findings shown in this paper are robust, independent of the specific form of impurity potential.

The long-range screened Coulomb potential has the form in real space as

V(\bm{r})=\pm\frac{e^{2}}{\kappa r}e^{-q_{s}r}

(65)

where
\kappa
is the static dielectric constant, scatters of
\pm
are randomly distributed with equal probability, and
q_{s}
is the Thomas-Fermi screening constant. It can be written in momentum space as

\displaystyle V(\bm{q})=

\displaystyle\int d\bm{r}V(\bm{r})e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}=\pm\int d\bm{r}\frac{e^{2}}{\kappa r}e^{-q_{s}r}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}=\pm\int rdrd\theta\frac{e^{2}}{\kappa r}e^{-(q_{s}+iq\cos\theta)r}=\pm\frac{e^{2}}{\kappa}\int drd\theta e^{-(q_{s}+iq\cos\theta)r}

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{e^{2}}{\kappa}\int d\theta\frac{e^{-(q_{s}+iq\cos\theta)r}}{q_{s}+iq\cos\theta}\bigg|^{0}_{+\infty}=\pm\frac{e^{2}}{\kappa}\int d\theta\frac{1}{q_{s}+iq\cos\theta}=\pm\frac{e^{2}}{\kappa}\frac{2}{\sqrt{q^{2}_{s}+q^{2}}}\arctan(\sqrt{\frac{q_{s}-iq}{q_{s}+iq}}\tan\frac{\theta}{2})\bigg|^{\pi}_{-\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{e^{2}}{\kappa}\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{q_{s}^{2}+q^{2}}}

Similar to the long-ranged Gaussian potential, we obtain the self-energy of long-range screened Coulomb potential by Born approximation

\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)=\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{V^{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})}{\mathcal{V}}G^{R}_{0}(\bm{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime},E)\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}

(67)

After plugging the expression of
V(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})
into this equation, it is obtained

\displaystyle\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{N_{i}}{\mathcal{V}}(\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\kappa})^{2}\frac{1}{q_{s}^{2}+|\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}|^{2}}G^{R}_{0}(\bm{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime},E)\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}

(68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{n_{i}V_{0}^{2}}{q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+k^{\prime 2}-2kk^{\prime}\cos\theta}G^{R}_{0}(\bm{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime},E)\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}

where
N_{i}
and
n_{i}=N_{i}/\mathcal{V}
are the number and concentration of impurities and
V_{0}=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\kappa}
. Then we the above equation under the domain
\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}},k\ll q_{s}
.

For
E>0
, the imaginary part of the self-energy is

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{n_{i}V_{0}^{2}}{q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+k^{\prime 2}-2kk^{\prime}\cos\theta}\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}[-\pi\delta(E-s^{\prime}\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})]

(69)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi n_{i}V_{0}^{2}}{2}\int\frac{k^{\prime}dk^{\prime}d\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1+s\cos\theta}{q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+k^{\prime 2}-2kk^{\prime}\cos\theta}\delta(E-\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{i}V_{0}^{2}\tilde{E}}{8\pi\hbar v_{f}}\int d\theta\frac{1+s\cos\theta}{q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{2}-2k\tilde{E}\cos\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{i}V_{0}^{2}\tilde{E}}{8\pi\hbar v_{f}}\left(-\frac{1}{2k\tilde{E}}\right)\int d\theta\frac{s(q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{2}-2k\tilde{E}\cos\theta)-[s(q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{2})+2k\tilde{E}]}{q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{2}-2k\tilde{E}\cos\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{16\pi\hbar v_{f}k}\left\{2\pi s-[s(q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{2})+2k\tilde{E}]\int d\theta\frac{1}{q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{2}-2k\tilde{E}\cos\theta}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{8\hbar v_{f}k}\left[s-\frac{s(q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{2})+2k\tilde{E}}{\sqrt{(q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{2}-2k\tilde{E})(q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{2}+2k\tilde{E})}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{8\hbar v_{f}k}s\left[1-\sqrt{\frac{q^{2}_{s}+(k+s\tilde{E})^{2}}{q^{2}_{s}+(k-s\tilde{E})^{2}}}\right]=\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{8\hbar v_{f}k}s\left[1-\sqrt{1+\frac{4sk\tilde{E}}{q^{2}_{s}+(k-s\tilde{E})^{2}}}\right]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{4\hbar v_{f}}\frac{\tilde{E}}{q^{2}_{s}+(k-s\tilde{E})^{2}}

where
\tilde{E}=\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}}
.

For
E<0
, the imaginary part of the self-energy is

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{n_{i}V_{0}^{2}}{q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+k^{\prime 2}-2kk^{\prime}\cos\theta}\frac{1+ss^{\prime}\cos\theta}{2}[-\pi\delta(E-s^{\prime}\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})]

(70)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi n_{i}V_{0}^{2}}{2}\int\frac{k^{\prime}dk^{\prime}d\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1-s\cos\theta}{q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+k^{\prime 2}-2kk^{\prime}\cos\theta}\delta(E+\hbar v_{f}k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}V_{0}^{2}\tilde{E}}{8\pi\hbar v_{f}}\int d\theta\frac{1-s\cos\theta}{q_{s}^{2}+k^{2}+\tilde{E}^{2}+2k\tilde{E}\cos\theta}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{4\hbar v_{f}}\frac{\tilde{E}}{q^{2}_{s}+(k-s\tilde{E})^{2}}

Combining two conditions, we can get

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)=-\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{4\hbar v_{f}}\frac{|\tilde{E}|}{q^{2}_{s}+(k-s\tilde{E})^{2}}

(71)

Then the corresponding real part of self-energy function can be calculated by the Kramer-Kronig relation

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\mathcal{P}\int^{E_{c}}_{-E_{c}}dE^{\prime}\frac{{\rm Im}\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E^{\prime})}{E^{\prime}-E}=-\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{4\pi\hbar v_{f}}\int^{\tilde{E}_{c}}_{-\tilde{E}_{c}}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\frac{1}{\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E}}\frac{|\tilde{E}^{\prime}|}{q^{2}_{s}+(k-s\tilde{E}^{\prime})^{2}}

(72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{4\pi\hbar v_{f}}\left\{\int^{\tilde{E}_{c}}_{0}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{(\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E})[q^{2}_{s}+(k-s\tilde{E}^{\prime})^{2}]}+\int_{-\tilde{E}_{c}}^{0}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\frac{-\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{(\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E})[q^{2}_{s}+(k-s\tilde{E}^{\prime})^{2}]}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{4\pi\hbar v_{f}}\left\{\int^{\tilde{E}_{c}}_{0}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{(\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E})[q^{2}_{s}+(k-s\tilde{E}^{\prime})^{2}]}+\int^{\tilde{E}_{c}}_{0}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{(-\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E})[q^{2}_{s}+(k+s\tilde{E}^{\prime})^{2}]}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{4\pi\hbar v_{f}}\int^{\tilde{E}_{c}}_{0}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\left\{\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{(\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E})[q^{2}_{s}+(k-s\tilde{E}^{\prime})^{2}]}-\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{(\tilde{E}^{\prime}+\tilde{E})[q^{2}_{s}+(k+s\tilde{E}^{\prime})^{2}]}\right\}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{4\pi\hbar v_{f}q^{2}_{s}}\int^{\tilde{E}_{c}}_{0}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\left\{\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E}}\left[1-\frac{(k-s\tilde{E}^{\prime})^{2}}{q^{2}_{s}}\right]-\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{\tilde{E}^{\prime}+\tilde{E}}\left[1-\frac{(k+s\tilde{E}^{\prime})^{2}}{q^{2}_{s}}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{4\pi\hbar v_{f}q^{2}_{s}}\int^{\tilde{E}_{c}}_{0}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\left[\left(\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E}}-\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{\tilde{E}^{\prime}+\tilde{E}}\right)(1-\frac{k^{2}}{q^{2}_{s}}-\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime 2}}{q^{2}_{s}})+\left(\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{\tilde{E}^{\prime}-\tilde{E}}+\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{\tilde{E}^{\prime}+\tilde{E}}\right)\frac{2sk\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{q^{2}_{s}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{2\pi\hbar v_{f}q^{2}_{s}}\int^{\tilde{E}_{c}}_{0}d\tilde{E}^{\prime}\left[\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime}}{\tilde{E}^{\prime 2}-\tilde{E}^{2}}\tilde{E}(1-\frac{k^{2}}{q^{2}_{s}})+\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime 3}}{\tilde{E}^{\prime 2}-\tilde{E}^{2}}\frac{2sk}{q^{2}_{s}}-\frac{\tilde{E}^{\prime 3}}{\tilde{E}^{\prime 2}-\tilde{E}^{2}}\frac{\tilde{E}}{q^{2}_{s}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{2\pi\hbar v_{f}q^{2}_{s}}\left\{\tilde{E}(\ln\tilde{E}_{c}-\ln\tilde{E})(1-\frac{k^{2}}{q^{2}_{s}})+\left[\frac{1}{2}\tilde{E}^{2}_{c}+\tilde{E}^{2}(\ln\tilde{E}_{c}-\ln\tilde{E})\right]\left(\frac{2sk}{q^{2}_{s}}-\frac{\tilde{E}}{q^{2}_{s}}\right)\right\}

where
E_{c}=\hbar v_{f}k_{c}
.

Thus, if we consider the domain
\frac{E}{\hbar v_{f}},k\ll q_{s}
, the self-energy function can be estimated as

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{2\pi(\hbar v_{f})^{2}q^{2}_{s}}E\ln|\frac{E}{E_{c}}|+\alpha E-s\alpha\hbar v_{f}k+O(kE^{2}\ln E,k^{2}E\ln E,E^{3}\ln E)

(73)

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{i}V^{2}_{0}}{4(\hbar v_{f})^{2}q^{2}_{s}}|E|+O(kE^{2}\ln E,k^{2}E\ln E,E^{3}\ln E)

(74)

where

\alpha=\frac{n_{i}V_{0}^{2}E^{2}_{c}}{2\pi(\hbar v_{f}q_{s})^{2}}

(75)

It can be seen from the above derivation that, the self-energy function of long-range Screened Coulomb potential has same expression as that of long-ranged Gaussian potential expect for the value of
\alpha
. Physically, it can be understood from the following picture: Around the Dirac point, the form of random potential is not important, because the wave-length of electron is longer than the spatial range of random potentials. So the different forms of random potentials give the similar results, as we shown in the paper.
However, away from the Dirac point, the form of disorder potential is relevant.

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{4\pi}[2\ln b+(1-b^{-2})\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}]

\displaystyle I^{(c)}=

\displaystyle\int_{[\Lambda/b,\Lambda]}\frac{d^{2}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{K}(-\bm{p})\mathcal{K}(\bm{p})\mathcal{G}^{0}(\bm{p},\omega)\mathcal{G}^{0}(-\bm{p},\omega)

(82)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{4}\int_{[\Lambda/b,\Lambda]}\frac{d^{2}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\xi^{2}p^{2}}\frac{1}{i\omega-\hbar v_{f}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma}}\frac{1}{i\omega+\hbar v_{f}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma}}=K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{4}\int_{[\Lambda/b,\Lambda]}\frac{d^{2}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\xi^{2}p^{2}}\frac{(i\omega+\hbar v_{f}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma})(i\omega-\hbar v_{f}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma})}{[\omega^{2}+(\hbar v_{f}p)^{2}]^{2}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}\int_{\Lambda/b}^{\Lambda}\frac{pdp}{2\pi}e^{-\xi^{2}p^{2}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}=-\frac{K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{4\pi}[{\rm Ei}(-\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2})-{\rm Ei}(-\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}/b^{2})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{4\pi}[2\ln b+(1-b^{-2})\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}]

\displaystyle I^{(d)}=

\displaystyle\int_{[\Lambda/b,\Lambda]}\frac{d^{2}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{K}(0)\mathcal{K}(-\bm{p})\mathcal{G}^{0}(\bm{p},\omega)\mathcal{G}^{0}(\bm{p},\omega)

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{4}\int_{[\Lambda/b,\Lambda]}\frac{d^{2}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\xi^{2}p^{2}/2}\frac{1}{i\omega-\hbar v_{f}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma}}\frac{1}{i\omega-\hbar v_{f}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma}}=K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{4}\int_{[\Lambda/b,\Lambda]}\frac{d^{2}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-\xi^{2}p^{2}/2}\frac{(i\omega+\hbar v_{f}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma})^{2}}{[\omega^{2}+(\hbar v_{f}p)^{2}]^{2}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}\int_{\Lambda/b}^{\Lambda}\frac{pdp}{2\pi}e^{-\xi^{2}p^{2}/2}\frac{1}{p^{2}}=\frac{K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{4\pi}[{\rm Ei}(-\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}/2)-{\rm Ei}(-\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}/2b^{2})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{4\pi}[2\ln b+(1-b^{-2})\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}/2]

where
{\rm Ei}(x)
is exponential integral function and can be expanded as
{\rm Ei}(-x)=\gamma+\ln x-x+o(x^{2})
. The results of RG diagrams (b) [
I^{(b)}
] and (c) [
I^{(c)}
] cancel each other out.

Plugging Eqs. (
79
)-(
83
) into Eq. (
78
), therefore, we can get the effective generating functional in the shell
|k|<\Lambda/b
as

\displaystyle\langle Z\rangle_{\text{eff}}=

\displaystyle\int D[\bar{\Psi}^{<},\Psi^{<}]e^{S_{0}[\bar{\Psi}^{<},\Psi^{<}]}\left\{1+\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\frac{d^{2}\bm{q}d^{2}\bm{k}d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{6}}\mathcal{K}(\bm{q})\bar{\Psi}^{<}_{\bm{k},\tau}\Psi^{<}_{\bm{k}-\bm{q},\tau}\Psi^{<}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q},\tau^{\prime}}\bar{\Psi}^{<}_{\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}+\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\bar{\Psi}^{<}_{\bm{k},\omega}\Psi^{<}_{\bm{k},\omega}(\Delta_{E}i\omega+\alpha\hbar v_{f}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\sigma})\right.

(84)

\displaystyle+\Delta_{K}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\frac{d^{2}\bm{q}d^{2}\bm{k}d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{6}}\bar{\Psi}^{<}_{\bm{k},\tau_{1}}\Psi^{<}_{\bm{k}-\bm{q},\tau_{1}}\bar{\Psi}^{<}_{\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau_{2}}\Psi^{<}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q},\tau_{2}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int D[\bar{\Psi},\Psi]_{\Lambda/b}

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\bar{\Psi}_{\bm{k},\omega}[(1+\Delta_{E})i\omega-(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\sigma}]\Psi_{\bm{k},\omega}+\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\frac{d^{2}\bm{q}d^{2}\bm{k}d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{6}}[\mathcal{K}(\bm{q})+\Delta_{K}]\bar{\Psi}_{\bm{k},\tau}\Psi_{\bm{k}-\bm{q},\tau}\Psi_{\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q},\tau^{\prime}}\bar{\Psi}_{\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}\right\}

with

\Delta_{E}=\frac{K_{0}\ln b}{2\pi};\ \ \ \alpha=\frac{K_{0}\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{8\pi}(1-b^{-2});\ \ \ \ \Delta_{K}=\frac{K_{0}^{2}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}}{4\pi}[2\ln b+(1-b^{-2})\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}/2]

(85)

is eliminated since it will be absorbed into the normalization of generating function.

C.0.4
Renormalization group flow

Let us now rescale momenta and fields in the effective generating functional according to

\bm{k}^{\prime}=b\bm{k};\ \ \ \bar{\Psi}^{\prime}(\Psi^{\prime})=b^{-3/2}\bar{\Psi}(\Psi)

(86)

so that the momentum
\bm{k}^{\prime}
is integrated over
|k^{\prime}|<\Lambda
. The rescaling of fields is to keep the free propagator unchanged. The rescaled effective generating functional is

\displaystyle\langle Z\rangle_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=\int D[\bar{\Psi}^{\prime},\Psi^{\prime}]_{\Lambda}

(87)

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\bar{\Psi}^{\prime}_{\bm{k}^{\prime},\omega}[b(1+\Delta_{E})i\omega-(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{\sigma}]\Psi_{\bm{k}^{\prime},\omega}+\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\frac{d^{2}\bm{q}d^{2}\bm{k}d^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{6}}[\mathcal{K}(\bm{q})+\Delta_{K}]\bar{\Psi}^{<}_{\bm{k},\tau}\Psi^{<}_{\bm{k}-\bm{q},\tau}\Psi^{<}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q},\tau^{\prime}}\bar{\Psi}^{<}_{\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}\right\}

which gives the transformation laws of energy, velocity and disorder

\left\{\begin{aligned} E^{\prime}=&b(1+\Delta_{E})E=b(1+\frac{K_{0}\ln b}{2\pi})E\\
v_{f}^{\prime}=&(1-\alpha)v_{f}=\left[1-\frac{K_{0}\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{8\pi}(1-b^{-2})\right]v_{f}\\
K_{0}^{\prime}=&K_{0}+\frac{K_{0}^{2}}{8\pi}[4\ln b+(1-b^{-2})\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}]\end{aligned}\right.

(88)

where we only consider the leading term of disorder coupling
\mathcal{K}(\bm{q})\to K_{0}(\hbar v_{f})^{2}
and do the analytic continuation for energy,
i\omega\to E+i0^{+}
.

Then we renormalization parameter
b=e^{l}\approx 1+l
and get the renormalization flows of energy, velocity and disorder

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{dE}{dl}=&(1+\frac{K_{0}}{2\pi})E\\
\frac{dv_{f}}{dl}=&-\frac{K_{0}\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{4\pi}v_{f}\\
\frac{dK_{0}}{dl}=&\frac{K_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}(2+\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2})\end{aligned}\right.

(89)

Here we would like to provide some remarks. First, by setting
\xi\rightarrow 0
, the flow equations Eq.
89
goes back to the existing results (e.g. Ref.
Ostrovsky
).
That is, the renormalization of velocity is zero for short-ranged disorder potential. This is the main reason why the previous work overlooked the renormalization of velocity.
Second, as we shown here under the low energy condition (
k\xi,E\xi<1
), a long-ranged fluctuation
\xi\neq 0
leads to renormalization of the velocity. Importantly, the effective velocity is reduced under the renormalization group flow.
Third, the renormalization group calculation is consistent with the calculation of self-energy function, because the momentum-dependent part of self-energy function, i.e.
\alpha\hbar v_{f}k
in Eq.
48
or Eq. 18 in the main text, effectively reduces velocity in energy dispersion of electron.
Taken all together, the independent renormalization group calculation further supports our conclusion shown in the main text.

The self-energy diagram (bubble diagram) of dc conductivity as shown in Fig.
9
(a) can be calculated by

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}^{0}(E)=\sigma_{xx}^{0,RA}(E)-{\rm Re}\left[\sigma_{xx}^{0,RR}(E)\right]

(90)

with

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}^{0,RA}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\frac{e^{2}\hbar}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}{\rm Tr}\left[G^{R}(\bm{k},E)v_{x}(\bm{k})G^{A}(\bm{k},E)v_{x}(\bm{k})\right]_{c}

(91)

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}^{0,RR}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\frac{e^{2}\hbar}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}{\rm Tr}\left[G^{R}(\bm{k},E)v_{x}(\bm{k})G^{R}(\bm{k},E)v_{x}(\bm{k})\right]_{c}

(92)

where the factor 4 denotes degeneracy of spin and valley,
{\rm Tr}[\cdots]
means the trace in chiral basis, and the subscript
c
indicates a disorder configuration average. We do the calculation in the eigen basis of pseudospin so that the velocity operator and Green’s function are expressed as

v_{x}(\bm{k})=v_{f}(\cos\theta_{\bm{k}}\sigma_{z}+\sin\theta_{\bm{k}}\sigma_{y})=v_{f}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\theta_{\bm{k}}&-i\sin\theta_{\bm{k}}\\
i\sin\theta_{\bm{k}}&-\cos\theta_{\bm{k}}\end{array}\right)

(93)

and

G^{L}(\bm{k},E)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{E-E_{\bm{k}+}-\Sigma(\bm{k}+,E)}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{E-E_{\bm{k}-}-\Sigma(\bm{k}-,E)}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g^{L}_{+}(\bm{k},E)&0\\
0&g^{L}_{-}(\bm{k},E)\end{array}\right)

(94)

where
L=R,A
denotes the retarded or advanced Green’s function and
E_{\bm{k}\pm}=\pm\hbar v_{f}k
is the eigenvalue. Plugging Eq.(
93
) and Eq.(
94
) into Eq.(
91
) and Eq.(
92
), the value of
\sigma_{xx}^{0,RA}(\sigma_{xx}^{0,RR})
is evaluated

\displaystyle\sigma^{0,LM}_{xx}(E)=

\displaystyle\frac{2e^{2}\hbar v^{2}_{f}}{\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}{\rm Tr}\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g^{L}_{+}&0\\
0&g^{L}_{-}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\theta_{\bm{k}}&-i\sin\theta_{\bm{k}}\\
i\sin\theta_{\bm{k}}&-\cos\theta_{\bm{k}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g^{M}_{+}&0\\
0&g^{M}_{-}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\theta_{\bm{k}}&-i\sin\theta_{\bm{k}}\\
i\sin\theta_{\bm{k}}&-\cos\theta_{\bm{k}}\end{array}\right)\right]

(95)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2e^{2}\hbar v^{2}_{f}}{\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left[\cos^{2}\theta_{\bm{k}}(g^{L}_{+}g^{M}_{+}+g^{L}_{-}g^{M}_{-})+\sin^{2}\theta_{\bm{k}}(g^{L}_{+}g^{M}_{-}+g^{L}_{-}g^{M}_{+})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}\hbar v^{2}_{f}}{\pi}\int\frac{kdk}{2\pi}\left[g^{L}_{+}g^{M}_{+}+g^{L}_{-}g^{M}_{-}+g^{L}_{+}g^{M}_{-}+g^{L}_{-}g^{M}_{+}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}\hbar v^{2}_{f}}{\pi}\int\frac{kdk}{2\pi}(g^{L}_{+}+g^{L}_{-})(g^{M}_{+}+g^{M}_{-})

where
L,M=R,A
. Since we assume the self-energy function depending on both energy and magnitude of wave vector as

\Sigma(\bm{k}s,E)=\Sigma_{1}(E)-s\alpha\hbar v_{f}k+i\Sigma_{2}(E),

(96)

the full Green’s functions can be written as

\displaystyle g^{R}_{+}(\bm{k},E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{E-\hbar v_{f}k-{\rm Re}\Sigma(E,k+)-i{\rm Im}\Sigma(E,k+)}=\frac{1}{a-(1-\alpha)v_{f}\hbar k+i\eta}

(97)

\displaystyle g^{R}_{-}(\bm{k},E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{E+\hbar v_{f}k-{\rm Re}\Sigma(E,k-)-i{\rm Im}\Sigma(E,k-)}=\frac{1}{a+(1-\alpha)v_{f}\hbar k+i\eta}

(98)

\displaystyle g^{A}_{+}(\bm{k},E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{E-\hbar v_{f}k-{\rm Re}\Sigma(E,k+)+i{\rm Im}\Sigma(E,k+)}=\frac{1}{a-(1-\alpha)v_{f}\hbar k-i\eta}

(99)

\displaystyle g^{A}_{-}(\bm{k},E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{E+\hbar v_{f}k-{\rm Re}\Sigma(E,k-)+i{\rm Im}\Sigma(E,k-)}=\frac{1}{a+(1-\alpha)v_{f}\hbar k-i\eta}

(100)

with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}a=E-\Sigma_{1}(E)\\
\eta=-\Sigma_{2}(E)\\
\end{array}\right.

(102)

Therefore we can obtain
\sigma^{0,RA}_{xx}(E)
and
{\rm Re}\sigma^{0,RR}_{xx}(E)

\displaystyle\sigma^{0,RA}_{xx}(E)=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}\hbar v^{2}_{f}}{\pi}\int\frac{kdk}{2\pi}\left[\frac{1}{a-(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k+i\eta}+\frac{1}{a+(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k+i\eta}\right]

(103)

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{a-(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k-i\eta}+\frac{1}{a+(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k-i\eta}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}\hbar v^{2}_{f}}{\pi}\int\frac{kdk}{2\pi}\frac{2(a+i\eta)}{(a+i\eta)^{2}-(1-\alpha)^{2}(\hbar v_{f}k)^{2}}\frac{2(a-i\eta)}{(a-i\eta)^{2}-(1-\alpha)^{2}(\hbar v_{f}k)^{2}}

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\sigma^{0,RR}_{xx}(E)=

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Re}\frac{e^{2}\hbar v^{2}_{f}}{\pi}\int\frac{kdk}{2\pi}\left[\frac{1}{a-(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k+i\eta}+\frac{1}{a+(1-\alpha)\hbar v_{f}k+i\eta}\right]^{2}

(104)

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Re}\frac{2e^{2}}{\pi h}\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)^{2}}\int dx\frac{(a+i\eta)^{2}}{\left[(a+i\eta)^{2}-x\right]^{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{2e^{2}}{\pi h}\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)^{2}}\frac{(a+i\eta)^{2}}{(a+i\eta)^{2}-x}\bigg|^{\infty}_{0}

\displaystyle-\frac{2e^{2}}{\pi h}\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)^{2}}

(106)

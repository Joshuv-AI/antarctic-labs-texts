Abstract
. For a twisted C ∗ C^{*} -dynamical system ( 𝒜 , ℝ n , α , e ) (\mathscr{A},\mathbb{R}^{n},\alpha,e) over a unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebra we establish a weakly parametric pseudodifferential calculus analogously to the celebrated weakly parametric calculus due to Grubb and Seeley [ 11 ] . If the C ∗ C^{*} -algebra 𝒜 \mathscr{A} has an α \alpha -invariant trace then we prove an expansion of the resolvent trace (with respect to the dual trace on multipliers) for suitable pseudodifferential multipliers. The question whether the expansion holds true as a Hilbert space trace expansion in concrete GNS spaces for 𝒜 \mathscr{A} will be addressed in a future publication.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to establish a weakly parametric pseudodifferential calculus for twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical systems. Let us first put this into perspective. Pseudodifferential operators were developed to be able to treat the resolvent of a differential operator as a “virtual differential operator” of negative order and hence to a large extent on an equal footing as differential operators. Soon it became clear from Seeley’s famous paper on complex powers
[
24
]
that in order to obtain the full strength of the results on the trace of the
\zeta
-function (or more or less equivalently the short time asymptotic expansion of the heat trace) it was necessary to extend the pseudodifferential calculus to a
calculus with parameters
,
cf
. e.g.,

[
25
]
. It is important to understand that this means that the resolvent parameter is essentially treated like a covariable and it should not be confused with the naive perception of being just an auxiliary parameter in the theory. However, as it turned out the parametric calculus works well only for resolvents of
differential operators
. This
subtlety was even overlooked in the early days and observed only much later in the paper by Duistermaat and Guillemin
[
8
]
. In order to
incorporate the resolvent of a pseudodifferential operator into the theory it was therefore necessary to establish a
weakly parametric calculus
which was fully worked out much later by Grubb and Seeley
[
11
]
. The weakly parametric calculus is indispensable for establishing the more subtle invariants of an elliptic operator as e.g., the noncommutative residue
[
12
,
26
,
27
]
. The latter is intimately related to the
\log t
term in the asymptotic expansion of
\operatorname{Tr}(Ae^{-t\Delta})
, say, as
t\to 0
, it vanishes for a differential operator
A
.

Pseudodifferential calculi are also of significance in the noncommutative setting
[
1
,
2
]
. On noncommutative tori, which form an interesting model case for a noncommutative space, meanwhile a rich spectral geometry has been established
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
16
,
19
]
. In
[
19
]
a full heat trace asymptotics for Laplace type operators on Heisenberg modules, a class of natural projective modules over noncommutative tori, was established and applied to obtain many of the spectral geometry results known from the conformal geometry of compact oriented surfaces. The main technical tool there was a parametric pseudodifferential calculus (à la Shubin
[
25
]
) for twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical systems. In a second step this was then used to obtain a heat trace asymptotic for the effective action on Heisenberg modules. The second step is nontrivial as the natural traces on the
C^{*}
-dynamical system and the Hilbert space trace on the Heisenberg module are only “asymptotically equal” (see
[
19
, Thm. 6.2]
). It should be noted that the Heisenberg modules are the natural noncommutative torus analogues of the classification of holomorphic vector bundles over elliptic curves (see, e.g.,
[
22
]
).

If one wants to push the analogy between the spectral geometry of noncommutative tori and compact surfaces further it is therefore natural to extend Grubb and Seeley’s weakly parametric calculus to this situation. The current paper is an important step in this direction. Namely, we work out the weakly parametric calculus for abstract twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical systems. The calculus here is reminiscent of the calculus on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
cf
.
[
25
, Chap. 4]
). What is missing, however, and which needs to be addressed in a future publication is the above mentioned transfer to the effective action on Heisenberg modules. The obstacle is that so far we are unable to prove the analog of
[
19
, Thm. 6.2]
about the asymptotic comparison between the abstract trace and the concrete Hilbert space trace for a weakly parametric operator family.

Let us now in some more detail sketch the set-up and main results of the paper. Let
(\mathscr{A},\mathbb{R}^{n},\alpha,e)
be a twisted
C^{*}
–dynamical system, i.e.,
\mathscr{A}
is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
\alpha
is a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{n}
action by automorphisms. The twisting
e(x,y)=e^{i\left\langle Bx,y\right\rangle}
is given by a skew-symmetric real
n\times n
matrix.
\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
denotes, as usual, the subalgebra of smooth elements with respect to the action. We now
study pseudodifferential multipliers on the Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}):=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\otimes\mathscr{A}
. The latter is the exterior tensor product (
cf
.
[
17
, Chap. 4]
) of the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathscr{A}
, and here
\mathscr{A}
is regarded as a Hilbert module over itself. More concretely
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
is the completion of the Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
with respect to the inner product,

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{*}g(x)dx,\qquad f,g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty}).

The twisted dynamical system induces on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
an adjoint (
2.2
) and a convolution product (
2.3
) which turn it into a
*
-algebra. Furthermore, the convolution product gives rise to a left regular representation of
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
by bounded adjointable multipliers on the Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\otimes\mathscr{A}
. Extending this left regular representation to more general function classes now leads naturally to “pseudodifferential multipliers”. For the usual Hörmander symbol class and
\mathscr{A}=\mathbb{C}
this specializes to a well-known pseudodifferential calculus on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
cf
.
[
25
, Chap. 4]
).

In Section
2
we extend the Hörmander symbol classes to our situation. This is a straightforward generalization of the Baaj-Julg symbol spaces
[
1
]
from the untwisted to the twisted case. Furthermore, we work out in some more detail the pseudodifferential calculus for a twisted

C^{*}
-dynamical system as indicated in
[
19
, §3.2]
. We follow also
[
13
,
14
]
. For a symbol
f\in\textup{S}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, the pseudodifferential multiplier
P_{f}
associated to
f
is defined by

(1.1)

(P_{f}u)(x):=(M_{f^{\vee}}u)(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\left\langle x,\xi\right\rangle}\alpha_{-x}(f(\xi+Bx))\hat{u}(\xi)d\xi,\quad u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty}).

The rules of calculus are worked out in Section
2
below. We also put particular emphasis on the asymptotic symbol formulas for
classical
operators which are a bit more involved here due to the twisting (Theorem
2.15
).

In Section
3
we prove
a basic boundedness result for pseudodifferential multipliers on
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
(Proposition
3.6
). As a tool we prove a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module version of the classical Schur’s test on boundedness for integral operators (Lemma
3.4
).

In Section
4
we develop the weakly parametric pseudodifferential calculus for pseudodifferential multipliers acting on the Hilbert module
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\otimes\mathscr{A}
. This is an adaption of Grubb and Seeley’s weakly parametric calculus for ordinary pseudodifferential operators
[
11
]
.

The final Section
5
contains our main results on the trace expansions, i.e., we have

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
5.5
)
.

Let
P
and
A
be classical (i.e.,
1
-step polyhomogeneous)
pseudodifferential multipliers with respective orders
m\in\mathbb{N}
and

\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that
P
is elliptic with parameter

\mu\in\Gamma
. Then, for
\lambda\in-\Gamma^{m}
and
k
with

-km+\omega<-n
, we have the asymptotic expansion,

\operatorname{Tr}_{\psi}\left[A(P-\lambda)^{-k}\right]\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{j}\lambda^{\frac{n+\omega-j}{m}-k}+\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\big(c_{l}^{\prime}\log{\lambda}+c_{l}^{\prime\prime}\big)\lambda^{-k-l}.

Here the coefficients
c_{j}
,
c_{l}^{\prime}
and
c_{l}^{\prime\prime}
are given by the the integral (over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) of the trace
\psi
of the respective symbols
f(\xi)\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}f_{m-j}(\xi)
and
a(\xi)\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}a_{\omega-j}(\xi)
of
P
and
A
.

We refer the reader to Definition
5.3
for the precise meaning of the notion of ellipticity with parameter. Furthermore,
\operatorname{Tr}_{\psi}
is the trace on pseudodifferential multipliers induced from an
\alpha
-invariant continuous trace
\psi
on
\mathscr{A}
(
cf
. (
2.20
)). If the twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system
(\mathscr{A},\mathbb{R}^{n},\alpha,e)
is projectively represented on a Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
then one obtains a representation of the pseudodifferential multipliers as operators on
\mathscr{H}
. In general
\operatorname{Tr}_{\psi}(P_{f})
does not coincide with the Hilbert space trace of the representation of
P_{f}
. In
[
19
, Thm. 6.2]
however, it was shown that for the standard parametric calculus the two traces coincide up to a summand of order
O(\lambda^{-N})
,
N
arbitrary. This suffices to deduce an asymptotic expansion result analogous to Theorem
1.1
also for the Hilbert space realization. For the weakly parametric calculus the method of loc. cit. does not work (see Remark
5.6
for more information on this point) and we have to leave this question for a future publication.

Pseudodifferential Calculus for Twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical Systems

In this section, we recall the main definitions and properties of pseudodifferential multipliers on twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical systems
[
19
,
20
]
. This is a generalization of the pseudodifferential calculus on
C^{*}
-dynamical systems described in
[
1
,
2
]
.

2.1.
Twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical systems

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
\alpha
be a continuous action of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
on
\mathscr{A}
by
C^{*}
-algebra automorphisms, i.e.,
\alpha_{t}\in\textup{Aut}(\mathscr{A})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the map
t\mapsto\alpha_{t}(a)
is norm-continuous for all
a\in\mathscr{A}
. Furthermore, let

e(x,y):=e^{i\left\langle Bx,y\right\rangle},\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
B=(b_{kl})
is a skew-symmetric real
n\times n
matrix and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
is the standard inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the quadruple
(\mathscr{A},\mathbb{R}^{n},\alpha,e)
forms a twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system.

Throughout the paper, we let
(\mathscr{A},\mathbb{R}^{n},\alpha,e)
be the twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system described above. We shall denote by
\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
the smooth subalgebra of
\mathscr{A}
induced by the action
\alpha
, i.e., those
a\in\mathscr{A}
such that the map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni t\mapsto\alpha_{t}(a)\in\mathscr{A}
is smooth. For
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, we define

\delta^{\gamma}a:=i^{-|\gamma|}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\big|_{t=0}\alpha_{t}(a),\qquad a\in\mathscr{A}^{\infty}.

\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
is a Fréchet space with respect to the locally convex topology generated by the semi-norms
a\mapsto\|\delta^{\gamma}a\|
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.

Let us denote by
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
the space of Schwartz class maps with values in
\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
. Let
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
. Its Fourier transform
\hat{f}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
is defined by

\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-i\left\langle x,\xi\right\rangle}f(x)dx,\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Furthermore, the inverse Fourier transform
f^{\vee}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
of
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
is defined by

f^{\vee}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\left\langle\xi,x\right\rangle}f(\xi){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where we have set
{\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi:=(2\pi)^{-n}d\xi
. The Fourier transform and the inverse Fourier transform induce continuous linear isomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
that are inverses of each other. We refer to
[
13
, Appendix B and Appendix C]
for a more detailed account on the integration and the Fourier transform of a map with values in locally convex spaces.

We can endow the Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
with the pre-
C^{*}
-module structure given by the inner product,

(2.1)

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{*}g(x)dx,\qquad f,g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty}).

For
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
, set

\displaystyle(af)(x)=\alpha_{-x}(a)f(x),\qquad a\in\mathscr{A}^{\infty},

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{U}_{y}f)(x)=e(x,-y)f(x-y).

Then
\boldsymbol{U}_{y}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is a projective family of unitaries such that, for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{U}_{x}^{*}=\boldsymbol{U}_{-x},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{U}_{x}\boldsymbol{U}_{y}=e(x,y)\boldsymbol{U}_{x+y},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{U}_{x}a\boldsymbol{U}_{-x}=\alpha_{x}(a),\qquad a\in\mathscr{A}^{\infty}.

Given
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
, we define the multiplier
M_{f}
associated with
f
by

M_{f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\boldsymbol{U}_{x}dx.

Then the space
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
becomes a
*
-algebra with respect to the product and the adjoint defined by
M_{f}\circ M_{g}=M_{f*g}
and
M_{f}^{*}=M_{f^{*}}
, where

(2.2)

\displaystyle f^{*}(x)=\alpha_{x}\left(f(-x)^{*}\right),

(2.3)

\displaystyle(f*g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)\alpha_{y}\big(g(x-y)\big)e(y,x)dy.

Suppose that the algebra
\mathscr{A}
is equipped with an
\alpha
-invariant continuous trace
\psi
. In this case,
\psi
induces the
dual trace
on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
which is given by

(2.4)

\widehat{\psi}(f):=\psi\big(f(0)\big)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi\big(\hat{f}(\xi)\big){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi,\qquad{\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi:=(2\pi)^{-n}d\xi.

2.2.
Symbols and pseudodifferential multipliers

Definition 2.1

(
[
1
,
2
]
)
.

The symbol space
\textup{S}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, consists of smooth maps
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
such that, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, there exists
C_{\alpha\beta}>0
such that

\big\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}f(\xi)\big\|\leq C_{\alpha\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-|\beta|},

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here we denote
\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

We endow
\textup{S}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, with the locally convex topology generated by the semi-norms,

(2.5)

p_{N}(f):=\sup_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{-m+|\beta|}\big\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}f(\xi)\big\|,\qquad N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

The space
\textup{S}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
is a Fréchet space with respect to these semi-norms (see, e.g.,
[
13
, Prop. 3.3]
for the proof).

Lemma 2.2

(see
[
13
, Lem. 3.5]
)
.

Let
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the product of
\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
gives rise to a continuous bilinear map from
\textup{S}^{m_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})\times\textup{S}^{m_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})

\textup{S}^{m_{1}+m_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
.

Definition 2.3

(
[

]

Let

,
m\in\mathbb{R}

There is a version of Borel’s lemma for
\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
-valued symbols.

Lemma 2.4

(see also
[
10
, Prop. 3.4]
and
[

, Lem. 3.10]

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and

f(\xi)\in\textup{S}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})

(

)
.

We say that a map

is

f(\lambda\xi)=\lambda^{m}f(\xi)\qquad\text{for all $\lambda\geq 1$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,1)$}.

Here

, denotes the open ball of radius

centered at the origin. Note that if

is homogeneous of degree

, then

.

Definition 2.5
.

The space of classical (
1
-step polyhomogeneous) symbols, denoted by
\textup{CS}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
,

, consists of maps

where

is meant in the sense of (

for each

.

Remark 2.6
.

Let

,

, be such that

(3)

Now we look for the asymptotic expansion of the composition product (

\partial_{x_{j}}\big|_{x=0}f(\xi+Bx)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}B_{kj}\partial_{\xi_{k}}f(\xi)=:\partial_{B,\xi_{j}}f(\xi),\qquad j=1,\ldots,n.

\displaystyle f^{B,\alpha}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\big|_{x=0}\alpha_{-x}\big(f(\xi+Bx)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}i^{|\beta|}\delta^{\beta}\big(\partial_{x}^{\gamma}\big|_{x=0}f(\xi+Bx)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}i^{|\beta|}\delta^{\beta}\partial_{B,\xi}^{\gamma}f(\xi).

(

3.
Boundedness

In this section, we study the boundedness of pseudodifferential multipliers with respect to the Hilbert
C^{*}
-norm. To this end, we first generalize the classical Schur’s test on the boundedness of integral operators (see, e.g.,
[
15
, Thm. 5.2]
).

Let us denote the space of continuous maps on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
vanishing at infinity (resp., Schwartz class maps on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) with values in
\mathscr{A}
by
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
(resp.,
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
). Let
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
be the Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over
\mathscr{A}
formed by completing
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|:=\|\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle\|^{\frac{1}{2}}
, where
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
is the inner product (
2.1
). Note that
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
is nothing but the exterior tensor product
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\otimes\mathscr{A}
(
cf
.
[
17
, Chap. 4]
), where
\mathscr{A}
is viewed as a Hilbert module over itself.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}
be a continuous map with
k(x,y)\geq 0
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, assume that there are positive measurable functions
p,q:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
and numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}k(x,y)q(y)dy\leq\alpha p(x),\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}k(x,y)p(x)dx\leq\beta q(y),\qquad\text{for $y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Then the
integral operator

:K:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})\rightarrow C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
defined by

(3.3)

Ku(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}k(x,y)u(y)dy,\qquad u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}),

extends by continuity to a bounded adjointable module endomorphism of the Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
with
\|K\|\leq\sqrt{\alpha\beta}
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as in
[
15
, Thm. 5.2]
. However, we recall and emphasize that for an element
u
of
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
, although the integral
\int u(x)^{*}u(x)dx
converges in
\mathscr{A}
, in general
\int\|u(x)\|^{2}dx
is not necessarily finite. We thus prove the boundedness in the case
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
first and then extend the map to
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
by continuity.

Given
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
we note that

(3.4)

Ku(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}k(x,y)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{q(y)}k(x,y)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{u(y)}{\sqrt{q(y)}}dy=\left\langle k(x,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{q(\cdot)},k(x,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{u(\cdot)}{\sqrt{q(\cdot)}}\right\rangle.

Hence the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Prop. 1.1]
) and (
3.1
) gives

{\left\langle k(x,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{q(\cdot)},k(x,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{u(\cdot)}{\sqrt{q(\cdot)}}\right\rangle}^{*}\left\langle k(x,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{q(\cdot)},k(x,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{u(\cdot)}{\sqrt{q(\cdot)}}\right\rangle\\
\leq\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}k(x,y)q(y)dy\right\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y)^{*}\frac{k(x,y)}{q(y)}u(y)dy\leq\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y)^{*}\frac{p(x)k(x,y)}{q(y)}u(y)dy.

Combining this with (
3.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle Ku,Ku\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\left\langle k(x,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{q(\cdot)},k(x,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{u(\cdot)}{\sqrt{q(\cdot)}}\right\rangle}^{*}\left\langle k(x,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{q(\cdot)},k(x,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{u(\cdot)}{\sqrt{q(\cdot)}}\right\rangle dx

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y)^{*}\frac{p(x)k(x,y)}{q(y)}u(y)dy\bigg)dx.

Recall that the inequality
c^{*}ac\leq c^{*}bc
holds for all
c\in\mathscr{A}
and self-adjoint elements
a
and
b
in
\mathscr{A}
such that
a\leq b
(
cf
.
[
21
, Thm. 2.2.5]
). By combining this with the assumptions (
3.2
) and
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y)^{*}\frac{p(x)k(x,y)}{q(y)}u(y)dx\bigg)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y)^{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{p(x)k(x,y)}{q(y)}dx\,u(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y)^{*}u(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\beta\|u\|_{\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})}^{2}<\infty.

It follows from this that the integrand
u(y)^{*}p(x)k(x,y)q(y)^{-1}u(y)
on the right-hand side of (
3.5
) is integrable with respect to the product measure
dxdy
and hence Fubini’s theorem for the integration of vector-valued maps applies (
cf
. e.g.,
[
13
, Prop. B.21]
). Thus, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle Ku,Ku\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y)^{*}\frac{p(x)k(x,y)}{q(y)}u(y)dx\bigg)dy

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y)^{*}u(y)dy

\displaystyle=\alpha\beta\left\langle u,u\right\rangle.

Furthermore, we have

\|Ku\|=\|\left\langle Ku,Ku\right\rangle\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sqrt{\alpha\beta}\|\left\langle u,u\right\rangle\|^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{\alpha\beta}\|u\|.

This shows that the map
K:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})\rightarrow C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
extends to a bounded linear map from
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
to itself with
\|K\|\leq\sqrt{\alpha\beta}
. Furthermore, it is clear that
K
is right
\mathscr{A}
-linear and adjointable, whose adjoint is the continuous extension to
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
of the integral operator associated with the kernel
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni(x,y)\mapsto k(y,x)\in\mathscr{A}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We need an extension of Lemma
3.1
to not necessarily non-negative continuous kernels
k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}
. One might be tempted to hope for a condition of the kind,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|k(x,y)|q(y)dy\leq\alpha p(x),\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|k(x,y)|p(x)dx\leq\beta q(y),\qquad\text{for $y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

We leave it as an open problem whether such a version of Schur’s test is valid for the Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
. The next weaker result, Lemma
3.4
, will suffice for our purposes.

Remark 3.3
.

Needless to say in the previous and the next lemma we could replace the pair
(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx)
by any
\sigma
-finite measure space
(X,\mu)
and the Hilbert module
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
by
\mathscr{H}(X,\mathscr{A},\mu):=L^{2}(X,\mu)\otimes\mathscr{A}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}
be continuous and suppose that there are positive measurable functions
p,q:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
and numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|k(x,y)\|q(y)dy\leq\alpha p(x),\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|k(x,y)\|p(x)dx\leq\beta q(y),\qquad\text{for $y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Then
Kf(x)=\int k(x,y)u(y)dy
extends by continuity to a bounded adjointable
\mathscr{A}
-module endomorphism of
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
with
K^{*}f(x)=\int k(y,x)^{*}u(y)dy
and
\|K\|\leq 4\sqrt{\alpha\beta}
.

Proof.

Let
|k|,k^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}
denote the continuous maps defined by
|k|(x,y)=|k(x,y)|
and
k^{*}(x,y)=k(x,y)^{*}
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We also set

\Re{k}=\frac{1}{2}(k+k^{*})\quad\text{and}\quad\Im{k}=\frac{1}{2i}(k-k^{*}).

Then we may write
k=k_{1}-k_{2}+i(k_{3}-k_{4})
, where
k_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}
,
1\leq j\leq 4
, are the continuous maps defined by

k_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(|\Re{k}|+\Re{k}),\quad k_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(|\Re{k}|-\Re{k}),\quad k_{3}=\frac{1}{2}(|\Im{k}|+\Im{k}),\quad k_{4}=\frac{1}{2}(|\Im{k}|-\Im{k}).

Note that
k_{j}(x,y)\geq 0
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note also that any of the
k_{j}
satisfies the assumptions (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) of Lemma
3.1
as we have
\|k_{j}(x,y)\|\leq\|k(x,y)\|
and hence

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}k_{j}(x,y)q(y)dy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|k_{j}(x,y)\|q(y)dy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|k(x,y)\|q(y)dy\leq\alpha p(x)\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and similarly for
\int k_{j}(x,y)p(x)dx
. Thus, Lemma
3.1
implies that

\|K\|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{4}\|K_{j}\|\leq 4\sqrt{\alpha\beta},

where
K_{j}
denotes the respective integral operator associated with
k_{j}
in the sense of (
3.3
),
1\leq j\leq 4
. Therefore,
K
extends to a bounded
\mathscr{A}
-module endomorphism by continuity, and its adjoint
K^{*}
is the continuous extension of the integral operator associated with the kernel
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni(x,y)\mapsto k(y,x)^{*}
. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
f\in\textup{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
. Suppose that
f(\xi)
is a normal element in
\mathscr{A}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and there are
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<a<b
such that
\operatorname{Sp}(f(\xi))\subset[a,b]
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{C}
containing
[a,b]
and
\varphi
be a holomorphic function on
\Omega
. Then the map
\xi\mapsto\varphi(f(\xi))
belongs to
\textup{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
, where
\varphi(f(\xi))
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is defined by using holomorphic functional calculus.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be a rectifiable contour that winds once around
\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{Sp}(f(\xi))
. We define
\varphi(f(\xi))
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by using holomorphic functional calculus, i.e., we set

(3.6)

\varphi(f(\xi))=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(z)(z-f(\xi))^{-1}dz.

We know by
[
3
]
that
\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
is closed under holomorphic functional calculus. Therefore, we have
\varphi(f(\xi))\in\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

By assumption
f(\xi)
is a normal element in
\mathscr{A}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and so
(z-f(\xi))^{-1}
is also normal for all
z\in\Gamma
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As the spectral radius of a normal element
x\in\mathscr{A}
agrees with
\|x\|
(see, e.g.,
[
21
]
), for each
z\in\Gamma
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|(z-f(\xi))^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{|\lambda|;\ \lambda\in\operatorname{Sp}\big((z-f(\xi))^{-1}\big)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{|z-\lambda|^{-1};\ \lambda\in\operatorname{Sp}\big(f(\xi)\big)\right\}.

As
\Gamma
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
and
\operatorname{Sp}(f(\xi))\subset[a,b]
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we see that there is
C>0
such that
|z-\lambda|^{-1}\leq C
for all
z\in\Gamma
and
\lambda\in\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{Sp}(f(\xi))
. Thus, we get

(3.7)

\left\|(z-f(\xi))^{-1}\right\|\leq C\qquad\text{for all $z\in\Gamma$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
. Then the partial derivative
\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}[(z-f(\xi))^{-1}]
can be written as a linear combination of terms of the form,

(3.8)

(z-f(\xi))^{-1}\left(\delta^{\alpha^{(1)}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta^{(1)}}f(\xi)\right)(z-f(\xi))^{-1}\cdots(z-f(\xi))^{-1}\left(\delta^{\alpha^{(l)}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta^{(l)}}f(\xi)\right)(z-f(\xi))^{-1},

where
\alpha^{(1)},\ldots,\alpha^{(l)}
and
\beta^{(1)},\ldots,\beta^{(l)}
are multi-orders such that
\alpha^{(1)}+\cdots+\alpha^{(l)}=\alpha
and
\beta^{(1)}+\cdots+\beta^{(l)}=\beta
. As
f\in\textup{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
we know that, for each
j=1,\ldots,l
, there is
C_{j}>0
such that
\|\delta^{\alpha^{(j)}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta^{(j)}}f(\xi)\|\leq C_{j}\langle\xi\rangle^{-|\beta^{(j)}|}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Combining this with (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) shows that there is
C_{\alpha\beta}>0
such that

(3.9)

\left\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\big[(z-f(\xi))^{-1}\big]\right\|\leq C_{\alpha\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-|\beta|}\qquad\text{for all $z\in\Gamma$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

It follows from this that the partial derivative
\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}
and the contour integral on the right-hand side of (
3.6
) can be swapped
[
13
, Prop. C.28]
. Furthermore, as
\delta^{\alpha}:\mathscr{A}^{\infty}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
is a continuous linear map,
\delta^{\alpha}
can also be interchanged with the same contour integral. Thus, by using (
3.9
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\big[\varphi\big(f(\xi)\big)\big]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}\left|\varphi\big(\gamma(t)\big)\right|\,\left\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\big[(\gamma(t)-f(\xi))^{-1}\big]\right\|\,|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\alpha\beta}}{2\pi}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}\left|\varphi\big(\gamma(t)\big)\right|\,|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|dt\cdot\langle\xi\rangle^{-|\beta|},

where
\gamma:[r_{0},r_{1}]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a parametrization of
\Gamma
. Thus, we see that
\varphi(f(\xi))\in\textup{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
f\in\textup{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
. Then the pseudodifferential multiplier
P_{f}
gives rise to a continuous linear map from
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
to itself. Furthermore, the map
f\mapsto P_{f}
gives rise to a continuous linear map from
\textup{S}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}))
for every
m<0
.

Proof.

Here we mimic the strategy of the proof of the boundedness of pseudodifferential operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
using Schur’s lemma in the literature (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
). However, as we are dealing with
\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
-valued symbols, here we use Lemma
3.4
instead of the ordinary Schur’s lemma (see, e.g.,
[
23
, Lem. 3.7]
) and Lemma
3.5
instead of
[
23
, Lem. 2.1]
for reducing the proof to the case of negative order symbols.

Let
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
and suppose that
f\in\textup{S}^{-n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
. Using (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) we can write

\displaystyle P_{f}u(x)

\displaystyle=\iint e^{i\left\langle x-y,\xi\right\rangle}\alpha_{-x}(f(\xi+Bx))u(y)dy{\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\left\langle x-y,\xi\right\rangle}\alpha_{-x}(f(\xi+Bx)){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi\right)u(y)dy

(3.10)

\displaystyle=:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x,y)u(y)dy.

As
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
and
f\in\textup{S}^{-n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
, all the integrands are absolutely integrable with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathscr{A}
. Note also that, for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|(x-y)^{\alpha}K(x,y)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(x-y)^{\alpha}e^{i\left\langle x-y,\xi\right\rangle}\alpha_{-x}(f(\xi+Bx)){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\left\langle x-y,\xi\right\rangle}\alpha_{-x}\big(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f(\xi+Bx)\big){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\|\alpha_{-x}\big(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f(\xi+Bx)\big)\right\|{\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f(\xi+Bx)\right\|{\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi.

We know that
\|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f(\xi+Bx)\|\leq p_{|\alpha|}(f)\langle\xi+Bx\rangle^{-n-1-|\alpha|}
for all
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
since
f\in\textup{S}^{-n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
. Here
p_{|\alpha|}
is the semi-norm on the space of standard symbols given in (
2.5
). It then follows that

\left\|(x-y)^{\alpha}K(x,y)\right\|\leq p_{|\alpha|}(f)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\xi+Bx\rangle^{-n-1-|\alpha|}{\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi=p_{|\alpha|}(f)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{-n-1-|\alpha|}{\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi<\infty.

By using this we deduce that there is
C>0
independent of
f
such that

(1+|x-y|^{2})^{n}\|K(x,y)\|\leq Cp_{2n}(f)\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It follows from this that there is a continuous semi-norm
p
on
\textup{S}^{-n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\|K(x,y)\right\|dy\leq p(f),\qquad\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\|K(x,y)\right\|dx\leq p(f).

Combining this with Lemma
3.4
and (
3.10
) shows that

\|P_{f}u\|\leq 4p(f)\|u\|\qquad\forall u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty}).

As
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
is dense in
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
this shows that
P_{f}
uniquely extends to a bounded linear operator on
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
. Furthermore, the map
f\mapsto P_{f}
gives rise to a continuous linear map from
\textup{S}^{-n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}))
.

We know by Theorem
2.8
and Theorem
2.9
that if
f\in\textup{S}^{-(n+1)/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
, then
f^{\star}\sharp f\in\textup{S}^{-n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
. Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Prop. 1.1]
) and the proof in the case of order
-n-1
symbols above we see that, for all
f\in\textup{S}^{-(n+1)/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
and
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
, we have

\|P_{f}u\|^{2}=\|\left\langle P_{f}u,P_{f}u\right\rangle\|=\|\left\langle P_{f^{\star}\sharp f}u,u\right\rangle\|\leq\|P_{f^{\star}\sharp f}u\|\|u\|\leq 4p(f^{\star}\sharp f)\|u\|^{2}.

Combining this with the continuity assertions in Theorem
2.8
and Theorem
2.9
we see that there is a continuous semi-norm
q
on
\textup{S}^{-(n+1)/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
such that

\|P_{f}u\|\leq q(f)\|u\|,\qquad\text{for $f\in\textup{S}^{-(n+1)/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})$, $u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})$}.

Since
\bigcup_{m<0}\textup{S}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\textup{S}^{-(n+1)/{2^{k}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
we can proceed by induction to show that the map
f\mapsto P_{f}
gives rise to a continuous linear map from
\textup{S}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}))
for every
m<0
.

Now suppose that
f\in\textup{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
. We set

g(\xi):=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|f(\xi)\|^{2}-f(\xi)^{*}f(\xi).

It follows from Lemma
2.2
that
f(\xi)^{*}f(\xi)\in\textup{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
, and hence
g(\xi)
belongs to
\textup{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
. Furthermore, we also see that, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

0\leq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|f(\xi)\|^{2}-\|f(\xi)^{*}f(\xi)\|\leq g(\xi)\leq\|g(\xi)\|\leq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|f(\xi)\|^{2}+\|f(\xi)^{*}f(\xi)\|\leq 2C<\infty,

where we have set
C=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|f(\xi)\|^{2}
and
\leq
is meant in the sense that, for
x,y\in\mathscr{A}
,
x\geq y
if
x-y\geq 0
in
\mathscr{A}
. Thus, we obtain

1\leq 1+g(\xi)\leq\|1+g(\xi)\|\leq 1+2C\qquad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

This shows that
\operatorname{Sp}(1+g(\xi))\subset[1,1+2C]
. As
z\mapsto z^{\frac{1}{2}}
is a holomorphic function on the domain containing
[1,1+2C]
it follows from Lemma
3.5
that
h(\xi):=(1+g(\xi))^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\textup{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
.

We know by Theorem
2.8
and Theorem
2.9
that both
f^{\star}\sharp f(\xi)-f(\xi)^{*}f(\xi)
and
h^{\star}\sharp h(\xi)-h(\xi)^{*}h(\xi)
are in
\textup{S}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
. Thus, there is
b\in\textup{S}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
such that

\displaystyle f^{\star}\sharp f(\xi)+h^{\star}\sharp h(\xi)

\displaystyle=f(\xi)^{*}f(\xi)+h(\xi)^{*}h(\xi)+b(\xi)

\displaystyle=f(\xi)^{*}f(\xi)+1+g(\xi)+b(\xi)

\displaystyle=f(\xi)^{*}f(\xi)+1+C-f(\xi)^{*}f(\xi)+b(\xi)

\displaystyle=1+C+b(\xi).

Combining this with Theorem
2.8
and Theorem
2.9
once again we see that, for all
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
, we have

(3.11)

\left\langle P_{f}u,P_{f}u\right\rangle\leq\left\langle P_{f}u,P_{f}u\right\rangle+\left\langle P_{h}u,P_{h}u\right\rangle=\left\langle P_{f^{\star}\sharp f+h^{\star}\sharp h}u,u\right\rangle=(1+C)\left\langle u,u\right\rangle+\left\langle P_{b}u,u\right\rangle.

Thanks to the above proof of the result in the case of negative order symbols and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules we know that there is a continuous semi-norm
p
on
\textup{S}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
such that

\|\left\langle P_{b}u,u\right\rangle\|\leq\|P_{b}u\|\|u\|\leq p(b)\|u\|^{2}\qquad\forall u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty}).

Combining this with (
3.11
) we get

\|P_{f}u\|^{2}\leq(1+C)\|u\|^{2}+\|\left\langle P_{b}u,u\right\rangle\|\leq(1+C+p(b))\|u\|^{2}\qquad\forall u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty}).

This shows that
P_{f}
uniquely extends to a bounded linear operator from
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A})
to itself. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The assertion about the continuity of the map
f\mapsto P_{f}
in Proposition
3.6
will be utilized in the construction of the resolvent in §
5
. For the sake of completeness, it is tempting to extend the continuity of the map
f\mapsto P_{f}
to the space of symbols of degree
0
. However, due to the lack of continuity of the holomorphic functional calculus map
f(\xi)\mapsto\varphi(f(\xi))
on
\textup{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
used in the proof, the continuity of the map
\textup{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})\ni f\mapsto P_{f}\in\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}))
cannot be obtained immediately from the proof of the boundedness of ordinary pseudodifferential operators in the literature. Thus, in this article, we only prove the continuity of the map
f\mapsto P_{f}
on the space of symbols of negative orders, which suffices for our purpose.

4.
Weakly Parametric Pseudodifferential Calculus

In this section, we introduce weakly parametric symbols and construct the weakly parametric pseudodifferential calculus in the setting of twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical systems.

In what follows we let
\Gamma
be an open sector in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. Note that
\Gamma
admits an exhaustion
\Gamma=\bigcup_{j\geq 0}\Gamma_{j}
, where the
\Gamma_{j}
are closed subsectors of
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma_{j}\subset\mathring{\Gamma}_{j+1}
.

By using Definition
2.1
of
\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
-valued symbols, we can define weakly parametric
\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
-valued symbols in the same way as in
[
11
, Def. 1.1]
as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
m,d\in\mathbb{R}
. The space
\textup{S}^{m,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
consists of maps
f(\xi,\mu)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
that are holomorphic with respect to
\mu\in\Gamma
and satisfy, for all
j\geq 0
,

\text{$\partial_{z}^{j}f(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{z})\in\textup{S}^{m+j}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})$ for $\tfrac{1}{z}\in\Gamma$},

with uniform estimates in
\textup{S}^{m+j}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
for
|z|\leq 1
and
\tfrac{1}{z}
in closed subsectors of
\Gamma
. Moreover, we set
\textup{S}^{m,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})=\mu^{d}\textup{S}^{m,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
; that is,
\textup{S}^{m,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
consists of maps
f(\xi,\mu)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
that are holomorphic with respect to
\mu\in\Gamma
such that, for all
j\geq 0
,

\text{$\partial_{z}^{j}(z^{d}f(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{z}))\in\textup{S}^{m+j}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})$ for $\tfrac{1}{z}\in\Gamma$},

with uniform estimates in
\textup{S}^{m+j}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
for
|z|\leq 1
and
\tfrac{1}{z}
in closed subsectors of
\Gamma
. We call these symbols
weakly parametric
.

Remark 4.2
.

In Definition
4.1
we adapt the same notations and conventions given in
[
11
, pp. 483–484]
. For example,
\partial_{z}^{j}f(\xi,\frac{1}{z})
means the
j
th
z
-derivative of the map
z\mapsto f(\xi,\frac{1}{z})
.

We endow
\textup{S}^{m,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
,
m,d\in\mathbb{R}
, with the locally convex topology generated by the semi-norms,

p_{N\Gamma^{\prime}}(f):=\sup_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+j\leq N}\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{1}{z}\in\Gamma^{\prime}\\
|z|\leq 1\end{subarray}}(1+|\xi|)^{-m-j+|\beta|}\left\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{z}^{j}\big(z^{d}f\big(\xi,\tfrac{1}{z}\big)\big)\right\|,

where
N
ranges over all non-negative integers and
\Gamma^{\prime}
ranges over all closed subsectors of
\Gamma
. The space
\textup{S}^{m,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
is a Fréchet space with respect to these semi-norms. Note also that, if
m\leq m^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime}-d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then we have a continuous inclusion
\textup{S}^{m,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})\subset\textup{S}^{m^{\prime},d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
.

All the properties of weakly parametric pseudodifferential calculus and their proofs hold
verbatim
in the setting of twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical systems, except for the composition formula (Theorem
4.13
and Theorem
4.14
). Therefore, in the rest of this section, we only state the results and omit proofs if the same arguments work
verbatim
in our setting.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
m_{j},d_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
j=1,2
. Then the product of
\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
gives rise to a continuous bilinear map from
\textup{S}^{m_{1},d_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})\times\textup{S}^{m_{2},d_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
to
\textup{S}^{m_{1}+m_{2},d_{1}+d_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
.

Adopting the notation of
[
11
]
, we shall denote

\displaystyle\textup{S}^{\infty,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty}):=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{R}}\textup{S}^{m,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty}),

\displaystyle\textup{S}^{-\infty,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty}):=\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{R}}\textup{S}^{m,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty}).

The following is the definition of asymptotic expansions, i.e., the analogue of
[
11
, Def. 1.8]
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
f(\xi,\mu)\in\textup{S}^{m,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
,
m,d\in\mathbb{R}
and
f_{j}(\xi,\mu)\in\textup{S}^{m-j,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
,
j\geq 0
. We say that
f(\xi,\mu)\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}f_{j}(\xi,\mu)
in
\textup{S}^{\infty,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
if

f(\xi,\mu)-\sum_{j<N}f_{j}(\xi,\mu)\in\textup{S}^{m-N,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})\qquad\text{for all $N$}.

The following is Borel’s lemma for weakly parametric symbols, the counterpart of
[
11
, Lem. 1.9]
in the setting of twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical systems.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
m,d\in\mathbb{R}
and
f_{j}(\xi,\mu)\in\textup{S}^{m-j,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
,
j\geq 0
. Then there exists
f(\xi,\mu)\in\textup{S}^{m,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
such that
f(\xi,\mu)\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}f_{j}(\xi,\mu)
in
\textup{S}^{\infty,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
.

The following definition of weakly polyhomogeneous symbols is the analogue of
[
11
, Def. 1.10]
. Here we only deal with
1
-step polyhomogeneous symbols, i.e., there is
m\in\mathbb{R}
such that
m_{j}=m-j
for all
j\geq 0
.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
f(\xi,\mu)\in\textup{S}^{\infty,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
is said to be
weakly polyhomogeneous
if there exist symbols
f_{m-j}(\xi,\mu)\in\textup{S}^{m-j-d,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
j=0,1,\ldots
, homogeneous in
(\xi,\mu)
for
|\xi|\geq 1
of degree
m-j
, such that
f(\xi,\mu)\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}f_{m-j}(\xi,\mu)
in
\textup{S}^{\infty,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
in the sense of Definition
4.4
.

Remark 4.7
.

Let
f(\xi,\mu)\in\textup{S}^{\infty,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
,
f(\xi,\mu)\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}f_{m-j}(\xi,\mu)
in
\textup{S}^{\infty,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
, be a weakly polyhomogeneous symbol as in Definition
4.6
. Then, for every
j\geq 0
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}f_{m-j}(\xi,\mu)
is in
\textup{S}^{m-j-|\beta|-d,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
and homogeneous in
(\xi,\mu)
for
|\xi|\geq 1
of degree

. Furthermore, it follows from the very definition of weakly polyhomogeneous symbols that, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}f(\xi,\mu)
is weakly polyhomogeneous and
\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}f(\xi,\mu)\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}f_{m-j}(\xi,\mu)
in
\textup{S}^{\infty,d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Gamma,\mathscr{A}^{\infty})
in the sense of Definition
4.4
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\Gamma\cup\{0\})\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{\infty}
be a smooth map such that
f
is homogeneous in
(\xi,\mu)
of degree
m\in\mathbb{Z}

5.1.

Lemma 5.1

\displaystyle=\int_{|\xi|\leq|\mu|}\psi(R_{M}^{h}(\xi,\mu)){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi-\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}\psi(R_{M}^{h}(\xi,\mu)){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}}\xi

By choosing

Remark 5.6
.

As addressed in
[
19
, §6]
, the trace
\operatorname{Tr}_{\psi}
on

may not agree with the Hilbert space trace of a representation. Thus, in the case of parameter dependent symbols, the comparison of the two traces should be done as in
[
19
, Thm. 6.2]
in order to derive the asymptotic expansion of the Hilbert space trace of the operator
A(P-\lambda)^{-k}
. However, the argument in
[
19
, §6]
for comparing the two traces is not applicable in the case of weakly parametric pseudodifferential calculus. The estimate in the proof of
[
19
, Lem. 6.1]
, which is a key ingredient in proving
[
19
, Thm. 6.2]
, is in complete analogy with the Shubin type parametric pseudodifferential calculus
[
25
, §9]

\lambda

\lambda
in the proof of
[
19

. However, in the case of weakly parametric symbols

, the
\xi
-derivatives do not change the rate of decay with respect to

(
cf
.
[
11

), and this is the reason why the method for comparing the two traces proposed in
[
19
, §6]
cannot be applied to the case of weakly parametric calculus. We plan to address this problem in a future project where we want to conduct the comparison of the two traces and derive the asymptotic expansion of the Hilbert space trace of
A(P-\lambda)^{-k}
. The derivation of such an asymptotic expansion is important in view of the fact that the coefficient of the logarithmic term

in the expansion of the Hilbert space trace of

is essential in the study of the noncommutative residue trace (see, e.g.,
[
18
]
for a detailed account on this point).

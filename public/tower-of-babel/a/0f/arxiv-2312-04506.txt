On non-visibility of Kobayashi geodesics and the geometry of the boundary Thanks: The author received support from the University Research School EUR-MINT (State support managed by the National Research Agency for Future Investments program bearing the reference ANR-18-EURE-0023).
Ahmed Yekta Ökten

Abstract
. We show that on convex domains with 𝒞 1 , α \mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha} -smooth boundary the limit set of non-visible Kobayashi geodesics are contained in a complex face. In ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} this implies the existence of a complex tangential line segment of non-Goldilocks point in the boundary. Conversely, we construct sequences of non-visible almost-geodesics on ( ℂ − \mathbb{C}- )convex domains whose boundary contains a complex tangential line segment of non-Goldilocks points in a specific direction.

1.
Introduction

The Kobayashi distance is a fundamental holomorphic invariant. The study of its metric geometric properties turned out to be an effective tool; for instance, Gromov hyperbolicity of the Kobayashi distance is exploited to obtain results about iteration theory and boundary extension of holomorphic maps. Zimmer
[
Zim2
]
showed that a smoothly bounded convex domain is Gromov hyperbolic if and only if it is of finite D’Angelo type, establishing a connection between the metric geometry of the Kobayashi distance and the Euclidean geometry of the boundary.

However, for many applications, the somewhat weaker property of visibility is sufficient. Informally speaking, it means that all geodesics joining points tending to two distinct given boundary points have to pass through the same compact set, staying away from the boundary. Bharali-Zimmer
[
BZ1
]
defined a class of domains called
Goldilocks domains
in terms of the growth of the Kobayashi distance and the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric, and they showed that such domains satisfy visibility property for almost-geodesics, i.e. the curves that approximate Kobayashi geodesics in a suitable sense. The paper
[
BZ2
]
defined the notion of
local Goldilocks points
in a similar manner and showed that local Goldilocks points cannot be in the limit set of almost-geodesics tending to the boundary.

In the case of convex domains, local Goldilocks points can be described in terms of the ”flatness” of the boundary. In particular finite type boundary points are local Goldilocks points, yet local Goldilocks point conditions allow infinite type to some extent. On the other hand,
[
BNT
, Proposition 5.4]
provides an example of a convex domain with a complex tangential line segment of infinite type points which fails the visibility property.

The goal of this note is to present some sufficient and necessary conditions for the failure of visibility on convex domains in terms of the Euclidean geometry of the boundary. Notably, we prove Theorem
11
which shows that on convex domains with
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-smooth boundary, Kobayashi geodesics (and also certain almost-geodesics) tending to the boundary must tend to a complex hyperplane. In particular, in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
(see Corollary
12
) this implies that the boundary is flat in the precise sense that it contains a complex tangential line segment of non-Goldilocks points. The proof of this result follows from Proposition
3
, where we improve several results in the literature that relate visibility to the growth of Kobayashi distance.

Conversely, inspired by
[
BNT
, Proposition 5.4]
we prove Theorem
17
, whose proof shows that convex domains in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
with a boundary containing a complex tangential line segment of points with a strong enough flatness (so in particular not Goldilocks) possess almost-geodesics that tend to the boundary. Consequently, we observe that the local Goldilocks point conditions are somewhat necessary for visibility, providing a partial converse to
[
BZ1
, Theorem 1.4]
. Theorem
17
extends to higher dimensions and to
\mathbb{C}
-convex domains under additional assumptions (see Corollaries
18
and
19
). Notably, the domains given in Corollary
19
are not Gromov hyperbolic with respect to the Kobayashi distance, and this improves
[
Zim2
, Theorem 1.5]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
z,w\in\Omega
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Recall that the Kobayashi pseudodistance
k_{\Omega}
is the largest pseudodistance
that does not exceed the Lempert function

l_{\Omega}(z,w):=\tanh^{-1}\tilde{l}_{\Omega}(z,w),

where
\Delta
is the
unit disc and
\tilde{l}_{\Omega}(z,w):=\inf\{|\alpha|:\exists\varphi\in\mathcal{O}(\Delta,\Omega)\hbox{ with }\varphi(0)=z,\varphi(\alpha)=w\}
. Explicitly, the Kobayashi length of any continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
is defined as

\displaystyle{l^{k}_{\Omega}(\gamma):=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}l_{\Omega}(z_{j-1},z_{j}):z_{0}=\gamma(0),z_{n}=\gamma(1),\>z_{j}\in\gamma([0,1])\right\}}

and

k_{\Omega}(z,w):=\displaystyle{\inf l^{k}_{\Omega}(\gamma)},
where the infimum is taken over all continuous curves joining
z,w\in\Omega
. A continuous curve attaining the infimum above is said to be a Kobayashi geodesic.

The Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric is given by

\kappa_{\Omega}(z;v)=\inf\{1/r:r>0,\exists\varphi\in\mathcal{O}(r\Delta,\Omega),\varphi(0)=z,\varphi^{\prime}(0)=v\}.

By
[
Ven
, Theorem 1.2]
, one may express
k_{\Omega}
as the inner distance associated with the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric. That is

k_{\Omega}(z,w)=\inf l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma),
where

l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma):=\int_{I}\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))dt

and the infimum is taken over all absolutely continuous curves joining
z
to
w
.

We say that a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is
hyperbolic
if
k_{\Omega}
is a distance and
complete hyperbolic
if
(\Omega,k_{\Omega})
is complete as a metric space. For instance, any convex domain containing no affine complex lines is complete hyperbolic. The Hopf-Rinow theorem
[
BH
, Proposition 3.7]
asserts that if
\Omega
is complete hyperbolic then any pair
z,w\in\Omega
is joined by a Kobayashi geodesic. In general, geodesics for the Kobayashi distance need not exist. However, the definition of the Kobayashi distance as an inner distance ensures the existence of what is termed ”almost geodesics” in
[
BZ1
, Definition 4.1]
. In this note, we will call them
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-geodesics:

Definition 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
be an absolutely continuous curve,
\lambda\geq 1
and
\epsilon\geq 0
. We say that
\gamma
is a
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-geodesic
if for all
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\in[0,1]
we have that

l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]})\leq\lambda k_{\Omega}(\gamma(t_{1}),\gamma(t_{2}))+\epsilon.

For brevity, throughout this note
(1,\epsilon)
-geodesics will be called ”
\epsilon
-geodesics”.

Since the geodesics for the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric need not exist, Bharali-Zimmer
[
BZ1
]
defined a notion of visibility in terms of
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-geodesics (see also the remark below
[
CMS
, Definition 1.1]
).

Definition 2
.

Let
\Omega
be a hyperbolic domain,
\lambda\geq 1
and
\epsilon\geq 0
. We say that the pair of distinct points
\{p,q\}\subset\partial\Omega
is a
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-visible pair if there exist neighbourhoods
U_{p},U_{q}
of
p,q
respectively and
K\subset\subset\Omega
such that if
\gamma:I\rightarrow\Omega
is a
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-geodesic joining a point
z\in\Omega\cap U_{p}
to a point
w\in\Omega\cap U_{q}
then
\gamma(I)\cap K\neq\emptyset
. We say that
\Omega
satisfies
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-visibility property if any pair of distinct points
\{p,q\}\subset\Omega
is a
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-visible pair. We say that
\Omega
satisfies the
visibility property
(respectively the
weak visibility property
) if it satisfies the
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-visibility property for any
\lambda\geq 1,\epsilon\geq 0
(respectively
\lambda=1
and
\epsilon\geq 0
).

Similarly, if
\Omega
is complete hyperbolic we say that a pair of distinct points
\{p,q\}\subset\partial\Omega
is a
geodesic visible pair
if there exist neighbourhoods
U_{p},U_{q}
of
p,q
respectively and
K\subset\subset\Omega
such that if
\gamma:I\rightarrow\Omega
is a Kobayashi geodesic joining a point
z\in\Omega\cap U_{p}
to a point
w\in\Omega\cap U_{q}
then
\gamma(I)\cap K\neq\emptyset
. We say that
\Omega
satisfies the
geodesic visibility property
if any pair of distinct points
\{p,q\}\subset\partial\Omega
is a geodesic visible pair.

Recall the definition of the Gromov product with respect to Kobayashi distance:

(z|w)_{o}^{\Omega}:=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(k_{\Omega}(z,o)+k_{\Omega}(w,o)-k_{\Omega}(z,w)\right),\>\>\>\>z,w\in\Omega.

By
[
BNT
, Proposition 2.4]
(see also
[
CMS
, Proposition 3.1]
) the visibility property is related to the growth of Kobayashi distance:

Proposition 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a complete hyperbolic domain. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

\Omega
satisfies the geodesic visibility property.

(2)

\Omega
satisfies the weak visibility property.

(3)

(1)

\forall p,q\in\partial\Omega\>\>\>\text{with}\>\>\>p\neq q,\>\>\>\limsup_{\Omega\times\Omega\ni(z,w)\rightarrow(p,q)}(z|w)_{o}^{\Omega}<\infty.

In Section
3
we will give a local version of Proposition
1
.

3.
Failure of visibility on complete hyperbolic domains

3.1.
Limits of non-visible geodesics and the Gromov product

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and set

\delta_{\Omega}(z):=\inf\{\|z-w\|:w\in\partial\Omega\}\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\delta_{\Omega}(z;v):=\sup\{r>0:z+\alpha v\in\Omega\>\>\>\text{for all}\>\>\>\alpha\in r\Delta\}.

Suppose that
\gamma_{n}:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
is a sequence of curves satisfying

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t)):t\in[0,1]\}=0,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}(0)=p
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}(1)=q
with
q\neq p
. We denote their limit set as

(2)

L:=\{p\in\partial\Omega:\text{$p$ is a subsequential limit of a sequence $\{x_{n}\}$ such that $\forall n\in\mathbb{N}$, $x_{n}\in\gamma_{n}([0,1])$}\}.

Proposition 2
.

L\subset\partial\Omega
is closed and connected, therefore it is a continuum.

Proof.

It is clear that
L
is a closed set as it is defined to be a limit set.

Note that
L
is connected if and only if it cannot be written as the union of two non-empty open seperated sets, that is for any two non-empty open sets
U,V\subset L
such that
L=U\cup V
we have
\overline{U}\cap V\neq\emptyset
. Let now
U_{1},U_{2}
be two open sets in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
L\subset U_{1}\cup U_{2}
. Set
V_{1}:=U_{1}\cap L
and
V_{2}:=U_{2}\cap L
, we will show that
\overline{V_{1}}\cap V_{2}\neq\emptyset
. We may assume that
p:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}(0)\in U_{1}\setminus U_{2}
and
q:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}(0)\in U_{2}\setminus U_{1}
otherwise
V_{1}\cap V_{2}\neq\emptyset
.

We will show that
\overline{V}_{1}\cap V_{2}\neq\emptyset
. Since each
\gamma_{n}([0,1])
is connected and
p\in U_{1}
and
q\notin U_{1}
we may find
x_{n}\in\gamma_{n}([0,1])
such that
x_{n}\in\partial U_{1}
. Since the images of
\gamma_{n}
tend to
\partial\Omega
, by looking at a subsequence if necessary we may set
r:=\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}
. Then
r\in\partial{U_{1}}\cap L
. Since
L=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
and
r\notin V_{1}
it follows that
r\in V_{2}
. As
\overline{V}_{1}=\overline{U}_{1}\cap L
it follows that
\overline{V}_{1}\cap V_{2}\neq\emptyset
. As
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are choosen arbitrarily the connectivity of
L
follows.

∎

Definition 3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a complete hyperbolic domain,
p\in\partial\Omega
. We say that
q\in\partial\Omega
is a
Gromov partner
of
p
if

\displaystyle{\limsup_{\Omega\times\Omega\ni(z,w)\to(p,q)}(z|w)^{\Omega}_{o}=\infty}.

We denote the set of Gromov partners of
p
by
G_{p}
.

It is clear that the above definition is independent from the base point
o
, and that for any
p\in\partial\Omega
we have
p\in G_{p}
.

The main result of this section is the following:

Proposition 3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a complete hyperbolic domain, and suppose that
\{p,q\}\in\partial\Omega
is not a weakly visible pair. Let
\gamma_{n}:[0,1]\to\Omega
be a sequence of
\epsilon
-geodesics such that
\max_{t\in[0,1]}\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t))\to 0
,
\gamma_{n}(0)\to p
and
\gamma_{n}(1)\to q
. Let
L
be as in (
2
).

(1)

There exists
r\in L\setminus\{p,q\}
with
r\in G_{p}\cap G_{q}
.

(2)

L\subset G_{p}\cup G_{q}
.

.

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{n}:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
be as above, set
z_{n}:=\gamma_{n}(0)
and
w_{n}:=\gamma_{n}(1)
. Fix
o\in\Omega
and set
h_{n}:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
to be
h_{n}(t):=2\left((z_{n}|\gamma_{n}(t))^{\Omega}_{o}-(w_{n}|\gamma_{n}(t))^{\Omega}_{o}\right)
. Then by a direct calculation, we have
h_{n}(0)=2k_{\Omega}(z_{n},o)-(k_{\Omega}(z_{n},o)+k_{\Omega}(w_{n},o)-k_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n}))
and
h_{n}(1)=(k_{\Omega}(z_{n},o)+k_{\Omega}(w_{n},o)-k_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n}))-2k_{\Omega}(w_{n},o)
so by the triangle inequality

h_{n}(0)=2(w_{n}|o)_{z_{n}}^{\Omega}\geq 0\>\>\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\>\>h_{n}(1)=-2(z_{n}|o)_{w_{n}}^{\Omega}\leq 0.

Note that
\Omega
is complete hyperbolic hence it is taut. This shows that
\kappa_{\Omega}
is continuous on
\Omega\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
and hence
k_{\Omega}
is continuous on
\Omega
. In particular, this implies that each
h_{n}
is a continuous function. An application of the intermediate value theorem to
h_{n}
shows that there exists a
\tau_{n}\in[0,1]
such that
h_{n}(\tau_{n})=0
, that is
(z_{n}|\gamma_{n}(\tau_{n}))^{\Omega}_{o}=(w_{n}|\gamma_{n}(\tau_{n}))^{\Omega}_{o}
. Set
x_{n}:=\gamma_{n}(\tau_{n})
. Then a direct calculation shows that

2(z_{n}|x_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}=2(w_{n}|x_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}=(z_{n}|x_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}+(w_{n}|x_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}\geq(z_{n}|w_{n})_{o}^{\Omega}+k_{\Omega}(x_{n},o)-\epsilon/2\geq k_{\Omega}(x_{n},o)-\epsilon/2.

As
\Omega
is complete hyperbolic
k_{\Omega}(x_{n},o)
tends to infinity, so both
(z_{n}|x_{n})^{\Omega}_{o},(w_{n}|x_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}
tend to infinity. By passing to a subsequence, we observe that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}=:r\in G_{p}\cap G_{q}
. If
r\notin\{p,q\}
we are done.

If not, without loss of generality assume that
r=p
. Take
\tau^{\prime}_{n}\in[\tau_{n},1]
such that
x^{\prime}_{n}:=\gamma_{n}(\tau^{\prime}_{n})
verifies
\|x^{\prime}_{n}-w_{n}\|=\|x^{\prime}_{n}-x_{n}\|
. Passing to a subsequence if necessary set
r^{\prime}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}x^{\prime}_{n}
. Clearly,
r^{\prime}\notin\{p,q\}
. As
x^{\prime}_{n}\in\gamma_{n}([\tau_{n},1])
we have

(x^{\prime}_{n}|w_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}-(x_{n}|w_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}=\dfrac{1}{2}[k_{\Omega}(x^{\prime}_{n},o)-k_{\Omega}(x_{n},o)+k_{\Omega}(x_{n},w_{n})-k_{\Omega}(x^{\prime}_{n},w_{n})]\geq\\
\dfrac{1}{2}[k_{\Omega}(x^{\prime}_{n},o)-k_{\Omega}(x_{n},o)+l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}|_{[c_{n},b_{n}]})-l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}|_{[c^{\prime}_{n},b_{n}]})-\epsilon]\geq\dfrac{1}{2}[k_{\Omega}(x^{\prime}_{n},o)-k_{\Omega}(x_{n},o)+k_{\Omega}(x^{\prime}_{n},x_{n})-\epsilon].

So by the triangle inequality we get

(x^{\prime}_{n}|w_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}\geq(x_{n}|w_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}-\epsilon/2
. Similarly,
(x^{\prime}_{n}|x_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}\geq(x_{n}|w_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}-\epsilon/2
. By construction
(x_{n}|w_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}
tends to infinity; hence so do
(x^{\prime}_{n}|w_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}
and
(x^{\prime}_{n}|x_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}
. As
x_{n}\rightarrow p
,
x^{\prime}_{n}\rightarrow r^{\prime}
and
w_{n}\rightarrow q
we have
r^{\prime}\in G_{p}\cap G_{q}
. We have the first statement.

To see the second statement, by the first statement assume that there exists
\tau_{n}\in[0,1]
with
(\gamma_{n}(\tau_{n})|z_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}
and
(\gamma_{n}(\tau_{n})|w_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}
tending to infinity. Suppose that
s\in L
, then there exists
\tau^{\prime}_{n}\in[0,1]
with
s_{n}:=\gamma_{n}(\tau^{\prime}_{n})
tending to
s
. Switching
p
and
q
if needed, by passing to a subsequence we may assume that
\tau_{n}\leq\tau^{\prime}_{n}
. As
s_{n}\in\gamma_{n}([\tau_{n},1])
similar calculations as above show that
(s_{n}|w_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}\geq(\gamma_{n}(\tau_{n})|w_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}-\epsilon/2
. Thus,
(s_{n}|w_{n})^{\Omega}_{o}
tends to infinity hence
s\in G_{q}
. As
s\in L
is arbitrary this finishes the proof.
∎

Unfortunately, it is not clear whether in the above scenario we have that
q\in G_{p}
.

The proof of Proposition
3
leads to the following corollary which improves the conclusion of
[
BNT
, Proposition 2.5]
and
[

.

Corollary 4
.

Let
\Omega
be a complete hyperbolic domain and suppose that
p\in\partial\Omega
is not a weakly visible point. Then, there exists a
\delta>0
such that whenever
\delta^{\prime}\leq\delta
, there exists
r\in G_{p}
such that
\|p-r\|=\delta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
p
is not a weakly visible point, then there exists
q\in\partial\Omega
different from
p
so that
\{p,q\}
is not a weakly visible pair. Let
\gamma_{n}:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
be a sequence of
\epsilon
-geodesics joining
\gamma_{n}(0)=:z_{n}
to
\gamma_{n}(1)=:w_{n}
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t)):t\in[0,1]\}=0
and
z_{n}\to p
,
w_{n}\to q
. Let
L
be as in (
2
). By Proposition
3
there exists
r\in L
, different from
p
such that
r\in G_{p}
. Then, there exists
x_{n}\in\gamma_{n}([0,1])
tending to
r
and
(z_{n}|x_{n})_{o}^{\Omega}
tending to infinity. Set
\delta:=\|p-r\|
and let
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,\delta)
be arbitrary. By connectivity of geodesics, looking at the tail of the sequence if necessary we may choose
x^{\prime}_{n}
in the part of
\gamma_{n}(I_{n})
joining
z_{n}
to
x_{n}
with
\|z_{n}-x^{\prime}_{n}\|=\delta^{\prime}
. The proof of Proposition
3
shows that
(x^{\prime}_{n}|z_{n})_{o}^{\Omega}\geq(z_{n}|x_{n})_{o}^{\Omega}-\epsilon/2
, thus
(x^{\prime}_{n}|z_{n})_{o}^{\Omega}
tends to infinity. Looking at a subsequence set
\lim_{n\to\infty}x^{\prime}_{n}=:r^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega
. Then
\|r^{\prime}-p\|=\delta^{\prime}
and
r^{\prime}\in G_{p}
. ∎

Remark 1
.

Taking
\epsilon=0
and replacing the Kobayashi-Royden length with the Kobayashi length, the proof of Proposition
3
shows that it also remains true when we consider the case where
\{p,q\}
is not a geodesic visible pair and we consider the limit set of Kobayashi geodesics tending to the boundary. Similarly, Corollary
4
remains true when
p
is not a geodesic visible point.

Remark 2
.

Observe that the proof of Corollary
4
shows that the set of Gromov partners of
p\in\partial\Omega
can be realized as a limit set of curves tending to the boundary. In particular, by Proposition

we see that

is a continuum.

Recall that a domain

We present the following estimates of Kobayashi distance and the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric which will be used in the upcoming sections. Note that (

Recall the notion of local Goldilocks points introduced in
[

following the notion of Goldilocks domains given in

Definition 4

Let

and

(1)

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\epsilon}}\frac{M_{\Omega,U}(r)dr}{r}<\infty

For some (hence any)

Note that as the boundaries of convex domains are Lipschitz they satisfy an interior cone condition (see
[

Let

Result 1

[

Let

Remark 4

It follows from the proof of the result above that local Goldilocks points cannot be in the limit set of

-geodesics that tend to the boundary for any

The goal of this section is to give a detailed version of Remark

in the case of convexity.

Let
\Omega

at

[

Let

Let
\Omega

[

Let

Theorem 11
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-boundary, where

. Suppose that
\Omega

\gamma_{n}:[0,1]\to\Omega

\epsilon

Unfortunately, if

5.

\mathbb{C}
-)convex domains

5.1.
Motivating example: Rate of contact with the complex tangent space

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a (
\mathbb{C}
-)convex domain,
p\in\partial\Omega
and
U
be a neighbourhood of
p
. By (
4
) one may see that the first condition in Definition
4
can be characterized in terms of the rate of contact with the complex tangent space near
p
. Explicitly,
p\in\partial\Omega
is a non-Goldilocks point if and only if for any sufficiently small neighbourhood of
U
of

we have some
\epsilon>0
with

(5)

\int^{\epsilon}_{0}\dfrac{N_{\Omega,U}(r)}{r}dr=\infty,

where
N_{\Omega,U}:=\sup\{\delta_{\Omega}(z;v):z\in\Omega\cap U\>\text{with}\>\delta_{\Omega}(z)\leq r,v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\>\text{with}\>\|v\|=1\}
.

Consider the special type of domains defined by

(6)

\Omega_{\Psi}:=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\operatorname{\rm{Re}}z_{2}\geq\Psi(\operatorname{\rm{Re}}z_{1})\},

where
\Psi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a smooth even convex function, increasing on
[0,\infty)
, verifying

\Psi(0)=0
. Since
\Omega_{\Psi}
is a convex domain, the second item of Definition
4
is satisfied on
\Omega_{\Psi}
. Moreover, by (

) it follows from
[

, Theorem 1.4]
and a direct calculation that if
\Psi
verifies

(7)

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{\Psi^{-1}(x)}{x}dx<\infty

for some (hence any)
\varepsilon>0
then
\Omega_{\Psi}
satisfies the visibility property.

On the other hand, note that if
\Psi
satisfies

(8)

\Psi(x)=o(e^{\frac{-{\pi}}{2x}}),\>\>\>\>\>x\>\>\text{near}\>\>0,

then it follows by
[

, Proposition 5.4]
that
\Omega_{\Psi}
fails the geodesic visibility (hence the weak visibility) property. In fact, we can easily extend this.

Proposition 13
.

Let
\Omega:=\Omega_{\Psi}
be defined as in (

c>0
with

(9)

\Psi(x)=o(e^{-\frac{c}{x}}),\>\>x\>\>\text{near}\>\>0.

\Omega_{\Psi}

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be as above. Note that we can choose a constant
r>0
such that the biholomorphism (of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
)
f_{r}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}
defined by
(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(rz_{1},z_{2})
maps
\Omega
onto
\Omega^{\prime}
where a defining function of
\Omega^{\prime}
verifies (
8
). In particular, by Proposition
3
it follows that there exists
p^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega^{\prime}
with
G_{p^{\prime}}\neq\{p^{\prime}\}
. Clearly, the map
f_{r}
preserves Euclidean distance up to a multiplicative constant. Then by the biholomorphic invariance of the Kobayashi distance, we conclude that there exists
p\in\partial\Omega
with
G_{p}\neq\{p\}
. By Corollary
5
we are done.
∎

In the examples above, seemingly, the Euclidean distance between points of
\partial\Omega\cap\{\text{Re}z_{1}=0\}
plays a part in whether or not they are a weakly visible or geodesic visible pair. See the proof of
[

for details.

Proposition

leaves us with a gap, as there are domains
\Omega_{\Psi}
defined as in (

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
containing no affine complex lines and
\pi_{\Omega}:\Omega\to\partial\Omega

z
in
\partial\Omega
is not unique, define

to be one of the points

verifying

\{X_{z},Y_{z}\}
be a basis consisting of complex tangential and complex normal unit vectors to
\partial\Omega
at
\pi_{\Omega}(z)
respectively. Explicitly, let
X_{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
be a unit vector in

and
Y_{z}:=(\pi_{\Omega}(z)-z)/\|\pi_{\Omega}(z)-z\|
. For

set

and

Lemma 14
.

[

, Proposition 14]

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
containing no affine complex lines. There exists
c>0
such that

(10)

\kappa_{\Omega}(z;v_{z})+\kappa_{\Omega}(z;v^{\perp}_{z})\geq\kappa_{\Omega}(z;v)\geq c(\kappa_{\Omega}(z;v_{z})+\kappa_{\Omega}(z;v^{\perp}_{z})),\>\>\>\>\>z\in\Omega,v\in\mathbb{C}^{2},

The lemma above is a weaker form of
[

, Proposition 14]
, which remains true on
\mathbb{C}
-convex domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
in terms of the minimal basis (see for instance
[

[

]
implies that if

Lemma

leads to the following proposition.

Proposition 15
.

Let
\Omega:=\Omega_{\Psi}
be given as in (
6

\Psi
does not verify (
7
).Then any

is not a visible point. Explicitly, for any

, there exist
(\lambda,0)
-geodesics
\gamma_{n}:[0,1]\to\Omega

\max_{t\in[0,1]}\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t))\to 0

\gamma_{n}(0)\to p
and
\gamma_{n}(1)\not\to p

We may assume that
p=(0,0)
.

Set
p_{n}:=(0,1/n)
and
\gamma_{n}:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
to be the curve given by
\gamma_{n}(t):=(it,f_{n}(t))

\gamma_{n}(0)=p_{n}
and
\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(i,0))=c\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f_{n}^{\prime}(t)))

Claim 1:

\displaystyle{\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t))=0}.

Proof of Claim 1:

A direct calculation shows that

(11)

\delta^{-1}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(i,0))=1/\Psi^{-1}(f_{n}(t))\>\>\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\>\>\delta^{-1}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f_{n}^{\prime}(t)))=f^{\prime}_{n}(t)/f_{n}(t).

(12)

\dfrac{\Psi^{-1}(f_{n}(t))f^{\prime}_{n}(t)}{f_{n}(t)}=c^{-1}.

Let
D_{n}:=\max_{t\in[0,1]}\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t))=\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(1)).
Then, by (

c^{-1}=\int_{0}^{1}c^{-1}dt=\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\Psi^{-1}(f_{n}(t))f^{\prime}_{n}(t)}{f_{n}(t)}dt=\int_{1/n}^{D_{n}}\dfrac{\Psi^{-1}(y)dy}{y}.

For large

\gamma_{n}
is a
(1+2c,0)
-geodesic.

By Claim 1, the images of
\gamma_{n}
tend to the boundary. Then for large

and

a basis consisting of a complex tangential and a complex normal unit vector to

\{(1,0),(0,1)\}

\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f^{\prime}_{n}(t)))\geq\delta^{-1}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f^{\prime}_{n}(t)))/2={f^{\prime}_{n}(t)}/{2f_{n}(t)}

\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f^{\prime}_{n}(t)))\leq\delta^{-1}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f^{\prime}_{n}(t)))/2={f^{\prime}_{n}(t)}/{2f_{n}(t)}
hence
\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f^{\prime}_{n}(t)))={f^{\prime}_{n}(t)}/{2f_{n}(t)}
. Then Lemma

and (

and

\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(t))=\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(i,f_{n}^{\prime}(t)))\leq\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f_{n}^{\prime}(t)))+\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(i,0))\leq

By choosing

5.2.
A generalization: Strongly non-Goldilocks points and non-visibility

The goal of this section is to extend Proposition
15
to the general case for convex domains with
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-boundary.

Definition 5
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. A
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth boundary point
p\in\partial\Omega
is said to be a
weakly Goldilocks point
if for some
\epsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\dfrac{\sup\{\kappa^{-1}_{\Omega}(p_{r^{\prime}};v):v\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\>\>\>\|v\|=1,\>\>\>r^{\prime}\leq r\}}{r}dr<\infty},

where
p_{r}:=p+r\eta_{p}
and
\eta_{p}
denotes inner unit normal to
\partial\Omega
given at
p
.

Points which are not weakly Goldilocks are called
strongly non-Goldilocks
.
It is clear that being a locally Goldilocks point in the sense of Definition
4
implies being a weakly Goldilocks point. A counter-example in the next subsection shows that there are points which are not locally
Goldilocks, but still are weakly Goldilocks,

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-convex domain then (
4
) implies that
p\in\partial\Omega
is a strongly non-Goldilocks point if and only if

(14)

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\dfrac{\sup\{\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r^{\prime}};v):v\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\>\>\>\|v\|=1,\>\>\>r^{\prime}\leq r\}}{r}dr=\infty}.

In fact, we can say even more if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
is convex.

Proposition 16
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
p\in\partial\Omega
be a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth boundary point and
X\in T^{\mathbb{C}}_{p}\partial\Omega
be a unit vector. Set
M_{\Omega,p}(r):=\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r};X).
Then
p
is a strongly non-Goldilocks point if and only if

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\dfrac{M_{\Omega,p}(r)}{r}dr=\infty}.

Proof.

As the other implication is clear, we only need to show that if
p
is a strongly non-Goldilocks point then we must have
\int_{0}^{\epsilon}{M_{\Omega,p}(r)}/{r}dr=\infty
.
Assume that
p\in\partial\Omega
is a strongly non-Goldilocks point.

Claim 1:
There exists
C>0
depending on
\Omega
, and
\delta_{1}>0
depending on
p
with

\sup\{\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r};v):v\in\mathbb{C}^{2},\>\>\>\|v\|=1\}\leq CM_{\Omega,p}(r)

if
r<\delta_{1}
.

To see Claim 1, let
\delta>0
be small enough so that for
r\in(0,\delta)
the closest point to
p_{r}
in
\partial\Omega
is given by
p
. Let
Y:=(p_{r}-p)/\|p_{r}-p\|
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
with
\|v\|=1
be arbitrary. Write
v=\alpha X+\beta Y
where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1
. Then by Lemma
9
and Lemma
14
we have

\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r};v)\leq\kappa^{-1}_{\Omega}(p_{r};v)\leq c^{-1}(|\alpha|\kappa_{\Omega}(p_{r};X)+|\beta|\kappa_{\Omega}(p_{r};Y))^{-1}\leq\dfrac{1}{2c}\kappa^{-1}_{\Omega}(p_{r};X)\leq\dfrac{1}{c}M_{\Omega,p}(r).

Note that above we use the fact that
\kappa_{\Omega}(p_{r};X)\leq\kappa_{\Omega}(p_{r};Y)
which follows from boundary regularity. Setting
\delta_{1}:=\delta
and taking the supremum over
v
, we have Claim 1.

Claim 2:
There exists
\delta_{2}>0
such that
M_{\Omega,p}(r)
is a monotone increasing function on
(0,\delta_{2})
.

Note that
M_{\Omega,p}(r)
is a concave function of
r
. To see this choose
r_{1}<r_{2}
small enough and consider the analytic discs given by
D_{1}(\zeta):=p_{r_{1}}+\zeta\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r_{1}};X)X
,
D_{2}(\zeta):=p_{r_{2}}+\zeta\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r_{2}};X)X
. Take
t\in[0,1]
and consider the point
p^{t}:=tp_{r_{1}}+(1-t)p_{r_{2}}\in\Omega
. As
D_{1}(\Delta),D_{2}(\Delta)\subset\Omega
we have by convexity that the image of
D_{t}(\zeta):=tD_{1}(\zeta)+(1-t)D_{2}(\zeta)
lies in
\Omega
. This implies that
M_{\Omega,p}(tr_{1}+(1-t)r_{2})=\delta_{\Omega}(p^{t};X)\geq t\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r_{1}};X)+(1-t)\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r_{2}};X)=tM_{\Omega,p}(r_{1})+(1-t)M_{\Omega,p}(r_{2})
. Now, since
M_{\Omega,p}(r)
is a concave function of
r
, it attains its maximum value at (at most) one point, say
\delta_{2}
, thus
M_{\Omega,p}(r)
is a monotone increasing function on
(0,\delta_{2})
hence Claim 2 follows.

Now, consider
r<\delta
where
\delta:=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
with
\delta_{1}
as in Claim 1 and
\delta_{2}
as in Claim 2. Then there exists
C>0
depending on
\Omega
such that for
r\in(0,\delta)
we have

\sup\{\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r^{\prime}};v):v\in\mathbb{C}^{2},\>\>\>\|v\|=1,\>\>\>r^{\prime}\leq r\}\leq CM_{\Omega,p}(r).

By Claims 1 and 2 and (
14
) we are done.
∎

Actually, if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
(not necessarily
n=2
) is a convex domain containing no affine complex lines with no non-trivial analytic discs in the boundary, then the proof of
[
Bra
, Lemma 2.3]
(see the result of D’Addezio in
[
BG
, Lemma A.1]
) implies that

\lim_{r\to 0}\sup\{\delta_{\Omega}(z;v):\delta_{\Omega}(z)\leq r,\>\>\>\|v\|=1\}=0.

Therefore if
\Omega
has
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
-smooth boundary a stronger statement than Claim 2 above holds. Namely, in that case the uniform inner ball condition implies that there exists
\delta>0
such that

\sup\{\delta_{\Omega}(z;v):v\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\>\>\>\|v\|=1,\>\>\>\delta_{\Omega}(z)\leq r\}=\sup\{\delta_{\Omega}(z;v):v\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\>\>\>\|v\|=1,\>\>\>\delta_{\Omega}(z)=r\}

for
r\in(0,\delta).

One generalization of Proposition
15
is the following:

Theorem 17
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be a convex domain containing no affine complex lines with
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth boundary and
p\in\partial\Omega
. Suppose that
p
is a strongly non-Goldilocks point and
F_{p}\neq\{p\}
. Then
p
is not a visible point. Explicitly, there exists
\lambda>1
such that
\Omega
has
(\lambda,0)
-geodesics
\gamma_{n}:[0,1]\to\Omega
with
\max_{t\in[0,1]}\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t))\to 0
,
\gamma_{n}(0)\to p
and
\gamma_{n}(1)\not\to p
.

Proof.

Let
q\in F_{p}
such that
q\neq p
then
X=(p-q)/\|(p-q)\|\in T_{p}^{\mathbb{C}}\partial\Omega
. Note that boundary regularity and convexity show that the inner unit normal to
\partial\Omega
coincides at any point in
F_{p}
.

Claim 1:
There exists a line segment
S\subset F_{p}
containing
p
and a function
H:(0,\delta)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
verifying

\int_{0}^{\delta}H(r)/rdr=\infty
and
M_{\Omega,s}(r)\geq H(r)
for any
s\in S
and
r\in(0,\delta).

To see Claim 1, note that by the proof of Proposition
16
there exists
\delta>0
such that
M_{\Omega,p}(r)
is monotone increasing function on
r\in(0,\delta)
and
\int_{0}^{\delta}M_{\Omega,p}(r)/rdr=\infty.
Let
S=\{(1-t)p+tq:t\in[0,1/2]\}
,
f_{r}(\zeta):=p_{r}+\zeta\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r};X)X
and
f_{r,t}(\zeta):=(1-t)f_{r}(\zeta)+tq_{r}
. Note that by convexity
S\subset F_{p}
and the images of
f_{r,t}
lie in
\Omega
. Consequently
\delta_{\Omega}(((1-t)p+tq)_{r};X)\geq 1/2M_{\Omega,p}(r)
. Setting
H(r):=1/2M_{\Omega,p}(r)
, Claim 1 follows.

Now let
S,H(r)
be as in Claim 1. We may assume that
p=(0,0),q=(i\tau,0)
for some
\tau>0
so we may write
S=\{(it,0):t\in[0,\tau/2]\}
. For any
n\geq 1
consider the curves
\gamma_{n}(t):=(i\tau t/2,f_{n}(t))
where
f_{n}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth increasing function satisfying
f_{n}(0)=1/n
. Suppose furthermore that
\gamma_{n}
verifies
\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f^{\prime}_{n}(t)))=\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(i\tau/2,0)).
Such a curve exists by the existence and uniqueness theorem of ODE’s.

Claim 2:

\displaystyle{\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t))=0}.

Thus for large enough
n
and
t\in[0,1]
we have
\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t))=f_{n}(t)
.

Proof of Claim 2:
Note that

\delta^{-1}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f^{\prime}_{n}(t)))=f^{\prime}_{n}(t)/f_{n}(t)\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\delta^{-1}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(i\tau/2,0))\leq\tau/H(f_{n}(t)).

Then the proof follows from the same arguments we provided in the proof of Proposition
15
, Claim 1. The only difference is we replace
\Psi^{-1}(f_{n}(t))
with
H(f_{n}(t))
.

Claim 3:
Each
\gamma_{n}
is a
(4,0)
-geodesic.

Proof of Claim 3:
Let
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\in[0,1]
be arbitrary. Then as
\gamma_{n}([0,1])
tend to the boundary and for large
n
and
t\in[0,1]
we have
\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t))=f_{n}(t)
, Lemma
8
and Lemma
14
implies that

k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t_{1}),\gamma_{n}(t_{2}))\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{f_{n}(t_{2})}{f_{n}(t_{1})}\right)

and

\displaystyle{l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]})=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(t))dt\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(i\tau/2,0))dt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f^{\prime}_{n}(t)))dt\leq}

\displaystyle{2\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\delta^{-1}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t);(0,f^{\prime}_{n}(t)))dt=2\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\dfrac{f^{\prime}_{n}(t)}{f_{n}(t)}dt=2\log\left(\dfrac{f_{n}(t_{2})}{f_{n}(t_{1})}\right)}.

Hence we have Claim 3.

By Claims 2 and 3, taking
\lambda=4
we are done.
∎

Theorem
17
extends to
\mathbb{C}
-convex domains in the following way:

Corollary 18
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-convex domain containing no affine complex lines,
p\in\partial\Omega
,
X\in T^{\mathbb{C}}_{p}\partial\Omega
be a unit vector vector and
S\subset T^{\mathbb{C}}_{p}\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega
be a line segment containing
p
such that for each
s\in S
we have
T^{\mathbb{R}}_{p}\partial\Omega=T^{\mathbb{R}}_{s}\partial\Omega
. Suppose that there is a function
H:[0,\delta]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for
s\in S,r\in(0,\delta)
we have
\delta_{\Omega}(s_{r};X)\geq H(r)
and

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\delta}\dfrac{H(r)}{r}dr=\infty}.

Then
\Omega
fails the visibility property. Explicitly, there exists
\lambda>1
such that for any
s\in S
,
\Omega
has
(\lambda,0)
-geodesics
\gamma_{n}:[0,1]\to\Omega
with
{\max_{t\in[0,1]}}\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}(t))\to 0
,
\gamma_{n}(0)\to p
and
\gamma_{n}(1)\to s
.

Proof.

Note that the main use of convexity in the proof of Theorem
17
is in the proof of Claim 1. Corollary
18
includes the conclusion of Claim 1 in its assumptions. Then, the proof of Corollary
18
follows from the arguments provided in the proofs of Claims 2 and 3 in the proof of Theorem
17
. The only difference is to use the weaker estimates given in
\mathbb{C}
-convex cases of Lemmas
8
and
9
and to apply the complete version of
[
NPZ
, Proposition 14]
.
∎

We are curious to see if the converse of Theorem
17
holds.

5.3.
An application: Gromov non-hyperbolicity of convex domains

In higher dimensions the complex line containing the complex tangential line segment may not agree with the complex line with the sufficient rate of contact, making the situation more complicated. To give an extension of Theorem
17
to higher dimensions we give the following definition.

Definition 6
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing no affine complex lines and let
S\subset\partial\Omega
be a line segment. We say that such a line segment is a
non-Goldilocks line segment
if:

(1)

There exists a complex hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
S\subset H\cap\partial\Omega
.

(2)

There exists
\delta_{0}>0
and a unit vector
X\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that for any
p\in S
and
r\in(0,\delta_{0})

p_{r}:=p+rX\in\Omega
and
\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r})=\|p-p_{r}\|=r
.

(3)

Let
s,q
be two distinct points of
S
and set
Y:=(s-q)/\|s-q\|
. There exists a function
H:(0,\delta_{0})\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
such that
\delta_{\Omega}(p_{r};Y)\geq H(r)
for all
p\in S
and
r\in(0,\delta_{0})
, moreover

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta_{0}}\dfrac{H(r)}{r}dr=\infty.

It is easy to see from its proof that the domains given in Theorem
17
contain a non-Goldilocks line segment in the boundary. In fact, by applying the complete version of
[
NPZ
, Proposition 14]
and imitating the proof of Theorem
17
one may extend it to higher dimensions as follows.

Corollary 19
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a convex domain containing no affine complex lines and assume that
\partial\Omega
contains a non-Goldilocks line segment
S
. Then any
p\in S
is not a visible point.

For brevity, we refer the reader to
[
BH
, Part III]
for details on Gromov hyperbolicity. Recall the following result:

Result 2
.

[
BGZ
, Theorem 1.5]

Let
\Omega
be a Gromov hyperbolic convex domain containing no affine complex lines. Then the identity map extends to a homeomorphism between Gromov compactification of
\Omega
(with respect to the Kobayashi distance) and the end compactification of its Euclidean closure.

In view of the result above, visibility property of Gromov hyperbolic metric spaces with respect to their Gromov boundary (see for instance
[
BH
, p. 428, Lemma 3.2]
) and the geodesic stability lemma (see for instance
[
BH
, p. 401, Theorem 1.7]
) imply the following remark:

Remark 7
.

Let
\Omega
be a Gromov hyperbolic convex domain containing no affine complex lines. Then
\Omega
satisfies the visibility property.

Thanks to Corollary
19
and the remark above, we give the following Gromov non-hyperbolicity criterion for convex domains with no boundary regularity.

Theorem 20
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a convex domain containing no affine complex lines and assume that
\partial\Omega
contains a non-Goldilocks line segment
S
. Then
\Omega
is not Gromov hyperbolic.

Notably, the above result improves
[
Zim2
, Theorem 1.6]
.

5.4.
Two examples: Goldilocks points, weakly Goldilocks points, and visible points

Remark 8
.

There exists a convex domain
\Omega
with smooth boundary, and
p\in\partial\Omega
such that
p\notin\partial_{lg}\Omega
but
p
is a weakly Goldilocks point.

Proof.

Let
\Psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\Psi(0):=0
and
\Psi(x):=e^{-1/\sqrt{|x|}}
if
x\neq 0
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
t_{j}:=2^{-j}
,
and
L_{j}(x):=(\Psi(t_{j})-\Psi(t_{j+1}))(x-t_{t+j})/(t_{j}-t_{j+1})+\Psi(t_{j+1})
. Let
\Psi_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by
\Psi_{0}(x):=\max\{\Psi(x),L_{n}(x):n\in 2\mathbb{N}\}
. Since
\Psi
is convex and
\Psi(x)\leq L_{j}(x)
if and only if
x\in[t_{j+1},t_{j}]
,
\Psi_{0}(x)
is a continuous convex function.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
be a smooth even function with
\text{supp}(\rho)\subset\subset(-1,1)
and
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\rho(x)dx=1
. Set
\rho_{\epsilon}(x):=\rho(x/\epsilon)/\epsilon
, then
\text{supp}(\rho_{\epsilon})\subset\subset(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
and
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\rho_{\epsilon}(x)dx=1
.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}
set
\Psi_{\infty}(x):=(\Psi_{0}\ast\rho_{t_{j}/4})(x)
if
x\in[3t_{j}/4,5t_{j}/4]
and
\Psi_{\infty}(x)=\Psi_{0}(x)
otherwise.

Claim 1:

\Psi_{\infty}
is a smooth convex function and
\Psi_{\infty}(x)\geq\Psi_{0}(x)
.

For
j>0
and
x\in[3t_{j}/4,5t_{j}/4]
we have that

\Psi_{\infty}^{(2)}(x)=(\Psi_{0}^{(2)}\ast\rho_{t_{j}/4})(x)
hence it follows from the convexity of
\Psi_{0}
that
\Psi_{\infty}
is also a convex function. Furthermore since
\rho
is even and
\Psi_{0}
is convex, by definition of
\Psi_{\infty}
one concludes that
\Psi_{\infty}(x)\geq\Psi_{0}(x)
. Note that due to our construction, the intervals
[3t_{j}/4,5t_{j}/4]
are of positive distance with one another. It is not difficult to see that this implies smoothness of
\Psi_{\infty}
away from the origin. To see that it is smooth at the origin we will show that

(15)

\lim_{x\to 0}{\Psi_{\infty}^{(n)}(x)}=0,\>\>\>\>\>\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Set
S_{0}:=\cup_{n=0}^{\infty}[3t_{n}/4,5t_{n}/4]
and
S_{1}:=\mathbb{R}\setminus S_{0}
. Then
\Psi_{\infty}(x)=\Psi_{0}(x)
for
x\in S_{1}
, consequently

\lim_{S_{1}\ni x\to 0}{\Psi_{\infty}^{(n)}(x)}=0,\>\>\>\>\>\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Fix
j>0
and let
x\in[3t_{j}/4,5t_{j}/4]
. Then,

\Psi_{\infty}^{(n)}(x)=(\Psi_{0}\ast\rho^{(n)}_{t_{j}/4})(x)
so

\|\Psi_{\infty}^{(n)}(x)\|\leq\frac{2^{2n+1}C_{n}\Psi_{0}(5t_{j}/4)}{t_{j}^{n}}\leq\frac{2^{2n+1}C_{n}\Psi(2t_{j})}{t_{j}^{n}},

where
C_{n}:=\max_{x\in[-1,1]}|\rho^{(n)}(x)|
. As
\lim_{x\to 0}\Psi(2x)/x^{n}=0
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
the above estimate leads to

\lim_{S_{0}\ni x\to 0}{\Psi_{\infty}^{(n)}(x)}=0,\>\>\>\>\>\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

and by (
15
) Claim 1 follows.

Let
\Omega:=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\text{Re}(z_{2})>\Psi_{\infty}(\text{Re}(z_{1}))\}
and
p:=(0,0)
. Claim 1 implies that
\Omega
is a convex domain containing no affine complex lines with smooth boundary.

Claim 2.

p\in\partial\Omega
is a weakly Goldilocks point.

Since
M_{\Omega,p}(r)=\Psi^{-1}_{\infty}(r)\leq\Psi^{-1}_{0}(r)\leq 1/\log^{2}(1/r)
we have that
p
is a weakly Goldilocks point.

Claim 3.

p\in\partial\Omega
is a non-Goldilocks point.

Let
U
be any neighbourhood of
p
. Keeping the notation above, let
j
be large enough so that, the complex line
\operatorname{\rm{Re}}z_{2}=L_{2j}(\operatorname{\rm{Re}}z_{1})
has a non-empty relatively open intersection with
U\cap\partial\Omega
. Take
p^{\prime}
in the relative interior of the intersection, denote the inner unit normal to
\partial\Omega
at
p^{\prime}
by
\eta_{p^{\prime}}
and let
X:=(1,(\Psi_{0}(t_{2j})-\Psi_{0}(t_{2j+1}))/(t_{2j}-t_{2j+1})).
Then, by a direct calculation we have

\lim_{r\to 0}\delta_{\Omega}(p^{\prime}+r\eta_{p^{\prime}};X/\|X\|)>0.

As
U
was arbitrary, we conclude that
p\notin\partial_{lg}\Omega
.

By Claims 2 and 3, we are done.
∎

Remark 9
.

There exists a convex domain
\Omega
with smooth boundary such that the set of non-visible points in
\partial\Omega
is not a closed set.

Proof.

Keeping the notation in the proof of Remark
8
consider the real hyperplanes
given by
\operatorname{\rm{Re}}z_{2}=L_{j}(\operatorname{\rm{Re}}z_{1})
. Set

\Psi_{0}(\operatorname{\rm{Re}}z_{1},\operatorname{\rm{Im}}z_{1},\operatorname{\rm{Im}}z_{2}):=\max\{\Psi(\sqrt{\|z_{1}\|^{2}+|\operatorname{\rm{Im}}(z_{2})|^{2}}),L_{n}(\operatorname{\rm{Re}}z_{1}):n\in 2\mathbb{N}\}.

By taking appropriate convolutions as in the proof of Remark
8
, one may find a smooth convex function
\Psi_{\infty}
that agrees with
\Psi_{0}
outside of small neighborhoods of the disjoint sets
\partial E_{2j}
, where

E_{2j}:=\left\{(z_{1},\operatorname{\rm{Im}}z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}:\Psi(\sqrt{\|z_{1}\|^{2}+|\operatorname{\rm{Im}}(z_{2})|^{2}})\leq L_{2j}(\operatorname{\rm{Re}}z_{1})\right\},

and so
\Psi_{\infty}
agrees with each
L_{2j}
in mutually disjoint neigbourhoods of
((1/2)(t_{2j}+t_{2j+1}),0)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
. Then the domain
\Omega:=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\operatorname{\rm{Re}}(z_{2})>\Psi_{\infty}(z)\}
is a convex domain containing no affine complex lines, with smooth boundary. The domain
\Omega
fails the visibility property as it contains non-trivial disjoint open complex faces denoted by
F_{j}\subset\{\operatorname{\rm{Re}}z_{1}>0\}
. In fact, for any
j
, one may find non-visible pairs
\{p_{j},q_{j}\}\in F_{j}
. Since
p_{j}\to p:=(0,0)
,
p
is a limit of non-visible points.
Note that
\partial\Omega
consists of strongly pseudoconvex points away from
F:=\{p\}\cup\left(\cup_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}\right)
. Furthermore,

F_{j}\subset\{(z_{1},\Psi_{\infty}(z_{1},y_{2})+iy_{2}):(z_{1},y_{2})\in E_{2j}\}
. But it is easy to see that
E_{2j}\subset[t_{2j+1},t_{2j}]\times i\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, so that for
(x_{1}+iy_{1},y_{2})\in E_{2j}
,

\frac{1}{2}(t_{2j+2}+t_{2j+1})<t_{2j+1}\leq x_{1}\leq\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}\leq\Psi^{-1}(L_{2j}(x_{1}))\leq t_{2j}<\frac{1}{2}(t_{2j-1}+t_{2j}).

Take
q\in\partial\Omega
with
q\neq p
then observe that by above any curve joining points tending to
p
and
q
respectively must have a part crossing the “sphere”

\{(z_{1},\Psi_{\infty}((z_{1},y_{2})+iy_{2}):\|(z_{1},y_{2})\|=\frac{1}{2}(t_{2j+1}+t_{2j})\},

and so
tending to
\partial\Omega\setminus F
. Then, the proof of
[
BZ2
, Theorem 1.4]
shows that
p
is a visible point, so the set of non-visible points in
\partial\Omega
is not closed.
∎

Note that the set of non-Goldilocks points of
\partial\Omega
is closed by definition. Then the example above shows that failure of the local Goldilocks point conditions cannot describe the set of non-visible points, as the set of non-visible points is not necessarily closed.

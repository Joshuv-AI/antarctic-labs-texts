Abstract
In this work, the biquaternion Z transformation method is proposed to solve a class of biquaternion recurrence relations. Biqueternion Z transform is an natural extension of the complex Z transform. In the design process, special norm presentation is employed to analyze the region of convergence of the biquaternion geometry sequence. In addition, some useful properties have been given. It is shown that the proposed properties is helpful to understand the biquaternion Z transform. Finally, several examples have been given to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed design method.

2.1
Biquaternions

The concept of biquaternion, a generalization of complex numbers, was first proposed by Hamilton in 1853. In general, we adopt the symbol
\mathbb{H(C)}
to denote the set of biquaternion and
\mathbb{H(R)}
to denote the set of real quaternion. For each biquaternion
\mathbf{q}

\mathbf{q}=q_{0}+q_{1}\mathbf{i}+q_{2}\mathbf{j}+q_{3}\mathbf{k},

(1)

where
q_{n}\in\mathbb{C},n=0,1,2,3
.
The basis elements
\{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}\}
obey the Hamilton’s multiplication rules

\displaystyle\mathbf{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{j}^{2}=\mathbf{k}^{2}=\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}\mathbf{k}=-1;

(2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{k},\mathbf{j}\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{i},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{i}=\mathbf{j};

\displaystyle\mathbf{j}\mathbf{i}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{j}=-\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i}\mathbf{k}=-\mathbf{j}.

For any biquaternion
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{H(C)}
, we write
\mathbf{p}=p_{0}+p_{1}\mathbf{i}+p_{2}\mathbf{j}+p_{3}\mathbf{k}
,
\mathbf{q}=q_{0}+q_{1}\mathbf{i}+q_{2}\mathbf{j}+q_{3}\mathbf{k}
, where
p_{n},q_{n}\in\mathbb{C},n=0,1,2,3
. Let
\mathbf{p}=p_{0}+\underline{\mathbf{p}}
, we use

be the scalar part of
\mathbf{p}

\underline{\mathbf{p}}=:Vec(\mathbf{p})
be the vector part of
\mathbf{p}

\overline{\mathbf{p}}=:p_{0}-\underline{\mathbf{p}}

\mathbf{p}

Define

\mathbf{p}\mathbf{q}:=p_{0}q_{0}-(\underline{\mathbf{p}},\ \underline{\mathbf{q}})+p_{0}\underline{\mathbf{q}}+q_{0}\underline{\mathbf{p}}+\underline{\mathbf{p}}\times\underline{\mathbf{q}}

In
[
18

Let

\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}:=\mathbf{x}\overline{\mathbf{x}}=|\mathbf{a}|^{2}-|\mathbf{b}|^{2}+2I[a_{0}b_{0}+(\underline{\mathbf{a}},\ \underline{\mathbf{b}})],

(3)

where

|\mathbf{a}|^{2}:=\mathbf{a}\overline{\mathbf{a}}

Besides, Gürlebeck give the real-valued norm in

Before disscussing the definition of the Z transform in the

An infinite sequence

\mathbf{f}_{0},\mathbf{f}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{f}_{n},\cdots

is unique if it exists. When that limit exists, the sequence is said to converge to

(7)

Let
\mathbf{f}_{n}\in\mathbb{H(C)}
,
(n=1,2,\ldots)

,

{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}}\mathbf{f}_{n}=\mathbf{f}_{1}+\mathbf{f}_{2}+\cdots+\mathbf{f}_{n}+\cdots

{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}}\mathbf{f}_{n}=\mathbf{S}.

2.3
Biquaternion Z transform

In this section, we define the Z transform in the
\mathbb{H(C)}
domain. Firstly, we introduce the biquaternion geometric sequence as follows.

Lemma 2.1

Let
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{H(C)}
, we have

{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}}\mathbf{y}^{n}=(1-\mathbf{y})^{-1},\quad\|\mathbf{y}\|<1.

(13)

Proof.

Firstly, let
\mathbf{S}_{N}\in\mathbb{H(C)}
. We use
\mathbf{S}_{N}(\mathbf{y})
to denote the partial sums of
{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}}\mathbf{y}^{n}
, that is

\mathbf{S}_{N}(\mathbf{y})={\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}}\mathbf{y}^{n}=1+\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{y}^{2}+\cdots+\mathbf{y}^{N-1}.

(14)

Then we multiply Eq. (
14
) by
\mathbf{y}
on the left-hand side,

\mathbf{y}\mathbf{S}_{N}(\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{y}{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}}\mathbf{y}^{n}=\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{y}^{2}+\cdots+\mathbf{y}^{N}.

(15)

Using Eq. (
14
)-(
15
)

(1-\mathbf{y})\mathbf{S}_{N}(\mathbf{y})=1-\mathbf{y}^{N}.

(16)

Thus we know

\mathbf{S}_{N}(\mathbf{y})=(1-\mathbf{y})^{-1}(1-\mathbf{y}^{N}).

(17)

When
\|\mathbf{y}\|<1
,
(1-\mathbf{y})\neq 0
,
(1-\mathbf{y})^{-1}
is well defined. Define

\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{y}):=(1-\mathbf{y})^{-1},

(18)

using Eq. (
17
)-(
18
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\rho}_{N}(\mathbf{y}):=

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{y})-\mathbf{S}_{N}(\mathbf{y})

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\mathbf{y})^{-1}-(1-\mathbf{y})^{-1}(1-\mathbf{y}^{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\mathbf{y})^{-1}\mathbf{y}^{N}.

Thus

\|\mathbf{\rho}_{N}(\mathbf{y})\|=\|(1-\mathbf{y})^{-1}\mathbf{y}^{N}\|=\|(1-\mathbf{y})^{-1}\|\|\mathbf{y}\|^{N},

(20)

and it is clear from this that the remainders
\rho_{N}(\mathbf{y})
tends to zero when
\|\mathbf{y}\|<1
but not when
\|\mathbf{y}\|\geq 1
. Therefore, the proof of equation (

Definition 2.1

(Biquaternion Z transform)
Let
\mathbf{f}=\{\mathbf{f}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
,
\mathbf{f}_{n},\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{H(C)}
.
The Z transform
\mathcal{X}[\mathbf{f}](\mathbf{x})
of
\mathbf{f}
is defined by

\mathcal{X}[\mathbf{f}](\mathbf{x}):=\mathbf{f}_{0}+\mathbf{f}_{1}{\mathbf{x}}^{-1}+\cdots+\mathbf{f}_{n}{\mathbf{x}}^{-n}+\cdots={\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}}\mathbf{f}_{n}{\mathbf{x}}^{-n}.

(21)

The region of convergence (ROC) is the set of all
\mathbf{x}
values that make the defined sequence converge, that is
\|\mathbf{x}\|>\sigma_{f}
. Among them,
\sigma_{f}~(0\leq{\|\sigma_{f}\|}<\infty)
is defined as the radius of convergence of the Z transform of
\mathbf{f}
.

Properties

Similar to complex Z transform, the biquaternion Z transform has many useful properties. First of all, the following theorem gives some calculation rules for Z transformation.
For the sake of simplicity, we have introduced some symbols for numerical sequence operations.
Here,
\sigma_{f}

{x}\in\mathbb{C}

Remark 4.1

The solving process of the right-side coefficient linear homogeneous biquaternion recurrence relations is as follows:

Step 1
. Apply the biquaternion z transform (
24
) on both sides of the given equation.

Step 2
. Use the basic properties in Theorem
3.1
to simplify the result of step 1.

Step 3
. Using quaternion algebra, the result of step 2 is reduced to a monadic equation about the z transformation of the sequence.

Step 4
. The biquaternion sequence is obtained by comparing Table
2
and step 3.

The three homogeneous biquaternion recurrence relations with right-side coefficients have been solved by the biquaternion z transformation method. In the following, let’s focus on the inhomogeneous biquaternion recurrence relations with the right-side coefficients.

Conclusion

The solution of a class of biquaternion recurrence relations has been solved in this paper. After introducing the z transformation of the biquaternion sequence, we give its basic properties.

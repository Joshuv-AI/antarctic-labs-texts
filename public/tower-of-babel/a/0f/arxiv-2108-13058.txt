Abstract
We address some fundamental questions about geometric analysis on Riemannian manifolds. The L p L^{p} -Calderón-Zygmund inequality is one of the cornerstones in the regularity theory of elliptic equations, and it has been asked under which geometric conditions it holds for a reasonable class of non-compact Riemannian manifolds, and to what extent assumptions on the derivative of curvature and on the injectivity radius of the manifold are necessary. In the present paper, for 1 < p < 2 1<p<2 , we give a positive answer for the validity of the L p L^{p} -Calderón-Zygmund inequality on a Riemannian manifold assuming only a lower bound on the Ricci curvature. It is well known that this alone is not sufficient for p > 2 p>2 . In this case we complement the study of Güneysu-Pigola (2015) and derive sufficient geometric criteria for the validity of the Calderón-Zygmund inequality under additional Kato class bounds on the Riemann curvature tensor and the covariant derivative of Ricci curvature. Bounds in the Kato class are integral conditions and much weaker than pointwise bounds. Throughout the proofs, probabilistic tools, like Hessian formulas and Bismut type representations for heat semigroups, play a significant role.

Introduction

The Hessian operator
\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits
, which contains all information of the second order derivatives, is a fundamental object in the second order smooth analysis.
To study the Hessian operator, one usually needs
the Calderón-Zygmund inequality that controls

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits
by the simpler Laplacian operator
\Delta

(see
[
6
,
20
,
35
,
26
,
36
]
and the references therein for its wide applications particularly in the regularity theory of elliptic equations).

In the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Calderón-Zygmund inequality says that
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
and

, it holds

\displaystyle\|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|\Delta u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(1.1)

where

is a constant depending only on

and

The inequality (

via their seminal
theory of singular integral operators based on the explicit
representation of the Green kernel of the Laplacian.
A remarkable consequence of inequality (

in the Euclidean space, the Sobolev norms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

The Calderón-Zygmund inequality (

\displaystyle\|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C\Big(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|Lu\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\Big),

sectional curvature where

fails for large values of

To begin with, we make some conventions on the notation. Throughout

If

is a similar potential theoretical approach that
represents

To overcome this drawback, Güneysu and Pigola
[

,

introduced two methods of proof for

avoiding the use of

has bounded Ricci curvature and a

that the Calderón-Zygmund

Let us sketch the main idea of the second method in

\displaystyle\left\|\,|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits(\Delta+\sigma)^{-1}u|\,\right\|_{p}\leq C\,\|u\|_{p},

\displaystyle\left\|\,|\nabla(\Delta_{1}+\sigma)^{-1/2}\circ d(\Delta_{0}+\sigma)^{-1/2}u|\,\right\|_{p}\leq C\,\|u\|_{p}.

(1.3)

Here and hereafter, we write
\|\cdot\|_{p}:=\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(M)}
for simplicity.
The problem is thus reduced to the study of conditions for boundedness

however is restricted to

; the constants

depend on dimension

,

,
\|R\|_{\infty}
,

and on the constants

,

such that

\displaystyle V(x,tr)\leq Ct^{d}{\rm e}^{Ctr}V(x,r)

(
LD)

for all

,

, where

.

Very recently, Baumgarth, Devyver and Güneysu
[
4

studied the covariant Riesz transform on

-forms. Their results can

)

but without the local volume doubling

. This comes from the fact that

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits\geq-K
for some

can be seen by the Bishop-Gromov comparison theorem and the well-known

seems difficult to establish

p
)
for

in this way, as
when trying to extend the machinery of
[
3
]
to

-boundedness of covariant Riesz transform for

Poincaré inequality, used explicitly in
[
3
]
, does not

The observations above raise the following questions:

1.

In the case
1<p<2
, is it possible to weaken the
assumptions on the Riemann curvature tensor
\|R\|_{\infty}
and

\|\nabla R\|_{\infty}
, e.g., replacing them by Ricci curvature
bounds?

2.

Without a lower control on the injectivity radius, under
which conditions on the manifold
M
, the inequality
CZ(
p
)

holds on
M
for
p>2
?

In order to answer the above two questions affirmatively, we develop a
new functional analytic method of proof for
CZ
(p)
that works
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
. Unlike the second method used in

[
26
,
36
]
that reduces
CZ(
p
)
to the boundedness

), our method makes use of the observation that

CZ(
p
)
is equivalent to the

-boundedness of the following

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits(\Delta+\sigma)^{-1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\sigma t}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}\,dt,

where

denotes the heat semigroup generated by
-\Delta
. Based on this observation, our strategy
to solve the above two questions is first establishing some Hessian heat kernel estimates and then bridging from Hessian heat kernel estimates to
CZ(
p
)
.

However, references on Hessian heat kernel estimates are sparsely to find in the literature,
especially when compared to the situation of heat kernel estimates and
gradient estimates for the heat kernel. Therefore, in the following section, we invest some
effort in deriving sharp Hessian heat kernel estimates first.
It turns out that there is a big difference
between the treatment of the cases
p<2
and
p>2
.
In particular, if
p\in(1,2)
, we only assume that

satisfies the following curvature condition:

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits\geq-K

(

for some

) then give rise to the local Gaussian upper bounds of the heat kernel and the local volume doubling condition

which enable us to deduce a series of

weighted off-diagonal estimates of the Hessian heat kernel (see Section

[

]
) allow to derive our first main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold satisfying
(

). Let

be fixed. Then there exists a constant

such that the operator
\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits(\Delta+\sigma)^{-1}

p
)
holds.

Theorem
1.1
answers question
1
affirmatively. Comparing Theorem
1.1
with existing results on

p
)
,
it should be pointed out first that the result is valid without any

and

, only a lower bound of

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits

For the case

that the curvature condition (

To formulate appropriate geometric conditions, we introduce some probabilistic quantities.
Denote by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{x}[f(X_{t})]=P_{t}f(x)=({\rm e}^{-\Delta t}f)(x).

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{x\in M}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}^{x}\big[|K(X_{s})|\big]\,ds=0.

The Kato class has been introduced in

Concerning criteria for functions to be in the Kato class the reader

To deal with the case

Condition (H)

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits\geq-K

\displaystyle|R|^{2}(x)\leq K_{1}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\big|\nabla\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp}+d^{*}R\big|^{2}(x)\leq K_{2}(x),\quad x\in M,

where for

\displaystyle|R|(x)=\sup\Big\{|R^{\#,\#}(v_{1},v_{2})|_{\mathop{\scriptscriptstyle\rm HS}\nolimits}(x)\colon\ v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{x}M,\ |v_{1}|\leq 1,\ |v_{2}|\leq 1\Big\},

and

\displaystyle\langle d^{*}R(v_{1},v_{2}),v_{3}\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{v_{3}}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp})(v_{1}),v_{2}\rangle-\langle(\nabla_{v_{2}}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp})(v_{3}),v_{1}\rangle,

with

Under the condition

, we are able to prove the

\displaystyle t\,|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f|(x)\leq C(1+\sqrt{t})\,{\rm e}^{(2K+\theta)t}\left(P_{t}|f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}(x)

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

be a complete Riemannian manifold satisfying
(

). Let

be fixed. Then there exists a constant

such that the operator
\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits\,(\Delta+\sigma)^{-1}

) and

\displaystyle|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f|\leq C{\rm e}^{c_{1}t}P_{c_{2}t}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits f|

2
Hessian heat kernel estimates

This section is divided into two parts: the first part is on

L^{2}
-estimates of the Hessian of heat kernels under the assumption
of a lower Ricci curvature bound; the second part is on the estimates
derived under condition
(H)
from the Hessian formula and Bismut
type formulas for the Hessian of semigroups, which are established by
means of the techniques from stochastic analysis.

2.1
L^{2}
-estimates for the Hessian of heat kernel

In this section, we assume Ricci curvature to be bounded below,
i.e. validity of condition (
Ric)
. Then, in particular, the
local doubling assumption (
LD
) with respect to
\mu
holds.
Then for
x
and
y\in M
, we obviously have

B(y,\!\sqrt{t})\subset B(x,\sqrt{t}+\rho(x,y))
. Thus if
(
LD
) holds, then

\displaystyle V(y,\!\sqrt{t})\leq V\Big(x,\sqrt{t}+\rho(x,y)\Big)\leq C\Big(1+\frac{\rho(x,y)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{d}\exp\left(C(\sqrt{t}+\rho(x,y))\right)V(x,\sqrt{t}).

(2.1)

It is well-known that, under a lower Ricci curvature bound, the heat
kernel allows an off-diagonal estimate
[
15
]
. The following
lemma gives a pointwise off-diagonal estimate for the heat kernel and
its time derivative.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that (
Ric
) holds. Then for any

\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, there exist constants
C
and
C_{1}>0

depending on the dimension
d
and
\alpha
such that for all

x,y\in M
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)+\left|\frac{\partial p_{t}}{\partial t}(x,y)\right|\leq\frac{C}{V(y,\!\sqrt{t})}\exp\left(-\alpha\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}+C_{1}Kt\right).

(2.2)

Proof.

The estimate of
p_{t}(x,y)
is an easy consequence of
(
2.1
) and
[
15
, Theorem 2]
or
[
42
, Theorem
2.4.4]
, where it is proved that for all
x,y\in M
and

,

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)\leq\frac{C}{V(y,\!\sqrt{t})}\exp\left(-\alpha\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}+C_{1}Kt\right).

(2.3)

The estimate for
|\frac{\partial p_{t}}{\partial t}(x,y)|
follows
from that of
p_{t}(x,y)
and the analytic property of the semigroup
(see
[

, Theorem 4]
or

).
∎

The following lemma gives weighted
L^{2}
-integral estimates for the
heat kernel, its gradient and its Laplacian.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that (
Ric
) holds. Let
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
be
as in Lemma
2.1
. For all

\gamma\in(0,2\alpha)
,
s>0
and
y\in M
,

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left[|p_{s}(x,y)|^{2}+s|\nabla_{x}p_{s}(x,y)|^{2}+s^{2}|\Delta_{x}p_{s}(x,y)|^{2}\right]{\rm e}^{\gamma\,\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{s}}\,\mu(dx)\leq\frac{C_{\gamma}}{V(y,\!\sqrt{s})}\,{\rm e}^{2\,C^{\prime}s},

where
C_{\gamma}>0
depends on
\gamma
and
C^{\prime}>0
on
\alpha
and

K
.

Proof.

By (
LD
), it is easy to see that for all
\gamma>0
,

s,t>0
and
y\in M
, there exist two positive constants

C_{\gamma}
(depending on
\gamma
and the constants in
(
LD
)) and
\tilde{C}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\rho(x,y)\geq\sqrt{t}}{\rm e}^{-2\gamma\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{s}}\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}\int_{M}{\rm e}^{-\gamma\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{s}}\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}V(y,(i+1)\sqrt{s}){\rm e}^{-\gamma i^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}V(y,\!\sqrt{s})\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(i+1)^{d}{\rm e}^{-\gamma i^{2}}{\rm e}^{C(i+1)\sqrt{s}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}{\rm e}^{C\sqrt{s}}V(y,\!\sqrt{s})\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(i+1)^{d}{\rm e}^{-\gamma i^{2}}{\rm e}^{\gamma i^{2}/2+C^{2}s/2}

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}{\rm e}^{C\sqrt{s}+C^{2}s/2}V(y,\!\sqrt{s})\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(i+1)^{d}{\rm e}^{-\gamma i^{2}/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma}V(y,\!\sqrt{s})\,{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}{\rm e}^{\tilde{C}s},

(2.4)

where the second inequality comes from condition (

). The
remainder of the proof follows from
[

, Lemmas 2.1-2.3]
.
∎

We now turn to the estimates for the Hessian of heat kernel. The
following lemma shows that the Hessian of heat semigroup also
satisfies an
L^{2}
-Gaffney off-diagonal estimate. Note that in the
following discussion the constant
C
will be different in different
lines without confusion.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that (
Ric
) holds. There exist constants
C,C_{2}>0

such that for all
t\in(0,\infty)
, all Borel subsets
E,F\subset M

with compact closure, and all
f\in L^{2}(M)
with
supp

f\subset E
,

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbbm{1}_{F}t\,|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f|\big\|_{2}\leq C(1+\sqrt{t})\,\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\,\rho^{2}(E,F)}{t}\right)\|f\|_{2}.

Proof.

Recall the following
L^{2}
-Gaffney off-diagonal estimate on
one-forms
[
4
]
. If (
Ric
) holds, then for

\alpha\in\Gamma_{L^{2}}(T^{*}M)
with support

{\rm supp}(\alpha)\subset E
and any
s\in(0,1)
, it holds

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbbm{1}_{F}\!\sqrt{s}\,|\nabla{\rm e}^{-s\Delta^{(1)}}\alpha|\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\,(1+\sqrt{s})\,\exp\left(-\frac{c_{1}\,\rho^{2}(E,F)}{s}\right)\|\mathbbm{1}_{E}\alpha\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2C\exp\left(-\frac{c_{1}\rho^{2}(E,F)}{s}\right)\|\mathbbm{1}_{E}\alpha\|_{2}

for some positive constants
C
and
c_{1}
. On the other hand, we
have Gaffney’s off-diagonal estimate for
|\nabla P_{t_{2}}f|
(see

[
3
, (3.1)]
), i.e. for all
f\in L^{2}(M)
with support
in
E
and
u>0
,

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbbm{1}_{F}\!\sqrt{u}\,|\nabla P_{u}f|\big\|_{2}\leq C\exp\left(-\frac{c_{2}\rho^{2}(E,F)}{u}\right)\|\mathbbm{1}_{E}f\|_{2}

for some positive constants
C
and
c_{2}
. For
t>0
, denoting by

\Delta^{(1)}
the Laplacian on one-forms, we may write

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f=\frac{\sqrt{\left(t-(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1)\right)}\,\nabla{\rm e}^{-\big(t-(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1)\big)\Delta^{(1)}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1}\,dP_{\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1}f\right)}{\sqrt{\left(t-(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1)\right)\big(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1\big)}}

so that using the composition rule of Gaffney’s off-diagonal estimate
(see
[
29
, Lemma 2.3]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbbm{1}_{E}t\,|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f|\big\|_{2}\leq C\frac{t}{\sqrt{\left(t-(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1)\right)(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1)}}\exp\left(-\frac{c\,\rho^{2}(E,F)}{\max\left\{\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1,t-(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1)\right\}}\right)\|\mathbbm{1}_{E}f\|_{2},

for some positive constants
C
and
c
. Note that for
0<t/2<1
,

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1,t-\big(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1\big)\right\}=\max\left\{\frac{t}{2},t-\frac{t}{2}\right\}=\frac{t}{2};\quad\frac{t}{\sqrt{\left(t-\big(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1\big)\right)(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1)}}=2,

and that for
t/2\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1,t-\big(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1\big)\right\}=\max\big\{1,t-1\big\}=t-1\leq t;\quad\frac{t}{\sqrt{\left(t-\big(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1\big)\right)(\frac{t}{2}\wedge 1)}}=\frac{t}{\sqrt{t-1}}\leq\sqrt{2t}.

Therefore, we conclude that there exist positive constants
C
and

C_{2}
such that

\big\|\mathbbm{1}_{F}t\,|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f|\big\|_{2}\leq C\,(1+\sqrt{t})\,\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\rho^{2}(E,F)}{t}\right)\|\mathbbm{1}_{E}f\|_{2}.\qed

The following proposition gives
L^{2}
-weighted estimates for the
Hessian of the heat kernel.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that (
Ric
) holds. Fix
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
as
in Lemma
2.1
. Then for all

\gamma\in(0,2\alpha)
,
s>0
and
y\in M
, there exists a constant

C>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits_{x}p_{s}(x,y)|^{2}\,\exp\left(\gamma\,\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{s}\right)\,\mu(dx)\leq\frac{C(1+Ks)\,{\rm e}^{2C^{\prime}s}}{s^{2}V(y,\!\sqrt{s})}

where
C^{\prime}>0
is the same constant as in Lemma
2.2
.

Proof.

We begin by integrating Bochner’s identity (
4.1
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\int|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\,\Delta{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}(\Delta|\nabla p_{s}|^{2})\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(-|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits p_{s}|^{2}_{\mathop{\scriptscriptstyle\rm HS}\nolimits}+g(\nabla\Delta p_{s},\nabla p_{s})-\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits(\nabla p_{s},\nabla p_{s})\right){\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx),

which then implies

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits p_{s}|_{\mathop{\scriptscriptstyle\rm HS}\nolimits}^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)

(2.5)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\Delta{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)+\int_{M}g(\nabla\Delta p_{s},\nabla p_{s})\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{M}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits(\nabla p_{s},\nabla p_{s})\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma}{s}\int_{M}|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm div}}{\text{\rm div}}{\text{\rm div}}{\text{\rm div}}}\nolimits\left({\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\rho(x,y){\nabla}\rho(\cdot,y)(x)\right)\,\mu(dx)+\int\Delta p_{s}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm div}}{\text{\rm div}}{\text{\rm div}}{\text{\rm div}}}\nolimits({\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\nabla p_{s})\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\quad-\int\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits(\nabla p_{s},\nabla p_{s})\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)=:\mathrm{I}_{1}+\mathrm{I}_{2}+\mathrm{I}_{3}

(with the sign convention
\Delta=\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm div}}{\text{\rm div}}{\text{\rm div}}{\text{\rm div}}}\nolimits\nabla
for the
divergence). Now for any
\beta\in(0,2\alpha)
, let

\displaystyle E(s,y,\beta):=\int_{M}\left[|p_{s}(x,y)|^{2}+s|\nabla_{x}p_{s}(x,y)|^{2}+s^{2}|\Delta_{x}p_{s}(x,y)|^{2}|\right]{\rm e}^{\beta\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx).

Then by condition (
Ric
) and Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{3}

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{M}|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)\leq\frac{K}{s}E(s,y,{\gamma})\leq\frac{K}{s}\frac{C_{\gamma}}{V(y,\!\sqrt{s})}\,{\rm e}^{2C^{\prime}s}.

(2.6)

For
\mathrm{I}_{2}
, using the fact
|\nabla\rho|\leq 1
, we deduce
from Lemma
2.2
again that

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(\Delta p_{s})^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)-\frac{2\gamma}{s}\int_{M}(\Delta p_{s})\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\rho(x,y)\langle\nabla\rho,\nabla p_{s}\rangle\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{M}(\Delta p_{s})^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{s^{2}}\int_{M}{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\rho^{2}(x,y)|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\gamma}}{s^{2}}E(s,y,{\gamma}^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{s^{2}}\frac{C_{\gamma^{\prime}}}{V(y,\!\sqrt{s})}\,{\rm e}^{2C^{\prime}s},

(2.7)

where
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}<2\alpha
with
\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma
sufficiently
small.

To bound
\mathrm{I}_{1}
, we write

\displaystyle\frac{s}{\gamma}\mathrm{I}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)+2\int_{M}|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\frac{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{s}\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{M}|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\rho(x,y)\Delta\rho(\cdot,y)(x)\,\mu(dx).

(2.8)

By the Laplacian comparison theorem (see e.g.
[
8
, p185]
),
we have

\displaystyle-\Delta\rho(\cdot,y)(x)\leq\frac{d-1}{\rho(x,y)}+K\rho(x,y)

outside of the cut-locus. This yields in particular

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{s}\int_{M}|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\rho(x,y)\Delta\rho(\cdot,y)(x)\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(d-1)\gamma}{s}\int_{M}|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)+K\int_{M}|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\frac{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{s}\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(d-1)\gamma}{s}\int_{M}|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)+K\int_{M}|\nabla p_{s}|^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\frac{\gamma^{\prime}\rho^{2}(x,y)}{s}}\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\gamma}(1+Ks)}{s^{2}}E(s,y,{\gamma}^{\prime})

with
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}<2\alpha
as in (
2.1
). Combining this
estimate with (
2.1
), it follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{1}\leq C_{\gamma}\frac{(1+Ks)}{s^{2}}E(s,y,{\gamma}^{\prime})\leq\frac{c(1+Ks)}{s^{2}\sqrt{V(y,\!\sqrt{s})}}\,{\rm e}^{2C^{\prime}s}.

(2.9)

Altogether (
2.5
) through (
2.9
), we
conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits p_{s}|_{\mathop{\scriptscriptstyle\rm HS}\nolimits}^{2}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)=\mathrm{I}_{1}+\mathrm{I}_{2}+\mathrm{I}_{3}\leq c\frac{(1+Ks)}{s^{2}\sqrt{V(y,\!\sqrt{s})}}\,{\rm e}^{2C^{\prime}s}.

which completes the proof of Proposition
2.4
by using the
fact

|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits p_{s}|^{2}\leq|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits p_{s}|^{2}_{\mathop{\scriptscriptstyle\rm HS}\nolimits}.\qed

Using Proposition
2.4
, we obtain the following

L^{2}
-integral estimates for Hessian of heat kernel.

Corollary 2.5
.

Assume (
Ric
) holds. Fix
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
as in
Lemma
2.1
. There exist
0<\beta<\alpha
and

C^{\prime\prime}>C^{\prime}>0
with
C^{\prime}
as in Lemma
2.2
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\rho(x,y)\geq t^{1/2}}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits_{x}p_{s}(x,y)|\,\mu(dx)\leq C\big(1+\sqrt{s}\big)\,{\rm e}^{C^{\prime\prime}s}\,{\rm e}^{-\beta t/s}s^{-1}

for all
y\in M
and
s,t>0
.

Proof.

Let
0<\beta<\alpha
. By Cauchy’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\rho(x,y)\geq t^{1/2}}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits_{x}p_{s}(x,y)|\,\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits_{x}p_{s}(x,y)|^{2}\,{\rm e}^{2\beta\,\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\rho(x,y)\geq t^{1/2}}{\rm e}^{-2\beta\,\rho^{2}(x,y)/s}\,\mu(dx)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\,{\rm e}^{C^{\prime}s}(1+\sqrt{s})}{s\sqrt{V(y,\!\sqrt{s})}}\sqrt{V(y,\!\sqrt{s})}\,{\rm e}^{-\beta t/s}\,{\rm e}^{\tilde{C}s}

\displaystyle=\frac{C(1+\sqrt{s})}{s}\,{\rm e}^{C^{\prime}s-\beta t/s}\,{\rm e}^{\tilde{C}s}\leq\frac{C(1+\sqrt{s})}{s}\,{\rm e}^{C^{\prime\prime}s-\beta t/s}

where the second inequality follows from Proposition
2.4

and inequality (
2.1
). This finishes the proof.
∎

2.2

Hessian of heat kernel

In this subsection, we establish some pointwise and
L^{p}
-integral
estimates for the Hessian of the heat kernel when

. To this end, let
us first introduce some necessary notations. For fixed
x\in M
, let

B_{t}
be the stochastic anti-development of
X.(x)
which is a
Brownian motion in
T_{x}M
. Let
/\!/_{t}\colon T_{x}M\to T_{X_{t}(x)}M

be the parallel transport and

\displaystyle DQ_{t}=-\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp}Q_{t}\,dt,\quad Q_{0}={\rm id}_{T_{x}M}

\displaystyle W_{t}(\cdot,w)=Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}R(/\!/_{r}\,dB_{r},Q_{r}(\cdot))Q_{r}(w)-Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}(\nabla\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp}+d^{*}R)(Q_{r}(\cdot),Q_{r}(w))\,dr.

\left\{\begin{aligned} DW_{t}(\cdot,w)&=R(/\!/_{t}dB_{t},Q_{t}(\cdot))Q_{t}(w)-(d^{*}R+\nabla\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp})(Q_{t}(\cdot),Q_{t}(w))\,dt-\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp}(W_{t}(\cdot,w))\,dt,\\

First, we collect some easy curvature estimates for Riemannian

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume that (

) holds. There exist constants

,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|R|^{2}(X_{s})\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp}+d^{*}R|^{2}(X_{s})\,ds\right]\leq C{\rm e}^{2\theta t}.

(2.11)

Proof.

By means of the trivial inequality
s\leq{\rm e}^{s}
for

, we see
that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|R|^{2}(X_{s})\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp}+d^{*}R|^{2}(X_{s})\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(X_{s})\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(X_{s})\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(X_{s})\,ds\right)\right]+\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(X_{s})\,ds\right)\right].

Recall in
[

, Lemma 3.9]
that for any

, there exist positive constants

and

such

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}|K(X_{s})|\,ds\right)\right]\leq C{\rm e}^{ct}.

This immediately completes the proof by substituting
K_{1}
and
K_{2}

as

.
∎

By this lemma and Bismut-type Hessian formula, we obtain the following

Proposition 2.7
.

Assume that (

) holds. Then, for any

, there

and

\displaystyle t\,|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f|(x)\leq C(1+\sqrt{t})\,{\rm e}^{(2K+\theta)t}\left(P_{t}|f|^{p}\right)^{1/p}(x);

(2.12)

\displaystyle t\,\big\||\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f|\,\big\|_{p}\leq C(1+\sqrt{t})\,{\rm e}^{(2K+\theta)t}\|f\|_{p}.

(2.13)

Proof.

We start by recalling the following Bismut type formula for

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}
(see
[

,

,
9
]
). Let

from

for

,

,

for

Then, for

, we have

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f))(v,w)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[f(X_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\langle W_{s}(\dot{k}_{s}v,w),/\!/_{s}\,dB_{s}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[f(X_{t})\int_{s}^{t}\langle Q_{r}(\dot{\ell}_{r}w),/\!/_{r}\,dB_{r}\rangle\int_{0}^{s}\langle Q_{r}(\dot{k}_{r}v),/\!/_{r}\,dB_{r}\rangle\right].

\displaystyle d|W_{s}(v,w)|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\,\langle R(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(v))Q_{s}(w),W_{s}(v,w)\rangle+2|R^{\sharp,\sharp}(Q_{s}(v),Q_{s}(w))|_{\rm HS}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad-2\,\langle(d^{*}R+\nabla\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp})(Q_{s}(v),Q_{s}(w)),W_{s}(v,w)\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle\quad-2\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits(W_{s}(v,w),W_{s}(v,w))\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\langle R(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(v))Q_{s}(w),W_{s}(v,w)\rangle+2{\rm e}^{4Ks}K_{1}(X_{s})\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+2\sqrt{K_{2}(X_{s})}\,{\rm e}^{2Ks}|W_{s}(v,w)|\,ds+2K|W_{s}(v,w)|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\langle R(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(v))Q_{s}(w),W_{s}(v,w)\rangle+2{\rm e}^{4Ks}K_{1}(X_{s})\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+(2\delta)^{-1}{\rm e}^{4Ks}K_{2}(X_{s})\,ds+(2K+\delta)|W_{s}(v,w)|^{2}\,ds.

From this and Lemma
2.6
there exist constants
C>0
and

\theta>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|W_{t\wedge\tau_{D}}(v,w)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2{\rm e}^{(2K+\delta)t}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{(2K-\delta)s}K_{1}(X_{s})\,ds\right]+(4\delta)^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{(2K-\delta)s}K_{2}(X_{s})\,ds\right]\right)

(2.15)

\displaystyle\leq 2\max\{1,(4\delta)^{-1}\}{\rm e}^{4Kt}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(X_{s})\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(X_{s})\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\,{\rm e}^{(4K+2\theta)t}.

Since the upper of
\mathbb{E}|W_{t\wedge\tau_{D}}(v,w)|^{2}
is independent
of the domain
D
, taking a sequence of domains
D_{n}
increasing to

M
, we conclude that

\mathbb{E}|W_{t}(v,w)|^{2}\leq C\,{\rm e}^{(4K+2\theta)t}.
All these estimates
allow to verify that formula (
2.14
) holds for the
deterministic functions
k_{s}=\frac{t-2s}{t}\vee 0
and

\ell_{s}=1\wedge\frac{2(t-s)}{t}
as well. Then, by estimate
(
2.15
) and
|Q_{s}|\leq{\rm e}^{Ks}
, we get for any
p\geq 2
,

\displaystyle|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{t}(P_{t}|f|^{p})^{1/p}\left[\int_{0}^{t/2}\mathbb{E}|W_{s}(v,w)|^{2}\,ds\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{t^{2}}(P_{t}|f|^{p})^{1/p}\mathbb{E}\left[\Big(\int_{0}^{t/2}\langle Q_{s}(w),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\Big)^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{t/2}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\Big)^{2}\Big|\mathscr{F}_{t/2}\Big]\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{t}(P_{t}|f|^{p})^{1/p}\left(\int_{0}^{t/2}{\rm e}^{(4K+2\theta)s}\,ds\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{t^{2}}(P_{t}|f|^{p})^{1/p}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t/2}{\rm e}^{2Ks}\,ds\right]^{1/2}\left(\int_{t/2}^{t}{\rm e}^{2Ks}\,ds\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\,{\rm e}^{(2K+\theta)t}(P_{t}|f|^{p})^{1/p}+\frac{C}{t}\,{\rm e}^{2Kt}(P_{t}|f|^{p})^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(1+\sqrt{t})}{t}\,{\rm e}^{(2K+\theta)t}(P_{t}|f|^{p})^{1/p}

(2.16)

which proves (
2.12
). Moreover, integration with respect to
the measure
\mu
yields

\displaystyle\mu\big(|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f|^{p}\big)^{1/p}\leq\frac{C(1+\sqrt{t})}{t}\,{\rm e}^{(2K+\theta)t}\mu(|f|^{p})^{1/p}.

This proves (
2.13
) and finishes the proof.
∎

The following proposition gives a pointwise estimate for the Hessian
of heat semigroup.

Proposition 2.8
.

Assume that (
H
) holds. Then there exist constants

C,\theta>0
such that

\displaystyle|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f|\leq{\rm e}^{2Kt}\left(P_{t}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C{\rm e}^{(2K+\theta)t}\,\left(P_{t}|df|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(2.17)

for
t>0
and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
.

Proof.

Recall the following relation

\displaystyle d\Delta f=\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits(df)-df(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp})

(2.18)

where
\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits(df)=\nabla^{2}df
.
Moreover, using the differential Bianchi identity

[
10
, p. 185]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits(\Delta f)=\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits f)-2(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits f)(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp}\otimes{\rm id}-R^{\#,\#})-df(d^{*}R+\nabla\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{\sharp}).

(2.19)

Now, for
s>0
and
v,w\in T_{x}M
, let

\displaystyle N_{s}(v,w):=(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t-s}f)(Q_{s}(v),Q_{s}(w))(X_{s})+(dP_{t-s}f)(W_{s}(v,w))(X_{s}).

We claim that
N_{s}
is a local martingale. Combining
(
2.18
), (
2.19
) and applying Itô’s formula, we
obtain

\displaystyle dN_{s}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\big(\nabla_{/\!/_{s}dB_{s}}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t-s}f\big)(Q_{s}(v),Q_{s}(w))+(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t-s}f)(\frac{D}{ds}Q_{s}(v),Q_{s}(w))\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t-s}f)(Q_{s}(v),\frac{D}{ds}Q_{s}(w))\,ds+\partial_{s}(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t-s}f)(Q_{s}(v),Q_{s}(w))\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t-s}f)(Q_{s}(v),Q_{s}(w))\,ds+(\nabla_{/\!/_{s}dB_{s}}dP_{t-s}f)(W_{s}(v,w))

\displaystyle\quad+(dP_{t-s}f)(DW_{s}(v,w))+\langle d(dP_{t-s}f),DW_{s}(v,w)\rangle+\partial_{s}(dP_{t-s}f)(W_{s}(v,w))\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}{\text{\rm tr}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits(dP_{t-s}f)(W_{s}(v,w))\,ds\overset{\text{\tiny m}}{=}0,

where
\overset{\text{\tiny m}}{=}
denotes equality modulo the differential of a local
martingale, and here we used for the quadratic covariation the
formula

\displaystyle[d(dP_{t-s}f),DW_{s}(v,w)]_{s}=(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t-s}f)(R^{\#,\#}(Q_{s}(v),Q_{s}(w)))\,ds.

Therefore,
N_{s}
is a local martingale. Recall that condition

\bf(H)
implies

\displaystyle|Q_{t}|^{2}\leq{\rm e}^{2Kt}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}|W_{t}(v,w)|^{2}\leq C{\rm e}^{(4K+2\theta)t}

for some positive constants. In view of estimate
(
2.2
),
|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t-s}f|
is bounded on

[0,t-\varepsilon]\times M
, and

|\nabla P_{t-s}f|\leq{\rm e}^{K(t-s)}\,\|\,|\nabla f|\,\|_{\infty}

as a consequence of the derivative formula (see e.g.
[
42
, Theorem 2.2.3]
):
for all
v\in T_{x}M
and
x\in M
,

\displaystyle\langle(\nabla P_{t}f)(x),v\rangle=\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\langle(\nabla f)(X_{t}),Q_{t}v\rangle\right].

(2.20)

Thus
N_{s}
is a martingale on the time
interval
[0,t-\varepsilon]
, and by taking expectation at time
0

and
t-\varepsilon
, we arrive at

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f)(v,w)=\mathbb{E}\Big[(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{\varepsilon}f)(Q_{t-\varepsilon}(v),Q_{t-\varepsilon}(w))(X_{t})+dP_{\varepsilon}f(W_{t-\varepsilon}(v,w))(X_{t-\varepsilon})\Big].

(2.21)

Letting
\varepsilon
tend to
0
, it then follows that

\displaystyle|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}f(v,w)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits f(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))(X_{t})\Big]\Big|+\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[df(W_{t}(v,w))(X_{t})\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq P_{t}\Big[|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits f|\,|Q_{t}(v)|\,|Q_{t}(w)|\Big]+(P_{t}|df|^{2})^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\Big[|W_{t}(v,w)|^{2}\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq(P_{t}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits f|^{2})^{1/2}\,{\rm e}^{2Kt}+C\,{\rm e}^{(2K+\theta)t}\,(P_{t}|df|^{2})^{1/2}.\qed

.

Proposition 2.10
.

Assume that (

) holds. Fix

\displaystyle|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits_{x}p_{t}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(1+\sqrt{t})}{tV(y,\!\sqrt{t})}\exp\left(-\beta\,\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}+\frac{1}{2}\big(C_{3}+\theta\big)t\right).

For

and

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits_{x}p_{2t}(x,y)=\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{t}(p_{t}(\cdot,y))(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B\cap 4B_{j})}\int_{B\cap 4B_{j}}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{kr^{2}}h|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{kr^{2}}h|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

We write

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{kr^{2}}h=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{kr^{2}}(h\mathbbm{1}_{C_{i}})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{kr^{2}}g_{i},

where

and
C_{i}
is as in (
3.32
). Next,
we distinguish the two regimes
i\leq i_{r}
and
i>i_{r}
where

is defined as in (
3.31
). In the regime
i\leq i_{r}
, by the

3.35
) in Lemma

, we
have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{kr^{2}}g_{i}|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\alpha_{1}4^{i}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{\mu(2^{i+1}B)}\int_{C_{i}}|\nabla h|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}+\left(\frac{1}{\mu(2^{i+1}B)}\int_{C_{i}}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits\,h|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\alpha_{1}4^{i}}\left(\left(\mathscr{M}_{\rm loc}(|\nabla h|^{2})(x)\right)^{1/2}+\left(\frac{1}{\mu(2^{i+1}B)}\int_{C_{i}}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits\,h|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}\right).

On the other hand, since
C_{i}\subset 2^{i+1}B
, we know

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(2^{i+1}B)}\int_{C_{i}}\big|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits\,h\big|^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(2^{i+1}B\cap 4B_{j})}\int_{(2^{i+1}B)\cap 4B_{j}}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits\,h|^{2}d\mu+\frac{1}{\mu(2^{i+1}B)}\int_{2^{i+1}B}\mathbbm{1}_{M\setminus 4B_{j}}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits\,h|^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{M}_{4B_{j}}(|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits\,h|^{2})(x)+\mathscr{M}_{\rm loc}(|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits\,h|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{M\setminus 4B_{j}})(x),

for any

. Hence in this case

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}\big|\sum_{i=1}^{i_{r}}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{kr^{2}}g_{i}\big|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}

(3.40)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{i_{r}}C{\rm e}^{-\alpha 4^{i}}\Big(\mathscr{M}_{\rm loc}(|\nabla h|^{2})+\mathscr{M}_{4B_{j}}(|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits\,h|^{2})+\mathscr{M}_{\rm loc}(|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits\,h|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{M\setminus 4B_{j}})\Big)^{1/2}(x).

For the second regime
i>i_{r}
, we proceed with inequality (
3.35
) in Lemma
3.8
that there exist

and

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{kr^{2}}g_{i}|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}\leq\frac{c_{1}{\rm e}^{-c_{2}4^{i}}}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(2^{i+1}B)}\int_{C_{i}}|h|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}.

(3.41)

On the other hand, since

, it is easy to see

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\mu(2^{i+1}B)}\int_{C_{i}}|h|^{2}\,d\mu\Big)^{1/2}

The contribution of the terms in the second regime

bounded by combining (

\displaystyle\sum_{i>i_{r}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits P_{kr^{2}}g_{i}|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}\leq\sum_{i>i_{r}}\frac{c_{1}{\rm e}^{-c_{2}4^{i}}}{r^{2}}\big(\mathscr{M}_{4B_{j}}(|f|^{2})(x)\big)^{1/2}

(3.43)

and it remains to recall that
1/r^{2}\leq 4^{i}/8
and

1/r\leq 2^{i}/{(2\sqrt{2})}
when

.

We then conclude from (

) and (

) that for any

and

, there exists a constant

independent of

such that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B\cap 4B_{j})}\int_{B\cap 4B_{j}}|\widetilde{T}P_{kr^{2}}f|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}\leq C(\mathscr{M}_{4B_{j}}(|\widetilde{T}f|^{2})+(S_{j}f)^{2})^{1/2}(x)

all

supported in
B_{j}
, all balls
B
in

and
all

, where the radius

of

and where

\displaystyle(S_{j}f)^{2}:=\mathscr{M}_{\rm loc}(|\widetilde{T}f|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{M\setminus 4B_{j}})+\mathscr{M}_{\rm loc}(|\nabla h|^{2})+\mathscr{M}_{4B_{j}}(|f|^{2}).

(3.44)

Our last step is to show that the operator
S_{j}
defined in
(

) is bounded from

to

for any

with operator norm independent of

. By

), we only need to show that the operators

\displaystyle\big\|\left(\mathscr{M}_{\rm loc}(|\nabla h|^{2})\right)^{1/2}\big\|_{p}\lesssim\|\nabla h\|_{p}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v(t)\,{\rm e}^{Kt}}{\sqrt{t}}\,dt\right)\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{j})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{j})}.

4
Appendix

In this appendix, we include the proof
of
(CZ)
(2)
in the case that the underlying manifold
M
has a lower bound Ricci curvature
for reader’s convenience (see
[
26
,
36
]
).
To be precise, let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold satisfying the curvature condition
(
Ric
). For any

u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
, Bochner’s formula gives

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits u|_{\mathop{\scriptscriptstyle\rm HS}\nolimits}^{2}-g(\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u)+\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits(\nabla u,\nabla u).

(4.1)

Integration by parts, together with the lower Ricci bound, leads to

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits u|_{\mathop{\scriptscriptstyle\rm HS}\nolimits}^{2}-\int_{M}g(\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u)+\int_{M}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits u|_{\mathop{\scriptscriptstyle\rm HS}\nolimits}^{2}-\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}-K\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits u|_{\mathop{\scriptscriptstyle\rm HS}\nolimits}^{2}-\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}-K\int_{M}u\Delta u.

Then, by estimating the last integral via Young’s inequality, we
obtain for any
\sigma>0
,

\displaystyle\|\,|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits u|\,\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\,|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits u|_{\mathop{\scriptscriptstyle\rm HS}\nolimits}\,\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{K\sigma^{2}}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left(1+\frac{K}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(4.2)

which establishes
CZ(2)
.

Remark 4.1
.

Inequality (
4.2
) extends from
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
to

u\in H^{2,2}(M)
. Thus, in particular, if
u\in L^{2}(M)
is a
distributional solution to the Poisson equation

\displaystyle\Delta u=f\quad\text{on }M

(4.3)

for some
f\in L^{2}(M)
, then (
4.2
) provides an
L^{2}
-Hessian
estimate of the solution
u
.

Remark 4.2
.

Recall that for
L^{2}
-gradient estimates of
u
in
(
4.3
), the lower bound on the Ricci tensor is not
needed. Indeed, integrating (
4.3
) by parts and using
Young inequality, one obtains directly for every
\sigma>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}=\int_{M}fu\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\int_{M}u^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\int_{M}f^{2},

which is the
L^{2}
-gradient estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.4)

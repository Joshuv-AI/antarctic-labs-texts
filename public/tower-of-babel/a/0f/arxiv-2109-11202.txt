Universal Bounds for Fractional Laplacian on a Bounded Open Domain in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n}
Lingzhong Zeng

Abstract
. Let Ω \Omega be a bounded open domain on the Euclidean space ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} and ℚ + \mathbb{Q}_{+} be the set of all positive rational numbers. In 2017, Chen and Zeng investigated the eigenvalues with higher order of the fractional Laplacian ( − Δ ) s | Ω \left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega} for s > 0 s>0 and s ∈ ℚ + s\in\mathbb{Q}_{+} , and they obtained a universal inequality of Yang type( Universal inequality and upper bounds of eigenvalues for non-integer poly-Laplacian on a bounded domain, Calculus of Variations and Partial Differential Equations, (2017) 56 :131 ). In the spirit of Chen and Zeng’s work, we study the eigenvalues of fractional Laplacian, and establish an inequality of eigenvalues with lower order under the same condition. Also, our eigenvalue inequality is universal and generalizes the eigenvalue inequality for the poly-harmonic operators given by Jost et al.( Universal bounds for eigenvalues of polyharmonic operator. Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 363 (4), 1821-1854 (2011) ).

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with piecewise smooth boundary

\partial\Omega
on an
n
-dimensional Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The Dirichlet eigenvalue problem of the poly-Laplacian with
any
order is described by

(1.1)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{l}u=\lambda u,\ \ &{\rm in}\ \ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u=\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\cdots=\dfrac{\partial^{l-1}u}{\partial\nu^{l-1}}=0,\ \ &{\rm on}\ \ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Delta
is the Laplacian and
\nu
denotes the outward unit
normal vector field of
\partial\Omega
. It is well known that the spectrum of the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) is
discrete and satisfies

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\cdots\rightarrow+\infty,

where each eigenvalue is repeated according to its multiplicity and
\lambda_{k}
denotes the
k^{th}
eigenvalue. When
l=1
, eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) is
called a fixed membrane problem. For this case, in 1956, Payne et al.
[
36
]
(Thompson-Colin
[
41
]
(for all
n\geq 3
)) proved a universal inequality as follows:

(1.2)

\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\leq\frac{4}{nk}\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}.

Furthermore, in various settings, many mathematicians extended the above universal inequality. In particular, Hile and Protter
[
28
]
proved the following universal inequality of eigenvalues:

(1.3)

\sum^{k}_{i=1}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}}\geq\frac{nk}{4},

which is sharper than inequality (
1.2
). Furthermore, Yang
[
45
]
made an amazing contribution to eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) (cf.
[
21
]
), and obtained a very sharp universal bound as follows:

(1.4)

\sum^{k}_{i=1}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}\leq\frac{4}{n}\sum^{k}_{i=1}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})\lambda_{i}.

Applying (
1.4
) and a celebrated recursion formula, Cheng and Yang gave a sharp upper bounds for the eigenvalues with respect to
k^{\frac{2}{n}}
( cf.
[
23
]
). In addition, for the case of degenerate elliptic operators, we refer the readers to
[
14
,
15
]
and references therein.

On the other hand, Payne et al.
[
36
]
investigated the lower order eigenvalues
and proved the following universal inequality in 1956:

(1.5)

\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\leq 6\lambda_{1},

for
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
and further proposed a famous conjecture for

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows:

(1.6)

\frac{\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}+\cdots+\lambda_{n+1}}{\lambda_{1}}\leq n\frac{\lambda_{2}(\mathbb{B}^{n})}{\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})},

where
\lambda_{i}(\mathbb{B}^{n})(i=1,2)
denotes the
i^{th}
eigenvalue of Laplacian on the ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with the same volume as the bounded domain
\Omega
, i.e.,
{\rm Vol}(\Omega)={\rm Vol}(\Omega^{\ast})
. Attacking this conjecture, Brands
[
12
]
improved (
1.5
) to the following:
\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\leq\lambda_{1}(3+\sqrt{7}),
when
n=2
.
Furthermore, Hile and Protter
[
28
]
proved

\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\leq 5.622\lambda_{1}.
In 1980, Marcellini
[
35
]
obtained
\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\leq(15+\sqrt{345})/6\lambda_{1}.
In 2011, Chen and Zheng
[
16
]
proved
\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\leq 5.3507\lambda_{1}.
For general case, Ashbaugh and Benguria

[
5
]
established an interesting universal inequality as follows:

(1.7)

\frac{\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}+\cdots+\lambda_{n+1}}{\lambda_{1}}\leq n+4,

for
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in 1993. For more references on the solution of
this conjecture, we refer the reader to

[
3
,
4
,
28
,
35
,
38
,

]
.

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{4l(2l+n-2)}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right),

which was obtained by Cheng et al. in

In 1998, Ashbaugh

(1.9)

\sum^{n}_{i=1}(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}_{i+1}-\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1})\leq 4\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1},

and

The proofs of (

\sum^{n+1}_{i=2}\lambda_{i}+\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}\frac{2(l-1)i}{2l+i-1}(\lambda_{n+1-i}-\lambda_{1})\leq(n+4l(2l-1))\lambda_{1},

Furthermore, we consider the fractional Laplace operator restricted to
\Omega
and denote it by
\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}
with
s>0,
which is defined as the pseudo-differential operator restricted to
\Omega
. In other words, the fractional Laplacian can be defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=:\textbf{ P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(z)}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}dz,

where
P.V.
denotes the principal value and
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. We note that fractional Laplacian is not a local operator when
s
is not a integer. Define the characteristic function
\chi_{\Omega}:t\mapsto\chi_{\Omega}(t)
by
\chi_{\Omega}(t)=1
when
x\in\Omega
, while
\chi_{\Omega}(t)=0
when
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\Omega
, then the special pseudo-differential operator can be represented as the Fourier transform of the function
u

[
34
]
, namely

\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}u:=\chi_{\Omega}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[|y|^{2s}\mathscr{F}\left[u\right]\right],

where
\mathscr{F}[u]
denotes the Fourier transform of a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\mathscr{F}[u](y)=\hat{u}(y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\sqrt{-1}x\cdot y}u(x)dx,

and the notation
\mathscr{F}^{-1}
is the inverse of Fourier transform. Here,
\mathscr{F}^{-1}
is given by

\mathscr{F}^{-1}[w](x)=\breve{w}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\sqrt{-1}x\cdot y}w(y)dy.

It is well known that the fractional Laplacian operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
can be considered as the infinitesimal generator of the symmetric
s
-stable process
[
11
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. Suppose that a stochastic process
X_{t}
has stationary independent increments and its transition density (i.e., convolution kernel)
p^{s}(t,x,z)=p^{s}(t,x-z),t>0,x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is determined by the following Fourier transform

\operatorname{Exp}\left(-t|y|^{2s}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\sqrt{-1}y\cdot z}p^{2s}(t,z)dz,\quad t>0,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

then we can say that the process
X_{t}
is an
n
-dimensional symmetric
s
-stable process with order
s\in(0,1]
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
( also see
[
7
,
8
,
10
]
)
There are two important examples of this process: one is the Cauchy process; and the other is the Brownian motion.
The fractional Laplacian operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the Riesz-Feller derivative of fractional order
2s>0
, i.e.
(-\Delta)^{s}=\frac{\partial^{2s}}{\partial|x|^{2s}}
, which appears in a wide class of physical systems including Lévy flights and stochastic interfaces. More detail information on Lévy process
will be found in a textbook
[
37
]
and a good survey
[
1
]
. We also refer the readers to an excellent article
[
17
]
, where some important and interesting literatures on fractional Laplacian have been given by Chen and Zeng.
We assume that
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\subset C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),
then the quadratic form
\left\langle\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}\phi,\phi\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\bar{\phi}(-\Delta)^{s}\phi dx

is positive on a dense subset of
L^{2}(\Omega),
and it can be extended to a unique minimal positive operator on
L^{2}(\Omega).
In addition, the fractional Laplacian operator
\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}
is self-adjoint with purely discrete spectrum. Specially, there is a sequence of discrete eigenvalues which can be ordered, after counting multiplicity, as

0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\cdots\rightarrow+\infty.

Comparing with the case of Laplacian, the eigenvalue of fractional Laplacian
\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}.
is much less studied, but it has recently received
more attention. Here, we only refer the reader to papers
[
10
,
17
,
27
,
43
,
47
,
48
]
and the references therein.
In particular, Chen and Zeng investigated the eigenvalues with higher order of fractional Laplacian. See
[
17
]
. Applying an important result due to Ilias and Makhoul
[
32
]
and a fine properties of commutators for some
operators, they proved an interesting result in
[
17
]
in 2017 as follows:

Theorem (Chen and Zeng’s Universal Inequality)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that
\left\{\lambda_{i}\right\}_{i\geq 1}
are eigenvalues of operator
\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega},
where
s=\frac{1}{m}
with the positive integer
m\geq 2
or
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
s\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}.
Then,

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\right]^{2}\leq\frac{4s(n+2s-2)}{n^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{s-1}{s}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{s}}\right).

By the above theorem and a variant of Chebyshev sum inequality, Chen and Zeng
[
17
]
deduced
an eigenvalue inequality of Yang type as follows:

(1.15)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{4s(n+2s-2)}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\lambda_{i},

which implies an upper bounds for the eigenvalues:

\lambda_{k+1}\leq\left(1+\frac{4s(n+2s-2)}{n^{2}}\right)k^{\frac{2s(n+2s-2)}{n^{2}}}\lambda_{1}.

As the above argument, Chen and Zeng only discussed the eigenvalues with higher order for the fractional Laplacian. However, they have not addressed the case of lower order. In this regard, it is natural to propose the following problem:

Problem
.

Can one obtain a universal inequality for the eigenvalues with lower order of the
fractional Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
?

Under the same assumption as Chen and Zeng’s Theorem, we answer the above problem. In details, we establish a key lemma in section
2
, and by utilizing this lemma, obtain an eigenvalue inequality in section
3
. This is what the following theorem says.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Universal inequality)
Let
\Omega
be a bounded open domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that
\mathbb{Q}_{+}
is the set of all positive rational numbers and
\left\{\lambda_{i}\right\}_{i\geq 1}
are eigenvalues of operator
\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega},
where
s=\frac{1}{m}
with the positive integer
m\geq 2
or
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
s\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}.
Then, we have

(1.16)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq[4s(n+2s-2)]^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark 1.1
.

Since the constant
[4s(n+2s-2)]^{\frac{1}{2}}
in (
1.16
) is
not dependent on
\Omega
, eigenvalue inequality (
1.16
) is universal.

2.
A key lemma and its proof

In this section, we shall prove a key lemma, which plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Firstly, let us explain the operation rules of the
fractional Laplacian in a bounded domain. Let
u
and
w
is two functions defined on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and we recall that the fractional Laplacian is defined by

\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}u:=\chi_{\Omega}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[|y|^{2s}\mathscr{F}\left[u\right]\right],

where

\mathscr{F}[u](y)=\hat{u}(y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\sqrt{-1}x\cdot y}u(x)dx,

and
\mathscr{F}^{-1}
is the inverse of Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}
, which is defined by

\mathscr{F}^{-1}[w](x)=\widehat{w}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\sqrt{-1}x\cdot y}w(y)dy.

In 2014, Chen proved the following Green formula. See
[
13
, Lemma 2.2]
. Here, we can also conclude it by an alternative method. Those two different techniques are based on the equivalent definitions of fractional Laplacian: the former is the principal value of integral, while the later is Fourier transformation.

Proposition 2.1
.

(Green Formula)
Let
w_{i}:\Omega(\subset\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be two functions satisfying
w_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
w_{i}(x)=0
when
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\Omega
, where
i=1,2
. Then,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w_{1}(x)\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}w_{2}(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}w_{2}(x)\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}w_{1}(x)dx.

Proof.

A straightforward calculation shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w_{1}(x)\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}w_{2}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\Omega}w_{1}(x)\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[|y|^{2s}\mathscr{F}\left[w_{2}(x)\right]\right]dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\Omega}(\chi_{\Omega}w_{1})(z)\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2s}e^{-\sqrt{-1}x\cdot y}w_{2}(x)dx\right]dz

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\Omega}w_{1}(z)\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2s}e^{-\sqrt{-1}x\cdot y}w_{2}(x)dx\right]dz,

since
w_{1}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
w_{1}(x)=0
when
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\Omega
.
Furthermore, the definition of inverse of Fourier transform implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w_{1}(x)\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}w_{2}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\Omega}w_{1}(z)\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\sqrt{-1}z\cdot y}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2s}e^{-\sqrt{-1}x\cdot y}w_{2}(x)dx\right]dy\right\}dz

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\Omega}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w_{1}(z)e^{\sqrt{-1}z\cdot y}|y|^{2s}e^{-\sqrt{-1}x\cdot y}w_{2}(x)dxdy\right\}dz

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\Omega}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w_{1}(z)e^{-\sqrt{-1}z\cdot y}|y|^{2s}e^{\sqrt{-1}x\cdot y}w_{2}(x)dxdy\right\}dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w_{2}(x)\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2s}e^{\sqrt{-1}x\cdot y}\left[\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\sqrt{-1}z\cdot y}w_{1}(z)dz\right]dy\right\}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\chi_{\Omega}w_{2})(x)\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[|y|^{2s}\mathscr{F}\left[w_{1}(x)\right]\right]dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w_{2}(x)\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}w_{1}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}w_{2}(x)\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}w_{1}(x)dx,

which gives (
2.1
). Thus, we finish the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
\{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\}
is an arbitrary Euclidean coordinate. Let
\lambda_{j}
and
u_{j}
denote the
j
-th eigenvalue and the corresponding normalized eigenfunction of
\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega},
respectively. Assume that

(2.2)

\int_{\Omega}h_{i}u_{1}u_{j+1}dv=0,\ \ for\ \ 1\leq j<i\leq n,

where
h_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then,

(2.3)

\sum^{n}_{i=1}(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{1}(h_{i})u_{1}dv\leq 2\left\{\sum^{n}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{2}(h_{i})u_{1}dv\sum^{n}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{i}dv\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

(2.4)

\Theta_{1}(h_{i})=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{1},

(2.5)

\Theta_{2}(h_{i})=\frac{1}{2}\left[2h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h^{2}_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}-(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}^{2}u_{1})\right],

(2.6)

\Psi_{i}=u_{1,i}^{2},

and

u_{1,i}=\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{i}}.

Proof.

Define function
\psi_{i}
as follows:

\psi_{i}=h_{i}u_{1}-\tau_{i}u_{1},

where

\tau_{i}=\int_{\Omega}h_{i}u_{1}^{2}dv.

According to
(
2.2
), one can conclude that

(2.7)

\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}u_{j+1}dv=0,\ \ for\ \ 0\leq j<i\leq n.

Moreover, it follows from (
2.7
) that

(2.8)

\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}h_{i}u_{1}dv=\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}^{2}dv.

Since
\psi_{i}
satisfies (
2.7
), by Rayleigh-Ritz inequality, we know that

(2.9)

\lambda_{i+1}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}\psi_{i}dv}{\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}^{2}dv}.

Utilizing (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we obtain

(2.10)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}\psi_{i}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}\Big]\psi_{i}dv+\int_{\Omega}h_{i}\psi_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}\Big]\psi_{i}dv+\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}^{2}dv.

Substituting (
2.10
) into (
2.9
), we
yield

(2.11)

(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{1})\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}^{2}dv\leq\int_{\Omega}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}\Big]\psi_{i}dv.

It follows from the Green formula (
2.1
) that,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}\Big]u_{1}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}h_{i}u_{1}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}dv-\int_{\Omega}h_{i}u_{1}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}dv

\displaystyle=0,

which implies that

(2.12)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}\Big]\psi_{i}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}\Big]h_{i}u_{1}dv.

Consequently,
plugging (
2.12
) into (
2.11
), one has

(2.13)

(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{1})\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}^{2}dv\leq\int_{\Omega}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}\Big]h_{i}u_{1}dv.

By the formula of partial integration, we infer that

(2.14)

\int_{\Omega}u_{1,i}u_{1}dv=0,

(2.15)

2\int_{\Omega}u_{1}\frac{\partial(h_{i}u_{1})}{\partial x_{i}}dv=\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\partial(h_{i}u_{1})}{\partial x_{i}}-h_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{1}\right]u_{1}dv,

and

(2.16)

-2\int_{\Omega}u_{1,i}\psi_{i}dv=2\int_{\Omega}u_{1}\frac{\partial(h_{i}u_{1})}{\partial x_{i}}dv+2\tau_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{1,i}u_{1}dv.

Hence, utilizing (
2.14
), (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), we obtain

(2.17)

\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{1}\right]u_{1}dv=-2\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}u_{1,i}dv.

Multiplying both sides of (
2.17
) by
(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and noticing (
2.13
), one can infer that

(2.18)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{1}\right]u_{1}dv

\displaystyle=-2(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}u_{1,i}dv

\displaystyle\leq\delta(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{1})\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i}^{2}dv+\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}u_{1,i}^{2}dv

\displaystyle\leq\delta\int_{\Omega}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}\Big]h_{i}u_{1}dv+\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}u_{1,i}^{2}dv,

where
\delta
is a positive constant.
Taking sum on
i
from
1
to
n
in (

), we get

(2.19)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\sum^{n}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}\Big]h_{i}u_{1}dv

\displaystyle-\delta\sum^{n}_{i=1}(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{1}\right]u_{1}dv+\sum^{n}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}u_{1,i}^{2}dv

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Here, the left-hand side of (
2.19
) is a quadratic polynomial of

\delta
. By direct calculation, we show that

(2.20)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}\Big]h_{i}u_{1}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}h_{i}\left[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}\right]u_{1}dv

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left[(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}^{2}u_{1})-h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})\right]u_{1}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[2h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h^{2}_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}-(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}^{2}u_{1})\right]u_{1}dv.

Combining (
2.19
) with (
2.20
), we get

(2.21)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\sum^{n}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}\left[2h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h^{2}_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}-(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}^{2}u_{1})\right]u_{1}dv

\displaystyle-\delta\sum^{n}_{i=1}(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(h_{i}u_{1})-h_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{1}\right]u_{1}dv+\sum^{n}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}u_{1,i}^{2}dv

\displaystyle\geq 0.

From (
2.11
),(
2.12
) and (
2.20
), we can conclude that

\int_{\Omega}\left[2h_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}u_{1})-h^{2}_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}-(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(h_{i}^{2}u_{1})\right]u_{1}dv\geq 0,

for
i=1,\cdots,n
,
which means that its discriminant must be non-positive.
Hence, from (
2.4
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
), and (
2.21
), we
yield (
2.3
). Therefore, we finish the proof of Lemma
2.1
.∎

Proposition 3.1
.

(See
[
31
, Theorem 1]
)
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real or complex inner product space. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a linear submanifold of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be a linear operator in
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose
l
is a positive integer and
u
is a fixed vector such that for all integers
0\leq r\leq q\leq l

\left|\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{q}u,u\right\rangle\right|=|\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{q-r}u,\mathcal{L}^{r}u\right\rangle|.

Then, for all integers
0\leq r\leq q\leq l
, when
q
is even, we have

(3.1)

\left|\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{r}u,u\right\rangle\right|\leq\left|\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{q}u,u\right\rangle\right|^{r/q}\langle u,u\rangle^{1-r/q}.

This inequality is also true for
q
odd and
0\leq r\leq q\leq l
. Moreover, there is a finite collection of operators
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{N}
in
\mathcal{H}
such that

(3.2)

\left|\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{q}u,u\right\rangle\right|=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{j}\mathcal{L}^{q-r}u,\mathcal{D}_{j}\mathcal{L}^{r-1}u\right\rangle\right|.

holds for each pair of integers
r
and
q
with
1\leq r\leq q\leq l
.

Remark 3.1
.

(See
[
31
, Remark (ii)]
or
[
17
, Remark 2.2]
)
If
\mathcal{L}
is any (not necessarily bounded) nonnegative self-adjoint operator, then (
3.2
) is satisfied by taking
N=1
and letting
\mathcal{D}_{1}
be any square root of
\mathcal{L}
. Hence, for nonnegative self-adjoint operators, the inequality (
3.1
) holds for all integers
q
.

By Proposition
3.1
, we shall prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that the function

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{2}(x_{i})u_{1}dv=s(n+2s-2)\lambda_{1}^{\frac{s-1}{s}},

Proof.

(3.4)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\Big((-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}x_{i}-x_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}\Big)u_{1}

\displaystyle=\chi_{\Omega}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[|y|^{2s}\mathscr{F}\left(x_{i}u_{1}\right)\right]-\chi_{\Omega}x_{i}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[|y|^{2s}\hat{u}_{1}\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\chi_{\Omega}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[|y|^{2s}\frac{\partial\hat{u}_{1}}{\partial y_{i}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\left(|y|^{2s}\hat{u}_{1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{-1}\chi_{\Omega}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[2s|y|^{2s-2}y_{i}\hat{u}_{1}\right],

where
u_{1}
denotes the first eigenfunction of
\left.(-\Delta)^{s}\right|_{\Omega}
.
According to (
2.5
) and the similar calculation as (
3.4
), one can infer by
putting
h_{i}=x_{i}
that

(3.5)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 2\Theta_{2}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=2x_{i}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(x_{i}u_{1})-x_{i}^{2}(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}u_{1}-(-\Delta)^{s}|_{\Omega}(x_{i}^{2}u_{1})

\displaystyle=2\chi_{\Omega}x_{i}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[|y|^{2s}\mathscr{F}\left(x_{i}u_{1}\right)\right]-\chi_{\Omega}x_{i}^{2}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(|y|^{2s}\hat{u}_{1}\right)-\chi_{\Omega}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[|y|^{2s}\mathscr{F}\left(x_{i}^{2}u_{1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\chi_{\Omega}\left[2\sqrt{-1}x_{i}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(|y|^{2s}\frac{\partial\hat{u}_{1}}{\partial y_{i}}\right)-x_{i}^{2}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(|y|^{2s}\hat{u}_{1}\right)+\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(|y|^{2s}\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{u}_{1}}{\partial y_{i}^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\chi_{\Omega}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[-2\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\left(|y|^{2s}\frac{\partial\hat{u}_{1}}{\partial y_{i}}\right)+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y_{i}^{2}}\left(|y|^{2s}\hat{u}_{1}\right)+|y|^{2s}\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{u}_{1}}{\partial y_{i}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\chi_{\Omega}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[\left(2s|y|^{2s-2}+2s(2s-2)|y|^{2s-4}y_{i}^{2}\right)\hat{u}_{1}\right].

Since the support of

is in

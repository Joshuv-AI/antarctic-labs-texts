Stirling numbers with level 2 2 and poly-Bernoulli numbers with level 2 2
Takao Komatsu

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce poly-Bernoulli numbers with level 2 2 , related to the Stirling numbers of the second kind with level 2 2 , and study several properties of poly-Bernoulli numbers with level 2 2 from their expressions, relations, and congruences. Poly-Bernoulli numbers with level 2 2 have strong connections with poly-Cauchy numbers with level 2 2 . In a special case, we can determine the denominators of Bernoulli numbers with level 2 2 by showing a von Staudt-Clausen like theorem. Keywords: Stirling numbers, poly-Bernoulli numbers, congruences, von Staudt-Clausen theorem. MR Subject Classifications: 11B73, 05A15, 05A19, 11A07, 11B37, 11B68, 11B75.

\left[\!\!\left[n\atop k\right]\!\!\right]_{s}=\left[\!\!\left[n-1\atop k-1\right]\!\!\right]_{s}+(n-1)^{s}\left[\!\!\left[n-1\atop k\right]\!\!\right]

2
Some expressions

In
[
12
]
, the Stirling numbers of the second kind with higher level are expressed explicitly as

\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop k\right\}\!\!\right\}_{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{j^{ns}}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}\quad(1\leq k\leq n)\,.

(4)

When
s=1
, by

(k-j)!j!=(-1)^{k-j}\prod_{i=0\atop i\neq j}^{k}(j-i)\,,

(5)

this is reduced to a famous expression of the original Stirling numbers of the second kind:

\left\{n\atop k\right\}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{k-j}j^{n}\quad(1\leq k\leq n)\,.

(6)

When
s=2
, (
4
) is reduced to an expression

\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop k\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}=\frac{2}{(2k)!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{2k}{k-j}j^{2n}\quad(1\leq k\leq n)

(7)

(
[
1
, Proposition 2.4 (xiii)]
). However, no explicit expression for
s\geq 3
has not been found yet. It implies that unfortunately,

\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop k\right\}\!\!\right\}_{3}\neq\frac{3}{(3k)!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{3k}{k-j}j^{3n}

or something like this.

There exists a different explicit expression from (
4
).

Theorem 1
.

For integers
n
and
k
with
2\leq k\leq n
,

\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop k\right\}\!\!\right\}_{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{j^{(k-1)s}(j^{(n-k+1)s}-k^{(n-k+1)s})}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}

with
\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop 1\right\}\!\!\right\}_{s}=1
(
n\geq 1
).

Remark.

When
s=1
in Theorem
1
, by (
5
) and

\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}j^{k-1}=0\,,

we have (
6
) again.

In
[
12
]
, the ordinary generating function of the Stirling numbers of the second kind with higher level is given by

\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop k\right\}\!\!\right\}_{s}x^{n}=\frac{x^{k}}{(1-x)(1-2^{s}x)\cdots(1-k^{s}x)}\,.

The exponential generating function of the Stirling numbers of the second kind with higher level is given as follows.

Theorem 2
.

For
k\geq 1
,

\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop k\right\}\!\!\right\}_{s}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{e^{j^{s}x}}{\prod_{i=1,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}+\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k!)^{s}}\,.

Remark.

1) When
s=1
in Theorem
2
, we have the exponential generating function of the original Stirling numbers of the second kind:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\left\{n\atop k\right\}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{k!}{(k-j)!j!}(e^{x})^{j}(-1)^{k-j}+(-1)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(e^{x}-1)^{k}}{k!}\,.

2) Another variation is similarly shown as follows.

\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop k\right\}\!\!\right\}_{s}\frac{x^{ns}}{(ns)!}=\frac{1}{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{e^{\zeta^{\ell}jx}}{\prod_{i=1,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}+\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k!)^{s}}\,.

where
\zeta:=e^{2\pi i/s}
is the
s
-th root of unity.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

1)
By (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop k\right\}\!\!\right\}_{s}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{j^{ns}}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}j^{ns}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}j^{ns}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{e^{j^{s}x}}{\prod_{i=1,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}+\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k!)^{s}}\,.

Here,

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}=\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(k!)^{s}}

and

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{j^{ns}}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}=0\quad(1\leq n\leq k-1)\,.

2) By Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop k\right\}\!\!\right\}_{s}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{j^{(k-1)s}(j^{(n-k+1)s}-k^{(n-k+1)s})}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}j^{ns}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{j}{k}\right)^{(k-1)s}k^{ns}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}j^{ns}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac{j}{k}\right)^{(k-1)s}k^{ns}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{e^{j^{s}x}}{\prod_{i=1,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}-0

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}j^{ns}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}+0

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{e^{j^{s}x}}{\prod_{i=1,i\neq j}^{k}(j^{s}-i^{s})}+\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k!)^{s}}\,.

∎

3
Poly-Bernoulli numbers with level
2

Poly-Bernoulli numbers are defined by the generating function

\frac{{\rm Li}_{k}(1-e^{-x})}{1-e^{-x}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

(8)

(
[
4
]
), where

{\rm Li}_{k}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{k}}

is the polylogarithm function.

When
k=1
, it is reduced to the generating function of the original Bernoulli numbers:

\frac{x}{1-e^{-x}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(1)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

with
B_{1}^{(1)}=1/2
. Another definition is given by

\frac{x}{e^{x}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

(9)

with
B_{1}=-1/2
.

Then, poly-Bernoulli numbers can be expressed explicitly in terms of the Stirling numbers of the second kind (
[
4
, Theorem 1]
):

B_{n}^{(k)}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left\{n\atop m\right\}\frac{(-1)^{n-m}m!}{(m+1)^{k}}\,.

(10)

There have been many generalizations of Bernoulli or poly-Bernoulli numbers. In this paper, we introduced poly-Bernoulli numbers with level
2
by using higher-level Stirling numbers (
[
11
,
12
]
).

In
[
10
]
,
poly-Cauchy numbers

\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{(k)}

with level
2
are defined by

{\rm Lif}_{2,k}({\rm arcsinh}x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\,,

(11)

where
{\rm arcsinh}x
is the inverse hyperbolic sine function and

{\rm Lif}_{2,k}(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2m}}{(2m)!(2m+1)^{k}}\,.

The function
{\rm Lif}_{2,k}(z)
is an analogue of
polylogarithm factorial
or
polyfactorial
function
{\rm Lif}_{k}(z)

[
5
,
6
]
, defined by

{\rm Lif}_{k}(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{m}}{m!(m+1)^{k}}\,.

By using the polyfactorial function, poly-Cauchy numbers (of the first kind)
c_{n}^{(k)}
are defined as

{\rm Lif}_{k}\bigl(\log(1+x)\bigr)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\,.

(12)

When
k=1
, by
{\rm Lif}_{1}(z)=(e^{z}-1)/z
,
c_{n}=c_{n}^{(1)}
are the original Cauchy numbers defined by

\frac{x}{\log(1+x)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\,.

Define the
polylogarithm function

{\rm Li}_{2,k}(z)

with level
2
by

{\rm Li}_{k,2}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)^{k}}\,.

(13)

Then,
poly-Bernoulli numbers

\mathfrak{B}_{n}^{(k)}

with level
2
are defined by

\frac{{\rm Li}_{2,k}\bigl(2\sin(x/2)\bigr)}{2\sin(x/2)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\,.

(14)

Note that
\mathfrak{B}_{n}^{(k)}=0
for odd
n
.

The generating function of the poly-Cauchy numbers with level
2
can be written in the form of iterated integrals (
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
):

\frac{1}{{\rm arcsinh}x}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{{\rm arcsinh}x\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\cdots\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{{\rm arcsinh}x\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}}_{k-1}\times x\underbrace{dx\cdots dx}_{k-1}\\
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\quad(k\geq 1)\,.

We can also write the generating function of the poly-Bernoulli numbers with level
2
in (
14
) in the form of iterated integrals.

Theorem 3
.

For
k\geq 1
, we have

\frac{1}{2\sin\frac{x}{2}}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{2\tan\frac{x}{2}}\cdots\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{2\tan\frac{x}{2}}}_{k-1}\times\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+2\sin\frac{x}{2}}{1-2\sin\frac{x}{2}}\underbrace{dx\cdots dx}_{k-1}\\
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\,.

Proof.

Since

\frac{d}{dz}{\rm Li}_{2,k}(z)=\frac{1}{z}{\rm Li}_{2,k-1}(z)\,,

we have

\displaystyle{\rm Li}_{2,k}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{{\rm Li}_{2,k-1}(z_{1})}{z_{1}}dz_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\frac{{\rm Li}_{2,k-2}(z_{2})}{z_{2}}dz_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\frac{dz_{2}}{z_{2}}\cdots\int_{0}^{z_{k-2}}\frac{{\rm Li}_{2,1}(z_{k-1})}{z_{k-1}}dz_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\frac{dz_{2}}{z_{2}}\cdots\int_{0}^{z_{k-2}}\frac{1}{z_{k-1}}\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+z_{k-1}}{1-z_{k-1}}dz_{k-1}\,.

Putting
z=z_{1}=\cdots=z_{k-1}=2\sin(x/2)
, we get

\frac{{\rm Li}_{2,k}\bigl(2\sin(x/2)\bigr)}{2\sin(x/2)}=\frac{1}{2\sin(x/2)}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\cos(x/2)}{2\sin(x/2)}dx\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\cos(x/2)}{2\sin(x/2)}dx\\
\cdots\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\cos(x/2)}{2\sin(x/2)}\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+2\sin(x/2)}{1-2\sin(x/2)}dx\,.

∎

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{0}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=1\,,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{2}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3^{k}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{4}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3^{k}}+\frac{24}{5^{k}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{6}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3^{k}}-\frac{120}{5^{k}}+\frac{720}{7^{k}}\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3^{k}}-\frac{2040}{5^{k}}+\frac{105840}{7^{k}}-\frac{32659200}{9^{k}}+\frac{3628800}{11^{k}}\,.

In this section, we shall show some explicit formulae and some recurrence relations.

Poly-Cauchy numbers with level
2
can be expressed explicitly in terms of the Stirling numbers of the second kind with level
2
(
[
8
, Theorem 1]
):

\mathfrak{C}_{2n}^{(k)}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left[\!\!\left[n\atop m\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\frac{(-4)^{n-m}}{(2m+1)^{k}}\,.

Poly-Bernoulli numbers with level
2
can be expressed explicitly in terms of the Stirling numbers of the second kind with level
2
. It is a natural extension of the expression in (
10
).

Theorem 4
.

For
n\geq 0
,

\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k)}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\frac{(-1)^{n-m}(2m)!}{(2m+1)^{k}}\,.

Proof.

We use the power series of powers of trigonometric functions

\left(2\sin\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2m}=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-m}\frac{(2m)!}{(2n)!}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}x^{2n}

(see
[
1
, Theorem 4.1.1 (4.1.1)]
). Then, by (
13
) and (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\bigl(2\sin(x/2)\bigr)^{2m}}{(2m+1)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+1)^{k}}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-m}\frac{(2m)!}{(2n)!}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}x^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\frac{(-1)^{n-m}(2m)!}{(2m+1)^{k}}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}\,.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides, we get the desired result.
∎

Next, we shall show an explicit formula without Stirling numbers.

Theorem 5
.

For integers
n
and
k
with
n\geq 0
,

\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k)}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{1}{(2m+1)^{k}}\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2m}=n-m\atop i_{1},\dots,i_{2m}\geq 0}\left(-\frac{1}{4}\right)^{n-m}\binom{2n}{2i_{1}+1,\cdots,2i_{2m}+1}\,,

where

\binom{2n}{2i_{1}+1,\cdots,2i_{2m}+1}=\frac{\bigl((2i_{1}+1)+\cdots+(2i_{2m}+1)\bigr)!}{(2i_{1}+1)!\cdots(2i_{2m}+1)!}

is the multinomial coefficient.

Proof.

Since

2\sin\frac{x}{2}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{(2\ell+1)!}\frac{x^{2\ell+1}}{2^{2\ell}}\,,

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+1)^{k}}\left(2\sin\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+1)^{k}}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{(2\ell+1)!}\frac{x^{2\ell+1}}{2^{2\ell}}\right)^{2m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+1)^{k}}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2m}=n-m\atop i_{1},\dots,i_{2m}\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2m}}}{(2i_{1}+1)!\cdots(2i_{2m}+1)!}\frac{x^{2n}}{2^{2i_{1}+\cdots+2i_{2m}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{1}{(2m+1)^{k}}\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2m}=n-m\atop i_{1},\dots,i_{2m}\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n-m}x^{2n}}{(2i_{1}+1)!\cdots(2i_{2m}+1)!2^{2(n-m)}}\,.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides, we get the desired result.
∎

Theorem 6

For integers
n

k
with
n\geq 0

\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k-1)}=\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k)}+(2n)!\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\frac{4\bigl((-1)^{n-m}-(-4)^{n-m}\bigr)B_{2n-2m}\mathfrak{B}_{2m+2}^{(k)}}{(2n-2m)!(2m+1)!}\,.

Proof.

From the definition in (

{\rm Li}_{2,k}\bigl(2\sin(x/2)\bigr)=2\sin(x/2)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}\,.

Differentiating both sides by

, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\cos(x/2)}{2\sin(x/2)}{\rm Li}_{2,k-1}\bigl(2\sin(x/2)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\cos\frac{x}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}+2\sin\frac{x}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{2n-1}}{(2n-1)!}\,.

Hence,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k-1)}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}+2\tan\frac{x}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n+2}^{(k)}\frac{x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}\,.

(15)

Since

\displaystyle 2\tan\frac{x}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n+2}^{(k)}\frac{x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{4\bigl((-1)^{n}-(-4)^{n}\bigr)B_{2n}}{(2n)!}x^{2n-1}\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n+2}^{(k)}\frac{x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\frac{4\bigl((-1)^{n-m}-(-4)^{n-m}\bigr)B_{2n-2m}\mathfrak{B}_{2m+2}^{(k)}}{(2n-2m)!(2m+1)!}x^{2n}\,,

comparing the coefficients of both sides of (
15
), we get the desired result.
∎

5
Relations with poly-Cauchy numbers with level
2

There exist some strong reasons why we define poly-Bernoulli numbers with level
2
as in (
14
). Poly-Cauchy numbers with level
2
can be expressed in terms of poly-Bernoulli numbers with level
2
.

Theorem 7
.

For integers
n
and
k
with
n\geq 1
,

\mathfrak{C}_{2n}^{(k)}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\frac{(-4)^{n-m}}{(2m)!}\left[\!\!\left[n\atop m\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\left[\!\!\left[m\atop l\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\mathfrak{B}_{2l}^{(k)}\,.

Remark.

Poly-Cauchy numbers can be expressed in terms of poly-Bernoulli numbers (
[
9
, Theorem 2.2]
):

c_{n}^{(k)}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{n-m}}{m!}\left[n\atop m\right]\left[m\atop l\right]B_{l}^{(k)}\,.

Proof of Theorem
7
.

By Theorem
4
and the orthogonal relation

\sum_{l=j}^{m}(-1)^{l-j}\left[\!\!\left[m\atop l\right]\!\!\right]_{s}\left\{\!\!\left\{l\atop j\right\}\!\!\right\}_{s}=\begin{cases}1&\text{$m=j$};\\
0&\text{$m\neq j$}\end{cases}\quad(s\geq 1)

(
[
12
, Theorem 5.1]
) together with
\left[\!\!\left[n\atop 0\right]\!\!\right]_{2}=0
(

), we have

\displaystyle{\rm RHS}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\frac{(-4)^{n-m}}{(2m)!}\left[\!\!\left[n\atop m\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\left[\!\!\left[m\atop l\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\left\{\!\!\left\{l\atop j\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\frac{(-1)^{l-j}(2j)!}{(2j+1)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{(-4)^{n-m}}{(2m)!}\left[\!\!\left[n\atop m\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{(2j)!}{(2j+1)^{k}}\sum_{l=j}^{m}(-1)^{l-j}\left[\!\!\left[m\atop l\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\left\{\!\!\left\{l\atop j\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{(-4)^{n-m}}{(2m)!}\left[\!\!\left[n\atop m\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\frac{(2m)!}{(2m+1)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{(-4)^{n-m}}{(2m+1)^{k}}\left[\!\!\left[n\atop m\right]\!\!\right]_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{C}_{2n}^{(k)}\quad(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Ko20}{}{}, \rm Theorem~1]})\,.

∎

On the contrary, poly-Bernoulli numbers can be expressed in terms of poly-Cauchy numbers (
[

,

]
):

B_{n}^{(k)}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}(-1)^{n-m}m!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\left\{m\atop l\right\}c_{l}^{(k)}\,.

Similarly, poly-Bernoulli numbers with level
2
can be expressed in terms of poly-Cauchy numbers with level

.

Theorem 8
.

For integers
n
and
k
with
n\geq 1
,

\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k)}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}(-1)^{n-m}4^{m-l}(2m)!\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\left\{\!\!\left\{m\atop l\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\mathfrak{C}_{2l}^{(k)}\,.

Proof.

Using another orthogonal relation

\sum_{l=j}^{m}(-1)^{l-j}\left\{\!\!\left\{m\atop l\right\}\!\!\right\}_{s}\left[\!\!\left[l\atop j\right]\!\!\right]_{s}=\begin{cases}1&\text{$m=j$};\\
0&\text{$m\neq j$}\end{cases}\quad(s\geq 1)

(
[
12
, Theorem 5.1]
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm RHS}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-m}(2m)!\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\sum_{l=0}^{m}4^{m-l}\left\{\!\!\left\{m\atop l\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\frac{(-4)^{l-j}}{(2j+1)^{k}}\left[\!\!\left[l\atop j\right]\!\!\right]_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-m}(2m)!\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{4^{m-j}}{(2j+1)^{k}}\sum_{l=j}^{m}(-1)^{l-j}\left\{\!\!\left\{m\atop l\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\left[\!\!\left[l\atop j\right]\!\!\right]_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-m}(2m)!}{(2m+1)^{k}}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(k)}\,.

∎

Other relations with Stirling numbers with level
2
are given as follows.

Theorem 9
.

For
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left[\!\!\left[n\atop m\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\mathfrak{B}_{2m}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2n+1)^{k}}\,,

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}4^{n-m}\mathfrak{C}_{2m}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2n+1)^{k}}\,.

(17)

Remark.

For poly-Bernoulli and poly-Cauchy numbers (
[
5
, Theorem 3]
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left[n\atop m\right]B_{m}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k}}\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left\{n\atop m\right\}c_{m}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k}}\,.

Proof of Theorem
9
.

By the orthogonal relations, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left[\!\!\left[n\atop m\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\mathfrak{B}_{2m}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left[\!\!\left[n\atop m\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\left\{\!\!\left\{m\atop l\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\frac{(-1)^{m-l}(2l)!}{(2l+1)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\frac{(2l)!}{(2l+1)^{k}}\sum_{m=l}^{n}(-1)^{m-l}\left[\!\!\left[n\atop m\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\left\{\!\!\left\{m\atop l\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2n)!}\frac{(2n)!}{(2n+1)^{k}}=\frac{1}{(2n+1)^{k}}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}4^{n-m}\mathfrak{C}_{2m}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}4^{n-m}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\left[\!\!\left[m\atop l\right]\!\!\right]_{2}\frac{(-4)^{m-l}}{(2l+1)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\frac{4^{n}}{(2l+1)^{k}}\left(-\frac{1}{4}\right)^{l}\sum_{m=l}^{n}(-1)^{m}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\left[\!\!\left[m\atop l\right]\!\!\right]_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{4^{n}}{(2n+1)^{k}}\left(-\frac{1}{4}\right)^{n}(-1)^{n}=\frac{1}{(2n+1)^{k}}\,.

∎

6
Double summation formula

The poly-Bernoulli numbers satisfy the duality formula
B_{n}^{(-k)}=B_{k}^{(-n)}
for
n,k\geq 1
, because of the symmetric formula

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(-k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}=\frac{e^{x+y}}{e^{x}+e^{y}-e^{x+y}}\,.

Though the corresponding duality formula does not always hold for other cases, we still have the double summation formula for poly-Bernoulli numbers with level
2
.

Theorem 10
.

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(-2k)}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}\frac{y^{2k}}{(2k)!}=\frac{\cos x\cosh y}{2(1-\cos x)(1-\cosh 2y)+\cos^{2}x}\,.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(-2k)}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}\frac{y^{2k}}{(2k)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(2\sin\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2m}(2m+1)^{2k}\frac{y^{2k}}{(2k)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(2\sin\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2m}\cosh\bigl((2m+1)y\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{y}}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(2\sin\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2m}e^{2my}+\frac{e^{-y}}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(2\sin\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2m}e^{-2my}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{y}}{2}\frac{1}{1-\bigl(2e^{y}\sin(x/2)\bigr)^{2}}+\frac{e^{-y}}{2}\frac{1}{1-\bigl(2e^{-y}\sin(x/2)\bigr)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e^{y}}{1-e^{2y}(1-\cos x)}+\frac{e^{-y}}{1-e^{-2y}(1-\cos x)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{e^{-y}\cos x+e^{y}\cos x}{2(1-\cos x)-(e^{2y}+e^{-2y})(1-\cos x)+\cos^{2}x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cos x\cosh y}{2(1-\cos x)(1-\cosh 2y)+\cos^{2}x}\,.

∎

For

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{B}_{16}\mod 1)-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{5}+\frac{1}{17}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{B}_{18}\mod 1)+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{7}+\frac{1}{19}

\displaystyle=1\,.

Proof of Theorem
14
.

From Theorem
4
and (
7
), notice that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{2n}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\frac{(-1)^{n-m}(2m)!}{2m+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{2(-1)^{n}}{2m+1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j}\binom{2m}{m-j}j^{2n}\,.

For
n\geq 1
, we see
m\geq 1
in the above summations.

Case 1.
When
2m+1
is composite, as
m\geq 4
,
(2m+1)|(2m)!
. Since
\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}
is an integer, every such a term of

\left\{\!\!\left\{n\atop m\right\}\!\!\right\}_{2}\frac{(-1)^{n-m}(2m)!}{2m+1}

is an integer.

Case 2.
When
2m+1=p
is prime,
p\geq 3
and
m=(p-1)/2
is an integer.
Now, consider the summation

\sum_{j=1}^{(p-1)/2}(-1)^{j}\binom{p-1}{(p-1)/2-j}j^{2n}\,.

Case 2.1.

If
\frac{p-1}{2}|n
, then by Fermat’s little theorem,

j^{2n}\equiv 1\pmod{p}\quad(j=1,2,\dots,p-1)\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{(p-1)/2}(-1)^{j}\binom{p-1}{(p-1)/2-j}j^{2n}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{(p-1)/2}(-1)^{j}\binom{p-1}{(p-1)/2-j}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle=-\binom{p-2}{(p-1)/2}\,.

The central binomial coefficient modulo prime yields

\displaystyle\binom{p-2}{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(-2)(-3)\cdots(-k-1)}{1\cdot 2\cdots k}=(-1)^{k}(k+1)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\frac{p+1}{2}\pmod{p}\quad\left(k=\frac{p-1}{2}\right)\,.

Thus, when
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, since

\binom{p-2}{(p-1)/2}\equiv\frac{p+1}{2}\pmod{p}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{2(-1)^{n}}{2m+1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j}\binom{2m}{m-j}j^{2n}

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{2(-1)^{n}}{p}\frac{p+1}{2}

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{(-1)^{n}}{p}\pmod{1}\,.

When
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, since

\binom{p-2}{(p-1)/2}\equiv\frac{p-1}{2}\pmod{p}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{2(-1)^{n}}{2m+1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j}\binom{2m}{m-j}j^{2n}

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{2(-1)^{n}}{p}\frac{p-1}{2}

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{p}\pmod{1}\,.

Case 2.2.

If
\frac{p-1}{2}\nmid n
, then by Fermat’s little theorem,

j^{2n}\equiv j^{2n-\nu(p-1)}\pmod{p}\quad(j=1,2,\dots,p-1)

for
0<2n-\nu(p-1)<p-1
with
\nu=\left\lfloor(2n)/(p-1)\right\rfloor
. Notice that
2n-\nu(p-1)
is even.
Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{(p-1)/2}(-1)^{j}\binom{p-1}{(p-1)/2-j}j^{2n}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{(p-1)/2}(-1)^{j}\binom{p-1}{(p-1)/2-j}j^{2n-\nu(p-1)}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{p}\,.

∎

In

, Stirling numbers of both kinds with higher level are discussed. One may wonder if poly-Bernoulli numbers with level

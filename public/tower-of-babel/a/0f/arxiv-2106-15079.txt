\textrm{d}\mu(\zeta)\equiv w(\zeta)\textrm{d}\zeta

and

Similarly, the bi-orthogonality condition (

2.1.
LDU Decomposition of
2j-k
and
j-2k
moment matrices

The linear space of polynomials
\{P_{n}(z)\}^{\infty}_{n=0}
,
\{Q_{n}(z)\}^{\infty}_{n=0}
, etc. have expansions in an appropriate basis,
in this case the monomial basis
\{z^{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=-\infty}
as befits a Páde approximation problem with two fixed singularities

0,\infty
. We are therefore led to consider the linear transformations from this preferred basis to our orthogonal system.
Let us denote the coefficients of
2j-k
and
j-2k
polynomials more precisely as

(2.22)

P_{n}(z;r)=\sum^{n}_{\ell=0}\mathcal{p}^{(r)}_{n,\ell}z^{\ell},\qquad Q_{n}(z;r)=\sum^{n}_{\ell=0}\mathcal{q}^{(r)}_{n,\ell}z^{\ell},\qquad R_{n}(z;s)=\sum^{n}_{\ell=0}\mathcal{r}^{(s)}_{n,\ell}z^{\ell},\qquad S_{n}(z;s)=\sum^{n}_{\ell=0}\mathcal{s}^{(s)}_{n,\ell}z^{\ell}.

with
\mathcal{p}^{(r)}_{n,n}=\mathcal{q}^{(r)}_{n,n}=\mathcal{r}^{(s)}_{n,n}=\mathcal{s}^{(s)}_{n,n}=1
. Let us also denote

(2.23)

\bm{Z}_{n}(z):=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
z\\
\vdots\\
z^{n}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{F}_{n}(z;r):=\begin{pmatrix}F_{0}(z;r)\\
F_{1}(z;r)\\
\vdots\\
F_{n}(z;r)\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bm{F}\in\{\bm{P},\bm{Q},\bm{R},\bm{S}\}.

We thus have

(2.24)

\bm{P}_{n}(z;r)=\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{n}^{(r)}\bm{Z}_{n}(z),\qquad\bm{Q}_{n}(z;r)=\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}^{(r)}\bm{Z}_{n}(z),\qquad\bm{R}_{n}(z;s)=\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{(s)}\bm{Z}_{n}(z),\qquad\bm{S}_{n}(z;s)=\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{(s)}\bm{Z}_{n}(z),

where
\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{n}^{(r)},\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}^{(r)},\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{(s)}
and
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{(s)}
are the following
(n+1)\times(n+1)
lower triangular matrices

(2.25)

\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{n}^{(r)}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\\
\mathcal{p}^{(r)}_{1,0}&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathcal{p}^{(r)}_{n,0}&\mathcal{p}^{(r)}_{n,1}&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}^{(r)}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\\
\mathcal{q}^{(r)}_{1,0}&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathcal{q}^{(r)}_{n,0}&\mathcal{q}^{(r)}_{n,1}&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix},

(2.26)

\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{(s)}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\\
\mathcal{\mathcal{r}}^{(s)}_{1,0}&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathcal{r}^{(s)}_{n,0}&\mathcal{r}^{(s)}_{n,1}&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{(s)}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\\
\mathcal{s}^{(s)}_{1,0}&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathcal{s}^{(s)}_{n,0}&\mathcal{s}^{(s)}_{n,1}&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix},

whose inverses are also lower diagonal with
1
’s on the main diagonal. Also let us denote the diagonal matrices of norms of polynomials by
\bm{h}^{(r)}_{n}
and
\bm{g}^{(r)}_{n}
:

(2.27)

\bm{h}^{(r)}_{n}:=\begin{pmatrix}h^{(r)}_{0}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&h^{(r)}_{n}\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{g}^{(s)}_{n}:=\begin{pmatrix}g^{(s)}_{0}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&g^{(s)}_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

In the following result we give the LDU decompositions of the moment matrices for the
2j-k
and
j-2k
systems which parallels the Toeplitz case closely,
see the unpublished work
[
62
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The LDU decompositions of
\bm{D}^{(r)}_{n+1}
and
\bm{E}^{(s)}_{n+1}
are given by

(2.28)

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{(r)}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\left[\bm{\mathcal{Q}}^{(r)}_{n}\right]^{-1}\bm{h}^{(r)}_{n}\left[\left(\bm{\mathcal{P}}^{(r)}_{n}\right)^{T}\right]^{-1},

(2.29)

\displaystyle\bm{E}^{(s)}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\left[\bm{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}_{n}\right]^{-1}\bm{g}^{(s)}_{n}\left[\left(\bm{\mathcal{R}}^{(s)}_{n}\right)^{T}\right]^{-1}.

Proof.

In this proof we drop the dependence of all functions and quantities on the offsets
r
and
s
for simplicity of notation. We have

(2.30)

\begin{split}h_{\nu}\delta_{\nu\mu}&=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}P_{\nu}(\zeta)Q_{\mu}(\zeta^{-2})\zeta^{-r}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}=\sum_{m=0}^{\nu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu}\mathcal{p}_{\nu,m}\mathcal{q}_{\mu,\ell}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\zeta^{m-2\ell-r}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\nu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu}\mathcal{p}_{\nu,m}\mathcal{q}_{\mu,\ell}w_{2\ell-m+r}=\sum_{m=0}^{\nu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu}\left(\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{n}\right)_{\nu,m}\left(\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}\right)_{\mu,\ell}\left(\bm{D}_{n+1}\right)_{\ell,m}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\nu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu}\left(\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}\right)_{\mu,\ell}\left(\bm{D}_{n+1}\right)_{\ell,m}\left(\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{n}^{T}\right)_{m,\nu}=\left(\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}\bm{D}_{n+1}\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{n}^{T}\right)_{\mu,\nu},\end{split}

which is equivalent to (
2.28
). For the decomposition of
\bm{E}^{(s)}_{n+1}
we consider

(2.31)

\begin{split}g_{\nu}\delta_{\nu\mu}&=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}R_{\nu}(\zeta^{2})S_{\mu}(\zeta^{-1})\zeta^{-s}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}=\sum_{m=0}^{\nu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu}\mathcal{r}_{\nu,m}\mathcal{s}_{\mu,\ell}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\zeta^{2m-\ell-s}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\nu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu}\mathcal{r}_{\nu,m}\mathcal{s}_{\mu,\ell}w_{\ell-2m+s}=\sum_{m=0}^{\nu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu}\left(\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{n}\right)_{\nu,m}\left(\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\right)_{\mu,\ell}\left(\bm{E}_{n+1}\right)_{\ell,m}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\nu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu}\left(\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\right)_{\mu,\ell}\left(\bm{E}_{n+1}\right)_{\ell,m}\left(\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{T}\right)_{m,\nu}=\left(\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\bm{E}_{n+1}\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{T}\right)_{\mu,\nu},\end{split}

which yields (
2.29
).
∎

3.1.
Connection of
2j-k
and
j-2k
polynomials

The
2j-k
and
j-2k
systems are intimately related by a duality through exploiting the freedom to choose suitable offsets.
This equivalence is only exhibited in a formal algebraic rather than an analytical sense and reflects a mapping of the interior of the unit circle to the exterior and vice-versa.
In the Toeplitz case one has a system of self-duality.
Consequently the first of such duality relations involves the determinants, which are related to one another if one selects the appropriate offset values. More precisely, we have

(3.11)

D^{(r)}_{n}=E^{(r+n-1)}_{n},

as these are reflections of each other across the main anti-diagonal. Indeed, for any
n\times n
matrix
\bm{\mathscr{M}}
we have
\det\bm{\mathscr{M}}^{\perp}=\det\bm{\mathscr{M}}
, which is due to the identity
\bm{\mathscr{M}}^{\perp}=\bm{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\bm{\mathscr{M}}^{T}\bm{\mathscr{A}}_{n},

where
\bm{\mathscr{A}}_{n}
is the
n\times n
matrix with ones on the anti-diagonal and zeros everywhere else,
\bm{\mathscr{M}}^{T}
is the transpose of the
n\times n
matrix
\bm{\mathscr{M}}
, and
\bm{\mathscr{M}}^{\perp}
is the reflection of the
n\times n
matrix
\bm{\mathscr{M}}
across its main anti-diagonal. Continuing the development of the duality theme our next result furnishes further details in regard to the polynomials themselves. But first let us recall a standard notation:
for any polynomial
p
of degree
n
, we denote the reciprocal polynomial by
p^{*}
, that is

(3.12)

p^{*}(z):=z^{n}p(z^{-1}).

.

and

(3.36)

\displaystyle K_{n}(z,\mathcal{z};r)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{h^{(r)}_{j}}Q_{j}(z;r)P_{j}(\mathcal{z};r),

(3.37)

\displaystyle L_{n}(z,\mathcal{z};s)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{g^{(s)}_{j}}S_{j}(z;s)R_{j}(\mathcal{z};s).

It is easy to see that
K_{n}(z,\mathcal{z};r)
and
L_{n}(z,\mathcal{z};s)
has the
reproducing properties
as described in the following equations:

(3.38)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}K_{n}(z,\zeta;r)Q_{\ell}(\zeta^{-2};r)\zeta^{-r}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}=\begin{cases}Q_{\ell}(z;r),&0\leq\ell\leq n,\\
0,&\ell>n,\end{cases}

(3.39)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}K_{n}(\zeta^{-2},\mathcal{z};r)P_{\ell}(\zeta;r)\zeta^{-r}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}=\begin{cases}P_{\ell}(\mathcal{z};r),&0\leq\ell\leq n,\\
0,&\ell>n,\end{cases}

(3.40)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}L_{n}(z,\zeta^{2};s)S_{\ell}(\zeta^{-1};s)\zeta^{-s}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}=\begin{cases}S_{\ell}(z;s),&0\leq\ell\leq n,\\
0,&\ell>n,\end{cases}

and

(3.41)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}L_{n}(\zeta^{-1},\mathcal{z};r)R_{\ell}(\zeta^{2};s)\zeta^{-s}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}=\begin{cases}R_{\ell}(\mathcal{z};s),&0\leq\ell\leq n,\\
0,&\ell>n.\end{cases}

Key properties flowing from these definitions are the normalisation relation

(3.42)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}K_{n}(\zeta^{-2},\zeta;r)=n+1,

and the projection relation

(3.43)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}K_{n}(z_{1},\zeta;r)K_{n}(\zeta^{-2},z_{2};r)=K_{n}(z_{1},z_{2};r).

Identical relations apply for the
L_{n}
kernel with the obvious modifications.

The above definitions of the reproducing kernels follows the pattern of the Toeplitz case and the following result linking them to
bordered moment determinants reinforces this similarity.
Although the Toeplitz case is not quoted often it is almost identical after making the obvious modifications.

Theorem 3.4
.

The reproducing kernels
K_{n}(z,\mathcal{z};r)
and
L_{n}(z,\mathcal{z};s)
can be represented in terms of the master determinants
\mathscr{D}_{r}(z;\mathcal{z})
and
\mathscr{E}_{s}(z;\mathcal{z})
as

(3.44)

\displaystyle z^{n+1}K_{n+1}(z^{-1},\mathcal{z};r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathscr{D}_{r}(z;\mathcal{z})}{D^{(r)}_{n+2}},

(3.45)

\displaystyle z^{n+1}L_{n+1}(z^{-1},\mathcal{z};s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathscr{E}_{s}(z;\mathcal{z})}{E^{(s)}_{n+2}}.

Proof.

We only give the proof for (
3.44
), as (
3.45
) can be obtained in a similar way. Define

(3.46)

\widehat{K}_{n}(z,\mathcal{z};r):=-\frac{1}{D^{(r)}_{n+1}}\det\begin{pmatrix}w_{r}&w_{r-1}&\cdots&w_{r-n}&1\\
w_{r+2}&w_{r+1}&\cdots&w_{r-n+2}&z\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
w_{r+2n}&w_{r+2n-1}&\cdots&w_{r+n}&z^{n}\\
1&\mathcal{z}&\cdots&\mathcal{z}^{n}&0\end{pmatrix}.

One can easily establish that
\widehat{K}
has the same reproducing properties as
K
. To this end, its action on monomials is given by

(3.47)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\widehat{K}_{n}(z,\zeta;r)\zeta^{-2\ell}\zeta^{-r}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}=z^{\ell},\quad 0\leq\ell\leq n,

and

(3.48)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\widehat{K}_{n}(\zeta^{-2},\mathcal{z};r)\zeta^{\ell}\zeta^{-r}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}=\mathcal{z}^{\ell},\quad 0\leq\ell\leq n.

Therefore, we immediately get

(3.49)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\widehat{K}_{n}(z,\zeta;r)Q_{\ell}(\zeta^{-2};r)\zeta^{-r}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}=Q_{\ell}(z;r),\quad 0\leq\ell\leq n,

and

(3.50)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\widehat{K}_{n}(\zeta^{-2},\mathcal{z};r)P_{\ell}(\zeta;r)\zeta^{-r}w(\zeta)\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}=P_{\ell}(\mathcal{z};r),\quad 0\leq\ell\leq n.

Now we show that

(3.51)

\widehat{K}_{n}(z,\mathcal{z};r)=K_{n}(z,\mathcal{z};r),

for all
z,\mathcal{z}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let us consider the following
(n+2)\times(n+2)
matrices

(3.52)

\bm{\widehat{K}_{n}}(z,\zeta;r)=\begin{pmatrix}w_{r}&w_{r-1}&\cdots&w_{r-n}&1\\
w_{r+2}&w_{r+1}&\cdots&w_{r-n+2}&z\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
w_{r+2n}&w_{r+2n-1}&\cdots&w_{r+n}&z^{n}\\
1&\zeta&\cdots&\zeta^{n}&0\end{pmatrix},

(3.53)

\bm{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}^{(r)}_{n}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
p^{(r)}_{1,0}&1&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
p^{(r)}_{n,0}&p^{(r)}_{n,1}&\cdots&1&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}}^{(r)}_{n}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
q^{(r)}_{1,0}&1&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
q^{(r)}_{n,0}&q^{(r)}_{n,1}&\cdots&1&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0&1\end{pmatrix},

Now observe that

(3.54)

\begin{split}\bm{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}}^{(r)}_{n}\bm{\widehat{K}_{n}}(z,\zeta;r)\left(\bm{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}^{(r)}_{n}\right)^{T}&=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\mathcal{Q}}^{(r)}_{n}&\bm{0}_{n+1}\\
\bm{0}^{T}_{n+1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{D}_{n+1}&\bm{Z}_{n}(z)\\
\bm{Z}^{T}_{n}(\zeta)&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\left(\bm{\mathcal{P}}^{(r)}_{n}\right)^{T}&\bm{0}_{n+1}\\
\bm{0}^{T}_{n+1}&1\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\mathcal{Q}}^{(r)}_{n}\bm{D}_{n+1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{P}}^{(r)}_{n}\right)^{T}&\bm{\mathcal{Q}}^{(r)}_{n}\bm{Z}_{n}(z)\\[5.0pt]
\bm{Z}^{T}_{n}(\zeta)\left(\bm{\mathcal{P}}^{(r)}_{n}\right)^{T}&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{h}^{(r)}_{n}&\bm{Q}_{n}(z;r)\\
\bm{P}^{T}_{n}(\zeta;r)&0\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

where the objects in the above equation are defined in subsection
2.1
.
Taking the determinant of both sides of the above equation yields (
3.51
). Finally by combining (
3.51
) and (
3.46
) and recalling (
2.3
) we obtain (
3.44
).
∎

Theorem 4.1
.

The third order pure-degree recurrence relations for the

(4.1)

and

(4.2)

\begin{split}Q^{*}_{n+3}(z;r)&-(1+\beta^{(r)}_{n+2}z)Q^{*}_{n+2}(z;r)+(\beta^{(r)}_{n+1}+\alpha^{(r+1)}_{n+1}+\beta^{(r+1)}_{n+1}+\alpha^{(r+2)}_{n+1})zQ^{*}_{n+1}(z;r)\\
&-(\beta^{(r+1)}_{n+1}+\alpha^{(r+2)}_{n+1})(\beta^{(r)}_{n}+\alpha^{(r+1)}_{n})z^{2}Q^{*}_{n}(z;r)=0,\end{split}

where

(4.3)

\displaystyle\delta^{(r)}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{h^{(r-1)}_{n}}{h^{(r)}_{n}},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\eta^{(r)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{n}^{(r+2)}D_{n+1}^{(r-1)}}{D^{(r)}_{n+1}D^{(r+1)}_{n}},

(4.5)

\displaystyle\beta^{(r)}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{h^{(r+2)}_{n}}{h^{(r)}_{n}},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\alpha^{(r)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{D^{(r-1)}_{n}D^{(r+2)}_{n+1}}{D^{(r)}_{n+1}D^{(r+1)}_{n}}.

Proof.

Let us consider the following Dodgson condensation identities (see (
2.2
)):

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1\\
n+2\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n&n+1&n+2\\
0&n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}=\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n&n+1\\
0&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}-\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n&n+1\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
0&n+2\end{matrix}\right\},\end{split}

(4.8)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1\\
n+2\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+1&n+2\\

0&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}-\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+1\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
0&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}.\end{split}

The identity (
4.7
) can be written as

(4.9)

\frac{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1\\
n+2\end{matrix}\right\}}{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n&n+1&n+2\\

0&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}-\frac{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n&n+1\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}}{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
0&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}.

So from (
3.16
), (
3.17
), (
3.19
), and (
2.9
) we have

(4.10)

D^{(r-1)}_{n}P_{n+1}(z;r)=D^{(r-1)}_{n}zP_{n}(z;r-1)-\frac{D_{n}^{(r)}D_{n+1}^{(r-1)}}{D_{n+1}^{(r)}}P_{n}(z;r).

Therefore from (
3.3
) we have

(4.11)

P_{n+1}(z;r)=zP_{n}(z;r-1)+\delta^{(r)}_{n}P_{n}(z;r),\qquad\delta^{(r)}_{n}=-\frac{h^{(r-1)}_{n}}{h^{(r)}_{n}}.

Let us also write (
4.8
) as

(4.12)

\frac{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1\\
n+2\end{matrix}\right\}}{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+1&n+2\\

0&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}-\frac{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+1\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}}{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
0&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}.

Therefore from (
3.16
), (
3.18
), (
3.24
), and (
2.9
) we arrive at

(4.13)

D^{(r+1)}_{n}P_{n+1}(z;r)=D^{(r+1)}_{n}zP_{n}(z;r+1)-\frac{D_{n}^{(r+2)}D_{n+1}^{(r-1)}}{D^{(r)}_{n+1}}P_{n}(z;r+2),

and thus

(4.14)

P_{n+1}(z;r)=zP_{n}(z;r+1)-\eta^{(r)}_{n}P_{n}(z;r+2),\qquad\eta^{(r)}_{n}=\frac{D_{n}^{(r+2)}D_{n+1}^{(r-1)}}{D^{(r)}_{n+1}D^{(r+1)}_{n}}.

From (
4.11

(4.15)

zP_{n}(z;r-1)=P_{n+1}(z;r)-\delta_{n}^{(r)}P_{n}(z;r).

From this, we immediately get

(4.16)

zP_{n}(z;r-2)=P_{n+1}(z;r-1)-\delta_{n}^{(r-1)}P_{n}(z;r-1).

Using (
4.15
) again we get

(4.17)

zP_{n}(z;r-2)=z^{-1}[P_{n+2}(z;r)-\delta_{n+1}^{(r)}P_{n+1}(z;r)]-\delta_{n}^{(r-1)}z^{-1}[P_{n+1}(z;r)-\delta_{n}^{(r)}P_{n}(z;r)],

and therefore

(4.18)

zP_{n}(z;r-2)=z^{-1}P_{n+2}(z;r)-z^{-1}(\delta_{n+1}^{(r)}+\delta_{n}^{(r-1)})P_{n+1}(z;r)+\delta_{n}^{(r-1)}\delta_{n}^{(r)}z^{-1}P_{n}(z;r).

On the other hand, from (
4.14
) we have

(4.19)

P_{n+1}(z;r-2)=zP_{n}(z;r-1)-\eta^{(r-2)}_{n}P_{n}(z;r).

Combining the last two equations with (

) and some simplification gives (
4.1
).

In order to prove (
4.2
) we need to consider the following Dodgson condensation identities:

(4.20)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+2\\
n+1\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+1&n+2\\
n&n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}=\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+2\\
n&n+1\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}-\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n&n+1\end{matrix}\right\},\end{split}

(4.21)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+2\\
n+1\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+1&n+2\\
0&n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}=\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+2\\
0&n+1\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}-\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
0&n+1\end{matrix}\right\}.\end{split}

Let us write (
4.20
) as

(4.22)

\frac{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+2\\
n+1\end{matrix}\right\}}{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+1&n+2\\

n&n+1\end{matrix}\right\}}{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}}\cdot\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}0&n+2\\

So from (

), (

D^{(r+2)}_{n}Q^{*}_{n+1}(z;r)=D^{(r+2)}_{n}Q^{*}_{n}(z;r+2)-\frac{D^{(r)}_{n}}{D^{(r)}_{n+1}}D^{(r+2)}_{n+1}zQ^{*}_{n}(z;r),

Now, let us write (

(4.25)

\frac{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+2\\
n+1\end{matrix}\right\}}{\mathscr{D}_{r}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\

), (

), (

D^{(r+1)}_{n}Q^{*}_{n+1}(z;r)=D^{(r+1)}_{n}Q^{*}_{n}(z;r+1)-\frac{D^{(r-1)}_{n}D^{(r+2)}_{n+1}}{D^{(r)}_{n+1}}zQ^{*}_{n}(z;r-1),

and thus

(4.27)

Q^{*}_{n+1}(z;r)=Q^{*}_{n}(z;r+1)-\alpha^{(r)}_{n}zQ^{*}_{n}(z;r-1),\qquad\alpha^{(r)}_{n}=\frac{D^{(r-1)}_{n}D^{(r+2)}_{n+1}}{D^{(r)}_{n+1}D^{(r+1)}_{n}}.

Replacing

for the

Theorem 4.2
.

The third order pure-degree recurrence relations for the
j-2k
polynomials are given by

(4.34)

\begin{split}R_{n+3}(z;s)&-\left(z+\varkappa^{(s)}_{n+2}\right)R_{n+2}(z;s)+\left(\varkappa^{(s)}_{n+1}+\rho^{(s-1)}_{n+1}+\rho^{(s)}_{n+2}+\varkappa^{(s)}_{n+2}\right)zR_{n+1}(z;s)\\
&-\left(\rho^{(s)}_{n+2}+\varkappa^{(s)}_{n+2}\right)\left(\varkappa^{(s)}_{n}+\rho^{(s-1)}_{n}\right)zR_{n}(z;s)=0,\end{split}

and

(4.35)

S^{*}_{n+3}(z;s)-\left(\gamma^{(s)}_{n+2}+\gamma^{(s+1)}_{n+1}\right)zS^{*}_{n+2}(z;s)-\left(1-\gamma^{(s+1)}_{n+1}\gamma^{(s)}_{n+1}z^{2}\right)S^{*}_{n+1}(z;s)+\left(\theta^{(s+1)}_{n+1}+\gamma^{(s+1)}_{n+1}\right)zS^{*}_{n}(z;s)=0.

where

(4.36)

\displaystyle\varkappa^{(s)}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{g^{(s-2)}_{n}}{g^{(s)}_{n}},

(4.37)

\displaystyle\rho^{(s)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{n}^{(s+1)}E_{n+1}^{(s-2)}}{E^{(s)}_{n+1}E^{(s-1)}_{n}},

(4.38)

\displaystyle\gamma^{(s)}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{g^{(s+1)}_{n}}{g^{(s)}_{n}},

(4.39)

\displaystyle\theta^{(s)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{E^{(s-2)}_{n}E^{(s+1)}_{n+1}}{E^{(s)}_{n+1}E^{(s-1)}_{n}}.

Proof.

In order to prove (
4.34
) we use the following Dodgson condensation identities

(4.40)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{E}_{s}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1\\
n+2\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{E}_{s}\left\{\begin{matrix}n&n+1&n+2\\
0&n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}=\mathscr{E}_{s}\left\{\begin{matrix}n&n+1\\
0&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{E}_{s}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}-\mathscr{E}_{s}\left\{\begin{matrix}n&n+1\\
n+1&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}\cdot\mathscr{E}_{s}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1&n+2\\
0&n+2\end{matrix}\right\}.\end{split}

(4.41)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{E}_{s}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1\\

4.2.
Relationships of polynomial tails, recurrence coefficients and determinants

There are many redundancies amongst the various coefficients that have been defined thus far.
In this subsection we provide the necessary relationships between these coefficients to remove the redundancy,
but also to link these to certain polynomial coefficients, in particular the tail coefficients,
and to the determinants and thus the norms of the polynomials. Such relations form an algorithmic path to compute these norms.

Lemma 4.5
.

There exist interrelationships between the recurrence coefficients
\alpha^{(r)}_{n}
,
\beta^{(r)}_{n}
,
\delta^{(r)}_{n}
, and
\eta^{(r)}_{n}
and also between
\varkappa^{(s)}_{n}
,
\rho^{(s)}_{n}
,
\gamma^{(s)}_{n}
, and

as described by

(4.113)

\displaystyle\delta^{(r)}_{n}\alpha^{(r)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\beta^{(r)}_{n}\eta^{(r)}_{n},

(4.114)

\displaystyle\varkappa^{(s)}_{n}\theta^{(s)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{(s)}_{n}\rho^{(s)}_{n}.

Proof.

These relationships simply follow from the definitions of the recurrence relations in terms of the
2j-k
and
j-2k
determinants.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

The
2j-k
and the
j-2k

(4.115)

\displaystyle\beta^{(r)}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\delta^{(r+1)}_{n}\delta^{(r+2)}_{n}},

(4.116)

\displaystyle\alpha^{(r)}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\eta^{(r)}_{n}}{\delta^{(r)}_{n}\delta^{(r+1)}_{n}\delta^{(r+2)}_{n}},

(4.117)

\displaystyle\varkappa^{(s)}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\gamma^{(s-1)}_{n}\gamma^{(s-2)}_{n}},

(4.118)

\displaystyle\rho^{(s)}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\theta^{(s)}_{n}}{\gamma^{(s)}_{n}\gamma^{(s-1)}_{n}\gamma^{(s-2)}_{n}}.

Proof.

From (

) we have

(4.119)

\beta^{(r)}_{n}=-\frac{h^{(r+2)}_{n}}{h^{(r)}_{n}}=-\frac{h^{(r+1)}_{n}}{h^{(r)}_{n}}\frac{h^{(r+2)}_{n}}{h^{(r+1)}_{n}}=-\frac{1}{\delta^{(r+1)}_{n}\delta^{(r+2)}_{n}},

by the definition of
\delta^{(r)}_{n}
in (

(4.120)

\alpha^{(r)}_{n}=\frac{D^{(r-1)}_{n}D^{(r+2)}_{n+1}}{D^{(r)}_{n+1}D^{(r+1)}_{n}}=\frac{D^{(r-1)}_{n}D^{(r+2)}_{n}}{D^{(r)}_{n}D^{(r+1)}_{n}}\frac{h^{(r+2)}_{n}}{h^{(r)}_{n}}.

Also from (

(4.121)

\eta^{(r)}_{n}=\frac{D_{n}^{(r+2)}D_{n+1}^{(r-1)}}{D^{(r)}_{n+1}D^{(r+1)}_{n}}=\frac{D_{n}^{(r+2)}D_{n}^{(r-1)}}{D^{(r)}_{n}D^{(r+1)}_{n}}\frac{h^{(r-1)}_{n}}{h^{(r)}_{n}}.

Combining the last two equations gives

(4.122)

\alpha^{(r)}_{n}=\frac{h^{(r+2)}_{n}}{h^{(r-1)}_{n}}\eta^{(r)}_{n}=\frac{h^{(r+2)}_{n}}{h^{(r+1)}_{n}}\frac{h^{(r+1)}_{n}}{h^{(r)}_{n}}\frac{h^{(r)}_{n}}{h^{(r-1)}_{n}}\eta^{(r)}_{n}=-\frac{\eta^{(r)}_{n}}{\delta^{(r)}_{n}\delta^{(r+1)}_{n}\delta^{(r+2)}_{n}},

again by the definition of
\delta^{(r)}_{n}
in (
4.11
). The relations (
4.117
) and (
4.118

∎

Theorem 4.10

The

5.
Multiple integral representations

The joint eigenvalue PDF of (
1.9
) is fundamental from our viewpoint and it is from this that we construct various marginal distributions,
otherwise known as
n
-particle correlation functions, through integration over a set of ”internal” variables,
the complement of
n
”external” variables.
All aspects of the theory will have representations of this form: the determinants, the bi-orthogonal polynomials and the reproducing kernels.
Thus our first result of this nature is the
0
-point correlation or normalisation integral.
This is the extension of the Toeplitz result, Eq.(2.2), of
[
35
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The moment determinants, and therefore the normalisation of the bi-orthogonal polynomials, possess the multiple integral representations

(5.1)

D_{n}^{(r)}=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{2}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r}_{j}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j}),

and

(5.2)

E_{n}^{(s)}=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{2}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-s}_{j}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta^{2}_{k}-\zeta^{2}_{j})(\zeta^{-1}_{k}-\zeta^{-1}_{j}).

Proof.

We have

(5.3)

\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})=\underset{1\leq j,k\leq n}{\det}\{\zeta^{k-1}_{j}\}=\underset{1\leq j,k\leq n}{\det}\{P_{k-1}(\zeta_{j})\},

and

(5.4)

\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j})=\underset{1\leq j,k\leq n}{\det}\{\zeta^{-2(k-1)}_{j}\}=\underset{1\leq j,k\leq n}{\det}\{Q_{k-1}(\zeta^{-2}_{j})\}.

So

(5.5)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{2}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r}_{j}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j})\\
&=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{2}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r}_{j}\underset{1\leq j,k\leq n}{\det}\{P_{k-1}(\zeta_{j})\}\underset{1\leq j,k\leq n}{\det}\{Q_{k-1}(\zeta^{-2}_{j})\}\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{2}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r}_{j}\underset{1\leq j,k\leq n}{\det}\{P_{k-1}(\zeta_{j})\}\prod_{j=1}^{n}Q_{j-1}(\zeta^{-2}_{j})\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{2}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r}_{j}\underset{1\leq j,k\leq n}{\det}\{P_{k-1}(\zeta_{j})Q_{j-1}(\zeta^{-2}_{j})\}\\
&=\underset{1\leq j,k\leq n}{\det}\left\{\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}P_{k-1}(\zeta)Q_{j-1}(\zeta^{-2})\right\}=\underset{0\leq j,k\leq n-1}{\det}\left\{h^{(r)}_{j}\delta_{j,k}\right\}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}h^{(r)}_{j}=D^{(r)}_{n},\end{split}

where we have used (
2.16
) and (
3.3
). The formula (
5.2
) can be established similarly using (
2.17
) and (
3.9
).
∎

So, in particular we obtain (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) in view of (
2.12
), (
2.13
), (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). The relationship (

) can be also found using the multiple integral formulae above. To that end notice that

(5.6)

(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j})=\frac{1}{\zeta_{k}\zeta_{j}}(\zeta^{-1}_{k}-\zeta^{-1}_{j})(\zeta^{2}_{k}-\zeta^{2}_{j}).

Therefore

(5.7)

\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j})=\psi\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta^{-1}_{k}-\zeta^{-1}_{j})(\zeta^{2}_{k}-\zeta^{2}_{j}),

where

\psi=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\frac{1}{\zeta_{k}\zeta_{j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\zeta^{1-n}_{j},

because

(5.9)

\psi^{2}=\prod_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}\frac{1}{\zeta_{k}\zeta_{j}}=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq j,k\leq n}\frac{1}{\zeta_{k}\zeta_{j}}}{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq j=k\leq n}\frac{1}{\zeta_{k}\zeta_{j}}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\zeta^{2}_{j}\prod_{1\leq j,k\leq n}\zeta^{-1}_{k}\zeta^{-1}_{j}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\zeta^{2-2n}_{j}.

Note that

(5.10)

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}]&=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{2}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r}_{j}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j})\\
&=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{2}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{1-n-r}_{j}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta^{-1}_{k}-\zeta^{-1}_{j})(\zeta^{2}_{k}-\zeta^{2}_{j})\\
&=\mathcal{E}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-(n+r-1)}],\end{split}

which is equivalent to (
3.11
). More generally we have the following result.

Theorem 5.2
.

For any integrable function
f
we have:

(5.11)

\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}f(\zeta)]=\mathcal{E}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-n-r+1}f(\zeta)],

or equivalently

(5.12)

\mathcal{E}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-s}f(\zeta)]=\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-s-1+n}f(\zeta)].

5.1.
Multiple integral formulae for the bi-orthogonal polynomials

It is well known that the bi-orthogonal polynomials in the Toeplitz system can be written as averages of characteristic polynomials over the unitary group,
see Eq.(2.16,18) of
[
35
]
. In the following Theorem we give the analogous results for the
2j-k
and
j-2k
polynomials.
Another interpretation of such integrals is as rational modifications of the weight, known as Christoffel-Darboux-Uvarov transformations of the underlying
bi-orthogonal system in the Toeplitz case, which was exhaustively treated in
[
72
]
.

Theorem 5.3
.

We have the following multiple integral representations for
2j-k
and
j-2k
polynomials:

(5.13)

\displaystyle P_{n}(z;r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D^{(r)}_{n}}\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}(z-\zeta)],

(5.14)

\displaystyle Q_{n}(z;r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D^{(r)}_{n}}\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}(z-\zeta^{-2})],

(5.15)

\displaystyle R_{n}(z;s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E^{(s)}_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-s}(z-\zeta^{2})],

(5.16)

\displaystyle S_{n}(z;s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E^{(s)}_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-s}(z-\zeta^{-1})].

Proof.

We only prove the formula for
P_{n}
as the other ones can be found similarly.

(5.17)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}(z-\zeta)]\\
&=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r}_{j}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-\zeta_{j})\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j})\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r}_{j}\prod_{0\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j}),\end{split}

where we have denoted
z
by
\zeta_{0}
. Therefore

(5.18)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}(z-\zeta)]\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r}_{j}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n+1\end{subarray}}{\det}\begin{pmatrix}P_{k-1}(\zeta_{0})\\
P_{k-1}(\zeta_{j})\end{pmatrix}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n\end{subarray}}{\det}\{Q_{k-1}(\zeta^{-2}_{j})\}\\
&=(-1)^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r}_{j}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n+1\end{subarray}}{\det}\begin{pmatrix}P_{k-1}(\zeta_{0})\\
P_{k-1}(\zeta_{j})\end{pmatrix}\prod_{j=1}^{n}Q_{j-1}(\zeta^{-2}_{j})\\
&=(-1)^{n}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n+1\end{subarray}}{\det}\begin{pmatrix}P_{k-1}(\zeta_{0})\\
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}P_{k-1}(\zeta)Q_{j-1}(\zeta^{-2})\end{pmatrix}\\
&=(-1)^{n}\det\begin{pmatrix}P_{0}(z)&\cdots&P_{n-1}(z)&P_{n}(z)\\
h^{(r)}_{0}&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&h^{(r)}_{n-1}&0\end{pmatrix}=P_{n}(z;r)\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}h^{(r)}_{j}=D^{(r)}_{n}P_{n}(z;r).\end{split}

∎

5.2.
Multiple integral formulae for the reproducing kernels

We will require the following important lemma, which is known as the ”integration over successive variables”, in a subsequent result.
This is the extension of the result in the Toeplitz case, which is easy to derive following the line of Proposition 5.1.2 of
[
30
]
.

Lemma 5.4

(Gaudin’s Theorem)
.

The integration over the final variable
\zeta_{m}
of the
m\times m
determinant constructed from reproducing kernel entries has the evaluation

(5.19)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{m}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{m}}w(\zeta_{m})\zeta^{-r}_{m}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k},\zeta_{j};r)\right]=(n-m+2)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m-1\\
1\leq k\leq m-1\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k},\zeta_{j};r)\right],

and

(5.20)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{m}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{m}}w(\zeta_{m})\zeta^{-s}_{m}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[L_{n}(\zeta^{-1}_{k},\zeta^{2}_{j};s)\right]=(n-m+2)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m-1\\
1\leq k\leq m-1\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[L_{n}(\zeta^{-1}_{k},\zeta^{2}_{j};s)\right].

Proof.

Let us use
j
and
k
respectively as indices of rows and columns. Expanding the determinant in the integrand along the last row gives

(5.21)

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k},\zeta_{j};r)\right]=\sum^{m-1}_{k=1}(-1)^{m+k}K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k},\zeta_{m};r)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m-1\\
1\leq\ell\leq m\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{\ell},\zeta_{j};r)\right]+K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{m},\zeta_{m};r)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m-1\\
1\leq k\leq m-1\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k},\zeta_{j};r)\right]

(5.22)

=\sum^{m-1}_{k=1}(-1)^{m+k}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m-1\\
1\leq\ell\leq m-1\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{\ell},\zeta_{j};r),K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k},\zeta_{m};r)K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{m},\zeta_{j};r)\right]+K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{m},\zeta_{m};r)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m-1\\
1\leq k\leq m-1\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k},\zeta_{j};r)\right].

Recalling the normalisation (
3.42
) and the projection relations (
3.43
) for the reproducing kernel we deduce

(5.23)

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{m}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{m}}w(\zeta_{m})\zeta^{-r}_{m}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k},\zeta_{j};r)\right]&=\sum^{m-1}_{k=1}(-1)^{m+k}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m-1\\
1\leq\ell\leq m-1\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{\ell},\zeta_{j};r),K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k},\zeta_{j};r)\right]\\
&+(n+1)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m-1\\
1\leq k\leq m-1\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k},\zeta_{j};r)\right],\end{split}

By
m-k-1
successive swaps of adjacent columns we have

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m-1\\
1\leq\ell\leq m-1\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{\ell},\zeta_{j};r),K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k},\zeta_{j};r)\right]=(-1)^{m-k-1}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m-1\\
1\leq\ell\leq m-1\end{subarray}}{\det}\left[K_{n}(\zeta^{-2}_{\ell},\zeta_{j};r)\right].

Equation (
5.19
) now follows by combining the last two equations. The proof of (
5.20
) is similar and we do not provide the details here.
∎

\mathscr{P}^{(\hat{r})}_{n}:=\{P_{m}(z;\hat{r})\}^{n}_{m=0},\qquad\mathscr{Q}^{(\hat{r})}_{n}:=\{Q_{m}(z;\hat{r})\}^{n}_{m=0},\qquad\mathscr{R}^{(\hat{s})}_{n}:=\{R_{m}(z;\hat{s})\}^{n}_{m=0},\qquad\mathscr{S}^{(\hat{s})}_{n}:=\{S_{m}(z;\hat{s})\}^{n}_{m=0}.

3.1

3.2
). The following theorem characterizes the number of polynomials needed to represent

and

respectively in terms of polynomials in the bases
\mathscr{P}^{(\hat{r})}_{n}
,
\mathscr{Q}^{(\hat{r})}_{n}
,

, and
\mathscr{S}^{(\hat{s})}_{n}

and

Theorem 5.8
.

Assume the non-vanishing requirements of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
so that the polynomial bases
\mathscr{P}^{(\hat{r})}_{n}
,
\mathscr{Q}^{(\hat{r})}_{n}
,
\mathscr{R}^{(\hat{s})}_{n}
, and
\mathscr{S}^{(\hat{s})}_{n}
are well-defined as in (
5.43
) and let

. Then

1.

If it is desired to express

as a linear combination of polynomials in the basis
\mathscr{P}^{(r-2m)}_{n}

n+1
polynomials in
\mathscr{P}^{(r-2m)}_{n}
. While if it is desired to express
P_{n}(z;r)
in terms of polynomials in the basis
\mathscr{P}^{(r+2m)}_{n}
, then one needs to only use the following
m+1
polynomials in the subset

\left\{P_{n}(z;r+2m),P_{n-1}(z;r+2m),\cdots,P_{n-m}(z;r+2m)\right\}\subset\mathscr{P}^{(r+2m)}_{n}.

If it is desired to express
zP_{n}(z;r)
as a linear combination of polynomials in
\mathscr{P}^{(r-2m+1)}_{n+1}

n+2
polynomials in
\mathscr{P}^{(r-2m+1)}_{n+1}
. While if it is desired to express
zP_{n}(z;r)
in terms of polynomials in
\mathscr{P}^{(r+2m-1)}_{n+1}

m+1
polynomials in the subset

n+1
polynomials in
\mathscr{Q}^{(r+m)}_{n}
. While if it is desired to express
Q_{n}(z;r)
in terms of polynomials in
\mathscr{Q}^{(r-m)}_{n}

m+1
polynomials in the subset

\{Q_{n}(z;r-m),Q_{n-1}(z;r-m),\cdots,Q_{n-m}(z;r-m)\}\subset\mathscr{Q}^{(r-m)}_{n}.

If it is desired to express

n+1
polynomials in
\mathscr{R}^{(s-m)}_{n}

m+1
polynomials in the subset

If it is desired to express

\mathscr{S}^{(s+2m)}_{n}

n+1
polynomials in
\mathscr{S}^{(s+2m)}_{n}

\mathscr{S}^{(s-2m)}_{n}

m+1
polynomials in the subset

\{S_{n}(z;s-2m),S_{n-1}(z;s-2m),\cdots,S_{n-m}(z;s-2m)\}\subset\mathscr{S}^{(s-2m)}_{n}.

6.

If it is desired to express
zS_{n}(z;s)
as a linear combination of polynomials in
\mathscr{S}^{(s+2m-1)}_{n+1}
, then one needs to use all
n+2
polynomials in
\mathscr{S}^{(s+2m-1)}_{n+1}

zS_{n}(z;s)
in terms of polynomials in
\mathscr{P}^{(s-2m+1)}_{n+1}
, then one needs to only use the
m+1
polynomials in the subset

\{S_{n+1}(z;s-2m+1),S_{n}(z;s-2m+1),\cdots,S_{n+1-m}(z;s-2m+1)\}\subset\mathscr{S}^{(s-2m+1)}_{n+1}.

Proof.

Theorem
5.7
establishes the statement of this theorem for expressing
P_{n}(z,r),\ Q_{n}(z,r),\ R_{n}(z,s)
and
S_{n}(z,s)
respectively in terms of polynomials in
\mathscr{P}^{(r-2)}_{n}
,
\mathscr{Q}^{(r+1)}_{n}
,
\mathscr{R}^{(s-1)}_{n}
, and
\mathscr{S}^{(s+2)}_{n}
, and the Corollary
5.7.1
establishes this for expressing
P_{n}(z,r),\ Q_{n}(z,r),\ R_{n}(z,s)
and
S_{n}(z,s)
respectively in terms of polynomials in
\mathscr{P}^{(r+2)}_{n}
,
\mathscr{Q}^{(r-1)}_{n}
,
\mathscr{R}^{(s+1)}_{n}
, and
\mathscr{S}^{(s-2)}_{n}
. The proof for general
m
follows from iterations of the aforementioned results of Theorem
5.7
and Corollary
5.7.1
, and the non-vanishing determinant requirements of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
ensure that the coefficients involved all exist and are non-zero. Let us now prove the first item for expressing
P_{n}(z;r)
in the basis
\mathscr{P}^{(r-2m)}_{n}
via induction. The base of the induction for
m=1
clearly holds because of (
5.28
). Passing from the induction hypothesis to the desired result is obvious because by (
5.28
) we have
P_{\ell}(z;r-2(m-1))=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\ell}\hat{A}_{\nu,\ell}P_{\nu}(z;r-2m)
, where the constants
\hat{A}_{\nu,\ell}
are also described in (
5.28
). Thus the induction hypothesis
P_{n}(z;r)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}A_{m,n}P_{\ell}(z;r-2(m-1))
, immediately implies the desired result. To prove the first item for expressing
P_{n}(z;r)
in the basis
\mathscr{P}^{(r+2m)}_{n}
we use induction again. The base of the induction clearly holds due to (
5.39
). Now the induction hypothesis

(5.44)

P_{n}(z;r)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}B_{m-1,n}P_{n-\ell}(z;r+2(m-1)),

in conjunction with

(5.45)

P_{n-\ell}(z;r+2(m-1))=P_{n-\ell}(z;r+2m)+\frac{h^{(r+2(m-1))}_{n-\ell}}{h^{(r+2m)}_{n-\ell-1}}P_{n-\ell-1}(z;r+2m),

yields the desired result. The proofs of the other items follow similarly, where the only extra needed relations are

(5.46)

\displaystyle zP_{n}(z;r)

\displaystyle=P_{n+1}(z;r+1)-\delta_{n}^{(r+1)}P_{n}(z;r+1),

(5.47)

\displaystyle zS_{n}(z;s)

\displaystyle=S_{n+1}(z;s-1)-\gamma_{n}^{(s-1)}S_{n}(z;s-1).

which are obtained from (
4.11
) and (
4.63
) respectively. ∎

In the Toeplitz case there exist relations which allow one of the polynomials to be expressed in terms of a linear combination of two of the other one,
see Eq.(2.21,22) of
[
35
]
. These two relations are also the components of the first order recurrence, Eq.(2.70), in the matrix variable, Eq.(2.69).
Furthermore these two relations are somewhat symmetrical. However for the present system we find the following single, unsymmetrical relation,
of
Q_{n}
in terms of a bilinear combination of the
P_{n}
, and the analogous relation for the
R_{n},S_{n}
system.

Theorem 5.9
.

It holds that

(5.48)

Q_{n}(z^{2};r)=\frac{D^{(r+2)}_{n}}{D^{(r)}_{n}}\frac{z^{2n+1}}{2}\left[P_{n+1}(z^{-1};r+2)P_{n}(-z^{-1};r+2)-P_{n+1}(-z^{-1};r+2)P_{n}(z^{-1};r+2)\right],

(5.49)

R_{n}(z^{2};s)=\frac{E^{(s-2)}_{n}}{E^{(s)}_{n}}\frac{z^{2n+1}}{2}\left[S_{n+1}(z^{-1};s-2)S_{n}(-z^{-1};s-2)-S_{n+1}(-z^{-1};s-2)S_{n}(z^{-1};s-2)\right].

Proof.

We only prove (
5.48
) as (
5.49
) can be proven in a similar way. From (
5.14
) we have

(5.50)

Q_{n}(z^{2};r)=\frac{1}{D^{(r)}_{n}}\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}(z^{2}-\zeta^{-2})].

Using
z-\zeta^{-1}=-\zeta^{-1}z(z^{-1}-\zeta)
,
z+\zeta^{-1}=-\zeta^{-1}z(-z^{-1}-\zeta)
, (
5.50
) and (
2.12
) we have

(5.51)

\begin{split}&D^{(r)}_{n}Q_{n}(z^{2};r)\\
&=\frac{z^{2n}}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r-2}_{j}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z^{-1}-\zeta_{j})(-z^{-1}-\zeta_{j})\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j}).\end{split}

Now, we temporarily denote
z^{-1}
and
-z^{-1}
respectively by
\zeta_{n+1}
and
\zeta_{n+2}
, and thus

(5.52)

\begin{split}&D^{(r)}_{n}Q_{n}(z^{2};r)\\
&=\frac{z^{2n}}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r-2}_{j}\frac{1}{\zeta_{n+2}-\zeta_{n+1}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n+2}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j})\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{z^{2n+1}}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r-2}_{j}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n+2}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j})\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{z^{2n+1}}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r-2}_{j}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n+2\end{subarray}}{\det}\begin{pmatrix}P_{k-1}(\zeta_{j};r+2)\\
P_{k-1}(z^{-1};r+2)\\
P_{k-1}(-z^{-1};r+2)\end{pmatrix}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n\end{subarray}}{\det}\{Q_{k-1}(\zeta^{-2}_{j};r+2)\}.\end{split}

We have used the offsets to be
r+2
,
due to presence of
\zeta^{-r-2}_{j}
and the fact that we are about to use the bi-orthogonality property of
P
’s and
Q
’s.
We have

(5.53)

\begin{split}&D^{(r)}_{n}Q_{n}(z^{2};r)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}z^{2n+1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r-2}_{j}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n+2\end{subarray}}{\det}\begin{pmatrix}P_{k-1}(\zeta_{j};r+2)\\
P_{k-1}(z^{-1};r+2)\\
P_{k-1}(-z^{-1};r+2)\end{pmatrix}\prod_{j=1}^{n}Q_{j-1}(\zeta^{-2}_{j};r+2)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}z^{2n+1}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n+2\end{subarray}}{\det}\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r-2}P_{k-1}(\zeta;r+2)Q_{j-1}(\zeta^{-2};r+2)\\
P_{k-1}(z^{-1};r+2)\\
P_{k-1}(-z^{-1};r+2)\end{pmatrix}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}z^{2n+1}\det\begin{pmatrix}h^{(r+2)}_{0}&\cdots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&h^{(r+2)}_{n-1}&0&0\\
P_{0}(z^{-1};r+2)&\cdots&P_{n-1}(z^{-1};r+2)&P_{n}(z^{-1};r+2)&P_{n+1}(z^{-1};r+2)\\
P_{0}(-z^{-1};r+2)&\cdots&P_{n-1}(-z^{-1};r+2)&P_{n}(-z^{-1};r+2)&P_{n+1}(-z^{-1};r+2)\\
\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

Therefore

(5.54)

D^{(r)}_{n}Q_{n}(z^{2};r)=-\frac{1}{2}z^{2n+1}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}h^{(r+2)}_{j}\left[P_{n}(z^{-1};r+2)P_{n+1}(-z^{-1};r+2)-P_{n+1}(z^{-1};r+2)P_{n}(-z^{-1};r+2)\right].

Noticing that
\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}h^{(r+2)}_{j}=D^{(r+2)}_{n}
proves (
5.48
).
∎

Remark 5.10
.

Since

z^{n+1}P_{n+1}(z^{-1};\cdot)\Bigg|_{z=0}=z^{n}P_{n}(z^{-1};\cdot)\Bigg|_{z=0}=1,\quad\text{and}\quad-z^{n+1}P_{n+1}(-z^{-1};\cdot)\Bigg|_{z=0}=z^{n}P_{n}(-z^{-1};\cdot)\Bigg|_{z=0}=(-1)^{n},

we observe that (

5.49

4.136
).

5.3.
The Christoffel-Darboux Identity

Having defined the reproducing kernel via a sum over products of bi-orthogonal polynomials and deduced that these polynomials satisfy third order difference equations
we seek the explicit evaluation of the sum involved in the kernel.
The well known Christoffel-Darboux sum in the Toeplitz result can be found in Prop. 2.5 of
[
35
]
.

Theorem 5.11
.

The Christoffel-Darboux identity for the
2j-k
and
j-2k
systems can be respectively written as

(5.55)

K_{n}(z^{2}_{2},z_{1};r)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{D_{n}^{(r+2)}}{D_{n+1}^{(r)}}\frac{z^{2n+1}_{2}}{z^{2}_{1}-z^{-2}_{2}}\det\begin{pmatrix}P_{n}(-z^{-1}_{2};r+2)&P_{n+1}(-z^{-1}_{2};r+2)&P_{n+2}(-z^{-1}_{2};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{n}(z^{-1}_{2};r+2)&P_{n+1}(z^{-1}_{2};r+2)&P_{n+2}(z^{-1}_{2};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{n}(z_{1};r+2)&P_{n+1}(z_{1};r+2)&P_{n+2}(z_{1};r+2)\\
\end{pmatrix},

and

(5.56)

L_{n}(z_{2},z^{2}_{1};s)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{E_{n}^{(s-2)}}{E_{n+1}^{(s)}}\frac{z^{2n+1}_{1}}{z^{-2}_{1}-z^{2}_{2}}\det\begin{pmatrix}S_{n}(z^{-1}_{1};s-2)&S_{n+1}(z^{-1}_{1};s-2)&S_{n+2}(z^{-1}_{1};s-2)\\[2.0pt]
S_{n}(-z^{-1}_{1};s-2)&S_{n+1}(-z^{-1}_{1};s-2)&S_{n+2}(-z^{-1}_{1};s-2)\\[2.0pt]
S_{n}(z_{2};s-2)&S_{n+1}(z_{2};s-2)&S_{n+2}(z_{2};s-2)\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

Using (
5.24
) we have

(5.57)

\begin{split}D^{(r)}_{n+1}K_{n}(z^{2}_{2},z_{1};r)&=\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}(z_{1}-\zeta)(z^{2}_{2}-\zeta^{-2})]\\
&=\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}(z_{1}-\zeta)(z_{2}-\zeta^{-1})(z_{2}+\zeta^{-1})]\\
&=\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r}(z_{1}-\zeta)z^{2}_{2}\zeta^{-2}(z^{-1}_{2}-\zeta)(-z^{-1}_{2}-\zeta)]\\
&=z^{2n}_{2}\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r-2}(z_{1}-\zeta)(z^{-1}_{2}-\zeta)(-z^{-1}_{2}-\zeta)].\end{split}

Let us temporarily denote
-z^{-1}_{2}
,
z^{-1}_{2}
, and
z_{1}
respectively by
\zeta_{n+1}
,
\zeta_{n+2}
, and
\zeta_{n+3}
. Note that

(5.58)

\begin{split}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n+3}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})=&(\zeta_{n+3}-\zeta_{n+2})(\zeta_{n+3}-\zeta_{n+1})(\zeta_{n+2}-\zeta_{n+1})\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\zeta_{n+3}-\zeta_{j})(\zeta_{n+2}-\zeta_{j})(\zeta_{n+1}-\zeta_{j})\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j}).\end{split}

We have

\begin{split}&D^{(r)}_{n+1}K_{n}(z^{2}_{2},z_{1};r)=z^{2n}_{2}\mathcal{D}_{n}[w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r-2}(z_{1}-\zeta)(z^{-1}_{2}-\zeta)(-z^{-1}_{2}-\zeta)]\\
&=\frac{z_{2}^{2n}}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r-2}_{j}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z_{1}-\zeta_{j})(z_{2}^{-1}-\zeta_{j})(-z_{2}^{-1}-\zeta_{j})\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j})\\
&=\frac{z_{2}^{2n+1}}{n!2(z_{1}-z^{-1}_{2})(z_{1}+z^{-1}_{2})}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r-2}_{j}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n+3}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{j})\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\zeta^{-2}_{k}-\zeta^{-2}_{j})\\
&=\frac{z_{2}^{2n+1}}{n!2(z_{1}-z^{-1}_{2})(z_{1}+z^{-1}_{2})}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r-2}_{j}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n+3\end{subarray}}{\det}\begin{pmatrix}P_{k-1}(\zeta_{j};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{k-1}(-z_{2}^{-1};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{k-1}(z_{2}^{-1};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{k-1}(z_{1};r+2)\end{pmatrix}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n\end{subarray}}{\det}\{Q_{k-1}(\zeta^{-2}_{j};r+2)\}\\
&=\frac{z_{2}^{2n+1}}{2(z^{2}_{1}-z^{-2}_{2})}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{1}}\cdots\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta_{n}}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w(\zeta_{j})\zeta^{-r-2}_{j}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n+3\end{subarray}}{\det}\begin{pmatrix}P_{k-1}(\zeta_{j};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{k-1}(-z_{2}^{-1};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{k-1}(z_{2}^{-1};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{k-1}(z_{1};r+2)\end{pmatrix}\prod_{j=1}^{n}Q_{j-1}(\zeta^{-2}_{j};r+2)\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}&=\frac{z_{2}^{2n+1}}{2(z^{2}_{1}-z^{-2}_{2})}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n+2\end{subarray}}{\det}\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}w(\zeta)\zeta^{-r-2}P_{k-1}(\zeta;r+2)Q_{j-1}(\zeta^{-2};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{k-1}(-z_{2}^{-1};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{k-1}(z_{2}^{-1};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{k-1}(z_{1};r+2)\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\frac{z_{2}^{2n+1}}{2(z^{2}_{1}-z^{-2}_{2})}\det\begin{pmatrix}h^{(r+2)}_{0}&\cdots&0&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&h^{(r+2)}_{n-1}&0&0&0\\[2.0pt]
P_{0}(-z_{2}^{-1};r+2)&\cdots&P_{n-1}(-z_{2}^{-1};r+2)&P_{n}(-z_{2}^{-1};r+2)&P_{n+1}(-z_{2}^{-1};r+2)&P_{n+2}(-z_{2}^{-1};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{0}(z_{2}^{-1};r+2)&\cdots&P_{n-1}(z_{2}^{-1};r+2)&P_{n}(z_{2}^{-1};r+2)&P_{n+1}(z_{2}^{-1};r+2)&P_{n+2}(z_{2}^{-1};r+2)\\[2.0pt]
P_{0}(z_{1};r+2)&\cdots&P_{n-1}(z_{1};r+2)&P_{n}(z_{1};r+2)&P_{n+1}(z_{1};r+2)&P_{n+2}(z_{1};r+2)\\
\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

Now (
5.55
) follows immediately by noticing
\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}h^{(r+2)}_{j}=D^{(r+2)}_{n}
.
∎

8.
Prospects of future work

In our study of
2j-k
and
j-2k
bi-orthogonal polynomial systems on the unit circle
we have concentrated on laying out the theory for generic class of weights and only considered the essential orthogonality structures. Even within this circumscribed area there are other important aspects that are yet to be investigated. Our purpose in this section is to bring the
2j-k
and
j-2k
systems to the attention of a wider audience of mathematicians by providing a (non-exhaustive) list of open problems which we believe are significant for the development of the theory of
2j-k
and
j-2k
systems, and consequently for the theory of
pj-qk
systems, with relatively prime
p
and
q
. One example of this appears to be the novel form of the joint density function for the
pj-qk
systems which can be rewritten as the product of differences

\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\left(\zeta^{q}_{k}-\zeta^{q}_{j}\right)\left(\zeta^{-p}_{k}-\zeta^{-p}_{j}\right)=4^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\zeta^{\frac{1}{2}(q-p)(n-1)}_{j}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\sin\left(\tfrac{1}{2}q(\theta_{k}-\theta_{j})\right)\sin\left(\tfrac{1}{2}p(\theta_{k}-\theta_{j})\right),

and features a primary repulsion of
\beta=2
as
\theta_{k}-\theta_{j}\to 0
along with weaker repulsions of
\beta=1
at as
\theta_{k}-\theta_{j}\to\pm\frac{2}{p}\pi,\pm\frac{2}{q}\pi,\ldots
.

There has been a growing interest in recent years about the asymptotic aspects of structured moment determinants other than the well-known cases of Toeplitz and Hankel. Among those studies are asymptotics of Toeplitz+Hankel determinants
[
26
,
40
]
, and recently the bordered Toeplitz determinants
[
9
]
. A successful Riemann-Hilbert formulation for
2j-k
and
j-2k
systems places them among the collection of structured moment determinants for which the large size asymptotics of the determinant and the large degree asymptotics of the corresponding systems of bi-orthogonal polynomials can be investigated. In this regard, the formulation of a (
3\times 3
) Riemann-Hilbert problem corresponding to these bi-orthogonal polynomial systems, both as a means of founding the whole theory upon this and deriving key results but also to pave the way for a suitable Deift-Zhou analysis will be the topic of a future publication. At a later stage, the asymptotic description of these determinants can be investigated when the symbol
w
is of Fisher-Hartwig type, similar to what has been done for Toeplitz
[
26
]
, Hankel
[
18
,
20
]
, and Muttalib-Borodin
[
19
]
determinants.

On a different but possibly related perspective, it would be interesting to search for possible Fredholm determinant representations for the
2j-k
and
j-2k
determinants in the same spirit as the Fredholm determinant representations for Toeplitz determinants which could also unveil a Riemann-Hilbert formulation (see
[
25
]
where the connection was first found for Toeplitz determinants, and also
[
49
]
). Also looking for the possible
2j-k/j-2k
analogue of the Fredholm sine-kernel determinant representations for gap probabilities in random matrix theory seems to be another important front to be investigated (see e.g.
[
54
]
and references therein). Recalling the last paragraph of §
1
, it would be interesting to ask if from the viewpoint of Operator Theory, the analogue of the (strong) Szegő limit theorem can be proven for the
2j-k/j-2k
determinants (See. e.g. the monographs
[
12
,
13
]
). If this is plausible, we could hope for arriving at yet another example of close interaction between Operator Theory and Riemann-Hilbert techniques.

What is mentioned above is only a short list and obviously the full list of
2j-k/j-2k
analogues of Toeplitz theory can not be enumerated in full here,
given the voluminous literature on the Toeplitz side.
Nevertheless we point out some other obvious candidates:
Rational approximations to the Carathéodory functions with a two-point (
z=0
and
z=\infty
) Hermite-Padé approximation of the associated functions;
Quadrature problems on the unit circle and Christoffel weights;
The elucidation of an analogue of the CMV matrix
[
15
]
in the Toeplitz case, i.e. the minimum banded representation of the spectral multiplication operator;
and the analogue of the discrete Fredholm determinant arising from the scattering theory approach to the matrix difference system for the Toeplitz system,
commonly known as the Geronimo-Case-Borodin-Okounkov identity
[
39
,
11
]
.

Tasks which we envisage completing progressively include the representation of the derivatives of the polynomials and associated functions in terms
of the fore-mentioned bases. In the context of semi-classical weights this will provide the pair of Lax operators of the integrable system.
We anticipate making progress on the question raised in
[
4
]

”It would be very interesting to determine whether or not there exists a differential equation arising from our formula (5) which plays a role for symplectic and orthogonal types that Painlevé III plays for unitary symmetry”.

In this section

Definition 3.1

A non-stationary dynamical system (or

is defined by

F_{i}^{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&f_{i+n-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i}:M_{i}\rightarrow M_{i+n}~~~\text{if }~~~n>0\\
&f_{i+n}^{-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i-1}^{-1}:M_{i}\rightarrow M_{i+n}~~~\text{if }~~~~~n<0\\
&I_{i}:M_{i}\longrightarrow M_{i}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{if }~~~~~~~~n=0,\end{array}\right.

where

is the identity on
M_{i}
. In this definition

may not be an isometry and the Lorentzian metric vary on each

. We also use of the name

-family for the non-stationary dynamical system (or

)
(M,\langle.,.\rangle,F)

Definition 3.2
.

An

-family

-family) up to a distribution

and a continuous splitting

Each vector of

or

ii)
The splitting

is

iii)
For each

,

and

|g_{F^{n}_{i}(p)}(D_{p}F^{n}_{i}(v),D_{p}F^{n}_{i}(v))|\leqslant c\lambda^{n}|g_{p}(v,v)|~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~if~~~~~v\in E^{s}(p)

and

|g_{F^{-n}_{i}(p)}(D_{p}F^{-n}_{i}(v),D_{p}F^{-n}_{i}(v))|\leqslant c\lambda^{n}|g_{p}(v,v)|~~~~~if~~~~~v\in E^{u}(p)

(iv)

The subspaces

and

\displaystyle=|g_{F^{n}_{i}(p)}(D_{p}F^{n}_{i}(v),D_{p}F^{n}_{i}(v))|

|g_{p}(P_{-t_{k}}(v_{kj})-v_{j},P_{-t_{k}}(v_{kj^{\prime}})-v_{j^{\prime}})|<\delta/(1+\mid\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{j}^{\prime}\mid).

Therefore

\mid g_{p}(P_{-t_{k}}(u_{k})-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}v_{j},P_{-t_{k}}(u_{k})-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}v_{j})\mid

=|g_{p}(\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}P_{-t_{k}}(v_{kj})-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}v_{j},\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}P_{-t_{k}}(v_{kj})-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}v_{j})\mid

=|\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{j^{\prime}}g_{p}(P_{-t_{k}}(v_{kj})-v_{j},P_{-t_{k}}(v_{kj^{\prime}})-v_{j^{\prime}})|

\leqslant\delta\mid\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{j^{\prime}}\mid/(1+\mid\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{j^{\prime}}\mid)\leqslant\delta~~~~~~(*)

Now we take an arbitrary unit vector
v=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}v_{j}\in E^{u}(p)
. Since
v_{nj}\rightarrow v_{j}
then
|g_{\gamma(t_{n})}(\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}v_{nj},\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}v_{nj})|=1
. Thus for given
\delta>0
we have

|g_{\gamma(t_{n})}(P_{t_{n}}(v_{j})-v_{nj},P_{t_{n}}(v_{j^{\prime}})-v_{nj^{\prime}})|<\delta/(1+\mid\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{j^{\prime}}\mid).

Consequently

\mid g_{\gamma(t_{n})}(P_{t_{n}}(v)-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}v_{nj},P_{t_{n}}(v)-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}v_{nj})\mid

=|g_{\gamma(t_{n})}(\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}P_{t_{n}}(v_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}v_{nj},\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}P_{t_{n}}(v_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}v_{nj})\mid

=|\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{j^{\prime}}g_{\gamma(t_{n})}(P_{t_{n}}(v_{j})-v_{nj},P_{t_{n}}(v_{j^{\prime}})-v_{nj^{\prime}})|

\leqslant\delta\mid\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{j^{\prime}}\mid/(1+\mid\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{j^{\prime}}\mid)\leqslant\delta~~~~~~(**)

The inequalities
(*)
and
(**)
implies
E^{u}(\gamma(t_{n}))
convergence to
E^{u}(p)
. The second part of Theorem can prove similarly.
∎

5.
Shadowing property on Lorentzian non-stationary dynamical system

In this section we define Lorentzian shadowing property of type
I
and type
II
on
L
-family
(M,\langle.,.\rangle,F)
and on the induced dynamic by it. We prove shadowing property of type
I
is invariant by uniformly conjugacy and if
L
-family
(M,\langle.,.\rangle,F)
has shadowing property of type
II
then it has shadowing property of type
I
.

Definition 5.1
.

Consider a
L
-family
(M,\langle.,.\rangle,F)
for
\delta>0
, the sequence
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
where
v_{i}\in T_{p}M_{i}
is said to be a
\delta
-pseudo orbit of type
I
if
|g_{i+1}(D_{p}f_{i}(v_{i}),v_{i+1})|<\delta
, for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For given
\epsilon>0
a
\delta
-pseudo orbit
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called to be
\epsilon
-traced by
w\in T_{p}M_{i}

if
|g_{i+n}(F_{i}^{n}(w),v_{i+n}))|<\epsilon
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 5.2
.

A
L
-family
(M,\langle.,.\rangle,F)
is said to have Lorentzian shadowing property of type
I
if for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that every
\delta
-pseudo orbit is
\epsilon
-traced by some vector in
T_{p}M_{i}
.

Consider two
L
-family
(M,g,F)
and
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g},\tilde{F})
. We say a homeomorphism
h:M\rightarrow\tilde{M}
is uniform continuous if for given
\epsilon>0
there exists an
\tilde{\epsilon}>0
such that
|g(v,w)|<\epsilon
implies
|\tilde{g}(h_{i}(v),h_{i}(w))|<\tilde{\epsilon}
where
v,w\in T_{p}M_{i}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 5.3
.

A topological conjugacy between
L
-family
(M,g,F)
and
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g},\tilde{F})
is a map
h:M\rightarrow\tilde{M}
such that for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,
h|_{M_{i}}=h_{i}:M_{i}\rightarrow\tilde{M}_{i}
is a homeomorphism and
h_{i+1}\circ f_{i}=\tilde{f}_{i}\circ h_{i}
.

Definition 5.4
.

A topological conjugacy
h:M\rightarrow\tilde{M}
is uniformly conjugate if
h_{i}:M_{i}\rightarrow\tilde{M}_{i}
and
h_{i}^{-1}:\tilde{M}_{i}\rightarrow M_{i}
are uniformly continuous.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
L
-family
(M,g,F)
be uniformly conjugate
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g},\tilde{F})
. Then
(M,g,F)
has shadowing property of type
I
if and only if
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g},\tilde{F})
has shadowing property of type
I
.

Proof.

Suppose
L
-family
(M,g,F)
has shadowing property of type
I
. Given
\epsilon>0
, because of uniform continuity of
h
there exists
0<\epsilon_{0}<\epsilon
such that
|g(u_{i},v_{i})|<\epsilon_{0}
implies
|\tilde{g}(h_{i}(u_{i}),h_{i}(v_{i}))|<\epsilon
for all
u_{i},v_{i}\in T_{p}M_{i}
. Since
L
-family
(M,g,F)
has shadowing property of type
I
there exists
0<\delta_{0}<\epsilon_{0}
such that for every
\delta_{0}
-pseudo orbit of
L
-family
(M,g,F)
is
\epsilon_{0}
-traced by
w\in T_{p}M_{i}
. By uniform continuous of
h^{-1}
, there exists
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
such that
|\tilde{g}(\tilde{u}_{i},\tilde{v}_{i})|<\delta
implies
g(h_{i}^{-1}(\tilde{u}_{i}),h_{i}^{-1}(\tilde{v}_{i}))<\delta_{0}
for any vector
\tilde{u}_{i},\tilde{v}_{i}\in T_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{M}_{i}
. Now we prove that every
\delta
-pseudo orbit of
L
-family
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g},\tilde{F})
is
\epsilon
-traced by some vector of
T_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{M}_{i}
. Suppose
\{\tilde{v}_{i}\}
is a
\delta
-pseudo orbit of
L
-family
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g},\tilde{F})
i.e

|\tilde{g}_{i+1}(\tilde{f}_{i}(\tilde{v}_{i}),\tilde{v}_{i+1})|<\delta
so
|g_{i+1}(h_{i+1}^{-1}\tilde{f}_{i}(\tilde{v}_{i}),h_{i+1}^{-1}(\tilde{v}_{i+1}))|<\delta_{0}
then
|g_{i+1}(f_{i}\circ h^{-1}_{i}(\tilde{v}_{i}),h_{i+1}^{-1}(\tilde{v}_{i+1}))|<\delta_{0}
. Hence
\{h_{i}^{-1}(\tilde{v}_{i})\}
is a
\delta_{0}
-pseudo orbit for
L
-family
(M,g,F)
. Thus there exist
w\in T_{p}M_{i}
such that

|g_{i+n}(F_{i}^{n}(w),h_{i+n}^{-1}(\tilde{v}_{i+n})|<\epsilon_{0}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. So by using uniform continuous of
h
we have
|\tilde{g}_{i+n}(h_{i+n}F_{i}^{n}(w),\tilde{v}_{i+n}|<\epsilon
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Because
h_{i+n}\circ F_{i}^{n}(w)=\tilde{F}_{i}^{n}\circ h_{i}(w)
so
|\tilde{g}_{i+n}(\tilde{F}_{i}^{n}(\tilde{w}),\tilde{v}_{i+n}|<\epsilon
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
where
h_{i}(w)=\tilde{w}\in T_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{M}_{i}
. Hence
\{\tilde{v}_{i}\}
is
\epsilon
- traced by
\tilde{w}
. The converse is proved by similar argument.

∎

For the next theorem we define a metric on all non-empty compact subspaces of
TM=\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}TM_{i}
. Because
T_{p}M_{i}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
where dim

we put usual topology on

. Suppose
\mathcal{K}(M_{i})
denotes the hyperspace of all non-empty compact subspaces of

is a smooth curve passing throught

. If

and

then

where

. So for two non-empty compact subspaces
A
and
B
of

and
T_{\gamma(\zeta)}M_{i}
we define
d(A,B)=max\{max~~d(u,B),max~~d(v,A):u\in A~~~and~~~v\in B\}
. Consider homeomorphism
D_{p}f_{i}:T_{p}M_{i}\rightarrow T_{f_{i}(p)}M_{i+1}
for

, it induces a homeomorphism
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{K}(M_{i})\rightarrow\mathcal{K}(M_{i+1})
by
\mathcal{F}(K)=D_{p}f_{i}(K)
for every
K\in\mathcal{K}(M_{i})
where
\mathcal{F}(K)=\{D_{p}f_{i}(k):k\in K\}
.
Now we extend definitions
5.1
and
5.2
on
\mathcal{K}(M)=\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{K}(M_{i})

Consider a
L
-family
(M,\langle.,.\rangle,F)
for
\delta>0
, the sequence
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
where
V_{i}\in\mathcal{K}(M_{i})
is said to be a
\delta
-pseudo orbit of type
II
if
d(\mathcal{F}(V_{i}),V_{i+1})<\delta
, for

.

For given
\epsilon>0
a
\delta
-pseudo orbit
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called to be
\epsilon
-traced by
W\in\mathcal{K}(M_{i})

if

for

.

A
L
-family
(M,\langle.,.\rangle,F)
is said to have Lorentzian shadowing property of type
II
if for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that every
\delta
-pseudo orbit is
\epsilon
-traced by some vector in
\mathcal{K}(M_{i})
.

Theorem 5.6
.

If
L
-family
(M,g,F)
has shadowing property of type

.

Proof.

Suppose
L
-family
(M,g,F)
has shadowing property of type

. Let

be a
\delta
-pseudo orbit of type

then
\{\{v_{i}\}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a

.

Then

6.
Conclusion

We extend the notion of Anosov family on a sequence of compact Riemannian manifolds
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
to Lorentzian Anosov family by using of a distributions
p\mapsto E^{n}(p)
. We see that when each
M_{i}
is a Lorentzian manifold then the tangent space of
M_{i}
at
p\in M_{i}
has a unique splitting to stable, unstable and a subset of null vectors which is also a vector space. We consider the behavior of this splitting by using of the distance function created by the unique torsion-free connection.

Lorentzian shadowing property of type
I
and type
II
on
L
-family
(M,\langle.,.\rangle,F)
and on the induced dynamic by it has been introduced. We prove shadowing property of type
I
is invariant by uniformly conjugacy. Also we prove if
L
-family
(M,g,F)
has shadowing property of type
II
then the induced dynamic of it has shadowing property of type
I
.

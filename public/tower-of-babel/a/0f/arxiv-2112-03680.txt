Tropical Poincaré duality spaces
Edvard Aksnes

Abstract
. The tropical fundamental class of a rational balanced polyhedral fan induces cap products between tropical cohomology and tropical Borel–Moore homology. When all these cap products are isomorphisms, the fan is said to be a tropical Poincaré duality space . If all the stars of faces also are such spaces, such as for fans of matroids, the fan is called a local tropical Poincaré duality space . In this article, we first give some necessary conditions for fans to be tropical Poincaré duality spaces and a classification in dimension one. Next, we prove that tropical Poincaré duality for the stars of all faces of dimension greater than zero and a vanishing condition implies tropical Poincaré duality of the fan. This leads to necessary and sufficient conditions for a fan to be a local tropical Poincaré duality space. Finally, we use such fans to show that certain abstract balanced polyhedral spaces satisfy tropical Poincaré duality.

Recently,

Organization

In
Section

2
, we set conventions for fans, stars and integer weights. Then we define cellular (co)sheaves and cellular (co)sheaf (co)homology.

In
Section

3
, we define the tropical multi-tangent cosheaves and sheaves, which we
use to define tropical (co)homology. This is used to describe a generalized version of the balancing condition in tropical geometry, to generalize beyond integer weights.

In
Section

4
, we define the TPD over a ring

1.1

In
Section

5

1.2

Finally, in
Section

2.1.
Cones, fans and stars

Let
N\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a lattice, and
N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the associated real vector space.

2.1
.

A
rational polyhedral cone

\sigma
in a lattice
N
is a set of the form

\sigma=\left\{\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}a_{i}v_{i}\mid a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\right\}\subset N

for vectors
v_{i}\in N
, such that
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}=\sigma\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
is closed and strictly convex, hence has a vertex at the origin.

The
lattice

L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma)
is the saturated sublattice of
N
generated by
\sigma
, and the
dimension
of a cone is the rank of
L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma)
.

Another cone
\tau
is said to be a
face
of
\sigma
if there is some element

m\in\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(N,\mathbb{Z})
, with
m(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\sigma
, i.e. a positive functional, such that
\tau=\{x\in\sigma\mid m(x)=0\}
.
Any face can also be exhibited by setting particular coefficients
a_{i}
to
0
.

For
\tau
a face of
\sigma
, the set
L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\tau)\subset L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma)
is a sublattice. For
\dim\tau=\dim\sigma-1
, we may select a
primitive integer vector

v_{\sigma/\tau}\in N
such that
L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma)=L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\tau)+\mathbb{Z}v_{\sigma/\tau}
.

2.2
.

A
rational polyhedral fan

\Sigma
is a finite collection of rational polyhedral cones in
N
such that:

•

For any cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, if
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
, then
\tau\in\Sigma
,

•

For
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma
, the intersection
\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2}
is a face of
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
.

The cones in
\Sigma
are also called
faces
, and the collection of faces of dimension
i
is denoted by
\Sigma^{i}
.
The
dimension of
\Sigma
is the supremum of the dimensions of cones of
\Sigma
.
We write
\tau\preceq\sigma
if
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
and
\tau\prec\sigma
if
\tau
is a proper face, which gives a partial ordering on
\Sigma
. We say that a face
\sigma\in\Sigma
is
maximal
if it is maximal with respect to the ordering
\preceq
. We will require that all fans are
pure dimensional
in the sense that all maximal by inclusion faces are of equal dimension.

Abusing notation, we also write
\Sigma
for the category associated to the partial ordering
\preceq
, whose objects are the cones
\sigma\in\Sigma
, with a morphism
\tau\to\sigma\in\Hom_{\Sigma}(\tau,\sigma)
if and only if
\tau\preceq\sigma
.

Note that all cones intersect in a common minimal cell, and since we required each cone to have a vertex, this is the unique vertex
v
in
\Sigma
. Moreover, a rational polyhedral fan corresponds to a
cell complex
in the sense of
[
She85
,
Cur14
]
, when considering the fan as glued abstractly from the interiors of the cones.

The

2.2.
Cellular sheaves and cellular cosheaves

One can define
cellular sheaves
and
cellular cosheaves
of modules on a polyhedral fan:

2.10
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring,
\Sigma
a rational polyhedral fan. Then:

•

A
cellular
R
-sheaf

\mathcal{G}
is a functor
\mathcal{G}\colon\Sigma\to\mathbf{Mod}_{R}
.

•

A
cellular
R
-cosheaf

\mathcal{F}
is a functor
\mathcal{F}\colon\Sigma^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{R}
.

A morphism of sheaves or cosheaves is simply a natural transformation of functors or contravariant functors, respectively.

2.11
.

The category
\Sigma
, when viewed as a set, can be given the
Alexandrov topology
, such that cellular sheaves and cosheaves in fact are sheaves and cosheaves with respect to this topology. For more on cellular sheaves and cosheaves, see
[
Cur14
]
.

We have considered the fan
\Sigma
as a category with morphisms
\tau\to\sigma
whenever
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
, so that a sheaf
\mathcal{G}
induces a map
\mathcal{G}(\tau)\to\mathcal{G}(\sigma)
, and a cosheaf
\mathcal{F}
induces a map
\mathcal{F}(\sigma)\to\mathcal{F}(\tau)
. This convention is in agreement with
[
Cur14
,
She85
]
, but reversed from
[
Bri97
,
How08
]
in the sense that their
sheaves
are our
cosheaves
, and vice versa.

2.12
.

Let
\Sigma
be a rational polyhedral fan. For a module
M
over a ring
R
, the
constant cosheaf
M^{\Sigma}
with values in
M
is the cosheaf defined as a functor
M^{\Sigma}\colon\Sigma^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{R}
taking all objects to
M
and all morphisms to
\id_{M}
.

Similarly, the
constant sheaf
M_{\Sigma}
with values in
M
is the sheaf defined as a functor
M_{\Sigma}\colon\Sigma\to\mathbf{Mod}_{R}
taking all objects to
M
and all morphisms to
\id_{M}
.

2.3.
Cellular homology and cohomology

Considering the fan
\Sigma
as a subset of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, we select an orientation for each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
. For each
\tau\prec\sigma
such that
\dim(\tau)=\dim(\sigma)-1
, we keep track of the relative orientations by writing
\mathcal{O}(\tau,\sigma)=1
if the restriction of the orientation of
\sigma
to
\tau
coincides with the orientation of
\tau
, and
\mathcal{O}(\tau,\sigma)=-1
if it reverses it. In the two next definitions, we use the orientation
\mathcal{O}(\tau,\sigma)=\pm 1
to construct certain (co)chain complexes for a given (co)sheaf. These definitions are equal to the ones in
[
Cur14
,
She85
,
KSW17
]
, and reversed from
[
Bri97
,
How08
]
, who index by codimension.

2.13
.

Given a cellular sheaf
\mathcal{G}
, the
cellular cochain groups
and
cellular cochain groups with compact support
are defined, respectively, by

C^{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{G})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\Sigma^{q}\\
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}\;\text{compact}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{G}(\sigma)\quad\text{and}\quad C_{c}^{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{G})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{q}}\mathcal{G}(\sigma),

for
q\geq 0
, where
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}
is as in
Definition

2.1
. The cellular cochain maps

d^{q}\colon C^{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{G})\to C^{q+1}(\Sigma,\mathcal{G})\quad\text{and}\quad d^{q}\colon C_{c}^{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{G})\to C_{c}^{q+1}(\Sigma,\mathcal{G})

are given component-wise for
\tau\in\Sigma^{q}
and
\sigma\in\Sigma^{q+1}
with
\tau\prec\sigma
by
d_{\tau\sigma}\colon\mathcal{G}(\tau)\to\mathcal{G}(\sigma)
, where

d_{\tau\sigma}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}(\tau,\sigma)\mathcal{G}(\tau\to\sigma).

If
\tau\not\preceq\sigma
, we let the map
d_{\tau\sigma}
be
0
.

The cohomology groups
H^{\bullet}(\Sigma,\mathcal{G})
and
H_{c}^{\bullet}(\Sigma,\mathcal{G})
of these complexes are the
cellular sheaf cohomology
and
cellular sheaf cohomology with compact support
with respect to the sheaf
\mathcal{G}
.

2.14
.

Given a cellular cosheaf
\mathcal{F}
, the
cellular chain group
and
Borel–Moore cellular chain groups
are defined, respectively, by

C_{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\Sigma^{q}\\
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}\;\text{compact}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{F}(\sigma)\quad\text{and}\quad C_{q}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\Sigma^{q}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{F}(\sigma),

for
q\geq 0
, where
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}
is as in
Definition

2.1
. The cellular chain maps

\partial_{q}\colon C_{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F})\to C_{q-1}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F})\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{q}\colon C_{q}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F})\to C_{q-1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F})

are given component-wise for
\sigma\in\Sigma^{q}
and
\tau\in\Sigma^{q-1}
by

, where

If

.

The homology groups

and

and

Proofs that the cellular (co)chain groups and maps defined above form (co)chain complexes can be found in
[

2.15
.

The above definitions of cellular cohomology work in the more general setting of polyhedral complexes. Since we are working with pointed polyhedral fans, the unique compact cell is the vertex

\mathcal{G}
on a fan

Similarly, for any cosheaf

are trivial for

\displaystyle H_{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F})=\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}(v)&for $q=0$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

.

Consider the fan from

is the homology of the complex

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}^{4}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{1}}

3.
Tropical geometry of fans

In this section, we introduce particular cellular (co)sheaves on fans which are of interest in tropical geometry. After examining some properties of the resulting
tropical (co)homology
, we use this to define the
balancing
condition in tropical geometry. Finally, we define the
tropical cap product
associated to a balancing of the fan. We then introduce particular sheaves of interest in tropical geometry. Next, we generalize the balancing condition on fans to weights in arbitrary rings, which finally leads to a treatment of tropical Poincaré duality over arbitrary commutative rings.

3.1.
Tropical sheaves and cosheaves

For tropical (co)homology, the following sheaves and cosheaves are of interest.

3.1
[
IKMZ19
, Definition 13]
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan of dimension
d
in
N
. For
\sigma\in\Sigma
, let
L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma)
be the lattice of integer points parallel to the cone
\sigma
. For
p=1,\dots,d
, the
p
-th multi-tangent cosheaf

\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the cellular
\mathbb{Z}
-cosheaf defined by the data:

•

For
\sigma\in\Sigma
, one has
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma)\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma\preceq\gamma}\bigwedge^{p}L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\gamma)\subseteq\bigwedge^{p}N
.

•

For
\tau\preceq\sigma
, the morphism
(\sigma\to\tau)\in\Hom_{\Sigma^{\mathrm{op}}}(\sigma,\tau)
becomes the map
\iota_{\sigma,\tau}\colon\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma)\to\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\tau)
, which is induced by the natural inclusion.

In the
p=0
case, we define
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma}
, with all maps being the identity.

Furthermore, the cellular cosheaf
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
also gives rise to a cellular sheaf

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
which is defined by
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}(\sigma)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma)^{*}
, with morphisms
\rho_{\tau,\sigma}\colon\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}(\tau)\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}(\sigma)
defined by dualizing
\iota_{\sigma,\tau}\colon\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma)\to\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\tau)
.

Finally, following
[
JRS18
]
, for any ring
R
, we define a cosheaf
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R}
by taking the tensor product
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R}(\sigma)=\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R
, giving an
R
-module, and tensoring the maps as well.
Dualizing yields a sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{R}^{p}
.

3.2
.

We compute some values of these cosheaves:

(1)

For

2.3

\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\tau_{i})=\mathbb{Z}

\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}(v)=\mathbb{Z}

(2)

For

2.4

3.3
.

For any
\mathbb{Z}
-module

M_{R}\coloneqq M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R

\bigwedge^{p}M_{R}\cong(\bigwedge^{p}M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R

\sigma\in\Sigma

3.4

Let
\Sigma
be a fan of dimension
d

\alpha\in\Sigma^{d}

Remark

3.3

\mathcal{F}_{d}^{R}(\alpha)=\bigwedge^{d}L_{R}(\alpha)\cong R

\Lambda_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{F}_{d}^{R}(\alpha)\cong(\bigwedge^{p}L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R

\Lambda_{\alpha}\otimes 1_{R}\in\mathcal{F}_{d}^{R}(\alpha)
.

3.5
.

In

2.4

for the ambient lattice

3.10

Let
\Sigma

\gamma\in\Sigma

\mathcal{F}_{R,\Sigma}^{p}

\mathcal{F}_{R,\gamma}^{p}
denote the

\Sigma

\gamma_{\succeq}
respectively. Then

H^{0}(\gamma_{\succeq},\mathcal{F}_{R,\gamma}^{p})\cong\mathcal{F}_{R,\Sigma}^{p}(\gamma).

Similarly, let
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R,\Sigma}
and
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R,\gamma}
denote the
p

\Sigma

Then the Borel–Moore homology
H_{q}^{BM}(\gamma_{\succeq},\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R,\gamma})
is isomorphic to the homology of the complex

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Sigma^{d}\\
\alpha\succ\gamma\end{subarray}}\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R,\Sigma}(\alpha)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\kappa\in\Sigma^{r+1}\\

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R,\Sigma}(\gamma)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{q}|_{\kappa\succ\gamma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{q}|_{\alpha\succ\gamma}}

{\partial_{q}^{\gamma}}=\oplus\partial_{\sigma\tau}
with the sum taken over all
\sigma,\tau\succeq\gamma
,
\sigma\in\Sigma^{q}
and
\tau\in\Sigma^{q-1}
.

Proof.

First, by
Definition

2.6
, we must choose a subdivision of the space with support given by the cones
\widetilde{\kappa}=\{t(x-y)\mid t\geq 0,x\in\kappa,y\in\gamma\}

for each
\kappa\succeq\gamma
, and we will have only one compact cell given by the created vertex
\widetilde{v}\in\gamma_{\succeq}
.
By
Remark

2.15
, we have
H^{0}(\gamma_{\succeq},\mathcal{F}_{R,\gamma}^{p})=\mathcal{F}_{R,\gamma}^{p}(\widetilde{v})
. Then, observe that for each
\kappa\succ\gamma
, the lattice is unchanged in the sense that
L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\widetilde{\kappa})=L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\kappa)
as subspaces of
N
, and each maximal dimensional face
\alpha
in the subdivision of
\gamma_{\succeq}
is a subspace of a
\widetilde{\kappa}
. In particular, this implies that
\mathcal{F}_{R,\gamma}^{p}(\widetilde{v})\cong\mathcal{F}_{R,\Sigma}^{p}(\gamma)
.

Next, observe that the Borel–Moore homology is the equal to the regular homology of the fan when seen as a subset of the one-point compactification
N\cup\{\infty\}
of the ambient lattice
N
. Then every cone
\sigma\in\gamma_{\succeq}
becomes a disk
\sigma_{\infty}
in

, and we have a CW complex structure on the underlying set of
\gamma_{\succeq}\cup\{\infty\}

Then, similarly to
[

and
[
JRS18

C_{q}^{BM}(\gamma_{\succeq},\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R})=\oplus_{\sigma\in\gamma_{\succeq}^{q}}\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R}(\sigma)=\oplus_{\sigma\in\gamma_{\succeq}^{q}}H_{q}(\sigma_{\infty},\partial(\sigma_{\infty}),\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R}(\sigma)),

where the right hand side becomes the cellular homology with coefficients in the local system induced by
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R}
of the CW complex
\gamma_{\succeq}\cup\{\infty\}
. This can be computed with an arbitrary CW structure. Therefore, the given complex, which is the local system homology of the CW structure induced on
|\gamma_{\succeq}\cup\{\infty\}|
by taking the non-subdivided cell structure of
\gamma_{\succeq}
will compute the Borel–Moore homology of the
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{R}

∎

3.2.
Balancing in tropical geometry

It was observed in
[
JRS18
, Remark 4.9]
and
[
MZ14
, Proposition 4.3]
that the balancing condition from
Definition

2.8
can be equivalently formulated as the condition that a particular tropical Borel–Moore chain is closed. In this subsection, we use this observation to form a generalization of the balancing condition to arbitrary commutative rings.

3.11
.

Let
R
be a ring. An
R
-weight function

w\colon\Sigma^{d}\to R
on a
d
-dimensional fan
\Sigma
is a function such that for all
\alpha\in\Sigma^{d}
, the weight
w(\alpha)
is not a zero-divisor. A pair
(\Sigma,w)
will be called an
R
-weighted fan
.

3.12
.

An
R
-weighted fan
(\Sigma,w)
is
R
-balanced
if
the fundamental chain
\mathrm{Ch}(\Sigma,w)
given by

\mathrm{Ch}(\Sigma,w)\coloneqq(w(\alpha)\Lambda_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{d}}\in C_{d}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d})

is a cycle, where the
\Lambda_{\alpha}
are chosen as in
Remark

3.4
. In this case, we have
H_{d}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d}^{R})\not=0
, together with an induced
fundamental class

[\Sigma,w]=[\mathrm{Ch}(\Sigma,w)]\in H_{d}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d}^{R}).

If
H_{d}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d}^{R})=\langle[\Sigma,w]\rangle\cong R
, we say that
\Sigma
is
uniquely
R
-balanced
by
w
.

3.13
.

We now compute the fundamental chain in the fan
\Sigma
of
Example

2.4
. Choose orientations such that the elements
\Lambda_{\sigma_{i}}
are as in
Example

3.5
. Moreover, we choose a weight function assigning the value
1
to each of the cones
\sigma_{i}
. Then the fundamental chain is:

(\Lambda_{\sigma_{i}})_{i=1}^{3}=(e_{1}\wedge e_{2},e_{1}\wedge e_{2},e_{1}\wedge e_{2})\in C_{2}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}).

It is then straightforward to check that, under the boundary map
\partial_{2}
, taking into account orientations, this chain is mapped to zero. For instance, for the component of
C_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}})
corresponding to
\tau_{1}
, we have

-e_{1}\wedge e_{2}+e_{1}\wedge e_{2}=0\in\mathcal{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\tau_{1}),

with the first
2
-wedge corresponding to
\sigma_{1}
and the second to
\sigma_{2}
.
Thus the fan is
\mathbb{Z}
-balanced, and there is a fundamental class
[\Sigma,w]\in H_{2}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{2}^{R})
. Moreover, it can be checked that this class generated the whole cohomology module, so that this fan is in fact uniquely
R
-balanced.

The above definition, which is equivalent to the usual balancing condition
[
BIMS15
, Definition 5.8]
, is similar in flavor to the description given by
[
BH17
, Theorem 2.9]
. We illustrate this with the following example.

3.14
.

In this example, we explicitly relate the above definition of balancing to the one given in
Definition

2.8
. Let
(\Sigma,w)
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-balanced fan of dimension
d
in the sense of
Definition

3.12
. Then, for each
\beta\in\Sigma^{d-1}
, we pick a generator
\Lambda_{\beta}\in L_{\mathbb{Z}}(\beta)
respecting the orientation. Now for each
\alpha\in\Sigma^{d}
with
\beta\prec\alpha
, the vector
v_{\alpha/\beta}
from
Definition

2.1
is such that
\Lambda_{\alpha}=\Lambda_{\beta}\wedge v_{\alpha/\beta}
.

Looking at the
\beta
-component of
\partial\colon C_{d}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d}^{\mathbb{Z}})\to C_{d-1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d}^{\mathbb{Z}})
, we have

\partial((w(\alpha)\Lambda_{\alpha}))_{\beta}=\sum_{\beta\prec\alpha}w(\alpha)\Lambda_{\alpha}=\Lambda_{\beta}\wedge\sum_{\beta\prec\alpha}w(\alpha)v_{\alpha/\beta}.

Therefore, the chain
\mathrm{Ch}(\Sigma,w)
is closed if and only if each of the faces
\beta
are balanced in the sense of
Definition

2.8
. Thus the definitions are equivalent.

3.15
.

Let
(\Sigma,w)
be an
R
-balanced fan and
\gamma\in\Sigma
a face. Then
(\gamma_{\succeq},w)
is
R
-balanced, where
w
is understood to be the weight function induced on
\gamma_{\succeq}
by
w
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

3.10
, we have that
H_{d}^{BM}(\gamma_{\succeq},\mathcal{F}_{d}^{R})
can be viewed as the kernel of the map

Tropical cap product

There is a cap product relating

3.16
[
Bou98

Let
M_{R}
be a rank

R
-module,
M_{R}^{*}
the dual module, and
0\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq d
. The

or

defined by

is the map

\displaystyle\lrcorner\,y\colon\bigwedge^{p_{1}}M_{R}^{*}

\displaystyle\to\bigwedge^{p_{2}-p_{1}}M_{R},

which is defined on

to be

x\,\lrcorner\,y=(-1)^{p_{1}(p_{1}-1)/2}\sum_{a}\mathrm{sign}(a)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{p_{1}}x_{i}(y_{a(i)})\right)y_{a(p+1)}\wedge\dots\wedge y_{a(d)},

where the sum is taken over all permutations

which are increasing on
1,\dots,p
and
p+1,\dots,d
, and is extended linearly.

3.17
.

In
[
Bou98
, Section III.11.10]

e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
be a basis of

e_{I}\coloneqq e_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{p_{2}}}\in\bigwedge^{p_{2}}M_{R}

\bigwedge^{p_{2}}M_{R}
. Letting

be the dual basis to the

for

. Then the contraction map defined by

is given by

\begin{cases}f_{K}\,\lrcorner\,e_{J}=0&if $K\not\subset J$,\\
f_{K}\,\lrcorner\,e_{J}=(-1)^{v+p_{1}(p_{1}-1)/2}\;e_{J\setminus K}&if $K\subseteq J$ and $p_{1}=|K|$,\end{cases}

where

such that

A proof that these contraction maps are the same as the formulation in terms of compositions given in

4.
Tropical Poincaré duality

In
Section

4.1
, we define TPD over a ring
R
, and give an example of a non-matroidal fan satisfying the duality. In
Section

4.2
, we give some necessary conditions for the duality to hold, along with a characterization by an Euler characteristic condition. Finally, in
Section

4.3

R
and study tropical fan hypersurfaces in

satisfying TPD. This forms a first step towards answering
1.1
.

4.1.
Definition and examples

In this subsection, we define what it means for a fan to satisfy TPD over a commutative ring
R
. When
R=\mathbb{Z}
, this is the definition from
[
JRS18
, Definition 5.2]
, and when
R=\mathbb{R}
, our definition can be shown to be equivalent to
[
JSS19
, Definition 4.12]
.

4.1
.

We say that an
R
-balanced rational polyhedral fan
\Sigma
of dimension
d
with weights
w
satisfies
tropical Poincaré duality over
R
if the cap product with the fundamental class

\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{p})\to H_{d-q}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d-p}^{R})

is an isomorphism for all
p,q=0,\dots,d
.

4.2
.

Returning again to
Example

2.4
, one can verify that all the possible cap products are isomorphisms, as we did explicitly in
Example

3.21
for the cap product
\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{1})\to H_{2}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}})

\mathbb{Z}
.

4.3
.

Similarly, explicit computations can be carried out for
Example

2.3
. Comparing back to
Example

3.8
, we have that
\dim_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R})=2
and
\dim_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}})=3
, while
\dim_{\mathbb{Z}}H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R})=2
and
\dim_{\mathbb{Z}}H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})=1
.

Thus the cap product maps

\displaystyle\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{0})

\displaystyle\to H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}),\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{1})

\displaystyle\to H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}})

are not isomorphisms, and the fan does not satisfy tropical Poincaré duality over
\mathbb{Z}
.

As mentioned in the introduction, the Bergman fans of matroids satisfy TPD over

and

4.2.
Necessary conditions for tropical Poincaré duality

We now turn to giving some necessary conditions for TPD to hold.

First, in light of
Proposition

3.7
, the Borel–Moore homology of fans satisfying TPD is concentrated in degree
d
. Indeed, by
Proposition

3.7
,
H^{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{p})=0
for
q\not=0
, hence the isomorphism
\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{p})\cong H_{d-q}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d-p}^{R})
gives
H_{q}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d-p}^{R})=0
for
q\not=d
.

Note also that, for
(\Sigma,w)
be an
R
-balanced fan satisfying TPD over
R
,
\Sigma
must be uniquely
R
-balanced by
w
. This is because the cap product maps
1\in R\cong H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{0})
(see
Proposition

3.7
) to
1\frown[\Sigma,w]=[\Sigma,w]\in H_{d}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d}^{R})
, which must be a generator. Then by
Definition

3.12
, the fan
\Sigma
is uniquely
R
-balanced.

4.5
.

For any ring
R
, the fan in
Section

2.1
is
R
-balanced, but not uniquely
R
-balanced by
Example

3.8
, hence it cannot satisfy TPD over
R
.

Now, assuming that we are working over a field
\mathbbm{k}
, and that the Borel–Moore homology of the fan vanishes in an appropriate way, we can determine that the fan satisfies TPD through an Euler characteristic argument.

4.6
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a field, and
(\Sigma,w)
be a
\mathbbm{k}
-balanced fan of dimension
d
. Suppose

for

. Then, for a given

, the cap product

\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{p})\to H_{d-q}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d-p}^{\mathbbm{k}})

is an isomorphism for all

if and only if

(1)

(-1)^{d}\chi(C_{\bullet}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d-p}^{\mathbbm{k}}))=\dim_{k}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{p}(v).

Moreover,
(\Sigma,w)
satisfies TPD over
\mathbbm{k}
if and only if
Equation

1
holds for all
p
.

Proof.

Since the only compact cell in
\Sigma
is the vertex

, we have

H^{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{p})=\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{p}(v)&if $q=0$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

By the vanishing condition on tropical Borel–Moore homology, the cap product
\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{q}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{p})\to H_{d-q}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d-p}^{\mathbbm{k}})

is then immediately an isomorphism for

, and

(2)

Since the cap product is injective by
Proposition

3.23
and we are working over a field, the maps

\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{p})\to H_{d}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d-p}^{\mathbbm{k}})

are isomorphisms if and only if
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{p})=\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{p}(v)
is equal to
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}H_{d}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{d-p}^{\mathbbm{k}})
. By

, this is exactly the claimed result.

4.7

\displaystyle\colon\quad\bigoplus_{\epsilon\in\Sigma^{1}}R\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}^{0}}R\to 0,\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle C_{\bullet}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R})

\displaystyle\colon\quad\bigoplus_{\epsilon\in\Sigma^{1}}\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R}(\epsilon)\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}^{1}}\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R}(v)\to 0.

Here

Similarly,
\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R}(v)=\sum_{\epsilon\in\Sigma^{1}}L_{R}(\epsilon)=\sum_{\epsilon\in\Sigma^{1}}\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R}(\epsilon)
, thus
\partial_{1}^{1}
is surjective, hence
H_{0}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R})=0
.
∎

4.8
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring, and
(\Sigma,w)
an
R
-balanced fan of dimension one.
Then
(\Sigma,w)
satisfies tropical Poincaré duality over
R
if and only if it is uniquely
R
-balanced and all the weights are units in
R
.

Proof.

We need to show that all four of the following cap products

(1)

\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{1}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{1})\to H_{0}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})
,

(2)

\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{1}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{0})\to H_{0}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R})
,

(3)

\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{0})\to H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R})
,

(4)

\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{1})\to H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})
,

are isomorphisms if and only if
(\Sigma,w)
is uniquely
R
-balanced and all the weights are units in
R
. We will show this in three parts:

(1)

First, we show that the maps (
1
) and (
2
) are trivial maps between zero-modules.

(2)

Then we show that (
3
) being an isomorphism is the definition of being uniquely
R
-balanced.

(3)

Finally, we show that (
4
) is an isomorphism if and only if
(\Sigma,w)
is uniquely
R
-balanced, with the added condition that all the weights are units in
R
.

In total, this will then show that
(\Sigma,w)
satisfies tropical Poincaré duality over
R
if and only if it is uniquely
R
-balanced and all the weights are units in
R
.

Beginning with
1
, by
Lemma

4.7
and
Proposition

3.7
, all involved modules are zero. Moreover the cap product map is zero by
Proposition

3.22
, hence the maps (
1
) and (
2
) are trivially isomorphisms.

Next for
2
, the map
\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{0})\to H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R})
is given by sending a scalar
\alpha\in H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{0})\cong R
to
\alpha\frown[\Sigma,w]
. The
0
-contraction of a scalar is multiplication by this scalar, so that
\alpha\frown[\Sigma,w]=\alpha\cdot[\Sigma,w]
. It is therefore an isomorphism if and only if
\langle[\Sigma,w]\rangle
generates
H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R})
, which is the definition of uniquely
R
-balanced (
Definition

3.12
).

Finally, we turn to
3
. We begin with some notation. Let
v
be the vertex of
\Sigma
and number the one-dimensional rays as
\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{m}
, with weights
w_{i}=w(\epsilon_{i})
.
The Borel–Moore cochain group is
C_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})=\oplus_{i=1}^{m}R
, which has a basis
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
, with
x_{i}
corresponding to
\epsilon_{i}
. The elements
x_{i}-x_{m}\in C_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})
, for
i=1,\dots,m-1
form a basis for
H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})=\ker(1\;1\;\dots\;1)
.
For each
\epsilon_{i}
, we select the generator
\Lambda_{i}\in L_{R}(\epsilon_{i})
compatible with the orientation of
\epsilon_{i}
, and let
\Theta_{i}\coloneqq\iota_{\epsilon_{i},v}(\Lambda_{i})
be its image under the inclusion
\iota_{\epsilon_{i},v}\colon\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R}(\epsilon_{i})\to\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R}(v)
.
Thus the fundamental class
[\Sigma,w]\in H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{1})
is explicitly the element
(w_{i}\Lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{m}\in H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R})=\ker((\iota_{\epsilon_{i},v})_{i=1}^{m})\subset C_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R})
.
The cap product map
\frown[\Sigma,w]\colon H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{1})\to H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})
takes a covector
\phi\in H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{1})=\mathcal{F}_{R}^{1}(v)
to the element

(w_{i}\phi(\Theta_{i}))_{i=1}^{m}\in H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R}).

Now, suppose all the weights
w_{i}
are units in
R
and
(\Sigma,w)
is uniquely
R
-balanced. Then the elements
w_{i}\Theta_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,m-1
, form a basis for
\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R}(v)
, with the corresponding dual basis
w_{i}^{-1}\Theta_{i}^{*}
for
\mathcal{F}_{R}^{1}(v)
. Then, for each
j=1,\dots,m-1
,

w_{j}^{-1}\Theta_{j}^{*}\frown[\Sigma,w]=(w_{i}w_{j}^{-1}\Theta_{j}^{*}(\Theta_{i}))_{i=1}^{m}=(0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,w_{m}w_{j}^{-1}\Theta_{j}^{*}(\Theta_{m})),

where the only two non-zero entries are in the
j
-th and
m
-th positions. Since this is a cycle in
C_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})
, we must have

1+w_{m}w_{j}^{-1}\Theta_{j}^{*}(\Theta_{m})=0,

so that

w_{j}^{-1}\Theta_{j}^{*}\frown[\Sigma,w]=x_{i}-x_{m}.

Thus the images of the basis elements
w_{j}^{-1}\Theta_{j}^{*}
of
\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R}(v)
form a basis of
H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})
, hence
\frown[\Sigma,w]
is an isomorphism.

For the converse direction, we show that if either the weights are non-units or the fan is not uniquely
R
-balanced, then the cap product is not an isomorphism.

First, suppose some weight
w_{k}
is not a unit in
R
. Then for any
\phi\in H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{R}^{1})=\mathcal{F}_{R}^{1}(v)
, the
k
-th component of
\phi\frown[\Sigma,w]
is contained in the ideal
\langle w_{k}\rangle\subset R
, which does not contain
1
. Hence the element
x_{k}-x_{m}
of
H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})
cannot be in the image of
\frown[\Sigma,w]
, which is therefore not surjective and hence not an isomorphism.

Finally, suppose that
\Sigma
is not uniquely
R
-balanced. Since
H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})
is free of rank
m-1
, we may assume that
\mathcal{F}_{R}^{1}(v)
is as well, otherwise there cannot be an isomorphism. Since
\Sigma
is not uniquely
R
-balanced,
\rank_{R}H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R})>1
, so that by working with the Euler characteristics, we must have
\rank_{R}\mathcal{F}_{1}^{R}(v)<m-1
. Dualizing, we obtain that
\rank_{R}\mathcal{F}_{R}^{1}(v)<m-1=\rank_{R}H_{1}^{BM}(\Sigma,\mathcal{F}_{0}^{R})
and so the cap product cannot be an isomorphism.
∎

4.11

Let

Proof.

By assumption, the very affine tropical cycle

We now turn to studying fans which satisfy TPD at every face. Using

. We use a different notion of the star of a face, but the equivalence of the definitions can be seen from
[

, Proposition 3.17]
, which in turn follows from the tropical Künneth theorem

, Theorem B]
.

6.
Tropical Poincaré duality for polyhedral spaces

In this section, we use the results of
Section

5

In
[

\mathbb{Z}

This suggests that the local TPD spaces characterized in

6.5

We refer to

Fuzzy Rate Analysis of Operators and its Applications in Linear Spaces


Abstract
In this paper, a new concept, the fuzzy rate of an operator in linear spaces is proposed for the very first time. Some properties and basic principles of it are studied. Fuzzy rate of an operator B B which is specific in a plane is discussed. As its application, a new fixed point existence theorem is proved. AMS Subject Classification. 46S40; 03E99; 26E50. Key Words and Phrases. Fuzzy rate; Operator; Membership function; Fixed point existence theorem.

Fuzzy Rate Analysis of Operators

In this section, we first give the concept of a fuzzy rate of an operator, then we show some basic properties of it.

Definition 3.1

Let
\cal X
be a linear space,
B:\cal X\to\cal X
be an operator,
\cal{B}(X)
=\{B|B:\cal X\to\cal X\}
,
F:\cal X

\to[0,1]
be a membership function over
\cal X
,
\cal P(X)
=\{F|F:\cal X
\to[0,1]\}
be a collection of all membership functions over
\cal X
. For any
\emptyset\neq\cal F(X)\subseteq\cal P(X)
, if

{\|B\|_{y}}=\sup_{F\in\cal F(X)}\frac{{F}(B(y))}{{F}(y)}

(3.1)

exists, then
{\|B\|_{y}}
is called a fuzzy rate of the operator
B
at the point
y\in\cal X
on
\cal F(X)
.

For Example 2.1,let
\cal F
(U)=\{F_{c_{(\mu,r)}}|\mu,r>0\}
by (2.2), we can achieve, obviously

\displaystyle{\|B\|}_{(0,r)}=\sup_{{F_{c_{(\mu,r)}}\in{\cal F}(U)}}\frac{{F_{c_{(\mu,r)}}(B(0,r))}}{{F_{c_{(\mu,r)}}((0,r))}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{{F_{c_{(\mu,r)}}\in{\cal F}(U)}}\frac{F_{c_{(\mu,r)}}((0,br))}{F_{c_{(\mu,r)}}((0,r))}

\displaystyle=\sup_{{F_{c_{(\mu,r)}}\in{\cal F}(U)}}\frac{e^{-(\frac{1}{b^{2}}-\mu)^{2}}}{e^{-(1-\mu)^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mu>0}e^{(1-\frac{1}{b^{4}})}e^{2\mu(\frac{1}{b^{2}}-1)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}+\infty,&|b|<1,\\
e^{(1-\frac{1}{b^{4}})},&|b|\geq 1.\end{cases}

At the same time, we have the following theorem about the relationship between a fuzzy rate of the operator and fuzzy sets.

Theorem 3.2

Let
\cal X
be a linear space,
B:\cal X\to\cal X
be an operator,
\cal{B}(X)
=\{B|B:\cal X\to\cal X\}
,
F:\cal X

\to[0,1]
be a membership function over
\cal X
,
\cal P(X)
=\{F|F:\cal X
\to[0,1]\}
be a collection of all membership functions over
\cal X
and
{\|B\|_{y}}
be the fuzzy rate of the operator
B
at the point
y\in\cal X
on
\cal F(X)
. Then for any
\emptyset\neq\cal F(X)\subseteq\cal P(X)
,
there exist two membership functions
{F},{G}\in\cal F(X)
such that, for each
y\in\cal X
,

{\|B\|_{y}}{F}(y)={G}(B(y)).

(3.2)

Proof.
For any
\emptyset\neq\cal F(X)\subseteq\cal P(X)
, since

{\|B\|_{y}}=\sup_{{F}\in\cal F(X)}\frac{{F}(B(y))}{{F}(y)}<+\infty,

then
{F}(y)\neq 0
.
\forall n
,
\exists

{G}_{n}\in\cal F(X)
, we arrive at

{\|B\|_{y}}\geq\frac{{G}_{n}(B(y))}{{G}_{n}(y)}>{\|B\|_{y}}-\frac{1}{n},

and

{\|B\|_{y}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{G}_{n}(B(y))}{{G}_{n}(y)}<+\infty

for each
y\in\cal X
.

Since
0<{G}_{n}(y),{G}_{n}(B(y))\leq 1
, there exist
{G}_{n_{k}}(y)\rightarrow{F}(y)
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
, and
{G}_{{n_{k}}_{m}}(B(y))\rightarrow{G}(B(y))
as
m\rightarrow+\infty
for any
y\in\cal X
.

If
0<{F}(y)\leq 1
, it follows that

{\|B\|_{y}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{G}_{n}(B(y))}{{G}_{n}(y)}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{{G}_{{n_{k}}_{m}}(B(y))}{{G}_{{n_{k}}_{m}}(y)}=\frac{\lim_{m\to\infty}{G}_{{n_{k}}_{m}}(B(y))}{\lim_{m\to\infty}{G}_{{n_{k}}_{m}}(y)}=\frac{{G}(B(y))}{{F}(y)},

and
{\|B\|_{y}}{F}(y)={G}(B(y))
for every
y\in\cal X
.

If
{F}(y)=0
, it implies that

{\|B\|_{y}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{G}_{n}(B(y))}{{G}_{n}(y)}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{{G}_{{n_{k}}_{m}}(B(y))}{{G}_{{n_{k}}_{m}}(y)}=\frac{\lim_{m\to\infty}{G}_{{n_{k}}_{m}}(B(y))}{\lim_{m\to\infty}{G}_{{n_{k}}_{m}}(y)}<+\infty,

and
\lim_{m\to\infty}{G}_{{n_{k}}_{m}}(B(y))=0
,
{G}(B(y))=\lim_{m\to\infty}{G}_{{n_{k}}_{m}}(B(y))=0
[
15
]
.

Therefore,
{\|B\|_{y}}{F}(y)={G}(B(y))
holds for any
y\in\cal X
.

It is easy to verify that the converse proposition of Theorem 3.2 holds. We reach

Theorem 3.3

Let
\cal X
be a linear space,
B:\cal X\to\cal X
be an operator,
\cal{B}(X)
=\{B|B:\cal X\to\cal X\}
,
F:\cal X

\to[0,1]
be a membership function over
\cal X
,
\cal P(X)
=\{F|F:\cal X
\to[0,1]\}
be a collection of all membership functions over
\cal X
. For any
\emptyset\neq\cal F(X)\subseteq\cal P(X)
, if there exist
{F}
,
{G}\in\cal F(X)
and
{F}(y)\neq 0
such that

{\|B\|_{y}}{F}(y)={G}(B(y)),

then for any
y\in\cal X
,

{\|B\|_{y}}=\sup_{{F}\in\cal F(X)}\frac{{F}(B(y))}{{F}(y)}=\frac{{G}(B(y))}{{F}(y)}<+\infty,

that is, the fuzzy rate of the operator
B
exists.

Theorem 3.4

\cal{B}(X)
=\{B|B:\cal X\to\cal X\}
,

\to[0,1]

\cal X

\to[0,1]\}

\cal X
. For any

,

and the identity operator

(1)
{\|B\|_{y}}>0

;

(2)
{\|I\|_{y}}=1
for any
y\in\cal X
;

(3) If

{\|aB_{1}\|_{y}}\leq{\|B_{1}\|_{(ay)}}{\|aI\|_{y}}
;

(4) If

for any

, then

{\|B_{1}\|}_{y}\geq{\|B_{2}\|}_{y};

(8) If

represents the fuzzy rate of the operator

on
{\cal F(X)}_{1}
and

, then for any

{\|B\|_{y}}_{{,\cal F(X)}_{1}}\leq{\|B\|_{y}}_{{,\cal F(X)}_{2}}.

Proof.
(1) It follows that
{\|B\|_{y}}\geq 0
for any

from Definition 3.1. On the other hand, if
{\|B\|_{y}}=0
, then
for any
{F}\in\cal F(X)
,
{F}(B(y))=0
. It is false because there exists a membership function
{F}\in\cal F(X)
where
{F}(z)=0.5
when

and
{F}(z)=0
when

.

(2) Because

is an identity operator, we obtain

{\|I\|_{y}}=\sup_{{F}\in\cal F(X)}\frac{{F}(I(y))}{{F}(y)}=\sup_{{F}\in\cal F(X)}\frac{{F}(I(y))}{{F}(y)}=1

for all
y\in\cal X
.

(3) If
B_{1}
is a linear operator and
a>0
is a real number, then

\displaystyle{\|aB_{1}\|_{y}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{{F}\in\cal F(X)}\frac{{F}(aB_{1}(y))}{{F}(y)}=\sup_{{F}\in\cal F(X)}(\frac{{F}(B_{1}(ay))}{{F}(ay)}\frac{{F}(ay)}{{F}(y)})

Applications–a new Fixed Point Existence Theorem

Fixed Point theory is very important and most generally useful one in classical function analysis.
In this section, we prove a new Fixed Point Existence Theorem with the fuzzy rate of the operator
B
as its application. First, we have

Lemma 4.1

Let
\cal X
be a linear space,
B:\cal X\to\cal X
be an operator,
\cal{B}(X)
=\{B|B:\cal X\to\cal X\}
,
F:\cal X

\to[0,1]
be a membership function over
\cal X
,
\cal P(X)
=\{F|F:\cal X
\to[0,1]\}
be a collection of all membership functions over
\cal X
. Let

{\|B\|_{y}}=\sup_{{F}\in\cal F(X)}\frac{{F}(B(y))}{{F}(y)}<+\infty

for
\emptyset\neq\cal F(X)\subseteq\cal P(X)
. If for
\delta\in(0,1]
, there exists a natural number
N
such that
{\|B^{n}\|_{y}}\geq\delta
as
n\geq N
, then there exists a
{F}_{0}\in\cal F(X)
such that

{F}_{0}(B^{n}(y))={F}_{0}(B^{n-1}(y)),

(4.1)

or

{F}_{0}(B(B^{n-1}(y)))={F}_{0}(B^{n-1}(y)),

(4.2)

for
n\geq N
.

Proof.
Note that
B^{n}(y)=B^{n-1}(B(y))
for
n=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot
.

If
\delta\in(0,1]
, there exists a natural number
N
such that
{\|B^{n}\|_{y}}\geq\delta
as
n\geq N
. Then we know

1\leq\frac{1}{\prod_{1\leq k\leq n}{\|B\|}_{B^{k-1}(y)}}\leq\frac{1}{{\|B^{n}\|}_{y}}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}<+\infty

for
n\geq N
by (2.3), and

0\leq\sum^{n}_{k=1}-\ln{\|B\|}_{B^{k-1}(y)}\leq-\ln{\|B^{n}\|}_{y}\leq-\ln\delta<+\infty,

hence
\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\ln{\|B\|}_{B^{k-1}(y)}=0
and
\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\|B\|}_{B^{k-1}(y)}=1
[
15
]
.

It follows that for any natural number
m
there exists a
M
, as
k>max\{M,N\}
such that

1-\frac{1}{m}<{\|B\|}_{B^{k-1}(y)}<1+\frac{1}{m}.

Since

{\|B\|}_{B^{k-1}(y)}=\sup_{{F}\in\cal F(X)}\frac{{F}(B(B^{k-1}(y)))}{{F}(B^{k-1}(y))}<+\infty,

there exists a membership function
{F}_{0}\in\cal F(X)
such that

1-\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{m}<{\|B\|}_{B^{k-1}(y)}-\frac{1}{m}<\frac{{F}_{0}(B(B^{k-1}(y)))}{{F}_{0}(B^{k-1}(y))}<{\|B\|}_{B^{k-1}(y)}+\frac{1}{m}<1+\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{m}.

Letting
m\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain

\frac{{F}_{0}(B(B^{k-1}(y)))}{{F}_{0}(B^{k-1}(y))}=1,

that’s to say,
{F}_{0}(B(B^{k-1}(y)))={F}_{0}(B^{k-1}(y))
.

Then, we give the definition of a quasi-fixed point of the operator
B
with respect to the membership function
{F}
.

Definition 4.2

Let
\cal X
be a linear space,
B:\cal X\to\cal X
be an operator,
\cal{B}(X)
=\{B|B:\cal X\to\cal X\}
,
F:\cal X

\to[0,1]
be a membership function over
\cal X
,
\cal P(X)
=\{F|F:\cal X
\to[0,1]\}
be a collection of all membership functions over
\cal X
.
For
y\in\cal X
, if there exists
{F}\in\cal P(X)
such that
{F}(B(y))={F}(y)
, then
y
is called a quasi-fixed point of the operator
B
with respect to
{F}
.

By the proof of Lemma 4.1,
B^{k-1}(y)
is a quasi-fixed point of the operator
B
with respect to
{F}
.

Now, Fixed Point Existence Theorem with respect to
{F}
is presented.

Theorem 4.3

(new Fixed Point Existence Theorem)
Let
\cal X
be a linear space,
B:\cal X\to\cal X
be an operator,
\cal{B}(X)
=\{B|B:\cal X\to\cal X\}
,
F:\cal X

\to[0,1]
be a membership function over
\cal X
,
\cal P(X)
=\{F|F:\cal X
\to[0,1]\}
be a collection of all membership functions over
\cal X
.
Presume

{\|B\|_{y}}=\sup_{{F}\in\cal F(X)}\frac{{F}(B(y))}{{F}(y)}<+\infty

for
\emptyset\neq\cal F(X)\subseteq\cal P(X).
If
\delta\in(0,1],
there exists a natural number
N
such that
{\|B^{n}\|_{y}}\geq\delta
as
n\geq N
. Then if there exists an injection functional
{F}_{0}\in\cal F(X)
such that

{F}_{0}(B^{n}(y))={F}_{0}(B^{n-1}(y)),

(4.3)

B^{n-1}(y)
is a fixed point of the operator
B
with respect to
{F}
, and
y
is a fixed point of the operator
B^{n}
with respect to
{F}
for
n\geq N
.

Proof.
It follows directly that the result holds from (4.2) and the injective condition of functional
{F}_{0}\in\cal F(X)
.

Like the classical fixed point theory applied to differential equations, we believe that the Fixed Point Existence Theorem with respect to fuzzy set
{F}
might be applied to fuzzy equations or fuzzy differential equations. They are worth further studying in the future.

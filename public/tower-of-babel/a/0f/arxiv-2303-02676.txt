1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, by a
measure preserving system
or a
system
, we mean a Lebesgue space
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
with an invertible measure preserving transformation
T:X\rightarrow X
. We write a system as
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
. A system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is ergodic if the only
T
-invariant subsets in
\mathcal{B}
have measure
0
or
1
.

In 1941, Wiener and Wintner strengthened the classical Birkhoff’s pointwise ergodic theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
21
]
)
For a system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
, and any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, there exists a full measure subset
X(f)
of
X
such that for any
x\in X(f)
, and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the limit

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{2\pi int}f(T^{n}x)

exists.

The core of the Wiener–Wintner Theorem is that
X(f)
is independent of the choice of
t
. Since
\mathbb{R}
is uncountable, the result leaded reseachers to focus on general phenomena in ergodic theory where samplings are good for an uncountable number of systems. And for the convergent behavior of some polynomial ergodic averages, the Wiener–Wintner Theorem can also help us to understand it.

In 2009, Host and Kra built the following result, which is called generalized Wiener–Wintner Theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
13
, Theorem 2.22]
)
For a system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
, and any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, there exists a full measure subset
X(f)
of
X
such that for any
x\in X(f)
, and any nilsequence
(
for definition, see Subsection
2.2
.
)

\textbf{b}=\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, the limit

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}b_{n}f(T^{n}x)

exists.

Note that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{e^{2\pi int}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a nilsequence since
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
is a
1
-step nilmanifold. So the above statements generalize Wiener-Wintner theorem. In addition, Host and Kra pointed out the following result in
[
14
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(
[
14
, Theorem 23.5]
)
Given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{d}
be distinct non-zero integers. Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be a measure preserving system. Then for any
f_{1},\cdots,f_{d}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and any nilsequence
\textbf{b}=\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
,

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}b_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)

exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

The averages which are mentioned in the above theorem are called multilinear Wiener-Wintner type ergodic averages. The above theorem guarantees the norm convergence of multilinear Wiener-Wintner type ergodic averages. For extra results related to the above theorem, one can see
[
16
, Theorem B.4]
.

Based on Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
, there is a natural question: can we generalize Theorem
1.2
to multilinear version under the hypothesis of pointwise convergence of multilinear ergodic averages?

For the bilinear case, one can find some results in
[
4
,
2
]
. For the general case, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{d}
be distinct non-zero integers. Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be a measure preserving system. Then for any
h_{1},\cdots,h_{d}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\prod_{j=1}^{d}h_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)

exists almost everywhere if and only if for any
f_{1},\cdots,f_{d}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, there exists full measure subset
X(f_{1},\cdots,f_{d})
of
X
such that for any
x\in X(f_{1},\cdots,f_{d})
, and any nilsequence
\textbf{b}=\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
,

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}b_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)

exists.

In 2015, Zorin-Kranich
[
22
, Corollary 1.4]
had pointed out the above theorem for ergodic systems and provided an inductive proof via harmonic analsis’s method. In this paper, the author will give a purely ergodic theoretic proof for the above theorem based on Host-Kra factor and determine related characteristic factor.

Based on the above theorem,
[
5
, Theorem]
,
[

[

Let

and

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}b_{n}f_{1}(T^{an}x)f_{2}(T^{bn}x)

exists.

Corollary 1.6
.

Given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{d}
be distinct non-zero integers. Let

be an ergodic distal measure preserving system

(1)

We remark for the

Next, we apply Theorem
1.4
to study some polynomial ergodic averages for multiple measure preserving transformations under lacking complete commutativity. Due to lacking complete commutativity, we have to do some restrictions for transformations such as zero entropy which can help us to use disjointness between zero entropy and completely positive entropy to attack some polynomial ergodic averages. The reason for which Theorem
1.4
can work for some polynomial ergodic averages is the characteristic behavior of
\infty
-step factor
(
for definition, see Remark
2.3
.
)
.

Recently, Frantzikinakis and Host established a result on norm convergence of some polynomial ergodic averages.

Theorem 1.7
.

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
)
Let
T,S
be invertible measure preserving transformations acting on a Lebesgue space
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
such that
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
has zero entropy and let
p(n)
be a non-constant non-linear integer coefficents polynomial. Then for any
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the limit

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)g(S^{p(n)}x)

exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

The zero entropy assumption on
T
in the above result is necessary. If there is no zero entropy restriction for
T
, one can find a counterexample from
[
9
, Proof of Proposition 1.4]
. Motivated by the above theorem, we establish the following result.

Theorem 1.8
.

Given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
T,S_{1},\cdots,S_{d}
be invertible measure preserving transformations acting on a Lebesgue space
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
such that
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
has zero entropy and
S_{1},\cdots,S_{d}
are commuting and let
p_{1}(n),\cdots,p_{d}(n)
be non-constant non-linear integer coefficents polynomials with distinct degrees. Let
a
and
b
be two distinct non-zero integers. Then for any
f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},\cdots,g_{d}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the limit

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{1}(T^{an}x)f_{2}(T^{bn}x)\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}(S_{j}^{p_{j}(n)}x)

exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

Preliminaries

In this section we recall some basic notions and results.

2.1.
Factor and joining

A
factor
of a system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is a
T
-invariant sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{B}
. A
factor map
from
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
to
(Y,\mathcal{D},\nu,S)
is a measurable map
\pi:X_{0}\rightarrow Y_{0}
with
\pi\circ T=S\circ\pi
and such that
\nu
is the image of
\mu
under
\pi
where
X_{0}
is a
T
-invariant full measurable subset of
X
and
Y_{0}
is a
S
-invariant full measurable subset of
Y
. In this case,
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{D})
is a factor of
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
and every factor of
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
can be obtained in this way.

A
joining
of two systems
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
and
(Y,\mathcal{D},\nu,S)
is a probability measure
\lambda
on
X\times Y
, invariant under
T\times S
and whose projections on
X
and
Y
are
\mu
and
\nu
respectively. Likely, we can define joining on more systems.

To study non-conventional ergodic averages on
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
,
Furstenberg’s self-joining
was introduced.

At first, we claim some notations. Given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
X^{d}
denote the space
X\times\cdots\times X(d\ times)
where an element of
X^{d}
can be written as
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})
and
\mathcal{B}^{\otimes d}
denote the product
\sigma
-algebra.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1}n,\cdots,a_{d}n\}
where
a_{1},\cdots,a_{d}
are distinct non-zero integers. We define a measure
\mu^{\mathcal{A}}_{d}
on
(X^{d},\mathcal{B}^{\otimes d})
by

\mu^{\mathcal{A}}_{d}(A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{d})=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{X}\prod_{i=1}^{d}T^{a_{i}n}1_{A_{i}}d\mu

where
A_{i}\in\mathcal{B},1\leq i\leq d
. Existence of the above limit can be guaranteed by
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
.
We refer to
\mu^{\mathcal{A}}_{d}
as the Furstenberg’s self-joining of
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
with respect to polynomial family
\mathcal{A}
in the light of its historical genesis in Furstenberg’s work on the ergodic-theoretic proof for Szemerédi’s theorem
[
11
]
. By an easy observation, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

(X^{d},\mathcal{B}^{\otimes d},\mu^{\mathcal{A}}_{d},T^{a_{1}}\times\cdots\times T^{a_{d}})
is a measure preserving system.

2.2.
Nilsystem and nilsequence

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. A
k
-step nilmanifold

X
is the quotient space
G/\Gamma
where
G
is a
k
-step nilpotent Lie group and
\Gamma
is a cocompact discrete subgroup of

. The group

acts on

by left translations. The unique invariant Borel probability measure on

under the action is called the Harr measure of
X
denoted by

. If

and

is the translation

, the system
(X,\mathcal{B}_{X},T_{a},m_{X})
is a
k
-step nilsystem
where
\mathcal{B}_{X}
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
X
.

A
basic
k
-step nilsequence
is the sequence
\{f(a^{n}\cdot x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
where
f\in C(X),a\in G,x\in X
. A
k
-step nilsequence
is a uniform limit of basic

-step nilsequences. Clearly, all

-step nilsequences is an invariant algebra of

[
14
, Chapter 11]

2.3.
Host-Kra seminorm and local seminorm

In 2005, Host and Kra introduced Host-Kra seminorm on
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
when they considered the norm convergence of non-conventional ergodic averages in

At first, we introdue some notations. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
V_{k}=\{0,1\}^{k}

V_{k}

\underline{\epsilon}=(\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{k})

\mu=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{w}dm(w)

with respect to

(X^{[1]},\mathcal{B}^{\otimes[1]},\mu^{[1]},T^{[1]})

\mu^{[k+1]}

(\Omega_{k},\mathcal{F}_{k},m_{k},S_{k})

\mathcal{I}(T^{[k]})

\sigma

2.4.
Furstenberg systems for sequences

Definition 2.9

Let

\Omega

3.1.
An estimation for cubic averages

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
V_{k}^{*}=V_{k}\backslash\{(0,\cdots,0)\}
. Define
\phi:V_{k}^{*}\rightarrow\{1,2,\cdots,2^{k}-1\},\underline{\epsilon}=(\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{k})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon_{i}\cdot 2^{i-1}
. For any
1\leq i\leq k
, let
V_{k,i}^{*}=\{\underline{\epsilon}\in V_{k}^{*}:\epsilon_{i}=0\}
. For any
1\leq i,j\leq k
with
i\neq j
, let
A^{j}_{i}=V_{k,i}^{*}\backslash V_{k,j}^{*}
.

Let
\{a_{j,n}\}_{1\leq j\leq 2^{k}-1,n\geq 0}
be a collection of bounded real sequences to be bounded by one. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Define
k
-step cubic averages

C(N;a_{1,n},\cdots,a_{2^{k}-1,n})=\frac{1}{N^{k}}\sum_{\underline{h}\in[N]^{k}}\prod_{\underline{\epsilon}\in V_{k}^{*}}a_{\phi(\underline{\epsilon}),\underline{\epsilon}\cdot\underline{h}}.

For example, when
k=3
,

C(N;a_{1,n},\cdots,a_{7,n})=\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{\underline{h}\in[N]^{3}}a_{1,h_{1}}a_{2,h_{2}}a_{3,h_{1}+h_{2}}a_{4,h_{3}}a_{5,h_{3}+h_{1}}a_{6,h_{3}+h_{2}}a_{7,h_{3}+h_{2}+h_{1}}.

When
k=2
, Assani built the following estimation.

Proposition 3.1
.

(
[
1
, Lemma 5]
)
Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\{c_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be three bound real sequences to be bounded by one. For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\Big|\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{n,m=0}^{N-1}a_{n}b_{m}c_{n+m}\Big|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}b_{m}c_{n+m}\Big|^{2}\leq\sup_{t}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=0}^{2(N-1)}e^{2\pi imt}c_{m}\Big|^{2}.

Actually, Assani also gave an estimation for
3
-step cubic averages in
[
1
, Lemma 6]
and pointed out that one can build similar result for usual case. Here, we give the specific statements of general case and provide a proof for the completeness. The essence of the following is to let one index vanish.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
k,N\in\mathbb{N}
with
k>2
, let
\{a_{j,n}\}_{1\leq j\leq 2^{k}-1,n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a collection of bounded real sequences to be bounded by one. Then

|C(N;a_{1,n},\cdots,a_{2^{k}-1,n})|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{N^{k}}\sum_{h_{1},\cdots,h_{k-2}=0}^{N-1}\sup_{t}\Big|\sum_{h_{k}=0}^{N-1}e^{2\pi ih_{k}t}\prod_{\underline{\epsilon}\in A^{k}_{k-1}}a_{\phi(\underline{\epsilon}),\epsilon_{k}h_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\epsilon_{i}h_{i}}\Big|^{2}

where
C
is an absolutely constant which can take value 2.

Proof.

\displaystyle|C(N;a_{1,n},\cdots,a_{2^{k}-1,n})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N^{k-1}}\sum_{h_{1},\cdots,h_{k-1}=0}^{N-1}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{h_{k}=0}^{N-1}\prod_{\underline{\epsilon}\in V_{k}^{*}\backslash V_{k,k}^{*}}a_{\phi(\underline{\epsilon}),\sum_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon_{i}h_{i}}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{k-1}}\sum_{h_{1},\cdots,h_{k-1}=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{h_{k}=0}^{N-1}e^{-2\pi ih_{k}t}\prod_{\underline{\epsilon}\in A^{k}_{k-1}}a_{\phi(\underline{\epsilon}),\epsilon_{k}h_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\epsilon_{i}h_{i}}\Big)\cdot

\displaystyle\ \ \ \Big(\sum_{m=0}^{2(N-1)}e^{2\pi imt}\prod_{\underline{\epsilon}\in V_{k}^{*}\backslash(V_{k,k-1}^{*}\cup V_{k,k}^{*})}a_{\phi(\underline{\epsilon}),m+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\epsilon_{i}h_{i}}\Big)e^{-2\pi ih_{k-1}t}dt\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N^{k-1}}\sum_{h_{1},\cdots,h_{k-2}=0}^{N-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big|\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{h_{k}=0}^{N-1}e^{-2\pi ih_{k}t}\prod_{\underline{\epsilon}\in A^{k}_{k-1}}a_{\phi(\underline{\epsilon}),\epsilon_{k}h_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\epsilon_{i}h_{i}}\Big)\cdot

\displaystyle\ \ \ \Big(\sum_{m=0}^{2(N-1)}e^{2\pi imt}\prod_{\underline{\epsilon}\in V_{k}^{*}\backslash(V_{k,k-1}^{*}\cup V_{k,k}^{*})}a_{\phi(\underline{\epsilon}),m+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\epsilon_{i}h_{i}}\Big)\Big|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N^{k-1}}\sum_{h_{1},\cdots,h_{k-2}=0}^{N-1}\Big((2(N-1)+1)\sup_{t}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{h_{k}=0}^{N-1}e^{-2\pi ih_{k}t}\prod_{\underline{\epsilon}\in A^{k}_{k-1}}a_{\phi(\underline{\epsilon}),\epsilon_{k}h_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\epsilon_{i}h_{i}}\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{N^{k-2}}\sum_{h_{1},\cdots,h_{k-2}=0}^{N-1}\sup_{t}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{h_{k}=0}^{N-1}e^{-2\pi ih_{k}t}\prod_{\underline{\epsilon}\in A^{k}_{k-1}}a_{\phi(\underline{\epsilon}),\epsilon_{k}h_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\epsilon_{i}h_{i}}\Big|^{2}

where the first inequality comes from Jesen’s inequality and the second inequality comes from Parseval’s identity. This finishes the proof.
∎

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N^{\prime\prime}_{x,k}}\sum_{n=1}^{N^{\prime\prime}_{x,k}}f_{1}(T^{an}x)f_{2}(T^{bn}x)\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}(S_{j}^{p_{j}(n)}x)=\int F_{0}\otimes G_{0}^{a}\otimes G_{0}^{b}d\rho_{x}=0.

It is a contradiction. So

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{1}(T^{an}x)f_{2}(T^{bn}x)\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}(S_{j}^{p_{j}(n)}x)=0

in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

Part II
: we verify the following: If for any
1\leq j\leq d
,
g_{j}
is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(S_{j})
, then

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{1}(T^{an}x)f_{2}(T^{bn}x)\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}(S_{j}^{p_{j}(n)}x)

exists almost everywhere.

By
[
14
, Theorem 14.15,16.10]
, we know that for any
1\leq j\leq d

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{1}(T^{an}x)f_{2}(T^{bn}x)\prod_{j=1}^{d}h_{j,m}(S_{j}^{p_{j}(n)}x)

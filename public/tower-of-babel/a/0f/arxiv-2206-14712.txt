Extremes of reflecting Gaussian processes on discrete Grid
Krzysztof Dȩbicki, Grigori Jasnovidov

Abstract




Abstract:
For
\{X(t),t\in G_{\delta}\}
a centered Gaussian process
with stationary increments on a discrete grid

G_{\delta}=\{0,\delta,2\delta,...\}
, where
\delta>0
,
we investigate
the stationary reflected process

Q_{\delta,X}(t)=\sup\limits_{s\in[t,\infty)\cap G_{\delta}}\big(X(s)-X(t)-c(s-t)\big),\ t\in G_{\delta}

with
c>0
.
We
derive the exact asymptotics of

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]\cap G_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,X}(t)>u\right)

and

\mathbb{P}\left(\inf\limits_{t\in[0,T]\cap G_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,X}(t)>u\right),

as
u\to\infty
, with
T>0
.
It appears that
\varphi=\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\sigma^{2}(u)}{u}
determines the asymptotics, leading to three
qualitatively different scenarios:

\varphi=0
,
\varphi\in(0,\infty)
and
\varphi=\infty
.

Key Words
: Gaussian process; storage process;
exact asymptotics; Piterbarg property; fractional Brownian motion;
discrete grid.

AMS Classification:
Primary 60G15; secondary 60G70, 60K25

1.
Introduction

For
X(t),t\geq 0
a centered Gaussian process with a.s. continuous sample paths,
stationary increments and
variance function
\sigma^{2}(t):=\var\left(X(t)\right)
such that
\sigma^{2}(0)=0
,
consider the
reflected
(at
0
) process

(1)

\hat{Q}_{X}(t)=X(t)-ct+\max\left(\hat{Q}_{X}(0),-\inf_{s\in[0,t]}(X(s)-cs)\right),\ t\geq 0,

where
c>0
.

Due to its relation with the solution of
the
Skorokhod problem
that describes the dynamics of
the
buffer content process

in a
fluid
queue fed by
X
and emptied at rate
c
,
properties of
\hat{Q}_{X}
have been investigated for a wide class of processes
X
,
see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
and references therein.
Within this framework, the process
\hat{Q}_{X}(t)

is called in the literature as the
storage process

[
3
,
5
]
.

Distributional properties of the unique stationary solution of (
1
), which has the following representation

(2)

Q_{X}(t)=\sup_{t\leq s}\left(X(s)-X(t)-c(s-t)\right),

were intensively analyzed.
In particular, the exact asymptotics of
\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{X}(0)>u\right)
,
as
u\to\infty
,
was derived for a wide class of Gaussian processes
X
;
see, e.g.,
[
2
,
6
,
4
,
7
]
and references therein.
An important direction of research on extremal behaviour of
the process
Q_{X}(t),t\geq 0
was initiated by Piterbarg
[
3
]
,
where asymptotics of
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}Q_{B_{\alpha}}(t)

was derived, with
B_{\alpha}(t)
a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Notably, the following asymptotic equivalence

\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{B_{\alpha}}(0)>u\right)\sim\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}Q_{B_{\alpha}}(t)>u\right)

for
\alpha>1/2
, called in
[
5
]
the
Piterbarg property
, took particular interest
and was further observed for more broad class of processes
X

[
5
,
8
,
4
]
.
Complementary, in
[
4
]
it was shown that if
\alpha>1/2
, then the process
Q_{B_{\alpha}}
also possesses

strong Piterbarg
property, i.e. for all
T>0

\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{B_{\alpha}}(0)>u\right)\sim\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{t\in[0,T]}Q_{B_{\alpha}}(t)>u\right)

as
u\to\infty
.

From the point of view of the stochastic modelling or simulation techniques,
discrete-time models
frequently appear to be more natural.
However, despite of its relevance in modelling of, e.g., queueing systems, much less is known
on distributional properties of the discrete counterpart of (
2
), i.e.,

(3)

\displaystyle Q_{\delta,X}(t)=\sup\limits_{s\in[t,\infty)\cap G_{\delta}}\big(X(s)-X(t)-c(s-t)\big),\ t\in G_{\delta},

where

G_{\delta}=\{0,\delta,2\delta,\ldots,\}
.
A notable exceptions are recent works
[
9
,
10
]
,
where the exact asymptotics of
\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{\delta,B_{\alpha}}(0)>u\right)
, as
u\to\infty
, was derived.

In this contribution we extend the findings of

[
9
,
11
,
10
]
to a more general class of
Gaussian processes with stationary increments and derive the
exact asymptotics of

(4)

\displaystyle\psi_{T,\delta}^{\sup}(u):=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]\cap G_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,X}(t)>u\right),\quad\psi^{\inf}_{T,\delta}(u):=\mathbb{P}\left(\inf\limits_{t\in[0,T]\cap G_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,X}(t)>u\right),

as
u\to\infty
, for
T>0
and
\delta>0
, complementing results for
continuous time given in
[
3
,
4
]
.

It appears that the influence of the grid size
\delta

on the asymptotics of (
4
) strongly depends on
the value of

(5)

\displaystyle\varphi:=\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sigma^{2}(u)}{u}\in[0,\infty],

leading to three scenarios:
\varphi=0
,
\varphi\in(0,\infty)
and
\varphi=\infty
.
The case
\varphi=\infty
leads to the same asymptotics as its continuous-time counterpart,
which reflects the
long-range dependance property
of
X
when its variance
\sigma^{2}
is
superlinear. On the other hand, the asymptotics for the case
\varphi=0

strongly depends on the grid size and is asymptotically negligible with respect to its continuous
counterpart. The third case
\varphi\in(0,\infty)
needs particularly precise analysis and
leads to the asymptotics which differs with the continuous-time case only by a constant (depending on
\delta
).

Outline of the paper.
We introduce notation and assumptions on the process
X
in Section
2
.
Then, in Theorem
2.1
we derive exact asymptotics of

\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{\delta,X}(0)>u\right)
as
u\to\infty
. Main results of this contribution are presented in Section
3
.
In Section
4
we illustrate the main findings of this paper by analysis of two important classes
of Gaussian processes, i.e. fractional Brownian motions and Gaussian integrated processes.
Section
5
consists of the proofs of the results derived in this paper.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

Let
X(t),t\in\mathbb{R}
be a centered Gaussian process with stationary increments, as introduced in Section
1
.
Suppose that

A
:
\sigma^{2}
is regularly varying at
\infty
with
index
2\alpha\in(0,2)

and
\sigma^{2}(t)
is twice continuously differentiable
for any
t\in(0,\infty)
.
Further, the first and second derivatives of
\sigma^{2}

are ultimately monotone.

We note that it follows straightforwardly from
A
that

\varphi=0
if
\alpha<1/2
while for
\alpha>1/2
we have
\varphi=\infty
.
It appears that case
\alpha=1/2
, i.e. when
\sigma^{2}
is asymptotically close to a linear function,
needs particularly precise analysis, for which the following condition is a tractable assumption:

B
: If
\sigma^{2}
satisfies
A
with
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, then

\varphi>0.

Condition
B
excludes the cases when
\alpha=1/2
but
\varphi=0
.
For this scenario we were able to give only partial results.
We refer to Remark
3.2
for the discussion of
the extension of the results derived in Section
3

to the case
\alpha=1/2,\varphi=0
under an additional constrain on the variance
function
\sigma^{2}
of
X
.

Conditions
A
and
B
are satisfied for a wide class of Gaussian processes with stationary increments,
including family of fractional Brownian
motions and integrated stationary Gaussian processes; see Section
4
for details.
We note that quantity
\varphi
already appeared in

[
7
]
, where it was observed that the form of the asymptotic behavior, as
u\to\infty
,
of
\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{X}(0)>u\right)

introduced in (
2
)
is
determined by the value of
\varphi
.
Let

(6)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \mathcal{H}_{\xi}(M)=\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in M}e^{\sqrt{2}\xi(t)-\var(\xi(t))}\right)\in(0,\infty),\quad\mathcal{G}_{\xi}(M)=\mathbb{E}\left(\inf\limits_{t\in M}e^{\sqrt{2}\xi(t)-\var(\xi(t))}\right)\in(0,\infty),

where
M
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}

and
\xi(t),\ t\in\mathbb{R}
is a Gaussian process
with stationary increments and a.s. continuous
sample paths.
Then, we define
Pickands constant
by

\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{\delta}=\lim_{S\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}([0,S]\cap\delta\mathbb{Z})}{S},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \delta\geq 0,

where we set

\delta\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{R}^{+}
if
\delta=0
. We refer to
[
12
]
for properties of

\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{0}
. In Lemma

5.3

we prove that for
\delta>0

it is sufficient to suppose that

\xi
satisfies
A
to claim that
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{\delta}\in(0,\infty)
.
Later on for
\delta=0
we simply write
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}

instead of
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{0}
.

Next, let us recall the findings of

[
7
]
[Proposition 2] (see also
[
8
]
[Theorems 3.1-3.3])
for the asymptotics of

\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{X}(0)>u\right)
, as
u\to\infty
, which will be a useful benchmark for the results
derived in the next section.
Let
\overleftarrow{\sigma}(t),t\geq 0

stand for the asymptotic inverse function of
\sigma
,
i.e.,
\overleftarrow{\sigma}(x)=\inf\{y\in[0,\infty):f(y)>x\}

(for details and properties of the asymptotic inverse
functions see, e.g.,
[
13
]
)
and let

(7)

\displaystyle t_{*}=\frac{\alpha}{c(1-\alpha)},\ \ \ \ m(u)=\inf\limits_{t>0}\frac{u(1+ct)}{\sigma(ut)},\ \ \ \ \Delta(u)=\begin{cases}\overleftarrow{\sigma}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\sigma^{2}(ut_{*})}{u(1+ct_{*})}\right),&\varphi\notin(0,\infty)\\
1,&\varphi\in(0,\infty).\end{cases}

Let for
X
such that
\varphi\in(0,\infty)
,

(8)

\displaystyle\eta(t)=\frac{c\sqrt{2}}{\varphi}X(t),\ \ \ \ \ t\geq 0.

As shown in
[
7
]
[Proposition 2], if
\sigma^{2}
is regularly varying at
0
with index
2\alpha_{0}\in(0,2]
and

A
is satisfied, then
for
\hat{Q}_{X}
defined in (
1
), we have

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{Q}_{X}(0)>u\right)\sim f(u)\Psi(m(u))\times\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}},&\varphi=\infty\\
\mathcal{H}_{\eta},&\varphi\in(0,\infty)\\
\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha_{0}}},&\varphi=0\end{cases},\quad u\to\infty,

where
\Psi
is the survival function of a standard Gaussian random
variable and

(10)

\displaystyle f(u)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi A}{B}}\frac{u}{m(u)\Delta(u)},\ \ \ \ A=\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)t_{*}^{\alpha}},\quad B=\frac{\alpha}{t_{*}^{\alpha+2}}.

The following result establishes the asymptotics of
\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{\delta,X}(0)>u\right)
for

\delta>0
as
u\to\infty
.
It generalizes the findings of
[
9
,
10
]
,
where the special case of
X
being a fractional Brownian motion was analyzed.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X(t),t\geq 0
be a centered Gaussian process
with continuous trajectories and stationary increments satisfying

A, B
.
Then, for
\delta>0
, as
u\to\infty
, it holds that

(11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{\delta,X}(0)>u\right)\sim\Psi(m(u))\times\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\alpha}u}{\delta c(1-\alpha)^{3/2}m(u)},&\varphi=0\\
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}^{\delta}f(u),&\varphi\in(0,\infty)\\
\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}}f(u),&\varphi=\infty.\end{cases}

Remark 2.2
.

Providing that both the asymptotics (
9
) and
(
11
) hold, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{\delta,X}(0)>u|Q_{X}(0)>u\right)=\begin{cases}0,&\varphi=0,\\
\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\eta}^{\delta}}{\mathcal{H}_{\eta}},&\varphi\in(0,\infty),\\
1,&\varphi=\infty.\end{cases}

3.
Main Results

In this section we derive the exact asymptotics of

(12)

\displaystyle\psi_{T,\delta}^{\sup}(u):=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,X}(t)>u\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi^{\inf}_{T,\delta}(u):=\mathbb{P}\left(\inf\limits_{t\in[0,T]_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,X}(t)>u\right),

as
u\to\infty
,
for
T>0
and
\delta>0
,
where for any real
a<b
and positive
\delta

[a,b]_{\delta}=[a,b]\cap\delta\mathbb{Z}.

We begin with the asymptotics of
\psi_{T,\delta}^{\sup}(u)
as
u\to\infty
.
Let in the following
[\cdot]
stand for the integer part.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X(t),t\geq 0
be a centered Gaussian process
with continuous trajectories and stationary increments satisfying

A-B
.
Then for
\delta>0
as
u\to\infty
it holds that

\displaystyle\psi_{T,\delta}^{\sup}(u)\sim\Psi(m(u))\times\begin{cases}(1+[\frac{T}{\delta}])\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\alpha}u}{\delta c(1-\alpha)^{3/2}m(u)},&\varphi=0\\
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}([0,T]_{\delta})\mathcal{H}_{\eta}^{\delta}f(u),&\varphi\in(0,\infty)\\
\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}}f(u),&\varphi=\infty.\end{cases}

Remark 3.2
.

It follows straightforwardly from the proof of Theorem
2.1
and
3.1

that condition
B
can be relaxed a bit.
Namely, if

\varphi=0
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
and
for
\kappa=\sqrt{c\!\!\inf\limits_{t\in\{\delta,2\delta,...\}}\sigma(t)}-\varepsilon
, with sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,

(13)

\displaystyle\sigma(u)\leq\kappa\frac{\sqrt{u}}{\ln^{1/4}u},\ \ \ \ u\to\infty

then both Theorem
2.1
and
3.1
hold.
If
\varphi=0
and
\alpha=1/2
in the Theorem
3.1
and
(
13
) does not hold, then
it follows from its proof that
(
11
) reduces to the upper bound.

Next we analyze the asymptotical behaviour of

\psi^{\inf}_{T,\delta}(u)
, as
u\to\infty
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X(t),t\geq 0
be a centered Gaussian process with continuous
trajectories and stationary increments satisfying

A-B
. Then for
\delta>0
as
u\to\infty
it holds that

\displaystyle\psi_{T,\delta}^{\inf}(u)\sim f(u)\Psi(m(u))\times\begin{cases}\mathcal{G}_{\eta}([0,T]_{\delta})\mathcal{H}_{\eta}^{\delta},&\varphi\in(0,\infty)\\
\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}},&\varphi=\infty.\end{cases}

If
\varphi=0
, then for the non-degenerated scenario (when set

[0,T]_{\delta}
consists of more than 1 element, i.e, for
T\geq\delta
)
it seems difficult to derive even
logarithmic asymptotics of

\psi_{T,\delta}^{\inf}(u)
.
One can argue that
\psi_{T,\delta}^{\inf}(u)

is exponentially smaller than

\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{\delta,X}(0)>u\right)
in this case, as
u\to\infty
.
We have the following proposition giving an
upper bound for
\psi_{T,\delta}^{\inf}(u)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
\varphi=0
and for some

\varepsilon>0

(14)

\displaystyle\sigma(u)\leq\frac{\sqrt{u}}{\ln^{1/4+\varepsilon}u},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ u\to\infty,

then for
T\geq\delta
with any

\widetilde{\mathbb{C}}<\frac{1+ct_{*}}{2t_{*}^{2\alpha}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]_{\delta}}\sigma(t)
it holds that

\displaystyle\psi_{T,\delta}^{\inf}(u)\leq\Psi(m(u))\Psi\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{C}}\frac{u}{\sigma^{2}(u)}\right),\ \ \ \ u\to\infty.

Remark 3.5
.

Comparing the asymptotics in Theorems
2.1
,
3.1
and
3.3

for case
\alpha>1/2
we observe that the process
Q_{\delta,X}
possesses the so-called strong Piterbarg property,
that is

\displaystyle\psi_{T,\delta}^{\sup}(u)\sim\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{\delta,X}(0)>u\right)\sim\psi_{T,\delta}^{\inf}(u),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ T\geq 0,\ u\to\infty.

The analogous property was observed for the continuous-times
analog of
Q_{\delta,X}
; see
[
4
]
.

4.
Examples

We illustrate the findings of this contribution by application of Theorems
3.1
and

3.3
to the family of fractional Brownian motions and Gaussian integrated processes.

4.1.
Fractional Brownian motion.

Let

C_{H}=\frac{c^{H}}{H^{H}(1-H)^{1-H}},\ \ D_{H}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}H^{H+1/2}}{c^{H+1}(1-H)^{H+1/2}},\ \ E_{H}=\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2H}}\sqrt{\pi}}{H^{1/2}(1-H)^{1/2}}.

Applying Theorems
2.1
,
3.1
and
3.3

for
X(t)=B_{H}(t)
being a standard fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
, we obtain the following results.

Corollary 4.1
.

As
u\to\infty
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{\delta,B_{H}}(0)>u\right)\sim\begin{cases}\frac{D_{H}u^{H}}{\delta}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}),&H<1/2\\
\mathcal{H}^{2c^{2}\delta}_{B_{1/2}}e^{-2cu},&H=1/2\\
\mathcal{H}_{B_{H}}E_{H}(C_{H}u^{1-H})^{1/H-1}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}),&H>1/2.\end{cases}

Corollary 4.2
.

For
T,\delta>0
as
u\to\infty
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,B_{H}}(t)>u\right)\sim\begin{cases}(1+[\frac{T}{\delta}])\frac{D_{H}u^{H}}{\delta}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}),&H<1/2\\
\mathcal{H}_{B_{1/2}}([0,2c^{2}T]_{2c^{2}\delta})\mathcal{H}^{2c^{2}\delta}_{B_{1/2}}e^{-2cu},&H=1/2\\
\mathcal{H}_{B_{H}}E_{H}(C_{H}u^{1-H})^{1/H-1}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}),&H>1/2.\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\inf\limits_{t\in[0,T]_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,B_{H}}(t)>u\right)\sim\begin{cases}\mathcal{G}_{B_{1/2}}([0,2c^{2}T]_{2c^{2}\delta})\mathcal{H}_{B_{1/2}}^{2c^{2}\delta}e^{-2cu},&H=1/2\\
\mathcal{H}_{B_{H}}E_{H}(C_{H}u^{1-H})^{1/H-1}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}),&H>1/2.\end{cases}

Note that Corollary
4.1
intersects with the results
in
[
10
,
9
,
11
]

while Corollary
4.2
provides a discrete counterpart
of Theorems 5-7 in
[
3
]
and Theorem 1 in

[
4
]
, respectively.

4.2.
Gaussian integrated processes.

For a stationary centered
Gaussian process with a.s. continuous sample paths
\zeta(s),\ s\geq 0

define the integrated process by

(15)

\displaystyle Z(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{t}\zeta(s)ds,\ \ \ \ \ t\geq 0.

This process is also Gaussian, has a.s. continuous
sample paths and stationary
increments. In what follows
we consider two classes
of processes
Z
,
which differ by property of
the correlation
function

R(t):=\mathbb{E}\left(\zeta(0)\zeta(t)\right)

of
\zeta
as
t\to\infty
.

SRD case.

Following, e.g.,
[
14
]
(see also

[
15
]
), we impose the
following conditions on the correlation of
\zeta
:

S1
:
R(t)\in C([0,\infty)),\ \lim\limits_{t\to\infty}tR(t)=0
;

S2
:
\int\limits_{0}^{t}R(s)ds>0
for all
t\in(0,\infty]
;

S3
:
\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{2}|R(t)|dt<\infty
.

The above assertions imply the existence of the
first and second derivatives of
\sigma^{2}_{Z}(t)=Var(Z(t))
and establish the asymptotic behavior
of
\sigma^{2}_{Z}(t)
at
\infty
(see e.g., Remark 6.1 in
[
14
]
):

\sigma^{2}_{Z}(t)=\frac{2}{G}t-2D+o(t^{-1}),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ t\to\infty,

where
G=1/\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}R(t)dt
and
D=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}tR(t)dt
.
Thus,
\sigma^{2}_{Z}
satisfies
A-B
with
\alpha=1/2
and

\varphi=\frac{2}{G}>0
. Hence
applying Theorems
2.1
,
3.1
and
3.3
, the following corollary holds.

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose that
\zeta
satisfies
S1-S3
. Then for
T\geq 0

and
\delta>0

as
u\to\infty

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{\delta,Z}(0)>u\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\mathcal{H}^{\delta}_{\xi}e^{-cGu},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,Z}(t)>u\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\mathcal{H}_{\xi}([0,T]_{\delta})\mathcal{H}^{\delta}_{\xi}e^{-cGu},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\inf\limits_{t\in[0,T]_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,Z}(t)>u\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\mathcal{G}_{\xi}([0,T]_{\delta})\mathcal{H}^{\delta}_{\xi}e^{-cGu},

where
\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{c^{2}Ge^{c^{2}G^{2}D}}
and

\xi(t)=cGZ(t)/\sqrt{2}
.

Note that the first asymptotics in Corollary
4.3

differs from its
continuous-time analog (Theorem 5.1 in
[
14
]
)
only by the corresponding Pickands constants.

LRD case.
Following, e.g.,
[
6
,
7
]
we characterize LRD case by
the following assumptions on the covariance function
R(t)
of the process
\zeta
:

L1
:
R(t)
is a continuous strictly positive function for
t\geq 0
;

L2
:
R(t)

is regularly varying at
\infty
with index
2\alpha-2
,

\alpha\in(1/2,1)
.

Under the above assumptions,
by Karamata’s theorem,
\sigma_{Z}^{2}
is regularly
varying at
\infty
with index
2\alpha
.
Since
2\alpha>1
we are in

\varphi=\infty
scenario.
Hence,
applying Theorems
2.1
,
3.1
and

3.3
we obtain the following result,
which shows that
Q_{\delta,Z}(t)
possesses the strong
Piterbarg property.

Corollary 4.4
.

Suppose that
\zeta
satisfies

L1-L2
. Then
as
u\to\infty
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\inf\limits_{t\in[0,T]_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,Z}(t)>u\right)\sim\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{\delta,Z}(0)>u\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]_{\delta}}Q_{\delta,Z}(t)>u\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}}f(u)\Psi(m(u)),

where
m(u)
and

5.1.
Proof of Theorem

Taking

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{\delta_{X}}(0)>u\right)\sim\mathbb{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}X_{u}(t)>m(u)\right),\ \ \ u\to\infty,

Case

.

We have by Bonferroni inequality

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{u}(t)>m(u)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}X_{u}(t)>m(u)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{u}(t)>m(u)\right)-\sum\limits_{t\neq s\in I(t_{u})}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{u}(t)>m(u),X_{u}(s)>m(u)\right).

There are less then

summands in the double-sum above, hence

(23)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t\neq s\in I(t_{u})}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{u}(t)>m(u),X_{u}(s)>m(u)\right)\leq\mathbb{C}\ln^{2}(u)u^{1/2-\varepsilon^{\prime}}\Psi(m(u)),\ \ \ \ u\to\infty.

Next we focus on calculation of the single sum in (
22
).
Since by Lemma
5.1

\sup\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}|\sigma_{X_{u}}(t)-1|\to 0
as

the following inequality (see, e.g., Lemma 2.1 in
[

)

(1-\frac{1}{x^{2}})\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}x}e^{-x^{2}/2}\leq\Psi(x)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}x}e^{-x^{2}/2},\ \ \ \ x>0,

implies as

(24)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{u}(t)>m(u)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}\Psi(\frac{m(u)}{\sigma_{X_{u}}(t)})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}\frac{\sigma_{X_{u}}(t)}{\sqrt{2\pi}m(u)}e^{-\frac{m^{2}(u)}{2\sigma^{2}_{X_{u}}(t)}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{m^{2}(u)}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}m(u)}\!\sum\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}e^{-\frac{m^{2}(u)}{2\sigma_{X_{u}}^{2}(t)}+\frac{m^{2}(u)}{2}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\Psi(m(u))\sum\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}e^{-\frac{m^{2}(u)}{2}\frac{(1-\sigma_{X_{u}}^{2}(t))}{\sigma^{2}_{X_{u}}(t)}}.

By Lemma
5.1
we have that as

the last sum above is
asymptotically equivalent to

(25)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}e^{-m^{2}(u)\frac{B}{2A}(t-t_{u})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t\in(-\frac{\ln u}{\sqrt{u}},\frac{\ln u}{\sqrt{u}})_{\delta/u}}e^{-m^{2}(u)\frac{B}{2A}t^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u}{\delta m(u)}\Big(\frac{\delta m(u)}{u}\sum\limits_{t\in(-\frac{m(u)\ln u}{\sqrt{u}},\frac{m(u)\ln u}{\sqrt{u}})_{\delta m(u)/u}}e^{-\frac{B}{2A}t^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{u}{\delta m(u)}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{B}{2A}t^{2}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u}{\delta m(u)}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi A}{B}}=\frac{u}{\delta m(u)}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\alpha}}{c(1-\alpha)^{3/2}},

where the asymptotic equivalence in (

)

\frac{\delta m(u)}{u}\to 0
and

Thus,

(26)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t\in I(t_{u})}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{u}(t)>m(u)\right)\sim\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\alpha}u\Psi(m(u))}{\delta c(1-\alpha)^{3/2}m(u)},\ \ \ u\to\infty

and hence by (

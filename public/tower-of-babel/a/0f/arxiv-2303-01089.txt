Abstract
For each integer n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , denote by T n T_{n} the map x ↦ n ​ x mod 1 x\mapsto nx\mod 1 from the circle group 𝕋 = ℝ / ℤ \mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z} into itself. Let p , q ≥ 2 p,q\geq 2 be two multiplicatively independent integers. Using Baire Category arguments, we show that generically a T p T_{p} -invariant probability measure μ \mu on 𝕋 \mathbb{T} with no atom has some large Fourier coefficients along the sequence ( q n ) n ≥ 0 (q^{n})_{n\geq 0} . In particular, ( T q n ​ μ ) n ≥ 0 (T_{q^{n}}\mu)_{n\geq 0} does not converges weak-star to the normalised Lebesgue measure on 𝕋 \mathbb{T} . This disproves a conjecture of Furstenberg and complements previous results of Johnson and Rudolph. In the spirit of previous work by Meiri and Lindenstrauss-Meiri-Peres, we study generalisations of our main result to certain classes of sequences ( c n ) n ≥ 0 (c_{n})_{n\geq 0} other than the sequences ( q n ) n ≥ 0 (q^{n})_{n\geq 0} , and also investigate the multidimensional setting.

Introduction and main results

1.a
Synopsis

In the late 1960s, Furstenberg proved significant results and proposed fascinating conjectures that aimed to express in various ways the heuristic principle that expansions in multiplicatively independent bases have no shared structure. For further details about this idea, readers can refer to the recent survey
[
42
]
which also outlines some progress in Furstenberg’s programme. Here, we shall list one result and three conjectures due to Furstenberg; some known partial results related to these conjectures will be mentioned in the following subsection.
In all these statements,

p,q\geq 2

are two fixed multiplicatively independent integers
. Recall that
p,q\geq 2
are called
multiplicatively independent
if
\log p/\log q\not\in\mathbb{Q}
. For each integer
n\geq 1
, denote by
T_{n}
the map
x\mapsto nx\mod 1
from the circle group
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, identified with
[0,1)
, into itself. A subset
F
of
\mathbb{T}
is said to be
T_{n}
-invariant if
T_{n}(F)\subset F
. Notice that
T_{n}
shifts the
n
-ary expansion of a real number and that each map
T_{n}
has many closed, infinite invariant subsets.

The following topological rigidity result has been proved in
[
18
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Furstenberg)
.

The only infinite closed subset
F
of
\mathbb{T}
which is simultaneously
T_{p}
- and
T_{q}
-invariant is
F=\mathbb{T}
.

Furstenberg formulated a conjecture, called Conjecture (C1) in
[
37
]
, which is stronger than Theorem
1.1
and deals with the asymptotic behaviour of a
T_{p}
–invariant subset under the action of
T_{q}
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
F
be an infinite closed
T_{p}
-invariant subset. Then the iterates
T_{q}^{n}(F)
converge in the Hausdorff distance to
\mathbb{T}
as
n
tends to infinity.

The measure-theoretical analogue statements of Theorem
1.1
and Conjecture
1.2
are both conjectural statements. Recall that a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}
is said to be
T_{n}
-invariant if
\mu=T_{n}\mu
, where
T_{n}\mu
is the measure defined by
T_{n}\mu(C)=\mu(T_{n}^{-1}C)
for every measurable set
C
. There are uncountably many
T_{n}
-invariant, or even ergodic, measures; see for instance
[
5
]
*p. 141. The next measure-theoretical rigidity conjecture, called Conjecture (C2) in
[
37
]
,
is the renowned
\times p
,
\times q
conjecture of Furstenberg, one of the most fundamental open questions in ergodic theory. We say that a probability measure is
continuous
if it has no atom.

Conjecture 1.3

(
\times p
,
\times q
conjecture)
.

The only continuous Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
which is simultaneously
T_{p}
- and
T_{q}
-invariant is the (normalised) Lebesgue measure
Leb
.

The natural analogue of Conjecture
1.2
for measures was also conjectured by Furstenberg: this is Conjecture (C3) in
[
37
]
and concerns now the convergence in the weak-star topology of a
T_{p}
-invariant measure under the action of
T_{q}
. We shall recall the definition of
w^{*}
convergence in Section
1.c
.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
\mu
be a continuous Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
which is
T_{p}
-invariant. Then
T_{q}^{n}\mu
converge
w^{*}
to
Leb
.

It is easy to see that Conjecture
1.4
implies the
\times p
,
\times q
conjecture (Conjecture
1.3
): suppose indeed that Conjecture
1.4
is true, and let
\mu
be a continuous and simultaneously
T_{p}
- and
T_{q}
-invariant measure as in Conjecture
1.3
. Since
T_{q}^{n}(\mu)={T_{q^{n}}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}
,
the Fourier coefficients of
\mu
verify
\widehat{\mu}(aq^{n})\rightarrow 0

for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Since
\mu
is
T_{q}
-invariant, this implies that
\widehat{\mu}(a)=0
for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, so that
\mu=\textrm{Leb}
.

The main aim of this manuscript is to show that generically (in the Baire Category sense), a continuous
T_{p}
-invariant probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}
has some large Fourier coefficients along the sequence
(q^{n})_{n\geq 0}
. This implies that the sequence
(T_{q}^{n}(\mu))_{n\geq 0}=(T_{q^{n}}\mu)_{n\geq 0}
does not converge
w^{*}
to
Leb
,
disproving
thus Conjecture
1.4
. The precise statement is given in Theorem
1.5
below.

It follows from our results and some results of Johnson and Rudolph in
[
26
]
that generically, in the Baire Category sense,
(T_{q^{n}}\mu)_{n\geq 0}
does not converge
w^{*}
to the Lebesgue measure, but the convergence of
T_{q^{n}}\mu
to
Leb
holds along a “large” sequence of integers (a sequence of upper density
1
); see Corollary
1.6
. This sheds some light on the complexity of the asymptotic behaviour of the action of
T_{q}
on a generic
T_{p}
-invariant measure. In the spirit of previous work by Meiri
[
39
]
and Lindenstrauss-Meiri-Peres
[
32
]
, we study generalisations of our main result
to certain classes of sequences
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
other than the sequences
(q^{n})_{n\geq 0}
, and also investigate the multidimensional setting.

Our methods are mainly functional-analytic, based for instance on Baire category methods and the Hahn-Banach theorem. We also use tools from classical harmonic analysis (Fourier coefficients of measures,
p
-Bernoulli measures), ergodic theory (the periodic specification property, the ergodic decomposition theorem) and elementary number theory. It is also interesting to note that, unlike most of the works on this topic, positive entropy does not play any role in the proofs.

1.b
Background

Without claiming completeness, we mention some previous contributions related to Theorem
1.1
and Conjectures
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.4
.

Many aspects of the dynamics of subsemigroups of
(T_{n})_{n\geq 1}
were discussed in the seminal paper
[
18
]
by Furstenberg. The proof of Theorem
1.1
in
[
18
]
used the disjointness of specific dynamical systems, a notion introduced in
[
18
]
. An elementary proof of Theorem
1.1
has been given by Boshernitzan
[
9
]
and an “effective” version has been proved in
[
10
]
by Bourgain, Lindenstrauss, Michel and Venkatesh. Starting with Berend
[
7
]
, several authors studied multidimensional generalisations of Theorem
1.1
.

Conjecture
1.2
is largely open. It is known that if
F
is a
T_{p}
-invariant subset of
\mathbb{T}
, then there exists a subsequence
(q^{n_{k}})
such that
T_{q}^{n_{k}}(F)
converges to
\mathbb{T}
in the Hausdorff metric; see for instance
[
30
]
*Lemma 2.1. Another result related to Conjecture
1.2
can be found in
[
38
]
*Th. 1.1. Starting with the papers
[
6
,
3
]
by Berend-Peres and Alon-Peres, several authors studied the so-called Glasner sets. A set
S
of integers is said to be a
Glasner set
if for every infinite closed subset
F
of
\mathbb{T}
, there exists a sequence
(c_{n})
of elements in
S
such that
T_{c_{n}}(F)
converges to
\mathbb{T}
in the Hausdorff metric. With this terminology, a result from
[
19
]
can be formulated as the fact that the set of integers is a Glasner set. Other quite small sets of integers are Glasner, like sets of positive (Banach) density or the sets of values assumed by any non-constant polynomial mapping the natural numbers to themselves. Note however that a finite union of lacunary sequences is not a Glasner set (
[
6
]
*Th. 1.4). Glasner sets have been also studied in the multidimensional setting.

The first result about the
\times p
,
\times q
conjecture has been proved by Lyons in
[
37
]
, the first place where Conjecture
1.3
appeared in print: if
p
and
q
are relatively prime, any probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
which is
T_{p}
- and
T_{q}
-invariant and
T_{p}
-exact (i.e. has completely positive entropy with respect to
T_{p}
), must be the Lebesgue measure.
Rudolph substantially strengthened this theorem in
[
41
]
, showing that the conclusion is true with only the weaker assumption that the measure is ergodic under the joint action of
T_{p}
and
T_{q}
, and of positive entropy under the action of
T_{p}
. Johnson
[
27
]
then generalised this to the case where
p
and
q
are multiplicatively independent. A different argument, along the lines of Lyons
[
37
]
, was given by Feldman
[
17
]
. Other different proofs were given by Host
[
24
]
and Parry
[
40
]
. In all these proofs the positive entropy remains a crucial assumption. The Rudolph-Johnson theorem has been used by Einsiedler and Fish
[
15
]
to prove that a continuous Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
invariant under the action of a multiplicative semigroup with positive lower logarithmic density is the normalised Lebesgue measure. An important advance was made by Katok and Spatzier
[
29
]
, who discovered that Rudolph’s proof can be extended to give partial information on invariant measures in much greater generality. We also mention the works
[
33
,
21
,
23
,
22
]
,
as well as the surveys
[
34
,
14
,
35
]
,
for an account of recent progress on measure rigidity for higher rank diagonal actions on homogeneous spaces.

Some partial results about Conjecture
1.4
(conjecture (C3) in
[
37
]
), which will be disproved in this manuscript, are also known. The study of convergence of the sequence
(T_{q^{n}}\mu)_{n\geq 0}
to the Lebesgue measure for certain classes of
T_{p}
-invariant measures
\mu
lies at the core of the works of Lyons
[
36
,
37
]
, Feldman and Smorodinsky
[
16
]
, Johnson and Rudolph
[
26
]
, and Host
[
24
]
. Given
p,q\geq 2
two multiplicatively independent integers, it is shown in
[
36
]
(see also
[
37
]
) that if
\mu
is a non-degenerate
p
-Bernoulli measure, then

T_{q^{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}
. This is the notation for the
w^{*}
-convergence towards the normalised Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
which is recalled in the next subsection.
The main result of
[
16
]
states that under the same assumption,
\mu
-almost every
x\in[0,1]
is normal to the base
q
. It is proved by Host in
[
24
]
that whenever
p
and
q
are relatively prime, any measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
which is ergodic and has positive entropy with respect to
T_{p}
is such that
\mu
-almost every
x\in[0,1]
is normal to the base
q
. The same statement has been proved by Lindenstrauss
[
33
]
under the
assumption that
p
does not divide any power of
q
(which is weaker than Host’s
assumption). The generalisation to the case where
p
and
q
are multiplicatively independent was obtained by Hochman and Shmerkin
[
21
]
. Several multidimensional generalisations are discussed in
[
1
,
2
]
. The Host-Lindenstrauss-Hochman-Shmerkin result implies easily a result by Johnson and Rudolph
[
26
]
that for every such ergodic and with positive entropy measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
,

\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}T_{q^{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}.

Johnson and Rudolph observe the following consequence: if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
is ergodic and of positive entropy with respect to
T_{p}
, then
T_{q^{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}
on a sequence of
Banach density one

(called a sequence of uniform full density in
[
26
]
). As a consequence, they obtain that the set

G^{\prime}_{p,q}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;{T_{q^{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}}\ \textrm{along a sequence of upper density 1}\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
. So, generically in the Baire Category sense, convergence of
T_{q^{n}}\mu
to the Lebesgue measure holds along a “large” sequence of integers. But the Baire Category arguments leave room for possible “bad” sequences where convergence to the Lebesgue measure, as predicted by Conjecture
1.4
, cannot be guaranteed. Quoting from
[
26
]
: “
As we have no examples showing such bad sequences can actually exist, perhaps it is possible by some more explicit investigation to eliminate these bad sequences along which convergence to the Lebesgue measure fails
”. Our first main result, which is Theorem
1.5
below, shows that generically such bad sequences do exist, and cannot be eliminated.

1.c
Notation

Denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
the space of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{T}
, and, for any
p\geq 2
, by
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
the space of
T_{p}
-invariant measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
. We endow
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
with the topology of
w^{*}
-convergence of measures, which turns it into a compact metrizable space.
Recall that given measures
\mu_{k}
,
k\geq 1
, and
\mu
belonging to
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
, we say that
\mu_{k}\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\mu
if

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\mu_{k}\longrightarrow\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\mu

as
k\longrightarrow+\infty
for every
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
, where
C(\mathbb{T})
is the space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{T}
, endowed with the sup norm
||\,.\,||_{\infty,\mathbb{T}}
on
\mathbb{T}
. This is equivalent to requiring that

\hat{\mu}_{k}(a)\longrightarrow\hat{\mu}(a)
for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, where the
a
-th Fourier coefficient of a measure
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
is defined in this manuscript as

\hat{\nu}(a)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}z^{a}d\nu(z).

We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathbb{T})
the set of continuous (i.e. non-atomic) measures on
\mathbb{T}
, and by
\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
the set of continuous
T_{p}
-invariant measures on
\mathbb{T}
.
Since
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
is
w^{*}
-closed in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
,

(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
is also a compact metrizable space. In particular,
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
is a Polish space, in which the Baire Category Theorem applies. Recall that a subset of a Polish space is called
residual
if it contains a dense
G_{\delta}
set (i.e. a countable intersection of dense open sets).

For our study of the multidimensional setting the following notation is required. For each
d\geq 2
, we denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
the set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, and by
\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
the set of continuous measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Given a matrix
A\in M_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\det(A)\neq 0
, we denote by
T_{A}
the associated transformation
\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto A\boldsymbol{x}\mod 1
of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
into itself. This transformation preserves the normalised Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, which we write as
\textrm{Leb}_{d}
. Notice that
T_{A}
is an ergodic transformation of
(\mathbb{T}^{d},\textrm{Leb}_{d})
if and only if no eigenvalue of
A
is a root of unity. The set of
T_{A}
-invariant measures on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, and
\mathcal{P}_{A,c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is the set of continuous
T_{A}
-invariant measures on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

1.d
Main results

Here is our first main result, showing that generically a continuous
T_{p}
-invariant probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}
has some large Fourier coefficients along the sequence
(q^{n})_{n\geq 0}
.

Theorem 1.5

(large Fourier coefficients)
.

Let
p,q\geq 2
be two
distinct integers. Then the set

S_{p,q}:=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}|\widehat{\mu}(q^{n})|>0\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
. In particular, the set

G_{p,q}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{q^{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\emph{Leb}\ \textrm{as}\ n\to+\infty\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
, thus disproving Conjecture
1.4
.

We should note that the proof of Theorem
1.5
does not require that
p
and
q
be multiplicatively independent; if
p
and
q
are powers of the same integer, then a simple and direct proof of Theorem
1.5
can be given. We stress that positive entropy does not play any role in the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By combining Theorem
1.5
with
[
26
]
*Theorem 8.2, we can derive the following corollary.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
p,q\geq 2
be two multiplicatively independent integers. Then the set of all measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
such that

T_{q^{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\emph{Leb}\ \textrm{as}\ n\to+\infty

and

{T_{q^{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{w^{*}}\emph{Leb}}\ \textrm{along a sequence of upper density 1}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.

Contrary to the proof of Theorem
1.5
, the proof of Theorem 8.2. from
[
26
]
uses arguments depending on positive entropy. We shall present an alternative harmonic analysis approach to
[
26
]
*Theorem 8.2 in Section
5
.

Meiri
[
39
]
and Lindenstrauss, Meiri and Peres
[
32
]
generalised the results from
[
24
]
and
[
26
]
to certain classes of sequences
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
other than the sequences
(q^{n})_{n\geq 0}
. More precisely (
[
39
]
), if the sequence of remainders
(c_{n}\mod p^{N})_{0\leq n<p^{N}}
,
N\geq 1
, satisfies certain combinatorial properties, then every
T_{p}
-invariant ergodic measure
\mu
of positive entropy is such that
(c_{n}x)_{n\geq 0}
is uniformly distributed mod
1
for
\mu
-almost every
x\in[0,1]
.
A weaker combinatorial condition on the sequence
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is introduced in
[
32
]
: if the so-called
p
-adic collision exponent

\Gamma_{p}((c_{n}))
is less that
2
, then every measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
which is ergodic and of positive entropy is
(c_{n})
-generic
in the sense that

\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}T_{c_{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}.

It follows that the set of measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
such that
T_{c_{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}
along a sequence of upper density
1
is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.

Conjecture
1.4
thus fits in a much broader framework: given a strictly increasing sequence of integers
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of integers, is it true that the set

G_{p,(c_{n})}:=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{c_{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\textrm{Leb}\quad\textrm{as}\ {n}\longrightarrow{+\infty}\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
? We prove in Theorem
2.1
below a very general criterion on the sequence
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
implying an affirmative answer to this question. It allows to deal with most of the classes of sequences considered in
[
39
]
and
[
32
]
, and we obtain for instance the following theorem, which complements
[
32
]
*Th. 1.4 and
[
39
]
*Th. B:

Theorem 1.7

(linear recurrent sequences as
(c_{n})
)
.

Let
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of integers satisfying a linear recursion of the form

c_{n}=a_{1}c_{n-1}+a_{2}c_{n-2}+\cdots+a_{L}c_{n-L},\quad n>L

for some
L\geq 1
and integer coefficients
a_{1},\dots,a_{L}
with
a_{L}\neq 0
. If the integers
a_{L}
and
p
are relatively prime, then the set

G_{p,(c_{n})}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;:\;T_{c_{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\emph{Leb}\quad\emph{as}\quad n\to+\infty\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.

Notice that when
L=1
and
a_{1}=q
, Theorem
1.7
reduces to the case studied in Theorem
1.5
, but with the additional requirement that
p
and
q
are relatively prime.

We also mention the following related result. In
[
4
]
, a continuous probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}
was constructed with the property that for any increasing sequence
(c_{n})
in the multiplicative semigroup
\{p^{m}q^{n}:m,n\geq 0\}
, one has
T_{c_{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\textrm{Leb}
. This disproved Conjectures (C4) and (C5) from
[
37
]
. However, it appears that the construction from
[
4
]
cannot be modified to produce a measure that is
T_{p}
-invariant.

We now move over to the multidimensional setting.
The equidistribution result
[
24
]
of Host was generalised to the multidimensional setting by Meiri-Peres
[
38
]
, Host
[
25
]
himself and Algom
[
1
]
.
The general framework of these works is the following:
given two endomorphisms
A
and
B
of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, study the equidistribution properties of the sequence
(B^{n}\boldsymbol{x})_{n\geq 0}
for
\mu
-almost every
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. This problem is studied in
[
25
]
when
\mu
is
A
-ergodic and has positive entropy, under the condition that
\det(A)
and

.

Let

1.e
Overview

The paper is organised as follows. We present in Section

2
Classes of times p-invariant measures with some large Fourier coefficients

In the whole section,
p\geq 2
will be a fixed integer. Let
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a strictly increasing sequence of integers. We say that
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies
assumption
(H) if the following is true:

(H)

There exist finitely many nonnegative integers
t_{1},\dots,t_{r},h_{1},\dots,h_{d}
with
h_{l}\neq 0
for every
l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, and an infinite subset
I
of
\mathbb{N}
such that for every
N\in I
, there exist
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
and
l\in\{1,\dots,d\}
with the property that
h_{l}c_{n}\equiv t_{i}\mod(p^{N}-1)
for infinitely many integers

.

Our aim in this section is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a strictly increasing sequence of integers satisfying assumption
(H)
. Then the set

G_{p,(c_{n})}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{c_{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\emph{\textrm{Leb}}\quad\textrm{as }n\longrightarrow+\infty\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.

Condition (H) may look somewhat technical, but it is actually a rather weak one. We shall exhibit in Section
3
many examples of sequences
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfying (H). In particular, the sequence
(c_{n})=(q^{n})
satisfies it for any
q\geq 2
. This disproves Conjecture
1.4
.

Assumption (H) is of the same nature as the congruence assumptions mod
p^{N}
which appear in the works of Host
[
24
]
and Meiri
[
39
]
, and which are formalised in terms of
p
-adic collision exponent in
[
32
]
. These two assumptions are nonetheless different, be it only because (H) involves congruences mod
(p^{N}-1)
, while the
p
-adic collision exponent is defined in terms of congruence mod
p^{N}
.

Our main tool for the proof of Theorem
2.1
is a density result for certain families of discrete
T_{p}
-invariant measures on
\mathbb{T}
.

2.a
Density of discrete times p-invariant measures

The periodic points of the transformation
T_{p}
are exactly the points
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
such that
\lambda^{p^{N}}=\lambda
for some
N\geq 1
. In this case, the probability measure
\mu_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{T}
, defined as

\mu_{\lambda}=\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\delta_{\{\lambda^{p^{j}}\}},

is a discrete
T_{p}
-invariant measure on
\mathbb{T}
whose support is the orbit of the point
\lambda
under the action of
T_{p}
.
It is ergodic for
T_{p}
, and the set of all such measures (where
\lambda
varies over the set of all
(p^{N}-1)
-th roots of 1,
N\geq 1
) is dense in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})

[
43
,
44
]
.

This density property is deeply linked to the fact that the dynamical system
(\mathbb{T},T_{p})
has the so-called
specification property
introduced by Bowen in
[
11
]
(see also
[
43
,
44
]
). Since it will be needed in the sequel, we recall here the definition from
[
43
]
. The setting is that of compact dynamical systems
(X,T)
, where
(X,d)
is a compact metric space and
T
is a continuous self-map of
X
. This property is often referred to as the
periodic specification property
, and it is the terminology we shall use here. The article
[
31
]
contains an overview of the specification property and its many variants.

Definition 2.2
.

The system
(X,T)
is said to have the
periodic specification property
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every integers

0\leq a_{1}\leq b_{1}
and
0\leq a_{2}\leq b_{2}
with
a_{2}-b_{1}>N_{\varepsilon}
, for every vectors
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
, and for every integer
d>b_{2}-a_{1}+N_{\varepsilon}
, there exists a periodic point
x
for
T
with period
d
such that

(i)

d(T^{j}x,T^{j}x_{1})<\varepsilon
for every
j=a_{1},\ldots,b_{1}
;

(ii)

d(T^{j}x,T^{j}x_{2})<\varepsilon
for every
j=a_{2},\ldots,b_{2}
.

If
x
is periodic for
T
with period
d
, the measure

\mu_{x}=\dfrac{1}{d}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\delta_{\{T^{j}x\}}

is called a
CO-measure
. Here
CO
stands for
Closed-Orbit
; see for instance Sigmund
[
44
]
. If
(X,T)
has the specification property, the set of CO-measures is dense in the set of
T
-invariant Borel probability measures on
X
(see
[
44
]
*Th. 1).

Let
(N_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a strictly increasing sequence of integers. We denote by
C_{p,(N_{k})}
the set of all
(p^{N_{k}}-1)
-th roots of
1
:

C_{p,(N_{k})}=\bigl\{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}\;;\;\lambda^{p^{N_{k}}-1}=1\quad\textrm{for some}\ k\geq 1\bigr\}.

Let
D_{p,(N_{k})}
be the family of CO-measures associated to elements
\lambda
of
C_{p,(N_{k})}
:

D_{p,(N_{k})}=\bigl\{\mu_{\lambda}\;;\;\lambda\in C_{p,(N_{k})}\bigr\}.

We are now going to prove the following density result, which will be crucial for the proof of Theorem
1.5
:

Theorem 2.3
.

The set
D_{p,(N_{k})}
is dense in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.

Proof.

Our aim is to show that given
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
,
f_{1},\dots,f_{l}
belonging to
C(\mathbb{T})
, and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\lambda\in C_{p,(N_{k})}
such that

\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{i}\,d\mu_{\lambda}-\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{i}\,d\mu\Bigr|<\varepsilon\quad\textrm{for every}\ i\in\{1,\ldots,l\}.

Since CO-measures are
w^{*}
-dense in
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
, we can suppose without loss of generality that
\mu
is a CO-measure, which we write as

\mu_{z}=\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\delta_{\{z^{p^{j}}\}}\quad\textrm{for some}\ z\in\mathbb{T}\ \textrm{and}\ N\geq 1\ \textrm{such that}\ z^{p^{N}-1}=1.

Because Lipschitz functions, with respect to the distance induced by
\mathbb{C}
on
\mathbb{T}
, are dense in
C(\mathbb{T})
by the Stone-Weierstrass theorem, we can also suppose without loss of generality that the functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{l}
are Lipschitz.
Let
C>0
be such that for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
and every
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{T}
,
|f_{i}(z_{1})-f_{i}(z_{2})|\leq C\,|z_{1}-z_{2}|
. We are looking for
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
and
k\geq 1
with
\lambda^{p^{N_{k}}-1}=1
such that

\Bigl|\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})-\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f_{i}(\lambda^{p^{j}})\bigr|<\varepsilon\quad\textrm{for every}\ i\in\{1,\ldots,l\}.

Fix
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. Let
N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
be given by the specification property. Let
k\geq 1
be such that
N_{k}>2N_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}+2
. Applying Definition
2.2
to
x_{1}=x_{2}=z
,
a_{1}=0
,
b_{1}=N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-2
,
a_{2}=b_{2}=b_{1}+N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}+1
, and
d=N_{k}
, we obtain the existence of
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
with
\lambda^{p^{N_{k}}}=\lambda
such that, for every
j=0,\ldots,N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-2
,

\bigl|z^{p^{j}}-\lambda^{p^{j}}\bigr|<\varepsilon^{\prime},\quad\textrm{ and hence }\quad\bigl|f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})-f_{i}(\lambda^{p^{j}})\bigr|\leq C\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Then, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
,

\Bigl|\dfrac{1}{N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-2}f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})-\dfrac{1}{N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-2}f_{i}(\lambda^{p^{j}})\Bigr|\leq C\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Now

\displaystyle\Bigl|\dfrac{1}{N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-2}f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})\Bigr|\leq\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-1}^{N_{k}-1}|f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})|

\displaystyle+\Bigl|\dfrac{1}{N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-1}-\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\Bigr|\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-2}|f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}+1}{N_{k}}\,\bigl|\bigl|f_{i}\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{T}}+\Bigl(1-\dfrac{N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-1}{N_{k}}\Bigr)\,\bigl|\bigl|f_{i}\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{4N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}+2}{N_{k}}\,\bigl|\bigl|f_{i}\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{T}}\leq 6\,\dfrac{N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}{N_{k}}\,\bigl|\bigl|f_{i}\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{T}}

and

\displaystyle\Bigl|\dfrac{1}{N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-2}f_{i}(\lambda^{p^{j}})

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f_{i}(\lambda^{p^{j}})\Bigr|\leq 6\,\dfrac{N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}{N_{k}}\,\bigl|\bigl|f_{i}\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{T}}.

Therefore

\Bigl|\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})-\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f_{i}(\lambda^{p^{j}})\Bigr|\leq C\varepsilon^{\prime}+12\,\dfrac{N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}{N_{k}}\,\bigl|\bigl|f_{i}\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{T}}.

Let now
r_{k}
be the unique integer with
r_{k}N\leq N_{k}<(r_{k}+1)N
. Then, proceeding in the same way as above, we obtain that

\displaystyle\Bigl|\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})-\dfrac{1}{r_{k}.N}\sum_{j=0}^{r_{k}.N-1}f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=r_{k}N}^{N_{k}-1}|f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})|+\Bigl|\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}-\dfrac{1}{r_{k}.N}\Bigr|\sum_{j=0}^{r_{k}N-1}|f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})|

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\dfrac{N}{N_{k}}\,\bigl|\bigl|f_{i}\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{T}}.

Since
N
is fixed and
{N_{k}\to+\infty}
, we can choose
N_{k}>2N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}+2
sufficiently large so that

\max(12\,\frac{N_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}{N_{k}},2\,\frac{N}{N_{k}})\,\bigl|\bigl|f_{i}\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{T}}<\varepsilon^{\prime}

for every
i\in\{1\ldots l\}
. As

\dfrac{1}{r_{k}.N}\sum_{j=0}^{r_{k}N-1}f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})=\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f_{i}(z^{p^{j}}),

we get that

\Bigl|\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f_{i}(z^{p^{j}})-\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f_{i}(\lambda^{p^{j}})\Bigr|\leq(C+2)\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Taking
\varepsilon^{\prime}
so small that
(C+2)\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon
yields the result we are looking for.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The argument presented above actually holds in a much more general setting, and shows the following result. Let
(X,T)
be a dynamical system with the periodic specification property. Given a strictly increasing sequence
(N_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of integers, let

C_{T,(N_{k})}=\bigl\{x\in X\;;\;T^{N_{k}}x=x\quad\textrm{for some}\ k\geq 1\bigr\}

denote the set of periodic points for
T
having a period within the set
\{N_{k}\;;\;k\geq 1\}
. Let

D_{T,(N_{k})}=\bigl\{\mu_{x}\;;\;x\in C_{T,(N_{k})}\bigr\}
. Then
D_{T,(N_{k})}
is dense in the set
\mathcal{P}_{T}(X)
of
T
-invariant Borel probability measures on
X
, endowed with the
w^{*}
-topology.

Other variations of the argument are possible, involving or not the periodic specification property.

.

, the set

.

The set

is clearly

Order the infinite set

as a strictly increasing sequence
(N_{k})_{k\geq 1}
.

We have the following:

Fact 2.7
.

Let
\mu

. There exist

and

such that

\widehat{\mu}{(h_{l}c_{n})}=\widehat{\mu}(t_{i})

.

There exists
\lambda\in C_{p,(N_{k})}
, with
\lambda^{p^{N_{k}}-1}=1

\mu=\mu_{\lambda}=\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}\delta_{\{\lambda^{p^{j}}\}}.

Since

43
]
, using the density of CO-measures in
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
. It can also be retrieved by using the following elementary observation: there exists a sequence
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of elements of
\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
such that
\mu_{k}\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\delta_{1}
.

The measures
\mu_{k}
can be constructed as Cantor-type measures, also called
p
-Bernoulli in
[
36
]
,
[
37
]
, or
[
16
]
: given a
p
-tuple

\Theta=(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{p-1})
of elements of
(0,1)
with
\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\theta_{i}=1
, let
m_{\Theta}
be the product measure

m_{\Theta}=\bigotimes_{n\geq 1}\,\,\Bigl(\,\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\theta_{j}\delta_{\{j\}}\,\Bigr)\quad\textrm{on}\quad\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}^{\mathbb{N}},

and let
\mu_{\Theta}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
be the image measure of
m_{\Theta}
by the map
{\Phi:\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{T}}
defined by

\Phi\bigl((\omega_{n})_{n\geq 1}\bigr)=\exp\Bigl(2i\pi\sum_{n\geq 1}\omega_{n}p^{-n}\Bigr).

Each measure
\mu_{\Theta}
is easily seen to belong to
\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
, and

{\mu_{\Theta}}\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}{\delta_{1}}
as
{\Theta\to(1,0,\dots,0).}

Once we have obtained a sequence
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of elements of
\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
such that
\mu_{k}\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\delta_{1}
, the density of
\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
in
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
immediately follows, since for each measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
,
(\mu_{k}*\mu)_{k\geq 1}
is a sequence of
T_{p}
-invariant continuous measures which converges
w^{*}
to
\mu
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

It follows from Lemma
2.6
and Fact
2.8
and from the Baire Category Theorem that for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
, the set
G_{p,(c_{n})}^{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
. By Fact
2.5
, the set

\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;\exists l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\;\;\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}|\widehat{\mu}(h_{l}c_{n})|>0\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
, and hence

G_{p,(c_{n})}{}
is residual as well. Theorem
2.1
is proved.
∎

3
Proofs of Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
, and further examples

In this section, we apply Theorem
2.1
to various classes of sequences
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, and show that generically in the Baire Category sense, a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
has infinitely many “large” Fourier coefficients along the sequence
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, or along some dilated sequence
(a.c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. We begin by proving Theorem
1.5
.

3.a
Disproving Conjecture (C3): proof of Theorem
1.5

Let
p\geq 2
. Given another integer
q\geq 2
(not necessarily multiplicatively independent from
p
), we consider the sequence
c_{n}=q^{n}
,
n\geq 0
. In order to show that the sets

S_{p,q}:=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}|\widehat{\mu}(q^{n})|>0\bigr\}

and

G_{p,q}{}=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{q^{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\textrm{Leb}\}

are dense in
(\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
it suffices to show that this sequence
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies assumption (H), and then to apply Theorem
2.1
. That assumption (H) is satisfied is a consequence of the following lemma, which relies on considerations from elementary number theory:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p,q\geq 2
. There exists an integer
N_{0}\geq 1
such that for every
a\geq 1
sufficiently large, the following assertion holds:

for every
N\in I:=N_{0}.\mathbb{N}+1
, there exists an integer
r_{N}\geq 1
such that

q^{a+k.r_{N}}\equiv q^{a}\mod(p^{N}-1)\quad\textrm{for every}\ k\geq 0.

Proof.

Write
q
as
q=q_{1}^{b_{1}}\!\!\dots q_{s}^{b_{s}}
, where
q_{1},\dots,q_{s}
are primes and
b_{1},\dots,b_{s}
are positive integers. Let
a_{0}\geq 1
be such that
1<p<q_{i}^{a_{0}b_{i}}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. A first step in the proof of Lemma
3.1
is to show the following

Fact 3.2
.

Let
u\geq 1
be a positive integer. Let
\gamma\geq 1
be such that for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
and every
v\in\{1,\ldots,u\}
,
q_{i}^{\gamma}
does not divide
p^{v}-1
.
There exist integers
N_{1}>u,\dots,N_{s}>u
such that for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
and every
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}N_{j}.\mathbb{N}
,

p^{N}\not\equiv 1\mod q_{i}^{\gamma}.

Proof.

Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. If
p^{N}\not\equiv 1\mod q_{i}^{\gamma}
for every
N\geq 1
, then clearly
p^{N}\not\equiv 1\mod q_{i}^{\gamma}
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}N_{j}.\mathbb{N}
, whatever the choice of the integers
N_{1},\dots,N_{s}
. So we can suppose without loss of generality that there exists an integer
N\geq 1
such that
p^{N}\equiv 1\mod q_{i}^{\gamma}
. Let
N_{i}
be the smallest such integer. Necessarily,
N_{i}>u
, since else
q_{i}^{\gamma}
would divide
p^{v}-1
for some
v\in\{1,\ldots,u\}
. Moreover any integer
N
such that
p^{N}\equiv 1\mod q_{i}^{\gamma}
is a multiple of
N_{i}
. It follows that
p^{N}\not\equiv 1\mod q_{i}^{\gamma}
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus N_{i}.\mathbb{N}
.
∎

We apply Fact
3.2
to
u=1
and
\gamma=a_{0}.\max_{1\leq i\leq s}b_{i}
. Let
N_{1},\ldots,N_{s}
be given by Fact
3.2
.
Since
N_{j}\geq 2
for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, the set
J:=\mathbb{N}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}N_{j}.\mathbb{N}
is infinite. Set
N_{0}=N_{1}\ldots N_{l}
. Then
I:=N_{0}.\mathbb{N}+1
is contained in
J
.

Fix
N\in I
. For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, let
0\leq a_{i,N}<\gamma
be the largest integer such that
q_{i}^{a_{i,N}}
divides
p^{N}-1
, and write
p^{N}-1=q_{i}^{a_{i,N}}s_{i,N}
for some integer
s_{i,N}\geq 1
with
\textrm{gcd}(s_{i,N},q_{i})=1
. By the Fermat-Euler Theorem, there exists
r_{i,N}\geq 1
such that
q_{i}^{r_{i,N}}\equiv 1\mod s_{i,N}
.
Set
r_{N}=r_{1,N}.r_{2,N}\dots r_{s,N}
. Then for every
l\geq 1
and every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
,
q_{i}^{l.r_{N}}\equiv 1\mod s_{i,N}
, so that

q_{i}^{a_{i,N}+l.r_{N}}\equiv q_{i}^{a_{i,N}}\mod(p^{N}-1)
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. If
a
is sufficiently large, we have
a_{i,N}<\gamma<a.b_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, so that
q_{i}^{a.b_{i}+l.r_{N}}\equiv q_{i}^{a.b_{i}}\mod(p^{N}-1)
. Applying this to
l=k.b_{i}
,
k\geq 1
, yields that

(q_{i}^{b_{i}})^{a+k.r_{N}}\equiv(q_{i}^{b_{i}})^{a}\mod(p^{N}-1)
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, i.e. that
q^{a+k.r_{N}}\equiv q^{a}\mod(p^{N}-1)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By Lemma
3.1
above, the sequence
(q^{n})_{n\geq 1}
satisfies assumption (H). The proof of Theorem
2.1
combined with Lemma
3.1
shows the density of

S_{p,q}:=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}|\widehat{\mu}(q^{n})|>0\bigr\}

in
(\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
does not make use of all the information provided by Lemma
3.1
: we apply it with
u=1
, the particular form of the set
I
is not used, and we only need the fact that for every
N\in I
, there exist infinitely many
n
’s such that
q^{n}\equiv q^{a}\mod(p^{N}-1)
. This additional information will be important, however, in the forthcoming proofs of Theorems
3.4
and
1.7
.

3.b
A generalisation of Theorem
1.5

In this section, we consider sequences
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of the following form:

c_{n}=f_{1}(n)q_{1}^{n}+f_{2}(n)q_{2}^{n}+\cdots+f_{d}(n)q_{d}^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, where for each
l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,
q_{l}\geq 2
is an integer and
f_{l}
is a polynomial with
coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
. This class of sequences is considered in
[
39
]
and
[
32
]
, where the following result is proved: if
p\geq 2
admits a prime factor
p^{*}
which does not divide one of the integers
q_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq d
, then any measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
which is ergodic and of positive entropy is
(c_{n})
-generic. It follows that the set

G^{\prime}_{p,(c_{n})}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{c_{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}\ \textrm{on a set of upper density}\ 1\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
– see Section
5.a
for details on this argument.

We complement this result by showing the following

Theorem 3.4
.

If
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a sequence of the form
c_{n}=f_{1}(n)q_{1}^{n}+f_{2}(n)q_{2}^{n}+\cdots+f_{d}(n)q_{d}^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, where for each
l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,
q_{l}\geq 2
is an integer and
f_{l}
is a polynomial with
coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
, then the set

G_{p,(c_{n})}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{c_{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\emph{\textrm{Leb}}\ \textrm{as}\ {n\to+\infty\bigr\}}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.

Proof.

Let us show that
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies assumption (H). By Lemma
3.1
, there exist integers
a\geq 1
and
N_{l}
,
1\leq l\leq d
, such that for every

l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
and every
N\in I_{l}:=N_{l}.\mathbb{N}+1
, there exists an integer
r_{l,N}\geq 1
such that for every
k\geq 0
,

q_{l}^{a+k.r_{l,N}}\equiv q_{l}^{a}\mod(p^{N}-1).

The set
I=\bigcap_{l=1}^{d}I_{l}
is infinite. If we set, for each
N\in I
,
r_{N}=r_{1,N}\dots r_{d,N}
, we get that for every
N\in I
and every
k\geq 0
,

q_{l}^{a+k.r_{N}}\equiv q_{l}^{a}\mod(p^{N}-1)\quad\textrm{for every}\ l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}.

(1)

For each
l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, write the polynomial
f_{l}
as

f_{l}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\Delta_{l}}b_{j}^{(l)}x^{j},\quad\textrm{where}\ b_{j}^{(l)}\in\mathbb{Z}\ \textrm{for every}\ j\in\{0,\ldots,\Delta_{l}\}.

For every
N\in I
and every integer

k^{\prime}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle f_{l}\bigl(a+k^{\prime}(p^{N}-1)r_{N}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\Delta_{l}}b_{j}^{(l)}\bigl(a+k^{\prime}(p^{N}-1)r_{N}\bigr)^{j}

and

\displaystyle\bigl(a+k^{\prime}(p^{N}-1)r_{N}\bigr)^{j}

\displaystyle\equiv a^{j}\mod(p^{N}-1)\quad\textrm{for every}\ j\geq 0.

Hence

\displaystyle f_{l}\bigl(a+k^{\prime}(p^{N}-1)r_{N}\bigr)

\displaystyle\equiv f_{l}(a)\mod(p^{N}-1)\quad\textrm{for every}\ l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}.

(2)

Putting together (
1
) and (
2
) yields that for every
N\in I
,

\displaystyle c_{a+k^{\prime}(p^{N}-1)r_{,N}}

\displaystyle\equiv c_{a}\mod(p^{N}-1)\quad\textrm{for every}\ k^{\prime}\geq 0,

which implies that assumption (H) is true. Theorem
3.4
thus follows from Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We notice that if
c_{n}=f(n)
for some polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
, then the set

\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{c_{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\textrm{Leb}\bigr\}

is also residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
. Remark that in this case, the sequence
(c_{n}x)_{n\geq 0}
is uniformly distributed mod 1 for every
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, and hence

\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\exp(2i\pi hc_{n}x)\to 0\quad\textrm{as }\quad N\to\infty\ \quad\textrm{for every}\ h\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.

(3)

If
\mu
belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
, integrating (
3
) with respect to the measure
\mu
yields that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\widehat{\mu}(hc_{n})

\displaystyle\to 0\quad\textrm{for every}\ h\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\quad\textrm{i.e.}\quad\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}T_{c_{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}.

3.c
Further examples: proof of Theorem
1.7

Let
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be defined by a linear recursion: there exist
L\geq 1
and coefficients

a_{1},\dots,a_{L}
in
\mathbb{Z}
with
a_{L}\neq 0
such that

c_{n}=a_{1}c_{n-1}+a_{2}c_{n-2}+\cdots+a_{L}c_{n-L}\quad\textrm{for every}\ n\geq L.

Let
p\geq 2
. Meiri introduces in
[
39
]
the following two assumptions on the sequence
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
:

(a)

(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
has no non-constant arithmetic subsequence;

(b)

a_{L}
and
p
are relatively prime.

It is observed in
[
39
]
*Prop. 5.1 that assumption (a) is satisfied as soon as the following property holds:

(a’)

if
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{L^{\prime}}
, with
1\leq L^{\prime}\leq L
, are the distinct roots of the recursion polynomial
p(x)=x^{L}-\sum_{l=1}^{L}a_{l}x^{L-l}
, then none of the quantities
\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}
,
1\leq i<j\leq L^{\prime}
is a root of unity.

If assumptions (a) and (b) are satisfied, any measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
which is ergodic and of positive entropy is such that

T_{c_{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}{}\textrm{Leb}
as
{n\to+\infty}
(this is a consequence of
[
39
]
Th. 5.2). In particular, the set

G^{\prime}_{p,(c_{n})}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{c_{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}\quad\textrm{along a set of upper density}\ 1\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
. See also
[
32
]
*Thm. 4.3.

Theorem
1.7
complements these results by showing that under the sole assumption (b), the set

G_{p,(c_{n})}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{c_{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\textrm{Leb}\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Consider the matrix
A\in M_{L}(\mathbb{Z})
given by

A=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\dots&\dots&a_{L}\\
1&0&\dots&\dots&0\\
0&1&\ddots&&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\dots&0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Setting
C_{n}={}^{\textrm{T}}\!\begin{pmatrix}c_{n}&c_{n-1}&\dots&c_{n-L+1}\end{pmatrix}
for every
n\geq L-1
, we have
C_{n+1}=AC_{n}
for every
n\geq L-1
. Since
\det A=(-1)^{L+1}a_{L}
and
a_{L}\neq 0
,
A
is invertible as a matrix of
M_{L}(\mathbb{Q})
, and

A^{-1}=\dfrac{(-1)^{L+1}}{a_{L}}\,\textrm{adj}\,(A)

where
\textrm{adj}\,(A)
, the adjoint (or adjugate) of
A
, is the transpose of the matrix of the cofactors of
A
. Observe that
\textrm{adj}\,(A)\in M_{L}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Let us decompose the integer
a_{L}
as
a_{L}=q_{1}^{b_{1}}\dots q_{s}^{b_{s}}
, where
q_{i}
is prime and
b_{i}\geq 1
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. By Fact
3.2
, there exist
\gamma\geq 1
and an infinite subset
I
of
\mathbb{N}
such that for every
N\in I
,
p^{N}\not\equiv 1\mod q_{i}^{\gamma}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. Hence, for every
N\in I
,
p^{N}-1
can be written as
p^{N}-1=q_{1}^{\beta_{1,N}}\!\!\!\dots\,q_{s}^{\beta_{s,N}}r_{N}
, where
0\leq\beta_{i,N}<\gamma
and
\textrm{gcd}(q_{i},r_{N})=1
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. Since the prime factors of
a_{L}
are exactly the
q_{i}
’s, it follows that
a_{L}
and
r_{N}
are relatively prime, and hence that
a_{L}
is invertible modulo
r_{N}
: there exists an integer
d_{N}
with
0\leq d_{N}<r_{N}
such that
a_{L}.d_{N}\equiv 1\mod r_{N}
.
Setting
B_{N}=(-1)^{L+1}d_{N}\,\textrm{adj}(A)
, we observe that
B_{N}\in M_{L}(\mathbb{Z})
and that
AB_{N}\equiv B_{N}A\equiv I\mod r_{N}
. So
A
is invertible modulo
r_{N}
, and its inverse is
B_{N}
.

Consider now the set of matrices in
M_{L}(\mathbb{Z})
consisting of all powers
A^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, of
A
, taken modulo
r_{N}
. This set being finite, there exist two integers
0\leq n_{1,N}<n_{2,N}
such that
A^{n_{1,N}}\equiv A^{n_{2,N}}\mod r_{N}
.
Setting
n_{N}=n_{2,N}-n_{1,N}
,
A^{n_{N}}\equiv I\mod r_{N}
, and thus the sequence
(C_{n})
taken modulo
r_{N}
is periodic, with period
n_{N}
. It follows that the sequence
(c_{n})
itself taken modulo
r_{N}
is periodic of period
n_{N}
, so that, in particular,
c_{jn_{N}}\equiv c_{0}\mod r_{N}
for every
j\geq 0
. Setting

h_{N}=q_{1}^{\beta_{1,N}}\!\!\!\dots\,q_{s}^{\beta_{s,N}}
and remembering that
p^{N}-1=h_{N}.r_{N}
, we obtain that

h_{N}.c_{jn_{N}}\equiv h_{N}c_{0}\mod(p^{N}-1)
for every
j\geq 0
. Since
0\leq\beta_{i,N}<\gamma
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, the set
\{h_{N}\;;\;N\in I\}
is finite and consists of non-zero integers. Assumption (H) is satisfied, and the proof is concluded as usual thanks to Theorem
2.1
.∎

4
The multidimensional case: proof of Theorem
1.8

In this section,
d\geq 2
is an integer, and
A,B\in M_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
are two
d\times d
matrices with integer coefficients such that
\det A\neq 0
and
\det B\neq 0
. The matrix
A
is supposed to be similar in
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
to a diagonal matrix
D
whose diagonal coefficients
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}
are not of modulus
1
. Let
P\in GL_{d}(\mathbb{C})
be such that
A=PDP^{-1}
. The matrix
B
is supposed to be invertible in
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, i.e.
\det B\neq 0
. Theorem
1.8
states that the set

G_{A,B}{}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{A,c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\;;\;T_{B^{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\textrm{Leb}\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
. The proof of Theorem
1.8
follows the same structure as those of Theorems
2.1
and
1.5
, but certain technical difficulties that come with the multidimensional setting must be overcome.

We begin by proving an analogue of Theorem
2.3
.

4.a
Some dense classes of discrete measures in the set of A-invariant measures

Let
(N_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a strictly increasing sequence of integers. Consider the set

\boldsymbol{C}_{A,(N_{k})}=\bigl\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\;;\;A^{N_{k}}\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}\quad\textrm{for some}\ k\geq 1\bigr\}

which consists of periodic points for
T_{A}
having a period within the set

\{N_{k}\;;\;k\geq 1\}
. For each
\boldsymbol{x}\in\boldsymbol{C}_{A,(N_{k})}
, let
\mu_{\boldsymbol{x}}
be the measure defined by

\mu_{\boldsymbol{x}}=\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}\delta_{\{A^{j}\boldsymbol{x}\}}.

It is a discrete
T_{A}
-invariant probability measure on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Set

\boldsymbol{D}_{A,(N_{k})}=\bigl\{\mu_{\boldsymbol{x}}\;;\;\boldsymbol{x}\in\boldsymbol{C}_{A,(N_{k})}\bigr\}.

Taking inspiration from Theorem
2.3
, we would like to show that the set
\boldsymbol{D}_{A,(N_{k})}
is dense in
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
. If
(\mathbb{T}^{d},T_{A})
has the periodic specification property, this is an immediate consequence of Remark
2.4
. However,
T_{A}
is known to have the periodic specification property only in the case where
A
is an hyperbolic automorphism of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, i.e.
\det A=\pm 1
and
A
has no eigenvalue of modulus
1
. Since
A
is not assumed here to be an automorphism of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we need to take a different route. It will lead to the following weaker result, which is fortunately sufficient for our purposes:

Theorem 4.1
.

The convex hull of the set
\boldsymbol{D}_{A,(N_{k})}
is dense in
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
.

Proof.

Denote by
\boldsymbol{F}_{A,(N_{k})}
the
w^{*}
-closure in
\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of the convex hull of

\boldsymbol{D}_{A,(N_{k})}
. This is a
w^{*}
-closed convex subset of
\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, and also of the Banach space
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of complex measures on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, endowed with the norm
||\mu||:=|\mu|(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. This space
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is the dual space of
(C(\mathbb{T}^{d}),||\,.\,||_{\infty,\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, the space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Our aim is to show that
\boldsymbol{F}_{A,(N_{k})}=\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Suppose that it is not the case, and that there exists a measure
\mu_{0}
belonging to
\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\setminus\boldsymbol{F}_{A,(N_{k})}
.

Applying the Hahn-Banach Theorem in the locally convex space
(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
, we obtain that there exists a
w^{*}
-continuous linear functional
L:\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, as well as real numbers
\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
such that

\Re e(L(\mu))\leq\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}\leq\Re e(L(\mu_{0}))

for every
\mu\in\boldsymbol{F}_{A,(N_{k})}
. Since any
w^{*}
-continuous functional on
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d})=C(\mathbb{T}^{d})^{*}
acts as integration against an element of
C(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, there exists a function
f\in C(\mathbb{T}^{d})
such that

\Re e\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\,d\mu\leq\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}\leq\Re e\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f\,d\mu_{0}

for every
\mu\in\boldsymbol{F}_{A,(N_{k})}
. The measures
\mu
and
\mu_{0}
being nonnegative, replacing
f
by its real part we can assume that
f
is real-valued, and thus that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\,d\mu\leq\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f\,d\mu_{0}\quad\textrm{for every}\ \mu\in\boldsymbol{F}_{A,(N_{k})}.

(4)

Moreover, it is possible to assume that
f
is a Lipschitz map on
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, endowed with the distance induced by the sup norm
||\,.\,||_{\infty,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We thus suppose that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|f(\boldsymbol{x}_{1})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{2})|\leq C\,\inf\left\{||\boldsymbol{x}_{1}-\boldsymbol{x}_{2}-\boldsymbol{l}||_{\infty,\mathbb{R}^{d}}\;;\;\boldsymbol{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\}\quad\textrm{for every }\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\,\boldsymbol{x}_{2}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

(5)

For any integer
k\geq 1
and any element
\boldsymbol{x}_{k}
of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
such that
(A^{{N_{k}}}-I)\boldsymbol{x}_{k}=0
, the measure

\mu_{\boldsymbol{x}_{k}}=\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}\delta_{\{A^{{j}}\boldsymbol{x}_{k}\}}
belongs to
\boldsymbol{F}_{A,(N_{k})}
. Applying (
4
) to this measure yields that

\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f(A^{{j}}\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\leq\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f\,d\mu_{0}.

(6)

Let now
\boldsymbol{x}
be an arbitrary element of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, and let
k\geq 1
. Consider the vector
\boldsymbol{y}_{k}=(A^{N_{k}}-I)\,\boldsymbol{x}
, seen as an element of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(and not as an element of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
). There exists

\boldsymbol{l}_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
||\boldsymbol{y}_{k}-\boldsymbol{l}_{k}||_{\infty,\mathbb{R}^{d}}\leq 1
.
Recalling that

, with

, we thus have

\displaystyle\bigl|\bigl|\,P(D^{N_{k}}-I)P^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{l}_{k}\,\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq 1,

so that

\displaystyle\bigl|\bigl|\,(D^{N_{k}}-I)P^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}-P^{-1}\boldsymbol{l}_{k}\,\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{C}^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq||P^{-1}||_{\infty}

(7)

where

is the norm of the matrix

seen as an endomorphism of
(\mathbb{C}^{d},||\,.\,||_{\infty,\mathbb{C}^{d}})
.
The inequality (
7

\sup_{1\leq i\leq d}\bigl|\,(\lambda_{i}^{N_{k}}-1)\,(P^{-1}\boldsymbol{x})_{i}-(P^{-1}\boldsymbol{l}_{k})_{i}\bigr|\leq||P^{-1}||_{\infty}.

(8)

Since no eigenvalue of

belongs to the unit circle,

is invertible in

and it is legitimate to set
\boldsymbol{z}_{k}=(A^{N_{k}}-I)^{-1}\boldsymbol{l}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\boldsymbol{x}_{k}
be
the corresponding element of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, obtained by taking mod

\boldsymbol{z}_{k}

Then
\boldsymbol{x}_{k}
belongs to
\boldsymbol{C}_{A,(N_{k})}
, with
(A^{N_{k}}-I)\boldsymbol{x}_{k}=0
in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Also,

\boldsymbol{z}_{k}=P(D^{N_{k}}-I)^{-1}P^{-1}\,\boldsymbol{l}_{k}
, so that

P^{-1}\boldsymbol{z}_{k}=\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{N_{k}}-1}\cdot\bigl(P^{-1}\boldsymbol{l}_{k}\bigr)_{i}\Bigr)_{1\leq i\leq d}.

It follows from (
8
) that for every
i\in\{1\ldots,d\}
,

\bigl|(P^{-1}\boldsymbol{x})_{i}-(P^{-1}\boldsymbol{z}_{k})_{i}\bigr|\leq\dfrac{||P^{-1}||_{\infty}}{|\lambda_{i}^{N_{k}}-1|}\cdot

(9)

By (
5
),

\displaystyle\bigl|\,f(A^{j}\boldsymbol{x})-f(A^{j}\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\,\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C\,\inf\left\{||A^{j}\boldsymbol{x}-A^{j}\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\boldsymbol{l}||_{\infty,\mathbb{R}^{d}}\;;\;\boldsymbol{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\bigl|\bigl|A^{j}\boldsymbol{x}-A^{j}\boldsymbol{z}_{k}\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,||P||_{\infty}\,\bigl|\bigl|\,D^{j}(P^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}-P^{-1}\boldsymbol{z}_{k})\,\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty,\mathbb{C}^{d}}

\displaystyle=C\,||P||_{\infty}\,\sup_{1\leq i\leq d}\,|\lambda_{i}^{j}|\,.\,\bigl|(P^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}-P^{-1}\boldsymbol{z}_{k})_{i}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C\,||P||_{\infty}\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}\,|\lambda_{i}^{j}|\,.\,\bigl|(P^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}-P^{-1}\boldsymbol{z}_{k})_{i}\bigr|.

Plugging into (
9
) yields that

\bigl|\,f(A^{j}\boldsymbol{x})-f(A^{j}\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\,\bigr|\leq C\,||P||_{\infty}\,.\,||P^{-1}||_{\infty}\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}\dfrac{|\lambda_{i}|^{j}}{|\lambda_{i}^{N_{k}}-1|}\cdot

Hence

\displaystyle\Bigl|\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f(A^{j}\boldsymbol{x})-\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f(A^{j}\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C\,||P||_{\infty}\,||P^{-1}||_{\infty}\,\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\dfrac{1}{|\lambda_{i}^{N_{k}}-1|}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}|\lambda_{i}|^{j}

\displaystyle\leq C\,||P||_{\infty}\,||P^{-1}||_{\infty}\,\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\dfrac{|\lambda_{i}|^{N_{k}}-1}{|\lambda_{i}^{N_{k}}-1|\,(|\lambda_{i}|-1)}\cdot

Notice that

for all

(|\lambda_{i}|^{N_{k}}-1)/|\lambda_{i}^{N_{k}}-1|\longrightarrow 1

({|\lambda_{i}|^{N_{k}}-1)/|\lambda_{i}^{N_{k}}-1|\longrightarrow-1}

We obtain the existence of a positive constant

such that

\sup_{k\geq 1}\,\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}\dfrac{|\lambda_{i}|^{N_{k}}-1}{|\lambda_{i}^{N_{k}}-1|\,(|\lambda_{i}|-1)}\leq C^{\prime}.

Thus there exists

such that

\Bigl|\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f(A^{j}\boldsymbol{x})-\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f(A^{j}\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\Bigr|\leq\dfrac{C^{\prime\prime}}{N_{k}}\quad\textrm{for every}\ k\geq 1.

(10)

The right hand bound in (

0
as

6
) and (
10

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f(A^{j}\boldsymbol{x})\leq\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f\,d\mu_{0}\quad\textrm{for every }\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

(11)

Let
\varepsilon>0
be such that
\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}-\varepsilon
. Applying the Ergodic Decomposition Theorem to the measure
\mu_{0}
yields the existence of an ergodic

-invariant measure

on

It then follows from (

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f(A^{j}\boldsymbol{x})\leq\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}-\varepsilon\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f\,d\nu_{0}

(12)

for every
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}

But since the measure

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{k}-1}f(A^{j}\boldsymbol{x})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f\,d\nu_{0}

Proof of Lemma
4.2

Since
\det(A-I)\neq 0

\gamma\geq 1
such that for every

,
p_{i}^{\gamma}
does not divide
\det(A-I)

\widehat{\mu}_{\rho}(\boldsymbol{h}_{0})=\rho\neq 0
. Also
\widehat{B^{lm_{0}}\mu_{\rho}}(\boldsymbol{h}_{0})=(1-\rho)\widehat{B^{lm_{0}}\mu}(\boldsymbol{h}_{0})+\rho=\widehat{\mu_{\rho}}(\boldsymbol{h}_{0})
for every
l\geq 0
, and hence
\mu_{\rho}
belongs to
G_{A,B}^{\,\gamma}
. We have thus shown that
G_{A,B}^{\,\gamma}
is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
.

Any measure
\mu\in G_{A,B}^{\,\gamma}
is such that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}|\widehat{B^{n}\mu}(\boldsymbol{h}_{0})|>0
, and hence (since
\boldsymbol{h}_{0}\neq\boldsymbol{0}
) such that

B^{n}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\textrm{Leb}_{d}
for every
\mu\in G_{A,B}^{\,\gamma}
. So the set

G_{A,B}^{\,0}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\;;\;B^{n}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\textrm{Leb}_{d}\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
.

In order to complete the proof, it remains to show the following analogue of Fact
2.8
:

Fact 4.5
.

The set
\mathcal{P}_{A,c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
.

Proof of Fact
4.5
.

As mentioned already in the proof of Fact
2.8
, the set
\mathcal{P}_{A,c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is known to be a
G_{\delta}
subset of
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
, so that only its density remains to be proved. The argument for this follows closely the proof of
[
43
]
*Th. 2, and reproves at the same time that
\mathcal{P}_{A,c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is
G_{\delta}
.

For every
\tau>0
, let

F_{\tau}:=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\;;\;\exists\,\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\ \textrm{such that}\ \mu(\{\boldsymbol{x}\})\geq\tau\bigr\}.

Then the set
F_{\tau}
is easily seen to be closed in
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
. Let us now show that
F_{\tau}
is nowhere dense. Let
(N_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a strictly increasing sequence of
prime numbers
such that
N_{1}>1/\tau
. By Theorem
4.1
, the convex hull of the set

\boldsymbol{D}_{A,(N_{k})}=\bigl\{\mu_{\boldsymbol{x}}\;;\;A^{N_{k}}\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}\ \textrm{for some}\ k\geq 1\bigr\}

is dense in
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
. Hence, given a non-empty open subset
\mathcal{V}
of
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
, there exist vectors
\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{x}_{r}
in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, integers
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1
and coefficients
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\geq 0
with
\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}=1
and
A^{N_{k_{i}}}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}=\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq r
such that

\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\mu_{\boldsymbol{x}_{i}}\quad\textrm{belongs to}\ \mathcal{V}.

Since
N_{k_{i}}
is prime, the minimal period of
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
is
N_{k_{i}}
, and thus

\mu_{\boldsymbol{x}_{i}}(\{\boldsymbol{x}\})\leq\dfrac{1}{N_{k_{i}}}<{\tau}\quad\textrm{for every}\ \boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

It follows that
\mu(\{\boldsymbol{x}\})<\tau
for every
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, and
\mu
does not belong to
F_{\tau}
. So
F_{\tau}
is nowhere dense in
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
, and

\mathcal{P}_{A,c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})=\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\setminus\bigcup_{l\geq 1}F_{2^{-l}}

is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
by the Baire Category Theorem.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.8
is completed by combining Fact
4.5
with the assertion that
G_{A,B}^{0}
is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
.
\square

Remark 4.6
.

The proof of Fact
4.5
would apply equally well to Fact
2.8
, but since the result is more standard in the one-dimensional case, we preferred to mention the classical arguments.

5
Further results and remarks

5.a
A complement to a result of Johnson and Rudolph

Let
p\geq 2
be an integer, and let
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of positive integers. We have recalled in the introduction and in Section
3
conditions on
(c_{n})
implying that each measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
which is ergodic and of positive entropy is
(c_{n})
-generic — thus showing that the set

G^{\prime}_{p,(c_{n})}:=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{c_{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}\quad\textrm{along a sequence of upper density}\ 1\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.
We present here an alternative harmonic analysis approach to this kind of result. It has the benefit of circumventing the arguments that depend on positive entropy, when applicable.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
, and let
{(c_{n})}_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of integers satisfying the following condition:

(\star)

there exists a sequence
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of elements of
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
such that

\mu_{k}\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\delta_{1}
, and moreover, the set

\bigl\{n\geq 1\;;\;|\widehat{\mu}_{k}(a.c_{n})|<\varepsilon\bigr\}

has density
1
for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, every
\varepsilon>0
and every
k\geq 1
.

Then the set
G^{\prime}_{p,(c_{n})}
is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.

A word about terminology: saying that a sequence
(\nu_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of measures converges to
\nu
along a subset of upper density
1
means that for any neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
of
\nu
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
, the set
\{n\geq 1\;;\;\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{V}\}
has upper density
1
, i. e.

\limsup_{N\to+\infty}\,\dfrac{1}{N}\,\#\,\bigl\{1\leq n\leq N\;;\;\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{V}\bigr\}=1.

This is equivalent to the following property: for any
a_{0}\geq 1
and any
\varepsilon>0
, the set

\bigl\{n\geq 1\;;\;|\widehat{\nu}_{n}(a)-\widehat{\nu}(a)|<\varepsilon\quad\textrm{for every}\ a\in\mathbb{Z}\ \textrm{with}\ |a|\leq a_{0}\bigr\}

has upper density
1
.
In this case, one can construct a strictly increasing sequence
(N_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of integers such that

\dfrac{1}{N_{k}}\,\#\,\bigl\{1\leq n\leq N_{k}\;;\;\forall\,|a|\leq k,\ |\widehat{\nu}_{n}(a)-\widehat{\nu}(a)|<2^{-k}\bigr\}\geq 1-2^{-k}

for every
k\geq 1
, and
N_{k+1}\geq 2^{k}N_{k}
. If we consider the strictly increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j\geq 1}
obtained by enumerating the set

D=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\bigl\{N_{k-1}<n\leq N_{k}\;;\;\forall\,|a|\leq k,\ |\widehat{\nu}_{n}(a)-\widehat{\nu}(a)|<2^{-k}\bigr\}

(with the convention that
N_{0}=0
), we obtain that
D=\{n_{j}\;;\;j\geq 1\}
has upper density
1
and that

\widehat{\nu}_{n_{j}}(a)\longrightarrow\widehat{\nu}(a)
as
j\longrightarrow+\infty
for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We first observe that the set
G^{\prime}_{p,(c_{n})}
can be written as
G^{\prime}_{p,(c_{n})}=\widetilde{G}_{p,(c_{n})}\cap\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{p,(c_{n})}=\bigl\{

\displaystyle\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;\forall\,N_{0},a_{0}\geq 1,\quad\forall\,\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}

\displaystyle\exists\,N>N_{0},\quad\exists\,F\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\quad\textrm{with}\ \#\,F\geq(1-\delta)N

\displaystyle\textrm{such that}\ \forall\,a\in\mathbb{Z}\ \textrm{with}\ 0<|a|\leq a_{0},\ \forall\,n\in F,\;|\widehat{\mu}(a.c_{n})|<\varepsilon\bigr\}.

The set
\widetilde{G}_{p,(c_{n})}
is clearly
G_{\delta}
in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
. Since
\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
is residual in
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
, it suffices to show that
\widetilde{G}_{p,(c_{n})}
is dense in
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
. In order to do this, we are going to exhibit a dense set of measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
with the following property:

\forall\,a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\ \forall\,\varepsilon>0,\ \textrm{the set}\ \{n\geq 1\;;\;|\widehat{\mu}(ac_{n})|<\varepsilon\}\ \textrm{has density 1.}

(13)

Since the intersection of finitely many sets of density
1
is again of density
1
, the measures in this set will be such that

\forall\,a_{0}\geq 1,\ \forall\,\varepsilon>0,\ \textrm{the set}\ \{n\geq 1\;;\;\forall\,0<|a|\leq a_{0},\ |\widehat{\mu}(ac_{n})|<\varepsilon\}\ \textrm{has density 1}

(14)

and hence upper density
1
. Such measures will hence belong to the set
G^{\prime}_{p,(c_{n})}
.

Our assumption
(\star)
states that the measures
\mu_{k}
,
k\geq 1
, satisfy (
13
). Fix
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
, and set
\nu_{k}=\mu_{k}*\nu
for every
n\geq 1
. For any

\varepsilon>0
, the set

\{n\geq 1\;;\;|\widehat{\nu}_{k}(a.c_{n})|<\varepsilon\}
has density
1
, and it follows that the measures
\nu_{k}
satisfy (
13
). Since
\nu_{k}\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\nu
as
{k\to+\infty,}
this concludes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Theorem
5.1
applies for instance to the case where
c_{n}=q^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, provided that
p,q\geq 2
are two multiplicatively independent integers, and allows to retrieve
[
26
]
*Th. 8.2, which states that
G^{\prime}_{p,(q^{n})}
is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.

To this aim, it suffices to exhibit a sequence
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of measures from
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
satisfying (
13
) and such that
\mu_{k}\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\delta_{1}
.
The measures that we shall consider are the Bernoulli convolutions
\mu_{\Theta}
introduced at the end of the proof of Theorem
1.5
, where
\Theta=(\theta_{0},\dots,\theta_{p-1})
is a
p
-tuple of elements of
(0,1)
with
\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\theta_{j}=1
.
They are
T_{p}
-invariant, and

\widehat{\mu}_{\Theta}(m)=\prod_{n\geq 1}\Bigl(\theta_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\theta_{j}e^{2i\pi mjp^{-n}}\Bigr)\quad\textrm{for every }m\in\mathbb{Z}.

It is shown by Lyons in
[
36
]
and by Feldman and Smorodinsky in
[
16
]
that
T_{q^{n}}\mu_{\Theta}\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}

as
{n\to+\infty.}
Since
\mu_{\Theta}\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\delta_{1}
as

{\Theta\to(1,0,\dots,0),}
assumption
(\star)
from Theorem
5.1
is satisfied, and
G^{\prime}_{p,(q^{n})}
is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.

The behaviour of the Fourier coefficients of Bernoulli convolutions has been studied extensively, particularly when
p
is equal to
2
or
3
;
see for instance the classical book
[
28
]
by Kahane and Salem. The reader is also encouraged to have a look at related recent papers like
[
12
,
45
]
and the references therein. An important work on the subject is that of Blum and Epstein
[
8
]
, where the authors provide upper and lower bounds on

|\widehat{\mu}_{\Theta}(m)|^{2}
which allow them to give a characterisation of sequences of positive integers along which
\widehat{\mu}_{\Theta}(m)
tends to
0
. In the case
p=2
, this characterisation is given in terms of the order of magnitude of
R(m)
, which is the number of
runs
,
i. e.
of maximal blocks of the same digit
0
or
1
, appearing in the binary expansion of
m
. Equivalently,
R(m)
is the number of digits changes in the binary expansion of
m
. Then as soon as
\Theta\neq(1/2,1/2)
(in which case
\mu_{\Theta}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
), there ex ist two constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that, for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\exp(-C_{2}R(m))\leq|\widehat{\mu}_{\Theta}(m)|\leq\exp(-C_{1}R(m)).

It follows that if
(m_{k})
is any strictly increasing sequence of integers, we have
\widehat{\mu}_{\Theta}(m_{k})\longrightarrow 0
as
k\longrightarrow+\infty
if and only if
R(m_{k})\longrightarrow+\infty
as
k\longrightarrow+\infty
.

For general
p
, here is the result proved by Blum and Epstein in
[
8
]
:

Theorem 5.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
p\geq 2
.
Let
\Theta=(\theta_{0},\dots,\theta_{p-1})
be a
p
-tuple of elements from
(0,1)
with the property that the polynomial
Q_{\Theta}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\theta_{j}z^{j}
does not vanish on
\mathbb{T}
. Let, for
m\geq 0
,

\psi(m)=R_{0}(m)+R_{p-1}(m)+N(m),

where
R_{0}(m)
is the number of maximal blocks of
0
s,
R_{p-1}(m)
is the number of maximal blocks of
(p-1)
s, and
N(m)
is the number of digits other than
0
and
p-1
in the expansion of
m
in base
p
. Then there exist two constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\exp(-C_{2}\psi(m))\leq|\widehat{\mu}_{\Theta}(m)|\leq\exp(-C_{1}\psi(m))\quad\textrm{for every}\ m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Hence
\widehat{\mu}_{\Theta}(m_{k})\longrightarrow 0
as
k\longrightarrow+\infty
if and only if
\psi(m_{k})\longrightarrow+\infty
as
k\longrightarrow+\infty
.

As a consequence, we obtain the following result, which we state using the notation from Theorem
5.2
:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of integers such that, for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, the sequence
(\psi(a.c_{n}))_{n\geq 0}
tends to infinity along a sequence of density
1
. Then the set
G^{\prime}_{p,(c_{n})}
is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies assumption
(\star)
of Theorem
5.1
. Let
\Theta=(\theta_{0},\dots,\theta_{p-1})
be a sequence of elements of
(0,1)
summing up to
1
, and suppose that
\theta_{0}>1/2
. Then

\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\theta_{j}<1/2
, and hence we have

|Q_{\Theta}(z)|\geq\theta_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\theta_{j}|z|^{j}>0

for every
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|\leq 1
. Thus the polynomial
Q_{\Theta}
does not vanish on
\mathbb{T}
. Let
(\Theta_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be such that
{\Theta_{k}\to(1,0,\dots,0)}
as
{k\to+\infty,}
and
Q_{\Theta_{k}}
does not vanish on
\mathbb{T}
. Then
\mu_{\Theta_{k}}\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\delta_{1}
as
{k\to+\infty}
. Moreover, the assumption of Proposition
5.3
combined with Theorem
5.2
implies that for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,
{\widehat{\mu}_{\Theta_{k}}(ac_{n})\to 0}
along a sequence of density
1
. The assumption
(\star)
is thus satisfied, and Proposition
5.3
follows.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

If
p,q\geq 2
are multiplicatively independent integers, and
c_{n}=q^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, it is shown in
[
16
]
*Prop. 1 that any word
w
in the letters
0,1,\dots,p-1
appears in the expansion of
q^{n}
in base
p
for every
n
belonging to a set of integers of density
1
. The proof given there can be extended to show that for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,
w
appears in the
expansion of
a.q^{n}
in base
p
for every
n
belonging to a set of integers of density
1
.

5.b
Some open questions

In Sections
2
and
3
, numerous examples of sequences
(c_{n})
were presented, for which the set

G_{p,(c_{n})}{}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{c_{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\textrm{Leb}\bigr\}

was found to be a residual subset of
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
.
The condition (H) presented in Section
2
is the most general one that we can provide for the residuality of
G_{p,(c_{n})}{}
to hold. However, it does not apply to
all
sequences
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, leaving the following intriguing question unanswered.

Question 5.5
.

Let
p\geq 2
, and let
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be any strictly increasing sequence of integers. Is it true that the set
G_{p,(c_{n})}{}
is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
?

If
\mu
is
(c_{n})
-generic, then

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}T_{c_{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}\quad\textrm{as}\ {N\to+\infty}.

It is thus natural to consider the set

G_{p,(c_{n})}^{\prime\prime}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}T_{c_{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}\quad\textrm{as }N\longrightarrow+\infty\bigr\}

and to ask the following question.

Question 5.6
.

Let
p\geq 2
, and let
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a strictly increasing sequence of integers. Is the set
G_{p,(c_{n})}^{\prime\prime}
residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
?

For the sequences considered in
[
24
,
26
,
39
,
32
]
, the density of the set
G_{p,(c_{n})}^{\prime\prime}{}
in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
follows from the result that ergodic measures of positive entropy in
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
are
(c_{n})
-generic. But the question of the residuality remains widely open, and it is actually not known if the set
G_{p,(q^{n})}^{\prime\prime}{}
is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{T}),w^{*})
when
q\geq 2
is an integer which is multiplicatively independent from
p
. This question is also connected to another conjecture from
[
37
]
, called (C7), which runs as follows and seems to be still open:

Conjecture 5.7
.

Let
p,q\geq 2
be multiplicatively independent integers. For any measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p,c}(\mathbb{T})
,
\mu
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{T}
is normal in base
q
, i.e.

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{2i\pi aq^{n}x}\to 0\quad\textrm{as}\quad N\to\infty\quad\textrm{for every}\ a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.

In our examination of Conjecture
1.4
in the multidimensional context, we have established conditions on matrices
A,B\in M_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
with non-zero determinant that imply that the set

G_{A,B}{}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{A,c}(\mathbb{T})\;;\;T_{B^{n}}\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{w^{*}}}\textrm{Leb}_{d}\bigr\}

is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
. These conditions (that
A
be diagonalisable in
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, that
\det A
and
\det B
be relatively prime…) arise due to technical difficulties in the proofs in the higher dimensional case. However, it may be that these conditions are not necessary; this is true in the one-dimensional setting.

Question 5.8
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
A,B\in M_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\det A\neq 0
and
\det B\neq 0
. Is it true that the set
G_{A,B}
is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
?

Let also

G_{A,B}^{\prime\prime}=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{A,c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\;;\;\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}T_{B^{n}}\mu\xrightarrow{\text{$w^{*}$}}\textrm{Leb}_{d}\quad\textrm{as }N\longrightarrow+\infty\bigr\}.

In analogy to Question
5.6
, one may also ask:

Question 5.9
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
A,B\in M_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\det A\neq 0
and
\det B\neq 0
. Is it true that the set
G_{A,B}^{\prime\prime}
is residual in
(\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),w^{*})
?

Warped product Quasi Bi-slant Submanifolds of Kaehler Manifolds
Mehraj Ahmad Lone ∗, Prince Majeed

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notion of warped product quasi bi-slant submanifolds in Kaehler manifolds. We have shown that every warped product quasi bi-slant submanifold in a Kaehler manifold is either a Riemannian product or a warped product quasi hemi slant submanifold. Furthermore, we provide examples for both cases.

\displaystyle J^{2}=-I,

\displaystyle(\bar{\nabla}_{X}J)Y=0,

(2.3)

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a submanifold of an almost Hermitian manifold
\bar{M}
. Then, we say

if there exists a pair of orthogonal distributions

of

(a)

3.
Quasi bi-slant submanifolds of Kaehler manifolds

In this section, we define and study quasi bi-slant submanifolds of Kaehler manifolds.

Definition 3.1
.

A submanifold
M
of an almost Hermitian manifold
\bar{M}
is called a quasi bi-slant submanifold if
there exist distributions
D
,
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
such that:

(a)
TM
admits the orthogonal direct decomposition as

\displaystyle TM=D\oplus D_{1}\oplus D_{2};

(b)
J(D)=D
i.e.,
D
is invariant;

(c)
J(D_{1})\perp D_{2}
;

(d) For any non-zero vector field
X\in(D_{1})_{x}
;
x\in M
; the angle
\theta_{1}
between
JX
and
(D_{1})_{x}
is constant and independent of the choice of point
x
and
X
in
(D_{1})_{x}
;

(e) For any non-zero vector field
Z\in(D_{2})_{y}
;
y\in M
; the angle
\theta_{2}
between
JZ
and
(D_{2})_{y}
is constant and independent of the choice of point
y
and
Z
in
(D_{2})_{y}
;

The angles
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{1}
are called slant angles of quasi bi-slant submanifold.

Remark 3.2
.

We can generalize the above definition by taking
TM=D\oplus D_{\theta_{1}}\oplus D_{\theta_{2}}...\oplus D_{\theta_{n}}
. Hence we can define multi-slant submanifolds, quasi multi-slant submanifolds etc.

Let
M
be a quasi bi-slant submanifold of an almost Hermitian manifold
\bar{M}
. We denote the projections
of
X\in\Gamma(TM)
on the distributions
D
,
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
by
P
,
Q
and
R
, respectively. Then we can write, for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)

\displaystyle X=PX+QX+RX,

(3.1)

we, can write

\displaystyle JX=\phi X+\omega X,

(3.2)

where
\phi X
and
\omega X
are tangential and normal components of
JX
on
M
, respectively.

Using (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle JX

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle JPX+JQX+JRX

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi PX+\omega PX+\phi QX+\omega QX+\phi RX+\omega RX.

Since
JD=D
, we have
\omega PX=0
. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle JX=\phi PX+\phi QX+\omega QX+\phi RX+\omega RX.

(3.4)

This means, for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
, we have

\displaystyle\phi X=\phi PX+\phi QX+\phi RX\ \ \textnormal{and}\ \ \omega X=\omega QX+\omega RX.

Thus, we have the following decomposition

\displaystyle J(TM)\subset D\oplus\phi D_{1}\oplus\omega D_{1}\oplus\phi D_{2}\oplus\omega D_{2}.

Since
\omega D_{1}\in(T^{\perp}M)
and
\omega D_{2}\in(T^{\perp}M)
, we have

\displaystyle T^{\perp}M=\omega D_{1}\oplus\omega D_{2}\oplus\mu,

(3.5)

where
\mu
is the orthogonal complement of
\omega D_{1}\oplus\omega D_{2}
in
(T^{\perp}M)
and it is invariant with respect to
J
.

For any
Z\in\Gamma(T^{\perp}M)
, we put

\displaystyle JZ=BZ+CZ,

where
BZ\in\Gamma(TM)
and
CZ\in\Gamma(T^{\perp}M)
.

.

Let

(i)
\phi^{2}X=-(\cos^{2}\theta_{1})X
,

(ii)
g(\phi X,\phi Y)=(\cos^{2}\theta_{1})g(X,Y)

(iii)

for any

Lemma 3.4
.

[

Let

\bar{M}

(i)
\phi^{2}Z=-(\cos^{2}\theta_{2})Z

(ii)

4.
Some Results on quasi bi-slant submanifolds

For a proper quasi bi-slant submanifold
M
of a kaehler manifold
\bar{M}
, the normal bundle of

is decomposed as

\displaystyle T^{\perp}M=\omega D_{1}\oplus\omega D_{2}\oplus\mu,

(4.1)

where
\mu
is the orthogonal complement of
\omega D_{1}\oplus\omega D_{2}

(T^{\perp}M)

The following results for proper quasi bi-slant submanifolds is given as:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
M
be a proper quasi bi-slant submanifold of a Kaehler manifold
\bar{M}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\cos^{2}\theta_{1})g(\nabla_{X}QY,\phi Z)+g(A_{\omega\phi QY}\phi Z,X)-(\sin^{2}\theta_{1})g(A_{\omega Z}X,QY)

(4.2)

\displaystyle+(\cos^{2}\theta_{2})g(\nabla_{X}RY,\phi Z)+g(A_{\omega\phi RY}\phi Z,X)-(\sin^{2}\theta_{2})g(A_{\omega Z}X,RY)

\displaystyle+g(\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\omega Z,\omega\phi QY)+g(\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\omega Z,\omega\phi RY)+g(A_{\omega QY}Z,X)

\displaystyle+g(A_{\omega RY}Z,X),

for any
X\in\Gamma(D_{1})
and
Y,Z\in\Gamma(D_{1}\oplus D_{2})
, where
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are slant angles of slant distribution
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, respectively.

Proof.

For any
X\in\Gamma(D_{1})
and
Y,Z\in\Gamma(D_{1}\oplus D_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,\phi Z)=g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}(QY+RY),JZ)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}(QY+RY),\omega Z).

Using (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
2.4
) and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(J\bar{\nabla}_{X}QY,Z)-g(J\bar{\nabla}_{X}RY,Z)-g(\sigma(X,QY),\omega Z)

\displaystyle-g(\sigma(X,RY),\omega Z).

Then by applying
\bar{\nabla}J=0
, and using (
2.6
) and (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}JQY,Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}JRY,Z)-g(\sigma(X,QY),\omega Z)

\displaystyle-g(\sigma(X,RY),\omega Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi QY,Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega QY,Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi RY,Z)

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega RY,Z)-g(A_{\omega Z}X,QY)-g(A_{\omega Z}X,RY).

On simplifying above equation and using (
2.5
), we arrive at

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi QY,Z)+g(A_{\omega QY}X,Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi RY,Z)

\displaystyle+g(A_{\omega RY}X,Z)-g(A_{\omega Z}X,QY)-g(A_{\omega Z}X,RY).

Using (
2.2
), the above equation can be re-written as

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(J\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi QY,JZ)+g(A_{\omega QY}X,Z)-g(J\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi RY,JZ)

\displaystyle+g(A_{\omega RY}X,Z)-g(A_{\omega Z}X,QY)-g(A_{\omega Z}X,RY).

Since, the shape operator
A
is self-adjoint, it follows from (
2.3
), (
2.6
) and (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}J\phi QY,\phi Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}J\phi QY,\omega Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}J\phi RY,\phi Z)

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}J\phi RY,\omega Z)+g(A_{\omega QY}Z,X)+g(A_{\omega RY}Z,X)

\displaystyle-g(A_{\omega Z}X,QY)-g(A_{\omega Z}X,RY)

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi^{2}QY,\phi Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega\phi QY,\phi Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi^{2}RY,\phi Z)

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega\phi RY,\phi Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi^{2}QY,\omega Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega\phi QY,\omega Z)

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi^{2}RY,\omega Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega\phi RY,\omega Z)+g(A_{\omega QY}Z,X)

\displaystyle+g(A_{\omega RY}Z,X)-g(A_{\omega Z}X,QY)-g(A_{\omega Z}X,RY).

Now, using (

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\cos^{2}\theta_{1})g(\nabla_{X}QY,\phi Z)+g(A_{\omega\phi QY}X,\phi Z)+(\cos^{2}\theta_{2})g(\nabla_{X}RY,\phi Z)

\displaystyle+g(A_{\omega\phi RY}X,\phi Z)+(\cos^{2}\theta_{1})g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QY,\omega Z)+g(\omega\phi QY,\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega Z)

\displaystyle(\cos^{2}\theta_{2})g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}RY,\omega Z)+g(\omega\phi RY,\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega Z)+g(A_{\omega QY}Z,X)

\displaystyle+g(A_{\omega RY}Z,X)-g(A_{\omega Z}X,QY)-g(A_{\omega Z}X,RY).

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,\phi Z)

5.
Warped product quasi bi-slant submanifolds of Kaehler manifold

Let
(M_{1},g_{1})
and
(M_{2},g_{2})
be two Riemannian manifolds and
f>0
, be a positive differentiable function on
M_{1}
. Consider the product manifold
M_{1}\times M_{2}
with its canonical projections
\pi:M_{1}\times M_{2}\rightarrow M_{1}
and
\rho:M_{1}\times M_{2}\rightarrow M_{2}
. The warped product
M=M_{1}\times_{f}M_{2}
is the product manifold
M_{1}\times M_{2}
equipped with the Riemannian metric
g
such that

\displaystyle g(X,Y)=g_{1}(\pi_{\ast}(X),\pi_{\ast}(Y))+(f\circ\pi)^{2}g_{2}(\rho_{\ast}(X),\rho_{\ast}(Y))

for any tangent vector
X,Y\in TM
, where
\ast
is the symbol for the tangent maps. It was proved in
[
2
]
that for any
X\in TM_{1}
and
Z\in TM_{2}
, the following holds

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}Z=\nabla_{Z}X=(Xlnf)Z

(5.1)

where
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
g
on
M
. A warped product manifold
M=M_{1}\times_{f}M_{2}
is said to be trivial if the warping function
f
is constant. If
M=M_{1}\times_{f}M_{2}
is a warped product manifold then
M_{1}
is totally geodesic and
M_{2}
is a totally umbilical (see
[
2
,
8
]
).

From now onwards, we assume the ambient manifold
\bar{M}
is Kaehler manifold and
M
is quasi bi-slant submanifold in
\bar{M}
.

Now we give the following useful lemma for later use.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
M=M_{1}\times_{f}M_{2}
, where
M_{2}=M_{\theta_{1}}\times M_{\theta_{2}}
be a warped product quasi bi-slant submanifold of a Kaehler manifold
\bar{M}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Y),\omega Z)=g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega QY)+g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega RY),

(5.2)

for any
X,Z\in\Gamma(M_{1})
and
Y\in\Gamma(M_{2})
.

Proof.

For any
X,Y\in\Gamma(M_{1})
and
Z\in\Gamma(M_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Y),\omega Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,\omega Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,JZ)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,\phi Z).

Using (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and the orthogonality of vector fields given in condition
(c)
of Definition 3.1, we find

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Y),\omega Z)=-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}JY,Z)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Z,Y).

Using (
2.4
), (
3.1
), (
3.2
), (
5.1
) and orthoganality of vector fields, we obtain

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Y),\omega Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi QY,Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega QY,Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi RY,Z)

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega RY,Z)-(Xlnf)g(\phi Z,Y).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Y,Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega QY,Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega RY,Z).

Thus, using (
2.5
) and (
5.1
), we arrive at

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Y),\omega Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Z,\phi Y)+g(A_{\omega QY}X,Z)+g(A_{\omega RY}X,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(Xlnf)g(Z,\phi Y)+g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega QY)+g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega RY).

Using the orthoganality of vector fields, we get

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Y),\omega Z)=g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega QY)+g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega RY).

Hence, the proof of lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
M=M_{1}\times_{f}M_{2}
, where
M_{2}=M_{\theta_{1}}\times M_{\theta_{2}}
be a warped product quasi bi-slant submanifold of a Kaehler manifold
\bar{M}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega W)=g(\sigma(X,W),\omega QZ)+g(\sigma(X,W),\omega RZ),

(5.3)

for any
X\in\Gamma(M_{1})
and
Z,W\in\Gamma(M_{2})
.

Proof.

For any
X\in\Gamma(M_{1})
and
Z,W\in\Gamma(M_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Z,JW)-g(\nabla_{X}Z,\phi W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}JZ,W)-g(\nabla_{X}Z,\phi W).

Using (
2.4
), (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi QZ,W)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega QZ,W)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi RZ,W)

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\omega RZ,W)-g(\nabla_{X}Z,\phi W).

On further simplification, we arrive at

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\phi QZlnf)g(X,W)+g(A_{\omega QZ}X,W)-(\phi RZlnf)g(X,W)

\displaystyle+g(A_{\omega RZ}X,W)-(Zlnf)g(X,W).

Using orthogonality of vector fields, we get

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega W)=g(A_{\omega QZ}X,W)+g(A_{\omega RZ}X,W).

Using (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega W)=g(\sigma(X,W),\omega QZ)+g(\sigma(X,W),\omega RZ).

Hence, the proof of lemma is complete.
∎

6.
Main Result

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
M=M_{1}\times_{f}M_{2}
where
M_{2}=M_{\theta_{1}}\times M_{\theta_{2}}
be a warped product quasi bi-slant submanifold with bi-slant angles
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
in a Kaehler manifold
\bar{M}
. Then one of the following two cases must occur:

(1) The warping function
f
is constant i.e.,
M
is Riemannian product;

(2)
\theta_{2}=\frac{\pi}{2}
, i.e.,
M
is a warped product quasi hemi-slant submanifold such that
M_{\theta_{2}}
is a totally real submanifold
M_{\perp}
of
\bar{M}
.

Proof.

Let
M=M_{1}\times_{f}M_{2}
where
M_{2}=M_{\theta_{1}}\times M_{\theta_{2}}
be a warped product quasi bi-slant submanifold of a Kaehler manifold
\bar{M}
with bi-slant angles
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega W)=g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}X,JW)-g(\nabla_{Z}X,\phi W),

(6.1)

for any
X\in\Gamma(M_{1})
and
Z,W\in\Gamma(M_{2})
. Thus, by using (
2.1
)-(
2.4
) and (
5.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}JX,W)-(Xlnf)g(Z,\phi W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi PX,W)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi QX,W)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\omega QX,W)

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi RX,W)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\omega RX,W)-(Xlnf)g(Z,\phi W).

Thus, it follows from (
2.5
) and (
5.1
)

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\phi PXlnf)g(Z,W)-(\phi QXlnf)g(Z,W)

\displaystyle+g(A_{\omega QX}Z,W)-(\phi RXlnf)g(Z,W)

\displaystyle+g(A_{\omega RX}Z,W)-(Xlnf)g(Z,\phi W).

Using (
2.6
), we arrive at

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,Z),\omega W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\phi PXlnf)g(Z,W)-(\phi QXlnf)g(Z,W)

(6.2)

\displaystyle+g(\sigma(Z,W),\omega QX)-(\phi RXlnf)g(Z,W)

\displaystyle+g(\sigma(Z,W),\omega RX)-(Xlnf)g(Z,\phi W).

Interchanging
Z
by
W
in (
6.2
) and using (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle g(\sigma(X,W),\omega Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\phi PXlnf)g(Z,W)-(\phi QXlnf)g(Z,W)

(6.3)

\displaystyle+g(\sigma(Z,W),\omega QX)-(\phi RXlnf)g(Z,W)

\displaystyle+g(\sigma(Z,W),\omega RX)+(Xlnf)g(Z,\phi W).

Subtracting (
6.2
) from (
6.3
) and by applying Lemma 5.2 , we arrive at

\displaystyle(Xlnf)g(Z,\phi W)=0.

(6.4)

Again, after interchanging
W
by
\phi W
in (
6.4
) and using Lemma 3.4, we get

\displaystyle(\cos^{2}\theta_{2})(Xlnf)g(Z,W)=0.

Therefore, either
f
is constant or
\cos\theta_{2}=0
holds. Consequently, either
M
is a Riemannian product manifold or
\theta_{2}=\frac{\pi}{2}
. In the second case,
M
is a warped product quasi hemi-slant submanifold which hasbeen studied in
[
24
]
.
∎

7.
Some examples on warped product quasi bi-slant submanifolds of Kaehler manifold.

Example 1
.

Let
\mathbb{E}^{2n}
be the Euclidean
2n
-space with the standard metric and let
\mathbb{C}^{n}
denotes the complex Euclidean
n
-space
(\mathbb{E}^{2n},J)
equipped with the canonical complex structure
J
defined as

\displaystyle J\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\bigg)=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}},\hskip 14.22636ptJ\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\bigg)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},1\leq i,j\leq n.

Consider a submanifold
M
of
\mathbb{C}^{6}
defined by

\displaystyle\chi(u,v,w,r,s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(u\cos\theta_{1},v\cos\theta_{1},u\sin\theta_{1},v\sin\theta_{1},w\cos\theta_{2},r\cos\theta_{2},

\displaystyle w\sin\theta_{2},r\sin\theta_{2},-u-w+v+r,u+w+v+r,s,t).

It is easy to see that the tangent bundle
TM
of
M
is spanned by the following vectors

\displaystyle Z_{1}=\cos\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+\sin\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}},

\displaystyle Z_{2}=\cos\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}+\sin\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}},

\displaystyle Z_{3}=\cos\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}+\sin\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{4}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}},

\displaystyle Z_{4}=\cos\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{3}}+\sin\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{4}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}},

\displaystyle Z_{5}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{6}},\hskip 14.22636ptZ_{6}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{6}}.

Then , clearly we obtain

\displaystyle JZ_{1}=\cos\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}+\sin\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}},

\displaystyle JZ_{2}=-\cos\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-\sin\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}},

\displaystyle JZ_{3}=\cos\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{3}}+\sin\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{4}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}},

\displaystyle JZ_{4}=-\cos\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}-\sin\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{4}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}},

\displaystyle JZ_{5}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{6}},\hskip 14.22636ptJZ_{6}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{6}}.

Then, we find that
D=span\{Z_{5},Z_{6}\}
is an invariant distribution,
D_{1}=span\{Z_{1},Z_{2}\}
is a proper slant distribution with slant angle
\theta_{1}=\cos^{-1}(\frac{1}{3})
and
D_{2}=span\{Z_{3},Z_{4}\}
is again a proper slant distribution with slant angle
\theta_{2}=\cos^{-1}(\frac{1}{3})
. Hence the submanifold
M
defined by
\chi
is a proper quasi bi-slant submanifold of
\mathbb{C}^{6}
.

It is easy to verify that
D
and
D_{1}\oplus D_{2}
are integrable. If we denote the integrable manifolds of
D
,
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
by
M_{T}
,
M_{\theta_{1}}
and
M_{\theta_{2}}
, respectively. Then the metric tensor
g
of product manifold
M
is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ds^{2}+dt^{2}+3(du^{2}+dv^{2})+3(dw^{2}+dr^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{M_{T}}+3g_{M_{2}},

such that,

\displaystyle g_{M_{T}}=ds^{2}+dt^{2}\hskip 14.22636ptand\hskip 14.22636ptg_{M_{2}}=3(du^{2}+dv^{2})+dw^{2}+dr^{2},

where
M_{2}=M_{\theta_{1}}\times M_{\theta_{2}}
. In this case the warping function
f=\sqrt{3}
a constant, and hence
M
is simply a Riemannian product.

Example 2
.

Consider a submanifold
M
of
\mathbb{C}^{5}
defined by

\displaystyle\chi(u,v,w,s,t)=(v\cos u,w\cos u,v\sin u,w\sin u,-v+w,v+w,0,0,s,t),

with almost complex structure
J
defined by

\displaystyle J\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\bigg)=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}},\hskip 14.22636ptJ\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\bigg)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},1\leq i,j\leq 5.

It is easy to see that its tangent space
TM
of
M
is spanned by the following vectors

\displaystyle v_{1}=-v\sin u\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+v\cos u\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}-w\sin u\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}+w\cos u\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}},

\displaystyle v_{2}=\cos u\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+\sin u\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{3}},

\displaystyle v_{3}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}+\cos u\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}+\sin u\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{3}},

\displaystyle v_{4}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}},\hskip 14.22636ptv_{6}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle Jv_{1}=-v\sin u\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}+v\cos u\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}}+w\sin u\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-w\cos u\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}},

\displaystyle Jv_{2}=\cos u\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}+\sin u\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{3}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}},

\displaystyle Jv_{3}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{3}}-\cos u\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-\sin u\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}.

\displaystyle Jv_{4}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}},\hskip 14.22636ptJv_{5}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}}.

Let us put
D=span\{v_{4},v_{5}\}
is an invariant distribution,
D_{1}=span\{v_{2},v_{3}\}
a proper slant distribution with slant angle
\theta_{1}=\cos^{-1}\big(\frac{1}{3}\big)
and
D_{2}=span\{v_{1}\}
an anti-invariant distribution with slant angle
\theta_{2}=\frac{\pi}{2}
. Hence the submanifold
M
defined by
\chi
is a quasi bi-slant submanifold of
\mathbb{C}^{5}
.

It is easy to verify that
D
and
D_{1}\oplus D_{2}
are integrable. If we denote the integrable manifolds of
D
,
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
by
M_{T}
,
M_{\theta_{1}}
and
M_{\perp}
, respectively. Then the metric tensor
g
of product manifold
M
is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=ds^{2}+dt^{2}+3(dv^{2}+dw^{2})+(v^{2}+w^{2})du^{2},

such that

\displaystyle g_{M_{T}}=ds^{2}+dt^{2}\hskip 14.22636ptand\hskip 14.22636ptg_{M_{2}}=3(dv^{2}+dw^{2})+du^{2},

where
M_{2}=M_{\theta_{1}}\times M_{\perp}
. In this case the warping function
f=\sqrt{v^{2}+w^{2}}
and hence

is a case of warped product quasi hemi-slant submanifold. So we have discussed both the case of Theorem 6.1.

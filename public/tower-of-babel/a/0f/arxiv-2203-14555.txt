A Comment on the “Photon position operator with commuting components” by Margaret Hawton
A. Jadczyk, A. M. Schlichtinger

Abstract
We show that the position operator with commuting components proposed by M. Hawton [M. Hawton, Phys. Rev. A 59 , 954 (1999)] and developed in subsequent papers, including the recent ones, does not have the properties required for a photon position operator. Depending on the exact interpretation of the results it either concerns a triplet of massless spin zero particles rather than the photon, or does not have the required covariance properties under space rotations.



I
Introduction

The problem of localizability of photons is usually discussed in term of existence of a self-adjoint position operator
\bf{Q}
having the required natural commutation relations with the generators of space translations, space rotations as well as the space and time inversions within a unitary representation of the Poincaré group acting on the Hilbert space of the photon states. It is well known (cf. e.g.
mourad
and references therein) that there is a unique solution to this problem: the so-called Pryce operator
pryce
with noncommuting components.

Photon wave function

Photons are defined through a particular unitary representation of the Poincaré group characterized by mass zero and helicity

II.1
The Lie algebra

In any unitary representation the ten self-adjoint generators of the Poincaré group satisfy the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle[P_{\mu},P_{\nu}]=0,\,[P_{\mu},M_{\rho\sigma}]=i(\eta_{\mu\rho}P_{\sigma}-\eta_{\mu\sigma}P_{\rho}),

\displaystyle[M_{\mu\nu},M_{\rho\sigma}]=i(\eta_{\nu\rho}M_{\mu\sigma}-\eta_{\mu\rho}M_{\nu\sigma}+\eta_{\mu\sigma}M_{\nu\rho}-\eta_{\nu\sigma}M_{\mu\rho}).

The rotation and the boost generators
{\bf M}
and
{\bf N}
are then defined as

M^{i}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{ijk}M_{jk},\,N^{i}=M^{0i}=M_{i0}.

Thus, for instance,
M^{1}=M^{23}=M_{23}
, etc.

II.2
The Pryce position operator

The standard requirements for the selfadjoint photon position operator
X^{i}=X^{i*}
involve covariance under space translations and rotations as well as invariance under space and time reflections, and that
X^{i}
leave the photon subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{ph}
invariant. They are expressed by the following formulas:

\begin{split}[M^{i},X^{j}]&=i\epsilon^{ijk}X^{k},\,[P^{i},X^{j}]=-i\delta^{ij},\\
\Pi X^{i}\Pi&=-X^{i},\,\Theta X^{i}\Theta=X^{i},\,[X^{i},\Lambda^{2}]=0.\end{split}

(6)

The last condition is equivalent to: if
{\bf f}({\mathbf{p}})=c({\mathbf{p}})\,{\mathbf{p}},
where
c({\mathbf{p}})
is any scalar valued complex function of
{\mathbf{p}}
, then
X^{i}{\bf f}({\mathbf{p}})=c^{i}({\mathbf{p}})\,{\mathbf{p}},
for some functions
c^{i}({\mathbf{p}}),
which is easier to verify in practice.

It is shown in Ref.
mourad
that the above problem has a unique solution. It is the so-called Pryce operator
{\bf X}_{P}
given by

X_{P}^{i}=i\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{i}}+\sum_{j,k}\epsilon_{ijk}\frac{P^{j}S_{k}}{P_{0}^{2}}.

(7)

The problem and its solution

The original representation
{\bf M},{\bf N}.

In this case the operator
\hat{\bf Q}
is not covariant under rotations,
[M^{i},\hat{Q}^{j}]\neq i\epsilon^{ijk}\hat{Q}^{j}.

\neq 0.

Thus Hawton position operator does not satisfy the condition of covariance under rotations of the original representation.

The representation

6

for this representation
is identically zero. The representation describes a triplet of spinless particles, not the photon.

The representation

In this case

\hat{\bf Q}=U{\bf Q}U^{*},

\tilde{\Lambda}=U\Lambda U^{*},

[\hat{\bf Q},\tilde{\Lambda}^{2}]=U[{\bf Q},\Lambda^{2}]U^{*}.

Abstract
. We present a new approach to ternary Boolean algebras in which negation is derived from the ternary operation. The key aspect is the replacement of complete commutativity by other axioms that do not require the ternary operation to be symmetric.

Primary 06E05, 06D30; Secondary 03G25, 03C48

1.
Introduction

In 1947, Grau
[
6
]
considered an algebraic structure
(A,p,\bar{()})
consisting of a set
A
, a ternary operation
p\colon{A^{3}\to A}
and a unary operation
\bar{()}\colon{A\to A}
satisfying the following axioms

(A1)

p(a,b,p(c,d,e))=p(p(a,b,c),d,p(a,b,e))

(A2)

p(a,b,b)=p(b,b,a)=b

(A3)

p(a,b,\bar{b})=p(\bar{b},b,a)
.

This structure was called ternary Boolean algebra. For every chosen element
0\in A
with
a\wedge b=p(a,0,b)
and
a\vee b=p(a,\bar{0},b)
, the system
(A,\vee,\wedge,\bar{()},0,\bar{0})
is a Boolean algebra. Every other choice of an element
0\in A
gives an isomorphic Boolean algebra. Furthermore, the following condition (called complete commutativity) is derived from the axioms

(1)

p(a,b,c)=p(a,c,b)=p(c,a,b)

and the ternary operation is uniquely determined as

(2)

\displaystyle p(a,b,c)=(a\wedge b)\vee(b\wedge c)\vee(c\wedge a).

In the same year, Birkhoff and Kiss
[
2
]
considered a system
(A,p,0,1)
satisfying the axioms

(B1)

p(0,a,1)=a

(B2)

p(a,b,a)=a

(B3)

p(p(a,b,c),d,e)=p(p(a,d,e),b,p(c,d,e))

(B4)

p(a,b,c)=p(b,a,c)=p(b,c,a)

and showed that it is the same as a distributive lattice. The meet and join operations are defined again as
a\wedge b=p(a,0,b)
and
a\vee b=p(a,1,b)
and the distributive lattice is bounded with
0
and
1
as bottom and top elements, respectively. It is a Boolean algebra when there exists a unary operation
\bar{()}\colon{A\to A}
such that
p(a,0,\bar{a})=0
and
p(a,1,\bar{a})=1
. Once more, the ternary operation
p
is determined by the formula (
2
).
Note that
(A1)
and
(B3)
are equivalent under
(B4)
. Birkhoff and Kiss called symmetry to axiom
(B4)
which is the same as Grau’s complete commutativity (
1
).

The purpose of this note is to show that condition
(B4)
can be replaced by another type of conditions, namely
p(a,0,b)=a
and
p(a,1,b)=b
. In this new setting, the meet and join operations are defined differently as:

a\wedge b=p(0,a,b)\quad\textrm{and}\quad a\vee b=p(a,b,1).

Since complete commutativity is no longer present, conditions
(A1)
and
(B3)
must be carefully reformulated as

p(a,p(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}),c)=p(p(a,b_{1},c),b_{2},p(a,b_{3},c)).

This gives rise to a different formula for the ternary operation
p
with the advantage that negation may be derived as

\bar{a}=p(1,a,0).

The resulting formula for the ternary operation is then

p(a,b,c)=(\bar{b}\wedge a)\vee(b\wedge c).

In summary, our result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(A,p,0,1)
be a system consisting of a set
A
, together with a ternary operation
p
and two constants
0,1\in A
satisfying the following conditions:

(C1)

p(0,a,1)=a

(C2)

p(a,b,a)=a

(C3)

p(a,p(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}),c)=p(p(a,b_{1},c),b_{2},p(a,b_{3},c))

(C4)

p(a,0,b)=a=p(b,1,a)
.

For
a+b=p(a,b,\bar{a})
,
a\wedge b=p(0,a,b)
,
a\vee b=p(a,b,1)
,
\bar{a}=p(1,a,0)
,
the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

The system
(A,+,\wedge,0,1)
is a Boolean ring

(ii)

The system
(A,\vee,\wedge,\bar{()},0,1)
is a Boolean algebra

(iii)

p(a,b,c)=(\bar{b}\wedge a)\vee(b\wedge c)

(iv)

p(0,a,b)=p(a,a,b)

(v)

p(a,b,1)=p(a,b,b)
.

The proof of this theorem is detailed in the next section.

3.
Conclusion

It is readily shown that the category of Boolean algebras is isomorphic to the category of systems
(A,p,0,1)
satisfying conditions
(C1)
to
(C4)
and condition
(iii)
which can be written as

or conditions
(iv)
or
(v)

which has the advantage of defining negation as

(B2)
,
(B3)
and

[

]
and are referred as median algebras in
[

7

If the Boolean algebra

in Theorem
1.1

a+b=(\overline{b}\wedge a)\vee(b\wedge\overline{a})=p(a,b,\bar{a})
then

(\bar{b}\wedge a)\vee(b\wedge c)=\bar{b}a+bc.

The expression
p(a,b,c)=\bar{b}a+bc
, with
\bar{b}=1-b
, has recently been used to model the unit interval as a mobi algebra structure
[
5
]
which has
(C1)
to
(C4)
amongst its axioms.

[

4

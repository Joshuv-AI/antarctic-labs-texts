Dissecting Lepton Number Violating Interactions in the Left-Right Symmetric Model: 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta decay, Møller scattering, and collider searches
Gang Li, Michael J. Ramsey-Musolf, Sebastián Urrutia Quiroga, Juan Carlos Vasquez

Abstract
In the context of the left-right symmetric model, we study the interplay of neutrinoless double beta ( 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta ) decay, parity-violating Møller scattering, and high-energy colliders, resulting from the Yukawa interaction of the right-handed doubly-charged scalar to electrons, which could evade the severe constraints from charged lepton flavor violation. The 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β {0\nu\beta\beta} decay amplitude receives additional contributions from right-handed sterile neutrinos. The half-life, calculated in the effective field theory framework, allows for an improved description of the contributions involving non-zero mixing between left- and right-handed W W bosons and those arising from exchanging a light right-handed neutrino. We find that the relative sensitivities between the low-energy (or high-precision) and high-energy experiments are affected by the left-right mixing. On the other hand, our results show how the interplay of collider and low-energy searches provides a manner to explore regions that are inaccessible to 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β {0\nu\beta\beta} decay experiments.

Left-Right Symmetric Model

The LRSM
[
81
,
72
,
73
,
92
,
93
]

extends the SM gauge group to
SU(3)_{\rm C}\times SU(2)_{\rm L}\times SU(2)_{\rm R}\times U(1)_{B-L}
, where
B
and
L
denote the SM abelian baryon and lepton quantum numbers. Henceforth, we denote the field representation under the
SU(2)_{\rm R,L}
and
U(1)_{B-L}
groups by
(X_{\rm R},Y_{\rm L},Z_{B-L})

The scalar sector consists of one bidoublet
\Phi\in(2,2^{\ast},0)
,

\displaystyle\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}^{0}&\phi_{2}^{+}\\
\phi_{1}^{-}&\phi_{2}^{0}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(1)

and two scalar triplets
\Delta_{L}\in(1,3,2)
,
\Delta_{R}\in(3,1,2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{L,R}=\begin{pmatrix}\delta_{L,R}^{+}/\sqrt{2}&\delta_{L,R}^{++}\\
\delta_{L,R}^{0}&-\delta_{L,R}^{+}/\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2)

The extended gauge group is broken after the neutral scalars acquire vevs

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{R}\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
v_{R}/\sqrt{2}&0\end{pmatrix}\;,\quad\langle\Delta_{L}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
v_{L}e^{{\rm i}\theta_{L}}/\sqrt{2}&0\end{pmatrix}\;,

\displaystyle\langle\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa/\sqrt{2}&0\\
0&\kappa^{\prime}e^{{\rm i}\alpha}/\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}\;,

(3)

where all parameters are real
[
41
]
. The right-handed scalar triplet vev,
v_{R}
, is taken to lie well above the electroweak scale
v=246\,\mathrm{GeV}
. The bidoublet vevs,
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
, satisfy
\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+\kappa^{\prime\,2}}=v
. The phases
\alpha
and
\theta_{L}
induce CP violation and will be set to zero (
\alpha,\,\theta_{L}=0
) for the purposes of this study.
The scalar potential of the triplet and bidoublet sector follows the usual form
[
41
,
36
,
68
,
69
]
, leading to a spectrum that allows the doubly charged scalars to be relatively light
[
8
,
42
]
. This is because the squared mass,
M_{\delta_{R}^{\pm\pm}}^{2}\simeq\rho_{2}v_{R}^{2}
, is set by the dimensionless coupling
\rho_{2}
from the scalar potential.

Right-handed neutrinos appear in the doublet
L_{R}=(N,\ell_{R})^{\sf T}
and are thus charged under
SU(2)_{\rm R}
and couple to right-handed gauge bosons. It also couples to the scalar sector via the Yukawa interactions

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y}\ni

\displaystyle-\bar{Q}_{L}(\mathbf{\Gamma_{q}}\Phi+\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{q}}\widetilde{\Phi})Q_{R}-\bar{L}_{L}(\mathbf{\Gamma_{l}}\Phi+\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{l}}\widetilde{\Phi})L_{R}

\displaystyle-\bar{L}_{L}^{c}\,{\rm i}\tau_{2}\,\Delta_{L}\,\mathbf{f_{L}}\,L_{L}-\bar{L}_{R}^{c}\,{\rm i}\tau_{2}\,\Delta_{R}\,\mathbf{f_{R}}\,L_{R}+\text{H.c.}\,,

(4)

where
\widetilde{\Phi}\equiv\tau_{2}\Phi^{*}\tau_{2}
with
\tau_{2}
being the second Pauli matrix,
\Psi^{c}_{L,R}=P_{R,L}\Psi^{c}
, and
\Psi^{c}=C\overline{\Psi}^{\sf T}
. The projectors
P_{R,L}=(1\pm\gamma_{5})/2
and

is the usual charge conjugation matrix.

After the electroweak symmetry breaking (EWSB), the leptonic Yukawa interactions in Eq. (
4
) give rise to neutrino masses through the type-I
[

,

,
100
,

,

and type-II

seesaw mechanisms in terms of the symmetric

matrix
[

\displaystyle M_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{L}&M_{D}\\
M_{D}^{\sf T}&M_{R}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(5)

where the Dirac and Majorana mass matrices are given by
M_{D}=(\kappa\mathbf{\Gamma_{l}}+\kappa^{\prime}\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{l}})/\sqrt{2}
and
M_{L,R}=\sqrt{2}\,\,\mathbf{f_{L,R}}\,v_{L,R}
, respectively,

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}_{m}=U^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{L}\\

The neutrinos

\displaystyle U=\mathcal{U}_{1}\mathcal{U}_{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&R\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\dagger}M_{n}\,\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\ast}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\nu}&0\\

\displaystyle=M_{L}-M_{D}M_{R}^{-1}M_{D}^{\sf T}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}M_{\nu}&0\\

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}W_{L}^{\pm}\\
W_{R}^{\pm}\end{pmatrix}=\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}\cos\zeta&-\sin\zeta\\

The charged gauge boson masses are

\displaystyle M_{W_{1}}

\displaystyle\simeq M_{W_{L}}=\dfrac{g_{L}v}{2}\equiv M_{W}\,,

\displaystyle M_{W_{2}}

\displaystyle\simeq M_{W_{R}}=\dfrac{g_{R}v_{R}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

(16)

\displaystyle\lambda=\dfrac{M_{W_{1}}^{2}}{M_{W_{2}}^{2}}\;,\quad\tan\beta=\dfrac{\kappa^{\prime}}{\kappa}\equiv\xi\;,

\displaystyle\tan\zeta=\lambda\sin(2\beta)=\frac{2\lambda\tan\beta}{1+\tan^{2}\beta}=\frac{2\lambda\xi}{1+\xi^{2}}\,.

4.2
Parity-violating Møller scattering

In Møller scattering
[
40
]

asymmetry in the scattering of polarized electrons off unpolarized electrons using the

[

This measurement could represent an enhancement in fractional precision exceeding five times that of the only other similar one done by the E158 experiment at SLAC
[
11

The MOLLER sensitivity can be expressed as the following lower bound:

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda}{\sqrt{|g_{RR}^{2}-g_{LL}^{2}|}}~\simeq~7.5\,\mathrm{TeV}\,.

(37)

Here,

\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{eff}}=\sum_{i,j=L,R}\frac{g_{ij}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}(\bar{e}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}e_{i})(\bar{e}_{j}\gamma_{\mu}e_{j})\,,

46

{0\nu\beta\beta}
decay that violates the lepton number by two units.
The leptonic Yukawa interactions in Eq. (

\displaystyle\left(\bar{e}_{L}e_{L}^{c}\right)\left(\bar{e}_{L}e_{L}^{c}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\bar{e}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}e_{L}\right)\left(\bar{e}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}e_{L}\right)\,.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we have studied the interplay of
{0\nu\beta\beta}
-decay, parity-violating Møller scattering, and collider searches in probing the Yukawa interaction of the right-handed doubly-charged scalar to electrons
in the left-right symmetric model. We investigate the sensitivities of the low-energy experiments and the high-energy colliders and find that they complement each other in exploring the region of Yukawa coupling down to
10^{-7}
.

We employ the effective field theory framework to calculate the
{0\nu\beta\beta}
-decay rate with the contributions arising from the mediation of right-handed neutrinos and the right-handed doubly-charged scalar, considering possible non-zero
W_{L}-W_{R}
mixing. Compared to the earlier work of Ref.
[
43
]
, the effective field theory framework provides a more realistic assessment of the
{0\nu\beta\beta}
-decay sensitivity and yields generally significantly greater parameter space reach.

Four regimes are considered depending on the magnitude of Yukawa coupling, or equivalently, the mass of the right-handed neutrino given Eq. (
43
).
The sensitivities of the KamLAND-Zen and ton-scale experiments are compared with those for the precision measurements of parity violation in the MOLLER experiment.

We find that in the absence of
W_{L}-W_{R}
mixing, the MOLLER sensitivity is consistently stronger than that in future ton-scale
{0\nu\beta\beta}
decay experiments for the regime where the doubly-charged scalar dominates the
{0\nu\beta\beta}
amplitude. For non-zero mixing, however, the relative sensitivity between
{0\nu\beta\beta}
decay searches in ton-scale experiments and precision measurements of parity violation in the MOLLER experiments depends on the mass of the doubly-charge scalar. Moreover, since MOLLER cannot prove the small
|(f_{R})_{ee}|
region,
{0\nu\beta\beta}
decay experiments have the unique sensitivity to exclude values of
|(f_{R})_{ee}|
as small as
10^{-7}
, independent of the doubly-charged scalar contribution.

Finally, the interplay of collider and low-energy searches provides a manner to explore regions that are inaccessible to
{0\nu\beta\beta}
decay experiments, as they are currently conceived. Even in the absence of positive results in the next generations of future leptons or hadrons colliders, we are still able to identify the underlying dominant mechanism in
{0\nu\beta\beta}
decay and explore its corresponding parameter region.

The subject of this article is the convex solution of the Monge–Ampère
equation

The suitable notion of a weak formulation for this equation, the

[

]

uses the subdifferential

1.2.
Basic properties

The Monge–Ampère measure has the following scaling properties: If

\det D^{2}u=f
, more generally if
\mu_{u}=fdx
, then

(1.3)

\mu_{cu\circ A}=c^{n}\left(\det L_{A}\right)^{2}f\circ Adx

for

, an affine map

,
and its linear map

. As an elliptic equation, the Monge–Ampère

and

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\leq v

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{v}

\displaystyle\text{in }\mathcal{U},

\displaystyle\leq v

The Monge–Ampère measure is superadditive

u(0,x^{\prime})-u(x)=-u_{0}(x)+\ell_{g}(0,x^{\prime})-\ell_{g}(x).

Hence, letting
C_{L,g}
be the Lipschitz constant of
\ell_{g}
, it
remains to find a constant
C_{n}>0
such that for every
x\in K_{1,1}

(5.1)

\left|u_{0}(x)\right|\geq\lambda^{1/n}C_{n}\overline{a}(x_{1})

for
\overline{a}
from Definition
10
. The scaling property
(
1.3
) reduces the statement to the normalized
case where
\lambda=1
. Let
B_{T}:=\{1\}\times\overline{B}_{\sqrt{2}}^{n-1}

be the top base of
K_{1,\sqrt{2}}
. If
u_{0}
vanished anywhere
in
\Omega
, the convexity would constrain it to vanish at all, contradicting

\mu_{u_{0}}\geq dx
. So
u_{0}<0
in
\Omega
and attains a negative
maximum on the compactum
B_{T}\subset K_{2,2}
. Therefore, there
exists a small constant
C>0
such that for the convex envelope
\overline{w}_{*}

of
\overline{w}(x)=\overline{a}(x_{1})b(x^{\prime})
from Definition
10
,
it holds that

•

C\overline{w}_{*}\geq u_{0}
on
B_{T}
and anyhow
C\overline{w}_{*}=0\geq u_{0}

everywhere else on
\partial K_{1,\sqrt{2}}
,

•

\mu_{C\overline{w}_{*}}\leq 1dx\leq\mu_{u_{0}}
.

(The latter condition is enabled by Lemma
15
and
the scaling properties.) Then the comparision principle (
1.4
)
says that
C\overline{w}_{*}\geq u_{0}|_{K_{1,\sqrt{2}}}
and we get
for
x\in K_{1,1}
, where
-b(x^{\prime})=1-x^{2}/2\geq 1/2
,

\displaystyle-u_{0}(x)

\displaystyle\geq-C\overline{w}_{*}(x)\geq-C\overline{w}(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}C\overline{a}(x_{1}).

(2) The statements are independent of addition or subtraction of an
affine function, so it suffices to show it for
u_{0}
. For
n=2

we have just seen that
u_{0}
is not Lipschitz at the flat piece.
As a continuous function on the closure of an open domain, it is not
possible to make it Lipschitz by removing a null set from the domain,
so
\left\|\nabla u_{0}\right\|_{\infty}=\infty
. For
n\geq 3
,
assume that the affine map
L
from the first step is the identity
without loss of generality. Start with the estimate

\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{0}(x)\right|^{p}dx\geq\int_{B_{1}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{\partial u_{0}}{\partial x_{1}}(x_{1},x^{\prime})\right|^{p}dx_{1}\,dx^{\prime}.

We claim that
\int_{0}^{1}\left|\partial_{1}u_{0}(x_{1},x^{\prime})\right|^{p}dx_{1}=\infty

for each
x^{\prime}\in B_{1}^{n-1}
, such that the whole integral is infinite.
If it were not infinite, for every
\delta>0
there would have to
be an
\varepsilon>0
such that

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left|\partial_{1}u_{0}(x_{1},x^{\prime})\right|^{p}dx_{1}<\delta

by the monotone convergence theorem. Contradicting that, the Jensen
inequality, the fundamental theorem of calculus (which is applicable
because a continuous convex function on a compactum is absolutely
continuous), and (

) yield

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left|\frac{\partial u_{0}}{\partial x_{1}}(x_{1},x^{\prime})\right|^{p}dx_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon\left(\frac{u_{0}(\varepsilon,x^{\prime})-u_{0}(0,x^{\prime})}{\varepsilon}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\geq C\varepsilon^{1+p(2/n{-}1)}=C

for

.
∎

Pertubative corrections for Hartree-Fock-like Algebraic Bethe Ansatz analogue.
Jean-David Moisset, Laurie Carrier, Paul A. Johnson

Abstract
Rayleigh-Schrödinger perturbation theory corrections are developed for an algebraic Bethe ansatz of individual electrons. Numerical results are ambiguous and would need either an orbital optimization or a configuration interaction singles solution to be satisfactory. Two different expressions are computed to be certain that results are correct.

II
ABA for individual electrons

The ABA provides the eigenvectors of the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{ABA}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{i}+g\sum_{ij}a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{j}

(1)

which describes an aufbau filling of the lowest single particle states
\{\varepsilon\}
along with an isotropic scattering
g
of electrons between each site. Obviously, this Hamiltonian may be solved exactly by diagonalization. The point here is to try to understand the ABA in a simple case so that we can use that information for RG, where the analogue of HF, APIG, is intractable.

We employ spin-orbitals that have the usual structure,

\displaystyle[a^{\dagger}_{i},a_{j}]_{+}=\delta_{ij}

(2)

for which
i
and
j
are a complete set of spin and orbital indices. The ABA is built from a set of objects in terms of a complex number
u
:

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{a^{\dagger}_{i}}{u-\varepsilon_{i}}

(3)

\displaystyle a(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{a_{i}}{u-\varepsilon_{i}}.

(4)

These objects have the structure

\displaystyle[a^{\dagger}(u),a(v)]_{+}=\frac{A(u)-A(v)}{u-v}

(5)

where the scalar function
A(u)
is

\displaystyle A(u)=\frac{1}{g}-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{u-\varepsilon_{i}}.

(6)

In the limit that
u
and
v
are the same, this is well defined, with the result

\displaystyle[a^{\dagger}(u),a(v)]_{+}=\frac{\partial A(u)}{\partial u}.

(7)

II.1
ABA solution

The point of the ABA is that eigenvectors of (
1
) are products of
a^{\dagger}(u)
whose arguments satisfy a set of equations. Usually these equations are coupled, but for this particular case they are not. We denote the ABA states based on their
rapidities

\{u\}
as

\displaystyle\ket{\{u\}}=a^{\dagger}(u_{1})a^{\dagger}(u_{2})\dots a^{\dagger}(u_{2M})\ket{\theta}

(8)

where the vacuum state
\ket{\theta}
is understood such that
a_{j}\ket{\theta}=a(u)\ket{\theta}=0
. We emphasize that there are
2M
electrons in
2N
spin-orbitals. The state
\{u\}
is a Slater determinant of
2M
electrons in the basis of
a^{\dagger}(u)
. To establish the action of (
1
) on this state, the strategy is to move
\hat{H}_{ABA}
to the right until it destroys the vacuum. First, it is easy to confirm that

\displaystyle[\hat{H}_{ABA},a^{\dagger}(u)]=ua^{\dagger}(u)-gA(u)\sum_{i}a^{\dagger}_{i}

(9)

and after a little work

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{ABA}\ket{\{u\}}=\sum_{a\in occ}u_{a}\ket{\{u\}}-g\sum_{a\in occ}A(u_{a})\sum_{i}a^{\dagger}_{i}\ket{\{u\}_{a}}.

(10)

Thus we have an eigenvector provided that for each
a

\displaystyle A(u_{a})=\frac{1}{g}-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{u_{a}-\varepsilon_{i}}=0.

(11)

These are called the Bethe ansatz equations (BAE). Usually they are coupled, and must be solved for each ABA state. In this particular case, the BAE are decoupled, and hence one solution suffices
for all
the ABA eigenvectors. The BAE (
11
) have
2N
solutions, but each eigenvector only contains
2M
. To be in line with HF, we will refer to the rapidities that are present in the ground state as
occupied
and those that do not as
virtual
. The summations over
a
in equation (
10
) are of course over only the occupied rapidities. In this particular case, the BAE may be solved by diagonalizing the matrix

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{1}+g&g&\dots&g\\
g&\varepsilon_{2}+g&\dots&g\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
g&g&\dots&\varepsilon_{2N}+g\end{pmatrix}\rightarrow\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}&0&\dots&0\\
0&u_{2}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&u_{2N}\end{pmatrix}

(12)

which is substantially more stable numerically.

II.2
Scalar Products

Scalar products are computed by the form factor approach.

;
faribault:2008
;
faribault:2010
;
GB:2011
Namely to evaluate
\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{j}|\{u\}}
move
a_{j}
to the right until it destroys the vacuum. This yields a sum over terms that are evaluated as specific limits of a general scalar product formula. For arbitrary
\{u\}
and
\{v\}
we can evaluate the scalar product easily as the determinant of a
2M\times 2M
matrix

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|\{u\}}=\det_{ab\in occ}\left(\frac{A(u_{a})-A(v_{b})}{u_{a}-v_{b}}\right).

(13)

Henceforth, we take one of the sets, say
\{v\}
to be on-shell, i.e.
A(v_{b})=0
for each
b
giving

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|\{u\}}=\prod_{c\in occ}A(v_{c})\det_{ab\in occ}\left(\frac{1}{u_{a}-v_{b}}\right).

(14)

In principal this expression could be simplified as the determinant of a Cauchy matrix has a simple closed form expression, but this is not productive as we will see briefly. All the required scalar products are specific limits of (
14
). In particular, the norm is the limit when
\{u\}\rightarrow\{v\}
:

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}=\prod_{c\in occ}\frac{\partial A(v_{c})}{\partial v_{c}}

(15)

since the only surviving terms come from indeterminate forms on the diagonal of the determinant.

To evaluate the 1-electron reduced density matrix (1-RDM) we will need the structure constants

\displaystyle[a_{j},a^{\dagger}(u)]_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u-\varepsilon_{j}}

(16)

\displaystyle[a^{\dagger}_{i},a^{\dagger}(u)]_{+}

\displaystyle=0

(17)

to move
a_{j}
to the right. The result is

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{j}|\{u\}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in occ}(-1)^{a-1}[a_{j},a^{\dagger}(u_{a})]_{+}\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i}|\{u\}_{a}}

(18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in occ}\frac{\braket{\{v\}|\{u\}_{a\rightarrow i}}}{u_{a}-\varepsilon_{i}}

(19)

where
\{u\}_{a}
denotes the set
\{u\}
without
u_{a}
, and the state
\ket{\{u\}_{a\rightarrow i}}
is
\ket{\{u\}}
with
a^{\dagger}(u_{a})
replaced with
a^{\dagger}_{i}

in the correct position
which removes the sign. Now, the original operators are the residues of the ABA operators

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}_{i}=\lim_{u\rightarrow\varepsilon_{i}}(u-\varepsilon_{i})a^{\dagger}(u)

(20)

which carries through to the scalar product (
14
)

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|\{u\}_{a\rightarrow\varepsilon_{i}}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{u_{a}\rightarrow i}(u_{a}-\varepsilon_{i})\braket{\{v\}|\{u\}}

(21)

\displaystyle=-\det G^{a}_{i}\prod_{c(\neq a)\in occ}A(u_{c}).

(22)

Here the matrix
G^{a}_{i}
is the matrix from (
14
) with the

th column replaced:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{u_{a}-v_{1}}\\
\frac{1}{u_{a}-v_{2}}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{1}{u_{a}-v_{M}}\end{pmatrix}\rightarrow\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{i}-v_{1}}\\
\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{i}-v_{2}}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{i}-v_{M}}\end{pmatrix}.

(23)

Taking the limit
\{u\}\rightarrow\{v\}
and normalizing we arrive at the 1-RDM elements

\displaystyle\gamma_{ij}=\frac{\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{j}|\{v\}}}{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}}=\sum_{a\in occ}\frac{1}{(v_{a}-\varepsilon_{i})(v_{a}-\varepsilon_{j})}\frac{1}{\frac{\partial A(v_{a})}{\partial v_{a}}}.

(24)

The ABA operators represent electrons in orthogonal but not normal orbitals. The quantity

\displaystyle\frac{\partial A(v_{a})}{\partial v_{a}}=\sum_{j}\frac{1}{(v_{a}-\varepsilon_{j})^{2}}

(25)

plays the role of the (diagonal) overlap matrix.

Similarly for two electron operators,

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{l}a_{k}|\{u\}}=\sum_{a<b\in occ}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{u_{a}-\varepsilon_{k}}&\frac{1}{u_{a}-\varepsilon_{l}}\\
\frac{1}{u_{b}-\varepsilon_{k}}&\frac{1}{u_{b}-\varepsilon_{l}}\end{vmatrix}\braket{\{v\}|\{u\}_{ab\rightarrow ij}}.

(26)

and

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|\{u\}_{ab\rightarrow ij}}=\det G^{ab}_{ij}\prod_{c(\neq a,b)\in occ}A(u_{c})

(27)

leads to the 2-electron reduced density matrix (2-RDM) elements

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=\frac{\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{l}a_{k}|\{v\}}}{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}}

(28)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a<b\in occ}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v_{a}-\varepsilon_{k}}&\frac{1}{v_{a}-\varepsilon_{l}}\\
\frac{1}{v_{b}-\varepsilon_{k}}&\frac{1}{v_{b}-\varepsilon_{l}}\end{vmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v_{a}-\varepsilon_{i}}&\frac{1}{v_{a}-\varepsilon_{j}}\\
\frac{1}{v_{b}-\varepsilon_{i}}&\frac{1}{v_{b}-\varepsilon_{j}}\end{vmatrix}\frac{1}{\frac{\partial A(v_{a})}{\partial v_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v_{b})}{\partial v_{b}}}

(29)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{ik}\gamma_{jl}-\gamma_{il}\gamma_{jk}.

(30)

The 2-RDM factors into 1-RDM information as the wavefunction is factored into 1-electron wavefunctions. This extends to any order

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(N)}_{i_{1}\dots i_{N}j_{1}\dots j_{N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i_{1}}\dots a^{\dagger}_{i_{N}}a_{j_{N}}\dots a_{j_{1}}|\{v\}}}{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}}

(31)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1}<\dots a_{N}\in occ}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v_{a_{1}}-\varepsilon_{j_{1}}}&\dots&\frac{1}{v_{a_{1}}-\varepsilon_{j_{N}}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{1}{v_{a_{N}}-\varepsilon_{j_{1}}}&\dots&\frac{1}{v_{a_{N}}-\varepsilon_{j_{N}}}\end{vmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v_{a_{1}}-\varepsilon_{i_{1}}}&\dots&\frac{1}{v_{a_{1}}-\varepsilon_{i_{N}}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{1}{v_{a_{N}}-\varepsilon_{i_{1}}}&\dots&\frac{1}{v_{a_{N}}-\varepsilon_{i_{N}}}\end{vmatrix}\frac{1}{\frac{\partial A(v_{a_{1}})}{\partial v_{a_{1}}}\dots{\frac{\partial A(v_{a_{N}})}{\partial v_{a_{N}}}}}

(32)

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}\gamma_{i_{1}j_{1}}&\dots&\gamma_{i_{1}j_{N}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\gamma_{i_{N}j_{1}}&\dots&\gamma_{i_{N}j_{N}}\end{vmatrix}.

(33)

Transition density matrix (TDM) elements are computed with the same approach and the results are even simpler. For single excitations, i.e. replacing one
v_{a}
with
v_{b}
, denoted set-wise as
\{v\}^{b}_{a}
, there is only one non-zero contribution. All others are proportional to
A(v_{b})
without a vanishing denominator and hence are identically zero. The non-zero contributions from single excitations are

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{j}|\{v\}^{b}_{a}}=\frac{1}{(v_{b}-\varepsilon_{j})(v_{a}-\varepsilon_{i})}\prod_{c(\neq a)\in occ}\frac{\partial A(v_{c})}{\partial v_{c}}

(34)

and

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{l}a_{k}|\{v\}^{b}_{a}}=\sum_{c(\neq a)\in occ}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v_{b}-\varepsilon_{k}}&\frac{1}{v_{b}-\varepsilon_{l}}\\
\frac{1}{v_{c}-\varepsilon_{k}}&\frac{1}{v_{c}-\varepsilon_{l}}\end{vmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v_{a}-\varepsilon_{k}}&\frac{1}{v_{a}-\varepsilon_{l}}\\
\frac{1}{v_{c}-\varepsilon_{k}}&\frac{1}{v_{c}-\varepsilon_{l}}\end{vmatrix}\prod_{d(\neq a,c)\in occ}\frac{\partial A(v_{d})}{\partial v_{d}}.

(35)

Double excitations have the only non-zero contributions

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{l}a_{k}|\{v\}^{cd}_{ab}}=\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v_{c}-\varepsilon_{k}}&\frac{1}{v_{c}-\varepsilon_{l}}\\
\frac{1}{v_{d}-\varepsilon_{k}}&\frac{1}{v_{d}-\varepsilon_{l}}\end{vmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v_{a}-\varepsilon_{i}}&\frac{1}{v_{a}-\varepsilon_{j}}\\
\frac{1}{v_{b}-\varepsilon_{i}}&\frac{1}{v_{b}-\varepsilon_{j}}\end{vmatrix}\prod_{c(\neq a,b)\in occ}\frac{\partial A(v_{c})}{\partial v_{c}}.

(36)

II.3
Unrestricted Orbitals

To arrive at restricted results, we work out the result in unrestricted orbitals before taking the limit where the
\alpha
and
\beta
elements are identical. The elements can be separated into two sets. It’s equivalent to having two separate ABA set-ups:

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\alpha}_{ABA}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{i}+g^{\alpha}\sum_{ij}a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{j}

(37)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\beta}_{ABA}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\varepsilon^{\beta}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{i}+g^{\beta}\sum_{ij}a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{j}

(38)

with solutions the roots of the equations:

\displaystyle A(v^{\alpha}):=\frac{1}{g^{\alpha}}-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{v^{\alpha}-\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{i}}

\displaystyle=0

(39)

\displaystyle A(v^{\beta}):=\frac{1}{g^{\beta}}-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{v^{\beta}-\varepsilon^{\beta}_{i}}

\displaystyle=0.

(40)

The scalar products simplify as the matrix entering in (
14
) becomes block diagonal. Specifically, it being understood that
\{v\}=\{v^{\alpha}\}\cup\{v^{\beta}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\hat{H}^{\alpha}_{ABA}+\hat{H}^{\beta}_{ABA}\right)\ket{\{v\}}=\left(\sum_{a\in occ}v^{\alpha}_{a}+v^{\beta}_{a}\right)\ket{\{v\}}

(41)

and the scalar product with an arbitrary ABA vector
\ket{\{u\}}
is

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|\{u\}}\prod_{c\in occ}A(u^{\alpha}_{c})A(u^{\beta}_{c})\det_{ab\in occ}\left(\frac{1}{u^{\alpha}_{a}-v^{\alpha}_{b}}\right)\det_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\in occ}\left(\frac{1}{u^{\beta}_{a^{\prime}}-v^{\beta}_{b^{\prime}}}\right).

(42)

The norm of the on-shell state becomes

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}=\prod_{a\in occ}\frac{\partial A(v^{\alpha}_{a})}{\partial v^{\alpha}_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\beta}_{a})}{\partial v^{\beta}_{a}}.

(43)

The 1-RDM elements are:

\displaystyle\gamma^{\sigma}_{ij}=\frac{\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}a_{j\sigma}|\{v\}}}{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}}=\sum_{a\in occ}\frac{1}{(v^{\sigma}_{a}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{i})(v^{\sigma}_{a}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{j})}\frac{1}{\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{a})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{a}}}

(44)

and the 2-RDM elements reduce to 1-RDM elements

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\sigma\tau}_{ijkl}=\frac{\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}a^{\dagger}_{j\tau}a_{l\tau}a_{k\tau}|\{v\}}}{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}}=\gamma^{\sigma}_{ik}\gamma^{\tau}_{jl}-\delta_{\sigma\tau}\gamma^{\sigma}_{il}\gamma^{\sigma}_{jk}.

(45)

As in the previous section, excited states are labelled by which occupieds are removed, and which virtuals are added, i.e. the state
\ket{\{v\}^{p\sigma}_{a\sigma}}
is the state with
a\sigma
occupied orbital replaced by the
p\sigma
virtual orbital.

We will require transition density matrix elements, for which
\sigma
denotes either spin, while
\bar{\sigma}
denotes the opposite spin of
\sigma
. They are obtained in the same manner as for the spin-orbital basis:

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}a_{j\sigma}|\{v\}^{p\sigma}_{a\sigma}}=\frac{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}}{(v^{\sigma}_{p}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{j})(v^{\sigma}_{a}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{i})\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{a})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{a}}}

(46)

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}a^{\dagger}_{j\sigma}a_{l\sigma}a_{k\sigma}|\{v\}^{p\sigma}_{a\sigma}}=\sum_{c(\neq a)\in occ}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{p}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{k}}&\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{p}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{l}}\\
\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{c}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{k}}&\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{c}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{l}}\end{vmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{a}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{i}}&\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{a}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{j}}\\
\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{c}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{i}}&\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{c}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{j}}\end{vmatrix}\frac{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}}{\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{a})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{c})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{c}}}.

(47)

Strictly speaking, the summation excludes the term
c=a
, but this term would give zero contribution so will be included to simplify later summations. The last TDM element for single excitations is

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}a^{\dagger}_{j\bar{\sigma}}a_{l\bar{\sigma}}a_{k\sigma}|\{v\}^{p\sigma}_{a\sigma}}=\frac{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}}{(v^{\sigma}_{p}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{k})(v^{\sigma}_{a}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{i})\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{a})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{a}}}\gamma^{\bar{\sigma}}_{jl}.

(48)

Double excitations only couple with the ground state through two-electron operators:

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}a^{\dagger}_{j\sigma}a_{l\sigma}a_{k\sigma}|\{v\}^{p\sigma q\sigma}_{a\sigma b\sigma}}=\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{p}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{k}}&\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{p}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{l}}\\
\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{q}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{k}}&\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{q}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{l}}\end{vmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{a}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{i}}&\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{a}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{j}}\\
\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{b}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{i}}&\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{b}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{j}}\end{vmatrix}\frac{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}}{\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{a})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{b})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{b}}}

(49)

and

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}a^{\dagger}_{j\bar{\sigma}}a_{l\bar{\sigma}}a_{k\sigma}|\{v\}^{p\sigma q\bar{\sigma}}_{a\sigma b\bar{\sigma}}}=\frac{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}}{(v^{\sigma}_{a}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{i})(v^{\bar{\sigma}}_{b}-\varepsilon^{\bar{\sigma}}_{j})(v^{\sigma}_{p}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{k})(v^{\bar{\sigma}}_{q}-\varepsilon^{\bar{\sigma}}_{l})\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{a})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\bar{\sigma}}_{b})}{\partial v^{\bar{\sigma}}_{b}}}.

(50)

All other TDM elements either vanish or will not connect through the Hamiltonian.

III
PT expressions: Primitive basis

We wish to solve the Coulomb Hamiltonian for molecules

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{C}=\sum_{ij}h_{ij}\sum_{\sigma}a^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}a_{\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ijkl}V_{ijkl}\sum_{\sigma\tau}a^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}a^{\dagger}_{j\tau}a_{l\tau}a_{k\sigma}

(51)

where the 1- and 2-electron integrals are expressed in a basis
\{\phi\}

\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle=\int d\mathbf{r}\phi^{*}_{i}(\mathbf{r})\left(-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}-\sum_{I}\frac{Z_{I}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{I}|}\right)\phi_{j}(\mathbf{r})

(52)

\displaystyle V_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}d\mathbf{r}_{2}\frac{\phi^{*}_{i}(\mathbf{r}_{1})\phi^{*}_{j}(\mathbf{r}_{2})\phi_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{1})\phi_{l}(\mathbf{r}_{2})}{|\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2}|}.

(53)

Using the ABA for electrons as a wavefunction ansatz, and expanding

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{C}\ket{\Psi_{k}}=E_{k}\ket{\Psi_{k}}

(54)

in Rayleigh-Schrödinger perturbation theory (RSPT), we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{C}

\displaystyle=\hat{H}_{0}+\lambda\hat{H}_{1}

(55)

\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle=E^{(0)}_{k}+\lambda E^{(1)}_{k}+\lambda^{2}E^{(2)}_{k}+\dots

(56)

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{k}}

\displaystyle=\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{k}}+\lambda\ket{\Psi^{(1)}_{k}}+\lambda^{2}\ket{\Psi^{(2)}_{k}}+\dots.

(57)

Collecting powers of
\lambda
, we highlight the zeroth order problem

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{k}}=E^{(0)}_{k}\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{k}}

(58)

which is identically the problem (
41
). The RSPT first-order correction to the wavefunctions are

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi^{(1)}_{k}}=\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{l}|\hat{H}_{1}|\Psi^{(0)}_{k}}}{E^{(0)}_{k}-E^{(0)}_{l}}\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{l}}

(59)

and since our first order Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{1}=\hat{H}_{C}-\left(\hat{H}^{\alpha}_{ABA}+\hat{H}^{\beta}_{ABA}\right)

(60)

and for
k\neq l
,

\displaystyle\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{l}|\left(\hat{H}^{\alpha}_{ABA}+\hat{H}^{\beta}_{ABA}\right)|\Psi^{(0)}_{k}}=0

(61)

we can instead write the first order correction as

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi^{(1)}_{k}}=\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{l}|\hat{H}_{C}|\Psi^{(0)}_{k}}}{E^{(0)}_{k}-E^{(0)}_{l}}\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{l}}.

(62)

The 2nd order energy is then

\displaystyle E^{(2)}_{k}=\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{|\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{l}|\hat{H}_{C}|\Psi^{(0)}_{k}}|^{2}}{E^{(0)}_{k}-E^{(0)}_{l}}.

(63)

The ABA excited states are simply ABA states with different “occupied” rapidities since the BAE (
11
) do not couple rapidities. Like the case for Hartree-Fock, the only excited states that couple with the ground state through the Hamiltonian are single and double excitations.

The parameters defining the ABA in an unrestricted basis are
\{\varepsilon^{\alpha}\},\{\varepsilon^{\beta}\},g^{\alpha},g^{\beta}
, and since the 2-RDM factors into the 1-RDM contributions
\gamma^{\alpha}_{ij},\gamma^{\beta}_{ij}
, as shown previously
carrier:2020
the variational ground state energy expression is

\displaystyle E^{ABA}_{U}

\displaystyle=E^{(0)}_{0}+E^{(1)}_{0}

(64)

\displaystyle=\min_{\{\varepsilon^{\alpha}\},\{\varepsilon^{\beta}\},g^{\alpha},g^{\beta}}\sum_{ij}h_{ij}\left(\gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}+\gamma^{\beta}_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ijkl}V_{ijkl}\left(\gamma^{\alpha}_{ik}\gamma^{\alpha}_{jl}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{il}\gamma^{\alpha}_{jk}+\gamma^{\alpha}_{ik}\gamma^{\beta}_{jl}+\gamma^{\beta}_{ij}\gamma^{\alpha}_{kl}+\gamma^{\beta}_{ik}\gamma^{\beta}_{jl}-\gamma^{\beta}_{il}\gamma^{\beta}_{jk}\right).

(65)

To arrive at a restricted expression, we take the
\alpha
and
\beta
parameters to be identical. This amounts to solving for one ABA Hamiltonian, and using states that use each rapidity twice (once for each spin projection). The resulting energy expression is

\displaystyle E^{ABA}_{R}

\displaystyle=\min_{\{\varepsilon\},g}2\sum_{ij}h_{ij}\gamma_{ij}+\sum_{ijkl}V_{ijkl}(2\gamma_{ik}\gamma_{jl}-\gamma_{il}\gamma_{jk})

(66)

with the 1-RDM elements:

\displaystyle\gamma_{ij}=\sum_{a\in occ}\frac{1}{(v_{a}-\varepsilon_{i})(v_{a}-\varepsilon_{j})}\frac{1}{\frac{\partial A(v_{a})}{\partial v_{a}}}.

(67)

We will now work out the 2nd-order energy correction. Our strategy is to work out the unrestricted expression first, then take the limit that the two spin projections are identical to get the restricted version.

The ABA ground state couples with single and double excitations through the Hamiltonian (
51
), so (
63
) becomes

\displaystyle E^{(2)}_{U,0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in occ\\
p\in virt\end{subarray}}\sum_{\sigma}\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{a}-v^{\sigma}_{p}}\frac{|\braket{\{v\}|\hat{H}_{C}|\{v\}^{p\sigma}_{a\sigma}}|^{2}}{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}\braket{\{v\}^{p\sigma}_{a\sigma}|\{v\}^{p\sigma}_{a\sigma}}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a<b\in occ\\
p<q\in virt\end{subarray}}\sum_{\sigma}\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{a}+v^{\sigma}_{b}-v^{\sigma}_{p}-v^{\sigma}_{q}}\frac{|\braket{\{v\}|\hat{H}_{C}|\{v\}^{p\sigma q\sigma}_{a\sigma b\sigma}}|^{2}}{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}\braket{\{v\}^{p\sigma q\sigma}_{a\sigma b\sigma}|\{v\}^{p\sigma q\sigma}_{a\sigma b\sigma}}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ab\in occ\\
pq\in virt\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{v^{\alpha}_{a}+v^{\beta}_{b}-v^{\alpha}_{p}-v^{\beta}_{q}}\frac{|\braket{\{v\}|\hat{H}_{C}|\{v\}^{p\alpha q\beta}_{a\alpha b\beta}}|^{2}}{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}\braket{\{v\}^{p\alpha q\beta}_{a\alpha b\beta}|\{v\}^{p\alpha q\beta}_{a\alpha b\beta}}}.

(68)

With the TDM elements computed in section (
II.3
), this becomes

\displaystyle E^{(2)}_{U,0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in occ\\
p\in virt\end{subarray}}\sum_{\sigma}\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{a}-v^{\sigma}_{p}}\frac{(D^{p\sigma}_{a\sigma})^{2}}{\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{a})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{p})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{p}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a<b\in occ\\
p<q\in virt\end{subarray}}\sum_{\sigma}\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{a}+v^{\sigma}_{b}-v^{\sigma}_{p}-v^{\sigma}_{q}}\frac{(W^{\sigma\sigma}_{abpq}-W^{\sigma\sigma}_{abqp})^{2}}{\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{a})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{b})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{b}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{p})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{q})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{q}}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ab\in occ\\
pq\in virt\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{v^{\alpha}_{a}+v^{\beta}_{b}-v^{\alpha}_{p}-v^{\beta}_{q}}\frac{(W^{\alpha\beta}_{abpq})^{2}}{\frac{\partial A(v^{\alpha}_{a})}{\partial v^{\alpha}_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\beta}_{b})}{\partial v^{\beta}_{b}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\alpha}_{p})}{\partial v^{\alpha}_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\beta}_{q})}{\partial v^{\beta}_{q}}}

(69)

where the coupling matrix elements for the singles are

\displaystyle D^{p\sigma}_{a\sigma}=t^{\sigma}_{ap}+\sum_{c\in occ}\left(\frac{W^{\sigma\sigma}_{acpc}-W^{\sigma\sigma}_{accp}}{\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{c})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{c}}}+\frac{W^{\sigma\bar{\sigma}}_{acpc}}{\frac{\partial A(v^{\bar{\sigma}}_{c})}{v^{\bar{\sigma}}_{c}}}\right)

(70)

and the integrals have been transformed directly:

\displaystyle t^{\sigma}_{ap}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}\frac{h_{ij}}{(v^{\sigma}_{a}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{i})(v^{\sigma}_{p}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{j})}

(71)

\displaystyle W^{\sigma\tau}_{abpq}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijkl}\frac{V_{ijkl}}{(v^{\sigma}_{a}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{i})(v^{\tau}_{v}-\varepsilon^{\tau}_{j})(v^{\sigma}_{p}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{k})(v^{\tau}_{q}-\varepsilon^{\tau}_{l})}.

(72)

The restricted expression is obtained in the limit that the two sets of parameters are identical. Specifically,

\displaystyle E^{(2)}_{R,0}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{a\in occ}\sum_{p\in virt}\frac{1}{v_{a}-v_{p}}\frac{(D^{p}_{a})^{2}}{\frac{\partial A(v_{a})}{\partial v_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v_{p})}{\partial v_{p}}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{ab\in occ}\sum_{pq\in virt}\frac{1}{v_{a}+v_{b}-v_{p}-v_{q}}\frac{W_{abpq}(2W_{abpq}-W_{abqp})}{\frac{\partial A(v_{a})}{\partial v_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v_{b})}{\partial v_{b}}\frac{\partial A(v_{p})}{\partial v_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v_{q})}{\partial v_{q}}}

(73)

where the matrix element for the singles contribution is

\displaystyle D^{p}_{a}=t_{ap}+\sum_{b\in occ}\frac{2W_{abpb}-W_{abbp}}{\frac{\partial A(v_{b})}{\partial v_{b}}}

(74)

and the integrals are

\displaystyle t_{ap}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}\frac{h_{ij}}{(v_{a}-\varepsilon_{i})(v_{p}-\varepsilon_{j})}

(75)

\displaystyle W_{abpq}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijkl}\frac{V_{ijkl}}{(v_{a}-\varepsilon_{i})(v_{b}-\varepsilon_{j})(v_{p}-\varepsilon_{k})(v_{q}-\varepsilon_{l})}.

(76)

The expression (
III
) is identical to the restricted MP2 expression
MEST
except that the orbitals are not normalized and there are non-zero contributions from single excitations.

IV
PT expressions: ABA basis

Expression (
III
) was computed using the Hamiltonian (
51
) in the primitive basis, using transition density matrix elements computed with the ABA. The integrals
h_{ij}
and
V_{ijkl}
end up being transformed directly. To be certain that (
III
) is correct, we can compute the 2nd order perturbation in another manner, by transforming (
51
) to the basis of ABA quasiparticles (the equivalent of molecular orbitals) and using particle-hole excitations to generate the relevant excited states. Notice that with the
complete
set of solutions to the BAE we get a linear transformation of the creation operators:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a^{\dagger}(v^{\sigma}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
a^{\dagger}(v^{\sigma}_{N})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{1}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{1}}&\dots&\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{1}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{N}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{N}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{1}}&\dots&\frac{1}{v^{\sigma}_{N}-\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{N}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\dagger}_{1\sigma}\\
\vdots\\
a^{\dagger}_{N\sigma}\end{pmatrix}

(77)

or

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}(\textbf{v}^{\sigma})=C^{\sigma}\textbf{a}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}.

(78)

The matrix
C
has a known explicit inverse,

\displaystyle C^{-1}_{Ii}=(v_{I}-\varepsilon_{i})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\neq I\\
k\neq i\end{subarray}}\frac{(\varepsilon_{i}-v_{K})(v_{I}-\varepsilon_{k})}{(v_{I}-v_{K})(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{k})}

(79)

but this expression is not optimal numerically as floating point precision will be lost rather quickly. As a result,
C
will be inverted numerically. Upper case letters have been used to label parameters in the ABA basis. Inverting (
78
) gives

\displaystyle\textbf{a}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}=(C^{\sigma})^{-1}a^{\dagger}(\textbf{v}^{\sigma}),

(80)

so that the Coulomb Hamiltonian can then be written in the ABA parameters

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{C}=\sum_{IJ}\sum_{\sigma}\tilde{h}^{\sigma}_{IJ}a^{\dagger}(v^{\sigma}_{I})a(v^{\sigma}_{J})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{IJKL}\sum_{\sigma\tau}\tilde{V}^{\sigma\tau}_{IJKL}a^{\dagger}(v^{\sigma}_{I})a^{\dagger}(v^{\tau}_{J})a(v^{\tau}_{L})a(v^{\sigma}_{K})

(81)

where the integrals have been transformed

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{\sigma}_{IJ}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}h_{ij}(C^{\sigma}_{Ii})^{-1}(C^{\sigma}_{Jj})^{-1}

(82)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}^{\sigma\tau}_{IJKL}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijkl}V_{ijkl}(C^{\sigma}_{Ii})^{-1}(C^{\tau}_{Jj})^{-1}(C^{\sigma}_{Kk})^{-1}(C^{\tau}_{Ll})^{-1}.

(83)

Excited ABA states are generated by acting on the ground state with the ABA operators. Specifically the single excitation from the
a
th occupied orbital with spin
\mu
to the
p
th virtual with spin
\nu
is written

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}(v^{\nu}_{p})a(v^{\mu}_{a})\ket{\{v\}}=\frac{\partial A(v^{\mu}_{a})}{\partial v^{\mu}_{a}}\ket{\{v\}^{p\nu}_{a\mu}}

(84)

and likewise double excitations are generated

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}(v^{\kappa}_{p})a^{\dagger}(v^{\eta}_{q})a(v^{\nu}_{b})a(v^{\mu}_{a})\ket{\{v\}}=\frac{\partial A(v^{\mu}_{a})}{\partial v^{\mu}_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\nu}_{b})}{\partial v^{\nu}_{b}}\ket{\{v\}^{p\kappa q\eta}_{a\mu b\nu}}.

(85)

Again, the 2nd order energy correction will have contributions from singles and doubles

\displaystyle E^{(2)}_{U,0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in occ\\
p\in virt\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mu\nu}\frac{1}{v^{\mu}_{a}-v^{\nu}_{p}}\frac{|\braket{\{v\}|\hat{H}_{C}|\{v\}^{p\nu}_{a\mu}}|^{2}}{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}\braket{\{v\}^{p\nu}_{a\mu}|\{v\}^{p\nu}_{a\mu}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ab\in occ\\
pq\in virt\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mu\nu\kappa\eta}\frac{1}{v^{\mu}_{a}+v^{\nu}_{b}-v^{\kappa}_{p}-v^{\eta}_{q}}\frac{|\braket{\{v\}|\hat{H}_{C}|\{v\}^{p\kappa q\eta}_{a\mu b\nu}}|^{2}}{\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}\braket{\{v\}^{p\kappa q\eta}_{a\mu b\nu}|\{v\}^{p\kappa q\eta}_{a\mu b\nu}}}

(86)

for which we will have to work out TDM elements. This may be done by Wick’s theorem as the ABA operators yield orbitals that are orthogonal but not normal. The coupling between the ABA ground state and single excitations has a contribution from 1-electron operators:

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}(v^{\sigma}_{I})a(v^{\sigma}_{J})|\{v\}^{p\nu}_{a\mu}}=\delta_{aI}\delta_{pJ}\delta_{\nu\sigma}\delta_{\mu\sigma}\frac{\partial A(v^{\nu}_{p})}{\partial v^{\nu}_{p}}\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}

(87)

and a more complicated contribution from 2-electron operators

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}(v^{\sigma}_{I})a^{\dagger}(v^{\tau}_{J})a(v^{\tau}_{L})a(v^{\sigma}_{K})|\{v\}^{p\nu}_{a\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial A(v^{\nu}_{p})}{\partial v^{\nu}_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\tau}_{L})}{\partial v^{\tau}_{L}}\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}\delta_{pK}\delta_{JL}\delta_{aI}\delta_{\nu\sigma}\delta_{\mu\sigma}\sum_{c\in occ}\delta_{cJ}

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial A(v^{\nu}_{p})}{\partial v^{\nu}_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{K})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{K}}\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}\delta_{pL}\delta_{IK}\delta_{aJ}\delta_{\nu\tau}\delta_{\mu\tau}\sum_{c\in occ}\delta_{cI}

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial A(v^{\nu}_{p})}{\partial v^{\nu}_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{L})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{L}}\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}\delta_{pK}\delta_{IL}\delta_{aJ}\delta_{\nu\sigma}\delta_{\mu\tau}\delta_{\sigma\tau}\sum_{c\in occ}\delta_{cI}

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial A(v^{\nu}_{p})}{\partial v^{\nu}_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{K})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{K}}\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}\delta_{pL}\delta_{JK}\delta_{aI}\delta_{\nu\tau}\delta_{\mu\sigma}\delta_{\sigma\tau}\sum_{c\in occ}\delta_{cJ}.

(88)

The only non-vanishing coupling between the ABA ground state and double excitations is

\displaystyle\braket{\{v\}|a^{\dagger}(v^{\sigma}_{I})a^{\dagger}(v^{\tau}_{J})a(v^{\tau}_{L})a(v^{\sigma}_{K})|\{v\}^{p\kappa q\eta}_{a\mu b\nu}}=\frac{\partial A(v^{\kappa}_{p})}{\partial v^{\kappa}_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\eta}_{q})}{\partial v^{\eta}_{q}}\braket{\{v\}|\{v\}}\times

\displaystyle\times(\delta_{aI}\delta_{bJ}\delta_{\mu\sigma}\delta_{\nu\tau}-\delta_{aJ}\delta_{bI}\delta_{\mu\tau}\delta_{\nu\sigma})(\delta_{pK}\delta_{qL}\delta_{\kappa\sigma}\delta_{\eta\tau}-\delta_{pL}\delta_{qK}\delta_{\kappa\tau}\delta_{\eta\sigma}).

(89)

The final expression for the unrestricted 2nd order correction is thus

\displaystyle E^{(2)}_{U,0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in occ\\
p\in virt\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mu}\frac{1}{v^{\mu}_{a}-v^{\mu}_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\mu}_{p})}{\partial v^{\mu}_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\mu}_{a})}{\partial v^{\mu}_{a}}(\tilde{D}^{p\mu}_{a\mu})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ab\in occ\\
pq\in virt\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mu\nu}\frac{\tilde{V}^{\mu\nu}_{pqab}\tilde{V}^{\mu\nu}_{pqab}}{v^{\mu}_{a}+v^{\nu}_{b}-v^{\mu}_{p}-v^{\nu}_{q}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\mu}_{p})}{\partial v^{\mu}_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\nu}_{q})}{\partial v^{\nu}_{q}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\mu}_{a})}{\partial v^{\mu}_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\nu}_{b})}{\partial v^{\nu}_{b}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ab\in occ\\
pq\in virt\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mu}\frac{\tilde{V}^{\mu\mu}_{pqab}\tilde{V}^{\mu\mu}_{pqba}}{v^{\mu}_{a}+v^{\mu}_{b}-v^{\mu}_{p}-v^{\mu}_{q}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\mu}_{p})}{\partial v^{\mu}_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\mu}_{q})}{\partial v^{\mu}_{q}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\mu}_{a})}{\partial v^{\mu}_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v^{\mu}_{b})}{\partial v^{\mu}_{b}}

(90)

where the singles contribution is

\displaystyle\tilde{D}^{p\mu}_{a\mu}=\tilde{h}^{\mu}_{pa}+\sum_{c\in occ}\left(\sum_{\sigma}\tilde{V}^{\sigma\mu}_{cpca}\frac{\partial A(v^{\sigma}_{c})}{\partial v^{\sigma}_{c}}-\tilde{V}^{\mu\mu}_{cpac}\frac{\partial A(v^{\mu}_{c})}{\partial v^{\mu}_{c}}\right).

(91)

Taking the restricted limit, we arrive at

\displaystyle E^{(2)}_{R,0}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in occ\\
p\in virt\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{v_{a}-v_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v_{p})}{\partial v_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v_{a})}{\partial v_{a}}\left(\tilde{h}_{pa}+\sum_{c\in occ}(2\tilde{V}_{cpca}-\tilde{V}_{cpac})\frac{\partial A(v_{c})}{\partial v_{c}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ab\in occ\\
pq\in virt\end{subarray}}\frac{(2\tilde{V}_{pqab}-\tilde{V}_{pqba})\tilde{V}_{pqab}}{v_{a}+v_{b}-v_{p}-v_{q}}\frac{\partial A(v_{p})}{\partial v_{p}}\frac{\partial A(v_{q})}{\partial v_{q}}\frac{\partial A(v_{a})}{\partial v_{a}}\frac{\partial A(v_{b})}{\partial v_{b}}.

(92)

Equation (
IV
) is quite similar to (
III
), except that the integrals have been transformed in the opposite manner.

V
Discussion

We derived two expressions, (
III
) and (
IV
), for the restricted 2nd order RSPT energy correction. Both were implemented, and found to give numerically the same results. Unfortunately these results were ambiguous. The ABA parameters
\{\varepsilon\},g
may be changed without affecting the mean-field energy (
66
), though they have a large effect on the 2nd-order energy. In addition, unlike the case for MP2, the singles couple to the ground state through
\hat{H}_{C}
. To study these effects we tried minimizing the energy functional

\displaystyle F=E^{ABA}_{R}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in occ\\
p\in virt\end{subarray}}|\braket{\{v\}|\hat{H}_{C}|\{v\}^{p}_{a}}|^{2}

(93)

which leads to numerically the same mean-field energy, and the singles do vanish.
But so do the doubles
and thus the 2nd order RSPT correction is numerically zero. The
N+1
variables
\{\varepsilon\},g
are enough to reproduce the ground state RHF energy as all that is required is an idempotent 1-RDM in the correct basis. However, they are not enough to reproduce the entire RHF spectrum and hence do not reproduce the MP2 perturbative correction. To fix this, one could employ a Hylleraas functional
hylleraas
to solve for the perturbative correction variationally. Solving the equations explicitly on paper would of course lead to the same expressions (
III
) and (
IV
). Instead one should consider variationally solving for the coefficients
\{c_{ki}\}

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi^{(1)}_{k}}=\sum_{i}c_{ki}\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{i}}

(94)

but this amounts to the same number of parameters as an orbital optimization. Including an orbital optimization would necessarily reproduce MP2 as the RHF solution is defined by the optimal orbitals. Similarly, we could solve the CI singles problem for ABA states as a reference wavefunction, but again this amounts to employing
N^{2}
parameters. Therefore we do not consider it productive to demonstrate numerically.

VI
Conclusion

Perturbative corrections for the ABA for individual electrons were calculated. The first expression (
III
) was computed in the primitive basis using TDM elements obtained from the ABA. The second expression (
IV
) was computed in the ABA basis using particle-hole excitations. Both expressions are in close analogy with MP2 corrections to RHF. Numerically computing these corrections lead to ambiguous results that could, in principle, be corrected with an orbital optimization or a CI singles solution. Going forward to pairs of electrons, we understand that an orbital optimization is required along with the ABA wavefunction. Care will be required when computing perturbative corrections.

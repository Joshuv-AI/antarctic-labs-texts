Strengthened chain theorems for different versions of 4-connectivity
Guoli Ding, Chengfu Qin

Abstract
The chain theorem of Tutte states that every 3-connected graph can be constructed from a wheel W n W_{n} by repeatedly adding edges and splitting vertices. It is not difficult to prove the following strengthening of this theorem: every non-wheel 3-connected graph can be constructed from W 4 W_{4} by repeatedly adding edges and splitting vertices. In this paper we similarly strengthen several chain theorems for various versions of 4-connectivity.

Introduction

If a graph
G
contains a minor
H
then by a
(G,H)
-chain
we mean a sequence
G_{0},G_{1},...,G_{t}
of graphs such that
G_{0}=G
,
G_{t}=H
, and each
G_{i}

(1\leq i\leq t)
is a minor of
G_{i-1}
. If
\cal H
is a class of graphs then by a
(G,\cal H)
-chain
we mean a
(G,H)
-chain for some
H\in\cal H
. A result that asserts the existence of a chain of certain type is often called a
chain theorem
. Probably the best known chain theorem is the following result of Tutte. Let
\mathcal{W}=\{W_{n}:n\geq 3\}
, where
W_{n}
is the wheel on
n+1
vertices. Let
|G|
and
\|G\|
denote the number of vertices and edges, respectively, of a graph
G
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

For every
3
-connected graph
G
there exists a
(G,\cal W)
-chain
G_{0},G_{1},...,G_{t}
of
3
-connected graphs such that
\|G_{i}\|=\|G_{i-1}\|-1
for all
i=1,...,t
.

This theorem is a very powerful induction tool for analyzing 3-connected graphs since it says that every 3-connected graph can be reduced to a wheel, by reducing only one edge at a time, while maintaining 3-connectivity. If we reverse this reduction process we see that every 3-connected graph can be constructed from a wheel by repeatedly performing “undeletion” and “uncontraction” (note that both operations preserve 3-connectivity). When such a chain theorem is used to generate all 3-connected graphs, it is desirable to limit the number of starting graphs (wheels). This in fact can be done. It is not difficult to show that every non-wheel 3-connected graph can be constructed using the same operations starting from
W_{4}
, instead of a general unknown wheel. For completeness we include a proof of this result at the end of the paper. From the algorithmic point of view, this strengthened chain theorem is much more efficient since, in order to generate all 3-connected graphs, there is no need for us to consider extensions of all wheels, it is enough for us to only consider extensions of a single wheel
W_{4}
.

Currently, there are no known chain theorems for 5- or higher connectivity. However, there are several results for different versions of 4-connectivity. The purpose of this paper is to obtain similar strengthenings for three chain theorems, respectively, for 4-connectivity, weakly 4-connectivity, and quasi 4-connectivity. In some cases we also refine the involved operations.

In the next three sections we discuss these three connectivities separately. Our main results are Theorem
2.3
, Theorem
3.2
, and Theorem
4.2
. Before closing this section we introduce a few definitions. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. For any

, let

: there exists

. Suppose

. We will write

for

. As usual,

is the

A

On 4-connected graphs

Let

(

]

This chain theorem has been strengthened as follows.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

For every
4
-connected graph

This result implies that every 4-connected graph

must contain a 4-connected minor on

) between the two hubs. Note that

, and

is planar while every

is nonplanar. Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{n},B_{n}^{+}:n\geq 4\}
. Then every

is 4-connected. Finally, let

be the operation illustrated in Figure

, where

, and

Operation

Theorem 2.3
.

For every
4
-connected graph
G\not\in\cal B\cup C\cup L
there exists a
(G,\{B_{4}^{+},B_{5}\})
-chain
G_{0},G_{1},...,G_{t}
of

-connected graphs such that, for each

, either
\|G_{i}\|=\|G_{i-1}\|-1
or
G_{i}=O_{1}(G_{i-1})
. In addition, if

is nonplanar then
G_{t}=B_{4}^{+}
.

The following is a useful lemma, which says that “splitting” a vertex in a
k
-connected graph also results in a
k
-connected graph.
This lemma suggests that Theorem
2.3
can be formulated in a slightly stronger form. Together with this lemma, Theorem
2.3
implies that a graph

is 4-connected if and only if there exists a
(G,\{B_{4}^{+},B_{5}\})
-chain

such that
\delta(G_{i})\geq 4
(

) and either
\|G_{i}\|=\|G_{i-1}\|-1
or
G_{i}=O_{1}(G_{i-1})
(

). This formulation is very useful for generating 4-connected graphs since it ensures that graphs generated according to the theorem are precisely 4-connected graphs that are not contained in
\cal B\cup C\cup L
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose a simple graph
G
has an edge
xy
such that
\min\{d_{G}(x),d_{G}(y)\}\geq k
and
si(G/xy)
is
k
-connected. Then
G
is also
k
-connected.

Proof. Suppose on the contrary that
G
is not
k
-connected. Then
G
has a
k^{\prime}
-separation
(G_{1},G_{2})
with
k^{\prime}<k
. Assume without loss of generality that
xy\in G_{1}
. If
G_{1}\cap G_{2}
contains both
x
and
y
then
G/xy
admits a
(k^{\prime}-1)
-cut, contradicting the
k
-connectivity of
si(G/xy)
. Hence, we may assume
x\in G_{1}\backslash G_{2}
. If
G_{1}\backslash G_{2}
contains a vertex different from
x
then
(G_{1}/xy,G_{2})
is a
k^{\prime}
-separation of

, again contradicting the
k
-connectivity of

. Consequently,

is the only vertex of
G_{1}\backslash G_{2}
, which is impossible as

. This contradiction proves the lemma. ∎

We also need two other small technical lemmas for proving Theorem

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

be a
k
-connected graph with a triangle

. If

is

-connected but

is not, then

Proof. First, since

is
k
-connected, we must have
d_{G}(z)=d_{si(G/xy)}(z)+1\geq k+1
. Next, suppose on the contrary that
d_{G}(x)>k
. Let us consider a
k^{\prime}
-separation
(G_{1},G_{2})
of

with

. Without loss of generality, let

. Then

and

, because otherwise

would induce a

-separation of

, which violates the

-connectivity of

. As a result,

. Since

,

contains a vertex different from

. It follows that
(G_{1},G_{2})
induces a
k^{\prime}
-separation of

, which violates the

-connectivity of

. This contradiction proves the lemma. ∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
and

be
k
-connected. If

is
k
-connected.

Proof. Suppose otherwise. Then
G
has a
k
-separation
(G_{1},G_{2})
with

. Since
si(G/xy)
is
k
-connected, we have

. By symmetry, let

. Then
x
is not adjacent to any vertex of

, which implies that

is a
(k-1)
-cut of

, a contradiction. ∎

2.3
. We consider the following two assertions.

(

)

G
has a
4
-connected minor

,

such that

Moreover,

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose a
4
-connected graph
G
does not satisfy
(*)
. If

that satisfies

then

has four distinct vertices

,

,

Proof. We divide the proof into a few claims.

Claim 1.

must be contained in a triangle.

Suppose

. Then

or

, for some

, and

is a hub of

. In other words,

is obtained from
B_{n+1}
or

by splitting a hub. Let

be a rim edge of

nor

is a triangle. Let

We first prove that

is 4-connected. From the choice of

, which imply

. In addition, note that

Next, we observe that, in

. From

.

Since

does not satisfy
(*)
, we deduce that

is planar but

is not. However, since

satisfies
(**)
,

must be nonplanar, implying that

. As a result,

. Since

, and thus

From now on we assume that

is contained in a triangle

.

Claim 2.

Neither

nor

is

-connected.

Suppose otherwise. By symmetry, let us assume that

is 4-connected. We first prove that

. To do so, we verify that

can almost play the role of

in
(*)
.

(1)
If

is nonplanar then

is also nonplanar.

To see this, note that

is nonplanar, as

satisfies
(**)
. Also note that

, as

is a triangle. Consequently,

, and thus

, is nonplanar, which proves (1).

(2)

.

Suppose on the contrary that
G^{\prime}\in\cal C\cup L
. Then every edge of
G^{\prime}
belongs to triangle of
G^{\prime}
. As a result,

contains a triangle

with

. It follows that

, which violates the 4-connectivity assumption on

. This contradiction proves (2).

Since
G
does not satisfy
(*)
, we deduce from (1), (2), and the fact

that
G^{\prime}=B
for some

. In other words,

, where

contains both
xy
and

. Since

and, by (
**
),

is 4-connected, we see that

is not incident with a hub of

. That is,

must be a rim edge. Since

is not an edge of

,

can not be a hub and thus

is also a rim edge. Now we see that

is nonplanar since it is obtained by adding a chord to the rim cycle of

. By (

),

is nonplanar, which means

is nonplanar and thus

. Let

be the axle of

. Then

satisfies

, which violates our assumption. This contradiction proves Claim 2.

From Claim 2 and Lemma

we deduce that

and

.

Claim 3.

e
is contained in exactly two triangles.

If

belongs to more than two triangles than

, since

. It follows that

If

belongs to only one triangle

, let

. Since

of degree five,

. Now, by (

),

satisfies (

), a contradiction. Hence,

is contained in exactly two triangles, which is Claim 3.

Now Claim 3 implies that
G\backslash\{x,y\}
has four distinct vertices

such that

and

.

Both

and

satisfy
(**)
.

By symmetry, we only need to consider

. By Lemma

,

is 4-connected. If

is planar then

and

, as

Lemma 2.8
.

If a

-connected graph

does not satisfy

.

On weakly 4-connected graphs

In this paper we also consider two variations of 4-connectivity. Let
G
be 3-connected. Then

is called

-connected
if every 3-separation
(G_{1},G_{2})
of

satisfies
\min\{\|G_{1}\|
,
\|G_{2}\|\}\leq 4
. We call
G

quasi
4
-connected
if every 3-separation
(G_{1},G_{2})
of

satisfies
\min\{|G_{1}|
,
|G_{2}|\}\leq 4
. Observe that if
\|G\|\leq 9
then

is weakly 4-connected if and only if

is 3-connected, and similarly, if
|G|\leq 6
then
G
is quasi 4-connected if and only if
G
is 3-connected. It is not difficult to see that weakly 4-connectivity implies quasi 4-connectivity. However, the reverse implication does not hold, as shown by
W_{5}
. Nevertheless, it is easy to see that a quasi 4-connected graph with ten or more edges is weakly 4-connected if and only if every cubic vertex is contained in at most one triangle. In this section we consider weakly 4-connected graphs. A chain theorem for these graphs has been obtained by Geelen and Zhou
[
4
]
. To describe this theorem we need some preparations.

A subgraph of a graph

is called a

if it consists of four edges

, and it satisfies

. The graph illustrated in Figure

. It is routine to verify that

is the only weakly 4-connected graph on seven vertices such that its edges can be partitioned into three paws. (One may consider the three possible ways to arrange the three triangles of the paws.)

.

Let

denote the set of planar biwheels

for

be the graph consisting of two paths

,

and a matching

(see Figure

by adding a pair of edges

, or

is the prism,

is the cube, and

. In addition,

is the planar dual of

while

is nonplanar. Let

Theorem 3.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

For every weakly
4
-connected graph
G
there exists a
(G,\{K_{4},\Pi\}\cup\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\diamond}\})
-chain of weakly
4
-connected graphs
G_{0},G_{1},...,G_{t}
such that, for each
i=1,...,t
, either
\|G_{i}\|=\|G_{i-1}\|-1
or
G_{i}=G_{i-1}\backslash e/f
, where
G_{i-1}
has a paw containing both
e
and
f
.

In
[
4
]
, this theorem was established and stated for general matroids. The formulation provided here is a straightforward restriction of this general result to graphic matroids. We will not get into a detailed matroidal discussion on how the restriction is obtained, since it would require some preparations on matroid theory. Instead, we provide a few remarks for readers who are interested in these details. First, it is well known that a connected graph is 3-connected if and only if its graphic matroid is 3-connected (see 8.1.9 of
[
6
]
). Also, for any 3-connected graph
G
and any
k\geq 4
, the graphic matroid of
G
has a 3-separation
(E_{1},E_{2})
with
\min\{|E_{1}|,|E_{2}|\}\geq k
if and only if
G
has a 3-separation
(G_{1},G_{2})
with
\min\{\|G_{1}\|,\|G_{2}\|\}\geq k
(see 4.1 of
[
1
]
). Therefore, a connected graph is weakly 4-connected if and only if its graphic matroid is weakly 4-connected. Moreover, a 4-element 3-separating set of the graphic matroid of
G
is exactly a paw of
G
. Finally, as we remarked earlier, a graphic matroid is a trident if and only if the graph is
\Pi
.

Let
K_{6}^{-}
denote the graph obtained from
K_{6}
by deleting an edge and let
K_{3,3}^{+}
denote the graph obtained from
K_{3,3}
by adding an edge. Let
\mathcal{A}_{3}=\mathcal{A}\cup\{A_{3},A_{3}^{\prime}\}
and
\mathcal{B}_{3}=\mathcal{B}\cup\{B_{3},B_{3}^{+}\}
. Our strengthened chain theorem requires the following operation. Let
G
contain seven distinct vertices as shown in Figure

, where
d(x)=d(x^{\prime})=3
and
d(z)=4
. Let
O_{2}(G)
denote
G/xz\backslash xy
.

Operation

, where

and

.

Theorem 3.2
.

For every weakly
4
-connected graph
G
not contained in
\{K_{4},W_{4},K_{6},K_{6}^{-}\}\cup\mathcal{A}_{3}\cup\mathcal{B}_{3}
there exists a
(G,\{K_{3,3}^{+},\Pi\})
-chain of weakly
4
-connected graphs
G_{0},G_{1},...,G_{t}
such that, for each
i=1,...,t
, either
\|G_{i}\|=\|G_{i-1}\|-1
or
G_{i}=O_{2}(G_{i-1})
.

We prove the theorem by proving a sequence of lemmas. We first prove that the 2-edge reduction stated in Theorem
3.1
can be replaced by the more restrictive operation
O_{2}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose a paw
P
of a weakly
4
-connected graph
G
contains two edges
e,f
such that
G\backslash e/f
is weakly
4
-connected. Then at least one of the following holds:

(i)
G=W_{4}
;

(ii)
G
has a weakly
4
-connected minor with
\|G\|-1
edges;

(iii)
G\backslash e/f=O_{2}(G)
.

Proof. Since
G\backslash e/f
is weakly
4
-connected, we must have
|G\backslash e/f|\geq 4
, which implies
|G|\geq 5
. Let
wx
,
xy
,
xz
,
yz
be the four edges of
P
.

Claim 1.

If
yw
or
zw
is an edge of
G
then either (i) or (ii) holds.

By symmetry, suppose
yw\in G
. Let
G_{1}=P+yw
and let
G_{2}
be formed by all other edges of
G
. Then
(G_{1},G_{2})
is a 3-separation of
G
. Since
G
is weakly 4-connected and
\|G_{1}\|\geq 5
, we must have
\|G_{2}\|\leq 4
. Since very vertex outside
G_{1}
is incident with at least three edges, any two of such vertices must be incident with at least five distinct edges. Therefore,
|G_{2}|=4
. Now it is clear that
G=W_{4}
or
B_{3}
, which proves Claim 1.

In the following we assume
yw,zw\not\in G
. Since
d(w)\geq 3
, we must have
|G|\geq 6
.

Claim 2.

If
d(y)=3
or
d(z)=3
then (ii) holds.

By symmetry, we assume
d(y)=3
. Let
v
be the third neighbor of
y
. Then
v\neq w
and thus
\{v,w,z\}
is a cut of
G
. Since
G
is weakly
4
-connected, we must have
|G|=6
and further,
G=A_{3}
. Now we see that (ii) holds, which proves Claim 2.

In the following we assume
d_{G}(y)\geq 4
and
d_{G}(z)\geq 4
.

Claim 3.

G\backslash yz
is 3-connected.

Suppose on the contrary that
G\backslash yz
has a 2-separation
(G_{1},G_{2})
. If some
G_{i}
contains both
y,z
then
(G_{i}+yz,G_{3-i})
would be a 2-separation of
G
, which is impossible. Hence, by symmetry, we may assume
y\in G_{1}\backslash G_{2}
and
z\in G_{2}\backslash G_{1}
. It follows that
V(G_{1}\cap G_{2})
consists of
x
and another vertex
v
. Again, by symmetry, we assume
xw\in G_{1}
. Then, as
d_{G}(x)=3
, we have
N_{G_{2}}(x)=\{z\}
. In addition, since
d_{G}(z)\geq 4
, we see that
|G_{2}|\geq 4
. Now,
\{v,z\}
is a 2-cut of
G
, a contradiction, which proves Claim 3.

If
G\backslash yz
is weakly 4-connected then (ii) holds. Hence, we assume that
G\backslash yz
is not weakly 4-connected. By Claim 3,
G\backslash yz
has a 3-separation
(G_{1},G_{2})
with
\|G_{i}\|\geq 5
for both
i=1,2
. Since
(G_{1},G_{2})
cannot be extended into a 3-separation of
G
, we may assume without loss of generality that
y\in G_{1}\backslash G_{2}
and
z\in G_{2}\backslash G_{1}
. Consequently,
x\in G_{1}\cap G_{2}
.

Claim 4.

w\not\in G_{1}\cap G_{2}
.

Suppose
w\in G_{1}\cap G_{2}
. Since
zw\not\in G
,
yw\not\in G
,
d_{G}(z)\geq 4
, and
d_{G}(y)\geq 4
, we must have
|G_{1}|\geq 5
and
|G_{2}|\geq 5
. It follows that both
(G_{1}\cup P,G_{2}\backslash x)
and
(G_{1}\backslash x,G_{2}\cup P)
are 3-separations of
G
. Therefore,
|G_{1}\backslash x|=|G_{2}\backslash x|=4
. Now it is straightforward to verify that
G
is the pyramid
\Pi
. This is impossible since
G\backslash e/f
is not weakly 4-connected. This contradiction proves Claim 4.

By Claim 4, we may assume without loss of generality that
w\in G_{1}\backslash G_{2}
. Then
N_{G_{2}}(x)=\{z\}
. Since
\|G_{2}\|\geq 5
, we have
\|G_{2}\backslash x\|\geq 4
and thus
|G_{2}\backslash x|\geq 4
. It follows that
(G_{1}\cup P,G_{2}\backslash x)
is a 3-separation of
G
. As a result, we must have
\|G_{2}\backslash x\|=4=|G_{2}\backslash x|=d_{G}(z)
. Thus
G
is as shown in Figure
3.3
. From
d_{G}(y)\geq 4
and
d_{G}(w),d_{G}(y^{\prime}),d_{G}(w^{\prime})\geq 3
we see that
\|G\|\geq 12
.

Now we need to determine the locations for
e
and
f
. Note that
f\neq yz
since
G/yz\backslash e
has a vertex of degree 2 (no matter how
e
is chosen), which should not happen in a weakly 4-connected graph. Then it is routine to see that there are three possibilities:
G/xz\backslash e\cong G/xw\backslash xy
,
G/xy\backslash e\cong G/xw\backslash xz
, and
G/xw\backslash yz
. Let
H
denote the resulting graph. In the last two cases, let
H_{1}=H\backslash\{z,x^{\prime}\}
and let
H_{2}
be the subgraph of
H
formed by the five edges incident with
z
or
x^{\prime}
. Then
(H_{1},H_{2})
is a 3-separation of
H
. Moreover, since
\|H\|=\|G\|-2\geq 10
, we conclude that
\|H_{1}\|\geq 5
and thus
H
is not weakly 4-connected. Therefore, the first case must happen and thus
H=O_{2}(G)
, so (iii) holds. ∎

Lemma 3.4
.

If
G\backslash e\in\cal A
then
G/f
is weakly 4-connected for some
f
disjoint from
e
.

Proof. Let
A=G\backslash e
and
n=|G|/2
. Then
A\in\{A_{n},A_{n}^{\prime}\}
and
G=A+e
. Let
x_{1},...,x_{n},y_{1},...,y_{n}
be the vertices of
A
and let their adjacency be specified as in the definition of
A_{n}
and
A_{n}^{\prime}
. In this proof the indices are taken modulo
n
. We first consider the case
n=4
.

Claim 1.

The lemma holds if
n=4
.

For
A=A_{4}
or
A_{4}^{\prime}
, it is straightforward to verify (separately) that, up to isomorphism, there are only two ways to add
e
to
A
, and we may assume they are
y_{3}x_{4}
and
y_{3}x_{1}
. So we have four cases, with two choices of
A
and two choices of
e
. Let
f=x_{2}x_{3}
. It is routine to check that
(A_{4}+y_{3}x_{4})/f\cong\Pi
and in the other three cases
G/f\cong K
, which (known as a kite) is the graph shown in Figure
4.2
. Since
\Pi
and
K
are weakly 4-connected, Claim 1 is proven.

In the following we assume
n\geq 5
. To simplify our analysis we first “place”
e
in a special position.

Claim 2.

We may assume without loss of generality that
e=y_{3}z
, where
z\not\in\{x_{2},y_{1}\}
.

Since
\{x_{1}y_{1},...,x_{n}y_{n}\}
is a perfect matching of
A
, there must exist distinct
i
and
j
such that
e
is between
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}
and
\{x_{j},y_{j}\}
. Because of the rotational circular symmetry between
\{x_{1},y_{1}\},...,\{x_{n},y_{n}\}
, we may assume
i=3
and
j=i+t
, where
1\leq t\leq n/2
. Then
4\leq j\leq 3+\lfloor n/2\rfloor\leq n
. Now, by the symmetry between
x_{3}
and
y_{3}
, we may assume
e=y_{3}z
, where
z\in\{x_{j},y_{j}\}
. Hence Claim 2 is satisfied.

In the following we prove that
f=x_{2}x_{3}
satisfies the lemma. We first observe that
A/f
is 3-connected, as deleting any vertex from it results in a 2-connected graph. Hence
G/f=A/f+e
is 3-connected. Therefore, if
G/f
is not weakly 4-connected,
G/f
must have a 3-separation
(G_{1},G_{2})
with
\|G_{2}\|\geq\|G_{1}\|\geq 5
. We need the following observation.

Claim 3.

G
is weakly
4
-connected.

For each vertex
v
of
A
, note that
A\backslash v
can be obtained from a 3-connected graph by subdividing each edge at most once, which implies that any 2-cut of
A\backslash v
may cut off at most one vertex. It follows that every 3-cut of
A
may cut off at most one vertex. That is, for every 3-separation
(H_{1},H_{2})
of
A
, we must have
\min\{|H_{1}|,|H_{2}|\}=4
. Since
A
has no triangles, we conclude that
\min\{\|H_{1}\|,\|H_{2}\|\}=3
, which implies
G=A+e
is weakly 4-connected, as every 3-separation of
G
induces a 3-separation of
A
.

Figure 3.4:
A\backslash\{x_{2},x_{3}\}
.

Claim 3 implies that, in the above 3-separation
(G_{1},G_{2})
, vertex
\bar{f}
must be contained in both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Let
S
denote the 3-cut
V(G_{1}\cap G_{2})
. It follows that
S\backslash\bar{f}
is a 2-cut of
G/f\backslash\bar{f}=G\backslash\{x_{2},x_{3}\}=A\backslash\{x_{2},x_{3}\}+e
. Since
n\geq 5
,
A\backslash\{x_{2},x_{3}\}
is a subdivision of a 3-connected graph (see Figure
3.4
). Thus any 2-cut of
A\backslash\{x_{2},x_{3}\}
can only separate some degree-2 vertices from the rest of the graph. It is straightforward to check that these cuts are:
\{y_{1},y_{3}\}
,
\{y_{1},y_{4}\}
,
\{y_{2},y_{4}\}
,
\{y_{4},x_{5}\}
, and
\{y_{1},z_{n}\}
, where
z_{n}=x_{n}
if
A=A_{n}
and
z_{n}=y_{n}
if
A=A_{n}^{\prime}
. By Claim 2,
\{y_{1},y_{4}\}
and
\{y_{2},y_{4}\}
are not 2-cuts of
A\backslash\{x_{2},x_{3}\}+e
. So we only need to consider the other three 2-cuts. If
S=\{\bar{f},y_{1},z_{n}\}
or
S=\{\bar{f},y_{4},x_{5}\}
, then
G_{1}
consists of the three edges incident with
x_{1}
or
x_{4}
, respectively. If
S=\{\bar{f},y_{1},y_{3}\}
, then
G_{1}
consists of the three edges incident with
y_{2}
, and possibly also
y_{3}\bar{f}
(
y_{3}y_{1}
is not an edge, by Claim 2). In all cases,
\|G_{1}\|\geq 5
is not satisfied. This contradiction proves that
G/f
is weakly 4-connected. ∎

The following can be considered as the dual of the last lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
e=xy
be an edge of a
3
-connected graph
G
. If
G/e\in\cal B
then
G
has an edge
f
such that
G\backslash f
is weakly
4
-connected with
\delta(G\backslash f)=3
.

Proof. Let
B=G/e
. Then
B=B_{n}
or
B_{n}^{+}
for some
n\geq 4
. Let
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
be the two hubs of
B
and let
C=v_{1}v_{2}...v_{n}v_{1}
be the rim cycle. We first make two observations.

Claim 1.

B\backslash h_{1}v_{1}
is quasi
4
-connected.

Since
B
is 4-connected, it follows that
B\backslash h_{1}v_{1}
is 3-connected. Now let
(H_{1},H_{2})
be a 3-separation of
B\backslash h_{1}v_{1}
. If
n=4
then
|H_{1}|+|H_{2}|=|B|+3=9
, which implies
\min\{|H_{1}|,|H_{2}|\}\leq 4
. So we assume
n\geq 5
. In this case it is routine to see that any two vertices different from
v_{1}
are linked in
B\backslash h_{1}v_{1}\backslash h_{2}v_{1}
by four independent paths. It follows that some
H_{i}
has to contain all vertices different from
v_{1}
. Therefore,
H_{3-i}
must contain precisely
v_{1}
and its three neighbors, implying
\min\{|H_{1}|,|H_{2}|\}\leq 4
again.

Claim 2.

Let
uv
be an edge of a
3
-connected graph
H
. If
H
is not quasi
4
-connected but
H/uv
is, then
H
has a cubic vertex

such that
|N_{H}(\{u,w\})|=3
or
|N_{H}(\{v,w\})|=3
.

Since
H
is 3-connected but not quasi 4-connected, it has a
3
-separation
(H_{1},H_{2})
with
\min\{|H_{1}|,|H_{2}|\}\geq 5
. By symmetry, let

. Note that
|\{u,v\}\cap V(H_{2})|\leq 1
because otherwise

would admit a 2-cut, which is not the case. It follows that
(H_{1}/uv,H_{2})
is a 3-separation of

. Since
H/uv
is quasi 4-connected and

, we must have
|H_{1}|=5
and
|\{u,v\}\cap V(H_{2})|=1
. Therefore,
V(H_{1}\backslash H_{2})
consists of a vertex
w
and exactly one of

and
v
. It follows that either
|N_{H}(\{u,w\})|=3
or
|N_{H}(\{v,w\})|=3
. Finally, note that

since
H
is 3-connected and
|H_{1}|=5
. If
d_{H}(w)=4
then

is a triangle, implying that
H/uv
has a parallel edge, a contradiction. Thus,
d_{H}(w)=3
, which completes the proof of Claim 2.

Now we prove the lemma by finding a required edge

. We first consider the case when
\bar{e}
is a rim vertex. Without loss of generality, let
\bar{e}=v_{2}
. Up to isomorphism, there are two ways to split

, as shown in Figure
3.5
. Let us assume by symmetry that
N_{G}(x)=\{y,v_{1},h_{1}\}
or
\{y,v_{1},v_{3}\}
. We prove that

satisfies the lemma. Let
H=G\backslash f
. Note that each cubic vertex of

(consisting of

, and possibly

) is contained in at most one triangle, so we only need to prove that

is quasi 4-connected. By Claim 1,
H/e
is quasi 4-connected. Then, by Lemma

,
H
is 3-connected.
Hence, by Claim 2, if
H
is not quasi 4-connected then
H
has a cubic vertex
z\neq x,y
such that
|N_{H}(\{t,z\})|=3
for

or

. Now it is routine to verify that no such

exist, which proves that

is weakly 4-connected.

Figure 3.5:

.

It remains to consider the case when
\bar{e}
is a hub of

. We assume that
B\neq B_{4}
because in this case
\bar{e}
can also be considered as a rim vertex and thus this is a case considered in the last paragraph. As a result, we have
d_{B}(h_{i})\geq 5
(

). Without loss of generality, let us assume
\bar{e}=h_{2}
and
d_{G}(x)\geq d_{G}(y)
. Then we have
d_{G}(x)\geq 4
. We further assume, without loss of generality, that
xv_{1},yv_{2}\in G
, as illustrated in Figure

. If

, else we choose

. We prove that our choice of

satisfies the lemma. Let

. If
f=h_{1}v_{2}
, neither of the two cubic vertices of

,
v_{2}
and

, is contained in a triangle. If

, each cubic vertex of

(consisting of

and possibly

) is contained in at most one triangle. Therefore, in both cases, to establish the weak 4-connectivity of

we only need to show that

is quasi 4-connected. Let

or 2 with

. Note that

is the only cubic vertex of

outside

. By Claim 1,
H/e
is quasi 4-connected. By Lemma

,

is 3-connected. Hence, by Claim 2, if

is not quasi 4-connected then
|N_{H}(\{v_{i},x\})|=3
or
|N_{H}(\{v_{i},y\})|=3

. Then we have

implies

and thus

properly contains

. Hence,

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose
G
is weakly
4
-connected and
G\not\in\{K_{4},W_{4},K_{6}^{-},\Pi\}\cup\cal A\cup B
. Then
G
must contain a weakly
4
-connected minor
H\not\in\{K_{4}\}\cup\cal A\cup B
such that either
\|H\|=\|G\|-1
or
H=O_{2}(G)
.

Proof. By Theorem
3.1
,
G
has a weakly 4-connected minor
G^{\prime}
such that either
\|G^{\prime}\|=\|G\|-1
or
G^{\prime}=G\backslash e/f
, where
G
has a paw containing both

. Then, by Lemma

5
On 3-connected graphs

In this section we prove the following strengthening of Tutte Theorem. Since 3-connectivity has been extensively studied in the literature, it is possible to obtain a shorter proof for this result using more powerful tools. However, we choose to present a more elementary proof, which is not long either.

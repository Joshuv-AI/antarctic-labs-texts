Character analogues of Cohen-type identities and related Voronoi summation formulas Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 11M06, 11T24. Keywords and phrases. Dirichlet character; Dirichlet- L L functions; Bessel functions; weighted divisor sums; Ramanujan’s lost notebook; Voronoï summation formula.
Debika Banerjee, Khyati Khurana

Abstract
. In [ 1 ] , B. C. Berndt and A. Zaharescu introduced the twisted divisor sums associated with the Dirichlet character while studying the Ramanujan’s type identity involving finite trigonometric sums and doubly infinite series of Bessel functions. Later, in a follow-up paper [ 2 ] , S. Kim extended the definition of the twisted divisor sums to twisted sums of divisor functions. In this paper, we derive identities associated with the aforementioned weighted divisor functions and the modified K K -Bessel function in light of recent results obtained by the first author and B. Maji [ 3 ] . Moreover, we provide a new expression for L ⁡ ( 1 , χ ) L(1,\chi) from which we establish the positivity of L ⁡ ( 1 , χ ) L(1,\chi) for any real primitive character χ \chi . In addition, we deduce Cohen-type identities and then exhibit the Voronoï-type summation formulas for them.



1.
Introduction

We begin by reminiscing about a beautiful identity due to Ramanujan involving the
K
-Bessel function, which is recorded on page 253 of his lost notebook. If
\alpha
and
\beta
are any two positive numbers such that
\alpha\beta=\pi^{2}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{-\nu}(n)n^{\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}(2n\alpha)\ -\sqrt{\beta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{-\nu}(n)n^{\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}(2n\beta)

(1.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Gamma\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)\zeta(\nu)\{\beta^{(1-\nu)/2}-\alpha^{(1-\nu)/2}\}+\ \frac{1}{4}\Gamma\left(-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)\zeta(-\nu)\{\beta^{(1+\nu)/2}-\alpha^{(1+\nu)/2}\},

where
\sigma_{k}(n)=\sum_{d|n}d^{k}
and
K_{\nu}(z)
denotes the modified Bessel function of order
\nu

[
4
, p. 78]
, which is defined as the following

(1.2)

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(z):=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{I_{-\nu}(z)-I_{\nu}(z)}{\sin{\pi\nu}},\ z\in\mathbb{C},\nu\notin\mathbb{Z},

with
I_{\nu}
being the Bessel function of the imaginary argument
[
4
, p. 77]
given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\frac{1}{2}z)^{\nu+2n}}{n!\Gamma{(\nu+n+1)}},\ z\in\mathbb{C}.

Later in 1955, Guinand
[
5

derived a formula almost similar to (

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{4}\gamma-\frac{1}{4}\log(4\beta)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d(n)K_{0}(2n\alpha)\right)=\sqrt{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{4}\gamma-\frac{1}{4}\log(4\alpha)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d(n)K_{0}(2n\beta)\right),

where

denotes Euler’s constant and
K_{0}(z)
is defined by the limit

(1.5)

\displaystyle K_{0}(z):=\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0}K_{\nu}(z).

Koshliakov, in 1929, proved the formula (

[

]

\displaystyle\sum_{a\leq n\leq b}\vskip-5.69046pt^{\prime}d(n)f(n)=\int_{a}^{b}(\log(x)+2\gamma)f(x)d\it{x}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d(n)\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\ (4K_{0}(4\pi\sqrt{nx})-2\pi Y_{0}(4\pi\sqrt{nx}))d\it{x},

(1.7)

\displaystyle Y_{\nu}(z):=\frac{J_{\nu}(z)\cos{\pi\nu}-J_{-\nu}(z)}{\sin{\pi\nu}},\ z\in\mathbb{C},\nu\notin\mathbb{Z},

(1.8)

\displaystyle Y_{n}(z):=\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0}Y_{\nu}(z),\ z\in\mathbb{C},\ n\in\mathbb{Z},

and
J_{\nu}(z)
denotes the Bessel function of the first kind of order
\nu

[
4
, p. 40]

(1.9)

\displaystyle J_{\nu}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}(\frac{1}{2}z)^{\nu+2n}}{n!\Gamma{(\nu+n+1)}},\ z\in\mathbb{C}.

The weighted divisor sums defined in (

and they studied Riesz sum-type identities associated with them. Recently A. Dixit and A. Kesarwani
[
23

studied a new generalization of the modified Bessel function of the second kind. They derived a formula analogous to (

2.
Main Results for the case

In this section, we consider

a non-negative integer and denote it by

The Dirichlet

(2.1)

\displaystyle L(s,\chi):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}},\ \ \Re(s)>1,

where

The Gauss sum of a Dirichlet character modulo

\displaystyle\tau(\chi):=\sum_{h=1}^{q}\chi(h)e^{2\pi ih/q}.

Since our results involve the modified

-Bessel function, it is important to state some related results. The asymptotic estimate for the

-Bessel function defined in (

[

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(x)=\left(\frac{\pi}{2x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-x}+O\left(\frac{e^{-x}}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\ \mbox{as}\ x\rightarrow\infty.

The above expression ensures the absolute convergence of all the infinite series defined in (
1.15
). Throughout this paper, we will consider

as

. We recall that

is defined by (

[

, p. 446]

\displaystyle K_{0}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x\cosh t}dt,

one can see that

. We also note the series representation of

\displaystyle K_{0}(x)=-\log\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)I_{0}(x)+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2m}}{(m!)^{2}}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(m+1)}{\Gamma(m+1)},

5.
Preliminaries

We begin this section by recalling and proving some important results which will be used throughout the paper.

The Mellin transform of a locally integrable function
f(x)
on
(0,\infty)
is defined by

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[f;s]=F(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\ t^{s-1}dt,

provided the integral converges. The basic properties of the Mellin transform follow
immediately from those of the Laplace transform since these transforms are intimately connected. The integral in (
5.1
) defines the Mellin transform in a vertical strip in the
s
plane whose boundaries are determined by the analytic structure of
f(x)
as
x\rightarrow 0+
and
x\rightarrow+\infty
. If we assume that
f(x)
satisfies the following growth condition

(5.2)

\displaystyle f(x)=\begin{cases}&O(x^{-a-\varepsilon})\ \ \mbox{as}\ x\rightarrow 0+,\\
&O(x^{-b+\varepsilon})\ \ \mbox{as}\ x\rightarrow+\infty,\end{cases}

where
\varepsilon>0
and
a<b
, then the integral (
5.1
) converges absolutely in the strip
a<\Re(s)<b
and defines an analytic function there in the strip.
This strip is known as the strip of analyticity of
\mathcal{M}[f;s]
.
Furthermore, the inversion formula for (
5.1
) follows directly from the corresponding inversion formula for the bilateral Laplace transform. Thus,

(5.3)

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}x^{-s}\mathcal{M}[f;s]ds\ \ \ \ (a<c<b),

which is valid at all points
x\geq 0
where
f(x)
is continuous.
For example,
\mathcal{M}[e^{x};s]=\Gamma(s)
for
\Re(s)>0
, and we have the corresponding Mellin’s inversion formula

\displaystyle e^{-y}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\Gamma(s)y^{-s}\mathrm{d}s,

valid for
\Re{(y)}>0
. The functional relations for
\Gamma(s)
are given by
[
31
, p. 73]

(5.4)

\displaystyle\Gamma(s+1)=

\displaystyle s\Gamma(s),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)=2^{1-2s}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(2s),

(5.5)

\displaystyle\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)}.

The following lemma states the asymptotic behaviour of
\Gamma(s)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
32
, p. 38]
In a vertical strip, for s=
\sigma+it
with
a\leq\sigma\leq b
and
|t|\geq 1
,

\displaystyle|\Gamma(s)|=(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}|t|^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\exp^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi|t|}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{|t|}\right)\right).

In our investigation, we shall require the following results related to the Mellin transform of derivatives of a function.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that
\phi
is
n
-times differentiable function and

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[\phi(t);s]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t)t^{s-1}dt=\Phi(s).

If
\phi
satisfies (
5.2
), then

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[\phi^{(n)}(t)t^{n};s]=(-1)^{n}\frac{\Gamma(s+n)}{\Gamma(s)}\Phi(s),

where
s\in\{w\in\mathbb{C};\ a<\Re(w)<b\}
, provided

(5.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0,\infty}t^{s+n-j-1}\phi^{(n-j-1)}(t)=0\ \ j=0,1,\cdots,n-1.

The proof relies on mathematical induction. Using integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[x\phi^{\prime}(x);s]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi^{\prime}(t)\ t^{s}dt=\left[t^{s}\phi(t)\right]_{0}^{\infty}-s\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t)\ t^{s-1}dt.

Noting
\phi(t)
satisfies (

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[x\phi^{\prime}(x);s]=-s\Phi(s)\ \ \mbox{for}\ a<\Re(s)<b.

Suppose the statement of the theorem is true for
n=N
and
\phi
is
N+1

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[x^{N+1}\phi^{(N+1)}(x);s]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{N+1}\phi^{(N+1)}(t)\ t^{s-1}dt

\displaystyle=\left[t^{s+N}\phi^{(N)}(t)\right]_{0}^{\infty}-(s+N)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{N}\phi^{(N)}(t)\ t^{s-1}dt.

As
\phi
satisfies (
5.8
), so we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[\phi^{(N+1)}(t)t^{N+1};s]=-(s+N)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{N}\phi^{(N)}(t)\ t^{s-1}dt=(-1)^{N+1}\frac{\Gamma(s+N+1)}{\Gamma(s)}\Phi(s),

and this completes the proof.
∎

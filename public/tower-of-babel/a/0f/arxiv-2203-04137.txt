Linearisability of divergence-free fields along invariant 2-tori
David Perrella, David Pfefferlé, Luchezar Stoyanov

Abstract
We find conditions under which the restriction of a divergence-free vector field B B to an invariant toroidal surface S S is linearisable. The main results are similar in conclusion to Arnold’s Structure Theorems but require weaker assumptions than the commutation [ B , ∇ × B ] = 0 [B,\nabla\times B]=0 . Relaxing the need for a first integral of B B (also known as a flux function), we assume the existence of a solution u : S → ℝ u:S\to\mathbb{R} to the cohomological equation B | S ​ ( u ) = ∂ n B B|_{S}(u)=\partial_{n}B on a toroidal surface S S mutually invariant to B B and ∇ × B \nabla\times B . The right hand side ∂ n B \partial_{n}B is a normal surface derivative available to vector fields tangent to S S . In this situation, we show that the field B B on S S is either identically zero or nowhere vanishing with B | S / ‖ B ‖ 2 | S B|_{S}/\|B\|^{2}|_{S} being linearisable. We are calling the latter the semi-linearisability of B B (with proportionality ‖ B ‖ 2 | S \|B\|^{2}|_{S} ). The non-vanishing property relies on Bers’ results in pseudo-analytic function theory about a generalised Laplace-Beltrami equation arising from Witten cohomology deformation. With the use of de Rham cohomology, we also point out a Diophantine integral condition where one can conclude that B | S B|_{S} itself is linearisable. The linearisability of B | S B|_{S} is fundamental to the so-called magnetic coordinates, which are central to the theory of magnetically confined plasmas.



I
Introduction

The linearisability of a vector field on a 2-torus is a well understood dynamical problem
Sternberg 1969
;
Kocsard 2009
. A vector field
X
on a 2-torus
S
is said to be
linearisable
if there exists numbers
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and a diffeomorphism
\Phi:S\to\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
\Phi_{*}X
is a constant vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
lowered to
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. That is,

\Phi_{*}X=a\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+b\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

(1)

A slightly weaker condition for
X
is the existence of a positive function
f
on
S
for which
X/f
is linearisable. We will say that such an
X
is
semi-linearisable with proportionality
f
. This is equivalent to the existence of a coordinate system in which the field-lines of
X
are straight
Sternberg 1969
. The field-lines of
X
are known as windings in such a coordinate system
Arnold 1974
.

This paper mainly concerns these properties when
X
is the vector field induced on an invariant 2-torus of a divergence-free vector field in an oriented Riemannian 3-manifold
M
with (or without) boundary. The motivation lies in producing and understanding magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equilibria
Kruskal and Kulsrud 1958
;
Hamada 1962
;
D’Haeseleer 1991
;
Etnyre and Ghrist 2000
, which are solutions to the system of equations

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot B

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(\nabla\times B)\times B

\displaystyle=\nabla\rho,

\displaystyle B\cdot n

\displaystyle=0\text{ on }\partial M,

where
\rho
is a function on
M
interpreted as pressure,
B
is the magnetic field in
M
, and
n
is the outward unit normal of
\partial M
.

The conjecture of Grad
Grad 1967
;
Bruno and Laurence 1996
remains unsettled; that smooth solutions with
p
admitting toroidally nested level sets only exist if
M
has a continuous isometry. One way to better understand this conjecture is to prove necessary structural features of solutions if they exist. A contribution by Arnold in this regard is his structure theorems
Arnold 1966a
;
Arnold 1966b
;
Arnold 1974
. In particular, Arnold obtained the following linearisablility result.

Theorem I.1

(
Arnold 1974
)
.

Suppose that
\rho
has a closed (compact, without boundary) regular level set. Then, the connected components of this level set are invariant tori of
B
and
\nabla\times B
and in some neighbourhood
U\subset M
of such a component, there exist a diffeomorphism
\Phi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times I
where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an interval, such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{*}B|_{U}

\displaystyle=a(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+b(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial y},

\displaystyle\Phi_{*}\nabla\times B|_{U}

\displaystyle=c(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+d(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial y},

where
a,b,c,d:I\to\mathbb{R}
are smooth functions and
z
is the projection onto the factor
I
.

Coordinates in which both
B
and
\nabla\times B
are linear are known as Hamada coordinates
Hamada 1962
. In magnetic confinement fusion, coordinates systems in which
B
is linear (regardless of
\nabla\times B
) are called ”straight field-lines” coordinates
D’Haeseleer 1991
;
Boozer 1982
.

Arnold also remarked
Arnold 1974
that in the case of
\nabla\rho=0
, assuming
B
is non-vanishing, then necessarily

i)

B
is a Beltrami field, namely
\nabla\times B=\lambda B
for some function
\lambda
,

ii)

\lambda
is a first integral and the closed regular level sets of
\lambda
are unions of tori, and

iii)

in a neighborhood
U\subset M
of such a 2-torus, there exists a diffeomorphism
\Phi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times I
and a positive function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
such that,

\displaystyle\Phi_{*}(B|_{U}/f)

\displaystyle=a(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+b(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

where again
a,b:I\to\mathbb{R}
are smooth functions and
z
is the projection onto the factor
I
.

In this paper, we will bring some more attention to this remark. More specifically, we show that it is not particularly a consequence of
B
being Beltrami field or even that of the non-vanishing of
B
. For instance, if one assumes a first integral (we will also state an “infinitesimal version” which features no first integral assumption), we have the following corollary of our methods.

Corollary I.1
.

Let
B
be a vector field on
M
which satisfies, for some function
\rho
,

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot B

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle B\cdot\nabla\rho

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(\nabla\times B)\cdot\nabla\rho

\displaystyle=0.

Let
S
be a closed connected component of a regular level set of
\rho
. Then, the following are equivalent

1.

B
is not identically zero on
S
and
S
is a 2-torus

2.

B
is non-vanishing on
S

and when this is the case, additionally assuming
S\cap\partial M=\emptyset
(or
S\subset\partial M
), there exists a neighbourhood
U\subset M
of
S
and a diffeomorphism
\Phi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times I
where
I
is an open (or half-open) interval in
\mathbb{R}
such that
B|_{U}
is non-vanishing and

\Phi_{*}\left(\frac{B|_{U}}{\|B\|^{2}|_{U}}\right)=a(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+b(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

where
a,b:I\to\mathbb{R}
are smooth functions and
z
is the projection onto the factor
I
.

A strong Beltrami field
B
satisfying
\nabla\times B=\lambda B
where
\lambda
is a constant, can have more complicated topology
Arnold 1974
;
Etnyre and Ghrist 2000
despite being MHD equilibria solutions
Bruno and Laurence 1996
;
Dewar et al. 2008
. Less has been said about the structure (in relation to linearisability) of strong Beltrami fields on invariant tori. In particular, if
\partial M
has a toroidal connected component and
B\cdot n=0
, this component is an invariant 2-torus of
B
and it is reasonable to ask of the linearisability properties of
B
here. This question has been linked with the aforementioned conjecture of Grad. In the Euclidean context,
Enciso et al. 2021
have shown that under non-degeneracy assumptions of a toroidial domain
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, piece-wise smooth MHD equilibria with non-constant pressure exist. The non-degeneracy assumptions include assuming the existence of a strong Beltrami field
B
for which
\partial M
is a Diophantine invariant 2-torus. That is, as in the context of the KAM Theorem, the vector field
X
(induced from
B
on
\partial M
) can be written in the form of Equation (
1
) where the vector
(a,b)
, known as
the frequency vector of
X
with respect to
\Phi
, is a Diophantine vector (a notion which will be defined in Section
II
). Of course, the frequency being Diophantine does not depend on the diffeomorphism
\Phi
chosen (see, for instance, Propositions
B.1
and
B.2
). The authors managed to show that the so-called thin toroidal domains are generically non-degenerate

. In view of Corollary
I.1
, the existence of a first integral of
B
will ensure some structure of
B
. Although, in face of this complicated topology, there is no reason to expect that a first integral exists.

As mentioned, the “infinitesimal version” of Corollary
I.1
does not assume a first integral. We will now state this version in the case most relevant to strong Beltrami fields; namely when
M
is embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
S
is a toroidal (connected) component of the boundary
\partial M
. For instance, relevant to the Stepped Pressure Equilibrium Code
Hudson et al. 2012
(SPEC, a program which numerically solves for MHD equilibria),
S
could be taken as either component of the boundary
\partial M
when
M
is a
hollow torus
, that is, when
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times[0,1]
.

Corollary I.2
.

Let
M
be embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the inherited Euclidean structure. Let
S
with a toroidal boundary component of
M
. Let
n:\partial M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the outward unit normal on
M
. Let
B
be a vector field on
M
satisfying, for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot B

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\nabla\times B

\displaystyle=\lambda B,

\displaystyle B|_{\partial M}\cdot n

\displaystyle=0.

Consider
B|_{S}
, the vector field
B
along
S
. Then, the following are equivalent.

1.

There exists a solution
u\in C^{\infty}(S)
to the cohomological equation,

B|_{S}(u)=\partial_{n}B.

2.

The vector field
B|_{S}
preserves a top-form
\mu
on
S
.

3.

Either
B=0
, or
B
is non-vanishing on
S
and
B|_{S}
is semi-linearisable with proportionality
\|B\|^{2}|_{S}
.

With such a solution

and closed curves
C_{1},C_{2}:[0,1]\to S\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
whose homology classes generate the first homology
H_{1}(S)
, form the integrals

I(C_{i},u)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\exp{(-u(C_{i}(t)))}\det(C_{i}^{\prime}(t),B(C_{i}(t)),n(C_{i}(t)))dt,~i\in\{1,2\}.

If
(I(C_{1},u),I(C_{2},u))
is Diophantine, then
S
is a Diophantine invariant 2-torus for
B|_{S}
. The vector
(I(C_{1},u),I(C_{2},u))
being Diophantine is independent of the solution
u
and curves
C_{1},C_{2}
chosen.

In Corollary
I.2
, if the field is zero on the boundary component
S
,
B|_{S}=0
, then from Gerner’s result
(
Gerner 2021
, Lemma 2.1)
we must have
B=0
on the entire domain
M
(see also Proposition
IV.1
). The function
\partial_{n}B
in Corollary
I.2
and elsewhere will be defined in Section
II
of the main text. It represents a derivative in the normal direction which may be evaluated using any local extension of

. In particular, if a first integral
\rho
of

is constant and regular on
S
, then
u=-\ln\|\nabla\rho\||_{S}
satisfies the cohomological equation in the corollary (this is Proposition
II.1
in Section
II
).

Aside from the 2-torus, the techniques used to show the main results may also be easily applied to the 2-sphere. This gives a similar result to that obtained by
Enciso and Peralta-Salas 2016
for divergence-free vector fields which are not necessarily Beltrami.

Corollary I.3
.

Let
B
be a divergence-free vector field on an oriented Riemannian 3-manifold
M
with boundary. Suppose that
B
and
\nabla\times B
have a mutual first integral
\rho
on
M
with a connected component
S
of a regular level set diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Then,
B
vanishes entirely in a neighbourhood of
S
.

Our other results about vector fields on a 2-torus will mostly be stated in terms of their winding number (or frequency ratios, as in
Arnold 1974
). We have employed a homology-dependent means of defining winding numbers, so that by way of de Rham cohomology, we may compute them with integrals like those in Corollary

. In the context of magnetic confinement

;

, the winding number corresponds to what is known as the rotational transform. The rotational transform plays an integral role in stability of magnetically confined plasmas
Wesson and Campbell 2011
. In a future paper, we will relate known rotational transform formulae to what is presented here.

This paper is structured as follows. First, in Section

, we state the main results with some preliminary definitions. In Section

and the elliptic PDE theory developed by Bers

;

. In Section

I.1
,
I.2
and
I.3
. In Section

II
Main results and definitions

In this section, we state the main results. For this, we will need to first define the normal surface derivative mentioned in the Introduction. For terminology with smooth manifold theory, we follow Lee’s book
Lee 2013
. Unless otherwise stated, everything is assumed to be smooth for convenience.

Definition II.1
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold with boundary with metric
g
. Let
S
be an orientable codimension
1
embedded submanifold with (or without) boundary. Let
\mathcal{V}:S\to TM
be a normal vector field to

\partial_{\mathcal{V}}B|_{p}=g|_{p}([V,B]|_{p},V|_{p}),

where

is a local vector field extending
\mathcal{V}|_{U\cap S}
. This function is called the
normal derivative of
B
with respect to
\mathcal{V}
along
S
.

We will show in Appendix
A
that Definition
II.1
is correct. With respect to this definition, the main part of our results giving Corollary
I.2
is the following.

Theorem II.1
.

Let

be an oriented Riemannian

-manifold with boundary. Let

be an embedded 2-torus in

. Let

be a vector field on

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot B|_{S}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\nabla\times B|_{S}\cdot n

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle=0.

Consider the

-related vector field

A particular application of Theorem

is granted in the special context of first integrals as follows.

Proposition II.1
.

Let

Theorem

is proven by the fact that

is a
P
-harmonic vector field on the 2-torus. We will now discuss the definitions and main results concerning these fields.

Let

be an oriented Riemannian manifold with differential

\delta=(-1)^{nk+n+1}\star d\star

\star
is the Hodge star operator acting on
k
-forms and

. Let

. A

is called

-harmonic
if,

\displaystyle d\omega

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\delta P\omega

\displaystyle=0.

Accordingly, a vector field

on

is called

-harmonic
if its flat (metric dual 1-form)

is a

-harmonic 1-form. Our result concerning
P
-harmonic 1-forms on the 2-torus is the following.

Theorem II.2
.

Let
S
be an oriented Riemannian 2-torus. Let
\mathcal{H}_{P}^{1}(S)
denote the real vector space of
P
-harmonic 1-forms on
S
. Then, the following holds.

1.

\dim\mathcal{H}_{P}^{1}(S)=2
.

2.

The map assigning a
P
-harmonic 1-form to its de Rham cohomology class,

\mathcal{H}_{P}^{1}(S)\ni\omega\mapsto[\omega]\in H^{1}_{\text{dR}}(S)

is a linear isomorphism.

3.

If
\omega\in\mathcal{H}_{P}^{1}(S)
, then
\omega
is either identically zero or non-vanishing.

The cohomology class of a closed 1-form dual to a vector field is not particularly telling of the field-line topology on a oriented Riemannian 2-torus. This will be illustrated by an example in Section
IV
. In particular, the strictly dual result to Theorem
II.2
does not address the field-line topology directly. Nevertheless,
P
-harmonic fields have a winding number, which is metric independent.

Instead of using fractions

to define the winding number, we will use elements
[(a,b)]
of the projective real line
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{R})=(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{0\})/{\sim}
where vectors

are considered equivalent

iff

and

are linearly dependent. This is to account for the case of

and to emphasise the connection with Diophantine vectors.

Definition II.2
.

Let
S
be a 2-torus. Then, a densely-non-vanishing vector field

is said be

if there exists a non-vanishing closed 1-form
\omega
on

such that
\omega(X)=0
. Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in H_{1}(S)
be generating classes of the first homology
H_{1}(S)
. Let
\omega
be a closed 1-form with non-trivial cohomology class
[\omega]\neq 0
such that
\omega(X)=0
. Set

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=-\int_{\gamma_{2}}\omega,

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma_{1}}\omega.

Then,

is said to have

if the vector
(a,b)
is Diophantine. The vector
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is non-zero and hence defines a class
[(a,b)]\in\mathbb{P(R)}
. The class
[(a,b)]
is called the

with respect to the generators
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
.

Here, a vector

The proof of correctness of Definition

-harmonic vector fields on the 2-torus is the following.

Theorem II.3
.

Let

where,

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=-\int_{\gamma_{2}}P\star X^{\flat},

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma_{1}}P\star X^{\flat}.

Theorem II.4
.

Let

. Then, the following are equivalent.

preserves a top-form

on

.

2.

Proof of the main results

Theorem

III.1
Computations with codimension 1 submanifolds

The computations we do here will be of higher generality than needed for Theorem
II.1
since no additional difficulty is met. For the following, let
M
be an oriented Riemannian manifold with boundary, with metric
g
and top form
\mu
. Let
S
be a codimension
1
oriented Riemannian submanifold with boundary. Let

be the outward unit vector field on

. Write
\imath:S\subset M
for the inclusion and

for the inherited area form on

Proposition III.1
.

Assume
\dim M=3
. Let
B
be a vector field on
M
. Set
b=B^{\flat}
and
\omega=\imath^{*}b
. Then

d\omega=g|_{S}(\nabla\times B|_{S},n)\mu_{S}.

Proof.

We have
d\omega=\imath^{*}db
. Relating the Hodge stars

on

and
\star_{S}
on

(see Proposition

in Appendix

\star_{S}\imath^{*}db=\imath^{*}(i_{n}\star db|_{S})=\imath^{*}(i_{n}(\nabla\times B|_{S})^{\sharp})=\imath^{*}(g|_{S}(n,\nabla\times B|_{S}))=g|_{S}(n,\nabla\times B|_{S}).

∎

Proposition III.2
.

Let
N
be a vector field on
M
such that
N|_{S}=n
. Let
B
be a vector field on
M
. Set

and
\omega=\imath^{*}b
. Let

be the codifferential on

\delta_{S}\omega=\nabla\cdot B-g(B,N)\nabla\cdot N-g([N,B],N)~|_{S}.

Proof.

We may write

\delta_{S}\omega=\star_{S}\mathcal{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}\mu_{S}.

We also have the
\imath
-relatedness,

\displaystyle T\imath\circ\omega^{\sharp}=(B-g(B,N)N)\circ\imath

\displaystyle\mu_{S}=\imath^{*}(i_{N}\mu).

Hence,

\mathcal{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}\mu_{S}=\imath^{*}\mathcal{L}_{B-g(B,N)N}i_{N}\mu.

With this,

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{B-g(B,N)N}i_{N}\mu&=\mathcal{L}_{B}i_{N}\mu-\mathcal{L}_{g(B,N)N}i_{N}\mu\\
&=([\mathcal{L}_{B},i_{N}]+i_{N}\mathcal{L}_{B})\mu-(i_{g(B,N)N}di_{X}\mu+di_{g(B,N)N}i_{N}\mu)\\
&=(i_{[B,N]}+i_{N}\mathcal{L}_{B})\mu-g(B,N)i_{N}di_{N}\mu\\
&=i_{[B,N]}\mu+i_{N}\mathcal{L}_{B}\mu-g(B,N)i_{N}(i_{N}d\mu+di_{N}\mu)\\
&=i_{[B,N]}\mu+i_{N}\mathcal{L}_{B}\mu-g(B,N)i_{N}\mathcal{L}_{X}\mu\\
&=i_{[B,N]}\mu+i_{N}(\nabla\cdot B)\mu-g(B,N)i_{N}(\nabla\cdot N)\mu\\
&=i_{[B,N]}\mu+(\nabla\cdot B-g(B,N)\nabla\cdot N)i_{N}\mu.\end{split}

Now, for any vector field
Y
on
M
, since
Y-g(X,Y)X
is
\iota
-related to a vector field
\tilde{Y}
on
S
and
\imath^{*}\mu\in\Omega^{n}(S)=\{0\}
, we get,

\begin{split}\imath^{*}(i_{Y}\mu)&=\imath^{*}(i_{Y-g(Y,N)N+g(Y,N)N}\mu)\\
&=\imath^{*}(i_{Y-g(Y,N)N}\mu)+g(Y,N)|_{S}\imath^{*}(i_{N}\mu)\\
&=i_{\tilde{Y}}\imath^{*}\mu+g(Y,N)|_{S}\mu_{S}\\
&=g(Y,N)|_{S}\mu_{S}.\end{split}

In particular,
\imath^{*}i_{[B,X]}\mu=g([B,X],X)|_{S}\mu_{S}
. Thus,

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}\mu_{S}&=\imath^{*}\mathcal{L}_{B-g(B,N)N}i_{N}\mu\\
&=\imath^{*}(i_{[B,N]}\mu+(\nabla\cdot B-g(B,N)\nabla\cdot N)i_{N}\mu)\\
&=g([B,N],N)|_{S}\mu_{S}+(\nabla\cdot B-g(B,N)\nabla\cdot N)|_{S}\mu_{S}\\
&=(g([B,N],N)+\nabla\cdot B-g(B,N)\nabla\cdot N)|_{S}\mu_{S}.\end{split}

Hence, we arrive at

\begin{split}\delta_{S}\omega&=\star_{S}\mathcal{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}\mu_{S}\\
&=g([B,N],N)+\nabla\cdot B-g(B,N)\nabla\cdot N~|_{S}\\
&=\nabla\cdot B-g(B,N)\nabla\cdot N-g([N,B],N)~|_{S}.\end{split}

∎

If

is tangent to

is the normal surface derivative along

in Definition
II.1
. Moreover, since the operations are local, we may drop the assumption that

has a vector field

). Thus, in the tangential case, we can rewrite our formula intrinsically in terms of

and
S
a follows.

Corollary III.1

III.2
P
-harmonic 1-forms and vector fields on an oriented Riemannian 2-torus

Here, we prove theorems
II.2
and
II.3
. Before specialising to tori, we will consider a general closed oriented Riemannian manifold

. Consider the space of
P
-harmonic

-forms,

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{P}(S)=\{\omega\in\Omega^{k}(S):d\omega=0,~\delta P\omega=0\}.

It is natural to “symmetrise” these equations by the linear automorphism on
\Omega^{1}(S)
given by multiplication
\omega\mapsto P^{-1/2}\omega
, giving the isomorphism,

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{P}(S)\cong\{\omega\in\Omega^{1}(S):d_{p}\omega=0,~\delta_{p}\omega=0\},

where
p=\ln\sqrt{P}
and, following Witten

in his 1982 paper on supersymmetry and Morse theory, for

, we set

\displaystyle d_{h}=e^{h}de^{-h}:\Omega^{k}(S)\to\Omega^{k+1}(S)

\displaystyle\delta_{h}=e^{-h}de^{h}:\Omega^{k}(S)\to\Omega^{k-1}(S).

Although in
Witten 1982
, the case of primary interest is
h
being a non-degenerate Morse function, it is observed for general

that the operators
d_{h}
,
\delta_{h}
are adjoints of one another with respect to the
L^{2}
inner product on
\Omega^{k}(M)
,
d_{h}^{2}=0
,
\delta_{h}^{2}=0
and the relation
d_{h}e^{-h}=e^{h}d
gives an isomorphism of the
k^{\text{th}}
de Rham cohomology group and the cohomology group

H^{k}_{\text{dR}}(S)\cong H^{k}_{\text{W-dR}}(S,h)=\ker(d_{h}:\Omega^{k}(S)\to\Omega^{k+1}(S))/\text{im}(d_{h}:\Omega^{k-1}(S)\to\Omega^{k}(S)).

The cohomology group
H^{k}_{\text{W-dR}}(S,h)
is now known in the literature as a Witten deformation of the cohomology group
H^{k}_{\text{dR}}(S)
. In addition, Witten
Witten 1982
notes that by standard arguments, the kernel of the associated Laplace operator,

\Delta_{h}=d_{h}\delta_{h}+\delta_{h}d_{h}:\Omega^{k}(S)\to\Omega^{k}(S)

has the same dimension as
H^{k}_{\text{dR}}(S)
, that is, the
k^{\text{th}}
Betti number
B^{k}
of
S
. Standard arguments include those present in the proof of the standard Hodge decomposition Theorem for the de Rham complex. These arguments may be adapted to a very large class of complexes known as elliptic complexes
Wells and García-Prada 1980
giving rise to a generalised Hodge decomposition theorem. Although, in our current position, we may already establish the following.

Proposition III.3
.

The assignment
\omega\mapsto[\omega]
of a closed
k
-form on
S
to its de Rham cohomology class gives an isomorphism
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{P}(S)\cong H^{k}_{\text{dR}}(S)
.

Proof.

We first consider the Witten deformation to the cohomology with some
h\in C^{\infty}(S)
. From the adjointness of
d_{h}
and
\delta_{h}
, we have at once that every
d_{h}
-exact
k
-form
\omega
which is
\delta_{h}
-co-closed, is necessarily
\omega=0
. Hence, the restricted linear quotient map

\ker d_{h}\cap\ker\delta_{h}\ni\omega\mapsto[\omega]_{\text{W}}\in H^{k}_{\text{W-dR}}(S,h),

is an injection. Again by adjointness,
\ker d_{h}\cap\ker\delta_{h}=\ker\Delta_{h}
. Hence,
\dim\ker d_{h}\cap\ker\delta_{h}=B^{k}=\dim H^{k}_{\text{W-dR}}(S,h)
. So that, by the rank-nullity theorem, this linear map is an isomorphism. Setting

and recalling the isomorphism
\omega\mapsto P^{-1/2}\omega
, the result follows.
∎

We will now draw our attention to tori. We record Proposition

in this instance.

.

Let

be an oriented Riemannian 2-torus. Then the assignment
\omega\mapsto[\omega]
of a closed 1-form on

. In particular,

.

We will now address the non-vanishing property of the elements of

. For this, we will need to recall elements of Riemann surface theory and in particular some of its extensions due to Bers in the 1950s. Our strategy is to use a conformal equivalence to show that elements of

give pseudo-analytic functions on a flat 2-torus; which by Bers’ work on generalising the Riemann-Roch theorem, have the non-vanishing property.

III.2.1
Elements of Riemann surface and pseudo-analytic function theory for the 2-torus

Here we will introduce the required results from Riemann surface theory and pseudo-analytic function theory. In the next section, we will apply these results and conclude with a proof of Theorem
II.2
.

Recall that a
Riemann surface
,
S
, is a connected 2-manifold with a holomorphic atlas
\mathcal{A}=\{(U_{\alpha},z_{\alpha})\}
; namely, the transition maps
z_{\alpha}\circ z_{\beta}^{-1}
of
\mathcal{A}
are holomorphic between the open subsets
z_{\beta}(U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta})
and
z_{\alpha}(U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta})
of the complex plane (i.e. of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
). Charts
(U,z)
on
S
with holomorphic transition maps
z\circ z_{\beta}^{-1}
for
(U_{\beta},z_{\beta})\in\mathcal{A}
will be called
holomorphic charts
.

Following
Bers 1953b
, if
F,G:S\to\mathbb{C}
are functions such that
\text{Im}(\overline{F}G)>0
,
(F,G)
is called a
generating pair
if at every point
p\in S
, for any holomorphic chart
(U,z)
on
S
about
p
, the representatives of
F,G
in the
(U,z)
are Hölder continuous. The pair
(F,G)
also defines a second pair
(F^{*},G^{*})
given by

\displaystyle F^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\overline{G}}{F\overline{G}-\overline{F}G},

\displaystyle G^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\overline{F}}{F\overline{G}-\overline{F}G}.

The pair
(F,G)
play similar roles to that of
1
and
i
play in the classical theory of holomorphic functions. Bers was able to conclude many similarities between holomorphic and pseudo-analytic functions; including definite orders of poles of meromorphic pseudo-analytic functions and differentials and a generalised Riemann-Roch theorem
Bers 1953b
. We are interested in Bers’ conclusion about differentials on a Riemann surface. We will now define these.

Bers
Bers 1953a
defines a
differential
,
W
, on a domain
D\subset S
(connected and open subset of
S
) to be an assignment
(U,z)\mapsto W/dz
where
(U,z)
is a holomorphic chart with
U\subset D
and
W/dz:U\to\mathbb{C}
is a function such that, given two holomorphic charts
(U,z)
,
(V,\tilde{z})
with
U,V\subset D
, for
p\in U\cap V
, we have

\frac{W}{dz}\bigg|_{p}=\frac{W}{d\tilde{z}}\bigg|_{p}\frac{d\tilde{z}}{dz}\bigg|_{p}

where
\frac{d\tilde{z}}{dz}|_{p}=(\tilde{z}\circ z^{-1})^{\prime}(z(p))
denotes the complex derivative of the holomorphic transition map
\tilde{z}\circ z^{-1}
. Setting
f=\frac{W}{dz}
and
\tilde{f}=\frac{W}{d\tilde{z}}
this relation is also denoted
fdz=\tilde{f}d\tilde{z}
. One may also multiply
W
by a function
f:S\to\mathbb{C}
obtaining a differential
fW
defined in the obvious way.

The differential
W
is said to be
continuous
(or have partial derivatives etc.) if
W/dz
is continuous for every holomorphic chart
(U,z)
with
U\subset D
. Similarly, the integral
\int_{\Gamma}W
along a continuously differentiable curve
C:[0,1]\to D\subset S
may be defined. That is,
\int_{\Gamma}W=\int_{0}^{1}k(t)dt
where
k:[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}
is a function such that, for
t\in[0,1]
, if
(U,z)
is a holomorphic chart with
U\subset D
, where
C(t)\in U
, then
(\varphi\circ C)^{\prime}(t)\in\mathbb{C}
is defined and we set

k(t)=\frac{W}{dz}\bigg|_{C(t)}(\varphi\circ C)^{\prime}(t).

With this, given a continuous differential
W
on
S
and a closed continuously differentiable curve
C:[0,1]\to S
, the number

\text{Re}\int_{C}F^{*}W-i\text{Re}\int_{C}G^{*}W

is called the
(F,G)
-period of
W
over
C
. If the
(F,G)
-period vanishes over any
C
homologous to zero, then
W
is called a
regular
(F,G)
-differential
. The following is a direct consequence of the generalised Riemann-Roch Theorem
(
Bers 1953b
, Page 163-164)
.

Theorem III.1
.

A regular
(F,G)
-differential
W
on a closed Riemann surface
S
of genus
g=1
is either identically zero or non-vanishing.

We will now apply this result in the case of
P
-harmonic forms on an oriented Riemannian 2-torus to prove Theorem
II.2
.

III.2.2

-harmonic 1-forms

The following
(

is a well-known means of giving any smooth surface a Riemann surface structure.

Proposition III.4
.

Let

compatible with the smooth structure on

of holomorphic charts satisfy
\star dx=dy
where

We now will now prepare an application of Bers’ differentials in the context of

-harmonic 1-forms. On any smooth manifold

, the set of smooth functions

-linear extension of integrals of continuous 1-forms along the curve

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

. Corollary

\displaystyle d\omega

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\delta P\omega

\displaystyle=0.

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle d\star p^{-1}\tilde{\omega}

\displaystyle=0.

Now, give

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=p,

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=i/p,

\displaystyle F^{*}

Consider the section

Consider now the neighbourhood
V=\{\nabla\rho\neq 0\}
of
S
in
M
. Set
\tilde{u}=\|\nabla\rho\|
and
N=\nabla f|_{V}/\tilde{u}|_{V}:V\to TM
. We have that

so that
B|_{S}\cdot n=0
and,

\partial_{n}B=g|_{S}([N,B],N)=\frac{[N,B](\rho)}{\tilde{u}}~\bigg|_{S}.

Next,
B(\rho)=0
and
N(\rho)=g(N,\nabla\rho)=\tilde{u}
so that,

\frac{[N,B](\rho)}{\tilde{u}}=\frac{N(B(\rho))-B(N(\rho))}{\tilde{u}}=-B(\tilde{u})/\tilde{u}=B(-\ln\tilde{u}).

Hence, since
B
is tangential, setting

, we have

\frac{[N,B](\rho)}{\tilde{u}}\bigg|_{S}=du(\imath^{*}B).

So that
du(\imath^{*}B)=\partial_{n}B
.
∎

.

Proof of Corollary

.

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle=0,

We will now address the example on the sphere from the introduction.

.

Our results also provide some easy bounds on the size of the space of divergence-free Beltrami fields for a special class of proportionality factors in a similar vein to
Enciso and Peralta-Salas 2016
;
Clelland and Klotz 2020
.

Corollary IV.1
.

Let
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(M)
be a smooth function on an oriented connected Riemannian 3-manifold with boundary which is constant and regular on
\partial M
, where
\partial M
is diffeomorphic to a 2-torus. Then, the space of vector fields
B
satisfying

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot B

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\nabla\times B

\displaystyle=\lambda B,

is at most two dimensional.

Proof.

Note that our assumptions on
\lambda
imply the vector fields under consideration are tangent to the boundary. Let
B
be a vector field satisfying
\nabla\cdot B=0
and
\nabla\times B=\lambda B
. Assume that
B
is not identically zero. Then, Vainshtein’s Lemma for abstract manifolds given by Gerner
(
Gerner 2021
, Lemma 2.1)
immediately implies that
B
does not entirely vanish on
\partial M
. Thus, by Proposition
II.1
and Theorem
II.1
we get that
B
is non-vanishing on
\partial M
. In particular, denoting by
\imath:\partial M\subset M
the inclusion and fixing a point
p\in\partial M
, the map
B\mapsto\imath^{*}B|_{p}
is a linear injection from the space of divergence-free Beltrami fields with proportionality factor
\lambda
into
T_{p}\partial M
.
∎

We will now illustrate with a simple example that the cohomological assumption in Theorem
II.1
is not redundant.

Example IV.1
.

We will first consider on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
the vector field

\hat{B}=f\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+f\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+h\frac{\partial}{\partial z},

where

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=(z+1)\cos{(x+y)},

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=(z^{2}+2z)\sin{(x+y)}.

Eventually, we will lower this vector field into the manifold
M=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
along with some of its properties. To this end, writing
\hat{S}
for the plane
\{z=0\}
, one finds

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\hat{B}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle h|_{\hat{S}}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\nabla\times\hat{B}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

\displaystyle=0.

and denoting by
\hat{\imath}:\hat{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
the inclusion, for any
u\in C^{\infty}(\hat{S})
, we get

du(\imath^{*}\hat{B})=u_{x}f|_{z=0}+u_{y}f|_{z=0}=(u_{x}+u_{y})\cos{(x+y)}.

on the other hand, since
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
is a geodesic vector field of unit length,

\partial_{\hat{n}}\hat{B}=\partial_{z}\cdot[\hat{B},\partial_{z}]=\partial_{z}(\hat{B}\cdot\partial_{z})=h_{z}|_{z=0}=2\sin{(x+y)}.

Hence,
du(\imath^{*}\hat{B})=\partial_{\hat{n}}\hat{B}
has no solutions. Since
f
and
h
are
2\pi
-periodic in
x
and
y
,
\hat{B}
descends to a vector field
B
on
M
. Given the inherited oriented Riemannian structure from
\mathbb{R}^{3}
on
M
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot B

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\nabla\times B|_{S}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\nabla\times B|_{S}\cdot n

\displaystyle=0,

where
S
is the 2-torus embedded in
M
lowered from
\hat{S}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with outward unit normal
n
. The other data shows that there does not exist a solution
u\in C^{\infty}(S)
to
du(\imath^{*}\hat{B})=\partial_{n}B
. This is expected from Theorem
II.1
since we see that
B
has many zeros on
S
but is not identically zero on
S
.

We will now illustrate how the cohomology class of a non-vanishing closed 1-form
\omega
on a 2-torus
S
does not explain the topology of the integral curves of
\omega^{\sharp}
.

Example IV.2
.

Consider the non-vanishing closed 1-forms,

\displaystyle\hat{\omega}

\displaystyle=dy,

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}

\displaystyle=d\sin{x}+dy=\cos{x}dx+dy.

Then,
\hat{\omega}
and
\hat{\eta}
descend to non-vanishing cohomologous 1-forms
\omega
and
\eta
on the 2-torus
S=\mathbb{R}^{2}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. We see that
\omega^{\sharp}
is periodic. However,
\eta^{\sharp}
is not periodic. To see this, consider
X=\hat{\eta}^{\sharp}
and let
C
be an integral curve of
X
and
T>0
. Writing
C=(C^{1},C^{2})
, we have

\dot{C}=(\dot{C}^{1},\dot{C}^{2})=(\cos C^{1},1).

In particular,

\dot{C}^{1}(t)^{2}=\cos{(C^{1}(t))}\dot{C}^{1}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\sin{(C^{1}(t))}.

Hence,

\int_{0}^{T}\cos^{2}{(C^{1}(t))}dt=\int_{0}^{T}\dot{C}^{1}(t)^{2}dt=\sin{(C^{1}(T))}-\sin{(C^{1}(0))}.

Now, assume
p=C(0)
is with
\cos p^{1}\neq 0
. Then, by continuity, we must have

\int_{0}^{T}\cos^{2}{(C^{1}(t))}dt>0.

So that

\sin{(C^{1}(T))}\neq\sin{(C^{1}(0))}.

Hence

\nexists k\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ such that }C^{1}(T)=C^{1}(0)+2\pi k.

Thus, in
S
,
C(T)+2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{2}\neq C(0)+2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Hence,
\eta^{\sharp}
is not periodic. On the other hand, the perpendicular vector fields
W=(\star\omega)^{\sharp}
and
H=(\star\eta)^{\sharp}
have the same winding number with respect to every pair of generators since
[\omega]=[\eta]
and the flows of
W
and
H
are explicitly given by

\displaystyle\psi^{W}(t,p+2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{2})

\displaystyle=p-t(1,0)+2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{2},

\displaystyle\psi^{H}(t,p+2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{2})

\displaystyle=p-t(1,0)+(0,\sin{p^{1}}+\sin{(t-p^{1})})+2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

The curves
t\mapsto\psi^{W}(2\pi t,p+2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{2})
and
t\mapsto\psi^{H}(2\pi t,p+2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{2})
,
t\in[0,1]
, are closed and easily seen to be homologous to the curve
C_{1}:[0,1]\to S
where
C_{1}(t)=[-t(1,0)]
. This situation holds much more generally; as will be discussed in a future paper which will further explore the rotational transform in the context of magnetic confinement fusion.

V
Discussion

Theorem
II.2
in the case of constant
P
may be proven with the classical Riemann-Roch Theorem. In fact, together with Calibi’s work on intrinsically harmonic forms
Calabi 1969
, one can establish the following known result.

Proposition V.1
.

Let
S
be an oriented 2-torus. Let
\omega
be a closed 1-form. Then, the following are equivalent.

1.

\delta\omega=0
for some metric
g
on
S
.

2.

\omega
is either identically zero or non-vanishing.

This is proven in the Appendix
B.2
and is the covariant approach we take to prove the other known result; namely Theorem
II.4
, which is also proven there. It would be preferable to shorten the proof of Theorem
II.2
by a suitable reduction to the constant
P
case. For instance, from the conclusion of Theorem
II.2
we get the following.

Corollary V.1
.

Let
S
be an oriented 2-torus and
0<P\in C^{\infty}(S)
. Let
g
be a metric on
S
and consider the
P
-harmonic 1-forms
\mathcal{H}_{P}^{1}(S,g)
on
(S,g)
. Let
\omega,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{P}^{1}(S,g)
form a basis for
\mathcal{H}_{P}^{1}(S,g)
. Then, there exists a metric
\tilde{g}
with induced Hodge star
\tilde{\star}
satisfying

\tilde{\star}\omega=\eta

so that
\mathcal{H}_{P}^{1}(S,g)=\mathcal{H}^{1}(S,\tilde{g})
, the harmonic 1-forms on
(S,\tilde{g})
.

However, the metrics in Corollary
V.1
are in a sense retrospective of the conclusion of Theorem
II.2
and are non-canonical. More precisely, the Witten-deformed co-differential
\delta_{p}=P^{-1}\delta P
on
(S,G)
has a different kernel to the co-differential
\tilde{\delta}
on
(S,\tilde{g})
when
P
is non-constant in Corollary
V.1
. This may be shown in higher generally as a comparison between different Witten-deformed co-differentials on
S
, without reference to Theorem
II.2
, as follows.

Proposition V.2
.

Let
S
be an oriented Riemannian 2-torus. Let
g
and
\tilde{g}
be Riemannian metrics on
S
. Let
0<P,Q\in C^{\infty}(S)
and consider the operators

\displaystyle\delta_{p}

\displaystyle=P^{-1}\delta P,

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}_{q}

\displaystyle=Q^{-1}\tilde{\delta}Q.

Suppose that

\ker\delta_{p}\subset\ker\tilde{\delta}_{q}.

Then
g
and
\tilde{g}
are conformally equivalent and
P=cQ
for some constant
c>0
.

Proof.

Let
R=P^{-1}Q
,
r=\ln\sqrt{R}
and consider
\tilde{\delta}_{r}=R^{-1}\tilde{\delta}R
. Then, we have
\ker\delta\subset\ker\tilde{\delta}_{r}
. Considering the bundle metrics
\langle,\rangle
and
\langle,\rangle_{\sim}
on
\Omega^{1}(S)
, we have for any
f\in C^{\infty}(S)
and
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(S)
that

\displaystyle\delta(f\omega)

\displaystyle=\delta f-\langle df,\omega\rangle,

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}_{r}(f\omega)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\delta}_{r}\omega-\langle df,\omega\rangle_{\sim}.

In particular, for any
f\in C^{\infty}(S)
and
g
-harmonic 1-form
\zeta
, because also
\delta_{w}\zeta=0
, we get

\displaystyle\delta(f\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\langle df,\zeta\rangle,

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}_{r}(f\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\langle df,\zeta\rangle_{\sim}.

Take two
g
-harmonic 1-forms
\chi^{1},\chi^{2}
which generate the first cohomololgy on
S
. Now, set
\zeta^{i}=\star\chi^{i}
. Then, for each point
p\in S
,
(\zeta^{1}|_{p},\zeta^{2}|_{p})
forms a basis of
\Lambda^{1}(T_{p}S)
. For
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, form
\zeta_{(a,b)}=a\zeta^{1}+b\zeta^{2}
. We claim that, for all
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
(a,b)=cq
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
, for any point
p\in S
, there exists
f\in C^{\infty}(S)
such that
df|_{p}\neq 0
and
\langle df,\zeta_{(a,b)}\rangle=0
.

Indeed, let
0\neq(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Consider the dual vector field
X=\zeta_{(a,b)}^{\sharp}
in the metric
g
. Then, we have the non-vanishing form
\omega=a\chi^{1}+b\chi^{2}
with
\omega(X)=0
. Hence,
X
has winding number
[(a,b)]
and is non-vanishing. By Theorem
II.4
, and Proposition
B.2
in Appendix
B.1
, this means there exists a diffeomorphism
\Phi:S\to\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and a function
0<f\in C^{\infty}(S)
such that

\Phi_{*}(X/f)=a\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+b\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

Now, assume that
(a,b)=cq
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. Then, there exists
0\neq(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
(m,n)\cdot(a,b)=0
. Consider
h_{1},h_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
given by

\displaystyle h_{1}

\displaystyle=\cos(2\pi(mx+ny)),

\displaystyle h_{2}

\displaystyle=\sin(2\pi(mx+ny)).

We have that,
h_{1},h_{2}
are invariant under
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-translations. Hence,
h_{1},h_{2}
descend to
h_{1}^{\prime},h_{2}^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2})
. Then, considering
f_{1}=h_{1}^{\prime}\circ\Phi,g_{2}=h_{2}^{\prime}\circ\Phi\in C^{\infty}(S)
, we see that
df_{1}(X)=0=df_{2}(X)
and that for any point
p\in S
, either
df_{1}|_{p}\neq 0
or
df_{2}|_{p}\neq 0
. This proves the claim.

With this, let
p\in S
. Then, let
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
(a,b)=cq
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. Then, consider the form
\eta=a\zeta^{1}|_{p}+b\zeta^{2}|_{p}\in\Lambda^{1}(T_{p}S)
. Then, take
f\in C^{\infty}(S)
such that
df|_{p}\neq 0
and
\langle df,\zeta_{(a,b)}\rangle=0
. Then, we have

\delta(f\zeta)=-\langle df,\zeta\rangle=0.

So that

-\langle df,\zeta\rangle_{\sim}=\tilde{\delta}_{r}(f\zeta)=0.

Hence, because
df|_{p}\neq 0
,

\langle\tilde{\star}\eta,\eta\rangle=0.

Hence, since
\mathbb{Q}^{2}
is dense in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with respect to the topology induced by the inner product
\langle,\rangle
on
\Lambda^{1}(T_{p}S)
, there exists a dense subset
S
such that, for all
\eta\in S
,
\langle\tilde{\star}\eta,\eta\rangle=0
. Thus, since
\tilde{\star}
is a linear operator,
\langle\tilde{\star}\eta,\eta\rangle=0
for all
\eta\in\Lambda^{1}(S)
. Now, considering
\star
on
\Lambda^{1}(T_{p}S)
, we see that, for all
\eta\in\Lambda^{1}(T_{p}S)
, there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\tilde{\star}\eta=c\star\eta
. Since
\tilde{\star}
and
\star
are linear operators and square to
-\text{Id}
, it follows that for
\eta\in\Lambda^{1}(S)
,
\tilde{\star}\eta=\star\eta
. In particular,
g|_{p}
and
\tilde{g}|_{p}
are conformally equivalent. Since
p\in S
was arbitrary,
g
and
\tilde{g}
are conformally equivalent.

On top-forms,
\tilde{\star}=\kappa\star
and as in the above, on 1-forms,
\tilde{\star}=\star
. Hence

\tilde{\delta}_{r}=R^{-1}\tilde{\delta}R=\kappa\delta_{r}.

Hence,
\ker\delta\subset\ker\delta_{r}
. Now, we have for
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(S)
that

\delta_{r}\omega=\delta\omega-\langle dw,\omega\rangle.

Hence, for any
g
-harmonic 1-form
\zeta
, we have

0=\delta_{r}\zeta=\delta\omega-\langle dr,\omega\rangle=0-\langle dr,\zeta\rangle=\langle dr,\zeta\rangle.

Thus,
r
is constant. Hence,
R
is constant. Thus,
P=cQ
for some constant
c>0
.
∎

In this way, it is seen that
P
-harmonic 1-forms on surfaces have their differences with harmonic 1-forms. On the other hand, they have many similarities: as seen in Section

, Theorem
II.2
, and Corollary
V.1
.

Abstract.
We prove some isomorphisms between exceptional
W
-algebras associated with exceptional simple Lie algebras.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this note is to prove three isomorphisms of vertex algebras, namely those listed in Theorem
1.1
below. All the vertex algebras in question are examples of
W
-algebras, by which we mean vertex algebras obtained via quantised Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction of affine vertex algebras
[
18
]

[
25
]
. These form a large class of vertex algebras with important applications in representation theory and theoretical physics.

The universal affine vertex algebra associated with a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and level parameter
k
is denoted
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
. The reduction takes as an additional parameter a nilpotent element
f\in\mathfrak{g}
and the resulting vertex algebra
W^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
, known as the universal
W
-algebra, depends up to isomorphism on
f
only through its adjoint orbit
\mathbb{O}=G\cdot f
.

For generic
k
the universal
W
-algebra
W^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is simple, while at special values of
k
the structure of its simple quotient can be very difficult to ascertain. For special levels of the form
k=-h^{\vee}+p/u
, introduced and studied by Kac and Wakimoto and called admissible levels
[
28
]
, the simple quotient
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
of
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
is in a certain sense “partially integrable” as a
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
-module. This partial integrability allows much to be said about the structure and representation theory of the simple quotient of
W^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
, which we denote by
W(\mathbb{O},p/u)
.

We prove the following theorem.

1.1 Theorem
.

There exist isomorphisms of vertex algebras

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textup{(1)}\quad W(E_{7}(a_{1}),19/16)&\cong W(B_{3},8/7),\\
\textup{(2)}\quad W(E_{7}(a_{1}),18/17)&\cong W(G_{2},4/17),\\
\textup{(3)}\quad W(E_{8}(a_{1}),31/27)&\cong W(G_{2},9/7).\end{split}

In the theorem statement we have used the Bala-Carter labeling of nilpotent orbits, so
E_{7}(a_{1})
is the subregular nilpotent orbit in
E_{7}
, etc., and on the right hand side the orbits are all principal. We expect a fourth isomorphism

(1.2)

\displaystyle\text{(4)}\quad W(E_{8}(a_{1}),31/28)

\displaystyle\cong W(F_{4},14/13),

whose proof should follow by the method employed in this article. The only obstruction is that certain OPE computations are too burdensome to carry out at the present time.

The
W
-algebras listed above are all examples of rational lisse vertex algebras. For each integer
u\geq 1
there is a nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}_{u}\subset\mathfrak{g}
for which
W(\mathbb{O}_{u},p/u)
is lisse
[
6
]
. In reference to the notion of exceptional pair introduced in
[
29
]
, we refer to such simple
W
-algebras as exceptional.

In
[
7
]
T. Arakawa and the first author have established rationality of a large class of exceptional
W
-algebras (see
[
33
]
in general), and have computed their fusion rules in some cases. E. Rowell
[
34
]
noticed relations between these fusion rules and those of certain simple affine vertex algebras at positive integer level:
W(E_{8}(a_{1}),31/27)
is related to
L_{5}(G_{2})
for instance. These coincidences are explained by combining the isomorphisms listed above with a calculation of fusion rules of principal
W
-algebras, due to Frenkel, Kac and Wakimoto
[
19
]
. Roughly speaking, the Grothendieck ring of the principal
W
-algebra
W(\mathfrak{g},p/u)
factorises into a product of the Grothendieck rings of
L_{p-h^{\vee}}(\mathfrak{g})
and
L_{u-h^{\vee}}(\mathfrak{g})
. Therefore taking either
p
or
u
close to
h^{\vee}
yields a principal
W
-algebra whose fusion rules are similar to those of a simple affine vertex algebra. With this picture in mind, confirmed by computation of central charges and asymptotic growths, the isomorphisms of Theorem
1.1
were discovered. They were then proved by developing character formulas, and using these together with facts about the representation theory of the Virasoro algebra, and explicit OPE computations.

Isomorphism of
W
-algebras has been investigated from several perspectives, and using a variety of methods. The Feigin-Frenkel isomorphism
[
18
]
between Langlands dual principal
W
-algebras is a very important example. Isomorphisms related to the triality phenomenon in theoretical physics
[
22
]

[
23
]
have been studied and proved in
[
32
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
13
]
. The notion of collapsing level has been introduced and studied in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
, motivated by applications
[
3
]
to the representation theory of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Classification of admissible collapsing levels has been approached in
[
8
]
using associated varieties and asymptotics of characters, and using similar techniques, some other isomorphisms between classes of
W
-algebras were also proved. See also
[
7
]
and
[
16
]
. Characters are also fundamental to the approach used in this note, not through their asymptotic properties, but rather to constrain OPE structures in low conformal weight.

2.
Background

2.1.
Vertex algebras

We recall that a vertex algebra (see
[
27
]
,
[
15
]
) consists of a vector space
V
, a distinguished vector
\mathbf{1}
called the vacuum vector, an endomorphism
T:V\rightarrow V
called the translation operator, and a collection of bilinear products
(a,b)\mapsto a_{(n)}b
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The formal series

Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{(n)}z^{-n-1}

is called the field associated with the state
a\in V
(the coefficients
a_{(n)}
are endomorphisms of
V
). These data are required to satisfy (1)
Y(a,z)b\in V((z))
for each pair
a,b\in V
, (2)
Y(\mathbf{1},z)=I_{V}
, (3)
T\mathbf{1}=0
and
[T,Y(a,z)]=\partial_{z}Y(a,z)
for all
a\in V
, (4)
Y(a,z)\mathbf{1}\in V[[z]]
and
Y(a,z)\mathbf{1}|_{z=0}=a
for all
a\in V
, and (5) for each pair
a,b\in V
there exists
N
such that
(z-w)^{N}[Y(a,z),Y(b,w)]=0
.

The vertex algebras we shall be concerned with in this paper are conformal. A conformal structure on a vertex algebra
V
is a grading
V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{n}
by finite dimensional subspaces
V_{n}
, and a vector
L\in V_{2}
such that if
Y(L,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}z^{-n-2}
then (1) the coefficients
L_{n}
furnish
V
with a representation of the Virasoro Lie algebra

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}L_{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}C,\quad[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\delta_{m,-n}\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}C,\quad[C,\mathcal{L}]=0,

in which
C
acts as
cI_{V}
for a constant
c
called the central charge, (2)
T=L_{-1}
, and (3) the subspace
V_{n}
is the eigenspace of
L_{0}
of eigenvalue
n
. If
a\in V_{\Delta}
then it is a straightforward consequence of the definitions that
a_{(n)}V_{k}\subset V_{k+\Delta-n-1}
. We call
\Delta
the conformal weight of
a
.

It is common to denote
a_{(-1)}b
as
:\!ab\!:
, and refer to this term as the normally ordered product of
a
and
b
. The coefficients
a_{(n)}b
for
n\geq 0
form what is called the operator product expansion (OPE) of
a
and
b
. It is convenient to gather these terms into the
\lambda
-bracket

[a_{\lambda}b]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}a_{(n)}b.

In any vertex algebra
V
the following commutator formula holds for all
a,b,c\in V
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]]-[b_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]]=[[a_{\lambda}b]_{\lambda+\mu}c],

as well as further relations involving
\lambda
-brackets and normally ordered products, such as the noncommutative Wick formula
[
27
, Section 3.3]

(2.2)

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}{:bc:}]=:[a_{\lambda}b]c:+:b[a_{\lambda}c]:+\int_{0}^{\lambda}[[a_{\lambda}b]_{\mu}c]\,d\mu.

We say
\{a^{1},\ldots,a^{s}\}\subset V
is a strong generating set if the terms

(2.3)

\displaystyle a^{i_{1}}_{(-n_{1}-1)}a^{i_{2}}_{(-n_{2}-1)}\cdots a^{i_{r}}_{(-n_{r}-1)}\mathbf{1},\quad\text{$r\geq 0$ and $n_{i}\geq 0$}

span
V
. From the axioms involving
\mathbf{1}
and
T
it follows that
(Ta)_{(n)}=-na_{(n-1)}
for all
a\in V
and all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, so one could equally say that
V
is strongly generated by a set
S
if it is spanned by normally ordered polynomials in arbitrary derivatives of elements of
S
.

In Section
4
below we shall use (
2.1
) to constrain possible OPE relations in vertex algebras in which we have good control of the conformal weights of generators and relations.

2.2.
Representation theory of the Virasoro Lie algebra

We review a few standard facts about highest weight modules over the Virasoro Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
that we will use in Section
4
. See
[
17
]
,
[
24
]
or
[
10
]
. We denote by
M(c,h)
the Verma module
U(\mathcal{L})\otimes_{U(\mathcal{L}_{+})}\mathbb{C}v
in which
\mathcal{L}_{+}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathbb{C}L_{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}C
acts on
v
as follows:
Cv=cv
,
L_{0}v=hv
and
L_{>0}v=0
. We denote by
L(c,h)
the irreducible quotient of
M(c,h)
, and for
h=0
we have the the vacuum module
V(c)=M(c,h)/U(\mathcal{L})L_{-1}v
.

Let

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1}{12}\left(c-13+\sqrt{(c-1)(c-25)}\right)\qquad\beta=\sqrt{(\nu+1)^{2}-4h\nu}.

In general the equation

r+\nu s+\beta=0

might have zero, one or infinitely many integer solutions
(r,s)
. For all the values of
c
relevant to this paper, we have
\nu\notin\mathbb{Q}
and therefore in each such case only zero or one solution
(r,s)
. In such cases the only Verma module linked with
M(c,h)
(besides itself) is
M(c,h+rs)
.

2.3.
Simple Lie algebras and nilpotent orbits

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra of rank
\ell
, with Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
and root system
\overline{\Delta}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. A choice of triangular decomposition yields a set
\overline{\Delta}_{+}
of positive roots, and its subset
\Pi=\{\overline{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\overline{\alpha}_{\ell}\}
of simple roots. The simple coroots are
\overline{\alpha}_{i}^{\vee}=2\nu^{-1}(\overline{\alpha}_{i})/(\overline{\alpha}_{i},\overline{\alpha}_{i})
, where
\nu:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is the linear isomorphism induced by the choice of non degenerate invariant bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot):\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
which, by convention, is normalised so that the highest root
\theta
has squared norm
2
. The fundamental weights
\{\varpi_{i}\}
are the elements of the basis of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
canonically dual to the basis
\{\overline{\alpha}_{i}^{\vee}\}
of
\mathfrak{h}
. It is well known that the Weyl vector, defined as
\overline{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\overline{\alpha}\in\overline{\Delta}_{+}}\overline{\alpha}
, coincides with the sum
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varpi_{i}
.

Now let
f\in\mathfrak{g}
be an
\ad
-nilpotent element. We choose an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{e,h,f\}
containing
f
(the existence of which is guaranteed by the Jacobson-Morozov theorem), and we consider the associated Dynkin grading
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{j\in(1/2)\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{j}
of
\mathfrak{g}
by eigenspaces of
\ad(h)
, namely
[h,x]=2jx
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{j}
. A nilpotent orbit is said to be even if the Dynkin grading of any of its elements is integral. In this note we will be concerned with a few specific nilpotent orbits, all of which are even. The centraliser
\mathfrak{g}^{f}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[f,x]=0\}
is a graded subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the Dynkin grading. By
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-theory
\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{-j})=\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{j})
, the graded components of
\mathfrak{g}^{f}
are concentrated in non-positive degrees, and
\dim(\mathfrak{g}^{f}_{-j})=\dim(\mathfrak{g}^{e}_{j})=\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{j})-\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{j+1})
for
j\geq 0
. So it is possible to read off the dimensions
\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{j})
from the list of degrees of a graded basis of
\mathfrak{g}^{e}
. In the principal case each degree appears at most once, and the degrees are precisely the exponents of the Lie algebra. We record the degrees for some relevant principal orbits

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{G_{2}}:{}&(1,5),\\
{B_{3}}:{}&(1,3,5),\\
{F_{4}}:{}&(1,5,7,11),\end{split}

and subregular orbits

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{E_{6}(a_{1})}:{}&(1,2,3,4,5,5,7),\\
{E_{7}(a_{1})}:{}&(1,3,5,5,7,8,9,11,13),\\
{E_{8}(a_{1})}:{}&(1,5,7,9,11,13,14,17,19,23).\end{split}

Here and throughout the paper we denote a nilpotent orbit by its Bala-Carter label
[
9
]

[
12
, Chapter 8]
. Since we may take a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
which contains
h
, it is evidently possible to arrange that the root space decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
be compatible with a given Dynkin grading. We fix such a choice and write
\overline{\Delta}_{>0}
for the set of roots associated with
\mathfrak{g}_{>0}=\bigoplus_{j>0}\mathfrak{g}_{j}
.

2.4.
Admissible weights and the Kac-Wakimoto character formula

For
\mathfrak{g}
as above we consider the untwisted affine Kac-Moody algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]\oplus\mathbb{C}K\oplus\mathbb{C}d
, where

[at^{m},bt^{n}]=[a,b]t^{m+n}+m\delta_{m,-n}(a,b)K,\quad[K,\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}]=0,\quad[d,at^{m}]=mat^{m},

with Cartan subalgebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{C}K\oplus\mathbb{C}d
. We write weights
\lambda\in\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
relative to a decomposition
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}=\mathfrak{h}^{*}\oplus\mathbb{C}\delta\oplus\mathbb{C}\Lambda_{0}
where
\Lambda_{0}(\mathfrak{h}+\mathbb{C}d)=\delta(\mathfrak{h}+\mathbb{C}K)=0
and
\Lambda_{0}(K)=\delta(d)=1
(see
[
26
]
).

The corresponding root system is the union of the sets
\widehat{\Delta}_{+}^{\text{re}}=\{\overline{\alpha}+n\delta\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
of real roots, and
\{n\delta\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}\}
of imaginary roots. Real coroots are defined as in the finite type case, and the corresponding fundamental weights are
\{\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{\ell}\}
where
\Lambda_{i}=\varpi_{i}+a_{i}^{\vee}\Lambda_{0}
. Here the integers
a_{i}^{\vee}=\left<\varpi_{i},\theta^{\vee}\right>
are the comarks, which can be read from
[
26
, pp. 54-55, p. 79]
.

A weight
\lambda\in\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is said to be dominant integral if

\displaystyle\left<\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\right>\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\quad\text{$i=0,1,\ldots,\ell$},

and the set
P_{+}
of such weights is just
\mathbb{Z}_{+}\{\Lambda_{0},\ldots,\Lambda_{\ell}\}
. The level of a weight
\lambda
is the number
\left<\lambda,K\right>
. For example the affine Weyl vector
\rho=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\Lambda_{i}
has level
h^{\vee}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}^{\vee}
the dual Coxeter number of
\mathfrak{g}
. Indeed
\rho=h^{\vee}\Lambda_{0}+\overline{\rho}
.

The formal character of the irreducible
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
-module of dominant integral highest weight
\lambda
is given by the Weyl-Kac character formula

\displaystyle\chi_{L(\lambda)}=\frac{A_{\lambda+\rho}}{D},\quad\text{where}\quad A_{\lambda}=\sum_{w\in\widehat{W}}\varepsilon(w)e^{w(\lambda)}\quad\text{and}\quad D=e^{\rho}\prod_{\alpha\in\widehat{\Delta}_{+}}(1-e^{-\alpha}).

The affine Weyl group
\widehat{W}
, which appears in these formulas, is the semidirect product
W\ltimes t_{Q^{\vee}}
, where
W
is the finite Weyl group and
t_{\alpha}
is the translation acting on
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
via

\displaystyle t_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\lambda+\lambda(K)\alpha-\left[(\alpha,\lambda)+\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}\lambda(K)\right]\delta

In this context one has the Weyl denominator formula

(2.6)

\displaystyle A_{\rho}=D.

The extended affine Weyl group
\widetilde{W}
, which will also appear below, is
W\ltimes t_{P^{\vee}}
where
P^{\vee}=Q^{*}
. The quotient
\widetilde{W}/\widehat{W}
is a finite abelian group isomorphic to
P/Q^{\vee}
.

The class of admissible weights
\lambda\in\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
was introduced by Kac and Wakimoto in
[
28
]
, and it was proven in that article that
\chi_{L(\lambda)}
admits a formula similar to the Weyl-Kac character formula for
\lambda
admissible.

For a weight
\lambda\in\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
we consider the subset of coroots

\Delta_{+}^{\vee,\text{re}}(\lambda)=\{\alpha^{\vee}\in\Delta_{+}^{\vee,\text{re}}\mid\lambda(\alpha^{\vee})\in\mathbb{Z}\}

and
\Pi^{\vee}(\lambda)
the system of simple coroots relative to
\Delta_{+}^{\vee,\text{re}}(\lambda)
. The weight
\lambda
is said to be admissible if it is dominant integral with respect to
\Pi^{\vee}(\lambda)
. The level
k
is said to be principal admissible if
k\Lambda_{0}
is admissible and furthermore

\displaystyle\Pi^{\vee}(k\Lambda_{0})=\{\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}^{\vee},-\theta^{\vee}+uK\}.

(If
\mathfrak{g}
is simply laced then this is, in fact, the only possible form that
\Pi^{\vee}(k\Lambda_{0})
can take. More generally
\Pi^{\vee}(k\Lambda_{0})
might take a similar form in which
\theta^{\vee}
is replaced by
\theta^{\vee}_{\text{long}}
, in which case
k
is said to be coprincipal admissible.) Concretely the principal admissible levels are
k=-h^{\vee}+p/u
where
p,u
are coprime positive integers with
p\geq h^{\vee}
and
u
coprime to the lacety
r^{\vee}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. We say that a weight
\lambda
is principal admissible of level
k
if
\Pi^{\vee}(\lambda)=y\left(\Pi^{\vee}(k\Lambda_{0})\right)
for some
y\in\widetilde{W}
. The principal admissible weights corresponding to a given choice
y=t_{\beta}\overline{y}
, where
\overline{y}\in W
and
\beta\in{P^{\vee}}
, are of the form

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lambda=y(\phi(\nu))-\rho,\quad\nu\in P_{+}^{p,\text{reg}}.

Here
\phi:\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is the isometry
\phi(k\Lambda_{0}+\overline{\lambda}+t\delta)=(k/u)\Lambda_{0}+\overline{\lambda}+ut\delta
and
P_{+}^{p,\text{reg}}
is the set of dominant integral weights at level
p
.

Upon coordinatising
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
as follows

\displaystyle(\tau,z,t)=-2\pi i\left(\tau\Lambda_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}z_{i}\alpha_{i}+t\delta\right),

Kac and Wakimoto obtain the following formula for the character of
L(\lambda)
, for
\lambda
principal admissible as above:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\chi_{L(\lambda)}(\tau,z,t)=\frac{A_{\nu}(u\overline{y}^{-1}t_{-\beta/u}(\tau,z/u,t/u^{2}))}{A_{\rho}(\tau,z,t)}.

In fact we shall typically suppress the coordinate
t
, and write

\displaystyle q=e^{-2\pi i\tau\delta},\quad y_{i}=e^{2\pi iz_{i}\overline{\alpha}_{i}}\quad\text{and}\quad\text{$y_{\alpha}=\prod_{i}y_{i}^{k_{i}}$ for $\alpha=\sum_{i}k_{i}\overline{\alpha}_{i}\in\overline{\Delta}_{+}$},

so in particular the Weyl denominator is

(2.9)

\displaystyle D(q,y_{i})=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})^{\ell}\prod_{\alpha\in\overline{\Delta}_{+}}(1-y_{\alpha}^{-1}q^{n-1})(1-y_{\alpha}q^{n}).

To illustrate we consider
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and
k=-2+2/5
, so the following two weights are principal admissible of level
k
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}=t_{-\varpi}(\phi(\rho))-\rho\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{1}=t_{-2\varpi}(\phi(\rho))-\rho.

The Weyl vector is
\rho=2\Lambda_{0}+\varpi
and the Weyl denominator is

\displaystyle A_{\rho}(q,y)=D(q,y)=y^{1/2}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-y^{-1}q^{n-1})(1-q^{n})(1-yq^{n}).

For
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
and
\lambda=\lambda_{1}
the numerator of (
2.8
) becomes, respectively,
D(q^{5},yq^{-1})
and
D(q^{5},yq^{-2})
. Thus one obtains product formulas

(2.10)

\displaystyle\chi_{L(\lambda_{0})}(q,y)=\frac{D(q^{5},yq^{-1})}{D(q,y)}\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{L(\lambda_{1})}(q,y)=\frac{D(q^{5},yq^{-2})}{D(q,y)}.

2.5.
Affine
W
-algebras

The universal affine vertex algebra of level
k
associated with finite simple
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
and its simple quotient
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
. As a
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
is the vacuum module
U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{+})}\mathbb{C}\mathbf{1}
, where
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}=\mathfrak{g}[t]+\mathbb{C}K+\mathbb{C}d
, acting on
\mathbf{1}
as
\mathfrak{g}[t]+\mathbb{C}d\mapsto 0
and
K\mapsto kI
. The vertex algebra structure is generated by quantum fields
a(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(at^{n})z^{-n-1}
, one for each
a\in\mathfrak{g}
. See
[
21
]
,
[
27
, Chapter 4]
.

If
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is a nilpotent orbit and
f\in\mathbb{O}
, the universal affine
W
-algebra
W^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is constructed via quantised Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction of
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
(defined in
[
18
]
for the principal orbit
\mathbb{O}
and
[
25
]
in general). For
k\neq-h^{\vee}
the algebra
W^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
possesses a conformal structure of central charge
[
25
, Theorem 2.2]

c=\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{0})-\frac{12}{k+h^{\vee}}\left|\overline{\rho}-(k+h^{\vee})\frac{h}{2}\right|^{2}.

The following is a special case of the important structural result
[
31
, Theorem 4.1]
.

2.1 Theorem
.

Let
\{x^{1},\ldots x^{r}\}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}^{f}
, homogeneous with respect to the Dynkin grading. Then
W^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
contains a strong generating set
\{W^{1},\ldots,W^{r}\}
where, if
x^{i}\in\mathfrak{g}^{f}_{j}

is

. Furthermore PBW monomials in

Since the vertex algebra

The structure of the simple quotient of

for the simple quotient in this case.

The simple quotient can be obtained by Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction of a specific irreducible highest weight

-module, more precisely

Here

is the “

-triple containing

[

[

The functor

is a certain cohomology of the complex

is the free fermion vertex superalgebra, it has a strong generating set identified with

and

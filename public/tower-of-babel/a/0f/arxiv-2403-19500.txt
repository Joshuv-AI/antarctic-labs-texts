Tensor Network-Constrained Kernel Machines as Gaussian Processes
Frederiek Wesel, Kim Batselier

Abstract
Tensor Networks ( tns )have recently been used to speed up kernel machines by constraining the model weights, yielding exponential computational and storage savings. In this paper we prove that the outputs of Canonical Polyadic Decomposition ( cpd )and Tensor Train ( tt )-constrained kernel machines recover a Gaussian Process ( gp ), which we fully characterize, when placing i.i.d. priors over their parameters. We analyze the convergence of both cpd and tt -constrained models, and show how tt yields models exhibiting more gp behavior compared to cpd , for the same number of model parameters. We empirically observe this behavior in two numerical experiments where we respectively analyze the convergence to the gp and the performance at prediction. We thereby establish a connection between tn -constrained kernel machines and gps .

Background

gps
are a collection of random variables such that any finite subset has a joint Gaussian distribution
[
39
]
. They provide a flexible formalism for modeling functions which inherently allows for the incorporation of prior knowledge and the production of uncertainty estimates in the form of a predictive distribution.
More specifically, a
gp
is fully specified by a mean function
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, typically chosen as zero, and a covariance or kernel function
k(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
:

f(\bm{x})\sim\mathcal{GP}(\mu,k(\bm{x},\cdot)).

Given a labeled dataset
\{(\bm{x}_{n},y_{n})\}_{n=1}^{N}
consisting of inputs
\bm{x}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
and i.i.d. noisy observations
y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
,
gps
can be used for modeling the underlying function
f
in classification or regression tasks by specifying a likelihood function.
For example the likelihood

p(y_{n}\mid f(\bm{x}_{n}))=\mathcal{N}(f(\bm{x}_{n}),\sigma^{2}),

(1)

yields a
gp
posterior which can be obtained in closed-form by conditioning the prior
gp
on the noisy observations.
Calculating the mean and covariance of such a posterior crucially requires instantiating and formally inverting the kernel matrix
\bm{K}
such that
k_{n,m}\isdef k(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{x}_{m})
. These operations respectively incur a computational cost of
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
and
\mathcal{O}(N^{3})
and therefore prohibit the processing of large-sampled datasets.

2.1
Basis Function Approximation

The prevailing approach in literature to circumvent the

[

;
37

{k}(\bm{x},\bm{x^{\prime}})\approx{\bm{\varphi}(\bm{x})}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\Lambda}\ \bm{\varphi}(\bm{x^{\prime}}),

(2)

where here

This finite-dimensional kernel approximation ensures a

[
39
]

Its associated

Once more considering a Gaussian likelihood (

whose mean and covariance require only the inversion of the matrix
{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bm{\varphi}(\bm{x}_{n}){\bm{\varphi}(\bm{x}_{n})}^{\mathrm{T}}}

P_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}=MDR_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}
and the number of

P_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}=M(D-2)R_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}^{2}+2MR_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}=\mathcal{O}(MDR_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}^{2})
.
Given the convergence rate of the
clt
for the expression in
Equation

11
to the
gp
in
Equation

3
, denoted as
\mathcal{O}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{R_{\vrule height=4.41676pt,depth=0.73616pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}}}})
with respect to the variable
P_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}
, we can establish the following corollary by substituting
R_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}
as a function of
P_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}
.

Corollary 3.3

(Convergence rate for
cpd
)
.

Under the conditions of
Theorem

3.1
, the function
f_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}(\bm{x})
converges in distribution to the
gp
defined by
Equation

3
. The convergence rate is given by:

f_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}(\bm{x})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\left(\left({\frac{MD}{P_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Due to their hierarchical structure,
tt
models are a composition of
R_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}^{D-1}
variables, but can be represented in a quadratic number of model parameters in
R_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}
, since
P_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}=\mathcal{O}(MDR_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}^{2})
. Expressing then the
clt
convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{R_{\vrule height=4.41676pt,depth=0.73616pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}}^{D-1}}})
as a function of
P_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}
yields the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4

(Convergence rate for
tt
)
.

Under the conditions of
Theorem

3.2
, the function
f_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}(\bm{x})
converges in distribution to the
gp
defined by
Equation

3
. The convergence rate is given by:

f_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}(\bm{x})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\left(\left({\frac{MD}{P_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}}}\right)^{\frac{D-1}{4}}\right).

Therefore, when dealing with identical models in terms of the number of basis functions (
M
), dimensionality of the inputs (
D
), and the number of model parameters (
P_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}=P_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}
),
f_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TT}}(\bm{x})
will converge at a polynomially faster rate than
f_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}(\bm{x})
, thus exhibiting
gp
behavior with a reduced number of model parameters.
In particular, based on
Corollaries

3.3
and
3.4
we expect the
gp
convergence rate of

models to be faster for
D\geq 3
.

These insights are relevant for practitioners engaged with
tn
-constrained kernel machines, as they shed light on the balance between the
gp
and (deep) neural network behavior inherent in these models. Notably,
cpd
and
tt
-constrained models, akin to shallow and

respectively, have the capacity to craft additional nonlinearities beyond the provided basis functions.
This characteristic can result in superior generalization when dealing with a limited number of parameters. However, as the parameter count increases, we expect these models to transition towards
gp
behavior, characterized by a fixed feature representation and static in comparison.

3.2
Consequences for
map
Estimation

As discussed in
Section

2.4
,
tn
-constrained kernel machines are typically trained in the
ml
or
map
framework by constraining the weights
\bm{w}
in the log-likelihood or log-posterior to be a
tn
.
In said
map
context, and e.g. when specifying a normal prior on the model weights
\bm{w}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\Lambda})
, the resulting regularization term
\Omega
is approximated by
\Omega_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TN}}
as

\Omega\isdef{\left\lvert\left\lvert\bm{\Lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{w}\right\rvert\right\rvert}_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\approx\Omega_{\vrule height=4.78339pt,depth=0.79727pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TN}}\isdef{\left\lvert\left\lvert\vrule height=6.00006pt,depth=1.00006pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TN}(\operatorname{\vrule height=6.00006pt,depth=1.00006pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{ten}}\left(\bm{\Lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{w}\right))\right\rvert\right\rvert}_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

where
\bm{\Lambda}=\otimes_{d=1}^{D}\bm{\Lambda}^{(d)}
.
For example, in case of
cpd
-constrained models we have

\Omega_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}={\left\lvert\left\lvert\odot_{d=1}^{D}\left({\bm{W}^{(d)}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{\Lambda}^{(d)}}^{-1}\bm{W}^{(d)}\right)\right\rvert\right\rvert}_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

(17)

This form of regularization is considered for
tt
by
Wahls et al. 2014
;
Novikov et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2018
and for
cpd
by
Wesel and Batselier 2021
;
Wesel and Batselier 2023
. It provides a Frobenius norm approximation of the regularization term which recovers the original
map
estimate as the hyperparameters of
\vrule height=6.00006pt,depth=1.00006pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TN}(\operatorname{\vrule height=6.00006pt,depth=1.00006pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{ten}}\left(\Lambda\bm{w}\right))
are chosen such that
\vrule height=6.00006pt,depth=1.00006pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TN}(\operatorname{\vrule height=6.00006pt,depth=1.00006pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{ten}}\left(\bm{\Lambda}\bm{w}\right))=\operatorname{\vrule height=6.00006pt,depth=1.00006pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{ten}}\left(\bm{\Lambda w}\right)
.
If we now consider the regularization term in the log-posterior of
Theorem

3.1
we end up with

\Omega_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{CPD}}\isdef R^{\frac{1}{D}}\sum_{d=1}^{D}{\left\lvert\left\lvert{\bm{{\Lambda}}^{(d)}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{W}^{(d)}\right\rvert\right\rvert}_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

(18)

This regularization has been applied without the scaling factor
R^{\frac{1}{D}}
and with
\bm{\Lambda}^{(d)}=\text{I}_{M_{d}}
, as observed in the work of
Kargas and Sidiropoulos 2021
, who may not have been aware of the underlying connection at that time.
It does not account for the model interactions across the dimensionality due to the i.d.d. assumptions on the cores.
Contrary to the regularization
\Omega_{\vrule height=4.91673pt,depth=0.81949pt,width=0.0pt\textnormal{TN}}
in
Equation

17
, it provides an approximation which recovers the log-prior
\Omega
and thus the
map
in the limit
.
These considerations point to the fact that if the practitioner is interested only in a
map
estimate which gives weight to the full prior
\bm{w}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\Lambda})
as well as possible, he might be more interested in the regularization of
Equation

17
.
Furthermore, the priors in
Theorems

3.1
and
3.2
provide a sensible initial guess for gradient-based optimization which is invariant w.r.t. the dimensionality of the inputs and the choice of rank hyperparameters. We hence directly address the initialization issues affecting
tn
-constrained kernel machines
[
2
]
by providing a sensible initialization strategy and regularization which does not suffer from vanishing or exploding gradients.

5
Related Work

Our contribution is closely tied to the links between Bayesian neural networks and
gps
, first established for single-layer single-output neural networks
[
31
;
32
]
having sigmoidal
[
49
]
, Gaussian
[
50
]
and rectified linear unit
[
5
]
as activation function.

This idea was extended to
dnns
by
Lee et al. 2018
and
Matthews et al. 2018
for the all the most common activation functions. It is important to note that the derivation of
Lee et al. 2018
makes use of recursive application of the
clt
to each layer of the network, while
Matthews et al. 2018
considers the case where the width of each layer grows simultaneously to the others, which is arguably more valuable in practice.

Further extensions have been proposed to
cnns
where the number of channels tends to infinity
[
33
;
14
]
, to
rnns
[
45
]
and to
dnns
having low-rank constraints on the weight matrices
[
30
]
.

In particular
Theorem

3.1
resembles the results of
Neal 1996a
;
Neal 1996b
;
Williams 1997
which relate infinite-width single layer neural networks to
gps
. The
cpd
rank corresponds exactly to the width of the neural network. The crucial difference lies however in the product structure, which is not present in neural networks and introduces a nonlinearity of different kind than the one induced by the activation function, which is linear in the
cpd
and more in general for any
tn
.
tts
on the other hand resemble
dnns
as they map the output of each core to the next one. However, in contrast to
dnns
, the inputs are processed over the depth of the network. For a more in depth discussion we refer the reader to
[
9
]
.

Likewise
Theorem

3.2
is the
tn
counterpart to the works of
Lee et al. 2018
;
Matthews et al. 2018
which relate finite depth neural networks to
gps
. The
tt
ranks are then equivalent to the width of each layer and the activation function is linear.
In contrast to the
dnns
case, the induced
gps
are degenerate.

6
Conclusion

In this paper we proved that
cpd
and
tt
-constrained kernel machines are
gps
in the limit or large
tn
ranks.
We characterized the target
gp
, analyzed the convergence behavior of both models and showed that compared to
cpd
,
tt
-based models converge faster to the
gp
when dealing with higher-dimensional inputs. We empirically demonstrated these properties by means of numerical experiments.

While the
gp
convergence is too slow to warrant these models as
gp
prior approximations, the insights we derived are useful in practice as they shed light on the effects of the choice of
tn
and regularization in these models.

(

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{R_{d}R_{d+1}}}{\bm{\varphi}^{(d)}(x_{d})}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\Lambda}^{(d)}\bm{\varphi}^{(d)}(x_{d}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{R_{2}}}\prod_{d=1}^{2}{\bm{\varphi}^{(d)}({x_{d}})}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\Lambda}^{(d)}\bm{\varphi}^{(d)}({x_{d}}).

B.3
gp
of
tt
-Constrained Kernel Machine in the Simultaneous Limit of the
tt
Ranks

In
Theorem

3.2
we prove by induction that the
tt
-constrained kernel machine converges to a
gp
by taking successive limits of the
tt
ranks. This result is analogous to the work of
Lee et al. 2018
, who prove that for the
dnns
, taking sequentially the limit of each layer.
A more practically useful result consists in the convergence in the
simultaneous
limit of
tt
ranks.

In deep learning
Matthews et al. 2018
prove convergence in the context of
dnns
over the widths of all layers simultaneously. Said theorem has been employed to prove
gp
convergence in the context of convolutional neural networks
[
Garriga-Alonso et al. 2018
]
and in the context of
dnns
where each weight matrix is of low rank
[
Nait-Saada et al. 2023
]
.

Seeing the similarity between
tt
-constrained kernel machines (
Equation

14
) and
dnns
and the technicality of the proof, similarly to
[
Garriga-Alonso et al. 2018
,
Nait-Saada et al. 2023
]
we draw a one-to-one map between the
tt
-constrained kernel machines and the
dnns
considered in
Matthews et al. 2018
. Convergence in the simultaneous limit is then guaranteed by
Matthews et al. 2018
.

We begin by restating the definitions of linear envelope property,
dnns
, linear envelope property and normal recursion as found in
Matthews et al. 2018
.
To make the comparison easier for the reader, we change the indexing notation to match the one in this paper.

Definition B.3

(Linear envelope property for nonlinearities
[
Matthews et al. 2018
]

A nonlinearity

\lvert t(u)\rvert<c+l\lvert u\rvert\ \forall u\in\mathbb{R}.

(25)

Definition B.4

(Fully connected

[

]
)

A fully connected deep neural with one-dimensional output and inputs

is defined recursively such that the initial step is

(26)

the activation step by nonlinear activation function

is given by

g_{r_{d}}^{(d)}=t(f_{r_{d}}^{(d)}),

(27)

and the subsequent layers are defined by the recursion

so that

is the output of the network. In the above,

(Width function

For a given fixed input

at depth

Notes on Milnor-Witt K-theory
F. Déglise

Abstract
. These notes are devoted to the foundations of Milnor-Witt K-theory of fields of arbitrary characteristic and without any perfectness assumptions. Extending the fundamental work of Morel, we establish all its functorial properties as stated in Feld’s theory of Milnor-Witt modules, with a special attention about twists. The main new result is a computation of transfers in the general (in particular inseparable) case in terms of Grothendieck (differential) trace maps. These notes are used as the foundation for an expository work on Chow-Witt groups. They are built upon a series of talks given at the Spring School “Invariants in Algebraic Geometry”, organized by Daniele Faenzi, Adrien Dubouloz and Ronan Terpereau.

1.2.
Barge and Morel obstruction theory

Extending Milnor’s ideas on characteristic classes,
and building on the ideas of motivic homotopy, Jean Barge and Fabien Morel

an algebraic-geometrical analogue of the Euler class

[

The technical innovation of their definition is the construction of an appropriate
algebraic analogue of the integral singular cohomology of a real manifold,
a cohomology ring that they call the

which one now simply calls the

While Milnor K-theory modulo

The study of this functor on fields is the main subject of the present

Before diving into the motivation for this work,

using works of Rost

1.4.
Transfers

In fact, most of the paper is devoted to the study of transfer maps on
Milnor-Witt K-theory. This is no wonder as it was a famous problem
left open by Bass and Tate for Milnor K-theory (
[
BT73
, I.§5]
), only
resolved by Kato in
[
Kat80
, §1.7, Prop. 5]
.
Given a finite field extension
E/k
, or
\varphi:k\rightarrow E
,
the transfer map has the form (see
4.4.6
):

(1.4.0.a)

\Tr_{E/k}^{MW}=\varphi^{*}:\operatorname{K}^{MW}_{n}(E,\omega_{E/k})\rightarrow\operatorname{K}^{MW}_{n}(k),

where
\omega_{E/k}
is the determinant of the cotangent complex of
E/k

(we call this the canonical module see
4.1.5
).
Here again, the twist is essential, though it is trivial
for (and only for) separable extensions.

For finitely generated field extension over a perfect base field of characteristic not
2
,
these transfer maps were introduced by Morel in
[
Mor12
, Chap. 4]
,
in the more general context of strongly
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant sheaves
but mostly avoiding twists. The theory was recast for Milnor-Witt K-theory,
still with the same restriction on fields, by Feld in
[
Fel20b
]
.

There are two methods to define transfers on functors defined on fields.
The first one is to follow the approach of Bass and Tate via residue maps
and what is called after Rost the
Weil reciprocity formula

(see
4.2.4
(2) for the case of the projective line).
The second one is by gluing known transfer maps. This is closer to the approach
of Fasel for defining pushforward on Chow-Witt groups
(see
[
Fas08
, Cor. 10.4.5]
).

Bass-Tate method.
In these notes, we develop the two approaches.
For the Bass-Tate method,
we have chosen to introduce Chow-Witt groups of Dedekind schemes.
This serves both as an illustration of the theory
and as convenient language to express the Weil reciprocity formula:
this amounts to a computation of Chow-Witt groups of quadratic
0
-cycles
of the projective line. We state it here for the convenience of the reader:

Theorem
.

(see
3.4.4
)
Let
k
be a field, and
\mathcal{L}
be an invertible sheaf on the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
.
We let
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
be the restriction of
\mathcal{L}
over the point at
\infty

and
\omega_{\infty}
be the conormal sheaf of the immersion
i_{\infty}:\{\infty\}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
.

Then the Chow-Witt group (in
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-degree
0
)
of quadratic divisors of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{L}

are given (induced by pushforward along

\widetilde{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k},\mathcal{L})\simeq\begin{cases}\GW(k,\omega_{\infty}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\infty})&\deg(\mathcal{L})\text{ even,}\\
\mathbb{Z}&\deg(\mathcal{L})\text{ odd.}\end{cases}

This theorem perfectly illustrates the role of twists in Milnor-Witt K-theory and Chow-Witt groups,
which is the major difference with Milnor K-theory and usual Chow groups.
It was first proved by Fasel in

Applying the above result when

The problem with this approach is to show

Glueing and differential traces.
Instead of proving this directly,
as is done by Morel and Feld,
we use the second mentioned approach.
On the one hand, one has well-defined transfer maps on Milnor K-theory after Kato.
For the Grothendieck-Witt part (based on inner product forms to deal with characteristic

we show that one can define twisted transfer maps by directly using the trace morphism

\Tr_{E/k}^{\omega}:\omega_{E/k}\rightarrow k.

Using this map and a classical method of Scharlau, one deduces transfers for twisted

12

Grothendieck-Witt groups and symmetric bilinear forms

2.1.1

A morphism

\displaystyle(E,\phi)\perp(F,\psi)\rightarrow(E\oplus F,\phi+\psi)

Therefore the set

Theorem 2.5.2
.

Consider as above a discretely valued field

The

(Res1)

\partial_{v}(\eta.\sigma)=\eta.\partial_{v}(\sigma)
, for all

(Res2)

For any uniformizer

Proof.

(See
[
Mor12
, 3.15, 3.21]
) We first choose some uniformizing parameter

\pi\in\mathcal{M}_{v}
of

A_{*}=\operatorname{K}^{MW}_{*}(\kappa_{v})[\xi]/(\xi-[-1].\xi)

Then the proof reduces to show that the canonical map:

K^{\times}\rightarrow A_{*},(u=a\pi^{m})\mapsto[\bar{a}]+m_{\epsilon}\langle\bar{a}\rangle.\xi

\Theta_{\pi}(\sigma)=s_{v}^{\pi}(\sigma)+\partial_{v}^{\pi}(\sigma).\xi

\displaystyle s_{v}^{\pi}:\operatorname{K}^{MW}_{*}(K)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{K}^{MW}_{*}(\kappa_{v})

\displaystyle\partial_{v}^{\pi}:\operatorname{K}^{MW}_{*}(K)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{K}^{MW}_{*-1}(\kappa_{v})

\partial_{v}(\sigma)=\partial_{v}^{\pi}(\sigma)\otimes\bar{\pi}^{*}.

∎

Consider the notation of the above theorem.

Remark 3.1.4

The fact the scheme

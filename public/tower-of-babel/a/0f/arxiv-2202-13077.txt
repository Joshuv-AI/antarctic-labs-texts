An insight into the q q -difference two-dimensional Toda lattice equation, q q -difference sine-Gordon equation and their integrability
C.X. Li, H.Y. Wang, Y.Q. Yao, S.F. Shen

Abstract
In our previous work [ 1 ] , we constructed quasi-Casoratian solutions to the noncommutative q q -difference two-dimensional Toda lattice ( q q -2DTL) equation by Darboux transformation, which we can prove produces the existing Casoratian solutions to the bilinear q q -2DTL equation obtained by Hirota’s bilinear method in commutative setting. It is actually true that one can not only construct solutions to soliton equations but also solutions to their corresponding B a ¨ \ddot{a} cklund transformations by their Darboux transformations and binary Darboux transformations. To be more specific, eigenfunctions produced by iterating Darboux transformations and binary Darboux transformations for soliton equations give nothing but determinant solutions to their B a ¨ \ddot{a} cklund transformations, individually. This reveals the profound connections between Darboux transformations and Hirota’s bilinear method. In this paper, we shall expound this viewpoint in the case of the q q -2DTL equation. First, we derive a generalized bilinear B a ¨ \ddot{a} cklund transformation and thus a generalized Lax pair for the bilinear q q -2DTL equation. And then we successfully construct the binary Darboux transformation for the q q -2DTL equation, based on which, Grammian solutions expressed in terms of quantum integrals are established for both the bilinear q q -2DTL equation and its bilinear B a ¨ \ddot{a} cklund transformation. In the end, by imposing the 2-periodic reductions on the corresponding results of the q q -2DTL equation, we derive a q q -difference sine-Gordon equation, a modified q q -difference sine-Gordon equation and obtain their corresponding solutions.

4.3
Quantum integral representation

We will first go over some results in quantum calculus
[
24
]
. Denote the
q
-shift operator
\hat{M}_{q}F(x)=F(qx)
. The
q
-difference operator is defined as

D_{q,x}F(x)=\frac{F(qx)-F(x)}{(q-1)x}=\frac{1}{(q-1)x}(\hat{M}_{q}-1)F(x).

Given a function
f(x)
, its anti-derivative
F(x)
for
D_{q,x}F(x)=f(x)
can be written as

F(x)=\frac{1}{1-\hat{M}_{q}}(1-q)xf(x)=(1-q)x\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{j}f(q^{j}x).

In other words, the anti-derivative of
f(x)
or the quantum integral of
f(x)
is

\int f(x)d_{q}x=F(x)=(1-q)x\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{j}f(q^{j}x).

The
q
-difference operator adopted in this paper is a bit different. However, we can still define quantum integrals similarly.
For the
q
-difference operator defined by

\displaystyle D_{1}F(x)=\frac{1}{(q-1)x}(\sigma_{1}-1)F(x),

assume that
D_{1}F(x)=f(x)
for a given function
f(x)
, then we have

\displaystyle F(x)=\frac{1}{1-\sigma_{1}}(1-q)xf(x)=(1-q)x\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{\alpha j}f(q^{\alpha j}x).

Therefore, we can write the quantum integral of
f(x)
formally as

\displaystyle\int f(x)d_{q^{\alpha}}x=F(x)=(1-q)x\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{\alpha j}f(q^{\alpha j}x).

Since

\displaystyle D_{1}\Omega(\theta_{n-1,j},\rho_{n-1,i})=\sigma_{1}(\rho_{n-1,i}^{\dagger})\theta_{n,j},

we have

\displaystyle\Omega(\theta_{n-1,j},\rho_{n-1,i})=\int\sigma_{1}(\rho_{n-1,i}^{\dagger})\theta_{n,j}d_{q^{\alpha}}x

which makes it possible to express the Grammian solution to the bilinear
q
-2DTL equation (
2.4
) in terms of quantum integals

\tau_{n}=\begin{vmatrix}\Omega(\Theta_{n-1},P_{n-1})\end{vmatrix}=\begin{vmatrix}\Omega(\theta_{n-1,j},\rho_{n-1,i})\end{vmatrix}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}=\begin{vmatrix}\int\sigma_{1}(\rho_{n-1,i}^{\dagger})\theta_{n,j}d_{q^{\alpha}}x\end{vmatrix}.

(4.26)

In the same way, one can express
\tau_{n}^{\prime}
in terms of quantum integrals.

Remark 4

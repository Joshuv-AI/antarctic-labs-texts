Abstract
. In this paper, we study the large deviation principle (LDP) for two types (Type I and Type II) of multiplicative Ising models. For Types I and II, the explicit formulas for the free energy functions and the associated rate functions are derived. Furthermore, we prove that those free energy functions are differentiable, which indicates that both systems are characterized by a lack of phase transition phenomena.

S_{N,1}^{\#}=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i^{2}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ S_{N,2}^{\#}=\sum_{i=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+1}^{N}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i^{2}}.

Then we have

Z(\beta)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{I}}}\exp\!\left\{\beta(S_{N,1}^{\#}+S_{N,2}^{\#})\right\}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{I}}}\exp\!\left\{\beta S_{N,1}^{\#}\right\}\cdot\exp\!\left\{\beta S_{N,2}^{\#}\right\}.

Based on the decomposition above, one labels every node of the subtree
\Delta_{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{2}-1}
with symbol ‘
+1
’. Thus, the upper bound of
S_{N,1}^{\#}
can be obtained as

(3.1)

\max\!\left(S_{N,1}^{\#}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}|\mathcal{T}_{i^{2}-1}|=1+\sum_{i=2}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\|M^{i^{2}-2}\|

(this is not the only way to reach the upper bound).
Since the symbols ‘
+1,-1
’ are arbitrary, it follows
-\max(S_{N,1}^{\#})\leq S_{N,1}^{\#}\leq\max(S_{N,1}^{\#})
. This implies

(3.2)

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{I}}}\frac{1}{M(\beta)}\exp\!\left\{\beta S_{N,2}^{\#}\right\}\leq Z(\beta)\leq\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{I}}}M(\beta)\exp\!\left\{\beta S_{N,2}^{\#}\right\},

where
M(\beta)=\exp\!\left\{\beta\max(S_{N,1}^{\#})\right\}
. On the other hand, there exist non-negative integers
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}
satisfying
a_{1}+b_{1}=|\Delta_{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{2}-1}|
and
a_{2}+b_{2}=|\Delta_{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{2}-1}|
such that

(3.3)

p^{a_{1}}(1-p)^{b_{1}}\frac{1}{M(\beta)}\leq\mathbb{E}_{p}\left(\exp\{\beta S_{N,1}^{\#}\}\right)\leq p^{a_{2}}(1-p)^{b_{2}}M(\beta).

Using (
1.7
) and (
3.1

(3.4)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Delta_{N^{2}-1}|}\log\mathbb{E}_{p}\left(\exp\{\beta S_{N,1}^{\#}\}\right)=0.

Hence, combining (
1.8
) and (
3.4
), one gets

\mathcal{F}_{p}(\beta)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathbb{E}_{p}\left(\exp\{\beta S_{N,2}^{\#}\}\right)}{|\Delta_{N^{2}-1}|}.

The proof is complete.

Proof.

Lemma 3.3

For

and

, each node (i.e. finite word) ending with ‘

vertices on the

Proof.

A notable fact is that if two spins can generate an Ising potential on trees, they are on the same path. Thus, the Ising model of Type I implies that for

and

’ on the

To accomplish this aim, we view this node ending with ‘

j

j
’ on the
1
-th level will generate on the

j

1
-th level will generate
R(k^{2}-k+1,j)
paths on the
(k^{2}-k+1)
-th level. This finishes the proof.

are the expectations when this node ending with ‘

Proof of Theorem

.

1.
By Proposition
3.1
, our aim is to compute

\lfloor\!\sqrt{N}\rfloor
-th level to the
(N\!-\!1)
-th level of
\Delta_{N^{2}\!-\!1}

\Delta_{\lfloor\!\sqrt{N}\rfloor\!-\!1}

Furthermore, taking the logarithm, (

\log\mathbb{E}_{p}\left(\exp\!\left\{\beta\!\sum_{k=\lfloor\!\sqrt{N}\rfloor\!+\!1}^{N}\!\sigma_{k}\sigma_{k^{2}}\right\}\right)=\!\sum_{k=\lfloor\!\sqrt{N}\rfloor\!+\!1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}C(k\!-\!1,j)\log\left[E_{j}^{+}\left(1\!+\!\frac{E_{j}^{-}}{E_{j}^{+}}\right)\right].

Therefore, combining the above equation, (
1.7
) and Remark

, we can get

\displaystyle F_{p}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Delta_{N^{2}-\!1}|}\!\sum_{k=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor\!+\!1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}C(k\!-\!1,j)\left[\log E_{j}^{+}+\log\left(1+\frac{E_{j}^{-}}{E_{j}^{+}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\!\sum_{k=\lfloor\!\sqrt{N}\rfloor\!+\!1}^{N}\!\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{C(k\!-\!1,j)R\left(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j\right)}{|\Delta_{N^{2}-\!1}|}\log\left[pe^{\beta}\!+\!(1\!-\!p)e^{-\!\beta}\right]\!+\!\mathcal{G}_{\#}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\log\left[pe^{\beta}+(1-p)e^{-\beta}\right]+\mathcal{G}_{\#}(\beta),

where

(3.10)

\mathcal{G}_{\#}(\beta)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{C(k\!-\!1,j)}{|\Delta_{N^{2}-1}|}\log\left(1+\frac{E_{j}^{-}}{E_{j}^{+}}\right).

2.
In order to show that
F_{p}(\beta)
is differentiable, we need to demonstrate that both
\mathcal{G}_{\#}(\beta)
and its derivatives converge uniformly. For the first statement, we define

(3.11)

B(\beta)\!=\!\frac{(1\!-\!p)e^{\beta}\!+\!pe^{-\beta}}{pe^{\beta}\!+\!(1\!-\!p)e^{-\beta}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathcal{L}(\beta)=\!\lim_{k\to\infty}\!\log\!\left(1\!+\!\!\frac{1\!-\!p}{p}\left[B(\beta)\right]^{R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)}\right).

Then we have

a. If

\mathcal{G}_{\#}(\beta)=0

b. If
B(\beta)=1

\mathcal{L}(\beta)=\log(1/p)

\mathcal{G}_{\#}(\beta)=0

c. If

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\#}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{C(k\!-\!1,j)}{|\Delta_{N^{2}-1}|}\log\left(\frac{1-p}{p}\left[B(\beta)\right]^{R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\log B(\beta).

In conclusion,

For the second statement, we only need to show that the series

\mathcal{S}_{\#}(\beta)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{C(k\!-\!1,j)}{|\Delta_{N^{2}-1}|}\left[\log\left(1+\frac{E_{j}^{-}}{E_{j}^{+}}\right)\right]^{\prime}

converges uniformly with regards to

. Actually, for
k\geq 3

1\leq j\leq d

\displaystyle\left[\log\left(1\!+\!\frac{E_{j}^{-}}{E_{j}^{+}}\right)\right]^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)\frac{1-p}{p}\left[B(\beta)\right]^{R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)\!-\!1}}{1+\frac{1-p}{p}\left[B(\beta)\right]^{R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)}}\cdot\left[B(\beta)\right]^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)\frac{1-p}{p}\left[B(\beta)\right]^{R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)\!-\!1}}{1+\frac{1-p}{p}\left[B(\beta)\right]^{R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)}}\cdot\frac{2(1-2p)}{(pe^{\beta}\!+\!(1\!-\!p)e^{-\beta})^{2}}\text{.}

It is not difficult to see that

\displaystyle\left\arrowvert\left[\log\left(1\!+\!\frac{E_{j}^{-}}{E_{j}^{+}}\right)\right]^{\prime}\right\arrowvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\arrowvert\frac{R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)\frac{1\!-\!p}{p}\!\left[B(\beta)\right]^{R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)\!-\!1}}{\frac{1-p}{p}\left[B(\beta)\right]^{R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)}}\right\arrowvert\!\!\left\arrowvert\frac{2(1\!-\!2p)}{(pe^{\beta}\!+\!(1\!-\!p)e^{-\!\beta})^{2}}\right\arrowvert

\displaystyle=R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)\left\arrowvert\frac{pe^{\beta}\!+\!(1\!-\!p)e^{-\beta}}{(1\!-\!p)e^{\beta}\!+\!pe^{-\beta}}\right\arrowvert\!\left\arrowvert\frac{2(1-2p)}{(pe^{\beta}\!+\!(1\!-\!p)e^{-\beta})^{2}}\right\arrowvert

\displaystyle=R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)\left\arrowvert\frac{2(1-2p)}{p(1\!-\!p)(e^{\beta}\!+\!e^{-\beta})^{2}\!+\!(1\!-\!2p)^{2}}\right\arrowvert

Then, for any bounded closed interval

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{C(k\!-\!1,j)R(k^{2}\!-\!k\!+\!1,j)}{|\Delta_{N^{2}-1}|}N_{a,b}

On the basis of the Weierstrass M-test, the uniform convergence of

is demonstrated. Therefore,

is differentiable because of the arbitrariness of

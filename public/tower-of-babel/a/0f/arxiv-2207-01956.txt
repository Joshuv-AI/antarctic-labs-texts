An embedding theorem for uniform measures on topological groups
Jan Pachl

Abstract
The embedding theorem of Roelcke and Dierolf for the completions of four standard uniform structures on topological groups and their quotients holds more generally for spaces of uniform measures. The natural mappings between the four spaces of uniform measures on a topological group are injective and their restrictions to positive cones are topological embeddings. The same holds for spaces of uniform measures on the quotient of a topological group by a neutral subgroup.

Summary

]

identify several situations in which the natural mapping

Here

The identity mappings

\mathsf{u_{\vee}}G\to\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G

\mathsf{u_{\vee}}G\to\mathsf{u_{R}}G

\mathsf{u_{L}}G\to\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G

and

uniquely extend to uniformly continuous mappings between completions

(1)

A special case of the embedding theorem
of Roelcke and Dierolf
[
3

states that the five mappings in the commutative diagram (1)
are topological embedding,

\widehat{\mathsf{u_{\vee}}G}
in
\widehat{\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G}

is the intersection of the images of
\widehat{\mathsf{u_{L}}G}

(2)

The current paper extends the embedding theorem to the larger spaces

By Theorem

and their restrictions to the positive cones

are topological embedding,

\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(\mathsf{u_{\vee}}G)
in
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G)

is the intersection of the images of
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(\mathsf{u_{L}}G)

(3)

When
{H}

Under the necessary assumption that

\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(\cdot)

(4)

Section

Again the result is an embedding theorem that

Embedding theorems in section
4

Roelcke and Dierolf also derive their embedding theorem from a general result,
formulated in terms of uniform entourages and Cauchy filters.
The approach in the current paper is somewhat different,

When

Besides

Preliminaries

Throughout the paper,
notation and terminology are as in
[
2
]
,
with some additions noted here.
Spaces and groups are by definition non-empty.
Functions are real-valued,
and linear spaces are over the field
\mathbb{R}
of reals.

When
T_{0}
and

are topological spaces,
a mapping
\varphi\colon T_{0}\to T_{1}

iff

T_{0}

.

2.1
Uniform spaces and pseudometrics

For the key results in this paper nothing is lost by assuming
that topological groups and uniform spaces are Hausdorff (a.k.a separated).
However, in dealing with quotient spaces it is hard to avoid
non-Hausdorff structures.
Following Isbell
[
1
]
,
I use the term
preuniformity
(or
preuniform structure
) for
what is a
uniformity
in the terminology of
[
3
]
;
that is, a structure that is not necessarily Hausdorff.
I reserve the term
uniformity
for a Hausdorff preuniformity.
A
preuniform space
is a set
S
with a preuniformity on
S
,
and a
uniform space
is a set
S
with a uniformity on
S
.

A preuniformity is defined by its set of uniformly continuous pseudometrics.
When
X
is a preuniform space,

\mathsf{UP}(X)
denotes the set of all its uniformly continuous pseudometrics.

For any set
\mathcal{P}
of pseudometrics on a set
S
,
the
preuniformity (projectively) induced
by
\mathcal{P}

is the coarsest preuniformity on
S
for which all pseudometrics in
\mathcal{P}

are uniformly continuous.
We also say that
S
with that preuniformity is the

preuniform space induced
by
\mathcal{P}
.
It is a uniform space if and only if
\mathcal{P}
separates the points of
S
.

For two preuniform spaces
X
and
Y
on the same set
S
,
the
supremum

X\vee Y
of
X
and
Y
is the coarsest
preuniform space on
S
that is finer than both
X
and
Y
.
The
infimum

X\wedge Y
of
X
and
Y
is the finest
preuniform space on
S
that is coarser than both
X
and
Y
.

The preuniformity of
X\vee Y
is induced by the set
\mathsf{UP}(X)\cup\mathsf{UP}(Y)

of pseudometrics on
S
,
and
\mathsf{UP}(X\wedge Y)=\mathsf{UP}(X)\cap\mathsf{UP}(Y)
.
A mapping from any preuniform space to
X\vee Y
is uniformly continuous
if and only if it is uniformly continuous as a mapping to
X
and to
Y
.
A mapping from
X\wedge Y
to any preuniform space is uniformly continuous
if and only if it is uniformly continuous as a mapping from
X
and from
Y
.

For a pseudometric
\Delta
on a set
S
and
x,z\,\mathord{\in}\,S
,
write
z^{\bullet}\!\Delta(x):=\Delta(x,z)
.
That defines a real-valued function
z^{\bullet}\!\Delta
on
S
.
If
X
is a preuniform space and
\Delta\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{UP}(X)
then
z^{\bullet}\!\Delta

is uniformly continuous on
X
because it is
\Delta
-Lipschitz.

For a pseudometric
\Delta
on a set
S
,
x\,\mathord{\in}\,S
and
non-empty subsets
A,B
of
S
, define

\displaystyle\Delta(A,B)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\Delta(a,b)\mid a\,\mathord{\in}\,A,b\,\mathord{\in}\,B\}

\displaystyle\Delta(x,A)

\displaystyle:=\Delta(A,x):=\Delta(A,\{x\})

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}(A,B)

\displaystyle:=\max\left(\sup_{a\in A}\Delta(a,B),\sup_{b\,\mathord{\in}\,B}\Delta(A,b)\right)

If
\Delta
is bounded then
\widetilde{\Delta}(A,B)
is finite.
In that case
\widetilde{\Delta}
is a pseudometric on the set of
non-empty subsets of
S
.

In the notation of
[
2
, §4.2]
,

\widetilde{\Delta}
is
\Delta^{\mathsf{H}}
.
For a uniform space
X
, pseudometrics
\widetilde{\Delta}
define
the
hyperspace uniformity

[
1
, Ch.II]
,
also known as the
Hausdorff uniformity

[
3
, Ch.5]
,
on the set of non-empty closed subsets of
X
.

2.2
Topological groups

Topological groups are assumed to be Hausdorff.
For a topological group
G
,
the identity element of
G
is denoted by
e_{G}
.
The uniform spaces
\mathsf{u_{R}}G
,
\mathsf{u_{L}}G
,
\mathsf{u_{\vee}}G
and
\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G

on the set
G
are defined as follows.
Since
G
is Hausdorff,

\mathsf{u_{R}}G
,
\mathsf{u_{L}}G
,
\mathsf{u_{\vee}}G
and
\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G
are in fact uniform,
not merely preuniform, spaces.

•

The
right uniformity
of
G

is induced by the set of all (equivalently, all bounded)
right invariant continuous pseudometrics on
G
.
The uniform space
\mathsf{u_{R}}G

(
\mathsf{r}G
in the notation of
[
2
]
)
is the set
G
with the right uniformity.

•

The
left uniformity
of
G

is induced by the set of all (equivalently, all bounded)
left invariant continuous pseudometrics on
G
.
The uniform space
\mathsf{u_{L}}G
is the set
G
with the left uniformity.

•

The uniformity of
\mathsf{u_{\vee}}G:=\mathsf{u_{L}}G\vee\mathsf{u_{R}}G

is the
upper uniformity
.

•

The uniformity of
\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G:=\mathsf{u_{L}}G\wedge\mathsf{u_{R}}G
is
the
lower
or
Roelcke uniformity
,

The upper uniformity is induced by the set of all
(equivalently, all bounded)
continuous pseudometrics on
G
that are right or left invariant.
Note however that in general the lower uniformity is not induced
by a set of pseudometrics that are both right and left invariant.

A subgroup
{H}
of a topological group
G
is
neutral
iff for every
neighbourhood
U
of
e_{G}
there exists a neighbourhood
V
of
e_{G}

such that
{H}V\subseteq U{H}
.

When
{H}
is a closed subgroup of a topological group
G
,
denote by
G/{H}
the set of left cosets
x{H}
,
x\,\mathord{\in}\,G
,
and by
q
the quotient mapping
G\to G/{H}
.
Structures on
G
yield the corresponding
final structures
on
G/{H}
:

•

The
quotient topology
is the finest topology on the set
G/{H}

for which the mapping
q
is continuous.

•

When
\alpha
is one of
R
,
L
,
\vee
and
\wedge
,
the corresponding
quotient preuniformity
is the finest
preuniformity on the set
G/{H}
for which the mapping
q

is uniformly continuous from
\mathsf{u_{\alpha}}G
to
G/{H}
.
The preuniform space
\mathsf{u_{\alpha}}G/{H}
is the set
G/{H}

with the quotient preuniformity.

Since
G
is assumed to be Hausdorff and
{H}
closed,

\mathsf{u_{R}}G/{H}
and
\mathsf{u_{\vee}}G/{H}
are in fact uniform spaces
and their topologies
coincide with the quotient topology of
G/{H}

[
3
, 5.21]
.
However,
\mathsf{u_{L}}G/{H}
and
\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G/{H}
need not be Hausdorff;
even when they are,
their topologies may be strictly coarser than the quotient
topology
[
3
, Ch.5]
.
But if
{H}
is a closed neutral subgroup of
G
then

\mathsf{u_{L}}G/{H}
and
\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G/{H}
are Hausdorff and their
topologies coincide with the quotient topology
[
3
, 5.28]
.

By Proposition 7.7 in
[
3
]
,
the preuniformity of
\mathsf{u_{L}}G/{H}
is induced
by the set of all left invariant continuous pseudometrics on
G/{H}
.

If
\Delta
is a bounded right invariant continuous pseudometric on
G

then
\widetilde{\Delta}(A,B)=\Delta(A,B)
for
A,B\,\mathord{\in}\,G/{H}
,
and the restriction of the pseudometric
\widetilde{\Delta}
to
G/{H}

is continuous in the quotient topology on
G/{H}
.
By Proposition 7.6 in
[
3
]
,
the uniformity of
\mathsf{u_{R}}G/{H}
is induced by the set of all
restrictions of
\widetilde{\Delta}
to
G/{H}
,
where
\Delta
runs through bounded right invariant continuous
pseudometrics on
G
.

2.3
Uniform measures

This subsection is a short review of definitions and results
in
[
2
]
.

When
H
is a Banach lattice with positive cone
H^{+}
,
for each
h\,\mathord{\in}\,H
the elements
h^{+},h^{-}\,\mathord{\in}\,H^{+}
are defined by

h^{+}:=h\vee 0
,
h^{-}:=(-h)\vee 0
.
Thus
h=h^{+}-h^{-}
.

When
X
is a uniform space,

\mathsf{U}(X)
denotes the space of uniformly continuous
real-valued functions on
X
,
and
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)
denotes the space of bounded functions in
\mathsf{U}(X)
.
With the sup norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
and the pointwise order,

\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)
is a Banach lattice.
A subset
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)
is said to be
\mathsf{UEB}(X)

if the functions in
\mathcal{H}
are uniformly equicontinuous
and uniformly bounded in the sup norm.

The Banach space dual
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}
of
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)

is also the order-bounded dual of
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)
.
It is a Banach lattice in which

\mathfrak{m}^{+}(f)=\sup\;\{\mathfrak{m}(g)\mid g\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{+}\;\text{and }g\leq f\}\quad\text{for }\mathfrak{m}\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast},f\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{+}.

Besides the norm topology,
two other topologies on
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}
are of interest:

•

The topology of uniform convergence on
\mathsf{UEB}(X)
sets,
called the
\mathsf{UEB}
topology
.

•

The topology of simple convergence on the elements of
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)
,
the
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)
-weak topology
.

The
\mathsf{UEB}
uniformity
on
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}
is the
additive uniformity of the
\mathsf{UEB}
topology.
Unless stated otherwise, in this paper the space
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}
and its subsets
are always considered as topological and uniform spaces with their

\mathsf{UEB}
topology and
\mathsf{UEB}
uniformity.

For
x\,\mathord{\in}\,X
, the
point mass at
x
is the functional

\partial(x)\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}
defined by
\partial(x)(f):=f(x)
for

f\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)
.
The mapping
\partial\colon X\to\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}
is a uniform isomorphism
between the uniform space
X
and its image

\partial(X)\subseteq\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}
with its
\mathsf{UEB}
uniformity.

We identify each
x\,\mathord{\in}\,X
with
\partial(x)\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}
,
and denote by
\widehat{X}
the
\mathsf{UEB}
closure of
X
in
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}
.
Then
\widehat{X}
with the
\mathsf{UEB}
uniformity is a complete
uniform space in which
X
is a uniform subspace.
Thus
\widehat{X}
is a uniform completion of
X
.

The space of finite linear combinations of point masses in
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}

(
molecular measures
on
X
) is denoted by
\mathsf{Mol}(X)
.
The
\mathsf{UEB}
closure of
\mathsf{Mol}(X)
in
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}
,
the space of
uniform measures
on
X
, is denoted by
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
.
The space
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
is
\mathsf{UEB}
complete,
and it is a band in the Banach lattice
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{\ast}
;
in particular
\mathfrak{m}^{+},\mathfrak{m}^{-}\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
whenever

\mathfrak{m}\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
.
The elements of
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
are characterized by a continuity property
that will be used further on:
A linear functional
\mathfrak{m}
on
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)
is in
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
if and only if

\mathfrak{m}
is continuous on every
\mathsf{UEB}(X)
subset of
\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)

in the topology of pointwise convergence on
X
.

From the foregoing we have

X\subseteq\widehat{X}\subseteq\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)^{+}\subseteq\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
.
The assignment of
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
to
X
is functorial:
Every uniformly continuous mapping
\varphi\colon X\to Y

uniquely extends to a continuous linear mapping

\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(\varphi)\colon\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)\to\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Y)
,
which maps
\widehat{X}
to
\widehat{Y}

and
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)^{+}
to
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Y)^{+}
.

3
General embedding theorem

This section deals with the spaces
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
,
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Y)
,

\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X\vee Y)
and
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X\wedge Y)

for two uniform spaces
X
and
Y
on the same set of points.
The sufficient condition in Theorem
3.3
will be used
in sections
4
and
5

to derive embedding theorems for topological groups and quotient spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a point-separating set of pseudometrics on a set
S
.
Let
X
be the uniform space induced by
\mathcal{P}
,
and let
Y
be another uniform space on
S
which is coarser than
X
.
Assume that
z^{\bullet}\!\Delta\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U}(Y)
for all
\Delta\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathcal{P}

and
z\,\mathord{\in}\,S
.
Let
\mathfrak{m}\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
,
f\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{+}
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists
g\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U_{b}}(Y)^{+}
such that
g\leq f
and

\lvert\mathfrak{m}(f)-\mathfrak{m}(g)\rvert<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

This is a minor variation of the proof of
[
2
, 6.21]
.
Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
be such that

\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{\prime}\lVert\mathfrak{m}\rVert+\varepsilon^{\prime}
.
By
[
2
, 2.6]

there are
r>0
and a pseudometric

\Delta=\Delta_{0}\vee\Delta_{1}\vee\dots\vee\Delta_{k}
,
where each
\Delta_{j}
is in
\mathcal{P}
,
such that
\lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert<\max(\varepsilon^{\prime},r\Delta(x,y))

for all
x,y\,\mathord{\in}\,S
.

For every finite set
K\subseteq S
and
y\,\mathord{\in}\,S
let

g_{K}(y):=\max_{x\in K}\;(f(x)-\varepsilon^{\prime}-r\Delta(x,y))^{+}
.
By the assumption, for every
x\,\mathord{\in}\,S
the function

x^{\bullet}\!\Delta=\max_{j}x^{\bullet}\!\Delta_{j}
is in
\mathsf{U}(Y)
.
Thus
g_{K}\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U_{b}}(Y)^{+}
.
For
x,y\,\mathord{\in}\,S
we have
f(x)-f(y)<\varepsilon^{\prime}+r\Delta(x,y)
,
hence
f(x)-\varepsilon^{\prime}-r\Delta(x,y)<f(y)
,
hence
g_{K}(y)\leq f(y)
.

The set
\{g_{K}\mid\text{finite }K\subseteq X\}
is
\mathsf{UEB}(X)
and
the net
\{g_{K}\}_{K}
indexed by the directed set of finite subsets of
X

converges pointwise upwards to some
f_{0}\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)^{+}
.
Clearly
f-\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq f_{0}\leq f
.
Since
\mathfrak{m}\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
,
there is
K
for which
\lvert\mathfrak{m}(f_{0})-\mathfrak{m}(g_{K})\rvert<\varepsilon^{\prime}
.
It follows that

\lvert\mathfrak{m}(f)-\mathfrak{m}(g_{K})\rvert\leq\lvert\mathfrak{m}(f)-\mathfrak{m}(f_{0})\rvert+\lvert\mathfrak{m}(f_{0})-\mathfrak{m}(g_{K})\rvert<\varepsilon^{\prime}\lVert\mathfrak{m}\rVert+\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon.\qed

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a point-separating set of pseudometrics on a set
S
.
Let
X
be the uniform space induced by
\mathcal{P}
.
Let
Y
be another uniform space on
S
which is coarser than
X
,
so that the identity mapping
\iota\colon X\to Y
is uniformly continuous.
Assume that
z^{\bullet}\!\Delta\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U}(Y)
for all
\Delta\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathcal{P}

and
z\,\mathord{\in}\,S
.

1.

The mapping
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(\iota)\colon\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)\to\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Y)

is injective.

2.

The restriction of the mapping
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(\iota)
to the positive cone

\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)^{+}
with its
\mathsf{UEB}
topology is a topological embedding
into
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Y)^{+}
with its
\mathsf{UEB}
topology.

3.

If
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(\iota)
maps
\mathfrak{m}\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
to
\mathfrak{n}\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Y)
then
it maps
\mathfrak{m}^{+}
to
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{m}^{-}
to
\mathfrak{n}^{-}

Proof.

In this section I apply Theorem

Although Theorem

When

is

uniformly continuous.
Less obviously,

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

be a topological group,

\Delta
a left invariant continuous pseudometric on

Define the pseudometric

\Delta^{\prime}(x,y):=\Delta(x^{-1}z,y^{-1}z)

3.3

The first step, an analogue of Lemma

Lemma 5.1

on

\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G/{H}
.
In fact,
Q_{\wedge}=\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G/{H}

[
3
, 5.31(c)]
.
On the other hand,
\mathsf{u_{\vee}}G/{H}
may be strictly finer
than
Q_{\vee}

[
3
, 6.22]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
G
be a topological group,
and
{H}
a closed neutral subgroup of
G
.
Write

Q_{L}=\mathsf{u_{L}}G/{H}
,

Q_{R}=\mathsf{u_{R}}G/{H}
,

Q_{\vee}=(\mathsf{u_{L}}G/{H})\vee(\mathsf{u_{R}}G/{H})
,
and
Q_{\wedge}=(\mathsf{u_{L}}G/{H})\wedge(\mathsf{u_{R}}G/{H})
.
For
\alpha\beta\,\mathord{\in}\,\{\vee L,\vee R,L\wedge,R\wedge,\vee\wedge\}

let
\iota_{\alpha\beta}\colon Q_{\alpha}\to Q_{\beta}

be the uniformly continuous identity mapping.

Then for every

\alpha\beta\,\mathord{\in}\,\{\vee L,\vee R,L\wedge,R\wedge,\vee\wedge\}

the mapping

\overline{\iota_{\alpha\beta}}=\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(\iota_{\alpha\beta})
from

\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Q_{\alpha})
to
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Q_{\beta})
is injective
and its restriction to the positive cone
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Q_{\alpha})^{+}

with its
\mathsf{UEB}
topology
is a topological embedding into
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Q_{\beta})^{+}

with its
\mathsf{UEB}
topology.
Moreover,

\overline{\iota_{\vee\wedge}}(\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Q_{\vee}))=\overline{\iota_{L\wedge}}(\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Q_{L}))\,\cap\,\overline{\iota_{R\wedge}}(\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Q_{R})).

Proof.

To apply Theorem
3.3
,
let
\mathcal{P}_{0}
be the set of left invariant continuous pseudometrics
on
G/{H}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{1}
be the set of the pseudometrics
\widetilde{\Delta}
on
G/{H}

where
\Delta
is a bounded right invariant continuous pseudometric on
G
.

By
[
3
, 7.7]
,

\mathcal{P}_{0}
induces
Q_{L}=\mathsf{u_{L}}G/{H}
.
By
[
3
, 7.6]
,

\mathcal{P}_{1}
induces
Q_{R}=\mathsf{u_{R}}G/{H}
.
The assumption in Theorem
3.3
holds
by Lemma
5.1
and Proposition
5.3
.
∎

If the natural mapping from
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Q_{\vee})
to
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(Q_{L})

is a topological embedding then in particular the topology
of
Q_{L}=\mathsf{u_{L}}G/{H}
is the same as the topology of
Q_{\vee}

which is the quotient topology of
G/{H}
.
Thus, by (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) in
5.3
,

{H}
being a neutral subgroup of
G
is not only sufficient
but also necessary for the conclusion of Theorem
5.4
.
For the same reason it is also a necessary condition for the conclusion
of the Roelcke–Dierolf embedding theorem
[
3
, 10.9]
.

6
Counterexample

For a uniform space
X
,
an “unbounded version” of
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
is the space
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(X)
of

free uniform measures

[
2
, Ch.10]
.
The topology of
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(X)
is that of uniform convergence on
uniformly equicontinuous pointwise bounded sets of functions,
called the
\mathsf{UE}
topology
.
As a linear space,

\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(X)
naturally identifies with a subspace of
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
,
but the
\mathsf{UE}
topology on
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(X)
is in general finer
that the
\mathsf{UEB}
topology that
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(X)
inherits from
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
.
The two topologies agree on
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(X)
if and only if

\mathsf{U}(X)=\mathsf{U_{b}}(X)
, and in that case
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(X)=\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
.

The following example shows that,
in spite of similarities between the spaces
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(X)
and
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(X)
,
the embedding theorems proved here for
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}
do not hold for
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}
.
When the spaces
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{u}}(\cdot)
are replaced by
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(\cdot)

in Theorems
4.2
and
5.4
,
the mappings
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(\iota_{\alpha\beta})
need not be topological embeddings.

Let
G
be a topological group such that there is an unbounded
function
f\,\mathord{\in}\,\mathsf{U}(\mathsf{u_{R}}G)
but all functions
in
\mathsf{U}(\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G)
are bounded.
For example, we may take
G
to be the infinite symmetric group
with the topology of pointwise convergence,
which satisfies
\mathsf{U}(\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G)=\mathsf{U_{b}}(\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G)

because
\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G
is precompact
[
3
, 9.14]
.

Take points
x_{j}\,\mathord{\in}\,G
such that
f(x_{j})\geq j
for
j=1,2,\dots
.
Define
\mathfrak{m}_{j}:=\frac{1}{f(x_{j})}\,\partial(x_{j})
,
j=1,2,\dots
.
Then
\lim_{j}\mathfrak{m}_{j}=0
in the
\mathsf{UE}
topology on
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G)^{+}
.
On the other hand,
\mathfrak{m}_{j}
do not converge to
0
in the
\mathsf{UE}
topology
on
\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(\mathsf{u_{R}}G)^{+}
because
\mathfrak{m}_{j}(f)=1
for every
j
.
Thus the natural mapping

\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(\mathsf{u_{R}}G)^{+}\to\mathsf{{\mathfrak{M}}_{F}}(\mathsf{u_{\wedge}}G)^{+}
is not a topological embedding.
